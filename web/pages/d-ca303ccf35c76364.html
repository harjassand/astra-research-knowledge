# Approximate image penalties: sparse-Hamiltonian gap transfer and access gate

**Decision.** Operator-norm approximation suffices to preserve the spectral promise gap, with an explicit additive error budget. It does not suffice to produce a sparse-Hamiltonian instance: a polynomial-time specified-entry evaluator, polynomial-size circuit, or operator-norm approximant can still have exponentially many nonzeros in a row. The exact incidence enforcer \(C_{\rm inc}\) from Cycle 3 has a polynomial-degree Neumann approximation to operator norm \(\eta\), but no polynomial row-list algorithm is established for that approximation. BMVZ supplies no approximation to an image penalty; it supplies a hard high-locality term generator and a promise gap for the logical Hamiltonian.

## 1. Gap-transfer theorem with a norm-approximate enforcer

Let \(H\) be a positive contraction on the logical space, with source promise
\[
\text{YES: }\lambda_{\min}(H)\le\epsilon,
\qquad
\text{NO: }\lambda_{\min}(H)\ge c.
\]
Let \(V\) be an isometry into the physical space. Suppose \(B,C\) are positive contractions on that space and
\[
\alpha H\le V^*BV\le\beta H,
\qquad
\ker C=\operatorname{ran}V,
\qquad
C\ge\gamma(I-VV^*),
\]
for known positive \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\). Set
\[
r=\min\{\alpha c,1\},\qquad \tau=\gamma/8,\qquad
a=\tau\beta\epsilon,\qquad d=\gamma r/16,\qquad \Delta=d-a.
\]
Assume \(\Delta>0\), and define \(K=C+\tau B\). Then
\[
\lambda_{\min}^{\rm YES}(K)\le a,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}^{\rm NO}(K)\ge d.
\tag{1}
\]

For YES, take a normalized logical ground vector \(\psi\) and use \(V\psi\). Since \(CV=0\), its energy is
\(\tau\langle\psi|V^*BV|\psi\rangle\le\tau\beta\epsilon=a\).

For NO, write any normalized physical vector as \(y=Vx+z\), where \(z\perp\operatorname{ran}V\), and put \(t=\|z\|\). The \(C\)-energy is at least \(\gamma t^2\). Since \(B\) is a positive contraction and \(V^*BV\ge\alpha H\ge\alpha cI\),
\[
\|B^{1/2}Vx\|\ge\sqrt r\,\|x\|,
\qquad
\|B^{1/2}z\|\le t.
\]
If \(t^2\ge r/16\), the \(C\)-energy alone is at least \(\gamma r/16=d\). Otherwise \(t<\sqrt r/4\) and \(\|x\|^2=1-t^2>15/16\). Consequently
\[
\|B^{1/2}y\|\ge \sqrt r\sqrt{15/16}-\sqrt r/4,
\]
whose square exceeds \(r/2\). The \(\tau B\)-energy is at least \(\tau r/2=\gamma r/16=d\). This proves (1) for every physical state, including states outside the encoded image. Also \(0\le K\le(1+\tau)I\); dividing \(K\) and both thresholds by \(1+\tau\) gives a normalized instance.

## 2. Perturbation, precision, and the row-list condition

Let \(\widetilde C\) be Hermitian and satisfy
\[
\|\widetilde C-C\|\le\eta.
\]
Weyl's inequality gives unshifted thresholds \(a+\eta\) in YES and \(d-\eta\) in NO, with separation at least \(\Delta-2\eta\). For a positive Hamiltonian, shift by the error:
\[
\widehat K=\widetilde C+\eta I+\tau B.
\]
Because \(\widetilde C+\eta I\ge C\ge0\), this is positive and
\[
\text{YES: }\lambda_{\min}(\widehat K)\le a+2\eta,
\qquad
\text{NO: }\lambda_{\min}(\widehat K)\ge d.
\tag{2}
\]
The gap is \(\Delta-2\eta\), and \(\|\widehat K\|\le1+2\eta+\tau\). The NO bound follows in Loewner order, not from a state-dependent perturbation argument.

For a sparse-Hamiltonian reduction, \(\widetilde C\) must also have a **uniform row-list oracle**. On every physical basis row, one uniform polynomial-time algorithm must list every nonzero column index and its coefficient (or provide a certified support superset of the same size). The worst-case list length must be \(s=\operatorname{poly}(n)\); row labels have \(m=\operatorname{poly}(n)\) bits; running time and output bit length are polynomial in instance length and requested precision. The entries must be Hermitian-consistent. A specified-entry evaluator that cannot enumerate a row is insufficient.

If each listed entry is rounded symmetrically to absolute error at most \(\mu\), the rounding error matrix \(E\) has at most \(s\) entries per row and column, hence
\[
\|E\|\le\max_i\sum_j|E_{ij}|\le s\mu.
\]
Let \(\bar C\) denote the Hermitian matrix represented by the rounded row list. Let \(\eta\) be the norm-approximation error, and let \(\nu\) bound any additional error in representing this penalty. Put
\[
\kappa=\eta+s\mu+\nu.
\]
Then \(\|\bar C-C\|\le\kappa\). In an actual reduction, \(\eta,\nu\), and the shift must have computable certified upper bounds; choose a rational/dyadic \(\kappa\ge\eta+s\mu+\nu\) with polynomial bit length. Shifting by \(\kappa I\) gives a positive matrix and the robust promise
\[
\text{YES threshold }a+2\kappa,\qquad
\text{NO threshold }d,\qquad
\text{gap }\Delta-2\kappa.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\kappa\le\Delta/4\) leaves gap at least \(\Delta/2\) before normalization. The norm is at most \(1+2\kappa+\tau\), so the normalized gap is at least
\[
\frac{\Delta/2}{1+2\kappa+\tau}.
\]
Adding \(\tau B\) and the diagonal shift increases row degree by at most one, since \(B\) is diagonal and one-sparse.

For one concrete precision allocation, take \(\eta\le\Delta/8\), \(s\mu\le\Delta/8\), and charge any remaining errors from the same \(\Delta/4\) budget. It suffices to request
\[
b\ge\left\lceil\log_2\frac{8s}{\Delta}\right\rceil
\]
fractional bits per listed entry when \(\mu\le2^{-b}\). More generally require \(\eta+s2^{-b}+\nu\le\Delta/4\). For inverse-polynomial \(\Delta\) and polynomial \(s\), this uses \(O(\log n)\) fractional bits. The oracle must still list support uniformly; coefficient precision alone does not determine which exact entries are nonzero. If positivity is not required, the unshifted perturbation has thresholds \(a+\kappa,d-\kappa\) and gap \(\Delta-2\kappa\).

## 3. Application to the Cycle-3 incidence enforcer

Let \(H_0\) be the normalized BMVZ logical Hamiltonian. Add a silent zero label of weight \(1/4\), so the active target is \(H'=(3/4)H_0\). In the complement-LCU isometry, \(B\) is the diagonal one-sparse nonzero-label test and
\[
V^*BV=2H'-H'^2,\qquad H'\le V^*BV\le2H'.
\]
The Cycle-3 incidence enforcer has \(\gamma=1/16\). Relative to \(H_0\),
\[
\alpha=3/4,\quad\beta=3/2,\quad\tau=1/128,\quad
r=\min\{3c/4,1\},\quad
a=3\epsilon/256,\quad d=r/256,\quad
\Delta=(r-3\epsilon)/256.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus a sparse-Hamiltonian transfer follows if a Hermitian polynomial-row approximation to \(C_{\rm inc}\) has total error
\[
\kappa\le(r-3\epsilon)/1024.
\tag{5}
\]
The unnormalized gap is then at least \((r-3\epsilon)/512\); normalization divides by at most \(1+2\kappa+1/128\). The source promise must satisfy \(r>3\epsilon\).

The exact operator is
\[
Tz=(z_a-K_aY^{-1}z_0)_{a>0},
\qquad C_{\rm inc}=T^*T/16,
\]
where \(Y=I-H'\ge I/4\), \(\|T\|\le4\), and
\(C_{\rm inc}\ge(1/16)(I-VV^*)\). Its exact kernel and constant gap are proved, but row and entry access through \(Y^{-1}\) is unresolved.

There is an operator-norm approximation. Define
\[
G_L=\sum_{k=0}^{L}(H')^k,\qquad
\|Y^{-1}-G_L\|\le\sigma_L:=4(3/4)^{L+1}.
\]
Replace \(Y^{-1}\) by \(G_L\) in \(T\), obtaining \(T_L\) and \(C_L=T_L^*T_L/16\). If \(W=(K_a)_{a>0}\) is the stacked nonzero branch map, then \(\|W\|\le1\), so \(\|T_L-T\|\le\sigma_L\). Therefore
\[
\|C_L-C_{\rm inc}\|
\le\frac{(2\|T\|+\sigma_L)\sigma_L}{16}
\le\frac{(8+\sigma_L)\sigma_L}{16}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(\sigma_L\le1\), this is at most \(9\sigma_L/16\). Choosing \(L=O(\log(1/\eta))\) makes (6) at most \(\eta\), at polynomial circuit/block-encoding cost in \(L,M,R\) and requested precision under the BMVZ term generator. Generally \(T_LV\ne0\), so this approximation need not preserve the exact image kernel; the promise transfer (2)–(3) needs only norm closeness.

This does **not** establish the row-list hypothesis. A repeated BMVZ clause is an OR over \(R\) constant-size factors and may have up to \(1+2^{5R}\) nonzeros in a computational row. A naive bound for powers of \(H'\) grows like powers of \(1+M2^{5R}\); forming \(T_L^*T_L\) can add support. Since \(R\) is polynomially large, these are superpolynomial upper bounds (not lower bounds; cancellations may occur). A polynomial circuit or block-encoding for \(C_L\) is not a uniform sparse row list.

The canonical projector \(C_0=I-VV^*\) also has polynomial-time specified-entry evaluation. But on the legally augmented seed family, Cycle 3 proves a row with at least \(\lceil5^R/D\rceil\) nonzeros for every PREPARE completion. This rules out a polynomial row-list for \(C_0\) on that family; it does not rule out a different sparse approximation to \(C_0\) or another exact enforcer. The existing rowwise \(\ell_2\)-approximation for rows of \(VV^*\) is not an operator-norm approximation.

## 4. Operator-norm closeness does not imply sparsity

Let the physical dimension be \(d_{\rm phys}=2^m\), and take any positive image penalty \(C\). For \(u=d_{\rm phys}^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)\), the perturbation
\[
E=t|u\rangle\langle u|=(t/d_{\rm phys})J
\]
is positive and has \(\|E\|=t<\eta\) for any \(0<t<\eta\). Choose \(t\) outside the finite set of values making any entry \(C_{ij}+t/d_{\rm phys}\) vanish. Then every entry of \(\widetilde C=C+E\) is nonzero: \(\widetilde C\ge0\), \(\|\widetilde C-C\|<\eta\), but every row has \(d_{\rm phys}\) nonzeros. This is a legal positive approximate penalty for the energy-transfer interface; the at-most-\(2\eta\) YES threshold cost in (2) covers its small encoded-image energy. It need not have the exact kernel of \(C\), since the approximate Hamiltonian promise requires separated low/high energies, not exact annihilation. An exact-kernel requirement would be an extra condition not implied by norm closeness.

The same example has row \(\ell_2\)-norm \(t/\sqrt{d_{\rm phys}}\), despite operator norm \(t\) and full row support. Thus neither Weyl stability nor rowwise small \(\ell_2\) tails certify support or row enumeration.

## 5. BMVZ consequence and status

BMVZ supplies a polynomial-size generator for weighted, iterated high-locality clause projectors and a QMA promise gap. That yields \(H_0\) and the local operations needed for the LCU circuit and Neumann polynomial. It does not construct \(C_{\rm inc}\), \(C_0\), or any approximation to an image penalty. Its expander/path amplification is on clause labels, not physical rows of \(I-VV^*\), and does not certify sparse matrix access. The BMVZ sparse-QPCP discussion explicitly leaves efficient sparse-matrix conversion of its generated constraints open.

* **Proved:** Given the common-encoding inequalities and a uniform polynomial-row Hermitian approximation \(\bar C\) with total error \(\kappa\le\Delta/4\), the shifted sum \(\bar C+\kappa I+\tau B\) is a positive polynomial-row Hamiltonian with gap (3), the stated precision requirement, and normalization cost.
* **Proved for \(C_{\rm inc}\):** A polynomial-degree Neumann polynomial gives a positive polynomial-circuit operator \(C_L\) with the norm error (6).
* **Not established:** \(C_L\), \(C_{\rm inc}\), or any other approximate/exact enforcer has a uniform polynomial row list for the complete BMVZ family. Circuit, block-encoding, and specified-entry access do not discharge this gate.
* **Not supplied by BMVZ:** An image-penalty approximation or physical-row oracle meeting that gate.

This is a conditional sparse-Hamiltonian reduction theorem, not a completed sparse-Hamiltonian reduction. The remaining gate is a support-and-uniform-row-oracle construction (or an impossibility theorem for candidate enforcers), not a sharper spectral perturbation estimate.

Even if that gate were closed, the conclusion would be a polynomial-row-sparse Hamiltonian on polynomially many qubits. It would not by itself be a standard constant-locality quantum-PCP Hamiltonian: the constructed enforcer can act across the full physical register, its row degree is only known conditionally to be polynomial rather than constant, and the source gap may be inverse-polynomial. Locality, constant row degree, and a constant normalized promise gap would need separate amplification/conversion arguments with their own error and access costs.
