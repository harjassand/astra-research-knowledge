1. Exact theorem and boundary conditions
----------------------------------------
There are S=L-1 spin-1/2 sites, L>=2, J>0, both endpoint fields included:

 Hcrit = J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-X_i X_{i+1}-Y_i Y_{i+1}-Z_i Z_{i+1})
                      +J(I-Z_1)+J(I-Z_S),
 n_i=(I-Z_i)/2, N=sum_i n_i, W=sum_{i=1}^{S-1}n_i n_{i+1},
 H_L=Hcrit-kappa L^{-2}W-lambda L^{-2}N.

At L=2, both endpoint terms act on the single site. Parameters kappa>=0
and lambda are fixed or lie in fixed compact sets; they are not allowed
to grow with L. All 2^{L-1} spin dimensions are present. At beta=tau L^2,
tau>0, this is the physical attraction V_L=-4J-kappa/L^2 and the scaled
coupling g_L=-kappa/L; it is not a fixed negative Lieb--Liniger coupling.

The audited ground statement is

 lim_L L inf spec H_L = e_*(kappa,lambda),
 e_* = min_{theta in H^1_0(0,1), 0<=theta<=pi}
       [(J/2)int theta'^2-kappa int rho^2-lambda int rho],
 rho=sin^2(theta/2).

The convergence is uniform on compact parameter sets. The stronger
kinetic statement concerns any normalized psi_L with
D_L=<psi_L,Hcrit psi_L><=C/L. Let Pi_L be the law under |psi_L|^2 of
the occupation measure r_X dx, where r_X=n_i on [i/L,(i+1)/L) and zero
on the first cell. In the compact weak space
 M={mu:0<=mu<=Lebesgue measure on [0,1]},
along Pi_L=>Pi the conclusion is

 liminf L D_L >= E_Pi I(rho),
 L^{-1}<sum_i V(i/L)n_i n_{i+1}> -> E_Pi int V rho^2,
 I(rho)=sup_{phi in C^1[0,1]}
        2J[-int rho phi'-int rho(1-rho)phi^2]
       =(J/2)int theta'^2 when finite.

Finite I includes vacuum and saturated plateaus; it forces BOTH zero
endpoint traces. Pi is a law of densities, not the mean density alone.
No strong L^1 convergence of the microscopic Bernoulli density is used.
Point-profile recovery suffices for ground energy. An arbitrary law Pi
recovered by pure vectors is not asserted.

2. Exact finite graph, maximum-spin gap, and moving particles
------------------------------------------------------------
The spin exchange satisfies I-sigma_i dot sigma_j=2(I-P_ij). Therefore

 D(psi)=2J sum_{unordered exclusion hops X~Y}|psi(X)-psi(Y)|^2
                         +2J E(n_1+n_S).

The normalization is correct; the unordered hop sum is not doubled.
Taking absolute values reduces this energy and preserves every diagonal
law, so all lower bounds extend to complex vectors by this reduction.

For a set U of M sites, with total-spin operator T_U and maximum-spin
projection P_U, the identity

 sum_{i<j in U}(I-P_ij)=M(M+2)/4-T_U^2 >= M(I-P_U)

follows from P_ij=2 S_i dot S_j+1/2. The first nonmaximum spin gives
exact deficit M. The M in the denominator of the block projection bound
is consequently correct, including nonconsecutive U.

For i<j, the adjacent-swap word has length 2(j-i)-1 and uses each bond
at most twice. I-product=sum(prefix)(I-current swap); the prefix is
unitary and no invariance of psi is assumed. Cauchy--Schwarz and
||(I-P)psi||^2=2<psi,(I-P)psi> give

 <I-P_ij> <=2[2(j-i)-1] sum_{k=i}^{j-1}<I-P_{k,k+1}>
           <=4(j-i) sum_{k=i}^{j-1}<I-P_{k,k+1}>.

For ||A||<=1 and p=<psi,(I-P_U)psi>, the compression error is
2sqrt(p)+p<=3sqrt(p). For an unnormalized vector u of mass w it is
2sqrt(wp)+p<=3sqrt(wp), since p<=w. This last version is essential.

3. Independent constants in one- and two-block replacement
----------------------------------------------------------
For ell>=2 in the symmetric ell-site block, with particle count K,
the contact is K(K-1)/[ell(ell-1)] and block density squared is K^2/ell^2.
Their difference is -K(ell-K)/[ell^2(ell-1)], bounded by 1/(ell-1).
It is legitimate to apply the compression error ONCE to their difference:
the diagonal difference observable has norm <=1.

At a bond splitting a length-ell block into a and ell-a sites,
sum_{left i,right j}(j-i)=ell a(ell-a)/2<=ell^3/8. Thus

 p_B <= (ell^2/2) sum_{bonds in B}e_b,
 L^{-1}sum_B p_B <= ell^3 D/(4JL),
 e_b=<I-P_b>, sum e_b<=D/(2J).

Incomplete windows cost O(ell/L), so the one-block error is bounded by
 ||V||[1/(ell-1)+3sqrt(ell^3 D/(4JL))+O(ell/L)].
For unnormalized pieces multiply the first and boundary terms by their
mass and replace the square root by sqrt(w ell^3 D(u)/(4JL)). Summing
pieces uses sum sqrt(w_alpha D_alpha)<=sqrt(sum D_alpha) when sum w=1.
It never divides by a small piece probability.

For two disjoint ell-site blocks with total count K, the hypergeometric
law yields
 E[(r_ell(i)-r_ell(i+d))^2 | K]=4p(1-p)/(2ell-1), p=K/(2ell),
bounded by 1/(2ell-1). For ell<=d<=a, the span length is at most R=a+ell.
There are at most 2ell^2 pairs in their union of 2ell sites. Therefore

 p_U<=4ell R sum_{bonds in span}e_b,
 L^{-1}sum_i p_U<=2ell R^2 D/(JL).

Each bond is in at most R translated spans for fixed d. Consequently
the averaged squared difference is <=1/(2ell-1)+3sqrt(2ell R^2D/(JL)).

For a=floor(delta L), fixed 0<delta<1/4, require a>=ell, eventually true.
Average r_ell(i+d) over d=0,...,a-1. It differs from r_a(i) by <=2ell/a.
Use |x+y|^2<=2|x|^2+2|y|^2, Jensen, the ell overlapping separations,
and omitted boundary windows. This gives exactly the safe bound (23):

 2/(2ell-1)+6sqrt(2ell(a+ell)^2D/(JL))
                   +2ell/a+8ell^2/a^2+O((a+ell)/L).

The limit order is L first at fixed ell,delta, then for example
ell=ceil(delta^{-2/3}) and delta down to zero. With D<=C/L, the squared
error is O_C(delta^{2/3}), and the contact error is O_C(delta^{1/3}).
No unproved local-equilibrium distribution is assumed in this argument.

4. Weak laws, first-cell repair, and uniform mesoscopic continuity
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The forward average r_a is defined on ALL cells i=0,...,S, with n_0=0
and zero extension outside the chain. This is different from the raw
density's first cell; v2 now explicitly uses the correct definition.
For mu=rho dx, rho_delta(x)=mu([x,x+delta])/delta, zero-extended after 1.
Because every mu in M has a 1-Lipschitz distribution function, weak
measure convergence gives uniform distribution-function convergence.
Thus mu->rho_delta is continuous from M weak to the uniform topology.
The mesh-to-box approximation is uniform O(1/(delta L)).

If the first smoothed cell were instead set to zero, that uniform claim
would be false (a configuration with the first a-1 sites occupied is a
counterexample). Its L^1 version would still suffice, with O(1/L) first
cell error. The owner adopted the cleaner all-cell definition, so no
such fallback is needed in v2.

For fixed delta, expectation of int V r_a^2 converges by continuity of
the profile functional. For each bounded rho, rho_delta->rho in L^2 as
delta->0; bounded convergence passes this through Pi. This closes the
contact limit and its summed subprobability version. Raw occupation laws
are only weak measure laws; no unjustified strong-density convergence
or reduction to a mean profile occurs.

5. Softmax IMS and why the random supremum is justified
------------------------------------------------------
Fix finite fields including zero, M>0, localization delta>0, BEFORE L.
F_alpha(r)=2J[-int r phi_alpha'-int r(1-r)phi_alpha^2] is L^1 Lipschitz
with C_F=2J max_alpha(||phi_alpha'||_infty+||phi_alpha||_infty^2).

An adjacent hop changes just two forward-window starts. Thus
||r_a(X)-r_a(Y)||_1<=2/(aL), including the boundary/ghost starts.
For eta_alpha=sqrt(softmax_alpha(MF)), differentiating along the
straight line in F gives

 sum_alpha(d eta_alpha)^2=(M^2/4)Var_p(dF_alpha)
                             <=(M^2/4)||dF||_infty^2.

Integrating this metric bound gives
sum|eta_alpha(X)-eta_alpha(Y)|^2<=M^2 C_F^2/(a^2L^2)=O(L^{-4}).
For u_alpha=eta_alpha psi and nonnegative psi, the exact graph identity is

 sum D(u_alpha)-D(psi)
       =2J sum_{unordered hops}psi(X)psi(Y) sum(eta_alpha(X)-eta_alpha(Y))^2.

There is no missing factor two. With maximum configuration degree <=L,
sum_edges psi(X)psi(Y)<=L/2. The total IMS error is at most
J M^2 C_F^2/(a^2L)=O(L^{-3}); after multiplication by L it vanishes.
Endpoint diagonal mass is exactly unchanged.

The localized empirical subprobability laws converge to
p_alpha,delta(rho)Pi(d rho), since fixed-delta weights are continuous.

estimates apply even to pieces whose masses tend to zero.

control. The quadratic term returns -2J int rho(1-rho)phi^2.

7. Fisher domain at density zero/one and both boundary traces
