## Strongest claim

Let (Y) be any random object, (A(Y)succeq0) a measurable (d\times d) matrix with (mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}A<\infty), and (f_L:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)) a measurable profile with

\[
J_L=\int_0^\infty \frac{f_L(u)^2}{u^2}\,du<\infty.
\]

For (T>0), define the joint experiment by

\[
Z_T\mid Y\sim\mathcal N\bigl(0,I_d+f_L(TA(Y))\bigr),
\]

where the profile is applied by spectral functional calculus. Then, in nats,

\[
\int_0^\infty I(Y;Z_T)\,\frac{dT}{T^2}
\;\le\;\frac{J_L}{4}\,\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}A(Y).\tag{1}
\]

This is additive over the *joint matrix spectrum* and makes no independence assumption on eigenmodes. If (A(Y)=\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid Y)) for a square-integrable latent vector (X\in\mathbb R^d), then total covariance gives

\[
\frac1d\int_0^\infty I(Y;Z_T)\,\frac{dT}{T^2}
\;\le\;\frac{J_L}{4d}\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Cov}(X)
\;\le\;\frac{J_L}{4}\|\operatorname{Cov}(X)\|_{op}.\tag{2}
\]

For a fixed spectral sensing map (B), replacing (A(Y)) by (B\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid Y)B^*\) gives the unnormalized right side (J_L\|\operatorname{Cov}(X)\|_{op}\operatorname{Tr}(B^*B)/4). Thus finite-rank or Hilbert–Schmidt observation fields have an explicit, charged spectral cost.

## Derivation

Write (F_T=f_L(TA(Y))\succeq0) and \(\bar F_T=\mathbb EF_T\). The mixture (Z_T) has mean zero and covariance (I+\bar F_T). Gaussian maximum entropy and the exact conditional Gaussian entropy yield

\[
I(Y;Z_T)\le\frac12\left[\log\det(I+\bar F_T)-\mathbb E\log\det(I+F_T)\right].\tag{3}
\]

For (g(M)=\log\det(I+M)), its Hessian on positive semidefinite matrices obeys

\[
D^2g(M)[H,H]
=-\operatorname{Tr}\!\left((I+M)^{-1}H(I+M)^{-1}H\right)
\ge-\|H\|_F^2,
\]

because \(\|(I+M)^{-1/2}\|_{op}\le1\). Taylor's theorem along the segment from \(\bar F_T\) to (F_T), followed by expectation (the linear term vanishes), gives

\[
\log\det(I+\bar F_T)-\mathbb E\log\det(I+F_T)
\le\frac12\mathbb E\|F_T-\bar F_T\|_F^2.
\]

Combining with (3) and \(\mathbb E\|F_T-\bar F_T\|_F^2\le\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(F_T^2)\) gives

\[
I(Y;Z_T)\le\frac14\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(f_L(TA(Y))^2\bigr).
\]

For each realization, diagonalize (A). For every eigenvalue \(a\ge0\),

\[
\int_0^\infty f_L(Ta)^2\,\frac{dT}{T^2}
=a\int_0^\infty f_L(u)^2\,\frac{du}{u^2}=aJ_L,
\]

with the zero-eigenvalue term equal to zero. Tonelli's theorem now proves (1). For a posterior covariance (C_Y=\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid Y)),

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X)=\mathbb EC_Y+\operatorname{Cov}(\mathbb E[X\mid Y]),
\]

so \(\mathbb EC_Y\preceq\operatorname{Cov}(X)\), proving (2). The same order argument gives \(\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(BC_YB^*)\le\|\operatorname{Cov}(X)\|_{op}\operatorname{Tr}(B^*B)\).

This has an I-MMSE interpretation. For (Y_\gamma=\sqrt\gamma X+G), (G\sim\mathcal N(0,I)), the vector I-MMSE identity gives

\[
\frac{d}{d\gamma}I(X;Y_\gamma)=\frac12\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid Y_\gamma).
\]

If (A(Y_\gamma)) is that posterior covariance, (1) becomes

\[
\int_0^\infty I(Y_\gamma;Z_T)\,\frac{dT}{T^2}
\le\frac{J_L}{2}\frac{d}{d\gamma}I(X;Y_\gamma).
\]

The Gaussian-channel entropy calculation controls the full covariance mixture at once; it avoids summing separate likelihood ratios. For example, (f(u)=u/(1+u)) has (J=1). More generally (f_L(u)=u/(L+u)) has (J_L=1/L), uniform for (L\ge1).

\[
P_0\le C_P(\mu_s)\le P_0+\frac{\sigma^2s}{P_0-s},\qquad 0<s<P_0.
