# Gaussian cloud correction with differential effective dimension

Worker r10_probability. Retrieved 6 October 2026. Status: reconstructed candidate corollary, with a complete proof below and finite formula checks; neither a validated new publication nor an independent validation of the source's whole sampler.

## Question and legal interface

Can one sample from N(E Phi(G), I_n) without estimating E Phi(G), when G~N(0,I_m), Phi is accessible by values and **exact** forward and transpose Jacobian-vector products, and the Jacobian has small Frobenius norm relative to its operator norm?

The supplied data are a globally L-Lipschitz map Phi:R^m→R^n, bounds L and r≥1, and oracles for Phi(g), D Phi(g)v, D Phi(g)^T w. Assume, almost everywhere,

    ||D Phi(g)||op ≤ L,   ||D Phi(g)||F² ≤ r L²,   0<L²≤1/4.

Pointwise rank(D Phi)≤r is sufficient. There is no assumption that all derivative ranges lie in one common r-dimensional subspace. Standard Gaussian generation, exact real arithmetic, and Brownian bridges at finitely many requested times are available. The expectation of Phi is not supplied. Query counts charge the value/Jacobian oracles; vector arithmetic, Gaussian generation, storage, and finite precision are separate costs.

## Source and author claims

[Chen, Chewi, Lu, Zhang, arXiv:2609.38710v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38710v1), submitted **30 September 2026 00:48:09 UTC**, develops a Gaussian cloud sampler. Theorem 1.1: for a C² potential with 1-Lipschitz gradient and logarithmic Sobolev constant κ, a supplied reference point satisfying ||∇V(x_ref)||≤sqrt(d/κ) permits TV error ε using O(κ(d+log(κd/ε))^(1/5) log^(13/2)(κd/ε)) gradient queries in expectation. The result has no higher-order smoothness assumption. This supplied reference point and LSI constant are part of the interface.

Sections 3.3 and 4.2 isolate the hidden fluctuation correction gadget. The authors' Lemma 3.5 and Theorem 4.2 use output dimension n in the clipping scale L²(sqrt(np)+p)+L⁴n. Their Appendix B implements derivatives via randomized, generally nonlinear secant actions, and Section 6.1 bounds a recursive call forest. I read these portions closely; I have not independently reconstructed their full HMC contraction, RGO, and gradient-only assembly proof.

The result below replaces n with the differential effective dimension r in the **exact derivative** gadget. This is our deduction. It does not replace d with r in the authors' entire sampler. Their maps need not satisfy the required bound, and their gradient-only secant routines need not preserve the linear-matrix norm properties used below.

## Candidate corollary

Run their Algorithm 3.4, retaining K terms and clipping its likelihood estimator at B∈(0,1]. Put

    e_p = C [L²(sqrt(rp)+p)+r L⁴],   p≥2.

There are universal constants C,c such that, provided Cr L^(2K+2)≤c, its output Y obeys

    TV(law Y, N(E Phi(G),I_n))
       ≤ C [r L^(2K+2) + B(e_p/B)^p].                    (R)

The expected numbers of Phi evaluations and Jacobian actions are ≤1+CB and CBK, up to universal multiplicative constants. In particular, p=ceil(C log(1/δ)), K=ceil(C log((r+1)/δ)), and e_p≤cB yield TV error O(δ). Ignoring accuracy logarithms, the permitted L scale is r^(-1/4), instead of n^(-1/4). This is a query statement; an n-coordinate output still costs Ω(n) arithmetic/random generation.

## Complete derivation

**1. Gaussian completion.** Let B_t be m-dimensional Brownian motion on [0,1], with G=B_1. Set

    A_t = E[D Phi(B_1)|F_t],
    M = Phi(B_1)-E Phi(B_1)=∫ A_t dB_t,
    Sigma = ∫ A_t A_t^T dt.

The martingale representation is the Clark–Ocone identity used by the source; Lipschitz Phi is Gaussian-Sobolev and the identity follows also by differentiating its heat semigroup. Conditional Jensen gives

    ||A_t||op≤L,   ||A_t||F²≤rL²,
    0≤Sigma≤L² I_n,   tr Sigma≤rL².                    (1)

For Z|B~N(0,I_n-Sigma), its conditional characteristic function gives

    E exp(i u^T(M+Z))
      =exp(-||u||²/2) E exp(i∫u^T A_t dB_t + ½∫||A_t^T u||²dt)
      =exp(-||u||²/2).

The complex exponential martingale is integrable because its absolute value is at most exp(L²||u||²/2). Thus Phi(G)+Z is exactly N(E Phi(G),I_n). Importantly, Sigma may be full rank.

**2. Correct Gaussian covariance by a likelihood series.** Under the proposal Q, B is ordinary Brownian and Z~N(0,I_n) independently. The ideal logarithmic tilt is

    w_infty(B,Z)=½ sum_{k≥1} [tr(Sigma^k)/k - Z^T Sigma^k Z].

This is the density ratio N(0,I-Sigma)/N(0,I) conditional on B. Its conditional normalizer is exactly one, so tilting does not alter B's marginal. Truncate at K to obtain w_K. Put H_K=sum_{k=1}^K Sigma^k and C_K=(I+H_K)^(-1). Then Z|B under the truncated tilt is N(0,C_K). Direct Gaussian integration gives

    log Z_K(B)=½ sum_{k≤K} tr(Sigma^k)/k -½ log det(I+H_K).

For eigenvalue λ∈[0,L²],

    C_K(λ)=(1-λ)/(1-λ^(K+1)),
    |C_K(λ)-(1-λ)| ≤ C λ^(K+1),
    |log Z_K| ≤ C sum_i λ_i^(K+1)
                ≤ C tr(Sigma)L^(2K) ≤ Cr L^(2K+2).   (2)

The conditional Gaussian KL formula yields TV≤C(sum_i λ_i^(2K+2))^(1/2)≤C sqrt(r)L^(2K+2). The Brownian marginal tilts by Z_K(B)/E Z_K(B), so (2) contributes ≤Cr L^(2K+2) to its TV distance. Combining conditional and marginal errors proves TV(P_K,P_infty)≤CrL^(2K+2).

**3. Unbiased matrix actions.** Conditional on the full B path, independently draw T~Unif[0,1] and G_1,G_2~N(0,I_m). Write

    D_j=D Phi(B_T+sqrt(1-T)G_j),   S=D_1 D_2^T.

Then E[S|B]=Sigma. Each S has

    ||S||op≤L², ||S||F≤sqrt(r)L², ||S||_*≤rL².       (3)

The last two follow from ||D_1 D_2^T||F≤||D_1||F||D_2||op and ||D_1 D_2^T||_*≤||D_1||F||D_2||F. Let S_1,...,S_K be conditionally independent and M_k=S_k...S_1. Their conditional means are Sigma^k. Products need not be symmetric or positive, but

    ||M_k||op≤L^(2k), ||M_k||F≤sqrt(r)L^(2k),
    ||M_k||_*≤r L^(2k).                              (4)

Using a fresh standard Z' and the same matrix realization in both terms, set

    W_K=½ sum_{k≤K} [Z'^T M_k Z'/k - Z^T M_k Z].

E[W_K|B,Z]=w_K. Only matrix actions are required. Each S action costs one transpose and one forward derivative product; applying on Z and Z' is four actions per retained term. No dense matrix need be formed.

**4. Rank-sensitive quadratic concentration, reconstructed.** For deterministic M, replace it by its symmetric part without changing the quadratic form. Diagonalize the symmetric part with eigenvalues a_i. For Z standard Gaussian,

    log E exp(t(Z^T M Z-tr M))
       = sum_i [-t a_i -½ log(1-2t a_i)]
       ≤ C t² ||M||F²,   |t|≤c/||M||op.

Chernoff's bound followed by integration of the resulting tail gives

    ||Z^T M Z-tr M||_Lp ≤ C(sqrt(p)||M||F+p||M||op).  (5)

This is the Gaussian case of classical Hanson–Wright concentration. It is independently derived here, not a new concentration theorem.

Under Q, the k=1 terms have identical trace and cancel in their mean. Equations (4)–(5) bound their Lp difference by CL²(sqrt(rp)+p). For k≥2, the noncancelling trace coefficient is (1/k-1)tr M_k and has magnitude ≤rL^(2k). Summing the geometric series gives

    ||W_K||_Lp(Q) ≤ C[L²(sqrt(rp)+p)+rL⁴]=e_p.       (6)

Under P_K, condition on B and all S matrices. Write Z=C_K^(1/2) Z_tilde, with Z_tilde standard Gaussian; Z' remains standard. The centered quadratic bounds retain (4) since ||C_K||op≤1. The difference in the first trace is at most

    |tr(M_k(C_K-I))|≤||M_k||_*||C_K-I||op
                              ≤Cr L^(2k+2).

Therefore (6) holds under P_K as well. This conditional derivation avoids incorrectly asserting independence of M_k across k: the products are dependent; only the separate bounds and triangle inequality were used.

**5. FORS and clipping.** Given any proposal ω and a conditionally iid estimator W∈[-B,B], J~Poisson(2B) and acceptance product ∏(B+W_j)/(2B) have conditional expected acceptance

    exp(2B((B+E W)/(2B)-1))=exp(E W-B).

Thus the returned law is proportional to exp(E W)Q. Without clipping, the estimator above is unbiased for w_K. With clipping, let tau=(|W_K|-B)_+. Markov's moment calculation gives

    E tau ≤ E|W_K|^p/B^(p-1) ≤ B(e_p/B)^p

under Q and P_K. For completeness, the clipping comparison can be reconstructed directly. Let w=w_K, tilde w=E[clip_B(W_K)|B,Z], A=E_Q exp w, and tilde A=E_Q exp tilde w. Conditional Jensen gives E_P|w-tilde w|≤E_P tau for P=Q,P_K. Since |tilde w|≤B, the elementary inequality |exp u-exp v|≤|u-v|(exp u+exp v) gives

    Delta:=∫|exp w-exp tilde w|dQ
       ≤ A E_P_K|w-tilde w| + exp(B) E_Q|w-tilde w|.

Also |A-tilde A|≤Delta. Comparing the two normalized densities and using the triangle inequality yields TV(P_K,Q_tilde_w)≤Delta/A. Equations (2) and B≤1 bound A away from zero and exp(B) by a universal constant. Thus the sum of integrated clipping tails contributes at most CB(e_p/B)^p. This reproduces the source's Lemma 4.1 in the unbiased case. Adding Step 2 proves (R).

FORS needs ≤exp(2B) attempts in expectation. More precisely, its expected number of estimator calls is ≤2B exp(2B), since the Poisson count is drawn afresh per attempt and the probability of reaching an attempt is determined by previous attempts. This gives the stated value and derivative counts.

## An adversarial example against common-subspace reasoning

Let m=1 and

    Phi_i(g)=u_i + L sin(i g)/(sqrt(n) i),   i=1,...,n.

Its derivative is the column (L/sqrt(n))(cos g,...,cos ng)^T, so rank≤1 and norm≤L. The derivative ranges collectively span R^n: the cosine functions have distinct Fourier frequencies, and any linear functional vanishing on every derivative must have all coefficients zero. The output covariance is

    Cov(Phi(G))_ij = L²/(2n i j)
       [exp(-(i-j)²/2)-exp(-(i+j)²/2)]

and is positive definite, because the sine functions are linearly independent under an everywhere positive Gaussian density. The mean is u; the formula is deliberately transparent for falsification. The extension's r=1 premise does not imply low rank of the global output covariance or require discovering a shared subspace. `checks.py` verifies a 12-output instance has covariance rank 12 and derivative-span rank 12.

## Decisive limitations and nearest prior art

The primary novelty candidate is the Frobenius-budget refinement of the exact cloud gadget. Gaussian quadratic concentration, Clark–Ocone, and Gaussian covariance completion are old ingredients. The paper itself supplies the correction mechanism and clipping lemma. Its Theorem 4.2 already allows robust nonlinear actions, but the dimension-sensitive Lemma 4.3 treats arbitrary odd Lipschitz maps. Our refinement exploits a stronger, linear-matrix oracle interface. No exhaustive novelty search has been completed; label this as a candidate corollary, not a foundational discovery.

Two load-bearing limitations remain. First, gradient secants in Appendix B are nonlinear functions of their directions: inequalities (3)–(5) do not automatically apply. Second, an expected Frobenius bound alone is weaker than the uniform bound; rare large covariance actions could defeat clipping. The leading falsifiable step is to construct unbiased gradient-only actions satisfying analogues of (3)–(6), or produce a smooth low-Frobenius map where these actions lose the gain. This must precede applying the bound to a large sampler.

Concrete uses: converting low-dimensional stochastic simulator outputs into Gaussian clouds around unknown expectations without Monte Carlo mean estimation; latent-state Bayesian computational building blocks; possible sampling dimension improvements when score-estimator noise has uniformly small differential energy. In each use, acquisition of Phi and derivative access must be charged, and linear algebra/random generation remains at least proportional to output size.

Unexplored alternatives: use a Schatten-norm budget instead of uniform rank; use only integrated derivative energy with an explicit tail condition; adapt the correction to anisotropic output covariance by whitening; combine with a structure-preserving automatic differentiation interface. None is proved here.
