# Supplied-graph acquisition with small full-congruence certificates

Status: independently proved below, with bounded executable acquisition and a separate finite cross-check. Priority is unknown. This is a consequence of a classical unary-closure interface, not a claim to resolve finite-lattice representability or its undecidability.

## Exact interface and source gate

Input is an explicit finite lattice order table (L), (|L|=n), and a complete colored graph (d:A^2\to L), (|A|=k\ge1). Assume symmetric colors, (d(p,q)=0\iff p=q), the join triangle inequality, and order reflection as in the release's theorem `f:criterion` in `build/sections/graph.tex`, family206. Set

\[
\theta_a=\{(p,q):d(p,q)\le a\},\qquad
\mathcal E=\{f:A\to A:d(f(p),f(q))\le d(p,q)\}.
\]

The first assumptions make every \(\theta_a\) an equivalence and make (a\mapsto\theta_a) an order embedding that preserves meets. An admissible map is exactly a map preserving all \(\theta_a\): one direction is immediate; the other applies preservation to (a=d(p,q)).

The paper's further condition quantifies over every nonempty seed edge set (S). Its marked graph has edges (f(e)), (e\in S,f\in\mathcal E), and must connect every pair in \(\theta_{\bigvee_{e\in S}d(e)}\). I reconstructed the complete forward and backward proof of this graph theorem. The release's Lean docs206 explicitly exclude its undecidability and subgroup-interval conclusions. I read the comparator and the actual `OAI.Algebra.Universal.GraphCriterion` proof, but did not run Lean or audit its imported foundations. A `sorry` in the challenge comparator is not a defect in the actual solution.

Nearest primary prior: DeMeo, arXiv1204.4305v3, 7May2012, §3.2/Theorem3.2.1 states the unary Galois-closure criterion and attributes it to work from1970; §3.3 discusses bad concrete embeddings. Kenney2010 and Pudlák1976 are the release's additional cited graphical prerequisites. I read DeMeo §3.2 and the selected §3.3 examples, not the whole thesis. The certificate bound below was derived independently; I have not established that it is new in this literature.

## C1. Two kinds of closure suffice

Let \(\kappa_e\) be equivalence closure of the images (f(e)) over all admissible (f). The release's all-seed condition is equivalent to the conjunction

\[
\kappa_{\{p,q\}}=\theta_{d(p,q)}\quad(p\ne q),\tag{C1a}
\]
\[
\theta_a\vee_{\operatorname{Eq}(A)}\theta_b=\theta_{a\vee_L b}\quad(a,b\in L).\tag{C1b}
\]

For sufficiency, equivalence closure of all marked edges for (S) is \(\bigvee_{e\in S}\kappa_e\). By C1a and repeated C1b, this equals \(\theta_{\bigvee d(e)}\). A loop has color0 and contributes only equality, so loops and the all-loop case cause no exception.

For necessity, C1a is the one-edge case. To get C1b without assuming it, order reflection implies

\[
a=\bigvee\{d(p,q):(p,q)\in\theta_a\}.
\]

Otherwise the join on the right is (c<a), and order reflection for (a\not\le c) supplies a contradictory pair. Apply the all-seed condition to all pairs of \(\theta_a\cup\theta_b\). Every admissible image of a seed remains in that same union, so its connectivity is contained in \(\theta_a\vee_{\operatorname{Eq}(A)}\theta_b\). The all-seed condition supplies the reverse required inclusion. The other inclusion follows from monotonicity.

This removes the (2^{\binom k2}) subset loop, but C1a alone would still enumerate (k^k) maps. The next certificate removes that enumeration from *verification* as well.

## C2. Polynomial-sized certificate and algebra

For each off-diagonal seed (e=\{p,q\}), supply a spanning forest for all blocks of \(\theta_{d(e)}\). Each forest edge \(\{u,v\}\) carries the complete table of an admissible map (f) with \(\{f(p),f(q)\}=\{u,v\}\). Duplicate map tables may be shared across forests.

There are at most (k-1) forest edges for each of \(\binom k2\) seeds. Thus at most

\[
r\le\binom k2(k-1)
\]

unary operations suffice, each with (k) entries. Certificate size is (O(k^4\log k)) bits plus the supplied lattice/color tables and forest indices. No operation-composition words, hidden oracle, or implicit field basis is required.

Verify every map against every source pair, verify every indicated endpoint image, check forest connectivity equals the color relation, and check C1b. If this succeeds, let \(\mathcal A=(A,F)\) use just those (r) unary maps. Every \(\theta_a\) is a congruence. Conversely, if \(\rho\in\operatorname{Con}\mathcal A\) and (e\in\rho), compatibility puts every corresponding forest edge in \(\rho\), hence \(\theta_{d(e)}\subseteq\rho\). Therefore

\[
\rho\supseteq\bigvee_{e\in\rho}\theta_{d(e)}
=\theta_{\bigvee_{e\in\rho}d(e)}\supseteq\rho.
\]

The equality in the middle uses C1b. Thus every congruence is exactly one \(\theta_a\), proving the *full* congruence claim.

Conversely, if the release's graph criterion holds, the complete admissible-image graph connects exactly each color relation by C1a. Select spanning trees inside its blocks and one admissible map for each selected edge. This supplies the asserted bound. Consequently every (k)-element finite algebra has a unary algebra on the same carrier with the same full congruence lattice and at most \(\binom k2(k-1)\) operations: first color its pairs by principal congruences and apply this construction. The operation list changes; no claim of term equivalence is made.

Given a valid lattice table, verification uses (O(k^3+n^2k^2+rk^2)) elementary indexed comparisons/unions and polynomial memory. Explicit table validation in the present implementation additionally uses (O(n^4)) work; it is charged. Index/label arithmetic has (O(\log\max(k,n)))-bit operands. Thus the supplied-coloring property has an NP certificate relative to its **explicit** (k^2+n^2) input, and bounded-carrier existence has such a certificate when carrier size is charged explicitly. This is not a polynomial theorem for a binary descriptor of a huge carrier.

## C3. Executed acquisition, without a free map oracle

`work/hessian_check12/cycle19/colored_graph_certificate.py` implements acquisition using a binary constraint problem with variables (f(a)\in A). All constraints are

\[
d(f(a),f(b))\le d(a,b),
\]

with two endpoints pinned for a proposed forest edge. Both orientations are tried. Minimum-domain branching and forward checking enumerate the remaining assignments exactly if necessary. A successful table is verified again; an exhausted query is UNSAT for that endpoint query only. A search cap returns `BUDGET_UNKNOWN`, never an UNSAT or a representability rejection. There are at most (2\binom k2^2) endpoint queries, each potentially exponential. A singleton or join defect returns `NO_GRAPH_WITNESS` for this supplied graph only. The implementation's positive verifier does not enumerate maps or seed subsets.

The declared caps cover lattice/carrier dimensions before new table work, search nodes, and output operation count. Counters record primitive comparisons, queries, nodes, joins, and forest edges, not a complete bit-operation count. Elapsed time and Python traced peak capture additional finite-run costs.

## C4. Discriminating controls

The owned check independently enumerates all maps and all nonempty nonloop seed subsets only on (k\le4), and enumerates every equivalence relation to check the acquired operation list's *actual* full congruences. Positive controls are the one-point case, a3-element chain coloring, the4-point Klein-group (M_3) coloring, and the full partition lattice on4points.

Two negative controls isolate different missing hypotheses:

* For Boolean4 on4points, take atom relations merging only01 and only23, and put all cross-block pairs at top. Every singleton orbit closes to its color, but joining the two atoms produces01|23, not the claimed universal top. So singleton closure alone falsely accepts.
* For (M_3) on4points, use atoms01|23,02|1|3, and03|12. Their pairwise joins/meets form an honest spanning (M_3), but an admissible singleton-image closure misses a block of its assigned color. So lattice embedding alone falsely accepts.

Missing forests, a zero search budget, a carrier cap, and floating-point color labels are independently rejected with the appropriate status. No false positive was found, and no advantage is inferred from their differing elapsed times. The universal theorem is the derivation C1–C2; these controls test the implementation, not all finite lattices.

## Downstream use and remaining acquisition gap

The immediate deliverable is a reusable certificate-producing service for concrete finite-lattice embeddings. It can replace an all-endomorphism/all-partition pass when experimenting with structured graph witnesses, and permits small independently verified operation lists to be archived. It addresses a verification and constrained-map bottleneck exposed by family206 while keeping the cycle17–18 supplied-versus-acquired boundary explicit.

It does not acquire a small carrier for an arbitrary abstract lattice, decide the global representation problem, port arbitrary quiver flags, or bypass the earlier explicit rational-output-height and rank-one-gadget obstructions. The release's later no-computable-carrier-bound conclusion remains dependent on its large, unaudited undecidability proof. The next falsifiable step is an independent C1–C2 reconstruction and a structured larger-carrier comparison against the classical unary-closure implementation, with endpoint-CSP search costs charged. No broader source headline is needed for this step.
