## 1. Finite required-supply storage from non-strict quasiconvexity

Let F be any homogeneous quartic polynomial quasiconvex at every affine matrix. Fix B and set f_B(A)=F(B+A)−F(B)−DF(B):A. Polynomial growth and Young's inequality give

    |f_B(A)|≤C_F(|B|²|A|²+|A|⁴).

Let X be mean-zero W3/4,4(T;Rᵐ), with the periodic Slobodeckij convention, and let E be the bounded fixed unit-slab extension from trace U to0. The incoming value p_B(U) takes the infimum of adjusted actions over **all finite positive widths** and all W1,4 compatible strips from0 toU. Moving means inside a strip are allowed; no minimizing path is assumed to exist.

Append a reversed fixed extension to any incoming strip. Its two outer profiles are0, so gluing them makes a periodic Sobolev potential. The integrated affine part vanishes on this closed strip. Periodic quasiconvexity gives

    J_B(incoming)+J_B(return)≥0.

The return's absolute action is bounded by C_F[|B|²e₂(U)²+e₄(U)⁴]. The fixed incoming extension gives the matching upper bound. Thus p_B is finite on the complete critical space, with the source's two-sided bound and p_B(0)=0. This uses no gradient bound for incoming nearly minimizing paths. Non-strict quasiconvexity controls their possible **negative action**, not their gradient norm.

The periodic consequence of Dirichlet quasiconvexity is legal: tile a fixed periodic Sobolev potential, cut it off near the boundary of larger rectangles, and use boundedness of the planar W1,4 potential plus fourth-power integrability to make the boundary contribution lower order. Smooth density passes the result to the glued strips. The arbitrary fixed width changes constants in that comparison, not its validity.

Constant ramps over width L have adjusted action tending to0, so prepending a ramp and translating an incoming path proves p_B(U+c)=p_B(U). Concatenation with a nearly minimizing incoming path gives p_B(W)−p_B(U)≤J_B(Φ) for every strip U→W. The original-action storage therefore is

    P_B(U)=F₁(B)·E_yU+p_B(U−E_yU).

The affine mean term is essential at nonzero B. There is no reflected-F assumption: reversed strips have the same derivative norms, but their actions are bounded separately.

## 5. Necessary clarification of the original limit wording

Original root line47 says “a fourth-moment-integrable limit of such laws.” This must be read as a **W4-convergent limit**, or as a weak limit with

    lim_(R→∞) sup_n ∫_|A|>R |A|⁴ dμ_n=0.

Either condition passes the G moment to the limit: W4 convergence does so by the proved moment estimate; weak convergence plus the uniform fourth-tail condition does so by continuous cutoff and the bound |G(A)|≤C|A|⁴. Merely requiring weak convergence and a finite fourth moment of the limit is insufficient. The original source already warns against weak convergence at line57; this supplement makes the theorem's limit quantifier unambiguous without changing the frozen text.

A concrete failure construction uses the very law ν. Partition a periodic cell into four rectangles with its four atom weights. In the interior of each rectangle set Φε(x)=A_i(x−x_i), and multiply it by a smooth cutoff that vanishes in an ε boundary layer. Sum the four disjointly supported pieces and extend periodically. Each Φε is a genuine smooth periodic vector potential, so its gradient law με has exactly zero mean and obeys EμεG≥0. Away from the shrinking boundary layers its gradient is exactly A_i; their laws therefore converge weakly toν. The limiting law has finite fourth moment5184, yet EνG<0.

The transition layers carry gradients of orderε⁻¹ on sets of orderε, and their fourth moments are not uniformly integrable (indeed a nonzero affine atom on a fixed face gives a contribution of orderε⁻³). This is precisely why no contradiction to G quasiconvexity or the W4 excluded ball occurs. A weak closure defined only by the limit's finite moment would containν itself and would make the stated separation false.
