# Frozen single-trajectory discriminator

2026-10-07. Question: can an executable variant of the acquired-rates-and-scale construction beat a competent retaining likelihood design when both acquire unknown ordinary rates and the rare rate from the same endpoint-only interface? This is a finite diagnostic, not validation of a uniform theorem or historical novelty.

## Interface and charged resources

The supplied graph has n=L+1 states, both directions of the path 0–1–…–L, and an ordinary edge L→0. The absent edge 0→L is added with unknown rate b. All ordinary rates are unknown within the supplied interval [.7,1.3], and 0<b≤1. Graph support, state labels, a calibrated clock and Markov homogeneity are supplied. Each learner receives only initial_label and read(lag)→label, plus graph, bounds and frozen algorithm settings. Hidden truth parameters, case lists, evaluation seeds, jump records and stationary distributions are excluded from learner configuration. The caller alone constructs the simulator and audits truth and hidden jumps.

Every replicate starts at the parameter-independent label 0; its initial observation costs one reading. There are no resets within a replicate. A request advances the single carried-state CTMC by its positive lag. The learner pays every endpoint reading and all elapsed simulated physical time, including source-state acquisition and complete returns. The caller also counts physical jumps and pseudorandom draws. A read cap of 200,000 and elapsed-time cap of 15,000 apply to each method/trial; cap exhaustion counts as failure, not a discarded observation. Independent replicates are separate experiments, not free resets available within an estimator.

The own-path Gillespie simulator adapts r17's endpoint sensor by retaining residual holding time. Thus paired methods with the same seed observe the same latent path at different times, until their different stopping times. Full jump records are unavailable to learners. This is floating-point simulation, not certified sampling of an exact real-arithmetic law. The model has an ideal clock with no minimum lag or per-read apparatus dead time; measured process time does not include CPU time.

## Why the literal conservative construction was not executed

The audited root theorem uses epsilon-dependent nuisance precision Delta, very small kappa, certified threshold/inversion arithmetic, stage confidence budgets and huge fresh calibration blocks. `proof_cost_ledger.py` evaluates one admissible conservative choice at epsilon=.25, delta=.1, envelope [.7,1.3]. Already stage 0 needs at least 8.36×10^24 useful departures for n=3 and 6.03×10^26 for n=4. Their deterministic lag products are at least 1.56×10^16 and 2.81×10^17 time units, before return overhead. This particular proof construction is not locally executable. These values are not lower bounds on every algorithm satisfying the theorem.

The implemented stage method substitutes practical constants; it does not inherit the theorem. No certified arithmetic or uniform confidence assertion is attached to these substitutions. The theorem and this empirical variant remain separate artifacts.

## Executed stage variant

At stage k, beta=2^(−k−1), t=.5 beta^(1/(L−1)). Fresh ordinary calibration obtains ceil(12/t) complete-return departures from each state; ordinary endpoint frequencies divided by t are clipped into [.7,1.3]. Fresh source-0 departures of size ceil(4/(beta t)) produce a frequency z. Stop when z exceeds the fitted probability at the known comparison parameter 1.5 beta. Return the selected bracket [beta,min(4 beta,1)]. There are at most 14 stages and the common read/time caps remain active.

After stopping, a fresh block of ceil(24/(beta t)) source-0 departures is inverted through the selected fitted model. The reported nominal 90% interval is a Wilson frequency interval transformed through that model and clipped to the selected bracket. It omits nuisance and adaptive bracket-selection uncertainty; it is not a valid joint confidence certificate. Stage outcomes, acquired generators, final counts and all cap paths are retained.

Each calibration or rare block uses first-departure outcomes from complete sampled-chain return cycles. Intervening endpoint readings and the final return are charged. The code never replaces the stream with independently sampled transition rows.

## Competent retaining control

The control retains transition counts grouped by chosen lag, starting state and ending state, including all states. Its joint endpoint likelihood fits every ordinary rate and b within the same supplied rate envelope. Because its lag decisions are functions of the observed history, the conditional transition likelihood remains the product of the chosen kernels; retaining adaptive actions does not justify conditioning away their selection effects in an interval guarantee.

Joint log-rate maximum likelihood uses analytic semigroup gradients, L-BFGS-B, warm starts and three additional fixed b starts (.04,.15,.6). It refits every 1,024 readings. Next lag is selected from [.015625,.03125,.0625,.125,.25,.5,1,2] by minimizing predicted b log-variance after a whole 1,024-reading block, accounting for nuisance correlations and expected visits from the current label. No stationary samples are given to it: the stationary vector used for predicted occupancy is calculated from its fitted generator.

The control starts at ordinary rates 1, b=.2 and lag .25, independent of truth. It stops after at least 2,048 readings when its nominal 90% asymptotic log-rate interval has half-width at most log(1.25). Its fitted-information intervals have no adaptive finite-sample coverage proof. The positive optimization floor b≥10^−6 is a numerical restriction, not an additional scientific promise; tested b values are much larger. All selected best fits in this run report optimization success, but this does not prove global likelihood optimality.

The retaining control is allowed to exploit the same timing mechanism and supplied graph. The theorem does not confer a special architecture advantage over this comparator. Computational costs of optimization and all sampled returns are charged.

## Freeze and evaluation

Two engineering pilot trajectories per method used distinct parameter values and seeds. An inaccurate n=4 stage pilot is preserved. Changes after pilots concerned carried-path coupling and exclusion of truth configuration; no outcome-based hyperparameter improvement followed. `FREEZE.json` hashes configuration, learners and sensor before evaluation. Their hashes still agree at closure. Its `evaluation_started:false` records the freeze instant, not the current run status.

Six fixed cases use n=3 or4 and b=.3,.15,.075. The ordinary vectors are in `config.json`; 32 paired independent simulation seeds per case yield 384 trajectories (192 per method). No cases, seeds or completed failures are removed. This small finite grid is not a uniform test over the parameter class. Raw records contain caller-only truth for audit, not learner input.

Accuracy means completed and |bhat−b|≤.25b. Coverage means completed and the reported interval contains b. Cap exhaustion is failure for both. Pointwise 95% Clopper–Pearson summaries for a fixed case describe its Monte Carlo replicate success probability under the simulation; pooled six-case proportions have no common-binomial confidence interpretation. Wall seconds measure learner execution plus simulator requests, excluding import, installation and setup. No robust cross-machine CPU speed comparison is inferred.

## Derivations supporting the control and audit

At a fixed lag, let P=exp(tQ), stationary row vector pi and Pi=1 pi. Expected starting-state visits during B readings from current label x are

    B pi + (e_x−pi)(I−P^B)(I−P+Pi)^−1.

This follows by summing e_x P^j for j=0,…,B−1 and decomposing the stationary eigenspace. The design's Fisher increment uses these visits and the full nuisance-correlated score d log P_xy/d log rate. The control's numerical positive uniformization and analytic derivative recurrence were checked against independently implemented SciPy expm and expm_frechet for n=3/4 at three lags. These are implementation checks, not a likelihood coverage theorem.

A decisive exact obstruction to the stage interval is immediate: its returned interval is contained in its selected bracket. If the bracket excludes b, no size of fresh final block can make that interval cover b. This logical implication allows a caller-only decomposition of misses without claiming a causal explanation for every residual miss. The true probability of a fresh final block is computed only after evaluation for this audit.

## Prior, boundary and next discriminating step

The nearest primary comparator is Barendregt–Webb–Kilpatrick, arXiv2205.10371v2 (2023-02-01), §2.3 Algorithm3 and §3.3.2 directed-ring experiments: adaptive endpoint timing already exists. The rigorous support-sensitive acquisition theorem is an internal independently reconstructed result, not external validation, and its historical novelty remains unresolved. Existing source snapshots and exact reading locations are preserved in the own sources directories.

The bounded run rejects practical advantage of this frozen reduced-constant stage variant in reading cost or coverage. It does not reject the root theorem, nor every valid adaptive stage procedure. A next falsifiable experiment would replace only the unprotected stage decisions by sequentially valid joint nuisance/scale certificates, freeze new settings before fresh seeds, and compare at matched empirically checked accuracy/coverage. The obstacle is acquiring such certificates without the enormous conservative constants or discarding useful retained transitions. Unknown support, hidden labels, fixed sensor lag and fixed additive entropy production remain separate interfaces.
