# Discriminating validation design: source effect vs target effect

## Question

Does the source response curve `d_S(z)` predict the average perturbation effect in a prespecified recipient cell domain after accounting for its measured pretreatment state composition, or does the recipient have a domain-specific response mechanism?

## Minimal design

1. Name one perturbation `A`, one primary scalar endpoint `Y∈[0,1]`, one target-engagement assay, and a finite set of pretreatment strata `Z` before looking at held-out outcomes. For a gene perturbation, retain one-to-one ortholog mapping as a separately declared step across species; for a drug, record compound, vehicle, delivered concentration, and assay timing. State whether the estimand is assignment effect or effect at a defined achieved engagement.
2. In source and each recipient domain, split each independent donor/sample into sibling culture wells and randomize within donor × plate/batch × pretreatment stratum to perturbation or matched negative control. A suitable control is non-targeting guide plus matched transfection for CRISPRi, or matched vehicle for a compound. Include mock delivery to isolate reagent/transfection effects and a positive assay control to show that the endpoint has dynamic range. Keep passage, seeding density, media lot, oxygenation, matrix, treatment duration, and harvest protocol fixed where possible; deliberately vary only the domain factor under study, and rotate arms across plates.
3. Measure pretreatment `Z` from a baseline aliquot or an upstream label; do not define adjustment strata from post-perturbation expression. Measure guide assignment and target engagement (RNA/protein) as assay checks. Preserve the primary estimate as randomized assignment (intention-to-treat); show target-engagement-stratified analyses as secondary and state their assumptions.
4. Lock the transfer predictor after source fit. In the recipient, provide only baseline `P_T(Z)` and the declared intervention metadata to the prediction; keep randomized recipient perturbation outcomes held out until scoring. Compare (a) source conditional response standardized to recipient baseline composition, (b) a no-change/control baseline, (c) source pooled mean-effect baseline, and (d) a simple linear/additive model with exactly the same source information. Report conditional response and whole-population endpoints separately, plus both delta/DE and calibrated distributional or functional metrics where relevant.
5. Use donor/well blocks as the independent units. Cells in one well improve measurement precision for that well, not the number of independent randomized biological units. For each domain, define donor-level matched differences `D_i = mean(Y|perturbation,i) − mean(Y|matched control,i) ∈[-1,1]`. The transport diagnostic is `Δ=E[D_T]−E[D_S]`, with inference clustered at the donor/sample randomized block.

## Conservative precision calculation

For `n` independent randomized biological blocks per domain and bounded outcomes, a Hoeffding bound for the difference of domain-specific mean paired effects is

`Pr(|Δ̂−Δ|≥h) ≤ 2 exp(−n h²/4)`.

Thus `n ≥ (4/h²) log(2K/α)` blocks per domain suffices for a familywise half-width `h` across `K` prespecified domain/stratum contrasts under the stated independent-block model. This is a conservative planning bound, not a power calculation for an RNA-seq endpoint; blinded pilot variance and donor heterogeneity should inform a sharper preregistered design. Sequenced cells are nested subsamples and do not replace donors/independent cultures. Charge donor/sample procurement, culture wells, guide/drug reagents, target-engagement assays, sequencing/proteomics reads, batch calibration, and analysis time. Costs scale at least linearly with number of randomized blocks and contexts; cross-species ortholog harmonization adds a separate mapping/measurement cost.

## Prespecified falsifiers and interpretation

- Transport prediction falsified at tolerance `h`: the recipient randomized effect contrast `Δ` has a confidence interval excluding `[-h,h]` after the prespecified `Z` standardization, with successful perturbation delivery and assay controls.
- Delivery failure: target engagement or positive controls fail; this falsifies the operational intervention match, not the biological response mechanism.
- Measurement failure: control or calibration distributions fail the preregistered cross-domain alignment criteria; no causal transport conclusion is made for that endpoint.
- Lack of source support: target has mass outside observed source `Z`; the estimand is not covered by the standardization formula.
- If source and recipient effects agree on tested strata, the evidence is finite and context-specific. It supports the declared domain and endpoints only. A cell line-to-organoid comparison does not establish transfer to intact tissue or an organism.

## Strong biological control precedent

Hagai et al. (Nature 2018) used low-passage primary dermal fibroblasts from four species from the same anatomical site, common culture conditions, untreated and mock-transfected controls, poly(I:C), species-aware IFN-β reagents with matched activity units, and additional cross-mammalian IFN in human/mouse to probe reagent differences. They reported both response divergence for cytokine/chemokine genes and more conserved regulation for transcription factors/kinases. This is a useful design precedent and an empirical warning against blanket invariance. It remains an in-vitro study, not evidence that a cell model transfers to intact tissues or clinical effects.
