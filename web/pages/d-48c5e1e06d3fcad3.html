  Abstract.

    
 Let     (   M  n   ,  g  )   (M^{n},g)    be a closed    n  n   -dimensional Riemannian manifold, where     g  =   (   g   i  ​  j    )    g=(g_{ij})    is     C  1   C^{1}   -smooth metric.
Consider the sequence of eigenfunctions     u  k   u_{k}    of the Laplace operator on    M  M   . Let    B  B    be a ball on    M  M   .
We prove that the number of nodal domains of     u  k   u_{k}    that intersect    B  B   
is not greater than

    
 

  
  
        C  1   ​     Volume  g   ​   (  B  )      Volume  g   ​   (  M  )     ​  k   +    C  2   ​   k    n  −  1   n      ,   C_{1}\frac{\textup{Volume}_{g}(B)}{\textup{Volume}_{g}(M)}k+C_{2}k^{\frac{n-1}{n}},    
  
 
 
    
 where     C  1   C_{1}   ,     C  2   C_{2}    depend on    M  M   .
The problem of local bounds for the volume and for the number of nodal domains was raised by Donnelly and Fefferman, who also proposed an idea how one can prove such bounds.
We combine their idea with two ingredients:
the recent sharp Remez type inequality for eigenfunctions and the Landis type growth lemma in narrow domains.

  


 
 Key words and phrases: 
Courant’s nodal domain theorem, nodal sets, Remez inequality, Landis’ growth lemma, Landis’ conjecture


 
  1.  Introduction


 
 Let     (   M  n   ,  g  )   (M^{n},g)    be a closed Riemannian manifold with     C  1   C^{1}   -smooth     g   i  ​  j    g_{ij}   .
The spectrum of the Laplace operator on    M  M    is discrete. There is a sequence of
eigenvalues

 

  
  
     0  =   λ  1   <   λ  2   ≤    λ  3   ​  …    0=\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\dots    
  
 
 
 that tend to    ∞  \infty   
and a sequence of (real) eigenfunctions     u  k   u_{k}    such that

 

  
  
         Δ  g   ​   u  k    +    λ  k   ​   u  k     =  0   .   \Delta_{g}u_{k}+\lambda_{k}u_{k}=0.    
  
 
 
 Our enumeration of eigenvalues is non-standard. We start with      λ  1   =  0   \lambda_{1}=0    and
     u  1   =  1   u_{1}=1    on    M  M   .
The nodal domains of     u  k   u_{k}    are the connected components of     M  ∖   Z   u  k     M\setminus Z_{u_{k}}   , where
    Z   u  k    Z_{u_{k}}    is the zero set of     u  k   u_{k}    (    Z   u  k    Z_{u_{k}}    is called the nodal set of     u  k   u_{k}   ).
The Courant nodal domain theorem states that the    k  k   -th eigenfunction     u  k   u_{k}    has
at most    k  k    nodal domains. If the multiplicity of an eigenvalue is more than 1, one may enumerate the eigenfunctions corresponding to this eigenvalue in any order.
Our main result is the local version of Courant’s theorem.



 

 
   Theorem 1.1   . 

 
  Consider a ball     B  =   {   x  ∈  M   :     d  g   ​   (  x  ,   x  0   )    <  r   }    B=\{x\in M:d_{g}(x,x_{0})<r\}    with center at      x  0   ∈  M   x_{0}\in M    and radius     r  <    r  0   ​   (  M  )     r<r_{0}(M)   .
For any eigenfunction     u  k   u_{k}   , the number of connected components of     B  ∖   Z   u  k     B\setminus Z_{u_{k}}    that intersect      1  2   ​  B   \frac{1}{2}B     ( the local number of nodal domains in    B  B    )  is not greater than 

 

  
  
        C  1   ​     Volume  g   ​   (  B  )      Volume  g   ​   (  M  )     ​  k   +    C  2   ​   k    n  −  1   n      ,   C_{1}\frac{\textup{Volume}_{g}(B)}{\textup{Volume}_{g}(M)}k+C_{2}k^{\frac{n-1}{n}},    
  
 
 
  where      C  1   ,   C  2    C_{1},C_{2}    depend only on     (  M  ,  g  )   (M,g)    and are independent of    k  k    and    B  B   . 





 
 The main issue addressed in this paper is why a nodal domain cannot be very long and narrow. A local bound on the volume of a nodal domain is proved in Theorem   1.2  .



 
 The starting point is the idea due to Donnelly and Fefferman  [ 7 ]  that one can use growth estimates for eigenfunctions to prove the local bounds for the volume and for the number of nodal domains. Donnelly and Fefferman showed in  [ 7 ]  that if    B  B    is a ball of radius     1  /    λ  k     1/\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}   , then the number of connected components of     B  ∖   Z   u  k     B\setminus Z_{u_{k}}    that intersect a twice smaller ball with the same center is at most
     C  1   ​   (  M  )   ​   k   C  n     C_{1}(M)k^{C_{n}}   , where     C  n   C_{n}    is an explicit constant strictly bigger than 1, which depends on the dimension    n  n    only.
Donnelly and Fefferman conjectured that     C  n   C_{n}    can be improved to 1 (like in the Courant theorem).
The constant     C  n   C_{n}    was improved by Chanillo and Muckenhoupt  [ 5 ]  and then by Lu  [ 19 ] , and by Han and
Lu  [ 13 ] . The arguments involved subtle versions of covering lemmas and improvements to BMO bounds for     log  ⁡   |   u  k   |    \log|u_{k}|   . However the improved     C  n   C_{n}    was still bigger than 1. In this work we don’t use the language of BMO norms, and argue in terms of closely related Remez type inequality. We discuss growth estimates of eigenfunctions in Section   2.1  .



 
 In the proof of Theorem   1.1   we combine the idea of Donnelly and Fefferman with two ingredients. The first ingredient is
the sharp Remez type inequality for eigenfunctions, which is related to a (resolved) conjecture by Landis on a three balls theorem for wild sets. The second ingredient is Landis type growth lemma for eigenfunctions. The lemma and the conjecture by Landis are independent statements. These two ingredients give two estimates of growth, which however compete with each other. Our local bound on the number
of nodal domains on scale     1  /    λ   k    1/\sqrt{\lambda}_{k}    is     C  ​   k    n  −  1   n     Ck^{\frac{n-1}{n}}   , which
is better than     C  ​  k   Ck   . The bound     C  ​  k   Ck    follows from the idea of Donnelly and Fefferman and the sharp Remez type inequality for eigenfunctions. The improvement from     C  ​  k   Ck    to     C  ​   k    n  −  1   n    ​    (   log  ⁡  k   )    n  −  1     Ck^{\frac{n-1}{n}}(\log k)^{n-1}    is obtained using the local volume bound for nodal domains proved in Section   3.1  . The proof uses one more tool due to Landis, which is discussed in Section   2.2  . This approach is similar to the one in  [ 20 ] , where estimates on the volume of a connected component of      B   1  /    λ  k      ∖   Z  ⁡   (   u  k   )     B_{1/\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}\setminus Z(u_{k})    are obtained under the additional assumption that the Riemannian metric is real-analytic.
Finally, the sharp bound     C  ​   k    n  −  1   n     Ck^{\frac{n-1}{n}}    follows by adding the argument of Fedor Nazarov in Section   3.2  . This sharp bound is new for spherical harmonics.



 
 We would like to mention that in dimension two there is an alternative approach due to the elegant idea of A. Eremenko (see Appendix), which however is two-dimensional only.



 
 Our local bound on the number of nodal domains is sharp.
When the dimension     n  =  2   n=2   , one may consider the unit sphere in     ℝ  3   \mathbb{R}^{3}   
and the restriction on     S  2   S^{2}    of the homogeneous harmonic polynomial      u  ⁡   (  x  ,  y  ,  z  )    =   ℜ  ⁡    (   x  +   i  ​  y    )   m     u(x,y,z)=\Re(x+iy)^{m}   .
It is a spherical harmonic with      λ  k   ≍  k  ≍   m  2    \lambda_{k}\asymp k\asymp m^{2}   , whose nodal set consists of    m  m    circles intersecting at the poles.
Let    x  x    be the North pole. Then the number of nodal domains intersecting a geodesic ball      B  r   ​   (  x  )    B_{r}(x)    on     S  2   S^{2}    is       ≍   k    \asymp\sqrt{k}   .
This example generalizes to higher dimensions, see  [ 20 , Proposition 7.1] .
On the other hand, consider an eigenfunction on the standard    n  n   -dimensional flat torus,     T  =    (   ℝ  /  ℤ   )   n    T=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{n}   , of the form

 

  
  
     u   (   x  1   ,  .  .  ,   x  n   )   =   ∏   j  =  1   n   sin   (  2  π  m   x  j   )   .   u(x_{1},..,x_{n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sin(2\pi mx_{j}).    
  
 
 
 The corresponding eigenvalue is      λ  k   ≍   k   2  /  n    ≍   m  2    \lambda_{k}\asymp k^{2/n}\asymp m^{2}   . The nodal domains are     m  n   m^{n}    cubes on the torus. If    B  B    is a ball of radius     r  >   C  /  m    r>C/m   , then the number of nodal domains intersecting    B  B    is       ≍    (   r  ​  m   )   n   ≍   k  ​   r  n     \asymp(rm)^{n}\asymp kr^{n}   .



 
  Notation.  By      B  r   ​   (  x  )    B_{r}(x)    we will denote a ball with center at    x  x    and radius    r  r    in local coordinates on    M  M   . So      B  r   ​   (  x  )    B_{r}(x)    can be identified with a Euclidean ball and the symbol     |    B  r   ​   (  x  )    |   |B_{r}(x)|    will be used not for the volume with respect to the metric    g  g   , but for the Euclidean volume of      B  r   ​   (  x  )    B_{r}(x)    in local coordinates. Note that these two volumes are comparable. By    B  B    we will denote a Euclidean ball in local coordinates on    M  M   , whose radius and center are not specified, and     B  r   B_{r}    will be used for any ball of radius    r  r   . By      r  0   ,  c  ,   c  1   ,   c  2   ,  …   r_{0},c,c_{1},c_{2},\dots    we will denote small constants and by     C  ,   C  1   ,  …   C,C_{1},\dots    large constants that may depend on     (  M  ,  g  )   (M,g)   , but are independent of    λ  \lambda   . In local coordinates we always assume that the operator     Δ  g   \Delta_{g}    is uniformly elliptic with bounded derivatives of the coefficients     g   i  ​  j    g_{ij}   .



 
 It is more convenient to argue in terms of     λ  k   \lambda_{k}    rather than in terms of    k  k   . The Weyl law states that
     λ  k   n  /  2    ∼   c  ​  k    \lambda_{k}^{n/2}\sim ck   . Theorem   1.1   follows from the next theorem.



 
   Theorem 1.2   . 

 
  Let    u  u    be a Laplace eigenfunction with eigenvalue     λ  >  2   \lambda>2    on a closed Riemannian manifold     (   M  n   ,  g  )   (M^{n},g)   , where    g  g    is     C  1   C^{1}    – smooth.    Remark 1.4   . 

 
 The local bound on the number of nodal domains is consistent (on the heuristic level) with the upper bound in Yau’s conjecture (see  [ 17 ] , [ 23 ] )
on the volume of nodal sets:

 

  
  
        H   n  −  1    ​   (   Z  u   )    ≤   C  ​   λ     ,   H^{n-1}(Z_{u})\leq C\sqrt{\lambda},

    C  ​    (   r  ​   λ    )   n    C(r\sqrt{\lambda})^{n}   . The heuristic obstacle in both questions is the same: why there cannot be many nodal domains that are long, twisting and narrow.

  2.  The main tools.


 
  2.1.  Remez type inequality for eigenfunctions.


 
 We note that this section is not self-contained. The first main ingredient of this paper is the Remez type inequality for eigenfunctions. The complete proof of this inequality is contained in the lecture notes  [ 16 ] , [ 18 ]  and we decided not to include it here.



 
 Let    u  u    be a Laplace eigenfunction with eigenvalue    λ  \lambda    on a closed Riemannian manifold     (   M  n   ,  g  )   (M^{n},g)   , where    g  g    is     C  1   C^{1}    – smooth in local coordinates. Donnelly and Fefferman  [ 6 ]  proved the following growth estimate for Laplace eigenfunctions (in  [ 6 ]  it is formulated for     C  ∞   C^{\infty}    – smooth metrics, but only     C  1   C^{1}    – smoothness of     g   i  ​  j    g_{ij}    is actually needed, see Appendix):

 for any ball     B  ⊂  M   B\subset M    and the twice smaller ball       1  2   ​  B   ⊂  M   \frac{1}{2}B\subset M    with the same center.



 
   Remark 2.1   . 

 
 The recent works  [ 2 ] , [ 8 ] , [ 9 ]  on the distribution of     L  2   L^{2}    mass of eigenfunctions imply
that for two-dimensional surfaces with negative curvature, one can improve     C  ​   λ    C\sqrt{\lambda}    in (  1  ) to     o  ⁡   (   λ   )    o(\sqrt{\lambda})   .





 
 In  [ 7 ]  Donnelly and Fefferman raised a question on the distribution of values of the eigenfunctions. One of the simplest versions of their question is the following. Let    C  C    be a large constant and normalize the eigenfunction    u  u    so that       sup  M    |  u  |    =  1   \sup_{M}|u|=1   .
Can     |  u  |   |u|    be     e   −   C  ​   λ      e^{-C\sqrt{\lambda}}    small on half of    M  M    (is it possible that the set where      |  u  |   <   e   −   C  ​   λ       |u|<e^{-C\sqrt{\lambda}}    has measure at least half of    M  M   )?



 
 The answer (see  [ 16 ] , [ 18 ] ) is that it cannot happen if    C  C    is large enough (depending on     (  M  ,  g  )   (M,g)    and independent of    λ  \lambda   ). This question is related to the Landis conjecture ( [ 14 ] ,p.169), which states that
if    h  h    is a solution to divergence type equation

the following version of three balls inequality for wild sets holds.



 
 Let    K  K    be a closed subset of      1  2   ​  B   \frac{1}{2}B    with positive volume      |  K  |   >  0   |K|>0   . There are constants     α  ∈   (  0  ,  1  )    \alpha\in(0,1)    and     C  >  0   C>0   , which depend only on    A  A    and     |  K  |   |K|   , such that

        |  h  |   ≤   C  ​   ε  α         in   ​   1  2   ​  B    .   |h|\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha}\quad\text{ in }\frac{1}{2}B.    
  
 
 
 The proof of the Landis conjecture is presented in the lecture notes  [ 16 ] .



 
 Finally, the Remez type inequality for eigenfunctions (see  [ 16 ] , [ 18 ] ) states that for any ball    B  B    in local coordinates on    M  M    and any set     E  ⊂  B   E\subset B    with positive volume, we have
