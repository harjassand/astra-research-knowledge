## 1. A separated integer filtration

Assign weights (p,s,u,F,J)=(-1,0,0,1,1) to P and take the quotient filtration on A. H has top homogeneous part H1=H+u of degree one. Because P is a graded domain, the initial form of any multiple CH is in (H1), and every such initial multiple occurs. Thus

  gr A=P/(H1).                                             (N1)

This is a domain: after inverting p it is

  C[p±1,X,Y,Z,u]/(XY−Z(Z+1)).

The quadric polynomial is irreducible (linear in Y with coprime coefficient X and constant Z(Z+1)); H1 is not divisible by p. Irreducibility after p-localization therefore implies irreducibility in the UFD P.

Negative filtration pieces are controlled. For m>0, every monomial of weight at most −m contains p^m, hence F_-m A⊂p^m A. Since A is a polynomial ring and p a nonzero nonunit, ∩p^m A=0. The filtration is exhaustive and separated, so every nonzero element has a least integer degree. The initial forms of p and F have degrees −1 and +1.

Inside B[τ±1], where

  B=C[X,Y,Z,u]/(XY−Z(Z+1)),
  sB=Y−X(X+u²),
  f0=X−sB²+u²,  g0=Z−sB X,  I=(f0,g0),

there is an exact identification

  gr A=G=B[τ,I/τ],  deg τ=−1, deg B=0.                     (N2)

Send (p,s,u,F,J) to (τ,sB,u,f0/τ,g0/τ). In the other direction use the formulas for X,Y,Z. The quadric identity verifies both substitutions without extra relations. For every ℓ>0 the degree-ℓ piece is

  G_ℓ=τ^-ℓ I^ℓ.                                           (N3)

Products can include powers τ^a, but τ^{a−b}I^b with b−a=ℓ has coefficient in I^b⊂I^ℓ; the generators I/τ give the reverse inclusion.

## 2. What a coordinate would force

Suppose A=C[p,q1,q2,q3]. Some qk must have strictly positive filtration degree, because otherwise every polynomial in these coordinates has degree at most zero, contradicting deg F=1. Choose j≠k and D=∂qj. This is a nonzero LND killing p and qk.

For the five original generators choose the finite maximum shift

  m=max_(Dg≠0)(deg Dg−deg g).

On a minimal filtered representative, Leibniz gives deg Da≤deg a+m. This induces a nonzero homogeneous derivation D0 of degree m on gr A: the maximum-attaining generator has a nonzero top image. Local nilpotence passes to the associated graded, because iterated nonzero top images would equal initial forms of D^r a, which eventually vanish. D0 kills τ and the positive-degree initial form η of qk.

Localize the invariant τ. Homogeneity gives

  D0(b)=τ^-m E(b),  b∈B,

where E is a nonzero LND on B. Nonzero follows because D0 kills τ but does not kill all of G; local nilpotence follows from D0^r(b)=τ^-rm E^r(b). By (N3), η=τ^-ℓh for some nonzero h∈I^ℓ, ℓ>0. Thus

  E(h)=0 for a nonzero h in a positive power of I.           (N4)

The requirement D0τ=0 is essential here. This comes from the assumed coordinate p and was not assumed for arbitrary homogeneous derivations.

## 3. A line bundle makes the ideal principal

Let

  C=C[a,d,b,c,u]/(ac−bd−1),
  X=ab, Y=dc, Z=db.

The fibre weights (a,d,b,c,u)=(1,1,−1,−1,0) make B the degree-zero ring. The opens Z+1 and Z cover Spec B. On the first, a is an invertible fibre coordinate:

  b=X/a, c=(Z+1)/a, d=Ya/(Z+1).

On the second, d is invertible:

  b=Z/d, c=Y/d, a=Xd/Z.

The transition is d=Ya/(Z+1). Thus Spec C is the complement of the zero section in a line bundle over Spec B, and B→C is injective.

Put

  v=a²(X+u²)−d²=a³b+a²u²−d².

Then

  IC=(v).                                                  (N5)

For a direct local proof, on a≠0 let ξ=d/a and j=X+u²−ξ². One gets

  f0=(1+2ξX−X²j)j,  g0=X²j,  v=a²j.

The coefficient pair is unimodular:

  (1−2ξX)(1+2ξX−X²j)+X²(4ξ²+j−2ξXj)=1.

At a prime containing a, the determinant relation makes b,d units; g0=-1 and v=-d² modulo a, hence both ideals are the unit ideal there. These cases prove global equality.

## 4. Why the additive action lifts

We reconstruct the normalization argument rather than treating a bundle lift as automatic. The two above charts have factorial rings B_(Z+1), B_Z and their overlap. Any line bundle is trivial on each chart; its transition is an overlap unit. Units remain independent of a polynomial parameter T, and the chart rings remain factorial after adjoining T. Therefore Pic(B)→Pic(B[T]) is an isomorphism.

For the additive action α determined by E, α*L and pr*L on A^1×Spec B agree at parameter zero and hence are isomorphic. Normalize an isomorphism to the identity at zero. Any two such isomorphisms differ by a unit of B[T], necessarily a unit of B independent of T and equal to one at zero. Thus the normalized lift is unique. The two composites over parameters T,S also differ by a unit independent of T,S and equal to one at zero; hence they agree. This proves the group law and gives an actual lifted Ga-action.

It acts linearly on the bundle and therefore commutes with fibre scaling. Its derivation Etilde on C is nonzero (restriction to B is E), locally nilpotent and of fibre degree zero. Equation (N4) and (N5) give h=v^ℓq in C, with h nonzero and Etilde(h)=0.

The kernel of an LND on a characteristic-zero domain is factorially closed. One proof uses the additive LND order ν(g)=max{r:D^rg≠0}, for which ν(gh)=ν(g)+ν(h): the top binomial product is nonzero in a domain. Thus ν(gh)=0 forces both orders zero. Consequently

  Etilde(v)=0.                                              (N6)

## 5. A second grading gives the contradiction

Use the auxiliary grading (a,d,b,c,u)=(0,1,−1,0,1), which preserves ac−bd−1. The top part of v is

  vtop=a²u²−d²=(au−d)(au+d),

of degree two; a³b has degree −1. A derivation of this finitely generated graded ring has a finite decomposition into homogeneous shifts. The highest nonzero part E' of Etilde is still a nonzero LND, still has fibre degree zero, and kills vtop by (N6).

Factorial closure fixes au−d and au+d. In characteristic zero this fixes d and au; another application of factorial closure fixes a and u. Thus E' fixes a,d,u.

Over K=C(a,d,u), the localization is K[b], with c=(1+bd)/a. A nonzero LND on K[b] has constant-in-b coefficient (otherwise its iterates never terminate). Writing

  q=cE'(b)−bE'(c)

and differentiating ac−bd=1 gives

  E'(b)=a q,  E'(c)=d q,  q∈K.

But q is already in C. The a and d charts yield

  C∩K=C[a±1,d,u]∩C[a,d±1,u]=C[a,d,u].

Both charts cover, because a,d have no common zero on ac−bd=1; their intersection equality also follows from the UFD C[a,d,u]. Since E' has fibre degree zero, q has fibre weight −2. Every monomial of C[a,d,u] has nonnegative fibre weight. Hence q=0, contradicting E'≠0.

This excludes (N4) and therefore excludes p being a coordinate.

## Adoption and scientific scope

The universal proof is the separated-filtration → positive invariant → normalized line-bundle lift → incompatible weight sequence. It does not rely on finitely many sample derivatives, absence of a critical point, or finite-degree coordinate searches. The circuit tests in the companion construction note do not execute or replace this proof.
