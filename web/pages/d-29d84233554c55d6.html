# Finite weighted scores: a general obstruction and a dimension-dependent repair

Worker `b_math`, cycle 3, 2026-10-06. The negative polynomial/Krylov mechanism was independently derived here, with the decisive circuit irreducibility argument exchanged by `r06_combinatorics`; `r03_quantum` independently certifies finite witnesses. The positive dimension-dependent interpolation theorem below is our deduction. Broader novelty has not been checked.

## Interface and quantifier boundary

For faithful `τ` and a known Hermitian generator `A`, define the complete generally non-Hermitian weighted score by
\[
-i[A,\tau]=(1-\lambda)\tau S_A^{\tau,\lambda}+\lambda S_A^{\tau,\lambda}\tau,
\qquad0<\lambda<1.
\]
The frame is fixed across all chosen weights, and ordinary irreducibility means `{A_a}'=CI`. The candidate state must match **every entire operator-valued score**, not only a selected loss or expectation.

**Negative theorem.** For every nonempty finite collection of prescribed interior weights Γ, there is a fixed ordinary irreducible Hermitian frame and two unequal faithful states matching all weights in Γ. The target may have condition number at most 2, the competitor has smallest eigenvalue at least `1/(4d)`, and their trace distance is at least `1/6`. The construction dimension grows with the number of weights. Endpoints are excluded essentially.

**Positive theorem at known dimension.** For fixed dimension `d≥2`, scores at `d(d−1)` weights with pairwise distinct `λ(1−λ)` identify all faithful states under any ordinary irreducible Hermitian frame. Thus the negative statement does not say that no finite dimension-dependent design exists.

Neither theorem supplies an efficient finite-copy estimation protocol. A dimension of `d=2^n` makes the positive number of weights exponential in the qubit count.

## Negative construction: Cauchy annihilation by a polynomial multiplier

Close Γ under complements: `Λ=Γ∪{1−λ:λ∈Γ}`. Let `k=|Λ|`, choose `d≥k+2`, and choose distinct `p_i∈[1,2]`. Let `D=diag(p_i)`, `e=1`, and the k-dimensional Krylov space
\[
W=\operatorname{span}\{e,De,\ldots,D^{k-1}e\}.
\]
Take any real symmetric matrix Z annihilating W, and define
\[
H=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}((1-\lambda)\mathbf L_D+\lambda\mathbf R_D)(Z),
\qquad A=D^{-1/2}HD^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]
The factors commute because left and right multiplication by D commute. Entrywise,
\[
H_{ij}=P(p_i,p_j)Z_{ij},\qquad
P(x,y)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}[(1-\lambda)x+\lambda y].
\]
Complement closure makes P symmetric, so H and A are Hermitian. For target `σ=D/Tr D`, its dressed weighted generator is
\[
(K_\lambda)_{ij}
=\frac{\sqrt{p_i p_j}}{(1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j}A_{ij}
=\prod_{\mu\in\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda\}}[(1-\mu)p_i+\mu p_j]Z_{ij}.
\tag{2}
\]
For each fixed row i, the multiplier in the final expression is a polynomial of degree at most k−1 in p_j. Thus `K_λe=0`, since Z annihilates the k moment vectors. Also `K_λ^T=K_{1−λ}`, so the complement-closed design gives `e^T K_λ=0` as well. Every K commutes with `P_w=ee^T/d`.

Let `v=(√p_i)/√Σ_jp_j` and choose the amplified fiber parameter
\[
Q=\tfrac12I+\tfrac d2P_w,\qquad
\rho=\sqrt\sigma Q\sqrt\sigma
=\tfrac12\sigma+\tfrac12vv^T.
\tag{3}
\]
Since `Tr(σP_w)=1/d`, both states are normalized. Q is positive and nonscalar; `ρ≠σ`. Commutation with every dressed K gives equality of the entire weighted scores at every selected λ, by the exact score-fiber equation. This is one unchanged frame and one unchanged state pair across all weights.

## Circuit frame: ordinary irreducibility is proved

For every subset S of size k+1, define the barycentric circuit
\[
(z_S)_i=\begin{cases}
\bigl(\prod_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}(p_i-p_j)\bigr)^{-1},&i\in S,\\
0,&i\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]
Lagrange interpolation's leading coefficient gives `Σ_i(z_S)_i p_i^r=0` for `r=0,…,k−1`. All its supported entries are nonzero. Set `Z_S=z_Sz_S^T` in (1).

Every interior factor has two strictly positive coefficients, hence
\[
P(x,y)=\sum_{r=0}^k c_r x^{k-r}y^r,\qquad c_r>0.
\]
On S, the kernel matrix `G_ij=P(p_i,p_j)` factors as a square Vandermonde matrix, an invertible anti-diagonal coefficient matrix, and its transpose. The nodes are distinct and every c_r is nonzero, so G is invertible. Multiplication on both sides by `diag(z_i/√p_i)` preserves invertibility. Therefore A_S is supported on S and is invertible there:
\[
\ker A_S=\operatorname{span}\{e_i:i\notin S\}.
\]
A common commutant preserves every such kernel and its orthogonal complement, so it commutes with all coordinate-subset projections. Their joint commutant is diagonal when `d≥k+2`. Every supported offdiagonal entry of A_S is nonzero. Every pair of indices belongs to some S, so commutation forces all diagonal entries equal. The whole ordinary frame therefore has scalar commutant. □

At the economical dimension `d=k+2`, only d matrices are needed: S is the complement of one coordinate. Each has precisely that coordinate as its kernel; the scalar-commutant conclusion follows directly. Positive rescaling of individual observables makes their operator norms at most one without changing any equality or irreducibility claim.

Endpoints make some c_r zero and invalidate this argument. This is consistent with the source's endpoint identifiability result rather than an omitted case.

## Faithfulness, constant separation, and exact rationality

Because `1≤p_i≤2`, `λ_minσ≥1/(2d)` and `κ(σ)≤2`. Equation (3) gives `ρ≥σ/2`, hence `λ_minρ≥1/(4d)` and `κ(ρ)≤4d`. Measuring the projector vv^T certifies
\[
D_{\rm tr}(\rho,\sigma)
\ge\tfrac12\bigl(1-\sum_i p_i^2/(\sum_i p_i)^2\bigr)
\ge\tfrac12(1-2/d)\ge1/6.
\]
Thus the finite-weight obstruction is not explained by an exponentially tiny target eigenvalue or an almost identical state pair. It does not establish that the chosen dense generator frame is inexpensive or well-conditioned uniformly in the number/locations of weights.

For rational prescribed λ, choose distinct rational `q_i=1+i/(4d)`, `p_i=q_i²∈[1,2]`. Then every circuit, frame matrix, state, and complete score in this construction is rational up to the conventional factor i in the score. Normalized generators can be chosen rational by dividing by the maximum absolute row sum. Exact finite replay is in `work/b_math/cycle3/finite_weight_replay.py` and `results/b_math/cycle3/finite_weight_replay.json`.

## Positive repair when dimension is fixed

For any fixed target τ, define the Hermitian part
\[
H_A^\tau(h)=\tfrac12(S_A^{\tau,\lambda}+S_A^{\tau,1-\lambda}),
\qquad h=\lambda(1-\lambda).
\]
The adjoint identity `S^{λ†}=S^{1−λ}` follows from Hermiticity of A and τ, so equality of complete scores at a weight supplies equality of this part. In a τ eigenbasis with positive eigenvalues p_i,
\[
(H_A^\tau(h))_{ij}
=\frac{i(p_i-p_j)(p_i+p_j)}{2[p_i p_j+h(p_i-p_j)^2]}A_{ij}.
\tag{4}
\]
Diagonal and equal-eigenvalue entries vanish. A common scalar denominator is the product over unequal unordered eigenvalue pairs; it has degree at most `R=d(d−1)/2` in h. Every matrix entry of H has numerator degree at most R−1, including after rotating back to the physical basis.

For two faithful states ρ,σ, the difference has a common denominator of degree at most 2R and numerator degree at most 2R−1. These denominators are nonzero on `[0,1/4]`. If scores agree at `2R=d(d−1)` weights with distinct h, every numerator has at least 2R distinct roots and is identically zero. Thus the Hermitian parts coincide for all h, and
\[
\int_0^1 S_A^{\rho,\lambda}d\lambda
=\int_0^1 H_A^\rho(\lambda(1-\lambda))d\lambda
=\int_0^1 H_A^\sigma(\lambda(1-\lambda))d\lambda
=\int_0^1 S_A^{\sigma,\lambda}d\lambda.
\]
The integral is exact entrywise logarithmic differentiation:
\[
\int_0^1 S_A^{\tau,\lambda}d\lambda=-i[A,\log\tau].
\]
Indeed `∫_0^1 (p_i−p_j)/[(1−λ)p_i+λp_j]dλ=log p_i−log p_j`. Therefore `[A_a,logρ−logσ]=0` for every frame generator. Ordinary irreducibility makes the logarithm difference scalar, and trace-one normalization yields `ρ=σ`. □

This upper bound is sufficient, not claimed optimal. Complementary weights give the same h and add no value to this interpolation argument. The construction proves failure for arbitrary finite designs as dimension grows, while this theorem recovers finite universal identifiability once dimension and sufficiently many distinct h values are fixed.

Concrete downstream consequence: two or three interior weights cannot be adopted as a universal fixed-frame repair merely because ordinary generators are irreducible. A repair claim must specify dimension, number of noncomplementary weights, implementability, and estimation precision. The positive theorem addresses exact identifiability only; its exponential directional count and potentially poor interpolation/statistical conditioning are charged limitations.
