# The source target has a Friedrichs-realizable extended local KS potential

## Result and status

The pinned paper proves exact nonrepresentability only for multipliers
\(v\in V=L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\), and explicitly avoids
claiming that its formal inverse potential defines an ordinary closed Hamiltonian.
Using the paper's own orbital equations, ground-state transform, and all-orbital
spectral-gap certificate, one can close that specific gap:

**Conditional internal theorem.** Under source Propositions 4.3 and 5.1 and the
weighted gap (4.23), the formal local function
\[
 g=\sqrt n\cos\theta>0,\qquad h=\sqrt n\sin\theta,\qquad
 V=\frac{\Delta g}{2g}
\]
defines a densely defined nonnegative closable form on
\(C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\). Its closure has a Friedrichs operator \(A_V\) whose
spatial ground state is \(\hat g=g/\sqrt2\) at energy 0, with a gap \(d>0\) on
\(\hat g^\perp\), and whose first excited eigenspace contains (h\) at energy
\(d\). The three-electron spinful noninteracting determinant
\[
 \Phi=(\hat g\uparrow)\wedge(\hat g\downarrow)\wedge(h\uparrow)
\]
is therefore a ground state of the Friedrichs realization of
\(\sum_{i=1}^3(-\frac12\Delta_i+V(x_i))\), and its density is exactly
\(2|\hat g|^2+|h|^2=g^2+h^2=n\).

The potential (V\) is the source's formal inverse potential and lies in
\(L^2_{\rm loc}\), but the pinned theorem excludes membership in
\(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\). This is a representation in an enlarged, precisely defined
class: the Friedrichs operator associated with the closure of its minimal local
Schrödinger form. It is **not** a result for standard (V\), conventional Fock/hybrid
GKS, common chemical molecules, or a practical construction. The source charge
remains finite but nonnumerical. This is an internal corollary of the pinned source,
not independent proof validation or a novelty/priority claim.

## Proof reconstruction

### A. Closability by the ground-state transform

Start with the sesquilinear form on (\mathcal D_0=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C)):
\[
 q_0[f]=\frac12\int|\nabla f|^2+\int V|f|^2.
\]
Both terms are finite because (V\in L^2_{\rm loc}). The source's local
ground-state identity applies to every (f\in\mathcal D_0) and gives
\[
 q_0[f]=\frac12\int g^2|\nabla u_f|^2\ge0,
 \qquad u_f:=f/g.
\]
The positive continuous (g\) is bounded above and below on each compact set. The
map (U:f\mapsto f/g) is unitary from (L^2(dx)) to (L^2(g^2dx)), and (q_0)
becomes the weighted gradient form (\frac12\int g^2|\nabla U f|^2).

Here is the closability argument without assuming global bounds on (g). Suppose
\(f_j\in\mathcal D_0\), (f_j\to0\) in (L^2(dx)), and
\(q_0[f_j-f_k]\to0\) as (j,k\to\infty). Then
\(g\nabla(f_j/g)\) is Cauchy in (L^2(dx)); call its limit (G\). On any compact
set (K), (f_j/g\to0) in (L^2(K)), while
\(\nabla(f_j/g)\) converges in (L^2(K)), because (g^{-1}\) is bounded on
\(K). Distributional differentiation forces that local gradient limit to be
zero. Hence (G=0) on every compact set, so (G=0) globally and
\(q_0[f_j]\to0\). Thus (q_0) is closable. Denote its closure by
\(\bar q\) with form domain (\mathcal Q\), and its nonnegative self-adjoint
Friedrichs operator by (A_V\).

### B. The source orbitals belong to the closed form domain

The source proves that \(g,h\in E_V\): each orbital lies in \(H^1\) and has
finite \(\int |V||f|^2\). Choose smooth cutoffs \(\chi_R\) equal to one on
\(B_R\), zero outside \(B_{2R}\), with \(|\nabla\chi_R|\le C/R\). For either
\(f=g\) or \(f=h\), the tail \((1-\chi_R)f\) tends to zero in \(H^1\), and
\[
\int |V||(1-\chi_R)f|^2\longrightarrow0
\]
by dominated convergence. Thus its explicit minimal-form expression tends to
zero in absolute value, so \(\chi_Rf\to f\) in the form norm.

Each \(\chi_Rf\) is compactly supported and in \(H^1\). Approximate it by
\(C_c^\infty\) functions supported in one fixed compact set. Strong \(H^1\)
convergence gives strong \(L^4\) convergence there; since \(V\in L^2\) on that
compact set, the potential term also converges. A diagonal sequence puts both
orbitals in the minimal-form closure. The source's compact-cutoff identity
\[
q_V[\chi_Rf]=\varepsilon\|\chi_Rf\|_2^2+
\frac12\int|\nabla\chi_R|^2|f|^2,
\qquad \varepsilon=0\text{ for }g,\quad d\text{ for }h,
\]
then gives \(\bar q[\hat g]=0\) and \(\bar q[h]=d\).

### C. The closed operator has the needed eigenstates and gap

For every test function (\varphi\in C_c^\infty), the source's distributional
equations and local integrability imply
\[
 \bar q(\hat g,\varphi)=0,\qquad
 \bar q(h,\varphi)=d\langle h,\varphi\rangle.
\]
The test functions are a form core by construction. Continuity in the form norm
extends these identities to every (\varphi\in\mathcal Q). The representation
theorem for closed forms then gives
\[
 A_V\hat g=0,\qquad A_Vh=dh.
\]
The weighted gap inequality holds on (C_c^\infty) and passes by form-norm
continuity to (\mathcal Q):
\[
 \bar q[f]\ge d\big(\|f\|_2^2-|\langle\hat g,f\rangle|^2\big).
\]
Therefore (\ker A_V=\operatorname{span}\{\hat g\}), and
\(A_V\ge d\) on its orthogonal complement. Since (h\perp\hat g\) and
\(A_Vh=dh), the bottom of the orthogonal-complement spectrum is exactly (d).

### D. Pauli filling gives a three-electron ground state

Extend (A_V) by the identity on the two-dimensional spin space. Its spatial
ground orbital \,\(\hat g\) has two spin states, so the one-particle ground
subspace has dimension two. On the orthogonal complement the one-particle energy
is at least (d). In the antisymmetric three-electron space, at least one particle
must lie outside that two-dimensional ground subspace. Equivalently, for the
one-particle density matrix (\gamma_\Gamma\) of any normalized 3-electron state
or ensemble, (0\le\gamma_\Gamma\le I), (\operatorname{Tr}\gamma_\Gamma=3),
and
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\big((I-P_{\hat g}\otimes I_{\rm spin})\gamma_\Gamma\big)
 \ge 3-\operatorname{rank}(P_{\hat g}\otimes I_{\rm spin})=1.
\]
Thus its many-body energy is at least (d). The determinant
\((\hat g\uparrow)\wedge(\hat g\downarrow)\wedge(h\uparrow)) attains (d), so it
is a ground state and has density (g^2+h^2=n).

## What is and is not established

- **Established conditionally/internal:** an ordinary self-adjoint, bounded-below
  local Schrödinger realization exists as the Friedrichs extension of the minimal
  form, and the source target is an exact pure ground-state density for its
  spin-independent three-electron noninteracting system.
- **Outside the pinned theorem:** (V\notin L^{3/2}+L^\infty); the form domain
  (\mathcal Q) need not equal (H^1). The extension is tied to the source's
  (g,h,n), not supplied independently by ordinary nuclear data.
- **Not established:** ordinary molecular (v)-representability, a
  spin-dependent-local remedy, a conventional Fock/screened-exchange/hybrid
  operator, practical computability, a modest integer charge, or experimental
  relevance. The charge and molecule parameters are inherited unchanged from the
  source's non-effective construction.
- **Prior/novelty status:** the weighted ground-state transform and Friedrichs
  closure are standard mathematical tools (the pinned source itself cites Frank–
  Seiringer for the transform). The consequence follows from their combination
  with the source's global weighted spectral gap and exact three-electron orbital
  filling. No priority claim is cleared. It is a scope extension, not a practical
  GKS method.
