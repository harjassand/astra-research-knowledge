Cosmic acceleration offers exceptional foundational consequences, but background expansion can admit different interacting and evolving dark-sector explanations. I concentrated on this question:
Can the fluctuations measured in a strange metal explain its electrical resistivity through one microscopic mechanism, without independently choosing the spectral and transport exponents?

The concrete material was Ce₃Pd₂₀Si₆. A June 2026 neutron-scattering study reports a scaling exponent
\[
\alpha=0.88\pm0.02
\]
near a magnetic field of \(1.73\,\mathrm T\), with a collapse over \(0.06\)–\(5\,\mathrm K\) and \(0.025\)–\(0.58\,\mathrm{meV}\). The paper connects this regime to previously measured approximately linear resistivity. Neutrons probe magnetic markers of the quadrupolar degrees of freedom, not the quadrupoles directly. These are published observations and interpretations—not measurements reanalysed here. Nature
The missing causal link is not simply a formula reproducing \(\alpha\). It is the connection between the fluctuating degrees of freedom, their coupling to the electronic bands, and the process by which current loses momentum.

2. The attempted organizing principle
The physical hypothesis was:
Perhaps competing orders create an extended set of soft modes whose available phase space converts a fractional dynamical exponent into exactly linear resistivity.
The indispensable quantity in the calculation is not the unweighted number of soft modes. It is their number weighted by their ability to degrade current.
Explicit assumptions
Set \(k_{\mathrm B}=\hbar=1\). Consider electrons coupled to a fluctuating field:
\[
H_{\mathrm{int}}
=
\sum_{a,b,\mathbf k,\mathbf q}
g_{ab}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)
c^\dagger_{b,\mathbf k+\mathbf q}c_{a,\mathbf k}\phi_{\mathbf q}.
\]
For this calculation, assume a smooth-band, weak-coupling electron description in the temperature window considered, an equilibrated fluctuation bath with a specified momentum sink, and a susceptibility
\[
\chi^R(\mathbf q,E,T)
=
\frac{Z}
{f(\mathbf q)+AT^\alpha W(E/T)},
\]
where \(f\geq0\), \(A,Z>0\), and
\[
W(x)=u(x)-iv(x),\qquad u(x),v(x)>0\quad(x>0).
\]
The vertex and electronic geometry must not contain additional singular temperature dependence. Identifying this susceptibility with the electron-coupled mode is also an assumption—not something established by neutron intensity alone. The inverse-susceptibility framework has antecedents in local quantum-critical theory. ar5iv
For current in the \(x\)-direction, a representative transport weight is
\[
\mathcal W_x(\mathbf q)\propto
\sum_{ab}\int d^d k\,
|g_{ab}(\mathbf k,\mathbf q)|^2
\left[
v_{a,x}(\mathbf k)-v_{b,x}(\mathbf k+\mathbf q)
\right]^2
\delta(\epsilon_{a,\mathbf k})
\delta(\epsilon_{b,\mathbf k+\mathbf q}).
\]
The squared velocity difference matters: frequent scattering need not strongly change the current.
Define
\[
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tr}}(E,T)
=
\int d^d q\,\mathcal W_x(\mathbf q)\chi''(\mathbf q,E,T).
\]
The leading current-mode collision functional then takes the form
\[
\mathcal R_J(T)
=
\frac{C}{T}\int_0^\infty
\frac{E\,\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tr}}(E,T)}
{\sinh^2(E/2T)}\,dE.
\tag{1}
\]
The thermal factor follows from
\[
\int d\epsilon\,
n_F(\epsilon)\,[1-n_F(\epsilon+E)]
=
\frac{E}{1-e^{-E/T}}.
\]
Combining it with Bose absorption gives \(E/[4\sinh^2(E/2T)]\); constants are absorbed into \(C\).
Equation (1) is not an exact conductivity formula for an arbitrary interacting metal. It determines resistivity only when this current mode controls transport. Otherwise, it is a variational collision functional; weakly scattered regions of a Fermi surface can conduct in parallel.
Deriving the phase-space relation
Define the current-weighted low-stiffness volume
\[
N_{\mathcal W}(\varepsilon)
=
\int d^dq\,\mathcal W_x(\mathbf q)
\,\mathbf1_{\{f(\mathbf q)<\varepsilon\}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose
\[
N_{\mathcal W}(\varepsilon)
\sim C_{\mathcal W}\varepsilon^\nu,
\qquad 0<\nu<2.
\tag{3}
\]
The transport spectrum can be written as a Stieltjes integral:
\[
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tr}}(E,T)
=
Z\int_0^\Lambda
\operatorname{Im}
\frac{1}{\varepsilon+AT^\alpha W(E/T)}
\,dN_{\mathcal W}(\varepsilon).
\]
Substitute \(E=Tx\) and \(\varepsilon=AT^\alpha y\):
\[
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tr}}(Tx,T)
\sim
ZC_{\mathcal W}\nu A^{\nu-1}
T^{\alpha(\nu-1)} I_\nu(W(x)),
\]
where
\[
I_\nu(z)
=
\int_0^\infty
y^{\nu-1}\operatorname{Im}\frac{1}{y+z}\,dy.
\]
For \(z=|z|e^{-i\theta}\), with \(0<\theta<\pi/2\),
\[
I_\nu(z)
=
\frac{\pi |z|^{\nu-1}
\sin[(1-\nu)\theta]}
{\sin(\pi\nu)},
\qquad \nu\neq1,
\]
and
\[
I_1(z)=\theta.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(0<\nu<1\), this is the beta-integral evaluation. For \(1<\nu<2\), subtracting the real term \(1/y\) makes the complex integral convergent without changing its imaginary part. The upper restriction \(\nu<2\) is substantive: the imaginary integrand behaves as \(y^{\nu-3}\) at large \(y\).
Equation (1) consequently gives
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_J(T)\propto
T^{\,1+\alpha(\nu-1)}.}
\tag{5}
\]
The coefficient is finite under suitable low-frequency regularity, such as \(u(0)>0\) and \(v(x)=O(x)\), together with a controlled high-frequency tail.
Why this initially looked promising
For a regular soft manifold with quadratic stiffness in \(c\) transverse directions and a smooth nonzero current weight,
\[
\nu=\frac c2.
\]
A regular soft line in three dimensions has \(c=2\), hence \(\nu=1\), giving a linear power independently of \(\alpha\).
Using \(\alpha=0.88\):
Assumed fluctuation geometry and weight	Conditional power of \(T\)
Exactly local response over a finite momentum volume	\(0.12\)
Regular soft surface in three dimensions	\(0.56\)
Quartic junction considered below, nonzero weight	\(0.78\)
Regular soft line in three dimensions	\(1.00\)
Isolated quadratic minimum in three dimensions	\(1.44\)


The local case follows directly from \(\mathcal B_{\mathrm{tr}}\propto T^{-\alpha}\); it is not an unjustified \(\nu\to0\) limit.
These are conditional powers, not measured material exponents. In particular, the local weak-coupling calculation cannot automatically remain controlled as \(T\to0\), because its scattering rate divided by \(T\) grows.
3. Constructing a microscopic candidate—and finding its first failure
The Ce \(8c\) sublattice motivates examining a simple-cubic exchange model, although the chemical crystal has a face-centred-cubic translation lattice. arXiv
Consider nearest-neighbour and face-diagonal scalar exchange:
\[
I(\mathbf q)
=
2J_1(c_x+c_y+c_z)
+
4J_2(c_xc_y+c_yc_z+c_zc_x),
\qquad c_i=\cos q_i.
\]
At \(J_1=4J_2>0\),
\[
f(\mathbf q)
=
I(\mathbf q)+12J_2
=
4J_2\sum_{i<j}(1+c_i)(1+c_j)
\geq0.
\tag{6}
\]
It vanishes on three lines:
\[
(\pi,\pi,q_z),\quad
(\pi,q_y,\pi),\quad
(q_x,\pi,\pi).
\]
This is an exact soft-line construction. The underlying frustrated model and its classical boundary are known, so neither is a new discovery. arXiv
The junction dominates the lines
The lines meet at \(R=(\pi,\pi,\pi)\). Expanding there,
\[
f(R+\mathbf k)
=
J_2P(\mathbf k)+O(|\mathbf k|^6),
\]
with
\[
P(\mathbf k)
=
k_x^2k_y^2+k_y^2k_z^2+k_z^2k_x^2.
\tag{7}
\]
The junction is quartic, not quadratic. Its low-energy neighbourhood cannot be ignored merely because the intersection itself has zero line length.
Homogeneity gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb R^3}
\mathbf1_{\{P(\mathbf k)<\varepsilon\}}\,d^3k
=
V_0\varepsilon^{3/4},
\]
where
\[
V_0
=
B\!\left(\frac14,\frac32\right)
B\!\left(\frac14,\frac14\right)
=
25.9279494071\ldots.
\tag{8}
\]
To obtain the coefficient, work in the positive octant at \(\varepsilon=1\):
\[
0<z<
\sqrt{\frac{1-x^2y^2}{x^2+y^2}},
\qquad xy<1.
\]
Set \(x=r\cos\phi\), \(y=r\sin\phi\), then
\(r=u/\sqrt{\sin\phi\cos\phi}\). The volume becomes
\[
8
\left[\int_0^1\sqrt{1-u^4}\,du\right]
\left[
\int_0^{\pi/2}
(\sin\phi\cos\phi)^{-1/2}\,d\phi
\right],
\]
which evaluates to (8).
The junction contributes \(\varepsilon^{3/4}\), whereas regular line segments contribute only \(\varepsilon\). Therefore the junction dominates for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\).
With nonvanishing current weight there,
\[
\mathcal R_J(T)\propto T^{1-\alpha/4}
=
T^{0.78},
\]
not \(T\).
The proposed explanation fails its first calculation beyond regular-manifold counting.
4. Treating the current correctly changes the answer again
The conventional \((111)\) vector is a reciprocal vector of the chemical FCC lattice. An unfolded zone-corner mode can therefore be a reduced-zone-centre mode with nontrivial internal-sublattice character. It is not automatically a uniform mode or a mode incapable of scattering electrons. arXiv
For an isolated smooth band near a reciprocal vector, the velocity change is \(O(|\mathbf k|)\). In the spherical three-dimensional example, the joint Fermi-surface factor is
\[
\int d^3p\,
\delta(\epsilon_p)\delta(\epsilon_{p+k})
\propto\frac1{|\mathbf k|}.
\]
The angular delta-function Jacobian supplies the inverse power. Multiplying by the squared current change gives a degree-one weight:
\[
\mathcal W(\mathbf k)\propto|\mathbf k|,
\]
up to angular factors.
For the quartic polynomial, define
\[
N_m(\varepsilon)
=
\int_{[-1,1]^3}
|\mathbf k|^m\mathbf1_{\{P(\mathbf k)<\varepsilon\}}\,d^3k.
\]
The asymptotics are
\[
N_0(\varepsilon)\sim V_0\varepsilon^{3/4},
\]
\[
N_1(\varepsilon)
\sim
\frac{3\pi}{2}\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon),
\tag{9}
\]
and, for \(m>1\),
\[
N_m(\varepsilon)
\sim
\frac{6\pi}{m-1}\varepsilon.
\tag{10}
\]
The logarithm has a direct geometric origin. Along one axis, at distance \(x\gg\varepsilon^{1/4}\), the transverse tube has area approximately \(\pi\varepsilon/x^2\). Summing six tubes gives
\[
6\pi\varepsilon
\int_{\varepsilon^{1/4}}^1x^{m-2}\,dx.
\]
At \(m=1\), that integral is logarithmic; the central region contributes only \(O(\varepsilon)\).
Substituting (9) into the transport integral gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_J(T)
=
C_1T\log(T_0/T)+C_2T+o(T),
\qquad C_1>0.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the generic intraband example does not restore an exactly linear leading term.
An additional vertex zero, \(g\propto|\mathbf k|\), would add two powers to the weight and recover a linear power. No material symmetry enforcing that zero was established. Adding it solely to obtain the desired answer would not explain the observation.
Interband processes, band crossings, finite-field anisotropy and incommensurability can change the result. That is precisely why the electron-coupled operator and its matrix elements cannot be omitted.
5. A sharper obstruction: the candidate connects the wrong orders
The experimental phase study discusses order near \((111)\), a soft mode near \((100)\), slight incommensurability, and different quadrupolar channels. An applied field also removes full cubic equivalence. These facts must constrain any microscopic identification. arXiv
In the attempted unfolded scalar model, write
\[
\Gamma=(0,0,0),\quad R=(\pi,\pi,\pi),\quad
M=(\pi,\pi,0),\quad X=(\pi,0,0).
\]
Their exchange energies are
\[
I_\Gamma=6J_1+12J_2,\qquad
I_R=-6J_1+12J_2,
\]
\[
I_M=-2J_1-4J_2,\qquad
I_X=2J_1-4J_2.
\]
Requiring \(I_R=I_X\) forces \(J_1=2J_2\). Then
\[
I_R=I_X=0,\qquad
I_M=-8J_2,\qquad
I_\Gamma=24J_2.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(J_2>0\), \(M\) lies lower. For \(J_2<0\), \(\Gamma\) lies lower. Only the trivial zero-exchange case avoids this obstruction.
Therefore:
This nontrivial scalar \(J_1\)-\(J_2\) spectrum cannot have \(R\) and \(X\) as degenerate global minima.

At the soft-line point \(J_1=4J_2\), \(R\) and \(M\) are soft, but \(f(X)=16J_2\).
This is an exact failure of the proposed scalar identification, not a theorem excluding the real multipolar material.
Competition does not force soft lines
I also checked the converse inference. Add a body-diagonal term
\[
8J_3c_xc_yc_z
\]
and take \(J_1=2,\ J_2=1,\ J_3=1\). Then
\[
I_R=I_X=-8,\qquad I_M=0,\qquad I_\Gamma=32.
\]
The polynomial is multiaffine in the three cosines, so every interior value is a convex combination of corner values. The minimizing corners are isolated; their quadratic stiffnesses are positive.
This is a counterexample to “competing \(R\)- and \(X\)-type orders necessarily produce soft lines.” It is not a proposed fitted repair of the failed material explanation.
6. Electronic feedback and momentum loss remain indispensable
The assumed fluctuations are not automatically self-consistent
In the same smooth-band approximation, a nonzero intraband coupling produces a low-momentum particle–hole damping term. In the ballistic regime \(E\ll v_F|\mathbf q|\),
\[
\operatorname{Im}\Pi^R(\mathbf q,E)
\propto
\int d^3p\,
[n_F(\epsilon_p)-n_F(\epsilon_{p+q})]
\delta(E-\epsilon_{p+q}+\epsilon_p)
\propto\frac{E}{v_F|\mathbf q|}.
\]
For quartic stiffness, the imposed fractional scaling gives
\[
|\mathbf q|\sim T^{\alpha/4}
\quad\text{at }E\sim T.
\]
The electronic damping then scales as
\[
T^{1-\alpha/4}.
\]
Relative to the assumed \(T^\alpha\) term, it scales as
\[
T^{1-5\alpha/4}.
\tag{13}
\]
It becomes more important at low temperature when
\[
\alpha>\frac45.
\]
For \(\alpha=0.88\), the relative exponent is \(-0.10\).
This is another conditional inconsistency of the simple construction. The coefficient, crossover temperature, relevant angular vertices and eventual interacting regime were not determined. Equation (11) therefore is not a self-consistent final material prediction.
Scattering and resistance are different quantities
Let electrons and neutral fluctuations have drift velocities \(v_e,v_b\), mutual friction \(\eta\), and neutral-to-lattice momentum relaxation \(\beta\). Steady force balance gives
\[
neE=\eta(v_e-v_b),\qquad
\eta(v_e-v_b)=\beta v_b.
\]
Since \(j=ne v_e\),
\[
\boxed{
\rho=\frac{\eta\beta}{(ne)^2(\eta+\beta)}.
}
\tag{14}
\]
Finite mutual scattering does not give finite resistance when the momentum sink vanishes.
For example, \(\eta\propto T^{0.12}\) and \(\beta\propto T\) yield \(\rho\sim T\). But that does not explain linear resistivity: the crucial linear relaxation law was inserted into \(\beta\).
Hybridization dynamics, vertex corrections and coupled transport channels already play important roles in Kondo-breakdown theories. The calculations here do not rule those theories out. They rule out shortcut identifications and the particular microscopic candidate tested. arXiv

4. The main mathematical construction
The observational obstacle
We observe angular positions and proper motions much more directly than the stars’ small separation along the line of sight. Multiplying both angular motions by one common distance introduces a spurious relative velocity when their true distances differ.
To leading order, this has the form
\[
w_x=v_x-\beta z,\qquad w_y=v_y,
\]
where \(z\) is the unknown depth difference, \(x\) is aligned with systemic transverse motion, and \(\beta\) describes the corresponding angular-motion shear.
Using independently inverted parallaxes does not automatically solve the problem: their uncertainty can exceed the binary’s physical separation.
The construction sought to cancel \(z\) algebraically, rather than infer it through an eccentricity or orbital-size prior.
Exact identity under a specified observation model
Assume a fixed observer plane and constant \(\beta\). Let
\[
L=xv_y-yv_x,\qquad
\tau=\dot L=x a_y-y a_x.
\]
Choose a differentiable function \(g(y)\), an antiderivative \(G'(y)=g(y)\), and define
\[
H(y)=yg(y)-G(y).
\]
Then \(H'(y)=yg'(y)\).
Construct the observable
\[
W_g
=-g'(y)w_y(xw_y-yw_x)-\beta H(y)v_z.
\]
The key identity is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\left[g(y)L+\beta zH(y)\right]
=-W_g+g(y)\tau.
}
\tag{1}
\]
The proof is short but contains the useful cancellation. First,
\[
xw_y-yw_x=L+\beta yz.
\]
Also,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(gL)=g'v_yL+g\tau,
\]
and
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(\beta zH)
=\beta v_zH+\beta zyg'v_y.
\]
Adding them and substituting the observed angular momentum proves (1).
For a stationary ensemble with integrable terms and no boundary contribution,
\[
\boxed{\langle W_g\rangle=\langle g(y)\tau\rangle.}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus central dynamics predict zero without specifying the eccentricity distribution. The price is a real assumption: the sampled ensemble must satisfy the stationarity condition, and the observation model must be valid.
A directional statistic
Let \(e_x,e_y\) be the projected components of the specified Galactic-field direction. Choosing
\[
g(y)=-e_xe_y|y|
\]
gives, almost everywhere,
\[
\boxed{

=\frac{e_xe_y}{G_NM}
\left[

-|y|w_xw_y
+\frac{\beta}{2}y|y|v_z
\right].
}
\tag{3}
\]
The ideal central-force prediction is
\[
\langle S_{\rm sh}\rangle=0.
\]
For the external-field force above, an additional axisymmetry assumption about the intrinsic position distribution gives a nonnegative expectation. The report supplies the sign proof.

2. The mathematical distinction that makes the test possible
An isotope measurement can count molecules with exactly one heavy atom, rather than count all heavy atoms. Those quantities need not determine one another.
Consider the two native backbone carbons. Let \(X_1,X_2\) indicate whether each is \(^{13}\mathrm C\), and define
\[

\qquad
c=\Pr(X_1=X_2=1).
\]
The probabilities of zero, one and two heavy carbon atoms are exactly
\[
P_0=1-2p+c,\qquad
P_1=2(p-c),\qquad
P_2=c.
\]
Define the carbon-only ratios
\[
r=\frac{P_1}{2P_0},
\qquad
t=\frac{P_2}{P_0}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
p=\frac{r+t}{1+2r+t},
\qquad
c=\frac{t}{1+2r+t}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
This inversion does not require independent isotope placement or identical abundances at the two positions.
The consequence is precise: the singly substituted ratio \(r\), by itself, does not determine the mean heavy-carbon abundance \(p\). The doubly substituted ratio \(t\) supplies the missing information in this two-carbon model.
For fixed \(p\) and \(r\),
\[
\boxed{
t=\frac{p(1+2r)-r}{1-p}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{p}{1-p}-r
=
\frac{c-p^2}{(1-p)(1-2p+c)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus a low singly substituted ratio at fixed mean abundance requires positive correlation between the two heavy-carbon indicators.
This is not reproduced merely by making two independent positions unequal. If their probabilities are \(p+h\) and \(p-h\), direct substitution gives
\[
r-\frac{p}{1-p}
=
\frac{h^2}{(1-p)[(1-p)^2-h^2]}
\geq0.
\]
That change goes in the opposite direction. The published positional interpretation instead assigns a lower mean to the backbone and compensates with an enriched carboxyl carbon.
The crucial experimental qualification
These equations concern native-carbon probabilities after the relevant instrumental and chemical corrections. I did not independently reconstruct those probabilities from the original spectra.
For the numerical comparison, I used the explicit working interface
\[
r=R_{\rm VPDB}\left(1+\frac{\delta_{\rm backbone}}{1000}\right),
\qquad R_{\rm VPDB}=0.011100,
\]
and converted the whole-molecule isotope value to an atom fraction. The reference convention comes from the supplement, but applying its corrected isotope value to this probability model remains an unverified interface. Derivatizing carbons, fragmentation response and correlations with other isotope systems require separate treatment. NASA Technical Reports Server
The predictions below are therefore conditional benchmarks, not a calibrated reinterpretation of the experiment.
3. Two explanations predict substantially different additional signals
I constructed two complete isotope distributions.
In the first, the backbone positions are independent and relatively \(^{13}\mathrm C\)-poor, with a \(^{13}\mathrm C\)-rich carboxyl position maintaining the observed whole-molecule average. Its backbone double-substitution ratio is
\[
t_{\rm positional}=r^2.
\]
In the second, all three native carbon positions have the same average heavy-carbon abundance. The two backbone positions are positively correlated, with their pair probability fixed by equation (2).
These distributions match the same specified whole-molecule average and singly substituted backbone ratio, but predict:
Predicted—not observed—native two-carbon quantity	Bennu benchmark	Murchison benchmark
\(P_2/P_0\), independent-backbone model	\(0.00008632\)	\(0.00011261\)
\(P_2/P_0\), equal-marginal correlated model	\(0.00204686\)	\(0.00069593\)
Ratio between those predictions	23.71	6.18


A probability distribution realizing the correlated benchmark can be written explicitly. Mix independent three-carbon molecules with heavy-carbon probability \(p_{\rm low}=r/(1+r)\), together with a fraction
\[
\lambda=\frac{p_{\rm bulk}-p_{\rm low}}{1-p_{\rm low}}
\]
of molecules carrying three heavy carbons. For Bennu’s benchmark, \(\lambda\approx0.001921\).
This proves mathematical feasibility. It does not identify an actual source, contaminant or reaction. The three-heavy-carbon construction is also not unique; it is only one distribution consistent with the specified pair constraints.
The calculation nevertheless turns an ambiguous explanation into a quantitative discriminator: the equal-marginal carbon-correlation hypothesis has to account for a much larger native doubly substituted population.
4. Testing a physical origin for that correlation
I investigated a particular source-mixture mechanism: molecules form within reservoirs having different heavy-carbon abundances, with carbon atoms sampled independently inside each reservoir. Molecules subsequently mix without exchanging their atoms.
Let \(P\) be the heavy-carbon atom fraction of the reservoir from which a molecule formed. Then
\[
p=\mathbb E[P],\qquad
c=\mathbb E[P^2],
\]
so
\[
\boxed{c-p^2=\operatorname{Var}(P).}
\tag{4}
\]
This provides a direct physical requirement, rather than an adjustable correlation parameter.
How extreme must a source be?
If every reservoir satisfies \(P\leq B\), then \(P^2\leq BP\), hence
\[
\boxed{B\geq\frac{c}{p}.}
\tag{5}
\]
The benchmark requires a reservoir reaching at least 18.05% \(^{13}\mathrm C\) for Bennu and 6.155% for Murchison. These are conditional source requirements—not measured reservoir compositions.
A sharper result applies when sources lie in a specified interval \(a\leq P\leq b\). Since
\[
(P-a)(b-P)\geq0,
\]
taking expectations yields
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(P)\leq(p-a)(b-p).
}
\tag{6}
\]
The bound is sharp: mixtures of the two endpoints attain it.
Comparison with independently measured organic material
A separate Bennu study reports organic-material isotope compositions spanning \(-326\) to \(+364\,‰\) in its comparison with aggregate material. I used that range as a defined source class, not as a bound on every carbon-bearing phase or historical precursor. Nature
At Bennu’s benchmark whole-molecule abundance, equation (6) permits covariance of at most
\[
1.40165\times10^{-5}.
\]
The correlated benchmark needs
\[
1.88210\times10^{-3},
\]
which is 134.28 times larger.
Equivalently, source mixing confined to that interval can depress the apparent backbone value by at most 1.306‰, not the benchmark’s 175‰.
That bounded source-mixture explanation fails.
The restriction cannot legitimately be extended to every Bennu source. The observations concern other aliquots and finite-resolution regions. Rare presolar phases have much more extreme isotope compositions; their existence does not demonstrate that they supplied these amino acids or preserved the required atom-pair correlations. Nature
I therefore did not rescue the model by inserting an unmeasured extreme precursor and calling it an explanation.
5. Why the predicted signal has not yet become empirical evidence
The measured fragment also contains carbon introduced during chemical preparation. Its raw double-substitution signal is not the native pair signal in the table. The preceding method paper documents the relevant fragment structure. Analytical Science Journals
Under independent native and reagent isotope distributions, their count-generating polynomials multiply. Writing \(u=P_1/P_0\) and \(t=P_2/P_0\),
\[
u_{\rm observed}=u_{\rm native}+u_{\rm reagent},
\]
\[
t_{\rm observed}
=t_{\rm native}
+u_{\rm native}u_{\rm reagent}
+t_{\rm reagent}.
\]
Thus the native signal requires
\[
\boxed{
t_{\rm native}
=t_{\rm observed}
-u_{\rm native}u_{\rm reagent}
-t_{\rm reagent}.
}
\tag{7}
\]
The 23.71-fold prediction must not be described as a 23.71-fold increase in a raw mass-spectrum peak.
There is also a mass-resolution issue. Two \(^{13}\mathrm C\) substitutions and one \(^{18}\mathrm O\) substitution differ by only
\[
0.00246468\ {\rm Da},
\]
calculated from NIST isotope masses. NIST
Using the reported instrument setting and fixed-transient resolution scaling, that corresponds to approximately 1.25 peak widths near the relevant mass. This does not prove the contributions cannot be separated; it means calibrated line shapes, standards and appropriate integration are necessary. A nominal mass-plus-two signal is not automatically a count of carbon pairs. Nature
The raw-data archive still did not yield its contents in this session. No actual doubly substituted isotope signal was acquired or fitted. Consequently, the central discrimination between the two benchmark distributions remains unperformed.
I also checked the inference from scan quality. A stable mixture of coeluting microscopic populations can produce exactly the same multinomial ion-count distribution as one effective population. Unimodal or approximately Gaussian scan statistics alone cannot identify the source history. That is a scoped mathematical limitation, not evidence that mixing occurred or that the authors’ quality controls were ineffective.
6. An additional proposed pattern failed independent observations
A separate exploratory candidate was a universal \(+15\,‰\) nitrogen-isotope offset between glycine and β-alanine, suggested by two rounded recent comparisons.
I checked that proposal against six same-study meteorite comparisons in Elsila and colleagues’ primary dataset. Their printed glycine-minus-β-alanine differences are
\[
-12,\;-6,\;-73,\;-16,\;-14,\;-9\,‰.
\]
All six central differences have the opposite sign. NASA Sciences & Exploration Directorate
The universal offset was not retained. Different extracts, precursor compositions and processing histories can genuinely differ; this is not a claim that the experimental papers contradict one another. It is a failure of the proposed universal rule, not something to repair by assigning a separate adjustable source composition to every meteorite.
7. Novelty, verification and the remaining scientific obstacle
The central conceptual mechanism is not new. Piasecki’s 2015 work explicitly discusses mixing-induced excess doubly substituted species and the apparent site-specific isotope effects that mixing can produce. Its terrestrial propane examples involved much smaller effects, but the conceptual antecedent is clear. CaltechTHESIS
The work retained here is a scoped quantitative application: exact probability inversion, two distinguishable benchmark distributions, a sharp source-composition bound, instrument-level qualifications and an independent failure test.
The code verifies four symbolic identities, checks the constrained distributions independently with linear programming, and tests inversion and reagent deconvolution on 1,000 reproducibly generated probability distributions. The largest numerical residual was approximately \(4.23\times10^{-10}\). Those are internal mathematical checks, not independent scientific validation.
