# Complete-monotonicity classification for finite reversible chains

**Date:** 10 October 2026.  
**Status:** An internally completed argument under the hypotheses below. Original priority and landmark significance are not established. This note is not a claim that the requested 8–10/10 research objective has been achieved. The accompanying symbolic checks concern an example, not a formal verification of the theorem.

## 1. Exact statement

Write
\[
\Delta_n^\circ=\{p\in(0,1)^n:\sum_i p_i=1\},
\]
with the conventional singleton simplex when \(n=1\). Probability distributions are row vectors. Suppose that, for every finite alphabet size \(n\), a finite-valued functional
\[
D_n:\Delta_n^\circ\times\Delta_n^\circ\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
\]
has the following properties.

**Regularity and normalization.** For each fixed full-support reference \(q\), the map \(p\mapsto D_n(p,q)\) is continuous throughout the open simplex and \(C^\infty\) in some neighborhood of \(q\). Also \(D_n(q,q)=0\). No analyticity, nondegenerate Hessian, or differentiability in \(q\) is assumed.

**Invariance under independent uninformative augmentation.** For all full-support \(p,q\in\Delta_n^\circ\) and \(r\in\Delta_m^\circ\),
\[
D_{nm}(p\otimes r,q\otimes r)=D_n(p,q),
\qquad
D_{mn}(r\otimes p,r\otimes q)=D_n(p,q).
\tag{1}
\]
The second equality is redundant when relabeling invariance is assumed, but it is stated explicitly to avoid that extra convention. Full tensor additivity is not assumed.

**Universal complete monotonicity.** For every full-support \(p,q\) and every irreducible continuous-time Markov generator \(L\) satisfying detailed balance
\[
q_iL_{ij}=q_jL_{ji},
\]
the function
\[
g(t)=D_n(pe^{tL},q)
\]
is completely monotone:
\[
(-1)^k g^{(k)}(t)\geq0\quad(k=0,1,2,\ldots;\ t>0).
\tag{2}
\]

### Classification theorem

These conditions hold **if and only if** there exists one power series, independent of the alphabet and of \(q\),
\[
\Psi(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_kz^k,
\qquad a_k\geq0,
\tag{3}
\]
with infinite radius of convergence, such that
\[
\boxed{D_n(p,q)=\Psi\!\left(\chi^2(p\Vert q)\right)},
\qquad
\chi^2(p\Vert q)=\sum_i\frac{(p_i-q_i)^2}{q_i}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, data processing is a **consequence**, not a hypothesis.

### Replication consequence

Every nonzero functional in this class obeys, for \(s=\chi^2(p\Vert q)>0\) and every integer \(N\geq1\),
\[
\boxed{
D_{n^N}(p^{\otimes N},q^{\otimes N})
\geq \frac{(1+s)^N-1}{s}\,D_n(p,q).
}
\tag{5}
\]
This bound is attained by \(D=c\chi^2\), so its universal factor cannot be improved.

Consequently, under the stated regularity, **there is no nonzero tensor-additive functional having universal complete monotonicity**. Even growth of order \(O(N)\) under independent replication is impossible within the classified class.

## 2. A spectral extraction lemma

We use the classical Bernstein representation theorem: a completely monotone function on \((0,\infty)\) is the Laplace transform of a positive measure on \([0,\infty)\). If it has a finite continuous limit at zero, the measure is finite and its total mass is that limit. A reference is Christian Berg, *Stieltjes–Pick–Bernstein–Schoenberg and their connection to complete monotonicity* (2007), Theorem 1.5.

**Lemma.** Let
\[
g(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda t}\,d\mu(\lambda)
\]
for a finite positive measure \(\mu\). Let \(S\subset[0,\infty)\) be locally finite. Suppose that, for every positive \(R\notin S\),
\[
g(t)=\sum_{s\in S,\ s<R}c_se^{-st}+O(e^{-Rt})
\quad(t\to\infty).
\tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
\mu=\sum_{s\in S}c_s\delta_s,
\qquad c_s\geq0.
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Order the elements of \(S\). The bound before the first possible exponent excludes positive measure below that exponent: positive mass on \([0,b]\) would make \(g(t)\geq \mu([0,b])e^{-bt}\), contradicting a strictly faster upper bound. At the first allowed exponent \(s\), (6) gives
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{st}g(t)=c_s.
\]
Once the measure is known to be supported on \([s,\infty)\), dominated convergence identifies this limit as \(\mu(\{s\})\). In particular \(c_s\geq0\). Subtract that atom, leaving a positive measure, and repeat. Local finiteness lets the induction cover every bounded interval; the asymptotic bounds exclude all gaps. No remaining positive measure can lie beyond every finite cutoff. This proves (7). \(\square\)

The lemma is important here because it uses asymptotic expansions, not convergent Taylor series. A smooth remainder invisible to every Taylor coefficient cannot survive as positive Laplace mass beyond every finite decay rate.

## 3. Realizing arbitrary eigenbases by valid Markov generators

Fix \(q\in\Delta_n^\circ\), with \(n\geq2\). Work on the real Hilbert space
\[
H_q=\{u\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_iq_iu_i=0\},
\qquad
\langle u,v\rangle_q=\sum_iq_iu_iv_i.
\]
The admissible density deviations form the open set
\[
U_q=\{u\in H_q:u_i>-1\ \text{for all }i\}.
\]
Define
\[
F_q(u)=D_n(q(1+u),q),
\]
where \(q(1+u)\) denotes componentwise multiplication. Then \(F_q\) is continuous on \(U_q\), smooth near zero, and \(F_q(0)=0\).

The reset generator
\[
L_0=\mathbf1 q-I
\]
has off-diagonal entries \((L_0)_{ij}=q_j>0\). On \(H_q\), it equals \(-I\).

Choose **any** \(q\)-orthonormal basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_d\), where \(d=n-1\). Choose positive numbers \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d\), rationally independent and sufficiently close to one, and define
\[
L\mathbf1=0,
\qquad Le_j=-\lambda_je_j.
\tag{8}
\]
The operator is \(q\)-self-adjoint and annihilates constants. Therefore its rows sum to zero and it satisfies detailed balance. Its matrix is arbitrarily close to \(L_0\), so all off-diagonal entries remain strictly positive. Thus it is an irreducible, reversible Markov generator.

This step does **not** assume that orthogonal rotations of probability vectors are stochastic maps. Instead, every orthonormal basis is realized as an eigenbasis of an actual Markov generator close to reset.

Reversibility gives the density-ratio evolution
\[
\frac{pe^{tL}}{q}-1=e^{tL}\left(\frac pq-1\right).
\tag{9}
\]
For \(u=\sum_jx_je_j\), this is \(\sum_jx_je^{-\lambda_jt}e_j\).

## 4. Positivity forces every Taylor jet to be radial

Write the formal Taylor expansion at zero in the chosen basis as
\[
F_q\left(\sum_jx_je_j\right)\sim
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N^d}c_\alpha x^\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]
Smoothness supplies Taylor expansions to every finite order, with controlled remainders near zero. Thus, along the orbit of (8), for every positive cutoff \(R\),
\[
F_q(e^{tL}u)
=
\sum_{\alpha\cdot\lambda<R}
 c_\alpha x^\alpha e^{-(\alpha\cdot\lambda)t}
+O(e^{-Rt}),
\tag{11}
\]
when \(R\) avoids the listed exponents. To see this directly, truncate the ordinary Taylor expansion at a degree \(M\) with \((M+1)\min_j\lambda_j>R\); terms above the cutoff are absorbed into the remainder. Terms of sufficiently high total degree cannot have exponent below \(R\).

Rational independence makes \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha\cdot\lambda\) injective. Its image is locally finite. Complete monotonicity, continuity at \(t=0\), Bernstein's theorem, and the extraction lemma therefore imply
\[
c_\alpha x^\alpha\geq0
\tag{12}
\]
for every sufficiently small real vector \(x\).

If any coordinate of \(\alpha\) is odd, changing that coordinate's sign in (12) forces \(c_\alpha=0\). If every coordinate is even, then \(c_\alpha\geq0\).

The basis was arbitrary. Consequently every homogeneous Taylor polynomial is invariant under reflection in every hyperplane of \(H_q\), and hence under its full orthogonal group. An orthogonally invariant homogeneous polynomial is zero in odd degree and a multiple of \(\|u\|_q^{2k}\) in degree \(2k\). The coefficients are nonnegative by (12). Therefore the Taylor jets are exactly
\[
F_q(u)\sim\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k},
\qquad a_k(q)\geq0.
\tag{13}
\]
This argument also applies when \(d=1\): the only nontrivial orthogonal transformation is reflection.

## 5. Recovering the functional globally without assuming analyticity

Take an arbitrary \(u\in U_q\), not necessarily small. Under reset,
\[
e^{tL_0}u=e^{-t}u.
\]
As \(t\to\infty\), the trajectory enters the smooth neighborhood of zero, where (13) gives the full asymptotic expansion
\[
F_q(e^{-t}u)\sim
\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k}e^{-2kt}.
\]
Apply Bernstein's theorem and the extraction lemma again. The positive representing measure is exactly
\[
\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k}\delta_{2k}.
\]
Taking \(t\downarrow0\), monotone convergence and continuity yield
\[
F_q(u)=\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k}.
\tag{14}
\]
The sum is finite because \(F_q(u)\) is finite. This proves an actual global series identity, not merely a statement about derivatives at equilibrium. It also excludes nonzero smooth flat remainders.

## 6. Independent augmentation identifies one entire series

For any full-support references \(q\) and \(r\),
\[
\chi^2(p\otimes r\Vert q\otimes r)=\chi^2(p\Vert q).
\]
Combining (1) and (14), and varying \(p\) near \(q\), identifies all power-series coefficients for reference \(q\otimes r\) with those for \(q\). Varying the other tensor factor identifies them with those for \(r\). Thus
\[
a_k(q)=a_k(q\otimes r)=a_k(r).
\]
They are universal coefficients \(a_k\); no continuity in \(q\) is needed.

To prove infinite radius of convergence, fix any \(z>0\), choose \(n>z+1\), and let \(q\) be uniform on \(n\) states. Put
\[
p=(1-\theta)q+\theta\delta_1,
\qquad
\theta=\sqrt{\frac z{n-1}}<1.
\]
This \(p\) has full support and \(\chi^2(p\Vert q)=z\). Equation (14) gives \(\sum_ka_kz^k=D_n(p,q)<\infty\). Since this holds for every positive \(z\), the series has infinite radius. The necessity of (3)–(4) is proved.

## 7. Converse and automatic data processing

Let \(D=\Psi(\chi^2)\) with (3). Continuity, smoothness, normalization, and (1) are immediate.

For a reversible generator, decompose the initial deviation into orthonormal eigenvectors:
\[
u=\sum_jb_je_j,
\qquad
\chi^2(p_t\Vert q)=\sum_jb_j^2e^{-2\lambda_jt}.
\tag{15}
\]
This is the Laplace transform of the finite positive measure
\(\sigma=\sum_jb_j^2\delta_{2\lambda_j}\). Then
\[
D(p_t,q)=\int e^{-\lambda t}\,d\mu(\lambda),
\qquad
\mu=\sum_{k\geq1}a_k\sigma^{*k}.
\tag{16}
\]
The measure is positive and finite: its total mass is
\(\sum_ka_k[\chi^2(p\Vert q)]^k=\Psi(\chi^2(p\Vert q))\).
Hence (2) holds. This completes the classification proof.

For data processing, let \(K\) be a stochastic matrix and remove any output coordinates with zero \(qK\)-mass. The output likelihood ratio is the conditional mean of the input likelihood ratio under the joint law \(q_iK_{ij}\). Conditional Jensen therefore gives
\[
\chi^2(pK\Vert qK)\leq\chi^2(p\Vert q).
\]
Because all \(a_k\) are nonnegative, \(\Psi\) is nondecreasing on \([0,\infty)\). Therefore
\[
D(pK,qK)\leq D(p,q).
\tag{17}
\]

## 8. The composition obstruction

For independent pairs with chi-square divergences \(s\) and \(t\),
\[
1+\chi^2(p\otimes r\Vert q\otimes v)=(1+s)(1+t).
\tag{18}
\]
Thus independent replication gives the exact identity
\[
D(p^{\otimes N},q^{\otimes N})
=\Psi((1+s)^N-1).
\tag{19}
\]
For \(z\geq s>0\), positivity of the coefficients implies
\[
\Psi(z)=\sum_{k\geq1}a_ks^k(z/s)^k
\geq(z/s)\Psi(s).
\]
Substitution into (19) proves (5).

There is also strict superadditivity for every nonzero member of the class whenever both input pairs differ:
\[
\Psi(s+t+st)>\Psi(s)+\Psi(t)\qquad(s,t>0).
\tag{20}
\]
Indeed, \((s+t+st)^k>s^k+t^k\) for every integer \(k\geq1\), and at least one coefficient is positive.

A tensor-additive normalized functional automatically satisfies (1), but (20) contradicts tensor additivity unless all coefficients vanish. The no-go theorem follows.

A formal alternative makes the sign obstruction explicit: additivity would require
\[
\Psi(s+t+st)=\Psi(s)+\Psi(t).
\]
Differentiating in \(t\) at zero gives \((1+s)\Psi'(s)=\Psi'(0)\), hence \(\Psi(s)=c\log(1+s)\). Its coefficient of \(s^2\) is \(-c/2\), incompatible with (3) unless \(c=0\).

## 9. An exact illustrative witness

This example illustrates the obstruction; it is not offered as a new counterexample or as a proof of the classification.

Take
\[
q=(1/10,9/10),\quad p=(7/10,3/10),\quad
L=\begin{pmatrix}-9/10&9/10\\1/10&-1/10\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The generator is irreducible and satisfies detailed balance. Its trajectory is
\(p_t=q+e^{-t}(p-q)\), with \(\chi^2(p_t\Vert q)=4e^{-2t}\).
The order-two Rényi divergence has profile
\[
f(t)=\log\sum_i\frac{p_t(i)^2}{q_i}
=\log(1+4e^{-2t}).
\]
At \(t_*=(\log2)/2\), exact differentiation yields
\[
f'''(t_*)=16/27>0,
\]
where complete monotonicity requires \(f'''\leq0\).

## 10. Scope, comparison, and status

The conclusion concerns finite-state reversible continuous-time chains and full-support distributions. The regularity condition is smoothness near equilibrium, not merely continuity. No theorem without that smoothness assumption is claimed. No claim about a fixed generator, Gaussian heat flow alone, quantum dynamics, or empirical physical laws follows automatically.

The entire positive series need not be linear. For example, \(\chi^2\), \((\chi^2)^2\), and \(e^{\chi^2}-1\) all satisfy the classification assumptions. It would be incorrect to conclude that Pearson chi-square is the only functional without imposing additional hypotheses.

Closest checked literature includes:

- Christian Berg (2007), *Stieltjes–Pick–Bernstein–Schoenberg and their connection to complete monotonicity*, especially Theorem 1.5. This supplies the classical positive-Laplace representation used in the proof.
- Chandra Nair (2023), *On signs of derivatives of entropy along Markov semi-groups: a collection of some recent results*. This includes finite-state entropy sign investigations; failure of complete monotonicity for ordinary entropy is not itself new.
- A. N. Gorban, P. A. Gorban, and G. Judge (2010), *Entropy: The Markov Ordering Approach*, arXiv:1003.1377. Its Markov-Lyapunov and additivity characterizations concern different hypotheses and should not be confused with the classification above.
- Yuzhou Gu and Mark Sellke (2026), *A Counterexample to the Gaussian Completely Monotone Conjecture*, arXiv:2605.11656. That Gaussian-heat counterexample is prior work, not a discovery of this session.
