# Cost-aware separation under perfect confounding

## Result status

This is an internally reconstructed, model-scoped candidate theorem with a complete elementary proof below. It is not a theorem about arbitrary causal models, has not been checked in a proof assistant, and carries no novelty or priority claim. The two-mechanism construction is deliberately narrow enough to expose the exact observation and cost interface.

## Experiment and mechanism interface

There is one treatment variable (X), one measured endpoint (Y), and an unobserved confounder (U). In passive data, (X=U). The candidate mechanisms are

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
H_0: & Y=\alpha+(\Delta/2)U+\epsilon,\\
H_1: & Y=\alpha+\Delta X-(\Delta/2)U+\epsilon,
\end{array}
\qquad \Delta>0.
\]

Under passive observation both give exactly (Y=\alpha+(\Delta/2)U+\epsilon), so no observational sample size separates them. Under a perfect intervention (\operatorname{do}(X=x)), the structural equation for (X) is replaced by (x), while the latent (U) and endpoint disturbance remain. The interventional means are (\alpha) under (H_0) and (\alpha+\Delta x) under (H_1). This is a direct-effect question; it does not identify an unrestricted DAG.

An intervention uses a fresh independent unit and returns one real-valued measurement

\[
Z=Y+\zeta_\tau,\qquad \zeta_\tau\sim N(0,1/\tau),
\]

where the precision (\tau>0) is chosen by the experimenter. The measurement noise is independent across units and from (U,\epsilon). The latent disturbance U is iid and mean-zero across units, with sub-Gaussian proxy at most (s_U^2); (\epsilon) is iid and mean-zero across units with sub-Gaussian proxy at most (s_\epsilon^2). Both are independent. The intercept (\alpha\) is unknown and unrestricted. A perfect intervention at magnitude (a=|x|\le A) costs (c_I(a)=c_0+\kappa_Ia^2), where c_0 includes acquisition/preparation of each fresh unit and intervention overhead; one endpoint measurement at precision (\tau) costs (c_M(\tau)=m_0+\gamma\tau), with (c_0+m_0>0,\kappa_I\ge0,\gamma>0\). Costs are paid per call.

Set

\[
B=(\Delta^2/4)s_U^2+s_\epsilon^2>0,\quad
V(\tau)=B+1/\tau,\quad
c(a,\tau)=c_0+m_0+\kappa_Ia^2+\gamma\tau.
\]

The values (A,\Delta,B,c_0,\kappa_I,m_0,\gamma) are supplied to the design rule. Acquiring and validating those values is outside this theorem and must be costed in a laboratory application. There is no carryover, treatment noncompliance, time drift, or shared-unit pairing.

## Sharp cost law

For a chosen magnitude and precision, define its least-favourable Gaussian information rate per total intervention-plus-measurement cost by

\[
\eta(a,\tau)=\frac{\Delta^2a^2}{2V(\tau)c(a,\tau)},\qquad
\eta_* = \sup_{0<a\le A,\,\tau>0}\eta(a,\tau).
\]

For any procedure that adaptively chooses actions and precisions, may stop at a data-dependent time, and has both mechanism-class error probabilities at most (\delta<1/2),

\[
\mathbb E_{H_0,\alpha=0}[C]\ge
\frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{\eta_*},\qquad
\mathbb E_{H_1,\alpha=0}[C]\ge
\frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{\eta_*},
\]

where (C) is the sum of every intervention and measurement charge, and

\[
\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)=(1-2\delta)\log\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}.
\]

The first inequality uses the admissible Gaussian subfamily (U\sim N(0,s_U^2)), (\epsilon\sim N(0,s_\epsilon^2)). Under either mechanism, after intervention (x), (Z\) has variance (V(\tau)); with the same (\alpha) under the two hypotheses, its mean difference is (\Delta x), so the per-call KL is exactly (\Delta^2x^2/(2V(\tau))\). The adaptive chain rule gives

\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_0^{\pi,\tau_\delta}\|P_1^{\pi,\tau_\delta})
\le \mathbb E_0\sum_{t\le\tau_\delta}\eta(a_t,\tau_t)c(a_t,\tau_t)
\le \eta_*\mathbb E_0 C.
\]

The policy's conditional action law is the same function of the observed history under both hypotheses, so it contributes no KL term. Applying data processing to the terminal decision gives the binary relative-entropy lower bound. Reversing the KL proves the (H_1) bound. This lower bound is valid for every adaptive procedure, even though the proof chooses a common intercept; uniform correctness over unknown (\alpha) must in particular hold at that pair.

The unknown intercept does make a single fixed-dose arm non-identifiable uniformly: its mean shift can be absorbed by a different intercept in the other mechanism class. A balanced design avoids this. For fresh units at (+a) and (-a), define

\[
D=(Z_{+a}-Z_{-a})/2,\qquad q=\Delta a.
\]

Then (\mathbb E_0D=0) and (\mathbb E_1D=q), independent of (\alpha). The paired confounder term is proportional to (U_+-U_-), which is symmetric for iid (U_+,U_-); changing its sign under (H_1) leaves its distribution unchanged. Thus the two paired laws are translates of the same noise law, and (D\) has sub-Gaussian variance proxy at most (v=(B+1/\tau)/2\).

A pair costs (2c(a,\tau)). Its least-favourable Gaussian KL is (q^2/(B+1/\tau)), so the KL-per-cost rate is exactly \(\eta(a,\tau)\). The best precommitted robust static allocation cannot improve this rate. For a fixed schedule \((x_i,\tau_i)\) and arbitrary intercepts under the two hypotheses, the least-favourable Gaussian KL minimizes over the intercept offset (d\) as

\[
\frac{\Delta^2}{2}\min_d\sum_i\frac{(x_i-d)^2}{V(\tau_i)}
\le \frac{\Delta^2}{2}\sum_i\frac{x_i^2}{V(\tau_i)}
\le \eta_*\sum_i c(|x_i|,\tau_i).
\]

Balanced (+a,-a) calls attain equality in the first bound at (d=0); repeating an optimizer of \(\eta\) attains the second. Hence adaptive action selection gives no larger minimax information-per-cost constant in this binary model. Adaptive stopping remains useful and is available to the static comparator as well.

## Closed-form design

For fixed (a), let (d(a)=c_0+m_0+\kappa_Ia^2>0). Differentiating \(\eta(a,\tau)\) in \(\tau\) gives

\[
\tau(a)=\sqrt{\frac{d(a)}{B\gamma}},\qquad
\max_{\tau>0}\eta(a,\tau)=
\frac{\Delta^2a^2}{2\gamma\left(1+\sqrt{Bd(a)/\gamma}\right)^2}.
\]

The last expression is increasing in (a^2), so (a_*=A) and

\[
\tau_* = \sqrt{\frac{c_0+m_0+\kappa_IA^2}{B\gamma}},\quad
\eta_* = \frac{\Delta^2A^2}{2\gamma\left(1+\sqrt{B(c_0+m_0+\kappa_IA^2)/\gamma}\right)^2}.
\]

For example, if (w=\sqrt{B(c_0+m_0+\kappa_Ia^2)/\gamma}\), the derivative of (a^2/(1+w)^2), with respect to (a^2), has the positive numerator (1+B(c_0+m_0)/(\gamma w)\). The optimum is therefore the strongest available intervention even after its quadratic cost is charged. Precision is finite because biological/confounder noise (B>0) eventually dominates readout noise.

## Implementable sequential procedure

For each fresh pair, run (\operatorname{do}(+A)) and (\operatorname{do}(-A)), measure each endpoint at precision (\tau_*\), and record (D_j=(Z_{j,+}-Z_{j,-})/2\). Set (q=\Delta A), (v=(B+1/\tau_*)/2), (S_0=0), and (h=\log(1/\delta)\). Update

\[
S_j=S_{j-1}+\frac{q}{v}\left(D_j-\frac q2\right).
\]

Stop and report (H_1) if (S_j\ge h), or report (H_0) if (S_j\le-h); otherwise acquire another pair. Under (H_0), the conditional sub-Gaussian bound gives

\[
\mathbb E_0\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac qv(D_j-q/2)\right)\mid\mathcal F_{j-1}\right]\le1,
\]

and under (H_1) the same holds with the exponent negated. Ville's inequality therefore gives each wrong-decision probability at most \(\delta\), uniformly over the supplied intercept and the stated disturbance class. The nonzero score drift and sub-Gaussian tails imply almost-sure stopping. At the least-favourable Gaussian point this is the ordinary SPRT: its expected total cost under either mechanism is

\[
\mathbb E_h C=\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\eta_*}(1+o(1)),\qquad \delta\downarrow0,
\]

which matches the lower bound's leading constant because (\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)\sim\log(1/\delta)\). Thus the law is sharp in its high-confidence asymptotic, not asserted as an exact finite-\(\delta\) equality.

`design.py` implements the closed-form choice and the paired e-process stopping rule. It is an executable model-level design, not a validated laboratory protocol.

## Scope limits and falsifiers

The result does not handle unknown intervention compliance, action-dependent latent distributions, endpoint drift between a pair, dependent experimental units, a misspecified sub-Gaussian bound, a continuum of near-null effects, several competing DAGs, or a cost for learning the supplied design parameters. A lab would falsify the interface if the paired-difference variance proxy exceeded (v), if (D)'s conditional mean departed from (0) or (q), or if intervention assignment changed the latent-unit distribution. If any such observation occurs, the current error guarantee and information rate do not apply.

## Prior-art boundary

The closest current general adaptive-design source found is Xu and Zhang's 2026 preprint FOCUS. It formulates fully online fixed-confidence recovery of a fully observed linear-Gaussian DAG and its edge weights, proves a max-min interventional-KL lower bound, reduces continuous intervention values to endpoints, and proposes an adaptive max-min allocation with tracking and sequential stopping. Its objective charges rounds/samples rather than the separate intervention and endpoint-precision costs here, and its stated model has independent structural noise rather than a hidden confounder.

Hou, Park, and Li's 2026 preprint directly addresses latent variables and selection. Its model-free system-subgraph/MAG target is identified using single-target intervention regimes on all observed system variables, with a two-stage inference procedure and constraint-query counts. This is a substantial adjacent prior, but it does not select interventions by per-action cost, tune measurement precision, or give the binary fixed-confidence law proved here. Hauser and Bühlmann's 2012 primary paper establishes that intervention distributions refine observational Markov equivalence classes under its graph assumptions.

Together these sources rule out presenting this as a new general adaptive causal-discovery principle. The candidate contribution remains only the explicit latent-confounding two-mechanism obstruction and its cost/precision-sensitive sharp specialization. The preprints are recent source evidence, not external review; priority and broader novelty remain unknown.
