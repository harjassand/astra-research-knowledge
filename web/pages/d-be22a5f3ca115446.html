1. EVERY FIXED GLOBAL ENTRY HAS A LEGAL COUNT

For a circuit with e edge gates and f field gates, baseline homogeneous P has
degree 2e+2f on N=4(e+f) distinct coordinates. Physical wires number W=2e+f;
there are 2f dummy coordinates. Q is the product of physical wire factors
(x_h+x_h') times the dummy elementary symmetric polynomial e_f.

Open the cyclic boundary on every active qubit. For fixed computational
strings a,b, pin the first row legs to a and the complemented last column
legs to 1-b. Put Delta=|a|-|b| on active qubits. Internal physical wires still
force exactly one selection. Selected boundary physical coordinates number
n_active+Delta, so P's total selected physical count is W+Delta. Hence P
selects f-Delta dummies and Q must use e_(f+Delta), not e_f.

After pinning/removing the 2 n_active boundary coordinates, the degrees are
 deg P'=N/2-n_active-Delta,
 deg Q'=N/2-n_active+Delta;
their sum is the remaining ground-set size. When the dummy rank lies outside
[0,2f] the entry is exactly zero. Otherwise the dummy uniform-matroid factor
is still homogeneous LC. Derivative/zero-variable pinnings preserve nonzero
multiaffine LC factors. P' is still a product of local tables with disjoint
coordinates; positive support seeds are acquired by inspecting each constant
size pinned table separately, rejecting a zero table. Q' support is explicit.
Its positive coefficients are one. Range bounds restrict the original rational
local products and thus have polynomial logarithmic length. An inactive qubit
contributes delta_(a_v,b_v), handled directly. If no variables remain, evaluate
the constant directly instead of applying the n>=1 theorem.

Consequently an unnormalized full entry K_(a,b) has a relative-error count
whose every-tape bit cost is polynomial in circuit size and accuracy. This
does not require constructing the exponentially many-entry matrix K.
K_(b,b)>=1 because B>=I and powers preserve that spectral bound. The true
denominator is therefore positive, though its normalized probability can
be exponentially small. Positive counts can output zero on failed tapes;
the implementation below explicitly handles that case. Zero numerators are
zero on every tape by the imported support-preservation contract.

2. EXACT VARIANCE LEMMA (COMPLETE PROOF)

Let sigma be ANY PSD trace-one matrix. Sample b with p_b=sigma_(b,b), and
for p_b>0 put g_b=(O sigma)_(b,b)/p_b. A zero diagonal has zero entire row
and column by PSD Cauchy--Schwarz, so define g_b=0 there. Then
 E g_b=sum_b (O sigma)_(b,b)=Tr(O sigma).
Using Cauchy--Schwarz on sigma^(1/2) O* e_b and sigma^(1/2) e_b,
 |(O sigma)_(b,b)|^2 <= (O sigma O*)_(b,b) sigma_(b,b).
Divide by p_b and sum to obtain
 E |g_b|^2 <= Tr(O sigma O*) <= ||O||^2.
This proof has no dimension or minimum-positive-diagonal factor.

In our real symmetric nonnegative sigma, replace Hermitian O by its entrywise
real part A=(O+conj O)/2. It is real symmetric, ||A||<=||O||<=1, keeps row
sparsity at most r, and Tr(O sigma)=Tr(A sigma). Then
 g_b=sum_a A_(b,a) K_(a,b)/K_(b,b)
is a real number computable using at most r+1 entry counts. Supplied row-list
algorithms and coefficient precision are part of the input and runtime.
All-n Pauli strings have r=1. Odd-Y strings have exactly zero expectation
for this real sigma/rho and need no stochastic estimate.

Let D be the ENTRYWISE absolute value of A and define
 u_b=(D sigma)_(b,b)/p_b>=0.
Every entry of A has modulus<=1. Hermiticity and r-row sparsity imply the same
column sparsity; max row sum of D<=r, so ||D||<=r. Applying the same lemma,
 E u_b^2<=r^2 and E u_b<=r.

3. FINITE-BIT REPAIR OF RARE ENTRIES AND FAILED TAPES

Fix 0<epsilon<=1, r>=1 (zero observable is handled directly),
 T=10/epsilon, eta=epsilon/(100r),
 delta_entry=epsilon/[100 T(r+1)],
 delta_sample=epsilon/(100T).
Use an independently seeded baseline diagonal sampler with TV error at most
delta_sample. At each sampled b, estimate K_(b,b) and the at most r needed
K_(a,b) to relative eta, individually boosted to failure delta_entry.
If the estimated denominator is zero return Y=0. Otherwise form the rational
ratio sum h_b and return Y=clip(h_b,[-T,T]). All loops and random tapes use
the inherited deterministic caps; clipping is rational arithmetic.

On the event all r+1 counts are relatively accurate, eta<=1/100 and
 |h_b-g_b|<=3 eta u_b.
Clipping is 1-Lipschitz. On a bad entry event the two clipped values differ
by at most 2T. Thus under the true diagonal law,
 |E Y-E clip(g)|<=3 eta E u+2T(r+1)delta_entry
                  <=.03 epsilon+.02 epsilon.
The exact clipping bias is at most
 E|g-clip(g)|<=E g^2/T<=.1 epsilon.
Replacing the diagonal law by its TV approximation changes expectation by
at most 2T delta_sample=.02 epsilon. Including the Gibbs approximation error,
total estimator bias is at most .22 epsilon (using the conservative .05
epsilon Gibbs bound). This is an expectation bound over failed tapes; no
union over every Monte Carlo call and no rare-event lower bound is needed.

The sample second moment also stays bounded. On good counts,
 Y^2<=h_b^2<=2g_b^2+18 eta^2 u_b^2.
On bad counts Y^2<=T^2. Consequently
 E_true_diagonal Y^2<=2+18 eta^2 r^2+T^2(r+1)delta_entry
                    <=2+.0018+.1=2.1018.
Replacing the law changes this NONNEGATIVE bounded expectation by at most
T^2 delta_sample=.1, so E Y^2<=2.2018.
With M=ceil(256/epsilon^2) independent complete draws, Chebyshev gives
 Pr[|mean(Y)-E Y|>epsilon/2]<=4*2.2018/(M epsilon^2)<.035.
The output therefore has additive error below epsilon with success above
3/4. Independent median amplification costs O(log(1/delta_final)) repeats.
Each FPRAS has polynomial encoded outputs on all tapes, so arbitrary bad
ratios still have polynomial bit lengths before clipping.
