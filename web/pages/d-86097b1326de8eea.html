# Literature assessment: robust broadcasting of a promised pure-state family

Prepared 2026-10-09. This is a bounded literature scan and novelty assessment, not a proof audit or a claim that a complete theorem has been announced. The candidate under review is: a full-domain 1→2 CPTP broadcaster whose two marginals each reconstruct every member of an arbitrary finite or infinite family E of pure states to half-trace error at most b implies one entanglement-breaking (EB) channel approximates the identity uniformly on E with error f(b)→0, independently of dimension, family size, and Holevo capacity. The proposed proof bound is O(b^(1/4)); the proof is still under audit. The supplied mixed-state extension counterexample is taken as given and is not assessed here.

## Short verdict

The exact ancestor is the no-broadcasting/no-cloning characterization: exact broadcasting of a set of quantum states is possible iff they commute pairwise; for pure states this means distinct members are orthogonal. The closest robust antecedent is Kretschmann–Schlingemann–Werner (KSW, 2008): if one marginal channel is close to identity on the *entire input channel* in cb norm, the other marginal must be close to a constant channel, dimension-independently. That does not appear to settle the candidate’s promised-family problem or its structural conclusion that one EB channel approximates identity on E.

The 2017 approximate-broadcasting literature optimizes average fidelity of finite ensembles, and the 2006–2011 cloning/state-estimation/de Finetti literature treats many-output or asymptotic regimes. Those are nearby but have different quantifiers and often dimension-, prior-, or output-count-dependent bounds. In the bounded search, I found no prior theorem with the candidate’s exact combination of: arbitrary pure-state promise set, 1→2, uniform worst-case half-trace errors, one common EB approximant, and no dimension/cardinality/capacity dependence. This is a scoped search, not an exhaustive proof of priority.

If the proof survives, I’d describe it as a substantial structural robustness theorem in mathematical quantum information: a dimension-free promised-set stability form of exact no-broadcasting. It is not a new universal no-cloning principle. Its strongest value is that it turns a local cloning promise on a set into one global measure-and-prepare approximation on that set, without an ensemble prior or capacity parameter. A calibrated assessment would be “strong and potentially significant, but specialized”; I would not call it a 10/10 or claim the exponent is sharp. The O(b^(1/4)) rate is the reported proof bound; I found no matching lower bound establishing optimality.

## Closest primary results

1. **Exact no-broadcasting.** H. Barnum, C. M. Caves, C. A. Fuchs, R. Jozsa, B. Schumacher, “Noncommuting Mixed States Cannot Be Broadcast,” *Physical Review Letters* 76 (1996), 2818–2821, [arXiv:quant-ph/9511010](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9511010), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.76.2818). One common channel exactly broadcasts a set iff the density operators commute pairwise. For a pure family, exact broadcasting is cloning, and pairwise distinct rays must be orthogonal. A common measurement-and-prepare copier then exists. This is the exact qualitative target, not a robust uniform-error theorem.

   A useful set-level generalization is H. Barnum, J. Barrett, M. Leifer, A. Wilce, “Cloning and Broadcasting in Generic Probabilistic Theories,” [arXiv:quant-ph/0611295](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0611295): broadcastable sets lie in a simplex generated by jointly distinguishable/cloneable states. In quantum theory this recovers commuting broadcastable sets.

2. **Closest robust no-broadcasting statement.** D. Kretschmann, D. Schlingemann, R. F. Werner, “The Information-Disturbance Tradeoff and the Continuity of Stinespring’s Representation,” *IEEE Transactions on Information Theory* 54 (2008), [arXiv:quant-ph/0605009](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0605009), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2008.917696). Their Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 state, in Heisenberg notation, that if a full-domain 1→2 channel has one marginal within ε of identity in cb norm, the other marginal is within 2√ε in cb norm of a completely depolarizing/constant channel. The bound is dimension-independent. They also give a dimension-free near-identity comparison between ordinary and cb norms, with a fourth-root estimate (Eq. 7), and explicitly call Corollary 4 a continuity estimate for no-broadcasting.

   **Difference:** KSW assumes channel-level closeness over all inputs (and stabilized tests), while the candidate assumes statewise closeness only on E. KSW’s conclusion is that the complementary/other branch is nearly constant; it does not construct one EB map that approximates identity on an arbitrary promised set. KSW means one should not claim the first dimension-free approximate no-broadcasting bound, but it leaves the family-wise inverse/classicality question open in the scanned sources.

3. **Entropic pairwise bounds.** M. Lemm, M. M. Wilde, “Information-theoretic limitations on approximate quantum cloning and broadcasting,” *Physical Review A* 96 (2017), 012304, [arXiv:1608.07569](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07569), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.96.012304). Theorem 4 gives quantitative restrictions for two fixed mixed states when the same channel approximately broadcasts one and approximately clones the other; e.g. the relative-entropy loss must dominate an output trace-distance term, with a factor (n−1)/2 under Pinsker. Appendix G also gives recoverability-style tradeoffs for two-state approximate broadcasts. These are genuine robust no-cloning/broadcasting results, but the statement is pairwise and entropic, not a uniform EB approximation for a whole pure family independent of ensemble information.

   A more direct two-pure-state benchmark is A. Chefles, S. M. Barnett, “Strategies and Networks for State-Dependent Quantum Cloning,” *Physical Review A* 60 (1999), 136, [arXiv:quant-ph/9812035](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9812035), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.60.136). For two equiprobable pure states with overlap s=|⟨ψ|φ⟩|, the optimal 1→2 **global** cloning fidelity is F* = cos²((acos(s²)−acos(s))/2). From this one can derive a simple marginal-error floor: if both clone marginals are within half-trace error b of the input state, then for each input the joint fidelity with |ψψ⟩ or |φφ⟩ is at least 1−2b (a union bound on the two local projector failures), hence b ≥ (1−F*)/2 for 0<s<1. This is a derived consequence of the global-fidelity result, not a theorem stated in that paper; it is pairwise and does not supply an EB approximant for an arbitrary family. The lower bound vanishes as s→0 or s→1.


5. **Ensemble quantumness / Holevo loss.** M. Horodecki, P. Horodecki, R. Horodecki, M. Piani, “Quantumness of ensemble from no-broadcasting principle,” *International Journal of Quantum Information* 4 (2006), 105, [arXiv:quant-ph/0506174](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0506174), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0219749906001748). The Holevo-information difference between original and duplicated ensembles vanishes iff the states can be broadcast. D. Janzing, B. Steudel, “Quantum broadcasting problem in classical low-power signal processing,” *Physical Review A* 75 (2007), 022309, [arXiv:quant-ph/0609162](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0609162), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.75.022309), show that a noncommuting ensemble cannot reproduce its Holevo information on both outputs. Both are ensemble/prior-information frameworks; neither supplies the candidate’s dimension-/capacity-independent trace-error stability conclusion.

6. **Cloning versus state estimation and many outputs.** J. Bae, A. Acín, “Asymptotic Quantum Cloning Is State Estimation,” *Physical Review Letters* 97 (2006), 030402, [arXiv:quant-ph/0603078](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0603078), establish the asymptotic single-clone equivalence for pure-state cloning and state estimation. G. Chiribella, G. M. D’Ariano, “Quantum Information Becomes Classical When Distributed to Many Users,” *Physical Review Letters* 97 (2006), 250503, [arXiv:quant-ph/0608007](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0608007), give O(1/M) (more generally O(k/M)) measure-and-prepare approximation for symmetric M-user output marginals, with dimension-dependent constants. G. Chiribella, “On quantum estimation, quantum cloning and finite quantum de Finetti theorems,” [arXiv:1010.1875](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1875), Theorems 2.1–2.2, gives explicit symmetric-channel k-output bounds of order d²k/M (or 2dk/M under symmetric-subspace support). These are important antecedents for EB/state-estimation approximations, but their large-M, symmetry and dimension-dependent setting is materially different from a 1→2 family-wise theorem. They should not be presented as already proving the candidate.

7. **EB versus 2-broadcastability.** M. Horodecki, P. W. Shor, M. B. Ruskai, “General Entanglement Breaking Channels,” *Reviews in Mathematical Physics* 15 (2003), 629–641, [arXiv:quant-ph/0302031](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302031), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129055X03001709), establish the measure-and-prepare characterization of EB channels. A 1→2 extension only makes a marginal 2-self-compatible; it need not be EB (for example, the universal qubit 1→2 cloner’s single-copy marginal is not EB). EB corresponds to measure-and-prepare and arbitrarily shareable/infinite extensions; see also Doherty–Parrilo–Spedalieri, “Complete family of separability criteria,” *Physical Review A* 69 (2004), 022308, [arXiv:quant-ph/0308032](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0308032), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.69.022308), for the extension hierarchy. This distinction matters: the candidate’s EB conclusion is a substantive structural upgrade over mere existence of a two-output broadcaster.

## Schur-multiplier ingredient

The exact Boolean-symbol rigidity used in the proposed proof is established operator-theory, not itself new. L. Livshits, “A note on 0–1 Schur multipliers,” *Linear Algebra and its Applications* 222 (1995), 15–22, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(93)00268-5), studied norm gaps for 0–1 Schur multipliers. M. K. Goh, H. Hatami, “Block Complexity and Idempotent Schur Multipliers,” *International Mathematics Research Notices* (2025), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnaf356), states the exact characterization: Boolean symbols of Schur-multiplier norm at most 1 are blocky (unions of disjoint all-one rectangles); a forbidden 2×2 pattern with exactly three ones has norm 2/√3. The recent paper studies structure/complexity of exact Boolean idempotents with bounded norm, not stability of an almost-idempotent channel/multiplier under the candidate’s quantum hypotheses.

Thus the possible novelty is the quantitative passage from approximate broadcasting on an arbitrary pure family to a globally defined measurement-and-prepare approximant, using near-idempotence, the known exact Schur-multiplier gap, measurement construction and minimax—not the 0/1 gap alone.

## Scope cautions

- Do not describe the claim as “a 1→2 broadcast channel is EB”; false in general. The claim is instead that its uniformly well-reconstructed *pure-state promise family* is uniformly well approximated by one EB channel.
- Do not present KSW as a direct proof: its hypothesis is full-channel cb closeness to identity; its output is a constant other branch.
- Do not merge this with average-fidelity ensemble optimization, Holevo-information loss, or asymptotic state-estimation results; their objective/quantifiers differ.
- Do not use dimension-dependent finite de Finetti bounds or M→∞ results to claim the dimension-free 1→2 case is already covered.
- Do not extrapolate to mixed input families; the supplied dyadic copy-ladder counterexample blocks that generalization.
