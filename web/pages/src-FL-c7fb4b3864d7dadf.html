# FL physical pivot: molten-iron impurity extraction

**Status: NOT_ACHIEVED as a new physical operation.** The 2026 work establishes a promising, strongly element-dependent *screening result*, especially for apparent Sn concentration reduction. It does not establish a mass-balanced refining process, Fe yield, energy use, or scale throughput. The strongest physically coherent next mechanism is staged export and renewal of the product-bearing FeS slag (and/or captured off-gas), but this is a conventional process concept whose benefit has not been measured here. I do not claim it as an invention.

## What the primary evidence establishes

Ambiru et al. report in *Scientific Reports* (published 26 September 2026; [article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-73440-8)) that Sn was depleted from the metal across several conditions, with the abstract stating an apparent 95.0% removal; Ni removal was negligible, Cu depletion was limited in multicomponent tests, and +1 V did not improve the three single-element screens. The rows below transcribe Table 1 of the authors' v1 preprint ([preprint DOI](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9676758/v1)); the direct PDF returned 403, but the ResearchSquare index surfaced the table and methods text. The final-version Supplementary Information (SI) was directly retrieved and read as the 4-page PDF in `primary/ambiru2026-supplement.pdf`; the final article HTML available at retrieval contained the abstract but not the main text.

The experiment is a small, hot batch screen: **30 g** initial Fe-based alloy under about **60 g** of laboratory oxysulfide slag (52 wt% Al₂O₃, 24 CaO, 10 SiO₂, 9 MgO, 5 FeS), **1873 K**, a **7200 s** pre-electrolysis hold, then **3600 s** at open circuit or fixed potential, with gas flow set to **2 L min⁻¹**. The method excerpt reports an additional 45-minute crucible descent and a one-hour ramp from 1300 °C to 1600 °C before the hold and operation; the minimum stated hot-zone sequence is thus 4 h 45 min through the end of the one-hour operation, excluding cooldown and handling. This is an experimental cycle, not a scale throughput. The melt is the working electrode, a Mo rod is the quasi-reference, and a Mo plate is the counter electrode. Potential is reported versus Mo quasi-reference; integrated charge is reported, but full-cell voltage and electrical energy are not. The 60 g slag inventory contains about **3 g FeS**, or 2 kg slag and 0.1 kg FeS per kg of nominal metal charge. For the multicomponent starting assay of 0.202 wt% Sn, this is about **67 mol FeS per mole of initial Sn**. FeS is therefore not shown to be stoichiometrically limiting; the staged-refresh hypothesis concerns product activity/transport and product export, none of which was measured. These are initial batch inventories, not measured consumable rates.

At unchanged 1,000 kg metal mass, the Table 1 Sn changes correspond to 1.48, 1.84, and 1.89 kg/t-equivalent for runs 7, 9, and 11, respectively; the SI-mean equivalent for run 11 is 1.89 kg/t. The single-element screens make the potential comparison especially clear: Sn changed from 0.500 to 0.062 wt% without bias (87.6% apparent concentration decrease) and from 0.502 to 0.084 wt% at +1 V (83.3%); Ni changed from 0.501 to 0.462 wt% without bias (7.8%) and from 0.501 to 0.465 wt% at +1 V (7.2%). These are one screen per condition, not independent replicates. The multicomponent rows likewise do not isolate the effect of voltage: their alloy compositions, gas conditions, charges, and measured element responses differ. They show a useful element-dependent assay pattern, not a validated voltage-selectivity curve.

## Internal data reconciliation and unmeasured iron loss

The SI S4 caption says Table 1's after-values are the arithmetic means of its upper, middle, and lower regional ICP-AES measurements. The arithmetic check largely reproduces the accessible author-preprint table to its printed precision through run 14; small differences (at or below 0.001 wt%) occur in several rounded means. The clearer differences are run 9 Cu (preprint 0.465 wt%, SI mean 0.461 wt%) and all four analytes in runs 15–16. The latter two rows appear crossed between versions: the SI means for run 15 (Cu/Sn/Ni/Cr = 0.4803/0.024/0.212/0.087 wt%) are close to the preprint's run 16 values, while the SI means for run 16 (0.4607/0.054/0.1843/0.0783 wt%) are close to the preprint's run 15 values. This suggests a possible row-label/version discrepancy, but its cause is unknown. For the headline run 11, the preprint's 0.199→0.010 wt% yields 94.97%, and the SI's three Sn regions (0.020, 0.004, 0.005 wt%) average to 0.00967 wt%, or 95.14%. Both support an approximately 95% concentration decrease; the SI mean rounds to 95.1%, not 95.0%, a difference of about 0.17 percentage point. Thus the previously suspected large run-11 mismatch does not survive direct source alignment. The remaining differences are a limited cross-version table-reproducibility issue; they do not establish misconduct or invalidate the underlying electrochemical result. The full, machine-readable reconciliation is in `results.json`; source transcriptions are kept separately in `data/`.

The preprint methods say representative metal and slag subsamples were analyzed by ICP-AES. The SI gives regional *metal* assays and a metal-side activity-coefficient calculation that explicitly takes Fe as the balance; it states that calculation is not a metal–slag equilibrium or partition model. The methods describe slag analyses, but the accessible sources do not provide closed initial-to-final total metal, slag, or off-gas inventories. Accordingly:

- **Fe loss / Fe recovery: UNKNOWN.** Fe was not measured as a closed mass inventory. Concentration enrichment alone cannot distinguish transfer of Sn from addition/removal of Fe, nor quantify Fe co-transfer.
- **Product phase and selectivity: not closed.** Representative slag assays are described in the methods, but no accessible complete mass × composition closure identifies how much Sn, Cu, or Ni finished in slag, gas/condensate, crucible, or electrode products.
- **Independent replication and uncertainty: UNKNOWN.** Three regions from one solidified sample are spatial subsamples, not three independent furnace runs. No run-to-run variance or analytical uncertainty is supplied with these screening entries.
- **Energy and process rate: UNKNOWN.** The table supplies set potential versus a Mo quasi-reference and signed charge. It does not supply full cell voltage, ∫Vcell I dt, furnace energy, current density, scale throughput, or a continuous-product handling rate. A quasi-reference electrode potential is not a cell voltage.

The signed charge can be converted into a *conditional* Fe²⁺ or Sn²⁺ Faraday equivalent, (m=M|Q|/(2F)); for example, 19 C corresponds to 5.5 mg Fe or 11.7 mg Sn if every net coulomb represented only that one two-electron reaction. These are not measured Fe losses, Sn yields, or energy. Competing partial currents, chemical transfer, product deposition, and the missing full-cell voltage prevent that inference. On a constant 30 g metal-mass assumption, run 11's concentration differences would imply roughly 0.055–0.057 g Sn leaves the metal, several times the one-reaction 19 C Sn²⁺ equivalent; this only flags that concentration and net charge cannot be equated without a full reaction and inventory balance.

## Mechanistic candidate and thermodynamic limit

The SI's standard formation energies at 1873 K permit a useful *conditional* sulfide-exchange calculation:

\[
\mathrm{Sn_{metal}+FeS_{slag}\rightleftharpoons SnS_{product}+Fe_{metal}}.
\]

Using its tabulated ΔG° values, ΔG° = −61.338 − (−55.124) = **−6.214 kJ per mole of SnS**. At 1873 K, RT = 15.57 kJ mol⁻¹, so the ideal standard-state equilibrium quotient is \(K=\exp(-\Delta G^\circ/RT)=1.49\). This is a modest preference in the chosen standard states, not a predicted partition coefficient. The SI lists SnS with a gas standard state, while slag activities and the actual Sn-bearing product speciation were not established. The corresponding exchange estimates are −16.088 kJ per Cu₂S, −5.833 kJ per Ni₃S₂, and −41.599 kJ per CrS, also standard-state checks only.

For the Sn exchange, \(\Delta G=\Delta G^\circ+RT\ln Q\), with \(Q=a_{SnS}a_{Fe}/(a_{FeS}a_{Sn})\). As Sn is depleted and the product chemical potential rises, the forward driving force falls; at \(Q=K\) it vanishes and above that it reverses. A sustained separation therefore needs accounted work input, a continuing chemical-potential sink (for example, export/capture of the actual Sn-bearing product), or both. Voltage can supply work and alter interfacial kinetics; the law does **not** imply that voltage cannot improve separation. A zero-mean or periodic waveform is not automatically work-free: the relevant electrical work is \(\int V_{WE-CE}I\,dt\), and it could drive net conversion if coupled to the reaction. The precise limitation is that a kinetic waveform, without a product sink or net work and without changing composition, cannot change the equilibrium condition fixed by \(Q=K\); in a closed system it can at most affect the path, rate, or transient state. The 2026 study uses constant potential, not pulses, and does not report full-cell work or a matched energy comparison. No unconditional no-pulsing theorem is claimed.

The physically coherent candidate worth testing is **staged removal of the product-bearing phase with FeS-slag renewal**, with the outgoing Sn-bearing phase identified and captured before it can return to the metal. If the carrier is volatile SnS, the outlet gas must be sampled and trapped; if it remains in slag, the slag must be bled and its Sn inventory recovered or safely processed. The published experiment already uses 2 L/min gas, so a gas-side sink may already operate; the data do not show which phase currently carries the Sn. This proposed staging/bleed is an ordinary extraction-process operation, not claimed as novel, and its benefit remains unmeasured. It addresses a plausible product-loading limit, not the weak Ni transfer or limited Cu removal.

## Prior art and the complete process-level comparator

Electrochemical Cu/C removal from molten iron is already published prior art. Paeng, Judge, and Azimi's 2024 study reports reducing Cu from 0.43 wt% to 790 ppmw (0.079 wt%) with an oxysulfide electrolyte while also removing carbon ([*Resources, Conservation and Recycling* 206, 107654](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2024.107654)). The later Paeng–Azimi 2025 techno-economic study targets HRC at 710 ppmw Cu, <10 ppmw S, and 197 ppmw C from 100% scrap feeds at 0.43 or 0.21 wt% Cu ([article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.4c02665); local 11-page supporting PDF: `primary/paeng2025-tea-si.pdf`). Its SI gives four one-tonne-basis scenarios, material/energy flows, OPEX, and plant CAPEX. The four case identifiers are preserved as the SI labels; the accessible SI alone does not define the main-text scenario names.

The SI also lists 1,440–1,940 kg of retained flux in cases using it, 60–480 kg of flux residue, and 40.8–56.0 kg of coal or CaC₂ in cases that use a reductant/desulfurizer. Case 4's negative OPEX is after a modeled credit for newly produced Fe; it is not a negative total plant cost. The SI's 2024 unit prices include calcium-aluminate flux $200/t, pyrite $250/t, Fe₂O₃ $104/t, CaC₂ $650/t, ZrB₂ electrode $0.13/g, and waste disposal $24/t. Plant component capital costs include $1,584.33/t capacity for the electrorefining cell, $96.88/t desulfurization, $278.52/t EAF, $273.30/t casting, and $246.53/t hot-strip mill. The article reports levelized HRC costs of $941.73/t for 0.43 wt% Cu scrap and $783.24/t for 0.21 wt% Cu scrap; at then-current HRC price its NPV/IRR are −$5.67m/4.9% and $225m/9.8%, respectively, with electrolyte and scrap costs significant. This is a modeled Cu/C industrial comparator, not a measured energy cost for the 2026 Sn/FeS screen. It is the strongest process-level baseline retrieved here; it already includes product quality, electricity, flux, coproduct Fe credits, OPEX, CAPEX, and an economic result.

The table transcribes the SI's reported case streams and totals; it is not an independent process simulation, nor an elemental closure by this audit. The comparator supplies a much more complete modeled energy/material/economic accounting than the 2026 batch screen, while remaining a modeled Cu/C route with its own assumptions.

## Decisive matched experiment

First decide whether product-phase renewal, rather than applied potential, is what extends Sn extraction. Use the same 30 g nominal Fe–0.5 wt% Cu–0.2 wt% Sn–0.2 wt% Ni–0.2 wt% Cr charge and 60 g total slag at 1873 K, the same 7200 s equilibration, 2 L/min gas, and 3600 s total contact time. Compare a single 60 g static slag contact with two sequential 30 g fresh-slag contacts (each 5 wt% FeS; 1.5 g FeS per stage), removing and retaining the first spent slag after 1800 s. Total slag and FeS input, temperature-time, and total contact time are then matched; confirm the half-charge slag still covers the metal and preserves the same exposed interface. Run both arms at open circuit first, with randomized order and at least three independent charges per arm. If slag renewal passes, repeat as a 2×2 with a constant-potential arm based on the published −1 V screen; measure actual WE–CE voltage and full electrical energy. Do not infer pulse benefit from this constant-potential comparison.

For every run, weigh and assay the initial/final metal, both slag outputs, crucible wash, electrode deposits, and a captured gas/condensate train for total Fe, Sn, Cu, Ni, and S. Identify the Sn-bearing phase/species; report analytical limits and recovery uncertainty. Measure full-cell \(\int V_{WE-CE}I\,dt\), furnace energy, gas and slag use, processing time, and product throughput. The candidate survives only if a larger **closed** Sn inventory reaches captured products at the same Fe recovery and energy/material budget than in the static baseline; if staged refresh only changes concentration while the missing Fe/product balance persists, or gives no matched advantage, reject the mechanism. The comparison can establish this bounded operating effect but would not establish novelty or an industrial process on its own.

## Scope and disposition

I audited the 2026 metal/slag opportunity, reconciled the reachable primary data, derived a standard-state thermodynamic check, and retrieved a complete existing Cu/C mass-energy and TEA baseline. I did not establish a novel extraction cycle, a Fe-loss advantage, energy efficiency, industrial throughput, or economic improvement. The strongest next evidence is the matched staged-slag mass-closure experiment above. Preserve **UNKNOWN** for Fe loss, product partition, complete cell/furnace energy, process costs of the Sn route, and novelty of a staged slag-export variant.
