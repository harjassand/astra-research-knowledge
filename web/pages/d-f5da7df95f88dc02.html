# Distribution shift: what must be acquired for target-risk control

## Result at a glance

Let P be the source law and Q the target law on representation-label pairs (Z,Y), with every predictor restricted to the acquired representation Z = φ(X). For any loss in [0,1], the sharp distribution-free transfer statement is

> sup_h |R_Q(h) − R_P(h)| ≤ TV(P,Q), and
> R_Q(hat h_P) − inf_h R_Q(h) ≤ R_P(hat h_P) − inf_h R_P(h) + min{2 TV(P,Q), 1}.

The 2 TV shift term is sharp as a supremum; the cap at 1 is sharp for 0–1 loss. Under covariate shift on the acquired representation, P(Y|Z) = Q(Y|Z), joint TV equals marginal TV. Ordinary source ERM therefore transfers when the two representation marginals are close. Importance-weighted ERM removes this population bias if the target/source ratio is known, but pays a finite-sample variance cost governed by E_P[w²] = 1 + χ²(Q_Z || P_Z) and requires target support to be covered by source support. Neither a domain-invariant representation nor unlabeled target covariates identifies a changed label conditional.

## Theorem 1: sharp TV transfer for source ERM

Assume ℓ(h(z),y) ∈ [0,1] and define R_D(h) = E_D[ℓ(h(Z),Y)]. Let δ = TV(P,Q) = sup_A |P(A) − Q(A)|. For any measurable f ∈ [0,1], the variational characterization of TV gives |E_Q f − E_P f| ≤ δ. Applying this to each loss yields sup_h |R_Q(h) − R_P(h)| ≤ δ.

For a source-risk minimizer h*_P, a target-risk minimizer h*_Q, and any output hat h, decompose:

> R_Q(hat h) − R_Q(h*_Q)
> = [R_Q(hat h) − R_P(hat h)] + [R_P(hat h) − R_P(h*_Q)] + [R_P(h*_Q) − R_Q(h*_Q)]
> ≤ R_P(hat h) − R_P(h*_P) + 2δ.

Since excess risk is at most 1, the transfer term can be written min{2δ,1}. For a finite class of size M, if hat h_n is empirical source ERM on n iid source examples and t = log(2M/α), Hoeffding plus a union bound gives, with probability at least 1−α,

> R_Q(hat h_n) − inf_h R_Q(h) ≤ min{1, 2√(t/(2n)) + 2δ}.

**Sharpness construction.** Take Z ∈ {0,1}, constant label Y=0, 0–1 loss, and H = {h₀(z)=z, h₁(z)=1−z}. Let P(Z=1)=p<1/2 and Q(Z=1)=p+δ. Then h₀ is the unique source minimizer and TV(P,Q)=δ. When p+δ>1/2, its target excess is 2(p+δ)−1. Taking p up to 1/2 gives 2δ for δ≤1/2. For δ>1/2, taking p=1−δ gives target excess 1. Thus the worst-case transfer term is exactly min{2δ,1} as a supremum. The one-predictor risk bound δ is attained by an indicator loss on a TV-maximizing event.

Under covariate shift P(Y|Z)=Q(Y|Z), the joint TV equals TV(P_Z,Q_Z), because the common conditional kernel factors out of the total-variation integral. Therefore ordinary source ERM's distribution-shift penalty is controlled by source/target feature-marginal TV. This is a useful certificate only when that TV is bounded or estimated with a valid guarantee.

## Theorem 2: weighted ERM under acquired covariate sufficiency and overlap

Assume predictors have the form g∘φ, and the following are justified properties of the acquired representation and the source/target data:

1. **Shared label-sufficient representation:** evaluated predictors factor through Z=φ(X), and there is one conditional law η(·|z) with P(Y|Z)=Q(Y|Z)=η on both domains.
2. **Target support covered by source:** Q_Z is absolutely continuous with respect to P_Z, with known density ratio w(z)=dQ_Z/dP_Z(z) and 0≤w≤B<∞.
3. **Finite predictor class and bounded loss:** M=|H_φ|<∞ and 0≤ℓ≤1.

Then R_Q(h)=E_P[w(Z)ℓ(h(Z),Y)] for every h∈H_φ. Define weighted ERM by minimizing

> Rhat_w(h) = (1/n) Σᵢ w(Zᵢ)ℓ(h(Zᵢ),Yᵢ), where (Zᵢ,Yᵢ) are iid from P.

Let κ = E_P[w²] = 1 + χ²(Q_Z || P_Z) and t = log(2M/α). Since 0≤wℓ≤B and Var_P(wℓ)≤E_P[w²]=κ, a two-sided Bernstein bound and union bound give, with probability at least 1−α,

> R_Q(hat h_w) − inf_h R_Q(h) ≤ min{1, 2√(2κt/n) + 4Bt/(3n)}.

These are conservative two-sided Bernstein constants. The explicit divergence dependence has leading order √((1+χ²(Q_Z||P_Z)) log(M/α)/n); B controls the tail. In effective-sample-size terms, n/κ is the relevant order for the leading term. This is an oracle-ratio theorem: exact w, the representation, the shared conditional, and overlap are assumptions, not discoveries supplied by the theorem.

If a fixed estimated ratio w̃, independent of the labeled sample, obeys 0≤w̃≤B̃, define b=E_P|w̃−w| and κ̃=E_P[w̃²]. The same proof gives target excess at most

> 2b + 2√(2κ̃t/n) + 4B̃t/(3n),

capped at 1. The 2b term is the price of ratio estimation. Independence can be obtained by estimating weights from separate unlabeled samples or by a valid sample-splitting/cross-fitting argument; same-sample dependence needs its own analysis.

## Why support and conditional invariance are load-bearing

**Uncovered target mass.** Let A have target mass Q_Z(A)=ρ but source mass P_Z(A)=0. Consider two worlds that agree on all source observations and all target covariates but give opposite deterministic labels on A. In each world, the label conditional can be shared by source and target; it is simply unobserved under the source on A. No algorithm receiving source labels and unlabeled target covariates can distinguish the worlds. For any randomized prediction rule, its worst-case expected target error on A is at least ρ/2; for a deterministic rule it is at least ρ in one of the worlds. This is an information obstruction, not a weakness of a particular density-ratio estimator. Target-labeled examples or a separately justified label rule on A are needed to remove it.

**Invariant features with changed labels.** Let Z be uniform on {0,1} in both domains, so P_Z=Q_Z, w=1, and the representation is perfectly domain-invariant. Let source labels be Y=1−Z and target labels be Y=Z. Source ERM in H={z,1−z} chooses 1−z, which has target error 1. Thus exact marginal invariance does not imply shared label sufficiency. This example deliberately violates covariate shift.

The positive theorem consequently requires both an acquired label-sufficient representation and an acquired overlap/ratio fact. A representation found only by matching unlabeled domain marginals is not, by itself, either fact.

## ERM, weighting, and matched same-source controls

For a finite class and the same n labeled source examples, ordinary ERM has high-probability bound 2√(t/(2n))+2δ, while oracle importance-weighted ERM has bound 2√(2κt/n)+4Bt/(3n). The first pays a shift-bias term and has lower sampling variance; the second removes covariate-shift bias but may have poor effective sample size when target density concentrates on a small source region. When shifts are mild, unweighted ERM can be better. When the density ratio is large or support is missing, the weighted guarantee degrades or disappears. Estimated weights add their own error term.

For a controlled empirical comparison, freeze one source-labeled sample and the representation, class, and optimizer, then fit (i) source ERM, (ii) oracle-ratio weighted ERM as a diagnostic ceiling, and (iii) estimated-ratio weighted ERM. Evaluate all three on the same held-out target labels. This paired same-source comparison holds label volume fixed and isolates the weighting operation. Report δ, ratio-estimation error where certifiable, maximum weight, second moment/effective sample size, and target risk. A cold target-only learner is a separate information control and must receive an explicitly matched target-label budget. The oracle-ratio arm is not deployable unless true ratio access is part of the interface.

The theorem treats φ and ratio access as assumptions. Any claim that the system acquired them must separately state the observations, learning algorithm, validation or certification procedure, and computation/data cost. No empirical experiment was run for this note.

## Primary-source literature check

- Ben-David, Lu, Luu, and Pál, [“Impossibility Theorems for Domain Adaptation,” AISTATS 2010](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v9/david10a.html). In the unlabeled-target setting, they prove that covariate shift alone does not ensure learnability and that reweighting can help one task while harming an observationally indistinguishable task. This supports the conditional/support caveat; it does not replace the explicit TV or finite-class bounds proved above.
- Ben-David et al., [“A Theory of Learning from Different Domains,” *Machine Learning* 79 (2010)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-009-5152-4). Their target-error bound uses source error, a classifier-induced domain divergence, and the existence of a classifier that performs well on both domains. This is a close established analogue to the shared-target-competence condition; the current note uses TV and common conditionals for an elementary sharp bound.
- Shimodaira, [“Improving predictive inference under covariate shift by weighting the log-likelihood function,” *Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference* 90 (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-3758(00)00115-4). This primary covariate-shift weighting paper derives weighted predictive densities and identifies the target/source covariate density ratio as asymptotically optimal under model misspecification.
- Cortes, Mohri, Riley, and Rostamizadeh, [“Sample Selection Bias Correction Theory,” ALT 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2775). They analyze how finite-sample error in estimated importance weights affects returned hypotheses. The b=E_P|w̃−w| term above is a direct loss-level perturbation; their stability-based results provide a more algorithm-specific analysis.
