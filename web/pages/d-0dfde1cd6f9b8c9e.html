## Exact result and scope

Let `f=P(x,y,z)+w Q(x,y)`, with P,Q complex polynomials and nonzero constant
Hessian determinant c. Then exactly the following structural alternative
is sufficient:

* If `P_zz=0`, write `P=R(x,y)+z S(x,y)`. The map `(S,Q)` is a plane Keller
  map, and the four-variable gradient is polynomially invertible or
  noninjective exactly when that plane map is. This is a reduction, not a
  resolution of the plane problem.
* If `P_zz` is not the zero polynomial, Q is affine-linear, and the entire
  four-variable gradient has a two-sided polynomial inverse. Its recovery
  is uniform in the other source coordinate, not merely pointwise.

The binary result passes without a new external geometry theorem. Using
Lin2609.06465v1 Theorem1, whose proof is independently reconstructed with
named established geometric prerequisites below, every complex
`f=P(x,y,z)+wQ(x,y,z)` with constant nonzero Hessian reduces to the binary
interface by a constant linear change in x,y,z. Thus a single zero constant
linear-direction pivot returns to the plane Keller lift or an invertible
case, in every polynomial degree. This conditional consequence does not
close unrestricted HC4 or the singleton-zero plane subclass.

## Fresh determinant and the binary curvature condition

In the order x,y,z,w, direct expansion of the free symmetric matrix gives

`det Hess f=(Qx Pyz-Qy Pxz)^2`
` -Pzz[Qy² Pxx-2QxQy Pxy+Qx² Pyy+w K_Q]`,

`K_Q=Qy² Qxx-2QxQy Qxy+Qx² Qyy`.

All signs agree with the root's formula. The coefficient of w is
`-Pzz K_Q`, so the polynomial domain forces `Pzz=0` or `K_Q=0`. A point
where grad Q=0 would make the last Hessian row zero, impossible. Therefore
Q is nonconstant and has no critical points on C².

Suppose `K_Q=0`. Let `D=Qy d/dx-Qx d/dy`. Wherever Qy is nonzero,

`D(Qx/Qy)=K_Q/Qy²=0`.

Take an irreducible component C of a fibre Q=c. The absence of critical
points makes it a smooth affine curve. D induces a nonzero derivation on
its function field. The constants of such a derivation are C: any
nonconstant element h makes the curve field finite separable over C(h),
so Dh=0 would make the derivation zero on a separable finite extension,
a contradiction. Hence the rational slope Qx/Qy is constant on C when Qy
does not vanish identically. The function `s x+y` for that constant slope s
also has D derivative zero, so C lies on an affine line. If Qy vanishes
identically on C, then Qx is nonzero there and Dy=-Qx; equivalently Dx=0,
so x is constant on C and C is a vertical line. A one-dimensional closed
irreducible subset of either affine line is the whole line.

Lines from different fibre values cannot meet. Over C any two nonparallel
affine lines do meet, so all the components outside one fixed fibre share
one direction v. Their union is a dense open subset of the source. Thus
`D_v Q=0` as a polynomial identity. Constant linear coordinates give
`Q=H(ell)`. Every value of the nonconstant affine coordinate ell occurs,
and grad Q is nowhere zero; a nonlinear H would have a root of H' over C.
Therefore H' is a nonzero constant and Q is affine-linear.

The global polynomial assumption is essential. On the chart y!=0 the
rational function Q=x/y satisfies `K_Q=0` and has no constant translation
direction. Its underlying fibre lines meet at a removed point. A rational
chart cannot be substituted for the globally polynomial hypothesis.

## Polynomial inverse in the affine-Q branch

Use a constant affine coordinate change in x,y to make `Q=x+gamma`.
The determinant identity reduces to

`det Hess_(y,z) P=-c`.

Over C(x), de Bondt1203.6605v3 Theorem2.1 gives a polynomial inverse of
`(P_y,P_z)` in y,z. Its Jacobian determinant is the unit -c of C[x]. The
formal-inverse coefficient descent proved in cycle12 puts that inverse in
C[x][Y_y,Y_z]. Explicit recovery is

`x=Y_w-gamma`, `(y,z)=H(x,Y_y,Y_z)`, `w=Y_x-P_x(x,y,z)`.

It is polynomial in all four target coordinates and is two-sided. The
named prior HC2 theorem is used with exactly the fraction-field scope
verified in the cycle12 primary snapshot. No arbitrary specialization or
pointwise inverse is being promoted to a uniform inverse.

The same argument is genuinely uniform over an arbitrary characteristic-
zero coefficient domain R when the source determinant c is a unit of R.
If the generic branch has Pzz!=0, the geometry over the algebraic closure
of Frac(R) forces Q affine; its nonlinear coefficients, already in R,
therefore vanish identically. Write `Q=alpha x+beta y+gamma` over R.
The last Hessian row is `(alpha,beta,0,0)`. Multiply by the w-column of
its polynomial inverse and evaluate all source variables at0 to obtain
`alpha r+beta s=1` with r,s in R. Hence

`[[alpha,beta],[-s,r]]` has determinant1 over R.

This gives a global parameter normalization `Q=X+gamma`; no chart division
by alpha or beta is necessary. HC2 over Frac(R[X]) and coefficient descent
to R[X] give the inverse above over R. Specializing Pzz to zero does not
break this inverse. The unit assumption is load-bearing: determinants
vanishing under a specialization are excluded; neither L=0 nor N=0 in a
particular Schur family is silently retained if that destroys the pivot or
the reduced Hessian determinant.

## The plane lift branch and its unresolved interface

When Pzz=0, polynomiality gives P=R+zS, and

`det Hess f=(Qx Sy-Qy Sx)^2`.

Thus `G=(S,Q)` has nonzero constant Jacobian. In base/dual blocks the
gradient is `(grad R+DG^T(z,w),G)`. A G inverse recovers the base first and
then the two dual variables using the inverse Jacobian. Conversely, equal-G
points give equal-gradient points by independently solving the dual linear
system at each base; a gradient inverse supplies a G right inverse, which
is dominant and therefore two-sided. This reconstructs the exact global
collision/invertibility equivalence.

No singleton-zero conclusion is added unless the candidate has the
component-ideal identity from the earlier coefficient class. Nothing here
proves every plane Keller map invertible. The original common-form family
with degree(Q)<=2 is Ni's prior Proposition6.1; the arbitrary-degree binary
coefficient classification is a broader programme deduction, with unknown
historical priority.

## Recent Lin theorem: independent proof gate

Xiaojin Lin, [*When is a polynomial in three variables cylindrical?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06465v1),
v1 submitted 2026-09-06 08:25:55UTC, Theorem1, claims for every nonconstant
Q in C[x,y,z], with no critical-locus condition,

`U(Q)=gradQ^T adj(HessQ) gradQ=0`
`  iff some nonzero constant v has D_v Q=0`.

I read the full primary TeX and HTML, and reconstructed its complete main
proof. The two old prerequisites are explicitly retained, rather than
called independently reproved here:

1. Bertini--Krull/indecomposable-spectrum finiteness: an indecomposable
   complex polynomial has irreducible Q-c for all but finitely many c.
   The quantitative bound is unnecessary to this application.
2. Piontkowski's rank-one affine cylinder theorem. The saved author
   preprint dated2002-06-27, *Developable varieties with all singularities
   at infinity*, p7 Corollary5 and its immediately preceding paragraph,
   says an affinely smooth developable variety of Gauss rank1 is a cone
   with its (d-1)-dimensional vertex at infinity. In P³, d=1 gives a point
   vertex. The definition of affine smoothness on p1 is exactly that all
   projective singularities lie in the chosen hyperplane. These locations
   were checked in text and rendered pages. Lin cites the journal paper
   Manuscripta Math106(2001)75--99; our2002 author snapshot is recorded as
   its own version. The full older moving-frame theorem is not reproved.

The independent reconstruction is as follows. With `Q=g(h)` and h
indecomposable, row operations on the bordered Hessian give
`U(Q)=(g'(h))^4 U(h)`. This identity is valid even for singular Hessians
and zeros of g': prove over g'(h)!=0 and extend as a polynomial identity.
Choose a smooth irreducible fibre h=c, avoiding the finite exceptional
sets. At a regular point, transform the tangent plane to the first two
coordinate axes; the bordered expression is the square of the nonzero
normal derivative times the determinant of the tangent Hessian block.
Thus U(h)=0 makes the projective Gauss rank at most1. This uses no Euclidean
metric or nonisotropic-normal assumption.

If the Gauss rank is0, the closure is a plane. If it is1, its projective
closure has all singularities at infinity and the verified old rank-one
theorem gives an affine cylinder. Its constant direction v makes D_v h
vanish on that fibre. Since h-c is irreducible and reduced,
`h-c` divides `D_v h`; degree(D_v h)<degree(h-c) forces D_v h=0 globally.
Then D_v Q=g'(h)D_v h=0. The reverse direction follows from HessQ v=0
and v.gradQ=0, including ranks<=1 where the adjugate is zero.

No defect was found. Gate status is PASS_WITH_NAMED_ESTABLISHED_PREREQUISITES.
For our HC4 application, gradQ is nowhere zero, making Q automatically
indecomposable: a nonlinear outer g has a derivative root, and any
nonconstant polynomial h hits that value by restriction to a suitable
affine line. Hence Q itself has a good irreducible fibre, and no
decomposition or singular-special-fibre exception remains.

In `f=P(x,y,z)+w Q(x,y,z)`, the free determinant is
`-gradQ^T adj(HessP+w HessQ) gradQ`.
Its leading w coefficient has degree2, **not3**, and is `-U(Q)`. Constant
determinant forces U(Q)=0, so the verified Lin gate reduces Q to two
variables and the binary result applies. The same transfer to any
characteristic-zero field follows by embedding the finitely generated
coefficient field in C and taking the kernel of the finite constant-
direction coefficient matrix back over that field. A uniform integral
axis over a coefficient domain is not claimed from this rank argument.

Lin's sharpness example `G4=x²y+xz+t` has U=0, gradient component1 in t,
and no nonzero translation direction. These exact claims were rebuilt;
thus no dimension4 cylinder theorem is inferred from Lin's dimension3
statement.

## Consequence for the peer's nonlinear triangular shear

After the prior U completion and residual normalizer, the peer studies
`L t²+a(x,y)t+N(y rho+2x eta)` and a triangular polynomial shear of rho
depending nonlinearly on eta. Whenever its exact critical elimination gives

It therefore has affine Q=Ny. If N!=0 and the resulting Hessian determinant
is a nonzero constant, the inverse branch above applies for arbitrary
degree of the remaining polynomial in x,y,eta. A global collision cannot
survive under those hypotheses. This is stronger than testing particular
shear degrees and also covers a polynomial critical solution without an
initial globally constant t-pivot. The legality and polynomiality of a
proposed critical elimination still have to be established separately.
