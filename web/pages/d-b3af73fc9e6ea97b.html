FAST-MIXING TWO-STATE HMM ACQUISITION FROM RAW LENGTH-THREE STRINGS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally reconstructed finite-sample theorem for a supplied
two-state family. Exact checks and Monte Carlo are scoped diagnostics. No
novelty or priority claim.

RESULT

For a stationary two-hidden-state binary HMM with both transition persistence
and emission contrast unknown, length-three raw strings suffice for a
matching parametric minimax rate. On the explicit fixed-margin family below,
uniform epsilon-accurate acquisition of the parameter pair from N iid raw
strings has

  N*(epsilon,delta) = Theta(epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta))

for 0<epsilon<=1/24 and 0<delta<=1/4. The upper bound is a two-moment,
observable/spectral estimator. The lower bound applies to every estimator
seeing all raw strings and follows from a pair of nearby HMMs. The constants
are not matched; a fully explicit upper constant is given. This is a
fixed-margin theorem, not a sharp characterization as the margins vanish.

The learner is told the two-state symmetric HMM form, binary alphabets,
stationary reset protocol, parameter rectangle, and margin values. It is not
told the transition or emission parameters and sees no hidden states. The
number of states is supplied as exactly two; no claim is made about learning
state count. This leaf uses iid raw trajectories, not scalar function
queries or intervention/query access.

1. MODEL CLASS AND OBSERVATION INTERFACE

Let hidden states S_t and observations Y_t lie in {-1,+1}. The unknown
parameters are theta,lambda in Theta=[1/2,2/3]^2. The hidden chain is
stationary with pi=(1/2,1/2) and transition matrix

  T_lambda = (1/2) [[1+lambda, 1-lambda], [1-lambda, 1+lambda]].

Conditional on the hidden path, emissions are independent with

  P(Y_t=S_t | S_t) = (1+theta)/2,
  P(Y_t=-S_t | S_t) = (1-theta)/2.

Equivalently, Y_t=S_t E_t for iid signs E_t with E[E_t]=theta. With rows
indexed by state and columns by observation, the emission matrix is

  O_theta = (1/2) [[1+theta,1-theta], [1-theta,1+theta]].

The corresponding observable operators may be written A_y=D_y T_lambda,
where D_y=diag((1+theta*y)/2,(1-theta*y)/2), so

  P_theta,lambda(y_1...y_m)
    = pi^T A_(y_1)...A_(y_m) 1.

Thus this is a rank-two observable-operator family with two unknown real
parameters, not a supplied numerical realization. The sign convention
theta>0 fixes the hidden-label orientation; the learner's target is the
observable parameter pair, which is invariant to renaming the latent states.

At each training episode the oracle independently draws S_1 from pi,
generates the HMM, and returns exactly the three raw observation bits
Y_1Y_2Y_3. It returns N iid such strings. Hidden states and parameters are
never returned. Every episode uses one stationary start/reset. There are no
query coordinates, labels, conditionals, counterexamples, or matrix
responses. The sampling/learning criterion is

  inf_estimator sup_(theta,lambda in Theta)
     P_theta,lambda{ max(|theta_hat-theta|,|lambda_hat-lambda|)>epsilon }
       <= delta.

This is parameter acquisition. The parameter pair determines the full
observable process and its predictive distributions.

2. ALL MARGINS, MIXING, AND WITNESS HORIZON

The transition flip probability is q=(1-lambda)/2 in [1/6,1/4]. Every
transition entry is at least 1/6, T_lambda is full rank, and its smallest
singular value is lambda>=1/2. The emission matrix has smallest singular
value theta>=1/2; the two emission rows have total-variation separation
theta>=1/2, and every emission probability is at least 1/6. The stationary
mass of each state is 1/2. Hence the hidden rank is exactly two and neither
state nor emission separation is approaching zero.

The hidden-chain nontrivial eigenvalue is lambda<=2/3. From either starting
state, total variation distance to pi after t transitions is lambda^t/2,
so the 1/4 mixing time is at most two transitions. The oracle supplies iid
stationary episodes; no burn-in or dependence correction is charged. The
mixing bound is still explicit, and this proof does not cover a single
uninterrupted stream.

The witness horizon is exactly three observations. For any k>=0,

  E[Y_t Y_(t+k)] = theta^2 lambda^k,

because E[Y_t|S_t]=theta*S_t and E[S_t S_(t+k)]=lambda^k. In particular,

  mu_1 = E[Y_1Y_2] = theta^2 lambda,
  mu_2 = E[Y_1Y_3] = theta^2 lambda^2.

The length-three law is

  P(y_1,y_2,y_3) = (1/8)[1 + mu_1(y_1y_2+y_2y_3)+mu_2 y_1y_3].

There are no odd moments by global sign symmetry. Thus the two moments
identify the length-three law and parameters:

  lambda = mu_2/mu_1,       theta = mu_1/sqrt(mu_2).

Length two does not suffice. For example,

  (theta_a,lambda_a)=(11/20,3/5),
  (theta_b,lambda_b)=(3/5,121/240)

both lie in Theta and have the same length-two correlation
mu_1=363/2000. Their lag-two correlations are 1089/10000 and 14641/160000,
respectively. Therefore their length-two raw-string laws are identical,
while a length-three string separates them. Three is the shortest uniform
witness horizon for the two unknown parameters in this family.

The minimum probability of any one length-three word is bounded below by
1/(2*6^5), using one fixed hidden path, so the full eight-outcome likelihood
has full support uniformly on Theta. Exact string probabilities are
computable from the displayed formula.

3. UPPER BOUND: A RAW-STRING MOMENT/SPECTRAL ESTIMATOR

For episode i, form U_i=Y_(i,1)Y_(i,2) and V_i=Y_(i,1)Y_(i,3), and set

  mu_1_hat = N^(-1) sum_i U_i,
  mu_2_hat = N^(-1) sum_i V_i.

Both sequences are iid across episodes and take values in {-1,+1}. Although
U_i and V_i within the same episode need not be independent, separate
Hoeffding bounds and a union bound suffice. Put a=1/2 and u=1/2 for the
lower margins theta>=a and lambda>=u. Then mu_1>=a^2 u=1/8 and
mu_2>=a^2 u^2=1/16. If both moment errors are at most alpha<=1/32, then
mu_1_hat>=mu_1/2 and mu_2_hat>=mu_2/2. Define the raw moment inverses

  lambda_tilde = mu_2_hat/mu_1_hat,
  theta_tilde = mu_1_hat/sqrt(mu_2_hat),

when denominators are positive, and use the center of Theta otherwise.
Project both estimates coordinatewise onto [1/2,2/3]. On the good moment
event, direct ratio bounds give

  |lambda_tilde-lambda|
    <= alpha (mu_1+mu_2)/(mu_1*mu_1_hat)
    <= 4 alpha/(1/8)^2 = 256 alpha.

For theta, split the error into numerator and reciprocal-square-root terms.
Since mu_2_hat>=1/32 and mu_2>=1/16,

  |theta_tilde-theta|
    <= alpha/sqrt(mu_2_hat)
       + mu_1 |1/sqrt(mu_2_hat)-1/sqrt(mu_2)|
    <= (4 sqrt(2) + 16 sqrt(32)) alpha < 100 alpha.

Thus both coordinate errors are at most 256 alpha. Set alpha=epsilon/512,
so the statistical estimate is within epsilon/2. Round each coordinate to
a dyadic grid of spacing epsilon/2; this adds at most epsilon/4. The returned
finite-bit estimate is within epsilon. For epsilon<=1/24, alpha<=1/32.

Hoeffding and a union bound give

  P(max_j |mu_j_hat-mu_j|>alpha) <= 4 exp(-N alpha^2/2).

Therefore

  N >= ceil(524288 epsilon^(-2) ln(4/delta))

suffices. The constant is conservative and comes from a simple inverse
Lipschitz bound; it is not asserted optimal. For a general fixed rectangle
theta in [a,b], lambda in [u,v] with 0<a<=b<1 and 0<u<=v<1, the same proof
works with

  C = max{4/(a^4 u^2), 3/(a^3 u^3)},
  N >= ceil(8 C^2 epsilon^(-2) ln(4/delta)),

provided epsilon is small enough that alpha=epsilon/(2C)<=a^2u^2/2.
These explicit inverse-margin factors display the loss as emission contrast
a or persistence u approaches zero. The stated theorem instantiates
a=u=1/2, b=v=2/3.

4. MATCHING RAW-DATA LOWER BOUND

Take lambda=1/2 in both hypotheses and

  H0: theta_0=1/2,
  H1: theta_1=1/2+3 epsilon.

For epsilon<=1/24 both parameters lie in Theta and are separated by
3epsilon. Any estimator accurate within epsilon under both hypotheses
induces a test with both errors at most delta (choose the nearer parameter).

For a single episode, first expose the hidden path S_1:S_3 as extra data.
Its distribution is the same under H0 and H1. Given that path, the three
emissions are independent Bernoulli variables, with success parameters
(1+theta)/2 or their complements. Data processing removes the hidden path,
and KL is bounded by three Bernoulli chi-square divergences:

  KL(P_H0^(3) || P_H1^(3))
    <= 3 (theta_1-theta_0)^2/(1-theta_1^2)
    <= (243/5) epsilon^2.

Here D(Ber(p)||Ber(q))<= (p-q)^2/[q(1-q)] and
1-theta_1^2>=1-(2/3)^2=5/9. For N iid raw strings, KL is at most
(243/5)N epsilon^2. A delta-correct binary test requires KL at least

  kl_delta = kl(1-delta || delta)
           = (1-2delta) ln((1-delta)/delta).

Consequently

  N >= [5 kl_delta/(243 epsilon^2)].

For 0<delta<=1/4, kl_delta is comparable to ln(1/delta). This lower bound
uses the complete raw length-three observations and unrestricted computation;
no estimator, including a full likelihood or spectral estimator, can improve
the epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta) order. Together with the upper bound it proves
the claimed matching minimax rate for the fixed margins. The constants are
not matched, and the explicit upper margin dependence is not claimed sharp.

5. MARGINS ARE LOAD-BEARING

If the emission-separation margin is removed and the state count is not
supplied, a two-state model can approach a one-state observable process.
At theta=0 the emissions are iid fair signs regardless of lambda, so the
observable process has rank one. For theta>0 and lambda>0, its lag-one
covariance theta^2 lambda is nonzero and the predictive process has rank
two. Relative to the iid fair length-three law P_0, the exact chi-square
divergence is

  chi^2(P_(theta,lambda) || P_0)
      = theta^4 (2 lambda^2 + lambda^4) <= 3 theta^4.

Hence N raw triples have KL at most 3N theta^4. For every fixed N, choosing
theta small makes rank-one versus rank-two selection impossible at any fixed
confidence. Similarly, if the transition singular-value margin is removed,
lambda=0 gives iid fair observations, while lambda>0 gives rank two, and
chi-square divergence to the iid law is at most 3 theta^4 lambda^2. These
are margin-collapse counterexamples, not members of the stated rectangle.
They show that the state count is not uniformly selectable without an
observable separation condition. The theorem supplies state count two and
the positive margins rather than claiming to acquire either.

Mixing is a separate axis: this rectangle has q>=1/6 and
tau_mix(1/4)<=2, avoiding slow-switching regimes. Because each training
string is independently reset to stationarity, the proof's sample rate has
no extra mixing-time factor. For a single stream, initial burn-in, dependence,
and episode segmentation costs would need a separate analysis; they are not
silently treated as iid here.

6. INFORMATION AND COMPUTATION COSTS

Each observation is one binary symbol. The complete acquired data cost is
exactly 3N raw bits, plus N stationary episode starts/resets. There are no
query-coordinate bits and no access to conditional probabilities. For the
explicit upper bound this is at most

  1,572,864 epsilon^(-2) ln(4/delta) + 3 bits

up to the ceiling convention. The two returned dyadic parameters need
2 ceil(log2(ceil(2/epsilon)+1)) bits, plus a fixed model-family identifier; output
rounding is already covered by the epsilon/2 statistical target. The family
description and margin constants are supplied and are not counted as
acquired information.

The moment estimator scans each episode once, uses O(N) arithmetic
operations, and stores two integer sums and a counter, requiring
O(log N + log(1/epsilon)) working bits. The final ratio and square root can
be evaluated to the output precision in polynomial time in
log N+log(1/epsilon). No hidden path or latent-state posterior is computed.

The strongest equally informed statistical comparator retains the full raw
data. Its likelihood is

  L(theta,lambda) = product_(i=1)^N P_theta,lambda(Y_(i,1:3)),

with the eight exact string probabilities in Section 2. The eight-symbol
histogram is sufficient for this likelihood. A full-likelihood MLE may
improve constants, but the two-moment estimator already matches its minimax
sample order, and the lower bound applies to it. A global-optimization
runtime bound for the MLE is not claimed. The two-moment method is also a
small spectral/method-of-moments learner: it extracts two observable lag
correlations and inverts them. It does not receive hidden-state labels or a
supplied numeric operator realization.

7. PRIMARY LITERATURE AND CLAIM STATUS

Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang give a spectral HMM learner under singular-value
separation assumptions and use short raw observation windows to recover
observable operators. This model has supplied state count two and explicit
singular-value lower bounds s_min(T)>=1/2 and s_min(O)>=1/2; the report's
estimator is the direct two-correlation specialization, not a reconstruction
of their general algorithm. See [“A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden
Markov Models,” JCSS 78 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2011.12.025)
and its [author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413).

Gassiat, Cleynen, and Robin prove population identifiability for finite-state
HMMs under full-rank transitions and linearly independent emission laws,
up to state permutation. Our two matrices are full rank, and the positive
theta convention fixes that label ambiguity. Population identifiability
alone is not the finite-sample rate proved here. See [their primary paper,
“Finite state space non parametric Hidden Markov Models are in general
identifiable”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657).

Baum and Petrie's classic work develops statistical inference for finite
state Markov functions and provides likelihood-estimation context; our KL
lower bound and moment upper bound are proved directly. See [“Statistical
Inference for Probabilistic Functions of Finite State Markov Chains,”
Annals of Mathematical Statistics 37 (1966)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177699147).

The source pass was targeted, not exhaustive. No novelty or priority claim
is made. The theorem is exact only for this known two-state symmetric family,
stationary iid length-three observation interface, fixed margins, and
parameter sup-norm loss. It does not establish unknown-state-count learning,
arbitrary HMM acquisition, one-stream rates, intervention advantages, or
general representation discovery. Independent mathematical review remains
open.
