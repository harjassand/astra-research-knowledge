CYCLE 3 RESULT: RANK-FREE FINITE-H PREDICTIVE RISK FROM ONE HMM PATH
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: conditional finite-sample theorem and nonergodic impossibility; no novelty or priority claim

CLAIM

From one stationary raw path, a finite-cover conditional-likelihood estimator
has a high-probability oracle inequality for expected finite-H predictive KL,
without estimating Hankel rank or using a singular-value gap. The guarantee
needs a known Doeblin minorization and a known positive emission floor. The
estimator is explicit but its exhaustive parameter-net search is exponential
in the supplied state cap. A saturated empirical conditional table gives a
same-block baseline. Without mixing, a two-state stationary counterexample
prevents uniform recovery of even the empty-history one-step law from a single
path, no matter how long that path is.

This is not a theorem about arbitrary stationary HMMs, prediction from an
infinite history, efficient global HMM maximum likelihood, parameter
identification, or a minimal semantic realization.

1. OBSERVATION INTERFACE AND LOSS

The input is one raw sequence X_1,...,X_T over a supplied finite alphabet
Sigma of size q>=2 (q=1 is trivial). The true HMM is stationary. No hidden state, parameter,
probability query, reset, characteristic-event dictionary, or selected-string
oracle is available. Fix context length h>=0 and future horizon H>=1, and set
L=h+H. For a stationary HMM Q, let q_Q(w) be its stationary probability of
the word w. On a length-L observed block define

  ell_Q(x_1:L) = -log q_Q(x_(h+1):L | x_1:h).

The target predictive risk is

  E_H^h(P,Q) = E_(X_1:L~P) [ KL( P(X_(h+1):L | X_1:h)
                                  || Q(X_(h+1):L | X_1:h) ) ].

It is the expected conditional log-loss of Q minus the true conditional
entropy. The expectation is over a stationary length-h observed history;
the theorem does not take h to infinity.

2. CLASS PROMISES AND THEOREM

For each hidden-state count d, define Theta_d(rho,e) as stationary d-state
HMMs with transition matrix A and emission probabilities E satisfying

  A_ij >= rho/d for every i,j,   0<rho<1,
  E_i(a) >= e for every state i and symbol a,   0<e<=1/q.

The known class constants rho and e may be supplied as rational lower bounds.
The truth is promised to lie in some Theta_d(rho,e). With a supplied cap D,
the allowed class is the union over 1<=d<=D. The minorization A_ij>=rho/d
gives A=rho U+(1-rho)R, where U has uniform rows and R is stochastic.
Therefore one transition contracts hidden-state total variation by at most
1-rho. It also gives a unique stationary distribution with pi_j>=rho/d.
The emission floor implies every candidate future string has conditional
probability at least e^H, so

  0 <= ell_Q <= M_H := H log(1/e).

No rank, observability, singular-value gap, emission-row independence, or
filter-stability premise is used.

Theorem 1 (known cap, finite-H conditional-KL oracle inequality). Choose
cover tolerance 0<tau<=1 and confidence 0<delta<1. For each d<=D construct
the rational finite net C_d(tau) below, let

  N_D(tau) = sum_(d=1)^D |C_d(tau)|,
  c_D = M_H sqrt( log(4 N_D(tau)/delta) / (2B) ),

and split the one path into B length-L blocks separated by g discarded
symbols. Set

  g = ceil( rho^(-1) log(2(B-1)/delta) )       when B>=2,
  g = 0                                        when B=1,
  T_req = B L + (B-1)g.

Fit the net model minimizing the empirical conditional negative log
likelihood on these B blocks. If the log-loss objective is evaluated with
uniform additive error at most zeta per candidate, the selected HMM Qhat
satisfies, with probability at least 1-delta,

  E_H^h(P,Qhat) <= inf_(d<=D,Q in Theta_d(rho,e)) E_H^h(P,Q)
                    + tau + 2 c_D + 2 zeta.                   (1)

If the true process is in the class, the oracle term is zero. Thus (1) is an
absolute finite-H predictive-KL bound as well as an oracle inequality. The
selected state count need not be the true count or a minimal realization.
The executable rule is: form every grid C_d(tau), compute each candidate's
stationary vector by a rational linear solve, score the observed blocks by
the forward recursion, and return a minimum-score candidate (fixed
lexicographic tie-break). This enumerates model parameters only; it does
not enumerate possible length-L words or query their probabilities.

3. CONSTRUCTIVE FINITE COVER

For each transition row in dimension d, write

  A_i = (rho/d) 1_d + (1-rho) r_i,  r_i in Delta_(d-1).

For each emission row, write

  E_i = e 1_q + (1-qe) s_i,  s_i in Delta_(q-1).

Replace each residual simplex by all denominator-K compositions. Largest-
remainder rounding approximates every simplex point in entrywise
absolute error at most 1/K, while every grid row remains stochastic and
obeys its lower floor. With the same K for transition and emission rows,
the net size is

  N_d(K) = binom(K+d-1,d-1)^d binom(K+q-1,q-1)^d.           (2)

Factors with zero-dimensional residual simplexes are 1.

Here is the log-likelihood continuity calculation that sets K. Let two
same-d models in the class differ entrywise by at most a=1/K in A and E.
Their stationary laws obey

  ||pi-pi'||_TV <= max_i ||A_i-A'_i||_TV / rho
                 <= d a/(2 rho),
  ||pi-pi'||_1 <= d a/rho,
  pi_j >= rho/d.

The first inequality follows by inserting pi A' between pi A and pi' A'
and using the Doeblin contraction of A'. Hence a stationary-coordinate
relative change is at most d^2 a/rho^2; a transition-entry relative
change is at most d a/rho; and an emission-entry relative change is at
most a/e. Define phi(u)=-log(1-u). For u<1, an entry with relative
change at most u has absolute log-ratio at most phi(u). A hidden-path
likelihood term of length s has one stationary factor, s-1 transition
factors, and s emission factors. Comparing termwise and summing positive
terms gives for every word w of length s

  |log q_Q(w)-log q_Q'(w)| <= Lambda_s,
  Lambda_s = phi(d^2/(K rho^2))
            + (s-1) phi(d/(K rho))
            + s phi(1/(K e)).

Take Lambda_0=0. A conditional log probability is a length-L joint log
probability minus a length-h context log probability. Since h<=L and all
three terms in Lambda_s are nonnegative, its pointwise change is at most
2 Lambda_L.

It is sufficient to choose the integer K=K_d(tau,L) at least

  max { d^2 / [rho^2 (1-exp(-tau/6))],
        d / [rho (1-exp(-tau/[6(L-1)]))],
        1 / [e (1-exp(-tau/[6L]))] },                       (3)

omitting the middle term when L=1. Then Lambda_L<=tau/2, so every
conditional block loss differs by at most tau between a model and its net
representative. Equations (2)-(3) give a finite explicit entropy bound;
for d,q>1,

  log N_d <= d(d-1) log( exp(1)(K_d+d-1)/(d-1) )
             + d(q-1) log( exp(1)(K_d+q-1)/(q-1) ),          (4)

with zero-dimensional terms omitted. The elementary bound
  log N_d <= [d(d-1)+d(q-1)] * (1+log(K_d+d+q))
is a safe convenient looser version; the additive 1 absorbs the factor e
in the standard binomial bound, including small K_d,d,q.

For rational 0<tau<=1, a simpler all-rational sufficient resolution is

  K_d = ceil max { 12 d^2/(rho^2 tau),
                   12 d(L-1)/(rho tau),
                   12 L/(e tau) },

again omitting the middle term when L=1. This follows from
1-exp(-x)>=x/2 for 0<=x<=1 and makes the grid construction directly
effective from rational class constants.

4. PROOF OF THEOREM 1

Use block starts 1, 1+(L+g), ..., 1+(B-1)(L+g). The input path need only
have length T_req. The first block has its stationary law. Conditional on
any previous selected blocks, the hidden-state posterior at the end of
the last selected block is some distribution nu. The g unused observations
are not used or conditioned on; marginalizing them propagates nu through
g+1 hidden transitions before the next selected block. Thus the next
block's hidden start law is within

  (1-rho)^(g+1) <= exp(-rho g)

of stationarity. Passing through the length-L emission/transition channel
cannot increase total variation. Couple each next block maximally to an
independent stationary block. A union bound gives a coupling of the B
selected blocks with B iid stationary blocks whose mismatch probability is
at most (B-1) exp(-rho g) <= delta/2. This derives and charges the
one-path dependence cost directly; no resets are being treated as free.

For iid blocks, every candidate loss lies in [0,M_H]. Hoeffding plus a
union bound over N_D net models gives, with failure probability at most
delta/2,

  sup_(Q in union C_d) | Rhat(Q)-R(Q) | <= c_D.

On the good coupling event, the actual selected blocks equal the coupled
iid blocks, so the same empirical-risk event applies to the estimator on
the one path. For any Q in any continuous Theta_d, the cover provides Q'
with pointwise conditional loss error <=tau, hence R(Q')<=R(Q)+tau. ERM
then gives

  R(Qhat) <= Rhat(Qhat)+c_D
            <= Rhat(Q')+c_D+2zeta
            <= R(Q')+2c_D+2zeta.

Take the infimum over continuous Q. Subtracting the common true conditional
entropy E_P[-log P(X_(h+1):L | X_1:h)] from both sides yields (1). The
two failure probabilities (sampling and coupling) sum to delta.

For target statistical slack epsilon_stat, it suffices to take

  B >= 2 M_H^2 log(4 N_D(tau)/delta) / epsilon_stat^2,

which gives risk at most oracle + tau + epsilon_stat + 2zeta. The one-path
symbol budget is T_req above, or T_req*ceil(log2 q) raw symbol bits. The
gaps themselves count as observed symbols.

5. UNKNOWN STATE COUNT

The supplied-cap theorem does not need to infer the rank. If no state cap
is supplied but the same common rho,e promises are known, one can use
structural risk minimization over all d>=1. Build C_d with K_d from (3),
set delta_d=delta*2^(-d-1), and define

  c_d = M_H sqrt( log(2 |C_d| / delta_d) / (2B) ).

Select Qhat from the union of nets minimizing Rhat(Q)+c_d. The confidence
allocations sum to delta/2, while the coupling uses delta/2. Simultaneous
Hoeffding bounds and the same penalized-ERM argument give

  E_H^h(P,Qhat) <= inf_(d>=1,Q in Theta_d(rho,e))
                    { E_H^h(P,Q) + tau + 2c_d + 2zeta }.     (5)

The search is finite for every B. For d>=2, K_d>=1 implies
|C_d|>=d^d, so c_d >= M_H sqrt(d log(d)/(2B)) -> infinity. A one-state
candidate has objective at most M_H+c_1 because every empirical loss is
at most M_H. Therefore any d with c_d>M_H+c_1 cannot minimize, and this
lower bound gives a computable finite cutoff. This result adapts over
state count but assumes common known mixing/emission floors. If those
floors are unavailable, it is not an adaptation theorem for arbitrary
stationary HMMs.

6. SAME-DATA SATURATED EMPIRICAL BASELINE

The same B blocks support a model-free conditional table. There are
K_ctx=q^h contexts and S=q^H future strings. For each context x, allow any
row g(.|x) with every future-string probability at least u=eta/S, for a
chosen 0<eta<1. Parameterize a row as u 1_S +(1-eta)r and put a
denominator-K0 composition grid on r. The table net has

  N_sat = binom(K0+S-1,S-1)^(K_ctx)

entries. Taking K0 >= 1/[u(1-exp(-tau_sat))] makes every table conditional
log loss within tau_sat of its net representative. The loss range is
M_sat=log(S/eta). ERM on the same blocks therefore has, with probability
at least 1-delta,

  E_H^h(P,ghat) <= -log(1-eta) + tau_sat
                    + 2 M_sat sqrt(log(4 N_sat/delta)/(2B)).

The comparator term follows by mixing the true conditional row with eta
times uniform: g=(1-eta)P(.|x)+eta Uniform has floor eta/S and
KL(P(.|x)||g(.|x))<=-log(1-eta). This is a genuinely same-data, saturated
empirical predictor; it needs no HMM model. Its table has q^h rows and q^H
outcomes per row, compared with O(d^2+dq) HMM parameters. Which wins
depends on h,H, state cap, and sample size. The saturated table can be a
better statistical choice for short contexts/horizons; its size grows
exponentially in them.

The raw path itself can be retained in T_req*ceil(log2 q) bits and used
for every overlapping context, or passed to a global full-path HMM
likelihood fit. Those methods see at least as much data as the gapped-block
estimators. The present concentration proof uses separated blocks and
does not prove that the overlapping-window table or full-path MLE is
inferior. The retained path's storage is linear in T; the fitted HMM
output is a compressed predictive descriptor. It is not shown minimal.

7. NONMIXING IMPOSSIBILITY: STATIONARITY ALONE IS NOT ENOUGH

Take two hidden states C in {0,1}, transition matrix I, stationary weights
(1/2,1/2), and observed alphabet {(c,y): c,y in {0,1}}. In hidden state c,
each time emits marker c and a bit Y~Bernoulli(theta_c), independently
over time. Compare

  M0: (theta_0,theta_1)=(1/2,1/4),
  M1: (theta_0,theta_1)=(1/2,3/4).

With probability 1/2 a path remains forever in hidden class 0. Conditional
on this event, the full raw training sequence has exactly the same law
under M0 and M1, even if its length is infinite. But the stationary
empty-history next-symbol laws P0 and P1 have total variation 1/4: the
class-1 mass is 1/2 and its two Bernoulli laws have TV 1/2. For any
prediction Q, Pinsker and the triangle inequality imply

  KL(P0||Q)+KL(P1||Q)
    >= 2[TV(P0,Q)^2+TV(P1,Q)^2]
    >= TV(P0,P1)^2 = 1/16.

On the indistinguishable class-0 path event, average over the two target
models and multiply by event probability 1/2. Thus every estimator from a
single path, including a full-path MLE, obeys

  max_(i=0,1) E_Mi KL(P_i||Qhat) >= 1/64 nats,

for any path length. This counterexample has a stationary HMM with two
states and no mixing. Its target is the allowed finite-H risk with h=0,
H=1. It does not prove impossibility when the risk conditions on an
infinite observed past; the target here deliberately does not.

8. COMPUTATION, PRECISION, AND MODEL OUTPUT

For supplied cap D, exhaustive enumeration uses
N_D=sum_d N_d(K_d) rational models. A forward pass evaluates one length-L
block likelihood in O(L d^2) arithmetic operations; computing its context
and future conditional uses the same forward pass and normalization at h.
The total arithmetic count is

  O( B L sum_(d=1)^D N_d(K_d) d^2 ).

This is finite and globally optimized over the cover, but exponential in
the parameter dimension; no polynomial-time claim is made. The common
forward algorithm evaluates the raw path/model likelihood without
enumerating the q^L possible words.

For rational rho,e, grid entries have bit height O(log K_d+log d+bits(rho,e)).
The stationary vector of each positive rational transition matrix is a
unique rational solution of a d-dimensional linear system; exact
elimination has bit complexity polynomial in d and the matrix-entry bit
height. A length-L exact forward probability has bit height polynomial
in L,d and those row bit lengths. Certified logarithms to per-block
additive error zeta require O(log(1/zeta)+log M_H) working precision
bits, in addition to the rational forward arithmetic. Approximate ERM
adds 2zeta in (1)/(5). The returned ordinary HMM stores O(d^2+dq)
rational parameters and stationary weights. This is a predictive model,
not a claim that hidden states have been identified or that the output is
a minimal semantic realization.

The estimator's observed data charge is one path of T_req symbols, not B
free independent resets. Its fitting rule discards the gaps only to get a
transparent independent-block comparison. A more efficient use of all
overlapping windows could improve the sample cost, but requires another
concentration proof.

9. COMPARISON WITH FULL-PATH LIKELIHOOD AND SPECTRAL METHODS

The full-path HMM MLE evaluates
  sum_(t=1)^T log q_theta(X_t | X_1:(t-1))
    = log q_theta(X_1:T)
by the forward algorithm. It retains all observations and may be
statistically more efficient. The theorem
above instead minimizes conditional finite-H log loss over many spaced
windows, matching the stated risk directly. The two objectives differ;
no conclusion that our estimator beats the full-path MLE follows. The
finite-net estimator pays an explicit exponential search cost, whereas a
continuous global MLE also has a nonconvex optimization problem. Baum,
Petrie, Soules, and Weiss (1970) is the primary likelihood-maximization
reference; it establishes an iterative maximization principle, not the
global finite-sample predictive-KL guarantee claimed here.

The strongest directly relevant raw-data spectral comparison found is
Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang (COLT 2009; JCSS 2012). Their learner estimates an
observable representation from raw triples, and their Theorem 7 controls
expected conditional one-step KL at every history under positive-operator
and rank conditions. Their sample bound depends polynomially on inverse
singular values of the observation and pair-probability matrices and on
their contraction quantities. The paper's algorithm takes hidden count m
as an input, assumes positive initial mass and full-column-rank transition
and emission matrices, and uses SVD. Its analysis samples independent
triples; the paper says a long stream can use a mixing-discounted effective
sample size, but does not prove the single-path block bound derived here.
More exactly, their Theorem 7 has an N bound of the form

  C [ m n_0(eps)/(eps^2 sigma_m(O)^2 sigma_m(P_(2,1))^2)
      + m/(sigma_m(O)^2 sigma_m(P_(2,1))^4)
        * ( m/(eps^2 alpha^2)
            + log(2/alpha)^4/(eps^4 alpha^4 gamma^4)
            + log(2/alpha)^4/(alpha^10 gamma^4) ) ] log(1/delta),

for their independent triple sample, where alpha lower-bounds entries of
their positive operators, gamma=inf_(||v||_1=1)||Ov||_1, and n_0(eps)
is their effective alphabet-size term. This is a stronger all-history
prediction result with polynomial algebra, but under observability and
stability margins that this theorem avoids.

The Doeblin/emission floors used here are not singular-value assumptions:
they ensure path mixing, bounded log loss, and a finite likelihood cover.
They do not imply distinct emission rows or minimality. Consequently,
rank-free prediction can hold while hidden parameterizations are
nonidentifiable.

Terwijn (ICGI 2002) proves a cryptographic-conditional computational
hardness result for general finite-length HMM learning. The reduction uses
acyclic HMMs and permits zero-probability outputs; it is outside this
positive, uniformly Doeblin, stationary class. It warns against calling
the finite-net procedure an efficient general HMM learner, but it does
not refute this restricted finite-H risk theorem.

Yu (Annals of Probability 1994) develops empirical-process bounds for
stationary beta-mixing sequences via coupling to independent blocks. Here
the coupling is reproved under the explicit Doeblin constant, with the
block gap, trajectory length, finite cover, loss bound, and precision
cost all displayed.

10. CLAIM LEDGER AND UNKNOWN

PROVED HERE (conditional on stated class promise): finite-H conditional-KL
oracle inequality from one stationary raw path; explicit rational
finite-cover entropy; an explicit dependence gap and raw-symbol charge;
state-count selection with penalties when common rho,e are known; and a
same-data saturated-table bound. No step computes exact Hankel rank.

COUNTEREXAMPLE: without a mixing guarantee, stationarity and a supplied
state cap do not permit uniform single-path recovery of the stationary
empty-history one-step law; the two-state lower bound remains positive as
path length tends to infinity.

NOT ESTABLISHED: polynomial-time estimation; adaptation when rho or e are
unknown and have no supplied positive lower bounds; infinite-history
prediction without filter stability; a full-path MLE bound under this
predictive loss; optimal dependence on B, h, H, d, or q; minimal or
identifiable hidden parameters; or a general arbitrary-HMM theorem.

UNKNOWN / EXACT GAPS

1. Can a polynomial-time optimizer achieve an entropy-scale version of
   (1) under the same floors? Not answered; enumeration is exponential in
   d^2+dq.
2. Can the emission floor be removed using a support-adaptive loss while
   retaining a finite expected KL bound? Not answered; the present proof
   uses Q(y|x)>=e^H to bound log loss.
3. Does full-path joint MLE, or an overlapping-window conditional MLE,
   satisfy a sharper finite-H predictive-KL oracle inequality under the
   same Doeblin promise? Not proved.
4. Can unknown rho,e be learned or penalized adaptively without losing
   the path-mixing and likelihood-cover guarantees? Not proved.
5. Can one extend this finite-context result to infinite-past stationary
   predictive KL without a filter-stability premise? Not proved.
6. The literature review below is targeted rather than exhaustive; no
   novelty or priority claim is supported.
