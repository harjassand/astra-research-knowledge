# Cycle 2: robust extinction-risk control and its observation gate

## Outcome

The global attack produced a valid conditional certificate and an exact no-go for whole-tumor claims without spatial coverage. It did not produce a new cancer-control principle. A common positive weight controls the expected abundance of a covered, finite set of transient phenotypes under every treatment matrix in a simultaneous rate region. Escape and external-source terms can then be bounded over a declared finite horizon. This is standard positive switched-system/branching machinery assembled into a data-to-decision interface.

The decisive biological-translation obstruction is that a finite accessible biopsy/plasma history does not bound an unobserved, zero-shedding refuge or a future source whose rate can change after observation. In that admissible class, an empty tumor and one with arbitrarily many resistant founders have identical observed data. Thus an unconditional whole-tumor risk certificate is impossible; return UNKNOWN unless coverage, shedding, and future-source assumptions have empirical lower/upper bounds.

The first-wave work is summarized in [FINAL_REPORT.md](FINAL_REPORT.md). Detailed derivations and independent attacks are linked below.

## Conditional robust branching theorem

Let \(X(t)\in\mathbb N^m\) count a prespecified list of at-risk transient phenotypes/genotypes. In a pulse action \(a\), a type-\(i\) cell produces an event with offspring vector \(y\) at per-cell rate \(\lambda^a_{i,y}\). Its count increment is \(y-e_i\), and the mean generator matrix \(A_a\) is Metzler. Let \(\eta(t)\ge0\) be external immigration into these tracked states. The CTMC generator is

$$
\mathcal L_{a,t}f(x)=
\sum_i x_i\sum_y\lambda^a_{i,y}[f(x+y-e_i)-f(x)]
\quad+\sum_j\eta_j(t)[f(x+e_j)-f(x)].
$$

Suppose the full unknown-rate vector belongs to a simultaneous confidence set \(\mathcal C_\alpha\) with coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), and find a single \(w\gg0,\delta>0\) such that, for every allowed action \(a\) and every rate vector in \(\mathcal C_\alpha\),

$$
w^\top A_a\le-\delta w^\top
\quad\text{componentwise},\qquad
w^\top\eta(t)\le h(t).
$$

Set \(V(x)=w^\top x\), \(V_0=w^\top X(0)\), and

$$
B(t)=e^{-\delta t}V_0+
\int_0^t e^{-\delta(t-s)}h(s)\,ds.
$$

Then

$$
\mathbb E[V(X(t))]\le B(t).
$$

**Proof.** For every state and every allowed realized action, the generator inequality gives
\(\mathcal L_{a,t}V(x)=w^\top A_ax+w^\top\eta(t)\le-\delta V(x)+h(t)\).
Dynkin’s formula yields the corresponding scalar differential inequality for expected \(V\); multiplying by \(e^{\delta t}\) and integrating gives the display. The pointwise condition makes the argument valid even if treatment is selected from the observed state, provided every realized matrix remains in the covered set. It is not necessary that \(\mathbb E[A(t)X(t)]=\mathbb E[A(t)]\mathbb E[X(t)]\).

For \(h=0\), \(V(X(t))\ge w_{\min}\) whenever \(X(t)\ne0\), so
\(\Pr(X(t)\ne0)\le V_0e^{-\delta t}/w_{\min}\). Zero is absorbing, and this bound tends to zero; extinction occurs almost surely from every finite initial state. This is a sufficient bound, not an exact extinction probability. If no common \(w,\delta\) exists, the test is inconclusive; the biological process may still be safe.

For a finite action family and a polytopal rate set, robust linear inequalities can be checked at vertices. For each candidate \(\delta\), search for normalized \(w\) by linear feasibility; bisection finds the largest certified margin. With a rectangular rate set, each signed coefficient selects its adverse endpoint. Separate unadjusted confidence intervals do not preserve coverage after selecting a favorable \(w\), action, or duration; build one simultaneous region first.

## From expected burden to a relapse-risk bound

At pulse end \(T\), let \(\bar q_i^+\) bound the post-pulse establishment probability of each residual type \(i\), and set
\[
C_R=\max_i\bar q_i^+/w_i.
\]
Under the independent branching continuation model, a union bound gives residual-founder risk at most \(C_RB(T)\). More generally, the same term is valid if each cell’s conditional establishment probability is bounded by \(\bar q_i^+\) under every allowed environment.

The continuation probability \(\bar q_i^+\) must be computed for the full post-pulse multitype process, including any post-pulse mutation or switching routes that can create a resistant lineage. If those routes are excluded from \(\bar q_i^+\), they need their own source term over the follow-up window, using an upper envelope for the post-pulse at-risk counts and the corresponding time-to-go establishment cap.

Now suppose a controlled type \(i\) produces a pulse-time escape founder of type \(j\) at rate at most \(\bar\kappa_{ji}(s)X_i(s)\). A founder born at pulse time \(s\) experiences the remaining schedule, so let \(\bar r_j(s;T)\) bound its probability of causing the declared endpoint after the remaining pulse and maintenance. Define

$$
C_E(s;T)=\max_i
\frac{\sum_j\bar\kappa_{ji}(s)\bar r_j(s;T)}{w_i}.
$$

Then pulse-time escape risk is at most
\(\int_0^T C_E(s;T)B(s)\,ds\).
For an external pulse source with founder intensity \(\bar\iota_j^{\rm pulse}(s)\), add
\(\int_0^T\sum_j\bar\iota_j^{\rm pulse}(s)\bar r_j(s;T)\,ds\).
For post-pulse external sources over a finite declared follow-up \([T,T+H]\), add
\(\int_T^{T+H}\sum_j\bar\iota_j^{\rm post}(s)\bar q_j^+\,ds\).
Together,

$$
\Pr(\text{endpoint by }T+H)\le
C_RB(T)+\int_0^T C_E(s;T)B(s)\,ds+
J_{\rm pulse}(T)+J_{\rm post}(T,H).
$$

The union bound does not require independent escape routes. Do not count a mutant daughter twice: either include it in the controlled mean generator or remove that daughter from \(A\) and count it once in the escape source. If the escape type is untracked or its source/success probability has no finite upper bound, report UNKNOWN.

The time-to-go probability is load-bearing. For a time-homogeneous pulse, let \(z_i(u)\) be the probability of no establishment when one type-\(i\) founder receives \(u\) more pulse time and then maintenance. It solves the backward PGF system

$$
\frac{dz_i}{du}=
\sum_y\lambda_{i,y}
\left(\prod_k z_k^{y_k}-z_i\right),
\qquad z_i(0)=1-\bar q_i^+.
$$

The escape-fate cap is \(\bar r_i(s;T)=1-z_i(T-s)\), maximized over the joint confidence set and allowed remaining schedule. For a time-varying schedule, compose the corresponding PGF flows backward across the remaining phases. Substituting post-pulse \(\bar q_i^+\) for \(\bar r_i(s;T)\) is safe only if the pulse does not affect that escape lineage; otherwise it can materially understate risk. Setting \(\bar r=1\) is safe but can be useless.

### Closed form for a restricted fixed-dose envelope

When \(h=0\), \(B(t)=V_0e^{-\delta t}\), \(C_E\) is a constant, escape fate is duration-independent, and a separate successful-source hazard is bounded by \(c\), the scalar envelope is

$$
U(T)=a e^{-\delta T}+\frac b\delta(1-e^{-\delta T})+cT,
$$

where \(a=C_RV_0\) and \(b=C_EV_0\). Its exact derivative is
\[
U'(T)=(b-\delta a)e^{-\delta T}+c.
\]
On \([0,T_{\max}]\), it is non-increasing exactly when \(\delta a\ge b\) and \(c\le(\delta a-b)e^{-\delta T_{\max}}\). On all \(T\ge0\), this requires \(c=0,\delta a\ge b\). If \(c>0\), longer pulses eventually increase this envelope. With \(c=0\), the smallest certified duration for \(b/\delta<\epsilon<a\) is
\[
T_\epsilon=\delta^{-1}\log\frac{a-b/\delta}{\epsilon-b/\delta}.
\]
No finite duration reaches a target at or below \(b/\delta\). This is a bound’s minimum, not a claim that nature’s actual relapse risk is monotone. If the probability that a new escape founder survives the remaining pulse depends on \(T-s\), use the full integral bound and optimize numerically over the permitted set.

For a pulse that also immigrates controlled types at constant weighted rate \(h\), the more general risk expression and first-crossing/Lambert-W cases are recorded in [cycle2_branch_certificate.md](cycle2_branch_certificate.md). Stable resistance with nonnegative net growth cannot be put under a strict all-type common contraction: its own matrix column requires \(g_Gw_G\le-\delta w_G\). Keep it as a time-to-go-priced escape route or explicitly model its pulse dynamics. An asymptote of an upper-bound formula is not a lower bound on true risk; a true positive floor requires a protected source with a duration-independent success chance.

## Finite-data acquisition interface

For a fully observed typed lineage history, let \(N_e(t)\) count events of type \(e\) and \(A_i(t)=\int_0^tX_i(s)\,ds\) be cell-time exposure. Under a constant per-cell hazard \(\theta_e\), the path likelihood is proportional to
\[
\theta_e^{N_e(t)}\exp[-\theta_eA_i(t)].
\]
For genuinely fixed/exogenous exposure, a zero-event one-sided upper limit is \(-\log(\alpha)/A_i\). In a branching process, however, cell-time exposure is often random and generated by the same events; automatic use of an exact Poisson/Garwood interval is not justified. A likelihood-ratio confidence sequence is valid under the predictable-intensity model: for fixed \(\eta<0\),
\[
U_t(\alpha;\eta)=
\frac{\log(1/\alpha)-\eta N_e(t)}
{(1-e^\eta)A_i(t)}.
\]
Mixture or Bonferroni-combined boundaries give simultaneous, anytime-valid rate regions; event-detection sensitivity \(\rho\) divides observed-rate bounds by \(\rho\). Missing/dead lineage branches, state-dependent censoring, unknown confusion matrices, or culture frailty require an explicit observation/random-effects model.

Direct single-cell limiting-dilution assays give binomial bounds for establishment probabilities. A representative hidden-clone sample gives an exact binomial/hypergeometric prevalence interval after detection sensitivity is calibrated. To claim an assay-derived risk with coverage \(1-\alpha\), form one joint \(\mathcal C_\alpha\) for action-specific birth/death/switch rates, clone counts, mutation/immigration, pulse-time success, residual establishment, assay sensitivity, and spatial sampling. Then optimize over the entire region and report \(\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha}U(T;\theta)\). Optimizing weights or schedules after observing data does not lose the \(1-\alpha\) coverage if the same simultaneous region covers every input.

Data costs are severe for rare events. With 24 one-sided rate channels and a familywise 5% error allocation, observing zero events requires \(A\ge\log(24/.05)/\theta^\star\) valid exposure per channel to certify hazard below \(\theta^\star\). This is 6,174 cell-days per channel at \(10^{-3}\)/day and 61,738 at \(10^{-4}\)/day, before culture-level variation. If a million independently sampled cells from a \(10^9\)-cell tumor produce zero resistance calls at 95% sensitivity 1, the ideal uniform-sampling prevalence limit is still about \(3.0\times10^{-6}\), permitting roughly 3,000 hidden founders. With per-founder establishment \(q=.01\), the chance of at least one establishment is then effectively one. This sampling calculation does not address an unsampled refuge.

## Exact spatial observation no-go

Take two admissible worlds with the same sampled tissue, imaging burden, and plasma data. In the first, an unsampled region contains no resistant cells. In the second, that region contains \(K\) resistant founders for any feasible \(K\), with zero or arbitrarily small shedding during the observation window. All observed data distributions are identical. For any finite observation rule, an upper confidence bound below \(K\) therefore has zero uniform coverage over this model class. No additional sequencing depth from the same accessible region repairs it.

The same construction applies to a future source: a sanctuary can begin seeding after the sampling window, leaving all past observations unchanged for any future source rate. Past non-detection bounds future flux only after assuming stationarity/drift and a positive detection/inclusion floor. Whole-tumor decisions must therefore be UNKNOWN when lesion/region inclusion and future source bounds are absent.

A possible test is a blinded spatial-barcode experiment with uniform versus single-region-concentrated founder placement. Partition each tumor into \(J=20\) regions; randomly cover \(b\) regions and vary per-region depth independently. Before within-region dropout, sampling 19 of 20 has a 95% chance of covering one unknown occupied region, while deeper sequencing of one region cannot improve that hit probability. For a formal 95%-coverage/95%-confidence claim, 59 independent holdout tumors with zero undercoverage are needed per declared stratum, since \(0.95^{59}=0.0485\). These are design calculations, not observed validation.

## Schedule algorithm and quantitative diagnostic

For a finite action library and finite horizon, a valid robust objective is the first-moment union bound on the number of resistant lineages reaching a prospectively specified threshold \(M\). A sensitive-source mean envelope and a backward finite-state CTMC for each resistant lineage yield an objective \(\Lambda_U\); any successful-lineage probability is at most \(\min(1,\Lambda_U)\). A backward Pareto dynamic program can minimize dose subject to \(\Lambda_U\le\epsilon\), with the entire simultaneous rate region in the worst case. This is a standard robust-DP application, not a new scheduling theorem.

The synthetic five-block example in [cycle2_schedule_algorithm.md](cycle2_schedule_algorithm.md) uses \(M=4\), \(\epsilon=.04\), and two actions. Exhaustive evaluation of all 32 schedules finds DHHHH as the unique minimum-dose schedule satisfying the adverse-rectangle first-moment bound: at midpoint quadrature \(N=100\), its bound is .032852, with an analytic integration bracket [.032670,.033034]. The midpoint-rate optimizer selects HHHHH, whose adverse-endpoint bound is .053385 (bracket [.053071,.053699]). All one-drug schedules except DHHHH fail the robust threshold. N=10,100,1000 convergence leaves the choice unchanged. The fixture uses synthetic rates, binary64 arithmetic, and a truncated uniformization series whose omitted Poisson tail is bounded above by \(6.5\times10^{-89}\) per exponential; it is finite approximate evidence, not an outward-rounded formal certificate, a tumor fit, or a clinical prediction. The stated endpoint is only “a lineage hits four cells within five blocks.”

## Recent primary prior art and novelty boundary

- Foo and Michor already model nonhomogeneous multitype birth–death processes under general time-varying dosing, resistance risk, pharmacokinetics, and schedule optimization ([2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2766072/); [2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2009.11.022)).
- Patil, Ahmed, Viossat, and Noble provide an analytical and simulation study of two-strike therapy, rescue founders, establishment, and optimal switching around the tumor nadir ([Genetics 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyaf255)).
- Dabi et al. directly compare sequential two-strike extinction therapy with combination therapy, varying switching time, mutation rate, dose, cross-resistance, and discrete versus quantitative resistance ([PLOS Computational Biology, 2026-09-18](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014803)).
- Common positive/copositive Lyapunov functions for switched positive systems are established control theory ([Fornasini & Valcher 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2010.2049918); [Wu & Sun 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfranklin.2013.12.018)).
- Sanctuary-mediated branching, mutation, and migration are themselves prior art ([Fu et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4366398/)); spatial clone heterogeneity and biopsy/cfDNA limits have recent primary study ([Serrano et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44320-026-00194-w)).
- Cancer phenotype-switching/extinction and lineage inference have additional prior art ([Gunnarsson et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7785289/); [Genthon et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXLife.1.013014); [Whiting et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7)).

Thus the PGF, common-weight inequality, risk decomposition, and schedule optimizer are not claimed as novel. The only narrow candidate difference is wrapping simultaneous finite-data regions and spatial coverage into a fail-closed decision interface and testing it on blinded holdouts. That composite is unvalidated, and priority is UNKNOWN. The cancer line does not presently meet the request for a new scientific advance.

## Validation gate, controls, and costs

For a covered organoid/preclinical assay, freeze the state list, treatment library, relapse threshold, event observation process, joint rate region, and robust schedule before holdout. Include the strongest equally informed midpoint/nominal branching optimizer, best fixed schedule, continuous treatment, mutation-specific treatment, and a nadir/two-strike controller. Match dose, assay information, latency, follow-up, and toxicity proxies or charge the differences. Split the total error budget between training-region coverage and held-out risk testing. The quantitative falsifier is a one-sided lower confidence limit for held-out relapse above the frozen risk upper bound; also fail if any event/source rate lies outside its simultaneous region, an untracked genotype seeds relapse, or the spatial sampling model undercovers.

Measure separate costs for cell-time exposure, independent cultures, live-cell imaging, barcode/genotype/state sequencing, limiting-dilution wells, sample regions, detection calibration, assay latency, drug exposure, and toxicity proxy. For a zero-event upper hazard \(10^{-3}\)/day, a single 95% channel needs 2,996 cell-days; familywise allocation over 24 channels needs 6,174 cell-days per channel. The finite schedule demonstration used \(H=5\), two actions, \(2^5=32\) schedules, and \(N=100\) quadrature points per block, with a smaller convergence sweep at 10 and 1,000. No biological fitting, organoid validation, mouse study, patient data analysis, or cost measurement was performed.
