# Jacobsthal: structured obstructions and an alignment-list sharpening

Selected TeX sources for Sections8–11 are preserved, but the complete83-page proof was not read or independently verified. This is a secondary mechanism assessment.

## Author's exact claim

Theorem1.1 claims h(k)≤Ck²/(log log(3k))² for every k≥1, uniformly over prime sets and signed interval starts. The stronger quantitative engine, Theorem1.2, says every choice of one forbidden class a_p mod p for all p≤z leaves at least cYV₀/B² survivors in [1,Y], with

Y=floor(z²/(log z)²), w=(log z)/(log log z)²,

B=(log z)/(log w), V₀=∏_{p≤w}(1−1/p).

Thus the guaranteed survivor scale is Y log w/(log z)² at a cutoff z≈sqrt(Y) log Y. This is genuinely uniform over adversarial classes, not a random-model statement. The constants are not provided effectively.

The proof's proposed mechanism is to evaluate a reference decreasing-prime tree, then recover rational alignment from any large discrepancy with the actual classes and stop aligned branches where exact counts dominate their reference contribution. A positive reference term alone does not establish the original survivor lower bound. The load-bearing transfer remains Proposition8.2's inverse estimate together with Propositions10.5–10.6's stopped comparison and coverage. These were not fully audited here.

## A checked independent sharpening inside Section8

For an isolated prime bin P with N primes ≥R, define alignment of a reduced rational A/D by D a_p≡A mod p. Suppose each candidate has |A|≤H_A and D≤H_D, and aligns on at least δN primes, δ>0 fixed.

Two distinct candidates align at a common prime only if it divides the nonzero integer A₁D₂−A₂D₁, whose absolute value is at most2H_AH_D. Consequently two alignment sets have intersection size at most

s=floor(log(2H_AH_D)/log R).

If height is polynomial in R, s is bounded independently of N. Let m=floor(1/δ)+1. If there were m candidates, the first Bonferroni inequality would give

N≥|union of their alignment sets|≥mδN−s·binom(m,2).

For N>s·binom(m,2)/(mδ−1), this is impossible. Therefore there are at most floor(1/δ) candidates for sufficiently large N. This sharpens the manuscript's convenient list bound ceil(2/δ²), without altering its mathematical setup. No independence of alignment events is assumed.

In the manuscript, (8.7) has δ=c_p, H_D=RZ^10 and H_A=S_qRZ^10. The determinant size is z^(2+α+o(1)), while every prime is at least z^α_min. Hence s is a fixed constant. The prime-bin cardinality tends to infinity for every fixed bin width ξ, so the sharpened list bound is independent of ξ; its eventual threshold in z may depend on ξ. This matches the needed order of choices.

This is a small constant/list-size improvement, not an improved Jacobsthal exponent. It is a standard packing consequence of bounded pair intersections, closely related to the larger sieve. It does not prove that a discrepant edge actually belongs to any member of this list. That discovery step is the substantive inverse theorem still requiring scrutiny.

## Consequences and limits

Theorem1.1 is equivalent via CRT to an interval avoiding k independently prescribed prime classes. It immediately handles a fixed affine condition a n+b≠0 mod p when a is invertible mod p, by converting it to one forbidden class. A prime dividing a either removes no inputs or makes the condition impossible, which must be handled separately.

It does not extend automatically to several forbidden classes per prime: at p=2, the two forbidden classes cover every integer. Any transfer to multiple forms must check a new local-density and higher-dimensional sieve interface. Nor does the non-effective absolute constant supply an executable finite search-length certificate.
