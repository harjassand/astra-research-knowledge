## 1. The rational Krylov span forced by a zero extactic

Write f(y)=(y^2)^TQ(y^2), H=Hess f, h=det H, F=adj H grad h, D=F dot grad. Let r be the least index for which

    y, Dy, ..., D^r y

are dependent over R(y). If the n-coordinate extactic vanishes, r<n. The first r vectors span a rational rank-r subspace W(y) of R(y)^n, and their dependence gives D^r y in W. Differentiating successively shows every higher D-iterate stays in W. Equivalently the r-row basis matrix obeys D B=A(y)B, a rational linear connection. Along any analytic nonsingular trajectory, W is consequently constant as an actual real subspace. The trajectory lies in its initial W for every finite time by analytic continuation; a pole of a rational representative does not invalidate that fixed linear identity.

Changing the speed by a nonzero scalar field and adding a radial field preserves this Krylov span, because successive derivatives change by a triangular row transformation. Thus a projective trajectory converging to a ray p yields p in its initial W.

Suppose a projective attracting ray p has an open basin. Generic initial points in that basin avoid the proper polynomial locus where a chosen W basis is undefined. For these points p belongs to W(y). The containment is a rational identity (all augmented rank minors vanish), so it extends to generic y everywhere. Hidden sign flips s commute with F and give W(sy)=sW(y). Applying the global containment at sy gives sp in W(y) for every s. If S is the support of p, its sign orbit spans precisely the coordinate space E_S. Hence

    E_S subset W(y) generically.                 (1)

The same conclusion holds for the union of supports of finitely many open-basin attractors. In particular a full-support attractor would contradict rank W<n immediately. This argument uses no local attraction rates and does not require a supplied metric.

An attracting manifold or a continuum of maxima is insufficient as stated: its limiting point can vary with the initial point, and then a fixed p containment on an open set has not been established. Neither finite Aut(f) nor mere existence of a curvature maximum supplies this missing premise.

## 2. No coordinate-pair ratio is a first integral, for any QPD

For every distinct i,j one has

    y_i F_j-y_j F_i != 0 as a polynomial.         (2)

This is universal in n, including all zero off-diagonal patterns. Here is a proof that does not assume a nonvanishing restriction of the full field to a binary face.

Fix I={i,j}, write z=(y_i,y_j), and let p(z)=a u^4+2b u^2v^2+c v^4 be the principal binary quartic. QPD gives a,c>0 and Delta=ac-b^2>0. Its four complex roots are distinct and noncoordinate; their squared-coordinate ratios are (-b +/- i sqrt Delta)/a. Its Hessian h_2 obeys

    h_2=48[b p+3 Delta u^2v^2].

Thus gcd(p,h_2)=1, and every nonzero real even quadratic in z is also coprime to p.

Partition the omitted coordinates into

    Z={k outside I: Q_ki=Q_kj=0},
    K={k outside I: (Q_ki,Q_kj)!=(0,0)}.

Set y_K=0 and y_Z=epsilon w. Because Q_ZI=0, the Hessian is block diagonal on this restriction, with blocks H_2(z), epsilon^2 H_Z(w), and scalar entries

    g_k(z)+epsilon^2 b_k(w),
    g_k(z)=4[Q_ki y_i^2+Q_kj y_j^2],  k in K.

Every g_k is a nonzero real even quadratic. Put R_epsilon=product_(k in K)(g_k+epsilon^2 b_k), R=R_0 and h_Z=det H_Z. Use h_Z=1 when Z is empty. Otherwise Q_ZZ>0, and h_Z is nonzero: minimize its squared-variable quadratic on a simplex, whose KKT conditions give a positive-definite Hessian at a point, as in the root's nonempty-H>0 lemma.

The tangential part of the full field is exactly

    F_I|restriction = epsilon^(4|Z|) h_Z^2
                     R_epsilon adj H_2 grad_z(h_2 R_epsilon).       (3)

If the left side of (2) vanished globally, the lowest epsilon coefficient in (3), followed by the binary Euler identity H_2 z=3 grad p, would give

    0 = 3 h_Z^2 R Jac(p,h_2 R).

All canceled factors are nonzero. The polynomial g=h_2 R is homogeneous of positive degree e=4+2|K| and coprime to the squarefree p. On v!=0, write p=v^4 P(t), g=v^e G(t). Their Jacobian is v^(e+2)[e P'(t)G(t)-4P(t)G'(t)]. At any simple root of P, P' and G are nonzero, so this expression is nonzero. Contradiction. This proves (2).

The epsilon scaling is only a proof of a nonzero polynomial coefficient. It introduces no small-number search, numerical limit, axis input or hidden condition bound into acquisition.

## 3. A useful but conditional coverage consequence

Combine (1) and (2). If some open-basin attractor, or a finite union of such attractors, has support union S of size at least n-2, E cannot vanish.

Indeed W contains E_S. If the complement has zero or one coordinate, y in W already forces rank W=n. If its complement is {i,j}, deficiency would leave a quotient W/E_S of dimension at most1. Its vectors y and Dy then force y_i F_j-y_j F_i=0, contradicting (2).

## 4. The positive-Hessian curvature identity and its exact limit

H y=3 grad f and homogeneity of h give

    Df=(2n/3)h^2.

On H>0 put V=H^-1 grad log h=F/h^2 and K=h/f^(n/2). Then

    V log K = (grad log h)^T H^-1(grad log h)-n^2/(3f) >= 0,

by Cauchy-Schwarz with y^T H y=12f and y dot grad log h=2n. Equality holds precisely when grad log h is proportional to grad f, i.e. when the projective field is stationary. On f=1, the projected field is the gradient of log K for the induced positive Hessian metric. Positive maxima exist in a positive-Hessian component, but their support and isolation remain substantive issues. The exact n4 example shows that no full-support stationary point need exist anywhere in H>0; this rules out the simplest universal attractor argument.
