SPECTRAL GEOMETRY CYCLE 2 — SU(2)^r PRODUCT HIGHEST-WEIGHT FAMILY
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. No independent audit or novelty claim.

PURPOSE

This extends the exact SU(m) profile in cycle2/RESULT.txt to the distinct
family G=SU(2)^r, r>=2 fixed, with highest weight (n omega,...,n omega).
It gives the carrier dimension, the full conjugation-Casimir spectrum and
multiplicities, the exact rank-Q spectral profile, its low-band Weyl law,
an explicit matching-order taper, and the exact rank-one extremizer. The
upper witness for Q>1 is not asserted to be the exact optimizer.

1. CARRIER, NORMALIZATION, AND SPECTRUM

Let V_n=Sym^n(C^2), the spin j=n/2 irreducible SU(2)-module, and let
  H_{n,r}=V_n tensor ... tensor V_n       (r factors),
  d_{n,r}=dim H_{n,r}=(n+1)^r.
This is the irreducible SU(2)^r highest-weight representation with highest
weight (n omega,...,n omega). Its matrix carrier End(H_{n,r}) has dimension
d_{n,r}^2=(n+1)^(2r).

In the fundamental representation choose Hermitian generators
T_a=sigma_a/2, a=1,2,3, so Tr_{C^2}(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2. Let T_a^(q)
act as the induced spin-j generator in factor q and as identity on the other
factors. Define the positive conjugation Casimir
  L(X)=sum_{q=1}^r sum_{a=1}^3
       [T_a^(q),[T_a^(q),X]],
  E(X)=<X,LX>_HS
      =sum_{q,a} ||[T_a^(q),X]||_HS^2.                    (1)
This normalization makes the spin-k Casimir k(k+1), with no extra factor 2.

Clebsch-Gordan gives
  End(V_n) = V_{n/2} tensor V_{n/2}^*
            = direct-sum_{k=0}^n V_k,
each summand once. Since End(H_{n,r}) is the tensor product of these r
endomorphism spaces, under conjugation by SU(2)^r,
  End(H_{n,r}) = direct-sum_{k in {0,...,n}^r}
                    (V_{k_1} box-times ... box-times V_{k_r}).
The sector indexed by k has eigenvalue and multiplicity
  lambda_k = sum_{q=1}^r k_q(k_q+1),
  m_k = product_{q=1}^r (2 k_q+1).                       (2)
The multiplicity sum is (sum_{k=0}^n(2k+1))^r=(n+1)^(2r), as required.

2. EXACT LOW-BAND PROFILE AND RANK LOWER BOUND

For real t>=0 define the cumulative low-band multiplicity
  D_{n,r}(t)=sum_{k_1=0}^n ... sum_{k_r=0}^n
                [product_q(2k_q+1)]
                1{sum_q k_q(k_q+1)<=t}.                  (3)
For each integer Q>=1 and every X with ||X||_HS=1 and rank(X)<=Q, the
partial-trace rank-profile theorem from cycle2/RESULT.txt gives
  E(X) >= Phi_{n,r}(Q)
       := integral_0^{r n(n+1)}
              [1-Q D_{n,r}(t)/d_{n,r}]_+ dt.              (4)
This is exact for all n,r,Q; the integrand is zero beyond the largest
Casimir r n(n+1). It uses carrier dimension d_{n,r}, not End dimension.

For an explicit finite sum, sort the distinct eigenvalues in (2) as
  0=theta_0<theta_1<...<theta_J=r n(n+1),
and put D_j=D_{n,r}(theta_j). Then
  Phi_{n,r}(Q)=sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(theta_{j+1}-theta_j)
                         [1-QD_j/d_{n,r}]_+.             (5)
The exact last positive interval is
  j_+(n,r,Q)=max({j: QD_j<d_{n,r}} union {-1});
terms j>j_+ vanish, and equality at the cutoff contributes zero. Formula
(3) retains collisions between different tuples k that have the same
Casimir; (5) aggregates their multiplicities before sorting.

3. LOW-ENERGY WEYL LAW AND RANK-PROFILE ASYMPTOTIC

Fix r. For 1<<t<<n^2,
  D_{n,r}(t)=t^r/r! + O_r(t^(r-1/2)).                     (6)
Proof by weighted cells. Set a_k=k(k+1), so a_{k+1}-a_k=2k+1. The
r-dimensional cells product_q [a_{k_q},a_{k_q+1}) have volume
product_q(2k_q+1). The union over sum_q a_{k_q}<=t therefore has volume
exactly D_{n,r}(t). For t=o(n^2), no carrier boundary is reached. Every
cell touching the simplex sum_q x_q<=t has side lengths O(sqrt(t)); hence
the union lies between the simplices with coordinate sum at most
t-O_r(sqrt(t)) and t+O_r(sqrt(t)). Their volumes are
(t+O_r(sqrt(t)))^r/r!, proving (6).

Let Q=Q_n be integers with 1<=Q=o(d_{n,r}). Define
  t_Q=(r! d_{n,r}/Q)^(1/r).
Then t_Q->infinity and t_Q=o(n^2), so (6) applies throughout the
contributing band. Substitution in (4) gives
  Phi_{n,r}(Q)
    = (r/(r+1)) (r! d_{n,r}/Q)^(1/r)
       + O_r(t_Q^(1/2))
    = (r/(r+1))(r!)^(1/r) (n+1) Q^(-1/r) (1+o(1)).    (7)
The main integral is int_0^{t_Q}(1-(t/t_Q)^r)dt
=r t_Q/(r+1); the O_r(t_Q^(1/2)) error follows by integrating the
O_r(t^(r-1/2)) discrepancy in (6). Thus (7) is an asymptotically sharp
Weyl-law lower profile, with an exact finite-n version in (3)-(5).

4. EXPLICIT HIGHEST-WEIGHT TAPER AND TWO-SIDED ORDER

Use the tensor occupation basis |i_1,...,i_r>, 0<=i_q<=n, where i_q
counts lowering from the highest weight in factor q, and write s=sum i_q.
For integer 0<=R<n set
  Q_R = #{i in Z_{>=0}^r: |i|<=R}=binom(R+r,r),
  Z_{r,R}=sum_{s=0}^R binom(s+r-1,r-1)(R+1-s)^2
        =binom(R+r+2,r+2)+binom(R+r+1,r+2),
and define the diagonal matrix
  X_R |i> = Z_{r,R}^(-1/2)(R+1-s)|i>,  s<=R,
           0,                                s>R.        (8)
Then rank(X_R)=Q_R and ||X_R||_HS=1.

For one SU(2) factor, the raising edge i_q -> i_q+1 has squared ladder
coefficient (n-i_q)(i_q+1). The two Hermitian root generators together
contribute this coefficient times |x_i-x_{i+e_q}|^2; the Cartan generator
commutes with X_R. Every edge starting in |i|<=R changes the taper by
Z^(-1/2), including boundary edges to |i|=R+1. Thus
  E(X_R)=W_{n,r,R}/Z_{r,R},
  W_{n,r,R}=sum_{|i|<=R} sum_{q=1}^r (n-i_q)(i_q+1)
      =r[n binom(R+r,r)
          +(n-2)binom(R+r,r+1)-2binom(R+r,r+2)].        (9)
The binomial expression follows by summing each layer |i|=s. For a fixed
coordinate q, its first and second factorial occupation moments are
  sum_{|i|=s} i_q = [z^s] z(1-z)^(-r-1)=binom(s+r-1,r),
  sum_{|i|=s} i_q(i_q-1)
       = [z^s] 2z^2(1-z)^(-r-2)=2binom(s+r-1,r+1).

For Q_R<=Q<Q_{R+1}=binom(R+r+1,r), use X_R. Since
  W <= r n[binom(R+r,r)+binom(R+r,r+1)],
  Z >= binom(R+r+2,r+2),
binomial ratios give
  E(X_R) <= r(r+2)n/(R+r+2)
          < [r(r+2)/(r!)^(1/r)] n Q^(-1/r).             (10)
The last step uses Q<binom(R+r+1,r)<=(R+r+1)^r/r!.

For a completely explicit two-sided range, assume n>=r>=2 and
  1<=Q<=min(floor((n+1)^r/4^(r+1)), binom(n,r)).         (11)
Then t0=((n+1)^r/(4^(r+1)Q))^(1/r)>=1 and R(Q)<n. Since
  D_{n,r}(t0) <= (floor(sqrt(t0))+1)^(2r) <= 4^r t0^r,
the profile (4) is at least 3t0/4. Combining with (10),
  c_r (n+1)Q^(-1/r) <= inf_{rank X<=Q, ||X||_HS=1} E(X)
                         <= C_r n Q^(-1/r),              (12)
where
  c_r=3/[4*4^((r+1)/r)],
  C_r=r(r+2)/(r!)^(1/r).
For every sequence Q=o((n+1)^r), the lower profile has the sharper
asymptotic (7), while (8) is an explicit upper witness of the same order.
If rank exactly Q is required, add arbitrarily small nonzero diagonal
entries on Q-Q_R extra basis vectors and renormalize; continuity of E makes
(10) an infimum bound at exact rank Q as well.

The taper is an explicit order-extremizer/upper witness for general Q,
not claimed to be the exact optimizer. At Q=1 it is an exact extremizer.
Indeed, if X=|u><v| is rank one and j=n/2, let m_q(u) be the three-vector
of spin expectations in factor q. Then
  E(X)=2 sum_{q=1}^r [j(j+1)-m_q(u) dot m_q(v)] >= r n,
because |m_q(u)|,|m_q(v)|<=j. Equality holds for
  X=|j,j>^{tensor r}<j,j|^{tensor r},
which is X_0 and has E(X_0)=r n.

5. CLAIM BOUNDARY AND FALSIFIERS

The representation, Casimir normalization, sector dimensions, exact band
profile, cutoff (11), and taper energy (9) are internally derived from the
standard SU(2) Clebsch-Gordan rule and the partial-trace rank-profile
theorem. No floating computation, approximation, sampler, or external
oracle enters. A normalization error in Tr(T_aT_b)=delta_ab/2, a missing
factor in the two-root-generator edge energy, or a mismatch in (9) would
falsify the formulas.

This is a matrix Casimir-energy theorem, not an operational quantum-memory
or Gibbs-orbit lower bound. It does not solve an acquisition problem. The
fixed-r Weyl asymptotic is not uniform as r grows. The explicit taper matches
the lower order but not its leading constant; exact optimizers for Q>1 are
UNKNOWN. Independent representation-theory audit and priority search are
also UNKNOWN.
