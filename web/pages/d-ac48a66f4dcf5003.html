# Conditional finite-extension transport and a published matrix-algebra use

Question: if a uniform deterministic polynomial-bit factorizer for dense polynomials over prime fields is established, does that actually remove the factorization oracle in a consequential published algorithm over general finite fields?

Yes, conditionally. The reduction below supplies an explicit polynomial-overhead bridge from prime fields to explicitly represented finite extensions. Combined with Ivanyos–Kutas–Rónyai Theorem1, it removes the stated finite-field factorization oracle in the explicit split matrix-algebra isomorphism problem over k(X). The full family142 premise remains conditional on its unvalidated analytic source. No priority or practical speed advantage is claimed for this standard norm-shift/Berlekamp combination.

## Inputs and theorem

Let k=Fp[α]/Π with supplied irreducible monic Π of degree e, p in binary, and f∈k[X] dense of degree n. Suppose a deterministic prime-field procedure factors a degree-d polynomial in time P(d,logp), polynomial in its arguments. Then a deterministic procedure completely factors f over k, including multiplicities, using polynomial overhead in n,e,logp and polynomially many calls P(en,logp).

The explicit field presentation is charged. The procedure need not construct a primitive element from an opaque field oracle. Coefficients are e-tuples in Fp; multiplication, inverses and Frobenius powers therefore have polynomial bit cost. Integers used for loop bounds are not treated as binary-sized numerical work.

Remove multiplicities by derivative/gcd recursion. If a derivative is zero, take coefficient pth roots using a↦a^{p^{e−1}}, reduce the degree by p, and restore multiplicities. This inverse Frobenius has exponent bit length O(e logp). Apply the following two branches only to monic square-free factors.

### Small p: enumerate the prime field, not all of k

If p≤max(n,n²e²), form A=k[X]/f as an Fp-algebra of dimension en and solve a^p=a by Fp-linear algebra. If f has r irreducible factors over k, A is a product of r finite fields, and its p-Frobenius fixed algebra is Fp^r. Its dimension is exactly r.

Dimension1 proves irreducibility. Otherwise a basis contains a nonscalar b; its component values belong to Fp and are not all equal. Enumerating c∈Fp and taking gcd(f,b−c) splits f. Recurse. The loop length p is polynomial in n,e, even when |k|=p^e is large. This is the ordinary Berlekamp fixed-algebra mechanism with its scalar field made explicit.

### Large p: a deterministic norm-shift separation

Assume e>1 and p>n²e². Put σ(a)=a^p and, for t∈Fp,

    a(t)=1+tα+⋯+t^{e−1}α^{e−1},  g_t(X)=f(X−a(t)).

Let the unknown irreducible factors of f be f_i, with degree r_i≤n. They are used only to prove termination. A collision between different Frobenius conjugates, after applying a common inverse conjugation, has the form

    f_i(X−a(t))=σ^s(f_j)(X−σ^s(a(t))),  0<s<e.

It requires r_i=r_j=r. Comparing the next-to-leading coefficient gives

    r(σ^s(a(t))−a(t))=σ^s(μ_j)−μ_i.

Since p>n, r is nonzero in Fp. The left side is a nonconstant polynomial in t of degree≤e−1: its linear coefficient is r(σ^sα−α)≠0 because α has degree e. At most e−1 values t cause each collision. There are at most n²(e−1) ordered triples(i,j,s), so fewer than n²e² bad values.

Among t=0,…,n²e² one succeeds; these are distinct because p>n²e². The observable test is simply that

    H_t(X)=∏_{s=0}^{e−1} σ^s(g_t)(X)

is square-free. It has degree en and coefficients in Fp. At a successful t, all conjugate component factors are distinct. The norm of each k-irreducible component is an Fp-irreducible polynomial: a proper Fp-factor would select a proper σ-stable subset of its transitive orbit, impossible. Factor H_t using the charged prime-field procedure. For each returned Fp-factor h, gcd_k(g_t,h) isolates exactly one component, and the reverse shift gives an irreducible factor of f. e1 uses the prime-field procedure directly.

Dense norm products and all gcds have degree at most en. There are at most n²e²+1 shift attempts per square-free call and polynomially many such calls. No integer factorization, primitive-root oracle or enumeration of p^e elements is needed. The bound is polynomial, not an assertion that it is competitive for large parameters.

## Downstream theorem, precise prior and scope

Ivanyos–Kutas–Rónyai, *Explicit isomorphisms of matrix algebras over function fields*, arXiv1508.07755v3,2January2017; FoCM18(2),2018,381–397, Theorem1 PDFp2: given structure constants for an algebra promised isomorphic to M_m(k(X)), a polynomial f-algorithm acquires an explicit isomorphism. Their f-algorithm is deterministic and calls an oracle for univariate factorization over k. Full15-page read. The published primary abstract also identifies applications to factorization in certain skew polynomial rings.

Substituting the above finite-extension reduction and a correct family142 prime-field learner makes this a uniform deterministic polynomial-bit explicit-isomorphism algorithm over every explicitly represented finite field. Costs include the structure-constant table, rational coefficient degrees/heights, e, logp, the published maximal-order/intersection computations, and all oracle substitutions.

This is a conditional deduction from an established downstream algorithm, not a new published application of the old matrix-algebra construction. It does not handle a generic algebra over a higher-genus function field merely by changing the symbol X, does not solve number-field integer factoring, and gives no inexpensive runtime bound.
