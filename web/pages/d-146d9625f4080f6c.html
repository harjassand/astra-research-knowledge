## Theorem 1: every truncated Mellin interval

For g≥1 and every 0<A<B<∞,

    g |∫_A^B J0(x) x^(2ig) dx/x| ≤ 100.                  (1)

Proof, reconstructed independently. Set t=2g≥2, y=log x, f(y)=J0(e^y). The Bessel equation gives f''=−x²f. With

    N=e^(ity)(itf−f'), R=x²−t²,

one has N'=R e^(ity)f exactly. Therefore on either nonresonant region

    ∫ e^(ity)f dy = [N/R] − ∫N(1/R)'dy.                (2)

The angular representation gives |J0|≤1, |J0'|≤1. On x≤t/2, |N|≤3t/2 and |R|≥3t²/4. The two endpoints cost 4/t, while the total variation of 1/R is at most 1/(3t²). Every low subinterval thus costs ≤9/(2t). This remains valid for arbitrarily small positive A; no divergent Mellin endpoint is introduced.

On x≥2t, |N|≤x+t≤3x/2, |R|≥3x²/4, and |d(1/R)/dx|≤32/(9x³). Each endpoint ratio ≤2/x≤1/t. The integral term costs at most (16/3)∫_(2t)^∞x^−2 dx=8/(3t), so every high subinterval costs ≤14/(3t).

For the middle slab, put H=√x J0(x). Direct substitution gives H''+[1+1/(4x²)]H=0. Let E_H=H²+H'²; then E_H'=−HH'/(2x²) and |E_H'|≤E_H/(4x²). From the differentiated angular representation at x=1, |J0'(1)|≤1/2: use |sin(cosθ)|≤|cosθ| and average cos²θ=1/2. Thus |H(1)|≤1, |H'(1)|≤1, E_H(1)≤2 and E_H(x)≤2e^(1/4)<4 for x≥1. In particular |H|,|H'|≤2.

Set α=Hcosx−H'sinx, β=Hsinx+H'cosx. Then α'=Hsinx/(4x²), β'=−Hcosx/(4x²), α²+β²=E_H. Write a±=(α∓iβ)/2; |a±|≤1 and |a±'|≤1/(4x²). Exactly,

    J0(x)=x^−1/2[a+(x)e^(ix)+a−(x)e^(−ix)].

For w±=a±x^−3/2 on any [L,U]⊂[t/2,2t], L≥1,

    |w±(U)|+∫_L^U |w±'|dx
       ≤2L^−3/2 +(1/10)L^−5/2 ≤(21/10)L^−3/2.       (3)

The phases φ±=tlogx±x have φ±''=−t/x², hence |φ±''|≥1/(4t) throughout the slab. Here is a complete elementary oscillatory estimate. For a phase with monotone derivative and |φ''|≥λ, divide a subinterval at |φ'|=√λ. The central piece has length ≤2/√λ. Each of the at most two outer pieces has a fixed-sign monotone derivative; integration by parts bounds its integral by 2/√λ (the reciprocal derivative's variation cancels one endpoint in this upper bound). Thus every unweighted subinterval integral is ≤6/√λ≤12√t. Abel integration and (3) give, for each sign,

    |∫_L^U w± e^(iφ±)dx| ≤12√t (21/10)(t/2)^−3/2.

Both signs together cost at most (252/5)2^(3/2)/t <143/t. Sum this with the low and high pieces, multiply by g=t/2: the result is <76<100. Empty pieces cause no problem; any [A,B] partitions into at most three pieces. This proves (1), including turning-point and endpoint cuts.

## Theorem 2: the original leading sector's UV gate closes

Our initial proof established

    ∫_0^∞ |J0(x)−1_(x<1)|dx/x ≤49/8,
    |I0−F|≤49πsg.                                      (4)

Equation (1), with A=bE,B=bQ and an irrelevant unit-modulus reference phase, gives |Iχ|≤800πs for g≥1, for **every real χ**. Since F≤8πs,

    |I0−F|≤πs min(49Gs,808) for every g>0.              (5)

For M²>0, E,Q,b may depend measurably on s and G. Then

    ∫_(M²)^∞ |I0−F| ds/s³
       ≤49πG log_+(1/(GM²))+808π min(G,1/M²).          (6)

This is O(G log(1/G)) for fixed M as G→0. The weighted difference is dominated for every G by 808π/s² and tends pointwise to zero by (4), so dominated convergence now applies, including moving b,E,Q. The original packet's failed UV envelope cannot be retained as a decisive obstruction after this new estimate.

The integral of I0 is absolutely convergent and ≥ minus (6). This is a quantitative approximate positivity result for this exact mathematical kernel. Its front F can itself depend on G; a nonzero limiting front requires a separately specified scaling. This result is not a proof of a dispersive gravity EFT inequality.

## Theorem 3: include the physical gamma phase

Adamo–Cristofoli–Tourkine 2112.09113v4, §2 equation (2.7), has (ℏ=1, q>0)

    i M_eik = (2πs/μ²) Γ(1−ig)/Γ(ig) (4μ²/q²)^(1−ig).

Using Γ(1+ig)=igΓ(ig),

    M_eik = (8πGs²/q²) U(g) (q²/(4μ²))^(ig),
    U(g)=Γ(1−ig)/Γ(1+ig)=e^(iχ(g)).                   (7)

The modulus of U is one at real g. Define χ continuously from χ(0)=0, not by a wrapped principal argument. Then χ=−2 Im logΓ(1+ig), χ'=−2Reψ(1+ig). The convergent digamma series gives

    Reψ(1+ig)=−γ+Σ_(n≥1)g²/[n(n²+g²)].

For 0≤g≤1, γ∈(0,1), Σ≤ζ(3)<5/4; hence |Reψ|≤5/4, |χ|≤(5/2)g≤3g. This uses elementary ζ(3)≤1+1/8+∫_2^∞x^−3dx=5/4, not an unexplained numerical constant.

Replace J0 by its step as in (4); this still costs 49πsg for any χ. Its integral is exactly

    Fχ=4πs[cosχ−cos(2z+χ)].

By |cos(a+χ)−cos a|≤|χ|, |Fχ−F|≤8πs|χ|≤24πsg. Therefore

    |Iχ−F|≤73πsg for 0<g≤1,
    |Iχ−F|≤808πs for g≥1.                            (8)

Consequently, for GM²≤1,

    ∫_(M²)^∞ |Iχ−F| ds/s³
       ≤73πG log(1/(GM²))+808πG;                     (9)

for GM²≥1 the bound is 808π/M². This explicitly retains the exact gamma phase of (7), with a rigorous O(G log(1/G)) budget. Matching at 1/G is deliberate: the small-g phase bound has only been proved for g≤1; extending it to an arbitrarily optimized matching scale would be unjustified.
