MAIN THEOREM (premise-compatible binary weak-Pinsker consequence).
Let G0 be a countable group and F a finite field of characteristic two. Suppose
a,b in F[G0] satisfy ab=1 and ba!=1. Write |F|=2^d, M=2^d-1, and set
                    G=(G0 x C_M) * Z.
Then, with uniform Haar measure, the binary Bernoulli G-action X=F2^G has
arbitrarily large finite-entropy direct Bernoulli factors. More explicitly there
are an integer m>=1 and a standard probability-measure-preserving G-system R
such that X ~=_G X^2 x R, and for every n>=1,
                    X ~=_G X^n x R^(n-1).
Consequently h_WP(G acting on F2^G)=infinity. If G0 is finitely presented, so is G.
The same action has Rokhlin entropy at most log(2), since its one-coordinate
binary partition is generating. This finite bound is immediate and does not
use the more delicate strict supremal bound in ASTRA N166.
No statement here asserts X ~=_G X^2, Bernoullicity of R, torsion-freeness of G,
or the analogous conclusion for the original group G0.

The algebraic premise is sufficient; it is not proved in this note. In particular
the current OpenAI Family 197 comparator supplies this precise type of premise
as its stated target but its comparator file itself contains sorry. Verification
of an OAI implementation, and verification of the underlying group construction,
are independent obligations not performed by this worker.

1. PRIME-FIELD CORNER TRANSFER.

The group algebra F2[C_M] is A=F2[t]/(t^M-1). Choose a generator alpha of F^*.
Its minimal polynomial f over F2 has degree d, since alpha generates the whole
field. It divides t^M-1. This polynomial is square-free, because M is odd and
its derivative is t^(M-1), coprime to t^M-1. Write t^M-1=f h. By the Chinese
remainder theorem,
                   A ~= F2[t]/f x F2[t]/h.
Here the second factor may be the zero ring when M=1. Let e correspond to (1,0).
Then e^2=e and eA is a copy of F. Let iota:F -> A be its embedding; iota(1)=e.
Coefficientwise extension gives an injective ring homomorphism
             iota:F[G0] -> F2[G0 x C_M],
with image supported in the corner e F2[G0 x C_M]. The central element e
commutes with the G0 coordinates. Define
                   a'=(1-e)+iota(a), b'=(1-e)+iota(b).
Cross products vanish, so
                   a'b'=(1-e)+iota(ab)=1,
                   b'a'-1=iota(ba-1)!=0.
Thus there is an actual scalar F2 group-ring witness, without taking roots of
a tensor power of a Bernoulli action. The construction uses a finite central
group extension. It does not yield a torsion-free witness.

The same argument works over the prime field F_p for any finite F_{p^d}, using
C_(p^d-1). The characteristic-two case is all that is needed for the theorem.
This finite-ring corner argument is classical algebra; no priority is claimed.

2. EFFECTIVE KERNEL AND ESSENTIAL FREENESS OF A LINEAR BLOCK IMAGE.

Lemma. Let L be a countably infinite group, k=F_q a finite field, and let
0!=r=sum_s r_s s belong to k[L]. Set S=supp(r), X=k^L with uniform product
measure, and
               (T_r u)(h)=sum_s r_s u(hs),  Y=T_r(X).
Equip Y with the image of Haar measure, which is Haar measure on this compact
image subgroup. Define
               H_r={t in L : t r=r},
               N_r=intersection_(h in L) h H_r h^(-1).
Then H_r is finite, N_r is the action kernel on Y, and the stabilizer of
Haar-almost every point of Y is exactly N_r. In particular Y is essentially
free precisely when N_r is trivial.

Proof. Any t in H_r permutes S by left multiplication. The orbit of a single
s in S gives an injection H_r -> S, so H_r is finite. For g,h in L the h-row
of (g-I)T_r is the difference of the forms
           u -> sum_s r_s u(g^(-1)hs),
           u -> sum_s r_s u(hs).
These rows are identical if and only if h^(-1)g^(-1)h r=r; this is equivalent
to h^(-1)gh in H_r. Thus g acts identically on Y iff g lies in N_r.

Fix g not in N_r and let J={h: h^(-1)gh not in H_r}. This set is infinite.
Indeed, if the centralizer C_L(g) is infinite, the left coset C_L(g)h0 lies
in J for any h0 in J. If the centralizer is finite, the complement of J is
a union of at most |H_r| finite left centralizer cosets, hence is finite.

For h in J the row difference is a nonzero linear form of uniform input
coordinates, and is therefore a uniform F_q random variable. Its input
support is contained in F_h=hS union g^(-1)hS. Inductively choose arbitrarily
many h_i in J with the F_(h_i) pairwise disjoint: intersection with a previously
chosen finite set excludes only finitely many possible h. Their row differences
are independent. Therefore
               Pr[g T_r(u)=T_r(u)] <= q^(-n)
for every n. This probability is zero. Countability of L lets us intersect
the resulting conull sets over g not in N_r. Every element of N_r fixes Y
pointwise, proving the assertion. QED.

This is an elementary reconstruction of a special case of the stronger theorem
of Tucker-Drob, Mixing actions of countable groups are almost free (2012):
nontrivial mixing actions have almost surely a constant finite normal stabilizer.
His paper also characterizes groups whose nontrivial Bernoulli factors are free
as the groups with no nontrivial finite normal subgroup. The kernel criterion
and freeness phenomenon are not presented as new results.

3. FREE PRODUCT REMOVES THE KERNEL.

Return to the transferred witness a',b' in F2[G1], where G1=G0 x C_M, and
embed G1 into G=G1*<z>. The natural group-ring embedding is injective, so the
same identities hold. Put r=1-b'a'!=0. Its support lies in G1. The subgroup
H_r of Lemma 2 lies in G1: tS=S with s,s' in S implies t=s's^(-1) in G1.
If n lies in the core N_r, then n belongs to G1 and z^(-1)n z belongs to G1.
For n!=1 the word z^(-1)n z is reduced in the free product and does not belong
to G1. Hence N_r={1}. Thus K=T_r(F2^G) is essentially free.

The argument did not use torsion-freeness. The new group can retain every
torsion element of G0 and the extra finite cyclic group. The important property
is the trivial finite normal action kernel. In fact G1*Z has no nontrivial
finite normal subgroup, also directly covered by Tucker-Drob's theorem.
If G0=<x_1,...,x_k | u_1,...,u_l>, an explicit presentation recipe is
  G=<x_1,...,x_k,t,z | u_1,...,u_l, t^M, [t,x_1],...,[t,x_k]>.
The free generator z has no added relations. This recipe does not materialize
the missing presentation or group-ring witness from an existential premise.

4. THE ALGEBRAIC PRODUCT SPLITTING.

Write A=T_(a'), B=T_(b'), P=T_r=I-BA on X=F2^G. Right convolution obeys
T_u T_v=T_(uv). Thus AB=I, P^2=P, AP=0 and PB=0. We have
                     K=im P=ker A.
The map
                     x -> (Ax,Px)
is an equivariant continuous compact-group isomorphism X -> X x K, with
inverse (y,k) -> By+k. It transports Haar measure to product Haar measure.
Iteration gives X ~=_G X x K^ell for every finite ell.

5. FINITE COLOR EXTRACTION, WITH ALL INVERSES EXPLICIT.

Let S=supp(r), d0=|SS^(-1)|, m=d0+1, and choose s0 in S. Let K0 be the invariant
conull Borel free part of K. Form the Borel graph on K0 with edges eta ~ t eta
for t in SS^(-1) minus {1}; its maximum degree is at most d0-1. Choose a proper
Borel coloring f:K0->{1,...,d0}. This is the classical finite-degree coloring
bound of Kechris-Solecki-Todorcevic. It can be reconstructed as follows.

A countable Borel algebra separating points contains, for each vertex, a set
containing it and excluding all of its finitely many neighbors. Choosing the
least such set gives a countable proper Borel coloring. Greedily processing
these countably many independent color classes gives a Borel maximal independent
set. Every vertex outside it has a neighbor in it, so removing it drops maximum
degree by at least one. Repeating gives D+1 Borel independent color classes for
any degree-D graph. Apply D=d0-1.

Set chi_eta(g)=f(g^(-1)eta). Then
  gS intersects hS, g!=h => chi_eta(g)!=chi_eta(h),
  chi_(t eta)(tg)=chi_eta(g).
Take independent Haar eta,kappa_1,...,kappa_(d0) in K and put
               T_eta(kappa)(g)=kappa_(chi_eta(g))(g).
Conditional on eta, write kappa_j=P U_j with independent uniform U_j in X.
Equal colors use disjoint input windows gS, and unequal colors use different
reservoirs. Every nonzero row parity is fair. Thus T_eta(kappa) is exactly a
binary Bernoulli field, conditionally on eta. It is independent of eta.

To obtain independence from the entire residual, not just the seed, use an
explicit linear right inverse. Let w_g=P delta_(g s0). Then w_g(g)=1. If h!=g
has the same color as g, then w_g(h)=0: any nonzero such value would imply
g s0 in gS intersect hS. Define
             [S_eta(y)]_j=sum_(g:chi_eta(g)=j) y(g)w_g.
The sum is finite at every coordinate because supp(w_g) is contained in
g s0 S^(-1). Its value belongs to the closed group K by taking finite partial
sums and a coordinatewise limit. Each fixed-seed map S_eta is continuous and
T_eta S_eta=I. Joint dependence on the seed is Borel.

Set r0=kappa-S_eta(T_eta kappa). Then T_eta r0=0. Define
             R={(eta,r0):eta in K0, r0 in ker T_eta}.
Its probability law is the pushforward of Haar on K^(d0+1) under
(eta,kappa)->(eta,(I-S_eta T_eta)kappa). Conditional on eta, the residual is Haar
on ker T_eta, since I-S_eta T_eta is a continuous projection onto that subgroup.

The desired map and its inverse are
  Phi(eta,kappa)=(T_eta kappa,(eta,kappa-S_eta(T_eta kappa))),
  Phi^(-1)(y,(eta,r0))=(eta,r0+S_eta y).
For fixed eta this is an isomorphism of compact groups
                     K^(d0) ~= X x ker T_eta.
Thus it sends Haar to product Haar. The X marginal does not depend on eta, so
the output is independent of the whole residual, not merely its base seed.
The equalities
  T_(t eta)(t kappa)=t T_eta kappa,
  S_(t eta)(t y)=t S_eta y
follow from the coloring identity and t w_g=w_(tg). They establish equivariance
of both maps. Therefore
                         K^m ~=_G X x R.

For a general scalar finite field F_q, replace w_g by r_(s0)^(-1)P delta_(g s0).
All the same formulas work with independent uniform F_q symbols. Binary corner
transfer was used above solely to match the desired binary entropy base.

6. INFINITE WEAK-PINSKER ENTROPY, WITHOUT RESIDUAL CANCELLATION.

Combine X ~= X x K^m with K^m ~= X x R to get
                         X ~= X^2 x R.
Apply this identity to one X factor repeatedly to obtain X ~= X^n x R^(n-1).
For any h>0 choose n with n log(2)>h. Choose finite probability bases L1,L2
with Shannon entropies h and n log(2)-h, respectively. Such finite bases exist
by continuity of entropy on finite probability simplices. Seward's all-countable-
groups equal-entropy Bernoulli isomorphism theorem gives
                         X^n ~= B_h x B_(n log(2)-h).
Hence B_h is an independent direct factor of X. Since h was arbitrary, the
definition of weak-Pinsker entropy yields h_WP(X)=infinity.

Taking countably many copies of K^m ~= X x R also gives
K^N ~= X^N x R^N. The nonatomic standard base F2^N of X^N absorbs any other
standard probability base. Therefore K^N absorbs every standard-base Bernoulli
shift. This is the earlier N253 stabilized statement with repaired premises.
It is not transferred back to X through any infinite factor map.

7. EXACT THINGS THE THEOREM DOES NOT PROVE, AND FAILED SHORTCUTS.

The decisive missing lemma remains:
  For the particular defect K=im(1-b'a') over the new group G and
  m=|supp(1-b'a') supp(1-b'a')^(-1)|+1, is K^m a nontrivial Bernoulli action?
Equivalently, is the explicit system X x R Bernoulli? A positive answer supplies
X ~= X x K^m ~= (F2 x L)^G with base entropy log(2)+H(L)>log(2). This gives an
unequal-entropy Bernoulli conjugacy, including when H(L)=infinity. The proof above
only supplies a direct Bernoulli factor of K^m; it does not prove a generating
Bernoulli partition. The free product and field amplification do not change that.

Austin's 2015/2016 Question 1.1 asks whether every complemented factor of a
Bernoulli system is Bernoulli, stating that he knew no affirmative nonamenable
case. Here K and its finite powers are complemented factors of X because
X ~= X x K^m. Thus the missing lemma belongs to a recognized difficult problem.
That historical statement alone is not a current universal nonresolution claim.
No available search result or manuscript upgrades its present status.

Failed shortcut A: finite dependence plus factor-of-iid plus finite powers.
Take an infinite finitely generated property-(T) group L, a finite generating set
D, and a nontrivial finite abelian group C. The map
           Delta:C^L -> (C^D)^L, Delta(x)(g,s)=x(gs)-x(g)
is a finite-range linear block factor. Its kernel is the diagonal copy of C,
so its image is conjugate to C^L/C. The image has finite dependence and dense
finitely supported homoclinic configurations. Every finite power of that image
is conjugate to (C^k)^L/C^k. None is Bernoulli, by the following reconstruction
from Popa's finite-target Bernoulli cocycle-superrigidity theorem.

Choose a Borel section of (C^k)^L -> (C^k)^L/C^k. The section defines a C^k-valued
cocycle over the quotient. If the quotient were Bernoulli, Popa's theorem would
untwist the cocycle to a homomorphism rho:L->C^k. Adjusting the section by the
transfer map would identify (C^k)^L with a skew product whose C^k coordinate is
acted on by rho. The latter is a nontrivial finite factor. This contradicts
mixing of the original Bernoulli L-action: ker(rho) is infinite and its elements
fix every point of that finite factor. This argument applies to every k>=1.
For this auxiliary counterexample we import the classical cocycle-superrigidity
theorem, not a model-space calculation. It does not refute the complemented
projective case; the quotient's compact kernel prevents the required splitting.

Failed shortcut B: drop torsion-freeness without checking the action kernel.
Let L=Z x C_3 and p=1+t+t^2 in F2[L], where t has order 3. Then p^2=p, and
im T_p is the Bernoulli Z-action made constant on C_3 cosets. It is an infinite
mixing, finite-dependent, complemented linear image. Every finite power has
C_3 in its kernel, whereas every nontrivial Bernoulli L-action is free. Thus
no finite power of this image is a nontrivial Bernoulli L-action. This example
does not satisfy X ~= X x K from a non-direct-finiteness witness, and it does
not refute the targeted defect conjecture. It shows precisely why the kernel
obligation in the torsion-bearing premise repair cannot be silently omitted.

Failed shortcut C: countable stabilization, absorption, or approximate decoding
does not supply a measurable inverse for the canonical infinite defect map.
For every scalar binary witness the augmentation of b' is one. Constants belong
to D=intersection_n B^n X. The map x -> (P A^j x)_j has the same value on x
and x+1. Any conull E intersects E+1 conull, so this map cannot be essentially
injective. The canonical tail projections B^n A^n have independent fair one-site
evaluations and cannot converge in probability along a deterministic subsequence.
These remain scoped obstructions to those particular maps.
