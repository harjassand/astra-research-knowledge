# A rate-free local-potential approximation barrier for the pinned KS density

## Main result and status

Let (X=L^1(\mathbb R^3)\cap L^3(\mathbb R^3)), with
\(\|f\|_X=\|f\|_1+\|f\|_3\), and let
\(V=L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)=X^*\), with the sum norm.
For a fixed particle number (N=3), allow arbitrary spinful fermionic ground-state
ensembles for the *noninteracting* Hamiltonian
\[
 H_s(v)=\sum_{i=1}^3\left(-\tfrac12\Delta_i+v(x_i)\right),
 \qquad v\text{ real and spin independent},
\]
and measure the local potential modulo its irrelevant additive constant by
\[
 \|[v]\|_{V/\mathbb R}:=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\|v+c\|_V.
\]
Write \(\mathcal R_C\) for the spin-summed densities of such ground-state ensembles
with \(\|[v]\|_{V/\mathbb R}\le C\).

**Standard-structure corollary, reconstructed here.** For every finite (C), \(\mathcal R_C\) is
closed in the strong (X)-norm. Consequently, for the fixed density (n_*) in the
pinned OpenAI/math paper—conditional on that paper's Theorem 1.1—
\[
 \delta_C:=\inf_{m\in\mathcal R_C}\|m-n_*\|_X>0
 \quad\text{whenever }\mathcal R_C\ne\varnothing.
\]
This follows from the strong/weak-* closed graph of the noninteracting
Lieb-functional subdifferential, or directly from the variational compactness proof
below. The mathematical statement is therefore a standard convex-analysis/DFT
consequence, not a novelty claim. The infimum is an *existence-only*, generally
non-effective separation; no numerical
value, lower-bound formula, practical error bar, or chemical failure is established.
The ensemble noninteracting kinetic functional's subgradient domain is dense in
its effective domain by the Brøndsted–Rockafellar theorem. Since the pinned
target has finite ensemble kinetic value, an approximating sequence in this
same spinful fixed-N exact-ground-state interface does exist. Any such sequence
converging to \(n_*\) in (L^1\cap L^3\) must have
\(\|[v_j]\|_{V/\mathbb R}\to\infty\). Thus the finite-budget error floors
decrease to zero as the budget grows, but with no known rate. This is a robust approximation obstruction
at each fixed local-potential norm budget, not merely the source's exact
nonrepresentability statement.

The source gives an exact but nonnumerically selected three-electron Coulomb target:
two integer charges (Z,Z) at \((0,0,\pm D/Z)\), (D=10^{200}), where (Z) is
specified by its Eq. (7.10) but has no effective numerical bound. The source theorem
excludes every real spin-independent local multiplier in (V), even with arbitrary
ground-state ensembles. Its (n_*\) has finite kinetic energy and belongs to (X):
the paper's source orbitals are in (H^1), and its density is a sum of their squared
moduli. Thus the theorem applies without adding a density-regularity premise.

## Proof

### 1. Form and density interfaces

For each fixed real (v\in V), multiplication by (v), summed over the three
coordinates, is infinitesimally form bounded relative to the free kinetic form on
the antisymmetric (H^1(\mathbb R^9;\mathbb C^{2^3})) space. For the (L^{3/2})
part, truncate (v_{3/2}) into a bounded part and a tail of arbitrarily small
(L^{3/2})-norm; Hölder and the three-dimensional Sobolev inequality bound the
tail by an arbitrarily small multiple of each coordinate's Dirichlet form. The
(L^\infty) part is a bounded form. Hence (H_s(v)) is defined by a lower-bounded
closed form on (H^1). This fact is pointwise in (v); the proof below does **not**
assume a uniform infinitesimal form-bound modulus over the (V)-ball.

For every finite-kinetic-energy ensemble state (\Gamma), the Hoffmann–Ostenhof
inequality and Sobolev embedding give
\[
 \rho_\Gamma\in X,\qquad \int\rho_\Gamma=3,
 \qquad \|\rho_\Gamma\|_3\le K_{\rm Sob}\,\mathrm{Tr}(T\Gamma),
\]
up to the fixed kinetic normalization. Here (T= -\tfrac12\sum_i\Delta_i\ge0).
The density pairing with (V=X^*) is therefore well-defined.

### 2. Uniform kinetic bound from the density convergence

Suppose (m_j\in\mathcal R_C) and (m_j\to m) strongly in (X). Choose a
representative (w_j=v_j+c_j) with a decomposition (w_j=a_j+b_j),
\[
 \|a_j\|_{3/2}+\|b_j\|_\infty\le C+o(1).
\]
Changing (v_j) by a constant does not change its ground-state space or density.
Let (\Gamma_j) be a normalized ground-state ensemble for (H_s(w_j)), let
\(E_j\) be its ground energy, and let (T_j=\mathrm{Tr}(T\Gamma_j)\).
Strong (X)-convergence makes \(\sup_j\|m_j\|_X=:K<\infty\), so
\[
 |\langle w_j,m_j\rangle|\le (C+1)K
\]
for all sufficiently large (j).

Fix one normalized smooth compactly supported Slater determinant (\Phi_0\) with
three spin orbitals. Its density (\rho_0\in X) and kinetic energy are finite, so
the variational principle yields
\[
 E_j\le \langle\Phi_0,H_s(w_j)\Phi_0\rangle
 \le T[\Phi_0]+(C+1)\|\rho_0\|_X=:M_C.
\]
On the other hand, because (\Gamma_j) is a ground ensemble,
\[
 E_j=T_j+\langle w_j,m_j\rangle\ge -(C+1)K.
\]
It follows directly that
\[
 0\le T_j=E_j-\langle w_j,m_j\rangle\le M_C+(C+1)K.
\]
This is the key uniform coercivity surrogate. Critical (L^{3/2}) concentration
cannot be dismissed by a uniform infinitesimal-form-bound claim; it is instead
controlled *along a strongly convergent, norm-bounded density sequence* by the
ground-energy identity and one fixed test state.

### 3. Weak compactness of the potentials

After passing to a subsequence, the bounded (a_j\) converge weakly in the reflexive
space (L^{3/2}), and the bounded (b_j\) converge weak-* in (L^\infty). The latter
subsequence exists sequentially because (L^1(\mathbb R^3)) is separable. Extract
further so that both component norms converge. Their limits (a,b) satisfy
\(\|a\|_{3/2}+\|b\|_\infty\le C\); put (w=a+b\in V\). Then
\[
 \langle w_j,f\rangle\longrightarrow\langle w,f\rangle
 \quad(f\in X).
\]
In particular, strong convergence of the densities and the uniform potential bound
imply
\[
 \langle w_j,m_j\rangle\longrightarrow\langle w,m\rangle.
\]

### 4. Trace-norm compactness of ground ensembles

The one-body densities (m_j\to m) in (L^1) imply uniform spatial tightness:
\[
 \sup_j\int_{|x|>R}m_j(x)\,dx\longrightarrow0\quad(R\to\infty).
\]
For the three-body probability measure of (\Gamma_j), the probability that any
coordinate lies outside the ball (B_R) is at most this tail integral. Combined
with the uniform kinetic bound above, this implies relative compactness of
\(\{\Gamma_j\}\) in trace norm. One explicit compactness argument is:

1. Multiply all particle coordinates by a smooth cutoff supported in a fixed
   bounded configuration-space box. The lost trace is bounded by the one-body tail;
   the gentle-measurement estimate makes the trace-norm error at most twice the
   square root of that loss.
2. The cutoff states have uniformly bounded (H^1) expectation, since derivatives
   of the cutoff are bounded and (T_j\) is bounded.
3. Rellich compactness on the bounded box gives a finite-rank projection (P) for
   which the discarded kinetic-bounded trace is uniformly small. A second
   gentle-measurement estimate puts each \(\Gamma_j\) within arbitrarily small
   trace norm of the bounded set (P\Gamma_jP), which is precompact because it is
   finite-dimensional.

Thus, along a further subsequence, (\Gamma_j\to\Gamma\) in trace norm. Positivity,
unit trace, and fermionic antisymmetry pass to the limit. The reduced-density map is
trace-norm continuous into (L^1) for Hermitian trace-class operators, so
\(\rho_\Gamma=m\). The positive kinetic form is lower semicontinuous:
\[
 \mathrm{Tr}(T\Gamma)\le\liminf_j T_j.
\]

### 5. The limit ensemble is a ground ensemble

Let (\mathcal E_j=E_j\) denote the ground energy of (H_s(w_j)), and
\(\mathcal E_0(w)=\inf_{\|\Phi\|=1}\langle\Phi,H_s(w)\Phi\rangle\), with the
infimum over the common form domain. For each fixed normalized form-domain state
\(\Phi\), its density (\rho_\Phi\in X), hence
\[
 \limsup_j\mathcal E_j\le\lim_j\langle\Phi,H_s(w_j)\Phi\rangle
 =\langle\Phi,H_s(w)\Phi\rangle.
\]
Taking the infimum over (\Phi) gives
\(\limsup_j\mathcal E_j\le\mathcal E_0(w)\). Meanwhile, lower
semicontinuity of (T), potential-pairing convergence, and
\(\mathrm{Tr}(T\Gamma_j)+\langle w_j,m_j\rangle=\mathcal E_j\) give
\[
 \langle\Gamma,H_s(w)\Gamma\rangle
 =\mathrm{Tr}(T\Gamma)+\langle w,m\rangle
 \le\liminf_j\mathcal E_j.
\]
The variational principle for the normalized (\Gamma) gives the opposite lower
bound \(\mathcal E_0(w)\le\langle\Gamma,H_s(w)\Gamma\rangle\). Therefore
\[
 \mathcal E_0(w)\le\langle\Gamma,H_s(w)\Gamma\rangle
 \le\liminf_j\mathcal E_j\le\limsup_j\mathcal E_j
 \le\mathcal E_0(w),
\]
so equality holds throughout. Thus (\Gamma\) attains the ground energy; for the
nonnegative operator (H_s(w)-\mathcal E_0(w)), zero expectation implies that
\(\Gamma\) is supported on its kernel. It is a ground-state ensemble with density
\(m), generated by (w\) and satisfying \(\|[w]\|_{V/\mathbb R}\le C\).
Therefore (m\in\mathcal R_C\), proving strong (X)-closedness.

### 6. Separation and scope

By the pinned source's Theorem 1.1, (n_*\notin\bigcup_C\mathcal R_C\). Since each
finite-budget set (\mathcal R_C\) is closed, its distance from (n_*\) is positive
whenever nonempty. This is a purely topological positive gap: the proof supplies no
procedure to compute \(\delta_C\), no usable rate in (C\), and no assertion about
other density metrics. For sufficiently large finite (C), (\mathcal R_C\) is
nonempty; e.g. the spin-independent Coulomb well \(-1/|x|\in V\) has a normalizable
three-electron noninteracting ground-state ensemble.

The claim is limited to exact ground-state ensembles, real scalar spin-independent
local multipliers in (V\), fixed (N=3), and strong (L^1\cap L^3\) density error.
It does not establish an error floor in (L^1) alone, (L^2), pointwise/trace-plane,
energy, or any finite-grid observable; nor does it cover spin-dependent, nonlocal,
or distributional potentials. The target charge is finite but non-effective, and
there is no common-chemistry or practical-DFT failure claim.

## Hostile gate and priority boundary

The independent hostile audit found no counterexample under the stated interface.
It tests critical (L^{3/2}) concentration, weak-* (L^\infty) drift, additive gauge,
escape, degeneracy, and trace-norm compactness; see
`bio_assay_transfer/BOUNDED_POTENTIAL_CLOSURE_AUDIT.md`. The proof's key response
to the uniform-form-bound concern is the explicit kinetic bound in Section 2.

The closure mechanism itself is standard: Lieb's convex ensemble formulation
supplies the lower-semicontinuous noninteracting kinetic functional, and the
subdifferential has a strong-density/weak-* potential closed graph. Penz et al.
explicitly connect ensemble v-representability density to Brøndsted–Rockafellar;
the 2022 exchange review states that interacting and noninteracting v-representable
sets are each dense in (L^1\cap L^3), while their intersection remains a distinct
open issue. This package records a precise fixed-budget corollary, not an original
or novelty-cleared advance. It remains conditional on the pinned source theorem and
does not remove its non-effective charge bottleneck.
