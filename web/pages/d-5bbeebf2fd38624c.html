## 1. How a height budget can be derived rather than supplied

The degree30 exact learner has bounded sample-coordinate/probe ranges, bounded quotient dimension, bounded chart/separator trials, and explicit polynomial rational-height arithmetic within one outer attempt. On its positive-probability complete event it outputs a correct rational frame. Thus some correct frame has height bounded by the deterministic one-attempt output-height bound, independently of executing its enormous operators. The polynomial bound follows from the inherited minor, rational-root and nullspace audits. This is an existence-of-bounded-representation consequence of that theorem, not an assertion that every hidden presentation or every random retry has bounded height.

Clearing each primal column's denominators gives an integer invertible V whose columns are the same axes. If each rational entry initially has numerator/denominator at most 2^h, column clearing bounds |V_ij| by 2^((n+1)h). Fix a proved budget K=2^s for such a V. A future efficient theorem must instantiate s from the inherited one-attempt bound; the lemmas below charge it throughout. Alternatively one can increase s, but doing so does not prove an efficient optimizer or justify an unspecified stopping rule. No numerical value of the universal inherited polynomial is asserted here.

For space reconstruction, clear the n denominators separately in each row of rational L. The resulting integer row entries are at most 2^((n+1)h); their squares' plain quadratic coefficient vectors form an integer N-by-n basis W of D_L, N=n(n+1)/2, with entries at most 2^H_W, H_W=2(n+1)h+1. For any invertible row chart I, C=W(W_I)^-1. Cofactor/Hadamard bounds give numerators and denominators of every reduced chart entry at most 2^H_D, with the safe

    H_D=n H_W+(n+1)ceil(log2(n+1)).                    (1)

This bounds every nonsingular coordinate chart of the complete rational D_L. It makes no claim that D_0 or the optimizing metric is rational.

## 2. Stable greedy chart followed by exact rational reconstruction

Suppose a **rational** N-by-n matrix Y has full rank, satisfies

    ||Y^T Y-I||_op<=1/2,
    ||P_range(Y)-P_D||_op<=epsilon,

where D is the true n-dimensional rational space and its chart-entry denominator bound is Qden=2^H_D. The range-error premise must come from a separate certified construction; it is not fitted evidence.

Choose rows greedily. After k selected independent rows, project every row onto the orthogonal complement of their row span and select one with maximum squared residual. This is exact rational Gram–Schmidt (the normal equations suffice); no irrational orthonormal basis is required. The sum of residual squares is tr(P_remaining Y^T Y)>=(n-k)/2, so the selected residual square is at least (n-k)/(2N). Therefore the chosen n-by-n row matrix B obeys

    |det B|>=sqrt(n!)/(2N)^(n/2),  ||B||_op<2,
    sigma_min(B)>=tau:=2^(-2n) N^(-n).                (2)

The last rational tau is deliberately smaller than the determinant/product bound. Row selection takes O(N n^3) rational arithmetic, with minor heights polynomial in the bit size of Y and n.

Write Y=Uhat K0 with Uhat orthonormal and K0=(Y^T Y)^1/2; only this proof uses a square root. Principal-angle alignment gives an orthonormal true basis U with ||Uhat-U||_op<=sqrt(2)epsilon. Since ||K0||<2, Y0=U K0 differs from Y by at most 3epsilon. If epsilon<=tau/6, the same true row chart B0=(Y0)_I is nonsingular and ||B0^-1||<=2/tau. The true and observed charts satisfy

    ||Y B^-1-Y0 B0^-1||_op
      <=3epsilon/tau+12epsilon/tau^2
      <=15epsilon/tau^2.                             (3)

Consequently, for

    epsilon<=tau^2/(60 Qden^2),                      (4)

each exact rational computed chart entry is within 1/(4Qden^2) of its true entry. Distinct rationals of denominator at most Qden differ by at least 1/Qden^2. A nearest-rational/continued-fraction procedure therefore recovers each entry uniquely; the target is a convergent since its error is below 1/(2q^2). One can select the unique convergent in the radius 1/(3Qden^2), which contains the true entry and cannot contain two admissible rationals. All arithmetic takes polynomial bit time in the input chart bit size and log Qden. No exponential search over row minors or a primitive algebraic field for M_* is necessary for this conditional module.

This rounds **D_L**. Rounding D_0 or the individual optimal residual is invalid in general. A failure to supply the hypotheses or find the admissible convergents returns UNKNOWN. Independently check rank and the complete terminal quartic/QPD identity after reconstruction.

## 3. Explicit conditioning from a bounded correct primal frame

Clear input denominators by one positive common factor and write the resulting integer quartic as F, with S_f=sum |coeff F|>=1. This scaling is known. Let V be a correct integer primal frame, |V_ij|<=K, and put

    U=S_f(nK)^4,
    lambda=2^(-n)/(nU)^(n-1),
    rho=lambda/[8n(nK)^4],
    mstar=lambda/[n(nK)^2].                          (5)

The transformed quartic F(Vy) has coefficient l1 norm at most U. Its mass matrix Q' has half-integer entries of magnitude at most U, so det Q'>=2^-n, lambda_max(Q')<=nU, and lambda_min(Q')>=lambda. Since V is integer invertible, ||V||<=nK and |det V|>=1; its inverse L has sigma_min(L)>=1/(nK). These are bounds derived from the budget, not a supplied condition number.

Take hidden mixed Gram diagonal entries epsilon0=lambda/(2n) and mass block Q'-(epsilon0/2)(J-I). Its mass minimum is at least 3lambda/4, and the whole hidden plain-feature Gram has minimum at least epsilon0. The plain quadratic action of L has least singular value at least sigma_min(L)^2/2 (compare the plain and Frobenius orthonormal features). After transport, an observed positive Gram R0 for F has

    R0>=rho I.                                      (6)

No R0 is provided to the proposed learner. It is a witness for the derived margin. For t=min(1/(2n),rho/(4n)), M0=tI has ||m(M0)||^2=nt^2<=rho/16. The Schur residual is at least rho/2 and the block LMI is at least rho/8, by its triangular Schur factorization with inverse norm at most two. Thus the feasible affine slice has a quantitative interior point. An explicit equality-nullspace parameterization has rational entries at most two and a polynomial norm: choose one Gram entry per quartic coefficient and treat the remaining entries as free. Each quartic monomial has at most three quadratic pairings. Hence a Euclidean inner ball of radius rho divided by a polynomial in N exists in that slice, simultaneously with the metric margin t.

The Gaussian quartic functional gives a known outer bound. Its plain feature moment has diagonal block 2I+J and mixed block I, hence is at least I. Every feasible PSD G therefore satisfies

    tr G<=E F(g)<=3S_f,
    tr M<=n sqrt(S_f)<=nS_f,
    ||[[G,m],[m^T,1]]||_op<=3S_f+1.                 (7)

Gaussian expectation here is just a known coefficient functional, not sampled data. The optimal diagonal weights obey d_i=1/(np_i), p^TQ'p=1, so d_i>=sqrt(lambda)/n and d_i<=sqrt(q'_ii)<=sqrt(U). Thus M_*>=mstar I and ||M_*||<=U(nK)^(2n-2), using |det V|>=1 to bound ||V^-1||. In true whitening, the nonzero optimal residual Gram eigenvalues are at least lambda/U^2: Q_white 1=n1, its residual is Q_white-J, and its restriction to 1-perpendicular is Q_white>=lambda/U^2 I. All logarithms of these margins are polynomial in n,s and input bit size. This does not yet furnish an optimization routine.

## 4. Central barrier and finite-height certificate existence

For rational mu>0 maximize the interior function

    Phi_mu=logdet M+mu logdet S,
    S=G-m(M)m(M)^T,
    A(G)=F.

Strict feasible and outer bounds ensure an interior maximizer. Stationarity in G gives K=mu S^-1=A^*lambda0 for a quartic functional. Metric stationarity gives w=Km, where 2w^T m(H)=tr(M^-1 H). Scaling H=M yields m^T K m=n/2. Therefore

    lambda0(F)=tr(KG)=mu N+n/2,
    alpha=m^T K m=n/2,
    c=lambda0(F)+alpha=n+mu N.                      (8)

The dual block [[K,-w],[-w^T,alpha]] is PSD by a zero Schur complement. This is a checkable-gap mechanism, not an assumed approximate optimizer gap. Evaluating the same functional on the Slater Gram in (6) gives

    lambda_min(S)>=tauS:=mu rho/(mu N+n/2),
    lambda_min(K)>=tauK:=mu/(3S_f).                 (9)

If mu N<=1/32, its exact gap and the posterior metric interval give M>=tauM I, tauM=mstar/2. The full block also has an explicit margin min(tauS,1)/[4(1+3S_f)]. All these bounds have polynomial logarithmic size, including their dependence on 1/mu.

Here is a conservative **existence of rational certificates** statement, without an optimizer oracle. Define a rational

    C0=2+N+3S_f+1/mu+1/tauS+1/tauM+1/tauK,
    xi=mu/[10^6 C0^30].                             (10)

Round the central M's independent entries, the free affine Gram coordinates and lambda0's coefficients to a rational grid of mesh at most xi. Recompute Gram pivot coordinates exactly so A(G_c)=F. There are at most three pairings per quartic coefficient, so ||G_c-G||_op<=6N xi and ||M_c-M||_F<=2N xi. The induced Schur error is at most 14 C0^2 xi; (10) preserves M_c>0, S_c>0 and K_c=A^*lambda_c>0. Form w_c from the **exact inverse** of M_c and set alpha_c=w_c^T K_c^-1 w_c, so the dual block is exactly PSD.

The inverse perturbation identity bounds ||w_c-w|| by 4 C0^3 xi, ||K_c^-1-K^-1|| by 2 C0^3 xi, and the Schur scalar's perturbation by a safe 40 C0^8 xi. The functional evaluation error is at most S_f xi. Hence the exact rational certificate has

    0<=c_c-n<=2mu N.                               (11)

All rounded variables, exact inverses, Schur scalar and their common denominators have bit size polynomial in n,log K, input bit size and log(1/mu). The coarse exponent in (10) is intentional. This proves sufficiently precise rational **certificates exist** at derived bounded precision; it does not prove the proposer finds the central point or its rounding. A complete small-SDP learner still needs a proved conditioned optimization/separation routine and must charge its phase I, every bit operation and output failure.

## 5. Composition boundary and the finite prototype

The posterior range gate supplies a computed error for the top residual space in candidate whitening. In true whitening the relative quadratic feature action has condition at most (1-a)^-2. If its transferred residual-space error is eta and ||M_c-M_*||_*F<=b, with eta<=1/4 and b<=sqrt(n)/4, adding the metric gives an augmented-space error at most 4eta+2b/sqrt(n). To see this, project the metric coefficient A_0 off the approximate residual space. The perpendicular component has norm at least sqrt(n)sqrt(1-eta^2)-b; its leakage off D_L is at most b+eta(sqrt(n)+b). Combining this new normalized direction with the old space gives the displayed safe bound. The final feature transport to observed plain coordinates has condition at most 2 kappa(M_*), bounded by (5) and the derived upper metric norm. This reduces the range precision requirement to explicit quantities and (4), but a certified spectral/basis routine must still deliver the rational Y premise.
