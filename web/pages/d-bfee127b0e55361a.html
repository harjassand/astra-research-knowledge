## A. Polynomial critical sections with arbitrary source-only repair — PASS

Producer: `notes/hessian_bridge12/cycle18/ALL_SOURCE_REPAIR_SECTION_CRITERION.md`. Separate independent checker: charges_design9.

Let R=C[B], K=Frac(R), det JG=kappa!=0, and L=Z.G(B,t)+Q(B,t), with Q independent of Z. For every Q the full doubled Hessian determinant is (-1)^n kappa^2. This is a source determinant identity, not an elimination certificate.

Suppose Z.G_t(B,s)+Q_t(B,s)=sigma has s in R[Z]. The Keller column excludes s independent of Z. Put m=deg_Z(s)>=1 and d=deg_t(G_t). If d>=1, the two leading possible Z degrees are md+1 and me, where e=deg_t(Q_t). Their leading coefficients are nonzero in the domain K[Z]. Cancellation forces m=1 and e=d+1. Write s=s0+ell.Z. Varying Z in ker(ell) at fixed ell.Z forces every G_t(t) to be parallel to ell. Its nonconstant scalar factor then divides det JG in K[t], impossible. Thus G=h+t g. Now degree comparison gives Q_t=q1(B)t+q0(B), with q1 nonzero, and s=(sigma-Z.g-q0)/q1. Global polynomiality forces q1 to divide every g_i. A cofactor row of JG at t=0 proves the g_i generate the unit ideal of R. Hence q1 is a nonzero constant. These conditions conversely supply a global section.

For n=1 the same conclusion follows immediately from G_t=kappa and the degree of Q_t(s); no transverse dual direction is required. The result applies at one fixed level and then supplies all levels. Under the conditions, the reduced Hessian determinant of the critical value of L-sigma t is (-1)^n kappa^2/q1. Transporting a collision requires separate equal-section and equal-gradient checks.

### Rational-section extension — analytic PASS, producer addendum pending at this gate

Suppose instead s is rational over K(Z), satisfies the critical equation, and E=L(B,s,Z)-sigma s is globally polynomial. The envelope identity is E_Z=G(B,s). Any irreducible dual denominator of reduced s has negative valuation. For a nonconstant polynomial G_i(t), the highest power of s has uniquely lowest valuation, so G_i(s) would have a pole. At least one G_i is nonconstant by the Keller column. Hence s belongs to K[Z]. The field argument above then forces G affine and Q_t=q1(B)t+q0(B). The exact envelope is

    E=Z.h+Q(B,0)-(Z.g+q0-sigma)^2/(2q1).

Its squared dual coefficients imply q1 divides each g_i^2. Every irreducible divisor of q1 would divide every g_i, contradicting unimodularity. Therefore q1 is a constant unit and s is globally polynomial. This does not yet adopt the algebraic-section extension, whose polynomial-Luroth premise is being independently checked.

### Algebraic-section extension — analytic PASS using classical Luroth

Root independently derived and reconstructed the following extension, subsequently matched by bridge and charges. Work over K=C(B). Put E0=K(G_1(t),...,G_n(t)) inside K(t). Classical Luroth gives E0=K(r) for some rational r. To obtain the needed POLYNOMIAL generator over this nonclosed field, choose a nonconstant polynomial G_i=F_i(r). A geometric pole beta of F_i has a nonempty fibre under the rational map r:P1->P1. Since G_i has its only pole at source infinity, that entire fibre is infinity. Therefore beta=r(infinity) is K-rational. A K-Mobius transformation moving beta to infinity changes r into a polynomial h. No rational expression F_j(h) representing any other polynomial G_j can have a finite pole: the nonconstant polynomial h would pull it back to a finite pole. Consequently every G_j=H_j(h) with H_j polynomial.

The derivative h' divides every G_j'. The Keller cofactor identity makes that derivative column unimodular in K[t]. Characteristic zero therefore forces h' constant and nonzero, hence deg h=1 and E0=K(t). This is a consequence of old Luroth/common-composition theory, not a new field theorem.

Now let s be ANY algebraic critical branch over K(Z), and suppose its selected critical value E=L(B,s,Z)-sigma s is a global polynomial. Derivations extend uniquely in characteristic zero and the critical equation cancels the s derivatives, so E_Z=G(B,s). If s were algebraic over K alone, the algebraically independent Z coordinates would force G_t(B,s)=0, contradicting the Keller determinant. Thus s is transcendental over K. The rational recovery identity t=R(G(t)) from E0=K(t) evaluates at s with a NONZERO denominator: substitution of a transcendental element is an injection on K(t). It follows that s=R(E_Z) lies in K(Z). The preceding rational gate then proves s is globally polynomial and Q_t has constant nonzero curvature, with G affine in the eliminated coordinate.

Conclusion: even algebraic selected branches cannot escape the source-only repair criterion if the selected critical value must be globally polynomial. This adds no statement about multivalued nonpolynomial outputs. ## B. Explicit canonical quiver output height — PASS

Producer: `notes/hessian_check12/cycle18/LEXICOGRAPHIC_QUIVER_HEIGHT_OBSTRUCTION.md`. Independent alternate construction: `notes/metric_geometry10/cycle18/LEXICOGRAPHIC_OUTPUT_HEIGHT_INDEPENDENT_GATE.md`.

For a chronological meet/join programme of subspaces in K^d, give each vertex ambient K^d. A first positive frame is forced full. A fixed generator has its matrix arrow from the frame and negative priority. A join has arrows from its parents and negative priority; a meet has arrows to its parents and positive priority. Weights with absolute values (d+1)^(N-1-i) enforce strict chronological lexicographic optimization: changing one dimension digit by one outweighs every possible later change.

The extension argument is decisive. With a feasible prefix fixed, put every future negative vertex at K^d and every future meet at zero. A future join receives arbitrary earlier subspaces into K^d; a future meet sends zero into any earlier subspace. Between future vertices the same facts apply. No later operation constrains the prefix further. Thus at each digit the extremal dimension forces exactly the intended image, sum or intersection, uniquely. The whole optimizer is unique over every field.

Root separately reconstructed the producer's six projective operations. Starting A=(a,0,1), intersections with the diagonal, y-axis and infinity successively yield B=(a,a,1), C=(0,a,1), J=(1,-a,0). The line through A in direction J meets the y-axis at (0,a^2,1); two further transfers yield (a^2,0,1). All joins have dimension two and intersections dimension one because their distinguishing coefficients include units, including at a=0 and in characteristic two. Iterating from a=2 produces 2^(2^k).

The compiled representation has 9+12k vertices of dimension three, 8+24k arrows with entries in {0,1,2}, and weight bit length (9+12k)^2 before sign encoding. It is acyclic of directed depth at most two: non-frame identity arrows go from point vertices to plane vertices. Input encoding is O(k^2). Either normalized chart of the final rational line contains a=2^(2^k) or 1/a, each of bit length 2^k+1. Arbitrary rational rescaling cannot remove the total output cost, since the ratio of two output coordinates equals a.

Therefore a polynomial original-binary-input bound on an explicit rational optimizer basis is impossible for this unrestricted interface, even with uniqueness, all-field dimension agreement and rigidity. This does not obstruct a polynomial scalar support certificate, a modular optimizer, or a succinct arithmetic-circuit output. It does not contradict algorithms polynomial in numeric weights or certify all dimension-polytope objectives at once.

Concrete downstream change: stop seeking a universal polynomial height bound for this quiver interface. Require an explicit output-sensitive theorem or a declared succinct/modular output service. Retain the specialized programme-recognizing support evaluator as a control, not as a general quiver optimizer. Classical projective arithmetic and quiver universality remain prior; priority of this exact compiler is unassessed.
