# ASTRA Meta-Genesis: a sharp anisotropic quartic quasiconvexity theorem

Research date: 14 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane.

## Status

This investigation establishes an exact-certificate theorem for the one-parameter family

\[
 F_\ell(A)=u^2+v^2+4w^2+\frac72q^2+\ell q(u+v),
 \quad A=(x\mid y),\quad
 u=|x|^2,\ v=|y|^2,\ w=x\cdot y,\ q=\det A.
\]

It proves the sharp equivalence of quasiconvexity and rank-one convexity in this family. In particular, it proves quasiconvexity of the previously retained example with \(\ell=-29/10\), which remains non-polyconvex.

**The full nine-coefficient left-SO(2)-invariant quartic family and the general planar Morrey problem are not resolved here. Historical novelty and external proof review remain unestablished.** The certificate has been independently reconstructed and checked with exact rational polynomial arithmetic, root isolation, and rational interval bounds. It has not been elaborated in a proof assistant.

The heat-flow/exact-two-form framework is credited prior work of Bruno and Pasqualotto [1]. The specific anisotropic endpoint, its certificate, the sharpness calculation, and the consequences below were derived in this investigation. No unverified Astra/OpenAI repository theorem is a premise.

## 1. Main theorem

Let \(\kappa\) be the unique root in \((2.95,2.96)\) of

\[
 m(X)=48X^6-2545X^4+29712X^2-97344.
\]

More precisely,

\[
 2.95538664457927<\kappa<2.95538664457928.
\]

### Theorem A

For every real \(\ell\),

\[
 \boxed{F_\ell\text{ is quasiconvex}
 \iff F_\ell\text{ is rank-one convex}
 \iff |\ell|\le\kappa.}
\]

For \(|\ell|<\kappa\), every bounded Lipschitz domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\), every constant \(A_0\), and every \(\varphi\in W^{1,4}_0(\Omega;\mathbb R^2)\) satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_\Omega [F_\ell(A_0+D\varphi)-F_\ell(A_0)]
 \ge \frac{1-|\ell|/\kappa}{6}
       \int_\Omega |D\varphi|^4.}
\]

Every member with \(11/4<|\ell|\le\kappa\) is not polyconvex.

These are homogeneous quartics invariant under left SO(2) and under a quarter-turn of the input coordinates. They are not invariant under all input rotations: on a fixed rank-one output subspace their restriction is \(s^4+4s^2t^2+t^4\), rather than a multiple of \((s^2+t^2)^2\). The rank-one restriction is not the square of a quadratic, so an invertible input-coordinate change cannot turn an individual isotropic quartic into this family.

## 2. A compatible-jet identity

Let a smooth map \(f\) have first and second derivatives at a point

\[
 x=f_{x_1},\quad y=f_{x_2},\qquad
 X=f_{x_1x_1},\quad Y=f_{x_1x_2},\quad Z=f_{x_2x_2}.
\]

All five letters are vectors in \(\mathbb R^2\). Put

\[
 A=(x\mid y),\qquad B_1=(X\mid Y),\qquad B_2=(Y\mid Z).
\]

The shared vector \(Y\) is the differential compatibility condition. Define

\[
 z_+=\begin{pmatrix}
 x\cdot X\\x\cdot Z\\y\cdot Y\\x\times Y\\y\times X\\y\times Z
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 z_-=\begin{pmatrix}
 -y\cdot Z\\-y\cdot X\\-x\cdot Y\\y\times Y\\x\times Z\\x\times X
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here \(p\times q=p_1q_2-p_2q_1\). For six real parameters \(t_0,\ldots,t_5\), let

\[
 S(\ell,t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 12&10-t_0/2&25-t_0/2-t_4-3t_5&2\ell+t_3&-3\ell&-3\ell-t_2\\
 10-t_0/2&8&t_0/2+t_4+3t_5-9&2\ell+t_2&-\ell+t_1+t_2&-\ell\\
 25-t_0/2-t_4-3t_5&t_0/2+t_4+3t_5-9&t_0&t_1&t_2&t_3\\
 2\ell+t_3&2\ell+t_2&t_1&11-2t_5&1-t_4&t_4\\
 -3\ell&-\ell+t_1+t_2&t_2&1-t_4&7&t_5\\
 -3\ell-t_2&-\ell&t_3&t_4&t_5&4
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Define three linear one-forms on matrix space by

\[
 \theta=x\times dx+y\times dy,\qquad
 \zeta=x\cdot dy-y\cdot dx,\qquad
 \theta_a=x\times dx-y\times dy.
\]

Here \(dx,dy\) are coordinate differentials of the columns, not spatial derivatives. Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_1&=-\frac{6\ell+t_1+3t_2-t_3}{4},&
 c_2&=-\frac{t_0+4t_5-20}{2},\\
 c_3&=\frac{20-t_0}{4},&
 c_4&=\ell-t_1-t_2,\\
 c_5&=\frac{4\ell+t_1+3t_2+t_3}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The required cubic primitive is the compact, explicit one-form

\[
 \boxed{\alpha=c_1(u+v)\theta+c_2q\theta
                +c_3(u+v)\zeta+c_4q\zeta
                +c_5(u-v)\theta_a.}
\]

The last term is input-anisotropic. It is invariant under a quarter-turn, but not under arbitrary input rotations. The first four types occur in the prior isotropic framework [1]; the additional direction retains the anisotropy needed for the present construction. This is an extension of that framework, not a claim to have invented heat-flow correction by exact forms.

### Lemma B: exact differential correction

For every real \(\ell,t\), every matrix \(A\), and every compatible pair \(B_1,B_2\) as above,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{i=1}^2 D^2F_\ell(A)[B_i,B_i]
 +(d\alpha)_A(B_1,B_2)
 =z_+^TS(\ell,t)z_++z_-^TS(\ell,t)z_-.
 }
\]

This is a polynomial identity, not an inequality or an SDP feasibility assertion. Direct expansion proves it. The standalone verifier differentiates both the quartic and the displayed one-form and verifies the identity symbolically with \(\ell,t_0,\ldots,t_5\) still independent. The parameter \(t_4\) changes the Gram representation without changing the polynomial one-form. The verifier also checks that this compact primitive has exactly the same exterior derivative as an independently acquired coordinate primitive.

## 3. The exact endpoint certificate

In the remainder of this section put \(\ell=\kappa\). Choose

\[
\begin{aligned}
t_0&=\frac{254064\kappa^4-12066397\kappa^2+87457992}{107712},\\
t_1&=\frac{\kappa(2551152\kappa^4-120422989\kappa^2+869186184)}{4582656},\\
t_2&=-\frac{\kappa(30008208\kappa^4-1412052067\kappa^2+10194361080)}{50409216},\\
t_3&=\frac{\kappa(2383152\kappa^4-111980897\kappa^2+796906920)}{3877632},\\
t_4&=\frac{64560\kappa^4-3046321\kappa^2+22009128}{71808},\\
t_5&=-\frac{641424\kappa^4-30344323\kappa^2+215742840}{646272}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let \(S_*=S(\kappa,t)\). Partition it into a top-left \(2\times2\) block \(C\), top-right block \(B\), and bottom-right \(4\times4\) block \(T\):

\[
 S_*=\begin{pmatrix}C&B\\B^T&T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}
 t_0&t_1&t_2&t_3\\
 t_1&11-2t_5&1-t_4&t_4\\
 t_2&1-t_4&7&t_5\\
 t_3&t_4&t_5&4
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

### Lemma C

The matrix \(T\) is positive definite, and

\[
 (\det T)C=B\operatorname{adj}(T)B^T.
\]

Consequently \(S_*\succeq0\), with rank exactly four.

### Exact verification of Lemma C

Work in the degree-six number field \(\mathbb Q[\kappa]\), reducing polynomial expressions by \(m(\kappa)=0\). The four entries of the displayed Schur-complement identity reduce to zero.

For the leading principal minors \(\Delta_j=\det T_{1:j,1:j}\), exact rational Horner interval arithmetic on

\[
 I=\left[\frac{295538664457927}{10^{14}},
          \frac{295538664457928}{10^{14}}\right]
\]

gives the following enclosures. The decimal endpoints in this display are terminating rationals and are deliberately wider than the computed bounds:

\[
\begin{aligned}
13.44712&<\Delta_1<13.44713,\\
73.04076&<\Delta_2<73.04077,\\
58.26760&<\Delta_3<58.26761,\\
28.91777&<\Delta_4<28.91779.
\end{aligned}
\]

The interval \(I\) contains exactly one root of \(m\); a rational Sturm count also identifies it as the unique root in \((2.95,2.96)\). All four minors are strictly positive, so Sylvester's criterion gives \(T\succ0\). The Schur complement vanishes, hence

\[
 S_*=\begin{pmatrix}BT^{-1}\\I_4\end{pmatrix}
 T\begin{pmatrix}T^{-1}B^T&I_4\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

## 4. From the certificate to all compatible oscillations

This section proves the analytic implication, rather than assuming gradient-moment completion.

Choose a square flat torus \(Q\), a smooth periodic vector function \(\varphi\), and a constant matrix \(A_0\). Set

\[
 \varphi_t=e^{t\Delta}\varphi,\qquad
 A_t=A_0+D\varphi_t.
\]

The matrix field \(A_t\) is periodic and remains a gradient of an affine-plus-periodic map. In particular, its derivative pair has the compatible form \((X\mid Y),(Y\mid Z)\). Define

\[
 \mathcal F(t)=\int_Q [F_\kappa(A_t)-F_\kappa(A_0)].
\]

Differentiation and periodic integration by parts give

\[
 -\mathcal F'(t)
 =\int_Q\sum_{i=1}^2D^2F_\kappa(A_t)[\partial_iA_t,\partial_iA_t].
\]

The pullback of an exact form is exact, so

\[
 \int_Q(d\alpha)_{A_t}(\partial_1A_t,\partial_2A_t)
 =\int_Qd(A_t^*\alpha)=0.
\]

Combining Lemmas B and C therefore yields

\[
 -\mathcal F'(t)=\int_Q
    (z_+(t)^TS_*z_+(t)+z_-(t)^TS_*z_-(t))\ge0.
\]

The nonconstant periodic heat modes decay, so \(D\varphi_t\to0\) smoothly as \(t\to\infty\) and \(\mathcal F(t)\to0\). Hence the following positive representation holds:

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_Q[F_\kappa(A_0+D\varphi)-F_\kappa(A_0)]
 =\int_0^\infty\!\int_Q
      (z_+^TS_*z_++z_-^TS_*z_-)\,dx\,dt\ge0.}
\]

Equivalently, each quadratic term on the right is the squared norm of a fixed linear transformation of the six bilinear compatible-jet quantities. The rank-four factorization in Lemma C supplies that transformation explicitly.

This controls arbitrary smooth periodic gradients, not a selected family of waves or an arbitrary law on matrices. Resonant interactions are included without a nonresonance assumption.

For a smooth compactly supported perturbation in a bounded domain, extend it by zero into a larger periodic square and apply the identity there. At the initial time the relative energy outside the original domain is zero. Finally, approximate any \(\varphi\in W^{1,4}_0\) in the \(W^{1,4}\) norm by smooth compactly supported maps. Quartic growth and Hölder's inequality make the integrals continuous under this approximation. Thus \(F_\kappa\) is quasiconvex.

An output reflection changes \(q\) to \(-q\) and leaves \(u,v,w\) unchanged. Therefore \(F_{-\kappa}\) is quasiconvex as well. For \(|\ell|\le\kappa\),

\[
 F_\ell=\frac{1+\ell/\kappa}{2}F_\kappa
       +\frac{1-\ell/\kappa}{2}F_{-\kappa},
\]

so the whole interval is quasiconvex.

## 5. The threshold is necessary

The acoustic matrix for the rank-one direction \(e_1\otimes(1,k)\) is

\[
 M_\ell(k)=\begin{pmatrix}
 12+8k^2&16k&-2\ell k&\ell(3+k^2)\\
 16k&8+12k^2&-\ell(1+3k^2)&2\ell k\\
 -2\ell k&-\ell(1+3k^2)&4+7k^2&k\\
 \ell(3+k^2)&2\ell k&k&7+4k^2
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(a_A=(a,b,c,d)^T\), direct differentiation gives

\[
 D^2F_\ell(A)[e_1\otimes(1,k),e_1\otimes(1,k)]
 =a_A^TM_\ell(k)a_A.
\]

The origin of the defining polynomial is also explicit. Put \(v_\ell=\ell^2\) and \(z=k^2/(1+k^2)^2\). Then

\[
 \frac{\det M_\ell(k)}{(1+k^2)^4}
 =3(32-v_\ell)(28-3v_\ell)
 +(504v_\ell-5952)z
 +768(v_\ell-5/2)z^2.
\]

The discriminant of this quadratic in \(z\) is

\[
 -576(48v_\ell^3-2545v_\ell^2+29712v_\ell-97344).
\]

The relevant double zero at \(\ell=\kappa\) has

\[
 z_* =\frac{248-21\kappa^2}{64\kappa^2-160}\in(0,1/4).
\]

Let \(j\in(0,1)\) be the smaller positive root of \(z_*(1+j)^2=j\), and put \(k=\sqrt j\). To supply an explicit nonzero null vector without relying on a determinant sign alone, solve

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 12+8j&16j&-2j\\
 16&8+12j&-(1+3j)\\
 -2\kappa^2&-\kappa^2(1+3j)&4+7j
 \end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\rho\\\sigma\\\chi\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}-3-j\\-2\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This matrix is invertible. Its determinant is

\[
 -12[(6\kappa^2-56)j^3+(\kappa^2-4)j^2
       +(4\kappa^2-40)j+\kappa^2-32]>216,
\]

using \(8<\kappa^2<9\) and \(0<j<1\).

Define

\[
 A_* =\begin{pmatrix}\kappa\rho&k\kappa\sigma\\k\chi&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Then \(M_\kappa(k)\operatorname{vec}(A_*)=0\). The first three equations are the displayed linear system; the fourth follows by reducing its numerator using

\[
 m(\kappa)=0,\qquad
 (248-21\kappa^2)(1+j)^2-(64\kappa^2-160)j=0.
\]

The verifier checks that exact polynomial reduction. Approximate values, used only for orientation, are

\[
 k\approx0.5048513584,\qquad
 A_*\approx\begin{pmatrix}-0.8778893063&0.2029501591\\-0.3572039876&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

At \(\ell=0\), \(M_0(k)\) splits into two positive-definite blocks. Their determinants are

\[
 96(k^2-1/4)^2+90>0,
 \qquad 28+64k^2+28k^4>0.
\]

Thus \(E_0=\operatorname{vec}(A_*)^TM_0(k)\operatorname{vec}(A_*)>0\). Since the acoustic expression is affine in \(\ell\) and vanishes at \(\kappa\),

\[
 \operatorname{vec}(A_*)^TM_\ell(k)\operatorname{vec}(A_*)
 =E_0(1-\ell/\kappa)<0\qquad(\ell>\kappa).
\]

Rank-one convexity fails for every \(\ell>\kappa\), and an output reflection handles \(\ell<-\kappa\). Since quasiconvexity implies rank-one convexity, this proves the necessity and completes the sharp classification.

## 6. Quantitative coercivity and non-polyconvexity

Set \(N(A)=|A|^4=(u+v)^2\) and

\[
 H_3(A)=(u+v)^2+4w^2
       =\frac{|x+y|^4+|x-y|^4}{2}.
\]

The exact identity

\[
 F_0=\frac12N+\frac12H_3+\frac32q^2
\]

shows that \(F_0-N/2\) is polyconvex: \(H_3\) is convex and \(q^2\) is a convex function of the determinant.

For \(0\le a=|\ell|/\kappa<1\), decompose

\[
 F_\ell=(1-a)F_0+aF_{\operatorname{sgn}(\ell)\kappa}.
\]

It follows that \(F_\ell-\frac{1-a}{2}N\) is quasiconvex. For any Euclidean vectors \(A,B\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |A+B|^4-|A|^4-4|A|^2A\!:\!B
 ={}&\frac13|B|^4+6(A\!:\!B+|B|^2/3)^2\\
 &+2(|A|^2|B|^2-(A\!:\!B)^2)
 \ge\frac13|B|^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

After integration the linear term vanishes, proving the quantitative bound in Theorem A.

For the retained example \(F_*=F_{-29/10}\), this gives

\[
 \int_\Omega[F_*(A_0+D\varphi)-F_*(A_0)]
 \ge c_*\int_\Omega|D\varphi|^4,
 \quad c_*=\frac{1-(29/10)/\kappa}{6}
 \approx0.00312348553338.
\]

For non-polyconvexity let \(h=|\ell|>11/4\) and, after an output reflection if needed, use \(F_{-h}\). With \(I=\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\), \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\),

\[
 F_{-h}(I)=11/2-2h<0,\qquad F_{-h}(J)=11/2+2h.
\]

For sufficiently large \(t\), put mass \(1/[2(1+t^2)]\) at each of \(tI,-tI\), and mass \(t^2/[2(1+t^2)]\) at each of \(J,-J\). The mean matrix and mean determinant are both zero, while its mean energy equals

\[
 \frac{t^2}{1+t^2}[t^2(11/2-2h)+(11/2+2h)]<0.
\]

This contradicts Jensen for any convex representation in \((A,\det A)\), proving non-polyconvexity. This distribution is used only to disprove polyconvexity; it is not asserted to be a compatible gradient law. The actual gradient inequality has already been proved in Section 4.

At \(h=29/10,t=7\), this is exactly the prior witness with probabilities \(1/100,1/100,49/100,49/100\) and expected energy \(-833/250\).

## 7. A limited extension beyond symmetry

This is a stability corollary, not a solution for general quartics.

Write an arbitrary homogeneous quartic perturbation in the four matrix entries as \(P(A)=\sum_{|\beta|=4}p_\beta A^\beta\), with \(\|p\|_1=\sum|p_\beta|\). Direct differentiation of a degree-four monomial gives

\[
 |D^2P(A)[B,B]|\le12\|p\|_1|A|^2|B|^2.
\]

Let \(\eta_*=(1-(29/10)/\kappa)/2=3c_*\). Since

\[
 D^2N(A)[B,B]=8(A\!:\!B)^2+4|A|^2|B|^2,
\]

\(\eta_*N+P\) is convex whenever \(\|p\|_1\le\eta_*/3=c_*\). The already established quasiconvexity of \(F_*-\eta_*N\) then gives quasiconvexity of \(F_*+P\). No rotational symmetry of \(P\) is needed.

Moreover, \((F_*+P)(I)\le-3/10+c_*<0\), so the same scaling-and-minor argument proves non-polyconvexity. Thus the proof also yields an explicit neighborhood of non-polyconvex quasiconvex quartics in all 35 coefficient directions. It does not classify the full quartic cone.

## 8. Why this is not an input-coordinate relabelling of known isotropic energies

The following auxiliary separation concerns a specified positive-mixture construction only. It is not a computational lower bound and does not replace the quasiconvexity proof above.

Consider the known rotationally invariant boundary energies

\[
 W_t(A)=|A|^4+4t|A|^2\det A+(6t^2-2)(\det A)^2,
 \quad |t|\le1,
\]

and their invertible input-coordinate transforms \(W_t(AS)\). Include arbitrary positive multiples, positive finite sums, and nonnegative determinant-square terms. The retained \(F_*\) is not in this cone.

To prove this, put

\[
 X=(s,r,w',d')=(u+v,u-v,2w,2q),\quad
 J_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1),\quad X^TJ_0X=0.
\]

Every transformed generator has the form

\[
 (v^TX)^2-\tfrac12(v^TJ_0v)(d')^2,
\]

where \(v^TJ_0v\ge0\). Therefore every positive sum under consideration has a representation

\[
 X^TZ X-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(J_0Z)(d')^2+\delta(d')^2,
 \qquad Z\succeq0,\quad\delta\ge0.
\]

In these variables,

\[
 F_* =\tfrac12s^2+\tfrac12r^2+(w')^2+\tfrac78(d')^2-\tfrac{29}{20}s d'.
\]

Quadratic polynomials representing the same function on the light cone differ by a multiple of \(X^TJ_0X\). Consequently a necessary representation would have, for some \(\tau\),

\[
 Z_{00}=\tfrac12+\tau,\quad Z_{11}=\tfrac12-\tau,
 \quad Z_{22}=1-\tau,\quad Z_{03}=-\tfrac{29}{40},
 \quad Z_{33}=\tfrac14+\tfrac\tau3-\tfrac{2\delta}{3}.
\]

Positivity forces \(-1/2\le\tau\le1/2\). The principal \((0,3)\) minor would require

\[
 \frac{841}{1600}
 \le (\tfrac12+\tau)Z_{33}
 \le(\tfrac12+\tau)(\tfrac14+\tfrac\tau3)
 \le\frac5{12},
\]

which is false. This proves the claimed exclusion. It does not rule out all other uses of known quasiconvex energies, general output-coordinate transformations, or unrelated sufficient criteria.

## 9. How equality cases supplied the constructive step

The successful discovery step was to impose acoustic equality cases on a candidate global positive representation.

For a plane-wave second derivative \(H=a\otimes b\otimes b\), the two derivatives of the gradient are proportional:

\[
 B_1=b_1(a\otimes b),\quad B_2=b_2(a\otimes b).
\]

Every two-form vanishes on this pair. If also

\[
 D^2F(A)[a\otimes b,a\otimes b]=0,
\]

then any positive corrected heat representation must vanish on the corresponding bilinear jet vector. Its positive Gram matrix must annihilate that vector.

At the endpoint above, the critical acoustic jet provides two independent kernel constraints for the scalar six-vector representation. Independence follows directly from the last two coordinates of the two kernel vectors: their determinant is proportional to $DC(1-j^2)$, where $D>216$ is the three-by-three determinant in Section 5 and $C=-24[\kappa^2(j-1)^2+4j^2-2j+4]<0$ is its third Cramer numerator. Restricting the correction to that forced rank-four face turns the remaining construction into exact linear equations over \(\mathbb Q[\kappa]\). The resulting matrix has a strictly positive four-dimensional block and a vanishing Schur complement, as proved above.

This is an application of elementary PSD kernel constraints and facial reduction, not a claim to have invented those general facts. Its demonstrated value here is that local equality cases determined the correction that proves a sharp global theorem. A stronger inequality guessed away from the acoustic equality set was not needed.

## 10. What was not established

The entire left-SO(2) family still permits unequal coefficients of \(qu\) and \(qv\), a \(qw\) term, and more general reflection-even terms. The proof here does not show that every rank-one-convex choice in that cone has a compatible heat correction, nor does it prove universal nine-moment completion.

Exploratory computations examined scalar and tensor-valued corrections in the larger quarter-turn sector, as well as a proposed universal positive linear transfer from acoustic blocks. Some tensor searches closed numerical gaps left by scalar searches on other sampled inputs. A universal transfer was not obtained. Numerical residuals do not prove infeasibility or separation of those correction cones. Crucially, the final sharp slice does **not** require the tensor terms: the exact scalar certificate in Sections 2–3 suffices. Tensor necessity for this slice would have been an incorrect conclusion.

The strongest preserved extension mechanism is to construct compatible corrections on the faces forced by all acoustic equality cases in the full left-SO(2) cone, allowing a changed heat metric or tensor components when necessary. The distinct counterexample direction remains a genuinely compatible resonant Fourier construction with exact moments and a separately certified rank-one-convex integrand. No such counterexample was produced here.

No repository write was performed. The selective GitHub searches did not supply a directly applicable additional theorem. The fixed-size acoustic and moment-completion representations from the prior report were research aids; neither is assumed to provide the missing global positivity.

## 11. Reproducibility and provenance

The principal proof is self-contained after standard heat-semigroup calculus, Stokes' theorem, elementary polynomial algebra, and the implication quasiconvexity => rank-one convexity. Its decisive finite identities are fully explicit above.

Run:

```bash
python verify_sharp_scalar.py
python verify_explicit.py
```

The first script is the principal standalone endpoint verifier. It imports SymPy only; all constants and the universal one-form are written in the script. It independently verifies the root, the PSD Schur certificate, the universal compatible-jet identity, the acoustic null witness, the differentiated acoustic matrix, and the interior/non-polyconvexity calculations.
