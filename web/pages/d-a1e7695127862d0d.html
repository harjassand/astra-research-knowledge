## Reconstructed nonvanishing argument

Set F=(1/2)M^(-1) grad f and D=F dot grad. In hidden coordinates D y_i=y_i H_i(y^2), so each coordinate ideal is invariant. The extactic E=det[D^k x_i] has homogeneous degree d=n+(h-1)n(n-1), if nonzero; every hidden linear form divides it by changing the column basis to contain that form.

Choose a vertex j of maximal H(e_j). For each i!=j, the differentiable strictly convex function phi(t)=H((1-t)e_j+t e_i) has phi'(0)<phi(1)-phi(0)<=0. The strict first inequality follows because equality of an endpoint secant and the initial supporting slope would force equality with the supporting affine function throughout the segment, contradicting strict convexity. Therefore H_i(e_j)-H_j(e_j)<0. No positivity of H(e_j) or H_j(e_j) is used.

For ratios r_i=y_i/y_j and time ds/dt=y_j^(2h-2), the autonomous ratio equation is r_i'=r_i[H_i(r^2,1)-H_j(r^2,1)]. A small cube around zero has every bracket uniformly negative and is invariant. Its solutions decay exponentially. Meanwhile (log y_j)'=H_j(r^2,1), which is bounded in that cube, so y_j is positive and finite at each finite s. It may tend to zero as s tends to infinity; division by y_j at each finite s remains legitimate. Thus a nonempty open cone approaches the j-axis projectively even when the maximizing vertex has negative value.

Induct on dimension. Strict convexity restricts to every positive-dimensional face. The one-coordinate base case is E=x_1 and does not require a nonvacuous strict-convexity assertion. The minor W formed from all coordinates except j and derivative rows 0,...,n-2 restricts to the nonzero lower-dimensional extactic on y_j=0. Hence W is not the zero polynomial.

If E=0, Cramer's rule over the real rational-function field yields y_j=sum c_i y_i and the corresponding derivative identities through row n-2. Determinant vanishing gives row n-1. Differentiating and subtracting successive identities gives W(Dc)=0, hence Dc_i=0. Pick a point in the attracting cone where W!=0. Such a point exists because a nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on an open set. The c_i are constant along the local trajectory. The identity y_j(s)=sum c_i(initial)y_i(s) then continues to every finite s by analyticity of the ratio/scale ODE. This uses fixed finite coefficients, not evaluations of rational representatives at later poles. Divide by y_j(s) and take s to infinity: 1=0. Therefore E!=0.

This reconstruction confirms the sensitive points: restriction is applied only to the polynomial minor, not rational first integrals; no distinct-rate hypothesis is needed; negative H_j is harmless; and the proof does not import a plane-only extactic theorem into higher dimensions.

## Complete candidate coverage and sound acceptance

    [d^(e-1)/dt^(e-1) grad E(u+tv)]_(t=r)

Thus repeated factors are handled and all true normals enter a pool of at most d vectors. Extras are permitted. Convert a normal c to w=M^(-1)c and score f(w)/(w^T M w)^h.
