# Adaptive auxiliary primes: independently reconstructed mechanism

Primary source: family020, Section3, Theorem3.1 and Lemmas3.2–3.4, pinned commit adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a. The manuscript was read throughout. This note checks the deletion mechanism and explains its interface; it does not externally validate the entire squarefree-values theorem.

## A general finite lemma

Let P be a finite pool of N tags, and associate to each subset S⊆P an integer potential R(S) with 0≤R(S)≤B. Assume R is nonincreasing under adding a tag. Suppose an associated certificate C(S) obeys the deletion stability condition

R(S)=R(S\{q}) ⇒ C(S)=C(S\{q}).

For every certificate C, let Bad(C)⊆P have cardinality at most b. Choose t uniformly from the T consecutive positive sizes ℓ,…,ℓ+T−1, with ℓ+T−1≤N. Choose S uniformly among t-subsets, and then q uniformly in S. Then

P(q∈Bad(C(S))) ≤ B/T + b/(N−ℓ−T+2).

Proof. Set F(t)=E R(S_t) for a uniform t-subset. Deleting a random member from a uniform t-subset leaves a uniform (t−1)-subset. If the record changes, its nonnegative integer increase is at least one. Therefore

P(record changes | t) ≤ F(t−1)−F(t).

Averaging over t telescopes and bounds this probability by B/T. Conditional on the deleted subset, q is uniform in its complement, of size N−t+1. Its chance of belonging to the deleted certificate's bad set is at most b/(N−t+1). If the record is stable, the full and deleted certificates agree. Thus the full bad event is contained in the union of the record-change event and the deleted-certificate bad event. No conditioning on stability is used in the latter bound. This proves the estimate.

## How the manuscript meets this interface

At each cutting stage, retain the entire bounded-degree space K_i of polynomials vanishing on the current simultaneous residue class. Adding a prime shrinks the class, so it enlarges K_i and cannot increase its evaluation codimension ρ_i. Until the first changed codimension, nested equal-dimensional kernels agree, so deterministic patch construction gives identical preceding patches. Encode the complete rank vector lexicographically by

R=Σ_i ρ_i ∏_{j>i}(M_j+1), with M_i=binom(N_ambient+D_i,N_ambient).

The resulting integer record is bounded by B₀=∏_i(M_i+1). Equality of full and deleted records gives equality of every kernel, patch, and evaluated Jacobian minor. These minors form C(S).

Lemma3.2 is indispensable: even if the original variety's coefficients are unbounded, evaluation on the finite integer point set produces controlled equations or a proper smaller-dimensional cut. The minor product is a nonzero integer of size at most X^C. A prime pool in [X^ζ,C_rX^ζ] therefore meets its prime divisors at most C/ζ+O(1) times. The finite lemma yields Lemma3.4's B₀/T₀+o(1) bound because pool cardinality tends to infinity while all block sizes and ζ are fixed.

Taking expectations over all points, applying Markov to the bad fraction in each block, and a union bound over r blocks yields one common block choice regular for at least half the point set. This is common-prime selection for an adaptively evolving geometry; it does not require the selected primes to be independent of the later minors.

## From regularity to a determinant count

For an h-dimensional patch with weighted Hilbert lower bound

H_A(T) ≥ T^h/(h!δ_h^h) − C_e T^(h−1),

choose L independent coordinate monomials. Their total weight is at most δ_h s_h(L)+O(L), where s_h(L) is the least sum of total degrees of L distinct monomials in h variables. A simultaneous residue class good at prime product Q has every evaluation determinant divisible by Q^s_h(L). Its archimedean bound is X^(δ_h s_h(L)+O(L)). If Q≥X^(δ_h+ν), first fix L large enough and then X large; every determinant is zero, giving a proper bounded-degree cut.

The scheduled cumulative prime thresholds are E_h=max_{h≤s≤r}δ_s. The block increments a_i=E_{h_i}−E_{h_i+1} are nonnegative. Counting residue classes branches with exponent h_i a_i, and summing telescopes to Σ_h E_h. Singular classes contribute controlled losses via regularity. This reconstructs the structural content of Theorem3.1:

|S| ≪_ε X^(E₁+…+E_r+ε),

provided the Hilbert alternative or uniformly small-count alternative holds for **every** bounded-degree rational subvariety encountered, not merely the initial variety.

## Costs, obstruction, and novelty

The dimension, cut degrees, rank budget B₀, block sizes T₀, and ζ are fixed before X grows. B₀ is a product of monomial-space dimensions and can be enormous. The lemma is a mathematical common-selection principle; it does not provide a practical polynomial-time reconstruction algorithm. Given a concrete finite point set, exact ranks and candidate-prime divisibility can be checked, but the cost of finding all algebraic patches is not accounted for by the asymptotic exponent alone.

The load-bearing stability condition cannot be replaced by a dimension count without nestedness and canonical patch choices. Likewise a polynomial-height witness must be nonzero: a zero minor is divisible by every auxiliary prime. The deletion proof itself does not establish the weighted Hilbert hypotheses needed later.

Nearest prior art identified in the manuscript: Heath-Brown's local p-adic determinant principle; Salberger's ordered Hilbert/divisibility estimates and simultaneous congruence conditions. Their full sources were not read in this investigation. The abstract finite lemma is a bounded-potential leave-one-out argument and is not claimed novel. The scientific candidate is its use to prevent adaptively constructed determinant cuts from exhausting the available good primes.
