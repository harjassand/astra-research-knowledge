## Mathematical composition

The input is an expanded rational homogeneous quartic f with some hidden rational L invertible and Q>0 such that f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2). Compute H=Hess f, h=det H, F=adj(H)grad h. The identities H_(f∘L)=L^T H_f(Lx)L and F_(f∘L)=det(L)^4 L^{-1}F_f(Lx) are correct, including singular-Hessian points since the identities are polynomial. Hidden sign flips therefore make their linear coordinate forms Darboux factors. If E=det[D^k x_i] is nonzero, its degree is n+2n(n-1)^2 and it contains every hidden coordinate hyperplane.

Nonvanishing for diagonal positive Q follows directly: z_i=q_i y_i^4 all have the same strictly positive derivative on the positive orthant. Locally z_i=a_i+s. The ordinary Wronskian of q_i^(-1/4)(a_i+s)^(1/4) is a nonzero Vandermonde times nonzero falling-factorial and coordinate factors when the a_i are distinct. Changing to D-time multiplies the determinant by a nonzero triangular factor. Thus nonvanishing holds for every diagonal Q, and polynomial dependence on Q makes it a proper Zariski-open condition. This argument does not exclude exceptional positive-definite Q.

## Universal identifiability — a further independently checked deduction

The worker proposed the following useful strengthening; root reconstructs it here. For the full QPD class, the unordered primal coordinate rays are unique among all real invertible coordinatewise-even representations with positive-definite squared contrast, up to scaling and permutation.

Suppose f has two such frames. The sign-reflection groups of both preserve the same coercive f. The full real f-stabilizer is compact; average a positive form over the compact closure of the generated group to obtain a common real SPD metric M. Invariance under each frame's sign flips makes M diagonal in each frame. After normalizing both frames with this common metric, each becomes an orthonormal basis, and the respective squared-contrast matrices remain PD by positive diagonal congruence.

On the common M-unit sphere, strict simplex convexity says the global maximizers are exactly those axes whose diagonal contrast entry is maximal, with both signs. The maximizing set is intrinsic to f and M, so the two bases share at least one axis. Restrict f and M to its common orthogonal complement. Both remaining frames still have PD principal contrasts. Induction on dimension shows every remaining axis is common as well. Dimension one is immediate.

This proves uniqueness, although the auxiliary averaging metric need not be rational and is not constructed. It also implies every real linear f-automorphism permutes the unique primal rays, with allowed scalings. In particular the final exact even-QPD representation verifier certifies the original unordered rays even on a lucky bad-line trial; no latent ground truth is required for that conclusion. This uniqueness argument is restricted to positive-definite quartic contrasts; do not generalize it automatically to arbitrary homogeneous H merely strictly convex on one simplex.
