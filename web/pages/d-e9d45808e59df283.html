## 1. Direct-envelope stripe recursion, with a C3 error ledger

## 5. Stable backward maps and differentiated branch defects

Both tail branches reach r_- under backward radial integration. The negative branch's actual gradient orientation is outward, and the positive branch's is inward. The geometry of each branch is still the same backward solution from its phase gate. In the tail, F_r has a fixed nonzero sign and |F_r|>=c aE. Introduce v=a(y-y_c(r)).     v'=a beta(r,v)v, beta+v partial_v beta>=c>0,
    |partial_r beta|<=C eta/R_j, |v_gate|<=2rho+O(eta).

Thus for u=a(h-r), the pullback V(u;h) solves

    partial_u V=-beta(h-u/a,V)V,
    |V|<=C(rho+eta)exp(-cu),
    |partial_h V|<=C[eta/R_j+(rho+eta)eta u/R_j]exp(-cu). (24)

The second estimate follows by differentiating this stable equation: its homogeneous coefficient is beta+v beta_v>=c, its forcing is -beta_r V, and its gate derivative is -a y_c'(h)=O(eta/R_j). Write f_c(r,v)=-Delta G/G_r at y=y_c(r)+v/a and set Phi_c=f_c-g_c. From the exact coefficients in (19),

## Appendix: nominal-crest derivatives without a hidden fast shift

This appendix spells out the parameter estimate in (22). In the virtual family use the relative phase theta=a(y-t_c) as an independent coordinate. The exact coefficient functions C,D in (19) have NO fast physical t factor. After writing

    mu_c=t_c/r, delta=1/(ar), q_K=K^2/r^2,

their kernel integrands, their normalized numerator Delta G/(a^2 E), and their normalized denominator G_r/(aE) extend smoothly in (mu_c,delta,q_K,theta) around (0,0,0,0). For example the apparently singular exponent is

    -(1/delta)[sqrt(1+q_K+(mu_c+delta theta)^2
                             -2sigma delta[u_1+(mu_c+delta theta)u_2]
                             +sigma^2 delta^2|u|^2)
                   -sqrt(1+q_K+(mu_c+delta theta)^2)],

These exact smooth functions can be differentiated under the integral. Physical slow t_c differentiation at fixed theta is r^(-1)partial_(mu_c); it never differentiates cos(theta) with a factor a. Radial derivatives act through r^(-1) times these dimensionless variables. On this family mu_c=O(eta), delta=O(eta^2), q_K=o(eta^3), with every fixed derivative of the origin coefficients retained.

The implicit equation G_t=0 has derivative in theta bounded away from zero. The reflection (mu_c,theta)->(-mu_c,-theta) makes its root odd. Taylor expansion and the first coefficient in (20) therefore yield

    theta_crit=-(1-h_sigma)mu_c+e_theta,
    |partial_r^j partial_(t_c)^l e_theta|
          <=C eta^3 R_j^(-j)L^(-l), j<=2,l<=1,       (A1)
    |theta_crit'|<=C eta/R_j,
    |partial_(t_c)theta_crit'|<=C/R_j^2.

The terms producing e_theta are of weighted order at least three: mu_c^3, mu_c delta, and smaller origin products. Terms of order delta or mu_c^2 alone cannot enter this odd root. This gives (A1) directly by the chain rule on the smooth kernel coefficients.

Define B0(r,theta,t_c)=Gtc_t/Gtc_r with theta held fixed. For |theta|<=2rho0 it has a B0_theta lower bound c>0 and all required derivatives uniformly bounded. Define the invariant relative phase by

    theta_c'=a B0(r,theta_c,t_c),
    theta_c(r_*)=theta_crit(r_*), r_*=r_j+L.

Let w=theta_c-theta_crit and

    q(r,w,t_c)=a integral_0^1 B0_theta(r,theta_crit+s w,t_c)ds.

Then q>=ca and the exact equations are

    w'=q(r,w,t_c)w-theta_crit', w(r_*)=0,
    w(r)=integral_r^(r_*) exp[-integral_r^s q(v,w(v),t_c)dv]
                                                   theta_crit'(s)ds. (A2)

These equations give |w|<=C eta/(aR_j)=C eta^3. Differentiation in t_c has stable coefficient q+w q_w=a B0_theta(r,theta_c,t_c)>=ca, forcing q_(t_c) w-partial_(t_c)theta_crit', and zero terminal data. At fixed w, |q_(t_c)|<=Ca/L: both the kernel coefficients and theta_crit depend softly on t_c, while their phase derivatives are bounded. Thus the forcing is bounded by

    C(a/L)eta^3+C/R_j^2=C eta/(R_j L).

The backward integral equation gives

    |partial_(t_c)w|<=C eta/(aR_j L)=C eta^3/L,
    |theta_c'|<=C eta/R_j.                             (A3)

The same estimates hold uniformly on the working r interval, a fixed macro distance from r_*. Terms from the remote terminal condition are exponential and can be absorbed in (A1)-(A3). The physical crest differences are w/a and partial_(t_c)w/a. These are the explicit derivative bounds requested in the independent audit.

For completeness the gate independently checked a stronger soft r bound. Differentiate the exact w equation in (A2), treating w_r as the unknown. Its stable coefficient is q+w q_w and its forcing is q_r w-theta_crit''. The forcing is O(eta/R_j^2). The terminal derivative at r_* is -theta_crit'(r_*), of size O(eta/R_j); on the working interval its contribution is exponentially suppressed by the fixed macro distance from r_*. Consequently

    |w_r|<=C eta/(aR_j^2)+exponential error
          <=C eta^3/R_j.                              (A4)

This gives ordinary soft r derivatives for the invariant-crest coefficients evaluated at the unequal radial gates, in addition to the nominal t_c derivative in (A3).

At the instantaneous crest the normalized cocycle f/a is a smooth function of (mu_c,delta,q_K). Its Taylor terms of weighted order two are

    -2mu_c^2+(-d+2h_sigma)delta-q_K.

The remaining value is O(eta^3) and its mu_c derivative O(eta^2). Consequently its physical t_c derivative is O(eta^2/R_j)=O(eta^3/L). Replacing theta_crit by theta_c changes f by at most Ca eta |w|<=Ca eta^4. The t_c derivative costs L^(-1) here, by (A3) and the soft derivative of f_theta; it is at most Ca eta^4/L. This proves the precise remainder bound in (22). The nominal crest comparison in (23) applies the mean value theorem to this soft parameter derivative; it does not differentiate a fast phase or regard a pointwise O(eta^3) bound as a derivative bound.
