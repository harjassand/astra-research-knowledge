DECISION

Both tools PASS as precise finite-dimensional statements, with the
common-Hilbert-space and strict-positivity scopes stated below. All
operator orientations, factors, convergences and uniqueness claims are
valid. The Hardy premise can also be proved directly by a short Toeplitz
and hyponormality argument included here, so that dependence is exposed.
No numerical test, proof-assistant certificate, broad literature scan,
external expert validation or historical-priority claim is made.

These tools do NOT establish a general entropy endpoint or an unrelated
operator order M>=0. In particular the raw Sylvester right-hand-side
block can be indefinite even for two scalar X_i. Its exact counterexample
is included in Section5, as a guard against a positivity-transfer shortcut.

1. PRECISE DOMAIN AND ANALYTIC FUNCTIONS

Take an arbitrary finite index set and self-adjoint operators X_i on
ONE common finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space H. This is necessary
for the products between functions of X_i and X_j in the stated formulas
to have meaning. No pairwise commutativity of the X_i is assumed.

Put T_i=tanh(X_i/2), S_i=sinh(X_i/2), C_i=cosh(X_i/2). Then C_i>0 is
invertible and q_i=||T_i||<1. The functions of a given X_i commute with
one another; functions of different X_i need not. Zero eigenvalues of
X_i and singular S_i are allowed and never divided by below.

On the open unit disc use the analytic branches
a(z)=(1-z^2)atanh(z)/z, a(0)=1,
b(z)=sqrt(1-z^2), b(0)=1.
The quotient at zero is removable. The coefficients are real, and

 a(z)=1-2 sum_(r>=1) z^(2r)/[(2r-1)(2r+1)].

The absolute coefficient sum is2, so a is bounded analytic and has a
continuous boundary function. For b the principal analytic square root
is unambiguous: Re(1-z^2)>0 on the open disc, and |b(z)|<=sqrt(2).
Its boundary value is continuous, with zeros at z=+/-1. Hence both are
bounded Hardy multipliers, and functional calculus at every T_i is valid.

Useful exact functional identities are

 T_i=S_i C_i^-1=C_i^-1 S_i,
 b(T_i)=C_i^-1,
 2S_i C_i a(T_i)=X_i.

The last identity follows from atanh(tanh(x/2))=x/2 on the real line,
with continuity at x=0. It requires no inverse S_i or T_i.

2. THE SUPPLIED HARDY FACT ALSO HAS A DIRECT RECONSTRUCTION

Let H^2 have orthonormal basis e_n=z^n, with normalized circle measure,
and let M_g be analytic multiplication. With P:H^2_perp directsum H^2
projection onto H^2 from L^2, Toeplitz compression gives

 M_a+M_a^*=T_(2Re a),
 M_b^*M_b=T_(|b|^2).

The elementary Fourier expansion
|sin theta|=2/pi-(4/pi)sum_(r>=1)cos(2r theta)/(4r^2-1)
follows by integrating sin(theta)cos(2r theta) on [0,pi]. Its coefficients
are absolutely summable. Together with the real coefficients of a,

 2Re a(e^(i theta))=pi|sin theta|
                    =(pi/2)|b(e^(i theta))|^2.

Consequently M_a+M_a^*=(pi/2)M_b^*M_b, with the stated exact factor.

For completeness analytic multiplication is hyponormal. Let B be
multiplication by the bounded boundary function b on L^2. It is normal,
and H^2 is invariant under B. Direct compression therefore gives

 M_b^*M_b-M_bM_b^*=P B(I-P)B^*P >=0.

The quadratic form is ||(I-P)B^*u||^2. Since pi/2>1,

 D=M_a+M_a^*-M_bM_b^*
  =(pi/2-1)M_b^*M_b+(M_b^*M_b-M_bM_b^*)>=0.

Thus the supplied D identity and positivity can be reconstructed without
reading the other worker's proof. This argument uses standard definitions
of Hardy multiplication and orthogonal compression, and explicitly shows
the two product orientations. Replacing M_bM_b^* by M_b^*M_b in the
definition of D would change the compressed kernel below.

3. EXACT GRAM ORIENTATION AND NORM CONVERGENCE

For each self-adjoint T_i define
V_i=K_(T_i):H->H^2 tensor H,
V_i v=sum_(n>=0)e_n tensor T_i^n v.
The series converges in norm and
||V_i v||^2=sum_n||T_i^n v||^2<=||v||^2/(1-q_i^2).
Thus V_i is bounded. Its cross Gram is the norm-convergent series

 G_ij=V_i^*V_j=sum_(n>=0)T_i^n T_j^n.

For g=a or b, their real coefficients and self-adjoint T_i give the
correct multiplier intertwining identity

 (M_g^* tensor I)V_i=V_i g(T_i).

Indeed the e_n coefficient on the left is sum_r g_r T_i^(n+r), exactly
T_i^n g(T_i). Convergence holds since q_i<1; bounded-multiplier convergence
or absolute coefficient/geometric summation justifies the calculation.
Taking its adjoint, rather than silently reversing products, yields

 V_i^*(M_a tensor I)V_j=a(T_i)G_ij,
 V_i^*(M_a^* tensor I)V_j=G_ij a(T_j),
 V_i^*(M_bM_b^* tensor I)V_j=b(T_i)G_ij b(T_j).

Using commutativity only within a single T_i, this is exactly

 V_i^*(D tensor I)V_j
 =sum_(n>=0)T_i^n[a(T_i)+a(T_j)-b(T_i)b(T_j)]T_j^n
 =N_ij.

For A_ij=a(T_i)+a(T_j)-b(T_i)b(T_j), every term has norm at most
q_i^n q_j^n ||A_ij||, so the supplied N series converges absolutely in
operator norm. The identities do not require T_iT_j=T_jT_i. They also
show N_ji=N_ij^*, as required for a positive block matrix.

Let W_i=(D^1/2 tensor I)V_i. Then N_ij=W_i^*W_j, and for any tuple v_i

 sum_ij <v_i,N_ij v_j>=||sum_i W_i v_i||^2>=0.

Therefore [N_ij] is PSD for every matrix dimension and every finite
family. With K_ij=2S_i N_ij S_j,
[K_ij]=2 diag(S_i)[N_ij]diag(S_i) is PSD by congruence. The S_i need
not be positive or invertible. Equivalently its Gram factors are
sqrt(2)W_i S_i, so negative eigenvalues of X_i cause no problem.

4. SYLVESTER IDENTITY, EVERY FACTOR, AND UNIQUENESS

The N series telescopes in norm to

 N_ij-T_i N_ij T_j=A_ij.                            (S1)

The left-right multiplication map Z->T_i ZT_j has norm at most q_iq_j<1.
Thus S1 has a UNIQUE bounded solution, its Neumann series above.

Insert K_ij=2S_iN_ijS_j into the proposed expression. No cross-index
commutativity is used:

 2[C_i K_ij C_j-S_i K_ij S_j]
 =4S_i C_i[N_ij-T_iN_ijT_j]S_j C_j
 =4S_i C_i A_ij S_j C_j.

Its three terms reduce respectively to

 4S_iC_i a(T_i)S_jC_j = X_i sinh(X_j),
 4S_iC_i a(T_j)S_jC_j = sinh(X_i) X_j,
 4S_iC_i b(T_i)b(T_j)S_jC_j =4S_iS_j.

The first two use 2S_lC_l=sinh(X_l) and
2S_lC_l a(T_l)=X_l. The third uses b(T_l)=C_l^-1 on its own side;
neither b(T_i)b(T_j) nor S_iS_j is reversed. Hence EXACTLY

 2[C_i K_ij C_j-S_i K_ij S_j]
 =X_i sinh(X_j)+sinh(X_i)X_j-4S_iS_j.              (S2)

Uniqueness for S2 does not follow by dividing by S_i or S_j, and is
not lost when they are singular. For any right side R_ij, left/right
multiplication by C_i^-1,C_j^-1 reduces S2 to

 2[Z-T_iZT_j]=C_i^-1 R_ij C_j^-1.

The same strict contraction proves uniqueness among all bounded operators.
For finite matrices this includes every possible matrix solution.

As an exact diagonal check, i=j yields K_ii=k(X_i), where
k(x)=x sinh(x)-cosh(x)+1. This follows from
N_ii=[2a(T_i)-b(T_i)^2]/[I-T_i^2] within the commuting functional calculus
and 2S_i^2=cosh(X_i)-I. At X_i=0, K_ii=0 with no limiting ambiguity.

5. EXACT NEGATIVE CONTROL: THE RAW RIGHT SIDE IS NOT A PSD KERNEL

The map [K_ij]->2[diag(C_i)K diag(C_j)-diag(S_i)K diag(S_j)] is a
DIFFERENCE of two positive congruences and is not positivity preserving.
It cannot be used to infer positivity of the raw right side in S2.

Even for scalars choose X_1=1, X_2=2 and v=(2,-1). Let x=(1,2),
s=(sinh(1/2),sinh(1)). The right side R has
R_ij=x_i sinh(x_j)+sinh(x_i)x_j-4s_i s_j. Since v dot x=0,

 v^*Rv=-4[2sinh(1/2)-sinh(1)]^2<0.

Strictness follows from sinh(1)=2sinh(1/2)cosh(1/2) and cosh(1/2)>1.
This is an analytic counterexample to an extra positivity-transfer claim,
not to the supplied K theorem or an entropy endpoint. It is why the
Sylvester identity must be used as an identity with its own inverse and
not as an order-preserving equivalence.

6. THE SCALAR f IDENTITY AND INTEGRAL ARE EXACT

For t>0 set x=(1/2)ln t. Elementary exponential identities give

 4sqrt(t)[x sinh(x)-cosh(x)+1]
 =(t-1)ln t-2(sqrt(t)-1)^2=f(t).

There is no sign/domain ambiguity: sqrt(t)>0 and ln t is real.

Two elementary integrals prove the claimed representation. First,

 I(t)=integral_0^infty dlambda/[(t+lambda)(1+lambda)]
     =ln(t)/(t-1), with I(1)=1.

Second,
 H(t)=integral_0^infty sqrt(lambda)dlambda/
                    [(t+lambda)(1+lambda)^2]
     =pi/[2(1+sqrt(t))^2].

To verify the latter without an imported table, put lambda=u^2 and
a=sqrt(t). Partial fractions and the arctangent integral yield

 F(a,b)=integral_0^infty u^2du/[(u^2+a^2)(u^2+b^2)]
       =pi/[2(a+b)] for all a,b>0.

Differentiating with respect to b is justified by domination on a local
positive b interval. It gives
integral u^2du/[(u^2+a^2)(u^2+b^2)^2]=pi/[4b(a+b)^2].
Set b=1 and multiply by2 from lambda=u^2. The formulas extend directly
through a=b by continuity. In particular H(1)=pi/8.

Combining these two integrals,

 (t-1)^2 integral_0^infty
 [(1+lambda)-(4/pi)sqrt(lambda)]/
 [(t+lambda)(1+lambda)^2] dlambda
 =(t-1)ln t-2(t-1)^2/(1+sqrt(t))^2=f(t).

At t=1 both sides are zero; the integrals themselves remain finite.
The exact coefficient4/pi is verified, not numerically estimated.

7. POSITIVE MEASURE AND ALL-DIMENSIONAL OPERATOR CONVEXITY

Let
 dmu(lambda)=[(1+lambda)-(4/pi)sqrt(lambda)]
                          dlambda/(1+lambda)^2.

Its numerator is strictly positive for every lambda>=0: with u=sqrt(lambda),
u^2-(4/pi)u+1=(u-2/pi)^2+1-4/pi^2>0, since pi>2.
Moreover0<=dmu/dlambda<=1/(1+lambda). This is a positive sigma-finite
measure with the integrability needed below; its total mass is infinite.

For each fixed lambda>=0,

 q_lambda(t)=(t-1)^2/(t+lambda)
            =t-(lambda+2)+(lambda+1)^2/(t+lambda).

The last term is a positive multiple of the operator-convex inverse
function on positive definite matrices, and the remaining part is affine.
One can prove inverse convexity directly: for every vector v,

 <v,A^-1 v>=sup_y[2Re<v,y>-<y,Ay>].

The supremum of affine functions of A is convex, so for A,B>0 the
quadratic-form inequalities give
(theta A+(1-theta)B)^-1<=theta A^-1+(1-theta)B^-1.
Apply this to A+lambda I and B+lambda I. Thus every q_lambda is
operator convex, without assuming A and B commute.

For spectra in a common compact interval [a,b] inside(0,infinity),

 ||q_lambda(A)||<=max{|a-1|,|b-1|}^2/(a+lambda).

The measure-density upper bound makes the Bochner integrand dominated
by a constant/[(a+lambda)(1+lambda)], integrable both at zero and
infinity. Therefore the integral converges in operator norm, uniformly
on such spectral intervals, and the scalar identity gives

 f(A)=integral_0^infty q_lambda(A)dmu(lambda).

Integrate the q_lambda operator-convex inequalities with the positive
measure, or first integrate on finite cutoffs and take norm limits.
The closed positive cone preserves the inequality in the limit. Hence

 f(theta A+(1-theta)B)<=theta f(A)+(1-theta)f(B)

for every finite matrix dimension, A,B>0, and0<=theta<=1. This proves
the exact claimed operator convexity on (0,infinity).

The affine and inverse pieces of q_lambda must NOT be integrated
separately over the infinite measure: those individual integrals
diverge. Their combined q_lambda is the convergent object. This is a
proof-scope clarification, not a correction to the stated representation.

8. EXACT SCOPE AND NONCONCLUSIONS

Tool1 is valid for arbitrary finite families of bounded self-adjoint
operators on a common finite H, with no commutativity across indices.
It also extends verbatim to bounded self-adjoint operators on any common
Hilbert space, because ||tanh(X_i/2)||<1 and the same norm arguments hold.
No unbounded-X domain, norm-one T limit, or bounded infinite-index block
operator is asserted. The finite-family positive-kernel statement suffices.

Tool2 is operator convex on strictly positive arguments. It applies in
every finite dimension and to bounded strictly positive operators with
spectrum inside [a,b] subset(0,infinity). The scalar f diverges at t=0,
so no finite-valued extension to singular positive semidefinite matrices
is claimed. No operator convexity of k under the nonlinear X-to-exp(2X)
change of variable is asserted.

Neither tool, alone or together, proves the unrelated order M>=0,
general J>=2E, weak regularity for all KMS generators, or any endpoint
closure. The raw-R negative control shows a specific tempting inference
is false. Any entropy consequence still needs its exact independent
bridge to these valid kernels/functional inequalities.
