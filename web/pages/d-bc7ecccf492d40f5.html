C1. NOTATION AND PRECISE RESULTS
Let H=(C^d)^{tensor N}, with N>=1, d>=2. For a partition lambda of N
with at most d rows, let
 H = direct_sum_lambda U_lambda tensor M_lambda
be Schur-Weyl decomposition. U_lambda is the irreducible polynomial U(d)
representation of highest weight lambda, padded with zeros to d entries;
M_lambda is the irreducible S_N representation. Write P_lambda for the
orthogonal projector onto the ENTIRE isotypic space U_lambda tensor M_lambda,
u_lambda=dim U_lambda, m_lambda=dim M_lambda, and ell(lambda) for its number
of nonzero rows. Thus Tr P_lambda = u_lambda m_lambda.

A pure k-producible vector factors over a partition of the labeled particles
into clusters, each of size at most k. A k-producible density is a convex
mixture of such pure vectors, allowing different partitions in different terms.
Entanglement depth is the least such k.

Theorem:
 (i) P_lambda/(u_lambda m_lambda) has depth EXACTLY ell(lambda).
 (ii) EVERY density supported entirely in this isotypic space has depth
      at least ell(lambda). It need not obey the matching upper bound.
 (iii) Every density invariant under BOTH U^{tensor N} for all U in U(d)
       and conjugation by every site permutation is d-producible.
       More precisely its depth is at most max_{q_lambda>0} ell(lambda).
 (iv) Any common local filter A^{tensor N}, conditioned on a nonzero outcome,
      preserves the upper bound in (iii). A filtered single normalized
      P_lambda still has depth exactly ell(lambda), even for singular A
      whenever its outcome is nonzero.
These results do not apply the upper bound to arbitrary Werner states
without permutation invariance. They do not give a matching lower bound
for arbitrary mixtures of lambda sectors.

C2. A COLUMN PRODUCT LIES ENTIRELY IN ONE SECTOR
Let h_c be the height of column c of lambda. On the sites of that column set
 a_{h_c} = |1 wedge 2 wedge ... wedge h_c>,
where the wedge is normalized by 1/sqrt(h_c!). Let
 w_lambda = tensor_product_over_columns a_{h_c}.              (C2.1)
It is a normalized product over clusters with largest size ell(lambda).

Let H_i=sum_sites |i><i| and E_ab=sum_sites |a><b|, a<b.
The vector (C2.1) has number weight
 H_i w_lambda = lambda_i w_lambda:
the basis label i occurs once in exactly lambda_i columns. Moreover each
column is killed by E_ab. If b exceeds its height, no factor contains b;
otherwise a<b is also present and replacing b by a gives a repeated wedge
label. Hence all total raising operators kill w_lambda.

In the Schur-Weyl decomposition each irreducible U_mu has a one-dimensional
highest-weight space, of weight mu. Projecting w_lambda into each summand
preserves its number weight and annihilation by raising operators. Therefore
its only possibly nonzero summand has mu=lambda. In particular
 P_lambda w_lambda = w_lambda.                              (C2.2)
There is no other higher representation sharing this highest weight.
We use U(d), with fixed polynomial degree N, so determinant-shift ambiguity
does not arise. Conjugation by SU(d) would give the same state symmetry.

C3. DOUBLE TWIRLING AND ALL DIMENSION FACTORS
Let T_U be normalized Haar conjugation by U^{tensor N}, and T_S the uniform
conjugation average over S_N. On one lambda summand, the two independent
actions are D_lambda(U) tensor I and I tensor R_lambda(pi), respectively.
Irreducibility and Schur's lemma give, for any trace-one X in that summand,
 (T_U T_S)(X) = I_{U_lambda}/u_lambda tensor
                I_{M_lambda}/m_lambda
              = P_lambda/(u_lambda m_lambda).              (C3.1)
This also follows by twirling one factor at a time and taking partial traces;
it remains true if X is entangled between the two representation factors.
Both twirls preserve trace. No multiplicity factor is absorbed into a
probability weight.

Apply (C3.1) to |w_lambda><w_lambda|. Every individual twirled sample is
 P_pi U^{tensor N} w_lambda, a product over relabeled column clusters.
The same U acts on all particles but is a tensor product of local unitaries;
permutations simply relabel the cluster partition. Thus every sample is
ell(lambda)-producible, proving the upper bound in (i).

For reference,
 u_lambda = product_{1<=i<j<=d}
               (lambda_i-lambda_j+j-i)/(j-i),
 m_lambda = N! / product_{boxes a in lambda} hook_length(a).
These are positive integers, and sum_lambda u_lambda m_lambda=d^N.

C4. EXPOSING A HIGHEST WEIGHT WITHOUT BREAKING A CLUSTER PRODUCT
Suppose a nonzero vector v lies in the lambda isotypic space and factors
over fixed clusters C_1,...,C_t, with all n_c=|C_c|<=k. We prove ell(lambda)<=k.

Let H_lambda be the orthogonal projector onto the highest-weight line in
U_lambda tensor the whole M_lambda. There exists a collective unitary U
with H_lambda U^{tensor N}v != 0. Otherwise v would be orthogonal to
D_lambda(U)^dagger(highest-line) tensor M_lambda for every U. The orbit
of the highest line spans U_lambda by irreducibility, which would force v=0.
This is an existence assertion, not an uncharged constructive sampling step.

Choose strictly decreasing real numbers a_1>...>a_d, for example a_i=d-i,
and F_t=diag(t^{a_1},...,t^{a_d}), t>0. In U_lambda every non-highest weight
mu is lambda minus a nonzero sum of positive roots. Consequently
 a dot mu < a dot lambda
for all such weights, and the highest-weight space has multiplicity one
in U_lambda. Hence
 t^{-(a dot lambda)} F_t^{tensor N} U^{tensor N} v
       tends to H_lambda U^{tensor N}v !=0, as t tends to infinity. (C4.1)
After normalization this is a definite unit vector v_infty of number
weight lambda, killed by every E_ab, a<b.

Each finite-t normalized vector in (C4.1) still factors over the SAME fixed
clusters: every operation is an identical one-particle invertible map.
The pure-product set for that fixed partition is compact and closed.
Taking a subsequence of its normalized cluster factors, the limiting vector
therefore also factors:
 v_infty = tensor_c u_c, with each ||u_c||=1.                (C4.2)
No claim that a generic Cartan filter alone sees the highest weight is made;
the prior collective unitary with nonzero projection is essential.

C5. ORTHOGONAL TANGENTS FORCE CLUSTER HIGHEST WEIGHTS
Let E_ab^{(c)} act collectively within cluster c. Total highest-weight
annihilation gives
 sum_c u_1 tensor ... tensor (E_ab^{(c)}u_c) tensor ... tensor u_t=0.
Project in cluster c onto u_c^perp and in every other cluster onto its
u_l line. All summands except c disappear, yielding
 E_ab^{(c)}u_c = e_{ab,c} u_c.
The finite-dimensional raising operator E_ab^{(c)} is nilpotent: each
application changes one b into a, and more than n_c applications vanish.
Thus e_{ab,c}=0. Each cluster is killed by all its raising operators.

Apply the same orthogonal projection to
 (sum_c H_i^{(c)})v_infty=lambda_i v_infty.
It follows that H_i^{(c)}u_c=mu_{c,i}u_c. Number-operator spectra show
mu_{c,i} are nonnegative integers summing to n_c, and
 lambda_i=sum_c mu_{c,i}.                                  (C5.1)
They form decreasing sequences. Indeed, since E_ab^{(c)}u_c=0 and
(E_ab^{(c)})^dagger=E_ba^{(c)},
 ||E_ba^{(c)}u_c||^2
    = <u_c|[E_ab^{(c)},E_ba^{(c)}]|u_c>
    = mu_{c,a}-mu_{c,b} >=0, a<b.                          (C5.2)
Thus each mu_c is a partition of n_c. Such a partition has at most n_c
nonzero rows. Since n_c<=k, mu_{c,i}=0 for all i>k. Equation (C5.1)
then forces lambda_i=0 for i>k, proving ell(lambda)<=k.
This argument uses physical cluster product structure, not a tensor product
assumed between the Schur representation and its multiplicity.

C6. POSITIVE SUPPORT EXTENDS THE LOWER BOUND TO MIXED STATES
Let rho=P_lambda rho P_lambda be k-producible. In any pure cluster-product
decomposition rho=sum_s p_s |v_s><v_s|,
 0=Tr[(I-P_lambda)rho]
   =sum_s p_s ||(I-P_lambda)v_s||^2.
Every term with p_s>0 is therefore entirely in the lambda sector.
C4-C5 force k>=ell(lambda). In finite dimension a finite decomposition is
available from compactness and Caratheodory, and the same nonnegative
integral argument works directly for a probability measure.
Combining this lower bound with C2-C3 proves (i) and (ii).

C7. CENTRAL MIXTURES, FILTERS, AND CASIMIR GIBBS STATES
Commutation with collective U(d) gives the form
 direct_sum_lambda I_{U_lambda} tensor B_lambda.
Additional commutation with all site permutations makes each B_lambda
scalar by irreducibility. Positivity and trace one therefore give exactly
 rho=sum_lambda q_lambda P_lambda/(u_lambda m_lambda),
 q_lambda>=0, sum q_lambda=1,
 q_lambda=Tr(P_lambda rho).                               (C7.1)
Apply C3 sectorwise to get (iii).

A^{tensor N} sends a pure cluster product to a cluster product or zero.
Conditioning a convex mixture on a nonzero total outcome merely reweights
its nonzero terms by their squared norms. This proves (iv)'s upper bound.
Moreover A^{tensor N} commutes with all permutations and hence with their
central isotypic projectors P_lambda. A filtered single-sector state remains
supported in that sector, so C6 supplies the same lower bound. This proves
the exact-depth assertion for any nonzero common-filter outcome.

Any finite-dimensional Gibbs density of a Hermitian operator that is scalar
on each lambda sector is central of the form (C7.1). In particular functions
of collective central Casimirs at ANY finite coupling/temperature have
depth <=d, independent of N. If B is any finite Hermitian one-particle
field, it commutes with the central Casimir operator; adding sum_i B_i
gives a common filter A=exp(B/2), so the same upper bound holds.
General collective anisotropy need not be central; no such conclusion is
drawn from C7 for an arbitrary quadratic Hamiltonian.

C8. NECESSARY SCOPE LIMITS
A sector mixture can have smaller depth than every occupied nonsymmetric
sector: q_lambda=u_lambda m_lambda/d^N gives I/d^N, which is fully separable.
One cannot take max row count as a lower bound on a mixed-sector state.

Nor is collective U invariance alone sufficient for the upper bound.
An explicit four-qubit pure singlet has computational amplitudes
 0011:2, 0101:1, 0110:-3, 1001:-3, 1010:1, 1100:2,
all other amplitudes zero, with squared norm 28.
It is the sum of the unnormalized (12)(34) singlet product and twice the
(13)(24) singlet product. J^2 and J_- annihilate it. It is invariant under
global SU(2), but not under all site permutations. Its Schmidt ranks are
2 at all four 1|3 cuts and 4 at all three distinct 2|2 cuts. Thus it is
genuinely four-partite entangled, of depth 4, despite lying in the
lambda=(2,2) isotypic space. The lower bound ell(lambda)=2 is consistent;
the matching upper bound concerns the normalized projector or its common
filters, not every state in that support.
These exact small ranks are checked by qubit_stationary_check.py.
