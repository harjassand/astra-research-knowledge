# Targeted prior-art audit: critical iid-qubit EB reconstruction floor

Date: 2026-10-08. Status: targeted internal-corpus and primary-source search; not a novelty certificate or an external proof review.

## Exact candidate under review

For the supplied family

\[
\rho_{r,v}^{\otimes n},\qquad \rho_{r,v}=(I+r\,v\cdot\sigma)/2,
\quad v\in S^2,\quad c_0\le r\sqrt n\le c_1,
\]

the target is full-state reconstruction in worst-case half trace distance. The candidate result is that every entanglement-breaking reconstruction channel, with arbitrary outcome space and arbitrary input-to-output Schur-sector transitions, has error at least \(c(c_0,c_1)r\); an explicit coherent measure-and-prepare channel has error at most \(2r\). Hence the optimum EB error is \(\Theta(r)=\Theta(n^{-1/2})\) on each fixed positive critical interval. The known radius is supplied, and no input-dependent memory register is free. The direct proof candidate and finite checks are in [Sol's result](../sol_collision_precision/RESULT.txt), Sections 12–13 and its evidence directory. Its corrected common score interval is \([-3/2,1/2]\), not \([-3a/2,a/2]\).

## Closest prior mechanisms and their scope

1. **Astra N39 fixed/resolved-gap spin twirl.** The 2026-10-07 `work/astra_prior/cards/N39-nontracial-boundaries.txt` calls the inherited package source-derived and externally unreviewed. The detailed `you-have-access-to-over-100-2/work/quantum_precision.md`, lines 14–39, has the same full-orbit iid-qubit interface, an EB floor of order \(\delta\) for resolved signal \(\kappa=n\delta^2\ge64\) when the exponential correction is negligible, and a weaker all-positive bound. Lines 96–177 derive the product-spin twirl and actual input/output sector coupling used by the current candidate. In the critical window \(\kappa\asymp1\), that earlier weaker bound is only of order \(r/\sqrt{\log(1/r)}\); it does not supply the sharp \(\Theta(r)\) conclusion. The N39 report itself (lines 318–326) leaves the optimal retained quantum dimension and sharp moving-collision transfer open.

2. **Astra N08/N10 critical experiments.** `work/astra_prior/cards/N08-block-critical-gaussian.txt`, lines 5–7, records an N08 Gaussian-comparison archive theorem only above its stated remainder regime and explicitly distinguishes the ordered-spin VMF observation in N10. The later `2026-10-07/you-have-access-to-over-100-5/work/quantum_collisions/ordered_spin_archive.md`, §7, lines 193–303, is the closest posterior-direction precedent: it derives a critical spherical von Mises–Fisher law for a *correlated ordered-spin Gibbs* system and proves \(e\ge b_\alpha/(2D)-a_V\), matched by a classical \(O(1/D)\) code. Its target is a memory-dimension/accuracy tradeoff in a limiting classical spherical experiment. It is not a finite-n, dimension-independent EB error floor for the iid qubit family above. The common first-moment/cap/posterior-direction ideas are prior mechanisms, so the candidate must not be presented as inventing those ideas.

3. **Posterior direction moments in qubit estimation.** Bagan, Ballester, Gill, Monras and Muñoz-Tapia, [“Optimal full estimation of qubit mixed states”](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0510158), derive the rotational first-moment formula and posterior direction bound in their SU(2)-covariant estimator. Their objective is one-copy mixed-state estimation under fidelity with a prior, not reconstruction of the whole n-copy state by an arbitrary EB channel. This source supplies a familiar ingredient, not the candidate's reconstruction theorem.

4. **Fixed-reference quantum LAN and compression.** Guţă, Bowles and Adesso, [“Quantum teleportation benchmarks for independent and identically-distributed spin states and displaced thermal states”](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.1843), formulate the full n-qubit MAP trace-norm reconstruction problem and derive an asymptotic minmax result through QLAN around a fixed reference. The local oscillator description uses the supplied fixed reference polarization (the transverse coordinates scale with \(1/(r_0\sqrt n)\)); the checked theorem gives no quantitative uniform remainder as that reference itself moves with \(r_0\asymp n^{-1/2}\). Thus it is close in task and metric, but does not state the critical triangular-sequence floor.

5. **Known-spectrum qubit compression.** Yang, Chiribella and Hayashi, [“Optimal Compression for Identically Prepared Qubit States”](https://arxiv.org/html/1606.02893), establish fixed-spectrum vanishing-error compression and a full-model hybrid result. In the known-spectrum construction the typical spin center is \(J_0\approx n(p-1/2)\), with fluctuations of order \(\sqrt n\); its state-conversion error is controlled by relative sector displacement \(|J-K|/J\). For \(p-1/2\asymp n^{-1/2}\), the center and fluctuations have the same order, so their fixed-spectrum asymptotic guarantee does not directly yield a vanishing conversion bound in the critical moving sequence. The paper does not state the EB error floor under review.

6. **Quantum memory necessity for fixed noncommuting families.** Yang, Bai, Chiribella and Hayashi, [“Compression for quantum population coding”](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.03372), prove that asymptotically faithful compression of generic fixed full-rank nondegenerate noncommuting families cannot use classical memory alone; their allocation can make the quantum/classical bit ratio arbitrarily small but not zero. Their theorem uses a fixed reference spectrum and Q-LAN. It does not establish a lower bound on the maximum quantum branch dimension for the triangular critical sequence, nor the sharper error requirement \(o(r)\). It is a nearby boundary for a separate follow-up, not a transfer of the required conclusion.

## Narrow conclusion

The targeted search found no exact prior theorem for the critical iid-qubit \(\Theta(r)\) EB reconstruction floor. It did find clear ancestry for the spin-twirl coupling (N39), the rotational posterior moment (Bagan et al.), and posterior-direction/cap arguments in another critical spherical experiment (the Astra ordered-spin theorem). The candidate-specific increment is the bounded quadratic posterior score combined with the exact spin-sector posterior-radius inequality and a uniformly positive critical spin window; its completed-square bound handles arbitrary EB output-sector changes and removes the earlier logarithm. This is an internal candidate pending independent mathematical and priority review, not a novelty claim. The previous local `c04_s01` signed-permutation classicality result is deliberately not rederived or treated as cleared by this audit.
