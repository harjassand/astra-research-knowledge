N12 — Cell-size laws under lineage memory and population selection
Frozen record: v1.txt (2026-10-09)
Status: internally derived candidate and finite-computed counterexample; no empirical confirmation and no novelty/priority claim.

## Exact question and answer

Can apparent adder or sizer behavior arise from how lineages or populations are sampled, together with inherited growth state, even when division does not read the cell's current size? Yes. I give (i) a kinematic identity showing that the standard added-size regression is a statistic of adjacent birth-size correlation, (ii) a finite-state, aperiodic, finite-noise hidden-growth program whose forward lineage has an exact adder law and a parallel program with an exact sizer law, although both are size-independent timers, and (iii) an exact counterfactual pair with identical complete unperturbed cell trajectories but different responses to a birth-volume intervention. A reproductive-value tilt can further turn the same forward adder slope into a sizer-like population-tree slope.

The model results do not show that measured bacterial adders are artifacts. Primary studies include single-cell lineage data and later molecular perturbations. The result concerns what an unperturbed regression alone identifies. The decisive experiment must intervene on size while recording the hidden state, age, complete lineage tree, and deaths. An adder-like response establishes a cell-autonomous size-restoring law; it does not, by itself, establish an active size sensor.

## Definitions and sampling measures

Size means cell volume throughout this record. For a cell in generation n, let B_n>0 be volume immediately after birth, A_n the elapsed age since that birth, T_n the division age, D_n=V_n(T_n^-) the volume immediately before division, and Δ_n=D_n−B_n the volume added during that cycle. Unless stated otherwise, division is symmetric, so either daughter has birth volume B_{n+1}=D_n/2. This is a forward lineage: one daughter is chosen by a rule fixed in advance, independently of its future growth or survival. “Tree” or “retrospective” sampling means choosing a cell from a growing population at a census time and tracing its ancestors; its lineages are weighted by reproductive output and generation time. A snapshot cell's age is not its generation time.

The calculations below concern conditional means and ordinary least-squares slopes. The regression of Δ on B is not the full conditional distribution of Δ given B. This distinction matters when conditional variances change with size.

## Kinematic identity: the slope is an autocorrelation statistic

Under symmetric division,

    Δ_n = D_n − B_n = 2 B_{n+1} − B_n.

Therefore, for any single specified measure μ on paired cycles with Var_μ(B_n)>0,

    β_μ := Cov_μ(B_n,Δ_n)/Var_μ(B_n)
         = 2 Cov_μ(B_n,B_{n+1})/Var_μ(B_n) − 1.             (1)

If the forward lineage is stationary and has finite, nonzero birth-size variance, then Var(B_{n+1})=Var(B_n) and

    β_forward = 2 ρ_1 − 1,                                  (2)

where ρ_1 is the lag-one Pearson correlation of birth volume. Thus the familiar adder slope 0 is equivalent to ρ_1=1/2, and the sizer slope −1 is equivalent to ρ_1=0. Equation (1) is kinematic; it makes no assumption about the division mechanism. In particular, the regression coefficient cannot identify whether B causes division timing.

With a random daughter fraction Φ_n, B_{n+1}=Φ_nD_n and Δ_n=B_{n+1}/Φ_n−B_n. Equation (1) then does not hold unless Φ_n=1/2 or the joint law of Φ and size is separately controlled. A pole-tracked daughter, a uniformly selected daughter, and a population-tip ancestor can therefore answer different statistical questions. Any use of (1) must report the daughter rule and division symmetry.

## Finite-noise stationary hidden-growth counterexamples

Let the hidden state H_n take values L,M,H with nominal birth volumes

    b_L=1−ε,   b_M=1,   b_H=1+ε,   ε=0.1.

At birth, H_n selects a positive growth program and a next state H_{n+1} from a Markov kernel P. A cell divides at a timer T_{H_n} independent of its actual birth volume. For the unperturbed process, set its volume trajectory on a transition i→j to

    F_ij(a) = b_i exp(λ_ij a),   0≤a≤T_i,
    λ_ij = log(2 b_j/b_i)/T_i.

Then D_n=2b_j, symmetric daughters are born at b_j, and H follows P. Every used growth factor is greater than one: for ε=0.1 the allowed factors lie between 1.8 and 2.2223. Random H transitions therefore create finite growth-rate and size variation, while all cells divide on a positive exponential growth trajectory.

### An apparent forward adder

Choose

    P_A = [[1/2, 1/2, 0],
           [1/2,   0, 1/2],
           [  0, 1/2, 1/2]].

This irreducible, aperiodic chain has stationary distribution π_A=(1/3,1/3,1/3) and eigenvalues 1, 1/2, −1/2, so deviations from stationarity mix geometrically at rate 1/2 per generation. Its stationary birth-volume variance is 2ε²/3=0.0066667. The three conditional next-birth means are

    E[b_{H_{n+1}}|L]=(b_L+b_M)/2=(b_L+1)/2,
    E[b_{H_{n+1}}|M]=(b_L+b_H)/2=1=(b_M+1)/2,
    E[b_{H_{n+1}}|H]=(b_M+b_H)/2=(b_H+1)/2.

Consequently,

    E[Δ_n | B_n=b_i] = 2 E[b_{H_{n+1}}|H_n=i] − b_i = 1

for all three birth sizes. A forward lineage therefore has a perfect conditional-mean adder and OLS slope 0, with a nonzero finite stationary size variance. Division time is a timer; no equation for H, λ, or T reads the cell's actual birth volume.

### An apparent forward sizer

With the same sizes, choose instead

    P_S = [[  0, 1,   0],
           [1/4, 1/2, 1/4],
           [  0, 1,   0]].

This chain is irreducible and aperiodic. It has stationary distribution π_S=(1/6,2/3,1/6), eigenvalues 1, 0, −1/2, and stationary volume variance ε²/3=0.0033333. Every row has E[b_{H_{n+1}}|H_n]=1, hence

    E[Δ_n | B_n=b_i] = 2−b_i.

The regression is an exact sizer, slope −1, despite fixed-age division and no dependence of the growth program on actual B. The randomness and mixing are in the inherited state transitions, not in size-dependent division.

## Exact observational equivalence and causal separation

The counterexamples can be strengthened from equal regressions to identical unperturbed cell trajectories. For each allowed transition i→j, fix its age T_i and the same nominal trajectory F_ij(a) above. Compare two structural models, both using the same P, timers, symmetric fission, and H transition draw.

1. Additive-increment model A:

       V_A(a;B,i,j) = B + F_ij(a) − b_i.

   Its division size is D_A=B+(2b_j−b_i), so Δ_A=2b_j−b_i is independent of B. The nominal trajectory at B=b_i is F_ij(a); after division, B'_A=b_j+(B−b_i)/2. A randomized birth-size perturbation δ is halved at each subsequent symmetric division: R_k=2^(−k)δ.

2. Multiplicative-timer model M:

       V_M(a;B,i,j) = (B/b_i) F_ij(a).

   Its division size is D_M=2b_j B/b_i, and the division age is still T_i. Its daughter birth is B'_M=(B/b_i)b_j. At nominal B=b_i, this gives exactly the same complete path F_ij(a), division time, division size, and daughter state as model A.

Thus the two models have identical finite-dimensional distributions for every unperturbed lineage observable (including full volume-versus-age paths), for both P_A and P_S. This remains true after population-tree reweighting because the baseline transition types, ages, and descendants are identical. Yet their counterfactual responses differ. In model M,

    ∂ E[Δ_M | do(B),i,j]/∂B = 2b_j/b_i − 1,
    ∂ B'_M/∂B = b_j/b_i,

which is positive for every allowed transition and is never the additive model's response 0 for Δ or 1/2 for daughter size. Along a fixed hidden-state path H_0,…,H_k,

    B_k^M(B_0) = (B_0/b_{H_0}) b_{H_k}.

An initial volume perturbation is a conserved proportional scale shift; it does not decay. In model A it contracts by 2^(−k). No analysis of unperturbed trajectories can choose between these structural models. A size intervention can.

The no-go is broader than this example. Suppose an exponential-growth, size-independent timer has per-cycle volume multiplier G_n>0 and the tracked daughter fraction is Φ_n∈(0,1), with (G_n,Φ_n) driven by a stationary exogenous inherited state and common noise path, not by actual B. Then

    B_{n+1}=A_n B_n,    A_n=Φ_nG_n,
    ∂B_{n+k}/∂B_n = ∏_{j=0}^{k−1} A_{n+j}.                 (3)

If log B has a stationary integrable distribution, stationarity forces E[log A_n]=0. Under ergodicity the intervention-response Lyapunov exponent is therefore zero, not negative. In the finite-state construction above, (3) telescopes exactly to b_{H_k}/b_{H_0}. If independent division-partition noise adds a nondegenerate log(2Φ_n) term, the log-size process diffuses (zero drift) or drifts (nonzero drift) unless a size-dependent mechanism, a state that responds to partition, or survival selection supplies a restoring term. A stationary natural size distribution under an autonomous multiplicative program is not evidence of attraction to a unique size distribution.

The additive twin is a necessary interpretive caution. Its perturbation response is genuinely restoring, but it need not actively sense B. A size-only experiment identifies the causal input-output law, not the molecular sensor. For example, with age-dependent absolute growth, V(a)=B+g_H(a), timer division gives Δ=g_H(T), independent of B, and symmetric daughter birth size contracts a volume perturbation by 1/2. Molecular claims require additional interventions on the proposed accumulator, growth law, or timer.

## Population selection and a reproducible tilted-tree example

For the multitype Bellman-Harris process with transition probabilities P_ij, two daughters per division, and state-dependent generation time T_i, a large growing population's division flux has growth exponent r satisfying

    ρ(M(r))=1,   M_ij(r)=2 P_ij exp(−r T_i).

Let v>0 satisfy M(r)v=v. A random extant tip's ancestral transition law is

    Q_ij = M_ij(r) v_j/v_i,   Σ_j Q_ij=1.                  (4)

This reproductive-value tilt is different from the forward lineage kernel P. For P_A, choose T=(0.2,1,2) in arbitrary time units while keeping G_ij=2b_j/b_i. The derivation script finds r=1.267499853, a stationary retrospective phase distribution (0.809374,0.185737,0.004889), and conditional retrospective mean increments (0.944784,0.809694,0.915852). Their OLS slope on b=(0.9,1,1.1) is −1.223503, although the forward-lineage slope is exactly 0. Every time is positive, each growth factor exceeds 1, and the lineage chain is finite and mixing. The population-tree slope is sizer-like because fast/slow cycle types are differently represented in a random-tip genealogy, not because the size-independent division timer senses size.

This numerical value is an illustrative model output, not an estimate for any organism. The general population/lineage modulation is already present in Genthon & Thomas's 2026 arXiv preprint (see literature comparison); I do not claim that effect as new. The model-specific point is that an autonomous hidden program can create the forward conditional-mean adder, while the standard tree tilt then produces a sizer-like retrospective slope.

## Decisive causal protocol and predictions

Use a growing microfluidic chamber that resolves complete trees rather than retaining only one pole lineage. Measure three-dimensional volume, cell age since birth, division age, growth trajectory, daughter fraction, candidate hidden-state/growth reporters, and every death, escape, or censoring event. At birth, randomize a short reversible perturbation that shifts volume by ±δ in one sister while applying a sham to the other; restore the identical medium before appreciable growth. Repeat at several δ values and across hidden-state strata, with randomized assignment at the sister-pair level. Analyze intention-to-treat outcomes for every assigned cell, including death and failure to divide; separately compute the forward-lineage and random-tip tree statistics. The perturbation must change volume without changing the hidden program or division machinery. This exclusion condition is essential: an osmotic pulse or septation perturbation that changes physiology cannot by itself identify a pure size effect. Use at least two independent volume-shift methods and reporters of growth and division state to test this condition.

Estimate the controlled causal response

    S_Δ(h) = ∂ E[Δ | do(B=b), H=h]/∂b,
    R_k(h) = ∂ E[B_{n+k}|do(B_n=b),H_n=h]/∂b,

and the causal age response ∂E[T|do(B),H]/∂B. In the idealized symmetric cases:

    physical adder: S_Δ=0, R_1=1/2, R_k=2^(−k);
    physical sizer: S_Δ=−1, R_1=0;
    size-independent exponential timer: S_Δ=G−1, R_1=G/2.

In the hidden Markov examples, the unperturbed slopes are exactly 0 or −1 but the randomized S_Δ is positive and R_k does not decay. A simple additional timing intervention (randomly lengthen the timer or alter growth rate at fixed B,H) distinguishes an additive age/growth program from a size-threshold rule: under the additive timer, Δ changes with the imposed growth interval while T is set by the timer; under a size-triggered rule, division is delayed/advanced to recover the target size. Track all daughters so tree weighting can be calculated rather than mistaken for cell-autonomous response.

The experiment can establish a cell-autonomous causal restoration law against survivor selection. It cannot establish an active size sensor from that result alone: a passive additive-growth adder is a counterexample. “Physical size regulation” should therefore be reported operationally as the response of division and descendants to a randomized volume perturbation, with molecular sensing reserved for mechanism-specific perturbations.

## Primary-source comparison and biological limits

1. Taheri-Araghi et al., “Cell-size control and homeostasis in bacteria,” Current Biology 25 (2015), 385–391, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2014.12.009. The primary study followed large numbers of E. coli and B. subtilis in mother-machine lineages under steady growth and reported constant mean added volume independent of newborn size. This is evidence for a forward-lineage phenomenological adder in the tested strains/conditions; it is not a proof that every cell type or condition is a universal adder, nor by itself a randomized birth-volume intervention.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.12.009

2. Campos et al., “A constant size extension drives bacterial cell size homeostasis,” Cell 159 (2014), 1433–1446, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2014.11.022. Single-cell imaging and modeling in E. coli and Caulobacter crescentus supported constant extension and size convergence. This provides a direct antecedent for the additive map's contraction; it does not support the claim that published adder data are merely sampling artifacts.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2014.11.022

3. Si et al., “Mechanistic origin of cell-size control and homeostasis in bacteria,” Current Biology 29 (2019), 1760–1770.e7, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2019.04.062. The authors proposed threshold accumulation of division proteins plus balanced biosynthesis as a division-adder mechanism, and used dynamic perturbations of cell-cycle protein production to alter initiation/division behavior. This is important mechanistic evidence beyond a size regression; it is not negated by the observational-equivalence construction.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.04.062

4. Tanouchi et al., “A noisy linear map underlies oscillations in cell size and gene expression in bacteria,” Nature 523 (2015), 357–360, DOI 10.1038/nature14562. The study measured long E. coli lineages and found transient multigeneration cell-size oscillations, modeled by a noisy linear size map. It establishes that lineage autocorrelation structure can be rich and averaged slopes can suppress it; it does not establish the hidden timer model here.
   https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14562

5. Wang et al., “Robust Growth of Escherichia coli,” Current Biology 20 (2010), 1099–1103, DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2010.04.045. In the tested E. coli strains, single-cycle growth-rate correlation was weak and its timescale was reported as less than one generation. This is adverse evidence against treating the multistate hidden program here as a realistic account of those E. coli conditions. The construction is an identifiability counterexample, not an empirical model claim.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2010.04.045

6. Genthon & Thomas, “Cell size control in bacteria is modulated through extrinsic noise, single-cell- and population-growth,” arXiv:2601.05193v1 (posted 2026-01-08). This is a primary theoretical preprint (not treated here as peer-reviewed). It derives distinct forward-lineage and population-tree stochastic maps and reports that noise can make an adder more sizer-like or timer-like in tree statistics. Therefore population modulation is prior work; novelty of the present hidden-state observational-equivalence construction is unresolved and no priority claim is made.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05193

The cited bacterial studies use length as a size proxy in some organisms/conditions; this record's equations use true volume and symmetric volume partition. A real test must measure volume or justify constant-width conversion, and must model division asymmetry, pole tracking, death, and censoring explicitly. No conclusion about mammalian cell cycles, yeast, or bacteria outside the cited conditions follows from these constructions.

## Counterexamples, alternatives, and unresolved inference

* Equation (2) fails for asymmetric division, a nonstationary lineage, or an event sample whose parent and child variables are not paired under the same measure. Those cases require the actual partition kernel and the actual sampling law.
* A fixed-time timer with iid size-independent exponential growth and no cell-state reset has log birth size equal to a random walk. It has no stationary finite-width lineage distribution without selection or feedback. The finite-state examples avoid that random walk by placing the natural trajectory on B=b_H; they deliberately reveal a conserved hidden scale, not an attracting size controller.
* Independent random partition fractions add a random walk to the timer's log size. The timer counterexample's stationary size distribution is therefore fragile to such noise. By contrast, an additive map B' = Φ(B+A+η), with Φ bounded below 1, is a contracting stochastic recurrence with a unique finite-variance stationary law under standard finite-moment assumptions. This law can be passive with respect to B and does not imply an active size sensor.
* Conditioning on survival can create a stationary retrospective lineage even when forward trajectories do not restore size. Such an analysis must include every initiated cell and count death, failed division, exit from the field, and missing observations as outcomes; dropping these cases can itself create the effect being tested.
* The exclusion restriction for the intervention is the main experimental gap. A size manipulation that changes osmolarity, septum placement, DNA replication, or division-protein abundance can directly alter the outcome. Matched sisters, multiple perturbation methods, short pulses, and reporter checks reduce but cannot logically eliminate unmeasured off-target effects.
* The current result is a candidate identifiability theorem plus an experimental protocol. It does not establish that growth-rate memory or tree weighting explains any published adder/sizer result, that the state chain is biologically realized, or that the protocol is technically available for every organism. External biological validation and novelty review remain open.

## Strongest supported conclusion

Under symmetric binary fission, an unperturbed added-size regression is a lag-one birth-size correlation. Even a mixing, finite-noise stationary hidden growth/timer program can produce exact conditional-mean adder or sizer laws with no dependence of division on actual size. In the explicit forward-adder program, changing timer durations and sampling ancestral paths from the growing tree changes the slope to −1.2235. More strongly, a size-independent multiplicative timer and a true additive-increment mechanism can be made exactly identical on all unperturbed cell trajectories while predicting different randomized-volume responses. Therefore the regression, and even perfect baseline time-lapse data, do not by themselves identify the causal size law. A randomized size perturbation with complete lineage/survival accounting identifies the operational response; separate growth/timing interventions are needed before naming a sensor.
