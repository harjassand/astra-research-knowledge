1. EXACT STATEMENT AND QUANTIFIERS

Let d be a positive integer, tau(A)=Tr(A)/d, and sigma=I/d. A quantum
channel acts in the Schrodinger picture on matrices. For any bistochastic
(completely positive, trace-preserving, unital) channel Theta define, on
Hermitian A,

  E_Theta(A)=tau[A(A-Theta(A))].

Let Phi be bistochastic and self-adjoint for the Hermitian Hilbert-Schmidt
pairing. Let Psi be ANY fixed bistochastic quantum channel, and let C>0,
such that on EVERY Hermitian A,

  E_Psi(A) <= C E_Phi(A).                                  (1)

No self-adjointness, HS positivity, entanglement-breaking property, or
compatibility premise is required of Psi for this analytic lemma. Physical
CP, TP and unitality ARE required; an arbitrary HS-ordered linear operator
is not a substitute. Then, simultaneously for EVERY density matrix rho,
with natural logarithms and HALF trace distances,

  D = D(rho||sigma) = Tr[rho log(d rho)],
  b = (1/2)||rho-Phi(rho)||_1,
  e = (1/2)||rho-Psi(rho)||_1,

one has

  e^3 <= (27 C/4) D b.                                    (2)

This includes singular rho, D=0, b=0, and d=1. Thus the same supplied Psi
works for all states; no state-dependent selection of a comparator occurs.

BROADCASTING COROLLARY. Suppose a CPTP B:M_d -> M_d tensor M_d has both
marginals Phi, and Phi is bistochastic and HS-self-adjoint. The supplied
universal C4 theorem chooses ONE finite pure-ensemble canonical EB Psi,
depending on the supplied channel/broadcaster, for which (1) holds with
C=4. Fix it once. Every density matrix rho then satisfies

  e^3 <= 27 D b,
  e <= min{1, 3(D b)^(1/3)},                               (3)
  e^2 <= 3*2^(2/3) (D+1) b^(2/3)
       <= 5 (D+1) b^(2/3).                                (4)

The reference is exactly sigma=I/d, and b is the residual of the supplied
Phi. There is no family, prior, Holevo-center or spectral-gap hypothesis.
Both marginals and the actual joint channel are needed only to invoke C4;
the analytic transfer is a theorem for every pair satisfying (1).

2. LEGAL REVERSIBLE ENTROPY FLOW

Write L=I-Phi. For t>=0 define

  Q_t=exp(-t L)=exp(-t) sum_(n=0)^infinity (t^n/n!) Phi^n.  (5)

Every Phi^n is CPTP and unital, and the scalar weights in (5) are
nonnegative and sum to one. Hence Q_t is a bistochastic quantum channel.
In finite dimension the series converges in norm. The self-adjointness of
Phi makes Q_t HS-self-adjoint, but its CP property comes from the Poisson
mixture, not from any positivity of Phi as an HS operator. In particular,
negative eigenvalues of Phi cause no exception.

First assume rho>0, put X=d rho, and define

  X_t=Q_t(X), rho_t=X_t/d, H(t)=tau[X_t log X_t].

If X>=a I for a>0, positivity and unitality give X_t>=a I. Also tau X_t=1.
Consequently H is differentiable and

  H'(t)=-tau[(L X_t) log X_t].                             (6)

Indeed the derivative of Tr[X_t log X_t] is
Tr[X'_t(log X_t+I)]; the identity term vanishes since tau X'_t=0.
The trace differentiation formula holds without X_t commuting with X'_t:
diagonalize X_t and use the diagonal first derivative of a trace spectral
function. No simultaneous diagonalization along the flow is assumed.

3. THE STATE-DEPENDENT CLASSICAL TRANSITION MATRIX IS LEGAL

At any fixed t, choose an orthonormal eigenbasis {|i>} of X_t, with
eigenvalues x_i>0, and set P_i=|i><i| and

  p_ij = Tr[P_i Phi(P_j)].

These are real nonnegative numbers by positivity of Phi. Unitality gives
sum_j p_ij=1; trace preservation gives sum_i p_ij=1; HS self-adjointness
gives p_ij=p_ji. Degenerate eigenvalues permit any orthonormal eigenbasis.
These entrywise transition probabilities do NOT become negative when
Phi has a negative HS eigenvalue. The basis and p_ij may change with t.

For any real function h of the x_i, direct expansion yields

  E_Phi(h(X_t))
    = (1/(2d)) sum_ij p_ij (h(x_i)-h(x_j))^2,              (7)

and (6) becomes

  -H'(t)
    = (1/(2d)) sum_ij p_ij (x_i-x_j)(log x_i-log x_j).      (8)

These identities require only the diagonal entries of Phi(P_j) in this
basis; Phi need not preserve the diagonal algebra. They are therefore
valid for fully noncommuting quantum states and channels.

For positive a,b,

  (a-b)(log a-log b) >= 4(sqrt(a)-sqrt(b))^2.               (9)

For a>b this is the integral Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
 (integral_b^a 1 ds)(integral_b^a ds/s)
 >= (integral_b^a ds/sqrt(s))^2;
the other cases follow by symmetry or equality. Substituting (9) in (8)
and using (7) gives

  -H'(t) >= 4 E_Phi(sqrt(X_t)).                            (10)

H(t)>=0 because tau X_t=1 and the scalar function x log x is convex:
tau X_t log X_t >= (tau X_t) log(tau X_t)=0. In particular, for every T>0,

  integral_0^T E_Phi(sqrt(X_t)) dt <= D/4.                 (11)

The integrand is continuous, so there exists s in [0,T] with

  E_Phi(sqrt(X_s)) <= D/(4T).                              (12)

The choice of s is only an analytic comparison point. It does not modify
Psi or prescribe a channel implementation.

4. QUANTUM JENSEN AND TRACE DISTANCE, WITH ALL FACTORS

For any bistochastic CPTP Psi and any X>=0 with tau X=1, let A=sqrt(X).
The positive block matrix

  [ X  A ] = [A] [A I]
  [ A  I ]   [I]

remains positive after applying id_2 tensor Psi. Since Psi(I)=I, its
Schur complement gives

  Psi(A)^2 <= Psi(X).

Psi(A)>=0, and square root is operator monotone on positive matrices.
Thus

  Psi(A) <= sqrt(Psi(X)).                                 (13)

For completeness, square-root monotonicity follows from
 sqrt(Y)=(1/pi) integral_0^infinity Y(Y+u I)^(-1) du/sqrt(u),
using inverse-order monotonicity and limits for singular Y. Inverse-order
monotonicity follows by conjugating 0<U<=V by U^(-1/2) and then inverting.

Multiplication inside a trace by positive A preserves the scalar order,
even though A and the difference in (13) may not commute. Therefore

  a := Tr[sqrt(X/d) sqrt(Psi(X)/d)]
     = tau[A sqrt(Psi(X))]
     >= tau[A Psi(A)]
     = 1-E_Psi(A).                                       (14)

Here 0<=E_Psi(A)<=1. The upper bound uses Tr[A Psi(A)]>=0. For the lower
bound, trace the Schwarz inequality to get tau Psi(A)^2<=tau A^2=1,
then use Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz to obtain tau A Psi(A)<=1.
Also 0<=a<=1 by Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz.

The following trace-distance bound needs no optimization over purifications.
The canonical vectorizations of sqrt(X/d) and sqrt(Psi(X)/d) are unit
vectors with overlap a and reduced states X/d and Psi(X)/d. The HALF
trace distance between their rank-one projectors is sqrt(1-a^2), as follows
by diagonalizing their span. Partial trace contracts Hermitian trace norm.
Consequently

  T(X/d,Psi(X)/d)^2 <= 1-a^2
                    <= 2 E_Psi(sqrt(X))-E_Psi(sqrt(X))^2
                    <= 2 E_Psi(sqrt(X)).                  (15)

Equivalently, root fidelity is at least the affinity a; the usual upper
Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality gives the same factors. The displayed
purification proof makes the HALF trace-distance convention explicit.
No assumption of a random-unitary realization is made anywhere.

Combining (1), (12), and (15) at X_s gives

  T(rho_s,Psi(rho_s)) <= sqrt(C D/(2T)).                    (16)

5. APPROXIMATE INVARIANCE OF THE ENTROPY FLOW

The derivative equation and commutation of Phi with its exponential give

  rho_s-rho = integral_0^s Q_r(Phi(rho)-rho) dr.

A positive trace-preserving map contracts Hermitian trace norm: apply it
to the positive and negative parts of a Hermitian matrix and use the
triangle inequality and trace preservation. Thus

  T(rho_s,rho) <= s b <= T b.                              (17)

Applying this same contraction to Psi and using two triangle inequalities
with the SAME fixed Psi yields

  e <= T(rho,rho_s)+T(rho_s,Psi(rho_s))
                     +T(Psi(rho_s),Psi(rho))
    <= 2 T b + sqrt(C D/(2T))                             (18)

for every T>0. This is the new power-producing interface: a near-fixed
state remains close for time T, while entropy dissipates enough square-
root Dirichlet energy during that interval to use the global comparator.
It replaces, for this tracial reversible problem, the exponential cutoff
parameter in the earlier smoothing route.

6. OPTIMIZATION AND ENDPOINTS

For D>0 and b>0 choose

  T = (C D/(32 b^2))^(1/3).

The square-root term in (18) then equals 4 b T. Thus e<=6 b T, whose cube
is exactly (27 C/4) D b. This proves (2) for positive rho and D,b>0.
No upper or lower constraint on T exists in (5)--(18).

If D=0, equality in strict scalar convexity of x log x forces every
eigenvalue of X to be 1; rho=sigma and Psi fixes rho, so e=0. If b=0,
let T tend to infinity in (18), obtaining e=0. These include d=1.
For b=1 the optimized formula remains valid; e<=1 always because it is
a HALF trace distance of density matrices. Taking the minimum with one
does not entail replacing Psi by another channel.

To derive (4) from C=4, square (3) and observe that for D>=0 the maximum
of D^(2/3)/(D+1) occurs at D=2, with value 2^(2/3)/3. Hence
  9 D^(2/3) <= 3*2^(2/3)(D+1) < 5(D+1).
The last strict numerical inequality is exact: (5/3)^3=125/27>4.

7. SINGULAR-STATE REGULARIZATION PRESERVES THE SAME COMPARATOR

For any possibly singular rho and 0<delta<1 set
  rho_delta=(1-delta)rho+delta sigma.
Since both channels fix sigma,
  b_delta=(1-delta)b, e_delta=(1-delta)e.
rho_delta is strictly positive, and x log x is continuous at zero under
the convention 0 log 0=0. Thus D_delta tends to D as delta tends to zero.
Applying (2) to rho_delta and taking the limit proves (2) for rho with
the original SAME Phi, Psi, C. The endpoint arguments extend by the same
limit, with no assumption of invariant support or a lower eigenvalue bound.
