## 2. Exact characteristic construction of H

Write

    A_r=(A_v+A_w)/2>0,
    A_t=(A_v-A_w)/2,
    alpha_sigma=A_r dr+A_t dt.

The kernel direction is the vector field X=A_w partial_v-A_v partial_w. Its t derivative is A_v+A_w>0. Reparameterize its curves by t:

    dr/dt=g(r,t)=(A_w-A_v)/(A_v+A_w), |g|<=1/3.        (4)

Take the inner triangle D: v,w>0, r<epsilon, with epsilon=epsilon_plus/2. For any point (r0,t0) in D, follow (4) from t0 to zero. Along this segment

    r(t)>=r0-|t-t0|/3>|t|,
    r(t)<=r0+|t0|/3<4epsilon/3<epsilon_plus.           (5)

The first inequality r(t)>|t| follows because |t| decreases monotonically toward zero, |t0|<r0, and r0-|t-t0|/3-|t|=(r0-|t0|)+2|t-t0|/3>0. Thus the whole trajectory stays in the larger physical positive-weight triangle. Its unique diagonal label is

    H(r0,t0)=r(0;r0,t0).                              (6)

Smooth ODE dependence gives XH=0. Its r0 derivative is

    H_r=exp[integral_(t0)^0 partial_r g(r(t),t)dt]>0. (7)

At every point, dH and alpha_sigma annihilate the same one-dimensional kernel. Matching their positive dr coefficients yields

    m_sigma=A_r/H_r>0,

## 3. A closed cycle has a strict negative interval

Let c(S) be a nonconstant smooth simple closed loop in D, flat at the endpoints of one fixed finite S interval. H(c(S)) has the same endpoint value. If H(c(S)) were constant, the image would lie in a single graph leaf r=r(t), which cannot contain a simple closed loop. Hence H(c(S)) is nonconstant. Its derivative must be negative on some open interval: if it were everywhere nonnegative, equality of endpoint values would force it identically zero. Continuity and (8) provide a smaller fixed interval I and a strict margin d>0 with

    alpha_sigma(c'(S))<=-d on I.                     (9)

The unweighted circulation can have either sign; (9) remains true for either orientation. A loop may have positive integral alpha_sigma while still having a strict negative interval. A retraced non-simple path contained in one kernel leaf is excluded from the stated simple-loop claim.
