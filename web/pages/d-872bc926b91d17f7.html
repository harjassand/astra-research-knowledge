# Source map and prior-art boundary

## Primary literature read

1. Haijie Xu and Chen Zhang, “FOCUS: Fixed-Confidence Online Causal Learning Using Sequential Adaptive Interventions,” arXiv:2609.32165v1, submitted 26 September 2026. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32165); [HTML source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32165v1). I read the abstract and the HTML sections on the model/action interface, fixed-confidence objective, information lower bound, endpoint reduction, and adaptive allocation (sections 3.1–4.3). It assumes a fully observed linear-Gaussian SEM with diagonal independent structural noise, permits passive samples and perfect single-node interventions, gives a max-min interventional KL lower bound, and proposes an online adaptive max-min/track-and-stop procedure. Its natural unit is a sampling round. The source does not model unobserved common causes or separately selectable measurement precision and per-action monetary cost. This is the closest current prior-art route; it rules out claiming that adaptive fixed-confidence causal design or a KL lower bound is new. It does not by itself imply the latent-confounding/cost/precision specialization here.

2. Xiaotian Hou, Kwangmoon Park, and Hongzhe Li, “Statistical Inference for Causal Discovery under Selection and Latent Variables via Single-Target Interventions,” arXiv:2609.28856v1, submitted 23 September 2026. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28856); [HTML source](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28856v1). I read the abstract, setup, latent/selection model, and identifiability/inference overview. The source gives a model-free MAG/system-subgraph target, says single-target interventions on every observed system variable are sufficient and worst-case necessary for unique identification, and gives a two-stage procedure with asymptotic family-wise error control under a first-stage power condition and at most (5d_X^2/2) statistical tests. This is a substantial adjacent latent-confounding prior. It measures constraint-query/test complexity and assumes supplied observational/interventional regimes; it does not solve the scalar fixed-confidence action-cost/precision allocation in this note. Its v1 is a recent preprint, not external review.

3. Alain Hauser and Peter Bühlmann, “Characterization and Greedy Learning of Interventional Markov Equivalence Classes of Directed Acyclic Graphs,” *Journal of Machine Learning Research* 13(79):2409–2464, 2012. [JMLR paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/hauser12a.html); [PDF](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume13/hauser12a/hauser12a.pdf). The paper extends DAG Markov equivalence to multiple intervention distributions and shows that interventions refine the observational equivalence partition under its assumptions. This supports the broad mechanism that experiments can orient observationally ambiguous causal models; it does not state the cost law in this note.
