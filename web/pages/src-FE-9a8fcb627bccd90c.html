1. Fixed model and information contract

There is a specified finite reversible network with grounded target, known
positive transient masses m, known incidence vectors d_e, and independent
conductance bounds l_e<=c_e<=u_e. A(c)=sum_e c_e d_e d_e^T and A(l)>0.
The objective is F(c)=m^T A(c)^(-1)m, or the equilibrium-conditioned mean
F(c)/sum_i m_i. All masses and possible moves are supplied. Graph discovery,
mass acquisition, physical calibration, interval validity, arithmetic and
query costs remain charged. No arbitrary-source or uncertain-mass extension
is asserted. Dividing by fixed total mass changes neither width ordering nor
the upper/lower ratio.

Let H=F(l), L=F(u), Delta=u_e-l_e. At the current box define

  v_-=d_e^T A(l)^(-1)m, R_-=d_e^T A(l)^(-1)d_e,
  v_+=d_e^T A(u)^(-1)m, R_+=d_e^T A(u)^(-1)d_e.

For 0<=D<=Delta set

  G_high(D)=D v_-^2/(1+D R_-),
  G_low(D) =D v_+^2/(1-D R_+).

The second denominator is positive since A(u)-D d_e d_e^T>=A(l)>0.

2. Exact theorem for a bounded-width interval measurement

Suppose one query is guaranteed to return a valid subinterval [a,b] of
[l_e,u_e] with b-a<=w, where 0<=w<=Delta. The information class after the
query is exactly the remaining independent box with this edge restricted to
[a,b]. Over ALL such legal interval outcomes, the largest remaining width
and largest upper/lower ratio occur at [l_e,l_e+w] or [u_e-w,u_e]. Therefore
with D=Delta-w the worst remaining ratio is exactly

  Q_e(w)=max{ H/(L+G_low(D)), (H-G_high(D))/L },               (P1)

and the guaranteed additive decrease is exactly

  min{G_low(D),G_high(D)}.                                 (P2)

Among equal-cost queries with the same stated interval-return contract,
minimizing P1 is exactly one-step minimax for multiplicative uncertainty.
If edge-specific resolutions w_e are available, insert their own values.
Selecting a measurement resolution at a cost C_e(w_e) is also possible by
comparing these finitely supplied options; no globally optimal budget policy
or measurement-cost law is supplied by P1.

Proof. Enlarging a returned interval can only enlarge its completion width
and ratio, by Rayleigh monotonicity. Any interval of width<=w extends within
[l_e,u_e] to one of width w. Write a=l_e+p and b=l_e+p+w, with 0<=p<=D.
The post-query pessimistic and optimistic matrices are

  B_-=A(l),
  B_+=A(u)-D d_e d_e^T,

each with the common update p d_e d_e^T. Since B_+>=B_->0, the source
rank-one endpoint theorem applies to their inverse quadratic forms. For
completeness, their Sherman-Morrison forms are f(p)=f0-p a0^2/(1+p r) and
g(p)=g0-p b0^2/(1+p s), with r>=s>=0. For lambda>=0 the derivative

  (f-lambda g)'=-a0^2/(1+p r)^2+lambda b0^2/(1+p s)^2

can change only from negative to positive because (1+p r)/(1+p s) is
nondecreasing. Thus f-lambda g has no interior strict maximum. Lambda=1
gives the width statement; lambda=max endpoint f/g gives the ratio bound.
The two endpoints p=0,D are legal width-w outcomes and attain the bound.
Sherman-Morrison relative to A(l),A(u) gives P1 and P2. QED.

For a measurement y with guaranteed absolute error |y-c_e|<=h, retain
[max(l_e,y-h),min(u_e,y+h)]. If 2h<Delta, the exact worst case is at
y=l_e+h or y=u_e-h, and P1 applies with w=2h. Outer clipped regimes are
monotone toward those central endpoints. If 2h>=Delta there is a legal y
whose interval is the whole prior, so the guaranteed improvement is zero.
Thus one may use w=min(2h,Delta) without pretending the rate became exact.

This theorem gives conditional deterministic coverage. To use statistical
rate confidence intervals adaptively, require conditional coverage given the
past (or a simultaneously valid confidence sequence). A preassigned failure
budget alpha_j with sum_j alpha_j<=alpha gives joint coverage at least
1-alpha by the union bound, even with adaptive edge choices, provided each
conditional bound is valid. Ordinary fixed-time intervals at an unaccounted
optional stopping time do not meet that premise. The physical cost needed to
reach h or w is outside this algebraic query theorem.

3. Factor-relative measurements require a different exact rule

Suppose instead a sensor returns y with c_e/κ<=y<=κ c_e, κ>=1. Put k=κ^2.
The retained interval is [max(l_e,y/κ),min(u_e,κ y)]. Assume first
k*l_e<u_e, and parameterize its unclipped part by a=y/κ and b=k*a.
Then a ranges from l_e to u_e/k. Put p=a-l_e and P=u_e/k-l_e.

Take B_-=A(l) and B_+=A(u)-(u_e-k*l_e)d_e d_e^T. Both are positive
definite and B_+>=B_-. Write

  f0=m^T B_-^(-1)m, a0=d_e^T B_-^(-1)m, r=d_e^T B_-^(-1)d_e,
  g0=m^T B_+^(-1)m, b0=d_e^T B_+^(-1)m, s=d_e^T B_+^(-1)d_e.

The extremal quadratic forms become

  f(p)=f0-p a0^2/(1+r p),
  g(p)=g0-k p b0^2/(1+k s p).                              (P3)

Unlike the exact or additive-width query, the common parameter now has
different slopes. Inverse ordering gives r>=s but need not give r>=k*s.
The derivative argument therefore permits an interior maximum. The two
clipped outer regimes are monotone toward their central endpoints and need
no extra candidates. If k*l_e>=u_e a whole-prior interval is possible and
the exact worst outcome is the original box. When l_e=0, any finite factor
error still gives the exact singleton at a zero outcome, and P3 remains
valid under the same grounded-SPD premise.

An exact constant-size candidate reduction nevertheless survives. Define

  alpha=f0, beta=f0*r-a0^2,
  gamma=g0, delta=k*(g0*s-b0^2).

Then the remaining ratio is

  R(p)=[(alpha+beta p)(1+k s p)] /
        [(gamma+delta p)(1+r p)]=N(p)/D(p).                 (P4)

All denominators are positive. If
N=n0+n1 p+n2 p^2 and D=d0+d1 p+d2 p^2, its derivative has numerator

  Z(p)=(n1*d0-n0*d1)
       +2*(n2*d0-n0*d2)*p
       +(n2*d1-n1*d2)*p^2.                                (P5)

Hence the exact worst ratio occurs at p=0,P or at at most two real roots
of this quadratic inside [0,P]. If Z vanishes identically the ratio is
constant. Rational inputs yield rational polynomial coefficients and
algebraic degree at most two; exact root isolation and algebraic comparison
can evaluate or outward-enclose these candidates. This does not remove the
cost of the required exact network solves or coefficient bit lengths.

For additive width, (f-g)' vanishes at at most one interior point unless
identically zero. Since the denominators are positive, its equation is

  |a0|*(1+k s p)=sqrt(k)*|b0|*(1+r p),                     (P6)

a linear equation over the algebraic field of sqrt(k). Endpoints plus the
valid root give the exact width supremum, including the zero-coefficient
monotone/constant cases. P5 and P6 are exact algebraic alternatives to an
incorrect blanket endpoint rule.

4. Exact factor-relative counterexample

Use two transient states with masses (2,8). The edge order is 0-to-sink,
1-to-sink, and 0-to-1. Prior lower bounds are (1,1/8,8), upper bounds
(2,513/8,10); all lower conductances are positive. Query the second edge
with κ=2, hence an unclipped outcome interval [a,4a], where
1/8<=a<=513/32. Direct inversion gives

  R(a)=2*(a+216)*(12*a+5)/[(2*a+141)*(9*a+8)].

At the central endpoints the ratios are

  R(1/8)=89908/41245,
  R(513/32)=1421100/408889,

while the interior legal answer interval [6,24] gives

  R(6)=5698/1581 > max{89908/41245,1421100/408889}.

The measurement value is y=12, compatible with rates in [6,24]. The exact
derivative is

  R'(a)=-12*(5221*a^2+1968*a-256936) /
         [(2*a+141)^2*(9*a+8)^2].

The unique positive critical point
(-984+638*sqrt(3298))/5221 lies in the legal interval and is the strict
global maximum on that interval. This is a counterexample to the named
factor-relative endpoint extension; it does not refute the source exact
query theorem or the additive-width theorem above. It uses the same fixed
mass/source/grounding scope and requires no molecular approximation.
