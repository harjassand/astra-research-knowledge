# ASTRA Meta-Genesis: joint profile circulation and exact cycle compensation

Research date: 14 October 2026 (Australia/Brisbane).

## Status and scope

This investigation does not prove that every rank-one-convex planar quartic is quasiconvex. It does not construct a compatible negative quasiconvexity gap. It develops a joint representation and two positive results which do not require resonance-separated carriers:

1. A two-column Bregman identity represents the full energy of an arbitrary slice-profile loop as nonnegative remainders plus one joint circulation. The representation is available for every smooth periodic potential, not only modulation templates.
2. For polynomial energies of degree at most four, vanishing polynomial circulation through degree three yields all-affine Jensen positivity. For two profile coordinates, six signed area moments suffice. This holds in every target dimension with two input coordinates, without rotational invariance. A concrete finite-difference operation constructs nontrivial interacting loops satisfying the condition; it does not preserve the energy of an arbitrary original loop.
3. An exact rational graph calibration proves a uniform all-affine inequality for every closed walk in a specified library of two interacting opposite-carrier profiles for the previously certified non-polyconvex integrand G. Negative strip energies are allowed. All 32,805 edge/width inequalities are verified exactly.

The circulation identity is not itself a positivity proof for unrestricted loops. The positive results impose either exact moment cancellation or a finite-library calibration. Their ingredients—rank-one supporting inequalities, Green's theorem, polynomial line integrals, finite differences, perspective convexity, and shortest-path potentials—are established mathematics. Historical priority for the particular combination has not been established. This is not a claim to have invented those general tools or a fundamentally new universally applicable variational principle.

Nine groups of exact checks passed. The preceding modulation verifier was also replayed with nine groups passing. Analytic proofs are given here; symbolic checks are not a formal proof of them. No external review, proof-assistant validation, repository update, or resolution of the full invariant cone is claimed.

## 2. A joint identity for arbitrary profile loops

Let F:R^(m x 2)->R be C^1 and rank-one convex. Work on a rectangle periodic in x and y, with x period L. Write E_y for normalized averaging in y. For a smooth periodic potential phi define

    P=(phi_x | 0),       Q=(0 | phi_y).

Both are rank-one matrices pointwise, although P+Q need not be rank one. For a rank-one increment Z put

    R_F(C,Z)=F(C+Z)-F(C)-DF(C)[Z] >= 0.

Fix an arbitrary constant affine matrix B. Direct subtraction gives

    F(B+P+Q)-F(B)
      = R_F(B+Q,P)+R_F(B,Q)
        +DF(B+Q)[P]+DF(B)[Q].                         (2.1)

The last term has zero y-average at each x.

View u(x)=phi(x,.) as a closed curve in the vector space of periodic target-valued profiles. Define a one-form on this profile space by

    alpha_(F,B)(u)[v]
       = E_y DF(B+(0|u_y))[(v|0)].                    (2.2)

Then

    L J_F(B,Dphi)
      = integral_0^L E_y [R_F(B+Q,P)+R_F(B,Q)] dx
        + integral_u alpha_(F,B),                    (2.3)

where J is the probability-normalized Jensen gap on the rectangle. Equation (2.3) is a global identity. It uses neither polar coordinates nor absence of zeros, and it imposes no affine-phase or frequency-separation condition.

The circulation is genuinely joint: alpha is evaluated at the complete profile u, not at one carrier at a time. It may have either sign. The positive remainders retain the full carrier interactions as well.

### Finite profile coordinates

For computation and explicit constructions choose arbitrary smooth periodic profiles psi_1,...,psi_d in R^m and write

    phi(x,y)=sum_j a_j(x) psi_j(y),

where a:[0,L]->R^d is a closed path. Then

    alpha_j(a)=E_y DF(B+(0|sum_i a_i psi_i'))[(psi_j|0)]. (2.4)

Every smooth periodic potential is approximable in W^(1,4) by such fields: first take a finite Fourier sum in y, then approximate the periodic coefficient curves in x by polygonal curves. This density statement does not impose any circulation cancellation on the approximants.

For a polynomial F of degree at most four, alpha_j is a polynomial in a of degree at most three. Thus the signed part of (2.3) depends only on the integrals

    integral_a a^beta da_j,      |beta|<=3.            (2.5)

These are a subset of finite-order polynomial path data, not the full path signature. Calling them polynomial circulation moments makes the exact scope clear.

## 3. Global quartic-circulation compensation

### Theorem 3.1

Let F be any rank-one-convex polynomial of degree at most four on R^(m x 2). Let a be a closed piecewise-C^1 path, or a closed W^(1,4) path, and let psi_j be periodic smooth profiles. Suppose

    integral_a a^beta da_j = 0
    for every j and every |beta|<=3.                  (3.1)

Then for every constant B,

    J_F(B,Dphi)>=0.                                    (3.2)

Let F_4 denote the homogeneous fourth-degree part and define

    mu_F=min{F_4(R): |R|=1, rank R=1}>=0.

Then the stronger conclusion is

    inf_B J_F(B,Dphi) >= (mu_F/6) E|Dphi|^4.            (3.3)

No rotational invariance is used. The theorem therefore covers the entire nine-coefficient left-SO(2) cone and, subject to rank-one convexity, all 35 homogeneous planar quartic coefficients. It also applies to larger target dimensions with two input coordinates; it does not assert quasiconvexity of those integrands for arbitrary gradients.

### Proof

Equation (3.1) makes the circulation (2.4) vanish for every F and every B under consideration. Equation (2.3) is then a sum of nonnegative terms, proving (3.2).

For the quantitative claim, restrict F to a rank-one line C+tR and write

    f(t)=At^4+Lt^3+C_2 t^2+Dt+E,       A=F_4(R)>=0.

When A>0, f''>=0 on the whole real line gives C_2>=3L^2/(8A), and

    A+L+C_2-A/3
       =3(L+4A/3)^2/(8A)+C_2-3L^2/(8A)>=0.

Thus R_F(C,R)>=F_4(R)/3. If A=0, f'' is a nonnegative linear polynomial, forcing L=0 and C_2>=0; the same result follows. Applying the bound to both rank-one increments in (2.3) gives

    J_F >= (mu_F/3) E(|phi_x|^4+|phi_y|^4)
         >= (mu_F/6) E|Dphi|^4.

This is uniform in B before taking any infimum. It neither freezes an optimizing direction nor replaces a Schur denominator. QED.

### Regularity and concentration

One-dimensional W^(1,4) coefficient paths are continuous and bounded. With fixed smooth profiles, all gradients lie in L^4, and the polynomial integrals are continuous under strong W^(1,4) approximation. This proves the stated nonsmooth-path version. Polygonal paths give continuous potentials with piecewise smooth compatible gradients; derivative jumps at x interfaces do not introduce a jump in the potential itself.

For zero-boundary rectangular examples, take all psi_j zero at the y endpoints and let the amplitude path begin and end at zero. The same proof applies. A critically rescaled zero-boundary example rho^(1/2)phi(x/rho) in two dimensions remains an example, and for homogeneous quartics both its fourth gradient integral and its completely optimized unnormalized gap are unchanged. The construction includes such concentrations. It does not express arbitrary concentrated profiles in this class.

## 4. Six signed area moments suffice for two joint amplitudes

Let d=2, with coordinates (r,s). Let w_a(r,s) be the integer winding number of the closed polygonal or piecewise smooth amplitude path. Define

    M_ij(a)=integral_R2 w_a(r,s) r^i s^j dr ds,
            i,j>=0, i+j<=2.                          (4.1)

The sign is the oriented winding sign; these are not moments of a positive probability distribution.

### Corollary 4.1

If all six moments

    M00, M10, M01, M20, M11, M02

vanish, Theorem 3.1 applies to

    phi=r(x) psi_1(y)+s(x) psi_2(y)

for arbitrary profiles psi_1,psi_2 and every rank-one-convex quartic.

### Proof

Green's theorem, including winding multiplicity, gives

    integral_a alpha_1 dr+alpha_2 ds
      = integral_R2 w_a(r,s)(partial_r alpha_2-partial_s alpha_1) dr ds.

The polynomial in parentheses has degree at most two. This proves the claim directly. It is also equivalent to (3.1) for polynomial one-forms: derivatives of their coefficients have degree at most two, and exact one-forms already integrate to zero.

For homogeneous quartics the curvature has total degree two jointly in B,r,s. Therefore the zero-area moment removes its quadratic-in-B part, the two first area moments remove its linear-in-B part, and the three quadratic area moments remove its B-independent part. Exact cancellation handles all affine directions together. Merely making the moments small does not, without further estimates, supply a uniform bound for arbitrarily large B.

More explicitly, with Q=(0|r psi_1'+s psi_2'),

    partial_r alpha_2-partial_s alpha_1
      = E_y { D^2F(B+Q)[(0|psi_1'),(psi_2|0)]
              -D^2F(B+Q)[(0|psi_2'),(psi_1|0)] }.       (4.2)

No sign is assigned to this curvature. The theorem controls its complete circulation by cancellation of the relevant moments.

## 5. A constructive cancellation operation with nonzero higher circulation

Given any closed amplitude loop Gamma in R^d and a fixed vector v, let T_v Gamma denote its translate. Form the oriented current

    Gamma -3 T_v Gamma +3 T_(2v) Gamma -T_(3v) Gamma.    (5.1)

Realize this with one traversal, three reverse traversals, three forward traversals, and one reverse traversal respectively. Join each loop to a common base point by a connector and its exact reversal. The result is one closed compatible amplitude path. The positive elapsed times of all traversals and connectors can be arbitrary.

### Theorem 5.1

The path in (5.1) satisfies (3.1), hence produces the Jensen inequality (3.3).

Proof. If alpha is a polynomial one-form of degree at most three, then

    P(t)=integral_(T_(tv) Gamma) alpha

is a polynomial in t of degree at most two. A possible cubic translation coefficient would be a constant one-form integrated around a closed loop, and is zero. The third finite difference

    P(0)-3P(1)+3P(2)-P(3)

therefore vanishes. Connectors cancel as oriented integrals. QED.

This operation is not an energy-preserving transformation of Gamma. It constructs a different field whose combined interaction is certified. It is not a decomposition theorem for an arbitrary given gradient. For degree at most p, the same argument uses a (p-1)-st finite difference and 2^(p-1) oriented loop traversals; the nonnegative Jensen statement follows without a quartic coercivity assertion.

### An explicit nontrivial path

Take Gamma to be the counterclockwise unit square [0,1]^2, translate it by (2j,0), j=0,1,2,3, and use coefficients 1,-3,3,-1. Connect to (0,0) along the bottom axis and return along the same segment. This gives a 46-segment closed polygonal path.

Its six moments in (4.1) vanish exactly, but

    integral w_a(r,s) r^3 dr ds = -48,
    integral_a r^4 ds = -192.                         (5.2)

Thus its full oriented current is nonzero. The cancellation is genuinely finite-order, not simple path retracing. No conclusion is asserted for all continuous rank-one-convex test functions; the test degree matters.

### Exact compatible field with a nonzero affine optimizer

Choose psi_1=(cos y,sin y), psi_2=(cos 2y,sin 2y), assign width one to every polygon segment, and use

    G(A)=3(u+v-2q)^2+(35/24)u^2+q^2-(5/2)qu.

The period in x is 46. With normalized averages, exact integration gives

    C=diag(56/23,458/69,56/23,458/69),
    b=(-3076/23,0,0,3064/23),
    E|Dphi|^4=191204/345,
    c=797092/345,

    B_opt=(-19549/92447,0,0,-96763/184894),
    Delta_G=73027952684/31894215.

The Hessian is positive definite and is supplied in loop_examples.json. The exact optimized value exceeds one half of the fourth gradient moment, as Theorem 3.1 predicts because mu_G=3. The result does not follow from zero cubic coupling: b is explicitly nonzero.

The rational acoustic certificate for G is reconstructed independently. Its 8-by-8 Gram has a positive 6-by-6 principal block with leading minors

    107/2, 642, 1605/8, 8445/16, 2815/8, 26865/4

and a zero remaining Schur complement. This proves its global rank-one convexity, not just finite angular sampling. A determinant-balanced scaling witness using +/-36 I and +/-diag(1,-1) proves non-polyconvexity; that probability law is not used as a compatible test.

## 6. Nonzero circulation can change fourth-order energy while H and b agree

For comparison, take a single counterclockwise unit square loop in amplitude space [1,2]^2, with unit time per edge, and profiles

    psi_1=(cos y,sin y),  psi_2=(cos 3y,sin 3y).

Reverse the same loop. Both resulting fields are genuine periodic gradients. They have the same covariance

    C=diag(1/2,145/12,1/2,145/12),

the same positive averaged Hessian for G, and b=0. Their fourth gradient moments both equal 25861/30. Nevertheless their fully optimized G gaps are

    Delta_forward=119299/40,
    Delta_reverse=106339/40,
    difference=324.                                      (6.1)

The zero cubic follows also from the half-period identity X(x,y+pi)=-X(x,y). The difference is carried by oriented fourth-order interaction. Both values are positive. This is not a quasiconvexity counterexample, but it rules out forgetting orientation in the profile loop when accounting for signed interactions.

## 7. Exact global compensation for nonzero currents: a profile graph

The vanishing-moment theorem does not cover every loop. A second operation certifies nonzero circulation by comparing complete strip actions with vertex potentials.

### 7.1 Compatible directed edges

Let U(y),V(y) be periodic profiles. For an edge of width tau>0 put

    phi(x,y)=(1-x/tau)U(y)+(x/tau)V(y),  0<=x<=tau.

Its gradient is exactly

    phi_x=(V-U)/tau,
    phi_y=(1-x/tau)U_y+(x/tau)V_y.

Closed walks concatenate these strips continuously, hence give actual periodic compatible potentials. A negative strip energy by itself is not a quasiconvexity counterexample: the strip does not close in x.

For a fixed B define the edge action

    W_B(U,V,tau)
      = integral_0^tau E_y [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)] dx.

For prescribed vertices and widths, a scalar potential P on vertices satisfying

    W_B(U,V,tau)>=P(V)-P(U)                              (7.1)

proves nonnegative energy for every finite closed walk, by telescoping. Negative edge costs are permitted. More generally, apply this to W_B minus eta times the fourth gradient integral to obtain a coercive bound.

This is the standard shortest-path/negative-cycle principle applied to exact compatible actions, not a new theorem of graph optimization. It avoids checking exponentially many closed walks individually. A negative closed walk for the original energy and a certified rank-one-convex F would be an actual counterexample; a failed attempted potential would not be one.

### 7.2 Exact certificate for G with opposite carriers

Use profiles

    U_(r,s)(y)=r exp(i y)+s exp(-i y),

identified with real two-component potentials. Vertices are

    (r,s)=(i/4,j/4),   i,j=0,...,8,

and allowed widths are

    1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2.

There are 81 vertices and 81^2*5=32,805 directed edge/width choices, including constant-profile edges. For each edge compute exactly

    w_e=integral G(Dphi_e) -(1/4) integral |Dphi_e|^4.

The rational potential in profile_graph_certificate.json satisfies

    w_e >= P(end)-P(start)

for every one of these 32,805 choices. All potential values have common denominator 245760. The verifier recomputes the edge costs from Laurent-polynomial Fourier integration, computes the integer shortest-path potential, and independently checks every edge inequality. Stored discovery output is not a premise.

There are 104 choices with negative raw G strip energy and 2,046 with negative adjusted cost w_e. The most negative raw edge is

    (5/4,2) -> (2,5/4),  width 1/2,
    integral G(Dphi_e)=-1431/1280.

The exact potential nevertheless satisfies all inequalities. Ninety-three edge/width inequalities are tight. No negative cycle of adjusted costs exists.

### Theorem 7.1 (the certified infinite class)

Every finite closed walk using these vertices and widths satisfies

    inf_B E[G(B+Dphi)-G(B)] >= (1/4) E|Dphi|^4.          (7.2)

Proof. The telescoping potential proves the zero-background bound for every closed walk. Because both carriers are odd, X(x,y+pi)=-X(x,y), so the cubic vector of every closed walk vanishes. For a rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic and a compatible gradient, H>=0: by reciprocity and Parseval, A:H A=E D^2G(A)[Dphi,Dphi] is a sum of nonnegative rank-one acoustic forms at the real and imaginary Fourier coefficients. Hence the full affine gap is c+(1/2)B:H B>=c, proving (7.2). QED.

This includes indefinitely long walks with arbitrary nonzero circulation and repeatedly interacting carriers. It is not a certificate for arbitrary real vertex values, arbitrary widths, arbitrary carrier profiles, or arbitrary integrands. Common amplitude scaling preserves the conclusion by homogeneity. It is an exact result for a specified infinite family, not a numerical lower bound for the unrestricted problem.

### 7.3 Exact integration and independently convex timing

For two distinct nonzero carriers n,m and real amplitudes r,s, write p1=r_x,p2=s_x and denote the matrix determinant by J. The required y averages are

    E u^2=p1^4+p2^4+4p1^2*p2^2,
    E v^2=n^4r^4+m^4s^4+4n^2m^2r^2s^2,
    E uv=(p1^2+p2^2)(n^2r^2+m^2s^2)+2nmrs*p1*p2,
    E w^2=(n*r*p2-m*s*p1)^2/2,
    E Ju=n*r*p1^3+m*s*p2^3+2m*s*p1^2*p2+2n*r*p1*p2^2,
    E Jv=n^3*r^3*p1+m^3*s^3*p2+2m*n^2*r^2*s*p2+2n*m^2*s^2*r*p1.

The notation J in this display denotes the determinant, not an input transformation. The remaining three odd-in-sine invariant averages are zero. The verifier reconstructs the averages with Laurent polynomials rather than trusting these display formulas.

On a straight edge, the action has the exact form sum_(j=0)^4 a_j tau^(1-j). For the original rank-one-convex energy at a fixed B, it is convex in tau. Indeed,

    d^2/dtau^2 {tau F(B+P/tau+Q)}
      =tau^(-3) D^2F(B+P/tau+Q)[P,P]>=0,

where P=(V-U|0) is rank one and Q=(0|(1-t)U_y+tV_y). Integrate over t and y. Thus timing a fixed geometric profile cycle can be optimized by a convex problem at fixed B. Joint optimization over B and geometric vertices is not asserted convex. Subtracting eta|Dphi|^4 can also destroy timing convexity; the graph certificate above uses only its specified finite width set.

## 8. A genuine reduction, and its limits

If a polygonal profile loop visits the same profile twice, it splits into two closed compatible loops. At each fixed B the unnormalized energies add exactly. Thus a negative loop at B contains a negative subloop at the same B; repeating removes repeated vertices. Full affine optimization gives the corresponding superadditivity of the unnormalized minima. This is a profile-level compatible extraction, not cutting gradients by arbitrary spatial indicators.

For a finite profile graph, the exact negative-cycle test either supplies a negative closed compatible walk or a telescoping vertex potential. For arbitrary gradients, finite Fourier approximation in y and polygonal approximation in x is dense in W^(1,4), so this representation does not exclude a smooth negative test merely because it has many resonances. But no single finite graph covers all its approximants. Enlarging a graph is a genuine enlargement of the searched class, not a proof by finite sampling.

The finite-difference construction of Section 5 also does not preserve the original optimized interaction. If an arbitrary negative profile existed, applying that operation would replace it with a different compensated field, not rule it out. Its mathematically useful content is a way to make signed, spatially correlated interactions cancel to exactly the degree seen by the test class.

A universal positive continuation would need a profile-space function P_B such that

    L_(F,B)(U,V)>=DP_B(U)[V]

for every periodic profile U and every velocity V, where L is the full averaged strip Lagrangian. Integrating along a closed path would then prove quasiconvexity. Such a domination is not supplied here. Closed-measure and Hamilton-Jacobi theory explain why this is a natural type of question, but do not prove its existence for these nonconvex variational integrands. Naming this condition is not an additional theorem.

One must control the negative circulation by the actual nonnegative remainders and their dependence on B, rather than demand each cross term nonnegative or make current moments small without an unbounded-background estimate.

## 9. Research record and significance assessment

An initial trial of a fixed polynomial calibration for two real opposite-carrier envelopes retained a negative numerical semidefinite margin for G. That was not an infeasibility proof and was not used as evidence of non-quasiconvexity. It motivated allowing joint profile loops rather than independently signing pair terms. Its exploratory SLSQP script is retained separately.

The main computational result is not another local optimizer minimum. It is a complete exact finite-graph certificate for every closed walk in the specified library, despite negative individual edges. No negative compatible cycle was found there. The finite proof remains a finite-library statement; it is not evidence of a lower bound over all graphs or all profiles.

What has been established is joint quartic-circulation compensation, an explicit nonzero-current construction annihilating the required finite moments, and a different exact counterexample/certification route through compatible profile cycles. The positivity conditions do not require resonance separation or an affine phase. They also are not automatic for general gradients.

The generic mathematical ingredients are known. This should not be described as a historically established new foundational operation or as resolution of the full cone. The current representation changes what can be certified and searched, but the universal domination of nonzero current remains open in this investigation. Repeating single-carrier searches or relying on more finite positive cycles would not settle that issue. Further concentrated inference is warranted only with a candidate joint profile-space calibration or a negative compatible cycle which survives exact checking; otherwise this branch should be preserved rather than treated as converging.
