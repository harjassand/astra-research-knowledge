# Exact multiwave repair responses, including all boundary defects

7 October 2026, cycle 10. **Our deduction; separate internal analytic gate PASS:** `notes/pde_gate10/MULTIWAVE_ACTUAL_COMPENSATION_GATE.md`. This is an exact cell lemma for the actual harmonic-repair mechanism. The companion note transfers a particular two-wave cell to actual globally repaired cores with a complete error ledger. This lemma alone does not classify arbitrary fixed covers or prove universal finite-Morse exclusion. Internal reconstruction is not external validation or a historical-priority determination.

## Interface

Let T^d be a flat periodic torus, volume C, and L_0=Delta+1. Let P,Q be smooth real functions satisfying L_0P=L_0Q=0. P is nonzero. Fix tau>0 and Omega={|P|<tau}, with the Dirichlet gap

    lambda_1(-Delta,Omega)>=Lambda>1.                    (1)

The normalized finite cosine families in the inherited construction acquire this gap by their uniform micro-sparsity Poincare estimate; choose rho small before tau. Do not assume it follows merely from the measure of Omega. All cell statements below retain (1). A periodic trigonometric packet of unit frequencies is an example, with arbitrary coefficients and phases consistent with the periodic torus.

Solve L_0r_Q=Q in Omega with r_Q in H_0^1(Omega), using periodic zero extension R_Q. Define the distributional residual

    A_Q=L_0 R_Q-Q.

The quotient A_Q/P is taken only on its support, where |P|>=tau; it is set to zero in the remaining inner collar. All boundary defects of the zero extension are included. Smoothness of the level boundary is NOT assumed.

## 1. The boundary defect is a finite signed measure

Let z_+,z_- solve (-Delta-1)z_+=Q_+, (-Delta-1)z_-=Q_- in Omega with zero trace. The gap gives existence, uniqueness and the maximum principle; z_+,z_- >=0, and r_Q=-z_++z_-.

For either positive solution, the zero-extension subsolution argument applied to -Delta z=Q_+ +z gives

    mu_+=Q_+ 1_Omega+Delta Z_++Z_+ >=0

distributionally, and similarly mu_-. A positive distribution is a positive Radon measure. Its support is in boundary(Omega), since the equation holds inside and everything vanishes outside. Testing against the constant periodic function gives

    mu_+(T^d)=integral_Omega Q_+ + integral_Omega z_+.

For completeness the subsolution step is variational on arbitrary open Omega. For a nonnegative smooth periodic test phi take phi theta_epsilon(z) in the Dirichlet weak equation, where theta_epsilon(0)=0, 0<=theta_epsilon<=1, theta_epsilon'>=0 and theta_epsilon(t)->1 for t>0. Composition preserves H_0^1 without any boundary regularity. The equation gives

    integral theta_epsilon(z) grad z dot grad phi
       + integral phi theta_epsilon'(z)|grad z|^2
          =integral (Q_+ +z)phi theta_epsilon(z).

Drop the nonnegative second term and let epsilon decrease to zero. The fact grad z=0 almost everywhere on {z=0} gives integral grad Z dot grad phi <=integral_Omega(Q_+ +z)phi. This is precisely mu_+>=0. Compactness of the torus and the constant test establish finite mass. No normal trace theorem is used.

The coercive L2 estimate and Cauchy-Schwarz give, for z_++z_-,

    integral(z_++z_-) <= |Omega| ||Q||_infinity/(Lambda-1).

Thus mu_Q=-mu_++mu_- has finite total variation, and

    L_0R_Q=Q 1_Omega+mu_Q,
    A_Q=-Q 1_(outside Omega)+mu_Q,
    ||A_Q||_TV/C <= ||Q||_infinity[1+1/(Lambda-1)].      (2)

Every boundary point has |P|=tau by continuity, including critical level points. This proves that A_Q/P is a well-defined finite signed measure. No normal derivative or boundary regularity has been inserted into (2).

## 2. An exact scalar response functional

Periodic distributional integration by parts with L_0P=0 gives

    0=pairing(L_0R_Q,P)
      =integral_Omega P Q + integral P dmu_Q.

On the support of mu_Q, 1/P=P/tau^2. Therefore the normalized response mean is exactly

    M_tau(P;Q)=mean(A_Q/P)
      =-mean[1_(outside) Q/P+1_Omega P Q/tau^2]
      =-mean[rho_tau(P)Q],                              (3)

where rho_tau(t)=t/tau^2 for |t|<tau and 1/t for |t|>=tau. It is continuous at both joining points. In particular,

    M_tau(P;P)=-mean[min(P^2/tau^2,1)]
              <=-(1-|Omega|/C)<0.                      (4)

Thus common-frequency response keeps a strict negative sign for ANY packet satisfying the cell interface. Its sign does not depend on a one-wave nodal pattern. The boundary term is explicitly

    mean(mu_P/P)=-mean(1_Omega P^2)/tau^2,

and strengthens, rather than compensates, the outside negative term.

For a smooth parameter family P_lambda with L_0 partial_lambda P_lambda=0, define

    j_tau(t)=t^2/(2tau^2) for |t|<=tau,
             log(|t|/tau)+1/2 for |t|>=tau,
    J_tau(P)=mean[j_tau(P)].

Since j_tau is C1 and its derivative is rho_tau,

    M_tau(P;partial_lambda P)=-partial_lambda J_tau(P). (5)

This derivative has no fixed sign. Mixture-weight and phase derivatives are not the common-frequency derivative in (4).

For Q=partial_i P, the unsmoothed response has zero mean: it is -mean(partial_i j_tau(P)). This statement does not require reflection symmetry. A fixed smoothing may change that mean; if P is even in coordinate i and the radial kernel is reflection invariant, the smoothed derivative response remains exactly odd and has zero mean.

## 3. The actual tilted radial smoothing has a uniform error bound

Use the same nonnegative normalized radial C-infinity kernel psi on R^(d+1), supported in the unit ball, as in the PDE construction. Removing exp(-A s) gives its fast-coordinate operator

    S_sigma h(zeta)=integral psi(v) exp(sigma v_1)
                                      h(zeta-sigma v_t)dv.

It acts on periodic measures by convolution. Let L=||grad P||_infinity. Choose sigma L<=tau/4. The smoothed residual S_sigma A_Q is supported where |P|>=3tau/4. Therefore

    M_(tau,sigma)(P;Q)=mean[(S_sigma A_Q)/P]             (6)

is a smooth zero-safe quotient; there is no division at a displaced zero.

Choose a smooth reciprocal cutoff g equal to 1/P where |P|>=tau/2 and zero where |P|<=tau/4. Then ||g||_infinity<=C/tau and ||grad g||_infinity<=C L/tau^2. Using the adjoint convolution and (2),

    |M_(tau,sigma)-M_tau|
      <= C ||Q||_infinity[1+1/(Lambda-1)]
                    [sigma L/tau^2+sigma^2/tau].       (7)

The tilted kernel's mass differs from one by O(sigma^2), because psi is even in v_1. Its translation contributes the Lipschitz term. This bound is uniform over any family with uniformly bounded P,Q gradients/amplitudes and the same Dirichlet gap. It does not require continuity of the domains as P varies, a Sard choice of tau for the entire family, or a model-agreement argument.

For normalized finite unit-wave packets, ||P||_infinity,||grad P||_infinity<=1. If the original sparsity gives |Omega|/C<=theta<1, one sufficiently small fixed sigma=O(tau^2(1-theta)) gives

    M_(tau,sigma)(P;P)<=-(1-theta)/2.                  (8)

All constants and sigma are fixed before the escaping radius limit. The original source construction permits this additional fixed smallness choice.

## 4. A two-wave derivative with the opposite compensation role

On T^2 put

    P_lambda(y,z)=lambda cos y+(1-lambda)cos z,
    Q_lambda=cos y-cos z, lambda in [7/10,4/5].

Both are exact L_0-Helmholtz functions. For tau<=1/100, the level boundaries |P_lambda|=tau are uniformly regular: the nonzero critical values have magnitude at least 2lambda-1>=2/5. Thus the cell solutions and smoothed residuals are smooth in lambda under a smooth level-set pullback, with all fixed finite derivatives bounded. Equation (3) also holds without this regularity assumption.

The required two-wave Dirichlet gap can also be proved directly, rather than deduced from small volume. Where |P_lambda|<=tau, |partial_yP_lambda|>=3/5: its square is lambda^2-(P_lambda-(1-lambda)cos z)^2 >=(7/10)^2-(31/100)^2>9/25. For each z the collar is the union of two y intervals, each of length <=10tau/3. Slice-wise one-dimensional Dirichlet Poincare and integration in z give

    lambda_1(-Delta,Omega_lambda)>=(3pi/(10tau))^2>16.

The compact-family micro-sparsity remains required for the global construction; this direct argument supplies the local two-wave gap independently.

For fixed z let d=(1-lambda)cos z. Since |d|<lambda, principal-value integration gives mean_y[1/(lambda cos y+d)]=0. For example the substitution t=tan(y/2) reduces this to the symmetric principal-value integral of 2/[lambda+d-(lambda-d)t^2], whose two logarithmic pole contributions cancel. Since Q_lambda/P_lambda=1/lambda-cos z/(lambda P_lambda),

    PV mean(Q_lambda/P_lambda)=1/lambda.

The tau regularization changes this by O(tau), uniformly over the displayed lambda interval. An explicit bound is obtained by changing y to p=P_lambda near the two simple roots. The root-summed Q density is

    F(p,z)=2(p-cos z)/[lambda^2 sqrt(1-((p-d)/lambda)^2)].

For |p|<=tau<=1/100 and lambda in the displayed interval, |partial_p F|<=16. The odd regularization error pairs only F(p,z)-F(0,z), so its normalized magnitude is <=32tau/(3pi). Therefore

    |M_tau(P_lambda;Q_lambda)+1/lambda|<=32tau/(3pi),
    M_tau(P_lambda;Q_lambda)<-6/5                       (9)

for tau<=1/100 (the weakest endpoint is lambda=4/5). Choose the one fixed sigma still smaller if needed so the uniform error (7) is <=1/20. Then

    M_(tau,sigma)(P_lambda;P_lambda)<-4/5,
    M_(tau,sigma)(P_lambda;Q_lambda)<-1.               (10)

The compact separated-frequency family supplies the gap and sparse fraction before this sigma choice. (10) is the quantitative cell sign used by the actual compensation model. The retained numerical quadrature checks the functional formula; it does not supply the proof of (9)-(10).
