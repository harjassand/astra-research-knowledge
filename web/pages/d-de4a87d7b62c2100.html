# Persistent cycle lift: ballistic TV, log-log slower entropy

## Result

Consider the continuous-time chain on
\(\Omega_n=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\times\{+1,-1\}\), \(n\ge 3\), with generator
\[
(L_nf)(x,v)=f(x+v,v)-f(x,v)+\frac1n\bigl(f(x,-v)-f(x,v)\bigr).
\]
The position makes a unit-rate step in its current direction; an independent rate-
\(1/n\) clock reverses the direction. The uniform law \(\pi_n(x,v)=1/(2n)\) is stationary and the chain is irreducible. Its total event rate is \(1+1/n\).

For fixed \(0<\varepsilon<1\), define worst-start total-variation and relative-entropy times by
\[
 t_{\rm TV}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\inf\{t:\max_z\|P_t(z,\cdot)-\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}\le\varepsilon\},\qquad
 t_{\rm KL}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\inf\{t:\max_z D(P_t(z,\cdot)\|\pi_n)\le\varepsilon\}.
\]
The finite-state mechanism gives
\[
 t_{\rm TV}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\Theta_\varepsilon(n),\qquad
 t_{\rm KL}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\Theta_\varepsilon(n\log\log n).
\]
More specifically, for every fixed \(a\in(0,1)\) and every fixed entropy threshold \(C<\infty\),
\[
 \max_zD(P_{a n\log\log n}(z,\cdot)\|\pi_n)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The entropy lower bound is caused by the event that the velocity has not flipped: its mass is \(e^{-t/n}\), concentrated around one ballistic location, and it still carries order \(e^{-t/n}\log n\) relative entropy. The entropy upper bound follows from the same order-\(n\) Doeblin block that yields TV mixing.

This is a clean finite-state hypocoercive example: the nonreversible real-part spectral gap is order \(1/n\), while the Poincaré gap of the additive reversibilization is order \(1/n^2\). The underlying symmetric cycle walk has TV time order \(n^2\). The order-\(n\) lifted speedup itself is established prior art; the potentially useful extension here is the order-sharp TV/KL separation for this continuous-time chain. No novelty or priority claim is made.

## Exact interface and assumptions

- State visible to the chain: position \(x\) and a persistent velocity bit \(v\). The velocity bit is part of the state and part of the stationary target.
- Motion clock: rate 1, with jump \((x,v)\mapsto(x+v,v)\).
- Flip clock: independent rate \(1/n\), with jump \((x,v)\mapsto(x,-v)\).
- Initial state for worst-start bounds: any point \((x,v)\); all point starts have equal distance by translation and reflection symmetry.
- Stationary target: uniform on all \(2n\) position-velocity pairs.
- Charged path cost: expected number of events by time \(t\) is \((1+1/n)t\). No oracle, learned input, or nonlocal position jump is used.
- Baseline: the rate-one symmetric walk on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with rates \(1/2\) to either neighbor. Averaging the lifted generator over a uniform velocity gives the baseline generator on position observables at the infinitesimal level. The projected position path is not itself Markov.

## Proof ledger

### Stationarity, irreducibility, and nonreversibility

At every state, outgoing rate is \(1+1/n\). There is one rate-1 incoming motion edge from \((x-v,v)\), and one rate-\(1/n\) incoming flip edge from \((x,-v)\), so column sums also equal \(1+1/n\); hence \(\pi_n\) is stationary. For \(n\ge3\), repeated flips and directed moves connect all states. Detailed balance fails because the rate-1 edge \((x,+)\to(x+1,+)\) has reverse rate zero.

For an observable depending only on position,
\[
 \frac12\sum_{v\in\{\pm1\}} L_n(f\circ\pi)(x,v)
 =\frac12[f(x+1)+f(x-1)-2f(x)],
\]
so the stationary-velocity average of the lifted infinitesimal motion is the rate-one symmetric cycle generator. This is the precise projection boundary; the full lifted position coordinate is not autonomous after time zero.

### Fourier spectrum and additive reversibilization

For \(\theta_k=2\pi k/n\), the Fourier block of \(L_n\), in velocity order \((+, -)\), is
\[
 B_k=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\theta_k}-1-1/n&1/n\\1/n&e^{-i\theta_k}-1-1/n\end{pmatrix},
\]
with eigenvalues
\[
 \lambda_{k,\pm}=\cos\theta_k-1-1/n\;\pm\;\sqrt{n^{-2}-\sin^2\theta_k}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the slowest nonconstant mode is \(k=1\) or \(n-1\), and
\[
 \gamma_{\rm Re}(L_n):=\min_{\lambda\ne0}(-\operatorname{Re}\lambda)
 =\frac1n+1-\cos(2\pi/n)=\Theta(1/n).
\]
Indeed, these first spatial modes are in the complex-root regime; the zero spatial mode has its other eigenvalue at \(-2/n\), and modes near \(\theta=\pi\) have real-part decay bounded below by a positive constant. The block is nonnormal when \(\sin\theta_k\ne0\), because its skew drift and symmetric flip matrices do not commute.

The additive reversibilization \(S_n=(L_n+L_n^*)/2\) has, in Fourier mode \(k\), eigenvalues \(\cos\theta_k-1\) and \(\cos\theta_k-1-2/n\). Consequently its Poincaré gap is
\[
 \gamma(S_n)=\min\{1-\cos(2\pi/n),2/n\}=1-\cos(2\pi/n)=\Theta(n^{-2})
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\). Thus the exact nonreversible real-part gap is larger than the additive-reversible gap by order \(n\).

For the rate-one reversible cycle baseline, the eigenvalues are \(\cos(2\pi k/n)-1\). Fourier expansion gives \(t_{\rm TV}(\varepsilon)=\Theta_\varepsilon(n^2)\). This comparison uses the same unit total position-jump rate; the lift adds only a rate-\(1/n\) flip clock and a factor-two state-space augmentation.

### Uniform minorization at time \(2n\): local-CLT lemma

The key finite-state lemma is that there are constants \(c>0\) and \(n_0\) such that
\[
 P_{2n}(z,z')\ge c/n\qquad(n\ge n_0,\ z,z'\in\Omega_n).
\]
Since \(\pi_n(z')=1/(2n)\), this is a Doeblin minorization \(P_{2n}(z,\cdot)\ge\delta\pi_n(\cdot)\) with fixed \(\delta=2c>0\) (decrease \(c\) if needed so \(\delta<1\)).

Here is the proof reduction and the only standard analytic input. If \(A,B\in[\alpha n,\beta n]\), \(A+B=2n\), and \(Z\sim\operatorname{Pois}(A)-\operatorname{Pois}(B)\), then for every fixed \(K\), the uniform local central limit theorem gives
\[
 \Pr(Z=k)\ge c_{\alpha,\beta,K}/\sqrt n
 \quad\text{whenever }|k-(A-B)|\le K\sqrt n.
\]
This follows directly from Stirling's formula for the Skellam mass; all means stay in a compact positive fraction of \(n\).

Fix a starting state and choose an integer representative \(k\in[-n/2,n/2]\) of the target position difference.

- To end with the opposite velocity, retain paths with exactly one flip, at time \(\tau\). This event has density \(e^{-2}n^{-1}\,d\tau\). Conditional on \(\tau\), the displacement is \(\operatorname{Pois}(\tau)-\operatorname{Pois}(2n-\tau)\). Center the interval of integration at \(\tau_0=n+k/2\), then integrate over a window of width \(c_0\sqrt n\). Both Poisson means remain between fixed positive multiples of \(n\), their difference in means is within \(O(\sqrt n)\) of \(k\), and the local-CLT lower bound is \(c_1/\sqrt n\). The integrated probability is at least \(c_2/n\).
- To end with the original velocity, retain paths with exactly two flips. Write their ordered times as \(u,u+d\). Their density is \(e^{-2}n^{-2}\,du\,dd\). For fixed gap \(d\), the total positive-direction exposure is \(2n-d\) and the negative-direction exposure is \(d\), so displacement has law \(\operatorname{Pois}(2n-d)-\operatorname{Pois}(d)\), independent of \(u\). Center \(d\) at \(d_0=n-k/2\), integrate over a width-\(c_0\sqrt n\) window, and integrate \(u\) over its available interval of length \(2n-d=\Theta(n)\). The same local-CLT bound yields a total contribution at least \(c_3/n\).

Reflection handles a negative initial velocity, and translation handles arbitrary starting position. Taking the smaller constant proves the uniform minorization. This proof charges only the stated Poisson motion and flip clocks. It uses no finite simulation as evidence.

The minorization contracts TV by \(1-\delta\) per block of length \(2n\), so \(t_{\rm TV}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=O_\varepsilon(n)\).

### Matching TV lower bound

Let \(J_t\sim\operatorname{Pois}(t)\) be the number of position jumps. Regardless of flips, the circular displacement from the starting position is at most \(J_t\). At \(t=an\), for fixed sufficiently small \(a>0\), the event \(J_t\le2an\) has probability tending to one. The set of position-velocity states within circular distance \(2an\) of the start has stationary mass \(4a+o(1)\). Therefore
\[
 \max_z\|P_{an}(z,\cdot)-\pi_n\|_{\rm TV}\ge1-4a-o(1).
\]
Choose \(a<(1-\varepsilon)/4\) to get \(t_{\rm TV}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\Omega_\varepsilon(n)\). Combined with the minorization, this proves the TV order sharply.

### Matching KL order: upper bound from the same block

Write \(P=P_{2n}=\delta\Pi+(1-\delta)R\), where \(\Pi(z,\cdot)=\pi_n\). The minorization makes \(R\) a Markov kernel, and stationarity of \(P\) implies \(\pi_nR=\pi_n\). For every initial law \(\mu\), convexity and data processing give
\[
 D(\mu P\|\pi_n)\le(1-\delta)D(\mu R\|\pi_n)\le(1-\delta)D(\mu\|\pi_n).
\]
A point mass starts with entropy \(\log(2n)\), so \(O(\log\log n)\) blocks suffice to make it at most any fixed positive threshold. Monotonicity under the remaining semigroup time gives \(t_{\rm KL}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=O_\varepsilon(n\log\log n)\).

### KL lower bound from the no-flip path

Start from \((0,+)\) and put \(t=an\log\log n\) for fixed \(0<a<1\). Let
\[
 W=\sqrt t\,\log n,
 \qquad A_n=\{(x,+):d_{\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}(x,t\bmod n)\le W\}.
\]
On the event of no velocity flip (probability \(e^{-t/n}=(\log n)^{-a}\)), the position is a rate-one Poisson count of mean \(t\). Chebyshev gives \(\Pr(|J_t-t|\le W)=1-o(1)\); hence
\[
 p_n:=P_t((0,+),A_n)\ge(\log n)^{-a}(1-o(1)).
\]
Under stationarity,
\[
 q_n:=\pi_n(A_n)\le(2W+1)/(2n)=O\!\left(\log n\sqrt{\frac{\log\log n}{n}}\right).
\]
Data processing under the indicator of \(A_n\) yields
\[
 D(P_t((0,+),\cdot)\|\pi_n)\ge d_{\rm Ber}(p_n\|q_n)
 \ge (1-o(1))\frac12(\log n)^{1-a}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The last estimate uses \(\log(p_n/q_n)=\tfrac12\log n-O(\log\log n)\). Thus no fixed KL threshold is reached by time \(an\log\log n\) for any fixed \(a<1\); this proves the order-sharp \(\Omega(n\log\log n)\) lower bound.

## Prior art and scope

1. Diaconis, Holmes, and Neal, “Analysis of a nonreversible Markov chain sampler,” *Annals of Applied Probability* 10(3) (2000), 726–752, DOI [10.1214/aoap/1019487508](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoap/1019487508), introduced/analyzed the persistent-velocity lift on the discrete circle. Eberle and Lörler’s 2024 primary article explicitly records that velocity flips of order \(1/n\) give lifted mixing \(\Theta(n)\) versus \(\Theta(n^2)\) for the symmetric walk, and calls this diffusive-to-ballistic acceleration ([official article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-024-01308-x), §1). Therefore neither that speedup nor the general fact that nonreversibility can beat additive reversibilization is new here. This note uses a continuous-time independent-clock generator and makes the entropy comparison explicit. The original Project Euclid full text/PDF was not retrievable in this session; the exact prior-art description was checked through Eberle–Lörler’s publisher version and the original DOI metadata, not by reconstructing the 2000 paper’s proof.
2. Chen, Lovász, and Pak, “Lifting Markov Chains to Speed up Mixing,” STOC 1999, DOI [10.1145/301250.301315](https://doi.org/10.1145/301250.301315), characterize lifting through multicommodity flows and show a square-root scale is the best possible general improvement. The cycle lift reaches the diameter order, which is the natural local-motion lower bound. The authors’ open PDF/abstract was checked for the stated general lifting limit; its detailed proof was not needed for the claims here.
3. Eberle and Lörler, “Non-reversible lifts of reversible diffusion processes and relaxation times,” DOI [10.1007/s00440-024-01308-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-024-01308-x), develop a continuous-time lift and relaxation framework, and identify a general spectral-gap question for lifts as open. The explicit finite Fourier calculation here is a cycle instance only; it does not settle their general problem.
4. A scope boundary is state augmentation: the velocity bit is essential to the construction. No claim is made that an arbitrary nonreversible chain supported only on the original cycle edges has the same speedup. The 2023 result “Analysis of a non-reversible Markov chain speedup by a single edge,” DOI [10.1017/jpr.2022.88](https://doi.org/10.1017/jpr.2022.88), studies a different drift-plus-added-edge mechanism.

## What is established and what remains open

- **Established in this report:** exact generator and stationary measure; exact Fourier blocks; real-part and additive-reversible spectral gap orders; TV order \(\Theta(n)\) via the explicitly stated uniform local-CLT minorization plus a speed lower bound; KL order \(\Theta(n\log\log n)\) via Doeblin contraction and a binary-entropy lower bound.
- **Proof input to formalize if this is carried into a paper:** the uniform Skellam local-CLT lower bound, derived from Stirling’s formula. The parameter ranges and where it is used are stated above. It is a standard finite-dimensional estimate, but the constants have not been expanded into a stand-alone lemma proof here.
- **Not established:** a total-variation profile limit or cutoff/non-cutoff classification; the leading constant in KL mixing; a general finite-graph or continuous-state theorem; novelty/priority of the KL lag. The apparent telegraph-process scaling limit and its no-flip singular component are a plausible next route, not an asserted result.
- **Falsification conditions:** a sequence of target states for which the one-/two-flip minorization falls below \(c/n\) would break the TV/KL upper bounds; an entropy estimate contradicting the no-flip event lower bound would falsify the KL lower bound. The latter is especially constrained because the binary data-processing inequality is exact.
