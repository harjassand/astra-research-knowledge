## 1. Exact input/output and parameters

The density comparability is an input hypothesis to this primitive. Its generation/verification from inherited discrepancy is a separate completion bridge, not an oracle silently added to the algorithm. The integral1 is exactly checkable using rational sphere monomial integrals. Neither R nor H is assumed bounded independently of the input.

Use

All ceilings are acquired by integer bit lengths and rational comparisons. A dyadic tolerance between delta/2 and delta may replace delta everywhere.

## 2. Explicit equal-w-mass cells

Write

    w_bar(z)=(1/(2pi))int_0^(2pi)
             w(sqrt(1-z²)cos phi,sqrt(1-z²)sin phi,z)dphi,
    C_N(z)=(1/2)int_z^1 w_bar(t)dt.

The longitude mean w_bar is a rational polynomial in z: odd x/y powers vanish; even x/y powers contribute a rational angular moment times an integer power of1-z². It satisfies `1/2<=w_bar<=3/2`. Hence C_N is a strictly decreasing rational polynomial with `1/4<=-C_N'<=3/4`. The analogous south-cap cumulative mass is `C_S(z)=1-C_N(z)`.

Let z_* be the unique weighted median `C_N(z_*)=1/2`. Both hemispherical pieces have mass1/2. Since both pieces have density at most3/2, their uniform areas are each at least1/3; thus `-1/3<=z_*<=1/3`.

For j=0,...,q, define northern cap boundaries by

    C_N(z_j^N)=j²/(2q²),

and southern boundaries by `C_S(z_j^S)=j²/(2q²)`. The j=q boundaries coincide at z_*. Ring j on either side has mass `(2j-1)/(2q²)`. Split it, from longitude0 through2pi, into `m_j=2j-1` wedges each of w-mass1/n. These unique longitude quantiles are specified by their cumulative weighted mass. North and south give precisely2q² cells; interiors are disjoint and boundaries have measure zero.

Here is a uniform diameter proof. On the north put `u=j/q`, `theta(u)=arccos(z^N(u))`, so cap mass is u²/2. Let `v=1-cos theta`. Comparability gives `2u²/3<=v<=2u²`; the median bound also gives v<=4/3. Therefore

    sin theta>=2u/3,    sin theta<=2u,
    dtheta/du<=4u/sin theta<=6.

The meridian span of a ring is at most6/q, including the continuous polar endpoint. The same proof works from the south pole. Its z-width Delta z satisfies

    ring mass <=(3/4)Delta z,
    Delta z >=4m_j/(3n).

The longitude mass derivative is at least Delta z/(8pi), so each wedge angle has width at most `8pi/(n Delta z)<=6pi/m_j`. The outer sine of the ring is at most2j/q. Move along a meridian then along a parallel to bound any pairwise cell distance by

    6/q+(2j/q)(6pi/m_j)
       <=(6+12pi)/q<50/q=h.                         (W5)

The whole first-ring longitude interval causes no exception. Convexity of these cells is not needed.

Define the true real weighted ambient centroids and anchors

    m_i=n int_(R_i) x w(x)d sigma(x),    c_i=m_i/|m_i|.

Pairwise cell distances below h imply `x dot m_i>=cos h`, `|m_i|>=cos h>1/2`, and `R_i subset B_h(c_i)`. This directly uses positivity and cell mass1/n. The weighted ambient tangential first moment about c_i is exactly zero.

## 3. Acquiring every quantile and centroid with polynomial bits

No weighted partition is supplied for free. Latitude quantiles are acquired by rational-polynomial interval bisection on[-1,1]. The derivative lower bound1/4 gives a uniform inverse Lipschitz constant4. If a midpoint enclosure contains the target, its residual and this inverse bound give a certified small root interval; no exact sign decision at a root is required. The known endpoints are the poles. Every nonpolar boundary is at distance at least `2/(3q²)` in z from its corresponding pole, supplying O(log q) guards for square roots/arcsines.

For a ring, parameterize longitude as phi=2pi t, t in[0,1]. Its cumulative mass derivative in t is at least Delta z/4, hence at least `m_j/(3n)>=1/(3n)`. Thus certified monotone bisection acquires all wedge boundaries with O(log n) extra inverse guards. Its full-ring endpoint mass is exactly m_j/n. All latitude and longitude intervals are acquired for the actual real quantiles specified above, not for slightly perturbed cells whose masses have only been numerically guessed.

Every needed integral separates into finitely many terms of

    int z^c(1-z²)^(b/2)dz * int cos^d(phi)sin^e(phi)dphi,

with nonnegative integer exponents at most R+1. The extra1 covers the coordinates for the centroid. These antiderivatives are explicitly acquired as follows:

- For even b, expand1-z² and integrate a rational polynomial.
- For odd b, expand its integer power and reduce to `int z^m sqrt(1-z²)dz`. For odd `m=2v+1`, substituting u=1-z² gives the finite sum `-sum_(j=0)^v (-1)^j binom(v,j)u^(j+3/2)/(2j+3)`.
- For even `m=2v`, put theta=arcsin z. The integral is `J_v(theta)-J_(v+1)(theta)`, where `J_0=theta` and `J_v=-(sin^(2v-1)theta cos theta)/(2v)+[(2v-1)/(2v)]J_(v-1)`. Substitute z and sqrt(1-z²) back.
- Longitude powers have a finite Fourier expansion from `cos phi=(exp(i phi)+exp(-i phi))/2` and `sin phi=(exp(i phi)-exp(-i phi))/(2i)`. The constant Fourier coefficient integrates to a multiple of phi; every other coefficient has an explicitly divided sine/cosine primitive.

These expansions use polynomially many rational operations. Each original monomial's primitive coefficients have height `O(H+R log(R+1))`; retaining that finite list avoids pretending different input denominators coincide. Combining the distinct coefficient denominators into w_bar, a CDF or a combined antiderivative is charged up to `O(poly(R)[H+R log(R+1)])` exact height. Absolute interval guards can still use the individual coefficient magnitudes and the logarithm of the number of terms; this distinction does not alter polynomial acquisition. Machin's formula and alternating geometric arctangent tails acquire pi. For arcsin, use `arcsin z=2atan[z/(1+sqrt(1-z²))]`; one more arctangent half-angle reduction makes the argument's absolute value at most `sqrt(2)-1<1/2`, giving a geometric tail. Rational square-root intervals are acquired by bisection. Trigonometric Taylor evaluation at bounded multiples up toO(R) of2pi is polynomial in R and requested bits.

Boundary uncertainty also has a direct stable geometric bound: since `|x w(x)|<=3/2`, its integral over boundary slivers is bounded by the sliver area times3/2. Multiplication by n and the polynomial antiderivative coefficients costs only O(log n+H+R log(R+1)) additional precision guards. If a coordinate square root is near a pole, squaring the requested absolute endpoint accuracy costs a factor2 in bit precision, not an exponential number of steps. The known lower bound|m_i|>1/2 makes normalization stable.

Use the north stereographic chart for northern cells and the south chart for southern cells. Every north point has z>=-1/3, hence its normalized centroid has z>=-2/3; the north chart denominator1+z is at least1/3. The south chart has the same lower bound for1-z. No sign oracle near the weighted median is needed. This acquires chart parameters and an initial distinct rational reset within the single delta budget, in polynomial bit time in `(R,H,log q,log(1/delta))`. Finitely many forbidden nodes are avoided by the reset below, rather than by rotating the density target.
