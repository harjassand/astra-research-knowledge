# Endpoint aliasing and active sensing for cell-state interventions

**Status:** internally reconstructed conditional theorem and executable finite-grid design. No biological data were analyzed. Mathematical correctness is proved below for the stated model; novelty and priority are unresolved.

## Candidate result

An endpoint state fraction can leave an intervention decision unidentified when the objective depends on the path, even in a two-state, time-homogeneous continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC). The issue survives infinitely many independent founder-level endpoint observations: two intervention regimes can have exactly the same terminal state distribution and opposite optimal actions for cumulative state occupancy. A single intermediate snapshot separates an explicit pair. The useful claim is this scoped decision-identifiability result and its design certificate; it is not a claim that lineage inference, Markov estimation, or active experimental design is new.

## Experiment and information contract

There are two randomized interventions, A and B, applied to independent founder lineages over a fixed horizon (T). Each founder starts in a verified state (X_0=0). Under an intervention, the focal cell has binary state (X_t\in\{0,1\}) and constant transition rates

\[
0\xrightarrow{\alpha}1,\qquad 1\xrightarrow{\beta}0,
\]

with α,β positive. For each intervention, the endpoint-only assay returns one binary state at (T) for each founder. The decision objective is expected cumulative occupancy

\[
J(u)=\mathbb E_u\!\left[\int_0^T X_t\,dt\right],
\]

and the policy selects the intervention with larger (J(u)). The model assumes independent randomized founders, no interference, no state-dependent division or death, no censoring, and a nondifferential reporter. These are mathematical assumptions, not claims about any cell system.

The information supplied to the design is the horizon, a pair of candidate kinetic regimes, the initial-state condition, the reporter calibration, and the endpoint objective. In an actual experiment, the state definition, calibrated readout, initial-state verification or sorting, and candidate-rate range must be acquired. The script takes the candidate regimes as supplied inputs; its computation does not pay for learning them from pilot data.

## Exact endpoint non-identifiability

Starting from state 0, the probability of state 1 at time (t) is

\[
p_{\alpha,\beta}(t)=\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t}),\qquad \lambda=\alpha+\beta.
\]

Fix a desired terminal probability (z\in(0,1)). For every

\[
\lambda>\lambda_0:=\frac{-\log(1-z)}{T},\qquad
\alpha_\lambda=\frac{\lambda z}{1-e^{-\lambda T}},\qquad
\beta_\lambda=\lambda-\alpha_\lambda,
\]

both rates are strictly positive and (p_{\alpha_\lambda,\beta_\lambda}(T)=z). The integrated occupancy is

\[
I(\lambda)=z\!\left(\frac{T}{1-e^{-\lambda T}}-\frac1\lambda\right).
\]

It is strictly increasing in λ: differentiating the parenthesized term gives (1/\lambda^2-T^2e^{-\lambda T}/(1-e^{-\lambda T})^2>0), equivalent to (2\sinh(\lambda T/2)>\lambda T). Thus terminal state probability does not identify cumulative occupancy.

For (z=1/2,T=1), take λslow (=0.7) and λfast (=10). This gives:

| Regime | α | β | (p(T)) | (J=I(\lambda)) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| slow | 0.6952518523 | 0.0047481477 | 0.5 | 0.2789312175 |
| fast | 5.0002270100 | 4.9997729900 | 0.5 | 0.4500227010 |

Define world (W_1) by assigning fast to A and slow to B; define (W_2) by swapping them. In both worlds, the endpoint-only data consist of the same number of independent Bernoulli(1/2) outcomes in each randomized arm. Therefore the full endpoint-data laws are identical for every sample size (total variation distance zero), while the best intervention switches. If a policy chooses A with probability (q), its regret is ((1-q)g) in (W_1) and (qg) in (W_2), where (g=0.1710914835T). Its worst-case regret is at least (g/2=0.0855457417T), for any number of endpoint-only founders. This is an observation-model impossibility, not a biological conclusion.

## Executable sensing rule and sample bound

Add one state read at a chosen intermediate time τ. Let (p_f(\tau)) and (p_s(\tau)) denote the fast and slow state-1 probabilities. In (W_1), the difference of arm means ̄Y_A-̄Y_B has expectation (d(\tau)=p_f(\tau)-p_s(\tau)); in (W_2) its expectation is (-d(\tau)). With (n) independent founders per arm, the sign rule has error at most

\[
\Pr(\text{wrong world})\le e^{-n d(\tau)^2}
\]

by Hoeffding's inequality. For a known reporter sensitivity Se and specificity Sp, the observed-probability separation is multiplied by the Youden index (J_R=\mathrm{Se}+\mathrm{Sp}-1), so replace (d) by (J_Rd). If the reporter is uncalibrated or differs by arm, this bound does not apply.

The script searches a grid of τ values and selects the least-cost time under the supplied per-founder cost model, breaking equal-cost ties by the larger probability gap. For constant assay cost, (T=1), grid size 20,000, and the rates above, it selects τ (=0.21215T); (p_f(\tau)=0.4400941), (p_s(\tau)=0.1370682), and (d=0.3030259). At δ (=0.05), (n=\lceil\log(1/\delta)/d^2\rceil=33) founders per arm, 66 total, give the computed Hoeffding bound 0.04831. With known 90% sensitivity and specificity, the same grid time requires 51 founders per arm (102 total). The search is (O(G)) time and (O(1)) extra memory for a grid of size (G). The continuous-time optimum is not certified; only the finite grid result is.

Run it with:

```sh
python3 work/agents/cell_control/cell_observation_design/endpoint_alias_sensing.py
```

The count is independent founder lineages, not descendants. It covers one intermediate binary assay per founder. It excludes initial-state sorting/verification, reporter calibration, culture and intervention costs, well/batch replication, and model acquisition. If the initial state is not supplied by sorting, add one baseline verification per founder or charge the sorting protocol. If terminal measurements are also collected, charge those reads separately. A repeated nondestructive reporter can reuse founders; a destructive assay needs separate cohorts at different times. With correlated founders in one well, the well or independently randomized block—not each cell—is the unit, and the displayed bound must be recomputed at that level.

The strongest equal-information control matters. In this no-division model, an aggregate cross-sectional snapshot at the same τ with the same randomized founders has the same Bernoulli observations and the same sample bound. Lineage identifiers do not improve this particular result. A tracked full path directly measures occupancy but has a different, higher imaging/data cost. Endpoint-only snapshots are the inadequate baseline because they omit the time at which the candidate models diverge.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

The primary literature already establishes several stronger and adjacent capabilities, so they delimit this candidate:

- Hormoz et al. (2016), *Cell Systems*, “Inferring Cell-State Transition Dynamics from Lineage Trees and Endpoint Single-Cell Measurements,” DOI [10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015), infer transition networks from pedigrees and endpoint gene-expression states. Their kin-correlation analysis uses pair correlations for reversible dynamics and triplet correlations for irreversible dynamics. The present two-world result does not apply when those pedigree correlations are observed.
- Jagannathan et al. (2020), *Bioinformatics*, “Transcompp: understanding phenotypic plasticity by estimating Markov transition rates for cell state transitions,” DOI [10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021), fits transition rates to multi-time-point population compositions and can include phenotype-specific proliferation estimates. Such a time series is more informative than the single terminal marginal used in the impossibility construction.
- Kuzmanovska et al. (2017), *BMC Systems Biology*, “Parameter inference for stochastic single-cell dynamics from lineage tree data,” DOI [10.1186/s12918-017-0425-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12918-017-0425-1), show that ancestry can materially improve inference for state changes at division and slow switching over multiple cell cycles. The present result assumes away these lineage mechanisms.
- “A lineage tree-based hidden Markov model quantifies cellular heterogeneity and plasticity” (2022), *Communications Biology*, DOI [10.1038/s42003-022-04208-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-022-04208-9), uses time-resolved lineage measurements and explicitly corrects finite-experiment censoring. It is a stronger lineage-resolved comparator when those inputs are available.
- Udomlumleart et al. (2021), *iScience*, “Lineages of embryonic stem cells show non-Markovian state transitions,” DOI [10.1016/j.isci.2021.102879](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2021.102879), reports lineage-level departures from a Markov model despite a population-level Markov fit. It supplies a direct model falsifier, not evidence that all cell transitions are non-Markovian.
- “Cellular proliferation biases clonal lineage tracing and trajectory inference” (2024), *Bioinformatics*, DOI [10.1093/bioinformatics/btae483](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae483), analyzes growth-dependent sampling bias in multi-time clone data. Any extension here that uses descendant cells must include proliferation, survival, and sampling mechanisms.
- Active timepoint selection is also established: Huynh and van der Schaar (ICML 2026, PMLR 306), “Active Timepoint Selection for Learning Measure-Valued Trajectories,” [PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/huynh26a.html), chooses measurement times sequentially to reduce uncertainty in distribution-valued trajectories, with single-cell biology as a motivating domain. The fixed two-regime grid search here is far narrower and does not claim novelty for active timepoint selection.
- Bayesian optimal perturbation design for haematopoiesis already allocates destructive sampling times under a latent-state ODE model: Parigini et al. (2021), *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*, DOI [10.1098/rsif.2020.0729](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0729). This also precludes a broad novelty claim for optimizing perturbation time points.

The mathematical claim is a path-reward aliasing construction for a specific binary CTMC observation contract, plus a finite-grid single-time sensing rule. It does not claim a new general theory of lineage inference, a new CTMC-identifiability result, an optimal general POMDP controller, general active timepoint selection, or novelty over the listed work. The broader biological question—how to design lineage-resolved intervention schedules robust to unknown birth/death, hidden state, reporter error, censoring, and non-Markov memory—remains open.

## Decisive falsifier and next gate

For the abstract result, the decisive algebraic check is direct substitution: both rate pairs produce (p(T)=1/2), while their occupancies differ as tabulated. The executable script independently reconstructs the pairs and the finite-grid schedule. A biological test would first predefine a reporter-based two-state phenotype and horizon, randomize independent founder lineages to interventions, and acquire dense time-lapse tracks plus an endpoint-only arm. The specific fast/slow example predicts identical endpoint fractions but early fractions near 0.44 versus 0.14 at (0.212T) and different cumulative occupancy. It is falsified as a model for that system if transition dwell times are not exponential/Markov, reporter error is differential or uncalibrated, state-dependent division/death/censoring changes the sampled population, or replicated measurements reject the predicted probabilities/occupancy ordering. The 33-per-arm bound is conditional on the two supplied models and independent founders; it is not a general biological sample-size recommendation.

The next mathematical gate is a robust schedule for confidence sets of rate pairs: maximize the least separation over all pairs of models that reverse the best intervention, accounting for time-varying assay cost and reporter uncertainty. If the worst-case separation is zero for every admissible time, the algorithm must return `UNKNOWN` and ask for a richer observation channel. The next biological gate is to compare that schedule against KCA/tHMM-style use of pedigree correlations under the same number of independent founders, sequencing/imaging budget, and dropout model. Novelty/priority remains unresolved pending that comparison and a fuller source review.
