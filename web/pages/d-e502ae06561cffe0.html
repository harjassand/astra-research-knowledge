Hard onsite projection kills I cap J !=empty. The target Born law is the
nonnegative squared-amplitude law on physical 0/u/d occupations, conditioned
on k if requested. Zero sectors must return exact zero, not a normalized
sample. The grandcanonical norm at fugacity one is sum_k c_k.

The graph G_F has sites as vertices and {i,j}, i!=j, as an edge exactly
when F_ij or F_ji is nonzero. Diagonal F is irrelevant: every surviving minor

No pair-matrix acquisition, Hamiltonian ground-state assumption, variational

An order has induced width w if, eliminating its sites and cliquing the

2. CHIRAL DETERMINANT IDENTITY

Introduce diagonal X=diag(x_i), Y=diag(y_i), and let

 D_F(x,y)=det [[X,F],[-F*,Y]],

where F* denotes Hermitian transpose. The following coefficient expansion
is exact as a formal polynomial:

 D_F(x,y)=sum_{|I|=|J|} |det F[I,J]|^2
                     product_{i notin I} x_i product_{j notin J} y_j. (1)

To prove (1), choose which diagonal entries of X and Y are used in a
determinant term. The unused top rows/columns form I; the unused bottom
rows/columns form J. The remaining matrix is
 [[0,F[I,J]],[-F*[J,I],0]].
It has zero determinant unless |I|=|J|. At common size k, exchanging the
two k-column blocks gives sign (-1)^k, and the k minus signs in the lower
left block cancel it. Its determinant is therefore |det F[I,J]|^2. Deleting
principal diagonal rows and columns has positive cofactor sign. This proves
each coefficient and hence (1), including singular and complex inputs.

Define a local linear functional T_i on the four possible monomials:

 T_i(1)=0, T_i(x_i)=1, T_i(y_i)=z, T_i(x_i y_i)=1.

Then product_i T_i applied to D_F is exactly sum_k c_k z^k. The four local
choices correspond respectively to forbidden double occupancy, down only,
up only, and empty. The z exponent counts up occupations; (1) ensures this
equals the pair count. For a prefix, simply set the functional to zero on
any physical 0/u/d choice excluded by that prefix. Spin-mode prefixes can
be converted into the permitted local choices in constant time per site.

3. FOUR-GENERATOR LOCAL GRASSMANN LIFT

Work in an exterior algebra over Q(i)[z] with generators ordered by sites,

 (bar u_i, u_i, bar d_i, d_i), i=0,...,n-1.

For every two distinct generators theta_a,theta_b, theta_a theta_b equals
minus theta_b theta_a, and theta_a^2=0. Put A_i=bar u_i u_i and
B_i=bar d_i d_i. Let integral denote extraction of the coefficient of the
full ordered product product_i A_i B_i, with that coefficient normalized
to +1. The standard determinant identity is

 integral exp(sum_{a,b} bar psi_a M_ab psi_b)=det M.       (2)

For completeness, expanding the exponential can supply a full monomial
only by choosing each bar psi and psi once. Thus terms are indexed by
permutations; sorting their generators produces the permutation sign,
and their coefficients multiply the corresponding matrix entries. This
is precisely the Leibniz determinant. The generator order convention makes
M=identity have coefficient +1, which fixes the only global sign.
Reordering spin labels into site order permutes both rows and columns of
M, leaving its determinant unchanged.

All bilinear terms have even degree and commute in this algebra. Hence
the diagonal part of (2) factorizes as product_i (1+x_i A_i)(1+y_i B_i).
Applying T_i to its factor gives the local hard weight

 P_i=A_i+z B_i+A_i B_i.                                  (3)

The off-diagonal factors for each directed nonzero entry F_ij, i!=j, are

 E_ij=(1+F_ij bar u_i d_j)
       (1-conjugate(F_ij) bar d_j u_i).                   (4)

Therefore the exact requested polynomial is

 Z_F(z)=integral [product_i P_i product_{i!=j,F_ij!=0} E_ij]. (5)

This has one local four-generator factor per site and a constant-size
even factor per support edge. It preserves complex interference inside
determinants; its intermediate coefficients need not be nonnegative.
There is no positive matching-lift assertion. Restricting any prefix only
removes terms from (3), without changing the support graph or width.

4. EXPLICIT ELIMINATION AND ITS SIGNS

Represent a message by a dictionary (generator subset, z degree) -> Q(i)
coefficient. Multiplication is the exterior product: products sharing a
generator vanish; otherwise sort the union and multiply by (-1)^N where
N counts inversions between the two input subsets. Add coincident terms.
This is implemented directly, including exact real and imaginary Fractions.

Initially factors have scopes {i} or {i,j}. At elimination of site i,
multiply all factors whose scope contains i, then extract only monomials
containing ALL FOUR generators of i and delete those four. The resulting
message has scope equal to the bucket's scope minus i. Keep factors outside
the bucket unchanged. Finish by multiplying the scalar messages.

This computes the integral (5). Indeed, only bucket factors contain any
generator of i, so factors outside can be pulled past that integral. Every
factor has even total degree, all messages remain even after removing four
generators, and a complete four-generator site block commutes with every
monomial. Thus moving that full block to perform its coefficient extraction
causes no additional sign. If some monomial lacks any generator of i, no
outside factor can complete it, so it contributes zero. This justifies
the local elimination step and proves correctness inductively for ANY order.
This is not an appeal to a sign-free contraction heuristic.

Factor scopes stay cliques in the graph formed by the order's fill edges.
Consequently the union of each elimination bucket has at most w+1 sites.
Only 4(w+1) exterior generators are ever needed in one multiplication.
The z degree never exceeds n. There are at most (n+1)2^{4(w+1)} table
entries, and disjoint-subset exterior multiplication has at most
3^{4(w+1)} choices times (n+1)^2 degree pairs. There are O(n+d) factor
multiplications in total. Polynomial scanning, support, width checking,
and dictionary overhead are additional. This proves the stated arithmetic
bound. Space is also poly(n)2^{O(w)}.

All rational arithmetic has polynomial-size operands. Clear real/imaginary
input denominators using their product D, with log D=O(L). Every original
edge coefficient occurs at most twice in an expanded term, so one may use
a common denominator dividing D^2 for every intermediate coefficient.
A loose expansion bound allows <=2^{2d}3^n terms, each multiplying <=2d
entry components of bit size O(L). Therefore every coefficient has
O((n+d)(L+1)) bits, with a harmless larger polynomial bound if a single
global denominator is used at each primitive arithmetic step. No numerical
condition number or inverse determinant magnitude is assumed. Exact zeros
and phases do not need an additive-to-relative approximation conversion.
