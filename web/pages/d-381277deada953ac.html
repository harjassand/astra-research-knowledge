## Exact interface

Let `P = conv(V)`, where `V` is a finite nonempty subset of `{0,1}^n`. All its distinct Boolean points are vertices: for a point `v`, the objective with coefficient `+1` at its one-coordinates and `-1` at its zero-coordinates uniquely maximizes at `v` among Boolean points. For nonsingleton `P`, define

`k(P) = max{|supp(u-v)| : [u,v] is an actual edge of P}`.

For `A` with arbitrarily many rows and `||A_:j||_2 <= 1`, and `y in P`, an admissible law is a distribution on `V` with `E[b] = y`. Define its radius as `max_{Pr(b)>0} ||A(b-y)||_infinity`, and `R(P)` as the supremum, over such `A,y`, of the minimum admissible-law radius. This is a hard bound on every supported outcome, not merely an expected bound or a high-probability guarantee.

The reported constructive interface additionally requires rational binary-encoded `A,y`, an integer upper bound `k >= k(P)`, and exact strong optimization over `P` for every rational objective, returning an optimum and an optimizing Boolean vertex. The claimed matroid specialization starts from an independence oracle. These computational reductions are source claims, not verified reductions in this intake.

## Longest-edge lower bound: complete reconstruction

Choose an edge `E = [u,v]` attaining `k(P)`, and put `y = (u+v)/2`. A face of a polytope is exposed by some linear functional: write `E = {z in P : a^T z = beta}`, with `a^T z <= beta` on `P`.

For any exact-mean law,

`0 = beta - a^T y = sum_b Pr(b) (beta - a^T b)`.

Every summand is nonnegative. Every vertex outside `E` has strictly positive slack. Thus every positive-probability vertex lies in `E`. An edge has exactly its two endpoint vertices, so the law is supported on `u,v`; its mean forces probabilities `1/2,1/2` because `u != v`.

Now use one feature row, `A_1j = sign(u_j-v_j)`, with zero when the coordinates agree. Its column norms are `0` or `1`, so it is admissible. Booleanity gives

`A(u-y) = (1/2) sum_j |u_j-v_j| = k(P)/2`,

`A(v-y) = -k(P)/2`.

Consequently every admissible law for this `A,y` has radius exactly `k(P)/2`, proving `R(P) >= k(P)/2`. The construction is already rational (`A` integral, `y` half-integral). It does not require enumerating edges algorithmically. No asserted upper bound is used.

For singleton `P`, `R(P)=0`; there are no edges and no nonvertex faces. The ratio minimum below must not be stated for that case.

## Face-ratio identity: complete reconstruction and required facts

For a nonvertex face `F`, write `d = dim(F) >= 1`, `L = lin(F-F)`, and

`U(F) = {j : z_j is not constant over F}`, `N(F) = |U(F)|`.

The needed polytope fact is that edge directions of `F` span `L`. One proof fixes a vertex `v` of `F`: its tangent cone is `cone{w-v : w a vertex of F}`, whose linear span is `L`. This is a pointed polyhedral cone. Its extreme rays generate it and are precisely the directions of edges incident to `v`. Hence one can choose `d` linearly independent incident edge directions `e_1,...,e_d` as a basis of `L`. This argument requires no simplicity assumption and works in the affine hull if `F` or `P` is lower dimensional.

Each such edge is an actual edge of `P`: an edge is a face of `F`, and a face of a face of a polytope is a face of the original polytope. Thus `|supp(e_l)| <= k(P)`.

Moreover, `U(F) = union_l supp(e_l)`. The inclusion from right to left is immediate. For the reverse inclusion, a coordinate vanishing on every basis direction vanishes on every vector of `L`, so that coordinate is constant on `F`. Therefore

`N(F) <= sum_l |supp(e_l)| <= d k(P)`,

and `dim(F)/N(F) >= 1/k(P)` for every nonvertex face. The longest edge itself is a nonvertex face with `d=1` and `N(F)=k(P)`, achieving equality. Thus

`min_{F a nonvertex face of P} dim(F)/N(F) = 1/k(P)`.

The minimum must include edge faces; taking only proper faces of dimension at least two would be a different assertion. Arbitrary differences of vertices are not interchangeable with actual edge directions.

For Boolean polytopes, constant coordinates on a face are Boolean. If `x` lies in `relint(F)`, every varying coordinate satisfies `0 < x_j < 1`; otherwise the supporting coordinate hyperplane would contain `x` in a proper face of `F`. Hence the varying-coordinate set equals the fractional-coordinate set at such an `x`. Under the affine map `x -> 2x-1`, it equals the active set `|x_j|<1`. Relative-interior membership in the current **original** minimal face is essential for this interpretation.

Intersecting its tangent with one exact cost equation loses at most one dimension:

`dim(L intersect ker(c^T)) >= d-1 >= N(F)/k(P)-1 >= N(F)/k-1`.

This validates the geometric input to a dimension-reserve argument. It does not validate the number or correctness of the additional spectral/barrier equations.

## Why a favorable response suffices for existence, conditionally

Fix rational `A,y` and a discrepancy allowance `B`. Let

`G = {b in V intersect F(y) : ||A(b-y)||_infinity < B}`.

Suppose an already-correct rounding routine returns a member of `G` with `c^T(b-y) <= 0` for **every rational** `c`. Then `y in conv(G)`: otherwise strict separation from the compact finite hull provides a real `c` with `c^T(b-y)>0` for every `b in G`; the strict finite margin survives approximation of `c` by a rational vector, contradicting the response contract.

Caratheodory gives at most `dim(F(y))+1` atoms. Removing affine dependencies leaves affinely independent Boolean atoms, so the unique weights solving their mean equation are rational when `y` is rational. This is only an existence reduction. It does not independently verify the handoff's ellipsoid iteration bound, query precision, weight-denominator estimate, or transcript extraction implementation.

This isolates the main load-bearing upper-bound component: producing correct favorable responses for arbitrary queried rational costs. A favorable response is sufficient for hull existence, but it is not a substitute for the strong optimization access assumed inside the rounding construction.

## Single most consequential adoption obligation

**Certify one complete polynomial-bit favorable-cost rounding routine, with its exact-face/exact-cost reset-and-progress invariant valid through the entire trajectory.** The intake has no full proof or implementation of this contract:

Given every admissible encoded `(P,A,y,k,c)`, return in charged polynomial time a Boolean `b in F(y)` with `c^T(b-y)<=0` and `||A(b-y)||_infinity < B(k)`.

The reset-and-progress invariant is the point I would audit first within that one obligation. Local negative-curvature calculations and finite tests do not by themselves show that rational directions, possibly arbitrarily short facet-hitting steps, face recovery, and denominator repair combine without losing the exact constraints, exceeding the trajectory-wide discrepancy allowance, or producing encodings growing with the current denominator. The handoff reports repairs for each issue; this intake does not establish that their composition works. This is an adoption gap, not a discovered counterexample.

Two small exact fixtures explain why this obligation has content:

1. In a product of nine rank-one two-element matroid bases, use first-pair marginals `(2/3,1/3)`, other pairs `(1/2,1/2)`, and cost `c=e_1`. The signed cost slice fixes `x_1=1/3, x_2=-1/3`, while all 18 coordinates are active (`18>8k=16`). A coordinatewise dyadic reset cannot preserve that exact cost slice. A basis-aware reset must retain the non-dyadic affine anchor.
2. In the rank-two five-element matroid whose only parallel pair is `{1,2}`, the bases are all two-element subsets except `{1,2}`, and `z_1+z_2<=1` is a valid rank inequality. The point `z=(3/10,3/10,7/15,7/15,7/15)` is a strictly positive mixture of all nine bases: weight `1/10` on each of the six cross pairs and `2/15` on each of the three pairs from `{3,4,5}`. Along `h=(3,3,-2,-2,-2)`, those weights interpolate to the uniform law on the six cross pairs at `t=1/15`, giving `(1/2,1/2,1/3,1/3,1/3)`. Any greater positive step violates the rank inequality. A proper face is reached while every coordinate stays fractional: exact affine ranks give dimension `4->3`. Appending six independent two-element rank-one blocks embeds this endpoint test in the active regime (`N=17>16`, dimension `10->9`). A routine that counts only integral-coordinate events misses the face drop.

## One conditional research-method use

Balanced randomized selection of a benchmarking or experiment panel with exact inclusion probabilities is a plausible use. Let each candidate be an element of a partition matroid with fixed stratum quotas; feasible panels are its bases, with `k(P)=2` and exact optimization available by selecting cheapest items within each stratum. Let rational `y` be a feasible fractional panel and let each candidate's column `A_:j` contain its known, preregistered diagnostic contributions, normalized so the **whole column vector** has Euclidean norm at most one.

If the reported upper-bound routine is correct, it yields a small rational law on feasible panels with inclusion probability exactly `y_j` for every candidate and with every realized panel's aggregate diagnostic vector within `<2209` of `Ay` in every row. The bound remains independent of panel size. The exact mean permits randomized designs with prescribed exposure; the hard feature guarantee can limit realized imbalance in known covariates. It supplies no guarantee on unknown outcomes, prefixes, additional feasibility intersections, or every realized panel's cost for all objectives. The general-polytope version still requires the exact strong optimizer and short actual edges; the simple quota example satisfies the optimizer contract explicitly.

The reported constant is large, so practical usefulness is unresolved. Scaling arbitrary columns by their maximum Euclidean norm scales the discrepancy bound by that norm; the column normalization cannot be replaced silently by independent row bounds. This is a conditional downstream use of the mathematical interface, not an assertion of a new research result.

They do not establish `R(P)<=1161k(P)`, polynomial-bit oracle implementation, external validity, or novelty.
