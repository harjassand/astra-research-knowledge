# Cycle 2: coupon design, quantitative upper bounds, and the remaining minimax gap

## 1. Boundary-response interface and periodic cell extraction

For a continuum scalar conductivity field (a(x)) on a cube (Q_R=(0,R)^d), let
\(\Lambda_{a,Q_R}:H^{1/2}(\partial Q_R)/\mathbb R\to H^{-1/2}(\partial Q_R)\)
be the exact Dirichlet-to-Neumann map. Its quadratic form is
\[
 q_a(g)=\langle g,\Lambda_{a,Q_R}g\rangle
 =\min_{u|_{\partial Q_R}=g}\int_{Q_R}\nabla u\cdot a\nabla u.
\]
For a macroscopic gradient \(\xi\), let \(\mathcal T_\xi\) be the traces of
\(u(x)=\xi\cdot x+v(x)\) with \(v\in H^1_{\rm per}(Q_R)\). Then
\[
 \xi\cdot A_R\xi
 =R^{-d}\inf_{g\in\mathcal T_\xi}q_a(g)
 =R^{-d}\inf_{v\in H^1_{\rm per}(Q_R)}
       \int_{Q_R}(\xi+\nabla v)\cdot a(\xi+\nabla v).
\]
Polarization recovers the symmetric cell tensor from the same quadratic form.
Thus the complete local response map is sufficient to compute the periodic RVE
cell tensor in the continuum variational model. A finite trace basis with \(q\)
degrees of freedom needs \(q\) boundary drives and \(q\) flux coefficients per
drive (\(q^2\) scalar entries); boundary-mode truncation, calibration, and the
induced tensor error must be charged separately. Below, an interface/solver
error \(\eta_{\rm resp}\) means the resulting tensor error, after minimization,
not an unconverted voltage tolerance.

This equivalence does not identify the probability law of an ordinary crop with
the law of a suitably periodized ensemble. The continuum RVE guarantee below is
for the latter construction. The paper cited below explicitly requires the
periodization to be constructed case by case. A physical snapshot periodically
repeated across its cut can have a different systematic error.

## 2. Strongest same-interface continuum RVE upper located

Use the scalar symmetric conductivity equation with \(I\le a\le K I\), a
stationary law, a supplied microscopic scale \(\ell\), and the scale-\(\ell\)
spectral-gap inequality of Fischer--Neukamm. For coupon side \(R\), put
\(s=R/\ell\ge2\). Also supply an \(R\)-periodic law \(\mathbb P_R\) which:

1. is supported on \(R\)-periodic coefficient fields;
2. has the required periodic spectral gap with correlation scale \(\ell\); and
3. agrees with the original field law on every central ball of radius \(R/4\).

This is the exact Definition 13/Theorem 14 interface in Fischer--Neukamm
(2021), not a consequence of a phase fraction or of an unquantified assertion
that correlations “look short-range.” Write \(Z_R\) for the tensor recovered
from a coupon sampled from \(\mathbb P_R\), and \(\mu_R=\mathbb E Z_R\).
For each fixed unit macroscopic direction, their theorem gives
\[
 |Z_R-\mu_R|\le \mathcal C_R s^{-d/2},\qquad
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left[(\mathcal C_R/C_0)^\nu\right]\le2,
\]
and the systematic error is bounded by
\[
 \|\mu_R-A_{\rm hom}\|\le b(s).
\]
Here \(C_0,\nu\) and all deterministic constants depend on dimension,
ellipticity/contrast and the spectral-gap parameter, not on \(R\). For
coefficients covered by their differentiable spectral-gap hypotheses, without
their additional microscopic regularity condition (R),
\[
 b(s)=C_b(d,K,\rho)\,s^{-q_d}(\log s)^{p_d},\quad
 q_d=\frac{d\wedge4}{2},\quad
 p_d=\begin{cases}(d\wedge4)/2+1/2,&d=2,4,\\0,&d\ne2,4.\end{cases}
\]
With the extra microscopic Lipschitz/regularity assumption (R), replace \(q_d\)
by \(d\wedge4\) and replace the critical logarithm exponent by
\(p_d=d\wedge4+1/2\) for \(d=2,4\), zero otherwise. The theorem’s constants
are functions of \(K\) (through ellipticity), but the paper does not provide an
optimized explicit polynomial in \(K\).

Scope caveat: the authors explicitly state (published pp. 364–365) that their
derivative-form spectral gap is for continuum-valued fields. For coefficient
fields taking values in a discrete set, an oscillation/finite-difference spectral
gap is available, but proving the needed corrector estimates requires an
additional decay result not supplied by their theorem. Therefore this continuum
upper bound is not automatically a bound for sharp binary phase fields.

### Coupon width and count sufficient conditions

Let \(s_\varepsilon\) be any \(s\ge2\) for which \(b(s)\le\varepsilon/4\).
This is the source-level width gate for a multi-coupon design. In two
dimensions with rough coefficients covered by the source hypotheses it is enough, up to the source
constant and the implicit logarithm, to choose
\[
 R\ \gtrsim\ \ell\,\frac{C_b}{\varepsilon}
             [\log(C_b/\varepsilon)]^{3/2}.
\]
If (R) holds, the corresponding gate improves to
\(R\gtrsim\ell(C_b/\varepsilon)^{1/2}
 [\log(C_b/\varepsilon)]^{5/4}\).

For one coupon, the stretched-exponential fluctuation estimate and a union
bound over the finite tensor entries give the sufficient high-confidence side
condition
\[
 s\ \ge\ \left(\frac{4C_0}{\varepsilon}
          [\log(2d^2/\delta)]^{1/\nu}\right)^{2/d}.
\]
Together with \(b(s)\le\varepsilon/4\) and
\(\eta_{\rm resp}\le\varepsilon/4\), this gives operator-norm tensor error at
most \(\varepsilon\) with probability at least \(1-\delta\), after absorbing
the fixed tensor-dimension norm factors into \(C_0,C_b\). This is a one-coupon
design, not a minimax necessity.

A cheaper-volume multi-coupon design can use a coordinatewise median of means.
The source tail bound implies an entrywise variance bound
\(\operatorname{Var}(Z_R)\le C_v(d,K,\rho)s^{-d}\). With independent coupons,
take group size and number of groups
\[
 m=\max\{1,\lceil64d^2 C_v s^{-d}\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil\},\qquad
 k=\lceil8\log(d^2/\delta)\rceil,\qquad N=mk.
\]
Chebyshev gives each group mean entry error at most \(\varepsilon/(4d)\) with
probability at least \(3/4\); the median and a union bound give tensor
statistical error at most \(\varepsilon/4\) with probability at least
\(1-\delta\). If also \(b(s)\le\varepsilon/4\) and
\(\eta_{\rm resp}\le\varepsilon/4\), the total tensor error is at most
\(3\varepsilon/4\). The charged sample volume is
\[
 V=NR^d\ \le\ C\ell^d\,[s^d+d^2 C_v\varepsilon^{-2}]
                  \log(d^2/\delta),
\]
with a dimension-universal numerical factor in \(C\). The required map
readings are \(Nq^2\) at boundary dimension \(q\), in addition to coupon
fabrication and the per-coupon minimization/linear solves. This is a sufficient
recipe conditional on a correctly periodized law and response precision; the
word “least” is not justified by this upper bound.

## 3. Stronger matched upper for a discrete iid resistor network

For the discrete \(\mathbb Z^d\) resistor network with iid edge conductances,
\(K^{-1}\le a_e\le1\), Gloria--Neukamm--Otto’s primary 2015 theorem (Theorem 2
of the long version) gives for the empirical mean of \(N\) independent
periodized RVE cell tensors, in \(d\ge2\),
\[
 \left(\mathbb E|A_{s,N}^{\rm RVE}-A_{\rm hom}|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le C(d,K)\left[N^{-1/2}s^{-d/2}+s^{-d}(\log s)^d\right].
\]
The variance term is for independent coupons; the second term is the bias of
the periodized iid ensemble and cannot be removed by increasing \(N\). This
is stronger than the general no-(R) continuum bias in Section 2, but it applies
to the lattice iid model, not every continuum finite-range medium. The theorem
states only \(C=C(d,\lambda)\), \(\lambda=1/K\), and does not expose an
optimized contrast polynomial. Robust median-of-means upgrades the variance
term to confidence \(1-\delta\) at a \(\log(1/\delta)\) coupon multiplier.

For the discrete coupon, the exact periodic-cell quadratic response is
\(A_s^{\rm RVE}\); equivalently it is obtained by minimizing the local
quadratic energy over affine-periodic traces, with boundary-crossing edges
included in the periodic cell. A full response acquisition therefore needs the
same charged boundary-mode budget described in Section 1; if the coupon is
instead an open graph with its cut bonds discarded, Theorem 2 does not
automatically apply to that altered interface.

## 4. Matched finite-range minimax theorem in a 1D series model

The exact lower/upper match is available in a restricted 1D two-phase model.
It is an electrical/thermal resistor chain, with each edge/cell of length
\(\ell\) independently having conductivity \(K\) with unknown probability
\(p\in[1/4,3/4]\), and conductivity 1 otherwise. The law is stationary under
cell translations and has one-cell dependence range. Write
\(\gamma=1-1/K\). The one-dimensional homogenized conductivity is exactly
\[
 A(p)=\left(\mathbb E[a^{-1}]\right)^{-1}
     =(1-\gamma p)^{-1}.
\]

For an aligned coupon of \(n\) cells, its complete two-terminal DtN map is
\(\Lambda=G\begin{psmallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{psmallmatrix}\), where
\[
 G=\left[\ell\sum_{j=1}^n a_j^{-1}\right]^{-1}
  =[\ell(n-\gamma B)]^{-1},\qquad
 B=\#\{j:a_j=K\}.
\]
Thus the boundary-response map determines the sufficient statistic \(B\)
exactly. Across \(N\) independent width-\(n\ell\) coupons, the total count
\(H=\sum B_i\) is \(\operatorname{Binomial}(M,p)\), \(M=nN\). The data law
contains exactly the same information about \(p\) as \(M\) iid Bernoulli
observations.

Define the contrast sensitivity
\[
 D_K=\max_{p\in[1/4,3/4]}A'(p)
     =\frac{\gamma}{(1-3\gamma/4)^2}.
\]
If \(\mathcal R_M\) is the minimax expected absolute error for estimating
\(A(p)\) from these coupon maps, then
\[
 \frac{D_K}{82\sqrt M}\ \le\ \mathcal R_M\ \le\ \frac{D_K}{2\sqrt M}.
\]
For the upper bound, clip \(\widehat p=H/M\) to \([1/4,3/4]\) and use
\(\widehat A=(1-\gamma\widehat p)^{-1}\); its Lipschitz constant is \(D_K\)
and \(\mathbb E|\widehat p-p|\le(2\sqrt M)^{-1}\).

For the lower bound set \(h=(16\sqrt M)^{-1}\), \(p_1=3/4\), and
\(p_0=3/4-2h\). Both belong to the parameter interval. With
\(m_1=1-3\gamma/4\),
\[
 A(p_1)-A(p_0)=\frac{2\gamma h}{m_1(m_1+2\gamma h)}
          \ge hD_K,
\]
since \(m_1\ge1/4\), \(h\le1/16\), and \(\gamma\le1\). The elementary
Bernoulli bound
\(D_{\rm KL}(\mathrm{Ber}(p_0)\|\mathrm{Ber}(p_1))
\le (p_1-p_0)^2/[p_1(1-p_1)]=(64/3)h^2\)
gives product KL at most \(1/12\). Pinsker yields TV at most
\(1/\sqrt{24}\); the two-point Le Cam inequality gives the displayed lower
bound (the numerical constant 82 is conservative).

There is also a matching confidence sample law. If
\(0<\varepsilon\le D_K/64\) and \(0<\delta\le1/8\), Hoeffding gives
\[
 M\ \ge\ \frac{D_K^2}{2\varepsilon^2}\log\frac2\delta
\]
as sufficient. Conversely, with \(h=4\varepsilon/D_K\), the pair
\(p_1=3/4, p_0=3/4-2h\) has effective-conductivity gap at least
\(4\varepsilon\), and KL at most \((1024/3)\varepsilon^2/D_K^2\) per
cell. Bretagnolle--Huber testing implies that any estimator with failure
probability at most \(\delta\) at both parameters must satisfy
\[
 M>\frac{3D_K^2}{1024\varepsilon^2}\log\frac1{4\delta}.
\]
Therefore the minimax cell count is \(\Theta(D_K^2\varepsilon^{-2}
\log(1/\delta))\) in this parameter regime, up to universal constants.

The width/count frontier is exact: for coupon width \(R=n\ell\), require
\(Nn\ge M_{\min}\); for count \(N\), require
\(R\ge\ell\lceil M_{\min}/N\rceil\). With no maximum coupon width, one coupon
of width \(M_{\min}\ell\) and \(M_{\min}\) one-cell coupons use the same
total material length \(V=M_{\min}\ell\). The resistance/impedance precision
must be below \(\gamma\ell/2\) to round each coupon response to the exact
integer count; if this fails, the stated exact-data theorem is not the
measurement model.

At \(K=100\), \(\gamma=99/100\) and \(D_K=158400/10609\approx14.9307\).
For \(\varepsilon=0.01,\delta=0.05\), the explicit Hoeffding sufficient count
is 4,111,743 cells; the two-point necessary threshold is about 10,511 cells.
These finite constants are loose, while their \(D_K^2\varepsilon^{-2}
\log(1/\delta)\) order matches. This is not a theorem for 2D/3D tensors.

## 5. Exact gap to a general finite-range minimax result

The first-wave stripe lower bound is not a finite-range lower bound. Its random
global phase makes the ensemble stationary/ergodic but gives infinite
dependence range. It rules out a uniform guarantee when no correlation-scale
bound is supplied; it does not show that \(N,R\) must scale in a specified way
inside a strict finite-range class.

For \(d\ge2\), I did not construct two distinct finite-range media whose laws
of complete local DtN maps are close while their homogenized tensors are
separated by a matching amount. Nor did I prove a lower bound of order
\(\ell^d\varepsilon^{-2}\) (or any sharper quantity) against all estimators
using the full response map. The RVE upper is not such a lower: it applies to
the periodized ensemble and uses a model-dependent corrector computation.
The explicit missing lemma is an estimator-independent two-model Le Cam
construction with (i) strict finite-range dependence at most \(\ell\),
(ii) the same contrast and supplied phase statistics, (iii) small TV between
the laws of \(N\) full coupon response maps of side \(R\), and (iv) a controlled
gap in \(A_{\rm hom}\). The previous shifted-stripe coupling only proves this
when global dependence is allowed.

The best completed capability is therefore: exact minimax design in the 1D
iid two-phase chain, plus theorem-backed sufficient design in the explicitly
quantified multi-D RVE classes, with a precise general-D lower-bound gap. It is
not a sharp general finite-correlation-length minimax theorem.
