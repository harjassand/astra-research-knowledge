# Epistasis scale: exact adaptive-query impossibility and calibrated repair

## Result

In the observation model where the latent two-locus phenotype is followed by an unknown strictly increasing assay response, a latent additive map and a latent map with pairwise interaction can induce exactly the same distribution of every possible assay result. This remains true for every adaptive genotype-query policy and every number of measurements. The exact construction and proof are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

The key paired worlds are `f0=x1+2x2`, `g0(u)=u+1` versus `f1=x1+2x2+7x1x2` and a strictly increasing piecewise-linear `g1` mapping latent levels `0,1,2,10` to observed means `1,2,3,4`. The observed Gaussian transcript laws coincide cell by cell, so the total variation distance is zero; worst-case classification error is at least `1/2`. This is an exact model identifiability statement, including the zero-noise case. It does not claim that interaction on a declared assay scale is unidentifiable.

With measurements delivered in the calibrated scale where epistasis is defined, the four-genotype factorial contrast estimates the pairwise coefficient with variance `4 sigma^2/r`. A level-`alpha` two-sided test has power at least `1-beta` for `|beta12|>=delta` if

`r >= ceil(4 sigma^2 (z_(1-alpha/2)+z_(1-beta))^2 / delta^2)`.

That costs `4r` genotype-phenotype measurements plus external calibration. Fewer than four distinct genotype cells cannot identify the interaction if intercept and main effects are unknown, regardless of repeat count or adaptivity.

## Prior-art judgment

Nonlinear phenotype scale inducing apparent epistasis is established in the genetics literature. Sailer and Harms explicitly demonstrate the effect and fit a restricted power-transform model to experimental maps ([2017 Genetics article](https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/205/3/1079/6066378), especially lines 59–72, 81–99, and 191–208 in the primary article). Contemporary work also handles noisy and incomplete genotype maps using variance-component prediction ([Zhou et al. 2022 PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9522415/)) and multiallelic sparse interaction representations ([2024 PLOS Computational Biology article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161127/)).

Therefore this package makes **no novelty or priority claim** for scale-induced epistasis. Its defensible contribution is an explicit nuisance-link observational-equivalence proof with quantification over adaptive queries, plus a calibrated measurement design and cost. Exact priority for that formal packaging has not been reviewed. Source details and boundaries are in [PRIOR_ART.md](PRIOR_ART.md).

An initial sparse-Walsh recovery direction was dropped as a novelty candidate because noisy parity learning is classic prior art (Blum, Kalai, and Wasserman, [JACM 2003](https://doi.org/10.1145/792538.792543)). No biological data were analyzed and no external proof review was obtained.

## Experiment

For a preregistered pair of mutations, measure all four genotypes in a randomized complete factorial with independent biological replicates and balanced batches. Define the phenotype scale before looking at the interaction contrast. If raw assay output is nonlinear, calibrate it using external standards over the relevant range rather than fitting the same four phenotypes under an additivity assumption. Set the biologically meaningful contrast `delta`, measurement variance `sigma^2`, type-I error `alpha`, and power `1-beta` in advance; the required replicate count follows from the formula above. Verify the calibrated contrast on an independent channel or run. A calibration failure or batch-dependent response rejects the measurement model, not the biological hypothesis.

For a general unrestricted monotone response curve, finitely many calibration standards cannot determine the curve everywhere. Calibration cost therefore requires either supplied full-range calibration or an explicit parametric/smoothness assumption with a measured error budget; that cost is not hidden inside the `4r` phenotype measurements.
