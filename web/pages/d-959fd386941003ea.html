# An independently reconstructed nonlinear rough-step interface

Worker `frontier_combin1`, cycle19. Pinned release `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`; 7 October 2026. This note concerns a reusable intermediate theorem in family164, not adoption of its full Hindman claim.

## Source and status

OpenAI's **Monochromatic finite sums and products in the positive integers**, title-source date 23 September 2026, claims the finite simultaneous theorem over positive integers. I read its introduction and combinatorial deduction, all of Section6, and selected cube-lifting and alignment arguments. The full manuscript was **not** read; its full headline remains unadopted.

Section6's Proposition 6.3 (`prop:rough-step`) compares unrestricted spatial averages of a polynomially coupled nilmanifold family with averages on most rough arithmetic steps. Lemma 6.2 (`lem:rough-interpolation`) is the arithmetic engine: small fractional parts of `t_z^e theta(z)` on a long progression of rough `t_z` force `theta(z)` to be close to an integer-valued rational polynomial. The source uses linear `t_z=t0+mz`, with smooth `m`.

The new deduction below replaces that divisor by a **nonlinear integer polynomial with smooth leading coefficient**. Its elementary arithmetic proof is complete here. The nilmanifold consequence uses the corrected equal-side quantitative Leibman theorem as an explicit external input, then independently reconstructs the source's induction. The exact external statement appears as Theorem 6.1 in the source, with its arbitrary-comparable-box consequence proved there. [Green–Tao's erratum, arXiv:1311.6170v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6170v3), last revised 14 August 2015, explicitly repairs the multiparameter result and the equal-side case; I verified the version and correction scope, but did not independently reprove its analytic theorem.

## Generalized exact-division lemma

Fix integers `e>=1`, `Q>=e` and a constant `A>0`. Let an integer `H>=2Q+2` be fixed before taking limits. Put `W=product_(p<=w) p`, where `w->infinity`. Suppose `S->infinity` and `Z/S^a->infinity` for every fixed `a>0`.

Let `f_w(z)` be a nonconstant polynomial in `Z[z]` of fixed degree `d`, whose nonzero leading coefficient has only prime factors at most `w`. Suppose, for integer `0<=z<H`,

`S <= f_w(z) <= C0 S` and `gcd(f_w(z),W)=1`,

where `C0` is fixed. Let `theta_w(z)` be real polynomial, with

`deg(f_w^e theta_w)<=Q` and `||f_w(z)^e theta_w(z)||_(R/Z) <= A(S/Z)^e`.

Then, for all sufficiently late terms, there is a rational polynomial `M_w` of degree at most `Q-ed`, integral at all those integer indices, with smooth coefficient denominators and

`|theta_w(z)-M_w(z)| <= C_Q A Z^(-e)` for `0<=z<H`.

The constant `C_Q` is independent of `H`; the sufficiently-late threshold may depend on `H`, the fixed degrees and `C0`. If `ed>Q`, the hypotheses require the zero polynomial `theta`; interpret the conclusion accordingly.

**Proof.** Let `T=f^e theta` and `epsilon=A(S/Z)^e`. Choose `Q+1` integer interpolation nodes in `[0,H-1]`, separated after division by `H-1` by at least `1/(2Q)`. Round each `T` value to a nearest integer and call their degree-at-most-Q Lagrange interpolant `R`. The Lagrange basis on the rescaled interval is bounded in terms of `Q` alone, so

`sup_[0,H-1] |R-T| <= C_Q epsilon`.

A fixed integer `D_H`, formed from the node differences, clears all coefficients of `R`. Thus `D_H R` is in `Z[z]`. The ordinary coefficients of `R-T` are `O_(Q,H)(epsilon)`.

The values of `f` at at least `d+1` fixed integer nodes are `O(S)`. Inverting their fixed Vandermonde matrix bounds all coefficients of `f` by `C_(d,H,C0) S`; its leading coefficient `a_d` is a nonzero integer. Perform polynomial long division by `f^e`. The remainder map is linear in the numerator coefficients, and its coefficients are rational expressions in the coefficients of `f`, with denominators powers of `a_d`. Since the degrees are fixed, there is a fixed exponent `B` for which the remainder of `R-T` has coefficients at most `C S^B epsilon` in absolute value. Because `T` is exactly divisible, this is also the remainder of `R`.

Each remainder coefficient is rational with denominator dividing `D_H a_d^B` after increasing B. A nonzero such rational has magnitude at least `1/(D_H |a_d|^B)>=C' S^(-B)`. The domination of all powers by `Z` makes `C S^B epsilon<C' S^(-B)` eventually. Every remainder coefficient is therefore exactly zero. Hence

`R=f^e M`, with `M in Q[z]` and `deg M<=Q-ed`.

The quotient coefficients have denominators whose prime factors occur only in `D_H` and `a_d`. The first integer is fixed, and the second is smooth by hypothesis, so all those primes are at most `w` eventually.

At every integer sample index, `R(z)` is within `(C_Q+1)epsilon` of an integer, while `R(z)` belongs to `D_H^(-1) Z`. It is therefore an integer eventually. The denominator of `M(z)` has only primes at most `w`, but `f(z)` has none of those primes. The exact integrality of `f(z)^e M(z)=R(z)` forces `M(z)` itself to be an integer. Finally divide the uniform interpolation error by `f(z)^e>=S^e`. This gives the asserted constant independent of H. QED.

The original source's multiplicity-at-a-rational-root argument is a special case. Long division avoids needing rational roots for a nonlinear divisor. The small numerical error is used only to force a bounded-denominator remainder to vanish exactly.

## Nonlinear progression-comparison theorem

Fix a connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group `G`, a lattice `Gamma`, a spatial dimension `n`, a bound on ordinary logarithmic polynomial degree, and fixed positive constants for box comparability and observable Lipschitz norm.

Let `w->infinity`, `W=product_(p<=w) p`, and let `L` be smooth with `W|L`. Let `S/L->infinity`, and sample the parameter set

`T=[S,2S) intersect (r+LZ)`.

Let `Q_w(t)` be an integer polynomial of a fixed positive degree d, with smooth nonzero leading coefficient. Suppose

`gcd(Q_w(r),W)=1`, and `S'<=Q_w(t)<=C0 S'` for every `t in T`,

where `S'->infinity`. Let `Z/(S+S')^a->infinity` for every fixed positive a. Let `P_w(t,x)` be G-valued, with logarithmic coordinates polynomial jointly in `(t,x)` of fixed bounded degree. Their coefficients are unrestricted and may change with w.

For every fixed tolerance eta, the proportion of `t in T` for which there exist a comparable box inside `[-CZ,CZ]^n`, an arbitrary residue vector b modulo `Q_w(t)`, and an observable F within the fixed Lipschitz bound such that

`| average_(integer x in box) F(P_w(t,x) Gamma)`

`  - average_(x in box intersect (b+Q_w(t) Z^n)) F(P_w(t,x) Gamma) | >= eta`

tends to zero. The box, b and F may be chosen separately for each t. The exceptional proportion is uniform over all allowed data sequences. No polynomial dependence of those three choices is required.

**Proof using the corrected quantitative Leibman input.** A positive exceptional proportion gives, for every fixed length H, a progression of bad parameter values `t_z=t0+mz` with smooth m: use finite Szemerédi on bounded blocks in the index coordinate `t=r+Lu`; the progression difference is `jL` with fixed bounded j, hence smooth for late w. Reparametrization preserves bounded joint polynomial degree. The sampled spatial step becomes the polynomial `q_z=Q_w(t0+mz)`. Its leading coefficient is the old leading coefficient times `m^d`, so is smooth; its sampled values are rough, because `Q_w(t_z)=Q_w(r) mod W`.

Prove that arbitrarily long bad progression families of this generalized kind cannot exist, inducting on dim G. Thin to a fixed horizontal character supplied by quantitative Leibman; characters of the required bounded size form a fixed finite list. If the unrestricted or restricted sample is eta-discrepant from Haar, write its step as `p_z=1` or `q_z`. For a horizontal polynomial `sum_I theta_I(z) x^I`, the sampled coefficient obstruction is

`|| p_z^|I| sum_(J>=I) binom(J,I) b_z^(J-I) theta_J(z) || <= A (p_z/Z)^|I|`.

Descending through spatial degrees, previously recovered integer coefficients contribute integers, and their errors contribute only `O((p_z/Z)^|I|)`, since `p_z=O(S')=o(Z)`. Thus

`|| q_z^|I| theta_I(z) || <= A' (S'/Z)^|I|`.

When `p_z=1`, multiply by the integer `q_z^|I|` to get the same bound. Apply the generalized exact-division lemma with divisor q_z to obtain an integer-valued rational polynomial `m_I(z)` and error `O(Z^(-|I|))`. Its degree is bounded by the old joint degree. The exactness threshold may depend on the fixed bad-progression length, which is allowed.

Thin again by finitely colouring the fractional parts of the constant spatial coefficient into short intervals. Van der Waerden gives arbitrarily long monochromatic subprogressions. On such a progression, a high enough finite difference of its floor is an integer of magnitude less than one; it vanishes. Hence this floor agrees with a rational polynomial of fixed bounded degree, with denominator dividing the appropriate factorial. This supplies the constant integer part too.

Split the horizontal polynomial into an integer polynomial in x and a bounded slowly varying real part. Choose a rational direction V on which the character equals one, and subtract the slow part on the left and the integer part on the right. The resulting G-valued map lies in the connected rational kernel of the character and retains bounded joint logarithmic polynomial degree, by finite BCH. This kernel has smaller dimension.

Freeze the slow factor on finitely many comparable subboxes and split x into residues modulo a fixed integer K so that the right rational factor has a fixed lattice coset. Since each q_z is rough, it is eventually coprime to K. Both sampling laws assign asymptotically identical weights to these pieces, and their conditional discrepancy on at least one piece remains bounded below. Reindex `x=u+Ky`: unrestricted samples become unrestricted integer y, restricted samples remain a residue class modulo q_z. The new common spatial scale `Z/K` still dominates all powers. The observables and boxes may vary with z, which was permitted from the start.

Finite choices of characters, rational directions, fixed residues and quotient lattices can be fixed on progressions of arbitrarily large length. Every parameter thinning preserves the smooth leading coefficient of q_z. This constructs a bad progression family in smaller group dimension, contradicting the induction. The zero-dimensional case has no discrepancy. The positive-exceptional-proportion assumption therefore fails. A sequence with a nonvanishing claimed supremum would itself be a violating allowed data sequence, proving the asserted uniformity. QED, conditional only on the stated published quantitative Leibman theorem and qualitative finite Szemerédi/van der Waerden inputs.

## Concrete new application and exact obstruction

Take `Q(t)=t^2+1`. For each small prime choose a residue r with `r^2+1 != 0`; at p=2 choose r=0, and for every larger prime there are fewer roots than residue classes. CRT supplies r modulo W. Choose any smooth L divisible by W and S with S/L tending to infinity. Then the sampled quadratic steps are all rough, their scale is comparable to S^2, and their leading coefficient is one. The theorem compares most steps `t^2+1` with unrestricted averages for any jointly polynomial nilsequence family, uniformly over the actual large box and spatial residue chosen after t.

This is a reusable input for transference/counting arguments with nonlinear divisibility constraints. It neither handles arbitrary parameter dependence nor proves the final simultaneous sums/products theorem.

The smooth-leading-coefficient restriction is decisive. If it is dropped, choose a prime `p_w>w`, `Q_w(t)=p_w t`, and the circle nilmanifold with

`P_w(t,x)=x/p_w mod1`, `F(y)=exp(2 pi i y)`.

Use rough t. Then Q_w(t) is rough, all polynomial degree bounds hold, and the unrestricted average tends to zero when Z/p_w tends to infinity. On any class `b+Q_w(t)Z`, F is identically `exp(2 pi i b/p_w)`, of modulus one. Thus **every** parameter is bad. Only the smooth-leading hypothesis was removed. This is an exact counterexample, not a finite search result.

Similarly, without joint polynomial dependence, `P_t(x)=x/t mod1` gives a discrepancy near one on every large step t. The source's uniformity is therefore a coupling theorem for controlled polynomial families, not a licence to choose arbitrary models after selecting the step.

## Prior art, scope, costs and next falsifiable step

The linear rough-step theorem and its proof skeleton come from family164, and quantitative orbit obstructions come from Green–Tao. I have not found or excluded an earlier general nonlinear formulation; global novelty is unverified. The arithmetic division lemma is an independently reconstructed generalization absent from the read Section6. The theorem's sampling proportion is asymptotic and qualitative: no rate, no useful explicit threshold, and no efficient evaluation/acquisition cost for the nilsequence models is established. Fixed group dimension, degree, Lipschitz bound and comparable-box constants remain fixed before the limit; uniformity does not allow them to grow without charge.

The next discriminating check is an independent reconstruction of the generalized long-division denominator bound and the smaller-kernel induction on a genuinely nonabelian nilmanifold. A circle-only computation cannot settle the joint nilpotent statement. If the induction is rejected, the elementary exact-division lemma and the two explicit interface counterexamples remain valid standalone results.
