## Exact quantitative statement

Let N>=k>=m>=1, 0<epsilon<1, S subset F_2^N fixed with |S|>=2^k. Put

\[
s=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil,\quad
a=\lceil\log_2(32/\epsilon^2)\rceil,
\qquad k\ge m+s+a.
\]

Uniform surjective linear L:F_2^N->F_2^m covers S with probability at least1-epsilon. In fact the derivation below gives the sharper failure bound epsilon(1-2^(-s-1)). This is a per-fixed-source seeded guarantee. It is not one map simultaneously covering every eligible S.

## Second moments with dependent sampled generators

Set d0=N-k+a. Sample d0 independent uniform vectors v_j; their span U0 can be rank deficient. Let q0=1-|S+U0|/2^N. For an independent uniform r and any fixed z, let

\[
X=\sum_{b\in F_2^{d0}}1_S\left(z+r+\sum_j b_jv_j\right).
\]

Distinct b,c give two independent uniform vector-valued affine forms: the rows (1,b),(1,c) are linearly independent over F_2. Thus the indicators are pairwise independent even though individual realized coefficient vectors may coincide when the sampled span is deficient. Writing p=|S|/2^N gives E X=2^d0 p=mu and Var X=mu(1-p). Chebyshev implies Pr[X=0]<=1/mu<=2^-a.

For fixed sampled v_j, averaging over r,z yields exactly q0 because translation by r permutes F_2^N. Consequently E q0<=2^-a<=epsilon^2/32 and Pr[q0>epsilon/16]<=epsilon/2. This validates counting coefficient vectors with multiplicity; replacing them by an assumed independent basis would be an unjustified change of distribution.

## Conditional doubling and the summable recursion

Given the past, add independent uniform w_i. The uncovered set changes from V to V intersect(V+w_i), so averaging ordered pairs gives E[q_i|past]=q_(i-1)^2 exactly. Define A_i=2^(i+1)/epsilon. Conditional Markov, followed by the union bound, gives

\[
\Pr[\exists i\le s:q_i>A_iq_{i-1}^2]
\le\sum_{i=1}^s\epsilon 2^{-i-1}
=\tfrac\epsilon2(1-2^{-s}).
\]

The zero-density case is automatic. On the good event, direct induction gives

\[
q_i\le \epsilon 2^{-i-3}2^{-2^i}.
\]

Its base is epsilon/16. Squaring at step i-1 and multiplying by A_i gives exactly the displayed step i. Since 2^s>=m, q_s<2^-m. The total bad-event probability is at most epsilon-epsilon/2^(s+1), including s=0 (m=1). No interchange E[q^2]=(E q)^2 is made; that would be false for a hyperplane source.

## Uniform kernel completion: explicit flag counting

There are l=d0+s<=N-m sampled vectors. Put t=N-m and U their span. Given U, sample uniformly a t-dimensional K containing U. This completion is always legal even at rank l or rank zero.

One can prove uniformity by GL(N,2) invariance, but the exact count also reconstructs the mechanism. Conditional on rank(U)=r, U is uniform among all r-subspaces. Let [b choose c]_2 denote a Gaussian binomial coefficient. For a fixed t-subspace K, the number of eligible U inside K is [t choose r]_2, while each U has [N-r choose t-r]_2 completions. Hence

\[
\Pr[K\mid \operatorname{rank}U=r]
=\frac{[t\choose r]_2}{[N\choose r]_2[N-r\choose t-r]_2}
=\frac1{[N\choose t]_2}.
\]

The equality counts flags U subset K in the two orders. Thus every rank mixture gives the exact uniform K distribution. A uniform isomorphism F_2^N/K->F_2^m then gives the exact uniform-surjective map: every kernel has the same number |GL(m,2)| of isomorphisms.

Crucially the completion is not conditioned on a coverage test. Conditioning or rejection based on S could bias the resulting kernel and would require access to S. Here the auxiliary vectors merely couple an unconditional uniform map with a favorable event; the actual map sampler need only know N,m.

If L has a missing output y, its entire K-coset is disjoint from S+U, because U subset K. The missing coset contains 2^(N-m) points, implying q_s>=2^-m. Therefore the good recursion event forces coverage. This establishes the claimed probability without a false independence assertion between K and the source-dependent coverage event.

## Fixed-time sampler with exactly mN random bits

The same parameters also allow sampling **every** m-by-N binary matrix uniformly, without rank checking or rejection. Conditional on full row rank its law is uniform-surjective. The rank failure probability is

\[
1-\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(1-2^{i-N})
\le\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}2^{i-N}<2^{m-N}
\le2^{-s-a}\le\frac{\epsilon^2}{32\cdot2^s}.
\]

Combining this with the retained recursion slack gives total coverage failure at most

\[
\epsilon-\frac{\epsilon}{2^{s+1}}+
\frac{\epsilon^2}{32\cdot2^s}<\epsilon.
\]

Thus an exact fixed-time, fixed-seed-length sampler is available at the **same** entropy threshold, not just the expected-polynomial-time uniform-surjective rejection sampler. Building its map requires mN fair bits, O(mN) description bits and at most m(N-1) fan-in-two XOR gates. Evaluation uses O(mN) bit operations; balanced implementation adds O(log N) depth. This refinement improves the explicit sampler interface, not the prior-art status: Alon et al.'s theorem already treats uniform all-linear maps.

## The Li–Ren–Zhong consequence uses a hypothetical rejected set, not an oracle

Use a supplied explicit polynomial-size G_n:F_2^n->F_2^N and output length m>n. Assume the appropriate weak demi-hardness interface: every legal nondeterministic polynomial-time adversary that rejects all of Range(G_n) must reject at least2^k ambient strings, eventually. For nonuniform G_n and uniform adversaries, give its circuit description as part of the adversary input, exactly as the source specifies. For a uniform generator family one should make the length index n recoverable or include the family tag n; this prevents any silent reconstruction of an arbitrary nonuniform circuit from the output length alone.

Fix an errorless SearchNP avoidance heuristic A. Construct B(z) to accept iff it can guess a binary matrix L from the sampler's support and an accepting branch of A(L composed G_n) that outputs Lz. This uses polynomial descriptions and computation; it is legal with the same G interface. If z=G_n(x), Lz is in the composed range for every L, so errorlessness makes every B branch reject. Let R be B's rejected set. Weak demi-hardness implies |R|>=2^k.

R is defined using **all** matrices existentially, and therefore is fixed before the actual random matrix is drawn. Proposition A, or the all-matrix refinement, applies to this fixed R without recognizing or enumerating it. If A succeeded on more than an epsilon fraction of maps, some successful map would also cover R. A's output y would equal Lz for some rejected z in R; the very same guessed L and branch would make B accept z, a contradiction. Thus A succeeds on at most epsilon of the sampled circuits.

This is a valid conditional hardness reduction without supplied-hard-set or membership-oracle assumptions. It does **not** construct a demi-hard G, verify that G has the assumed hardness, recognize good maps, produce a deterministic missing output, or prove a circuit lower bound. The hard-string count includes range strings where nonmembership is false; the assumption must genuinely exceed the trivial <=2^n count.

At m=n+1 and epsilon=1/2, a=7 and the exact sufficient count is 2^(n+8+ceil(log2(n+1))). Since 2^ceil(log2(n+1))<=2(n+1)<=4n, the convenient sufficient count1024 n2^n is valid for n>=1. If ambient N cannot accommodate this count, the premise is impossible, not a new hardness conclusion.

For constant-degree GF(2) output polynomials, linear XOR postprocessing preserves algebraic degree and polynomial size. This makes the reduction a conditional hardness amplifier within the same algebraic-degree class at nearoptimal support threshold. Dense XOR generally destroys NC0 locality and has no useful constant normalized-Hamming Lipschitz guarantee; the remote-point theorem cannot be substituted here.

## Proof-system version and prior-art boundary

For a fixed proof system, threshold and G, an explicit short-proof transformation is necessary. Semantic implication alone does not justify a bound on proof size. Ren–Wang–Zhong's Definition3 and Claim4 provide simple parity reductions for the Tseitin circuit encoding: each newly introduced linear gate is an XOR of previous gate variables, its clauses become tautologies, and output clauses become XORs of original output axioms. A proof system closed under those reductions gives the required polynomial length bound. The cited definition and argument are complete at saved PDF pp.16–17. Surjectivity of L is not needed for the gate-substitution argument; the good coverage event itself implies surjectivity.

A fixed proof threshold does not automatically produce one family simultaneously hard at every polynomial threshold or against every proof system. The September source separately treats that issue using an optimal advised system/selection argument; it is not proved by Proposition A alone. r14 correctly keeps this limitation explicit.
