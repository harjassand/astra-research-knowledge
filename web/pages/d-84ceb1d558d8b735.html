## A. General exact lemma

Let C be a finite abelian group of size M, and let normalized vectors psi_c = U_c psi_0 form a group-uniform ensemble. Let Phi have these vectors as columns, and G=Phi†Phi. For this clean version assume G is positive definite. The group action makes G translation invariant, so the normalized character vectors chi/sqrt(M) diagonalize G. Write G chi=lambda_chi chi, lambda_chi>0, and

    s = Tr(sqrt(G))/M.

The diagonal of sqrt(G) is constant and equals s. Set Mu=Phi G^(-1/2). Then Mu†Mu=I, so its columns mu_c are orthonormal; their rank-one projectors are the PGM effects on the ensemble span. Moreover

    <mu_c,psi_c> = s,
    Mu chi = Phi chi / sqrt(lambda_chi).

Suppose a coherent decoder and its inverse realize these projective measurement effects. Prepare

    M^(-1/2) sum_c chi_c psi_c |c>_R,

run the decoder, add its output into R, postselect R=0, and undo the decoder. This is exactly the source's correct-codeword postselection interface. The correctly labelled component of psi_c is s times the decoded image of mu_c. Consequently the postselection probability is s², independently of the character, and undoing gives

    s M^(-1/2) sum_c chi_c mu_c |0>_decoder.

After normalization the output is therefore exactly

    Phi chi / sqrt(M lambda_chi).

No small-error assumption and no sign average enters this equality. The proof requires the coherent projective PGM, not merely a decoder with the same classical success rate, and not a measurement oracle with inaccessible garbage. Any reversible dilation of these projective effects works: on mu_c the outcome c is deterministic, so undoing maps the correctly decoded component back to s mu_c.

Norm normalization is material. The ideal unnormalized sum has norm sqrt(lambda_chi), while the accepted state has norm s. Their proportionality, not an absolute unnormalized error estimate, is what proves exactness.

## B. Consequence for the commuting QD-HDQI interface

The primary noise ensemble is shifted by C=ker(H), where H is the Pauli-label matrix. A fixed sign y supplies the character (-1)^(y·c), together with the known physical phase correction in the source. After its unitary codeword-to-syndrome transformation and Bell inverse, the normalized coherent sum is the desired canonical Gibbs purification.

For finite inverse temperature, the source's Gram identity gives lambda_y=K_y>0 because the relevant exponential is strictly positive. Thus efficient reversible PGM implementation with success s² bounded below by an inverse polynomial gives exact fixed-sign Gibbs preparation in the ideal real-gate model. Finite circuit precision must be charged separately.

Since the PGM is optimal for this group-uniform ensemble, if any decoder has failure p_fail then s²>=1-p_fail. This proves a success lower bound; it does NOT convert an arbitrary efficient decoder into an efficient PGM circuit.

If the implemented decoder unitary differs from the exact coherent PGM by operator norm at most epsilon, use of it and its inverse changes the unnormalized accepted vector by at most 2 epsilon. For epsilon<=s/4, its acceptance probability is at least (s-2 epsilon)² and its normalized pure output has trace-norm error at most 8 epsilon/s. Subsequent unitary transforms and partial trace do not increase the error. This is a supplied operator-norm circuit approximation interface, not a consequence of average decoding error.

## C. Exact obstruction to upgrading random signs by Pauli frames

Conjugating the physical Hamiltonian by a Pauli R changes its sign vector by b in im(H^T)=C-perp, because the symplectic form is invertible on physical Pauli labels. For every c in C, b·c=0. Therefore averaging over physically undoable Pauli-frame signs y+C-perp leaves every character on C unchanged. In particular the off-diagonal factor (-1)^[y·(c1+c2)] in the source's interference proof is unchanged when c1+c2 belongs to C.

The Gram eigenvalue K_y is likewise constant throughout this coset. The source's full-sign averaging argument cannot be acquired through this frame randomization. Identity-product relations have invariant frustration signs. This is a proof-interface obstruction, not a counterexample to the source's average-case theorem or to the exact PGM lemma.

For the elementary commuting triangle P1=Z1,P2=Z2,P3=Z1Z2, C={000,111}. At beta=log(2), tanh(beta)=3/5 and the aligned unsigned choice has

    K = 1 + (3/5)^3 = 152/125,

whereas the other relation-sign coset has K=98/125. Pauli frames stay in a parity coset; a full uniform sign average has mean K=1.

A stronger asymptotic boundary is also explicit. Take L disjoint triangles with overlap a_L=L^(-3/4). Their constant-size optimal block decoders have total failure at most L a_L²/2=(1/2)L^(-1/2), yet the aligned fixed-sign normalization is (1+a_L)^L, which diverges as exp(Theta(L^(1/4))). Thus vanishing decoding error alone cannot make the source's unnormalized fixed-sign normalization/interference estimates small. This does NOT imply the normalized prepared Gibbs state is inaccurate: the projective PGM lemma above actually prepares each of these triangles exactly and efficiently.

## D. Costs, strongest controls, and unresolved acquisition

The exact lemma charges preparation of the coefficient/codeword state, coherent PGM plus inverse, known codeword/syndrome/Bell transforms, and mean 1/s² attempts. Approximation requires an acquired operator-norm implementation and the error bound above. Generic PGM matrix dimensions are exponential in dim(C). A generic inverse-square-root implementation may depend on the smallest nonzero ensemble eigenvalue, not merely success probability. No circuit for the programme's actual code is acquired here.

For L independent triangles, the Gram eigenvalues are products of 1±a. The normalized ensemble has smallest nonzero eigenvalue ((1-a)/2)^L. Its reciprocal grows exponentially even at fixed a, although the explicit tensor-product constant-size PGM bypasses that generic bound. This establishes failure of a generic condition-number certificate to imply an efficient implementation; it is not an implementation lower bound.

The nearest older primary is Eldar–Forney, quant-ph/0005132v2 / IEEE IT47(3),858–872(2001), for orthogonal SRM columns and group-uniform optimality. Its polar derivation (`rev-srm-it2.tex420–758`) and complete GU Fourier/optimality proof (`1406–1857`, Theorem SRM for GU state sets) have been read. Gilyen et al2006.16924v2, revised1June2022, provides an algorithmic PGM/Petz implementation with explicit state/block-encoding and spectral parameters. Its Theorem1 and all four implementation/error steps (`prl-resubmission.tex374–511`) and PGM specialization (`519–543`) have been read. It assumes acquired block encodings or state purifications and a channel Stinespring circuit. Up to logarithmic factors its cost is

    sqrt(dE kappa_bar) [kappa_bar Nbar + Nchannel
                   + Nsigma min(kappa_sigma,dE kappa_bar/epsilon²)].

Here kappa means the RECIPROCAL minimum nonzero density eigenvalue, not a largest/smallest ratio. For our uniform pure ensemble, dE=M, kappa_sigma=M, and kappa_bar=M/lambda_min(G). The paper's displayed PGM-specialization expression for kappa_sigma as `min_x p_x kappa_x` is inconsistent with its preceding definition; the direct block-diagonal spectrum gives `max_x kappa_x/p_x`. We use this direct computation and Theorem1, not the inconsistent substitution. This is a scope correction to that displayed specialization, not a refutation of the general algorithm.

The proof acquires an approximation to the coherent pretty-good-instrument isometry and an amplified unitary block encoding. A channel diamond guarantee alone is not our interface. More precisely, if a supplied circuit U satisfies ||U I0-V||<=xi for the ideal coherent projective-PGM isometry V, the same 2xi accepted-vector and 8xi/s output bounds follow: U†V-I0 has the same norm, and the ideal accepted vector lies in ran(V). A unitary (1,epsilon)-block encoding of V has input-subspace error at most epsilon+sqrt(2epsilon+epsilon²), because its omitted-block squared norm is at most2epsilon+epsilon². Thus the old algorithm can meet this interface by charging epsilon=O(xi²). Its generic resource dependence still gives no polynomial acquisition for our code. No claim about an arbitrary chosen unitary completion follows merely from a measurement's classical error or channel diamond error.
