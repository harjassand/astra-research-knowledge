## Mechanisms considered and exact dispositions

1. **Nanoconfined liquid ribs through a freezing ionocaloric solvent.** A possible liquid-conduction pathway addresses a genuine local transport issue. It does not lower membrane area resistance; the inequality in `membrane_limit.md` kills the claimed geometry-only escape. The proposed >=100 W/L target was never demonstrated or justified at acceptable membrane area/cost. Rejected.
2. **Membrane-free, electrode-based salt extraction.** Capacitive and Faradaic deionization are expressly described in the original inventors' patent with 2022 priority. This is known prior art, not a new invention here. [US20260029170A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260029170A1/en).
3. **Distributed near-isothermal water-vapor diaphragm/peristaltic compression.** Diaphragm water-vapor compression was developed in NASA's 1970 water-recovery work; progressive electrostatic diaphragm refrigeration and valveless peristaltic compression also have direct prior art. Packing these into a shallow laminate is insufficient to establish a distinct conversion mechanism. Rejected as the requested invention, even where numerically possible. [NASA primary report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710013038/downloads/19710013038.pdf), [Purdue primary compressor course](https://web.mit.edu/parmstr/Public/2012CmprShortCourse/2012CmprSC12.pdf), [peristaltic compressor patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2021226131A1/en).
4. **Liquid-piston isothermal refrigeration.** This is already experimental technology: a 2025 ORNL CO2 refrigeration compressor reports approximately 90% isothermal efficiency. That is compressor P–V efficiency, not a demonstrated new whole-system COP or low-cost water-compressor result. [ORNL author record](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/development-of-a-near-isothermal-transcritical-cosub2sub-compress/).

## Quantitative closure of the discarded water route

`envelope.py` uses an ideal vapor and incompressible liquid with constant heat capacities. It integrates Clausius–Clapeyron consistently; it is deliberately not a replacement for [IAPWS](https://www.iapws.org/newform.html). Numerical precision in its JSON is computational precision, not accuracy of a physical prototype.

Let c_vp and c_lp be vapor and liquid constant-pressure heat capacities, R the specific vapor gas constant, and L(T) = L_0 + (c_vp-c_lp)(T-T_0). Neglect liquid specific volume. Saturation is

    ln[p_s(T)/p_0] = [(L_0-(c_vp-c_lp)T_0)(1/T_0-1/T)
                        +(c_vp-c_lp) ln(T/T_0)] / R.

A vapor stream entering at saturated T_c cannot be compressed isothermally at T_c while remaining vapor: it would condense. For the favorable hot-wall design, preheat the suction vapor to T_h using the sink, compress isothermally there, condense at T_h, and throttle the liquid back to p_s(T_c). Then

    q_c = L(T_c) - c_lp(T_h-T_c),
    w_iso = R T_h ln[p_s(T_h)/p_s(T_c)],
    rho_suction = p_s(T_c)/(R T_h),
    suction_volume_rate = Qdot_c/(rho_suction q_c).

The entropy balance is closed: w_iso - q_c(T_h-T_c)/T_c = T_h(S_preheat + S_throttle), with

    S_preheat = c_vp[ln(T_h/T_c) - (T_h-T_c)/T_h],
    S_throttle = c_lp[ln(T_c/T_h) + (T_h-T_c)/T_c].

Both terms are nonnegative. The script asserts this identity and that modeled COP never exceeds Carnot. The inlet preheat is not counted as free external energy; it is heat absorbed from the hot reservoir and enters this entropy balance.

At 10 -> 35 degC and 1 kW, the optimistic model requires roughly 49 L/s of hot-preheated suction vapor. With frequency f and effective stroke g, total swept active area is A = volume_rate/(fg). At 5 Hz and 10 mm effective stroke it is roughly 1 m², with roughly 10 L swept volume. This excludes clearance, backflow, manifolds, frames, motor, valves, and vacuum shell. The modeled ideal COP is about 11; a COP-6 target leaves only about 75 W for **all** additional losses. At continuous 5 Hz, each flexure experiences about 158 million cycles per year. None of these gates has been met by a new candidate here.

Integrating the condenser into the compression pocket creates an additional measurable gate: retained liquid at T_h releases c_lp(T_h-T_c) per kilogram when exposed again to the low suction pressure. The retained hot film budget for a fraction epsilon of the useful stroke heat is

    delta_f <= epsilon rho_suction g q_c/[rho_liquid c_lp(T_h-T_c)].

Under the same optimistic geometry, the area-averaged retained-film limit for a 5% heat penalty is of order 0.1 micrometre. This is a conditional engineering constraint, not a proof that dropwise drainage is impossible. It does prevent treating wet surfaces, dead liquid, and repeated flash cooling as cost-free.

## Decisive tests, if independently revisited

For the ionocaloric branch, first measure useful cycle heat Q, net transported charge with both ionic strokes, R_A, duty, current efficiency, and total electrical/auxiliary input on the **same closed cycle**. A scaffold versus an equally thin ordinary-channel control at equal membrane area, chemistry, temperatures, and work would falsify a claimed escape if output tracks membrane conductance rather than the scaffold. No new construction protocol is recommended.

For a future water-compressor proposal, a dry-cell calorimetric test must jointly demonstrate swept vapor mass, p–V work, wall heat transfer, total motor input, leak/purge work, clearance losses, and flexure life. Empty-cycle mechanical losses must fit the derived total parasitic budget. A wet integrated condenser additionally needs measured retained liquid mass and its flash penalty. Passing such tests would establish an engineered compressor result; it would not establish originality over the cited diaphragm/peristaltic/liquid-piston art.

The 2025 non-eutectic NaI–EC study also reports that simple lever-rule solid fractions can misestimate cooling capacity. Any quantitative successor should directly integrate calorimetry rather than silently treat the original ideal phase diagram as exact. [Primary study, Energy Conversion and Management (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enconman.2025.120324).
