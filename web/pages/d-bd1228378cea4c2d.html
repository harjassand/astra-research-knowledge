The proof also gives the relative C^r density needed for finite-stage smooth

There are three material scope limits:

1. The valid surjectivity is onto the TWO TRACELESS ENTRIES on any real

I. EXACT RELATIVE STATEMENT RECONSTRUCTED

Let g0 be the unit round metric on the connected sphere S^2 and
mu = dvol(g0)/(4*pi). For w in a convex C^2-open neighborhood U of zero
in C^infinity(S^2), set

  rho(w) = exp(2w)/integral exp(2w) dmu,
  h0(w) = rho(w) g0,
  h(w) = Phi(w)^* h0(w),

where Phi is the deterministic flow constructed in II. Then h(w) has the
round area form pointwise and is isometric to h0(w).

Fix a finite spectral position p >= 1, an integer r >= 0, and epsilon > 0.
Let w0:[0,1] -> U be smooth, and suppose h0(w0(0)) and h0(w0(1)) are
simple through p, meaning

  lambda_1 < lambda_2 < ... < lambda_p < lambda_(p+1).

There is a smooth w_eps:[0,1] -> U which is simple through p at every
time, equals w0 on whole neighborhoods of both endpoints, and satisfies

  ||w_eps-w0||_(C^r([0,1] x S^2)) < epsilon,
  ||h(w_eps)-h(w0)||_(C^r([0,1] x S^2)) < epsilon.

The same conclusion holds for finitely many prescribed C^r seminorm bounds
at once, or as sequential density in the full smooth topology. The endpoint
jets of every order are identical. If w0 is constant near its endpoints,
w_eps is also constant there.

More generally, one can fix a neighborhood of any compact set K of times
on which w0 is already simple through p. Continuity and compactness give
a uniformly simple neighborhood of K; choose the perturbation cutoff to
vanish there. For a finite set of intentional crossing segments, each can
be fixed exactly, provided its endpoints have simple collars and the frozen
segment already has precisely the permitted coincidences. Apply the proof
on the complementary intervals; no claim of simplicity is made at the
permitted crossings. This preserves their entire crossing germs and jets.

The parity version requires w0 and all perturbation directions to be even
under x -> -x. It provides the analogous conclusion separately in each
parity list, for any fixed finite pair of cutoffs, with no prohibition of
cross-parity equality. These qualifications are part of the theorem.

II. AREA RESTORATION AND PARAMETRIC REGULARITY

Since integral rho(w) dmu = 1, the interpolation

  rho_tau,w = 1 + tau (rho(w)-1),  0 <= tau <= 1,

is strictly positive and has constant integral. Let phi_w be the unique
mean-zero solution of

  Delta_0 phi_w = -(rho(w)-1),

using the nonpositive Laplacian convention. Define

  X_tau,w = rho_tau,w^(-1) grad_0 phi_w.

Then div_0(rho_tau,w X_tau,w) = -(rho(w)-1) = -d_tau rho_tau,w.
The complete flow on the compact sphere, starting at the identity, satisfies

  d_tau Phi_tau,w^*(rho_tau,w dmu) = 0.

Consequently Phi_1,w^*(rho(w) dmu) = dmu. Set Phi(w)=Phi_1,w.
Because the area form of rho g0 in dimension two is rho dvol(g0),
this proves dvol(h(w)) = dvol(g0) exactly, not approximately.

The inverse of Delta_0 on mean-zero functions is a continuous linear
elliptic inverse. For every smooth finite-parameter family w(t,z,x),
phi, X, their time-one flow, and h(w(t,z)) depend smoothly on (t,z,x).
This is sufficient for every smooth norm used in a finite-stage construction.
There is no required infinite-dimensional differentiability theorem here.

The gauge depends on the CURRENT w, not on the history of its spectral-time
path. Thus equal w values give equal metrics, and a closed/periodic path
stays closed/periodic. A perturbation identically zero on a time collar
leaves the gauged metric identically unchanged there, hence leaves all jets.

If w is even, rho and phi are even; the gradient field is equivariant under
the antipodal isometry of g0. The flow commutes with that isometry and both
metric descriptions preserve the same even/odd spaces.

For completeness, the source's C^2-only uniform closeness estimate works.
If sigma=||w||_(C^2) is small, normalization gives rho-1 small in C^1 and
C^(0,alpha), for a fixed alpha in (0,1). The fixed-sphere Schauder estimate
gives ||phi||_(C^(2,alpha)) <= C sigma. Therefore ||X_tau,w||_(C^1)
<= C sigma uniformly in tau, using positivity of rho_tau,w. The variational
equation for the flow bounds D Phi and its inverse by exp(C sigma).
Multiplying Phi^* g0 by rho composed with Phi gives the claimed tensor
closeness, without an unstated high-derivative bound on arbitrary w in U.

III. SPLITTING FORMULA AND PRODUCT INDEPENDENCE

In dimension two, the probability-normalized Dirichlet form is

  q(f,g) = integral <grad_0 f, grad_0 g> dmu,

independent of w. The mass form is m_w(f,g)=integral fg rho(w) dmu.
For a conformal direction xi, put f_xi=xi-integral xi rho(w) dmu.
Then d_rho = 2 rho f_xi. For a repeated positive eigenvalue lambda and
an m_w-orthonormal real basis u_i, first differentiation gives

  d_M_ij = -2 lambda integral u_i u_j f_xi rho dmu.

Equivalently, L(w)=-Delta_h0(w)=rho^(-1)(-Delta_0), so
d_L=-2 f_xi L. Compressing to the eigenspace gives the same formula.
The mean term is scalar and drops from the traceless entries.

For an orthonormal pair u,v of that SAME eigenvalue, define

  U1=u^2-v^2,  U2=2uv.

Both have mean zero for rho dmu. The derivative of the two coordinates
(M11-M22, 2M12) is

  -2 lambda (integral U1 xi rho dmu, integral U2 xi rho dmu).

Suppose a U1+b U2 is identically zero with (a,b) nonzero. Its expression
as a quadratic form in (u,v) has determinant -(a^2+b^2), so its zero set
is exactly two distinct lines through the origin. Choose a connected open
set where (u,v) is nonzero. Its image must lie in one line because the
two lines intersect only at zero. One fixed nonzero linear combination
alpha u+beta v thus vanishes on this open set. It solves the same scalar
eigenvalue equation, so unique continuation on the connected sphere makes
it vanish everywhere. That contradicts orthonormality.

Thus U1,U2 are linearly independent. Their two-by-two Gram matrix in
L^2(rho dmu) is positive definite. Taking xi=U1 and xi=U2 proves that
the displayed derivative is onto R^2. Every such smooth direction can
be used with sufficiently small amplitude inside the C^2-open set U.
No quantitative lower bound on this Gram determinant uniform in lambda
or p is claimed or required.

If u,v have the same parity, U1,U2 are both even, so the same proof works
within even conformal directions. If the parities differ, U2 is odd and
all its integrals against even xi vanish. The restricted derivative then
has at most one independent traceless entry: global-spectrum noncrossing
in the even family would be an invalid extension of this lemma.

IV. GAUGE DOES NOT ALTER THE SPLITTING MATRIX

The pullback T_w=Phi(w)^* is a unitary map from L^2(rho(w) dmu) onto
L^2(dmu), because of the exact area identity. The fixed-space operator is

  A(w)=T_w L(w) T_w^(-1).

At a fixed w, differentiating gives the transported variation plus a
commutator [K,A], where K=(d_T)T^(-1), on smooth functions. If P projects
onto a lambda-eigenspace, then

  P[K,A]P = lambda PKP-lambda PKP = 0.

Hence its compression is precisely the original splitting matrix in the
transported orthonormal basis. K is a first-order differential operator
on these smooth eigenfunctions, so all displayed matrix elements are
defined; no bounded-operator claim about K on all of L^2 is needed.

In a smoothly moving orthonormal cluster frame, the derivative of the
frame contributes zero at a scalar matrix lambda I: differentiating
orthonormality cancels the lambda terms. This justifies using these
variations for the smooth local cluster matrix.

V. FINITE-CUTOFF AVOIDANCE, INCLUDING HIGHER MULTIPLICITY

1. Compact collection of relevant repeated pairs.

Along the compact smooth preliminary path, the densities and their
reciprocals are uniformly bounded, as are their derivatives of every fixed
order. Min-max comparison bounds lambda_(p+1) above and lambda_2 away
from zero. Choose Lambda larger than the uniform cutoff bound. Every
entire repeated cluster meeting the cutoff has eigenvalue below Lambda;
the comparison with round eigenvalues also bounds the total number of
eigenvalues below Lambda uniformly.

Consider (t,lambda,u,v), where 0<lambda<=Lambda, u,v are real and
mass-orthonormal and both solve

  -Delta_0 u=lambda rho(w0(t)) u.

Uniform elliptic estimates and Sobolev embedding give uniform bounds
for every spatial derivative. Diagonal subsequence extraction makes this
set compact in the smooth topology. The uniform first-positive-eigenvalue
bound excludes a limiting lambda=0; orthonormality survives the limit.

2. One finite direction pool suffices.

For every such pair, III supplies two directions with a nonzero derivative
determinant. This condition is open in the smooth topology of the pair.
A finite cover of the compact set supplies finitely many fixed directions
xi_1,...,xi_D which have pair rank two everywhere along the original path.

This rank remains valid for relevant repeated pairs in all sufficiently
small finite-pool perturbations of the path. Otherwise a failing sequence
t_n,z_n -> t_*,0 and associated eigenfunction pairs has a smooth limiting
pair on the original path, by the same elliptic compactness. The original
nonzero determinant remains nonzero near its limit, contradiction.

The pool need NOT prescribe all clusters simultaneously. Every bad chart
only requires its own two independent parameter coordinates.

3. Endpoints and relative collars.

Simple endpoints have simple collars with a uniform positive minimum gap.
Choose a smooth chi which is zero on smaller endpoint collars and one
outside the larger simple collars; put all transitions inside the simple
collars. Small perturbations preserve simplicity throughout the transitions.
Use the finite family

  w(t,z)=w0(t)+chi(t) sum_j z_j xi_j.

It stays in U for sufficiently small z, since the preliminary path is
compact in the open set U. At every possible bad point, chi=1. This
same construction fixes extra prescribed simple compact sets or permitted
crossing segments as described in I.

4. Smooth cluster charts.

In the fixed-area gauge, A(t,z):H^2(S^2)->L^2(dmu) is smooth with a
common domain. Around a bad point theta_*=(t_*,z_*), choose a contour
enclosing one ENTIRE repeated eigenvalue cluster, with strict gaps outside.
The resolvent at theta_* maps L^2 to H^2 by elliptic regularity. For nearby
theta the Neumann-series inverse is smooth, so its contour integral gives
a smooth finite-rank spectral projection. Projecting a smooth basis and
orthonormalizing gives a smooth real frame and symmetric cluster matrix
M(theta). Spatial smoothness follows by elliptic regularity.

Suppose the entire repeated block has indices r,...,s and multiplicity
m=s-r+1>=2. At its center M=lambda I. Choose its first two frame vectors
to be any orthonormal pair, and define

  F(theta)=(M11-M22, 2M12).

The derivative D_z F has rank two at the center by the finite pool.
After shrinking the chart it remains rank two. Its zero set is a smooth
codimension-two submanifold of (time,parameter) space.

5. Correct containment and countable covering.

Whenever the SAME ENTIRE block r,...,s remains equal in this chart,
M is scalar and F=0. Partial repetitions after this m-fold cluster splits
are NOT asserted to lie in this chart's zero set. They have separate
charts centered at those points.

For each fixed r,s take the stratum where r,...,s is the entire repeated
block and the exterior gaps are strict. The stratum is second countable
as a subspace of [0,1] x R^D, so the centered chart cover has a countable
subcover. Within any such chart, the stratum is contained in that chart's
F=0 set. Union over r,s therefore covers EVERY bad point by countably
many codimension-two submanifolds. This avoids the erroneous claim that
two equations describe all partial repetitions of a large cluster.

6. Why almost every arbitrarily small z avoids all bad points.

Since rank is in the z coordinates, each local zero set is a smooth graph
for two z coordinates over t and the other D-2 coordinates. Its projection
to R^D is locally a Lipschitz image of a (D-1)-dimensional domain, hence
has D-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. Countable coordinate charts
and the preceding countable stratum covers keep this union null.
Every ball about z=0 therefore contains parameters whose entire path
has no bad point. This proves finite-cutoff avoidance.

The identical argument with no time variable gives endpoint density:
the zero sets now have dimension D-2 and their union is null in R^D.
For the parity version, form a common finite pool from SAME-PARITY pairs
in both lists. All directions are even. The two lists' countable bad
unions remain null; cross-parity blocks are intentionally not included.

VI. C^r AND SMOOTH DENSITY ARE ACTUALLY CONSEQUENCES

The source says the coefficients z can be arbitrarily small. For the
FIXED finite direction pool and cutoff, the exact estimate is

  ||w(t,z)-w0(t)||_(C^r([0,1] x S^2)) <= C_r |z|,

with finite C_r depending on this path, chi, and the selected directions.
The gauged metrics are smooth in (t,z,x) on a small compact parameter
ball. The mean-value theorem in z, with all mixed time/spatial derivatives
up to order r, similarly gives

  ||h(w(t,z))-h(w0(t))||_(C^r) <= C'_r |z|.

Choose a nonbad z with |z| less than epsilon divided by both constants.
This proves the asserted relative C^r strengthening. A sequence of nonbad
z_n -> 0 gives convergence in every fixed derivative order, hence in the
smooth topology. The constants can grow arbitrarily with the cutoff;
the proof supplies existence, not a cutoff-uniform effective algorithm.

VI.a. SEPARATE INFINITE-DIMENSIONAL BAIRE CONSEQUENCE

There is a precise additional consequence, but it is not part of the
finite-p matrix argument itself. The space C^infinity(S^2) is Frechet;
U is an open Baire subspace. For each p the set of parameters simple
through p is open and dense by the finite argument. Their intersection
is therefore residual and dense: fully simple metrics exist in U.

For paths, prescribe endpoint jets of finite or infinite order, including
FULLY SIMPLE endpoint values, and assume the resulting path class is
nonempty. The jet equalities define a closed affine subspace of
C^infinity([0,1] x S^2). Restricting to paths with image in U is open
because the time interval is compact. This is a Baire space. In this
space, paths simple through p at every time form an open set, since
the finitely many spectral gaps have a positive minimum on the compact
interval. They form a dense set by the relative construction, whose
endpoint cutoff vanishes on whole collars and therefore preserves all
prescribed jets. The countable intersection over p is residual and dense.
It consists of paths with fully simple spectrum at EVERY time.

The finite-p safe collar may depend on p. FULLY SIMPLE endpoint values
alone do not imply that an arbitrarily prescribed FIXED endpoint germ is
fully simple on some uniform-width collar: higher-index degeneracies of
that germ may accumulate toward the endpoint. If a fixed endpoint
germ/collar is imposed instead of jets, its complete admissibility must
be assumed; constant fully simple endpoint collars suffice.

This Baire consequence supplies no all-index positive gap, no uniform
derivative/direction bound, no constructive algorithm, and no prescribed
crossing program. A space with intentional double crossings needs its
own compatible relative class and openness/density checks; the finite
protected-crossing version in I is available once those data are supplied.

VII. COUNTEREXAMPLE/SCOPE ATTACKS AND THEIR OUTCOME

A. Full-matrix derivative control after area normalization: FALSE.
For a round degree-l eigenspace of dimension m=2l+1, an orthonormal
real basis obeys sum_i u_i^2=m by the addition theorem. Therefore

  tr(d_M)=-2 lambda integral m (xi-mean xi) dmu=0.

For l=1 one can see this without representation theory: take
u_i=sqrt(3) x_i on S^2, so sum_i u_i^2=3 identically. Thus the derivative
is never onto all of Sym_m at such a normalized metric. This does not
refute Lemma 2.1, which asks only for traceless pair control.

B. Even-only avoidance of global-spectrum crossings: UNSUPPORTED/FALSE
as a general inference from these lemmas. The off-diagonal derivative
between opposite parities vanishes. A sign change of an even-versus-odd
eigenvalue difference along a path forces an equality by continuity.
The source excludes this claim explicitly.

C. Arbitrary fixed nonsimple endpoints: FALSE. For p>=2 the round
sphere fails the source's simplicity convention. A path ending at that
fixed metric cannot be simple through p at every time. The relative
theorem in I includes the required endpoint compatibility.

D. Connectedness cannot be dropped. On a disconnected union, choose
same-eigenvalue u and v supported on different components; uv=0, and
the two traceless functions have rank at most one. Source S^2 is connected.

E. Higher multiplicities and changing cluster sizes: source proof PASSES.
The exact-block countable covering in V is the necessary repair. It does
not confuse scalar whole-cluster conditions with partial repetitions.

F. Infinite parameter domains / growing cutoff uniformity: NOT SUPPLIED.
The argument projects codimension-two sets from a one-time-parameter
family. A two-dimensional path domain would give projected dimension D,
so the same null-set argument would not establish avoidance. Infinite
cutoff claims require an additional countable/Baire or diagonal argument,
and quantitative speed/gap bounds must be checked separately.
