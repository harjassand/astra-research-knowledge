# A11 — Observable-specific Poisson certificates across a slow mode

Date: 2026-10-09  
Status: internally derived theorem and exact finite-state illustration; finite-tested algebraic checks; novelty, external proof review, and computational advantage unresolved. This is not a validated breakthrough claim.

## Target and information contract

Target: certify stationary expectations and finite-run time averages for a physically meaningful class of multiscale Markov dynamics even when the full chain has arbitrarily slow mixing.

The proposed method requires the exact generator decomposition $L_\epsilon=L_0+\epsilon L_1$, an observable $f$, and a candidate bounded corrector $u$. The unknown invariant law is not an input. The decomposition, local transition rules, and their numerical representation are supplied model information; if discovered from code or data, that acquisition cost must be included. For a simulated path, the full state is assumed observable to the simulator. A corrector proposed from a trajectory is only a candidate until its residual and bounds are checked over the complete relevant state space or by a sound local verification rule.

The certificate is target-specific. It can be informative when slow modes change the invariant law greatly but barely change the target average. It reports a wide interval or no certificate when those modes change the target value. It does not infer hidden rates or a mixing envelope from one path.

## Theorem: slow-fast generator certificate

Let $X_t$ be a nonexplosive Markov process with extended generator $L_\epsilon=L_0+\epsilon L_1$, where $\epsilon\ge0$. Assume $u$ is in the extended-generator domain, $u$ is bounded, and the Dynkin martingale for $u(X_t)$ is valid. For constants $c\in\mathbb R$ and $\delta,s\ge0$, suppose the following are verified pointwise:

\[
r(x):=f(x)-c+L_0u(x),\qquad \|r\|_\infty\le\delta,
\qquad \|L_1u\|_\infty\le s.
\tag{1}
\]

Then every invariant probability measure $\pi$ in the generator domain satisfies

\[
|\pi f-c|=|\pi r+\epsilon\pi L_1u|\le\delta+\epsilon s.
\tag{2}
\]

For any initial distribution, define the path mean $\bar f_T=T^{-1}\int_0^T f(X_t)\,dt$. Suppose further that the Dynkin martingale has jumps bounded by $b$ and predictable quadratic variation density bounded by $V$:

\[
|\Delta u|\le b,\qquad \frac{d\langle M^u\rangle_t}{dt}\le V.
\tag{3}
\]

For every $0<\alpha<1$, with probability at least $1-\alpha$,

\[
|\bar f_T-c|\le\delta+\epsilon s+
\frac{\operatorname{osc}(u)}{T}+
\frac{\sqrt{2VT\log(2/\alpha)}+(b/3)\log(2/\alpha)}{T}.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus every invariant mean is in $c\pm(\delta+\epsilon s)$, and a single finite trajectory from an arbitrary initial state is within the larger radius in (4) with the stated probability. For deterministic ODEs $\dot x=F_0(x)+\epsilon F_1(x)$, take $L_i=F_i\cdot\nabla$; the martingale terms vanish and (4) reduces to the exact pathwise bound

\[
|T^{-1}\!\int_0^T f(x_t)dt-c|\le\delta+\epsilon s+\operatorname{osc}(u)/T.
\]

### Proof

Invariance gives $\pi L_\epsilon u=0$. From (1), $f-c=r-L_0u=r-L_\epsilon u+\epsilon L_1u$. Integrating against any invariant $\pi$ proves (2); uniqueness, ergodicity, and a spectral gap are not used.

Dynkin's formula gives $M_T=u(X_T)-u(X_0)-\int_0^T L_\epsilon u(X_t)dt$. Therefore

\[
\int_0^T(f(X_t)-c)dt=
\int_0^T(r+\epsilon L_1u)(X_t)dt-u(X_T)+u(X_0)+M_T.
\]

The first integral is at most $T(\delta+\epsilon s)$ in absolute value, and the endpoint term is at most $\operatorname{osc}(u)$. A Freedman inequality for a compensated jump martingale with jumps bounded by $b$ and predictable quadratic variation at most $VT$ gives

\[
\Pr\{|M_T|>\sqrt{2VT x}+(b/3)x\}\le2e^{-x}.
\]

Set $x=\log(2/\alpha)$ and divide by $T$, proving (4). For an ODE, $u(x_T)-u(x_0)=\int_0^T L_\epsilon u(x_t)dt$ exactly, so no concentration term is needed. $\square$

For a finite-state CTMC with rates $q_{xy}$, the verification quantities are explicit:

\[
L_i u(x)=\sum_y q^{(i)}_{xy}(u(y)-u(x)),\quad
\Gamma_{L_\epsilon}(u)(x)=\sum_y q^\epsilon_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^2,
\]

and one may take $V=\max_x\Gamma_{L_\epsilon}(u)(x)$, $b=\max_{q^\epsilon_{xy}>0}|u(y)-u(x)|$. For a transition-reward observable with reward $g(x,y)$ on each jump, replace $f$ by its compensator $h(x)=\sum_yq_{xy}g(x,y)$ in (1)–(2). In (4), the martingale jump is $g(x,y)+u(y)-u(x)$, so the exact bracket is bounded by $\max_x\sum_yq_{xy}[g(x,y)+u(y)-u(x)]^2$. This covers reaction counts and transport currents when their jump rewards and compensators are part of the supplied model.

## Local finite-range construction and its exact scope

Let a finite graph have $N$ sites and uniformly bounded degree. At site $i$, let $z_i$ be a slow state and $y_i$ a fast state. Assume:

1. $L_0=\sum_i K_{\alpha_i(z)}$ changes only $y_i$; the local environment $\alpha_i(z)$ is determined by a fixed-radius neighborhood of $z$.
2. $L_1$ has total slow-event exit rate at most $q$ per site (with local rates specified by fixed-radius rules), each slow event changes only a bounded number of slow coordinates, and one slow event changes at most $m$ local environments.
3. The local observable is $f_N=N^{-1}\sum_i g_{\alpha_i(z)}(y_i)$. A number $c$ and local functions $v_\alpha$ are found and checked on every local environment and fast state so that

\[
|g_\alpha(y)-c+K_\alpha v_\alpha(y)|\le\delta.
\tag{5}
\]

Set $u_N=N^{-1}\sum_i v_{\alpha_i(z)}(y_i)$ and $R_v=\max_{\alpha,y}v_\alpha(y)-\min_{\alpha,y}v_\alpha(y)$. Then the global residual in (1) is at most $\delta$, and

\[
\operatorname{osc}(u_N)\le R_v,\qquad
\|L_1u_N\|_\infty\le q m R_v.
\tag{6}
\]

If fast exit rates per site are bounded by $\kappa$, one has the conservative carré-du-champ estimate

\[
\Gamma_{L_\epsilon}(u_N)\le
\frac{\kappa R_v^2+\epsilon q m^2R_v^2}{N},\qquad
\max|\Delta u_N|\le\frac{\max(1,m)R_v}{N}.
\tag{7}
\]

Consequently every stationary mean of the intensive observable lies in

\[
\pi f_N\in c\pm(\delta+\epsilon q mR_v),
\tag{8}
\]

and the finite-trajectory bound follows by substituting (6)–(7) into (4). The certificate avoids enumerating the $N$-site state space: the exact residual check enumerates only all local environment/fast-state pairs, whose count is exponential in the stencil radius and local alphabet sizes but independent of $N$. The local rule tables, graph degree, $q,\kappa,m$, all arithmetic precision, and simulator event count remain charged. The global state space is exponential in $N$; a dense invariant-vector computation is therefore not a fair proxy for an equally informed local solver.

The local table is not an assumed exact closure: a candidate is accepted only after all local residual inequalities (5) and all rate bounds are verified. If $c,v_\alpha$ are selected using trajectory data, that adaptivity does not compromise the result because acceptance uses a complete deterministic residual check. If the rate table is estimated rather than supplied, uniform statistical error bounds and their acquisition cost are additional obligations not solved here.

## Exact four-state stress test

Use a slow label $z\in\{0,1\}$ and fast occupancy $y\in\{0,1\}$. With $p=1/2$, the $L_0$ rates flip $y$ in sector $z=0$ at rate $1/2$ in either direction and in sector $z=1$ at rate $1$ in either direction. Let $L_1$ flip $z=0\to1$ at rate $1+y$, and $z=1\to0$ at rate $2-y$, preserving $y$. The full chain is irreducible for every $\epsilon>0$, while its exact characteristic polynomial is

\[
\det(\lambda I-Q_\epsilon)=
\frac12\lambda(\lambda+3\epsilon)
\bigl(2\lambda^2+6(1+\epsilon)\lambda+4+9\epsilon\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]

For $0<\epsilon\le 1/100$, its slow eigenvalue is $-3\epsilon$; the other nonzero eigenvalues remain bounded away from zero. Thus its spectral relaxation time diverges as $1/(3\epsilon)$. This eigenvalue statement does not assert a total-variation mixing prefactor.

For $f(z,y)=y$, take $c=1/2$ and $u(z,y)=y/\kappa_z$, with $\kappa_0=1,\kappa_1=2$. Direct calculation gives

\[
L_0u=-(y-1/2),\quad r=0,\quad
L_1u=(0,-1,0,1/2),
\]

in state order $(00,01,10,11)$. Thus $s=1$, $\operatorname{osc}(u)=1$, $b=1$, and $V=1/2+\epsilon/2$. Every stationary mean satisfies $|\pi y-1/2|\le\epsilon$. Solving the four-state stationary equations exactly gives

\[
\pi y=\frac{2(2\epsilon+1)}{9\epsilon+4},\qquad
\left|\pi y-\frac12\right|=\frac{\epsilon}{2(9\epsilon+4)}\le\frac{\epsilon}{8}.
\tag{10}
\]

For $N$ independent copies, use $f_N=N^{-1}\sum_i y_i$ and $u_N=N^{-1}\sum_i y_i/\kappa_{z_i}$. Then $r=0$, $\|L_1u_N\|_\infty\le1$, $V=(1/2+\epsilon/2)/N$, jump bound $b=1/N$, and endpoint bound $1/T$. Therefore

\[
\Pr\!\left\{\left|\bar f_{N,T}-\frac12\right|\le
\epsilon+\frac1T+
\frac{\sqrt{2(1/2+\epsilon/2)T\log(2/\alpha)/N}
+\log(2/\alpha)/(3N)}{T}\right\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{11}
\]

For $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, $N=1000$, $T=10^4$, and $\alpha=.05$, the right-hand radius in (11) is about $7.07\times10^{-4}$, while the slow eigenvalue is $-3\times10^{-8}$. This is an analytical bound, not a simulated empirical result. At these parameters a Gillespie run would require on the order of $10^7$ local events over the stated horizon; that event count is an estimate from the total rates, not a measured runtime. The one-site executable check below uses exact rational arithmetic and does not generate a trajectory.

## Falsifiers and failure modes

* If slow sectors have different fast invariant means, no common $c$ with small fast residual need exist. Example: $L_0=0$ on two states and $f(z)=z$ gives $\inf_c\|f-c\|_\infty=1/2$; arbitrary slow rates can choose stationary means near either endpoint. The certificate correctly refuses a narrow mean.
* The hidden-metastability obstruction in the pinned Astra record N185 applies when topology/count are unrestricted and the generator/mixing are not supplied. It rules out uniformly honest substantial-rate certification from one finite stationary trajectory on that extension-closed class. This certificate escapes that contract only because it requires the exact generator decomposition and globally verified rates/corrector.
* The corrector can have large range or residual, and the slow term $\epsilon\|L_1u\|_\infty$ can be large. A small global spectral gap alone is not a failure, but a target coupled to the slow mode is.
* Finite trajectory samples cannot verify a pointwise generator residual over an unobserved state space. Continuous-state interval/SOS verification, local rule enumeration, or another sound global verifier must be supplied and fully charged. For a general dense state grid the cost can be exponential in dimension.
* A wrong generator, wrong decomposition, hidden state, or unbounded rate invalidates the certificate unless a separate model discrepancy term and a valid observation-state interface are proved.

## Resource and baseline accounting

For a finite model with $n$ states and $m_Q$ directed nonzero rates, a dense candidate search has $n+1$ real variables $(u,c)$, $2n$ residual inequalities, and $2n$ slow-term inequalities for an LP minimizing $\delta+\epsilon s$. Exact rational LP bit cost is not included in an abstract polynomial claim; it depends on coefficient bit lengths and solver pivots. Direct verification costs $O(m_Q)$ generator operations and outward-rounded arithmetic at the chosen precision. Computing $V$ costs another $O(m_Q)$. Simulating $T$ costs the actual number of CTMC jumps / simulator calls; the pathwise theorem charges the resulting $T,b,V,\alpha$.

For the finite-range construction, candidate search and complete verification cost $O(A_Y A_Z^{B_r})$ table entries for local alphabet sizes $A_Y,A_Z$, stencil size $B_r$, plus the LP cost over those entries. Evaluation and simulation are $O(N)$ per global state/event sweep; the bound (11) improves as $1/\sqrt{NT}$ for the independent-copy example. No performance advantage over an equally informed solver exploiting the same product/local structure has been established. On the four-state example, exact full stationary solve is already trivial and is an equally informed baseline.

Ulam/validated transfer-operator methods are the more general deterministic-dynamics comparator. Galatolo, Monge, Nisoli, and Poloni's 2023 framework constructs finite-element/Ulam reductions, validates invariant-density error, and uses a coarse-fine certificate to infer convergence-to-equilibrium bounds from a finite reduced matrix; their practical checks include interval transition matrices, a finite-dimensional fixed point, and a power $m$ with a validated contraction constant $C_m<1$. That can certify broader invariant-density information, but needs the relevant regularization/Lasota–Yorke setting and computes a global convergence certificate. The present candidate instead certifies one observable's expectation and trajectory mean from a verified corrector, with cost controlled by the local witness and no global mixing estimate. It is not a replacement for Ulam where a density, multiple observables, or general mixing statistics are needed.

Goluskin's 2016/2017 Lorenz work uses auxiliary functions and SOS optimization to bound infinite-time averages for every solution, including chaotic trajectories, with interval arithmetic around an SDP. That is a close deterministic precedent for the $F\cdot\nabla u$ form of the certificate. The stochastic generator perturbation and finite-trajectory Freedman term here are ordinary generator/martingale steps, not established novelty. Classical Poisson-equation martingale representations and concentration are also prior art: Glynn–Meyn (1996) explicitly connects Poisson solutions to additive-functional martingales and proves Lyapunov conditions for solution bounds. Fuh–Zhang (2000) prove tail/moment inequalities using Poisson-equation martingales without uniform ergodicity. Consequently this report makes no novelty or priority claim for the core decomposition.

Standard Monte Carlo on genuinely independent stationary draws is a valid comparator and needs no mixing-time estimate; it requires a source of those stationary independent starts, whose cost is not free. For one correlated trajectory, textbook effective-sample-size/CLT intervals need additional dependence assumptions. The candidate's certified interval is not shown to dominate IID Monte Carlo or an equally informed local Poisson solver. The main unresolved computational gate is a prospective study on a genuinely interacting local model where an automatically acquired local corrector passes complete verification and yields a useful interval at materially lower total cost than (i) full validated transfer/Ulam, (ii) matched local solver, and (iii) equally informed independent-start / standard-MC baselines.
