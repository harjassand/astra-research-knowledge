THE OTHER RECTANGULAR BRANCH
M_i^T M_j=M_j^T M_i and symmetry imply L T4=L=T4 L. Real U,V give
S T10=S=T6 S. Hence the original formulas reduce to A=S,
R=Ad_K S and B=R^dagger=S^dagger Ad_K. Since K=K^T=K^-1,

(ABA)^dagger Ad_K = S^dagger C S S^dagger C = BAB.

The EB cone is closed under adjoints: a separable Choi matrix gives a

separable if J(F) is. Unitary CP precomposition also preserves EB. The
certificate for ABA therefore proves BAB EB without a second decomposition.

ORIGINATOR VERIFIER'S POSITIVITY ALGORITHM
The det routine is fraction-free Bareiss elimination, with row swaps and a
recorded determinant sign. It starts previous=1, performs exact divisions
by the preceding pivot, and asserts each remainder is zero. For integer
inputs this is a correct determinant recurrence. The function returns 0
when a pivot column has no available nonzero row, as required for singularity.
Sylvester's criterion says a real symmetric matrix is strictly positive
definite iff all its leading principal minors are positive. The verifier
checks symmetry and all six leading principal minors for each output matrix
and each shifted margin matrix. Thus its mathematical positivity inference
is sound. My rational LDL check does not reuse that determinant routine.

ROBUSTNESS: ALL COMPLEX HERMITIAN PERTURBATIONS
The first ten input vectors are exactly the coordinate e_i, and all their
output factors exceed I6/100000. It follows constructively that
H - mu I60 is separable, mu=1/100000: subtract mu I6 from each of these
first ten output factors and retain every remaining product term.

For a d-dimensional factor, use the real vector-space basis of Hermitian
matrices P_i=E_ii, F^R_ij=E_ij+E_ji, F^I_ij=i(E_ij-E_ji), i<j. Assign
D(P_i)=P_i and D(F^R_ij)=D(F^I_ij)=P_i+P_j. Every D is diagonal PSD,
D+F and D-F are PSD, and sum_alpha D_alpha=(2d-1)I_d.
The analogous e-dimensional basis has positive diagonal E_beta and G_beta.

Every Hermitian perturbation Delta on d tensor e has a unique real expansion
Delta=sum c_alpha,beta F_alpha tensor G_beta. If epsilon is its maximum
absolute complex entry, every real coefficient satisfies |c|<=epsilon.
Here is the decisive complex off-diagonal case. For i<j and a<b, set
r=Delta_(i,a),(j,b), s=Delta_(i,b),(j,a). The four coefficients are
c_RR=(Re r+Re s)/2, c_II=(Re s-Re r)/2,
c_RI=(Im r-Im s)/2, c_IR=(Im r+Im s)/2.
Each has magnitude at most epsilon. In the one-off-diagonal-factor cases,
coefficients are simply real or imaginary parts of a single entry, and the
diagonal-diagonal case is its real entry. Thus the bound is valid for the
full complex Hermitian space, including imaginary generators.

For each real c, the matrix
|c| D_alpha tensor E_beta + c F_alpha tensor G_beta
is separable, since it equals half the sum of
|c|(D_alpha+sign(c)F_alpha) tensor (E_beta+G_beta), and
|c|(D_alpha-sign(c)F_alpha) tensor (E_beta-G_beta).
The total allocated diagonal matrix
L=sum |c| D_alpha tensor E_beta
satisfies L <= epsilon(2d-1)(2e-1)I. Because L is diagonal,
mu I-L is separable whenever epsilon(2d-1)(2e-1)<=mu, by its nonnegative
coordinate-product expansion. Consequently mu I+Delta is separable.
Adding H-mu I gives the claimed H+Delta separability.

For d=10,e=6, (2d-1)(2e-1)=19*11=209, so the exact sufficient radius is
epsilon<=1/(100000*209)=1/20900000. The closed inequality is allowed;
strict inequality gives an open neighborhood. No general separable-ball
theorem is imported and no real-symmetric-span restriction remains.
