# Cancer resistance ancestry: assay identifiability and a minimal ex-vivo discriminator

**Status:** scoped mathematical result plus literature reconstruction; not a new biological discovery, clinical recommendation, or external validation of the model. The biology and both broad assay ideas below have substantial prior art. The contribution is an explicit observation interface, exact equivalence/lower bounds, and a falsifiable minimum design for a restricted model.

## Question and interface

Can standard longitudinal burden and common-driver measurements distinguish a rare, stable resistant clone already present before therapy from a drug-tolerant population generated or selected during treatment? If not, what smallest added experiment identifies a decision-relevant parameter?

The routine observation here is tumor or culture burden at times $t_1,\ldots,t_m$, plus any common driver measured in all cells. It does not include lineage-resolved sequencing, direct pretreatment single-cell resistance phenotyping, or functional post-washout rechallenge. This is an explicit boundary: a sufficiently sensitive lineage/genotype assay changes the interface.

## Exact on-treatment observational equivalence

Consider two latent-state models under one fixed drug, $u=1$.

- **G, pre-existing stable resistance:** before treatment, a fraction $f_0$ carries a stable resistant alteration. At treatment start the sensitive and resistant counts are $S_0=(1-f_0)N_0$ and $R_0=f_0N_0$.
- **P, drug-associated tolerant state:** before treatment, those cells are sensitive, but treatment instantaneously places fraction $f_0$ in an observable drug-tolerant state $P$, leaving $S_0=(1-f_0)N_0$. This idealized fast transition represents induction or selection completed before the first post-dose assay.

In either model, while drug remains present let
$$
\dot S=-dS,\qquad \dot R=gR,
$$
where $R$ denotes the resistant/tolerant state in the relevant model. Let the assay emit only total burden $Y=S+R$ and a common-driver signal shared by both states. Then
$$
Y(t)=(1-f_0)N_0 e^{-dt}+f_0N_0e^{gt}.
$$

The routine observation distributions are identical if the same measurement-noise kernel is applied to these emissions. Their total variation distance is exactly zero. No test or treatment policy that is a function only of these data can identify the latent origin; with equal priors, any origin classifier has error at least $1/2$. This is a hidden-state non-identifiability result, not a claim that all cancer trajectories are equivalent. A measured resistance genotype, different growth rates, or a lineage marker can break the equivalence.

**Policy consequence inside this model:** on-treatment burden alone cannot determine whether a drug holiday restores sensitivity. The washout predictions below make G retain a stable component and P lose tolerance. Thus the same on-treatment history can imply different ex-vivo follow-up actions.

## Minimal perturbation for a mixed stable/reversible model

At the end of a standardized treatment pulse, split one well-mixed culture or organoid sample randomly into washout arms. Let $a\ge0$ be the stable resistant fraction and $b\ge0$ the reversible persister fraction at washout time zero, with $a+b=f_0\le1$. During drug-free washout, assume:

1. persisters revert to sensitivity at a constant rate $\beta>0$;
2. resistant, persister, and reverted descendants have equal net growth over the measured interval. If rates differ, they must be estimated in separate state/lineage controls and the formula replaced by the corresponding growth-switching model; a bulk vehicle growth curve alone is not enough;
3. no new resistance arises during washout;
4. the assay measures the pre-challenge tolerant fraction, or the fixed rechallenge is short enough not to induce new tolerance during measurement.

The resistant/tolerant fraction after washout duration $\tau$ is
$$
f(\tau)=a+b e^{-\beta\tau}.
$$

For $\Delta>0$, assay matched aliquots at $0,\Delta,2\Delta$, and write $f_j=f(j\Delta)$. If $f_0>f_1>f_2$, then
$$
q=\frac{f_1-f_2}{f_0-f_1}=e^{-\beta\Delta},\qquad
\beta=-\frac{\log q}{\Delta},\qquad
b=\frac{f_0-f_1}{1-q},\qquad a=f_0-b.
$$
For a nonnegative mixture, the observed ratio must satisfy $0<q\le f_1/f_0$; this is the feasibility condition $a\ge0$. When it holds, the three exact fractions identify the stable component, reversible component, and reversion rate in this model. The ratio of successive drops must be constant, giving a direct falsifier. The curve is a standard first-order decay-to-plateau model, and the three-point algebra is elementary; neither is claimed as a novel method. The literature audit found direct cancer prior art for washout/rechallenge and evolutionary-path experiments, but did not establish that no earlier paper has written this exact parameterization.

**Minimality within the model.** With only $f_0,f_1$, for any feasible $q\in(0,f_1/f_0]$, set $\beta=-\log(q)/\Delta$, $b=(f_0-f_1)/(1-q)$, and $a=f_0-b$. These distinct triples produce the same two observations, so the mixed-model parameters are not identifiable from two washout durations. Three durations including time zero are therefore minimal for identifying all three unknowns. If the question is only to distinguish pure endpoints $b=0$ versus $a=0$, one nonzero washout duration suffices noiselessly when $\beta$ is known and positive; that is a weaker decision problem.

For the equally spaced design, the Jacobian of $(f_0,f_1,f_2)$ with respect to $(a,b,\beta)$ has determinant $-b\Delta q(1-q)^2$. The local information matrix therefore has full rank when $b>0$, $\beta>0$, and $\Delta>0$. At the pure-stable boundary $b=0$, $\beta$ cannot be estimated. As $q\to1$ (washout too short) or $q\to0$ (the first interval already reaches the plateau), the determinant tends to zero and estimation becomes ill-conditioned. A pilot should place $q=e^{-\beta\Delta}$ away from 0 and 1; $q\approx e^{-1}$ is a reasonable illustrative design point, not a proven optimum.

**Experimental implementation.** Use one shared starting culture and randomize matched aliquots to no washout (0), one washout duration $\Delta$, and a second duration $2\Delta$; run a common short rechallenge or pre-challenge single-cell state readout at each endpoint. If the time-zero phenotypic fraction is already measured, the added design is two washout arms. Include state/lineage-specific off-drug growth controls and reference cultures with known stable resistance and known reversible persister behavior. Costs are one split culture per added duration, drug-free expansion for up to $2\Delta$ (or matched cell doublings), the same cell-count/state assay at each endpoint, and independent biological replicates. Monetary cost is lab-specific and not imputed.

**Illustrative predictions, not fitted biology.** For $a=0.02$, $b=0.03$, $\beta=0.1$ per day, and $\Delta=10$ days, the tolerant fractions are 0.0500, 0.0310, and 0.0241 at 0, 10, and 20 days. A pure stable model predicts 0.0500 at all three times; a pure reversible model with the same initial fraction and rate predicts 0.0500, 0.0184, and 0.0068. In the mixed example, the consecutive drops have ratio $q=e^{-1}\approx0.368$. These values specify a prospective assay target, not a clinical dosing schedule.

**Sampling noise and assay cost.** If $k_i$ of $n_i$ independently sampled cells are called tolerant at time $i$, then $k_i\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(n_i,p_i)$ with $p_i=f_i$ for a perfect assay. Use a constrained binomial likelihood for $(a,b,\beta)$, not the raw ratio estimator: finite counts can make the estimated ratio negative or greater than one. In the illustrative example, 1,000 independent cells per timepoint give binomial standard errors for $(f_0,f_1,f_2)$ of about $(0.0069,0.0055,0.0048)$. The smaller second drop $f_1-f_2\approx0.0070$ is only about one standard error at that cell count. Roughly 9,900 independent cells per endpoint would be needed for a nominal 3-standard-error separation of those two fractions under the ideal binomial model. Culture-level variability makes this optimistic; independent biological cultures, not just many cells from one well, are required to estimate overdispersion.

If sensitivity $s$ and false-positive rate $c$ are known and stable across washout arms, observed positive probability is $p_i=c+(s-c)f_i$; the latent fractions can be corrected when $s>c$. Unknown assay sensitivity is confounded with the levels $a,b$, and state-dependent sensitivity can invalidate the exponential form, so include known stable-resistant and reversible-persister controls. The assay cost includes calibration material and replicate cultures as well as single-cell counts.

## The washout curve does not identify rebound risk

The three-point design identifies $(a,b,\beta)$ only for its washout observation model. It does **not** identify the chance of a *new stable resistant founder from persisters* after drug is restarted. Add an on-drug persister-to-stable-resistance event rate $\mu$, which is inactive during drug-free washout. For persister count trajectory $P(t)$ during a fixed rechallenge horizon $T$, assume independent rare conversion events with intensity $\mu P(t)$. Then
$$
R_T=1-\exp\left(-\mu\int_0^T P(t)\,dt\right).
$$

The complete three-point washout data are unchanged for every $\mu$, but for any positive cell-time exposure $C=\int_0^T P(t)\,dt$, choosing $\mu=-\log(1-r)/C$ gives any desired probability $r\in(0,1)$ of at least one new stable founder. This remains a counterexample when $a>0$: the same pre-existing clone is present in both models, while the added persister-derived founders can change whether a specified resistant-burden threshold is crossed. Total threshold risk also depends on stable-clone fitness, absolute persister count, resistant growth rate, and the threshold definition. The protocol can estimate a reversible fraction and reversion timescale; it cannot by itself select a holiday/rechallenge policy or predict patient-level relapse risk.

In the stated conversion model, the minimum added kind of observation for $\mu$ is an on-drug rechallenge outcome from independent matched cultures with known starting persister counts and known exposure $C$, followed long enough to detect a stable resistant genotype/phenotype. If $K$ of $m$ cultures acquire a stable founder and detection is perfect, then $K\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(m,1-e^{-\mu C})$ and $\widehat{\mu}=-\log(1-K/m)/C$. With zero events, a one-sided 95% upper bound is $\mu\le-\log(0.05)/(mC)$. Imperfect detection sensitivity $s_R$ changes the event probability to $s_R(1-e^{-\mu C})$; calibrate $s_R$ or use direct clone genotyping. This simple estimator is already dominated by a cancer-specific assay in Russo et al. (2022), which used a two-step fluctuation design to separately estimate spontaneous and persister mutation rates. They seeded 1,920 independent wells, counted early resistant colonies after 3–4 weeks and late persister-derived colonies after 10–11 weeks, and reported a 7–50-fold higher persister mutation rate under targeted therapy. The extra cost is substantial, but the design directly targets the missing $\mu$ and is the strongest informed comparator. This branch does not propose a novel assay.

## A separate exact bound on negative pretreatment sampling

If $n$ independent pretreatment cells/molecules are sampled with perfect sensitivity and none contains a candidate resistance alteration, then under prevalence $p$,
$$
\Pr(\text{zero detections}\mid p)=(1-p)^n.
$$
The one-sided 95% upper bound after zero detections is exactly
$$
p_{95}=1-0.05^{1/n}\approx 2.996/n.
$$
Equivalently, detecting a clone of prevalence $p$ with at least 95% probability requires $n\ge \log(0.05)/\log(1-p)\approx3/p$ independent, perfectly assayed cells. At $p=1/25{,}000$, this is about 74,900 cells; at $p=1/50{,}000$, about 149,800 cells. At $n=1{,}000$, miss probabilities are approximately 96.1% and 98.0%. With per-cell sensitivity $s$, replace $p$ by $sp$, increasing required sample size by roughly $1/s$. Correlated molecules, spatial sampling, allelic dropout, and tumor purity make this independent-perfect-sample bound optimistic.

A negative baseline test therefore cannot establish that resistance arose de novo. The effective number of independent tumor cells sampled by a clinical biopsy has to be measured or supplied.

## Why this is decision-relevant, and what could falsify it

For the restricted mixture model, a large estimated $a$ predicts that drug-free washout leaves a stable resistant fraction; a large $b$ with appreciable $\beta$ predicts restoration of sensitivity after sufficient washout. These results motivate different ex-vivo follow-up experiments, but do not authorize or predict patient treatment.

The model is rejected if matched washout fractions fail the geometric-drop relation, if measured no-drug growth rates explain the apparent decay, if the post-washout assay induces tolerance during readout, or if new mutations arise on the assay timescale. A stable resistance allele that remains while phenotype decays also shows that the simple stable-clone state is inadequate. Real tumors can contain both paths, several reversible timescales, microenvironment-driven states, drug-delivery gradients, cell death, and ongoing mutation; the three-parameter formula then does not identify the full biology. It distinguishes only stable and reversible components under this assay, not every cause of resistance or the historical origin of every cell.

A competing design is the classic sister-culture fluctuation test: independently expand many cultures before identical drug selection and compare cross-culture resistant-colony variance. Pre-existing heritable mutations produce jackpot-like overdispersion relative to independent post-exposure induction. This is useful but not novel: Shaffer et al. (2017) already applied a cancer-specific fluctuation analysis, while noting that clone-to-clone growth/plating differences and short-term state inheritance can also inflate dispersion.

The washout-only curve is an intentionally narrow reduction of existing model classes, not a frontier model. Chisholm et al. (2015) already formulated individual-based and integro-differential models of reversible phenotypic evolution under drug exposure, combining nongenetic fluctuations, stress-induced adaptation, selection, and drug-free resensitization. Russo et al. (2022) explicitly estimated persister-to-resistant mutation rates and thereby attacks the hidden-$\mu$ counterexample experimentally. Gunnarsson, Foo, and Leder (2023) gave a likelihood-based identifiability analysis for cell-division, death, and phenotype-switching models: fraction data identify fewer parameters than cell-number data, and practical recovery depends on multiple timepoints, replicates, noise, and initial conditions. Gevertz et al. (2025) fit a drug-induced resistance model to total cell counts and found practical identifiability in a sensitive/resistant two-state model but loss of practical identifiability after adding a quiescent subpopulation. These works make the simple three-point fit a didactic special case only.

## Primary literature and evidence boundary

- Luria, S. E. & Delbrück, M. (1943), “Mutations of Bacteria from Virus Sensitivity to Virus Resistance,” *Genetics* 28:491–511. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/28.6.491). Origin of the independent-culture fluctuation design, in bacteria.
- Sharma, S. V. et al. (2010), “A chromatin-mediated reversible drug-tolerant state in cancer cell subpopulations,” *Cell* 141:69–80. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2010.02.027) · [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2851638/). In PC9 cells the paper reports roughly 0.3% drug-tolerant persisters after 9 days of EGFR-TKI exposure; persisters regained sensitivity after drug-free expansion within about 9 doublings. Expanded persisters took about 90 doublings (20–30 passages) to regain sensitivity, showing why one washout duration is not portable across states.
- Hata, A. N. et al. (2016), “Tumor cells can follow distinct evolutionary paths to become resistant to epidermal growth factor receptor inhibition,” *Nature Medicine* 22:262–269. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm.4040) · [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4900892/). In PC9 EGFR-mutant NSCLC cultures, early T790M resistance was traced to pre-existing cells, while late T790M also evolved from initially T790M-negative drug-tolerant cells. They estimate pretreatment T790M near 1 in 25,000–50,000 cells and report distinct apoptotic responses of early versus drug-tolerant-derived T790M clones to a later-generation EGFR inhibitor. The same genotype need not encode the same path or subsequent functional response.
- Shaffer, S. M. et al. (2017), “Rare cell variability and drug-induced reprogramming as a mode of cancer drug resistance,” *Nature* 546:431–435. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22794) · [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5542814/). The authors applied Luria–Delbrück fluctuation analysis to melanoma cell lines and argued for non-heritable rare-cell resistance followed by drug-induced reprogramming. Their supplementary discussion notes that clone-to-clone proliferation/plating differences or short-term state inheritance can contribute to overdispersion; the fluctuation statistic is not a universal origin oracle.
- Chisholm, R. H. et al. (2015), “Emergence of drug tolerance in cancer cell populations: an evolutionary outcome of selection, nongenetic instability, and stress-induced adaptation,” *Cancer Research* 75:930–939. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-14-2103) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25627977/). The paper already models reversible phenotype evolution under treatment with multiple mechanisms and compares outcomes with drug-free resensitization.
- Russo, M. et al. (2022), “A modified fluctuation-test framework characterizes the population dynamics and mutation rate of colorectal cancer persister cells,” *Nature Genetics* 54:976–984. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01105-z) · [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9279152/). A two-step mammalian-cell fluctuation test estimates spontaneous and persister-derived resistance rates; the authors report 7–50-fold elevated persister mutation rates under targeted therapy and use 1,920 independent culture wells.
- Gunnarsson, E. B., Foo, J. & Leder, K. (2023), “Statistical inference of the rates of cell proliferation and phenotypic switching in cancer,” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 568:111497. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111497) · [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10312912/). Gives a likelihood-based framework and structural/practical identifiability analysis; cell-fraction data expose fewer parameters than cell-number data.
- Gevertz, J. L. et al. (2025), “Understanding therapeutic tolerance through a mathematical model of drug-induced resistance,” *npj Systems Biology and Applications* 11:30. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-025-00511-3) · [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11982405/). Bulk total-cell trajectories can identify a calibrated two-state sensitive/resistant model in their dataset, but practical identifiability is lost in finer-grained models that include quiescence.

## Verdict for the parent synthesis

The routine-assay equivalence and negative-sampling bound are clean, reusable lower bounds. The three-timepoint theorem gives a minimal ex-vivo design for estimating a restricted stable-plus-reversible mixture, with precise rank/conditioning conditions. But the washout curve alone cannot identify rebound probability: an unobserved on-drug conversion hazard $\mu$ yields an exact counterexample. Underlying biology, reversible-persister behavior, fluctuation tests, and multiple evolutionary paths are established prior work, especially Sharma 2010, Shaffer 2017, and Hata 2016. Treat this as a rigorous identifiability audit and assay refinement, **not** as a new cancer-evolution discovery or a clinical recommendation. The next useful gate is reanalysis of existing withdrawal/rechallenge data with growth normalization, calibrated detection, biological replicate variance, and a one-timescale versus multi-timescale model comparison; no such reanalysis was performed here.
