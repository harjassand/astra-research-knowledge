## Exact theorem

Fix n≥3. Let a:[K0,∞)→[1,∞) be smooth, nondecreasing, tend to infinity, and obey

    |a^(j)(R)|≤C_j a(R)R^(−j), j≥1,
    a(R)=R^(o(1)).                                      (A)

The last condition means a(R)≤R^δ eventually for each δ>0. There is one fixed K≥K0 and one real smooth pair u≠0,V on R^n solving −Δu+Vu=0, with R=(K²+|x|²)^(1/2),

    Φ(R)=∫_K^R a(s)ds,
    |u(x)|≤C exp(−Φ(R)),
    |D^mV(x)|≤C_m a(R)^(m+3/2)R^(−1/2), m≥0.           (B)

The constants may depend on a,n,the once-fixed parameters and m. Both u and Δu lie in L² by the exponential envelope, so u∈H² and is a threshold eigenstate. Each derivative in (B) tends to zero. Since a→∞, Φ(R)/R→∞, so u decays faster than every fixed exponential. No fixed superlinear power, uniform-in-order bound, relative symbol estimate, prescribed norm, or nonnegative quadratic form follows.

## Prescribed arbitrarily slow loss

**Further deduction:** given any positive function L(r)→∞, one can choose an admissible a above so that eventually a(R)^(3/2)≤L(R/2). Consequently the theorem yields

    |V(x)|≤C(1+|x|)^(−1/2)L(|x|), |x| sufficiently large,

and still every fixed derivative vanishes and u is faster than every fixed exponential. Write ℒ(r)=inf_(s≥r)L(s), its nondecreasing lower envelope. Since L→∞, ℒ→∞; restrict to a sufficiently large radius where ℒ≥1. We assert the loss bound eventually only: arbitrary positive L can have infimum zero on a finite compact interval, so no global comparison to the original L is inferred there.

Here is an explicit admissible diagonal construction. Let S(t) be a fixed smooth nondecreasing step, zero for t≤0 and one for t≥1. Choose increasing T_j with T_(j+1)≥T_j+2, T_j≥e^j, and ℒ(e^(T_j)/2)≥(j+2)^(3/2). Put

    a(R)=1+Σ_(j≥1) S(log R−T_j).

The sum is locally finite. Its value tends to infinity and is nondecreasing. At most one transition is active; each fixed t-derivative is uniformly bounded by the corresponding derivative norm of S. Composition with logR gives |a^(k)(R)|≤C_k/R^k≤C_ka/R^k. The condition T_j≥e^j gives a(R)≤2+log(log R) for large R, so a is subpower. Between T_j and T_(j+1) in log coordinates its value is at most j+1, while ℒ(R/2) is at least (j+2)^(3/2). Thus a(R)^(3/2)≤ℒ(R/2) eventually. For r=|x|≥K/√3, R/2≤r and ℒ(R/2)≤ℒ(r)≤L(r), proving the desired eventual bound. No approximation of L itself by a differentiable function is needed.

This result stays above the critical O(r^(−1/2)) amplitude. The endpoint is already excluded by Cruz-Sampedro1999 Theorem1: set ε=1/2, hence δ=0 in 3δ=max(0,1−2ε); a solution with exp(τr)u∈L² for every τ>0 has compact support. For our smooth bounded potentials, local UCP then makes u identically zero. This source theorem covers complex potentials as well and real E, so certainly E=0 in our real class. We read its exact statement and proof pp143–146; do not describe the endpoint as open. The arbitrary-loss theorem therefore approaches an established amplitude boundary from above, with the strict unbounded loss essential. It does not answer whether the operator has infinitely many negative eigenvalues.
