## 1. The original chart

Work in characteristic zero. In R=Q[x1,x2,x3,x4], write

  X=x2²−x4²+x1x3,
  f=x1−2X[X(x2+x4)+x1x4].

Use the five-generator presentation

  P=Q[p,s,u,F,J],  X=s²−u²+pF,
  H=X²F−(1+2sX)J−pJ²−u,  A=P/(H).

The literal identity

  XY−Z(Z+1)=p(H+u),
  Y=s+X(X+u²),  Z=sX+pJ                                      (C1)

will be used repeatedly. Put

  M=[[F,s−u],[s+u,−p]],  e=(X,−J)^t,  n=(J,X)^t.

Then −det M=X and e^t n=0. The matrix B=ne^t satisfies B²=0, so

  M'=(I−2B)M,  M=(I+2B)M'                                   (C2)

are polynomial inverse substitutions. Their determinants are one; hence X,J are unchanged. The half-difference of the new off-diagonal entries is u'=u−e^tMe, so H=−J−u'. On H=0, identify p'=x1,s'=x2,F'=x3,J=x4 and u'=−J. The inverse source presentation is

  F=(1+2JX)x3−2J²(x2−J),
  a=(1+2JX)(x2+J)+2J²x1,
  b=2X²x3+(1−2XJ)(x2−J),
  s=(a+b)/2,  u=(b−a)/2,  p=f.                              (C3)

Every expression is polynomial. This proves A≅R before discussing stabilization or noncoordinateness.

## 2. The finite exponential and its inverse

After inverting p, (p,X,Y,Z,u) are polynomial coordinates:

  s=Y−X(X+u²),  F=(X−s²+u²)/p,  J=(Z−sX)/p.

The derivation Δ=−p∂u fixes p,X,Y,Z and restricts to P:

  Δu=−p, Δs=2pXu, ΔF=−4sXu−2u, ΔJ=−2X²u.

It is locally nilpotent because it is a constant derivative in the localized coordinates. Equation (C1) gives ΔH=p. For an invariant parameter t, let

  V+=t(2u−pt).

The polynomial flow exp(tΔ) is

  u+=u−pt,  s+=s+pXV+,
  F+=F−2sXV+−pX²(V+)²−2ut+pt²,
  J+=J−X²V+.                                                (C4)

For exp(−tΔ), put V−=t(2u+pt) and use

  u−=u+pt,  s−=s−pXV−,
  F−=F+2sXV−−pX²(V−)²+2ut+pt²,
  J−=J+X²V−.                                                (C5)

These preserve X=s²−u²+pF,Y,Z and are mutual polynomial inverses; (C1) gives H+=H+pt and H−=H−pt. The formulas make finiteness explicit: Δ²u=0, Δ³s=Δ³J=0, Δ⁵F=0.

## 3. Complete forward and inverse circuits

For arbitrary source inputs (x1,x2,x3,x4,w), reconstruct (p,s,u,F,J) by (C3), then apply (C5) with t=w. Call the resulting coordinates S,U,Fm,Jm. Define

  X0=S²−U²,
  N=(1−2SX0)Fm+4S²Jm,
  Q0=2X0 Fm²+p Fm³−2S Fm Jm−Jm²,
  W=w+Q0.

The forward map is

  Ψ=(p,S,U,N,W).                                             (C6)

For arbitrary independent target inputs (p,S,U,N,W), put

  X0=S²−U²,  L=U−pW,
  Fm=4S²L+(1+2SX0)N,
  Jm=−(1−2SX0)L+X0²N,
  Q0=2X0 Fm²+p Fm³−2S Fm Jm−Jm²,
  w=W−Q0,  X=X0+pFm.                                       (C7)

Apply (C4) with parameter w to (p,S,U,Fm,Jm). Call its output s,u,F,J. Form M from these, then M'=(I−2ne^t)M as in (C2), and output (p',s',F',J,w), where s' is the half-sum of the off-diagonal entries.

Why this is a universal inverse, including p=0: the coefficient determinant of (F,J)→(L,N), with L=X0²F−(1+2SX0)J, is one, and

  H=L−U+pQ0.                                                (C8)

For the forward map H=0 becomes H−+pw=0, so L=U−pW. Thus (C7) reverses the transfer without a localization. Conversely (C7) ensures H−=−pw; (C4) restores H=0, and (C2) restores the original four variables. All three pairs of operations are explicit polynomial inverses. This proves Ψ is an automorphism of Q^5, and hence of C^5. Its first component is f. In algebra-map convention Ψ* sends the target first coordinate to f; its inverse sends f to the original first coordinate.

The Jacobian is constant one: a polynomial inverse makes its determinant a nonzero constant; at the origin, the linear terms are (x1,x2,−x4,x3,w), whose determinant is one.

## 4. A commuting frame acquired on the original four-space

On A[p^-1]=Q[p±1,X,Y,Z], take D_i=p²∂i, for i=X,Y,Z. These restrict to global derivations of A. In fact they already preserve H on P. Set

  A_X=Y, A_Y=X, A_Z=−(2Z+1),
  B_i=p·1_(i=Y)−p(2X+u²)·1_(i=X)−2Xu A_i.

Their values on all source generators are

  D_i p=0,
  D_i u=p A_i,  D_i s=p B_i,
  D_i F=p·1_(i=X)−2sB_i+2uA_i,
  D_i J=p·1_(i=Z)−XB_i−sp·1_(i=X).                          (C11)

Direct differentiation gives D_i(X,Y,Z)=p²e_i. Applying these to (C1) gives D_i H=0 identically. On P[p^-1] they are

  p²∂i+p A_i∂u.

The A_i are the respective partial derivatives of XY−Z(Z+1), so their cross-derivatives agree and the D_i commute. Each is locally nilpotent: along its flow, X,Y or Z is translated linearly and u changes by a polynomial of degree at most two in the time. The inverse expressions for s,F,J are polynomials in these localized variables; preservation of P then gives local nilpotence on P and A. On A[p^-1] they are exactly p²∂i. Consequently they are linearly independent over A, and

  ker D_X ∩ ker D_Y ∩ ker D_Z = Q[p].                        (C12)

For the last global step, the localized common kernel is Q[p±1]. If a Laurent polynomial with an actual negative power were in A, multiply by its smallest power of p and reduce modulo p. A/(p)=Q[s,u,N] is a nonzero domain; the nonzero constant term cannot vanish there. Thus A∩Q[p±1]=Q[p].

## 5. Exact ranks and the common fixed scheme

On p≠0, the three outputs (X,Y,Z) have derivative matrix p²I3, so orbit rank is exactly three.

Modulo p, write X=s²−u², Z=sX and k=1+2sX. Equation (C11) becomes

  (D_i p,D_i s,D_i u,D_i F,D_i J)
      = A_i(0,0,0,2uk,2uX²).                                (C13)

The A_i do not simultaneously vanish: A_Y=X and A_Z=−k. Also

  (1−2sX)k+4s²X²=1.                                        (C14)

Thus the final vector is nonzero precisely when u≠0. The pointwise orbit rank is exactly one on p=0,u≠0 and zero on p=u=0. Since A/(p,u)=Q[s,N], the common fixed set is an affine plane.

This is scheme-theoretically the ideal (p,u), not only a pointwise claim. Let J_fix be the ideal of all generator values D_i(g). It contains D_X X=p² and

  D_Zu+2sD_Yu+2J D_XX=−p,                                  (C15)

so p∈J_fix. Modulo p, put E=D_Z+2sD_Y. By (C13),

  (1−2sX)E(F)+4s²E(J)=−2u.                                 (C16)

Hence u∈J_fix in characteristic zero. Conversely every value in (C11) belongs to (p,u), proving equality.

## 6. What this combination enables, and what it does not

The acquired object simultaneously has all fibres A^3 (source Proposition 2.3), the polynomial section λ↦(λ,0,0,0), an explicit stable chart with constant Jacobian one, three commuting complete LNDs independent over the ring with kernel Q[f], and an exactly located rank-drop plane. Once the separate noncoordinate obstruction is independently adopted, these give a concrete adversarial input for recognizing coordinates from generic commuting flows, affine-space fibres, a section or stable charts.

The stable chart also gives a polynomial certificate that the four original partial derivatives of f generate the unit ideal. Differentiate f∘Ψ^-1=p, substitute Ψ(x,0), and use the first four components of ∂pΨ^-1 as the coefficients. There is no fifth derivative of f. Thus the failure is not an ambient critical point; the September24 companion has that easier obstruction and is scientifically different.

After stabilization, translations in S,U,N,W give a global full-rank commuting four-frame along p; restriction to w=0 is an additional tangency obligation and does not transfer that frame back. The original three-frame instead has the precise degeneration (C13). A full-rank frame on every original fibre, or a way to remove its rank drop while preserving global completeness, would be a substantially stronger input and is not supplied here.
