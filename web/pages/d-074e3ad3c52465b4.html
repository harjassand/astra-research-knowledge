2026-10-08

Target: outputs/research/sol_bond_renewal/RESULT.txt, especially Sections 2--5.
Audit boundary: full-span q=2 gap words with every observed gap label occurring

VERDICT

I found no fatal counterexample in the overlapping-group/degree-two argument,
the component-tail estimate, or the conversion of that estimate into a
polynomial lower bound on the row-factorial correction mean. The edge-overlap
case is correctly isolated and is essential to the path count. The subsequent
FPRAS composition has polynomial outer costs under the stated explicit-N
encoding and the rational finite-bit repair described below. The source Ising
paper itself assumes unit-cost exact real arithmetic, so the bit-model claim is
the packet's additional internal argument, not a theorem stated by that paper.

One wording point should be made explicit in Section 3: “remove loops with
their factor r” is valid for self-loop edges in the zero-field Ising gadget
after the triangle replacement. It is not valid if read as removing a loop of
the original matrix interaction M_r as a scalar. For an original matrix loop,
M_r(s,s)=r^(2+2s), which depends on s. The triangle replacement retains the two
spokes to the ghost; only its Ising self-loop contributes the scalar r. With
that intended order, the loop case is correct and is not a gap.

1. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE COUNTING IDENTITIES

For a word w=(g_1,...,g_m), the hidden path has m+1 binary state variables
u_0,...,u_m. For a gap label g appearing twice at positions a<b, expanding
the factorial product by permutations of equal transition-pair colors gives
the factor

  1 + 1[u_(a-1)=u_(b-1)] 1[u_a=u_b].

Thus a bond bit sigma_g=1 adds the two equality edges
{a-1,b-1} and {a,b}; sigma_g=0 adds neither. For a full bond assignment,
let E_sigma be the union of its selected edges on the fixed vertex set
{0,...,m}, and let kappa_sigma count all connected components, including
isolates. Every component can be assigned either binary state independently,
so

  U(sigma)=2^kappa_sigma,
  W=sum_sigma U(sigma)=sum_u a(u).

The row denominator depends on the number of origins u_0,...,u_(m-1) in
state 0. If component C contains a_C of those origins, then sum_C a_C=m and

  B(sigma)=sum_(T subset components) 1/[s_T!(m-s_T)!],
  s_T=sum_(C in T) a_C,
  Z(w)=sum_sigma B(sigma).

The endpoint u_m has origin-weight zero. This is why a component containing
only that endpoint still contributes a factor two to U and to the component
coloring count, but contributes zero to s_T. The coefficient DP for
prod_C(1+z^a_C) computes B exactly in O(m^2) integer operations; its
coefficients have O(m) bits.

2. OVERLAPPING EQUALITY GROUPS: ADVERSARIAL CHECK

Form the potential simple graph E_all by taking the union of the two edges
for every doubled label, whether or not its bond is selected. A vertex t can
be incident to at most one start equality: it can only arise from gap position
t+1, whose label has at most one other occurrence. Likewise it can be
incident to at most one end equality, from gap position t. Hence the simple
graph has maximum degree two.

An edge can have two owners only when it is simultaneously the start equality
of one doubled-label group and the end equality of another. At each endpoint,
these are precisely the only possible start/end incidences, and they coincide
with that same edge. After taking the simple union, neither endpoint has any
other incident edge. Thus every multiply-owned edge is an isolated
two-vertex component of E_all. It must not be charged as requiring both owner
bonds. The artifact explicitly handles this exception correctly.

Consequently any simple path of length L>=2 uses edges with unique owners.
Each selected bond contributes at most two edges, so a path of L edges
requires at least ceil(L/2) distinct bond bits to be selected. The edge
owner is unique along the path; the isolated shared-edge exception cannot
occur on such a path.

For any partial bond assignment, setting one additional bit to 1 only adds
equality edges. Component count cannot increase, so the selected branch's U
weight is at most the unselected branch's U weight term-by-term, and hence
also after summing over unspecified bits. Therefore every selected-bit
conditional under mu_U(sigma)=U(sigma)/W is at most 1/2. Revealing the t
distinct owner bits of a fixed path one at a time gives

  Pr_muU[all t owner bits are 1] <= 2^(-t).

This argument does not assume independent bonds. It uses only monotonicity of
2^kappa under adding edges. The lower conditional bound used later is also
valid: adding one group can reduce kappa by at most two, so U_1/U_0 is in
[1/4,1] term-by-term. Thus the sequential selected-branch probability
U_1/(U_0+U_1) lies in [1/5,1/2], including when some edges already coincide
or their endpoints are already connected.

3. COMPONENT TAIL AND ROW-CORRECTION MEAN

Write n=m+1 for the number of potential-graph vertices. A degree-two simple
graph has at most 2n directed simple paths of a fixed positive length L:
choose a starting vertex and one of at most two directions; after the first
step, the continuation is forced. If a selected component has at least L+1
vertices, it contains a simple path of length L, including when the component
is a cycle. The union bound and the preceding owner-bit estimate give, for
L>=2,

  Pr_muU[max component size >= L+1]
    <= 2n 2^(-ceil(L/2))
    <= 2n 2^(-L/2).

Set L0=ceil(log_2(64 n^2)). Then this tail is at most 1/4. On the complementary
event every component has at most L0 vertices, so a_max=max_C a_C<=L0.
Also 2^(-L0)>=1/(128 n^2). Small-m cases with L0>m only make the bad event
empty or leave the same bound valid; m=0 is handled exactly outside this
argument.

For a fixed sigma, independently color its kappa components uniformly. Let
R_0 be the total origin weight colored zero. Then

  E R_0=m/2,
  Var(R_0)=1/4 sum_C a_C^2 <= m a_max/4.

For p=r/m, the binomial type estimate gives
binom(m,r)>=exp(m H(p))/(m+1), while the central binomial coefficient is at
most 2^m. With t=2p-1,

  D(p||1/2)=sum_(k>=1) t^(2k)/[2k(2k-1)] <= t^2 log 2.

It follows pointwise that

  binom(m,r)/binom(m,floor(m/2))
    >= (m+1)^(-1) exp(-4 log(2)(r-m/2)^2/m).

Because exp(-cx) is convex, Jensen's inequality and the variance bound imply

  g(sigma)=E_colors[binom(m,R_0)/binom(m,floor(m/2))]
    >= 2^(-a_max)/(m+1).

Combining this with the good-event probability and a_max<=L0 yields

  E_muU g >= (3/4) 2^(-L0)/(m+1)
           >= 3/[512(m+1)^3]
           >= p0 := 1/[1024(m+1)^3].

The constants check: log_2(64 n^2) makes 2n*2^(-L0/2)<=1/4, and
ceil(x)<=x+1 gives 2^(-L0)>=1/(128n^2). The exponent in the fixed-sigma
bound is 4 log(2)*Var/m <= log(2)*a_max, hence the factor 2^(-a_max).
No distributional assumption about a hidden source and no independence
assumption about the bond bits enters this lower bound.

This tail argument is specific to multiplicity at most two. It relies on
each doubled-label group having exactly two equality edges and on each path
edge having at most one owner except for the isolated shared-edge case. It
does not justify the same correction bound for labels with multiplicity >=3.

4. WHY THE LOWER BOUND IS ENOUGH FOR A RELATIVE FPRAS

The exact correction identity is

  C_m=binom(m,floor(m/2))/m!,
  g(sigma)=B(sigma)/(U(sigma) C_m),
  Z(w)=W C_m E_muU g.

Here 0<g<=1. Since E_muU g>=p0, additive TV error xi in the sampler changes
its mean by at most xi and can be made a small relative error with
xi=epsilon p0/32. The packet's self-reduction samples each of the L doubled
bond bits by estimating both positive conditional partition functions
W_(h0),W_(h1). Their ratio is bounded in [1/4,1], so neither branch has
exponentially small probability. Approximation failure is handled by a valid
fallback branch; finite-bit Bernoulli sampling error and per-branch failures
are included in a sequential coupling bound. With the stated allocations,
the complete sample law nu is within xi TV of mu_U.

The resulting mean mu'=E_nu g satisfies |mu'-mu|<=xi and mu'>=p0/2.
Taking

  T=ceil(1536 epsilon^(-2) p0^(-1) log(8/delta))

independent complete samples gives a multiplicative Chernoff estimate of mu'
within epsilon/8 with failure at most delta/2 (the displayed constant is
conservative). The TV bias is at most epsilon*mu/32, so the sample mean is
within epsilon/4 of mu. Independently estimate W within epsilon/8 with
failure delta/4 and multiply by C_m and the sample mean. For epsilon<1,
(1+epsilon/8)(1+epsilon/4)-1 < epsilon, and the lower product is also
above 1-epsilon. The total stated failure is at most delta. This is relative
approximation even if Z/(2K_N) is very small: the sampled observable is the
bounded correction g, whose mean has the proved inverse-polynomial lower
bound, not the rare likelihood event itself.

5. CONDITIONAL PARTITIONS REALLY ARE FERROMAGNETIC ISING INSTANCES

For each doubled-label four-port tensor, the three conditional statuses are
free: 1+delta(A=C)delta(B=D); forced identity: 1; and forced swap:
delta(A=C)delta(B=D). Apply T=2^(-1/2)[[1,1],[1,-1]] to every port.
The equality-pair tensor is invariant under T^T T=I, while the four-port
all-ones tensor becomes 4*1[A=B=C=D=0]. Hence the transformed tensors are,
respectively,

  free: 4*1[A=B=C=D=0]+1[A=C]1[B=D],
  identity: 4*1[A=B=C=D=0],
  swap: 1[A=C]1[B=D].

Install the start-pair and end-pair wires for every doubled label. The free
term then contributes the two-spin matrix M=[[5,1],[1,1]] between its wire
components. A forced identity pins both components to zero and contributes
scalar 4; a forced swap adds wires with no interaction. A singleton's
all-ones two-port tensor becomes 2*1[A=B=0], and the two free word-end
vectors each become sqrt(2) times the zero basis vector. Thus, with s the
number of singleton labels and i the number of forced-identity labels,

  W_h = 2^(1+s+2i) Z_M(G_h; all specified spins 0).

This checks the exact interface used in self-reduction. The Fourier wire graph
G_h is distinct from the selected equality graph E_sigma used in Sections
2--3; the degree-two tail proof does not assume any degree bound on G_h.

To call a zero-field Ising FPRAS, approximate 5 by r^4 with rational r>1 and
replace M by M_r=[[r^4,1],[1,1]], where for spins s,t in {-1,+1} and state
zero mapped to +1,

  M_r(s,t)=r^(1+s+t+st).

With e free interactions and |log(r^4/5)|<=rho/(8 max(1,e)), every
configuration and the partition sum change by a factor between
exp(-rho/8) and exp(rho/8). Rational bisection needs O(log(m/rho)) bits,
since e=O(m). For each original interaction vw, replace it by the three
zero-field Ising edges vw, v*, w* with equal-spin factor r and unequal-spin
factor r^(-1), using one common ghost vertex *. Identify every pinned
component with *. Conditional on s_*=+1, the triangle contributes
r^(s_v s_w+s_v+s_w); multiplication by r gives M_r(s_v,s_w). The ghost -
slice has equal total weight by global spin flip, so

  Z_Mr(G_h; pins 0)=r^e Z_Ising(H; zero field)/2.

The gadget has O(m) vertices and edges; all couplings are ferromagnetic.
For an original matrix self-loop v=w, the triangle has a loop vv and two
parallel v* spokes. After the per-interaction prefactor r, conditional on
the ghost these reproduce M_r(s_v,s_v)=r^(2+2s_v). Only the Ising loop is a
scalar r; the two spokes must remain. This is the precise interpretation of
loop removal noted in the verdict.

6. COST AND PRIMARY-SOURCE CHECK

Let L<=m/2 be the doubled-label count. For L>=1, p0^(-1)=O(m^3),
T=O(m^3 epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta)), and one correction sample uses O(m)
conditional W calls. The relative tolerance and failure probability of an
individual branch estimate are each Theta(epsilon p0/L), hence their inverse
relative tolerance is O(m^4/epsilon) and their log inverse failure cost is
O(log(m/epsilon)). The separate top-level W estimate has tolerance
epsilon/8 and failure delta/4. For L=0 the bond sample is deterministic.

Each conditional graph and its ghost-triangle Ising graph have O(m) vertices
and O(m) interactions. Self-loop edges of the Ising gadget contribute the
scalar r and can be removed; parallel edges of multiplicity t combine into
one interaction with activity

  lambda=(r^(2t)-1)/(r^(2t)+1),
  cosh(t log r)=(r^t+r^(-t))/2.

These are rational with polynomial bit length when r is rational and t=O(m).
The annealing penalties mu_k=(v-k)/v are rational for gadget vertex count v;
the final target mu=0 is estimated by the indicator 1[|odd(X)|=0] from
samples at mu=1/v, so the sampler is never invoked at zero field.

Primary source checked: Jerrum--Sinclair, “Polynomial-time approximation
algorithms for the Ising model,” author PDF
https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sinclair/ising.pdf (Theorem 2, printed
p. 9/PDF p. 11; Theorem 5, printed pp. 12--13/PDF pp. 14--15; exact-real
arithmetic convention, printed p. 8/PDF p. 10). Theorem 2 gives a generator
within TV delta in O(M^2 mu^(-8)(log delta^(-1)+M)) steps, where M is the
number of nonzero interactions; Theorem 5 anneals in at most v levels and
uses polynomially many generator samples. The theorem applies to arbitrary
ferromagnetic interaction strengths and a uniform field, including zero
field; this construction calls it only at zero or rational uniform fields.
The paper explicitly assumes perfect exact real arithmetic with unit-cost
standard functions, so its printed FPRAS alone is not a bit-model theorem.

Internal finite-bit check: for our rational r, all edge lambdas and cosh
factors above are rational; all sampled observables are rational because
their exponents are at most v. A one-edge toggle changes |odd(X)| by 0 or 2,
so the Metropolis acceptance ratio is computed from one rational lambda and
at most mu^(+/-2), hence has polynomial bit length. Simulate a fixed T_step
steps of one generator using b-bit dyadic transition thresholds, with
per-step kernel error at most tau/(2 T_step); approximate finite uniform edge
choice to the remaining tau/2. Coupling bounds the simulated output law's
TV error by tau, in addition to the source mixing tolerance. Choosing
b=O(log(T_step/tau)+log M) suffices, and all these quantities are polynomial
or logarithmic in m, epsilon^(-1), and the requested confidence parameters.
No unbounded rejection loop is needed. This supports a polynomial-bit
specialization for these rational instances, but remains an internal repair
of the source's real-arithmetic presentation.

All coefficient DP values for g are at most 2^(m+1) (O(m) bits). They can be
stored over a common denominator 2^(m+1)binom(m,floor(m/2)); the sample mean
adds O(log T) bits. C_m has O(m log m) bits, and the known K_N has polynomial
bit length and is exactly computable by the specified count DP. Thus output
arithmetic is polynomial. Because the actual input is an explicit length-N
binary block, m<=N-1 and numeric durations are not treated as a succinct,
exponentially encoded horizon. The exact-hardness construction has N
polynomial in its graph size, so its query words meet the same encoding.

7. EXACT HARDNESS IS A DIFFERENT CLAIM

The companion hidden-renewal proof uses fresh-gap padding and exact rational
interpolation to recover W=sum_u a(u) from exact Z(w) queries, then maps W
to a pinned ferromagnetic tensor partition and invokes Govorov--Cai--Dyer,
arXiv:2002.02021v1, Theorem 3.2. Its hard words have each original label
twice and each padding label once; the bond FPRAS therefore applies to each
individual full-span query. This is compatible with exact #P-hardness:
FPRAS output is approximate, while the interpolation is an exact Turing
reduction with alternating coefficients and no approximation-preserving
error analysis. No AP-hardness or exponential approximation lower bound
follows from that exact reduction. In particular, the restricted FPRAS is
not a FPRAS for censored prefixes, arbitrary multiplicity, the whole
projected code, or the terminal suffix-averaged optimal predictor.

The canonical row-weighted bond mass B is not log-supermodular: the legal
word (1,1,2,2) gives B(00)=35/6, B(10)=5/6, B(01)=5/4, B(11)=1/12, so
B(00)B(11)=35/72 < 25/24=B(10)B(01). Thus direct FKG sampling of B would be
invalid. The audited construction avoids this: it samples the unweighted
U-law through ferromagnetic Ising conditionals and inserts the explicit
importance correction g, whose expectation is bounded below as above.
