\begin{abstract}
No major open conjecture is resolved. The strongest new derivations are a robust exclusion of the retained Clifford-reflection positive cone for amplified stabilizer tests, a strengthened polynomial-identity-testing reduction with an explicit noncommutative witness, and a low-degree statistic that detects rare algebraic dependencies in a proposed uniform planted-graph sampler. Positive results include an exact Gaussian phase-reconstruction formula and a sharper formulation of the sparse-encoding interface. All priority claims remain unestablished. Complete branch reports, scripts, and audits accompany this note; finite checks support the algebra but do not replace the proofs.
\end{abstract}

\section{A robust obstruction to the retained Clifford positive cone}
Let the data Hilbert space contain disjoint blocks of sizes $k_i\geq3$, possibly with additional spectator qubits. Let $\Pi_i$ be a rank-one stabilizer projector on block $i$ and define
\[
 Q=\bigotimes_i(I-\Pi_i)\otimes I_{\rm spectator},\qquad h=I-Q,
 \qquad \rho_Q=\frac{Q}{\Tr Q}.
\]
Consider the cone generated by penalties
\[
 T=I-\prod_a\frac{I+R_a}{2},
\]
where the $R_a$ are commuting Hermitian Clifford involutions on the entire data Hilbert space. Different penalties need not commute. This is exactly the logical positive-test class in the retained construction; the result concerns this cone on the same space, not arbitrary auxiliary encodings.

\begin{theorem}[Robust positive-cone exclusion]\label{thm:cone}
Every nonzero such penalty satisfies $\Tr(\rho_QT)\geq1/7$. Therefore, for any nonnegative combination $B=\sum_jw_jT_j$, if
\[
 \alpha h\ \leq\ B\ \leq\ \beta h+\delta I,\qquad \alpha>0,
\]
then $\delta\geq\alpha/7$. In particular, no nonzero positive combination is dominated by a constant multiple of $h$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conjugate each block by a Clifford taking $\Pi_i$ to $|0^{k_i}\rangle\langle0^{k_i}|$. The state $\rho_Q$ becomes the uniform mixture of computational strings for which every tested block is nonzero; spectator bits are uniform. We prove
\[
 \Tr\!\left(\rho_Q\frac{I-R}{2}\right)\geq\frac17
 \tag{1}
\]
for every Hermitian Clifford involution $R\ne I$.

If $R$ is nonmonomial, every computational-basis column is a stabilizer state supported on at least two equally weighted basis vectors. Thus $|R_{xx}|\leq1/\sqrt2$, and the left side of (1) is at least $(1-1/\sqrt2)/2>1/7$.

If $R$ is monomial, its basis permutation is affine over $\F_2$. Every nonconstant parity under the allowed-string distribution has bias at most $1/7$ in absolute value: a nonzero parity on a $k$-bit nonzero block has expectation $-1/(2^k-1)$, and independent blocks multiply these biases. If the affine permutation is not the identity, one coordinate displacement is a nonzero affine Boolean function, equal to one with probability at least $3/7$. Moved strings contribute $1/2$ to the rejection probability, giving at least $3/14$.

It remains to consider a diagonal $R$. A diagonal Clifford with eigenvalues $\pm1$ has the form $R|x\rangle=(-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle$, where $f$ is a Boolean polynomial of degree at most two. Constant and nonconstant linear $f$ satisfy (1) immediately. If the coefficient of $x_ax_b$ is one, condition on the other bits. When both selected bits lie in a tested block, the event that the remaining bits of that block are nonzero has probability
\[
 \frac{2^k-4}{2^k-1}\geq\frac47.
\]
On this event the selected pair is uniform. A two-bit polynomial with mixed coefficient one is nonzero on at least one of its four inputs, giving $1/7$. If the bits are in different tested blocks, the corresponding event has probability at least $(6/7)^2$, yielding at least $9/49>1/7$. Spectator bits only improve the estimate. This proves (1).

If $T\ne0$, at least one $R_a\ne I$, and commuting-projector order gives $T\geq(I-R_a)/2$. Hence $\Tr(\rho_QT)\geq1/7$. Let $W$ be the total weight of nonzero summands. Then $\Tr(\rho_QB)\geq W/7$ and $\|B\|\leq W$. Since $h$ is a nonzero projection, $B\geq\alpha h$ implies $W\geq\alpha$. Finally, the upper sandwich and $h\rho_Q=0$ imply $\Tr(\rho_QB)\leq\delta$.
\end{proof}

The constant $1/7$ is attained for a single three-qubit block by the diagonal Clifford with phase $(-1)^{(1+x_1)(1+x_2)}$: precisely one of the seven nonzero strings is rejected. Exhaustive exact enumeration of the 127 nonidentity Boolean quadratic phases on three bits confirms this diagnostic.

An elementary exact version is also informative. The allowed strings contain an anchor with all tested bits one and every string within Hamming distance two of it. A Clifford fixing their span pointwise must be the identity: after translating the anchor to zero, its action on $0,e_i$ forces its affine permutation to be the identity, and its action on $e_i+e_j$ eliminates every quadratic phase. Thus there are no nontrivial Clifford checks fixing this whole kernel.

\paragraph{Relation to the hard family.}
The formal amplification theorem does not itself assert that all seed terms are rank-one stabilizer projectors. The quantum audit gives a separate combination of the bounded-degree Feynman--Kitaev construction \cite{abn} with BJSW's stabilizer decomposition \cite{bjsw}. The added SWAP propagation core has a stabilizer eigenbasis; the decomposition therefore retains bounded degree up to a constant. Smaller rank-one local terms can be padded with fresh unconstrained qubits and split into at most eight rank-one five-qubit terms. This is a derived reduction sketch with the detailed interface in the audit, not a theorem attributed verbatim to BMVZ. The cone theorem itself needs none of that hardness claim.

\paragraph{What survives.}
This excludes same-space positive conversion through the retained cone, even with vanishing additive error. It does not exclude signed expansions, tests outside that cone, non-termwise global constructions, general basis changes, or new auxiliary-space isometries. Those distinctions are essential.

\section{Sparse positive encoding: a simpler interface and a real norm barrier}
Exact invariance of the code under a test is unnecessary for soundness. The relevant control follows from positivity. Its linear bound is established nullspace-projection prior art \cite{afh,cgw}; the sharp form below is derived and proved here without a novelty claim.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp positive compression bound]\label{thm:compression}
Let $P$ be an orthogonal projection, $CP=0$, $C\geq\gamma(I-P)$, $0\leq B\leq LI$, and $h_0=\lambda_{\min}(PBP|_{\operatorname{ran}P})$. Then
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(C+B)\geq
 \frac{\gamma+L-\sqrt{(\gamma+L)^2-4\gamma h_0}}2
 \geq\frac{\gamma h_0}{\gamma+L}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The first bound is sharp in dimension two, without assuming $[B,P]=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to replace $C$ by $\gamma(I-P)$. For $a=\gamma-\lambda>0$, positivity of $B+\gamma(I-P)-\lambda I$ is equivalent to
$\gamma P(B+aI)^{-1}P\leq P$. The scalar chord bound on $[0,L]$ gives
\[
 (B+aI)^{-1}\leq a^{-1}I-\frac{B}{a(a+L)}.
\]
The compressed inequality follows if $\lambda(\gamma+L-\lambda)\leq\gamma h_0$. Choose the smaller root, using continuity at endpoints. For sharpness take $P=|0\rangle\langle0|$, $C=\operatorname{diag}(0,\gamma)$, and $B=L|v\rangle\langle v|$ with $v=(\sqrt{h_0/L},\sqrt{1-h_0/L})$. Its sum has trace $\gamma+L$ and determinant $\gamma h_0$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, if $C,B$ are sparse positive contractions, $P=VV^\dagger$, $C\geq\gamma(I-P)$, and $\alpha H\leq V^\dagger BV\leq\beta H$, then $K=(C+B)/2$ satisfies
\[
 \frac{\gamma\alpha}{2(\gamma+1)}\lambda_{\min}(H)
 \leq\lambda_{\min}(K)\leq\frac\beta2\lambda_{\min}(H).
 \tag{3}
\]
This includes exact zero completeness. It removes an extra construction obligation, but does not construct $B$ or $C$.

For commuting local forbidden projectors let $S=\sum_{i=1}^t\Pi_i$ and $F=I-\prod_i(I-\Pi_i)$. Integer spectrum gives, for $a=\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)$,
\[
 (1-\varepsilon)F\leq S(S+aI)^{-1}\leq F.
 \tag{4}
\]
This is exact kernel-preserving spectral flattening. The sparse positive matrix
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}I&\sqrt a I\\\sqrt a I&S+aI\end{pmatrix}
\]
has (4)'s middle term as the Schur complement of its lower block. However, $\|G\|=\Theta(t)$ when all violations coexist. Normalization loses an inverse factor of $t$.

\begin{lemma}[Affine syndrome barrier]
Suppose syndrome blocks have the form $K_x=K_0+\sum_{i=1}^t x_iA_i$ on a fixed auxiliary space, $0\leq K_x\leq I$ for all $x\in\{0,1\}^t$, and $\lambda_{\min}(K_0)=0$. If $\lambda_{\min}(K_x)\geq c$ for every $x\ne0$, then $c\leq1/t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a unit $v\in\ker K_0$, every singleton syndrome implies $\langle v,A_iv\rangle\geq c$. The all-ones syndrome gives $1\geq\langle v,K_{\mathbf1}v\rangle\geq tc$. No positivity assumption on individual $A_i$ is used.
\end{proof}
Thus simply adding fixed ancillas to an affine, syndrome-preserving realization cannot repair this norm loss. Nonlinear syndrome dependence or mixing falls outside the lemma.

\section{A same-basis sparsity--gap tradeoff}
Let $P_t=(I_4-J_4/4)^{\otimes t}$ and $h_t=I-P_t$. The tensor-code minimum-support observation applies to $P_t$, not directly to $h_t$: any nonzero Hermitian $Q$ with $\ker P_t\subseteq\ker Q$ has a nonzero row of support at least $2^t$. The reason is that each nonzero row lies in the tensor product of $t$ four-coordinate zero-sum spaces, whose minimum nonzero support multiplies from $2$ to $2^t$.

For the complementary penalty, an independent Fourier argument gives a different, stronger-than-polynomial obstruction in the normalized constant-gap regime.
\begin{theorem}
If $0\leq K\leq I$, $\ker K=\ker h_t$, and $K\geq\gamma h_t$, and each row of $K$ has at most $s$ nonzeros, then
\[
 \gamma\leq O\!\left(\frac{\log^2(s+1)}{t}\right).
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular, a constant normalized gap requires $s=\exp(\Omega(\sqrt t))$ in these coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof mechanism]
The Walsh multiplier of $P_t$ is $f(z)=\prod_{i=1}^t\mathbf1\{z_i\ne0\}$ for $z_i\in\F_2^2$. An operator-norm $1/4$ approximant with row sparsity $u$ can be averaged over translations. Its multiplier $g$ approximates $f$ uniformly, while its Fourier coefficient norm satisfies $A=\|\widehat g\|_1\leq(5/4)\sqrt u$, by row Cauchy--Schwarz before averaging.

Sample each $z_i=0$ with probability $p$ and each other value with probability $(1-p)/3$. Every nonzero block character then has expectation $(4p-1)/3$. Hence $\mathbb E g(z)$ is a polynomial in this quantity with coefficient absolute sum at most $A$, while $\mathbb E f(z)=(1-p)^t$. On $p\in[0,1/2]$, truncating block degree above $d$ costs at most $A3^{-d}$. Choose $d=O(1+\log A)$ to make this at most $1/32$. The resulting polynomial has constant bounded supremum and changes by a positive constant between $p=0$ and $p=2/t$. Its derivative somewhere is $\Omega(t)$, whereas Markov's inequality bounds it by $O(d^2)$. Thus $u=\exp(\Omega(\sqrt t))$.

Now approximate the step on $[\gamma,1]$ by the Chebyshev polynomial
\[
 p_d(x)=1-
 \frac{T_d((1+\gamma-2x)/(1-\gamma))}
 {T_d((1+\gamma)/(1-\gamma))}.
\]
It vanishes at zero and is within $1/4$ of one on $[\gamma,1]$ with $d=O(\gamma^{-1/2})$. Thus $I-p_d(K)$ approximates $P_t$ and has at most $(s+1)^d$ entries per row. Substituting the additive lower bound gives (5). The case $\gamma=1$ is direct by continuity or $K=h_t$.
\end{proof}
The simple sparse tester $K=t^{-1}\sum_i(J_4/4)_i$ has the correct kernel, gap $1/t$, and row support $1+3t$. This gives a concrete comparison. Neither (5) nor the tensor-code bound applies after arbitrary changes of basis or auxiliary encoding.

\section{PIT remains in the last rank even with a full free-field witness}
\begin{theorem}[Strengthened exact-rank reduction]
Given a commutative algebraic branching program over $\mathbb Q$ computing $p$, one can construct in polynomial time a homogeneous linear pencil of size $N=\poly(|p|_{\rm ABP})$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item it has a supplied scalar point of rank $N-1$;
\item its commutative rank is $N$ exactly when $p\not\equiv0$, otherwise $N-1$;
\item a supplied $2\times2$ matrix substitution makes its noncommutative blow-up full rank $2N$, in both cases.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lift path products of the ABP to a noncommutative polynomial $q$ with abelianization $p$. Build an ABP for $g=qz+ab-ba$ with fresh variables. If $U$ is its strictly upper-triangular adjacency matrix, source $s$, and sink $t$, set
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}I-U&e_t\\e_s^T&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The upper block is unitriangular, and its Schur complement is $-g$. In commuting variables, $\det L=-zp$. At all variable values zero, this complement is zero, giving rank $N-1$. Homogenizing the affine pencil with a variable $\lambda$ preserves the identity test because its $\lambda=1$ slice is $L$.

For the explicit blow-up, substitute $\lambda=I_2$, $x_i=z=0$, $a=E_{12}$, and $b=E_{21}$. The upper block remains invertible, and its complement is $-\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, invertible regardless of $p$.
\end{proof}
The pencil can additionally be made symmetric while preserving a one-rank deficit. With $e$ the last basis vector and $M$ the homogenized pencil, use
\[
 \mathcal S=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda ee^T&M\\M^T&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is $(-1)^N(\det M)^2$. At the supplied scalar point, the left nullvector of $M$ has last coordinate one. The rank-one upper block removes exactly one of the two null directions of the ordinary symmetric dilation, giving rank $2N-1$. At the supplied matrix substitution, both off-diagonal blocks remain invertible: transposing the coefficient pencil amounts to transposing the matrix variables before the full transpose, which reverses the sign of the invertible commutator. Hence the symmetric blow-up is invertible as well. The detailed kernel calculation and exact ranks are in the algebra report.

This is a reduction, not an unconditional lower bound. It strengthens the retained codimension-one construction by supplying a constant-dimensional full noncommutative-rank witness, even under symmetry. The fixed-accuracy commutative-rank approximation \cite{rankapprox} and new noncommutative-rank algorithm \cite{ncrank} do not by those guarantees alone resolve this promised class. The ABP determinant construction and commutator ingredients are standard; priority for their precise combination is not asserted.

\section{Rare algebraic relations defeat a tempting uniform sampler}
Mao's construction uses latent evaluation vectors $v_i=\phi(x_i)$ of bounded-degree binary polynomials and a uniform alternating bilinear form $B$, defining graph edges $X_{ij}=B(v_i,v_j)$. Carefully fixed evaluation points have no short dependencies. Sampling fresh random points would remove nonuniform advice, but the following argument shows that it destroys the required low-degree bound. This does not refute Mao's fixed construction.

\begin{theorem}[Amplification of a rare latent relation]\label{thm:rare}
Suppose the latent-vector distribution is exchangeable and a specified $w$-set $U$ satisfies $\sum_{i\in U}v_i=0$ with probability $p_n$. For any fixed $s>w$ and independently resampled edge rate $\varepsilon<1$,
\[
 \Adv_{\leq ws}\ \geq\ (1-\varepsilon)^{ws}p_n
 \sqrt{\binom nw\binom{n-w}s}
 \tag{6}
\]
against the uniform graph null. Thus bounded low-degree advantage at degrees tending to infinity requires every fixed-size latent relation to have superpolynomially small probability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For disjoint $U,S$ of sizes $w,s$, define the Fourier character
\[
 \chi_{U,S}=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in U,j\in S}X_{ij}}
 =(-1)^{B(\sum_Uv_i,\sum_Sv_j)}.
\]
Average over $B$. The expectation is either zero or one, and is one whenever $\sum_Uv_i=0$. There are no negative terms to cancel. Under independent resampling its mean is multiplied by $(1-\varepsilon)^{ws}$. Sum over all $\binom nw\binom{n-w}s$ disjoint pairs. Their complete-bipartite edge sets are distinct because $s>w$, so their characters are orthogonal under $G(n,1/2)$. The null variance is the number of summands and the planted mean is at least that number times $(1-\varepsilon)^{ws}p_n$. Normalizing proves (6).
\end{proof}

For degree-$r$ Reed--Muller features, take $w=2^{r+1}$. Every affine $(r+1)$-flat has zero feature sum. With $q=2^m$ and sampling without replacement, a specified $w$-set forms such a flat with probability
\[
 p_n=\frac{(q/2^{r+1}){m\brack r+1}_2\,w!}{(q)_w}
 =\Theta_r\!\left(q^{-(w-r-2)}\right).
 \tag{7}
\]
For $m=b\log_2n+O(1)$, this is only polynomially small. Choose a fixed $s>\max\{w,2b(w-r-2)-w\}$. Equation (6) then diverges as a positive power of $n$ at the fixed degree $ws$, even after fixed-rate noise. Randomizing or hiding the polynomial subspace does not help: the flat identity holds for every degree-$r$ polynomial.

Avoiding affine flats alone is insufficient. The symmetric difference of two $(r+1)$-flats meeting in dimension $r-1$ is a dependency of size $3\cdot2^r$ that contains no $(r+1)$-flat. The unresolved constructive target is full evaluation-matroid girth with a low-degree latent description and a uniform efficient sampler. Primal distance or hitting-set guarantees for punctured codes are not substitutes for the dual-distance condition.

This is a general lesson with a precise mechanism: rarity in total variation does not ensure small low-degree likelihood-ratio norm. A rare latent relation can be reused across many different queried edges. The displayed statistic establishes divergent normalized expectation; it is not by itself a proof of strong detection with vanishing two-sided error under the alternative.

\section{A positive finite result in Gaussian phase reconstruction}
Ordinary reduced-state spectra are invariant under product-basis complex conjugation, so they cannot determine orientation-sensitive response. The use of modular commutators for chirality is established prior work \cite{kim,fan}; the following finite formula was derived here and checked directly, without claiming priority.

Use the convention $C_{ij}=\Tr(\rho c_j^\dagger c_i)$ and $J(1,2,3)=i\Tr(\rho[K_{12},K_{23}])$, where $K_R=-\log\rho_R$ is embedded in the three-mode system. Let $C$ be faithful and number-conserving, initially with $C_{ii}=1/2$. On three modes write $C=I/2+X$, $a=|C_{12}|>0$, $b=|C_{23}|>0$, and $W=C_{12}C_{23}C_{31}$. Pair covariance spectra determine edge magnitudes. The triple covariance spectrum and ordered modular commutator determine
\[
 \Re W=\frac16\Tr X^3,\qquad
 J(1,2,3)=-\frac{8\operatorname{artanh}(2a)\operatorname{artanh}(2b)}{ab}\Im W.
 \tag{8}
\]
Indeed a Gaussian modular Hamiltonian has one-particle matrix $k_R=\log[(I-C_R)C_R^{-1}]$. The pair's off-diagonal entry is $-2\operatorname{artanh}(2a)C_{12}/a$. Using $[c^\dagger Ac,c^\dagger Dc]=c^\dagger[A,D]c$ gives $J=i\Tr(C[k_{12},k_{23}])$ and (8). Closed length-three walks give the first identity.

More generally, half filling is unnecessary when the individual occupations $d_i=C_{ii}$ are supplied. Put $f(x)=\log((1-x)/x)$ and let
\[
 a_{ij}=\frac{f(\lambda^{ij}_+)-f(\lambda^{ij}_-)}{\lambda^{ij}_+-\lambda^{ij}_-},
\]
where $\lambda^{ij}_\pm$ are the pair covariance eigenvalues, using the continuous derivative at equality. The pair modular entry is $a_{ij}C_{ij}$. Its diagonal contributions cancel in the traced commutator, yielding
\[
 J(1,2,3)=-2a_{12}a_{23}\Im W,
\]
\[
 6\Re W=\Tr C_{123}^3-\sum_i d_i^3
 -3\sum_{i<j}(d_i+d_j)|C_{ij}|^2.
\]
Pair determinants give $|C_{ij}|^2=d_id_j-\lambda^{ij}_+\lambda^{ij}_-$. These formulas were independently checked in the full Fock space at unequal occupations.

If every root spoke $C_{1i}$ is nonzero, these data reconstruct $C$ up to on-site $U(1)$ phases. Gauge-fix $C_{1i}>0$, then
\[
 C_{ij}=\frac{W_{1ij}}{|C_{1i}||C_{1j}|}.
\]
These are one-particle covariance spectra and occupations, not merely entropy scalars or unlabelled many-body spectra. Gaussianity, faithfulness, and nonzero root links are assumptions; nonroot edges may vanish. The final gauge-reconstruction step has direct Bargmann-invariant prior art \cite{chien}; the particular modular formula has no established priority here. For equal small links $t$ at half filling, $J=-32t^3\sin\phi+O(t^5)$; weak links cause a severe conditioning problem. The result does not establish an interaction-stable thermodynamic reconstruction theorem.

\section{Other branches and why they did not take over}
\paragraph{Codes.} Constant qLTC soundness measures relative Hamming distance, not a constant normalized spectral gap. For an error-detecting stabilizer code with normalized weighted local penalties, one-site errors give $\gamma\leq\sum_j w_j|\supp h_j|/n$. A $k$-local tester therefore has $\gamma\leq k/n$. Global small-bias parity products of stabilizer checks are Pauli monomials and do yield polynomial-row, constant-gap sparse code penalties. This recovers an enforcement component, not arbitrary noncommuting amplified verification. An exact three-clause two-qubit example also shows that replacing an existential violated stabilizer check by a classical selector can destroy a zero-energy witness; its optimized energy becomes $(3-\sqrt5)/6$.

\paragraph{PDE.} The branch derived a static cone-flux inequality for divergence-free $F$ with $\|F\|_{L^{3/2,\infty}}\leq M$:
\[
 r^{-1}\int_{B_r}|F|\leq CM L^{-2/3}
 +Cr^{-1}\int_{B_{6Lr}}|F|\,|\xi-e|.
\]
Uniform VMO of the oriented direction $\xi$ makes the left side vanish uniformly as $r\to0$. A logarithmic local-BMO modulus gives decay $O(M(\log\log(1/r)/\log(1/r))^{2/3})$. The assumptions are additional to finite-energy Navier--Stokes, and a thin smooth toroidal vorticity family retains a nonvanishing weak endpoint norm while this Morrey mass vanishes. Thus neither a regularity theorem nor a singularity was obtained. The full estimate and independent audit are in the packet.

\paragraph{Discrete geometry.} The clean-code/sunflower branch found an exact finite certificate obstruction, amplified by Cartesian products, forcing a proposed rooted linear-certificate constant to be at least $4/3$. The block and product proof are in the branch report. This is a diagnostic for that proof strategy, not progress on the unrestricted sunflower bound. Recent bounded-VC-dimension results do not automatically control the relevant product quotients.

\section{Validation and remaining obligations}
The packet separates derivations, external results, numerical diagnostics, and open claims. Root checks exhaust all 127 nonidentity diagonal three-bit Clifford phases, test the compression inequality on 1,000 random positive matrices with arbitrary coupling, verify its equality-case characteristic polynomial symbolically, count 210 distinct small biclique Fourier characters, and check 512 affine-cube parity identities. Branch scripts check the algebraic blow-up, selector counterexample, finite clean code, and Gaussian/Fock identities. No finite check certifies an asymptotic conjecture or literature priority.

The largest quantum gap remains constructive: polynomial-row positive tests and a constant-gap sparse consistency operator for one common auxiliary encoding of the actual hard family. Positivity supplies the soundness bound once those objects exist; it does not supply them. The original positive cone cannot provide the needed same-space conversion. The largest learning gap is uniform high-girth low-degree latent structure, with rare dependency events controlled strongly enough for the full low-degree norm. The largest algebra gap remains a method recognizing abelianized path identities despite an explicit free-field inverse. The largest physics gap is robust, interaction-stable reconstruction with size-uniform error control.

The completed progress is therefore a set of technically concrete exclusions, a stronger reduction, and a finite reconstruction mechanism. It is not a revolutionary breakthrough, and this record should not be cited as settling quantum PCP, PIT, the sunflower conjecture, or Navier--Stokes.
