# Bounded local-Pauli irreducibility search

2026-10-06. **Unresolved:** whether the state-dependent SLD transforms of the local single-qubit Xi,Zi frame are irreducible for every full-rank sigma. The arbitrary-frame and involution-frame counterexamples do not settle this because those frames lack the local tensor and Pauli relations.

The search minimizes the smallest eigenvalue gamma^2 of the Hermitian commutator Gram matrix on traceless unit-Hilbert--Schmidt matrices X:

    gamma^2(sigma)=min_(X=X*, TrX=0, ||X||_HS=1) sum_a ||[X,T_sigma(A_a)]||_HS^2.

This is zero if and only if the transformed Hermitian frame has a nontrivial commutant. Parameterization sigma=alpha I+(1-d alpha) exp(-H)/Tr exp(-H) imposes the fixed full-rank floor alpha. H is expanded in an orthonormal traceless Hermitian basis; each coefficient is bounded in [-8,8]. L-BFGS-B uses an independently derived analytic gradient. A random directional finite-difference check before each experiment disagrees by only2.7e-9 to5.6e-9. Repeated eigenvalues of the minimum Gram eigenvalue may make the objective nondifferentiable, so convergence is only local optimizer evidence.

No collision was found. The best spectra approach `(alpha,...,alpha,1-(d-1)alpha)` and their dominant eigenvectors approach product states. The reference state is sigma=alpha I+(1-d alpha)|0...0><0...0|. One-site purity of the best d=8 dominant eigenvector is about.999999999988. Bell/GHZ floored states have larger gaps in the checked cases. These observations suggest a candidate worst-case family but prove neither a global lower bound nor universal irreducibility. Some starts reached the maximum iteration count; their outputs remain in the result JSON.

The exact analytic gradient uses the Sylvester identity

    I_sigma(K_a)=sqrt(sigma) A_a sqrt(sigma),
    I_sigma(delta K_a)=delta sqrt(sigma) A_a sqrt(sigma)
                       +sqrt(sigma) A_a delta sqrt(sigma)
                       -I_(delta sigma)(K_a),

and delta sqrt(sigma) solves sqrt(sigma) delta sqrt(sigma)+delta sqrt(sigma) sqrt(sigma)=delta sigma. For a minimizing X, d||[X,K]||^2/dK=2[X,[X,K]]. Applying the self-adjoint Sylvester inverses and the Gibbs Frechet map gives the implemented gradient; all matrices remain small dense matrices.
