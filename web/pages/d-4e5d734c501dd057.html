Abstract
An important dynamical property of biological interaction networks is persistence, which intuitively means that “no species goes extinct”. It has been conjectured that dynamical system models of weakly reversible networks (i.e., networks for which each reaction is part of a cycle) are persistent. The property of persistence is also related to the well known global attractor conjecture. An approach for the proof of the global attractor conjecture uses an embedding of weakly reversible dynamical systems into toric differential inclusions. We show that the larger class of endotactic dynamical systems can also be embedded into toric differential inclusions. Moreover, we show that, essentially, endotactic networks form the largest class of networks with this property.
1 Introduction
Mathematical models of biological interaction networks are often power-law dynamical systems, given by
d​𝒙d​t=∑i=1r𝒙𝒔i​𝒘i,\displaystyle\frac{d\boldsymbol{x}}{dt}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{s}_{i}}\boldsymbol{w}_{i},
(1)
where 𝒙∈ℝ>0n\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}, 𝒔i,𝒘i∈ℝn\boldsymbol{s}_{i},\boldsymbol{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}, and 𝒙𝒔=x1s1​x2s2​…​xnsn\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{s}}=x_{1}^{s_{1}}x_{2}^{s_{2}}...x_{n}^{s_{n}}. In particular, all polynomial dynamical systems are of the form given by (1). A key question in this context is the following: Given an initial condition 𝒙⁡(0)∈ℝ>0n\boldsymbol{x}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}, is it true that none of the species become extinct, i.e., lim inft→∞𝒙i​(t)>0\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}(t)>0 for all ii? It has been conjectured that if the underlying network is weakly reversible, then no species can become extinct [13]. This has been called the Persistence Conjecture [10] and has been an open problem for several decades. The property of persistence is related to another conjecture called the Global Attractor Conjecture, which asserts the existence of a unique globally attracting equilibrium for complex balanced systems [9]. Several special cases of this conjecture have been proved using a variety of approaches [3, 10, 21, 16], and a proof of this conjecture in full generality has been proposed recently in [7]. A key component of this proof relies on the machinery of differential inclusions to build zero-separating surfaces and eventually prove the existence of a unique globally attracting steady state. More specifically, the proof uses the embedding of weakly reversible dynamical systems into toric differential inclusions. The main contribution of this paper is to show that a much larger class of dynamical systems called endotactic dynamical systems can also be embedded into toric differential inclusions. In addition, we show that endotactic dynamical systems are, essentially, the largest class of dynamical systems that possess this property.
This paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we introduce reaction networks, which are directed graphs in Euclidean space, called Euclidean embedded graphs (or E-graphs). In particular, we define
endotactic E-graphs, which can be thought of as “inward pointing” reaction networks. We also introduce dynamical systems generated by E-graphs. To incorporate the uncertainty associated with measuring the rate constants in the network, we allow them to take values in a bounded interval, and we refer to these more general (non-autonomous) systems as variable-kk dynamical systems. In Section 3, we introduce the notions of persistence and permanence and describe their connection to the Global Attractor Conjecture. In Section 4, we describe polyhedral fans and toric differential inclusions. In Section 5, we state our main result, given by Theorem 5.2, which says that any variable-kk endotactic dynamical system can be embedded into a toric differential inclusion. In Section 6, in Theorem 6.1, we give a converse to Theorem 5.2. More precisely, we show that if any variable-kk dynamical system generated by an E-graph can be embedded into a toric differential inclusion, then this E-graph must be endotactic. We conclude that among all E-graphs, it is endotactic E-graphs that are most naturally associated with toric differential inclusions.
2 Euclidean embedded graphs and endotactic networks
An Euclidean embedded graph (abbreviated as E-graph) is a finite directed graph 𝒢=(V,E)\mathcal{G}=(V,E), where V⊂ℝnV\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} is the set of vertices and EE is the set of edges [7, 8]. Every edge 𝒔i→𝒔i′∈E\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}\in E has a source vertex 𝒔i∈V\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\in V, a target vertex 𝒔i′∈V\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}\in V and an edge vector 𝒔i′−𝒔i\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}-\boldsymbol{s}_{i}. We will also write 𝒔i→𝒔i′∈E\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\to\boldsymbol{s}_{i}^{\prime}\in E. Figure 1 shows a few examples of Euclidean embedded graphs. A reaction network can be regarded as a Euclidean embedded graph 𝒢=(V,E)\mathcal{G}=(V,E), where EE is the set of reactions [8].

An E-graph 𝒢=(V,E)\mathcal{G}=(V,E) is reversible if for every edge 𝒔i→𝒔i′∈E\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}\in E, we also have 𝒔i′→𝒔i∈E\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\in E. An E-graph is weakly reversible if every edge in EE is part of some cycle. Figure 1(b) shows a reversible E-graph, and Figure 1(c) shows a weakly reversible E-graph. Note that every reversible E-graph is weakly reversible. In this paper, we are interested in endotactic E-graphs, which include the set of weakly reversible E-graphs. Endotactic E-graphs have originally been introduced in [10], where persistence of two-dimensional endotactic networks was proved. Since then, several equivalent formulations of endotactic E-graphs have been given, including some that have been used for checking the condition of endotacticity algorithmically [18]. We use the formulation presented in [18] as our definition of an endotactic E-graph:
Definition 2.1.
Let 𝒢=(V,E)\mathcal{G}=(V,E) be an E-graph. Then 𝒢\mathcal{G} is endotactic if for every 𝒘∈ℝn\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} and 𝒔i→𝒔i′∈E\boldsymbol{s}_{i}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}\in E such that 𝒘⋅(𝒔i′−𝒔i)<0\boldsymbol{w}\cdot(\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{i}-\boldsymbol{s}_{i})<0, there exists 𝒔j→𝒔j′∈E\boldsymbol{s}_{j}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{j}\in E such that 𝒘⋅(𝒔j′−𝒔j)>0\boldsymbol{w}\cdot(\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}_{j}-\boldsymbol{s}_{j})>0 and 𝒘⋅𝒔j<𝒘⋅𝒔i\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{s}_{j}<\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{s}_{i}.
Note that an E-graph is endotactic iff all its one-dimensional projections are endotactic [10].
For two dimensional E-graphs, it suffices to check if its one-dimensional projections are endotactic for a finite set of vectors given by (i) the inward pointing normals to the boundary of the convex hull of source vertices and (ii) the vectors corresponding to the cartesian axes [10]. Other equivalent methods of checking whether a network is endotactic include the “parallel sweep test” introduced in [10]. See Figure 2 for examples.

Persistence Conjecture: Weakly reversible dynamical systems are persistent.
Extended Persistence Conjecture: Endotactic variable-kk dynamical systems are persistent.
Permanence Conjecture: Weakly reversible dynamical systems are permanent.
Extended Permanence Conjecture: Endotactic variable-kk dynamical systems are permanent.
Global Attractor Conjecture: For complex-balanced dynamical systems, each stoichiometric compatibility class has a unique globally attracting equilibrium.
It is known that the Extended Permanence Conjecture implies all the other conjectures. In particular, the Extended Permanence Conjecture implies the Permanence conjecture, which implies the Persistence conjecture. Further, it can be shown that persistence of variable-kk weakly reversible dynamical systems in dimension nn implies the Global Attractor Conjecture in dimension n+1n+1 [10, 7]. We now describe various results towards the proof of these conjectures.
The persistence properties of dynamical systems on the positive orthant are strongly related to the existence of ω\omega-limit points on the boundary of the positive orthant. It is known from [24, 25] that ω\omega-limit points of complex balanced dynamical systems must be equilibrium points (either positive or on the boundary). Among the equilibrium points on the boundary, the vertices [2, 9] and facet interiors [4] of the stoichiometric compatibility classes are forbidden from containing ω\omega-limit points. In addition, Angeli, De Leenheer and Sontag have shown that for conservative networks, no boundary equilibria can be ω\omega-limit points if every siphon contains a linear conserved quantitiy [5]. For more details about how siphons are related to the structure of reaction networks, see [15, 11].
Anderson [3] has proved the Global Attractor Conjecture for networks with a single connected component. Moreover, Craciun, Nazarov and Pantea [10] have proved the Extended Permanence Conjecture for the two dimensional case and have used this to prove the Global Attractor Conjecture in the three dimensional case. These results have been extended by Pantea [21] to endotactic dynamical systems with two dimensional stoichiometric subspace and to complex balanced dynamical systems with three dimensional stoichiometric subspace. Gopalkrishnan, Miller and Shiu [16] have proved the permanence of variable-kk strongly endotactic networks in any dimension. More recently, Craciun [7] has proposed a proof of the Global Attractor Conjecture in full generality by using an embedding of weakly reversible dynamical systems into toric differential inclusions.
As shown in [7], toric differential inclusions can be used in the construction of invariant regions, and invariant regions can be used to prove persistence and permanence [10, 21, 16] of some classes of power-law dynamical systems. Therefore, the results presented here point to a future avenue for proving the Extended Persistence and Extended Permanence Conjectures.
4 Polyhedral fans and toric differential inclusions
Polyhedral cones form the basic building blocks of polyhedral fans and toric differential inclusions. We will define these notions explicitly in this section. A set CC is a polyhedral cone [22, 27] if there exists a finite set of vectors 𝒗1,𝒗2,…,𝒗m\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\boldsymbol{v}_{2},...,\boldsymbol{v}_{m} such that

5 Embedding endotactic dynamical systems into toric differential inclusions

The goal of this section is to show that any variable-kk endotactic dynamical system can be embedded into a toric differential inclusion. We sketch the main idea of the proof. Consider an endotactic E-graph that generates this variable-kk endotactic dynamical system. Note that different monomials 𝒙𝒔i\boldsymbol{x}^{\boldsymbol{s}_{i}} in (4) have different magnitudes in different parts of the state space. 7 Examples
It has been shown in [8] that variable-kk weakly reversible dynamical systems can be embedded into toric differential inclusions. Theorem 5.2 shows that the larger class of variable-kk endotactic dynamical systems can be embedded into toric differential inclusions. It is notable that this embedding into toric differential inclusions also works for dynamical systems generated by endotactic E-graphs that are (in general) not weakly reversible. Such networks manifest themselves quite naturally in biological networks, as we show below.
7.1 Circadian clock network.
This network is part of a model for the daily sleep/wake cycle in living organisms.
For some choices of parameter values, the dynamics of this network can give rise to oscillations that repeat approximately every 24 hours. In [18], Johnston, Pantea and Donnell consider several versions of a circadian clock introduced in [19], with variable-kk mass-action kinetics.
