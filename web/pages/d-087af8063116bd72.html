NON-GAUSSIAN QUANTUM QUERY CONVERSE AND SPECTRAL LOWER BOUND
Date: 2026-10-07
Status: internally proved below under an explicit interface. Historical priority and external proof review are unresolved. No publication claim.

DECISIVE RESULT

The Gaussian coordinate-query converse extends to non-Gaussian canonical exponential families, including correlated sufficient statistics. Different coordinates may be decoded by incompatible POVMs. The proof never constructs a simultaneous quantum output. A bounded proper score calibrated to each binary likelihood ratio gives a distortion proportional to epsilon/r_i, rather than epsilon/r_i^2. Finite-register strong subadditivity then gives a joint-entropy precision converse.

For independent coordinates and uniform source regularity this yields the spectral bound

    log D >= (1/2) sum_i log_+(r_i/(kappa epsilon)),         (S)

where kappa is a finite source-dependent constant. The sum is over any finite or countable collection of queries; it is justified by applying the finite theorem to every finite subset. This is an infinite-dimensional converse without any Gaussian-to-quantum approximation premise. A matching upper requires a separate compiler theorem; this report proves no such upper.

The earlier full-reconstruction posterior small-ball theorem in source card M already proves the smooth s/2 exponent for one common output. The extension established here is the query-dependent, correlated and spectral version. It synthesizes standard quantum entropy and proper-scoring tools; novelty is not established.

1. RESOURCE INTERFACE

Observation and public-seed spaces are standard Borel. A blind encoder sees one X and an independent public seed R and retains rho_(X,R) in a finite-dimensional hybrid algebra

    A_R = direct_sum_a M_{d_a(R)},  D_R=sum_a d_a(R)<=D.

All input-dependent classical registers count in D. The memory is retained before the query index is supplied. For each query i, a decoder depending on i and R performs an arbitrary POVM on that same stored state and emits one real value Y_i. Only the selected query needs to be answered; the memory can be destroyed. No commutativity, sequential readout or simultaneous-output condition is imposed. Measurable matrix entries and POVM effects define the relevant kernels. Public R may be continuous but is independent of X and the unknown parameter.

No retained X, input-dependent uncharged record, parameter-sign oracle, feedback, or uncharged entangled receiver reference is allowed. Independent decoder ancillas/private randomness are absorbed into the POVM. Padding the seed-dependent hybrid algebra embeds it into a D-dimensional Hilbert space. The cost is log_2 D, not the number of real entries specifying rho.

2. ABSTRACT QUANTUM RANDOM-ACCESS MOMENT THEOREM

Let Z=(Z_1,...,Z_s) be any vector of source features. On a source event B of mass p>0, assume its conditional law is absolutely continuous, compactly supported and has finite differential entropy h_B (natural logarithms). Each query j has its own actual scalar estimate A_j, satisfying

    E |Z_j-A_j|^alpha <= eta_j,       alpha>0.

The estimates are considered in their respective query experiments. Then

    log D >= h_B-s log C_alpha
                    -(1/alpha) sum_j log(eta_j/p),         (A1)

where

    C_alpha=2 Gamma(1+1/alpha) (alpha e)^(1/alpha).

For alpha=2:

    log D >= h_B-(1/2) sum_j log(2 pi e eta_j/p).          (A2)

Equivalently D>=exp(h_B)[p/(2 pi e)]^(s/2) product eta_j^(-1/2). If sum eta_j<=eta, AM-GM gives D>=exp(h_B)[s p/(2 pi e eta)]^(s/2). In particular eta_j<=epsilon gives D>=c epsilon^(-s/alpha). Independence of coordinates is unnecessary.

Proof, entirely through finite quantum registers:

Condition the source on B; R remains independent. The conditioned error is <=eta_j/p. Fix delta>0 and put K_j=floor(Z_j/delta), K=(K_1,...,K_s). Compact support makes these finite classical registers. Average encoder states within their source cells. Holevo's dimension cap and strong subadditivity, followed by data processing separately for each query, give

    H(K)-log D <= H(K|M,R)
               <= sum_j H(K_j|M,R)
               <= sum_j H(K_j|A_j,R).                    (A3)

The last terms belong to different measurement experiments. The same reduced source-memory ensemble makes all separate inequalities valid; a POVM jointly emitting all A_j is never asserted.

For independent auxiliary U_j~Uniform[0,1), disjoint translated intervals give

    H(K_j|A_j,R)=h(delta(K_j+U_j)-A_j | A_j,R)-log delta.

The variable inside differs from Z_j-A_j by at most delta. Its alpha-moment is bounded by

    m_j(delta)=( (eta_j/p)^(1/alpha)+delta )^alpha, alpha>=1,
    m_j(delta)=eta_j/p+delta^alpha,                0<alpha<=1.

The elementary maximum-entropy moment inequality is

    h(V)<=log C_alpha+(1/alpha)log E|V|^alpha.

It follows by relative entropy against the normalized density proportional to exp(-|v|^alpha/(alpha m)); no Gaussian source assumption is involved. Conditioning reduces classical entropy. Finally the exact cell-volume inequality gives

    h_B <= H(K)+s log delta.

Insert these inequalities in (A3), cancel log delta and send delta to zero. This proves (A1). Only finite-register quantum entropies and ordinary classical density entropies were used. Public-seed conditional entropies mean integrals over seed values, not an entropy assigned to a continuous quantum register. The full independent audit is in rac_check/REPORT.txt.

Correlations are already accounted for by h_B. Equivalently, when all differential entropies exist, the usual sum of scalar information lower bounds loses exactly the total correlation sum h(Z_j)-h(Z). Thus summing separate Holevo bounds without this correction would be invalid for correlated sources.

3. FINITE-HYPOTHESIS POSTERIOR COORDINATE CONSEQUENCE

For a dominated experiment with finite hypotheses theta=1,...,m, choose a positive prior pi and posterior w_j(X)=P(theta=j|X). The scalar Brier loss

    ell_j(theta,a)=(1_{theta=j}-a)^2,  a in [0,1]

has exact conditional regret (w_j(X)-a)^2. Therefore if the retained state supports each of s selected Brier decisions with expected Bayes regret <=epsilon, and the selected posterior vector (w_{j_1},...,w_{j_s}) has an absolutely continuous finite-entropy law on a positive-mass patch, (A2) proves D>=c epsilon^(-s/2). The posterior image dimension s, with s<=m-1, is the relevant dimension.

The same implication holds if each query separately reconstructs the scalar law of w_j(X) to TV<=epsilon under every hypothesis: apply the fixed rule a=Y_j to the scalar output. The bounded loss range [0,1] makes the excess Bayes risk <=epsilon, even when the accuracy guarantee averages over R.

For an s-dimensional smooth embedded posterior manifold with a nonzero regular source density, choose a nonsingular s by s coordinate-projection minor and shrink to a compact positive-mass chart. Its selected coordinates meet (A2). This proves the parent hypothesis's lower exponent under actual output regret. Full natural-parameter dimension alone is insufficient: a one-parameter Bernoulli experiment has a two-point sufficient observation and D=2 exact reconstruction, while an s-dimensional raw observation may have a constant posterior and D=1.

An oracle providing exact Born expectations has a different interface. A single qubit rho_(x,y)=(I+x sigma_x+y sigma_z)/2 can represent a two-dimensional disk with both observable means exactly equal to x,y. Its actual once-measured scalar outputs have nonzero variance, so this does not defeat the theorem.

4. CALIBRATED PROPER LOSS FOR GENERAL BINARY EXPONENTIAL PAIRS

Let mu be any common reference law of X and T_i(X) a real statistic. Assume A_i(t)=log E_mu exp(t T_i) is finite at t=+-r_i, r_i>0. Define two parameter alternatives

    dP_i^+/dmu=exp(r_i T_i-A_i(r_i)),
    dP_i^-/dmu=exp(-r_i T_i-A_i(-r_i)).

The experiment need only contain these pairs; the query i is not the hidden sign. Set

    b_i=[A_i(r_i)-A_i(-r_i)]/(2r_i),
    K_i=[A_i(r_i)+A_i(-r_i)]/2 >=0.

For the equal binary prior, the posterior and mixture are exactly

    p_i(x)=(1+tanh(r_i[T_i(x)-b_i]))/2,
    dQ_i/dmu=e^(-K_i) cosh(r_i[T_i-b_i])>=e^(-K_i).       (E1)

Choose any known c_i>=r_i and put

    u_i(p)=tanh((c_i/r_i) artanh(2p-1)),  p in (0,1),
    u_i(0)=-1, u_i(1)=1,
    F_i(p)=integral_{1/2}^p u_i(t) dt,
    a_i=F_i(0)=F_i(1).

The loss for sign label z in {0,1} and report q in [0,1] is

    ell_i(z,q)=a_i-F_i(q)-(z-q)u_i(q).                   (E2)

This is a bounded proper score with range [0,1]. Indeed u_i is increasing and antisymmetric around 1/2. Its four endpoint losses are ell(0,0)=ell(1,1)=0 and ell(0,1)=ell(1,0)=1. On the interior

    partial_q ell(0,q)=q u_i'(q)>=0,
    partial_q ell(1,q)=-(1-q)u_i'(q)<=0.

Thus endpoint limits establish the full range. Its conditional regret is the Bregman divergence D_{F_i}(p,q). Inverting v=u_i(p) gives

    p(v)=[1+tanh((r_i/c_i) artanh v)]/2,
    p'(v)=(r_i/(2c_i))
      sech^2((r_i/c_i) artanh v)/(1-v^2) >= r_i/(2c_i).

The last inequality uses r_i/c_i<=1. Changing variables in the Bregman integral (with the proper reversed orientation when p<q) therefore yields

    D_{F_i}(p,q) >= (r_i/(4c_i)) [u_i(p)-u_i(q)]^2.       (E3)

This works for arbitrary q, including reports outside the posterior range; restricting or projecting actions is unnecessary.

Suppose query i separately reconstructs the law of T_i under the two alternatives:

    TV(L_{P_i^+}(Y_i),L_{P_i^+}(T_i))<=epsilon_i,
    TV(L_{P_i^-}(Y_i),L_{P_i^-}(T_i))<=epsilon_i.         (E4)

Use the fixed report q=p_i(Y_i). Blindness implies that the conditional sign posterior given X, the public seed and Y_i is still p_i(X). Thus the score risk difference equals nonnegative expected Bregman regret. Because ell_i lies in [0,1], (E4) gives

    E_{Q_i} D_{F_i}(p_i(X),p_i(Y_i)) <=epsilon_i.

Combine (E1)-(E3), and define the actual queried feature/output

    U_i=tanh(c_i[T_i(X)-b_i]),
    V_i=tanh(c_i[Y_i-b_i]).

Then under the same common reference input mu,

    delta_i:=E_mu(U_i-V_i)^2
       <=4 c_i e^(K_i) epsilon_i/r_i.                  (E5)

The bound holds with arbitrary seed averaging. Every i has its own coupling with common (X,R); this is enough for (A2). The epsilon/r_i scale is the key improvement over using ordinary Brier loss without calibrating its geometry.

5. CORRELATED NON-GAUSSIAN QUERY THEOREM

Suppose, for selected I={1,...,s}, U_I under mu has a jointly absolutely continuous finite-entropy law h(U_I). It is already bounded in [-1,1]^s. Apply (A2) to (E5):

    log D >= h(U_I)
       +(1/2) sum_{i in I} log(r_i/epsilon_i)
       -(1/2) sum_{i in I} log(8 pi e c_i)
       -(1/2) sum_{i in I} K_i.                        (E6)

No independence or Gaussian assumption appears. If only a positive-mass patch B has such a law, replace h(U_I) by h(U_I|B) and add (s/2)log mu(B).

If the sufficient-statistic vector T_I itself has a density, finite h(T_I) and integrable log cosh(c_i[T_i-b_i]), its separate monotone transforms give the exact Jacobian formula

    h(U_I)=h(T_I)+sum_i log c_i
                   -2 sum_i E_mu log cosh(c_i[T_i-b_i]).

Consequently a convenient equivalent lower is

    log D >= h(T_I)+(1/2) sum_i log(r_i c_i/epsilon_i)
      -(s/2)log(8 pi e)-(1/2)sum_i K_i
      -2 sum_i E_mu log cosh(c_i[T_i-b_i]).             (E7)

With fixed nonzero r_i,c_i and a regular positive-mass s-dimensional statistic chart, (E6) proves D>=c epsilon^(-s/2) even if only a single scalar coordinate query is chosen later. The entropy/chart and calibration constants must be checked; a smooth parameterization does not replace them.

6. PRODUCT AND COUNTABLE SPECTRAL CONSEQUENCES

Take a finite or countable product reference mu=product_i mu_i, with T_i=X_i and independent coordinates. Each two-point alternative tilts only coordinate i. For c_i=1 and 0<r_i<=1, put

    h_i=h(tanh(X_i-b_i)),
    C_i=(1/2)log(8 pi e)+(1/2)K_i-h_i.

For every finite subset I, (E6) gives

    log D >= sum_{i in I} [ (1/2)log(r_i/epsilon_i)-C_i ].

Choose only positive terms and exhaust the index set:

    log D >= sum_i [ (1/2)log(r_i/epsilon_i)-C_i ]_+.      (E8)

This passage requires no infinite-dimensional quantum entropy. The encoder is a measurable state map on the countable product standard-Borel observation space; a finite selected-vector ensemble is obtained by averaging over all remaining coordinates. If the sum diverges, no finite D can meet all stated scalar-query guarantees.

If C_i<=C uniformly and epsilon_i<=epsilon, write kappa=exp(2C). Then (S) follows. A simple checkable sufficient condition is: mu_i symmetric and centered, h(X_i)>=h_0, E X_i^2<=V, and A_i(t)<=K t^2/2 for |t|<=1. Symmetry gives b_i=0, K_i=A_i(r_i)<=K/2, and log cosh x<=x^2/2 gives h_i>=h_0-V. Hence one may take

    C=V+K/4+(1/2)log(8 pi e)-h_0.                     (E9)

Standard Gaussian coordinates recover exactly C=5/4+log 2 from N36. The same theorem applies to any non-Gaussian source satisfying the displayed entropy and mgf conditions. For example fixed bounded continuous positive densities on a compact interval, bounded away from zero, have finite uniform entropy and mgf constants. No Gaussian-equivalence bridge is needed.

Without symmetry, retain the exact b_i,K_i,h_i in (E8). One sufficient bound for centered sources is h_i>=h(X_i)-E(X_i-b_i)^2. If A_i(+-r)<=K r^2/2 and Jensen gives A_i(+-r)>=0, then |b_i|<=K r_i/4 and K_i<=K r_i^2/2, yielding explicit uniform constants whenever the source entropies and second moments are bounded uniformly. The symmetric case has the cleaner constant (E9).

For a full canonical product ellipsoid {theta_i=r_i v_i: sum v_i^2<=1}, all pair alternatives +-r_i e_i are present, so (E8) lower-bounds its memory. The full infinite product likelihood requires its own existence assumptions; this lower already holds for the countable subexperiment of one-coordinate alternatives and does not assume such a likelihood without proof.

Illustrative spectral orders with uniform C (only lower bounds):

  * r_i=a i^(-beta), beta>0: log D=Omega(epsilon^(-1/beta)). More precisely the right side of (S) is (beta/2)(a/(kappa epsilon))^(1/beta)+O(log(1/epsilon)).
  * r_i=a exp(-b i), b>0: log D >= [log(a/(kappa epsilon))]^2/(4b)+O(log(1/epsilon)).
  * Infinitely many r_i>=r_*>0: if epsilon<r_*/kappa, finite D is impossible.

No upper or equality is inferred from these formulas. Constants and admissibility of the source must be retained when comparing to a classical ellipsoid compiler.

6A. GENERAL CALIBRATED MONOTONE SENSOR THEOREM

The exponential form is unnecessary. The following abstract sensor version also covers the affine density paths used by the parent upper compiler.

For each query i let P_i^+,P_i^- be any binary experiment with mixture Q_i=(P_i^++P_i^-)/2. Assume Q_i>=a_i mu as measures for a common reference input mu, where a_i>0. Let its equal-prior positive-label posterior be

    p_i(X)=g_i(T_i(X)).

Assume there is a known nondecreasing L_i-Lipschitz map u_i:[0,1]->[-1,1] and a fixed scalar-output report q_i(y) such that

    U_i=u_i(p_i(X))=psi_i(T_i(X)),
    u_i(q_i(y))=V_i(y),
    q_i(T_i(X))=p_i(X) almost surely.

Thus the full-data scalar target report is a Bayes report. The output report can include clipping to the known target support. The chosen psi_i need only have a regular joint source law on the selected coordinates; raw observation dimension is irrelevant.

Put F_i(q)=integral_0^q u_i(t)dt and use the endpoint Bregman score

    ell_i(z,q)=F_i(z)-F_i(q)-(z-q)u_i(q), z in {0,1}.

It is nonnegative and proper. Its exact upper range is

    B_i=max{ integral_0^1[u_i(1)-u_i(t)]dt,
             integral_0^1[u_i(t)-u_i(0)]dt } <=2.

Monotonicity of endpoint losses proves that no interior value exceeds these cross-endpoint values. If u_i is antisymmetric around 1/2 with endpoints +-1, B_i=1 as in the exponential calibration.

The scalar Lipschitz/co-coercivity bound is

    D_{F_i}(p,q)>=(u_i(p)-u_i(q))^2/(2L_i).              (G1)

For p>q, write d=u_i(p)-u_i(q)>=0. Lipschitzness implies u_i(t)-u_i(q)>=max(0,d-L_i(p-t)). Integrating on [q,p] gives the triangular area d^2/(2L_i). For p<q apply the same argument to the reversed Bregman integral. Flat portions and endpoints require no strict inverse or differentiability.

If query i reconstructs the scalar T_i laws to TV<=epsilon_i under both binary alternatives, the fixed report q_i(Y_i) gives expected score regret <=B_i epsilon_i. Consequently

    E_mu(U_i-V_i(Y_i))^2
       <=(2 B_i L_i/a_i) epsilon_i.                    (G2)

Apply the finite-register theorem to any selected feature vector with joint density and finite entropy:

    log D >= h(U_I)
         -(1/2) sum_i log(4 pi e B_i L_i epsilon_i/a_i). (G3)

On a positive-mass patch add (|I|/2)log mu(B) and use conditional h. This theorem covers correlated sensors, arbitrary public seeds, and incompatible query POVMs. It gives exponent |I|/2 whenever B_i,L_i,a_i are fixed finite constants and the selected feature map has a regular full-dimensional patch.

AFFINE PRODUCT COROLLARY

Let each coordinate lie in [-1,1], with common product law mu=product_i mu_i. Choose known C^1 functions h_i satisfying

    integral h_i dmu_i=0,  ||h_i||_infinity<=1/2,
    h_i'(x)>=c_i>0 on [-1,1].

For 0<r_i<=1 define the one-coordinate alternatives

    dP_i^+/dmu=1+r_i h_i(X_i),
    dP_i^-/dmu=1-r_i h_i(X_i).

They are normalized positive densities; Q_i=mu exactly. Their binary posterior is p_i(x)=[1+r_i h_i(x)]/2. Define

    u_i(p)=clip_to_[-1,1] h_i^(-1)((2p-1)/r_i),

where outside the image interval the inverse is extended constantly to its endpoint. This is nondecreasing, bounded and L_i<=2/(c_i r_i)-Lipschitz. On the posterior range u_i(p_i(X_i))=X_i. Set q_i(y)=p_i(clip_to_[-1,1] y); this is a fixed report valid for all decoder outputs.

The theorem gives, for each separately reconstructed scalar query,

    E_mu(X_i-clip Y_i)^2 <=8 epsilon_i/(c_i r_i),         (G4)

using B_i<=2. If each source density is bounded above by M_i, then h(X_i)>=-log M_i. For every finite selected subset,

    log D >=(1/2)sum_i log(r_i/epsilon_i)
                 -sum_i[log M_i+(1/2)log(16 pi e/c_i)].

Exhaust the positive terms to obtain the countable spectral lower

    log D >=(1/2)sum_i log_+(r_i/(A_i epsilon_i)),
    A_i=16 pi e M_i^2/c_i.                             (G5)

If M_i<=M and c_i>=c uniformly, one can replace A_i by A=16 pi e M^2/c. If the calibration is antisymmetric, use B_i=1 and improve 16 pi e to 8 pi e. No Gaussianity or log-mgf hypothesis is needed. Additional smoothness of h_i and compact positive Lipschitz reference densities, as required by the parent's constructive upper, is compatible with this lower but is not used to assert an upper here.

For a product path g_t(x)=1+t h_i(x), the ellipsoid parameter class t_i=r_i v_i with sum v_i^2<=1 contains all these alternatives. Thus (G5) is the exact lower counterpart of the parent's proposed graded-hat compiler. Whether that upper has the same infinite spectral order is an independent proof obligation.

7. SCOPED GAPS, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND PRIOR ART

The theorem fails with free source-dependent classical memory, a sign oracle, an encoder informed of the later query, or an exact-Born-expectation oracle. It is not a theorem about the number of floating-point parameters in a quantum state. If pre-shared entanglement is allowed while only the transmitted D-dimensional register is charged, dense coding can change the information cap to 2 log D; that is outside the stated interface.

The posterior/source density condition cannot be omitted. Raw observation dimension, number of natural parameters, and cardinality of hypotheses alone do not determine the exponent. Finite exact sufficient alphabets and lower-rank queried posterior maps are decisive counterexamples to broader wording.

For common-output reconstruction, use the already established localized trace/posterior-regret theorem in work/agents/statistical_memory/sources/d-450ebb158d2c763a.txt. For the Gaussian calibrated query theorem, the exact earlier proof is work/agents/statistical_memory/sources/d-09516fa0e77f5538.txt. This report materially extends the latter's hypotheses but does not claim a new general source-compression principle; the entropic ingredients are established.
