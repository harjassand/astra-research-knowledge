CONSTRAINED SMALL-MASS EXPANSION AT THE CONTINUUM TRICRITICAL POINT
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the all-shape constrained expansion below; FINITE-EVIDENCE for the exact coefficient algebra and sine-trial quadrature; UNKNOWN for novelty/priority.

THEOREM
-------
Let J>0, lambda_c=2J pi^2, kappa_t=lambda_c/3, and for theta in H^1_0(0,1) set

  rho(theta)=sin^2(theta/2),
  E(theta)=(J/2) integral |theta'|^2 - kappa_t integral rho(theta)^2
                                      - lambda_c integral rho(theta).

For p>0 define the constrained minimum

  F(p)=inf { E(theta): theta in H^1_0(0,1), integral rho(theta)=p }.

There are constants p_0(J)>0 and C(J)<infinity such that, for 0<p<p_0,

  | F(p) - (4 lambda_c/9) p^3 | <= C(J) p^4.

Thus the exact reduced sixth-order coefficient in mass is

  C_mass = 4 lambda_c/9 = 8 J pi^2/9.

The infimum is over all admissible shapes, not merely a sine ansatz. If theta is restricted to the first Dirichlet mode theta=t sin(pi x), the fixed-amplitude coefficient is instead lambda_c/1152 in front of t^6; since p=t^2/8+O(t^4), that converts to the same 4 lambda_c/9 coefficient in p^3. Non-sine-mode relaxation cannot alter this p^3 coefficient. In fact every constrained minimizer has its component orthogonal to the first sine mode of H^1 norm O_J(p^2), and hence its positive quadratic complement energy is only O_J(p^4).

1. Expand the energy before imposing the constraint
---------------------------------------------------
For |z| small, Taylor's theorem gives the uniform expansions

  rho(z)   = z^2/4 - z^4/48 + z^6/1440 + O(z^8),
  rho(z)^2 = z^4/16 - z^6/96 + O(z^8).

Substitution into E yields

  E(theta) = Q(theta)
       + (lambda_c/48-kappa_t/16) integral theta^4
       + (kappa_t/96-lambda_c/1440) integral theta^6
       + R_8(theta),

where

  Q(theta)=(J/2) integral |theta'|^2-(lambda_c/4) integral theta^2,
  |R_8(theta)| <= C_J integral |theta|^8

whenever ||theta||_infinity is sufficiently small. Since kappa_t=lambda_c/3 and lambda_c=2J pi^2, the quartic coefficient is exactly zero and

  kappa_t/96-lambda_c/1440 = lambda_c/360.

Therefore

  E(theta)=Q(theta)+(lambda_c/360) integral theta^6+R_8(theta).       (1)

The positive sign in (1) is important. This is a constrained energy at the tricritical parameters; the -lambda_c p term remains in E and is constant only after p is fixed.

2. First-mode trial: fixed amplitude is not fixed mass
------------------------------------------------------
Take the unnormalized first sine phi_1(x)=sin(pi x) and theta=t phi_1. The exact sine moments are

  integral phi_1^2=1/2,  integral phi_1^4=3/8,  integral phi_1^6=5/16.

Because Q(phi_1)=0 and the quartic energy coefficient vanishes, (1) gives

  E(t phi_1)=(lambda_c/360)(5/16)t^6+O_J(t^8)
           =(lambda_c/1152)t^6+O_J(t^8).                            (2)

The corresponding mass is

  p(t)=t^2/8-t^4/128+t^6/4608+O(t^8).                              (3)

In particular t^2=8p+O(p^2), so (2) becomes

  E(t(p)phi_1)=(4lambda_c/9)p^3+O_J(p^4).                         (4)

The factor between the coefficient of t^6 in (2) and the coefficient of p^3 in (4) is 8^3=512. Reusing lambda_c/1152 as a coefficient of p^3 would therefore be a normalization error. If instead the amplitude is defined with the normalized eigenfunction sqrt(2)sin(pi x), the fixed-amplitude coefficient is lambda_c/144 in front of that amplitude to the sixth power, and the mass leading term is amplitude squared divided by four; the same mass coefficient results.

3. Minimize over every non-sine mode at fixed mass
---------------------------------------------------
We prove the two-sided O(p^4) remainder for the global constrained infimum. Constants below may depend on J but not on p.

Existence and smallness of minimizers. Equation (4) supplies an admissible competitor with energy at most C p^3 for all small p. For any minimizing sequence with E(theta)<=C p^3+1, write K=(J/2)||theta'||_2^2. Since 0<=rho<=1 and integral rho=p,

  integral rho^2 <= p^2,
  K=E(theta)+kappa_t integral rho^2+lambda_c p <= C_J p.

Poincare's inequality on H^1_0(0,1) gives ||theta||_2^2<=pi^-2||theta'||_2^2, and the one-dimensional Sobolev bound gives ||theta||_infinity<=C||theta'||_2. Hence

  ||theta||_(H^1)=O_J(sqrt(p)),  ||theta||_infinity=O_J(sqrt(p)).       (5)

The constraint is nonempty by (3). Choose a minimizing sequence within p^4 of F(p); since the sine trial gives F(p)<=C_Jp^3, every sufficiently late term has E(theta)<=C'_Jp^3. The bound (5), weak H^1 compactness, and compact H^1(0,1)->C([0,1]) convergence give a minimizer: the mass and nonlinear potential integrals pass to the limit, while the kinetic term is weakly lower semicontinuous. In particular, all minimizers at sufficiently small p lie in the Taylor regime of (1). Also

  integral |theta|^8 <= ||theta||_infinity^6 ||theta||_2^2=O_J(p^4),

so R_8=O_J(p^4).

Spectral decomposition and gap. Let e_1=sqrt(2)sin(pi x), let t=<theta,e_1>_L2, and write theta=t e_1+eta with eta orthogonal to e_1. In the Dirichlet sine basis,

  Q(theta)=Q(eta)
       =(J pi^2/2) sum_(k>=2) (k^2-1) eta_k^2 >= c_J ||eta||_(H^1)^2. (6)

In particular Q is nonnegative and coercive on every non-sine mode. From (1), (5), positivity of the sixth-order term, and the trial upper bound F(p)<=C_Jp^3, equations (1) and (6) imply

  Q(eta)<=C_Jp^3,  ||eta||_(H^1)=O_J(p^(3/2)).                      (7)

Mass fixes the leading first-mode amplitude. The mass expansion is

  p=(1/4)||theta||_2^2-(1/48)integral theta^4
                 +(1/1440)integral theta^6+O(integral |theta|^8).

Using (5), (7), and ||theta||_2^2=t^2+||eta||_2^2 gives

  t^2=4p+O_J(p^2),  t^6=64p^3+O_J(p^4).                          (8)

Also, the binomial expansion and (7) give

  integral theta^6=t^6 integral e_1^6+O_J(p^4)
                  =(5/2)t^6+O_J(p^4)
                  =160p^3+O_J(p^4).                              (9)

For the first error in (9), the largest cross term is O(|t|^5||eta||_1)=O(p^4); all terms with at least two eta factors are smaller. Equations (1), (6), and (9) now yield, for every minimizer,

  E(theta)=Q(eta)+(lambda_c/360)(160p^3)+O_J(p^4)
          =Q(eta)+(4lambda_c/9)p^3+O_J(p^4).                     (10)

Since Q(eta)>=0, (10) is the lower bound F(p)>=(4lambda_c/9)p^3-C_Jp^4. The pure-sine trial (4) is the matching upper bound. This proves the theorem and shows that allowing all non-sine modes cannot lower the p^3 coefficient.

Moreover, comparing (10) with the trial upper bound gives Q(eta)=O_J(p^4) for a minimizer. By coercivity (6),

  ||eta||_(H^1)=O_J(p^2).                                        (11)

This last estimate makes the separation especially transparent: non-sine relaxation cannot contribute at order p^3. The proof does not claim that it leaves the p^4 coefficient unchanged.

4. Fixed amplitude versus constrained mass
------------------------------------------
There are two different reductions.

* Fixed sine amplitude: impose theta=t sin(pi x). The energy expansion is (2), and its sixth-order coefficient is lambda_c/1152. The constraint mass is not t^2; it is (3).
* Fixed mass: fix integral rho=p and minimize over the first-mode amplitude and all higher modes. The leading conversion is t^2=8p+O(p^2) in the unnormalized convention, and the all-shape theorem gives F(p)=(4lambda_c/9)p^3+O(p^4).

One can also fix the first-mode projection t and locally relax eta orthogonal to e_1. The complement Hessian A=J(-d^2-pi^2) restricted to e_1-perp is invertible from H^1_0 intersect e_1-perp to its dual by the gap (6). The projected Euler equation is A eta=-P_perp[6(lambda_c/360)(t e_1+eta)^5+O_Hminus1(||t e_1+eta||_(H^1)^7)]. The implicit-function theorem near (t,eta)=(0,0) gives eta(t)=O_J(t^5), since there is no quartic term. Substitution shows the relaxation changes the energy relative to the pure sine only by O_J(t^10), not at order t^6. This fixed-projection statement is a local amplitude reduction and is not a substitute for the global fixed-p proof above.
