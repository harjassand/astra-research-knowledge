Local Broadcasting of Quantum Correlations
Marco Piani
Affiliation: SUPA and Department of Physics, University of Strathclyde, Glasgow G4 0NG, UK
Abstract
Operations that are trivial in the classical world, like accessing information without introducing any change or disturbance, or like copying information, become non-trivial in the quantum world. In this note we discuss several limitations in the local redistributing correlations, when it comes to dealing with bipartite quantum states. In particular, we focus on the task of local broadcasting, by discussing relevant no-go theorems, and by quantifying the non-classicality of correlations in terms of the degree to which local broadcasting is possible in an approximate fashion.
I Cloning, broadcasting, and local broadcasting
I.1 Cloning
A key aspect of quantum information, which strongly differentiates it from classical information, is the inability to freely copy quantum states. Suppose we are given a quantum system S1S_{1} in an unknown state |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle, and that we want to put – equivalently, prepare – another system S2S_{2} (let us say, of similar physical nature, and initially in some fiducial state |0⟩|0\rangle) in the same state, without changing the state of the system that was given to us. Thinking in terms of a unitary evolution UU, what we want to accomplish is the following:
U​|ψ⟩S1​|0⟩S2​|0⟩E=|ψ⟩S1​|ψ⟩S2​|ξψ⟩E,U|\psi\rangle_{S_{1}}|0\rangle_{S_{2}}|0\rangle_{E}=|\psi\rangle_{S_{1}}|\psi\rangle_{S_{2}}|\xi_{\psi}\rangle_{E},
(1)
where EE is an ancillary system that we may want to use in the process, initially prepared in some fiducial state |0⟩E|0\rangle_{E} independent of |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle and ending up in a state |ξψ⟩|\xi_{\psi}\rangle, which potentially depends on |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle. Consider now two known states |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle and |ψ′⟩|\psi^{\prime}\rangle, for both of which we assume (1) to hold for the same unitary UU.
By taking the inner product of the left-hand and of the right-hand sides of the two occurrencies (one for ψ\psi, and one for ψ′\psi^{\prime}) of (1), and using the fact that UU preserves inner products, we arrive at the relation
⟨ψ′|ψ⟩=⟨ψ′|ψ⟩2​⟨ξψ′|ξψ⟩.\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle=\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle^{2}\langle\xi_{\psi^{\prime}}|\xi_{\psi}\rangle.
(2)
Given that the modulus of the inner product of two normalized vector states is always less or equal to 1, the latter relation can be satisfied only if |⟨ψ′|ψ⟩||\langle\psi^{\prime}|\psi\rangle| is either 0 (ψ\psi and ψ′\psi^{\prime} are orthogonal) or 1 (ψ\psi and ψ′\psi^{\prime} are the same). This is the content of the no-cloning theorem [1, 2], which says that, within the quantum formalism, there is no physical process able to clone pure quantum states that are not orthogonal.
In the general case where one adopts the formalism of density matrices and channels, the cloning of a state ρ\rho of a system SS by means of an S→S1​S2S\rightarrow S_{1}S_{2} channel Λ\Lambda corresponds to the request
Λ⁡[ρS]=ρS1⊗ρS2,\Lambda[\rho_{S}]=\rho_{S_{1}}\otimes\rho_{S_{2}},
(3)
where the introduction of a system onto which to copy the state and the possibility of using an ancillary system in the process are already taken into account by the quantum channel formalism. It is useful to recall that every quantum channel ΛS→S′\Lambda_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}} from a system SS to a system S′S^{\prime} (of potentially different dimensionality) can be seen as the result of an isometry VS→S′​EV_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}E} from SS to a combined system S′​ES^{\prime}E, followed by the tracing out of EE:
ΛS→S′​[ρS]=TrE⁡(VS→S′​E​ρS​VS→S′​E†)\Lambda_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}}[\rho_{S}]=\Tr_{E}\left(V_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}E}\rho_{S}V_{S\rightarrow S^{\prime}E}^{\dagger}\right)
(4)
This is known as Stinespring or isometric dilation of quantum channels [3]. In the case of cloning, the output system S′S^{\prime} consists of two copies of the input system SS, that is, S′=S1​S2S^{\prime}=S_{1}S_{2}.
Consider the fidelity between two states ρ\rho and σ\sigma [4],
F⁡(ρ,σ):=Tr⁡(ρ​σ​ρ)=‖ρ​σ‖1.F(\rho,\sigma):=\Tr\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\right)=\|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}\|_{1}.
(5)
In the rightmost expression, ∥⋅∥1\|\cdot\|_{1} indicates the 1-norm (also called trace norm), ‖X‖1:=Tr⁡X​X†=Tr⁡X†​X\|X\|_{1}:=\Tr\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}=\Tr\sqrt{X^{\dagger}X}. Such an expression shows explicitly that the fidelity is symmetric in ρ\rho and σ\sigma. The fidelity satisfies 0≤F⁡(ρ,σ)≤10\leq F(\rho,\sigma)\leq 1, with F⁡(ρ,σ)=0F(\rho,\sigma)=0 if and only if ρ\rho and σ\sigma are orthogonal, and F⁡(ρ,σ)=1F(\rho,\sigma)=1 if and only if ρ=σ\rho=\sigma. Furthermore, it is multiplicative on tensor states,
F⁡(ρ⊗ρ′,σ⊗σ′)=F⁡(ρ,σ)​F​(ρ′,σ′),F(\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime},\sigma\otimes\sigma^{\prime})=F(\rho,\sigma)F(\rho^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}),
(6)
and it is monotone under quantum channels, i.e.,
F⁡(Γ⁡[ρ],Γ⁡[σ])≥F⁡(ρ,σ),F(\Gamma[\rho],\Gamma[\sigma])\geq F(\rho,\sigma),
for any pair of states ρ,σ\rho,\sigma and any quantum channel Γ\Gamma.
Suppose now that ρ\rho and ρ′\rho^{\prime} can each be cloned by the action of the same Λ\Lambda.
Then we have
F⁡(ρ,ρ′)≤F⁡(Λ⁡[ρ],Λ⁡[ρ′])=F⁡(ρ⊗ρ,ρ′⊗ρ′)=F⁡(ρ,ρ′)​F​(ρ,ρ′).F(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\leq F(\Lambda[\rho],\Lambda[\rho^{\prime}])=F(\rho\otimes\rho,\rho^{\prime}\otimes\rho^{\prime})=F(\rho,\rho^{\prime})F(\rho,\rho^{\prime}).
(7)
The inequality is due to the monotonicity of the fidelity under quantum channels; the first equality is just the hypothesis that Λ\Lambda is able to clone both ρ\rho and ρ′\rho^{\prime}; the second equality is due to the multiplicativity of fidelity on tensor states. Since 0≤F⁡(ρ,ρ′)≤10\leq F(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\leq 1, Eq. (7) can hold only in two situations: either F⁡(ρ,ρ′)=0F(\rho,\rho^{\prime})=0 (the states are orthogonal) or F⁡(ρ,ρ′)=1F(\rho,\rho^{\prime})=1 (the two states are actually the same state). Notice that in both latter circumstances the two states commute, [ρ,ρ′]=0[\rho,\rho^{\prime}]=0, and they do so in a trivial way. Thus, we find that, when it comes to cloning, the conditions for it to be possible do not vary when moving from pure states to the consideration of general mixed states.
I.2 Broadcasting
In the general framework of density operators, though, one can relax the condition of cloning to that of ‘broadcasting’, for which we only require that ρ~S1​S2=ΛS→S1​S2​[ρS]\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}S_{2}}=\Lambda_{S\rightarrow S_{1}S_{2}}[\rho_{S}] is such that its marginals ρ~S1=TrS2⁡(ρ~S1​S2)\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}}=\Tr_{S_{2}}(\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}S_{2}}) and ρ~S2=TrS1⁡(ρ~S1​S2)\tilde{\rho}_{S_{2}}=\Tr_{S_{1}}(\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}S_{2}}) satisfy
ρ~S1=ρ~S2=ρS.\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}}=\tilde{\rho}_{S_{2}}=\rho_{S}.
(8)
This means that we require that ‘having two copies of ρ\rho’ is only achieved at the level of reduced states of the output systems, and the copies are not necessarily independent, that is, we allow ρ~S1​S2≠ρ~S1⊗ρ~S1\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}S_{2}}\neq\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{S_{1}}.
It should be clear that mixed density matrices that are the convex combinations of a fixed set of orthonormal pure states, can be broadcast in the above sense. Indeed, for any fixed set {|ψi⟩}\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\} of states respecting |⟨ψi|ψj⟩|=δi​j|\langle\psi_{i}|\psi_{j}\rangle|=\delta_{ij}, there is a channel Λ\Lambda such that
Λ⁡[∑ipi​|ψi⟩​⟨ψi|S]=∑ipi​|ψi⟩​⟨ψi|S1⊗|ψi⟩​⟨ψi|S2,\Lambda\left[\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S}\right]=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S_{1}}\otimes|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S_{2}},
(9)
for any
any probability distribution {pi}\{p_{i}\}. Indeed, it is enough to consider a channel Λ\Lambda that clones the orthogonal pure states {|ψi⟩}\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}—something we well know to be possible—and to exploit the linearity of the action of a channel. One immediately checks that the state on the right-hand side of (9) is such that the condition (8) is satisfied with respect to ρS=∑ipi​|ψi⟩​⟨ψi|S\rho_{S}=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|_{S}. What is less trivial is that this is the only case where broadcasting of mixed quantum states is possible; this fact is captured by the no-broadcasting theorem [5].
Theorem 1.
Two mixed states ρ\rho and ρ′\rho^{\prime} can be broadcast simultaneously if and only if they admit a spectral decomposition with the same eigenvectors, that is, if and only if they commute, [ρ,ρ′]=0[\rho,\rho^{\prime}]=0.
Notice that, this theorem is immediately extended to a collection of states, since pairwise commutation of Hermitian operators implies joint commutation.
I.3 Local broadcasting
It is worth stressing that, for a fixed and known state ρ\rho, there is no problem with ‘cloning’, as knowing the state allows one to create as many copies of it as one wants. Nonetheless, limitations kick in again even for a single state if we consider restrictions on the operations, like allowing only local operations in a distributed setting. Then, the broadcasting of a single state of a distributed system cannot be performed arbitrarily. For example, consider the case where Alice and Bob would like to clone the maximally entangled state |ψ+⟩A​B=12​(|00⟩A​B+|11⟩A​B)|\psi^{+}\rangle_{AB}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle_{AB}+|11\rangle_{AB}). Notice that, since we are considering a pure state, we are dealing with actual cloning, not with broadcasting, that is, the target state is composed of independent copies. If Alice and Bob could use global operations, with no limit on what they can do across their laboratories, then Alice and Bob could certainly produce two copies of |ψ+⟩A​B|\psi^{+}\rangle_{AB}—even from scratch, without using the fact that they shared one copy of the state to begin. If instead they can only implement fully local quantum channels of the form ΛA​B=ΛA⊗ΛB\Lambda_{AB}=\Lambda_{A}\otimes\Lambda_{B}, then they cannot transform one copy of |ψ+⟩|\psi^{+}\rangle into two copies of it, because they cannot increase the entanglement they share. Actually, the task would be impossible even if they were allowed to communicate classically, that is, allowed to apply Local Operations aided by Classical Communication (LOCC) [6].
Is there a general no-go theorem for local broadcasting, that goes beyond considerations related to entanglement? Yes, there is! In Ref. [7] the following was proved.
Theorem 2.
Let ρA​B\rho_{AB} be a bipartite state. There exist local maps ΛA→A1​A2\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}} and ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} such that
ρ~A1​A2​B2​B2=(ΛA→A1​A2⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B]\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{2}}=(\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}]
(10)
satisfies
ρ~A1​B1=ρ~A2​B2=ρA​B,\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}B_{1}}=\tilde{\rho}_{A_{2}B_{2}}=\rho_{AB},
(11)
if and only if ρA​B\rho_{AB} is classical-classical, that is,
ρA​B=∑i​jpi​j​|ai⟩​⟨ai|A⊗|bj⟩​⟨bj|B,\rho_{AB}=\sum_{ij}p_{ij}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|_{A}\otimes|b_{j}\rangle\langle b_{j}|_{B},
(12)
with {|ai⟩A}\{|a_{i}\rangle_{A}\} ({|bj⟩B}\{|b_{j}\rangle_{B}\}) some orthonormal basis for AA (BB).
The previous result can be considered a no-local-broadcasting theorem, more precisely a two-sided no-local broadcasting theorem, that spells out a limitation about broadcasting in a distributed setting, when only local operations are allowed. In the next sections we will prove it, and even prove a one-sided version of it. Before we do that, we will recall some entropic quantifiers of distinguishability of quantum states and of correlations, and their relation with quantum recoverability.
II Entropy, relative entropy, mutual information, and conditional mutual information
II.1 Entropy
We begin by recalling the notion of von Neumann entropy [4].
Definition 1.
The von Neumann Entropy a quantum state ρ\rho is a quantifier of how mixed ρ\rho is, and is defined as
S⁡(ρ):=−Tr⁡(ρ​log2​ρ).S(\rho):=-\Tr(\rho\log_{2}\rho).
(13)
In the following we will sometimes consider the entropy of subsystems. The entropy S​(X)ρS(X)_{\rho} of a subsystem XX of a bipartite system X​YXY in a global state ρ=ρX​Y\rho=\rho_{XY} is defined as
S​(X)ρ:=S⁡(ρX),S(X)_{\rho}:=S(\rho_{X}),
(14)
where ρX\rho_{X} is the reduced state of XX. Notice that, when considering a multipartite system, we can always think of the bipartition into, on one side, the system of interest and, on the other side, all the other systems.
II.2 Relative entropy
Relative entropy is a quantifier of the distinguishability of two quantum states [4].
Definition 2.
The relative entropy S(ρ∥σ)S(\rho\|\sigma) between a quantum state ρ\rho and a quantum state σ\sigma is defined as
S(ρ∥σ)={Tr⁡(ρ​log2​ρ)−Tr⁡(ρ​log2​σ)supp​(ρ)⊆supp​(σ)+∞otherwise.S(\rho\|\sigma)=\begin{cases}\Tr(\rho\log_{2}\rho)-\Tr(\rho\log_{2}\sigma)&\textrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\textrm{supp}(\sigma)\\
+\infty&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}
(15)
Notice that the relative entropy is not symmetric in the two arguments ρ\rho and σ\sigma. In the following we will always consider the first case, supp​(ρ)⊆supp​(σ)\textrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\textrm{supp}(\sigma).
It holds that S(ρ∥σ)≥0S(\rho\|\sigma)\geq 0, with equality, S(ρ∥σ)=0S(\rho\|\sigma)=0, if and only if ρ=σ\rho=\sigma. Furthermore the relative entropy is monotone under channels, that is,
S(ρ∥σ)≥S(Γ[ρ]∥Γ[σ]),S(\rho\|\sigma)\geq S(\Gamma[\rho]\|\Gamma[\sigma]),
(16)
for any pair of states ρ,σ\rho,\sigma and any quantum channel Γ\Gamma.
The latter relation is often called the ‘data-processing inequality’ for relative entropy; operationally, it follows directly form the interpretation of relative entropy as measure of distinguishability between the two states [8].
II.3 Mutual information
Mutual information is a quantifier of correlations encoded in a bipartite quantum state, that can be understood as the relative entropy between the state and the product of its marginals [4, 8].
Definition 3.
The mutual information I​(A:B)ρI(A:B)_{\rho} between systems AA and BB in a state ρ=ρA​B\rho=\rho_{AB} can be defined as
I(A:B)ρ:=S(ρA​B∥ρA⊗ρB)=S(A)ρ+S(B)ρ−S(AB)ρ.I(A:B)_{\rho}:=S(\rho_{AB}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=S(A)_{\rho}+S(B)_{\rho}-S(AB)_{\rho}.
(17)
The rightmost side of (17) comes from computing the relative entropy for the specific choice of states ρ\rho and σ\sigma in (15), and proves that mutual information is symmetric under the exchange of AA and BB. A consequence of the data-processing inequality for relative entropy is that mutual information is monotone under fully local quantum channels of the form ΛA→A′⊗ΛB→B′\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}: if ρ′=ρA′​B′′=(ΛA→A′⊗ΛB→B′)​[ρA​B]\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=(\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}})[\rho_{AB}], then I​(A:B)ρ′≤I​(A:B)ρI(A:B)_{\rho^{\prime}}\leq I(A:B)_{\rho}. Notice that, since ΛA→A′⊗ΛB→B′=(ΛA→A′⊗idB)∘(idA⊗ΛB→B′)\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}=(\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes{\rm id}_{B})\circ({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}), monotonicity of mutual information under fully local quantum channel is equivalent to the monotonicity of mutual information under both channels that act non-trivially only on AA, i.e., of the form ΛA→A′⊗idB\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes{\rm id}_{B}, and channels that act non-trivially only on BB.
II.4 Conditional mutual information
Let us consider a tripartite system A​B​CABC in a state ρ=ρA​B​C\rho=\rho_{ABC}.
It is useful to introduce the notion of conditional mutual information between AA and CC, conditioned on BB, defined as [4, 3]
I(A:C|B)ρ\displaystyle I(A:C|B)_{\rho}
:=I​(A:B​C)ρ−I​(A:B)ρ\displaystyle:=I(A:BC)_{\rho}-I(A:B)_{\rho}
(18)
=S​(A​B)ρ+S​(B​C)ρ−S​(A​B​C)ρ−S​(B)ρ.\displaystyle=S(AB)_{\rho}+S(BC)_{\rho}-S(ABC)_{\rho}-S(B)_{\rho}.
The last expression for the conditional mutual information in (18) proves that I(A:C|B)ρI(A:C|B)_{\rho} is symmetric between AA and CC; indeed, it holds
I(A:C|B)ρ=I(A:BC)ρ−I(A:B)ρ=I(AB:C)ρ−I(C:B)ρ.I(A:C|B)_{\rho}=I(A:BC)_{\rho}-I(A:B)_{\rho}=I(AB:C)_{\rho}-I(C:B)_{\rho}.
Conditional mutual information I(A:C|B)ρI(A:C|B)_{\rho} is non-negative, a fact known also as the strong subadditivity of von Neumann entropy [4, 3], and of the foremost importance in quantum information theory. It has an interpretation as the amount of correlations, as measured by mutual information, lost between AA and B​CBC when CC gets discarded.
Notice that, if we consider the tripartite state ρA​B′​E=VB→B′​E​ρA​B​VB→B′​E†\rho_{AB^{\prime}E}=V_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}E}\rho_{AB}V_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}E}^{\dagger}, that arises from the isometry step in the implementation of an arbitrary local channel ΛB→B′\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}, the mutual information between AA and B′​EB^{\prime}E is the same as the mutual information between AA and BB, before the action of the channel. On the other hand, the mutual information after the action of the channel, is the mutual information between AA and B′B^{\prime}, after having discarded EE. Thus we see that the fact that conditional mutual information
is non-negative is equivalent to the fact that mutual information is monotone under fully local operations.
III Quantum recoverability
The data processing inequality (16) can be refined, and linked to the issue of the recoverability of the action of the quantum channel Γ\Gamma on ρ\rho [9, 10].
Theorem 3.
Given two states ρ\rho and σ\sigma, and a channel Γ\Gamma, there is a recovery channel R=Rσ,ΓR=R_{\sigma,\Gamma} that depends only on σ\sigma and Γ\Gamma such that
S(ρ||σ)−S(Γ[ρ]∥Γ[σ])≥−log2F2(ρ,(R∘Γ)[ρ])\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)-S(\Gamma[\rho]\|\Gamma[\sigma])\geq-\log_{2}F^{2}(\rho,(R\circ\Gamma)[\rho])
(19)
(R∘Γ)​[σ]=σ.\displaystyle(R\circ\Gamma)[\sigma]=\sigma.
(20)
Notice that the right-hand side of (19) is non-negative, so that indeed (19) constitutes a strengthening of (16). In Theorem 3, the recovery channel RR always recovers σ\sigma from Γ⁡[σ]\Gamma[\sigma] perfectly. On the other hand, how well RR recovers ρ\rho from Γ⁡[ρ]\Gamma[\rho]—that is, how large the fidelity F​(ρ,(R∘Γ)​[ρ])F(\rho,(R\circ\Gamma)[\rho]) can be—depends on the decrease of the relative entropy under the action of Γ\Gamma: the fidelity is large—close to 11—if the decrease in the relative entropy is small, since (19) is equivalent to
F(ρ,(R∘Γ)[ρ])≥2−12(S(ρ||σ)−S(Γ[ρ]∥Γ[σ])).F(\rho,(R\circ\Gamma)[\rho])\geq 2^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(S(\rho||\sigma)-S(\Gamma[\rho]\|\Gamma[\sigma])\right)}.
Notice that the state ρ\rho is perfectly recovered by RR from Γ⁡[ρ]\Gamma[\rho]—that is, the fidelity of (R∘Γ)​[ρ](R\circ\Gamma)[\rho] with ρ\rho is equal to 11—if there is no decrease of the relative entropy. The case of equality in the left-hand side of (19) had already been considered by Petz, who was able to provide an explicit form for a perfect recovery map RP=Rσ,ΓPR^{P}=R^{P}_{\sigma,\Gamma} [11, 12, 13] for such a case:
Rσ,ΓP[τ]=σ1/2Γ†[(Γ[σ])−1/2τ(Γ[σ])−1/2]σ1/2.R^{P}_{\sigma,\Gamma}[\tau]=\sigma^{1/2}\Gamma^{\dagger}\left[(\Gamma[\sigma])^{-1/2}\tau(\Gamma[\sigma])^{-1/2}\right]\sigma^{1/2}.
(21)
Here Γ†\Gamma^{\dagger} is the map dual to Γ\Gamma, i.e., such that Tr⁡(X†​Γ​[Y])=Tr⁡((Γ†​[X])†​Y)\Tr(X^{\dagger}\Gamma[Y])=\Tr((\Gamma^{\dagger}[X])^{\dagger}Y) for all X,YX,Y. In particular, since Γ\Gamma is a channel that admits a Kraus decomposition, Γ⁡[Y]=∑iKi​Y​Ki†\Gamma[Y]=\sum_{i}K_{i}YK_{i}^{\dagger}, with Kraus operators KiK_{i}, then Γ†\Gamma^{\dagger} acts as follows: Γ†​[X]=∑iKi†​X​Ki\Gamma^{\dagger}[X]=\sum_{i}K_{i}^{\dagger}XK_{i}. One verifies that RPR^{P} is completely positive and trace-preserving, hence a channel. In the general case covered by Theorem 3, which considers a non-vanishing decrease in the relative entropy, it has been proven that the recovery channel RR can be chosen to have some close connection with the structure of the Petz recovery channel [9, 10], but a discussion of the present status in the study of the expression of the best recovery map—in the general case of imperfect recovery—goes beyond the scope of these notes.
III.1 Quantum recoverability and mutual information
Given that mutual information is as a special case of relative entropy, it should be no surprise that Theorem 3 can be specialized to the case of mutual information. Actually, Theorem 3 can be seen as a generalization of a theorem previously derived by Fawzi and Renner about mutual information [14].
Theorem 4.
For any state ρ=ρA​B​C\rho=\rho_{ABC} there is a recovery channel RB→B​CR_{B\rightarrow BC} such that
F(ρA​B​C,RB→B​C[ρA​B])≥2−12I(A:C|B)ρ.F(\rho_{ABC},R_{B\rightarrow BC}[\rho_{AB}])\geq 2^{-\frac{1}{2}I(A:C|B)_{\rho}}.
Theorem 4 says that, if correlations between AA and B​CBC do not decrease too much because of the loss (that is, the tracing out) of CC, then the total state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} can be recovered pretty well by means of a channel RB→B​CR_{B\rightarrow BC} acting on ρA​B\rho_{AB}; more in detail, it is possible to choose such a map so that it only depends on ρB​C\rho_{BC}, and not on the full state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} [15].
IV Proof of the no-local-broadcasting theorem
We will use what we recalled about quantum recoverability in Section III to prove the no-local-broadcasting Theorem 2; we will do so by leveraging the no-broadcasting Theorem 1. We will use an intermediate step that one could call the no-unilocal-broadcasting theorem [16, 17].
IV.1 No-unilocal-brodcasting
We say that a bipartite state ρA​B\rho_{AB} can be locally broadcast on BB if there exists a local map ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} such that
ρ~A​B1​B2=(idA⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B]\tilde{\rho}_{AB_{1}B_{2}}=({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}]
(22)
satisfies
ρ~A​B1=ρ~A​B2=ρA​B.\tilde{\rho}_{AB_{1}}=\tilde{\rho}_{AB_{2}}=\rho_{AB}.
(23)
The following holds [16, 17].
Theorem 5.
A bipartite state ρA​B\rho_{AB} can be locally broadcast on BB if and only if ρA​B\rho_{AB} is classical on BB, that is,
ρA​B=∑jpj​ρjA⊗|bj⟩​⟨bj|B\rho_{AB}=\sum_{j}p_{j}\rho^{A}_{j}\otimes|b_{j}\rangle\langle b_{j}|_{B}
(24)
with {|bj⟩B}\{|b_{j}\rangle_{B}\} some orthonormal basis for BB.
In order to prove Theorem 5 we will need the following lemma, for which we provide a more direct proof than the one given in Ref. [17].
Lemma 1.
Any bipartite state ρA​B\rho_{AB} admits a decomposition of the form

(25)
with {pi}\{p_{i}\} a probability distribution, {FiA}\{F^{A}_{i}\} a collection of linearly independent operators on AA, and ρiB\rho^{B}_{i} normalized states on BB.
Notice that the operators {FiA}\{F^{A}_{i}\} in (25) are in general non-positive; this must be the case, because otherwise the lemma would claim that every bipartite state can be written as the convex combination of tensor products of positive operators, while we know that the latter applies—by definition—only to unentangled states.
Proof.
(of Lemma 1) We will consider a minimal informationally complete POVM on AA, which means a collection of linearly independent operators {EiA}\{E^{A}_{i}\} that form a valid POVM, i.e., EiA≥0E^{A}_{i}\geq 0, ∑iEiA=IA\sum_{i}E^{A}_{i}=I_{A}, and at the same time constitute a basis for the space of operators on AA [18]. A minimal informationally complete POVM constitutes a quantum frame [19]. We can then consider a dual frame {Fi}\{F_{i}\} to it, that is, a collection of linearly independent operators such that
XA=∑iTr⁡(Ei​X)​Fi∀X.X_{A}=\sum_{i}\Tr(E_{i}X)F_{i}\quad\forall X.
Then, one has
ρA​B=∑ipi​Fi⊗ρiB,\rho_{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}F_{i}\otimes\rho^{B}_{i},
with
ρiB=1pi​TrA⁡(EiA⊗𝟙B​ρA​B)pi=TrA​B⁡(EiA⊗𝟙B​ρA​B).\rho^{B}_{i}=\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Tr_{A}(E^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\rho_{AB})\quad p_{i}=\Tr_{AB}(E^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B}\rho_{AB}).
(26)
Notice that the ρiB\rho^{B}_{i}’s are normalized states, thanks to the fact that {Ei}\{E_{i}\} is a POVM; for the same reason, {pi}\{p_{i}\} is a valid probability distribution.
∎
We are now in the position to prove Theorem 5.
Proof.
(of Theorem 5) That a state classical on BB can be locally broadcast on BB is trivial. To prove the other direction, let us consider a map ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} that achieves the local broadcasting of ρA​B\rho_{AB} on BB. We are going to prove that ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} broadcasts the individual states ρiB\rho^{B}_{i} of (26); then, the no-broadcasting Theorem 1 will allow us to conclude that all the ρiB\rho^{B}_{i} can be diagonalized in a same basis, which is equivalent to saying that ρA​B\rho_{AB} is classical on BB.
That ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} broadcasts the individual states ρiB\rho^{B}_{i} is true, since we have (we focus on the B1B_{1} output for concreteness, but the same goes for B2B_{2}):
TrB2⁡(ΓB→B1​B2​[ρiB])\displaystyle\Tr_{B_{2}}(\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}}[\rho^{B}_{i}])
=1pi​TrA​B2⁡((EiA⊗𝟙B1​B2)​(idA⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B])\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Tr_{AB_{2}}((E^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B_{1}B_{2}})({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}])
=1pi​TrA⁡((EiA⊗𝟙B1)​ρ~A​B1)\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Tr_{A}((E^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B_{1}})\tilde{\rho}_{AB_{1}})
=1pi​TrA⁡((EiA⊗𝟙B)​ρA​B)\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{i}}\Tr_{A}((E^{A}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}_{B})\rho_{AB})
=ρiB.\displaystyle=\rho^{B}_{i}.
The first equality is due to the definition of conditional state (26), and the fact that the POVM {EiA}\{E_{i}^{A}\} and the map ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} operate on different systems; the second and third equalities are due to the broadcasting conditions. ∎∎
IV.2 No-local-broadcasting
We are now in the position to give a straightforward proof of Theorem 2.
Proof.
(of Theorem 2) That a classical-classical state can be locally broadcast is trivial. To prove the other direction, let us assume that ρA​B\rho_{AB} can be locally broadcast, and let ΛA→A1​A2\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}} and ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} be the locally broadcasting maps, so that ρ~A1​A2​B2​B2=(ΛA→A1​A2⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B]\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{2}}=(\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}] satisfies the broadcasting conditions (11). We will prove that ρA​B\rho_{AB} can also be locally broadcast on both AA and BB, and hence it is classical on both AA and BB, which means it is classical-classical. For the sake of concreteness we will focus on proving that ρA​B\rho_{AB} can be locally broadcast on BB. A similar proof can be followed to prove classicality on AA.
Besides ρA​B\rho_{AB} and ρ~A1​A2​B1​B2\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{1}B_{2}} defined above, it is convenient to consider also
ρ~A​B1​B2′:=(idA⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B].\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{AB_{1}B_{2}}:=({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}].
Notice that it holds
ρ~A1​A2​B1​B2=(ΛA→A1​A2⊗idB)​[ρ~A​B1​B2′].\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{1}B_{2}}=(\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes{\rm id}_{B})[\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{AB_{1}B_{2}}].
The key point is that one goes from ρA​B\rho_{AB}, to ρ~A​B1​B2′\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{AB_{1}B_{2}}, to ρ~A1​A2​B1​B2\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{1}B_{2}} by a sequence of local operations. This, together to monotonicity of mutual information under local operations (including partial trace), implies
I​(A1:B1)ρ~≤I​(A1​A2:B1)ρ~≤I​(A:B1)ρ~′≤I​(A:B1​B2)ρ~′≤I​(A:B)ρ.I(A_{1}:B_{1})_{\tilde{\rho}}\leq I(A_{1}A_{2}:B_{1})_{\tilde{\rho}}\leq I(A:B_{1})_{\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}}\leq I(A:B_{1}B_{2})_{\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}}\leq I(A:B)_{\rho}.
Notice that, because of the broadcasting conditions, the leftmost quantity is actually equal to the rightmost quantity, hence, all the mutual information quantities in the latter equation are equal. In particular, I​(A:B1)ρ~′=I​(A:B)ρI(A:B_{1})_{\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}}=I(A:B)_{\rho}. Moreover,
ρ~A​B1′=(idA⊗ΓB→B1′)​[ρA​B],\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{AB_{1}}=\big({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Gamma^{\prime}_{B\rightarrow B_{1}}\big)[\rho_{AB}],
with ΓB→B1′=(idB1⊗TrB2)∘ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma^{\prime}_{B\rightarrow B_{1}}=({\rm id}_{B_{1}}\otimes\Tr_{B_{2}})\circ\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}}. It follows then from Theorem 4 that there is a recovery channels RB1→B(1)R^{(1)}_{B_{1}\rightarrow B} such that
(idA⊗(RB1→B(1)∘ΓB→B1′))​[ρA​B]=ρA​B({\rm id}_{A}\otimes(R^{(1)}_{B_{1}\rightarrow B}\circ\Gamma^{\prime}_{B\rightarrow B_{1}}))[\rho_{AB}]=\rho_{AB}
One can argue similarly about B2B_{2}. We arrive at the conclusion that there are two channels RB1→B1(1)R^{(1)}_{B_{1}\rightarrow B_{1}} and RB2→B2(2)R^{(2)}_{B_{2}\rightarrow B_{2}} such that
(RB1→B1(1)⊗RB2→B2(2))∘ΓB→B1​B2(R^{(1)}_{B_{1}\rightarrow B_{1}}\otimes R^{(2)}_{B_{2}\rightarrow B_{2}})\circ\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}}
locally broadcasts ρA​B\rho_{AB} on BB. ∎∎
V Brodcasting mutual information
The characterization of recoverability in terms of mutual information captured by Theorem 4 is such—one could say, ‘strong enough’—that the local (or unilocal) broadcasting of quantum states, understood in a structural sense—that is, in terms the density matrices—is fully equivalent to the broadcasting of the correlations contained in the states, as quantified by mutual information. More precisely, one can state a no-go theorem for local broadcasting, which, at least at face value, is more general than Theorem 2.
Theorem 6.
Let ρA​B\rho_{AB} be a bipartite state. There exist local maps ΛA→A1​A2\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}} and ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} such that
ρ~A1​A2​B2​B2=(ΛA→A1​A2⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B]\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{2}}=(\Lambda_{A\rightarrow A_{1}A_{2}}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}]
(27)
satisfies
I​(A1:B1)ρ~=I​(A2:B2)ρ~=I​(A:B)ρA​BI(A_{1}:B_{1})_{\tilde{\rho}}=I(A_{2}:B_{2})_{\tilde{\rho}}=I(A:B)_{\rho_{AB}}
(28)
if and only if ρA​B\rho_{AB} is classical-classical.
This version of the no-local-brodcasting theorem would indeed appears to be more general than Theorem 2 because if ρ~A1​A2​B2​B2\tilde{\rho}_{A_{1}A_{2}B_{2}B_{2}} satisfies the broadcasting conditions (11), then it satisfies also conditions (27), but the opposite is not immediately evident. Nonetheless, it is true—and we know it thanks to Theorem 4. Similarly, one can have an alternative version of the no-unilocal-brodcasting theorem.
Theorem 7.
Let ρA​B\rho_{AB} be a bipartite state. There exists a local map ΓB→B1​B2\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}} such that
ρ~A​B1​B2=(idA⊗ΓB→B1​B2)​[ρA​B]\tilde{\rho}_{AB_{1}B_{2}}=({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Gamma_{B\rightarrow B_{1}B_{2}})[\rho_{AB}]
(29)
satisfies
I​(A:B1)ρ~=I​(A:B2)ρ~=I​(A:B)ρ.I(A:B_{1})_{\tilde{\rho}}=I(A:B_{2})_{\tilde{\rho}}=I(A:B)_{\rho}.
(30)
if and only if ρA​B\rho_{AB} is classical on BB.
VI Quantifying non-classical correlations through broadcasting
So far we have characterized quantum correlations only qualitatively, via no-go theorems. Moreover, we have made use of Theorem 4 only for the case of exact recoverability. In this section, we delineate some ways in which local and unilocal broadcasting can be used to quantify the degree of non-classicality of correlations of the state ρA​B\rho_{AB} under scrutiny. Interestingly, we will connect broadcasting to the well established quantifier of non-classical correlations known as quantum discord. The latter is an asymmetric quantity, based on the notion of minimal loss of correlations, as measured by mutual information, when one tries to ‘extract’ such correlations and map them into a classical register. More explicitly, consider quantum-to-classical channels ℳB→B′​[σB]=∑iTr⁡(Mi​σB)​|i⟩​⟨i|B′\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}[\sigma_{B}]=\sum_{i}\Tr(M_{i}\sigma_{B})|i\rangle\langle i|_{B^{\prime}}, where {Mi}\{M_{i}\} is a POVM on BB, and {|i⟩}\{|i\rangle\} is an orthonormal basis for the ‘classical register’ B′B^{\prime}.
We define ρA​B′′=(idA⊗ℳB→B′)​[ρA​B]\rho^{\prime}_{AB^{\prime}}=({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}})[\rho_{AB}]. Then, the discord of ρA​B\rho_{AB} on BB is equal to
D​(A|B)ρ:=I​(A:B)ρ−maxℳB→B′⁡I​(A:B′)ρ′,D(A|B)_{\rho}:=I(A:B)_{\rho}-\max_{\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}}I(A:B^{\prime})_{\rho^{\prime}},
(31)
Here the maximum is over all quantum-to-classical channels. Notice that discord can be rewritten as
D(A|B)ρ:=minVB→B′​EI(A:E|B′)V​ρ​V†,D(A|B)_{\rho}:=\min_{V_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}E}}I(A:E|B^{\prime})_{V\rho V^{\dagger}},
(32)
where V=VB→B′​EV=V_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}E} is any isometry that realizes a quantum-to-classical channel ℳB→B′\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}. Discord vanishes only for the quantum-classical states [20, 21, 22]; in all other cases, there is a loss of correlations—a measured by quantum mutual information—in the local quantum-to-classical mapping. Nonetheless, one can try to recover ρA​B\rho_{AB} from the quantum-classical state ρA​B′\rho_{AB^{\prime}}, via a recovery channel RB′→BR_{B^{\prime}\rightarrow B}. It is easy to check that, without loss of generality, such a recovery map consists of a preparation procedure, so that the composition of measurement ℳ\mathcal{M} and preparation/recovery RR gives rise to a so-called entanglement breaking map, RB′→B∘ℳB→B′​[σB]=ΛBEB​[σB]=∑iTr⁡(Mi​σB)​τBiR_{B^{\prime}\rightarrow B}\circ\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}[\sigma_{B}]=\Lambda^{\textrm{EB}}_{B}[\sigma_{B}]=\sum_{i}\Tr(M_{i}\sigma_{B})\tau_{B}^{i}, for all σB\sigma_{B}, with {τBi}\{\tau_{B}^{i}\} a collection of states. Let us define [23]
FBEB​(ρA​B):=maxΛBEB⁡F⁡(ρA​B,(idA⊗ΛBEB)​[ρA​B]).F^{\textrm{EB}}_{B}(\rho_{AB}):=\max_{\Lambda^{\textrm{EB}}_{B}}F\big(\rho_{AB},({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Lambda^{\textrm{EB}}_{B})[\rho_{AB}]\big).
(33)
Then, Theorem 4 implies [23]
D​(A|B)ρ≥−2​log2​FBEB​(ρA​B).D(A|B)_{\rho}\geq-2\log_{2}F^{\textrm{EB}}_{B}(\rho_{AB}).
(34)
VI.1 Imperfect structural local broadcasting
Although cloning and broadcasting of general unknown states is not possible, as formalized by the no-cloning and no-broadcasting theorems, one can consider how well the task can be achieved, at least in an approximate sense. This corresponds to the relative large topic of optimal (albeit not perfect) quantum cloners (see, e.g., [24, 25]). The same applies to local or unilocal broadcasting. For example, one can consider the state-dependent 11

VI.2 Imperfect local broadcasting of mutual information
In the same way in which we have cast the no-local-broadcasting and no-unilocal-brodcasting theorem in terms of broadcasting mutual information, so we can approach the issue of approximate broadcasting. We will focus on unilocal broadcasting. Then, for any channel ΛB→B′n\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime n}}, where B′n=B1′​B2′​…​Bn′B^{\prime n}=B^{\prime}_{1}B^{\prime}_{2}\ldots B^{\prime}_{n}, and each B1′B^{\prime}_{1}, B2′B^{\prime}_{2}, …, Bn′B^{\prime}_{n} could potentially have a dimensionality different from that of BB, one can define the average loss of correlations in broadcasting as
I​(A:B)ρA​B−1n​∑i=1nI​(A:Bi′)(idA⊗ΛB→B′n)​[ρA​B]I(A:B)_{\rho_{AB}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(A:B^{\prime}_{i})_{({\rm id}_{A}\otimes\Lambda_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime n}})[\rho_{AB}]}
(36)
The mutual information version of the no-unilocal-broadcasting theorem, Theorem 7, says that such an average loss is strictly positive for all n≥2n\geq 2, if ρA​B\rho_{AB} is not quantum-classical. One proves that such an average loss actually converges to exactly the quantum discord (31), with nn going to infinity [29].
VII Conclusions
It is hard to overestimate the importance of the no-cloning theorem in our understanding of quantum mechanics and quantum information, and the pivotal role it has played in the latter field. For example, born from the attempts to reconcile entanglement with a principle of no-faster than light signalling, it contributed to the development of quantum cryptography. We have seen that the counterpart of no-cloning in the scenario where one considers mixed states is no-broadcasting.
When it comes to distributed system, and to the study of the limitations in the local manipulation of correlations, other no-go theorems can be derived, like the no-local-broadcasting theorem or the no-unilocal-broadcasting theorem. It is worth emphasizing one last time that the latter no-go results apply to single distributed quantum states, contrary to the no-cloning and no-broadcasting theorems, which instead deal with multiple states.
The quantification of the limits in the local manipulation of correlations provide a sound and physically meaningful way to quantify the non-classicality of correlations. Interestingly, this field of research connects directly to recent and exciting advances in our understanding of quantum recoverability. The latter connection can also be exploited for an efficient numerical approach to the quantification of the quantumness of correlations, allowing, for example, the derivation of reliable numerical bounds to quantum discord.
