## 1. Exact interface of the source result

Source Theorem1.1 concerns a **countable discrete group** G and normalized maps μ:G→U(H), for every complex Hilbert space H, including infinite-dimensional spaces. Defect is sup over **all pairs** g,h∈G in the full operator norm. Strong stability asks for correction to a genuine representation on the **same H**, uniformly over **all g**. The source claims that every nonamenable G has, for each δ>0, a separable infinite-dimensional witness with defect<δ and distance>1/10 from every genuine representation. Finite-dimensional Ulam stability is a different property.

Verified source locations: Lemma3.2, PDFpp7–8, prescribed-subspace metric transfer; Lemma3.3,p9, exact fixing by a polar factor; Proposition5.1,pp13–15, pair/triple circuit comparison; Lemma6.2,p17, signed quadratic form. Sourcepp7–8 were also rendered and inspected to remove extraction ambiguity. ## 2. A general layered correction theorem

**Theorem D (our abstraction with heterogeneous errors).** Let H=⊕ₖ₌₀ᴺHₖ. Let Mₖ be unitaries acting only on Hₖ₋₁⊕Hₖ. For k<N, assume Mₖ₊₁ acts only on Qₖ₊₁Hₖ⊕Hₖ₊₁, where Qₖ₊₁ is an orthogonal projection on Hₖ; thus it is the identity on the complementary part of Hₖ. Let Kₖ be unitaries acting only on Hₖ, with

\[
\|K_k-I\|\le t_k<1/2,\qquad
\|(K_k-I)Q_{k+1}\|\le e_k\le1/2,
\]

and set Qᴺ₊₁=0,eᴺ=0. Write W=Mᴺ⋯M¹ and W′=(KᴺMᴺ)⋯(K¹M¹). Then

\[
\|W'-W\|\le\max_k(t_k+3e_k)+3\sum_k e_k. \tag{D}
\]

The bound is independent of dimensions and group-location multiplicities, and it applies to infinite orthogonal direct sums. It is useful when each eₖ is much smaller than tₖ: the principal error has a maximum bound, and only leakage into future inputs is summed.

**Proof.** First fix a single unitary K and projection Q. Relative to QH⊕(I-Q)H write K=(a b;c d). The hypotheses imply ‖b‖,‖c‖≤e, ‖a-I‖≤e, and ‖d-I‖≤t<1. Both K-I on Q and its adjoint compression have the same bound, since (K*−I)Q=−K*(K−I)Q. In particular the entire top row `[a-I b]=Q(K-I)` has norm at most e. Replacing K by diag(I,d) therefore costs at most2e, by adding that row and the lower-left block. The invertible d has polar unitary v=d(d*d)⁻¹/², with d*d=I-b*b; hence ‖v-d‖≤1-√(1-e²)≤e². Thus K̃=I⊕v fixes Q exactly, preserves its complement, satisfies ‖K̃-K‖≤3e, and ‖K̃-I‖≤t+3e. These estimates require no finite dimension.

Apply this construction to each Kₖ. Telescoping unitary products changes W′ by at most3∑eₖ. The corrected K̃ₖ commutes with every later Mⱼ: it is identity on Qₖ₊₁Hₖ and on Hₖ₊₁, and later layers beyond k+1 use disjoint levels. Move all K̃ₖ to the left. They are supported on distinct Hₖ, so

\[
\|\widetilde K_N\cdots\widetilde K_1-I\|
=\max_k\|\widetilde K_k-I\|.
\]

The triangle inequality yields(D). ∎

**Why future-input fixing is essential.** Take one-dimensional levels, Mₖ the full swap of Hₖ₋₁,Hₖ, and Kₖ=eⁱᵗ on Hₖ. The input in H₀ picks up phase eⁱᴺᵗ under W′. For t=π/N, ‖W′−W‖=2 although max‖Kₖ−I‖=2sin(π/(2N))→0. Original supports being orthogonal does not justify the maximum estimate after transport. Here Qₖ₊₁=I, so the small-leakage hypothesis correctly fails. The evidence code evaluates this adversarial example and an exactly fixed-input example.

**Application to source Proposition5.1.** Lemma3.2 provides Kₖ−I=O(h) and (Kₖ−I)Qₖ₊₁=O(hη). Therefore(D) gives O(h)+O(Nhη), with arrival-frame replacement separately costing O(Nη). This reconstructs the source's decisive estimate. The commuting argument relies on the exact support property of the next exchange and the precise priority projection; arbitrary near-unitary products do not satisfy this conclusion.

## 3. A robust finite certificate against representation fitting

The source's exact signed-frame cancellation can tolerate quantified frame error. The following statement is independent of the boundary-action construction.

**Data.** Let I be a finite product label set. For each d∈{1,…,L}, single out a coordinate X_d; write I=X_d×J_d. Suppose uᵢ,rᵢ∈H are unit vectors and sᵢ∈G. On Cᴵ let γ_d=f_d(X_d)Γ_d, where f_d:X_d→{−1,1}, Γ_d is a selfadjoint contraction on Cᴶᵈ, and γ_d preserves the X_d coordinate. For every nonzero (Γ_d)ⱼₖ, require that the corresponding group labels have a common prefix:

\[
s_{(x,j)}=P_{x,j,k}Q_j,\quad s_{(x,k)}=P_{x,j,k}Q_k,
\]

where the suffixes Q_j,Q_k do not depend on x. Suppose also u_(x,j)=E_j a_x, where each E_j:Cᵐᵈ→H is an isometry independent of x, and each a_x is a unit vector. This is the prefix/suffix independence used in source Lemma6.2. Define

\[
R_d=\sum_{x\in X_d}f_d(x)a_xa_x^*,\qquad
\alpha=\sum_d |c_d|\,\frac{\|R_d\|_1}{|X_d|},\quad
D=\sum_dc_d\gamma_d,
\]

where ‖·‖₁ is matrix trace norm, c_d are real, and require ‖D‖≤1. Set r=|I|⁻¹/²∑eᵢ⊗rᵢ, and suppose c=⟨r,(D⊗I)r⟩>α.

**Theorem E (our robust extension).** For any unitary representation ρ:G→U(H), define ψ=|I|⁻¹/²∑eᵢ⊗ρ(sᵢ)uᵢ. Then |⟨ψ,(D⊗I)ψ⟩|≤α. If a map μ is defined at the finite set{sᵢ}, put

\[
e^2=|I|^{-1}\sum_i\|\mu(s_i)u_i-r_i\|^2.
\]

Every unitary representation satisfies

\[
\max_i\|\mu(s_i)-\rho(s_i)\|\ge\frac{c-\alpha}{2}-e. \tag{E}
\]

This is a finite obstruction certificate once its vector, design, and group-word hypotheses are supplied. It does not certify that μ has small defect over all group pairs.

**Proof.** Fix d and ρ. Prefix cancellation replaces ρ(s_(x,j)⁻¹s_(x,k)) by ρ(Q_j⁻¹Q_k) for every contributing pair. Define an isometry V:Cᵐᵈ→Cᴶᵈ⊗H by

\[
Va=|J_d|^{-1/2}\sum_{j\in J_d}e_j\otimes\rho(Q_j)E_ja.
\]

Its norm is one because each E_j and each ρ(Q_j) is an isometry. Thus B_d=V*(Γ_d⊗I)V is a contraction. Expanding the finite label sum and then summing x gives

\[
\langle\psi,(\gamma_d\otimes I)\psi\rangle
=|X_d|^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(B_dR_d).
\]

Trace duality bounds this by ‖R_d‖₁/|X_d|. Summing with c_d proves the α estimate. Both ψ and r have norm one, so the difference of their D expectations is at most2‖ψ−r‖. On the other hand, if ε=maxᵢ‖μ(sᵢ)-ρ(sᵢ)‖, Minkowski's inequality in the orthogonal label sum gives ‖ψ−r‖≤ε+e. Therefore c-α≤2(ε+e), proving(E). ∎

For the source, each sign class is an exact orthonormal basis, R_d=0, c_d=1/√L, D²=I, and c=1/2. Hence the certificate lower bound is1/4−e. Its proof uses only finitely many labelled vectors and genuine word products, despite the full witness μ acting on an infinite-dimensional space. Under imperfect signed designs with ‖R_d‖₁/|X_d|≤τ, one gets α≤√Lτ; a growing L therefore requires a shrinking balance error. This is an explicit precision cost absent from a bare assertion that approximate frames should suffice.

## 4. Costs and limits of the claimed global construction

The source sets N=L+1 and h=1/(2√L); estimates give defect O(h+Nη+Nhη) and transport RMS error O(Nη+Nhη+√(Nβ)). A sufficient parameter scale as δ→0 is L=O(δ⁻²), η=O(δ³), and β=O(δ⁶/log(1/δ)), after fixing universal constants. The last scale comes from the displayed sufficient condition2H(8β)+16βlog9<η²/32. These are sufficient choices for the proof, not lower bounds on all possible constructions.

Alphabet dimensions must be sufficiently large, and the finite lists from the boundary action can be arbitrarily larger than the probabilistic frame bound. No computable group-uniform list-size bound or flag-evaluation oracle is supplied. The local coordinate space is a tensor product of2N+1 alphabets, not a single matrix of polynomial dimension. Moreover H has a copy of every level at every g∈G. Consequently the theorem does not give an efficient finite-matrix algorithm for discovering nonamenability or evaluating the witness from an arbitrary presentation.

An elementary necessary sparse-frame bound helps delimit the finite cost. If an m×m orthogonal matrix O has every a×a submatrix norm≤η, averaging squared Frobenius norms over the subsets gives average a²/m. Since each such squared Frobenius norm is at most aη², necessarily a/m≤η². In the two-basis frame condition, taking a=floor(4βm) yields approximately4β≤η² for large m. Once two labels are allowed, the single-entry bound forces m≥η⁻², because some entry of any orthogonal matrix has magnitude≥m⁻¹/². These are universal algebraic constraints; the source's stronger entropy requirement is its chosen sufficient construction.

The flags themselves force each |Xₖ| and |Yₖ₋₁| to be at least1/β. For any departure and x, (F2) gives an E edge; at its arrival, (F3) bounds the number of E labels by β|Xₖ|, so that integer bound is at least1. Conversely, (F3) gives an F edge at every arrival and y because β<1; (F2) at its departure forces β|Yₖ₋₁|≥1. The final local coordinate dimension is therefore at least

\[
\prod_{k=1}^N(|X_k|/2)\prod_{d=0}^{N-1}|Y_d|
\ge (1/(2\beta^2))^N.
\]

For the sufficient scales above, this is exp(Ω(δ⁻²log(1/δ))) before accounting for boundary-list enlargement, all group locations, or certificate acquisition. This lower bound is for this flag/tensor mechanism at those parameter choices; it is not an impossibility theorem for every strong-Ulam witness construction.

## 5. Prior art and best next work

[Burger–Ozawa–Thom, *On Ulam stability*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0565v1),4 October2010 / Israel J.Math.193(2013),109–129, distinguishes finite-dimensional and strong stability and supplies the free-subgroup obstruction. Its verified abstract and the introductory theorem passage were read; no full prior-proof audit is claimed. The release's innovation is the claimed extension to all nonamenable countable groups, via boundary flags and the dimension-free priority transfer. TheoremD is an explicit abstract repackaging; TheoremE adds a quantified robustness interface. Their proof status is local and independent of the global nonamenability input.

Concrete use: apply(D) to a sequential isometry/compiler design when each correction nearly fixes the next active input; apply(E) to reject an attempted exact unitary-representation fit using a finite algebraic certificate. Neither tool creates the required boundary flags, balanced frames, or a small global defect from sampled data.
