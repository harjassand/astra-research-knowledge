# Constructive matching geometry and an explicit rational rounder

## Verdict

The release item linked as result 113 contains two different papers. The structural paper, *Entropy and Face Dimension of the Perfect-Matching Polytope*, proves a sharp supported-face codimension estimate by gluing directions across tight odd cuts, then turns that rank bound into a maximum-entropy inequality through a covariance second-variation argument. That proof does not construct an entropy-maximizing law or a rounding algorithm. The companion paper, *A Fully Polynomial Randomized Approximation Scheme for Perfect Matchings in General Graphs*, is a separate weighted-chain sampler and counting algorithm; its interface and proof architecture are recorded in `sources/PRIMARY_SOURCES.md` so the two are not conflated.

For an explicit family, I give an exact rational sampler on a disjoint union of (r) copies of (K_{2,2}). It accepts arbitrary rational edge marginals and one supplied scalar feature row, returns one perfect matching, preserves every actual edge marginal exactly in its induced law, and bounds every output's scalar feature error by (2\max_i|a_i|\le2). The routine uses only (O(r)) rational dependent-rounding steps and no optimization or separation oracle. The corresponding arbitrary-marginal **vector**-feature law remains the missing service; centered vector features reduce to Komlós signing and are covered by the current rational algorithm of Li.

Status: the source theorems below are source-reported; the face-geometry reconstruction and the scalar rounding theorem are internally derived here; the implementation was exercised on one rational fixture. This is not independent validation of the source papers or a novelty/priority claim.

## Release mechanism reconstructed

The pinned source is OpenAI Math commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`. The entropy paper considers a loopless labelled multigraph (G=(V,E)) with (|V|=2m\ge2), perfect-matching polytope (P(G)), and a feasible edge marginal (x\in P(G)). Write

\[
F(x)=-\sum_{e\in E}x_e\ln x_e,\qquad
B(x)=-\sum_{e\in E}(1-x_e)\ln(1-x_e),
\]

and let (H(x)) be the maximum Shannon entropy among all laws on perfect matchings with mean incidence vector (x). Its Theorem 1.1 states

\[
F(x)-(2-2/m)B(x)\le H(x)\le F(x),
\]

including boundary marginals. The upper bound is Shearer. The lower bound has two linked steps.

First, Edmonds' odd-cut description identifies the minimal face (F_x) and its tangent space (T(x)). Every law with mean (x) is supported on matching atoms in (F_x), and

\[
|\operatorname{supp}x|-\dim F_x\le3m-2.
\]

For the induction proving this bound, choose a nontrivial tight odd cut (C=\delta(S)). Contract either shore to obtain two smaller even graphs. Matching laws on the two contractions glue if their (C)-coordinates agree: draw one common crossing edge from the shared marginal vector, then independently draw the two conditioned contracted matchings and splice them. For face directions, each contracted direction has zero coordinate sum on (C), from the degree equation at its contracted vertex. Thus the compatibility map takes values in the ((|C|-1))-dimensional zero-sum subspace, costing at most (|C|-1) dimensions. With

\[
n_1+n_2=2m+2,\qquad s_1+s_2=|E|+|C|,
\]

this gives (d(x)\ge d_1+d_2-|C|+1) and hence parent codimension at most the sum of child codimensions minus one. This saved condition closes the recurrence at (3m-2). If there is no tight nontrivial odd cut, the degree equations alone give the required rank bound. The paper also supplies a laminar-rank proof in Appendix A.

Second, the maximum-entropy law on the minimal face is a positive exponential-family law. Its covariance (K) has image (T(x)) and rank (d=\dim F_x). At a fractional local maximum of (F-cB-H), differentiating exponential tilts within that same face and summing the second-variation inequalities over the positive eigenvalues of (K) gives

\[
0\ge d-s+(c+1)m,\qquad s=|E|.
\]

Using (d\ge s-3m+2) contradicts a positive maximum for (c>2-2/M), where (2M) is the original vertex count and the reduced fractional face has (2m\le2M) vertices. Taking (c\downarrow2-2/M) proves the lower entropy bound. This is a geometric/analytic existence proof; it does not provide a polynomial-time oracle for (H(x)), sample from its maximizing law, or round (x) with feature control.

The companion FPRAS paper has a different contract: for a finite simple graph and rational (0<\epsilon<1,0<\delta<1/2), it returns a rational estimate of the unweighted perfect-matching count with relative error (\epsilon) and failure probability (\delta), returns zero with certainty when the count is zero, and has worst-case bit time polynomial in input length, (\epsilon^{-1}), and \(\log\delta^{-1}\). Its sampler operates on a constructed weighted colored graph, with a subdivision, tree bags, marked edges, and a replicated-tier product chain. It is not the entropy paper's covariance proof and does not solve the hard-feature marginal-rounding interface studied below.

## Explicit family and exact input/output contract

Let (G_r=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^r K_{2,2}), where block (i) has vertices (L_{i,0},L_{i,1},R_{i,0},R_{i,1}), diagonal matching (D_i=\{L_{i,0}R_{i,0},L_{i,1}R_{i,1}\}), and off-diagonal matching (O_i=\{L_{i,0}R_{i,1},L_{i,1}R_{i,0}\}). Every perfect matching is identified with a bit vector (z\in\{0,1\}^r), selecting (D_i) for (z_i=1) and (O_i) for (z_i=0). Hence (P(G_r)\cong[0,1]^r).

The input is:

- rational (p_i\in[0,1]); the four actual edge marginals in block (i) are (p_i,p_i,1-p_i,1-p_i), with (p_i) on the diagonal edges;
- a rational scalar (a_i\in[-1,1]) per block;
- one feature coefficient on each actual graph edge: (+a_i/2) on the two diagonal edges and (-a_i/2) on the two off-diagonal edges;
- an exact `randbelow(q)` primitive returning a uniform integer from (0) to (q-1).

For a matching (z), its feature discrepancy from the supplied mean is

\[
\langle A,\mathbf1_M-x\rangle
=2\sum_{i=1}^r a_i(z_i-p_i).
\]

The code [k22_scalar_marginal_sampler.py](k22_scalar_marginal_sampler.py) returns one explicit perfect matching. Over its exact rational transition probabilities, every sampled output obeys

\[
\mathbb E z_i=p_i\quad(1\le i\le r),\qquad
|\langle A,\mathbf1_M-x\rangle|\le2\max_i|a_i|\le2.
\]

Thus the law has the prescribed marginal on **each of the (4r) actual graph edges**. At an interior point, `supp x` is all (4r) edges. At (p_i\in\{0,1\}), the two forced edges in that block are still respected. Every graph edge belongs to some perfect matching. In the polytope itself each cube edge changes one block coordinate, and the difference of its endpoint matching incidences is supported on exactly the four actual edges of that (K_{2,2}) block.

### Proof of the sampler guarantee

Maintain (z\in[0,1]^r), initially (z=p). While at least two coordinates (i,j) are fractional, choose the first two in a fixed order. If not both (a_i,a_j) vanish, set (d_i=a_j,d_j=-a_i); otherwise set (d_i=1,d_j=0). In either case the move direction is nonzero and (a_i d_i+a_jd_j=0). Let ([t_-,t_+]) be the exact rational interval for which the two updated coordinates remain in ([0,1]). Since a changing coordinate is fractional, (t_-<0<t_+). Choose (t_-) with probability (t_+/(t_+-t_-)) and (t_+) with probability (-t_-/(t_+-t_-)), then update ((z_i,z_j)\leftarrow(z_i,z_j)+t(d_i,d_j)).

The probabilities are rational, have mean step zero, and preserve (a\cdot z) on each branch. Each coordinate's conditional expectation is unchanged. At either endpoint at least one of (z_i,z_j) becomes integral, so there are at most (r-1) pair steps. If one fraction (z_k) remains, round it to one with probability (z_k) and zero otherwise. The final move preserves its conditional mean and changes (a\cdot z) by at most (|a_k|\le\|a\|_\infty). Therefore

\[
|a\cdot(z_{\rm out}-p)|\le\|a\|_\infty,
\]

and multiplying by two gives the claimed feature bound. Conditional-expectation preservation at every step yields (\mathbb E z=p), so both diagonal and off-diagonal edge marginals are exact. No asymptotic or numerical approximation enters the proof.

### Oracle, precision, cost, and output

There is no optimization/separation call. Membership of a proposed (x) in this product matching polytope is checked by the block edge equalities and (0\le p_i\le1), in (O(r)) exact rational operations. A maximum-weight perfect matching also separates blockwise: compare the diagonal-pair edge-weight sum with the off-diagonal-pair sum in each block, using (O(r)) exact additions/comparisons. The sampler itself uses neither service.

For (r) blocks and total binary input length (L), one sample uses at most (r-1) pair updates and one final Bernoulli draw, with rational arithmetic only. Along a path, each update merges at most two active rational expressions and eliminates at least one of them; each input coefficient can enter at most (r) such merges. A conservative bound is (O(rL)) bits per active rational/probability. Thus there are (O(r)) rational updates on (O(rL))-bit operands; using schoolbook integer arithmetic, a conservative arithmetic bound is (O(r^3L^2)) bit operations. Rejection from (k=\lceil\log_2q\rceil) fair bits implements `randbelow(q)` with acceptance probability at least (1/2), hence expected fewer than (2k) bits per draw and (O(r^2L)) expected random bits in total. This is an expected-random-bit Las Vegas implementation; rejection has no finite worst-case bit bound. The output is one list of (2r) labelled graph edges, encoded in (O(r\log r)) bits. The law is represented implicitly by the sampler, not by an enumerated list of all supported matchings; its support can be exponential.

## Counterexample to a decomposition-transfer claim

At the center (p_i=1/2), take (a_i=1) for every block. The maximum-entropy law is the uniform product law on all (2^r) matchings: any joint law with these one-coordinate means has entropy at most (r\ln2), with equality at independence. Its all-diagonal atom has feature error (r), and its all-off-diagonal atom has error (-r). Thus the claim “maximum entropy plus exact marginals implies every supported matching has (O(1)) feature error” is false, with a sharp linear witness.

The same witness applies to a valid greedy Birkhoff-von Neumann subtraction on the corresponding block-diagonal doubly stochastic matrix: subtract the global all-diagonal permutation with coefficient (1/2), then the global all-off-diagonal permutation with coefficient (1/2). Both atoms have error (r), although for even (r) a balanced sign assignment gives a two-atom exact-marginal law with zero error. This refutes safety of that decomposition rule for feature control; it does not say every decomposition is bad.

## Centered vector case and the unresolved gate

For (v_i\in\mathbb Q^d) with (\|v_i\|_2\le1), put (+v_i/2) on the two diagonal edges and (-v_i/2) on the two off-diagonal edges. At the center, any matching has feature discrepancy \(\sum_i\sigma_i v_i\). Li's primary result, Theorem 1.1 of arXiv:2609.30044v1, gives a deterministic signing with \(\|\sum_i\sigma_i v_i\|_\infty<99\), in (O(r^2\log r)) updates and (O(dr+r^{\omega+2}\log^3r)) real arithmetic/comparison operations; rational inputs have polynomial bit complexity. The antipodal pair of matchings ((\sigma,-\sigma)), each with probability (1/2), has exact center marginals and the same bound on both atoms. Thus the centered multifeature K2,2 case is algorithmically reducible to a primary Komlós-signing theorem.

The missing service is the arbitrary-marginal vector analogue: given rational (p\in[0,1]^r), rational columns (v_i\) of Euclidean norm at most one, and (\mu_i=2p_i-1), construct in polynomial bit time a compact rational law on sign vectors with (\mathbb E\sigma=\mu) and with every supported atom satisfying

\[
\left\|\sum_i(\sigma_i-\mu_i)v_i\right\|_\infty\le C
\]

for an absolute (C). Li's theorem outputs one signing; it supplies no law with prescribed nonzero mean. The one-row sampler above proves this contract with (C=2) for scalar columns, but I have not proved it for vector columns. The exact K2,2 family removes matching optimization, odd-cut separation, and representation-acquisition as excuses: this mean-preserving multifeature law is the remaining service.

\[
p=(1/7,2/5,3/4,5/9,1/3),\qquad
a=(1/2,-2/3,1/4,3/5,-1),
\]

using Python 3.14.3 with a seeded `getrandbits` test fixture passed through the exact rejection routine. It returned choices ((0,0,0,1,1)), ten actual edges, and exact discrepancy (-659/840\), within the proved bound 2. This checks one executable path and exact output encoding only; the seeded fixture does not validate randomness quality, and the universal claim rests on the algebraic proof above. The implementation is a sampler, not an explicit polynomial-support decomposition output, and uses an ideal uniform-integer interface whose fair-bit implementation has expected rather than worst-case random-bit cost.
