Optimal adaptive sampling for a symmetric
two-state continuous time Markov chain
Jon Michel∗
February 20, 2018
Abstract
We consider the optimal sampling times for a symmetric two-state
continuous time Markov chain. We ﬁrst consider sampling times of
the form ti = iτ and ﬁnd the optimal τ to minimize the asymptotic
variance of our estimated parameter. This optimalτ depends upon the
true unknown parameters and so it is infeasible in practice. To address
this, we consider propose an adaptive scheme which we requires no
knowledge of the true underlying parameter, we show that this method
is asymptotically equivalent to the optimal ﬁxed time design.
Keywords: continuous time Markov chain, optimal design, martingales
1 Introduction
We consider the following problem: X(t), t∈ R, is a symmetric two-state
continuous time Markov chain1 and we can chooseK time periods (t1,....,t K)
at which we will observeX(ti). We are interested in determining how to select
ti so as to optimally infer the underlying parameter of X(t).

In the second section we will restrict our attention to the setting where
ti = iτ for some τ ∈ (0,∞). The optimal choice of τ will depend upon
the true, unknown, parameter of X(t), and as such the optimal sampling
scheme will be infeasible to a researcher. This issue is common in problems
of optimal experimental design, see, for example, Chernoﬀ (1953).
We address this issue in the third section by proposing a sampling scheme
where sampling times ti are allowed to depend upon past observations. Our
suggested scheme, which requires no knowledge of the true parameter, is
then shown to be asymptotically equivalent to having sampled at the opti-
mal ﬁxed time frame. This sort of adaptive method is similar to those found
in stochastic approximation problems, see, for example, Anderson and Taylor
(1976), Lai and Robbins (1982), Robbins and Monro (1951), and Kiefer and
Wolfowitz (1952), however a major diﬀerence here is that our adaptive sam-
pling time will not be a linear function of observations and past sampling
times. This question is also somewhat reminiscent of questions related to
sequential analysis, see Chernoﬀ (1959), for example, and the mathematical
techniques we use will reﬂect this.
This question is motivated by Hong, Li, and Wang (2015) who study the
implications that time aggregated data has on the estimation of dynamic
discrete choice models. These authors consider optimal estimation periods
for a discrete time Markov chain, and ﬁnd analytical results for a speciﬁc
form of Markov chains and provide numerical results for more general cases.
We extend this work by considering the analogous question for continuous
time Markov chains. This is motivated by recent work on continuous time
dynamic choice models, such as those found in Arcidiacono, Bayer, Blevins,
and Ellickson (2016). In that setting time aggregated data is also an issue,
perhaps more so since these models do not have a minimal time period that
can be considered.
In the statistics literature the question of designing optimal experiments
for Markov processes has been given little attention, some exceptions are
Ehrenfeld (1962), Becker and Kersting (1983), and Pagendam and Pollett
(2010). There authors mostly consider the optimal design for either birth
or death processes. In addition, their sampling times ( t1,...,t K) must be
speciﬁed ahead of time and are not allowed to depend upon new information,
such as prior X(ti), unlike our sampling scheme considered in section three.

4 Conclusion
In this paper we addressed the problem of how often a symmetric two-state
continuous time Markov chain should be sampled. We showed that the opti-
mal time, T (α), depended upon the knowledge of the, yet, unknown param-
eterα, as is common in many questions on optimal experimental design. We
proposed a sampling scheme that was asymptotically equivalent to the ﬁxed
time design, yet did not require knowledge of α. This approach to optimal
sampling could, likely, be extended to other Markov processes, such a birth
and death processes.
