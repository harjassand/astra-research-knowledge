# Source notes: energy-aware feedback control

All source facts below were checked against primary paper manuscripts / arXiv pages on 2026-10-08. The notes record source-reported statements and their exact interface; they do not establish the papers' independent correctness or priority.

## Tran, Ngo, Das (2026), arXiv:2607.08119v1

Primary: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08119

- PDF pp. 1-2, lines 105-159: deterministic LTI plant `Xdot=AX+Bu`, sensor switch `z`, cases where control is tied to sensing; general integral cost.
- PDF p. 3, lines 409-416 and p. 4, lines 473-496: they state that a `u'Ru` term can be included, but their simulations find TPBVP convergence complicated and leave this for future investigation.
- PDF pp. 5-6, lines 897-964: the first-order model has known `a,b,x0`, fixed feedback gain `u=-kzx`, and objective `integral[(q1+q2z)x^2+sz]`; `q2 z x^2` is identified as actuator input-cost representation.
- PDF pp. 6-7, lines 1004-1112: Proposition 2 gives the infinite-horizon fixed-gain, one-switch formula under open- and closed-loop stability. Its switching cost is time-active sensing `s integral z dt`.
- PDF p. 2, lines 74-94: the paper claims there is no rigorous study directly incorporating sensing-cost into optimal control. This broad statement conflicts with the primary papers below.
- Limitation: the scalar result is for a deterministic known initial condition and fixed gain; sensor availability is coupled to control availability in the relevant mode. There is no process-noise/measurement-noise information acquisition cost in that model.

## Molin and Hirche (MTNS 2010), "Optimal Event-triggered Control under Costly Observations"

Primary: https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1081989/972955.pdf

- PDF p. 2, equations (1)-(3), lines 111-178: stochastic Itô dynamics with Brownian disturbance, sensor scheduler with complete state information, controller gets transmissions, known plant statistics, and objective `E[integral(x'Qx+u'Ru)dt + lambda k_T]`.
- PDF pp. 2-4, Theorem 1, lines 201-216 and 422-450: optimal controller is time-varying LQG feedback on conditional state estimate; estimation error is the controlled jump-diffusion; observation policy optimizes quadratic estimation error cost plus per-transmission price.
- Direct relevance: both observation resource use and actuator effort are charged. Difference from our degeneracy lemma: Brownian process noise makes the controller-side state prediction imperfect, and sensor updates have value.

## Tzoumas, Carlone, Pappas, Jadbabaie (IEEE TAC 2020), "LQG Control and Sensing Co-Design"

Primary: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08376

- PDF pp. 1-3, lines 247-322: noisy linear Gaussian state system, noisy sensor outputs, each sensor has a cost representing e.g. power/bandwidth, and LQG objective includes both expected state quadratic costs and actuator-input quadratic costs.
- PDF pp. 3-4, theorem 1, lines 358-414: for a fixed sensor set, optimal controller design separates from sensor-set selection through Kalman estimation and Riccati control.
- Direct relevance: prior formal LQG co-design with sensor power and actuator energy; difference: finite sensor-set selection over finite horizon, not a continuous time-on sensor variable.
