The classical proof uses the equality D(p||sigma)=E_sigma[f log f] for
f=p/sigma. A naive matrix replacement uses f=sigma^(-1/2)rho sigma^(-1/2)
and Tr[sigma f log f]. This quantity is NOT the supplied Umegaki
  D(rho||sigma)=Tr[rho(log rho-log sigma)].
In fact it can diverge while genuine D stays bounded in FIXED dimension two.

For integer n>=2 let delta=2^(-n), sigma=diag(1-delta,delta), and
  |phi>=(sqrt(1-1/n),sqrt(1/n))^T, rho=|phi><phi|.
Genuine entropy is exactly
  D(rho||sigma)=ln2-(1-1/n)ln(1-delta)
              <=ln2+delta/(1-delta)<2.
The positive relative-density f is rank one with its nonzero eigenvalue
  a=<phi|sigma^(-1)|phi>
    =(1-1/n)/(1-delta)+2^n/n.
Since Tr sigma f=1, its weighted relative-density entropy is
  Tr[sigma f log f]=ln a>=n ln2-ln n ->infinity.

This remains true for explicit FAITHFUL tested states. Put eta=delta^2 and
  rho_eta=(1-eta)rho+eta sigma>0,
  f_eta=(1-eta)f+eta I.
Convexity of genuine relative entropy gives
  D(rho_eta||sigma)<2.
If P is the rank-one projection onto sigma^(-1/2)|phi>, then
Tr sigma P=1/a. The two eigenvalues of f_eta are
  a_eta=(1-eta)a+eta, eta.
Hence exactly
  Tr[sigma f_eta log f_eta]
    =(a_eta/a)ln a_eta+eta(1-1/a)ln eta
    >=(1-eta)ln a-h_2(eta)
    >=(15/16)(n ln2-ln n)-ln2 ->infinity.
Here a>=1, eta<=1/16, and h_2<=ln2. The first lower bound uses
ln a_eta>=ln a+ln(1-eta) and keeps the sign of ln eta correctly.
