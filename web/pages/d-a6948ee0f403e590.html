LEAF 19 — N129 / N47 hyperbolic group-ring literature audit
Date: 2026-10-08 (Australia/Brisbane)

DECISION
The proposed N129 conclusion is incompatible with a published Bartels–Lück–Reich (BLR) theorem. Their hyperbolic-groups paper proves the prime-characteristic Kaplansky idempotent conclusion for a field of characteristic p whenever every finite subgroup of G is a p-group. A torsion-free word-hyperbolic G satisfies these hypotheses for F_2: its finite subgroups are trivial, hence 2-groups, and the field case requires no soficity. The result says the only idempotents in F_2[G] are 0 and 1.

N129 claims elements a,b,c in F_2[G] with ab=1, ac=0, c!=0, while claiming G torsion-free word-hyperbolic. These algebraic identities imply a nontrivial idempotent e=1-ba: (ba)^2=b(ab)a=ba, so e^2=e; ba!=1 because otherwise c=b(ac)=0; and ba!=0 because a(ba)b=(ab)^2=1. Thus e!=0 and e!=1. BLR's theorem rules this out. This is an incompatibility among N129's stated assumptions/conclusion and an external-established result; it does not establish that BLR is false, and it does not certify N129's conditional proof.

EXACT PRIMARY RESULT AND SCOPE
1. Arthur Bartels, Wolfgang Lück, Holger Reich, “The K-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjecture for hyperbolic groups,” Inventiones Mathematicae 172(1) (2008), 29–70, DOI 10.1007/s00222-007-0093-7. Primary full-text copy: https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/blr.pdf ; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701434 . The arXiv record dates the first submission 2007-01-16; the PDF records receipt 2007-03-05 and revised receipt 2007-10-14; journal publication is 2008.

The paper's main theorem says every word-hyperbolic group satisfies the K-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjecture with coefficients (in particular, arbitrary coefficient rings). Its applications section states: if R is a field of prime characteristic p, or if R is a skew-field of prime characteristic p and G is sofic, and every finite subgroup of G is a p-group, then RG satisfies the Kaplansky conjecture: 0 and 1 are the only idempotents. The sofic condition is only in the skew-field alternative, not the field alternative. Torsion-free gives that every finite subgroup is trivial. Taking R=F_2 therefore applies directly to N129's claimed G.

2. Independent explicit confirmation in the same authors' companion: Bartels, Lück, Reich, “On the Farrell–Jones Conjecture and its applications,” Journal of Topology 1(1) (January 2008), 57–86, DOI 10.1112/jtopol/jtm008. Publisher page reports first publication online 2007-10-28: https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/jtopol/jtm008 ; primary full text: https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/blr2.pdf ; arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703548 . Its Theorem 1.12 is a general idempotent theorem requiring K_0(R)⊗Q -> K_0(RG)⊗Q to be an isomorphism and, in addition, one of (i) RG stably finite, (ii) R a characteristic-zero field, or (iii) R a skew-field and G sofic. That theorem alone does not supply the F_2 case for an unknown nonsofic ring. However, immediately afterward, §1.6 reports Formanek's prime-characteristic result for a field F: no p-torsion and no nonidentity g conjugate to g^(p^k) for k>=1; it says torsion-free word-hyperbolic groups meet those conditions, hence all idempotents in FG are trivial for such G and any field F. So the apparent gap from the generic Theorem 1.12 is closed by a distinct hyperbolic/field result in the same published paper.

The primary hyperbolic paper's relevant statement is in the “Kaplansky Conjecture for prime characteristic” application (PDF page 3; printed journal pagination begins at 29). The companion's generic Theorem 1.12 is printed at p. 62; its Formanek/hyperbolic paragraph is printed at pp. 63–64. Source access/verification date for both: 2026-10-08.
