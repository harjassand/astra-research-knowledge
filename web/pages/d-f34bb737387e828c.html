# Exact means force face membership: a directly reconstructed boundary

Status: root mathematical reconstruction of elementary statements reported in the supplied handoff; no novelty claim. This does not prove D's constructive upper bound. It is a useful constraint on proposed downstream applications.

Let P=conv(V), V subset {0,1}^n, and let a probability law on V have mean y. If a supporting functional c has c^T x <= h on P and c^T y=h, then the nonnegative random variable h-c^T X has expectation zero. Every positive-probability atom therefore belongs to the exposed face {x:c^T x=h}. The same holds for the minimal face of y by taking a functional exposing that face.

If [u,v] is an actual edge and y=(u+v)/2, all atoms must be on this edge. There are no other Boolean points in its relative interior: on any coordinate where u and v differ, a strict convex combination lies strictly between 0 and 1. The law is thus exactly one-half at each endpoint. Set the one-row matrix A_j=sign(u_j-v_j), with zero on equal coordinates. Every column has Euclidean norm at most one. Then A(u-y)=|supp(u-v)|/2 and A(v-y)=-|supp(u-v)|/2. Consequently any universal exact-marginal hard-discrepancy bound is at least k(P)/2. This is an exact obstruction, independent of algorithmic access.

For any nonvertex face F, its edge directions span its tangent space. One justification is connectivity of its vertex-edge graph: differences from a fixed vertex are sums of path-edge directions, while vertex differences span the affine hull. Select dim(F) linearly independent edge directions. Every varying coordinate of F occurs in at least one selected direction, since their span is the full tangent space. Each direction has at most k(P) nonzero coordinates. Thus N(F) <= k(P) dim(F). An edge attaining k(P) has dimension 1 and N(F)=k(P), so

    min_{nonvertex faces F} dim(F)/N(F) = 1/k(P).

The singleton case must be separated; the minimum is then undefined, and rounding is trivial.

## Two simple constraints can create a long edge

Take the even cycle C_(2r), r>=2. On its edge set, form one partition matroid from the incident edges at each odd vertex and another from the incident edges at each even vertex. A common base is exactly a perfect matching. There are precisely two: the two alternating matchings u,v. Their convex hull is a single edge with support 2r, whereas each individual matroid base polytope has edges of support 2.

At y=(1/2,...,1/2), exact marginal preservation forces the equal mixture of those two matchings. The alternating one-row feature A has unit-norm columns and discrepancy r on both outcomes. Thus no dimension-independent hard discrepancy bound can be imported from either separate matroid to arbitrary matroid intersection. Efficient optimization over this particular intersection does not help: the obstruction is geometric, not computational.

Concrete use in this programme: this criterion rejects the unqualified proposal to apply D to a joint constrained experimental design by treating its component matroids separately. Any positive application must inspect the actual joint polytope, relax exact marginals, relax its hard bound, or add structure excluding these long edges. This is intake screening, not an achieved scientific-design improvement.
