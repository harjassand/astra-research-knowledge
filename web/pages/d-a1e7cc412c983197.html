This fresh investigation established no transformative technological capability,
measured physical improvement, or historically original invention. It produced
one conditional control construction with a precise model advantage, a confined
metal-export architecture whose essential physical interface remains missing,
and exact obstructions to two attractive acceleration claims.

\section{Geometric protection: the strongest conditional principle}
\label{sec:control}

\subsection{Exact model and a scalar matched-baseline obstruction}

Consider a prepared target and one initially resonant accepting transition.
Its coupling is $J$ and its memory kernel is $e^{-\kappa s}$, $\kappa>0$.
All frequencies are angular frequencies. A differential level shift $q(t)$
gives $u(t)=e^{-i\theta(t)}$, $\theta'=q$. Assume a stationary or long-time
averaged autocorrelation $C$, $C(0)=1$, and a mean-square derivative with
$\langle q^2\rangle\le A^2$.
Bochner's theorem and Parseval give a probability spectral measure $\mu$ with
\[
 C(s)=\int e^{-i\omega s}\,d\mu(\omega),\qquad
 \int\omega^2\,d\mu\le A^2.
\]
In the second-order, weak-transfer limit the leakage rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
 \Gamma=2J^2\int\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^2+\omega^2}\,d\mu(\omega).
\end{equation}
This is not an exact formula for arbitrary strong coupling or nonlinear
reactive molecular dynamics. A periodic waveform is included by averaging
its starting phase. The weak-coupling limit observes times of order $J^{-2}$.

\begin{theorem}[Scalar static baseline]\label{thm:scalar}
Every admissible scalar control satisfies
\[
 \Gamma\ge\frac{2J^2\kappa}{\kappa^2+A^2}.
\]
The constant shift $q(t)=A$ attains equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The function $f(x)=\kappa/(\kappa^2+x)$ is decreasing and convex on
$[0,\infty)$. Jensen's inequality in \eqref{eq:overlap} gives
$\int f(\omega^2)d\mu\ge f(\int\omega^2d\mu)\ge f(A^2)$.
A constant shift has one spectral line at $A$.
\end{proof}

Thus scalar noise, synchronization and bandwidth do not improve the resonant
Lorentzian baseline at equal RMS differential shift. For multiple independent
resonant channels driven by one scalar field, the termwise bounds are attained
simultaneously by a static field. This statement does not extend automatically
to vector controls or to a different bath.

A channel with zero differential control response is exactly immune to the
waveform. For example,
\[
 V=J(bc^\dagger d^\dagger+b^\dagger cd),\quad
 H_c/\hbar=E(t)(a_b n_b+a_c n_c+a_d n_d)
\]
commutes with $H_c$ if $a_b=a_c+a_d$. Different single-mode frequencies do not
exclude such a multiphonon channel. Also, integrating \eqref{eq:overlap} over
a perfectly flat continuum of initial detunings leaves total spectral weight
unchanged. A broad bath cannot be replaced by a single accepting line without
evidence.

\subsection{The representation change: unknown orientation, fixed norm}

For one molecular species, suppose the differential linear-Stark vector is
$d_0n$, with $d_0>0$ fixed and $n\in S^2$ unknown but constant during a cycle.
A vector electric field gives
\[
 u_n(t)=e^{-id_0n\cdot\Theta(t)},\qquad \dot\Theta=E(t),\qquad
 \langle|E|^2\rangle\le E_0^2.
\]
The target population is prepared before protection. One common pump capable
of tracking all molecular orientations is not assumed or constructed.
A constant field has isotropic-ensemble leakage
\begin{equation}\label{eq:staticensemble}
 \frac{\Gamma_{\rm static}}{\Gamma_0}
 =\frac{\arctan a}{a},\qquad
 a=\frac{d_0E_0}{\kappa},\quad \Gamma_0=\frac{2J^2}{\kappa}.
\end{equation}
The near-perpendicular orientations cause its $1/a$ asymptotic tail.
This is an orientation-averaged initial rate, not a single ensemble lifetime.
Frozen orientations give $S(s)=\int e^{-\Gamma_ns}d\sigma(n)$.
For a static field the mean lifetime is already
$\int\Gamma_n^{-1}d\sigma=(1+a^2/3)/\Gamma_0$, while its worst-orientation
rate remains $\Gamma_0$. The uniform theorem addresses that vulnerable
orientation, rather than claiming a new mean-lifetime scaling.
Also, Jensen over the joint orientation/filter measure gives
$\Gamma_{\rm avg}/\Gamma_0\ge(1+a^2/3)^{-1}$ for arbitrary vector control.

The new internal construction uses a phase trajectory whose \emph{time}
measure reproduces spherical moments, including the time spent moving.
It does not assume instantaneous jumps between ideal quadrature points.

\begin{lemma}[Transit-compensated positive cubature]\label{lem:transit}
Let $\sigma$ be normalized area measure on $S^2$ and
$\{x_k,w_k\}$ a positive cubature exact through degree $2t$.
Let $\nu$ be the normalized arclength measure of a closed path visiting all
$x_k$. Set
\[
 K_t(x,y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^t(2\ell+1)P_\ell(x\cdot y),\quad
 h_t(x)=\int K_t(x,y)d\nu(y),\quad
 b_k=w_k[1-\delta h_t(x_k)],
\]
where $0<\delta\le[2(t+1)^2]^{-1}$. Then $b_k\ge w_k/2$,
$\sum b_k=1-\delta$, and for every restricted polynomial $p$ of degree
at most $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \sum_k b_kp(x_k)+\delta\int p\,d\nu=\int p\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The spherical reproducing kernel gives
$\int h_tp\,d\sigma=\int p\,d\nu$.
Moreover $|h_t|\le\sum_{\ell\le t}(2\ell+1)=(t+1)^2$.
The product $h_tp$ has degree at most $2t$, so its cubature sum equals its
sphere integral. Subtract $\delta$ times that identity from the cubature
identity for $p$ to obtain \eqref{eq:moments}. Taking $p=1$ proves the total
mass assertion. The kernel bound proves positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit orientation-uniform model protection]\label{thm:vector}
In the stated one-channel weak-transfer model there is a continuous periodic
phase path with finite RMS field and period $T$ such that, uniformly for
all $n\in S^2$,
\begin{align}
 \frac{\Gamma_n}{\Gamma_0}
 &\le4\epsilon_t^2+\frac{\kappa^2}{\kappa^2+\Omega^2},
 &\epsilon_t&=\frac{\pi^{t+1}}{(t+1)!},\quad\Omega=2\pi/T,\label{eq:vectorbound}\\
 \frac{\Omega}{\kappa}
 &\ge\frac{a}{\pi\sqrt2(t+1)(t+2)}.\label{eq:explicitperiod}
\end{align}
The field is bounded and piecewise smooth; its abrupt starts and stops are
removed by the finite-bandwidth construction below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use $t+1$ Gauss--Legendre latitudes and $2t+1$ equally spaced azimuths.
The product rule is positive and exact through degree $2t$: the azimuthal
average kills every nonconstant harmonic of that degree, and the remaining
polynomial in latitude is integrated by Gauss--Legendre.
On a phase sphere of radius $R=\pi/d_0$, traverse each complete latitude ring,
join the rings along one meridian, and return on that meridian. Its length
$L$ is at most $2\pi R(t+2)$.

Spend fraction $\delta=[2(t+1)^2]^{-1}$ of the cycle traversing at constant
speed, and fraction $b_k$ dwelling at $Rx_k$. Lemma~\ref{lem:transit} gives its
time-moment identity. Its RMS field is $L/(T\sqrt\delta)$; choose
$T=L/(E_0\sqrt\delta)$. This proves \eqref{eq:explicitperiod}.
A constant translation makes $\Theta(0)=0$ if required and changes only an
irrelevant orientation-dependent constant phase.

The degree-$t$ Taylor polynomial of $e^{-i\pi n\cdot x}$ has uniform
remainder at most $\epsilon_t$, by the real integral remainder formula.
Its exact spherical average is $\sinc\pi=0$.
The moment identity therefore bounds the zero Fourier coefficient of $u_n$
by $2\epsilon_t$, for every orientation. All other Fourier lines are at
integer multiples of $\Omega$. Apply Parseval and \eqref{eq:overlap} to obtain
\eqref{eq:vectorbound}.
\end{proof}

Choosing $t$ of order $\log a/\log\log a$ gives an upper bound
$O(t^4/a^2)$, eventually below the static ensemble's $O(1/a)$.
This proves that the scalar obstruction does not exhaust the vector
interface. It is a conditional mathematical consequence, not proof of a
new physical effect or a major chemical capability.

\subsection{Finite bandwidth, acquisition and stronger prior mathematics}

Let $\Theta_K$ be the Fej\'er approximation through harmonic $K$, and
$B=K\Omega$. The multiplier is $(1-|m|/(K+1))_+$.
Parseval bounds
\[
 \|\Theta-\Theta_K\|_{L^2}\le\frac{E_0}{\Omega(K+1)}.
\]
The positive normalized Fej\'er kernel contracts the RMS and peak field.
The implemented field is bandlimited. Since
$|e^{ix}-e^{iy}|\le|x-y|$, its zero coefficient is at most
$2\epsilon_t+d_0E_0/[\Omega(K+1)]$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandwidth}
 \frac{\Gamma_{n,K}}{\Gamma_0}
 \le\left(2\epsilon_t+\frac{d_0E_0}{B}\right)^2
       +\frac{\kappa^2}{\kappa^2+\Omega^2}.
\end{equation}
For $2\epsilon_t\le\kappa/\Omega$ and
$B\ge d_0E_0\Omega/\kappa$, this is at most $5(\kappa/\Omega)^2$.
Thus finite bandwidth is available, but it is explicitly costly.

The explicit construction has $O(t^2)$ nodes/stops, peak field
$E_0/\sqrt\delta$, and $O(K)$ Fourier coefficients after filtering.
Numerical synthesis needs charged precision in the nodes, integrals, timing
and delivered field. A differential-phase error bounded by $\eta$ adds a
carrier defect at most $\eta$, giving a contribution
$(2\epsilon_t+\eta)^2\Gamma_0$. Certified algebraic root isolation and
quadrature can approximate the supplied mathematical design; neither acquires
the molecule's actual Stark vectors, bath, rotation or reactive potential.

\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13152}{Ehler--Gr\"ochenig's original design-curve theorem}
already gives $S^2$ design curves of length $O(t)$, improving the existence
scaling to $O(t^2/a^2)$ under constant-speed traversal.
That imported theorem is distinct from our explicit travel-compensation proof;
its existence does not supply a calibrated apparatus.
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01988v1}{Continuous unitary-design control}
also already exists. Its full noncommuting controllability differs from the
orientation-unknown commuting Stark interface here. This distinction is
necessary for a valid comparison and does not establish historical originality.
\href{https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/584082}{Sugawara's 2011 laser construction}
already isolates molecular subspaces against vibrational redistribution.
No superiority over that stronger physical-control family was established.
\href{https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2220852120}{Wilcken et al.'s nanoantenna experiments}
also measure altered vibrational free-induction decays and infer coupled
population-lifetime changes through a constrained oscillator model. That is
an experimental interface with its own limits, not evidence for our waveform
or a direct chemical-yield demonstration.

A reduced-model probe used 200 orientation nodes, 32768 time samples and a
64-harmonic Fej\'er cutoff. The following are \emph{ensemble-grid numerical thresholds for the initial
orientation-averaged rate}, not single-exponential lifetime improvements,
all-orientation certificates or experiments. The degree-2
transit-compensated waveform gives:

\toprule
Leakage reduction & Static $A_{\rm rms}/\kappa$ & Vector $A_{\rm rms}/\kappa$ & Vector $A_{\rm peak}/\kappa$ & $B/\kappa$\\

At $100\times$ the RMS shift is about $1.70\times$ smaller, but the peak shift

The rigorous uniform bound is more conservative than this orientation grid.

No same-species, measured \emph{differential transition} Stark vector and
acceptor kernel satisfying the resonant weak-transfer assumptions was obtained.
Combining a single-mode slope from one species with a linewidth from another
would not calibrate the model. In particular, a strongly coupled, initially
detuned example cannot inherit the table's leakage predictions.
Additional accepting channels have other slope norms; a zero-slope channel
remains immune. Norm spread changes the ideal spherical carrier to
$\sinc(\pi |d|/d_0)$, producing a residual floor. Molecular rotation during a
cycle changes the phase law. Preparation, anharmonic chemistry, competing
reactions, field damage and wall-plug losses remain separate obligations.

A decisive next experiment would first measure the relevant transition slopes,
$J$, detuning and memory kernel in one declared species. Prepare the target
population, apply the recorded three-axis waveform, and measure population
lifetimes with orientation-resolved controls. Compare bias-off, best static
field and the strongest available coherent control under equal peak, RMS,
bandwidth and preparation accounting. A disagreement with the calibrated
population prediction falsifies the model-to-device bridge. Chemical selectivity
requires a subsequent product-yield experiment. No such experiment occurred.

\section{Confined solid-iron export: a physical architecture with missing admission}

\subsection{Mechanism and its demonstrated antecedents}

The desired operation reduces iron upstream of a gangue-retaining boundary,
deconstructs admitted metal at a contact, transports atoms through a confined
path and rebuilds/harvests metal downstream. It seeks to avoid melting gangue,
not merely reproduce fine-ore reduction.
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1302.5709v2}{Coh et al. (2013)} experimentally moved
largely crystalline Fe through smaller CNT constrictions; their surface
reconstruction explanation and possible refining application are prior art.
\href{https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9071}{Melinte et al. (2015)}
demonstrated export onto graphene and reverse admission of prepared external
nanoparticles. Their interface is nearby, conductive and nanoscale. Carbon
reaction, carbide formation and tube changes prevent treating the wall as
inert. Neither result demonstrates raw-ore extraction or bulk throughput.

A permanently continuous, hot 3 nm Fe shell is not a justified material.
\href{https://www.nature.com/articles/srep46725}{Fe-film dewetting on alumina}
is experimentally used to form catalyst particles. The more credible mutation
is an independently conducting capillary with a confined moving/regrowing Fe
plug. It must repeatedly acquire fresh metal from actual reduced ore, retain
source contact as the domain shrinks, reject impurities, harvest downstream
metal and preserve or restart a transport root. Those are required services,
not consequences of surface diffusivity.

\subsection{Governing model and conditional flux}

For capillary radius $r$, active length $L$, open fraction $f$, surface molar
density $\Gamma_s$, diffusivity $D_s$ and temperature $T$, stipulate
\begin{align}
 \mu&=\mu_0+\gamma V_m\mathcal K+V_m\sigma_h+RT\log a_{\rm Fe},\\
 J_{\rm mol}&=\frac{2f}{r}\frac{D_s\Gamma_s}{RT}
          \left[Z^*F E-\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial x}\right].\label{eq:ironflux}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal K$ is mean-curvature sum, compression raises $\mu$, $V_m$ is
molar volume, $F$ is Faraday's constant, and $Z^*$ is effective migration
charge. All circumference active, uniform coefficients, connected metal,
open source/sink and no poisoning are assumed.

The \href{https://doi.org/10.2320/matertrans1960.20.523}{Nii--Yoshihara clean-$\alpha$-Fe measurement}
supplies $D_s=10\exp[-228000/(RT)]\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ in dry purified hydrogen
at 1073--1163 K. It does not measure a dirty ore contact or Fe/CNT interface;
moisture changes the kinetics. We use its law only for optimistic arithmetic.

For $T=1163\,\mathrm K$, $r=L=30\,\mathrm{nm}$, $f=0.2$, a 0.3 nm mobile
atomic layer, assumed $Z^*=10$, a 3 nm conducting Fe shell and assumed
conductivity $10^6\,\mathrm{S/m}$, the model gives:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Conditional quantity & Value\\
\midrule
Clean free-Fe $D_s$ & $5.75\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$\\
Passive flux with maintained $100\,\mathrm{J/mol}$ drop & $22.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}h^{-1}}$\\
Field for $10\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}h^{-1}}$ without opposition & $1.54\,\mathrm{kV/m}$\\
Voltage across active layer & $46.1\,\mu\mathrm V$\\
Projected current density & $64.5\,\mathrm{MA/m^2}$\\

Increasing both $r,L$ from 30 to 100 nm cuts passive flux by about 11.1 and
raises the target-flux active loss to about $3.19\,\mathrm{MWh/t}$ under the
same supplied coefficients. Neither value is a device simulation.
A full Fe plug has larger conducting cross-section and, if one keeps this
same hot free-surface model, about $4.76$ times the thin-shell local loss.
A separately conducting CNT is a different model: its measured current cannot
be combined with the Fe-shell $Z^*$ and free-surface $D_s$ as though all were
one validated interface.

\subsection{Exact failed interfaces}

\paragraph{Admission and recovery.}
Path conservation gives
\[
 \dot N_{\rm path}=S_{\rm feed}-J_{\rm export}-Q_{\rm loss},\qquad
 \int J_{\rm export}\,dt\le N_{\rm path}(0)+\int S_{\rm feed}\,dt.
\]
A short metal burst may simply drain the seed or shell. A gangue cage can admit
reducing gas while containing no metal-transport path to the boundary. In this
admitted counterexample, metallization can be complete and export zero.
Recovery is bounded by the source-connected iron fraction; grade and
metallization alone do not establish 94\% recovery. Fracture, liberation or
surface transport over gangue must be constructed and charged.

\paragraph{Stress, curvature and purity.}
The example's electromigration work is only about $44.5\,\mathrm{J/mol}$.
A hydrostatic difference of about $6.27\,\mathrm{MPa}$ cancels it in
\eqref{eq:ironflux}. Open growing surfaces may relieve stress, but constrained
contacts can stall. An assumed $\gamma=1\,\mathrm{J/m^2}$ gives a spherical
30 nm curvature scale near $473\,\mathrm{J/mol}$; a maintained favorable
$100\,\mathrm{J/mol}$ drop is not automatic. Coarsening or a new narrow entry
can reverse it. A small pore rejects large fragments, not automatically
surface-mobile or dissolved S, P, Ni, Cu, C or scaffold species. Atomwise
reconstruction is not a universal purification theorem.

\paragraph{Electrical topology and collection.}
Let total online area be $A$, projected current density $j$, local voltage $v$,
and product mass flux $m$. Dividing into $N$ equal cells and connecting them
\emph{electrically} in series gives
\[
 I=\frac{jA}{N},\qquad V=Nv,\qquad P=jAv,\qquad \dot M=mA,
\]
provided every cell has independent material feed and product access. Passing
the same atoms through all $N$ cells instead gives $\dot M=mA/N$.
A conducting common product rail shorts differing node potentials.
One concrete escape is isolated batch compartments, de-energized and equalized
before common harvesting. Downtime, duplicate cartridges and service costs
remain within the system boundary.

Contacts of specific resistivity $\rho_c$ and actual area at most $gA/N$
satisfy, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact}
 \frac{P_c}{A}\ge\frac{\rho_cj^2}{g}.
\end{equation}
Series count cancels. A flare can enlarge real contact area only with an
explicit spreading and fabrication construction.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional conductor-volume/storage cost]\label{thm:wire}
Suppose $N_g$ connections in the equal-cell batch architecture each carry
$I=jA/N$ between node planes separated by at least $H$, with conductor
conductivity at most $\sigma$. If their total heat is at most $\eta jAv$, then
\[
 \frac{V_{\rm cond}}{A}\ge
 \frac{jH^2}{\sigma\eta v}\left(\frac{N_g}{N}\right)^2.
\]
If a run lasts $\tau$, feed contains $C_{\rm Fe}$ recoverable Fe mass per
volume, and product density is $\rho_{\rm Fe}$, then in the normal storage-stack
geometry one may substitute
$H\ge m\tau(C_{\rm Fe}^{-1}+\rho_{\rm Fe}^{-1})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every transverse plane of a connection carries net current $I$.
Cauchy--Schwarz over its conducting area, then along the span, gives
$P_lV_l\ge I^2H^2/\sigma$. Summing and applying Cauchy--Schwarz again gives
$P_{\rm total}V_{\rm cond}\ge(N_gIH/\sqrt\sigma)^2$.
Insert the heat allowance. Feed and product storage require respective
heights at least $m\tau/C_{\rm Fe}$ and $m\tau/\rho_{\rm Fe}$; their sum gives
the stated substitution. Branching or detours do not remove the net-plane
current bound within this geometry.
\end{proof}

For illustrative $j=60\,\mathrm{MA/m^2}$, $v=30\,\mu\mathrm V$,
$H=200\,\mu\mathrm m$, $\sigma=10^7\,\mathrm{S/m}$ and $\eta=0.1$, the bound
is about 80 litres of conductor per online square metre when $N_g/N\simeq1$.
It is a specified-geometry lower bound, not a universal no-go for all reactors.
Continuous electrically disconnected product packets might reduce storage
height, but their formation, collection and source isolation are an additional
unproved operation. A $300000$-cell illustrative circuit lowers terminal
current to $200\,\mathrm A$ at $9\,\mathrm V$, while retaining these internal
costs and separate feed/product compartments.

\subsection{Complete energy boundary and a falsifiable milestone}

An illustrative 45\%-Fe hematite-plus-inert-gangue feed at 94\% export recovery
requires $2.364\,\mathrm t$ ore per tonne exported Fe and leaves about
$0.843\,\mathrm t$ gangue. Stoichiometric hydrogen is at least
$54.15\,\mathrm{kg/t}$ if only exported Fe is reduced, and about
$55.87\,\mathrm{kg/t}$ for the declared 97\% metallization.
With the explicit assumed heat capacities and fusion duty in the model,
avoided gangue heating from 890 to $1600^\circ\mathrm C$ plus melting is only
$0.260\,\mathrm{MWh/t\,Fe}$. The optimistic $0.297\,\mathrm{MWh/t}$ active
export electricity already matches that benefit before contacts. Heating ore,
hydrogen generation, gas recycling, membrane manufacture/replacement,
liberation, purity finishing, Fe losses, gangue service and final Fe melting
are additional required costs. This calculation establishes no energy advantage.

The next useful engineering milestone is sustained \emph{feed-derived} atom
admission, rather than another flux extrapolation. On a supported coupon,
isotope-label the oxide Fe, record all seed/collector Fe inventory and meter
all electrical contacts. Controls include bias off/reversed, blocked pores,
preloaded Fe without ore and a prepared pure-Fe source. Require isotope-resolved
export exceeding 100 times accessible preloaded path Fe, repeated replenishment
and harvesting, full Fe/gangue/carbon balance and product impurity analysis.
Then test a declared real untreated fine ore. The 100-fold criterion is a
proposed falsification gate, not a claimed measurement.
If the path only discharges its own Fe, consumes its wall, fails source contact
or co-transports unacceptable impurities, the proposed extraction interface
fails even if nanoscale migration remains real. No such test has been run.

\section{Rare-event acquisition: exact kinetics can remain hidden}

The proposed scientific-computing capability was trustworthy pathway and rate
acceleration without a supplied route, committor or transition-state map.
Automatic basin boosts overlap
\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.78.3908}{Voter's hyperdynamics};
corrected learned proposals already occur in FlowRES. The missing general
operation is not another proposal density, but acquisition of an important
unseen kinetic gate. The following finite-state construction makes that boundary
exact. It is not a universal lower bound on atomistic simulation.

\subsection{Exact oracle contract and construction}

Let $M=2^d$, with known states $A,B,u_j,v_j$ for $j\in\{0,1\}^d$.
The nonzero off-diagonal rates are
\[
 q(A,u_j)=q(B,v_j)=1/M,\quad q(u_j,A)=q(v_j,B)=1,\quad
 q(u_j,v_j)=q(v_j,u_j)=\theta_j>0.
\]
Diagonals make row sums zero. Topology, endpoints and all displayed known
rates are supplied. An adaptive query at any chosen $j$ reveals exact
$\theta_j$, hence both unknown local rows, at unit cost.
Exact sampling of the known equilibrium is free. Arithmetic and randomness
may even be free for the lower bound. Listing $M$ successors costs $M$ outputs
and reveals no $\theta_j$.

Detailed balance gives the same law for every parameter vector:
\[
 \pi(A)=\pi(B)=1/4,\qquad \pi(u_j)=\pi(v_j)=1/(4M).
\]
Write $f(x)=x/(1+2x)$ and $S=\sum_j f(\theta_j)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact route and clock]\label{thm:corridor}
The committor with boundary values $h(A)=0,h(B)=1$, stationary reactive flux,
successful-excursion route law and mean passage time are
\[
 h(u_j)=f(\theta_j),\quad
 h(v_j)=\frac{1+\theta_j}{1+2\theta_j},\quad
 \operatorname{cap}(A,B)=\frac{S}{4M},\quad
 w_j=\frac{f(\theta_j)}{S},\quad \E_A\tau_B=\frac{2M}{S}.
\]
For $s\ge0$, writing
$b(s)=M^{-1}\sum_j\theta_j/(s+1+2\theta_j)$,
\[
 \E_Ae^{-s\tau_B}=\frac{b(s)}{s(s+2)+b(s)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two interior harmonic equations are
$(1+\theta)h(u)=\theta h(v)$ and
$(1+\theta)h(v)=1+\theta h(u)$; solve them and sum equilibrium successful
fluxes from $A$ to get the capacity and route law.
Each attempt consists of an $A$ holding time of mean one and an interior
excursion of mean one: the total exit hazard from $\{u_j,v_j\}$ to
$\{A,B\}$ is identically one. Its success probability averaged over the
uniformly chosen corridor is $S/M$. Renewal gives $T=2+(1-S/M)T$.
For the transform $F_x$, the backward equations give
\[
 (s+1)F_A=M^{-1}\sum_jF_{u_j},\quad
 F_{u_j}=\frac{(s+1+\theta_j)F_A+\theta_j}{(s+1)(s+1+2\theta_j)}.
\]
Substitution yields the displayed transform; its value at zero is one.
\end{proof}

Set $\varepsilon=M^{-2}$. In model 0 every $\theta_j=\varepsilon$;
in model $J$ one hidden $\theta_J=1$. Then
\[
 T_0=2(M^2+2),\qquad
 T_J=\frac{6M(M^2+2)}{M^2+3M-1},\qquad
 w_J=\frac{M^2+2}{M^2+3M-1}\ge1-\frac3M.
\]
Equilibrium is identical while the mean changes from approximately $2M^2$
to $6M$ and the unseen corridor dominates reactive flux.

\begin{theorem}[Acquisition lower bound]\label{thm:query}
An algorithm making at most $q$ adaptive exact local-rate queries has
transcript total-variation distance at most $q/M$ between model 0 and a
uniformly hidden model $J$. Hence:
\begin{itemize}
\item For $M\ge3$, mean estimation within 25\% with failure probability at
most $\alpha$ on every model requires $q\ge(1-2\alpha)M$.
\item A route sampler with TV error at most $\delta$ on every hidden model
requires $q\ge M(w_J-\delta)-1$.
\item Full passage-law sampling with TV error at most $\delta$ on every model
requires $q/M\ge1/2-12/M-2\delta$. Thus $M\ge96$, $\delta=1/16$ imply
$q\ge M/4$.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Couple random bits and known samples. Until the hidden label is queried,
transcripts agree. A uniformly hidden label lies among the null transcript's
$q$ inspected labels with probability at most $q/M$.
The 25\%-relative intervals for $T_0,T_J$ are disjoint for $M\ge3$;
successful estimation therefore distinguishes with total error at most
$2\alpha$. For route sampling the chance of outputting the hidden label is
at most $(q+1)/M$, even with one final unqueried guess.
For the full clock, a central crossing under model 0 has instantaneous rate
at most $M^{-2}$, so $P_0(\tau_B\le12M)\le12/M$.
Since $T_J\le6M$, Markov's inequality gives
$P_J(\tau_B\le12M)\ge1/2$. Triangle inequality for the two sampling errors
and data processing yield the asserted bound.
\end{proof}

The equally informed baseline queries all $M$ gates and computes these
quantities in $O(M)$ arithmetic, matching acquisition order. This is exponential
in label length $d$, but linear in an explicitly listed graph's size.
Parameters require $O(d)$ bits. The lower bound grants exact rates, free
randomness and unlimited computation; precision improvements cannot evade it.
It says nothing against additional symbolic structure that identifies a gate
or globally certifies a restricted effective response.

Finite low-order fits, held-out force agreement and endpoint equilibrium do
not certify absence of an uninspected spike. Sparse microscopic forces need
not imply a sparse committor or eliminated kinetic generator. A proof-carrying
factorization with a verified tractable elimination width is a stronger possible
interface, but no new acquisition mechanism supplying it was found.
The exact rational verifier independently solves generators for
$M=2,3,4,8,16,32$ in both model classes and checks committors, capacity,
mean times and three transform values. These finite checks corroborate
implementation; the all-$M$ argument is the proof above.

\section{A retained negative in molecular manufacturing}

An independent route sought exponentially accurate assembly with a molecular
evidence counter. Its headline overlaps existing
\href{https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1119911109}{nonequilibrium proofreading walks}
and \href{https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1314081110}{biochemical likelihood accumulation}.
A complete finite-retention calculation exposes an additional hidden cost.
A captured substrate has counter states $0,\ldots,N-1$, upward/downward rates
$u,d$, reject state $-1$, accept state $N$, and escape rate $\mu$.
For
\[
 r_\pm=\frac{u+d+\mu\pm\sqrt{(u+d+\mu)^2-4ud}}{2u},
\]
its acceptance probability is
\[
 h(i)=\frac{r_+^{i+1}-r_-^{i+1}}{r_+^{N+1}-r_-^{N+1}}.
\]
Direct substitution solves the killed birth--death Dirichlet problem.
For $(u_R,d_R)=(2,1)$ and $(u_W,d_W)=(1,2)$,
$h_W(0)/h_R(0)=2^{-N}$ for every $\mu\ge0$.
Yet every fixed $\mu>0$ makes correct yield decay exponentially in $N$.
Finite capture capacity therefore limits correct-product throughput by the
same factor, even with free reset.

This is not a universal assembly impossibility: choosing a fixed latch with
$\mu=C/N$ gives
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}h_R(0)
 =(1-d_R/u_R)e^{-C/(u_R-d_R)}>0.
\]
An assumed Arrhenius escape law needs only a logarithmically growing retention
barrier. Capture, release, fuel and implementation of selective counter rates
remain due. Accuracy without yield and retention accounting did not create
new manufacturing power; this route was abandoned.
