## 1. Li–Ren–Zhong, 19 September 2026, arXiv:2609.23228v1

Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23228v1 . Read Sections 1.1–1.5, 2.2–2.4, 2.7, 3.1–3.2, 4.1–4.3, the complete relevant proofs of Theorems 3.3/3.5/3.7/4.1, and Section 6.1's Lipschitz definition. Version/date is verified on the abstract record, not inferred from a search snippet.

Author claims: a seeded zero-error support disperser converts sufficiently dense sets of hard-to-prove nonmembership statements into generators where every output has a hard nonmembership statement. Projection families cost no circuit complexity overhead but need a stronger density regime. Higher-independence hashing reaches barely nontrivial hardness, where hard-string count is only O(n) times 2^n. SearchNP avoidance is obtained by constructing a nondeterministic machine that guesses a compression and the avoidance solver's branch. The paper also treats remote points with separate metric hypotheses.

Reconstruction/deduction: the transfer needs support coverage, rather than total variation extraction. This distinction permits the old linear support-coverage theorem to supply the seeded family while preserving algebraic degree. Proposition C in our proof note reconstructs the nondeterministic transfer without an oracle recognizing hard strings. Dense linear support coverage does not meet a useful Lipschitz or NC0 locality condition; those are separate obligations. The source's fixed 100n2^n threshold is not reproduced by our conservative explicit constants.

Scientific hinge: producing or proving a weakly demi-hard generator remains unresolved. This paper supplies a conditional conversion interface, not the hard object itself. A source-free claim of unconditional computational progress would be incorrect.

## 2. Alon–Dietzfelbinger–Miltersen–Petrank–Tardos, September 1999, Linear Hash Functions

Primary author snapshot: https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/linhash13.pdf . Read Sections 1 and 2.1–2.3, including Theorem 7, Proposition 2.2, Lemmas 2.1/2.3; also Section 3.2. Journal identification is JACM 46(5), pp.667–683. The saved version is the 15-page author-hosted preprint, so theorem pagination refers to that snapshot.

Author claims: binary linear maps cover every fixed support of size at least c_epsilon*m*2^m with high probability. Conversely, some supports of size order m*2^m cannot be mapped onto m bits by any linear transformation. The proof explicitly permits dependent sampled vectors and uses the relationship between random quotient kernels and random linear maps.

Reconstruction/deduction: Proposition A retains the same asymptotic order but proves a simple explicit bound k>=m+ceil(log2 m)+ceil(log2(32/epsilon^2)). Its two stages are a second moment bound for affine coefficient forms followed by random doubling with summable conditional Markov failures. An equivariant random completion supplies the exact uniform-surjective distribution. The hyperplane example refutes the tempting recursion E[q_i]<=E[q_(i-1)]^2; conditional squaring must remain conditional.

Novelty: support coverage, random span arguments, and the m*2^m order are established prior art. Our explicit constants and cross-paper consequence have UNKNOWN novelty. Do not rebrand the old theorem as a new discovery.

## 3. Ren–Wang–Zhong, 23 January 2026, ITCS 2026

Primary: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.111 . Read introduction, Definitions 1/3/8/9/11/12/17, Theorem 2, and Section 3.1's full Claim 4/Theorem 5 proof (saved PDF pp.14–18). Publisher date 2026-01-23 verified; outside recent window, used as direct prerequisite.

Author claims: demi-bits can yield hard range avoidance and proof complexity generators using hash composition. The proof system must admit reductions with specified syntactic/proof-length properties. Pairwise hashing has a stronger output-entropy requirement; its stated regime includes N>=2m+1. Simple parity reductions permit transfer from a refutation of a compressed image equation to a refutation of the original equation.

Reconstruction/deduction: the semantic inclusion G(x)=z implies LG(x)=Lz is not by itself a proof-complexity theorem. Proposition B therefore explicitly assumes a polynomial refutation transformation and a length bound b. A hard set at threshold b transfers to a compressed instance at threshold ell. Different thresholds may need different chosen maps; one generator family hard at all thresholds is not silently inferred.

Comparator: linear postprocessing and parity-compatible proof reductions already occur here. The candidate contribution is reaching the small hard-set regime with support coverage while preserving degree, not inventing linear composition.

## 4. Dhar–Dvir, 2024, linear hashing with l-infinity guarantees

Primary: https://theoretics.episciences.org/13338 . Read introduction, Section 2's Theorems 2.1–2.4/Lemma 2.3 and Section 3 interpretation. Publisher records 2024-04-03, saved PDF header says 2024-03-31, and arXiv v3 is 2024-03-29. These are distinct version/publication records, preserved rather than normalized to one guessed date.

Author claims: most linear hashes are pointwise close to uniform with small entropy loss; two-sided Kakeya bounds furnish the strong guarantees. Such relative l-infinity control is stronger than merely full support. Its parameter formulas matter, particularly field size and entropy requirements.

Deduction: relative pointwise error strictly below one implies full support. Therefore this is an alternative linear route to the recent conversion, and a stronger established result against which any claimed novelty must be compared. We did not independently reconstruct its Kakeya proof and do not import a theorem with unverified constants. The elementary proof is useful for exact scope/constant transparency, not automatically stronger science.

## 5. Guruswami–Lyu–Wang, 15 September 2022, RANDOM 2022

Primary: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2022.20 . Read introduction, Sections 3.1–3.3, Appendix A.1's complete Lemmas 34/35 and Corollary 36 (saved PDF pp.18–19).

Author claims: low-depth range avoidance connects to rigidity, codes and pseudorandomness; special small-locality regimes admit algorithms. For larger stretch, a polynomial-size biased hitting set contains a missing output. The cited Corollary 36 regime has m>2^(4k+1)n^(k−1)+n and a hitting-set bound polynomial in m.

Critical interface: existence of a missing output inside an explicit small candidate list does not mean a deterministic polynomial algorithm can select it. The presented selection uses an NP oracle. This must be charged; it is a competent known baseline in an oracle model, not a free algorithm for general Avoid. A dense degree-preserving conditional hardness conversion is neither an algorithmic improvement over this nor a refutation of it.

## 6. Gajulapalli–Golovnev–Nagargoje–Saraogi, 4 September 2023, RANDOM 2023

Primary: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2023.65 . Read introduction, Theorem 9/Corollary 10's complete reduction (saved PDF pp.11–12), and Section 4 Lemmas 11/13 plus Corollaries 12/14 (pp.12–14).

Author claims: degree-2 range avoidance at the appropriate stretch implies explicit rigidity and consequences for superlinear linear-circuit lower bounds; constant-depth subclasses admit algorithms under structural restrictions. The AffineReduce route uses a modulator and affine envelopes after branching, rather than treating arbitrary nonlinear systems as linear.

Deduction: preserving degree through compression keeps a genuinely consequential restricted Avoid interface intact. It does not solve it. In particular our supplied G's hard-set property cannot be acquired by declaring that degree-2 Avoid should be hard. Algorithmic comparators already exploit restricted structure and branch costs, so no broad capability advantage follows from a finite hash experiment.

## Discriminating construction and unresolved alternatives

The completed construction is Proposition A plus the transfer statements B/C. Proposition D is a universal locality obstruction w>=(1-epsilon)N/k; it explains why sparse compression cannot meet arbitrary ambient stretch with constant coordinate dependence. Proposition E supplies a concrete infinite family of size-2^m supports that no binary linear map to m bits can cover. Neither obstruction refutes source-specific or restricted-support compression.

The second construction is a complete arbitrary-weight multiplicity profile proof. Its broad utility is actively discriminated: a 100-bit Bernoulli(2/5) product source has a supplied/computable ambient L_100 density norm exceeding2^20, so our20-bit weighted tail bound is vacuous, while the exact pairwise collision calculation bounds expected TV below3.3e-12. This is a limitation of the controlling statistic, not an uncharged hard-set-membership issue.

Promising unresolved alternatives: a structured hard-set promise might allow sparse maps at nearoptimal entropy loss; a family preserving an appropriate normalized Hamming Lipschitz bound could reach remote-point consequences. Neither is established. Projections are the existing strong baseline when the source's density promise allows them; dense linear maps are useful only where preserving degree matters and losing locality is acceptable.
