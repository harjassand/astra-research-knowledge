# Cycle 6: collective-correlation obstruction to the direct approximate-Markov route

## Status

There is an exact noncommuting sequence of finite state families and explicit full-domain two-receiver CPTP maps with uniformly bounded Holevo capacity and marginal error tending to zero, for which the natural cq state of the supplied broadcaster has
\[
 I(X:B\mid C)=I(X:C\mid B)\longrightarrow c/2>0.
\]
No recovery channel acting on either receiver alone can reconstruct that joint reference state with vanishing trace error. This refutes a dimension-free inference from the **actual supplied map's** small marginal residual and bounded family capacity to a vanishing-conditional-mutual-information/recoverability premise.

This is not a broadcasting-to-EB counterexample. A full-domain replacement EB channel reconstructs every state with error tending to zero. In fact, in this family the same one-output replacement marginal admits both a high-CMI Bell-code extension and a zero-CMI product extension. Thus marginal data alone do not determine the Markov structure of the chosen joint output. The unresolved target remains whether bounded Holevo capacity and existence of accurate broadcasters always imply a common EB simulator.

The witness is distinct from the prior balanced-sign purification obstruction: here the reference is the classical ensemble label, and the signal is stored as *synergistic information across both receivers*. Both receiver marginals are independent of the label.

## Family and reference state

Let \(d\ge2\), \(\tau=I/d\), and choose two mutually unbiased bases \(\{|0,j\rangle\}_{j=0}^{d-1}\), \(\{|1,j\rangle\}_{j=0}^{d-1}\) of \(\mathbb C^d\). One may take the computational basis and the discrete Fourier basis. For \(0<\epsilon<1\), set
\[
 \rho_{b,j}=(1-\epsilon)\tau+\epsilon|b,j\rangle\langle b,j|,
 \quad b\in\{0,1\},\ j\in\mathbb Z_d.
\]
Use the uniform classical-label reference
\[
 \Omega_{XA}=\frac1{2d}\sum_{b,j}|b,j\rangle\langle b,j|_X\otimes\rho_{b,j},
 \qquad \Omega_A=\tau.
\]
Each signal is full rank. The family is noncommuting: for a computational-basis projector \(P_{0,j}\) and a Fourier-basis projector \(P_{1,k}\),
\[
 [\rho_{0,j},\rho_{1,k}]=\epsilon^2[P_{0,j},P_{1,k}]\ne0.
\]
Therefore the example is not relying on an exactly broadcastable commuting family.

## Exact Holevo capacity

All states have the same spectrum
\[
 q=\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)/d,\qquad r=(1-\epsilon)/d\quad\text{(multiplicity }d-1\text{)}.
\]
For any prior, the average-state entropy is at most \(\log d\). The uniform prior averages the two complete bases to \(\tau\), attaining this maximum, so the Holevo capacity is exactly
\[
 C(\mathcal E_d)=\log d-S(\rho_{b,j})
 =q\log(dq)+(1-q)\log(1-\epsilon).
\]
Concavity of entropy yields the useful bound \(C(\mathcal E_d)\le\epsilon\log d\). For any fixed \(c>0\), take
\[
 \epsilon_d=c/\log d
\]
for all sufficiently large \(d\). Then \(C(\mathcal E_d)\le c\), \(C(\mathcal E_d)\to c\), and \(\epsilon_d\to0\). Natural logarithms are used throughout.

## One full-domain broadcaster with hidden joint label information

Write \(P_{b,j}=|b,j\rangle\langle b,j|\) and \(M_{b,j}=P_{b,j}/2\). The \(2d\) effects form a POVM because each basis sums to \(I\). For \(b=0,1\) and \(j\in\mathbb Z_d\), use the generalized Bell kets
\[
 |\Phi_{b,j}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}e^{2\pi i js/d}|s\rangle_B|s+b\pmod d\rangle_C.
\]
They are orthonormal, maximally entangled, and their \(B\) and \(C\) marginals are both \(\tau\). There are \(2d\le d^2\) such kets for \(d\ge2\).

Define the measure-and-prepare CPTP map
\[
 \mathcal B(Z)=\sum_{b,j}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{b,j}Z)\,|\Phi_{b,j}\rangle\langle\Phi_{b,j}|.
\]
Since the effects are rank one, Kraus operators are \(K_{b,j}=|\Phi_{b,j}\rangle\langle b,j|/\sqrt2\) for each \(b,j\), and \(\sum_{b,j}K_{b,j}^*K_{b,j}=\sum_{b,j}P_{b,j}/2=I\). This defines the channel on the entire input algebra. Each Bell preparation has local state \(\tau\), hence both marginals are the same replacement channel
\[
 \Lambda_B(Z)=\Lambda_C(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}(Z)\tau.
\]
For every family member,
\[
 T(\Lambda_B(\rho_{b,j}),\rho_{b,j})
 =T(\Lambda_C(\rho_{b,j}),\rho_{b,j})
 =\epsilon(1-1/d).
\]
Write \(\beta_d\) for the supplied map's actual worst marginal error; then \(\beta_d=\epsilon(1-1/d)\) and the optimal \(b_2(\mathcal E_d)\le\beta_d\to0\). The same replacement channel \(\Theta(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}(Z)\tau\) is a full-domain EB decoder and satisfies
\[
 e_{\rm EB}(\mathcal E_d)\le\epsilon(1-1/d)\to0.
\]

## Exact conditional mutual information

For the label \(x=(b,j)\), the broadcast output is Bell diagonal, with probabilities
\[
 p((b',k)\mid(b,j))=\operatorname{Tr}(M_{b',k}\rho_{b,j})=
 \begin{cases}
  q/2,&b'=b,\ k=j,\\
  (1-\epsilon)/(2d),&b'=b,\ k\ne j,\\
  1/(2d),&b'\ne b.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its first two cases follow from orthogonality within one basis, and the last from mutual unbiasedness. Under the uniform label prior, every Bell-measurement outcome is uniform on \(2d\) possibilities. Since the output states are diagonal in orthogonal Bell projectors, their Holevo information equals the classical mutual information of this channel:
\[
 I(X:BC)=I(X:(b',k))
 =\log(2d)+\frac q2\log\frac q2
 +(d-1)\frac{1-\epsilon}{2d}\log\frac{1-\epsilon}{2d}
 +\frac12\log\frac1{2d}
 =\frac12\big[q\log(dq)+(1-q)\log(1-\epsilon)\big]
 =\frac12 C(\mathcal E_d).
\]
The Bell preparations make each output marginal exactly \(\tau\), independent of \(X\). Therefore \(I(X:C)=I(X:B)=0\), and the chain rule gives
\[
 I(X:B\mid C)=I(X:BC)-I(X:C)=\frac12 C(\mathcal E_d),
 \quad
 I(X:C\mid B)=\frac12 C(\mathcal E_d).
\]
For \(\epsilon_d=c/\log d\), both tend to \(c/2\), even though the supplied broadcaster residual tends to zero. The unconditional receiver state is uniform on the \(2d\) selected Bell states, so \(I(B:C)=\log(d/2)\); the conditional value is \(I(B:C\mid X)=\log(d/2)+C(\mathcal E_d)/2\). This distinction is another reason to keep the conditioning systems and state fixed. The label information is collective: both local mutual informations vanish, but the joint output carries \(C(\mathcal E_d)/2\). This is not a mistake from treating quantum conditioning as classical conditioning.

The CMI is operationally visible: measure both receivers in the Bell basis. The outcome is the POVM label \((b',k)\) and has mutual information exactly \(C(\mathcal E_d)/2\) about the family member. Measuring either receiver alone gives a state \(I/d\) for every label and zero mutual information.

## Receiver-only recovery is quantitatively impossible for this state

The state \(\Omega_{XC}\) is the product \(I_X/(2d)\otimes\tau_C\). For any recovery channel \(\mathcal R:C\to C'B\),
\[
 (\operatorname{id}_X\otimes\mathcal R)(\Omega_{XC})
 =I_X/(2d)\otimes\sigma_{C'B}
\]
for some \(\sigma\). It is independent of \(X\). Donald's identity for the cq ensemble gives
\[
 D\!\left(\Omega_{XBC}\middle\|I_X/(2d)\otimes\sigma_{BC}\right)
 =I(X:BC)_\Omega+D(\Omega_{BC}\|\sigma_{BC})
 \ge C(\mathcal E_d)/2.
\]
Quantum Pinsker's inequality (with nats and half trace distance) implies
\[
 T\!\left(\Omega_{XBC},(\operatorname{id}_X\otimes\mathcal R)(\Omega_{XC})\right)
 \ge\sqrt{C(\mathcal E_d)/4}\longrightarrow\sqrt c/2.
\]
The same statement holds when attempting recovery from \(B\) by symmetry. So the specified tripartite state is not recoverable from either single-receiver marginal, despite small broadcasting error and bounded capacity.

This is exactly the direction relevant to Fawzi–Renner: small \(I(X:B\mid C)\) guarantees recovery of the specified state from \(XC\); it does not make recovery automatic when that CMI is nonzero. Here a direct relative-entropy lower bound proves no recovery sequence can converge in trace distance. The theorem also does not convert a general recovery channel into a POVM on the original input.

## Same marginal, different extension; why this is not a target counterexample

The one-output marginal \(\Lambda(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}(Z)\tau\) has the product extension
\[
 \mathcal B_0(Z)=\operatorname{Tr}(Z)\,\tau_B\otimes\tau_C.
\]
It has exactly the same marginal channels and family residual as \(\mathcal B\), but \(I(X:B\mid C)=0\) for its output cq state. Thus no CMI estimate for the *chosen joint extension* follows from its marginal channel alone, and an existence proof could in principle select a better extension. The target itself is easy here: \(\Theta=\Lambda\) is EB and has error \(\epsilon(1-1/d)\). The example refutes a tempting intermediate lemma; it neither refutes an existence theorem that optimizes over extensions nor gives \(\liminf e_{\rm EB}>0\).

The family is noncommuting, so exact broadcasting is forbidden at each finite \(d\) by the no-broadcasting theorem; nevertheless the displayed full-domain broadcaster has an error upper bound tending to zero. This fact is not used in the CMI derivation. The exact family and map are self-contained; no quantitative lower bound on the optimal \(b_2\) is claimed.

## Costs and acquisition boundary

This is an existence construction with its measurement frame written explicitly. The broadcaster uses a \(2d\)-outcome input POVM \(\{P_{b,j}/2\}\), and prepares one of \(2d\) generalized Bell states. In a black-box setting, acquiring/calibrating those projectors is a real cost; the capacity bound does not provide the POVM. The classical reference register \(X\) is not accessible to the broadcaster or EB decoder and requires \(\log_2(2d)\) random bits to sample under the prior. To expose the joint information, a Bell-basis measurement on both receivers has \(d^2\) possible outcomes; it is not a local decoder and requires access to both outputs. The product replacement EB channel needs no family measurement, minimax or adaptive frame: it prepares \(I/d\), implementable by drawing a uniform basis index and preparing that state (\(\log_2d\) random bits). No claim of black-box or efficient construction is made.

The checker below provides exact rational matrix controls for \(d=2,\epsilon=1/2\) and exact probability-law checks for small rational fixtures. The general Fourier/Bell orthogonality and entropy limits are proved analytically, not inferred from finite computations.

## Exact UNKNOWN gates

- **Refuted:** a universal modulus \(f(C_0,b)\to0\) for \(I(X:B\mid C)\) of an arbitrary supplied broadcaster, based only on \(C(\mathcal E)\le C_0\) and its statewise marginal error \(b\). Taking \(c\le C_0\) above gives \(b\to0\) but \(I(X:B\mid C)\to c/2\). The same applies in the reverse direction.
- **Unknown:** whether the existence of a good broadcaster always permits selecting an extension with a useful low-CMI/recovery structure; this example has both high-CMI and zero-CMI extensions.
- **Unknown:** whether an appropriate recovered cq state yields a single full-domain input POVM and one common EB preparation channel with error controlled only by \(C_0\) and \(b_2\). A recovery operation is a general channel; making it entanglement breaking or obtaining one common posterior measurement remains a separate measured-information gate. Conditional priors and acquired frames cannot be allowed to vary label-by-label without charging their cost.
- **Unknown:** the global bounded-Holevo broadcasting-to-EB theorem or a countersequence with \(\liminf e_{\rm EB}>0\). The present construction has \(e_{\rm EB}\le b\to0\).
- **Scoped positive result:** the cycle5 fixed-sandwich leaf proves \(I(X:B\mid A)\le L_\theta r_p\) under its faithful theta-sandwich, tracial, selfadjoint and actual-extension hypotheses. This family is outside that interface because its largest likelihood ratio relative to \(\tau\) is \(1+(d-1)\epsilon_d\to\infty\). That result is compatible with this obstruction and still does not provide the common-POVM conversion.

The next gate is to either prove an extension-selection theorem plus a charged recovery-to-common-POVM step, or bypass receiver-only recovery with a direct common-EB argument that handles high-likelihood tails and collective output correlations. The current witness settles only that one may not assume the supplied two-receiver state is approximately Markov.
