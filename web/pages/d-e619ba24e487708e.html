# Primary-route audit: polynomial rows for the complement-LCU image

Date: 2026-10-08  
Scope: primary constructions in LCU/block-encoding, matrix and Hamiltonian sparsification, and quantum gap amplification. The question is whether any theorem supplies a polynomial-time row-list oracle for a constant-gap positive image enforcer for the explicit complement-LCU map in [00_INTERFACE_AND_TENSOR_FORMULA.md](00_INTERFACE_AND_TENSOR_FORMULA.md).

## Exact target and route verdict

For the positive projector list $H=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\pi_jh_j$, with $0\preceq H\preceq I$, let $R_j=I-2h_j$, prepare a label state with amplitudes $(1/\sqrt 2,\sqrt{\pi_1/2},\ldots,\sqrt{\pi_M/2})$, and set

$$
U=(A^*\otimes I)\operatorname{SELECT}(I,R_1,\ldots,R_M)(A\otimes I),\quad
V|\psi\rangle=U(|0\rangle|\psi\rangle),\quad
B=(I-|0\rangle\langle0|)\otimes I.
$$

The exact identities are $\langle0|U|0\rangle=I-H$, $V^*BV=2H-H^2$, and $H\preceq V^*BV\preceq2H$. The diagonal projection $B$ has one nonzero per row. The canonical enforcer $C_0=UBU^*=I-VV^*$ is also a projection and has gap one. The remaining gate is a positive contraction $C$ with $\ker C=\operatorname{ran}V$, $C\succeq\gamma(I-VV^*)$ for constant $\gamma$, and a polynomial-time algorithm listing every nonzero column in each computational-basis row.

**No primary theorem audited here supplies that row-list oracle for this image, or a different enforcer with the same all-state comparison and contraction/gap requirements.** The closest results require input interfaces or preserve output objects that do not match. This is an interface mismatch, not an impossibility theorem for every alternative isometry or enforcer. The one explicit PREPARE completion analyzed in [01_canonical_C.md](01_canonical_C.md) separately has a $5^R$-entry row in $C_0$; the present audit does not extend that fixed-completion lower bound to other completions.

## Block-encoding provides circuit access, not row access

LCU gives a polynomial-size circuit for $U$, and $C_0$ has the circuit $UBU^*$. This is operator-circuit access. It says nothing by itself about the locations of nonzero entries in a computational-basis row.

The direction of the standard sparse-access theorem is the reverse. Gilyén, Su, Low, and Wiebe, Lemma 48 in [*Quantum singular value transformation and beyond*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01838), take an $s_r$-row-sparse and $s_c$-column-sparse matrix $A\in\mathbb C^{2^w\times2^w}$ with entries bounded by one, **given** row-index, column-index, and entry oracles $O_r,O_c,O_A$. They construct a $(\sqrt{s_rs_c},w+3,\epsilon)$ block-encoding using one call to each index oracle and two calls to the entry oracle, plus polylogarithmic additional gates. The theorem assumes sparsity and both location oracles; it does not infer them from a block-encoding or circuit. QSVT transforms an operator supplied in block-encoded form but likewise does not return row locations.

There is no general circuit-to-row-list implication, even for a conjugated diagonal projection. Let $D=|0^n\rangle\langle0^n|$ and $W=H^{\otimes n}$. Then $D$ is diagonal and one-sparse per row, $W$ has a linear-size circuit, and $WDW^*=|+^n\rangle\langle+^n|$ has all $2^n$ entries nonzero in every row. In the actual LCU formula, a specified $C_0$ entry can be evaluated by a polynomial sum over term pairs. That is entry access, not a method to enumerate the combined nonzero columns after cancellations.

## Expander-walk quantum gap amplification

Aharonov, Arad, Landau, and Vazirani, [*The Detectability Lemma and Quantum Gap Amplification*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3412), prove a walk-amplification lemma for a projector system on a bounded-degree expander. For every $t$-walk $\mathcal B=(e_1,\ldots,e_t)$, the new constraint projects onto the orthogonal complement of the intersection of the old accepting spaces along the walk. Their bound is

$$
\operatorname{QUNSAT}(G^t)\ge
c(\lambda)K(q,d,\theta)\min\{t\operatorname{QUNSAT}(G),1\},
$$

where the displayed constants are independent of graph size. Each new test acts on up to $t+1$ vertices, or $O(t\log q)$ qubits in their notation, and the explicit walk list has $O(|E|d^{t-1})$ tests. Fixed $t$ gives only fixed-factor amplification. Raising an inverse-polynomial violation fraction to a constant by this lemma takes $t=\Theta(1/\operatorname{QUNSAT})$; for inverse-polynomial input gap, direct enumeration then has superpolynomial size and the constraint locality grows polynomially. The construction produces a new constraint sum, not an isometry whose image is the specified LCU range, and not a diagonal $B$ with the required all-state compression.

The standard Hamiltonian qPCP conjecture asks for QMA-hardness of constant-relative-promise-gap local Hamiltonian. Aharonov, Arad, and Vidick formulate it as a conjecture in [their survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7495); its current arXiv version still describes the conjecture as unresolved. If a qPCP reduction outputs a polynomial list of fixed-locality terms, then that output Hamiltonian has a polynomial row enumerator: for $m'$ $k$-local terms, each basis row has at most $m'2^k$ candidate columns before duplicates are combined. But qPCP is a promise-gap decision reduction. Its conjecture does not require the output Hamiltonian to have an exact kernel equal to a prescribed common isometry image, or to supply an all-state compression $V^*BV\asymp H$. Constant-gap QMA-hardness and this positive-encoding interface are different claims.

## Hamiltonian sparsification

**Aharonov–Zhou's positive degree-reduction theorem.** In [*Hamiltonian sparsification and gap-simulations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.11084), the coherent degree-reduction result assumes an explicitly specified $O(1)$-local Hamiltonian $H$, a quasi-groundspace with quasi-gap $\gamma=\Omega(1/\operatorname{poly}(n))$, energy spread $w$, and $\|H\|=O(\operatorname{poly}(n))$. It constructs an $O(1)$-local, $O(1)$-degree Hamiltonian with $O(\operatorname{poly}(n)/\epsilon^2)$ terms, term norm $O(\operatorname{poly}(n,\epsilon^{-1}))$, and $\epsilon$-incoherent groundspace simulation with energy spread $w+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(n))$. The construction uses phase estimation and circuit-to-Hamiltonian. Given the output local-term list, one can enumerate a computational-basis row by visiting each term and its at most $2^k$ assignments on its constant-size support.

This is the closest positive mechanism, but it does not match the target. The present amplified clauses are $h_j=I-\bigotimes_{r=1}^{R}Q_{j,r}$ and have locality $O(R)$, with growing $R$. They are not an $O(1)$-local input list. The LCU entry expansion has signed terms with support up to $10R$, not a polynomial list of constant-local PSD terms. Replacing each clause by a sum of its per-copy violation projectors changes an OR cost into a count and can lose a factor $R$, so it is not a constant-factor all-state replacement.

The theorem also assumes an inverse-polynomial **spectral gap above a chosen input quasi-groundspace**. A yes/no promise gap for the minimum energy does not imply a lower bound on the first excited energy in yes instances; the supplied interface gives no such spectral-gap parameter. Finally, the output simulates only a designated low-energy subspace. It does not produce an enforcer whose kernel is $\operatorname{ran}V$, the image of all logical vectors. Its gap is preserved at the input scale rather than amplified: scaling to constant gap increases the norm, while normalizing to $0\preceq C\preceq I$ can shrink the gap back to inverse polynomial. The theorem therefore does not give a contractive constant-gap enforcer for this LCU image.

Aharonov–Zhou also prove a no-go for constant-strength degree reduction on particular families and a qPCP-* limitation for a particular $H_{\mathrm{oneone}}$ family under near-perfect coherence. The latter uses its own localized-encoding and error/norm parameters. The authors distinguish promise-gap reductions from spectral-gap simulation and do not prove the constant-error impossibility relevant to qPCP. These results are not a no-go for this complement-LCU image.

**Term sparsification.** Basu, Brakensiek, and Putterman, [*Many Hamiltonians Are Sparsifiable*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02211), take an $n$-qubit Hamiltonian presented as a sum of $m$ **fixed-$r$-local PSD terms**, and output a weighted subset of those same terms satisfying

$$
(1-\epsilon)H\preceq\widetilde H\preceq(1+\epsilon)H
$$

on every state. The theorem covers Pauli terms, several random full-rank classes, and arbitrary local terms of rank at least $2^r-1$ (the arbitrary nullity-at-most-one/QSAT case, with $\widetilde O(\epsilon^{-2}n^2)$ selected terms); the Pauli case has $\widetilde O_r(\epsilon^{-2}n)$ terms. This is genuine all-state order preservation and a retained fixed-locality term list supports row enumeration. It does not apply to $C_0$: its direct PSD decomposition $C_0=\sum_{a\ne0}U(|a\rangle\langle a|\otimes I)U^*$ has polynomially many **global** circuit-conjugated terms, not fixed-local terms. Expanding the tensor factors instead yields support $O(R)$ and exponentially many local-basis components. The theorem also does not construct an isometry or enforcer from circuit access, and its arbitrary-term result is not a generic sparsifier theorem for low-rank local projectors such as the rank-one seed factors.

**Explicit-matrix sparsification.** Herbert and Subramanian, [*Spectral sparsification of matrix inputs as a preprocessing step for quantum algorithms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02861), start with an explicit real symmetric matrix/graph representation with $m$ nonzero entries and apply effective-resistance sampling. The goal is a locally computable row-sparse approximation for subsequent Hamiltonian simulation; their stated preprocessing is $O(m\|H\|t\log n/\epsilon)$. Their analysis has additional assumptions, including reality, sufficient row sampling, and commutation in its simulation-error estimate, and its row-sparsity guarantee is probabilistic/asymptotic. This is not a circuit/block-encoding input algorithm. Feeding it $C_0$ requires enumerating the matrix entries first; the $5^R$ row for the fixed completion in [01_canonical_C.md](01_canonical_C.md) already makes explicit input size superpolynomial. Approximate time-evolution preservation also does not preserve an exact prescribed kernel or the exact $B$-compression.

## What would change the verdict

A positive route still needs an explicit bridge absent from these theorems: from the circuit/term-pair representation of this $U$ to either (a) a polynomial-size fixed-local PSD term list for a contraction whose exact kernel is $\operatorname{ran}V$ and whose gap is constant, or (b) a direct row-enumeration theorem proving polynomial combined support after cancellation. A block-encoding, fast specified-entry evaluation, constant-promise-gap qPCP output, or low-energy simulator is insufficient. The exact lower bound in [01_canonical_C.md](01_canonical_C.md) settles only the fixed PREPARE completion; no unrestricted no-go is claimed here.
