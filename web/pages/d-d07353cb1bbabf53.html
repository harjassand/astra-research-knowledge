# A quantitative cardinality barrier for the shrinking-shell compiler

2026-10-07. Status: **independent internal gate PASS**, reconstructed by charge_precision in `notes/charge_precision/NEAR_SHELL_BARRIER_GATE.md`. This is a scoped obstruction and a selection result, not an improved equilibrium lower bound. It applies to the single supplied cubic-grid template as well as arbitrary targets. It does not rely on counting parameters needed to realize arbitrary jets.

## Question and interface

Can the existing cubic harmonic grid be compiled with o(D²) positive point charges by exploiting its 24 null waves, cyclic symmetry, or six planar harmonic waves? No such construction is proved here. The result below shows that the present near-spherical, small-moment realization architecture cannot achieve this improvement, even for one fixed template. A successful smaller construction must change that architecture substantially.

Use normalized area on S² and real orthonormal spherical harmonics Y_lm, with Y_00=1. Let positive weights w_i sum to 1 and let b_i=r_i omega_i, with omega_i in S² and |r_i−1|≤eta<1. For d≥1 put

    m_lm = sum_i w_i r_i^l Y_lm(omega_i),  1≤l≤2d,
    mu = ||m||_2,
    S_n = sum_(l=1)^n (2l+1)l² = n(n+1)(3n²+5n+1)/6,
    K_d(eta) = (d+1) eta (1+eta)^(2d−1) sqrt(S_(2d)).

**Theorem.** If

    (d+1)mu + K_d(eta) < 1,                         (1)

then the number N of sites is at least (d+1)². The weights need not be equal. Repeated directions do not defeat the conclusion.

For eta=0 and mu=0 this recovers the familiar positive-quadrature square-test lower bound on a spherical 2d-design. The novelty of the explicit perturbation formulation has not been investigated and is not claimed.

## Complete derivation

Suppose N<(d+1)². The space of real spherical polynomials of degree at most d has dimension sum_(l=0)^d(2l+1)=(d+1)². Evaluation at the N directions therefore has a nonzero kernel. Choose q in this kernel with ||q||_(L²(S²))=1, so q(omega_i)=0 for every i.

The addition formula and Cauchy–Schwarz give

    ||q||_infinity ≤ sqrt(sum_(l=0)^d(2l+1)) = d+1.

Let f=q² on S², and write f=sum_(l=0)^(2d) f_l, where f_l is its degree-l harmonic part. The constant coefficient f_00 is 1, and

    ||f||_2 ≤ ||q||_infinity ||q||_2 ≤ d+1.

Let h be the harmonic polynomial extending f from the sphere:

    h(r omega)=sum_(l=0)^(2d) r^l f_l(omega).

It has h(0)=1. At the source directions f(omega_i)=0. For |r−1|≤eta, the mean-value theorem for r^l gives

    |r^l−1| ≤ eta l (1+eta)^(l−1).

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the harmonic coefficients and the addition formula at a fixed omega_i yields

    |h(r_i omega_i)|
      = |h(r_i omega_i)−f(omega_i)|
      ≤ ||f||_2 [sum_(l=1)^(2d) (2l+1)(r_i^l−1)²]^(1/2)
      ≤ K_d(eta).

On the other hand, averaging h over the weighted sites gives exactly

    sum_i w_i h(b_i) = 1 + sum_(l=1)^(2d) sum_m f_lm m_lm.

Its distance from 1 is at most (d+1)mu. Positivity and normalization of the w_i imply the absolute value of the left side is at most K_d(eta). Thus

    1 ≤ (d+1)mu + K_d(eta),

contradicting (1). This proves the theorem. The positivity is load-bearing: signed weights would replace the unit mass by their total variation.

## Coulomb interpretation

For charges q_i>0 at a_i=R_i omega_i, normalize the potential by any common positive factor and define

    W=sum_i q_i/R_i,
    w_i=(q_i/R_i)/W,
    b_i=omega_i/R_i.

These are Kelvin-transformed sites and positive normalized weights. The normalized nonconstant Newton moments are

    m_lm = [sum_i q_i R_i^(-l−1)Y_lm(omega_i)] / W.

Therefore the theorem applies directly to a Coulomb configuration whose radii are near a common shell and whose normalized low moments are small. It is a statement about source geometry and moment size, independent of how the target moments were selected. In particular, the fact that the target polynomial is a one-parameter family or a sum of few null waves does not remove it.

The annular hypothesis matters. One far-away positive charge has small normalized moments near the origin; its Kelvin radius tends to zero, not to one. This theorem makes no lower-cardinality assertion for such geometry and gives no universal upper bound on the number of equilibria of N charges.

## Application to the actual radial inverse

In `notes/root_cycle7/EQUAL_CHARGE_JET_COMPILER.md`, the inverse works on ||h||≤r with

    r=1/[64(D+1)^5],
    ||B h||_infinity ≤ (D+1)||h||/2.

Thus every source produced inside that inverse ball satisfies

    |R_i−1|≤s_0=1/[128(D+1)^4],
    eta≤s_0/(1−s_0).

The requested moment vector A through degree D obeys ||A||≤r/2. The average constant moment W lies between 1/(1+s_0) and 1/(1−s_0), hence the normalized moment subvector through 2d, with d=floor(D/2), satisfies

    mu≤(r/2)(1+s_0).

For D≥2, condition (1) holds with a very large margin. A simple uniform bound is enough: eta≤1/[64(D+1)^4], sqrt(S_(2d))≤D(D+1), and (1+eta)^(2d−1)<2. Therefore

    K_d(eta)≤1/[32(D+1)],
    (d+1)mu≤1/[64(D+1)^4],

whose sum is below 1. The exact checker verifies the less-relaxed algebraic bound at several D values using rational arithmetic and squaring only positive quantities.

Consequently **any positive source realization satisfying this inverse-domain geometry and target size requires**

    N ≥ (floor(D/2)+1)².                            (2)

This conclusion already applies before considering the cubic-grid critical count. It is not merely the M=D(D+2) rank requirement of the arbitrary-jet right inverse: (2) constrains a single small target moment vector, no local surjectivity assumed. Fixed finite cyclic or polyhedral symmetry cannot evade the theorem while keeping these hypotheses.

For the audited template D=256m, (2) reads N≥(128m+1)². This does not forbid a different realization with fewer sources and a different degree/geometry relation. It does rule out the proposed shortcut of replacing the O(D²) design by a much smaller near-shell pattern inside the same small-jet regime solely because the packet has a short formula.

## What was tried and what remains open

The packet has the exact decomposition

    cos(kx)cos(ky)cosh(sqrt(2)kz)
      = [cos(k(x+y))+cos(k(x−y))] cosh(sqrt(2)kz)/2.

Each bracketed term is planar harmonic in u=(x±y)/sqrt(2), v=z and independent of the third coordinate. Across three cyclic terms there are six planar waves. This suggests approximating planar logarithmic potentials by parallel line charges and then discretizing the lines. The last step introduces a third-dimensional approximation requirement at exponentially small grid tolerances. No subquadratic charge count or rigorous lower bound for every such line construction is established. It remains an alternative, not a result.

A more useful next falsifiable target is a direct point-source construction on a region comparable to its source distances, whose low moments are not all small after normalization. Other escapes include a substantially thick annulus, a non-shrinking representation of a different harmonic template, or a construction that preserves only critical-point neighborhoods without matching an entire high-degree jet. The theorem does not exclude any of these. Signed charges also escape the positivity argument, but would violate the positive/equal-charge objective.

## Prior and evidence

The classical exact-design lower bound is in P. Delsarte, J. M. Goethals and J. J. Seidel, *Spherical codes and designs*, Geometriae Dedicata 6 (1977), 363–388, Theorems 5.11–5.12. The primary scan's introduction and these theorem/proof passages were read; the entire paper was not audited. In ambient dimension 3 their even-strength bound specializes to (d+1)². Our square-test proof also covers unequal positive weights; no reliance on that extension being stated in the source is needed.
