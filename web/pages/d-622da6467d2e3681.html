RACAH DIAGONAL DELOCALIZATION IN THE SUBLINEAR HIGH-z REGIME
Date: 8 October 2026
Target recurrence: outputs/research/sol_classicality/RACAH_MIDDLE_COMPACTNESS.txt, equation (4).
Status: analytic transfer-matrix / spectral-mass proof candidate. It proves |eta_l(l)|=O(z^{-1/2}) along every sequence l/n->0, z->infinity, with an absolute implied constant after fixed asymptotic thresholds. No finite scan is used. Historical priority and external proof review remain UNKNOWN.

RESULT

Let n=2j, d=n+1, N=n(n+2), L=l(l+1), z=L/sqrt(N), and let u_0,...,u_n be the real Racah column in (4), normalized by sum_k u_k^2=1. For every sequence with

  n->infinity, l/n->0, z->infinity,

there is an absolute C such that, eventually,

  u_l^2 <= C/n,       |eta_l(l)| <= C/sqrt(z).             (A)

In particular eta_l(l)->0, not 1. The key point is a quantitative mass-spreading statement: the recurrence forces mass at least c n u_l^2 over k in [l,n/2]. If eta_l(l) were bounded away from zero, this lower bound would be c z and would contradict unit column norm.

The diagonal normalization is essential. Exactly,

  u_l=sqrt((2l+1)^2) {j j l; j j l}
      =(-1)^n (2l+1) eta_l(l)/d,
  |u_l|=(2l+1)|eta_l(l)|/d.                              (B)

Thus eta near 1 would put mass |u_l|^2~4l^2/n^2 at the single site k=l. This is not ruled out by normalization alone. The recurrence spreads that mass over a macroscopic oscillatory interval, giving a total mass of order l^2/n~z.

1. TARGET SCALING AND EXACT COEFFICIENT REWRITE

Write m=l and s=m/n. The assumptions imply m->infinity, s->0, and

  z~m^2/n,       m^2/n->infinity,       1/m^2=o(s^2).      (C)

For integer k>=1 set beta_k=(1-1/(4k^2))^{-1/2}. The given off-diagonal coefficients have the exact form

  B_(k-1)=(d^2-k^2) beta_k/4,
  B_k=(d^2-(k+1)^2) beta_(k+1)/4,
  A_k=(d^2-k^2-k-1)/2.                                  (D)

Define

  D_k=B_k+B_(k-1)-A_k,
  g_k=sqrt(B_k B_(k-1)),
  r_k=sqrt(B_k/B_(k-1)),
  T_k=(L-A_k)/g_k.

Substitution in (D) gives the exact positive expression

  D_k=1/4 + 1/4{[d^2-(k+1)^2](beta_(k+1)-1)
                         +(d^2-k^2)(beta_k-1)}.          (E)

Also B_k/g_k=r_k and B_(k-1)/g_k=1/r_k, so

  T_k=-2 +(L+D_k)/g_k -(r_k+r_k^(-1)-2),
  r_k+r_k^(-1)-2=(r_k-1)^2/r_k.                          (F)

These identities check the lower-edge sign: after the alternating-sign transform, the local transfer trace is -T_k, just below +2.

On the interval I=[m,floor(n/2)], all constants below are absolute and uniform once (C) holds sufficiently strongly:

  B_k, g_k ~= n^2,
  D_k ~= n^2/k^2,
  L/g_k ~= s^2,
  D_k/g_k=O(k^(-2)),
  r_k-1=O(k/n^2+k^(-3)),
  r_(k+1)-r_k=O(n^(-2)+k^(-4)).                            (G)

Here ~= means upper and lower bounds by fixed positive constants. To see the beta estimates, beta_k-1 is between c/k^2 and C/k^2, while its first and second derivatives are O(k^-3) and O(k^-4). Apply this to (D)-(E); for r use the first and second differences of

  log B(x)=log(d^2-x^2)-log 4 - (1/2)log(1-1/(4x^2)).

Since k<=n/2, d^2-k^2 is comparable to n^2. Since k>=m and 1/m^2=o(s^2), the D_k/g_k correction in (F) is o(s^2). The r-correction in (F) is also o(s^2). Consequently

  c s^2 <= T_k+2 <= C s^2,
  phi_k:=arccos(-T_k/2) satisfies c s<=phi_k<=C s.          (H)

Thus every site in this interval is strictly in the oscillatory region; no turning point is crossed.

The total-variation and phase-block estimates needed below follow from the same explicit smooth expressions. In particular,

  sum_I |r_(k+1)-r_k| = O(n^(-1)+m^(-3)),
  Var_I(L/g_k)=O(s^2),
  Var_I(D_k/g_k)=O(m^(-2)),
  Var_I((r_k-1)^2/r_k)=O((n^(-1)+m^(-3))^2).

For the D/g estimate, differentiating its smooth extension from (E) gives |(D/g)'|=O(k^-3). For L/g, B_k is decreasing, so 1/g_k is monotone and its total variation is O(n^-2). For the last estimate, differentiate r+r^-1-2 and use (G). Hence

  sum_I |T_(k+1)-T_k|=O(s^2+m^(-2)),
  sum_I ( |Delta r_k|/s + |Delta T_k|/s^2 )=O(1+z^(-2)). (I)

The right side is bounded. More explicitly, uniformly on I,

  sup |Delta r_k|/s=O(1/(nm)+n/m^5),
  sup |Delta T_k|/s^2=O(1/n+n^2/m^5),

so the per-step errors tend to zero. On any block of length M comparable to 1/s, the two partial sums obey

  sum_block |Delta T_k|/s^2=O(m^(-1)+z^(-3))+o(1),
  sum_block |Delta r_k|/s=O(m^(-2)+n^2/m^6)=o(1).         (J)

For (J), use |(L/g)'|=O(s^2/n), |(D/g)'|=O(k^-3), and M=O(n/m). The phase changes by o(s) across such a block; moreover M times this phase variation is o(1), so the accumulated phase is uniformly close to the constant-frequency block phase.

2. ADIABATIC OSCILLATION / BLOCK OBSERVABILITY

Put v_k=(-1)^k u_k and

  Y_k=(sqrt(B_k) v_(k+1), sqrt(B_k) v_k)^T.

The recurrence at site k is exactly

  Y_k=M_k Y_(k-1),
  M_k=[[-T_k, -1/r_k],[r_k,0]],
  det(M_k)=1, trace(M_k)=-T_k=2 cos(phi_k).               (K)

A diagonalizer is

  S_k=[[1,1],[r_k exp(-i phi_k), r_k exp(i phi_k)]],
  S_k^(-1) M_k S_k=diag(exp(i phi_k),exp(-i phi_k)).       (L)

By (G)-(H), ||S_k||<=C and ||S_k^(-1)||<=C/s. Therefore, for

  c_k=S_k^(-1)Y_k,

we have

  c_k=D_k^phase (I+E_k)c_(k-1),
  ||E_k||<=C( |r_k-r_(k-1)|/s
                      +|T_k-T_(k-1)|/s^2 ).              (M)

The diagonal phase matrix D_k^phase is unitary. Equations (I) and (M) imply

  C_0^(-1)||c_m|| <= ||c_k|| <= C_0||c_m||              (N)

throughout I, for one absolute C_0. Indeed, the individual errors tend to zero and their sum is bounded, so the products of (1+||E_k||) and (1-||E_k||) stay between fixed positive constants.

For completeness, the needed lower mass per phase block is an elementary oscillation estimate, not a stationary-phase citation. Partition all but a terminal o(n) part of I into consecutive blocks I_q of length M_q nearest pi/phi at the block's first site. By (H), M_q~=1/s, there are ~=m blocks, and the block lengths sum to ~=n. By (J), the accumulated E_k error on a block is o(1). Also sup_block|phi_k-phi_start|=o(s), and M_q sup_block|phi_k-phi_start|=o(1); hence the accumulated phase is uniformly within o(1) of the frozen linear phase. The diagonalizing matrices vary by o(1) as well. Thus Y's second coordinate differs in block l2 norm by o(sqrt(M_q)||c_start||) from the frozen oscillator

  r [a exp(i q phi)+b exp(-i q phi)],

where (a,b)=c_start and r~=1. For M nearest pi/phi,

  |sum_(q=0)^(M-1) exp(2 i q phi)|
       =|sin(M phi)/sin(phi)|=O(1),

because |M phi-pi|<=phi/2. Expanding the square therefore gives

  sum_(k in I_q) |(Y_k)_2|^2 >= c M_q ||c_start||^2.     (O)

The cross term is only O(||c_start||^2), while the diagonal terms contribute M_q(|a|^2+|b|^2); the o(1) block perturbation preserves a fixed fraction of this lower bound. This works for every initial phase/amplitude, including a local node, because an entire near-period block is summed.

By (N), (O), and B_k<=C n^2,

  sum_(k in I) u_k^2
     >= c sum_q M_q ||c_m||^2/n^2
     >= c ||c_m||^2/n.

Since ||S_m||<=C and B_m>=c n^2,

  ||c_m|| >= ||Y_m||/C >= sqrt(B_m)|u_m|/C >= c n |u_m|.

Hence the unit column normalization forces

  1=sum_(k=0)^n u_k^2 >= c n u_m^2,
  u_m^2<=C/n.                                             (P)

This is the requested mass accounting: if |eta_m(m)|>=epsilon, (B) and (P) would force

  sum_(k in [m,n/2]} u_k^2 >= c n (2m+1)^2 epsilon^2/d^2
                              >= c' epsilon^2 m^2/n
                              ~= c' epsilon^2 z,

which diverges. More generally, combining (B) and (P) gives

  |eta_m(m)| <= C d/((2m+1)sqrt(n)) <= C'/sqrt(z),

proving (A).

3. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SCALAR GATE; WHAT THIS DOES NOT PROVIDE

In this regime the coherent eigenvalue satisfies mu_m->0 (as already shown from -log(mu_m)>=L/d). Thus

  q_(n,m)=(1-eta_m(m))/(1-mu_m)^2 -> 1.

Together with the preceding fixed-ratio, logarithmic, endpoint, and fixed-z results, this excludes a q->0 sequence entirely: any such sequence had already been forced into exactly this regime, where q->1. It follows by contradiction that inf_(n>=1,1<=m<=n) q_(n,m)>0. This is a non-effective existence conclusion; this attack gives no explicit universal lower bound for q, no finite-spin threshold, and no effective compiler cutoff. Through the earlier q-to-channel conversion, it gives some finite dimension-free squared coherent-EB comparison constant, but not an explicit value and not the sharp factor-two Loewner comparison. It does not by itself construct a compatible-channel counterexample, and it does not settle the unrestricted N93 theorem. It closes the specific residual coherent scalar obstruction if the transfer/block estimates above pass independent line-by-line review.

4. NORMALIZATION AND SOURCE CHECK

The column normalization used here is exactly

  sum_(k=0)^n (2k+1)(2m+1) {j j k; j j m}^2=1,

which is DLMF 34.5.14 under the stated equal-spin substitution. The recurrence is the one in RACAH_MIDDLE_COMPACTNESS.txt, obtained there from DLMF 34.5.11-13. The diagonal factor in (B) is (2m+1)/d, not sqrt(2m+1)/d; confusing it with the zero-row entry would invalidate the mass contradiction. The exact normalized zeroth entry is u_0^2=(2m+1)/d^2, consistent with (B) and with 6j orthogonality. The DLMF scope is §34.5(iii)-(iv): https://dlmf.nist.gov/34.5 .

This argument uses only the exact recurrence and exact unit column norm. It imports no uniform 6j asymptotic, Bessel approximation, finite scan, or uncharged channel assumption. The remaining proof-review target is the adiabatic block lemma application (M)-(O), especially the uniform total-variation bounds (I)-(J); if any of those fail, the correct status reverts to UNKNOWN rather than to evidence from finite scans.
