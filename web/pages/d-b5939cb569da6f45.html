CYCLE 5 PRIMARY STOCHASTIC LITERATURE AUDIT
Local inverse-polynomial support correction under substrate-preserving
catalyst channels
Date checked: 2026-10-08
Status: bounded source audit; no novelty or priority claim.

EXECUTIVE FINDING
=================
The closest source coverage is conditional, and no theorem located covers
the whole stated extension for arbitrary positive rates by weak
reversibility alone. Three useful antecedents have narrower scopes:

1. Anderson--Cappelletti--Fan--Kim (ACFK, arXiv 2023 / SIAM JADS 2025)
   gives L2 exponential ergodicity for the complex-balanced slice of the
   target. Its layered birth/death hypothesis is already supplied by the
   paired base reactions, and it allows arbitrary additional reaction
   channels. It is not an all-rate theorem.
2. Xu (arXiv v1, 2026) proves exponential ergodicity for first-order
   endotactic stochastic mass-action systems and gives a higher-order
   extension under a strict sublinear signed-drift bound plus a common
   closed-class condition. In Xu's total-molecularity terminology, the
   target's z_B-translated catalyst channels are first-order only when
   z_B=0. In a fixed-label class, a translated activation whose z_B uses
   active B coordinates has polynomial active-B rate and is not
   generically sublinear. The catalyst-only first-order subsystem also
   freezes active B and does not
   have a full active class (with varying B_i) as a common closed class.
3. Douc--Fort--Guillin (2006 preprint / SPA 2009) gives an abstract
   subgeometric Foster route for strong Markov processes. It accepts an
   already-proved extended-generator inequality of the form
   AV <= -phi(V)+b 1_C, plus irreducibility/small-set conditions. It does
   not provide a chemical-network criterion or a support correction.

The exact local term

  R^(b_i) epsilon_i h_i(a)/(1+b_i)^D,
  h_i(a)=1{sum_(j in N(i)) a_j=0},

is a candidate Lyapunov component, not a theorem hypothesis found in the
primary stochastic sources checked. A factor polynomial in b_i does not
automatically control a switch whose rate is polynomial in other
substrate coordinates z_B. This is a generator-accounting obligation, not
a recurrence counterexample. Existing strong-endotactic, tier, and
single-linkage results also do not turn weak reversibility by itself into
the desired conclusion. The target remains theorem-covered on its
complex-balanced slice; arbitrary-rate recurrence and the proposed local
correction remain outside the source results identified here.

1. SPARSE TARGET AND STOCHASTIC ORDER SEMANTICS
================================================
Take finite catalyst and substrate sets J and I and a finite bipartite
support E subset J x I. For every j, include 0 <-> A_j. For every
(j,i) in E include

  A_j <-> A_j+B_i,
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i,   k_ji >= 1.

All rates are fixed positive finite constants and all stochastic
mass-action propensities use falling factorials

  lambda_(y->y')(x)=kappa_(y->y') product_l (x_l)_(y_l).

Add finitely many reversible pairs, with the same substrate vector z_B
on each side:

  A_j+z_B <-> A_l+z_B,
  A_j+z_B <-> z_B,
  z_B in N_0^I, |z_B| <= m.

Here “catalyst source order at most one” refers only to the A-coordinate
degree: the added source contains one A_j, or no catalyst in z_B for the
reverse activation. It is not the same as total reactant molecularity.
Xu defines first order by total source molecularity at most one, so
A_j+z_B is first order exactly when z_B=0. For |z_B|>0 its total source
order is 1+|z_B|; the base reverse-autocatalysis source
(k_ji+1)A_j+B_i has order k_ji+2 >= 3.

Write N(i)={j:(j,i) in E}, I_+={i:N(i) is nonempty}, and
I_0=I\\I_+. Isolated B_i counts are invariant. The sparse base makes
each fixed-I_0-label fiber irreducible, by clearing/building each active
B_i through one chosen neighbor and using 0 <-> A_j. Added reversible
channels preserve those labels and do not remove the base paths. Thus
all recurrence/ergodicity statements here are classwise for fixed
isolated-B labels. The correction under audit is only for i in I_+:

  V(a,b)=A + sum_(i in I_+) R^(b_i)
       [1+epsilon_i 1{sum_(j in N(i))a_j=0}/(1+b_i)^D]
       + K 1{A=0} (optional),

where A=sum_j a_j and D>=m. Isolated B labels are fixed parameters and
are not terms in this local correction.

The primary-literature search was for this local inverse-polynomial
support gate and for general drift results that could accept it. This
report does not independently prove or refute the candidate's generator
inequality.

2. PRIMARY RESULTS THAT ARE CLOSEST TO THE INTERFACE
===================================================

2.1 Xu: first-order endotactic systems and SUB extensions
---------------------------------------------------------
Chuang Xu, “Exponential ergodicity of first order endotactic stochastic
reaction systems,” arXiv:2601.00176v1, submitted 2026-01-01; manuscript
dated 2026-08-24 at the HTML source checked. Primary versions:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00176v1
https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00176

Theorem 3.10 states that a first-order SMART is exponentially ergodic if
it is endotactic, strongly endotactic, or weakly reversible. First order
means each source has total molecularity <=1; products may have larger
molecularity. The theorem is not a higher-order WR result.

The higher-order Theorem 4.1 assumes a subreaction system
(Rhat,Lambdahat) contained in the target that is a first-order endotactic
SMART. For the zero-complex weak component (Rhat)^0, its net-flow matrix
A_0 must admit v_0>0 with A_0 v_0<0. The signed contribution from every
reaction outside (Rhat)^0, measured against (v_0,1), must be bounded above
for all but finitely many states by a strictly sublinear f, meaning
f(x)/||x||_1 -> 0. Finally, the class Gamma on which the conclusion is
claimed must be a common closed communicating class for both the full
system and Rhat. Under these assumptions the full system is exponentially
ergodic on Gamma. Corollary 4.2 gives easy sufficient alternatives:
outside reactions have bounded signed contribution, are nonincreasing
along v, or are componentwise decreasing; the common-class hypothesis
remains.

Mapping to the target:

* The z_B=0 catalyst-only channels can be placed in a first-order
  subnetwork containing 0 <-> A_j and A_j <-> A_l. With death and
  immigration this is a plausible stable first-order endotactic core.
  But it preserves every B count. The full paired base changes B, so a
  full-network irreducible class is not a common closed class of this
  catalyst-only core.
* Each A_j+z_B <-> A_l+z_B is a translation of the monomolecular
  catalyst conversion A_j <-> A_l, but not itself first order for
  |z_B|>0. Equal catalyst weights make the linear total-catalyst increment
  zero for a pure conversion; this does not control the local h_i jump in
  the proposed V, and the theorem's displayed drift test concerns a
  positive linear functional.
* In the deactivation pair, the reverse activation z_B -> A_j+z_B is
  enabled on the catalyst-free face. Write z_+ for its restriction to
  active B coordinates. On a fixed isolated-label class, its rate is a
  fixed factor times (b_+)_(z_+), while its catalyst-weighted increment
  is positive. If |z_+|=1 this is generally order ||b_+||, not strictly
  sublinear; if |z_+|>1 it can be higher order. If |z_+|=0, the frozen
  substrate factor is constant and this reaction can be first order in
  the projected class. No generic rate cancellation is available when
  active B occurs, because rates are arbitrary and the opposite
  deactivation source is disabled when a_j=0. Thus the SUB condition is
  not automatic.
* The base A_j -> A_j+B_i channel itself has positive B increment and
  rate proportional to a_j. To use SUB it must be included in a valid
  first-order stable core or balanced in the signed outside-drift bound;
  the paired reverse A_j+B_i -> A_j is second order and cannot be part of
  a first-order core. The common-class obstruction still applies.

The paper's examples of higher-order joints/translations therefore show
that translation structure can be useful when the exact SUB inequality
and common-class condition hold; they do not cover every translated
catalyst pair. In particular, “the channel is a translation of a
first-order reaction” alone is insufficient.

2.2 Anderson--Kim: tier/Foster positive recurrence
--------------------------------------------------
David F. Anderson and Jinsu Kim, “Some Network Conditions for Positive
Recurrence of Stochastically Modeled Reaction Networks,”
arXiv:1710.11263v3 (revision 2018-08-22); SIAM J. Appl. Math. 78(5)
(2018), 2692-2713, DOI 10.1137/17M1161427.
Primary versions:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11263v3
https://arxiv.org/html/1710.11263v3
https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1161427

Theorem 4.2 assumes that for every tier sequence x_n, the top stochastic
source tier T^{S,1}_{x_n} intersects D_{x_n}, the set of source complexes
of descending reactions along that sequence. It concludes, for every
choice of rates, positive recurrence of each state in each closed
irreducible component and finite expected time to enter the union of
those components. Corollary 4.3 uses the stronger condition that the
top deterministic tier T^{D,1} equals the top stochastic tier T^{S,1}
and D is nonempty for every tier sequence. These are sufficient
conditions; they are not a characterization of recurrence.

For the sparse target with I_+ nonempty, take the catalyst-free sequence
  a_j=0 for every j, b_i=n for every i in I_+, n -> infinity,
while fixing all isolated-B labels.
In the deterministic tier comparison using x_n vee 1, reactions
A_j+B_i -> A_j have ratio (x_n vee 1)^(A_j-(A_j+B_i))=1/n, so their
sources A_j+B_i are descending sources in D. Their stochastic
propensities vanish identically on this sequence because all A_j=0.
All other possible enabled source complexes are B-only: 0 and the z_B
complexes from reverse activations z_B -> A_j+z_B. Each activation has equal
substrate exponents on its source and product and hence is not descending
on this sequence. The A-containing catalyst conversions and deactivations
are disabled; their reverse directions do not decrease the
(x vee 1)-monomial along the sequence. Consequently the top stochastic
source tier has no source in D on this ray. The Anderson--Kim sufficient
condition fails for this explicit tier sequence. This does not imply
transience, null recurrence, or failure of another Foster function.

2.3 Strong endotactic stochastic recurrence result
--------------------------------------------------
Anderson, Cappelletti, Kim, and Nguyen, “Tier Structure of Strongly
Endotactic Reaction Networks,” arXiv:1808.05328v2 (revision 2019-10-03);
Stochastic Processes and their Applications 130(12) (2020), 7218-7259,
DOI 10.1016/j.spa.2020.07.012.
Primary versions:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05328v2
https://arxiv.org/html/1808.05328v2
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2020.07.012

Theorem 7.2 is not “strong endotactic implies CTMC recurrence.” Starting
with a strongly endotactic network whose maximal source order is m, for
each species S_i it requires a nonempty finite family of pure-species
drains a S_i -> sum_j r'_j S_j with a>=m-1 and sum_j r'_j<=a-1. It also
requires max product molecularity among these drains to be strictly less
than min source molecularity among them. One may add reactions with
source order at most m-2. Under these assumptions and any positive rate
constants, each state in a closed irreducible component is positive
recurrent, with finite expected time to the union of closed classes.

Here the base maximal source order is at least k_ji+2>=3. The provided
channels contain no pure B_i drain a B_i -> lower molecularity; for A_j,
the reverse-autocatalytic drain is mixed in B_i, and A_j+z_B -> z_B is
mixed unless z_B=0. When z_B=0, A_j -> 0 has order 1, below the
required a>=m-1>=2. Thus the additional drain hypotheses fail. This
remains true even if a separate geometric check showed that a particular
member of the family was strongly endotactic. Strong-endotactic examples
that are transient in the cited paper are not weakly reversible; they
are not counterexamples to this target.

2.4 Complex balance, product form, and exact coverage
----------------------------------------------------
Anderson, Cappelletti, Fan, and Kim, “A New Path Method for Exponential
Ergodicity of Markov Processes on Z^d, with Applications to Stochastic
Reaction Networks,” arXiv:2309.06970v2 (2023-09-29 revision); SIAM J.
Appl. Dyn. Syst. 24(2) (2025), 1668-1710, DOI 10.1137/24M1665933.
Primary versions:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06970v2
https://arxiv.org/html/2309.06970v2
https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1665933

Corollary 3.5 assumes a positive complex-balanced equilibrium c and a
partition S_0,S_1,...,S_m of all species. S_0 is nonempty and every
X_i in S_0 has both 0 -> X_i and X_i -> 0. For each X_i in later block
S_l, there must be an earlier species X_j in S_0 union ... union
S_(l-1) for which both N^+ X_j -> N^+ X_j+X_i and
N^- X_j+X_i -> N^- X_j are reactions, with N^+,N^- nonnegative
integers. The conclusion is L2 exponential ergodicity and, for each
fixed epsilon>0, a mixing-time bound tau_x^epsilon <= C_epsilon
||x|| ln ||x||. The constants are per model; the corollary does not
assert a uniform rate-box bound.

Exact fit on the complex-balanced slice: put all catalysts in S_0 and
active substrates in S_1. Each A_j has 0 <-> A_j, and every i in I_+
has a neighbor j supplying the base birth/death pair
A_j -> A_j+B_i and A_j+B_i -> A_j. The added catalyst conversions,
deactivations, and higher-order autocatalytic reactions are allowed as
additional reactions. If I_0 is empty, Corollary 3.5 applies directly to
the full species network whenever it is complex balanced. With isolated
B labels, restrict to one fixed-label class and project onto active
species. Since every reaction preserves the same isolated-B vector on
its two sides, the reaction network splits into isolated-exponent
strata; complex balance at c holds within each stratum. In the fixed
label CTMC, each stratum is multiplied by a nonnegative constant
falling-factorial factor from the frozen labels. Summing these strata
preserves complex balance at the common active-coordinate equilibrium.
Thus the corollary covers this classwise projected system on the full
complex-balanced slice. This remains a special rate subset, not an
all-positive-rates result.

Anderson, Craciun, and Kurtz, “Product-form Stationary Distributions for
Deficiency Zero Chemical Reaction Networks,” arXiv:0803.3042v4
(revision 2010-01-31); Bulletin of Mathematical Biology 72 (2010),
1947-1970. https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3042v4
https://arxiv.org/html/0803.3042

Theorem 4.1: if a deterministic mass-action system has a positive
complex-balanced equilibrium c, the stochastic model has stationary
product-Poisson law product_i exp(-c_i)c_i^(x_i)/x_i!, uniquely on the
full irreducible lattice and normalized on each closed class if
reducible. Theorem 4.2 adds the structural assumption of deficiency zero
and weak reversibility to obtain this for every positive rate choice.
Complex balance is not implied by reversibility of each edge alone.
For the sparse base with I_+ nonempty, the deficiency is
2|E|-|I_+|>0 (each active substrate has at least one incident edge).
Added pairs do not provide a blanket all-rate product-form conclusion.

2.5 Weak reversibility with one linkage class; order restriction
---------------------------------------------------------------
Anderson, Cappelletti, and Kim, “Stochastically Modeled Weakly Reversible
Reaction Networks with a Single Linkage Class,” arXiv:1904.08967v2
(revision 2020-01-16); Journal of Applied Probability 57(3) (2020),
792-810, DOI 10.1017/jpr.2020.28.
Primary versions:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08967v2
https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08967v2
https://doi.org/10.1017/jpr.2020.28

Theorem 4.1 requires a weakly reversible binary network, a single linkage
class, and for every species S_i at least one of S_i or 2S_i to be a
complex. It gives positive recurrence for the associated CTMC. The sparse
base has one linkage class, but violates binary-ness because its
reverse-autocatalytic source has order k_ji+2>=3; it also lacks a B_i or
2B_i pure complex for each active B_i. Added substrate-preserving pairs
can create further linkage classes and can add nonbinary complexes, so
the single-linkage theorem does not generally cover the extension. An
added deactivation pair may supply a missing pure B_i complex, but no
addition removes the base's nonbinary source complex. Thus even an
extension retaining one linkage class is outside Theorem 4.1's binary
hypothesis.

3. SUBGEOMETRIC FOSTER ANTECEDENT AND LOCAL DENOMINATOR BOUNDARY
================================================================
Douc, Fort, and Guillin, “Subgeometric Rates of Convergence of f-Ergodic
Strong Markov Processes,” arXiv:math/0605791v1 (2006-05-31); Stochastic
Processes and their Applications 119(3) (2009), 897-923,
DOI 10.1016/j.spa.2008.03.007.
Primary versions:
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605791v1
https://arxiv.org/html/math/0605791
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2008.03.007

Their standing setup is a time-homogeneous strong Markov process with
cad-lag paths. Theorem 3.11 says: if there is a closed petite set C, a
cad-lag V in the extended-generator domain with V>=1, an increasing
differentiable concave positive phi, and finite b such that

  A V(x) <= -phi(V(x)) + b 1_C(x)  for every x,

then the process satisfies their process-level drift condition
D(C,V,phi,b). Proposition 3.9 gives positive Harris recurrence and an
invariant probability with pi(phi o V)<infinity when additionally
sup_C V<infinity. Theorem 3.10 obtains subgeometric f-ergodicity when
some skeleton chain is irreducible, D holds, sup_C V<infinity, and
phi'(u)->0. Its rate and convergence norm trade off; it is not a
single total-variation rate for free. For polynomial rates, the usual
choice is phi(v) comparable to v^alpha, 0<alpha<1. The theorem is
abstract and contains no reaction graph assumptions and no prescribed
formula for V.

For the current local-corrector target, a separately proved drift of the
form

  LV <= C - c V/(1+log V)^D

would fit this abstract route: at large v use phi(v) comparable to
v/(log v)^D, with a positive increasing concave extension below a finite
threshold. Then phi'(v)->0 and H_phi(v)=integral_1^v ds/phi(s) is of order
(log v)^(D+1). The DFG subgeometric scale
r_*(t)=phi(H_phi^(-1)(t)) is consequently of stretched-exponential type
exp(c t^(1/(D+1))) up to polynomial factors. Their Theorem 3.10 supplies
an f-norm/rate trade-off under its skeleton irreducibility and petite-set
assumptions. This identifies an existing consequence mechanism for the
drift shape; it does not provide the model-specific drift proof or a
precedent for the local denominator itself.

On each fixed-isolated-label active class, irreducibility is available
from the sparse base network, and finite V-sublevel sets would be petite
for a nonexplosive countable-state CTMC. But those facts do not supply the
essential generator inequality. To use the subgeometric route, one must
prove the exact local-denominator candidate is in the extended-generator
domain and establish the inequality for all states, not just asymptotic
states sampled on rays. If the drift proved is only LV<=-1 outside a
finite set, that establishes recurrence (subject to nonexplosion) but
does not by itself establish subgeometric convergence. If LV<=C-cV,
the result is geometric instead. Subgeometric theory is therefore a
flexible consequence engine after a correct drift proof, not antecedent
coverage of the local gate.

The local denominator reduces one particular jump increment but does not
by itself resolve mixed-coordinate channel rates. For a deactivation
Aj+z_B -> z_B, a last-neighbor switch can change the i-th correction by
order R^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D, while the channel rate contains
(b)_(z_B). If z_B uses only B_i and b_i grows, D>=|z_B| leaves at most
an O(R^(b_i)) switch term, which could be paid by the base active-edge
removal term of order b_i R^(b_i). But if a switch of h_i is driven by
a z_B whose growing coordinates are other B_l, the product
(b)_(z_B) R^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D need not be controlled by b_i's own
denominator or by an i-edge sink. Catalyst conversions can toggle
several h_i at once. The optional K 1{A=0} also changes on a
deactivation-to-zero jump without the inverse-polynomial attenuation.
These are exact drift obligations to check against the full reaction
list; a comparison against the largest active substrate coordinate may
pay cross-coordinate terms, but the i-local denominator alone does not.
The separate Cycle 5 internal derivation uses such a max-coordinate
comparison; that derivation is not a literature antecedent and is not
independently verified by this audit. None of these observations shows
the target CTMC fails to recur.

4. SUPPORT-CORRECTION ANTECEDENT AND WHAT IS NOT COVERED
=======================================================
The direct published antecedents found use other mechanisms:

* Anderson--Kim tiers use descending source complexes on every tier
  sequence, not an indicator of local catalyst support multiplied by an
  exponential of one substrate count.
* Xu's translated-core result uses a positive linear functional and a
  signed sublinear bound, not a local exponential/reciprocal-polynomial
  correction.
* ACFK's path/Poincare method covers the complex-balanced layered
  subfamily, including this added reaction grammar; for isolated-B
  labels the statement is applied to each projected class. It requires
  complex balance and does not cover arbitrary positive rates.
* Douc--Fort--Guillin permits any V satisfying its generator drift and
  regularity conditions; it neither specifies nor appears to use this
  h_i(a) R^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D construction for reaction networks.

No exact primary stochastic antecedent was located for the proposed
local denominator under arbitrary reversible catalyst conversions and
deactivations. This is a bounded search statement only. It does not
support a novelty claim. Also, an exact Cycle 5 checker currently in the
workspace, verify_global_denominator_obstruction.py, treats a different
global factor epsilon/(1+b_1+b_2)^D and an exact ray for that global
ansatz. Its scoped failure says nothing decisive about the present local
denominator, and is not a CTMC recurrence counterexample.

5. BOUNDARY SUMMARY
===================
Established from the sources:

* Added paired channels keep the reaction graph weakly reversible, but
  standard stochastic mass-action WR alone is not a theorem of general
  higher-dimensional positive recurrence in the sources checked.
* For full positive complex-balanced parameterizations, product form
  holds (normalized on each closed class). ACFK Corollary 3.5 gives L2
  exponential ergodicity classwise: every active i in I_+ has a neighbor
  catalyst supplying the required catalytic birth/death pair. Isolated
  labels are fixed in the projected class, and the projected network is
  complex balanced on this parameter slice.
* Binary single-linkage, tier-descending, and strongly endotactic-with-
  pure-drains criteria have explicit hypotheses not met automatically
  here. Failure of these sufficient tests is not evidence of
  nonrecurrence.
* Generic subgeometric Foster theorems apply only after deriving a
  model-specific global drift and verifying the process hypotheses.
* A catalyst-linear source (A-coordinate degree one) is not necessarily
  “first order” in Xu's total-molecularity convention. A_j+z_B is first
  order only for z_B=0.

Still outside the source coverage identified here:

* all-positive-rate positive recurrence for the higher-order sparse
  paired network plus arbitrary finite reversible
  substrate-preserving conversions/deactivations;
* geometric/subgeometric rate bounds for that all-rate extension;
* validity of the proposed local inverse-polynomial support correction
  for arbitrary mixed z_B supports and arbitrary rates;
* any novelty/priority claim for that corrector.

The closest in-workspace, nonliterature comparator is the Cycle 4 proof
candidate for the sparse paired base and unary catalyst conversions
A_j <-> A_l with z_B=0. It uses
a factorial-log catalyst potential, not the present substrate-local
denominator, and does not establish the arbitrary nonzero-z_B extension.
Likewise, the Cycle 4 sparse support-switch proof is for its stated
paired grammar; its failure on a separate WR example refutes only that
instantiated ansatz, not recurrence. Neither internal result changes the
primary-literature boundary above.

Evidence/status: primary-source theorem statements checked directly in
the linked arXiv HTML/preprints and publisher metadata. This audit does
not certify an internal Foster derivation and does not report external
expert review.
