1. Precise complex-disk statement and analytic branches

For |z|<1 define
 A(z)=(1−z^2)atanh(z)/z, A(0)=1,
 B(z)=sqrt(1−z^2), B(0)=1,
 N(z,w)=[A(z)+conj(A(w))−B(z)conj(B(w))]/[1−z conj(w)].
Then N is a positive-definite kernel on the unit disk. For any finite list
of distinct disk nodes its Gram matrix is strictly positive definite.

Branch conventions are essential and are valid globally on this disk:
 atanh(z)=(1/2)Log[(1+z)/(1−z)], with the analytic principal logarithm.
The Cayley ratio (1+z)/(1−z) has positive real part, so this logarithm is
single-valued and analytic. Also Re(1−z^2)>0 for |z|<1; its principal square
root is analytic and has B(0)=1. In particular B(t)>0 for real −1<t<1.
The singularity of A at zero is removable. Its series is
 A(z)=1−sum_{n>=1} 2z^(2n)/[(2n−1)(2n+1)].
The coefficient absolute sum is finite (it telescopes), so A is bounded
analytic and continuous on the closed disk. At z=1 and z=−1 its boundary
value is zero, since (1−z^2) times the logarithmic singularity tends to zero.
B is bounded analytic, norm at most sqrt(2), and continuous up to the circle,
with zero boundary values at z=±1. Thus both multiplication operators below
are bounded; no unbounded multiplier or domain assumption is hidden.

2. Exact circle identity, including the endpoint branches

Write z=e^(i theta), 0<theta<2pi away from theta=pi. The Cayley ratio is
 i cot(theta/2). Thus the boundary atanh has imaginary part
 +(pi/4) for 0<theta<pi,
 −(pi/4) for pi<theta<2pi,
and real part (1/2)log|cot(theta/2)|. Since
 (1−z^2)/z=−2i sin(theta),
we obtain exactly
 2Re A(e^(i theta))=pi |sin(theta)|.
Meanwhile |B(e^(i theta))|^2=|1−e^(2i theta)|=2|sin(theta)|. Therefore
 A+conj(A)=(pi/2)|B|^2 on the unit circle.
At z=±1 both sides tend to zero; the equality is valid there by continuity
and in any event almost-everywhere equality would suffice for Hardy operators.
As a branch control, at z=i one gets A(i)=pi/2, B(i)=sqrt(2), giving the same
identity without a sign ambiguity. At z=−i the same real values occur.

3. Hardy operators and the load-bearing orientation

Define H2 as the closed span of nonnegative Fourier modes in L2 of the unit
circle, with inner product linear in the second argument. Let P+ be its
orthogonal projection. For bounded analytic F, multiplication M_F on H2 has
adjoint M_F* f=P+(conj(F)f). The Toeplitz operator with symbol h is
T_h f=P+(h f). It follows directly that
 M_A+M_A*=T_{A+conj(A)}=(pi/2)T_{|B|^2}=(pi/2)M_B* M_B.
The LAST equality is M_B* M_B, not M_B M_B*. This order is verified by
 <f,M_B* M_B g>=<Bf,Bg>=integral |B|^2 conj(f)g.

For every f in H2, projection contractivity gives
 ||M_B* f||=||P+(conj(B)f)||<=||conj(B)f||=||Bf||=||M_B f||.
Consequently M_B* M_B−M_B M_B*>=0. Therefore the bounded self-adjoint operator
 D=M_A+M_A*−M_B M_B*
  =(pi/2)M_B* M_B−M_B M_B*
  =(pi/2−1)M_B* M_B+(M_B* M_B−M_B M_B*)
is positive semidefinite, since pi/2>1. In fact <f,Df> is strictly positive
for every nonzero f: B has no interior zeros, so M_B is injective, and the
first term gives a strictly positive quadratic form for nonzero f. No
uniform positive lower spectral gap is claimed.

4. Reproducing kernel computation proves the claimed N, not its transpose

Let q_w(z)=1/[1−z conj(w)] be the Szego reproducing vector, satisfying
<q_z,f>=f(z) and M_F* q_w=conj(F(w))q_w. Then direct evaluation gives
 <q_z,M_A q_w>=A(z)/[1−z conj(w)],
 <q_z,M_A* q_w>=conj(A(w))/[1−z conj(w)],
 <q_z,M_B M_B* q_w>=B(z)conj(B(w))/[1−z conj(w)].
Thus <q_z,Dq_w>=N(z,w). For any finite disk nodes z_j and complex c_j,
 sum_ij conj(c_i)c_j N(z_i,z_j)
   =<sum_i c_i q_{z_i},D sum_j c_j q_{z_j}>>=0.
This establishes the exact PSD kernel claim. For distinct nodes the q_z
vectors are linearly independent: the finite rational combination has
distinct poles 1/conj(z) for every nonzero node, so a vanishing combination
has zero coefficients, and a possible z=0 constant term vanishes afterward.
Combined with strict quadratic positivity of D this gives strict finite
positive definiteness for distinct nodes.

5. Exact real-variable congruence, signs, zero endpoints and diagonal

For any real x,y put t=tanh(x/2), u=tanh(y/2), so t,u lie in (−1,1), and
 S(x)=sqrt(2)sinh(x/2), a REAL scaling which may be negative or zero.
For real t,u, the disk kernel has real values; direct hyperbolic substitution
gives exactly
 K(x,y)=T(x,y)/[2cosh((x−y)/2)]=S(x)S(y)N(t,u).
Indeed, with alpha=x/2 and beta=y/2,
 A(t)=alpha/[sinh(alpha)cosh(alpha)],
 B(t)=sech(alpha),
 1−tu=cosh(alpha−beta)/[cosh(alpha)cosh(beta)],
with continuous values at alpha=0. Substituting yields the stated T numerator.
Thus every finite real K matrix is a diagonal congruence of a PSD N matrix.
Negative values of S do not alter positivity: the quadratic form is evaluated
on coefficients S(x_i)c_i. If x=0 then S=0 and the entire K row/column is
zero, agreeing exactly with T(0,y)=0. There is no division by the vanishing
real sinh factor in the final identity or its boundary interpretation.
For x!=0,
 N(t,t)=x/t−1,
 K(x,x)=2sinh(x/2)^2[x/t−1]=x sinh(x)−cosh(x)+1=k(x).
At x=0 the diagonal is zero by continuity. Consequently the PSD claim gives
the requested pair inequality K(x,y)^2<=k(x)k(y) on ALL real x,y.
For a finite list of distinct nonzero real x_i the K matrix is strictly
positive definite. Repeated nodes or zero rows naturally destroy strictness.
