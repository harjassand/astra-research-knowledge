## 1. Exact interface and theorem

    Q_ij>0 for every i,j.

This is positivity of the hidden squared-contrast entries, not positivity of the expanded x coefficients. The partition, hidden axes, Q, L, a metric, attraction rates and coefficient-height/condition bounds are not supplied.

Then F=adj(Hess f)grad(det Hess f) has nonzero coordinate extactic E_F. ## 2. One exact hereditary family retains the full dimension

Fix the original full dimension n. For every nonempty active set I define

    f_I=(y_I^2)^TQ_II(y_I^2),
    H_I=Hess f_I,  h_I=det H_I,
    g_l^I=4 sum_(k in I)Q_lk y_k^2       (l outside I),
    R_I=product_(l outside I)g_l^I,
    G_I=R_I adj H_I grad(h_I R_I),
    D_I=-G_I dot grad.

Use R_I=1 when no coordinate is omitted. Every g_l^I is a nonzero positive-coefficient quadratic. All G_I have the same homogeneous degree4n-3 and preserve all coordinate faces by parity. The top field G_{all} is exactly F.

For t in I, put J=I\{t}. On y_t=0, H_I splits into H_J and g_t^J, while h_I=g_t^J h_J and R_I restricts to the product over formerly omitted rows. The new R_J is precisely g_t^J times that product. Its normal derivative vanishes by parity. Therefore the tangential field restricts **exactly** as

    G_I|_(y_t=0)=G_J.                           (1)

No unspecified scalar or wrong lower-dimensional canonical field is substituted. This family incorporates the curvature contribution from every removed row.

## 3. Axis slopes are independent of which coordinates remain active

Fix i,j in I, i!=j, and write a=Q_ii, b_j=Q_ij. At e_i, the active Hessian is diagonal with12a in coordinate i and4b_j in the others. Put

    c=h_I(e_i)=12a product_(k in I\{i})4Q_ik,
    rho=R_I(e_i)=product_(l outside I)4Q_li.

These are positive. The scalar h_I R_I has degree2n, so grad_i(h_I R_I)(e_i)=2n c rho and

    (G_I)_i(e_i)=n(c rho)^2/(6a).

The Hessian determinant derivative follows directly from trace identities:

    partial_jj h_I(e_i)/c
      =6Q_jj/b_j+2 sum_(k in I\{i,j})Q_jk/Q_ik-2b_j/a.

The extra product contributes

    partial_jj R_I(e_i)/rho
      =2 sum_(l outside I)Q_lj/Q_li.

Also partial_j(adj H_I)_ji(e_i)=-c/(6a). Substituting into partial_j(G_I)_j, and subtracting its radial coordinate value, gives

    [partial_j(G_I)_j(e_i)-(G_I)_i(e_i)]/[(c rho)^2/2]
       = Btilde_ij,

    Btilde_ij=3Q_jj/Q_ij^2
               +sum_(k outside {i,j})Q_jk/(Q_ik Q_ij)
               -(n+1)/Q_ii.                     (2)

Every term involves the **full original matrix** and the full original n. Thus the sign in(2) is identical on every active set containing i,j. This identity includes the omitted-row factors; using only the principal Q_II formula with N+1 would be incorrect.

All off-diagonal transverse linearization entries vanish by sign symmetry. If Btilde_ij>0 for every j in I\{i}, the projective field -G_I has a strictly negative diagonal linearization at e_i and hence an open local attracting basin.

## 4. The bad-slope graph has no directed cycle

Define normalized correlations

    r_ij=Q_ij/sqrt(Q_ii Q_jj),   r_ii=1.

QPD's two-by-two principal inequalities and strict entrywise positivity give0<r_ij<1 for i!=j. These real quantities are proof devices, never acquisition inputs. Equation(2) is Q_ii^-1 times

    B_ij=3/r_ij^2+sum_(k outside {i,j})r_jk/(r_ik r_ij)-(n+1).       (3)

Draw a directed edge i->j if B_ij<=0. We prove this graph acyclic, including zero-slope edges.

Suppose distinct indices i_1,...,i_ell form a directed cycle, ell>=2, with i_(ell+1)=i_1. Put e_t=r_(i_t,i_(t+1)), so0<e_t<1. Sum(3) along that cycle. The first terms satisfy

    3 sum_t e_t^-2 >3ell.                       (4)

For each index k outside the cycle, its ell positive ratio terms have product

    product_t r_(i_(t+1),k)/[r_(i_t,k)e_t]
       =product_t e_t^-1>1.

Arithmetic-geometric mean therefore makes their sum greater than ell. Summing over n-ell outside indices contributes at least ell(n-ell), with strictness whenever such indices exist.

For internal k, there are ell(ell-2) terms, since the two endpoints are excluded in each row. When ell=2 there are none. When ell>2, the product of their numerator/denominator ratios excluding e_t is1: for fixed k in the cycle, the full cyclic product r_next,k/r_current,k telescopes to1; removing the incoming and outgoing endpoint terms leaves the incoming-edge correlation divided by the outgoing-edge correlation. Multiplication over all k cancels these ratios. The remaining product is

    product_t e_t^[-(ell-2)]>1.

AMGM makes the total internal contribution greater than ell(ell-2). Combining these lower bounds with the ell copies of -(n+1) gives

    sum_t B_(i_t,i_(t+1))
       >3ell+ell(n-ell)+ell(ell-2)-ell(n+1)=0.     (5)

This contradicts every cycle edge being nonpositive. The bad-slope graph is acyclic.

Every finite induced subgraph has a sink. Therefore every nonempty active I with at least two elements contains an i with B_ij>0 for every other active j. By(2), this is a strictly attracting axis of -G_I. No topological ordering or hidden slope computation is supplied to the algorithm.

## 5. Extactic induction with fixed analytic coefficients

For each I of size N let E_I=det[D_I^r y_i], r=0,...,N-1. Base N=1 is E_I=y_i!=0. Suppose all smaller active sets have nonzero extactic. Choose an attracting sink i from Section4. The polynomial minor M formed from columns j!=i and rows0,...,N-2 restricts on y_i=0, by(1), to E_(I\{i}). Thus M is nonzero.

If E_I=0, use M to solve over the rational-function field

    D_I^r y_i=sum_(j!=i)c_j D_I^r y_j,
        r=0,...,N-2.

The zero full determinant provides the same relation at r=N-1. Differentiating and subtracting the shifted rows gives M(D_I c)=0, hence D_I c_j=0. Every c_j is homogeneous of degree zero because every coordinate column has the same degree in each row. Therefore each is also invariant under radial normalization.

Choose a generic initial point in the sink's open attracting chart with M!=0. The c_j are finite there. Their constant initial values give a fixed linear identity y_i=sum c_j(initial)y_j locally. Analytic continuation of the normalized polynomial projective ODE extends this identity to every finite time, even if a rational representative later has a pole. Dividing by y_i and taking the attracting limit gives1=0. Hence E_I!=0.

Induction reaches the full active set. D_I=-G_I changes the implemented positive-field extactic by only the nonzero factor(-1)^[N(N-1)/2], so E_F is nonzero as claimed. Tied rates do not matter: the proof requires only that every transverse ratio tends to zero.
