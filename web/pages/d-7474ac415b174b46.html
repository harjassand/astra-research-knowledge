## Strongest independent claim

For the closed two-species conversion network

\[
mA\longrightarrow (m-1)A+B,\qquad
mB\longrightarrow A+(m-1)B,
\]
with fixed total count \(A+B=V\), integer \(m\ge1\), and predictable rates
\(k_A,k_B\in[\kappa,K]\) where \(0<\kappa\le K\), the previous sharp
finite-horizon exit result can be extended to an explicit uniform return
certificate. If \(m\le L<V/2\) and

\[
\mu_V:=V^{1-m}\{\kappa(V-L)_m-K(L)_m\}>0,
\]
then every admissible history-dependent controller, from every state
\(j=A<L\), hits \(L\) by a stopping time \(\tau_L\) satisfying

\[
\mathbb E_j\tau_L\le \frac{L-j}{\mu_V}.
\]
The right boundary has the same bound after exchanging \(A,B\). This is a
return-to-core result for this nearest-neighbor conservative family, not for
general endotactic networks.

There is also an explicit exponential return tail. Put

\[
S_L=\kappa+K(L/V)^m,\quad
\theta_V=\frac{\mu_V}{2VS_L},\quad
\alpha_V=\frac{\mu_V^2}{4VS_L}.
\]
Then \(0<\theta_V\le1/2\), and, for every \(t\ge0\),

\[
\Pr_j(\tau_L>t)\le
\min\{1,\exp(\theta_V(L-j)-\alpha_Vt)\}.
\]
Writing \(G=L-(m-1)\), a uniform form is

\[
\sup_{j\in\{m-1,\ldots,L-1\}}
\Pr_j\!\left(\tau_L>\frac{4G}{\mu_V}\right)
\le \exp\!\left(-\frac{\mu_VG}{2VS_L}\right).
\]
All constants are rational when the rates and \(V,L\) are rational/integer
inputs.

## Derivation

At count state \(j\), the actual upward and downward intensities are

\[
b_j=k_BV^{1-m}(V-j)_m,\qquad d_j=k_AV^{1-m}(j)_m,
\]
where \((x)_m=x(x-1)\cdots(x-m+1)\), interpreted as zero if \(x<m\).
The state space is the finite interval \(\{m-1,\ldots,V-m+1\}\); each jump
changes \(j\) by one and total intensity is at most \(2KV\). The process is
therefore nonexplosive at each fixed volume, even for predictable controls.

For \(j<L\), control bounds give

\[
b_j-d_j\ge V^{1-m}\{\kappa(V-j)_m-K(j)_m\}\ge\mu_V.
\]
The last expression decreases with \(j\) on the count interval: the first
falling factorial decreases and the second increases. Stopping the count
process at \(\tau_L\), its value never exceeds \(L\). The stopped compensator
identity therefore gives

\[
\mu_V\,\mathbb E(t\wedge\tau_L)
\le \mathbb E[J_{t\wedge\tau_L}]-j\le L-j.
\]
Monotone convergence proves the mean bound.

For the tail, consider the killed function

\[
Y_t=\mathbf 1_{\{\tau_L>t\}}e^{-\theta(J_t-j)}.
\]
On a live state \(j<L\), its generator is no larger than the generator
without killing, because an upward jump from \(L-1\) is killed. The expression
for the latter generator divided by \(Y_t\) is increasing in the downward
rate and decreasing in the upward rate. Thus its maximum over all allowed
controls is attained at

\[
\bar b_j=\kappa V^{1-m}(V-j)_m,\qquad
\bar d_j=K V^{1-m}(j)_m.
\]
For \(j<L\), \(\bar b_j-\bar d_j\ge\mu_V\) and
\(\bar b_j+\bar d_j\le VS_L\). Since \(\mu_V\le\kappa V\le VS_L\),
\(\theta_V\le1/2\). For \(0\le\theta\le1/2\), \(e^{\pm\theta}-1\)
is bounded above by \(\pm\theta+\theta^2\) (using
\(e^{1/2}<2\)). Hence

\[
\frac{\mathcal L_tY}{Y}
\le-\theta_V\mu_V+\theta_V^2VS_L
=-\frac{\theta_V\mu_V}{2}=-\alpha_V.
\]
Dynkin/Gronwall yields \(\mathbb E Y_t\le e^{-\alpha_Vt}\). On
\(\{\tau_L>t\}\), \(J_t\le L-1\), so
\(Y_t\ge e^{-\theta_V(L-j)}\), proving the tail bound. This proof uses only
pointwise predictable-rate bounds and the finite-state process; it assumes no
time-homogeneous policy.

The condition \(\mu_V>0\) is effective from rational inputs. Let

\[
f(x)=\kappa(1-x)^m-Kx^m,\qquad
p=\frac{1}{1+(K/\kappa)^{1/m}}.
\]
Then \(f\) is strictly decreasing and \(f(q)>0\) iff \(q<p\). For rational
\(q\in(0,1/2)\) with \(f(q)>0\), set

\[
\eta=\frac{f(q)}{2m(\kappa+K)},\quad u=q+\eta,\quad \delta=f(u)>0.
\]
The derivative bound \(|f'(x)|\le m(\kappa+K)\) implies
\(f(u)\ge f(q)/2\), so \(u<p\). For even
\[
V\ge V_0:=\min\{v\in2\mathbb N:
v\ge\max(2m,m/q,(m-1)/\eta)\},\quad L=\lfloor qV\rfloor,
\]
we have \(m\le L<V/2\), and

\[
\frac{\mu_V}{V}
=\kappa\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}\left(1-\frac{L+r}{V}\right)
-K\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}\frac{L-r}{V}
\ge \kappa(1-u)^m-Kq^m\ge f(u)=\delta.
\]
Consequently the return mean is at most \(q/\delta\), independent of \(V\)
above this explicit threshold, and the return tail exponent is linear in \(V\).

For the static lower extremal controller \((k_A,k_B)=(K,\kappa)\), the exact
mean return from \(m-1\) to \(L\) is also rational. With

\[
W_j=(\kappa/K)^j\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}\binom{V-2r}{j-r},\quad
b_j^- = \kappa V^{1-m}(V-j)_m,
\]
the standard backward-equation differences telescope to

\[
\mathbb E_{m-1}^{-}\tau_L
=\sum_{k=m-1}^{L-1}
\frac{\sum_{r=m-1}^{k}W_r}{b_k^-W_k}.
\]
The worker script verifies this equation exactly for its fixtures.

## Resource audit and earned limitation

For fixed \(m\), rational \(\kappa,K,q\) of total bit size \(B\), the
return-certificate arithmetic uses \(O(m)\) products/comparisons on integers
with \(O(B+m\log V)\) bits. Its volume threshold is computable exactly, but
may be numerically exponential in \(B\). For the previous exit-risk ratio,
the \(O(mV)\)-operation weight recurrence can use a common denominator
\(b^V\) for \(\rho=a/b=\kappa/K\); each summed weight then has numerator bit
length at most \(O(V(m+B))\). With classical integer multiplication this
gives a pseudopolynomial bit-time bound in \(V\), not a polynomial bound in
the binary length \(\log V\). A horizon \(T\) and target error add their
encoded bit lengths to the exact comparison. The exact static mean-return
formula below is an exact finite sum, but I have not established a tight
output/common-denominator bit bound for that sum; the rational drift upper
bound avoids relying on it.

The return mechanism does permit a bounded expected number of actual reaction
events before reaching the core:

\[
\mathbb E N_{\tau_L}\le
K V\{1+(L/V)^m\}\frac{L-j}{\mu_V}.
\]
For fixed rational data with \(q<p\), this is \(O(V)\); a simulator still
needs a computable controller, propensities, and a finite-precision waiting
time/randomness policy. The probability proof is not a finite-bit simulator.

There is no uniform positive escape exponent over all short rational inputs.
For the true bimolecular case \(m=2\), choose \(M=2^b\), \(\kappa=1\),
\(K=M^2\), \(p=1/(M+1)\), and \(q=M/(M+1)^2=p-(M+1)^{-2}\). The rate and threshold
encodings have \(O(b)\) bits, but

\[
\Delta=2D(q\Vert p)\sim(M+1)^{-3}=2^{-3b+O(1)}.
\]
This follows by the quadratic Taylor expansion of Bernoulli relative entropy
at \(q=p\). The exact scalar exit theorem gives exponent \(V\Delta\) for
this instance, so a fixed input-independent claim of safety to horizons
\(e^{\gamma V}\) fails for large enough \(b\), even though every fixed
instance has a positive exponent. This is a margin-dependence result, not a
lower bound on all algorithms. For this family the explicit \(V_0\) above is
on the order of \(M^3\); this is the threshold of this conservative return
certificate, not a proved necessary volume.
