## 5. Quantum Gibbs comparison without a false exponential order

For any common reducing projector T of A and B with ||B||<=v, minmax INSIDE each T block gives ordered eigenvalue changes at most v. Consequently

    p_T(A+B)<=exp(2beta v)p_T(A).

This is a scalar trace consequence of common-block minmax; exp is not assumed operator-monotone. Taking A=H0,B=V,T=P proves the initial norm-based comparison.

There is a stronger amplitude-independent comparison. Split H0=H_out+H_touch, where H_touch contains the positive original penalties meeting X. H_out acts outside X, 0<=H_touch<=M I, M<=sum_touch h_a<=g0s. If T reduces A and H_touch, ordered eigenvalues lie between those of A and A+M. Thus the bad numerator is at most its value before addition, while the total partition function is at least exp(-beta M) times its previous value. Adding H_touch costs at most exp(beta M) in the bad fraction. Deleting it in the reverse comparison also costs at most exp(beta M).

Compute the union FIRST in rho_out of H_out. Every F_X translation costs ZERO since rho_out is maximally mixed on X. A bath Pauli of weight<=q costs at most exp(beta g0q) in the commuting H_out Gibbs measure. Hence

    p_P(H_out)<=4^s M_b exp(beta g0q) p1(H_out).

Positive comparison on the P1 blocks gives p1(H_out)<=exp(beta M)p1(H0). Next H_out+V factorizes across X/X^c and P=I_X tensor P_out; the X partition factor cancels exactly for ANY V. Finally add H_touch on the P blocks, costing exp(beta M). Therefore

    p_H(Pbad)<=4^s M_b exp[beta(2M+g0q)] p1,0.
