Question screened
-----------------
Let x range over F_2^n. An unknown invertible affine map gives u=Lx+c. In
most-significant-first order the hidden rank bits are

    q_i(x) = u_i + g_i(u_1,...,u_{i-1}),    deg(g_i) <= d,

with addition over F_2. The map Q=(q_1,...,q_n) is a permutation. The only
oracle response is the chosen-pair bit

    C(x,y) = [int Q(x) < int Q(y)].

No coordinate labels, bit labels, ranks, target coefficients, hidden affine
map, prefix toggles, or fibers are supplied. The proposed output is an exact
full rank decoder, with arbitrary-prefix execution after acquisition.

Model-count check for comparison with query lower bounds
--------------------------------------------------------
For a known latent basis and fixed significance order, the number of free
coefficients in all g_i is

    D = sum_{i=1}^n sum_{k=0}^d binom(i-1,k)
      = sum_{k=0}^d binom(n,k+1).

Each coefficient choice gives a distinct permutation and therefore a distinct
total order. Thus any exact identification procedure for this subclass needs
at least D binary answers in the worst case. For fixed d>=2, n M_d is about
(d+1)D asymptotically. This is a counting observation from the stated model,
not an external-literature novelty claim. It assumes the full output order,
not merely approximate prediction, must be identified.

Findings
--------

1. Exact active preference elicitation is the closest comparator-interface
   prior. Booth, Chevaleyre, Lang, Mengin, and Sombattheera, “Learning various
   classes of models of lexicographic orderings” (2009), section 4, PDF p. 8,
   gives an active teacher to whom the learner submits an outcome pair
   {alpha,beta}; the teacher returns which outcome is preferred. The paper
   studies six lexicographic preference classes over binary attributes and
   gives exact identification protocols attaining the log-of-class-size
   lower bound. Its model permits local preferences to depend on more
   important attribute values (PDF pp. 3-4); when the latent attributes u are
   known, the target q_i=u_i+g_i(u_<i) is a lexicographic order with such
   conditional binary local preferences. The paper's unrestricted
   conditional-preference class has exponentially many local tables; it does
   not impose a low-degree coefficient representation or recover a hidden
   affine attribute basis. Thus it is a strong exact-oracle/model prior, but
   does not establish the target polynomial query bound or decoder.

   Primary URL: https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~lang/papers/bclms09.pdf
   Local retrieved copy: lang_2009.pdf
   SHA-256: 65dbaddf33146859d3ef2ea482dab1a56ce8235e3b18632705dbfeb0de3aa8d5
   Exact scope: PDF p. 8, “Preference elicitation/active learning”; PDF pp. 3-4,
   conditional local-preference model; p. 8, Proposition 4 and its query-count
   table. The paper explicitly says Dombi et al. (2007) already settled the
   fixed-preference/unconditional-importance case.

   Dombi, Imreh, Vincze, “Learning lexicographic orders,” European Journal of
   Operational Research 183(2), 748-756 (2007), DOI
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2006.10.029, is the cited earlier active
   result for that fixed local-preference case. Its attribute criteria are
   explicit; it does not supply an unknown affine feature map.

2. Broader pairwise-comparison learning has a different guarantee. Balcan,
   Vitercik, and White, “Learning Combinatorial Functions from Pairwise
   Comparisons” (arXiv:1605.09227, 2016), defines comparator learning and
   treats pairwise comparison data for several combinatorial function classes
   (including sparse Fourier support and bounded nonlinear interactions).
   Its principal guarantee is PAC/distributional small comparison error, with
   separation conditions for many classes, not exact identification of a
   finite lexicographic rank permutation from adversarially chosen pairs.
   It supplies no hidden affine triangular decoder.

   Primary URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09227
   PDF URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.09227
   Local retrieved copy: balcan_vitercik_white_2016.pdf
   SHA-256: 916d407e04e2cd8015e42f5b1ce28ec3946491752c0fff00c4d75316366337ea
   Exact scope: PDF pp. 1-3, especially section 1.1 and Table 1 (definition,
   guarantees, and listed classes).

3. The triangular map form itself is classical algebra. Maubach, “Triangular
   polynomial maps in characteristic p” (arXiv:1106.5800), section 2.1, PDF
   pp. 4-5, defines strictly triangular maps and gives the representation
   (x_1+g_1, x_2+g_2(x_1), ..., x_n+g_n(x_1,...,x_{n-1})); it states that
   strictly triangular maps are automatically automorphisms. Over F_2 the
   target's quadratic maps are a subfamily in the visible latent coordinates.
   The article concerns polynomial automorphisms, iteration, and their
   algebraic structure; it does not acquire an unknown affine encoding from
   one-bit rank comparisons. The operation's novelty candidate is therefore
   not the triangular permutation family by itself.

   Primary URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5800
   PDF URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.5800
   Local retrieved copy: maubach_2011.pdf
   SHA-256: cfb9296a2e9d490de200c5f4ced5eb33090e3218a036a913a4db6f598507536b
   Exact scope: PDF pp. 4-5, section 2.1. The arXiv file is version 3; its
   first page contains its version/date metadata.

4. Triangular multivariate-quadratic cryptanalysis is also established, but
   with substantially richer observations. Wolf, Braeken, and Preneel,
   “Efficient Cryptanalysis of RSE(2)PKC and RSSE(2)PKC,” Cryptology ePrint
   Archive 2004/237, describes step-wise triangular public-key schemes based
   on multivariate quadratic polynomials and inversion/structural attacks
   using chains of kernels of the private-key polynomials. The ePrint record
   says the attacks compute message/signature from ciphertext/message or
   recover an equivalent secret key. Those interfaces expose public
   polynomial equations and cryptosystem outputs; they are not a single
   chosen-pair lexicographic comparison bit. This is a structural/cryptanalytic
   control, not a comparator-only prior.

   Primary URL: https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/237
   Direct PDF URL: https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/237.pdf
   Retrieved evidence: the primary ePrint record's title, authors, abstract,

   Exact scope: ePrint record abstract and publication metadata; SCN 2004,
   LNCS 3352, pp. 294-309 as given in the ePrint record.

5. Affine equivalence of explicit quadratic systems is a further adjacent
   cryptographic/computer-algebra area. Berthomieu, Faugere, and Perret,
   “Polynomial-Time Algorithms for Quadratic Isomorphism of Polynomials: The
   Regular Case” (arXiv:1307.4974), starts from explicit polynomial tuples and
   asks for an invertible variable transformation taking one tuple to another
   (IP1S/IP). The input contains coefficient representations of quadratic
   forms, much more than ordinal comparison answers. It does not claim to
   recover this exact hidden rank map from the comparator.
