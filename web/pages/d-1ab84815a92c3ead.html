POSITIVE PARTIAL-FLAG / SCHUR-MOMENT AUDIT
2026-10-08
Scope: GLOBAL_CAPTURE.txt §§5–7 and JOINT_CAPTURE.txt §§6–8 only.

VERDICT

The positive fiber normalization and radial envelope are valid under the stated fixed-spectrum and typical-carrier hypotheses. The displayed thermal remainder is indeed n^{-2+o(1)} uniformly for k <= C sqrt(n log n), m >= c n; after averaging the exact Schur moments, the corresponding identity-spin thermal contribution is O(n^{-2}). The RSK tail/moment constants in (8.5)–(8.6) check out. The weighted partial-flag beta ratio is exact. These are conditional analytic estimates, not consequences of the finite numerical checks listed in GLOBAL_CAPTURE §10.

1. POSITIVE FIBER MIXTURE AND ITS NORMALIZATION

Write lambda=(lambda_1,lambda_2,lambda_3), k=lambda_1-lambda_2, m=lambda_2-lambda_3, r=k+1, and d=dim V_lambda. For a two-plane W with orthonormal frame, let G_W=W^* A W, A=diag(p_1,p_2,p_3). Schur's lemma gives the exact resolution

  integral P_W dW = (r/d) I,

so, up to the common det(A)^{lambda_3} factor, the Gibbs operator has the positive representation

  pi_lambda(A) = (d/r) integral pi(A^{1/2}) P_W pi(A^{1/2}) dW.

The Cartan product on the top U(2) fiber is Sym^k(W) tensor det(W)^m. QR of A^{1/2}W therefore makes the fiber Gram operator det(G_W)^m Sym^k(G_W). In particular, after normalizing, this is a genuine positive mixture of rotated spin-k/2 Gibbs states, with classical plane weight proportional to

  w(W) = det(G_W)^m Tr Sym^k(G_W).

No frame is observed or supplied by this representation.

Here is the normalization constant behind (7.2). Put Z_0=Tr Sym^k diag(p_1,p_2), and divide w by (p_1p_2)^m Z_0. If v is the normal line to W and u=1-|v_3|^2, the CP^2 radial Haar law is 2u du. Cauchy–Binet gives

  det(G_W)/(p_1p_2)
   = 1-(1-p_3/p_1)|v_1|^2-(1-p_3/p_2)|v_2|^2.

For p_3/p_i <= qbar=(1+b/a)/2<1 eventually, this is at most exp(-g_0 u), g_0=1-qbar. Compression eigenvalue interlacing gives Tr Sym^k(G_W)<=Z_0.

For the matching lower normalization, on u<=1/m choose the graph frame of W over span(e_1,e_2). Its compression satisfies ||G_W-diag(p_1,p_2)||<=4p_1u. Since p_1/p_2 is uniformly bounded, Weyl's inequality gives both eigenvalues at least p_i(1-C_a u); hence Tr Sym^k(G_W)>=c_a Z_0 for k<=m. Also det(G_W)/(p_1p_2)>=1-u, so its mth power is bounded below by (1-1/m)^m>=c>0. The cap has Haar mass integral_0^{1/m}2u du=m^{-2}. Thus the normalized plane partition function is at least c_a m^{-2}, proving the marginal envelope

  d nu(u) <= C_a m^2 2u exp(-g_0 m u) du.                 (A)

The constants depend only on the fixed a,c envelope. This lower-cap argument needs k<=m; it does not assert a uniform lower bound if k is much larger than m.

2. TEMPERATURE AND THE THERMAL REMAINDER

Let mu_1>=mu_2 be the eigenvalues of G_W and gamma_W=log(mu_1/mu_2). Since p_1>=p_2,

  A >= p_2 I-(p_2-p_3)|e_3><e_3|.

After compression to W, ||P_W e_3||^2=u, so mu_2>=p_2-(p_2-p_3)u; also mu_1<=p_1. Equivalently, for x in W, orthogonality x^*v=0 gives |x_3|^2<=u. As p_3=b is bounded away from zero,

  0<=gamma_W<=gamma_c+C_a u,
  gamma_c=log(p_1/p_2)=O(n^{-1/2}).                       (B)

This verifies the stated Rayleigh bound (7.3). Combining (A) and (B), and using j=k/2=o(m), absorbs exp(3j C_a u) into exp(-g_0 m u). Integrating u and u^2 against the resulting envelope yields

  E gamma_W <= gamma_c+C/m,
  E[gamma_W^2 exp(3j gamma_W)]
       <= C exp(3j gamma_c)(gamma_c^2+m^{-2}).             (C)

For example, m^2 integral 2u exp(-c m u)du=O(1), while m^2 integral 2u*u^2 exp(-c m u)du=O(m^{-2}); the cross term gamma_c/m is absorbed by gamma_c^2+m^{-2}.

Averaging the exact fiber thermal term in GLOBAL_CAPTURE (6.2), whose internal part is (2h+2h^2 exp(3h))/m^2 with h=j gamma_W, gives

  C/m^2 { j(gamma_c+m^{-1})
          +j^2 exp(3j gamma_c)(gamma_c^2+m^{-2}) }.       (D)

On k<=C sqrt(n log n), m>=c n, this is bounded by

  n^{-2} O(log(n) exp(C sqrt(log n))) = n^{-2+o(1)},

since j gamma_c=O(sqrt(log n)) and log(log n)+C sqrt(log n)=o(log n). The leakage term 12/(K+2) is separate and is not part of this thermal remainder. This establishes the claimed scaling, conditional on the exact channel estimate (6.2) and typical-carrier window.

For JOINT_CAPTURE's exact-spin branch, h=k gamma_c/2 and the same expression is m^{-2} E[1+h+h^2 exp(3h)]. The RSK moment bound below makes this O(n^{-2}) on the good set. For tapered branches, (C) combined with E[k exp(Ak/sqrt(n))]=O_A(sqrt(n)) gives the stated O(1/(sqrt(n)q)) scale for the k-dependent thermal term in (8.4).

3. WEIGHTED PARTIAL-FLAG MOMENT

The operator inequality JOINT_CAPTURE (6.1) has a precise positive-component proof. For the spin basis index q=0,...,k in P_0, the root SU(2)_(13) highest weight is M_q=m+k-q>=m. At number N<=m, the squared norm of the rotated component is binom(M_q,N)u^N(1-u)^(M_q-N). Against radial measure 2u du, the ratio of the u-weighted to unweighted beta integral is

  Beta(N+3,M_q-N+1)/Beta(N+2,M_q-N+1)
       = (N+2)/(M_q+3) <= (N+2)/(m+3).

Right-K twirling makes these positive rank-one components sum to the full unweighted Schur average (r/d)P_N. Since every component has the displayed ratio bounded by the same factor, positivity gives

  H_N <= [(N+2)/(m+3)] (r/d) P_N.

Thus on filter support N<=K+1 the factor is at most (K+3)/(m+3), which is O(K/m) when K=o(m). The estimate is not uniformly O(1/m) for all N<=m: at N near m its factor is order one. The JOINT use is on the low-number support, so this qualification is consistent with the stated asymptotic regime.

4. RSK SCHUR MOMENTS AND CONSTANTS

For iid letters with probabilities p_1>=p_2>=p_3, RSK shape lambda has probability f^lambda s_lambda(p). The same expression follows directly from Schur–Weyl decomposition: the lambda block of A^{tensor n} has trace Tr pi_lambda(A) dim(S_lambda)=s_lambda(p)f^lambda. Let N_i(t) count letter i through time t and V_i=max_{t<=n}|N_i(t)-p_i t|. A weakly increasing subsequence on a three-letter alphabet consists of a 1-block, then a 2-block, then a 3-block. Its length is the maximum over two cuts of the corresponding three interval counts. Replacing each interval count by its mean plus endpoint deviations gives

  lambda_1 <= n p_1+2(V_1+V_2+V_3).

Greene's theorem and the valid choice of the disjoint subsequences consisting of all 1s and all 2s give lambda_1+lambda_2>=N_1(n)+N_2(n). Therefore

  k=lambda_1-lambda_2
    <=2lambda_1-N_1(n)-N_2(n)
    <=n(p_1-p_2)+5(V_1+V_2+V_3).

The coefficient 5 is correct: after substituting the first bound, the endpoint term |(N_1-np_1)+(N_2-np_2)| equals |N_3-np_3|<=V_3, adding one more V_3 to the coefficient 4 sum.

Each centered letter-count process is a martingale with increments of range one. Maximal Azuma/Hoeffding gives Pr(V_i>x sqrt(n))<=2 exp(-x^2/2) (a deliberately loose valid constant). Since n(p_1-p_2)=c sqrt(n), if k/sqrt(n)>c_1+x then some V_i>x sqrt(n)/15, so

  Pr(k/sqrt(n)>c_1+x)<=6 exp(-x^2/450).

Gaussian-tail integration yields, for every fixed A>=0,

  E exp(Ak/sqrt(n))<=C_A,
  E[k exp(Ak/sqrt(n))]<=C_A sqrt(n).

Because gamma_c sqrt(n) is uniformly bounded, these imply E[h+h^2 exp(3h)]=O(1) for h=k gamma_c/2. Thus the identity-spin thermal contribution is O(n^-2) after restricting to good carriers with m>=c n. Separately, GLOBAL_CAPTURE/TRANSVERSE_GATE §9 supplies the good-set cutoff k<=C_alpha sqrt(n log n), m>=m_0 n and bad mass <=n^(-alpha-2); this follows there from an entropy tail, not from (8.6) alone.

Primary prior support checked: O'Donnell and Wright, “Efficient quantum tomography II,” arXiv:1612.00034v1 [quant-ph], submitted 2016-11-30, https://arxiv.org/html/1612.00034. Theorem 1.2 states Greene's characterization; Definition 1.3 defines iid alpha-random words and the Schur–Weyl distribution as their RSK shape; Fact 1.15 identifies weak Schur sampling on rho^tensor n with that distribution. These support the standard identifications, not the present maximal-martingale tail calculation.

5. ASSUMPTIONS VERSUS FINITE EVIDENCE

Analytic scope/inputs: fixed 1/3<a<1/2 and bounded positive c; p_1>=p_2>p_3>0 for large n; exact U(3) Schur decomposition, Cartan-product top fiber and Schur projector average; k<=m for the cap normalization; k=o(m) for exponential absorption; and the good Schur window with m>=m_0 n, k<=C_alpha sqrt(n log n), bad mass <=n^(-alpha-2). The last window is the separate entropy argument cited above. The moment bounds (8.5)–(8.6) are analytic and do not require finite sampling.

Finite evidence only: GLOBAL_CAPTURE §10 reports finite taper/beta cases, 16 positive-fiber trace normalizations and a few finite cubature channels with small numerical discrepancies. These can check conventions and those instances; they do not prove the universal cap lower bound, envelope (A), the beta-ratio operator inequality, the Schur tail, or the asymptotic n^{-2+o(1)} estimate. I found no counterexample in the audited claims and make no novelty assessment.
