1. EXACT THEOREM AND SUPPLIED TARGET

Let B be any finite-dimensional local system, sigma>0 a fixed faithful density,
and Braw:B->B1 tensor B2 ONE full-domain CPTP broadcaster, with B1,B2 identified
with B. Let E be any nonempty family of joint states rho_RB, with arbitrary
finite untouched references, such that every local marginal is rho_B=sigma.
Suppose for this ONE broadcaster, on the WHOLE family,

 max_j T[(id_R tensor Braw_j)(rho_RB),rho_RB] <= b,  0<=b<=1,

where T=half the trace norm. The family may be infinite and its reference
dimensions may vary. No rank/faithfulness condition is required for the tested
rho_RB, and no mutual-information assumption is used.

Then there is ONE full-domain CPTP broadcaster Bfix, constructed from Braw and
sigma ONLY, whose two marginals equal a channel Phi_fix satisfying

 Phi_fix(sigma)=sigma,
 sup_{rho in E} T[(id_R tensor Phi_fix)(rho),rho]
     <= min{1, b+sqrt(delta)+delta/2}
     <= min{1, sqrt(b)+(3/2)b}
     <= min{1, (5/2)sqrt(b)},

where delta=T(Phi(sigma),sigma)<=b and Phi=(Braw_1+Braw_2)/2. Thus Phi_fix is
exactly sigma stationary and self-compatible. There is no inverse minimum-
eigenvalue factor, dimension factor, state-dependent local channel, or
reference dependence. This theorem does NOT assert KMS symmetry of Phi_fix.

The task supplied only the exact fixed-margin repair target and the old gate
scope. The dominating-operator filter, channel completion, and quantitative
reference bound below were derived independently here. No new source or
sibling proof was needed for these finite-matrix calculations.

2. FIRST MAKE ONE COMMON RAW MARGINAL

Let Swap denote the unitary exchanging B1 and B2. Set

 Bsym(X)=[Braw(X)+Swap Braw(X) Swap*]/2,
 Phi=(Braw_1+Braw_2)/2,
 eta=Phi(sigma).

Bsym is full-domain CPTP and has both marginals Phi. Convexity gives the same
per-marginal residual <=b for every reference/state of E. Since rho_B=sigma,
every output (id_R tensor Phi)(rho) has local marginal eta. Trace-norm
contraction under tracing out R gives

 delta=T(eta,sigma)<=b.

This fixed output marginal, shared by ALL family members and references, is
the load-bearing hypothesis for the gentle repair. No leakage/MI conversion
is needed. eta may be rank deficient even though sigma is faithful.

3. ONE EXPLICIT FULL-DOMAIN STATE-REPAIR CHANNEL

For any density eta and any faithful sigma define

 Delta=eta-sigma,
 M=(eta+sigma+|Delta|)/2.

Both M-eta=(-Delta+|Delta|)/2 and M-sigma=(Delta+|Delta|)/2 are PSD, so

 M>=eta, M>=sigma>0, Tr M=1+delta,
 delta=(1/2)||eta-sigma||_1.

Use the actual contraction filter

 K=sqrt(sigma) M^(-1/2),
 F=I-K* K=I-M^(-1/2)sigma M^(-1/2)>=0.

Then K* K<=I and the success output on eta is

 omega=K eta K*<=sigma.

This last inequality follows directly from
M^(-1/2)eta M^(-1/2)<=I, followed by the sqrt(sigma) congruence. Let

 p=Tr[F eta]=1-Tr omega=Tr(sigma-omega).

The failure probability is small, with no inverse-eigenvalue estimate:

 0<=p<=Tr[F M]=Tr M-Tr sigma=delta.                (3.1)

If delta=0, then eta=sigma, M=sigma, K=I and use Repair=id.
If delta>0, p>0: otherwise omega=sigma from their PSD difference and equal
trace. Since K is invertible and sigma faithful, this would imply eta faithful.
Tr F eta=0 would force F=0, hence M=sigma and delta=0, a contradiction.

For delta>0 define the fixed density theta=(sigma-omega)/p and the local map

 Repair(X)=K X K*+Tr(F X) theta.                   (3.2)

It is CP on the entire matrix algebra: the first term is one Kraus filter and
the second is a positive-effect measure-and-prepare map. It is trace preserving
because K*K+F=I. Its action at the specified output marginal is exactly

 Repair(eta)=omega+p theta=sigma.                  (3.3)

Explicitly, if F=sum_j f_j|j><j| and theta=sum_k t_k|k><k| are their separate
spectral resolutions, the additional Kraus operators sqrt(f_j t_k)|k><j|
give exactly Tr(FX)theta and sum of Kraus squares F. The two spectral bases
need not coincide.

Every parameter depends only on eta and sigma, never on rho_RB, its reference,
its purification or a chosen member of E. This is a full-domain legal channel,
not merely an extension state chosen separately for each input.

Although an inverse square root appears in its formula, the ACTUAL filter
K is a contraction. The error/success bounds contain no norm of M^(-1), no
lambda_min(sigma), and no state-rank factor. No efficient numerical synthesis
or uniform conditioning claim is being made.

4. WEIGHTED FILTER DISTURBANCE IS AT MOST delta

Because eta<=M and (I-K)*(I-K)>=0,

 ||(I-K)sqrt(eta)||_2^2
   =Tr[(I-K)*(I-K)eta]
   <=Tr[(I-K)*(I-K)M]
   =||(I-K)sqrt(M)||_2^2
   =||sqrt(M)-sqrt(sigma)||_2^2.                  (4.1)

The last equality uses K sqrt(M)=sqrt(sigma), which is exact for this order
of the matrix factors. Using M>=sigma and operator monotonicity of square root,

 Tr[sqrt(M)sqrt(sigma)]>=Tr sigma=1,

since sqrt(M)-sqrt(sigma) and sqrt(sigma) are PSD and the trace of their product
is nonnegative. Consequently

 ||sqrt(M)-sqrt(sigma)||_2^2
   =Tr M+Tr sigma-2Tr[sqrt(M)sqrt(sigma)]<=delta.   (4.2)

The trace above is real even when M and sigma do not commute. Combining (4.1)
and (4.2) proves the weighted operator disturbance needed for a channel bound.
It is stronger information than the mere closeness of two marginal states.

The square-root order can also be checked directly without a source premise:
sqrt(M)-sqrt(sigma)=(1/pi)integral_0^infinity t^(1/2)
[(sigma+t)^-1-(M+t)^-1]dt>=0. The inverse order holds because M>=sigma>0;
the displayed difference integral converges in the finite dimension.

5. RIGOROUS UNIFORM QUANTUM-REFERENCE BOUND

Let xi_RB be ANY joint density with xi_B=eta, and let |v> purify it on an
additional finite system S. Apply K only on B and put |w>=(I_RS tensor K)|v>.
Then

 ||v||=1, ||w||<=1,
 ||v-w||^2=Tr[eta(I-K)*(I-K)]<=delta.

The elementary rank-one estimate gives

 (1/2)|| |v><v|-|w><w| ||_1
   <=(1/2)(||v||+||w||)||v-w||<=sqrt(delta).       (5.1)

After partial trace over S, the success-filter output is within the same
half-trace distance of xi_RB. The completed failure output of (3.2) on xi_RB
is PSD and has trace p, since its local effect is F and xi_B=eta. Its trace
norm contribution is therefore exactly p; including it in the half-trace
triangle gives

 T[(id_R tensor Repair)(xi_RB),xi_RB]
      <=sqrt(delta)+p/2<=sqrt(delta)+delta/2.       (5.2)

This proves the gentle-state-to-CHANNEL transfer explicitly. The same single
Repair works on EVERY extension of eta, including entangled states and
arbitrary finite references. No reference mutual information enters the proof.
The proof uses a purification solely to estimate a pre-existing fixed channel;
it does not select a new recovery map or new extension for each state.

6. ACTUAL REPAIRED BROADCASTER AND FAMILY ERROR

Use Repair from (3.2) identically on both outputs:

 Bfix=(Repair tensor Repair) o Bsym,
 Phi_fix=Repair o Phi.

The joint map is full-domain CPTP; its two marginals are exactly Phi_fix,
because the other local Repair is trace preserving. Equation (3.3) proves
Phi_fix(sigma)=sigma. This is the explicit self-compatibility witness, with no
separate channel-extension assumption.

For each rho_RB in E, the intermediate joint state
xi=(id_R tensor Phi)(rho) has xi_B=eta, so (5.2) and the raw residual give

 T[(id_R tensor Phi_fix)(rho),rho]
   <=T[(id_R tensor Repair)(xi),xi]+T(xi,rho)
   <=sqrt(delta)+delta/2+b.

The inequalities in Section1 now follow from delta<=b and b<=1. The b0 branch
gives Repair=id and exact stationary raw Phi. The b1 branch remains bounded by
the general maximum T<=1. All family members use the SAME joint map Bfix.

Optionally the full broadcast output itself changes by at most

outputs of any reference-extended Bsym state; both local marginals initially
equal eta and the first local TP repair leaves the other marginal eta.
This compares Bfix with Bsym on the family, not with an ideal joint copy state.

8. FAILED ROUTE: LARGE COMMON POSITIVE LOWER PART

A tempting repair starts with omega<=eta and omega<=sigma, Tr omega close1,
then filters eta down to omega. Such a large common Loewner lower part need
not exist despite small trace distance, even with faithful sigma. The present
proof instead uses the explicit common UPPER part M and its contraction filter.

Here is an exact failure family for the common-lower-part shortcut. For
0<t<1/2 let eta=|0><0|,
|phi>=sqrt(1-t^2)|0>+t|1>,
|phi_perp>=-t|0>+sqrt(1-t^2)|1>,
sigma=(1-t^4)|phi><phi|+t^4|phi_perp><phi_perp|>0.
Then T(eta,sigma)<=t+t^4 ->0. Every positive omega<=eta is c|0><0|. The
rank-one Loewner condition omega<=sigma forces

 c<=1/<0|sigma^-1|0>
   =1/[(1-t^2)/(1-t^4)+t^2/t^4]<=t^2 ->0.

Thus this route has no dimension-free trace-close common lower part. It is
a failure of that specific construction, not a legal counterexample to the
stationarity theorem. The new M-filter never assumes such a lower part.

Likewise a state-specific Uhlmann extension need not preserve the untouched
reference marginal and does not itself furnish one full-domain local channel.
No such unproved transfer is used: (3.2) is fixed beforehand and (5.2) proves
its simultaneous reference effect directly.
