## Errata to the frozen INITIAL

`INITIAL.txt` is immutable. Two corrections are recorded here.

1. The displayed formula for the multi-index Fisher derivative has an extra
   denominator. The correct formula is

   \[
   \partial^\alpha F_{ij}(0)=(-1)^{|\alpha|}|\alpha|!\,
   \tau^{|\alpha|+2}\,\mathbb E_\mu[x_i x_j x^\alpha],
   \]

   with no division by \(\alpha!\). The factor
   \(|\alpha|!/\alpha!\) is the coefficient of \(v^\alpha\) in the Taylor
   series, before differentiating. Moment matching still proves equality of
   every claimed jet, since the corrected derivative multiplies the same
   matched moment in both experiments.

2. `finite_jet_counterexample.py` used the Taylor-coefficient factor when it
   labeled quantities as derivatives. Thus its displayed values are not the
   actual derivatives, although its equality residuals remain zero for the
   same reason. `finite_jet_derivative_check.py` and its JSON output below
   recompute the actual derivatives. The finite quadrature law is atomic;
   its likelihood is a positive analytic Radon--Nikodym density relative to
   its own atomic reference measure, not a positive Lebesgue density on the
   cube. The pair uses two different reference measures on the same cube.

The exact interface also requires the **decoder**, as well as the encoder,
to be independent of \(v\). If the decoder receives \(v\), it can ignore the
archive and sample \(P_v\), making \(D=1\) possible. This was implicit in the
blind archive task and is made explicit here.

## Corrected lower-bound proof

Fix a coordinate \(i\), draw \(J\in\{0,1\}\) uniformly, and use the
parameter \(v=(2J-1)e_i\). Under this prior the input marginal is exactly
\(\mu_c\), and

\[
  p(x):=\Pr(J=1\mid X=x)=\frac{1+\tau x_i}{2}.
\]

Assume the decoded output is clipped coordinatewise to the cube; clipping is
a post-processing and cannot increase TV from the target, so write
\(Z=\operatorname{clip}(Y)\). Put
\(p_-=(1-\tau)/2\), \(p_+=(1+\tau)/2\), and use the posterior action
\(a(z_i)=(1+\tau z_i)/2\). The losses

\[
 \ell(0,a)=\frac{a^2-p_-^2}{\tau},\qquad
 \ell(1,a)=\frac{(1-a)^2-(1-p_+)^2}{\tau}
\]

lie in \([0,1]\) for \(a\in[p_-,p_+]\). They are strictly proper on this
posterior interval: at posterior probability \(p\), the excess expected loss
of action \(a\) over action \(p\) is exactly \((a-p)^2/\tau\).

Let \(R_*\) be the Bayes risk under the target joint law \((J,X)\). The target
joint law \((J,Y)\), when \(Y\mid J\sim P_{(2J-1)e_i}\), is the same law, so
the decoder action \(a(Y_i)\) has risk \(R_*\) under the ideal output. The
uniform TV guarantee and \(0\le\ell\le1\) imply that its actual risk exceeds
this by at most \(\epsilon\). On the actual channel, \(J-X-Q-Y\) is a Markov
chain because both code stages are parameter-blind. Conditioning first on
\(X\) gives the exact regret identity

\[
  R_{\rm actual}-R_*
    =\frac1\tau\mathbb E\big[(a(Z_i)-p(X))^2\big]
    =\frac\tau4\mathbb E\big[(X_i-Z_i)^2\big].
\]

Therefore \(\mathbb E(X_i-Z_i)^2\le4\epsilon/\tau\). Although this argument
uses a different sign prior for each \(i\), the unconditional input marginal
is \(\mu_c\) in every case and the encoder/decoder channel is fixed and
parameter-independent. Hence these are simultaneous coordinate distortion
bounds for one common joint channel \(X\sim\mu_c\mapsto Z\).

For \(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([-1,1]^m)\), the coordinates are independent
and \(h(X_i)=\ln2\). For any joint reconstruction \(Z\),

\[
 I(X;Z)\ge \sum_i\left[\ln2-
 \frac12\ln\!\left(2\pi e\,\mathbb E(X_i-Z_i)^2\right)\right]
 \ge \frac m2\ln\!\left(\frac{\tau}{2\pi e\epsilon}\right).
\]

The first inequality follows from conditional-entropy subadditivity and
\(h(X_i\mid Z_i)=h(X_i-Z_i\mid Z_i)\le
\frac12\ln(2\pi e\,\mathbb E(X_i-Z_i)^2)\). If \(Q\) is a quantum state of
dimension \(D\) and \(Z\) is produced from it by any decoder, data processing
and Holevo give \(I(X;Z)\le\chi(X:Q)\le\ln D\). This proves the stated
\((m/2)\log_2(\tau/(2\pi e\epsilon))\) lower bound for classical, hybrid,
and arbitrary unassisted quantum memories under the parameter-blind
interface. No compatible simultaneous coordinate measurement is assumed:
the output is the full decoded vector and the mutual-information bound uses
that one output channel.

## General posterior-regret acquisition principle

The preceding argument is an instance of a reusable lemma. Let \(\Pi\) be a
prior on parameters \(\theta\), let \(X\mid\theta\sim P_\theta\), and let
\(X\to Q\to Y\) be any parameter-blind archive channel satisfying
\(\sup_\theta\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L(Y\mid\theta),P_\theta)\le\epsilon\).
Choose a bounded loss \(\ell(\theta,a(y))\in[0,1]\) and let \(a(x)\) be a
Bayes action for the posterior \(\Pi(\cdot\mid X=x)\). Since the ideal joint
law of \((\theta,Y)\) equals that of \((\theta,X)\), total variation bounds
the actual expected loss by the ideal Bayes risk plus \(\epsilon\). The
Markov factorization \(\theta-X-Y\) then yields

\[
 \mathbb E\,\mathcal R_\ell\bigl(\Pi(\cdot\mid X),a(Y)\bigr)\le\epsilon,
\]

where \(\mathcal R_\ell\) is posterior excess risk. Thus any rate-distortion
lower bound for the posterior map under this regret distortion is also a
blind-archive memory lower bound, with quantum memories handled by Holevo.
This identifies an acquisition object that contains joint posterior
structure and is not recoverable from a finite Fisher jet alone. The lemma is
an interface-specific consequence of Bayes-risk decomposition and
total-variation duality, not a claim to invent proper scoring or
decision-theoretic quantization. A positive upper bound still needs a
constructive reference-preserving channel; a posterior entropy condition
alone does not supply its sampling or runtime costs.

## Finite-jet generalization and boundary

The Gauss rule is a convenient tensor-product instance, not essential. Let
\(\mu\) be any absolutely continuous probability measure on compact
\([-1,1]^m\) with mean zero, positive definite covariance \(\Sigma\), and
finite differential entropy \(h(\mu)>-\infty\). Fix \(t=K+2\). The vector of all
monomials of total degree at most \(t\) has dimension
\(N=\binom{m+t}{t}\); its expectation lies in the convex hull of its compact
image. Carathéodory's theorem therefore gives a positive atomic probability
\(\nu\) on at most \(N\) cube points that matches all these moments. In
particular, it matches \(\Sigma\). For sufficiently small fixed \(\tau>0\),
the affine likelihood family \(1+\tau\langle v,x\rangle\) is positive under
both references. Its Fisher \(K\)-jet and score-product tensors through
order \(K+2\) coincide, the atomic experiment has an exact \(N\)-label
archive, and the continuous reference experiment obeys
\(I(X;Z)\ge h(\mu)-\frac m2\ln(8\pi e\epsilon/\tau)\), by coordinatewise
maximum-entropy bounds using the regret distortions. This is a general finite-moment
mechanism for finite-jet blindness; no external quadrature theorem is
needed beyond the finite-dimensional convex-hull argument.

There is no same-family all-orders upgrade by this moment argument. If all
Fisher derivatives at zero match for compactly supported references, then
\(\int x_i^2 x^\alpha\,d\mu\) match for every \(i,\alpha\). Polynomial
density on the cube implies \(x_i^2\mu=x_i^2\nu\) as measures for each
coordinate. The references must therefore coincide away from the origin; a
continuous full-support law cannot equal a finite atomic law there. The
quantifier remains “for each finite \(K\), a new pair”.

## Literature boundary checked after the cross-phase release

Hayashi and Tan, *Minimum Rates of Approximate Sufficient Statistics*,
arXiv:1612.02542v2, §§II--III, explicitly define blind encoders from
\(X^n\) and decoders that output distributions, with relative-entropy or
variational-distance fidelity; their principal \((d/2)\log n\) result is an
\(n\)-i.i.d.-sample asymptotic. It is close interface prior art and prevents
presenting blind distribution reconstruction as new. It does not directly
state the present single-draw, \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), fixed-family
finite-jet contrast or its arbitrary-dimension quantum lower.

Varshney and Varshney, *Quantization of Prior Probabilities for Hypothesis
Testing*, IEEE Trans. Signal Process. 56 (2008), arXiv:0805.4338, use mean
Bayes-risk error as a distortion for quantizing priors and derive a
high-resolution distortion-rate approximation. That is a direct antecedent
for the decision-regret viewpoint. Buscemi, *Comparison of quantum
statistical models: equivalent conditions for sufficiency*, Commun. Math.
Phys. 310 (2012), arXiv:1004.3794v4, extends Blackwell--Sherman--Stein-style
decision-theoretic sufficiency to quantum models. These sources lower the
novelty claim for the general lemma. The remaining internally derived
candidate is the concrete finite-jet/finite-alphabet versus continuous
archive construction under the stated one-sample interface; its novelty and
priority remain unknown.

The finite cubature step is also established mathematics: positive
polynomial-exact quadrature for compactly supported measures is covered by
Tchakaloff-type theorems (for example Curto--Fialkow, *A duality proof of
Tchakaloff's theorem*, J. Math. Anal. Appl. 269 (2002),
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-247X(02)00034-3). Here it can be derived
directly from the convex-hull form of Carathéodory's theorem. The outward
search found no exact archive-memory theorem for this particular application,
but was not an exhaustive priority search.
