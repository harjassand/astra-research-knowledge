1. EXACT MOMENT-DOMAIN NECESSITY FOR COMPACT DISTRIBUTIONS

Let H be a complex Hilbert space. Let T be a compactly supported,
selfadjoint, norm-continuous B(H)-valued distribution on R^m, T(1)=I.
Let nu be a scalar probability and suppose the distribution T*nu is a
normalized positive operator-valued measure M. The same conclusion applies
if M is specified by the Fourier identity Mhat=That nuhat: Fourier
uniqueness for tempered distributions identifies it with T*nu.

Define the ACTUAL FINITE MOMENT DOMAIN

 D_nu = {g in R^m : m_nu(g)=integral exp(g.x) nu(dx) < infinity}.

For EVERY g in D_nu, including a boundary point at which the moment is
finite, the bounded positive operator integral exists and

 L_M(g) = integral exp(g.x) M(dx) = m_nu(g) H_T(g),
 H_T(g) = T_y(exp(g.y)).                                      (1)

Furthermore H_T(g) >= lambda_g I for some lambda_g>0.
No symmetry of D_nu and no finiteness at -g are needed. The conclusion is
not just about the interior of D_nu, and not about all real tilts if the
noise moments are only locally finite.

Proof of the transform identity, without substituting complex Fourier
arguments. Choose a compact K containing a neighborhood of supp(T), an
integer N, and C such that

 ||T(phi)|| <= C max_{|alpha|<=N} sup_K |partial^alpha phi|.     (2)

T acts on an arbitrary smooth function by first multiplying by a fixed
cutoff equal to one near its support; absorb that cutoff in (2). Choose
chi_R(x)=chi(x/R), R>=1, with chi smooth, nonnegative, compactly supported,
equal to one near zero and radially nonincreasing. Then chi_R increases to
one and all derivatives through N are bounded independently of R.
For phi_R(x)=exp(g.x)chi_R(x), the product rule gives

 ||T_y(phi_R(y+z))|| <= C_g exp(g.z),                          (3)

where C_g does not depend on R or z. Indeed y stays in K and every
derivative is exp(g.(y+z)) times a fixed finite sum of bounded derivatives
of chi_R and powers of g. For each fixed z, phi_R(y+z) tends in C^N(K) to
exp(g.(y+z)). Dominated convergence in the Bochner integral against nu
therefore gives

 M(phi_R) = integral T_y(phi_R(y+z)) nu(dz)
          -> m_nu(g)H_T(g)                                  (4)

in operator norm. Positivity and chi_R increasing show, for every h,
that <h,M(phi_R)h> increases to integral exp(g.x)d<h,M(x)h>.
Its norm is uniformly bounded by (3). Thus the weak/strong operator
integral exists, is bounded and positive, and equals (4). Polarization
gives the operator identity. This proof works at finite boundary moments.

Proof of the uniform positive lower bound. The Fourier operator
Mhat(u)=That(u)nuhat(u) is norm-continuous at zero and equals I there:
That is norm-entire because T has compact support, and nuhat is a scalar
continuous characteristic function. This supplies UNIFORM tightness over
all unit-vector states, which an arbitrary normalized POVM need not have.
For completeness, put f_epsilon(x)=exp(-epsilon |x|^2). Its normalized
Fourier representation is an average of exp(iu.x) with a Gaussian
probability in u converging to delta_0. Fubini in each matrix coefficient
and norm continuity of Mhat give

 K_epsilon := integral f_epsilon(x) M(dx) -> I in norm.

Choose epsilon with K_epsilon >= 3I/4, then choose R with
exp(-epsilon R^2)<=1/4. Since f_epsilon <= 1_{B_R}+1/4,

 M(B_R) >= K_epsilon-I/4 >= I/2.

Consequently, for every g at which L_M(g) exists,

 L_M(g) >= exp(-|g|R) M(B_R) >= (1/2)exp(-|g|R) I.

Since m_nu(g)>0, dividing (1) proves strict, uniformly bounded
invertibility of H_T(g). The bound can depend on g. It is not one bound
uniform over all g.

Why D_nu cannot be silently enlarged. Fix 0<q<1 and set

 T=(delta_0-q delta_1)/(1-q),
 nu=(1-q) sum_{n>=0} q^n delta_n.

Then T*nu=delta_0 is positive, D_nu=(-infinity,-log q), and
H_T(g)=(1-q exp(g))/(1-q). H_T vanishes at -log q and is negative beyond
it, although the output delta_0 has all exponential moments. At the
excluded boundary the NOISE moment is infinite. Thus output and noise
moment domains need not agree. The exact necessity is on D_nu, not on
the potentially larger output domain.

2. THE ANTICOMMUTATOR LEMMA: CORRECT AND ALREADY CLASSICAL

Lemma. For bounded selfadjoint A,C, if

 C exp(sA)+exp(sA)C >= 0 for every s>=0,

then C>=0 and [A,C]=0.

Independent reconstruction of the pasted Liouville proof. At s=0,
C>=0. Set S_r=exp(-rC), r>=0. For fixed s>=0,

 d/dr [S_r exp(sA) S_r]
   = -S_r[C exp(sA)+exp(sA)C]S_r <= 0.

Integration gives S_r exp(sA)S_r <= exp(sA), hence

 ||exp(sA/2) S_r exp(-sA/2)|| <=1.

For fixed r the norm-entire function F_r(z)=exp(zA/2)S_r exp(-zA/2)
satisfies F_r(-s)=F_r(s)^* for real s. Thus the estimate holds on both
real half-axes. Also

 F_r(s+it)=exp(itA/2)F_r(s)exp(-itA/2),

so ||F_r(z)||<=1 everywhere. Scalar Liouville applied to each matrix
coefficient makes F_r constant. Differentiation at zero gives
[A,S_r]=0. Differentiate in r at zero to obtain [A,C]=0.
Every step is in the operator norm; finite dimension is not needed.

EXACT PRIORITY TRANSFER. Uchiyama, Pacific J. Math. 161 (1993), 385-392,
Theorem 1 on printed 385-386/PDF 2-3, proves for bounded nonnegative C,D:

 [C,D]=0 iff CD^n+D^nC>=0 for n=1,2,... .                    (5)

The original theorem and its proof were acquired and read; the theorem
page was visually checked. Set D=exp(A), use s=n in the lemma, and use
the positivity of C from s=0. Equation (5) yields [C,exp(A)]=0.
Since A=log(exp(A)) in continuous functional calculus, [C,A]=0.
This is an immediate arbitrary-Hilbert-space corollary of the 1993
theorem. Dimension independence, avoiding eigenvectors, and the absence
of an eigenvalue nondegeneracy hypothesis cannot be claimed as a new
commutativity theorem here. The Liouville proof is an independent proof
of a classical consequence; its first-publication priority was not traced.

Uchiyama's own proof operation was also reconstructed. Scale 0<=D<=I.
Positive powers imply positive anticommutators with exp(tD), t>0;
congruence by exp(-tD) extends this to negative t. Positivity of
C exp((t+s)D)+exp((t+s)D)C, congruenced by exp(-sD), yields

 exp(-sD)C exp(tD)+exp(tD)C exp(-sD)>=0.

Integrating s>=0 against s^{n-1}exp(-lambda s) gives resolvent powers.
Congruence by (D+lambda)^n and passage lambda down to zero give
C D^n exp(tD)+D^n exp(tD)C>=0. Replace t by -t, congruence by
exp(tD), and multiply by exp(-t). This gives
exp(-t(I-D)) C D^n+D^n C exp(-t(I-D))>=0. Repeat the resolvent
integration for I-D and congruence by (I-D+lambda)^k, then let lambda
decrease to zero:

 C D^n(I-D)^k+D^n(I-D)^k C>=0  (n,k>=0).

Every polynomial strictly positive on [0,1] has a Bernstein expansion
with nonnegative coefficients after raising its Bernstein degree.
Uniform approximation extends positivity to continuous nonnegative
f(D), then bounded strong approximation to each spectral projection P.
The elementary projection lemma CP+PC>=0 => CP=PC follows by testing
vectors in ran(P) and ker(P). Hence C commutes with all spectral
projections of D and with D. This is the source's full mechanism.

Ricker-Vath 2002 supplies still weaker classical sufficient conditions.
Its Corollary 3, printed 39, says that bounded nonnegative C,D commute
iff, for some integer N>=1 and d>=0,

 D^{kN}C+C D^{kN}+d D^{kN} >=0 for every k>=1.               (6)

Indeed apply (5) to D^N and C+(d/2)I, then recover D by the continuous
Nth root. Positivity at all except finitely many integer powers is
therefore enough: choose N beyond the last exception. Their Example 1,
printed 36, shows no fixed FINITE list of powers suffices, even in C^2.
This last warning is also classical and cannot be promoted as a new
finite-testing obstruction for the anticommutator problem.

3. MARGENAU-HILL ALL-EXPONENTIAL REGULARIZATION IFF COMMUTATION

Let A,B be bounded selfadjoint on arbitrary H and

 J(u,v)=1/2[exp(iuA)exp(ivB)+exp(ivB)exp(iuA)].

There exists a scalar probability nu with m_nu(g)<infinity for every
g in R^2 and a normalized POVM M with Mhat=nuhat J iff [A,B]=0.

The inverse Fourier transform T_J is a compact B(H)-valued distribution:
J is entire of exponential type supported in the rectangle
[-||A||,||A||] x [-||B||,||B||] by the operator-valued Paley-Wiener
argument (or apply the scalar theorem to matrix coefficients, retaining
the common norm bounds). It is bounded on real frequency space and is
therefore a finite-order tempered distribution. Compact support is not
an assumption about finite spectra or existence of eigenvectors.

Section 1 now gives

 L_M(s,t)=m_nu(s,t) 1/2[exp(sA)exp(tB)+exp(tB)exp(sA)] >=0.

Fix t_0!=0, set C=exp(t_0 B), and divide by the positive scalar m_nu.
Section 2, or directly (5) with D=exp(A), gives [A,C]=0.
Because B=t_0^{-1}log C, this implies [A,B]=0.
Conversely a commuting pair has a joint spectral measure; convolving it
with delta_0 or any all-exponential probability gives the required M.

Only the selected tilts are needed for necessity: it suffices that
D_nu contain {(hn,t_0):n=1,2,...} for some h>0 and t_0!=0. This is
stronger than a claim about arbitrary locally exponential noise and
weaker than requiring every exponential moment. A bounded neighborhood
of tilts does NOT by itself imply commutation.

For A=sigma_z, B=sigma_x and s=t=log 3,

 H_J(s,t)=(1/9)[[45,20],[20,5]],
 v=(1,-2)/sqrt(5), <v,H_J(s,t)v>=-1/3.

This exact witness is consistent with the all-H proof; it does not
replace it. The original new-looking formulation as a scalar

For the ORDERED product K(u,v)=exp(iuA)exp(ivB), no moment hypothesis
is needed. A scalar probability characteristic function is nonzero in
a neighborhood of zero. Hermitian symmetry of a POVM characteristic
operator and nuhat(-w)=conj(nuhat(w)) imply there

 exp(-iuA)exp(-ivB)=exp(-ivB)exp(-iuA).

Mixed norm differentiation at (0,0) yields AB=BA. The converse is the

4. UNBOUNDED C-INFINITY SMOOTHING COUNTEREXAMPLE, WITH DOMAINS

Let H=direct_sum_{n>=1} C^2, let Z=sigma_z, X=sigma_x, and set

 A_n=2n I+n Z=diag(3n,n),  B_n=exp(-sqrt(n))X,
 A=direct_sum A_n,          B=direct_sum B_n.

A is the selfadjoint multiplication operator on

 D(A)={h: sum_n [9n^2|h_{n,1}|^2+n^2|h_{n,2}|^2]<infinity}.

It is positive, A>=I. (A+I)^{-1} is compact because its two eigenvalue
sequences tend to zero. For every beta>0,
tr exp(-beta A)=sum_n[exp(-3beta n)+exp(-beta n)]<infinity.
B is bounded and trace class, with ||B||_1=2sum_n exp(-sqrt(n)).

For each integer k>=0, A^kB is bounded and trace class: its two block
singular values are (3n)^k exp(-sqrt(n)) and n^k exp(-sqrt(n)).
Thus B maps H into D(A^k) for every k. Likewise B A^k initially on
D(A^k) extends boundedly. On the finite-block core,

 ad_{A_n}^k(B_n)
   =exp(-sqrt(n)) [[0,(2n)^k],[(-2n)^k,0]],

with operator norm (2n)^k exp(-sqrt(n)) and trace norm twice that.
These sequences are bounded and summable for each fixed k. Consequently
the iterated commutators extend to bounded trace-class operators.
The direct block formula and dominated convergence of these summable
derivatives show r -> exp(irA)B exp(-irA) is C-infinity in trace norm,
and therefore in operator norm. There is no assertion of analyticity.

Suppose a scalar probability nu and normalized POVM M satisfy for all
real u,v the strong-operator Fourier identity

 Mhat(u,v)=nuhat(u,v) exp(i(uA+vB)).                           (7)

For u!=0, uA+vB is selfadjoint on D(A) by bounded perturbation; for u=0
it is the bounded vB. Each two-dimensional reducing block therefore
compresses (7) to a normalized POVM M_n with the SAME scalar noise.
This is an exact reducing compression, not an arbitrary compression of
a noncommuting exponential. The global characteristic operator need
not be norm-continuous at zero; Section 1 is applied only blockwise.

The proof excludes a stronger noise class than stated in the input.
It is enough that for ONE s!=0

 integral (1+Z_2^2)exp(s Z_1) nu(dZ) < infinity.              (8)

All moments in a neighborhood of zero imply (8) for some s>0, by
choosing (s,+eta) and (s,-eta) inside that neighborhood and using
Z_2^2 <= C_eta[exp(eta Z_2)+exp(-eta Z_2)]. The tilted variance

 v_s=Var_{nu_s}(Z_2),
 dnu_s=exp(sZ_1)dnu/m_nu(s,0),

is then finite and is COMMON to every block.

Here is the complete finite-block calculation. Take any a>0, real b,
and A_0=cI+aZ, B_0=bX. Put x=sa, D=exp(sA_0). Normalize the positive
tilted POVM by

 N(E)=[m_nu(s,0)]^{-1}D^{-1/2}
       [integral_E exp(s y_1) M_0(dy)]D^{-1/2}.

It has total mass I. The compact-distribution cutoff proof extends to
exp(sy_1)y_2^j for j=0,1,2 under (8): its finite-order bounds are
C exp(sZ_1)(1+Z_2^2). Hence all relevant moments are genuine positive
measure integrals, even when no open moment neighborhood is assumed.

Remove the scalar shift c and define

 Q(t)=exp(-xZ/2) exp(xZ+tbX) exp(-xZ/2).

Writing mu_s=E_{nu_s}Z_2 and using differentiation at zero as a notation
for the resulting finite moment identities gives

 L_1 := integral y_2 N(dy)=mu_s I+Q'(0),
 L_2 := integral y_2^2 N(dy)
      =(v_s+mu_s^2)I+2mu_s Q'(0)+Q''(0).

Positivity of the moment Gram block

 [[I,L_1],[L_1,L_2]] >=0

follows by integrating (h+y_2 k)^*N(dy)(h+y_2 k). Its Schur complement
therefore gives

 v_s I+Q''(0)-Q'(0)^2 >=0.                                  (9)

For r(t)=sqrt(x^2+b^2t^2),

 exp(xZ+tbX)=cosh r I + [sinh r/r](xZ+tbX).

The continuous values at x=0 are understood. Direct differentiation
gives

 K=Q'(0)=b(sinh x/x)X,
 Q''(0)=b^2 diag(
   exp(-x)[sinh x/x+cosh x/x-sinh x/x^2],
   exp( x)[sinh x/x-cosh x/x+sinh x/x^2]).

Subtracting K^2 gives the exact identity

 Q''(0)-K^2 = -b^2 [sinh(2x)-2x]/(2x^2) Z.                 (10)

Thus (9) implies

 v_s >= b^2 |sinh(2sa)-2sa|/(2a^2s^2).                    (11)

For s>0 this is precisely the pasted bound. For s<0 the absolute value
is necessary; the largest eigenvalue selects the opposite diagonal.
The cI shift cancels identically during the normalized tilt.

In the specified blocks a=n and b=exp(-sqrt(n)), (11) requires

 v_s >= exp(-2sqrt(n)) [sinh(2|s|n)-2|s|n]/(2n^2s^2)
      ~ exp(2|s|n-2sqrt(n))/(4n^2s^2) -> infinity.

This contradicts finite v_s and proves the impossibility under (8),
hence under a nonzero exponential neighborhood. No unbounded operator
Laplace transform was substituted into (7); all exponential operations
in the proof occur in exact finite reducing blocks.

For general blocks a_n -> infinity, (11) implies the necessary condition

 sup_n |b_n| exp(|s|a_n)/a_n < infinity                      (12)

whenever the common noise satisfies (8). This is necessary, not a
sufficient unbounded classification. Multiplying every b_n by any fixed
positive number preserves impossibility while shrinking any prescribed
finite collection of commutator norms.

The mechanism is ordinary exponential versus polynomial control:
n^k exp(-sqrt(n)) is summable for every fixed k, whereas
exp(|s|n-sqrt(n))/n diverges for every fixed |s|>0. Smoothness of every
commutator order does not entail the exponential orbit control that
this exact representation requires. The two-by-two covariance argument
uses the classical Schwarz inequality for positive operator integrals.
Exact historical priority of the specified C-infinity counterexample
and of (11) in this regularization setting remains UNCLEARED.

Every finite truncation is bounded. This proof gives no obstruction to
finite-energy or finite-resolution approximations, no ordinary

For |zeta|=1 define E_zeta=I-D/zeta and

 Phi_D(zeta)=E_zeta^{-1}+E_zeta^{-*}-I.

 E_zeta^* Phi_D(zeta) E_zeta=I-D^*D>=0.                     (13)

EXACT TRANSFER LIMIT. Equation (14) represents holomorphic functions of
ONE generally nonnormal matrix after a matrix-dependent similarity.
It does not represent arbitrary functions of two selfadjoint operators,
does not preserve exp(i(uA+vB)) in all real directions, and is not a
scalar translation-invariant convolution multiplier.
Writing C=A+iB does not fix this: recovering A and B separately requires
C^*. For the Pauli example C=Z+iX, C^2=0, so every holomorphic h(C)
belongs to span_C{I,C}; the family exp(i(uZ+vX)) does not belong to that
span for arbitrary independent real u,v. For example exp(iuZ), u not a
multiple of pi, has a nonzero Z coefficient and zero X coefficient,
which cannot equal alpha I+beta(Z+iX). The similarity is generally
nonunitary and changes the selfadjoint observable interface as well.

Therefore the audited positive-boundary proof supplies no exact
scalar-noise regularizer or new operation satisfying the present
two-observable contract. It is a precise comparator and a demonstrated
