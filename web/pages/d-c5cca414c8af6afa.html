## Verdict

The stated randomized relative estimator for

    c_k(F) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J = empty} |det F[I,J]|^2

is supported by a coherent universal derivation, not just the peer's finite tests. I independently reconstructed its key identities and variance bound. I also ran the peer's exact checker unchanged: it passed n=2 and n=4, including all 2^n sign vectors, every k in range, equality of the square-root coefficient and principal-Pfaffian norm, and equality of the sign average with brute-force c_k. This audit did not establish priority or independently implement the full polynomial-bit characteristic-polynomial routine.

The result is a substantial tractable boundary: arbitrary F is relatively approximable in 9^k poly(n,L,epsilon^-1,log(1/delta)) time. It is polynomial for fixed k and k=O(log n), but remains exponential at the half-filled k=Theta(n) target. It therefore neither supplies the requested polynomial-bit #P reduction nor resolves general c_k.

## Independent reconstruction

Let u_i=e_i, let v_i be row_i(F) transposed, and let V have columns (u_1,v_1,...,u_n,v_n). For independent uniform signs s_i, define

    A_s = sum_i s_i (u_i v_i^T - v_i u_i^T) = V J_s V^T,

where J_s is block diagonal with 2 by 2 blocks [[0,s_i],[-s_i,0]]. Thus A_s is skew-symmetric.

For a row set R of size 2k, Pfaffian Cauchy-Binet expands Pf(A_s[R]) as a sum over pair sets S of size k:

    Pf(A_s[R]) = sum_{|S|=k} (product_{i in S} s_i) det(V[R,P(S)]),

up to the fixed sign convention of the pair-column order. The characters product_{i in S}s_i are orthogonal. Therefore

    E_s |Pf(A_s[R])|^2 = sum_{|S|=k} |det(V[R,P(S)])|^2.

Set X_k(A)=sum_{|R|=2k}|Pf(A[R])|^2. Summing over R and applying Cauchy-Binet to each pair-column matrix gives

    E_s X_k(A_s) = sum_{|S|=k} det(V[:,P(S)]* V[:,P(S)]) = c_k(F).

For the last equality, expand each 2k-row minor of [I_S,F_S]. A nonzero term must include the k identity rows S, leaving k rows J disjoint from S; the residual determinant is, up to transpose, det F[S,J]. This is exactly the target sum.

For complex skew-symmetric A, unitary congruence reduces A to 2 by 2 skew blocks with singular values sigma_j and possibly a zero mode. It follows that

    det(I+t A* A) = product_j (1+t sigma_j^2)^2,
    X_k(A) = coefficient of t^k in product_j (1+t sigma_j^2).

The second equality follows either by evaluating principal Pfaffians in the block form or by their exterior-power transformation under unitary congruence. Consequently X_k(A) is the coefficient q_k in the formal square root of D(t)=det(I+t A* A). If D(t)=sum_j d_j t^j and Q(t)=sum_j q_j t^j with Q(t)^2=D(t), q_0=1 and

    q_m = (d_m - sum_{j=1}^{m-1} q_j q_{m-j})/2.

All arithmetic is exact over rational-complex input; no numerical eigenvalues or approximate square roots are required.

For fixed R, p_R(s)=Pf(A_s[R]) is a homogeneous degree-k multilinear polynomial in Boolean signs. Scalar degree-k hypercontractivity gives ||Re p_R||_4 <= 3^(k/2)||Re p_R||_2 and likewise for Im. By Minkowski,

    ||p_R||_4 <= sqrt(2) 3^(k/2) ||p_R||_2,
    || |p_R|^2 ||_2 <= 2 3^k E|p_R|^2.

Another Minkowski inequality across R then yields

    ||X_k(A_s)||_2 <= 2 3^k sum_R E|p_R(s)|^2
                    = 2 3^k c_k(F).

Thus E[X_k^2] <= 4*9^k c_k(F)^2. A mean of ceil(16*9^k/epsilon^2) independent samples has relative error at most epsilon with probability at least 3/4 by Chebyshev. The median of O(log(1/delta)) independent batch means gives confidence 1-delta. If c_k=0, each nonnegative sample is exactly zero. If c_k>0, the same bound controls the probability of a zero-valued sample.

Each sample is computed without enumerating row sets: compute d_0,...,d_k from det(I+t A_s* A_s) by an exact polynomial determinant/characteristic-polynomial routine, then apply the recurrence. The peer states polynomial bit complexity, with rational bit-growth controlled by determinant bounds; this is plausible from the displayed construction and was not separately benchmarked here. The sample count is the exponential-in-k boundary.
