# Equal-charge harmonic-jet compiler and superlinear equilibrium count

7 October 2026. Root reconstruction of r01's final pre-interruption suggestion. **Complete proof; two separate independent internal gates PASS; historical novelty UNKNOWN.** The variable-positive-charge harmonic-grid/cubature route is already described in Shiva Kintali's August 2026 technical manuscript. This note changes the charge interface: every charge is exactly 1; their positions may move. It does not claim a practical charge-placement algorithm or a revolutionary discovery.

## Statement and supplied versus acquired data

Let P be a real harmonic polynomial on R^3 of degree at most D>=1, and fix finitely many of its nondegenerate critical points in a bounded region. Suppose an exact spherical 2D-design of N distinct points omega_i on S^2 is supplied. There are N distinct sites a_i, with radii arbitrarily close to 1, carrying charges all exactly 1, whose Coulomb potential has at least that many nondegenerate critical points. The new points occur in an arbitrarily small ball about the origin. A further arbitrarily small change of the sites, keeping unit strengths, makes the whole potential Morse with finite critical set.

The design can be selected existentially with N=O(D^2), and indeed for every N>C(2D)^2, by Bondarenko--Radchenko--Viazovska, arXiv:1303.5991v2, Theorem 1. This is an established prerequisite, not an acquired efficient exact design algorithm. The polynomial and its critical-point witnesses are also supplied to this compiler. We quantify the radial inverse conditional on those inputs; no polynomial bit bound for finding the design or for the full construction is inferred.

## Exact moment derivative and a right inverse

Use normalized area measure on S^2. Let Y_lm, 1<=l<=D, 1<=m<=2l+1, be a real orthonormal spherical-harmonic basis. Write M=D(D+2), and E_(lm),i=Y_lm(omega_i). Since the product of two such harmonics has degree at most 2D, exact design quadrature gives

    E E^T=N I_M,    E 1=0.

For t in R^N with |t_i|<1/2, put a_i=(1+t_i)omega_i. Define the nonconstant normalized Newton moments

    A_lm(t)=(1/N) sum_i (1+t_i)^(-l-1) Y_lm(omega_i).

Then A(0)=0 and J=DA(0)=-(1/N) Lambda E, where Lambda_(lm),(lm)=l+1. Thus

    B=-E^T Lambda^(-1),    J B=I_M.                 (1)

Only radial changes have been used; all strengths remain equal. Surjectivity is a statement about the entire degree<=D harmonic jet, not one selected multipole. Distinct directions guarantee distinct sites at every positive radius.

The addition formula sum_m Y_lm(omega)^2=2l+1 gives

    ||B h||_infinity <= sqrt(sum_(l=1)^D (2l+1)/(l+1)^2)||h||_2
                     <= (D+1)||h||_2/2.           (2)

The last loose bound follows from 1/(l+1)^2<=1/4 and M<(D+1)^2.

## Quantitative nonlinear inverse

Set F(h)=A(Bh), K=16(D+1)^5, r=1/(4K). In the region ||Bh||_infinity<=s=1/[2(D+1)], the vector second derivative of A satisfies

    ||D^2 A(t)[v,w]||_2
       <= 8(D+1)(D+2)sqrt(M) ||v||_infinity ||w||_infinity.

To see this, differentiate each (1+t_i)^(-l-1) twice, bound (1-s)^(-D-3)<=8, and apply the triangle inequality to the N summands. The vector of Y values has norm sqrt(M) by the addition formula. The power bound follows from -log(1-s)<=s/(1-s): (D+3)/(2D+1)<=4/3 and exp(4/3)<8.

Using (2) on both inputs yields ||D^2F||<=4(D+1)^5<=K. The ball ||h||<=r is inside that region. Also F(0)=0 and DF(0)=I. For any target z with ||z||<=r/2, the map

    T_z(h)=z-[F(h)-h]

maps the closed radius-r ball into itself, since ||T_z(h)||<=r/2+Kr^2/2=5r/8. Its Lipschitz constant is at most Kr=1/4. Banach contraction gives h(z), F(h(z))=z, with ||h(z)||<=2||z||. The fixed-point iteration is an explicit conditional inverse; its real arithmetic does not supply the exact design coordinates or their bit complexity.

The constants are deliberately loose. They also allow target z to tend to zero, making all radii as close to 1 as desired.

## Rescaled arbitrary harmonic polynomial

Write P=P_0+sum_(l=1)^D sum_m p_lm |y|^l Y_lm(y/|y|). Let

    S^2=sum_(l,m) (2l+1)^2 p_lm^2.

Choose 0<epsilon<=r/[2 max(1,S)], fixed independently of rho. For 0<rho<=1, use the target

    z_lm=(2l+1) epsilon rho^(D-l) p_lm.

Its norm is <=epsilon S<=r/2, so the radial inverse supplies sites a_i(rho) with radii in [1-s,1+s]. Let

    V_rho(x)=(1/N)sum_i 1/|x-a_i(rho)|.

Multiplying this potential by N changes every strength from 1/N to 1 and does not change its critical points. The Newton addition formula in the above normalization is

    1/|R omega-x|=sum_(l>=0) R^(-l-1)|x|^l
                         (1/(2l+1))sum_m Y_lm(omega)Y_lm(x/|x|).

Consequently all nonconstant terms through D agree exactly, and

    [V_rho(rho y)-V_rho(0)]/(epsilon rho^D)
       =P(y)-P_0 + E_rho(y),
    E_rho ->0 in C^2 on every fixed compact set.       (3)

Here is a uniform tail justification despite rho-dependent sites. Let a_min=1-s>=3/4, fix a ball B_R containing the desired points, and impose rho(R+1)<a_min/2. The Legendre bound |P_l(cos theta)|<=1 bounds the unscaled remainder on B_(R+1) by

    a_min^(-1) [rho(R+1)/a_min]^(D+1)
                          /[1-rho(R+1)/a_min].

The remainder is harmonic in y. Interior mean-value/Poisson derivative estimates on unit balls bound its C^2 norm on B_R by a dimension-only constant times this supremum. After division by epsilon rho^D the bound is C_(D,R) rho/epsilon, which tends to zero. This argument controls values and first two derivatives; a pointwise Taylor remainder alone would not suffice.

Each isolated nondegenerate critical point persists under sufficiently small C^2 perturbation, by the inverse-function/contraction argument for its gradient. Disjoint witness balls ensure distinct critical points. Their Hessians remain invertible. The scale rho may need to be extremely small; a small number of charges is not a claim of low placement precision or accessible experimental signal.

## Fixed equal strengths and a globally Morse finite configuration

Let configuration a range over distinct sites, and set G(a,x)=grad_x sum_i |x-a_i|^(-1) on x different from all sites. Its derivative with respect to any one site a_i is minus the Hessian in x of that Coulomb kernel. That Hessian has eigenvalues 2/|x-a_i|^3 in the radial direction and -1/|x-a_i|^3 in the two transverse directions. Hence this parameter derivative is invertible everywhere: G is a submersion. Parametric transversality implies generic arbitrarily small perturbations of the site positions make x->G(a,x) transverse to 0. Thus the potential is Morse, with all strengths still exactly 1. A sufficiently small perturbation preserves the finitely many already certified critical points.

For a fixed positive configuration all critical points lie in the convex hull of its sites: the equilibrium equation expresses x as the strictly positive weighted average of a_i with weights |x-a_i|^(-3). A small neighborhood of each site has no critical point because its singular gradient dominates the bounded gradients from all other sites. The critical set is therefore closed in a compact region separated from the sites. A Morse critical set is discrete there and thus finite. No mixed-sign finiteness theorem is needed.

## Superlinear equal-charge consequence

r09's separate proof constructs harmonic degree D<=256m polynomials with at least 8m^3 nondegenerate real critical points. Its independent gate now passes: `notes/harmonic_grid_audit/audit.md`. Combining it with the independently gated compiler and the established design theorem yields, for every sufficiently large N, N unit charges with at least c N^(3/2) equilibria for one universal c>0. For an explicit quantifier choice, enlarge the design constant to C_0>=max(1,C), and take m=floor(sqrt(N)/(1024sqrt(C_0))). Then C(512m)^2<=N/4<N, while m>=sqrt(N)/(2048sqrt(C_0)) for sufficiently large N. Thus one admissible c is 8/[2048^3 C_0^(3/2)]. No numerical value of the design constant is supplied by this note. The all-N design theorem is essential: padding a sparse sequence with arbitrarily small charges would violate the equal-strength interface.

Cycle8 correction to the preceding necessity statement: the all-N design theorem supplies this near-unit-shell proof, but is not essential to the unrestricted all-N equal-charge conclusion. Additional charges of strength exactly +1 may be put sufficiently far away, making their gradient and Hessian on the retained witness balls arbitrarily small. `notes/charge_precision/DISTANT_UNIT_PADDING.md` gives explicit bit and derivative bounds. This legal padding leaves the common near-unit shell; padding by tiny unequal strengths would still violate the interface.
