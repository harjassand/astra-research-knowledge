Abstract
The subadditivity–doubling–rotation (SDR) technique is a powerful route to Gaussian optimality in classical information theory and relies on strict subadditivity and its equality-case analysis, where double Markovity is a standard tool. We establish quantum analogues of double Markovity. For tripartite states, we characterize the simultaneous Markov conditions A–B–C and A–C–B via compatible projective measurements on B and C that induce a common classical label J yielding A–J–(B,C). For strictly positive four-party states, we show that A–(B,D)–C and A–(C,D)–B hold if and only if A–D–(B,C) holds. These results remove a key bottleneck in extending SDR-type arguments to quantum systems.
I Introduction
Gaussian distributions play a distinguished role not only in classical information theory,
but also in quantum information theory, where Gaussian extremality questions arise in a variety
of entropy inequalities and optimization problems for quantum systems.
A recurring theme—spanning functional inequalities and multi-terminal problems—is that
extremizers of information-theoretic functionals under second-moment constraints are often Gaussian.
In the classical setting, a particularly robust route to Gaussian extremality is the
subadditivity–doubling–rotation (SDR) proof paradigm.
At the heart of SDR lies a strict form of subadditivity: one needs to identify when
equality in a subadditivity inequality forces an independence (or conditional independence)
structure.
A widely used tool for establishing such equality conditions is the double Markovity
property of Markov chains (cf. [1, Exercise 16.25]), which, informally, says that
if AA is conditionally independent of CC given BB and also conditionally independent of BB
given CC, then BB and CC must share a common “summary” variable that screens off AA.
Historically, the “doubling–rotation” idea originates in functional analysis:
it appeared in Lieb’s work [2] on Gaussian kernels and in Carlen’s work [3]
on Gaussian optimality for the log-Sobolev inequality.
In information theory, it was brought to the forefront by Geng and Nair [4]
in the study of Gaussian broadcast channels, where it was combined with subadditivity to
establish Gaussian optimality.
Since then, SDR-type arguments have been used to prove a number of information inequalities
[5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10], and their use in proving the entropy power
inequality was developed in [11].
While quantum information theory has analogues of many classical inequalities, extending the SDR
paradigm to quantum systems faces a conceptual bottleneck: an appropriate quantum counterpart
of double Markovity has not been available in a form suitable for equality-case analysis.
The purpose of this paper is to fill this gap by establishing two quantum versions of double Markovity
that parallel the most commonly used classical formulations. These results remove an important obstacle
in transporting the SDR methodology to quantum information-theoretic settings.
To formulate these quantum analogues precisely, we next recall the notion of a quantum Markov chain
and state the corresponding “double Markovity” question in quantum terms.
We work with finite-dimensional quantum systems A,B,C,…A,B,C,\ldots with Hilbert spaces
ℋA,ℋB,ℋC,…\mathcal{H}_{A},\mathcal{H}_{B},\mathcal{H}_{C},\ldots.
A (density) state on AA is a positive semidefinite operator ρA\rho_{A} with Tr⁡ρA=1\Tr\rho_{A}=1;
for a composite system A​BAB we write ρA​B\rho_{AB} for a joint state and
ρA=TrB⁡ρA​B\rho_{A}=\Tr_{B}\rho_{AB} for the marginal.
To express Markov-type conditional independence in quantum terms, we use the conditional mutual
information (CMI) defined from von Neumann entropies, which are defined as
S⁡(A):=S⁡(ρA):=−Tr⁡(ρA​log⁡ρA)S(A):=S(\rho_{A}):=-\Tr(\rho_{A}\log\rho_{A}).
We also write the conditional entropy as S⁡(A|B):=S⁡(A​B)−S⁡(B)S(A|B):=S(AB)-S(B).
For a tripartite state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC}, the CMI is
I⁡(A;C|B):=S⁡(A​B)+S⁡(B​C)−S⁡(B)−S⁡(A​B​C).\displaystyle I(A;C|B):=S(AB)+S(BC)-S(B)-S(ABC).
(1)
Strong subadditivity asserts I⁡(A;C|B)≥0I(A;C|B)\geq 0 for all ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC}, and the equality case
I⁡(A;C|B)=0I(A;C|B)=0 is known to be equivalent to ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} being a quantum Markov state
with a specific structural decomposition [12].
Motivated by the classical identity “A−B−CA-B-C is Markov ⇔I⁡(A;C|B)=0\Leftrightarrow I(A;C|B)=0”,
we say that A−B−CA-B-C forms a (quantum) Markov chain if I⁡(A;C|B)=0I(A;C|B)=0.
The classical double Markovity phenomenon concerns the simultaneous validity of
A−B−CA-B-C and A−C−BA-C-B.
In the classical case, this implies the existence of a common random variable JJ
that is a function of BB and also a function of CC, such that
A−J−(B,C)A-J-(B,C) is Markov.
This statement is one of the key devices for converting equality in subadditivity-type
inequalities into an independence structure, and hence is tightly intertwined with SDR
arguments.
In the quantum case, however, the non-commutativity of observables means that one cannot
a priori expect a common function of BB and CC in the same sense,
and it is unclear what structural statement replaces it.
Our first main result provides an exact quantum analogue:
we show that “A−B−CA-B-C and A−C−BA-C-B” is equivalent to the existence of compatible
projective measurements on BB and CC (equivalently, a common block structure)
that induce a classical label JJ for which
A−J−(B,C)A-J-(B,C) holds.
A second, equally important, variant of double Markovity appears in SDR-based proofs of
entropy power type inequalities, where one works with conditional Markov relations
involving an auxiliary variable and requires a full-support assumption to rule out
degenerate cases.
We establish a quantum counterpart of this conditional double Markovity:
for a strictly positive joint state ρA​B​C​D\rho_{ABCD},
the pair of Markov relations A−(B,D)−CA-(B,D)-C and A−(C,D)−BA-(C,D)-B is equivalent to A−D−(B,C)A-D-(B,C).
The proof relies on a refinement of the structural decomposition of quantum Markov states,
including a uniqueness property of a minimal direct-sum decomposition under full support.
The main contributions of this work are as follows.
•
Quantum double Markovity.
For a tripartite state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC}, we characterize the simultaneous Markov conditions
A−B−CA-B-C and A−C−BA-C-B in terms of a block-diagonal structure induced by PVMs on BB and CC,
and we show that the resulting classical index JJ renders A−J−(B,C)A-J-(B,C) Markov.
This is stated precisely in Theorem 1.
•
Conditional quantum double Markovity under full support.
For a strictly positive ρA​B​C​D\rho_{ABCD}, we prove that
A−(B,D)−CA-(B,D)-C and A−(C,D)−BA-(C,D)-B hold if and only if A−D−(B,C)A-D-(B,C) holds.
This is stated in Theorem 2.
To prove the second result, we introduce the minimality of the decomposition introduced in
[12].
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section II, we review the SDR paradigm and explain how double Markovity enters
equality-case analyses; in particular, we present two classical versions of double Markovity
that are most frequently used in the literature.
Sections III and IV prove their quantum extensions, establishing Theorems 1 and 2, respectively.
We conclude with a brief discussion of implications for extending SDR arguments to
quantum information theory.
II The “subadditivity–doubling-rotation” technique and double Markovity
We first recall the classical SDR template and two classical double-Markovity lemmas that will be extended to quantum systems.The idea of the subadditive–doubling rotation technique can be summarized as follows:
Let ff be some functional defined on probability distributions. Let f⁡(X)f(X) be a short-hand notation for f⁡(pX)f(p_{X}). Suppose ff has the following properties:

IV-A Minimal decomposition
The direct-sum/tensor-product decomposition used throughout this paper is the one appearing in
the structure theorem for quantum Markov states in [12].
For our purposes, we introduce a minimal version of such a decomposition tailored to a
single strictly positive bipartite state ρX​Y\rho_{XY}.
In particular, we formulate an explicit minimality criterion and prove a uniqueness property
under full support, which will be a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem 2.
The system ℋY{\cal H}_{Y} can be decomposed as
ℋY\displaystyle{\cal H}_{Y}
=⨁kℋY1|k⊗ℋY2|k.\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k}{\cal H}_{Y_{1}|k}\otimes{\cal H}_{Y_{2}|k}.
(13)
The state ρX​Y\rho_{XY} has the form
ρX​Y\displaystyle\rho_{XY}
=⨁kρX,Y1|k⊗ρY2|k\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{k}\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k}\otimes\rho_{Y_{2}|k}
(14)
where ρX,Y1|k\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k}, ρY2|k\rho_{Y_{2}|k} are
strictly positive matrices on
ℋX⊗ℋY1|k{\cal H}_{X}\otimes{\cal H}_{Y_{1}|k} and ℋY2|k{\cal H}_{Y_{2}|k}.
We say that the above decomposition (13)
is minimal with respect to XX and ρX​Y\rho_{XY}
when the following conditions hold.
(i)
There is no strict subspace of ℋY1|k{\cal H}_{Y_{1}|k} that can be served as a component of a decomposition.
(ii)
There is no pair k,k′k,k^{\prime} such that
ρX,Y1|k\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k} and ρX,Y1|k′\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k^{\prime}} are related via a unitary transformation.
A minimal decomposition exists because
(i)
If ρX,Y1|k\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k} can be written as direct sum ⨁lρX,Y1|k,l\bigoplus_{l}\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k,l}, we obtain a refinement by replacing the original block by direct sum of smaller blocks ⊕lρX,Y1|k,l⊗ρY2|k\oplus_{l}\rho_{X,Y_{1}|k,l}\otimes\rho_{Y_{2}|k}.
(ii)
If there is a pair k,k′k,k^{\prime} in the sense of (2), combining ℋY​2|k{\cal H}_{Y2|k} and
ℋY​2|k′{\cal H}_{Y2|k^{\prime}} via direct sum removes such a pair.
In general, a minimal decomposition is not unique. If the support of ρY2|k\rho_{Y_{2}|k} is strictly smaller than ℋY2|k{\cal H}_{Y_{2}|k},
we focus on the kernel ker⁡(ρY2|k)\ker(\rho_{Y_{2}|k}), which is its orthogonal subspace to the support supp⁡(ρY​2|k)\operatorname{supp}(\rho_{Y2|k})
in ℋY​2|k{\cal H}_{Y2|k}.
The subspace ℋY1|k⊗ker⁡(ρY2|k)\mathcal{H}_{Y_{1}|k}\otimes\ker(\rho_{Y_{2}|k}) carries no weight in the state ρX​Y\rho_{XY}, so it could be reassigned to a different block k′k^{\prime}, without harming the decomposition. That is:
ℋY1|k⊗ker⁡(ρY2|k)≅ℋY1|k′⊗ker⁡(ρY2|k)\mathcal{H}_{Y_{1}|k}\otimes\ker(\rho_{Y_{2}|k})\cong\mathcal{H}_{Y_{1}|k^{\prime}}\otimes\ker(\rho_{Y_{2}|k})
for any k′≠kk^{\prime}\neq k, without changing the state. This ambiguity shows that when ρY2|k\rho_{Y_{2}|k} is not full rank, the minimal decomposition is not unique.
In contrast, when the state ρX​Y\rho_{XY} on the joint system X​YXY
has full support, there uniquely exists a minimal decomposition with respect to XX and
a strictly positive density matrix ρX​Y\rho_{XY}.

V Conclusion
In this paper, we have established quantum versions of the double Markovity phenomenon and have provided two structural characterizations that parallel the most commonly used classical formulations.
First, for a tripartite state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC}, we have shown that the simultaneous Markov conditions
A−B−CA\!-\!B\!-\!C and A−C−BA\!-\!C\!-\!B are equivalent to the existence of projective measurements on BB and CC
that induce a common classical label JJ and render A−J−(B,C)A\!-\!J\!-\!(B,C) a Markov chain (Theorem 1).
This characterization has identified an explicitly classical piece of information—the index JJ—that captures the part of BB and CC relevant to screening off AA, thereby providing a faithful quantum analogue of the classical “common summary variable” picture.
Second, under a full-support (strictly positive) assumption, we have established a conditional double Markovity statement for four-party states:
for ρA​B​C​D>0\rho_{ABCD}>0, the pair of Markov relations A−(B,D)−CA\!-\!(B,D)\!-\!C and A−(C,D)−BA\!-\!(C,D)\!-\!B
has been shown to be equivalent to A−D−(B,C)A\!-\!D\!-\!(B,C) (Theorem 2).
The proof has relied on a refined use of the structural decomposition of quantum Markov states, together with a uniqueness property of a minimal direct-sum decomposition under full support.
Taken together, these results have removed a key bottleneck in extending the subadditivity–doubling–rotation methodology to quantum settings.
In particular, they have supplied the missing equality-case mechanism that is frequently required when strict subadditivity is invoked in SDR-type arguments.
We expect that the present structural theorems will serve as a reusable tool in future work on quantum Gaussian extremality and related information inequalities, where one needs to convert Markov-type conclusions into explicit structural constraints on quantum states.
