## 1. General Stieltjes logdet geometry, with all diagonal constants retained

For a real negative core use positive masses m_i=y_i^2 and z_i=log m_i. Write Y=diag(sqrt m), P=diag(m). Direct quartic differentiation gives

    H=4diag(Qm)+8YQY,
    K=Y^-1HY^-1=8Q+4diag(Qm/m),
    h=(product m_i)detK.                               (3)

Here Q can have unequal diagonal entries. Along a log line z+t u, each nonconstant contribution to K is in a diagonal entry, with

    K''_ii=-4sum_(j!=i)b_ij exp(z_j-z_i)(u_j-u_i)^2<=0.

Therefore on KSPD,

    (logh)''=tr(K^-1 K'')-tr(K^-1 K'K^-1 K')<=0.        (4)

The second trace is the squared Frobenius norm of the symmetric conjugate K^-1/2 K'K^-1/2. Negative exponential diagonal terms also make K Loewner-concave in z, so its SPD log domain is convex. Only local nonpositive curvature at the critical point is needed by this theorem. Neither global concavity of logh-(n/2)logf on HSPD nor general linear-mass determinant-root concavity is asserted.

## 2. An exact diagonal-resolvent identity identifies the critical point

The ordinary linear mass pencil

    A(m)=2Q diag(m)+diag(Qm)

is nonsymmetric, but Y A(m)Y^-1=H/4, hence h=4^n detA(m). At m=1, Q1=c1 for our core, so A(1)=A_*=2Q+cI is symmetric SPD and H(1)=4A_*. Laplacian positivity gives Q>=cI and A_*>=3cI. Also f(1)=1^TQ1=n c>0 and Qm=c1>0.

Let alpha_i=partial_(z_i)logh at m=1. The derivative of A with respect to m_i is 2Q e_i e_i^T+diag(Qe_i). For N=A_*^-1 and d_i=N_ii,

    alpha_i=2(NQ)_ii+sum_j Q_ji d_j.

Since N(2Q+cI)=I, the first term is1-cd_i. Consequently the exact identity is

    alpha=1+(Q-cI)d=1+L_B d.                           (5)

Thus condition(1) makes alpha exactly1. Direct differentiation of f(e^z) gives grad_z logf=2/n at m=1. The scale-free potential

    G(z)=logh(e^z)-(n/2)logf(e^z)

therefore has zero full gradient at z=0. It is invariant under adding a constant to all z_i. A permutation-symmetry argument is sufficient when transitivity holds, but(5) establishes the weaker resolvent criterion without assuming transitivity or requiring equal diagonal entries of Q.

For a connected graph, L_B has kernel precisely the constant vectors: u^TL_Bu=sum_(i<j)b_ij(u_i-u_j)^2. Hence(1) is also necessary for this particular equal-mass point to be critical. Equal row sums alone do not prove it. They are not silently promoted to a full theorem.

## 3. Strict curvature in every projective direction

For arbitrary QPD and positive m, let C=PQP and a=C1, so f=1^TC1. The exact logf Hessian is

    Hess_z logf=(2/f)(C+diag(a))-(4/f^2)aa^T.            (6)

At m=1 in our core, C=Q, a=c1, f=nc. For every u perpendicular to1,

    u^T Hess_z logf u=2/(nc) [u^TQ u+c||u||^2].        (7)

Combine(4),(7) and Q=cI+L_B>=cI:

    u^T Hess G(0)u
        <=-(1/c)[u^TQ u+c||u||^2]
        <=-2||u||^2 <0 foru!=0.                       (8)

This is an exact nondegeneracy certificate at the critical point. It applies for every dimension and all allowed edge weights, with no conditioning bound or finite-fixture inference. The numerical constant in log coordinates is not a robustness theorem in unknown observed coordinates. In particular original diagonal scaling/L can be arbitrarily conditioned.

There is also an explicit graph certificate for the entire Hessian at this point. Write P_0=I-11^T/n and let N=A_*^-1. Directly from(3),

    K'[u]=4diag(L_B u),
    K''_ii=-4sum_j b_ij(u_j-u_i)^2,
    K^-1=N/4 at m=1.

Consequently, for any constant-row Stieltjes core, balanced or otherwise,

    Hess logh=-L_[b_ij(d_i+d_j)]-L_B(N Hadamard N)L_B. (10)

The first Laplacian has edge weights b_ij(d_i+d_j). Under(1), d is constant on each connected component, so it becomes 2diag(d)L_B. Combining(6) gives the exact identity

    -Hess G(0)=2P_0+L_B/c+2diag(d)L_B
                         +L_B(N Hadamard N)L_B.       (11)

Every summand is PSD. The third is symmetric PSD because diag(d) is constant on each graph component, and the last is PSD by the Schur product theorem. Thus(11) independently displays strict projective curvature >=2P_0. The graph formula does not remove the gradient condition: for an unbalanced row-regular core, Hess G at equal masses may be negative while its gradient is nonzero.
