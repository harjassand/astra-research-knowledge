 1. Introduction 

 
 1.1. Background 

 
 Let V V be a bounded, measurable function on ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} .
A well-known conjecture of Landis [ 5 , 6 ] asserts that there exists a constant c > 0 c>0 large enough such that any real-valued solution of Δ ​ u = V ​ u \Delta u=Vu on ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} which satisfies the bound | u ⁡ ( x ) | ≤ e − c ​ | x | |u(x)|\leq e^{-c|x|} must be trivial. Meshkov showed [ 7 ] that an analogous result does not hold for complex-valued solutions, where the decay threshold is of order e − c ​ | x | 4 / 3 e^{-c|x|^{4/3}} . The real-valued case of the conjecture distinguishes between its qualitative version (i.e. any solution satisfying the decay on ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} is trivial) as well as a quantitative version of the conjecture (explicit lower bounds for a nontrivial solution). The original conjecture with exponential decay is sometimes referred to as the strong Landis conjecture, as opposed to weaker versions which allow for a small loss in the exponent. 
 
 
 A weak version of Landis’ conjecture was proved by Logunov, Malinnikova, Nadirashvilli and Nazarov [ 9 ] in dimension n = 2 n=2 . In dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , the conjecture was recently shown to be false by Frank and Ivanisvili [ 4 ] . Their counterexamples match Meshkov’s decay treshold. However, in the case of radial potentials the qualitative version of the conjecture holds in any dimension, as was shown by Rossi in 2018 [ 10 ] . 
 
 
 A natural generalization of Landis’ conjecture is to extend the problem to potentials which may grow at infinity, and one expects that the decay threshold ought to depend on the growth of the potential. For example, a sharp decay threshold has been established by Davey for the case of potentials with polynomial growth [ 3 ] utilising the methods of [ 9 ] .
