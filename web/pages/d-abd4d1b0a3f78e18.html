# Causal-inference scout — 7 October 2026

**Recommendation:** advance one restricted active-acquisition theorem: unknown linear latent coordinates and their DAG can be recovered from **2K+3 covariance environments**, despite arbitrary hidden confounding and full-rank measurement noise, provided unknown deterministic clamp targets strongly separate the coordinates and independent additive-noise profiles can be paired while the nuisance law remains fixed. A complete population proof and a deterministic robustness module are below. This is a potentially useful composition of older covariance-shift identification and recent latent support-lattice identification. Novelty is **not established**; the probe services are strong and supplied. It does not solve general representation acquisition.

## Internally derived population theorem

For d≥2, use A_ij for the coefficient on Z_j in the equation for Z_i, so G=I−A. A is an unknown DAG matrix. B∈R^(p×d) is unknown and has full column rank. Observations are X=BZ+ε. Finite second moments suffice.

For each clamp setting k=1,…,K, an unknown target set I_k fixes those latent coordinates to deterministic constants. After centering, Z_i=0 for i∈I_k. Write S_k=I_k^c, B_S=B[:,S], and G_S=I−A[S,S]. The remaining model is

\[
 Z_S^{k\ell}=G_S^{-1}(h_S^k+\xi_S^{k\ell}),\qquad X^{k\ell}=B_S Z_S^{k\ell}+\varepsilon^k.
\]

The pair (h^k,ε^k) has an arbitrary joint law, fixed across profile labels ℓ within setting k. Its components may be correlated, so both hidden confounding and correlated sensor noise are allowed. Its law may change arbitrarily across different k. The injected ξ is independent of that pair and has diagonal covariance D_kℓ. Its strength and coordinate labels need not be known. These are **additive structural-noise probes**, not passive observations.

The supplied probe conditions are:

1. For every j≠i, some clamp setting keeps j and clamps i, and some keeps i and clamps j. This is strong separation; target labels are unknown to the learner.
2. Two profiles ℓ=0,1 are available for each k. Every entry of E_k=D_k1−D_k0 on S_k is nonzero. Mixed signs are allowed.
3. There is an unclamped baseline k=0 with three profiles ℓ=0,1,2. Set E_1=D_01−D_00 and E_2=D_02−D_00. Require E_1≻0 and pairwise distinct ratios (E_2)_ii/(E_1)_ii. Other nonsingular definite contrast conventions also work.
4. A, B, clamp constants, and the nuisance law stay fixed across the paired profiles in a setting. The nuisance need not be small.

**Conclusion:** B's column lines and every I_k are identifiable up to a common permutation. B itself is identifiable up to coordinate scaling and permutation. A is identifiable up to the same coordinate gauge; its DAG is identifiable up to permutation. There is an explicit population reconstruction requiring 2K+3 covariance matrices. If K≤c log₂d with fixed c, the simple enumeration algorithm below is polynomial in d and p.

**Proof, step 1 — nuisance cancellation.** Let Σ_kℓ=Cov(X^kℓ). Every covariance and cross-covariance involving (h^k,ε^k) is fixed across ℓ. Independence of ξ cancels mixed terms. Therefore

\[
 C_k:=\Sigma_{k1}-\Sigma_{k0}
 =B_{S_k}G_{S_k}^{-1}E_kG_{S_k}^{-T}B_{S_k}^T.
\]

G_S is invertible because A is a DAG, and E_k is nonsingular. Thus **col(C_k)=span{b_j:j∈S_k}**. Positive semidefiniteness of C_k is unnecessary. Full-rank measurement noise no longer hides the support subspace.

**Step 2 — acquire the latent column lines without target labels.** Set U_k=col(C_k). Full column rank of B implies

\[
 \bigcap_{k\in T}U_k=\operatorname{span}\{b_j:j\in\bigcap_{k\in T}S_k\}.
\]

For each j, take the settings in which j is kept. Strong separation guarantees their support intersection is {j}. Hence the one-dimensional intersections among all nonempty T⊆[K] are exactly the d distinct lines span(b_j), with duplicates discarded. Enumerate those intersections, recover normalized representatives, then recover S_k by line containment in U_k. No target labels or B columns were supplied.

**Step 3 — acquire the DAG.** Choose the recovered B gauge and apply its pseudoinverse to the two baseline contrasts. In the true gauge they are

\[
 V_1=G^{-1}E_1G^{-T},\qquad V_2=G^{-1}E_2G^{-T}.
\]

The generalized eigenvectors solving V_2 t=λV_1 t are the columns of G^T, each up to a nonzero scalar, and up to eigenvector ordering. Distinct ratios guarantee one-dimensional eigenspaces. Transpose these columns into candidate rows of G. A DAG permits exactly one row permutation having a nonzero diagonal: a different successful matching would contain a permutation cycle, and the corresponding off-diagonal entries of G would form a directed cycle in A. Normalize that unique diagonal to one. This reconstructs G and A. A monomial change of the recovered B gauge only permutes and rescales the latent coordinates. ∎

This argument does **not** identify the separate sensor and confounder laws or reconstruct Z exactly for each noisy X. Even in one dimension with known Z∼N(0,v), ε∼N(0,w), Var(Z|X)=vw/(v+w)>0. Model identifiability and individual-state recovery must remain separate.

## Internally derived robustness module

Normalize B's columns and put κ=σ_max(B)/σ_min(B). Suppose the minimum nonzero singular value of every C_k is at least γ>0 and estimated contrasts satisfy ||Ĉ_k−C_k||≤η<γ/4. Recover U_k by retaining eigenvectors of the symmetric Ĉ_k with |λ|≥γ/2. The rank is correct, and the projector error obeys

\[
 \|\widehat P_k-P_k\|\le\frac{\eta}{\gamma-\eta}\le\frac{2\eta}{\gamma}.
\]

Indeed, multiplying the estimated eigenvector equation by I−P_k gives (I−P_k)ŨΛ=(I−P_k)(Ĉ_k−C_k)Ũ. Invert the retained Λ; equal dimensions identify the projector norm with the largest sine angle.

For any nonempty T define a **sum**, rather than an ordered product, of complementary projectors:

\[
 H_T=\sum_{k\in T}(I-P_k).
\]

Its kernel is ∩U_k, and every positive eigenvalue is at least κ^(−2). To see the gap, write v=Ba+v_perp. The distance to B's coordinate subspace indexed by S_k is at least σ_min(B)||a[I_k]||, and distances to their intersection are at most σ_max(B)||a[outside intersection]||. Summing the squared distances counts every excluded coordinate at least once; the orthogonal component contributes |T|||v_perp||². Thus

\[
 v^T H_Tv\ge\kappa^{-2}\operatorname{dist}(v,\ker H_T)^2.
\]

The estimated H differs by at most 2|T|η/γ. If that is ≤1/(4κ²), thresholding at 1/(2κ²) recovers the intersection dimension, and its projector error is at most

\[
 e_T\le 4|T|\kappa^2\eta/\gamma.
\]

Distinct normalized B column lines are separated in projector norm by at least 1/κ. Consequently the sufficient condition

\[
 \eta<\frac{\gamma}{32K\kappa^3}
\]

allows the one-dimensional intersections to be clustered at distance 1/(2κ), with correct target membership. This is a conservative, explicit deterministic bound, not a sharp minimax rate. η can include sample error, soft-clamp leakage, and between-profile nuisance drift, if their contrast error is actually bounded. The graph stage additionally needs a quantified variance-ratio eigengap, minimum nonzero edge size, and stable diagonal normalization; those costs cannot be dropped just because ratios are qualitatively distinct.

For centered Gaussian observations, let L=max||Σ_kℓ|| and r=max tr(Σ_kℓ)/||Σ_kℓ||. A usual simultaneous covariance bound gives contrast error of order

\[
 \eta\lesssim L\left[\sqrt{\frac{r+\log(M/\alpha)}{n}}+
 \frac{r+\log(M/\alpha)}{n}\right],\qquad M=2K+3.
\]

Combining this with the module supplies a sufficient sample budget for decoder/target acquisition. This uses **total physical covariance**, including nuisance; r may equal p. It does not establish a complete finite-sample graph theorem.

## Decisive failure tests and lower-bound attempts

**Only additive noise interventions are insufficient when B is unknown.** Let C be any full-rank mixing matrix. For any DAG A, set B=C(I−A). Then under every independent structural-noise profile,

\[
 X=B(I-A)^{-1}\nu=C\nu.
\]

All profile laws are identical for every choice of A. Unlimited noise-profile samples identify source-noise response coordinates at most; they cannot separate the causal-coordinate mixing from its DAG. The deterministic-clamp service above does real identification work. An equally informed ICA/joint-diagonalization retaining baseline makes this obstruction immediate.

**Profile-varying sensor drift gives a population ambiguity floor.** In d=p=2, compare B=I and B'=R_θ with an empty DAG. Give both the same strongly separating clamp sets. If a signal covariance is S_kℓ in model 1, use sensor noise covariance

\[
 \Omega'_{k\ell}=\tau I+S_{k\ell}-R_\theta S_{k\ell}R_\theta^T
\]

in model 2. For τ sufficiently large this is positive definite. Both models give exactly the same centered Gaussian observed law in every setting, although their latent column lines differ by |sin θ|. Noise shifts of size γ imply nuisance drift O(γ|sin θ|). The triangle inequality therefore gives worst-case representation error at least c min{1,ρ/γ} when between-profile covariance drift up to ρ is allowed. No number of samples removes this error. This is an internally derived obstruction; the constant-accurate lower-bound family can be chosen with distinct baseline noise ratios.

**Weak excitation forces sample cost even without drift.** For two such rotated Gaussian models with fixed sensor noise τI and excitation of order γ, per-sample KL is O(γ² sin²θ/τ²). Across N observations, choosing sin θ≈min{1,τ/(γ√N)} keeps the experiments statistically close. A two-point testing argument yields the corresponding necessary representation-error scale. This is a standard local statistical mechanism, not claimed as novel. It falsifies any rate independent of excitation strength or sensor variance.

**Stochastic hard interventions are not automatically clamps.** If targeted latent coordinates retain nonzero variance, col(C_k) need not be span(B_Sk). A thresholded version requires a genuine leakage bound. Merely calling an intervention “hard” supplies no support rank deficiency. Other score-based CRL theorems may handle that setting; this proof does not.

## Strongest next question and expected value

The strongest next theorem is an **honest finite-sample certificate for a requested causal functional**, with total error decomposed into sample covariance noise, imperfect clamp leakage, between-profile drift, decoder conditioning, and variance-ratio separation. Can it attain the necessary drift/excitation floor while requiring less than complete DAG recovery, and can the pairing/invariance promise itself be tested with held-out profiles? Testing invariance cannot prove arbitrary unseen-profile invariance; the legal experiment class must be explicit.

Expected value: medium-high for a short mathematical depth wave, because the population construction is complete, has a concrete measurement-noise gap against the closest recent baseline, and admits an immediate adversarial audit. Stop or downgrade if the exact theorem is already in noisy ICA/CRL, if obtaining legal independent excitation is the real application bottleneck, or if the intended significance requires unknown nonlinear representations or arbitrary interventions. Success would establish a useful restricted active acquisition result, not the archive campaign's universal acquisition objective.

The proximal alternative should stay as a comparator. For a linear inverse problem Th=m, the scalar ⟨ℓ,h⟩ is identified iff ℓ⊥ker(T); if ℓ=T* q, it equals ⟨q,m⟩ without reconstructing h. Identifiability corresponds to closure of the adjoint range, whereas stable/regular estimation needs stronger range control. The cited proximal paper already proves the central distinction. The new burden would be acquiring useful q and certifying approximation/conditioning with the same data and costs as the baseline, rather than declaring compressed causal knowledge from bridge existence.
