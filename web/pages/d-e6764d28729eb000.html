# Targeted prior-art ledger: sparse resolvents and exact circuit relations

**Audit date:** 2026-10-08. **Question:** can the image-penalty interface needed by the complement-LCU construction be supplied from a polynomial-size circuit or the BMVZ tensor-OR term generator? In particular, is there a primary theorem giving polynomial row-list access to \((I-H)^{-1}\), or an exact positive sparse graph relation with the same image kernel and a constant normalized gap?

## Verdict

The strongest closing result found is a **conditional sparse-row inverse theorem**: if \(H\) itself is \(d\)-row-sparse with a uniform row-list oracle and \(0\preceq H\preceq(1-\delta)I\), then a truncated Neumann series gives a positive-definite polynomial-row approximation to \((I-H)^{-1}\). For constant \(d,\delta^{-1}\), inverse-polynomial norm error costs degree \(O(\log n)\) and row degree \(d^{O(\log n)}=\operatorname{poly}(n)\). This theorem's input oracle is precisely the missing item for BMVZ: its tensor-OR terms are polynomial-time generated/measurable but are not supplied with polynomial row lists. The BMVZ paper itself identifies exploiting their tensor structure to get sparse-Hamiltonian access as open.

There is also a **direct, exact sparse-QMA fallback** for the tensor-OR promise: replace each OR projector by the normalized sum of its commuting local violation projections. It preserves the exact zero space and costs at most a factor \(R\) in the normalized soundness gap. Since BMVZ's \(R\) is polynomial, this yields an inverse-polynomial-gap sparse Hamiltonian, not a constant-gap sparse-QPCP output and not the desired common-image enforcer.

For general polynomial-size local-gate circuits, the Feynman--Kitaev propagation relation gives exact sparse rows and an exact history-state kernel, but its normalized path gap is \(\Theta(T^{-2})\). Constant-gap clock graphs require long-range relative circuit unitaries; those may be dense in the computational basis. Thus the circuit relation route also stops at an explicit gap-versus-row-access boundary.

No claim of exhaustive literature coverage or priority is made. These are source/interface comparisons, with internally derived constructions distinguished from published theorems.

## Exact target and access convention

In the Cycle-3 complement-LCU route, the logical Hamiltonian is a positive contraction \(H\), the distinguished compression block is

\[
Y=I-H\succeq \tfrac14 I,
\]

and the incidence-style image enforcer uses blocks involving \(K_aY^{-1}\), where \(K_a=\langle a|U|0\rangle\) are branches of the LCU unitary. The target is a **uniform row-list oracle**: on every computational-basis row it must enumerate all nonzero columns and give their coefficients to requested precision in polynomial worst-case time, with polynomial output length. A gate circuit, a block-encoding, a specified-entry evaluator, a term sampler, and a sparse-matrix row oracle are different input/output interfaces. Exact kernel means \(\ker C=\operatorname{ran}V\); a constant gap means \(C\succeq\gamma(I-VV^*)\) with \(\gamma\) independent of instance size while \(\|C\|\le1\).

The inherited cards [L02](../../../../../work/astra_prior/cards/L02-commuting-repetition.txt) and [N11](../../../../../work/astra_prior/cards/N11-repetition-pcp-audit.txt) concern an arbitrary-commuting-operator **game threshold tail**, with finite game, value, answer alphabet, and component resampling-gap hypotheses. Their cubic deviation bound and anchored specialization do not construct a Hamiltonian, an inverse oracle, or a polynomial row list. In particular, there is no supplied theorem translating their game parameter into the BMVZ physical-row oracle or an exact image-kernel penalty.

## Theorem that closes the inverse interface when the input really is sparse

Let \(H\) be an \(N\times N\) Hermitian matrix with at most \(d\) nonzeros per row, a uniform complete row-list oracle, and

\[
0\preceq H\preceq(1-\delta)I,\qquad 0<\delta\le1.
\]

For an integer \(L\ge0\), define \(G_L=\sum_{k=0}^{L}H^k\). Then \(G_L\succ0\), each row has at most

\[
1+d+d^2+\cdots+d^L\le (d+1)^{L+1}
\]

candidate columns, and

\[
\|(I-H)^{-1}-G_L\|
\le \frac{(1-\delta)^{L+1}}{\delta}.
\]

The bound follows by summing the positive operator geometric tail; the row support follows by composing the row-list oracle along walks of length at most \(L\), merging duplicates. For target error \(\eta\), take

\[
L+1\ge \frac{\log(1/(\delta\eta))}{-\log(1-\delta)}.
\]

If \(d=O(1)\), \(\delta=\Omega(1)\), and \(\eta\ge1/\operatorname{poly}(n)\), both degree and row-list cost are polynomial. If \(d=\operatorname{poly}(n)\), this bound is generally only quasi-polynomial for logarithmic \(L\); no polynomial claim follows without stronger structure. This is an elementary derived theorem, not a new literature priority claim.

The BMVZ complement block has a favorable spectral margin \(Y\succeq I/4\), so conditioning is not the obstruction: the same geometric truncation has error at most \(4(3/4)^{L+1}\). But BMVZ's \(H\) is a sum of tensor-OR terms whose individual row supports can be exponential in the polynomially growing tensor length. The sparse-input hypothesis above is absent. A polynomial circuit for the truncated series therefore does not yield a polynomial row list.

## Exact circuit relation: Feynman--Kitaev line clock

Let \(U_T\cdots U_1\) be a circuit with polynomially many constant-qubit gates, and take a clock with legal basis \(\{|t\rangle:0\le t\le T\}\). The history isometry is

\[
V|\psi\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt{T+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{T}|t\rangle\,U_t\cdots U_1|\psi\rangle,
\]

where the empty prefix at \(t=0\) is \(I\). Define edge constraints

\[
e_t=\frac12\bigl(|t\rangle I-|t+1\rangle U_{t+1}\bigr)
                   \bigl(\langle t|I-\langle t+1|U_{t+1}^*\bigr),
\qquad C_{\rm prop}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}e_t.
\]

Then \(C_{\rm prop}\succeq0\) and its kernel on the legal clock space is exactly \(\operatorname{ran}V\): zero energy forces the adjacent propagation equations, which recursively determine all clock slices from the initial data vector. A clock-validity diagonal penalty can be added if the \(T+1\)-level clock is embedded in \(\lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil\) qubits; it excludes unused clock labels without changing the legal-space relation. Each edge has constant row degree when its gate acts on \(O(1)\) data qubits; the sum has a uniform polynomial row-list from the gate list. After constant normalization, however, the first nonzero eigenvalue remains \(\Theta((T+1)^{-2})\), the path-Laplacian gap.

This is the strongest exact sparse graph-relation construction found for arbitrary circuit histories: it meets exact kernel and row access, but not constant normalized gap. Scaling the path Laplacian by \(T^2\) makes its norm grow with \(T\); renormalizing it restores the inverse-square gap. Replacing path edges with expander edges would introduce relative prefix unitaries \(U_t\cdots U_{s+1}\) on data. They need not have sparse rows (e.g. a prefix applying Hadamard to all \(n\) qubits is dense in the computational basis). This last comparison is a direct interface calculation, not an impossibility theorem for every clock construction.

## Published positive penalties and the remaining access mismatch

**Detectability lemma.** Anshu--Arad--Vidick, arXiv:1602.01210v3 (2016-05-23), consider a frustration-free projector Hamiltonian \(H=\sum_iQ_i\) with each \(Q_i\) noncommuting with at most \(g\) other terms and spectral gap \(\gamma>0\). For an ordered product \(A=\prod_i(I-Q_i)\), their Corollary 3 gives

\[
\|A\psi^\perp\|^2\le (1+\gamma/g^2)^{-1}
\quad(\psi^\perp\perp\ker H).
\]

Thus \(C_{\rm DL}=I-A^*A\) is a positive contraction with exact kernel \(\ker H\) and gap at least \(\gamma/(g^2+\gamma)\). This preserves the input gap up to a constant when \(g=O(1)\); it does not amplify an inverse-polynomial \(\gamma\) to a constant. The operator \(A\) has a polynomial-size product/circuit description, but that description alone is not a polynomial row-list oracle. Their local coarse-graining gap amplification assumes a frustration-free local lattice system and raises interaction range to \(O(\gamma^{-1/2})\); for inverse-polynomial \(\gamma\), the corresponding unrestricted qubit row support need not be polynomial. It does not encode the BMVZ all-logical-vector LCU image.

**Explicit graph sparsification.** Spielman--Srivastava, arXiv:0803.0929 (v1 2008-03-06; v4 2009-11-18), take an explicit weighted graph and return an explicit weighted subgraph with \(O(N\log N/\varepsilon^2)\) edges whose Laplacian obeys \((1-\varepsilon)L\preceq\widetilde L\preceq(1+\varepsilon)L\). The sandwich preserves the exact kernel and preserves nonzero eigenvalues within relative factors, but cannot turn a path gap \(\Theta(T^{-2})\) into a constant. Its input/output is an explicit \(N\)-vertex graph; if \(N=2^{\mathrm{poly}(n)}\), the emitted graph is not a polynomial-size circuit-succinct row oracle in \(n\). It is not a constructor for a graph relation from a circuit description.

**Sparse linear-system algorithms.** Harrow--Hassidim--Lloyd, arXiv:0811.3171 (submitted 2008-11-19; final arXiv v3 2009-09-30; PRL 2009), assume a sparse well-conditioned matrix oracle and produce a quantum state proportional to \(A^{-1}|b\rangle\), for expectation-value tasks. This is a *sparse input to inverse-state output* result; it does not return a classical row-list oracle for \(A^{-1}\), and it cannot be reversed into one by the theorem.

There is a useful caution even at constant condition number. For the adjacency matrix \(A_N\) of the path on \(N\) vertices, let

\[
H_N=\tfrac14 I+\tfrac18A_N.
\]

This is a positive contraction, is at most 3-sparse, and \(I-H_N=\tfrac34(I-A_N/6)\succeq\tfrac12 I\), so its condition number is at most 2. Yet

\[
(I-H_N)^{-1}=\tfrac43\sum_{k\ge0}(A_N/6)^k
\]

has strictly positive \((i,j)\) entry for every pair \(i,j\), since the path has a walk between every pair. The exact inverse is dense despite constant conditioning. This does **not** refute the polynomial-row Neumann *approximation* above: logarithmic-degree truncations can have polynomial row support at inverse-polynomial accuracy. It does refute an exact inverse-sparsity implication from a sparse, well-conditioned input.

## BMVZ tensor-OR: exact sparse-QMA fallback, but no constant-gap image enforcer

The primary BMVZ source is Bergamaschi--Metger--Vidick--Zhang, *Derandomised tensor product gap amplification for quantum Hamiltonians*, arXiv:2510.01333v1 (2025-10-01). Its Theorem 1.4 gives a deterministic QMA-hard family with polynomially many terms, each an \(O(n)\)-fold tensor product of \(O(1)\)-local projections, with negligible YES energy and constant NO energy. The formal iterated theorem (Theorem 7.3) gives polynomial term count and polynomially growing locality from an equitable, constant-commuting-layer input family; it retains negligible completeness and produces constant soundness. The source explicitly defines sparse QPCP through efficiently computable sparse-matrix entries and says that black-box sparsification is too costly/inefficient for its structured terms, leaving use of tensor-product structure as an open question (Section 1.2, "Sparse quantum PCP"). Thus the source itself does not claim a sparse matrix row oracle for its terms.

For a BMVZ clause

\[
h_j=I-\bigotimes_{r=1}^{R}(I-P_{j,r}),
\]

the \(P_{j,r}\) are commuting projections on disjoint copies and each is \(k\)-local for fixed \(k\) (the construction's base terms are 5-local). Put \(S_j=\sum_{r=1}^{R}P_{j,r}\). In the joint eigenbasis, if exactly \(q\) factors reject, \(h_j\) has eigenvalue \(1_{q\ge1}\) and \(S_j\) has eigenvalue \(q\). Consequently

\[
h_j\preceq S_j\preceq R h_j,\qquad \ker h_j=\ker S_j.
\]

For normalized \(H=\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_jh_j\), \(w_j\ge0\), \(\sum_jw_j=1\), define

\[
\widehat H=\frac1R\sum_{j=1}^{M}w_jS_j.
\]

Then

\[
H/R\preceq\widehat H\preceq H\preceq I,
\qquad \ker\widehat H=\ker H.
\]

If the source promise is YES \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\le\epsilon\) or NO \(\lambda_{\min}(H)\ge c\), the output has YES \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat H)\le\epsilon\) and NO \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat H)\ge c/R\). With polynomial \(R,M\), this is an exact sparse positive-contraction promise-gap reduction with inverse-polynomial gap. Every row can be enumerated by looping over \(MR\) constant-local factors, listing at most \(2^k\) candidate columns per factor and merging duplicates: row degree at most \(MR2^k\), polynomial. Runtime and coefficient-bit cost are polynomial when the BMVZ factor generator, weights, and local matrix entries have polynomial-time/poly-bit descriptions; these are part of the output-construction contract and should be charged explicitly.

This is a derived factor-sum conversion, not a source-attributed novelty claim. The loss \(R\) is real at the level of this comparison: the OR pays once for any violated factor, whereas the sum pays once per violation. Since BMVZ has polynomially growing \(R\), this does not preserve its constant promise gap after contraction normalization. Leaving the sum unnormalized gives constant NO energy but norm up to \(R\), which is a different sparse-LH normalization. The conversion also does not give \(C\) with \(\ker C=\operatorname{ran}V\), nor the all-state compression \(V^*BV\asymp H\).

## Additive approximation: what it proves and what it does not

If an exact positive enforcer \(C\) and compression test \(B\) already imply a YES/NO promise interval \([a,d]\) for \(K=C+\tau B\), and a Hermitian row-list matrix \(\widetilde C\) satisfies \(\|\widetilde C-C\|\le\eta\), Weyl's inequality transfers the raw interval to \([a+\eta,d-\eta]\). For an explicitly positive output, use \(\widetilde K=\widetilde C+\eta I+\tau B\): then \(\widetilde K\succeq K\) in Loewner order, YES energy is at most \(a+2\eta\), and NO energy at least \(d\). Hence \(\eta\le(d-a)/4\) retains at least half the gap before normalization. This is sufficient for a sparse-QMA reduction **only when** \(\widetilde C\) has a uniform polynomial row-list oracle; operator norm closeness, a circuit, or specified-entry access does not supply that oracle.

For the Cycle-3 incidence enforcer, the Neumann truncation has a polynomial-size circuit and a geometric operator-norm error because \(Y\succeq I/4\). Its row support is unresolved: BMVZ clauses are not sparse, and the naive support bound for a degree-\(L\) polynomial in their tensor-OR sum is exponential already at degree one. No cancellation theorem or complete row-list for the resulting positive penalty was found. The exact input/output gap is therefore **spectral approximation known, sparse access unknown**.
