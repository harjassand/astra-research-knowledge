# Group frontier: exact structural tools and a finite-model obstruction

Worker: frontier_groups, cycle19. Date: 7 October 2026. Pinned release commit: `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`.

This note reconstructs the algebraic mechanism of family250 and derives three local results: rank four is enough for its affine envelope; an r-ary splitting makes the image of the Steinberg kernel central of exponent dividing r-1; and the exact best finite-matrix error for the splitting equations is computable. The source's general Boone–Higman theorem is **not** thereby independently validated: the programme-to-monoid compiler is a separate load-bearing premise. No external novelty claim is made for our local extensions.

## 2. Rank-four r-ary kernel theorem

**Theorem A (our extension).** Let R be a unital associative ring, let ψ:R→R be additive and multiplicative, possibly nonunital, and set P=ψ(1). Suppose, for an integer r≥2, there are U₀,…,Uᵣ₋₁,V₀,…,Vᵣ₋₁ in PRP commuting with ψ(R), such that

\[
V_iU_j=\delta_{ij}P,\qquad \sum_{i=0}^{r-1}U_iV_i=P.
\]

Write π:St₄(R)→E₄(R) and θ(xᵢⱼ(a))=xᵢⱼ(ψ(a)). Then θ(kerπ) is central in St₄(R), and every element of θ(kerπ) has order dividing r-1. In particular, binary splitting kills the kernel. If the same ψ admits two such systems of arities r and s with gcd(r-1,s-1)=1, it also kills the kernel; the two splitting systems need not commute with one another.

**Proof, including the index reduction.** Use only the relations

\[
x_{ij}(a)x_{ij}(b)=x_{ij}(a+b),\quad
[x_{ij}(a),x_{jk}(b)]=x_{ik}(ab),\quad
[x_{ij}(a),x_{kl}(b)]=1\ (i\ne l,j\ne k).
\]

1. **An omitted index gives centrality.** If a root word w uses indices in J and k∉J, define Cₖ(c)=∏ᵢ∈ⱼxᵢₖ(cᵢ) and Rₖ(q)=∏ᵢ∈ⱼxₖᵢ(qᵢ). If A is w's matrix on J, the root relations give wCₖ(c)w⁻¹=Cₖ(Ac) and wRₖ(q)w⁻¹=Rₖ(qA⁻¹). This follows for one root by adding acⱼ to cᵢ and subtracting qᵢa from qⱼ, then for products. If w omits4 and its matrix is the identity, it commutes with every root meeting4. Every remaining root is [xᵢ₄(a),x₄ⱼ(1)], so w is central. There are three supported indices and one unused index; six indices are unnecessary.

2. **Equivalent orthogonal corners give central kernel images.** Suppose E,F are orthogonal idempotents and d=FdE, d*=Ed*F, d*d=E, dd*=F. The asterisk is a name, not an involution. Set W=x₁₄(d)x₄₁(-d*)x₁₄(d). Its matrix and inverse on coordinates(1,4) are

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1-F&d\\-d^*&1-E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1-F&-d\\d^*&1-E\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For y=EyE and k∈{2,3}, the preceding row/column calculation gives the exact Steinberg identities

\[
Wx_{1k}(y)W^{-1}=x_{1k}(y),\quad Wx_{4k}(y)W^{-1}=x_{1k}(dy),
\]
\[
Wx_{k1}(y)W^{-1}=x_{k1}(y),\quad Wx_{k4}(y)W^{-1}=x_{k1}(yd^*).
\]

The two roots internal to(1,4) can first be written as [x₁ₖ(y),xₖ₄(E)] and [x₄ₖ(y),xₖ₁(E)]. Their conjugates omit4 by the displayed identities. Other roots already omit4. Thus any matrix-kernel word over ERE is conjugate to an omitted-index kernel word and is central. The original word is central too.

3. **The corner images agree.** Also assume E,F,d,d* commute with ψ(R). Put b=d+d*+1-E-F. Then b²=1 and bEb⁻¹=F. For a unit a put wᵢⱼ(a)=xᵢⱼ(a)xⱼᵢ(-a⁻¹)xᵢⱼ(a), hᵢⱼ(a)=wᵢⱼ(a)wᵢⱼ(-1), and use

\[
D=h_{12}(b)h_{34}(b).
\]

Its matrix has b at every diagonal position. More strongly, the omitted-index row/column identities give Dxᵢⱼ(y)D⁻¹=xᵢⱼ(byb⁻¹) for roots crossing the two pairs. For a root within either pair, choose k in the other pair and write xᵢⱼ(y)=[xᵢₖ(y),xₖⱼ(1)]; the cross-pair identities prove the same formula. No third pair is used.

For z∈kerπ, let z_E,z_F be its images under the multiplicative coefficient maps a↦ψ(a)E and a↦ψ(a)F. Nonunital maps send a root-word matrix g to I+λ⁽⁴⁾(g-I), so these images still have identity matrix. Part2 makes them central. The exact conjugation by D interchanges them, hence z_E=z_F.

4. **Orthogonal corner subgroups commute.** Roots with coefficients a∈ERE and c∈FRF commute because ac=ca=0, except that opposite roots need a short reduction. Write xᵢⱼ(a)=[xᵢₖ(a),xₖⱼ(E)] for k distinct from i,j. The opposite root xⱼᵢ(c) commutes with each factor because ca=Ec=0, so it commutes with this commutator. Consequently an orthogonal coefficient sum splits the whole root word into a product of corner images without a residual central factor.

5. **r-ary splitting.** Set Eᵢ=UᵢVᵢ and E₀ᵢ=U₀UᵢVᵢV₀. Then P=∑Eᵢ, E₀=∑E₀ᵢ, and these are orthogonal decompositions. Each Eᵢ with i≠1 is orthogonal and equivalent to E₁. Each E₀ᵢ is also orthogonal and equivalent to E₁: its equivalence is given by d=U₁VᵢV₀ and d*=U₀UᵢV₁. All these elements commute with ψ(R). Part3 therefore identifies every z_Eᵢ and z_E₀ᵢ with one central element c=z_E₀. Part4 gives c=cʳ and θ(z)=∏ᵢz_Eᵢ=cʳ=c. Thus cʳ⁻¹=1. For two arities, θ(z) is the same element with two coprime order bounds; Bézout gives θ(z)=1. ∎

The proof also works in every even rank n≥4 by pairing all indices. It does not establish rank3 for an arbitrary ring: the all-coordinate involution conjugator cannot be formed by this paired argument in odd rank.

## 3. A smaller affine construction, conditional on Theorem2.2

The source's algebra package B,A,φ,p, and separating tensor vector b* is unchanged. Put R=B⊗Bᵒᵖ and use

\[
\Gamma_0=B^4\rtimes\operatorname{St}_4(R),\qquad
\Theta(m,h)=(\varphi^{(4)}(m),\theta(h)).
\]

The semidirect-product finite presentation in source Lemma3.2 already assumes only n≥4. Every auxiliary-index choice excludes at most three indices. Its module kernel is generated by y⊗1-1⊗yᵒᵖ for finitely many algebra generators y. Krstić–McCool therefore supplies St₄(R)'s finite presentation, and the relative presentation supplies Γ₀'s. TheoremA kills the matrix kernel in one step.

Form the ordinary finite mapping-torus presentation ⟨Γ₀,t | t⁻¹xt=Θ(x)⟩, with x ranging over a finite generating set. The direct limit is M∞⁴⋊E∞, because the coefficient and elementary-matrix transitions are injective and the Steinberg kernel is killed. The extra stable letter shifts stages. The source's separating b* detects each nonzero entry of g-I in one advance, so the affine action on M∞⁴ is faithful. The proper support φ(1)≠1 makes the additive stages strictly increasing and detects the stage shift.

To send a nonzero vector to(0,0,0,e), advance to a stage with all entries in the simple algebra A. Any nonzero coordinate m can generate any d∈A by a finite sum ∑aνmbν. If the nonzero coordinate is not4, set coordinate4 to e; otherwise first set coordinate1 to e, then set coordinate4 to e. Use coordinate4 to kill the other three. Thus the elementary part is transitive on nonzero vectors, and translations make the affine action two-transitive. The zero stabilizer is E∞⋊⟨t⟩, generated by t and finitely many base Steinberg generators.

Finally, source Lemma2.1 enlarges G to Ĝ with [(g,g),s]=(g,1). Its units a_g and b yield

\[

=\operatorname{diag}(\nu((g,1)),1,1,1).

These balanced diagonals are elementary; only indices1,2,3 are needed. Hence the input embeds. ## 4. Exact optimal error for a finite-matrix splitting model

**Theorem B (our quantitative obstruction).** Fix r≥2 and d≥1. For complex d×d matrices sᵢ,tᵢ define

\[
\epsilon=\max\left\{
\max_{i,j}\|t_is_j-\delta_{ij}I_d\|,
\left\|\sum_i s_it_i-I_d\right\|\right\}.
\]

Then the infimum over all such matrices, for every d, is

\[
\epsilon_r^*=\max\left\{\frac1r,\frac{r-1}{r+1}\right\}
=\begin{cases}\tfrac12&r=2,\\\frac{r-1}{r+1}&r\ge3.\end{cases}
\]

The same value holds if all tᵢ=sᵢ* and all sᵢ are contractions. This is a universal theorem; the numerical file verifies the attaining matrices only.

**Proof.** Let S=(s₀ … sᵣ₋₁):Cʳᵈ→Cᵈ and T=(t₀ … tᵣ₋₁)ᵀ. Since rank(TS)≤d<rd, TS-I has operator norm at least1. Its r² blocks each have norm≤ε; for a block vector x,

\[
\|(TS-I)x\|\le r\epsilon\|x\|.
\]

Thus ε≥1/r. Trace cyclicity gives tr(ST)=tr(TS). The diagonal block errors imply |tr(TS)-rd|≤rdε, while the partition error implies |tr(ST)-d|≤dε. Consequently (r-1)d≤(r+1)dε, or ε≥(r-1)/(r+1).

For r=2 take sᵢ=tᵢ=I/√2. The diagonal errors and off-diagonal products have norm1/2, and the partition error is0. For r≥3 take sᵢ=tᵢ=√(2/(r+1))I. Diagonal and partition errors are(r-1)/(r+1), while off-diagonal products have norm2/(r+1), no larger. These are adjoint pairs of contractions and attain the lower bound. ∎

For a nonzero orthogonal corner projection p, restrict to pCᵈ and use its identity. Arbitrarily enlarging the ambient matrix size does not evade the bound. For exact matrices over any field, including F₂, the simpler dimension argument works: S and T would give mutually inverse maps Vʳ↔V for V=pFᵈ, forcing r dimV=dimV and therefore p=0. Trace in positive characteristic is unnecessary.

## 5. A precise incompatibility between families247 and250

**Corollary C (our structural barrier).** An algebra separated by finite-dimensional algebra representations cannot contain a nonzero corner with the r-ary splitting identities, for any r≥2. Every representation kills the corner identity by the preceding dimension argument, so separation would force that identity to be zero.

In particular, any nonnegatively graded algebra R=⊕ₙ≥₀Rₙ with each Rₙ finite-dimensional has this property: its finite-dimensional degree quotients R/R≥D separate every element (an element has only finitely many nonzero homogeneous terms). Each finite quotient acts faithfully by its left regular representation. Hence no nonzero properly infinite corner can occur, even in a finite matrix algebra over R.

Family247's `ring-phases.tex`, the proposition labelled `prop:ring-input` and the paragraph preceding its phase lemma, supply exactly this locally finite grading over F₂. Its final construction uses these finite degree quotients to detect matrices, then separately handles the Steinberg kernel. Family250's Theorem2.2 requires a nonzero proper corner p and binary splitting pairs. Those coefficient-ring mechanisms cannot be placed in the same separated algebra while retaining p. If one additionally adopts247's matrix-nil ideal I and R/I=F₂, every idempotent of R is0 or1: an idempotent reducing to0 is nilpotent, and one reducing to1 has nilpotent idempotent complement. This is an even earlier obstruction to250's proper support.

This is an incompatibility of **ring mechanisms**, not a contradiction between the papers or a claim that arbitrary group representations of the affine envelope factor through ring representations. The group constructions may coexist. A successful combination must move to different rings or different categories, or give up separation by finite-dimensional quotients.

## 6. Prior art, consequences, and the next discriminating step

Leavitt's module-type relations are old; the elementary dimension obstruction is standard. The quantitative minimax expression above is derived here, with no claim of literature priority. The source's binary kernel argument is new claimed input; our rank-four and r-ary extension follows the exact relations and is a modest construction improvement.

[Khanh, arXiv2609.08428v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08428v2), dated21 September2026, Theorem5.4, treats the specific binary algebra L_F₂(1,2) and proves Stₙ→GLₙ an isomorphism for n≥3. The present arbitrary-ring coefficient-map theorem has a different interface. Its abstract/date and theorem/proof passage were read; the entire paper was not. [Zaremsky, arXiv2405.18354v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18354v2), dated25 November2024 in version metadata, TheoremA/CorollaryB, proves the point-stabilizer/pair-orbit criterion used by250. Its HTML body prints a later dynamic date; metadata and manuscript date are kept separate. We read the introduction and theorem passage, not its complete proof.

Concrete use: TheoremA lets future affine-envelope constructions trade a binary corner for coprime arities and use four coordinates. TheoremB gives a dimension-independent rejection threshold for numerical corner models. CorollaryC prevents an unproductive attempt to combine residual-finite degree truncations with the properly infinite coefficient package.

Next falsifiable step: independently lift the rank-four conjugation identities in a generic Steinberg presentation, then audit AppendixA LemmaA.1 and §A.5 with explicit malformed sectors and reverse transitions. Matrix identities alone do not certify equality in St₄. Our exact prefix-algebra computation checks coefficient arithmetic and the displayed matrix conjugations; the theorem's lift is supplied by the proof above. The hard global compiler remains the best next allocation if Boone–Higman is the priority. For new mathematics, look for a coefficient package with two nonbinary coprime arities in a category where binary splitting is unavailable; the theorem specifies exactly what would then follow.
