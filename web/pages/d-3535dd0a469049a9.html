# Catalytic-cycle thermodynamic bounds: result and scope

## Verdict

For an autonomous finite $N$-state unicycle, I reconstructed a sharp current bound under a cap on the **sum of bare transition-rate constants**:

\[
|J|\leq \frac{K}{N^2}\tanh\!\left(\frac{|A|}{2N}\right),\quad
K=\sum_i(k_i^++k_i^-),\quad A=\sum_i\log(k_i^+/k_i^-).
\]

It has an exact inverse: to sustain target turnover $J$ under $K\le K_{\max}$, a resolved, tight-coupled unicycle needs

\[
|A|\geq2N\operatorname{artanh}\!\left(\frac{N^2|J|}{K_{\max}}\right),
\]

and therefore at least $2N*k_B*|J|*artanh(N^2*|J|/K_max)$ entropy production per unit time under local detailed balance. The bound is attained by the uniform ring. This is a candidate arbitrary-$N$ extension of a 2026 source-reported three-state current-dissipation frontier. Its novelty and priority are **unresolved**, so I do not claim a new scientific discovery.

The strongest practical conclusion is a boundary: **affinity alone cannot cap turnover, and barrier height alone cannot determine drive work, efficiency, or dissipation.** A measured rate budget or stationary traffic is needed for a speed/dissipation envelope, and a full stoichiometric and load ledger is needed for energetic efficiency. This autonomous theorem does not bound a periodically driven pump without accounting for protocol work and the external clock.

The complete assumptions, proof, inverse consequence, and counterexamples are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md); a finite deterministic diagnostic is in [finite_check.py](finite_check.py).

## Exact model and consequences

The model is an irreducible autonomous CTMC on a directed ring, with one separately identified forward/backward channel on every edge, positive rates, stationary distribution $p_i>0$, and common net current $J$. For $N=2$, “ring” means two distinct parallel channels between the states. Set $K=sum_i(k_i^+ + k_i^-)$ and $A=sum_i log(k_i^+/k_i^-)$. For $A != 0$, equality holds iff $p_i=1/N$ and each stationary edge affinity is $A/N$; when $A=0$, every detailed-balance unicycle has $J=0$ and trivially saturates the zero bound. A uniform rate vector attains equality for all $A$. The algebraic inequality does not require LDB. The thermodynamic identity $Sdot_tot=k_B*A*J$ does require an LDB account that includes all reservoirs.

An illustrative parameter point is $N=3$, $K_max=1/s$, and $J=0.05/s$. The inverse requires $A >= 2.9082$ (about $2.91*k_B*T$ of net drive work per tightly coupled completed cycle); it implies a dissipation floor $Sdot_tot >= 0.1454*k_B/s$. At $A=3$, the current envelope is $J <= 0.05135/s$. These are model calculations, not measured enzyme parameters.

There is a separate established bound when the constraint is **stationary edge traffic** $T=sum_i(p_i*k_i^+ + p_(i+1)*k_i^-)$:

\[
|J|\leq \frac{T}{N}\tanh\!\left(\frac{|A|}{2N}\right).
\]

This is the Equal Traffic Principle (ETP), with equality at equal traffic on every edge. The two budgets are not interchangeable: at the uniform saturator $T=K/N$, but generally $T\ne K/N$. $K$ is a bare-parameter sum; $T$ is the expected number of transitions per unit time. A paper may call either a “kinetic budget,” so the operational definition must be stated.

Under additional tight coupling—one chemical input and one mechanical output per net cycle, no slipping or side cycles—let $A_f$ be dimensionless fuel drive and $l\in[0,A_f]$ the dimensionless load work per cycle. Then $A=A_f-l$ and the pointwise output-power envelope is

\[
P_{out}(l)\leq k_BT\frac{K}{N^2}l\tanh\!\left(\frac{A_f-l}{2N}\right).
\]

This is a conditional operating-envelope statement, not a prediction of a fixed catalyst’s efficiency at maximum power: that also depends on the load dependence of its rates and whether the rates can be retuned to the saturating family. The general second-law bound $0\leq\eta\leq1$ remains, but no extra efficiency conclusion follows from a barrier amplitude or a product current alone.

The precise primary-source distinction is important. Mottes et al. present the $N=3$ expression under $K_tot=sum_i(k_i^+ + k_i^-)$. Yang and Dill instead define $tau_i=p_i*k_i^+ + p_(i+1)*k_i^-$ and use $T=sum_i tau_i$. Yang and Dill distinguish thermodynamic work/efficiency from processivity $J/T$ and describe barrier controls as traffic-routing knobs at fixed cycle force. These are related cycle statements under different constraints, not competing versions of one budget.

## Best discriminator and full measurement/control ledger

The sharpest discriminator is a matched $N=4$ or $N=5$ resolved-ring assay/design comparison, because the $N=3$ bare-rate frontier is already in the 2026 literature and ETP is established for general $N$. First establish a ring with all $2N$ directional channels resolved and no branch, parallel hidden route, missed intermediate, or slippage. Estimate the unconstrained optimum by exact stationary solves over all positive rate vectors at the same measured $A,K$; compare it to the uniform-rate saturator and a prespecified rate-asymmetric design with the same $A,K$. This gives an equally informed comparator that uses the full transition table, not only a heuristic or local optimizer. A verified rate vector whose current exceeds (1), outside joint measurement/model uncertainty, is a direct falsifier. If physical traffic $T$, rather than bare rate sum $K$, is controlled, compare to ETP envelope (6).

Required acquisitions and controls:

1. **Resolve states and channels.** Calibrate a state reporter (e.g. multi-color FRET or a validated single-molecule state classifier), transition identity, missed-event probability, detector dead time, and temporal resolution. Check for additional states and parallel routes by held-out trajectory likelihood and residual dwell/transition structure. State lumping changes $N$, $K$, and the bound’s applicability.
2. **Acquire both directional hazards on every edge.** For state $i$, record exposures $E_i^+=integral 1_(X_t=i) dt$, $E_i^-=integral 1_(X_t=i+1) dt$, and counts $C_i^+,C_i^-$. Estimate rates by $khat_i^+=C_i^+/E_i^+$ and $khat_i^-=C_i^-/E_i^-$; estimate $K,A,p,J,T$ jointly. Large-count planning gives approximately $Var(Ahat) ~= sum_i(1/C_i^+ + 1/C_i^-)$. If every directional count is $C$, a nominal 95% affinity half-width $delta_A$ calls for $C >= 2N*(1.96/delta_A)^2$ counts per direction per edge; rare reverse events near stall/high drive can dominate run time. Use trajectory-block bootstrap or a full likelihood for final intervals because transition counts share one path.
3. **Calibrate the thermodynamic drive independently.** Record temperature, pH, ionic strength, and activities/concentrations of every fuel/product reservoir (for ATP hydrolysis: ATP, ADP, and Pi); calibrate force or torque and displacement. Compare $A_kin=sum_i log(k_i^+/k_i^-)$ with the LDB prediction from chemical potentials and load work. A disagreement indicates a missing state, hidden coupling, nonstationarity, or measurement bias.
4. **Measure power and efficiency inputs.** Count consumed fuel and product formation; measure load-force times displacement/rotation. Check stoichiometric coupling and quantify reverse steps, ATPase slippage, uncoupled cycles, and side products. A matched turnover assay alone measures neither fuel use nor work output.
5. **Run controls.** Include an equilibrium/no-net-drive control, load reversal or stall point, no-fuel/chemical blank where applicable, detector/label calibration, replicate trajectories, and a same-$A,K$ barrier-redistribution control. For dynamic modulation, separately measure waveform period/amplitude, state-energy work, barrier-control work, actuator/clock energy, and protocol hysteresis; do not fold them into $A$ without deriving the appropriate driven-process accounting.
6. **Charge resources.** Report labeling and state-mapping effort, sample/trajectory time, time-resolution and photon budget, chemical titration and buffer preparation, force/torque calibration, catalyst engineering/barrier-tuning effort, replicate count, data exclusion/missing-event rates, stationary-solver/optimization runtime, and uncertainty propagation. Monetary or instrument estimates depend on the actual platform and were not invented here. $K$ is not itself an energy cost.

The uniform-rate design saturates the mathematical bound when the kinetic parameters are independently tunable under the same global $A,K$. Real transition-state energies, chemical stoichiometry, mutation accessibility, and load laws may make that design infeasible; in that case (1) remains a capacity envelope, not a guaranteed achieved performance. The experiment should compare the envelope with the actual feasible design set rather than assuming saturation.

## Prior-art and adjacent-source audit

- Mottes, Zhu & Brenner, [“Gradient-based optimization of exact stochastic kinetic models,” arXiv:2601.14183v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14183), Supplement S7.1–S7.2, gives $N=3$ formula $J_star=K_tot/9*tanh(A/6)$, its near-/far-drive limits, and simulation optimization against the curve. This is direct prior art for the $N=3$ theorem and Pareto frontier.
- Yang & Dill, [“Nonequilibrium Theory for Molecular Machine Design,” arXiv:2605.09105](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09105), especially Eq. (7), SI Eqs. (S34)–(S36), gives the N-step ETP under total edge traffic and applies it to a two-edge F1-ATPase model. The authors’ [accepted Phys. Rev. Research version](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/accepted/10.1103/c8kc-rklj) was listed October 2, 2026. The paper explicitly restricts torque to avoid mechanical slippage and preserve tight coupling, which limits its motor-efficiency claims.
- Pietzonka, Barato & Seifert, [“Universal bounds on current fluctuations,” Phys. Rev. E 93, 052145 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.93.052145), concerns current fluctuations/large deviations; it is not this sharp mean-current versus bare-rate-sum interface.
- Dal Cengio, Lecomte & Polettini, [“Geometry of Nonequilibrium Reaction Networks,” Phys. Rev. X 13, 021040 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.13.021040), develops cycle/cocycle structure for reaction networks; it supports keeping cycle-resolved currents and costs explicit.
- Busiello, Gupta & Maritan, [“Coarse-grained entropy production with multiple reservoirs,” Phys. Rev. Research 2, 043257 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.043257), shows why multiple coupled processes and coarse-graining affect entropy accounting. Generic observed-current caveats are also treated in [“Effective Affinity for Generic Currents in Markov Processes,” J. Stat. Phys. (2025)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10955-025-03433-w); the paper establishes $a^*\bar j\leq\dot s$, with equality only in special uncoupled-current situations.
- Efficiency at maximum power depends on model and constitutive assumptions in existing engine models; see Schmiedl & Seifert, [“Efficiency at maximum power: An analytically solvable model for stochastic heat engines,” EPL 81, 20003 (2008)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.4097), and Tu, [“Efficiency at maximum power of Feynman’s ratchet as a heat engine”](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.1482). Thus the load-envelope maximizer above is not promoted to a universal EMP theorem.
- I reviewed the pinned Astra chemistry and adjacent stochastic-reaction records required for this task: N34, N35, N76, and N77 from commit [`8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83/cards/N35-controlled-chemical-safety.txt). Those address reaction recognition, finite-horizon molecular-jump safety, an event-chain growth bound, and stationary nonuniform-tightness respectively; none supplies this cycle-affinity/rate-sum current theorem. They reinforce that a finite-horizon safety envelope or reaction-certificate claim must not be relabeled as catalytic efficiency or stationary turnover.
