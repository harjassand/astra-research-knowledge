## Our spectral deduction: finite negative index

Let H=−div(A∇)+V on L²(X), defined by its closed H¹ quadratic form

    q[f]=∫_X A∇f·∇f+V|f|².

Its coefficients obey λI≤A≤ΛI, and let M=||V||∞. Split X into D=T²×[−2,2] and the two exterior half-cylinders. Neumann bracketing enlarges the form domain to the direct sum of the three H¹ spaces. Both exterior forms are nonnegative because V=0 there. On the bounded core,

    q_D[f]≥λ∫_D|∇f|²−M∫_D|f|².

The Neumann Laplacian on the compact core has discrete eigenvalues μ_j→∞. Its comparison form is negative on at most the finite-dimensional span with λμ_j<M (include equality for a harmless upper bound). Thus the bracketed form has finite negative index, and restricting its form domain back to global H¹ cannot increase that index. Consequently H has finitely many negative eigenvalues, counting multiplicity. No detailed sign/average of the authors' V is needed.

The authors' u is a genuine L² threshold nullstate: the torus is compact, u has the stated axial decay, and uniform local elliptic energy estimates on overlapping unit cylinders give global H¹ from u∈L² and Hu=0. The distributional equation and form definition then place u in the operator domain with Hu=0. Global H² also follows from the bounded Lipschitz coefficients, but is not required for the form argument.

There is at least one negative eigenvalue. If q≥0 globally, replacing u by |u| preserves its zero form. The nonnegative form Cauchy–Schwarz inequality makes |u| a weak nullstate. Elliptic regularity and Harnack give a positive solution; uniform unit-cylinder Harnack chains imply an axial lower bound c exp(−C|x|), contradicting superexponential decay. This argument uses uniform ellipticity and globally bounded low-order coefficients, all provided in the author construction.

The essential spectral bottom is0: exterior form is nonnegative, so no essential spectrum lies below0. Conversely smooth tests constant on the torus and supported in arbitrarily long disjoint far-out axial intervals have normalized form energies O(length^(−2)). Min–max gives essential bottom≤0. Thus the nullstate lies at the bottom of essential spectrum, above a nonempty finite negative spectrum.

## Consequence and exact boundary

This is an actual counterexample to the statement "finite Morse index excludes real superexponentially decaying threshold states" for general smooth uniformly elliptic operators on X. In fact the exterior equation is div(A∇u)=0 with a manifestly nonnegative exterior form and arbitrary signed inner trace. Proposition4.2 already directly defeats the broad signed-exterior lower-bound lemma in this coefficient class.

It does not answer the Euclidean Schrödinger question: the domain is a cylinder and the principal coefficient A varies. A ground-state transform of the scalar-potential problem produces the special matrix ψ²I (or a Laplacian plus exact-gradient drift); it does not give arbitrary uniformly elliptic A. Here A is anisotropic. No coordinate change or reduction to V alone is supplied. Future spectral hypotheses must therefore preserve the principal-part and geometry interface rather than transfer an exterior claim across all elliptic operators.
