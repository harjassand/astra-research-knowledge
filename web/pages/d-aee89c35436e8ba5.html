4. Common cutoff and coercivity: main lines 116--179
---------------------------------------------------
The fixed zeta is one on B_2 and supported in B_3. Set k_z=2P(z,5).
At s in [5,6], k_s=2P(z,s) and

 Q_s(k_s)=H[(q_r-n_r^2)+(n_r-k_s)^2]<=H dot P.

For a_s=k_s-k_z>=0, expansion gives precisely
Q_s(k_z)=Q_s(k_s)+2a_s H(n_r-k_s)+a_s^2H. The cross-term sign is PLUS.
Since 0<=a_s<=2w with w=P(z,6)-P(z,5), w<=N, and the integral of n_r
on this fixed band is bounded, the integral of Q_s(k_z) is O(w).
The lower kernel bound 2p 6^-n(9/25)^(p-1) on B_3 is correct:
|y-z|<=4 while s>=5. It makes the fixed-cutoff energy E_z<=Cw.
No nonsummable fixed-frequency floor is introduced.

For the coercivity inequality E(b,q)+J(a)>=c_*, the integral
v=int zeta dnu is >=1/(2p), e=int zeta dd<=K, and |F|<=sqrt(ve).
Thus J(a)>= (sqrt(v)|a|-sqrt(e))^2 bounds a on any minimizing sequence;
b in [1,2N] and q in closed B_1 are compact parameters. Weak-star
compactness passes fixed kernels and H(0,1)=1.

If the limiting J is zero, PSD annihilates each (a_i,e_i) wherever zeta>0.
The resulting f=-a nu, beta=aa^t nu, d=|a|^2nu are exact. From grad nu
and div f the same d equals 2|a|^2nu, forcing a=0. The limit then has
E(b,q)=b^2 int zeta dnu>=1/(2p), contradictory. This proves a uniform
positive c_* and includes boundary b=1.

The C-independent constants B and c_* are justified: the coercivity
compactness argument can be performed on M alone, retaining just PSD,

the stress identity in that limiting class. The latter identity is
needed earlier for the exact frequency algebra, not for this coercivity
contradiction. Thus allowing different finite C does not defeat the
advertised B(n,p,N). No effective numerical value of c_* is asserted.

5. Regression for all finite gamma: main lines 181--209
------------------------------------------------------
For probability gamma on good centers in B_1, C_z is its spatial
covariance and lambda its smallest eigenvalue. If lambda=0, gamma is
carried by an affine hyperplane, so the desired best-plane error is zero.
Otherwise a=C_z^-1 int (z-qbar)(k_z-bbar) dgamma is the valid least-squares
coefficient and t=Var(k_z)-a^t C_z a>=0. Expanding the PSD energy gives

 int E_z dgamma=E(bbar,qbar)+int zeta
        [tr(C_z(beta+a f^t+f a^t+aa^t nu))+t dnu].             (5.1)

The covariance cross signs are PLUS because both centered components
of (-k_z,y-z) have minus signs. The sheared beta is PSD, hence its
contraction with C_z is >=lambda times its trace. Also
lambda<=tr C_z<=int|z|^2 dgamma<=1. Consequently the right side is
>=lambda[E(bbar,qbar)+J(a)]>=c_* lambda. This permits qbar outside Z:
the coercivity lemma was proved for EVERY q in closed B_1.

Combining with the common-cutoff defect estimate gives (2.1). Dividing
arbitrary finite nonzero gamma by its mass and multiplying back is exact;
zero gamma is trivial. Restricting gamma to B_r(x) before doing this
preserves the same tuple and frequency. There is no finiteness-of-H^k
premise, independent-variance assumption, or center-density premise.

8. AT, Borel decomposition and variable cutoffs: main lines 290--333,396--417
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
P_0 is the limit of a countable radius family of continuous frequencies
on their positive-height domains. It is Borel on Z. The tail sets Z_j
are Borel, increase, and cover EVERY point of Z by inward frequency
convergence. Their tail bound holds for every center, not just for some
preselected almost-everywhere subset. A finite R_j-ball cover of B_1,
combined with the previously proved small-tail packing, gives finite
H^k(Z_j). Fubini with its small-scale upper growth yields the displayed
finite averaged Jones integral. The positive remaining cutoff R_j makes
the contribution from [R_j,1] finite by total mass alone.

Primary AT: Azzam--Tolsa, Characterization of n-rectifiability in terms of
Jones' square function: Part II, GAFA25 (2015), DOI
10.1007/s00039-015-0334-7. Corollary 1.3 in the primary accepted author PDF
and in arXiv:1501.01572v2 has exactly the finite-H^k measurable-set scope
used here. Its underlying Theorem 1.1 retains positive finite UPPER
density; the corollary supplies it for this Hausdorff restriction. No
positive lower density at all scales is needed or presumed.

The author's beta definition uses closed balls, whereas the proof may
use open ones. For a fixed center and finite measure there are at most
countably many radii with positive sphere mass. The two square functions
therefore agree after ds/s integration; this makes the source alignment
exact. This is a precision completion, not a change to the theorem or
tail constants. Thus each Z_j is countably k-rectifiable, and their
countable union is both rectifiable and critically sigma-finite.

For n=1, the stated convex-density/UCP argument is valid. An alternative
already in my frozen baseline proves it without using a separate UCP
consequence: beta=d, mu=d+c dx, and the sign/domination inequalities give
either no good center or a strictly convex nonnegative nu density. Good
centers are its zeros, hence at most one. Neither route imports positive-
dimensional Reifenberg at k=0. The flat quadratic control has exactly
P=1/2 and zero plane error; it checks the non-strict threshold.

For the original variable-cutoff corollary, the older positive-height
producer was rechecked: a first-contact zero-height ball gives bounded P
on a finite logarithmic interval, then a uniform integral bound on n_r^2,
contradicting integrability of dot log H down to zero height. This uses
the same tuple and local |mu|<=Cd, not a harmonic unique-continuation axiom.
It supplies continuous positive H on common compact room regions before
any division. At each countable common comparison cutoff, choose a base
radius a with 2a strictly below it and B_(4a) inside the room. P(x,2a)
then has a finite maximum over the compact base center region. Good-center
monotonicity supplies a finite N for all smaller radii on that piece.
Local C, center cutoffs, and spatial rooms are all handled by countable
restrictions. No upper bound at every center outside the good set or
global cutoff is silently assumed.
