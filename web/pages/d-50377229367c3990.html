## 1. Interface that passes

One fixed target, independent of sampled characteristics, is

    P(X) = sum_j f_j(X) exp(g_j(X)/h_j(X)).

Here `k>=1`, `d>=0`, `w>=2` are integers; `f_j,g_j,h_j` are integer
multivariate polynomials of total degree at most `d`; each `h_j` is
nonzero; and every cross difference

    D_jt = g_j h_t - g_t h_j,  j<t,

is a nonzero integer polynomial. Every coefficient of `f_j,h_j,D_jt`
has absolute value at most `w`. Thus `deg D_jt<=2d`. This height promise
does **not** bound the individual `g_j` coefficients. Zero `f_j` are
allowed. Distinct exponent fractions are a promise, or must have been
condensed and certified with that work charged.

The exact parameterized oracle is

    Eval(p,q,a,u,v) = sum_j f_j(u) a^[g_j(v)/h_j(v) mod q] mod p,

undefined if any `h_j(v)=0 mod q`. Both characteristics are primes,
`q | p-1`, `a` has order `q`, `u in F_p^n`, and `v in F_q^n`. The
residue exponent is represented in `{0,...,q-1}`; this choice is
immaterial because `a^q=1`. Evaluation uses the same fixed integer
input for every field query.

The tester receives `n,k,d,w` and this oracle. It need not receive
expanded coefficient lists. The promises, all description reads, any
expansion/condensation, and the actual oracle cost remain separate
charged obligations. A fixed-field evaluator, approximate real
evaluator, or floating-point evaluator does not implement this oracle.
General rational circuits `P/P'` require separate denominator analysis;
this gate is for the displayed sum.

## 2. Integer norm and modular root multiplicity

Let `A` be an integer `m by m` matrix with nonzero determinant and
nullity `r` over `F_p`. Choose `m-r` rows spanning its row space modulo
`p`, permute them to the top, and subtract integer lifts of their
linear combinations from the other rows. Determinant-preserving row
operations leave the last `r` rows divisible entrywise by `p`.
Multilinearity gives

    p^r | det A.                                           (1)

This also covers `r=m`. It does not assume square-free determinant or
only one root at a bad prime.

Fix prime `q` and a nonzero `C in Z[z]` with `deg C<=q-1`, fewer than
`q` nonzero coefficients, and `||C||_1<=W`. Write

    Phi_q = 1+z+...+z^(q-1),   R = Res(Phi_q,C).

Irreducibility of `Phi_q` and the degree bound show that `R=0` would
force `C` to be a constant multiple of `Phi_q`, contradicting its
support. Hence `R` is a nonzero integer. The complex product formula
and the triangle inequality give

    |R| = product_(t=1)^(q-1) |C(omega^t)| <= W^(q-1).       (2)

Multiplication by `C` on the free integer module `Z[z]/(Phi_q)` has
determinant `R`. For prime `p=1 mod q`, `p>q`, and `Phi_q` splits
into `q-1` distinct nonidentity `q`th roots over `F_p`. Chinese
remaindering makes the multiplication map diagonal with eigenvalues
`C(a)`. Its nullity is precisely the number `r_p` of these roots where
`C(a)=0`. Equation (1) yields `p^(r_p)|R`.

For a finite set `S` of distinct such primes, coprimality implies

    product_(p in S) p^(r_p) | R,
    sum_(p in S) r_p log2 p <= (q-1) log2 W.                 (3)

Let `ell_p=floor(log2 p)`, `T_p=sum ell_p`, and `b_W=ceil(log2 W)`.
Draw `p` with probability `ell_p/T_p` and then a uniform nonidentity
`q`th root. Since `q` is prime these are exactly the primitive roots.
The exact average and (3) give

    Pr[C(a)=0] = sum_p ell_p r_p / ((q-1)T_p)
               <= log2 W/T_p <= b_W/T_p.                    (4)

All coefficients may disappear modulo a particular `p`: then
`r_p=q-1`, and that event is already charged. No resultant computation,
list of bad primes, field-size density promise, or coefficient witness
acquisition is needed by the algorithm.

## 3. Inner prime capacity and exact conditioning

For each of the `L=k+binom(k,2)` nonzero polynomials `h_j,D_jt`, choose
one nonzero integer coefficient `c` with `|c|<=w`. The choices are
proof witnesses fixed before sampling `q`. For distinct primes in a
finite set `Q`,

    sum_(q in Q, q|c) floor(log2 q) <= log2|c|.

Consequently, sampling `q` with weight `ell_q=floor(log2 q)` and
capacity `T_q=sum ell_q` has bad-characteristic probability at most

    L ceil(log2 w)/T_q.                                    (5)

Outside this event each `h_j,D_jt` is nonzero modulo `q`. With uniform
`v in F_q^n`, Schwartz–Zippel and a union bound cost at most

    [kd+2d binom(k,2)]/q = dk^2/q                           (6)

for a denominator zero or coincident exponent residues. On its
complement, `alpha_j=g_j(v)/h_j(v)` are distinct elements of `F_q`.

For a nonzero target at least one `f_j` is nonzero. Choose a fixed
monomial `X^mu` with nonzero integer coefficient vector

    eta_j = [X^mu] f_j,  sum_j |eta_j| <= kw.

Place these coefficients at the distinct positions `alpha_j` to form
`C_v(z)`. It is nonzero with support at most `k<q`, degree at most
`q-1`, and norm at most `kw`. Crucially these are coefficient
extractions, not evaluated integer values `f_j(u)`. Their height is
independent of the magnitude of `u,v` and of the sampled prime `p`.

For each fixed good `q,v`, the polynomial `C_v` is independent of `p`.
If `H_(a,v)(X)=sum f_j(X)a^alpha_j` is the zero polynomial over
`F_p`, its selected coefficient `C_v(a)` vanishes, so (4) charges the
event by `ceil(log2(kw))/T_p(q)`. Otherwise uniform `u` costs at most
`d/p`. This includes complete disappearance of `f` coefficients.

Drawing `p` before `v` is harmless provided they are independent
conditional on `q`. Lazy pool acquisition may depend on `q` and the
promised parameters only. A public pool may even be known before the
input is fixed: the inequalities are uniform over every fixed input
with the same height promise. The forbidden dependence is adapting
the target to the realized random characteristic, or presenting
different integer coefficient lifts to different field queries.

## 4. The composed tester and completeness

For certified pools with `q>k`, define `q_min`, `p_min`, and
`T_p,min=min_q T_p(q)`. Draw all samples as above, set
`a=gamma_p^r` for uniform `r in {1,...,q-1}`, and accept if `Eval` is
zero or undefined. For every fixed nonzero admitted target,

    Pr[accept] <= L b_w/T_q + dk^2/q_min
                  + d/p_min + b_(kw)/T_p,min.                (7)

The proof is the four paid events (5), (6), zero selected coefficient,
and point evaluation of a nonzero polynomial. In particular no
`g(v)/h(v)` integer numerator height is smuggled into the norm bound.

For completeness, an identity with distinct rational exponent
fractions must have every `f_j=0`. If one `f_j` were nonzero, choose
a rational point avoiding the zero sets of that polynomial and all
`h_j,D_jt`. Such a point exists because a nonzero polynomial cannot
vanish on all rational points. At that point the exponent values are
distinct rational numbers and some coefficient is a nonzero rational
number. Lindemann–Weierstrass linear independence of exponentials of
distinct algebraic numbers contradicts zero. Thus every defined query
for a genuine zero target is zero; undefined queries are accepted.
This proof also handles constant polynomials (`d=0`).

Put

    U=max{k,6d(k^2+1)},
    T_q>=6L b_w,    T_p(q)>=6b_(kw),    every q>U.

Because `p>q`, the degree terms together are less than `1/6`; each
capacity term is at most `1/6`. Therefore (7) is strictly less than
`1/2`. For the same fixed input and certified pools, fresh independent
samples over `t` repetitions give error less than `2^-t`.

The tester remains randomized. Exact uniform/weighted sampling from
finite integer ranges can use binary-bit rejection with expected
polynomial cost. A bounded worst-case number of random bits is not
automatic for ranges whose size is not a power of two. This distinction
does not change deterministic acquisition or the exact soundness bound.

## 5. Deterministic acquisition: every rejected candidate is charged

For `n>=3`, the central binomial coefficient satisfies

    4^n/(2n+1) <= binom(2n,n) <= (2n)^pi(2n).

The lower bound uses the largest of `2n+1` coefficients summing to
`4^n`. For the upper bound, Legendre's formula writes each prime
valuation as a sum of terms in `{0,1}`, at most
`floor(log_p(2n))`; its prime-power contribution is at most `2n`.
Since `2n+1<=2^n`,

    pi(2n) >= n/log2(2n).

For integers `U,M>=1`, take `2n=16(U+M)^2`. Writing `t=U+M`,
`log2(16t^2)<=8t`, so there are at least `t` primes up to that bound.
At most `U` lie below or at `U`; hence at least `M` lie above `U`.

With `A=6L b_w`, `ell_q0=floor(log2(U+1))>=1`, and
`M_q=ceil(A/ell_q0)`, enumerate integers above `U`, retaining distinct
trial-division-certified primes until capacity `A`. Stop by

    q_max <= Q_max=16(U+M_q)^2.                             (8)

Trial division of every encountered candidate costs at most
`O(Q_max^(3/2) polylog Q_max)` bit operations using elementary
arithmetic. This is a loose bound including failures, not an optimal
primality algorithm claim.

The external prerequisite is the uniform effective least-prime theorem:
there are absolute effective `C>=1` and an integer exponent `Lambda=6`
such that every unit residue class `a mod m`, `m>=2`, contains a prime
`p<=C m^6`. This follows from Xylouris's effective exponent `5.2`,
enlarging the constant if needed. The definition takes the maximum
over **all** unit residue classes; it is not an average-modulus result.
[Xylouris, arXiv0906.2749v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2749v1),
printed pp6–9, especially Theorem 1.1.

For a fixed prime `q` and required count `M`, acquire a prime

    ell>max(q,M),   ell<=16(max(q,M)+1)^2.

For each `t=1,...,M`, CRT gives one class modulo `q ell` satisfying
`a_t=1 mod q` and `a_t=t mod ell`. Because `q!=ell` and
`1<=t<=M<ell`, every class is a unit. The classes are distinct, and
uniform Linnik supplies one prime in each below `C(q ell)^6`.
These primes are distinct because their residues modulo `ell` differ.
All are `1 mod q`. This closes the many-prime quantifier without
assuming that repeated applications to one class yield different
primes, or importing a density statement.

For `D=6b_(kw)`, let `M_p(q)=ceil(D/floor(log2(q+1)))`. The ordinary
progression stream `p=jq+1`, `j=1,2,...`, may simply retain every
certified prime and stop at capacity `D`. The CRT proof bounds its
stopping point by

    p_max(q) <= C[16q(max(q,M_p(q))+1)^2]^6.                (9)

The algorithm need not implement CRT or know the numerical value of
`C`. Equation (9) proves its stopping-time bound. Trial division
including rejected progression candidates again costs at most
`O(p_max^(3/2) polylog p_max)` bit operations.

For each certified `p`, try `h=2,...,p-1` until
`gamma=h^((p-1)/q)!=1 mod p`. Cyclicity of `F_p^*` makes the map's
image have order `q`, so a suitable `h` exists. The tests
`gamma!=1` and `gamma^q=1` certify exact order because `q` is prime.
This costs at most `p-2` modular powers and needs no factorization of
`p-1` or supplied primitive generator.

Since `L<=k^2`, `b_(kw)<=b_w+ceil(log2 k)`, and `M_p<=6b_(kw)`,
(8)–(9) bound every numerical field size, pool length, and acquisition
operation by a polynomial in **numerical** `d,k,log w`. Materializing
all `S_q` multiplies by a polynomial number of inner primes; lazy
acquisition is also valid. Field widths are asymptotically
`O(log(d+1)+log(k+1)+log(log w+1))`, plus an absolute constant
contribution from `C`. No useful concrete maximum field width follows
here because that effective constant is not numerically instantiated.
