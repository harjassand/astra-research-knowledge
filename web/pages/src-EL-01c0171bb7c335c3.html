# Conditional hazard error: an acquisition obstruction and three exact interfaces

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: internally reconstructed finite theorem and acquisition boundary; independent review and historical originality remain UNKNOWN. This note is separate from, and does not alter, the frozen `RESEARCH_NOTE.md` or its acquisition theorem. The original Astra N718 argument remains unreviewed research capital.

## 1. The stronger quantity

Use the finite hidden jump-process and frozen visible-history predictor interface of the main note. Write

    r_t = Q^{J_t} E(X_t,:),
    rbar_t = E[r_t | H_t],
    e_t = rbar_t - vhat(t,H_t).

Here H_t includes the actual visible history, the current action and any recorded controller randomness. Predictable versions are used at jumps. All target exit rates and the predictor's total visible exit rate are at most R. The complete generator row toward visible labels is used, including its negative current-label entry. Let U be independent uniform occupation time on [0,T]. Define

    D_raw  = E ||r_U - vhat(U,H_U)||^2,
    D_cond = E ||rbar_U - vhat(U,H_U)||^2.

Orthogonal projection gives the exact decomposition

    D_raw = D_cond + E ||r_U-rbar_U||^2.                 (1)

The last term is conditional hidden rate variance. It can be positive when the predictor reproduces the entire visible path exactly. The visible conditional intensity itself gives the path comparison bound

    TV(P_visible, P_predictor) <= min(1, T sqrt((k-1)D_cond)),

plus any initial visible-law discrepancy. To see this, couple the two history-dependent visible jump processes while their histories agree, using common destination-label jumps at the minimum of their two intensities. The discrepancy intensity is at most the sum of absolute off-current-label intensity differences. Its expected time integral is at most T sqrt((k-1)D_cond) by Cauchy-Schwarz. This needs no hidden comparison process.

The extension question is whether reset, independent random stopping, ordinary probes and product-gain probes can acquire D_cond with a budget polynomial in the horizon and inverse accuracy, without hidden-history matching. The answer is negative in the general finite class already allowed by the main theorem. Exact synthesis and known gains do not repair it.

## 2. A six-label family with the same generator and probe library

Fix m>=1, N=2^m and M=N/2. A context h=(i,b) consists of a pair ID i in {1,...,M} and a bit b in {0,1}. Identify it bijectively with an m-bit string (h_1,...,h_m). A latent sign z is in {-1,+1}.

Hidden states are (h,z,j), 0<=j<=m+1, and one absorbing state B. The generator is the same in every model:

* (h,z,j) -> (h,z,j+1) at rate 1 for 0<=j<=m;
* (h,z,m+1) -> B at rate r_z=c+delta z, where c=1/2 and delta=1/4;
* there are no other off-diagonal rates.

There are 2N(m+2)+1 hidden states. The size is disclosed and exponential in m; no short description of an arbitrary alternative preparation is asserted. The visible labels are the four pairs (j mod 2,h_j) for 1<=j<=m, together with A and B. At j=0 the fixed visible label is (0,0); at j=m+1 it is A. Every prefix step changes parity and is visible. Thus the visible history reveals all m bits of h after m jumps, using six labels in total. The next rate-one step enters A. The hidden context and sign never change within an episode.

Reset prepares j=0. The null preparation is

    P_0(H=h,Z=z) = 1/(2N).

For any fixed vector xi=(xi_1,...,xi_M) in {-1,+1}^M, the alternative preparation is

    P_xi(H=(i,b), Z=z)
      = [1 + alpha z (-1)^b xi_i]/(2N),   alpha=1/2.    (2)

These are independent repeatable resets within each model. Every H is uniform; Z is fair marginally in every alternative, not just on average over xi. Each conditional sign mass is at least 1/4. The only change between models is the unknown repeatable preparation. The generator, visible map, available actuation, known rate bound and frozen predictor are identical. The initial visible label is fixed.

The standard geometric probe library is available. For every visible label a, take

    g_{a,+}(x,y)=2^[e_a(y)-e_a(x)],
    g_{a,-}(x,y)=2^[-e_a(y)+e_a(x)].

Products multiply these gains on the same edge. Primitive gains lie in [1/2,2], products in [1/4,4], and all used exit rates are at most 4. Internal edges, if present, would be neutral. In this family every edge is visible. The signed source operator V_a is synthesized exactly by

    V_a = (K_{a,+}-K_{a,-})/3,                         (3)

since (2^d-2^-d)/3=d/2 for d in {-1,0,1}, and both sides have zero row sums. The coefficient l1 budget is 2/3. Therefore the obstruction below persists with a constant number of known controls, exact source synthesis, bounded product rates and no gain calibration problem.

No allowed probe changes the selected h or z, creates another sign, or returns a spent episode to its preparation. Even arbitrary adaptive switching among these generators only changes clocks along the same preselected hidden path.

## 3. One explicit null predictor is perfect

The predictor records the number of visible prefix jumps and the bits they reveal. At depth j<m it assigns rate 1/2 to each possible next-bit label and total exit rate 1. At depth m it assigns rate 1 to A. At B it assigns zero. At A, if s is time since entry, it assigns the hazard

    lambda_0(s) = c-delta tanh(delta s).                (4)

This is the exact null visible conditional hazard. Before A, preselected bits are independent fair bits and prefix clocks are independent rate-one exponentials. At A the initial hidden sign is fair, and its two survival probabilities are exp(-(c+delta)s) and exp(-(c-delta)s). Their posterior average rate is (4). Consequently

    D_cond(P_0)=0

for every T. There is no predictor fitting or policy transfer in this statement: the same explicit frozen code is supplied to all experiments.

For an alternative, the full context has been revealed when A is entered. Its prior conditional sign mean is

    theta_h = alpha (-1)^b xi_i in {-1/2,+1/2}.

The A survival function and conditional hazard are

    S_theta(s) = exp(-cs)[cosh(delta s)-theta sinh(delta s)],

    lambda_theta(s)
      = c + delta [theta-tanh(delta s)]/[1-theta tanh(delta s)].

Their exact difference is

    lambda_theta(s)-lambda_0(s)
      = delta theta [1-tanh^2(delta s)]/[1-theta tanh(delta s)]. (5)

The full visible row difference at A has two nonzero entries, the negative hazard difference and the positive hazard difference. Its squared norm is twice the square of (5).

Let a=m+1, T=4a, and let tau be A's arrival time. Then tau has the Erlang(a,1) law and is independent of (H,Z). For 0<=s<=1,

    tanh(delta s)<=1/4,
    |lambda_theta(s)-lambda_0(s)| >= 5/48,
    S_theta(s)>=exp(-3/4)>1/3.

The middle bound follows directly from delta alpha=1/8, 1-tanh^2>=15/16 and 1-theta tanh<=9/8. Markov's inequality gives

    P(tau<=T-1) >= 1-a/(4a-1) >= 2/3.

Integrating just the first unit of terminal time, whenever it fits before T, proves for every xi

    D_cond(P_xi)
      >= (1/T)(2/3)(2)(5/48)^2(1/3)
       = 25/(5184 T) > 1/(256 T).                     (6)

This is an exact analytic lower bound. Numerical integration in the separate verifier is a diagnostic, not its premise.

The raw discrepancy from the main theorem is exactly the same under the null and all alternatives, for every horizon:

    D_raw(P_xi)=D_raw(P_0).                            (7)

At every j<m the hidden row error has squared norm 1/2, regardless of the actual next bit; at j=m and B it is zero. At A the raw squared error is 2(r_z-lambda_0(s))^2. Terminal occupancy, averaged over contexts, depends only on the fair marginal of Z and on tau, which is common. This proves (7). Thus even an exact active Gram measurement of D_raw cannot subtract the conditional variance here.

The visible alternatives also have a nonvanishing path-law discrepancy. Retain, on tau<=T-1, the full context and the indicator of departure from A during its first unit. Conditional departure probabilities differ by

    F_theta(1)-F_0(1) = theta exp(-c)sinh(delta).

The context is uniform and the arrival density is common, so the total variation distance of this retained subexperiment is

    P(tau<=T-1) alpha exp(-c)sinh(delta)
      >= (2/3)alpha exp(-c)sinh(delta) > 1/32.

For the last elementary bound, exp(-1/2)>1/2 and sinh(1/4)>1/4 suffice. The event realizing this distance depends on xi. Learning which history-dependent event to use is the difficult part.

## 4. Lower bound for the complete active interface

**Theorem.** Suppose an algorithm can independently reset the preparation and, within each reset, use arbitrary adaptive random stopping, all the primitive and product settings above, arbitrary visible readouts and arbitrary observation time. If at most r resets are used, the total variation distance between its null transcript law and its transcript law averaged over independent uniform xi_i is at most

    r(r-1)/(2M).                                      (8)

This remains true for a strictly stronger oracle that, immediately after every reset and at no cost, reveals the entire preselected pair (H,Z).

**Proof.** Given (H,Z), the generator and all control kernels are the same in every model. A controller can be simulated with common fresh randomness conditional on these preselected variables. Probes, random clocks, postselection and all their visible records are therefore a common stochastic channel from one oracle sample (H,Z) per reset. They cannot distinguish better than those oracle samples. If the algorithm adapts across resets or stops early, simulate r independent oracle samples in advance and reveal only the requested ones; its complete transcript is still a common channel of them.

For each oracle sample write I for the pair ID and W=(-1)^b Z. Under the null, the I's are independent uniform on {1,...,M}, and the W's are independent fair signs. Under alternative xi,

    P(W=w | I=i,xi)=(1+alpha w xi_i)/2.

The b bits are independent fair ancillary variables in both cases. Now average over a single xi vector chosen at the start and kept fixed across all resets. Conditional on the event that all sampled I's are distinct, each W uses a fresh independent unknown xi_i; after averaging it is fair and independent of all other W's. Hence the two complete oracle data laws agree exactly on that event. The I law and collision probability are common. A union bound over reset pairs gives (8). Data processing proves the active-transcript assertion. This proof is finite and does not use any asymptotic conditional-independence theorem. QED.

For a tester having both null and worst-alternative error at most 1/3, the sum-of-errors bound requires transcript TV>=1/3. Thus

    r(r-1) >= 2M/3,
    r = Omega(sqrt(M)) = Omega(2^(m/2)).               (9)

This applies, in particular, to either of these promised outputs:

* an additive estimator of D_cond with error at most 1/(1024T) and success probability at least 2/3;
* a sound upper certificate C with probability at least 2/3 under every model, which also returns C<=1/(512T) with probability at least 2/3 under the perfect null predictor.

Threshold C or the estimate at 1/(512T). Equation (6) separates the two cases. An upper bound that always returns a large value is not excluded: perfect-predictor completeness is part of the promise being tested. Expected-reset budgets satisfy the same order lower bound when uniformly bounded under the null and each alternative, by truncation at a constant multiple of the budget and the same sum-of-errors argument.

Here T=O(m), inverse requested accuracy is O(m), k=6, there is one target action, primitive count 12, product-rate bound 4, and synthesis budget 2/3. Consequently no budget polynomial jointly in these interface parameters, independent of hidden or history count, can universally acquire the less conservative quantity while recognizing all perfect predictors.

This is not a coverage failure between training and deployment. Acquisition and evaluation use exactly the same repeatable preparation and the same target process. Every context is equally likely, every sign conditional on context has positive constant mass, the visible initial label is fixed, and the hard association is present in the actual occupation histories being tested. The missing resource is repeated conditional information about one context among exponentially many contexts. A physical realization of arbitrary xi, its preparation cost and its exponential description are not asserted cheap. The lower bound supplies even the generator and latent sign readout for free, making this initial-association barrier explicit.

The statement uses a finite reducible episodic model and an unknown preparation. It is not a lower bound for an additional promise of a known preparation, irreducibility with a uniform mixing bound, or a supplied small conditional predictive representation.

## 5. Why a bounded single-copy observable cannot remove the variance

A tempting attack is to search for another positive product-flux identity whose expectation is D_cond. That would have to overcome a basic affinity obstruction. The average of the preparations in (2), over independent uniform xi, is exactly P_0's hidden preparation. Every fixed one-reset active experiment is a common stochastic channel of that preparation, so the expectation of any integrable one-reset statistic is affine in it. But D_cond is zero at P_0 and strictly positive at every P_xi. Therefore no one-reset statistic, bounded or otherwise integrable throughout this finite family, can be an unbiased estimator of D_cond. This includes arbitrary one-episode adaptive products and random stopping; it does not merely exclude the existing Gram identity.

With r resets, if a fixed statistic Z_r satisfies |Z_r|<=B and is unbiased for D_cond on this family, (6) and (8) force

    1/(256T) <= |E_mix Z_r-E_0 Z_r|
              <= 2B TV(mix,0)
              <= B r(r-1)/M.

Thus, for r>=2,

    B >= M/[256 T r(r-1)].                            (10)

A polynomial number of resets cannot hide the obstruction in an unbiased bounded-magnitude readout; its required range is exponential. Unbiasedness is stronger than the certificate promise, while (9) already covers arbitrary biased estimators and adaptive decision procedures with finite success guarantees.

The failed linear-identity attack changes the mathematical task: the unknown object is a conditional mean square, not a same-state rate square. Products multiply edge gains inside one hidden draw. They do not turn that draw into two independent draws from its visible-history posterior.

## 6. The birthday bound is a matched resource cost

The sqrt(M) scale is not only a loose lower-bound artifact in the stronger oracle. It is attained, up to constants for fixed alpha, by ordinary episodes of this family.

Run an episode until A, discarding it if tau>T-1. Acceptance has probability at least 2/3 and is independent of H and Z. On acceptance record the context and B_1, the indicator that A is left during its first unit. Put

    f_z = 1-exp(-(c+delta z)),
    a_0=(f_+ + f_-)/2,
    d_0=(f_+ - f_-)/2>0,
    V=(-1)^b (B_1-a_0)/d_0.

Then |V|<=K for an absolute constant K and

    E[V | I=i,xi]=alpha xi_i,
    E_0[V | I=i]=0.

For q independent accepted episodes form the observable collision statistic

    S = sum_{j<l} 1{I_j=I_l} V_j V_l.

Under the null E S=0. Under every fixed alternative,

    E S = A_q alpha^2/M,    A_q=q(q-1)/2.

Disjoint-pair covariances vanish. A pair summand has second moment at most K^4/M. For two distinct summands sharing one index, the alternative covariance is

    alpha^2 [E(V^2)-alpha^2]/M^2 <= alpha^2 K^2/M^2;

the null covariance is zero. There are 3 binom(q,3) unordered pairs of summands sharing one index. Consequently

    Var_0(S) <= A_q K^4/M,
    Var_xi(S) <= A_q K^4/M + 6 binom(q,3) alpha^2 K^2/M^2. (11)

Threshold S at half its alternative mean. Chebyshev's inequality and (11) give errors at most 1/3 for q=C sqrt(M), with a sufficiently large absolute C, uniformly as M grows; enlarging C covers the finite small M cases. Since alpha, K are constants and accepted episodes have constant probability, O(sqrt(M)) expected resets suffice. A fixed reset cap with the same order and a larger constant gives the same success guarantee. Each episode costs at most T of observation. No active probe is needed for this upper comparison.

Thus the hard family exposes a genuine matched birthday cost, not an unidentifiable functional or a claim that every conditional problem requires exponential samples. There are finitely many context probabilities; enough repeated contexts identify them.

## 7. Exact interfaces that change the conclusion

### 7.1 Observable path dual witnesses

For any fixed bounded predictable vector critic f(t,H_t), the visible jump compensator identity is

    E int f dot dE(Y_t) = E int f dot rbar_t dt.

Therefore

    L(f) = (2/T) E int f dot [dE(Y_t)-vhat(t,H_t)dt]
                         - (1/T) E int ||f||^2 dt,

    D_cond = sup_f L(f),
    D_cond-L(f) = (1/T) E int ||e_t-f(t,H_t)||^2 dt.    (12)

The supremum is achieved by f=e, whose norm is at most 2sqrt(2)R. Every frozen critic gives an observable lower bound on D_cond without any history matching or hidden state information. This is an exact falsification operation. Absence of a detected critic gives an upper certificate only if a supplied approximation or complexity promise ensures that the search can approximate e. In the hard family, the sign pattern xi is precisely the missing exponential critic description/acquisition.

A conservative finite acquisition bound is explicit. For a critic with ||f||<=F, its single-path score Z_f obeys

    |Z_f| <= (2sqrt(2)F/T) N_T + 2sqrt(2)FR+F^2,

where N_T is visible jump count, dominated by Poisson(lambda), lambda=RT. Zero the score when N_T>L, without conditioning or discarding resets. The absolute truncation bias is at most

    (4sqrt(2)FR+F^2) P(Poisson(lambda)>=L).

The elementary exponential-moment bound makes this <=epsilon/2 by taking

    L >= (e-1)lambda + log[2(4sqrt(2)FR+F^2)/epsilon].

The retained score is bounded by

    K_L=(2sqrt(2)F/T)L+2sqrt(2)FR+F^2.

For a frozen finite library of h critics, n>=8 K_L^2 epsilon^-2 log(2h/zeta) independent episodes estimates all L(f) to epsilon with probability at least 1-zeta. Numerical integration of the predictable critics and predictor is additionally charged. If the critic is fitted, use independent validation or a justified uniform complexity bound. This operation can efficiently disprove a proposed predictor when a simple witness is available; it does not bypass (9) for universal completeness.

### 7.2 Independent conditional replicas

Suppose there is an extra operation: after an unprobed random stop with observed history H_U, prepare X_1 and X_2 independently from the posterior law Law(X_U | H_U), keeping the same history, time and target action. Then

    D_cond = E <r(X_1)-vhat(H_U), r(X_2)-vhat(H_U)>.    (13)

Independent short continuations give its rate cross-moment. In particular the mixed endpoint derivative of

    E <E(Y^1_{h_1})-E(Y_U), E(Y^2_{h_2})-E(Y_U)>

at (h_1,h_2)=(0,0) is E||rbar_U||^2. Tagged first derivatives acquire the predictor cross terms. Finite time jets can be acquired as in the main note, now from a joint conditional-preparation experiment. Gains are unnecessary for this particular cross-moment once the conditional replica operation is supplied.

Copying the actual hidden state is different: two independent future branches from the same X_U give E||r_U||^2, and substituting this in (13) yields D_raw. They are not independent posterior draws conditional only on the visible history. Likewise applying two gain factors on one edge produces a same-state moment. Neither operation supplies (13).

Ordinary reset/rejection can realize conditional replicas only at its actual matching cost. Exact matching of continuous histories need not even have positive probability. In this finite family the relevant context is discrete and uniform; matching a specified context by fresh resets costs N in expectation, whereas pooling accidental matches yields the sqrt(N) aggregate-testing scale of Sections 4 and 6. A native conditional reset/resampling primitive would remove that cost and is a genuinely stronger acquisition interface. Its correctness cannot be inferred from the product law.

### 7.3 A supplied finite conditional closure

Let C=C(U,H_U) be a known observable context with at most L states, including enough information to make the predictor C-measurable. Suppose a supplied closure guarantee gives

    delta_C^2 = E ||rbar_U-E[rbar_U | C]||^2 <= delta^2.

Let mu_c=P(C=c) and m_c=E[1{C=c}(r_U-vhat)]. Then

    D_cond = sum_{c:mu_c>0} ||m_c||^2/mu_c + delta_C^2. (14)

The first moments m_c are acquired by context-tagged first-time jets with the tag and predictor frozen at the stop, or from the compensator identity in (12). The masses are ordinary occupation observations. No product probe or hidden reconstruction is needed.

There is no need to divide by arbitrarily small measured masses: since ||r_U-vhat||<=2sqrt(2)R, omitting all contexts with mu_c<eta/L loses at most 8R^2 eta in (14). A robust empirical version uses simultaneous mass errors <=eta/(4L), retains cells with muhat_c>=eta/(2L), and divides only by muhat_c-eta/(4L)>0. Every retained true mass is at least eta/(4L), and omitted total mass is below 3eta/4. Moment confidence intervals supply a conservative upper bound for each retained numerator. Thus fixed L, a supplied closure error delta and charged tagging/predictor computation give a polynomial finite acquisition procedure with controlled truncation, statistical and jet errors.

The closure error is a substantive supplied or independently justified gate. It is not certified by observing the finite cell moments alone; the counterexample has exponentially many contexts, and a small unvalidated representation cannot silently stand in for them. Predictor quantization, if needed to make it C-measurable, requires an additional explicit error allowance.

## 8. Source and scope receipts

The source of product-gain completion is the full original Astra N718 proof, pinned at revision `5fc72a144d77c31b400efc0768afd7833c702362`, read in the main investigation. High-cardinality conditional distribution testing is an established subject. [Canonne, Diakonikolas, Kane and Stewart, Testing Conditional Independence of Discrete Distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11560) gives general upper and lower bounds with explicit dependence on conditioning-domain size. Its full theorem is not imported here; the simpler paired-sign birthday proof in Section 4 is self-contained. [Chakraborty, Fischer, Goldhirsh and Matsliah, On the Power of Conditional Samples in Distribution Testing](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2012/154/) explicitly distinguishes independent conditional sampling from ordinary sampling. Its conditional oracle is broader than the posterior-replica interface in Section 7.2; this is a conceptual interface comparison, not an attribution of a new sampling oracle. The two public abstracts were reopened on 2026-10-12.

The contributed claim is scoped: under the actual reset/random-stop/product-gain interface, a universal removal of conditional hidden-rate variance meets an exponential history-association acquisition barrier, even with exact known source synthesis. The exact counterexample, transcript domination, finite collision lower bound and observable alternatives are internally reconstructed here. This does not establish a first result in conditional-independence testing, a new general martingale duality, a physical demonstration, or a foundational capability already achieved.
