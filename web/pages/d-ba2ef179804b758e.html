A. NEW PRIOR-ART CONTACT: RUELLE 1982 AND 1984

David Ruelle's primary article “Large Volume Limit of the Distribution of Characteristic Exponents in Turbulence,” Commun. Math. Phys. 87 (1982), 287–302, is at
https://www.ihes.fr/~ruelle/PUBLICATIONS/%5B73%5D.pdf

This is more directly relevant than the citation list in the earlier entropy-power audit. Section 1 studies the Navier–Stokes evolution of a divergence-free velocity field in a bounded Ω⊂R^d, d=2 or 3, with viscosity, forcing/boundary input, and an invariant probability measure of compact support on the state space of velocity fields; in d=3 a neighborhood of the support must have a well-defined evolution. The variational equation and its Lyapunov exponents are those of perturbations of the VELOCITY FIELD, not the particle map generated by a frozen velocity v(x). Theorem 2.1 bounds sums/moments of positive state-space exponents by dissipation-dependent terms, and Corollary 2.2 then bounds the Navier–Stokes KS invariant by those positive exponents. The paper explicitly says that for 2D the information-production rate is bounded by a multiple of energy-dissipation rate at fixed viscosity. Thus “entropy/information production is bounded by dissipation” is established prior art by 1982. Its entropy is PDE state-space entropy, and its constant/functional depends on dimension, viscosity, volume/domain, and Lieb–Thirring-type estimates; it is not the sharp coefficient-4 inequality for particle-map volume entropy under the candidate's smooth steady 3D interface. The PDF attributes the infinite-dimensional KS/exponent step to Ruelle's finite-dimensional proof with extension via private communication, so that part is not independently replayed here.

Ruelle, “Characteristic Exponents for a Viscous Fluid Subjected to Time Dependent Forces,” Commun. Math. Phys. 93 (1984), 285–300, primary IHES PDF:
https://repo-archives.ihes.fr/FONDS_IHES/I_Prepublications/RUELLE/1977-1990/P_84_01/P_84_01_web.pdf
The source explicitly extends characteristic-exponent, KS-entropy, and attractor-dimension estimates for incompressible Navier–Stokes in bounded regions of R²/R³ to time-dependent forcing. Again the state variable is the fluid velocity field, with state-space variational equation. The candidate is an autonomous particle flow on a finite-dimensional closed Riemannian 3-manifold, not a Navier–Stokes attractor entropy theorem. The 1984 article also stresses unresolved 3D well-posedness and imposes the relevant smooth evolution/support hypotheses; it does not prove the candidate's particle-entropy estimate.

Exact quantitative comparison that survives this audit:
- Candidate: H=h_μ(φ_1), natural logs, particle flow, fixed clock; H²≤E|Def X|²/2; under its chosen PDE W=2ν∫|Def X|² dVol, so W≥4ν V H². The coefficient 4 is attained by the Sol suspension. It is a lower bound on sustaining power for supplied particle entropy, equivalently an upper bound on particle entropy at fixed power.
- Ruelle 1982/84: h_state/positive state-space exponent sums for Navier–Stokes evolution bounded above by dissipation controls, with explicit dimension/viscosity/domain estimates; 2D gives linear-in-dissipation information rate at fixed ν. This neither implies nor contradicts the candidate coefficient because h_state and h_μ(φ_1) are different dynamical systems/observables. At an exact steady solution, the Navier–Stokes state-space orbit may be an equilibrium with zero state-space entropy while its particle flow has H>0.

B. EXACT RUELLE/PESIN HYPOTHESIS CHECK AND CONSEQUENCES

Ruelle 1978 primary PDF, Theorem 2(b): https://www.ihes.fr/~ruelle/PUBLICATIONS/%5B51%5D.pdf. For a C¹ map of a compact C∞ manifold and any invariant probability ρ, h_ρ(f)≤∫Σ_{λ_i(x)>0}m_i(x)λ_i(x)dρ. No ergodicity is required. For smooth nowhere-zero autonomous X, φ_1 meets those assumptions and the finite-dimensional exponents are χ,0,−χ, so the positive sum is χ. The normal-cocycle derivation gives Eχ≤Es. With zeros, the restriction μ|{X≠0}/α is invariant but need not be equivalent to ambient Riemannian volume; Ruelle remains the safe imported inequality there.

Pesin 1977 primary English PDF: https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=rm&option_lang=eng&paperid=3219&what=fullteng. The introduction (pp.55–56) says the normalized invariant measure is equivalent to Riemannian volume; Theorem 5.1 (p.81) assumes a C² measure-preserving diffeomorphism in this smooth-volume framework and gives equality h=integral of positive exponents. Therefore, for the nowhere-zero smooth candidate interface, a stronger import is available: H=Eχ, not just H≤Eχ. Ruelle is still more robust and safer for invariant restrictions/zero sets. Pesin equality's proof is not needed to verify the elementary strain/power factors, but its exact hypotheses must be stated if used for equality examples or rigidity.

C. PRIOR ART THAT SUBSTANTIALLY OVERLAPS THE EQUALITY-RIGIDITY CLAIM

Étienne Ghys, “Déformations de flots d’Anosov et de groupes fuchsiens,” Ann. Inst. Fourier 42 (1992), 209–247, primary article record and abstract:
https://www.numdam.org/item/AIF_1992__42_1-2_209_0/
The abstract says that it studies Anosov flows on compact 3-manifolds with C∞ weak stable/unstable distributions and classifies them when they preserve volume. The equality proof in the candidate yields an Anosov flow with smooth strong eigenline bundles and hence smooth weak bundles. This is a direct prior-art overlap for broad classification of the exact-equality flow. The paper's full classification was not reconstructed here; no precise claim-by-claim subsumption should be made until its theorem statements are compared.

Jacopo De Simoi, Martin Leguil, Kurt Vinhage, Yun Yang, “Entropy rigidity for 3D conservative Anosov flows and dispersing billiards,” arXiv:2003.09345v3, primary HTML:
https://arxiv.org/html/2003.09345
Their Theorem A assumes k≥5, a C^k Anosov flow on a compact connected 3-manifold preserving smooth volume, and proves h_top=h_volume iff C^{k−ε}-conjugate to an algebraic flow. Candidate equality gives C∞ uniform expansion/contraction at the exact rate H and a smooth invariant frame. Under the standard transitive-Anosov equilibrium-state formalism, the constant unstable Jacobian makes smooth volume the equilibrium state for a constant potential, so h_top=h_volume; hence Theorem A is a strong likely consequence of established rigidity. I have not fully replayed this reduction from the primary paper, so mark it as a compelling prior-art route rather than a proven subsumption. The candidate's local metric frame/bracket statement may be finer than conjugacy classification, but algebraic-flow rigidity itself is not a new consequence to claim.

Benoist–Foulon–Labourie 1992 primary abstract:
https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/articles/10.1090/S0894-0347-1992-1124979-1/
Their theorem is specifically about Anosov flows with smooth strong stable/unstable distributions AND a contact canonical 1-form, classifying them (up to finite cover and smooth time change) as geodesic flows of compact locally symmetric negative-curvature spaces. Do not apply it unqualified to the Sol suspension: contactness is not part of the baseline and is not established for Sol. It is adjacent prior art for the SL(2,R)/geodesic equality branch.

D. A TEST THAT SHOWS THE 3D TURBULENCE PAPER USES EXTRA TIME-DEPENDENCE ASSUMPTIONS

Biswas–Manoharan–Joy, “Topological entropy of stationary three-dimensional turbulence,” arXiv:2506.20973v3, primary HTML:
https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20973v3
The paper defines S by asymptotic material-line length growth (its Eqs. (1)–(2)); Eqs. (9)–(18) proceed through a log-normal/CLT approximation, a decorrelation-time choice, and stationary/ergodic Eulerian sampling. The text explicitly says that the material line must sample independent eigenvalues and angles over times much larger than τ. This is a statistical model with numerical validation, not a primary theorem that marginal stationarity alone yields the formula.

Exact process check: let a stationary process be uniform on the two-point periodic orbit of the shift, with factors f_0=2, f_1=1/2 alternating. The shift on this 2-cycle is ergodic with uniform measure. Its one-time marginals satisfy E log f=0 and E f²=17/8, but every length-m product returns to 1 for even m and has exponential rate 0 as m→∞. The Eq. (15)-type independent/lognormal marginal calculation would instead assign (1/2)E log f+(1/4)log E f²=(1/4)log(17/8)>0 (in units τ=1). Hence identical stationary/ergodic one-time distributions do not determine the product-growth rate; temporal dependence matters. This is not a counterexample to the paper's extra independent-sampling model or a claimed physical Navier–Stokes realization. It proves why that assumption is a genuine load-bearing boundary, not a consequence of stationarity alone. A tiny exact check is saved in check_correlated_products.py.

The paper also treats line-growth “topological entropy” as its practical observable. For a general volume-preserving 3D particle map, a line tracks at most the top stretching direction, whereas the Pesin volume entropy sums all positive exponents; these coincide in the candidate's autonomous 3D flow because its neutral flow-line exponent and incompressibility force χ,0,−χ, but not generically for a time-dependent 3D flow with two positive exponents. This is another reason its result is relevant prior art but not the same theorem.

E. DIFFUSION / POWER CONVENTIONS AND HYDRODYNAMIC ENTROPY

Samavaki–Tuomela, “Navier–Stokes equations on Riemannian manifolds,” arXiv:1812.09015v2, primary HTML https://arxiv.org/html/1812.09015v2. They define the unhalved deformation tensor S_u=∇u+(∇u)^* and L u=div(S_u); their viscous term −ν L is exactly −2ν div Def under Def=(∇u+(∇u)^*)/2. Thus the factor 2 in candidate's PDE is a normalization match. The source separately derives how L differs from Bochner/Hodge Laplacians by curvature terms on manifolds, so these operators cannot be interchanged without changing W.

Hoeijmakers et al., PRE 81, 066302 (2010), APS primary abstract https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.81.066302, studies “global hydrodynamic entropy” ⟨s⟩ and viscous dissipation ⟨ε⟩ in a controlled isothermal vortex-array model. This is thermodynamic/control entropy, not KS entropy of the particle map; only the abstract was available here.
