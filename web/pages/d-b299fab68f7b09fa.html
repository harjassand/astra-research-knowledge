# Dynamic catalysis CYCLE2 report

## Verdict and highest-ceiling line

The exact finite-horizon identity and the zero-current result for fixed barriers are already contained in the Rahav–Horowitz–Jarzynski (RHJ) stochastic-pump framework. The cycle-space formula below is a Laplacian/Hodge representation of their integrated-flow identity, not a new no-pumping theorem.

The strongest defensible extension from this track is a quantitative actuator-allocation law for small barrier changes around a prescribed periodic well waveform, with an explicit (O(\eta^2)) remainder under a measurable mixing-gap assumption. A symmetric three-state Arrhenius ring supplies a second, fully specified witness: its high-frequency driven current has a closed-form dependence on well and barrier amplitudes, it exactly saturates the established equal-traffic bound for the averaged unicycle, and its well-energy work per internal ring completion diverges as the barrier swing goes to zero. None of this prices the physical barrier gate, clock, or measurement apparatus, and the internal ring current is not a product yield without an explicit chemical stoichiometry and chemostat model.

The high-ceiling next target is therefore not another closed-network no-go result. It is a physically calibrated minimum-work schedule for a specified catalytic network with chemostat affinities, measured actuator power, and a static fuel-driven control matched on activity and yield. The pointwise leakage ceiling is not shown attainable by admissible periodic dynamics; its control-theoretic sharpness remains open.

This report has three distinct models in view:

## Rate convention, units, and graph geometry

Let the connected graph have (n) states and oriented edges (e=(i,j)). The incidence matrix (B\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}) has (+1) at the source (i) and (-1) at the target (j). Positive edge current is from (i) to (j), so the column probability vector obeys
\[
\dot p=-B j,\qquad B\Phi=p(0)-p(T),\quad \Phi_e=\int_0^T j_e(t)\,dt.
\]

For a frozen detailed-balance Arrhenius edge with symmetric transition-state energy (B_e(t)), state well energy (E_i(t)), inverse thermal energy \(\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}\), and attempt frequency \(\nu_e\) in s\(^{-1}\), use
\[
 k_{i\to j}(t)=\nu_e e^{-\beta(B_e(t)-E_i(t))}=c_e(t)g_i(t),\quad
 k_{j\to i}(t)=c_e(t)g_j(t),
\]
where \(c_e(t)=\nu_e e^{-\beta B_e(t)}\) has units s\(^{-1}\), \(g_i(t)=e^{\beta E_i(t)}\) is dimensionless, \(q_i(t)=p_i(t)g_i(t)\), and \(j_e(t)=c_e(t)(q_i-q_j)\). No normalization such as \(\sum_i g_i=1\) is imposed. A common energy-reference shift rescales \(g\) and \(c\) inversely, leaving rates and the bounds invariant.

For fixed baseline barriers \(c_e^0>0\), let \(C_0=\operatorname{diag}(c_e^0)\), \(L_0=BC_0B^\top\), and assume the graph is connected. \(R_C=\sum_{e\in C}1/c_e^0\) is the cycle resistance for a simple ring; it has units seconds. Integrated edge flux \(\Phi_e\) and per-site cycle flux per period are dimensionless counts; dividing by protocol time gives s\(^{-1}\) per catalytic site.

## RHJ prior art and finite-cycle endpoint identity

RHJ Eq. 4 gives the integrated current on an edge as a stationary contribution plus a redistribution term,
\[
\phi_{mn}=\int_0^T\left[J^s_{mn}(\lambda(t))+V_{mn}(\lambda(t))\cdot\dot p(t)\right]dt.
\]
For detailed-balance Arrhenius rates, (J^s=0). RHJ Eq. 11a writes the integrated current as (\int V_{mn}(\lambda)\cdot\dot p\,dt), and Eq. 11b expresses (V) through the branching matrix. If barriers are fixed, that (V) is constant, so the integrated current depends only on (p(T)-p(0)). Their no-pumping theorem says a cyclic detailed-balance protocol produces zero integrated cycle current when only wells vary at fixed barriers; varying barriers alone also gives no pump. Both well and barrier modulation are required for a nonzero closed stochastic pump.

Here is the same finite-horizon identity in graph form. With fixed barriers, (j=C_0B^\top q), so
\[
\Phi=C_0B^\top Q,\quad Q=\int_0^Tq(t)dt,
\]
and node balance gives (L_0Q=p(0)-p(T)). Since (B^\top\mathbf 1=0),
\[
\boxed{\Phi=C_0B^\top L_0^\dagger[p(0)-p(T)]}. \tag{1}
\]
The dagger is the Moore–Penrose inverse, acting componentwise on the zero-sum endpoint difference. This holds for a nonperiodic finite protocol as well as a periodic one. In a periodic state (p(T)=p(0)), every edge has zero integrated flux. Equation (1) is equivalent, up to edge-orientation sign and RHJ gauge, to their fixed-barrier Eq. 4/11; it is not a new identity or priority claim.

This representation assumes a state-potential flow (j=C_0B^\top q). It does not cover a nonzero chemostat cycle affinity. With fuel/product reservoirs, (J^s) in RHJ Eq. 4 can be nonzero even at fixed parameters, and total turnover is not constrained to vanish. The closed-system no-pumping statement applies only to the pumped/excess part after separating this baseline current.

## Exact cycle-space leakage identity and small-gate law

Write barrier actuation as (c_e(t)=c_e^0[1+\epsilon_e(t)]), with (1+\epsilon_e>0). For the *actual driven distribution* (p_\epsilon), put
\[
r_e=\int_0^T c_e^0\epsilon_e(t)(q_i^\epsilon-q_j^\epsilon)dt,
\quad P=I-C_0B^\top L_0^\dagger B.
\]
Decomposing the current into its fixed-conductance part plus the barrier perturbation gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\Phi=C_0B^\top L_0^\dagger[p(0)-p(T)]+Pr}. \tag{2}
\]
(P) is an idempotent projection onto the cycle space \(\ker B\), along the fixed-barrier cut-flow subspace. This is a Hodge/Laplacian form of the RHJ current, not a distinct no-pumping theorem.

For a periodic (N)-unicycle, all integrated edge currents equal the cycle current (J_C), and summing (\Phi_e/c_e^0) around the ring yields
\[
J_C=\frac{1}{R_C}\sum_{e\in C}\int_0^T\epsilon_e(t)(q_i^\epsilon-q_j^\epsilon)dt.
\]
Thus, if \(|\epsilon_e(t)|\le\eta\),
\[
|J_C|\le\frac{\eta}{R_C}\int_0^T\sum_{e\in C}|q_i^\epsilon-q_j^\epsilon|dt
\le\frac{2\eta g_{\max}T}{R_C},\qquad g_{\max}=\max_{i,t}g_i(t). \tag{3}
\]
The second inequality uses \(q_i\ge0\), \(\sum_i q_i=\sum_i p_i g_i\le g_{\max}\), and the cycle-graph inequality \(\sum_e|q_i-q_j|\le2(\max_iq_i-\min_iq_i)\le2g_{\max}\). For a three-edge equal-conductance triangle, \(R_C=3/c_0\), so the rate bound is \(|J_C|/T\le (2/3)c_0\eta g_{\max}\). Here \(T\) denotes the protocol horizon in seconds; physical traffic below is denoted \(\mathcal T\). Units are consistent: \(R_C\) is seconds and \(J_C/T\) is s\(^{-1}\) per site.

For a fixed periodic well waveform with baseline periodic state (p_0(t)), define (D_eq^0=q_i^0-q_j^0). The linear response functional is
\[
J_{\rm lin}(\epsilon)=R_C^{-1}\int_0^T\sum_e\epsilon_e(t)D_eq^0(t)dt.
\]
If each edge can be modulated independently with signed bound \(|\epsilon_e|\le\eta\) and there are no slew-rate/bandwidth restrictions, its exact linear optimum for the chosen direction is attained by the feed-forward waveform
\[
\epsilon_e(t)=\eta\,\operatorname{sign}(D_eq^0(t)),\quad
J_{\rm lin}^{\max}=\frac{\eta}{R_C}\int_0^T\sum_e|D_eq^0(t)|dt. \tag{4}
\]
This is a first-order actuator-allocation rule. It is not the nonlinear optimum; its feasibility as a low-cost physical gate requires actuator bandwidth and energy data.

A quantitative remainder follows under a measurable mixing assumption. Suppose the fixed-barrier baseline propagator (U_0(t,s)) satisfies
\[
\|U_0(t,s)x\|_1\le M e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|x\|_1
\quad\text{for all zero-sum }x,\ t\ge s,
\]
and let \(\Lambda_0=\sup_t\max_i\sum_{e\ni i}c_e^0g_i(t)\) be the maximum baseline escape rate. Since the perturbation generator has \(\ell_1\)-operator norm at most \(2\eta\Lambda_0\), periodic Duhamel response gives
\[
\|p_\epsilon(t)-p_0(t)\|_1\le 2M\eta\Lambda_0/\lambda.
\]
For a simple ring (degree two), this implies the explicit second-order control error
\[
\boxed{|J_C-J_{\rm lin}(\epsilon)|\le
\frac{4M g_{\max}\Lambda_0T}{R_C\lambda}\eta^2.} \tag{5}
\]
The mixing gap is an assumption to measure/certify; it fails to be uniform if the baseline schedule lets rates approach zero without a separate contraction bound.

The state-independent bound (3) is a pointwise capacity ceiling, not a demonstrated sharp periodic-control capacity. Instantaneous equality requires (q) concentrated on one node and zero on another. Periodic master-equation constraints couple that concentration to outgoing rates and re-preparation. No admissible periodic waveform approaching the full factor (2g_{\max}/R_C) was found here. The explicit fast rotating-well protocol below approaches one third of this pointwise ceiling as its well gap grows, because its fast-limit occupancy is uniform. Whether other well waveforms can do better is unresolved.

## Explicit three-state periodic witness

Use a ring oriented (0\to1\to2\to0), equal attempt frequencies \(\nu=1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), dimensionless well gap (A=\beta\Delta E=4), half-range barrier swing (d=\beta\Delta B=0.1), mean barrier (B_0=0.5k_BT), and equal phase duration \(\tau=0.02\,\mathrm{s}\) (period (3\tau=0.06\,\mathrm{s}), (f=16.667\,\mathrm{Hz})). The per-edge barrier peak-to-peak span is (2d k_BT=0.2k_BT). State energies and edge barriers are:

| phase | well energies \((E_0,E_1,E_2)/k_BT\) | barriers \((B_{01},B_{12},B_{20})/k_BT\) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | \((0,-A,-A)\) | \((B_0-d,B_0,B_0+d)\) |
| 1 | \((-A,0,-A)\) | \((B_0+d,B_0-d,B_0)\) |
| 2 | \((-A,-A,0)\) | \((B_0,B_0+d,B_0-d)\) |

Numerically these barriers are ((0.4,0.5,0.6)), ((0.6,0.4,0.5)), ((0.5,0.6,0.4)k_BT). Every transition state is above the highest well, all rates are positive, and each frozen phase is detailed balanced.

For compact phase rate matrices let (c_0=\nu e^{-B_0}=0.6065306597\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}), (q=e^{-A}), (x=e^d), (y=e^{-d}). List edge rates as forward/reverse in orientation (01,12,20), normalized by (c_0):

| phase | (01:(k^+,k^-)/c_0) | (12:(k^+,k^-)/c_0) | (20:(k^+,k^-)/c_0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | \((x,xq)\) | \((q,q)\) | \((yq,y)\) |
| 1 | \((yq,y)\) | \((x,xq)\) | \((q,q)\) |
| 2 | \((q,q)\) | \((yq,y)\) | \((x,xq)\) |

These are (k_{i\to j}=\nu e^{-\beta(B_e-E_i)}), (k_{j\to i}=\nu e^{-\beta(B_e-E_j)}). With a column generator (Q_r) satisfying \(\dot p=Q_rp\), the exact matrices are
\[
Q_0=c_0\begin{pmatrix}
-(x+y)&xq&yq\\
x&-q(x+1)&q\\
y&q&-q(y+1)
\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q_1=c_0\begin{pmatrix}
-q(y+1)&y&q\\
yq&-(y+x)&xq\\
q&x&-q(x+1)
\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q_2=c_0\begin{pmatrix}
-q(x+1)&q&x\\
q&-q(y+1)&y\\
xq&yq&-(x+y)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each column sums to zero. Ratios of the two rates on an edge equal (e^{\beta(E_i-E_j)}), verifying detailed balance separately in each phase.

In the fast-switching limit, the Floquet generator is the phase average \(\bar Q=(Q_0+Q_1+Q_2)/3\). It has equal clockwise and reverse rates on every edge,
\[
u=\frac{c_0}{3}\left[e^d+e^{-A}(1+e^{-d})\right],\qquad
w=\frac{c_0}{3}\left[e^{-d}+e^{-A}(1+e^d)\right]. \tag{6}
\]
Its stationary distribution is uniform, so the per-edge cycle current and physical jump traffic are
\[
J_\infty=\frac{u-w}{3}=\frac{2c_0}{9}(1-e^{-A})\sinh d,\qquad
\mathcal T_\infty=u+w. \tag{7}
\]
At \(A=4,d=0.1,B_0=0.5\), these are \(u=0.2304936261\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(w=0.1907326575\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(\mathcal T_\infty=0.4212262836\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) per site, \(J_\infty=0.01325365620\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) per site, and apparent cycle affinity \(\mathcal A_{\rm eff}=3\ln(u/w)=0.5680513828\).

Yang–Dill’s 2026 equal-traffic principle for an N-unicycle states \(J\le (\mathcal T/N)\tanh(\mathcal F_{\rm cycle}/N)\), where \(\mathcal T=\sum_e\tau_e\) is total *physical edge traffic* and \(\mathcal F_{\rm cycle}=\mathcal A/2\) under the standard cycle-affinity convention. Thus \(J\le (\mathcal T/N)\tanh(\mathcal A/(2N))\). Equation (6) has equal traffic on all three edges and saturates this bound exactly:
\[
J_\infty=(\mathcal T_\infty/3)\tanh(\mathcal A_{\rm eff}/6).
\]
This is equality for the averaged CTMC, not an equality between physical control work and chemical work in the driven process. If instead \(K=\sum_i(k_i^++k_i^-)\) denotes the *unweighted bare rate-constant sum*, then here \(K=3\mathcal T_\infty\); \(K/N^2\tanh(\mathcal A/(2N))\) is an algebraic rewrite only because the stationary distribution is uniform. Do not identify \(K\) with traffic in a nonuniform system.

## Work law and what it does not price

For instantaneous well-energy jumps, the state-well work per site per period is
\[
W_{\rm well}=\sum_{\rm switches}\sum_i p_i(t_r^-)\Delta E_i(t_r).
\]
For the three-state protocol, let (a=c_0(1-e^{-A})/3). The first-order phase drifts at uniform occupancy are (v_0=a[-(x+y),x,y]), (v_1=a[y,-(x+y),x]), and (v_2=a[x,y,-(x+y)]). Expanding the periodic state at phase boundaries gives
\[
\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\frac{W_{\rm well}}{3\tau}
=\frac{2c_0A}{3}(1-e^{-A})\cosh d.
\]
Together with (7), this yields the model-specific fast-limit ratio
\[
\boxed{\lim_{\tau\downarrow0}\frac{W_{\rm well}}{J_C}
=3A\coth(d)\;k_BT\quad\text{per internal ring completion}.} \tag{8}
\]
This is not a general catalytic law. It follows for this symmetric three-state, equal-dwell rotating-well/traveling-barrier construction in the fast-switching limit; finite-period corrections are nonzero. At (d\to0), the output current vanishes linearly while the well-work rate remains finite, so this component’s work per internal completion diverges like (3A/d\,k_BT).

The coarse-grained Arrhenius model contains no barrier-controller coordinate or transition-state population. It therefore does not determine the work needed to move (B_e(t)), clock/controller dissipation, or measurement energy. Equation (8) is strictly the state-well work implied by the specified jump model. The physical total work must be measured or obtained after adding an explicit gate Hamiltonian/controller, e.g. electrical input \(\int V I\,dt\) with nonfaradaic blank subtraction, mechanical force–strain work, or absorbed optical energy. Frequency alone is not energy. Canavan et al. explicitly separate their turnover-efficiency metric from net energy consumption and say the latter depends on whether the perturbation is light, charge, strain, and its efficiency.

## Dynamic catalysis literature and mechanism attack

- RHJ 2008 is the relevant prior art for finite integrated flows and the no-pumping theorem. Its Eq. 4 includes a stationary-current term; Eq. 11a/11b reduce the detailed-balance current to a branching/barrier-dependent line integral. A catalytic cycle with chemostat affinity must keep the stationary term and cannot be called a closed stochastic pump.
- Foley & Razdan, *iScience* 27 (2024), 109543, derive two mechanisms—high-frequency “quasi-static” averaging and intermediate-frequency “stepwise” programming—and a didactic \(A+*\to I*\to B+*\) coverage ODE. Their square-wave programs sequence adsorption and conversion/desorption states; their CO-oxidation/Pt(111) rate enhancement of about 20-fold versus a static Sabatier maximum is a model prediction under specified scaling relations and waveform, not a universal theorem. The source emphasizes that programs need full state energies and dwell times; amplitude/frequency alone need not identify mechanism.
- Murphy et al., *Journal of Catalysis* 430 (2024), 115343, model catalytic loops where a two-state catalyst perturbation changes adsorbate binding energies and activation energies through BEP/linear-scaling relations. A 3-state A*↔B*↔C* loop was simulated at 3.3 Hz with a specified square-wave sequence; their predicted loop TOF is based on a surface limit cycle, and leakage/reverse loop segments occur. This is closer to product catalysis than the closed pump, but it remains a model with imposed rate/energy maps.
- Canavan et al., *ACS Catalysis* 15 (2025), 653–663, define turnover efficiency relative to applied oscillation frequency and analyze a leaky ratchet and low-surface-participation regime. Their paper explicitly notes that turnover efficiency/effective-rate metrics do not themselves account for net energy consumption; actuator type and efficiency matter. This confirms that frequency-based TOE is not joules-per-product.
- Yang & Dill, *Nature Communications* 17 (2026), article 4980, establish the equal-traffic principle for unicycles. The equality reached here is their uniform-traffic equality applied to the averaged generator, not a new thermodynamic bound.
- Barato & Seifert, *New Journal of Physics* 19 (2017), 073021, explicitly model the controller as a thermodynamic system and show that the cost of generating external control can differ from work delivered to the driven machine. A physical energy claim requires that controller/gate model.

## Measurable follow-up and decisive discriminator

A chemistry-level experiment must first identify a catalytic network and its stoichiometry, reservoirs, and state/transition-state response to the control variable. Calibrate \(E_i(t)\), \(B_e(t)\), forward/reverse rate constants, reactant activities, product chemical potential, and the fixed-condition best static control at the same mean surface condition and temperature. Then test a three-state square wave with measured dwell times and phase amplitudes; collect surface coverages and bidirectional elementary-step fluxes over full limit cycles, and quantify gas-phase product independently by calibrated GC/MS or equivalent. Record actual actuator work in the same runs (electrical \(\int VI\,dt\), force–strain work, or absorbed optical energy), including nonfaradaic/blank and controller baselines.

The minimum matched controls are: (i) fixed barriers with the same well waveform, (ii) fixed wells with the same barrier waveform, (iii) the mirrored barrier–well phase relation, (iv) phase-order reversal, (v) the best static Sabatier condition under identical feed/temperature and, when chemical affinity is allowed, (vi) a static fuel-driven unicycle matched on measured traffic and product current. Predicted signatures of the closed pump are zero cycle current under either one-parameter-only control, a sign reversal when the relative barrier wave is mirrored, and edge-integrated flux equality at a periodic limit cycle. A failure of these signatures falsifies the fitted pump mechanism; observing internal ring circulation without stoichiometric product release does not establish catalysis. The decisive practical outcome is net product per *measured total actuator plus chemical input energy*, not TOF divided by drive frequency.

5. Equal-traffic saturation: established external theorem applied to the averaged generator.
6. Full pointwise (2g_{\max}/R_C) capacity attainability by an admissible periodic (p(t)): UNKNOWN. Current construction attains only one third asymptotically in the large-well-gap fast-limit family. Need solve the periodic optimal-control problem or prove an additional dynamic constraint.
7. Physical barrier actuator, clock, and measurement energy; mapping ring current to stoichiometric product flux; robustness to finite reservoirs, site interactions, mass transport, and catalyst fatigue: UNKNOWN.
