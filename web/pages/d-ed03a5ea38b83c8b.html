c02_s03 — finite-bit symmetric Dicke coherent compiler
7 October 2026 UTC. Internally derived linear-algebra construction and bit proof.
No CAD, real quantifier elimination, or free exact-transcendental oracle.

TARGET / OUTPUT
Input: n>=1, rational 0<=delta<=1/4, rational h (arbitrary binary magnitude),
and rational 0<epsilon<=1; L includes rational input bit lengths. Target
rho=sum_k a_k |D_n,k><D_n,k| / sum_k a_k,
a_k=exp[-delta(k-n/2)^2/n+h(k-n/2)].
Output: at most (floor(n/2)+1)(n+1)=O(n^2) exact pure iid product descriptors,
nonnegative rational weights summing to1, approximating rho in trace distance
D=(1/2)||rho-rhohat||_1<=epsilon. Common one-qubit factors can be described by
rational p in[0,1], sqrt(p), sqrt(1-p), and zeta^(j), where zeta is the exact
(n+1)th root of unity. These are finite algebraic descriptors of polynomial
size. This is a finite-bit mixture compiler, not a dense 2^n state-vector output,
hardware preparation, relative accuracy on rare populations, or a benchmark
claim about practical constants. Its bit work is polynomial in n,L,log(1/epsilon).

BACKGROUND USED
c07_s02's density construction, freshly reconstructed in
axial/INDEPENDENT_CONTOUR_AUDIT.txt, proves normalized coherent density f_delta
has a strictly positive lower bound for all n>=2, delta<=1/4. The node/weight
estimates below were suggested by root and reconstructed here. Everything
else is finite matrix algebra, rational arithmetic, Gaussian Taylor bounds,
and an explicit gap-free rational Jacobi diagonalization.

1. LARGE BINARY FIELD REPAIR
A precision requirement proportional to |h| would be EXPONENTIAL in the
binary bit length when h=2^L. It is not an acceptable bit bound.
Let B=4+ceil(log2(1/epsilon)), an exactly computable integer. If |h|>=B,
return the pure extremal all-up/down state. For h>=0 and j>=1,
a_(n-j)/a_n=exp[-hj+delta j(1-j/n)]<=exp[-j(h-delta)].
Thus the total off-extremal population is at most q/(1-q), q=e^(-h+delta).
Because delta<=1/4, e^(15/4)>8 and log2>=log, q<=epsilon/8, so this trace
distance is <=epsilon/7<epsilon/4. The negative-field case is reflection.
This branch needs no evaluation of exp(h). In the remaining branch |h|<B,
so n|h| is polynomial in n and log(1/epsilon).
The stronger exact threshold |h|>=delta+log(2/epsilon) also works, but the
integer threshold avoids an additional logarithm evaluation.

2. EXPLICIT NORMALIZED DENSITY FLOOR
For n>=2, the audited contour/correction density satisfies
P_delta(p)>=(n+1)(27/64) exp[-delta(n+2)^2/(4n)].
Apply the local filter diag(e^-h/4,e^h/4). Write
a(p)=e^-h/2(1-p)+e^h/2 p, y=e^h/2 p/a(p), dy/dp=1/a(p)^2.
The pushed UNNORMALIZED density is P_delta(p)a(p)^(n+2). Its normalizer is
at most (n+1)e^(n|h|/2). Consequently the normalized field density obeys
f_delta,h(y)>=(27/64)exp[-delta(n+2)^2/(4n)-(n+1)|h|]
 >=(27/64)exp[-delta n-(n+1)B].
The inequality (n+2)^2/(4n)<=n holds for n>=2. A convenient rational floor is
c=2^-C, C=2+ceil(n/2)+2(n+1)B,
since delta<=1/4 and e^-x>=2^-2x. All log(1/c) costs are now polynomial.
The n1 case needs only one node p=u1 and two phase labels and can be handled
separately; it is never a large moment system.

3. ACQUIRED ORDINARY MOMENTS, NOT q_k
Let pi_k=a_k/sum_j a_j. The ordinary p-moments are
u_l=sum_{k>=l} [C(k,l)/C(n,l)] pi_k, 0<=l<=n.
This follows from C(n,k)C(k,l)=C(n,l)C(n-l,k-l) and the binomial theorem.
In particular u0=1 and all summands are nonnegative. It is NOT the incorrect
ordinary-moment interpretation of q_k=pi_k/C(n,k).

These moments are acquired by charged rational intervals for finite exponential
sums. Subtract the largest rational exponent r_k from all r_k. Then the needed
exp(-x_k) have 0<=x_k<=n(B+1). For x>=0, use the rational Taylor sum
S_T=sum_{j=0}^T x^j/j! and tail bound
0<=e^x-S_T<=2 x^(T+1)/(T+1)! when T+2>=2x.
Taking T>=6x+P+4 yields tail <=2^-P using j!>=(j/e)^j and e<3.
Thus e^-x lies in [1/(S_T+2next),1/S_T] with width<=2^-P.
Positive sums and denominator intervals with lower bound>=1 give u_l intervals
of width at most 2(n+1)^2*2^-P. Choose P accordingly and round their midpoints
to dyadic rationals with error<=zeta, retaining u0=1 exactly.
The exponent size, Taylor degree, coefficient bit lengths, and moment work are
polynomial in n,L,P. No tiny target probability is acquired relatively.

4. EXPLICIT GRAM CONDITIONING
Let d=floor(n/2)+1 and H=(u_i+j), 0<=i,j<d. Since f>=c on[0,1],
H>=c A0(d), where A_r(d)[i,j]=1/(i+j+r+1).
The Cauchy determinant is
 det A_r = product_{i<j}(j-i)^2 / product_{i,j}(i+j+r+1).
For r0,1,2, lambda_max<=d and
lambda_min A_r >= (4d)^-(d^2+d).
For the (1-p) weighted Gram, restrict its integral to[0,1/2]:
 integral (1-p)v(p)v(p)^T dp >=2^-2d lambda_min(A0) I.
A common rational lower bound for H, the p-weighted Grams used below,
and the (1-p)-weighted Gram is
alpha=c 2^-2d (4d)^-(d^2+d)>0.
Its reciprocal is large numerically, but its BIT LENGTH is
O(n log(1/epsilon)+n^2 log(n+1)), which is the relevant cost.

5. EXACT GAUSSIAN MATRIX FOR ODD n
For n=2m+1, d=m+1, set K=(u_i+j+1), i,j0..m.
Both K and H-K are positive definite with the bounds above. Let H=LL^T
be the positive-diagonal Cholesky factorization and
J=L^-1 K L^-T. Then 0<J<I. Since u0=1, L^T e0=e0.
For j<m, K e_j=H e_(j+1), so
J^j e0=L^T e_j for 0<=j<=m.
Therefore e0^T J^ell e0=u_ell for ell<=2m, by splitting ell=i+j with
0<=i,j<=m. For the remaining odd moment,
e0^T J^(2m+1)e0=(L^T e_m)^T J(L^T e_m)=u_(2m+1).
The Krylov vectors e0,J e0,...,J^m e0 span R^d. Hence J has d distinct
eigenvalues in(0,1), every eigenvector has a nonzero first component, and
its spectral measure at e0 gives positive Gaussian quadrature weights.
This proves the needed exactness without an imported orthogonal-polynomial
or algebraic-root-acquisition oracle.

6. EXACT RADAU MATRIX FOR EVEN n, ENDPOINT0
For n=2m, d=m+1, H again uses moments through2m. The shifted matrix K has
only one unknown entry, K_mm=u_(2m+1). Let A=(u_i+j+1)_{i,j<m},
v_i=u_(i+m+1), and set K_mm=v^T A^-1 v. All inputs here are known.
Then K=[[A,v],[v^T,v^T A^-1 v]]>=0 has rank m, hence one zero eigenvalue.
To prove H-K>0, let K_true be the matrix from the EXISTING positive density's
next moment. Its Schur complement is nonnegative, so the true last entry is
at least v^T A^-1 v. Thus H-K >= H-K_true >=alpha I.
The same J=L^-1KL^-T and Krylov identity show spectral exactness through2m.
It has one node0 and m distinct nodes in(0,1), all with positive weights.
This is the Gauss-Radau rule with LEFT endpoint0. No division by a small node,
negative endpoint residual, or unknown moment acquisition is needed.
For n2, this recovers nodes0,u2/u1 and weights1-u1^2/u2,u1^2/u2.

7. QUADRATURE WEIGHT AND NODE-GAP BOUNDS
For either rule, let ell_i be the cardinal polynomial of degree m at its d
nodes. Quadrature is exact on ell_i^2, so
w_i=integral ell_i(p)^2 f(p)dp >=c integral ell_i^2.
The shifted Legendre evaluation kernel satisfies
sum_l=0^m (2l+1)R_l(p)^2<=(m+1)^2=d^2.
Since ell_i(x_i)=1, this implies w_i>=c/d^2.
Also integral ell_i^2<=1/c, so ||ell_i||_infinity<=d/sqrt(c).
Markov's inequality on[0,1] gives ||ell_i'||<=2m^2 d/sqrt(c).
Because ell_i(x_i)-ell_i(x_j)=1, each pair of nodes is separated by
 g>=sqrt(c)/(2m^2 d)>=sqrt(c)/(2d^3), for m>=1.
These constants were suggested by root and independently reconstructed.
The m0 Gaussian case is explicit. Radau's endpoint0 is covered by the same
gap argument. These bounds can be used for a practical eigensolver; the
construction below also proves termination without gap-dependent convergence.

8. MATRIX ACQUISITION WITH CHARGED ERRORS
Let each acquired moment have absolute error<=zeta. Matrix entry errors give
||Hhat-H||<=d zeta. When d zeta<=alpha/2, Hhat>=alpha I/2.
For odd n, ||Khat-K||<=d zeta. For even n, complete Khat by its own exact
Schur formula; ||Khat-K||<=10 d^2 zeta/alpha^2. This follows from
||Ahat^-1||<=2/alpha, ||v||<=sqrt(d), ||vhat-v||<=sqrt(d)zeta, and the
inverse perturbation identity. Khat is EXACTLY PSD by construction.
Taking zeta<=alpha^3/(20d^2) also preserves Hhat-Khat>0.

The Cholesky factor varies smoothly on this positive-definite segment. To
bound its perturbation, differentiate H(t)=L(t)L(t)^T. With
E=L^-1 H' L^-T and B=L^-1 L', E=B+B^T, B lower triangular.
Thus ||B||_F<=||E||_F<=2||H'||_F/alpha and
||L'||_F<=2sqrt(2d)||H'||_F/alpha. Integrating gives
||chol(Hhat)-L||_F<=3 d^(3/2)zeta/alpha.
Compute a rational lower-triangular Lr within Frobenius error e of chol(Hhat)
by interval square roots/divisions; its positive pivots are >=sqrt(alpha/2).
The finite Cholesky recursion has polynomially many operations, denominators
bounded below, and amplification at most (32d/alpha)^(2d^2), a sufficient
crude bound. Thus polynomial extra working bits suffice, with no free exact
square roots. Retain its first row (1,0,...) exactly.

Let Jr=Lr^-1 Khat Lr^-T, computed in exact rational arithmetic. If
zeta<=tau alpha^3/(256d^3) and e<=tau alpha^2/(32d), tau<=1, then
||Jr-J||<=64d^3 zeta/alpha^3+8d e/alpha^2<=tau/2.
Here ||L^-1||<=alpha^-1/2 and the inverse perturbation identity give the bound.
Jr is exactly PSD. Rescale M=Jr/(1+tau); then 0<=M<I and
||M-J||<=3tau/2. For even n its rank remains m exactly.
All inversions use rational elimination and have polynomial bit complexity
(minor/determinant bounds control reduced numerator and denominator lengths).

9. FINITE RATIONAL ORTHOGONAL DIAGONALIZATION, NO EIGENVALUE-GAP ORACLE
This core is actually implemented in rational_jacobi.py with EXACT descent
checks and dyadic square-root intervals, without a floating eigensolve.
Maintain an exact rational orthogonal Q (initially I), T=Q^T M Q.
If its squared off-diagonal Frobenius norm exceeds tau^2, choose the largest
absolute off-diagonal pivot b=T_pq. For a=T_pp,d=T_qq, an ideal real Jacobi
rotation has tan(theta)=sign(z)/(|z|+sqrt(1+z^2)), z=(d-a)/(2b), with
an explicit theta=pi/4 choice when z0. Its unit-circle parameter is
t=tan(theta/2)=tan(theta)/(1+sqrt(1+tan(theta)^2)).
Acquire these square roots by integer isqrt: floor(sqrt(x)*2^P)/2^P and
its next dyadic bound enclose sqrt(x) EXACTLY. Round t to a rational dyadic,
then set c=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2), s=2t/(1+t^2). This rational rotation is exactly
orthogonal regardless of how close it is to the ideal angle.

Its new pivot is bnew=cs(a-d)+(c^2-s^2)b. Accept it only if the exact rational
inequality 2*bnew^2<=b^2 holds; otherwise double scalar precision. The entire
off-diagonal norm squared then falls by at least b^2, since the other paired
entries preserve their squared sums. With M_pairs=d(d-1)/2, max-pivot b^2
is at least offnorm^2/(2M_pairs). Thus the acceptance certificate itself
guarantees contraction by 1-1/(2M_pairs) and termination after
O(M_pairs log(d/tau)) rotations. It is not inferred from a numerical solver.
Before stopping, |b|>=tau/sqrt(2M_pairs). Also ||M||<=1 and |a-d|<=1.
The scalar formulas have positive denominators>=1 or>=2, and their error is
O(2^-P); precision O(log(d/tau)) therefore makes the acceptance test pass.
This supplies a polynomial bound on refinement, without dividing by a node gap.
The code's initial scalar precision is deliberately larger and its exact
acceptance check protects the actual output. Rational matrix products grow in
bit length at most additively over the polynomial number of rotations.

Take nodes x_i=T_ii and weights w_i=Q_0i^2. They are rational, x_i in[0,1],
w_i>=0, sum_i w_i=1 exactly. For even n, the smallest diagonal is <=tau by
Weyl/min-max since M has eigenvalue0; replace that node by EXACTLY0. This
changes the represented symmetric matrix by at most tau. Thus its matrix
Jout=Q diag(x_i)Q^T differs from the exact quadrature J by <=4tau.

For strictly positive weights and distinct interior nodes impose additionally
 tau<=c/(1024d^4).
The true gap bound and a direct spectral residual argument associate each
Q column to its ordered true eigenvector: its perpendicular norm is bounded
by twice ||Jout-J||/g, and after choosing sign its first-component error is
at most 12*||Jout-J||/g <=48tau/g. Since the true first-component magnitude
is >=sqrt(c)/d, this cap gives w_i>=c/(4d^2), node gaps remain >=g/2, and
only the prescribed Radau node is zero. No eigenvector sign needs to be
chosen in the algorithm because its weights are squared.
The optional cap costs polynomial extra bits. Nonnegative weights alone
already suffice for a positive state. Endpoint clipping is harmless in the
alternative numerical pipeline because coherent states are Holder continuous
with D(|phi(p)>^n,|phi(q)>^n)<=sqrt(n|p-q|).

10. ERROR TO THE STATE AND PHASE LABELS
For contractions J,Jout,
|e0^T(Jout^l-J^l)e0|<=l||Jout-J||<=4l tau
by power telescoping. Converting moments to the Dicke population,
B_n,k(p)=C(n,k)sum_r=0^(n-k)(-1)^r C(n-k,r) p^(k+r).
The sum of absolute coefficients over all k is 3^n. Hence the reconstructed
population L1 error is <=4n3^n tau. The trace distance of the two diagonal
Dicke states is at most 2n3^n tau. Choose
 tau<=epsilon/(16n3^n)
and the optional positive-weight cap above. This yields D<=epsilon/8 in the
nontrivial branch, leaving slack for any desired additional output rounding.
All log(1/tau) and moment/factor/rotation working bit counts are polynomial.
No rare-population relative error is claimed.

For each node use the n+1 phases zeta^j, j0..n, each with weight w_i/(n+1).
The sum kills every off-diagonal Dicke entry exactly: |k-l|<=n, so only k=l
is divisible by n+1. All descriptors are pure iid products and all weights
are nonnegative rational numbers summing to1. The exact algebraic phase
encoding avoids an uncharged floating phase error; evaluating it to any
requested additional amplitude accuracy is itself polynomial in n and bits.
