STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE RESULT

Let sigma_B be any fixed normal state on a separable Hilbert space, and
let C:B->B tensor B be an actual normal full-domain CPTP broadcaster.
There is ONE normal full-domain entanglement-breaking (EB) channel Lambda_B
preserving sigma_B, chosen only from C and sigma_B, with this property:
for EVERY separable quantum reference A and normal rho_AB with
rho_B=sigma_B and finite mutual information
 I=D(rho_AB || rho_A tensor sigma_B),
define the actual joint marginal error
 b=max_j T((id_A tensor C_j)(rho_AB),rho_AB).
Then, for 0<b<1,
 e=T((id_A tensor Lambda_B)(rho_AB),rho_AB)
   <= min{1, 6 sqrt[(I+1)/ln(1/b)]}.
The SAME Lambda_B fixes every b=0 extension exactly, even when I is infinite.
At b=1 use the trivial upper bound 1. T is HALF trace norm; logs are natural.

No dimension, minimum-eigenvalue, Hamiltonian, or finite-entropy assumption
on sigma_B is required. Infinite-dimensional EB means outputs belong to
the trace-norm-closed convex hull of normal product states; an exact finite
or countable measurement alphabet is not asserted. In finite dimensions,
the map has a physical measure-and-prepare construction. The theorem is
an existence result, not an efficient acquisition or tomography algorithm.

This controls joint trace distance while leaving A untouched. It therefore
gives proximity to separable states and approximate reconstruction through

bounded classical memory alphabet. A mediator's mutual information with A
is bounded by I(A:B), but its own entropy need not be small.
