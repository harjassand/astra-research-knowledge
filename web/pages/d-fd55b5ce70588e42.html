## Exact author claim

Theorem 1.1, OpenAI, dated September 23, 2026 in `build/main.tex`: there exists one finite universal coefficient C, for every dimension n, every centered Lebesgue log-concave probability density μ, and every a>0 satisfying

\[
\sup_{\|\theta\|=1}\mathbb E\exp(\langle X,\theta\rangle^2/a^2)\le2,
\]

such that `Ent_mu(f²) <= C a² ∫|∇f|² dmu` for every compact smooth real f. The support may be a convex body, the potential may be nonsmooth or infinite outside support, and no isotropy or positive curvature is assumed. Centering is a translation convention, not a new restriction on an uncentered target.

We use R for `Ent(f²) <= 2R ∫|∇f|²`, so the conclusion is R<=C a²/2. The Fisher convention is `I(p|mu)=∫|∇log(dp/dmu)|² dp`, giving `H(p|mu)<=R I/2`. Stationary diffusion has generator `Δ+∇log μ·∇` and Fisher entropy loss rate `-I`; common Gaussian convolution of variance r has generator Δ/2 and rate `-I/2`. Confusing these conventions introduces factors of two into the prediction and sampling statements.

For a centered Gaussian of largest variance v, the exact smallest a in this Orlicz convention is `a²=8v/3`; its R is v. A moment-generating bound `E exp(tZ)<=exp(vt²/2)` also implies this Orlicz bound by integrating `exp(√2 G Z/a)` over a scalar standard Gaussian G. An upper covariance bound alone supplies no such reverse implication.

## Proof dependency reconstruction

### Critical normalization and alternatives: Section 3

Assume the ratio R/a² is unbounded. Compact truncation and a sufficiently weak convex quadratic penalty preserve an offending compact test quotient; the penalty gives a finite LSI constant. Dilation then makes R=1 and a→0. The centering cost is an absolute factor. This reduction does not assume compactness of all original measures or a support radius bounded across dimensions.

At fixed Gaussian variance s, posterior differentiation yields

\[
\nabla V_s=(y-b_s)/s,\quad M_s=D b_s=\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid y)/s,
\quad K_s=D^2V_s=(I-M_s)/s,\quad 0\preceq M_s\preceq I.
\]

The last bound comes from curvature of the Gaussian posterior, not from the original marginal subgaussianity. The continuity velocity ∇V_s/2 expands distances; its inverse transports the later heat law to the earlier one by a contraction. Thus R_s is nondecreasing, while product LSI gives R_s<=1+s. Compact Gaussian-tail form embedding and the entropy-defect bound isolate the two alternatives: a positive optimizer when R_s exceeds its Poincaré constant, or a gap eigenfunction when the constants agree.

At an optimizer, changing reference from μ_s to η=f²μ_s preserves the exact Fisher-minus-entropy deficit. It gives R(η)<=1/λ and, for w=∇log f²,

\[
A_\eta w=(\lambda I-K_s)w,\quad I=\mathbb E_\eta|w|^2=2\lambda H(\eta\mid\mu_s).
\]

In the gap branch η=μ_s and w=∇φ satisfy the same operator equation. Translation tests make the mean and curvature energies small using the original linear MGF bound. A two-sided contact calculation with transported optimizers or eigenfunctions identifies R_s'; this forces R_s→1. At s=1/8 the fields have negligible mean and `(A-1)w=o_L2(√I)`. Keeping both branches is essential; entropy attainment is not assumed at the Poincaré threshold.

### The new entropy-loss comparison: Lemma 4.2

The exact interface is: smooth positive p,q on R^d; q log-concave; finite H(p|q), finite original-score energy I, and one finite exponential-square moment under p. Set w=∇log(p/q) and `wbar_r=E_p[w(Y)|Y+√rG]`. The assertion is

\[
2[H(p\mid q)-H(p*\gamma_z\mid q*\gamma_z)]
\le\int_0^z\mathbb E_p|\bar w_r|^2\,dr.
\]

The predicted original score is generally not the positive-time relative score. The distinction is real; a direct heat-score substitution would not prove this lemma.

Independent reconstruction of its comparison: take N copies and first observe precision b=1/z. Add precision δ on the orthogonal complement of the difference between the two retained posterior means. The observed means then agree, so the additional output KL is O(δ²), uniformly over the mesh at fixed N and total extra precision u. The base posterior has matrix curvature
`B=bI+δ∑(I-P_i)` and unchanged relative score W. Its curvature LSI bounds the remaining KL by `E W* B^{-1} W/2`.

The matrix inverse is controlled through a Hilbert-space variational comparison. For e_i denoting conditional expectation on the **full** observation array, the minimizer of the relaxed quadratic functional has positive weights summing to one on nested projection increments. Its residual g_i is centered block by block conditional on the full past. The blocks remain independent because all full observation arrays were independently generated; the retained histories need not factor. If `Z_ij=tr Cov(g_ij|F_i)`, rank(P_i)<=1 gives

\[
\mathbb E|P_i g_i|^2\le\mathbb E\max_j Z_{ij}
\le K+2N\mathbb E[|w|^2\mathbf1_{|w|^2>K/2}].
\]

After division by N, infimizing over K makes the discarded-direction cost vanish. This uses only square integrability of the score and does not illicitly condition independent copies on their coupled retained histories. The relaxed minimum is a telescoping sum of unpredicted-score energies at precision b+iδ. Refining the mesh, then increasing N, then letting u→∞ yields exactly the asserted prediction integral. These limits are in that order; reversing the first two would require missing uniform exponential-moment bounds.

Closed-form Gaussian checks and a smooth non-log-concave counterexample are in the companion note. They confirm the factors and expose the necessity of q log-concavity.

### Tensors and matrices: Sections 4–6

The entropy comparison and efficient score probes squeeze the normalized prediction curve to `m(r)=e^{-r}` for each fixed 0<r<1/4. Its linearization supplies the gap branch. Gaussian Bessel orthogonality saturates at successive derivative levels; low-noise diagonal choices followed by spectral smoothing produce symmetric tensors T_l with norm²~I, `(A-1)T_l=o(√I)`, and `DT_l≈T_{l+1}`. No dimension factor is introduced by cross-covariance with coordinate tests: the adjoint map norm is bounded by the covariance operator norm, and coefficient squares are summed afterward.

For the strict branch the covariance-tail estimate permits operator-norm clipping before the differentiated weighted-divergence identity. It is insufficient merely to assume the formal product `(Dw)DT_l` is in L². The source constructs clipped, semigroup-smoothed tests P with `(Dw)P=o_L2(√I)`; this resolves that potential domain gap. The gap branch has Dw absent from the drift and does not need this tail step.

Average one-slot reductions over a long high-degree band and let ρ=ΨΨ*, R=√ρ. Curvature and deterministic-subspace exclusions give `||ER||_HS→0`. Poincaré forces square-root energy at least one. The decisive high-slot inequality (Lemma 6.2) bounds a two-slot correlation by `tr(RHRH)` through weighted operator/trace norm interpolation. Combining it with the Sylvester identity `D_iR=(H_iR+RH_i)/2` forces energy→1 and weighted commutators→0. This is a load-bearing place for a further proof gate; replacing its geometric weighting by arithmetic weighting would lose the sharpness.

### The information contradiction: Section 7

Use bounded covariance `I+f_L(Tρ)`, where `f_L(q)=q/(1+q/L)`. LSI bounds information by Gaussian likelihood Fisher energy. The entrywise divided-difference multiplier is bounded by 9 on comparable eigenvalues and by `4log(1+L)` elsewhere; the latter multiplies a vanishing commutator energy at fixed L. Thus the integrated information has limsup<=9/4.

An integrated scalar functional-calculus bound transfers the whole positive T axis to σ. The exact substitution `x=Tβ²` yields `dT/T²=β² dx/x²`, which matches the unnormalized spectral block weight. Polynomial tests of the conditional OU score, with the input-map bound, then give a scalar information lower bound with factor at most four. Polynomial approximation occurs on compact parameter rectangles after the sequence limit; axis exhaustion is afterward.

For scalar ℓ=exp(√(8t)n−4t), a Gaussian variance mixture detects rare large ℓ values. On `ℓ>=e^{2t}` and `h=xℓ∈[h0,t]`, a Gaussian threshold event has conditional probability bounded below and marginal probability exponentially small in h. Binary KL gives a lower bound proportional to h. Integrating yields `c E[ℓ 1_{ℓ>=e^{2t}}] log(t/h0)`, and the tilted-normal calculation gives `E[ℓ 1_{ℓ>=e^{2t}}]=Φ(√(t/2))>=1/2`. This diverges; t,L were fixed before every counterexample limit. A finite large t is chosen only at the end, so no uniform error rate in t is claimed.

The replay gives a sufficient coarse log t about 919.24 when the published constants 9/4 and 4 are used. This illustrates why the contradiction does not hand an implementer a useful numerical C; it is not a bound on the unknown LSI constant.
