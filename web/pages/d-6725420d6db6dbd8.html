CYCLE TWO THEOREM: A FAST-MIXING, WELL-OBSERVED K^2-PARAMETER PREDICTION LOWER BOUND
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the stated model and loss; UNKNOWN for novelty and external validation.

Question and conclusion
-----------------------
Can a raw-trajectory learner face a growing minimax acquisition cost even
when the finite-state source is rapidly mixing, every one-symbol context has
the same mass, and both transition and observation operators have constant
normalized singular values? Yes. A K-state family with K^2/4 independently
varying transition coordinates has expected one-step predictive total
variation risk of order K/sqrt(n) from one stationary raw path of n symbols.
The matching acquisition scale is K^2/epsilon^2. The result is a standard
parametric-dimensional obstruction made explicit under charged mixing,
history-mass, transition, emission, and observability margins; it is not a
rare-switch, one-path/de Bruijn, or latent-basis ambiguity construction.

The theorem applies directly to a finite-causal-state K-symbol Markov source
(the deterministic-emission endpoint below). A second endpoint has a
full-support K-state HMM observation channel with a one-symbol-context
forecasting task. In that noisy endpoint the source has finite Hankel rank K;
we do not claim that its set of posterior predictive distributions is finite.
The task is explicitly the conditional next-symbol law given the most recent
single symbol. For the deterministic-emission endpoint this is also the full
past-optimal prediction law.

Model, interface, and exact promises
------------------------------------
Let K=2m >= 2, index states by (a,u) in [m] x {0,1}, and let

    C = [[1,-1],[-1,1]],       A_theta = Theta tensor C,
    Theta in [-a0,a0]^(m x m), a0=1/8,
    P_theta = rho I + (1-rho) 11^T/K + A_theta/K, rho=1/2.

The learner is given K, a0, rho, the entire block/checkerboard family,
the state-indexing convention and symbol labels, and the observation channel
E below. It is given one stationary raw observation path X_1,...,X_n; the
hidden-state realizations are never observed. Transition entries, the
predictive matrix, and parameter coefficients are not supplied. The
initial hidden state S_1 is uniform. For the fully observed endpoint s=1,
E=I and X_t=S_t. For the full-support HMM endpoint fix s=3/4 and use

    E = s I + (1-s) 11^T/K,
    E[x,i] = Pr[X_t=x | S_t=i].

The matrix E is symmetric and doubly stochastic. Hidden states evolve by
P_theta and observations are conditionally independent through E. In either
case, write

    Q_theta[x,y] = Pr_theta(X_(t+1)=y | X_t=x),
    J_theta[x,y] = Pr_theta(X_t=x,X_(t+1)=y) = Q_theta[x,y]/K.

The task receives a one-symbol context x and returns a distribution over the
next symbol. Its loss after training on the n-symbol path is

    R(theta,Qhat) = E_theta[ (1/K) sum_x TV(Qhat(.|x),Q_theta(.|x)) ]
                  = E_theta[ (1/(2K)) ||Qhat-Q_theta||_1 ].

The expectation is over the training path; equivalently the test context is
an independent stationary symbol, which is uniform on [K]. Estimators may be
arbitrary functions of the entire path and may know the parameterized family.
Set N=n-1, the number of observed transitions.

Theorem (matching expected-TV minimax rate)
------------------------------------------
For every even K >= 2, and for either fixed channel s=1 or s=3/4, let

    R*_K,N = inf_Qhat sup_{theta in [-1/8,1/8]^(K/2 x K/2)} R(theta,Qhat).

For N>=1,

    (s^2/8) min{1/8, (sqrt(3)/16) K/sqrt(N)}
       <= R*_K,N
       <= min{1, sqrt(25/3) K/sqrt(N)}.                 (1)

The N=0 lower is interpreted with K/sqrt(N)=infinity. Thus, for fixed
constants a0 and s, the minimax risk is Theta(min{1,K/sqrt(N)}), and achieving
expected risk at most epsilon (for epsilon <= s^2/64) requires

    N >= (3 s^4 / 16384) K^2/epsilon^2                 (2)

up to the integer endpoint convention, while N >= (25/3)K^2/epsilon^2 is a
sufficient condition from the explicit estimator. The numerical constants
are deliberately conservative. The conclusion is the K^2/epsilon^2 rate,
not a sharp leading constant.

The family satisfies all the following simultaneously:

* exactly K hidden states and K output symbols; B=m^2=K^2/4 free scalar
  transition coordinates;
* stationary hidden and observed distributions are exactly uniform, so the
  minimum one-symbol history mass is exactly 1/K;
* every transition probability is at least 3/(8K); for s=3/4 every emission
  probability is at least 1/(4K);
* the hidden-chain Dobrushin coefficient is at most 5/8, hence the worst-start
  total-variation mixing time to distance 1/4 is at most 3 transitions;
* sigma_min(P_theta) >= 3/8; sigma_min(E)=s; sigma_min(Q_theta) >=
  s^2(3/8), equal to at least 27/128 when s=3/4;
* the raw two-symbol joint/one-step Hankel block has
  sigma_min(J_theta) >= 3s^2/(8K), equal to 27/(128K) when s=3/4;
* the witness and prediction horizon is one observed symbol followed by one
  predicted symbol. The block J is full rank K. Thus the observable linear
  rank is K; for s=1 the K observed states are exactly K distinct predictive
  states.

The raw, unnormalized moment singular value is not hidden: it scales as 1/K.
The conditionalized predictor Q and emission operator retain constant
singular margins. Likewise the absolute transition/emission floors scale as
1/K, as row normalization requires; their K-normalized margins are fixed.

Proof
-----
1. Stochasticity, stationarity, and margins.

Every row and column of C sums to zero. Therefore every row and column of
A_theta sums to zero, and P_theta is doubly stochastic. Its stationary law
is uniform. For an off-diagonal transition entry the unperturbed value is
1/(2K) and the perturbation has magnitude at most a0/K, so
P_theta[i,j]>=3/(8K). Diagonal entries are larger than this lower bound.

The Frobenius norm calculation

    ||A_theta||_F^2 = 4 sum_(a,b) theta_ab^2
                    <= 4m^2 a0^2 = K^2 a0^2

implies ||A_theta/K||_2 <= a0. The base matrix
P_0=rho I+(1-rho)11^T/K has singular values 1 and rho (multiplicity K-1).
By the singular-value perturbation inequality,
sigma_min(P_theta)>=rho-a0=3/8.

For any row i, the perturbation from the corresponding row of P_0 has L1
norm at most 2m a0/K=a0, hence row total variation at most a0/2. The
Dobrushin coefficient, the maximum TV distance between two rows, is at most
rho+a0=5/8 by the triangle inequality. Dobrushin contraction gives
sup_mu ||mu P_theta^t-uniform||_TV <= (5/8)^t. At t=3 this is 125/512 < 1/4,
so t_mix(1/4)<=3.

E has eigenvalue 1 on the uniform vector and s on its orthogonal complement.
It is doubly stochastic, so the observations are uniform. For s=3/4 its
minimum entry is (1-s)/K=1/(4K). Given any past, the hidden-state posterior
is some probability vector. Since every transition entry is at least
3/(8K), and each row of E sums to one, the next-symbol probability is at
least 3/(8K). Consequently every length-h observed history has mass at
least K^(-1)(3/(8K))^(h-1); at h=1 the exact minimum is 1/K.

2. Observable prediction and singular values.

With the stated orientation of E, uniform hidden stationarity gives
Pr(S_t=i | X_t=x)=E[x,i]. Therefore Q_theta=E P_theta E^T=E P_theta E.
Both E and P_0 preserve the uniform direction and its orthogonal complement.
The perturbation A_theta annihilates the uniform direction on both sides,
and E acts as sI on the zero-sum subspace. Hence

    Q_theta = Q_0 + s^2 A_theta/K.

On the uniform direction Q_theta has singular value 1. On its orthogonal
complement Q_0 has minimum singular value s^2 rho, and the perturbation has
operator norm at most s^2 a0. Thus sigma_min(Q_theta)>=s^2(rho-a0)=3s^2/8.
Since J_theta=Q_theta/K, sigma_min(J_theta)>=3s^2/(8K). In particular J is
an invertible length-one context/future block, giving Hankel rank at least
K. A K-state HMM gives Hankel rank at most K, so the rank is exactly K.
At s=1, the observed chain itself is Markov and its invertible transition
matrix has K distinct one-step predictive rows.

3. Neighboring raw-trajectory KL bound.

Restrict to the hypercube theta_ab in {+Delta,-Delta}, with 0<Delta<=a0.
Let theta and theta' differ in one coordinate (a,b). The corresponding 2x2
block of P changes by 2Delta C/K. Exactly two transition rows are affected;
in each such row exactly two entries change, each by magnitude 2Delta/K.
All entries of the denominator transition matrix are at least c0/K, where
c0=1-rho-a0=3/8. For each affected row,

    chi^2(P_theta[i,.] || P_theta'[i,.])
       <= 2(2Delta/K)^2 / (c0/K)
       = 8 Delta^2/(c0 K).

Use D(p||q)<=chi^2(p||q), weight the two rows by their stationary mass 1/K,
and use the Markov path chain rule. For the hidden path of N transitions,

    D(P_theta^(S_1:S_n) || P_theta'^(S_1:S_n))
       <= 16 N Delta^2/(c0 K^2)
       = 128 N Delta^2/(3K^2).                         (3)

The observed sequence is obtained from the hidden path through the same
emission channel under both models, so data processing makes (3) an upper
bound on the KL divergence between the actual raw observation-path laws.
For s=1 this is equality. Pinsker then gives neighboring observation-path
TV at most 1/2 whenever N<=3K^2/(256 Delta^2).

4. Assouad reduction to predictive risk.

For each block b=(a,b), let C_b be C embedded in the corresponding 2x2
context/future block and zero elsewhere. Given any output kernel Qhat, decode
the bit sign from sign <C_b,Qhat-Q_0>. The true inner product is

    <C_b,Q_theta-Q_0> = 4s^2 theta_ab/K.

If the decoded sign is wrong (ties are assigned to either fixed sign), the L1 error
on that block is at least 4s^2 Delta/K, since every entry of C_b has
magnitude one. The B=m^2 blocks are disjoint. If H is the number of decoded
bit errors, then

    R(theta,Qhat) >= 2s^2 Delta H/K^2.                 (4)

Under the uniform prior on the hypercube, condition on all bits except one.
For that bit, the two data laws are the corresponding neighbors and any
binary test has average error at least (1-TV_neighbor)/2 >=1/4. Averaging
over the nuisance bits preserves this bound for every coordinate, so each
bit test has average error at least 1/4 if the condition following (3) holds.
Therefore E H>=B/4=K^2/16. Averaging (4) gives Bayes risk at least
s^2 Delta/8, and minimax risk is at least this large. Choose

    Delta = min{a0, (sqrt(3)/16) K/sqrt(N)}.

This choice obeys the neighbor-TV condition, yielding the lower half of (1).
Alternatively, setting Delta=8epsilon/s^2 gives risk at least epsilon when
epsilon<=s^2 a0/8 and N<=3s^4 K^2/(16384 epsilon^2), which is (2).

The hypercube has B=K^2/4 independent signs. A single-bit neighbor changes
only two context rows, but a dense pair of corners (all +Delta versus all
-Delta) differs by TV s^2 Delta in every context row. The lower bound is not
an isolated rare-context discrepancy: Assouad sums uncertainty over a
quadratic number of dense local directions, and a constant fraction of the
contexts carry aggregate predictive error.

5. Matching upper bound from the same raw path.

For z=(x,y), let I_t(z)=1{(X_t,X_(t+1))=z} and estimate J[z] by its frequency
over the N adjacent pairs. The hidden Dobrushin coefficient alpha<=5/8
controls pair-event covariance. At lag one, |Cov(I_t,I_(t+1))|<=2J[z]. For
lag k>=2, condition on the hidden state at t+1; contraction for the k-1
intervening transitions gives |Cov(I_t,I_(t+k))|<=J[z] alpha^(k-1). Thus

    Var(Jhat[z]) <= (25/3) J[z]/N.

By Cauchy-Schwarz and row-stochasticity of Q,

    sum_(x,y) sqrt(J[x,y])
      = K^(-1/2) sum_x sum_y sqrt(Q[x,y]) <= K.

So E||Jhat-J||_1 <= sqrt(25/3) K/sqrt(N). Convert K Jhat rowwise to a
probability kernel by projecting each row onto the simplex in L1. Since the
true Q row is feasible, this increases the row L1 error by at most a factor
two. Therefore the expected loss is at most E||Jhat-J||_1, proving the
upper half of (1). This uses exactly the legal raw path and no context-query
or hidden-state oracle.

Interpretation of the margins and the fixed-K boundary
-----------------------------------------------------
This theorem refutes an obstruction-free claim based only on constant
mixing, constant emission/transition singular values, and constant
conditionalized observability. It does not keep every *unnormalized*
probability floor or raw Hankel singular value constant as K grows:

    min_x pi_X(x)=1/K,
    min_ij P_ij >= 3/(8K),
    min_xi E[x,i] = 1/(4K) for s=3/4,
    sigma_min(J) >= 27/(128K).

These are charged in the result. The normalized margins are respectively
K*pi_min=1, K*min P>=3/8, K*min E=1/4, and sigma_min(Q)>=27/128.
If a user instead requires a K-independent entrywise lower bound eta>0 on
every entry of a stochastic K-by-K row, normalization forces K<=1/eta.
Moreover if all K contexts have mass 1/K, any unnormalized joint context/future
block J has each row L1 mass 1/K and hence sigma_min(J)<=1/K. A constant lower
bound on this raw singular value is therefore incompatible with growing K
under uniform context mass. Requiring fixed K, fixed alphabet, and the stated
one-step task removes the growing ceiling: (1) gives finite sample
complexity O(K^2/epsilon^2), with all constants fixed. No unbounded lower is
claimed at fixed K.

Costs and strongest matched baselines
-------------------------------------
Supplied to the learner: K, the block parameterization, labels, rho, a0,
the channel E, and the promise of stationarity. This is more structural
information than teacher-free acquisition normally receives. Acquired:
the raw path, empirical pair counts, and all unknown theta-dependent
predictive coefficients. State count and alphabet are K, the witness horizon
is one, and there are B=K^2/4 independent real coordinates. At target
precision epsilon, a finite b-bit grid for each coefficient needs
O(K^2 log(1/epsilon)) bits; this is a description-size accounting, not a
claim that the real-parameter class itself is finite. The observed path costs
n ceil(log2 K) bits if stored, or O(K^2 log n) counter bits in a streaming
pair-count implementation. Streaming takes O(n) symbol updates; the output
kernel has K^2 entries, and row projection is polynomial in K. The theorem's
sample lower is information-theoretic, not a computational-hardness claim.

The strongest same-data baseline is the empirical two-symbol law followed by
the rowwise L1 projection above; it gets the matching O(K/sqrt(n)) risk.
The Assouad lower is against every estimator, including a maximum-likelihood
or spectral method that knows the family, emission matrix, labels, stationary
law, transition baseline, and block basis. Thus supplying a predictive
coordinate basis alone does not remove the lower: this construction already
has a common known basis and known feature map. Supplying the numerical
predictive operator Q_theta (equivalently all theta coefficients) does remove
it: the answer can then be returned exactly with no trajectory. An interface
that returns each exact conditional probability vector Q_theta(.|x) for all
K contexts costs K vector replies and O(K^2) numbers and replaces passive
statistical acquisition by oracle acquisition. Merely revealing current
state/context labels is insufficient; the strict s=1 endpoint already reveals
them in every observation.

Falsifiers, limitations, and UNKNOWN gates
------------------------------------------
An algebraic falsifier is a rational K fixture violating row/column
stochasticity, uniform stationarity, a stated margin, or Q=EPE. A statistical
falsifier would invalidate either the neighbor path-KL calculation or the
pair-frequency variance bound. A novelty falsifier is a primary source
already proving this same restricted HMM/Markov family, expected-TV loss,
charged margins, and constants/rate; the sources below establish close
general Markov-chain results, so no novelty claim is made.

This result is limited to expected total variation and the one-symbol-context
task. It does not prove a lower bound for KL loss, all-history prediction in
the noisy HMM endpoint, unbounded witness horizons, or active exact conditional
queries. A uniform raw Hankel singular gap independent of K is deliberately
not promised and, for this joint block, cannot coexist with the stated
uniform context masses. The noisy full-support HMM has rank K but can have
infinitely many posterior predictive states; only its finite-state generator
and finite-rank realization are asserted. The s=1 Markov endpoint supplies
the strict finite-causal-state instance. External review, literature
exhaustiveness, sharp leading constants, and priority remain UNKNOWN.

Primary-source comparison and read ledger
-----------------------------------------
1. Y. Han, S. Jana, Y. Wu, “Optimal prediction of Markov chains with and
   without spectral gap conditions,” NeurIPS 34 (2021), primary paper:
   https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/5d69dc892ba6e79fda0c6a1e286f24c5-Paper.pdf
   Their Theorem 1 gives the larger unrestricted KL prediction rate when
   mixing can be arbitrarily slow; Theorem 3 gives an O(k^2/n) upper under
   sufficient spectral-gap conditions. The present result isolates a
   matching TV lower on a positive fast-mixing, doubly stochastic family and
   extends the raw-observation lower through a fixed noisy full-support
   channel. This is related prior art, not a novelty claim.
2. G. Wolfer and A. Kontorovich, “Minimax Learning of Ergodic Markov Chains,”
   PMLR 98 (2019), primary article:
   https://proceedings.mlr.press/v98/wolfer19a.html
   They study single-trajectory parameter learning with a TV-type metric and
   make spectral-gap and minimum stationary mass costs explicit. This is a
   direct baseline for the s=1 fully observed endpoint; the metric and
   restricted family here differ.
3. D. Hsu, S. M. Kakade, T. Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden
   Markov Models,” JCSS 78(5), 1460-1480 (2012), primary paper:
   https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~djhsu/papers/hmm.pdf
   Their abstract and setup concern learning from observation samples under
   singular-separation assumptions. It is a raw-observation HMM baseline;
   the present information lower allows even more supplied structure and
   does not claim an algorithmic advantage over spectral methods.
