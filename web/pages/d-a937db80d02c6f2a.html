1. STRONGEST RESULT: A SHARP UNIVERSAL SEPARABILITY THRESHOLD
Let J_a=(1/2) sum_i sigma_a^(i), acting on the ENTIRE N-qubit Hilbert space.
For every integer N>=1, every finite alpha>=0, every finite real h, and

                    delta <= 2 log 2,

the state

       rho = exp[(alpha J^2-delta J_z^2)/N+h J_z] / Z

is exactly fully separable, including every finite negative delta. Local factors in the product mixture may differ.
This is a full-state statement, including all total-spin multiplicities.
It does not assert that the whole state is an iid mixture.

The constant 2 log 2 is the largest one that works uniformly over N and alpha.
For N=2, the exact partial-transpose condition is

       (1-exp(-alpha))^2 <= 4 exp(-delta).

Every delta>2 log 2 therefore has a FINITE alpha giving an entangled state.
The uniform optimum is not the exact phase boundary of each individual
finite-(N,alpha) model; many states above it remain separable.

In physical units k_B=1, for
H=-g J^2/N+u J_z^2/N-h_phys J_z, with g,u>=0 and finite T>0, the sufficient temperature is

                         T >= u/(2 log 2).

It is independent of N, ferromagnetic strength g, and finite uniform field.
No arbitrary interaction graph, nonuniform field, negative alpha or large
biaxial-anisotropy extension is being inferred.

state into a hierarchical sampler of positive rational one-qubit density

Its theoretical cost is polynomial in N, rational input bit length, and
log(1/epsilon). The admitted rational domain includes every delta<=2log2,
with no lower bound and no numerical-magnitude restriction on alpha or h. The general algorithm explicitly charges optimization,
exponential acquisition, normalization, precision and finite random bits.
It does not assume an oracle supplying the desired product decomposition.

Two useful mechanisms make this possible:
 - The isotropic state lies in the convex hull of N+1 explicit coherent-subset
   states. Their physical spin-sector probabilities have a closed rational
   formula. Fitting the already-filtered columns avoids a worst-case global
   filter condition factor. An aligned extremal state supplies a normalizer
   lower bound independent of the numerical magnitudes of alpha and h.
 - Each conditional symmetric block has a Gaussian or Schur-Radau quadrature.
   Only N zero-field rules are needed when using the proved positive-tilt
   reuse interface. A gap-free rational Jacobi construction gives a finite-bit
   proof; the practical implementation instead proposes numerical nodes and
   certifies the actual rounded positive ensemble using exact arithmetic.

3. PHYSICAL SPINS AND A NEW DYNAMICAL CONSEQUENCE
For physical spins j_i, write M=2 sum_i j_i. Local symmetric virtual-qubit
projection transfers the whole-state theorem to every finite signed

                   delta <= 2 N log(2)/M.

For equal spin j this reads delta<=log(2)/j. A rational count dynamic program
samples the projection posterior without exponentially rare rejection. Its
cost is polynomial in numerical M, input bits and log(1/epsilon), hence in N
for fixed physical spins. The proof, precision costs, opposite field-basis
conventions and bounded exact projection fixtures were independently checked.
There is no higher-spin optimality or physical hardware claim.

For ideal collective decay Gamma D[J_-], put E=Jz+N/2 and R=J_+J_-.
Every fully separable state obeys <R> >= <E>^2/N. The conserved spin-sector
quantity beta=< (N+1)/2-sqrt(J^2+1/4) > supplies <E(t)> >= beta.
With C=<J^2>+parity(N)/4, a sector absorption-time bound proves

                  <R(t)> <= 2 C exp(-Gamma t/2).

Thus every full-rank admitted thermal state, although initially separable,
becomes entangled at a finite explicitly bounded time. Starting from I/2^N,
t=10/Gamma suffices for every N>=2. These statements have an independent
proof reconstruction and exact small checks. The two-qubit common-reservoir
effect is already known (Li--Xu); priority of the precise all-N bounds is
unsettled. The rate, small-beta time cost and ideal collective-noise model are
part of the interface. The stated bath generator is not automatically a
microscopic thermalizer of the original quadratic Gibbs Hamiltonian. Separate
state preparation or a Hamiltonian quench, bath engineering and their physical
costs would be needed for a concrete experiment. No experiment was performed.
