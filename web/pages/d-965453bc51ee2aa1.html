T21 | FINITE F2 OBSTRUCTION AND A COMPLETE MODULE INVARIANT
Record: v1 (frozen initial substantial derivation)
Status: no transformative algorithm obtained; one exact finite counterexample is internally verified; a complete invariant is proved for the regular square-matrix-space subclass, but no efficient canonization of that invariant is obtained.

1. Exact target and input model

Target sought: a deterministic exact algorithm which, given two dense tensors
T,T' in U tensor V tensor W over F_q, with dim U=dim V=dim W=n, decides
whether they lie in the same GL(U) x GL(V) x GL(W) orbit and returns a
transporting triple when they do. The desired worst-case bound is
poly(n, log q) bit operations. This is an orbit question over the supplied
field F_q; equality of orbit closures over an algebraic closure is not the
decision relation. The related alternating-matrix-space target is equivalence
under congruence on the matrix side and basis change on the space side.

For the finite obstruction below, q=2 and n=3. A 3-tensor is represented by
the 3-dimensional matrix code C spanned by its three slices. Tensor
equivalence is exactly C' = P C Q^{-1} for some P,Q in GL_3(F_2), with the
third GL_3 action accounting for change of basis in the slice space.

The live primary-frontier search found:

* Li, Li, Qiao, Tao, and Wang, arXiv:2604.00591v1 (1 Apr 2026),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00591. They define the trace hull of a matrix
  code C <= M_n(F_q) by H(C)=C intersect C^perp for
  <A,B>=Tr(AB), an invariant under simultaneous matrix conjugacy. For
  n-dimensional random codes in M_n(F_q), their algorithm uses a
  one-dimensional hull, a unique simple F_q eigenvalue, and two further
  pinned matrices that generate M_n. It gives polynomial time on a
  1/Theta(q) fraction for algebra isomorphism and matrix-code conjugacy;
  the matrix-code statement is for n-dimensional codes. Their Problem 1.4
  explicitly leaves average-case subexponential algorithms for 3-tensor,
  cubic-form, and alternating-trilinear-form isomorphism open. Their
  Problem 1.5 asks to extend the matrix-code result to arbitrary code
  dimension. These are source-reported results, not independently audited
  here. Relevant locations in v1: Theorems 1.1-1.3, Problems 1.4-1.5,
  the hull discussion in Sec. 1.4.1, and the algorithms in Secs. 3-4.

* Grochow and Qiao, SIAM J. Comput. 52 (2023),
  https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1441110. Their theorem reduces 3-tensor
  isomorphism to matrix-space isometry, matrix-space conjugacy, algebra
  isomorphism, and related TI-complete problems. Over finite fields, the
  straightforward general upper bound enumerates GL(n,F_q), with
  q^{Theta(n^2)} group size; the paper records q^{O(d^2)} algorithms
  when one matrix-space dimension d is small. This makes the all-sides-
  growing regime the relevant target here.

* Ivanyos, Mendoza, Qiao, Sun, and Zhang, FOCS 2024 / SIAM J. Comput.,
  https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1719839. This is a primary source for the
  N^{tilde O(sqrt(log N))} algorithm for p-groups of Frattini class 2.
  The 2026 paper above reports the induced q^{tilde O(L^{1.5})} tensor
  isomorphism bound when combined with linear-length reductions. No claim
  here sharpens that bound.

* Brooksbank, Maglione, and Wilson, “Tensor Isomorphism by conjugacy of
  Lie algebras,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04046 (updated 22 Mar 2026).
  Their derivation-densor method computes a derivation Lie algebra and
  compresses the tensor into a densor space. It yields polynomial time
  for the stated Chevalley-type, one-dimensional-densor cases. The paper
  also explains why this fails to control generic tensors: the derivation
  algebra can be minimal, leaving a large normalizer quotient to search.

3. Attempted compatibility statistic

For a matrix code C <= Hom(U,V), define its rank enumerator
  R_C(r) = |{A in C : rank(A)=r}|.
For each d, define the evaluation-image profile
  E_C(d) = multiset over all d-dimensional S <= U of
           dim(span{A(s): A in C, s in S}).
For k >= 1 and an ordered tuple X=(x_1,...,x_k) in U^k, let
  ev_X : C -> V^k,       A |-> (Ax_1,...,Ax_k),
and define K_C(k) to be the multiset of ranks of ev_X over all X in U^k.
These data are invariant under independent changes of basis in U and V and
under replacing a basis of C. K_C(k) is a joint, multi-input refinement of
the one-vector singular-space profile. The first attempted idea was that
several simultaneous evaluations might record enough compatibility to
control the global orbit.

The following exact example refutes this particular family of refinements,
even with k=4, and also matches the two-sided adjoint algebra.

Let E_ij denote the 3 x 3 matrix unit over F_2, and set
  A_1 = E_12,
  A_2 = E_22 + E_31 + E_32,
  A_3 = E_21 + E_33,
  C   = span_F2{A_1,A_2,A_3}.
In ordinary matrices,
  A_1 = [0 1 0; 0 0 0; 0 0 0],
  A_2 = [0 0 0; 0 1 0; 1 1 0],
  A_3 = [0 0 0; 1 0 0; 0 0 1].
Let C^t={A^t:A in C}. The associated linear matrix is
  M_C(x,y,z) = x A_1 + y A_2 + z A_3
             = [0 x 0; z y 0; y y z],
and det M_C = x z^2 = det(M_C^t).

Internal exact calculations over F_2 give:

  Profile                         C                 C^t
  R_C(0),...,R_C(3)             (1,1,4,2)          (1,1,4,2)
  E_C(0)                         (0)                (0)
  E_C(1)                         (1,2,2,2,2,3,3)  (1,2,2,2,2,3,3)
  E_C(2)                         (2,3,3,3,3,3,3)  (2,3,3,3,3,3,3)
  E_C(3)                         (3)                (3)
  K_C(1)                         (1,1,4,2)          (1,1,4,2)
  K_C(2)                         (1,3,18,42)        (1,3,18,42)
  K_C(3)                         (1,7,70,434)       (1,7,70,434)
  K_C(4)                         (1,15,270,3810)    (1,15,270,3810)
  dim Adj(C)                     1                 1

Here Adj(C)={(X,Y): XA=AY for every A in C}. It always contains (I,I);
dimension one over F_2 therefore gives Adj(C)={(0,0),(I,I)}, the same
one-dimensional algebra F_2 on both sides. The matrix-code stabilizer sizes
are both 2. Since the slices are independent, this also gives equal tensor
automorphism-group sizes for these two representatives.

They are nevertheless in different tensor orbits over F_2. The exact
canonical keys computed by exhaustive minimization over all 168^2=28,224
left/right transformations are
  min_{P,Q in GL_3(F_2)} code(P C Q^{-1})
      = (0,1,80,81,160,161,240,241),
  min_{P,Q in GL_3(F_2)} code(P C^t Q^{-1})
      = (0,1,20,21,160,161,180,181).
Each code is recorded as its sorted set of nine-bit row-major matrix
encodings, with bit i representing the (i//3,i%3) entry. Since the keys
differ, no P,Q carry one code to the other. The third-mode GL_3 action only
changes a basis of a code; it does not change the underlying subspace, so
this is also a nonisomorphism certificate for the corresponding 3-tensors.

The full reproducible finite search and orbit-key calculation are in
small_f2_counterexamples.py and search_3x3_transpose_obstruction.py. The
latter enumerates GL_3(F_2), all 16 subspaces of F_2^3 for the evaluation
profile, all tuples through k=4, and the two-sided adjoint linear system.
This is finite-tested exact evidence only; it is not an asymptotic lower
bound and it has not had external review. The matrices may become equivalent
over an extension field; that question is UNKNOWN here.

4. Candidate orbit-complete invariant for regular square matrix spaces

There is a precise global compatibility object that separates information
lost by rank counts and determinant polynomials. Let
  S=F_q[x_1,...,x_m],
  M_C(x)=sum_{i=1}^m x_i A_i in Mat_n(S_1),
and suppose det M_C is not the zero polynomial. Define the graded S-module
  G_C = coker(M_C : S(-1)^n -> S^n).
Use its isomorphism class modulo linear changes of variables x |-> R x as
the invariant.

Claim (proved here). On the class det M_C != 0, G_C modulo GL_m changes of
variables is a complete invariant for the GL_n x GL_n x GL_m orbit of the
tensor.

Proof. If M_D(x)=P M_C(Rx) Q for constant invertible P,Q,R, the associated
cokernel modules are isomorphic after the corresponding graded coordinate
automorphism of S. Conversely, assume the modules are isomorphic after a
linear coordinate change, and absorb that coordinate change into M_D. Since
S is a domain and det M_C != 0, the map M_C:S(-1)^n -> S^n is injective:
if M_C v=0, multiplication by its adjugate gives det(M_C)v=0, hence v=0.
Thus
  0 -> S(-1)^n --M_C--> S^n -> G_C -> 0
is a free resolution. It is minimal because every entry of M_C has positive
degree. Minimal graded free resolutions over the polynomial ring are unique
up to graded chain isomorphism. A degree-zero graded automorphism of S^n,
or of S(-1)^n, is a constant invertible matrix over F_q. Therefore an
isomorphism G_C ~= G_D lifts to constant P,Q with M_D=P M_C Q. This is
exact matrix-space equivalence. Reintroducing the variable coordinate
change gives exactly the third GL_n action. QED.

For the finite pair above, the determinant polynomial is the same nonzero
polynomial x z^2, but the modules G_C and G_C^t cannot be isomorphic after
any linear variable change over F_2: the proved converse would imply an
orbit equivalence, which the exhaustive keys refute. So the module retains
global presentation compatibility that the determinant and the profiles
above lose.

This invariant does not give the requested algorithm. The module presentation
has essentially the same size as the tensor, and testing its isomorphism
while also choosing a GL_m coordinate change is not shown to be easier than
the original orbit problem. The proof uses the original linear presentation;
it gives no polynomial-time canonical form, no bit-complexity bound for a
module-isomorphism procedure, and no reduction in search over GL_m. The
invariant is a complete reformulation on the regular square class, not a
transformative algorithm. If det M_C is identically zero, the displayed
length-one resolution argument does not apply; a full minimal resolution
can have further syzygies, and no corresponding efficient classification is
derived here.

For alternating matrix spaces, applying the same construction to an
alternating linear matrix gives a cokernel module that is complete for
independent left/right matrix-space equivalence. Alternating-space
isometry is stricter: it requires the two sides to be linked by transpose.
An enriched module with its induced self-duality may be the right object,
but the chain-level converse and its behavior in characteristic two have
not been established in this record. No finite-group isomorphism algorithm
follows.

5. Failed alternatives and remaining mathematical gap

* Extending the trace-hull method by enumerating elements of a higher-
  dimensional hull costs q^h when dim H=h. That is not polynomial in
  log q for variable q, and does not control the residual centralizer in
  worst-case families. The one-dimensional-hull randomized algorithm from
  arXiv:2604.00591 is not generalized here.
* The rank enumerator, evaluation-image profile, and K_C(k) through k=4
  all fail on the explicit C,C^t pair. The two-sided adjoint algebra also
  agrees. Thus refining only these local distributions does not determine
  the orbit.
* The determinant polynomial agrees on the pair. The full cokernel module
  is complete in the regular case, but comparing it under a variable
  change has not yielded a smaller or faster problem.
* The derivation-densor method is effective when global symmetry makes its
  derivation algebra and densor tractable. Its source paper notes that for
  generic tensors the derivation algebra may be minimal, leaving a large
  normalizer quotient; that is the regime this target must address.

No orbit-closure implication is used. A polynomial invariant such as det
can at best identify a coarse orbit datum; the exact module statement above
is over F_q and preserves the F_q orbit relation. The finite obstruction
has only been checked over F_2, so its behavior after field extension is
unresolved. Automorphism groups in the example were compared only by their
finite order (2), not by a complete classification of their embeddings.
