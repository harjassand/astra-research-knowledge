1. EXACT THEOREM

Let rho_x be a probability ensemble of density matrices on finite H,
sigma=int rho_x dx>0, and [rho_x,sigma]=0 for almost every x. Let Phi be
ANY CPTP map H->H, with Kraus A_i, rho'_x=Phi(rho_x), xi=Phi(sigma), and

  e=int T(rho_x,rho'_x)dx, T(X,Y)=||X-Y||_1/2.

For every public A>0 put

  L_x=sigma^(-1/2)rho_x sigma^(-1/2),
  G_x=L_x(A+L_x)^(-1), B_i=A_i sqrt(sigma).

Then

  A sum_i int ||[G_x,B_i]||_HS^2 dx <= (5/2)e.          (1)

No assumption [xi,L_x]=0, [xi,rho'_x]=0, xi=sigma, unitality,
covariance, sector preservation or nonsingularity of xi is required.
The G field is an analytic test determined by the source; no unknown x
or input-dependent record is supplied to an archive by this construction.

2. OUTPUT SYMMETRIC LOGARITHMIC DERIVATIVE AND SUPPORT

Suppress x. Define M=rho+A sigma>0 and M'=Phi(M)=rho'+A xi. The input G
is the Hermitian solution of {M,G}=2rho, because the source commutes with
sigma. It commutes with sigma and satisfies 0<=G<=I.

Let E=supp xi. Since sigma>0, rho<=c_x sigma for some finite c_x, so
rho'<=c_x xi and supp rho' is contained in E. Every A_i has range in E:
0=Tr(E_perp xi)=sum_i||E_perp A_i sqrt(sigma)||_HS^2, and sigma is invertible.
Thus M' is strictly positive on E and zero off E. On E define

  G'=2 int_0^infinity exp(-tM') rho' exp(-tM')dt.        (2)

The integral converges, solves {M',G'}=2rho', and is positive. Moreover

  I_E-G'=2A int_0^infinity exp(-tM') xi exp(-tM')dt>=0,

so 0<=G'<=I_E. Extend G' by zero on E_perp. The full-space Sylvester
equation still holds. G need not commute with E or G', and the proof
below keeps these cross-support entries. No target mass is discarded.

3. TWO EXACT POSITIVE DEFICITS

For any Hermitian H let

  q_in(H)=2Tr H rho-Tr M H^2,
  q_out(H)=2Tr H rho'-Tr M' H^2.

Put V=Tr M G^2=Tr rho G and V'=Tr M' G'^2=Tr rho' G'.
The trace equalities follow from the respective Sylvester equations.
For D_i=G'A_i-A_iG, exact expansion and TP give

  sum_i Tr D_i M D_i^dag
   =Tr M' G'^2+Tr M G^2
                   -Tr G' Phi(MG+GM)
   =V'+V-2Tr G' rho'=V-V'=:J>=0.                     (3)

This is a Kraus remainder of the quadratic/SLD data-processing deficit.
Because M>=A sigma and G commutes with sigma,

  J>=A sum_i||D_i sqrt(sigma)||_HS^2
    =A sum_i||G'B_i-B_iG||_HS^2.                      (4)

Completing the trace square with {M',G'}=2rho' gives, for every Hermitian H,

  V'-q_out(H)=Tr M'(G'-H)^2>=0.                        (5)

No commutation between H and M' is used: the two cross terms combine
to Tr H{M',G'}. Taking H=G, define R=V'-q_out(G). Then

  R=Tr M'(G'-G)^2>=A Tr xi(G'-G)^2.                    (6)

This is a full-space identity. If G mixes E and E_perp, the E-diagonal
of (G'-G)^2 includes exactly those off-support squared entries. Replacing
G by EGE here would lose them and would not justify the final commutator.

An exact two-dimensional falsifier of that incorrect crop is useful. Take
rho0=|0><0|, rho1=|1><1| with prior (1/3,2/3), sigma=diag(1/3,2/3), and
Phi(X)=Tr(X)|+><+|. For x=0 and A=1, G=diag(3/4,0), E=|+><+|,
