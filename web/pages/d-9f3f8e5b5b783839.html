## Quadratic phase family

Choose an odd prime p≥max(3,m) and distinct labels t_i in F_p. For each (a,b) in F_p² define
\[
 \varepsilon_i(a,b)=\exp(2\pi i(a t_i+b t_i^2)/p).
\]
Uniform averaging over these p² assignments has exactly
\[
 E\,\overline{\varepsilon_i}\varepsilon_j=\delta_{ij},
\]
and
\[
 E\,\overline{\varepsilon_i}\varepsilon_j
          \overline{\varepsilon_k}\varepsilon_l
 =
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\{i,k\}=\{j,l\}\text{ as multisets},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Proof: finite character orthogonality says the second expression is nonzero exactly when
t_i+t_k=t_j+t_l and t_i²+t_k²=t_j²+t_l² in F_p. Since p is odd, these equations also give t_it_k=t_jt_l. The two unordered pairs are the root multisets of the same monic quadratic, so they agree, including repetitions. Conversely equality of the multisets clearly gives phase one. The second-moment claim uses injectivity of the first label coordinate.

This does not assert four-wise independence. A label t_i=0 has constant phase one, which causes no problem: the proof needs the displayed balanced second and fourth moments rather than vanishing individual first moments.

Set z=Σ√p_i ε_i Z_i. The exact noncommuting fourth-moment expansion is therefore
\[
 E(z^*z)^2=A^2+\sum_i p_i Z_i^*BZ_i
                 -\sum_i p_i^2(Z_i^*Z_i)^2.
\]
Its factor order is identical to the source's Steinhaus expansion. The subtracted overlap is positive, so Eφ|z|⁴≤C, while E∥Tzξ∥²≥γ.

## Total finite-selection cost and an optimal-order lower bound

An elementary deterministic search can acquire p by testing candidates from max(3,m), with trial division; Bertrand's postulate bounds p≤2m for m≥2 (m=2 can use p=3). Thus at most 4m² phase assignments suffice for m≥2, and 9 for m=1. Prime acquisition costs O(m^{3/2} poly(log m)) bit operations under this deliberately simple implementation. Each assignment uses m modular evaluations; the full scan uses O(m³) field operations plus at most p² supplied clipping/filter/score evaluations and the input read/acquisition cost. A pth root of unity has a short exact specification and can be numerically evaluated at charged precision. A polynomial total bit theorem still requires certified implementations of the supplied operator services.

The support order cannot be improved for an exact universal matching family of unit phases with positive weights: the m(m+1)/2 functions ε_iε_k, i≤k, are orthonormal in L² of that family by the required balanced fourth moments. If its support has N points then this Hilbert space has dimension N. Hence N≥m(m+1)/2. The p² family is within an absolute constant of this support lower bound.

This lower bound is for exact universal moment matching. It does not rule out smaller approximate families, exploiting particular input Z_i/T, or adaptive witness search.
