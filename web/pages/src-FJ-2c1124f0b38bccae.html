Quantitative compatibility beyond exact contact rigidity
I obtained a quantitative compatibility estimate at every acoustic contact of a non-polyconvex quartic in the full left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant cone. It controls the cubic coupling that can survive near a singular averaged Hessian.
The estimate yields a genuine positive Jensen inequality for gradients close to a contact matrix line, uniformly over an entire plane of affine backgrounds, including backgrounds escaping to infinity. The estimate itself applies to arbitrary compatible gradients; the closeness assumption enters only the positivity corollary.
I also established two limits on the approach. The transverse \(L^4\) norm in the estimate cannot be replaced by an \(L^p\) norm with \(p<4\). And the unrestricted shear transformation is exactly equivalent to the original variational problem—it does not, by itself, simplify the admissible residuals.
The complete nine-coefficient quasiconvexity implication remains unproved. No compatible negative quasiconvexity gap was constructed. The remaining issue is now the interaction between this fixed-contact estimate and the moving background direction selected by full quadratic minimization.

1. The new quantitative theorem
Let \(F\) be a non-polyconvex, rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic invariant under left \(SO(2)\). All nine coefficients are permitted:
\[
F=A_cu^2+B_cv^2+Cuv+Ew^2+Guw+H_0vw+Lqu+Mqv+Nqw.
\]
There is no requirement that \(L=M\), that \(N=0\), or that the input variables have additional symmetry.
An acoustic contact is a pair \(A\ne0\), \(R\ne0\), with \(R\) rank one and
\[
D^2F(A)[R,R]=0.
\]
Normalize \(|R|=1\).
For a mean-zero compatible gradient \(X=D\varphi\), define
\[
s=X:R,\qquad Y=X-sR,
\]
\[
h=\mathbb E D^2F(X)[A,A],\qquad
\beta=\mathbb E DF(X)[A],\qquad
g=\mathbb E D^2F(X)[A,R].
\]
Averages and norms are probability-normalized on the periodic cell. Zero-boundary \(W^{1,4}\) potentials are also covered.
The field \(Y\) is a pointwise matrix projection. It is not assumed to be a gradient.
Theorem — Cubic contact compensation
There is a finite constant \(K=K(F,A,R)\) such that every such compatible gradient satisfies
\[
\boxed{
|\beta|
\le
K\sqrt h\,\|X\|_4\|Y\|_4,
}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
|g|
\le
K\sqrt h\,\|Y\|_2.
}
\tag{2}
\]
These inequalities have no Fourier cutoff, nonresonance assumption or amplitude restriction. The constant is not asserted to be uniform over all integrands or all contact pairs.
When \(h>0\), the first inequality gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\beta^2}{2h}
\le
\frac{K^2}{2}\|X\|_4^2\|Y\|_4^2.
}
\tag{3}
\]
This is the quantitative step missing from exact nullspace closure. It controls the potentially singular cubic loss by the fourth-moment mass transverse to the contact line, not merely by covariance.
That distinction matters. Covariance can collapse while transverse fourth-moment mass remains concentrated. The earlier concentration example does exactly that. The estimate preserves the information that its limiting covariance discards.
2. Reconstructing the contact geometry
The proof starts from the quartic reciprocity identity
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=D^2F(X)[A,A].
\]
Rank-one convexity and the indefinite Finsler lemma give
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=P_A(X)+\lambda_A\det X,
\qquad P_A\succeq0.
\tag{4}
\]
The quadratic decomposition is established mathematics; its indefinite hypothesis is essential. arXiv
Compatibility supplies \(\mathbb E\det X=0\), so
\[
h=\mathbb E P_A(X)\ge0.
\]
At a contact, \(P_A(R)=0\), hence \(P_AR=0\).
There is also a useful global polynomial consequence. Put
\[
\mathcal C_A(X)=DF(X)[A].
\]
Reciprocity makes \(D^2F(R)\) positive semidefinite. Contact equality therefore implies \(D^2F(R)A=0\), and quartic tensor symmetry gives
\[
\boxed{
D^2\mathcal C_A(X)[R,R]=0
\qquad\text{for every }X.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal C_A(sR+Y)\) contains at most one power of \(s\).
Differentiating at acoustic zeros has substantial prior history. Gutiérrez’s work already develops cubic-contact and fourth-order resonant conditions; I am not claiming that step as a new method. heldermann-verlag.de
An additional algebraic simplification
I proved:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}D^2F(R)\le2
\text{ at a nonzero rank-one }R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F\text{ is polyconvex}.
}
\tag{6}
\]
When \(F(R)>0\), the conclusion is stronger: \(F\) is an explicit convex sum of two fourth powers of norms of linear maps.
To see the decisive algebra, normalize \(R=e_{11}\), with \(A_c=F(e_{11})>0\). Its Hessian is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
12A_c&3G&0&3L\\
3G&2C+2E&-L&N\\
0&-L&4A_c&G\\
3L&N&G&2C
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The \((a,c)\) block is positive definite. Rank at most two forces a zero Schur complement:
\[
C=\frac{G^2+3L^2}{8A_c},\qquad
E=\frac{G^2-L^2}{4A_c},\qquad
N=\frac{GL}{2A_c}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
F=A_c|x_{\mathbb C}+\kappa y_{\mathbb C}|^4
+B'v^2+H'vw+M'qv,
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{G-\mathrm iL}{4A_c}.
\]
At backgrounds satisfying \(x_{\mathbb C}+\kappa y_{\mathbb C}=0\), the first summand has zero Hessian. The acoustic quadratic in the rank-one direction \(e_1\otimes(1,t)\) vanishes at \(t=0\). Its derivative there is
\[
2\left[H'(3b^2+d^2)+2M'bd\right].
\]
Nonnegativity for both signs of \(t\), at every background, forces \(H'=M'=0\). A further rank-one test gives \(B'\ge0\). The case \(F(R)=0\) follows from the degenerate binary rank-one restriction.
Therefore a singular rank-one Hessian of a non-polyconvex integrand has exactly a one-dimensional kernel.

3. The operation supplying the missing estimate
In the Type II coordinates, write
\[
X=D\varphi=
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},
\qquad r=d-\lambda a.
\]
Then
\[
h\ge\nu\bigl(\|c\|_2^2+\|r\|_2^2\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]
The difficult term is \(\mathbb E a^3\). It need not vanish. Determinant balance alone does not control it in the required way.
Step 1: repair the differential constraint at the potential level
Define
\[
\varphi_1^0
=
\varphi_1+\lambda^{-1}\partial_1\Delta^{-1}r,
\qquad
\varphi_2^0
=
\varphi_2-\partial_2\Delta^{-1}r.
\tag{9}
\]
This is a linear Hodge correction. Unlike projecting matrix values onto the contact plane, it preserves gradient compatibility.
For \(X_0=D\varphi^0\),
\[
d_0=\lambda a_0,\qquad
\|X-X_0\|_p\le C_{p,\lambda}\|r\|_p,\quad p=2,4.
\tag{10}
\]
The corrected potential therefore has a scalar stream representation
\[
\varphi_1^0=\lambda^{-1}\psi_2,\qquad
\varphi_2^0=\psi_1,
\]
so
\[
a_0=\lambda^{-1}\psi_{12},\qquad
b_0=\lambda^{-1}\psi_{22},\qquad
c_0=\psi_{11}.
\tag{11}
\]
The uncontrolled tangent coordinate has become a mixed derivative of one scalar potential.
Step 2: use mixed-derivative interpolation
The required estimate is
\[
\boxed{
\|\psi_{12}\|_{8/3}^{\,2}
\le C\|\psi_{11}\|_2\|\psi_{22}\|_4.
}
\tag{12}
\]
Mixed-derivative interpolation is established harmonic analysis. The proof in the note specifies the exact exponents used rather than importing a stronger endpoint statement. Springer
For completeness, use the analytic family
\[
|D_1|^{2z}|D_2|^{2(1-z)}\psi
\]
on \(0\le\operatorname{Re}z\le1\). On one boundary it is controlled in \(L^2\) by \(\psi_{11}\); on the other, imaginary-power multiplier bounds control it in \(L^4\) by \(\psi_{22}\). Interpolation at \(z=1/2\) yields \(L^{8/3}\). Directional Hilbert transforms recover \(\psi_{12}\). No \(L^\infty\) endpoint is assumed.
Equations (10)–(12) give
\[
\|a_0\|_{8/3}^{\,2}
\le C(\|c\|_2+\|r\|_2)\|X\|_4,
\qquad
\|a_0\|_4\le C\|Y\|_4.
\]
Hence
\[
\int|a_0|^3
\le\|a_0\|_{8/3}^{\,2}\|a_0\|_4.
\]
Controlling \(a^3-a_0^3\) by Hölder gives
\[
\boxed{
|\mathbb E a^3|
\le
C(\|c\|_2+\|r\|_2)\|X\|_4\|Y\|_4.
}
\tag{13}
\]
Step 3: recover the complete cubic
The contact algebra says \(\mathcal C_A-\kappa a^3\) vanishes on \(c=r=0\), while (5) forbids quadratic dependence on \(b\). Therefore
\[
|\mathcal C_A(X)-\kappa a^3|
\le
C(|c|+|r|)|Y|(|b|+|Y|).
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (8), (13), (14) and Hölder proves (1).
Differentiating the cubic along \(R=e_{12}\) removes \(\kappa a^3\). The remaining quadratic is bounded by
\[
C(|c|+|r|)|Y|,
\]
which proves (2).
The important representation change is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{contact-plane defect}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{compatible potential correction}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{mixed derivative}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{quantitative cubic control}.
}
\]
The analytic argument also applies without rotational symmetry whenever a quartic has these contact types and cubic identities. What is specific to the nine-coefficient cone is the proof that these types exhaust its non-polyconvex contacts.
4. A genuine global Jensen theorem, including escaping backgrounds
The estimate has a direct variational consequence.
Theorem — Contact-plane positivity
For each \(F,A,R\) above, there are \(\delta,\eta>0\) such that
\[
\|Y\|_2\le\delta\|s\|_2,
\qquad
\|Y\|_4\le\delta\|s\|_4
\tag{15}
\]
imply
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\!\left[
F(tA+uR+X)-F(tA+uR)
\right]
\ge
\eta\left(\|s\|_4^4+u^2\|s\|_2^2\right)
}
\tag{16}
\]
for every \(t,u\in\mathbb R\).
The affine coefficients need not be bounded. The perturbation may contain arbitrarily many resonant modes and may concentrate, provided the two relative transverse bounds hold.
Proof
Set \(m_j=\mathbb Es^j\) and \(f_0=F(R)>0\). The pure-line gap is
\[
J_F(uR,sR)
=f_0(m_4+4um_3+6u^2m_2).
\]
Since \(m_3^2\le m_2m_4\),
\[
J_F(uR,sR)\ge\frac{f_0}{4}(m_4+u^2m_2).
\tag{17}
\]
Quartic expansion and the two norm assumptions give
\[
|c_X-f_0m_4|\le C\delta m_4,
\]
\[
|b_X:R-4f_0m_3|\le C\delta\sqrt{m_2m_4},
\]
\[
|R:H_XR-12f_0m_2|\le C\delta m_2.
\tag{18}
\]
Now minimize over the possibly escaping coefficient \(t\). The subtracted term is
\[
\frac{(\beta+ug)^2}{2h}.
\]
The new estimates yield
\[
\frac{(\beta+ug)^2}{2h}
\le
K^2\left(\|X\|_4^2\|Y\|_4^2+u^2\|Y\|_2^2\right)
\le
4K^2\delta^2(m_4+u^2m_2).
\tag{19}
\]
If \(h=0\), both \(\beta\) and \(g\) vanish and no division is required.
For sufficiently small \(\delta\), the errors in (18)–(19) are absorbed by (17). This proves (16).
There is also a simpler contact-ray result: sufficiently small \(\|Y\|_4/\|X\|_4\) alone guarantees
\[
\mathbb E[F(tA+X)-F(tA)]
\ge\eta\|X\|_4^4
\qquad\text{for every }t.
\tag{20}
\]
These are global inequalities for actual gradients, not acoustic certificates. They remain restricted in affine-background directions and perturbation geometry; they are not full quasiconvexity of \(F\).

6. Shear straightening is an exact conjugacy, not an unrestricted reduction
For a determinant-preserving contact shear,
\[
\Psi_h(x)=x+A^{-1}a_0h(b_0\cdot x),
\qquad
b_0\cdot A^{-1}a_0=0,
\]
the inverse subtracts the same shear and \(\det D\Psi_h=1\).
For fixed smooth periodic \(h\), the map
\[
w\longmapsto v=w\circ\Psi_h^{-1}
\]
is a bijection of periodic residual potentials. Writing \(S_h=D\Psi_h\), the exact identity is
\[
\int F(A+Rh'+Dw)=\int F((A+Dv)S_h).
\]
Since \(a_0h+w\) ranges over every periodic potential as \(w\) does,
\[
\boxed{
\inf_v\int[F((A+Dv)S_h)-F(A)]
=
\inf_\varphi\int[F(A+D\varphi)-F(A)].
}
\tag{25}
\]
Thus the unrestricted transformed problem is precisely the original problem. Any gain must come from an additional proved estimate, not from treating the transformed residual as independent of \(S_h\).
A tempting stronger assertion fails even for the convex quartic \(F(B)=|B|^4\). Set \(A=I\), \(R=e_{12}\), \(h'=k\cos y\), and choose \(v=-e_1h(y)\). Then
\[
(I+Dv)S_h=I,
\]
but
\[
\boxed{
\int\left[|(I+Dv)S_h|^4-|S_h|^4\right]
=-2k^2-\frac38k^4<0.
}
\tag{26}
\]
The failure is caused by changing the reference to the spatially varying \(F(AS_h)\). It is not a quasiconvexity counterexample.

7. The precise remaining obstruction
The estimate controls a fixed contact direction. Full background minimization can rotate that direction.
In an orthonormal decomposition into a contact-background direction and its complement, write
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}h&k^T\\k&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b=\begin{pmatrix}\beta\\\gamma\end{pmatrix},
\qquad D\succ0.
\]
Then the fully optimized gap is
\[
\boxed{
c-\frac12\gamma^TD^{-1}\gamma
-\frac12
\frac{(\beta-k^TD^{-1}\gamma)^2}
{h-k^TD^{-1}k}.
}
\tag{27}
\]
The new theorem bounds \(\beta\) relative to \(h\), and a specific cross term involving \(R\). It does not establish the corresponding bound for the reduced numerator and denominator in (27). The reduced denominator can be much smaller than \(h\); substituting \(h\) would lose the required control.
This is why neither directional continuity (24) nor contact-plane positivity (16) is being promoted to a full solution.
The strongest new conclusion is therefore:
Differential compatibility quantitatively suppresses cubic contact coupling by transverse fourth-moment mass. This yields global positivity for two unrestricted affine-background directions. Determinant balance cannot supply that suppression, and lower-order transverse norms cannot retain it under concentration.

What is still missing is a compatibility estimate stable under elimination of the complementary background block, retaining concentration and resonant correlations as the minimizing direction moves—or an actual compatible gradient making (27) negative.
The present result advances from exact contact rigidity to a norm-controlled variational theorem. It does not yet establish the universal local-to-global principle.
