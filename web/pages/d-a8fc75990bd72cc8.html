1. RESULT

The cap-uniform information estimate of release093 cannot be deduced from
Poincare, spectral saturation, vanishing field mean, covariance boundedness,
commutativity, or absence of deterministic finite-rank concentration alone.
There are explicit compact log-concave product laws with normalized Poincare
constant one and matrix fields satisfying

  E tr R^2 = E |DR|^2 = 1,
  ||E R||_HS -> 0,
  E tr(Pi R^2) = rank(Pi)/n for every deterministic projection Pi,

whose actual covariance-observation information integral converges, at each
fixed cap L>=1, to

  kappa_L = (1/2)[((L+1)/L) log(L+1) - 1].

Consequently the successive limit diverges as (1/2)log L. All matrix
commutators in this construction vanish. The scalar building block is the
exact gradient of the first Neumann eigenfunction, not an arbitrary spike.
The Poincare spectral formulas are established prior work, credited below.
The exact information limit and its tensorized obstruction are derived here.

Crucial limitation: Cov/P -> 1/4 rather than zero. The example therefore
DOES NOT refute KLS or rule out an estimate exploiting the genuinely anomalous
KLS premise Cov/P -> 0, the heat-smoothed posterior covariance identities,
or the complete high-order hierarchy. Boundary/domain differences are stated
in section 6. This is a proof of failure of an overgeneralized replacement.

2. GENERAL SHARP SCALAR INFORMATION THEOREM

Let ell>=0 be a supplied random variable with E ell=1. Fix L>=1 and set

  f_L(u)=u/(1+u/L),
  Z_T | ell ~ N(0,1+f_L(T ell)),
  J_L(ell)= integral_0^infty I(ell;Z_T) dT/T^2.

Theorem 1. For every such ell,

  0 <= J_L(ell) <= kappa_L.

Moreover, if ell_j has E ell_j=1 and m_j=E sqrt(ell_j)->0, then for each
fixed L,

  J_L(ell_j) -> kappa_L.

More quantitatively there is a finite C_L, depending only on fixed L, such
that

  0 <= kappa_L-J_L(ell) <= C_L (E sqrt(ell))^(1/L).

The exponent here is only a convenient bound, not asserted optimal.

Proof. Write gamma=N(0,1), P_{T,ell}=N(0,1+f_L(T ell)), and Q_T for the
mixture marginal. The relative-entropy chain rule gives exactly

  I(ell;Z_T) = E D(P_{T,ell} || gamma) - D(Q_T || gamma).

All terms are finite for each T, because 1<=Var(P)<=1+L. The first term is

  (1/2) E [f_L(T ell)-log(1+f_L(T ell))].

By Tonelli and u=T ell, its whole-scale integral is kappa_L E ell=kappa_L.
The zero ell case contributes zero. Put g(u)=f_L(u)-log(1+f_L(u)). Since
g(u)/u vanishes at zero and infinity, integration by parts gives

  integral_0^infty g(u) du/u^2
    = integral_0^infty du/[(1+u/L)^2(1+(1+1/L)u)]
    = ((L+1)/L)log(L+1)-1.

For the final equality, set v=u/L and integrate
L/[(1+v)^2(1+(L+1)v)] by partial fractions. Thus

  kappa_L-J_L(ell) = integral_0^infty D(Q_T || gamma) dT/T^2.        (1)

We now bound the deficit. Let p=1+1/(2L), so 1<p<=3/2. The density of
N(0,c) relative to gamma is

  r_c(z)=c^(-1/2) exp((1-1/c)z^2/2),   1<=c<=1+L.

Its c-derivative is r_c(z)(-1/(2c)+z^2/(2c^2)). Its L^p(gamma) norm is
uniformly finite over that compact c interval: the Gaussian integral is
finite because

  p(1-1/c) <= p L/(1+L) = (L+1/2)/(L+1) < 1.

Consequently ||r_c-1||_p <= B_L(c-1). With c=1+f_L(T ell),
f_L(T ell)<=L min(T ell,1), so Minkowski gives

  ||dQ_T/dgamma-1||_p <= B'_L a(T),
  a(T):=E min(T ell,1).

For every 1<p<=2 there is a finite b_p such that
u log u-u+1 <= b_p |u-1|^p for all u>=0. This follows by continuity, the
quadratic Taylor expansion at one, and u log u=o(u^p) at infinity. Hence

  D(Q_T || gamma) <= C_L a(T)^p.

Let m=E sqrt(ell)<=1. The elementary inequalities min(v,1)<=v and
min(v,1)<=sqrt(v) imply

  a(T) <= min(T,m sqrt(T),1).

For 0<m<=1, split the integral at T=m^2 and T=m^(-2). Then

  integral_0^infty a(T)^p dT/T^2
    <= m^(2p-2)/(p-1) + m^(2p-2)/(1-p/2) + m^2
    <= C_p m^(2p-2).

Substitution into (1), with 2p-2=1/L, proves the quantitative claim and
therefore the exact limit. The case m=0 cannot occur when E ell=1. QED.

Theorem 1 is an unbounded information functional whose best mean-only
whole-scale control is logarithmic in the cap. It reveals precisely the
extra strength of release093's LSI upper bound: replacing it with the sharp
mean/Poincare-scale bound leaves the logarithmic lower bound compatible.

3. EXPLICIT EXACT FIRST-GRADIENT EIGENFIELD

Let A>0 and let X_A have density e^(-x)/(1-e^(-A)) on [0,A]. This is a
compact log-concave Lebesgue density. Put

  q_A=pi/A,
  lambda_A=1/4+q_A^2,
  P_A=lambda_A^(-1),
  r_A(x)=sqrt(2(1-e^(-A))/A) e^(x/2) sin(q_A x).

The optimal Poincare constant is P_A. This is Proposition 5 in Roustant,
Barthe, Iooss, arXiv:1612.03689v1, in its same-sign interval case.
For completeness, the operator is A_mu=-d^2/dx^2+d/dx with Neumann boundary
conditions. Under f=e^(x/2)u it is conjugate to -u''+u/4 with Robin boundary
conditions u'+u/2=0. Its zero eigenfunction is u=e^(-x/2); all remaining
eigenvalues are 1/4+(k pi/A)^2, k>=1. For positive eigenvalues the endpoint
conditions yield sin(k pi)=0. Thus the least positive eigenvalue is lambda_A.
Equivalently the first eigenfunction can be written

  phi_A=(-r_A'+r_A)/lambda_A.

Direct differentiation gives -r_A''+r_A'=lambda_A r_A, so phi_A'=r_A.
Because r_A vanishes at both endpoints, phi_A has Neumann boundary
conditions and A_mu phi_A=lambda_A phi_A. Integration by parts gives
E phi_A=0. These statements concern the usual Neumann form on the support;
they do not identify r_A itself as a Neumann-domain function.

Elementary integration yields exactly

  E r_A^2 = 1,
  E (r_A')^2 = lambda_A,
  E r_A' = E r_A = m_A,
  m_A = sqrt(2/A) q_A(1+e^(-A/2))
          / [sqrt(1-e^(-A)) lambda_A] = O(A^(-3/2)).

For the energy calculation, the cross integral of sin(qx)cos(qx) over
[0,A] is zero; both squared trigonometric integrals are A/2.
The mean derivative identity follows from integrating (r_A e^(-x))'.

The mean/variance of the law are

  E X_A = 1 - A/(e^A-1),
  Var X_A = 1 - A^2 e^A/(e^A-1)^2.

Define the centered, normalized variable

  Y_A=(X_A-E X_A)/sqrt(P_A),
  R_A(y)=r_A(sqrt(P_A)y+E X_A).

Then P(Y_A)=1 exactly,

  E R_A^2=E |R_A'|^2=1,
  E R_A=m_A->0,
  E R_A'=sqrt(P_A)m_A->0,
  Cov(Y_A)=Var(X_A)/P_A -> 1/4.

The potential is affine in the interior, so its interior Hessian is zero.
The vector field R_A is the gradient of the normalized first eigenfunction
phi_A/sqrt(P_A), expressed in the y coordinate. Its gradient equation holds
in the interior with boundary conditions inherited from the differentiated
Neumann eigenfunction.

For ell_A=R_A(Y_A)^2, Theorem 1 gives the exact fixed-cap limit

  lim_(A->infty) integral_0^infty I(Y_A;Z_{T,L}) dT/T^2 = kappa_L.    (2)

The information with Y_A equals the information with ell_A because the
observation depends on Y_A only through ell_A. It need not be an invertible
map. For every finite A, ell_A is bounded, so the integrals are finite.

4. INDEPENDENT-PRODUCT TENSORIZATION AND DIFFUSION

Take n independent copies Y_1,...,Y_n of Y_A. Their product log-concave law
has optimal Poincare constant one: tensorization proves the upper bound and
a single-coordinate gap test proves equality. Put

  R_(A,n)(y)=n^(-1/2) diag(R_A(y_1),...,R_A(y_n)).

Then

  E tr R_(A,n)^2=1,
  E |D R_(A,n)|^2=1,
  ||E R_(A,n)||_HS=m_A->0,
  E R_(A,n)^2=I/n.

In particular every deterministic projection Pi of rank k satisfies
E tr(Pi R_(A,n)^2)=k/n. As n->infty, no deterministic fixed-rank subspace
captures nonzero field energy. The base covariance is
(Var X_A/P_A) I, so its operator norm tends to 1/4 and is dimension-free.

The covariance observations have diagonal covariance with independent pairs
(Y_i,Z_i). Therefore mutual information is exactly additive:

  I(Y;Z_(T,L))=n I(Y_A;Z_(T/n,L)).

Substitution u=T/n gives the exact whole-axis identity

  J_L(R_(A,n))=J_L(R_A)                                             (3)

for every finite A,n,L. Thus (2) survives any independent choice n=n(A),
including n(A)->infty, while every fixed-rank projection vanishes.
This strengthens the inherited static diffuse-block scaling check: the field
is random, gradients and exact first-gap saturation are retained, and the
functional is actual KL mutual information, not a homogeneous quadratic probe.

Every R and derivative is diagonal, so matrix commutators vanish exactly.
On its positive support the Sylvester equation D_iR=(H_iR+RH_i)/2 holds with
H_i the diagonal logarithmic derivative of R_A in coordinate i. These H_i
are not uniformly bounded near support endpoints; no such boundedness is
claimed. Their expected squared action on R is finite and equals the unit
Dirichlet energy. Boundary qualifications matter for using a full hierarchy.

5. MATRIX MEAN-ONLY UPPER BOUND (CONSEQUENTIAL WEAKER THEOREM)

Let Y be arbitrary and R(Y) any finite-dimensional PSD field with
E tr R^2<infty. For C_T=I+f_L(T R^2) and Z_T|Y~N(0,C_T), comparison to a
standard Gaussian and the entropy chain rule give

  integral_0^infty I(Y;Z_T) dT/T^2 <= kappa_L E tr R^2.

Proof: the conditional Gaussian entropy relative to the standard Gaussian
is (1/2)tr[f_L(T R^2)-log(I+f_L(T R^2))]. Diagonalize R^2, apply the scalar
whole-axis integral above to each eigenvalue, then use Tonelli. No
commutativity, Poincare, derivative bound, smoothness or log-concavity is
needed. This is the sharp asymptotic universal constant, because the scalar
fields in (2) approach equality. It is not a KLS closing estimate: its
logarithmic cap growth matches the obstruction and the source's lower-bound
order.
