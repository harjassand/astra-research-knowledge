Abstract
We show that logconcave probability measures along the Gaussian cooling
path have thin-shell stability, generalizing the thin-shell theorem.
This result leads to improved complexity for the fundamental problem
of sampling an arbitrary logconcave distribution from a cold start.
For (near-)isotropic logconcave distributions, the complexity is nearly
$n^{2.5}$ , improving the previous bound of  $n^{2.75}$ , and matching
the complexity of the abstract Speedy walk.
1 Introduction
The study of the complexity of logconcave sampling in high dimension
has led to powerful algorithmic techniques and beautiful geometric
phenomena. It is known that an arbitrary logconcave distribution in
$\mathbb{R}^{n}$  with density proportional to  $e^{-V(x)}$  for some convex function
$V$  can be sampled from a warm start11
1
Roughly speaking, a warm start means that the initial distribution
is already close to the target distribution. using  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}C_{\mathsf{PI}}\operatorname{polylog}\frac{1}{\varepsilon})$  evaluations of
$V$  [23]. This is a tight bound for known methods
and conjectured to be the best possible for any algorithm with only
oracle query access to the target density. Here, the Poincaré constant
$C_{\mathsf{PI}}$  is conjectured to be a universal constant for isotropic logconcave
distributions (by the celebrated Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits (KLS)
conjecture [13]) and known to be bounded by  $O(\log n)$
[17].
In this paper, we focus on the problem of logconcave sampling from
a cold start (i.e., no warm start), given access to an evaluation
oracle for  $V$ . For the methods that achieve the best known bounds
from a warm start, such as the  $\mathsf{Ball\ walk}$  [14] and  $\mathsf{\mathsf{In\text{-}and\text{-}Out}}$
[19, 21], initiating them from an arbitrary point
could lead to very high complexity. This is due to the fact that for
points near the boundary, these methods could make a large number
of attempts (queries) before making a single proper step; moreover,
this is unavoidable.  $\mathsf{Hit\text{-}and\text{-}Run}$  has also been shown to have similar complexity
from a warm start [31, 24], and while it has the advantage
of polynomial complexity from an arbitrary interior starting point,
the known analysis incurs an additional factor of dimension, i.e.,
with complexity scaling as  $n^{3}$ .
Sampling is a key ingredient in algorithms for volume computation
[10, 29, 14, 32, 9],
integration, and rounding [30, 12, 21].
To give complete guarantees for the latter problems, researchers have
addressed the challenge of generating a warm start efficiently, with
Kannan, Lovász, and Simonovits [14] giving an algorithm
for convex bodies with complexity roughly  $n^{5}$ . The main idea
is annealing, i.e., a sequence of distributions, each one providing
a warm start for the next. The starting distribution is easy to sample,
and the final distribution is the target. Lovász and Vempala improved
this complexity to  $n^{4}$  [32] and extended the
approach to logconcave distributions [30]. In fact, a
key idea was to use a sequence of logconcave distributions in the
annealing schedule, which provably achieves an improvement over using
a sequence of uniform distributions even for the case of convex bodies
[10, 14].
A decade later, Cousins and Vempala [9] introduced
$\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$ , an accelerated adaptation of the previous annealing schemes,
and combined it with the  $\mathsf{Ball\ walk}:\mathsf{R}_{\infty}\to\mathsf{TV}$ 22
2
Recall the  $q$ -Rényi divergence:  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu):=\frac{q}{q-1}\log\,\lVert\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}\mu}}{{\mathrm{d}\nu}}\rVert_{L^{q}(\nu)}$
for  $q>1$ . It is monotonic in  $q$ , and satisfies  $2\,\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}_{\mathsf{TV}}\leq\mathsf{KL}=\lim_{q\downarrow 1}\mathsf{R}_{q}\leq\mathsf{R}_{2}=\log(1+\chi^{2})\leq\mathsf{R}_{\infty}=\log\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{\nu}\nicefrac{{\mathrm{d}\mu}}{{\mathrm{d}\nu}}$ .
The notation  $\mathsf{R}_{\infty}\to\mathsf{TV}$  means that the sampler requires
an initial distribution with  $\mathsf{R}_{\infty}$ -warmness and its output
distribution is close to the target in total variation (TV) distance. to obtain  $n^{3}$  complexity for well-rounded distributions33
3
A distribution is well-rounded if a level set of constant measure
contains a ball of constant radius, and the trace of its covariance
is  $\widetilde{O}(n)$ . Any near-isotropic logconcave distribution, with
covariance close to identity, is well-rounded.. In particular, this implies that for an arbitrary near-isotropic
distribution, sampling from a cold start has cubic complexity. The
main idea in  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$  was an adaptive schedule, which efficiently accelerated
the rate of progress towards the target, and this cubic complexity
remained the state-of-the-art till 2025. We will discuss this in more
detail presently.
The work of Lee and Vempala [27, 28] suggests
that further improvement might be possible. They showed that the
$\mathsf{Speedy\ walk}$ , an abstract process introduced by [14] and
used only for analysis, has iteration complexity  $n^{2.5}$  (not query
complexity) and that this bound is tight. The  $\mathsf{Speedy\ walk}$  counts only proper
steps of the  $\mathsf{Ball\ walk}$  and not wasted steps, which are needed by any
known implementation; whether this  $n^{2.5}$  iteration complexity
can be matched by an actual algorithm in oracle-query complexity has
remained an elusive question.
For uniform sampling, Kook, Vempala, and Zhang [18, 19]
developed the  $\mathsf{\mathsf{In\text{-}and\text{-}Out}}$  algorithm (also known as the proximal sampler
[26]), using algorithmic diffusion and obtaining
a  $\mathsf{R}_{\infty}\to\mathsf{R}_{q}$  guarantee. Beyond its stronger output
guarantee, the algorithmic connection to heat flow makes tools from
Markov semigroup theory, functional inequalities, and convex geometry
available for its analysis. Subsequently, Kook and Zhang [25]
refined the analysis of  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$  to obtain  $\mathsf{R}_{\infty}$ -warmness
with the same cubic complexity, but warm-start generation was still
a bottleneck toward subcubic complexity from a cold start.  $\mathsf{\mathsf{In\text{-}and\text{-}Out}}$
and the annealing algorithm were extended to general logconcave distributions
through the exponential lifting technique [21], matching
the complexity of uniform sampling.
These ideas were seminal for the first improvement in cold start sampling
since [9]: using a more careful annealing schedule,
refining the bound on the log-Sobolev constant of logconcave probability
measures with compact support, and relying on the stronger and faster
end-to-end sampling guarantee in  $q$ -Rényi divergence, [20, 22]
improved the complexity of cold start sampling to  $n^{2.75}$ , the
first subcubic bound. They also gave an example showing a lower bound
of  $n^{8/3}$  for their general method. The motivation for the present
paper is to understand the best possible complexity of sampling from
a cold start.
Our main algorithmic result here is a refinement of  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$ , which,
together with  $\mathsf{\mathsf{In\text{-}and\text{-}Out}}$ , achieves the complexity of nearly  $n^{2.5}$
for arbitrary near-isotropic logconcave distributions. In other words,
it matches the known iteration complexity of the abstract  $\mathsf{Speedy\ walk}$  without
any overhead. The core idea of the proof is a new stability phenomenon:
while the Poincaré constant of logconcave distributions along the
$\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$  path can vary substantially (and thus using it, as in previous
work, leads to higher complexity), the thin-shell constant is
stable along this path, and this suffices! We will discuss this in
more detail shortly, after presenting the main results precisely.
1.1 Results
Setup.
Let  $V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}$  be a convex function such that
$\mathrm{d}\pi(x)\propto e^{-V(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x$  is a full-dimensional probability
measure in  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ . Following the convention in [21],
let us denote its ground set by  $\mathsf{L}_{g}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:V(x)-\inf V\leq 10n\}$ ,
and standardize the setup by assuming  $B(0,1)\subset\mathsf{L}_{g}$  (via
translation and scaling). For the uniform distribution on a convex
body  $\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , this corresponds to  $B(0,1)\subseteq\mathcal{K}$ . We
assume access to its zeroth-order oracle (i.e., evaluation oracle
for  $V$  telling us  $V(x)$  for a queried point  $x$ ).
We denote the barycenter by  $m:=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}X$ , the covariance matrix
by  $\Sigma:=\operatorname{cov}_{\pi}X=\operatorname{cov}\pi$ , and its operator norm by  $\Lambda:=\lVert\operatorname{cov}\pi\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}$ .
Since the barycenter  $m$  is not necessarily at the origin, we also
define  $L:=\lVert\Sigma+mm^{\mathsf{T}}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}$  and  $R^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\lVert\cdot\rVert^{2}]$ .
Note that  $\Lambda\leq L,\operatorname{tr}\Sigma\leq R^{2}$  in general. For  $t>0$ ,
we define a Gaussian tilt of  $\pi$  as  $\pi_{t}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto\exp(-\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{2t})\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x)$ ,
which can be thought of as the continuous version of  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$ . The stability
proofs themselves use only logconcavity and these parameters, whereas
the ground-set normalization is needed only for the sampling results.
Before we proceed, we define the anisotropic thin-shell constant
$\mathsf{Q}_{n}$  of logconcave measures as the smallest number such that
every isotropic logconcave distribution  $\nu$  in  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$  and every
symmetric matrix  $M$  satisfy
$\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(Z^{\mathsf{T}}MZ)\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,.$
(1.1)
The ordinary thin-shell conjecture [1, 4],
which was proven by Klartag and Lehec [16] in 2025, posits
$\mathsf{Q}_{n}=O(1)$  when  $M=I_{n}$ . Recently, Chen and Klartag showed
that  $\mathsf{Q}_{n}=8$  is sharp when  $M=I_{n}$  [6].
For symmetric  $M$ , it holds that  $\mathsf{Q}_{n}\lesssim\log n$  due
to Klartag’s KLS bound [17].
Result 1: Thin-shell stability along Gaussian tilts.
Consider the family of Gaussian tilts  $\{\pi_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}$  that
interpolates between  $\pi_{0}=\delta_{0}$  and  $\pi_{\infty}=\pi$ .
The first result shows that the variance of  $\lVert X\rVert^{2}$  can be
bounded in terms of the barycenter and covariance matrix of the base
measure  $\pi$ .
Theorem 1.1 (Stability of thin-shell estimates).
Let
$\pi$  be any logconcave probability measure in  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$  with  $m=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}X$
and  $\Sigma=\operatorname{cov}\pi$ , and  $\pi_{t}$  be its Gaussian tilt for  $t>0$ .
Then,
$\sup_{t>0}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})^{2}\,\bigl[\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{2})+\lVert m\rVert^{2}\,\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{2})+m^{\mathsf{T}}\Sigma m\bigr)^{1/2}\bigr]=\widetilde{O}(R^{2}L\wedge R^{3}\Lambda^{1/2})\,.$
In particular, when  $\pi$  is isotropic, this result implies that
the thin-shell constant remains bounded:  $\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})^{2}$ .
Note that this extends the thin-shell bound [16, 6]
to all  $t$  (up to  $\log$  factors). Its stronger version would
be stability of the covariance operator norm, but this is false in
general [3, 22]; see §1.2.1
for more details.
Result 2: Faster warm-start generation for logconcave distributions.
We start with warm-start generation for the uniform distribution on
a convex body. Using the thin-shell stability result, our algorithm
follows the  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$  scheme of [22] with more aggressive
updates to  $\sigma^{2}$ . Roughly speaking, it updates  $\sigma^{2}$
by a factor of  $(1+\sigma^{2}/\mathsf{V}^{1/2})$  in each phase, where
$\mathsf{V}$  is the thin-shell bound on  $\sup_{t>0}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$ .
Using  $\mathsf{\mathsf{In\text{-}and\text{-}Out}}$  for truncated Gaussians, whose query complexity is  $n^{2}\sigma^{2}$
from an  $O(1)$ -warm start in  $\mathsf{R}_{q}$ , doubling a given  $\sigma^{2}$
requires  $n^{2}\sigma^{2}\times\mathsf{V}^{1/2}/\sigma^{2}=n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2}$
queries. Since there are logarithmically many doublings, the total
query complexity for warm-start generation is  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2})$ .
This is simply  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5})$  for isotropic uniform distributions.
Hence, we “algorithmically” achieve the iteration complexity of
the  $\mathsf{Speedy\ walk}$  in [28].
Theorem 1.2 (Faster warm start for uniform sampling).
Let  $\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$  be a convex body with  $B(0,1)\subset\mathcal{K}$  and
$\pi$  uniform on  $\mathcal{K}$ . Algorithm 3.5 outputs
a law  $\nu$  satisfying  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu\mathbin{\|}\pi)\leq 1$  for  $q\geq 1$ ,
using  $\widetilde{O}(q^{1/2}n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2})=\widetilde{O}\bigl(q^{1/2}n^{2}\min\{RL^{1/2},R^{3/2}\Lambda^{1/4}\}\bigr)$
expected membership queries. When  $\pi$  is nearly isotropic (i.e.,
$\operatorname{cov}\pi\approx I_{n}$ ), the total complexity is  $\widetilde{O}(q^{1/2}n^{2.5})$ .
This improves the previously best complexity [20, 22].
It is conceivable that  $n^{2.5}$  is the best possible complexity
for cold-start sampling in light of the  $n^{2.5}$  lower bound for
the iteration complexity of the  $\mathsf{Speedy\ walk}$  [28].
For general logconcave distributions, we use the exponential lift
[21], streamline the tilted Gaussian annealing in
[22], and accelerate it using thin-shell stability.
Theorem 1.3 (Faster warm start for logconcave sampling).
Let  $V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}$  be a convex function, and suppose
that  $\pi\propto e^{-V}$  satisfies  $B(0,1)\subseteq\mathsf{L}_{g}$ .
Given access to an evaluation oracle for  $V$ , Algorithm 4.5
outputs a law  $\nu$  satisfying  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu\mathbin{\|}\pi)\leq 2$  for
$q\geq 1$ , using  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5}+q^{1/2}n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2})$  expected
evaluation queries, which can be bounded as
$\widetilde{O}\bigl(n^{2.5}+q^{1/2}n^{2}\min\{RL^{1/2},R^{3/2}\Lambda^{1/4}\}\bigr)\,.$
When  $\pi$  is nearly isotropic (i.e.,  $\operatorname{cov}\pi\approx I_{n}$ ), the
total complexity is  $\widetilde{O}(q^{1/2}n^{2.5})$ .
1.2 Technical overview
Our proofs are organized around a direct connection between  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$
and thin-shell stability. We first recall the intuition behind  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$
[8, 9], focusing on how thin-shell stability
determines its annealing rate. We then outline the proof of thin-shell
stability and explain how it leads to a faster annealing schedule
for logconcave distributions.
Gaussian cooling and variance of thin shells.
Let  $\pi$  be a logconcave distribution on  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , and consider the
Gaussian-tilted family  $\pi_{\sigma^{2}}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto\exp(-\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})\,\pi(\mathrm{d}x)$ .
$\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$  gradually increases  $\sigma^{2}$ , thereby weakening the effect
of Gaussian tilts and interpolating toward the target  $\pi$ . To understand
the annealing rate, we take a geometric view of  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$ . The fluctuation
scale of  $\lVert X\rVert^{2}$  is  $\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})^{1/2}$ .
Geometrically, when we increase  $\sigma^{2}$ , the next annealing
distribution should overlap substantially with the thin shell of the
previous annealing distribution  $\pi_{\sigma^{2}}$  so that consecutive
distributions are “close” enough for a sampler to mix rapidly toward
the next distribution. Hence, heuristically,  $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{new}}}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$
should lie in  $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]\pm O(\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})^{1/2})$ .

To make this intuition concrete, let  $\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{new}}=(1+\alpha)\,\sigma^{2}$
for a small increment  $\alpha$ . Direct computation gives  $\partial_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]=\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})/(2\sigma^{4})$ .
Thus, the center of the thin shell moves roughly by  $\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^{2}}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$ .
Since we want this change to be smaller than the annulus width  $O(\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})^{1/2})$ ,
this suggests the multiplicative step  $\alpha\lesssim\sigma^{2}/\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})^{1/2}$ .
Note that a larger variance has two competing effects: it gives a
larger annulus, but it moves its center even faster, so  $\sigma^{2}/\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})^{1/2}$
is the ideal balance between these two effects.
This identifies  $\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$  as a fundamental
quantity underlying  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$  and motivates bounding it uniformly. Previous
work bounds it by  $\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\leq 4\sigma^{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$
using the strong logconcavity of  $\pi_{\sigma^{2}}$  and the Poincaré
inequality (or Brascamp–Lieb). Since  $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$
can be made  $O(R^{2})$  by a suitable truncation, the previous bound
yields  $\alpha\asymp\sigma/R$ . On the other hand, a uniform bound
$\sup_{\sigma^{2}>0}\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{\sigma^{2}}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\leq\mathsf{V}$
suggests  $\alpha\asymp\sigma^{2}/\mathsf{V}^{1/2}$ . Once  $\sigma^{2}\geq 1$ ,
this suggests a faster annealing schedule than those in previous work.
Theorem 1.1 establishes such a  $\sigma^{2}$ -independent
uniform bound along  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$ .
1.2.1 Thin-shell stability of Gaussian tilts
To build intuition, consider an isotropic logconcave probability measure
$\nu$  on  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , and let  $\mathrm{d}\nu_{s}\propto e^{-s\,\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu$
denote its Gaussian tilt for  $s\in[0,\infty]$ , where we use the inverse
parameter  $s=1/(2t)$  for the sake of exposition. Prior work [20]
proposed a stronger version of the thin-shell stability conjecture
as part of an attempt to design a faster warm-start algorithm, based
on geometric intuition for stability of the largest eigenvalue of
$\operatorname{cov}\nu_{s}$ . Indeed, by (
PI
),
$\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\leq 4C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\nu_{s})\,\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{s}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]\lesssim_{\log n}\lVert\operatorname{cov}\nu_{s}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\,\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]=n\,\lVert\operatorname{cov}\nu_{s}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\,,$
where the second inequality follows from  $C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\nu_{s})\lesssim\lVert\operatorname{cov}\nu_{s}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\log n$
(i.e., the logarithmic KLS bound) and  $\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{s}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$
(which follows by differentiating  $\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{s}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$  with
respect to  $s$ ). Following the same reasoning, one might conjecture
that  $\lambda_{s}:=\lVert\operatorname{cov}\nu_{s}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}$  should be  $O(\lVert\operatorname{cov}\nu\rVert_{\mathsf{op}})=O(1)$ .
However, it was later disproved by [22] using Bizeul’s
example [3].
A natural alternative is to directly control  $\mathsf{V}_{s}:=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$
without going through (
PI
). Hence, we study the thin-shell
variance  $\mathsf{V}_{s}$  as  $s$  decreases from  $\infty$  to  $0$ . Two
competing factors determine  $\mathsf{V}_{s}$ : (i) the base measure  $\nu$
and (ii) the variance  $\frac{1}{2s}$  of the Gaussian factor. When
$s$  is large (so the Gaussian variance is small), the Gaussian factor
pulls the  $\nu$ -mass toward the origin. In this regime, the effect
of the base measure  $\nu$  should be limited. Hence,  $\nu_{s}$  is
concentrated near the origin, and intuitively,  $\mathsf{V}_{s}$  should
be small. As  $s$  decreases (so the Gaussian variance is larger),
we expect the  $\nu_{s}$ -measure to become more dispersed, which should
in turn increase  $\mathsf{V}_{s}$ . However, when  $s$  is sufficiently
small, the Gaussian weight is  $\Theta(1)$  throughout the effective
support of  $\nu$ , which contains most of the  $\nu$ -measure (e.g.,
$\lVert x\rVert=O(n^{1/2})$ ). In this regime, the Gaussian tilt has little
effect. Hence,  $\nu_{s}$  should be distributed similar to the isotropic
measure  $\nu$ , and we expect  $\mathsf{V}_{s}\approx\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$ .
By the thin-shell theorem, we expect  $\mathsf{V}_{s}=O(n)$ . In summary,
as  $s$  decreases from  $\infty$  to  $0$ , the thin-shell variance
should initially increase as the Gaussian factor weakens. Once the
effect of the original isotropic measure becomes dominant, however,
$\mathsf{V}_{s}$  should remain  $O(n)$ . Thus, a plausible conjecture
is that  $\mathsf{V}_{s}=O(n)$  for all  $s\in[0,\infty]$ .
Decomposition of variance.
For a general logconcave measure  $\pi$  with  $m=\mathbb{E}X$  and  $\Sigma=\operatorname{cov}\pi$ ,
let  $Z=\Sigma^{-1/2}(X-m)$  for  $X\sim\pi$ , and let  $\nu$  be the
law of  $Z$ , which is isotropic and logconcave. Under this change
of variables, the tilted law  $\pi_{t}$  becomes
$\nu_{s}(\mathrm{d}z)\propto\exp(-s\,\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}z+m\rVert^{2})\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)\qquad\text{for }s=(2t)^{-1}\,,$
and  $\lVert X\rVert^{2}=\lVert\Sigma^{1/2}Z+m\rVert^{2}$ . Hence, to bound  $\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$ ,
we consider the following problem: for  $M\succ 0$  and  $u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ ,
define  $q_{u}(x)=\lVert M^{1/2}x+u\rVert^{2}$  under the tilt  $\nu_{s}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto e^{-sq_{u}(x)}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}x)$
for  $s\in[0,\infty]$  (later we can simply take  $M=\Sigma$  and  $u=m$ ).
Write  $b_{s}$  and  $B_{s}$  for the mean and covariance of  $\nu_{s}$ ,
and set  $y_{s}=M^{1/2}b_{s}+u$  and  $M_{s}=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}$ .
Isotropizing  $\nu_{s}$  and applying the definition of  $\mathsf{Q}_{n}$
gives
$\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q_{u}\lesssim\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})+y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\,.$
Hence, it suffices to control  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})$  and the contribution
of the moving mean  $y_{s}$ . The Brascamp–Lieb inequality yields
$M_{s}\preceq(2s)^{-1}\,\mathrm{Id}$ , but applying this bound independently
in every direction loses a factor of  $n$  and becomes vacuous as  $s\downarrow 0$ .
As mentioned earlier, the plausible alternative of proving  $\lVert B_{s}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\lesssim\lVert B_{0}\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}$
is also false: covariance operator-norm stability under Gaussian tilting
is false in general. This suggests controlling the eigenvalues collectively,
rather than the largest eigenvalue.
Collective spectral control of  $M_{s}$ .
Consider first the centered case  $u=0$ . Let  $\lambda_{i}(s)$  be
the eigenvalues of  $M_{s}$ , and let  $P_{s}$  and  $N_{s}$  denote
the total increase and decrease of these eigenvalues relative to  $M_{0}=M$ :
$N_{s}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(s)<\lambda_{i}(0)}\bigl(\lambda_{i}(0)-\lambda_{i}(s)\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad P_{s}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(s)>\lambda_{i}(0)}\bigl(\lambda_{i}(s)-\lambda_{i}(0)\bigr)\,.$
Note that  $\operatorname{tr}M_{s}-\operatorname{tr}M_{0}=P_{s}-N_{s}$ . The score identity (Lemma 2.3)
gives  $\partial_{s}\mathbb{E}_{s}[X^{\mathsf{T}}MX]=-\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q_{u}$ , which relates
$N_{s}$ ,  $P_{s}$ , and  $\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$  as follows:  $\Delta_{s}:=\int^{s}_{0}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{r}}q_{u}\,\mathrm{d}r=N_{s}-P_{s}-\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$ ,
so  $P_{s}+\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq N_{s}$ . Also, differentiating  $\lambda_{i}(s)$
along the same path gives  $\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}(Z^{2}_{s,i},q_{u})$ ,
where  $Z_{s}$  is the random variable for isotropization of  $\nu_{s}$ .
The log-spectrum estimate yields  $N^{2}_{s}\leq s\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,\Delta_{s}$ .
Since  $\Delta_{s}\leq N_{s}$ , both spectral gain and squared mean
displacement are  $O(s)$ . Combining these relations yields  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})$
and  $y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$  with
$\mathsf{Q}_{n}\lesssim\log n$  (Lemma 2.5). Note
that we avoid the naïve factor- $n$  loss: instead of using the
same worst-case bound on every eigenvalue, we control the  $\ell_{2}$ -norm
of the entire log-spectrum collectively. The detailed proof appears
in §2.1.
Extension to the shifted case.
When  $u\neq 0$ , the same argument starts from  $y_{0}=u$ , and the
identity becomes  $\Delta_{s}=N_{s}-P_{s}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}-\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$ .
This change adds  $O(\lVert u\rVert^{2}/s)$  to  $\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q_{u}$ , which
is useful but vacuous near the untilted endpoint (i.e.,  $s=0$ ). At
that endpoint, the same decomposition and the quadratic-form estimate
directly bound  $\operatorname{Var}_{\nu}q_{u}$  in terms of  $G:=\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu$ .
Moreover, the score identity and reverse Hölder inequality for
quadratic polynomials give  $|\partial_{s}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q_{u}|\lesssim(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q_{u})^{3/2}$ .
Solving this Grönwall-type inequality, we use the resulting bound
up to  $s_{0}\asymp[(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)G]^{-1/2}$  and the spectral estimate
thereafter. Concatenating the two regimes yields the additional term
$\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}$  in Theorem 2.1. Putting
these together, we obtain the bound  $\mathsf{V}=\widetilde{O}(R^{2}L\wedge R^{3}\Lambda^{1/2})$ .

Special case: uniform sampling.
For a convex body containing the unit ball, we can sample the initial

General case: logconcave sampling.
Given a convex potential  $V$  and an evaluation oracle for it, we
use the lifting technique [21]; instead of sampling
directly from  $\pi\propto e^{-V}$ , we sample from a density proportional
to  $e^{-nt}\,\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{K}}(x,t)$ , where  $\mathcal{K}=\{(x,t):V(x)\leq nt\}$  is
the epigraph of  $V/n$ . Its  $X$ -marginal is exactly proportional
to  $e^{-V(x)}$ . Suitable truncation  $\bar{\mathcal{K}}$  of  $\mathcal{K}$  contains
at least half of the lifted measure and ensures a finite log-Sobolev
constant. Since the truncated  $X$ -marginal has density at most twice
that of  $\pi$ , its parameters  $R,L,\Lambda$  are bounded by universal
multiples of those of  $\pi$ . Hence, the required thin-shell upper
bound increases by at most a universal factor.
On the truncated lift, we use the two-parameter annealing distribution
from [21]:
$\mu_{\sigma^{2},\rho}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}t)\propto\exp\bigl(-\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}-\rho t\bigr)\,\mathds{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\,.$
Using the proximal sampler for this distribution [22],
we follow the path  $(n^{-1},1)\to(n^{-1},n)\to(\sigma^{2}_{\text{last}},n)$
in the  $(\sigma^{2},\rho)$ -plane, streamlining the annealing schedules
proposed in [20, 22]. The first segment changes
only  $\rho$  while keeping the strongly logconcave Gaussian component
fixed. Once  $\rho=n$ , the  $X$ -marginal is exactly the Gaussian tilt
of the truncated target, so the second segment can reuse the schedule
and theory developed for uniform sampling.
For the first phase  $(n^{-1},1)\to(n^{-1},n)$ , we establish a new
closeness bound. As in the analysis of the  $\mathsf{R}_{q}$  bound between
consecutive annealing distributions, we have to bound  $\operatorname{Var}_{(\sigma^{2},\rho)}T:=\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{\sigma^{2},\rho}}T$ .
Varentropy of the joint logconcave density [11],
together with the Poincaré bound for its strongly logconcave  $X$ -marginal,
gives  $\operatorname{Var}_{(n^{-1},\rho)}T\lesssim n/\rho^{2}$  for  $1\leq\rho\leq n$ .
Hence, for  $q_{0}=\widetilde{\Theta}(1)$ , we multiply  $\rho$  by
$1+\Theta(1/\sqrt{q_{0}\,(q_{0}+n)})$  at each phase. There are  $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{n})$
such phases, and each costs  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2})$  queries (since the proximal
sampler costs  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}(\sigma^{2}\vee 1))$  from an  $O(1)$ -warm
start), using  $\widetilde{O}(n^{5/2})$  queries. We maintain the same base
order  $q_{0}$  while increasing  $\sigma^{2}$  to  $1$ . Thereafter,
using the hypercontractivity of the proximal sampler again (to boost
the Rényi order from  $q_{0}$  to  $q$ ), we use the thin-shell update
up to  $\sigma^{2}_{\text{last}}$ . These Gaussian-annealing phases
cost  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}})$  queries as in the uniform case.
Finally, data processing under  $(x,t)\mapsto x$  and the constant-mass
truncation transfer the lifted warm start to the original target.
1.3 Preliminaries
We use the same symbol for a distribution and its density. Both  $a\lesssim b$
and  $a=O(b)$  mean  $a\leq cb$  for a universal constant  $c>0$ .  $a=\Omega(b)$
means  $a\gtrsim b$ , and  $a\asymp b$  means  $a=O(b)$  and  $a=\Omega(b)$ .
Lastly,  $a=\widetilde{O}(b)$  means  $a=O(b\operatorname{polylog}b)$ . For a positive
semidefinite matrix  $\Sigma$ ,  $\lVert\Sigma\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}$  denotes the
operator norm of  $\Sigma$ . We use  $a\vee b$  and  $a\wedge b$  to

Logconcavity.
We call a function  $f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)$  logconcave if  $-\log f$
is convex in  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , and call a probability measure  $\pi$  (or distribution)
logconcave if it has a logconcave density with respect to Lebesgue
measure. We assume that all logconcave distributions considered in
this work are full-dimensional44

For  $t\geq 0$ ,  $\pi$  is called  $t$ -strongly logconcave if
$-\log\pi$  is  $t$ -strongly convex (i.e.,  $-\log\pi-\frac{t}{2}\,|\cdot|^{2}$
is convex). Logconcavity is preserved under multiplication, and a

Probability.

2 Thin-shell stability of Gaussian tilts
In this section, we show that the thin-shell variance remains bounded
for Gaussian tilts; motivated by (1.1), a natural anisotropic
version of the conjecture would be  $\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}(X^{\mathsf{T}}MX)\lesssim_{\log n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})$
for every  $s\geq 0$ . In §2.1, we provide
the proof of this conjecture, and in §2.2,
we provide the following generalization to shifted quadratic functions:
Theorem 2.1 (Shifted anisotropic quadratic tilt).
Let
$\nu$  be an isotropic logconcave probability measure on  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ ,
$M\succeq 0$ , and  $u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ . Set  $q_{u}(x)=\lVert M^{1/2}x+u\rVert^{2}$
and define the probability measure  $\nu_{s}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto e^{-sq_{u}(x)}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}x)$
for  $s\geq 0$ . Then, for every  $s\geq 0$ ,
$\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q_{u}\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})^{2}\,\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu}\bigr)\,.$
Using this result, we readily obtain the thin-shell stability result
(Theorem 1.1) needed for faster  $\mathsf{Gaussian\ cooling}$ .
2.1 Proof idea: the centered case
We first present the proof in the centered case of Theorem 2.1
(i.e.,  $u=0$ ).
Theorem 2.2 (Centered anisotropic quadratic tilt).
Let  $\nu$  be an isotropic logconcave probability measure on  $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ ,
and  $\nu_{s}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto e^{-s\,\langle x,Mx\rangle}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}x)$  for  $s\geq 0$
and  $M\succeq 0$ . Then,
$\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}(X^{\mathsf{T}}MX)\leq(2\mathsf{Q}^{2}_{n}+6\mathsf{Q}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\qquad\text{for every }s\geq 0\,.$
(2.1)
We first prove the theorem for  $M\succ 0$ . Denote the barycenter and
covariance of  $\nu_{s}$  by  $b_{s}=\mathbb{E}_{s}X$  and  $B_{s}=\operatorname{cov}_{s}X$ .
Under  $x\mapsto y=T(x):=M^{1/2}x$ , the pushforward  $Y_{s}\sim T_{\#}\nu_{s}$
has barycenter  $y_{s}=M^{1/2}b_{s}$  and covariance  $M_{s}=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}$ .
Isotropy of  $\nu$  gives  $b_{0}=0$  and  $B_{0}=\mathrm{Id}$ , so  $y_{0}=0$
and  $M_{0}=M$ . Although  $\nu$  is centered, the quadratic tilt  $\nu_{s}$
need not preserve its mean.
Controlling the covariance and the moving mean.
Isotropize  $\nu_{s}$  by  $Z:=M^{-1/2}_{s}M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})$  for  $X\sim\nu_{s}$ .
We now bound the variance of the target quadratic form  $q(x):=x^{\mathsf{T}}Mx$ .
We have the decomposition
$q(x)=x^{\mathsf{T}}Mx=(x-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}M(x-b_{s})+2b^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}Mx-b^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}Mb_{s}=z^{\mathsf{T}}M_{s}z+2y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M^{1/2}_{s}z+b^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}Mb_{s}\,.$
Hence,  $\mathbb{E}_{s}q=\operatorname{tr}M_{s}+\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$  and  $q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q=(Z^{\mathsf{T}}M_{s}Z-\operatorname{tr}M_{s})+2y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M^{1/2}_{s}Z.$
Since  $\operatorname{Var}_{s}q=\lVert q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\nu_{s})}$ , the triangle
inequality in  $L^{2}$  gives
$\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{s}q}\leq\lVert z^{\mathsf{T}}M_{s}z-\operatorname{tr}M_{s}\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu_{s})}+2\,\lVert y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M^{1/2}_{s}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\nu_{s})}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})}+2\sqrt{y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}}\,.$
(2.2)
Here, (1.1) bounds the first term by  $[\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})]^{1/2}$ ,
while  $\lVert y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M^{1/2}_{s}z\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\nu_{s})}=\mathbb{E}_{s}[(y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M^{1/2}_{s}Z)^{2}]=y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}$ .
Therefore, the proof reduces to controlling (i) the covariance
contribution  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})$  and (ii) the moving-mean contribution
$y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}$ .
We now bound these two terms. By Brascamp–Lieb,  $M_{s}\preceq(2s)^{-1}\mathrm{Id}$ ,
so each eigenvalue  $(\lambda_{i}(s))_{i\in[n]}$  of  $M_{s}$  is bounded
by  $(2s)^{-1}$ . However, applying this bound directly to  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})$
would lose a factor of the dimension  $n$  and would blow up as  $s\downarrow 0$ .
To refine the analysis, we track changes in the eigenvalues collectively
relative to  $M_{0}=M$  and relate their total increase to their total
decrease, rather than applying the same worst-case bound to all  $n$
eigenvalues. To make this precise, let us define the total spectral
“loss” and “gain” by
$N_{s}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(s)<\lambda_{i}(0)}\bigl(\lambda_{i}(0)-\lambda_{i}(s)\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad P_{s}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}(s)>\lambda_{i}(0)}\bigl(\lambda_{i}(s)-\lambda_{i}(0)\bigr)\,.$
Note that  $\operatorname{tr}M_{s}-\operatorname{tr}M_{0}=P_{s}-N_{s}$ .
We first track how much  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})$  has changed from  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})$ :
since  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})-\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})=\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}(s)^{2}-\lambda_{i}(0)^{2})$ ,
$\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})-\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\leq\sum_{\lambda_{i}(s)>\lambda_{i}(0)}\bigl(\lambda_{i}(s)^{2}-\lambda_{i}(0)^{2}\bigr)\underset{(i)}{\leq}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{\lambda_{i}(s)>\lambda_{i}(0)}\bigl(\lambda_{i}(s)-\lambda_{i}(0)\bigr)=\frac{P_{s}}{s}\,,$
where  $(i)$  follows from  $\lambda_{i}(s)+\lambda_{i}(0)\leq 2\lambda_{i}(s)\leq s^{-1}$
for an eigenvalue with  $\lambda_{i}(s)>\lambda_{i}(0)$ . Thus, the
two target terms are bounded as
$\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})+\frac{P_{s}}{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\leq\frac{\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}}{2s}\,.$
(2.3)
Hence, it suffices to bound  $P_{s}$  and  $\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$  in terms
of  $s$ . An elementary identity couples  $P_{s}$ ,  $N_{s}$ , and  $\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$ .
Coupling  $P_{s}$ ,  $N_{s}$ , and  $\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$ .

Since  $M_{s}=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}$ , (2.8)

$\partial_{s}M_{s}=-M^{1/2}\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}},q\bigr)\,M^{1/2}\,.$
(2.9)
By perturbation theory for symmetric matrix paths [15, Theorem 5.4 and Theorem 6.8],
the eigenvalues  $\lambda_{i}(s)$  of  $M_{s}$  may be labeled differentiably,
and at each  $s$ , one may choose an orthonormal eigenbasis  $(u_{i})^{n}_{i=1}$
of  $M_{s}$  such that  $\partial_{s}\lambda_{i}(s)=u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}(\partial_{s}M_{s})u_{i}$ .
Hence, by (2.9),
$\partial_{s}\lambda_{i}(s)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl\{\bigl(u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})\bigr)^{2},q\bigr\}\,.$
(2.10)
Using  $M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})=M^{1/2}_{s}Z$  and  $M^{1/2}_{s}u_{i}=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(s)}\,u_{i}$ ,
$u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}M^{1/2}(X-b_{s})=u^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}M^{1/2}_{s}Z=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(s)}\,Z_{i}\,.$
Substituting this into (2.10) yields  $\partial_{s}\lambda_{i}(s)=-\lambda_{i}(s)\operatorname{cov}_{s}(Z^{2}_{i},q)$ ,
which completes the proof.
∎

is a Gaussian tilt of an isotropic logconcave probability measure

are its barycenter and covariance, respectively.
Lemma 2.5 (Properties of  $M_{s}$  and  $y_{s}$ ).
The barycenter  $y_{s}=M^{1/2}b_{s}$  and covariance  $M_{s}=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}$
satisfy the following bounds for  $s\geq 0$ :
•
$P_{s}+\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq N_{s}\leq s\cdot\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$
•
$\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})$
•
$y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$
Proof.
For every unit vector  $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ , by Cauchy–Schwarz followed
by the definition of  $\mathsf{Q}_{n}$  for the quadratic form  $\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}Z^{2}_{i}$ ,
$\Bigl|\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\,\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)\Bigr|=\Bigl|\operatorname{cov}_{s}\Bigl(\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}Z^{2}_{i},q\Bigr)\Bigr|\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}}\,\lVert\alpha\rVert\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q}\,.$
Hence,  $\sum_{i}[\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)]^{2}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}q$ .
Substituting this into (2.6),
$\sum_{i}\log^{2}\frac{\lambda_{i}(s)}{\lambda_{i}(0)}\leq s\int^{s}_{0}\sum_{i}\bigl(\partial_{r}\log\lambda_{i}(r)\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq s\mathsf{Q}_{n}\int^{s}_{0}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{r}}q\,\mathrm{d}r=s\mathsf{Q}_{n}\Delta_{s}\,.$
(2.11)
Using  $\Delta_{s}\leq N_{s}$  and substituting this into (2.5),

Hence,
$(P_{s}+\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq)N_{s}\leq s\cdot\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}\,.$
Thus,  $P_{s},N_{s},\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}=O(s)$  as desired. Substituting
this into (2.3), we obtain  $\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})$
and  $y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,\mathsf{Q}_{n}$ .
∎

Remark 2.6 (Collective control of eigenvalues).
The proof controls the eigenvalues

considering the total continuous change in the trace (specifically,
$N_{s}$  and  $P_{s}$  terms), we obtain a useful bound. If we attempt

but summing them up in a naïve way would lead to a factor of  $n$ .
A further consequence is the second item of Lemma 2.5
(i.e., the stability of the Schatten  $2$ -norm along the Gaussian

$\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{0})\,(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,.$
As noted earlier, this type of bound does not hold for individual

2.2 Stability under shifted Gaussian tilts
We now consider the shifted quadratic:  $q_{u}(x)=\lVert M^{1/2}x+u\rVert^{2}$ .
In this case, the initial transformed mean is  $y_{0}=u$ , and we retain
the same variance decomposition:
$\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_{s}q}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})}+2\sqrt{y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}}\,.$
(2.12)
The identity becomes  $\Delta_{s}=N_{s}-P_{s}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}-\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$ ,
so  $P_{s}+\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq N_{s}$  is replaced by the weaker bound
$P_{s},\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert u\rVert^{2}+N_{s}$ . Applying Brascamp–Lieb
again, this extra  $\lVert u\rVert^{2}$  term yields  $\lVert u\rVert^{2}/s$ . The
resulting bound is useful for moderate and large  $s$  but vacuous
at the untilted endpoint (i.e.,  $s=0$ ).
The proof follows the same steps and separately treats the regime
near  $s=0$ , solving the Grönwall-type inequality  $|v_{s}^{\prime}|\lesssim v^{3/2}_{s}$ .
Proof of Theorem 2.1.
Assume  $M\succ 0$  and
abbreviate  $q=q_{u}$ . Define  $v_{s}:=\operatorname{Var}_{s}q$ ,  $G:=\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu$ ,
and
$b_{s}:=\mathbb{E}_{s}X\,,\qquad B_{s}:=\operatorname{cov}_{s}X\,,\qquad M_{s}:=M^{1/2}B_{s}M^{1/2}\,,\qquad y_{s}:=M^{1/2}b_{s}+u\,.$
By isotropy, we have  $B_{0}=\mathrm{Id}$ ,  $M_{0}=M$ , and  $y_{0}=u$ .
By Lemma 2.3, we similarly have
$(\mathbb{E}_{s}q)^{\prime}=-v_{s}\,,\qquad\Delta_{s}:=\mathbb{E}_{0}q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q=\int^{s}_{0}v_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\,,\qquad B_{s}^{\prime}=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}\bigl((X-b_{s})(X-b_{s})^{\mathsf{T}},q\bigr)\,.$
Let  $\lambda_{i}(s)$  be an absolutely continuous labeling of the
eigenvalues of  $M_{s}$ . The calculation in the preceding subsection
gives the same identity:  $\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)=-\operatorname{cov}_{s}(Z^{2}_{i},q)$ .
Hence,
$\sum_{i}\bigl(\partial_{s}\log\lambda_{i}(s)\bigr)^{2}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}v_{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{i}\log^{2}\frac{\lambda_{i}(s)}{\lambda_{i}(0)}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}s\Delta_{s}\,.$
Define  $N_{s}$  and  $P_{s}$  as before. Since  $\mathbb{E}_{s}q=\operatorname{tr}M_{s}+\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}$ ,
$\Delta_{s}=N_{s}-P_{s}+\lVert u\rVert^{2}-\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta_{s},P_{s},\lVert y_{s}\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert u\rVert^{2}+N_{s}\,.$
Then,  $N_{s}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s\Delta_{s}}\leq\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s\,(\lVert u\rVert^{2}+N_{s})}$ .
Solving this,
$N_{s}\leq\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s+\lVert u\rVert\sqrt{\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})\,s}\,.$
Combining this with the bounds on  $P_{s}$  and  $y_{s}$  gives
$\operatorname{tr}(M^{2}_{s})\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\frac{\lVert u\rVert^{2}}{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y^{\mathsf{T}}_{s}M_{s}y_{s}\lesssim\mathsf{Q}_{n}\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\frac{\lVert u\rVert^{2}}{s}\,.$
Substituting these bounds into (2.12),
$v_{s}\lesssim(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+\mathsf{Q}^{2}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,\frac{\lVert u\rVert^{2}}{s}\,.$
(2.13)
The last term is the only singular term introduced by the shift, and
it becomes vacuous as  $s$  goes to  $0$ . However, at  $s=0$ , the same
decomposition gives  $v_{0}\leq(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)\,G$ . To combine the two
regimes, we propagate the endpoint bound to small  $s$ .
Handling small  $s$ .
By Lemma 2.3,  $v_{s}^{\prime}=-\mathbb{E}_{s}[(q-\mathbb{E}_{s}q)^{3}]$ .
By the polynomial reverse Hölder inequality [5, Theorem 7],
$|v_{s}^{\prime}|\lesssim v^{3/2}_{s}$ . Let  $\bar{v}:=(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)\,G$
and  $s_{0}=c\bar{v}^{-1/2}$  for sufficiently small  $c>0$ . Since
$M\succ 0$  and  $\nu_{s}$  is full-dimensional,  $v_{s}>0$ . Integrating
the differential inequality, for  $s\in[0,s_{0}]$ , we have  $v_{s}\leq 4\bar{v}$ .
Since  $u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu\leq\lVert u\rVert^{2}\lVert M\rVert_{\mathsf{op}}\leq\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}$ ,
we have  $G=\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+u^{\mathsf{T}}Mu\leq\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}$ .
Hence,  $v_{s}\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,(\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G})$ .
For  $s\geq s_{0}$ , the pointwise bound (2.13)
yields
$v_{s}\lesssim(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+\mathsf{Q}^{2}_{n})\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})\,\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{(\mathsf{Q}_{n}+4)\,G}\,.$
Therefore,
$v_{s}\lesssim(1+\mathsf{Q}_{n})^{2}\,\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(M^{2})+\lVert u\rVert^{2}\sqrt{G}\bigr)\,.$
This proves the claim when  $M\succ 0$ . For  $M\succeq 0$ , apply the
result to  $M+\varepsilon I_{n}$  and let  $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ ;
dominated convergence follows from the finite fourth moment of  $\nu$ .
∎

3 Faster annealing for uniform sampling
In §3.1, we relate variance stability
to Rényi divergence between consecutive Gaussian annealing distributions.
This determines the step size in the Gaussian variance  $\sigma^{2}$
in our annealing scheme. Then, in §3.2, we give
a faster version of Gaussian cooling, proving Theorem 1.2.
To go through the main calculations, the reader might finding it convenient
to think of  $q$  as a fixed constant, say  $2$ .
3.1 Rényi closeness of annealing distributions
For a probability measure  $\nu$ , let  $\nu_{s}(\mathrm{d}x)\propto e^{-s\,\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}x)$
for  $s\geq 0$ , and  $F(s):=\log\int e^{-s\,\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\,\nu(\mathrm{d}x)$ .
Differentiating gives  $F^{\prime}(s)=-\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{s}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$  and  $F^{\prime\prime}(s)=\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$ .
Recall  $q$ -Rényi divergence:  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu\mathbin{\|}\nu):=\frac{1}{q-1}\log\int(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\nu})^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu$
for  $q>1$ .
Lemma 3.1.
Let  $q>1$  and  $s\geq s^{\prime}\geq 0$ . Then,
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{s^{\prime}})\leq\frac{q}{2}\,(s-s^{\prime})^{2}\sup_{t\in[s^{\prime},qs-(q-1)\,s^{\prime}]}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\,.$
(3.1)
Proof.
Let  $Z(s):=\int e^{-s\,\lVert x\rVert^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)$ . For  $\Delta:=s-s^{\prime}$ ,
the density ratio is  $\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s^{\prime}}}(x)=\frac{Z(s^{\prime})}{Z(s)}\,e^{-\Delta\,\lVert x\rVert^{2}}$ ,
and
$\int\bigl(\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{s^{\prime}}}\bigr)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{s^{\prime}}=\frac{Z(s^{\prime})^{q-1}\,Z\bigl(q\,s-(q-1)\,s^{\prime}\bigr)}{Z(s)^{q}}\,.$
Thus,
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{s^{\prime}})=\frac{F\bigl(s+(q-1)\,\Delta\bigr)-qF(s)+(q-1)\,F(s-\Delta)}{q-1}\,.$
Let  $I:=[s^{\prime},s+(q-1)\Delta]$  and  $V_{I}:=\sup_{t\in I}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})=\sup_{t\in I}F^{\prime\prime}(t)$ .
By Taylor expansion,
$\displaystyle F\bigl(s+(q-1)\,\Delta\bigr)$
$\displaystyle\leq F(s)+(q-1)\,\Delta F^{\prime}(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,(q-1)^{2}\Delta^{2}\,,$
$\displaystyle F(s-\Delta)$
$\displaystyle\leq F(s)-\Delta F^{\prime}(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,\Delta^{2}\,.$
Substituting these estimates into  $\mathsf{R}_{q}$ ,
$\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu_{s}\mathbin{\|}\nu_{s^{\prime}})$
$\displaystyle\leq\frac{F(s)+(q-1)\,\Delta F^{\prime}(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,(q-1)^{2}\Delta^{2}-qF(s)+(q-1)\,\bigl(F(s)-\Delta F^{\prime}(s)+\frac{V_{I}}{2}\,\Delta^{2}\bigr)}{q-1}$
$\displaystyle=\frac{qV_{I}}{2}\,\Delta^{2}\,,$
which completes the proof.
∎
This result does not require logconcavity of  $\nu$ . For  $\sigma^{2}>0$ ,
define  $\mu_{\sigma^{2}}:=\nu_{1/(2\sigma^{2})}$ . The preceding lemma
gives the following bounds; apply Lemma 3.1 with
$s=(2\sigma^{2})^{-1}$  and  $s^{\prime}=(2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha))^{-1}$ ,
and take  $s^{\prime}=0$  for the second bound.
Corollary 3.2 (Closeness).
Let  $\mathsf{V}:=\sup_{s\geq 0}\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{s}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})$ .
For every  $q>1$ ,  $\sigma^{2}>0$ , and  $\alpha>0$ ,
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)})\leq\frac{q\mathsf{V}}{8\sigma^{4}}\,\bigl(\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\bigr)^{2}\leq\frac{q\mathsf{V}\alpha^{2}}{8\sigma^{4}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbin{\|}\nu)\leq\frac{q\mathsf{V}}{8\sigma^{4}}\,.$
In particular, the update  $\sigma^{2}\mapsto\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)$
with  $\alpha\lesssim\sigma^{2}/\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}$  maintains  $O(1)$ -closeness,
and  $\sigma^{2}\gtrsim\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}$  ensures terminal  $O(1)$ -warmness.
Remark 3.3 (Comparison with prior closeness results).
[20, Lemma 4.5] bounds  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)})$
by  $qD^{2}\alpha^{2}/\sigma^{2}$  for compactly supported logconcave
$\nu$  with support diameter  $D$ . Later, [22, Lemma 4.2]
gives the bound  $q^{2}R^{2}\alpha^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})$  for the same
quantity when  $\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]=R^{2}\leq D^{2}$ .
Our estimate (3.1) yields an improved bound.
For every  $t>0$ , the measure  $\nu_{t}$  is  $2t$ -strongly logconcave.
By Brascamp–Lieb or (
PI
) with  $C_{\mathsf{PI}}(\nu_{t})\leq 1/(2t)$ ,
$\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{t}}(\lVert X\rVert^{2})\leq\frac{1}{2t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}}[4\,\lVert X\rVert^{2}]=\frac{2}{t}\,\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]\leq\frac{2R^{2}}{t}\,,$
where the last inequality follows from  $\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[\lVert X\rVert^{2}]$ .
For  $s=(2\sigma^{2})^{-1}$  and  $s^{\prime}=[2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)]^{-1}$
with  $t\geq s^{\prime}$ , we have
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\mu_{\sigma^{2}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}(1+\alpha)})\leq\frac{q}{2}\,\bigl(\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)}\bigr)^{2}\,\frac{2R^{2}}{s^{\prime}}=\frac{qR^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}\,(1+\alpha)}\,.$
This improves the  $q^{2}$ -dependence of the second bound to  $q$ .
It also recovers the compact-support bound in the present convex-body

3.2 Gaussian cooling through thin-shell stability
Let  $\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$  be a convex body with  $B(0,1)\subset\mathcal{K}$ , and
let  $\pi$  be uniform on  $\mathcal{K}$ . Fix a Rényi order  $q$  and take
$\mathsf{V}=\widetilde{O}(R^{2}L\wedge R^{3}\sqrt{\Lambda})$  by Theorem 1.1.
We propose the following annealing schedule:
•
Let  $\sigma^{2}_{\textup{start}}:=1/n$  and  $\sigma^{2}_{\textup{last}}:=\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}$
be the initial and terminal variance parameters.
•
At phase  $i$ , the annealing distribution is  $\mu_{\sigma^{2}_{i}}\propto\exp\{-\lVert x\rVert^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}_{i})\}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{K}}$ .
For  $\sigma^{2}_{i}\in[\sigma^{2}_{\textup{start}},\sigma^{2}_{\textup{last}}]$ ,
define  $\sigma^{2}_{i+1}$  recursively by

(3.2)

$h>0$  and threshold  $\tau$ , set  $s:=\sigma^{2}/(h+\sigma^{2})\in(0,1)$ .
One iteration of  $\mathsf{PS}_{\textup{Gauss}}$  consists of the following two steps:
•
[Forward] Draw  $y_{k+1}\sim\mathsf{N}(x_{k},hI_{n})$ .
•
[Backward] Repeatedly draw  $x_{k+1}\sim\mathsf{N}(sy_{k+1},shI_{n})$
until  $x_{k+1}\in\mathcal{K}$ . If the number of rejection trials exceeds  $\tau$ ,
declare failure.
This sampler follows the annealing path efficiently because the previous
annealing distribution is an  $O(1)$ -warm start for the next target
distribution.

$Z=16NM_{2}/\eta$ . Assume  $M_{q_{0}}\leq 10$  and  $q_{0}\geq 1+\log\tau$ .
•
The latter condition is ensured by  $q_{0}\geq 2\vee O(\log\frac{N}{\eta})$ .
•
With probability at least  $1-\eta$ ,  $\mathsf{PS}_{\textup{Gauss}}$  completes all  $N$

has law  $\nu$  satisfying  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}})\leq\varepsilon+2\log\frac{1}{1-\eta}$ ,
using  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\sigma^{2}\log\frac{q}{\varepsilon}\log^{5}\frac{1}{\eta})$
membership queries in expectation. The corresponding uncapped chain
(i.e.,  $\tau=\infty$ ) has output law  $\bar{\nu}$  satisfying  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\bar{\nu}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{\sigma^{2}})\leq\varepsilon$ .

at stage  $i$ . Let  $J$  be the first stage with  $\sigma^{2}_{J}\geq\sigma^{2}_{\textup{last}}$ .

we first consider the ideal truncated-Gaussian sampler where
every threshold is removed (i.e.,  $\tau=\infty$ ) and then bound the
bias introduced by the cap. Let  $\bar{\nu}_{i}$  be its output law
after phase  $i$  (so  $\bar{\nu}_{0}=\mu_{t_{0}}$ ).
The uncapped mixing estimate in Proposition 3.4,
applied with input order  $q$ , output order  $2q$ , and accuracy  $1/20$ ,
ensures  $\mathsf{R}_{2q}(\bar{\nu}_{i}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{t_{i}})\leq\frac{1}{20}$
for  $0\leq i\leq J$ . For  $i<J$ , the update is  $\alpha_{i}=t_{i}/\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}$ ,
so Corollary 3.2 gives  $\mathsf{R}_{2q}(\mu_{t_{i}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{t_{i+1}})\leq 1/4$ .
By the weak triangle inequality,
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\bar{\nu}_{i}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{t_{i+1}})\leq\frac{q-1/2}{q-1}\,\mathsf{R}_{2q}(\bar{\nu}_{i}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{t_{i}})+\mathsf{R}_{2q-1}(\mu_{t_{i}}\mathbin{\|}\mu_{t_{i+1}})\leq\frac{13}{40}\,.$
Hence, the warmness satisfies  $M_{q}<e$ . The schedule gives  $J=O(n\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}})$
and  $\sigma^{2}_{i}\leq 2\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}$ , so  $\log\tau_{i}\lesssim\log(en^{3}q(1\vee\mathsf{V}))$ .
Since  $q\geq q_{\min}$ , choosing the hidden constant in  $q_{\min}$
sufficiently large ensures  $q\geq 1+\log\tau_{i}$  at every phase,
as required by Proposition 3.4.
We now return to the capped algorithm with threshold  $\tau$ . Couple
a capped warm-start algorithm to the ideal warm-start algorithm, using
the same randomness until a cap is hit, and let  $S$  be the event
that all  $J$  phases succeed. The per-phase failure estimate in Proposition 3.4
is taken under the ideal input law. As in [22, High-level idea of analysis],
the union bound gives  $\mathbb{P}(S)\geq 1-\sum^{J}_{i=1}\eta_{i}\geq 99/100$ .
On  $S$ , the capped and ideal paths coincide, so restarting independent
full attempts until success returns  $\nu=\operatorname{law}(\bar{X}_{J}\mid S)$ .
At the terminal index,  $\mathsf{R}_{2q}(\mu_{t_{J}}\mathbin{\|}\pi)\leq 1/4$  by
Corollary 3.2, and applying the weak triangle inequality
gives  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\bar{\nu}_{J}\mathbin{\|}\pi)\leq 13/40$ . Finally,  $\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}_{J}}(x)=\frac{\mathbb{P}(S\mid\bar{X}_{J}=x)}{\mathbb{P}(S)}\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(S)}$ ,
so
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu\mathbin{\|}\pi)\leq\mathsf{R}_{q}(\bar{\nu}_{J}\mathbin{\|}\pi)+\frac{q}{q-1}\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(S)}<1\,.$
It remains to bound the query complexity. Starting from a given  $\sigma^{2}_{i}$ ,
doubling this value requires  $O(\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}/\sigma^{2}_{i})$  phases.
Since the query complexity in each phase during doubling is  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\sigma^{2}_{i}\log q\log^{5}J)$ ,
the total query cost of a doubling is  $\widetilde{O}(q^{1/2}n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2})$ .
Since there are only logarithmically many doublings, the total query
complexity is
$\widetilde{O}(q^{1/2}n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2})\times O\bigl(\log_{2}(n\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}})\bigr)=\widetilde{O}(q^{1/2}n^{2}\mathsf{V}^{1/2})\,.$
If  $C$  denotes the cost of one full attempt, then the restart wrapper
has expected cost  $\mathbb{E}C_{\mathrm{restart}}=\frac{\mathbb{E}C}{\mathbb{P}(S)}\leq\frac{100}{99}\,\mathbb{E}C$ .
Thus, restarting increases the cost by only a universal factor.

As in the proof of Theorem 1.2, we couple the
capped and ideal paths until a cap is hit; the success event  $S$
satisfies  $\mathbb{P}(S)\geq 99/100$ . Write  $\nu^{X,T}=\operatorname{law}(Z_{J}\mid S)$ .
The terminal bound and the weak triangle inequality yield  $\mathsf{R}_{q}(\bar{\nu}_{J}\mathbin{\|}\bar{\pi}^{X,T})\leq 13/40$ .
Since  $\mathrm{d}\nu^{X,T}/\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}_{J}\leq 1/\mathbb{P}(S)$ ,
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu^{X,T}\mathbin{\|}\bar{\pi}^{X,T})\leq\frac{13}{40}+\frac{q}{q-1}\log\frac{100}{99}<1\,.$
Since  $\bar{\pi}^{X,T}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}t)=p^{-1}\,\mathds{1}_{\bar{\mathcal{K}}}(x,t)\,\pi^{X,T}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}t)$
for  $p=\pi^{X,T}(\bar{\mathcal{K}})\geq 1/2$ , data processing under  $(x,t)\mapsto x$
gives
$\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu^{X}\mathbin{\|}\pi)\underset{\textup{DPI}}{\leq}\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu^{X,T}\mathbin{\|}\pi^{X,T})=\mathsf{R}_{q}(\nu^{X,T}\mathbin{\|}\bar{\pi}^{X,T})+\log\frac{1}{p}\leq 1+\log 2<2\,.$
It remains to bound the query complexity. Rejection sampling gives
an exact initial sample and uses  $O(1)$  expected evaluation queries.
In Phase I, the update in (4.3) requires
$O(\sqrt{q_{0}\,(q_{0}+n)}\log n)$  phases. Each phase requires  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2})$
queries, so
$\text{Phase I cost}\leq\widetilde{O}(n^{2})\times O(\sqrt{q_{0}\,(q_{0}+n)}\log n)=\widetilde{O}(n^{5/2})\,.$
For Phase II, the universal updates below  $\sigma^{2}=1$  use  $O(\sqrt{q_{0}n}\log n)$
phases, and each phase needs  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2})$  queries. After  $\sigma^{2}\geq 1$ ,
since the doubling of an initial  $\sigma^{2}$  requires  $O(\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}}/\sigma^{2})$
phases, and each phase needs  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\sigma^{2})$  queries,
the doubling requires  $\widetilde{O}(n^{2}\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}})$  queries. Summing
the query costs of the two loops,
$\text{Phase II cost}\leq\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5}+n^{2}\sqrt{q\mathsf{V}})=\widetilde{O}\bigl(n^{5/2}+n^{2}\sqrt{q}\,(R\sqrt{L}\wedge R^{3/2}\Lambda^{1/4})\bigr)\,.$
Finally, since  $\mathbb{P}(S)\geq 99/100$ , the restart wrapper multiplies the
expected cost by at most  $100/99$ , which completes the proof.
