EXACT OPERATOR-CONVEX BUILDING BLOCK, NOT AN ENTROPY BRIDGE

The scalar function
  f(t)=(t-1)logt-2(sqrt(t)-1)^2, t>0,
is operator convex. It is related to the Hardy hyperbolic potential by
  f(t)=4sqrt(t)k((logt)/2),
  k(x)=x sinhx-coshx+1.

Exact proof and constants:
  (t-1)logt=(t-1)^2 integral_0^infinity
                        d lambda/[(t+lambda)(1+lambda)],
  (sqrt(t)-1)^2=(2/pi)(t-1)^2 integral_0^infinity
             sqrt(lambda)d lambda/[(t+lambda)(1+lambda)^2].
The second identity follows from the standard scalar square-root integral
by subtracting its value and first derivative at t=1; it can also be checked
by the substitution lambda=x^2 and elementary partial fractions. Hence
  f(t)=(t-1)^2 integral_0^infinity
   [(1+lambda)-(4/pi)sqrt(lambda)]
       /[(t+lambda)(1+lambda)^2] d lambda.
The numerator is nonnegative because 1+lambda>=2sqrt(lambda) and pi>2.
For every lambda>=0,
  (t-1)^2/(t+lambda)
    =t-(2+lambda)+(1+lambda)^2/(t+lambda)
is operator convex: its non-affine part is a positive multiple of inverse,
which is operator convex by the positive-block Schur complement inequality.
The integral is a positive combination of these functions and converges
uniformly on compact positive spectral intervals. Functional calculus thus
proves the operator-convex claim in every finite dimension.
