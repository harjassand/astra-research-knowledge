# Effective pressure and observable certificates from the finite hierarchy

## 1. Exact input and the decisive theorem interface

Input consists of even arity p>=2, rational alpha>0, an explicitly finite
disorder law with rational probabilities and computable interaction entries,
and an explicitly finite field law. Rational entries are a sufficient subclass.
The Panchenko--Talagrand factorization, independence, strict product and
positivity conditions are promised or separately proved for that model. They
are not silently verified by the following numerical procedure. Symmetric
finite rational even-spin couplings and finite weighted even-K SAT are concrete
legal subclasses. Indices are independent with replacement and counts are
Poisson(alpha*N), exactly as in the source.

The relevant source locations are model:theorem/main:formula, upper:bound,
reduction:stability and models:ground. In particular, upper:bound states

    F_N <= B_r(zeta,m) for EVERY N and EVERY legal finite-depth trial.

The claimed main theorem identifies lim_N F_N with inf_{r,zeta,m} B_r. Together
these imply F_N <= F_infinity for every N. No monotonicity of N->F_N is needed.
This order relation is what permits finite-volume computations to certify a
lower bound; convergence alone would not do so.

nonconstructive perturbation-parameter/subsequence selection at fixed depth,
an analytic identity from t=1/j and u about j^(-j), and N->infinity before

Its most delicate outstanding independent-audit target is the aligned-copy

## 2. Why rational finite trees are a dense enumerable trial class

Use magnetization z=tanh(x), so the terminal spin probabilities are exactly
(1+s*z)/2. Replace R by its compact completion [-1,1]. Define E_0=[-1,1] and
E_{j+1}=P(E_j) with weak topology. Every E_j is compact metrizable. Inductively,
finite-support laws with rational weights on a fixed dense countable subset
are dense: approximate a probability measure using a finite cover by small
balls, move each cell's mass to a dense point, then approximate cell weights
by nonnegative rationals summing to one. Use terminal rational z in (-1,1),
not endpoint messages. Each such message has the legal computable field
x=atanh(z). Apply the construction at every level, including the outer root
law. Thus finite rational nested trees can be enumerated without testing any
infinite invariance equation.

For fixed physical count k and fixed disorder, the site/edge terminal
functions are continuous and strictly positive on the finite product of E_0.
For positive continuous g and exponent a>0, the map

    (a,nu) -> log integral(exp(a*g)) dnu / a

is continuous on any compact set of positive exponents. Product measures
depend continuously on their marginal laws. Induction through the power means
and then the outer expectation proves continuity in the nested trial law and
in 0<m_1<...<m_r<1. This argument also applies when g is a bounded logarithm,
as in the actual recursion. Rational strictly increasing exponents are dense.

Poisson counts can be removed from the continuity argument by the uniform
bound |log T(site)|<=H+k*B, |log T(edge)|<=B when |h|<=H and |theta|<=B.
The resulting integrable Poisson tail is independent of the trial law. For
integrable disorder the source bound |h|+sum_j B(theta_j) provides the same
dominated-convergence argument. Therefore the infimum over all legal trials
equals the infimum over the enumerable rational finite trees. No density
claim about an invariant exchangeable-spin constraint set is required.

## 3. Complete finite evaluation and the halting sandwich

Let a be the interaction atom count and b the field atom count. Truncating the
physical Poisson count at J reduces F_N to finite sums and computable exp/log.
A deliberately crude term count is

    sum_{k=0}^J a^k * N^(p*k) * b^N * 2^N.

Repeated energies can be aggregated exactly; that is an acceleration, not an
unpaid oracle. For lambda=alpha*N, the omitted normalized absolute pressure is
bounded by

    (1+H)*Pr(Pois(lambda)>J) + (B/N)*E[Pois(lambda) 1_{>J}].

Here log 2<=1. The analogous omitted site term for a trial has lambda=alpha*p
and bound H*Pr(K>J)+B*E[K 1_{>J}]; the edge has no count tail. Exact rational
majorants, for J+1>lambda, are

    Pr(K>J) <= lambda^(J+1)/(J+1)! /(1-lambda/(J+2)),
    E[K 1_{>J}] <= lambda * lambda^J/J! /(1-lambda/(J+1)).

These follow by discarding e^(-lambda)<=1 and bounding successive term ratios.
They tend to zero and are computable. Finite nested means use positive sums,
log and exp(a*log v); outward rational enclosures can be refined to any
requested precision. If each nested node has at most c atoms and a term uses
ell labels, a brute evaluator has at most c^(ell*(r+1)) full assignments,
apart from polynomial bookkeeping and physical atom choices. The dependence
on J, N, depth, branching and requested precision is charged, potentially huge.

At stage s, evaluate F_s and the first s enumerated trials with enclosure width
<=2^(-s). Keep L=max(all finite-volume lower endpoints) and U=min(all trial
upper endpoints). Every stage proves L<=F_infinity<=U. Stop when U-L<epsilon.
The lower endpoints approach F_infinity by convergence, and trial upper
endpoints approach it by the dense infimum. Consequently the algorithm halts.
This is an effective stopping algorithm, not an explicit convergence rate or
polynomial-time theorem. It need not discover an optimal hierarchy or a Gibbs
state. A finite resource cap may correctly return UNKNOWN before halting.

Effective nonfinite laws are also possible if a finite approximation plus a
computable L1 error D=E max|theta-theta'| and d=E|h-h'| is acquired. Numerical
pressure error is <=alpha*D+d and trial error <=alpha*(2p-1)*D+d. The
approximating law need not satisfy the factorization: it is used only to
numerically approximate the original quantities, with these errors, never as
an independently admissible model. A first-moment existence assertion does
not supply this effective error modulus.

## 4. Acquired mean energy, magnetization, entropy and relative entropy

For the temperature family theta_beta=beta*theta_0 and h_beta=beta*H, assume
admissibility for every queried beta>0. The pressure F(beta) is convex and
finite. At a rational beta where F is differentiable, the intervals

    [(F(beta)-F(beta-h))/h, (F(beta+h)-F(beta))/h]

contain F'(beta) and shrink to it as rational h decreases to zero. Evaluate
the three pressures with error o(h), for example O(h^2), and stop when the
outward secant interval has width below epsilon. Differentiability guarantees
halting; it is a promise, not a separately decided property. At a corner the
procedure can remain UNKNOWN, rather than output a selected phase derivative.
Convex convergence of F_N to F gives F_N'(beta)->F'(beta): the same secant
inequalities squeeze the finite-volume derivatives, first N then h. Hence the
acquired scalar is the thermodynamic expected unscaled score -H_0 per spin,
where H_0 is the physical Hamiltonian in the source sign convention. The
physical Hamiltonian energy density is its negative.

The Gibbs entropy density is F(beta)-beta*F'(beta). For two temperatures with
the same base disorder, exact finite-volume Gibbs algebra gives the relative
entropy per spin from beta to beta_0 as

    (beta-beta_0)*F_N'(beta) - F_N(beta) + F_N(beta_0).

It therefore has a computable thermodynamic limit at differentiable beta.
The same construction works for an external field t, leaving theta fixed and
replacing the iid field by h+t: F(t) is convex, 1-Lipschitz, and F'(t) is the
mean magnetization at differentiability. The temperature entropy formula is
not incorrectly applied to a field derivative while omitting interaction
energy. These scalar thermodynamic observables do not acquire local marginals
or a sample from the Gibbs distribution.

## 5. An acquired Chernoff rate without differentiability

Let C=alpha*E B(theta)+E|h| be any acquired rational upper bound. Adding a
uniform field t gives F(t)>=|t|-C by retaining the all-plus or all-minus spin
in the partition sum, and F(0)<=log 2+C<=1+C. Take rational m with |m|<1
(a computable real m with an acquired positive distance from +/-1 also works).
Define

    J(m)=sup_t {t*m-[F(t)-F(0)]},
    R=(2*C+1)/(1-|m|).

For |t|>=R, t*m-F(t)<=-(1+C)<=-F(0), so the supremum can be restricted to
[-R,R]. The objective is (1+|m|)-Lipschitz. A uniform rational grid of mesh d
with endpoints has maximum within (1+|m|)*d/2 of the exact supremum. Outward
pressure evaluation at all O(R/d) grid points and at zero therefore yields a
halting certificate for J(m), with an explicit additional query count. This
uses no differentiability, phase choice or unknown optimizer.

This is a computable Chernoff exponent, not an asserted full large-deviation
principle. For magnetization M_N=N^(-1)sum_i sigma_i and any rho>0, finite Gibbs
algebra yields

    (1/N) E log mu_N(|M_N-m|<=rho)
      <= F_N(t)-F_N(0)-t*m+|t|*rho.

Choose an optimizing t in [-R,R] and take limsup to obtain an upper bound
-J(m)+R*rho. A positive certified lower endpoint for J thus acquires an
geometric-mean thermodynamic exclusion bound on that magnetization window:
the quantity is E log(mu_N(window)), not log E(mu_N(window)). No annealed
exclusion-probability estimate is acquired. It does not prove a matching lower
bound, locate a critical surface or assert finite-N accuracy without a separate
convergence rate.

## 6. Ground scores and strict comparisons

For symmetric even-spin and finite weighted even-K SAT models, the source
zero-temperature corollary gives g<=F(beta)/beta<=g+log 2/beta. Choose a
rational beta with 1/beta small enough and compute a pressure bracket [L,U].
Then [L/beta-1/beta,U/beta] encloses g; this acquires the limiting expected
ground score to any requested tolerance. Unit-weight SAT yields the limiting
violation density -g. Zero violation density is not a satisfiability threshold
or a statement about the probability of zero violated clauses.

Strict pressure differences between two legal finite inputs can be decided
by refining their certificates until disjoint. Equality need not terminate.
These are usable thermodynamic scalar/phase-gap tasks with all evaluation
work charged, not general efficient combinatorial optimization.

## 7. The first-moment sample obstruction and nearest prior

If the disorder is provided only by samples and integrability, no fixed
distribution-free finite sample budget can acquire the pressure with a
nontrivial uniform confidence guarantee. Set theta(s)=X spin independently,
h=0, alpha=1 and b=0. Then F_N=F_infinity=log 2+EX for every N. Compare X=0
with X=A with probability epsilon, zero otherwise, where A=1/epsilon. Both
are integrable and even have EX<=1. For n samples choose epsilon=1/(10*n).
The identical all-zero transcript has probability (1-epsilon)^n>=9/10 under
the second law, while the pressures differ by one. An interval of width less
than one cannot cover both values on this transcript. Coupling bounds the
transcript total variation by n*epsilon, giving the usual two-point lower
bound on uniform success. Adaptive algorithms capped at n draws face the
same coupling. A public effective tail/modulus or a finite law encoding is
essential. This does not contradict the thermodynamic formula.

Panchenko--Talagrand 2004, Theorem 4 / (5.10), already supplies these finite
hierarchical upper bounds; exactness appears there as the conjectural final
display. Panchenko 2013, Theorems 1--2 / (22)--(24), already gives an exact
formula over invariant exchangeable-spin distributions, whose admission
requires all invariance equations. I inspected these primary theorem
excerpts, not their full proofs. The release's unrestricted finite-tree
infimum removes that particular admission condition from the enumeration.
It does not establish that pressure was previously noncomputable, nor that
no other effective approximation method existed. Secants and compact-grid
Legendre computation are ordinary convex-analysis deductions. Priority of
the combined acquisition statement is UNKNOWN; no new-theorem novelty claim.
