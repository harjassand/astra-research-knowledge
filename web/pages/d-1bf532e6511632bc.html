# Independent gate: binary identity and blocks of size at most two

Worker `metric_geometry10`, 2026-10-07, cycle11. **Mathematical PASS** for universal nonvanishing of the polynomial Krylov determinant when the hidden positive-definite Q is block diagonal with blocks of size one or two. The block decomposition is used only in the proof and is not supplied to acquisition.

## Intrinsic construction

Let H=Hess(f), h=det(H), T the normalized four-linear tensor with f(x)=T(x^4), and

\[
C=\operatorname{adj}(H)(T:\operatorname{adj}(H)),\qquad
A=H^{-1}(T:H^{-1})=C/h^2
\]

on h≠0. Put K=det[x,Cx,…,C^(n−1)x]. These are coefficient-only polynomial/rational operations. A change x=L^−1y sends A_x to L^−1A_yL and C_x to det(L)^4L^−1C_yL. Thus

\[
K_x(x)=\det(L)^{2n(n-1)-1}K_y(Lx),
\]

so nonvanishing can be proved in hidden coordinates. The normalization of T matters for the numerical constants below; using fourth derivatives multiplies C by 24 and does not affect nonvanishing.

## Exact binary calculation

For f=a x^4+2b x²y²+c y^4, a,c>0 and Δ=ac−b²>0, set d=12ac+4b²/3. Then

\[
\operatorname{adj}(H)=
\begin{pmatrix}4bx^2+12cy^2&-8bxy\\-8bxy&12ax^2+4by^2\end{pmatrix},
\]

\[
T:\operatorname{adj}(H)=
\begin{pmatrix}8abx^2+dy^2&-16b^2xy/3\\-16b^2xy/3&dx^2+8bcy^2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Multiplying these matrices and taking x(C(x,y))_2−y(C(x,y))_1 gives the exact polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{\det[(x,y)^T,C(x,y)^T]=144\Delta xy(ax^4-cy^4).}
\]

The x²y² coefficient cancels exactly. This is nonzero for every positive-definite binary Q, including b=0.

At (1,0), C=diag(32ab²,144a²c+16ab²). For b≠0 the spectrum is nonzero and simple because the difference is 16a(9ac−b²)>0. However that observation alone does not prove vector cyclicity: (1,0) has only one nonzero coordinate. The derivative C_21 at y=0 is −128ab², so the first Krylov coefficient is 144aΔ. The full boxed identity supplies the required cyclicity directly.

For b≠0, h and det(C) are nonzero at this axis point. For b=0, det(C)=144²a³c³x⁴y⁴ and h=144acx²y², so both are nonzero off the axes. Together with the boxed K, this proves that every binary block has a real point where h≠0, A is invertible, and the point vector is cyclic for A. The three polynomial open conditions have nonempty intersection. A one-dimensional block f=a x⁴ has A=1/(144a x⁴), so the same statement is immediate.

## Block scaling and universal nonvanishing

For hidden block-diagonal Q, f is the sum of independent block quartics. H and A are block diagonal. Choose one real point in each block satisfying the preceding three conditions. The normalized block matrix scales as

\[
A_b(t_bx_b)=t_b^{-4}A_b(x_b),\qquad t_b>0.
\]

Each block has a cyclic input vector and no zero eigenvalue. Its minimal polynomial therefore has degree equal to its block size. Positive scales can be chosen successively so that these minimal polynomials are pairwise coprime: a collision of a nonzero eigenvalue with a previously fixed eigenvalue excludes only finitely many scales. This reasoning works for complex spectra and does not assume diagonalizability.

The Chinese remainder theorem then says that the direct-sum vector is cyclic for the direct-sum matrix: its annihilating polynomial is the least common multiple of the block minimal polynomials, now their product of total degree n. Consequently det[x,Ax,…,A^(n−1)x] is nonzero at the chosen scaled point. Since h≠0 there,

\[
K=h^{n(n-1)}\det[x,Ax,\ldots,A^{n-1}x]\ne0.
\]

This proves universal nonvanishing in the stated block-size subclass for arbitrary dimension. Scaling choices and the hidden partition are existence witnesses in the proof, not acquisition inputs.
