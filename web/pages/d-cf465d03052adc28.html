# N49: finite-depth networked-inference audit

## Finding and source correction

The useful capability is an exact, local upward Bayes recursion plus a finite-depth advantage guarantee extracted from the N49 proof. This supports a controlled four-class sensing test, but it does not by itself establish a physical control or safety result.

The primary N49 manuscript is [*A Capacity Criterion for Four-State Potts Reconstruction on Trees*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-Capacity-Criterion-for-Four-State-Potts-Reconstruction-on-Trees-October-5-2026/four-state-capacity.pdf), dated 5 October 2026. Its model is narrower than the Astra card [N49-weighted-potts-flow.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/main/cards/N49-weighted-potts-flow.txt): the manuscript uses one common, known `0 < λ < 1` on every edge of a deterministic rooted tree with a fixed finite upper bound `B` on the number of children. It does **not** prove a criterion for edge-varying `λ_e`, arbitrary reversible channels, or general side information. The card's edge-indexed formula/title is therefore not supported by the linked paper.

For the common-channel model, the manuscript defines

`Cap_{3,λ}(T) = sup |θ|`, over nonnegative conserved flows to infinity satisfying
`sup_ray Σ_{e on ray} (λ^{-2|e|} θ(e))^2 ≤ 1`.

Here `|e|` is the depth of the child endpoint and `|θ|` is total flow out of the root. The theorem states reconstruction from the spins at level `n` iff this capacity is positive, for every bounded-degree deterministic tree, including irregular growth and the exponential Kesten–Stigum boundary. It excludes `λ=1` (an infinite ray is perfectly informative while this capacity is zero). The tree is known to the observer.

Status is conditional: the paper is a source preprint, not independent validation. Its proof explicitly imports a preserved-class/moment-saving proposition from the companion [*The Reconstruction Threshold for the Ferromagnetic Four-State Potts Model*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/The-Reconstruction-Threshold-for-the-Ferromagnetic-Four-State-Potts-Model-October-5-2026/four-state-potts.pdf), and says that certificate proof is not repeated. That companion is also a preprint. The flow/capacity reduction is written out in N49; the imported posterior-law inequality is a load-bearing proof dependency to inspect or independently verify before treating the theorem as settled.

## Finite-depth Bayes interface

Let `X_ρ` be uniform on four categories. For an edge `(p,v)`, use `K_λ(a,b)=λ 1{a=b}+(1−λ)/4`. Each node may have a conditionally independent observation with likelihood `O_v(y_v|b)`; set this likelihood to 1 when there is no local observation. For a realized observation record, define upward messages

`φ_v(b) = O_v(y_v|b) ∏_{w child of v} m_{w→v}(b)`,

`m_{v→p}(a) = Σ_b K_λ(a,b) φ_v(b) = λ φ_v(a) + (1−λ)(Σ_b φ_v(b))/4`.

At the root, normalize `π(a) O_ρ(y_ρ|a) ∏_w m_{w→ρ}(a)` to obtain the exact posterior. Arbitrary positive message rescalings cancel at the root. This is ordinary sum-product on a tree, not a new N49 algorithm. The Potts channel's rank-one-plus-identity form makes the edge update cheaper than a generic 4×4 matrix-vector multiply.

For level-spin observations, put `p_n = P(X_ρ=· | X_{L_n})`, `a_n = E TV(p_n,u)`, and let the MAP advantage be `A_n = P(MAP(X_{L_n})=X_ρ)−1/4`. Pointwise, `TV(p,u)/3 ≤ max_i p_i−1/4 ≤ TV(p,u)`, hence `a_n/3 ≤ A_n ≤ a_n`. N49's proof gives more than asymptotic reconstruction: for any positive admissible flow `θ`, it scales that flow by `ε>0` and constructs a randomized degradation of the boundary observations such that, for every finite `n≥1`,

`a_n ≥ M/16` and therefore `A_n ≥ M/48`, where `M=ε|θ|>0`.

The proof chooses `ε` small enough using a finite constant depending on `(B,λ)` and `Q=1+(Bλ²)^2`; its displayed loss condition is `C₂[Q ε²/(1−λ)+√Q ε/(1−λ)] ≤ 1/2`. The paper does not give a numerical value for `C₂`. Thus this is a genuine finite-depth existence bound but not yet an operational numerical guarantee until the proof constants and a concrete infinite-flow witness are supplied. The paper also gives `m_n/8 ≤ a_n ≤ (1/2)√m_n`, with `m_n=4 E||p_n−u||²`; these are posterior-information conversions, not new BP.

There is a simple sensor extension. If every observed leaf has a known independent four-state symmetric sensor channel `P_{s_v}` with `s_v ≥ s₀ > 0`, the flow proof's extra terminal channel `P_{t_v}` can be simulated from the sensor output by post-processing with `P_{t_v/s_v}`, since Potts parameters compose multiplicatively. In the proof `t_v=√(X_v/3)≤√(ε/3)`, so additionally choose `ε≤3s₀²`. The original sensor record is more informative than this degraded record, giving the same lower bound. This corollary requires a uniform positive reliability floor and the same homogeneous transition model; it does not cover arbitrary sensor confusion matrices or unknown reliability.

## Computation, communication, calibration

For one realized sample, each internal node multiplies its local four-state likelihood by incoming child messages, applies the four-entry Potts update, and sends one normalized four-state likelihood vector upward. A normalized vector has three independent log-likelihood ratios. With `b` bits per ratio, the ideal payload is `3b` bits per edge per sample (`3b|E|` total), plus framing, synchronization, retransmission, and latency costs. Exact real-valued BP has no finite-bit payload; quantization must be charged and tested. The per-sample work is `O(4|E|)` arithmetic for this structured Potts update (or `O(16|E|)` for generic known 4×4 channels), plus local sensor acquisition. Computing an observed root posterior is linear in tree size; computing expected Bayes accuracy by enumerating all possible leaf records is generally exponential in the number of leaves, so use Monte Carlo trials or a justified compressed message-distribution method for evaluation.

For a q-ary symmetric sensor, record `P(correct)=(1+3s)/4`; `s` is its nontrivial eigenvalue. Calibrating a common edge `λ` requires labeled parent/child state pairs, since `P(child=parent)=(1+3λ)/4`; with `N` independent pairs, `λ̂=(4 p̂_match−1)/3` and Hoeffding gives `P(|λ̂−λ|≥δ)≤2 exp(−9Nδ²/8)`. Sensor calibration similarly requires a known injected node state. Without state labels or independent calibration references, edge reliability, sensor reliability, and root prevalence can be confounded. N49 assumes `λ` known and supplies no identification theorem.

Two design objectives are available, with different evidential status. For a fixed finite tree and known model, optimize sensor placement, sensor reliability, quantization bits, and calibration trials against exact BP Bayes reward minus acquisition/communication cost; evaluate expected reward by exhaustive enumeration only on small trees or by independent held-out Monte Carlo. Do not presume MAP reward is submodular. Separately, on a finite tree truncation, maximize the flow mass subject to conservation, nonnegativity, and each root-to-leaf constraint `Σ (λ^{-2|e|} θ_e)^2≤1`; these are second-order-cone constraints. This is an optimizable capacity proxy/flow-certificate search. A finite truncation's positive capacity alone does not certify infinite-tree reconstruction or finite-depth accuracy; an explicit positive flow valid to infinity is needed for the N49 theorem.

## Prior art and what is conditional-new

- Exact tree sum-product is standard; see Kschischang, Frey & Loeliger, [“Factor Graphs and the Sum-Product Algorithm”](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.910572), IEEE TIT 47 (2001). The message recursion and distributed implementation are not a novelty claim.
- Evans, Kenyon, Peres & Schulman, [“Broadcasting on Trees and the Ising Model”](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-applied-probability/volume-10/issue-2/Broadcasting-on-trees-and-the-Ising-model/10.1214/aoap/1019487349.full), Ann. Appl. Probab. 10 (2000), establish broad binary/Ising tree and electrical-flow connections. Mossel & Peres, [“Information Flow on Trees”](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0107033), Ann. Appl. Probab. 13 (2003), study general channels, Potts cases, and the distinction between census and full boundary information. These make tree reconstruction and flow screens established ideas.
- Pemantle & Peres, [“The Critical Ising Model on Trees, Concave Recursions and Nonlinear Capacity”](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503137), Ann. Probab. 38 (2010), give exact nonlinear-capacity criteria for Ising on general trees. N49 itself distinguishes their free-boundary `L²`/squared-resistance and plus-boundary `L³`/unsquared-resistance criteria from its `L³`/`λ^{-2|e|}` criterion.
- Sly, [“Reconstruction for the Potts Model”](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.1208), Ann. Probab. 39 (2011), establishes the large-degree 3-state regular-tree KS threshold and non-tightness for at least 5 states. Mossel, Sly & Sohn, [“Exact Phase Transitions for Stochastic Block Models and Reconstruction on Trees”](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-53/issue-3/Exact-phase-transitions-for-stochastic-block-models-and-reconstruction-on/10.1214/24-AOP1723.full), Ann. Probab. 53 (2025), prove q=3,4 nonreconstruction results for Galton–Watson families of sufficiently large mean under their hypotheses, with q=4 antiferromagnetic behavior degree-dependent. The 2026 companion preprint claims the all-degree regular and Poisson ferromagnetic q=4 threshold, not the N49 arbitrary deterministic-tree criterion.

**Conditional novelty:** if the imported q=4 posterior moment-saving certificate and the N49 proof survive independent review, the substantive new result is the exact `L³`-capacity criterion on arbitrary bounded-degree deterministic trees for the homogeneous ferromagnetic q=4 channel, including critical/irregular growth. Its proof also implies a positive all-depth Bayes-advantage floor whenever a supplied infinite flow has positive mass. Existing BP does not prove this threshold, and cited q=4 literature does not cover arbitrary deterministic tree geometry. Novelty does not extend to edge-varying channels, arbitrary side information, or an actuator guarantee.

## Decisive interface and attack

Use a controlled four-mode broadcast emulator or instrument hierarchy: publish the rooted adjacency list and `B`; randomize balanced root labels; record ground-truth parent/child categories during independent calibration runs; freeze one common `λ`; and collect leaf observations with known q-symmetric reliabilities (or direct labels). On each held-out episode, run distributed upward BP and log posterior, MAP correctness, bytes, latency, sensor energy, packet loss, and calibration uncertainty. Compare with a no-data 25% baseline, best-leaf classifier, majority/census, and a centralized exact-BP oracle under the same sensors and channel model. Keep the flow certificate and `λ` fixed before held-out evaluation. A strong discriminator is a bounded-degree nonregular tree family near the exponential Kesten–Stigum growth boundary (which is `dλ²=1` for a regular tree), where a scalar growth heuristic is inconclusive but an explicit flow-capacity witness predicts reconstruction.

The measured quantity should include empirical `E TV(p,u)` and MAP advantage with held-out confidence intervals, not just reconstruction accuracy on one root state. The finite lower-bound constant must first be made numerical to use `M/48` as a preregistered pass threshold. A finite-depth run cannot by itself establish the infinite asymptotic criterion, and no source result converts MAP classification into closed-loop stability, safety, or energy-control performance. Loops, correlated/common-cause noise, unknown topology, non-Potts transitions, and asynchronous actuator effects require separate models and baselines.
