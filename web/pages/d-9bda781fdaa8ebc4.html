## 1. Exact physical derivative certificate

Let S_v,S_w be the two explicitly supplied 8×8 integer skews in the b_compute witness JSON. The real rational Cayley rotations V=(I−S_v)(I+S_v)^{-1}, W=(I−S_w)(I+S_w)^{-1} are orthogonal. The inverses exist over the reals: (I+S)x=0 implies x^T(I+S)x=||x||²=0. The exact determinants are

    det(I+S_v)=6532105,    det(I+S_w)=1314051.

The Bell column labelled P is vec(P)/√8 for the three-site real tensor products of I,X,Z,J. Each P is orthogonal, and trace orthogonality gives U_Bell^T U_Bell=I. Its two three-site reduced states are PP^T/8 and P^TP/8, both I/8. Consequently every column of U_0=(V⊗W)U_Bell has zero expectation of every physical X_i,Z_i, since those one-site generators are traceless. Thus B_a=U_0^T A_a U_0 is real symmetric with exactly zero diagonal. The B_a entries are rational: the factors 1/√8 occur twice.

Set C_ii=1 and C_ij(s)=2s^{|i−j|}/(1+s^{2|i−j|}) off the diagonal. With Ω(y) a linear combination of the selected 756 coordinate skews, let

    F_a(y,s)=R ( C(s) ⊙ [e^{−Ω(y)} B_a e^{Ω(y)}] ) 1,

where R keeps the first 63 coordinates after projection perpendicular to 1. This map is real analytic near (0,0). F(0,0)=0. At s=0, C removes every offdiagonal entry. Its y-derivative is therefore the first 63 diagonal entries of [B_a,Ω]. The full diagonal has trace zero, so the projection subtraction does nothing. For Ω=E_ij−E_ji, i<j, the only nonzero diagonal entries are −2(B_a)_ij at i and +2(B_a)_ij at j. This matches the certificate's sign and row conventions.

## 2. Real root: no singular-limit substitution

The nonsingular square real derivative supplies the ordinary real implicit-function conclusion: a real y(s) near zero with F(y(s),s)=0. Importantly, no physical state is asserted at s=0, where the population prescription is singular. Only positive finite s is used. The negative-s extension need not be physically interpretable; it is a legitimate analytic extension of the equations used to find a positive root.

We also reconstructed the explicit contraction proof, eliminating reliance on an unspecified IFT neighborhood. A common denominator for every J entry is

    K=756·1512^{755}·Q_den^{756} ≥ ||J^{-1}||_2.

are valid for |s|≤1/2. Here R has norm ≤1, the row-sum operator has Frobenius-to-vector norm √d, and |C_ij(s)|≤2|s| off the diagonal. The sharper first bound is 2d√q|s|. ## 3. Faithfulness, trace, inequality, and score equality

At this positive s, set p_i=s^{2i}/Z, Z=Σ_{j=0}^{63}s^{2j}, D=diag(√p_i), and U=U_0 e^{Ω(y(s))}. All p_i are strictly positive and U is real orthogonal. The physical state σ=UD²U^T is faithful and normalized. Its multiplier is exactly C(s), since 2√(p_i p_j)/(p_i+p_j)=2s^{|i−j|}/(1+s^{2|i−j|}).

The equation F=0 really imposes constant row sum: if the projected vector has first 63 coordinates zero, its sum-zero constraint forces the last coordinate zero too. Hence each symmetric dressed matrix K_a=C(s)⊙(U^T A_a U) has 1 as an eigenvector and commutes with P=11^T/64.

Define Q=(1−1/64)I+P and ρ=UDQD U^T. Its eigenvalues before congruence are 63/64 (multiplicity 63) and 127/64 (multiplicity 1), so ρ is faithful. Every Q diagonal entry is 1, whence Trρ=Σp_i=1. Q≠I and D is invertible, so ρ≠σ. These facts do not depend on a small-error estimate.

To reconstruct the fiber implication rather than cite it, work in the U eigenbasis. The unique target SLD satisfies

    (L_a)_ij=2i(p_i−p_j)(A_a)_ij/(p_i+p_j).

Let B=A−iL/2 and E=A+iL/2. Direct substitution gives D^{-1}BD=DED^{-1}=K. The target equation is Bσ=σE. For the candidate DQD the same score equation is equivalent to KQ=QK, which holds because Q is a scalar combination of I and P. Thus the same L solves the candidate SLD equation. Faithfulness makes the anticommutator map invertible, so this solution is uniquely L_a(ρ). All twelve physical SLD operators agree exactly.

There is no extra normalization/phase constraint hidden in this argument. Imaginary SLD entries arise from −i times a real skew commutator, yielding Hermitian scores as required. Equality for local Y_i directions has not been established and must not be inferred from algebraic generation by X_i,Z_i.

### Hostile local-Y test: this particular pair is distinguished

We went beyond that caveat and tested the omitted physical Y_i directions exactly. In the real eigenbasis write U(y)^T Y_i U(y)=i M_i(y), with M_i(y) real skew and norm one. Its dressed row sum divided by i s has leading term

    v_i=2 (nearest-neighbor-index part of M_i(0)) 1.

Here “nearest-neighbor-index” means matrix indices |j−k|=1, not physical-neighbor sites. Since y(s)=O(s), the leading term is unaffected by the IFT correction. Our independent modular reconstruction finds 50 nonzero projected coordinates for each of the six Y_i leading vectors. The entries are rational with the same common denominator Q_den, so some leading coordinate has absolute value at least 1/Q_den.

This failure holds at the explicit s_*, not just at an unspecified smaller parameter. Entrywise conjugation variation is at most 2√2||y||. Dividing the multiplier by s, distance-one entries differ from 2 by at most 2s², and all distance≥2 entries are at most 2s. Summing at most 63 offdiagonal entries bounds each leading-row error by

    252√2||y||+126s+4s² ≤ 400(||y||+s).

At the contraction root ||y||≤2KAs, so this is at most 1200KAs_*=75/(KL)<1/(2Q_den). The last inequality is certified by KL>150Q_den. Hence each local-Y dressed row vector is nonzero at s_*. A purely imaginary Hermitian matrix has zero quadratic form on the real vector 1; therefore it preserves the real line spanned by 1 only when its row sum vanishes. The nonzero row vector implies [K_{Y_i},P]≠0, and the same direct fiber algebra implies L_{Y_i}(ρ)≠L_{Y_i}(σ).

Thus adding any one of the six local Y_i score directions distinguishes this **particular** pair. This does not prove that the augmented frame identifies every faithful state or that its entire dressed commutant is scalar. It sharply limits the existing witness and supplies a concrete repair experiment rather than an untested conjecture.

## 4. Tensorization and a common known Gibbs family

For N=6k use σ_N=σ^{⊗k}, ρ_N=ρ^{⊗k}. For a local generator on block b, substituting L_a⊗I into the anticommutator equation factors out the other density matrices and verifies its score. Product faithfulness gives uniqueness. Thus the fixed local X/Z scores agree on every site. The states remain different because their first-block reduced states are σ and ρ.

Predeclare the full set of 4095 nonidentity Pauli strings on each six-site block. This set is independent of the witness and is the same for both states. For τ in {σ,ρ}, h_τ=−logτ−c_τ I expands uniquely in that basis with real finite coefficients. Scalar shifts cancel in normalized Gibbs states. Every term has weight at most six; any term intersects at most the other 4094 terms in its own block and no term on another block. This is precisely a uniformly bounded locality and term-overlap-degree class, though its constants are large. It is not a claim about two-local interactions, a connected interaction graph, or an arbitrarily prescribed sparse Hamiltonian term list.

The common inverse temperature can be made known without inspecting the unknown root or comparing real log coefficients. Since s≤1/2, Z≤4/3 and

    λ_min(σ)≥(3/4)s^{126},
    ρ≥(63/64)σ,    λ_min(ρ)≥(189/256)s^{126}.

Let b=ceil(log_2(1/s)), computed by integer bit length for s_*. Every density eigenvalue is ≤1, so ||−logτ||≤126 ln(1/s)+ln(256/189)<126b+1. A Pauli coefficient obeys |Tr(P_j(−logτ))/64|≤||−logτ||; centering does not change nonidentity coefficients. Consequently the explicit common integer

    β=126b+1=18957835

suffices. Define θ_{τ,j}=h_{τ,j}/β. All coefficients are in [−1,1], and τ=e^{−βΣθ_{τ,j}P_j}/Tr e^{−βΣθ_{τ,j}P_j}. On N sites sum disjoint copies of the same block Hamiltonian. Exponentials and partition functions factor, producing exactly τ^{⊗k} at that same β, independent of k. Since the no-identity Pauli basis is independent, distinct normalized states have distinct coefficient vectors.

This establishes a fixed family with one explicit finite known temperature and two feasible parameter vectors sharing every specified symmetric score. It is an existence counterexample to universal all-temperature identification, not an acquisition algorithm for either vector or an experimentally accessible collision. The paper's high-temperature threshold is not implicated.

Because the target-weighted symmetric-score discrepancy is a sum of nonnegative squared operator differences, both feasible parameter vectors have discrepancy zero and are global minimizers when σ is the target. This establishes the relevant nonidentifiability inside this model, without assuming anything about an optimizer finding either vector.
