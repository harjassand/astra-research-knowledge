LAMINAR JASTROW AUDIT — 7 October 2026
Assigned mechanism: laminar occupation factors multiplying a supplied Slater determinant.

STATUS AND STRONGEST RESULT
The proposed exchange argument is correct. I give a complete elementary proof
below, including zero entries and hard lower/upper quotas. Consequently the
weight and its complementary polynomial satisfy the Lorentzian/log-concavity
hypothesis needed by Chen–Liu. Exact positive-support, prefix feasibility, rank,
and common-support zero detection have polynomial explicit acquisition programs.
The resulting weighted Slater counting/coherent-preparation transfer remains
CONDITIONAL on the Chen–Liu v1 import and the existing N63 coherent compiler;
the counting algorithm and quantum compiler were not implemented here.

The structural theorem itself is established prior art: this is laminar
concavity in discrete convex analysis, going back at least to Murota (2003),
Note 6.11. An extension from disjoint factors to a hierarchy is useful scope,
but is not a newly discovered discrete-concavity theorem. The counterexample
below sharply rejects unrestricted crossing repulsive interactions.

1. EXACT INTERFACE
Let U=[m], 0<=r<=m, and let L be an explicitly given laminar family of subsets:
two members are disjoint or one contains the other. Each L has a supplied table
b_L(0),...,b_L(|L|) of nonnegative rationals. Positive entries are exactly the
integer interval [ell_L,u_L], and

  b_L(k)^2 >= b_L(k-1)b_L(k+1).

An identically zero table, or an empty-set factor b_empty(0)=0, makes everything
zero and is handled immediately. Positive empty-set factors are constants.
Define, for all S subset U,

  w(S) = 1[|S|=r] product_(L in L) b_L(|S intersect L|).

No promise of a positive feasible S is needed: the DP below decides this.
The theorem applies to any fixed finite m,r, any hierarchy depth, arbitrary
rational magnitudes, and arbitrary allowed intervals. It does not require
monotonicity of b_L, equal-size blocks, full table support, or a favorable
projection probability. Rational log-concavity and laminarity can themselves be
checked exactly in polynomial bit time.

For the Slater transfer, A in Q(i)^(m x r) is supplied explicitly with full
column rank. For rows in increasing order set a(S)=det A_S and

  Z = sum_(|S|=r) |a(S)|^2 w(S),
  |psi> = Z^(-1/2) sum_(|S|=r) a(S) sqrt(w(S)) |S>, if Z>0.

A acquisition, orbitals/interaction optimization, and a physical Hamiltonian
whose ground state is psi are not acquired by this result.

2. COMPLETE EXCHANGE PROOF
Adjoin U and all singleton leaves to the family, assigning neutral factors 1
where absent. Organize it as a rooted containment tree. Children of each node
partition that node: singleton leaves fill any residual elements. For distinct
nonempty subsets this completed tree has at most 2m-1 nodes.

Take X,Y with w(X),w(Y)>0 and any a in X\Y. For every tree node V put

  d_V = |X intersect V| - |Y intersect V|.

At leaf {a}, d=1, and at U, d=0. Ascend from {a} to the first node P with
d_P<=0. Every node strictly below P on the a-path has positive d; in particular
the child C of P containing a has d_C>0. Since d_P=sum_children d_C and d_P<=0,
some different child of P has negative d. Descend from that child, always
choosing a negative-d child, which exists since child discrepancies sum to the
parent discrepancy. At the eventual singleton {b}, d=-1, so b is in Y\X.

Exchange a and b simultaneously:

  X' = X-a+b,  Y' = Y+a-b.

Counts at P and its ancestors do not change. Counts off the two branches do not
change. For nodes on the a-branch below P, their count pair (p,q) changes to
(p-1,q+1), with p>q. For nodes on the b-branch it changes to (p+1,q-1), with
p<q. Therefore every affected count pair moves toward equality (when the
difference is one, the pair merely swaps).

For any interval-supported log-concave positive sequence b, its ratios
rho(k)=b(k)/b(k-1) are nonincreasing on the positive interval. Thus p>q gives

  b(p-1)b(q+1) / [b(p)b(q)] = rho(q+1)/rho(p) >= 1.

Both new indices remain in the interval because they are between p and q.
The p<q case is the same with the two arguments exchanged. Multiply these
inequalities over affected nodes; unchanged factors cancel. We obtain

  w(X)w(Y) <= w(X-a+b)w(Y+a-b),

and both exchanged weights are positive. This is precisely log-M-concavity on
the Boolean fixed-cardinality domain. The zero-support case is vacuous.

An immediate corollary is that the positive support B_w is the basis family of
a rank-r matroid whenever nonempty, by the basis-exchange characterization.
This is the support matroid; do not conflate its independence oracle with the
usual upper-quota-only definition of a laminar matroid.

3. COMPLEMENT AND LORENTZIAN IMPORT
Define g(T)=w(U\T). It has degree m-r and coefficients

  g(T) = 1[|T|=m-r] product_L b_L(|L|-|T intersect L|).

Each reversed table is still interval-supported and log-concave; the family is
unchanged, so the preceding proof applies directly to g. Equivalently, swapping
the roles of U\X and U\Y proves the complementary exchange inequality.
This argument does NOT assume that arbitrary Lorentzian polynomials are closed
under taking complementary coefficients.

Brändén–Huh Corollary 3.16 says that M-concavity of the logarithms of normalized
coefficients implies the Lorentzian property. Boolean monomials have alpha!=1,
so there is no factorial normalization discrepancy here. Their Theorem 2.30
then gives complete log-concavity. Hence both sum_S w(S)x^S and sum_T g(T)x^T
are Lorentzian, or zero.

The determinant polynomial

  f_A(x)=det(A^* diag(x) A)=sum_(|S|=r) |det A_S|^2 x^S

is homogeneous multiaffine log-concave. Therefore

  [x_1...x_m] f_A(x) (sum_T g(T)x^T) = Z.

This is the Chen–Liu complementary-degree convolution interface. Its Thm 5.1
requires coefficient value oracles, one separate positive support point for
each polynomial, and coefficient-range upper bounds. The arithmetic/oracle
bound on every execution is O(m^10 B^2 eta^-2 log^2(2B/eta)), where
B=m+ln(R_f R_g); the rational bit implementation is Remark A.4. Exact zero is
acquired independently below. These statements are imports from the v1 paper,
not a reproof or executable implementation of that FPRAS.

4. EXACT SUPPORT, PREFIX, RANK, AND ZERO ACQUISITION
4a. Tree feasibility and witness in O(m+|L|) integer operations after tree setup.
For disjoint required I and deleted J, ask whether a positive S contains I and
avoids J. At a leaf use the attainable-count interval [1,1] if required,
[0,0] if deleted, and [0,1] otherwise. Intersect with any singleton constraint.
At an internal node V, its children have intervals [a_C,b_C]. The exact set of
attainable counts is

  [sum_C a_C, sum_C b_C] intersect [ell_V,u_V].

This is exact because sums of integer intervals are integer intervals and the
subtrees are disjoint. Empty child intervals propagate failure. At the root
also intersect with [r,r]. Nonemptiness decides feasibility. To acquire an
actual S, propagate the desired root count r downward: start each child at its
lower endpoint and distribute the remaining count within the child capacities.
This also takes O(m+|L|) operations; counts have O(log m) bits.

4b. Support-matroid oracles are explicit.
Once B_w is nonempty, K is independent precisely when the DP finds a B_w-base
containing K. This supplies an independence program, not a free opaque oracle.
Greedily considering elements of T with this oracle computes rank_Mw(T) in
O(m(m+|L|)) integer operations. Positive initialization for g is the complement
of a DP-acquired w-base. Initialization for f_A follows from rational row rank.

4c. Prefixes preserve the interface without an exponential prefix table.
For I required, J deleted, and V=U\(I union J), the residual weight is

  w_IJ(T) = 1[|T|=r-|I|] product_L b_L(|I intersect L|+|T intersect (L intersect V)|).

The sets L intersect V are laminar. Coinciding sets can be merged by multiplying
their shifted tables; pointwise products of positive log-concave sequences are
log-concave, with interval support given by interval intersections. Empty sets
contribute constants. The original global coefficient-height and range bounds
remain valid.

The residual determinant polynomial is (partial_I f_A) with all variables in
I union J set to zero. A coefficient for T is the original exact rational
|det A_(I union T)|^2. Stable/Lorentzian differentiation and exclusion preserve
the required log-concavity. Gaussian elimination obtains a separate positive
determinant-prefix support when one exists.

4d. Exact common-support zero detection.
If either separate prefix support is empty, its mass is zero. Otherwise I is
independent in both support matroids, and deletion of J retains the requested
residual rank t=r-|I|. Run ordinary shortest-augmenting-path matroid intersection
on V for (M_A/I)\J and (M_w/I)\J. Its two independence programs are

  K -> rank(A_(I union K)) = |I|+|K|,
  K -> tree-DP feasible with I union K required and J deleted.

There is a nonzero-amplitude completion exactly when the maximum common
independent set has size t. Standard cardinality matroid intersection uses
O(m^3) independence calls, each with the explicit polynomial bit costs above.
It supplies a positive completion when one exists. On failure it can also
supply an Edmonds min-max partition Q with

  rank_M1(Q)+rank_M2(V\Q) < t,

whose exact ranks are independently verifiable by Gaussian elimination and the
DP/greedy support oracle. Z or the prefix mass is then exactly zero.

These are exact support/rank/zero claims, NOT exact evaluation of the weighted
determinant partition function. The latter is the approximate-counting task.

5. BIT COSTS AND COHERENT-COMPILER CONSEQUENCE
Let L_A be the binary length of A and L_tbl that of all table entries. Let h_L
bound numerator/denominator bit length of every positive entry of table L.
Then 2^-h_L <= b_L(k) <= 2^h_L, so

  ln R_w <= 2 ln(2) sum_L h_L <= O(L_tbl).

Counts for a queried set are obtained bottom-up on the tree, and the product
of table entries has polynomial bit length. Clearing A's denominators with D
gives C=DA in Z[i]^(m x r). If H bounds |C_ij|, nonzero coefficient values obey

  D^-2r <= |det A_S|^2 <= D^-2r (r H^2)^r,

by integrality and Hadamard. Thus ln R_f=O(r L_A+r log r); the same bounds work
uniformly for every prefix using the original minors. All value, support,
zero, phase, and rank procedures are explicit bounded polynomial programs and
can be made reversible. They do not require a minimum singular-value promise.

With the N63 compiler, the only new input to coherent amplitude preparation
is the weight inside child-prefix counts. Positive Jastrow factors introduce
no extra phase; determinant phases are acquired exactly/approximately as in
N63. The fixed padded random tape shared by all prefixes at each depth and the
weighted bad-prefix argument remain applicable. The inherited compiler takes
eta=eps/(16m), beta=delta eps^2/(64m^2); its count portion has the inherited
O(m^13 B^2 eps^-2 log^2(2Bm/eps) log(m/(delta eps))) arithmetic/oracle bound,
plus polynomial acquisition/reversible-arithmetic/gate-precision costs.
Its trace-error guarantee is over compiler randomness, with no inverse Z or
inverse filter-success probability. A good compiler seed is not efficiently
recognized. These conditional circuit claims were NOT executed here.

6. PHYSICAL SCOPE AND IMPORTANT ENCODING BOUNDARY
Write n_i for occupation and N_L=sum_(i in L) n_i. The covered diagonal filter is

  J = product_L sqrt(b_L(N_L)),

including hard interval projectors. A physically natural subclass has
b_L(k)=exp(-2 V_L(k)), with V_L discretely convex on its permitted interval.
It includes arbitrary convex hierarchical charge penalties, many-body terms,
and repulsive quadratic Jastrow factors

  V_L(k)=lambda_L binom(k,2)+h_L k,   lambda_L>=0.

The pair coupling is v_ij=sum_(L contains i,j) lambda_L. These are repulsive
hierarchical (ultrametric-similarity) couplings: v_ij>=min(v_ik,v_jk). General
nearest-neighbor lattice repulsion has no such property. Higher-order convex
V_L need not reduce to pair interactions. Positive factors above one can be
rescaled per table to give contractions without changing the normalized state.

RATIONAL TABLES are the exact polynomial bit interface. Supplying rational
lambda_L in binary and writing exp(-2lambda_L binom(k,2)) is different. The
log-range parameter can be exponential in the coupling's binary input length.
For convex-cost exponentials, rational approximations preserving log-concavity
can be acquired, but the cost depends polynomially on the NUMERICAL total
log-range Lambda=sum_L(max_k 2V_L(k)-min_k 2V_L(k)), not only log Lambda.
Chen–Liu C.19 already makes this boundary explicit for disjoint congestion.
It must also be charged here; arbitrarily large inverse temperature is not free.

By contrast, tables b_L(k)=t_L^binom(k,2), rational 0<t_L<=1 of h_L bits,
are exactly covered. Their explicit-table bit size is O(sum_L h_L |L|^3),
and log-range is O(sum_L h_L |L|^2), both polynomial in m and the rational t
encoding. A depth-m hierarchy can make the explicit table as large as O(h m^4).

If approximated tables have the same support and every configuration obeys
|log(w'(S)/w(S))|<=kappa, then normalized amplitude ratios lie in
[exp(-kappa),exp(kappa)]. Hence the pure-state trace error is at most
||psi'-psi||_2 <= exp(kappa)-1, independent of Z. This justifies spending a
small additional precision budget on irrational Gibbs weights, with the
numerical log-range cost retained.

7. CROSSING-INTERACTION COUNTEREXAMPLE (EXACT)
Set m=4,r=2 and use crossing sets {1,2} and {2,3}. Both tables are
b(0)=b(1)=1,b(2)=t=1/8. These tables are positive and log-concave; their filters
are ordinary repulsive pair penalties. The six pair weights are

  w12=w23=t;  w13=w14=w24=w34=1.

Take X={1,3},Y={2,4},a=1. Either b=2 or b=4 gives exchanged product t<1,
whereas w(X)w(Y)=1. Thus the weight is not log-M-concave.
More strongly its degree-two generating polynomial has Hessian

  H = [[0,t,1,1], [t,0,t,1], [1,t,0,1], [1,1,1,0]].

Vector (1,0,-1,0) has eigenvalue -1. On its orthogonal symmetric subspace,
the orthonormal quotient is

  Q = [[1,sqrt(2)t,sqrt(2)], [sqrt(2)t,0,1], [sqrt(2),1,0]].

det Q=4t-1=-1/2 and trace Q=1; (0,1,-1) has negative quadratic form. The
quotient therefore has exactly two positive eigenvalues and one negative.
H has two positive eigenvalues, violating quadratic Lorentzian/log-concavity.
The complementary polynomial has penalized edges {3,4},{1,4}, and is the same
example after permutation (1 3)(2 4). This rejects the required counting import
for unrestricted crossing repulsion. It is NOT a hardness theorem.
