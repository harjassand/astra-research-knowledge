## Published Ciani example: a rational quotient bridge with a torsor distinction

The published numerical polynomial is

\[
F(X,Y,Z)=X^4+Y^4+Z^4.
\]

Primary location: Lercier–Ritzenthaler–Rovetta–Sijsling, [arXiv:1403.0562v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.0562v1), Theorem 3.3, the \(G_{96}\) case, PDF p5. Its v1 submission is 3 March 2014; the preserved PDF has a later compilation date. The polynomial itself is the source example, while the following coordinate mixing is ours:

\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad f(x)=F(Lx).
\]

The input to acquisition is the expanded \(f\), with \(L\) withheld. The returned frame was normalized using rational fourth roots of its observed diagonal coefficients. This normalization is available on this Fermat fixture because those coefficients are exact rational fourth powers. It is not promised on a general Ciani input. The normalized recovered coordinate forms were

\[
\ell_1=-x_1-x_2,\quad \ell_2=-x_2,\quad \ell_3=x_0+x_1+x_2,
\qquad f=\ell_1^4+\ell_2^4+\ell_3^4.
\]

For each acquired reflection flipping \(\ell_i\), let \(j,k\) be the remaining indices and set

\[
u=\ell_j/\ell_k,\qquad v=\ell_i^2/\ell_k^2.
\]

The code checks the full rational identity

\[
(v^2+u^4+1)\ell_k^4=f
\]

and exact invariance of both functions under the acquired reflection. Thus the quotient is the genus-one curve

\[
C_i:\ v^2=-(u^4+1).
\]

The function-field extension is obtained by adjoining \(w=\ell_i/\ell_k\), where \(w^2=v\). The function \(v\) has a simple zero at each of the four points with \(u^4=-1\); it is not a square in the quotient field. Therefore the quotient map has degree two. Its four geometric branch points give genus one by Riemann–Hurwitz.

**The quotient is a torsor, not a rationally pointed elliptic curve.** It has no real affine point because its right side is strictly negative; at infinity the other chart has \((v/u^2)^2=-1\), so there is no real point there either. In particular it cannot be \(\mathbb Q\)-isomorphic to an elliptic curve with a rational origin. This prevents an overreading of Lachaud–Ritzenthaler's wording about changes of variables mapping quotient quartics to elliptic curves.

The quotient's **Jacobian** is nevertheless rationally defined. Fisher, [arXiv:2208.14977v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14977v1), §2 equations (1)–(2), PDF p2, fixes the binary-quartic convention

\[
I=12ae-3bd+c^2,\quad
J=72ace-27ad^2-27b^2e+9bcd-2c^3,
\quad E_{I,J}:Y^2=X^3-27IX-27J.
\]

For \(-u^4-1\), \(I=12,J=0\). The raw model is \(Y_0^2=X_0^3-324X_0\). The code re-expands the rational scaling \(X_0=9X,Y_0=27Y\) to give

\[
\operatorname{Jac}(C_i)\simeq_{\mathbb Q} E:\ Y^2=X^3-4X.
\]

A separate independently reconstructed rational map is

\[
C_i\longrightarrow E_0:Y^2=X^3+X,\qquad (X,Y)=(-u^2,-uv).
\]

It has degree two. Composing with the standard degree-two isogeny

\[
E_0\longrightarrow E,\qquad X'=X+1/X,\quad Y'=Y(1-1/X^2)
\]

gives a degree-four map from the torsor to its Jacobian. Both target-equation identities were verified in the exact quotient ring. This is not a degree-one identification of the torsor with its Jacobian.

## Independent reconstruction of the Ciani Jacobian splitting

Let \(J=\operatorname{Jac}(X)\), let \(\pi_i:X\to C_i\) be the three degree-two quotient maps, and put

\[
\Phi=(\pi_{1*},\pi_{2*},\pi_{3*}):J\to\prod_i\operatorname{Jac}(C_i),
\qquad \Psi=\sum_i\pi_i^*.
\]

The full Klein-four quotient has genus zero. Its invariant differential space is zero, while each \(C_i\) contributes its distinct one-dimensional nontrivial character. Norm and pullback satisfy

\[
\pi_{i*}\pi_i^*=[2],\qquad \pi_{i*}\pi_j^*=0\ (i\ne j),
\qquad \Psi\Phi=\sum_i(1+\sigma_i)=[2].
\]

For the off-diagonal relation, the two character spaces differ, so the differential of the composite is zero; in characteristic zero a homomorphism of abelian varieties with zero differential is zero. Also \(1+\sum_i\sigma_i=0\) on \(J\), since the group quotient has zero Jacobian. Thus \(\Phi\Psi=[2]\) on the product and \(\Psi\Phi=[2]\) on \(J\). The norm/pullback maps are dual under the canonical polarizations, so

\[
(\deg\Phi)^2=\deg[2]=2^6,\qquad \deg\Phi=8,
\]

and its kernel has geometric \(\mathbb F_2\)-rank three inside \(J[2]\). This reconstructs the classical \((2,2,2)\)-isogeny to \(E^3\); it does not assert all geometric kernel points are rational. It uses quotient Jacobians and remains valid when the quotient curves lack rational points. Primary prior: Lachaud–Ritzenthaler, [arXiv:0710.3303v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3303v1), §2.3 and Proposition 2.3.2, PDF p6.

The executable also counts points on the original published Fermat curve and the mixed coefficient input over twelve primes \(5\le p\le43\), using disjoint projective charts, and counts \(E(\mathbb F_p)\). Every record satisfies

\[
\#X(\mathbb F_p)=p+1-3\bigl(p+1-\#E(\mathbb F_p)\bigr).
\]

This finite arithmetic check supports transcription; it does not prove the isogeny or a universal trace formula.

## Variable-dimensional scientific use: quartic amplitude energies

An exact mathematical class is an \(n\)-component sign-symmetric amplitude energy in an unknown rational linear calibration. In a physical interpretation, \(\ell_i(x)\) are latent order parameters, \(Q\) is the positive-definite squared-amplitude coupling, and \(g\) sets tunable quadratic mode preferences. This is a mathematical template, not a fit to a measured physical system.

Let \(V\) be any returned exact QPD frame, \(z=V^{-1}x\), and \(Q'\succ0\) satisfy

\[
f(Vz)=(z^2)^TQ'z^2.
\]

Compute \(A=V^TCV\). If \(A\) is diagonal, write \(a_i=A_{ii}\). Since every \(m\ge0\) is represented by \(z_i=\pm\sqrt{m_i}\), the nonconvex original problem is exactly

\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb R^n}\{f(x)-g(x)\}
=\min_{m\ge0}\{m^TQ'm-a^Tm\}.                 \tag{1}
\]

The right side is coercive and strictly convex. It has a unique optimum \(m_*\). Its exact KKT system is

\[
m_*\ge0,\quad \mu=2Q'm_*-a\ge0,\quad \mu_i m_{*,i}=0.       \tag{2}
\]

The support \(S=\{i:m_{*,i}>0\}\) gives

\[
m_{*,S}=\tfrac12Q'_{SS}{}^{-1}a_S,\qquad m_{*,S^c}=0.
\]

Thus the optimum and multipliers are rational with polynomial coefficient bits, by determinant height bounds on the rational principal system. The real minimizing coordinates need not be rational; they have the compact exact description

\[
x=V(\varepsilon_i\sqrt{m_{*,i}})_i,\qquad
\varepsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}\text{ on }S.
\]

Listing all \(2^{|S|}\) sign choices is not a polynomial-output task. The compiler returns the compact description.

### Sharp global certificate and certificate-size simplification

Put \(P=f-g\) and \(P_*=-m_*^TQ'm_*\). Expanding (2) gives the exact identity

\[
P(x)-P_*=(m-m_*)^TQ'(m-m_*)+\sum_i\mu_i\ell_i(x)^2,
\quad m_i=\ell_i(x)^2.                        \tag{3}
\]

For rational \(Q'=BDB^T\) with positive diagonal \(D\), (3) is a rational **weighted SOS** consisting of \(n\) shifted quadratic factors \(B^T(m-m_*)\), plus at most \(n\) linear factors for positive multipliers. All positive rational weights, factors, and the sharp rational constant \(P_*\) are explicitly emitted and re-expanded in the original coordinates. A minimizing point attains equality, so this is a global-minimum certificate, not merely a loose bound.

Dense factor storage is \(O(n^3)\) rational coefficients. The general order-two SOS Gram formulation uses \(N=\binom{n+2}{2}\) monomials and an \(N\times N\) positive-semidefinite matrix. The code constructs that **same established formulation's feasible matrix** from (3), so it certifies exactly the same optimum. Smaller constructive structure does not prove a complexity separation or an empirical speedup.

### Complete real critical-point landscape

In recovered coordinates,

\[
\partial_{z_i}P=2z_i\bigl(2(Q'm)_i-a_i\bigr).
\]

At any real critical point, each nonzero-coordinate support \(T\) imposes \(2Q'_{TT}m_T=a_T\). There is at most one squared-amplitude solution per support and at most \(3^n\) distinct critical points. For an inactive coordinate \(i\),

\[
\partial^2_{z_i z_i}P=2\bigl(2(Q'm)_i-a_i\bigr).
\]

If all inactive multipliers were nonnegative, that point would satisfy (2) and hence be globally minimizing by strict convexity. Consequently every nonglobal real critical point has an inactive coordinate with a strictly negative Hessian direction. **Every local minimum is global.** The proof includes activation thresholds where some inactive multiplier is zero. A negative direction survives the invertible coordinate change by Hessian congruence. No gradient-flow convergence rate is claimed.

m_T=\tfrac12Q'_{TT}{}^{-1}a_T>0,\qquad
Q'_{T^cT}Q'_{TT}{}^{-1}a_T-a_{T^c}\ge0.       \tag{4}

These are rational linear inequalities in the query coefficients \(a\).
