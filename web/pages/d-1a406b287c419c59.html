# N08 — heterogeneous rate-and-state nucleation

Status: internally derived restricted result plus finite-dimensional counterexample. No new physical law, empirical validation, priority claim, or real-earthquake forecast is established. “Stable profile” below means an attractor of a specified quasi-static aging-law model after fixing its blow-up-time phase; it does not mean a validated prediction of an earthquake.

Version note: v2 preserves the frozen v1 record and corrects its Eq. (2) transcription (the elastic and weakening terms are added). It then gives a finite-dimensional full-memory stable-manifold result and a conditional weighted-space continuum linearization. The new theorem is restricted to a fixed active set and does not classify the physical side of an arrest/runaway threshold.

Version note v3: v1 and v2 are preserved unchanged. This version adds an implicit-function argument that removes the time-gauge codimension when the candidate blow-up time is allowed to vary, proving a local open runaway basin in physical initial data for the exact autonomous reduced model.

## Exact target and model

The target is to replace a patchwise spring-slider stiffness comparison by a criterion that keeps the state memory, the spatial elastic operator, and spatially varying friction parameters. There are two different questions that must stay separate:

1. When does steady sliding first lose infinitesimal stability?
2. Which nonlinear trajectories develop a localized finite-time slip-rate blow-up, and which arrest?

The first is a linear operator-pencil problem. The second is a nonlinear basin problem. A critical nucleus found in the second problem is not the same object as a critical wavelength or stiffness from the first.

Use the aging law and logarithmic rate-and-state law

\[
 f(x,t)=f_0+a(x)\log(V/V_0)+b(x)\log(V_0\theta/D_c(x)),\qquad
 \dot\theta=1-V\theta/D_c(x),
\]

with positive \(a,b,D_c,\sigma\), positive slip rate, and fixed effective normal stress \(\sigma(x)\). Let \(\mathcal L\) be the quasi-static elastic stress-rate operator, so \(\dot\tau_{el}=\mathcal L[V]\). For a fault between elastic half-spaces, the anti-plane Fourier symbol is \(\widehat{\mathcal L}(k)=-\mu |k|/2\); the in-plane coefficient is \(-\mu|k|/[2(1-\nu)]\). Write \(K=-\mathcal L\), positive on the chosen displacement space after the rigid/zero mode is removed by the loading and boundary conditions. For a thin-slab limit, \(\mathcal L[V]=\bar E h V_{xx}\), hence \(K=-\bar E h\partial_{xx}\) with the stated boundary conditions.

The asymptotic blow-up calculation below assumes quasi-static elasticity, aging-law state evolution, bounded external stressing rate, bounded \(\dot\sigma\), and no slip-coupled pore-pressure, dilatancy, thermal-pressurization, or inertia term. If \(\dot\sigma\) itself diverges at the slip-rate scale, the reduction changes. Ray and Viesca make this bounded-normal-stress-rate restriction explicitly (2017, §3.1, pp. 8217–8218).

## Nonlinear self-similar profile and its exact variational equation

Assume a finite blow-up time \(t_i\), set \(t_f=t_i-t\), and test the self-similar form

\[
V(x,t)=\frac{D_c(x)}{t_f}W(x),\qquad W(x)>0
\]

on a yet unknown compact active patch. The state equation becomes

\[
\dot\theta+\frac{W}{t_f}\theta=1.
\]

For constant \(W\), its solution is \(\theta=Ct_f^W+t_f/(W-1)\) when \(W\ne1\). Thus \(\dot\theta/\theta\sim-W/t_f\) for \(W<1\), \(\dot\theta/\theta\sim-1/t_f\) for \(W>1\), and the logarithmic solution at \(W=1\) has the same leading logarithmic derivative \(-1/t_f\). Also \(\dot V/V=1/t_f\). Equating the leading \(1/t_f\) stress and strength rates gives

\[
\mathcal L[D_cW;x]=\sigma(x)\{a(x)-b(x)\min(W(x),1)\},
\]

or, using \(K=-\mathcal L\),

\[
K[D_cW]=\sigma\,[b\min(W,1)-a]. \tag{1}
\]

This is a nonlinear free-boundary problem: the support of \(W\) is unknown. A crack-tip or support-edge condition must rule out a nonphysical stress-rate singularity; for the crack equivalence used in the primary source this is the nonsingular-tip condition. A fixed support calculation is only a restricted model unless its boundary conditions also encode the moving-edge perturbations.

Equation (1) is exactly the equilibrium equation of a spatially heterogeneous, linearly slip-weakening crack. With \(u=D_cW\), the peak-to-residual weakening slope is \(\sigma b/D_c\), and the background overstress relative to peak strength is \(\sigma a\). An energy whose first variation is (1) is

\[
\Pi[W]=\frac12\langle D_cW,KD_cW\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
+\int_\Omega \sigma D_c\left[aW-bG(W)\right]dx,
\quad
G(W)=\begin{cases}W^2/2,&0\le W\le1,\\ W-1/2,&W\ge1.\end{cases} \tag{2}
\]

On an active patch, \(\delta\Pi/\delta W=D_c\{K[D_cW]+\sigma[a-b\min(W,1)]\}\). Hence stationary points of (2) solve (1) exactly. Outside the active patch, a unilateral/contact inequality must be supplied; equation (1) is imposed where the fault is sliding.

This equivalence is established by Viesca (2016, equations 3.9–3.10) and Ray–Viesca (2017, equations 20–21, pp. 8218–8219). It is a static profile equation, not yet a basin criterion for the original rate-and-state dynamics.

## What the full memory dynamics adds

The exact transformed aging-law variables used in Viesca (2016, equations 3.2–3.6) and Ray–Viesca (2017, equations 25–32) are

\[
\Phi=1-\frac{D_c}{V\theta},\qquad
s=-\log(t_f/t_0),\qquad V=\frac{D_cW}{t_f}.
\]

Under the assumptions above, the complete two-field similarity flow is

\[
W_s=W\left\{\frac{\mathcal L[D_cW]}{\sigma a}+\frac baW\Phi-1\right\},\qquad
\Phi_s=(1-\Phi)\left\{\frac{\mathcal L[D_cW]}{\sigma a}+\left(\frac ba-1\right)W\Phi\right\}. \tag{3}
\]

Its fixed points satisfy \(\Phi=1\) where \(W\le1\) and \(\Phi=1/W\) where \(W>1\); substituting either branch recovers (1). The split at \(W=1\) is the state-memory effect. It cannot be removed by replacing \(b\) everywhere with \(b-a\), which belongs to the small-perturbation steady-sliding calculation.

There is an exact gradient reduction on the all-\(W<1\) branch, including state perturbations. In a finite spatial discretization, take \(K=K^T>0\), and let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(D_c)\), \(S=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma)\); the products below are pointwise. Put \(Z=1-\Phi\),

\[
E(W)=\frac12(DW)^TK(DW)+\sum_i\sigma_iD_i\left(a_iW_i-\frac{b_i}{2}W_i^2\right),\qquad
M(W)=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{W_i}{\sigma_i a_iD_i}\right).
\]

Since \(\nabla E=D[KDW+S(a-bW)]\), equations (3) become exactly

\[
W_s=-M(W)\nabla E(W)-\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac baW^2\right)Z,\qquad
Z_s=-Z\odot\left[-\frac{KDW}{\sigma a}+\left(\frac ba-1\right)W(1-Z)\right]. \tag{4}
\]

Thus, for \(Z=0\) and \(W<1\), the slip-rate profile evolves by a gradient flow with positive diagonal mobility. For a profile \(W^*<1\) solving (1), the memory error has linear decay rate \(1-W_i^*>0\). The full linearization at \((W^*,Z=0)\) is block triangular:

\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}
-M_*H&-\operatorname{diag}((b/a)(W^*)^2)\\
0&-\operatorname{diag}(1-W^*)
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
H=DKD-\operatorname{diag}(\sigma D b). \tag{5}
\]

The nonlocal operator and all spatially varying \(D_c,\sigma,b\) occur in the self-adjoint Hessian \(H\). The coefficient \(a\) sets the profile/background loading and the positive mobility. Also \(HW^*=-\sigma D a\), so \(-M_*HW^*=W^*\). This eigenvalue \(+1\) is the known blow-up-time shift mode; homogeneous material also has a spatial-translation zero mode. Because \(-M_*H\) is similar to the symmetric matrix \(-M_*^{1/2}HM_*^{1/2}\), its other eigenvalues are real. For a fixed-active-set finite model, after fixing the blow-up time (and translation where present), the profile attracts exactly when \(H\) has one negative direction for the time-shift mode, no additional negative directions, and only the symmetry null modes. Equivalently, the Hessian's Morse index is one, modulo those symmetries.

This yields a computable variational profile criterion: solve (1) with its support/boundary conditions, then count the negative eigenvalues of (5), or use the min–max values

\[
\lambda_j(H)=\min_{\dim S=j}\;\max_{0\ne h\in S}\frac{h^THh}{h^Th}.
\]

It is a criterion for attraction to an asymptotic blow-up profile, not an energy-level test for whether an arbitrary initial earthquake will run away. The critical profile is a saddle: the negative direction in \(H\) is the blow-up-time gauge. Equal values of \(\Pi\) do not identify which side of a basin boundary a given state occupies.

For a fixed-active-set finite discretization, if \(W^*\) is strictly between 0 and 1, the time phase is fixed, and (5) has no other neutral or growing mode, the reduced Jacobian is Hurwitz. A computable local Lyapunov function then follows by solving \(J^TP+PJ=-I\): for error \(X=(W-W^*,Z)\) and a local remainder bound \(\|R(X)\|\le c\|X\|^2\), \(X^TPX\) decreases whenever \(\|X\|<1/(4\|P\|c)\). This is a local basin for the discretized similarity dynamics and includes the state-memory field. The phase reduction, moving support, and continuum limit still need proof before treating this as a general fault theorem.

### Exact homogeneous check

For a thin-slab fault with uniform coefficients, let \(\xi=x/L_{bh}\), \(L_{bh}=\sqrt{\bar E hD_c/(\sigma b)}\). When \(0<a/b<1/2\), Viesca's exact profile is \(W^*(\xi)=(a/b)(1+\cos\xi)\) on \((-\pi,\pi)\), with \(W^*<1\). The reduced energy, up to a positive scale, is

\[
E(W)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left[\frac12(W')^2-\frac12W^2+\frac abW\right]d\xi,
\quad H=-\partial_{\xi\xi}-1.
\]

With Dirichlet perturbations, the Hessian eigenvalues are \(n^2/4-1\): one negative mode, a zero translation mode, and positive remaining modes. The time mode has similarity growth rate \(+1\), while the other modes decay. This reproduces the source-reported attraction of the thin-slab profile for \(a/b<1/2\), modulo translation and blow-up time (Viesca 2016, equations 4.2 and 5.7–5.10). At \(a/b=1/2\), the profile touches \(W=1\), so the strict memory-decay gap closes; this is the boundary of this reduction.

For half-spaces, Viesca reports the all-\(W\le1\) stable similarity branch up to \(a/b\simeq0.3781\); beyond, the profile has a \(W>1\) region and the two-field stability analysis is needed. In the thin-slab case the profile likewise enters \(W>1\) for \(a/b>1/2\). These are model thresholds for similarity-profile attraction, not the steady-sliding stiffness threshold and not empirical rupture thresholds.

## Steady-sliding onset: exact memory-bearing operator pencil

Around steady sliding \(V=V_0\), \(\theta=D_c/V_0\), let \(u\) be slip perturbation and \(z=\delta\log\theta\). Linearizing aging and friction gives

\[
\dot z=-\dot u/D_c-(V_0/D_c)z,\qquad
-Ku=\sigma\left(a\dot u/V_0+bz\right).
\]

For an \(e^{st}\) perturbation, eliminate \(z=-su/(V_0+sD_c)\). The exact heterogeneous operator pencil is

\[
P(s)u=\left[K+\frac{s}{V_0}S A-sS B(V_0I+sD)^{-1}\right]u=0, \tag{6}
\]

where \(A=\operatorname{diag}(a)\), \(B=\operatorname{diag}(b)\). Multiplying pointwise by \(V_0I+sD\) gives the quadratic pencil

\[
\left[V_0K+s(DK+S(A-B))+s^2SAD/V_0\right]u=0. \tag{7}
\]

For one spring-slider, (7) gives

\[
\frac{\sigma aD_c}{V_0}s^2+[D_c k+\sigma(a-b)]s+V_0k=0,
\]

so the neutral stiffness is \(k_c=\sigma(b-a)/D_c\), exactly the Rice–Ruina result for this law. In a heterogeneous continuum, \(DK\) need not be symmetric and the state-memory factors do not commute with nonlocal elasticity. A single local stiffness or the smallest eigenvalue of \(K-S(B-A)/D\) is not, in general, the exact stability boundary; (6)–(7) retain the full frequency-dependent memory and spatial coupling.

### Two-patch finite counterexample to sitewise stiffness comparison

The script `two_patch_linear_counterexample.py` uses the exact pencil (7) with \(V_0=D_c=\sigma=1\), \(a=(0.5,0.5)\), \(b=(2.1,1.9)\), and grounded two-node stiffness

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The sitewise thresholds are \(k_{c,i}=b_i-a_i=(1.6,1.4)\), while both diagonal margins \(K_{ii}-k_{c,i}\) are positive, \((0.4,0.6)\). But the exact matrix pencil is \(0.5s^2I+s\begin{pmatrix}0.4&-1\\-1&0.6\end{pmatrix}+K\); its roots include \(0.50375171\pm1.32333586i\), an unstable collective oscillatory mode. The two-node model is a finite grounded elastic network, not a calibrated half-space kernel or empirical fault. It refutes the sitewise diagonal comparison, not the properly posed operator-pencil analysis.

## Nonlinear obstruction beyond the variational branch

For \(W>1\), the fixed point uses \(\Phi=1/W\), and (4) no longer reduces to a gradient flow in \(W\) with a decaying memory error: the second field feeds back at leading order. The static energy (2) still has the correct Euler equation, but its Hessian alone is not the full two-field stability operator. Viesca (2016, §§5–6) reports eigenvalue crossings and cascades of Hopf bifurcations in the transformed flow (thin-slab: symmetry-breaking at \(a/b=1/2\), first even Hopf near 0.94; half-space thresholds differ), and reports limit cycles after Hopf. Those calculations rule out a global smooth gradient-flow representation with symmetric positive-definite mobility in the corresponding regimes: such a flow has a Jacobian similar to a symmetric matrix at equilibrium and cannot have a nonreal pair. A verified periodic orbit would also rule out any strict Lyapunov function on its full basin. The published spectral/Hopf values are source-reported numerical analysis; they have not been independently reproduced here.

This leaves the central finite-amplitude question open: the stable manifold of the critical profile, with the full \((V,\theta)\) state and the appropriate dynamic/radiation terms, determines which perturbations arrest and which enter runaway. An energy value by itself is not that manifold. The finite-dimensional local Lyapunov construction above only certifies trajectories already near a known similarity profile after phase fixing. At high slip rates the quasi-static law may cease to apply; no model result here validates an earthquake forecast.

## Phase II — full-memory stability, the time gauge, and a local nonlinear result

This section replaces the final sentence of v1's “next mathematical gate” only as to the fixed-support full-memory calculation: the weighted linear operator and a relative stable-manifold Lyapunov function are now derived below. The moving-support theorem and a physical arrest/runaway classification remain open.

### Exact finite-dimensional spectrum, with signs and multiplicities

Work on a fixed finite active set with N nodes, K=Kᵀ>0, and a profile u=W* satisfying 0<u_i<1. Write p_i=b_i/a_i,

\[
M_* = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{u_i}{\sigma_i a_iD_i}\right),\quad
H=DKD-\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_iD_i b_i),\quad
A=-M_*H,\quad
B=-\operatorname{diag}(p_i u_i^2),\quad
G=\operatorname{diag}(1-u_i).
\]

The full linearization in (w,z)=(W-u,Z) is w'=Aw+Bz, z'=-Gz. In particular, A, not H, determines the growth rates. Since M_*>0,

\[
M_*^{-1/2} A M_*^{1/2}=-M_*^{1/2}HM_*^{1/2}=:T.
\]

Thus A has only real eigenvalues. Congruence preserves the inertia of H and M_*^{1/2}HM_*^{1/2}; the sign reversal in T means that the number of positive growth eigenvalues of A equals the number of negative directions of H. This is an inertia statement, not equality of the unweighted Hessian eigenvalues and growth rates.

The equilibrium equation gives Hu=-σDa, so Au=u and

\[
u^THu=-\sum_i \sigma_iD_i a_i u_i<0.
\]

This proves at least one negative direction of H and one +1 eigenvalue. It does not prove that this is the only growing mode. Exactly one growing mode follows only under the additional, checkable hypothesis ind_-(H)=1. If H has a translation kernel, it must be removed by an actual symmetry restriction/phase condition or handled as a center mode; the theorem below assumes a pinned profile or a fixed symmetry subspace on which H is nonsingular. The full block-triangular Jacobian has spectrum spec(A)∪{-G_ii}. A repeated negative eigenvalue between these blocks can create a Jordan block, but cannot create an additional growing eigenvalue.

### A computable nonlinear theorem on the stable manifold

Assume, in addition to the strict 0<u_i<1 margins, that H is nonsingular and has exactly one negative eigenvalue. Let

\[
\ell^T=\frac{u^TM_*^{-1}}{u^TM_*^{-1}u},\qquad \ell^Tu=1.
\]

Then \ell^TA=\ell^T. The A-stable subspace is E_s=ker ell^T, and H is positive definite on E_s: for v∈E_s, v^THu=-v^TM_*^{-1}u=0, while a negative direction in E_s would give a second negative direction independent of u. The full Jacobian has one unstable eigenvalue +1 and all other eigenvalues strictly in the left half-plane. Finite-dimensional stable-manifold theory therefore gives a local C² invariant stable manifold S of codimension one.

The tangent space of S has a useful explicit graph. Decompose w=v+uα, with v∈E_s, and solve the linearized equations for the coefficient α=ell^T w. Since

\[
\alpha'=\alpha+\ell^TBz,\qquad z'=-Gz,
\]

the coefficient of the growing e^s mode vanishes exactly when

\[
\alpha=-\ell^TB(I+G)^{-1}z=:\chi^Tz. \tag{8}
\]

Thus T_(u,0)S={(v+uχ^Tz,z):v∈E_s}. This equation exposes the role of memory: a nonzero state perturbation generally requires a compensating component in the time-gauge direction to lie on the profile's stable manifold.

On the full all-W<1 branch, set y=∇E(W), C(W)=diag(p_iW_i²), and

\[
q_i(W,Z)=-\frac{(KDW)_i}{\sigma_i a_i}+(p_i-1)W_i(1-Z_i).
\]

The exact equations are W'=-M(W)y-C(W)Z and Z_i'=-q_iZ_i. Therefore the profile energy satisfies

\[
\frac{dE}{ds}=-y^TM(W)y-y^TC(W)Z. \tag{9}
\]

At (u,0), q_i=1-u_i. Put δ=min_i(1-u_i)>0. On a sufficiently small ball, q_i≥δ/2, M(W)⪰mI>0, and ||C(W)||≤c. Also Z_i≥0 is forward invariant because each state equation is multiplicative, and W_i>0 is invariant for the same reason.

Define the state-error storage and augmented functional

\[
R_Z(Z)=\frac12\|Z\|_2^2,\qquad \mathcal V(W,Z)=E(W)-E(u)+\gamma R_Z(Z).
\]

This is a quadratic state-error term, not the Kullback–Leibler relative entropy (which is singular when its reference state is Z*=0). Young's inequality and the state equation give, while the trajectory stays in that ball,

\[
\mathcal V'
\le-\frac m2\|y\|_2^2-
\left(\frac{\gamma\delta}{2}-\frac{c^2}{2m}\right)\|Z\|_2^2. \tag{10}
\]

The quadratic part of E(W)-E(u) is 1/2 w^THw. On the tangent graph (8), it is

\[
\frac12v^THv+\frac12(u^THu)(\chi^TZ)^2.
\]

Consequently V is positive definite on T_(u,0)S if

\[
\gamma>(-u^THu)\|\chi\|_2^2,
\]

and it decreases strictly there if also γ>c²/(mδ). Choose γ larger than both bounds. Positive definiteness persists on a sufficiently small neighborhood in the nonlinear manifold S. Since H is nonsingular, y=Hw+O(||w||²) controls ||w|| locally. Equation (10) then gives a strict local Lyapunov function on S, and every sufficiently close physically admissible point of S converges exponentially to (u,0) in similarity time.

This is a relative local basin theorem for the finite, fixed-support model. It gives a local invariant neighborhood inside a codimension-one stable manifold, with inequalities (8)–(10) and constants computable from the profile, coefficients, and local derivative bounds. It is not an open basin in the unphased (W,Z) space: the single excluded direction is the +1 blow-up-time gauge. The calculation does not prove that changing the guessed t_i defines a smooth nonlinear quotient, and it does not show that either side of this gauge direction means “arrest” or “runaway.” If translation is a symmetry, a second phase condition is needed before applying this result.

For a dense N-node model, assembling K and H costs O(N²) storage; a dense eigendecomposition of H or T costs O(N³) arithmetic. The tangent memory graph (8) costs O(N) once the left eigenvector is known because B and G are diagonal. The nonlinear stable manifold and a certified basin radius require higher-order remainder bounds; the eigensolve does not provide them, and no general complexity or mesh-uniform bound is established here.

### What the state entropy does and does not buy

On 0<W<1, Z*=0 lies on the boundary of the physical cone Z≥0. A standard relative entropy Z log(Z/Z*)-Z+Z* is not defined there. The quadratic R_Z=1/2||Z||² is regular and, as shown above, can augment the crack/profile energy into a genuine Lyapunov function on the local stable manifold under strict memory-gap and index-one assumptions. It is not a global selection functional on all states.

On the W>1 branch the fixed point has Z*=1-1/W*>0, so a KL term is defined, but its derivative is

\[
\frac{d}{ds}\sum_i\left[Z_i\log\frac{Z_i}{Z_i^*}-Z_i+Z_i^*\right]
=-\sum_i Z_i\log\frac{Z_i}{Z_i^*}\,q_i(W,Z).
\]

The factors q_i depend on the nonlocal elastic field and change sign near a coupled instability; this expression has no fixed sign. A separable “profile energy plus state entropy” therefore does not yield a global Lyapunov inequality by this argument.

A nonreal eigenpair only rules out a smooth gradient representation X'=-G(X)∇F(X) with G(X) symmetric positive definite: at an equilibrium its Jacobian is similar to a symmetric matrix. It does not rule out Lyapunov functions. For example,

\[
x'=-x-y,\qquad y'=x-y,\qquad F=x^2+y^2,
\]

has eigenvalues -1±i and F'=-2F. In contrast, a genuine nonconstant periodic orbit rules out a strictly decreasing Lyapunov function on any region containing that orbit, since integrating F'<0 over one period contradicts return to the same state. Viesca (2016, §5, especially the discussion following the first Hopf bifurcation) reports numerically observed periodic oscillations after Hopf and discusses limit cycles; the source's Hopf and oscillation evidence is model evidence, not an independent proof of an exact periodic orbit for heterogeneous faults. This evidence blocks an unsupported global-gradient claim but does not exclude every possible non-strict or basin-restricted Lyapunov construction.

### Fixed-support continuum linearization and the edge obstruction

There is a formal continuum counterpart that clarifies what a fixed-support theorem would require. Let Ω_a be a prescribed active domain and write m(x)=W*(x)/(σ(x)a(x)D_c(x)), g(x)=1-W*(x), C_*(x)=(b/a)(W*)². Suppose K is self-adjoint positive on the chosen displacement domain, the profile is strictly below one with g(x)≥δ>0, m(x)>0 almost everywhere in Ω_a, and the Hessian form

\[
h[w]=\|K^{1/2}(D_cw)\|_2^2-\int_{\Omega_a}\sigma D_cb\,|w|^2dx
\]

has a closed realization under the fixed active-set boundary conditions. In the weighted space

\[
\mathcal H_m=L^2(\Omega_a,m^{-1}dx),\qquad
\langle u,v\rangle_m=\int_{\Omega_a}\frac{u\bar v}{m}dx,
\]

the slip part A=-M_*H is formally symmetric because

\[
\langle u,-M_*Hv\rangle_m=-\langle u,Hv\rangle_{L^2}.
\]

Under the corresponding closed-form hypothesis, the unitary map u↦m^{-1/2}u identifies it with the form realization of -m^{1/2}Hm^{1/2}. This weighted formulation does not require m^{-1} to be bounded. It shows how a vanishing mobility at an edge can still admit a self-adjoint linear stability problem, provided the active domain and form domain are fixed. The associated generalized Rayleigh quotient is

\[
\lambda[w]=-\frac{h[w]}{\int_{\Omega_a}|w|^2/m\,dx}.
\]

Negative directions of h are positive growth directions, but an index count alone does not give a spectral gap: the essential spectrum may approach zero. A stable continuum profile therefore also needs a verified gap on the complement of the +1 time mode and any spatial symmetries. The source-reported thin-layer singular spectrum includes a stable continuous part bounded away from zero in its stated homogeneous setting; that does not establish the gap for arbitrary heterogeneity.

The full continuum linearization remains upper triangular: z_s=-g z, and the off-diagonal map z↦-C_*z is bounded from ordinary L² into H_m if p,σ,a,D_c,W* are bounded, because

\[
\frac{C_*^2}{m}=p^2\sigma aD_c(W^*)^3.
\]

Thus, under a closed-form realization and a stable spectral gap, the full linear model has the same one-mode count as the weighted slip operator plus memory decay at rate at least δ. A repeated negative eigenvalue can produce polynomial transient factors; any smaller decay rate than the spectral gap still controls such finite Jordan multiplicities.

This does not yet give the physical moving-support continuum theorem. If W*=0 on a nonempty exterior, m=0 there and the weighted space must be restricted to the active set. Perturbing the support then changes the domain and introduces a unilateral contact/free-boundary condition; the fixed-domain Hessian does not count those shape variations. Even when m>0 almost everywhere but tends to zero at the edge, M^{-1/2} is unbounded in ordinary L², and uniform mesh coercivity or nonlinear smoothing cannot be inferred from the finite-grid similarity. At W*=1, the memory gap δ vanishes and the all-W<1 theorem stops. At W*=0, the mobility and the state-coupling coefficient both degenerate, so an edge theorem must choose weighted spaces and prove the active-set complementarity rather than insert the boundary node into the interior result.

The exact continuum energy identity on a fixed all-W<1 active set is nevertheless useful. For strong solutions with all integrals finite,

\[
E'=-\int m(W)|y|^2dx-\int pW^2yZ\,dx
\le-\frac12\int m(W)|y|^2dx+
\frac12\int p^2\sigma aD_cW^3 Z^2dx.
\]

If q≥δ/2 and p²σaD_cW³≤C_0, then adding (γ/2)∫Z²dx with γ>C_0/δ makes the derivative strictly dissipative in the weighted energy-gradient and state-error terms. This is an exact conditional dissipation estimate. To turn it into a continuum nonlinear basin theorem still requires: a well-posed fixed-domain or moving-domain evolution in a specified function space; coercivity of the augmented energy on the nonlinear stable manifold; a spectral gap; and bounds uniform at the support edge. None is supplied by the profile Euler equation alone.

## Additional result — selecting blow-up time gives an open physical basin

The codimension-one stable manifold in similarity coordinates is the correct place to encode convergence when the blow-up time T has been fixed in advance. It need not be a codimension-one set of physical initial conditions: changing T moves the transformed initial velocity along the +1 gauge direction. In the exact autonomous model, this can be proved locally rather than assumed.

Fix a finite node model with fixed positive normal stress, no external shear stressing term, and the rate-and-state law and elasticity used above. In this case the stress-rate balance is exactly

\[
\sigma_i\left(a_i\frac{\dot V_i}{V_i}+b_i\frac{\dot\theta_i}{\theta_i}\right)
=\mathcal L[V]_i,\qquad
\dot\theta_i=1-\frac{V_i\theta_i}{D_i}.
\]

For any candidate T>t_0, define \(\tau=T-t\), \(s=-\log(\tau/\tau_c)\), and

\[
W_i=\frac{\tau V_i}{D_i},\qquad Z_i=\frac{D_i}{V_i\theta_i}.
\]

Because \(\dot\tau=-1\), this is the exact autonomous similarity system previously used, for every candidate T, not only for the actual blow-up time. At the initial time t_0, the coordinate map is

\[
\Gamma(T;V_0,\theta_0)
=\left(\frac{(T-t_0)V_0}{D},\,\frac{D}{V_0\theta_0}\right). \tag{11}
\]

Products and quotients are componentwise. At fixed \(\tau>0\) the inverse map is \(V=DW/\tau\), \(\theta=\tau/(WZ)\), so positive finite \((V,\theta)\) and positive \((W,Z)\) correspond smoothly. The initial value of s depends on T, but the exact reduced system is autonomous, so shifting the origin of s does not change whether its trajectory converges to the fixed point.

Take a profile u satisfying the finite-dimensional hypotheses in the preceding section, and its local stable manifold S. Its tangent graph (8) allows points with Z>0 arbitrarily close to zero: choose a small positive z and v=0, then the nonlinear manifold has W=u+uχᵀz+O(||z||²), which remains strictly between zero and one for sufficiently small z. Choose one such point q=(W̄,Z̄)∈S with Z̄_i>0, and any \(\tau_0>0\). It corresponds to a finite, positive physical initial state

\[
V_{0,i}=\frac{D_i\bar W_i}{\tau_0},\qquad
\theta_{0,i}=\frac{\tau_0}{\bar W_i\bar Z_i},\qquad
T_0=t_0+\tau_0. \tag{12}
\]

Let F(W,Z)=0 be a C² local defining equation for S near q. Its conormal at (u,0), normalized by \(\ell^Tu=1\), is \((\ell^T,-\chi^T)\); at q it differs by O(||q-(u,0)||). Holding the physical state fixed and differentiating (11) with respect to T gives

\[
\partial_T W_0=\frac{V_0}{D}=\frac{\bar W}{\tau_0},\qquad
\partial_T Z_0=0.
\]

Therefore, at (T_0,V_0,\theta_0),

\[
\partial_T\bigl[F(\Gamma(T;V_0,\theta_0))\bigr]
=\frac{1+O(\|q-(u,0)\|)}{\tau_0}\ne0
\]

when q is chosen sufficiently close to (u,0). The implicit-function theorem supplies neighborhoods U of (V_0,\theta_0) in the positive physical state space and I of T_0, plus a unique smooth T=T(X) for X∈U, such that

\[
\Gamma(T(X);X)\in S.
\]

Every such transformed trajectory remains in the local stable set and converges to (u,0). Reconstructing the physical variables gives \(V_i(t)=D_iW_i(s)/(T(X)-t)\); since u_i>0, the slip rate diverges at the finite time T(X) with the selected self-similar profile. Thus U is an open set of physical initial conditions that run away in this exact reduced model. The blow-up time is determined locally by the condition of lying on S; its derivative can be obtained by differentiating the scalar equation \(F(\Gamma(T(X);X))=0\).

This closes one local phase-selection gap: the +1 direction is exactly the derivative generated by varying the candidate T, and the stable manifold becomes an open runaway basin after T is allowed to adjust. The result is stronger than linear profile stability because it gives nonlinear finite-time divergence for an open physical neighborhood. It still does not give a threshold separating arrest from runaway away from that neighborhood, characterize the global basin boundary, or validate the constitutive model as an earthquake forecast. The baseline state in (12) is a mathematical model state near a known runaway-profile orbit, not an empirically observed fault state.

The v3 argument above assumes fixed normal stress and zero external shear-stressing rate, so its similarity equations are autonomous. The v4 addendum below proves persistence of the open basin for bounded C^1 external shear stressing with fixed normal stress. Time-varying normal stress and merely bounded measurable shear forcing remain unresolved.

## Addendum — bounded external shear loading

The exact open-basin argument in v3 assumed zero external shear-stressing rate. A smooth bounded stressing rate can be included because its contribution is multiplied by the remaining time in the similarity variables. Here normal stress and friction parameters remain time independent.

Let \(r_i(t)=\dot\tau_{0,i}(t)/(\sigma_i a_i)\), and assume \(r\) and \(\dot r\) are bounded on the time interval considered. The stress-rate balance is

\[
\sigma_i\left(a_i\frac{\dot V_i}{V_i}+b_i\frac{\dot\theta_i}{\theta_i}\right)
=\mathcal L[V]_i+\dot\tau_{0,i}(t),\qquad
\dot\theta_i=1-\frac{V_i\theta_i}{D_i}.
\]

With \(Z_i=D_i/(V_i\theta_i)\), \(\tau=T-t\), and \(W_i=\tau V_i/D_i\), direct differentiation gives

\[
W_s=F_W(W,Z)+\tau\,W\odot r(t),\qquad
Z_s=F_Z(W,Z)-\tau\,Z\odot r(t), \tag{13}
\]

where \(F=(F_W,F_Z)\) is the autonomous vector field in (3)–(4), and \(\odot\) is componentwise multiplication. For a fixed physical start time \(t_0\), write \(\tau_0=T-t_0\) and \(\eta=-\log(\tau/\tau_0)\). Then \(t=t_0+\tau_0(1-e^{-\eta})\), and (13) becomes

\[
X_\eta=F(X)+\tau_0 e^{-\eta}R\!\left(X,t_0+\tau_0(1-e^{-\eta})\right),\qquad
R(W,Z,t)=(W\odot r(t),-Z\odot r(t)). \tag{14}
\]

On any fixed neighborhood of the profile, bounded \(r,\dot r\) imply

\[
\| \tau_0e^{-\eta}R\|_{C^1_X}\le C\tau_0e^{-\eta},\qquad
\|\partial_{\tau_0}(\tau_0e^{-\eta}R)\|_{C^1_X}\le C e^{-\eta}, \tag{15}
\]

with C independent of sufficiently small \(\tau_0\). The second estimate uses the bounded time derivative of r.

Assume the finite-dimensional autonomous profile hypotheses from v3: the similarity Jacobian has one simple eigenvalue +1, while its remaining spectrum lies strictly in the left half-plane, with no translation zero mode in the chosen pinned/phase-fixed subspace. Write deviations from \((u,0)\) as \(x=(a,y)\), where a is the +1 coordinate and \(y\) is in the stable subspace. In these coordinates,

\[
a'=a+N_u(x)+\mathcal R_u(\eta,x;\tau_0),\qquad
y'=J_s y+N_s(x)+\mathcal R_s(\eta,x;\tau_0), \tag{16}
\]

where \(N(x)=O(\|x\|^2)\), \(J_s\) is Hurwitz, and \(\mathcal R\) is the forcing in (14) with the bounds (15). Choose \(0<\beta<\min\{1,\gamma_s\}\), where \(\gamma_s\) is any exponential decay rate for \(e^{J_s\eta}\). For a prescribed small stable coordinate \(y_0\), a solution tending to the profile must satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
a(\eta)&=-\int_\eta^\infty e^{\eta-v}
   \bigl[N_u(x(v))+\mathcal R_u(v,x(v);\tau_0)\bigr]\,dv,\\
y(\eta)&=e^{J_s\eta}y_0+
   \int_0^\eta e^{J_s(\eta-v)}
   \bigl[N_s(x(v))+\mathcal R_s(v,x(v);\tau_0)\bigr]\,dv .
\end{aligned} \tag{17}
\]

On the Banach space with norm \(\|x\|_\beta=\sup_{\eta\ge0}e^{\beta\eta}\|x(\eta)\|\), the forcing terms in (17) have norm at most \(C\tau_0\); the quadratic terms have Lipschitz constant \(O(\|x\|_\beta)\). For sufficiently small \(\|y_0\|\) and \(\tau_0\), (17) is a contraction on a small ball. It defines a \(C^1\) nonautonomous stable graph \(a=\psi_{\tau_0}(y_0)\), whose trajectories converge to \((u,0)\). Differentiating the contraction equation with respect to \(\tau_0\), using the second bound in (15), gives \(\|\partial_{\tau_0}\psi_{\tau_0}\|\le C'\) locally. The graph differs from the autonomous one by \(O(\tau_0)\). This is a finite-dimensional Lyapunov–Perron argument; it does not use a continuum stable-manifold theorem.

Let \(F_{\tau_0}(W,Z)=0\) be a local defining equation for this nonautonomous stable set at the initial similarity time \(\eta=0\), normalized so that its W-normal tends to \(\ell^T\) as \(\tau_0\to0\) and the point approaches \((u,0)\). The physical-to-similarity map remains

\[
\Gamma(T;X)=\left(\frac{(T-t_0)V_0}{D},\,\frac{D}{V_0\theta_0}\right),\qquad X=(V_0,\theta_0),
\]

and its T derivative is \((W_0/\tau_0,0)\). The \(\tau_0\)-derivative of \(F_{\tau_0}\) is bounded by the stable-graph estimate. Hence, at a sufficiently late baseline with \(\tau_0>0\) small and \(W_0\) close to u,

\[
\partial_T\!\left[F_{\tau_0}(\Gamma(T;X))\right]
=\frac{1+O(\|W_0-u\|+\|Z_0\|+\tau_0)}{\tau_0}+O(1)\ne0. \tag{18}
\]

Choose a point on this stable set with \(Z_0>0\), which exists by the stable graph and positivity invariance, and map it back to finite \(V_0,\theta_0>0\). The implicit-function theorem applied to (18) gives an open neighborhood of physical initial states \(X\) and a C^1 selected blow-up time \(T(X)>t_0\) for which the transformed trajectory lies on the nonautonomous stable set. Reconstruction again yields \(V_i(t)\sim D_i u_i/(T(X)-t)\). Thus the open finite-time runaway basin persists for bounded \(C^1\) external shear loading in the finite pinned model.

This extension uses bounded \(\dot\tau_0\) and bounded \(\ddot\tau_0\) (equivalently bounded r and \(\dot r\)); it does not cover merely measurable bounded forcing, time-varying normal stress, or loss of the fixed active-set assumptions. The size of the guaranteed physical neighborhood can shrink with \(\tau_0\), and no uniform lower bound on that size is proved. This remains a local runaway guarantee near a constructed profile orbit; it does not provide a global arrest/runaway threshold, a real earthquake forecast, or a moving-support continuum theorem.
