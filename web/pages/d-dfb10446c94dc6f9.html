## Question and interface

For each n≥3 and 0<β<1/2, can one construct real smooth V,u on all of R^n, u≠0, such that

    −Δu+Vu=0,
    |V(x)|≤C(1+|x|)^(−β),
    |u(x)|≤C exp(−c|x|^p),    p=(4−2β)/3 ?

No derivative bounds on V, sign condition, prescribed norm, prescribed eigenvalue other than zero, explicit finite algorithm for the global cover, or uniform constants as β→1/2 are supplied or claimed. Both V and u are constructed together. This is not acquisition of an unknown physical potential.

**Author claims:** FI prove the bounded-potential β=0 case with p=4/3 (Theorem 1). Their Sections 3–10 construct separated tangent waves, prove sparse small-value sets, solve a weighted Dirichlet correction there, and smooth at a variable radius. Their Appendix A explains the two error scales a/ℓ and a²ℓ/r. Filonov–Krymskii 2311.14491v2 previously use harmonic repair and variable smoothing on a cylinder; their Theorem 0.1 is not a global radial-decay result on R^n. Cruz-Sampedro 1999, Theorems 1–2, constructs the exponent p for complex power-decaying potentials on R² (Theorem2), and supplies the matching unique-continuation upper restriction in every dimension n≥2 (Theorem1). These construction and upper-bound dimensions must not be conflated.

**Our deduction proposed below:** changing the macroscopic patch scale to ℓ=√(R/a), and retaining the microscopic scale 1/a, gives the same exponent for smooth real potentials when n≥3. The harmonic correction must gain κ/a² rather than merely 1/a². We prove that gain by reconstructing a general weighted sparse-domain estimate.

## 1. Scales and their exact balance

Fix β∈(0,1/2). Set

    α=(1−2β)/3 ∈ (0,1/3),
    s=(1−α)/2, d=(1+α)/2,
    R(x)=(K²+|x|²)^(1/2), a=R^α, ℓ=R^s, κ=R^(−β),
    Φ(x)=(R(x)^(1+α)−K^(1+α))/(1+α), E=exp(−Φ).

The large constant K≥1 is chosen last. All constants before that choice may depend on n,α and fixed covering constants, but not on K. Then

    a/ℓ = a²ℓ/R = κ,
    ℓ/R = 1/(aℓ) = κ/a² = R^(−d),
    ℓ^(−2)≤κ, a/R≤κ, R^(−2)≤κ.

The derivative identities and estimates are

    ∇Φ=x R^(α−1), |∇Φ|≤a,
    |∇Φ|²−a²=−K²R^(2α−2),
    ΔΦ=nR^(α−1)+(α−1)|x|²R^(α−3),
    |D²Φ|≤C a/R,
    |∇a|≤C a/R, |D²a|≤C a/R²,
    |∇ℓ|≤C ℓ/R.

In particular ℓ is as slowly varying as required for a bounded-overlap cover when K is large. On a ball of radius Cℓ(x), R,a,ℓ,κ are mutually comparable to their values at x, and

    |a(y)²−a(x)²|≤C a(x)² ℓ(x)/R(x)=Cκ(x).

On a microscopic ball |y−x|≤ρ/a(x), 0<ρ≤1, all the power weights are uniformly comparable, and

    exp(−Cρ)≤E(y)/E(x)≤exp(Cρ).

These constants are uniform in K sufficiently large. This last assertion uses |∇Φ|≤a and comparability along the segment, not comparison of E across a macro ball.

## 2. Cover, coloring, and an approximate solution

Here are the needed covering facts and their proof mechanism, to make clear where FI is used. A positive radius r with |∇r|≤1/3 admits a countable cover by balls of radius cr(x_j), with enlarged balls of radius Cr(x_j) having bounded overlap and bounded intersection degree. Select a maximal disjoint family of balls of radius c_0 r(x_j). Lipschitz comparability makes their fixed enlargements cover. At a point of an enlarged ball, all other intersecting centers have comparable radii and lie in a fixed larger ball; packing the disjoint cores bounds their number by a constant depending only on dimension and enlargement ratios. Countability follows from disjoint cores containing rational points. Smooth bumps on those balls, divided by their positive locally finite sum, give nonnegative χ_j with

    Σχ_j=1, supp χ_j⊂B(x_j,Cℓ_j),
    |D^mχ_j|≤C_m ℓ_j^(−m),  m=1,2.

Apply this construction with r=ℓ and fixed sufficiently small enlargement constants. It is exactly the radius-independent geometric mechanism of FI Lemmas 6–9. The graph of overlapping enlarged balls has degree at most D_n. A microscopic ball sees at most M_n indices, since 1/a≪ℓ uniformly once K is large; use a further fixed enlargement and the same packing argument.

For each center x_j≠0 choose t_j∈S^(n−1) perpendicular to x_j. At x_j=0 choose any auxiliary normal first. Choose the directions greedily so that for overlapping enlarged balls

    min(|t_i−t_j|,|t_i+t_j|)≥δ_n>0.

This is possible because each tangent sphere is S^(n−2), and the measure of its intersection with any ambient cap of radius δ is at most C_nδ^(n−2). At most 2D_n caps are forbidden. Choose δ_n with 2D_n C_nδ_n^(n−2)<1. This is the decisive n≥3 step (FI Lemma 11); it is not valid on the two-point tangent sphere in n=2.

Define

    q(x)=Σ_j χ_j(x) cos(a_j t_j·(x−x_j)), a_j=a(x_j), f=Eq.

Then q is real smooth, |q|≤1, |∇q|≤Ca and |D²q|≤Ca². Expand its Laplacian. Terms containing derivatives of χ_j have sizes at most ℓ^(−2) and a/ℓ; the mismatch between a_j² and a(x)² has size at most a²ℓ/R. Therefore

    |Δq+a²q|≤Cκ.

The gradient cross term also obeys |∇Φ·∇q|≤Cκ. The cutoff part has size a/ℓ. For each plane-wave part, tangency gives x·t_j=(x−x_j)·t_j, so

    |∇Φ(x)·(a_j t_j)|
      ≤C a_j R(x)^(α−1) ℓ_j
      ≤C a(x)²ℓ(x)/R(x)=Cκ(x).

This estimate is valid near the origin too: it does not divide by |x|.

Using Δ(Eq)/E=Δq−2∇Φ·∇q+(|∇Φ|²−ΔΦ)q proves

    |Δf|≤CE[κ+K² R^(2α−2)|q|].                    (A)

Also |∇f|≤CaE and |D²f|≤Ca²E. The second summand in (A) is important near the regularized origin; it must not be silently absorbed into a K-independent multiple of κ.

## 3. Uniform sparsity near zeros

Fix 0<ρ≤1 and 0<θ<1. At each x write y=x+z/a(x), |z|≤ρ. Freeze the partition weights at x:

    P_x(z)=Σ_j χ_j(x) cos(ξ_j·z+γ_j),
    ξ_j=(a_j/a(x))t_j, γ_j=a_jt_j·(x−x_j).

There are at most M_n nonzero terms, coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one, 1/2≤|ξ_j|≤2, and distinct frequency pairs remain separated by δ_n/2 for K sufficiently large. Indeed a_j/a(x)=1+O(ℓ/R)=1+O(K^(−d)) wherever χ_j(x)>0. The freezing error, including terms whose support enters the micro ball, is

    sup_|z|≤ρ |q(x+z/a(x))−P_x(z)|≤Cρ/(aℓ)≤Cρ K^(−d).  (B)

All such finite trigonometric polynomials form a compact family in C^0(B(0,ρ)). None vanishes identically: restrict the distinct ±ξ_j exponentials to a generic line and use the Vandermonde determinant; at least one coefficient is nonzero. Each consequently has a measure-zero analytic zero set. Compactness gives a uniform h>0 with

    |{|P|<4h}∩B(0,ρ)|≤θ |B(0,ρ)|

for every family member. Otherwise uniformly convergent P_k with thresholds h_k↓0 would yield a limit with a positive-measure zero set. Choose τ=3h, decreased if necessary to τ≤3/4, and K large enough that (B)≤h. Then

    Ω={|q|<τ},
    |Ω∩B(x,ρ/a(x))|≤θ |B(x,ρ/a(x))|, all x.       (C)

This reconstructs FI Lemmas 17–20 and Proposition 21 at the changed macro scale. It does not assume quantitative transversality of the individual zeros.

## 4. Reusable sparse-domain weighted inversion lemma

**Our reconstructed lemma.** Suppose a≥a_min>0 and ρ/a has a bounded-overlap comparable-radius cover. Suppose an open U satisfies (C) with Ω replaced by U. Let h>0 be smooth, hE∈L², and suppose a,h,E have uniform comparability on B(x,ρ/a(x)). For θ sufficiently small depending on those local comparability constants and n, the Dirichlet solution of

    −Δz=hE on U, z∈H¹_0(U)

satisfies

    0≤z≤Cρ² h a^(−2)E.                           (D)

The constants are independent of U and its boundary geometry. For our two weights h_1=κ and h_2=R^(2α−2), they can be chosen independent of large K and 0<ρ≤1.

**Proof.** Extend v∈H¹_0(U) by zero. On each micro-cover ball it vanishes on at least the fraction 1−θ. Subtract its ball average and apply usual Poincaré; the zero-set fraction bounds that average. Summing over a bounded-overlap cover yields

    ∫a²|v|²≤Cρ²∫|∇v|².

Thus the Dirichlet form is coercive on H¹_0(U), and Lax–Milgram gives a unique solution for any L² right side. Testing with the negative part gives z≥0. Its zero extension Z satisfies −ΔZ≤hE 1_U distributionally. One proof uses the test φ min(1,z/ε), φ≥0, in the weak Dirichlet equation; the additional gradient term is nonnegative. Let ε↓0, using ∇z=0 a.e. on {z=0}.

For any x∈U and b>0, subtract the ball Green potential for (hE)1_U from Z. The remainder is distributionally subharmonic. The mean-value inequality and the fundamental-solution bound for n≥3 yield

    z(x)≤C_n∫_(U∩B(x,b)) h(y)E(y)|x−y|^(2−n)dy
           +C_n b^(−n)∫_(U∩B(x,b)) z(y)dy.       (E)

Take b=ρ/a(x), and first solve on a bounded smooth subdomain D⊂U. Put Ψ=ρ²ha^(−2)E and M_D=sup_D z/Ψ<∞. Local comparison bounds the first term of (E) by CΨ(x). The second is at most Cθ M_D Ψ(x), by (C). Taking sup gives M_D≤C+CθM_D. Choose θ with Cθ≤1/2. Hence z≤2CΨ on every such D, uniformly. Exhaust U by nested bounded smooth domains. The coercive energy estimate bounds these solutions in H¹_0(U), their weak limit solves the global problem, and the closed convex order interval 0≤z≤2CΨ is weakly closed in L². Interior regularity upgrades its bound pointwise. This proves (D). This is the argument of FI Lemmas 22–27, with an arbitrary locally comparable weight replacing A^(−k).

The gain a^(−2) is the load-bearing fact. An unweighted global norm estimate, or just asserting that division by q is harmless, would not prove this construction.

## 5. Harmonic repair and parameter order

Fix θ small enough for (D) for both h_1,h_2. Fix ρ>0 small enough that the product of the constants in (A) and (D) times ρ² is ≤1/40. Then obtain τ>0 from Section 3 for these θ,ρ. Finally choose K sufficiently large for all cover, separation, freezing conditions and

    Cρ² K^(−d)≤τ/40.

Solve −Δw=Δf on Ω, w∈H¹_0(Ω). The right side is L² by (A). Compare its positive and negative parts against the two solutions in (D). Since |q|<τ on Ω,

    |w|≤Cρ² E[κ/a²+τK²/R²]
         =Cρ² E[R^(−d)+τK²/R²]
         ≤(τ/20)E.                              (F)

The second summand is controlled by ρ before τ is chosen, since K²/R²≤1. The first is controlled by K after τ is chosen. No circular choice of parameters occurs. Moreover, for fixed K,

    |w|≤C_K κ a^(−2)E.                          (G)

For the second term, K²R^(−2)/(κa^(−2))=K²R^(d−2)≤K^d. Let W be the zero extension of w and f*=f+W. Then f*∈H¹_loc, harmonic and smooth on Ω, and |f*|≤(1+τ/20)E globally. No assertion of smoothness across ∂Ω is made.

## 6. Variable-radius smoothing, without differentiating W

Choose a nonnegative smooth normalized radial ψ supported in B(0,1). Let σ>0 be so small that |q(y)−q(x)|≤τ/8 for |y−x|≤ε(x):=σ/a(x). This follows from |∇q|≤Ca and micro comparability; σ is fixed after τ. Define

    F(x)=∫ψ(z)f*(x−ε(x)z)dz.

The variable kernel K_x(y)=ε(x)^(−n)ψ((x−y)/ε(x)) makes F smooth. If |q(x)|<τ/2, its entire mollifying ball is in Ω. Radial averaging of the harmonic f* gives F=f* there. Consequently ΔF=0 on the open set O={|q|<τ/2}. If |q(x)|≥τ/2, q has constant sign and |q|≥3τ/8 on the ball; (F) preserves that sign for f*. Positivity of ψ and local E comparison imply

    |F(x)|≥cτE(x), x∉O.                         (H)

Globally |F|≤CE. To bound ΔF split F=G+H_0, smoothing f and W separately. The radius satisfies

    |∇ε|≤C/(Ra), |D²ε|≤C/(R²a).

Chain rule for G produces the average of Δf plus terms bounded by

    |∇ε| |D²f|, |∇ε|² |D²f|, |Δε| |∇f|.

These have sizes aE/R, E/R², E/R², all ≤CκE. The average of Δf is also ≤C_KκE: by (A), K²R^(2α−2)≤C_Kκ, since 2α−2+β<0. Thus |ΔG|≤C_KκE.

For H_0 differentiate only K_x(y). Put t(x)=1/ε=a/σ. On its support |x−y|≤1/t, while |∇t|≤Ct/R and |D²t|≤Ct/R². Differentiating t^nψ(t(x−y)) at most twice yields

    |Δ_x K_x(y)|≤C a(x)^(n+2),
    supp_y Δ_xK_x⊂B(x,ε(x)).

Integrate against W, use (G) and micro comparability, and multiply by the support volume O(a^(−n)). This gives

    |ΔH_0|≤C a^(n+2) a^(−n) [C_K κ a^(−2)E]
            ≤C_KκE.

No derivative of the possibly nonsmooth zero extension is used. Therefore

    |ΔF|≤C_KκE.                                 (I)

## 7. Potential, nontriviality, and sharpness interface

On O define V=0; on U={F≠0} define V=ΔF/F. By (H), O∪U=R^n. On their overlap ΔF=0, so the definitions glue to a smooth real potential. Equations (H)–(I) give |V|≤Cκ everywhere. Each micro ball contains a point with |q|≥τ by (C), and (H) then gives F≠0. Finally E≤C_K exp(−c|x|^(1+α)). Taking u=F gives the proposed theorem.

The same derivation includes β=0, recovering FI's endpoint through a different regularization. We do not claim β≥1/2: our current argument assumes α>0, a positive growing frequency and p>1. At β=1/2 this scheme only reaches an exponential envelope; for β>1/2 a_min and global coercivity would need a different treatment.

The constructed F is in L² and ΔF=VF∈L², so the Fourier characterization yields H²; this supplies Cruz-Sampedro's regularity hypothesis. His Theorem 1 implies that no nonzero solution with this potential bound can have decay exp(−c|x|^(p+ε)), ε>0: that would give exp(τ|x|^p)u∈L² for every τ>0, hence compact support, then zero by local unique continuation. Thus this is sharp in the power, not in c. The real planar comparator is Davey 2512.09839v1, Theorem 1 (near-sharp exponent 1−β/2 with arbitrary epsilon under its normalization and exponential-growth assumptions). Our n≥3 construction has faster decay, so the dimensional distinction persists for decaying potentials. It supplies no planar counterexample.

## Consequence, prior, and next falsifiable step

If this derivation passes independent reconstruction, arbitrary smooth real potentials that tend to zero as slowly as |x|^(−β), β<1/2, still admit globally superexponentially localized zero-energy states in dimensions n≥3. Mere reality and weak pointwise decay would therefore not improve the complex unique-continuation power in these dimensions. A strategy for improved real estimates must use further regularity, sign, geometry, or decay hypotheses. Since V→0, standard relatively compact perturbation theory gives essential spectrum [0,∞); this would be a threshold eigenfunction, not a negative bound-state energy. At least a negative part of V is necessary, since nonnegative V would force ∫|∇u|²+∫Vu²=0.
