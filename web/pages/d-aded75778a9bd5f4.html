## Direct stability interface, with no auxiliary qubits

The original actual qubit graph is connected by induction through the connected seed and connected replacement/gluing interfaces. Graph edges have physical length at most2. Bounded density therefore bounds graph balls and shells by G r^3; Yin–Lucas Definition7 is safely satisfied with formal dimension4. No unjustified r^2 shell bound is used.

If a global stabilizer has weight M<d_s, split its support into induced-graph connected components. Each is separately closed, since a check meeting two components would join them, and each is a stabilizer because its weight is below distance. Each component of size M_j lies in a physical ball of radius2(M_j-1); the new filling theorem and bounded check density express it with at most K M_j^3 checks. Summing gives

    f(M)=K M^3                                      (3)

as a valid check-soundness function, with K fixed in recursion depth. This is the connected-component argument of prior Yin–Lucas Proposition17 with the newly proved ambient filling inserted; it does not presume their stronger fixed-collar condition.

The summability premise(B25) can be checked directly. For f(x)=Kx^3, K>=1, the primary recurrence(B22) has increments at least1/(Delta K^(1/3)), so its ftilde(r)>=c0 r^(1/3). The shell bound gives

    sum_r gamma(r) exp(-delta ftilde(r))
       <=C0(1+delta^(-12)) <=C1 exp(C2/delta).        (4)

Thus the formal theorem applies with alpha=1. This direct estimate avoids any delicate sharp discrete ansatz in the primary Lemma24.

For V=sum_X V_X of body bound q_v, expand each term into Paulis, with coefficient l1 at most2^(q_v)||V_X||, and enlarge each Pauli's support to the union of its actual check neighborhoods. These supports need not be connected. Their size is at most(Delta+1)q_v, and charging all input terms whose enlarged support meets a fixed qubit gives

                  exp[kappa1(Delta+1)q_v] J.         (5)

## Prior, downstream use and remaining obstacle

Balasubramanian–Davydova–Lin2605.10943v1 supplies the recursive memory and its unperturbed thermal theorem; its Section1.1 leaves a local-consistency/spectral-stability proof for future work. Our deduction supplies proportional original-generator localization for the explicit X--Z variant and its conservative admitted constants. Yin–Lucas2411.01002v2 supplies the general spectral theorem; BHM1001.0344 supplies the stabilizer reduced-kernel criterion. The local filling, correctability, polynomial check count and explicit application are internally checked deductions. Historical priority remains UNKNOWN; the targeted search is not an exhaustive novelty certificate.

The concrete downstream chain is already exercised: actual replacement maps -> uniform anchor -> distance and check-count soundness -> the legal original-system stability interface. It gives a reusable method for investigating other recursively embedded stabilizer constructions without executing astronomical local patch matrices. The root Clifford-grid bridge remains a separate reusable reduction, but is unnecessary for the direct Yin–Lucas application.

The principal unresolved scientific obstacle is **thermal robustness under noncommuting perturbations**. The original lifetime proof uses exact stabilizer syndromes, commuting energies and a thermal encoding for the unperturbed Hamiltonian. A stable ground-state band does not transfer those finite-temperature estimates or a Davies generator to the perturbed Hamiltonian. Efficient finite-bit geometric acquisition and thermal preparation are also unproved here. No feasible memory scale, experimentally relevant threshold or revolutionary breakthrough is claimed.
