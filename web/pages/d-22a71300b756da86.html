1. Definitions and supplied premises

Finite faithful sigma, s=sqrt(sigma), Gamma(X)=sXs, and positive KMS QMS
generator L. Its Hilbert-Schmidt implementation is
 H=Gamma^(1/2)L Gamma^(-1/2).
The independently audited legal-noise representation supplies a sum of
 H_B(Q)={V_B,Q}/2-BQB, B=B*, V_B=2 Lyap_s^(-1)(BsB).

For arbitrary complex f and finite p>1, let A=Gamma^(1/p)f=U|A|,
 I_(a,p)^polar(f)=Gamma^(-1/a)[U|A|^(p/a)],
 Ep(f)=p/[2(p-1)] Re <I_(p',p)^polar(f),L f>_sigma.
The Cycle09 lower-profile candidate, now independently reconstructed and
under exposed audit, gives
 Ep(f)>=c_p E2(I_(2,p)^polar f),
 c_p=p sin(pi/p)/[2(p-1)].
For p2, E2(g)=<g,Lg>_sigma=Tr Q*H(Q), Q=Gamma^(1/2)g.
The polar version follows exactly from the signed-Hermitian theorem applied
to D_f=[[0,f],[f*,0]], stationary state (I2/2) tensor sigma. No positive
log-Sobolev constant for that untouched-reference generator is presumed.

Assume just the ordinary POSITIVE-input 2-LSI
 E2(g)>=alpha2 Ent2(g), g>=0,
 Ent2(g)=Z/2 D(rho||sigma),

We will transfer that ordinary positive LSI to arbitrary complex observables
using the elementary Kato average, not an invalid tensor-product LSI.

2. Exact Kato average for every legal noise

For arbitrary square complex Q, write Q=U|Q| and choose a unitary extension
of its polar partial isometry. Then |Q*|=U|Q|U*. For Hermitian B set
 C=|Q|^(1/2)B|Q|^(1/2),
 A0=|Q|^(1/2)U*BU|Q|^(1/2),
both Hermitian. The real Hilbert-Schmidt inequality
 Tr A0 C <= [Tr A0^2+Tr C^2]/2
proves
 Re Tr Q* B Q B
 <= [Tr |Q*| B |Q*| B + Tr |Q| B |Q| B]/2.
The drift is exactly
 Tr Q*{V,Q}/2 = Tr V(QQ*+Q*Q)/2.
It agrees with the average of the two absolute-value drift terms. Therefore
 E_H(Q)>= [E_H(|Q|)+E_H(|Q*|)]/2.
This holds termwise, hence for all finite KMS generators. It does not require
V commute with Q or any modular covariance. Singular Q causes no problem.

Let Z=Tr Q*Q>0, rho_R=|Q|^2/Z, rho_L=|Q*|^2/Z. Applying the assumed positive
LSI separately to Gamma^(-1/2)|Q| and Gamma^(-1/2)|Q*| gives
 E_H(Q)>=alpha2 Z [D(rho_R||sigma)+D(rho_L||sigma)]/4.
Define D_av=(D(rho_R||sigma)+D(rho_L||sigma))/2. Then E_H(Q)>=alpha2 Z D_av/2.
The labels L,R here are left/right polar densities on the SAME local space,
not additional reference systems.

3. Exact derivative for arbitrary complex weighted Schatten norms

Let f_t=exp(-tL)f, p=p(t)>1 finite,
 A=Gamma^(1/p)f_t, Q=U|A|^(p/2), Z=Tr|A|^p,
 N=||f_t||_(p,sigma)=Z^(1/p).
Use rho_R=|A|^p/Z and rho_L=U|A|^p U*/Z. They have equal entropy, including
singular inputs. For fixed f_t, differentiation in p gives
 partial_p A= -[log(s)A+A log(s)]/p^2,
 partial_p Z=Tr |A|^p log|A|
              -Tr[(|A*|^p+|A|^p)log(s)]/p.
Thus, EXACTLY,
 partial_p log N = D_av/p^2.
At fixed p, differentiating f_t gives
 partial_t log N= -2(p-1) Ep(f_t)/(p Z).
For complex inputs the reference-density term is the average of TWO genuine
Umegaki divergences. Replacing it by only the right density would be wrong.

Combining the full-p profile with the Kato/positive-LSI average yields
 d/dt log||f_t||_(p(t),sigma)
 <= [p'(t)/p(t)^2-alpha2 sin(pi/p(t))/2] D_av.
Schatten p-power is differentiable for p>1 even at zero singular values,
with x log x=0 at x0; alternatively take a regular approximation and pass
by continuity on each compact interval of p in (1,infinity). For nonzero f,
finite-dimensional exp(-tL) is invertible as a linear map, so Z never vanishes.

4. Integrated curve and converse

Choose p'(t)=(alpha2/2)p(t)^2 sin(pi/p(t)), p(0)=p0>1. Then
 tan[pi/(2p(t))]=exp(-pi alpha2 t/2) tan[pi/(2p0)].
The preceding derivative is nonpositive. Hence for all complex f,
 ||exp(-tL)f||_(p(t),sigma)<=||f||_(p0,sigma).
No conditional/amalgamated reference norm or complete LSI claim is made.
The equation defines finite p(t)>p0 for every finite t if alpha2>0. For
alpha2=0 it is the constant curve and the same argument gives contraction.

Conversely, if this norm inequality holds from p0=2 for all positive f and
all sufficiently small t>=0, differentiating at t0 gives the positive 2-LSI
with constant alpha2: at p2, p'=2alpha2, and the exact derivative is
 alpha2 D(rho||sigma)/2-E2(f)/Z<=0.
Therefore positive 2-LSI is equivalent to this particular full-complex
hypercontractive curve in finite faithful KMS dynamics, PROVIDED the new
universal profile premise is correct.

The curve is a sufficient universal consequence, NOT claimed to be the
optimal universal curve from alpha2 alone. Sharp pointwise Ep comparison
does not by itself prove sharp simultaneous LSI or hypercontractivity.

5. Positive p-LSI constants

For positive f, the exact source entropy identity is
 Ent_p(f)=(2/p)Ent2(I_(2,p)f).
Subject to verifying that explicit source normalization, the profile gives

claim. The positive p1 endpoint follows from the separately audited08
