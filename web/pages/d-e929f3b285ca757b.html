# r07_analysis: sparse analytic inverse measurements and the price of random identifiability

Status: independently derived conditional theorem, an exact sharpness construction for s=1, and an exact conditioning-tail obstruction. No claim of a new foundational discovery; generic analytic measurement counting has old prior art. Date: 2026-10-06.

## Exact question and interface

An unknown a belongs to the union S_s of coordinate subspaces of dimension at most s in a fixed d-dimensional real linear parameter space embedded in a Banach space X. An open set A specifies admissibility. Measurements m(a,z) are supplied by a known physical forward problem; choosing z does not grant an arbitrary projection of the parameter. Observations are L scalar values at independent designs z_i. Does full-data separation imply almost-sure identification of every sparse a with fewer samples than the bound stated in Alberti et al., arXiv:2608.14324v1, Theorem 2.5?

Two permissible architectures are exactly those of that paper: (i) connected q-dimensional real-analytic design manifold Z, a sampling law absolutely continuous with respect to volume, jointly real-analytic scalar m on A×Z, and m(a,·)≠m(b,·) for every a≠b in the admissible model; (ii) analytic injective forward map into bounded multilinear forms on a separable Hilbert space, full-support/nondegenerate Gaussian designs, and the density assumption on the physical input map. Scalars may be real or complex; only codimension at least one is used. The samples, dictionary, model and forward operator are supplied. No reconstruction algorithm or acquisition of the dictionary is implied.

## Author claims versus our deductions

The recent paper proves generic exact identification with 2d+1 samples on d-dimensional analytic manifolds. Its sparse corollary uses L≥2 min(d,2s)+1, commonly 4s+1; its proof applies the manifold theorem to the union of the two supports. It explicitly leaves quantitative stability and reconstruction open. Locations: Theorems 2.2, 2.5; proof of Theorem 2.5 in §4.3; §1.5.

Our deduction: direct incidence over pairs of support strata gives L≥2s+1 under the same hypotheses. This is a sharper sufficient count, not a contradiction of the authors' valid bound. A compact polynomial family below proves that two samples can fail almost surely for s=1. A separate family demonstrates arbitrarily bad inverse-conditioning tails even with one parameter and a perfectly conditioned full-data operator.

## Proposition 1: improved sparse count

Let 1≤s≤d. Under either architecture above, L≥2s+1 independent samples identify every a∈A∩S_s almost surely.

Proof for finite designs. For each supports I,J with |I|,|J|≤s, let V_I,V_J denote their coordinate subspaces and

P_IJ = {(a,b)∈(A∩V_I)×(A∩V_J): a≠b}.

This is an open subset of the product of the two subspaces, after removing the closed equality locus. It is a second-countable real-analytic manifold of dimension |I|+|J|≤2s. Cover P_IJ by countably many compact semianalytic sets K, each lying in coordinate charts. On a fixed K define Φ((a,b),z)=m(a,z)−m(b,z). For every (a,b), Φ is nonzero as an analytic function of z; its zero locus has dimension ≤q−1. The incidence set

Etilde_K = {(a,b,z_1,…,z_L): (a,b)∈K, Φ((a,b),z_i)=0 ∀i}

therefore has dimension ≤2s+L(q−1). Its projection to Z^L is locally subanalytic (use relative compactness of K and local compactness in design charts) and has no larger dimension. When L>2s this is strictly smaller than Lq, so it is volume-null, hence probability-null. Union over countably many K and finitely many support pairs. Every pair of distinct sparse parameters appears in one of these sets. QED.

Gaussian proof. On each compact K, Φ is a continuous family of nonzero bounded multilinear forms. A finite-dimensional covariance-eigenvector truncation retains a nonzero leading homogeneous polynomial for every pair (compactness plus a finite subcover). Condition on Gaussian tails. Each translated truncated Φ retains that nonzero leading term, so each design zero locus has codimension at least one. Apply the same finite-dimensional incidence count with pair dimension ≤2s; then integrate over tails using independence and Fubini. The bad set for fixed K is closed, hence measurable. This reproduces the actual Gaussian argument of §4.3 while replacing its pair dimension by |I|+|J|. QED.

This also works for a countable union of analytic model strata each of dimension ≤s, provided full-data separation holds across different strata; pairs need not live on the same stratum. More generally the count is one greater than the maximal admissible pair dimension, rather than twice the dimension of a convenient containing subspace.

## Proposition 2: an explicit one-sparse sharpness example

Take X=R², A=R², s=1, Z=(-0.3,-0.2), and uniform design distribution. Let

m((x,y),z)=x²+zx³+z²x+z³y.

The full-data map is injective: equality on an interval gives equality of polynomial coefficients, and the z² and z³ coefficients respectively give x and y.

For every distinct pair z_1,z_2 in Z, set v=z_1 z_2, w=−(z_1+z_2), D=4w−3v², and

t=(v+√D)/2, u=(v−√D)/2.

Here 0.04<v<0.09 and 0.4<w<0.6, so D>0, t≠u and −1<t,u<1. The roots satisfy t+u=v and tu=v²−w; consequently t²+tu+u²=w. Divide the measurement difference by t−u:

[m((t,0),z)−m((u,0),z)]/(t−u)
= (t+u)+z(t²+tu+u²)+z²
= v+wz+z² = (z−z_1)(z−z_2).

Thus the two measurements cannot distinguish distinct one-sparse parameters (t,0),(u,0) even on the compact set S_1∩[-1,1]². Two independent uniform samples are distinct almost surely, so the failure probability is one. Three samples identify the sparse set almost surely by Proposition 1. This establishes sharpness at s=1, not sharpness for every s. No noise, derivatives or training are involved.

## Proposition 3: exact heavy tails of inverse conditioning

Take scalar θ∈[-1,1], a positive integer p, Z=(0,1), uniform designs and m(θ,z)=θ z^p. The full-data forward map into C([0,1]) has ||F_θ−F_η||∞=|θ−η|, so full-data identifiability and its differential have constant one. For L sampled observations the exact Euclidean lower-Lipschitz constant is

κ=(Σ_i z_i^(2p))^(1/2)>0 almost surely.

For 0<t≤1,

Pr(κ≤t)=C_Lp t^(L/p),
C_Lp=Γ(1+1/(2p))^L / Γ(1+L/(2p)).

Derivation. Set x_i=z_i^(2p), α=1/(2p). Each x_i has density α x_i^(α−1) on (0,1). When Σx_i≤t²≤1 no upper coordinate constraint is active. Substitute x_i=t²r_i, integrate over the positive simplex Σr_i≤1, and evaluate its Dirichlet integral Γ(α)^L/Γ(1+Lα). Multiplying by α^L gives the stated constant and t^(2Lα).

In particular E[κ^(−q)] is finite iff q<L/p: use the tail-integral identity for a nonnegative random variable and the exact polynomial small-κ tail. For the generic manifold count L=3 (dimension one), E[κ^−1]=∞ whenever p≥3. For fixed L and t<1, Pr(κ≤t)→1 as p→∞ because C_Lp→1 and t^(L/p)→1. Dimension, compact parameter diameter, full-data differential lower bound, and bounded real measurement amplitudes therefore do not imply any positive dimension-only high-confidence sampled stability constant.

Worst-case additive noise of Euclidean norm η produces parameter error up to η/κ for the unconstrained least-squares inverse, attained by noise in the design-column direction. For θ restricted to [-1,1] this amplification applies locally before clipping; infinite Eκ^−1 is a statement about inverse condition number, not infinite expected error of a bounded estimator. Gaussian parameter estimation has conditional variance σ²/κ² before clipping. Maintaining this distinction is essential.

A supplied design z=1 instead has κ=1 from one observation. The obstruction is to uncontrolled random designs, not information-theoretic recovery when an informative experiment can be selected. The exact high-confidence lower bound in the range c≤1 is c_δ=(δ/C_Lp)^(p/L), showing precisely the price of the analytic vanishing order p. Restricted design z≥a>0 gives κ≥√L a^p, still exponentially small with p at fixed a<1.

## Conditional stability extension

For a d-dimensional analytic manifold, add full-data tangent separation: D_a m(a,·)[v] is not identically zero for every nonzero tangent vector v. The local unit-tangent incidence manifold has dimension 2d−1. The same dimension count gives sampled immersion almost surely for L≥2d; pair separation requires L≥2d+1. On a compact K, simultaneous injection and immersion imply κ_K=inf_{a≠b∈K}||G(a)−G(b)||/||a−b||>0. A proof takes any sequence of normalized secants: distinct limiting endpoints contradict injection if the limit vanishes; coincident endpoints converge, in a chart, to a normalized nonzero tangent derivative. This is exactly the compact normalized-secant mechanism already in Lemma 5.2 of the recent paper. The result is sample-dependent; Proposition 3 blocks a universal quantitative constant. Independent reconstruction is being reviewed by worker team_a_inverse.

## Nearest prior art and novelty status

Sontag, J. Nonlinear Science 12 (2002), pp.553–583, Theorem 2 and Lemma 2.1, already gives the 2r+1 generic count for a supplied analytic response family, with the same pair-incidence dimension argument. It cites an earlier 1996 proof. His §3.2 proves the general count is sharp. We inspected the original primary PDF, not a search-snippet date. Thus the basic finite analytic family principle is not new in August 2026. Its extension to infinite-dimensional Gaussian physical measurements and its inverse-PDE applications require their separate assessment.

Cârstea, arXiv:2605.06354v1 (7 May 2026), Theorems 3.1 and 4.3, derives compact Hölder stability and qualitative finite scalar determinacy from analytic exact recovery; no effective sample list or constants. Bourgeois 2013 and Alberti–Arroyo–Santacesaria 2023 are cited there as earlier differential-injectivity/compact-model stability results; those two were not independently read here and are not adopted as verified theorem statements.

The support-pair improvement is a direct refinement of a recent explicitly stated corollary. It follows from a much older argument and likely belongs to known stratified-identifiability principles. The explicit compact polynomial sharpness example and exact conditioning-tail calculation are independently constructed here; no broad novelty claim follows from a short unsuccessful keyword search. A publication-level novelty assessment remains open.

Other recent leads inspected but deferred: Pan–Bölcskei arXiv:2606.27285v1, §§4.2–4.3 (governing-ODE identifiability from complete solution-set Hausdorff distance, not a finite passive trajectory interface); Li–Zhu–Li arXiv:2605.16211v1, §4.3 (Onsager factorization identifiability needs state-independent transport and spectral irreducibility). Neither is adopted as a theorem without full proof audit. The latter might support a future graph-based quantitative irreducibility/conditioning theorem.

## Downstream use and next falsifiable steps

The sparse count can guide exact experiment design for nonlinear PDE coefficients with a supplied sparse dictionary; it halves the published sufficient count in the genuinely sparse regime. It remains noiseless, nonconstructive and assumes full-data injectivity. Training a neural inverse does not supply those assumptions.

The sharper consequential research direction is a measured-design stability theorem charging a uniform small-ball profile of normalized secants and their complexity, then deriving an efficient certified acquisition strategy. The counterexample fixes one necessary charge: analytic vanishing order or an equivalent anti-concentration parameter. A candidate interface assumes normalized secants h satisfy Pr_z(|h(z)|≤t)≤A t^β and h is Lipschitz in a compact pair/tangent parameterization of dimension ≤2s. A covering plus union bound suggests failure probability proportional to t^(βL−2s), with constants charging charts, Lipschitz bounds and density; cancellation near the diagonal must be treated through a normalized-secant compactification. Proposition 4 below proves the conditional covering lemma now; obtaining its hypotheses and optimal exponents for a real PDE remains unresolved.

Immediate external checks: (1) find exact stratified sparse prior art and compare Proposition1; (2) have an independent reader rederive the support-pair proof and adversarially audit the sharpness construction; (3) test the small-ball/net bound on m(θ,z)=θz^p, where the exact exponent L/p is known; (4) instantiate a nontrivial elliptic model where the necessary design anti-concentration constants can actually be certified. The fourth is the scientific bottleneck toward robust, useful PDE acquisition.

## Proposition 4: complete conditional quantitative sampling lemma

The next-step covering argument can be proved now without asserting that its inputs are available from a PDE. Let H be a nonempty compact class of everywhere-defined measurable scalar functions on the design space, compact in uniform norm. Assume (a) its uniform covering numbers obey N(H,ε)≤B ε^(−D) for 0<ε≤ε_0; and (b) uniformly over h∈H, Pr_z(|h(z)|≤u)≤A u^β for 0<u≤u_0, with supplied constants A,B,β>0 and D≥0. Let z_i be L independent samples and

κ_L=inf_{h∈H}(Σ_i |h(z_i)|²)^(1/2).

Then, for 0<t≤min(ε_0,u_0/2),

Pr(κ_L≤t)≤ B A^L 2^(βL) t^(βL−D).

Proof. A uniform t-net H_t has size at most B t^(−D). Compactness makes the infimum attainable. If κ_L≤t, a minimizing h has |h(z_i)|≤t for each i. Choose its t-net representative h_0. Then |h_0(z_i)|≤2t for all i. For each fixed h_0 independence and (b) bound this probability by [A(2t)^β]^L. A union bound over H_t proves the estimate. Uniform compactness also makes κ_L measurable: it is an infimum over a countable dense subset of H of measurable functions. QED.

For a supplied class of normalized parameter secants

H={z↦[m(a,z)−m(b,z)]/||a−b||:a≠b in K},

replace it by its uniform closure and require both hypotheses for that closure. Then κ_L is precisely the sampled forward lower-Lipschitz constant. If βL>D the lemma gives explicit high-confidence inverse bounds, and Eκ_L^(−q)<∞ for q<βL−D (the probability estimate near zero suffices). At equality this proof supplies no positive exponent; it is not a lower bound proving failure.

The monomial obstruction has H={±z^p}, D=0, β=1/p, A=1. The lemma's exponent L/p exactly matches Proposition3's exact exponent, though its factor2^(L/p) is loose. Thus the mathematical charge missing from dimension-only uniqueness is quantitatively visible and the conditional sufficient lemma is calibrated to an exact example.

For finite-dimensional C² forward families over compact convex Euclidean K and compact designs, normalized secants admit a parameterization (a,v,r) with v on a unit sphere and 0≤r≤diam(K), via h=∫_0^1 D_a m(a+τrv,z)[v] dτ. This has dimension 2d and bounded first derivatives if the relevant C² bounds are supplied, giving a route to D=2d entropy with explicit chart constants. It still requires the uniform small-ball bound, and neither analyticity nor full-data conditioning supplies a model-independent β, as Proposition3 proves. For unions of sparse supports, obtaining optimal entropy D≤2s without silently introducing extra ambient or blowup constants remains an instance-specific obligation. No global PDE computation or efficient inversion is claimed by this lemma.
