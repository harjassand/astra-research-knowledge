CLAIM AND INTERFACE

For every fixed dimension m>=1 and every finite jet order K>=0, there are two
strictly positive analytic one-sample experiments on the same coordinate cube
whose Fisher metric K-jets at a reference parameter agree exactly, whose
Fisher metric at that point is identical, and whose Fisher metrics stay
uniformly comparable throughout the parameter ball, while their archive costs
have different precision dependence:

  finite quadrature experiment: B(epsilon) <= m log2(n), for every epsilon;
  continuous experiment:        B_q(epsilon) >= (m/2) log2(tau/(2 pi e epsilon)).

Here n=ceil((K+3)/2), tau=1/(4 sqrt(m)), and the lower bound is for all
sufficiently small epsilon. It applies to classical, hybrid, and arbitrary
unassisted quantum memory under the stated interface. Thus no fixed finite
local Fisher jet (even augmented by the score-product tensors through order
K+2) determines whether one-sample archive memory stays bounded or diverges.
This strengthens the prior same-Fisher-spectrum product-versus-shared-scalar
counterexample in work/cycle1/statistical_memory.txt. It does NOT exhibit one
pair matching every infinite-order derivative or the entire Fisher metric
field.

The interface is one draw X from a known family {P_v: ||v||_2<=1}; a blind
encoder sees X but not v and stores a state of total dimension D (all
input-dependent registers count); a decoder produces a fresh observation Y
with sup_v TV(Law_v(Y),P_v)<=epsilon, where TV is half L1. Independent shared
randomness is allowed and charged at every seed. There is no receiver
entanglement or retained input-dependent side information. Cost is log2(D)
bits. Continuous output sampling, codebook/model description, and run time
are not charged by this abstract interface; the lower bound concerns only D.

CONSTRUCTION AND EXACT DERIVATION

Let lambda be normalized Lebesgue measure on [-1,1]. Let nu_n be the
probability measure given by the n-point Gauss-Legendre rule for lambda, and
let mu_c=lambda^m and mu_d=nu_n^m on X=[-1,1]^m. Set tau=1/(4 sqrt(m)).
For either reference measure mu in {mu_c,mu_d}, define

  dP^mu_v(x)/dmu(x) = 1 + tau <v,x>,       ||v||_2<=1.

For every x in the cube, |tau <v,x>|<=tau ||x||_2<=1/4. Both likelihoods
therefore lie in [3/4,5/4], are analytic in v, and have common support within
their respective reference experiment. The Gauss rule has positive weights,
so mu_d is a legitimate finite-support reference law with n^m atoms.

Gauss-Legendre quadrature with n nodes is exact for every univariate
polynomial of degree <=2n-1. Since 2n-1>=K+2, its tensor product gives

  E_mu_c[x^alpha] = E_mu_d[x^alpha]  for every multi-index |alpha|<=K+2.

The Fisher matrix at v is

  F^mu_ij(v)=tau^2 E_mu[x_i x_j/(1+tau <v,x>)].

On ||v||<=1 the denominator is bounded away from zero, so its Taylor series
at 0 converges. For every multi-index alpha,

  partial^alpha F^mu_ij(0)
    =(-1)^|alpha| |alpha|!/alpha! * tau^(|alpha|+2)
       E_mu[x_i x_j x^alpha].

Consequently all derivatives with |alpha|<=K match exactly between the two
experiments. In particular,

  F^mu_c(0)=F^mu_d(0)=(tau^2/3) I_m.

The score at 0 is S_i(x)=tau x_i. Every score-product tensor
E[S_{i_1}...S_{i_r}] through order r=K+2 also matches by the same moment
identity. The full metrics need not agree away from 0; both satisfy the
uniform matrix bounds

  (4/5)(tau^2/3) I_m <= F^mu(v) <= (4/3)(tau^2/3) I_m,

because 3/4<=1+tau <v,x> <=5/4 and both reference laws have covariance I_m/3.

For mu_d, the encoder stores the finite atom index and the decoder returns
that atom. This reproduces P^mu_d_v exactly for every v, with D=n^m; it is a
parameter-independent identity channel, so B_d(epsilon)<=m log2(n) for all
epsilon, including zero.

For mu_c, restrict the family to the 2m points v=+/-e_i. For each coordinate
i, their equal mixture is mu_c and the posterior sign probability at x is
(1+tau x_i)/2. A bounded proper binary score with curvature 2/tau on this
posterior interval has regret tau(x_i-y_i)^2/4. TV error epsilon for both
signs forces the average regret to be at most epsilon, hence an estimate
obtained from the i-query measurement of the stored state has
E[(X_i-Y_i)^2]<=4 epsilon/tau. Since X_i is uniform on [-1,1],
h(X_i)=ln 2 and the entropy-distortion inequality gives

  I(X_i;Q) >= (1/2) ln(tau/(2 pi e epsilon)).

The X_i are independent under the common reference mu_c. For a hybrid
quantum memory, Holevo's bound and the cq chain rule imply

  sum_i I(X_i;Q) <= I(X_1,...,X_m;Q) <= ln D.

The decoder may be granted the query i and only asked to return coordinate i;
this relaxes the original full-law task, so its lower bound applies to the
original code. Dividing by ln 2 proves the stated m/2 logarithmic lower bound.
This lower-bound route is the bounded-posterior-regret / quantum random-access
argument; it does not assume a classical encoder or a compatible set of
measurements.

CONSEQUENCE AND SCOPE

The phenomenon is stronger than matching the Fisher spectrum: any prescribed
finite Taylor jet of the Fisher metric, plus finitely many score-product
tensors, can be made identical while the archive rate changes from O(1) to
Omega(log(1/epsilon)). The construction is finite for each fixed (m,K), with
n^m atoms. Its constants and alphabet can grow rapidly with K and m, but are
independent of epsilon. A finite-jet researcher who takes epsilon to zero
after fixing K cannot infer the archive rate from that jet. The decisive
object must include global joint likelihood/posterior structure or a
decision-theoretic acquisition quantity; the result does not identify a
single universal replacement statistic.

This has a direct measurement-design reading: two instruments can have the
same local sensitivity, and any fixed finite number of its derivatives, yet
one instrument's raw observation is already a finite sufficient alphabet
while the other requires unbounded memory to preserve its full sampling law.
It is a mathematical sensor result, not a validated physical instrument or
an engineering recommendation.
