# Symmetry obstruction for signed catalyst selectivity

## Interface

Fix a reaction, solvent, temperature, reductant, concentration, and an achiral environment. The candidate is a chiral catalyst structure `c`, including its absolute stereochemistry and coordination state. The signed target is

\[
y(c)=\log(k_R(c)/k_S(c)),
\]

where `R` and `S` are predeclared product configurations and the rate constants refer to the same pair of competing product-forming channels. A measured endpoint can instead be signed ee, `ee=(n_R-n_S)/(n_R+n_S)`, provided the assay is in the kinetic regime where the product ratio estimates the rate ratio.

Let `tau(c)` be the exact mirror image of the catalyst. In a parity-even molecular Hamiltonian, with all other reaction components achiral and held fixed, reflection exchanges the R- and S-forming pathways. Thus

\[
y(\tau c)=-y(c),\qquad ee(\tau c)=-ee(c).
\]

This is the physical symmetry assumption. For practical chemistry it excludes a chiral solvent/additive, enantiopurity differences that materially alter active catalyst composition, catalyst aggregation that differs between the pair, and product racemization or post-reaction stereochemical editing.

## Theorem (representation obstruction)

Let `phi` be any input representation satisfying `phi(tau c)=phi(c)`. For every deterministic predictor `f(phi(c))=a`, the mean squared prediction error on the balanced mirror pair is at least `y(c)^2`:

\[
\frac{(a-y(c))^2+(a+y(c))^2}{2}=a^2+y(c)^2\ge y(c)^2.
\]

The same statement holds in expectation for randomized predictors because the two inputs induce the same output distribution. A signed-major-product classifier based only on `phi` cannot be correct on both members of a nonzero mirror pair; with equal pair weighting its accuracy is at most one half. This is an information-interface impossibility, independent of model class, sample size, or optimizer.

For an equiprobable mirror-pair label `C in {c,tau c}`, any representation `U` that predicts the desired product hand with error probability at most `alpha <= 1/2` must carry at least `1-h_2(alpha)` bits about `C`, by Fano's inequality. One stereochemical bit is both necessary and sufficient to distinguish the two members of this pair. This does not mean one bit is enough to predict selectivity magnitude or yield.

**Proof.** Apply the reflection equalities above and the representation invariance. The two inputs map to the same predictor input, so the predictor returns the same value on both. Expanding the two squared errors gives the displayed identity. A fixed binary prediction gets exactly one of two opposite labels right. The randomized version follows by conditioning on its random output and averaging. The information bound is binary Fano; recording the orientation bit attains zero orientation error. QED.

## Transition-state conversion and a selection certificate

Under two-channel transition-state theory with equal transmission/prefactors,

\[
ee=\tanh\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{2RT}\right),\qquad
\Delta=G_S^\ddagger-G_R^\ddagger=RT\log(k_R/k_S).
\]

Hence target signed `ee >= e_*` is equivalent to `Delta >= 2RT atanh(e_*)`. At 298.15 K, the threshold for 90% ee is about 1.745 kcal/mol. A 0.98 kcal/mol barrier difference corresponds to about 68% ee, matching the conversion used in the 2026 prospective Ni-catalysis study.

For a finite candidate set with true signed selectivities `Delta_i`, estimates `hatDelta_i`, and a **uniform** bound `|hatDelta_i-Delta_i| <= epsilon`, selecting `ihat=argmax_i hatDelta_i` has simple regret at most `2 epsilon`:

\[
\max_i Delta_i-Delta_{ihat}\le 2\epsilon.
\]

Also, `hatDelta_ihat-epsilon >= 2RT atanh(e_*)` certifies the target threshold under the assumptions. These are elementary argmax bounds, but they expose why test-set MAE/RMSE is not a selection certificate: an average error does not imply the needed uniform bound on the model-selected winner. If the assay interval contributes an additional certified `epsilon_assay`, use the total error bound `epsilon_model + epsilon_assay`; do not substitute an empirical MAE for either bound.

## Scope limits

The obstruction vanishes if the target is unsigned `|ee|` or `|Delta|`; those targets are mirror invariant and can support magnitude-only selection, though they cannot tell a chemist which enantiomer will predominate. For regioselectivity or chemoselectivity, replace mirror reflection by an involution that exchanges the named product channels; the same algebra applies only if the physical operation really exchanges those channels under the supplied conditions.

No catalyst structures were synthesized or assayed in this work. This is a conditional theorem and an experiment-design discriminator, not a new catalyst, validated predictor, or priority claim.
