# N19 — Microscopic nonreciprocal propulsion, interface scaling, and the wavelength-selection obstruction

Date: 2026-10-09
Campaign phase: Phase II, bounded natural-science investigation
Scope: conserved binary mixtures with physically implementable nonreciprocal interactions; seek a selection law for finite traveling domains without assuming NRCH coefficients.

## Claim ledger

| Claim | Status | Scope |
|---|---|---|

No novelty or priority claim is made. The central useful result is a microscopic identity plus its conditional size scaling, not a completed universal wavelength-selection law.

## 1. Exact particle model and identity

Take N overdamped particles on a substrate in a periodic domain. Each particle has a conserved species label $s_i\in\{A,B\}$, the same drag coefficient $\gamma>0$, and Langevin dynamics

\[
\gamma\dot{\mathbf r}_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\mathbf F_{i\leftarrow j}+\boldsymbol\xi_i,
\qquad
\langle\xi_i^\alpha(t)\xi_j^\beta(t')\rangle=2\gamma k_BT\,\delta_{ij}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta(t-t').
\]

For a pair of species (a,b), let the force be generated by an even radial pair kernel $U_{ab}(\mathbf r)=U_{ab}(-\mathbf r)$:

\[
\mathbf F_{i\leftarrow j}=-\nabla U_{s_i s_j}(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j).
\]

This is a physically realizable effective model when forces are generated by species-dependent chemical production and phoretic mobility; the source comparison below gives a primary microscopic example. The energy source is an external chemical or feedback drive. These pair potentials do not define a global equilibrium energy if $U_{AB}\ne U_{BA}$.

Define

\[
U_s=\tfrac12(U_{AB}+U_{BA}),\qquad
U_a=\tfrac12(U_{AB}-U_{BA}).
\]

For a cross pair with displacement $\mathbf r=\mathbf r_i^A-\mathbf r_j^B$, the force on A is $-\nabla U_{AB}(\mathbf r)$. By evenness, the force on B from A is $+\nabla U_{BA}(\mathbf r)$. Their sum is therefore

\[
\mathbf F_{A\leftarrow B}+\mathbf F_{B\leftarrow A}
=-2\nabla U_a(\mathbf r).
\]

For a same-species pair, evenness and identical same-species interactions give zero pair-summed force. Summing the Langevin equations yields the exact center-of-mass equation

\[
\gamma N\,d\mathbf R_{\rm cm}
=-2\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}\nabla U_a(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j)\,dt
+\sum_i\sqrt{2\gamma k_BT}\,d\mathbf W_i.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\gamma N\,\langle\dot{\mathbf R}_{\rm cm}\rangle
=-2\left\langle\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}\nabla U_a(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j)\right\rangle.
\tag{2}
\]

This is exact at finite particle number. No closure, gradient expansion, or continuum coefficient has been assumed. Reciprocal interactions $U_a\equiv0$ imply zero mean COM drift. Nonreciprocity is necessary in this model but is not sufficient: an even unlike-pair distribution gives zero in (2), because $\nabla U_a$ is odd. A registered A/B pattern therefore cannot propel itself through this central pair-force mechanism. Propulsion requires a phase-lagged, oriented unlike-pair correlation.

If the pair correlations admit a continuum description, define the distinct-particle density $P^{(2)}_{AB}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$. Then

\[
\gamma N\langle\dot{\mathbf R}_{\rm cm}\rangle
=-2\int d\mathbf x\,d\mathbf y\;P^{(2)}_{AB}(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\,\nabla U_a(\mathbf x-\mathbf y).
\tag{3}
\]

Replacing $P^{(2)}_{AB}$ by $\rho_A(\mathbf x)\rho_B(\mathbf y)$ is a mean-field approximation, not part of the exact identity. On a 1D periodic box, under that factorization and Fourier convention $\hat f(k)=\int_0^L f(x)e^{-ikx}dx$, (3) becomes

\[
\langle V_{\rm cm}\rangle
=-\frac{4}{\gamma N L}\sum_{k>0}k\widehat U_a(k)
\operatorname{Im}\!\left[\hat\rho_A(k)\hat\rho_B(k)^*\right].
\tag{4}
\]

The precise normalization in (4) follows the stated Fourier convention. The physical content is convention-independent: only the odd part of the A–B cross-spectrum contributes. If $\rho_B(x)=\rho_A(x-\delta)$ for one dominant mode k, the contribution is proportional to $\widehat U_a(k)|\hat\rho_A(k)|^2\sin(k\delta)$. It reverses under either $\delta\mapsto-\delta$ or $U_a\mapsto-U_a$. This supplies a distinct measurable signature: changing the sign of the species-specific chemical mobility or reversing the relative A/B lag must reverse the mean domain velocity, all else equal.

## 2. Interface-area / volume law

Assume a domain of characteristic radius R has fixed interior densities and a fixed-width interface; the screened interaction kernel has range a independent of R. Assume also that the pair correlations and interfacial composition profile converge locally as R grows, and that the oriented integral in (3) has a nonzero limiting value per unit interfacial area. Only unlike pairs within O(a) of the interface then contribute appreciably, so

\[
F_{\rm nr}(R)=\mathcal f_{\rm int}\,S(R)+o(S(R)),\qquad
N(R)=\rho_{\rm bulk}V(R)+o(V(R)).
\]

For a shape-similar d-dimensional domain, $S/V=d/R$ for a sphere, hence

\[
V_{\rm cm}(R)=\frac{\mathcal f_{\rm int}}{\gamma\rho_{\rm bulk}}\frac{S(R)}{V(R)}+o(R^{-1})
=\frac{d\,\mathcal f_{\rm int}}{\gamma\rho_{\rm bulk}}\frac1R+o(R^{-1}).
\tag{5}
\]

Here $\mathcal f_{\rm int}$ is the signed microscopic force per interfacial measure, determined by the antisymmetric cross kernel and the interfacial pair distribution. It is not universal. Equation (5) is a conditional surface/volume scaling result, not a consequence of conservation alone. It fails if the pair interaction is unscreened or long-ranged enough to make bulk pairs contribute, if the oriented interfacial integral cancels between opposite interfaces, if the aggregate is not mechanically coherent, or if drag is not extensive. In those cases the power of R can differ or the COM speed can vanish.

The finite 1D calculation in `com_sum_rule.py` uses $U_a(r)=\exp(-r^2/2)$ and two flat unlike slabs meeting at one interface. For $R\ge8$, the force has saturated to $-62.6657068658$ in the chosen units while $R V_{\rm cm}$ is $-6.26657068658$. The speed decreases as $1/R$, with exponentially small finite-range corrections. This tests the pair-sum identity and geometric scaling only. The file does not simulate aggregation, a steady state, or experimental matter.

## 3. Coarsening-arrest implication and explicit limitation

If two mechanically identical domains have the same internal composition and cross-correlation, (2) gives the same velocity for both. If their separation exceeds the screened interaction range, they exert no interdomain force. A periodic train of such domains therefore has no force selecting its center-to-center gap: once the cluster shapes and velocities are fixed, any gap above the interaction range translates unchanged. The total species numbers remain conserved. This is an exact observation for the rigid-cluster reduction; it demonstrates how a microscopic nonreciprocal drive can coexist with a continuous family of wavelengths.

The reduction assumes the existence of internally bound, coherently translating domains. Equation (2) alone does not prove that such clusters form from a homogeneous initial state or dominate coarsening. Strong reciprocal cohesion or an experimentally established bound state is an additional premise. It would be invalid to infer a generic phase diagram or a selected length from this construction. The model instead gives a decisive obstruction to a universal selection rule based only on conservation plus nonreciprocity: microscopic force range, interfacial pair structure, and the initial pattern can matter independently.

The neutral spacing family can be stated exactly at the rigid-cluster level. Let identical clusters have identical internal configuration $Q$, drag $\Gamma$, and nonreciprocal force $F_*(Q)\ne0$. Suppose all pair forces between distinct clusters have compact support and their gaps exceed the support range. Then $\dot X_m=F_*(Q)/\Gamma$ for every cluster $m$, so every gap $\ell_m=X_{m+1}-X_m$ obeys $\dot\ell_m=0$. A uniform train is an exact translating state for every admissible period; the linearized gap modes have eigenvalue zero. This is neutral persistence, not attraction to a wavelength. A mechanically stable version follows from a standard perturbation argument. Choose a finite reciprocal spring network with an isolated relative-coordinate minimum $Q_0$ and positive Hessian after removing translations. Add nonreciprocal cross forces of amplitude $\epsilon$. The relative steady-state equations are the reciprocal restoring force balanced against the nonreciprocal force after subtracting the cluster-mean force. The implicit-function theorem gives a nearby relative equilibrium $Q_\epsilon$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon$, and stability persists by continuity of the damped linear spectrum. Choose an asymmetric one-interface configuration for which the summed force in (2) is nonzero; then the cluster translates with $F_*(Q_\epsilon)=\epsilon F_1+O(\epsilon^2)$. Repeating the same finite cluster, with compact-support intercluster forces and gaps larger than their range, gives the exact neutral train. Reciprocal springs set shape but sum to zero in the cluster COM equation. The 1D script checks the nonzero one-interface force for a fixed profile.

The reduction assumes the existence of internally bound, coherently translating domains. At nonzero thermal noise, neutral cluster gaps diffuse and can eventually collide; the exact neutral family is a zero-noise deterministic result unless repulsive barriers or other physics stabilize the gaps.

This contrasts with two limits already present in primary literature:

* Brauns & Marchetti's minimal NRCH model has traveling domains whose collisions cease when domains share a common velocity; it explicitly reports stable wavelengths from interface width to system size and says there is no preferred wavelength. In its model, selection depends on initial propagation directions and neighboring-droplet correlations. Its speed argument estimates $v\propto1/L_+ +1/L_-$ from conserved mass fluxes. This is a field-model result and not a microscopic theorem.
* Mason, Jack & Bruna derive exact hydrodynamics from the active-passive exclusion lattice gas. They prove that a traveling sharp interface joining two stationary bulk phases is impossible unless the phase densities agree: the integrated conservation equation forces equal bulk density for a nonzero traveling speed. Their large-domain matched asymptotics instead gives an interface which is static at leading order and an overall traveling-wave speed $c/L$. The article explicitly leaves finite-scale patterns and wavelength selection open. This is the closest exact microscopic analogue to (5), with nonreciprocity emerging from driven exclusion rather than imposed pair-potential asymmetry.

The current candidate extends this pattern to an exact finite-particle force sum and makes its prerequisites explicit. It does not solve the open finite-domain wavelength problem for the active-passive lattice gas or prove the source's behavior for another microscopic model.

## 4. Stability, extremal cases, and drive/fuel accounting

The center-of-mass identity is independent of linear stability. A traveling pattern must first exist and be dynamically stable; equation (2) supplies a drift once its pair correlations are known. Thus a linear spinodal does not imply arrested coarsening, and the force sum does not provide a nonlinear stability criterion.

Exact degenerate cases:

1. $U_{AB}=U_{BA}$: $U_a=0$, so the internal-force contribution to mean COM drift vanishes identically for every configuration.
2. $N_A=0$ or $N_B=0$: the cross sum is empty.
3. Even unlike-pair distribution $P^{(2)}_{AB}(r)=P^{(2)}_{AB}(-r)$: the integrand in (3) is odd and the force vanishes despite nonreciprocity.
4. Mirrored interface or domain patterns: contributions can cancel exactly. A nonzero drift needs a signed interfacial coefficient, not simply large area.
5. Long-range mediation: interface localization fails. For an unscreened chemical field, bulk pairs may produce an extensive force $O(\mathrm{Vol})$, invalidating the speed scaling $v\sim R^{-1}$. Screening length must be measured or calculated.

Drive: the asymmetric pair law is not an equilibrium interaction. In a feedback implementation the controllers supply the work $\dot W_{\rm drive}=\sum_i\mathbf F_i^{\rm nr}\cdot\dot{\mathbf r}_i$; in a chemical implementation the free-energy source is reaction turnover. In the deterministic overdamped rigid-cluster limit, force balance gives the translational mechanical power

\[
\dot W_{\rm drive}\ge\gamma N |V_{\rm cm}|^2,
\tag{6}
\]

For deterministic rigid translation, equality holds; internal rearrangement or chemical diffusion adds dissipation. If fuel free energy per turnover is $\Delta\mu$, an ideal-converter lower budget is $\dot N_{\rm fuel}\Delta\mu\ge\gamma N |V_{\rm cm}|^2$. For finite-temperature Langevin dynamics, (6) is only the mean-drift dissipation scale: full stochastic entropy production and exact fuel turnover require local-detailed-balance rates and a specified chemical cycle. The force model does not specify chemical stoichiometry or efficiency, so an exact fuel rate is unresolved; claiming one from $U_{AB}-U_{BA}$ alone would be unsupported. Under the conditional law (5), deterministic translation-only power scales as $R^{d-2}$ for one domain, since $N\sim R^d$ and $V^2\sim R^{-2}$.

For the exact APLG lattice process, the supplied primary paper identifies its driven hop bias $v_0$, rotational flips at $D_R$, and diffusive attempt rate $D_T/h^2$. A local-detailed-balance implementation can assign a fuel work increment to each biased accepted hop; the paper's hydrodynamic equations alone do not provide a chemical cycle or fuel stoichiometry. Hence I do not claim that an APLG wave speed by itself quantifies energetic efficiency.

## 7. Exact remaining gates and alternatives

1. **Finite-domain stability:** derive a specific pair-chemical or exclusion model with a stable finite aggregate, then compute the full linear spectrum including shape, concentration, and polarization modes. Strong bonds make the toy rigid-cluster result exact but do not establish that free phase-separating matter supplies such bonds.
2. **Formation from homogeneous data:** show whether the same microscopic model nucleates finite clusters, macroscopically coarsens, or reaches a traveling train. No initial-condition basin or transition rate has been derived.
3. **Collision law:** calculate domain–domain scattering/merger from the two-cluster microscopic dynamics, including dependence on masses, phase lag, screening, and orientation. Equal isolated speeds prevent collisions only when their pair structure and composition match.
4. **Selection:** test whether a finite selected wavelength exists after weak nonzero interdomain coupling/noise. A generic screened pair model may give an initial-condition-dependent family, while additional long-range repulsion, reaction, hydrodynamics, or nonlocal chemical fields could select a scale.
5. **Fuel:** specify a chemical cycle, reaction stoichiometry, and force/mobility relation to convert the necessary mechanical lower bound (6) into a quantitative fuel consumption and entropy-production law.
6. **Model class:** state a theorem-level admissible class. The current counterexample rules out selection from only conservation + nonreciprocity in a broad class allowing internally bound finite clusters with screened interactions, but it does not rule out a universal law under more restrictive assumptions (for example a particular homogeneous quench, interaction kernel, noise level, and thermodynamic limit).
7. **Long-range alternative:** derive the scaling crossover when chemical screening length $\ell_s$ is comparable to $R$; for $R$ below or above $\ell_s$, the interaction sum can change from volume-like to interface-like.

Most promising next calculation: start from the explicit phoretic microscopic pair law, retain chemical screening and Brownian noise, derive the finite-domain COM current from its actual two-particle correlation function, and determine whether the signed interfacial coefficient is nonzero after both interfaces of a periodic domain are included. This will decide whether (5) applies to a phase-separated traveling band rather than a deliberately prepared one-interface aggregate.
