SCHUR CUTOFF CHECK — exact algebra, 2026-10-07

Status: direct finite-dimensional proof below; external priority unchecked.
Input: E=[[1/2,sqrt(2)/4],[sqrt(2)/4,3/4]], H=diag(1,2).
For k excited sites, a hard energy ceiling Q is exactly a Hamming-weight
ceiling b=floor(Q)-k. Only b>=0 sectors are admitted; b may be capped at k.

1. EXACT SCHUR DECOMPOSITION AND WEIGHT SHIFT

Let V=C^2 with basis l,h. The simultaneous GL(2) x S_k decomposition is

 V^tensor k = direct_sum_{r=0}^{floor(k/2)}
             (det^r tensor Sym^{k-2r} V) tensor M_{(k-r,r)}.

The multiplicity is dim M=binom(k,r)-binom(k,r-1), with binom(k,-1)=0.
For diag(1,z), det^r contributes z^r and the j-th normalized Dicke basis
vector in Sym^{k-2r} contributes z^j. Thus the physical Hamming weight is
EXACTLY r+j, not j and not 2r+j. Hence the compressed E^tensor k block is

 (det E)^r [Sym^{k-2r}(E)]_{0<=i,j<=min(k-2r,b-r)},

with r<=min(b,floor(k/2)). Here det E=1/4. Since the Schur transform is
unitary for U(2), the symmetric-power matrices use normalized Dicke bases;
the polynomial GL(2) representation extends the same block identity to E.
Multiplicity has no effect on the minimum eigenvalue.

Standard decomposition source: Methods, Eqs. (26)-(29) of
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-024-00804-1
The determinant twist/weight shift is derived here directly from diagonal
matrices. Do not use that paper's incidental asymptotic multiplicity claim
near Eq. (12); only the exact representation formulas are needed.

2. EXACT SYMMETRIC-POWER MATRIX

Write E=[[a,c],[c,d]]. For m>=0 and i,j in 0..m,

 S_m(E)[i,j] = sqrt(binom(m,i)/binom(m,j))
     * sum_{t=max(0,i+j-m)}^{min(i,j)}
       binom(i,t) binom(m-i,j-t)
       d^t c^{i+j-2t} a^{m-i-j+t}.

Proof: for one fixed output word of weight i, choose t high inputs among
its i high output positions and j-t among its m-i low output positions;
then sum over all binom(m,i) output words and normalize both Dicke states.
This formula is symmetric in i,j after the binomial identity is applied.

3. EXACT r=0 DOMINATION — NO NUMERICAL ASSUMPTION

Set s=1/sqrt(2), L=[[1,0],[s,1]]. Then E=(1/2) L L^T.
On the computational basis of subsets x of {1,...,k}, L^tensor k has
entry s^{|x|-|y|} if y subset x, and zero otherwise. Let P_b project onto
|x|<=b and T_b=P_b L^tensor k P_b, acting on that subspace.

Since L only raises Hamming weight, P_b L^tensor k=P_b L^tensor k P_b.
Consequently the principal compression is A_{k,b}=2^{-k}T_b T_b^T.
Triangularity/downset closure also gives T_b^{-1}=P_b(L^{-1})^tensor k P_b.
If J has diagonal entries (-1)^{|x|}, L^{-1}=diag(1,-1)L diag(1,-1), so

 J A_{k,b}^{-1} J = 2^k T_b^T T_b.

This is entrywise nonnegative. For every admitted basis word y,
(T_b^T T_b)[empty,y]>0: the summand z=y is positive. Its graph is therefore
connected, including the trivial b=0 case, and Perron-Frobenius gives a
unique largest eigenvector, strictly positive in this sign gauge.
The matrix commutes with every site permutation, so uniqueness and
positivity force this Perron vector to be permutation invariant. J also
commutes with permutations. Thus the UNIQUE smallest-eigenvalue vector of
A_{k,b} is fully symmetric. In the Schur decomposition it is in r=0.
Every nonempty r>0 block has strictly larger minimum eigenvalue.

In particular,

 lambda_min(A_{k,b}) = 2^{-k}/||T_b||_2^2
                    = 2^{-k}/||T^{sym}_{k,b}||_2^2,

where the second equality follows from the same Perron argument, and

 T^{sym}_{k,b}[i,j] = 1_{i>=j} s^{i-j} binom(i,j)
                         sqrt(binom(k,i)/binom(k,j))
 = 1_{i>=j} s^{i-j} binom(k-j,i-j)
                         sqrt(binom(k,j)/binom(k,i)),  0<=i,j<=min(k,b).

This is a stable alternative to finding exponentially small eigenvalues
of a dense positive matrix: calculate the largest singular value of a
nonnegative lower-triangular matrix and form log p=-k log 2-2 log ||T||.

EXACT SPECTRAL RECIPROCITY: T_b T_b^T and T_b^T T_b have identical
spectra. Hence the displayed inverse identity gives

 spec(A_{k,b}^{-1})=4^k spec(A_{k,b}),
 lambda_min(A_{k,b})=4^{-k}/lambda_max(A_{k,b}).

The same identity holds blockwise: the r-th Schur block is
2^{-k} T^{sym}_{k-2r,b-r}(T^{sym}_{k-2r,b-r})^T, since
(det E)^r 2^{-(k-2r)}=2^{-k}. Its reciprocal factor remains 4^{-k}.
An alternative exact proof of r=0 domination now uses positivity of A:
all computational entries of A are positive, so its top PF eigenvector
is unique and permutation invariant. Reciprocity transfers domination
of the maximum eigenvalue to domination of the minimum eigenvalue.

More general theorem: r=0 domination holds for any real SPD 2x2 E with
nonzero offdiagonal, under the same initial Hamming-weight downset cutoff.
Gauge its offdiagonal positive, write E=L diag(d0,d1)L^T with L unipotent
lower triangular and positive offdiagonal, and replace T_b^T T_b above by
T_b^T diag(d0,d1)^(-tensor k) T_b. The same positivity/connectivity proof
works. The scalar D special to this E enables the simple norm formula.

5. LIMITING EXPONENT

Let a_{k,i}=[Sym^k(E)]_{ii} and define
S_Q=sum_{k+i=Q,0<=i<=k} 4^k a_{k,i}. Direct symmetric-power generating
algebra gives

 sum_{Q>=0} S_Q u^Q
 =sum_{k>=0} trace(Sym^k(4u E diag(1,u)))
 =1/det(I-4u E diag(1,u))
 =1/(1-2u-3u^2+4u^3)
 =1/[(1-u)(1-u-4u^2)].

The identity sum trace Sym^k(M)=1/det(I-M) follows by diagonalizing the
2x2 matrix on a dense open set, summing (1-lambda_1)^(-1)
(1-lambda_2)^(-1), and extending by polynomial coefficient identities.
No external asymptotic theorem is needed: the coefficients of
1/(1-u-4u^2) satisfy F_Q=F_{Q-1}+4F_{Q-2} and grow at rate
R=(1+sqrt(17))/2>1. The extra factor 1/(1-u) cumulatively sums them,
preserving lim (log S_Q)/Q=log R.

More explicitly, if R_-=(1-sqrt(17))/2, then
F_Q=(R^{Q+1}-R_-^{Q+1})/sqrt(17), F_0=F_1=1,
and S_Q=sum_{j=0}^Q F_j. Since |R_-|<R, F_Q and S_Q are positive
Theta(R^Q). There are no vanishing coefficients or parity subsequences.

Let W_Q=1/p_ent(Q), with arbitrary number of parallel sites and integer
hard ceiling Q. Reciprocity and r=0 domination give

 W_Q=max_{0<=k<=Q} 4^k lambda_max(A^{sym}_{k,min(k,Q-k)}).

For each k, trace/(k+1)<=lambda_max<=trace on this symmetric block.
Each term 4^k a_{k,i} at k+i=Q is a legal diagonal Rayleigh quotient,
so W_Q>=max_{k+i=Q}4^k a_{k,i}>=S_Q/(Q+1). For the upper bound,
lambda_max<=trace gives W_Q<=sum_{k+i<=Q}4^k a_{k,i}
=sum_{q=0}^Q S_q. These polynomial/cumulative comparisons yield EXACTLY

 lim_{Q->infinity} -log(p_ent(Q))/Q
=log((1+sqrt(17))/2)=0.9406136421... .

The exact-budget coefficient S_Q is only a proof device: the feasible
set is <=Q throughout. The lower bound selects one legal term with
k+i=Q; the upper bound sums all terms with k+i<=Q. Thus there is no
silent exact-budget restriction. For real Q use floor(Q), which gives
the same limiting exponent. The bound implies constants c,C>0 with
c R^{-Q}<=p_ent(Q)<=C(Q+1)R^{-Q} for all integer Q>=0.

CAVEAT: all spectral trace bounds here use the SYMMETRIC block, whose
