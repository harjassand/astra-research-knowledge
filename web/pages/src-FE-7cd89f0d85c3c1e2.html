2. Exactly what the packet establishes

For a specified finite microscopic move set, positive known transient masses
m, grounded incidence vectors d_e and conductances c_e, the objective is

  F(c)=m^T [sum_e c_e d_e d_e^T]^(-1)m,
  tau(c)=F(c)/sum_i m_i.

The initial law is equilibrium conditioned on the entire supplied transient
domain. It is neither an arbitrary prepared-source MFPT nor automatically a
ligand unbinding rate from a binding-prepared ensemble. The independent box
l_e<=c_e<=u_e requires a grounded positive-definite lower matrix. Zero lower
edges are permitted only if the remaining guaranteed-positive graph connects
every transient component to absorption. Otherwise the pessimistic mean may
be infinite and a separate connectivity/coverage acquisition is necessary.

The sharp completion interval follows from inverse order. Both all-lower and
all-upper completions are admissible in the independent box, so the interval
cannot be tightened from this transcript alone without a stronger premise.
This sharpness is relative to that box, not to the smaller correlated East
family used in the benchmark.

The exact single-edge endpoint principle reconstructs successfully. Express
the post-query extrema as common rank-one updates to ordered positive matrices.
Sherman-Morrison reduces the derivative of f-lambda*g to two squared rational
terms. Inverse ordering permits a negative-to-positive derivative change only,
so its maximum is at an endpoint. Lambda=1 gives remaining additive width;
the larger endpoint f/g gives the ratio theorem. The stated formulas therefore
give exact one-step minimax acquisition among equal-cost exact edge queries.
They do not prove exact minimax selection by the floating implementation, a
globally optimal adaptive policy, or efficient arbitrary-batch selection.

The prototype separates a public dictionary from the private parent ordering
and full true facilitation array. A callback computes a selected microscopic
rate on demand. Its learner consults the callback only on its selected index;
no trajectories, committor, static samples or gate ordering enter its query
scores. However, all 512 transient configurations, all 2,816 possible moves,
analytic product masses, q, softness and valid per-edge envelopes are supplied.
Thus it avoids important-pathway input and trajectory waiting in that model,
but retains explicit exponentially many microscopic states and a strong local
calibrated-rate oracle. A metastable-basin rate oracle in real molecular work
could conceal the original saddle/basin/gate discovery problem.

4. New finite-precision theorem and a corrected relative-error rule

The full proof is in derived/FINITE_PRECISION_QUERY_THEOREM.txt. If a single
edge query returns a valid interval of width at most w within its prior
width Delta, the exact worst remaining additive and ratio uncertainty occurs
at [l,l+w] or [u-w,u]. Put D=Delta-w. The source endpoint-gain formulas remain
valid with D replacing Delta:

  G_high=D*v_-^2/(1+D*R_-),
  G_low =D*v_+^2/(1-D*R_+),
  Q=max{F_hi/(F_lo+G_low),(F_hi-G_high)/F_lo}.

Proof: enlarge a shorter returned interval to width w, then parameterize its
lower endpoint by l+p. Both completion matrices have the same rank-one
update p*d*d^T and remain ordered. The original theorem applies on
0<=p<=Delta-w. Endpoint intervals are legal and attain the bound. An
absolute-error sensor |y-c|<=h has w=min(2h,Delta); if its error is too large
to shrink the full prior, guaranteed improvement is zero.

Factor-relative precision behaves differently. For sensor factor kappa the
unclipped interval is [a,k*a], k=kappa^2. Its extremal matrices now have
different slopes in a, so the common-update endpoint proof no longer applies.
The two-state example with masses(2,8), lower conductances(1,1/8,8) and
upper(2,513/8,10), querying the second grounded edge with kappa=2, has

  R(a)=2(a+216)(12a+5)/[(2a+141)(9a+8)].

Its legal endpoint ratios are89908/41245 and1421100/408889, whereas the
interior interval[6,24] gives5698/1581, strictly larger than both. The proof
provides a corrected exact one-step rule: R is a ratio of quadratic
polynomials, so its maximum lies at the endpoints or at at most two real
stationary points given by an explicitly stated quadratic. Additive width
needs at most one interior stationary candidate. Rational data permit exact
algebraic evaluation or outward root isolation. Dense solves and actual
measurement costs remain charged.

This supplies finite-precision acquisition mathematics without asserting an
atomistic sensor. For statistical rate intervals, simultaneous/adaptively
valid coverage is an additional premise; conditional failure budgets can be
summed. A fixed-time interval is not automatically valid under arbitrary
optional stopping. Historical priority of these elementary resolvent and
rational-function consequences is UNKNOWN.
