## What the new proof supplies before Vey

All five body sections, bibliography and proof-overview figure were read from exact commit snapshots. The proof has distinct roles for compactness, semialgebraicity and noncollapse:

1. Boundedness, peak functions and compact sweeping eliminate singular points. Finite Noether normalization controls holomorphic families near possible singularities. Compact sweeping also supplies a uniform local radius for the intrinsic Kobayashi metric; a chain argument gives proper metric balls and a continuity principle for analytic-disc deformations.
2. Semialgebraic boundary decomposition and ramified analytic preparation give joint limits of normal-offset distance functions. Joint dependence on the moving base parameter is required; pointwise normal limits do not suffice. Weighted peak maxima select a boundary point with independent conormal rows.
3. Bishop-disc deformations, regularized to remain inside U by the continuity principle, produce actual included balls of radius comparable to the scaling parameter. This shows the limiting excluded cone does not fill all normal space. It is the lower, noncollapse half of the convergence.
4. Independent peaks bound every normal component of forward scaling maps. Included balls and excluded-point approximation, together with proper intrinsic metric balls, control the inverse maps. The two-sided limit is therefore a global biholomorphism onto the selected component of a quadratic model, rather than only an injection into a larger formal tangent domain.
5. A holomorphic quadratic correction removes pure quadratic terms. Independent positive linear supports give an explicit bounded Cayley realization of Im(w)−H(z,z)∈C. Real translations, rotations and exponent1/2 dilations make it an S-domain. The original proper compact-quotient action transfers to this actual model, allowing Vey's theorem.

The research-improving mechanism is the separation between **forward peak control** and **inverse noncollapse control**. Replacing the latter by existence of a formal semialgebraic normal cone would leave a genuine gap. No quantitative algorithm extracting this model from polynomial input is given or inferred.

## Exact failure of an automatic Keller transfer

For the published noninjective Keller map F:C³→C³, constant nonzero Jacobian gives a local biholomorphism, but it does not provide a global covering over all of C³, a proper map, a compact quotient, or equivariance under a cocompact deck group. These are precisely the missing hypotheses. Its source and target are affine and noncompact; the normal-projective statement cannot be applied directly. Its source C³ cannot itself be biholomorphic to a bounded domain: composing a purported bounded embedding with every coordinate gives a bounded entire function on C³, hence a constant by Liouville.

One can cover a torus by C³ after choosing a translation lattice, but that unrelated quotient does not make F descend. In fact the natural polynomial-equivariance repair is already too rigid. If a polynomial map H:C^n→C^n satisfies

    H(z+λ)=H(z)+ρ(λ)

for every λ in a lattice whose complex span is C^n, differentiation gives DH(z+λ)=DH(z). Fix z and λ. Each entry of DH(z+tλ) is a polynomial in t that is1-periodic; comparing the leading coefficients of p(t+1)−p(t) makes it constant. Thus its directional derivative along λ vanishes. Complex spanning of the lattice gives all derivatives of DH zero, so H is affine. This older elementary rigidity cannot retain the actual nonlinear collision. It does not exclude arbitrary nonlinear deck groups or other compactifications.

Family057's quasi-projective statement likewise supplies no finite-sheet obstruction by itself. Every finite group is virtually nilpotent of class≤2, because its trivial subgroup has finite index. The “virtually” condition is therefore vacuous for a finite permutation monodromy group; an additional uniform index or stronger action constraint would be needed.

These deductions preserve the prior cycle's actual boundary gate: singleton finite fibres and local invertibility can coexist with missing sheets escaping to infinity. A proper compact-quotient geometric interface cannot be assumed to repair that defect.
