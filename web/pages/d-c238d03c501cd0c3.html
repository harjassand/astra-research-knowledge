## Interface and encoding correction

Input is a finite quiver, possibly with loops, cycles or parallel arrows, dimensions alpha_v>=0, and explicit dense rational matrices for all arrows a:t->h of shape alpha_h by alpha_t. Multiplying **each entire arrow matrix by one nonzero common denominator** produces an integer representation with entries of modulus at most 2^H. This preserves all rational and complex subrepresentations. Columnwise clearing, appropriate for the original packing spans, is not justified here: it can change an arrow as a linear map. The integerized representation is what is reduced modulo p, including primes dividing an original denominator. There is no claim that the original rational entries themselves are defined in Fp at those primes.

For K, let D_K be the finite set of dimension vectors of its subrepresentations and T_K=conv(D_K). The target is T_Fp=T_Q simultaneously for all real linear functionals, and hence for all signed King weights. It is not D_Fp=D_Q.

A statement polynomial in total numerical dimension A is not automatically polynomial in binary input bits: an isolated vertex could have a huge binary-encoded dimension and no matrix entries. Preprocess zero-dimensional vertices and their arrows. Every remaining isolated vertex factors exactly as the known interval [0,alpha_v] in T_K, over every field. Retain these factors symbolically with binary endpoints; exclude them from the certificate bound. Let A denote the total dimension of the remaining **active** vertices and E their arrow count. Each active vertex is incident to a dense matrix with positive dimensions, and

    A <= sum_arrows (alpha_t+alpha_h) <= 2 sum_arrows alpha_t alpha_h.

Thus A is polynomially bounded by the number of supplied dense entries. H after arrowwise clearing is also polynomial in their total bit encoding. If A=0, the dimension polytope is just the known product of isolated intervals and p=2 suffices deterministically. Sparse/compressed huge-dimensional encodings receive only the expanded-dimension bound; no polynomial-bit claim in their succinct encoding is established.

## Convex descent, with arbitrary signed scores

Subrepresentations are closed under coordinatewise meet and join: integer linear arrows preserve intersections and sums. For any real signed vector sigma,

    score(W)=sum_v sigma_v dim W_v

is **modular**, since dimension is modular at each vertex. The finite dimension-vector set gives an attained maximum M. Two maximizers have both meet and join maximizing. A maximizer of largest total dimension is therefore the unique largest maximizer.

Choose that maximizer over C. Its fixed-dimension subrepresentation locus is a closed rational locus in a product of Grassmannians. A nonempty chart has a point over Qbar by the weak Nullstellensatz. This point has the same dimension vector and score and therefore the same largest total dimension, so it equals the unique maximizer. Galois invariance of each canonical echelon basis descends the entire subrepresentation to Q. Consequently every signed real linear functional has the same maximum over D_Q and D_C. Equality of support functions of finite convex polytopes gives

    T_Q=T_R=T_C.

The Frobenius argument similarly gives T_Fp=T_Fpbar. Nonnegativity is unnecessary here because the score is modular, unlike the packing intersection defect. No assertion of rational realization of an arbitrary complex dimension vector is made.

For any rational subrepresentation W, choose at each vertex its saturated lattice L_v=W_v intersect Z^(alpha_v). Integer arrows satisfy B_a L_t subset L_h. Every L_v is primitive, so reducing these lattices gives a subrepresentation of the same dimension vector at **every prime**. Thus

    D_Q subset D_Fp, and T_Q subset T_Fp,

with no arrow-rank or admissibility assumption. A denominator divisor or arrow collapse is not silently excluded; it can create additional finite-field profiles, which are handled by the next certificate union.

## Product charts, polynomial equations and explicit constants

Fix a dimension vector k with 0<=k_v<=alpha_v. On each standard product chart, Y_v is an alpha_v-by-k_v basis with a chosen pivot-row block equal to the identity. It has n=sum_v k_v(alpha_v-k_v)<=A^2 variables. Every genuine subrepresentation lies in one such chart over its field. Arrow closure is equivalent to

    B_a Y_t - Y_h (B_a Y_t)_{P_h} = 0,

where P_h are the pivot rows of Y_h. The pivot block makes the coefficient matrix on the right unique. This is an exact equality over any field, including characteristic two and self-loops, and needs no localization or nonvanishing constraint.

Only nonpivot head rows can give nonzero equations, at most (alpha_h-k_h)k_t per arrow. Therefore s<=E A^2. The degree is at most two. Expanding one equation gives at most alpha_t(1+k_h)<=(A+1)^2 coefficient-bearing products, each of coefficient modulus <=2^H. Even if a loop identifies variables and monomials combine, each coefficient has modulus at most (A+1)^2 2^H. Its natural-log height is bounded by H log2+2log(A+1).

The number of chart choices over **all** dimension vectors is exactly

    G = product_v sum_(k=0)^alpha_v binom(alpha_v,k) = 2^A.

It is not necessary to enumerate these charts or decide which profiles are realizable. Use the following integer bounds, reconstructed from the same [Krick–Pardo–Sombra Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/9911094v1):

    N=A^2, e=max(1,E),
    h_q=H+2 ceil(log_2(A+1))+3,
    ell_q=ceil(log_2(e+1))+2 ceil(log_2(A+1)),
    C_q=4N(N+1) 2^N [h_q+ell_q+(N+7)*2*(N+3)],
    B_q=2^(A+1) C_q.

For any complex-empty chart of a fixed profile, KPS supplies a nonzero integer A_chart in its equation ideal with natural-log height at most C_q. The inequalities n<=N, degree<=2, h<=h_q, log s<=ell_q and log(n+1)<=N+3 give the displayed domination. If n=0 or all generators are constant, a nonzero generator is already a certificate; if all equations vanish identically, that chart is nonempty. This explicitly handles zero/full vertex dimensions and constant-arrow obstructions.

A profile empty over C has every chart empty. At most 2C_q primes can admit a point over Fpbar on each such chart. The union of all complex-empty charts has at most B_q primes. There is no extra arrow-rank term. The old modular-inconsistency principle is already present in [Hägele–Morais–Pardo–Sombra, Introduction pp.1–2](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/handle/10915/83447); only this application and its constants are deductions in this audit.

If p avoids the union and a finite-field subrepresentation has dimension k, then the complex locus of that same dimension vector cannot be empty, because its chart certificate would forbid the observed point. Thus D_Fp subset D_C. Combining this with the primitive-lattice inclusion and convex descent gives

    T_Q subset T_Fp subset T_C=T_Q.

This proves full-polytope equality without lifting its individual points. It also proves equality for the full representation after restoring isolated intervals. The event preserves all signed linear objectives and, in particular, the semistability cone defined by sigma dot alpha=0 and sigma dot k<=0 for every subrepresentation dimension k. There is no union bound over requested or adaptive King weights.

## Decisive boundary examples and exact checks

Take one vertex of dimension two and the rational rotation arrow

    R = [[0,-1],[1,0]].

Its rational invariant-line profile is impossible: an eigenvalue on a rational line would be rational and satisfy lambda^2+1=0. Complex eigenlines exist. Thus D_Q={0,2}, D_C={0,1,2}, but both convex dimension polytopes are [0,2]. Modulo 5 the line span(1,2) is invariant because R(1,2)=(-2,1)=3(1,2) modulo 5. There is no rational invariant line of that dimension to lift. Nevertheless its full convex dimension polytope is preserved. This is a concrete obstruction to upgrading polytope equality to profile equality or flag acquisition, even at a prime that is good for the polytope.

For two one-dimensional vertices with one arrow [7], the rational profiles are (0,0),(0,1),(1,1). At p=7 the zero arrow admits (1,0) and enlarges the triangle to a square. The forbidden profile's chart has no variables and the single equation 7=0; its arithmetic certificate is exactly 7. Rank loss is therefore accounted for through incidence, without an extra admissibility condition. The rational arrow [7/2] cleared as the entire arrow [7] also has this presentation; p=2 is permitted and preserves the dimension polytope although original denominator reduction would be undefined.
