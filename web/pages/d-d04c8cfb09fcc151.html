## Exact interface and author claims

An order-n, physical-dimension-d Gaussian MPS has n cores and uniform bond dimension chi. Real core entry variances are chi^(-1/2) at the two ends and chi^(-1) in the interior. This produces an isotropic vector w in R^(d^n): E ww^T=I. The complex construction uses circular complex Gaussians with the corresponding variance convention.

The supplied primitive is a subroutine that returns A w as an MPS of bounded output bond dimension. The paper counts calls to this primitive, MPS inner products and linear combinations. A supplied MPO of bond R can be applied exactly with output bond R chi; sums, powers and evolution can cause rank growth. In particular, compact H does not imply compact exp(-beta H)w uniformly in beta, tolerance and every probe.

Author results: Theorem 1.2 controls quadratic forms using a Frobenius term and a nuclear-norm term. Theorem 3.2 yields subspace/Nystrom approximations with O(r) probes when chi is proportional to n. Theorem 3.4 has O(1/epsilon) trace queries, but chi proportional to n/epsilon; its total arithmetic need not improve over a simpler estimator. GramNystrom's stated numerical shift loses roughly half the double-precision digits. Appendix A uses approximate GSE-TDVP1 imaginary-time evolution and prescribed truncation/time steps; this does not by itself certify exact-oracle hypotheses.

Our deduction: ordinary Girard–Hutchinson with chi proportional to n needs O(epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta)) probes for a uniform PSD relative-error guarantee; the sketch needs O(nd chi^2) numbers, O(d n^3) for chi proportional to n. Exact Gaussian sampling and exact arithmetic belong to the ideal theorem; Gaussian generation, contractions, residual norm integration and all rounding errors must be charged/certified in a bit implementation.

## Independently reconstructed rank-one worst case

Take x=e_0 tensor ... tensor e_0, ||x||=1, A=xx^T and q=w^T A w. Restrict each core to physical index 0. Contract from left to right. Conditional on the current row vector v, the next interior row has chi independent Gaussian coordinates, variance ||v||^2/chi. Therefore

E ||v_next||^2 = ||v||^2,

E ||v_next||^4 = (1+2/chi)||v||^4.

The first row has E||v||^2=chi^(1/2), E||v||^4=chi(1+2/chi). The final scalar has conditional second moment chi^(-1/2)||v||^2 and fourth moment 3chi^(-1)||v||^4. Multiplying gives

**E q=1, E q^2=3(1+2/chi)^(n-1), Var(q)=3(1+2/chi)^(n-1)-1.**

This exact proof confirms the paper's worst-case variance scaling without relying on its diagram proof. It is a variance obstruction for this estimator/distribution. Variance alone is NOT a high-probability sample lower bound for every algorithm, and it is not a lower bound for every tensor-sketch distribution.

Failed alternative: normalizing a Gaussian product vector does remove identity noise. Its distribution remains isotropic after multiplying by sqrt(d^n), because independent physical orthogonal invariance forces the mean outer product to be scalar. However, at chi=1, independent sphere vectors u_i yield, for the same product rank-one A,

E q^2 = [3d/(d+2)]^n.

This follows from E u_0^2=1/d and E u_0^4=3/[d(d+2)]. For d=2 the exponential factor is 1.5^n. Normalization alone therefore does not remove the generic worst case. Haar-isometric MPS already have substantial older prior art; Lami–De Nardis–Turkeshi 2409.13023v2 Eq.(12) and following discussion also distinguish pointwise anticoncentration scaling n/chi from numerically observed frame-potential n/chi^2 scaling. Do not use average design closeness as a uniform quadratic-form theorem.

## New explicit diagnostic: exact flat-spectrum fluctuation

For A=I, q=||w||^2, N=d^n. At each site the three Wick pairings give normalized physical weights a=(1,1/d,1/d). Each bond gives transfer T_ij=1 if i=j, 1/chi otherwise. Thus, defining v_1=a and

v_(j+1),k = a_k sum_i v_j,i T_ik,

we get **E q^2/N^2 = sum_k v_n,k**, and hence Var(q)/N^2=sum v_n-1. This is an exact finite sum reconstructed directly by Wick contraction: equal bond pairings have chi^2 free values; unequal ones have chi; at a physical site the identity pairing has d^2 free values, the other two have d. End/core variance factors normalize each bond by chi^2. For n=1 the formula reduces to 1+2/d, the standard Gaussian value.

By symmetry this is a 2-by-2 recurrence for x=v_identity and y=sum v_other:

[x_next; y_next] = [[1,1/chi],[2/(d chi),(1+1/chi)/d]] [x;y],

[x_1;y_1]=[1;2/d].

Its dominant eigenvalue is

lambda_+ = [1+b+sqrt((1-b)^2+8/(d chi^2))]/2, b=(1+1/chi)/d.

For fixed d>1 and chi tending to infinity, lambda_+=1+2/[(d-1)chi^2]+O(chi^-3), and the leading spectral coefficient tends to one. Therefore, if chi=c sqrt(n) with c>0 fixed and n tends to infinity,

**Var(||w||^2)/N^2 -> exp(2/[(d-1)c^2])-1.**

The subdominant eigenvalue stays below one and its contribution vanishes. This is an exact asymptotic consequence of the finite recurrence, not an empirically inferred exponent. It explains why the *identity* can use chi about sqrt(n) while a product rank-one matrix still requires chi about n for bounded relative variance. It does not extend that improvement to all PSD matrices.

## Conditional inexact-oracle partition certificate

Input: Hermitian H, beta>=0, a **certified** lower bound E0<=lambda_min(H), and K=H-E0 I>=0. For each frozen isotropic probe w, compute any piecewise C1 MPS curve y:[0,t]->R^N, t=beta/2, with y(0)=w. Define r=y'+Ky, and allow compression jumps Delta y_j at times t_j. Assume a verified upper bound

eta >= integral_0^t ||r(s)|| ds + sum_j ||Delta y_j||

including initial representation and arithmetic errors if present. Duhamel's formula and ||exp(-sK)||<=1 give

||exp(-tK)w-y(t)||<=eta.

Thus the exact quadratic sample q=w^T exp(-beta K)w obeys

L=[max(0,||y||-eta)]^2 <= q <= U=(||y||+eta)^2.

For l frozen probes let Lbar,Ubar be their averages. Set Z_K=tr exp(-beta K). For real Gaussian MPS, the paper's variance theorem and ||A||_F<=tr A for PSD A give

Var(q) <= V (tr A)^2, V=3(1+2/chi)^(n-1)-1.

Choose l>=V/(delta epsilon_s^2). Chebyshev gives with probability at least 1-delta, simultaneously

**Z_K in [Lbar/(1+epsilon_s), Ubar/(1-epsilon_s)].**

The physical partition interval is multiplied by exp(-beta E0). The event is solely over the frozen probes. Oracle refinement can depend on these probes and their residuals without changing the event. Changing probe count or chi adaptively requires a separate stopping/error-spending argument.

Median-of-means option: each group has m>=4V/epsilon_s^2 iid probes. The exact mean of each group fails with probability at most 1/4. With odd g>=8log(1/delta) independent groups, Hoeffding bounds majority failure by delta. Taking medians of group Lbar and Ubar gives the same interval formula, since coordinatewise inequalities preserve medians. This yields O(V epsilon_s^-2 log(1/delta)) probes. This proof requires the numerical bounds to hold for every evaluated probe, not just on average.

The integral may be computed by certified quadrature/enclosures of MPS residual norms. Plain sampled residual points are insufficient. The simple fully specified polynomial construction below avoids such quadrature but may grow bond exponentially.

## Explicit trajectory and finite discriminating replay

On a step of length Delta from state y_j, use degree-p Taylor curve

y_j(s)=sum_(k=0)^p (-sK)^k y_j/k!.

Its exact residual is (-1)^p s^p K^(p+1)y_j/p!. Thus the step contributes at most Delta^(p+1)||K^(p+1)y_j||/(p+1)! to eta. Add any compression jump norm. Exact MPO products and MPS norm contractions can evaluate this formula; p+1 powers multiply bond dimension up to R^(p+1), and repeated uncompressed steps can be exponentially expensive. There is no hidden claim that this is an efficient full-scale implementation.

`work/r20_scientific_computation/trace_certificate.py` reconstructs this for a dense seven-site transverse-field Ising model H=-sum ZZ-0.7sum X at beta=1, with the valid local lower bound E0=-[(n-1)+0.7n]. Against independent numpy eigendecomposition, all five residual inequalities and sample intervals pass in float64. Degree 6,16 steps uses112 K-products, actual vector error5.48e-8, residual bound9.95e-6, relative interval width0.00041958. Degree2,8 steps gives a vacuous interval width129.29 times the sample. This is finite diagnostic evidence, **not outward-rounded certification** and not large-n performance.

Failure retained: 5000 samples substantially underestimate the theoretical second moment in heavy-tailed low-chi cases (n8,chi2: observed63.98 versus exact384). A small Monte Carlo variance estimate cannot certify this distribution. This result motivated exact recurrence rather than declaring agreement. JSON records preserve the mismatch.

## Decisive remaining obstruction and equally informed comparator

To convert the source's exponential-scale *query theorem* into a reliable physical solver, one must supply compressed imaginary-time trajectories whose *global residual and compression-jump norm* can be verified cheaply enough to make the interval useful. Operator compactness, numerical truncation cutoffs and observed stabilization do not establish this. The residual bridge shows exactly what sufficient acquisition must return, while exposing its cost.

An input-relative absolute tolerance is insufficient uniformly: for K=sum_i (I-Z_i)/2,

Z_K/2^n=[(1+exp(-beta))/2]^n.

The RMS evolved-probe norm divided by RMS input norm is sqrt(Z_K/2^n), which at beta1,n200 is3.1753e-17. An input-relative norm error can overwhelm the desired trace even on this easy commuting model. This is a warning about a *certificate/tolerance interface*, not proof of exponential required bit cost or computational hardness. Exploiting local product structure solves that example exactly.

Competent comparators receive the same H/MPO, beta, arithmetic, lower-energy information and access to residual contractions: certified Krylov/TDVP/TEBD, known transfer matrices for commuting chains, direct MPO trace contraction, or source Nystrom++ with complete oracle-error accounting. Dense Gaussian traces are conceptual baselines only at exponential dimension. Rank-one/identity examples are mechanism tests, not a claimed performance comparison.

Nearest prior art: Gaussian tensorized projections (Rakhshan–Rabusseau, cited in source; originals not read here), stochastic MPS/thermal typicality, standard randomized Nystrom/Nystrom++, and residual-based exponential error bounds (Jawecki–Auzinger–Koch1809.03369v2 Sec2, Prop2, Thm3.1 read). The physical source already says its nuclear-error result uses prior machinery while its Frobenius guarantee is new. Our residual theorem is a composition of established tools, not a new semigroup theorem. Exact identity recurrence is a deduction from the source's Wick transfer; historical priority unverified.

Concrete downstream use: the trace interval is directly usable to certify finite-temperature free-energy calculations using compressed Hamiltonian evolution. For a positive interval [Zlo,Zhi], free energy lies in[-log Zhi/beta,-log Zlo/beta]. It can discriminate numerical apparent thermodynamic features from approximation bias without dense states. It does not infer the Hamiltonian from observations.
