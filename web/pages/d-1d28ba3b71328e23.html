# RNA pulse-order design: exact benchmark, biological boundary, and verdict

## Outcome

I derived an exact reversible three-state pulse-order law and an optimal pulse-width rule for that model. The order swap transfers a precisely quantified amount of probability from state 1 to state 2 while leaving state 0 unchanged; consequently, any shared downstream binary assay reports the kinetic transfer multiplied by the state-2 versus state-1 competence gap. This gives a compact way to design and interpret a matched order-swap experiment.

This is **not a new demonstrated RNA mechanism or a consequential biological advance**. The model’s selective barrier-only actuators have no identified RNA realization, and the best recent riboswitch sources instead show ligand/cation-dependent equilibrium populations. General molecular temporal control and cotranscriptional RNA path memory are established prior art. The CTMC calculation is a conditional, low-dimensional benchmark with **novelty UNKNOWN**.

## Exact conditional result

For states `0 <-> 1 <-> 2`, let input A set edge conductances `(k, epsilon)` and B set `(epsilon, k)`, with each edge rate applied equally in both directions. Both generators are symmetric, reversible, and share the uniform stationary law. Starting in state 0, applying A for `u` then B for `v`, versus B for `v` then A for `u`, gives

\[
p_{AB}-p_{BA}=(0,-\Delta,+\Delta),\qquad
\Delta=(k^2-\epsilon^2)h(u)h(v),
\]

where \(h(t)=(e^{-\lambda_-t}-e^{-\lambda_+t})/(2\rho)\), \(\rho=\sqrt{k^2-k\epsilon+\epsilon^2}\), and \(\lambda_\pm=k+\epsilon\pm\rho\). The exact formula holds for all positive pulse durations, not only the short-time commutator approximation. If `k > epsilon`, AB increases occupancy of state 2; equality gives no signal; `k < epsilon` reverses the sign.

The useful measurement consequence is the exact factorization

\[
\Delta_{out}=\Delta(r_2-r_1),
\]

where `r_i` is the probability that a common downstream assay succeeds when it begins in state i. Thus a calibrated structural order contrast and matched functional order contrast identify the conditional competence difference `r2-r1` without needing the absolute baseline `r0`. This is valid only if both schedules share the same downstream map and the expected AB/BA state difference remains the state-1/state-2 exchange.

With equal actuator time-cost and an at-most pulse-time budget `B`, `log h` is strictly concave. The optimal schedule uses `u=v=min(B/2,t*)`, where `t* = log(lambda_plus/lambda_minus)/(2 rho)` for positive leakage. For total time beyond `2 t*`, the best control leaves time unused; extra time allows both generators to mix toward their same equilibrium and erases history.

## Model fixture and finite evidence

For illustrative `k=1 s^-1`, `epsilon=0.02 s^-1`, and one-second pulses, the exact state-2 probabilities are 0.1895874 under AB and 0.0111755 under BA, for contrast 0.1784119. Both fixed-input distributions relax to the same uniform stationary law. The exact peak for equal pulse durations is at about 2.126 s; a coarse numerical sweep reaches contrast 0.217898 near 2.12 s.

For independent duration errors of up to 0.05 s around each one-second pulse, the exact model-specific worst-case contrast is at least 0.173030. Hoeffding’s bound gives 201 independent paired AB/BA binary output runs for 95% one-sided sign detection under that assumed effect, or 986 pairs for a two-sided 95% interval with half-width equal to half that margin. These are conservative model-based counts; they are not molecule counts when molecules share a reaction batch and omit batch-to-batch uncertainty. The assumed rates are illustrative and not obtained from RNA data.

`pulse_order.py` checks the closed form against an independent uniformization implementation for four symmetric/asymmetric pulse fixtures, including the sign reversal when `k<epsilon`. Errors are around `1e-16` in these floating-point checks. It verifies the state-exchange vector, short-time commutator coefficient, duration sweep, and an explicit delayed-readout counterexample. The numerical output is [pulse_order_results.json](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c9_rna_design/pulse_order_results.json). Script dependencies are Python standard library only. The script compiled and ran successfully; no experimental data, RNA sequence model, wet-lab run, or formal proof assistant was used.

## Hostile attacks and what survives

An internal hostile audit confirmed the exact formula but judged it a standard noncommuting-CTMC specialization. The matched state-exchange output identity is useful, but it does not outperform a full kinetic model supplied with the same generators, readout map, and pulse waveform. The full time-inhomogeneous CTMC computes any such pulse schedule and can include ligand-bound states, feedback, pause-clock coupling, timing offsets, and more than three conformations. Our closed form is cheaper to interpret, not more predictive under equal information.

The algebra’s load-bearing assumptions are restrictive: state-0 initialization; exact mirrored transitions; only two adjacent transitions; identical equilibrium distributions; no differential forward/reverse effects; equal known pulse-stage conditions; and a prompt or otherwise calibrated readout. Starting at equilibrium destroys the order contrast. Cross-talk equal to the strong rate removes the contrast. Above that rate, the sign reverses. A common delayed post-pulse reversible process can also reverse a single-state readout: for the illustrative one-second pulse case, a symmetric common generator with edge conductances `(0.01, 1, 0.1) s^-1` changes state-2 contrast from `+0.178412` to `-0.000780` after a ten-second delay. Therefore pulse-duration jitter bounds alone do not establish alignment robustness. A release-to-termination map or state readout must be modeled separately.

The simple zero-leak result is not enough: positive leakage is included in the exact formula, but its physical source is still an abstract cross-talk edge. A shared-equilibrium condition helps isolate path effects; it is precisely what typical ligand-controlled RNA violates. Nuisance rate perturbations and initial-state contamination have explicit norm bounds in the proof note, but no RNA assay has supplied the required rate and initialization margins.

## RNA application candidate and equal-information controls

The most direct assay candidate is a roadblocked, co-transcriptional GTR order-swap experiment. Romero et al. (2026) already demonstrate the needed experimental ingredients: stalling elongation complexes at transcript checkpoints, smFRET, chemical probing, KCl/glycine conditions, and transcription readthrough. A possible follow-up would use equal integrated K+ and glycine exposures in the orders K+→glycine and glycine→K+, then apply identical final buffer and release the stalled complex. Structural occupancy and readthrough would be measured on matched aliquots. Controls would include simultaneous addition, single-input and vehicle conditions, equal ionic-strength substitution, a common long hold, and binding-site variants. The decisive candidate observation is a reproducible order-dependent structural/readthrough contrast after equal-dose controls, with loss after a long common equilibration. It would test pulse-history sensitivity in a named riboswitch.

That proposed experiment is not predicted by the exact theorem: Romero et al. report that K+ and glycine alter the Bsu-GTR structural ensemble, so these ligands do not meet the theorem’s common-equilibrium edge-only premise. Jespersen et al.’s 1 October 2026 cryo-EM study independently finds Mg2+–glycine synergy and a large shift in the fully folded ensemble for a different tandem aptamer construct; it explicitly lacks the expression platform and cotranscriptional polymerase. The 2026 sources establish an active, complex frontier rather than a calibrated input pulse law. The earlier pause/folding papers and the RNA strand-displacement temporal logic literature also preempt generic claims that transcription pauses, transient folding paths, or molecular input order can encode outputs.

## Verdict and next gate

The best surviving result is an exact assay-design lemma for a restrictive reversible CTMC, not a genuine new RNA capability. The physical actuator, sequence, full kinetic fit, feedback and release model, and experimentally measurable joint competence gap remain unresolved. **No novel scientific advance is claimed.**

The decisive next gate is an actual order-swap pilot on the roadblocked GTR or another named RNA system, with synchronized pulse timestamps, independent structural and functional readouts, and a full measured time-inhomogeneous CTMC baseline. If the order contrast is absent after matching doses, or if an equally informed full kinetic model explains everything without an identifiable competence gap, this candidate should be closed. If a robust state-exchange contrast survives those controls, then search for a sequence/actuator design principle beyond standard kinetic control; until then, novelty stays **UNKNOWN**.
