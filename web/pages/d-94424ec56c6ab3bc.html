# Independent gate: the dense distinct-residual pencil witness

## Question, interface and scope

The observed input is the expanded rational quartic

\[
f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2),\qquad L\in GL_n(\mathbb Q),\quad Q=Q^T>0.
\]

An unknown permutation is promised to split Q into blocks. Each block is either an arbitrary PD block of size at most three, covered by the independently gated cycle12 theorem, or has a proof-only real representation

\[
Q_b=D[\operatorname{diag}(a_i)+b\mathbf1\mathbf1^T]D,
\quad D>0\text{ diagonal},\quad a_i>0\text{ pairwise distinct},
\quad 1+b\sum_i a_i^{-1}>0.
\]

The last inequality is the exact PD condition for this rank-one update of a positive diagonal. No representation or block decomposition is obtained as input. A proof-only real change of variables (z=D^{1/2}y) reduces one block to

\[
f(y)=\sum_i a_i y_i^4+b(\sum_i y_i^2)^2.
\]

Such changes preserve polynomial nonvanishing over the real field. The eventual learner works only with the original rational coefficients and rational hidden hyperplanes. Repeated residual parameters in a larger block are outside the new witness theorem.

Define (T=f^{(4)}/24), (H=12T(x,x,\cdot,\cdot)), (h=\det H),

\[
F=\operatorname{adj}(H)\nabla h,quad
V=F/h^2=H^{-1}\nabla\log h,quad
L_u=12H^{-1}T(x,u,\cdot,\cdot),\quad
N_\tau=\operatorname{Jac}V+\tau L_V.
\]

On (h\ne0), (L_u x=u) follows immediately from the definition of H. No associativity, power associativity or Jordan identity is assumed.

## Reconstruction of the witness

Write (S=\sum_i a_i^{-1}), (A=\operatorname{diag}(a_i)),

\[
y_i=a_i^{-1/2},\quad d=4(3+bS),\quad \rho=12(1+bS),
\quad \beta=8b/(d\rho).
\]

Both \(d\) and \(\rho\) are positive: (bS>-1) gives (3+bS>2). Direct differentiation gives

\[
H=12\operatorname{diag}(a_i y_i^2)+4b(y^Ty)I+8byy^T.
\]

At the witness (a_i y_i^2=1),

\[
H=dI+8byy^T,
\quad X=H^{-1}=d^{-1}I-\beta yy^T,
\quad Xy=y/\rho,
\quad \operatorname{tr}X=(n-1)/d+1/\rho.
\]

The inverse is an exact rank-one inverse; it is valid for (b=0) as well as every admitted negative b. In particular the witness is in an H-positive patch.

Let \(\alpha=\nabla\log h\). Independently using \(\alpha_i=\operatorname{tr}(X\partial_iH)\),

\[
\alpha_i/y_i=24a_iX_{ii}+8b\operatorname{tr}X+16b(Xy)_i/y_i
             =24a_i/d+B,
\]

where

\[
B=8b(n-1)/d+24b/\rho-192b/(d\rho).
\]

The last term uses (a_i y_i^2=1), so it is common to all coordinates. Since (y^TAy=n), multiplying by X yields

\[
V=cAy+ey,
\quad c=24/d^2>0,
\quad e=B/d-\beta(24n/d+BS).
\]

The common scalar e can be negative or zero. No attraction or full-support assertion for V is required.

The polarized tensor is

\[
12T(y,u,\cdot,\cdot)=12\operatorname{diag}(a_i y_i u_i)
 +4b[(y^Tu)I+yu^T+uy^T].
\]

Set (W=y^TV=cn+eS). At the witness (a_i y_iV_i=ca_i+e), so

\[
12T(y,V,\cdot,\cdot)=12cA+(12e+4bW)I+4b(yV^T+Vy^T).
\]

Multiplying by X therefore gives

\[
L_V=\gamma A+\delta I+E,
\quad \gamma=12c/d=288/d^3>0,
\quad \delta=(12e+4bW)/d,
\quad \operatorname{im}E\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{y,Ay\}.
\]

Indeed the rank-one part of X applied to the diagonal and scalar terms has image y; the tensor's two outer products have image in span(y,V), and X sends that span into span(y,Ay). This verifies the perturbation-image claim without dropping an off-diagonal term. The unit relation independently gives (L_Vy=V=cAy+ey).

Let (w_k=A^ky). For every (k\ge1),

\[
L_V^ky=c\gamma^{k-1}w_k+\operatorname{span}\{w_0,\ldots,w_{k-1}\}.
\]

For (k=1) this is the unit relation. For the induction, the diagonal principal part raises the highest index by one, the scalar part does not, and E sends every vector to span(w0,w1). Every lower-index summand raises by at most one; this also covers the step from k=1. Hence

\[
\det[y,L_Vy,\ldots,L_V^{n-1}y]
 =c^{n-1}\gamma^{(n-1)(n-2)/2}
   \Big(\prod_i y_i\Big)\prod_{i<j}(a_j-a_i)\ne0.
\]

This is an exact parameter-family calculation. A finite rank check plays no role in the inference.

## Parameter exceptions and unknown-block composition

For this fixed witness the Nτ Krylov determinant is a polynomial in τ of degree at most (n(n-1)/2). Its leading coefficient is the nonzero determinant just proved. Thus at most (n(n-1)/2) parameters can make the intrinsic rational determinant identically zero. A parameter might be bad at this witness but good elsewhere; counting all witness zeros is a conservative bound.

The universal invertibility primitive needed for block composition is separate. Every strictly positive homogeneous quartic has an H-positive point: at a minimum on the Euclidean unit sphere, the constrained Hessian is nonnegative on the tangent space, \(\nabla f=4fx\), the tangent restriction of H is at least \(4fI\), and the radial direction is positive and H-orthogonal to the tangent. On an H-positive component of the compact level \(f=1\), h has a positive interior maximum because its boundary has h=0. Homogeneity and constrained stationarity give

\[
\nabla h=(nh/2)\nabla f,
\qquad V=(n/2)H^{-1}\nabla f=(n/6)x,
\qquad L_V=(n/6)I.
\]

The maximum exists on the closure of that component; positivity forces it into its interior. Consequently \(\det L_V\) is not identically zero. This is an existence proof, not an optimization subroutine or an acquired metric. It excludes at most b additional parameters for the determinant of an individual size-b block.

On a direct sum, V, JacV and LV split exactly, since \(\log h=\sum_b\log h_b\). For good τ choose a point in each block where h, determinant and cyclicity are all nonzero; these nonempty polynomial-open conditions intersect. Scaling the block input by a nonzero scalar s multiplies Nτ by \(s^{-4}\). All spectra are nonzero, so finitely many scale ratios cause cross-block spectral collisions. Choose proof-only positive rational scales outside those finite sets. Within-block cyclicity gives a full-degree minimal polynomial, and pairwise spectral separation makes these minimal polynomials coprime. The polynomial Chinese remainder theorem makes the direct-sum initial vector cyclic. Repeated eigenvalues and Jordan blocks inside an individual cyclic block do not invalidate this argument.

For unknown block dimensions \(b_j\), a conservative total bad-parameter count is

\[
\sum_j b_j(b_j+1)/2\le n(n+1)/2.
\]

Fresh uniform τ in \(\{0,\ldots,R\}\), \(R=\max(8n,2n(n+1))\), has good probability at least 3/4. Fresh charged line selection has conditional good probability at least 3/4 by the prior line lemma, so complete trials succeed with probability at least 9/16 and expected count at most 16/9. Parameter bits are O(log n). Freezing an unlucky τ cannot support this acquisition theorem.

## Polynomial interface and cost charge

Clearing the intrinsic rational matrix gives

\[
P_\tau=h^3N_\tau
=h\operatorname{Jac}F-2F(\nabla h)^T+12\tau\operatorname{adj}H\,T(x,F,\cdot,\cdot).
\]

It is polynomial of degree \(6n-4\). For a coordinate change \(f_A(z)=f(Az)\),

\[
P_{f_A}(z)=\det(A)^6A^{-1}P_f(Az)A,
\]

and the degree-d determinant

\[
K_\tau=\det[x,P_\tau x,\ldots,P_\tau^{n-1}x],
\qquad d=n+(6n-4)n(n-1)/2
\]

transforms by \(\det(A)^{3n(n-1)-1}\). Hidden sign symmetry gives \(P(Sx)=SP(x)S\), hence K changes by detS and every hidden coordinate hyperplane divides K. This divisibility alone would not prove nonvanishing; the witness and block argument provide that missing premise.

The coefficient-only implementation uses the prior second ambient dual circuit of dimension \(1+n+n(n+1)/2\) to evaluate K and its ambient gradient on rational lines, without expanding K multivariately. The identity \(F=24\operatorname{adj}H(T:\operatorname{adj}H)x\) supplies a polynomial circuit. Matrix determinant/adjugate routines can use divisions only by known integer constants; rational factorization and linear algebra have standard polynomial-bit algorithms. Circuit size, degree and rational coefficient height are polynomial in n, the expanded rational input length, and the charged parameter/line bit lengths. Algebraic hidden witness values and hidden condition bounds do not enter that accounting. The existing exact reflection checks, rational invariant-SPD construction, axis selection and weighted-square terminal certificate still have to run; this witness changes their admitted input coverage, not their interface.

No degree reduction, practical speed advantage, growing-n execution or worst-case SymPy runtime is established here. The lower-degree flow and Hessian-contraction detectors remain equally informed baselines. Historical novelty is UNKNOWN.

## Exact boundaries and downstream use

An immediate distinct consequence is an LV-only detector for an unknown mixture of the distinct-residual rank-one blocks and arbitrary PD blocks of sizes at most two. In dimension two, the unit identity gives \(\det[x,L_Vx]=\det[x,V]\), which is the independently proved binary flow determinant. Singletons are automatic. The proved Vandermonde determinant supplies LV cyclicity on every larger admitted block, and universal LV invertibility plus the same block-scale/CRT proof supplies full cyclicity. Thus no scalar parameter is needed for this narrower mixed promise. Its polynomial matrix is

\[
P_L=h^3L_V=12\operatorname{adj}H\,T(x,F,\cdot,\cdot),
\]

of the same degree \(6n-4\), with the same sign divisibility and determinant degree. K and its ambient gradient now need only a first ambient dual circuit of dimension \(1+n\), because F has the polynomial contraction circuit given above and JacF is absent. A fresh line has success probability at least 3/4 and expected trials at most 4/3. This is a concrete formal circuit/probability improvement on this subclass, not an established runtime advantage. The arbitrary ternary-block guarantee remains attached to the pencil; LV-only ternary cyclicity has not been proved.

The dense n4 instance \(a=(1,10,20,30)\), \(b=-4/5\) is PD because \(1+b\sum a_i^{-1}=4/75\). Its leading Q minors are \(1/5,6/5,16,320\), its off-diagonal triangle signs are negative, and the earlier sufficient axis slope is \(\beta_{41}=-2025/1168\). Thus it lies beyond the earlier positive/sign-balanced and sufficient-slope coverage. This is not a flow-detector failure.

A repeated-residual stress family is n10,

\[
a=(1,1,1000,\ldots,1000)\quad(8\text{ large entries}),\qquad b=-49/100.
\]

Here \(\sum a_i^{-1}=251/125\) and \(1+b\sum a_i^{-1}=201/12500>0\). Every axis has a negative transverse slope to one of the two small-residual indices: small-to-small has \(3/b^2+11/[b(1+b)]<0\), and large-to-small has \(3/b^2+11000/[b(1000+b)]<0\). Every axis also has a positive transverse slope towards a large-residual index: replace the first term in each displayed slope by \(3000/b^2\), and it is positive. Thus every full-space axis has both transverse signs; reversing the field does not supply an attracting axis. This defeats the coordinate-axis-attraction sufficient argument. At the reciprocal witness A has only two distinct characters; E also has its row space in span(y,Ay), so the Krylov orbit stays in that two-dimensional span and cannot recover the eight group-complement directions. This is an exact obstruction to this witness, not to generic cyclicity of Nτ.

The consequential proved use is expected polynomial-bit coefficient-only acquisition for the enlarged unknown-block family.
