# Full nonlinear chain rule and the pure-power completion class

Worker hessian_bridge12, cycle14, 7 October 2026. The actual quartic completion is excluded, and a larger all-degree class is classified with explicit polynomial gradient inverses. This is a class obstruction, not a degree-nine impossibility or HC4 resolution. The pure-power coefficient is required to be nonzero for the classification.

## Exact question, assumptions and main consequence

The degree-nine leading faces suggest the mixed fibre form \(x\eta-k\rho\). Does completing its quartic, or replacing it by another pure power with arbitrary base coefficients, retain a collision while making the reduced Hessian determinant constant?

Consider over \(\mathbb C\)

\[
 \Psi=B(x,y)+C(x,y)\rho+D(x,y)\eta
                       +E(x,y)(x\eta-k\rho)^d,
\]

where \(k\in\mathbb C^*\), \(d\ge2\) is an integer, and \(B,C,D,E\) are arbitrary polynomials. The all-degree leading coefficient proves that a constant Hessian determinant forces \(E_y=0\). For **\(d\ge3\), \(E\ne0\)**, constant nonzero Hessian determinant forces the explicit normal form below, whose gradient is a polynomial automorphism. Thus this class cannot retain any gradient collision.

The exponent-two class is not classified here; its top-coefficient condition is proved. The identically zero \(E\) class is excluded from the theorem, rather than silently subsumed into it. Neither arbitrary additional non-affine fibre terms nor source charts mixing old base/pivot coordinates are classified.

## Global chart and the missing Hessian term

Set

\[
 q=x\eta-k\rho,\qquad K=-C/k,\qquad H=D+xC/k=D-xK,
\]

\[
 P=B+Kq+Eq^d,\qquad \Psi_{\rm new}=P+\eta H.
\]

The map \((x,y,\rho,\eta)\mapsto(x,y,q,\eta)\) is a global polynomial source automorphism with determinant \(-k\) and inverse \(\rho=(x\eta-q)/k\). However it is nonlinear, so its determinant does not imply Hessian invariance. Its only nonzero second derivative is \(\partial_{x\eta}q=1\). If \(M\) has entries \(M_{x\eta}=M_{\eta x}=1\) and all other entries zero, its Jacobian \(J\) satisfies \(J^TMJ=M\). The full chain rule is exactly

\[
 H_{\rm old}\Psi
 =J^T\left(H_{\rm new}\Psi_{\rm new}+P_qM\right)J.
\]

Therefore the actual old-coordinate determinant is \(k^2\) times the bordered determinant with covector

\[
 a=H_x+P_q,\qquad b=H_y,
\]

not simply \((H_x,H_y)\). Directly,

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\det H_{\rm old}\Psi}{k^2}
 =(aP_{yq}-bP_{xq})^2
 -P_{qq}\left[
 b^2(P_{xx}+\eta H_{xx})
 -2ab(P_{xy}+\eta H_{xy})
 +a^2(P_{yy}+\eta H_{yy})\right].}
\]

The checker differentiates a quartic with function-valued arbitrary coefficients and verifies both the full chain rule and this exact determinant identity. The universal exponent proof below uses degree algebra, not extrapolation from the quartic check.

## Highest-power obstruction for every integer \(d\ge2\)

The leading \(q\)-terms are

\[
 a=dEq^{d-1}+\text{lower},\quad
 P_{yq}=dE_yq^{d-1}+\text{lower},\quad
 P_{qq}=d(d-1)Eq^{d-2},\quad
 P_{yy}=E_{yy}q^d+\text{lower}.
\]

The first square supplies \(d^4E^2E_y^2q^{4d-4}\), and the last term supplies
\(-d^3(d-1)E^3E_{yy}q^{4d-4}\). All other terms have strictly lower \(q\)-degree for \(d\ge2\). Thus

\[
 [q^{4d-4}]\det H\Psi
 =k^2d^3E^2\left[d(E_y)^2-(d-1)EE_{yy}\right].
\]

If \(E\) has positive degree \(m\) in \(y\), its leading coefficient in the bracket is multiplied by

\[
 dm^2-(d-1)m(m-1)=m(m+d-1)\ne0.
\]

Hence the determinant cannot be constant. Over the polynomial domain this proves \(E_y=0\), including the vacuous \(E=0\) case. No condition on \(B,C,D\) was used.

For the actual suggested quartic coefficient \(E=-xy/8\), this coefficient is

\[
 [q^{12}]\det H\Psi=k^2x^4y^2/16\ne0.
\]

Therefore no choice of the arbitrary affine-fibre/base coefficients \(B,C,D\) can repair that quartic completion. In particular this is not merely a mismatch at two selected points.

## Classification for nonzero \(E\) and \(d\ge3\)

Assume \(E\ne0\), \(d\ge3\), and the determinant is constant nonzero. The preceding argument gives \(E=E(x)\).

1. The coefficient of \(q^{3d-3}\) is
   \(-d^3(d-1)E^3K_{yy}\). Thus \(K_{yy}=0\).
   The next coefficient, \(q^{3d-4}\), is
   \(-d^3(d-1)E^3(B_{yy}+\eta H_{yy})\), so
   \(B_{yy}=H_{yy}=0\). The competing square and remaining determinant terms have degree at most \(2d-2\), strictly below \(3d-4\) for \(d\ge3\). This is exactly where exponent two cannot be silently included.

2. Write \(H=h_1(x)y+h_0(x)\). The coefficient of \(\eta\) in the full determinant is, after division by the nonzero \(-P_{qq}\),
   \(b^2H_{xx}-2abH_{xy}\), with \(b=h_1(x)\).
   If \(b\ne0\), its \(q^{d-1}\) coefficient forces \(h_1'=0\), and its remaining coefficient forces \(h_0''=0\). Thus \(H=a_0x+by+h_0\), with constant \(b\ne0\).
   The alternative \(b=0\) makes the determinant equal \((aK_y)^2\), because the just-proved \(P_{yy}=0\). Since \(a\) has the nonzero \(dEq^{d-1}\) term, this square is either zero or nonconstant. The nonzero constant determinant excludes that alternative.

3. Write \(K=k_1(x)y+k_0(x)\). The coefficient of \(q^{2d-2}\) is

   \[
   d^2(Ek_1-bE')^2
   -d(d-1)E\left[b^2E''-2dEb k_1'\right].
   \]

   If \(k_1\ne0\), and \(\deg_xE=m\), \(\deg_xk_1=n\), the term
   \(d^2E^2k_1^2\) has degree \(2m+2n\), strictly exceeding all other terms, whose degrees are at most \(2m+n-1\) or \(2m-2\). It cannot cancel. Hence \(k_1=0\).

   The same equation reduces to
   \(d b^2[d(E')^2-(d-1)EE'']=0\). The positive-degree leading factor in \(x\) is again \(m(m+d-1)\), so \(E=e_0\) is a nonzero constant.

4. With \(K=k_0(x)\), \(E=e_0\), \(H\) affine and \(B_{yy}=0\), the exact determinant simplifies to

   \[
   b^2(k_0')^2-d(d-1)e_0q^{d-2}
   \left[b^2(B_{xx}+k_0''q)
         -2b(a_0+k_0+de_0q^{d-1})B_{xy}\right].
   \]

   The distinct positive powers \(q^{2d-3},q^{d-1},q^{d-2}\) force, in this order,
   \(B_{xy}=0\), \(k_0''=0\), \(B_{xx}=0\). Thus

   \[
   B=b_x\,x+b_y\,y+b_0,\quad K=cx+d_k,\quad
   H=a_0x+by+h_0,\quad E=e_0\ne0.
   \]

   Here \(b_x,b_y,b_0,a_0,b,h_0,c,d_k,e_0\) are scalar constants, and nonzero determinant further requires \(bc\ne0\). The actual determinant is exactly
   \(k^2b^2c^2\).

All steps are polynomial identities or degree obstructions. The only divisions are by fixed nonzero scalars or by nonzero polynomials to infer a polynomial-domain identity; no local inverse at an exceptional root is being substituted for a global result.

## Explicit global gradient inverse

Let the four old-coordinate gradient outputs be \((u_x,u_y,u_\rho,u_\eta)\). Set

\[
 v=-u_\rho/k,\qquad
 \eta=(u_y-b_y)/b,\qquad
 q=[u_x-b_x-\eta(a_0+v)]/c,
\]
\[
 x=[v-d_k-de_0q^{d-1}]/c,\qquad
 y=[u_\eta-h_0-(a_0+v)x]/b,\qquad
 \rho=(x\eta-q)/k.
\]

These are polynomials in the outputs because \(k,b,c\) are nonzero constants. Substitution into the original gradient equations recovers all four outputs identically. Consequently every constant nonzero Hessian member of this class has an explicitly invertible gradient, so retaining the actual collision is impossible. This conclusion does not invoke HC4, HC3, HC2 or a plane Keller theorem.
