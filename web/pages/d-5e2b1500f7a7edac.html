R2 — FULL-DIMENSIONAL THIN-NOISE CORRELATED EXPERIMENT
Worker c09_l04; 2026-10-07. This revision does not modify INITIAL.txt.

RESULT

The frozen same-Fisher counterexample used a shared-scalar score vector on a
curve. I have replaced that singular side by a full-dimensional score law: a
small independent uniform perturbation is added to every coordinate, with
amplitudes decaying like exp(-i^2). Both observation families remain analytic,
full-support, and uniformly strongly log-concave. Their tangent Fisher
matrices are exactly the same diagonal matrix. The product family still needs
Omega(log^2(1/epsilon)) bits, while a positive, blind classical codec for the
thin-noise correlated family uses at most

    (1/(3 ln 2)) (ln(1/epsilon))^(3/2) + O(ln(1/epsilon))

bits. This is a strict order separation even though the second normalized
score vector is absolutely continuous in every finite dimension. It is a
full-dimensional but highly anisotropic tube, not a density bounded below on
the whole score cube. The result therefore does not violate the thicker
joint-posterior hypothesis recorded by c09_l03. It strengthens the earlier
singular-curve boundary example, but does not resolve novelty or priority.

MODEL AND INTERFACE

Fix a=10^-3, b=1/10, s_i=a exp(-i), beta_i=b exp(-i^2), and
alpha_i=sqrt(1-beta_i^2/3). Let phi and Phi be the standard normal density
and CDF, g(x)=2 Phi(x)-1, and psi_i(x)=sqrt(2) cos(2 pi i Phi(x)). For each
finite d and ||v||_2<=1 define the product experiment on R^d

    p_v(x)=product_{i=1}^d phi(x_i)[1+s_i v_i g(x_i)],

and the correlated experiment on R^(d+1), with coordinates x_0,...,x_d,

    z_i(x)= [alpha_i psi_i(x_0)+beta_i g(x_i)]/sqrt(3),
    c_v(x)=product_{j=0}^d phi(x_j) [1+sum_{i=1}^d s_i v_i z_i(x)].

The one-sample archive interface is the one frozen in INITIAL: the encoder
receives X but not v; it retains a classical label (or any hybrid memory,
for the product lower bound) of total dimension D; after storage the decoder
must output a fresh Y with

    sup_{||v||_2<=1} TV(Law_v(Y), P_v) <= epsilon.

All input-dependent registers count. Independent shared randomness does not
count as a source of information. The lower bound permits later coordinate
queries and query-specific decoder measurements. The classical upper bound
uses one combined label and fresh decoder randomness. Exact Phi/Phi^-1,
random-bit generation, declared model acquisition, and finite-precision
simulation costs remain outside this memory-only interface.

ANALYTICITY, POSITIVITY, AND STRONG LOG-CONCAVITY

Under the Gaussian reference mu, g(X) is uniform on [-1,1]. Also
T=Phi(X_0) is uniform on (0,1), so the psi_i are centered orthonormal
functions. Since beta_i<=b,

    |z_i| <= (sqrt(2)+b)/sqrt(3) < 1.

Moreover sum_i s_i^2 < a^2/6, because sum_{i>=1} exp(-2i)=1/(e^2-1)<1/6.
Thus |sum_i s_i v_i z_i| <= ||v||_2 (sum_i s_i^2 z_i^2)^(1/2)
<= a/sqrt(6). Both likelihood multipliers are strictly positive. Their
densities are real analytic and have full support because they are positive
analytic multiples of Gaussian densities.

For P, each log-density Hessian coordinate is

    -1 + s_i v_i g''(x_i)/(1+s_i v_i g(x_i))
        - (s_i v_i g'(x_i)/(1+s_i v_i g(x_i)))^2.

Here ||g''||_infinity=2/sqrt(2 pi e)<1. Therefore

    nabla^2 log p_v <= [-1+a ||g''||_infinity/(1-a)] I < -0.9995 I.

For C write h_v=sum_i s_i v_i z_i. Its Hessian is diagonal: the x_0 entry
is the sum of the psi_i'' terms, and the x_i entry is the beta_i g'' term.
The explicit formula

    psi_i''(x)=sqrt(2)[-(2 pi i)^2 cos(2 pi i Phi(x)) phi(x)^2
                         +(2 pi i)x sin(2 pi i Phi(x)) phi(x)]

and sup phi=(2 pi)^(-1/2), sup |x|phi=(2 pi e)^(-1/2) give
||psi_i''||_infinity <= 9 sqrt(2) i^2. The exact series identity

    sum_{i>=1} i^4 q^i=q(1+11q+11q^2+q^3)/(1-q)^5

at q=e^-2 gives sum_i i^4 e^-2i<1. Cauchy-Schwarz then yields

    ||nabla^2 h_v||_op <= max(9 sqrt(2/3) a,
                              a b ||g''||_infinity/sqrt(3)) < 0.00735.

For any positive multiplier 1+h,

    nabla^2 log(1+h)=nabla^2 h/(1+h)
                       - nabla h nabla h^T/(1+h)^2.

Using |h|<=a/sqrt(6),

    nabla^2 log c_v <= [-1+0.00735/(1-a/sqrt(6))] I < -0.9926 I.

All constants are uniform over finite d and the full unit parameter ball.

SAME TANGENT FISHER COVARIANCE AND GLOBAL COMPARABILITY

For P the score at v=0 is s_i g(X_i). For C it is s_i z_i(X). Under mu,
g(X_i) are independent, centered, and have variance 1/3. The z_i are also
centered, and

    E[z_i z_j] = (alpha_i alpha_j/3) 1_{i=j}
                    +(beta_i beta_j/9) 1_{i=j}
              = (1/3) 1_{i=j},

since alpha_i^2+beta_i^2/3=1. Cross terms vanish by independence and
centering. Consequently both tangent Fisher matrices are exactly

    I_P(0)=I_C(0)=diag(s_i^2/3 : 1<=i<=d).

This is also a global comparison. P remains a product and its i-th Fisher
entry is s_i^2 E[g(X)^2/(1+s_i v_i g(X))], between
(s_i^2/3)/(1+a) and (s_i^2/3)/(1-a). For C,

    I_C(v)[w,w]=E_mu[(sum_i s_i w_i z_i)^2 /
                          (1+sum_i s_i v_i z_i)].

The denominator is between 1-a/sqrt(6) and 1+a/sqrt(6), while the numerator
has expectation sum_i s_i^2 w_i^2/3. Thus both full Fisher metrics are
uniformly equivalent to the same diagonal matrix with constants independent
of d and v.

The normalized feature vectors sqrt(3)g(X_i) and sqrt(3)z_i both have
identity covariance. The first has independent uniform coordinates. The
second has an absolutely continuous joint law for every finite d: conditional
on T=Phi(X_0), its coordinates are independent uniforms on intervals of
positive widths 2 beta_i/sqrt(3). Hence the score law is full-dimensional,
although its mass occupies a thin tube and its coordinate thickness shrinks
superexponentially with i. This is not a singular-curve construction.

PRODUCT ARCHIVE LOWER BOUND

Fix coordinate i and compare v=+e_i with v=-e_i. The two P likelihoods
relative to mu are exactly 1 +/- s_i U_i, where U_i=g(X_i) is uniform on
[-1,1]. Their equal mixture is mu, and the posterior sign probability is
(1+s_i U_i)/2. The bounded proper-score argument from INITIAL gives, for the
encoder's actual coupling and decoded coordinate Y_i,

    E_mu[(g(X_i)-g(Y_i))^2] <= 4 epsilon/s_i.                 (1)

One direct derivation uses a convex Savage potential with derivative
clip((2p-1)/s_i,[-1,1]). Its loss is bounded in [0,1]. On the posterior
interval corresponding to U_i in [-1,1], its Bregman regret for reporting
the value g(Y_i) is exactly s_i(g(X_i)-g(Y_i))^2/4. The uniform TV guarantee
at the two signs bounds the excess bounded risk by epsilon, giving (1).

Since h(U_i)=ln 2, the conditional maximum-entropy inequality applied to
(1) yields

    I(U_i;g(Y_i),S) >= (1/2) ln[s_i/(2 pi e epsilon)]

whenever the right side is positive; S is independent shared randomness.
For any finite active set, the U_i are independent. Quantum conditional
mutual-information chain rules and strong subadditivity imply that the sum
of these per-coordinate informations, even with a distinct decoder
measurement for every i, is at most I(U_1,...,U_d;Q|S)<=ln D for a
dimension-D retained hybrid memory Q. Therefore

    log_2 D >= (1/2) sum_{i:s_i>2 pi e epsilon}
                              log_2[s_i/(2 pi e epsilon)].       (2)

Take d>=ceil(ln(1/epsilon)+O(1)). Since s_i=a e^-i, (2) gives

    log_2 D >= (ln(1/epsilon))^2/(4 ln 2)-O(ln(1/epsilon)).

The cost includes every input-dependent classical or quantum register. This
is an ideal information lower bound, not a finite physical implementation.

CORRELATED FAMILY: POSITIVE BLIND ARCHIVE

Transform the C reference coordinates to T=Phi(X_0) and
U_i=g(X_i). Under mu these are independent uniforms on (0,1) and [-1,1].
The likelihood multiplier becomes

    f_v(t,u)=1+(1/sqrt(3)) sum_i s_i v_i
                                   [alpha_i psi_i(t)+beta_i u_i].

The codec below is a product of positive self-adjoint Durrmeyer kernels,
so it preserves the reference measure. Applying its adjoint to the displayed
affine density replaces each feature by its kernel mean; no approximation
term is multiplied by an unknown normalizer.

For T, use J_0 periodic triangular hats eta_j, j=0,...,J_0-1, centered at
j/J_0. The encoder emits j with probability eta_j(t); the decoder draws
t' from density J_0 eta_j(t'). The resulting reference-symmetric Markov
kernel is

    K_0(t,u)=J_0 sum_j eta_j(t)eta_j(u).

It fixes uniform measure. Given output u, the input is a mixture of the two
neighboring hats with mean u and displacement at most 2/J_0. Taylor's
theorem with the zero first moment gives

    ||K_0 psi_i-psi_i||_infinity
       <= 2 ||psi_i''||_infinity/J_0^2
       = 8 sqrt(2) pi^2 i^2/J_0^2.

For ||v||_2<=1 and alpha_i<=1, the shared T-feature density error is at
most

    C_T/J_0^2,  C_T=8 sqrt(2) pi^2 a/sqrt(3),

because sum_i e^-i i^2 |v_i|<=sqrt(sum_i i^4 e^-2i)<=1. Set

    J_0=max(5, ceil(sqrt(2 C_T/epsilon))).

Then the shared-feature density error is at most epsilon/2 in L-infinity,
and costs log_2 J_0=(1/2)log_2(1/epsilon)+O(1) bits.

For each U_i, use the nodal mesh

    u_j=-1+6(j/J_i)^2-4(j/J_i)^3,  j=0,...,J_i.

Let eta_j be the piecewise-linear nodal hats. Define the self-adjoint
Durrmeyer kernel by

    K_i f(u)=sum_j eta_j(u) [integral eta_j(w) f(w) dw/2]
                                      / [integral eta_j(w) dw/2].

The encoder stores one of J_i+1 labels; the decoder samples the normalized
hat for that label. Each kernel is positive, Markov, and preserves the
uniform reference. Write h_j=u_j-u_{j-1}; the conditional mean of the
uniform-base hat at an interior node is

    m_j=u_j+(h_{j+1}-h_j)/3.

At endpoints the same formula holds with the missing adjacent spacing set
to zero. The output mean K_i id(u) is the linear interpolation of these
m_j values. Since the mesh is generated by q(t)=-1+6t^2-4t^3 and
sup|q''|=12, consecutive spacings differ by at most 12/J_i^2. Hence

    ||K_i id-id||_infinity<=4/J_i^2.                            (3)

Let q_i=s_i beta_i=a b exp(-i-i^2), and let k be the largest index with
q_i>sqrt(3) epsilon/16 (take k=0 if none). For each i<=k set

    J_i=max(2, ceil(sqrt(16 q_i/(sqrt(3) epsilon)))).

Then q_i/J_i^2<=sqrt(3)epsilon/16. Under the reference the errors
e_i=K_i id-id are centered and independent across i. By (3), the active
noise part of the density error has squared L2 norm at most

    (16/3) sum_{i<=k} (q_i v_i/J_i^2)^2
        <= epsilon^2/16,

where the final inequality uses sum_i v_i^2<=1. Coordinates i>k use no
input-dependent label: the decoder emits a fresh uniform U_i, so K_i id=0.
Their omitted noise error has L2 norm

    (1/3) (sum_{i>k} q_i^2 v_i^2)^(1/2)
        <= q_{k+1}/3 <= sqrt(3)epsilon/48.

For finite d, sums stop at d. The combined noise error therefore has L2
norm at most [1/4+sqrt(3)/48]epsilon < 0.287 epsilon by the triangle
inequality. Together with shared-feature L-infinity error <=epsilon/2,
the full density error has L1 norm <0.787epsilon. Thus decoded TV is
less than epsilon. The codec is blind to v, positive, classical, and
reference preserving.

For L=ln(1/epsilon), k=sqrt(L)+O(1), since q_i=a b exp(-i-i^2). The
combined label count is at most J_0 product_{i<=k}(J_i+1), so

    log_2 D <= log_2 J_0 + sum_{i<=k} log_2(J_i+1)
       <= (1/(2 ln 2)) sum_{i<=k}(L-i^2-i)+O(L)
       = (1/(3 ln 2))L^(3/2)+O(L).                              (4)

In the last step k=sqrt(L)+O(1), sum_{i<=k}i^2=k^3/3+O(k^2), and
sum_{i<=k}i=k^2/2+O(k). Constants a,b and the label ceilings contribute
only O(L). This is a constructive upper bound for the same memory-only
interface as (2).

CONSEQUENCE AND BOUNDARY

Both tangent Fisher spectra have singular values s_i/sqrt(3). The usual
spectral count sum_i[log_2(s_i/epsilon)]_+ is Theta(L^2). The product
lower (2) is Omega(L^2), while the full-dimensional correlated archive
upper (4) is O(L^(3/2)). Thus covariance-only estimates cannot pin down
archive order even among analytic, full-support, uniformly strongly
log-concave observation models, and even when each finite-dimensional score
law is absolutely continuous.

The score law for C is not bounded below throughout a fixed cube: the
independent coordinate thickness beta_i decreases as exp(-i^2). It does
not satisfy the uniform joint-posterior thickness/log-gradient assumption
of the cross-read c09_l03 conditional theorem. The c09_l03 product-side
example remains valid; R2 illustrates why its thickness condition is
substantive. This does not refute that conditional theorem. The result also
does not claim a novel information-theoretic lower technique, a practical
finite-bit codec, or external priority. It only provides a scoped explicit
counterexample to a dimension-uniform estimate based on Fisher covariance
alone.

shared-scalar side. R1 changed the product feature to 2Phi-1, making its
score law exactly uniform on a cube and aligning it with the thick-score
side of c09_l03. R2 is a new deeper attack: the correlated family now has
nonzero independent feature thickness in every finite coordinate, with a
graded positive archive. INITIAL.txt has not been edited.
