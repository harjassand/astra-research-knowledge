# Consequences of a fixed zero-free strip, with supplied inputs charged

This note proves transfers from a precisely stated analytic premise. It does not prove that premise. The strongest campaign candidate for it is the general-field `Re s>49/50` extension in `RETUNING_PROOF.md`.

## A smoothed Chebotarev witness lemma

Let `0<delta<1/2` be fixed. Let `L/Q` be a finite Galois number field of degree `d`, absolute discriminant `D`, and Galois group `G`. Suppose its Dedekind zeta function has no nontrivial zero in `Re s>1-delta`. Then, for every conjugacy class `C` in `G`, there is a rational prime ell unramified in L with Frobenius class C and
\[
\ell\le C_\delta(\log D+d+2)^{1/\delta}.
\]
The constant depends only on delta and one fixed smooth weight, not on the field, class, or group. More generally, excluding an arbitrary additional finite set of rational primes with product `R`, one may replace `log D+d+2` by `log D+log R+d+2`.

### Proof

Choose `sigma` in C and let `H=<sigma>`, of order m. Set `F=L^H`. Then `L/F` is cyclic and `n_F=d/m`. By abelian factorization,
\[
\zeta_L(s)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat H}L_F(s,\chi).
\]
The nonprincipal factors are entire and the principal factor has a pole only at 1. Their zero multisets therefore combine, with multiplicities, to the zero multiset of `zeta_L`; away from 1 there is no pole cancellation. The assumed strip controls all those factors.

Fix nonnegative nonzero `varphi` supported inside `(1,2)` and let `Phi` be its Mellin transform. The ordinary smoothed Hecke explicit formula and character orthogonality give a weighted count of prime-ideal powers with Artin symbol sigma:
\[
S_\sigma(x)=x\Phi(1)/m+
O_\varphi\left(\frac{x^{1-\delta}}m(\log D+d)\right),
\]
apart from the finite ramified Euler factors. This uniform dependency can be proved either by summing conductor-based Hecke zero counts and using the conductor-discriminant formula, or by noting that the sum of all absolute smoothed zero contributions is bounded by the zero count of `zeta_L`:
\[
\sum_\rho|\Phi(\rho)|\ll_\varphi\log D+d.
\]
The coefficients attached to each character in orthogonality have modulus `1/m`. The residues at the trivial zeros and the contour remainder have the same conductor-discriminant accounting. The factors removed at ramified primes have at worst `O(n_F log D log(2x))` total weighted contribution; this deliberately loose bound suffices.

The contributions from prime-ideal powers of exponent at least two and prime ideals of rational residue degree at least two are at most
\[
O_\varphi(n_F\sqrt x\log^2(2x)).
\]
The contributions at a prescribed finite set of excluded rational primes are bounded by
`O_varphi(n_F(log D+log R)log(2x))`. Hence a positive remaining contribution comes from a first-power prime ideal of F with rational residue degree one, at a rational prime ell not excluded. Its Frobenius in `L/F` is sigma; the Frobenius class in `L/Q` is therefore C.

Put `A=log D+log R+d+2` and `x=T A^(1/delta)`. The explicit-formula error divided by the main term is `O(T^(-delta))`. The other two relative errors are bounded by
\[
O\left(d x^{-1/2}\log^2(2x)+d(\log D+\log R)x^{-1}\log(2x)\right).
\]
For fixed delta below 1/2, their powers of A are negative after substitution; choosing the absolute T sufficiently large makes all errors smaller than, say, half the main term, uniformly for `A>=2`. A prime lies in `(x,2x)`, yielding the claimed constant. At the boundary `delta=1/2`, an additional logarithmic factor is needed with this elementary treatment; it is irrelevant to `delta=1/50`.

Under the candidate strip, the bound is thus
\[
\ell\ll (\log D+d+2)^{50}.
\]

This lemma is classical in mechanism. The prospective novelty is obtaining its fixed-width premise over all number fields from the source's generalized cubic-theta construction and the re-tuning, not a new Chebotarev principle.

## A deterministic search interface

A fully charged algorithmic input is:

* a monic irreducible `f in Z[X]` whose degree d quotient field L is promised Galois;
* all d automorphisms of L, represented as rational polynomials in the primitive element, with their full coefficient data;
* a specified conjugacy class represented in these automorphism tables.

These data must be supplied or acquired and charged. Constructing a Galois closure, all automorphisms, or class tables from a short arbitrary polynomial is not included. In particular the Galois degree can grow like the factorial of the input polynomial's degree.

Exclude the primes dividing the polynomial discriminant and the denominators of the automorphism polynomials. Their product has logarithm polynomially bounded by the displayed input data, and no factorization is needed to exclude them: divisibility tests suffice. The field discriminant divides the polynomial discriminant, so its logarithm is bounded too.

Search rational primes in increasing order by deterministic trial division. For each good ell, factor f modulo ell by the elementary Berlekamp method with its complete scalar loop, costing polynomially in the numerical ell and d. Choose an irreducible factor h. In `F_ell[X]/h`, compute `X^ell` and compare it with each supplied automorphism image. Squarefreeness guarantees that the images of all conjugates are distinct modulo the chosen prime. Exactly one automorphism is the Frobenius there. Return ell when its conjugacy class is the requested one.

The witness lemma bounds the numerical search length by a fixed polynomial of the supplied input length. Trial division, the scalar loop, modular powering, and automorphism comparisons have fixed polynomial cost in that length. The method uses no randomness and no integer-factorization oracle. It stores the supplied tables and a single residue algebra/factorization at a time; all retained input-dependent data are polynomially bounded.

The exponent 50 is a prime-size bound, not the exact bit exponent of the whole implementation. The constants are not numerically supplied, so the algorithm simply searches until success; the theorem would bound its running time. The bound is theoretical and is not practical at ordinary input sizes.

## Degree-one ray-class generators without a class-number factor

Suppose the same fixed strip holds for every finite-order Hecke character of a number field K. Let `mathfrak m` be a ray modulus (including any prescribed real signs). Put
\[
A=\log D_K+\log N\mathfrak m+n_K+2.
\]
The ray class group is generated by prime ideals of rational degree one and norm at most `C_delta A^(1/delta)`, coprime to the modulus.

If the claimed prime ideals generated a proper subgroup, a nontrivial finite ray-class character chi would be trivial on all of them. Compare the smoothed principal prime-ideal sum with the chi-twisted sum. Their difference has main term `x Phi(1)` and error
`O(x^(1-delta) A)` from the two explicit formulas. With `x=T A^(1/delta)`, it is positive. If every degree-one prime in the support had chi value one, only higher-degree prime ideals, higher powers, or primes at the modulus could contribute; their bounds are as in the witness proof and are too small. Contradiction.

This route does not pass to the Hilbert/ray class field and thereby does not incur its possibly enormous class number as a degree parameter. It produces a provably sufficient small ideal generating set. It does **not** compute enough relations, a class-group presentation, a unit group, regulators, or discrete logarithms in polynomial time.

For cyclotomic K, this consequence already follows from the released field scope once its uniform strip is accepted. General K requires the generalized-field candidate. Under the `49/50` candidate, the numerical generator bound has exponent 50.

## Family 142 resource consequence

For `B=20+(n+1)(ceil(log_2 p)+1)`, the source bounds the relevant Kummer discriminant logarithms and degrees by `O(B^3)`. Merely replacing its strip by delta=1/50 would give `E=O(B^150)`. Keeping the coefficient `1/q` in `Psi_K-q^-1 Psi_M` gives the stronger uniform error parameter
\[
\log D_K+d_K+q^{-1}(\log D_M+d_M)
\ll q(\log p+\log q+2).
\]
Therefore the sharper result is
\[
\ell_q\ll[q(\log p+\log q+2)]^{50},\qquad
E\ll B^{50}(1+\log B)^{50}.
\]
The proof is recorded in `SHARPER_AUXILIARY_PRIMES.md`. It uses only cyclotomic-base Hecke characters; the generalized-field candidate is not needed. The scale constant is absolute and does not require an effective numerical onset from the original fixed-field theta estimates.

The source's supplied algebraic complexity proposition then gives
\[
O(B^{500000}+B^{100}E^{20})=O(B^{500000}).
\]
The old small-characteristic cutoff dominates this coarse bound, so an analytic improvement alone does not justify claiming a useful factorization runtime.

There is a further plausible implementation re-accounting: the source explicitly lists all interpolation grids below `B^400+1`, norm-solver characteristic requirements below `B^8`, and all derivative polynomial degrees below the same grid bound. Replacing its `p>B^200000` branch cutoff by `p>B^402` should preserve those requirements. Its own indexed cost accounting would then be `O(B^20004+B^41 E^10)`, with the small-prime branch `O(B^1402)`. A sequential simulation would be `O(B^40008)` and hence `O(B^41000)` with slack. This last branch change has not been independently reconstructed through all family-142 algebraic routines and is recorded as a separate candidate, not a certified algorithm bound.

## Prior art and limits

* Goldmakher–Louvel, Theorem 1.1 and Definition 1: quadratic Hecke families and their `(M+N)(MN)^epsilon` sieve over arbitrary number fields. https://arxiv.org/html/1112.1642
* Kazhdan–Patterson: general global field/number field metaplectic theta constructions, with root-of-unity hypotheses; its erratum remains essential to the source normalization. https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_1984__59__35_0.pdf
* Thorner–Zaman: cyclic/abelian subgroup reduction, Hecke characters, conductor-discriminant accounting, and unconditional Chebotarev estimates whose current proven field dependence differs from a fixed strip. https://arxiv.org/html/1803.02823
* Bach–Sorenson: small prime ideals with prescribed Artin symbol under extended RH, with essentially quadratic dependence on log discriminant plus degree. https://digitalcommons.butler.edu/facsch_papers/1121/

The search found no established unconditional uniform `49/50` Hecke strip over all number fields, but that limited search is not proof of priority. The parameter optimization and field-scope transfer are the proposed new work. All substantive consequences inherit the unreplayed theta/reflection gate, and the exact arithmetic certificate cannot resolve it.
