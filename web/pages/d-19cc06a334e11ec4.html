# Random finite-grid weight: an exact-identification escape, without a stability theorem

Status: **proved polynomial-rank deduction, independently audited by b_math**. This complements the finite-list counterexample rather than contradicting it. External novelty is unverified. No learning-rate, measurement-cost or state-calibration guarantee is claimed.

## Interface and quantifier

Fix a Hilbert dimension \(d\ge2\), a finite Hermitian frame \(\{A_a\}\) with ordinary common commutant \(\mathbb CI\), and an arbitrary faithful target \(\sigma\). These must be fixed before an independent random weight is drawn. For one weight \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), supply the whole generally non-Hermitian weighted score tuple
\[
 -i[A_a,\sigma]=\lambda\sigma S_a+(1-\lambda)S_a\sigma.
\]
Pointwise in every fixed target/frame, the tuple identifies the target for all but at most
\[
 B=(d^2-1)d(d-1)
\]
interior weights. Consequently one weight drawn uniformly from N distinct interior grid points has identification-failure probability at most \(\min(1,B/N)\). Choose a grid of \(N\ge B/\eta\) points to make this probability at most \(\eta\); sampling its index uses \(O(\log d+\log(1/\eta))\) random bits. The number N counts possible definitions, not measurements at N different weights.

The quantifier is \(\sup_{\sigma,A}\Pr_\lambda[\text{failure}]\le B/N\), with each target/frame fixed before the draw. It is **not** a probability that one realized weight identifies all targets simultaneously. The latter assertion is false by the counterexamples. Adversarial target selection after seeing the random weight is also outside this statement.

## Polynomial proof

In the target eigenbasis, with eigenvalues \(p_i>0\), define
\[
 (K_{a,\lambda})_{ij}=
 \frac{\sqrt{p_ip_j}}{\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j}(A_a)_{ij}.
\]
An equal-score competing state must have a non-scalar whitened Hermitian matrix Y commuting with every K. Thus a scalar **ordinary complex** commutant of the K is already sufficient for identification; imposing the adjoints only strengthens this sufficient condition.

Let \(\mathcal V=\{M:\operatorname{Tr}M=0\}\), with complex dimension \(r=d^2-1\), and consider
\[
 \mathcal T_\lambda:\mathcal V\longrightarrow
  \bigoplus_a\mathbb C^{d\times d},\qquad
 M\longmapsto([M,K_{a,\lambda}])_a.
\]
At the endpoint \(\lambda=1\),
\(K_{a,1}=D^{-1/2}A_aD^{1/2}\).
This is a common similarity of the original frame, so its complex commutant is scalar and \(\mathcal T_1\) has rank r. This is an independently reconstructed endpoint argument, not an assumption about interior weights.

Use the common scalar denominator
\[
 Q(\lambda)=\prod_{i\ne j}[\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j].
\]
It is strictly positive on \([0,1]\), and its polynomial degree is at most \(q=d(d-1)\), allowing repeated eigenvalues. The diagonal entries of K are constant; every offdiagonal denominator is a factor of Q. Therefore the matrix of \(Q(\lambda)\mathcal T_\lambda\) has polynomial entries of degree at most q. Since it has rank r at1, at least one r-by-r minor is a nonzero polynomial \(f(\lambda)\), with degree at most rq=B and \(f(1)\ne0\).

Any interior weight with non-scalar K commutant has rank smaller than r and must be a zero of f. There are at most B distinct such weights. Failure of score identification is a subset of this exceptional set. Coefficients can be irrational or complex; a nonzero one-variable complex polynomial still has at most its degree in distinct roots. No lower bound on the target minimum eigenvalue is needed for this exact rank argument.

## What this does and does not repair

The finite-list theorem constructs collisions whenever the complement-closed list count is at most d−2. The present escape uses a random choice from a grid larger than a degree bound of order d4, with only one score interface realized. For n qubits, the possible-grid size is exponential in n, but its random index has O(n) bits. This is a statement about exact supplied scores, not about acquiring them.

Choose grid points in a closed interval \([\delta,1-\delta]\) if endpoint amplification is unwanted. Entrywise score multipliers then satisfy
\[
 \left|\frac{p_i-p_j}{\lambda p_i+(1-\lambda)p_j}\right|
 \le\frac1\delta,
\]
so \(\|S_a\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\delta^{-1}\|A_a\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\). This simple bound does not imply an operator-norm, efficient tomography or sample-complexity guarantee independent of dimension.

Exact nonzero rank can be arbitrarily ill-conditioned. As the target spectrum separates in a locally hollow eigenbasis, all interior filtered generators can become very small. A random grid avoids exact rank-collapse roots but provides no lower bound on the resulting commutator gap or on conversion from approximate scores to state error. Proving a useful stability bound under explicit target/frame hypotheses is the load-bearing missing step. Accordingly this cannot yet replace the high-temperature or KMS quantitative learning results.

Nearest prior mechanism is the endpoint and filtered-fiber criterion in Dong–Leng, arXiv:2609.28391v1, AppendixA. The polynomial exceptional-set bound is our deduction here. This distinction prevents the finite-weight obstruction from being overstated as an impossibility theorem for all randomized score-definition interfaces.
