# Independent source-proof audit: Markov cotype of `ℓ1` and the ambient-`L1` extension route

## Verdict

I found no gap in the load-bearing proof on pages 3–7 of the pinned family-332 release. The cut encoding, cubic/quartic estimate, stopped-chain accounting, geometric-to-Cesàro comparison, and final constant `3024 = 108 × 28` are internally consistent, including the time factor `t` and the stationary-chain indices.

The release's theorem, if correct, conditionally implies a stronger result than its printed Corollary 6.2: every Lipschitz map from an arbitrary subset of any metric space with finite Markov type 2 into any real ambient `L1(Ω,μ)` extends to the whole source, with a universal factor times `12√21 M₂(X)`. This includes nonseparable sources and arbitrary measure spaces. The deduction transfers cotype to ambient `L1` by finite-cut recovery, applies Mendel–Naor's finite-extension theorem, globalizes by weak*-compactness (or their ultrafilter/bidual route), and projects from the bidual using the norm-one L-projection of `L1`. It does not assert extension into an arbitrary closed subspace of `L1`.

**Evidence status:** the `ℓ1` result is source-reported in a pinned, newly posted manuscript. This audit is an independent internal reconstruction, not peer review, external validation, or proof formalization. The ambient-`L1` extension is a conditional theorem derived here from that premise and cited Banach-space results. Novelty/priority of this further deduction remains unknown.

## Reconstruction and adversarial checks

### 1. Finite cut encoding and recovery

For finitely many `x₁,…,xₙ ∈ ℓ1`, the release uses one coordinate for each nonempty proper subset `B ⊂ [n]`. For original coordinate `k`, set

`v(k) = min_i x_i(k)`,

`b_B(k) = (min_{i∈B} x_i(k) − max_{i∉B} x_i(k))₊`,

`w_B = ||b_B||₁`, and `z_i(B) = 1{i∈B}`. Discard zero weights and set

`T(u) = v + Σ_B u_B b_B`.

The bounds `|v(k)| ≤ Σ_i |x_i(k)|` and `b_B(k) ≤ 2Σ_i |x_i(k)|` put the vectors in `ℓ1`. Sorting the finite values `x_i(k)` shows that the positive cuts at coordinate `k` are nested upper cuts and their gaps reconstruct each `x_i(k)`. It follows pointwise, then after summing/integrating, that

`T(z_i)=x_i`,

`||x_i−x_j||₁ = Σ_B w_B |z_i(B)−z_j(B)| = Σ_B w_B |z_i(B)−z_j(B)|²`,

and `||T(u)−T(u')||₁ ≤ Σ_B w_B|u_B−u'_B|`.

This establishes both the finite metric isometry into the binary cut code and a contractive return to the full ambient space. For arbitrary real `L1(Ω,μ)`, use measurable representatives of the finitely many input classes; finite pointwise minima, maxima, and positive parts are measurable, and the same displayed integrable bounds put `v,b_B` in `L1`. No common σ-finite support is needed. The case of an empty cut family means the inputs coincide and is handled separately.

### 2. Cubic smoothing and the quartic potential

On the finite weighted coordinate cube `Q=[0,1]^B`, the release uses `φ(r)=3r²−2r³` coordinatewise. For a binary center `e`, write `a=u−e`, `δ=u'−u`, and `F_e(u)=||u−e||_H⁴`. Its exact remainder is

`R := F_e(u')−F_e(u)−4||a||_H²⟨a,δ⟩_H`

`  = 2||a||_H²||δ||_H² + (2⟨a,δ⟩_H+||δ||_H²)² ≥ 0`.

Since `|φ'(r)|=6r(1−r) ≤ 6|r−d|` for either bit `d∈{0,1}`, integration along each coordinate segment and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz give

`||Φ(u')−Φ(u)||_{1,w} ≤ 6||a||_H||δ||_H + 3||δ||_H²`.

The source derives `||a||_H²||δ||_H² ≤ R/2` and `||δ||_H⁴ ≤ 4R`; squaring the preceding bound with `(r+s)²≤2r²+2s²` gives `72(R/2)+18(4R)=108R`. I checked the identity and each constant; this is the key deterministic inequality, and no sign or dimension dependence is missing.

For the martingale corollary, the center `e` can be random so long as it is binary and `F₀`-measurable. At time `m`, `4||M_m−e||²(M_m−e)` is `F_m`-measurable and bounded because the cut set and weights are finite. Pairing with the martingale increment therefore has conditional expectation zero. The fourth-power potentials are bounded by `(Σ_B w_B)²`; bounded convergence passes the terminal potential to the a.s. limit, and monotone convergence passes the nonnegative jump sum. This justifies the infinite *sum over martingale times* in Corollary 3.2; it is not an unproved interchange of a nonintegrable term.

### 3. Geometric stopping versus the finite Cesàro average

Let `D(s)=E d(x_{X_s},x_{X_0})²` for a stationary chain and `W=t⁻¹Σ_{s=1}^tD(s)`. Triangle inequality, Minkowski in `L2`, and stationarity give `√D(r+s)≤√D(r)+√D(s)`. Taking `r=1,…,t` in `D(t)≤2D(r)+2D(t−r)` and averaging yields `D(t)≤4W`. For `s=jt+r`, `0≤r<t`, repeated application gives `D(s)≤2j²D(t)+2D(r)`.

For independent `S` with `P(S=s)=p q^s`, `p=1/(t+1)`, `q=t/(t+1)`, put `J=⌊S/t⌋`, `R=S−tJ`. The geometric second moment is `E S²=2t²+t`, so `EJ²≤3`. Also

`P(R=r)=p q^r/(1−q^t) ≤ 2p ≤ 2/t`,

because `q^t≤1/2`. Therefore `E D(R)≤2W`, and `E D(S)≤2·3·4W+2·2W=28W`. The estimates include `t=1`, where `R=0`. The step connecting `D(r+s)` to `D(r),D(s)` uses stationarity, not reversibility.

### 4. Stopped-chain costs, time weights, and constant

The finite-state `A`-walk starts under its stationary law `π`. At each live time it dies with probability `p` at the current index or survives and takes an `A`-step with probability `q`. With `h_i=E_i z_{X_S}`, the resolvent identity is `h_i=pz_i+qΣ_j a_{ij}h_j`. Thus assigning `h_i` while alive and `z_i` after death gives an `H`-valued martingale, eventually constant almost surely. Center it at the initial binary vector `e=z_{X₀}`.

Let

`B = Σ_i π_i ||z_i−Φ(h_i)||_{1,w}²`,

`E_A = Σ_{i,j} π_i a_{ij} ||Φ(h_i)−Φ(h_j)||_{1,w}²`.

At time `m`, the live event has probability `q^m` and is independent of the stationary index `X_m`. A death jump contributes `p q^m B`, and a live transition contributes `q^{m+1} E_A`. Summing the geometric series gives

`E Σ_m ||Φ(M_{m+1})−Φ(M_m)||_{1,w}² = B + (q/p) E_A = B + t E_A`.

The binary center makes the terminal potential `||z_{X_S}−z_{X₀}||_H⁴ = ||x_{X_S}−x_{X₀}||₁²`. Contractivity of `T` transfers the modified-point costs to `ℓ1`. Corollary 3.2 contributes 108; the endpoint comparison contributes 28. Hence the right-hand coefficient is `108·28 = 3024 = (12√21)²`. There is no extra factor of `t`: it is exactly `q/p` and matches the `t` multiplying the one-step energy in Definition 1.1.

The proof only uses `πA=π`, as the paper's Remark 5.1 says; detailed balance is not used. Zero entries of `π` are harmless, and no positivity condition on every state mass is needed. No step found in the proof relies on unmentioned irreducibility, aperiodicity, or convergence to equilibrium; the separate geometric killing ensures almost-sure termination.

## Conditional transfer to every ambient real `L1`

Fix finite data `x_i∈L1(Ω,μ)` and a finite reversible chain `(π,A)`. Repeat the cut construction pointwise in `Ω`, obtaining finite binary vectors `z_i∈{0,1}^B`, weights `w_B=||b_B||_1>0`, and a contractive affine reconstruction `T:R^B→L1` with the same pairwise distance identity. Rescale coordinate `B` by `w_B` to identify the weighted finite `ℓ1` code with ordinary `ℓ1^d`. Apply the release's `N₂(ℓ1)≤C` theorem to the code points. The resulting witness in infinite-dimensional `ℓ1` may use extra coordinates; apply the norm-one projection onto the first `d` coordinates, which fixes the input codes and cannot increase either left-hand cost. Pull this projected vector back through the weight rescaling, and apply `T`. Each left-hand distance is contractively bounded by its cut-code distance; each right-hand input distance is exactly the original `L1` distance. Thus the same `C²` works for every finite test in ambient `L1`:

`N₂(L1(Ω,μ;R)) ≤ 12√21`.

This argument is about the full ambient target. Markov cotype is not inherited by arbitrary subspaces: the Mendel–Naor closed-subspace obstruction inside `ℓ1` is compatible because the cotype witnesses in the ambient target can leave that subspace. No counterexample to the ambient `L1` statement follows from the subspace result.

## Conditional global extension deduction, including nonseparable sources

Let `Y=L1(Ω,μ;R)`, let `(X,d_X)` have Markov type 2 constant `M₂(X)<∞`, take any subset `Z⊆X`, and let `f:Z→Y` be Lipschitz. For a finitely supported probability measure `α` on the Banach target, define its barycenter by its linear mean. If `π` is any finitely supported coupling of `α,β`, then

`||∫y dα−∫y' dβ|| ≤ ∫||y−y'|| dπ ≤ (∫||y−y'||²dπ)^{1/2}`.

Taking the infimum over couplings shows `Y` is `W₂`-barycentric with `Γ=1`. The ambient-`L1` cotype transfer supplies `N₂(Y)≤12√21`. Mendel–Naor Theorem 1.11 therefore gives extensions to every **finite** `S⊂X`, agreeing on `S∩Z`, with Lipschitz constant at most a universal MN factor times `M₂(X)·12√21·Lip(f)`.

The paragraph after MN Theorem 1.11 explicitly turns this finite-extension property into a global map into the ultrapower `Y_U`, using the index set of all finite subsets of `X` and an ultrafilter containing the tails. The source set may be arbitrary; no countable dense set or sequential subsequence is used. For Banach `Y`, the same paragraph cites local reflexivity to embed `Y**` isometrically in `Y_U` with a norm-one projection onto that copy. The exact compatibility needed at boundary points is explicit in Heinrich's Proposition 6.7: the isometric embedding of `Y**` restricts to the canonical embedding of `Y`. Projecting the ultrapower extension and pulling back through this embedding therefore gives a global extension into `Y**` that agrees with the canonical copy of `f`.

There is also a direct compactness proof that uses only the finite-extension conclusion and avoids matching ultrafilters. Write `L_* = C_MN M₂(X)(12√21)Lip(f)`, with `C_MN≥1` an enlarged universal factor, and suppose `Z` is nonempty. Fix `z₀∈Z`. For each `x∈X`, set `R_x=||f(z₀)||+L_*d_X(x,z₀)` and consider the product of weak*-compact balls `K=∏_{x∈X} B_{Y**}(R_x)`. Impose the closed constraints `||F(x)−F(y)||≤L_*d_X(x,y)` for each pair and `F(z)=Jf(z)` for each `z∈Z`. The pair constraints are weak*-closed because the norm is weak*-lower-semicontinuous on `Y**`; the boundary constraints are closed as well. Any finite subfamily mentions only finitely many source points. Include those points and `z₀` in one finite set `S`; MN Theorem 1.11 supplies an extension on `S` with the required finite Lipschitz bound and exact boundary values. Its values lie in the corresponding balls by the triangle inequality. Put zero in all unmentioned product coordinates. This satisfies the selected finite family of constraints, so the full family has the finite-intersection property. Tychonoff compactness yields a global `Y**`-valued extension. If `Z` is empty, a constant map suffices. This argument makes the absence of a source-separability hypothesis explicit.

The standard L-embeddedness theorem for every `L1(μ)` supplies a norm-one projection `P:Y**→JY`, where `J:Y→Y**` is canonical. Since `P` is the identity on `JY`, composing the bidual-valued extension with `J⁻¹P` preserves all prescribed values and does not increase its Lipschitz constant. Therefore, **conditional on the release's cotype theorem**,

`Lip(F) ≤ C_MN · 12√21 · M₂(X) · Lip(f)`,

where `C_MN<∞` is the universal but unspecified constant hidden in Mendel–Naor's `≲`. This holds for arbitrary (possibly nonseparable) metric sources of finite Markov type 2 and any real ambient `L1(Ω,μ)`. For any real Hilbert space, including nonseparable Hilbert spaces, `M₂=1`, so this yields the requested universal Hilbert-to-ambient-`L1` extension bound. The release itself prints only the `ℓ1=c₀*` case in Corollary 6.2; the ambient-`L1` and nonseparable-source statement is the formal deduction above, not a quotation from the release.

## Limits and falsifiers

- The release's source theorem is not treated as an accepted or externally validated theorem. A proof defect in any cited step would invalidate the conditional deductions.
- The extension multiplier is existential and non-numerical because the MN theorem uses `≲`; no explicit total constant is claimed.
- This does not give an algorithm for computing a global extension, a finite-precision implementation, or an extension constrained to a proper subspace of `L1`.
- The subspace failure theorem is a warning against strengthening the *target cotype* claim to all closed subspaces. It is not a counterexample to extension into ambient `L1`.
- Earlier primary sources recorded Hilbert-to-`ℓ1` extension as open, including Lee–Naor (2005, §1.4), and later literature calls the `ℓ1` target problem a major open question; the pinned release postdates those sources. This audit's literature check is targeted, not exhaustive. Priority for the ambient-`L1` deduction is `UNKNOWN`.
