# Log-frequency all-order audit

7 October2026. **Independent reconstruction PASS**, with the quantifiers stated below. This audits r15's LOG_FREQUENCY_REGULARITY.md separately from the first-derivative power theorem. It is internal mathematical validation; novelty and external expert validation remain unestablished.

## The theorem being checked

For each n>=3, one can choose a single real smooth potential V and a nonzero real smooth solution u on all of R^n with -Delta u+Vu=0, such that

    |u(x)|<=C exp(-c|x|log(2+|x|)),
    |partial^mu V(x)|<=C_mu (1+|x|)^(-1/2)
                               [log(2+|x|)]^(|mu|+3/2),

for every Cartesian multi-index mu. The potential and state are the same for all mu. Constants C_mu may depend arbitrarily on mu, n and the once-fixed construction parameters. In particular every fixed derivative tends to zero, although the oscillation wavelength shrinks. No symbol, analytic, Gevrey or uniform-in-order estimate follows. The decay is stronger than every fixed exponential, but weaker than exp(-r^(1+epsilon)) for every fixed epsilon>0.

## 1. Radial calculus and the single K

Fix K>e^4 and put R=(K^2+|x|^2)^(1/2), a=log R, ell=(R/a)^(1/2), kappa=a^(3/2)R^(-1/2), eta=(Ra)^(-1/2). Then a/ell=a^2 ell/R=kappa, ell/R=1/(a ell)=eta and eta=kappa/a^2. Both eta and kappa decrease for R>=e^4. Also ell^(-2)=a/R<=kappa and a>=4.

For every fixed j>=1, differentiation of R gives |D^j R|<=C_j R^(1-j), with constants independent of K: every term is a constant times a monomial in x divided by a power of R, and |x|<=R. For fixed real gamma,delta, ordinary scalar differentiation of R^gamma(log R)^delta gives

    |(d/dR)^j[R^gamma(log R)^delta]|
       <=C_(j,gamma,delta) R^(gamma-j)(log R)^delta,

because each extra factor 1/log R is at most1/4. Cartesian composition gives the corresponding bound for every jet. No increase of K depending on j is needed. This applies to a,ell,kappa,eta and the two repair weights kappa and a^2/R^2.

For Phi=Rlog R-R-(Klog K-K), direct differentiation yields grad Phi=(a/R)x and

    |grad Phi|^2-a^2=-K^2 a^2/R^2,
    |D^j Phi|<=C_j a R^(1-j), j>=1.

The exponential chain rule for E=exp(-Phi) expresses D^j E as E times a finite sum of products of jets of Phi whose total order is j. Each Phi jet of order r is at most C_r a^r since aR>=1. Hence |D^j E|<=C_j a^j E, for every fixed j and the same K.

The construction needs K sufficiently large only for a finite list: slowly varying cover, tangent-frequency separation after macro comparison, microscopic visibility, freezing error, and the zeroth-order correction size. Those conditions are stated before any arbitrary derivative order enters. Fix K satisfying them. All later C_j may enlarge, but none is used to demand a new smallness inequality. This is the key quantifier check: it constructs one pair u,V, rather than a sequence of pairs indexed by derivative order.

## 2. Geometry and weighted repair remain valid

The macro radius obeys |grad ell|<=C eta(K), so the bounded-degree variable-radius cover and smooth partition from the power audit apply. Its overlap bound and tangent separation are independent of K. A single fixed smooth bump supplies derivative estimates |D^j chi_i|<=C_j ell_i^(-j) for every j, because the locally finite normalizing sum is at least1 and has bounded overlap. The constants can grow with j.

On an active macro patch, a_i/a(x)=1+O(eta(K)). On a microscopic ball of radius rho/a, the coefficient-freezing error is O(rho eta(K)). The same compact separated-frequency family, independent of K, gives its analytic sublevel threshold tau for fixed theta,rho. Thus the sparse set Omega={|q|<tau} is obtained at one sufficiently large K. No limit of q as K tends to infinity is used.

The boundary-independent weighted sparse inverse already reconstructed in WEIGHTED_LANDIS_HOSTILE_AUDIT.md applies with h_1=kappa and h_2=a^2/R^2. Their local comparison constants on rho/a balls are uniform in large K and rho<=1; all higher jet constants are irrelevant to the inverse. Its bound is C rho^2 h/a^2 E. The approximate forcing has size CE[kappa+tau K^2 a^2/R^2] in Omega, so its correction satisfies

## 3. Every differentiated canceled residual

Define q=sum chi_i cos_i, with cos_i=cos(a_i t_i dot (x-x_i)), a_i constant on its patch and x_i dot t_i=0. Since ell^(-1)<=a, all jets satisfy |D^j q|<=C_j a^j. Local comparability remains valid after taking derivatives, since derivatives of a cutoff are supported on the same enlarged patch.

Let J=Delta q+a^2q. The explicit cancellation is

    J=sum[(Delta chi_i)cos_i
                +2 grad chi_i dot grad cos_i
                +(a^2-a_i^2)chi_i cos_i].

The first two base envelopes are ell^(-2) and a/ell, both at most kappa. Each differentiation of a wave costs a and each differentiation of a cutoff costs ell^(-1)<=a. For the mismatch coefficient, its zeroth envelope is a^2 ell/R=kappa. For k>=1,

    |D^k(a^2-a_i^2)|<=C_k a^2/R^k<=C_k kappa*a^k.

The ratio of the last expression to kappa*a^k is (aR)^(1/2-k), at most1 for k>=1. Multi-index Leibniz therefore gives |D^m J|<=C_m kappa*a^m.

For T=grad Phi dot grad q, the partition-gradient coefficient has jets bounded by

    C_k (a/ell)*a^k=C_k kappa*a^k.

This follows by Leibniz from |D^r grad Phi|<=C_r a/R^r and |D^(r+1)chi_i|<=C_r ell^(-r-1), using 1/R<=a and ell^(-1)<=a. The tangent-wave coefficient is h_i=a_i(a/R)(x dot t_i). At order0, tangency makes |x dot t_i|<=C ell, giving |h_i|<=C kappa. At order k>=1, the only two types of terms come from leaving the linear factor undifferentiated or differentiating it once:

    |D^k h_i|<=C_k a^2[ell/R^(k+1)+1/R^k]
                <=C_k kappa*a^k.

The first summand divided by kappa*a^k is (aR)^(-k); the second ratio is (aR)^(1/2-k). Both are bounded for every k>=1. Further wave derivatives cost a. Thus |D^m T|<=C_m kappa*a^m.

The final radial coefficient is B=|grad Phi|^2-a^2-Delta Phi=-K^2 a^2/R^2-Delta Phi. Jets of Delta Phi have size at most C_k a/R^(k+1)<=C_k kappa*a^k. The first coefficient is at most C_K kappa at order0; further jets cost R^(-k)<=a^k. Hence |D^k B|<=C_(k,K) kappa*a^k.

Since Delta f=E[J-2T+Bq], multi-index Leibniz with the proved jets of E and q gives

    |D^m Delta f|<=C_(m,K) kappa*a^m E, all m>=0.      (B)

Also |D^m f|<=C_m a^mE. Smoothness alone would not imply(B); the differentiated tangent and mismatch coefficients are indispensable.

## 4. All-order kernel and composition estimates

Fix the same sigma after tau and use epsilon=sigma/a and a fixed nonnegative radial normalized C-infinity compactly supported mollifier. Scalar radial calculus gives |D^j epsilon|<=C_j epsilon/R^j for all j>=1. Let Y=x-epsilon z. Then |DY|<=C and |D^jY|<=C_j a^(j-1) for j>=2; these latter loose bounds follow from epsilon/R^j<=a^(j-1).

A multivariate composition derivative of total order m is a finite sum of terms with k derivatives of the outer function and k jets of Y of positive orders r_1,...,r_k, with sum r_i=m. The product of these jets has envelope a^(sum(r_i-1))=a^(m-k), treating first-order jets as bounded. If the outer function has jet envelope a^(p+k)E, the result is a^(p+m)E. This supplies the explicit all-order composition rule used below; constants count finitely many partitions at each fixed m.

For the smooth part G=integral psi(z)f(Y)dz, differentiate its exact Laplacian identity from the main audit. The leading term uses(B) as outer function; the composition rule gives C_(m,K) kappa*a^mE after micro comparison. For the other terms, coefficient jets preserve their base envelopes:

    D^j(grad epsilon)=O(a^(-1)R^(-1-j)),
    D^j(|grad epsilon|^2)=O(a^(-2)R^(-2-j)),
    D^j(Delta epsilon)=O(a^(-1)R^(-2-j)).

At total differentiation order m the respective composed f-jets cost a^(m-j+2), a^(m-j+2), a^(m-j+1). Thus their products are bounded respectively by

    C_m a^(m+1)/R,
    C_m a^m/R^2,
    C_m a^m/R^2,

times E, since (aR)^(-j)<=1. All are at most C_m kappa*a^mE. Therefore |D^m Delta G|<=C_(m,K) kappa*a^mE. The direct composition rule also gives |D^m G|<=C_m a^mE.

For H, differentiate its kernel against W, not W. Write t=a/sigma, zeta=t(x-y), and K_x(y)=t^n psi(zeta). On its support |x-y|<=1/t. For every r>=1,

    |D_x^r zeta|<=C_r t R^(1-r)<=C_r a^r,
    |D_x^r t^n|<=C_r t^n R^(-r).

The first estimate includes the differentiated linear x-y factor; there are no higher derivatives of that factor. Leibniz and the same finite composition partitions imply

    |D_x^j K_x(y)|<=C_j a^(n+j), all j>=0.

Its derivatives retain support in the same ball because psi and every derivative are compactly supported there. Their integrals against the locally integrable W are differentiable on compact x-sets, whose smoothing radii are bounded above and below. By(A), support volume O(a^(-n)) and micro comparison yield

    |D^j H|<=C_(j,K) kappa*a^(j-2)E.

For j=m+2 this proves |D^m Delta H|<=C_(m,K) kappa*a^mE. For j=m it gives |D^m H|<=C_(m,K) a^mE since eta=kappa/a^2<=1. Hence

    |D^m F|<=C_(m,K) a^mE,
    |D^m Delta F|<=C_(m,K) kappa*a^mE.                 (C)

This argument never presupposes an all-order bound on the harmonic correction or on its irregular zero extension.

## 5. Quotient induction and the global quantifier

The zero neighborhoods are harmonic and the potential is identically zero on their open set O. Outside O, |F|>=c tau E. For a multi-index mu, differentiate VF=Delta F and isolate the term F partial^mu V:

    F partial^mu V=partial^mu Delta F
        -sum_(gamma<mu) binomial(mu,gamma)
                      (partial^gamma V)(partial^(mu-gamma)F).

Assume lower-order bounds |partial^gamma V|<=C_gamma kappa*a^|gamma|. Each summand is bounded by C_mu kappa*a^|mu| E using(C); division by F proves the same bound at order mu. The base order is the separately proved safe quotient. On O all derivatives vanish. The two smooth definitions agree on their open overlap, so the bounds include the interface and every possible zero. This proves all multi-indices by induction for the same u,V and the already fixed K.

At infinity R is comparable to1+|x| and log R to log(2+|x|), with fixed-K constants on the remaining compact region. Thus kappa*a^m=R^(-1/2)(log R)^(m+3/2) tends to zero for every fixed m, proving the claimed global envelope. E has the r log r envelope. Sparsity provides points with |q|>=tau, so F is nonzero. No derivative-order-dependent K, new coloring or new PDE solution is introduced.

## Spectral filter, independently reconstructed

SPECTRAL_STRUCTURE_FILTER.md also **passes** in its stated standard-functional-analysis interface. Since V is bounded real and tends to0, H=-Delta+V is self-adjoint on H^2; u is a zero-energy L^2 eigenfunction because Delta u=Vu in L^2. Multiplication by V composed with the free resolvent is compact: truncate V to a bounded region, use local Rellich compactness of the H^2 resolvent output, then make the exterior operator norm small with the exterior sup of V. Hence essential spectrum is[0,infinity).

If the quadratic form of H were nonnegative, q(|u|)=q(u)=0 by the real Sobolev absolute-value identity. Positivity implies q(|u|,v)=0 for every H^1 test v, by considering the discriminant of q(|u|+t v). Thus |u| is a nonnegative nonzero weak solution. Local regularity and Harnack make it strictly positive. Unit-ball Harnack constants depend only on n and the global bound on V, so an overlapping ball chain gives |u(x)|>=c exp(-C|x|). This contradicts the global superexponential upper envelope. Consequently inf spectrum(H)<0. Since essential spectrum starts at0 and H is bounded below, at least one negative discrete eigenvalue exists. The zero state is a threshold state above negative spectrum; it is not a nonnegative-operator or ground-state example.
