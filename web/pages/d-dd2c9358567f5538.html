No breakthrough claim. This is an algorithm/interface result, not a consequence of acquiring a hard source. Source metadata and recoverable snapshots are in `sources/r14_computation/cycle6/`.

## Exact question and proposed answer

Can sparse polynomial GCD computation attain quasi-linear dependence on the number of variables n, a supplied upper bound T on the GCD term count, and numeric total degree D, without ordinary derivatives, a primitive root, discrete logarithms, or a characteristic-greater-than-degree assumption?

Proposed Monte Carlo theorem: let nonzero sparse A,B belong to K[x_1,...,x_n], where K is a finite field of cardinality Q with its representation/arithmetic supplied. Let D=max(1,deg A,deg B), t_A=#A, t_B=#B, and let supplied T>=#gcd(A,B). Given 0<delta<1, the algorithm below returns the lex-leading-coefficient-normalized GCD with probability at least 1-delta, in

  soft-O(n R T D + n R (t_A+t_B) log(D+1)) field operations,

plus soft-O(n R (t_A+t_B+TD) log(TD+1)) integer-label/index bit operations and representation-dependent coefficient dictionary costs. Here

  M=16T, W=M+1,
  R=max(8,ceil(32 ln(16(n+1)T^2/delta))), m=R(n+1),
  L=(m+1)T+1,
  C=m(2WD^2+4D)+D*(L*(L+1)/2+1).

Require Q-1>=2C/delta. There is no restriction on char K. Field multiplications/inversions and equality/order costs must be charged in the concrete representation. For conventional finite-field bit strings, sorting/matching keys has soft-linear bit overhead in total key length; it is not a quadratic all-pairs comparison. Fast univariate GCD is an explicit standard primitive, not a property of the prototype.

This statement supplies a sufficiently large field, not an arbitrary small base field at zero acquisition cost. Extending a small finite field is a plausible standard completion, but construction and conversion costs are not included in this theorem. T is supplied; adaptive unknown-T verification is not yet proved here. Output on a bad random event may be incorrect or FAILURE; this is not a Las Vegas guarantee. D is numeric, not log D.

## Author claims versus our deductions and nearest prior art

Huang–Gao, arXiv:2609.08074v1 (8 Sep 2026), Theorem 3 and §§3.1–3.2, claim sparse GCD complexity soft-O(nTD) for characteristic zero or char greater than the relevant partial GCD-degree bound. Their exponent recovery uses derivatives after short Hensel lifting. Their finite-field and integer algorithms also handle missing term bounds and verification; those features are not silently inherited here.

Arnold–Roche, arXiv:1401.6694v2 (2 May 2014), §§3.2,4,5, already establish O(T)-range random integer Kronecker weights and diversified-coefficient majority recovery of singleton terms. They reconstruct exponents through n-dimensional linear systems. Majority selection and polynomial-size diversification are established prior art, not our discoveries.

Huang–Gao, arXiv:1712.05481v1 (15 Dec 2017), §3.1 and Theorems 3.12/3.14, already use substitutions s+p e_i to read coordinate exponents, avoiding the linear systems and obtaining soft-O(nTD) interpolation. The additive-shift idea is established. Their exact-polynomial oracle does not immediately supply normalized sparse GCD shadows with weight-dependent monomial offsets. The precise novelty candidate is the combination of common normalization at y=1, reference-term subtraction to cancel these offsets, coordinate minima to restore absolute exponents, and generic coprime-cofactor preservation at unchanged degree scale. Whether this combination is already in GCD literature is UNKNOWN. A competent baseline must include adapting Arnold–Roche to exponent differences, and existing Huang–Monagan/Demin–van der Hoeven GCD normalization.

Qiu et al., arXiv:2606.12144v1 (10 Jun 2026), §2/Theorem 18: a solver polynomial in sparsity, log D, and log Q would imply NP subset BPP, even on promised sparse outputs. Our numeric-D cost does not evade that barrier.

## Full construction

1. Compute the known monomial content v_i=min(val_{x_i} A,val_{x_i} B). Replace A,B by x^{-v}A,x^{-v}B. Their GCD F has minimum coordinate exponent zero in every coordinate. This uses sparse input supports only. Normalize the final output by lexicographic leading coefficient.
2. Draw R independent vectors s_j uniformly from {1,...,M}^n. Consider m weights s_j and s_j+e_i (1<=i<=n). Draw a common b uniformly from (K^*)^n, independently of all weights.
3. For each weight w, form A(b_1 y^{w_1},...,b_n y^{w_n}), and B likewise. Remove each observed univariate monomial content separately, compute their monic univariate GCD, and divide this GCD by its value at y=1. Zero inputs or zero normalization values return FAILURE. Let H_w be the result.
4. Count coefficients occurring in the R base H_{s_j}. A coefficient repeated within a single image is unusable. Retain coefficients occurring in at least R/2 base images. On the good event below these retained coefficients are precisely the individual normalized term signatures of F. Choose any retained coefficient as reference; choice needs no knowledge of the answer.
5. For each retained signature c_t and coordinate i, find a round in which c_t and the reference c_r occur in both H_s and H_{s+e_i}. If their univariate degree labels are d_w(t), set

   Delta_{t,i}=[d_{s+e_i}(t)-d_{s+e_i}(r)]-[d_s(t)-d_s(r)].

   Reject missing or mutually inconsistent data. Recover absolute exponents by

   e_{t,i}=Delta_{t,i}-min_u Delta_{u,i}.

6. Recover coefficients c_t/b^{e_t}, restore x^v, and divide by the lexicographic leading coefficient. Return the sparse result. These coefficients initially reconstruct F/F(b), so the final normalization fixes its field unit.

## Lemma A: actual univariate GCDs acquire the claimed shadows

Write reduced A=FU and B=FV, where gcd(U,V)=1. All total degrees are at most D. For fixed positive integer weight w with coordinates <=W, introduce algebraically independent X_i. In the Laurent polynomial ring K[X_1^{+/-1},...,X_n^{+/-1},y^{+/-1}], the map X_i -> X_i y^{w_i}, y->y is an automorphism, with inverse X_i -> X_i y^{-w_i}. Hence U(X y^w) and V(X y^w) remain coprime there. Localizing the X variables to K(X) preserves coprimality up to units. Remove the lowest powers of y to obtain P,Q in K[X][y]; they have no common y factor, and are coprime in K(X)[y].

Their resultant Res_y(P,Q) is a nonzero polynomial in X (if both have y degree zero, use resultant 1 and separately exclude their nonzero constant values). Its total X degree is at most

  (deg_y P)(deg_X Q)+(deg_y Q)(deg_X P)<=2WD^2.

Exclude zeros of the leading and lowest coefficients of P,Q, costing at most 4D in total degree. At all remaining b, specialization preserves the y degrees and minima and leaves coprime specialized cofactors. Therefore the univariate GCD obtained after removing the observed input valuations is a scalar multiple of

  y^{-k_w} F(b y^w), k_w=min_{e in supp F} w dot e,

provided the minimal F bucket is nonzero. The nonvanishing/diversification lemma below guarantees that bucket condition. Because y^{-k_w}=1 at y=1, dividing by the observed GCD value at one cancels its arbitrary monic unit and gives

  H_w(y)=y^{-k_w}F(b y^w)/F(b).

No cofactor oracle is used computationally. U,V only witness the probability bound. For all m weights, the specialization failure probability is at most m(2WD^2+4D)/(Q-1) by the nonzero-grid polynomial bound. Powers and labels are integers; Laurent automorphism and resultants do not require separability or nonzero derivatives. It is unnecessary to assume the GCD is squarefree.

## Lemma B: only encountered buckets need diversification

Condition on all selected weights. Each univariate bucket is a nonempty subset S of F's t<=T terms, with formal coefficient

  P_S(X)=sum_{e in S} a_e X^e.

There are at most mT such encountered buckets and T singleton polynomials; additionally include F(X). Thus there are at most L=(m+1)T+1 distinct nonzero polynomials. Distinct subsets define distinct polynomials: their monomial supports differ and the coefficients are nonzero, in every characteristic. Every difference of two distinct bucket polynomials is nonzero of degree at most D. Every bucket itself is nonzero of degree at most D. Excluding their zeros, their pairwise-difference zeros, and F(b)=0 costs at most

  D*(L*(L+1)/2+1)/(Q-1).

This excludes vanished buckets, aliases between unrelated clusters, and singleton coefficients accidentally equaling other bucket coefficients. It does not require enumeration of 2^T subsets or knowledge of F. The event is used only in the analysis; the finite actual set is determined independently of b by the chosen weights. Common division by F(b) preserves all distinctions.

## Lemma C: frequent coefficients acquire a valid reference and exactly the support

For distinct integer exponent vectors e,f, fix a coordinate where e_i!=f_i and fix all other random coordinates of s. The equality s dot(e-f)=0 determines at most one integer value of s_i. Thus its probability is <=1/M. The same holds for (s+e_k) dot(e-f)=0, since the added coordinate difference is a fixed integer constant.

For each true term, the probability of collision in one base image is <=(T-1)/M<=1/16. Base rounds are independent. Hoeffding gives

  Pr[a fixed term collides in >=R/2 rounds] <= exp(-49R/128) <= exp(-R/4).

With probability >=1-T exp(-R/4), every true term is singleton in strictly more than half the base images. Its signature then occurs more than R/2 times. Conversely, suppose a non-singleton bucket signature occurred at least R/2 times. Lemma B makes those occurrences represent the SAME formal subset S. Any term in S would then collide in at least R/2 rounds, contradicting the preceding event. Thus exactly all true signatures pass the frequency filter, including a valid reference. This majority reasoning is inherited from Arnold–Roche, with a strict inequality to avoid threshold ambiguity.

For each ordered pair of true terms (t,r), coordinate i, and one round, the union of collisions of these two terms in the base and shifted image has probability <=4(T-1)/M<=1/4. Hence the probability that no round simultaneously isolates that pair in both images is <=4^{-R}. A union bound over <=nT^2 pair-coordinate choices gives <=nT^2*4^{-R}. This covers a reference selected adaptively from the frequent signatures. Total hash failure is at most T exp(-R/4)+nT^2*4^{-R}<=delta/2 for the declared R.

## Lemma D: integer differences remove both scalar and monomial ambiguity

On all good events, the coefficient signature of an isolated term a_t x^{e_t} is c_t=a_t b^{e_t}/F(b), identical across weights, and its label is d_w(t)=w dot e_t-k_w. Pair subtraction cancels k_w, and subtraction across w=s and w=s+e_i gives Delta_{t,i}=e_{t,i}-e_{r,i} as an integer, not a field residue. By the known removed monomial content, min_t e_{t,i}=0. Therefore min_t Delta_{t,i}=-e_{r,i}, and the coordinate-minimum formula recovers every absolute e_t. Dividing signatures by b^{e_t} recovers a_t/F(b). Final field-unit normalization recovers the canonical GCD. This also covers a constant/one-term GCD; all differences are zero and the input monomial content supplies its support.

Summing Lemma A/B probabilities gives <=C/(Q-1)<=delta/2. Adding Lemma C proves the proposed Monte Carlo correctness guarantee.

## Complete cost and competent controls

All images have degree <=WD=O(TD). There are R(n+1) GCDs. Using quasi-linear dense polynomial multiplication and fast univariate GCD, these cost soft-O(nRTD) field operations, including dense image materialization, valuation scans and normalization. The prototype instead uses sparse schoolbook Euclidean division and does not validate this asymptotic primitive.

At the one common base point b, evaluate every sparse input coefficient a_e b^e once by binary exponentiation: O(n(t_A+t_B) log(D+1)) field multiplications. For each base weight compute all degree dot products in O(n(t_A+t_B)) integer operations. Each shifted image reuses its base labels and adds e_i, so forming n+1 images costs O(n(t_A+t_B)) additions after dot products per round, not an additional n-fold coefficient-evaluation cost. Sort/combine equal integer labels; sorting overhead is logarithmic. Store/filter shadow coefficient keys and labels in total soft-O(nRT) field-representation key operations on the good event, or soft-O(nRTD) on arbitrary outputs. Compare all available pair votes in O(nRT) integer operations, then reconstruct coefficients in O(nT log(D+1)) field operations.

A bounded implementation must stop if the frequent-signature count exceeds T, and reject negative, duplicate, or degree>D recovered exponents, so bad events do not create unbounded reconstruction time. The prototype limits frequent count and rejects degree violations/duplicate recovered exponents. Its randomness and field size remain diagnostic rather than admitted to the formal theorem budget. Failed normalization/specialization returns FAILURE, without silently restarting at uncharged expected cost.

The strongest control is existing interpolation applied to the SAME normalized-shadow interface: Arnold–Roche-style linear systems can recover exponent differences and use the same coordinate minima, at added n-dimensional solve cost. Huang–Gao 2017 already avoids those solves for exact polynomial oracles, so no claim of first additive-shift exponent reconstruction is appropriate. Huang–Monagan 2024 gives soft-O(nTD^2) GCD without large-characteristic conditions according to the Sep2026 primary prior-art table; the potential improvement is one numeric degree factor, conditional on this theorem's validity and interface/novelty audit. The Sep2026 derivative algorithm is an equally relevant faster control wherever its characteristic condition holds. Runtime comparisons must include its implementation and use fast GCD on both sides before claiming speedups.

Finite success is not a proof, independent validation, or a fast-GCD benchmark.

Initial implementation failure: Counter.update(dict) summed exponent labels instead of counting coefficient appearances. Preserved in `shadow_gcd_initial_bug.json`; fixed to update dict keys. This is an implementation bug, not a scientific counterexample. The first thought of requiring globally distinct values for all 2^T subset sums was abandoned: only the actual buckets encountered under b-independent selected weights matter. Pretending the reference support is supplied was also abandoned: frequent singleton acquisition supplies it.

No extension-field construction, unknown-T stopping rule, Las Vegas verification, base-field bit complexity, or integer coefficient-height bound has been proved here. The formal large-Q bound is conservative and typically much larger than the fixture field. Novelty may collapse to a known normalization technique. Independent mathematical audit must inspect Laurent/resultant preservation, the adaptive-reference union bound, and field-size/cost accounting.
