## Acoustic and optical pressure measurement

For the first-order model, each colour gives `z_i = p a_i + d`, where `a_i=3 A_R(nu_i)/(2 R T)` is set by gas dispersion and `d` is the unknown shared cavity-length response. One colour cannot distinguish `p` from `d`; two colours can when `a_blue != a_red`, with `p_hat=(z_blue-z_red)/(a_blue-a_red)`. The published implementation solves a more exact nonlinear refractivity/virial system for optical pressure and cavity-length change. A coarse pressure input is still needed to choose the integer mode branch within about 3.2 kPa.

The source uses 194.368624 THz and 473.611873 THz, helium at about 303 K, a 15 cm dual-band cavity, two frequency references sharing a comb/GPS-disciplined base and synchronous counters. It reports 100 pressure/temperature/refractivity quadruplets from 0.05 to 0.5 MPa. The cavity deformation reaches about 370 nm; linear-fit length residuals are 18 pm. A chamber pressure step of 0.1 MPa takes about 650 s to reach 0.1 mK gradients. The combined helium uncertainty is 5.7 ppm, dominated by 4.0 ppm dispersion theory, then 3.0 ppm apparatus statistics and 2.5 ppm temperature. Optical pressure is thus decoupled from the accuracy of the mechanical comparison pressure after mode identification, but still depends on accurate helium optical theory and thermodynamic temperature.

The primary worker's calculation reproduces a 3.006 ppm readout uncertainty at 250 kPa under independent 200 Hz/channel noise, consistent with the paper's 3.0 ppm statistical term. My multi-colour extension states the covariance-aware GLS estimator for `z=p a+X beta+epsilon`: pressure is identifiable iff `a` is outside the nuisance span, and the GLS residual gives an over-identification test when there are more colours than fitted parameters. With 3 colours and a shared-offset nuisance, there is one lack-of-fit degree of freedom. The exact caveat is equally important: an unmodelled error parallel to `a` aliases pressure for any number of colours. More wavelengths do not remove helium-dispersion theory uncertainty or test an arbitrary nuisance that lies in the pressure direction.

The source itself notes that a 750 THz blue channel could increase the two-colour signal by about threefold. A synthetic three-colour fixture with a hypothetical 750 THz channel, independent 200 Hz noise and the first-order helium expansion gives 0.638 ppm readout uncertainty at 250 kPa. This is only a design calculation. A new high-reflectivity band, channel covariance, mirror phase, and higher-order dispersion would need measurement before expecting that gain. The saved diagnostic injects a synthetic channel offset to exercise the residual and an exact parallel-error counterexample to exercise the non-identifiability.

Direct prior art includes optical pressure refractometry and two-gas distortion correction. The bounded novelty boundary is the 2025 same-fill synchronous two-frequency experimental realization using gas dispersion to separate pressure from shared cavity deformation, not pressure-from-light or nuisance elimination as a general method. The 2026 compact dual-cavity preprint improves portability/repeatability but calibrates deformation against a piston gauge. A decisive next experiment adds a third synchronous colour on a second cavity material and varies pressure, temperature, and fill history; measure the full covariance and preregister the residual threshold. A fourth colour is needed to fit one specified chromatic coefficient while retaining a model-check degree of freedom.

## Nanoscale calorimetry

Gómez-Torres et al.'s July 2026 arXiv preprint reports 50 microsecond Joule pulses in differential suspended SiN/Pt calorimeters to access heat capacity from hBN-encapsulated monolayer CrSBr through bulk-like flakes, under in-plane field. The methods paper's working equation reconstructs differential heat capacity from current, bridge voltage, cell temperature slopes, and thermometer resistance slopes. The CrSBr analysis additionally estimates hBN addenda and subtracts a Debye-type phonon background to infer magnetic entropy. The preprint reports the field- and layer-dependent anomalies; the arithmetic reconstruction of rounded transition-line values agrees in scale with the source fit, but is not raw-data replay. Source data were inaccessible through an anti-bot challenge during this audit.

The paired-field identity cancels any field-invariant additive platform/encapsulant contribution before integrating `(C(T,H)-C(T,H0))/T`. This yields total field-induced specimen entropy conditional on same-device pairing and a field-invariant addenda background. It does not yield spin-only entropy: a September 2026 bulk PRB paper reports precursor spin-phonon coupling, so lattice heat capacity can itself vary with field. H–H0–H ordering, empty-cell/hBN-only field sweeps, and field-dependent Raman or another spin-lattice separation are needed for that attribution. The experimental result demonstrates direct scalar thermodynamics for the specific CrSBr devices, not local heat-capacity imaging or universal sensitivity to monolayers.

The decisive next gate is obtaining and replaying the deposited curves, running an H–H0–H field order with shared temperature grid and full covariance, and comparing total specimen field-induced entropy with the source's background-subtracted spin entropy. Claim and source boundaries, source-reported device specifications, and costs are in [nanoscale report](nanoscale_calorimetry/FINAL_REPORT.md) and [ledger](nanoscale_calorimetry/CLAIMS.json); its finite source-table arithmetic is reproducible with [audit_source_table.py](nanoscale_calorimetry/audit_source_table.py).

## SThM calibration design (secondary)

For the source's `Y=c+a k/(b+k)` response, transforming to `z=k/(b+k)` maps local parameter gradients to quadratic-regression rows. The classical three-support D/G design therefore places equal mass at the two transformed endpoints and midpoint. On the source's example interval `[0.1,10] W m^-1 K^-1` with six reference readings, the midpoint is 0.373236 W m^-1 K^-1; under exact standards and equal independent noise, its local information determinant is 1.967 times a six-point log grid's and its maximum response leverage is 3 versus 5.719. This is conditional design, not a device result; standard-conductivity certificates, shared-reference covariance, tip drift, roughness, and model fit must be included in a real design. At 10 W m^-1 K^-1 the illustrative inverse SD remains 0.815 W m^-1 K^-1, so saturation remains decisive.
