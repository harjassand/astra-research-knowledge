# Experimental gate for an mmax-based B-cell force sensor

## Objective

Test whether force feedback based on a running maximum of local BCR–antigen–tether cluster size improves antigen-affinity ranking under dose heterogeneity after accounting for antigen-presenting membrane mobility, time, and true mechanical work.

## Assay design

Use primary B1-8 B cells or an equivalently calibrated BCR-affinity series with known soluble affinity/off-rate measurements. Present the same defined trivalent NIP-DNA antigen over a logarithmic surface-density series spanning below, near, and above the candidate threshold. Use DOPC-like mobile and DPPC-like less-mobile bilayers, measuring diffusion/mobility by single-particle tracking or FRAP rather than treating lipid identity as the causal variable. Keep tether sequence, tether rupture threshold, antigen valency, ligand labeling, and BCR density fixed or independently measured.

Acquire simultaneous time-resolved maps of local BCR–antigen cluster size, force/tension sensor signal, extraction events, contact lifetime, total extracted antigen, and antigen delivery to MHC-II compartments. Sampling cadence must resolve the predicted early force onset; include a cadence pilot against the reported sub-10-s DPPC extraction and delayed DOPC onset. Quantify accessible antigen pool and movement into the synapse, since high mobility can recruit additional ligand during the assay.

## Locked model comparison

Before held-out evaluation, fit force schedules using training densities only. Compare:

1. the preregistered `F(mmax)` family with threshold and ramp parameters;
2. a time-only `F(t)` family optimized with the same training densities and affinity series;
3. a no-feedback baseline.

Give all policies the same calibration data, force ceiling, and prior information. Report affinity ranking within matched-dose pairs and with independent dose draws separately. Match or stratify by integrated force-displacement work and contact duration; do not use `Σ(F/m)x` as a substitute for experimental work. Evaluate prespecified held-out low/high densities and both mobility conditions without retuning.

## Pass/fail observations

Evidence for a transferable mmax controller requires (i) measured force onset to track the running maximum after dose and mobility adjustment, (ii) held-out dose ranking to improve against the dose-informed, parameter-optimized time-only policy, and (iii) the improvement to remain on the duration/physical-work Pareto frontier with low censoring. It should replicate across tether strengths or include them as explicit state variables.

The candidate fails as a dose-robust mechanism if force onset tracks time or membrane mobility after conditioning on `mmax`; if affinity ranking advantage vanishes under same-dose comparison; if low-dose contacts fail to reach the ramp and the policy's rank depends on long right-censored contacts; or if an equally informed time-only controller matches ranking at equal measured work and contact time.

## Next gate

No new biological claim should be made from the present simulations. The next decisive datum is a held-out-dose, two-mobility time series jointly measuring local cluster maximum, force onset, and extracted antigen. The reported B-cell mobility experiment supplies the tools and motivates this factorial control; it does not validate `mmax` feedback.
