\begin{abstract}
No original foundational breakthrough was established in this campaign. Thirty independent-context explorations, four adversarial comparisons, and a five-investigator Sol depth effort produced scoped constructions, prior-art identifications, and exact method obstructions. The most developed new attempt concerned repairing normalized Hilbert--Schmidt approximate representations while charging dimension changes. This note gives the self-contained matrix arguments that survived internal checking, a conditional constructive update, and the precise unresolved step. None proves flexible stability of an infinite group, a new non-hyperlinear group, or an advance of the requested significance. Originality of the local formulations is unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Target and evidence boundary}
For $A\in M_n(\mathbb C)$ write $\|A\|_{2,n}^2=\tr(A^*A)/n$. This normalization is essential. A finitely presented group $\Gamma=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ has a tuple of unitary matrices $U_s$ with small relator errors. The intended conclusion was an exact representation on a space of dimension $N=n+o(n)$ whose compressed generators approximate $U_s$. Dimension changes and generator perturbations must both vanish in the stated normalized metric.

Strict repair on dimension $n$ is already impossible for infinite hyperlinear property-(T) groups \cite{BL}. The cohomological unnormalized Frobenius stability theorem \cite{DGLT} does not imply its normalized counterpart. The flexible target remains unproved here. The root and depth investigators attempted spectral deletion, support saturation, full unitary correction, exact dilation, and compatibility of modular-group representations. Each positive statement below preserves the extra input it requires.

The other reviewed routes were low-dimensional law acquisition, nonlinear coarse simulation, and quantum simulation/fault tolerance. Their surviving constructions either substantially overlap established methods or require an unresolved acquisition, conditioning, or scaling theorem. The accompanying ledger records all thirty directions and distinguishes author-reported derivations from focused internal review. Fresh conversation histories supplied separation of proposals; the investigators shared tools and a filesystem, so this was not statistical independence or external validation.

\section{Exact cost of compressing a unitary}
All norms in this section use the original dimension $n$, including matrices acting on a corner.
\begin{lemma}[Polar repair formula]
Let $P$ be an orthogonal projection, $Q=I-P$, $U\in U(n)$, and $A=QUQ|_{Q\mathbb C^n}$. Put $T=QU^*PUQ|_{Q\mathbb C^n}$. Then $0\leq T\leq I_Q$ and
\[
\inf_{V\in U(Q\mathbb C^n)}\|A-V\|_{2,n}^2
=\frac1n\tr\bigl(I_Q-\sqrt{I_Q-T}\bigr)^2.
\]
Consequently this squared distance lies between $\tr(T^2)/(4n)$ and $\tr(T^2)/n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$A^*A=I_Q-T$. If the singular values of $A$ are $s_j$, the nearest-unitary squared Frobenius distance is $\sum_j(1-s_j)^2$. Indeed, expand $\|A-V\|_F^2$ and maximize $\operatorname{Re}\tr(V^*A)$ using a singular-value decomposition; completing the polar partial isometry on its kernel attains the maximum $\sum_j s_j$. If $t_j$ are the eigenvalues of $T$, then $s_j=\sqrt{1-t_j}$. For $0\leq t\leq1$,
\[
\frac{t}{2}\leq1-\sqrt{1-t}=\frac{t}{1+\sqrt{1-t}}\leq t.
\]
Squaring and summing proves the bounds.
\end{proof}

This shows why a small rank loss cannot be treated as free. The controlling quantity is a fourth-power leakage expression, $\tr[(B^*B)^2]/n$ with $B=PUQ$, rather than rank alone.

\begin{proposition}[An exact amplification example]
For every integer $q\geq2$, there are matrices in dimension $n=q^4$ and an error $E$ with $\|E\|_{2,n}=\eps=q^{-2}$ such that deleting a fraction $\eps$ of the dimensions kills $E$ exactly, but the compressed unitary requires repair of size $\sqrt{\eps}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose orthogonal $k=q^2$ dimensional spaces $B,C$. Let $U$ swap $B,C$ and fix the remaining space, $E=q^{-1}P_B$, and $Q=I-P_B$. Then $EQ=0$ and $\|E\|_{2,n}=q^{-1}\sqrt{k/n}=q^{-2}$. On $Q\mathbb C^n$, $QUQ$ has exactly $k$ zero singular values and all other singular values equal to one. The preceding formula gives optimal repair distance $\sqrt{k/n}=q^{-1}$.
\end{proof}
This example disproves a norm-only compression lemma. By itself it says nothing about a particular group's stability.

\section{The obstruction persists with genuine group relations}
\begin{theorem}[Expansion forces a boundary cost]
Suppose an exact irreducible representation $\rho:\Gamma\to U(n)$ has a finite set $S$ and constant $\kappa>0$ such that every traceless $X$ satisfies
\[
\max_{s\in S}\|\rho(s)X\rho(s)^*-X\|_{2,n}\geq\kappa\|X\|_{2,n}.
\]
For every rank-$k$ projection $P$, with $0<r=k/n<1$ and $Q=I-P$, some $s\in S$ obeys
\[
\inf_{V\in U(Q\mathbb C^n)}\|Q\rho(s)Q-V\|_{2,n}
\geq\frac{\kappa^2}{4}(1-r)\sqrt r.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the assumed gap to $P-rI$, whose squared normalized norm is $r(1-r)$. For some $U=\rho(s)$,
$\|[U,P]\|_{2,n}^2\geq\kappa^2r(1-r)$.
The two off-diagonal blocks of a unitary have equal squared Frobenius norms; hence, for $T=QU^*PUQ$,
\[
\frac{\tr T}{n}=\|PUQ\|_{2,n}^2
=\frac12\|[U,P]\|_{2,n}^2
\geq\frac{\kappa^2}{2}r(1-r).
\]
Since $\rank T\leq k$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\tr(T^2)/n\geq(\tr T/n)^2/r$.
Insert this in the lower bound of Lemma 1 and take square roots.
\end{proof}
The hypothesis is explicit. Property (T) supplies a gap for irreducible finite-dimensional representations using a fixed Kazhdan set; unbounded irreducible dimensions are a separate requirement when taking a sequence.

To put the issue inside a finite presentation, add a redundant generator $z$ and relation $z=1$. Keep the original generators equal to $\rho(s)$ and set $U_z=e^{iaP}$, $0<a\leq1$. The only new defining-relator defect has size $2\sin(a/2)\sqrt r$. Deleting the support of this defect removes it but incurs the lower bound above on some original generator. Nevertheless, simply replacing $U_z$ by $I$ repairs the tuple. Thus this is a counterexample to the proposed \emph{method}, not to flexible stability.

An exact representation on an $(n-o(n))$-dimensional corner, close to the compressed original tuple, would give a strict same-dimensional repair after adding a trivial representation on the deleted space. The off-diagonal and deleted blocks contribute only $O(\sqrt{o(n)/n})$. Therefore deletion followed by exact corner closure cannot evade the known strict-stability obstruction. A successful flexible argument must genuinely use enlargement or another operation.

\section{A coefficient-action failure under normalization}
A second obstruction occurs before solving a cohomological equation. Take $\Gamma=C_2=\langle s\mid s^2=1\rangle$ and, for $n\geq2$, let
\[
U_n=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\pi/4},e^{-i\pi/4},1,\ldots,1).
\]
Then $\|U_n^2-I\|_{2,n}=2/\sqrt n\to0$, so these define asymptotic representations. But $X_n=\sqrt n E_{12}$ has normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm one, and
\[
\operatorname{Ad}(U_n)X_n=iX_n,
\qquad \operatorname{Ad}(U_n)^2X_n=-X_n.
\]
Consequently the induced conjugation operator on the full Hilbert ultraproduct of the spaces $(M_n,\|\cdot\|_{2,n})$ does not satisfy $s^2=1$. It is not a representation of $C_2$, so group-cohomology vanishing cannot be invoked on that space as though it were.

On sequences uniformly bounded in operator norm, an error $K_n\to I$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm satisfies
\[
\|K_nX_nK_n^*-X_n\|_{2,n}
\leq2\sup_n\|X_n\|_\op\,\|K_n-I\|_{2,n}\to0.
\]
The action is valid on the closed subspace generated by these sequences. This does not solve the problem: $\sqrt nE_{12}$ is orthogonal in the ultralimit to every such bounded sequence, because its pairing has modulus at most $\sup_n\|X_n\|_\op/\sqrt n$. Normalized defect vectors can concentrate in precisely this omitted component. Even the stable finite group $C_2$ exhibits the method failure.

\section{A constructive full-unitary update, with its debt exposed}
The next operation keeps the original space and applies complete unitary corrections. It avoids the compression error, at the price of an explicit low-rank remainder.
\begin{theorem}[Conditional update with rank debt]
Let $U_s\in U(n)$ and let supplied skew-adjoint corrections $b_s$ satisfy $\max_s\|b_s\|_{2,n}\leq C\eps$. For a relator word $w$ of length $L$, define
\[
F_b(t)=w((e^{tb_s}U_s)_s),\quad
D=F_b(0)-I,\quad \mathcal L_U(b)=F_b'(0).
\]
Assume the supplied linear residual $E=D+\mathcal L_U(b)$ satisfies $\|E\|_{2,n}\leq\eta$. For any $\tau,\sigma>0$ there is an exact decomposition
\[
F_b(1)-I=R_{\rm diffuse}+R_{\rm debt}
\]
with
\begin{align*}
\|R_{\rm diffuse}\|_{2,n}&\leq\eta+\tfrac12L^2\tau C\eps,\\
\rank(R_{\rm debt})/n&\leq2L|S|C^2\eps^2/\tau^2+\eta^2/\sigma^2,\\
\|R_{\rm debt}\|_\op&\leq2+\sigma+\tfrac12L^2\tau^2.
\end{align*}
Each corrected generator moves by at most $C\eps$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm and remains exactly unitary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Split $b_s=l_s+h_s$ by spectral cutoff of $|b_s|$ at $\tau$. Then $\|l_s\|_\op\leq\tau$, $\|l_s\|_{2,n}\leq C\eps$, and $k_s=\rank h_s\leq nC^2\eps^2/\tau^2$.
In the low-frequency path $F_l(t)$, each of the $L^2$ second-derivative terms contains two $l$ factors and otherwise unitary factors, including when a letter is inverted. Thus its Hilbert--Schmidt norm is at most $\tau C\eps$, and its operator norm at most $\tau^2$. Taylor's integral remainder $Q=\int_0^1(1-t)F_l''(t)\,dt$ obeys the corresponding bounds with factor $L^2/2$.

Therefore $F_l(1)-I=E-\mathcal L_U(h)+Q$. The rank of $\mathcal L_U(h)$ is at most the sum of $k_s$ over the letters of $w$. The commuting spectral decomposition of each $b_s$ also implies $\rank(e^{b_s}-e^{l_s})\leq k_s$. Telescoping the word shows that $F_b(1)-F_l(1)$ has at most that same summed rank. Hence
\[
F_b(1)-I=E+Q+Z,\qquad
\rank Z\leq2\sum_{\text{letters of }w}k_s.
\]
Finally split $E=E_{\rm lo}+E_{\rm hi}$ by singular values at $\sigma$. The low part has operator norm at most $\sigma$ and Hilbert--Schmidt norm at most $\eta$; the high part has rank at most $n\eta^2/\sigma^2$. Set $R_{\rm diffuse}=E_{\rm lo}+Q$ and $R_{\rm debt}=Z+E_{\rm hi}$. Its operator bound follows by subtracting the diffuse part from the difference of two unitaries. The generator movement bound follows from $|e^{ix}-1|\leq|x|$.
\end{proof}

With $\tau=\eps^\alpha$, $0<\alpha<1$, the nonlinear diffuse term is $O(\eps^{1+\alpha})$ and its rank debt is $O(\eps^{2-2\alpha})n$. This is \emph{not} superlinear total error: the bounded debt may have Hilbert--Schmidt size $O(\eps^{1-\alpha})$, larger than $\eps$. The existence of the supplied $b_s$ and the removal of the compatible debt by charged enlargement are both unproved. A small ordinary residual need not even lie in the tangent image; its admissibility cannot be assumed.

\section{Why low rank does not supply free dimension credit}
If a unitary on a larger space compresses exactly to an already unitary $U_s$ on the original space, the original space is invariant: equality of norms forces its off-diagonal block to vanish. Exact compression for every generator therefore cannot repair any nonzero relator defect. Generator edits are necessary.

Even allowing edits, a universal low-rank completion theorem is false. A standard obstruction uses $\mathrm{BS}(2,3)=\langle a,t\mid ta^2t^{-1}=a^3\rangle$. The word $v=[a,tat^{-1}]$ is nontrivial by Britton's lemma but trivial in every finite quotient. Indeed, if $a$ has finite order $d$, equality of the orders of $a^2,a^3$ forces $\gcd(d,6)=1$; the defining relation then makes $tat^{-1}$ a power of $a$.

Every finite-dimensional unitary representation also kills $v$, by residual finiteness of finitely generated linear groups in characteristic zero. Soficity of Baumslag--Solitar groups supplies permutation approximations with defining-relator rank $o(n)$ but $\|v(U)-I\|_{2,n}\to\sqrt2$. Cornulier gives the soficity input explicitly \cite{Cornulier}; the precise finite-residual and linear-group source interfaces are recorded in the adversarial supplement.

For completeness, the resulting dimension accounting is elementary. Suppose exact enlarged generators $V_s$ act on dimension $n+m$, and their upper-left compressions differ from $U_s$ by at most $\delta$ in norm normalized by $n$. Padding $U_s$ by $I_m$ gives
\[
\|U_s\oplus I_m-V_s\|_{2,n}^2\leq\delta^2+6m/n:
\]
the two cross blocks contribute at most $2m/n$ and the lower-right block at most $4m/n$. Telescoping the length-eight word $v$ yields
\[
\sqrt2-o(1)\leq8\sqrt{\delta^2+6m/n}.
\]
Thus $m=o(n)$ forces $\delta\geq1/(4\sqrt2)-o(1)$. This rules out generic completion of $o(n)$ rank debt, not completion for a specially justified high-rank group class. The qualitative obstruction is established mathematics, not a novelty claim of this campaign.

\section{What remains unproved and what was checked}
The decisive missing result is a presentation-specific construction of suitable corrections or exact dilations, together with a compatible low-rank-debt completion theorem permitting genuine $o(n)$ enlargement and vanishing generator edits. No such result was obtained for a consequential infinite group. Uniform edge-intertwiner gaps also fail in the unrestricted modular gluing problem; the independent consequence report supplies a fixed two-dimensional example. This rejects that shortcut, not the gluing question itself.

A separate compatibility check matters: a central extension $1\to\mathbb Z\to\widetilde\Gamma\to\Gamma\to1$ with finite abelianization of $\widetilde\Gamma$ forces $H^2(\Gamma;\mathbb R)\ne0$. The five-term sequence contains $0=H^1(\widetilde\Gamma;\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb R)\to H^2(\Gamma;\mathbb R)$, so its transgression is injective. One cannot directly apply a vanishing-$H^2$ hypothesis to this base group.

The current predecessor comparison includes strict-stability obstructions \cite{BL}, unnormalized cohomological repair \cite{DGLT}, and the conditional arithmetic consequences and weaker stability results in the June 2026 version of \cite{DV}. A September 2026 preprint already claims non-hyperlinear groups by another route \cite{ALT}; that is a source-reported result, not independently certified here. Merely obtaining the existence conclusion would therefore not establish this campaign's originality.

\textbf{Verification.} The displayed elementary arguments were reconstructed and challenged internally. The executable supplement checks four exact rational excision cases, exact two-state stationary probabilities, thirteen seeded polar-formula cases, six finite Weyl-tuple gap cases, and three concentrated-vector examples. These diagnostics passed. They are consistency checks, not proofs of the infinite-group target, formal proof-assistant verification, or external certification. The supplied-input conditional theorem has no end-to-end runtime guarantee and demonstrates no practical computational advantage.

\textbf{Actual significance.} The campaign established useful method boundaries and a conditional matrix update. It did not remove a foundational barrier. No result is supported as an 8--10/10 discovery. The full archive preserves substantive work and exact next gates without treating them as mission completion.
