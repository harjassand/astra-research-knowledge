Cycle 09 raw-power candidate: bounded primary-literature comparison

Scope and verdict

This is a bounded primary-source check of the supplied candidate, not a proof audit. The candidate is recorded as a premise only:

For finite-dimensional B with faithful state sigma and a fixed CPTP broadcaster B:B -> B1 B2, define b_B(rho)=max_j (1/2)||rho-(id_R tensor B_j)(rho)||_1 and e_Psi(rho)=(1/2)||rho-(id_R tensor Psi)(rho)||_1. The proposed conclusion is that one pure sigma-Petz canonical EB map Psi, depending only on (B,sigma), works for every finite reference R and every rho_RB with rho_B=sigma, with e_Psi(rho)^3 <= K I(R:B)_rho sqrt(b_B(rho)), K=240(2+sqrt(2))/pi. Thus an I<=C family would have common-map error at most (KC)^(1/3)b^(1/6), equivalently squared error at most (KC)^(2/3)b^(1/3). The internal candidate, constant, and proof status were not independently checked here.

No exact primary theorem matching this interface surfaced in this bounded pass. The closest prior results give (i) an exact, qualitative common-channel endpoint for state sets, (ii) an EB approximation for channels with many exact symmetric outputs whose error depends on local dimension/output count, and (iii) state-specific or prior-average fidelity optimizations for approximate local broadcasting. None supplies the proposed dimension-free, information-weighted trace bound for one EB map over all extensions of a fixed faithful marginal. This is a source comparison, not a novelty or priority finding.

1. Exact common-channel endpoint: no broadcasting of noncommuting states

Barnum, Caves, Fuchs, Jozsa, and Schumacher, “Noncommuting Mixed States Cannot Be Broadcast,” PRL 76 (1996), 2818–2821, arXiv:quant-ph/9511010v1, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.76.2818. In the two-state formulation (PDF p. 3, Eq. (1) and following statement; converse proof pp. 8–9), a single physical channel that reproduces both input states as both output marginals exists iff the two states commute. The theorem concerns a shared channel for a set; a finite set is therefore broadcastable exactly when its members commute pairwise.

This is the exact endpoint most relevant to the candidate's flagged extensions: classical-reference extensions encode ensembles of B states whose average is sigma, and a zero-error common broadcaster must broadcast every such ensemble with the same channel. The old theorem gives a qualitative commutativity obstruction, not a stability modulus when the marginal errors are positive. It has no mutual-information budget and gives no dimension-free approximation to one EB map.

For correlations, Piani, Horodecki, and Horodecki, “No-local-broadcasting theorem for quantum correlations,” PRL 100 (2008), 090502, arXiv:0707.0848v2, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.090502, prove that a bipartite state can be locally broadcast on both parties only when it is classical-classical (Theorem 3, PDF p. 5). Piani’s later one-sided statement is more directly aligned: “Local Broadcasting of Quantum Correlations,” chapter in *Lectures on General Quantum Correlations and Their Applications* (2017), arXiv:1608.02650v1, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-53412-1_9, Theorem 5 (PDF p. 6) says a fixed rho_AB can be broadcast locally on B iff rho_AB=sum_j p_j rho_A^j tensor |b_j><b_j|_B. These are per-state exact characterizations. They do not give one common map with a quantitative error bound over all rho_RB sharing sigma.

2. Closest channel-level common EB approximation: finite shareability/de Finetti

The closest result to a single EB map that is uniform over arbitrary reference extensions is Piani, “Hierarchy of efficiently computable and faithful lower bounds to quantum discord,” PRL 117 (2016), 080401, arXiv:1501.06855v1, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.117.080401. Its Lemma 1 (PDF p. 3, Eq. (4)) states: if a channel on B has a k-Bose-symmetric extension, then there is one EB channel Lambda_EB such that

  sup_{rho_AB} (1/2)|| (id_A tensor Lambda_sym)(rho_AB) - (id_A tensor Lambda_EB)(rho_AB) ||_1 <= dim(B)/k.

The supremum is over all finite reference/input states; the EB map is chosen for the extended channel, not separately for rho. This is a genuine same-map, reference-uniform conclusion. But the extension must have output supported in the fully symmetric subspace (“Bose-symmetric”), the extension count k is the resource, and the bound carries dim(B)/k. At k=2 it gives no dimension-free small error as dimension grows. It has no premise involving a small two-marginal copy error b_B or I(R:B).

The underlying finite-output version is Chiribella, “On quantum estimation, quantum cloning and finite quantum de Finetti theorems,” arXiv:1010.1875v4 (1 Nov 2010), LNCS 6519 (2011), pp. 9–25, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-18073-6_2. Theorem 5 (PDF pp. 11–12) says a permutation-invariant M-output broadcast channel admits a measure-and-prepare comparison channel whose k-output restriction is diamond-close, with the symmetric-subspace bound 2 d k/M and, for general permutation-invariant outputs, 2 d^2 k/M. Taking k=1 gives a common EB approximation to the one-output marginal, but it becomes useful only when M grows relative to d (or d^2). A two-output approximate broadcaster does not meet that large-M premise. This theorem allows permutation-invariant output, whereas Piani’s sharper channel lemma asks for support in the fully symmetric subspace.

These finite-shareability results establish an important but different tradeoff: many exact symmetric shares force a channel toward EB, at a dimension-dependent rate. They do not turn a small statewise marginal error for a fixed two-output channel into the candidate's information-weighted, common EB reconstruction.

3. Approximate local broadcasting and finite ensembles

Piani’s 2017 chapter also defines the state-specific unilocal broadcasting fidelity F^max_{B,1->2}(rho_AB) as an optimization over symmetric-output channels for that one rho (Eq. (35), PDF p. 8). Its EB fidelity F^EB_B(rho) is optimized separately for that same state (Eq. (33)); recoverability gives D(A|B)_rho >= -2 log_2 F^EB_B(rho), and F^max >= F^EB, hence D(A|B)_rho >= -2 log_2 F^max (Eqs. (34)–(35)). This direction says poor local broadcast fidelity witnesses discord. It does not upper-bound discord from small broadcast error, and it does not select an EB map common across different states or references. The chapter further notes that the average mutual-information loss over n outputs converges to discord as n grows (Eq. (36) and text on PDF p. 9); it supplies no finite-n dimension-free trace estimate of the candidate form.

Xie, Fang, Wang, and Duan, “Approximate broadcasting of quantum correlations,” PRA 96 (2017), 022302, arXiv:1705.06071v2 (8 Aug 2017), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.96.022302, gives an SDP for the optimal symmetric unilocal n-broadcasting fidelity of one bipartite state (Theorem 5, PDF p. 5) and for one common channel broadcasting a finite ensemble eta={p_i,rho_i} under prior-weighted average fidelity (Theorem 12, PDF p. 8, Eq. (26)–(29)). The second result shares a channel across the finite input set, but its objective is an average, not a worst-case bound; it has no arbitrary quantum reference, no fixed-marginal extension class, and no EB reconstruction conclusion or dimension-free rate in terms of I and b.

Exact interface gap

The decisive bridge not supplied by the located literature is: from one raw two-output CPTP channel B whose two marginal actions are uniformly close to identity on the extension family {rho_RB: rho_B=sigma, I(R:B)_rho<=C}, produce one EB map Lambda depending only on (B,sigma), with a dimension- and reference-independent worst-case trace error tending to zero at a stated rate in (C,b). Existing exact results address b=0/commutativity; de Finetti results trade many exact symmetric outputs for dimension-dependent error; approximate local-broadcast results optimize a state or a finite prior average. None matches all of the candidate’s raw-channel, fixed-faithful-marginal, all-reference, single-map, trace-norm, and information-budget quantifiers.

If the internal theorem survives independent proof review, its substantive consequence relative to this source set would be a dimension-free quantitative stability theorem for the exact no-broadcast/classicality boundary, uniform over all quantum references and obtained from only two raw output marginals. This is conditional significance, not an assertion of priority or novelty. No further search was conducted.
