# A single-vector alternative to the weighted orthogonal landscape

7 October 2026. r06 reported the core sphere observation immediately before a model-access interruption. Root independently reconstructed it and added the quantitative statement below. **Independent internal gate PASS:** `notes/equal_charge_audit/SPHERE_AXIS_ESCAPE_GATE.md`; no polynomial-bit acquisition theorem or novelty claim. The general weighted landscape on O(n) remains open, but it need not be the only route to coefficient-only axis acquisition.

## Promise and observable interface

The source interface is `notes/r16_proof_methods/WEIGHTED_ORTHOGONAL_LANDSCAPE.md`: expanded rational quartic coefficients and an acquired whitening metric, with hidden orthogonal O and hidden G positive definite. Assume n>=2 below; n=1 is the trivial already-acquired axis, handled separately. On the unit sphere the observable quartic is

    q(z)=m^T Gm, m=(Oz) entrywise squared.

No G, hidden axes or target commuting family is supplied. Evaluations, gradient and sphere Hessian can be computed from coefficients. Below set y=Oz only for the proof. Let mu=lambda_min(G)>0.

## All non-axis stationary points have ascent

For ||y||=1 write g=Gm and q=m^Tg. The tangent gradient is

    grad_S q=4 y entrywise multiplied by (g-q1).

At a stationary point, every active coordinate i with y_i!=0 therefore has g_i=q. For a unit tangent v supported on those active coordinates, the unit-speed great circle y(t)=y cos t+v sin t gives

    q''(0)=8(y circ v)^T G(y circ v)+4 sum_i v_i^2(g_i-q).

The second term vanishes. If at least two coordinates are active, choose

    v=(y_j e_i-y_i e_j)/sqrt(m_i+m_j).

Then v is unit and tangent, and

    q''(0)=8 m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j) (e_i-e_j)^T G(e_i-e_j)
           >=16 mu m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j)>0.             (1)

Every local maximum on the sphere must therefore be a hidden axis. Some hidden axes may be saddles or minima: for example G=[[1,2],[2,5]] is positive definite and the first axis has positive curvature toward the second. A method seeking a local maximum need not be able to choose which axis it finds.

Once an axis is acquired exactly, restriction to its orthogonal complement removes that hidden coordinate and keeps a positive-definite principal submatrix of G. Repeating exact local-maximum acquisition would recover a complete frame. This is a mathematical reduction, not a charged algorithm: finding the extrema, approximate deflation and exact reconstruction remain obligations.

## Quantitative approximate-stationary exclusion

Suppose ||grad_S q(y)||_2<=epsilon. Then |g_i-q|<=epsilon/(4sqrt(m_i)) for each active coordinate. If m_i>=m_j>=delta>0, the same tangent direction satisfies

    q''(0)>=16mu m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j)
               -epsilon [m_j/sqrt(m_i)+m_i/sqrt(m_j)]/(m_i+m_j)
           >=8mu delta-epsilon/sqrt(delta).        (2)

Thus if epsilon<=4mu delta^(3/2), any point with at least two squared coordinates >=delta has an ascent direction of curvature >=4mu delta. In particular, if the largest tangent Hessian eigenvalue is strictly less than 4mu delta and that gradient bound holds, the second-largest m_i is <delta. Hence some hidden axis e_i obeys

    min_sign ||y-sign*e_i||_2^2
       =2(1-sqrt(m_i))<=2(1-m_i)<2(n-1)delta.       (3)

The strict inequality prevents an equality-threshold ambiguity. All constants are explicit and conditional on the latent margin mu; the learner does not automatically receive or know it.

## What this changes and what it leaves open

The full O(n) weighted strict-saddle conjecture is unnecessary for this *alternative* qualitative deflation route. Standard optimization methods may use the observable sphere gradient/Hessian, but a polynomial-bit theorem still needs an input-acquired conditioning bound, finite precision saddle escape, accumulated approximate-deflation stability and exact rational factor-subspace recovery. A hidden mu in a mathematical estimate must not be silently treated as a supplied stopping threshold. No successful coefficient-only implementation is reported here.

The underlying fact is strict convexity of m^TGm on the probability simplex, pulled back through squared sphere coordinates. This is an elementary structural argument with likely broad prior; historical comparison to tensor/ICA hidden-convexity methods is pending. It is useful because it changes the next investigation, not because a new landscape theorem has been advertised.
