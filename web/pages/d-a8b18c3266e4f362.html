## Findings on the three initial reports

**c10_s01 — reciprocal Lyapunov function for two-layer catalysts.** I found no
algebraic error in the displayed all-state drift. The decisive boundary case
is correctly split: when the parent count `S=0`, there are no root deaths and
the root-immigration contribution is exactly `-c B/2`; when `S>=1`, the
reciprocal decrement and child-death term give the stated uniform negative
coefficient. The root weights absorb each child-birth term. The nonexplosion,
return, and moment bounds then follow from localized Dynkin inequalities.
The supplied checker replayed without writing to the peer directory: 1,548
states, 48 zero-catalyst states, and the reported `LV(0,60)=-27` and positive
exponential-witness ratio all reproduced. These checks do not establish the
infinite-state inequalities; the written derivation does.

The main limitation is scope and priority. For fixed rates this is exactly a
stochastic monomolecular child process in an autonomous stochastic environment:
the roots form the environment, child births have rates linear in roots, and
child deaths have rates linear in both environment and child counts. Cappelletti,
Pal Majumder, and Wiuf's primary paper gives this same root-catalysed birth /
environment-catalysed death template explicitly, proves ergodicity under
conditions that include this class, and characterizes the stationary law as a
Poisson mixture. Their displayed example has two independently produced and
degraded environment species, one catalysing child birth and the other child
death; their theorem also permits multiple monomolecular child species. This
substantially overlaps the fixed-rate recurrence and stationary-distribution
consequences of c10_s01, including its non-complex-balanced example. It does
not cover c10_s01's arbitrary predictable, history-dependent rate-control
quantifier, nor does it supply this exact rational certificate construction.
Those are the narrow remaining deltas; neither has cleared novelty review.
[Cappelletti et al., arXiv:1912.00401](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00401)

**c10_s02 — counterexamples to direct stochastic transfers.** The two
obstructions survive independent reconstruction. On `(a,0,1)`, the available
residual-factorial minimum is `(a-1)!`; the enabled jumps change its exponential
by ratios `1,a,1`, with rates `a,1,1`, so `L exp(F)/exp(F)=a-1` and
`LF=log(a)>0`. On `(0,n,0)`, the two enabled jumps preserve `F`. For a
globally coercive concave `W`, each coordinate-ray increment must be strictly
positive: concavity makes discrete secants nonincreasing, so a nonpositive
increment would contradict coercivity along that ray. Both enabled jumps from
`(0,n,0)` increase one of those coordinates, hence `LW>0`; the same argument
rules out a finite minimum of affine forms. The detailed-balance and component
identities were independently checked on 3,753 enabled edges, alongside 119
factorial-witness values and 120 zero-A/B-ray values. The global statements
are the symbolic ray arguments, not extrapolations from those finite checks.

The example is not a recurrence counterexample. Its communicating class and
conditioned product-Poisson invariant law are proved in the report, and the
positive stationary boundary mass at fixed volume rules out all-time strict
positivity, not finite-horizon safety or high-volume concentration. The
weakly-reversible deficiency-zero product-form result is established prior
art. No gap found in the scoped counterexample; its import is that deterministic
permanence and the named concave/factorial architectures do not automatically
transfer to stochastic pathwise positivity.

**c10_s03 — rational LP/Foster plus constructive boundary recovery.** I
replayed the three fixture functions without running the peer `__main__` block
or writing into its directory. The two claimed certificates and the
`NO_CERTIFICATE_IN_ADMITTED_FOSTER_CLASS` rejection reproduced; exact
diagnostics totaled 525 drift states, 3,459 rate-vertex/state pairs, 30 drain
words, and 30 production-dominance checks. The product-Poisson balanced-cross
example is a valid warning that this compiler's rejection is not a recurrence
failure. I found no direct soundness contradiction in the source-wise
endpoint constraints, square-completion bound, drain/production words, or
conditional hazard lower bound. The finite replay does not validate the
claimed worst-case bit exponent; that is the least independently audited
piece. The exponential recovery constant is also a computable bound, not a
practical physical-time guarantee.

Its admitted class is deliberately narrower than its result language might
suggest: it requires weak reversibility, unary drains, support-production
closure, and its normalized one-step `D` inequalities. Its two-layer-catalytic
peers generally fail the unary-drain test for children. Its detailed-balanced
balanced-cross example fails only this one-step certificate. Published
linear/quadratic Foster optimization and support-exhaustion already supply
major pieces of the method; the exact rational packaging and quantitative
path-probability bounds need theorem-by-theorem comparison. Relevant primary
sources include [Gupta, Briat & Khammash (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003669),
[Gupta & Khammash (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2879), and
[Anderson & Kim (2018)](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.11263).

## Strongest post-exposure continuation: coupled competition under control

After freezing the independent `INITIAL`, I derived a distinct coupled family
that transfers the same exponential recovery/truncation interface to a
non-weakly-reversible population model. Fix `d` species and a survivor map
`s(i,j) in {i,j}` for every unordered pair `i<j`. Reactions are

```text
0       -> A_i                 intensity lambda_i(t) in [1,2]
2 A_i  -> A_i                 intensity mu_ii(t) binom(x_i,2), mu_ii(t) in [1,4]
A_i+A_j -> A_s(i,j)           intensity mu_ij(t) x_i x_j, mu_ij(t) in [1,4]
```

Every pairwise event removes exactly one individual. The controls may depend
predictably on the full past and may be randomized. With `n=sum_i x_i` and
`W(x)=2^n`, each birth contributes `lambda_i` to `LW/W`, and every pair event
contributes `-mu/2` times its unordered-pair propensity. Since

```text
sum_i binom(x_i,2) + sum_{i<j} x_i x_j = binom(n,2),
```

the common controlled generator satisfies

```text
LW/W <= 2d - (1/2) binom(n,2).
```

Let `n_*` be the least integer with the right side at most `-1`. Outside
`C={n<n_*}`, `LW<=-2^n`, so stopped Dynkin gives
`E_x tau_C <= 2^(n-n_*)`. A finite exact global bound is

```text
B_d = max_{0<=m<n_*} 2^m [2d - (1/2) binom(m,2)],
```

because all later drift upper bounds are negative. Stopped Dynkin then gives
`sup_policy P_x(tau_N<=T) <= (2^n+B_d T)/2^N`. Coupling the full process to a
finite model with an absorbing exit state yields the same additive error for
every `[0,1]` finite-horizon path payoff, including optimal values over all
allowed policies. The finite-state Bellman Hamiltonian is affine in each
reaction rate, so endpoint actions suffice. The cutoff theorem does not itself
implement or certify a numerical HJB solver.

This network is strongly endotactic. For a direction `w` with
`M=max_i w_i>0`, a maximizing complex is `2 A_k` for `w_k=M`; its pair-loss
reaction has strictly negative projection `-M`, and every maximizing mixed
pair has both coordinates at `M` and also loses one `M`-coordinate. If `M=0`
and `w` is nonzero, the zero complex is maximizing; all maximizing pair edges
have projection zero, every birth edge has projection at most zero, and a
birth of a species with `w_i<0` is strictly inward. The graph is not weakly
reversible. Thus this is outside c10_s03's admission condition while still
having an explicit controlled certificate.

For `d=2`, `n_*=5` and the exact global bound is `B_2=20`. Starting at `(1,1)`,
with horizon `T=100` and requested error `epsilon=0.01`, the minimal cutoff is
`N=18`: the exit bound is `2004/2^18 = 501/65536 < 0.01`, while `N=17` gives
`501/32768 > 0.01`. The finite model has 171 non-sink states and a conservative
maximum total reaction intensity of 548 per time unit. This gives a concrete
all-policy finite-horizon computation problem with a certified state/error
budget, not just a recurrence statement. For fixed `d`, state count is
`binom(N-1+d,d)=O(N^d)` with `N=O(log(T/epsilon))` once the network and initial
state are fixed.

`sol_audit_checks.py` verified the coupled generator inequality at 44,484
exact independent rate-endpoint/state fixtures across `d=1,2,3`; it also
replayed the three Sol checkers and independently checked the c10_s02 witness.
The symbolic pair-count identity and stopped-Dynkin argument, not those finite
fixtures, prove the unbounded-state statements. There is no CTMDP/HJB solver,
stationary-tail computation, sampled trajectory, or physical cost model in
this continuation. The exact interface, rational rates, 171-state cutoff,
and rate cap are now reviewable inputs to a future solver run.

The surrounding computational ingredients have substantial prior art:
Munsky–Khammash's finite-state projection method gives finite-time truncation
certificates based on sink mass; Salamati–Soudjani–Majumdar give Lyapunov-based
time-bounded reachability and CTMDP policy synthesis; Baumann–Sandmann give
Foster-based truncation errors for long-run averages. This report's narrow
consequence is an explicit all-predictable-policy count cutoff for the
specified coupled family. It is not a new generic FSP or CTMDP theorem, and the
control application has not been externally validated.
[Munsky & Khammash (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2145882),
[Salamati et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06112),
[Baumann & Sandmann (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1239/jap/1445543835),
[Xu (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05340),
[Soloveichik (2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.1030).
