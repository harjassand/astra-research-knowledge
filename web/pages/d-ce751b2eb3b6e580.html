EXACT SU(3) CONJUGATION SPECTRUM FOR A REGULAR HIGHEST WEIGHT
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact finite decomposition and projector profile, derived by the
GL(3) Littlewood-Richardson rule. No asymptotic exponent is claimed.

PROBLEM AND NORMALIZATION

Let a,b be positive integers, S=a+b, and let V_(a,b) be the irreducible
SU(3) representation with Dynkin labels (a,b). Its dimension is

  d_{a,b}=dim V_(a,b)=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2.

The dual is V_(b,a), so

  End(V_(a,b)) ~= V_(a,b) tensor V_(b,a).                       (1)

Choose Hermitian generators t_A of su(3), A=1,...,8, normalized by
Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2, and let

  L(X)=sum_A [t_A,[t_A,X]],
  E(X)=<X,LX>_HS=sum_A ||[t_A,X]||_HS^2.                        (2)

In this convention the Casimir on Dynkin label (mu,nu) is

  C_2(mu,nu)=(mu^2+mu*nu+nu^2+3mu+3nu)/3.                     (3)

For example C_2(1,0)=4/3 and C_2(1,1)=3. The eigenvalue of L on every
copy of V_(mu,nu) in (1) is C_2(mu,nu).

1. EXACT MULTIPLICITY FORMULA

Put S=a+b. For output labels mu,nu>=0, define

  q=(mu+2nu)/3.

If q is not an integer, set m_{mu,nu}(a,b)=0. If it is an integer, define

  t=S-q,
  r_1=mu+nu-q=(2mu+nu)/3,
  r_2=a+nu-q.

If t<0, r_1>S, or r_2<0, set m_{mu,nu}(a,b)=0. Otherwise put

  L_{mu,nu}=max(0,q-b,nu-q),
  U_{mu,nu}=min(r_1,r_2,nu,a,q),

and

  m_{mu,nu}(a,b)=max(0,U_{mu,nu}-L_{mu,nu}+1).                 (4)

All quantities in (4) are integers on the stated support. In particular
the output set is finite: q<=S, or equivalently mu+2nu<=3S. The support
conditions t>=0 and r_2>=0 say that the output partition below contains
the first input partition; r_1<=S is the content constraint that its first
skew row can be filled by the available S symbols 1.

Thus the exact decomposition, including multiplicities, is

  End(V_(a,b)) ~= direct_sum_{mu,nu>=0}
                      m_{mu,nu}(a,b) V_(mu,nu).                (5)

The sum is over the finite support from (4). In particular, multiplicity
is not generally one: for every a,b>0, m_{1,1}(a,b)=2.

2. SELF-CONTAINED LITTLEWOOD-RICHARDSON DERIVATION

Use the GL(3) partitions

  lambda=(S,b,0),       lambda^*=(S,a,0).

Their restrictions to SU(3) have highest weights (a,b) and (b,a),
respectively. The product of their GL(3) characters is

  s_lambda s_{lambda^*}=sum_{Omega} c_{lambda,lambda^*}^{Omega}s_Omega,

where c is the Littlewood-Richardson coefficient. Every Omega has size
|lambda|+|lambda^*|=3S and at most three rows. An SU(3) output label
(mu,nu) has the unique GL(3) lift of this size

  Omega=(mu+nu+t,nu+t,t),   t=S-(mu+2nu)/3.                    (6)

The integrality of t is exactly the triality condition mu+2nu=0 mod 3;
nonnegative t is necessary for Omega to be a partition. Because the total
size is fixed at 3S, distinct GL(3) partitions in the product cannot
collapse to the same SU(3) highest weight after restriction. Therefore the
SU(3) multiplicity is exactly c_{lambda,lambda^*}^{Omega}.

The skew shape Omega/lambda has row lengths

  r_1=Omega_1-S=mu+nu-q,
  r_2=Omega_2-b=a+nu-q,
  r_3=Omega_3=t.                                               (7)

The LR content lambda^*=(S,a,0) means S entries 1 and a entries 2, with
no entries 3. Rows are weakly increasing, hence each row consists of 1s
followed by 2s. The LR reading word reads each row right-to-left, from top
to bottom. The first row must therefore consist entirely of 1s; any 2 in
it would make the first letter violate the ballot condition.

Let u be the number of 2s in row 2. The number in row 3 is then a-u.
Each choice of integer u determines at most one tableau, since the 2s
occupy the right ends of their rows. The tableau conditions give the
following interval constraints.

Lower bounds:

  u>=0,
  u>=a-r_3=q-b,                  (row 3 has enough boxes),
  u>=Omega_2-S=nu-q.             (row 1/row 2 column strictness).

Upper bounds:

  u<=r_2,                         (row 2 has enough boxes),
  u<=r_1,                         (ballot condition after row 2),
  u<=r_1+r_2-a=q,                 (ballot condition in row 3),
  u<=Omega_2-Omega_3=nu,          (row 2/row 3 column strictness),
  u<=a,                           (nonnegative row 3 content),
  u<=a-max(0,r_3-b)=min(a,q),     (row 3 2s cover its overlap).

The lower endpoint is L_{mu,nu} and the upper endpoint is U_{mu,nu} in
(4). The LR coefficient is the number of integers in this interval, which
is precisely (4). This proves (5) and the multiplicity formula without
appealing to an SU(3) multiplicity mnemonic.

3. EXACT CASIMIRS, MULTIPLICITIES, AND PROJECTOR DIMENSIONS

For each supported label, the isotypic projector has rank

  m_{mu,nu}(a,b) dim V_(mu,nu),
  dim V_(mu,nu)=(mu+1)(nu+1)(mu+nu+2)/2,                         (8)

and Casimir eigenvalue (3). If different labels have the same value of
C_2, their dimensions are added in the same spectral projector. Thus the
exact cumulative projector dimension is

  D_{a,b}(T)=sum_{mu,nu>=0} m_{mu,nu}(a,b)
                (mu+1)(nu+1)(mu+nu+2)/2
                * 1{mu^2+mu*nu+nu^2+3mu+3nu <= 3T},             (9)

where only labels satisfying the support conditions in (4) are included.
The factor 3 in the indicator clears the denominator in (3). In
particular D(0)=1, D(T)=d_{a,b}^2 once T>=S(S+2), and

  max supported Casimir = C_2(S,S)=S(S+2).                     (10)

The top label (S,S) occurs once by (4). It is the tensor-product highest
weight; every other constituent is dominated by it, and C_2 strictly
increases along strict dominance between dominant SU(3) weights.

For rank budget 1<=Q<=d_{a,b}, the exact rank-spectral lower profile is

  Phi_{a,b}(Q)=integral_0^{S(S+2)}
                  [1-Q D_{a,b}(T)/d_{a,b}]_+ dT.              (11)

Every X with ||X||_HS=1 and rank(X)<=Q obeys E(X)>=Phi_{a,b}(Q). This is
the partial-trace/Schmidt-rank bound: for the projector Pi_T onto Casimir
<=T, Schur's lemma gives Tr_2(Pi_T)=D(T) I/d_{a,b}; vectorized X has
Schmidt rank at most Q, so its low-band mass is at most QD(T)/d_{a,b}.
Integrating the complementary mass over T gives (11). No equality of this
lower profile with the optimization infimum is asserted.

For an exact finite sum, sort the distinct supported Casimirs as

  0=theta_0<theta_1<...<theta_J=S(S+2),
  D_j=D_{a,b}(theta_j).

Then

  Phi_{a,b}(Q)=sum_{j=0}^{J-1}(theta_{j+1}-theta_j)
                         [1-QD_j/d_{a,b}]_+.                  (12)

The exact last positive interval is

  j_+(a,b,Q)=max({j in {0,...,J-1}: QD_j<d_{a,b}} union {-1}).

Terms j>j_+ vanish; equality QD_j=d_{a,b} contributes zero. Formulae
(9)-(12) preserve Casimir collisions and multiplicity. They give a finite
cutoff/profile for every a,b,Q; no asymptotic counting approximation is
used.

4. SMALL EXACT CHECKS

(a,b)=(1,1): d=8 and

  8 tensor 8 = (0,0) + 2(1,1) + (3,0) + (0,3) + (2,2).

The dimensions sum as 1+16+10+10+27=64. Their Casimirs are respectively
0,3,6,6,8, so D(T) is 1,17,37,64 on [0,3), [3,6), [6,8), [8,infty).
The adjoint occurs twice, in agreement with (4).

(a,b)=(1,2): d=15 and

  15 tensor 15bar = (0,0)+2(1,1)+(3,0)+(0,3)
                 +2(2,2)+(1,4)+(4,1)+(3,3).

The dimension sum is 1+16+20+54+70+64=225. The distinct Casimirs are
0,3,6,8,12,15; D(T) on the successive half-open intervals is
1,17,37,91,161,225.

(a,b)=(2,2): d=27. Grouping equal conjugate pairs, the decomposition is

  (0,0) + 2(1,1) + (3,0)+(0,3) + 3(2,2)
  +2(4,1)+2(1,4) +2(3,3)
  +(6,0)+(0,6)+(5,2)+(2,5)+(4,4).

The dimension sum by increasing Casimir 0,3,6,8,12,15,18,20,24 is
1+16+20+81+140+128+56+162+125=729=27^2. This check includes
multiplicities greater than two and the top eigenvalue S(S+2)=24.

An exact-integer enumeration of formula (4) for 1<=a,b<=8 also verifies
the dimension identity

  sum_{mu,nu} m_{mu,nu}(a,b) dim V_(mu,nu)=d_{a,b}^2

for every tested pair. This is a finite consistency diagnostic, not a proof;
the proof is the tableau count above.

5. CLAIM BOUNDARY

Equations (4)-(12) are exact for each regular pair a,b>0 in the stated
normalization. They do not imply a Weyl-law exponent for a,b->infinity.
Any such asymptotic requires a separate lattice-point analysis of the
support/multiplicity function in (4), including the quadratic Casimir
cutoff and the regime of a/b. That counting problem is left open here.

The only imported combinatorial theorem is the classical GL(3)
Littlewood-Richardson rule; its complete specialization to this product is
expanded in Section 2. For background on polyhedral descriptions of tensor
product multiplicities see Berenstein-Zelevinsky, “Tensor product
multiplicities, canonical bases and totally positive varieties,”
arXiv:math/9912012. No priority or novelty claim is made.
