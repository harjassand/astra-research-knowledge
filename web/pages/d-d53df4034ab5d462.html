# What linear algebra can and cannot acquire from the hidden quartic

2026-10-07. Our deductions below; historical novelty is UNKNOWN. Exact finite fixtures accompany proofs, not replace them. Root input is expanded rational p(x)=( (Lx)^2-a )^T Q( (Lx)^2-a ), Q positive definite, a positive, Qa positive, L rational invertible. After the observable quadratic metric is whitened, its quartic is h(z)=((Oz)^2)^T G((Oz)^2), with hidden orthogonal O and G positive definite. The following arguments use y=Oz only for analysis.

## Direct infinitesimal polynomial symmetry is always zero

Let h(y)=(y^2)^T G(y^2), and define its full linear stabilizer Lie algebra by matrices A with Dh(y)[Ay] identically zero. This is the usual linear system in the coefficients of A; it can be computed without hidden axes.

We have partial_i h=4 y_i sum_k G_ik y_k^2. The coefficient of y_i^4 in Dh[Ay] is 4 G_ii A_ii, so A_ii=0. For distinct i,j the coefficients of y_i^3 y_j and y_i y_j^3 are respectively

    4(G_ii A_ij + G_ij A_ji),
    4(G_ij A_ij + G_jj A_ji).

The principal 2 by 2 matrix with entries G_ii,G_ij,G_jj is positive definite, hence invertible. Therefore A_ij=A_ji=0. This proves the whole GL_n Lie algebra is zero, for every promised G, not just generic G. An invertible coordinate change conjugates the algebra. The leading homogeneous component also shows the full inhomogeneous p has zero linear stabilizer Lie algebra.

This rules out extracting axes from that direct symmetry Lie algebra. It does not rule out discrete symmetries, stabilizers of derived polynomial spaces, ideals, tensor algebras, or nonlinear vector fields. Those have different interfaces. In particular the Darboux/extactic proposal pursued by proof_linear is not excluded.

## Hessian commutants recover exactly the coupling blocks

The Hessian polynomial is

    H_h(y)=4 diag(G m)+8 diag(y) G diag(y), m=y^2.

Its coefficient at y_k^2 is the diagonal matrix

    4 diag(G[:,k]+2 G_kk e_k).

These n matrices span all diagonal matrices: their diagonal vectors are the columns of B=G+2diag(G), which is positive definite and invertible. Its coefficient at y_i y_j, i<j, is

    8 G_ij (E_ij+E_ji).

Consequently, let Gamma be the graph with edge ij iff G_ij is nonzero. A matrix commuting with every Hessian coefficient must commute with every diagonal matrix and is therefore diagonal itself. Commuting with the edge coefficient forces its ith and jth diagonal entries equal. Conversely a diagonal matrix constant on every connected component commutes with every coefficient. Thus

    Comm(span{H_h(y): y in R^n})
      = {diag(c): c is constant on each connected component of Gamma}.

The associative algebra generated by the Hessian coefficients is the direct sum of full real matrix algebras on those components. To see this, the algebra contains every E_ii; edge generators and E_ii give E_ij, and products along paths give every within-component matrix unit. No coefficient connects components.

After whitening, orthogonal coordinate changes conjugate the Hessian coefficient space. Hence its commutant can expose hidden disconnected blocks by linear algebra. If Gamma is connected it contains only scalar matrices and exposes no axes. If all offdiagonal G_ij are nonzero, the Hessian coefficient span itself is all Sym_n. Only in the diagonal case does this elementary commutant recover the full axis algebra. Raw Hessians before whitening transform by congruence; do not silently treat them as conjugate operators without the metric.

## The coupled sphere problem is genuinely outside a separable interface

The 2014/2016 hidden-convexity papers below assume a sum of univariate functions of hidden coordinates. A restricted coupled family does reduce to that form on the sphere: if G=diag(d)+1c^T+c1^T, then for sum m_i=1,

    m^T Gm = sum_i d_i m_i^2 + 2 sum_i c_i m_i.

Not every G has this form. More strongly, take n=4 and suppose m^T Gm=sum_i f_i(m_i) on the simplex interior, with C^2 functions f_i. Eliminate m_4=1-m_1-m_2-m_3. For any distinct i,j in {1,2,3}, its mixed second derivative equals both

    2(G_ij-G_i4-G_j4+G_44) and f_4''(m_4).

Comparing pairs (1,2) and (1,3) forces G_12+G_34=G_13+G_24. Failure of this necessary identity rules out even arbitrary C^2 separable functions in these same hidden axes on the sphere, not only quartic univariate summands. An even coordinate representation sum_i g_i(y_i) would give such f_i(m_i)=g_i(sqrt(m_i)) on the positive orthant.

An exact original-promise fixture is

    G=2I+J/4+(E_12+E_21)/8,
    a=(32,32,34,34)/101, Q=G, L=I, Qa=1.

G is positive definite, a positive, and the acquired metric is I. Its four-point difference is 1/8; its Hessian span has dimension10, commutant dimension1, and linear stabilizer Lie algebra dimension0. This does not prove there is no alternative representation in a different axis frame; it proves the usual same-frame separable theorem cannot be transferred simply by renaming terms.
