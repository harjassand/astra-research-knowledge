STATUS UPDATE

The exact same-energy/same-helicity counterexample in v1 has a further constraint in its 2-D closed model: the mean-square magnetic flux function (anastrophy), a Casimir absent from generic 3-D MHD. Its approximate conservation makes the fast initial rate energetically transient for the chosen lowest-wavenumber background. This gives a more informative two-part result: singular current concentration can make an instantaneous resistive flux-transfer rate independent of eta_m, while the ideal Casimir budget independently caps the amount of magnetic energy accessible over a fixed time. The ingredients (2-D selective decay, anastrophy, coarea/isoperimetric bounds) are known mathematical tools; no novelty or priority claim is made for this synthesis.

1. EXACT 2-D FLUX-FUNCTION CASIMIRS

Use the v1 2-D periodic, zero-guide-field model with B=(partial_y A,-partial_x A,0), div u=0, and periodic gauge fixed by mean(A)=0. The induction equation is equivalent to

  partial_t A + u dot grad A = eta_m Delta A.                 (9)

For any F in C^2 on the range of A, define C_F=integral F(A) d^2x per unit z-length. Periodicity and incompressibility give

  dC_F/dt
    = integral F'(A)(-u dot grad A + eta_m Delta A) d^2x
    = -eta_m integral F''(A)|grad A|^2 d^2x.                 (10)

In ideal MHD, all C_F are conserved; equivalently, the area-distribution (pushforward measure) of A is preserved by the area-preserving flow. In resistive MHD, for ||F''||_infinity<=K,

  |C_F(t)-C_F(0)|
    <= 2 mu_0 eta_m K integral_0^t E_{B_perp}(s) ds
    <= 2 mu_0 eta_m K t E_tot(0),                             (11)

for unforced, closed, incompressible MHD, where E_{B_perp} is the in-plane magnetic energy. This is O(eta_m) at fixed t under a uniform total-energy bound. It is stronger in this 2-D setting than the O(sqrt(eta_m)) helicity estimate in v1, but it depends on translational symmetry and does not transfer to generic 3-D turbulence.

Take F(A)=A^2/2 (anastrophy A_2). Then the identity is exact:

  dA_2/dt = -eta_m integral |grad A|^2 d^2x
           = -2 mu_0 eta_m E_{B_perp}.                       (12)

The lowest nonzero Fourier wavenumber is k_1=2 pi/L. Since mean(A)=0, Poincare's inequality gives

  E_{B_perp}(t) = (1/(2 mu_0)) integral |grad A|^2
                >= k_1^2 A_2(t)/mu_0.                        (13)

Combining (12) with the energy bound yields, for fixed T,

  E_{B_perp}(t)
    >= (k_1^2/mu_0) [ A_2(0)-2 mu_0 eta_m T E_tot(0) ]_+,
       0<=t<=T.                                               (14)

Therefore

  E_{B_perp}(0)-E_{B_perp}(t)
    <= E_{B_perp}(0)-k_1^2 A_2(0)/mu_0
       +2 k_1^2 eta_m T E_tot(0),                             (15)

with the obvious nonnegative-energy truncation if the bracket in (14) is negative. In the eta_m->0 limit, the maximum magnetic energy available in this 2-D closed model is no greater than E_{B_perp}(0)-k_1^2 A_2(0)/mu_0. The floor may be higher because additional ideal topology is not captured by A_2.

The ratio

  R_A = E_{B_perp}(0) / (k_1^2 A_2(0)/mu_0) >= 1

is an exact initial-data indicator of the maximum energy fraction that can be released under this constraint: the limiting upper bound on the released fraction of initial in-plane magnetic energy is 1-1/R_A. It does not predict that the system reaches this floor or that reconnection is fast.

2. FULL FLUX-DISTRIBUTION VARIATIONAL FLOOR (CANDIDATE)

Equation (11), for every fixed smooth F, implies weak preservation of the flux-value distribution in the vanishing-resistivity limit. More precisely, fix A_0 and T, and consider smooth solutions A_eta with uniformly bounded total energy and mean-zero gauge. For any sequence eta_n->0 and any times t_n in [0,T], the H^1 bound on A_eta(t_n), Poincare's inequality, and compactness on the torus give a subsequence converging strongly in L^2 to A_*. The maximum principle bounds the common range of A. Equation (11) then implies integral F(A_*)=integral F(A_0) for every smooth F on that range, hence A_* and A_0 have the same value distribution. Weak H^1 lower semicontinuity gives a relaxed limiting energy floor

  E_eq(mu_0) := (1/(2 mu_0)) inf { integral |grad a|^2 d^2x :
                                      a in H^1, distribution(a)=mu_0 },

where mu_0 is the pushforward of area measure by A_0. This is a variational lower bound on any subsequential ideal-limit state. It only constrains the flux-value distribution; it discards the spatial arrangement and field-line connectivity, so it is an optimistic lower floor and does not establish dynamical attainability.

There is a direct isoperimetric lower bound for E_eq. Let m(s)=|{A_0>s}| and let I_Omega(a)=inf{Per(G): G subset Omega, |G|=a} be the isoperimetric profile of the 2-D domain. For regular s, coarea gives

  -m'(s) = integral_{A_0=s} |grad A_0|^{-1} d ell,
  integral |grad A_0|^2 d^2x
    = integral ds integral_{A_0=s}|grad A_0| d ell.

By Cauchy–Schwarz on each level set and Per({A_0>s})>=I_Omega(m(s)),

  integral |grad A_0|^2 d^2x
    >= integral_{min A_0}^{max A_0}
         I_Omega(m(s))^2/[-m'(s)] ds.                          (16)

Critical values can be handled by the standard coarea/BV limiting formulation; (16) is stated first for smooth A_0 with regular levels almost everywhere. The right side depends only on the initial value distribution and the domain. It is therefore an explicit, geometry-aware floor even when the total 3-D helicity is zero. Poincare's bound (13) may be stronger for some distributions; the valid combined floor is the maximum of the two bounds. Equation (16) need not be sharp because isoperimetric minimizers for separate areas need not form a nested family. A sharp evaluation of E_eq on the square torus and a proof of attainability are unresolved.

If this candidate survives further review, the closure-free available-energy ceiling in a 2-D vanishing-eta limit is

  E_{B_perp}(0) - E_eq(mu_0),

with a computable but possibly nonsharp replacement obtained from (16). This ceiling and the current-support condition (6) address different quantities: the distribution gives an energy budget; singular-current concentration is necessary for an O(1) instantaneous rate. Neither alone, nor their combination, selects a sustained rate or a relaxation trajectory.

3. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE v1 EXPLICIT RATE FAMILY

On the 2 pi by 2 pi torus, A_0=cos x-cos y has k_1=1, E_{B_perp}(0)=2 pi^2, and mean-zero A_2(0)=2 pi^2. It saturates (13), so its limiting 2-D energy floor equals its initial energy. The normalized fast family from v1 also has E_{B_perp}=2 pi^2 exactly. Its mean-zero anastrophy approaches 2 pi^2 from below; therefore the ideal-limit energy-release cap is O(epsilon), not O(1), even though its initial flux-transfer rate tends to 1.

The diagnostic diagnostics_v2.py computes the exact one-dimensional factorized integrals for this family (including subtraction of the flux-function mean before applying Poincare). It gives:

   0.050       19.7392088        19.7381477          0.0010612
   0.025       19.7392088        19.7386782          0.0005306
   0.010       19.7392088        19.7389965          0.0002123
   0.005       19.7392088        19.7391027          0.0001061

The cap scales as approximately 0.0212 epsilon over this tested range. For a fixed T, the finite-resistivity correction in (15) is also O(epsilon), so the total 2-D magnetic energy released over that time still tends to zero for this family. This reconciles its O(1) initial rate with its O(epsilon)-scale flux perturbation and energy budget. The table evaluates initial data, not a time evolution.

4. COMPARISON AND LIMITATIONS

The mean-square vector-potential constraint and selective decay in 2-D MHD are established before this campaign; Ting, Matthaeus, and Montgomery numerically study the competition between selective decay and dynamic alignment and explicitly contrast 2-D mean-square vector potential with 3-D helicity. Equations (10)–(15) are exact consequences of the resistive induction equation and Poincare's inequality; they sharpen the finite-time accounting but do not claim to introduce anastrophy. Symmetry-reduced Hamiltonian MHD also has large Casimir families and energy-Casimir principles; this is established framework, not novelty evidence.

The extension from A_2 to the entire value distribution is mathematically natural because A is an advected scalar in ideal 2-D MHD, and its resistive Casimir drift is explicit. The coarea/perimeter expression (16) is an internally derived application of standard analysis. No primary source was found in this bounded search that uses this exact distribution-based floor to quantify magnetic energy released by reconnection; that absence is not evidence of novelty. A thorough literature search and independent mathematical review remain required before any priority statement.

Guide-field check: a constant B_g zhat does not alter (9) or the local X/O flux-rate equation, but it adds a guide-field energy that A's distribution does not constrain. The floor here applies only to the in-plane magnetic energy. If B_g is fixed and uniform, its contribution is constant; if it is dynamical, this 2-D scalar argument does not bound its energy. In a periodic domain with net guide flux, absolute 3-D helicity is not available as in v1, and a relative-helicity/reference-field treatment is separate.

Boundary check: (9)–(16) use periodic boundaries and zero normal flux in the reduced plane. With line-tied or open boundaries, C_F acquires advective and diffusive boundary terms; they must be included. Photospheric injection or CME export invalidates a closed-volume Casimir budget unless measured and subtracted.

Dimensional check: eta_m has units L^2/T; A_2 has units B^2 L^4 per unit invariant length; eta_m integral E_B dt has the same units. Poincare contributes k_1^2, yielding an energy. Equation (16) has units B^2 L^2 for integral |grad A|^2.

Ideal-limit check: the Casimir near-conservation is compatible with nonuniform current and finite Ohmic power. It does not imply a uniform ideal limit of curl B. The explicit family has an O(1/epsilon) current sheet, and the local flux derivative remains O(1). Its no-O(1)-release conclusion follows from anastrophy plus Poincare, not from a claim that the rate vanishes.

5. UPDATED CLAIM LEDGER AND NEXT GATE

Internally derived in v2: equation (10) for all 2-D flux-function Casimirs; the finite-time estimate (11); the exact anastrophy identity (12); Poincare energy floor and finite-time release cap (13)–(15); the equimeasurable limiting-state compactness argument; and the coarea/isoperimetric lower bound (16). The calculations assume a smooth incompressible 2-D resistive-MHD solution, periodic boundaries, zero mean A, and a uniform total-energy budget.

Finite-tested: diagnostics_v2.py checks the explicit family’s energy normalization, mean-zero anastrophy, and limiting release cap at five epsilon values. It does not evolve the PDE.

Candidate: the combined distribution-based energy floor plus current-concentration criterion is a closure-free 2-D constraint pair. Novelty is unresolved.

Refuted: the explicit O(1) instantaneous rate family is not evidence for O(1) sustained reconnection or O(1) energy release. Under its own 2-D anastrophy budget and fixed-time energy bound, available magnetic energy tends to zero.

Still unresolved: whether a smooth large-scale initial configuration with an O(1) gap above E_eq can dynamically form and replenish a resistive current sheet for O(1) time without assuming an empirical plasmoid threshold/turbulent closure; whether the full-distribution floor is sharper than anastrophy for physically relevant flux distributions; how line-tied guide-field and 3-D relative-helicity constraints modify the energy floor; and whether a corresponding 3-D reachable-state variational principle exists.

Next decisive gate: evaluate E_eq or a certified lower bound for a nontrivial 2-D current-sheet/flux-rope initial family with E_B(0)-E_eq=O(1), then determine whether the resistive MHD equations alone can realize O(1) flux transfer and O(1) energy release over fixed Alfvén time as eta_m->0. This must include a sustained solution or a rigorous obstruction; an instantaneous initial derivative is insufficient.
