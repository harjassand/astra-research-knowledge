## Exact interface

Keep the cycle14 quadratic chart
`psi2=h0+G1(rho+eta²)+G2[eta+(rho+eta²)²]`,
where `h0=-(beta+45/4)²`, `beta=y²(6x²y²+15xy+4)`,
and `G=(2y,4x)D2`. Let R be an arbitrary polynomial repair of total
degree<=7, and set `psi=psi2+R=h0+u`. Thus deg u<=7. Impose
nonzero constant full Hessian determinant and the necessary origin-fibre
membership `psi_rho,psi_eta in(x,y)`. Full source ideal equality is stronger
and is not inferred from membership.

The previous degree-six theorem does not carry over: the unrepairable
degree-seven fibre term can now be changed, and quadratic repair Hessians
contribute at the same degree as the linear cofactor term. In particular
the first degree-seven equation does **not** force R7_rhorho=0.

## First five unrestricted graded equations

Write the Hessian in base/residual blocks as
`H=[[Hb+Au,B],[B^T,C]]`, `C=Hess_(rho,eta)u`. Every entry of Hu
has degree<=5 and every entry of Hb degree<=10. The exact block identity is

`det H=det(Hb+Au)det C+(det B)²`
`      -tr[adj(Hb+Au) B adj(C) B^T]`.                  (1)

A determinant term containing zero or one fixed Hb entry has degree at
most20 or25. The leading base potential is h12=-36x4y8, with

`det(Hb)_20=-456192x6y14`,
`adj(Hb)_10=-144x²y6 [[14x²,-8xy],[-8xy,3y²]]`.

Therefore determinant degrees30--26 recursively force the homogeneous
degrees10,9,8,7,6 of det C to vanish. Put C=sum(Cj), deg Cj=j.
The five exact equations are

```
det C5=0,
tr(adj C5 C4)=0,
tr(adj C5 C3)+det C4=0,
tr(adj C5 C2)+tr(adj C4 C3)=0,
tr(adj C5 C1)+tr(adj C4 C2)+det C3=0.               (2)
```

Lower parts of the base determinant multiply already vanished parts of
det C, so none can invalidate this triangular implication. Origin
membership makes every C entry belong to(x,y), so C0=0 and
det C belongs to(x,y)². Its degree-five part D5 consequently has fibre
degree<=3. The next full-Hessian equation, degree25, is

`det(Hb)_20 D5=tr[adj(Hb)_10 B5 adj(C5) B5^T]`.       (3)

This is the first balance with a single high base-Hessian factor. It is
an exact necessary equation, not a determinant-constancy certificate.

## A new restriction on variable leading directions

Let W7 be the degree-seven part of u. If C5=0, W7 is fibre affine
and no leading direction is asserted. Otherwise det C5=0. Subtracting
its fibre-affine part and using the named established binary Hesse result
(de Bondt1203.6605v3 Theorem2.4(i)) over K=C(x,y) gives

`W7=L1(x,y)rho+L2(x,y)eta+B7(x,y)+sum_(m>=2) Em(x,y) q^m`,
`q=A(x,y)rho+B(x,y)eta`.

The fraction-field result alone is not a legal coordinate chart. Take A,B
coprime polynomials. Homogeneity of the Hessian ratio makes A/B invariant
under common scaling of x,y; its primitive numerator and denominator are
therefore homogeneous of the same degree d0. Gauss's lemma for the
primitive polynomial q makes each Em polynomial, rather than rational.
Its degree is7-m(d0+1). If the direction is nonconstant, d0>=1,
and m>=2 gives d0<=2. This leaves only d0=1,m<=3, or d0=2,m=2.

Let v=(A,B)^T and n=(-B,A)^T. With F(q)=sum Em q^m,
`C5=Fqq vv^T`, `adj C5=Fqq nn^T`. The mixed-block contraction is

`B5 n=Fq omega+r`,
`omega_i=A partial_i B-B partial_i A`,
`r_i=n^T partial_i(L1,L2)^T`.

Euler's identity for homogeneous A,B gives
`omega_x=y W`, `omega_y=-x W`, with homogeneous W of degree2d0-2.
W is nonzero exactly when the direction is nonconstant. The weighted
base contraction is

`14x²omega_x²-16xyomega_x omega_y+3y²omega_y²`
`        =33x²y²W²`.                                (4)

If m=3,d0=1, the right side of(3) has a unique fibre-degree-five
coefficient proportional to Fqq Fq² times(4). Its coefficient is nonzero.
D5 has fibre degree<=3, so this is impossible. Thus every variable
leading direction must be quadratic in the fibre.

Write that nonlinear part E q². From(3), its fibre-quadratic coefficient
in D5 is

`E³ W² q²/(12x²y6)`.                               (5)

Because q is primitive, polynomiality of every coefficient of(5) requires
`x²y6 | E³W²`. For d0=2, E has degree1 and W degree2;
deg(E³W²)=7<8, so the divisibility is impossible. For d0=1, W is
a nonzero constant and E has degree3. The divisibility forces x|E and
y²|E, hence E=lambda x y² for a nonzero constant lambda.

**New necessary leading-form theorem.** Any nonconstant primitive
base-dependent leading fibre direction in a constant-determinant
degree-seven repair satisfying origin membership has exactly the form

`(W7)_nonlinear=lambda x y²[A1(x,y)rho+B1(x,y)eta]²`,

where A1,B1 are independent homogeneous linear forms and lambda!=0.
Constant fibre directions and C5=0 remain separate open branches.

## The remaining degree25 equation in this branch

Put Delta=det[(A1x,A1y),(B1x,B1y)]!=0 and let r_x,r_y be the
degree-six base polynomials defined above. Then the full equation(3) is

```
D5=lambda³ Delta² x q²/12
   +lambda² Delta(-2x r_x+y r_y)q/(36x y³)
   +lambda[14x²r_x²-16xy r_x r_y+3y²r_y²]/(1584x³y6).   (6)
```

Thus polynomiality additionally requires
`xy³ | -2x r_x+y r_y` and
`x³y6 | 14x²r_x²-16xy r_x r_y+3y²r_y²`.
These conditions constrain the still-free fibre-affine leading terms and
the lower Cj; equations(2) and(6) must hold together. They do not
construct a repair or imply full ideal equality.

The linear form q itself is not a global fibre coordinate over C[x,y]:
its coefficients generate(x,y), so its Jacobian row vanishes at the base
origin and has no unimodular complement. Nevertheless such a q can occur
inside a genuine polynomial shear. The independently constructed control
in LEGAL_VARIABLE_DIRECTION_CHART.md demonstrates exactly this distinction:
its source chart is globally legal, preserves the actual witness pair,
and realizes the leading form, but fails equation(3). A rational
normalization by q cannot be silently used to preserve Hessian determinant
or collision.
