I. Exact deterministic theorem
------------------------------
Let F be a finite rose, with signed alphabet T and its fixed-point-free inverse involution. Let Gamma -> F be an immersion of a finite graph, and let X be F with a cone attached on every connected component of Gamma. Let G = pi_1(X).

Supply integers L >= 3 and D_0 >= 1, and a real epsilon in (0, 1/4]. Assume:

(Girth) Every positive cyclically immersed closed path in Gamma has length at least L. Ordinary girth at least L implies this.

(Closures) Every positive immersed segment S in Gamma can be completed to a cyclically immersed closed path S J without cancelling original occurrences, with |J| <= D_0 L. An intact cyclically immersed loop needs no closure.

Set

  c_sep = 2 sqrt(2) / (1 - sqrt(2/3)),
  U any integer >= max(3, ceil(96 D_0 / epsilon)),
  eta = min(1/(32 U), epsilon/(64 U)),
  K_0 = ceil((c_sep/eta)^2),
  C = K_0 (U + D_0),
  I = ceil(192 C).

An allowed comparison pairs two disjoint intervals of positions, carrying mutually inverse strings in reverse order. All comparison positions are disjoint across the system. Each matched pair of positions must traverse different underlying unoriented edges of Gamma. Repeated traversals anywhere else, including repeated paths and comparisons between different positions of the same path, are allowed.

(Closed bounded exclusion) There is no system of at most K_0 positive cyclically immersed closed paths in Gamma, with total occurrence length H_Z satisfying L <= H_Z <= C L, at most I allowed comparisons, and at most epsilon H_Z unpaired positions.

Then, in the finite presentation on the rose generators with one maximal-tree fundamental-cycle relator for each non-tree graph edge,

  Area(w) < (64/epsilon) |w|

for every nonempty cyclically freely reduced nullword w. The corresponding non-strict linear upper bound holds for all nullwords, with the empty word assigned area zero. Consequently G is word-hyperbolic by the standard equivalence between a finite presentation with a linear Dehn function and word-hyperbolicity.

The supplied graph is not required to embed in G. The outer word is not required to lift to Gamma. The graph maps of only the inner loops are used in the length-reducing surgery.

II. Proof of the deterministic theorem
--------------------------------------
1. Cone pictures and the minimum being optimized

Fix a nonempty cyclically freely reduced nullword w, n = |w|. A finite van Kampen filling can be represented by pairwise disjoint inner disks D_i, each with a closed boundary path gamma_i in a component of Gamma, and a complementary punctured disk mapping to F. The exterior circle retains the exact word w.

Here is a justification that does not assume a graph lift of w. In a component Lambda choose a maximal tree and attach one ordinary relator disk to Lambda for each basis loop of pi_1(Lambda). This relative complex is simply connected and acyclic: the boundary map from these disks identifies their free cellular group with H_1(Lambda; Z). Hence it is contractible. It is homotopy equivalent to the abstract cone K_Lambda relative to Lambda, by extending the identity on Lambda across the relative cells in both directions and extending the two homotopies in the contractible targets. Attaching these replacements to F therefore gives a homotopy equivalence relative to F. Standard preimages of small interior disks in the resulting relator cells produce the required picture, while all intervening material retracts to F. The exterior is fixed during the relative replacement. Alternatively a usual planar van Kampen diagram yields the same picture after rounding its relator cells and moving all degenerate material into the rose.

Minimize H = sum_i |gamma_i| over all such fillings of this fixed exterior word. H is a nonnegative integer and the set of fillings is nonempty, so a minimum exists. A collar homotopy can cyclically tighten any inner graph path; the replacement cap still factors through its own abstract cone. Remove constant inner boundaries. If no positive inner loop remained, w would bound in F, impossible for a nonempty freely reduced word in a free group. Thus H >= L and every remaining inner loop is positive and cyclically immersed.

This minimum is over fillings of w, not over a fixed relative homotopy class of fillings. Disk surgery is permitted to discard a sphere while preserving a filling of w. That observation is essential for the self-pair case.

2. Pairing arcs and same-edge surgery

Choose one regular point in each unoriented rose edge. Put the complementary map in piecewise-linear general position at these points while fixing boundary words. Its preimages consist of finitely many disjoint embedded arcs and circles. Ignore the circles. Each boundary occurrence supplies exactly one endpoint of an arc, so the arcs pair all occurrences. With coherent disk orientations the two paired signed letters are inverse. Add a formal exterior disk to turn the punctured disk picture into a sphere; this formal disk has no map to X.

Suppose an arc pairs two inner occurrences of the same underlying graph edge e. Because the signed letters are inverse, their specified graph lifts traverse e in opposite directions. A sufficiently narrow band around the arc maps into the open interval of that rose edge and has the lift to the interior of this very edge e. The lift agrees with both boundary lifts. Thus the band and all incident inner disks factor through the same abstract cone. This is a factorization through a contractible cone; it does not assert that the cone's image in X is an embedded contractible subspace.

There are exactly two inner cases.

Distinct inner disks: with graph-boundary words e P and e^{-1} Q, splice the two disks along the band. Their union is a disk. The resulting closed graph boundary tightens to P Q, of length |P| + |Q|, two less than the old total. The graph endpoints match because e and e^{-1} are the same graph edge. Replace the union by one cone disk. The exterior word remains exactly w.

One inner disk: its boundary has the form e P e^{-1} Q, where P and Q are closed graph paths at the terminal and initial vertices of e respectively. Disk plus band is an annulus embedded in the sphere. Its complement has two disk components. Exactly one contains the formal exterior disk. Keep this component, cap its new inner boundary through the same cone, and remove the formal exterior disk. The result still fills w. Its new cap is one of P or Q, and all other retained inner disks were already present. Since |P| + |Q| is the old inner length minus two, its total H is at most the old H minus two. Tightening lowers it further if possible. A constant cap can be removed.

Both cases contradict minimality. Every inner--inner pairing therefore uses different underlying unoriented graph edges. Inner--outer and outer--outer arcs are not subjected to this surgery. They may use arbitrary rose letters; no outer graph lift has been introduced.

3. Bounded pieces and the complete closure budget

Assume for a contradiction that n <= epsilon H/64. Keep every inner loop of length at most U L intact. For a longer loop of integer length h, set r = ceil(h/(U L)) and divide it into r consecutive segments of nearly equal integer lengths. Since h > U L,

  h/r > U L/2,
  floor(h/r) >= U L/3,
  ceil(h/r) <= U L.

The middle assertion uses U L >= 9, so the integer rounding margin is sufficient. Close each split segment using the supplied closure hypothesis. Index it at its first original occurrence so its closure is appended at the end. Intact loops keep their chosen indexing points and receive no added occurrences.

Let N be the number of inner pieces and A the total appended length. Every intact loop has at least L occurrences by girth, and every split segment has at least U L/3 >= L original occurrences. Hence

  N L <= H,
  |original part of a piece| <= U L,
  |closed piece| <= (U + D_0)L.

For the split loops, their number of pieces is at most 3 H/(U L), giving

  A <= 3 D_0 H/U <= epsilon H/32.

Retain only the pairings of original occurrences. All appended positions are unpaired. A closure can traverse an edge already used in original segments; that causes no violation, since its positions remain unpaired and global edge-disjointness is not part of the hypothesis.

4. The planar interval count for a formal outer disk

This is the point where a graph lift of w might mistakenly be required. It is unnecessary. The combinatorial statement used here involves only reduced circle words and the inverse-letter arc pairing.

Let p be the number of original inner disks plus the formal exterior disk. Thicken all disks and arcs into a regular neighborhood R. Let E be the number of pairing arcs. For every connected complementary region Q in the sphere, let ell_Q be its number of band-side incidences, which equals its number of intervening disk-boundary gaps. Each boundary component of R meets a band because every disk has a positive word and every occurrence is paired. Euler characteristic gives

  chi(R) = p - E,
  sum_Q chi(Q) = 2 - p + E,
  sum_Q ell_Q = 2 E,
  sum_Q (ell_Q - 2 chi(Q)) = 2 p - 4.

These equations allow R to be disconnected and complementary regions to have multiple boundary components.

A gap is good when its complementary region is a disk digon bounded by two different bands. Opposite good gaps pair as an involution. A disk monogon would pair neighboring occurrences of inverse letters, contradicting cyclic free reduction; none exists, including on the outer circle w. A digon using the same band twice can occur only at a degree-one boundary gap; at most p such digons and 2p such gap incidences occur. Every other region is a disk with ell_Q >= 3, or a planar surface with at least two boundary components. It satisfies

  ell_Q <= 3(ell_Q - 2 chi(Q)).

Consequently the number B of non-good gaps is at most 8p (a safe bound even if one exceptional break were allowed).

Treat the outer circle as one intact piece; the total designated piece count is P = N + 1, with p <= P. Cut at every non-good gap, at one indexing gap on every original circle, and at every designated segment boundary. The extra marked gaps number at most p + P. When a marked gap is good, cut at its opposite good gap as well. The cut set is stable under the good-gap involution and has cardinality

  J <= B + 2(p + P) <= 12P.

Each circle has a cut, so its remaining maximal runs are linear intervals within designated pieces. Across any uncut adjacency, arc pairing reverses the adjacent partner order. Stability of the cuts ensures that a maximal run is paired with an entire maximal run. No run is paired with itself: an odd run would have a self-paired middle occurrence; an even run would have inverse adjacent middle letters. Thus the runs pair in distinct twos and give at most

  J/2 <= 6(N + 1) <= 12 H/L

interval comparisons. The last inequality uses N L <= H and H >= L. This proof does not refer to underlying graph edges or lifts at any outer occurrence. The surviving inner comparisons inherit the distinct-edge property from step 2.

Delete the exterior vertex and all its incident arcs. At most n inner positions lose their partners, since there were n outer occurrences and pairing is one-to-one. Outer--outer pairs cause no inner loss. A comparison either involves the exterior and is wholly discarded, or is wholly retained between inner pieces.

5. Why splitting disks preserves planarity

The graph of remaining original pairing arcs on the N pieces is a planar multigraph. At an old disk, the arc ends of every piece form a consecutive block in cyclic order. Replace its old vertex by one new vertex for each such block. Draw its incident ends to that vertex inside its own sector near the original boundary interval. These sectors and their connecting stars can be chosen disjoint. The original arcs lie outside the old disk and remain disjoint. This works for self-arcs as well as arcs joining different old disks. Closing paths creates no pairing arcs, so their graph-edge traversals are irrelevant to this planar embedding.

Arbitrarily splitting a vertex would not preserve planarity in general. The argument specifically uses consecutive blocks of ends. This condition is guaranteed by cutting consecutive boundary segments and by the comparison cuts in step 4.

6. Separator lemma, including disconnected graphs and deleted vertices

Erase loops and replace parallel edges by single edges for separator purposes; these changes preserve all connected components after any vertex deletion. The Lipton--Tarjan theorem supplies at most 2 sqrt(2s) removed vertices in a simple planar graph of order s, leaving component sizes at most 2s/3. Recursively apply it to components larger than K_0, starting independently on original components.

At a call of size s > K_0, charge each participating original vertex 2 sqrt(2)/sqrt(s). The total charge equals the upper bound for that separator. Along the ancestry of any original vertex, sizes decrease by a factor at most 2/3 until it is deleted or reaches a component of size at most K_0. Reading backwards from the last charged size gives a total per vertex at most

  [2 sqrt(2)/sqrt(K_0)] sum_{j >= 0}(2/3)^{j/2}
    = c_sep/sqrt(K_0).

This includes vertices removed by an earlier separator. Thus at most c_sep N/sqrt(K_0) <= eta N vertices are removed. Every survivor component has at most K_0 piece vertices.

Let M be the original occurrence length of removed pieces. Then

  M <= eta N U L <= eta U H <= epsilon H/64.

Every deleted original position had at most one partner, so at most M surviving original positions additionally lose partners. A comparison is fully retained or fully discarded because each of its intervals belongs to one piece. No surviving comparison joins different survivor components: its pairing arc would connect those components. The surviving comparisons are still intervals, without requiring additional cuts.

7. Cluster selection with all losses accounted for

For each surviving component Z, include its appended closures and let H_Z be its total closed-path length, b_Z its number of unpaired positions, and I_Z its number of retained comparisons. These totals satisfy

  sum_Z H_Z >= H - M >= 63 H/64,
  H_Z <= K_0(U + D_0)L = C L,
  sum_Z b_Z <= n + M + A <= epsilon H/16,
  sum_Z I_Z <= 12 H/L.

The first inequality uses epsilon <= 1 and deliberately leaves slack. For the third inequality, the exterior loss is epsilon H/64, separator loss epsilon H/64, and closure loss epsilon H/32; their sum is epsilon H/16.

Clusters with b_Z > epsilon H_Z have total H_Z at most H/16. Clusters with I_Z > 192 H_Z/L have total H_Z at most (L/192)(12 H/L) = H/16. Even if these two bad classes overlap, deleting both costs at most H/8. Thus at least

  (63/64 - 1/16 - 1/16) H = 55 H/64 > 0

survives. Every piece in such a cluster is a positive cyclically immersed closed path, so girth gives H_Z >= L. Its at most K_0 paths have H_Z <= C L, b_Z <= epsilon H_Z, and I_Z <= 192 H_Z/L <= 192 C <= I. This is exactly a forbidden closed bounded system, with all retained comparisons pairing distinct underlying graph edges. Contradiction. Therefore H < 64 n/epsilon.

8. Ordinary finite presentation area

For a component Lambda choose a tree root o, tree paths t_v from o to v, and the relator r_e = t_init(e) e t_term(e)^{-1} for each positively chosen non-tree edge. Tree-edge versions freely reduce to the empty word, and reversing e gives r_e^{-1}.

For any closed graph path gamma = e_1 ... e_h at v, inserting successive tree paths and telescoping gives in the free group

  t_v gamma t_v^{-1} = r_{e_1} ... r_{e_h}.

After tree-edge factors are removed, this uses at most h defining relators or their inverses. Conjugating back preserves the number of relation cells. Filling each inner picture disk therefore costs at most its boundary length, and the complementary rose surface requires no relation cells. Total area is at most H. This proves the stated bound. Free reduction, cyclic reduction and conjugating whiskers add no relation cells, giving the linear Dehn bound for general nullwords.

I checked the contracts and the main algebra of the conditioning, diameter and bounded-pattern proofs. Relevant gates are:

P1. The girth-conditioned matching law must be nonempty. The primary switching construction selects a bad short-cycle edge and a distant edge of the same matching, and switches their targets. The crude expectation of bad edges is o(n); the set of edges within distance 2L is o(n); and each matching has order np. These properties supply a distant clean edge and preserve prescribed label types. This is not ordinary independence after conditioning.

P2. The additional-edge conditional bound is
  P_L(e | S) <= 1/(np - |S| - r_n),
  r_n = vp + O(136^(2L+2)) = o(n).
Counting switches injectively gives the stated upper bound. For fixed E <= C_1 L it yields (np)^(-E) exp(o(L)); the error is uniform in that fixed range. The diameter proof instead applies the single-prescription bound to a small fixed fraction of n edges, keeping all denominators above np/2. It does not extrapolate the O(L)-edge corollary to a linear number of edges.

P3. The diameter argument requires expansion only of sets of order at most rho n. Each failed expanding set of k vertices forces at least ceil(129k/2) edges in a containing set of 2k vertices. The conditional prescription bound controls their union bound by [C_3(k/n)^62.5]^k. Small-set expansion makes every radius-O(log n) ball contain more than rho n vertices; balls along a geodesic then pack to give a fixed multiple of log n for diameter, without claiming global connectedness.

P4. The bounded-pattern proof must count the image graph and distinct edge prescriptions rather than occurrences. Its multiplicity stages have sum_j E_j = H and sum_j(V_j - E_j - k_j) <= 0, cancelling any positive net power of n. A common near-aligned block grid treats repeated positions and comparisons. Link constraints bound free word choices, while one occurrence's word weight per multiplicity stage supplies the square-weight decay. This is the load-bearing primary mechanism.

P5. Its stage combination is legitimate only as a product of deterministic first-moment bounds followed by a minimum, not as independence of random stages. The source explicitly uses P(pattern) <= min_j B_j and then bounds min_j B_j by the geometric mean of the numbers B_j. That part of the logical transfer is sound.

IV. Explicit negative control: why the almost-paired condition matters
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Let Gamma be a four-cycle, immersed in the rose on a,b with cyclic word a b a^{-1} b^{-1}. The cone group is Z^2. Girth is 4. Any immersed segment runs consistently around this circle, so appending at most three edges gives a cyclically immersed loop; D_0 = 1 suffices for L = 4. This control has degree two and is used for the cleaned deterministic criterion, not the source degree-129 construction.

Take an r by r square patch of the ordinary commutator diagram. Every square supplies one copy of the positive loop a b a^{-1} b^{-1}, so there are r^2 identical closed paths in Gamma, with H = 4r^2. Compare each pair of occurrences across an interior grid edge. Adjacent horizontal squares pair the b edge with the distinct b^{-1} graph edge; adjacent vertical squares pair the a edge with the distinct a^{-1} graph edge. There are

  I_r = 2r(r - 1)

one-letter comparisons, using all interior occurrences in disjoint pairs. The unpaired positions are exactly the perimeter, b_r = 4r. Thus b_r/H = 1/r.

For any supplied epsilon choose r = ceil(1/epsilon). These systems have unpaired fraction at most epsilon. With the deterministic extraction parameters and D_0 = 1, they fit inside K_0 and C and their comparison count fits inside I. Consequently the closed bounded exclusion fails explicitly. The criterion does not accidentally classify this Z^2 example as hyperbolic. Dropping the nearly-paired exclusion and retaining asphericity alone cannot imply hyperbolicity; the finite torus is already an aspherical classifying complex for Z^2.

This example also prevents an illegitimate repair of the random theorem by forbidding repeated loops or requiring every occurrence's graph edge to be globally distinct. The intended exclusion really does allow repeated paths and only constrains the two positions in an individual comparison.

Runnable evidence: verify_audit.py checks this exact square-patch construction, free reduction, distinct-edge comparison pairs, disjoint position usage, and the parameter inequalities at several rational epsilons. The checks illustrate the theorem's scopes; they do not prove the planar lemma or the random theorem.

A conditional existence theorem implies eventual termination of this exhaustive search, but no practical runtime, explicit size threshold, concrete presentation, or expanded a,b,c was supplied. VII. Additional attack requested by root: the source conjunction
---------------------------------------------------------------
After banking the disk criterion, root asked whether algebraic/type parity could force a bounded nearly-paired closed system at some arbitrarily large but fixed K_0, contrary to the source's quantifiers. I attacked that conjunction in two ways: the synchronous fibre product that most directly expresses the parity algebra, and a complete line-level reconstruction of the all-closed bounded-pattern first-moment mechanism. A. Why odd degrees do not by themselves force a small closed system

The synchronous fibre product on vertex pairs (x,y) has an edge for each common outgoing label. The degree of (x,y) is |S_x intersect S_y|. In A by B these degrees are all odd. In the off-diagonal part of A by A they are likewise odd. A non-backtracking closed path in this fibre product gives two closed immersed graph paths with the same label; reversing one path gives a fully paired closed system. For off-diagonal pairs, its synchronized occurrences traverse different underlying graph edges. Thus a short fibre-product cycle would be a genuine falsifier of the claimed exclusion.

However, odd degree does not force any cycle. An odd-degree finite tree has even order and fits the handshaking cancellation perfectly: K_2, a star with an odd number of leaves, and disjoint unions of such trees are elementary examples. The diagonal component in A by A is exceptional and does contain cycles, but its synchronized pairs traverse the same graph edge and are excluded from the comparison class. Its one even-degree root and odd vertex count explain ab=1; they do not supply an admissible closed system.

For A by B, the mean product degree over all vertex pairs is exactly |T| p^2, approximately 1.008 for q=128. Therefore its coarse edge/vertex count is compatible with a forest; it is far below the mean degree two that would force a cycle by counting alone. The rare equal-line pairs have large degree 129, while most different-line pairs have degree one. A branching estimate that uses only mean degree is insufficient. The relevant non-backtracking, type-weighted second-moment kernel is the square-weight matrix below, and it is strictly subcritical. No claim has been proved that every prescribed-type fibre product can actually be a forest; only the proposed parity-only forced-cycle argument has been refuted.

B. Exact spectral check and a genuine threshold control

The squared turn-weight matrix can be reduced to three categories: O_* (the seven ordinary letters paired with extra letters), O_0 (the other ordinary letters), and E (the seven extra letters). Its equitable quotient, in that order, is

  Q = [ 7p^2,          (v-7)p^2,       6/4       ]
      [ 7/(q+1)^2,     (v-8)/(q+1)^2, 7p^2      ]
      [ 6/(q+1)^2,     (v-7)/(q+1)^2, 7p^2      ].

The counts include the unique excluded inverse successor. Any positive category-constant vector lifts to a positive vector on the full alphabet. For q=128, f=(4,1,1) satisfies Qf <= lambda f, with the exact rational lambda stored in CHECKS.json and

  lambda = 0.9939371997570104... < 1.

Consequently the full squared kernel has spectral radius below one, without numerical eigenvalue assumptions. Its word sum is at most 4|T| lambda^(h-1). The proof's strict decay margin is real and not an error from comparing 128 with the alphabet size.

As a sharp diagnostic of this part of the mechanism, solving (I-Q)f=1 by exact rational elimination gives f<0 componentwise for q=4,8,16. Then u=-f>0 and Qu=u+1>u, a certificate that their quotient spectral radii exceed one. For q=32,64,128 the same solve gives f>0 and Qf=f-1<f, a certificate of subcriticality. Thus simply lowering the source projective-plane order to 16 would invalidate its squared-word-decay route. This is a real mechanism boundary, but it does not refute the fixed q=128 source. All checks were three by three rational arithmetic; no large matrix, graph or random instance was materialized.

C. Independent all-closed bounded-pattern reconstruction

Here is the full mechanism in a form that exposes its exact hypotheses. Consider the prescribed-type conditioned matching model with a fixed alphabet, turn counts np w(t,u)+O(1), inverse symmetry w(t,u)=w(bar(u),bar(t)), fixed 0<w<=1 on reduced turns, and exponentially summable squared word weights. Suppose girth is at least L, and for each fixed C_1 every injective labelled graph prescription with at most C_1 L distinct edges has probability at most (np)^(-E) exp(o(L)), uniformly in this fixed range. The source switching proof supplies this last contract in its actual model. Then the following argument excludes all-closed nearly-paired systems at every fixed K_0,C,I with a single epsilon chosen beforehand.

(i) Bounded image complexity. A putative system has total occurrence length H<=CL and its image Delta has at most CL edges and girth at least L. Removing leaves, removing circle components, and suppressing degree-two vertices gives a weighted multigraph D of minimum degree at least three, whose total edge length is at most CL. If it has r edges, uniformly starting a non-backtracking walk of k edges makes its expected weighted length at most kCL/r. At least half of its probability is on walks of length at most 2kCL/r. Since each walk has probability at most (2r)^(-1)2^(-(k-1)), at least r2^(k-1) short walks exist. For k=ceil(log_2(8r))+1 there are more such walks than ordered endpoint pairs. Two distinct short reduced walks with the same endpoints contain a cycle of length at most 4kCL/r. Hence

  r <= 4C(ceil(log_2(8r))+1),

which bounds r in terms of fixed C. Circle components number at most C. This bounds the image cycle rank. In the all-closed case the image has no leaves. Mark every vertex of degree other than two and every chosen loop starting point. The resulting number of chains is bounded in terms of C and K_0. Their interiors are disjoint and degree two.

(ii) Multiplicity is bounded, even with repetitions. Every path traverses whole chains. Two successive uses of the same oriented edge by one immersed path start at the same graph vertex, and the intervening nonempty reduced loop contains a cycle. Their starting positions are at least L apart. Summing across orientations and paths gives m_e<=2(C+K_0)=:M up to ceiling. Chain multiplicities are constant because there are no interior path endpoints. The number of chain traversals is therefore bounded. An unlabelled pattern records the bounded chain graph, integer chain lengths, side choices, bounded traversal sequences, and the fixed number of comparison endpoints. There are only L^O(1) such patterns. The exponent here may depend heavily on fixed C,K_0,I; it is not uniform if they grow.

(iii) The decisive power-of-n cancellation. At level j retain the underlying edges with multiplicity at least j; write E_j,V_j. Then sum_j E_j=H. At each image vertex all traversal incidences are paired by immersed turns with incidences on different half-edges. Therefore

  2 max_{e incident to z} m_e <= sum_{e incident to z} m_e.

The vertex occurs in exactly max m_e levels. Summing over vertices shows sum_j V_j<=H and therefore sum_j(V_j-E_j)<=0. This is what pays for positions repeated many times on the same underlying edge. Counting each occurrence as an independent edge would be wrong; the actual proof does not do so.

(iv) A uniformly nearly aligned block grid. Split every comparison when it crosses a chain end. In oriented chain coordinates every subcomparison is x -> sigma x+a with sigma=+/-1 and integer a. There are only a bounded number d_1 of offsets, depending on the fixed pattern bounds. Set N=floor(L^(1/[2(d_1+1)])), R=floor(sqrt(N)). Pigeonhole the N^(d_1)+1 vectors of fractional parts of ell a/L into N^(d_1) cubes. This yields 1<=u<=N^(d_1) with dist(ua/L,Z)<=1/N for all offsets. Choose u deterministically from the pattern, and set s=L/(uR). Uniformly,

  L/[N^(d_1) sqrt(N)] <= s <= L/R,
  dist(a/s,Z) <= R/N.

Thus s tends to infinity, s=o(L), and the relative grid displacement tends to zero. The lower bound is a positive power of L, greater than L^(1/2). Divide each chain into full blocks of s+O(1) edge positions plus one end fragment. Every traversal uses the same subdivision. Let kappa tend to zero while dominating R/N and 10/s. Match two block occurrences if their comparison covers at least (1-kappa)s contiguous positions in each. Such matches form a partial matching because two portions longer than half a block cannot be disjoint in the same occurrence. Outside the boundedly many block occurrences near chain/comparison endpoints, every unmatched block is wholly unpaired. Hence, writing b_* for unmatched full-block occurrences and b_0 for all unpaired positions,

  s b_* <= b_0 + O(s+L/s) = b_0+o(L).

The bounded endpoint count, rather than a count proportional to individual edges, is important here.

(v) Word constraints on underlying blocks. Forget occurrence indices to obtain a link multigraph on the underlying blocks. A known word on one block determines all but kappa s+O(1) letters of a neighboring block. Propagation along a spanning tree of a link component U costs at most exp(rho_L s |U|), rho_L->0. A self-link on one block gives even stronger control. A translation self-link has a nonzero integer step a: step zero would compare the same underlying graph edges. The equality graph on positions is a forest with at least (1-kappa)s distinct equality edges, leaving at most kappa s+O(1) free letters. A reflection self-link is impossible: the two long overlapping inverse-reflected intervals intersect in a reflection-invariant interval, forcing either a letter equal to its inverse or adjacent inverse letters in a reduced word.

For a component without self-links let S_U=sum_i m_i, z_U=max_i m_i and b_U be its unmatched occurrence count. The block with z_U occurrences has at least z_U-b_U matched occurrences, all partnering among the S_U-z_U occurrences of other blocks. Therefore 2z_U<=S_U+b_U. This conclusion uses disjoint compared positions, not global edge-disjointness.

(vi) Retaining both word entropy and weights. For each block word W_i set d_i=floor(-log P(W_i)). The fixed minimum positive turn weight gives d_i<=a_0 s. Squared-word decay gives d_i>=delta s/2 and at most exp(2d_i-delta s) words in bin d_i, for sufficiently large s. There are O(L/s) blocks and O(s) possible bins each. Enlarging patterns with all bins costs exp(O((L/s)log(s+2)))=exp(o(L)); the original polynomial count remains subexponential.

At stage j only blocks with m_i>=j are present. Consider only stage label assignments that extend as strings to the full pattern; a true full realization supplies such an assignment. For a link component represented at stage j, its stage projections have no more choices than its full assignments. Without self-links, use the minimum-bin block as seed even when it is absent from this stage. It contributes exp(2 min_i d_i-delta s), while tree propagation costs exp(rho_L s|U|). With a self-link there is no free seed word. All end fragments cost exp(O(s)) because the number of chains and multiplicities are bounded. Thus the logarithmic stage label count is at most

  o(L) + sum_{U without self-link, z_U>=j}(2 min_i d_i-delta s).

The use of extendible projections is not circular. It is an upper bound on possible strings, not an assumption that absent blocks can be embedded. Every actual full graph realization necessarily projects into this string set.

(vii) Stage embedding counts. For one fixed stage labelling, its chain internal vertices supply np w(t,u)+O(1) choices each; its marked vertices supply at most n choices. If Q_j chains are active there are E_j-Q_j internal vertices. Multiplication of type counts and distinct edge-prescription probabilities gives

  n^(V_j-E_j) p^(-Q_j) (product of internal turn weights) exp(o(L)).

The number Q_j is bounded. Discarding turn factors except the internal factors of full blocks increases this product because w<=1. Its retained block factors are at most exp(-sum_{i:m_i>=j}d_i). Thus the fixed-label expected count is at most

  n^(V_j-E_j) exp(-sum_{i:m_i>=j}d_i+o(L)).

Multiply by the stage label-count bound to obtain a deterministic first-moment bound B_j for the random count Z_j. Any full realization has Z_j>=1 at every stage, so its probability is at most min_j B_j. No random-stage independence is assumed.

(viii) Final sign calculation. Multiply the deterministic B_j over stages. The net power of n is nonpositive by (iii). For a no-self-link component, with d_min=min_i d_i,

  z_U(2d_min-delta s) - sum_i m_i d_i
    <= (2z_U-S_U)d_min-delta s z_U
    <= a_0 s b_U - (delta s/2)S_U.

If 2z_U-S_U<=0 use d_min>=delta s/2; if it is positive use d_min<=a_0s and 2z_U-S_U<=b_U. A self-link component has no positive seed term and contributes at most -(delta s/2)S_U. Full block occurrences have total length H+o(L), and s sum_U b_U<=b_0+o(L). The number of stages is at most the fixed M. Therefore

  log product_j B_j <= -(delta/2)H+a_0 b_0+o(L).

Choose epsilon=min(1/4,delta/[16(1+a_0)]) before the pattern bounds. If b_0<=epsilon H and H>=L, the right side is at most -delta H/4 for sufficiently large n, uniformly over patterns within those fixed bounds. Some stage then has B_j<=exp(-delta H/(4M)). Summing over exp(o(L)) enlarged patterns gives

  P_L(forbidden closed system) <= exp(-delta L/(4M)+o(L)) -> 0.

Every dependence here is permitted to be on fixed C,K_0,I. Very large choices slow the asymptotics and make the acquisition infeasible, but they do not change the sign or require a uniform estimate with growing parameters.

D. Audit disposition after this additional attack

The forced-pattern route based on degree parity failed for an explicit mathematical reason. The source's q128 contraction was verified exactly and a supercritical smaller-q control was found. The repeated-image bounded-pattern proof was reconstructed through its image complexity, multiplicity incidence, simultaneous grid, self-link entropy, bin weights, stage projections and final minimum. No precise logical or arithmetic failure was found in that chain.

This is more than checking the statement's quantifiers, but it still does not reconcile the published theorem conflict, constitute an external independent mathematical certification, or produce an explicit admissible graph. The overall counterexample status remains UNKNOWN.
