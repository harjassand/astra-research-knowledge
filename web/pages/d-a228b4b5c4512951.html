2. Exact positive entropy conversion

For positive f define A=Gamma^(1/p)f, rho=A^p, N=Tr rho=||f||_(p,sigma)^p.
The source positive entropy uses

  S_p(f)=Gamma^(-1/p)(A log A)-{f,log sigma}/(2p),
  Ent_p(f)=<I_(q,p)f,S_p(f)>_sigma-N log||f||_(p,sigma).

Cyclicity and commutation of log sigma with its own powers give

  <I_(q,p)f,Gamma^(-1/p)(A log A)>_sigma=Tr(rho log A),
  <I_(q,p)f,{f,log sigma}>_sigma=2Tr(rho log sigma).

Since log rho=p log A and log||f||_(p,sigma)=(log N)/p,

  Ent_p(f)=[Tr(rho log rho)-Tr(rho log sigma)-N log N]/p. (10)

Now g=I_(2,p)f=Gamma^(-1/2)(rho^(1/2)) is positive and
(Gamma^(1/2)g)^2=rho. Hence EXACTLY

No trace-one normalization of rho is needed for (10)-(11).

4. Independent norm derivative, including every factor of p

UCP ensures f_t stays strictly positive: f_0>=epsilon I implies
f_t>=epsilon I. Put A=Gamma^(1/p)f_t, rho=A^p, N=Tr rho.

  partial_t N=-p<I_(q,p)f_t,Lf_t>_sigma=-2(p-1)E_p(f_t).

This derivative is valid for noncommuting f_t and sigma; no scalar
commutation was used in differentiating the trace.

For theta=pi/(2p), (14) gives

6. Independent CP polar-block extension to ALL complex inputs

Here is an elementary norm-extension theorem, separate from energies:

If T:M_d->M_d is CP and ||T(A)||_v<=||A||_u for every positive A, where
1<=u,v<infinity, then the same bound holds for every complex X.

Proof. Positive semidefinite A are included by continuity if the bound
was initially established only for A>0. For the polar decomposition
X=U|X|, the block

  [ |X*|  X  ] = [U] |X| [U* I] >=0
  [  X*  |X| ]   [I]

is positive on C^2 tensor C^d, using U|X|U*=|X*|. Complete positivity
of T therefore makes

  [ A  Y ] >=0, A=T(|X*|), B=T(|X|), Y=T(X).
  [ Y* B ]

For delta>0 the same block with A_delta=A+delta I and B_delta=B+delta I
is positive. The Schur complement gives a contraction C_delta with

  Y=A_delta^(1/2) C_delta B_delta^(1/2).

Schatten Holder and the ideal property give

  ||Y||_v<=||A_delta^(1/2)||_(2v)||C_delta B_delta^(1/2)||_(2v)
          <=sqrt(||A_delta||_v ||B_delta||_v).

Let delta decrease to zero and use the POSITIVE-input bound on A and B:

  ||T(X)||_v<=sqrt(||T(|X*|)||_v||T(|X|)||_v)
             <=sqrt(||X*||_u||X||_u)=||X||_u.        (17)

This proves the theorem without a Dirichlet energy on the block algebra.
The use of id_2 tensor T is only CP positivity preservation. No positive
norm bound or LSI on that amplified algebra was assumed or used.

Apply the theorem with u=p0, v=p(t) and the CP transferred map

  T=Gamma^(1/p(t)) P_t Gamma^(-1/p0).

Its filters and inverse filters are CP. For positive A, the physical
observable f=Gamma^(-1/p0)A is positive. Equation(15) is exactly
||T(A)||_(p(t))<=||A||_(p0). Hence (17) gives

  ||P_t X||_(p(t),sigma)<=||X||_(p0,sigma)

It does not establish a completely bounded mixed norm or the same norm
