# A continuous calibration for interacting opposite Fourier carriers

## Statement

Identify R² with C, use normalized integration in y∈R/(2πZ), and let

\[
 G(A)=3(u+v-2j)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+j^2-\frac52ju,
 \quad u=|A_1|^2,\ v=|A_2|^2,\ j=\det A.
\]

For every L>0, every pair of **complex** periodic amplitudes
α,β∈W^{1,4}(R/(LZ);C), and every constant B∈R^{2×2}, set

\[
 \phi(x,y)=\alpha(x)e^{iy}+\beta(x)e^{-iy}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\inf_B\mathbb E\,[G(B+D\phi)-G(B)]
       =\mathbb E G(D\phi)\ge\tfrac12\mathbb E|D\phi|^4.}
\]

The amplitudes, phases, x period, variation speed and polygonal strip widths
are arbitrary. No circulation cancellation or finite graph is imposed. Thus
the theorem includes every R²-valued first-harmonic transverse profile
A(x)cos y+C(x)sin y with arbitrary vector-valued A,C, including interacting
clockwise and counterclockwise carriers. It controls the complete signed
interaction rather than assigning nonnegative energy to each transition.

This is a restricted-family theorem for the particular non-polyconvex G.
It does not establish quasiconvexity of G for arbitrary gradients, the complete
left-invariant quartic implication, or a new physical operation. Historical
originality and external mathematical review are UNKNOWN.

## The storage inequality

Define an invariant potential on the four-real-dimensional profile space by

\[
 \mathcal P(\alpha,\beta)
 =-\frac{16}{3}|\alpha|^4+\frac{25}{4}|\beta|^4
   -\frac{11}{6}|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2.
\]

The stronger pointwise assertion is

\[
 \mathbb E_y G(D\phi)-\tfrac12\mathbb E_y|D\phi|^4
 \ge \frac{d}{dx}\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta). \tag{1}
\]

For its proof, at each x use a constant output rotation and a constant
y-translation to make α=r and β=s real. The two phases can be fixed
independently by these two symmetries. Transform their **actual** derivatives
by the same constants and write α'=p+it, β'=q+ih. There is no differentiation
of an x-dependent coordinate change. At zero amplitudes any phase may be
chosen; the resulting polynomial inequality still applies. These symmetries
preserve G, the gradient norm, and the invariant storage derivative.

In this gauge the complex columns are

\[
 X=(p+it)e^{iy}+(q+ih)e^{-iy},\qquad
 Y=ir e^{iy}-is e^{-iy}.
\]

Set u=|X|²,v=|Y|²,j=Im(conj(X)Y). Let L=E_y G(X|Y), N=E_y(u+v)² and
P(r,s)=−16r⁴/3+25s⁴/4−11r²s²/6. Exact Laurent averaging yields

\[
 L-P_rp-P_sq-N/2=z_{12}^TQ_{12}z_{12}+z_9^TQ_9z_9, \tag{2}
\]

with

\[
 z_{12}=(r^2,rp,rt,p^2,pt,t^2,s^2,sq,sh,q^2,qh,h^2),
 \quad z_9=(rs,rq,rh,ps,pq,ph,ts,tq,th).
\]

The complete rational matrices are in `CONTINUUM_STORAGE_CERTIFICATE.json`.
Every leading principal minor is strictly positive. The separate executable
`verify_continuum_storage.py` reconstructs L and N directly from the columns,
checks every coefficient in (2) and Sylvester's criterion by exact arithmetic.
The certificate uses rational numbers with small denominators. Numerical SDP
optimization discovered a proposal; it is not a premise of (2) or positivity.

Because P depends only on squared radii, its derivative in this gauge equals
the derivative of the invariant potential, even when t,h are nonzero.
Equation (2) proves (1). In one dimension W^{1,4} amplitudes are bounded and
absolutely continuous, so the polynomial chain rule is valid. Integrating
over the closed x path makes the potential difference zero and proves the
zero-background bound. Polygonal paths are included without a density limit.

## Why every affine background is covered

Put X=Dφ. The half-turn identity X(x,y+π)=−X(x,y) kills the odd terms in the
quartic expansion, giving

\[
 \mathbb E[G(B+X)-G(B)]
 =\mathbb E G(X)+\tfrac12\mathbb E D^2G(X)[B,B]
 =\mathbb E G(X)+\tfrac12\mathbb E D^2G(B)[X,X]. \tag{3}
\]

The second equality is polarization of a homogeneous quartic. At each fixed
B, D²G(B) is a constant quadratic form nonnegative on rank-one directions.
Indeed the existing explicit G acoustic Gram is reconstructed in the checker
from the differentiated polynomial, its positive leading six-by-six block,
rank six and zero Schur complement; left rotational invariance supplies every
output direction. Fourier/Parseval for a periodic gradient now makes the last
term in (3) nonnegative: each real sine/cosine gradient coefficient is an
output vector tensored with the real spatial wave vector. This argument is
valid for the actual W^{1,4} gradient, hence also W^{1,2}; no inverse averaged
Hessian, finite Fourier cutoff in x, or bound on B is required. B=0 gives
equality in the affine infimum assertion.

## Consequences, scaling, and limits

For any strip joining two arbitrary complex profiles in this family,
integrating (1) gives

\[
 W_0(U,V,\tau)-\tfrac12\int|D\phi|^4
 \ge\mathcal P(V)-\mathcal P(U),\qquad\tau>0.
\]

It permits negative transitions. In particular it includes the source's
edge (5/4,2)→(2,5/4), τ=1/2, whose raw action is −1431/1280. It replaces
the finite 81-profile/five-width graph certificate by a continuous one,
and strengthens that family's 1/4 margin to 1/2. The conceptual advance is
the continuous, four-real-dimensional storage inequality, not the constant.
The original finite certificate remains preserved as an earlier exact result.

For any positive integer k, the same assertion holds with e^{±iky} and
potential k³𝒫. Insert kr,ks in (2): L(k r,k s,p,q,t,h) has storage derivative
k³(P_rp+P_sq). Negative k merely exchanges the two carriers and the potential
accordingly. The assertion does not sum across distinct k: their nonlinear
resonances are precisely the missing interaction.

To reach arbitrary profiles one needs a storage functional on complete
transverse profiles controlling all such resonances, or a compatibility-
preserving reduction to already certified families with a justified energy
comparison. The source's finite-difference loop operation supplies neither;
it changes the gradient and can add unbounded connector cost. Density of
Fourier/polygonal profiles does not transfer a one-frequency certificate to
their sums. A failure of a proposed polynomial storage ansatz is likewise
not an exact negative compatible cycle.

The general potential/closed-cycle method is established variational machinery,
as are SOS certificates and Fourier compatibility. This record claims an
explicit internally checked certificate and its stated family consequence;
it does not claim historical priority for the method. The scope is materially
broader than a single fixed carrier or finite graph, but remains far from a
universal quasiconvexity theorem or a civilisation-enabling capability.
