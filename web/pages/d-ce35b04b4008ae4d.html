S01 CONSTRUCTIVE FINITE-BIT BRIDGE FOR S02'S STOPPED DIFFUSION
7 October 2026. INITIAL.txt is unchanged. Cross exposure was authorized.

STATUS AND ATTRIBUTION

This is a new mathematical compiler construction, independently derived after
auditing S02's stopped-diffusion theorem. It is not executed stopped-SDE code,
external validation, a fixed-gate compiler, or a practical runtime claim. S02
originates the positive stopped diffusion and exponential whole-state product
mixture theorem. The uniform weight-moment lemma and the discretization/bit
bridge below originate with S01. The basis-free drift simplification is also
in S02's independently written STOPPED_ADDENDUM.txt; it is shared independent
reconstruction, not an exclusive S01 contribution.

THE CONSTRUCTION THEOREM CANDIDATE

Supply rational symmetric A and vector b, public fixed rational M,B with
||A||<=M and |b|<=B, integer N, and rational 0<eta<1/4. For sufficiently large
N>=N0(M,B), there is a randomized finite-bit classical procedure which emits
a positive rational one-qubit density description and requests N copies of
that qubit from a native preparation interface. Its averaged N-qubit output
has trace distance at most C_(M,B) exp(-c_(M,B)N)+eta from rho_N(A,b).
The classical bit cost is polynomial in N, eta^-1 and the rational input
length, at fixed M,B. All uniform constants and N0 are computable from the
elementary bounds below. Native preparation costs N*C_native(eta/(100N),Lout)
and N physical emissions, separately charged. No arbitrary-real parameter,
normal-sampling, eigenvector, exact stopping-time, or normalization oracle.

This changes the known-parameter preparation interface; the unchanged
parameter-independent quartic decoder still has its N^-1/2 error floor.
For eta exponentially small in N, this compiler's runtime is exponential;
the theorem does not claim polynomial-N preparation at exponential accuracy.
For small N one can use the existing baseline compiler and its finite-state
error certificate; that does not extend this statement to an exponentially
small cone-distance floor at every finite N. A vacuous trace-distance bound
one is always available for omitted N<N0. The substantive claim is large N.

1. ACQUIRED COEFFICIENTS, GAP, AND CONTINUOUS INITIAL LAW

Put Lambda=M+1, Lc=2M+1, C=A+Lambda I, s=N^(3/4), and
r0^2=1/[16(Lc+1)]. On |m|<=r0 the exact generator identity audited in
S02_STOPPED_DIFFUSION_AUDIT.txt gives

 a(m)=(N-1)/(2N^(3/2))(I-mm^T)Cm+(I-mm^T)b/(2s),
 D(m)=(I-mm^T)C(I-mm^T)-[m]_cross C[m]_cross^T,
 V(m)=N(N-1)m^TCm/(4N^(3/2))+N Tr C/(4N^(3/2))+N b.m/(2s).

The exact covariance is D/(2N^(3/2)); 3/4 I<=D<=Lc I throughout this ball.
Use its ordinary 3-by-3 Cholesky factor F(m), rather than eigenaxes. All
Cholesky pivots have a known positive lower bound depending only on the
3/4 margin and Lc, so F and its first two derivatives have computable fixed
M bounds. Choosing this covariance factor leaves the diffusion law and the
generator unchanged. The stopped law is uniquely determined by its smooth
local coefficients. Numerical Cholesky needs only rational operations and
scalar square roots, with certified positive radicands and denominators.

The seed has t0=2/N-Lambda/N^(3/2)>0. Its exact heat-field radial density is
 proportional to r^2 sinch(r/2) exp[-r^2/(4t0)+N log cosh(r/2)],
with a uniform direction, and m_seed=tanh(r/2)u. Instead of replacing every
bad seed |m_seed|>r0/2 by zero, project m_seed onto the closed ball r0/2.
This changes its seed state by at most 2 pbad in raw trace norm, exactly the
same upper bound as the bad-to-zero replacement. Projection is continuous
and 1-Lipschitz, so no undecidable threshold comparison is introduced into
the numerical coupling. It cannot increase the seed radius, preserving
the weight-moment bounds. The explicit uniform pbad bound is in the audit.

2. A SMOOTH CUTOFF REPLACES THE HITTING-TIME NUMERICAL GATE

Let r*=3r0/4. Set chi=1 for |m|<=r*, chi=0 for |m|>=r0. Between them set
t=(|m|^2-r*^2)/(r0^2-r*^2) and chi=1-10t^3+15t^4-6t^5. Define globally

 A_N=chi a, Sigma_N=chi F/sqrt(2N^(3/2)), U_N=chi V,

with all three zero outside r0. The zero value and first two zero derivatives
of chi at the outer endpoint make these global C^2 bounded coefficients.
The scalar |m|^2 thresholds are rational; sqrt D is computed only strictly
inside the support, where its gap is known. There is no PSD query outside.

Their supremum and Lipschitz bounds can be computed by polynomial coefficient
bounds and the Cholesky pivot margin. Uniformly in N>=1 at fixed M,B,
 ||A_N||_infty+Lip(A_N)+||Sigma_N||_infty+Lip(Sigma_N)<=K_(M,B),
 |U_N|<=hN, Lip(U_N)<=K_(M,B) sqrt N,
where hN=Lc sqrt N/4+Lc/(2sqrt N)+(B/2)N^(1/4). These constants do not
hide N in a Lipschitz bound. The first bound retains the more useful
pointwise estimates |A_N(z)|<=Lc|z|/(2sqrt N)+B/(2N^(3/4)) and
Sigma_N Sigma_N^T<=Lc I/(2N^(3/2)).

The global SDE dX=A_N(X)dt+Sigma_N(X)dW has a unique strong solution by
the usual Picard iteration; the bounded coefficients provide the L2
contraction estimates directly. Started inside r0, it stays in the ball:
at the first boundary hit all coefficients are zero, and the constant
continuation is the unique solution thereafter. Its weight is
w=exp(integral_0^1 U_N(X_t)dt)>0. Every branch k_N(X_1) is positive.

Couple it to S02's original stopped process with the same local covariance
factor and initial projected seed. They agree until reaching r*. For large
N the integrated drift bound is <=r0/8, so from |m0|<=r0/2 reaching r*
requires a martingale excursion >=r0/8. The coordinate exponential-
martingale argument from the audit yields P(hit r* by 1)<=6exp(-c_* N^1.5)
with c_*>0 depending only on M. The uniform second moments proved below
therefore bound their raw weighted-state difference by
 (sqrt(E w_original^2)+sqrt(E w_cutoff^2)) sqrt(P(hit r*))
 <=K_(M,B)exp(-c_*N^1.5/2).
Their masses have the same bound. Hence cutoff normalization has only this
additional error. S02's raw target error remains Ce^-cN. This comparison
does not resolve or approximate a numerical boundary hitting time.

3. UNIFORM MOMENTS FOR BOTH THE SDE AND THE ACTUAL EULER LAW

Use m steps h=1/m. The unprojected global Euler chain is
 Y_(k+1)=Y_k+h A_N(Y_k)+sqrt(h) Sigma_N(Y_k) Z_k,
with independent standard 3D normals Z_k. Define
 w_E=exp(h sum_(k=0)^(m-1) U_N(Y_k)).
Euler nodes can overshoot the ball; coefficients outside are zero and remain
defined. We project ONLY its final endpoint onto the unit Bloch ball to emit
a positive kernel. We do not project each update or assume positivity of
the unprojected kernel along the path.

Let L=Lc/(2sqrt N)<=1/4, F0=B/(2N^(3/4)), and Q be the supremum of the
martingale part (continuous or discrete). Ordinary or discrete Gronwall gives
 sup |X| or max |Y| <= e^L(|m0|+F0+Q),
so its square is <=6(|m0|^2+F0^2+Q^2). The discrete noise has conditional
normal increments with each coordinate variance <=h Lc/(2N^(3/2)).
Thus exp(lambda M_k-lambda^2 q_k/2) is a nonnegative exponential martingale
in discrete time as well. Doob, optimizing lambda, and a six-way union give
 P(Q>u)<=6 exp[-u^2 N^(3/2)/(3Lc)]
for both laws, without independence of coordinate paths or m-dependent loss.

The cutoff potential obeys everywhere, including overshoots,
 U_N(z)<=(Lc/4+1)sqrt N |z|^2+Tr C/(4sqrt N)+B^2/16.
If V<0 multiplying by chi only makes it less negative; the displayed upper
bound is nonnegative, so the inequality still holds. Consequently for any
fixed p>=1, define A_p=p(Lc/4+1), beta_p=6A_p and
C_p0=exp[p(3Lc/4+B^2/16)+(3/2)A_p B^2]. Then
 w^p or w_E^p <=C_p0 exp[beta_p sqrt N(|m0|^2+Q^2)].
When N>=6Lc beta_p, integrate the Q tail to obtain, conditionally on m0,
 E exp(beta_p sqrt N Q^2)<=1+18Lc beta_p/(N-3Lc beta_p)<=7.

The projected heat seed has sqrt N |m0|^2<=R^2/4, R=N^(1/4)r. Section 4
of S02_STOPPED_DIFFUSION_AUDIT.txt supplies the explicit uniform seed moment
 J(K)<=Lbase^-1[e^(384(K+1/24)^2)1536^(3/4)/2
                         +e^(1/16)sqrt(pi)/4],
 K=beta_p/4, Lbase=(7/3)e^(-Lambda/2-1/12),
under N>=Lambda^2 and sqrt N>=8(K+1)/delta_HS. It follows that
 E w^p, E w_E^p <=7 C_p0 J(beta_p/4)=:K_p(M,B).
This is uniform over EVERY step count m. It closes the Euler moment gate,
rather than replacing a stopped process by an unrelated worst-weight bound.

4. STRONG DISCRETIZATION AND WHOLE-DENSITY ERROR

Couple X and the continuous Euler interpolation using the same Brownian
motion. Write barY_t=Y_floor(t/h). Because coefficients are bounded,
 E|Y_t-barY_t|^2<=2||A_N||^2 h^2+2||Sigma_N||_F^2 h<=K h.
Apply Ito to |X_t-Y_t|^2. In the drift split A_N(X)-A_N(barY) into
A_N(X)-A_N(Y) and A_N(Y)-A_N(barY); use 2uv<=u^2+v^2. The analogous
noise split costs 2 Lip(Sigma_N)^2(|X-Y|^2+|Y-barY|^2). Hence
 E|X_t-Y_t|^2<=K integral_0^t E|X_u-Y_u|^2du+K h,
and Gronwall gives sup_t E|X_t-Y_t|^2<=K e^K h. All K here are computable
fixed M,B constants from Section 2. This is a derivation of the required
strong rate; no uncharged Euler theorem or stopping-time regularity import.

Let u=log w and u_E=log w_E. Jensen in time and Lip(U_N)<=K sqrt N give
 E|u-u_E|^2<=K^2 N integral_0^1 E|X_t-barY_t|^2dt<=K' N h.
Since |e^u-e^v|<=(e^u+e^v)|u-v|, Cauchy-Schwarz and Section 3 give
 E|w-w_E|<=sqrt(2(Ew^2+Ew_E^2)) sqrt(E|u-u_E|^2)
              <=K'' sqrt(Nh).

For Bloch vectors in the unit ball, T(k_N(x),k_N(y))<=N|x-y|/2 by the
single-qubit formula and tensor telescoping. Orthogonal projection Pi_1 is
1-Lipschitz and fixes X_1, so |X_1-Pi_1Y_m|<=|X_1-Y_m|. Therefore
 ||E[w k_N(X_1)]-E[w_E k_N(Pi_1Y_m)]||_1
 <=E|w-w_E|+N E[w|X_1-Pi_1Y_m|]
 <=K_(M,B) N sqrt h.
The cutoff target mean weight is >=1-o(1), by its target raw error and the
target trace lower bound one. It is >=1/2 for computably sufficiently large
N. Choose m>=K_(M,B)^2 N^2 eta^-2 with enough constant slack. The Euler
mean is then >=1/4, and normalization changes the distance by at most a
constant times that same raw error. Allocate <=eta/10 to this step.

5. IMPORTANCE RESAMPLING: R PROPOSALS, NOT exp(O(sqrt N))

Draw R independent Euler seed/paths, obtaining w_i>0 and endpoints z_i in
the Bloch ball. Select i with probability w_i/sum_j w_j, then emit k_N(z_i).
Let mu=Ew_E>=1/4, muhat=R^-1 sum_i w_i. For a test function |f|<=1,
nu_hat(f)=R^-1 sum_i w_i f(z_i) satisfies |nu_hat(f)|<=muhat. Thus

 |E[nu_hat(f)/muhat]-E[w_E f]/mu|
 =|E[nu_hat(f)(1/muhat-1/mu)]|
 <=E|muhat-mu|/mu<=sqrt(Var(w_E)/R)/mu.

There is no bad denominator event, since weights are strictly positive.
The same argument is valid for quantum tests on emitted products. With
K_2 from Section 3, R=O_(M,B)(eta^-2) allocates <=eta/10. This is a law of
the single randomized output, not concentration of one fixed empirical
measure in an uncountable state space.

6. FINITE RANDOM BITS, GUARDED OPERATIONS, AND THE COST

The seed draw is an ACQUIRED baseline component, not new here: the exact
heat-seed rejection construction and its interval coupling are in
work/cycle1/spin_finite_precision_compiler.txt Sections on seed sampling,
and its repairs in spin_finite_precision_audit.txt. Replace baseline M by
Lambda. That procedure samples this identical heat-field radial law, has a
known fixed positive acceptance lower bound for large N, capped proposal
count Kseed=O_(M)(log(R/eta)), and finite-bit seed-decision disagreement
bounded by a sum of explicit tolerances. Choose those tolerances so the
probability ANY of the R seeds disagrees under its continuous coupling is
<=eta/100. Its guarded algebraic scale and tanh computation also provide a
declared pointwise Bloch error, rather than merely quantum closeness of a
nonunique decomposition. Initial radius projection is 1-Lipschitz. Use a
certified LOWER radius r0/2 when realizing it to retain exact positivity.

There are at most d=3R(m+Kseed) scalar Gaussian draws. Choose a rational
L>=sqrt(2 log(200d/eta)); 2d exp(-L^2/2)<=eta/100. Use the baseline's
endpoint-safe clipped inverse-CDF routine. On the retained event every
Gaussian coordinate is within a chosen xi of its ideal value. The routine
uses a dyadic uniform mesh <=xi phi(L)/32, certified CDF intervals of the
same accuracy, and stops at an ambiguous comparison with a returned xi-
close inverse. Its Taylor/range-reduced elementary CDF construction has
polynomial bit work in P+L^2, where P=log2(xi^-1); no exact comparison is
silently assumed. The tail, including clipped endpoints, is charged once.

Global finite-path stability needs only a conservative bound. On |Z|<=sqrt3 L,
one Euler update has Lipschitz factor <=1+h K+sqrt h K sqrt3 L. The product
over m steps is <=exp[K+sqrt3 K L sqrt m]=:S. Per-step normal/coefficient/
rounding error xi, with completed-update guard multipliers included, gives
endpoint error <=K(m+sqrt m L)S xi; the same bound controls every node.
Potential quadrature error is <=K sqrt N times the node error plus its own
evaluation tolerance. Set xi small enough that every endpoint has error
<=eta/(100N) and every accumulated logweight error <=eta/100. A sufficient
working precision is

 P=O_(M,B)(L sqrt m+sqrt N+Lin+log(NmR/eta)).

The sqrt N term permits fully conservative exp/logweight guard ranges; it
does not require computing any success probability e^-sqrtN. P is polynomial
in N, eta^-1 and Lin after the m,R choices. All coefficients at rational
nodes are rational polynomials followed by guarded scalar roots; the known
Cholesky margin handles their denominators. Outside r0 they are zero.
Rational squared radii decide cutoff regions. The finite-precision endpoint
projection is computed with a certified norm upper bound and, if needed,
a slight inward rescaling; this gives an exactly positive rational Bloch
vector and preserves its declared O(xi) error. No eigenaxis gaps are needed.

For logweights u_i, subtract max u_i before exponentiation. The exact shifted
weights lie in [e^-2hN,1] and their sum is >=1. Certified relative logweight
errors eta/100 change the categorical law by O(eta/100). Approximate their
normalization by rational intervals, normalize positive rational midpoints,
then round the first R-1 probabilities down to a common dyadic denominator;
assign the residual to the last index. With q>=log2(100R/eta), total variation
error is <=R 2^-q. One q-bit uniform integer and exact cumulative comparisons
sample this law. This is bounded finite-bit selection, with no rejection
tail or exact real categorical oracle.

On the common retained/coupled event, endpoint positivity makes each output
valid and endpoint errors cost <=eta/100 in trace distance after tensoring.
Weight and categorical errors have the same explicit allocations. Outside
that event, emitted branches remain positive (the code construction always
projects), so total trace-distance cost is at most its probability. Allocate
native local preparation error eta/(100N), which tensors to <=eta/100.
The sum of seed exhaustion/disagreement, Gaussian tail, numerical branches,
weights/selection, native preparation, resampling and Euler allocations is
<eta; unused slack may absorb rational ceilings and conservative constants.

There are O_(M,B)(R(m+Kseed))=O_(M,B)(N^2 eta^-4+eta^-2 log(1/eta))
scalar draws and constant-size matrix updates, plus N native emissions.
Each update and CDF/root/exp evaluation has polynomial bit cost in P+Lin+L^2.
The procedure can retain O(R) endpoint/weight records and only one Euler path
at a time; it does not require O(Rm) stored paths. Input reading/certification,
seed generation, elementary evaluations, all random bits, dyadic selection,
projection, N local preparations and emissions are charged. Fixed M,B constants
are large, with no asserted useful numerical threshold or hardware advantage.
