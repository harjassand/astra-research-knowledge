1. The fixed-coefficient theorem
Retain
\[
J_r(A)=\alpha H(A)+\beta u_A^2+j_A^2+r u_Aj_A,
\]
where
\[
\alpha=\frac{3r}{8(r+2)},\qquad
\beta=\frac{r(r-1)}6,\qquad
t=\frac{2r+1}{3},\qquad r>0.
\]
For any matrix \(B\), write
\[
u_B=|Be_1|^2,\quad v_B=|Be_2|^2,\quad
w_B=(Be_1)\cdot(Be_2),\quad j_B=\det B.
\]
Define
\[
k_*(r)=
\frac{4(r+2)}
{3\bigl(\sqrt{r^2+8r+4}+r+2\bigr)}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
L_{r,k}(B)=
\begin{pmatrix}
t^2u_B+(1-k)v_B+2t j_B&k w_B\\
k w_B&(1-k)u_B+v_B-2j_B
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\]
Theorem A — Quasiconvexity on an explicit background cone
For every \(r>0\),
\[
\boxed{
L_{r,k_*}(B)\succeq0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_\Omega[J_r(B+D\phi)-J_r(B)]\ge0
}
\]
for every bounded Lipschitz planar domain \(\Omega\) and every
\[
\phi\in W^{1,4}_0(\Omega;\mathbb R^2).
\]
The periodic version also holds.
This controls all admissible perturbations at the specified background. There is no small-amplitude, finite-frequency, orientation-preserving, or invertibility restriction on \(B+D\phi\).
Every nonzero singular background lies in the strict region
\[
L_{r,k_*}(B)\succ0.
\]
Consequently, the certified set includes an open conical neighbourhood of the entire nonzero rank-one background cone. The zero background is also covered.
This is a sufficient background condition, not an equivalence with quasiconvexity. A background outside this cone has not thereby been shown to fail the variational inequality.
Theorem B — Quantitative singular-background coercivity
For each fixed \(r>0\), there is \(c_r>0\) such that, for every singular \(B\),
\[
\boxed{
\int_\Omega[J_r(B+D\phi)-J_r(B)]
\ge
c_r\left(
|B|^2\int_\Omega|D\phi|^2+
\int_\Omega|D\phi|^4
\right).
}
\]
The constant is independent of the domain and the scale of \(B\). It can be uniform when \(r\) ranges over a compact subset of \((0,\infty)\).
For \(r=1\), the proof gives the explicit bounds

2. The operation: translate differences of quadratic invariants
The construction does not differentiate energy along heat flow. It uses a supporting inequality obtained from the quadratic invariants of \(A\) and \(B\).
Let
\[
V=A-B,\qquad g=A^TA,\qquad g_B=B^TB.
\]
The algebraic identity is
\[
\boxed{
\det(g-g_B)-(\det A-\det B)^2
=
-\left|\mathcal C\!\left(V\operatorname{adj}B\right)\right|^2,
}
\]
where
\[
\mathcal CX=(X_{11}-X_{22},\,X_{12}+X_{21}).
\]
This holds for arbitrary matrices, including singular \(B\). It converts a determinant of quadratic-invariant differences into a nonpositive square.
Put
\[
z=
\begin{pmatrix}
u_A-u_B\\
v_A-v_B\\
j_A-j_B
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Delta w=w_A-w_B,
\]
and define
\[
M_{r,k}=
\begin{pmatrix}
t^2&1-k&2t\\
1-k&1&-2\\
2t&-2&4+2k+1/\alpha
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With
\[
\lambda_{r,k}(B)
=
(2+8\alpha+4\alpha k)j_B
+(r-4\alpha)u_B-4\alpha v_B,
\]
the universal identity is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&J_r(A)-J_r(B)-DJ_r(B):V
-\lambda_{r,k}(B)\det V\\
&\qquad=
\alpha z^TM_{r,k}z
+2\alpha k(\Delta w)^2
+2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{2}
V_{i,:}L_{r,k}(B)V_{i,:}^{T}.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The identity is exact for every \(A,B,r,k\) in its algebraic domain. The parameter \(k\) changes the representation, not the integrand.
When its blocks are positive semidefinite, this is a global supporting inequality at \(B\), affine in the minors \((A,\det A)\). For a compatible zero-boundary or periodic perturbation,
\[
\int D\phi=0,\qquad \int\det D\phi=0.
\]
The linear and determinant terms therefore disappear on integration. Quartic growth and strong \(W^{1,4}\) approximation give the complete Sobolev statement.
The determinant identities, null-Lagrangian principle, and supporting-plane method are inherited tools. The object supplied here is the family-specific exact translation, its positive parameter range, and its background geometry.
Why positivity holds for every \(r>0\)
The determinant of \(M_{r,k}\) factors as
\[
\det M_{r,k}
=
\frac{2(-3k+2r+4)}{27r}
\left[
9rk^2+6(r+2)^2k-4(r+2)^2
\right].
\]
The positive root of the bracket is exactly \(k_*(r)\). At that root, the first two leading principal minors are positive and the determinant is zero, so
\[
M_{r,k_*}\succeq0.
\]
There is also a particularly simple rational choice valid on the entire positive parameter range:
\[
\boxed{k=\frac23.}
\]
Its three leading principal minors are
\[
\boxed{
t^2,\qquad
\frac{4r(r+1)}9,\qquad
\frac{16(r+1)}{27}.
}
\]
All are strictly positive for every \(r>0\).
Thus the singular-background theorem requires neither a bounded parameter interval nor a sequence of individual rational certificates.
3. Why every singular background is covered
By left rotational invariance, a nonzero singular background can be written
\[
B=e_1\otimes(p,q).
\]
Then
\[
L_{r,k}(B)=
\begin{pmatrix}
t^2p^2+(1-k)q^2&kpq\\
kpq&(1-k)p^2+q^2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant
\[
\boxed{
(1-k)(tp^2-q^2)^2
+(t+1)(t+1-2k)p^2q^2.
}
\]
At \(k=k_*\), both coefficients are strictly positive. At the rational choice \(k=2/3\), this becomes
\[
\frac13(tp^2-q^2)^2
+(t+1)(t-\tfrac13)p^2q^2>0,
\]
because \(t>1/3\).
This proves positivity for every singular background direction, not just a coordinate representative.
Positive definiteness gives a quadratic lower bound proportional to
\[
|B|^2|A-B|^2.
\]
At \(B=0\), the positive invariant block gives a fourth-power lower bound. Homogeneity and compactness combine them into the mixed estimate in Theorem B.
The \(r=1\) proof is particularly short
For singular \(B\), let
\[
\Delta u=u_A-u_B,\quad
\Delta v=v_A-v_B,\quad
\Delta w=w_A-w_B,\quad j=\det A.
\]
The supporting residual is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
R={}&J_1(A)-J_1(B)-DJ_1(B):(A-B)
-\frac{u_B-v_B}{2}\det(A-B)\\
={}&
\frac18(\Delta u+2j)^2
+\frac18(\Delta v-2j)^2
+\frac14(\Delta w)^2
+\frac34j^2
+\frac14|(A-B)B^T|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Set \(V=A-B\), \(S=|V|^2\), and \(X=B:V\). Since \(B\) is singular,
\[
|VB^T|^2\ge X^2,
\qquad
\Delta u+\Delta v=S+2X.
\]
Consequently,
\[
R\ge
\frac{(S+2X)^2}{16}+\frac{X^2}{4}
=
\frac{S^2}{32}+\frac{(S+4X)^2}{32}
\ge\frac{|V|^4}{32}.
\]
This yields the explicit integrated margin directly. No Beurling estimate and no extra \(H\) subtraction are needed.
The previous shear-family decomposition transfers this result to every positive-coupling ROC member of that four-dimensional family. Combined with the supplied negative-sector theorem, this proves quasiconvexity at every singular affine background throughout the ROC shear-contact family. It still does not prove quasiconvexity at every background.
