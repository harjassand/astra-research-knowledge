# Every interior score weight admits an irreducible-frame collision

Status: **exact algebraic counterexample family**, with an independently replayable polynomial certificate of irreducibility for every interior weight. This is our deduction. External novelty/priority has not been established. It answers the proposed ordinary-frame-irreducibility repair negatively, including weights different from one half. It does not answer fixed physical local X/Y/Z frame identification.

## Exact interface and quantifiers

Fix any real \(0<\lambda<1\), and define the entire generally non-Hermitian weighted score by
\[
 -i[A,\omega]=(1-\lambda)\omega S_A^{\omega,\lambda}
                   +\lambda S_A^{\omega,\lambda}\omega.
\]
For faithful \(\omega\), this linear equation uniquely defines the score: in its eigenbasis every denominator \((1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j\) is strictly positive. Reversing the convention exchanges \(\lambda\) and \(1-\lambda\) and leaves the result below unchanged.

**Theorem.** There are two fixed, distinct rational faithful density matrices \(\sigma,\rho\) on dimension four such that, for every \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), two real symmetric observables \(A_1(\lambda),A_2(\lambda)\) have scalar ordinary joint commutant and their entire weighted scores coincide at \(\sigma\) and \(\rho\). The observables depend on \(\lambda\); the states do not. Rational \(\lambda\) gives rational observables and scores.

This is a counterexample for each chosen weight, not a claim that one unchanged frame collides simultaneously for all weights. The endpoint weights do not belong to the theorem; our endpoint frames are reducible, consistent with endpoint identification under ordinary irreducibility.

## Construction

All matrices below are real. Define
\[
 D=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 E=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 S=\operatorname{diag}(D,E),\quad
 G=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\3/5&4/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 L=S(G\otimes I_2),\quad \tau=LL^T.
\]
Let \(H_1=X\), \(H_2=Z\), and
\[
 R_a=S(I_2\otimes H_a)S^{-1},\qquad
 P=\operatorname{diag}(I_2,0),\qquad Y=\frac6{11}I_4+P.
\]
Since \(GG^T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&3/5\\3/5&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), its multiplicity matrix commutes with \(I_2\otimes H_a\). Consequently
\[
 R_a\tau=\tau R_a^T.
\]
The matrices \(R_a,R_a^T\) are block diagonal and commute with \(P,Y\). Let
\[
 h=\lambda(1-\lambda),\qquad
 K_a=\lambda R_a+(1-\lambda)R_a^T,
\]
\[
 B_a=\lambda K_a+(1-\lambda)\tau K_a\tau^{-1}
     =R_a+h(R_a^T+\tau R_a\tau^{-1}-2R_a),
\]
\[
 A_a=L^{-1}B_aL,\qquad
 T_a=L^TK_aL^{-T}-L^{-1}K_aL.
\]
These formulas use rational arithmetic apart from the freely chosen real parameter \(\lambda\).

The two density matrices are
\[
 \sigma=\frac{L^TL}{11},\qquad
 \rho=\frac{L^TYL}{11}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Tr}\tau=11\), \(\operatorname{Tr}(\tau P)=5\), so \(\operatorname{Tr}(\tau Y)=6+5=11\). Both states are faithful because \(L\) is invertible and \(Y\) has eigenvalues \(17/11\) and \(6/11\). They differ because \(Y\ne I\). Fully explicit forms are
\[
 \sigma=\frac1{275}\begin{pmatrix}
 34&9&12&12\\9&145&12&60\\12&12&16&16\\12&60&16&80
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \rho=\frac1{3025}\begin{pmatrix}
 479&54&72&72\\54&1970&72&360\\72&72&96&96\\72&360&96&576
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

## Hermiticity and equality of the entire weighted scores

Set \(q=2\lambda-1\). Directly,
\[
 \lambda K_a-(1-\lambda)K_a^T=qR_a.
\]
Thus
\[
 B_a\tau-\tau B_a^T
 =q(R_a\tau-\tau R_a^T)=0.
\]
Since \(\tau=LL^T\), this proves \(A_a=L^{-1}B_aL=A_a^T\), including at \(\lambda=1/2\); no division by \(q\) is needed.

For any symmetric matrix \(Z\) commuting with \(K_a\), set \(\omega_Z=L^TZL\), without normalization. Multiplying all terms by \(L\) on the left and \(L^{-1}\) on the right gives
\[
 L(-[A_a,\omega_Z])L^{-1}=\tau ZB_a-B_a\tau Z,
\]
\[
 L\bigl((1-\lambda)\omega_ZT_a+\lambda T_a\omega_Z\bigr)L^{-1}
 =(1-\lambda)\tau Z(\tau K_a\tau^{-1}-K_a)
     +\lambda(\tau K_a-K_a\tau)Z.
\]
Insert \(B_a=\lambda K_a+(1-\lambda)\tau K_a\tau^{-1}\) in the first expression. The two expressions agree because \(ZK_a=K_aZ\). For \(Z=I\) and \(Z=Y\), divide by 11 and multiply by \(i\):
\[
 -i[A_a,\sigma]=(1-\lambda)\sigma(iT_a)+\lambda(iT_a)\sigma,
\]
\[
 -i[A_a,\rho]=(1-\lambda)\rho(iT_a)+\lambda(iT_a)\rho.
\]
Uniqueness of each weighted score proves
\(S_a^{\sigma,\lambda}=S_a^{\rho,\lambda}=iT_a\).
This is a universal matrix identity for all real \(\lambda\), not an inference from sampled parameter values. The exact code additionally substitutes \(1/4,1/2,2/3,3/4\) and directly verifies both whole-score equations.

## Universal ordinary irreducibility certificate

Ordinary commutants of \(A_a\) and \(B_a\) are related by the invertible similarity \(M\mapsto LML^{-1}\), so their complex vector-space dimensions coincide. Each \(B_a(h)\) is affine in \(h\). Write the linear map
\[
 \mathcal C_h:M\longmapsto([M,B_1(h)],[M,B_2(h)])
\]
using 32 equations and 16 variables. Scalars always lie in its kernel. Restrict the domain by \(M_{33}=0\), leaving 15 variables; this restriction removes the scalar direction. Two selected 15-by-15 minors of this restricted map have determinant polynomials \(f(h),g(h)\) of degree at most 15. The saved exact coefficient arrays and row lists yield
\[
 \gcd_{\mathbb Q[h]}(f,g)=h^7.
\]
Therefore their only common complex zero is \(h=0\). For every \(h\ne0\), at least one minor is nonzero, the restricted map is injective and the full joint commutant is exactly the scalar matrices. In particular, \(h=\lambda(1-\lambda)>0\) for every interior weight.

Each determinant polynomial is reconstructed at the 16 exact integer points \(h=0,\ldots,15\) by rational interpolation. This is a universal algebraic certificate because the determinant degree bound is known in advance and 16 values uniquely determine a degree-at-most-15 polynomial. Extra exact determinant substitutions at \(h=1/4,2/7\) check the implementation; they are not the basis of the universal conclusion. Polynomial Euclidean division is exact `Fraction` arithmetic. ## Mechanism, prior-art boundary and consequence

The paired \(K,K^\dagger\) commutant criterion is stronger than the symmetric SLD commutant criterion, but it still admits the block projection \(P\) here. The crucial freedom is that \(R_a\tau=\tau R_a^\dagger\) permits a positive metric with off-block multiplicity correlations. The original Hermitian observables can become irreducible after the weighted reconstruction even while both filtered operators retain a common reducing block. This is the load-bearing obstruction to inferring filtered irreducibility from ordinary irreducibility at a generic asymmetric weight.

The physical-coordinate formulas simplify further. Put \(\Sigma=L^TL=11\sigma\) and \(H_a=I_2\otimes X,Z\). Then
\[
 A_a(h)=H_a+h(\Sigma^{-1}H_a\Sigma+\Sigma H_a\Sigma^{-1}-2H_a),
\]
\[
 T_a(\lambda)=\lambda\Sigma H_a\Sigma^{-1}
 -(1-\lambda)\Sigma^{-1}H_a\Sigma+(1-2\lambda)H_a.
\]
These expressions also permit a direct rational polynomial reconstruction independent of the similarity-chart derivation.

The state obstruction does not require an extreme spectrum. Exact inversion gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma^{-1})=3025/64\), so
\[
 \sigma\ge\frac{64}{3025}I,\qquad
 \rho\ge\frac6{11}\sigma\ge\frac{384}{33275}I.
\]
The rank-one coordinate measurement gives trace distance at least
\(|\rho_{00}-\sigma_{00}|=21/605\). Thus the state pair stays quantitatively separated and faithful for the entire weight range. Since \(\|\Sigma\|\|\Sigma^{-1}\|\le3025/64\),
\[
 \|A_a(h)\|_{\rm op}\le1+h(2\kappa(\Sigma)+2)
 \le\frac{3217}{128}\qquad(0\le h\le1/4).
\]
Scaling both observables by \(128/3217\) makes them uniformly norm at most one without changing their irreducibility or exact score collision. On any closed weight interval \([\delta,1-\delta]\), \(0<\delta<1/2\), continuity and scalar commutants give a strictly positive minimum ordinary commutator gap, while score identification still fails identically.
