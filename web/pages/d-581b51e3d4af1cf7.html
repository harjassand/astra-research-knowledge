# Exact gamma phase: a repaired contour theorem, not a gravity no-go

Date: 2026-10-06. Interface: the four-dimensional fixed-background shock/eikonal gamma fixture, not the complete dynamical gravitational amplitude or a finite-resolution detector amplitude. Source: Adamo–Cristofoli–Tourkine, arXiv:2112.09113v4 (2022-08-06), §2 Eq. (2.7), with conventional positive-transfer normalization. Bellazzini et al., arXiv:2512.13780v2 (2026-04-17), §§3–5, is the recent question motivating this calculation.

## Exact theorem

Fix G>0, 0<E≤q≤Q, a=log(q²/E²), C(q)=8πG/q², and

    U(g)=Γ(1−ig)/Γ(1+ig), φ_a(g)=U(g)e^(iag).
    F_+(s,q)=C(q)s² φ_a(Gs), Im s>0;
    F_−(s,q)=overline{F_+(overline{s},q)}, Im s<0.

These branches are holomorphic in their open half-planes, hermitian analytic, and formally crossing even under s→−s (crossing exchanges branches). U has upper-half-plane zeros at g=in, n=1,2,…, and no upper poles. F is a mathematical stitched fixture; the two branches have real-axis cuts, not an analytic neighborhood across the origin. The shared second-order small-s coefficient is C. This formal evenness is not the exact physical fixed-t crossing law: massless kinematics at t=−q² has u=q²−s. A physical crossing-symmetric subtraction variable and its shifted kernel require a separate reconstruction; the present theorem uses precisely the asymptotic 1/s³ kernel of the proposed large-circle gate.

Let A_R(q) be the counterclockwise full large-circle integral of the stitched branches F(s,q)/s³, with their boundary values at the real-axis crossings. Then

    K_T(a)=∫_0^T Im[U(g)e^(iag)] dg/g,
    A_R(q)=2πi C(q)−4i C(q) K_(GR)(a),                 (1)
    K_∞(a)=π/2−π J0(2e^(a/2)),                         (2)
    A_∞(q)=4πi C(q) J0(2q/E).                          (3)

Consequently, the repaired twice-subtracted coefficient identity is

    C(q) = (2/π)∫_0^∞ Im F_+(s,q) ds/s³
           + A_∞(q)/(2πi)
         = C(q)[1−2J0(2q/E)] + 2C(q)J0(2q/E).        (4)

The cut integral in (4) need not be positive: fixed-q imaginary parts are not the forward optical theorem. No physical positivity conclusion follows from this identity alone.

## Proof of the contour identity

On an upper semicircle, apply Cauchy to C φ_a(Gs)/s with a small clockwise semicircle around zero. The paired real segments contribute 2iC K_(GR); the small indent tends −iπC because φ_a(0)=1. Thus the upper large arc is iπC−2iC K_(GR). Hermitian analyticity makes the oriented lower arc minus the conjugate of the upper one. Since the latter is purely imaginary, the two contributions agree. This proves (1) at every finite radius. Crossing follows from U(−z)=1/U(z) and overline{U(overline z)}=1/U(z).

## Proof of the Mellin identity, including the half-pole

The classical Mellin transform (NIST DLMF 10.22.43) is

    M(σ)=∫_0^∞ J0(x)x^(σ−1)dx
         =2^(σ−1) Γ(σ/2)/Γ(1−σ/2).

Use 0<c<1/2, where the original x integral is absolutely convergent. The function e^(cy)J0(e^y) is integrable on the real y axis and smooth; Mellin/Fourier inversion gives

    J0(x)=(1/2πi)∫_(c−i∞)^(c+i∞) M(σ)x^(−σ)dσ,

with symmetric improper convergence. Set x=2e^(a/2). At σ=−2ig,

    M(−2ig)x^(2ig)=i U(g)e^(iag)/(2g).

Parametrizing the upward imaginary axis (g decreases from +∞ to −∞), its principal-value contribution to inversion is

    −(1/2πi) PV∫_R U(g)e^(iag)dg/g = −K_∞(a)/π.

M(σ)x^(−σ)=1/σ+O(1) at σ=0. Moving c down to zero from the right contributes +1/2 of this residue, not a full residue and not zero. Hence J0(x)=1/2−K_∞/π, proving (2).

Here the limiting principal value and the improper real integral are justified rather than assumed: for real g→∞, log Γ Stirling asymptotics and their differentiated remainders give

    χ(g)=arg U(g), χ'(g)=−2 log g+O(g^−2),
    χ''(g)=−2/g+O(g^−3).

For a in any fixed compact interval, θ'=a+χ' is eventually negative with |θ'|≥log g. Integration by parts of e^(iθ)/g then bounds the tail by O(1/(T log T)), uniformly in that interval. On vertical Mellin lines 0≤c≤c0<1/2, the same Stirling expansion gives amplitudes O(|t|^(c−1)), derivative O(|t|^(c−2)), and phase derivative of order log|t|. The tails are uniformly O(T^(c0−1)/log T). Separate a fixed small neighborhood of σ=0, subtract its 1/σ pole, and pass c→0 on the remaining compact segment by continuity; the pole contributes half its residue by direct integration. These estimates justify both the boundary limit and (2). They are uniform over q∈[E,Q] for each fixed E,Q, allowing smearing after R→∞. They are not uniform in a→∞ before R→∞.

## Exact smear remainder and scaling repair

For a real smearing ψ(q)=q f(q), with ||f||∞≤L, the normalized contour remainder is exactly

    R_E[ψ] := (1/2πi)∫_E^Q ψ(q) A_∞(q)dq
             =16πG∫_E^Q f(q)J0(2q/E)dq/q.            (5)

An elementary Bessel-ODE estimate suffices for a universal constant. Put H(x)=√x J0(x); then H''+(1+1/(4x²))H=0. Its energy H²+H'² has logarithmic derivative bounded in modulus by 1/(4x²). At x=1, the angular integral gives |J0(1)|≤1 and |J0'(1)|≤1/2, so energy≤2. Thus energy≤2e^(1/4)<4 for x≥1 and |J0(x)|≤2/√x. Therefore

    |R_E[ψ]| ≤16πG L∫_1^∞ |J0(2x)|dx/x
              ≤32√2 π G L.                          (6)

This is uniform in E,Q and proves that this exact fixture's nonzero fixed-resolution arc is an O(G) error, even with E=Q exp(−κ/G). It is consistent with Bellazzini v2's admitted approximate gravitational bounds, §5 Eq. (5.17), which retain O(GM²) corrections after appropriate dimensionless normalization. It does not prove those bounds for the full physical amplitude: radiation, recoil, detector matching, crossed-channel structure, and complex-plane remainder control are absent here.

For ψ=q and Q/E→∞, the error divided by G tends the generally nonzero constant

    16π∫_1^∞ J0(2x)dx/x
     =16π[−γ+Σ_(k≥1) (−1)^(k−1)/(2k(k!)²)].          (7)

The equality follows also from DLMF 10.22.39. Thus (6) cannot be upgraded to o(G) in general. An E-dependent unbounded f is outside (6).

## What changes in the proposed export

Bellazzini v2 Eq. (3.11) uses the pure phase e^(iaGs); for U=1, K_∞=π/2 (a>0), and its Eq. (3.12) averaged arc vanishes exactly. Retaining the exact four-dimensional gamma phase gives (3), not that exact zero. The endpoints a=0 must also be handled carefully even for the pure phase; q=E is measure zero for ordinary smearing. Our fixture supplies a constructive corrected dispersion with a uniformly controlled error at the paper's admitted accuracy. It is not a refutation of leading scaling positivity or an identification of the source's full stripped amplitude with this fixed-background fixture.

The decisive physical missing lemma remains: a full finite-resolution transition object with the requisite analytic/crossing branches, matching positive detector effects to its absorptive discontinuities, and a controlled complex remainder. Merely having a real-axis unitary channel or a pointwise-analytic soft-factor ratio does not supply this lemma. A locally uniform regulator limit would suffice for analytic inheritance; pointwise convergence alone does not.

## Stronger pointwise normalization obstruction, narrowly scoped

At g=iy, y=n+1/2, reflection gives |U(iy)|=Γ(1+y)Γ(y)/π∼2y^(2y)e^(−2y). Any fixed-q phase contributes only e^(−ay). Thus ordinary uniform polynomial boundedness fails dramatically. Moreover no zero-free holomorphic multiplier Z on the upper half-plane can make nonzero ZU uniformly polynomially bounded on an entire translated upper half-plane while retaining these zeros. If such a bound held, translation by iη and division by (w+i)^N would produce a bounded holomorphic function with zeros w_n=i(n−η). The Cayley images have 1−|ζ_n|∼2/n, violating the disk Blaschke condition, obtained directly from Jensen's formula. This argument does not apply merely to fixed-angle sectors, to an averaged-arc bound, or to the full amplitude whose eikonal approximation is only known on the real axis. In particular, it does not negate (6).
