## Reconstruction of the load-bearing argument

1. Smooth Fourier localization (F_ρ(x)=\int g_ρ(t)σ_t(x)dt) has ∥F_ρ∥≤C_1 independent of band width, and (F_ρ(x)ξ=f_ρ(D)xξ), where (f_ρ(D)) is a Hilbert contraction. After approximation and normalization, (y_nξ) has norm one, band radius (2δ_n), averaged response ≥2γ, and φ(y_ny_n^*)≤e^{s+1}. There is no uniform ∥y_n∥ bound.
2. For fixed (n), set (A_k=\intσ_t(y^*y)dμ_k(t)), (B_k=\intσ_t(yy^*)dμ_k(t)), and ψ_k=\intψ∘σ_{-t}dμ_k(t), where ψ(x)=φ(y^*xy). The Hilbert mean gives (A_kξ→ξ). The predual norm mean gives ∥ψ_k-φ∥→0. The varying test is legal because ∥B_k∥≤∥y∥² for this fixed (n); take predual error at most ∥y∥^{-2}. No uniform-in-(n) limit is used.
3. The ultrafilter-positive response meets the cofinite moment-good times. Strong-star continuity on bounded orbits gives a finite probability average (z_i=σ_{t_i}(y)), (p_i), with
   \[ \sum_i p_i\|Tz_iξ\|^2\geγ,qquad φ(A^2)+\sum_i p_iφ(z_i^*Bz_i)\le C=4+e^{s+1}, \]
   (A=\sum p_i z_i^*z_i), (B=\sum p_i z_i z_i^*).
4. With independent Steinhaus phases and (z=\sum\sqrt{p_i}ε_i z_i), the exact noncommuting expansion is
   \[ \mathbb E(z^*z)^2=A^2+\sum_i p_i z_i^*Bz_i-\sum_i p_i^2(z_i^*z_i)^2. \]
   Only the two pairings survive; their overlap is subtracted. Hence ᵓφ|z|^4≤C.
5. Polar clipping (z^{[K]}=u\min(|z|,K)) gives ∥z^{[K]}∥≤K and ᵓ∥(z-z^{[K]})ξ∥²≤C/K². The second filter fixes (zξ), restores the desired band, and cannot increase this Hilbert error. Minkowski gives ((ᵓ∥Tvξ∥²)^{1/2}≥√γ-∥T∥√C/K). A compact phase-torus maximizer selects one witness.

This reconstruction uses the state on ((z^*z)^2), not a trace and not an adjoint-error bound. The two different ergodic limits are essential to controlling the nontracial fourth moment.

## Derived finite-certificate compiler

This proposition starts *after* acquisition of the finite orbit/moment certificate. It does not need scalar centralizer or modular dynamics.

Input: a finite family (Z_1,…,Z_m) in a unital C* or von Neumann algebra acting on (H), or a specified domain closed under the clipping functional calculus below; a unit ξ with φ(x)=⟨xξ,ξ⟩; (p_i≥0), ∑p_i=1; (T) with ∥T∥≤L; positive certified γ,C; and a bounded linear map (F) with ∥F∥≤C_1. A supplied Hilbert contraction (Q) obeys (F(x)ξ=Qxξ) and (QZ_iξ=Z_iξ). The two finite inequalities in item 3 hold with (Z_i). If spectral localization is wanted, the input also certifies the range of (Q) lies in the target spectral band. All these services/certificates are charged inputs.

Choose a rational (K≥4L\sqrt{C/γ}). There is an explicitly enumerable family of at most ((4\max(1,m))^4) phase assignments for which one
\[ v=F\left[\left(\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}ε_i Z_i\right)^{[K]}\right] \]
has ∥v∥≤C_1K and ∥Tvξ∥≥3√γ/4. In particular a certified scan can output one with ∥Tvξ∥≥√γ/2.

Construction and proof: let (r=\max(2,\lceil\log_2 m\rceil)), (q=2^r≤4\max(1,m)), choose distinct (t_iin\mathbb F_q), and enumerate the (q^4) polynomials (a_0+a_1t+a_2t^2+a_3t^3). Map two independent binary coordinate functionals of each value to (1,i,-1,-i). Values at any at most four distinct points are uniform independent by the Vandermonde matrix. Consequently these phases are four-wise independent fourth roots; ᵓε=ᵓε²=0, so the second and fourth moment identities above are unchanged. Clipping and the contraction (Q) give mean squared response at least (9γ/16), hence some assignment has that response.

The conclusion is a finite exact operator expression, with certified numerical evaluation permitted. It is not an exact rational matrix claim: √p_i and polar clipping may be algebraic or computable rather than rational. With score error at most γ/16, accept an estimated score at least 5γ/16, certifying a true score at leastγ/4; some estimate is at leastγ/2. It takes at most (q^4) clipping/filter/score evaluations, (O(q^4m)) field operations, and the input acquisition/reading cost. Field construction can be charged by enumerating degree-(r) irreducible polynomials with elementary polynomial gcd tests, (O(q\,\mathrm{poly}(r))) bit operations. If supplied evaluation costs are (B_{\rm clip},B_F,B_{\rm score}), the scan charges (q^4(B_{\rm clip}+B_F+B_{\rm score}+\mathrm{poly}(m,r))). A general polynomial bit bound is **not** asserted without a representation and certified implementation of those services.

An independent random draw from this finite family succeeds at score at least γ/4 with probability at least
\[ \frac{5γ^2}{256C_1^2L^4C}, \]
because every score is at most (L^2C_1^2K^2), the mean is at least (9γ/16), and (K=4L\sqrt{C/γ}) (or charge the actual larger (K) in the denominator). This is a quantitative witness-selection statement; it supplies no missing orbit certificate.

## Conditional obstruction to a uniform averaging budget

Additional premise: for each (n) there is a unit (h_n\perpξ) in the (D)-band ([-δ_n,δ_n]), δ_n↓0. This is a spectral premise; it has not been inferred here from scalar centralizer alone.

Apply source Theorem 4.1 with (T=I-P_ξ), (s=0). (T) commutes with (U_t), so ∥TU_th_n∥=1 for every (t). There are (v_n) with ∥v_n∥≤C_*, ∥Tv_nξ∥≥η, and spectral bands ([ -4δ_n,4δ_n]). Set (w_n=v_n-φ(v_n)1). Then φ(w_n)=0, ∥w_n∥≤2C_*, (w_nξ=Tv_nξ), and its band is preserved because (Dξ=0). Set (x_n=w_n/(2C_*)); ∥x_n∥≤1 and ∥x_nξ∥≥η/(2C_*)).

For interval average (A_R(x)=(2R)^{-1}\int_{-R}^Rσ_t(x)dt), the spectral theorem gives
\[ \|(A_R(x_n)-x_n)ξ\|\le4Rδ_n\|x_nξ\|. \]
For each (R>0), choose (n) with (4Rδ_n≤1/2). Then
\[ \sup_{\|x\|\le1, φ(x)=0}\|A_R(x)ξ\|\ge η/(4C_*). \]
Thus the source's pointwise Hilbert mean convergence has no uniform operator-unit-ball rate under the additional spectral premise. Weak mixing or scalar centralizer cannot simply be converted into a finite implementation budget. This is a source-derived boundary, not a physical memory no-go.

## Primary-prior sharpening of the clipping constant

Substantive prerequisite reading of Haagerup–Musat, arXiv:math/0611160v3 (23 May 2007), pp.5–8, Eq. (2.10), Lemma 2.6 and Eqs. (2.15)–(2.20), plus p.10 Remark 2.8, gives a sharper scalar bound already present in that primary:
\[ (r-K)_+\le r^2/(4K),\qquad (r-K)_+^2\le r^4/(16K^2). \]
Indeed for r>K, maximize ((r-K)/r^2), whose maximum is (1/(4K)) at r=2K. Functional calculus and the positive state therefore replace the clipping error (C/K^2) above by (C/(16K^2)), with no tracial assumption. All other steps are unchanged.

Consequently the source's Theorem 4.1 witness radius (C_*=2C_1L\sqrt{C/γ}) can be reduced by a factor four to (C_*=(C_1L/2)\sqrt{C/γ}) while retaining response √γ/2. For the finite compiler's more generous response (3√γ/4), it suffices to choose (K\ge L\sqrt{C/γ}), rather than (4L\sqrt{C/γ}). At that exact threshold the random success bound improves to (5γ^2/(16C_1^2L^4C)). For a larger rational K, use the actual denominator (L^2C_1^2K^2), as before.

This is a verified prior-derived strengthening of the release's deliberately loose constant, not a novel clipping inequality. The already-executed finite fixture retains its frozen K=9 and the original conservative comparison; no rerun or stronger-constant execution is implied. The state/averaging acquisition boundary is unaffected.

## Why fourth-moment acquisition cannot be omitted

In (M_2), take ρ_δ=diag(δ,1-δ), (Z_δ=δ^{-1/2}|0⟩⟨0|), and (T) the projection onto its standard-space rare diagonal component. Then φ_δ(Z_δ^*Z_δ)=1 and ∥TZ_δξ_δ∥=1, but φ_δ|Z_δ|^4=δ^{-1}. For every ∥v∥≤K, ∥Tvξ_δ∥≤K√δ. A bounded witness cannot be recovered uniformly from only the second-moment response. These finite states do **not** have scalar centralizer; this is a counterexample to dropping the certificate from our finite proposition, not to the source theorem.
