1. GENERAL OVERLAP LEMMA, INCLUDING EXACT INDEX CONVENTIONS

R,A,B are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces; A and B need not have the same
dimension. Fix coordinate bases. For a matrix X:R-index x A-index, write
vec X=sum_(i,j) X_ij |i>_R |j>_A. Let P_RA and Q_RB be projections, and
choose HS-orthonormal matrices X_a and Y_b whose vectorizations span their
ranges. Put alpha=||Tr_R P||_infinity and beta=||Tr_R Q||_infinity.
Then

  ||(P_RA tensor I_B)(Q_RB tensor I_A)|| <= sqrt(alpha beta).        (1)

The tensor factor order throughout is R,A,B. To prove (1), define isometric
range inclusions

 S_P : directsum_a H_B -> R tensor A tensor B,
       (v_a)_a -> sum_a (vec X_a)_RA tensor v_a,B;
 S_Q : directsum_b H_A -> R tensor A tensor B,
       (w_b)_b -> sum_b sum_(i,t) Y_b,it |i>_R tensor w_b,A tensor |t>_B.

The (b,a) block of T=S_Q^* S_P, mapping H_B to H_A, is exactly

  T_ba=X_a^T conjugate(Y_b).                                    (2)

Indeed its (h,t) matrix entry is sum_i X_a,ih conjugate(Y_b,it).
For fixed b and input v=(v_a), operator Cauchy-Schwarz for the row
operator [X_a^T]_a gives

 ||sum_a X_a^T conjugate(Y_b) v_a||^2
 <= ||sum_a X_a^T conjugate(X_a)|| sum_a ||conjugate(Y_b) v_a||^2.

The first matrix is Tr_R P (entry h,t=sum_(a,i) X_a,ih conjugate(X_a,it)).
Summing over b and using

  sum_b conjugate(Y_b)^* conjugate(Y_b)
     =sum_b Y_b^T conjugate(Y_b)=Tr_R Q

gives ||Tv||^2<=alpha beta sum_a ||v_a||^2. Since both S are isometries,
||P_RA Q_RB||=||S_Q^* S_P||. This proves (1) without an invariant-basis,
recoupling, semiclassical or representation assumption.

2. POSITIVE EFFECTS AND A GENERAL STAR BOUND

The same proof holds for positive operators H_RA,K_RB: take spectral
vectorizations scaled by square roots of their eigenvalues. The range maps
S_H,S_K now satisfy S_H S_H^*=H_RA, S_K S_K^*=K_RB, rather than being
isometries. Their polar decompositions show

 ||H_RA^(1/2) K_RB^(1/2)||=||S_H^* S_K||
 <=sqrt(||Tr_R H|| ||Tr_R K||).                                (3)

Equivalently, the diagonal blocks of the Gram matrix for [S_H S_K] have
norms ||H||,||K|| and the off-diagonal block has the bound in (3). In the
identical-effects case, H on R tensor A with A=B and fixed bases,

  H_RA+H_RB <= (||H||+||Tr_R H||) I_RAB.                         (4)

This follows by bounding the two-by-two scalar Gram quadratic form. It
holds even without invariance and does not require H to be a projection.
For a projection P with Tr_R P=(s/d)I_A, where dim A=d and rank P=s,
(4) specializes to

  P_RA+P_RB <= (1+s/d) I.                                      (5)

For q receivers and the same P, pairwise (1) and the q-by-q Gram bound
give sum_i P_RAi <= [1+(q-1)s/d] I. If every reduced marginal has the same
P-probability p, then p<=1/q+(1-1/q)s/d. No q-output extension is inferred
from two-output compatibility.

3. ACTUAL COMPATIBLE CHANNEL CONSEQUENCE

Let Phi:M_d->M_d be Heisenberg UCP, tau-preserving. Assume an ACTUAL
Heisenberg UCP J:M_d tensor M_d->M_d with both marginal evaluations Phi.
Its Schrödinger adjoint J_* is CPTP in the forward direction. The normalized
Choi density Omega_RAB=(id_R tensor J_*)(|Omega_d><Omega_d|) has both reduced
marginals rho_RA=(id tensor Phi_*)(|Omega_d><Omega_d|), with the transpose
convention fixed by |Omega_d>=d^(-1/2)sum_i |i,i>.

For an effect 0<=H<=I on R tensor A with Tr_R H=h I_A, (4) gives

  Tr(rho_RA H) <= (1+h)/2.                                    (6)

No covariance of J is required. If Phi and H are invariant under a compact
irreducible carrier on A (and the conjugate carrier on R), Schur's lemma
supplies h=Tr(H)/d. In particular any invariant projection of rank s obeys
(6) with h=s/d. A bound is useful only when s<d; this limitation is explicit.

More generally let M>=0 be an invariant pair operator with smallest
eigenvalue 0 and Tr_R M scalar. For t>0 put H_t=(I-M/t)_+. This is an
invariant positive contraction, and H_t>=I-M/t by spectral calculus.
Combining with (6) yields the exact spectral-mass lower bound

 Tr(rho_RA M) >= (t/2)[1-Tr(H_t)/d].                            (7)

Negative right sides carry no force and can be replaced by 0. This is a
statement about an actual two-output Choi extension, not just CP positivity.

4. MULTIPLICITY-FREE FRAME AND SYMMETRIC SU(m) SPECIALIZATION

For a real Hermitian irreducible conjugation sector W_l of rank r_l, take
a tau-orthonormal basis B_l,a and form the invariant pair score

 F_l=sum_a B_l,a^T tensor B_l,a,
 M_l=I-F_l/r_l.

The k=0 maximally entangled vector has F_l-eigenvalue r_l. With the exact
mode conventions in SU_M_MODE_IDENTITY_PROOF.txt, on the pair sector W_k
one has F_l/r_l=eta_k(l). Positivity M_l>=0 follows from eta_k(l)<=1,
which also follows because D_l=sum_a B_l,a X B_l,a/r_l is UCP and acts by
eta_l(k)=eta_k(l) on W_k. The Choi marginal expectation is

  Tr(rho_RA M_l)=1-lambda_l.

For H_N=Sym^N(C^m), r_k and eta_k(l) are exactly those in the banked SU(m)
proof. Define

 R_(m,N,l)(t) = (1/d_N) sum_(k=0..N) r_k [1-(1-eta_k(l))/t]_+.

Then every actual compatible covariant bistochastic channel satisfies

  1-lambda_l >= (t/2)[1-R_(m,N,l)(t)]_+ for every t>0.            (8)

This is an explicit scalar necessary constraint on ACTUAL compatibility.
It is stronger information than merely saying a covariant CP channel has
lambda_l=sum_k p_k eta_k(l) for an arbitrary probability vector p.

For a cumulative projection P_<=K, telescoping ranks give
rank(P_<=K)=sum_(k<=K)r_k=d_K^2, where d_K=binom(K+m-1,m-1).
Hence any actual marginal environment weights satisfy

  sum_(k<=K) p_k <= (1+d_K^2/d_N)/2.                           (9)

If h_l=min_(k>K)(1-eta_k(l)), then CP contraction gives nonnegative
individual deficits, and (9) implies

  1-lambda_l >= [1-d_K^2/d_N]_+ h_l/2.                        (10)

The exact spectral-mass formula (8) is preferable when near-1 modes are
not a consecutive low-degree band.

7. AN UNCONDITIONAL LARGE-SECTOR THEOREM, WITHOUT RECOUPLING

Root independently audited Sections1--6 above and found no overlap/star
flaw. Root also supplied the exact orthogonality split below. The following
proof gives a broader statement which does not require a scalar 6j table,
a multiplicity-free carrier, connectedness, or covariance of the extension.
It does require the actual extension and the supplied covariance/frame.

Let U be an irreducible unitary carrier on H of dimension d, for ANY compact
group. Let W be a real invariant subspace of Hermitian matrices, dimension
r, with a Hermitian tau-orthonormal basis B_1,...,B_r. Assume Phi is UCP,
tau-preserving and ACTUALLY two-output compatible as in Section3. Define

  lambda_bar=(1/r)sum_a tau(B_a Phi(B_a)).

Then, for every 0<t<1,

  1-lambda_bar >= (t/2)[1-d/(r(1-t)^2)]_+.                     (12)

In particular,

  r>=8d  implies  lambda_bar<=7/8.                            (13)

Proof. Invariance and irreducibility give sum_a B_a^2=rI, since this sum
commutes with U and its tau trace is r. Therefore D(X)=sum_a B_a X B_a/r
is unital, tau-preserving, self-adjoint CP. Its normalized-L2 operator norm
is at most1 by Kadison--Schwarz and tau preservation. Vectorization of D
is exactly F/r, with F=sum_a B_a^T tensor B_a. This identification requires
no decomposition of the conjugation representation. Thus M=I-F/r>=0,
and F/r is Hermitian with all eigenvalues in[-1,1]. The maximally entangled
identity vector is a 1-eigenvector. The invariant effect H_t=(I-M/t)_+
is a function of F/r. Consequently Tr_R H_t=(TrH_t/d)I.

Every eigenvalue f contributing to H_t satisfies f>1-t>0. The elementary
scalar estimate [1-(1-f)/t]_+ <= f^2/(1-t)^2 yields

  TrH_t <= Tr(F/r)^2/(1-t)^2 = d^2/[r(1-t)^2].

The last equality is exact:
Tr(F^2)=sum_(a,b)Tr(B_a^T B_b^T)Tr(B_a B_b)=d^2r.
Section3's actual compatibility bound now proves (12). At t=1/2 and
r>=8d the spectral-mass term is at most1/2, giving 1-lambda_bar>=1/8.
The proof is finite-dimensional and does not import a recoupling identity.

If Phi is additionally group-covariant and self-adjoint on normalized L2,
its real Hermitian eigenspaces are group invariant. For any irreducible
real conjugation constituent of dimension r>=8d inside such an eigenspace,
Phi acts there by a real eigenvalue lambda, so (13) gives lambda<=7/8.
Thus if H_large is the sum of isotypic spaces whose irreducible real
constituents all have dimension at least8d,

  (I-Phi)|H_large >= (1/8) I_H_large.                          (14)

Multiplicity spaces are allowed: diagonalize the self-adjoint covariance
matrix there and select an invariant real irreducible copy in each
scalar eigenspace. The proof uses the real dimensions of Hermitian
constituents, not the dimension of the entire isotypic space. Combining
many low-dimensional copies does not license the criterion (14).

Choose any rank-one pure seed and its twirled measure-and-prepare channel
Psi(X)=d integral Tr(P_g X)P_g dg. It is EB, group-covariant, unital,
tau-preserving, self-adjoint and positive as an L2 operator; hence
0<=Psi<=I. Covariance preserves H_large and therefore, on H_large,

  (I-Psi)^2 <= I <= 8(I-Phi).                                 (15)

Unlike the earlier scalar all-mode proof, no commutation of the Phi and
Psi multiplicity matrices is needed for (15). They may mix each isotypic
multiplicity space independently. The same common pure-orbit measurement
therefore gives half trace error at most sqrt(8 theta r_lik)/2 for a
bounded tracial likelihood family supported in H_large with radius
||H_x||_(2,tau)^2<=r_lik and relative Dirichlet bound
< H_x,(I-Phi)H_x > <= theta ||H_x||_2^2. This is only the supplied
large-sector support promise, not the unrestricted capacity interface.

For Sym^N(C^m), every W_l has the exact real dimension r_l in the banked
proof. Thus (15) covers ALL m,N jointly on the supplied band r_l>=8d_N,
using the SAME canonical coherent Psi_N. It also applies to high-dimensional
real constituents of regular SU3 carriers, without a scalar multiplicity-
free hypothesis. Lower-dimensional sectors remain the exact missing gate.
No claim that (12)--(15) are historically new is made.

9. NONEMPTY REGULAR SU3 COROLLARY AND A GENERAL RANK RATIO

The threshold8d in Section7 was a convenient choice, not an intrinsic
boundary. For any supplied kappa>1, set t=1-kappa^(-1/4). Equation(12)
shows that every invariant Hermitian eigen-copy of rank r>=kappa d obeys

  1-lambda >= (1-kappa^(-1/4))(1-kappa^(-1/2))/2.               (16)

Thus the squared orbit-EB constant is at most
2/[(1-kappa^(-1/4))(1-kappa^(-1/2))]. In particular the simpler rational
choice r>=4d,t=1/4 gives 1-lambda>=5/72>1/16, so

  (I-Psi)^2 <=16(I-Phi) on that eigen-copy.                    (17)

This refinement makes the regular SU3 corollary nonempty. Merely saying
"high sectors of regular SU3" at the8d threshold might cover no self-dual
highest sector, since its ratio approaches8 from below; we charge this
boundary explicitly rather than presuming that band is populated.

Let R=V_(a,b) be a regular SU3 carrier, a,b>=1, and put s=a+b. The Weyl
dimension formula, with the three positive roots alpha1,alpha2,alpha1+alpha2,
gives d=(a+1)(b+1)(s+2)/2. The complex conjugation representation is
V_(a,b) tensor V_(b,a). Its highest constituent V_(s,s) has multiplicity
one: the highest-weight line in the tensor product is the tensor product
of its two highest-weight lines, and every other constituent has lower
highest weight. Its exact complex dimension is r=(s+1)^3.

Hermitian adjoint is an anti-linear equivariant involution on End(R).
V_(s,s) is self-dual and has multiplicity one, so adjoint preserves this
constituent. Its fixed real Hermitian form therefore has real dimension r.
Any covariant Hermiticity-preserving Phi is scalar there; the eigenvalue
is real because this real Hermitian form is preserved. Full-algebra L2
self-adjointness is NOT needed for this particular multiplicity-one sector.
The same reasoning makes every orbit measure-and-prepare Psi scalar there,
with multiplier in[0,1].

Since (a+1)(b+1)<=(s+2)^2/4,

  r/d >=8[(s+1)/(s+2)]^3.

For s>=3 this is at least512/125>4. Hence every regular carrier except
V_(1,1) has r>=4d, and every ACTUAL compatible covariant bistochastic
marginal on that carrier satisfies(17) on V_(s,s), using ANY one pure-orbit
EB comparator. For V_(1,1), r/d=27/8>3; taking t=1/4 directly in(12)
gives 1-lambda>=1/24, so squared constant24 suffices. Therefore constant24
uniformly covers this highest Hermitian score sector of EVERY regular
SU3 carrier a,b>=1. Constant16 applies as soon as a+b>=3.

This is a nonempty growing score space, r=(a+b+1)^3, with an exact supplied
support promise. It does not cover adjoint or other lower sectors; it does
not settle the matrix-valued low-multiplicity gate or the full regular-SU3
EB theorem. The same one orbit is used for all supplied likelihood states;
choosing one seed is explicit, but general finite exact cubature synthesis
and physical acquisition remain separate costs.

11. EXPERIMENT-LEVEL TRANSFER WHEN GROUP INVARIANCE IS SUPPLIED

Exact covariance of an unknown broadcaster need not be assumed if the
EXPERIMENT itself has the supplied group symmetry. Let E be a family of
rho_x=(I+H_x)/d with ||H_x||_infinity<=theta<1, and assume E is closed
under every conjugation U_g rho U_g^*. The representation U and this
closure are supplied; they are not inferred from bounded capacity.

Suppose one unknown-member-independent CPTP broadcaster B:M_d->M_d⊗M_d
has both marginal half-trace errors at most b on E. First average B with
its output swap; both resulting marginals are the same channel T, with
error at most b. Then group-twirl the broadcaster:

  B_twirled(rho)=integral (U_g^* tensor U_g^*)
                 B(U_g rho U_g^*) (U_g tensor U_g) dg.

This is one CPTP broadcaster and has an exactly covariant identical
marginal T_twirled. Since U is irreducible, T_twirled(I) is scalar; trace
preservation makes that scalar1, so the twirled marginal is bistochastic.
Convexity and experiment closure give the SAME uniform
error b, since for each g the argument U_g rho_x U_g^* is another admitted
experiment member. Its Heisenberg adjoint Phi=T_twirled^dagger therefore
has actual compatible UCP reverse extension. Twirling does not infer a
self-adjoint full multiplicity matrix; Section10 addresses that issue.

If the supplied likelihoods lie in the high-dimensional isotypic sum,
the same orbit EB comparator from(18) gives

  e_EB(E) <= sqrt(C theta b/2),                                (19)

with C=8 for8d, C=16 for4d, or the rank-ratio constant in(16). Indeed
<H,(I-S)H>=<H,(I-T_twirled)H>
<=||H||_infinity ||(I-T_twirled)H||_(1,tau)<=2theta b;
then ||(I-Psi)H||_(1,tau)<=||(I-Psi)H||_(2,tau) and(18) prove(19).
The same common measurement works for all members; no prior-dependent
measurement or unknown-state access is used.

Likewise the full fixed-m theorem SU_M_SYMMETRIC_PROOF.txt yields(19)
with C=C_m on the ENTIRE algebra of Sym^N(C^m), for every such
G-invariant experiment and all N. Its multiplicity-free covariance gives
real self-adjoint mode scalars automatically. For the regular-SU3 highest-
sector experiment, Section9 yields C=24 uniformly over a,b>=1, or16 for
s=a+b>=3. These are supplied symmetry/support subclasses of the original
broadcasting interface, with an unknown covariant broadcaster replaced

Such bounded-likelihood experiments already have a uniform capacity bound:

Holevo quantity for any prior is at most its mean D(rho_x||I/d).
Thus they are genuine bounded-capacity subclasses without a family-size
factor. This does not say general bounded capacity supplies bounded
likelihoods relative to I/d or group invariance.

The twirl is an existence/proof operation, not a claim of free acquisition
