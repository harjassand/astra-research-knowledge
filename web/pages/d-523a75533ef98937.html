## Exact target and new source interface

Imported sparse-PCP target: for the actual constant-promise-gap amplified hard Hamiltonian H, construct one common efficiently described isometry V, polynomial-row positive contractions C and B with P=VV*, ker C=ran V, C>=gamma(I-P), and alpha H<=V*BV<=beta H. Constants alpha,beta,gamma must be independent of instance size and amplification depth. Row enumeration, entry computation, coefficient bits, normalization and invalid encodings are charged. Positivity can remove the obligation that B preserve the encoded space, via the imported compression inequality, but does not construct B or C. Actual seed projectors include five-qubit rank-one Clifford projectors; their reflections need not be Clifford.

New source: OpenAI, *Polynomial-Time Unitary Synthesis from a Boolean Oracle*, October 5, 2026, pinned release commit adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a. Its main theorem: a uniform polynomial-size circuit A_n over H,T,T*,CNOT and one Boolean oracle can approximate every n-qubit unitary channel in full diamond norm <=1/2 after a suitable target-dependent unrestricted Boolean truth table is supplied. It provides no efficient classical acquisition of that table.

Sections 3--7 were read technically. The decisive reusable mechanism is Section 4 Proposition fields:sparsity: two nearly isometric Fourier field encoders have at most L nonzero entries per row. Their columns can be exponentially dense. They arise from a contracting block V=[[A,B],[C,D]], ||D||<=3/4, and feedback F(z)=A+BZ(I-DZ)^{-1}C. Matched encoders approximately intertwine F with V. Section 5 implements these encoders by arbitrary table-controlled column preparations and amplitude amplification; its eventual Fourier transform returns to phase coordinates. Polynomial circuit size is not polynomial sparsity of the circuit's full matrix.

The statements below prove something more precise than merely observing missing table construction. Even the exact Fourier encoders in the new manuscript can lack every positive constant-gap sparse enforcement operator in the same coordinates.

## The row-sparse enforcement obstruction

### General sharp row lemma

Let c be a unit vector supported on indices {0,1,...,m}, with

  |c_0|^2=p_0>0.

Let 0<=K<=I be Hermitian and Kc=0. Put a=K_00 and let N be the off-diagonal neighbors of row 0 among the remaining support of c. Define p_N=sum_{j in N}|c_j|^2. Then

  a <= p_N/(p_0+p_N).                                         (1)

If additionally K>=gamma(I-|c><c|) on this coordinate subspace, then

  gamma(1-p_0) <= p_N/(p_0+p_N).                               (2)

Proof. If a=0 there is nothing to show. The kernel equation in row 0 and Cauchy--Schwarz give

  a^2 p_0 = |sum_{j in N} K_0j c_j|^2
           <= p_N sum_{j in N}|K_0j|^2.

Since K^2<=K, sum_{j!=0}|K_0j|^2<=(K^2)_00-a^2<=a-a^2. Hence a^2 p_0<=p_N(a-a^2). Dividing by a>0 gives a(p_0+p_N)<=p_N, proving (1). The gap inequality tested on |0> gives a>=gamma(1-p_0), proving (2). No sign or phase condition on K is used. Couplings to auxiliary coordinates on which c vanishes do not enter the kernel equation and cannot enlarge p_N; they only consume part of the row's squared-norm budget.

### Peaked uniform-tail instance

Take

  c=(1/sqrt(2),1/sqrt(2m),...,1/sqrt(2m)).

If row 0 has r off-diagonal nonzeros in this tail, p_N=r/(2m), and (2) yields

  gamma <= 2r/(m+r).                                          (3)

Thus r>=gamma m/(2-gamma), and any fixed gamma>0 requires Omega(m) nonzeros in this row. The inequality is sharp at full support: K=I-|c><c| has gamma=1, r=m, and a=1/2.

This applies to an image with additional completely occupied orthogonal coordinate sectors. Specifically, if P contains |c><c| in one sector and contains all basis vectors in disjoint sectors, then <0|(I-P)|0>=1/2 still, and Kc=0 still. Arbitrary other coordinates and couplings are allowed; the same row bound holds.

### Exact realization inside the new Fourier fields

Let k=m+1 and d=k+1. On k ports and one internal coordinate define

  U_c = [[I_k-cc*, c], [c*, 0]].                               (4)

Direct multiplication gives U_c*=U_c and U_c^2=I_d: (I-cc*)^2+cc*=I, (I-cc*)c=0, and c*c=1. Thus this is a unitary block matrix with internal order s=1 and D=0. It satisfies every hypothesis of the field construction in source Section 4, for every truncation length L>=1.

All terms of degree >=2 vanish. In the source's Fourier coordinates, its forward encoder E maps

  port |a> -> |frequency 0, a>,
  internal |*> -> |frequency -v_1> tensor c,

where v_1=(1,1,...,1) in the specified finite phase group. The nonzero frequencies and zero are distinct. E*E=I exactly. Every row of E has precisely one or zero nonzero entries, although the internal input column has k nonzero entries. The image projection is

  P_E = |frequency 0><frequency 0| tensor I_k
        + |frequency -v_1><frequency -v_1| tensor |c><c|.       (5)

There may be many unused frequency coordinates. For any positive contraction K with ker K=ran E and K>=gamma(I-P_E), choose row (frequency -v_1, port 0). It satisfies the hypotheses of the peaked-tail row lemma, including all possible couplings to the unused frequency sectors. Hence its support is at least

  1 + gamma(k-1)/(2-gamma).

Taking d=2^n, k=2^n-1 makes this exponential in n for fixed gamma, despite E being a 1-row-sparse exact isometry on only polynomially many address qubits. No oracle-complexity assumption was needed for the obstruction.

The field lemma allows arbitrary k,s. Its prescribed recursion also has a variant meeting the large-order partition exactly: for any k>=s, choose an s by k matrix Q with orthonormal rows and first row c*, and set

  U_Q = [[I_k-Q*Q, Q*], [Q, 0_s]].                            (4a)

This is Hermitian unitary by the same multiplication. With D=0, the Fourier encoder maps port a to (frequency 0,a), and internal input i to Q_i* at its own frequency -v_i. The v_i are distinct, so every output row is still 1-sparse. In the -v_1 sector the image is exactly span(c), and the row obstruction is unchanged. For d=2^n and the source's prescribed s=floor(2^n/[4096(n+1)^2]) or 1, k=d-s>=s, so the construction obeys those field orders. Identity preconditioning is admissible because the block already contracts. This proves failure of automatic image enforcement even for admissible field orders; it does not assert that every target or every prescribed preconditioner necessarily creates this mass profile.

### Robust approximate variant

Suppose instead ||Kc||<=epsilon, with the same positivity, norm and diagonal lower bound a>=gamma/2. The row residual gives

  a/sqrt(2) <= epsilon + sqrt((a-a^2)r/(2m))
              <= epsilon + sqrt(r/(8m)).

Therefore

  r >= 8m (gamma/(2sqrt(2))-epsilon)_+^2.                      (6)

In particular, epsilon<=gamma/(4sqrt(2)) implies r>=m gamma^2/4. Constant error sufficiently below the enforcement gap does not repair the exponential row requirement. This is a same-coordinate result; it is not a lower bound against every auxiliary isometry or basis change.

## A different obstruction: table compilation loses the supplied-oracle interface

In source Section 6 the structure generator never computes target-dependent permutations, random sign choices, column amplitudes, Fourier coefficients, row-support lists or recursively prescribed matrices. Those choices are assigned to tables. Section 7 then stores phase-sensitive one-qubit approximation words in an unrestricted Boolean function. A circuit for a seed Hamiltonian does not automatically permit polynomial-time row/entry evaluation of these new tables.

The Fourier rows identify at most L possible columns by Newton power sums, but the row coefficients include sums over orderings of visits and products of target matrix entries. Recognizing the possible input list is distinct from acquiring these coefficients. Our proof of (3) makes no complexity claim about computing them and remains valid even if every coefficient and oracle bit is supplied free.

The usual Hermitian dilation of E, [[I,-E*],[-E,I]], is positive with bounded norm and a constant image-graph gap when E is exactly isometric, but its rows corresponding to the input coordinates inherit E's column density. Equation (3) excludes replacing that dense enforcement merely by another sparse positive operator with the same image in the displayed Fourier coordinates and constant normalized gap. A further encoding, not only a different choice of edges on these coordinates, is required.

## Inverse-polynomial precision is available conditionally

Constant error is not the decisive synthesis obstruction. From the source's step estimate, each of fewer than

  J=8*4096*(n+1)^3

levels adds at most 18(3/4)^L clean-workspace operator error. For desired 0<epsilon<1 take

  L=max(1,ceil(log(36J/epsilon)/log(4/3))).                    (7)

Then total skeleton error <=epsilon/2. The source power-sum label uses b=nL+ceil(log2(L+1)), K=b(L+1), and h=ceil(log2(L+1)). Summing its actual level costs gives

  qubits <= t+O(J(K+n+h)),
  slots S = O(J[L(K+n+h)+K+n]).                              (8)

These are polynomial in n and log(1/epsilon). Compile each slot with eta=1/h_eta, h_eta an integer at least 2(S+1)/epsilon. Its accumulated error is <=epsilon/2. The source's stated weak one-qubit length bound O(eta^{-3}) already makes the compiled resources polynomial in n and 1/epsilon.

A sharper internal deduction was independently developed by pde_gate9: retain the source's actual Solovay--Kitaev recurrence epsilon_next<=C epsilon^(3/2), length_next<=5 length rather than weakening the error decrease to halving. Once a fixed initial net has log(1/epsilon_0)>2log C, put x_j=log(1/epsilon_j)-2log C. Then x_{j+1}>=(3/2)x_j, so j=O(log log(1/xi)) refinements suffice, and the word length is O(log(1/xi)^(log_(3/2)5)), safely O(log^4(2/xi)). The fixed initial net and length constants remain hardcoded existential constants; target word selection remains inside the oracle. This argument preserves determinant-one words and the compiler's separate scalar-phase word.

For clean error epsilon=2^-B, choose the integer schedule L=4(B+ceil(log2(36J))). Since 4log2(4/3)>1, 18J(3/4)^L<=2^-B/2. Equations (8) become Q=O((n+1)^4 L^2) qubits and S=O((n+1)^4 L^3) slots, for no initial labels and L>=1. Compiling eta<=2^-B/[2(S+1)] with the stronger word bound makes gates, queries, width, address bits, and circuit-structure generation all polynomial in n and B. Thus even inverse-exponential precision has polynomial oracle-circuit resources, conditional on an unrestricted target-dependent table.

The overall clean-workspace operator error is <=epsilon, giving full diamond error <=2epsilon after tracing workspace. The skeleton's structure remains independent of the target. pde_gate9's independently owned full precision note is the companion source for its detailed cost composition; the elementary parameter proof here is self-contained.

## Combined area-law and repetition checks

Family265 PEPS theorem assumes fixed local dimension q, interaction norm J, and a constant lower full-system excitation gap Delta, with a unique ground state on an open L by L grid. It produces some PEPS of bond dimension C L^c and global normalized vector error <=1/L. Its introduction expressly supplies Omega to the initial party, and the tensors can depend arbitrarily on Omega. It makes no acquisition or contraction claim. Moreover, this excitation gap is not the normalized ground-energy promise gap of sparse PCP, whose hard instances may be nonlocal or degenerate.

The area-law companion exports positive quasi-local contractions k_i annihilating Omega with sum_i k_i>=const*(H-E0). The individual norms are bounded but there are O(volume) anchors. Dividing the sum by its possible norm loses an extensive factor. Their support after truncation at accuracy inverse-polynomial is polylogarithmic in lattice radius, with a Hilbert dimension and dense row support not bounded polynomially by the theorem. E0, Omega-dependent construction and truncation errors are also charged. This is no common positive sparse encoding of the actual amplified five-qubit hard family.

Family277 amplifies two noncommunicating players' games even for joint finite-dimensional entangled strategies. Its barycenter proof creates a legal shared vector by existence, and the game's fixed POVMs are not efficiently acquired sparse positive tests. Neither the original threshold rate nor our spectral improvement constructs the global Hamiltonian code-enforcement and test operators needed by the imported interface. Thus neither gap amplification nor row-sparse unitary synthesis composes automatically with PEPS existence into sparse PCP or continuum state preparation.

## Prior art and remaining alternatives

The elementary inequality (1) is a positive-contraction kernel row-mass constraint; weighted graph Laplacians and reversible-chain sparsification are close prior art. No newness is claimed without a targeted search. The exponential family283 application is independently explicit and differs from the imported tensor support/Markov-polynomial obstruction. It constrains exact same-coordinate image enforcement of a peaked rank-one vector, including a robust approximate-kernel variant.

It does not constrain arbitrary basis rotations: a rotation taking c to a basis vector gives diagonal enforcement immediately, but acquiring and representing that rotation must be charged. Additional auxiliary encodings that spread the heavy coordinate into many copies can evade the row-mass lemma because the encoded stationary vector changes. Those are active alternatives. A useful next target is a positive sparse auxiliary encoding of general matched row-sparse Fourier fields with a constant gap, together with polynomial-time row and coefficient acquisition from the amplified Hamiltonian input. The release has not supplied that service.
