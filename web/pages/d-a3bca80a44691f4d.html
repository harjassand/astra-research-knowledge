# Cycle 3: acquire linear stochastic dynamics by calibrated noise perturbations

**Disposition: retain a narrow experiment-design lead, subject to prior-art and physical-control checks.** This is a complete mathematical construction with a finite-data certificate, not a claim of a foundational breakthrough. The useful mechanism is to change calibrated noise rather than remove drift edges: covariance differences then obey Lyapunov equations with known right-hand sides. One new stationary condition suffices generically when baseline noise is independent; two suffice with arbitrary correlated baseline noise. The reconstructed proof does not rely on the September paper's unproved rank genericity assumptions. A hostile independent audit found the generic proof sound and supplied an adversarial obstruction to deterministic uniform designs.

## Exact question, resources and quantifiers

Observe all coordinates of an n-dimensional overdamped OU process

\[
 dX=(-AX+b)dt+\sqrt{D_0}\,dW,
 \qquad \Re\operatorname{spec}(A)>0,\quad D_0\succ0.
\]

Here D0 is **diffusion power**; the diffusion coefficient in the Fokker–Planck equation is D0/2. A, b, D0 and the graph are unknown. The baseline stationary covariance satisfies AΣ0+Σ0Aᵀ=D0 and mean μ0=A⁻¹b.

Supplied physical intervention: add independent calibrated white noise with a known symmetric positive definite power increment Q, leaving A and b unchanged. The perturbed covariance ΣQ satisfies AΣQ+ΣQAᵀ=D0+Q. All-state stationary ensembles, the OU class and intervention invariance are supplied assumptions. Knowing these assumptions is not an inference result of the covariance protocol. The almost-sure claim requires a continuous full-dimensional probe distribution; finite-bit control grids require separate rank checks or anti-concentration guarantees. Noise calibration supplies a physical time scale; uncalibrated increments leave a scaling ambiguity.

Acquired quantities: Σ0, ΣQ, their uncertainty, the moment-system rank/gap, A and the baseline diffusion within the supplied class. No graph, eigenmodes or rotation plane is supplied.

Main quantifier: **for every fixed admissible (A,D0), almost every Q in the full SPD cone succeeds** in the diagonal-noise case. Q is randomized after the fixed system exists, from a distribution absolutely continuous in the n(n+1)/2-dimensional symmetric-matrix space. This is not a single deterministic Q that works for every unknown system, not a theorem for diagonal-only noise controls, and not a finite expected-cost guarantee over all random probes.

## Theorem 1: one calibrated increment, unknown positive diagonal baseline noise

Assume D0=diag(d), d>0 in the measured physical coordinates. Define the square moment operator

\[
 P_{\Sigma_0,\Sigma_1}(A,d)=
 (A\Sigma_0+\Sigma_0A^T-\operatorname{diag}d,
  A\Sigma_1+\Sigma_1A^T-\operatorname{diag}d).
\]

Each output is symmetric, so the operator has n(n+1) scalar input and output coordinates. If P is invertible, its equation P(A,d)=(0,Q) identifies A and d among all real candidate matrices; then b=Aμ0. For every fixed stable A and positive diagonal D0, P is invertible for almost every SPD increment Q.

### Complete constructive proof of genericity

Let V=Σ0^(1/2), W=Σ0^(-1/2), B=WAV. Since D0>0,

\[
 B+B^T=WD_0W\succ0.
\]

Choose a diagonal T with distinct positive entries, sufficiently close to I that BT+TBᵀ remains positive definite. This is possible by continuity at T=I. For any ε>0, set

\[
 C=I+\varepsilon T,
 \quad\Sigma_1=VCV,
 \quad Q=\varepsilon V(BT+TB^T)V\succ0.
\]

Then AΣ1+Σ1Aᵀ=D0+Q, so the proposed Σ1 is the actual unique stationary covariance under this admissible noise increment. This witness is used to prove existence; the acquisition algorithm does not know A and cannot construct this witness from A.

For any competing parameters, whiten their drift as B'=S+K, Sᵀ=S, Kᵀ=−K. The baseline equation forces S=½W diag(d') W. The second equation becomes

\[
 SC+CS+[K,C]=2S+WQW.
\]

C is diagonal with distinct c_i>1. Its diagonal equations are

\[
 (c_i-1)\sum_j W_{ij}^2 d'_j=(WQW)_{ii}.
\]

The coefficient matrix diag(c_i−1)(W∘W) is invertible. Indeed W is SPD, and its entrywise square W∘W is SPD by the Schur product theorem. One direct proof is to regard W as a Gram matrix of independent vectors; its entrywise square is the Gram matrix of their tensor squares. Equivalently, for t≠0,
tr[diag(t)W diag(t)W]>0 after whitening W, proving positive definiteness. Thus d' and S are unique. Each off-diagonal equation then fixes

\[
 K_{ij}=
 \frac{(WQW)_{ij}-(c_i+c_j-2)S_{ij}}{c_j-c_i}.
\]

Consequently P is invertible at this SPD witness.

For fixed Σ0, stationary Σ1 is an affine function of Q through the inverse Lyapunov map. This map is invertible on symmetric matrices for stable A. The entries of P are affine in Q; its determinant is a real polynomial in the independent entries of Q. The witness proves the polynomial is nonzero. Its zero set has Lebesgue measure zero, so any full-dimensional continuous random SPD probe succeeds almost surely. This proves the theorem for arbitrary n and every fixed admissible system, rather than extrapolating from finite numerical tests.

### O(n³) reconstruction from acquired covariances

For a general acquired exact covariance pair, diagonalize C=WΣ1W=U diag(c) Uᵀ and set Z=UᵀW, Qe=ZQZᵀ. If the c_i are distinct, none equals 1, and

\[
 F=\operatorname{diag}(c_i-1)(Z\circ Z)
\]

is invertible, solve Fd=diag(Qe). Set Se=½Z diag(d) Zᵀ and recover Kij by the displayed off-diagonal formula in this eigenbasis. Transform A=VU(Se+Ke)UᵀW. Check d>0, stability, both moment residuals, and equality of baseline and perturbed means within uncertainty. This costs dense O(n³) arithmetic and O(n²) storage. These conditions are also necessary for invertibility: a repeated eigenvalue admits a nonzero commuting skew K with d=0, while a nonzero d in kerF gives S and off-diagonal K satisfying the homogeneous system. Because the true increment is SPD, C−I is SPD and no c_i equals1. This exact characterization and its converse were independently sharpened by the hostile auditor.

One additional stationary setting is minimal: without it, the baseline equation has n(n+1)/2 constraints on n²+n parameters. Physical admissibility is open, so small nonzero kernel directions preserve positive d and stability; b can be changed to Aμ0 to keep the stationary mean.

## Theorem 2: two increments, arbitrary unknown correlated baseline noise

With unrestricted SPD D0, acquire baseline Σ0 and two perturbed covariances Σ1,Σ2 for known SPD increments Q1,Q2. Set Γj=Σj−Σ0. Positivity follows from

\[
 \Gamma_j=\int_0^\infty e^{-At}Q_j e^{-A^Tt}\,dt\succ0,
 \qquad A\Gamma_j+\Gamma_jA^T=Q_j.
\]

Whiten Γ1 using W=Γ1^(-1/2), V=Γ1^(1/2), B=WAV and C=WΓ2W. The first equation fixes S=(B+Bᵀ)/2=WQ1W/2. With Q2e=WQ2W, the second gives

\[
 [K,C]=Q_{2e}-SC-CS.
\]

If C has distinct eigenvalues, K is uniquely recovered by dividing off-diagonal entries by c_j−c_i. For every fixed A and Q1, almost every full SPD Q2 gives simple C: the Lyapunov inverse and whitening are invertible linear maps, and symmetric matrices with repeated eigenvalues form a measure-zero algebraic set. Alternatively, C=I+εT with distinct diagonal T near zero provides an admissible SPD witness by the same continuity argument. Then recover D0=AΣ0+Σ0Aᵀ and b=Aμ0.

Two increments are minimal when n≥2 and no baseline-noise structure is supplied. With only Γ1 and Q1, take any nonzero skew Ω and replace A by A+ηΩΓ1⁻¹. The increment Lyapunov equation is unchanged. Replace baseline D0 by A'Σ0+Σ0A'ᵀ and b by A'μ0. For sufficiently small η, D0' remains SPD and A' stable. Hence both complete Gaussian stationary laws remain unchanged while parameters differ. For n=1 one increment suffices.

## Fully acquired finite-data certificate

This is a parameter-stability theorem conditional on the supplied OU experiment, not a goodness-of-model theorem.

Let \hatΣ0,\hatΣ1 be acquired covariance estimates with simultaneous operator-norm error bounds ε0,ε1. Let \hat Q approximate the calibrated increment with Frobenius error at most εQ. Build \hat P from the estimates using Frobenius-isometric symmetric coordinates (diagonals unchanged, off-diagonals multiplied by sqrt2). Let θ=(vec A,d), \hatθ any reconstructed parameter vector, r=||\hat P\hatθ−(0,\hat Q)||, and

\[
 \Delta_P=2\sqrt{\varepsilon_0^2+\varepsilon_1^2},
 \qquad \hat\gamma=\sigma_{\min}(\hat P).
\]

The covariance perturbation satisfies ||\hat P−P||≤ΔP because ||AE+EAᵀ||F≤2||E||op||A||F. If \hatγ>ΔP, then

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|\hat\theta-\theta\|\leq
 \frac{r+\Delta_P\|\hat\theta\|+\varepsilon_Q}
      {\hat\gamma-\Delta_P}.\quad}
\]

Proof: \hatP(\hatθ−θ) equals the estimator residual minus the covariance-operator and calibration errors at θ. Hence \hatγ||\hatθ−θ||≤r+ΔP||θ||+εQ. Substitute ||θ||≤||\hatθ||+||\hatθ−θ|| and rearrange. The same argument applies to the two-increment operator for A; increment covariance errors include the shared baseline error. Any certified conservative lower bound for the measured singular gap can replace \hatγ. The measured singular gap is the certificate; source/model rank assumptions are not silently acquired.

The direct SVD of an n(n+1)-dimensional operator costs O(n⁶) dense arithmetic and O(n⁴) storage; it is optional. A conservative certificate is available in O(n³) from the same acquired matrices. For n≥2, let a=1/λminΣ0, κ=κ(Σ0), h=||C−I||op, f=σminF and g=minpair|c_i−c_j|. Define ℓ=a/(2f)+(1+h a/f)/g, z=sqrtκ ℓ+1/f and β=sqrt[(sqrtκ/2+(1+h)z)²+z²]. Then σminP≥1/(aβ). Proof: for whitened residual norms r0,r1, the diagonal equations imply ||d||≤t/f with t=r1+(1+h)r0; ||S||≤(a t/f+r0)/2 and ||K||≤t(1+h a/f)/g. Recovering A costs sqrtκ, combining the A and d norms gives the displayed β, and unwhitening residuals costs a. For n1 omit K and its g term. This gives a measurable, inexpensive conservative gap that can replace the direct SVD in the finite-data bound. Both the auditor and our code checked this independently. For the known-right-hand two-increment variant, a cheaper conservative lower gap bound follows by whitening Γ1. Let g=min_{i≠j}|c_i−c_j|, c=||C||op and β=sqrt[(1/2+c/g)²+1/g²]. Then its original-coordinate moment operator has minimum singular value at least

\[
 \gamma\geq
 \frac{\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_1)}
 {\sqrt{\kappa(\Gamma_1)}\,\beta}.
\]

To see this, write B=S+K. The first whitened residual has norm 2||S||F. The second implies g||K||F≤r1+c r0. Combining gives ||B||F≤β sqrt(r0²+r1²); whitening residuals costs 1/λminΓ1 and recovering A costs sqrtκΓ1. This bound is conservative. Repeated covariance eigenvalues can leave a skew ambiguity even when the noise increment itself has distinct eigenvalues.

### An elementary acquisition bound without a supplied covariance scale

For N independent stationary Gaussian snapshots, use the centered unbiased sample covariance, with m=N−1. Orthogonal transformation of the sample index shows it has the same covariance law as m independent zero-mean Gaussian vectors. Wick's formula gives

\[
 \mathbb E\|\hat\Sigma-\Sigma\|_F^2
 =\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2+
               \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^2)}{m}
 \leq\frac{n(n+1)}m\|\Sigma\|_{op}^2.
\]

Markov's inequality therefore gives, with probability ≥1−δ, ||error||op≤ρ||Σ||op, where ρ=sqrt[n(n+1)/(mδ)]. If ρ<1, ||Σ||op≤||\hatΣ||op/(1−ρ), so the wholly acquired confidence radius is

\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\|\hat\Sigma\|_{op}.
\]

This explicit bound is intentionally conservative: n² and δ⁻¹ cost, rather than the optimal Gaussian operator-concentration dependence. It needs no supplied variance scale. Multiple settings use a union bound. An adaptive doubling schedule must preallocate a summable error budget to every covariance acquisition; repeatedly checking a fixed confidence bound is not valid. For each realized invertible experiment the certificates converge with growing independent data, but almost-sure probe rank does not imply a uniformly bounded or finite expected acquisition cost over random probes.

Independent stationary ensembles are a substantive resource. A single correlated trajectory does not give N independent snapshots. Burn-in, mixing rates, stationarity preparation, measurement noise, tail departures from the OU class, and control calibration have not been solved by this theorem.

## Exact physical obstruction motivating the protocol

Let A0=λI−c(e0 sᵀ+s e0ᵀ), s=(0,1,...,1), λ>c sqrt(n−1), D0=2I. Its graph is a strongly connected bidirectional star. Let a set of row knockouts include k leaf nodes. Define H by x0=0, sᵀx=0, bᵀx=0 and xj=0 for each knocked-out leaf. Then dimH≥n−k−3. Each baseline or knocked-out A0 acts as λI on H, as does its transpose; H is a reducing subspace. Its covariance on H is λ⁻¹I and its mean on H is zero.

If dimH≥2, choose orthonormal u,v in H and K=ω(uvᵀ−vuᵀ). Aω=A0+K has the same baseline and all selected interventional stationary Gaussian laws. The knockout rows of K vanish, and K commutes with each relevant covariance and annihilates its mean. Stability holds for every ω because the symmetric part remains strictly positive. Thus k≤n−5 cannot uniformly distinguish all competitors in this symmetric family; a necessary condition to exclude this construction is k≥n−4. This is an Ω(n) lower bound for these row controls, including adaptive choices based only on stationary laws: all observed responses are the same, so the adversary can retain a rotation in the remaining contrast subspace.

The baseline A0 is reversible. Aω creates current −K(x−μ)ρ, with entropy production

\[
 \sigma=\operatorname{tr}(K\Sigma_0K^T)=2\omega^2/\lambda.
\]

It is arbitrarily large despite identical stationary intervention data. This does **not** refute the September17 generic result: the true symmetric star is exceptional, and competitors can add leaf-to-leaf edges. A supplied complete star support would exclude those competitors; a supplied SCC partition alone does not. Complete support cannot be claimed acquired from the unchanged data.

### Independent hostile audit: no deterministic one-increment uniform design

The auditor `/root/check_wcrg` independently reconstructed the generic proof and supplied this stronger quantifier obstruction. For any fixed 2×2 SPD Q=[[a,b],[b,c]], take A=rI and D0=diag(a,c). Then Σ0=D0/(2r), Γ=Q/(2r). Let J=[[0,−1],[1,0]] and H=JQ⁻¹. HΓ+ΓHᵀ=0 while HΣ0+Σ0Hᵀ=diag(ab,−bc)/(r detQ) is diagonal. A'=A+ηH and D0'=D0+η(HΣ0+Σ0Hᵀ) preserve both covariances. Small nonzero η preserves stability and positive diagonal noise. Therefore no preselected Q works uniformly over all unknown diagonal baseline-noise systems, even with isotropic baseline drift. The system in this obstruction depends on Q; it does not contradict fixed-system randomized probing.

## Sharp statistical cost in a two-dimensional rotation

The positive protocol identifies rotation at exact precision, but high dissipation can be costly to quantify. Set A=λI+ωJ, baseline D0=2I, and a calibrated positive noise increment Q=diag(2+η,2−η), 0<η<2. The post-intervention power is diag(4+η,4−η). The covariance is

\[
 \Sigma_\omega=2\lambda^{-1}I+
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&-a\end{pmatrix},
 \quad a=\frac{\eta\lambda}{2(\lambda^2+\omega^2)},
 \quad b=-\frac{\eta\omega}{2(\lambda^2+\omega^2)}.
\]

Its eigenvalue gap is η/sqrt(λ²+ω²), shrinking like 1/|ω|. For λ=1,

\[
 D_{KL}(N(0,\Sigma_\omega)\|N(0,\Sigma_{2\omega}))
 =\frac{\eta^2}{128\omega^2}+O(\omega^{-3}).
\]

The expansion follows by δΣ having leading off-diagonal magnitude η/(4ω), and Gaussian KL=¼tr[(Σ⁻¹δΣ)²]+higher orders. Since N independent observations multiply KL by N, Pinsker/Le Cam imply N=Ω(ω²/η²) for constant-error discrimination of ω from 2ω. Conversely, the empirical off-diagonal covariance has bounded variance and the mean difference is Θ(η/ω), so a Chebyshev test gives N=O(ω²/η²) at fixed error when these two hypotheses are supplied. This is a sharp fixed-hypothesis acquisition rate. Their baseline entropy productions differ by a factor4. Detecting nonzero dissipation versus zero is easier and does not have this same lower bound. Unknown rotation orientation and model class can increase discovery costs.

A timed observation is a strong retaining baseline. On the rotation plane, lag covariance is λ⁻¹e^(-λτ)exp(-ωJτ). A supplied frequency bound |ω|≤Ω and a calibrated lag τ<π/Ω remove rotational aliasing and allow the principal matrix logarithm. Without that bound a single lag aliases ω+2πm/τ. Two incommensurate exact lags identify ω, but approximate aliases can be arbitrarily close at large frequencies; exact identifiability is not a uniform finite-precision guarantee. These elementary timing alternatives must be compared with the physical cost of correlated-noise calibration.

## Downstream use, prior art and novelty status

Use the result to design a scientifically discriminating experiment: randomize a calibrated additive noise covariance, acquire the resulting stationary covariance, and report a quantitative parameter certificate or unresolved small gap. The theorem can recover irreversible drift without pretending that stationary distributions acquire reversibility, microscopic reactions, or the OU hypothesis itself. This can support physical network discovery where trajectories or precise knockout mechanisms are difficult but external stochastic forcing is available. Full correlated-noise injection is a much stronger control than a single gene knockout and may be implausible in some biological settings; laboratory oscillator/feedback networks are more credible immediate targets.

The nearest recent sources and older prerequisites are documented in READING.md. Stationary Lyapunov inference, skew ambiguities, commutator inversion and probing dynamics with external forcing are established ideas. The minimal-setting protocol with **unknown diagonal diffusion, one calibrated covariance increment, and a for-every-fixed-system genericity proof** is our candidate contribution; the targeted search did not establish an identical prior theorem, but absence from that search does not establish novelty. No publication-level novelty is claimed. An independent auditor found Dettling's 2024 thesis §4.4 already jointly estimates drift and diagonal volatility with regularization; augmentation of the moment system is prior art. Anderson1969 and Hayden2014 concern output time-covariance/spectral realization. The stronger prior baseline is direct timed system identification with calibrated clocks and an anti-alias bound; the relevant comparison charges that additional information.
