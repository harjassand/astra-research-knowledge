# C4: symmetry assay for a hidden three-state cycle

## Result and interface

For stationary, independent replicate pairs from one time-invariant emission channel, empirical pair asymmetry distinguishes the reversible cycle from the specified directed cycle without estimating the unknown emission matrix. A finite-sample uniform guarantee requires a certified positive lower bound on the smallest singular value of that matrix. The robust lag is the first-lobe maximizer under the largest allowed symmetric relaxation rate.

The lower bound below covers independent two-time pairs at lag \(h\), plus any separate independent lag-\(\tau\) endpoint-control pairs. It does not cover observing the same lineage at \(0,h,\tau\): the joint three-time law can carry information beyond either pair marginal. That assay needs a separate likelihood calculation.

## Model

The hidden states are \(\mathbb Z_3\). The continuous-time generator has clockwise rate \((s+d)/2\), counterclockwise rate \((s-d)/2\), and diagonal entries \(-s\). Its stationary law is uniform. Under \(H_0\), \(d=0\). Under \(H_1\),
\[
d=\frac{4\pi}{\sqrt 3\,\tau},\qquad
s\in[s_{\min},s_{\max}],\qquad s_{\min}>d.
\]
Thus both directed rates are positive. The same unknown \(s\) is used under either hypothesis.

The emission channel is one unknown column-stochastic matrix \(E\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\), where \(E_{y,x}=\Pr(Y=y\mid X=x)\). It is shared across both pair endpoints and both hypotheses. The test assumes a certified margin
\[
\sigma_{\min}(E)\ge \sigma_0>0.
\]
Each data unit is an independent replicate initialized in the stationary hidden law and observed at times \(0\) and \(h>0\). The period \(\tau\) and interval \([s_{\min},s_{\max}]\) are known.

Stationarity and the time-invariant shared emission channel are load-bearing. With different emission matrices at the two endpoints, a reversible hidden chain can yield an asymmetric observed pair matrix. If \(E\) can be arbitrarily close to rank one, the observed skew can be arbitrarily small; if \(E=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3\), the hidden process is completely hidden.

## Hidden and observed pair skew

Let \(P_s^d(h)\) be the hidden transition matrix. Its nontrivial eigenvalues are
\[
-\frac{3s}{2}\pm i\frac{\sqrt3d}{2}.
\]
Write
\[
q_s(h)=e^{-3sh/2},\qquad \phi(h)=\frac{\sqrt3dh}{2}=\frac{2\pi h}{\tau},\qquad
J=\frac{\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top}{3}.
\]
For the clockwise orientation, put
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A^2=-3(I-J),\qquad \|A\|_F=\sqrt6.
\]
Then
\[
P_s^d(h)=J+q_s(h)\left(\cos\phi\,(I-J)+\frac{\sin\phi}{\sqrt3}A\right).
\]
Transposing \(A\) reverses the orientation and changes only the sign of the skew. Directly,
\[
\|P_s^d(h)-P_s^d(h)^\top\|_F
=2\sqrt2\,q_s(h)|\sin\phi(h)|.
\]

The observed stationary pair-probability matrix is
\[
C_h=\frac13 E P_s^d(h)E^\top,\qquad
S_h=C_h-C_h^\top
=\frac13 E(P_s^d(h)-P_s^d(h)^\top)E^\top.
\]
Under \(H_0\), \(P_s^0(h)\) is symmetric, hence \(S_h=0\) for every \(E\). Under \(H_1\), singular-value perturbation gives
\[
\|S_h\|_F\ge
\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\sigma_{\min}(E)^2
e^{-3sh/2}|\sin(2\pi h/\tau)|.
\]
Thus \(S_h\) is an unknown-\(E\) symmetry statistic: it requires no emission inversion, only a lower bound on its conditioning for power.

## Robust choice of lag

For fixed \(h\), the weakest signal over the allowed \(s\)-interval is at \(s_{\max}\). Let
\[
a=\frac{3s_{\max}}2,\qquad \omega=\frac{2\pi}{\tau},\qquad
x=\frac{\omega}{a}=\frac{4\pi}{3s_{\max}\tau}.
\]
Since \(s_{\min}>d\), \(s_{\max}\tau>4\pi/\sqrt3\), so \(0<x<1/\sqrt3\). The robust objective is \(e^{-ah}|\sin(\omega h)|\). Its first-lobe maximum solves \(\omega\cos(\omega h)=a\sin(\omega h)\), giving
\[
h_*=\frac1\omega\arctan\left(\frac\omega a\right)
=\frac{\tau}{2\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{4\pi}{3s_{\max}\tau}\right).
\]
Later lobes repeat the sine factor after \(\tau/2\), while the exponential envelope shrinks by \(e^{-a\tau/2}<1\); hence this is the global robust maximizer.

At this lag,
\[
\phi_*=\arctan x<\frac\pi6,\qquad
q_*=\exp\left(-\frac{\arctan x}{x}\right),\qquad
g_*:=q_*\sin\phi_*
=\exp\left(-\frac{\arctan x}{x}\right)\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}.
\]
The uniform skew floor is
\[
\Delta_*=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}\sigma_0^2g_*.
\]
The endpoint \(h=\tau\) is an exact alias: \(\phi=2\pi\), so \(P_s^d(\tau)=P_s^0(\tau)\). The skew test also has no power at any \(h\in(\tau/2)\mathbb Z\). If the instrument supports only a finite lag grid, maximize the robust objective over that grid and exclude those alias lags.

## Symmetry test and finite-sample upper bound

For an observed pair \((Y_0,Y_h)\), define
\[
Z=e_{Y_0}e_{Y_h}^\top-e_{Y_h}e_{Y_0}^\top,\qquad
\widehat S=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n Z_i.
\]
Then \(\mathbb E Z=S_h\), and \(\widehat S=\widehat C_h-\widehat C_h^\top\). Each \(\|Z_i\|_F\le\sqrt2\). Independence gives
\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat S-S_h\|_F
\le\sqrt{\mathbb E\|\widehat S-S_h\|_F^2}
\le\sqrt{2/n}.
\]
Replacing one pair changes \(\|\widehat S-S_h\|_F\) by at most \(2\sqrt2/n\). McDiarmid therefore yields
\[
\Pr\left(\|\widehat S-S_h\|_F\ge\sqrt{2/n}+t\right)
\le e^{-nt^2/4}.
\]

At \(h_*\), reject \(H_0\) iff \(\|\widehat S\|_F\ge\Delta_*/2\). Under \(H_0\), a false rejection requires estimation error at least \(\Delta_*/2\). Under \(H_1\), a false acceptance also requires error at least \(\Delta_*/2\). Each type-I and type-II error is therefore at most
\[
\exp\left[-\frac n4\left(\frac{\Delta_*}{2}-\sqrt{\frac2n}\right)_+^2\right].
\]
For any \(0<\alpha<1/2\), the sufficient sample count
\[
n_{\rm pair}=
\left\lceil
\frac{(2\sqrt2+4\sqrt{\ln(1/\alpha)})^2}{\Delta_*^2}
\right\rceil
\]
makes each testing error at most \(\alpha\). This is a conservative finite-sample bound, not an optimality claim. The statistic never estimates \(E\); the certified margin sets its threshold.

## Calibration and full assay cost

If a trusted instrument certificate supplies \(\sigma_0\), charge its acquisition as a one-time cost \(C_{\rm cal}\); the test needs no labeled emitter samples. Otherwise, calibrate with \(m_E\) independent emissions from each of the three known hidden states. Estimate all nine entries of \(E\). For \(\eta>0\), a union bound over the nine Bernoulli coordinates gives
\[
\Pr\left(\max_{y,x}|\widehat E_{yx}-E_{yx}|>\eta\right)
\le18e^{-2m_E\eta^2}.
\]
On the complementary event, \(\|\widehat E-E\|_{\rm op}\le\|\widehat E-E\|_F\le3\eta\), so
\[
L_E:=\sigma_{\min}(\widehat E)-3\eta
\]
is a valid lower confidence bound. Failure probability is at most \(\alpha_{\rm cal}\) when
\[
m_E=\left\lceil\frac{\ln(18/\alpha_{\rm cal})}{2\eta^2}\right\rceil.
\]
Use the assay only if \(L_E\ge\sigma_0\). A margin promise \(\sigma_{\min}(E)\ge\sigma_0+6\eta\) guarantees acceptance with probability at least \(1-\alpha_{\rm cal}\); without that slack the protocol may return UNKNOWN.

For the lower-bound channel
\[
E_\kappa=\kappa I+(1-\kappa)\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3,\qquad 0<\kappa\le1,
\]
the singular values are \(1,\kappa,\kappa\). Taking \(\eta=\kappa/12\) and \(\sigma_0=\kappa/2\) uses
\[
m_E=\left\lceil\frac{72}{\kappa^2}\ln\frac{18}{\alpha_{\rm cal}}\right\rceil
\]
standards per hidden state, or \(3m_E\) calibration emissions. This presumes known-state preparation or labeling; charge that work separately. The standards are used only for single-time emission calibration, not for transition measurements.

For unconditional decision error at most \(\delta\), allocate \(\alpha=\alpha_{\rm cal}=\delta/2\), certify the margin first, and charge
\[
N_{\rm emitted}=2n_{\rm pair}+3m_E
\]
emission records, plus \(n_{\rm pair}\) stationary process resets and \(n_{\rm pair}h_*\) total replicate exposure. Add the cost of labeled-state preparations. If a separate endpoint-alias control uses \(n_\tau\) independent pairs, add \(2n_\tau\) emissions, \(n_\tau\) resets, and \(n_\tau\tau\) replicate exposure. Those pairs carry zero hypothesis information because their lag-\(\tau\) pair laws agree. This accounting treats lag-\(h_*\) and lag-\(\tau\) data as independent replicate arms. Reusing the same lineage at \(0,h_*,\tau\) can create a more informative three-time law and is outside the lower bound below.

## Le Cam lower bound at \(E_\kappa\)

Fix \(E=E_\kappa\) and an admissible \(s\). Write
\[
r=\kappa^2e^{-3sh/2},\qquad c=\cos\phi,\qquad v=\sin\phi.
\]
The nine observed pair probabilities are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &H_0&H_1\\\hline
\text{each of 3 diagonal cells}&a_0=(1+2r)/9&a_1=(1+2rc)/9\\
\text{each of 3 clockwise cells}&b_0=(1-r)/9&b_+=(1-rc+\sqrt3rv)/9\\
\text{each of 3 counterclockwise cells}&b_0=(1-r)/9&b_-=(1-rc-\sqrt3rv)/9
\end{array}
\]
For \(h>0\), \(r<1\) and all \(H_0\) cells are positive. Summing the cellwise chi-square terms gives the exact divergence
\[
\Xi_\kappa(s,h):=\chi^2(p_1\|p_0)
=\frac{4r^2(1-c)^2}{3(1+2r)}
+\frac{r^2}{1-r}\left(\frac{2(1-c)^2}{3}+2v^2\right).
\]
At \(h=\tau\), \(c=1,v=0\), so \(\Xi=0\). For fixed nonalias \(s,h\),
\[
\Xi_\kappa(s,h)=4\kappa^4e^{-3sh}(1-\cos\phi)+O(\kappa^6)
\quad\text{as }\kappa\downarrow0.
\]
Thus small emission singular value creates a \(\kappa^{-4}\) testing cost. At \(\kappa=0\), \(E_0=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3\) emits a uniform symbol independently of the hidden state, so the two observed laws are identical for every sample size.

For \(n\) independent lag-\(h\) pairs,
\[
1+\chi^2(p_1^{\otimes n}\|p_0^{\otimes n})
=(1+\Xi_\kappa(s,h))^n.
\]
Any test with both errors at most \(\alpha<1/2\) requires total variation at least \(1-2\alpha\), while
\(\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}\). Therefore
\[
n_{\rm pair}\ge
\frac{\ln(1+4(1-2\alpha)^2)}
{\ln(1+\Xi_\kappa(s,h))}.
\]
For a procedure uniform over unknown \(s\in[s_{\min},s_{\max}]\), specialize to the admissible hardest value \(s=s_{\max}\). At \(h=h_*\), substitute \(r=\kappa^2q_*\) and \(\phi=\phi_*\). This lower bound is \(\Omega(\kappa^{-4})\) as \(\kappa\) becomes small, matching the upper test's dependence on the certified emission floor up to constants. An independent emission-calibration sample contributes no divergence between the hypotheses because \(E_\kappa\) is identical under both. Separate independent lag-\(\tau\) endpoint pairs likewise have identical distributions and add cost but no information. This lower bound does not cover a same-lineage three-time assay or direct hidden-state readout/control.

## Assumption attacks and status

- **No emission-margin promise:** at \(E=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3\), \(H_0,H_1\) are observationally identical forever. A positive certified singular-value floor is necessary for uniform finite-sample power.
- **Emitter drift:** if the two endpoints use different \(E_0,E_h\), \(C=E_0P E_h^\top/3\) need not be symmetric under \(H_0\). Use a time-matched sensor calibration or drift control.
- **No stationarity:** a nonstationary initial hidden law can produce asymmetric lag pairs under a reversible generator. Prepare/verify stationarity or model the transient.
- **Wrong gap:** \(h=\tau\) is an exact law alias; the skew test also has zero signal at \(h\in(\tau/2)\mathbb Z\).
- **Dependent pairs:** overlapping pairs from one long path are not iid. The displayed concentration and chi-square tensorization require independent reset pairs; a long-trajectory design needs a separate dependence and mixing analysis.
- **Calibration boundary:** finite data cannot certify an exact singular-value boundary without margin. The stated UNKNOWN outcome and \(6\eta\) slack make that limitation explicit.

The bounds are internally derived for the stated three-state cycle. No physical measurement, implementation benchmark, or external proof review was performed. The next gate is the joint three-time likelihood if the same replicate is read at both \(h_*\) and \(\tau\), followed by an equal-cost comparison with the independent-pair design.
