# Positive extension: exact bounded blocks for thermal cluster strips

7 October 2026. Independent deduction from the positive cut-absorption mechanism in Appendix A of [2609.35973v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35973v1). This is a restricted quantitative construction, not a claimed historical discovery. No complete independent review has yet been obtained.

## Exact interface and result

Supply a known open rectangular graph with w rows and n columns, its nearest-neighbour CZ circuit U, and a homogeneous rational t in [0,1). Set rho_t=(I+tX)/2 and

rho=U rho_t^(tensor wn) U†.

Equivalently rho is the Gibbs state of H=-J sum_v K_v, K_v=X_v product_(u neighbouring v) Z_u, with t=tanh(beta J). All graph structure, coupling, temperature and stabilizer coordinates are supplied. Neither a hidden Hamiltonian nor a circuit is acquired. For the rational bit statement the interface is t, not an exact exponential of an arbitrary real beta.

For even n, define m=2w, q=[(1+t)/(1-t)]^(2w), kappa=q², and an integer B>=ln kappa. One completely rational choice is B=max(0,bitlength(num(kappa))-bitlength(den(kappa))+1). For t>0 choose

L=1+ceil[kappa(4w+1+B)].

Then rho has an exact positive mixture of pure states, each factorizing over contiguous complete-column blocks with at most 8wL spins. Odd n is handled by one extra column, increasing this bound by w. At t=0 use the maximally mixed product decomposition. There is an explicit sampler using O(wn) local algebraic operations for fixed w, without enumerating exponentially many superblock spectral tuples. Temperature enters the maximum block size as

8wL=O(w²(1+beta J) exp(8 beta J w)).

The implied constants here are numerical. This improves the double-exponential general-temperature upper bound for this supplied-circuit subclass. It does not improve computation of the partition function Z=[2 cosh(beta J)]^(wn), which is already elementary, or ordinary Gibbs preparation/sampling by known Clifford/graph-state methods.

## Cut absorption with an explicit circuit, rather than an assumed evolution primitive

Partition the input sites into pairs of complete columns. Each elementary block has m=2w qubits and normalized input operator G=rho_t^(tensor m). Let E be the product of the w CZ gates crossing the midpoint cut within these two columns. Let a=1/q, G'=a E†GE, Delta=G-G'. The smallest/largest eigenvalues of G have ratio q. Hence

G'<=G<=kappa G', and 0<=Delta<=(1-1/kappa)G.

This follows directly from g_min I<=G,E†GE<=g_max I, not a numerical domination test. E†=E. After E is applied, G' becomes aG, a product across the physical midpoint cut. All CZ gates commute, so U E equals the known circuit with this entire crossing edge set deleted. It factorizes across the chosen complete-column cut. This cancellation is exact; no general noncommuting finite-depth cancellation lemma is assumed.

Group the elementary blocks into superblocks of length ell between L and 2L-1. For M>=L elementary blocks, take floor(M/L) groups and distribute the remaining fewer than L blocks among them. If M<L, output the usual known-circuit decomposition of the whole chain, whose size already obeys the bound. There is no costly construction of a nonexistent length-L superblock when temperature is low and system size is small.

For one superblock define, with block index 0,...,ell-1,

Q0=G'_0 tensor G_1 tensor ... tensor G_(ell-1) + Delta_0 tensor ... tensor Delta_(ell-1),

Qb=Delta_0 tensor ... tensor Delta_(b-1) tensor G'_b tensor G_(b+1) tensor ... tensor G_(ell-1), 1<=b<ell.

Their sum is G^(tensor ell), by the telescoping product identity. For b>=1 spectral decompositions of all positive factors give pure local states, and the central G'_b is decomposed as E_b† times a product-state decomposition of aG. After U, that crossing gate set is cancelled. Thus each term has at least this physical cut.

For Q0 diagonalize the local d-by-d matrix S=G^(-1/2)Delta G^(-1/2), d=2^m:

S=sum_t lambda_t |v_t><v_t|, lambda_t in [0,s], s=1-1/kappa.

Put z_t=G^(1/2)v_t, A_t=|z_t><z_t|, e_t=tr A_t. For the other ell-1 blocks, a tuple T leaves central operator G'+r_T Delta, r_T=product lambda_t. Conjugate this central operator by E and whiten by the PRODUCT B0=aG. The result is I+r_T K, where

K=B0^(-1/2) E Delta E B0^(-1/2)>=0, ||K||<=kappa-1.

For ell>=L,

||r_T K|| <=(kappa-1)s^(L-1)<=kappa exp[-(4w+1+B)]<=exp[-4w-1]<1/(2*4^m).

Expand r_T K=sum_P c_P P in the 4^m tensor-Pauli basis. Let c_I be the identity coefficient and R=sum_(P!=I)|c_P|. Each |c_P|<=||r_T K||, so R<1/2. In particular

I+r_T K=(1+c_I-R)I+sum_(P!=I)|c_P|(I+sign(c_P)P)

is a positive sum. Every I+sign(P)P is diagonal in a product Pauli eigenbasis. Filtering those product vectors by B0^(1/2), also a product operator, yields actual positive pure PRODUCT components across the midpoint. Apply E† to their central vectors in the input representation, then U; the crossing gate set cancels again. Different superblocks choose cuts independently. Each group has fewer than 2L candidate cuts, spaced two columns apart, so every segment between two chosen cuts has fewer than 8L columns (this bound deliberately allows boundary/remainder slack). Thus at most 8wL spins occur in each pure component block.

## Sampler: the exponential spectral tuple list is unnecessary

Let alpha=tr Delta=1-a. Since tr G=1, sum_t e_t=1 and sum_t lambda_t e_t=alpha. The traces of the superblock terms are

w0=a+alpha^ell, wb=a alpha^b for b=1,...,ell-1.

These sum to one exactly. Draw b from these probabilities. For b>=1 use independently sampled spectral components of Delta before b, G after b, and the product eigencomponents of aG at b, undoing E at b. The local spectral tables are finite d-dimensional tables computed from the supplied input, not queries to a compressed evolution oracle.

For b=0, the unnormalized trace weight of an other-block tuple T is

(a+alpha r_T) product_(j>=1)e_(t_j)

= a product e_(t_j) + alpha product [lambda_(t_j)e_(t_j)].

Therefore sample a two-way branch with masses a and alpha^ell. In the first branch independently draw each t with probability e_t; in the second branch independently draw each t with probability lambda_t e_t/alpha. Then compute r_T, and draw a central pure product component of B0^(1/2)(I+r_T K)B0^(1/2), weighted by its actual squared norm. The central table uses at most O(4^m 2^m) Pauli eigencomponents. Component VECTORS can be precomputed: their scalar coefficients depend affinely on r_T, while their local product vectors depend only on B0 and the Pauli basis. No central eigensolve or field extension depending on the tuple is needed. This is an exact trace-weight identity, not a mean-field approximation or an independence assumption about the physical Gibbs state.

Consequently local preprocessing costs O(d³+4^m 2^m) arithmetic operations; each sample costs O(wn*2^{O(w)}) arithmetic/output operations. Product weights and r_T use O(ell) operations, and the number of discrete draws is O(wn), each on a table of at most 2^{O(w)} entries. A sampled pure state is output as two-column state vectors and a list of the retained CZ gates. This compact circuit representation has O(wn*2^{2w}) coefficients, not 2^(8wL) dense block amplitudes. Converting it into a chain MPS has bond at most 2^{O(w)} because a vertical cut crosses w CZ edges and initial elementary states occupy two columns. For w=1 the rank at every cut is at most2; the designated deleted cuts have rank1.

## Bit cost and exactness boundary

The arithmetic bound is not a linear BIT bound. A homogeneous rational t with b input bits gives one repeated set of local matrices. Their entries, eigensystems, normalizations and Pauli eigenvectors are algebraic numbers in a single number field of degree bounded by a computable function of d alone. One deliberately loose degree bound, allowing both S and Delta splitting fields and local normalization roots, is 8(d!)² 2^(2d), enlarged by a fixed-d factor if a primitive-element construction needs it. No new eigenproblem is introduced with system size. Exact local root isolation and sign determination can be performed on the finitely many fixed-degree polynomial equations; their cost is charged as C(w)*poly_w(b), potentially enormous as w grows.

Global scalar products have algebraic logarithmic heights O(C(w)*wn*b). Retaining exact field elements rather than one-unit-cost scalars therefore requires O(C(w)*wn*b) bits per scalar, with O(wn) scalar operations. Schoolbook fixed-field arithmetic gives a conservative O(C(w)*(wn)^3*b²) bit-work bound for products and tables after preprocessing, and O(C(w)*(wn)^2*b) storage if all intermediate products are retained. This accounts for field arithmetic only. Exact draws use fair random bits and comparison of an infinite uniform binary string with an algebraic cumulative probability. The expected required precision has an exponentially decaying tail (a uniform value lies within 2^-k of one fixed threshold with probability at most2^(1-k)); local root isolation/comparison work must be included. Write T_w(h) for the actual cost of fixed-degree root isolation/sign comparisons at coefficient bitlength h. An explicit fully charged bound is

C(w)*poly_w(b) + O(C(w)*(wn)^3*b²) + O(wn*2^{O(w)}) E[T_w(C(w)*wn*b+k)],

where k has the foregoing geometric tail. Rational Sturm sequences, root-separation bounds and binary subdivision give T_w(h)<=C'(w)(h+1)^{C'(w)} for a computable fixed-width constant. This is a constructive polynomial bound at FIXED w, not a claim that the displayed cubic arithmetic bound also covers comparisons or practical low-width implementation. I have not implemented that exact number-field sampler, and do not claim the float64 prototype certifies its probabilities. General inhomogeneous operators would introduce many different fields; the preceding fixed-field polynomial bit argument does not automatically extend to them.

If t is instead obtained from a finite-precision beta J, ||rho_t^(tensor N)-rho_t'^(tensor N)||_1<=N|t-t'|. Unitary conjugation preserves this bound. Computing a rational t' within epsilon/N is therefore an additional charged approximation, and yields an epsilon-close state, not the exact original transcendental Gibbs state. The exact block property is separately proved for every real t<1 by the same operator inequalities; efficient bit sampling refers to the stated rational interface.

## A quantitative measurement consequence

Each pure component has a full-column cut between any two column regions separated by more than8L columns. Any protocol consisting of LOCC among columns, with initially unentangled ancillary systems and arbitrary postselection, preserves separability across every such component's separating cut. The cut can depend on the component. For any fixed measurement outcome, the two final end-region states are therefore a positive mixture of products, hence separable. Nonzero-probability postselection cannot evade this argument. Thus single-copy localizable end-to-end entanglement is EXACTLY zero beyond8L.

At fixed beta J>0, the existence of nonzero entanglement over distance R columns implies

R<=O(w(1+beta J) exp(8 beta J w)),

and hence w>=ln R/(8 beta J)-O_beta(log log R). This is a necessary logarithmic-width condition for these supplied homogeneous thermal cluster strips. It is not a claim about arbitrary noncommuting Hamiltonians, multiple-copy distillation, nonlocal quantum communication, or a protocol permitted to apply entangling gates between columns. The underlying zero-localizable-entanglement implication is already a corollary of the September source. The quantitatively explicit strip-width estimate is our deduction; historical priority remains unverified.

The prototype drops Pauli coefficients smaller than1e-16 and can underflow very small products. Its source explicitly labels it a finite float64 discriminator, not the exact bit sampler.

The first exact-rational diagnostic failed while printing a power with more than4300 decimal digits. That failure is preserved as evidence that scalar bit cost grows with superblock length. Revised code reports denominator bit length and uses binary-search exponentiation rather than a linear sequence of exact rational powers. At t=1/2 the exact slack denominator has4007 bits. At t=99/100 the conservative bound is L56,456,611,237 and8L451,652,889,896, illustrating that single-exponential does not mean experimentally useful at very low temperatures.

Strong controls: the ordinary graph-state spectral mixture samples independent X eigenstates then applies U, using O(wn) work and an O(2^{O(w)})-bond TN. Stabilizer/Clifford methods handle Pauli observables efficiently, and direct product thermodynamics eliminates any trace-estimation task here. Stochastic Lanczos/Chebyshev or free-fermion machinery is unnecessary for this diagonalizable class. They are not disadvantaged by hiding the supplied U: every comparator gets it. The new output relative to that elementary mixture is a finite bound on contiguous physical block size and an exact positive decomposition realizing that bound.
