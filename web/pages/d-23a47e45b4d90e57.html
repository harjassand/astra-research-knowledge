1. EXACT POSITIVE CONSTRUCTION AND Duhamel AUDIT

Let s=N^(3/4), C=A+MI>=0, t0=2/N-M/s^2>=0, and diagonalize C with eigenvalues
lambda_i in [0,2M] and axes n_i. Put F_i=n_i.J, c_i=lambda_i/s^2,
H=Q_C/s^2+b.J/s. Then K=t0 J^2+H and the Casimir commutes with everything.

For t>0, on each finite SU(2) representation,
  e^(tJ^2)=integral e^(y.J) nu_t(dy),
  nu_t(dy)=(4pi t)^(-3/2)e^(-t/4)e^(-|y|^2/(4t))
             sinh(|y|/2)/(|y|/2) d^3y.
This is proved by rotation invariance, the spin-j character
sinh((j+1/2)r)/sinh(r/2), and differentiating the Gaussian cosh integral.
nu_t has mass one by the j=0 case. Since e^(y.J)=e^(y.sigma/2)^(tensor N),
the normalized seed is an iid product mixture. At t0=0 use the identity seed.

Define
  L(X)={H,X}/2 - sum_i c_i[F_i,[F_i,X]]/4.
Each quadratic factor has the EXACT Gaussian identity
  e^(delta L_i)(X)=E_Z e^(sqrt(c_i delta/2)ZF_i) X
                            e^(sqrt(c_i delta/2)ZF_i), Z~N(0,1).
The field factor is a collective local sandwich. Every path filter is g^(tensor N).
Finite Trotter products preserve positive iid mixtures; their finite-dimensional
limit does also. Thus W=e^L(e^(t0J^2)) is positive and sigma=W/Tr W is fully
separable. The negative double commutator is compatible with CP because the
anti-commutator term supplies the required Ito drift. Dropping that drift would
be an error; the displayed L includes it exactly.

In spin j, Y_j=e^(L_j)I_j. For positive X,
  Tr e^(uL_j)X <=e^(u||H_j||)Tr X,
since Tr L_jX=Tr H_jX. Jordan decomposition gives the same bound on Hermitian
trace norm. Duhamel against e^H gives a residual consisting only of the double
commutators. Rotation differentiation and the exponential simplex formula give
  ||[F,[F,e^(qH)]]||_1
    <=d_j e^(q||H||)[q||[F,[F,H]]||+q^2||[F,H]||^2].
The coefficients are q and q^2; integrating q and q^2 over [0,1] gives 1/2,1/3.
No invalid operator-monotonicity assertion about exp is used.

Rotation derivatives of C are derivatives of A: the MI term vanishes.
With x_j=j(j+1)/s^2 and ell_j=j/s,
  ||H_j||<=2Mx_j+B ell_j,
  D1_i<=2Mx_j+B ell_j, D2_i<=4Mx_j+B ell_j,
  sum c_i<=6M/s^2.
Tr exp(H_j)>=d_j by trace Jensen, since Tr Q_C>=0 and Tr b.J=0.
Consequently target normalization is at least seed normalization. Normalizing
two positive operators costs at most raw trace-norm error divided by target Z
in T distance. There is no omitted exponential normalization penalty.

2. SHARPENED PUBLIC FINITE-N CERTIFICATE

Use spins j=0,1,...,N/2 for even N and 1/2,3/2,...,N/2 for odd N. Put
  a_j=(2j+1)^2/(N/2+j+1) binom(N,N/2-j) exp[2j(j+1)/N],
  Z0=sum_j a_j exp(-Mx_j),
  P_j=2Mx_j+(B/2)ell_j+(2Mx_j+B ell_j)^2/3,
  U=sum_j a_j exp(Mx_j+B ell_j)P_j.
Then for t0>=0,
  T(rho,sigma)<=min(1, E_N), E_N=3M U/(2s^2 Z0).             (A)
These sums require O(N) scalar terms; log-sum-exp avoids large 2^N factors.
For M=0 the construction is exact for every N and every field.

Presentation repairs identified and accepted by the child: replacing
j(j+1)/s^2 by r_j^2 with r_j=(j+1/2)/s too early creates an omitted
M/(4s^2) in a subsequent exponent. Use the exact x_j norm before rounding.
The same repair applies to the finite Trotter exponent. These were repairable
finite-N inequalities, not fatal flaws in the mechanism.

3. AN EXPLICIT UNIFORM CONSTANT — NO UNSPECIFIED TAIL LEMMA

For N>=256, elementary Stirling plus binomial entropy gives
  a_j <=64*2^N*(2j+1)^2/N^(3/2) * exp[-2j^4/(3N^3)].       (B)
Details: for j<=N/4, binom<=2*2^N/sqrt(N) times exp[-2j^2/N-4j^4/(3N^3)].
The Gibbs quadratic cancels and exp(2j/N)<=e. For j>N/4 use the entropy
bound without its sqrt(N) prefactor, and absorb sqrt(N) using
sqrt(N)exp[-N/384]<=sqrt(192)e^(-1/2). This is below the constant 64.

In the lattice interval s/8<=j<=s/4 there are at least s/16 spins. Stirling
lower bounds binom by 2^N exp(-N I)/(4sqrt N). For u=2j/N<=1/2,
  I(u)<=u^2/2+u^4/9.
Also x_j<=1/8. Thus
  Z0 >=2^N s/1024 * exp[-M/8-1/144].                       (C)

Set r=j/s and D=2M+B/2+(2M+B)^2/3. Then P_j<=D(r+1)^4 and x_j<=(r+1)^2.
Young inequalities give
  Mx_j+B ell_j <=r^4/3+6M^2+2M+B^(4/3).
The function (r+1)^6e^(-r^4/3) is unimodal, and its lattice sum is at most
512s: bound its integral by 32 times the 0th and 6th quartic moments and its
supremum by 32[1+(9/2)^(3/2)e^(-3/2)]. Equations (B)–(C) therefore prove
  U/Z0 <=2^27 D exp[6M^2+17M/8+B^(4/3)+1/144].

  Cstar=3M*2^26 D exp[6M^2+17M/8+B^(4/3)+1/144],

useful public certificate. For all smaller N choose I/2^N and replace Cstar by
max(Cstar,N0^(3/2)), N0=max(256,ceil(M^2)); T<=1 proves the all-N bound.

4. FINITE TROTTER, WEIGHT MOMENTS AND EXPLICIT REAL-ARITHMETIC SAMPLER

nu_t0(dy)[2cosh(|y|/2)]^N, put tau_y=e^(y.sigma/2)/[2cosh(|y|/2)], draw 3m
independent standard Gaussians for the finite filter product g, and set
  w(y,g)=[Tr(g tau_y g^*)]^N.
The normalized sigma_m is the product tau_(y,g)^(tensor N) under the
trace-weighted law wq/Ew. Sampling the unweighted law and ignoring w is wrong.

Averaging g proves the claim. This is not a claim that w(y,g)>=1 branchwise.

For p replicas, every factor's adjoint applied to I is centrally dominated;

The sinch inequality follows termwise from (2k+1)!>=6^k k!. Central
domination is valid because the Casimir commutes with all filters; no arbitrary
