# Conditional calculation and identifiability counterexample

## Source-constrained PDE reduction

The released Supplementary Information and pinned simulator use

\[
\partial_t\rho+\partial_x(\rho v)
  =\bigl(k_A(1-\phi)+k_B\phi\bigr)\rho,
\]
\[
\partial_t\phi+v\partial_x\phi
  =D\left(\partial_{xx}\phi+\frac{\partial_x\rho}{\rho}\partial_x\phi\right)
   +(k_B-k_A)\phi(1-\phi)+k_D(1-\phi),
\]
with

\[
k_i(\rho)=\frac1\tau\frac{\rho_i^c-\rho}{\rho_i^c},\qquad
E(\phi)=E_A+(E_B-E_A)\phi,\qquad
k_D=\alpha(E-E_A).
\]

Freeze the leading edge at the A-homeostatic state \(\rho=\rho_A^c\), freeze \(v=v_A\) and remove that uniform advection by a Galilean change of coordinates. Then

\[
k_A=0,\qquad
\Delta k:=k_B-k_A=\frac{\rho_B^c-\rho_A^c}{\tau\rho_B^c}>0,
\]
and \(k_D=\alpha\Delta E\phi\), where \(\Delta E=E_B-E_A\). The scalar closure is therefore exactly

\[
u_t=D u_{xx}+r u(1-u),\qquad r=\Delta k+\alpha\Delta E.
\]

This is a **conditional homogeneous-leading-edge reduction**. It is not the full moving-density, force-balanced source PDE: there, \(\rho\), \(v\), and the growth terms depend on the evolving profile.

## Tail selection in the scalar comparator

For an exponential leading edge \(u\sim e^{-q(x-ct)}\), substitution in the linearized equation \(u_t=D u_{xx}+r u\) gives

\[
c(q)=Dq+\frac rq.
\]

For Fisher–KPP logistic growth and an initial tail \(u_0(x)\sim e^{-q_0x}\), classical selection gives \(c(q_0)\) when \(q_0<q_*:=\sqrt{r/D}\), and the minimal pulled value \(c_*=2\sqrt{Dr}\) for a sufficiently steep tail. Fisher–KPP initial-tail selection is established prior art (the Supplement cites Fisher, Kolmogorov–Petrovskii–Piskunov, and van Saarloos); no novelty is claimed for it.

The source simulator initializes

\[
\phi_0(x)=\frac{1-\tanh(a_\phi x/L)}2
         =\frac1{1+e^{2a_\phi x/L}},
\]
so its far-ahead exponent is \(q_0=2a_\phi/L\). With `aPhi=4`, `L=1500 μm`, `D=15 μm²/h`, converted model stiffnesses `eO=12960`, `eM=388.8`, `alpha=3.5e-6`, `τ=10 h`, `ρA^c=.0067`, and `ρB^c=.0077`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E&=12571.2, & \Delta k&=0.0129870\;\mathrm h^{-1},\\
\alpha\Delta E&=0.0439992\;\mathrm h^{-1},
&r&=0.0569862\;\mathrm h^{-1},\\
q_0&=0.0053333\;\mu\mathrm m^{-1},
&q_*&=0.0616367\;\mu\mathrm m^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the initialized scalar comparator is far in the shallow-tail regime: \(q_0/q_*=0.0865\), with tail length 187.5 μm versus 16.22 μm at the pulled threshold. It predicts

\[
c(q_0)=10.7649\;\mu\mathrm m/\mathrm h,
\qquad c_*=1.8491\;\mu\mathrm m/\mathrm h.
\]

The local model-output replay estimates a \(\phi=1/2\) level speed of 21.7353 μm/h, a sampled tissue velocity of 10.9302 μm/h, and a mean density-gradient term \(D\partial_x\log\rho=0.00318\) μm/h at that level. The reduced decomposition `V + c(q0) − D∂x log rho` gives 21.6920 μm/h, 0.0433 μm/h below that level estimate. This close agreement is a diagnostic for the **released model output under the chosen line and window**; it is not an asymptotic proof for the full PDE or evidence that biological skull growth is a shallow-tail KPP wave.

## Counterexample to recovering differentiation gain from one speed

In this scalar comparator, suppose only \(D,\Delta E,\Delta k\), and one adjusted relative speed \(c\) are available, while the initial tail exponent \(q\) is not measured for the biological front. For every \(q\) satisfying

\[
q>0,\qquad q(c-Dq)>\Delta k,\qquad 2Dq<c,
\]
set

\[
\alpha(q)=\frac{q(c-Dq)-\Delta k}{\Delta E}.
\]

Then \(\alpha(q)>0\), \(r(q)=q(c-Dq)\), \(q<\sqrt{r(q)/D}\), and the shallow-tail selected speed is exactly

\[
Dq+\frac{r(q)}q=Dq+(c-Dq)=c.
\]

The inequalities are strict, so they hold on an open interval whenever one admissible pair exists. Consequently a single wave speed does not uniquely identify \(\alpha\) in this source-constrained scalar model unless the initial tail is separately known.

For the replayed adjusted speed \(c=10.8082254\) μm/h, choose:

| Assumed initial tail \(q\) (μm⁻¹) | Implied \(\alpha\) (simulator units) | \(q_*\) (μm⁻¹) | Selected speed (μm/h) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0053333 (source simulator seed) | 3.5184×10⁻⁶ | 0.06176 | 10.80823 |
| 0.0100000 | 7.4452×10⁻⁶ | 0.08429 | 10.80823 |
| 0.0200000 | 1.5685×10⁻⁵ | 0.11837 | 10.80823 |

All three are positive and shallow-tail selected. These are exact substitutions up to displayed rounding. The first reconstructs the supplied simulator coefficient closely; the other two are explicit non-unique inversions of the same speed observation. This does not refute the paper’s experiments or its full PDE. It blocks a stronger interpretation of a front-speed fit as a unique differentiation-sensitivity estimate without a measured in-vivo leading-edge tail and independent speed validation.

## Estimator and model-output adversarial checks

The replay script reads the pinned output matrices and reproduces three different finite-model speed definitions:

1. Linear regression of the continuously interpolated `φ=0.5` level over 0–8 h: 21.7353 μm/h.
2. The companion Rmd’s strict `φ<0.5` row-minimum displacement and origin-constrained fit: 20.5881 μm/h. In R, `seq(0:nT)` uses its one-argument length form, so the literal `T.all=(seq(0:nT)-1)/nT*T.max` spans 0 to 8 h.
3. The notebook’s two-window shift objective (Mathematica time indices 20 and 50; 30 output steps apart; three grid cells of shift): 18.75 μm/h.

The third estimator’s notebook expression is `Abs[Table[Sum[φ(x,t)-φ(x+shift,t+Δt),{x}],...]]`, not a pointwise absolute/L1 mismatch. On the source matrices, the selected shift gives mean absolute signed-sum error 0.0372829 but mean spatial L1 shape error 0.414950. Minimizing L1 happens to choose the same three-cell grid shift for this output, so the velocity argmin survives this single replay; the reported residual is nevertheless not the shape error described in the prose. The physical candidate speed spacing is `15 μm/(30·0.08 h)=6.25 μm/h`; the exported notebook field `deltavUnits=0.5` is `15/30` per output-index unit and is not that physical speed increment.

The three estimates are all properties of the released finite simulation; they are not competing measurements of nature. They show why a moving-front speed must be defined and replayed before it is inserted into an asymptotic selection law. The source’s finite-grid threshold fit and its coarse two-window alignment fit need not equal an asymptotic traveling-wave speed.

## Strongest equally informed baselines and limits

- **Advection only:** 10.9302 μm/h sampled from the released velocity field at `φ=0.5`.
- **Pulled scalar KPP plus sampled advection:** about 12.776 μm/h after density-gradient correction; about 8.96 μm/h below the released `φ=0.5` OLS estimate. It is not the right scalar branch for the source’s shallow initial tail.
- **Shallow-tail scalar KPP plus sampled advection:** 21.6920 μm/h; within 0.0433 μm/h of the replayed `φ=0.5` level speed, but about 2.99 μm/h above the notebook’s two-window shift estimate.
- **Released ex-vivo interface data:** independently fit 0–6 h OLS slopes of 12.15, 10.89, 15.00, and 25.67 μm/h; mean 15.93 and sample SD 6.72 μm/h across four data sets. The released model line speed falls within that across-data-set SD. This is a descriptive comparison, not a held-out validation.
- **Released tracked-cell speed:** source summary gives 13.17±4.09 μm/h at the bone front; the model `V` value is 10.93 μm/h at the simulated `φ=0.5` line. Cell tracks are not the same observable as the Eulerian tissue-velocity field.

The full source system is nonlinear and moving: density-dependent proliferation and force-balance velocity interact with the fraction field. The source’s `D` is a relative-fraction diffusion estimate; the source itself leaves active cell migration versus bulk displacement unresolved. A local advection decomposition does not establish a uniform drift or pulled/pushed status in the full model. The code exposes that boundary explicitly.

## Independent replay of the full discrete PDE

`replay_coupled_fd.py` transcribes the source notebook’s selected `pdeDiscretized1` scheme: centered spatial derivatives, forward Euler in time, Dirichlet endpoint values, and a force-balance solve at each grid time. For interior nodes, the velocity equation is the tridiagonal linear system

\[
\eta\frac{v_{i+1}-2v_i+v_{i-1}}{\Delta x^2}-\gamma v_i
= E_i\frac{\rho_{i+1}-\rho_{i-1}}{2\Delta x\rho_i}
 +(E_O-E_M)\frac{\phi_{i+1}-\phi_{i-1}}{2\Delta x}\log(\rho_i/\rho_h).
\]

The notebook parameter/mesh choices and all three output matrices are taken from the pinned source. At `Nx=200, Nt=100`, the independent solver’s maximum absolute/RMSE differences from the released matrices are:

| Field | Maximum absolute difference | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| `φ` | below `6×10⁻¹⁵` | `1.81×10⁻¹⁵` |
| `ρ` | below `6×10⁻¹⁷` | `1.27×10⁻¹⁷` |
| `V` | below `4×10⁻¹³` | `4.81×10⁻¹⁴` |

The same source-profile `φ=0.5` OLS estimate changes from `21.73529 μm/h` at `Nx=200,Nt=100` to `21.73416 μm/h` at `Nx=3200,Nt=25600`. The high-resolution solver optionally applies a discrete sine transform to the same tridiagonal matrix; at `Nx=800`, it agrees with the Thomas algorithm to about `10⁻¹⁴` in front speed and tissue velocity. This verifies the public numerical model and its base-profile convergence. It does not validate the PDE against nature, test all parameter values, or prove asymptotic convergence of a travelling wave.

## Exact level-set kinematics and matched finite-window velocities

For any regular `φ=1/2` level `x_f(t)` of the source PDE, implicit differentiation gives the exact continuum identity

\[
\dot x_f=v(x_f,t)-D\frac{\phi_{xx}}{\phi_x}(x_f,t)
 -D\partial_x\log\rho(x_f,t)-\frac{R(\rho,\phi)}{\phi_x(x_f,t)},
\]

where `R=(k_B-k_A)φ(1−φ)+k_D(1−φ)`. This is kinematics from the source PDE, not an independent propagation law. The solver evaluates the terms by interpolation at the same moving level and compares their sum with the finite-difference level velocity. For the source seed, the refined 0–8 h mean terms are approximately

```text
v                                  +10.923 μm/h
−D φ_xx/φ_x                         −0.0076 μm/h
−D ∂x log ρ                         −0.0031 μm/h
−R/φ_x                             +10.862 μm/h
sum                                +21.775 μm/h
finite-difference level derivative +21.775 μm/h
```

At steep initial profiles, the front accelerates markedly in the first two hours. Therefore the comparison subtracts the time-integrated velocity sampled along the `φ=1/2` trajectory from the end-to-end front secant over the same interval. An OLS fit over all frames cannot be subtracted from a differently weighted mean `V`; that earlier calculation was discarded. The corrected matched-window table is in `FINAL_REPORT.md` and comes from the same source solver.

## Full-model speed-only non-identifiability construction

The speed-to-`α` alias is not confined to the scalar KPP equation. Holding all notebook parameters fixed except the matched initial-profile steepness and fitted `α`, the independently replayed nonlinear model gives:

```text
aρ = aφ = 4, α=3.5e−6:   8 h φ=1/2 secant 21.77502 μm/h
aρ = aφ = 8, α=1.934e−6: 8 h φ=1/2 secant 21.77423 μm/h
```

Both runs are finite, use `Nx=3200,Nt=25600`, and the `aφ=8` seed remains in the shallow scalar branch (`q₀/q*≈0.214`). Their front-speed discrepancy is less than `0.001 μm/h`, although `α` differs by `44.7%`. The second run’s mean Eulerian velocity sampled at the level is `17.998 μm/h`, compared with `10.923 μm/h` for the first. Thus the alias is specifically **speed-only**: a separately measured initial profile or a matched velocity field can reject it. It does not refute the paper’s reported joint calibration, which uses additional observables and fixes the initial steepness.

The finite-grid mean level speed relative to local tissue flow also changes sign for extreme matched profile slopes. At `Nx=3200,Nt=25600`, the 0–8 h estimates are positive `+0.147 μm/h` at `aφ=110` and negative `−0.132 μm/h` at `aφ=120`; the 6–8 h window is already slightly negative at 110. The sign is continuous in the finite-dimensional seed parameter as long as the run remains regular and the level remains unique, so the discretized model has a zero between these values for the 0–8 h estimand. That zero is not a robust threshold: it is time-window dependent and occurs at an initial tail length `6.5 μm`, below the density-derived characteristic cell spacing `11–12 μm`. The source mesh spacing is `15 μm`. Treat it as a failure boundary for continuum design claims, not a tissue capability.
