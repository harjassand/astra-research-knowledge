# Infinite-dimensional Gaussian experiment archives: a candidate memory theorem

Status at 2026-10-07: new internal derivation, not external validation or novelty clearance. The polynomial-ellipsoid construction below does not assume N30, an OpenAI-release theorem, or a quantum-to-Gaussian approximation. The sparse converse was reconstructed jointly with the root and `sparse_gaussian_converse`; exact leading sparse constants should be checked against that worker's complete proof. An independent sequence audit has checked the conditional-decoder upper bound and the uniformity in the amplitude parameter.

## 1. What changed relative to the inherited capital

N30 is only a supplied summary. Its proof and scripts are absent. Its fixed-dimensional constants cannot be used in a growing-dimensional or infinite-dimensional application.

The useful new interface is an infinite Gaussian sequence with an explicitly supplied ellipsoid of allowable means. The encoder needs only finitely many observations, and the decoder regenerates the discarded coordinates from a parameter-independent reference. A coordinatewise TV allocation would introduce an avoidable dimension logarithm. Conditional exponential-family KL and the ellipsoid's quadratic constraint remove it.

An independent mechanism gives a stronger warning about sparse families: even the finite parameter set `{0, +/- r e_i : i <= d}` forces a number of retained bits or qubits proportional to `d log(r/epsilon)`, when that ratio is sufficiently large. The number of hypotheses is only `2d+1`; preserving the whole experiment requires retaining noise information in every potential score direction before the active direction is known.

## 2. Exact mathematical interface

The observation space is the standard Borel product `Omega = R^N`. For `theta` in `ell_2`, observe one sequence with independent coordinates

`X_j ~ N(theta_j, 1)`.

The model and all axes are supplied. They are not acquired from observations. For a fixed exponent `alpha > 0` and known amplitude `A > 0`, the parameter set is

`Theta_{alpha,A} = { theta : sum_{j>=1} j^{2alpha} theta_j^2 <= A^2 }`.

Write `a_j = A j^{-alpha}`, so equivalently `sum (theta_j/a_j)^2 <= 1`.

The blind encoder receives `X`, not `theta`. It retains a hybrid memory algebra `direct_sum_b M_{d_b}` of total dimension `D = sum_b d_b`. Every retained input-dependent register counts. The decoder is a parameter-independent stochastic quantum measurement/kernel producing a fresh `Y in R^N`. The condition is

`sup_{theta in Theta} TV(Law_theta(Y), P_theta) <= epsilon`,

where TV is half the L1 distance. Independent shared classical seeds are permitted; free entangled receiver references and uncharged input-dependent records are excluded. The charged retained-memory cost is `B = log_2 D`. For a classical archive, `D` is its finite alphabet size; a fixed binary representation costs at most `ceil(log_2 D)` bits, or at most one extra bit per coordinate if the indices are stored separately.

The criterion preserves every bounded statistical decision rule within this declared model. It preserves the statistical law, not the particular observed sequence or its external correlations.

## 3. Candidate theorem: polynomial axes

There are absolute constants `epsilon_0 > 0` and `C_0 > 1`, and constants `0 < c_alpha <= C_alpha < infinity` depending only on `alpha`, such that simultaneously for all `A > 0`, `0 < epsilon <= epsilon_0`, and `A/epsilon >= C_0`,

`c_alpha (A/epsilon)^{1/alpha} <= B_q(epsilon; Theta_{alpha,A}) <= B_cl(epsilon; Theta_{alpha,A}) <= C_alpha (A/epsilon)^{1/alpha}`.

Thus arbitrary joint quantum/hybrid encoders do not improve the order of retained bits/qubits. This is an infinite-dimensional theorem with uniform amplitude dependence, not substitution into a fixed-dimensional constant.

If `A <= sqrt(2pi) epsilon`, the one-label decoder `P_0` already has error at most epsilon, since `TV(N(theta,I),N(0,I)) = 2Phi(||theta||/2)-1 <= ||theta||/sqrt(2pi) <= A/sqrt(2pi)`. No assertion of a sharp transition constant is made.

## 4. Direct classical construction and proof

### 4.1 Scalar conditional Gaussian archive

Let `R >= 0` be supplied, `0 < h <= 1`, and `|theta| <= R`. Put

`T = R + sqrt(2 log(8/h^2))`.

Partition `[-T,T]` into `M = ceil(2T/h)` equal intervals, each of width `w = 2T/M <= h`, together with the two exterior rays. Encode the cell `C` containing `X`. Decode `C` by drawing from `N(0,1)` conditioned on that same cell. Denote the output law by `Q_theta`.

The reference law is preserved exactly: `Q_0 = N(0,1)`. The scalar alphabet has `M+2` labels. The encoder is deterministic and uses no shared seed.

**Claim.** `KL(P_theta || Q_theta) <= theta^2 h^2 / 4`.

Proof: `Q_theta(dy) = P_theta(C) P_0(dy|C)` on each cell. The chain rule gives

`KL(P_theta || Q_theta) = sum_C P_theta(C) KL(P_theta(.|C) || P_0(.|C))`.

For the conditioned reference on a cell, define `psi_C(t) = log E_{P_0(.|C)} exp(tX)`. The conditioned tilted family is `N(t,1)` restricted to `C`. Its conditional divergence from the null is

`t psi_C'(t) - psi_C(t) = integral_0^t s psi_C''(s) ds`.

This expression is nonnegative also when `t < 0` because both the sign of the integrand and the integration orientation reverse. On a central interval of width at most `h`, variance is at most `h^2/4`, independently of the reference measure (Popoviciu's elementary bound). Thus its conditional KL is at most `theta^2 h^2/8`.

On either ray, `psi_C''(s)` is the variance of a one-sided truncated unit-variance Gaussian, hence at most 1. This can be verified explicitly: for truncation above `z`, let `lambda(z)=phi(z)/(1-Phi(z))`. The variance is `1 + z lambda(z) - lambda(z)^2 <= 1`, because `lambda(z) >= z`; for `z > 0`, `phi(z)=integral_z^infinity u phi(u) du >= z(1-Phi(z))`, and for `z <= 0` the assertion is immediate. Lower-ray truncation follows by reflection. Therefore each ray's conditional KL is at most `theta^2/2`.

The actual shifted observation satisfies

`P_theta(|X|>T) <= 2 exp(-(T-R)^2/2) = h^2/4`.

Adding the central and tail contributions proves `theta^2 h^2/8 + (theta^2/2)(h^2/4) = theta^2h^2/4`.

This is a completely explicit finite alphabet construction. It uses a known reference conditioner rather than estimating or supplying the unknown mean.

### 4.1a Conditional sampling does not require rare-event rejection

The Gaussian conditioner can itself be prepared from fresh ideal uniform randomness, without a stored CDF table or a supplied truncated-normal oracle. For a central cell `[b,b+w]`, let `x_*` be its point closest to zero. Propose X uniformly on the cell and accept with probability

`exp(-(X^2-x_*^2)/2)`.

The accepted density is exactly the conditioned standard normal. Since the cell is inside `[-T,T]`, `X^2-x_*^2<=2T h`, so the acceptance probability is at least `exp(-Th)`. For the coordinate choices below, `z=a/epsilon>=1` and `h=1/z`, giving

`Th=epsilon+sqrt(2ln(8z^2))/z <= 1+sqrt(2ln8)`.

Thus the expected number of proposals is less than 21 uniformly in the signal amplitude, coordinate, and precision. The inequality follows from `ln z<=(z^2-1)/2` and `2ln8>2`.

For the positive exterior ray `[T,infinity)`, propose `X=T+E` with `E~Exp(rate T)` and accept with probability `exp(-E^2/2)`. Its accepted density is proportional to `exp(-TE-E^2/2)`, exactly the desired conditional Gaussian. Jensen gives acceptance at least `exp(-E E^2/2)=exp(-1/T^2)`. Since `T>=sqrt(2ln8)>2`, fewer than 1.28 proposals are needed on average. Reflection handles the negative ray. An exponential proposal is `-ln U/T` from a fresh uniform U.

These are expected ideal-real operation counts, not a claim that a literal real-valued output is produced by finitely many fair bits. Known-model arithmetic precision and a rounded-output interface must still be specified for a bit-machine implementation. The construction does rule out an unacknowledged `exp(A^2)` cost from conditioning by naive rejection of full standard-normal draws.

### 4.2 Infinite ellipsoid archive

Retain precisely the coordinates with `a_j > epsilon`; their number is `k <= t = (A/epsilon)^{1/alpha}`. For each retained coordinate use the scalar construction with

`R_j = a_j`, `h_j = epsilon/a_j`.

For all other coordinates store no label and decode an independent `N(0,1)`. Conditioned on the vector of retained labels, decode the retained coordinates independently with the scalar null-conditioners.

Let `Q_theta` be the product output law. For an omitted coordinate, its KL contribution is exactly `theta_j^2/2`. For a retained coordinate it is at most `theta_j^2 h_j^2/4`. Consequently

`KL(P_theta || Q_theta) <= (1/2) sum_j theta_j^2 min{1, epsilon^2/a_j^2} <= epsilon^2/2`.

The final inequality follows directly from `sum theta_j^2/a_j^2 <= 1`: each coefficient is at most `epsilon^2/a_j^2`. Pinsker then gives `TV(P_theta,Q_theta) <= epsilon/2`, leaving an explicit margin.

Infinite products cause no hidden accumulation: every finite-prefix KL obeys the same bound, and the relative entropy on the countable product is the supremum of the increasing finite-prefix entropies. Equivalently, the discarded Gaussian shift has square-summable mean and its entropy is the sum of the scalar entropies.

### 4.3 Uniform retained-bit count

For an active coordinate put `z_j = a_j/epsilon > 1`. Then `h_j=1/z_j` and

`M_j+2 <= 2 epsilon z_j^2 + 2 z_j sqrt(2 log(8 z_j^2)) + 3 <= C z_j^2`

for an absolute `C`, simultaneously for `epsilon <= 1` and all `z_j >= 1`. For example, `log z <= (z^2-1)/2` bounds the square root by an absolute constant times `z`.

Thus

`B_cl <= sum_{j<=k} log_2(M_j+2) <= k log_2 C + 2 sum_{j<=k} log_2(a_j/epsilon)`.

Writing `t=(A/epsilon)^{1/alpha}` and using `log(k!) >= k log k - k`,

`sum_{j<=k} log(a_j/epsilon) = alpha [k log t - log(k!)] <= alpha [k log(t/k)+k] <= alpha t`.

Therefore `B_cl <= [log_2 C + 2alpha/log 2] t`. Separately storing each coordinate's binary index adds at most `k <= t` bits. No exponentially large codebook needs to be instantiated.

## 5. Quantum/hybrid lower bound from weak sparse score directions

This is the converse mechanism independently being written in `work/agents/sparse_gaussian_converse/report.md`. The following proof records the load-bearing steps so the ellipsoid theorem is not left dependent on an unexplained worker claim.

### 5.1 A dimension-uniform sparse lemma

For any `d >= 1`, `0 < r <= 1`, and reconstruction error epsilon on `{0,+/-r e_i : i<=d}`,

`log D >= (d/2) log(r/epsilon) - C d`

in natural logarithms, with an absolute constant `C` and no dimension-dependent threshold or constant.

For each coordinate separately, use the equal prior on `theta = +/- r e_i`. Its scalar posterior sign mean is `m_r(x)=tanh(rx)`. Define

`F_r(m)=sqrt(r^2+m^2)`, `H_r(x)=m_r(x)/sqrt(r^2+m_r(x)^2)`.

Use the bounded proper tangent loss for true sign `z in {-1,+1}` and posterior action `u in [-1,1]`:

`ell_z(u) = -F_r(u) - F_r'(u)(z-u) = -(r^2+zu)/sqrt(r^2+u^2)`.

Its oscillation on the action interval is at most 2, uniformly in r. Under a posterior mean m, its excess Bayes risk is the Bregman divergence `D_F(m,u)`.

For `v(m)=(r,m)/sqrt(r^2+m^2)`, the exact identity is

`D_F(m,u) = [sqrt(r^2+m^2)/2] ||v(m)-v(u)||^2 >= (r/2) [H(m)-H(u)]^2`.

Apply action `u=m_r(Y_i)` to the decoder's output. Approximate reconstruction of the two sign laws changes the bounded loss risk by at most `2epsilon`. The full-data posterior action is optimal conditional on X, regardless of randomization in the encoder/decoder. Hence under the sign-mixture input,

`E (H_r(X_i)-H_r(Y_i))^2 <= 4epsilon/r`.

The sign-mixture density relative to the common full null product law is `exp(-r^2/2) cosh(rX_i) >= exp(-1/2)`. Transfer gives, simultaneously for every i under the *same* `P_0`,

`E_{P_0} (H_r(X_i)-H_r(Y_i))^2 <= 4exp(1/2) epsilon/r`.

This common null law is essential: its coordinates are independent, so the per-coordinate converse can add rather than merely take a maximum.

### 5.2 Entropy and quantum dimension

For `G~N(0,1)`, `U=H_r(G)` has differential entropy uniformly bounded below. Indeed

`H_r'(x) = sech^2(rx) / (1+(tanh(rx)/r)^2)^{3/2}`,

so, using `tanh(rx)/r <= |x|` in absolute value and `log cosh(rx) <= |x|`,

`log H_r'(x) >= -2|x| - (3/2)log(1+x^2)`.

Therefore `h(U) >= h(G) - 2E|G| - (3/2)Elog(1+G^2) >= -C_h`, uniformly in `0<r<=1`.

Under the common null, the `U_i=H_r(X_i)` are independent. With `V_i=H_r(Y_i)` and the preceding distortion bound, conditional entropy and maximum entropy at fixed second moment give

`I(U;V) >= sum_i h(U_i) - (1/2)sum_i log(2pi e E(U_i-V_i)^2) >= (d/2)log(r/epsilon)-Cd`.

The finite hybrid dimension controls this mutual information even for arbitrary coupled, mixed encoders. Write the encoded state as `sigma_x` and the decoder POVM as `M(dy)`. Since `0 <= sigma_x <= I`,

`K_x(dy)=Tr(sigma_x M(dy)) <= Tr M(dy)=mu(dy)`, `mu(Omega)=D`.

With the probability measure `nu=mu/D`, `KL(K_x||nu) <= log D`. The KL chain rule then yields `I(X;Y) <= log D`. The same argument applies conditional on an independent shared seed; `I(U;V) <= I(X;Y,seed) <= log D` because the seed is independent of X. The Bregman loss argument can average over seeds, so it does not require the target error separately for each seed.

Data processing proves the sparse lemma. Countably many null ancillary coordinates can be averaged into encoder randomization, so the same lemma applies to a finite block of alternatives inside an infinite experiment.

### 5.3 Apply the sparse lemma to the ellipsoid

Fix a sufficiently large absolute constant K so that `(1/2)log K-C` is positive. Put `r=Kepsilon` and require `epsilon <= 1/K`. Let

`d=floor((A/(Kepsilon))^{1/alpha})`.

For `i<=d`, `a_i>=r`, so every needle `+/-r e_i` belongs to the ellipsoid. The sparse lemma implies `B_q >= c d`. If `A/epsilon` exceeds another fixed threshold, flooring loses only an absolute factor, giving the theorem's lower bound.

### 5.4 Independent sharper loss and explicit constants

The independent sequence audit found a simpler uniform transformation: define a convex function on posterior probabilities by

`F_r'(p) = tanh(r^{-1} artanh(2p-1))`, `F_r(1/2)=0`.

Extend the derivative by its limiting values -1 and +1 at the endpoints. Symmetry gives `c_r=F_r(0)=F_r(1)`. Its proper losses

`ell_s(q)=c_r-F_r(q)-(s-q)F_r'(q)`, `s in {0,1}`,

lie in `[0,1]`: the s=0 loss increases from 0 to 1 because its derivative is `qF_r''(q)>=0`; the s=1 loss decreases from 1 to 0 because its derivative is `-(1-q)F_r''(q)<=0`.

For the Gaussian sign posterior `p_r(x)=(1+tanh(rx))/2`, `F_r'(p_r(x))=tanh x`. Let `u=F_r'(p)`. Its inverse is `p(u)=(1+tanh(r artanh u))/2`, and

`dp/du=(r/2) sech^2(r artanh u)/(1-u^2) >= r/2`,

since `r<=1`. Changing variables in the Bregman integral gives `D_F(p,q)>= (r/4)(u(p)-u(q))^2`. Therefore under the common null,

`E(tanh X_i-tanh Y_i)^2 <= 4exp(1/2) epsilon/r`.

Now `h(tanh G)=h(G)-2Elog cosh G >= h(G)-1`, using `log cosh x<=x^2/2`. The exact sparse bound becomes

`ln D >= (d/2)ln(r/epsilon) - (5/4+ln 2)d`.

Thus `r=128epsilon<=1` gives `lnD>=0.4828679d` with an explicit constant. This improves the generic constants in the smooth-F proof; it does not change the theorem's order. The audit's full report should remain as independent evidence, rather than being treated as external validation.

### 5.5 Later query-dependent quantum decoding

The lower also survives a weaker decoder interface. After encoding, let the receiver choose one coordinate i and use an arbitrary i-dependent POVM, returning only a scalar `Y_i`. Require that scalar output to have TV error at most epsilon for both means `+/-r e_i`. No common jointly generated Y vector is assumed.

The preceding bounded-loss argument gives a per-coordinate distortion for `U_i=tanh X_i` and `V_i=tanh Y_i`, hence `I(U_i;V_i)>= (1/2)ln(r/epsilon)-(5/4+ln2)`. Let Q denote the finite hybrid quantum memory under the common null input. Data processing gives `I(U_i;V_i)<=I(U_i;Q)`. Independence and the quantum mutual-information chain rule imply

`sum_i I(U_i;Q) <= I(U_1,...,U_d;Q) <= ln D`.

For example, `I(U_i;Q|U_{<i})=I(U_i;Q,U_{<i})>=I(U_i;Q)` because the `U_i` are independent. The final bound is the Holevo information capacity of a D-dimensional state, `S(E sigma_U)-E S(sigma_U)<=lnD`. Continuous variables can be handled by increasing finite measurable partitions; the relevant information quantities are relative-entropic and bounded by lnD. Independent shared seeds are conditioned on throughout, preserving the null product independence.

Consequently the same lower applies even to a single later coordinate query with a quantum measurement chosen after storage. Noncommuting decoder measurements cannot evade this ambient score-retention requirement. This is a continuous random-access consequence of the established Holevo/Nayak method, combined with the newly reconstructed statistical-loss reduction; random-access lower bounds themselves are prior art.

## 6. Consequences with exact supplied models

### 6.1 White noise

For the exact white-noise experiment `dZ(t)=f(t)dt+n^{-1/2}dW(t)` in a supplied orthonormal basis, with coefficient ellipsoid `sum j^{2alpha} f_j^2<=L^2`, whitening gives `theta_j=sqrt(n)f_j` and `A=sqrt(n)L`. Thus the candidate retained-memory order is

`B = Theta_alpha((sqrt(n)L/epsilon)^{1/alpha})`.

No regression/density-estimation comparison is assumed. Transferring this law to another nonparametric experiment requires parameter-independent comparisons whose total errors are much smaller than epsilon, including any dimension-dependent remainders.

For fixed reconstruction tolerance, the exponent of n is `1/(2alpha)`. This is larger than the `1/(2alpha+1)` number-of-bits threshold sufficient for one squared-error minimax estimator in the classical quantized-estimation literature. This difference reflects the full bounded-decision preservation criterion. Neither rate should be relabeled as the other task.

### 6.2 Known mildly ill-posed inverse sequence

Suppose the supplied model is `Z_j=kappa_j f_j+n^{-1/2}xi_j`, with independent Gaussian noise and exact singular directions. If `kappa_j=j^{-p}` and `sum j^{2beta} f_j^2<=L^2`, then `theta_j=sqrt(n)kappa_j f_j` has axes `sqrt(n)L j^{-(beta+p)}`. The theorem gives

`B = Theta_{beta+p}((sqrt(n)L/epsilon)^{1/(beta+p)})`.

Noise-only kernel directions can be regenerated. Unknown operator, basis, noise covariance, singular values, and model misspecification are not covered. The archive reconstructs the observable experiment, not an inverse solution with prescribed function-estimation accuracy.

### 6.3 Exponential axes: uniform amplitude order

Let `a_j=A exp(-c j^p)`, with supplied `c,p>0` and arbitrary `A>0`; the parameter set is again `sum(theta_j/a_j)^2<=1`. For `epsilon<=1/8` and `L=ln(A/epsilon)` sufficiently large depending only on c,p, the same construction and the two lower mechanisms below give

`B_q, B_cl = Theta_{c,p}(L^{1+1/p})`,

uniformly in A. This covers growing sample-size amplitudes as well as shrinking reconstruction tolerance. It does not rely on a sharp scalar hat lemma.

**Upper.** Retain the `k=O_{c,p}(L^{1/p})` axes above epsilon, use the same conditional Gaussian bins, and use `ln(M_j+2)<=C+2ln(a_j/epsilon)`. Since each positive logarithm is at most L, the bit count is `O_{c,p}(kL)=O_{c,p}(L^{1+1/p})`. The full-product KL bound is unchanged because it depends only on the ellipsoid constraint, not on the decay law.

**Lower when `A epsilon<=1`.** Choose the common sparse radius `r=sqrt(A epsilon)<=1` and

`d=floor((L/(2c))^{1/p})`.

For every `j<=d`, `a_j>=A exp(-L/2)=r`, so all admitted needles belong to the ellipsoid. Also `ln(r/epsilon)=L/2`. The dimension-uniform sparse bound gives `lnD>=d(L/4-C)=Omega_{c,p}(L^{1+1/p})` once L is sufficiently large. This covers fixed A, small A, and the entire precision-dominated regime.

**Lower when `A epsilon>1`.** Put `L_A=ln A`. Since `epsilon<1`, `L_A>L/2`. Choose

`k=floor((L_A/(4c))^{1/p})`.

The finite cube `|theta_j|<=a_j/sqrt(k)` for `j<=k`, with all other coordinates zero, lies inside the ellipsoid. On every active coordinate place a uniformly spaced lattice of spacing

`Delta=sqrt(8 ln(16k))`.

Nearest-lattice decoding of the actual observation fails with probability at most `2k exp(-Delta^2/8)=1/8`. The number of coordinate messages is at least `a_j/(sqrt(k)Delta)` when this ratio exceeds one. Because `ln a_j>=3L_A/4`, for sufficiently large L_A (depending only on c,p),

`ln M_j >= 3L_A/4 - (1/2)ln k - ln Delta >= L_A/2`.

Thus the total number of messages satisfies `ln M>=kL_A/2`. Uniform archive TV error at most epsilon<=1/8 changes the nearest-lattice message error by at most epsilon, leaving message error at most 1/4. Fano yields

`I(message;Y)>= (3/4)ln M-ln2`.

The retained-state dimension bounds this accessible information by lnD, by the same trace-domination argument applied to the message-averaged states. Hence `lnD=Omega_{c,p}(kL_A)=Omega_{c,p}(L^{1+1/p})`. The packing argument is standard prior art; its role here is to supply the strong-signal regime that weak needles alone cannot cover.

**Sharp leading candidate in the weak fixed-amplitude regime only.** For fixed `0<A<=1`, a separate sparse sign prior with radius `r_i=a_i` gives `lnD >= (1/2)sum_i ln(a_i/epsilon)-Ck` for axes above a large constant multiple of epsilon. Its leading term is

`[p/(2(p+1)ln2)] c^{-1/p} ln(A/epsilon)^{1+1/p}`

in bits. A matching sharp upper requires the separate no-tail-log exact-reference scalar hat lemma being reconstructed by `sparse_gaussian_upper`. That worker additionally reports a squared-density bound `chi^2(Q_theta||P_theta)<=C theta^2/J^4`; if fully reconstructed, it tensorizes by reverse KL and can remove dimension allocation factors for weak ellipsoids. The lemma's dimension-uniform scalar constant, tails, and true stochastic implementation must be audited before these sharper statements are asserted. The simple KL-bin proof establishes matching order, not this leading coefficient. This coefficient is not claimed uniformly in growing A.

### 6.4 The orders are already necessary for one later spectral query

Consider the weaker task in which the archive is made first, then a coordinate j is supplied, and the receiver uses a j-dependent decoder to return only `Y_j` with `TV(Law_theta(Y_j),N(theta_j,1))<=epsilon` for every admitted theta and every j. A quantum receiver need perform only that single measurement; there is no requirement that incompatible measurements coexist or be nondestructive.

The polynomial lower and the precision-dominated exponential lower apply unchanged by Section 5.5. For the strong exponential regime, take the same admitted product cube of independent uniformly distributed coordinate messages, but use the constant scalar grid spacing `Delta=sqrt(8ln16)`. For each j, actual-observation nearest-grid error is at most 1/8, so the queried decoder's corresponding message error is at most 1/4. Scalar Fano gives

`I(M_j;Y_j)>= (3/4)ln M_j-ln2`.

Independence and Holevo/data processing imply `sum_j I(M_j;Y_j,seed)<=lnD`, even for different j-dependent POVMs. Thus the same strong lower `Omega(k lnA)` holds. This argument uses finite classical message variables, so no continuous cq entropy extension is needed. The conditional Gaussian construction above provides the whole output law and therefore also solves every individual query. Consequently both displayed order laws hold even under this weaker later-single-coordinate criterion.

The later query cannot carry an uncounted copy of the original observation. The result does not imply support for indefinitely repeated nondestructive queries to a finite quantum state. The classical upper can reuse its finite cell labels, with the stated marginal guarantee for each draw; their joint law is not claimed close to independent draws from the unknown source.

## 7. Resource accounting and limits

* Supplied: axes, basis, alpha/A or equivalent singular model, target epsilon, and the usual fresh ideal uniform random source for stochastic kernels. Conditional Gaussian samplers are constructed explicitly above. No inverse operator or representation is learned.
* Acquired: only the first `k=O((A/epsilon)^{1/alpha})` real coordinates of the observed sequence. No other input-dependent record survives.
* Retained: the vector of bin indices, total binary storage `O_alpha(k)`; the known-model program and parameters are input-independent.
* Ideal-real arithmetic encoder work: O(k) bin comparisons/floors after evaluating the supplied per-coordinate thresholds. No alphabet-sized table is constructed. An actual bit-complexity guarantee needs a precision/input-name specification and computable supplied parameters.
* Decoder: each retained coordinate is a univariate conditioned-normal draw with an explicit rejection sampler requiring fewer than 21 proposals in expectation, uniformly in A; each discarded coordinate is fresh N(0,1). No CDF table or rare-event reference conditioning is required. This defines a countable-product kernel. A finite prefix of length N can be generated lazily with O(N) expected ideal-real sampling operations, but a literal infinite real sequence is not emitted in finite time.
* Exact continuous TV cannot be verified by a finite floating-point sampler whose output is supported on a lattice: that lattice law and a continuous Gaussian have TV 1. A physically meaningful finite-bit output criterion or an ideal continuous source is a separate interface. Finite scripts test numerical formulas and finite-prefix behavior only.
* Independent shared random seeds are allowed and do not evade the lower. A free entangled receiver state can change the communication accounting and is excluded.
* The theorem concerns retained information. It does not give efficient acquisition of the basis/operator, a universal archive under an unknown model, or efficient execution of arbitrary infinite-observation decision rules.

## 9. Decisive falsification targets and retained failures

1. A counterexample to the scalar ray conditional-variance bound or to the sign of the negative-theta KL integral would break the explicit upper. Both have direct exact formulas and are independently being checked.
