C07_L07 EXACT/INTERVAL SPIN CERTIFICATES — 7 October 2026

STATUS AND CLAIM
For rational public M,B>=0 and each integer N>=1 with 4N>M^2, the script
spin_certificates.py computes a finite dyadic upper enclosure of the supplied
finite-N separability certificate

  E_N = min(1, (3M/(2N^(3/2))) U_N/D_N),
  D_N = sum_j a_j exp(-M r_j^2-B r_j),
  U_N = sum_j a_j exp(M r_j^2+B r_j)
       [4M r_j^2+(B/2)r_j+(4M r_j^2+B r_j)^2/3],
  a_j=(2j+1)^2/(N+1) binom(N+1,N/2-j) exp(2j(j+1)/N),
  r_j=(j+1/2)/N^(3/4).

Every arithmetic operation in the public sum is outward rounded on a fixed
binary grid. The positivity of D_N is certified by a strictly positive lower
endpoint. The exact multiplicity check sums (2j+1)m_N(j)=2^N. The size test
4N>M^2 is an exact rational comparison equivalent to t0>0. This evaluates the
bound already proved in the baseline; it does not prove a sharper theorem or
claim optimality.

The exponential enclosure uses monotonicity and range reduction to y in
[0,1/2]. The nonnegative Taylor terms through degree p+16 are accumulated with
outward rounding. The tail is at most twice the first omitted term, since each
successive term ratio is at most 1/2. Repeated interval squaring undoes the
range reduction. Negative arguments are enclosed by interval reciprocals.
Fourth roots for N^(3/4), and square roots for the leading N factor, use exact
integer root calculations on scaled rationals. The finite sector summation and
all binomial coefficients are exact integers/rationals before enclosure.

The same artifact implements a rational 3x3, gap-free square-root routine. It
checks C>=0 and C<=2M I by the seven principal minors (all exactly rational).
For requested q>0, set alpha=(q/4)^2 and D=C+alpha I. Then
alpha I<=D<=(2M+alpha)I. Choose dyadic gamma>=sqrt(2M+alpha) with overshoot
at most q/4; since sqrt(2M+alpha)>=q/4, gamma<=2sqrt(2M+alpha). The exact
Newton map F(X)=X/2+(D X^-1+X^-1 D)/4 starts at gamma I and commutes with D.
For each eigenvalue lambda, z=x/sqrt(lambda) obeys z_next=(z+1/z)/2. While
z>=2, z_next/z<=5/8; after z<=2, e=z-1 obeys e_next<=e^2/2. The code computes
integer warm-up and quadratic iteration counts by rational inequalities, not
floating logarithms or eigenvalue decisions. It obtains exact rational updates
and rounds symmetric entries to a dyadic grid.

For the rounded recursion, the exact iterate has least eigenvalue >=sqrt(alpha).
If the computed iterate is within sqrt(alpha)/4, its inverse norm is at most
2/sqrt(alpha). The inverse identity gives the conservative one-step Lipschitz
constant Lnewt=1+4(2M+alpha)/alpha. The code chooses each rounding error r no
larger than min(q,sqrt(alpha))/(16 k Lnewt^k), where k is the total number of
steps. A 3x3 entrywise nearest-grid rounding contributes operator error at most
its Frobenius bound 3/(2*2^P). Telescoping therefore keeps the iteration error
below min(q,sqrt(alpha))/16 and closes the invertibility induction. The exact
Newton error is <=q/4 and the regularization bias
||sqrt(C+alpha I)-sqrt(C)||<=sqrt(alpha)=q/4. Including rounding gives the
stated total 33q/64<q. No eigenvalue gap, eigenspace choice, or eigenvector
continuity is used.

EXECUTABLE EVIDENCE

outward interval finite-N certificates for N=1,M=1.99 (near the t0 boundary),
N=2,M=2.8 (near 2 sqrt(2)), and N=8,64,M=0.1,B=0.2; an M=0 exact-zero case;
and exact rejections at/beyond the t0 boundary. The square-root fixtures include
C=0, a rationally rotated spectrum (0,10^-40,1/5), and a repeated eigenvalue.
They verify exact PSD/cap checks, positive definiteness of every rounded Newton
iterate, and an exact Frobenius error check for C=0. A negative eigenvalue
of -2^-200 and an eigenvalue exceeding 2M are rejected exactly. This is bounded
local execution evidence; the general q error guarantee follows from the
written perturbation inequalities, not from those fixtures.

INPUTS, COSTS, AND LIMITS
Inputs are rational M,B,N for the sector routine; exact rational symmetric C,
rational M, and rational q for the square-root routine. The declared model
supplies C=A+M I>=0 and ||C||<=2M. The routine does not acquire unknown
coefficients, nor does it implement interval midpoint acquisition from an
arbitrary coefficient oracle. Sector work is O(N) terms with fixed-point
precision p; the exp routine uses O(p) Taylor steps per evaluation, plus
range-reduction work and output bits proportional to the exponent magnitude.
Thus its bit work is polynomial in N and requested precision when public M,B
are fixed; for growing M,B, their numeric magnitudes and the resulting output
range must also be charged. The matrix routine uses a fixed 3x3 rational
inversion and k exact updates at P guard bits; P is chosen from the displayed
Lnewt^k stability budget. Native one-qubit
preparation, random-normal sampling, and physical gate compilation are not
implemented here. The product-preparation theorem and its imported assumptions
remain those in the baseline.
