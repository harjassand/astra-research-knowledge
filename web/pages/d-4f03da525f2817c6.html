# Unknown ordinary rates preserve the rare-edge endpoint exponent

6 October 2026. Root-requested finite-graph acquisition lane; no children. Status: complete exact-observation construction and proof below; numerical fixtures and independent gates are recorded separately. The ordinary rates are acquired from passive data, never inserted as known values in the rare-rate inversion. Fixed graph/accuracy constants can be poor. This is not an unknown-support or hidden-state theorem.

## Interface and theorem

The directed baseline graph G0 is known, finite, irreducible, on n>=3 labeled states. Every present ordinary edge has an unknown rate in the supplied compact interval [qmin,qmax], 0<qmin<=qmax<infinity. All absent ordinary edges remain absent. The baseline edge u->v is absent, and its shortest baseline path length is the known integer L>=2. The actual generator is Q=A+bB, where A has precisely the supplied baseline support and B=e_u(e_v-e_u)^T. A single unknown b is bracketed in [beta,2beta], 0<beta<=1/2. The initial state is fixed independently of parameters. Each passive read gives the full state and calibrated timestamp; there is no access inside an interval, reset, jump sensor, or supplied stationary distribution.

For every fixed desired relative error epsilon in(0,1] and failure tolerance delta in(0,1), an explicit fixed-lag, no-reset estimator satisfies

    Pr{|bhat-b|<=epsilon beta} >=1-delta,
    E endpoint reads <= C(G0,qmin,qmax,epsilon,delta)
                           beta^(-L/(L-1)),
    E physical time <= C(...) / beta.

The same exponent holds with a deterministic cap at fixed delta. All nuisance-rate acquisition, source-state finding and rejected/non-useful readings are charged. For nontrivial fixed precision/confidence (for example epsilon<1/3 and delta<=0.1), the known-baseline subfamily supplies the existing minimax endpoint lower bound; hence this enlarged nuisance family has the same optimal exponent. The lower claim is not made for loose accuracies already met by the supplied bracket. Its physical-time lower bound remains order1/b. Those lower bounds are the independently reconstructed results in r17 GENERAL_GRAPH_CANDIDATE.md and r08 cycle5/general_graph_gate.md, not a new proof of them here.

The proof only needs constant ordinary-rate precision at fixed epsilon. A separate fixed-additive entropy-production goal may need precision shrinking as1/log(1/b); that objective is not silently equated with relative estimation of b.

## Uniform supplied constants

Let dmax be the maximum ordinary outdegree, R=dmax qmax, C=2(R+1), D=2dmax, T=R+1 and J=C²/2. All depend only on supplied bounds and graph, not on unknown rates. For each pair x,y choose a shortest baseline directed path of length d_xy (d_xx=0), and set

    h_xy=exp(-(R+1)) qmin^(d_xy)/d_xy!,
    eta=min{1/(2n), min_xy h_xy}>0.

Exactly following that path and making no other jump proves P_Q(1)[x,y]>=h_xy uniformly over every nuisance rate and b<=1. Thus the true stationary law satisfies pi_i>=eta, and every lag in[1,infinity) has all entries>=eta.

Set

    Astar=D C^(L-1) exp(C)/(L-1)!,
    Kstar=Astar+4D,
    e=epsilon/2,
    Delta=min{1, e/[8Kstar]}.

Choose any explicitly supplied computable kappa>0 satisfying

    kappa<=min{1, 1/[4(R+1)], 1/(2C),
                 (L!/R^L)^(1/(L-1)), Delta/(2J)}.

Our lag is t=kappa beta^(1/(L-1)). This choice may be very conservative. It preserves the exponent uniformly for the entire local bracket, rather than invoking an unquantified sufficiently-small-b threshold.

For a rational arithmetic implementation one may instead choose a dyadic lag between half this value and this value. The proof uses only t^(L-1)<=kappa^(L-1)beta and the stated upper bounds on t; halving t changes costs by a fixed factor. Such a lag is computably acquired from the supplied bracket and bounds, without inserting the unknown b into the clock.

## Load-bearing nuisance sensitivity

Suppose A and Ahat have the same supplied support, all positive rates in the compact interval, and maximum ordinary-edge rate error<=Delta. Let E=Ahat-A. Its infinity matrix norm is <=D Delta. Interpolate A_theta=A+theta E, which remains an admitted generator. We prove

    |[exp(t(Ahat+bB))-exp(t(A+bB))]_(u,v)|
       <= Astar Delta t^L + 2D Delta b t².     (S)

At b=0, expand the derivative in theta:

    partial_theta exp(tA_theta)
      =sum_(k>=1) t^k/k! sum_(j=0)^(k-1)
                                   A_theta^j E A_theta^(k-1-j).

Every nonzero monomial uses only baseline off-diagonal edges and diagonal stays. An entry u,v needs at least L factors, regardless of which one is marked E. Hence all terms k<L vanish. Taking norms in the remaining series yields

    |partial_theta exp(tA_theta)_(u,v)|
      <=D Delta sum_(k>=L) t^k C^(k-1)/(k-1)!
      <=Astar Delta t^L, 0<t<=1.

This is exactly where the known support matters: an unknown shortcut would destroy the t^L factor.

To bound the b-dependent correction, differentiate a second time. The mixed Duhamel derivative is the sum of two ordered integrals, with one E insertion and one B insertion. Every intervening propagator exp(s(A_theta+cB)) is stochastic for c in[0,b], so its infinity norm equals1. Each triangular integration domain has area t²/2. Since ||B||_infinity=2,

    ||partial_c partial_theta exp(t(A_theta+cB))||_infinity
       <=2D Delta t².

Integrate c from0 to b and theta from0 to1 to prove(S). No matrix logarithm, embeddability inference, commutation of E and B, reversibility or diagonalization is assumed.

At our lag t^(L-1)=kappa^(L-1) beta, and b<=2beta. Thus(S) is bounded by

    Delta [Astar kappa^(L-1)+4D t] beta t
       <=Delta Kstar beta t<=e beta t/8.       (SCALE)

The nuisance uncertainty need only be constant in b to meet this target.

## Stage 1: actually acquire all ordinary rates

For each state i, collect m0 complete return cycles of the sampled chain observed at lag t. The first transition in each cycle is a useful departure from i; retain its endpoint. Strong Markov at each observed return makes these departure endpoints iid with row law P_Q(t)[i,:]. Complete the last cycle before moving to the next state block. Across ordinary target labels in one row the counts are multinomial; independence across columns is unnecessary.

For each supplied ordinary edge i->j, let phat_ij be its empirical frequency. Define

    qhat_ij=clip_[qmin,qmax](phat_ij/t),
    Ahat_ii=-sum_(j!=i) qhat_ij,

and keep every absent baseline off-diagonal entry, including u,v, exactly zero. Thus Ahat is a valid generator on the supplied graph, rather than the logarithm of an arbitrary empirical stochastic matrix.

The exact semigroup remainder satisfies

    exp(tQ)-I-tQ=integral_0^t (t-s) Q² exp(sQ) ds,
    ||exp(tQ)-I-tQ||_infinity<=C²t²/2=Jt².

For every ordinary edge Q_ij=A_ij, so |P_Q(t)[i,j]/t-A_ij|<=Jt<=Delta/2. Also P_Q(t)[i,j]<=Tt, by the probability of at least one actual jump. Let m_edges be the known number of ordinary edges and set ell0=log(6m_edges/delta). Bernstein and a union bound show the simultaneous rate-error event max|qhat-q|<=Delta has failure<=delta/3 if

    m0=ceil{[8T+(4/3)Delta]ell0/(t Delta²)}.

Clipping shrinks error to every true rate in the supplied interval. The unknown rare edge's effect is already included in the semigroup remainder: no independence or zero-rare approximation of the actual data law is inserted. Since Delta is fixed, this stage requires O(1/t) useful departures, not O(1/(beta t)).

## Stage 2: invert the acquired model, with bias charged

After calibration, collect m1 complete sampled return cycles at u in a fresh block. Let phat be the proportion whose first endpoint is v. Conditional on the entire calibration history, these Bernoulli outcomes are iid with true mean r=P_Q(t)[u,v]. Calibration affects only the retained Ahat, not the physical generator or observation clock.

Define the **estimated** model function

    rhat(c)=[exp(t(Ahat+cB))]_(u,v), c in[beta,2beta].

For every admitted baseline, coupling the added rare clock with baseline motion gives

    P_(A+cB)(t)[u,v] <=ct+(Rt)^L/L!<=3beta t.

Differentiating in c gives

    rhat'(c)=integral_0^t P_(Ahat+cB)(s)[u,u]
                    [P_(Ahat+cB)(t-s)[v,v]
                     -P_(Ahat+cB)(t-s)[u,v]] ds.

Every diagonal is at least exp(-Tt)>=exp(-1/4), and an off-diagonal probability is at most Tt<=1/4. Therefore rhat'(c)>=t/4 throughout the bracket. This monotonicity is uniform even after data-dependent calibration.

Let ell1=log(6/delta), and

    m1=ceil{400 ell1/(e² beta t)}.

Bernstein with true mean<=3beta t gives |phat-r|<=e beta t/8 with failure<=delta/3. On the calibration event, (SCALE) gives |r-rhat(b)|<=e beta t/8. Define bexact by projecting phat onto [rhat(beta),rhat(2beta)] and inverting rhat. Since rhat(b) is inside that interval, projection cannot increase its distance from phat. Hence

    |bexact-b| <=(4/t)(e beta t/8+e beta t/8)
                 <=e beta=epsilon beta/2.

This is not inversion using a known A. The acquired nuisance error explicitly consumes half the statistical error ledger.

## No-reset acquisition and all physical costs

Let k=ceil(1/t). At k-step blocks the transition matrix is P_Q(kt), kt in[1,1+t], and has all entries>=eta. Consequently the expected number of endpoint reads to first find any desired state is at most k/eta. Each completed return cycle at state i has mean length1/pi_i<=1/eta by Kac's identity for the finite sampled chain. This identity applies also to self-loops (a one-step return); charging a worst-case hit after every departure would spuriously insert an extra1/t.

There are n ordinary calibration blocks plus the rare block. Starting arbitrarily and changing blocks incurs at most n+1 initial hits. Uniformly over the unknown rates,

    H=[(n+1)k+n m0+m1]/eta,
    E N_total<=H,
    E T_total=t E N_total<=t H,
    E actual jumps<=(R+1)t H.

No hidden preparation, resets, stationary sampling or supplied-label oracle is used. For fixed graph/bounds/epsilon/delta,

    H=O(beta^(-L/(L-1))),
    tH=O(1/beta).

Calibration and all initial hits contribute only O(1/t) readings and O(1) physical time. To obtain a deterministic endpoint cap, stop with a declared failure if N reaches ceil(3H/delta). Markov gives cap failure<=delta/3. The three failure allocations sum to delta. The cap also bounds elapsed time deterministically by t ceil(3H/delta), and preserves the exponents at fixed confidence.

## Computation, retained bits and finite precision

The statistical theorem above is for calibrated exact endpoint observations. Counting needs O(n²) integers, each with O(log Ncap) bits; all passive timestamps/state records may additionally be retained at O(Ncap) entries. Supplied qmin,qmax,beta and clock units must be computable, with an explicit precision interface. Arbitrary noncomputable real inputs do not provide an algorithm.

There is a stable finite-arithmetic implementation; this does not require a noise-sensitive matrix logarithm. Keep e=epsilon/2 above, leaving epsilon beta/2 for numerical inversion. At C t<=1/2, truncate the exponential series after K terms. Its infinity-norm tail is at most exp(1/2)2^(-(K+1)); therefore K=O(log(1/(epsilon beta t))) terms suffice for a per-evaluation error xi=epsilon beta t/128. Exact rational arithmetic can be used when t is rational; otherwise enclose t and the input rates with certified intervals. Global Duhamel gives probability perturbation at most t||Delta Q||_infinity+C|Delta t|, so choose input enclosures making this <=xi. This conservative numerical condition costs O(log(1/(epsilon beta t))) bits for fixed graph, separate from the physical observation clock.

For inversion, evaluate rhat(beta),rhat(2beta) and perform monotone bisection. With each probability evaluation error<=xi, compare its enclosing interval to phat. If the intervals overlap, then |rhat(c)-phat|<=2xi; comparing to the exact clipped inverse gives |c-bexact|<=8xi/t<=epsilon beta/16 (the same bound holds if the clipped inverse is an endpoint). Otherwise discard the certified side. Stop when the retained rate interval has width<=epsilon beta/4, and return its midpoint. Then numerical rate error<=epsilon beta/4, leaving slack inside the total epsilon beta target. It takes O(log(1/epsilon)) probability evaluations, plus endpoint checks; dimensions are fixed. Straight Taylor multiplication costs O(n³ K) arithmetic per evaluation. Rounding is enclosed or bounded; ordinary-rate approximation must also be charged, either as an extra Delta/4 margin in stage1 or inside the numerical probability budget. The former replaces Delta by a smaller constant and leaves the same exponents.

Sensor timestamp quantization, state-label error, detector dead time, physical clock jitter, and finite-bit input acquisition are not implied away by this arithmetic proof. A specific noisy-clock law is a changed observation interface. The reference theorem observes calibrated lag/timestamps, as did the supplied-baseline result.

## Consequence, obstructions, comparison and prior art

The shortest baseline detour exponent is preserved after acquiring unknown compact ordinary rates. A competent equally informed comparator can use the same estimator: this is an information/measurement consequence, not an agent advantage. Ordinary rates can be estimated much less accurately than b because graph support suppresses their contribution to the rare endpoint by t^L. Unknown support would remove that suppression; another unknown rate tending to zero would remove the uniform eta/bounded-rate constants. Those are real interface boundaries.

Fixed additive entropy-production accuracy is distinct. Its leading log(1/b) coefficient involves the unknown stationary reverse flux, so constant nuisance error can yield an unbounded additive bias. r08 identified this obstruction. Obtaining nuisance precision O(1/log(1/b)) is plausibly cheaper than rare acquisition on a local bracket, but requires a separate precision/conditioning theorem; no EP conclusion is asserted here.

Nearest established ingredients are first-order generator estimation, matrix exponential inversion, Bernstein bounds and regenerative sampling. Israel–Rosenthal–Wei's author snapshot (revised October2000, published Mathematical Finance April2001) §2 Theorems1–2 gives a convergent logarithm series near the identity and §3 stresses that empirical inversion need not yield nonnegative generator entries. Our construction uses known support and clipped P/t rates instead. Duffie–Glynn2004 develops identification/GMM from random-time observations; its timing interface differs from this calibrated fixed-lag design. Neither read excerpt supplies this boundary-rate, shortest-detour finite-cost theorem. Barendregt–Webb–Kilpatrick arXiv2205.10371v2 (verified1February2023) Section2.3 Algorithm3 already optimizes successive endpoint sample times by expected posterior covariance for arbitrary finite CTMCs, and Section3.3.2 Eq12 supplies matrix-exponential ring likelihoods and asymmetric-rate experiments. This is a closer competent adaptive comparator; the reviewed excerpts do not give the shortest-detour boundary minimax/acquisition theorem. Historical novelty is UNVERIFIED, not established by missing matches in these readings.

## Fixed minimum lag is a separate nuisance problem

Root's supplied-baseline sensor-resolution theorem permits a minimum lag tau and chooses t=max(tau,kappa beta^(1/(L-1))). The known-baseline tradeoff cannot silently be combined with this nuisance theorem. At a general lag, (S) requires ordinary-rate accuracy of order

    epsilon beta/[t^(L-1)+beta t]

to retain a rare-probability bias of order epsilon beta t. At fixed tau>0 this target shrinks proportionally to beta, whereas the first-order P/t discretization error is only bounded by Jt. More samples do not remove that deterministic bias. A much smaller calibration lag would repair it only if the observation interface allowed that lag; it is not legal under the same dead-time floor. A joint generator likelihood or high-order inversion might close that separate case. This note proves neither its impossibility nor its matching resource bound.
