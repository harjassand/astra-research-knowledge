## Statement and exact consequence

Let Q be a real symmetric positive-definite3-by-3 matrix and

    f(y)=(y^{circ2})^T Q y^{circ2},
    H=Hess f, h=det H, F=adj H grad h, D=F dot grad.

Then

    E(y)=det[y_i; F_i; D F_i]_(i=1,2,3)

is not identically zero. Polynomial GL covariance extends this to f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2), any real invertible L. For the original expanded rational input promise with rational unknown L,Q, the cycle9 rational line/reflection/metric/certificate composition consequently has expected polynomial bit cost on **every ternary input**. No numerical metric, attraction basin or condition number is supplied.

The proof uses three-variable projective geometry. It does not identify higher-dimensional extactic vanishing with a fixed ratio of linear forms.

## 1. A zero three-coordinate extactic gives a pencil of projective lines

Work over C. First suppose the projectivized homogeneous field is nonzero, i.e. y and F are generically linearly independent. On that open set put C(y)=y cross F(y). If E=0, linear algebra over C(y) gives

    D F=A(y)y+B(y)F.

Therefore

    D C = y cross D F=B(y)C.

Ratios of the three components of C are rational first integrals. Along each nonsingular integral curve the projective normal[C] is constant. Since C(y)^T y=0, that curve lies on the fixed projective line defined by C(initial)^T y=0. A nonconstant projective integral curve is a nonempty analytic open subset of that line, so its line is invariant globally by polynomial continuation.

Here is a self-contained argument that the generic lines form one pencil. Represent the projectivized field by the homogeneous one-form C(y) dot dy (up to the usual cross-product encoding), and remove the common polynomial divisor of its components. Its remaining singular set in P² is finite: a positive-dimensional common zero set would contain an irreducible projective curve, whose homogeneous equation would divide every component, contrary to primitivity.

The closure Γ of the normal-map image y->[C(y)] is irreducible. Its dimension is1: the generic fibers contain a line leaf, while a constant normal would force the same nonzero linear form to vanish at all generic y. Fix a generic invariant line L in Γ. Every other generic invariant line intersects L at a singular point, because a regular one-dimensional foliation cannot have two distinct tangent directions at their intersection. The intersection map Γ--->L has image in the finite singular set on L. Irreducibility makes that rational map constant. Thus every generic leaf line passes through one fixed point p, and Γ is the corresponding projective pencil.

The formulation using C·dy needs only its annihilated tangent direction; concretely C=y cross F is orthogonal to both radial y and projective direction F. Removing a common factor changes neither tangent directions nor the leaf lines. All statements are at generic nonsingular points and extend by polynomial identities.

Every hidden coordinate sign flip commutes with F and preserves its projective foliation. It consequently preserves the unique pencil center p. The only projective points fixed by all independent coordinate sign flips are the three coordinate-axis points. Hence p=e_i for some i. In coordinates on either affine chart not containing p, tangent motion along this pencil means

    y_j F_k-y_k F_j=0 identically,

where {j,k} are the other two indices.

If F is projectively radial from the start, this same pair identity holds for every choice of i. It is therefore enough to rule out that identity for each possible coordinate center, which also disposes of the fully radial case.

## 2. The binary principal quartic and its Hessian are coprime

Write the binary principal restriction as

    p(u,v)=a u^4+2b u²v²+c v^4,

where a,c>0 and ac-b²>0. Its roots on P¹ have squared ratio

    (u/v)²=(-b +/- i sqrt(ac-b²))/a.

Both ratios are nonreal and nonzero. Their four square roots are distinct, so p is squarefree over C. Its Hessian determinant is

    h_2=48[ab u^4+(3ac-b²)u²v²+bc v^4]
       =48[b p+3(ac-b²)u²v²].                  (1)

At every root of p, u,v are nonzero and h_2!=0. Therefore gcd(p,h_2)=1 over C.

For every nonzero real even quadratic q=r u²+s v², one likewise has gcd(p,q)=1: its squared-coordinate root ratio is real (or a coordinate axis), while p's two squared-coordinate root ratios are nonreal. Hence gcd(p,q h_2)=1.

## 3. Exclude every possible coordinate pencil center

Fix i, and let z=(y_j,y_k). Put

    q(z)=4[Q_ij y_j²+Q_ik y_k²],
    p(z)=f|_(y_i=0),   H_2=Hess p, h_2=det H_2.

At y_i=0 the full Hessian splits as diag(q,H_2), with the block positions permuted. Its determinant is q h_2, and the adjugate lower block is q adj H_2. Since the normal derivative vanishes by parity, the restricted tangential field is

    F_rest|_(y_i=0)=q adj H_2 grad(q h_2).        (2)

For every binary degree-four p and any polynomial g, Euler's identity H_2 z=3 grad p gives the polynomial identity

    det[z, adj H_2 grad g]=3 Jac(p,g).           (3)

If q is not identically zero, the proposed pencil identity restricted to this face, together with(2)-(3), yields

    Jac(p,q h_2)=0.

Let g=q h_2, homogeneous degree6. On v!=0 write p=v^4 P(u/v), g=v^6 G(u/v). The zero Jacobian says6P'G-4PG'=0, so G²/P³ is constant. Equivalently g²=d p³ with d!=0 because g is nonzero. This contradicts the coprimality in Section2 (or the odd multiplicity of every simple p factor in p³).

If q is identically zero, then Q_ij=Q_ik=0, and the quartic splits as

    f=Q_ii y_i^4+p(z).

Its Hessian is block diagonal for all y. Direct determinant/adjugate multiplication gives

    F_rest=(12Q_ii)^2 y_i^4 adj H_2 grad h_2.

The pencil identity would therefore imply Jac(p,h_2)=0 after canceling a nonzero polynomial factor. But the nonzero classical binary sextic is

    Jac(p,h_2)=1152 uv(ac-b²)(a u^4-c v^4),

which is impossible. Positive Q_ii and PD of the binary principal block ensure every canceled factor is nonzero.

Thus no coordinate center can carry the projective pencil, and F cannot be radial. Section1 rules out E=0. QED.

## 4. Exact scope, prior and next higher-dimensional obstruction

Only positivity of diagonal entries and PD of all two-by-two principal blocks were used in the nonvanishing proof. Full QPD is retained for the acquisition/certificate composition because its metric existence and axis identifiability need coercivity and positive squared contrast. No wider promise is silently adopted.

The projective-line argument is closely related to Pereira's planar extactic/first-integral theorem. Classical binary quartic Hessian and sextic identities are prior. The proof above reconstructs the specific ternary detector consequence directly; its historical novelty is unverified. Ternary rational frame acquisition can already be implemented through established smooth-quartic automorphism routines and commuting involutions, as recorded by `metric_prior`; this is not a first ternary acquisition claim.

The argument stops in dimension3. In P², a nonconstant trajectory contained in a line contains an open subset of that entire line, making the line invariant. In P³ or higher, a trajectory lying in a hyperplane need not make that whole hyperplane invariant; intersections of hyperplanes also leave positive-dimensional tangent spaces. Consequently neither the finite-singular-set pencil argument nor a fixed linear-ratio conclusion transfers automatically. A universal arbitrary-n theorem still requires another mechanism or an exact PD counterexample.
