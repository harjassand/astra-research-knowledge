    D C[v]=R Ω(v) R,

Thus ||D C[v]||_F≤√2||v|| and ||D² C[u,v]||_F≤2||u||||v||. For B(x)=C(x)^T B_0 C(x), with ||B_0||_2=1, the four terms of its second derivative give

    ||D² B[u,v]||_F≤8||u||||v||.

## Frozen-ball theorem

Let x_0,s be exact rationals (dyadic floats interpreted exactly are allowed), J_0=D F(x_0), and B an arbitrary specified real square matrix. Suppose validated arithmetic supplies

    η≥||B F(x_0)||_2,
    δ≥||I−B J_0||_2,  δ<1,
    b≥||B||_2.

If 4b L_2 η<(1−δ)², choose r=2η/(1−δ). On the real closed ball ||x−x_0||≤r, G(x)=x−BF(x) has derivative norm at most δ+bL_2r<(1+δ)/2<1 and displaces the center by at most η. Consequently it maps the ball into itself and is a contraction. Its unique fixed point solves F=0: δ<1 makes B J_0 invertible, so B is invertible. This is a genuine finite-s root enclosure. A small floating residual without the interval bounds is insufficient.

Frozen artifacts must include: exact s; x_0; ordered coordinate pairs and Ω convention; rational base witness and hash; F,J_0 and approximate inverse B arrays with datatype; rigorous residual/Jacobian-rounding bounds; η,δ,b; chosen radius; and all failed candidates. Numerical singular values are diagnostics only. ## Efficient Jacobian evaluation identity

Let T_v=C^T D C[v]=R^T Ω(v)R, so D B[v]=[B,T_v]. For coordinate (i,j), put H=B R^T, P=M_s R^T, Q=M_s H, where M_s is the spectral Schur multiplier matrix. Then the full unprojected derivative row vector is

    H[:,i]·P[:,j]−H[:,j]·P[:,i]−R[i,:]·Q[:,j]+R[j,:]·Q[:,i].

Products here are entrywise. Subtract its mean and retain the first d−1 coordinates. This computes all Jacobian columns from a few d×d products per physical generator, rather than differentiating a full matrix inverse for each of 756 directions. Exact/outward arithmetic is still required for a certificate.
