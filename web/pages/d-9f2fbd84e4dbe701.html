1. Compactness topology and linear extension
T(H_out)=K(H_out)*, and K(H_out) is separable. The bounded trace-norm ball
is therefore weak-star compact and metrizable, permitting sequential
subsequence extraction. All channel images of a finite-rank matrix unit
have norm<=1. A countable diagonal subsequence handles every matrix unit.
Finite linear combinations converge linearly; lower semicontinuity gives
||Gamma X||_1<=||X||_1 on that dense span. Its trace-norm extension is unique.
Uniform contraction permits approximation before the compact-test pairing,
so the SAME subsequence converges on EVERY input in T(H_in), including
uncountably many promised states. No diagonalization over labels is used.

2. Complete positivity, lost trace, and normality
For a positive finite matrix amplification input, finite-rank input/output
compressions reduce positivity to finite matrices. Entrywise compact-test
convergence of positive Lambda_n outputs preserves positivity. Input
trace-norm approximation removes the finite-input restriction. Thus every
finite amplification of Gamma is positive. Trace nonincrease follows from
Tr Gamma(X)=sup_P Tr(P Gamma(X))<=Tr X for positive X and finite-rank P.
A bounded predual map T(H_in)->T(H_out) automatically has a normal dual
map B(H_out)->B(H_in). Normality is established by this predual, not assumed
from a weak limit of Heisenberg maps. Gamma is a legitimate normal CP
subchannel even when all of the output trace escapes.

3. Entanglement breaking under the weaker limit
For finite ancillary dimension k, amplification is a finite matrix of
trace-class entries, hence converges against compact output tests. A
finite-rank local output compression has finite-dimensional range, where
this becomes norm convergence. Local compression preserves the cone of
unnormalized separable positive operators, which is closed in that finite
space. Increasing the output projection then gives trace-norm convergence
to the positive trace-class limit, preserving separability in its defined
closed cone. Infinite ancillary normal states can be compressed on the
ancillary marginal; the discarded positive mass tends to zero, and CP
trace-nonincreasing maps are trace-norm contractions. This proves the
claim for every ancilla. A nonseparable ancillary ambient space causes no
issue because a normal state's marginal has separable support.

This argument does not infer weak-star closure from Shirokov's separate
strong-closure theorem. It proves the required subchannel closure directly.
It also makes no rank-one Kraus or discrete-measure assumption about the
original EB sequence.

4. Escaped-mass lower bound and common completion
The pinching inequality in the source is exact. For P finite rank, Q=I-P,
 ||y_n-tau||_1>=||P(y_n-tau)P||_1+Tr Q(y_n-tau)Q
              =||P(y_n-tau)P||_1+Tr P tau-Tr P y_n.
For fixed P, its finite corner converges. Letting P increase to I then gives
 liminf ||y_n-tau||_1>=||y-tau||_1+1-Tr y.
The extra term, absent from generic weak-star norm lower semicontinuity,
is precisely what pays for restoring trace.

The defect functional is Tr(MX), M=I-Gamma*(I)>=0. One FIXED normal
output preparation sigma0 completes Gamma to Lambda(X)=Gamma(X)+Tr(MX)sigma0.
Both CP and EB survive this sum of CP EB subchannels, and total trace is
exactly restored. For every promised rho and its normal target tau_rho,
 ||Lambda(rho)-tau_rho||_1
 <=||Gamma(rho)-tau_rho||_1+1-Tr Gamma(rho)
 <=liminf_n||Lambda_n(rho)-tau_rho||_1.
The same sigma0, map, and subsequence work for all targets. A minimizing
sequence has each error<=e+1/n, so Lambda attains the infimum e.
No interchange of a supremum with a weak limit is required.

5. Boundary/control examples and scope
Preparation channels onto |n><n| converge weak-star to zero, showing why
trace preservation cannot be assumed at the intermediate limit. The lost

allows an arbitrary normalized completion without increasing that bound.
The proof handles this extreme case, singular outputs, uncountable input
families and repeated labels with distinct prescribed targets.

The theorem allows arbitrary normal EB channels. Requiring finitely many
outcomes, a bounded alphabet, bounded output energy, a specific invariant
state, covariance without a compatible completion, or other physical
implementation restrictions changes the feasible class; this audit does
not cover those constrained optimization problems.

The separate finite-outcome-density argument for trace-compact input
families is also valid: compress the input uniformly, restrict a chosen

finite-input image, then use finite-dimensional EB decomposition and lift.

attainment. The frozen commuting infinite-linear-span example correctly
