# Stochastic-to-deterministic gates for density-dependent reaction networks

**Status:** internal reconstruction of an exact finite-volume counterexample and an exact stationary crossover; standard ingredients and related chemical examples are established prior art. No novelty or priority claim. The numerical table is a direct evaluation of exact birth–death recurrences, not a simulation or an independent mathematical validation.

## Main finding

The stable positive equilibrium of a fluid-limit ODE does not imply stochastic permanence, even for a one-dimensional, bounded, density-dependent mass-action CRN. In the simplest SIS/autocatalytic model, every finite-volume chain eventually loses its catalyst/infective count with probability one, while its quasi-stationary law concentrates at the stable positive ODE equilibrium and its mean extinction time is exponential in volume.

A sharper boundary test is available by adding a nucleation reaction whose per-molecule rate vanishes as η_V = exp(−αV). This addition disappears from the fluid-limit vector field uniformly, but the true finite-V stationary limit changes at an explicit action threshold Δ. The invariant law converges to the extinct boundary if α>Δ and to the positive equilibrium if α≤Δ. This identifies a missing gate for long-time inference: the boundary source scale must be compared with the rare-exit action, even when it is invisible to all fixed-time fluid limits.

The zero-immigration counterexample and the critical rare-seeding calculation below are exact for the stated finite state model. They do not establish a general theorem for arbitrary CRNs.

## Model and deterministic limit

Use integer counts (A,B) with A+B=V and reactions

\[
 A+B\xrightarrow{\beta/V}2B,\qquad B\xrightarrow{\delta}A.
\]

Writing i=B, the density-dependent count rates are

\[
\lambda_i^V=\beta i(1-i/V),\quad i=0,\ldots,V-1;\qquad
\mu_i^V=\delta i,\quad i=1,\ldots,V.
\]

For X^V=B/V, the fluid equation is

\[
\dot x=f(x)=\beta x(1-x)-\delta x
=x[(\beta-\delta)-\beta x].
\]

For R_0=\beta/\delta>1, x*=1-1/R_0 is globally attracting from each x(0)>0, with f'(x*)=-(\beta-\delta)<0. The extinction state 0 is an invariant, unstable boundary equilibrium of the ODE.

For each finite V, state 0 is absorbing and is accessible from every i>0 by a finite string of deaths. Since the chain has finite state space, absorption occurs almost surely from every state. Thus the unique invariant probability is \(\delta_0\), while the fluid ODE from a positive start converges to x*>0.

For an even-V sequence started at i_V=Vx*, and any 0<e<x*, fixed-horizon density-dependent LLN convergence gives

\[
\lim_{V\to\infty}\Pr(X^V(t)>e)=1\quad\text{for each fixed }t,
\]
while for every finite V, \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\Pr(X^V(t)>e)=0\). Hence

\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{V\to\infty}\Pr(X^V(t)>e)=1,
\qquad
\lim_{V\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\Pr(X^V(t)>e)=0.
\]

The two limits do not commute. The fluid LLN controls compact time intervals. It cannot be exchanged with the long-time limit or used by itself to infer a stationary law, survival, or permanence.

This is the elementary version of Keizer's paradox in an autocatalytic biochemical system. The precise statement is model-specific, but the mechanism is not novel: Vellela and Qian explicitly compare finite-time deterministic agreement, eventual stochastic extinction, the true stochastic steady state, and the quasi-stationary state for an autocatalytic reaction. See [their primary article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11538-006-9188-3). Kurtz's density-dependent limit theorem is the finite-time baseline, not an infinite-time theorem ([Kurtz 1971](https://doi.org/10.2307/3211904); [Kurtz 1978](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4149(78)90020-0)).

## Rare extinction scale, derived from the exact recursion

Let \(\tau_0=\inf\{t:B(t)=0\}\). The first-step equations for \(q_i=\mathbb E_i\tau_0\), with \(q_0=0\), imply for \(D_i=q_i-q_{i-1}\)

\[
D_i=\frac1{\mu_i}+\frac{\lambda_i}{\mu_i}D_{i+1},\qquad D_V=1/\mu_V.
\]

Iterating gives the exact formula

\[
\mathbb E_i\tau_0=
\sum_{k=1}^i\sum_{j=k}^V\frac1{\mu_j}
\prod_{\ell=k}^{j-1}\frac{\lambda_\ell}{\mu_\ell}.
\]

Here \(\lambda_\ell/\mu_\ell=R_0(1-\ell/V)\). The largest products in the double sum run from O(1) up to j near x*V. Riemann sums (or one-dimensional Laplace asymptotics) give, for i/V\to x* from the endemic region,

\[
\lim_{V\to\infty}\frac1V\log\mathbb E_i\tau_0
=\Delta,
\quad
\Delta=\int_0^{x*}\log\frac{\beta(1-x)}\delta\,dx
=\log R_0-1+R_0^{-1}>0.
\]

For R_0=2, x*=1/2 and \(\Delta=\log2-1/2=0.19314718056\). The script `sis_boundary.py` evaluates the exact recurrence in log space. For V=40, 80, 160, 320, 640, and 1280, its \(V^{-1}\log\mathbb E_{V/2}\tau_0\) values are respectively 0.19054, 0.18662, 0.18753, 0.18921, 0.19063, and 0.19161, approaching the predicted 0.19315. The slow convergence reflects a polynomial prefactor; no Gillespie run is being passed off as evidence of this asymptotic.

The killed chain on {1,...,V} is finite and irreducible, so it has a unique quasi-stationary distribution (QSD), given by the normalized positive left Perron eigenvector of its transient generator. If it starts in that QSD, survival is exactly exponential in time. For this classic supercritical SIS process, established work analyzes the QSD and extinction time; it finds a QSD centered near the endemic equilibrium and an exponential-in-V extinction time. The exact recurrence above independently establishes the leading exponent for the mean hitting time from a fixed endemic start, not the QSD eigenvalue asymptotics. Primary references include [Kryscio–Lefèvre (1989)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3214374) and [Nåsell (1996)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1428186). A chemical-network path LDP and Wentzell–Freidlin theory are available under stated topological conditions, not as a blanket implication for every CRN ([Agazzi–Dembo–Eckmann](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126)).

## Same fluid limit, different stationary limits under vanishing nucleation

Add a source reaction

\[
A\xrightarrow{\eta_V}B,\qquad \eta_V=e^{-\alpha V},\quad \alpha>0.
\]

Now the count birth rate is

\[
\lambda_i^V=(V-i)\left(\beta i/V+\eta_V\right),\qquad \mu_i^V=\delta i.
\]

The concentration drift is \(f_V(x)=f(x)+\eta_V(1-x)\), so \(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f_V(x)-f(x)|\le e^{-\alpha V}\). The same ODE is obtained on every fixed time interval, by the ordinary density-dependent LLN/Gronwall argument. For every finite V with \(\eta_V>0\), however, the chain is irreducible and has a true stationary law \(\pi_V\).

Since it is a birth-death chain,

\[
\frac{\pi_V(i)}{\pi_V(0)}=w_i,
\quad w_0=1,\quad
w_i=\prod_{k=1}^i\frac{\lambda_{k-1}^V}{\delta k}.
\]

An exact rearrangement is

\[
w_i=\frac{V\eta_V}{\delta}\,\frac{R_0^{i-1}}{i}
\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}(1-j/V)
\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(1+\frac{\eta_V V}{\beta j}\right).
\]

The final product is uniformly 1+o(1), because its logarithm is at most \((\eta_V V/\beta)H_{V-1}\to0\). For i/V\to x in (0,1),

\[
\frac1V\log w_i\to -\alpha+G(x),\qquad
G(x)=\int_0^x\log(R_0(1-u))\,du.
\]

G has a unique maximum at x*=1-1/R_0, of height \(G(x*)=\Delta\). The finite-state partition sum therefore yields

\[
\pi_V\Rightarrow\begin{cases}
\delta_{x*},&0<\alpha\le\Delta,\\
\delta_0,&\alpha>\Delta.
\end{cases}
\]

At the critical scale \(\alpha=\Delta\), a saddle-point calculation gives \(\pi_V(0)\sim \delta x*R_0/\sqrt{2\pi V}\), so the equality case also converges to the positive equilibrium. This threshold is not visible in \(f_V\to f\): it arises from comparing the total nucleation scale to the action for rare extinction.

The script computes the exact stationary weights in log space. At \(\beta=2,\delta=1\), \(\Delta=0.193147...\). For \(\alpha=0.10\), the V=640 stationary mean is 0.4984 and \(\pi_V(0)=2.0\times10^{-28}\). At critical \(\alpha=\Delta\), V=640 gives mean 0.4907 and \(\pi_V(0)=0.0155\), consistent with the \(V^{-1/2}\) critical asymptotic. At \(\alpha=0.27>\Delta\), V=160 gives mean 7.2\times10^{-5} and \(\pi_V(0)=0.99985\). These are exact finite-state product-form computations up to floating-point summation in log space, not sampled trajectories. This extension is an internal derivation; the focused prior-art check found established extinction/QSD/Keizer-paradox literature, but did not establish that this rare-nucleation threshold is new.

## Where compact-interior convergence fails

1. **The LLN is local in time.** Its martingale remainder is O_P(V^{-1/2}) over fixed T; stability of the ODE controls this on a fixed horizon. A rare fluctuation with probability exp(-V\Delta) per metastable episode is invisible at fixed T but accumulates over times of order exp(V\Delta).
2. **The boundary is a different kinetic regime.** At B=0, the infection/autocatalysis propensity is exactly zero. For lattice counts O(1), falling-factorial propensities can differ relatively by order one from monomial ODE rates even when their absolute concentration drift error is O(1/V). The event that matters is created by a long sequence of count-level fluctuations.
3. **QSD and stationary measure answer different questions.** The QSD is the long-time law conditioned on survival; the true invariant law of the zero-source finite chain is all mass at extinction. Quasi-stationary concentration at x* does not establish survival forever.
4. **A supplied finite-horizon exit certificate is not permanence.** P/N35 certifies a conservative exponential time window from a supplied strict-inward polytope. The same Astra package explicitly gives an inward compact buffer that is exited almost surely at every finite V, and an endemic SIS-like network with an absorbing extinction state. The certificate is a safety-window result, not a QSD law or asymptotically sharp quasipotential.
5. **Stoichiometric classes do not encode stochastic communication classes.** U/N77 supplies a separate infinite-state obstruction: one deterministic class B+C=2 has an established common complex-balanced attractor, yet it contains infinitely many stochastic closed positive-recurrent components with stationary laws escaping every common compact set. Its pathwise invariant A-B(B+1)/2=n is not a linear conservation law. This refutes classwise uniform tightness, not componentwise recurrence; the U evidence is a summary only.

## Decisive empirical/computational falsifier

A physically interpretable realization is an autocatalytic template system: A is a substrate, B catalyzes conversion A+B\to2B, and B spontaneously reverts B\to A. Work in isolated droplets with measured total A+B=V. The no-seed model predicts B/V\to x* over fixed times but eventual extinction for each finite V; at R_0=2 the metastable extinction action is 0.193147 per molecule. Add a calibrated spontaneous B-nucleation channel A\to B at rate \eta_V per A and vary its measured exponential scale across \alpha=\Delta.

Measure V, single-event transition counts, A/B copy-number trajectories, extinction events, and the no-B nucleation rate. The preregistered falsifier is a stationary distribution that stays near zero for \alpha<\Delta or near x* for \alpha>\Delta as V grows, after independently confirming the stated rates and no omitted pathways. The strongest informed control is the same droplet chemistry with the nucleation channel measured and varied, paired with (i) the ODE initialized at the same concentration, and (ii) the exact finite-state birth-death stationary weights/QSD rather than an equally under-resolved Monte Carlo estimate. A discrete-time Gillespie experiment at \alpha=0 is not able to demonstrate probability-one extinction; a finite survival record only bounds the hazard. At the critical source levels, exponentially small source-rate measurement is a serious acquisition limit, so moderate-V exact master-equation calculations are the clean first discriminator. No physical experiment or Gillespie simulation was performed here.
