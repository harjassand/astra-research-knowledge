## What the adversarial reconstruction established

`RECONSTRUCTION.md` records the mathematical checks, rather than relying on the finite diagnostics:

* **Essential-source signs and ties.** A positive arrow from a source tied for the maximum essential score is forbidden even if another tied arrow is negative. When an entire higher tier is orthogonal, it really is omitted. This validates the coordinate bounds and the successive (G,H,Z) zero-projection cases.
* **Only possible positive-(A) cubic vector.** Total-direction geometry bounds cubic products to degree three; the coordinate and (w=(2,1)) signs then leave only ((1,-2)). The strict \(\kappa>0\) uses the already-established (v_B<0), not an unjustified strict sign for every summand.
* **The kink is accounted for exactly.** The cubic generator is (a b(b-1)[2b g(a)+R(a)]), with (g(a)\le-\kappa). An explicit finite envelope for (R(a)) gives the claimed global bound. It includes all cutoff crossings and the (b=0,1) factorial corrections.
* **Arbitrarily large quadratic products are paid for.** At source (2B), the dangerous coefficient of (b^3) vanishes for (a\le K-D); beyond that strip the chosen (K) guarantees (T_B\le\kappa a). This establishes the central interface that an ordinary untruncated cross term fails on the (B) axis.
* **The remaining degrees are correct.** The (2A) and (A+B) leading terms have only quadratic remainders outside the strip, and bounded-(a) strip corrections also have at most quadratic growth in (b). Lower-order reaction increments and rates yield at most quadratic growth globally.
* **Degenerate aggregates are covered.** The no-pure classification and all (G,H,Z) tiers are exhaustive. The mixed dependency's \(\alpha=0\), \(\beta=0\), and both-positive cases are valid, including both zero. Its all-cubic-conservative case is handled before strict coercivity is invoked.
* **Both-pure coercivity is uniform over states.** The pure-axis losses are strictly positive. The supplied derivative bound makes the two axis collars negative; any cubic total loss gives a positive loss bound over the closed middle interval. Choosing the stated positive perturbation gives a negative quartic term at every angle.
* **Disabled cubic reactions are included.** An explicit exact remainder constant (C_3=\sum_{r\text{ cubic}}k_r(20D_r+2D_r^2)), with (D_r=|d_{r,A}|+|d_{r,B}|), bounds the difference between the actual and leading cubic generator by (C_3N^3). Thus the negative quartic dominates all quadratic-source and discrete corrections, including coordinates zero, one, or two.
* **Foster applies to the minimal process.** Finite sublevels have bounded rate, and their possible first-exit states are finite because the reaction set and jump vectors are finite. Stopped Dynkin–Gronwall proves the required exit probability bound without assuming moments of the un-stopped chain. No irreducibility or boundary activation premise is needed.
