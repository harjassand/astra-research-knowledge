## J1. General trace-epigraph estimate

Let Q:R^m->Sym_r be homogeneous quadratic and PSD at every real input. Let E_Q be the source's exact trace epigraph, including its range condition at singular A. Suppose x_epsilon=(A,t,y), for 0<epsilon<epsilon0, satisfies

    A >= a epsilon^alpha I,     tr(A^-1 Q(y)) <= M,
    t - tr(A^-1 Q(y)) <= -kappa epsilon^nu,

where a,M,kappa,alpha,nu are positive constants. All norms are fixed Euclidean/operator norms, and r,m,Q are fixed. Then there is c>0 such that

    dist(x_epsilon,E_Q) >= c epsilon^(nu+alpha).

The trace bound is part of the interface. In the source it follows from bounded t and the precise residual asymptotic -kappa epsilon^nu+o(epsilon^nu).

## J2. Proof by relative inverse perturbation

Write a perturbation as (Delta A,Delta t,Delta y), and let eta bound all three norms. If eta<=a epsilon^alpha/2, put B=A^-1/2 Delta A A^-1/2. Then ||B||<=eta/(a epsilon^alpha)<=1/2 and A'=A+Delta A is positive definite. With S=A^-1/2 Q(y) A^-1/2 PSD and tr S<=M,

    |tr(A'^-1 Q(y))-tr(A^-1 Q(y))|
      = |tr(((I+B)^-1-I)S)|
      <= 2 M eta/(a epsilon^alpha).

This avoids the unnecessary product of two inverse norms. Equivalently, Q(y)<=M A because a PSD matrix is bounded by its trace times the identity.

For each positive definite A', q_A'(z)=tr(A'^-1 Q(z)) is a nonnegative scalar quadratic form. Its polarization obeys Cauchy–Schwarz. There is C_Q with ||Q(z)||<=C_Q||z||². Since A'>=A/2,

    q_A'(y)<=2M,
    q_A'(Delta y)<=2r C_Q eta²/(a epsilon^alpha).

Consequently

    |q_A'(y+Delta y)-q_A'(y)|
      <= C1 epsilon^(-alpha/2) eta + C2 epsilon^(-alpha) eta².

Adding |Delta t| shows that, for eta<=1, the residual changes by at most C3 epsilon^(-alpha) eta, after restricting epsilon<=1. Choose eta<=c epsilon^(nu+alpha) with c sufficiently small relative to kappa,C3. The perturbed residual is still negative. For sufficiently small epsilon the relative-positivity hypothesis also holds because nu>0. Thus no feasible epigraph point lies in this ball. Its A coordinate would be positive definite, so the singular range clause creates no omitted case. This proves J1.

## J3. Why any finite affine SDP lift implies arbitrarily high jet proximity

The source's Lemma5.5 is reconstructed as follows. For a polynomial patch p(s) in a set with an exact finite affine SDP lift, semialgebraic stratification of the feasible Gram-factor relation yields analytic lift variables u(s) and Gram factors F(s) on a nonempty open subpatch. A positive functional lambda on squares through degree b applied to their degree-b Taylor polynomials gives

    P_epsilon = L(lambda p(c+epsilon z), lambda u_[b](epsilon,z)),
    H_epsilon = lambda(F_[b] F_[b]^T) >= 0,
    P_epsilon-H_epsilon = O(epsilon^(b+1)).

Here b>=deg p and lambda(1)=1 retain the polynomial patch and the affine constant exactly. Let K0 be the common kernel of all feasible pencil values. Every analytic selected value annihilates K0, hence its Taylor coefficients and P_epsilon do too. A feasible maximum-rank pencil value P* is positive definite on K0-perp: averaging with any feasible value and maximality of rank proves ker P*=K0. Mixing P_epsilon with weight epsilon^b of P* repairs the O(epsilon^(b+1)) negative part on K0-perp, while leaving K0 zero. Affineness yields a genuinely feasible projected point at distance O(epsilon^b). No Slater condition or closure of a projection is assumed.

The positive-extension lemma is also elementary: to add the next homogeneous degree layer to a positive definite finite moment matrix, assign the unassigned top even moments as a sufficiently large multiple of Gaussian homogeneous moments. Their block is positive definite and dominates the fixed mixed-block Schur complement. Iterate. This supplies finite positive extensions, not representing measures or an affordable implementation.

## J4. Consequence for the source's quartic patch

The source gives alpha=nu=4 and a degree-four polynomial patch. Its bounded trace and strict residual meet J1, so

    dist(x_epsilon,E_Q) >= c epsilon^8.

Use Lemma5.5 with b=9. The positive functional through degree four can be extended through degree18; the tested quartic coordinates are unchanged. The putative lift then supplies feasible points at O(epsilon^9), contradicting this lower bound. Thus the final local contradiction needs degree18 moments rather than the source's degree32 extension with b=16. The source's b=16 is valid; the lower budget is our deduction. b=8 does not yield a contradiction merely from the stated orders, because constants at that order matter.

In seven variables the polynomial test space has binomial(16,7)=11440 monomials at b=9, versus binomial(23,7)=245157 at b=16. This is a formal certificate-size comparison, not measured solver cost. Dense exact moment storage, the unknown margins, generic seed/subspace construction, and real coefficient heights remain charged and unacquired.
