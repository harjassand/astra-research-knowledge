## 1. Result and resource being optimized

Let a finite-dimensional system have a fixed binary parity projection (P), with initial state supported in (I-P). A bath and arbitrary reference may be included in the system. On every genuine classical history, the Hamiltonian is

\[
H(t)=H_{\rm even}(t)+V(t),\qquad [H_{\rm even}(t),P]=0,
\]

where (V) is parity odd and \(\|V(t)\|\le j(t)\). Between these evolutions the controller may apply complete instruments whose Kraus operators preserve the two parity sectors. Instruments, controls, and stopping decisions may depend causally on every past classical record. No branch is normalized away from the experiment.

The resource is **expected charged odd action**

\[
\mathcal A=\mathbb E\int_0^\tau j(t)\,dt.
\]

The norm bound is on the complete modeled system, uniformly over its bath and reference, not a reduced-state or prepared-bath calibration. Choosing (j=\|V\|) is permissible when this norm is known. A supplied larger envelope remains charged as that larger envelope. The stopping time \(\tau\) is causal.

A recorded success must certify parity (P) exactly: on every success branch its actual conditional output state is supported in (P). A final exact parity measurement is a permissible way to produce such a record. Halting unsuccessfully is also allowed. Detector calls, even-parity controls, feedback, and elapsed time are separate resources; they are not counted in \(\mathcal A\).

**Theorem 1.** Every finite causal protocol under this contract obeys

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{success})\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{\mathcal A}{k_*}\right\},
\qquad k_*=\frac{\pi+2}{4}=1.285398163397\ldots . \tag{1}
\]

The same bound holds for countable stopping protocols obtained as limits of finite prefixes, when the success event is the increasing union of successes at finite prefixes and the charged action is the increasing accumulated action. In particular a protocol succeeding almost surely has expected charged odd action at least (k_*).

The constant is sharp in infimum: for every positive \(\delta\) there is a finite protocol with success probability exactly one and expected action below \(k_*+\delta\). An almost surely terminating countable protocol can also approach the same action. The infimum itself is not asserted to be attained by a finite protocol. The matching weak-loop family is established prior art; the finite fallback below was added during internal reconstruction.

For any budget \(C\ge0\), the supremum success probability over finite protocols is exactly

\[
\sup_{\mathcal A\le C}\Pr(\mathrm{success})
=\min\{1,C/k_*\}. \tag{2}
\]

The supremum in (2) uses an initial classical activation coin and limits of finite weak-probe protocols. It need not be a maximum.

## 2. A universal population-speed inequality

For a normalized conditional state put (p=\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho)\). Purify the state, including all reference systems, and write its vector as (u+v), with (u\in\operatorname{ran}(I-P)), (v\in\operatorname{ran}P). Then \(\|u\|^2=1-p\), \(\|v\|^2=p\). The even Hamiltonian does not change (p). The odd block of (V) has norm at most (j), so

\[
\left|\frac{dp}{dt}\right|
\le 2j\|u\|\|v\|=2j\sqrt{p(1-p)}. \tag{3}
\]

This proof also covers mixed states through the purification. No assumption that the parity is a physical qubit, that sector dimensions agree, or that the bath factors is used.

Writing \(\theta=\arcsin\sqrt p\), (3) gives \(|d\theta/dt|\le j\). The integrated inequality holds at the endpoints (p=0,1) by continuity/differential comparison, so it does not rely on dividing by zero there.

## 3. The concave potential

Define

\[
\Phi(p)=\frac1{k_*}
\begin{cases}
\arcsin\sqrt p,&0\le p\le\tfrac12,\\
\tfrac\pi4+p-\tfrac12,&\tfrac12\le p\le1.
\end{cases} \tag{4}
\]

It has the following exact properties:

1. \(\Phi(0)=0\), \(\Phi(1)=1\), and \(\Phi\ge0\).
2. \(\Phi\) is concave: its derivative on \((0,1/2)\) is \([2k_*\sqrt{p(1-p)}]^{-1}\), which decreases to \(1/k_*\); the derivative on \((1/2,1)\) is the constant \(1/k_*\).
3. Under Hamiltonian evolution,

   \[
   \frac{d}{dt}\Phi(p(t))\le j(t)/k_*. \tag{5}
   \]

For (5), as a function of \(\theta\), the first piece has derivative \(1/k_*\), while the second has derivative \(\sin(2\theta)/k_*\le1/k_*\). Combine with (3). This also gives an integrated proof that is regular at (p=0,1).

Consider a complete parity-preserving instrument \(\{K_y\}\), including all hidden Kraus indices in the analysis. Let \(w_y\) be the actual probability of outcome (y), and \(p_y\) the parity population of its normalized conditional state when (w_y>0). Since \([K_y,P]=0\) and \(\sum_yK_y^*K_y=I\),

\[
\sum_yw_yp_y=p. \tag{6}
\]

Therefore Jensen's inequality gives

\[
\sum_y w_y\Phi(p_y)\le\Phi(p). \tag{7}
\]

This is the reason actual outcome feedback is safe in this theorem: a complete instrument cannot increase the average potential. A normalized successful filter alone would not satisfy (7), and is not an allowed free operation.

Combine (5) and (7) along the full finite causal tree. The initial potential is zero. Every successful terminal branch has potential one, and unsuccessful branches have nonnegative potential. Thus

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{success})\le\mathbb E\Phi(p_\tau)
\le \mathcal A/k_*.
\]

This proves (1). For a countable stopping protocol, apply the finite-prefix theorem to success by prefix (m), halting all remaining branches as unsuccessful at that prefix. Monotone convergence applies both to the success probability and to the nonnegative action. No optional-stopping assertion about an unbounded martingale is required.

## 4. The established saturating weak-herald controller

This construction uses one qubit, no bath, and real rotations

\[
R(\theta)|0\rangle=\cos\theta|0\rangle+\sin\theta|1\rangle.
\]

They can be generated by a parity-odd Pauli Hamiltonian with charged action \(\theta\). The new required instrument is

\[
C_\epsilon=\sqrt\epsilon\,P_1,
\qquad N_\epsilon=P_0+\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\,P_1,
\qquad 0<\epsilon\le1. \tag{8}
\]

The two effects sum to identity. A click certifies odd parity exactly. The no-click branch retains coherence; it is not a complete pinch. This instrument is an additional stipulated operation, not something supplied by a reduced coherence multiplier or inferred from the original private-pinch model.

Protocol:

1. Rotate to \(\theta_0=\pi/4\).
2. Apply (8). On a click, stop with success.
3. On no click the normalized angle is

   \[
   \theta_-=\arctan\sqrt{1-\epsilon}.
   \]

   Rotate forward by

   \[
   d_\epsilon=\pi/4-\theta_-
   \]

   to return to \(\theta_0\), and repeat.

Every attempt has click probability \(p_\epsilon=\epsilon/2\), conditional on reaching it. Hence the countable protocol succeeds almost surely and has

\[
\mathbb E N_{\rm probe}=2/\epsilon,
\qquad
\mathbb E N_{\rm feedback}=(2-\epsilon)/\epsilon, \tag{9}
\]

and expected odd action

\[
A_\infty(\epsilon)=\frac\pi4+
\frac{2-\epsilon}{\epsilon}
\left(\frac\pi4-\arctan\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\right). \tag{10}
\]

As \(\epsilon\downarrow0\),

\[
A_\infty(\epsilon)\longrightarrow\frac\pi4+\frac12=k_*.
\]

For a transparent exact expansion set

\[
u=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}{1+\sqrt{1-\epsilon}}.
\]

Then \(d_\epsilon=\arctan u\) and \((2-\epsilon)/\epsilon=(1+u^2)/(2u)\). Consequently

\[
A_\infty(\epsilon)=k_*+\epsilon^2/48+O(\epsilon^3). \tag{11}
\]

Thus the action infimum has a diverging expected number of probes and feedback decisions. It is not a zero-cost limiting controller.

### Finite version with an actual final complete measurement

Make at most \(n\) weak probes, restoring \(\theta_0\) after every no-click, including the last. If all \(n\) probes fail, perform an exact final parity measurement at \(\theta_0\). Stop on any earlier click; an exact final verification may also be applied on that already-odd branch without odd action. Count odd outcomes of this final instrument as successes.

Write \(r=1-\epsilon/2\). Directly summing the complete branches gives

\[
P_n=1-\tfrac12r^n,
\quad
A_n=\frac\pi4+d_\epsilon\frac{r(1-r^n)}{1-r},
\quad
\mathbb E N_{\rm weak}=\frac{1-r^n}{1-r}. \tag{12}
\]

The expected number of feedback rotations is \(r(1-r^n)/(1-r)\). An additional actual final complete measurement on every run costs one such boundary instrument if the application requires it.

For **exact success probability one in a finite circuit**, after that final complete measurement apply a \(\pi/2\) odd rotation only on its remaining even branch, then measure again. The branch is genuinely known even, so this fallback makes it odd with certainty. Its probability is \(\tfrac12r^n\), hence the extra expected action is \((\pi/4)r^n\). Thus

\[
P_n^{\rm exact}=1,
\qquad A_n^{\rm exact}=A_n+(\pi/4)r^n. \tag{12a}
\]

Its worst-case charged odd action is at most \(\pi/4+nd_\epsilon+\pi/2\). With a final complete verification on every branch it uses at most \(n+2\) measurement calls; its expected total is \((1-r^n)/(1-r)+1+\tfrac12r^n\). These finite time/count caps are distinct from the smaller expected values. First taking \(n\) large and then \(\epsilon\) small proves the finite exact-success infimum in Theorem 1. This fallback observation was supplied by `gf_causal_adversary` during internal reconstruction.

For \(\epsilon=1/2\), \(n=12\), the success probability is exactly

\[
P_{12}=1-\frac12(3/4)^{12}.
\]

The saved check computes the finite Kraus branches and verifies (12) directly. Its action-to-success ratio is below the best complete-cut restart ratio. This is a finite witness; it is not dependent on an infinite number of operations.

## 5. Complete-cut restart is a different, weaker interface

If every attempt starts in even parity, has a precommitted charged envelope \(a\), and ends with a complete parity cut, then its success chance is at most \(s(a)=\sin^2(\min\{a,\pi/2\})\). The cost is charged at the beginning of the attempt, including unsuccessful ones. For any causal succession of such attempts,

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{success})\le \mathcal A/k_{\rm cut},
\quad
k_{\rm cut}=\min_{a>0}\frac{a}{s(a)}.
\]

The minimizer \(\alpha\in(\pi/4,\pi/2)\) solves \(\tan\alpha=2\alpha\), giving

\[
\alpha=1.165561185\ldots,
\quad \sin^2\alpha=0.844579\ldots,
\quad k_{\rm cut}=1.380050\ldots . \tag{13}
\]

Proof: each attempt's conditional success probability is at most \(s(a)\le a/k_{\rm cut}\). Sum over the mutually disjoint first-success attempts and over their true reach probabilities. Repeated angle-\(\alpha\) attempts attain the corresponding mean cost in the countable model, and truncated/activated protocols prove sharpness in supremum.

The scalar optimization in (13) is historically established in quantum search restart: Boyer, Brassard, Høyer and Tapp derive the corresponding equation and success probability in *Tight bounds on quantum searching*, arXiv:quant-ph/9605034, printed p. 2. No invention claim is made for it. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9605034).

It would be false to promote (13) to a universal theorem for arbitrary parity-preserving instruments and actual adaptive action costs. The finite protocol in (12) is a legal counterexample to that promotion. Its no-click coherence is the extra resource absent from complete-cut restart.

## 6. Mean resources do not tensor: exact joint frontier

For \(d\) parities assume every operation other than \(V_i\) preserves parity \(i\), and \(\|V_i\|\le j_i\). Let

\[
C_i=\mathbb E\int_0^\tau j_i(t)\,dt
\]

be unconditional mean costs in a single complete causal experiment. Apply Theorem 1 separately to each parity; other coordinates and the full controller are part of its bath/reference. Then

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{all\ selected\ succeed})
\le \min_i\min\{1,C_i/k_*\}. \tag{14}
\]

For this enlarged weak-instrument interface, (14) is the exact supremum over finite protocols with these cost caps. To approach it, a common classical activation coin turns on all parents together. Conditional on activation, run independent finite versions of (12), choosing their error probabilities to approach zero and their action-to-success ratios to approach \(k_*\). Let the activation probability approach \(\min_i\min\{1,C_i/k_*\}\) from below. Every cost constraint is then met for sufficiently accurate finite protocols, and the all-success probability approaches the right side of (14). The controller needs only a common classical bit; quantum bath correlations are unnecessary.

The analogous exact supremum for complete-cut attempts is \(\min_i\min\{1,C_i/k_{\rm cut}\}\), under the attempt-billing contract of Section 5.

An elementary exact failure of a product of mean-derived factors already uses no weak probe. Let a common bit activate with probability \(1/4\). On activation rotate each of two initially-even qubits through \(\pi/2\), measure and stop; otherwise do nothing and report no success. Then

\[
\Pr(B_1)=\Pr(B_2)=\Pr(B_1B_2)=1/4,
\qquad C_1=C_2=\pi/8.
\]

Thus a product of the actual marginal probabilities would be \(1/16\), while the joint probability is \(1/4\). Products of the mean-cost singleton certificates also fail. Conditioning on the activation bit and averaging a **product of conditional factors** is legitimate; multiplying their separate averages is not.

This is not a counterexample to the supplied fixed-schedule theorem or to independent causal-test multiplicativity. The mean cost constraint belongs to the actual shared strategy; it is not a uniformly enforced property of each fixed local tester against every prover.

## 7. Full costs and receiving limits

At unit odd-drive norm the expected drive time of (10) is \(A_\infty\). At norm limit \(J\), it is \(A_\infty/J\), provided the stipulated rotations are attainable. If each weak probe takes time \(\tau_p\) and each feedback decision adds latency \(\tau_f\), with the odd drive absent during those slots, the expected serial time is at least

\[
A_\infty/J+(2/\epsilon)\tau_p+((2-\epsilon)/\epsilon)\tau_f,
\]

plus entry/exit, reset, communication, and reservation costs. A fixed positive probe cost makes the limiting weak protocol diverge in this full resource. For a supplied per-probe action-equivalent cost \(c\), the family (10) costs \(A_\infty+2c/\epsilon\), before any final verification cost. The saved check compares this family with the complete-cut benchmark \(\min_a(a+c)/s(a)\); that comparison is a design comparison, not a proof of a global optimum with detector cost.

If unwanted odd interactions continue during the probe or feedback, merely adding their norm action does not establish the exact channel (8). The full-joint instrument must first be qualified, or a separately justified perturbation/comb bound must be used. A marginal dephasing experiment cannot establish (8).

The memory-support packet requires exact entering and final full-syndrome instruments, full reservations, propagated error-footprint centralizer conditions, and a correct good-CP decomposition. A weak click supplies only the stated binary parity guarantee. It does not supply a full syndrome or a qualified memory controller. Its no-click operator is in the binary stabilizer algebra when \(P\) is an actual accessible syndrome projection, but that algebraic observation alone does not meet the scheduled-memory lifecycle contract.

The original fixed-schedule Q theorem remains a separate ideal-model result. This theorem gives a sharp resource boundary for a different, explicit feedback interface. No physical threshold, improved lifetime, scalable device, external verification, or settled historical novelty follows.

## 8. Exact known-pure-state guaranteed-transition value

For initial parity population \(p_0\), the same proof gives

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{success})\le\Phi(p_0)+\mathcal A/k_*.
\]

This is a lower-bound statement for every state and bath. It is not an asserted sharp finite-budget success curve for every mixed or unknown state.

For a **known pure qubit**

\[
|\psi_\theta\rangle=\cos\theta|0\rangle+\sin\theta|1\rangle,
\qquad 0\le\theta\le\pi/2,
\]

in the broad controllable model of Section 1, the infimum expected odd action required for success probability one is exactly

\[
V(\theta)=k_*\big(1-\Phi(\sin^2\theta)\big)
=\begin{cases}
k_*-\theta,&0\le\theta\le\pi/4,\\
\cos^2\theta,&\pi/4\le\theta\le\pi/2.
\end{cases} \tag{15}
\]

The lower bound follows from the preceding inequality. For the matching upper infimum when \(\theta\le\pi/4\), rotate to \(\pi/4\) at cost \(\pi/4-\theta\), then use the established balanced weak loop. Its remaining expected-action infimum, after that initial preparation, is \(1/2\).

When \(p=\sin^2\theta\ge1/2\), first use the complete instrument (8) with \(\epsilon=(2p-1)/p\). Its click probability is \(2p-1\). On no click, whose probability is \(2(1-p)\), the normalized state is exactly balanced. Applying the balanced weak loop only there has expected-action infimum \(2(1-p)\times(1/2)=1-p\). The successful branch is included with its actual probability. At \(p=1/2\) the first filter is unnecessary; at \(p=1\) success is already available at zero odd action.

The infimum allows a sequence of finite exact-success protocols with increasing probe caps, using the fallback in (12a). It is not a bounded worst-case resource claim at the infimum. Positive detector/feedback costs change it. Attainability for an arbitrary mixed/unknown bath state is not claimed.

## 9. Exact relation to the closest inspected predecessor

Fang–Heunen use amplitude amplification within its invariant good/bad two-dimensional subspace, the same diagonal weak instrument, and a restored checkpoint angle \(\alpha\). Their Eq. (11) is

\[
E(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha+
\frac{\cos^2\alpha(\alpha-\beta)}{\cos^2\beta-\cos^2\alpha}.
\]

Setting \(\alpha=\pi/4\), \(\beta=\arctan\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\) gives (10) identically. Their Eqs. (12)–(13) establish the same constant as the infimum within that one-checkpoint family. Thus the exact loop and scalar optimization in Section 4 are prior art.

The inspected predecessor's stated family-optimality result does not itself quantify over arbitrary time-dependent odd Hamiltonians, arbitrary finite bath/reference states, or all complete parity-preserving instruments and adaptive stopping. The potential proof in Sections 2–3 supplies that broader operation-model lower bound; (15) and (14) expose associated resource boundaries. This is a substantive quantifier extension relative to the **inspected statement**, not a settled historical-priority claim.

It is also not an unrestricted quantum-query lower bound. To convert charged norm action into oracle calls one must prove the appropriate invariant-subspace/action-per-call embedding for every candidate algorithm. The small Grover rotation angle is not automatically a uniform operator-norm action bound on an arbitrary algorithm's larger accessible Hilbert space.
