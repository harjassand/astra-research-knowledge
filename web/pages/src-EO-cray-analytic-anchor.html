1. The obstruction selected
The independent screen covered goal-directed inverse inference, response computation in chaotic systems, multireference imaging, and oscillatory field integrals. I selected the last because it presented two distinct obstructions that a single mechanism might address.
The first is cancellation. Consider
\[
Z(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-V(x)+\zeta W(x)}\,dx.
\]
At \(\zeta=i\), contributions have different complex phases. Sampling from the positive reference law proportional to \(e^{-V}\) gives the estimator
\[
\frac{Z(i)}{Z(0)}=\mathbb E[e^{iW(X)}].
\]
Writing this expectation as \(a\), the sample mean from \(N\) independent observations has relative mean-squared error
\[
\boxed{\frac{|a|^{-2}-1}{N}.}
\]
An exponentially small average phase therefore makes this particular estimator exponentially expensive.
The second obstruction is correctness after avoiding cancellation. Complex Langevin methods replace the oscillatory integral with a positive stochastic process on complex variables. But the process can converge and still reproduce the wrong integration cycle. Stationarity, small residuals, and several standard diagnostics do not settle that question. This remains an explicit issue in the primary literature. DOI
The operational question became:
Can the correct integration cycle, mixing rate, and estimator variance be certified from the supplied coefficients before trusting the simulation?

2. The proposed operation: anchor the proof, not the noisy data
The construction uses a complex parameter \(\zeta\) and the process
\[
dX_t=-\nabla S_\zeta(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dB_t,
\qquad
S_\zeta=V-\zeta W,
\]
where \(B_t\) is ordinary real Brownian motion, while \(X_t\) takes complex values.
The proposed correctness operation is analytic anchoring:
Establish a common domain in which the stochastic processes remain confined and contract uniformly. Prove that their stationary polynomial expectations are holomorphic functions of \(\zeta\). Identify those functions on a real interval, where the process samples an ordinary positive Gibbs distribution. Then extend the identity to the complex target.
This does not extrapolate a finite set of noisy real-parameter measurements. The algorithm runs at the required complex parameter values. Analytic continuation transports the correctness proof, not the measured data.
Complex Langevin itself is established. So are implicit solvers and earlier invariant-strip analyses. Neither is claimed as an invention here. arXiv
The candidate contribution is the combination of an acquired multivariate coefficient certificate, an argument selecting the real integration cycle, and quantitative computational bounds.
3. An explicit model where the required certificate is obtainable
Let \(G\) be a graph with \(n\) vertices and maximum degree \(D\). The supplied input consists of rational coefficients:
\[
A=A^\top,\qquad C=C^\top,\qquad a,c>0.
\]
The matrix \(A\) is strictly row-diagonally dominant with positive diagonal. Off-diagonal entries of \(C\) lie on graph edges. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
V(x)={}&\frac12x^\top Ax+\frac a4\sum_i x_i^4\\
&+\frac c4\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}
\left[(x_i+x_j)^4+(x_i-x_j)^4\right],
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
W(x)=\frac12x^\top Cx.
\]
The matrix \(C\) can have full rank. This is not another fixed-rank construction.
The primary output is
\[
L=\log\frac{Z(i)}{Z(0)},
\]
with the logarithm continuously continued from \(\zeta=0\). Additive error \(\epsilon\) in this complex logarithm gives relative error at most \(e^\epsilon-1\) in the represented ratio. The procedure also supports fixed-degree holomorphic observables and the less demanding intensive output \(L/n\).
The finite certificate
Choose an open parameter rectangle
\[
|\Re\zeta|<u_0,\qquad |\Im\zeta|<v_0,
\qquad v_0>1.
\]
Compute
\[
m=\min_i\left(A_{ii}-\sum_{j\ne i}|A_{ij}|\right),
\]
\[
c_1=\max_i\sum_j|C_{ij}|,
\qquad
c_2^2=\max_i\sum_j C_{ij}^2,
\]
and
\[
m_*=m-u_0c_1,\qquad
\theta_*=\min\left\{\frac{3a}{1+2D},\,3c\right\}.
\]
The construction accepts a radius \(r>0\) when
\[
\boxed{\lambda=m_*-(3a+24cD)r^2>0}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\eta=m_*r-(a+8cD)r^3
-\frac{v_0^2c_2^2}{4r\theta_*}>0.
}
\]
These are directly checkable coefficient inequalities. They contain no unknown stationary law, partition function, spectral gap, or experimental oracle.
The radius itself need not be supplied. Setting \(s=r^2\) reduces its acquisition to
\[
m_*s-(a+8cD)s^2-\frac{v_0^2c_2^2}{4\theta_*}>0,
\]
intersected with
\[
0<s<\frac{m_*}{3a+24cD}.
\]
This is a concave quadratic feasibility problem. The delivered code either acquires a rational radius or rejects this sufficient test.
What the inequalities prove
They establish an invariant complex tube
\[
\mathcal T=\{x+iy:|y_i|\le r\}
\]
and the contraction inequality
\[
\Re\left\langle z-z',
\nabla S_\zeta(z)-\nabla S_\zeta(z')\right\rangle
\ge\lambda\|z-z'\|^2.
\]
Consequently, two trajectories driven by identical noise satisfy
\[
\boxed{\|X_t-X'_t\|\le e^{-\lambda t}\|X_0-X'_0\|.}
\]
The nontrivial part is confinement under imaginary neighbour interactions. At the face \(y_i=r\), an incident paired quartic supplies the lower bound
\[
6cr\bigl(|x_i|-|x_j|\bigr)^2-8cr^3.
\]
Together with the onsite quartic, these terms control all neighbouring real directions. Completing the square absorbs the otherwise unbounded imaginary quadratic forcing.
This mechanism depends on the paired quartics being present in the target. Adding them to another model would change the scientific answer.
4. The main theorem and its proof mechanism
Let \(\mu_\zeta\) denote the invariant law of the complex process. The central result derived in the note is
\[
\boxed{
Z(\zeta)\,\mathbb E_{\mu_\zeta}O(X)
=
\int_{\mathbb R^n}O(x)e^{-S_\zeta(x)}\,dx
}
\]
for every holomorphic polynomial \(O\), throughout the certified parameter rectangle.
The proof is supplied in the packet, but has not received independent review.
First: construct an analytic stochastic operation
Use the implicit step
\[
Y_{k+1}+h\nabla S_\zeta(Y_{k+1})
=
Y_k+\sqrt{2h}\,\xi_k,
\qquad \xi_k\sim N(0,I_n).
\]
Strong accretivity proves existence and uniqueness of this root inside the tube. The root map contracts by
\[
q_h=(1+h\lambda)^{-1}.
\]
Its Jacobian is nonsingular, so the holomorphic implicit-function theorem makes each finite trajectory holomorphic in \(\zeta\).
Uniform polynomial moments allow expectations to pass through parameter contour integrals. Contraction then gives a holomorphic stationary expectation for each step size. A uniform-in-time discretisation estimate allows \(h\to0\).
This avoids assuming analyticity in stochastic time—an assumption with known pitfalls in complex Langevin arguments. DOI
Second: identify the real anchor
For real \(\zeta\) near zero, the process stays real and samples the ordinary strongly convex Gibbs law. Therefore
\[
Z(\zeta)\,\mathbb E_{\mu_\zeta}O(X)
=
\int O(x)e^{-S_\zeta(x)}dx
\]
holds on a real interval.
Both sides are holomorphic functions of \(\zeta\). The identity theorem extends their equality to the complex rectangle.
Third: prove nonvanishing without assuming it
Crucially, the preceding argument establishes a product identity. It does not first divide by the potentially tiny or vanishing \(Z\).
Taking \(O=W\) gives
\[
Z'(\zeta)=Z(\zeta)\,\mathbb E_{\mu_\zeta}W(X).
\]
The coefficient on the right is holomorphic. Since \(Z(0)>0\), this scalar differential equation implies that \(Z\) has no zeros in the rectangle.
Only then is division legitimate:
\[
\boxed{
L=\int_0^1 i\,\mathbb E_{\mu_{it}}W(X)\,dt.
}
\]
The answer is obtained by estimating polynomial moments, rather than resolving an exponentially small phase average.
General necessary-and-sufficient correctness conditions already exist in the literature. The proposed distinction is a finite coefficient test for this model family, not the first mathematical criterion for complex Langevin correctness. arXiv
5. Why stability alone does not suffice
A concrete counterexample separates contraction from correctness.
Consider the real target
\[
S(z)=\frac{z^4}{4}-\frac{z^2}{2}.
\]
Its desired real-cycle second moment is positive.
Now use the process
\[
dZ=(Z^3-Z)\,dt+i\sqrt2\,dB.
\]
Starting on \(Z=iY\) gives
\[
dY=-(Y^3+Y)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dB.
\]
This process is strongly contracting. Nevertheless, it produces
\[
\mathbb E[Z^2]=-\mathbb E[Y^2]<0.
\]
Independent quadrature gives
\[
\text{desired real-cycle moment}\approx1.04179729649,
\]
\[
\text{contractive imaginary-cycle moment}\approx-0.46791991697.
\]
Thus a stable stochastic computation can return an answer with the wrong sign. The analytic anchor identifies the intended integral through a stronger argument than convergence.
This example illustrates the known integration-cycle obstruction; it is not presented as the first discovery of wrong-cycle convergence.
6. End-to-end resource accounting
The algorithm receives explicit coefficients, acquires and checks the tube certificate, chooses parameter-quadrature nodes, simulates independently restarted trajectories, estimates \(iW\), and integrates the resulting means.
No equilibrium specimens or hidden-state measurements are supplied.
Bias, variance, and mixing
The proof gives computable constants with
\[
\mathbb E\|X\|^2\le n v_2,
\qquad
v_2=\lambda^{-1}+r^2.
\]
For either real or imaginary part of the endpoint estimator,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}[iW(Y)]
\le \frac{n c_1^2v_2}{\lambda}.
}
\]
The uniform discretisation analysis gives
\[
\boxed{
\left|\mathbb E W(X)-\mathbb E W(Y_h)\right|
\le n c_1\sqrt{v_2}\,C_{\mathrm{step}}h,
}
\]
where \(C_{\mathrm{step}}\) is explicitly computed from coefficient bounds and polynomial moments.
Mixing error decreases exponentially with burn-in. Analytic parameter quadrature needs \(O(\log(n/\epsilon))\) nodes for a fixed-width certified rectangle.
In the bounded-degree, bounded-coefficient, fixed-positive-margin regime, the resulting construction has a conservative arithmetic bound
\[
\widetilde O(n^3\epsilon^{-3})
\]
for additive error \(\epsilon\) in the full logarithm \(L\), including a polynomial-time acquired implicit-root procedure.
This is not a theorem polynomial only in the binary lengths of arbitrarily large couplings. Large coefficients and small certificate margins can make the numerical problem expensive.
For the intensive answer \(L/n\), the variance bound becomes \(O(1/n)\) and discretisation bias becomes \(O(h)\). That is a materially easier output contract—but does not estimate the full partition ratio to relative error \(\epsilon\).
Precision and root solving are charged
An implicit root residual \(\tau\) produces root error at most
\[
\frac{\tau}{1+h\lambda},
\]
and accumulated state error at most
\[
\frac{\tau}{h\lambda}.
\]
The note gives a fallback bounded-domain monotone solver with acquired Lipschitz bounds, Gaussian tail truncation, finite-precision randomness, and explicit error allocation. Sparse sequential storage is linear in the model size, apart from precision and small quadrature summaries.
The delivered benchmark uses floating-point damped Newton steps, not the fully certified fallback implementation.
Physical model acquisition remains separate. The theorem takes a specified polynomial action as input; it does not establish that an experimental material is described by that action. No laboratory experiment or physical-time acceleration was performed.

8. What failed when extending the mechanism
I pursued different operations rather than stopping when the simplest domain failed.
Onsite quartic confinement alone does not control generic imaginary neighbour interactions. At a proposed boundary \(y_i=r_i\), set \(x_i=0\) and send a neighbouring \(x_j\) to either infinity. The imaginary gradient contains a term proportional to
\[
-C_{ij}x_j.
\]
No finite rectangular imaginary tube can remain invariant.
The paired-quartic construction supplies the missing quadratic control of \(x_j\). That closes the certificate for the stated family—but only because those interactions are actual model terms.
Quadratic whitening transfers rather than removes the obstruction. In the scalar action
\[
S(z)=\frac12(m-ic)z^2+\frac g4z^4,
\]
the substitution
\[
z=(m-ic)^{-1/2}w
\]
makes the quadratic coefficient real but replaces the quartic coefficient by
\[
\frac{g}{(m-ic)^2}.
\]
Its imaginary cubic drift generally points outward in one real direction. A fixed strip can fail again.
A curved tube is not automatically a repair. With unchanged horizontal Brownian noise, a boundary with a nonzero real normal component can be crossed by the noise. A broader construction must change the noise geometry and account for the corresponding drift corrections—not merely propose a better-shaped region.
These failures identify the missing operation more precisely than “find a better sampler.”
9. The exact remaining scientific-computation problem
The mathematical object worth retaining is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{acquired confinement and contraction}
\;+\;
\text{a real positive anchor}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\text{correct complex observables}.
}
\]
The next decisive theorem would acquire a same-answer stochastic geometry for conventional onsite-quartic fields with generic imaginary neighbour couplings, without requiring the paired quartics and without supplying an expensive transport map as an oracle.
A precise candidate is a holomorphic noise kernel
\[
K_\zeta=B_\zeta B_\zeta^\top
\]
that is real positive definite on the anchor interval. Its construction must include the divergence correction to the drift, avoid inaccessible poles, preserve a domain compatible with the diffusion directions, and yield quantitative mixing, moment, and numerical-error bounds. Finite diagnostics or a learned kernel would not establish those properties.
Two additional obligations remain inseparable from that theorem: the error bounds must be useful at actual computational budgets, and a matched nonfactorising benchmark must show an advantage over specialised contour, tensor, interpolation, or stochastic methods.
