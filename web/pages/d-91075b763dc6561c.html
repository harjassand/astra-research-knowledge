CYCLE2 RESULT: RAW-TRAJECTORY RECOVERY OF AN ANCHORED SPARSE PREDICTIVE DICTIONARY
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: CONDITIONAL FINITE-SAMPLE THEOREM; internally derived; no external proof or novelty review

1. CLAIM AND INTERFACE

This result gives a positive teacher-free acquisition theorem for an unknown predictive feature dictionary from raw finite-state trajectories. The observed process is a stationary, finite-alphabet, first-order Markov chain. Its transition matrix has an unknown stochastic factorization P = Theta Q. The rows of Q are unknown probability-vector atoms, and the rows of Theta are unknown sparse mixtures. Exactly one observed state per atom is a pure anchor. An explicit convex-hull margin makes those anchors detectable from empirical raw transition rows.

The features are the unknown functions phi_j(x)=Theta[x,j] on the raw state alphabet. The one-step predictive function space is col(Theta), so recovering Theta recovers a sparse observable predictive subspace, not merely a latent parameterization. The theorem recovers the dictionary and sparse coordinates up to permutation, with finite-sample error bounds and a held-out validation option.

Observation contract: only the ordered raw states X_1,...,X_T are observed. The learner receives no hidden labels, feature values, transition-row queries, interventions, group tests, or teacher-supplied dictionary. It cannot select the next state/context. All probability estimates are formed from state-transition pairs seen in the stream. A known raw alphabet is part of the data format; the unknown objects are Q and Theta, not an arbitrary user-supplied feature list.

Scope boundary: this is a fully observable Markov chain, not a general HMM with hidden current state. Its result is a concrete positive subclass, not a theorem for arbitrary latent observable dictionaries. Full raw data are the strongest baseline; the theorem establishes learnability under identifiable structure, not an acquisition advantage over retained trajectories.

2. MODEL AND ASSUMPTIONS

Let the observable alphabet be S={1,...,d}, d>=r>=2. The chain is stationary with transition matrix P and stationary law pi. Write P_x=P[x,:] for a row transition distribution. The matrix itself is unknown.

(A1) Sparse stochastic factorization and rank. There are an unknown rank r, an unknown row-stochastic Q in [0,1]^{r x d}, and an unknown row-stochastic Theta in [0,1]^{d x r} such that

    P = Theta Q,       P_x = sum_{j=1}^r Theta[x,j] q_j.

Every row Theta[x,:] has at most s nonzero entries. The r x d matrix Q has full row rank with sigma_min(Q)>=sigma>0. Because each atom q_j is a probability vector, this is a normalized nonnegative factorization. Since anchors below place I_r among the rows of Theta, rank(Theta)=r, hence rank(P)=r.

(A2) Pure anchors and no near-pure impostors. There are exactly r distinct raw states a_1,...,a_r with Theta[a_j,:]=e_j. Every other state x satisfies max_j Theta[x,j]<=1-gamma, with gamma>0. Thus the dictionary is not supplied: each atom is the raw transition law at an unknown anchor state, and the anchor state IDs must be found from trajectory data. The condition also prevents a non-anchor row from being arbitrarily close to a pure atom in mixture weight.

(A3) Atom separation. In L1 distance, define

    Delta = min_j dist_1(q_j, conv{q_k : k != j}) > 0.

This is a quantitative extreme-point margin. A1-A3 imply the convex hull of all transition rows has exactly the q_j as its vertices, and the only rows equal to those vertices are the anchors. They therefore identify Q, the anchor states, and (by full row rank of Q) every Theta row, all up to a joint permutation.

(A4) Sparse-coordinate separation. Every nonzero coefficient Theta[x,j] is at least kappa>0. This assumption is used only for exact support recovery. Removing it still gives a coefficient/value approximation bound, but not a guarantee that tiny nonzero entries are distinguished from zero.

(A5) Coverage and dependence. pi_x>=pi_0>0 for every observable state. The stationary chain is absolutely regular with beta(k)<=exp(-k/tau), where beta is in the total-variation convention for which Berbee/Yu independent-block coupling has mismatch probability at most beta(k). The sample bound uses tau and pi_0. An arbitrary initial state is allowed only if a separate uniform burn-in bound sup_x ||P^B(x,:)-pi||_TV<=exp(-B/tau) is known; then B charged raw symbols are discarded. The main statement starts in stationarity and has no burn-in.

(A6) Known class bounds. The learner is given valid lower bounds gamma_0<=gamma, Delta_0<=Delta, sigma_0<=sigma, kappa_0<=kappa, pi_0, and an upper bound s_0>=s, plus a mixing upper envelope tau. It is not given r, Q, Theta, anchors, or their state IDs. Replace s by s_0 and the margins by their supplied lower bounds in algorithmic thresholds. These descriptor values and their bit lengths are charged below.

3. PREDICTIVE OBJECT AND IDENTIFIABILITY

Define the unknown feature functions phi_j:S-> [0,1] by phi_j(x)=Theta[x,j]. For any task payoff g:S->[ -G,G ], the one-step prediction from raw state x is

    E[g(X_{t+1}) | X_t=x] = P_x g = sum_j phi_j(x) <q_j,g>.

Thus all one-step predictive functions x |-> P_x g lie in col(Theta). Since Q has full row rank, the map g |-> Qg is onto R^r, so the span of these one-step predictive functions is exactly col(Theta), of dimension r. Each state has at most s active observable features. For every h>=1, P^h=Theta Q P^{h-1}; therefore all h-step prediction functions remain in the same feature subspace.

Identifiability is from the raw process law, rather than a naming convention for hidden atoms. The row polytope conv{P_x:x in S} equals conv{q_1,...,q_r}: every row is a mixture and every q_j occurs as P_{a_j}. Atom separation makes its vertices exactly q_j. The unique state rows at these vertices are the anchors. Once Q is ordered by the anchor IDs, P_x=Theta[x,:]Q has a unique coefficient vector because Q has full row rank. A different representation can only jointly permute atoms/features. This is why the condition is substantive: it supplies a recoverable geometric signature inside the raw transition law.

4. ESTIMATOR

From a stationary raw path, retain N adjacent transition pairs (X_{t_i},X_{t_i+1}) with pair starts t_i spaced b indices apart. This leaves b-2 unselected symbols between pairs and a dependence gap of b-1 indices from the end of one pair to the start of the next. For each x, form the empirical conditional row P_hat_x from all retained pairs whose first coordinate is x. If any state receives no pair, return UNKNOWN. The paired rows are created solely by scanning the trajectory; there is no query for a chosen x.

Anchor detection: for each x compute

    r_hat(x) = dist_1(P_hat_x, conv{P_hat_y : y != x}).

Return x as an anchor candidate iff r_hat(x)>gamma_0 Delta_0/2. If the candidate count is not in [2,d], or later certificates fail, return UNKNOWN. Let the detected IDs be a_hat_1,...,a_hat_rhat in increasing raw-state order and set q_hat_j=P_hat_{a_hat_j}.

Sparse coordinates: for each x choose a minimizer

    theta_hat_x in argmin { ||P_hat_x - theta Q_hat||_1 : theta in simplex(rhat), ||theta||_0<=s_0 }.

This can be computed by enumerating supports of size at most s_0 and solving an LP on each support. Use a fixed deterministic tie rule. Return the dictionary (Q_hat,Theta_hat), sparse supports after thresholding at kappa_0/2, and P_hat_fact=Theta_hat Q_hat. The row ordering is fixed by the anchor raw-state IDs. If requested precision, rank, row-stochasticity, or numerical certificates fail, return UNKNOWN.

The residual-distance and coefficient optimization problems have rational input when empirical counts are used. They can be solved by exact rational LPs; the support enumeration can be exponential in s_0. No polynomial-time claim for growing s_0 is made.

5. FINITE-SAMPLE THEOREM

THEOREM (unknown anchored sparse predictive subspace from raw trajectories).
Assume A1-A6. Let 0<epsilon<=1 satisfy

    epsilon <= min{ gamma_0 Delta_0/8,
                     kappa_0 sigma_0/16,
                     sigma_0/(32 sqrt(d)) }.

For any 0<delta<1, an empirical transition-row estimator with N sufficiently large as stated in Section 6 has, with probability at least 1-delta, exactly r detected anchors and the following bounds after a permutation of columns:

    max_j ||q_hat_j-q_j||_1 <= epsilon,                         (5.1)
    max_x ||theta_hat_x-Theta[x,:]||_2 <= 4 epsilon/sigma,      (5.2)
    max_x ||P_hat_fact[x,:]-P_x||_1 <= 3 epsilon.               (5.3)

The supports recovered by {j: theta_hat_xj>kappa_0/2} equal the true support of every row. The predictive subspace col(Theta_hat) has dimension r and

    ||sin Ang(col(Theta_hat), col(Theta))||_op
       <= (4 sqrt(d) epsilon/sigma)/(1-4 sqrt(d) epsilon/sigma) <= 1/7.

Also, the atom-row subspace obeys

    ||sin Ang(row(Q_hat),row(Q))||_op <= 2 sqrt(r) epsilon/sigma.

For any h>=1 and any payoff g with ||g||_infinity<=G, propagation through the reconstructed stochastic matrix gives

    max_x |(P_hat_fact^h g)(x) - (P^h g)(x)| <= 3 h epsilon G.

The rank r is not an input: it is the number of detected anchors. Exact recovery statements are conditional on the quantitative anchor/coordinate margins. The process rank is the ordinary real rank of P and equals the dimension of the one-step predictive function space under A1.

PROOF.

First consider the population residual. If x=a_j, every other anchor row has coefficient zero on q_j, and every non-anchor row has coefficient at most 1-gamma on q_j. Every z in conv{P_y:y!=a_j} can therefore be written z=alpha q_j+(1-alpha)q' with alpha<=1-gamma and q' in conv{q_k:k!=j}. Hence

    ||q_j-z||_1=(1-alpha)||q_j-q'||_1 >= gamma Delta.

So dist_1(P_{a_j},conv{P_y:y!=a_j})>=gamma Delta. If x is not an anchor, its row P_x is a convex combination of the anchor rows, all of which remain in the hull after x is excluded; its population residual is zero.

On the event max_x||P_hat_x-P_x||_1<=epsilon, moving the queried point changes a distance by at most epsilon and moving every point of a convex hull by at most epsilon changes its Hausdorff distance by at most epsilon. Thus each residual changes by at most 2epsilon. For anchors, r_hat(a_j)>=gamma Delta-2epsilon>=3gamma_0 Delta_0/4; for non-anchors r_hat(x)<=2epsilon<=gamma_0 Delta_0/4. Threshold gamma_0 Delta_0/2 detects exactly the r anchors, and q_hat_j is an empirical estimate of q_j.

For any x, the true sparse row Theta[x,:] is feasible in the coefficient minimization. Since each q_hat_j is epsilon-close in L1 and P_hat_x is epsilon-close to P_x, its objective value is at most 2epsilon. The minimizer therefore satisfies ||P_hat_x-theta_hat_x Q_hat||_1<=2epsilon. Also ||theta_hat_x(Q_hat-Q)||_1<=epsilon because theta_hat_x is a probability vector and every atom row has error at most epsilon. By the triangle inequality,

    ||(theta_hat_x-Theta[x,:])Q||_1 <= 4epsilon.

For any row vector v, ||vQ||_1>=||vQ||_2>=sigma_min(Q)||v||_2. This yields (5.2). If a true nonzero coordinate is at least kappa and a fitted coordinate is zero, (5.2) implies kappa<=4epsilon/sigma, contradicted by epsilon<=kappa_0 sigma_0/16. More generally every fitted coordinate differs by at most 4epsilon/sigma<=kappa_0/4, so threshold kappa_0/2 retains exactly the true support. The factorized row prediction error obeys

    ||theta_hat_x Q_hat - P_x||_1
       <= ||theta_hat_x Q_hat-P_hat_x||_1+||P_hat_x-P_x||_1
       <= 3epsilon,

which is (5.3).

The coefficient matrix error has Frobenius norm at most 4sqrt(d)epsilon/sigma. Since the anchor rows include I_r, sigma_min(Theta)>=1. Under epsilon<=sigma_0/(32sqrt(d)), the error is at most 1/8; the standard singular-subspace perturbation bound gives sine-angle at most e/(1-e)<=1/7. For Q, ||Q_hat-Q||_op<=||Q_hat-Q||_F<=sqrt(r)epsilon; sigma_min(Q)>=sigma_0 and epsilon<=sigma_0/(32sqrt(d)) imply the displayed conservative bound 2sqrt(r)epsilon/sigma. Finally, P and P_hat_fact are stochastic matrices. The row-L1 induced operator norm of a stochastic matrix is one, so telescoping P_hat_fact^h-P^h gives at most h||P_hat_fact-P||_{infinity->infinity}<=3h epsilon. Duality with ||g||_infinity<=G proves the transfer bound. QED.

6. RAW SAMPLE AND MIXING COST

For iid stationary transition pairs, each pair has law Pr(X=x,X'=y)=pi_x P_xy. Let m0(epsilon,delta')=ceil[2 epsilon^(-2)(d ln 2+ln(4d/delta'))]. Weissman et al.'s empirical-distribution L1 inequality gives row error at most epsilon from m0 conditional transitions with failure at most delta'/(4d). A binomial lower-tail bound gives at least N pi_0/2 visits to every state except with probability at most d exp(-N pi_0/8). It is sufficient to take

    N(epsilon,delta') = ceil max{
        2 m0(epsilon,delta')/pi_0,
        8 ln(4d/delta')/pi_0 }.

Then all d empirical transition rows are within epsilon in L1 with probability at least 1-delta'/2 under iid pairs. The order is

    N = O((d+ln(d/delta'))/(pi_0 epsilon^2)).

For the single dependent stationary trajectory, let b=1+ceil[tau ln(2N/delta_mix)] and retain pairs at starts t_i=1+(i-1)b. The time-index gap from the end of one pair to the start of the next is b-1 (there are b-2 unselected symbols between the pairs). Absolute-regularity coupling makes their joint law equal to N independent pairs except with probability at most (N-1) beta(b-1)<=delta_mix/2. Therefore the same iid row-estimation guarantee holds with an added delta_mix/2 failure probability. The required raw path length is

    T=(N-1)b+2 raw symbols,

and unused intermediate symbols are still charged. Taking delta_mix+delta'/2<=delta proves the theorem's probability statement. With a nonstationary start and the additional uniform mixing condition in A5, prepend B=ceil[tau ln(2/delta_mix)] burn-in symbols (or the corresponding stated bound) and charge them; the main theorem assumes stationary start.

There is no adaptive query-count benefit: the learner receives no control over which state appears. It collects O(N) adjacent raw pairs, at total stream cost O(N tau log(N/delta)) symbols. The effective transition sample count per state is governed by stationary coverage pi_0; dependence enters through the logarithmic spacing cost for this conservative independent-block proof. Sharper Markov-chain concentration could improve the log factor and constants, but is not needed for this theorem.

7. HELD-OUT VALIDATION AND LATER-TASK TRANSFER

The theorem is a class-conditional guarantee; a separate holdout is not needed to prove (5.1)-(5.3) under A1-A6. If the intended use needs a data-based predictive check, reserve N_val separated pairs not used for fitting, with epsilon_val=eta/4 and confidence delta_val. Estimate every validation row and accept the fixed reconstructed predictor P_tilde only if

    max_x ||P_tilde_x-P_hat_val,x||_1 <= 3eta/4.

On the validation event max_x||P_hat_val,x-P_x||_1<=eta/4, acceptance certifies max_x||P_tilde_x-P_x||_1<=eta. Conversely, if training guarantees max_x||P_tilde_x-P_x||_1<=eta/2, then the check passes on that event. To ensure the latter after quantization, fit with 3epsilon<=eta/4 and quantize with row error at most eta/4. Training and validation pair sets can be separated in one stream; use the mixing spacing based on N_train+N_val, and charge the full raw trajectory. The validation cost is another O((d+ln(d/delta_val))/(pi_0 eta^2)) selected pairs, plus its mixing gaps. A holdout check validates predictive transition error only; it cannot certify that the anchor model or global sparsity assumption is true outside the observed distribution.

Transfer example: a later task supplies a new state reward g, with |g(x)|<=G, but no new transition labels. The acquired dictionary computes its one-step conditional reward as Theta(Qg); its h-step value forecast is P_tilde^h g. If the one-step model error is at most eta in row L1, the h-step forecast error is at most h eta G uniformly over all starting states. Thus one acquired feature subspace serves later state-reward tasks without re-learning the transition representation. Action-dependent or nonstationary task dynamics are outside this transfer claim.

8. CODING, PRECISION, COMPUTATION, AND STORAGE

Raw data and sampling. Each observed state symbol costs ceil(log2 d) bits. The proof uses N transition pairs but the user pays for T raw states, including the b-1 skipped states between selected pairs. Retaining the full path costs T ceil(log2 d) bits. No separately queried scalar response is available; per-state estimates use the observed transition counts. Per-row empirical probabilities have rational denominators at most N. A dense exact transition table uses O(d^2 log(N+1)) count bits, plus row counts and state labels.

Dictionary code. With a shared alphabet/decoder and B-bit simplex-grid quantization, a code for (Q,Theta) needs at most

    r ceil(log2 d)                                   anchor IDs,
    r(d-1)B + O(rd log d)                            atom probabilities,
    d[ceil(log2(s+1))+s ceil(log2 r)+(s-1)B]          sparse supports and weights,

bits, plus a header, rational/model class parameters, and decoder code. A simplex-grid quantizer can be chosen with ||q_j-q_tilde_j||_1<=2d 2^-B and ||Theta[x,:]-Theta_tilde[x,:]||_1<=2s 2^-B; hence the reconstructed row error increases by at most 2(d+s)2^-B. Pick B>=ceil(log2(8h(d+s)/eta)) to make this no more than eta/(4h), adjusting upward if a different accuracy budget is chosen. This code is explicit and not assumed free; it may exceed raw-path storage when d,r,s or requested precision make it uneconomical. The descriptor bounds gamma_0,Delta_0,sigma_0,kappa_0,pi_0,tau and their encodings must also be transmitted if they are not shared.

Validation and sensing bits. Validation consumes the separate raw holdout trajectory described above and an independent confidence allocation. The block spacing b is itself O(log(N/delta)) time steps per retained transition pair, so total generated symbols, including spacing, dominate the sensing cost. If there is a reset/burn-in cost, it is added separately.

Arithmetic. Anchor detection solves d L1-distance-to-convex-hull LPs in dimension d. Sparse coefficient recovery solves at most sum_{j=0}^{s_0} binom(r,j) simplex-constrained L1 regression LPs per state; exact support recovery is therefore exponential in s_0 in this direct implementation. Subspace extraction needs a certified rank-revealing factorization of matrices of size d x r and r x d. Rational inputs allow exact LPs; matrix singular vectors may instead be computed with certified interval arithmetic to a stated tolerance. No uncharged numerical oracle is assumed. Outputting dense Theta or P costs Omega(dr) or Omega(d^2) numbers; the sparse code can avoid this output cost only when its encoded supports and weights are actually written.

9. STRONGEST RETAINED-TRAJECTORY BASELINE

The matched baseline stores all T raw symbols and all T-1 observed transitions, estimates every row from every visit, and can then run any anchor or non-anchor estimator. It can simulate the algorithm above by discarding the same intermediate pairs. It therefore has at least the same information and usually more row samples than the spaced-pair proof estimator, at the same generated-trajectory cost. It can also validate on a held-out suffix/segment. For any loss, the best estimator on the full retained trajectory has risk no greater than any deterministic or randomized postprocessing of a subsampled trajectory. This theorem does not claim lower sample complexity than full-data estimation or a gain from adaptive sensing.

Full-data fitting costs O(T) to scan transitions, O(d^2) row-count slots, and a dense factorization up to O(d^3); the sparse method trades that for exact rowwise convex-hull LPs and support enumeration, which can be worse computationally. Its possible advantage is a compact reusable predictive code of size roughly rdB+dsB when r,s<<d and the gaps are not too small. The raw trajectory remains the strongest statistical baseline, and all costs are counted before claiming a compression or transfer gain.

10. ESTABLISHED RESULTS VERSUS THIS DERIVATION

Established: separable/nonnegative matrix factorization uses unknown pure rows/anchors to identify a factorization from the complete matrix; Arora, Ge, Kannan, and Moitra give a polynomial-time algorithm under separability for matrix input. Empirical L1 concentration for finite-alphabet distributions is established by Weissman et al. Absolute-regularity block coupling to independent blocks is established in the mixing empirical-process literature, including Yu. Standard singular-subspace perturbation inequalities are used for the reported subspace stability.

Derived here: specialize the raw transition matrix of a finite observable Markov chain to a row-stochastic sparse factorization, prove that the explicit no-near-pure plus atom-separation margins give an anchor residual gap gamma Delta, combine per-row multinomial estimation with a beta-mixing path coupling, and propagate coefficient/feature/prediction error through later Markov tasks. The exact constants in this leaf are direct conservative derivations from those ingredients. This is not a novelty, priority, or literature-completeness claim; no external proof review was performed.

11. UNKNOWN AND FAILURE BOUNDARIES

UNKNOWN-1: Hidden-state HMMs. The current theorem observes the Markov state itself. It does not prove that anchor states/features can be recovered from an HMM output stream when the predictive state is hidden or the observations are non-Markovian.

UNKNOWN-2: No anchor/identifiability condition. A raw process law determines its predictive subspace but need not determine an intended sparse basis. General change-of-basis or alternative nonnegative factorizations may leave the full source law unchanged. The result does not select a canonical dictionary in that case.

UNKNOWN-3: Vanishing margins. If gamma Delta or sigma tends to zero, the residual classification or coordinate/subspace recovery is ill-conditioned, and the displayed sample/precision guarantee diverges. A matching minimax lower bound for this exact Markov factor class has not been proved here.

UNKNOWN-4: Adaptive advantage and optimality. Full retained trajectories dominate the subsampled implementation. No adaptive-versus-passive sample separation or matching lower bound is claimed. The beta-mixing independent-block spacing is conservative; optimal dependence on tau is open here.

UNKNOWN-5: Computation. The finite-sample theorem is statistical. The direct sparse fit enumerates supports; efficient algorithms for growing s_0 under the exact stochastic constraints, and certified arithmetic bit complexity at scale, are not established.

UNKNOWN-6: Validation. A fresh held-out transition sample checks prediction over states with adequate stationary coverage. It does not prove global factorization, exact anchor uniqueness, an unobserved state’s prediction, or downstream performance under distribution shift.
