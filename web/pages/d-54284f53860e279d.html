STRONGEST CLAIM

The signed ambient-hole route and the aggregate capacity argument are internally consistent at the stated interfaces, but they do not yield a hard-BCS counting algorithm. A useful strengthening is that the capacity proof's row-balance condition is always attainable as a law-preserving diagonal congruence whenever all two-hole ratios are positive. This is an existence statement only: the congruence depends on the exact two-hole partition ratios, and no polynomial-time acquisition procedure for them is supplied. The remaining algorithmic gate is still a computable microscopic two-replica Poincare inequality for determinant-squared paired bases.

1. SIGNED-HOLE STABILITY, RECONSTRUCTED

Let F be an n by n complex matrix, n even. For even R subset [n], set
  Z_R = sum_{I subset R, |I|=|R|/2} |det F[I,R\I]|^2,
  f(U)=Z_[n]\U,
with f(U)=0 for odd |U| and Z_empty=1. Form the Hermitian chiral matrix
  H = [[0,2F],[2F*,0]]
with coordinates u_1,...,u_n,v_1,...,v_n. The polynomial
  P(u,v)=det(diag(u,v)-H)
has no zero when all coordinates are in the upper half-plane: the imaginary part of the matrix is the strictly positive diagonal matrix diag(Im u, Im v), so a null vector is impossible. Its coefficients are real since H is Hermitian.

Apply 2^(-n) product_i(∂_{u_i}+∂_{v_i}) and then identify u_i=v_i=x_i. Each derivative chooses one coordinate from the i-th chiral pair. A surviving principal minor has equal numbers k of upper and lower indices; it vanishes unless those index sets I,J are disjoint, and then its determinant is (-1)^k 2^(2k)|det F[I,J]|^2. Its selection weight is 2^(-2k), leaving coefficient (-1)^k. Stability is preserved by these positive directional derivatives and identification. The output is therefore the nonzero real-stable multiaffine polynomial
  Q_F(x)=sum_{U even} (-1)^((n-|U|)/2) f(U) x^U.
Rotating x to i x gives the right-half-plane-stable polynomial h(x)=(-1)^(n/2)Q_F(i x)=sum_U f(U)x^U. This is a statement about the ambient-hole coefficients; it does not make the distinct hard pair-label generating polynomial stable.

A positive coordinate auxiliary pair (e_i,e_j) of activity w changes f(U) to f(U)+w f(U union {i,j}) when U avoids i,j, by expansion along its two coordinate columns. Equivalently Q changes to Q-w∂_i∂_jQ. Its multiaffine algebraic symbol is [(z_i+y_i)(z_j+y_j)-w] product_{k notin{i,j}}(z_k+y_k). Each z_k+y_k is in the upper half-plane; a product of two such numbers cannot be a nonnegative real w. The symbol is stable, so the Borcea-Branden symbol criterion gives closure under any sequence of these coordinate-pair auxiliaries. This does not justify coherent arbitrary-vector-pair insertion.

2. ALL-SIZE ROOT EXCHANGE

For any even S,T and a in D=S triangle T, signed-hole stability implies
  sqrt(f(S)f(T)) <= sum_{j in D, j!=a} sqrt(f(S triangle {a,j}) f(T triangle {a,j})).       (E)
This covers every symmetric-difference size and a common occupied core. Here is the coefficient reduction. Let h=sum_U f(U)x^U. Reciprocal-twist every coordinate in T:
  h^T(x)=x^T h((1/x_i)_{i in T},(x_i)_{i notin T}).
Inversion preserves the right half-plane, h^T is stable, and its coefficient at U is f(T triangle U). Specialize outside D to zero; this remains stable and is nonzero when f(T)>0. Put x_a=1 and x_j=w_j z for j in D\{a}, with every w_j>0. The resulting real polynomial p(z) is right-half-plane stable and has odd degree d=|D|-1. Its four endpoint coefficients are
  a_0=f(T), a_d=f(S) product_j w_j,
  a_1=sum_j f(T triangle {a,j})w_j,
  a_(d-1)=(product_j w_j) sum_j f(S triangle {a,j})/w_j.
A real odd-degree polynomial with positive endpoints and no root in Re z>0 obeys a_0a_d<=a_1a_(d-1): write p(z)=a_0 product_l(1+r_l z), where Re r_l>=0. Conjugate pairs contribute nonnegative amounts to both endpoint ratios; odd degree supplies at least one real r_l>0, whose contributions multiply to one. Thus the product of the two ratios is at least one. Substitution gives
  f(S)f(T) <= (sum_j A_jw_j)(sum_j B_j/w_j),
where A_j=f(T triangle {a,j}) and B_j=f(S triangle {a,j}). The infimum over positive w is (sum_j sqrt(A_jB_j))^2 by Cauchy-Schwarz; zero cases follow by limits. Taking roots proves (E). This avoids dividing by a possibly zero coefficient. The exchange constant one is sharp on a product of disjoint coordinate pairs.

3. WHAT CAPACITY PROVES

For the original determinant-pair system plus positive coordinate pairs, normalize g(U)=f(U)/f(empty), let r_i=max_{j!=i}g({i,j}), and suppose 1/4<=r_i<=1 at the balanced input. Let B_ij be the maximum bottleneck activity over coordinate paths from i to j, and Phi_B(U) the maximum product of B_ij over pairings of U. Coordinate-column expansion gives g(U)<=g(U\{i,j})/lambda_ij whenever {i,j} is a hole pair. The |D|=4 case of (E) gives g(U)<=144 for four holes in the intermediate range r_i in [1/8,4]. Relocating one hole a along a simple B-strong path toward another hole z, and applying (E) to S and {z,p} where g(zp)=r_z, yields
  sqrt(g(S)/r_a) <= sqrt(g(S\{a} union {z})/r_z) + 192/sqrt(B).
The z-already-a-hole endpoint costs at most sqrt(32)/sqrt(B). At most n-2 relocation steps and r_i<=4 give g(U)<=147456 n^2/B for |U|=2 or 4. The leading relocation coefficient is one, so the path errors add rather than multiply.

Inflating every coordinate edge by t B_ij/D0, D0=10^8(n+1)^4, gives exact derivative identities for log f(empty) and two-hole ratios. The preceding bound limits the total relative derivative by binom(n,2)147456n^2/D0 < 0.00073728. Continuity keeps intermediate row maxima inside [1/8,4] and changes f(empty) by a factor below e^.001<2. Expanding a fixed pairing of holes into its coordinate lines then proves
  g(U) Phi_B(U) <= 2 D0^(|U|/2).
This is an aggregate partition inequality. Its proof uses no sampler and does not evaluate f(U), but it assumes balance and says nothing by itself about mixing or acquisition.

4. BALANCE EXISTS; ACQUISITION DOES NOT FOLLOW

Suppose all a_ij=g({i,j}) are positive. Write c_ij=log a_ij and consider the closed polyhedron y_i+y_j<=-c_ij. It is feasible by taking every y_i sufficiently negative. For n>=3, each coordinate is bounded above by adding constraints on {i,j},{i,k},{j,k}: 2y_i<=-c_ij-c_ik+c_jk for distinct j,k. Thus maximizing sum_i y_i has a finite attained optimum. At an optimum, every i has a tight incident constraint, since otherwise y_i could be increased slightly. Hence the scaled edge values a'_ij=exp(y_i+y_j)a_ij all lie in (0,1], with max_j a'_ij=1 for every i. For n=2 choose y_1+y_2=-c_12.

This scaling is implemented geometrically by F'=D F D with D_ii=exp(-y_i/2): each minor F[I,R\I] is multiplied by product_{i in R}D_ii, independent of I. Thus every full-basis weight at U=empty receives the same multiplier, while g(U) changes by exp(sum_{i in U}y_i). If coordinate-pair auxiliaries are present, scale their two vectors by the same ambient D; this changes their activities but preserves the full-basis law. Therefore the balance band is mathematically attainable whenever the required ratios are positive. However, the LP input is the exact list g({i,j}); these are sums of squared minors, not individual determinant queries. The support/witness algorithm for linear matroid parity is not a coefficient-value oracle. The prior work provides no polynomial-time way to acquire these ratios or their estimates under the same interface. Balance is not a fatal existence assumption, but it remains an unclosed computational gate.

5. MICROSCOPIC OBSTRUCTION AND PRECISE REMAINDER

For the hard pair-label fiber take F to be the permutation matrix of the directed cycle sigma(i)=i+1 mod 2q. A state is a q-set I with J=I^c. The minor F[I,J] is nonzero exactly when sigma(I)=J. This requires I to be a maximum independent set of the even cycle, so I is precisely the odd or the even vertices. Both amplitudes have modulus one; there are exactly two hard states, at Hamming distance q. Thus any chain confined to this hard fiber and changing fewer than q pair labels per move is disconnected. This rules out a direct bounded-local-update sampler for the original hard fiber. It is not a mixing lower bound for the enlarged state space with positive coordinate auxiliaries, where paths may leave the hard fiber.

The required stronger object is a reversible, polynomial-bit computable kernel on microscopic augmented pair configurations A with point law pi(A) proportional to (product of line activities) times |det V_A|^2, and a polynomial L such that, uniformly on the annealing path,
  Var_pi f_1 <= L E_{P,pi tensor pi}[(A,B) -> f_1(A)+f_2(B)]
for all real f_1,f_2. Determinant ratios for proposed local moves can be computed from rational input, but the energy comparison is missing. Product-edge overlay swaps preserve matching weight and have bounded preimage encodings; squared determinants do not inherit those properties. A reported four-row reassignment has product squared-weight ratio delta^4 with delta=2^-L despite unit activities, so comparable activities alone cannot justify the graph proof's weight-ratio step. Exact determinant evaluation and finite-field support/witness routines do not bound path congestion. The shared-core common-cut projection can annihilate a nonzero two-replica tensor, and a global positive pairing-orbit encoding loses exponentially on tensor powers; these exclude those shortcuts, not every localized or mismatch-register kernel.
