# Exponential raw semi-invariant degree with an all-prime packing polytope

7 October2026. Independently derived companion to the random-prime reduction. No priority claim. The older Derksen--Makam alternating-chain obstruction and the fresh Maar star-gap obstruction are close controls, not confirmations of originality.

## 1. Question and exact claim

Can a uniform polynomial-degree family of quiver semi-invariants detect all semistable subspace stars, and hence provide the desired complete-polytope good-prime certificate beyond rank two?

For every k>=0 we construct a 0/1 subspace star with r=m=3(k+1), each leaf dimension at most THREE, total quiver vertex dimension 10k+9, and dense input size O(k^2). Its packing polytope is the same over EVERY field. Nevertheless, the smallest degree of a positive-degree complex SL-quiver invariant nonzero at this representation is exactly

rD=3(k+1)2^(k+1).

Thus polynomial RAW degree cannot uniformly supply a nonvanishing invariant even for leaf ranks<=3. This is not an algorithm lower bound, a compressed-circuit lower bound, or an obstruction to the companion random-prime method. In fact the example has cheap coordinate LP geometry and no bad primes.

## 2. The coordinate incidence matrix

There are variables a_i,b_i,z_i for i=0,...,k. Form the square 0/1 matrix A whose rows express the following loads:

For i<k: a_i+z_i, b_i+z_i, a_i+b_i+a_(i+1).

For i=k: a_k+z_k, b_k+z_k, a_k+b_k.

Let S_j be the coordinate subspace spanned by e_l for rows l with A_(l,j)=1, and let V_j be its standard coordinate inclusion into C^r. Dimensions are d(a_0)=2, d(a_i)=3 for i>=1, d(b_i)=d(z_i)=2. Their sum is 7k+6.

The equation Ax=1 has the unique solution

b_i=a_i, z_i=1-a_i, a_k=1/2, a_i=(1-a_(i+1))/2,

a_i=1/3+(-1/2)^(k-i)/6.

All coordinates are positive. Eliminate z_i using its first row, and b_i using the difference of the first two rows. The remaining upper-bidiagonal equations are 2a_i+a_(i+1)=1 for i<k and 2a_k=1. Hence |det A|=2^(k+1)=D. In particular a_0 has reduced denominator D: its numerator before reduction is [2^(k+1)+(-1)^k]/3, an odd integer. The integrality follows modulo3, and oddness follows because division by3 preserves parity.

## 3. The packing polytope over every field

For coordinate subspaces described by any 0/1 incidence matrix A,

P_K={x>=0:Ax<=1}

over every field K. Necessity follows by testing the one-dimensional coordinate subspaces. For sufficiency, put a basis of any W in row RREF and let P be its pivot-coordinate set. Projection W->K^P is an isomorphism. For a coordinate subspace S_J, the image of W intersect S_J is contained in K^(P intersect J), so dim(W intersect S_J)<=|P intersect J|. Therefore, for nonnegative x,

sum_j x_j dim(W intersect S_j)<=sum_(l in P)(Ax)_l<=|P|=dim W.

For this square invertible A, the balanced face sum_j d_j x_j=r has a unique point x*: the sum of all row loads is r, and each load is <=1, so every load equals1 and Ax*=1. A is inverted over the REAL weight space; its characteristic-two determinant vanishing does not change this real coordinate LP or invalidate the all-field proof.

## 4. Necessary character size and degree

Use the standard star action V_j -> g_0 V_j g_j^(-1), with center dimension r and leaf dimensions d_j. Fix the semi-invariant convention

f(g^(-1)V)=chi_sigma(g)f(V), chi_sigma(g)=product_v det(g_v)^(sigma_v).

For a polynomial semi-invariant, scaling leaf j by tI forces its arrow multidegree to be d_j sigma_j, so sigma_j>=0. Scaling the center by tI forces total degree T=-r sigma_0.

For each ambient coordinate l, scale its center basis vector by t, and in each leaf containing l scale the corresponding leaf basis vector by t. This is an actual stabilizer of V. If f(V)!=0 then its character on this stabilizer is trivial; hence

sigma_0+sum_(j:A_(l,j)=1) sigma_j=0 for every l,

or A sigma_leaf=(-sigma_0)1. By uniqueness, sigma_leaf=lambda x*, lambda=-sigma_0. If degree is positive, lambda is a positive integer. Since a_0 has reduced denominator D, integrality of sigma_leaf forces D to divide lambda. Thus T=r lambda>=rD.

Every homogeneous SL-invariant decomposes under the residual determinant torus of the GL product into polynomial character components. A nonzero evaluation has a character component with nonzero evaluation. Consequently all positive-degree SL-invariants of degree below rD vanish at V; arbitrary cancellation between character components cannot bypass the bound. Constants are excluded, as appropriate for nullcone witnesses.

## 5. Matching nonvanishing determinant at degree rD

Set sigma_j=D x*_j, nonnegative integers. Then sum_j d_j sigma_j=rD. Let M=sum_j sigma_j, choose distinct integers z_1,...,z_M, and form the D x M Vandermonde matrix T with entries T_(t,h)=z_h^(t-1). Partition its columns into T_j of sizes sigma_j. Define

f(A_1,...,A_m)=det[ T_1 tensor A_1 | ... | T_m tensor A_m ].

This is a square rD matrix, so f has degree rD. Under inverse group action, the center contributes det(g_0)^(-D) and leaf j contributes det(g_j)^(sigma_j); it has exactly the character required above.

At the coordinate inclusions V_j, permute rows and columns by ambient coordinate l. The matrix becomes block diagonal, with one D x D block for each l. That block consists of the T columns belonging to leaves containing l. There are exactly sum_j A_(l,j) sigma_j=D such columns, each with a distinct z_h. Every block is a nonsingular Vandermonde matrix. Thus f(V)!=0, proving that the lower bound rD is attained exactly.

This explicit witness is exponentially large in k. It is an EXISTENCE construction used to prove the exact minimum, not an efficient acquisition routine. With z_h=1,...,M, individual constants have bit length O(D log M); expanding or storing the determinant can cost exponentially. No uncharged low-bit expanded certificate is asserted.

## 6. Nearest prior and what the example discriminates

Derksen--Makam1603.00417v1, Section9/Proposition1.5, constructs an alternating double-arrow CHAIN with dimension vector (2,3,...,3,1), a unique effective character ray with weights growing as powers of2, and an exponential minimum nonvanishing character size. Their fixed-weight bounds and global bounds must be distinguished: fixed-weight polynomial dependence is in the NUMERICAL character size, not its binary encoding. Their arbitrary-characteristic paper1610.06617v1 gives polynomial bounds for GL-invariant rings, while its SL/semi-invariant bounds retain exponential vertex dependence; the polynomial GL theorem does not contradict this acyclic star example.

Maar2606.02805v1, Section6.1 Proposition6.1/Lemma6.2, already proves exponentially small SL-gap for star quivers through projected-cube geometry. Its leaf dimensions need not be<=3, and it does not state this exact minimum degree or all-prime packing polytope in the portions read. Thus the broad exponential star obstruction is PRIOR; priority for this rank-three degree variant remains UNKNOWN. Section5 Theorem5.1's augmented GL sigma-semistability gap bound depends on numeric |sigma|, so the exponential forced character in this family is fully compatible with it.

The useful conclusion is narrow and consequential: a proposed proof that all subspace stars have a polynomial RAW-degree nonvanishing semi-invariant is false even with integral coordinate inputs and leaf ranks<=3. On the other hand, degree failure supplies no evidence of bad-prime difficulty: this same family has an identical polytope over every prime. The randomized arithmetic-count reduction addresses a different invariant and survives this control.
