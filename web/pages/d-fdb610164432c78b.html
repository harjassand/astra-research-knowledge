4. MASTER EB INEQUALITY: SECTOR CHANGES INCLUDED (SUPERSEDED LOWER ROUTE)

Define, for all integer s>=0,

  f(s)=Z_s(q)/(s+1)=(1/(1-q)) integral_q^1 x^s dx,
  t_L=1/f(L),     b_L=t_L^2 f(2L),
  T_L=Z_L(q^2)/Z_L(q)=(1+q^(L+1))/(1+q),
  g_L=T_L-t_L f(2L).                                     (4.1)

For S={L:L<=J}, C=max_(L in S)t_L, and eta_J=P(L not in S), every EB
reconstruction with Haar-average error at most e satisfies

  (3/2)e >= C^(-1) sum_(L in S) P_L t_L g_L - eta_J.       (4.2)

Worst-case error dominates Haar-average error, so (4.2) applies to the contract.

Proof. An arbitrary EB map can be written as a POVM {M_y} followed by prepared
states sigma_y. Pinching the input/output Schur sectors changes neither the
model nor the tested scores. Denote input block M_(y,L), prepared output block
sigma_(y,K), and s_(y,K)=Tr sigma_(y,K). Set

  W_LK=P_L integral [Tr M_(y,L)/(L+1)] s_(y,K) dy.          (4.3)

These nonnegative numbers form a probability coupling. Its input marginal is
P_L. Its output marginal Q_K is the Haar-average decoded sector law, because
integral tau_(L,v) dv=I/(L+1). Measuring sectors contracts trace distance, hence
TV(P,Q)<=e. This is the step that permits arbitrary output-sector changes.

The exact product twirl satisfies

  ||integral tau_(L,v) tensor q^(K/2-J_v) dv||_op
       = Z_(L+K)(q)/[(L+K+1)Z_L(q)].                      (4.4)

For completeness, Clebsch-Gordan splits V_L tensor V_K into spins
M=L+K,L+K-2,...,|L-K|, each once. The twirl scalar on spin M is
q^((L+K-M)/2) Z_M/[(M+1)Z_L]. After multiplication by q^(-(L+K)/2), its
dependence on M is sinh((M+1)beta/2)/(M+1), beta=-log q, which increases with
M. The maximum is therefore M=L+K. This is a same-sign product twirl;
there is no Choi transpose or opposite-spin representation in (4.4).

Use the output effect

  E_v=direct_sum_(K in S) (t_K/C) q^(K/2-J_v),             (4.5)

zero on other sectors. It is bounded between 0 and I. On a retained input/
output pair (L,K), (4.4) bounds the decoded Haar score by

  C^(-1) t_L t_K f(L+K) W_LK
    <= (2C)^(-1)(b_L+b_K) W_LK,                          (4.6)

where the final inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz for the positive measure in
(4.1). On input L outside S, the contribution is at most its total mass
eta_J, since E_v<=I. Also b_K<=t_K<=C, because f(2K)<=f(K).
Summing (4.6) with the coupling gives a decoded score at most

  C^(-1)sum_S P_L b_L + e/2 + eta_J.                     (4.7)

Indeed the second marginal term differs from its P marginal by at most e/2
after its factor 1/2 and TV(P,Q)<=e. The target score is
C^(-1)sum_S P_L t_L T_L. Every effect changes its expectation by at most e
under half trace distance. Subtraction proves (4.2). The argument uses only
positive integrals and works for an arbitrary outcome space. END PROOF.

5. POSITIVE SPIN GAP AND LOGARITHMIC CUTOFF (SUPERSEDED LOWER ROUTE)

For every L and 0<=q<=1,

  g_L>=0,
  g_L>=r/2-1/[2(2L+1)].                                 (5.1)

The first inequality is equivalently

  L sum_(s=0)^L q^(2s) >= (L+1) sum_(s=0)^(L-1) q^(2s+1).

It follows by the midpoint convexity of the exponential sequence: the odd
sum is at most the even sum minus half its two endpoints, while the even sum
is at most (L+1) times half those endpoints. Both are exact finite inequalities.

For the second inequality put h=L+1. After substituting (4.1) and cancelling
positive factors, the needed scalar inequality is

  h(1+q)(1-q^(h-1)) <= (2h-1)(1-q^h).

For h>=2 its difference is
F(q)=(h-1)-h q+h q^(h-1)-(h-1)q^h, with
F'(q)=h[-1+(h-1)q^(h-2)(1-q)]<=0 and F(1)=0. The derivative bound follows
by maximizing q^(h-2)(1-q); h=1 is immediate. This proves (5.1). Consequently

  L>=ceil(1/r) implies g_L>=r/4.                          (5.2)

Moreover t_L>=1. With beta=log((1+r)/(1-r))<=8r/3,

  t_L <= beta(L+1)/(1-exp[-beta(L+1)])
      <= 1+beta(L+1).                                    (5.3)

The last scalar inequality is y/(1-e^(-y))<=1+y.

Take t=2sqrt(log(1/r)) and J=min{n,ceil(nr+t sqrt(n))}. From (3.5),
eta_J<=C0(1+t)r^2. From (5.3) and (1.1), C<=C1(c1)(1+t). Equations

  sum_(L in S) P_L t_L g_L

For sufficiently small r, alpha0-eta_J>=alpha0/2 and

  B_L(X)=(L+1) integral <L,u|X|L,u>|L,u><L,u| dmu(u).

  b_k=Z_L(q)^(-1) E[(1-(1-q)X)^L],
  X~Beta(k+1,L-k+1).                                    (6.1)

  b_k >=q^[L(k+1)/(L+2)]/Z_L(q)
      >=q^(k+1)/Z_L(q).                                 (6.2)

1-q=2r/(1+r), uniformly in n and v.

8. EXACT CANONICAL OSCILLATOR TAIL AND PARTIAL-COLLISION OBSTRUCTION

The highest-weight Fock isometry V_L maps |L/2,L/2-k> to |k>, k=0,...,L.

  theta_q=(1-q)sum_(k>=0)q^k|k><k|.

the omitted tail has the same total mass. Therefore

  T(theta_(L,q),theta_q)=q^(L+1)                          (8.1)

exactly. With the exact Schur label retained, the global canonical replacement
error is

  A_n(r)=sum_L P_L q^(L+1).                              (8.2)

Let kappa=nr^2. The exact Casimir identity and EL>=nr give
E(L-nr)^2<=3n and EL<=nr+sqrt(3n). Jensen and beta<=8r/3 imply

  A_n>=exp[-(8/3)(kappa+sqrt(3kappa)+r)].                 (8.3)

On L>nr/2, q^(L+1)<=exp(-kappa). On the complementary event, L>=|sum Z_i|
implies sum Z_i<=nr/2, whose binomial Hoeffding probability is at most
exp(-kappa/8). Hence

  A_n<=exp(-kappa)+exp(-kappa/8)<=2exp(-kappa/8).          (8.4)

Consequently A_n->0 exactly when kappa->infinity, among arbitrary sequences
0<r<=1/2. In particular the canonical thermal replacement cannot justify
accuracy epsilon unless kappa is at least of logarithmic order in 1/epsilon
up to these explicit constants. This is a necessary gate for this construction
and a sufficient base-thermal gate; it says nothing by itself about rotations.

If r_n sqrt(n)->c>0, the Schur spin X=L/sqrt(n) has limiting density

  f_c(x)=(x/c)[phi(x-c)-phi(x+c)],  x>=0.                 (8.5)

pi_k=(2/sqrt(n))phi(x-c)+o(n^(-1/2)), and

uniform; the second-moment bound makes the mass tight. Thus the parity lattice

  A_n -> A(c)=integral_0^infinity f_c(x)e^(-2cx)dx
             =3e^(4c^2)Phi(-3c)-Phi(-c)>0.               (8.6)

The last equality uses
integral_0^infinity x phi(x+a)dx=phi(a)-a Phi(-a); the phi terms cancel.
Strict positivity also follows directly from the positive integrand in (8.6),
so numerical cancellation is not the proof. For example A(1)=0.06245055...
and A(3)=0.00010978..., both independent of n.

  p_n=(a+c/(2sqrt(n)), a-c/(2sqrt(n)), 1-2a),

12. REUSABLE BOUNDED POSTERIOR-SCORE LEMMA

This lemma states the actual supplied premise required for transfer. A
covariance label alone is not a substitute for its posterior-radius bound.

Let v have a supplied probability prior mu on a subset of the unit Euclidean
ball in R^s. Let the quantum experiment be

  R_v=direct_sum_L P_L tau_(L,v),                         (12.1)

where P_L is known and independent of v, each tau_(L,v) is a density operator,
and the output must be in this same direct-sum Hilbert space. Suppose supplied
numbers 0<=a_L<=A<=1 and Hermitian vector observables V_L satisfy:

(i) For every positive M with positive integrated probability in sector L,

  || [integral v Tr(M tau_(L,v)) dmu(v)] /
         [integral Tr(M tau_(L,v)) dmu(v)] ||_2 <= a_L.    (12.2)

(ii) For every unit vector u, ||u.V_L||_op<=1.

(iii) Define b_L=integral Tr[tau_(L,v) v.V_L]dmu(v).

Then any EB full-density reconstruction with mu-average half trace error e
satisfies, for A>0,

  e >= [sum_L P_L a_L(b_L-a_L)]/(2A).                    (12.3)

If the numerator is nonpositive the bound is simply uninformative. Countably
many sectors are also permitted if the displayed integrals exist; all witness
operators stay bounded. The statement allows arbitrary EB outcome spaces and
input-output sector changes. It does not assume output probabilities equal P.

Proof. An EB channel measures M_y and prepares sigma_y. Sector pinching may be
inserted because the target and witness are block diagonal. Define the output
score

  F_K(v)=a_K v.V_K-(a_K^2/2)I.                           (12.4)

For each input L, outcome y and output K, the normalized input posterior mean
m_(y,L) has norm at most a_L by (12.2), and the prepared output vector
w_(y,K)=Tr(sigma_(y,K) V_K)/Tr sigma_(y,K) has norm at most 1 by (ii). The
conditional averaged score is therefore at most

  a_K a_L-a_K^2/2
    =a_L^2/2-(a_K-a_L)^2/2 <= a_L^2/2.                   (12.5)

After averaging over all outcomes/output sectors, the input marginal P gives
decoded score at most sum_L P_L a_L^2/2. The target score is
sum_L P_L[a_L b_L-a_L^2/2]. Their difference is at least the numerator in
(12.3). No tail cutoff or output-marginal approximation has entered.

The best common interval guaranteed by the hypotheses is

  -A-A^2/2 <= F_K(v) <= A-A^2/2.                         (12.6)

Indeed the lower endpoint -a-a^2/2 decreases in a, while a-a^2/2 increases
for 0<=a<=1. Thus the common shift F+A^2 I/2 has norm at most A, and

  E_v=[F(v)+(A+A^2/2)I]/(2A)                            (12.7)

is an effect. Its averaged target-decoded difference is the score difference
divided by 2A and cannot exceed e. This proves (12.3). The interval width 2A
is the best uniform scalar normalization implied solely by these hypotheses,

endpoints. If A=0, the score is zero and this mechanism gives no converse.
