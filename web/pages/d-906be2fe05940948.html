## Model and operator

Let Z=tau eta, with tau positive and independent of independent symmetric eta_i. Assume E eta_i^2=1, kappa4(eta_i)>0, E tau^2=1, finite fourth moments. Put c=E tau^4 and beta_i=c kappa4(eta_i)>0. Standardization and Jensen give c>=1. Then

    E(u^T Z)^4=c[3||u||^4]+sum_i beta_i u_i^4.

Use the fourth-moment tensor S, not the cumulant, as a selfadjoint operator on the Hilbert space Sym_n with Frobenius inner product. The tensor of ||u||^4 has entries(delta_ij delta_kl+delta_ik delta_jl+delta_il delta_jk)/3. The polynomial first display has factor3c, so its tensor is **c times the three covariance pairings**, and the adopted operator is

    K(M)=c tr(M)I+2c M+sum_i beta_i E_ii <E_ii,M>.       (1)

An initial draft incorrectly used (c/3)tr(M)I+(2c/3)M for the isotropic part. That is the operator for a polynomial coefficient c||u||^4, whereas this model has 3c||u||^4. The erroneous normalization was corrected before any claim was independently gated or executed; equation(1) is the convention throughout.

Let P0(M)=M-tr(M)I/n, and work on H0=Sym_n intersect I-perp, dimension d0=n(n+1)/2-1. Let D0 be the n-1 dimensional subspace of traceless diagonal matrices. Compress (1) to H0:

    K0=2c I_H0+P0[sum_i beta_i |E_ii><E_ii|]P0
       =lambda0 I_H0+B,
    lambda0=2c, B>=0, rank(B)=n-1, range(B)=D0.     (2)

For any nonzero traceless diagonal M, <M,BM>=sum beta_i M_ii^2>0, proving rank/range. For an unknown orthogonal true frame, conjugate (2) by its isometry on symmetric matrices: D0 becomes the true diagonal-traceless subspace Dt0. No covariance, hidden frame or nearby branch is supplied in the certificate below. Its actual positive-common-variance model promise is supplied, identically to every comparator. Fourth-cumulant QPD alone does not imply (2).

## Posterior interface

Suppose an observed selfadjoint operator Khat on H0 has a rigorous simultaneous error bound

    ||Khat-K0||op<=e.

A data-dependent proposed orthonormal frame defines candidate diagonal-traceless subspace Dc0 and its orthogonal projector P. On P's range and its complement define observed compressions Ahat=PKhatP, Chat=Pperp Khat Pperp, Bhat=Pperp Khat P. Obtain certified scalar bounds

    a<=lambda_min(Ahat), c_obs>=lambda_max(Chat),
    b>=||Bhat||op, g=a-c_obs-2e.

The reused letter c_obs is unrelated to the model fourth scale c. Matrix intervals, rational congruence/LDL checks and an outward Frobenius bound for the cross block may be used to compute these scalars. Numeric eigenvalues alone are not a certificate. If g<=0, return UNKNOWN_SPECTRAL_SEPARATION. If g>0, put eta=(b+e)/g. Candidate acquisition can use the ordinary spectral/eigenmatrix or MaxKurt algorithms; the theorem applies to every adaptive proposal on the same valid input-error event.

## Global graph proof; no local branch

Write A=PK0P and Bcross=Pperp K0 P. Because K0-lambda0 I is positive semidefinite, lambda0<=lambda_max(Pperp K0Pperp)<=c_obs+e. Also A>= (a-e)I. Thus

    A-lambda0 I >=g I>0,
    ||Bcross||op<=b+e.

The n-1 columns of (K0-lambda0 I)P have full rank by their invertible top block A-lambda0 I. They lie in Dt0, which has the same dimension, so they span Dt0. Relative to Dc0 and its complement, Dt0 is therefore exactly the graph

    Zgraph=Bcross(A-lambda0 I)^(-1),
    ||Zgraph||op<=eta.                          (3)

There is no hidden closeness assumption in (3). For a graph, its largest principal-angle sine is at most

    s=eta/sqrt(1+eta^2).                         (4)

One can prove (4) by the orthonormal graph matrix [I;Z](I+Z^T Z)^(-1/2); its lower singular values are sigma(Z)/sqrt(1+sigma(Z)^2). Consequently ||(I-Pt0)P||op<=s.

## From subspace operator distance to all axes, without sqrt(n)

Put coordinates in the candidate frame and let R be the true orthogonal mixing matrix. For each candidate coordinate unit e_i, the matrix E_i=e_i e_i^T has norm1, and E_i-I/n belongs to Dc0 with norm sqrt(1-1/n). The full true diagonal projector is Pt=Pt0+|I/sqrt(n)><I/sqrt(n)|. Hence

    1-sum_j R_ji^4
      =||(I-Pt)E_i||F^2
      =||(I-Pt0)(E_i-I/n)||F^2
      <=(1-1/n) eta^2/(1+eta^2)=r_axis.          (5)

For w_j=R_ji^2, sum_j w_j=1 and sum_j w_j^2<=max_j w_j, so each candidate axis has some true axis with squared sine<=r_axis. If r_axis<1/2, that entry has w_j>1/2. No two candidate axes can choose the same true axis, because each row of R has sum of squares1. Thus the choices form a unique signed/permuted matching, with every matched projective squared sine bounded by r_axis. This is an all-axis maximum bound, not the sum of deficits; no sqrt(n) error factor appears.

If r_axis>=1/2, retain an ambiguity set; a projective matching is not certified. Signs remain intrinsically unobservable from the even moment model. Ordinary Euclidean axes in observed coordinates additionally pay covariance/metric transport and conditioning, exactly as in cycle13.

## Estimated metric: why trace removal can matter

Let W be a fixed candidate whitener and C0=W^T Cov(X)W satisfy (1-zeta)I<=C0<=(1+zeta)I. Ideal true whitening uses C0^-1/2. In candidate coordinates, write U for the corresponding orthogonal latent mixing. The population fourth-moment operator is the congruence by Sym^2(C0^1/2) of

    2c I +sum_i beta_i |u_i u_i^T><u_i u_i^T|
       +c |I><I|.

After compressing to H0, the last term is c|P0(C0)><P0(C0)| and has norm at most c n zeta^2. Thus the large isotropic rank-one term causes **second-order** n zeta^2 leakage, not an uncharged first-order n zeta error. The remaining congruence varies by at most (2zeta+zeta^2)(2c+beta_max). This is a mathematical transport bound; no observed upper bound on c,beta_max or stochastic operator error has yet been supplied.

To bound these nuisance scales from a posterior, put M0=||K0||op on H0. Then 2c<=M0 and beta_max<=n M0/(n-1): test B on P0(u_i u_i^T) to obtain beta_i(1-1/n)<=||B||op<=M0. Consequently

    ||P0 K_candidate P0-K0||op <=k(zeta,n)M0,
    k=(2zeta+zeta^2)[1+n/(n-1)]+n zeta^2/2.     (6)

If an independently valid observed fourth-moment operator error epsilon4 is available and k<1, then

    M0<=(||Khat||op+epsilon4)/(1-k),
    e<=epsilon4+k(||Khat||op+epsilon4)/(1-k).     (7)

The model scalar conventions in (1) imply c=M0-related lambda0/2; this is why the final n zeta^2/2 in (6) is correct. All metric and mean-estimation costs remain charged. Eq(7) does not permit concentration of adaptively whitened rows as if they were iid. A possible legal route is a fixed-size first sample for mean/whitener, an independent second sample for traceless quadratic-feature operators, conditional confidence then joint coverage.
