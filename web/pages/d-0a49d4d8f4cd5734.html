PROJECTED FERMION AUDIT — 7 October 2026
Status: source-conditioned corollaries with explicit proofs of the transfers. The Chen–Liu source proof was not re-audited here. No implementation of its FPRAS or quantum compiler was executed. Exact fixtures are scoped diagnostics only. Priority remains unverified.

DECISIVE FINDING
The proposed norm/sampling/observable consequence survives, and the cleanest input class is broader: an arbitrary supplied number-conserving Slater determinant, including spin mixing, projected to one fermion per site. Conditional counting also gives randomized polynomial-size QUANTUM preparation circuits for the normalized projected state, without inverse projection-success dependence. It would be incorrect to assert that coherent preparation cannot follow. Purely classical full-state reconstruction and generic dense-observable simulation do not follow.

PRECISE INTERFACE
There are n sites and two site-ordered modes per site. Supply A in (Q+iQ)^(2n by n), full column rank, with total encoding length L. The unprojected state is the exterior product of A's n column orbitals. Its amplitude on the configuration selecting one mode per site is
  a(sigma)=det A_T(sigma),
with rows/modes in increasing site order. Set
  Z=sum_sigma |a(sigma)|^2.
Exact matroid intersection decides Z=0 and, when positive, finds a nonzero configuration. A normalized projected state exists only when Z>0. All stated algorithms either report Z=0 or operate on this nonzero case.

Conditioned on Chen–Liu arXiv:2610.06724v1 Theorem 1.6 and its bit-complexity Remark A.4:
(1) Z has an FPRAS, with confidence amplification logarithmic in inverse failure probability.
(2) The computational-basis occupation law p(sigma)=|a(sigma)|^2/Z has a polynomial-time TV sampler of any inverse-polynomial accuracy.
(3) Given a polynomial-time row-enumeration routine for a polynomial-row-sparse Hermitian O, rational complex entries of polynomial bit length, and a supplied numerical bound ||O||<=M=poly(L,n), its expectation has a polynomial-time additive-epsilon randomized estimator. Sample count is O(M^2 epsilon^-2 log(delta^-1)); sampler cost also depends polynomially on M/epsilon.
(4) A uniform randomized classical compiler outputs a polynomial-size quantum circuit which prepares the normalized projected pure state to trace distance epsilon with probability >=1-delta over the compiler's classical seeds. Gate count/compilation time are polynomial in L,n,epsilon^-1,log(delta^-1), independent of inverse projection probability. This assumes ordinary reversible arithmetic and quantum gate synthesis, not a classical decoder for arbitrary measurement results. Good seeds are not certified by the argument.

DETERMINANT / PARTITION REDUCTION
On N=2n variables, define
  f_A(x)=det(A* D_x A)=sum_(|T|=n) |det A_T|^2 x^T,
  g(x)=product_(sites i) (x_(i,up)+x_(i,down)).
Cauchy–Binet gives the expansion. For positive x, A*D_x A is positive definite, so log f_A is concave by concavity of log determinant on Hermitian positive-definite matrices. Also log g is a sum of concave logs of positive linear forms. The all-squarefree coefficient [x_all N] f_A g equals Z: the second factor chooses exactly the complementary mode at each site. Its coefficients are 0 or 1. This is the physics specialization of Chen–Liu Appendix C.13(1), determinant weights constrained by a matroid, not an independent new counting theorem.

Feasibility: nonzero minors of A define its represented rank-n row matroid. One-per-site sets define a rank-n partition matroid. A nonzero projected configuration is exactly a common base. Standard deterministic matroid intersection with exact rational complex rank oracles supplies the feasibility decision/witness. Full column rank alone is insufficient.

Separate spin-sector input in the assignment is recovered by block orbitals. If up orbitals are created first and down orbitals second, while the physical spin basis is site ordered, then for S={i_1<...<i_r}, using 1-based sites,
  a(S)=(-1)^(sum_(k=1)^r(i_k-k)) det U_S det V_(S^c).
The exponent counts down-spin creators that must cross later up-spin creators. Norm calculations erase this sign; off-diagonal O must use the same basis convention. With general A already in site mode order, no separate sign convention is needed.

PINNING / SELF-REDUCTION PROOF
For any prefix of sites whose spins are fixed, let F be the forced occupied modes, delete their opposite modes, and let R be the modes on the remaining m sites. The residual weight is |det A_(F union T)|^2 for |T|=m. If the forced rows are dependent, the prefix has zero mass. Otherwise choose an invertible rational complex column transform C such that A_F C=[I 0]. Writing B for the last m columns of the transformed residual rows gives
  |det A_(F union T)|^2=|det C|^-2 |det B_T|^2.
Thus its generating polynomial is a positive constant times det(B*D_x B), again log-concave whenever a completion exists. The other polynomial is still the product of the m remaining site pair sums. Determinant/rank queries and an initial basis are acquired by exact linear algebra; feasibility is again matroid intersection. Pinning introduces no rare-event rejection.

At a positive prefix h, approximate its two child masses Z_0(h),Z_1(h) to relative error gamma with per-call failure <=beta. Exact infeasible children are set to zero. Normalize the two nonnegative estimates to define q(1|h); if a bad call yields no usable value, choose any exactly feasible child. At a good node, |q(1|h)-p(1|h)|<=2gamma for gamma<=1/2. Coupling one path at a time, the final sampler TV is at most n times the per-node error/failure budget, plus rational coin-rounding errors. Take gamma,beta,rounding error O(tau/n), amplify each FPRAS, and use fresh randomness per sample. This obtains TV<=tau in polynomial worst-case time. Unsupported sampler outputs can alternatively be assigned local-estimator value zero, although the exact-feasibility construction prevents them.

FINITE-BIT COST
Take Q to be the product of all denominators in the real and imaginary entries of A. Since L is the TOTAL input length, Q<=2^L. W=QA has Gaussian-integer entries of modulus at most H=2^(2L+1). A nonzero n by n Gaussian-integer determinant has modulus >=1, while |det W_T|<=n! H^n. Consequently the positive-coefficient range obeys
  log_2 R <=2n(2L+1+log_2 n).
All pinned coefficients are a subset of these same original minor squares, so the same range bound works throughout self-reduction. Original-minor value oracles return rationals of polynomial encoding length; fraction-free determinant/rank routines have polynomial bit cost. No inverse-smallest-amplitude magnitude is charged: large ratios require polynomially many bits, not their numerical magnitude. Chen–Liu Remark A.4 supplies bounded random-bit/bit-runtime handling for the imported FPRAS. Its high polynomial exponent remains an important practical limitation.

The normalized unprojected Slater norm is det(A*A), so the physical success probability is Z/det(A*A). For separate U,V, it is Z/[det(U*U)det(V*V)]. This probability may be exponentially small, yet the range/bit and sampler budgets above remain polynomial in the supplied encoding.

OBSERVABLE ESTIMATOR WITH CLIPPING
Let a be the amplitude vector in the full spin basis, with zeros outside the relevant support. For a(S)!=0, define
  E_loc(S)=(Oa)_S/a(S)=sum_T O_ST a(T)/a(S).
Then E_p E_loc=<O> and
  E_p |E_loc|^2=(1/Z) sum_(a(S)!=0) |(Oa)_S|^2
                  <=||Oa||^2/Z<=M^2.
The first inequality can be strict at nodes. Hermiticity makes <O> real. Set X=Re E_loc and h=clip(X,-K,K), assigning h=0 at nodes. When 0<epsilon<M, use
  K=4M^2/epsilon,  tau=epsilon^2/(32M^2).
(For epsilon>=M, output zero.) If TV(q,p)<=tau, then
  |E_q h-<O>| <= M^2/K+2K tau <=epsilon/2,
  E_q h^2 <= M^2+2K^2 tau <=2M^2.
For N=ceil(32M^2/epsilon^2) independent q samples, Chebyshev bounds the probability the block mean differs from E_q h by >=epsilon/2 by <=1/4. A median of O(log(delta^-1)) independent block means supplies confidence 1-delta. Each h is computed with exact rational determinant ratios and row enumeration, so even exponentially large local-estimator magnitudes introduce only polynomial bit length. Approximate arithmetic can also be budgeted below the additive target. This is stronger than using only Hoeffding with bound K, which would give an unnecessary epsilon^-4 sample count.

QUANTUM PREPARATION: WEIGHTED-PREFIX ERROR, NOT AN EXPONENTIAL UNION BOUND
Fix two independent FPRAS random seeds at each depth, shared by all prefixes at that depth. Every seed-fixed subroutine is now a deterministic polynomial-time function of h. This defines a complete conditional probability tree q_seed, even though the tree is never explicitly enumerated. For p_j(h), the ideal marginal mass of a depth-j prefix, let
  B_seed=sum_(j=0)^(n-1) sum_h p_j(h) 1[at least one child estimate at h is bad].
No independence across h is needed. The FPRAS guarantee holds for each specified input, hence
  E_seed B_seed <=2n beta.
Sequential maximal coupling, or telescoping Markov kernels, gives exactly
  TV(p,q_seed) <=sum_j sum_h p_j(h)|p(1|h)-q_seed(1|h)|
               <=2n gamma+B_seed.
Take gamma=epsilon^2/(32n), beta=delta epsilon^2/(32n). Markov gives B_seed<=epsilon^2/16 with probability >=1-delta; therefore TV(p,q_seed)<=epsilon^2/8. Failure amplification costs only log(n/(delta epsilon^2)). Exact infeasibility detection ensures q_seed has the correct support even at bad prefixes.

Reversibly compute q_seed(1|h) from the prefix, rotate the next qubit to sqrt(1-q)|0>+sqrt(q)|1>, and uncompute its work registers. Repeating prepares sum_S sqrt(q_seed(S))|S> without tabulating the exponentially many prefixes. Compute det A_T and apply its unit-modulus phase a(S)/|a(S)|, then uncompute. Let |phi_seed> denote the ideal resulting state and |psi> the target. Their overlap is the real nonnegative quantity
  <psi|phi_seed>=sum_S sqrt(p(S)q_seed(S))
                >=sum_S min(p(S),q_seed(S))=1-TV(p,q_seed).
The pure-state trace distance (half the trace norm) thus obeys
  D(psi,phi_seed)=sqrt(1-|<psi|phi_seed>|^2)
                 <=sqrt(2 TV(p,q_seed))<=epsilon/2.
This fully certifies TV-to-Hellinger-to-trace conversion despite arbitrarily rare bad prefixes. It also makes explicit why no all-prefix union bound is necessary.

Seed-fixed FPRAS algorithms have a polynomial worst-case runtime/randomness bound, by the imported bit implementation. Their reversible circuits have polynomial size. Conditional probabilities have polynomial bit length. Square-root branch rotations are synthesized with total operator error <=epsilon/4; the deterministic phase routine has total error <=epsilon/4. Nonzero determinant numerators are Gaussian integers with modulus >=1 and polynomial bit length, so phase normalization needs polynomial bit precision even when the original amplitude is tiny. Triangle inequality completes trace distance <=epsilon. This is a transfer of the standard conditional-mass state-preparation idea, not a novel generic state-preparation primitive.

PHYSICS CONSEQUENCES AND EXACT LIMITS
All fixed-locality spin observables and local Hamiltonians satisfy the sparse-row interface. On two copies, Swap_A has one nonzero entry per row and norm one for ANY subsystem A, while the product-state occupation law is sampled by two independent samplers. The same estimator therefore approximates Tr(rho_A^2) ADDITIVELY in polynomial time. Fixed higher-copy permutation observables are likewise accessible. Renyi entropy -log purity to additive accuracy generally needs relative purity accuracy and can retain inverse-purity cost; no general efficient entanglement-spectrum or dense-observable algorithm follows.

The norm/sampling/preparation claims can also be extended to a single number-conserving Slater determinant projected onto any matroid base family for which polynomial-time independence routines are supplied, using its dual-base polynomial as the second factor. This includes doped no-double-occupancy Slater states via a truncated partition matroid. Coherent compilation requires a concrete reversible implementation of the independence routine; an ordinary classical black-box oracle alone is not automatically a coherent oracle. The present clean theorem uses the explicit single-occupancy partition constraint. Arbitrary paired BCS/Pfaffian inputs or arbitrary Jastrow factors have not been covered by these arguments.

An exact limitation on classical tensor-network reconstruction: pair site j with site m+j for n=2m, and take U=V with column j equal to one on those two sites and zero elsewhere. The projected state is a product of m singlets across the first-m / last-m site cut, up to a constant phase. Its Schmidt coefficients are all 2^(-m/2). A matrix product state in this site order with bond dimension D has squared overlap at most D/2^m. Trace distance <=epsilon thus requires D>=(1-epsilon^2)2^m. Efficient circuit preparation and amplitude-query access are compatible with exponentially large required MPS bond dimension; no stronger generic impossibility of succinct classical descriptions is claimed, since A itself already supplies one.

COUNTEREXAMPLES / FAILURE MODES (all rational)
1. n=2,r=1,U=V=(1,0)^T: both full column rank, but Z=0. Nonzero projection must be acquired, not assumed from sector ranks.
2. U=(eta,1)^T,V=(1,1)^T gives a=(eta,-1), p=(eta^2,1)/(1+eta^2). O=-X has norm one, local values (1/eta,eta), exact second moment one. Moving TV mass tau from the second state to the first changes the UNCLIPPED estimator mean by tau(1/eta-eta), unbounded as eta->0. At eta=1/1000,tau=1/100 it changes the mean by 9.99999, although the true expectation is about 0.002. TV alone is insufficient.
3. U=(1,1)^T with V=(1,1)^T versus V=(1,-1)^T gives orthogonal projected states (1,-1) and (-1,-1), while both occupation laws are (1/2,1/2). A sampling-only decoder loses phase. Explicit A supplies the missing phase oracle, which is why the quantum preparation consequence is stronger than sampling alone.
4. n=2m,r=m,U=V with column j equal to (1,1) on the j-th disjoint pair and zero elsewhere: Z=2^m, unprojected norm=4^m, projection success=2^-m. This integer-input family exposes inverse-success rejection cost while satisfying all polynomial-height hypotheses.
