# Nonequilibrium biomolecular automata: Rule 166 is a slow one-sided switch, not robust memory

## Opportunity and verdict

The highest-ceiling source-backed opportunity in this lane is programmable
protein-scale memory or finite-state control: local post-translational state
would select fuel-driven transitions, and changing enzyme sets would encode
inputs. Kocka, Husain & Agudo-Canalejo’s 2026 *PRX Life* paper gives a
thermodynamically consistent model for this, but no molecular implementation
of the required local-triplet enzyme rule is known. The authors identify
concrete realization as future work. Existing switchable protein assemblies
and phosphorylation systems supply possible components, not a molecular
automaton.

I attacked the paper’s most striking kinetic lead, Rule 166’s exponential
relaxation. The exact reduction shows that it is a **one-sided, exponentially
slow error avalanche**, not a symmetric self-correcting memory. After one 1
defect is seeded in an all-zero ring, the probability of eventual wrong-majority
readout is one, while the mean delay is \((2^M-M-1)/k_{\rm cat}\),
\(M=(N+1)/2\). In the opposite direction, a single zero in an all-one ring
has probability \(1/[2(1-2^{-M})]\), approaching one half, of causing a
wrong-majority readout before repair. Its long source-reported relaxation
therefore cannot be read as a robust two-bit memory guarantee.

Finite chemical drive makes the asymmetry sharper. Rule 166’s all-zero state
has no enzyme-mediated exit even at finite affinity, but all-one has exact
per-visit exit rate \(N(r+k_{\rm spo})\), where
\(r=k_{\rm cat}/(1+e^A)\). Without spontaneous leak, an exponential-in-\(N\)
all-one residence time requires affinity \(A\) to grow linearly with \(N\).
At any finite affinity with zero spontaneous leak, all nonzero configurations
communicate; the source’s two-trap picture is singular in the infinite-drive
limit.

Rule 232 is the strongest memory control in this source. Its finite-affinity
one-error clean-correction race has the exact probability
\[
\frac{q+k_{\rm spo}}{q+(N-3)r+Nk_{\rm spo}},\qquad
q=k_{\rm cat}\frac{e^A}{1+e^A},\quad r=k_{\rm cat}\frac{1}{1+e^A}.
\]
This gives an explicit drive/leak threshold for near-certain *next-jump*
repair, but not a complete memory-lifetime theorem. The broad research gate
survives as a theoretical design target; this audit does not establish a new
biomolecular capability, sequence design, or experimental performance.

## Finite-state resource comparison

I compared Rule 166 with Rule 232 using the source reaction channels, the same
odd-ring size \(N=7\), the same majority decoder, the same spontaneous rate
\(k_{\rm spo}=10^{-3}\) in baseline \(k_{\rm cat}=1\) units, and matched total
catalytic capacity \(\sum_p k_p=2\). Rates use \(e^A=100\), or
\(A=\log 100\): per-species capacity is \(1/2\) for Rule 166’s four enzymes
and 1 for Rule 232’s two.
The killed generators and fuel-reward systems were solved exactly over
\(\mathbb{Q}\); the logarithmic work conversion is rounded.

| Rule | Mean time to first wrong majority | Gross fuel hydrolyses | Reverse fuel returns | Net fuel consumed | Reaction-channel chemical work in \(k_BT\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 166 | 164.9001 | 17.4402 | 0.2470 | 17.1932 | 79.1776 |
| 232 | 1955.2539 | 140.4379 | 129.2242 | 11.2136 | 51.6407 |

This rational-rate fixture is a reproducible channel-accounting check, not a
biological parameter regime or evidence of a general Rule 232 advantage. Under
it, Rule 232 gives about 11.9 times the majority-readout lifetime and lower net
chemical work from the fuel reactions, despite more gross turnover and
extensive reverse cycling. This is conditional on the paper’s
equal-subunit-energy/common-fuel kinetics, a clamped reservoir, symmetric
spontaneous flips, and matched \(\sum k_p\); it is not calibrated to a real
complex. The model’s prepared-state information free energy, enzyme synthesis
and assembly, ATP depletion, cross-reactivity, and protein-implementation
barriers are not in this number. `exact_rate_audit.py` records every channel and
the exact rational systems, rather than inferring chemical work from the net
state graph.

## Evidence and limits

The proof in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) derives all-N run-length bounds. The
finite-state checker [rule166_reconstruction.py](rule166_reconstruction.py)
matches its two Rule 166 formulae for \(N=3,5,7,9,11\); the exact rate audit
[exact_rate_audit.py](exact_rate_audit.py) reproduces the resource comparison
with zero algebraic residual. The separate exploratory code
[molecular_automata_design.py](molecular_automata_design.py) uses binary64 for
general parameter sweeps and labels its linear residuals as diagnostics.

The source’s \(\tau_{10}\) metric is computed from exact-state occupancy
\(P(X_t=0^N)=0.9\), and its error analysis uses attractor splitting. Neither is
the majority-decoded first-passage quantity computed here. I keep these as
different observables.

The source’s Rule 166 exponential relaxation and Rule 232 single-error
correction are already reported findings. The run-length reduction and
finite-affinity/resource diagnostic are internal reconstructions, not
priority-cleared novelty. An experimental system would need measured rates for
each local context, measured enzyme cross-talk and spontaneous leakage, finite
fuel accounting, and a majority-readout test against Rule 232 at matched enzyme
capacity. Until that interface is demonstrated, the realization claim remains
**UNKNOWN**.
