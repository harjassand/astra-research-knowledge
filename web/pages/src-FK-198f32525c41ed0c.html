# Global quartic compatibility: what recombines and what remains

## Status

No complete global positivity proof or compatible negative gap was obtained. The exact results below identify a safe recombination operation and a concrete obstruction to extending it to correlated profiles. The strongest actionable global route is the finite PSD moment-completion criterion already available for the nine-coefficient left-​SO(2)-invariant quartic class. Its missing premise is precisely that gradient compatibility supplies the completion for every affine comparison and every compatible profile.

The supplied moving theorem proves an all-background positive gap only in its stated relative near-line regime and under its transverse spectral hypotheses. It does not cover a general internally resonant profile with substantial transverse fourth moment. The singular averaged-Hessian face result, sharp heat certificates, contact-shear identity, and fixed-contact cubic bounds remain within their recorded scopes; none by itself fills the positive-definite, large-transverse sector.

## A law-mixture operation that is valid

For a probability law \(\mu\) with finite fourth moment, set

\[
 g_\mu(B)=\int [F(B+X)-F(B)]\,d\mu(X),\qquad
 \Delta_\mu=\inf_B g_\mu(B).
\]

If \(\mu=\sum_j\theta_j\mu_j\) is a convex mixture of laws, then \(g_\mu(B)=\sum_j\theta_jg_{\mu_j}(B)\). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\Delta_\mu\geq\sum_j\theta_j\Delta_{\mu_j}.}
\]

This follows by taking the infimum of the sum: \(\inf_B\sum_j\theta_jg_j(B)\geq\sum_j\theta_j\inf_Bg_j(B)\). It does not require independence between the random variables used to define the component laws. Thus a genuine probability mixture of covered centered, determinant-balanced compatible laws with nonnegative gaps stays nonnegative, with the same optimized affine background.

This operation does not apply to pointwise superposition \(X=X_1+X_2\). The law of a sum is generally not the convex mixture of the laws of its summands; quartic polarization creates mixed terms. Nor does conditioning a gradient on an arbitrary spatial region produce a centered determinant-balanced gradient law: subtracting the local mean changes the boundary data, and the determinant null-Lagrangian identity is tied to the full affine trace. A general decomposition into covered pieces therefore needs a compatibility-preserving construction and a uniform error bound in \(B\), not just a measure-theoretic partition.

The same infimum argument proves superadditivity for exact disjoint-support profile gluing: if zero-boundary compatible profiles have disjoint supports, their background-gap functions add, so the optimized gap of their sum is at least the sum of the individual optimized gaps. This rules out a negative gap created solely by gluing separately nonnegative disjoint profiles through one escaping affine background. It says nothing about a single resonant profile or about decomposing an arbitrary gradient into disjoint profiles.

## Null-Lagrangian gauge: exact limit

For a zero-boundary or periodic gradient perturbation \(X=D\varphi\), \(\mathbb E X=0\) and \(\mathbb E\det X=0\). For every constant background \(B\),

\[
 \det(B+X)-\det B=\operatorname{cof}B:X+\det X,
\]

so its mean is zero. Adding any linear combination of the ordinary null Lagrangians \(1\), \(A_{ij}\), and \(\det A\) to \(F\) leaves \(g_\mu(B)\), and hence \(\Delta_\mu\), unchanged for every \(B\). A constant determinant gauge cannot repair a negative profile or turn a correlated decomposition into a positive one.

The pointwise indefinite-Finsler representation \(D^2F(A)=P_A+\lambda_AJ\) does not supply such a gauge: \(\lambda_A\) generally varies with \(A\). Multiplying a null Lagrangian by a state-dependent coefficient is not a null Lagrangian; the derivative of that coefficient contributes to the spatial integral. A variable gauge must be shown to arise from an exact/closed differential form. The closed heat certificates for the recorded sharp slices do this for those slices only; their existence is not a universal selection theorem.

## A global compatibility certificate, and its exact missing lemma

For the nine-dimensional left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant quartic class, the N794 source gives an exact finite-dimensional duality statement. Write the nine basis polynomials as \(f_1,\ldots,f_9\), the acoustic Gram matrix as

\[
 Q(p,K)=\sum_{i=1}^9p_iB_i+\sum_{j=1}^6k_jC_j,
\]

where \(K\) is the skew Gram block. For a law \(\mu\) and comparison matrix \(A_0\), define \(m_i=\mathbb E f_i(A)-f_i(A_0)\). Then Jensen's inequality for **every** rank-one-convex quartic in this symmetry class is equivalent to existence of \(Y\succeq0\) such that

\[
 \langle B_i,Y\rangle=m_i\quad(1\leq i\leq9),\qquad
 \langle C_j,Y\rangle=0\quad(1\leq j\leq6).
\]

For a compatible field \(A=A_0+D\varphi\), the unresolved global lemma is that the gradient/curl constraints force this PSD completion for every \(A_0\) and every periodic or zero-boundary \(W^{1,4}\) field. If it fails for one compatible field and one \(A_0\), a separating dual vector gives an exact quartic and a genuine compatible negative Jensen gap. This criterion combines the whole correlated profile at the moment level and does not presume positivity of separately selected pieces. It is limited to the left-\(SO(2)\) cone; the moving theorem handles the full 35-coefficient space only in its stated local sector.

The criterion sharpens the global task into a concrete alternative: prove compatibility-to-completion for all affine comparisons, or find an exact compatible field whose moment vector has no completion. The incoming near-line theorem supplies this positivity locally in a different representation, but neither that local result nor the singular-face classification proves the completion in the positive-definite, large-transverse sector.

## Exact compatible resonance check

I independently implemented exact Laurent-coefficient integration for the supplied tangent quartic

\[
 G=3(u+v-2q)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+q^2-\frac52qu,
 \quad u=a^2+c^2,\ v=b^2+d^2,\ w=ab+cd,\ q=ad-bc,
\]

on

\[
 \psi=\cos(x+y)+\cos(x+2y)+\cos(2x+3y),\qquad
 \varphi=(\tfrac23\psi_y,\psi_x),\qquad X=D\varphi.
\]

The three frequencies satisfy \((1,1)+(1,2)=(2,3)\), so this is a smooth periodic resonant gradient, not a probability mixture of three one-wave laws. The covariance has exact rank three. Exact torus integration gives \(H=\mathbb E D^2G(X)\succ0\) (its four leading principal minors are positive rational numbers),

\[
 \mathbb E DG(X)[I]=\frac{85}{18}\ne0,
 \qquad
 \Delta_G(X)=\frac{6488961661323546029}{309344140144800}>0.
\]

For the named contact line \(R=e_{12}\), the relative fourth-moment ratio is
\[
 \frac{\|X-(X:R)R\|_4^4}{\|X:R\|_4^4}=\frac{27833}{9440},
\]
so this example has substantial transverse fourth moment at that contact. It is outside the supplied moving theorem's spectral scope for this \(G\): the cited contact has a rank-two transverse Finsler remainder, whereas that theorem assumes a simple transverse contact. The exact positive gap is only a finite profile check. It neither proves the moment completion for other fields nor establishes full quasiconvexity of \(G\).

The independent check is in [exact_resonant_profile.py](exact_resonant_profile.py), with the complete rational moments and affine minimizer in [exact_resonant_profile.json](exact_resonant_profile.json). It used Python 3.14.3 and SymPy 1.14.0. The computation verifies this profile's Laurent moments and optimized gap; rank-one convexity of \(G\) is the separate source certificate, not a consequence of this script.

## Lamination claim found in primary literature

Pablo Pedregal's [arXiv:1905.06571v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06571v5), dated 18 June 2026, claims that every two-dimensional, two-component homogeneous gradient Young measure is a laminate, which would give a global profile operation far stronger than the one needed here. I read the arXiv HTML from the introduction through the proof of Proposition 7.1 and conclusion. The decisive step is the universal feasibility assertion in Section 7: a fixed set of scalar decomposition directions must realize an arbitrary second-component gradient by weights that also make each final split a convex segment. The accessed proof uses a dimension count for the constrained linear map and a neighborhood argument around one feasible datum. It does not, in those paragraphs, establish independence of all imposed constraints or that every target gradient has weights satisfying the required segment inequalities. Dimension equality and full linear span alone do not imply that global positive-feasibility conclusion. I did not independently refute Proposition 7.1; this is a substantive proof obligation, so the preprint is recorded as a potentially relevant claim rather than used as a theorem.

This preprint's stated result would settle much more than quartic compatibility. I accessed only its arXiv preprint here; publication and independent review status were not checked. The separate Bruno–Pasqualotto primary preprint [arXiv:2608.06367v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06367) proves a heat-flow result for the Dacorogna–Marcellini energy and reports a corollary for quartics invariant under both left and right rotations. That symmetry class is narrower than the full left-\(SO(2)\) cone and does not supply the missing compatibility lemma above. N797's exact tensor certificate extends its own sharp slice while explicitly leaving the full cone open.

## Precise remaining step

For the left-\(SO(2)\) class, prove or refute:

> For every periodic or zero-boundary \(\varphi\in W^{1,4}(\Omega;\mathbb R^2)\) and every constant \(A_0\), the nine moments \(m_i=\fint_\Omega f_i(A_0+D\varphi)-f_i(A_0)\) admit a PSD completion \(Y\) satisfying the 15 linear equations above.

This is the exact whole-profile lemma that would combine covered and internally resonant sectors without requiring each correlated component to have nonnegative gap. For the full 35-coefficient class, an analogous certificate or a different global coercive residual is still needed. A profile decomposition route must preserve affine boundary data, determinant balance, and the entire \(B\)-dependent gap with an error controlled by the total fourth moment. No such decomposition was proved here. The contact-specific reduced-Schur estimate from N806 remains useful near a contact but is insufficient for large transverse mass.

## Scope and claim boundary

The primary N794/N797/N800/N803/N806 records are internal source material at the pinned capital revision. This report establishes an elementary concavity identity, an exact null-Lagrangian invariance, the explicit finite-moment completion obligation, and one independent exact compatible profile computation. It does not prove the completion lemma, a global residual inequality, or a negative compatible gap. External mathematical correctness, novelty, and the universal lamination claim remain unverified.
