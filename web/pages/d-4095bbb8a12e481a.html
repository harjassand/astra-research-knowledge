# Cycle 2: phase retrieval with an all-outcomes photon budget

## Finding

A phase-retrieval model can be realized as a one-photon POVM followed by exactly $N$ photon outcomes. The correct shot-noise geometry is the magnitude/Hellinger map, not unweighted Euclidean error in raw intensities. A random Parseval frame gives a conditional candidate with $m=O(d)$ output channels, a constant state-vector magnitude lower modulus with high probability, and a uniform expected squared phase-error bound of order $m/N$.

Bruß–Macchiavello's optimal state-estimation theorem supplies the matching $\Omega(d/N)$ Haar-average lower bound for every estimator, even allowing collective measurements. Therefore $N=\Theta(d/\varepsilon^2)$ is supported in order for expected squared phase-distance error when $m=\Theta(d)$ and the modulus stays bounded below. The upper bound uses a global nonconvex fit; a finite net makes the estimator finite but exponential in $d$. The frame theorem hides universal constants in its sample-size and failure bounds. This is a mathematical count law, not a hardware-level advantage.

## Count model and exact affinity

Let $x\in\mathbb C^d$ be a normalized unknown pure mode. Let $B\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}$ have rows $b_j^*$ and satisfy $B^*B=I_d$. Then
\[
E_j=b_jb_j^*,\qquad j=1,\ldots,m
\]
is a complete rank-one POVM, and one photon produces outcome $j$ with probability
\[
p_j(x)=\langle x,E_jx\rangle=|b_j^*x|^2,\qquad \sum_jp_j(x)=1.
\]

With exactly $N$ copies measured by this same POVM, the full counts obey $K\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(N,p(x))$ and $\sum_jK_j=N$. With a Poissonian source of mean $N$, the output counts are independent $Y_j\sim\mathrm{Pois}(Np_j(x))$ and their total is $\mathrm{Pois}(N)$. Thus $N$ is the total photon budget, not a per-channel budget. Conditioning the Poisson model on total count $N$ gives the fixed-$N$ multinomial model.

Let $A(p,q)=\sum_j\sqrt{p_jq_j}$ be the one-photon Bhattacharyya coefficient. Summing the Poisson masses gives the exact product affinity
\[
A(P_x^{\rm Pois},P_y^{\rm Pois})
=\exp\!\left[-\frac N2\sum_j(\sqrt{p_j(x)}-\sqrt{p_j(y)})^2\right]
=\exp\!\left[-\frac N2\bigl\||Bx|-|By|\bigr\|_2^2\right].
\]
For fixed $N$, the multinomial affinity is $A(p(x),p(y))^N$ and is at most the same exponential, since
\[
A(p(x),p(y))=1-\tfrac12\bigl\||Bx|-|By|\bigr\|_2^2
\le \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12\bigl\||Bx|-|By|\bigr\|_2^2\right].
\]

Define the phase-invariant state-vector metric
\[
d_\phi(x,y)=\min_{|\omega|=1}\|x-\omega y\|_2.
\]
Any global lower modulus
\[
\||Bx|-|By|\|_2\ge \ell\,d_\phi(x,y)
\]
therefore gives the exact pairwise bound
\[
A(P_x^{(N)},P_y^{(N)})
\le \exp\!\left[-\frac{N\ell^2}{2}d_\phi(x,y)^2\right].
\]

For simple hypotheses $x_0$ versus $x_1$, the strongest equally informed fixed-frame test is the full Neyman–Pearson likelihood ratio. Under fixed $N$ it is
\[
\log\frac{P_{x_1}(K)}{P_{x_0}(K)}
=\sum_jK_j\log\frac{p_j(x_1)}{p_j(x_0)},
\]
with support conventions and randomization at a discrete boundary. Under independent Poisson counts it is
\[
\sum_jY_j\log\frac{p_j(x_1)}{p_j(x_0)}
-N\sum_j\bigl(p_j(x_1)-p_j(x_0)\bigr),
\]
whose final term vanishes for normalized states. A rare prior changes the likelihood threshold to $(1-\pi)L_{FP}/(\pi L_{FN})$; it does not change the photon accounting.

With known additive dark-count means $c_j\ge0$, independent counts have means $\lambda_j(x)=Np_j(x)+c_j$ and exact affinity
\[
A(P_x,P_y)=\exp\!\left[-\frac12\sum_j
\left(\sqrt{Np_j(x)+c_j}-\sqrt{Np_j(y)+c_j}\right)^2\right].
\]
The likelihood must use the full means. Large background can reduce the modulus, so the zero-background guarantee below does not automatically persist. Background calibration consumes a separate resource. If the nuisance families overlap in the full outcome law, uniform testing or estimation is impossible.

All outcomes belong in the record: output ports, setting labels, no-click/loss events, and herald/failure flags. For multiple settings with fixed allocations $N_s$, charge $\sum_sN_s=N$ and multiply their affinities. Random setting selection can be encoded as a single POVM outcome that includes the setting label. Uniform loss adds a no-click effect and scales information by efficiency; it does not make detected photons equivalent to incident photons.

## Random Parseval frame and uniform estimator

Xia, Xu, and Xu study the magnitude map for a standard Gaussian sensing matrix. Let $G\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}$ have independent standard complex-Gaussian entries, and define
\[
L_G=\inf_{x\not\sim y}\frac{\||Gx|-|Gy|\|_2}{d_\phi(x,y)},
\qquad
\beta_0^{\mathbb C}=\sqrt{\frac4{4-\pi}}\simeq2.159.
\]
Their Theorem 4.3 states that for $0<\delta<0.05$ and
$m\gtrsim\log(1/\delta)\delta^{-2}d$,
\[
\Pr\{L_G/\sqrt m\ge1/\beta_0^{\mathbb C}-\delta\}
\ge1-4e^{-c_2\delta^2m},
\]
where $c_2>0$ is universal. Their Lemma 4.2 states that for $0<\rho<1$ and $m\gtrsim\rho^{-2}d$,
\[
\Pr\{\lambda_{\max}(G^*G)\le m(1+\rho)\}
\ge1-2e^{-c_1\rho^2m},
\]
where $c_1>0$ is universal. Neither the constants $c_i$ nor the implicit constants in $\gtrsim$ are numerically specified.

Polar-normalize
\[
B=G(G^*G)^{-1/2}.
\]
Then $B^*B=I$ exactly, and the row effects $E_j=b_jb_j^*$ form a valid rank-one POVM. For all $x,y$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\||Bx|-|By|\|_2
&=\||G(G^*G)^{-1/2}x|-|G(G^*G)^{-1/2}y|\|_2\\
&\ge L_G\,d_\phi((G^*G)^{-1/2}x,(G^*G)^{-1/2}y)\\
&\ge \frac{L_G}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(G^*G)}}d_\phi(x,y).
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence, with probability at least
\[
1-4e^{-c_2\delta^2m}-2e^{-c_1\rho^2m},
\]
the POVM obeys the uniform modulus
\[
\boxed{\ell\ge\frac{1/\beta_0^{\mathbb C}-\delta}{\sqrt{1+\rho}}.}
\]
Fixing $\delta,\rho$ as constants gives $m=O(d)$, with hidden multipliers from the source theorem. It does not give a certified numerical cutoff for $m/d$ or failure exponents. The random frame must be calibrated. Abstract POVM implementability does not price the optical network, ancillas, source, or readout.

For a Poissonian source with mean photon budget $N$, use the rate estimate $\hat p_j=Y_j/N$ and choose
\[
\hat x\in\arg\min_{\|z\|_2=1}\|\sqrt{\hat p}-|Bz|\|_2.
\]
For any coordinate with $p_j(x)>0$, the Poisson variance gives
\[
\mathbb E_x(\sqrt{\hat p_j}-\sqrt{p_j(x)})^2
\le\frac{\mathbb E_x(\hat p_j-p_j(x))^2}{p_j(x)}
=\frac1N.
\]
Zero-probability coordinates contribute zero. Summing gives
\[
\mathbb E_x\|\sqrt{\hat p}-\sqrt{p(x)}\|_2^2\le\frac mN.
\]
The fitted residual is no larger than the true state's residual. A triangle inequality and the frame modulus prove
\[
\boxed{\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathbb E_xd_\phi(\hat x,x)^2
\le\frac{4m}{N\ell^2}.}
\]
This is a uniform expected-risk bound, not a tail bound. Markov gives probability at least $1-\zeta$ of
$d_\phi^2\le4m/(N\ell^2\zeta)$.

For exactly $N$ photons, replace $Y/N$ by $K/N$ in the estimator. The multinomial coordinate variance is $p_j(1-p_j)/N$, so the same argument sharpens the noise bound to $(m-1)/N$ and yields
\[
\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathbb E_xd_\phi(\hat x,x)^2\le\frac{4(m-1)}{N\ell^2}.
\]

Because $B^*B=I$, $z\mapsto|Bz|$ is 1-Lipschitz under $d_\phi$. Replacing the continuous minimization by an $\epsilon$-net gives a finite estimator. Its conservative bound is
\[
\sup_x\mathbb E_xd_\phi(\hat x,x)^2
\le\frac{8(m-1)/N+2\epsilon^2}{\ell^2}.
\]
The net has exponential size in $d$, so this construction does not establish efficient computation.

For $m=\kappa d$ and fixed positive $\ell$, expected squared phase error at most $\varepsilon^2$ is guaranteed if
\[
N\ge\frac{4(m-1)}{\ell^2\varepsilon^2}=O(d/\varepsilon^2).
\]

## Matching lower bound and equally informed controls

Bruß and Macchiavello prove that the optimal universal average fidelity for estimating an unknown pure $d$-dimensional state from exactly $N$ identical copies is
\[
F_{\rm opt}(N,d)=\frac{N+1}{N+d}.
\]
Their bound allows collective measurements. For Haar-average phase distance, put
$c=|\langle x,\hat x\rangle|$. Then
\[
d_\phi(x,\hat x)^2=2(1-c)\ge1-c^2,
\]
and every estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_x\mathbb E_xd_\phi(\hat x,x)^2
\ge\mathbb E_{x\sim{\rm Haar}}d_\phi(\hat x,x)^2
\ge1-F_{\rm opt}(N,d)
=\frac{d-1}{N+d}.
\]
For $N\gg d$, this is $\Omega(d/N)$. Achieving mean squared error at most $\varepsilon^2$ requires
$N\ge(d-1)/\varepsilon^2-d$. This is a strong photon-count lower bound because it allows arbitrary collective measurements. It assumes the physical input really is $N$ copies of one unknown pure $d$-mode state. Loss, background, nuisance, and hardware cost can only worsen or alter the model.

The upper and lower bounds match in order, $\Theta(d/N)$, for an ideal calibrated random Parseval POVM with $m=\Theta(d)$ and fixed frame modulus. They do not match in finite constants. The upper uses a conservative minimum-distance bound and hidden random-frame constants; the lower is a Haar-average quantum bound. This does not prove the minimum possible POVM outcome count for phase retrieval.

The full multinomial maximum-likelihood estimate
\[
\hat x_{\rm ML}\in\arg\max_{\|z\|=1}\sum_jK_j\log p_j(z)
\]
is the natural exact-model statistical baseline for the same frame and counts. With known background and independent Poisson observations, the exact log-likelihood is
\[
\sum_j\left[Y_j\log\bigl(Np_j(z)+c_j\bigr)-\bigl(Np_j(z)+c_j\bigr)\right]
\]
up to terms independent of $z$. The Hellinger estimator gives a transparent risk guarantee; neither estimator has a polynomial-time guarantee here. Raw-intensity least squares is another equally informed control but ignores the shot-noise weighting.

## Relation to raw-intensity $\ell_2$ conditioning

Peng–Han–Huang study the deterministic raw-intensity map
$\Psi_A(x)=(|a_j^*x|^2)_j$ with unnormalized output $\ell_2$ error and trace-norm state distance. Their complex universal condition-number lower bound is $\beta_{\Psi_A}^{\ell_2}\ge2$. The predecessor note [nonlinear inverse report](../nonlinear_inverse/FINAL_REPORT.md) internally reconstructs the equality case: $\beta=2$ exactly for weighted projective 2-designs, which require at least $d^2$ nonzero rows. This optimizes additive raw-intensity conditioning, not photon statistics.

For a projective 2-design with $m$ unit rows $u_j$, the POVM effects are
$E_j=(d/m)u_ju_j^*$, and $p_j(x)=(d/m)|u_j^*x|^2$. The predecessor's raw-intensity lower constant is $L=\sqrt{m/[2d(d+1)]}$ relative to
$d_*(x,y)=\|xx^*-yy^*\|_*$. Since every unit-row amplitude is at most one,
\[
\|\sqrt{p(x)}-\sqrt{p(y)}\|_2
=\sqrt{d/m}\,\||Ax|-|Ay|\|_2
\ge\frac{d_*(x,y)}{2\sqrt{2(d+1)}}.
\]
Thus design-POVM Poisson affinity is bounded by
$\exp[-Nd_*^2/(16(d+1))]$. This transfer is correct but loses a factor of order $\sqrt d$ in the magnitude modulus relative to the random Parseval state-vector bound. Exact $\beta=2$ design POVMs spend at least $d^2$ output channels. Random Parseval phase-retrieval frames can use $O(d)$ outcomes, though they need not be 2-designs or attain $\beta=2$.

The Poisson Fisher information in a smooth parameter direction $v$ is
\[
I_x(v)=N\sum_{j:p_j(x)>0}\frac{(\partial_vp_j(x))^2}{p_j(x)}
=4N\sum_{j:p_j(x)>0}(\partial_v\sqrt{p_j(x)})^2.
\]
This heteroscedastic weighting is absent from ordinary raw-intensity $\ell_2$ conditioning. At zero-probability bins the Fisher expression is nonregular, while the exact Hellinger affinity remains valid.

## Direct Poisson phase-retrieval prior art

Dirksen, Krahmer, Römer, and Salanevich, [*Spectral method for low-dose Poisson and Bernoulli phase retrieval*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13263), give a direct Poisson recovery theorem. Their model is real $x\in\mathbb R^n$, i.i.d. standard Gaussian $a_i$, and independent
$y_i\sim\mathrm{Pois}(|\langle a_i,x\rangle|^2)$. Set $\alpha=\|x\|^2$,
$Y=m^{-1}\sum_i y_i a_i a_i^T$, and let $x_0$ be its top eigenvector scaled to norm squared $\alpha$. Theorem 5 assumes $m\ge n\log n$ and gives, for each fixed $\beta>0$, absolute but unspecified constants $C_\beta,\hat C_\beta,c_\beta>0$ such that
\[
\Pr\!\left\{
\frac{\min_{s\in\{-1,1\}}\|x_0-sx\|^2}{\|x\|^2}
\le2C_\beta\left(1+\frac{\hat C_\beta}{\alpha}\right)
\log(m)\sqrt{\log(n)}\sqrt{\frac nm}
\right\}
\ge1-n^{-\beta}-m e^{-c_\beta n}-m^{-\beta}.
\]
Before conditioning on random design, the expected total count is $m\alpha$. Charge this as a total expected count across rows, not as if each row receives a free photon allotment. The theorem concerns a spectral estimator with an unnormalized Gaussian design; it is not a normalized one-photon POVM or a fixed-total-$N$ result. It has no additive background and its displayed rate does not match the $\Theta(d/N)$ order obtained conditionally above.

Li, Lange, and Fessler's [*Poisson Phase Retrieval in Very Low-count Regimes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00861) develops Poisson-likelihood algorithms and simulations, including observed-Fisher step sizes. It is algorithmic prior art, not a uniform finite-sample recovery theorem for this POVM model.

## Costs, Astra boundaries, and unknowns

The model charges $N$ incident photons/copies. A physical comparison must also price frame calibration, background calibration exposure, output channels, settings, detector loss/no-click outcomes, herald attempts, source preparation, ancilla/control energy, readout, precision, and estimator runtime. A setting used on a photon is part of the record; no basis or channel receives a free allotment of $N$ photons.

The local [N45 photon-counting boundary card](../quantum_classical_sensing/N45-photon-counting-boundaries.txt) distinguishes fixed-cardinality from variable-total count laws and limits conditional Gaussian-photon sampling claims. The first-cycle [rare-event report](FINAL_REPORT.md) gives the stopped-KL, false-alarm, calibration, and herald-cost accounting. Neither artifact supplies a physical implementation or removes its calibration cost.

Established here: exact fixed-$N$/Poissonized count laws and affinity; a conditional random Parseval-POVM modulus; a uniform Hellinger-fit risk upper bound; a matching-order quantum average-risk lower bound; and the exact NP statistic for two known states. Unknown: an efficient polynomial-time estimator; explicit finite constants for $m/d$, $\ell$, and failure probability; robustness to frame/background calibration error, loss, saturation, or heralding; the sharp minimax constant under one-copy rank-one POVMs; and any laboratory-level apparatus-energy advantage. No novelty or hardware-advantage claim is made.
