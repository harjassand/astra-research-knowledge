1. PRODUCT KERNEL AND EXACT OPERATOR DIFFERENTIAL IDENTITIES

Let F_1,...,F_m, m=d^2-1, be a real Hilbert-Schmidt orthonormal basis of
traceless Hermitian d-by-d matrices, Tr(F_a F_b)=delta_ab. For a density
matrix rho define k_N(rho)=rho^tensor N. For any Hermitian F put

 f_F=Tr(F rho),    T_F=sum_{i=1}^N F^(i),
 v_F(rho)={F,rho}-2 f_F rho,
 w_F(rho)=-i[F,rho],
 B_F=2N f_F+v_F.dot grad,    R_F=w_F.dot grad.

Both vector fields are Hermitian and traceless. Derivatives are on the affine
space of trace-one Hermitian matrices, with the Hilbert-Schmidt Euclidean
metric. The identities are EXACT for every N and every rho:

 {T_F,k_N}=B_F k_N,
 [T_F,k_N]=i R_F k_N,
 {(T_F)^2,k_N}/2=(B_F^2-R_F^2)k_N/4.             (1)

The first identity follows by summing derivatives of one tensor factor:
 dk_N[v_F]={T_F,k_N}-2N f_F k_N. The second follows the same way.
The differential operators commute with constant left/right matrix actions.
Consequently B_F^2 k_N=(left T_F+right T_F)^2 k_N and
R_F^2 k_N=-(left T_F-right T_F)^2 k_N. Subtracting gives (1).
No commuting Hamiltonians, coherent-state oracle or large-N expansion enter.

Take a real symmetric coefficient matrix C with 0<nu I<=C<=L I. Put
K=Tr C. Diagonalizing C merely writes the following algebra compactly:
C=sum_j lambda_j e_j e_j^T, G_j=sum_a (e_j)_a F_a. Each G_j has HS norm one,
hence operator norm at most one. Define

 Q_C=sum_ab C_ab {T_(F_a),T_(F_b)}/2=sum_j lambda_j (T_(G_j))^2,
 H_N=q_N Q_C+ell_N T_b,

where q_N>=0, b=b*, Tr b=0, ||b||_HS<=B, and ell_N is real. The quantum
sandwich generator M_H(X)={H_N,X}/2 satisfies

 M_H k_N=G_N k_N,
 G_N=V_N+a_N.dot grad+(q_N/4) D_C:Hess,           (2)

with EXPLICIT coefficients

 D_C=sum_j lambda_j(v_j tensor v_j-w_j tensor w_j),
 a_N=q_N sum_j lambda_j[N f_j v_j+(Dv_j[v_j]-Dw_j[w_j])/4]
                         +(ell_N/2) v_b,
 V_N=q_N sum_j lambda_j[N(N-1) f_j^2+N Tr(rho G_j^2)]
                         +ell_N N Tr(rho b).             (3)

Here v_j=v_(G_j), w_j=w_(G_j), and
 Dv_F[z]={F,z}-2Tr(Fz)rho-2f_F z,    Dw_F[z]=-i[F,z].
In particular V_N=Tr(H_N k_N) exactly. Formula (3) is polynomial in the
matrix entries of rho, C and b. Eigenvectors are not an acquisition premise:
one may contract the same bilinear formulas directly with C_ab. All input
coefficients and the reference below are known parameters.

2. EXACT NONCENTRAL KMS IDENTITY AND POSITIVE NEIGHBORHOOD

For traceless Hermitian test Z let a=Tr(rho Z) and Z_c=Z-a I. In the complete
orthonormal basis the isotropic coefficient C=I has diffusion form

 <Z,D_I Z>_HS
 =||{rho,Z}-2a rho||_HS^2-||i[rho,Z]||_HS^2
 =4 Tr(rho Z_c rho Z_c).                           (4)

To derive it, the dual of F->v_F is {rho,Z}-2a rho, which is traceless; the
dual rotation is i[rho,Z]. Parseval therefore gives the first line. Expanding
the squares gives 4Tr(rho Z rho Z)-8aTr(rho^2 Z)+4a^2Tr(rho^2), the second.
Thus (4) is 4 times a centered sandwich/KMS quadratic form with sandwich
matrix rho. Its normalized KMS reference is omega=rho^2/Tr(rho^2), NOT rho
itself: it equals 4Tr(rho^2)<Z_c,Z_c>_omega.

For rho>=u I, (4)>=4u^2||Z_c||_HS^2>=4u^2||Z||_HS^2. The last inequality
uses Tr Z=0: ||Z-aI||_HS^2=||Z||_HS^2+d a^2. This proves isotropic positivity
at every faithful, possibly noncentral, rho. It is not the qubit cross-product
formula and does not assume that F, rho or Z commute.

Since nu I<=C<=L I, the separate filtering and rotation forms give

 D_C >= nu D_I-(L-nu) W W^T,
 ||W||_op<=2||rho||_op<=2.                            (5)

Now supply a reference sigma>=mu I, Tr sigma=1, with a known mu>0. Set

 r=mu/2,    U={rho:Tr rho=1, rho*=rho, ||rho-sigma||_HS<=r}.

Every rho in U is a density matrix and rho>=mu I/2. If

 nu I<=C<=nu(1+mu^2/8) I,                            (6)

then (4)-(5) yield the public certificate

 D_C >= (nu mu^2/2) I                                (7)

throughout U. An upper bound on every positive D_C is
D_C<=sum lambda_j v_j tensor v_j<=16K I, since ||v_j||_HS<=4.
Both gap and upper bound are acquired from supplied finite matrix bounds.
A positive square root of D_C is smooth on a neighborhood of U by (7).

This is the precise limitation: an arbitrary anisotropic C does not follow
from KMS contraction alone. Condition (6) becomes restrictive as sigma becomes
nearly pure; replacing it by arbitrary C would be an unsupported inference.
