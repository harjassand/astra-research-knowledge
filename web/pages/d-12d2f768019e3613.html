## Exact posterior-cone lemma

An experiment has uniform input $I\in[4]$, arbitrary observation $Y$, posterior $p(Y)\in\Delta_4$, and posterior law $\mu$ under the marginal law of $Y$. Symmetry means invariance of this law under every permutation of the four coordinates; it is not a pointwise condition on $p$. For $x=Lp$, with the matrix $L$ in 02-experiments.tex lines 44–55, set
\[
A=x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2,\quad B=x_1x_2x_3,\quad
C=x_1^2x_2^2+x_1^2x_3^2+x_2^2x_3^2.
\]

The source defines, exactly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
H&=B-\tfrac1{10}A^2-\tfrac3{20}C+\tfrac3{10}AB-\tfrac1{50}A^3+\tfrac7{100}AC-\tfrac{51}{100}B^2,\\
F&=4A-3B+\tfrac7{10}A^2-4C-\tfrac45AB,\\
G&=1-\tfrac{161}{100}A-\tfrac{31}{10}B+\tfrac{773}{1000}A^2+4C-\tfrac{199}{50}AB.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let $\mathcal C$ be the class of symmetric posterior laws satisfying $\mathbb E_\mu H\ge0$. The class contains the uninformative law and the law uniform on the four revealing posteriors: $H(\unif)=0$, $H(e_i)=13/100$. It is not defined by $H(p)\ge0$ pointwise; for example, $H(1/2,1/2,0,0)=-3/25$.

For every $p\in\Delta_4$, the pinned source proves $F(p)\ge0$, $G(p)\ge0$, $H(p)\ge-1/2$, and for every $0\le t\le1$, $H(tLp)\ge t^3H(Lp)$. For $\mu,\nu\in\mathcal C$, it then proves:

1. Every symmetric mixture with a mixing label observed and independent of the input, every channel step $P_t$ for all $t\in[0,1]$, and every conditionally independent product $\mu\star\nu$ remain in $\mathcal C$.
2. With $m(\mu)=\mathbb E_\mu A$,
\[
m(\mu\star\nu)\le m(\mu)+m(\nu)-\frac{\mathbb E_\mu A^2\,m(\nu)+m(\mu)\,\mathbb E_\nu A^2}{1000}.
\]
By Jensen the saving is at least $[m(\mu)^2m(\nu)+m(\mu)m(\nu)^2]/1000$; for equal means $b$, it is at least $2b^3/1000$.

The exact product rule is important. Under independent marginal posterior draws $p\sim\mu,q\sim\nu$, define $z=4\langle p,q\rangle$, $P_i=4p_iq_i/z$ when $z>0$. The actual joint observation law weights $\mu\otimes\nu$ by $z$; $p,q$ are not independent under that weighted law. For $z=0$, choose any $P$: the weighted summand is zero. The certificate uses permutation averages
\[
[z\phi(P)](p,q)=\frac1{24}\sum_{\pi\in S_4}z_\pi\phi(P^\pi),\qquad
U_i^\pi=4p_iq_{\pi(i)},\quad P^\pi=U^\pi/z_\pi,
\]
with zero summands at $z_\pi=0$. It establishes
\[
\begin{aligned}
[zA(P)]&\le A(p)+A(q)-H(p)F(q)-F(p)H(q)-A(p)A(q)(A(p)+A(q))/1000,\\
[zH(P)]&\ge H(p)G(q)+G(p)H(q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Integrating against the independent marginal laws proves closure and the moment saving. Source 05 proves these polynomial signs by rational coefficient certificates on a finite subdivision of the closed ordered-probability simplex product; source 06 proves coverage and boundary continuity. Tied coordinates are included in the closed ordered chamber and its shared faces; no distinct-coordinate assumption is used. The finite exact code is part of the proof artifact, not an analytic closed-form certificate. My small Python checker rerun confirms the four scalar coefficient minima; it does not repeat the product subdivision.

### Channel endpoints and zero denominators

Under $P_t$, a posterior about the parent is $\unif+t(p-\unif)$, so $x\mapsto tx$, $m\mapsto t^2m$, and $\mathbb E H(t x)\ge t^3\mathbb E H(x)$. At $t=0$ it is the uninformative posterior; at $t=1$ it is the identity. Both are included in the cone lemma. At a product boundary $z=0$, all $U_i=0$; $z\phi(P)$ is assigned zero and has continuous extension because $A,H$ are bounded on $\Delta_4$. In the weighted-capacity theorem, the channel inputs are $0<\lambda_e\le1$: $\lambda_e=1$ is an exact identity edge with zero potential increment. Edges with $\lambda_e=0$ must be removed first; downstream observations behind such an edge are independent of the root, while $R_w=0$ would make the displayed criterion undefined.

## Hostile audit of the weighted-flow iff

The candidate statement is for an infinite locally finite rooted tree, not a uniformly bounded-degree tree. Root spin is uniform on four states; each edge $e=(v,w)$ independently applies $P_{\lambda_e}$ conditionally on its parent, with known $\lambda_e\in(0,1]$; the whole tree/channel assignment and every depth-$n$ spin are supplied. Define
\[
R_\rho=1,\quad R_w=R_v\lambda_e^2,\quad
V(\theta,\xi)=\sum_{e=(v,w)\in\xi}(1-\lambda_e^4)(\theta_e/R_w)^2.
\]
For nonnegative conserved flow $\theta$ with finite positive root mass, the Astra-derived candidate says $\lim_n a_n>0$ iff some such flow has $\sup_\xi V(\theta,\xi)<\infty$. Scaling $\theta$ normalizes a finite bound to one.

**Reconstruction implies flow.** Let $m_v^{(n)}$ be the quadratic information at $v$ from level-$n$ descendants and $m_v=\lim_n m_v^{(n)}\in[0,3]$. Data processing gives the limit; the posterior-TV bounds give $a_\infty>0\Rightarrow m_\rho>0$. At each vertex the finite child set has branch means $b_w=\lambda_e^2m_w$. The cone lemma gives $m_v\le S-cD$, where $S=\sum_wb_w$, $c=1/1000$, and $D=\max_{w\ne w'}b_wb_{w'}(b_w+b_{w'})$, with $D=0$ for fewer than two children. If maximizing pairs tie, any maximizing pair works. Setting $K=c/3$, $y(m)=m+Km^3$ makes the pair operation subadditive: $y(m_{12})\le a+b-cab(a+b)+K(a+b)^3\le y(a)+y(b)$. Iterating over the finitely many children removes the degree factor; the one-child channel identity contributes the separate decrement $K\lambda_e^2(1-\lambda_e^4)m_w^3$.

With $\delta=K/(1+9K)^3$, channel scaling and $m_w\le3$ yield
\[
y_v\le\sum_w r_e y_w[1-\delta(1-r_e^2)y_w^2]
\le\sum_w\frac{r_e y_w}{\sqrt{1+2\delta(1-r_e^2)y_w^2}},\quad r_e=\lambda_e^2.
\]
For $z_v=\sqrt{2\delta}\,y_v$, choose $d_{vw}\le r_e/\sqrt{1+(1-r_e^2)z_w^2}$ such that $z_v=\sum_wd_{vw}z_w$. If the upper-bound sum is positive, scale all its terms by $z_v/\sum_w r_ez_w/\sqrt{1+(1-r_e^2)z_w^2}\le1$; if it is zero, the recursion forces $z_v=0$ and take $d=0$. This choice has no tie-breaking dependency. Put $\ell_\rho=1$, $\ell_w=\ell_vd_{vw}$, $\theta(v,w)=\ell_wz_w$, $g_v=\ell_v/R_v$. Then conservation gives root mass $z_\rho>0$, and
\[
g_w^2[1+(1-r_e^2)z_w^2]\le g_v^2,\qquad
(1-r_e^2)(\theta_e/R_w)^2\le g_v^2-g_w^2.
\]
Summing along any ray telescopes to at most $g_\rho^2=1$. Local finiteness is used exactly for finite sums/maxima at each vertex; no uniform degree bound is needed.

**Flow implies reconstruction.** Write $\theta(v)$ for the total flow entering $v$ (and $\theta(\rho)=b$). Let the normalized flow have mass $b>0$, scale it by $\eta$, and set $X_v=\eta\theta(v)/R_v$, $X_\rho=\eta b$. At each positive-flow vertex $p_e=\theta(w)/\theta(v)$ sums to one and $X_v=\sum_e r_eX_w$. Since child flow never exceeds parent flow,
\[
X_w^2\le X_v^2+(1-r_e^2)X_w^2.
\]
Thus the unit path-potential bound implies $X_v\le\eta\sqrt{b^2+1}$; choosing $\eta$ small makes every terminal channel $t_v=\sqrt{X_v/3}$ legal. Independently degrading each depth-$n$ spin by $P_{t_v}$ gives terminal $m_v=X_v$; product subadditivity preserves $m_v\le X_v$.

The boundary-sensitive part is the local moment estimate used in composition. For arbitrary symmetric laws (no strict channel smoothing), $Z=1+s\ge0$, $s=x\cdot y\in[-1,3]$, and $N_i=Z(LP)_i$. Since $P\in\Delta_4$, $|N_i|\le Z$. The quadratic identity leaves a nonnegative remainder and gives
\[
m(\mu\star\nu)\ge m_\mu+m_\nu-7(h_\mu m_\nu+m_\mu h_\nu).
\]
For the third-moment upper bound,
\[
Z^{-2}-(1-2s)=s^2(3+2s)/Z^2;
\]
the remainder in the weighted expectation is at most $Z(3+2s)s^2\le36s^2$, including its zero extension at $Z=0$. Since $\mathbb E s^2=m_\mu m_\nu/3$, this contributes at most $12m_\mu m_\nu$. The l1 coefficient sum of $N_1N_2N_3(1-2s)$ is at most $4^3(1+2\cdot3)=448$; terms linear in either vector average to zero, pure terms are the two third moments, and every remaining mixed monomial is bounded by $m_\mu m_\nu$. Thus $C=460$ suffices for
\[
h(\mu\star\nu)\le h_\mu+h_\nu+460m_\mu m_\nu.
\]
This repairs the original constant-$\lambda$ proof's $C(\lambda)$ denominator bound at $\lambda=1$ and removes any need for a uniform gap from one.

For degree-free aggregation, set $b_e=r_em_w=p_eX_v$, $k_w=h_w/X_w\le1$, and $s_v=1-\sum_ep_e^2=2\sum_{e<f}p_ep_f$. Sequential product estimates give
\[
k_v\le\sum_ep_e\lambda_ek_w+(C/2)X_vs_v,\qquad
D_v/X_v\le7X_v\sum_ep_e\lambda_e(1-p_e)k_w+(7C/2)X_v^2s_v,
\]
where $D_v=\sum_er_em_w-m_v\ge0$. Pair costs use $\sum_{e<f}p_ep_f=s_v/2$, not the number of children. A random flow ray chooses edge $e$ with probability $p_e$; it has depth-$i$ marginal $\theta(v)/(\eta b)$. The occupation potential $F(X,a)=X^2+aX+a^2/4$, updated by $X'=pX/\lambda^2$, $a'=\lambda[a+(1-p)X]$, obeys the source-page inequality
\[
F(X',a')-F(X,a)\le-\tfrac34(1-p)X^2-\tfrac12(1-p)aX+2(1-\lambda^4)X'^2.
\]
At $\lambda=1$ its exact expansion supplies the negative terms; for $0<\lambda<1$, completing the square gives the displayed potential remainder. The resulting occupation bound makes the total relative information loss less than $1/2$ for one $\eta>0$ independent of depth, tree degree, and all edge parameters. Hence every original depth-$n$ observation has $a_n\ge\eta b/16>0$.

## Boundary/failure ledger

- The source capacity paper's theorem (its main.tex Theorem 1.1) assumes one constant $0<\lambda<1$ and a uniform branching cap $B$. It does not prove N49's arbitrary-edge/unbounded-degree extension. That extension depends on the separate, direct flow and occupation arguments above.
- The extension is specific to the symmetric four-state Potts channels and a uniform root. It does not cover arbitrary reversible edge matrices, dependent branch side information, cycles, or signed channels. The pinned Astra derivation gives a signed-channel failure witness $H=-1409/24300$ at the output of the revealing posterior under $\lambda=-1/3$; taking $|\lambda|$ is not a valid preservation argument.
- Zero edges must be pruned; unit edges are genuinely covered. The cone lemma includes both channel endpoints, and the universal $C=460$ bound is what makes the $\lambda=1$ case of the sufficiency proof valid.
- The one-ray test verifies the weight: with $\Lambda_n=\prod_{j\le n}\lambda_j$, $a_n=3\Lambda_n/4$ and $V=\theta^2\sum_j(\Lambda_j^{-4}-\Lambda_{j-1}^{-4})=\theta^2(\Lambda_\infty^{-4}-1)$. Thus reconstruction iff $\prod_j\lambda_j>0$. For $\lambda_j=1-2^{-j-1}$, positive product reconstructs despite divergent unweighted path sum; this refutes an unweighted heterogeneous extension, not the weighted criterion.
- No finite-depth reconstruction rate, efficient flow-finding procedure, topology/channel-acquisition cost, sensor/communication/energy budget, or prior-art/priority conclusion is proved. The sensor-network application remains an asymptotic threshold under a fully supplied tree, known channel parameters, and all leaf readings.
