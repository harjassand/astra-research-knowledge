This strengthens INITIAL's generic Gaussian observation obstruction. It uses
the SAME heat-smoothed compact log-concave reference, but replaces the narrow
sigmoid field by an analytic smoothing of a compact cubic ramp. The derivative
energy does not pay the smoothing bandwidth. Variance contrast is now K=64R,
and any universal contrast-only factor in

 I(Y;Z) <= C P(mu) F(K) E J(Y)

must satisfy F(K) >= c K/(log K)^2 along an unbounded sequence. The same

1. REFERENCE AND ANALYTIC SMOOTHING

Fix R>=32. Let X have exp(-x)/Z_R density on [0,2R], Z_R=1-exp(-2R),
G~N(0,1) independent, and Y=X+G. Center by a known translation if desired.
INITIAL derives

 Cov(Y)<=2, P(mu_R)<=5,
 rho_R(y)=exp(-y+1/2)/Z_R
     [Phi(2R-y+1)-Phi(1-y)] <= exp(-y+1/2)/Z_R.             (1)

On [R+1,R+2] the bracket is >=1/2 for R>=4. The reference is positive,
analytic, and log-concave, and is explicitly obtained by Gaussian heat flow.

Define the C1 ramp H_0(y) by 0 below R-1, 1 above R, and
3u^2-2u^3 for u=y-R+1 in [0,1]. It has 0<=H_0'<=3/2,
with derivative supported in [R-1,R]. Put

 K=64R, M=log K,
 sigma^2=[2 log(2M/log 2)]^(-1) <=1,
 H(y)=E[H_0(y+sigma G)].

Equivalently the exact compact-integral formula is

 H(y)=int_0^1 6u(1-u) Phi((y-R+1-u)/sigma) du.               (2)

This is an explicitly computable real-analytic function, 0<H<1.
No sampling of the rare event is needed to define H. The integral is part
of the supplied mathematical model, not an already implemented compiler.

Since Phi(-1/sigma)<=exp[-1/(2sigma^2)]=(log 2)/(2M),

 y<=R-2 implies H(y)<=(log 2)/(2M),
 y>=R+1 implies H(y)>=1-(log 2)/(2M).                       (3)

Let A(y)=exp(M H(y)); then 1<=A<=K and

 y<=R-2: A(y)<=sqrt(2),
 y>=R+1: A(y)>=K/sqrt(2).                                 (4)

2. EXPECTED NORMALIZED FISHER COST

For the scalar centered Gaussian q(z|y)=N(0,A(y)),
J(y)=(1/2)M^2 H'(y)^2. Gaussian averaging and Jensen give

 int exp(-y) H'(y)^2 dy
 <= E int exp(-y) H_0'(y+sigma G)^2dy
 = exp(sigma^2/2) int exp(-t) H_0'(t)^2dt
 <= (9/4)(e-1) exp(sigma^2/2) exp(-R).                     (5)

Combining with (1),

 E J(Y) <= [9(e-1)/8] exp(1/2+sigma^2/2)/Z_R
                 * exp(-R)(log K)^2
          <= [9e(e-1)/(8 Z_R)] exp(-R)(log K)^2.            (6)

The derivative smoothing did NOT introduce a sigma^(-1) loss. This removes
the extra sqrt(log log K) that a naive single-sigmoid construction incurs.

3. ACTUAL GAUSSIAN INFORMATION LOWER

Let B=1{Y>R-2}, E=1{|Z|>sqrt(8R)}. On B=0 the Gaussian variance is <=sqrt2<2,
so Pr(E=1|B=0)<=2 exp(-2R). Also

 p=Pr(B=1)<=exp(-R+5/2)/Z_R,
 log(1/p)>=R/2 for R>=32.                                 (7)

On [R+1,R+2], A>=K/sqrt2>=32R, and the Gaussian small-ball bound gives
Pr(E=1|Y=y)>=1-sqrt(2/pi)/2>=1/2. Hence

 s=Pr(E=1)>= exp(1/2)/(4 Z_R)
                [exp(-R-1)-exp(-R-2)]
          >= (1-exp(-1))/(4 sqrt e) exp(-R).                (8)

eta=Pr(B=0|E=1)<=[8 sqrt e/(1-exp(-1))] exp(-R)<=1/4.
Therefore

 KL(B|E=1||B)>=(1-eta)log(1/p)-h_2(eta)>=R/4,
 I(Y;Z)>=I(B;E)>=s KL(B|E=1||B)
       >=(1-exp(-1))/(16 sqrt e) R exp(-R).                 (9)

All inequalities above hold for R>=32. Combining P(mu_R)<=5 with (6),(9),

 I(Y;Z)/(P(mu_R) E J(Y))
   >= Z_R/[90 e^(5/2)] * R/(log K)^2
   = Z_R/[5760 e^(5/2)] * K/(log K)^2.                    (10)

Thus a universal factor F(K)=O(1), O(log^a K), or O(K^a) for any fixed a<1
cannot work across these positive analytic Gaussian covariance fields.
No dimension-growth issue is used; reference and channel are scalar.

4. FIXED SCALE WINDOW, STILL OF THE FORM 1+T Rfield^2

For T in [1,2] put

 A_T(y)=1+T(A(y)-1),
 Rfield(y)=sqrt(A(y)-1),
 Z_T|Y=y ~ N(0,1+T Rfield(y)^2).

Rfield is real analytic since A(y)>1 at every finite y. On B=0,
A_T<=1+2(sqrt2-1)<2, while on [R+1,R+2], A_T>=A>=32R.
The SAME B,E event proof gives the SAME information lower (9), uniformly
for T in [1,2]. The normalized covariance derivative obeys

 |partial_y log A_T|
   =T |A'|/[1+T(A-1)] <=2|A'|/A,
 E J_T<=4 E J_1.                                         (11)

Consequently

 int_1^2 I(Y;Z_T) dT/T >=(log2) c R exp(-R),
 int_1^2 E J_T dT/T <=4(log2) C exp(-R)(log K)^2,

and their ratio, even multiplied by the bounded Poincare constant, diverges
at least as K/(log K)^2. Merely integrating a covariance-only Fisher bound
over scales does not repair it in the class of arbitrary analytic fields.

This is a bounded-window statement. It does NOT assert that the actual N75
full scale-integral, weight, cap f_L, derived-field identities, or critical
normalization are refuted. Those were not fully supplied. In particular the
reference Cov/P does not tend to zero, so the hypothetical KLS-counterexample
sequence remains a different interface.

5. A POSITIVE POINCARE REPLACEMENT WITH RAW MATRIX-GRADIENT COST

Let mu on R^n have Poincare constant P. Supply a weakly differentiable
real symmetric covariance field A(x) on an m-dimensional real Gaussian output,
with alpha I<=A(x), finite second entry moments and square-integrable
derivatives. Let Z|X=x~N(0,A(x)), and set Abar=E A(X).
The KL-mixture identity with Q0=N(0,Abar) gives

 I(X;Z) = E KL(N(0,A(X))||Q0)-KL(Law Z||Q0)
        <= (1/2)[log det Abar-E log det A(X)].               (12)

This can also be justified by finite truncation if differential entropies
are inconvenient. The Gaussian KL formula is exact; its trace term averages
to m. Let F(A)=-logdet A. On A>=alpha I,

 D^2F(A)[H,H]=tr(A^(-1)H A^(-1)H)<=alpha^(-2)||H||_F^2.

Taylor with integral remainder at Abar cancels the linear term after
averaging, giving

 I(X;Z)<=1/(4alpha^2) E||A(X)-Abar||_F^2
        <=P/(4alpha^2) E sum_a ||partial_a A(X)||_F^2.       (13)

The last step is entrywise Poincare; no factor m or n appears. This is an
elementary usable bound. It needs no log-Sobolev constant, only the ACTUAL
raw Frobenius gradient and its finite charged expectation.

If also A<=beta I, K=beta/alpha, the channel Fisher trace is

 J_q(x)=(1/2)sum_a
        ||A^(-1/2)(partial_a A)A^(-1/2)||_F^2,

and ||partial_a A||_F<=beta
||A^(-1/2)(partial_a A)A^(-1/2)||_F. Hence

 I(X;Z)<=(K^2 P/2) E J_q(X).                               (14)

The contrast-dependent upper K^2 does not contradict the necessary
Omega(K/log^2K) factor in (10). Their remaining gap is explicit.
For a source field A_T=I+f_L(T Rfield^2), (13) reduces the desired scale
estimate to the ACQUIRED bound

 int E sum_a ||partial_a f_L(T Rfield(X)^2)||_F^2 dT/T.

A scalar Lipschitz bound on f_L alone only gives an elementary T^2 derivative
bound and does not control the full scale integral. Supplying Rfield and its
gradient bound is not proving acquisition or critical closure. This is the
next exact gate; no unsupported transfer into KLS is made.
