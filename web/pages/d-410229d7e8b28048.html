NEW CHECKPOINT CLAIM

The c09_s03 saturated covariance-field inequality survives my independent
algebra audit, but it is not a dimension-free constant unless its joint
log-field variance is itself controlled uniformly. Its data-acquired version
is useful only with a supplied log-range/tail certificate. Under the stated
sample-only access model and merely finite log second moment, no algorithm can
return a finite high-confidence upper certificate uniformly over all fields.
Thus the theorem does not make the N75 field, its law, its log-spectrum, or its
acquisition cost free. This is an acquisition boundary, not a refutation of
the c09_s03 theorem.

1. INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE MATRIX BOUND

The peer theorem in outputs/round6/frozen_initials/c09_s03/INITIAL.txt states,
for positive definite B(Y), f_L(x)=Lx/(L+x), and
Z_T|Y ~ N(0,I+f_L(TB(Y))),

  integral_0^infinity I(Y;Z_T) dT/T
    <= (L^2/24) E||log B - E log B||_F^2.

I independently checked its key steps. At a fixed scale, Gaussian maximum
entropy gives I<=1/2(logdet E A-E logdet A), A=I+f_L(TB). The Hessian of
-logdet on C>=I is tr(C^-1 H C^-1 H)<=||H||_F^2, yielding
I<=1/8 E||f_L(TB)-f_L(TB')||_F^2 for an independent copy B'. For noncommuting
B,C, spectral expansion of the Frobenius square gives weights
|<u_i,v_j>|^2; their row and column sums are one. The scalar integral is
L^2[(b+c)/(b-c) log(b/c)-2], bounded by L^2(log(b/c))^2/6. Summing the
weighted squared log-eigenvalue differences equals ||log B-log C||_F^2,
without a commuting assumption. Pair variance then gives the displayed
constant. I found no algebraic defect in this argument.

This report is not an external proof certification. The peer's exact rational
script covers diagonal rational tables and kernel identities, not arbitrary
noncommuting matrix inputs or the general theorem. The theorem's finite
log-second-moment hypothesis is essential; singular B or mismatched null
spaces cannot be regularized at zero cost.

2. DIMENSION CHARGE IS REAL

Let Y=(Y_1,...,Y_m) have iid Rademacher coordinates and
B(Y)=diag(exp(aY_1),...,exp(aY_m)), a>0. Then

  E log B=0,
  E||log B-E log B||_F^2=m a^2.

At each T, the coordinates of Z_T are independent after marginalizing Y,
and

  I(Y;Z_T)=sum_i I(Y_i;Z_i)=m I_1(T).

For every T>0, the two conditional centered Gaussian variances differ because
f_L is strictly increasing, so I_1(T)>0. The integral is finite by the peer
upper bound and positive (continuity on any compact interval inside (0,infty)).
It therefore scales exactly as m times a positive one-coordinate integral.
Consequently the c09_s03 theorem is dimension-independent only in its
coefficient, not in its information consequence: a uniform-in-m conclusion
requires a separate bound on the Frobenius log-field variance or a normalized
per-mode target. The same construction also shows why an O(1) KLS/Poincare
constant cannot alone remove an extensive joint covariance-field information
cost.

Representation costs depend on access model. An explicit finite table for
this example has 2^m rows and m log-eigenvalues per row (Theta(m 2^m)
scalars); a compact evaluator needs only O(m) coordinate work per call but
still takes m input bits/outputs per sample. Neither is included in a theorem
that only charges the eventual archive memory. A rational table with bit-size
H also pays the full input bit length and log-interval precision; the
c09_s03 certificate's stated diagonal-table pair loop costs O(K^2 m)
rational operations for K rows, with centered-moment optimization not yet
implemented. General dense matrix-log/eigendecomposition costs are outside
that implementation.

3. SAMPLE-ONLY LOG-VARIANCE ACQUISITION IMPOSSIBILITY

Suppose the only access to an unknown reference/field is n iid evaluations
of X=log B(Y), and no certified range, tail, or fourth-moment bound is given.
Even for a scalar field, set X=0 with probability 1-p and X=K with
probability p. Its variance is p(1-p)K^2. The chance that all n observations
equal zero is (1-p)^n, and this transcript is exactly the transcript under the
constant field X=0.

Formal consequence: fix confidence error delta<1. Any estimator that returns
a finite upper bound with probability at least 1-delta0 on the constant
instance, for some delta0<1-delta, has a finite quantile U on the all-zero
transcript. Choose p so (1-p)^n(1-delta0)>delta, then choose K so
p(1-p)K^2>U. On the rare-field instance, with probability greater than
delta the method sees the same all-zero data and returns a bound below the
true variance. Thus no uniformly valid finite high-confidence upper can be
obtained from finitely many samples under only finite second moment. The
algorithm can remain valid by returning infinity on that transcript, but then
it has not acquired a usable certificate. The exact Fraction diagnostic in
acquisition_cost_audit.py exhibits n=100, p=1/200, K=1000: population variance
is 4975 while the chance of missing the rare state is about 0.606.

This precisely delimits the optional Hoeffding acquisition route in
c09_s03: its bound W=sum_i(c_i-a_i)^2 is a supplied certificate, not something
learned for free from samples. With that range supplied, its pair statistic
lies in [0,W/2], and its sampling contribution to the information bound is
at most tau/2 if

  n >= L^4 W^2 log(1/delta)/(1152 tau^2).

It also charges 2n source draws, O(nm) diagonal log-field evaluations,
precision eta (including the stated deterministic error
2 eta sum_i(c_i-a_i)+2m eta^2), and the range certificate. If B is a dense
noncommuting matrix, the supplied diagonal implementation does not apply;
numerical matrix-log/eigenvalue precision and its computational cost require
a new implementation.
