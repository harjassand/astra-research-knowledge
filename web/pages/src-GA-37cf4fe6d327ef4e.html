## 1. The theorem, with every quantifier

Let averages be normalized on the square torus of period `2 pi`, and retain

\[
J(A)=\frac18((a-d)^2+(b+c)^2)^2+(ad-bc)^2
       +(a^2+c^2)(ad-bc),\quad
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Let

\[
\phi=(f,g),\qquad f(x,y)=F(x)+H(y),
\]

where `F,H` are arbitrary real periodic `W^{1,4}` functions and `g` is any
real periodic `W^{1,4}(T^2)` function. Constants are irrelevant. Write

\[
a=F'(x),\ b=H'(y),\ c=g_x,\ d=g_y,
\quad
M_4=a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4+
 a^2b^2+a^2c^2+a^2d^2+b^2c^2+b^2d^2+c^2d^2.
\]

Then for **every real scalar `s`**, including zero and negative scalars,

\[
\boxed{\langle J(sI+D\phi)-2s^4\rangle
\ge\frac1{128}\langle M_4+s^2(a^2+c^2+d^2)\rangle.}       \tag{1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\langle J(sI+D\phi)-J(sI)\rangle
\ge\frac1{256}\langle|D\phi|^4\rangle
 +\frac{s^2}{128}\langle a^2+c^2+d^2\rangle\ge0.}       \tag{2}
\]

No restriction is imposed on the size, sign, determinant, concentration,
Fourier support, injectivity, or two-dimensional dependence of `g`. There is
no restriction on either periodic one-dimensional profile beyond `W^{1,4}`.
This is an exact theorem for the unchanged quartic, not a completion by a
positive distortion term.

The theorem is stated on the torus. It does not assert that an arbitrary
zero-boundary map has separated first potential, or that localization preserves
that separation. The pointwise certificate below applies on any domain, and
(1) also applies whenever its stress pairing and first derivative mean vanish.
By left rotation invariance it transfers to backgrounds `sU` and maps `U phi`
for `U in SO(2)`. It does not assert that rotating only the perturbation while
holding the background `sI` preserves the separated subclass.

## 2. The nonlinear stress and exact polynomial identity

For arbitrary real `a,b,c,d,s`, define

\[
\sigma_1(s,b)=-3s^2b+\frac12b^3,
\qquad
\sigma_2(s,a)=3s^3+7s^2a+5sa^2+\frac12a^3,              \tag{3}
\]

and set `sigma=(sigma_1,sigma_2)`. There is the exact identity

\[
\boxed{J\!\begin{pmatrix}s+a&b\\c&s+d\end{pmatrix}-2s^4
 =5s^3a+\sigma_1(s,b)c+\sigma_2(s,a)d+G(a,b,c,d,s).}     \tag{4}
\]

The residual is `G=G_4+sG_3+s^2G_2`, where

\[
G_2=4a^2+c^2+d^2,
\]
\[
G_3=a^3-4abc+ac^2+2ad^2-2bcd+c^2d,
\]
\[
\begin{split}
G_4={}&\frac18a^4+\frac14a^2b^2-\frac12a^2bc
 +\frac14a^2c^2+\frac74a^2d^2-\frac12ab^2d-3abcd
 +\frac12ac^2d-\frac12ad^3\\
&+\frac18b^4+\frac74b^2c^2+\frac14b^2d^2
 -\frac12bc^3+\frac12bcd^2+\frac18c^4
 +\frac14c^2d^2+\frac18d^4.
\end{split}
\]

Equivalently `G_4=J(a,b,c,d)-(a^3 d+b^3 c)/2`. All formulas use the original
row convention: the second potential has row `(c,d)=(g_x,g_y)`.

This stress takes the exact second-row reference stress along each separate
coordinate axis and retains those two nonlinear one-variable entries together.
It is not the simple constant-multiple cofactor stress. Its divergence-free
property depends on the separated-coordinate first potential.

## 3. Exact positive Gram certificate

Use the two quadratic monomial vectors

\[
z_E=(ds,d^2,c^2,bc,b^2,as,ad,a^2)^T,
\qquad
z_O=(cs,cd,bd,ac,ab)^T.
\]

Then exact rational multiplication gives

\[
G=z_E^TQ_Ez_E+z_O^TQ_Oz_O,                              \tag{5}
\]

where

\[
Q_E=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&3/16&-31/32&0&0&29/32&-1/32\\
0&1/8&-1/16&1/32&-1/96&3/32&-1/4&1/32\\
3/16&-1/16&1/8&-1/4&1/24&3/32&9/32&-1/32\\
-31/32&1/32&-1/4&5/3&0&-49/32&-33/32&-5/32\\
0&-1/96&1/24&0&1/8&0&-3/32&-5/96\\
0&3/32&3/32&-49/32&0&4&1/32&1/2\\
29/32&-1/4&9/32&-33/32&-3/32&1/32&27/16&0\\
-1/32&1/32&-1/32&-5/32&-5/96&1/2&0&1/8
\end{pmatrix},
\]

\[
Q_O=\begin{pmatrix}
1&5/16&-1/32&13/32&-15/32\\
5/16&3/8&7/32&-1/32&-5/16\\
-1/32&7/32&13/48&-5/32&-5/32\\

-15/32&-5/16&-5/32&-3/32&17/48

Their exact LDL pivots, in the displayed orders, are respectively

\[
1,\frac18,\frac{15}{256},\frac{323}{480},
\frac{6539}{65664},\frac{7983605}{14228864},

and

\[

Every pivot is positive, so (5) is an exact positive sum-of-squares
certificate. Separately, the verifier computes all leading principal minors

\[
\boxed{G\ge\frac1{128}(|z_E|^2+|z_O|^2)
=\frac1{128}\{M_4+s^2(a^2+c^2+d^2)\}.}                 \tag{6}
\]

The positive certificate (5), the analytic null-pairing argument, and the
quantitative shifted-matrix verification are separate obligations. No floating
eigenvalue is used as a premise of (5) or (6).

## 4. Compatibility, integration, and Sobolev passage

For `f=F(x)+H(y)`, `a` depends only on `x` and `b` only on `y`. Consequently
`sigma_1(s,b)` depends only on `y`, and `sigma_2(s,a)` only on `x`. For smooth
periodic data this proves

\[
\operatorname{div}\sigma=\partial_x\sigma_1+\partial_y\sigma_2=0,
\quad
\langle\sigma_1g_x+\sigma_2g_y\rangle=0.              \tag{7}
\]

Also `<a>=0`. Integrating (4), then using (6)-(7), proves (1). Finally

\[
M_4=\frac12|D\phi|^4+\frac12(a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4)
\ge\frac12|D\phi|^4,
\]

which proves (2).

For Sobolev data, take periodic smooth approximations of `F,H` in
`W^{1,4}(T)` and of `g` in `W^{1,4}(T^2)`. The separated form is preserved.
All quartic polynomial integrals converge under strong `L^4` convergence of
the gradients. The stress is cubic in those gradients and belongs to
`L^{4/3}`, while `(g_x,g_y)` belongs to `L^4`; their pairings converge by
Hölder. Thus (4)-(7) pass to the limit and prove (1)-(2) for the full stated
class. The argument treats all `s` algebraically, so no limiting or sign
argument is required for `s=0` or `s<0`.

## 5. Consequence for the signed cubic discriminant

For the same separated-first-potential class, define the unchanged target

\[
A=\langle J(D\phi)\rangle,\quad
C=\langle a^3+5a^2d-4abc+ac^2+2ad^2-2bcd+c^2d\rangle,
\quad Q=\langle(2a+d)^2+c^2\rangle.
\]

The usual conformal expansion is `A+sC+s^2Q`. Let
`R=<a^2+c^2+d^2>` and `M=<M_4>`. Equation (1) for every `s` gives

\[
\boxed{C^2\le4\left(A-\frac M{128}\right)
                    \left(Q-\frac R{128}\right).}       \tag{8}
\]

Indeed the quadratic on the right of (1), moved to the left, is nonnegative
for every real scalar. Its constant coefficient is positive for a nonzero
map; its quadratic coefficient is nonnegative. Here compatibility and
separation give `<ad>=0`, so `Q=4<a^2>+<c^2+d^2>` and `R>=Q/4`.

An optional uniform but deliberately nonsharp numerical margin follows.
Pointwise

\[
J(V)\le\frac{5+\sqrt{13}}{16}|V|^4\le\frac9{16}|V|^4.
\]

This follows from `J/S^2=1/8+(3y^2+2xy)/8`,
`x=(u-v)/S`, `y=2j/S`, and `x^2+y^2<=1`. Since
`M>=<|V|^4>/2>=8A/9`, (8) implies, when `AQ>0`,

\[
\boxed{\frac{C^2}{4AQ}\le
\frac{143}{144}\frac{511}{512}
=\frac{73073}{73728}<1.}                               \tag{9}
\]

No sharpness is claimed for any displayed quantitative constant. The
qualitative advance is the unlimited second-potential class, not optimization
of this rational margin. If `Q=0`, the cubic is zero and the same conclusion
has its natural degenerate meaning.

## 6. Exact remaining general-first-potential obstruction

For an arbitrary smooth periodic first potential, use precisely the same
stress (3). Compatibility gives `a_y=b_x=f_xy`. Direct chain rule yields

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{div}\sigma
=\left(4s^2+10sa+\frac32(a^2+b^2)\right)f_{xy}.}        \tag{10}
\]

Consequently the pointwise certificate remains valid, but its stress is no
longer generally divergence-free. The actual Jensen energy satisfies

\[
\begin{split}
\langle J(sI+D\phi)-2s^4\rangle
={}&\langle G\rangle+\langle\sigma\cdot Dg\rangle\\
={}&\langle G\rangle-
\left\langle g\left(4s^2+10sf_x+\frac32|Df|^2\right)f_{xy}\right\rangle.
\end{split}                                           \tag{11}
\]

For rough maps the first equality, with the `L^{4/3}`-`L^4` stress pairing,
is the well-defined statement; the second is its smooth representation.
Thus one explicit, sufficient all-gradient obligation would be

\[
\langle\sigma\cdot Dg\rangle\ge-\langle G\rangle
\quad\text{for all compatible periodic }(f,g).          \tag{12}
\]

It is equivalent to the original conformal Jensen inequality after (4), not
an assertion that the particular stress itself is already a dual feasible
calibration. Neither the sharper budget (6) nor an absolute-value estimate
for the mixed source is asserted to satisfy (12) universally. Its cancellation
may depend on all entries of `Dg`. The construction leaves a single explicit
nonlinear mixed source that vanishes for separated first potentials.

The coefficient in (10) is not nonnegative everywhere. A sign restriction
on it would not by itself control the signed pairing with `g`, either. No
all-gradient proof is obtained by treating (10) as a positive defect.

## 7. Concentration and evidence scope

This argument never assumes uniform integrability or compactness of an
extremizing sequence. Equations (4)-(7) are exact local algebra and actual
compatible stress pairing. The bounds hold for arbitrarily concentrated
one-dimensional profiles and arbitrary concentrated `g`. Simultaneous compact
concentration of a general two-dimensional `f` need not preserve the separated
form, so this subclass theorem is not promoted to the full concentration
problem. Equations (10)-(12) retain that missing mechanism explicitly.

Discovery used one polynomial stress hypothesis. A numerical positive-Gram
search and a pointwise diagnostic helped obtain the rational matrices. The
final theorem uses only the displayed polynomial identity, exact rational
positivity, and the analytic integration/density proof. Exploratory numerical
files are explicitly diagnostics; they are not all-gradient bounds or
counterexamples. No optimizer over the target compatible-gradient problem was
run here.

Classical machinery: a positive rational Gram matrix proves a polynomial
sum of squares; periodic integration by parts, Hölder, and strong Sobolev
density are standard. The source reports supplied the unchanged problem,
existing row-convex duality, exact shear elimination, and failed norm routes.
Their independent proofs are not required by (3)-(7). The present subclass
may be subsumed by a broader existing laminate theorem; that priority question
has not been checked. No claim is made that polynomial SOS, nonlinear
calibration, separated fields, or conditional Jensen are new subjects.
