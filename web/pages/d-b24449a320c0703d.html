The full nonlinear weak endpoint for a local qubit generator with an arbitrary
quantum reference remains UNRESOLVED. The theorem below is a local operator
inequality and is not relabeled a solution of that full target.

THEOREM. In any finite dimension let sigma>0 be a faithful density matrix and
let L be the positive Heisenberg generator of a sigma-stationary KMS-symmetric
QMS, with exp(-tL) unital CP. Put

  s=sqrt(sigma), d=sigma^(1/4),
  Gamma(X)=s X s, H=Gamma^(1/2)L Gamma^(-1/2),
  delta=ad(log s),
  C=cosh(delta/2), A=(delta/2)/sinh(delta/2), A(0)=1.

These last operators are spectral functions on complex Hilbert-Schmidt space.
For EVERY matrix Y, including non-Hermitian Y,

  2 Re <H(CY),AY>_HS >= <Y,HY>_HS.                    (1)

Equivalently, C H A+A H C-H is positive semidefinite. Consequently, for every
Hermitian tangent Y with Tr(sY)=0 and a faithful density curve

  rho(epsilon)=sigma+epsilon(sY+Ys)+O(epsilon^2),

the quadratic coefficients of

  J(rho)=Tr[L_*(rho)(log rho-log sigma)],
  E(rho)=Tr[sqrt(rho) H(sqrt(rho))]

satisfy J_2>=2E_2. This applies to arbitrary untouched finite quantum references
with any faithful product stationary state, and does not require primitivity
or modular covariance. It is thus a complete stationary weak Hessian theorem.

2. Contractivity bridge: no GNS symmetry or modular covariance assumption
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write T_t=exp(-tL) and S_t=exp(-tH). Let

  Delta=L_s R_s^(-1)=exp(delta), D=Delta^(1/2)=exp(delta/2).

Here L_s and R_s denote left and right matrix multiplication. They commute and
are positive selfadjoint operators on complex Hilbert-Schmidt space. The
transformed map is S_t(Y)=d T_t(d^(-1)Y d^(-1)) d. KMS symmetry makes H
selfadjoint; unitality gives H(s)=0.

Kadison-Schwarz and sigma-stationarity give contraction of the GNS norm even
though the semigroup is not assumed GNS-symmetric:

  Tr[sigma T_t(X)^* T_t(X)]
    <=Tr[sigma T_t(X^*X)]=Tr[sigma X^*X].             (4)

With Y=dXd, direct cyclic trace calculation gives

  Tr[sigma X^*X]=Tr[s Y^* s^(-1)Y]
               =<Y,Delta^(-1)Y>_HS.

Consequently S_t^* Delta^(-1) S_t<=Delta^(-1). Since H is selfadjoint,
differentiating at t=0 yields

  H Delta^(-1)+Delta^(-1)H>=0.

Congruence by D gives the crucial positive operator

  H_tilde=(D H D^(-1)+D^(-1)H D)/2>=0.               (5)

This uses positivity and stationarity through (4), not commutation of H with
delta. Replacing the contractivity argument with an assumed modular covariance
would lose the legal KMS class and is unnecessary.

3. Schur product closes the operator inequality
-----------------------------------------------
Choose an orthonormal eigenbasis of delta on complex Hilbert-Schmidt space,
and denote its real eigenvalues x_i. Concretely the matrix units in an s
eigenbasis have x_(a,b)=log(s_a)-log(s_b). In this basis,

  (H_tilde)_ij=cosh((x_i-x_j)/2) H_ij,
  (C H A+A H C-H)_ij=f(x_i,x_j) H_ij.

Therefore the exact identity is

  C H A+A H C-H = H_tilde Schur [G(x_i,x_j)].         (6)

The first matrix in the Schur product is PSD by (5); the second is PSD by the
full Hardy kernel (2)-(3). The finite-dimensional Schur product theorem gives
nonnegativity of (6). Its quadratic form is exactly
2 Re <H(CY),AY>-<Y,HY>, proving (1) for all complex Y. This is an operator
argument with arbitrary multiplicities, not a scalar Bloch substitution.

4. Exact stationary expansion and constants
--------------------------------------------
For the tangent curve stated above, Frechet differentiation of the square root
gives sqrt(rho)=s+epsilon Y+O(epsilon^2). Put

  r=s^(-1/2)rho s^(-1/2),
  z=s^(1/2)(log rho-2log s)s^(1/2).

In an s eigenbasis,

  r=s+2epsilon C Y+O(epsilon^2),
  z=2epsilon A Y+O(epsilon^2).                         (7)

For the second identity, the log divided difference applied to (sY+Ys) has
coefficient 2(log s_i-log s_j)/(s_i-s_j); multiplication by sqrt(s_i s_j)
turns it into 2a_0(log s_i-log s_j). Diagonal and repeated-eigenvalue entries
are their continuous limits.

Since L_*=Gamma^(1/2)H Gamma^(-1/2), J=Tr[H(r)z]. Using H(s)=0 in (7),

  J(rho)=4epsilon^2 <H(CY),AY>+O(epsilon^3),
  E(rho)=epsilon^2 <Y,HY>+O(epsilon^3).                (8)

The relevant inner products are real for Hermitian Y because A,C are even
functions of delta and preserve adjoints. Combining (1) and (8) yields
J_2>=2E_2. No uncharged normalization term contributes: H(s)=0 makes every
second-order correction to sqrt(rho) disappear from E_2, and z(0)=0 and H(r(0))=0
make it disappear from J_2. Trace normalization restricts the tangent by
Tr(sY)=0, but (1) is stronger and holds without that restriction.

5. Quantum-reference scope and exact remaining nonlinear obligation
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Let the physical generator be id_R tensor L_B, with faithful product stationary
state tau_R tensor sigma_B. The corresponding weighted generator is
id_HS(R) tensor H_B, independently of tau_R. It is KMS-symmetric for that
product state. The log(tau_R) term in J vanishes because L_B(I_B)=0. Thus the
stationary expansion at EVERY faithful product tau_R tensor sigma_B satisfies
the complete Hessian theorem, for arbitrary joint Hermitian tangent and any
finite reference dimension. The proof actually applies to all local dimensions.

For the full nonlinear target, choose tau_R=I_R/dim(R), so s has the two local
qubit eigenvalues repeated. The known exact global noise gap still requires,
for every faithful joint rho and local Hermitian noise B=B_0 tensor I_R,

  F_B=Tr[BsBW]-Tr[BrBz]+2Tr[BqBq]>=0,
  q=sqrt(rho), r=s^(-1/2)q^2s^(-1/2),
  z=s^(1/2)(log q^2-2log s)s^(1/2),
  W=ell_s^(-1)((rz+zr)/2-2q^2).                       (9)

This stationary Schur factorization does not identify a corresponding PSD
factor for (9) away from rho=s^2. Integrating the stationary theorem along an
arbitrary state path would change the stationary density used in (4) while
leaving the physical generator fixed; that step is not justified. Neither the
qubit scalar Bloch log/root identities nor scalar angular concavity can be
applied to noncommuting reference blocks. No full nonlinear domination or
legal counterexample is asserted in this frozen baseline.
