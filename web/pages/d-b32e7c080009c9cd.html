# Finite-cycle QEC metrology: the channel-rank boundary

**Verdict.** A fixed, nonzero syndrome interval changes the asymptotic metrology class of the transverse-noise qubit at every nonzero operating frequency: the exact one-cycle channel has full Kraus span, so the optimal quantum Fisher information (QFI) is only linear in the number of cycles, even with arbitrary ancillas, feedback, and noiseless controls between cycles. There is a singular exception at exact resonance, where the channel loses rank and the channel-estimation criterion instead permits quadratic QFI. This is a theorem-level strengthening of a recent CSS-code calculation, but it is an application of established channel-estimation theory; novelty and priority are unverified.

## Target and Astra boundary

The selected mechanism is **finite-time quantum error correction for transverse-noise metrology**. It concerns whether finite-rate recovery preserves Heisenberg scaling in noisy spin sensing, a question with direct implications for magnetic-field sensing and spectroscopy.

The archive is a navigation and source-reported ledger, not proof evidence. No Astra card is used as a premise.

The fresh target is Liaubaitė, Ramacciotti, and Raußendorf, [*CSS codes for Quantum Metrology with Discrete-time Error Correction*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40022), arXiv v1 submitted 30 September 2026. I checked the live arXiv record on 8 October 2026: it still lists only v1, with no journal reference, so this input is a recent unrefereed preprint and its status can change. They study a fixed CSS code, logical-plus input, a binary stabilizer-projection readout, and ideal instantaneous recovery every interval \(\tau\). For a local \(Z\)-signal under independent transverse \(X\)-noise, they derive a finite Heisenberg-like window and an eventual SQL-like crossover for the Steane and Shor examples. Their per-cycle sensitivity envelope decays like

\[
 t\exp[-D_{1/2}\sqrt{\tau t}-D_1\tau t],
\]

with, at small \(\tau\), \((D_{1/2},D_1)\sim(9\gamma/(2\sqrt2),9\gamma^2)\) for Steane and \((\sqrt6\,\gamma/2,3\gamma^2)\) for Shor. Thus their fixed-code turnover is at time order \((\gamma^2\tau)^{-1}\). The 2021 Shettell et al. paper already studied finite-rate recovery and nonindefinite HL behavior in specified schemes; this report does not claim the finite-window observation as new.

## Exact channel and reconstructed result

Use the convention in the 2026 preprint: one sensing qubit evolves for a fixed \(\tau>0\) under

\[
\mathcal E_{\omega,\tau}=e^{\tau\mathcal L_\omega},\qquad
\mathcal L_\omega(\rho)=-i[\omega Z,\rho]+\gamma(X\rho X-\rho),\qquad \gamma>0.
\]

One channel use costs one exposure of duration \(\tau\). A \(Q\)-use protocol may entangle probes and ancillas and apply arbitrary parameter-independent quantum controls or recovery between uses. The environment is Markovian and resets between slots. The operating point \(\omega_0\) is treated locally, as in QFI metrology.

**Claim.** Let \(\mathfrak F_Q(\omega_0;\tau,\gamma)\) be the largest output QFI over all parallel or sequential/adaptive strategies using \(Q\) copies of \(\mathcal E_{\omega,\tau}\), with arbitrary noiseless ancillas. For every fixed \(\tau,\gamma>0\):

1. If \(\omega_0\ne0\), then \(\mathfrak F_Q=\Theta(Q)\). A finite constant is computable from the channel-extension semidefinite program at \((\omega_0,\tau,\gamma)\).
2. At \(\omega_0=0\), the Kraus-span signal has the component \(cZ\) outside the noise span, where
   \[
   c=\frac{1-e^{-2\gamma\tau}}{2\gamma}>0.
   \]
   The HNKS channel theorem therefore gives \(\mathfrak F_Q=\Theta(Q^2)\); with the paper's normalization the asymptotic coefficient is \(\lim_{Q\to\infty}\mathfrak F_Q/Q^2=4c^2\).

This is a **pointwise local** statement. The constant in the SQL regime is not uniform as \(\omega_0\to0\), and the quadratic statement at exactly zero does not imply a uniform Bayesian or minimax HL over an interval containing nonzero detunings.

### Proof of the Kraus-span statements

The quantum-jump Kraus density for ordered jump times \(0<t_1<\dots<t_m<\tau\) is

\[
K_m(\mathbf t)=e^{-\gamma\tau/2}\gamma^{m/2}
 e^{-i\omega Z(\tau-t_m)}X e^{-i\omega Z(t_m-t_{m-1})}\cdots X e^{-i\omega Zt_1},
\]

with the no-jump operator \(K_0=e^{-\gamma\tau/2}e^{-i\omega Z\tau}\). At a fixed nonzero \(\omega\), left multiplication by the inverse no-jump unitary leaves the Kraus operator span unchanged. The one-jump paths become

\[
e^{-\gamma\tau/2}\sqrt\gamma\,[\cos(2\omega s)X\ \pm\ \sin(2\omega s)Y],
\]

so varying \(s\) over any nonempty interval spans both \(X\) and \(Y\). A two-jump path with gap \(\Delta=t_2-t_1\) becomes a nonzero scalar multiple of \(e^{i2\omega\Delta Z}\); choosing \(\Delta\in(0,\tau)\) with \(\sin(2\omega\Delta)\ne0\) supplies the missing \(Z\) direction alongside \(I\). Each selected path has positive density on a neighborhood of jump times. Hence the Choi matrix is positive definite and the Kraus operators span \(M_2(\mathbb C)\).

Since \(K_0\) is invertible, \(\{K_i^\dagger K_0\}\) spans all of \(M_2\) as well. Thus the Hermitian Kraus-product span \(\mathcal S=\operatorname{span}_{\rm H}\{K_i^\dagger K_j\}\) is all Hermitian qubit operators. The signal tangent is necessarily in \(\mathcal S\), so the Hamiltonian-not-in-Kraus-span (HNKS) criterion fails. Zhou and Jiang's channel theorem then gives \(\Theta(Q)\) QFI for parallel and arbitrary sequential strategies. It also supplies a finite linear upper bound of the form

\[
\mathfrak F_Q\le 4Q\min_{h:\,\beta(h)=0}\|\alpha(h)\|,
\]

where \(h\) is a Kraus-gauge matrix, \(\beta(h)=iK^\dagger(\dot K-ihK)\), and \(\alpha(h)=(\dot K-ihK)^\dagger(\dot K-ihK)\). This coefficient is an SDP quantity; it was not numerically optimized here.

The tangent is nonzero at every \(\omega\ne0\), so the linear result is genuinely \(\Theta(Q)\), not just an upper bound. The Heisenberg-picture \(XY\) transfer block has the form

\[
e^{-\gamma\tau}\left[\cosh(\delta\tau)I_2+\frac{\sinh(\delta\tau)}{\delta}
\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&-2\omega\\2\omega&-\gamma\end{pmatrix}\right],\qquad \delta^2=\gamma^2-4\omega^2.
\]

For real \(\delta\), the derivative of its trace is nonzero when \(\omega\ne0\). For imaginary \(\delta=i\nu\), that trace derivative is nonzero unless \(\sin(\nu\tau)=0\); at such points the derivative of the off-diagonal entry \(-2\omega e^{-\gamma\tau}\sin(\nu\tau)/\nu\) is nonzero. The \(\delta\to0\) limit also has nonzero trace derivative. Thus the channel has positive one-use QFI at every nonzero operating point.

At \(\omega=0\), every jump trajectory is proportional to \(I\) for even \(m\) and \(X\) for odd \(m\). Consequently \(\mathcal S=\operatorname{span}_{\rm H}\{I,X\}\). Write the inter-jump gaps as \(\Delta_0,\dots,\Delta_m\), summing to \(\tau\). Direct differentiation gives

\[
iK_m^\dagger\partial_\omega K_m\big|_{0}
=e^{-\gamma\tau}\gamma^m\left(\Delta_0-\Delta_1+\cdots+(-1)^m\Delta_m\right)Z.
\]

Conditioned on \(m\) Poisson jumps, the gaps have the uniform Dirichlet distribution and mean \(\tau/(m+1)\) each. The alternating sum therefore averages to \(\tau/(m+1)\) for even \(m\) and zero for odd \(m\). Summing over the Poisson weights, with \(x=\gamma\tau\), yields

\[
i\sum_m\int K_m^\dagger\partial_\omega K_m\,d\mathbf t\big|_0
=\tau e^{-x}\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k+1)!}Z
=\frac{1-e^{-2\gamma\tau}}{2\gamma}Z=cZ.
\]

This is orthogonal to \(\operatorname{span}_{\rm H}\{I,X\}\), so HNKS holds at resonance. Its distance in operator norm from that span is exactly \(c\), giving the stated asymptotic coefficient under the HNKS theorem. The channel entries are analytic in \(\omega\). For a one-parameter analytic Hermitian Choi matrix, local analytic eigenvalue/eigenvector branches exist; because each eigenvalue is nonnegative on both sides of zero, a branch that vanishes there has even-order vanishing (or is identically zero). A signed analytic square root then gives a differentiable four-Kraus chart through the rank change. This fixed-length chart is nonminimal exactly at zero, but the channel-estimation proof uses a finite differentiable Kraus family and the HNKS space is invariant under Kraus representation, so the theorem applies pointwise at zero. The source states its theorem in rank-\(r\) notation and does not separately discuss a changing minimal Kraus rank; therefore this rank-change chart argument is our reconstructed bridge, not an independently reviewed extension. The trajectory-dilation value determines the gauge-invariant signal component modulo \(\mathcal S\).

## Bounded diagnostic and practical interface

The falsifier was set before the check: for \(\gamma,\tau>0\), a nonzero \(\omega\) point with a zero Choi eigenvalue would refute the claimed full-rank mechanism; at \(\omega=0\), any strictly positive fourth Choi eigenvalue would refute the rank-drop description.

I computed the exact matrix exponential of the 4-dimensional Liouvillian at 80 decimal digits, assembled the normalized Choi matrix, and diagonalized its realification with `mpmath`. At \(\gamma=\tau=0.1\), its eigenvalues were:

| Operating point | Normalized Choi eigenvalues |
|---|---|
| \(\omega=3\) | \(2.8311\times10^{-6},\ 2.9172\times10^{-4},\ 9.6089\times10^{-3},\ 0.9900965\) |
| \(\omega=0.1\) | \(3.2999\times10^{-9},\ 3.3001\times10^{-7},\ 0.0099003,\ 0.9900993\) |
| \(\omega=0\) | \(0,\ 0,\ 0.0099007,\ 0.9900993\) |

These three numerical points are diagnostics only; the proof is the trajectory-span argument above. The reproducible script is [finite_channel_check.py](finite_channel_check.py).

For a physical check, calibrate \(\gamma\) and fixed cycle time \(\tau\), reconstruct the single-cycle qubit process using an informationally complete set of preparations and Pauli measurements, and sweep the local operating detuning through zero. Estimate the Choi rank/eigenvalue floor and the channel-estimation SDP coefficient away from resonance, then compare multi-cycle QFI against the best available adaptive controls. The strongest mathematical comparator here is already the channel theorem, which optimizes over arbitrary controls and ancillas; code-specific CSS readout is weaker. This experiment requires local frequency calibration and control of cross-cycle environmental memory. It does not establish a useful global estimator by itself.

The accounting is deliberately optimistic: each channel use costs \(\tau\) sensing time, while error-correction gates, ancillas, resets, control work, and final measurements are noiseless and uncharged. Thus the nonzero-frequency SQL ceiling survives generous control assumptions. For the resonance HL, the asymptotic protocol needs increasing code/ancilla resources and high-quality controls; their qubit count, circuit depth, and energy are not included in the QFI coefficient. If the true \(\omega\) must be learned from scratch, acquisition of the local operating point and the singular nonuniformity around zero require a separate global-risk analysis.

## Status and next gate

The finite-step nonzero-frequency SQL claim is an internally reconstructed application of the published HNKS theorem to the specified Lindblad channel. The exact-resonance Kraus-span computation is reconstructed, and the same published theorem applies pointwise provided the analytic fixed-length Kraus chart argument above is accepted; that bridge has not received independent proof review. The cited arXiv record for the QEC target was current as v1 on 8 October 2026; the HNKS paper's current record is v3, last revised 3 April 2021, and published as PRX Quantum 2, 010343 (2021). The Choi script checks only three parameter points. No hardware data, optimal SDP coefficients, global estimator, or proof of priority was produced.

The decisive next gate is to write the finite four-Kraus chart explicitly and solve the channel-extension SDPs as \(\omega\to0\) and \(\tau\to0\). That would quantify the resource scale at which the SQL asymptotic regime becomes visible and separate the exact-resonance singularity from a merely finite-sample crossover. Primary literature already documents finite-rate QEC sensing, so any novelty claim must survive a direct comparison with [Shettell et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02823), the 2026 preprint, and other work on discrete-time recovery.
