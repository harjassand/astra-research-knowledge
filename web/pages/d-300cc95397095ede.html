## 1. Initialization and large-to-medium transfer

At initialization or transfer from large to medium, `d=0`, `q<=r^2`,
`r<=R`, and `G<=q`. The original barrier formula gives

`beta u <= -h0 R/(2r)+25/4`.

The PSD inequality `p p^T<=q diag(p)` yields, with `z=R/r>=1`,

`sum_sigma w p p^T`

` <= 2a(K/R^2) z^2 exp(25/4-h0 z/2) diag(p)`.

For `h0>=4`, the scalar factor decreases for `z>=1`, because its
logarithmic derivative is `2/z-h0/2<=0`. Inequality (I) therefore makes
the pair strictly smaller than the row's former diagonal contribution.
It also forces the exponent at `z=1` negative for all admitted choices
below, so both initial barriers are negative. Summing over rows gives
`W+D_L<=I` and `L<=1+1/K<8`, hence initial `Phi<=0`.

The explicit `h0>25/2` premise is necessary when arbitrarily tiny `a`
is allowed: (I) alone does not force negative barriers when
`2aK/R^2<1`. All choices certified here have `a>=12` and `h0>=19`.

## 3. Universal choices, with exact algebraic admission proofs

Because K is integer, `R^2<=K< (R+1)^2` implies
`K<=R^2+2R` and `K/R^2<=1+2/R<=5/4`.

**Choice A: `a=12`, `h0=20`, `K=32(k+1)`.** Here `R>=8`.

for e gives `e>8/3`, and `(8/3)^7>900`, so `e^(7/2)>30` and the
initialization inequality follows.

Reserve requires `K/k>13K/R^2+94/5`. Since
`k=(K-32)/32`, its difference equals

`32K/(K-32)-13K/R^2-94/5`.

At fixed R this decreases in K, so substitute its maximal possible

`1/5-26/R+1024/(R^2+2R-32)>0`.

Multiplying by the positive denominator `5R(R^2+2R-32)` leaves

`R[(R-64)^2+732]+4160>0`.

Hence Choice A works for every integer `k>=1`, with

`B_A=10R^2+2K+1<=384k+385`.

**Choice B: `a=32`, `h0=22`, `K=24(k+2)`.** This retains the original
Gram prefactor and has `K>=72`, `R>=8`. Initialization is bounded by

`R[(R-64)^2+1356]+6240>0`.

`B_B=11R^2+2K+1<=312k+625`.

**Choice C: `k=2`, `a=12`, `h0=19`, `K=64`, `R=8`.** The reserve
