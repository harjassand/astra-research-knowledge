# Continuum identification does not imply finite-cost single-current recovery

2026-10-06. Lead independently derived theorem; adversarial reconstruction by check_vector_measurements passed. This strengthens that worker's sharp deterministic Fisher comparison to a valid randomized-design minimax statement. The response family is abstract, compact, symmetric, positive and has infinite-rank secants and tangents. A continuum conductivity realization is not claimed; historical novelty is unresolved.

## Exact resource and source interface

Fix n>=2 and d=n(n+1)/2. The unknown A is a real symmetric n-by-n matrix with Frobenius norm at most R>0. Fix τ>0 with τR<1. Let H=Y=ℓ²(N;R^n). Known weights satisfy 1=w₁>=w₂>=…>0 and w_l→0. The supplied forward operator is

 F_A=⊕_{l>=1} w_l(I_n+τA).

A single current f=(f_l) has hard energy Σ_l||f_l||²<=E. It may be randomized, with its realized value revealed to the estimator; all random design variables are independent of A. A finite detector map P:Y→R^b has unit-norm rows, hence operator norm <=√b. Repeated or nonorthogonal tests are allowed, with independent noise for each acquired scalar. The restricted orthonormal-row interface has operator norm <=1 and gives a sharper version below. It may depend on the realized current and known model, but not A. Acquired observations are

 y=P F_A f+σz,  z~N(0,I_b),

with independent noise, fixed σ>0, and b<=B. This makes current energy, detector normalization and noise per acquired scalar explicit. Arbitrary noiseless full response is outside this finite data interface. No excitation overhead is assumed. Estimation loss is ||Ahat−A||_F².

Each F_A is compact, self-adjoint and strictly positive in quadratic-form sense. It is not coercive on the whole infinite-dimensional H. Every nonzero secant and tangent has infinite rank, since its nonzero matrix block recurs at infinitely many positive weights. Full-support Gaussian f has its first n block vectors spanning R^n almost surely; complete response, or exact first n blocks, then determines A. Normalizing to energy E preserves this spanning fact. Thus the Cycle1 continuum identification and all-moments result applies to this family. Nothing below contradicts it.

## Theorem: randomized single-current minimax lower bound

For every permitted single-current design and estimator,

 sup_{||A||_F<=R} E_design,noise ||Ahat−A||_F²
 >= R²(d−n)/d − 2τR³ w₂ sqrt(BE)/σ.                (1)

The right side can be replaced by its nonnegative part. It holds for every bank of at most B unit-norm tests, including repeated/nonorthogonal tests and a bank chosen using the realized random current. If rows are required to be orthonormal per current, replace √B by one throughout. More generally a certified operator gain ζ=||P|| gives the subtraction 2τR³w₂ζ√E/σ. The bound is deliberately conservative.

### Proof

Use a prior uniform on the Frobenius sphere ||A||_F=R in the d-dimensional Euclidean space Sym(n), independent of all design randomness. First condition on the entire realized design, fixing f and P.

Grant the statistic A f₁ exactly. The original finite observation is a garbling of the augmented experiment

 X=(A f₁, V),
 V=τ P_tail (w_l A f_l)_{l>=2}+σz,

where P_tail is the restriction of P to all output blocks after the first. Indeed the known baseline P(w_l f_l)_l and the known first-block signal τP₁ A f₁ can be added to V to reproduce y with the correct iid Gaussian noise. Granting exact first-block data can only decrease optimal risk.

Compare this with X₀=(A f₁, σz), in which V contains no parameter information. Its prior Bayes risk is at least R²(d−n)/d. For f₁!=0, the linear map A→A f₁ has rank n on Sym(n). Exact data determine the orthogonal projection of A onto its n-dimensional row space. Reflection symmetry of the spherical prior makes the conditional mean of the unseen component zero, and isotropy gives expected unseen squared norm R²(d−n)/d. If f₁=0, the risk is R², which is larger.

For every A in the prior support, the weak mean obeys

 ||τ P_tail(w_l A f_l)_{l>=2}||
 <=√B τ w₂ ||A||op (Σ_{l>=2}||f_l||²)^(1/2)
 <=τR w₂ sqrt(BE).

The joint prior-and-observation KL divergence between X and X₀ is therefore at most τ²R²w₂²BE/(2σ²). The first statistic has the same deterministic value under both experiments, and conditional Gaussian laws have equal covariance, so there is no singular-statistic ambiguity. Marginalizing A cannot increase divergence or total variation. Pinsker gives TV<=τR w₂sqrt(BE)/(2σ).

Project any estimator onto the Frobenius ball of radius R. This never increases squared error for admitted truths and makes loss at most4R². A bounded-loss expectation differs between the two joint experiments by at most4R² times their total variation. Consequently optimal Bayes risk for X is at least

 R²(d−n)/d−4R²·τR w₂sqrt(BE)/(2σ),

which is (1). The original observation has at least that risk. The same prior and bound hold for every conditioned design, so averaging over any known randomized current/detectors preserves the inequality. Bayes risk under a prior supported on the fixed parameter ball is a lower bound for frequentist minimax risk. The truth was never selected after seeing the current. QED.

Optional slightly sharper elementary constant: two equal-covariance Gaussian laws with mean difference μ have TV=2Φ(||μ||/(2σ))−1, by their likelihood-ratio half-space. Integrating this conditional bound over the prior yields risk at least R²(d−n)/d−4R²[2Φ(τRw₂sqrt(BE)/(2σ))−1], and Φ'<=1/sqrt(2π) gives subtraction4τR³w₂sqrt(BE)/(σsqrt(2π)). Equation(1) remains the independently checked conservative form.

## Matched optimized multiple-current construction

Use n deterministic currents supported only in block1:

 f_{j,1}=sqrt(E/n)e_j, all other blocks zero.

For each current read the n first-block coordinate voltages. Total energy E and acquired scalar count B=n². Assemble the data matrix

 Y=s(I+τA)+σZ,  s=sqrt(E/n),

with n² independent standard Gaussian entries in Z. The symmetrized estimator

 Ahat=[sym(Y)/s−I]/τ

has uniform risk

 E||Ahat−A||_F²=σ² n d/(τ²E).                       (2)

The diagonal noise contributes n and the symmetric off-diagonal noise contributes n(n−1)/2 to expected squared Frobenius norm, giving d. Projection onto the parameter ball can only reduce risk. This is a finite O(n³)-or-better linear algebra reconstruction, with no nonlinear optimizer or truth-dependent measurement selection.

The single-current lower bound holds with the same maximum B=n² readings and energy E; arbitrary finite unit-norm detector banks cannot overcome it. It is an advantage from reusing a strong physical response channel in separate experiments. The one-input continuum theorem uses distinct spatial blocks to obtain independent constraints, and those blocks can be weak.

## Explicit scalable separation

For n=2, d=3, R=1/2, τ=1, E=1, σ=1/100 and w₂=1/100000, (1) is

 single-current minimax risk >=1/12−1/2000 ≈0.08283333,

whereas (2) gives

 multiple-current risk <=6/10000=0.0006.

Both use at most four scalar readings and hard energy one. The single arm may choose arbitrary repeated/nonorthogonal unit tests; the displayed bound already charges their possible √B gain. The ratio of these certified bounds exceeds138. Other positive weights may decay arbitrarily, for example w_l=w₂·2^(−(l−2)) for l>=2; all continuum rank hypotheses remain true.

More generally choose a sequence σ→0 and w₂=σ² while keeping n,R,τ,E fixed. The one-current lower bound approaches the positive sphere-prior floor R²(d−n)/d, while the multiple-current upper bound decreases as σ². Thus no bound based only on model dimension, continuum infinite rank, fixed input power, and finite scalar count can guarantee competitive single-current noisy recovery uniformly over this admitted class. This is a family-wise nonuniformity conclusion; it does not assert a positive error floor as σ→0 for one fixed spectrum w₂>0.

## Sharp deterministic information criterion and remaining physical gap

The separately audited exact Fisher optimum is

 λ_min(I_single)=τ²E/[σ² Σ_{l=1}^n w_l^(−2)],
 λ_min(I_multi)=τ²E/(nσ²),

at the same n² reading budget. This quantifies the cost of independent one-input channels: one input must buy n linearly independent block vectors at spectral costs w_l^(−2); multiple inputs can reuse block1 n times. Infinite rank counts available channels, whereas these finite costs depend on their signal strengths and reuse rules.

The admitted operator family is not yet embedded in a scalar conductivity PDE. Therefore this refutes a generic inference from the Cycle1 rank theorem to finite-cost advantage, while leaving a PDE-specific theorem open. A constructive PDE result must bound finite normalized-secant channel strengths, input bandwidth, detector noise and acquisition costs with an equally informed optimized multi-current control.
