# C4 prior-art audit: controlled three-cycle alias and a shortest separator

## Verdict

The $d=0$ versus $d_*=4\pi/(\sqrt3\,\tau)$ construction is a direct specialization of the classical Markov embedding problem, including the repeated-positive-eigenvalue $3\times3$ case. It is not a new generator-identifiability phenomenon. The action-menu extension and one-gap separator are short consequences of the same Fourier calculation; I found no theorem in the cited literature that states this exact controlled-interface result as a package. The population-level shortest-word claim is correct for a stationary start and a common full-row-rank emission channel, with one correction: an extra gap breaks the alias when $ch/\tau\notin\mathbb Z$, but its pair skew is nonzero only when $2ch/\tau\notin\mathbb Z$.

## Exact calculation and boundary

Use row generators, let $C$ be the clockwise 3-cycle permutation matrix, $\Pi=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/3$, and $J=(C-C^\top)/\sqrt3$. Then $J^\top=-J$, $J^2=-(I-\Pi)$, and

\[
Q(s,d)=\frac{s}{2}(C+C^\top-2I)+\frac{d}{2}(C-C^\top),\qquad
e^{tQ(s,d)}=\Pi+e^{-3st/2}\{\cos(\theta)(I-\Pi)+\sin(\theta)J\},
\quad \theta=\frac{\sqrt3dt}{2}.
\]

At $d=d_*$ and $t=c_a\tau$, $\theta=2\pi c_a$. For every integer multiplier $c_a$, the transition matrix equals the $d=0$ matrix. Both generators are doubly stochastic, so the same stationary initial law is $\pi=\mathbf1/3$. With shared emissions $E$, factorization of any finite action/output word uses only these identical action kernels, the same $E$, and the same initial law. Induction therefore gives equality of the complete output laws for every fixed or adaptive action policy, including resettable episodes. No action-word learner can separate them with this interface, even with unlimited exact population access.

This is not an impossibility for richer interfaces: observing a jump time, querying arbitrary real durations, allowing a noninteger multiplier at the base interval, or measuring a gap not aliased by the phase can separate this pair. Positive rates in both worlds require $s>d_*$; for a whole unknown interval $[s_{\min},s_{\max}]$, require $s_{\min}>d_*$. This rate condition is necessary.

The strongest parameter-box counterattack is immediate: if a supplied bound has $s_{\max}\le d_*$, the proposed second positive-rate world is outside the class. Conversely, the alias requires the high-mixing regime: $\lambda=e^{-3s\tau/2}<e^{-2\pi\sqrt3}$. It lies well outside standard near-identity uniqueness conditions such as the sufficient $\min_i M_{ii}>1/2$ criterion reviewed in Baake–Sumner, §2, Theorem 2.14. A single fixed-lag alias theorem should therefore state its rate-box condition.

For an extra gap $h$ under multiplier $c$, set $\theta=2\pi ch/\tau$. One stationary output has law $\pi E$ in both worlds. If $E\in\mathbb R^{3\times m}$ has full row rank, the two-output law is

\[
J_d=\frac13 E^\top e^{hcQ(s,d)}E.
\]

Because $E$ has a right inverse, $E^\top(P_d-P_0)E=0$ implies $P_d=P_0$. Thus the pair laws differ exactly when $ch/\tau\notin\mathbb Z$. Since one observation never distinguishes the stationary marginals, two emissions with one such gap are the shortest population-level sampling word. A direct skew statistic is more restrictive:

\[
J_d-J_d^\top=\frac{2}{3}e^{-3sch/2}\sin(\theta)\,E^\top J E.
\]

It is nonzero iff $2ch/\tau\notin\mathbb Z$. For example, $h/\tau=1/2,c=1$ breaks the matrix alias (phase $\pi$) but produces a symmetric pair law, so a non-integer gap alone does not guarantee pair skew. If noncommensurate means irrational $h/\tau$, this issue disappears. A finite-sample guarantee also needs quantitative margins: if $\sigma_{\min}(E)\ge\kappa>0$, then pair-law TV separation is at least

\[
\frac{\sqrt2}{3}\,e^{-3sch/2}\kappa^2\,|\sin(\pi ch/\tau)|.
\]

So full rank alone gives pointwise separation, not a uniform sample bound; the margin collapses as $E$ approaches rank deficiency, as the phase approaches an integer, or as $sh$ grows. For a compact rate interval and fixed $h,c$ away from integer phase, this lower bound supplies a uniform margin once a lower singular-value bound is supplied.

**Matched word control and costs.** With two emissions and the same initial stationary law, two samples at the base gap $\tau$ remain exactly identical under both worlds, even with direct hidden-state visibility; two emissions with an allowed alias-breaking $h$ suffice at the population level. Thus the separator gains information from the gap, not from word length or a larger action search. Its basic episode uses one reset/start, one controlled gap, and two emissions; a finite-sample test needs independent episodes and a declared TV margin. The stated model supplies no lower bound on emission conditioning or phase margin, so no uniform sample, precision, runtime, or storage guarantee follows from the word claim alone.

## Primary literature: what it does and does not establish

- **Exact embedding prior art.** Baake and Sumner, [*Embedding of Markov matrices for $d\le4$*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-024-02112-w), §3.1, publisher HTML lines 350–382, treats a 3-state Markov matrix with Jordan form $\operatorname{diag}(1,\lambda,\lambda)$, $0<\lambda<1$. Lemma 3.1 says it is embeddable and further solutions may exist; Remark 3.3 enumerates the nonprincipal branch candidates and gives $|k|\le|\log\lambda|/(2\pi\sqrt3)$. Our endpoint matrix is $P_\tau=\Pi+\lambda(I-\Pi)$, $\lambda=e^{-3s\tau/2}$; the principal generator is the $k=0$ branch and the shifted pair uses conjugate branch indices $+1,-1$ (one branch-pair parameter $|k|=1$). The condition $s>d_*$ allows this branch pair, and the explicit positive rates verify it is a generator. This is the closest exact primary classification. The source explains the repeated eigenspace and branch mechanism; the displayed rate pair follows by choosing the circulant/Fourier basis.
- Casanellas, Fernández-Sánchez, and Roca-Lacostena, [*The embedding problem for Markov matrices*](https://doi.org/10.5565/PUBLMAT6712308), Theorem 4.5 (arXiv HTML lines 263–287), lists all generators for matrices with pairwise distinct eigenvalues. It is relevant general machinery, but it does not directly cover this fixture because $P_\tau$ has repeated eigenvalue $\lambda,\lambda$. Do not cite that theorem alone as the exact result here.
- **HMM identifiability.** Gassiat, Cleynen, and Robin, [*Finite state space non parametric Hidden Markov Models are in general identifiable*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657), Theorem 2.1 (arXiv HTML lines 73–79), assumes known state count, linearly independent emission distributions, and a full-rank stationary latent transition matrix; it identifies the transition matrix and emissions from three consecutive observations, up to state permutation. Every $e^{hcQ}$ here is invertible, so this theorem applies to the sampled HMM at a fixed gap. It identifies the discrete skeleton $P_{hc}$, not a unique continuous-time generator. At $\tau$, both worlds have the same skeleton, so HMM identifiability cannot remove the embedding alias. At an alias-breaking $h$, it supplies a standard route to recovering $P_h$; the two-sample common-$E$ argument above is a sharper pairwise distinguisher, not a general unknown-HMM factorization theorem.
- **Fixed versus random observation times.** Duffie and Glynn, [*Estimation of Continuous-Time Markov Processes Sampled at Random Time Intervals*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00553.x), *Econometrica* 72 (2004), pp. 1773–1808, state on pp. 1784–1785 (PDF lines 472–500) that fixed deterministic sampling can alias different CTMCs; Proposition 4 gives identification under their random-arrival assumptions, including parameter-independent arrival intensity. This is prior art for the fixed-grid obstruction and a richer random-time cure. It does not state the exact theorem that one prescribed second deterministic gap separates this cycle pair; that fact follows directly from the phase formula.
- **Active automata.** Angluin, [*Learning Regular Sets from Queries and Counterexamples*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401(87)90052-6), §1.1 (1987, pp. 88–89; PDF lines 45–58), defines membership and equivalence queries with counterexamples. This is not a result for continuous-time generators or stochastic emissions. When every available action kernel is identical in the two worlds, their complete word laws coincide, so no membership/equivalence or adaptive action-word strategy has a distinguishing counterexample. That is a direct interface-equivalence argument, not a new active-automata theorem.

## Claim disposition

| Claim | C4 disposition |
|---|---|
| Distinct CTMC generators can share a fixed-lag transition matrix | **Established prior art:** Markov embedding; exact repeated-positive 3-state case in Baake–Sumner §3.1 Lemma 3.1/Remark 3.3. |
| Integer rate multipliers at the same $\tau$ preserve the alias for all adaptive words | **Direct derivation:** every available action kernel is equal; stronger than passive same-law and independent of sample size. No separate controlled-action theorem is needed. |
| One non-integer-ratio gap $h/\tau\notin\mathbb Z$ separates stationary pair laws under common full-rank $E$ | **Direct derivation:** true at population level; requires $s>d_*$, shared stationary start/emissions, and full row rank. |
| That condition always produces nonzero observed pair skew | **Refuted as stated:** need $2ch/\tau\notin\mathbb Z$; at half-integer phase, the pair law still separates but is symmetric. |
| Two output samples are minimum | **Survives with scope:** for stationary-start output words, a one-sample marginal is invariant and a single alias-breaking gap gives a two-emission separator. With known pure hidden-state initialization, the one-sample lower bound does not apply. |
| Full-rank $E$ alone gives a uniform finite-sample query count | **Refuted:** no lower bound on $\sigma_{\min}(E)$, phase margin, or $sh$ separation is supplied. |

This is a focused literature check, not an exhaustive priority search. No experiments or finite-sample power simulation were needed for the exact population-law claims above.
