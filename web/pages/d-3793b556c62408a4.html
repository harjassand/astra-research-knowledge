2. Exact exchange form, positivity, and compact profile space
------------------------------------------------------------
Configurations X are subsets of {1,...,S}. X~Y means one permitted
nearest-neighbor exclusion hop. The exact quadratic form is

 D(psi)=2J sum_{unordered X~Y}|psi(X)-psi(Y)|^2
                +2J sum_X (n_1(X)+n_S(X))|psi(X)|^2.     (10)

Replacing psi by |psi| leaves every diagonal observable and Pi_L unchanged
and cannot increase D. Thus every lower-bound argument may use nonnegative
real psi. Its exchange-only form is

 D_ex(psi)=2J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}<psi,(I-P_{i,i+1})psi>.

For a swap P, <I-P>=sum over its unordered nontrivial orbit edges of
|psi(X)-psi(PX)|^2, so this agrees with (10). Write
e_i=<psi,(I-P_{i,i+1})psi>; then sum_i e_i<=D(psi)/(2J).

The compact space M consists of measures mu on [0,1] satisfying 0<=mu<=dx,
with weak topology. It is closed in the compact finite-measure space with
mass<=1. Every r_X dx lies in M, so the probability laws Pi_L are tight.
Every limiting measure has a unique L^infinity density rho, 0<=rho<=1,
modulo null sets. Box averages and the functionals used below are Borel.

3. Complete-graph projections and the moving-particle estimate
-------------------------------------------------------------
For a set U of M distinct sites let P_U be the orthogonal projection onto
the fully permutation-symmetric spin subspace on U, acting as identity
outside U. Let Q_U=I-P_U. The exact spin identity is

 sum_{i<j in U}(I-P_ij)
   =(M/2)(M/2+1)-T_U(T_U+1)>=M Q_U.                     (11)

Here T_U is total spin; the first nonmaximum spin M/2-1 has deficit M.
Thus for a normalized psi

 p_U:=<psi,Q_U psi>
             <=M^{-1}sum_{i<j in U}<psi,(I-P_ij)psi>.    (12)

For i<j, write their transposition as an adjacent-swap word of length
2(j-i)-1, with each bond appearing at most twice. Telescoping the unitary
word and Cauchy--Schwarz gives the exact form inequality

 <I-P_ij><=4(j-i)sum_{k=i}^{j-1}<I-P_{k,k+1}>.           (13)

Indeed ||(I-P)psi||^2=2<I-P>, and unitarity of each prefix removes it
from the norm. This is the same elementary moving-particle estimate used
in sol_marginal_xxz §3; no invariance of psi under the prefixes is needed.

Let A be any self-adjoint diagonal observable supported on U with ||A||<=1.
Then

 |<psi,A psi>-<P_U psi,A P_U psi>|<=2 sqrt(p_U)+p_U
                                                    <=3 sqrt(p_U). (14)

All these statements also hold for unnormalized u, weight w=||u||^2,
with the error 2sqrt(w<u,Q_Uu>)+<u,Q_Uu><=3sqrt(w<u,Q_Uu>).
This unnormalized version will matter for arbitrarily small localization
weights in Section 6.

4. One-block contact replacement, with an explicit energy bound
---------------------------------------------------------------
Fix an integer ell>=2. For B_i={i,...,i+ell-1} inside the chain put
K_i=sum_{j in B_i}n_j and r_ell(i)=K_i/ell. Symmetry within the block gives

 P_B n_i n_{i+1}P_B
               =K_i(K_i-1)/[ell(ell-1)] P_B,
 P_B r_ell(i)^2 P_B=(K_i^2/ell^2)P_B.                   (15)

Their coefficient difference has magnitude at most 1/(ell-1). The exact
expression is -K_i(ell-K_i)/[ell^2(ell-1)]. Apply (14) to their difference,
whose norm is <=1. Then

 |<n_i n_{i+1}>-<r_ell(i)^2>|
                   <=1/(ell-1)+3 sqrt(p_{B_i}).         (16)

At a bond k in a length-ell block,
sum_{a<=k<b}(b-a)=ell a(ell-a)/2<=ell^3/8. Combining (12)--(13) yields

 p_{B_i}<=(ell^2/2)sum_{k in B_i's bonds} e_k.

Each bond belongs to at most ell such windows. Therefore

 (1/L)sum_i p_{B_i}
              <=ell^3 D(psi)/(4J L).                    (17)

Consequently, for any bounded deterministic weights V_i,

 |(1/L)sum_i V_i[<n_i n_{i+1}>-<r_ell(i)^2>]|
 <=||V||_infinity[1/(ell-1)
          +3 sqrt(ell^3 D(psi)/(4J L))+O(ell/L)].        (18)

The O(ell/L) only covers incomplete boundary windows; all observables there
are bounded by one. In the normalized low-energy regime (5), the square-root
error is O_C(ell^(3/2)/L). For unnormalized u, every 1/(ell-1) and boundary
term is multiplied by w, and the square root becomes
3sqrt(w ell^3 D(u)/(4J L)). Summing over a finite family u_alpha with
sum||u_alpha||^2=1 and sum D(u_alpha)<=C'/L gives the same bound, by
sum sqrt(w_alpha D(u_alpha))<=sqrt(sum D(u_alpha)). This fact avoids a
hidden division by a rare localization probability.

5. Two-block variance and mesoscopic replacement
------------------------------------------------
Let B_i and B_{i+d} be disjoint length-ell windows, ell<=d<=a, and
U=B_i union B_{i+d}. In the maximum-spin subspace on U, conditioned on the
total particle count K, the split is hypergeometric. Its exact variance is

 P_U [r_ell(i)-r_ell(i+d)]^2 P_U
       =[4p(1-p)/(2ell-1)]P_U<=P_U/(2ell-1),
                    p=K/(2ell).                         (19)

The squared difference has norm<=1. Thus (14) implies

 <[r_ell(i)-r_ell(i+d)]^2>
                    <=1/(2ell-1)+3sqrt(p_U).             (20)

The diameter of U is at most a+ell. There are at most 2ell^2 pairs in U.
Equations (12)--(13), followed by averaging over translations i, give

 p_U<=4ell(a+ell)sum_{k in its span}e_k,
 (1/L)sum_i p_U
                <=4ell(a+ell)^2 D(psi)/(2J L).           (21)

Each span contains a given bond for at most a+ell translations. For fixed
d the translation-averaged version of (20) is therefore bounded by

 1/(2ell-1)+3sqrt[2ell(a+ell)^2 D(psi)/(J L)].            (22)

In (5) this is 1/(2ell-1)+3sqrt(2C/J)sqrt(ell)(a+ell)/L.
The same weighted bound holds on summing unnormalized localized pieces:
their masses sum to one and their total D replaces D(psi).

Set a=floor(delta L), with fixed 0<delta<1/4, and require a>=ell, which
holds for all sufficiently large L at fixed ell,delta. Extend n_j=0 off
the chain, INCLUDING n_0=0. Define the forward average
r_a(i)=a^{-1}sum_{j=i}^{i+a-1}n_j for i=0,...,S. Its step function uses
EVERY cell [i/L,(i+1)/L), including the first cell at i=0. The average
of r_ell(i+d), d=0,...,a-1, differs from r_a(i) by at most 2ell/a, pointwise.
Jensen bounds its squared difference from r_ell(i) by the average of the
two-block squared differences. The ell overlapping separations contribute
at most ell/a; omitted boundary windows contribute O((a+ell)/L). Thus

 (1/L)sum_i <|r_ell(i)-r_a(i)|^2>
 <=2/(2ell-1)+6sqrt[2ell(a+ell)^2 D(psi)/(J L)]
             +2ell/a+8ell^2/a^2+O((a+ell)/L).            (23)

The bound is deliberately nonsharp; its limit order is decisive. The
contact difference between r_ell^2 and r_a^2 is at most twice their absolute
difference, so (18), (23), and Cauchy--Schwarz imply, under (5),

 limsup_L |(1/L)sum_i V_i[<n_i n_{i+1}>-<r_a(i)^2>]|
 <=||V||_infinity [1/(ell-1)
       +2sqrt(2/(2ell-1)+6sqrt(2C/J)sqrt(ell)delta+O(delta))
       +O(delta)].                                     (24)

First take L->infinity at fixed ell,delta. Then take, for example,
ell=ceil(delta^(-2/3)) and delta->0. The right side tends to zero (an
O_C(delta^(1/3)) bound is available). No elliptic regularity, entropy bound,
source transition matrix, or independently sampled configuration is supplied
to this replacement lemma. It follows solely from exchange energy (5).

For a measure mu=rho dx in M write

 rho_delta(x)=delta^{-1}mu([x,x+delta]),

with zero extension off [0,1]. The grid averages r_a(i), extended as step
functions, differ uniformly by O(1/(delta L)) from this forward box average
of r_X dx. At fixed delta, mu->rho_delta is continuous from M's weak topology
to uniform topology: distribution functions converge uniformly, since they
are all 1-Lipschitz. Consequently along Pi_L->Pi,

 E_{Pi_L}integral V r_a^2 -> E_Pi integral V rho_delta^2.  (25)

As delta->0, rho_delta->rho in L^2 for every bounded density. Dominated
convergence over Pi gives E_Pi integral V rho_delta^2->E_Pi integral V rho^2.
Equations (24)--(25) prove (7). Linear occupations follow directly by weak
convergence. They prove the same contact statement with a finite family
of unnormalized localized pieces whenever the masses sum to one, their
total exchange energy is O(1/L), and their limiting empirical laws exist.

6. Softmax IMS localization without a leading gradient loss
-----------------------------------------------------------
Fix finitely many fields phi_alpha in C^1([0,1]), including phi_0=0, and a
fixed M>0. For a bounded density r define

 F_alpha(r)=2J[-integral r phi_alpha'
                  -integral r(1-r)phi_alpha^2].          (26)

For fixed delta and a=floor(delta L), use the step function r_a(X) and put

 p_alpha,L(X)=exp[M F_alpha(r_a(X))]
                      /sum_beta exp[M F_beta(r_a(X))],
 chi_alpha,L(X)=sqrt(p_alpha,L(X)),
 u_alpha(X)=chi_alpha,L(X)psi(X).                        (27)

The chi form a real diagonal partition of unity: sum chi_alpha^2=1.
If X,Y differ by one hop, their forward a-window counts differ at ONLY
two window starts (a window that ends at the departing site and a window
that starts at the arrival site). Thus

 ||r_a(X)-r_a(Y)||_{L^1(0,1)}<=2/(a L).                  (28)

The function r(1-r) is 1-Lipschitz on [0,1], so each F_alpha changes by
O_{fields}(1/(aL)). Square-root softmax has bounded derivatives for fixed
M and the finite field family; therefore

 sum_alpha|chi_alpha(X)-chi_alpha(Y)|^2
                     <=C_{fields,M,delta} L^(-4).       (29)

The finite graph IMS identity is EXACT for nonnegative psi:

 sum_alpha D(u_alpha)=D(psi)
    +2J sum_{X~Y}psi(X)psi(Y)
                         sum_alpha|chi_alpha(X)-chi_alpha(Y)|^2. (30)

The endpoint diagonal terms do not change. Bound psi(X)psi(Y) by half
their squared sum. Every configuration has at most S-1<=L allowed hops,
so (29)--(30) give

 0<=sum_alpha D(u_alpha)-D(psi)<=C_{fields,M,delta}L^-3.  (31)

Thus the total localized energy remains C'/L in (5), and multiplying the
localization error by L makes it vanish. This is why a hard projection of
large blocks, which discards or changes boundary gradients, is unnecessary.
All delta,M,field choices are held fixed while L grows.

At fixed delta the limiting weights are continuous functions of mu in M:

 p_alpha,delta(rho)=softmax_alpha[M F_alpha(rho_delta)].

The grid functionals converge uniformly to these continuous functionals,
with O_{fields}(1/(delta L)) error. Hence the empirical law for |u_alpha|^2
converges to the SUBPROBABILITY measure p_alpha,delta(rho)Pi(d rho).
No piece is divided by its weight; the unnormalized bounds in Sections4--5
apply to all pieces together, even if an individual weight tends to zero.

7. Exact positive test function and the random-profile kinetic liminf
---------------------------------------------------------------------
For phi in C^1([0,1]) take h(x)=integral_0^x phi(t)dt and

 f_h(X)=exp[sum_i h(i/L)n_i(X)]>0,
 d_i=h((i+1)/L)-h(i/L).

The finite positive-ground-function identity gives, for every real u,

 D(u)>=sum_X |u(X)|^2 (Hcrit f_h)(X)/f_h(X),             (32)

because the discarded form is 2J sum f_h(X)f_h(Y)|u(X)/f_h(X)-
u(Y)/f_h(Y)|^2. The exact quotient is

 (Hcrit f_h)/f_h
 =2J sum_i [n_i(1-n_{i+1})(1-exp(d_i))
            +n_{i+1}(1-n_i)(1-exp(-d_i))]
                      +2J(n_1+n_S).                    (33)

Expand at fixed h, with d_i=O(1/L):

 (Hcrit f_h)/f_h
 =-2J sum_i d_i(n_i-n_{i+1})
     -J sum_i d_i^2(n_i+n_{i+1}-2n_i n_{i+1})
       +2J(n_1+n_S)+O_h(L^-2).                          (34)

The remainder is uniform over configurations, since sum|d_i|^3=O_h(L^-2).
After multiplying by L, summation by parts in the first term gives

 -2J L sum_i d_i(n_i-n_{i+1})
   =-2J integral r_X phi'+o_h(1)
                       +O_h(n_1+n_S).                  (35)

This uses the uniform finite-difference convergence to h''=phi', and the
two endpoint coefficients Ld_1,Ld_{S-1} remain O_h(1). Under (5), the total
endpoint occupation has expectation <=D(psi)/(2J)=O(1/L). The same holds
on summing localized pieces, because their endpoint masses sum exactly to
the original mass. Thus the endpoint error in (35) vanishes. Dropping the
positive endpoint term in (34) is legitimate, but its energy bound is
retained for this boundary-error control.

The squared-difference term in (34), after multiplication by L, is a
weighted linear occupation minus twice a weighted contact term. Apply
the replacement proved in Section5, including its unnormalized summed
version. For the pieces u_alpha, use their own field phi_alpha. Equations
(31)--(35), with Pi_L->Pi, yield at each fixed delta,M,finite field family,

 liminf L D(psi_L)
       >=E_Pi sum_alpha p_alpha,delta(rho)F_alpha(rho).   (36)

The local contact replacement here is at an independent small scale
epsilon->0 AFTER L; delta stays fixed in the localization. This distinction
prevents treating a fixed smoothed density as the microscopic contact law.
The limiting subprobabilities are p_alpha,delta Pi, and then the replacement
returns their actual rho^2. Its total-energy bounds are uniform over the
finite pieces, without a lower bound on any piece's probability.

Now let delta->0 at fixed M and fields. Each rho_delta->rho in L^2, so
F_alpha(rho_delta)->F_alpha(rho), and dominated convergence gives

 liminf L D >=E_Pi sum_alpha p_alpha(rho)F_alpha(rho),
       p_alpha(rho)=softmax_alpha[M F_alpha(rho)].       (37)

For any finite numbers F_alpha, their softmax-weighted mean obeys

 sum_alpha p_alpha F_alpha >=max_alpha F_alpha-log(K)/M, (38)

where K is the number of fields. This is the entropy identity for logsumexp;
it does not require positivity of the F_alpha. Taking M->infinity gives

 liminf L D >=E_Pi max_alpha F_alpha(rho).               (39)

Finally choose a countable C^1-dense family (e.g. rational-coefficient
polynomials), include zero, and increase finite prefixes. Each F is
continuous in the C^1 norm uniformly over 0<=rho<=1. Thus the increasing
maxima have supremum I in (8). Monotone convergence proves (6).

This is the load-bearing sup/expectation step. A single fixed h would give

8. Identification of the dual: Fisher energy and exact Dirichlet traces
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Let chi(rho)=rho(1-rho), 0<=rho<=1, and assume I(rho)<infinity. Put

 ell(phi)=-integral rho phi', Q(phi)=integral chi(rho)phi^2.

Scaling phi by every real t in (8) shows

 |ell(phi)|^2<=(2 I/J)Q(phi).                            (40)

When Q=0, finiteness forces ell=0. C^1 is dense in L^2(chi dx), so ell
extends continuously there. Riesz gives g in L^2(chi dx) with

 -integral rho phi'=integral chi g phi, all phi in C^1.  (41)

On compactly supported tests this means rho'=chi g distributionally.
Since chi g is integrable, rho has an absolutely continuous representative
on [0,1]. Integration by parts in (41), now for arbitrary endpoint values
of phi, forces BOTH traces rho(0)=rho(1)=0. These boundary traces are not
assumed, and tests are not restricted to compact support. Restricting to
compactly supported h would create a potentially weaker mean-current dual.

Completing the square and using density of C^1 gives the exact value

 I(rho)=(J/2)integral chi g^2
       =(J/2)integral rho'^2/[rho(1-rho)].               (42)

Use zero for the integrand on level sets rho=0 or1, where rho'=0 a.e.
To justify theta=2arcsin sqrt(rho) in H^1, regularize
rho_epsilon=epsilon+(1-2epsilon)rho, 0<epsilon<1/2. Since

 chi(rho_epsilon)=epsilon(1-epsilon)+(1-2epsilon)^2chi(rho),

theta_epsilon=2arcsin sqrt(rho_epsilon) has derivative squared <=rho'^2/chi.
The uniform H^1 bound and pointwise convergence give theta in H^1. The
boundary values theta_epsilon(0)=theta_epsilon(1)=2arcsin sqrt(epsilon)
tend to zero, hence theta in H^1_0. On 0<rho<1,
theta'=rho'/sqrt(chi); on the two level sets theta'=0 a.e. This proves (9).

Conversely every theta in H^1_0, 0<=theta<=pi gives rho=sin^2(theta/2),
rho'=sqrt(chi)theta', zero traces, and the completion-of-squares supremum
in (8) equals (J/2)integral theta'^2. Thus (8)--(9) are an equivalence,
including saturated plateaus and vacuum, without a positive density floor.

The bound in (6) implies E_Pi I<=C, so I is finite Pi-almost surely. It
therefore identifies the entire random-profile law's domain, not just its

9. Ground-energy liminf and coherent recovery
----------------------------------------------
Vacuum gives E_min,L<=0. Since W<=N<=L-1, an approximate ground vector
has D_L<=C/L with C fixed on compact kappa,lambda sets. Replace it by its

and the exact linear/contact limits imply

 liminf L E_min,L
 >=E_Pi[I(rho)-kappa integral rho^2-lambda integral rho]
 >=e_*(kappa,lambda).                                   (43)

For the reverse bound first use a smooth real theta in H^1_0 with values
in [0,pi]. Take the product state with on-site spinor

 cos(theta(i/L)/2)|0>+sin(theta(i/L)/2)|1>.

Its exact exchange-bond expectation is J[1-cos(theta((i+1)/L)-theta(i/L))].
Riemann sums give L times the exchange energy ->(J/2)integral theta'^2.
The two endpoint occupations are O_theta(L^-2), so their L-scaled killing
energy vanishes. The product occupations give N/L->integral rho and
W/L->integral rho^2. Therefore

 L<psi_theta,L,H_L psi_theta,L>->E_{kappa,lambda}(theta). (44)

These product states also have empirical law concentrating on rho dx:
each bounded continuous linear empirical observable has variance O(1/L),
and a countable determining family gives weak concentration. This supplies
point-profile recovery for the random-law lower bound, not an assumed
classical representation of arbitrary quantum states.

Smooth constrained Dirichlet profiles are dense for the energy in (2).
For example extend theta by zero, mollify with a nonnegative kernel, and
use a smooth cutoff near the boundary; H^1 convergence controls energy
and 0<=theta<=pi can be retained. Apply (44) to a fixed approximation and
then a diagonal sequence. Variational min-max proves limsup<=e_*, and (43)
proves (3). Calling this a ground-energy Gamma argument means the precise
liminf-plus-point-recovery statements above; a separate arbitrary-mixture
pure-state recovery is not needed or claimed here.

Both L E_min,L and e_* are Lipschitz in kappa,lambda with respective
constants <=1, because W/L,N/L and integral rho^2,integral rho are <=1.
The finite-net argument extends (3) uniformly over compact sets, and also
allows kappa_L->kappa,lambda_L->lambda within a bounded set.
