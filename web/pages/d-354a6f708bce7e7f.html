INDEPENDENT TAIL-UPPER AND ENDPOINT AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: quantum_tail_precision.txt §§6–7 only.
Provenance: separate audit from tail_upper.txt; that file was left untouched.

VERDICT

I find no error in the §6 filter tail bound, its Rademacher moment constant
M(c1), or the Hoeffding exponent. The exact branch cutoff is Q=n+1, while
Q=n still tapers the top L=n sector. The endpoint conversion is valid with
constants depending on c0,c1. For clarity, write an explicit integer
ceiling, establish that the log is positive on the claimed small-error
range, and use the exact c1-scaled transition points for the unknown-radius
ball. These points differ from n^(-1), n^(-1/2) by fixed factors; the
canonical displayed orders remain valid up to envelope-dependent
constants.

1. INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE §6 PER-SPIN AND TAIL STEPS

Put t=atanh(r), beta=2t, and L=2j. From the whole-state estimate (10.4),
on a nonidentity branch Q<d=L+1,

  e_j <= (3/(2Q))(beta+beta^2 j/3)e^(beta j)
       = (3t+t^2 L)e^(tL)/Q.

For r≤1/2, t≤4r/3, so 3t≤4r and t^2L≤(16/9)r^2L≤4r^2L. Hence

  e_j <= (4r/Q)(1+rL)e^(tL).                              (1.1)

Branches with L+1≤Q use the identity channel and contribute zero. Since
L,Q are integers, L+1>Q implies L≥Q. Multiplication by the stated Schur
upper weight (11.1) yields the coefficient 8r/(Qn), the polynomial
(L+1)^2(1+rL), and the exponential e^(2tL). The unbiased Rademacher sum
X has exactly the pi_0 parity law on L; restricting to X≥Q therefore
produces (6.1) without a parity or factor-of-two loss. On this tail the
integrand is nonnegative and equals Y=(|X|+1)^2(1+r|X|)e^(2tX).

2. MOMENT CONSTANT AND HOEFFDING EXPONENT

The envelope inequality is

  (|X|+1)^4(1+r|X|)^2
    <=16(X^4+1+r^2X^6+r^2X^2).

The square of Y has exponential e^(4tX). For any p≥1, Cauchy–Schwarz
and the Rademacher mgf give

  E[X^(2p)e^(4tX)]
    <=sqrt(E X^(4p) E e^(8tX))
    <=sqrt((4p-1)!! n^(2p)) e^(16nt^2).

Here E e^(uX)=(cosh u)^n≤e^(nu^2/2), and coefficient comparison also
gives E X^(2k)≤(2k-1)!!n^k. The required even moments are
E X^8≤105n^4, E X^12≤10395n^6, and E X^4≤3n^2. For the constant term,
E e^(4tX)≤e^(8nt^2)≤e^(16nt^2). Using nr^2≤c1^2 gives

  E Y^2 <=16n^2 e^(16nt^2)
      [sqrt(105)+1+c1^2(sqrt(10395)+sqrt(3))].

After the outer square root and t≤4r/3,

  sqrt(EY^2) <=M(c1)n,
  M(c1)=4 sqrt[sqrt(105)+1+c1^2(sqrt(10395)+sqrt(3))]
                 e^(128c1^2/9),

which matches (6.3). In particular, the exponent is 128c1^2/9 because
the square root changes e^(16nt^2) to e^(8nt^2).

Hoeffding gives P(X≥Q)≤e^(-Q^2/(2n)). Thus

  E[Y 1_(X≥Q)] <=sqrt(EY^2) sqrt(P(X≥Q))
                 <=M(c1)n e^(-Q^2/(4n)).

This verifies (6.2) and the exponent 1/(4n). Substitution proves

  e <=8M(c1)(r/Q)e^(-Q^2/(4n)),

for all integer Q≥1. If Q>n, the event is empty. For Q≥1/r,
r/Q≤r^2≤c1^2/n, proving (6.5) with C_U=8M(c1)c1^2.

3. ROUNDING AND THE EXACT-SCHUR CUTOFF

For fixed r>0 and epsilon<c_*/n, use the integer budget

  Q_try=ceil(max{1/r, 2 sqrt(n log(C_U/(n epsilon)))}).

The logarithm is positive because C_U>c_*. For completeness, let
z=c1^2 and note c0≤c1, C_T=8192/3, and

  c_*=e^(-2c1^2/3)e^(-6144/c0^2)/(384c1).

The bounds M(c1)≥4sqrt(10)e^(128z/9) and c0≤c1 give

  C_U/c_* >= 32sqrt(10)·384 z^(3/2)
                    e^((134/9)z+6144/z) > 1.

For z≤1, z^(3/2)e^(6144/z) is decreasing and at least e^6144; for z≥1,
z^(3/2)≥1 and (134/9)z+6144/z≥2sqrt((134/9)·6144). Thus
C_U/(n epsilon)>C_U/c_*>1 in the stated range. If Q_try≤n, taking
Q=Q_try certifies e≤epsilon by (6.5). If Q_try>n, take Q=n+1: every
Schur spin has d=L+1≤n+1, so every branch is identity and recovery is
exact. Q=n is not the exact cutoff; its top L=n sector still uses the
tapered filter. Rounding changes the nonsaturated budget by at most one.

With kappa=r sqrt(n)∈[c0,c1], write u=log(r^2/epsilon). Then

  log(C_U/(n epsilon))=u+log(C_U/kappa^2).

The second term is bounded in terms of c0,c1. Also c_*<c0^2: putting
z=c0^2 and using c1≥c0 gives

  c_*/c0^2 <= e^(-6144/z)/(384 z^(3/2)) <1.

For z≤1 the last factor is increasing in z and at most e^(-6144)/384;
for z≥1 it is at most 1/384. Hence
epsilon<c_*/n implies u≥log(c0^2/c_*)>0. Therefore the tail budget is
comparable to sqrt(n(1+u)); the floor 1/r is Theta(sqrt(n)) and, when it
dominates, u is bounded. Capping at n+1 gives the stated fine-precision
order. The cap becomes active at u≥n+1+1/n because
sqrt(n(1+u))≥n+1. The converse supplies Omega(n) once u is order n, and
the exact n+1 code supplies the matching upper bound.

The middle interval c_*/n≤epsilon<r^2 is correctly handled by the coarser
e≤C r/Q result: u is bounded there and Q=Theta(sqrt(n)) suffices and is
necessary. At epsilon=r^2, the coarse and fine formulas both give
Theta(sqrt(n)).

4. UNKNOWN-RADIUS TRANSITIONS AND CAVEATS

For the ball ||b||≤R, R=c1/sqrt(n), the code itself does not use r.
The bound (6.4) is monotone in r and uniformly valid for sufficiently
large n that R≤1/2, so substituting R is valid; at r=0 the unital code is
exact. The clean transition scales retain c1:

  Q=Theta(R/epsilon)
       =Theta(c1/(sqrt(n)epsilon)),       R^2≤epsilon≤R;

  Q=Theta(min{n+1, sqrt(n(1+log(R^2/epsilon)))}),
                                      0<epsilon<R^2.

For epsilon≥R, if that tolerance range is included, Q=Theta(1) (with
constants depending on the code bound and c1). The fixed boundary sphere
r=R gives the lower bound; replacing r by R gives the upper. At
epsilon=R^2 the two displayed regimes both have order sqrt(n). These
exact transitions are c1^2/n and c1/sqrt(n). The n^-1 and n^-1/2 cutoffs
in quantum_tail_precision.txt §7 suppress fixed c1 factors, which is
legitimate for its order statement but obscures exact interval placement.
If c1>1 and the requested accuracy domain extends to R, the omitted
interval n^-1/2<epsilon≤R has Q=Theta(1); state that endpoint or restrict
the accuracy domain to epsilon≤n^-1/2. This is consistent with the
endpoints audit already in tail_audit/endpoints.txt.

DIFFERENCES FROM THE FIRST AUDIT

There is no disagreement with tail_audit/tail_upper.txt on the §6
calculation: both obtain the same M(c1), Hoeffding exponent, and exact
branch accounting. This separate report adds the explicit proof that the
logarithm in the inversion is positive, distinguishes Q=n from exact
Q=n+1, and records the exact c1-scaled ball endpoints. It agrees with
tail_audit/endpoints.txt on those transition scales. No source-priority,
converse, or runtime claim is audited here.
