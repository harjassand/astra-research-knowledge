Research outcome: conditional-envelope closure of hidden kinetics
I replaced explicit microscopic completion with a source-aware construction that bounds an unrepresented region through its possible effects on equilibrium mass, mobility and reaction—not through a list of its states.
The construction reduces the hidden-region problem to two different source-weighted kinetic equations on retained coordinates. Under its stated physical bounds, the hidden region’s equilibrium distribution, partition function, internal transition rates and mixing time need not be computed.
I also implemented a non-enumerative solver with exact error checks. It produced a mean-lifetime enclosure for a 128-spin system with \(2^{128}\) transient configurations, and a true-continuum enclosure for a 128-coordinate diffusion, without a configuration grid.
The decisive limitation remains visible: when the allowed uncertainty lies in the dominant trap, the bound can remain extremely broad. This is substantive progress on compact kinetic certification, not a demonstrated solution to general hidden-gate molecular prediction. The historical novelty of the specific construction is unresolved, and no state-of-the-art molecular performance advantage has been established.

1. What information can replace hidden-state enumeration?
The preceding Schur decomposition established that a hidden region contributes more than a boundary conductance:
\[
F=\gamma+b^\top K^{-1}b.
\]
A hidden dead end can leave the homogeneous boundary response unchanged while making the internal residence contribution \(\gamma\) arbitrarily large. Therefore, boundary-response fitting alone is insufficient.    RESEARCH_NOTE
The change here is to bound the combined source response before eliminating hidden coordinates, rather than reconstruct \(K\), \(b\) and \(\gamma\) separately.
I investigated three alternatives.
Low-rank response fitting alone can compress a solution but does not establish that omitted components are kinetically irrelevant. An executed continuum test below demonstrates this: the projected equations were solved to near floating-point precision, while the residual of the actual differential operator remained substantial.
Short-time or small-amplitude measurements alone retain the hidden-tail problem. The survival laws
\[
e^{-t}
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1-p)e^{-t}+pe^{-t/T}
\]
differ by at most \(p\), yet their means differ by approximately \(pT\). Without an additional tail or observability constraint, accurate short-time fitting need not determine the mean.
Global bounds compiled from local physical structure provide a different possibility: constrain whole unvisited regions simultaneously. This is the mechanism developed here.
A precise obstruction for unrestricted energy-and-force queries
Consider reversible diffusion on a bounded domain with a specified absorbing boundary. Run a deterministic procedure making finitely many point queries of a zero potential and its derivatives.
An unqueried open ball remains. Inside it, construct a smooth potential well of depth \(H\), with the potential unchanged outside a slightly larger unqueried ball. Every queried energy, force and derivative remains identical.
A variational test function equal to one over the well, with its gradient outside the perturbed region, gives
\[
\tau_H\ge C e^{H},
\]
for a positive constant \(C\) independent of \(H\), when energies are measured in thermal units.
Thus finite pointwise energy-and-force observations cannot provide a uniformly valid finite upper lifetime bound over unrestricted smooth potentials. The report supplies the construction and normalization argument.
This is not an impossibility result for molecular science. It identifies what changes the situation: an explicit interaction law, verified global regularity, a physical inequality, or stronger measurement information that excludes the unobserved well. Sparse agreement with a fitted force field does not itself supply that exclusion.
2. The main construction: two-source conditional closure
Write the full configuration as \((x,y)\), where \(x\) contains retained elementary coordinates and \(y\) is the unrepresented region.
Let \(\pi(dx)\) be a tractable reference measure on \(x\). Let \(\nu(dy)\) be any normalized hidden reference measure. Its density, interactions, dimension and normalizing constant need not be evaluated.
The actual equilibrium measure need not factorize. Write its unnormalized density relative to \(\pi\nu\) as \(w(x,y)\), and suppose physical information establishes
\[
\boxed{\ell(x)\le w(x,y)\le u(x).}
\]
Define
\[
Z_\ell=\langle \ell,1\rangle_\pi,
\qquad
Z_u=\langle u,1\rangle_\pi.
\]
Let \(A_\theta\) be the positive weighted operator associated with the full reversible dynamics and its specified absorption or reaction. In the reference Hilbert space, the mean-time equation is
\[
A_\theta t=w.
\]
The equilibrium-conditioned mean is therefore
\[
\tau_\theta
=
\frac{\langle w,A_\theta^{-1}w\rangle_{\pi\nu}}
{\langle w,1\rangle_{\pi\nu}}.
\]
The distinction between this weighted operator and simply minus the generator is essential when \(w\) is nonconstant.
Required kinetic bounds
Suppose retained-coordinate operators \(A_-\) and \(A_+\) have positivity-preserving inverses and satisfy two quadratic-form inequalities:
\[
\mathfrak a_\theta[f]
\ge
\int \mathfrak a_-[f(\cdot,y)]\,\nu(dy)
\quad\text{for arbitrary }f(x,y),
\]
and
\[
\mathfrak a_\theta[f(x)]
\le
\mathfrak a_+[f]
\quad\text{for functions depending only on }x.
\]
The first supplies a conservative lower bound on transport and reaction throughout the unrepresented region. The second bounds the cost of a retained-coordinate trial field.
For block-diagonal reversible diffusion, pointwise mobility and reaction bounds supply these inequalities. Hidden-coordinate diffusion contributes a nonnegative term and vanishes on functions of \(x\) alone. Arbitrary cross-coordinate diffusion requires a separate full-form argument; it is not automatically covered.
Conditional-envelope theorem
Under these hypotheses and the stated integrability and boundary conditions,
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\langle \ell,A_+^{-1}\ell\rangle_\pi}{Z_u}
\;\le\;
\tau_\theta
\;\le\;
\frac{\langle u,A_-^{-1}u\rangle_\pi}{Z_\ell}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
The sources deliberately differ from the weights used to construct the operators. This accounts for the possibility that an unrepresented region changes both how much equilibrium mass is present and how long that mass remains.
The compact description is
\[
(\ell,u,A_-,A_+),
\]
not a hidden-state list. Equation (1) does not require a hidden equilibrium sampler, hidden transition rates, bath mixing time or timescale separation.
Proof
For the upper bound, operator order gives
\[
A_\theta^{-1}
\preceq
A_-^{-1}\otimes I.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\langle w,A_\theta^{-1}w\rangle
\le
\int
\langle w(\cdot,y),A_-^{-1}w(\cdot,y)\rangle_\pi
\,\nu(dy)
\le
\langle u,A_-^{-1}u\rangle_\pi.
\]
The last step uses positivity preservation and \(0<w\le u\). Dividing by the actual source mass, which is at least \(Z_\ell\), proves the upper bound.
For the lower bound, take
\[
v=A_+^{-1}\ell\ge0.
\]
The variational principle yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle w,A_\theta^{-1}w\rangle
&\ge 2\langle w,v\rangle-\mathfrak a_\theta[v]\\
&\ge 2\langle\ell,v\rangle_\pi-\mathfrak a_+[v]\\
&=\langle\ell,A_+^{-1}\ell\rangle_\pi.
\end{aligned}
\]
The actual normalization is at most \(Z_u\), completing the proof.
These are conservative bounds, not necessarily attainable extrema over all hidden physical models.
How the inputs can be obtained
For an explicit physical model
\[
U(x,y)=U_g(x)+U_b(y)+\sum_\alpha V_\alpha(x,y),
\]
globally justified local interaction ranges
\[
V_\alpha^-(x)\le V_\alpha(x,y)\le V_\alpha^+(x)
\]
give
\[
\ell(x)=
\exp\!\left[-\beta\sum_\alpha V_\alpha^+(x)\right],
\qquad
u(x)=
\exp\!\left[-\beta\sum_\alpha V_\alpha^-(x)\right].
\]
For bounded analytic features and parameter intervals, this can be compiled through local interval calculations. The hidden potential \(U_b\), its partition function and its internal metastability disappear into \(\nu\).
This is a concrete sufficient access model, not a claim that bounding arbitrary atomistic interactions globally is cheap. Obtaining each range, handling singular interactions, and controlling mobility and the target definition remain charged inputs. No experiment establishing such global bounds for a real molecule was performed.

4. Solving the retained equations without enumerating their configurations
Equation (1) would merely relocate the difficulty if its retained equations still required a full state list.
I therefore built a compressed response solver with a separate, exact certificate. Tensor compression itself is established: quantized tensor trains have long been used for chemical master equations, and functional tensor methods also address high-dimensional differential equations. Those are important comparators, not new discoveries of this investigation. PLOS
A certificate that preserves the source response
For a positive self-adjoint operator \(H\), a collection of source columns \(B\), and approximate responses \(V\), define
\[
R=B-HV,
\]
\[
G_0=B^*V+V^*B-V^*HV.
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
B^*H^{-1}B
=
G_0+R^*H^{-1}R.
\]
If physical structure supplies an efficiently usable lower operator bound
\[
0<C\preceq H,
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
G_0
\preceq
B^*H^{-1}B
\preceq
G_0+R^*C^{-1}R.
}
\tag{2}
\]
The source columns can include the mass source as well as boundary loads. The hidden internal-residence contribution is then retained rather than dropped.
For one normalized equilibrium source, this becomes
\[
L=2\langle1,v\rangle-\langle v,Hv\rangle,
\qquad r=1-Hv,
\]
\[
\boxed{L\le\tau\le L+\langle r,C^{-1}r\rangle.}
\tag{3}
\]
These variational and functional error-estimation ingredients are established mathematics. Learned auxiliary fields and rigorous error majorants also have existing literature. The candidate contribution here is the particular source-aware closure and its computable spectral certificate, not the general residual principle. arXiv
Bounding omitted modes collectively
A global estimate such as
\[
\langle r,C^{-1}r\rangle
\le \|r\|^2/\lambda_{\min}(C)
\]
can be extremely pessimistic when rare gating makes the minimum eigenvalue small.
For a separable lower operator
\[
C=cI+\lambda\sum_i Q_i,
\]
where \(Q_i\) projects onto local nonconstant modes, let \(P_j\) select components involving exactly \(j\) nonconstant coordinates. Then
\[
\langle r,C^{-1}r\rangle
=
\sum_{j=0}^n
\frac{\|P_jr\|^2}{c+\lambda j}.
\tag{4}
\]
The weights can be computed by contracting small tensor matrices. The implementation evaluates selected sectors and rigorously bounds the remaining sectors collectively. It does not list their configurations.
For continuous variables, a further refinement separates components by their maximum local polynomial degree. This was materially useful: on one hard instance, it reduced the interval ratio from approximately 2.02 to 1.081 without changing the fitted response at all.
Exact checks, not just stable floating estimates
Floating-point optimization proposes tensor coefficients. They are converted into an explicitly defined dyadic rational witness. Integer contractions and rational arithmetic then evaluate the source, energy, residual and spectral bounds.
For the continuum tests, the verifier includes polynomial components outside the fitted space. In a three-coordinate diagnostic, the projected residual norm squared was approximately numerical zero, while the actual continuum residual norm squared was
\[
0.3622350401.
\]
Thus a converged truncated solve would have been an inadequate certificate.
The packet contains 4,253 exact rational algebra and implementation assertions, 20 additional mass-changing perturbation tests, and fresh replays of both largest witnesses. These are internal checks, not external proof review or formal verification.

6. A continuous test without a microscopic move alphabet
To remove the discrete rate-query interface, I constructed a reversible gated diffusion on \([0,1]^n\):
\[
\mathcal L f
=
\sum_i a_i(x)
\left[
x_i(1-x_i)\partial_{ii}f
+
(\alpha-\rho x_i)\partial_i f
\right],
\]
where
\[
a_1=1,\qquad
a_i=\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)x_{i-1},
\]
and the reaction hazard is
\[
k(x)=\kappa[\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)x_n].
\]
The equilibrium reference is a product beta distribution with
\[
\rho=\alpha+\beta,\qquad q=\alpha/\rho,\qquad \kappa=\rho q.
\]
This is an authored continuous gating diagnostic, not an independently validated atomistic model. Its logarithmic endpoint potential and degenerate endpoint mobility are explicit model features, not a realistic smooth molecular force field.
The inputs are local differential coefficients and a specified reaction law—not a list of possible microscopic transitions.

8. What has been gained, and what is still missing
The construction now has three concrete components:
A hidden-region description: bounds on source density, kinetic forms and reaction effects, rather than hidden configurations.
A source-aware closure: equation (1) bounds the entire lifetime contribution, including the mass and internal residence that a source-blind boundary model can miss.
A non-enumerative validation mechanism: compressed responses are checked against the full specified operator, with omitted spectral components explicitly charged.
The remaining limitations are not cosmetic.
First, the global physical envelopes must actually be established. A supplied bounded interaction law can constrain an enormous region; a few accurate force measurements cannot generally do so.
Second, the large continuum implementation currently covers the specified analytic model and constant-envelope bath extensions. The nonconstant conditional closure is implemented on a small retained graph. A unified large continuous solver for general nonconstant interaction envelopes has not yet been constructed.
Third, a compact tensor representation is not guaranteed. For \(n\) coordinates, local basis size \(d\) and tensor rank \(r\), storage is \(O(ndr^2)\), but the required rank and local resolution can grow sharply. The difficult rarity test demonstrates that failure in practice.
Fourth, the observable is the mean from the specified equilibrium-conditioned preparation. It is not automatically a ligand’s experimentally prepared dissociation time. A different preparation requires its own represented source and uncertainty accounting.
The exact next scientific obstruction
The most important unresolved example is the broad dominant-trap envelope, not another enormous bath with a harmless interface.
Independent worst-case bounds allow hidden source mass to become large while escape conductance becomes small. A physically useful refinement must capture the constraints linking those quantities—for example, how a verified interaction, geometric bottleneck and mobility law jointly restrict mass accumulation and escape.
Such a refinement must be obtainable without measuring the metastable escape rate itself, preparing the hidden equilibrium ensemble, or reconstructing the hidden state space.
The progress here is that microscopic enumeration is no longer built into the representation or certificate. The remaining task is to acquire sufficiently informative global physical constraints when the unrepresented region contains the decisive gate. The current method certifies benign omissions and detects consequential unresolved ones; it does not yet discover enough information to resolve the latter.
