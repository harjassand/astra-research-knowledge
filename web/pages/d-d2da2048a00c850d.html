## Modular form of the sufficient condition

Keep the affine one-observation family and bounded centered features from `INITIAL.txt`. The proof uses two distinct properties of the feature law:

1. **Entropy thickness:** for every finite coordinate set `I`, `h(Z_I) >= -H |I|`.
2. **Mesh-centroid regularity:** take the graded nodes `u_j=-1+6(j/J)^2-4(j/J)^3` and their nonnegative linear hats `phi_j`. For every finite `m`, coordinate `i<=m`, mesh vector `(J_1,...,J_m)`, and tuple `j_-i` of other labels with positive probability, form

   `q(t) = integral g_[m](t,z_-i) product_(k != i) phi_(k,j_k)(z_k) dz_-i`.

   Require that every own-coordinate conditional hat centroid obeys

   `| integral t q(t) phi_(i,j)(t) dt / integral q(t) phi_(i,j)(t) dt - u_j | <= A/J_i^2`,

   with one constant `A` independent of `m,i,j` and the mesh vector.

The first property alone gives the converse. The second, together with exact hat reproduction and the mesh geometry in `INITIAL.txt`, gives the positive archive. This separation identifies exactly what a weaker replacement for the joint log-gradient hypothesis must preserve: an entropy lower bound extensive in the number of coordinates, and a second-order local bias bound after conditioning on the labels of all other coordinates. Pairwise covariance/Fisher information implies neither property.

The uniform coordinatewise log-gradient condition in `INITIAL.txt` implies both modules. The entropy derivation there gives `H=max(0,2L-ln 2)`. For the centroid module, fix all other labels and differentiate under the integral; positivity and `|partial_i log g_[m]|<=L` yield `|q'(t)|<=Lq(t)`. Thus `q` is positive and log-Lipschitz wherever the hat has mass.

Here is the local tilt estimate with constants. Let `P_0(dt)` be normalized Lebesgue measure weighted by one hat. Its support is within `delta=6/J_i` of the node and its mean is within `4/J_i^2` of the node. The tilted distribution is `dP_q=q dP_0/E_0 q`; hence

`E_q[T]-E_0[T] = Cov_0(T,q(T))/E_0 q(T)`.

For independent `T,T'~P_0`, the covariance is one half of `E[(T-T')(q(T)-q(T'))]`. The derivative bound gives `|q(t)-q(t')|<=L q_max |t-t'|` on the support, so

`|Cov_0(T,q(T))| <= L q_max Var_0(T) <= L q_max delta^2`.

Also `q_max/E_0 q <= q_max/q_min <= exp(2L delta)`. If `J_i>=J_0=max(3,ceil(6L))`, then `L delta<=1`; consequently the tilted centroid displacement is at most `36 e^2 L/J_i^2`. Adding the Lebesgue centroid error proves the centroid module with the slightly sharper `A=4+36e^2 L`. `INITIAL` used the looser valid constant `C_L=4e+36e^2L`; the lower value here is only a constant improvement and does not change any rate.

For this implication, differentiation under the integral is legitimate: the domain is compact, `g_[m]` is `C^1`, and `|partial_i g|<=Lg` gives an integrable derivative bound. If some label tuple has zero probability, its encoder probability is zero under every `P_v` because `f_v>0`; omit that label in the decoder. Strict positivity of `g_[m]` makes every tensor-hat label positive in the stated log-gradient model.

## Reassembled upper and lower arguments

Given the centroid module, conditionally label each coordinate independently with its hat evaluated at `Z_i(X)`. Store the full tuple. The decoder for label `j` draws from `mu_j(dx)=phi_j(Z(x)) mu(dx)/q_j`, where `q_j=E_mu phi_j(Z)`. This is a positive measure-prepare channel and fixes the reference law because `sum_j q_j mu_j=mu`. Its score output is

`K Z_i(y)=sum_j phi_j(Z(y)) E_mu[Z_i(X)|J=j]`.

Uniform centroid error and `sum_j phi_j(Z(y)) u_(j_i)=Z_i(y)` give `|KZ_i-Z_i|<=A/J_i^2` pointwise. Therefore, for the retained affine part,

`|K f_v^ret-f_v^ret| <= A (sum_(i<=m) s_i^2/J_i^4)^(1/2)`

by Cauchy--Schwarz in `v`. Since a Markov kernel contracts sup norm, dropping a tail with Euclidean weight norm at most `epsilon/4` changes the input/output density difference by at most `2||s_>m||_2<=epsilon/2`. Taking `J_i>=sqrt(2 A s_i sqrt(m)/epsilon)` and at least `J_0` makes the retained difference at most `epsilon/2`. Thus the density difference is at most `epsilon`, and the total-variation error is at most `epsilon/2`. The alphabet has size at most `product_i(J_i+1)`. For `s_i=a exp(-bi)`, the tail cutoff is `m=b^-1 ln(a/epsilon)+O_(a,b)(1)`; summing the logs gives the same leading `ln^2(a/epsilon)/(4b ln 2)` bits, with an `O_(a,b,L)(ln(1/epsilon) ln ln(1/epsilon))` overhead. This is a finite-label mathematical construction; the reference sampler, numerical precision, runtime, and finite-bit simulation of a continuous `Y` are outside its charged interface.

The arbitrary-hybrid converse is independent of the mesh. For each `i`, put a uniform sign prior on `P_(+e_i),P_(-e_i)`, whose mixture is exactly `mu`. Let `M_i=E_mu[(Z_i(X)-Z_i(Y))^2]` under the single reference coupling induced by the fixed encoder and decoder. Uniform TV control gives `|E[T Z_i(Y)]-s_i E_mu Z_i^2|<=2 epsilon` and `|E Z_i(Y)^2-E_mu Z_i^2|<=2 epsilon`. The posterior identity `E[T|X]=s_i Z_i(X)` and the Markov channel imply `E[T Z_i(Y)]=s_i E[Z_i(X)Z_i(Y)]`. Expanding the squared error yields `M_i<=4epsilon/s_i+2epsilon<=6epsilon/s_i`, since `s_i<1`.

For finite `I`, `Zhat_I=Z_I(Y)` is a postprocessing of the `D`-dimensional hybrid state, so Holevo and data processing give `I(Z_I(X);Zhat_I)<=ln D`. Differential entropy subadditivity and the conditional Gaussian maximum-entropy inequality, using `Z_i(Y)` as an estimator, yield

`h(Z_I|Zhat_I) <= (1/2) sum_(i in I) ln(2 pi e M_i)`.

Combining with entropy thickness proves `ln D >= (1/2) sum_(i in I) ln(s_i/epsilon) - |I|(H+(1/2)ln(12 pi e))`. Choosing `I={i:s_i>=12 pi e exp(2H) epsilon}` gives the stated positive partial sum. With shared randomness `R` independent of `X`, use the randomized channel for the moment/error calculation and note `I(Z_I(X);Q,R)=I(Z_I(X);Q|R)<=ln D`; data processing then gives the same bound. No per-seed accuracy is needed. For fixed `d`, only this truncated sum is asserted; the log-squared asymptotic requires `d` to reach the epsilon-dependent cutoff.

## Fisher counterexample and overlap audit

To match product-uniform scores exactly, take `Z_i(x)=x_i` with independent `x_i~Unif[-1,1]`, and `Z_i(t)=sqrt(2/3) cos(2 pi i t)` with one uniform `t~Unif[0,1]`. Both have covariance `(1/3) I` for distinct positive integer modes, hence the affine families have the same Fisher matrix `(1/3) diag(s_i^2)` at zero. In the second family every two-or-more-coordinate score law is supported on a `C^1` curve, so it has no full-dimensional Lebesgue density and fails both the stated density hypothesis and the entropy-thickness condition. This is the exact gate that excludes the equal-Fisher pair; it is not a claim that singular laws are impossible to compress by other means.
