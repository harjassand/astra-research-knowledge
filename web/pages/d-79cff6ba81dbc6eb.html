THE ADMITTED CONCAVE HIGHER-SPIN CLASS IS NOT GENERICALLY SEPARABLE
c06_s01 phase-2 exact boundary; 7 October 2026.

Take two spin-1 sites, H0=S1x S2x+S1y S2y-S1z S2z, physical energy -H0.
This is admitted: alpha=1,gamma=-1,b=c=0. Rotating the second site by pi
about z sends H0 to -S1.S2. The total-spin j=0,1,2 sectors then have H0
eigenvalues 2,1,-1 with multiplicities 1,3,5 respectively. At beta=2 the
unique maximally entangled singlet's Gibbs mass is

 p0=1/(1+3e^-2+5e^-6).

Since e^2>sum_(k=0)^4 2^k/k!=7, p0>343/495. Every product state of two
spin-1 systems has singlet overlap <=1/3 by its equal Schmidt coefficients;
convexity extends this to every separable state. The singlet measurement
therefore gives

 inf_separable_sigma T(rho0,sigma)>343/495-1/3=178/495.

This is an admitted explicit rational beta/coupling instance with a constant
whole-state distance from ALL positive mixtures of local spin states.

It survives genuine concave onsite potentials. Put
H=H0-(1/100)[(S1z)^2+(S2z)^2]; then ||H-H0||<=1/50. This adds physical
positive single-ion energies, and the f(m)=-m^2/100 tables are concave.
For Gibbs states of generators beta H,beta H0, the identity for relative
entropy, scalar eigenvalue minmax for log partition functions, and
|Tr rho(H-H0)|<=||H-H0|| imply

 D(rho||rho0)<=2beta||H-H0||<=2/25.

Quantum Pinsker in natural logarithms gives T(rho,rho0)<=sqrt(D/2)<=1/5.
The triangle inequality therefore yields

 inf_separable_sigma T(rho,sigma)>178/495-1/5=79/495.

No local-sampling difficulty or computational-hardness conclusion follows.
The conditional partition/measurement-counting algorithm may still apply.
This exact entanglement witness explains why the new positive-mixture result
in HIGHER_SPIN_POSITIVE_MIXTURE.txt is limited to the specified collective
critical scaling and cannot be asserted for arbitrary graphs/potentials.

The proof uses standard spin addition, Schmidt overlap and quantum Pinsker;
these are established tools. The boundary is not claimed historically new.

Information, Theorem5.38, https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/TQI/TQI.pdf.
