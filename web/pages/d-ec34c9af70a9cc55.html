# Independent audit: calibrated OU noise increments

Audit date: 2026-10-06. Worker: check_wcrg. No subworkers. This audits r04's reconstructed theorem, not an author theorem. Read r04's `noise_increment.py` and `RESULT.md`; independent symbolic and numeric code imports neither. Prior-art readings below are explicitly excerpts. The earlier quantum consequence audit remains complete in `quantum_consequence_audit.md`.

**Verdict:** the arbitrary-dimension generic theorem passes. One calibrated full symmetric SPD diffusion-power increment identifies arbitrary stable drift and unknown positive diagonal baseline power, for every fixed truth and almost every increment. Two increments suffice, and are necessary for n≥2, when baseline power is unrestricted SPD. These are exact stationary-law results with a quantitative conditional parameter certificate. They do not establish feasible physical control, a uniform sample budget, or historical priority. The sharp additional obstruction is that no prescribed increment works uniformly over unknown diagonal baselines, already in dimension two.

## Exact interface and independently reconstructed proof

The supplied class is dX=(-AX+b)dt+sqrt(D0)dW, Re spec(A)>0, with all physical coordinates observed. D0 denotes power, not its square root and not the Fokker–Planck diffusion D0/2. Add an independent Brownian force B dWc with BBᵀ=Q≻0, leaving A,b unchanged. Observe baseline and perturbed stationary covariances Σ0,Σ1. Then

AΣ0+Σ0Aᵀ=D0, AΣ1+Σ1Aᵀ=D0+Q, μ=A⁻¹b.

For D0=diag(d)>0, the joint linear operator P(A,d) consists of these two left sides minus diag(d). Symmetric Frobenius-isometric coordinates give n(n+1) outputs and n²+n inputs. Its invertibility gives unique real A,d; b=Aμ follows from the baseline mean. Mean equality across settings is a model diagnostic, not a proof that the supplied class is true.

Fix the truth before drawing Q. Let V=Σ0^(1/2), W=V⁻¹, B=WAV. Then B+Bᵀ=WD0W≻0 even when A has an indefinite Euclidean symmetric part. Pick distinct positive diagonal T arbitrarily close to I, so BT+TBᵀ≻0 by openness. Set Σ1=V(I+εT)V and Q=εV(BT+TBᵀ)V for ε>0. This is a physically admissible SPD witness and satisfies the true Lyapunov equation. It proves existence, not an implementable design from unknown A.

For a homogeneous joint kernel (H,e), write Y=WHV and E=Wdiag(e)W. Then Y+Yᵀ=E and YT+TYᵀ=0. The latter diagonal equations imply t_i E_ii=0, hence (W∘W)e=0. For nonzero e,

eᵀ(W∘W)e=tr[diag(e)Wdiag(e)W]=||W^(1/2)diag(e)W^(1/2)||F²>0.

Thus e=0; Y is skew, and YT−TY=0 implies every Y_ij=0 because T is simple. P is invertible at this witness. For fixed truth, Σ1=Σ0+L_A⁻¹(Q) is affine in symmetric Q, with L_A(H)=AH+HAᵀ invertible since all eigenvalue sums have positive real parts. det P is a nonzero polynomial in the n(n+1)/2 independent entries of Q. Its zero set has Lebesgue measure zero. Any absolutely continuous probe distribution on the full SPD cone therefore succeeds almost surely, for each fixed truth.

Zero new settings cannot identify the diagonal-baseline class: the baseline linear operator has fewer outputs than inputs and a nonzero kernel; small kernel moves preserve stable A and positive diagonal D0 because admissibility is open. Unknown b can preserve μ. This includes n=1's baseline scale ambiguity.

For arbitrary unknown full D0, differences Γj=Σj−Σ0 obey AΓj+ΓjAᵀ=Qj, Γj≻0. Whiten Γ1. This fixes the symmetric part S of the transformed A. If C=Γ1^(-1/2)Γ2Γ1^(-1/2) has simple spectrum, its equation fixes the skew part via (c_j−c_i)Kij. For fixed A and any fixed Q1≻0, Q2→C is an invertible linear map on symmetric matrices; the repeated-eigenvalue discriminant is a nonzero polynomial, hence almost every SPD Q2 gives simple C. Recover D0 and b from the baseline. With just one Γ, A'=A+ηΩΓ⁻¹ for nonzero skew Ω leaves its Lyapunov equation unchanged. Set D0'=A'Σ0+Σ0A'ᵀ and b'=A'μ. Small η preserves stability and D0'≻0. Thus baseline plus one perturbed law cannot identify full baseline power for n≥2; baseline plus two is minimal. For n=1 one increment is sufficient.

## Exact obstruction to a universal deterministic increment

For **every** prescribed 2×2 SPD Q=[[a,b],[b,c]], a,c>0, δ=ac−b²>0, choose A=rI, r>0, and D0=diag(a,c). Then Σ0=D0/(2r), Γ=Q/(2r), Σ1=Σ0+Γ. Let J=[[0,1],[-1,0]], and

H=JQ⁻¹=(1/δ)[[-b,a],[-c,b]],

E=HΣ0+Σ0Hᵀ=(1/(rδ))diag(−ab,bc).

HΓ+ΓHᵀ=0, so (H,diag E) is a nonzero homogeneous kernel of P. For small nonzero η, A'=rI+ηH and D0'=D0+ηE are admissible and yield both same covariances under the same Q. In fact H has trace0 and determinant1/δ, so its eigenvalues are ±i/√δ and A' remains positive stable for every real η; only positivity of D0' bounds η. Set both b=0, so means also agree. Gaussian stationary covariances and means determine the complete stationary laws.

Rational replay: Q=[[2,1],[1,3]], r=1, η=1/3 gives A'=[[14/15,2/15],[-1/5,16/15]], D0'=diag(28/15,16/5). Both baseline covariances diag(1,3/2) and perturbed covariances [[2,1/2],[1/2,3]] agree exactly. Symbolic code verifies the whole parameterized identity. This obstruction even keeps the original drift rI independent of Q; the baseline power is selected as a function of Q. It refutes ∃Q∀(A,D0), not ∀fixed(A,D0) a.e.Q.

The full-cone polynomial argument does not by itself imply genericity on the lower-dimensional diagonal-control cone: the determinant could vanish identically on a subspace. Our deterministic obstruction also applies to each prescribed diagonal Q (choose D0=Q), but does not rule out randomized diagonal controls for each fixed truth. Indeed A=rI, diagonal D0 and generic diagonal Q have distinct covariance ratios and are identifiable by the same F test. A general diagonal-control theorem remains unaudited here. An absolutely continuous full-cone distribution is a mathematical assumption; a finite-bit grid is discrete, so its failure probability needs its own anti-concentration or directly checked rank argument.

## Exact invertibility test and cheap observable conditioning

Diagonalize C=WΣ1W=Udiag(c)Uᵀ, put Z=UᵀW and F=diag(c−1)(Z∘Z). Since Q≻0, Σ1−Σ0≻0 and every c_i>1. P is invertible **if and only if** C is simple and F is invertible. Repeated c creates a skew kernel supported in its eigenspace with d=0. If c is simple but F is singular, choose nonzero d in ker F, set S=Zdiag(d)Zᵀ/2 and choose all offdiagonal K by the commutator equation; diagonals vanish by Fd=0, giving a kernel. This corrects r04's early description of these conditions as merely sufficient.

The exact adversarial example above has C eigenvalue gap √(2/3)=0.81649..., but det(Z∘Z)=0. Healthy eigenvalue separation alone is insufficient. Use both gaps.

Here is an independent O(n³) lower bound for σmin(P), with no full n²-sized operator SVD. Let λ=min eig Σ0, κ=cond Σ0, f=σmin(F)>0, c=||C||op, h=||C−I||op, g=min_{i≠j}|c_i−c_j|>0. For n=1 set the skew coefficient below to zero. Define

u=√(1+c²)/f, v=(||Z||op² u+1)/2, w=(√2+2h v)/g,

β=√[κ(v²+w²)+u²],  γcheap=λ/β.

For arbitrary θ=(vec A,d), transform output residuals to eigenbasis as R0,R1, drift as B=S+K, E=Zdiag(d)Zᵀ. Then 2S−E=R0, BC+CBᵀ−E=R1. Diagonals give Fd=diag R1−diag(c)diag R0, hence ||d||≤u r, r=√(||R0||F²+||R1||F²). This implies ||S||≤v r. Subtracting equations gives [K,C]=R1−R0−S(C−I)−(C−I)S, so ||K||≤w r. Symmetric and skew parts are Frobenius-orthogonal; ||A||≤√κ √(v²+w²)r, and consequently ||θ||≤β r. Whitening each original output multiplies Frobenius norm by at most 1/λ, so ||Pθ||≥γcheap||θ||. Eigen-decomposition, n×n SVD and matrix products cost O(n³) arithmetic/O(n²) storage. The bound can be conservative; it is a lower bound, not the actual gap.

The same derivation applies to empirical covariances whenever the measured conditions hold, giving an observable bound for σmin(Phat). Combine it with the following perturbation certificate; do not use f,g without covariance uncertainty.

## Finite-data statement, physical costs, and what remains unproved

On a joint event ||Σhat_j−Σj||op≤εj, ||Qhat−Q||F≤εQ, ΔP=2√(ε0²+ε1²) bounds ||Phat−P||. For any fit θhat, residual rfit=||Phatθhat−(0,Qhat)|| and any certified lower gap γhat for Phat satisfying γhat>ΔP,

||θhat−θ||≤[rfit+ΔP||θhat||+εQ]/(γhat−ΔP).

Reconstructing this inequality by first bounding Phat(θhat−θ), then using ||θ||≤||θhat||+||error||, verifies it. No positivity/stability inference is hidden in this inequality; those model checks remain separate.

For m=N−1 independent stationary Gaussian snapshots, E||Σhat−Σ||F²=[(trΣ)²+trΣ²]/m≤n(n+1)||Σ||op²/m. Markov gives a conservative acquired radius ε=ρ||Σhat||op/(1−ρ), ρ=√[n(n+1)/(mδ)]<1. Use a union budget over settings and a summable budget over adaptive sample batches. This verifies r04's elementary acquisition bound. The numerical checks perturb true covariance matrices by controlled errors; they do not constitute acquisition of stationary samples.

Full SPD forcing is physically implementable in an ideal additive model by choosing B=Q^(1/2) and applying mixed independent Gaussian force channels. Independence refers to the added Brownian source relative to the baseline; the components of B dWc are generally correlated. Independent per-coordinate amplitude controls supply only diagonal Q, a strictly weaker actuator. A positive increment cannot remove baseline power. Calibration of all entries supplies a physical time scale. Measurement noise, unobserved coordinates, state-dependent diffusion, intervention-induced drift/input changes, preparation, burn-in and sample dependence are outside the theorem. Independent stationary ensembles require resets/preparation or justified mixing; a single long trajectory is not automatically iid. Almost-sure rank supplies neither a uniform lower gap nor bounded expected sample cost. Existing large-rotation obstructions underscore this; I did not independently audit r04's star/EPR proof here.
