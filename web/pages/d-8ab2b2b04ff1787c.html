# Energy-charged covariance and single-current conditioning

This is our elementary derivation; no novelty claim. Exact question: Given known finite detector map T(theta):R^p->R^h, parameter metric Euclidean and compact class K, certify uniform noisy recovery at total physical input energy E. Let S be the closure of normalized secant matrices A=(T(theta)-T(eta))/||theta-eta||, and assume ||A||op<=B. Detector rank h, input dimension p, energy norm, noise norm and costs to acquire S are explicit interface constants. Complex problems can be realified, charging doubled dimension.

## Deterministic covariance design

For Q>=0, tr Q=1, define c(Q)=inf_(A in S) tr(A Q A^T). If Q=sum_(j=1)^k lambda_j u_j u_j^T, the k currents f_j=sqrt(E lambda_j)u_j have total squared energy E and

sum_j ||A f_j||²=E tr(A Q A^T)>=E c(Q).

Thus the complete detector output stack separates all parameter pairs with inverse Lipschitz constant 1/sqrt(Ec). If truth and a feasible estimate each have stack residual <=eta, their parameter distance <=2eta/sqrt(Ec). This is information/conditioning, not an efficient reconstruction claim. With noise <=epsilon per scalar detector coordinate, eta<=epsilon sqrt(h k), explicitly charging the number of outputs. Repeating identical measurements does not make adversarial noise disappear.

For a finite operator-net {A_l} of radius r in operator norm, maximize t subject to Q>=0, trQ=1, tr(A_l Q A_l^T)>=t for each l. This is an SDP once all matrices and net coverage are certified. For every A within r of a center, |tr(AQA^T)-tr(A_l QA_l^T)|<=2B r if all centers obey norm<=B. Hence c(Q)>=t-2Br. If true S is merely sampled without coverage, the certificate is invalid. A continuum separation lower bound has not been acquired by a sampled optimizer.

## Independent fixed-energy draws: exact concentration/net bound

Sample u_j with probability lambda_j from the eigenvectors of Q, independently n times. Each draw has unit energy and E[u u^T]=Q. Use actual current f_i=sqrt(E/n)u_i, fixing total energy E for every outcome. For any A, X_i=||A u_i||² in [0,B²] with mean >=c. For an operator-net of radius r=c/(8B), Hoeffding with downward deviation c/4 and a union bound shows

P(any center's mean <3c/4) <= N(S,c/(8B)) exp[-n c²/(8B^4)].

On the complementary event, every A has empirical mean >=c/2 because quadratic values differ by <=2Br=c/4. Therefore n>= (8B^4/c²) log[N(S,c/(8B))/delta] guarantees inverse Lipschitz constant <=sqrt(2/(Ec)) with probability >=1-delta. Noise error <=2eta sqrt(2/(Ec)); eta charges all h n noisy scalar outputs. This is deliberately elementary/conservative. Exact rank(Q)-current factorization already guarantees better conditioning without probability or n; random draws are useful when that schedule is constrained or measurements arrive in a specified stochastic architecture.

## One genuinely fixed-energy normalized Gaussian current

Take g~N(0,I_p), f=sqrt(E) Q^(1/2)g/||Q^(1/2)g||. This normalization enforces energy E but its covariance generally is NOT E Q. Assume every A in S has at least r positive eigenvalues of AQA^T >=nu>0. Necessarily r<=min(h,rank Q); this is a charged spectral small-ball assumption, not a consequence of generic rank alone. For one A,

||A Q^(1/2)g||² stochastically dominates nu chi²_r.

For 0<t²<=r nu, Laplace transform and Markov give P(||A Q^(1/2)g||<=t) <=(e t²/(r nu))^(r/2). Indeed E exp(-s chi²_r)=(1+2s)^(-r/2), optimize s=(r nu/t²-1)/(2nu), then omit exp(-t²/(2nu))<=1.

Also E||Q^(1/2)g||²=1, so P(denominator²>1/delta_E)<=delta_E. Use a net radius a/sqrt(E) so approximation costs at most a. For every center to separate by >=2a, it suffices that its unnormalized Gaussian output exceeds 2a/sqrt(E delta_E). Consequently

P(inf_A ||Af||<a) <= delta_E + N(S,a/sqrt(E)) [4e a²/(E delta_E r nu)]^(r/2),

provided 4a²/(E delta_E)<=r nu. If N(S,z)<=(H/z)^q, this becomes

delta_E + H^q E^(q/2) [4e/(E delta_E r nu)]^(r/2) a^(r-q).

For r>q, let delta_E=delta/2, J denote the coefficient of a^(r-q), and choose a<=min(sqrt(E)r0, sqrt(E delta_E r nu)/2, [(delta/2)/J]^(1/(r-q))). Then uniform conditioning holds with probability >=1-delta and noisy error <=2eta/a. For r<=q this argument supplies no useful shrinking-a confidence limit; it does not prove impossibility, and special geometries may perform substantially better. Isotropic Q=I/p is the ordinary uniform spherical current and nu equals the squared r-th singular value of A divided by p.

The net centers must belong to S and inherit the same spectral bound. Pointwise uniform norms avoid uncountable null-set ambiguities. Finite matrix rank counts alone do not furnish nu. Covariance can distribute energy into directions missed by a single realization: e.g. S={v^T:||v||=1} in p>=2 has c(I/p)=1/p, but every single input f is annihilated by some v. Thus covariance optimality and universal single-current observability are distinct.

## Weak coercivity gives upper constants; lower constants remain separate

For L(theta)=L0+sum theta_j L_j symmetric and L(theta)>=alpha I, ||P||<=p_in, ||D||<=d_out, and T=D L^-1 P, set kappa=(sum ||L_j||²)^(1/2). Resolvent identity gives B<=d_out p_in kappa/alpha². Its parameter second derivative has bilinear norm <=2d_out p_in kappa²/alpha³. All these are finite verified matrix constants; continuum PDE analogues require chosen Hilbert/trace norms and their embedding constants. They control normalized-secant entropy via a compact convex model box. They do not guarantee c(Q)>0 or nu>0: multiply all parameter perturbations L_j by epsilon, preserving alpha and upper bounds, and all sensitivities tend to zero. Or let two L_j have identical accessible P,D compression, creating nonidentifiability.
