## Question, supplied interface and costs

Can a useful acquired report be a calibrated output law while hidden kinetic identity remains unidentifiable? Can an allowed preparation change turn a nearly invisible failure of an exact aggregate law into an economical test?

The fixture is a two-species first-order mass-action network. Species interconvert at rate k>0 and leak at rates lambda+delta and lambda-delta, with lambda>|delta|. The true parameter delta is fixed across all trials and belongs to the supplied finite catalogue {0,-epsilon,+epsilon}. Lambda, k, epsilon, assay noise sigma>0, species identities, the kinetic template, and legal preparations are supplied. These are substantive scientific assumptions. They are not learned in the diagnostic. A reset creates a fresh known physical preparation; within-trial concentrations evolve normally. Independent assay noise is drawn afresh after each reset. An unreset carryover experiment is outside the declared channel interface.

An experiment has four charged actions: reset, prepare one unit of material, wait, assay. Preparation may be balanced (x1=x2=1/2) or asymmetric (x1=1,x2=0). The duration of each wait is recorded; the numerical comparison uses duration t=1, so both action count and total waiting time scale with sample count (requiring T>=1 for that numerical menu). The information lower bounds allow t in a fixed bounded interval [0,T], with T>0 and at least one allowed positive wait time for matching upper bounds. Unlimited concentration, free long waits, repeated free observations, species-resolved assays and unknown carryover are excluded. If asymmetric preparation costs more, replace its four-action cost by its actual cost; the comparison is not globally optimal over undeclared experimental apparatus.

Continuous observations are Z=m(t)+N(0,sigma^2), where m=x1+x2. A second, separate finite-alphabet fixture reports Y=1{Z>exp(-lambda*t)} at the single fixed t=1. Its noise is explicitly sigma=.1, rather than the continuous table's sigma=.01. A Gaussian likelihood calculation is proved below independently; it is not a corollary of a finite-alphabet conversion theorem.

## Exact dynamics and the critical locus

With m=x1+x2 and d=x1-x2, and externally known inputs u_m=u1+u2, u_d=u1-u2,

    m' = -lambda*m - delta*d + u_m,
    d' = -delta*m - (lambda+2k)*d + u_d.                    (1)

The specified aggregate m has an autonomous first-order law for all physical initial states exactly when delta=0. Sufficiency follows from (1). For necessity, choose two nonnegative states of equal positive total mass but different d; their m derivatives differ by -delta times the difference of d. Thus no single-valued m'=f(m,u_m) describes both when delta!=0. This statement concerns the fixed measured output m; it does not exclude another parameter-dependent linear eigenvector reduction.

The recent generic/critical lumping paper gives the relevant context: its Proposition 3 concerns a fixed parameter-independent lumping map valid for all parameters in an open admissible set. That quantifier cannot be replaced by existence of some parameter-dependent eigenvector at each parameter value. Our critical condition for the specified map T=(1,1) is the elementary rowspace invariance condition TA in span(T), namely equality of the two leakage rates. The paper is not claimed to have discovered our experiment comparison.

For a pulse m(0)=1, d(0)=j and no later input, define omega=sqrt(k^2+delta^2). Write the matrix in (1) as -(lambda+k)I+J, with

    J = [[k,-delta],[-delta,-k]],  J^2=omega^2 I.

Expanding the exponential in even and odd powers gives

    exp(Jt)=I*cosh(omega*t)+(J/omega)*sinh(omega*t).

Consequently

    m_delta(t;j)=exp(-(lambda+k)t)
       *[cosh(omega*t)+(k-delta*j)/omega*sinh(omega*t)],     (2)
    d_delta(t;j)=exp(-(lambda+k)t)
       *[j*cosh(omega*t)+(-delta-k*j)/omega*sinh(omega*t)].  (3)

These are exact, including delta=0 since k>0. Positivity is inherited from the original chemical matrix, whose off-diagonals are k>0.

## A sufficient acquired law and a strict obstruction to global identification

Balanced preparations and inputs have j=0 and u_d=0. Replacing delta by -delta and d by -d leaves m and (1) unchanged. Uniqueness of the linear ODE implies that every balanced mass trajectory is identical for the two signs. The same holds for every adaptive policy using only previous aggregate assays: induct on experiments, coupling its random seed and Gaussian noises, then apply the identical conditional channel at the chosen balanced experiment. No quantity of such evidence identifies the sign. With equal prior on +/-epsilon, every sign classifier has success at most 1/2.

Nevertheless, the report r=delta^2 identifies an exact reusable output law. From (1), eliminating d for smooth balanced input gives

    m'' + 2(lambda+k)m' + [lambda*(lambda+2k)-r]m
       = u_m' + (lambda+2k)u_m,                            (4)

with m'(0)=-lambda*m(0)+u_m(0) when d(0)=0. Equivalently its zero-state transfer function is

    H_r(s)=(s+lambda+2k)/[(s+lambda)(s+lambda+2k)-r].        (5)

Convolution extends (5) to pulse inputs without differentiating their distributions informally. At r=0 a pole cancels and the first-order total-mass law is recovered. Thus learning the two-valued r in this catalogue is enough for the entire declared menu of balanced total-mass forecasts. It is not enough for species forecasts: by (3), x1=(m+d)/2 differs between the two signs at every t>0. Nor is it enough for asymmetric-input mass forecasts. Expanding the target menu changes the required acquisition task and exposes a separating legal preparation.

A full-parameter-identification metric assigns unavoidable sign error to an exactly sufficient total-mass report. This is a mathematical mismatch between that metric and this target menu, not a claim of an actual scoring error in ALDER or ULTRADISCOVERY. Target prediction metrics alone also require declared coverage: balanced forecasts cannot certify species or arbitrary interventions. There is no inference here from success on one report to a universal report-law conversion.

If finite catalogue selection identifies r correctly with probability at least 1-eta, the exact law is available on that shared event. Repeated balanced forecast queries need no new union penalty on model correctness. This depends on the supplied exact finite catalogue. An estimated continuous parameter generally yields only confidence/error bounds over declared bounded targets, not exact global laws for arbitrary future policies.

## Breaking symmetry changes the statistical order

Expand (2) at delta=0, for fixed positive t. Since omega=k+delta^2/(2k)+O(delta^4), balanced mass has

    m_delta(t;0)-exp(-lambda*t) = b(t)*delta^2+O(delta^4),
    b(t)=exp(-lambda*t)*[t/(2k)-(1-exp(-2kt))/(4k^2)] > 0. (6)

To verify the coefficient, differentiate cosh(omega*t)+(k/omega)sinh(omega*t) with respect to omega at k, then multiply by d omega/d(delta^2)=1/(2k). Its numerator is t*exp(kt)-sinh(kt)/k. Multiplication by exp(-(lambda+k)t) gives (6). Positivity follows from 1-exp(-z)<z for z=2kt>0.

The asymmetric j=1 term has a nonzero first derivative:

    m_delta(t;1)-exp(-lambda*t) = -a(t)*delta+O(delta^2),
    a(t)=exp(-lambda*t)*(1-exp(-2kt))/(2k) > 0.           (7)

No actual learned system is credited with finding this preparation. The source of the advantage is a changed observation geometry available through an explicitly charged action.

## Independent Gaussian testing derivation

For the binary hypotheses delta=0 versus delta=+epsilon, repeat one fixed experiment n times with known equal Gaussian variance sigma^2. Its sample mean has variance sigma^2/n. The likelihood ratio is monotone in this mean, and the midpoint of the two means gives equal errors

    e_n = Phi(-sqrt(n)*abs(mu_1-mu_0)/(2*sigma)).

This midpoint rule is Bayes-optimal for equal priors by pointwise comparison of the two densities. Its equal errors make it minimax: every rule's maximum error is at least its equal-prior average error, whereas this rule attains the minimum average and has equal errors. Thus for 0<eta<1/2 the exact smallest positive integer fixed sample count is

    n*=ceil(4*sigma^2*[Phi^{-1}(1-eta)]^2/(mu_1-mu_0)^2). (8)

Equations (6)-(8) give n_balanced=Theta(epsilon^-4) and n_asymmetric=Theta(epsilon^-2) as epsilon tends to zero, with k,lambda,t,sigma,eta fixed. These are binary testing counts, not three-way full-catalogue identification counts.

The order separation also survives adaptation within the declared bounded-time fresh-unit-pulse menu. Smoothness and evenness give a uniform |mu_epsilon(t;0)-mu_0(t;0)|<=C*epsilon^2 for 0<=t<=T and small epsilon. Gaussian per-observation KL is the squared mean gap divided by 2*sigma^2. Conditional on any observed history, an adaptive strategy picks the same experiment rule under both hypotheses. The KL chain rule therefore bounds transcript KL by n*C'*epsilon^4 for a hard budget of n assays. A test with both errors at most eta has transcript TV at least 1-2eta; Pinsker gives KL>=2(1-2eta)^2. Hence every such strategy needs Omega(epsilon^-4) observations. Equation (8) supplies a matching fixed-time upper bound. For the asymmetric menu the uniform gap is O(epsilon), the same argument gives Omega(epsilon^-2), and (7)-(8) give the matching upper bound. This is a fixed/hard-budget theorem; unbounded stopping-time cost identities are not asserted.

## Finite alphabet, finite unknown parameter and positive channel floor

At the fixed terminal time, threshold the Gaussian assay at mu_0=exp(-lambda*t). Then

    p_delta(j)=P(Y=1)=Phi((m_delta(t;j)-mu_0)/sigma).       (9)

All catalogue probabilities lie strictly between zero and one. For the finite two-experiment, three-parameter menu, beta=min{p_delta(j),1-p_delta(j)} is a supplied/computed positive floor; it is not inferred from observing a finite sample without assumptions. Here unknown hidden identity is delta, held fixed across trials; physical state reset and within-trial motion define the channel.

The derivative Phi'(0)=1/sqrt(2*pi)>0 implies p_epsilon(0)-1/2=Theta(epsilon^2), while the three asymmetric probabilities have pairwise minimum separation Delta=Theta(epsilon). The two sign probabilities are exactly equal for balanced preparations.

For balanced binary quotient selection use the midpoint threshold between .5 and p_epsilon(0). Hoeffding's one-sided bound gives each error <=exp(-n*gap^2/2), so

    n_balanced >= ceil(2*log(1/eta)/gap^2)                 (10)

is sufficient to learn r. For nearest-mean three-way asymmetric classification, an error requires |sample proportion-p_true|>=Delta/2. The two-sided bound gives the sufficient count

    n_asymmetric >= ceil(2*log(2/eta)/Delta^2).            (11)

These are sufficient bounds, not the exact minimal counts. Their orders match the Gaussian lower bounds, since deterministic thresholding cannot increase KL. Alternatively, Bernoulli KL satisfies D(p||q)<=(p-q)^2/[q(1-q)] by log(u)<=u-1, and the positive floor bounds the denominator uniformly. The finite-alphabet fixture therefore has the same fourth-power versus second-power information cost, without appealing to a continuous-alphabet extension of another theorem.

## Separate, honest finite calibration example

This example is NOT a preprocessing procedure for heterogeneous leakage in (1). In a different supplied class, let x_next=rho*P*x+u, with a known conservation template 1^T P=1^T, nonnegative P and inputs for physical positivity, finite rho in {.5,.75}, and unknown internal P. Then m_next=rho*m+1^T u. Let a globally affine sensor have unknown s in {1,2}, b in {0,1}, and known fresh Gaussian sigma. Three independent protocols (zero assay; immediate known-unit assay; one-step known-unit assay) have means (b,b+s,b+s*rho). Their minimum L-infinity catalogue separation is 1/4. Repeat each R times and choose the nearest triple in L-infinity norm. Selection is correct whenever all three sample means are within 1/8 of their truths. A Gaussian tail union bound gives failure at most 6*exp(-R/(128*sigma^2)); thus R>=ceil(128*sigma^2*log(6/eta)) suffices. Protocol costs are respectively 2,3,4, giving 9R total actions.

This acquires s,b,rho, but not P, the conservation template, global affine sensor class, sigma or independent noise. Arbitrarily different conservative P matrices remain observationally equivalent for all total-mass inputs. Inferring the template from finite data remains unresolved. If the real mechanism has unequal leakage, fitting this common-retention calibration silently assumes the very closure one should test; the procedure is then invalid. The numerical reaction fixture accordingly supplies its nuisance parameters or requires a separately charged scientifically adequate calibration.

Linear lumping, transfer functions, symmetry quotients, Gaussian discrimination, and design based on observability/information are classical. The recent lumping paper clarifies exceptional parameter sets; approximate constrained lumping offers finite-horizon error bounds for a supplied ODE. ALDER explicitly selects cost-aware candidate disagreement, but supplies state representation and a mathematical grammar. Our combined worked fixture and cost derivation were reconstructed here; no general novelty claim is made.

The downstream use is concrete: testing near an exceptional aggregate law using a balanced preparation can be vastly more expensive than changing composition. A useful report should state its preserved outputs and legal preparations so that later target changes trigger new identification. The exact unsolved foundational step is acquiring the kinetic representation, calibrated noise/instrument class, and legal separating preparation from real interfaces without supplying the useful symmetry to the learner. Our result does not remove that step.
