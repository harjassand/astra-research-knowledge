## C1: cancellation is a termwise operator identity

A definite-syndrome term V_(S,s) acts trivially outside S and commutes with every check not wholly contained in S. Hence each flipped check is contained in S. Set

    P_S = product_(C subset S) (I+C)/2, Q_S=I-P_S,
    H_S = sum_(C subset S) lambda_C(I-C)/2.

The kernel of H_S is P_S and its spectrum on Q_S is at least1, also for redundant checks. Equations(C10–C15) define

    Pcal V = P_S V P_S+Q_S V Q_S,
    Pcalperp V = P_S V Q_S+Q_S V P_S,
    A_(S,s) = P_S V Q_S H_S^-1-H_S^-1 Q_S V P_S.

All multiplying factors act in S and commute with all checks. Thus A has the same strong support and syndrome. It is anti-Hermitian for Hermitian input terms; otherwise pair adjoints. Outside checks commute with A, so [H0,A]=[H_S,A]. Using H_S P_S=0 and H_S H_S^-1 Q_S=Q_S gives, for each individual support/syndrome label,

    [H0,A_(S,s)] = -Q_S V P_S-P_S V Q_S,
    [H0,A_(S,s)]+V_(S,s)=Pcal V_(S,s).                  (1)

This is exact before multiplication by any global codespace projector. Both local projections contract operator norm. Orthogonality of the off-diagonal blocks gives ||A||<=||Pcalperp V||. A state in V_(S,s)P_S flips |s| checks, so ||A||<=||Pcalperp V||/|s| for nonempty s. Empty syndrome gives A=0.

## C5: exact-syndrome proof of far-commutator vanishing

Lemma27 requires

    Pcalperp_(S union S') [D_(S',s'),A_(S,s)]=0           (3)

when D is locally block diagonal, |S'|<d_s, and

    |S'|<tilde-f(dist(S',C)) for every flipped check C in s. (4)

The source first annihilates a projector on an enlarged region, then tersely descends to P_(S union S'). Mere commutation with an enlarged ground projector does not by itself justify that descent. The following two-case proof closes the intended step using exactly the source assumptions, with no extra support or norm charge.

**Nonempty s'.** Some flipped check is contained in S', so P_S'DP_S'=0. Local block diagonality [D,P_S']=0 strengthens this to DP_S'=P_S'D=0. Condition(4) places A's flipped checks outside S', hence [A,P_S']=0. Since P_(S union S')<=P_S', both [D,A]P_(S union S') and P_(S union S')[D,A] vanish. This proves(3), even if the commutator is nonzero.

**Empty s'.** Expand D into Paulis on S'. Each component commutes with all checks; weight below d makes it a stabilizer up to scalar phase. Lemma23's minimal-generator localization and(4) give a representation using no check in A's syndrome. A commutes with every chosen generator, so [D,A]=0 exactly. If A's syndrome is empty, A=0 already.

The strict inequality in(4) resolves the boundary: the minimal-expansion argument excludes a selected generator acting at that distance or farther. No inclusive-buffer assumption is needed. This proof does not infer local word classification from global annihilation.

## Localization prerequisite reconstructed from count soundness

For a minimal generator representation of a stabilizer supported away from site i, sort selected checks by the largest graph distance of their sites from i. Actual check supports are cliques of diameter1. Every prefix is cardinality-minimal: replacing it with fewer checks would shorten the full representation.

Before reaching the final stabilizer's support, a prefix's interior cancels and its remaining support lies on the next shell. A prefix containing x checks has weight at least min(d_c,f^-1(x)); otherwise count soundness shortens it. Each next-layer check can cancel at most Delta sites. Hence cumulative check count is at least

    g(1)=1,
    g(r+1)=g(r)+min(d_c,f^-1(g(r)))/Delta.               (5)

The complete stabilizer obeys f(|S'|)>=g(r). With tilde-f(r)=f^-1(g(r)), a stabilizer on |S'|<tilde-f(r) cannot require a selected generator at distance r or beyond. This is the exact contrapositive used in(4), corresponding to B22–B24/Lemma23.

This is existence of a minimal representation, not an efficient generator-search algorithm.
