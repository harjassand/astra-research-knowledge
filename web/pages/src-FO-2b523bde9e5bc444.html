# Competing physical route: retained optical domain writing

**Decision:** the highest-evidence retained structural-variant route found is 532-nm continuous-wave domain writing in iron-doped lithium niobate (Fe:LiNbO₃). It does make persistent, inversion-related ferroelectric domains. The result is an established optical poling/domain-engineering method, however, and its writing bias comes from photovoltaic charge separation plus a conductive boundary, with possible pyroelectric contribution. It does not demonstrate coherent optical-phase selection or a new GaAs antiphase-boundary repair operation. Its exposure, preparation, readout, and missing cycling/cost comparisons prevent a supported claim that it has higher expected value than the GaAs selective-melting hypothesis.

This is a scoped literature assessment, not an acquired experiment. No sample, laser, phase control, or independent physical measurement was available in this work. Physical capability for the proposed GaAs route and any cross-material transfer remain **UNKNOWN**.

## Strongest retained-writing platform

Sebastián-Vicente et al. (2024) irradiated an initially monodomain, z-cut, 1-mm Fe:LiNbO₃ crystal containing 0.25 mol% Fe with 532-nm CW light while the sample was immersed in ultrapure water. The reported absorption coefficient was 20.4 cm⁻¹; the incident face was +c and inversion was observed on the −c face. Conductive liquid was essential to the demonstrated reproducible pattern: low-conductivity media gave sparse, irregular domains and some inversion at the other face. The authors propose an interplay between the bulk photovoltaic effect and electrostatic screening at the crystal/liquid interface, and do not rule out pyroelectric contributions at higher intensities. They explicitly leave the complete mechanism unresolved.

This is retained structure, not merely a sign flip in an optical probe. The authors combined selective HF etching, surface profilometry, SEM, pyroelectric particle trapping, and 3D Čerenkov SHG microscopy. The inverted regions remained after the photovoltaic field had dark-decayed over days; later pyroelectric tests were conducted weeks after irradiation to rule out the residual photovoltaic field as the readout cause. That establishes persistence on the reported days-to-weeks observation scale, not device endurance or lifetime.

Asché et al. (2025) extended this platform from a Gaussian spot to spatially amplitude-shaped, parallel writing. A 532-nm CW laser and amplitude spatial light modulator (SLM) wrote domains from about 300 nm to over 400 µm laterally. The sample was the same class of z-cut 1-mm Fe:LiNbO₃ (0.25 mol% Fe, 20.4 cm⁻¹ absorption, Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ ≈ 0.13), fully immersed in deionized water of conductivity 11.6 µS/cm; the beam was focused on the −c face. Three-dimensional SHG scans and post-etch SEM/bright-field measurements characterize actual domain geometry. The paper demonstrates pattern shaping and parallel induction, not optical-phase encoding or a measured write/erase endurance cycle.

## What selects the sign

For two inversion-related polar variants, write their polarizations as \(\mathbf P_s=s\mathbf P_0\), with \(s\in\{-1,+1\}\). Their electric-field contribution to free-energy density is

\[
f_s=f_0-\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf P_s,
\qquad f_{+}-f_{-}=-2\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf P_0.
\]

Thus the sign-selecting operation is an inversion-odd field component along the polar axis. A scalar intensity mask alone is inversion-even; it specifies where and how much light is delivered, but not which of two otherwise symmetric variants is lower in energy. In Fe:LiNbO₃, absorbed visible light creates photovoltaic charge, and the conductive liquid screens surface charge. The crystal/liquid boundary, c-axis orientation, and charge transport provide the sign asymmetry. The 2025 structured-light system modulates amplitude and exposure with an SLM; it does not lock a carrier-envelope phase or a relative \(\omega/2\omega\) phase. Calling this a coherent-phase-selective operation would therefore misstate its control variable.

This known field-coupling identity is a mechanism check, not a new law. It also explains why the route cannot simply be transferred to GaAs: GaAs antiphase-boundary polarity and the polar LiNbO₃ order parameter are different structural coordinates, defects, and switching kernels. The incoming GaAs hypothesis still requires a measured phase-odd retained boundary response in its own specimen and geometry.

## Optical dose and complete cost boundary

In the 2024 paper, a 532-nm exposure of 86.4 W/cm² for 60 s is 5.184 kJ/cm²; the paper estimates about 620 J/cm² as a threshold exposure for quasi-circular domain formation under its tested geometry. In the 2025 structured-writing study, the exposure window for uniform domains shaped like the mask is approximately 8–16 kJ/cm². A reported lattice/ring used 537 W/cm² for 30 s (16.11 kJ/cm²); a large, roughly 400-µm ring used 14 W/cm² for 1200 s (16.8 kJ/cm²). These products are incident areal dose as reported by the papers. At that structured-writing window, uniformly exposing 1 mm² corresponds to **80–160 J incident on the specimen**. This area normalization is arithmetic, not a full pattern energy or process comparison.

Those values exclude laser wall-plug energy, SLM and objective losses, calibration, alignment, sample growth and monodomain preparation, cleaning, water handling/conductivity control, exposure yield, any thermal conditioning, and structural verification. They are not measured absorbed energy. The cited Fe:LiNbO₃ absorption coefficient gives a nominal 1/e attenuation length of about 0.49 mm; a one-pass Beer–Lambert estimate through 1 mm would transmit about 13%, but this estimate ignores the focused intensity distribution, reflections, interfaces, and the actual writing geometry, so it is not used as a measured absorbed dose.

Readout is also a real cost. The 2024 3D SHGM method used a pulsed 800-nm, 80-MHz, 90-fs probe with 270-mW source average power, attenuated to below 100 mW in the sample plane to avoid writing, then reconstructed domains from xyz scans. Surface chemical etching with 49% HF is destructive. The 2025 paper adds SEM and far-field SHG k-space measurements. No matched total-energy, throughput, yield, or cost-per-good-device comparison against electrical poling, thermal methods, or GaAs annealing/template growth is reported.

The optical papers report no observed crystal damage in the tested regimes, but do not provide a cycling/endurance series, controlled erase/rewrite demonstration, or long-term stability distribution. At high intensity the 2024 authors leave pyroelectric contributions open. In the 2025 work, domain fronts overgrow when exposure is high; close neighbors merge, slight SLM/beam nonuniformity changes the result, and deeper patterns develop internal spikes. Written domains extend to roughly 250 µm in a 1-mm crystal rather than through its full thickness. A 3.6-µm-spaced lattice was not uniformly merged everywhere. These are practical limits even for the demonstrated LiNbO₃ use case.

## Competing methods and the coherent-phonon test

| Route | Retained sign-selected structure? | Evidence and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical poling of LiNbO₃ | Yes; it is the direct, established ferroelectric baseline. | Room-temperature external-field periodic poling was reported in 1993. Visible-CW Fe:LiNbO₃ avoids patterned electrodes for its demonstrated geometry, but no matched whole-process energy, yield, or device benchmark establishes superiority. |
| Laser/thermal domain engineering | Yes in reported ferroelectric patterning workflows. | Existing approaches include focused UV writing, fs-laser domain writing, and pyroelectric-field-assisted seed-then-heat methods. One reported method heats fs-laser-seeded LiNbO₃ to 220–300 °C and obtains a retained 15 µm × 6.3 µm lattice after cooling. They spend optical/thermal processing energy and have their own resolution/throughput constraints; their existence makes generic light/heat-driven domain engineering non-novel. |
| Fe:LiNbO₃ visible-CW photovoltaic writing | Yes, with days-scale reported retention. | Primary experimental positive control. Established by 2024; structured-amplitude patterns demonstrated in 2025. Not carrier-phase selective, high exposure, Fe/liquid/interface dependent, no endurance or matched-cost result. |
| LiNbO₃ resonant phonon | No retained reversal established. | Mankowsky et al. used carrier-envelope-phase-stable 19-THz, 150-fs mid-IR pulses. Above 60 mJ/cm² the phase-sensitive SH field flips by 180° from about 0 to 200 fs, but the reversed polarization reaches only about 40% of equilibrium and rapidly returns. The authors state that a nonvolatile memory unit cannot be built from the demonstrated effect. |
| SrTiO₃ resonant phonon | A metastable, spatially bipolar polar state is reported, including evidence for oppositely oriented domains; no reversible single-variant bit-write is demonstrated. | Nova et al. report polar order lasting hours after repeated resonant mid-IR pumping. The induced pattern is inhomogeneous and the proposed photoflexoelectric mechanism creates a pair of opposing regions; the paper does not show phase-controlled switching between selected retained states. The main experiment used 15-µm, 1-kHz pulses and saturated after minutes; measurements were performed at 4 K, with the response detectable up to room temperature. |
| KNbO₃ coherent-pulse protocol; h-BN sliding route | Not experimentally demonstrated as a retained optical write. | The 2022 KNbO₃ paper is a first-principles/effective-Hamiltonian simulation; its partial reversal back-switches, and its stated modeled fields are around 5–14 MV/cm (the authors note effective-model fields can be about 20 times experiment). The 2024 h-BN paper is also a theoretical first-principles proposal: separate experiments had shown sliding ferroelectricity and photoexcited interlayer shear, but not their combination into retained laser switching. |
| GaAs post-growth APB annealing | Yes, as boundary-density reduction in the target material. | Barrett et al. report MOCVD GaAs on on-axis Si(001), capped with 20 nm ALD Al₂O₃ and annealed in Ar. At 650 °C for 7.5 min the measured surface APB density fell from 0.10 to 0.014 µm⁻¹; at ≥650 °C it fell to about 0.010 µm⁻¹ in under 10 min, with depth profiles near the detection limit through most of the film after 650 °C for 10 min. This is direct GaAs evidence and a serious baseline, although it uses a high-temperature cap/anneal/removal sequence, leaves nonzero surface defects, and is not a phase-selected rewrite. |
| Orientation-patterned GaAs template and regrowth | Yes, during fabrication. | Granger et al. demonstrate opposite-[001]/[00-1] domains patterned by wafer bonding, lithography/etch, and MBE regrowth; their 11-µm-wide, 7-µm-high waveguide has an 85-µm quasi-phase-matching period and generates broadband mid-IR output. This creates the intended alternating polarity in a new device rather than repairing an existing defective layer. It is an established device-fabrication comparator, not a post-growth light-write. |

The coherent phonon results are valuable transient symmetry control, and the SrTiO₃ experiment reports a real metastable bipolar polar-domain pattern. Neither supplies the requested operation: reproducible, phase-selectable, inversion-related structure that remains after excitation under a complete cost and cycling budget. A transient 180° SHG phase flip is not evidence of a retained state; a metastable SHG signal and induced current are not, by themselves, a reversible write/erase protocol.

## Consequence for the GaAs question

The Fe:LiNbO₃ route has stronger evidence for retained *ferroelectric* domain writing than the GaAs phase-selective-melting hypothesis currently has for retained GaAs variant selection. It is therefore a useful experimental comparator and a proven operation in its own material. But it is not a higher-value GaAs route: it is already prior art, relies on a material-specific photovoltaic/screening boundary, consumes substantial incident dose for well-formed structured domains, writes only a surface-attached fraction of the crystal thickness, and has no matched cost or cycling advantage.

The strongest direct same-material comparator is already thermal: Barrett et al. measured a surface APB-density reduction from 0.10 to 0.014 µm⁻¹ after 650 °C for 7.5 min on GaAs/Si, using a 20-nm ALD Al₂O₃ cap and Ar anneal; at 650 °C for 10 min, their depth profiles put APBs near detection limits through most of the film. That is a meaningful high-temperature process with residual surface APBs, not a free or defect-free rewrite. On the device side, Granger et al. demonstrate orientation-patterned GaAs made by bonding opposite crystal-axis wafers, lithographically defining domains, and MBE regrowth. Their 85-µm polarity period and 7-µm-high waveguide deliver a functional mid-IR nonlinear device, but the polarity pattern is engineered during fabrication rather than written into an already grown defective target. Together these show that actual-target thermal repair and template-defined growth are stronger practical comparators than cross-material LiNbO₃. A proposed new optical operation would need to improve a matched GaAs output over both while counting its own preparation, source/phase control, heat, readout, yield, damage, and retention.

The only potentially distinct operation still worth testing is **phase-reversed retained output at fixed dose and thermal history** in the actual target material. For GaAs this means, on the same pre-mapped opposite-variant specimen, compare \(\phi\) and \(\phi+\pi\) for phase-locked \(\omega/2\omega\) illumination at equal single-colour fluences, with randomized-phase, separated-pulse, single-colour, and matched-temperature controls. Measure the phase-odd carrier/deposited-energy response first, then post-drive boundary motion and stoichiometry by an independent structural assay after the optical field has vanished and over a declared retention interval. Count conversion/stabilization, specimen preparation, calibration, cooling/thermal history, all optical energy, damage, readout, and yield against matched annealing and templated-growth controls. A transient SHG/current contrast, mobility-only increase, selective ablation, or uncontrolled regrowth fails this gate. Until such a result exists, no new retained-selective operation or practical advantage is established.

## Status and reproducibility

- **Measured retained optical structural switch:** established in Fe:LiNbO₃ by the cited primary experiments; persistence was observed for days after photovoltaic-field decay.
- **Coherent-phase-selective retained switch:** not established by the sources reviewed.
- **Transfer to GaAs selective APB repair:** unqualified; no transfer inference is made.
- **Cost superiority, cycling life, industrial scaling, and practical expected value:** UNKNOWN.
