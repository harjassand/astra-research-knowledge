# Stochastic reaction recurrence: proof reconstruction and boundary

## Outcome and scope

The clearest conclusion is a strict separation among four statements:

1. a deterministic ODE has a common classwise attractor;
2. a jump process stays in a supplied compact region up to a finite horizon with high probability;
3. each closed communicating class is nonexplosive and positive recurrent;
4. stationary laws across all such classes in one deterministic stoichiometric class are uniformly tight.

None of the first three statements implies the fourth in general. The Astra N77 network gives an explicit counterexample to that transfer while every displayed stochastic class is itself positive recurrent. Separate primary literature gives strongly endotactic, hence deterministically permanent, networks whose stochastic mass-action processes are transient or explosive. Those two networks are not weakly reversible, so they do not refute a recurrence conjecture restricted to arbitrary-dimensional weakly reversible networks.

The N76 and N77 cards trace to a supplied summary only; the underlying U packet, code and logs were not present in the retrieved repository. I reconstructed N76's algebra and independently checked the N77 example below, but did not replay the source-reported 8,034-state/25,456-transition and 216-fixture diagnostics. Status labels below distinguish those limits.

## N76: exact boundary identity, but no physical-time drift

Use stochastic mass-action propensities

\[
\lambda_{y\to y'}(x)=\kappa_{y\to y'}x^{\underline y},\qquad
x^{\underline y}=\prod_i\frac{x_i!}{(x_i-y_i)!},
\]

with a disabled reaction assigned propensity zero. For a finite complex set \(\mathcal C\), let the available sources at \(x\in\mathbb N_0^d\) be \(\mathcal C(x)=\{y\in\mathcal C:y\le x\}\), and define

\[
F(x)=\min_{y\in\mathcal C(x)}\log((x-y)!),\qquad
M(x)=\max_{y\in\mathcal C(x)}x^{\underline y},
\]

where vector factorials are coordinatewise products. If \(y\to y'\) fires, put \(z=x-y+y'\). Then \(z\ge y'\), so \(y'\) is an available source candidate at \(z\), and

\[
z-y'=x-y.
\]

Therefore \(F(z)\le\log((x-y)!)\). Also

\[
F(x)=\log(x!)-\log M(x),
\]

because \((x-y)!=x!/x^{\underline y}\). Combining these identities gives, for every enabled reaction including at the zero-molecule boundary,

\[
F(x-y+y')-F(x)\le\log\frac{M(x)}{x^{\underline y}}.
\]

This proves the discrete correction in N76 without replacing falling factorials by monomials. It handles availability switches exactly.

For the embedded jump chain \(X_j\), the next-reaction probability is \(\kappa_r x^{\underline y_r}/q(x)\), where \(q(x)=\sum_r\kappa_r x^{\underline y_r}\). In a finite weakly reversible network every complex has an outgoing reaction. Let \(\kappa_{\min}=\min_r\kappa_r>0\). A source attaining \(M(x)\) contributes at least \(\kappa_{\min}M(x)\) to \(q(x)\). Hence

\[
\mathbb E[e^{F(X_{j+1})-F(X_j)}\mid X_j=x]
\le \frac{M(x)}{q(x)}\sum_r\kappa_r
\le \frac{\sum_r\kappa_r}{\kappa_{\min}}=:C_{\rm rate}.
\]

Taking \(C_{\rm rate}\ge1\), the process \(e^{F(X_j)-F(X_0)-j\log C_{\rm rate}}\) is a nonnegative supermartingale. Doob's maximal inequality yields

\[
\Pr\!\left(\max_{j\le N}F(X_j)\ge F(X_0)+N\log C_{\rm rate}+u\right)\le e^{-u}.
\]

This is an upper-growth envelope per reaction event. It gives neither negative drift toward a finite set nor a bound on elapsed holding times \(E_j/q(X_j)\). It therefore proves neither positive recurrence nor nonexplosion in physical time. A recurrence proof still needs a classwise physical-time Foster–Lyapunov inequality (for example, a proper \(U\) with \(\mathcal LU\le-\epsilon\) outside a finite set, together with the usual regularity/nonexplosion argument), or a coarse-graining theorem that preserves return probabilities and controls the accumulated holding time of fast boundary cycles.

The exact identity is reconstructed here from [N76](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/systems_metabolism/astra/cards__N76-factorial-reaction-boundary.txt). Its event-chain bound and the 8,034/25,456 finite verification counts remain source-reported in the supplied U summary; the original derivation/code/logs were not supplied.

## N77: positive recurrence in each class, failure of uniform tightness

Consider the four-species network, with all rate constants one,

\[
2C\longrightarrow A+B+C\longrightarrow 3A+2B\longrightarrow2C,
\qquad 2C\rightleftarrows2C+D.
\]

Its complexes are \(2C,A+B+C,3A+2B,2C+D\). They form one linkage class; the stoichiometric rank is three, so the deficiency is \(4-1-3=0\). At \(c=(1,1,1,1)\), each complex has equal incoming and outgoing total flux, so the network is complex balanced. The only linear conservation law is \(B+C\), and the positive deterministic class through \(c\) is \(B+C=2\). The global-attractor theorem for complex-balanced systems gives convergence of every positive deterministic trajectory in this class to \(c\) [Craciun, arXiv:1501.02860].

For each integer \(n\ge0\), the stochastic CTMC has the closed communicating class

\[
\Gamma_n=\{(n,0,2,d),\ (n+1,1,1,d),\ (n+3,2,0,d):d\in\mathbb N_0\}.
\]

The three population phases cycle in one direction. Their rates are respectively

\[
2,\qquad n+1,\qquad 2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3).
\]

At phase \((n,0,2,d)\), the extra coordinate has transitions \(d\to d+1\) at rate 2 and \(d\to d-1\) at rate \(2d\). All five reactions preserve \(B+C=2\), and all three cycle transitions preserve the nonlinear pathwise quantity

\[
I(A,B)=A-\frac{B(B+1)}2=n.
\]

Thus \(\Gamma_n\) is closed. It is irreducible because the phase cycle visits the first phase and the immigration/death moves there connect every \(d\). It is nonexplosive: phase changes have rates bounded by \(\max\{2,n+1,2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)\}\); births of \(D\) have rate at most 2, hence finitely many occur on every bounded time interval, and deaths cannot outnumber the initial \(D\) count plus births.

An invariant probability on \(\Gamma_n\) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_n(n,0,2,d)&=Z_n^{-1}\frac12\frac{e^{-1}}{d!},\\
\pi_n(n+1,1,1,d)&=Z_n^{-1}\frac1{n+1}\frac{e^{-1}}{d!},\\
\pi_n(n+3,2,0,d)&=Z_n^{-1}\frac1{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}\frac{e^{-1}}{d!},
\end{aligned}
\]

with \(Z_n=\frac12+\frac1{n+1}+\frac1{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}\). To verify global balance, the unnormalized phase fluxes agree:

\[
\tfrac12\cdot2=1,\quad
\tfrac1{n+1}\cdot(n+1)=1,\quad
\tfrac1{2N}\cdot2N=1,
\quad N=(n+1)(n+2)(n+3).
\]

For the \(D\) transitions, the Poisson factor satisfies \(p_{d-1}=d p_d\) and \((d+1)p_{d+1}=p_d\), which balances the rates 2 and \(2d\) in the first phase. Since \(\Gamma_n\) is irreducible and nonexplosive and has an invariant probability, it is positive recurrent. This also follows from the complex-balanced product-form theorem of Anderson, Craciun and Kurtz, but the local balance above makes the particular class law explicit.

Every state in \(\Gamma_n\) has \(A\ge n\). Consequently, for every compact \(K\subset\mathbb R_+^4\), \(\pi_n(K)=0\) once \(n>\sup_{x\in K}x_A\). The family \((\pi_n)_{n\ge0}\) is not tight, even though every \(\pi_n\) is a positive-recurrent stationary law in the same linear stoichiometric plane \(B+C=2\), and every \(\Gamma_n\) contains the strictly positive state \((n+1,1,1,1)\).

The nonlinear invariant is not conserved by the deterministic ODE. For example, at \((A,B,C,D)=(2,1,1,1)\), the ordinary mass-action vector field has \(\dot A=-19\), \(\dot B=-13\), so \(\dot I=\dot A-(B+\tfrac12)\dot B=\tfrac12\ne0\). This is the exact discrete/continuous mechanism: the CTMC's falling-factorial availability preserves a hidden class label that the real-monomial ODE does not preserve.

This refutes only the proposed **uniform-tightness-across-classes** transfer. It does not refute componentwise positive recurrence; every displayed component is positive recurrent. It also does not say that the full CTMC has no stationary probability: complex balance supplies a product-Poisson stationary law on the full state space, and a mixture over components can remain tight. The source card's report of 216 fixtures and explicit balance checks was not replayed; the class, law, normalization, balance equations, nonexplosion and tightness failure above were reconstructed directly from the supplied network and rates. See [N77](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/systems_metabolism/astra/cards__N77-stationary-tightness-counterexample.txt) and the product-form source [ACK (2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3042).

## Primary stochastic counterexamples to a broad N33-to-recurrence transfer

Anderson, Cappelletti, Kim and Nguyen's *Tier structure of strongly endotactic reaction networks* provides two decisive examples. Strongly endotactic networks have permanent deterministic mass-action trajectories, but stochastic mass-action need not be recurrent.

**Transient example (their Example 3.1).** The network is

\[
0\to2A+B\to4A+4B\to A.
\]

For any fixed positive rate constants, the paper constructs a positive-probability route to a state \((a,0)\), then repeats a three-reaction cycle increasing \(A\) by one each time. Starting a cycle at \((n,0)\), the zero-order reaction fires first. At \((n+2,1)\), the probability that \(2A+B\to4A+4B\) fires next is

\[
p_1(n)=\frac{\kappa_1(n+2)(n+1)}{\kappa_1(n+2)(n+1)+\kappa_0}=1-O(n^{-2}).
\]

At \((n+4,4)\), the probability that \(4A+4B\to A\) fires next is

\[
p_2(n)=\frac{\kappa_2(n+4)(n+3)(n+2)(n+1)\,4!}{\kappa_2(n+4)(n+3)(n+2)(n+1)\,4!+4\kappa_1(n+4)(n+3)+\kappa_0}=1-O(n^{-2}).
\]

The product of these success probabilities over all \(n\) is positive because the sum of failure probabilities converges. Hence there is positive probability of escaping to infinity without return, so every state is transient. This example is strongly endotactic but not weakly reversible.

**Explosive example (their Example 3.2).** The network is

\[
0\to2A\to4A+B\to6A+4B\to3A.
\]

Its last three reactions have a positive-probability infinite successful sequence from every initial state after a finite access path; each cycle increases \(A\) by one. Starting each cycle at \((n,0)\), the desired rates at the three choices are proportional to \((n)_2\), \((n+2)_4\), and \((n+4)_6\,4!\); their competing rates grow at strictly lower orders. The failure probabilities are therefore \(O(n^{-2})\), and the infinite product of success probabilities is positive. The holding-time expectations along the prescribed transitions are bounded by a convergent sum of orders \(n^{-2}+n^{-4}+n^{-6}\). Thus explosion occurs with positive probability for every positive fixed rate choice. This is also strongly endotactic and not weakly reversible.

These published examples decisively block deriving stochastic nonexplosion or recurrence from deterministic permanence alone under N33's all-endotactic scope. They do not settle the more restrictive weakly reversible case targeted by N76. The inspected source has one prose line in Example 3.1 saying \(4A+4B\to0\) where the displayed network and the cycle calculation use \(4A+4B\to A\); the report follows the displayed network and cycle, and treats the isolated prose occurrence as a source typo. Primary source: [Anderson et al. (arXiv:1808.05328v2), Examples 3.1–3.2 and Theorem 7.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1808.05328v2).

## Actual extension theorems and their limits

There are positive theorems, but they require mechanisms absent from N76's event-chain estimate.

- **One-dimensional recurrence.** Wiuf and Xu prove that a one-dimensional weakly reversible stochastic mass-action reaction network is positive recurrent on each positive irreducible class, with exponential ergodicity, under their H2–H3 hypotheses (stoichiometric dimension one and both positive and negative reaction directions; mass action supplies the availability condition). The broader one-dimensional endotactic theorem also gives nonexplosion and positive recurrence when the state space consists of closed communicating classes. This is a real classwise recurrence theorem, but it is dimension one. [Wiuf–Xu, arXiv:2012.07954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07954).
- **Bimolecular nonexplosion.** Xu proves that every second-order endotactic stochastic mass-action system is nonexplosive; in particular, every bimolecular weakly reversible stochastic mass-action system is nonexplosive. This controls explosion under a molecularity restriction, but it does not establish positive recurrence for arbitrary infinite classes. [Xu, arXiv:2409.05340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05340).
- **Added high-order drains.** Theorem 7.2 of Anderson et al. proves positive recurrence of each closed irreducible component after adjoining, for each species \(S_i\), a nonempty family of drain reactions \(aS_i\to\sum_j r'_jS_j\) with \(a\ge m-1\) and \(\sum_jr'_j\le a-1\), where \(m\) is the maximum source molecularity of the base strongly endotactic network. It also assumes the strict ordering \(\max_{r\in R'}\|y'_r\|_1<\min_{r\in R'}\|y_r\|_1\). Optional added reactions have source molecularity at most \(m-2\). A binary strongly endotactic network plus all species outflows and selected inflows is a stated corollary. The proof constructs a proper physical-time Lyapunov function with generator drift bounded above by \(-1\) outside a finite set, which supplies nonexplosion and finite expected return. The added drains are exactly the structure that the transient counterexample lacks.

The 2023 note *Any Stochastic Reaction Network has a Stationary Measure* is not a positive-recurrence bridge: it explicitly allows an invariant measure with infinite total mass, so it does not give a stationary probability or recurrence. [Xu, arXiv:2312.07590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07590).

## What N33–N35 do and do not provide

**N33 (deterministic permanence).** Its contract is a finite power-law reaction diagram on the positive orthant, with rates \(k_e(t)\in[\kappa,K]\) measurable in time and a positive deterministic stoichiometric class \(P=(c+S)\cap\mathbb R_{>0}^d\). Its candidate theorem supplies one compact convex positive absorber for each fixed class, common across rate paths, and a finite entry-time bound for each initial point independent of the chosen rate path. This is a deterministic ODE/differential-inclusion statement. It has no jump generator, falling-factorial intensities, holding times, or stationary-law estimates. The N77 and published strongly endotactic examples isolate two failures of promoting it directly.

**N34 (exact certificates).** The rational checker can certify a supplied finite candidate's local invariance, box-conditional entry, a globally absorbing set when containment of the entire positive class is separately certified, or a finite certificate chain for a supplied initial polytope. A finite search budget can return `UNKNOWN`; there is no finite all-scale synthesis theorem for arbitrary unbounded classes. Even a valid ODE certificate proves no CTMC Foster drift because \(\mathcal L U(x)=\sum_r\lambda_r(x)[U(x+\nu_r)-U(x)]\) is a different object from \(\nabla U(x)\cdot f(x)\).

**N35 (stopped finite-horizon safety).** Under a supplied buffered lattice start, compact polytope, strict inward collars, bounded predictable controls and factorial propensities, N35 bounds \(\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\) by an initial-buffer term plus a term proportional to \(VT e^{-cV}\). That is a finite-horizon exit theorem. Letting \(T\to\infty\) makes this upper bound useless; the theorem does not establish infinite-time survival, nonexplosion after exit, classwise recurrence, or stationary tightness. A direct counterexample is the N35 finite-volume network \(A\rightleftarrows B\), \(N_A+N_B=V\), with both rates one: \(N_A\) is an irreducible finite birth–death CTMC on \(\{0,\ldots,V\}\), hence positive recurrent, and it visits both zero-species endpoints infinitely often almost surely, even though the deterministic concentration converges to \(1/2\). N35's finite exponential-horizon reliability and infinite-time boundary recurrence can both be true.

## Precise missing lemma and rejected transfers

For the general weakly reversible mass-action target, the missing stochastic bridge is not another deterministic absorber or another stopped exit estimate. One needs, **for each closed integer communicating class \(\Gamma\)**, a proper classwise function \(U_\Gamma\) and a physical-time generator bound strong enough to imply finite expected return to a finite subset and nonexplosion, or a recurrence-preserving reduction of the embedded event chain that accounts for all expected holding times. To claim uniform stationary tightness over a family of classes, a further estimate must be uniform in the hidden class parameter; N77 proves this latter estimate cannot follow from a common deterministic attractor alone.

The following routes were therefore rejected:

- replacing \(x^{\underline y}\) by \(x^y\) at a boundary state;
- treating an upper moment per reaction event as a negative recurrence drift;
- dividing or rescaling by the total reaction rate without proving the resulting holding-time sum diverges;
- taking an N35 finite-time exit bound to \(T=\infty\);
- inferring a common compact support for stationary laws from N33's deterministic absorber;
- using strongly endotactic transient/explosive examples as counterexamples to weakly reversible recurrence, since those examples are not weakly reversible;
- treating an arbitrary invariant measure as a finite stationary probability.
