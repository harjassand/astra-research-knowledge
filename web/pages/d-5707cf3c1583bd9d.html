GROUPS/TOPLOGY: SIMPLE NONSOFICITY AND LINEAR PERMUTATION STABILITY
7 October 2026. Internally proved general lemma; no claim of priority.

Statement.
Let S=<X|R> be an infinite finitely presented simple group, with X and R
finite. Put
  defect(sigma)=max_{r in R} d_H(r(sigma),1),
  movement(sigma)=max_{x in X} d_H(sigma_x,1),
for any assignment sigma_x in Sym(D), any positive integer D. Then the
following are equivalent:
  (a) S is nonsofic;
  (b) S is permutation stable on the same set of D points;
  (c) There is a finite C, depending on this presentation, such that
      movement(sigma) <= C defect(sigma) for every D and every sigma.
The exact finite actions of S are all trivial. No infinite-dimensional,
unitary, rank-metric, property-(T), or computational compiler statement is
part of the theorem.

1. Finite obstruction form of nonsoficity.
For a finitely presented group, soficity is equivalent to: for every finite
list of nonidentity words W and every delta>0, there is some D and sigma
with defect(sigma)<delta and every w in W moving at least half the points.

Here is why the half-separation formulation suffices. For a chosen finite
group test, choose words representing its elements and include all
nonidentity pairwise differences in W. Each visible multiplication error
is a null word and hence a finite product of conjugates of defining
relators; call the largest such area A. Its evaluated Hamming error is
at most A delta. The diagonal action on D^k tuples is a homomorphism
Sym(D)->Sym(D^k), and
  d_H(P^diag(k),Q^diag(k))=1-(1-d_H(P,Q))^k <= k d_H(P,Q).
A nonidentity word with fixed fraction <=1/2 acquires fixed fraction
<=2^-k. First fix k for the desired final separation, then choose delta
small enough to pay k A delta. This gives the ordinary sofic test. The
reverse implication is immediate. Null-word areas are finite here;
neither an area algorithm nor a uniform input cost is being assumed.

Therefore nonsoficity supplies a FINITE list w_1,...,w_M of nonidentity
words and some 0<delta_*<=1 such that no assignment of any degree has
  defect(sigma)<=delta_*, all Fix(w_i(sigma))/D<=1/2.
If the initial negation uses strict defects, shrink delta_* to obtain this
inclusive version. The obstruction list/threshold are existential unless
a separate certificate actually supplies them.

2. Normal-generation certificates.
Each nonidentity w_i normally generates S by simplicity. For every x in X
and i, there is a FREE-GROUP identity expressing x as a product of
conjugates of w_i^{+/-1} and r^{+/-1}, r in R. Let A>=1 bound the number
of w_i factors in all these finitely many identities, and B>=0 bound the
number of R factors. Bi-invariance and the triangle inequality give
  movement(sigma) <= A d_H(w_i(sigma),1) + B defect(sigma)
for EVERY i. Once these word identities are supplied, verification is
free reduction. Enumeration acquires them under the promise that the
group is simple and the listed w_i are nonidentity; no running-time bound
or small certificate length is asserted.

3. Linear collapse.
Write v=movement(sigma), delta=defect(sigma). Suppose delta<=delta_*/2,
v>2B delta, and v>4A delta/delta_*. Then each w_i moves at least v/(2A).
Choose the INTEGER
  k=ceil(2A/v).
Its diagonal fixed fraction is <=exp(-k v/(2A))<=exp(-1)<1/2, and the
diagonal relator defect is <=k delta <=(2A/v+1)delta<delta_*.
This contradicts the supplied obstruction. Hence, at all degrees,
  v <= C delta,
  C=max{2B,4A/delta_*,2/delta_*}.
The last term handles delta>delta_*/2 by v<=1. This is a rational, finite
constant if delta_* and the finite certificates are supplied. It is not
an efficiently acquired or presentation-size-uniform constant.

4. Equivalence and exact actions.
An infinite simple group has no nontrivial homomorphism to Sym(D): a
nontrivial homomorphism would be injective, which is impossible into a
finite group. Thus (c) gives (b), with the trivial action as the same-degree
correction. If S were sofic, increasingly accurate approximations of a
finite presentation would have nontrivial generators moving almost all
points; they cannot converge to the only exact action, the trivial one.
Therefore (b) implies (a). Together with section 3 this proves the theorem.

Equivalent ultraproduct check.
Failure of qualitative collapse supplies relator defects tending to zero
and one fixed generator moving >=epsilon after a subsequence. The Hamming
metric ultraproduct gives a nontrivial homomorphism from S. Simplicity makes
it injective. Every finite list has positive minimum separation, and
diagonal powers amplify that separation to a sofic test. This independently
checks (a)->(b), but the finite certificate proof above is what gives the
linear rate.

5. Consequence for finite complexes.
The finite presentation complex can be subdivided to a finite simplicial
2-complex. A permutation-valued 1-cochain, after fixing a spanning-tree
gauge, is an assignment of permutations to presentation generators.
The bound says its distance to a coboundary is bounded by a presentation-
dependent constant times its triangle coboundary defect. Finite
subdivision and gauge fixing change constants by finite factors. Thus an
audited finitely presented simple nonsofic S gives a finite complex with
infinite fundamental group, only trivial connected finite covers, and a
linear permutation coboundary-expansion inequality at every permutation
degree. A simplicial complex, explicit constants and gauges have NOT been
materialized for the Astra candidate.

6. Prior-art and import scope.
The standard direction 'sofic + permutation stable implies residually
finite' is in Becker--Lubotzky--Thom, Stability and invariant random
subgroups, primary author/public manuscript:
  https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10190747
The present proof adds the simple-group converse and its elementary linear
rate to the working ledger. Bounded primary-source searches found no
explicit prior statement of this precise equivalence/rate; that is NOT a
priority finding. All ingredients are elementary standard mechanisms.
No external result asserting existence of S was used to prove the
conditional structural lemma. Such existence is a separate load-bearing
input for an actual example.

Falsification boundaries.
Simplicity cannot be dropped: G*Z has nontrivial exact finite actions from
the Z quotient even when G is nonsofic. The finite relator interface cannot
be replaced by an uncharged infinite relation list. The proof does not
give a finite universal certificate-acquisition algorithm for arbitrary
presentations. Approximate unitary norm errors do not obey the same
diagonal fixed-point amplification identity.
