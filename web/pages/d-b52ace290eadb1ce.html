## Strongest claim

**Conditional thermal-count transfer.** Let \(H=\frac12R^TGR+c\) be a finite-mode, stable, centered quadratic bosonic Hamiltonian, with \(G>0\), inverse temperature \(\beta>0\), and Gibbs state \(\rho_\beta\). Write its supplied real covariance as \(V_\beta\), in the convention where vacuum covariance is \(I/2\), and let \(K_\beta=V_\beta-I/2\). Apply a known passive mode transformation and uniform pure loss of power transmissivity \(0\leq\eta\leq1/10\), with vacuum in every loss port, then measure all output modes by photon-number-resolving detection. Assume the active-support condition \(R_0=\mathcal P(K_0)>0\) required by the supplied N72 certificate, where \(K_0=\eta U K_\beta U^T\) and \(U\) is the real passive transformation.

The output count law \(P_\beta^\eta\) has the **ideal centered-reset sampler** \(Q_\beta^\eta\) with the candidate bound
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_\beta^\eta,Q_\beta^\eta)
\leq \min\left\{1,\frac{5184}{25}\,\Phi_\beta(\eta)\right\},\qquad
\Phi_\beta(\eta)=\eta^3\sum_a x_a^2\left(x_a+\frac1{1-\eta}\right),
\]
where \(x_a\) are the eigenvalues of \(K_\beta\). Equivalently,
\[
\Phi_\beta(\eta)=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(K_0+\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}I\right)K_0^2\right].
\]
This is a measurement-law statement for a mixed quadratic Gibbs input after **vacuum pure loss**. It is conditional on the N71/N72 source-derived Schur-flow/reset theorem; it is not a certified new reset theorem. It does not approximate the Gibbs density operator in trace norm, compute its partition function, cover nonquadratic Gibbs states or displaced states, or cover a thermal loss environment.

## Derivation of the transfer

1. Williamson diagonalization writes a stable quadratic Gibbs state as a centered Gaussian state. Its thermofield-double purification is Gaussian: for normal frequency \(\omega_j>0\), the thermal factor is
   \[
   \sqrt{1-e^{-\beta\omega_j}}\sum_{n\geq0}e^{-\beta\omega_j n/2}|n,n\rangle,
   \quad \tanh r_j=e^{-\beta\omega_j/2}.
   \]
   Apply the Hamiltonian's Gaussian diagonalizer to the system half. Bloch–Messiah then writes this finite-mode pure Gaussian purification as passive optics acting on independent single-mode squeezed vacua; the final passive factor fixes vacuum and drops out. The purifying modes are traced after the system output is selected.

2. If \(P\) selects the physical modes from the purification and \(W\) is the optional passive output circuit, the source-to-detected-mode matrix for uniform vacuum loss is
   \[
   T=\sqrt\eta\,W P U_{\rm BM}.
   \]
   Since \(P U_{\rm BM}\) has orthonormal rows, \(TT^*=\eta I\). This is a legal contraction from independent squeezed-vacuum sources to the measured modes; the actual loss environment is vacuum. The TFD modes are source modes, not a thermal loss bath.

3. In the T72 weighted-projection argument, the realified Gram matrix is \(C=LL^T=\eta I\), hence the passive/odd covariance defect satisfies \(D=XR_0^{-1}X-R_0\preceq C\). For \(\eta\leq1/10\), choose the passive shift \(B=C(I-C)^{-1}=\eta(1-\eta)^{-1}I\). Operator monotonicity gives \(B\succeq D(I-uD)^{-1}\) for \(0\leq u\leq1\), and \(\|B\|\leq1/9\). The supplied T72 stopped-reset theorem then yields \(\operatorname{TV}\leq(5184/25)\operatorname{Tr}[(K_0+B)K_0^2]\).

4. Because \(U\) is orthogonal and \(B\) is scalar, trace invariance gives
   \[
   \operatorname{Tr}[(K_0+B)K_0^2]
   =\eta^3\left(\operatorname{Tr}K_\beta^3+\frac{\operatorname{Tr}K_\beta^2}{1-\eta}\right)
   =\eta^3\sum_a x_a^2\left(x_a+\frac1{1-\eta}\right).
   \]
   This formula is nonnegative: physicality gives \(x_a\geq-1/2\), while \((1-\eta)^{-1}>1\).

The result controls the full joint PNR distribution and therefore every event probability additively. For any \(|f(\mathbf n)|\leq1\), it gives \(|\mathbb E_P f-\mathbb E_Q f|\leq2\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\). It does not give relative accuracy for an individual rare Fock pattern.

## Exact example and executable evidence

Take one parametric oscillator
\[
H=\omega a^\dagger a+\frac g2(a^2+a^{\dagger2}),\quad \omega=1,\ g=-3/5.
\]
It is stable, with normal frequency \(\widetilde\omega=4/5\) and \(\tanh(2s)=g/\omega=-3/5\), so \(e^{-2s}=2\) in the convention used for the q quadrature. Choose \(\beta\widetilde\omega=\log4\). Then \(n_\beta=1/3\), and
\[
V_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(5/3,5/12),\qquad
K_\beta=\operatorname{diag}(7/6,-1/12).
\]
The state is nonclassical in the P-representation sense because one covariance eigenvalue is below vacuum. For \(\eta=1/1000\), the covariance decomposition has \(D=(7/39)\eta\), \(B=1/999\), and
\[
\Phi_1=1701419/575424000000000.
\]
For 10,000 independent copies, exact rational arithmetic gives expected detected count \(65/12\) and the ideal-reset upper bound \(1701419/277500000\approx0.00613124\). The candidate finite-bit constant from N73 gives \(1701419/90931200\approx0.0187111\) before its extra additive precision error. The code [thermal_tfd_exact.py](thermal_tfd_exact.py) reproduces these fractions and checks \(D\leq\eta\); it verifies the algebra and budget arithmetic only, not the imported reset theorem.

For \(m\) identical copies with fixed pre-loss covariance and \(\eta_m=c m^{-1/3}\), the bound remains constant-order while the exact mean count grows as \(\Theta(m^{2/3})\). This is the same exponent as Lim's uniform-loss reset result, now transferred to a mixed, genuinely squeezed quadratic Gibbs family; it is not an improvement to that exponent.

## Costs, prior art, and status boundaries

The ideal reset cost inherited from T is expected arithmetic
\[
O((1+E_0)[d^3+Md^2+d\,b_{\rm inv}]),\quad
E_0=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}K_0=\tfrac\eta2\operatorname{Tr}K_\beta,
\]
with active rank \(d\), output modes \(M\), and charged scalar-survival inversion count \(b_{\rm inv}\); storage is \(O(Md+d^2)\). Covariance/Hamiltonian acquisition is not included. T73 supplies a rational-input finite-bit candidate with polynomial bit cost and extra \(\epsilon\), but its full high-precision engine was not executed and its schedule is impractical. Generic \(e^{-\beta H}\) covariances are not rational: a rational covariance approximation needs a separate, charged TV-continuity bound.

This transfer is distinct from exact positive-P sampling of a number-conserving thermal Gibbs state, which is already available and is the better comparator there. It matters only when the quadratic Gibbs covariance is nonclassical and the covariance certificate is small. Prior primary sources already study finite-temperature Gaussian boson sampling, loss, and thermal environments; the new step here is the TFD-to-vacuum-loss interface plus T72's covariance-only certificate, not the existence of thermal sampling. Oh et al. (2023) use a tunable MPS approximation to the pure Gaussian component of lossy GBS. This candidate has no MPS bond-width dependence when its reset certificate is small, but offers only PNR-law accuracy and has not been benchmarked against that method.

An important failure boundary is a nonvacuum loss bath. If the signal transmission is zero but the environment has thermal mean \(n_B>0\), the output is thermal rather than vacuum; treating the signal-only matrix as \(T=0\) would incorrectly give \(S_3=0\) and an exact-vacuum conclusion. Purifying and including the bath as additional squeezed sources changes the effective source-to-output contraction and can remove the small-spectrum condition. Nonlinear interacting Gibbs states and displacement are likewise outside this derivation. Finally, additive TV error cannot yield a relative approximation to a tensor-network Fock amplitude: estimating an event of probability \(p\) to relative error \(\delta\) by samples requires order \(1/(p\delta^2)\) calls, and the TV guarantee itself only controls absolute error. No hafnian/permanent FPRAS or tensor-entry approximation follows.
