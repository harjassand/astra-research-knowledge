## 1. Normalize without changing degree

Put a_i=sqrt(q_ii)>0 and m_i=M_i/a_i, s_i=a_iS_i. The normalized matrix has diagonal1 and correlations r_ij=q_ij/(a_i a_j). A_i=a_i² A'_i, H_ij=a_ia_jH'_ij, and B=a_1a_2a_3B'. Thus all polynomial total degrees are preserved. Diagonal sign changes of both M_i,S_i change r_ij by the product of their signs and B by the product of all three signs. Permutations likewise preserve the problem.

A real degree3 solution implies a rational degree3 solution for the original rational q: modulo C, equality of coefficients is a finite linear system over Q in the unknown b and membership multipliers of whatever finite degree supplies a lift. A real solution to a rational linear system has a rational solution by Gaussian elimination. More directly, evaluation in the finite Q-algebra gives a rational linear system for the degree3 representative. This descent makes no acquisition algorithm compute square roots of hidden q.

Hence prove existence/construction after normalization, then transport. In the rest of the note write normalized variables again as m,s.

## 2. Generic, diagonal and one-edge cases

If the three squared correlations are pairwise distinct, C consists only of the all-root branch. The odd inverse b0 of B in Q[s]/(s_i²−1) lies in the four-dimensional span(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_1s_2s_3): multiplication by B is an invertible8×8 matrix, and simultaneous-sign parity makes the inverse odd. This gives degree≤3 with no further interpolation. Invertibility follows at the eight real points from B/(s_1s_2s_3)=3−sum r_ij>0.

If all correlations vanish, all64 diagonal points occur and

    b=(8/81) product_i(s_i+2m_i).

On either root or mass branch, (2/3)(s_i+2m_i)=1/s_i, so bB=1. This is not an extrapolation to nonzero correlations; the cycle11 transformed-root-inverse counterexample already rejected that extension.

If exactly one edge is nonzero, relabel it23 with correlation c. The finite scheme has only the all-root branch and the branch with isolated mass index1. In both branches s_2²=s_3²=1 and the same isolated reciprocal identity applies. Since B=s_1(3s_2s_3−c), take

    b=(2/3)(s_1+2m_1)(3s_2s_3+c)/(9−c²).

The denominator is positive because |c|<1. Every expression has total degree3.

## 3. Exactly two equal nonzero squared correlations

Permute and sign-switch so r_12=r_13=a>0, r_23=c, with a²≠c². This includes the two-edge case c=0. PD is exactly −1<c<1 and2a²<1+c. The finite scheme has all-root8, one-mass1 dimension4, and optionally the two-mass2,3 branch dimension2. No other non-root branches occur.

Put

    D0=(3−c)²−4a²,
    N=(3−c)²−2a²(1+c).

Both are positive: D0>(1−c)(7−c)>0 and N>8(1−c)>0 under PD. Also3+c>0. Define

    k0=((3−c)/D0+1/(3+c))/2,
    k1=((3−c)/D0−1/(3+c))/2,
    k2=a/D0,
    b0=k0s_1s_2s_3+k1s_1+k2(s_2+s_3).

On root points with s_2s_3=−1, B=−(3+c)s_1 and k1−k0=−1/(3+c). On root points with s_2=s_3=s, B=(3−c)s_1−2as and b0=[(3−c)s_1+2as]/D0. Thus b0 is the exact all-root inverse.

On the one-mass1 branch, write u_1=z, u_2=u_3=s with s²=1 and z²+azs=2. Then m_1=z/2, s_1=(z+as)/2, and

    B=((3−c)z−a(1+c)s)/2,
    B^-1=((3−c)z+4as)/N.

The latter product reduces to1 by the displayed quadratic, directly. Put

    lambda=2(3−c)/N−(3−c)/D0,
    eta=2a/N−a(7−c)/(4D0),
    b1=b0+μ_1[lambda m_1+eta(s_2+s_3)].

The correction vanishes on the all-root branch and supplies precisely B^-1−b0 on the one-mass branch. It has total degree3.

The optional two-mass2,3 branch occurs only when c≠0 and a²=2c²/(1+c); PD then forces −1/3<c<1. There u_1=s, u_2=u_3=(a/c)s, and s²=1. Put d=(1+c)a/(2c), so s_2=s_3=ds and m_2=m_3=(a/(2c))s. Then B=(3/2)(1−c)s. Since μ_1=0 and μ_2=μ_3=1, define

    theta=(c/a)[2/(3(1−c))−(k0d²+k1+2k2d)],
    b=b1+theta(μ_2m_2+μ_3m_3).

This is the required inverse on the optional branch and changes neither of the first two branch types. If the optional branch is empty, take theta=0 and b=b1; no division by c is used at c=0. The denominator a is nonzero in this stratum.

## 4. All three squared correlations equal and nonzero

Sign-switch to the exchangeable matrix with every off-diagonal r, where r has the triangle-product sign. Its PD interval is −1/2<r<1 and r≠0. The finite scheme has all-root8, three one-mass branches of length4 each, and all-mass2; every two-mass branch is empty under strict PD.

Use the preceding k0,k1,k2,lambda,eta with a=c=r. (The algebraic formulas and denominator positivity hold for negative r as well.) Write

    bstar=b0+sum_i μ_i[lambda m_i+eta sum_(j≠i)s_j].

It is correct on all-root and every one-mass branch by permutation symmetry. On all-mass points, all m_i=m, all s_i=(1+r)m and m²=1/(2(1+r)). The inverse of B there is4m/[3(1−r)]. Define the scalar error coefficient

    E=(1+r)[k0(1+r)/2+3k2]+3lambda+6eta(1+r)−4/[3(1−r)],

and linear forms

    K_i=s_i−m_i−(r/2)sum_(j≠i)s_j.

K_i vanishes at the one-mass i branch; μ_i vanishes at root indices. On all-mass, K_i=−r²m. Hence

    b=bstar+[E/(3r²)]sum_i μ_iK_i

fixes exactly the all-mass error, leaving the other branch types unchanged. Degree is again≤3. The sole additional denominator r² is nonzero on this stratum; r=0 was handled by the diagonal formula. The scalar E uses k0 and3k2 because b0=k0sprod+k2sum s when a=c=r.
