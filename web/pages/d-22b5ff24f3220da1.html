COMMUTING SCALAR ORACLES: IDENTIFIABILITY, QUERY LOWER BOUNDS, AND PIT GATE
Date: 2026-10-07
Scope: F of characteristic zero, Ai mutually commuting m x m matrices,
f(x)=lambda (I-sum xi Ai)^(-1) gamma. Only scalar point evaluations.
All transfer proofs below are internal derivations, not external validation.

DECISIVE FINDINGS
1. Under the commuting promise there is no information loss in scalar
commutativization: the entire f determines all original word coefficients.
2. A deterministic adaptive zero-test or exact learner for all size <=2n+2
commuting nilpotent systems needs at least binom(n+2,3) scalar point queries.
The subclass contains EVERY homogeneous cubic. This excludes overly small
query bounds, but is polynomial and does not obstruct a poly(n,m) learner.
3. Exact identification already needs >=2m scalar queries when n=1. This
holds for arbitrary adaptive point choices, by a linear fiber argument.
4. A literal partial-oracle model requiring every query to be guaranteed
defined is impossible even at m=1. Pole flags or a supplied safe domain
are required. This is an interface failure, not an algebraic PIT barrier.
5. The current commutative primary source arXiv:2609.34436 gives an explicit
polynomial hitting set for small partial-derivative spaces. For any commuting
resolvent the degree-(m-1) Taylor truncation has PD dimension <=m^2 and is
zero iff f is zero. Thus there is now a direct polynomial scalar PIT transfer,
assuming poles are recognizable. No superpolynomial PIT obstruction follows.

I. EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY
Write alpha=(alpha_1,...,alpha_n), k=|alpha|, A^alpha=product Ai^alpha_i.
Expansion at the always-defined origin gives
  [x^alpha]f = (k!/alpha!) lambda A^alpha gamma.
All words with multiset alpha have coefficient lambda A^alpha gamma, since
the Ai commute. Characteristic zero makes k!/alpha! invertible. Hence f
uniquely determines every word coefficient. Equality of scalar rational
functions implies equality of these full formal word series.

Minimal reachable and observable realizations are unique up to similarity.
Here is the direct proof. If (A,lambda,gamma) and (B,mu,delta) are minimal
and have identical moments, define S(A^alpha gamma)=B^alpha delta and extend
linearly. For any relation sum c_alpha A^alpha gamma=0, multiplying by every
lambda A^beta gives sum c_alpha moment(alpha+beta)=0. The corresponding
sum c_alpha B^alpha delta is therefore unobservable in B and must be zero.
Thus S is well-defined. Reachability makes it onto; reversing the argument
makes it injective. It satisfies S Ai=Bi S, S gamma=delta, lambda=mu S.

Original nonminimal matrices are not identifiable. For example, append any
invisible commuting block Ci to a one-state realization using block-diagonal
Ai=diag(ai,Ci), lambda=(1,0,...,0), gamma=(1,0,...,0)^T. The resulting
f=1/(1-a dot x) is independent of the Ci. Distinct hidden spectra prevent
similarity. This does not conflict with minimal-realization uniqueness.

II. A CONCRETE CUBIC QUERY LOWER BOUND
Let C=(C_ijk) be an arbitrary symmetric order-three tensor. Use the vector
space V=F u0 + F^n (v_j) + F^n (w_j) + F u3, of dimension 2n+2. Define
  Ai u0=v_i;
  Ai v_j=sum_k C_ijk w_k;
  Ai w_j=delta_ij u3;
  Ai u3=0.
All Ai commute: on u0 this uses C_ijk=C_jik; on v_l it uses
C_jli=C_ilj; on w_l and u3 both length-two products are zero. They raise
the grading, so any product of four matrices is zero.
Set gamma=u0 and lambda=u3*. For X=sum xi Ai,
  lambda X^k gamma=0 for k !=3,
  lambda X^3 gamma=sum_ijk C_ijk xi xj xk.
Consequently f(x)=sum_ijk C_ijk xi xj xk. Every homogeneous cubic appears:
for a desired monomial coefficient c_alpha with |alpha|=3 set each tensor
entry with that multiset equal to c_alpha/(3!/alpha!).

The space of homogeneous cubics has D=binom(n+2,3) dimensions. Follow the
zero transcript of any deterministic adaptive point-query algorithm making
q<D queries. Its resulting query points x_1,...,x_q are now fixed, regardless
of how they were selected. The D-dimensional cubic coefficient space has a
nonzero element P annihilated by all q evaluation linear functionals. Its
commuting nilpotent realization has the same all-zero transcript as the
zero system. Thus the algorithm cannot be correct on both. This proves
q>=D for zero-testing, and hence for exact learning, for size bounded by
2n+2. The argument makes NO regularity/arithmetic restriction on adaptive
point choices. All queries are valid because these systems are nilpotent.

Special cases: all linear forms fit in two states, giving q>=n; arbitrary
quadratics fit in n+2 states using grading (1,n,1), giving
q>=binom(n+1,2). The cubic bound is q=Omega(n m^2) along m=2n+2.
These are polynomial information lower bounds, not a negative answer to
the parent's poly(n,m) question. The promise here is realization size <=m;
the zero-transcript proof does not assume every supplied system is minimal.

III. UNIVARIATE EXACT-IDENTIFICATION LOWER BOUND 2m
Every rational function p(t)/q(t), deg p<m, deg q<=m, q(0)=1 admits an
m-state one-matrix realization (e.g. its coefficient recurrence companion
realization). Coprime pairs with deg q=m have minimal dimension m.
There are 2m free coefficients. Take one such coprime full-degree input on
which an alleged algorithm makes q0<2m defined queries. Fix its transcript
(t_j,y_j). All pairs compatible with it obey linear equations
  p(t_j)-y_j q(t_j)=0,
in those 2m coefficients. Their affine solution fiber has dimension at
least 2m-q0>0. It contains the original coprime pair. Along a nonzero
direction, all but finitely many field parameters preserve full denominator
degree, coprimality, and nonzero denominator at the finitely many query
points. Select a distinct such pair. Normalization q(0)=1 and coprimality
ensure it is a distinct rational function. The same outputs make all
adaptive queries recur unchanged. Therefore q0<2m cannot identify all
systems. Again, no assumption of algebraic adaptive rules is needed.
For zero-testing, arbitrary degree <=m-1 univariate polynomials fit in m
nilpotent states and give the weaker sharp elementary lower bound q>=m.

IV. THE ORACLE-DOMAIN OBSTRUCTION
Suppose the oracle is a partial map that returns a scalar ONLY when
I-sum xi Ai is invertible, and an algorithm must guarantee every call is
defined for every admissible input. For m=1, lambda=gamma=1,
  f_a(x)=1/(1-a dot x).
All inputs agree at x=0. At any nonzero proposed query x*, some a satisfies
a dot x*=1, making that query invalid. Thus the only universally safe point
is 0, and its value does not identify a. Repeated origin queries do not help.
The same issue persists after the all-one origin transcript. A recognized
undefined/pole symbol removes this artificial obstruction; a supplied
safe neighborhood also removes it, but its acquisition and representation
would then be part of the interface. A random point has zero pole chance
under an ideal continuous distribution, but finite-grid and uniform
worst-case validity remain distinct guarantees.

V. NEW PRIMARY PIT SOURCE AND ITS TRANSFER
Primary source: Shubham Bhardwaj and Ramprasad Saptharishi, Hitting Sets
for Polynomials with Small Partial Derivative Spaces, arXiv:2609.34436v1,
28 September 2026.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34436
  https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34436v1
Theorem 1.1 (equivalently Theorem 3.1): characteristic zero, PD dimension
<=r; the map Gi(t)=sum_{k=1}^B (i t)^k/k with B=n r^2+r preserves nonzeroness.
Thus for degree d, evaluate Gi on any dB+1 distinct field points.
Current independent audit: read the complete proof, HTML lines125-309.
Its constant-field, Wronskian, and valuation steps survive this pass. Choose
a derivative basis adapted to the polynomial-degree filtration in Lemma2.9;
the text's ordering assertion implicitly uses that routine basis choice.
No substantive gap found. This is an internal proof audit, not peer review.

For the transfer define X(x)=sum xi Ai and
  T_L(x)=sum_{k=0}^L lambda X(x)^k gamma.
For a multi-index beta of degree d<=L, commutativity yields
  partial^beta T_L = lambda A^beta [sum_{k=d}^L (k)_d X^(k-d)] gamma,
where (k)_d=k!/(k-d)!. For each fixed d this is a linear image of the row
lambda A^beta in F^m, so that order-d derivative space has dimension <=m.
Taking the union over d proves PDdim(T_L)<=(L+1)m. For L=m-1 this is <=m^2.

T_(m-1)=0 iff f=0: if it is zero, each homogeneous component
lambda X^k gamma for k<m is zero. Cayley-Hamilton applied to X over F[x]
expresses every higher power as a polynomial-coefficient combination of
the first m powers. Thus every lambda X^k gamma vanishes, and f=0.
The converse is immediate from homogeneous Taylor expansion.

Set r=m^2, B=n m^4+m^2. The source gives a hitting list V={G(t):t in T}
for T_(m-1), with |T|=(m-1)B+1=O(nm^5). For any nonzero f, at least one
v in V makes T_(m-1)(v)!=0. Then the univariate rational f(sv) is nonzero,
with numerator degree <=m-1 and denominator degree <=m. Any 2m distinct
scales s contain a defined, nonzero value: there are at most m forbidden
denominator roots plus at most m-1 numerator roots. Therefore the list
  H={s G(t):t in T, s in S}, |S|=2m,
is a polynomial scalar rational hitting list of size O(nm^6), with
recognizable-pole semantics. Query every point and accept nonzero iff a
defined nonzero output occurs. This establishes deterministic scalar PIT,
not by itself a complete realization-learning algorithm.

For a globally nilpotent tuple, f=T_(m-1) directly, and |V|=O(nm^5)
scalar queries suffice with no pole caveat.

VI. A NECESSARY FACTORIAL CAUTION
One cannot improve PDdim(f)<=m for raw nilpotent resolvents by identifying
them with exponential generating functions. Counterexample: m=5, J a
regular nilpotent Jordan block with ones above the diagonal, Ai=J^i for
i=1,2,3,4, lambda=e1^T, gamma=e5. Then
  f=x4+2 x1 x3+x2^2+3 x1^2 x2+x1^4.
Its partial derivatives span six independent polynomials:
  f; 2x3+6x1x2+4x1^3; 2x2+3x1^2;
  6x2+12x1^2; x1; 1.
All other derivatives lie in this span. Thus PDdim(f)=6>m=5.
The exponential series lambda exp(X) gamma DOES have derivative span <=m,
but its coefficients have different radial factorial weights. The m^2
truncation bound above explicitly charges the different derivative orders.

VII. WHY EASY CIRCUIT REDUCTIONS DO NOT GIVE A SUPERPOLYNOMIAL BARRIER
For P=product_{j=1}^d ell_j(x), use the square-zero algebra
  R=F[z1,...,zd]/(z1^2,...,zd^2), dim R=2^d.
Take Ai=multiplication by sum_j a_ji zj if ell_j=sum_i a_ji xi;
gamma=1; lambda extracts z1...zd and divides by d!. Then all Ai commute,
all degree>d products vanish, and lambda(I-X)^(-1)gamma=P(x).
A sum of s such products has a direct-sum realization of size <=s 2^d.
If the d linear forms are independent, 2^d is also the minimal size: P is
homogeneous, its exponential-normalized polynomial is P/d!, and its
2^d squarefree partial derivatives are independent. Any commuting
realization of those homogeneous moments must have size at least that
derivative-space rank. The same conclusion follows from its moment Hankel.
For a monomial product xi^ai, the corresponding size is product_i(ai+1).
Thus even a very small ordinary product circuit can require exponentially
many commuting states. Polynomial time in m does not derandomize general
polynomial-circuit PIT through this elementary construction.

VIII. GENERIC RANDOMIZED EVIDENCE VERSUS EFFECTIVE LEARNING
For a fixed pair of distinct m-state systems, their scalar difference has
a numerator of degree <=2m-1 after clearing both degree-m determinants.
Schwartz-Zippel on a finite grid of size K gives collision probability at
most (2m-1)/K, and each individual denominator pole probability <=m/K.
This easily supplies randomized equality tests for fixed candidates.
It neither acquires an unknown minimal realization nor supplies a
deterministic learner. A low number of scalar parameters also does not
justify solving a nonlinear parameter-identification system in polynomial
arithmetic time. The parent must exhibit its actual reconstruction.

FAILED ROUTES / NOVELTY LIMITS
- Word-order ambiguity fails as an obstruction under mutual commutativity.
- The claim that this nilpotent class still has only known quasipolynomial
  deterministic hitting sets is outdated after arXiv:2609.34436.
- General commutative circuit PIT hardness does not transfer with polynomial
  m via square-zero product realizations: m is exponential in product length.
- PDdim(f)<=m is false for ordinary resolvent generating weights.
- None of the information lower bounds above is superpolynomial in n,m.
- This report establishes scalar PIT and identifiability, not a finished
  scalar realization learner. Parent's supplied construction is separate.
