DETERMINISTIC RATIONAL-COMPLEX LINEAR-PARITY SUPPORT AUDIT
7 October 2026. Agent counting_gate_audit. Final interface audit at this checkpoint.

VERDICT
The proposed finite-field reduction is valid. It acquires exact maximum
cardinality and an actual independent paired witness over Q(i), hence an
exact support / zero decision for positive squared-Gram paired partitions
and forced/excluded prefixes. It removes the PIT false-zero error budget.
It supplies no partition approximation, sampler, mixing, energy comparison,
or FPRAS.

PRIMARY IMPORTED THEOREM
Hamada and Iwata, A Shortest Augmenting Path Algorithm for Linear Matroid
Parity, arXiv:2610.03030v1, submitted 2 October 2026:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03030v1
Theorem 1.1 gives deterministic UNWEIGHTED linear matroid parity in
O(m r^2 log r) field operations, or O(m r^2) with fast multiplication, for an
r-by-m representation over a field K under uniform field cost. Sections
2.1/2.2 seek and maintain the actual independent paired column witness J.
Rank-deficient matrices are first reduced to full row rank. There is no
large-field or characteristic restriction; odd characteristic also avoids
characteristic-two ambiguity. No weighted optimization theorem is needed.
This is an imported optimization/witness theorem, not a counting theorem.
Its input/output/field-cost interface was checked in the primary text; its
entire augmenting-path proof was not independently re-proved or implemented.

EXACT REDUCTION AND BIT BOUND
Let A be an r-by-m rational-complex matrix with paired columns, and let
L>=1 be its full rational encoding length. Multiply A by the product D of
all positive real/imaginary component denominators. Then G=DA is Gaussian
integer, D has O(L) bits, and the nonzero common scalar preserves column
independence over Q(i). A loose bound is |G_ij|<=2^(2L+1).

Any nonzero s-by-s minor delta, s<=r, obeys
 |delta|<=s! 2^((2L+1)s).
Its Gaussian norm N(delta)=delta conjugate(delta) is a positive integer.
For r>=1 the computable numeric integer
 B=1+2r ceil(log2(max{2,r}))+(4L+2)r
satisfies 1<=N(delta)<2^B. B itself, not just its bit length, is polynomial
in r,L. The rank-zero case is trivial.

Choose a nonzero square minor delta of an unknown optimal independent
paired set's nu columns. Among B+1 distinct odd primes some p avoids its
norm: otherwise their product, at least 3^(B+1)>2^B, would divide the smaller
positive integer N(delta). The algorithm need not know delta or the optimum.

DETERMINISTIC PRIME / FIELD CONSTRUCTION
Sieve up to x=2(B+2)^4 and take the first B+1 odd primes; x is polynomial
as a numeric value. An elementary proof that the sieve succeeds avoids an
extra prime-distribution import. For even x,
 binom(x,x/2)>=2^x/(x+1).
For every prime p<=x, its valuation in this binomial is a sum of at most
floor(log_p x) zero/one terms, so p to that valuation is at most x. Hence
 pi(x) log x >= x log2-log(x+1).
With M=B+2>=3 and x=2M^4, log x<=M^2, log(x+1)<=2M^2, and log2>=1/2.
Therefore pi(x)>=(M^4-2M^2)/M^2=M^2-2>=M=B+2. Excluding 2 leaves the
required B+1 odd primes. Sieve time/space is polynomial in r,L.

For p congruent 1 mod4, scan the p residues to find rho^2=-1 modp, and map
a+ib to a+b rho in F_p. Numeric p is polynomial in B, so this exhaustive
root construction is polynomial and deterministic. For p congruent 3 mod4,
use the explicit field F_p[t]/(t^2+1) and map i to t. Arithmetic/inversion
has polynomial bit cost in log p; no randomized extension or factor oracle
is hidden.

At p avoiding N(delta), the split case has
 N(delta)=(a+b rho)(a-b rho) modp,
so both embeddings of delta are nonzero. In the inert case, nonzero norm
also gives a nonzero field element. Thus some tested field preserves the
entire fixed optimum. Row-reduce each modular matrix separately, retaining
the original column names and pairs; full row rank need not survive at
every prime.

NO SPURIOUS WITNESS, EXACT OPTIMUM
Every modular independent set has a nonzero square minor image, whose
Gaussian-integer determinant was therefore nonzero before reduction.
Each modular witness lifts to an independent set over Q(i), so its parity
cardinality never exceeds the characteristic-zero optimum. At the good
prime the fixed optimal witness survives, making the modular optimum at
least that optimum. Taking the largest returned witness over the tested
primes is exactly optimal. Rational-complex elimination may verify the
lifted witness directly but is not needed for this argument.

PREFIX / CONDITIONED SUPPORT
For forced pairs P and excluded pairs E, test exact rational-complex
independence of the forced columns; dependence means zero squared-Gram
mass. Otherwise quotient remaining columns by the forced span using
Gaussian elimination, remove forced/excluded pairs, and run the deterministic
parity procedure. A size-k extension exists iff the residual maximum pair
count is at least k-|P|: an independent larger union of pairs can be trimmed.
Determinant bounds give polynomial bit length for the quotient matrix.
Zero-activity pairs are excluded; strictly positive weights do not alter
support. This applies to every adaptively visited prefix with a cap based
on its explicitly encoded matrix.

For full-basis partitions on a retained ambient set, restrict the matrix to
those coordinates and ask whether the maximum independent paired size
reaches the required half dimension. Below full dimension, Gram positivity
still equals column independence. Only the original representation is
reduced modulo primes; reducing a Hermitian Gram matrix is unnecessary
and could introduce finite-field isotropy concerns.

EVERY-EXECUTION COST AND LIMIT
At most B+1 parity instances occur, over fields with at most p^2 elements,
p<=2(B+2)^4. Clearing denominators, sieve/root scanning, row elimination,
quotient formation, and the imported deterministic field algorithm all have
polynomial bit cost on every execution. No expected-time rejection, random
PIT, or uncertain zero answer is used. The optimization theorem remains an
explicit imported dependency, rather than an implemented counting routine.
Exact support acquisition does not estimate positive squared determinants
or their sum; the microscopic sampling/energy gate remains open.
