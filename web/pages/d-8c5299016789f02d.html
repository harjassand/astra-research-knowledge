SPECTRAL GEOMETRY CYCLE 2 — NEAR-FULL-RANK CAPACITY PROFILE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally derived bounds and exact capacity reformulation. The r=1
leading asymptotic is matched for all matrices. For fixed r>1 the exponent is
matched, but the sharp leading constant for the unrestricted matrix problem
is not established here.

QUESTION AND NOTATION

Fix r and let

  H_{n,r}=V_{n/2}^{tensor r},   d=(n+1)^r,
  L(X)=sum_{q=1}^r sum_{a=1}^3 [T_a^(q),[T_a^(q),X]],
  E(X)=<X,LX>=sum_{q,a} ||[T_a^(q),X]||_HS^2,

with T_a=sigma_a/2 on the fundamental representation. Thus the conjugation
sector indexed by ell=(ell_1,...,ell_r) has Casimir

  lambda_ell=sum_q ell_q(ell_q+1),
  multiplicity product_q(2 ell_q+1),       0<=ell_q<=n.

Write h=d-Q for the rank deficiency, rho=h/d, and

  I_{n,r}(d-h)=inf{E(X): ||X||_HS=1, rank(X)<=d-h}.

The near-full regime below is fixed r, n->infinity, 1<=h=o(d). Every
normalization and constant uses the Casimir convention above.

1. EXACT REFORMULATION AS A KERNEL CAPACITY

For an h-dimensional subspace K<=H, let P_K be its orthogonal projection and
define the (operator-valued) Casimir capacity

  Cap_L(K)=inf{ E(A): Tr A=0, A P_K=P_K },
  C_{n,r}(h)=inf_{dim K=h} Cap_L(K).                    (1)

This is the right near-full-rank object. If X has rank at most d-h, choose an
h-dimensional K in ker X. When c=Tr(X)/d is nonzero, put A=I-X/c. Then
Tr A=0, A P_K=P_K, and

  ||X||_HS^2=|c|^2(d+||A||_HS^2),
  E(X)=|c|^2 E(A).

Conversely every such (K,A) gives a matrix proportional to I-A of rank at
most d-h. Since the first positive Casimir is 2, a traceless normalized X
has E(X)>=2. The capacity upper bounds below are o(d), so the c=0 branch
cannot minimize in this regime. Also ||A||_HS^2<=E(A)/2. It follows that,
once C_{n,r}(h)=o(d),

  C_{n,r}(h)/(d+C_{n,r}(h)/2)
       <= I_{n,r}(d-h) <= C_{n,r}(h)/d,               (2)

and hence I_{n,r}(d-h)=(1+o(1))C_{n,r}(h)/d. Formula (1) is exact at
finite n; (2) only replaces its normalization by d.

For K spanned by occupation vectors indexed by A subset of {0,...,n}^r,
the capacity in (1) is exactly a weighted-graph capacity. The diagonal
occupation subspace is invariant under L; averaging over the maximal torus
is an energy-contracting conditional expectation, preserves the trace and
the diagonal boundary values, and turns A P_K=P_K into f_i=1 for i in A.
Thus a diagonal minimizer is sufficient for this coordinate K.

The occupation lattice has vertices i=(i_1,...,i_r), 0<=i_q<=n, and edge
conductance

  c_q(i)=(n-i_q)(i_q+1)  on {i,i+e_q}.

For diagonal f,

  E(diag f)=sum_{i,q:i_q<n} c_q(i)|f_i-f_{i+e_q}|^2.              (3)

Let G_n be the mean-zero Green kernel of this graph Laplacian. For a finite
hole set A, let (G_n)_A=(G_n(i,j))_{i,j in A}. Its exact graph capacity is

  Cap_n(A)=1_A^T (G_n)_A^{-1} 1_A.                             (4)

The minimizer is the Green equilibrium potential, equal to one on A and
mean zero. In particular Cap_n({i})=1/G_n(i,i). This gives an exact
occupation-lattice formula for each coordinate-kernel candidate. It does
not by itself prove that a coordinate K minimizes (1) among all subspaces.

The one-dimensional path eigenvalues are ell(ell+1), ell=0,...,n. If
psi_ell are real orthonormal eigenvectors for counting measure, the product
Green kernel is exactly

  G_n(i,j)=sum_{ell in {0,...,n}^r, ell!=0}
      product_q psi_{ell_q}(i_q)psi_{ell_q}(j_q)
      / sum_q ell_q(ell_q+1).                                  (5)

At the corner i=0, the one-factor spectral weight is explicit:

  a_{n,ell}=|psi_ell(0)|^2
      =(2ell+1)(n!)^2/((n-ell)!(n+ell+1)!).                     (6)

Consequently

  G_n(0,0)=sum_{ell_1,...,ell_r=0}^n{}'
       product_q a_{n,ell_q}/sum_q ell_q(ell_q+1).              (7)

The prime omits the all-zero tuple. For r=1, summing (7), or using path
resistances, gives the exact identity

  G_{n,1}(0,0)=H_n/(n+1)-n/(n+1)^2
              =(log n+gamma-1+o(1))/n.                         (8)

For fixed r>1, set y=ell(ell+1)/n. Uniformly on ell=O(sqrt(n)), (6) is
asymptotic to the mesh mass e^{-y}dy. Thus (7) is the product exponential
integral

  n G_{n,r}(0,0) ->
     integral_{(0,infty)^r} exp(-sum y_q)/(sum y_q) dy
     = E[1/(Y_1+...+Y_r)] = 1/(r-1),                            (9)

where the Y_q are independent Exp(1). The integral is finite exactly when
r>1. For a single missing occupation state at a corner, therefore, the
diagonal capacity is asymptotic to (r-1)n for r>1, and to n/log n for r=1.
The Green potential gives a rank d-1 witness with normalized energy

  (r-1)n/d (1+o(1))             for fixed r>1,
  1/log n (1+o(1))              for r=1.                         (10)

The correction from ||A||_HS^2 in (2) is negligible: it is at most half the
capacity, which is o(d). Equation (10) is an exact leading constant for
the corner-kernel construction; for r>1 it is not claimed to be the
unrestricted rank d-1 optimum.

2. FIXED SMALL h AND THE DISCRETE GREEN CONSTANT

For fixed occupation indices a,b near one corner, the spectral ratio
psi_ell(a)/psi_ell(0) tends, at y=ell(ell+1)/n fixed, to the Laguerre
polynomial L_a(y). Hence for fixed r>1 and fixed vectors
a,b in Z_{>=0}^r,

  n G_n(a,b) -> K_r(a,b)
   := integral_{(0,infty)^r} exp(-sum y_q)
          product_q L_{a_q}(y_q)L_{b_q}
          /(sum y_q) dy.                                       (11)

This is finite for r>1, and K_r(0,0)=1/(r-1). For a fixed finite corner
set A, (4) therefore gives

  Cap_n(A) = n [1_A^T (K_r)_A^{-1}1_A+o(1)].                   (12)

Thus the exact finite-h constant in the coordinate-kernel class is a
discrete Green-capacity constant, not the continuum volume constant. A
sharp full-matrix constant for fixed h>1 additionally requires minimizing
(1) over non-coordinate h-planes; this note does not identify that
minimizer. At h=1, (9) identifies the corner candidate constant r-1.

For r=1, the kernel in (11) has a logarithmic divergence. For every fixed
finite corner set A, the divergent part of (G_n)_A is
(log n/n) 1_A 1_A^T, so (4) gives Cap_n(A)=(1+o(1))n/log n, independent
of the fixed number and arrangement of holes at leading order.

3. GROWING HOLE SETS: CONTINUUM CAPACITY AND CONSTANT

Let h->infinity with h=o(n^r), and first restrict to occupation-diagonal
matrices. Put R=(r!h)^(1/r); the corner simplex

  A_R={i: sum_q i_q<=R}

has |A_R|=binom(floor(R)+r,r)=(1+o(1))h. Near this corner
c_q(i)=n(i_q+1)(1+O(R/n)). The number of vertices in layer s is
binom(s+r-1,r-1), and its total outward conductance is

  C_s=sum_{|i|=s,q} (n-i_q)(i_q+1)
      =(1+o(1)) n s^r/(r-1)!                                  (13)

for 1<<s<<n. The radial equilibrium taper f(s)=(R/s)^(r-1) for s>R,
f=1 for s<=R, has

  E(f)=(1+o(1)) n/(r-1)! integral_R^infty
                  s^r ((r-1)R^(r-1)s^(-r))^2 ds
       =(1+o(1)) n R^(r-1)/(r-2)!.                             (14)

After subtracting its mean and rescaling to keep f=1 on A_R, the trace-zero
condition changes (14) by 1+o(1): its mean is O((R/n)^(r-1)) and its
Hilbert-Schmidt norm squared is at most E(f)/2=o(d). This gives the
occupation-diagonal upper profile

  I_{n,r}(d-h) <= (1+o(1)) C_r rho^(1-1/r),
  C_r=(r!)^((r-1)/r)/(r-2)!,           r>1.                    (15)

The shape constant in (15) is the continuum isocapacitary constant for
this diagonal model. Indeed, the limiting energy is
integral_{R_+^r} sum_q x_q |partial_q f|^2 dx. Under
x_q=|z_q|^2/4 this is (4pi)^(-r) times the ordinary Dirichlet energy of
functions radial in each coordinate pair on R^(2r); the simplex sum x_q<=R
maps to the Euclidean ball of radius 2 sqrt(R). The ball minimizes Newtonian
capacity at fixed volume. This establishes the sharp continuum constant
among occupation-lattice shapes when 1<<h<<n^r, not among all matrix
kernel subspaces.

For r=1, C_s~n s and the resistance coordinate is logarithmic. For
1<=h=o(n), pin f=1 on {0,...,h-1}, taper linearly in cumulative resistance
to zero by s=floor(n/2), and subtract/rescale its mean. Since

  sum_{s=h}^{floor(n/2)} 1/((n-s)(s+1))
       =(1+o(1)) log(n/h)/n,

its energy is (1+o(1))n/log(n/h). Consequently the full rank problem has
the upper bound

  I_{n,1}(n+1-h) <= (1+o(1))/log(1/rho).                       (16)

4. A UNIVERSAL GREEN LOWER BOUND FOR ALL MATRICES

The simple low-band bound Phi(d-h) is too weak here. Since D(t)=1 on
[0,2), and (1-rho)D(t)>=1 for t>=2 when rho->0, it gives only

  Phi(d-h)=2rho.                                                (17)

The kernel condition supplies a stronger all-matrix lower bound. Let
P=P_K for any h-dimensional subspace of ker X, c=Tr(X)/d, Y=X-cI, and
Z=P-rho I. Then Tr Y=0, <Z,Y>=-hc, and

  E(X)=E(Y) >= h^2 |c|^2 / <Z,L^{-1}Z>.                       (18)

For P/sqrt(h), the low-band mass in the positive spectrum is at most
min{1-rho, rho(D(t)-1)}: the zero-mode mass is exactly rho, and the
rank-h projector theorem bounds total mass below t by hD(t)/d. Put

  B_{n,r}(rho)=(1-rho)/Lambda
       + integral_2^Lambda min{1-rho,rho(D(t)-1)} dt/t^2,
  Lambda=r n(n+1).                                              (19)

Layer cake applied to 1/lambda in (18) yields
<Z,L^{-1}Z> <= h B_{n,r}(rho). The Poincare gap also gives
E(X)>=2(1-d|c|^2). Optimizing these two inequalities over
eta=d|c|^2 in [0,1] gives the finite lower bound

  I_{n,r}(d-h) >= 2 a/(2+a),   a=rho/B_{n,r}(rho).              (20)

For fixed r and rho->0, t_*=(r!/rho)^(1/r) is asymptotic in scale to
n h^(-1/r); it diverges and is O_r(n), hence lies in the weighted-cell
Weyl range 1<<t_*<<n^2. Since D(t)=t^r/r!+O_r(t^(r-1/2)), (19) gives

  B_{n,r}(rho)=(1+o(1)) [r/(r-1)] (rho/r!)^(1/r),   r>1,
  B_{n,1}(rho)=(1+o(1)) rho log(1/rho).                       (21)

Therefore, for unrestricted matrices,

  I_{n,r}(d-h) >= (1-o(1)) [(r-1)/r](r!)^(1/r)
                         rho^(1-1/r),                 r>1,
  I_{n,1}(n+1-h) >= (1-o(1))/log(1/rho).                       (22)

No log-Sobolev inequality is used. The argument uses the whole spectral
Green integral and the kernel of X, rather than only the first spectral
gap.

5. WHAT IS AND IS NOT SETTLED

For fixed r=1 and 1<=h=o(n), (16) and (22) match, including the leading
constant:

  I_{n,1}(n+1-h)=(1+o(1))/log((n+1)/h).                         (23)

Thus the proposed 1/log(1/rho) law is correct with constant one throughout
the near-full regime, including h=1 and fixed h>1. The scalar profile
(17) is smaller by a logarithmic factor.

For each fixed r>1, the proposed rho^(1-1/r) law is supported by matching
all-matrix lower and occupation-diagonal upper bounds. In the growing-hole
regime 1<<h<<n^r, the explicit constants currently proved are

  [(r-1)/r](r!)^(1/r) <= liminf I/rho^(1-1/r),
  limsup I/rho^(1-1/r) <= (r!)^((r-1)/r)/(r-2)!.

For h=1, the corner Green capacity gives the upper coefficient r-1, while
(22) gives the lower coefficient [(r-1)/r](r!)^(1/r). For fixed h>1,
(11)-(12) give the exact coefficient for each coordinate-kernel pattern.
The sharp unrestricted leading constant for r>1 is UNKNOWN here: it
requires proving which h-plane minimizes (1), or a matrix-valued
isocapacitary inequality. In particular, the corner constant r-1 must not
be called the global k=1 constant until the maximizer of
<P_v-I/d,L^{-1}(P_v-I/d)> over all unit v is identified.

All scales above assume fixed r. They are not uniform as r grows. The
occupation continuum constant further assumes h->infinity and R=o(n);
for fixed h use the discrete Green matrix (11), not (15). This note is a
Casimir-energy/capacity result only and does not transfer by itself to a
channel or quantum-memory reconstruction claim.
