# Cyclic length as a computable obstruction, and a quadratic family

Status, 7 October 2026: release family 314 was read in full (30-page PDF); the
profinite recognition argument was independently reconstructed. The main
chromatic theorem has not been independently verified in full. The group
construction and its cyclic-length formula below have an elementary proof
independent of that theorem. Its chromatic consequences are conditional on the
release theorem. No originality claim is made for this corollary.

## Exact question and supplied interface

Given a prime p, a finite p-group G supplied either by a multiplication table or
by a proved presentation, a specified embedded subgroup H, and target height
n >= 0, determine the least integer r for which every finite p-local genuine
G-spectrum X satisfies

    K(n+r)_*(Phi^H X)=0  =>  K(n)_*(Phi^G X)=0.

This is a statement about compact retracts of genuine equivariant finite
spectra. It does not directly give a procedure for acquiring a spectrum,
computing its Morava homology, or finding a separating witness. The input must
identify the subgroup embedding, not only its isomorphism class.

## Independent reconstruction of the recognition mechanism

Write the congruence hypothesis on a finitely generated pro-p group E as

    sum_{V open in U, [U:V]=p^h} (|X^V|-|X|) in p^(h+1) Z_(p)

for every open U <= E, every finite continuous U-set X, and every h >= 0.
Suppose every finite section has at most l generators, and
|Hom_cts(E,F)|/|F| belongs to Z_(p) for every finite F.

1. If E is nontrivial and its abelianization is finite of order p^b, its maps
   to C_(p^r) eventually number p^b. The quotient p^(b-r) then violates the
   integrality hypothesis. Thus E surjects onto Z_p. A chosen lift of 1 splits
   this epimorphism, so E = N semidirect Z_p.

2. Restricting the action to p^s Z_p makes it trivial on any fixed finite
   quotient of N for sufficiently large s. To preserve every required finite
   test, take O_M equal to the intersection of kernels of maps N -> Sym(M).
   This is characteristic and open because N is finitely generated. Both the
   shrinking semidirect product and N x Z_p have the common quotient
   (N/O_M) x C_(p^a), with p^a >= M. Induction to the ambient group encodes a
   subgroup U and its finite set X as permutation actions of bounded degree.
   It also captures all subgroups of the tested index. Hence the complete
   congruence and finite-section bounds pass to the direct product. The limit
   is a limit of finite tests, not an asserted isomorphism of the pro-p groups.

3. If K is nontrivial, choose its finite Frattini quotient F. A closed subgroup
   surjecting onto F must be K: otherwise a maximal subgroup in a finite
   quotient gives an index-p kernel containing it. Clear the denominators of
   the full Burnside idempotent of F, with integer D, and inflate to
   K x Z_p^d. Its mark is D precisely on subgroups projecting onto K and its
   underlying cardinality is zero. The congruence forces D*c_h divisible by
   p^(h+1), where c_h counts these subgroups of index p^h. Therefore c_h -> 0
   p-adically.

4. Independently, if W <= Z_p^d is the projection and V is intersection with
   K, such a subgroup is the graph of a surjection W -> K/V. There is no
   automorphism denominator. Thus

    c_h = sum_{V normal open in K, [K:V]=p^i, i<=h}
          a_(h-i)(d) * |Sur_cts(Z_p^d,K/V)|,

   where sum_j a_j(d)t^j = product_{u=0}^{d-1}(1-p^u t)^(-1).
   The coefficients tend p-adically to
   c_d = product_{u=1}^{d-1}(1-p^u)^(-1), a nonzero unit. If K^ab is finite,
   only finitely many V contribute, and the limit is c_d times a positive
   integer (including V=K). This contradicts c_h -> 0. Thus every remaining
   nontrivial K has a new Z_p quotient.

5. After d extractions the remaining product K_d x Z_p^d still has the section
   bound. If K_d is nontrivial, it surjects onto C_p, giving an elementary
   abelian quotient of rank d+1. Hence the process stops in at most l steps.
   Taking images of the closed series in a finite quotient gives a cyclic
   subnormal chain, with repetitions deleted.

This reconstructed implication is a reusable profinite recognition principle.
Its hypotheses quantify over infinitely many targets and tests. It is not a
finite decision algorithm for an arbitrary supplied pro-p group.

## Our group construction and complete derivation

Let p be an odd prime, d >= 2, V=F_p^d and Z=Lambda^2 V. Define G_d=V x Z by

    (v,z)(w,t)=(v+w, z+t+(1/2) v wedge w).

The operation is associative: the central correction satisfies
B(v,w)+B(v+w,u)=B(w,u)+B(v,w+u), by bilinearity. The identity is (0,0) and
the inverse is (-v,-z). Since v wedge v=0, the kth power is (kv,kz), and every
nonidentity element has order p. Direct calculation gives

    [(v,z),(w,t)]=(0,v wedge w).

The wedge basis is generated by commutators of the d standard basis vectors.
Hence [G_d,G_d]=Z, G_d/[G_d,G_d]=V, and G_d needs exactly d generators. It has
order p^N, where N=d+binom(d,2)=d(d+1)/2.

More generally, if any finite p-group G has exponent p, so does every subgroup
and every quotient of a subgroup. Each nontrivial cyclic subnormal factor
therefore has order p. Every cyclic chain H -> G consequently has length
log_p[G:H]. Such a chain exists because a finite p-group has an index-p
subnormal chain above every subgroup. In detail, a proper subgroup H is
strictly contained in its normalizer; an order-p subgroup of N_G(H)/H gives
H normal in K of index p. Repeat until the full group is reached. Thus

    ell(G,H)=log_p[G:H]                 (unconditional group theory),
    r_n(G_d,1)=d(d+1)/2                (conditional on release Theorem 1.1).

An explicit upper certificate for G_d first adjoins the binom(d,2) central
basis vectors one at a time, then the d vectors of V. Every factor has order
p; after the center is installed each remaining intermediate subgroup is
normal in G_d. The exponent-p argument is the matching lower certificate.
No exponential subgroup enumeration is required for this family.

The elementary-quotient lower bound for H=1 only sees the Frattini quotient V,
of rank d. The claimed sharp value exceeds it by binom(d,2), an unbounded
quadratic gap. The order-27, two-generator case is the first member. For target
height 0 the claimed sharpness supplies a finite genuine G_d-spectrum which
is K(N-1)-acyclic underneath and has rationally nonzero full fixed points.
The paper supplies this spectrum by prime separation, not by explicit cells.

## Evidence, costs, prior art, and next falsifiable step

Run `python3 work/frontier_topology/cycle19/cyclic_length.py`. The exact
independent engine enumerates subgroups, tests normality and cyclic quotients,
then performs breadth-first search on the resulting graph. The output
CYCLIC_LENGTH_CERTIFICATES.json includes all subgroups, all relative lengths,
and shortest chains to the full group. It checked associativity exhaustively
for C4 x C2, D8, and G_2 at p=3. For G_2 it enumerated 19 subgroups and verified
ell(K,H)=log_3[K:H] for every containment. It also confirmed losses 2 and 1 for
the two differently embedded C2's in C4 x C2, and length 2 for a D8 reflection.
These checks validate those finite computations only.

For a multiplication-table input of order M, storing the table costs M^2
entries; subgroup enumeration can be large and is not claimed efficient.
One closure calculation takes at most O(M^2) table operations. Subgroup
enumeration performs at most S*M such calculations, giving O(S M^3), before
the edge tests. Constructing all edges by the simple implementation takes
at most O(S^2 M^3) table operations: each candidate pair uses at most M
closure tests plus O(M^2) conjugacy tests. Breadth-first searches take
O(S(S+E)). The table, cached closures, edges and distances occupy at most
O(M^2+S M^2+S^2) integer entries (before serialization). These loose upper
bounds charge the brute-force implementation; no polynomial claim in a
succinct group description is made. The succinct
G_d operation takes O(d^2) field operations; a group element uses
N log_2(p) bits. The explicit chain has N symbolic stages. This cost account
does not include acquiring the group law or computing any Morava homology.

Nearest checked primary prior art: Kuhn and Lloyd, arXiv:2008.00330v2
(uploaded 27 September 2022; manuscript revised 22 July 2022; published AJM
146 (2024)). Section 2.2 presents the proposed equality and Section 9.2,
Example 9.5, already records the odd-primary extraspecial exponent-p gap.
Theorem 2.13 settles the central-C2-extension family. Our d=2 example is
therefore a known unresolved comparison at the odd prime in that source,
not a new group-theoretic discovery. The d-variable construction is a
transparent general consequence, with novelty unclaimed.

Concrete downstream use: replace generator-rank heuristics by exact
cyclic-section certificates when planning fixed-point tests or constructing
the subgroup-height specialization order. For finite spectra, the result
would impose t(H)<=t(K)+ell(K,H) on every H<=K. A failure disproves a proposed
type profile. Computing the invariant alone does not realize that profile.

An attractive cross-family route is to use family 308's claimed finite
Smith-Toda constructions as underlying spectra for fixed-point witnesses.
The decisive missing interface is a genuine G_d-action and control of all
geometric fixed points. Inflating an ordinary type-N complex leaves both
underlying and full fixed points at the same type, so it supplies no witness.
Inflating a known elementary-abelian rank-N witness is also unavailable:
G_d has no rank-N elementary abelian quotient when d<N. Varying the prime
in 308 additionally prevents arbitrary fixed-prime conclusions. Thus the
two release headlines do not by themselves constitute a witness compiler.
