C04_L03 INITIAL — direct broadcaster-slice instrument route
Timestamp: 2026-10-07 13:25 UTC. Model metadata remains UNKNOWN.
Status: conditional c=2 reduction proved; universal existence of the required slices remains open.

Strongest claim at checkpoint

A direct Stinespring/instrument route would prove the universal factor-two EB Dirichlet bound if one can choose a rank-one projective measurement on one receiver of a symmetric broadcaster so that each measured branch dominates its canonical measure-and-prepare branch at half weight. This is a concrete sufficient gate, stronger and more local than merely asking whether 2 Phi - Psi is CP. The derivation below is exact. I do not establish that the gate always has a solution.

Setup and derivation

Let A=M_d with normalized trace tau, and let Phi:A->A be unital, trace-preserving, selfadjoint and positive as an L2(tau) operator. Let Gamma:A tensor A->A be a supplied UCP symmetric broadcaster with Gamma(X tensor I)=Gamma(I tensor X)=Phi(X). Choose a rank-one projective resolution P_1,...,P_d on the receiver being measured. Define CP branches

  Lambda_y(X) = Gamma(X tensor P_y).

They form an instrument because sum_y Lambda_y=Phi and sum_y Lambda_y(I)=I. Their effects are

  E_y = Lambda_y(I) = Gamma(I tensor P_y) = Phi(P_y),

so sum_y E_y=I and tau(E_y)=tau(P_y)=1/d. Define the canonical EB branch with the same effect,

  R_y(X) = tau(E_y X) E_y / tau(E_y),    Psi = sum_y R_y.

Each R_y is CP, its effect is E_y, and Psi is bistochastic, selfadjoint and positive on L2. Since Phi is selfadjoint,

  tau(E_y X) = tau(P_y Phi(X)),
  Psi(X) = Phi( P(Phi(X)) ) = Phi P Phi(X),

where P(X)=sum_y tau(P_y X) P_y is the trace-preserving pinching. Thus the EB candidate is exactly induced by the projective measurement of one broadcaster output followed by its canonical reconstruction.

Suppose, for this choice of measurement, every residual branch

  L_y = 2 Lambda_y - R_y

is CP. Then L_y(I)=E_y, so L=sum_y L_y=2 Phi-Psi is unital; since Phi and Psi preserve tau, L is also trace-preserving. It is selfadjoint because Phi and Psi are. Kadison-Schwarz plus trace preservation makes L an L2 contraction, hence L<=I as a selfadjoint Hilbert-space operator. Therefore

  2(I-Phi) - (I-Psi) = I + Psi - 2 Phi = I-L >= 0.

This is the desired Dirichlet inequality with c=2. Moreover {L_y} is a bistochastic instrument with effects E_y. Its instrument-append joint channel has marginals L and the canonical EB map Psi; symmetrizing the receiver order gives common marginal (L+Psi)/2=Phi. Hence this sufficient gate directly decomposes the original marginal and does not require the later Petz correction used for the broader supplied-instrument class.

The condition is sectorwise CP domination, 2 J(Lambda_y) >= J(R_y), for all y. A weaker route could prove the aggregate 2 Phi-Psi CP and then separately find an instrument for that L with the same effects; the branchwise condition supplies both requirements at once.

Exact warning: arbitrary Kraus/Stinespring coordinates do not meet this gate

Take the exact Z-dephasing channel on M_2, Phi(X)=P_0 X P_0 + P_1 X P_1. It is selfcompatible: Gamma(X tensor Y)=sum_{i=0,1} Tr(P_i X) Tr(P_i Y) P_i is the full-domain measure-and-prepare broadcaster. But the equivalent Kraus family K_0=I/sqrt(2), K_1=Z/sqrt(2) gives effects E_0=E_1=I/2. Its canonical EB map is replacement, Psi(X)=tau(X)I, so for H=Z,

  <Z,(I-Psi)Z>_tau=1 while <Z,(I-Phi)Z>_tau=0.

The branch residuals are not CP: the Choi matrix of 2 Lambda_0-R_0 is |Omega><Omega|-(I tensor I)/4, with eigenvalues 7/4,-1/4,-1/4,-1/4. In contrast, slicing the supplied pointer-basis broadcaster gives Lambda_i(X)=Tr(P_i X)P_i and E_i=P_i; then R_i=Lambda_i and the construction succeeds exactly. The failure is therefore of arbitrary Kraus coordinates, not of the target theorem or the appropriate broadcaster-adapted basis. Exact rational reproduction is in stinespring_slice_diagnostic.py.

Sharpness check for the target constant

For the optimal universal 1->2 qudit cloner, the one-clone marginal is the depolarizing channel Phi_d(X)=lambda_d X+(1-lambda_d)tau(X)I with lambda_d=(d+2)/(2(d+1)); it is selfcompatible by the supplied cloning map. Haar-twirling any EB candidate that satisfies a universal Dirichlet comparison preserves the comparison and yields a depolarizing EB map with shrink mu<=1/(d+1). On traceless H the comparison forces 1-mu<=c(1-lambda_d), hence c>= [d/(d+1)]/[d/(2(d+1))]=2. This benchmark is imported from Werner's universal cloning theorem; it shows factor two is the best possible constant if the universal result holds, not that it holds.
