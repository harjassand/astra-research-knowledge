N51 residual-feature/BFS descriptor learner: independent audit
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: conditional constructive theorem for exact matrix-oracle descriptor acquisition. The residual-feature and quotient arguments survive this audit under the stated hitting-list premise. This is not an externally validated all-field theorem, not syntax-tree recovery, and not a practical construction.

Verdict

The proposed learner avoids formula enumeration. Given a polynomial-size list that hits every feasible affine pencil of the required order, its residual detector is injective, only at most R independent residuals are acquired, breadth-first closure uses at most kR successor tests, and every post-centre oracle query preserves the original inverse-gate domain. The output is a valid affine matrix-inverse descriptor on the entire original formula domain.

The proof is conditional in an important way. The released September 2026 N51 manuscript proves its list theorem over Q and includes a corollary for rational affine pencils. That is enough for the Q-valued descriptor learner after the hidden Q coefficients are specialized into the acquired series. N51's separate all-field/all-extension integer strong-pencil extension is present only in the local source-derived, unreviewed package examined here; the public Q statement does not establish that wider coefficient-field claim. Exact formula-tree reconstruction and a small scalar formula do not follow.

Model and notation

Let Phi be an admissible noncommutative rational formula over a coefficient field F, with n variables and tree size at most s. The oracle accepts tuples of square matrices over F, returns the entire output matrix exactly, and returns an explicit UNDEFINED status when some original inverse gate cannot be evaluated. It supports every polynomial dimension requested. In the Q bit model, suppose the hidden rational constants have height at most h bits. Let H0 be the regular-centre list, with length L0 and largest dimension d0. After selecting a centre A of dimension d, put

    k = n d^2,       R = (s+1)d,       B1 = R^2+1.

Let H1 be the residual-pencil hitting list on k variables for pencil order B1, with length L1, largest tuple dimension e1, and largest entry height b1. For the public Q construction, both lists are explicit rational lists with polynomial size, dimensions, and entry bit lengths in their unary parameters. The local extension claims an integer list over arbitrary F with a simultaneous feasible-pencil guarantee; its status is kept separate below.

Reconstructed invariant and feature proof

At a centre A where Phi(A) is defined, the formula has a domain-preserving affine linearization

    Phi(X) = u L(X)^(-1) v,       L(X)=L0+sum_i Li Xi,

of order r at most s+1, such that L(X) is invertible whenever the original formula tree is defined at X. The entries of this linearization can be built recursively from the formula using constants and 0, 1, -1. The learner need not know u, v, or L.

Perturb every entry of every centre matrix independently:

    Xi = Ai + sum_(a,b) Eab y_(i,a,b).

There are k letters y_(i,a,b). For the scalar series g(Y)=Phi(X)_(1,1), expanding at A gives a proper recognizable series of dimension at most R=rd:

    g(Y)=c (I - sum_j Tj yj)^(-1) b.

This dimension bound follows by flattening L(A+Delta) to an rd by rd pencil. Every residual combination shares the same transitions Tj and therefore also has dimension at most R.

For any nonzero recognizable scalar series f(w)=a Tw b of dimension at most R, choose a shortest word w of length ell with f(w) nonzero. Its ell+1 prefix rows aT_prefix are linearly independent: a dependence at prefix length j, multiplied by the remaining suffix of w, would express f(w) as a combination of shorter-word coefficients, all zero by minimality. Therefore ell<R. Thus the truncation

    Pf = sum_(|w|<R) f(w) w

is nonzero exactly when f is nonzero. A width-R realization unrolled for R layers represents Pf by an affine inverse pencil of order at most R^2; augmenting by one scalar Schur-complement row and column gives a feasible affine pencil of order at most R^2+1 whenever Pf is nonzero. Feasibility follows from the standard free-field linearization criterion: a nonzero polynomial is invertible at some matrix evaluation over an extension.

For each tuple Z in H1 of dimension e, form Nj=Zj tensor JR, where JR is the R by R nilpotent upper shift. Define D(f) by concatenating all entries of f(N) over the list. This is a linear feature map. If f is nonzero, the pencil for Pf has an invertible hit at some Z, so Pf(Z) is nonzero. At least one homogeneous component Pf,l(Z) is then nonzero. In f(N), the l-th block superdiagonal is exactly Pf,l(Z), because every letter contains the same shift JR. Hence f(N) is nonzero and D(f) is injective on the whole residual space.

This proves the load-bearing detector claim without enumerating formulas, words of unrestricted length, or all candidate realizations. The detector's query family is finite because H1 is finite; its injectivity is universal because the residual-state dimension is bounded.

BFS invariant and query bound

Start from the empty-prefix residual. For each retained independent prefix p and each of the k letters, acquire the feature vector of the successor residual partial_(p j) g. Keep it exactly when it increases rank; otherwise solve for its coordinates in the current basis. Injectivity of D turns every feature dependence into a true residual identity. Since the residual space has dimension at most R, at most R basis prefixes are retained. Closing all retained rows under all letters proves by induction that they span every residual. The resulting transition coordinates and constant coefficients yield a proper linear representation for g.

Each feature vector uses L1 matrix-oracle calls; at most one initial batch and k successor batches per basis row are needed. At most R additional zero-tail coefficient calls recover the constant outputs. Therefore

    Q <= L0 + (1+kR)L1 + R.

The number of retained residual features is at most R, not the number of possible prefixes. Feature-vector length is at most

    Mfeat = sum_(Z in H1) (e_Z R)^2 <= L1 (e1 R)^2.

Every post-centre residual query is an upper-block-triangular perturbation of A. A forced chain of ell prefix blocks and a final graded tail N encodes partial_p g(N) as one cross block. Since N_j=Zj tensor JR is strictly upper triangular, all perturbation blocks are strictly upper triangular after one fixed permutation. At every original inverse node, the queried operand is upper triangular with the same invertible operand-at-A on every diagonal entry. Thus all post-centre queries are defined in the original tree's domain; no unknown singularity test is needed there.

For an order-e feature tuple, tail dimension is eR and a tested prefix has length at most R. The corresponding original input dimension is at most d(R+1)R e1. Include the maximum centre-list dimension as well: the overall query dimension is bounded by

    Dmax <= max(d0, d(R+1)R e1).

This accounts for the centre search even if a list generator emits tuples of different dimensions. Under either list claim, L0, L1, d0, e1, and all list-entry heights are polynomial in n and s. So are Q, Dmax, the feature-vector size, and the total number of returned field elements, at most Q Dmax^2.

Domain preservation after specialization

The learned BFS representation is the reachable/observable quotient of the hidden g realization. Write W=span{cT_p} for reachable rows and K={a in W : a Tw b=0 for every word w} for unobservable rows. The map a -> (w -> aTw b) identifies residuals with W/K. Both W and K are invariant under right multiplication by every Tj, so the learned transition matrices are the induced maps on W/K.

Specialize y_(i,a,b) to delta_ab xi - (Ai)_(a,b). Then the ambient hidden pencil becomes, up to fixed tensor permutations,

    L(A)^(-1) L(Id tensor X).

For any X where the original formula is defined, L(X) is invertible by the domain-preserving linearization. Therefore L(Id tensor X), and hence the ambient pencil, is invertible. Choose an ambient basis adapted to K subset W subset ambient state space. The ambient transition pencil is block triangular with the learned quotient pencil as one diagonal block. An invertible block-triangular matrix has every diagonal block invertible, so the learned descriptor is defined at every original-domain X. Its value agrees with Phi there by the series identity and the specialization.

This establishes domain containment:

    original syntax-tree domain subset learned descriptor domain.

Equality of these domains is neither needed nor proved. For example, a formula such as x x^(-1) has the semantic value 1 but its original syntax tree is undefined at singular x; a learned descriptor for 1 may be defined there.

Minimality: the exact boundary

The BFS quotient is minimal as a recognizable linear representation of the scalar formal series g in the generic-entry letters: its dimension is the Hankel rank, equivalently the dimension of W/K. The resulting descriptor is an explicit representative of Phi's free-skew-field equivalence class, but it is not a canonical representative. After specializing the generic letters, it is only a valid descriptor for Phi. No argument makes it a minimal semantic affine pencil for Phi, a minimum-order formula, or the hidden syntax tree.

Concrete size gap: let Phi(x)=x^(-1), and let the acquired regular centre A be an invertible d by d matrix. Then g(Y)=((A+Y)^(-1))_(1,1). Its generic noncommutative realization has reachable and observable dimension d: the letters Eab allow a nonzero reachable row to produce each coordinate row, and subsequent letters can read each coordinate against the output column. Thus the BFS output has q=d for this series, even though Phi itself has a one-state descriptor x^(-1). This is not a correctness defect; it shows why “minimal residual realization” must be qualified as minimal for g only. The centre dimension d can be large because it comes from a hitting list.

Bit height, exactness, and precision

For Q-valued hidden constants of height at most h, every query coordinate is assembled from the centre, a hitting-list coordinate, and 0/1 block entries, so its height is polynomial in n,s. At any original-defined query of dimension D<=Dmax, a size-O(s) affine linearization produces an inverse matrix of order O(sD). Its entries have polynomial height in h and the query height; each output entry is a ratio of minors. Standard determinant height bounds therefore give polynomial output bit height

    Hout = poly(s, Dmax, h, query-entry-height).

The full reply has at most Dmax^2 rationals, so total response bits over all calls are at most Q Dmax^2 times this polynomial bound. The exact Gaussian eliminations for BFS operate on polynomial-size feature arrays whose entries have height at most Hout; minor bounds keep the learned descriptor coefficients at polynomial height as well. The theorem has polynomial query count independent of h, but no h-independent total bit bound. Even a constant formula with an h-bit rational constant forces h bits in a response.

For an abstract coefficient field, the statement is only a count of oracle calls and exact field operations/equality tests. “Polynomial bit time” requires a representation and cost model for F. The local all-field list does not supply encodings for arbitrary field elements returned by the oracle.

The described learner is exact. A noisy version needs a known coefficient-height bound, exact defined/undefined status for centre acquisition, and enough absolute precision to recover each rational response and preserve all rank decisions. With an Hout-bit rational bound, distinct candidate rationals have separation at least 2^(-O(Hout)); feature-rank minors add a polynomial factor to the exponent. This yields a conservative exponentially small sufficient noise scale, not numerical stability. Without a height or separation promise, constants 0 and delta I are indistinguishable under additive noise eta whenever |delta|<2 eta. Neither N51 nor N68 supplies a stable noisy decoder.

Comparison with formula-supplied evaluation

If the size-s formula tree is supplied, evaluate it directly at the requested D by D tuple: additions cost O(D^2), products and inverses cost O(D^omega) field operations, giving O(s D^omega) field operations in the usual dense-arithmetic model. Exact rational evaluation has polynomial bit growth in s,D,h and input height; the formula's size-O(s) linearization gives a single O(sD)-order determinant/minor bound. The supplied tree also reports its own inverse-gate failures. It requires one requested evaluation and no hitting lists, residual basis, or full-output learning transcript.

The descriptor learner pays Q potentially enormous full matrix responses of up to Dmax dimensions and extensive exact linear algebra to produce a reusable representation. Evaluating its order-q descriptor later costs O(q D^omega), with q<=R=(s+1)d; the hitting-list centre dimension d can make that descriptor larger than the supplied size-s tree. It is useful when the formula is withheld and reusable descriptor access is the goal, not as a faster way to evaluate one known formula on one input.

N68 and older sparse-polynomial reconstruction

N68's local exact result acquires an s-sparse noncommutative polynomial from one 2s-dimensional exact full-matrix response over Q, with a degree-free word code and an output-sensitive Prony/Hankel decoder. The Lakhani–Mukhopadhyay primary source ECCC TR26-120 rev2 supplies the affine-action/PIT mechanism and dimension lower bound; the local report derives the stronger sparse interpolation consequence. Arvind–Mukhopadhyay–Srinivasan (ECCC TR08-025, 2008) already gives deterministic black-box sparse-polynomial reconstruction with polynomial dependence on degree, sparsity, and variables, and its ABP reconstruction assumes gate-output access.

N68 does not replace the N51 detector: a size-s rational formula can have exponentially many monomials, and its generic rational series can have infinitely many nonzero coefficients. Even the finite truncation Pf can have exponentially many words. N68's sparsity promise is not implied by formula size. N51 supplies a bounded-state/pencil detector instead of assuming sparse support or receiving intermediate gate outputs.

Public theorem, local extension, and exact gaps

1. Public Q case. The OpenAI N51 source (dated 2026-09-24) states a deterministic polynomial-bit, polynomial-size rational list for Q-coefficient noncommutative rational formulas with no separate inverse-height or rational-constant-height bound. Its generator section proves a rational affine-pencil corollary: every feasible rational pencil of bounded order has an invertible hit. This corollary is exactly the interface needed for the feature pencil of Pf. The center can be acquired even when Phi is zero by applying the formula hitting theorem to the wrapper 1+0*Phi, whose syntax retains Phi's inverse gates and whose value is 1 wherever the original tree is defined. Thus the descriptor construction is a conditional consequence of the public Q theorem, not an all-field consequence.
2. Local all-field extension. N51's local card and R audit claim a single integer list works after reduction into every field, including arbitrary extension coefficients, and simultaneously hits families of feasible affine pencils. This is the premise that permits the exact same proof over all F. Its derivation is source-derived/unreviewed here; the public Q paper does not prove it. Keep the all-field result marked conditional on that local theorem.
3. No independent re-proof of the complete N51 q-Newton/descent/exceptional-characteristic generator was attempted here. The stated list-size and entry-height bounds are inherited claims, not independently validated. The local diagnostic reports already about 4.91e62 tuples and 2.06e69 dense integer entries at n=B=1; the complete generator and descriptor learner are not run. The only cited execution is a reduced toy replay (70 exact cases, 120 calls), which does not test the promised-size list.
4. The formula-to-affine-linearization implication used for domain preservation is standard and appears in Hrubeš–Wigderson 2015. The proof requires the forward direction “original formula defined implies the associated pencil is invertible”; rational-function equivalence alone would not suffice. The quotient argument above supplies the missing domain step, conditional on this linearization invariant.
5. No scalar-formula reconstruction or exact syntax recovery follows. Hrubeš–Wigderson prove that entries of the inverse of a generic n by n matrix have polynomial-size circuits but require 2^(Omega(n)) formula size. A polynomial-order inverse descriptor is therefore a different output model.
6. No separate historical-priority claim is made for residual realization/BFS or the descriptor learner. Minimal recognizable realizations and residual closure are standard weighted-automaton ideas; N68 is a different sparse-polynomial acquisition contract.
