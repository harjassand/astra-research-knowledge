\[

## C2. Exact spherical-tree capacity formula

Let N_n be the number of vertices at level n of a spherically symmetric leafless rooted tree. All depth-n edges have resistance R_n. For p in {2,3}, capacity is the supremum of the mass M of a conserved nonnegative flow θ satisfying

\[
\sup_\xi\sum_{e\in\xi}(R_e\theta_e)^{p-1}\le1.
\]

The uniform flow θ_e=M/N_n has the same potential on every ray, namely M^(p-1) S_p, where

\[
S_p=\sum_{n\ge1}(R_n/N_n)^{p-1}.
\tag{C2}
\]

Conversely sample a uniform ray, whose depth-n edge is uniform on that level. For every conserved flow, the average edge load is M/N_n. Jensen applied to each finite partial path sum gives an expected potential at least M^(p-1) times the corresponding partial sum in C2. A potential bounded by 1 on every ray therefore forces M^(p-1) S_p≤1. Thus, including the divergent case,

\[
\operatorname{Cap}_p(T)=S_p^{-1/(p-1)}.
\tag{C3}
\]

This is an explicit optimization: no comparison to a continuum flow or unattained supremum is involved. The uniform-flow principle is already in the older nonlinear-capacity literature; the new input is its four-state reconstruction interface with squared resistance.

## C3. An explicit bounded-degree discriminator

Set λ=1/√2 and

\[
j_n=\max\{j\ge0:2^{4j}\le(n+1)^3\},\quad
k_n=n+j_n,\quad N_n=2^{k_n},\quad k_0=0.
\tag{C4}
\]

Every vertex of level n−1 has b_n=2^(k_n−k_(n−1)) children. The increment j_n−j_(n−1) is 0 or 1, so b_n is 2 or 4. The tree is leafless, computable, and has child bound B=4. Evaluating j_n is an integer bit-length calculation, avoiding a logarithm rounding oracle. In particular,

\[
\tfrac12 2^n(n+1)^{3/4}<N_n\le2^n(n+1)^{3/4},\qquad
R_n/N_n=2^{-j_n}.
\tag{C5}
\]

Hence

\[
\sum_n R_n/N_n=\infty,
\qquad
\sum_n(R_n/N_n)^2
\le4\sum_{n\ge1}(n+1)^{-3/2}\le8.
\tag{C6}
\]

The uniform flow of **rational mass 1/3** has L3 potential at most 8/9 and is explicitly admissible. Its L2 potential diverges for every positive mass. Therefore this same tree and channel correlation have:

* binary q=2 nonreconstruction, by the published free-boundary theorem;
* four-state q=4 reconstruction, conditional on the release's preserved-class certificate.

Both have the critical exponential rate N_n^(1/n)→2=λ^(-2). More generally, for N_n comparable to λ^(-2n)(n+1)^α, the four-state capacity criterion changes at α=1/2, whereas the binary free-boundary criterion changes at α=1. In the interval 1/2<α≤1 the two answers differ. This is a deduction from the two exact theorem interfaces, not a new nonlinear-capacity method or a claim about plus boundary conditions.

## C4. Acquiring a conservative four-state information margin

This section reconstructs and makes explicit the constants in the primary sufficiency proof. It inherits only the preserved-class subadditivity input stated in C1. Let x=Lp and y=Lq be the source's three four-state coordinates. A=|x|² lies in [0,3], m=EA, and h=|E(x1x2x3)|≤m. For a product whose second input passed through channel λ, write a=m(p), b=m(q), Z=1+x·y and

\[
N_i=x_i+y_i+x_jy_k+x_ky_j,\qquad (i,j,k)\text{ distinct}.
\]

The actual product posterior law is the independent marginal pair law multiplied by Z; treating it as the unweighted independent pair law would invalidate the estimates. Here Z≥1−λ, |N_i|≤4, E(x·y)²=ab/3. By symmetry, the exact quadratic expansion gives

\[
m(p*q)\ge a+b-7(h(p)b+a h(q)).
\tag{C7}
\]

To bound the third moment, use the exact identity

\[
(1+s)^{-2}=1-2s+\frac{s^2(3+2s)}{(1+s)^2},\qquad s=x\cdot y.
\]

The polynomial N1N2N3(1−2s) has coefficient ℓ1 norm at most 4³·7=448. Its pure terms are x1x2x3 and y1y2y3. Other terms of degree one in either independent symmetric input average to zero. Every remaining mixed monomial has degree at least two in each input, so its absolute expectation is at most ab, because the coordinates are in [−1,1]. The rational remainder has absolute expectation at most 64·9·ab/[3(1−λ)²]. Therefore

\[
h(p*q)\le h(p)+h(q)+
\left[448+\frac{192}{(1-\lambda)^2}\right]ab.
\tag{C8}
\]

For λ=1/√2<3/4, a common constant C=4000 is valid in C7 and C8. Sequentially combine the at most four child branches. The primary's partial products have m≤X_v by preserved-class subadditivity, where X_v=λ^(-2|v|)εθ(v). With H_v=λ³Σ_child h_child, this gives

\[
h_v\le H_v+C X_v^2,\qquad
0\le D_v=\lambda^2\sum m_{child}-m_v
\le 2C X_v H_v+C^2X_v^3.
\tag{C9}
\]

Thus C1=2²⁴ covers the two source recursions, and C2=2⁴⁸ covers their unfolded loss. Each insertion costs C1 only once; there is no C1^depth factor. For the flow of mass 1/3, θ is explicitly uniform; scaled flow Θ=εθ has mass M=ε/3. Admissibility gives the source's path bound

\[
\sum_{i\ge0}X_{V_i}^2\le Qε^2,\qquad
Q=1+(B\lambda^2)^2=5.

At level n degrade each observed spin independently by t_v=√(X_v/3). Its terminal m is exactly X_v. Summing all deficits along a flow-sampled ray gives

\[
\frac{\text{total deficit}}M
\le C2\left(\frac{Qε^2+\sqrt Q ε}{1-\lambda}\right)
<32 C2 ε\quad (0<ε\le1).
\tag{C10}
\]

For completeness the summable kernel is acquired algebraically: with r=λ² and a depth-j descendant u of a depth-i ancestor,
r^i λ^(3(j−i)) X_u = λ^(j−i) Θ(u).
The weighted vertex sum is consequently an expectation of X_(V_i) X_(V_j) along the same ray, bounded by Qε² for each depth difference by Cauchy–Schwarz. The terminal term is bounded by √Q ε, followed by the geometric sum 1/(1−λ). This uses the actual tree posterior recursion.
