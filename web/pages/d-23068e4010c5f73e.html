# Decisive 1D comparator and a reusable-Pauli reduction

2026-10-06. Status: primary theorem/interface checked; reduction independently derived; b_math hostile conversion audit PASS, conditional on the directly checked source support/norm/expectation premises. It is a corollary/comparator, not a claimed historical discovery. No implementation or finite-copy experimental validation has been performed. ## Exact source comparator

[Chen–Anshu–Nguyen, *Learning quantum Gibbs states locally and efficiently*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02706v1), submitted 3Apr2025 15:42:23UTC; primary abs currently has only v1. TheoremI.2 and TheoremIV.3 learn every supplied bounded-locality Pauli coefficient on a D-dimensional lattice to additive epsilon at any known positive beta. Copies are `O(e^{O(beta^{cD})} beta^{-2} epsilon^{-2} log^{D+1}(1/epsilon) log(n/delta))`; total runtime, including classical processing and quantum gates, is `O(n log(n/delta) e^{O(beta^{c'D})} beta^{-2} epsilon^{-2} log^{c''D}(1/epsilon))`. Locality/degree are fixed. The algorithm uses coherent local measurements rather than SOS. Its max-coordinate guarantee implies normalized coefficient RMS epsilon when the parameter count is used for normalization.

Read exact AlgorithmIV.3, ConditionIV.2, TheoremIV.2/IV.3 and their cost/precision arguments, plus AppendixA Eq190–192 and time truncation. AlgorithmIV.3 halves a known coefficient-error radius eta. The search radius ell0 and local-net spacing kappa0 depend on beta, not eta or n. Its measurement radius is `ell=O(beta^4+beta log(1/eta))`; measurement candidates are local perturbations of the current H0. Low-beta bootstrapping is specified after TheoremIV.3. Full earlier identifiability lemmas have not all been independently reproved.

These are author claims. The following compilation into a single reusable product-Pauli dataset is ours, using the source's explicit support/norm premises. It gives a competent comparator in the proposed acquisition interface.

## Uniform local matrix interface

Fix a 1D finite-range open chain with supplied Pauli supports, fixed interaction degree/locality, coefficients in[-1,1], known beta>0, desired epsilon in(0,1/2], and failure delta. The identity coefficient is excluded as unidentifiable. Set effective beta `b=max(beta,1/d)` for the source's low-beta rescaling; requested final precision in the rescaled coefficients is proportional to `epsilon min(1,beta d)`. For clarity below beta>=1/d; for smaller beta apply that exact rescaling before choosing constants.

The source's minimum final iteration radius eta_min is between constant multiples of epsilon. Every queried Q is supported inside one interval of at most

`s = min(n, ceil(C_{q,d}(beta^4+beta log(1/epsilon)+1)))`

sites, after enlarging the constant to cover both neighborhoods of A and O and endpoint truncation. ConditionIV.2 supplies `Omega'=O(beta^2)` and `alpha=2d exp(200(d+q)beta log(d beta))`. AppendixA Eq191–192 gives a UNIFORM bound

`||Q|| <= M_beta = C_{q,d} e^{beta Omega'/2}/sqrt(beta)`

for ||A||<=1, ||O||<=2, with the factor2 included in C. It holds for every Hermitian local candidate G,K because real-time conjugations are unitary. It does not use a target eigenbasis, global spectral floor, or successful guess. Q can be complex/non-Hermitian; both the norm duality and absolute-error argument remain valid.

Suppose one classical dataset supplies, for every length-s interval L, a Hermitian trace-one matrix hatrho_L satisfying `||hatrho_L-rho_L||_1<=tau`. Then EVERY supported candidate Q, including candidates chosen adaptively from this dataset, obeys

`|Tr((hatrho_L-rho_L) Q)| <= tau M_beta`.

Choose `tau=epsilon sqrt(beta)/(C alpha M_beta)`, with sufficiently large constant C, and local operator computation error `zeta=epsilon sqrt(beta)/(C alpha)`. Since `||hatrho_L||_1<=1+tau`,

`|Tr(hatrho_L Qhat)-Tr(rho_L Q)| <= tau M_beta+(1+tau)zeta`.

This is at most the source's required `sqrt(beta) eta/(80 alpha)` for every eta>=eta_min by increasing C. Thus the same deterministic local minimax searches and their halving proof can be run offline. No extra union bound over an adaptive Hamiltonian family is needed: trace error controls all matrices in its norm ball simultaneously. Positivity of hatrho is unnecessary because only approximate expectations are used; one may project it if desired and account for extra error. The original proof uses only its expectation-error guarantee in AlgorithmIV.3; it does not require observations to come from its chosen coherent measurement implementation.

## One physically explicit Pauli dataset

On each independent target copy measure each qubit in an independently uniform X,Y,Z basis, retaining basis labels and signed outcomes. This is one fixed, nonadaptive acquisition scheme. For each Pauli P supported in an interval L of length at most s, let w(P) be its weight. The single-copy unbiased estimator of `r_P=Tr(rho P)` is zero unless the measured bases match P on all nonidentity sites, and is then `3^{w(P)}` times the outcome product. It has magnitude at most3^s.

For t>0, Hoeffding plus a union bound over at most n4^s coefficients yields simultaneous coefficient error at most t with probability1-delta using

`N = O(9^s t^{-2} log(n4^s/delta))`

copies. The identity coefficient is set exactly to1. Construct

`hatrho_L = 2^{-|L|} sum_{P on L} hatr_P P`.

Pauli orthogonality gives `||hatrho_L-rho_L||_2^2 = 2^{-|L|} sum_P |hatr_P-r_P|^2 <= 2^{|L|} t^2`; rank/Frobenius comparison gives trace error at most `2^{|L|}t`. Taking `t=tau/2^s` proves

`N = O(36^s tau^{-2} log(n4^s/delta))`.

All intervals share these same copies; overlap does not invalidate the union bound or require independence between coefficient estimators. Storing raw outcomes costs O(nN) bits up to confidence/precision overhead. A deliberately simple implementation evaluates all interval/Pauli estimators in O(nN4^s s) operations; this is near-linear in n up to log(n/delta), polynomial in1/epsilon at each fixed beta. The entire target acquisition uses product Pauli measurements and no model evolution, Gibbs preparation or target modular access.

This crude bound is sufficient for selection; optimized classical-shadow variance bounds are unnecessary. It is not the author's near-optimal epsilon^{-2} coherent-measurement copy theorem. The polynomial epsilon exponent and enormous beta-dependent constants must be retained in any comparison.

## Charged offline computation

At each iteration the source searches a finite set of local correction pairs. ConditionIV.2 makes the number per site `S_beta=exp(poly(beta))`, independent of epsilon,n. There are O(log(1/epsilon)) iterations. The background H0 changes, but each Q depends only on its length-s local restriction, available from the current coefficient list. Starting from H0=0, each coefficient update has magnitude at most eta=2^{-k}; their geometric sum is at most2. The temporary queried corrections obey the same constant bound after enlarging it by1. Thus EVERY queried local Hamiltonian has O(s) operator norm even on an unsuccessful estimation history. One may also clip updated coefficients to[-1,1], which cannot increase their distance to the promised true coefficients, but the bound does not require clipping.

Compute Q on a dense `D=2^s` local matrix space, using its explicit two-real-time integral Eq190. AppendixA/A.1 supplies Gaussian/exponential tail truncation times poly_beta(log(1/epsilon)). The known kernel functions are computable; real-time integrand derivatives are bounded using `||[H_L,A(t)]||<=2||H_L||||A||`, and `||H_L||=O(s)` under the bounded local coefficient promise. A finite rectangular quadrature with polynomially many points in s,1/zeta and beta-dependent kernel bounds therefore suffices. Dense local matrix exponentials/products have polynomial(D,s,log(1/zeta)) bit cost, including the coefficient bit precision. No global target matrix is computed. Direct diagonalization may be replaced by certified matrix exponential evaluation to avoid any eigenvalue-separation assumption.

Consequently a conservative total classical-work bound has form

`n log(n/delta) C_beta (1/epsilon)^{c_beta} polylog(1/epsilon)`

and a copy bound of the same form without n, for constants fixed by the admitted beta, locality and degree. To see polynomial rather than quasipolynomial accuracy dependence explicitly, `2^s<=exp(O(beta^4)) (1/epsilon)^{O(beta)}` and tau is constant_beta times epsilon. Multiply only finitely many polynomial powers of these quantities, S_beta and log iterations.

The source's exact algorithmic theorem and standard tomography conversion, not a new integrated-score optimization theorem, supply this comparator. Historical publication of this particular corollary is not asserted. A competent equally informed baseline can make the conversion whether or not it was separately published.

## Selection consequence and remaining possible contributions

The proposed chain-DP bound `n exp[O_beta(log^2(1/epsilon))]` is quasipolynomial in inverse accuracy at fixed beta. The comparator above has polynomial inverse-accuracy dependence and stronger max-coordinate accuracy under the same known finite-range1D parameter interface, even with one reusable product-Pauli dataset. Thus the DP construction cannot be selected on a broad near-linear-n/non-SOS/nonadaptive-Pauli recovery advantage. Explicit numerical beta constants may differ; no uniform domination in beta or practical runtime is claimed.

Integrated-score population stability and its measurable observable can still be interesting mathematical results. To justify an algorithmic priority, establish an independently audited feature not already reproducible by this baseline, such as a materially better charged beta dependence or accuracy exponent, a supplied-support weakening, or a new model-testing certificate. A finite demonstration cannot replace that comparison.

## Other primary local-reconstruction routes checked

[Alhambra–Capel–Gondolf–Ruiz-de-Alarcón–Scalet, *Conditional Independence of1D Gibbs States with Applications to Efficient Learning*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00023-026-01655-6), published/version of record5May2026. ArXiv2402.18500v1 is28Feb2024; read recoverable latest v4 3Oct2025 plus published theorem/proof excerpts. Corollary4.5 constructs an MPO from local marginals of size O_beta(log(n/epsilon)/loglog(n/epsilon)); Theorem4.9 gives global trace-error epsilon, sample/classical cost poly(n/epsilon)log(1/delta), assuming translation-invariant interactions. It directly learns the state rather than physical Hamiltonian coefficients. Hence it is relevant recent-window reconstruction prior but is not by itself the strongest matching coefficient-recovery comparator.

[Artymowicz, *Efficient Hamiltonian learning from Gibbs states*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18061v2), v1 26Mar2024; v2 23Sep2024. Its §3 matrix energy-entropy-balance SDP fits a Hamiltonian to local correlations, with a hierarchy of necessary convex constraints and a usable dual certificate. Read §2–3 theorem/algorithm interface. The source does not give an unrestricted fixed-locality worst-case coefficient-convergence rate; a100-qubit numerical chain is not such a theorem. It therefore supplies practical entropy-fit prior rather than the decisive uniform algorithm above.

[Kato–Brandão, *Quantum Approximate Markov Chains are Thermal*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06636v3), v1 21 September 2016, v3 24 April 2019. Theorem 1 and its full §III.2 proof show that an epsilon-approximate open quantum Markov chain admits SOME coarse nearest-neighbor Gibbs approximation with relative entropy at most m epsilon. The proof uses the maximum-entropy extension of adjacent marginals (§III.1, Eqs10–15), entropy telescoping, and reverse-information closure. It supplies neither an efficient optimizer for that extension nor recovery of the original physical Hamiltonian coefficients with bounded terms. Theorem 4 supplies stretched-exponential recovery locality for all-temperature short-range 1D Gibbs states; Appendix A Lemma11 gives factorially decaying locality of Araki perturbation operators. Thus these are structural prerequisites, not a stronger matching recovery algorithm than Chen–Anshu–Nguyen. Small conditional mutual information is an expectation/entropy statement; it alone does not establish an operator-norm estimate for the requested log-marginal residual. Such a claim would need a separate modular-locality theorem and its exact assumptions.
