1. What survives reconstruction—and what it does not imply
For a centered compatible gradient \(X=D\varphi\), write
\[
C=\mathbb E[\operatorname{vec}(X)\operatorname{vec}(X)^T],
\qquad
H=\mathbb E D^2F(X),\qquad
b=\mathbb E DF(X),\qquad c=\mathbb EF(X).
\]
Quartic polarization gives
\[
\mathbb E[F(A_0+X)-F(A_0)]
=c+b:A_0+\frac12A_0:H A_0.
\tag{1}
\]
I reconstructed the previous nullspace argument. Its key ingredients remain valid:
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=D^2F(X)[A,A],
\]
and, because rank-one matrices are precisely the null cone of the planar determinant,
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=P_A(X)+\lambda_A\det X,
\qquad P_A\succeq0.
\tag{2}
\]
The quadratic decomposition is the established indefinite case of the non-strict Finsler lemma, not a new ingredient. arXiv
Determinant balance gives \(H\succeq0\). If \(A\in\ker H\), the law is supported on \(\ker P_A\). Acoustic equality there propagates, through quartic tensor symmetry, into
\[
DF(X)[A]=0
\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
\]
Thus \(b\in\operatorname{ran}H\), including singular \(H\), and
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_F(X):=\min_{A_0}\mathbb E[F(A_0+X)-F(A_0)]
=c-\frac12b^TH^\dagger b.
}
\tag{3}
\]

3. Stronger rigidity across the complete nine-coefficient cone
The obstruction above does not invalidate quantitative analysis altogether. It identifies what must be separated: second-moment directional collapse versus fourth-moment concentration.
The first can now be controlled more sharply.
Define the explicitly polyconvex class
\[
\mathcal E=
\left\{
\gamma(\det A)^2+
|\alpha x_{\mathbb C}+\beta y_{\mathbb C}|^4:
\gamma\ge0,\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C
\right\},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_2=\mathcal E\ \cup\

a|Ar|^4+

a\ge0,\ r\in\mathbb R^2,\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C

}

The second class is convex in the matrix entries.
Theorem — Two-contact rigidity
For a left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic \(F\), the following are equivalent:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\exists\,C\succeq0,\quad
\mathbb E\det X=0,\quad
\operatorname{rank}C\ge2,\quad H_F(C)\text{ singular};\\
&\exists\text{ independent rank-one }R,S,\quad
D^2F(R)+D^2F(S)\text{ singular};\\
&F\in\mathcal P_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Here \(H_F(C)\) denotes the linear map taking a second moment to its averaged quartic Hessian.
Therefore, for every non-polyconvex member of the full nine-coefficient cone,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}C\ge2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
H_F(C)\succ0.
}
\tag{14}
\]
This improves the previous threshold from covariance rank three to rank two. It also identifies the complete exception, rather than merely excluding some coefficient slices. The previous theorem only gave the rank-three statement.    ASTRA_FULL_CONE_RIGIDITY
Why two contacts are enough
Choose \(A\ne0\) in the nullspace of \(H_F(C)\). From (2),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_AC)=0,
\]
so
\[
\operatorname{ran}C\subseteq\ker P_A,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}P_A\le2.
\tag{15}
\]
The new part is classifying this rank-two remainder.
If its kernel \(U\) is two-dimensional and supports a positive balanced covariance, the determinant on \(U\) must be either indefinite or identically zero. A nonzero semidefinite quadratic form could not have zero average under a covariance positive on all of \(U\).
For an invertible \(A\), normalize it to \(I\) and use conformal/anticonformal coordinates
\[
X=iI+jJ+kK+lL,\qquad z=i+\mathrm ij,\quad w=k+\mathrm il,
\qquad \det X=|z|^2-|w|^2.
\]
The complete invariant polynomial has nine real parameters:
\[
F=f|z|^4+\beta|z|^2|w|^2+g|w|^4+
\operatorname{Re}\!\left(
\delta z^2\bar w^2+\epsilon|z|^2z\bar w+\eta|w|^2z\bar w
\right).
\]
After a rotation, take \(\epsilon=a\ge0\). Write
\[
p=12f-\lambda,\qquad r=4f-\lambda.
\]
When \(p,r>0\), rank two forces the kernel to be
\[
i=-3ak/p,\qquad j=-al/r.
\tag{16}
\]
If the determinant is indefinite there, the reconstructed cubic-closure theorem requires \(\partial_iF|_U=0\). The difference of two coefficients of this cubic is exactly
\[
-\frac{4\lambda^3a^3}{r^2p^3}.
\]
Its vanishing forces \(\lambda a=0\), but either possibility makes the determinant restriction semidefinite—not indefinite. This branch is impossible.
If the determinant vanishes identically on \(U\), the complete polynomial reduces to
\[
F=f|z+w|^4+(g-f)|w|^4,\qquad g-f\ge0,
\]
which is in \(\mathcal P_2\).
The rank-one distinguished background and singular upper-block cases are handled separately in the proof. They also give only the explicit class (12); no invertibility or genericity assumption is left in the theorem.
A quantitative covariance estimate
For \(F\notin\mathcal P_2\), define the local acoustic modulus
\[
\mu_F(s)=
\min_{\substack{|R|=|S|=1,\ \det R=\det S=0\\
1-(R:S)^2\ge s^2}}
\lambda_{\min}\!\left(D^2F(R)+D^2F(S)\right).
\tag{17}
\]
Two-contact rigidity and compactness imply \(\mu_F(s)>0\) for \(s>0\).
For a determinant-balanced covariance, set
\[
M_2=\operatorname{tr}C,\qquad
\tau=1-\frac{\lambda_{\max}(C)}{M_2}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\lambda_{\min}H_F(C)
\ge
\frac{M_2\tau}{4}\,
\mu_F\!\left(\sqrt{\tau/2}\right).
}
\tag{18}
\]
The proof decomposes \(C\) into at most four rank-one matrix atoms. For two independent normalized atoms,
\[
\mathbb E[1-(R:S)^2]
=
1-\operatorname{tr}\bigl[(C/M_2)^2\bigr]\ge\tau.
\]
A positive fraction of pairs are therefore separated, and their acoustic Hessian sums have the lower bound (17).
Thus, for a fixed non-polyconvex \(F\), an ill-conditioned normalized averaged Hessian must accompany covariance collapse toward one matrix line. This is quantitative control derived from local positivity. It does not control the fourth-moment mass hidden in a shrinking region, as the construction in Section 2 demonstrates.
4. The remaining contact geometry admits a nonlinear shear operation
The semidefinite contact geometry is real, not an artifact. For \(G\) in (4),
\[
D^2G(I)[X,X]
=
\frac12(3a-2d)^2+\frac56c^2-\det X.
\tag{19}
\]
The positive remainder has kernel
\[
U=\{c=0,\ d=3a/2\},
\qquad
\det|_U=\frac32a^2.
\]
Its unique null direction is the shear line \(\mathbb Re_{12}\), and
\[
DG(X)[I]|_U=-\frac5{12}a^3.
\]
The following identifies this structure throughout the non-polyconvex cone.
Theorem — Contact shear and cubic invariance
Suppose \(F\) is a non-polyconvex rank-one-convex quartic in the full invariant cone, \(A\) is invertible, and
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=P(X)+\lambda\det X,
\qquad P\succeq0,\quad\operatorname{rank}P=2.
\]
Assume the determinant restricted to \(U=\ker P\) is semidefinite of rank one. Let \(R\ne0\) span its radical. Then
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}R)=0,
\qquad
DF(X+tR)[A]=DF(X)[A]\quad(X\in U).
}
\tag{20}
\]
In particular,
\[
\det(A+tR)=\det A
\quad\text{for every }t.
\]
The proof requires more than setting derivatives to zero at an acoustic contact. In the normalized variables above, there are two candidate tangent branches. One has an actual second-order acoustic variation equal to
\[
-\frac{(p-3r)^4}{324r^2V}<0,
\qquad V>0.
\tag{21}
\]
The proof explicitly varies both the background and rank-one direction to attain this coefficient. It is therefore a genuine contradiction to rank-one convexity, not failure of a proposed certificate. The surviving branch has trace-free radical and the cubic invariance in (20).
Straightening a finite contact mode
Write
\[
R=a_0\otimes b_0,\qquad v_0=A^{-1}a_0.
\]
Equation (20) says \(b_0\cdot v_0=0\). For a scalar profile \(h\), define
\[
\boxed{
\Psi_h(x)=x+v_0h(b_0\cdot x),
\qquad
\Psi_h^{-1}(x)=x-v_0h(b_0\cdot x).
}
\tag{22}
\]
These are exact inverses, and
\[
\det D\Psi_h=1.
\]
For a periodic admissible direction and profile, this is a volume-preserving torus diffeomorphism.
For a residual potential \(w\), put
\[
\widetilde w=w\circ\Psi_h^{-1}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\int F\!\left(A+Rh'(b_0\cdot x)+Dw(x)\right)\,dx\\
&\quad=
\int F\!\left(
(A+D\widetilde w(z))
[I+v_0\otimes b_0\,h'(b_0\cdot z)]
\right)\,dz.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{23}
\]
This is an actual nonlinear coordinate operation: it straightens an arbitrarily large finite contact shear while preserving volume and gradient compatibility.
It is not yet a positivity theorem for the residual. The transformed integrand varies with position and is correlated with \(D\widetilde w\). Averaging away that dependence would be unjustified.
The pure contact shear itself has
\[
\int[F(A+Rh')-F(A)]
=
F(R)\int(h')^4\ge0.
\tag{24}
\]
The remaining problem is its interaction with the residual in (23), not the contact mode alone.
This route does not assume the heat-flow/exact-form framework. That framework and the left-and-right rotational quartic theorem are established in Bruno and Pasqualotto’s recent work; neither supplies the spatially varying inequality now required. arXiv
5. Retaining the concentration defect instead of discarding it
The example in Section 2 suggests keeping the finite contribution lost by the ordinary limiting pair \((H_0,b_0)\).
For a zero-boundary profile, critical two-dimensional scaling gives
\[
\psi_\rho(x)=\rho^{1/2}\psi(x/\rho),
\]
\[
H_\rho=\rho H,\qquad b_\rho=\sqrt\rho\,b,\qquad c_\rho=c.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\Delta_F(D\psi_\rho)=\Delta_F(D\psi).}
\tag{25}
\]
Every hypothetical negative profile could be concentrated arbitrarily close to the zero averaged Hessian without changing its optimized deficit. Small absolute Hessian norm is therefore not a simplification of the problem.
Homogeneous concentration scaling is not new. Recent higher-dimensional work also exploits it, but does not resolve the planar \(2\times2\) question. arXiv
Exact finite-profile formula
Let a base gradient have \(H_0\succeq0\), \(b_0=0\), and vanish where finitely many disjoint profiles will be inserted. Put
\[
N=\ker H_0.
\]
Insert profiles \(\psi_j\) at scales \(\rho\theta_j\). Exactly,
\[
H_\rho=H_0+\rho\sum_j\theta_jH_j,\qquad
b_\rho=\sqrt\rho\sum_j\sqrt{\theta_j}b_j,\qquad
c_\rho=c_0+\sum_jc_j.
\]
Define on \(N\)
\[
K=P_N\left(\sum_j\theta_jH_j\right)P_N,
\qquad
d=P_N\sum_j\sqrt{\theta_j}b_j.
\]
If \(K\succ0\) on \(N\), then
\[
\boxed{
\lim_{\rho\downarrow0}\Delta_F(D\varphi_\rho)
=
c_0+\sum_jc_j-\frac12d^TK^{-1}d.
}
\tag{26}
\]
This formula retains the finite concentration defect explicitly.
It also yields a useful exclusion:
\[
d^TK^{-1}d
\le
\sum_j b_j^TH_j^\dagger b_j,
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
\lim\Delta_F(D\varphi_\rho)
\ge c_0+\sum_j\Delta_F(D\psi_j).
}
\tag{27}
\]
The inequality follows from ordinary quadratic duality: the common background is a restricted choice compared with optimizing each profile’s background independently. Separate nonnegative profiles cannot become a negative construction merely by sharing a diverging affine background.
This is an exact gluing theorem, not a general profile decomposition for arbitrary gradient sequences. It does not prove that the internally resonant profiles have nonnegative gaps.
6. Counterexample tests and the remaining mathematical operation
A separate exact test constructed a bounded, centered, determinant-balanced law for \(G\) with positive-definite \(H\) and a negative optimized gap. It is not compatible. Its support lies in a two-dimensional matrix subspace, where actual periodic gradients satisfy rank-one Jensen through the two-phase Fourier decomposition.
That test confirms a precise limitation: determinant balance, covariance information, bounded support, and \(H\succ0\) still do not encode the needed fourth-order differential compatibility.
The actual compatible Fourier search used the exactly certified \(G\), cutoff three, a dealiased \(13\times13\) grid, six initializations, and 800 optimization steps per initialization. The smallest directly reevaluated gap was approximately
\[
1.1651868296208177.
\]
No negative gap was found. These are local-search observations, not a lower bound or a quasiconvexity certificate.
The investigation therefore changes the remaining task in three concrete ways.
First, second-moment degeneracy is now quantitatively localized. Outside an explicit polyconvex class, a poorly conditioned normalized averaged Hessian requires covariance collapse toward one matrix line.
Second, the ordinary singular limit loses a finite optimized contribution. The compatible construction proves that this is not a technical concern. Any limit argument must retain concentration data and the possibility of an escaping affine background.
Third, the relevant tangent contact can be straightened exactly. But this creates a spatially varying rank-one-convex energy, and the correlation between that coefficient field and the residual gradient remains uncontrolled.
The missing operation is therefore not another proof that \(H\succeq0\), nor continuity of its inverse. It is a compatibility estimate that controls the residual resonances after contact-shear straightening while retaining the finite concentration defect—or an explicit compatible profile violating that estimate.
The full local-to-global barrier remains. What is established here is a stronger full-cone rigidity theorem, a quantitative acoustic control of covariance degeneration, an exact nonlinear contact transformation, and a compatible obstruction showing why singular-face positivity cannot be extended by simply passing to a limit.
