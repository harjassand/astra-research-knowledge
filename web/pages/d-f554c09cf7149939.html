# Exact cone controls: the detector gap and a forward-inclusive construction

2026-10-06. Supplement to exact_legendre_export.md. These are independent controls, not a completion of the actual STU gravity cone. r10 first communicated the detector-gap no-go; the proof below independently reconstructs it.

## A nontrivial all-l cone cannot have a forward gap

Let h be any finite signed measure on [-1,1] supported in [-1,1-epsilon], epsilon>0, and define a_l=integral P_l(z)dh(z). If every a_l>=0, then h=0.

For 0<rho<1 the exact Poisson kernel generating function is

    sum_(l>=0) (2l+1)rho^l P_l(z)
       =(1-rho^2)/(1-2rho*z+rho^2)^(3/2).

Derive it by applying 2rho*d/drho+1 to the Legendre generating function. The series is uniformly absolutely convergent for fixed rho, since |P_l(z)|<=1. Thus its integral against h equals sum (2l+1)rho^l a_l. On the support of h its denominator is bounded away from zero as rho->1; the integral tends to zero, since its numerator tends to zero. If any a_l>0 the nonnegative series is at least (2l+1)rho^l a_l and has positive limiting lower bound, contradiction. Therefore all a_l=0. Legendre polynomials span all polynomials; polynomials are uniformly dense in continuous functions on [-1,1], so the finite measure h is zero.

For an even-l cone, apply the same proof to h_even=(h(z)+h(-z))/2, with support away from both endpoints. It proves h_even=0. If the original support is wholly in [0,1-epsilon] and contains no atom at zero, then h_even=0 implies h=0 because the two reflected supports are disjoint. For a density, the single endpoint z=0 has zero measure and causes no exception. A general measure can have an atom there, but its symmetrization retains that atom, so it also vanishes. Thus the forward-branch gravity density is zero in this case as well. For a general signed measure supported on both hemispheres, a nonzero odd measure has zero even coefficients: one must retain this caveat.

The source's exact fixed-s coefficients with E>0 and a bounded integrable smearing yield precisely a measure supported away from z=1. Its STU forward branch has z>=0; the gravity even-l version obeys the same obstruction. This does not refute the source: EqA.28-A.29 extends the coefficient integral to q=0 before requiring its cone. It shows why that extension is load-bearing, rather than an innocuous finite-detector truncation. A nonzero finite-cut cone cannot be repaired merely by making an all-l approximation error small.

## Forward-inclusive fixed-transfer cone: explicit positive control

For R>0 let h(x)=1_(|x|<=R/2) on Euclidean R^3. Its normalized radial autocorrelation is

    f_R(r) = integral h(x)h(x+r*e)dx / integral h(x)^2dx
           = (1-r/R)^2(1+r/(2R)), 0<=r<=R,
           =0, r>R.

The formula follows from the volume of the intersection of two balls of radius R/2: two caps with height (R-r)/2, divided by the ball volume. In particular f_R(0)=1 and f_R is continuous.

For any Euclidean points x_i and real coefficients c_i,

    sum_ij c_i*c_j f_R(|x_i-x_j|)
      = integral [sum_i c_i h(y-x_i)]^2dy / volume(ball) >=0.

Restriction to the sphere of radius a=sqrt(s)/2 preserves this Gram identity. The restricted kernel depends only on z=n dot m, with distance a|n-m|=sqrt(s(1-z)/2). The induced integral operator on the sphere is positive: the same square identity holds with sum replaced by an integral against any square-integrable function. Rotational invariance and the spherical-harmonic addition identity give its eigenvalue on degree l as

    k_l = 2*pi integral_-1^1 f_R(sqrt(s(1-z)/2))*P_l(z)dz >=0.

One can check this normalization by taking the m=0 harmonic at the north pole and integrating its azimuthal average. Positivity of the operator then forces this real eigenvalue to be nonnegative. This is the classical positive-definite-kernel restriction mechanism, not a new theorem.

If R<=sqrt(s), the change of variable z=1-2r^2/s gives

    integral_0^R r f_R(r) P_l(1-2r^2/s)dr
       =(s/4) integral_-1^1 f_R(sqrt(s(1-z)/2))*P_l(z)dz
       =s*k_l/(8*pi) >=0, all l>=0.                    (1)

Thus psi(r)=r*f_R(r), extended to zero, is an explicit admissible cone control for the fixed-transfer kernel with a positive q-independent dispersion prefactor, simultaneously for every s>=R^2. It avoids any finite partial-wave grid or Bessel approximation. It is an exact construction, not evidence that arbitrary two-dimensional Hankel-positive smearings are exact spherical-cone positive.

The actual gravity source uses an STU transfer and a q-dependent dispersion weight. The single s-independent psi(q) does not map automatically to r*f_R(r) at all s. Signed moment constraints on Wilson coefficients can also destroy positivity when combining kernels. Therefore this construction does not establish the source's gravity cone or its optimized inequalities. Its downstream use is an exact positive control against which a proposed general cone exporter can be tested.

Truncation to E=1/10 produces a negative coefficient in every tested s; at s=1,l=4 the full coefficient is34/15015 but its truncated value is-3622379123439/2002000000000000. These are exact fractions, not signs inferred from floating quadrature. The finite examples discriminate the full and truncated interfaces; the universal no-go follows from the Poisson proof, not this scan. Complete values: results/r12_geometry/cycle5/cone_controls_check.json.

Positive-definite restrictions to spheres and nonnegative spherical coefficients are the classical Schoenberg mechanism; compact radial autocorrelations are classical.
