SU(3) adjoint carrier: multiplicity blocks and an orbit-EB obstruction
======================================================================

Status and scope
----------------

This is an exact finite-dimensional test case for covariance with a genuine
multiplicity space.  The carrier is the regular SU(3) irrep V_(1,1), the
adjoint representation of dimension d=8.  It gives (i) the full block sizes
of covariant marginal Choi operators, (ii) a necessary-and-sufficient block
SDP for symmetric 1-to-2 extensions, and (iii) a legal extendible marginal
that no normalized covariant orbit measure-and-prepare design can dominate
with coefficient 1 on its adjoint transfer block.

This does not disprove a scaled comparison, a squared comparison, or a
dimension-free theorem with a constant larger than one.  The explicit
Clebsch/Racah coefficient matrices for the whole extension SDP are not
expanded below; the exact feasible set is specified as the image of the
listed PSD blocks under their partial-trace recoupling map.  No claim about
arbitrary compact groups follows.

Conventions
-----------

Let V=C^8 with the real adjoint SU(3) representation in the color basis
|a>, a=1,...,8.  Let lambda_a be the standard Hermitian Gell-Mann matrices,
Tr(lambda_a lambda_b)=2 delta_ab, and set

  f_abc = Tr([lambda_a,lambda_b] lambda_c)/(4 i),
  d_abc = Tr({lambda_a,lambda_b} lambda_c)/4.

Thus f is real and antisymmetric, d is real and symmetric, and
sum_ab f_abe f_abf=3 delta_ef,
sum_ab d_abe d_abf=(5/3) delta_ef.  The normalized Choi convention is
J(Phi)=(id tensor Phi)(|Omega><Omega|), with
|Omega>=8^(-1/2) sum_a |a>_barV |a>_V; hence Tr J=1.  Since the adjoint is
real, barV is identified with V only after this convention is fixed.

1. Marginal Choi block structure
--------------------------------

The adjoint tensor square is

  8 tensor 8 = 1 + 8_s + 8_a + 10 + 10bar + 27,

where Sym^2(8)=1+8_s+27 and wedge^2(8)=8_a+10+10bar.  The dimensions sum
to 64.  One direct projector construction, used in the check script, is

  P_1[(ab),(cd)]   = delta_ab delta_cd/8,
  P_8s[(ab),(cd)]  = (3/5) sum_e d_abe d_cde,
  P_8a[(ab),(cd)]  = (1/3) sum_e f_abe f_cde,
  P_27             = (I+F)/2 - P_1 - P_8s,
  P_(10+10bar)     = (I-F)/2 - P_8a,

with F the output-swap operator.  The two 10-dimensional summands are
separate inequivalent SU(3) irreps; the last displayed projector is their
sum.  The formulas are exact and the ranks are 1,8,8,27,20 respectively.

The commutant form of a covariant normalized Choi operator is

  J = x_1 I_1  + I_8 tensor X_8
      + x_10 I_10 + x_10bar I_10bar + x_27 I_27,

where X_8 is a 2-by-2 Hermitian matrix and x_1,x_10,x_10bar,x_27 are real.
Complete positivity is exactly

  X_8 >= 0,  x_1,x_10,x_10bar,x_27 >= 0.

In this notation Tr J=x_1+8 Tr(X_8)+10x_10+10x_10bar+27x_27=1.  Covariance
and this normalization already imply both Choi marginals are I/8: each
one-body partial trace commutes with an irreducible representation, hence is
a scalar identity, and its trace fixes the scalar.  Thus there are no extra
TP or unital constraints on this block form.  This is a useful peculiarity
of an irreducible carrier with equal input and output representation.

2. Exact symmetric-extension Choi feasibility set
--------------------------------------------------

An extension Choi operator K acts on barV_A tensor V_B tensor V_C.  A
channel is symmetrically 1-to-2 extendible iff there is a K satisfying

  K >= 0,
  [K, bar(U_g)_A tensor U_g_B tensor U_g_C]=0 for every g in SU(3),
  F_BC K F_BC = K,
  J(Phi)=Tr_C K.

The last two conditions imply Tr_B K=Tr_C K.  Normalization Tr K=1 makes
the input marginal I/8 by covariance and Schur's lemma.  Conversely, an
extension can be output-swap averaged and group-twirled without changing a
covariant marginal, so these conditions are necessary and sufficient.  The
swap condition means that K is block diagonal between the symmetric and
antisymmetric output sectors; it does NOT mean that K is supported only on
the symmetric sector.

The output decompositions are

  Sym^2(8) = 1 + 8 + 27,
  wedge^2(8) = 8 + 10 + 10bar.

Coupling the input 8 to these sectors gives the following exact isotypic
multiplicities.  The label d_lambda is the irrep dimension; m^+ and m^-
are its multiplicities in bar8 tensor Sym^2(8) and bar8 tensor wedge^2(8).

  lambda          d_lambda       m^+       m^-
  1                   1            1         1
  8                   8            4         4
  10                 10            2         2
  10bar              10            2         2
  27                 27            3         3
  64                 64            1         0
  35                 35            1         1
  35bar              35            1         1

For a check of these products without relying on a tensor-product table, use
3 tensor 3bar=1+8 and
V_(p,q) tensor 3=V_(p+1,q)+V_(p-1,q+1)+V_(p,q-1), omitting negative labels,
with the conjugate rule for 3bar.  For example,
27 tensor 3=(3,2)+(1,3)+(2,1); tensoring each term by 3bar and subtracting
one copy of 27 gives

  8 tensor 27 = (3,3)+(4,1)+(1,4)+2(2,2)+(3,0)+(0,3)+(1,1),

whose dimensions sum to 216.  The table's plus and minus dimension checks are
288=8*36 and 224=8*28.

Choose orthonormal SU(3) intertwiners
T^epsilon_(lambda,alpha):V_lambda -> barV tensor W_epsilon,
where W_+=Sym^2(V), W_-=wedge^2(V), and alpha ranges over the table's
multiplicity.  Then the complete invariant, swap-fixed positive cone is
parameterized by

  K_epsilon = sum_lambda sum_(alpha,beta)
        (X^epsilon_lambda)_(alpha,beta)
        sum_(r=1)^(d_lambda)
          |T^epsilon_(lambda,alpha)e_r><T^epsilon_(lambda,beta)e_r|,
  K = K_+ + K_-,
  X^epsilon_lambda >= 0,
  sum_(epsilon,lambda) d_lambda Tr(X^epsilon_lambda)=1.

The matrix sizes of X^epsilon_lambda are exactly m^epsilon_lambda in the
table.  Define the fixed recoupling matrices

  R^epsilon_(lambda,alpha,beta) = Tr_C sum_r
       |T^epsilon_(lambda,alpha)e_r><T^epsilon_(lambda,beta)e_r|.

Then the actual marginal feasibility equations are

  J = sum_(epsilon,lambda,alpha,beta)
        (X^epsilon_lambda)_(alpha,beta) R^epsilon_(lambda,alpha,beta),
  X^epsilon_lambda >= 0,
  sum_(epsilon,lambda) d_lambda Tr(X^epsilon_lambda)=1.

This is a finite exact spectrahedral-image characterization, not merely a
necessary Choi witness inequality.  It also makes explicit where further
work would be needed for a scalar closed form: compute the Racah/partial-
trace coefficient matrices R in a fixed CG gauge.  The projector witness
below does not require those full matrices.

3. A legal extendible marginal with large multiplicity-block trace
------------------------------------------------------------------

For any invariant orthogonal projector P contained in Sym^2(V), of rank r,
define

  Gamma_P(X) = (4/r) P [(X tensor I)+(I tensor X)] P.

This symmetrized compression is covariant, completely positive, and
output-swap invariant.  Indeed Tr_2 P=(r/8)I by irreducibility, so

  Tr Gamma_P(X)=(4/r) [Tr((X tensor I)P)+Tr((I tensor X)P)]=Tr X,

and Gamma_P(I)=(8/r)P, whose one-body marginal is I.  Thus it is an
explicit symmetric extension of

  Phi_P(X) = Tr_2 Gamma_P(X).

Take P=P_27, r=27.  For fixed e define orthonormal adjoint-copy bases in
operator space End(V), with normalized Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
tau(A,B)=Tr(A^dagger B)/8:

  (B_s,e)_(ac) = sqrt(24/5) d_ace,
  (B_a,e)_(ac) = i sqrt(8/3) f_ace.

The identities above give tau(B_u,e,B_v,f)=delta_uv delta_ef.  Direct exact
contraction of the displayed projectors gives the channel transfer matrix
on these two adjoint copies:

  [ tau(B_v,e, Phi_P27(B_u,e)) ]_(v,u)
       = diag(14/25, 2/3), for every e=1,...,8.

The trace of this 2-by-2 multiplicity block is 92/75.

4. What a covariant orbit EB design can capture
-----------------------------------------------

For normalized pure seeds alpha,beta in V, the covariant orbit
measure-and-prepare channel

  E_(alpha,beta)(X) = 8 integral_SU(3)
      Tr(rho_(alpha,g) X) rho_(beta,g) dg,
  rho_(alpha,g)=U_g|alpha><alpha|U_g^dagger,

is bistochastic, covariant and entanglement breaking.  The POVM effects
8 rho_(alpha,g) dg integrate to I, and the prepared orbit also averages to
I/8.  Let

  q_(u,e)(alpha)=tau(B_u,e, |alpha><alpha|),  u in {s,a}.

Schur orthogonality for the real adjoint irrep gives its transfer matrix on
the two adjoint copies exactly as

  M_(v,u) = 8 sum_e q_(v,e)(beta) q_(u,e)(alpha).

In particular a same-seed orbit has a positive semidefinite Gram matrix.
The seed alpha=(|1>+i|2>)/sqrt(2) gives

  M_8 = diag(1/5,1/3),

so one orbit can excite both multiplicity directions.  The basis seed |1>
instead gives diag(1/5,0).  Thus multiplicity by itself does not force a
single orbit seed to miss a copy; the seed's adjoint-copy moment matrix
matters.  The complex seed does control the P_27 adjoint block with scale
c=14/5, since max{(14/25)/(1/5),(2/3)/(1/3)}=14/5.  The result below is
therefore an exact obstruction to unit-scale orbit domination, not to every
finite scaled comparison.

There is nevertheless an exact capacity bound for every pair of normalized
seeds and every convex mixture of such orbit channels.  In tau norm,

  ||rho-I/8||_tau^2 = 7/64,
  sum_(u=s,a,e) |q_(u,e)(alpha)|^2 <= 7/64.

Consequently, by Cauchy-Schwarz,

  |Tr M_8| = 8 |sum_(u,e) q_(u,e)(beta)q_(u,e)(alpha)| <= 7/8.

Convex mixtures preserve Tr M_8<=7/8.  Since the legal symmetric-extension
marginal Phi_P27 has Tr M_8=92/75>7/8, no normalized covariant orbit
measure-and-prepare channel, nor convex mixture of these orbit designs, can
Loewner-dominate Phi_P27 on the full adjoint multiplicity block with unit
coefficient.  If one permits a scalar c in Phi_P27|_8 <= c M_8, this trace
argument only forces c >= (92/75)/(7/8)=736/525; it does not rule out a
finite scaled comparison.  It also says nothing by itself about a squared
comparison.

5. Reproducibility and source boundary
--------------------------------------

`multiplicity_blocks_check.py` constructs the Gell-Mann f,d tensors exactly
in SymPy; forms P_1, P_8s, P_8a, P_27, and P_(10+10bar); verifies ranks,
orthogonal projector identities, completeness, and one-body partial traces;
then computes the symmetrized-compression marginal Phi_P exactly and checks
the full 16-by-16 adjoint transfer block, including the vanishing cross-
label entries, for P_8s and P_27.  It also evaluates the orbit-seed Gram
matrices above.
Run from the repository root with:

  python3 outputs/research/classicality/cycle4/multiplicity_blocks_check.py

The tensor-product multiplicities are derived with the elementary SU(3)
fundamental LR rule and dimension checks shown above.  The transfer and
moment calculations are exact symbolic contractions, not floating-point
fits.  No external Clebsch tables or literature formulas are imported.  The
unexpanded R matrices in the extension SDP remain a concrete coefficient-
table gap; the image characterization and the explicit P_27 witness are
already exact.

External cross-check boundary: Slansky, “Group theory for unified model
building,” Table 24 (pp. 91–92), lists the SU(3) fundamental and 8 tensor
products, including 8 tensor 8 = 1+8_s+8_a+10+10bar+27.  It is used only as
a cross-check of the representation labels; the extension cone, channel
contractions, and orbit bound above are derived here.  [Source PDF](https://www.iitg.ac.in/cet/gian/2017/ppoulose/gian_ksb/Slansky_GT.pdf).

6. Extension to every full-domain EB channel: exact order and limits
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The orbit-mixture obstruction extends to arbitrary EB channels on the full
M_8 domain, provided the comparison is an order of superoperators on the
normalized-trace Hilbert space.  It does not assert an order in the CP-map
cone and it does not, by itself, give an experiment-level reconstruction
lower bound.

Let U_g be the irreducible adjoint representation on V=C^8, let
Ad_g(X)=U_g X U_g^dagger on M_8, and use
<X,Y>_tau=Tr(X^dagger Y)/8.  The relevant block is the full 16-dimensional
isotypic subspace

  W_8 = W_8s direct-sum W_8a
      = span_C{B_s,e,B_a,e : e=1,...,8}.

Each of W_8s, W_8a, and W_8 is invariant under the SU(3) conjugation action,
since each is an isotypic component (or sum of components).  Therefore
Haar-twirling preserves a superoperator-order hypothesis restricted to any
of these subspaces, as well as one imposed on all of L^2(tau).

The target Phi_P27 is SU(3)-covariant, bistochastic, and self-adjoint on
this Hilbert space; its transfer matrix on W_8 is
M^Phi_8=diag(14/25,2/3) tensor I_8.  In particular
Phi_P27(B_a,e)=(2/3)B_a,e.

For an arbitrary full-domain EB CPTP map E, define its group twirl by

  T(E) = integral Ad_g^(-1) o E o Ad_g dg.

Pre/post-composition by unitaries and convex mixing preserve EB and CPTP,
so T(E) is EB and CPTP.  It is SU(3)-covariant.  It is also unital:
T(E)(I) commutes with the irreducible U_g and has trace 8, hence equals I.
Thus T(E) is bistochastic.  If E is bistochastic, E^dagger is also a
bistochastic EB channel: writing E(X)=sum_j Tr(F_j X) sigma_j, where
{F_j} is a POVM and sigma_j are states, bistochasticity gives
sum_j Tr(F_j) sigma_j=I.  Then

  E^dagger(X)=sum_j Tr((Tr F_j)sigma_j X) F_j/(Tr F_j)

is a measure-and-prepare channel (zero-trace terms are omitted).  Therefore

  E_sym = (T(E)+T(E)^dagger)/2

is again an EB bistochastic covariant channel and is self-adjoint on
L^2(tau).  This optional symmetrization handles a comparison formulated
only for the symmetric part of a nonsymmetric candidate.

Every covariant EB channel is a convex mixture of the normalized orbit
channels from §4.  To see this, write its normalized Choi state as a
separable mixture of product states and apply the group twirl to each
product.  A twirled product of seed states alpha,beta gives

  E_(alpha,beta)(X)=8 integral Tr(rho_(alpha,g)X) rho_(beta,g) dg,

which is trace-preserving and unital because each orbit average is I/8.
Mixed seeds can be spectrally decomposed, so pure seed pairs suffice.

There is a sharper exact bound for the antisymmetric adjoint copy.  For a
pure seed |psi>=sum_a psi_a|a>, put t_a=lambda_a/2 and
A=sum_a psi_a t_a.  Then Tr(A^dagger A)=1/2 and, with the conventions in
§4,

  q_(a,e)(psi) = i/sqrt(24) sum_(b,c) f_bce conjugate(psi_b) psi_c,
  [A^dagger,A] = i sum_e (sum_(b,c) f_bce conjugate(psi_b)psi_c) t_e.

Since Tr(t_e t_f)=delta_ef/2,

  sum_e |q_(a,e)(psi)|^2
    = Tr([A^dagger,A]^2)/12.

Set R=A^dagger A and S=AA^dagger.  They are positive, have the same
singular-value squares, and Tr R=Tr S=1/2.  Thus

  Tr([A^dagger,A]^2)=Tr((R-S)^2)
       =2Tr(R^2)-2Tr(RS)
       <=2(Tr R)^2=1/2,

so sum_e |q_(a,e)(psi)|^2<=1/24.  This is sharp: the seed
psi=(|1>+i|2>)/sqrt(2) gives A=|1><2|/sqrt(2), with R and S orthogonal
rank-one operators.  Mixed seeds obey the same bound by convexity of the
norm square, and arbitrary input/output seeds obey the corresponding
Cauchy-Schwarz bound.

Consequently every normalized orbit EB channel satisfies

  M_(a,a)=8 sum_e q_(a,e)(beta)q_(a,e)(alpha) <= 1/3,

and so does every convex mixture and every full-domain EB channel after
Haar twirling.  This improves the earlier trace bound for the present
comparison.  It gives the following exact statements.

* Direct superoperator-order obstruction: if E is any full-domain EB
  channel and E-Phi_P27 is positive semidefinite as an operator on
  L^2(tau), then twirling preserves the order because Phi_P27 is
  covariant.  Compression to W_8a would require M_(a,a)>=2/3, contradicting
  M_(a,a)<=1/3.  The same contradiction applies if the hypothesized order
  is explicitly imposed only on the symmetric part of the transfer
  operator, after replacing T(E) by E_sym.  Thus no arbitrary EB channel
  dominates Phi_P27 in this specified superoperator order.

* Sharp adjoint-block deficit factor: if an arbitrary EB channel E is
  claimed to satisfy

    I - E <= C (I - Phi_P27)

  in L^2(tau) superoperator order on the full domain, or just on W_8, then
  twirling (and, if the comparison means symmetric parts, taking E_sym)
  preserves the inequality.  Testing B_a,e gives

    1-M_(a,a) <= C(1-2/3)=C/3.

  Since M_(a,a)<=1/3, this forces C>=2.  The complex orbit seed in §4
  has M_8=diag(1/5,1/3), and hence satisfies the inequality on W_8 at
  C=2: its two deficits are 4/5 and 2/3, while the right side has entries
  22/25 and 2/3.  Therefore 2 is sharp for this adjoint block.  This does
  not establish that the same seed satisfies the operator inequality on
  all other SU(3) sectors of M_8.

These are channel-operator statements, not lower bounds on
e_EB(S)=inf_(E in EB) sup_(rho in S) (1/2)||E(rho)-rho||_1 for an experiment
S.  The twirl need not preserve performance on a non-SU(3)-invariant state
family, and failure of a sufficient L^2 operator comparison does not
imply a trace-distance lower bound.  A separate invariant experiment
family and a direct trace-norm argument would be required for an e_EB
claim.
