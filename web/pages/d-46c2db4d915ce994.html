1. All-KMS Lyapunov representation: every direction checked

X sigma^(1/2), d=sigma^(1/4), s=sqrt(sigma), and H=Gamma^(1/2)L Gamma^(-1/2).
The CP sandwich transport gives exp(−tH)(X)=d exp(−tL)(d^(-1)Xd^(-1)) d,
which is CP. KMS symmetry Gamma L=L_* Gamma implies H is HS-self-adjoint;
L(I)=0 implies H(s)=0. There is no implicit modular-covariance assumption.

For any HS-self-adjoint CP map S, average a Kraus representation and its
HS adjoint and split each Kraus into Hermitian real/imaginary parts. This
gives Hermitian Kraus operators. In a fixed Hermitian HS-orthonormal basis
F0=I/sqrt(m), Fa traceless for a>0, their expansion coefficients are real.
Hence S_t(X)=sum_ab C_ab(t)Fa X Fb with real symmetric PSD C(t). The maps
X->Fa X Fb form a complex superoperator basis (vectorization gives
Fb^T tensor Fa), so C(t) is unique and analytic because exp(−tH) is analytic.
At t=0 only C00=m is nonzero. Positivity for t>=0 makes the traceless block
of C'(0) PSD. Diagonalizing it yields traceless Hermitian Bj, and collecting
the identity/traceless cross terms gives exactly
 H(X)=(VX+XV)/2−sum_j Bj X Bj.
The signs and normalization factors in the origin construction check out:
gamma=C'00/m, W=sum_a C'0a Fa/sqrt(m), V=−gamma I−2W.
The stationarity equation H(s)=0 is ell_s(V)=sum Bj s Bj, where
ell_s(V)=(sV+Vs)/2. Since s>0, ell_s inverse multiplies entry ij by
2/(s_i+s_j), and gives

 H=sum_j H_(Bj).

Converse legality also passes: V_B is Hermitian; the drift sandwich and the
noise CP Dyson expansion give a CP semigroup exp(−tH_B). It fixes s, so
transport gives a unital CP semigroup. HS self-adjointness gives KMS symmetry
and transport gives stationarity of sigma. Similarity to a unital CP semigroup
whose norm/spectral radius is at most1 implies H_B>=0. Identity shifts cancel
