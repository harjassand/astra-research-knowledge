## Intrinsic operator and projector lemma

At either degree D=29,30 let Mtilde:QQ^R->QQ^m be the observed coefficient/evaluation operator with SOURCES AS COLUMNS, and let G=Mtilde Mtilde^T. After input denominator clearing and the64P normalization, G is an integer symmetric PSD operator. Let a be the integer probe, b=Ga. If b=0, return z=a. Otherwise acquire an integer polynomial q of degree<=m with q(0)!=0 and verify the exact identity

    q(G)b=0.                                             (1)

Then

    z=q(G)a/q(0)                                         (2)

is the UNIQUE orthogonal projection of a onto ker G=ker Mtilde^T. Indeed Gz=q(G)b/q(0)=0. Also q(X)-q(0)=Xh(X), so a-z=-G h(G)a/q(0) belongs to im G. Orthogonal direct decomposition of the symmetric operator proves the claim. Directly verify Mtilde^T z=0 before forming the dual target row. This lemma needs neither a full matrix minimal polynomial nor a rank certificate, known cyclicity, eigenspace dimension, floating approximation or latent aligned metric. Any q satisfying (1) and q(0)!=0 is sufficient.

## Integer height bound and bad primes

Let a0>=1 be an acquired upper bound on the absolute entries of Mtilde (coefficient templates or streamed evaluations). Let A=max(2,R*a0²), so every entry of G is at most A. Let L>=1 bound the absolute probe entries and put kappa=1+log2(mA)+log2(L). Source/target fixed-degree evaluation/template bounds in the companion note compute a0 from the expanded f and sampled integer coordinates; no latent height or condition number is supplied.

The relative minimal polynomial q_Q of the Krylov sequence b,Gb,... over QQ is monic integral: it divides the monic integer characteristic polynomial of the integer matrix G, and Gauss's lemma applies. Since b is in im G and G is symmetric PSD, every root of q_Q is a distinct positive eigenvalue of G. Each is at most ||G||2<=mA. Thus every integer coefficient has absolute value at most

    H=(1+mA)^m.                                          (3)

The degree d<=m and q_Q(0)!=0. This is a proof-only polynomial; neither G nor its characteristic polynomial is expanded or supplied.

The first d Krylov columns are independent. Some d-by-d integer minor Delta is nonzero. The jth column G^j b has entries bounded by (mA)^(j+1)*L, so Hadamard gives a safe bound |Delta|<=2^K with, for instance,

    K=2 + m*ceil(log2(m)) + m²*ceil(log2(mA))
         + m*ceil(log2(max(2,L))).                        (4)

This is deliberately coarse; K=O(m²*kappa), and calculating the integer logarithms/bounds has polynomial-bit cost. No Krylov columns or minors are stored. If p does not divide Delta, the Krylov rank remains d over Fp. Reduction of q_Q gives a monic relation of degree d, so the finite-field relative minimal polynomial equals q_Q mod p. Bad primes are thus contained in the prime divisors of this ONE nonzero minor; root discriminants need not be guessed or supplied.

Choose

    s=max(16,2H+1,64K,16m),

and independently sample a uniform prime p from [s,s²]. This is a polynomial-BIT range, not a polynomial-value range: log p=O(m*kappa). Liu-Nguyen-Yang arXiv2507.02433v1 §3.5 Theorem5 supplies the prime sampling algorithm with failure<=s^(-2) and O(log^O(1)s) bit operations/O(log s) bits for one prime; its Lemma3.15 gives

    Pr[p divides Delta] <= 2log2|Delta|/s <=1/32.          (5)

Their sampler is a standard prerequisite rather than our construction. The formal low-space learner includes the sampler's bounded failure in its good event and does NOT require a separate arbitrary primality-certification implementation. With a candidate modulus, perform inverses by extended gcd and reject a nonunit; a composite candidate can only be used if the final integer annihilation certificate passes. Such an acceptance remains sound by(1)–(2), regardless of primality. On sampler success the candidate is a uniform prime, so the field proof applies. This avoids hiding an uncharged primality-prover workspace behind the claimed storage bound. A particular library nextprime call/seeded example is not an implementation of the uniform-prime theorem. An optional exact primality test must have its own time/space charged and cannot automatically inherit(6).

## Scalar modular recurrence, centered lift and verification

Independently sample u uniformly in Fp^m and compute the first2m scalar terms

    u^T b, u^T Gb, ..., u^T G^(2m-1)b mod p.

Each step is two implicit source-operator applications; reduce every multiply-add modulo p. Use Berlekamp-Massey with the standard reciprocal/monic normalization to obtain the scalar minimal recurrence q_p. Kaltofen-Saunders1991 §2 Lemma1 bounds failure to see the full relative polynomial by d/p<=m/p<=1/16. On a good prime and good u, q_p=q_Q mod p. Because p>2H, the centered coefficient lift is EXACTLY q_Q, without CRT, reconstruction ambiguity or a guessed denominator. Reject if the lifted degree exceeds m, any coefficient exceeds H, or q(0)=0.

Evaluate q(G)b over the INTEGERS by Horner and require (1). If this fails, retry the modulus/scalar projection. If it passes, evaluate q(G)a, divide by the one integer q(0), reduce the vector's shared denominator, and check Mtilde^T z=0 exactly. An unlucky modulus, finite-field rank or recurrence can never create an invalid accepted projector: exact identity(1) is the algebraic certificate. Conditional on the fixed input/probe, the good event has probability at least

    1-1/32-1/16-1/16² > 3/4.

Thus the inner verified projector is Las Vegas with expected fewer than4/3 trials. Composite candidates are not identified as primes; they are simply checked modular recurrences whose acceptance requires the integer certificate. The special b=0 branch is immediate. Every modular attempt is bounded by2m operator applications and O(m²) scalar modular arithmetic, including bounded extended-gcd inverses; exact Horner/verification adds O(m) operator applications. No guessed rank or matrix cyclicity appears in this count.

## Height, representation, time and storage

All modular vector/coefficient entries use O(log p)=O(m*kappa) bits. Centered candidate coefficients use at mostH, of the same height. In Horner, the kth partial polynomial vector has norm bounded by a sum of at mostm+1 terms of magnitude H*(mA)^m*L. Hence every exact integer vector and its implicit source temporary has O(m*kappa) bits. Dividing by q(0), bounded byH, preserves this order. This avoids the O(m²*kappa) rational intermediate heights of the canonical exact-CG implementation. Single shared denominators/gcd reduction are still used. The polynomial q and at most2m scalar recurrence terms are charged as O(m²*kappa) bits; they are not a hidden m-by-m Krylov matrix.

Use the same coefficient branch when C<=R+N and streaming evaluation branch otherwise.
