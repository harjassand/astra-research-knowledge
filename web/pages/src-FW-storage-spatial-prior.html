# Primary-source comparison: effective storage approximation

15 October 2026. Comparison with `THEOREM.md` and [finite-horizon proof](../storage_horizon/FINITE_HORIZON_STORAGE.md). This is a targeted literature comparison, not a historical novelty or priority determination. No source code was installed or executed for this research.

## Conclusion

The surrounding architecture is established: variational discretization, compact polynomial optimization, heat localization, and effective extrema. The particular operation supplied by the theorem is an explicit, uniform near-minimizer reduction that turns finite-dimensional lower bounds into certified lower bounds for unrestricted storage. The cited results do not directly provide its buffered exterior-subtraction and exact-endpoint-repair certificate.

## Closest variational precedent

Giovanni Fantuzzi and Federico Fuentes, *Global minimization of polynomial integral functionals*, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 46(4), 2024, DOI [10.1137/23M1592584](https://doi.org/10.1137/23M1592584). [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18801v3).

Their bounded finite-element discretization yields compact polynomial optimization problems, followed by sparse moment-SOS relaxation. Assumption 2 covers polynomial growth, coercivity and quasiconvexity; inhomogeneous Dirichlet data are handled by subtracting an extension. Proposition 6 establishes convergence of discrete minima by a Gamma-convergence-type argument. Theorem 12 combines discretization and relaxation limits, with uniqueness needed for its minimizer-convergence conclusion.

The decisive distinction is stated explicitly after Theorem 12: finite SDP values need not be upper or lower bounds on the continuum minimum; they are lower bounds on a discrete upper bound. Thus convergence alone does not furnish the finite discretization gap needed for a certified continuum lower bound. The theorem under comparison supplies that gap explicitly through heat regularization and boundary repair, without identifying a continuum minimizer.

## Effective Galerkin/control precedent

Mickaël D. Chekroun, Axel Kröner and Honghu Liu, *Galerkin approximations of nonlinear optimal control problems in Hilbert spaces*, Electronic Journal of Differential Equations 2017(189), 1–40. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.00427).

The paper distinguishes trajectory-wise approximation from the uniform residual-energy condition A7 used for value convergence. Theorem 2.3 bounds value error by unresolved spectral energy along optimal trajectories for the full and truncated problems. Remark 2.4 emphasizes that the optimizer changes with truncation, so its residual need not vanish merely from convergence for each fixed control.

This is a strong conceptual precedent for requiring uniform control before passing approximation through optimization. Turning its estimates into computable certificates requires effective residual and constant bounds. Its controlled evolution trajectories and optimal-control hypotheses differ from the arbitrary gradient paths in the storage problem. The supplied heat construction creates uniform spectral tails for relevant competitors rather than assuming an original gradient-tail budget.

## Effective compact minimization

Xiaolin Ge and Anil Nerode, *Effective content of the calculus of variations I: Semi-continuity and the chattering lemma*, Annals of Pure and Applied Logic 78 (1996), 127–146. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0168-0072(95)00034-8). [Author-paper full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220394037_Effective_Content_of_the_Calculus_of_Variations_I_Semi-Continuity_and_the_Chattering_Lemma).

Theorem 2.1 computes minimum values for their recursive lower-semicontinuous functions on recursive rectangles. Their definition requires an effectively Hausdorff-approximable bounded epigraph. This is stronger data than ordinary lower semicontinuity. Quasiconvexity and the direct method do not automatically provide that representation for an infinite-dimensional variational problem. Theorem 2.3 separately explains how convexity provides effective continuity under their hypotheses; Morrey quasiconvexity must not be substituted for ordinary convexity in that statement.

Klaus Weihrauch and Xizhong Zheng, *Computability on continuous, lower semi-continuous and upper semi-continuous real functions*, Theoretical Computer Science 234 (2000), 109–133. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(98)00045-0).

This paper studies representations of function spaces and computability of extrema. The final computable polynomial-on-a-box optimization stage belongs to this established effective-analysis setting. A finite grid with a computable continuity modulus certifies the minimum value even when finding a particular exact minimizer is a different problem.

## Heat localization and energy monotonicity

Liangpan Li and Alexander Strohmaier, *Heat kernel estimates for general boundary problems*, Journal of Spectral Theory 6 (2016), 903–919. [Primary paper](https://ems.press/journals/jst/articles/14417).

Their explicit “not feeling the boundary” estimates establish a broad precedent for exponentially small short-time influence across a spatial buffer. The storage construction uses the simpler explicit periodic Gaussian kernel. The general heat-kernel theorem does not itself provide nonlinear-energy exterior cancellation or the exact-trace recovery map.

Giuseppe Bruno and Federico Pasqualotto, *Quasiconvexity for the Dacorogna–Marcellini Energy*, 2026 preprint. [Primary full text, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1).

Lemma 3 uses decreasing relative energy along componentwise heat flow and large-time decay to prove quasiconvexity of a nonconvex quartic gradient energy. It concerns whole-space perturbations, not a storage computation or fixed-boundary approximation theorem. Its energy family also differs from the particular anisotropic Heat18 premise here, so it does not independently certify that premise. Heat-flow monotonicity is additional structure, not a consequence that should be silently inferred from quasiconvexity alone.

## Other global lower-bound approaches

Alexander Chernyavsky, Jason J. Bramburger, Giovanni Fantuzzi and David Goluskin, *Convex relaxations of integral variational problems: pointwise dual relaxation and sum-of-squares optimization*. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03079).

This constructs pointwise-dual/SOS lower bounds and proves sharpness for specified classes. It is a useful alternative source of continuum lower certificates when its sharpness hypotheses apply, rather than a general substitute for spatial error control.

Giovanni Fantuzzi and Ian Tobasco, *Sharpness and non-sharpness of occupation measure bounds for integral variational problems*. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13570).

The paper identifies non-sharp cases, including multidimensional examples where occupation measures omit gradient-compatibility constraints. Consequently, polynomial data alone do not justify assuming a universally sharp occupation-measure or pointwise-dual hierarchy.

Jean B. Lasserre, *Global Optimization with Polynomials and the Problem of Moments*, SIAM Journal on Optimization 11 (2001), 796–817. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1052623400366802).

Moment/positive-polynomial methods are an established route to global polynomial optimization over compact semialgebraic sets. They contextualize the final coefficient-box problem, but do not by themselves quantify its approximation to the original continuum storage problem. The explicit grid proof avoids relying on numerical SDP solver output as an exact certificate.

## Exact operation isolated by the comparison

The following description is analysis of the supplied proofs, not a claim copied from an external source:

1. A computable horizon gap and coercive sublevel estimate bound relevant near-minimizers.
2. Padding places unknown rough data a positive distance from the exterior region.
3. Global periodic heat monotonicity controls total action.
4. Exterior subtraction cancels the full known return action. Only its explicitly bounded smoothing perturbation remains; the return need not have small or positive action.
5. Off-diagonal heat estimates make exact endpoint repair uniformly cheap.
6. Heat-generated Fourier tails yield a computable cutoff without requiring the unknown competitor's original gradient-tail modulus.
7. A compact coefficient box consists of actual endpoint-correct admissible paths and has an explicit two-sided comparison with unrestricted storage.
8. Global grid evaluation with outward enclosures and an explicit polynomial derivative bound produces a terminating interval certificate.

In symbols, the essential analytic comparison is p <= m_K <= p_N + spatial_error, with p_N <= p + horizon_error. Therefore a certified lower bound for m_K must have both errors subtracted before it becomes a lower bound for p. The grid upper bound is already the value of admissible competitors, hence bounds p from above.

This is a uniform one-sided action approximation of relevant near-minimizers, not merely density of smooth functions or qualitative convergence of minima. It does not require strong approximation of every original rough gradient at an effectively chosen scale.

## Scope and cautions

- The theorem certifies unrestricted storage. Its slightly enlarged duration does not automatically certify the unchanged fixed-horizon problem.
- Fixed nonconstant Dirichlet heat flow is not asserted to decrease the energy. Global periodic heat is followed by restriction and explicit repair.
- Finite Fourier endpoints require computable coefficients and certified enclosures. General endpoints require an effective strong critical-tail approximation together with the applicable bounded-ball continuity estimate.
- Finite physical measurements alone supply neither that strong-tail information nor a uniform critical-norm reconstruction guarantee.
- Brute-force termination is computability, not practical complexity. A computable minimum value is also distinct from computing an exact minimizer.
- Qualitative Gamma convergence, density and compactness cannot be used as effective error moduli without additional argument or represented data.
- The finite-horizon truncation theorem and spatial reduction perform separate jobs. The former does not eliminate the latter's necessity.
- The safest historical conclusion is that these primary sources establish much of the surrounding machinery, while their stated theorems do not directly supply this particular buffered exterior-subtraction certificate. Search absence establishes no novelty or priority.
