# Cycle 2: calibrated local Gibbs prediction and laboratory readout

## Result and evidence status

There is a conditional end-to-end finite-system test for a local Gibbs marginal. It combines the N90 classical predictor for a nominal easy-plane XXZ Hamiltonian with (a) a nonzero, explicit Hamiltonian-calibration budget, (b) a state-preparation/thermalization error budget, and (c) finite-sample local measurements. The resulting inequalities compare full local Pauli tomography, a targeted local Pauli measurement, and q-copy cyclic-permutation measurements at equal total system-copy budget.

The calibration and finite-sample inequalities below are proved directly from finite-dimensional trace-distance continuity and Hoeffding's inequality. The *algorithmic realization* of the nominal predictor remains conditional on Chen–Liu's homogeneous coefficient FPRAS and the N90 reduction. N90 is a source-derived, internally audited candidate: its imported FPRAS has not been implemented here or externally certified, and the field-lift/easy-plane extension has no established priority. This is a mathematical-model result; no material system, device, or nature claim is validated.

## Model and calibration interface

On a finite loopless graph with \(n\) spin-½ sites, let the nominal model be

\[
 H^+=\sum_{e=uv}\{\alpha_e(X_uX_v+Y_uY_v)+\gamma_eZ_uZ_v\}
       +\sum_v(b_vX_v+c_vZ_v),
 \qquad \alpha_e\ge |\gamma_e|,\quad b_v\ge0,
\]

with rational coefficients, arbitrary rational \(c_v\), and physical Hamiltonian \(H_0=-H^+\). Write \(\rho_0=e^{-\hat\beta H_0}/Z_0=e^{\hat\beta H^+}/Z_0\), and

\[
 C=3\sum_e\alpha_e+\sum_v(b_v+|c_v|),\qquad \|H_0\|\le C.
\]

For a specified \(k\)-site set \(S\), N90 conditionally returns a classical density matrix estimate \(\widehat\rho_{0,S}\) with
\[
 \Pr[\|\widehat\rho_{0,S}-\rho_{0,S}\|_1\le\epsilon_M]\ge1-\delta_M,
\]
in time polynomial in input bit length, \(n\), numerical \(\hat\beta C\), 4^k, \(1/\epsilon_M\), and \(\log(1/\delta_M)\). It does not prepare a global quantum Gibbs state.

Let the actual Hamiltonian and inverse temperature be H_*=H_0+E and \(\beta_*\), with actual Gibbs state \(\rho_*=e^{-\beta_*H_*}/Z_*\). Take a nonzero Hamiltonian error bound \(\|E\|\le\eta_H\) and a temperature error bound \(|\beta_*-\hat\beta|\le\eta_\beta\). Then the exponent mismatch is bounded by
\[
 \| -\beta_*H_*+\hat\beta H_0\|
 \le \beta_*\eta_H+\eta_\beta C=:\kappa.
\]
For coefficient-wise intervals in this model, plus an omitted-interaction/remainder term \(R\), one safe bound is
\[
 \|E\|\le 2\sum_e|\delta\alpha_e|+\sum_e|\delta\gamma_e|
       +\sum_v(|\delta b_v|+|\delta c_v|)+\|R\|.
\]
If all edge and site coefficient intervals have radius \(u\), this gives \(\|E\|\le(3|E(G)|+2n)u+\|R\|\). For maximum degree \(\Delta_G\), the coefficient term is at most \((1.5\Delta_G+2)nu\). Thus a fixed global exponent budget generally requires coefficient precision on the order of \(1/(\beta n)\) under this argument.

The laboratory source is assumed to return independent, identically distributed copies of one state \(\tau\) with
\[
 T(\tau,\rho_*)=\tfrac12\|\tau-\rho_*\|_1\le\epsilon_{\rm prep}.
\]
This assumption charges a per-copy preparation error but does not assert that a device can meet it, or give a thermalization time. Calibration intervals, omitted interactions, and preparation error must be established separately.

## Robustness lemma and end-to-end bounds

For finite Hermitian \(K,L\), their normalized Gibbs states obey
\[
 T(\rho_K,\rho_L)\le\tanh(\|K-L\|/2).
\]
One proof writes the Jeffreys divergence as
\(J=\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho_K-\rho_L)(K-L)]\le2\kappa T\), measures the positive spectral subspace of \(\rho_K-\rho_L\), and applies relative-entropy data processing to obtain \(J\ge4T\operatorname{artanh}T\). Consequently, for every subsystem \(S\),
\[
 \|\tau_S-\rho_{0,S}\|_1\le2\{\epsilon_{\rm prep}+\tanh(\kappa/2)\}=:\Delta_S.
\]
The continuity bound is dimension independent but depends on a *global* exponent norm.

**Local-observable experiment.** Fix a Hermitian \(O_S\) with \(\|O_S\|_\infty\le1\); for a Pauli string, measure that local Pauli basis on each of \(N\) independent copies. The outcomes lie in \([-1,1]\). With probability at least \(1-\delta_E\),
\[
 |\bar O-\operatorname{Tr}(O_S\widehat\rho_{0,S})|
 \le \sqrt{2\log(2/\delta_E)/N}
       +\epsilon_M+\Delta_S.
\]
The failure probability is at most \(\delta_E+\delta_M\). Therefore an observed residual above this radius rejects at least one of the stated predictor, calibration, preparation, or sampling premises. For a norm-\(L\) observable, multiply the right side by \(L\). A single pre-registered Pauli requires
\[
 N\ge 2\eta^{-2}\log(2/\delta_E)
\]
to estimate its expectation to additive \(\eta\); there is no reason to pay for full tomography if that one scalar is the target.

**Full local Pauli tomography.** Set \(d=2^k\), \(M=4^k-1\), and measure each nonidentity \(k\)-qubit Pauli string \(m\) times. A union bound and Pauli orthogonality give a trace-one Hermitian linear estimate \(A_S\) satisfying
\[
 \|A_S-\tau_S\|_1\le e_T,
 \qquad e_T=\sqrt{\frac{2M\log(2M/\delta_E)}{m}},
\]
with probability at least \(1-\delta_E\). If \(D=\|A_S-\widehat\rho_{0,S}\|_1\), then \(D\le e_T+\epsilon_M+\Delta_S\) on the joint good event. Conversely, the reverse triangle inequality makes \(D\pm(e_T+\epsilon_M)\) a confidence interval for \(\|\tau_S-\rho_{0,S}\|_1\). Positivity projection and trace normalization can turn \(A_S\) into a density matrix at a conservative extra factor of four in its trace-norm error, as in the N90 RDM reconstruction.

**q-copy local purity experiment.** For integer q≥2, let U_{q,S} cyclically permute the q replicas on S, and measure the Hermitian observable \(W_{q,S}=(U_{q,S}+U_{q,S}^\dagger)/2\). Its norm is at most one and
\[
 \mathbb E W_{q,S}=p_q(\tau_S):=\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_S^q).
\]
For \(q=2\), \(W\) is the ordinary two-copy SWAP. For \(q>2\), a controlled cyclic permutation with an ancilla Hadamard test gives a \(\pm1\) outcome with this expectation. If (r\) independent measurement groups are run, each using q separately prepared copies, then
\[
 \Pr[|\widehat p_q-p_q(\tau_S)|>a]\le2e^{-ra^2/2},\qquad
 a=\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta_E)/r}.
\]
For density matrices \(A,B\), telescoping powers gives
\( |\operatorname{Tr}(A^q)-\operatorname{Tr}(B^q)|\le q\|A-B\|_1\). Thus, comparing the measured value with the N90 RDM prediction \(\widehat p_{q,0}=\operatorname{Tr}(\widehat\rho_{0,S}^q)\),
\[
 |\widehat p_q-\widehat p_{q,0}|
 \le a+q\epsilon_M+2q\{\epsilon_{\rm prep}+\tanh(\kappa/2)\}
\]
with failure probability at most \(\delta_E+\delta_M\).

N97 offers a conditional model-side alternative: its relative-counting route estimates the nominal \(p_q(\rho_{0,S})\) to relative error \(\epsilon_R\), with no inverse-purity factor in its classical running time. If its estimate is \(\widetilde p_{q,0}\), then the data/model comparison radius can replace \(q\epsilon_M\) above by \(\epsilon_R\widetilde p_{q,0}/(1-\epsilon_R)\). This improves only the *model-side calculation*. It does not remove physical swap-shot variance.

## Equal-copy-budget comparison

For a total budget \(B\) of prepared whole-system copies and failure level \(\delta\):

| Route | Output | Statistical confidence radius using B copies |
|---|---|---|
| One targeted Pauli | One local expectation | \(\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)/B}\) |
| Uniform full Pauli tomography | All \(4^k-1\) Pauli means and an RDM | With \(m=\lfloor B/M\rfloor\): \(e_T(B)=\sqrt{2M\log(2M/\delta)/m}\) in trace norm |
| q-copy cyclic permutation | One \(q\)-purity | With \(r=\lfloor B/q\rfloor\): \(e_q(B)=\sqrt{2\log(2/\delta)/r}\) |

The estimates answer different questions. For additive error \(\eta\), this specified full-tomography protocol uses at most the sufficient bound \(B\ge2M^2\eta^{-2}\log(2M/\delta)\) for raw trace-norm radius \(\eta\). A direct q-copy measurement needs \(B\ge2q\eta^{-2}\log(2/\delta)\) for the one scalar \(p_q\). If purity is computed from a positivity-projected tomographic RDM, the conservative error is \(4q e_T(B)\), so the sufficient absolute-error budget is \(B\ge32q^2M^2\eta^{-2}\log(2M/\delta)\). These are bounds for the protocols specified here, not minimax lower bounds; optimized tomography, randomized measurements, or a targeted Pauli setting can change constants and rates.

For relative error \(\epsilon\) in the physical purity, the q-copy Hoeffding bound gives
\[
 B_q\ge \frac{2q\log(2/\delta)}{\epsilon^2p_q(\tau_S)^2}.
\]
Since \(p_q\ge d^{1-q}\), a worst-case sufficient budget is
\[
 B_q\ge 2q\,d^{2(q-1)}\epsilon^{-2}\log(2/\delta).
\]
The latter is an upper bound on the copies sufficient for the specified estimator, not a sample-complexity lower bound. Using projected full tomography to obtain the same worst-case relative guarantee gives the cruder sufficient bound
\[
 B_{\rm tomo}\ge 32q^2M^2d^{2(q-1)}\epsilon^{-2}\log(2M/\delta).
\]
Hence direct cyclic measurement has a much smaller stated guarantee for the *single purity scalar*; tomography supplies the full RDM and supports arbitrary local observables. N97's relative classical estimator has no inverse-purity factor, but no classical calculation substitutes for experimental copies in the direct measurement.

## Concrete finite test fixture

For a reproducible small-system check, take a four-site path with \(\alpha=1\), \(\gamma=1/2\) on all three edges, \(b_v=1/4\), \(c=(1/10,-3/20,1/5,-1/10)\), and \(\hat\beta=7/10\). These rational inputs satisfy the N90 cone; \(C=10.55\). For \(S=\{1,2\}\), dense double-precision diagonalization of \(H^+\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(X_1X_2\rho_{0,S})\approx0.47024547,
 \qquad \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{0,S}^2)\approx0.39719966.
\]
The calculation is a finite model benchmark, not an experiment or an independent validation of N90.

Take a nonzero Hamiltonian allowance \(\|E\|\le0.01\), exact inverse temperature for this fixture, \(\epsilon_{\rm prep}=0.003\), model tolerance \(\epsilon_M=0.002\), and \(\delta_M=\delta_E=0.01\). Then \(\kappa\le0.007\), \(2\tanh(\kappa/2)\approx0.00699997\), and the preparation-plus-calibration contribution to a Pauli mean is at most \(0.013\). With \(B=211{,}934\) whole-system copies and \(\delta_E=0.01\):

| Protocol | Allocation/output | Sampling bound | Total comparison radius around the predictor (including \(\epsilon_M\), calibration, and preparation) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Target \(X_1X_2\) | All \(B\) copies; one local setting | 0.0071 | 0.0221 |
| Full two-qubit tomography | \(M=15\), \(m=14{,}128\) per Pauli; all local means | Per-Pauli 0.0337; RDM trace norm 0.1304 | RDM 0.1454 |
| \(q=2\) SWAP | \(r=105{,}967\) two-copy groups; \(B=2r\) | Purity 0.0100 | Purity 0.0400 |

The corresponding model-centered predictions are approximately 0.47025 for \(X_1X_2\) and 0.39720 for purity. The bounds are simultaneous only for each separately run protocol with its assigned failure probability; several tests require a further union-bound allocation. The full tomography line is deliberately uniform across settings. A targeted \(X_1X_2\) measurement beats that allocation on this one expectation, while the SWAP line measures a different scalar. This is why “equal budget” cannot support a uniform winner independent of output target.

## Cost ledger, failure modes, and nature boundary

The N90 CPU cost is polynomial in the finite input size and the numerical value \(\hat\beta C\), not just its logarithm, and in (4^k,1/\epsilon_M,\log(1/\delta_M)\). Hamiltonian coefficient acquisition is an input/calibration expense; the counting algorithm does not learn the coefficients from the material. A direct Pauli experiment consumes one independently prepared system per shot. q-copy estimation consumes \(q r\) whole-system copies, plus controlled cyclic-permutation/ancilla hardware and readout. If a sequential preparation takes time \(t_{\rm prep}\), the copy contribution is \(Bt_{\rm prep}\); parallel replicas reduce wall-clock only with \(q\) source channels. Energy and gate costs can be recorded symbolically as \(B E_{\rm prep}\) and an architecture-specific permutation-gadget cost. No finite bound on \(t_{\rm prep}\), energy, gate error, or useful multi-copy coupling follows from N90/N97. N90's computational-basis Gibbs sampler is not a quantum-state preparation routine.

The main load-bearing robustness gap is extensive calibration. If the per-term interval radius \(u>0\) is held fixed on a bounded-degree graph, the proved bound has \(\kappa=O(\beta n u)\) and the resulting global continuity estimate becomes uninformative as \(n\) grows. The true local marginal might be more stable under locality, temperature, or clustering hypotheses, but no such theorem has been established here. N52's reference-weighted L2 continuity theorem does not close this gap: it needs a separate uniform local-to-central L2 approximation, which its source explicitly leaves open.

The discriminating finite experiment is: fix \(S\), \(X_1X_2\), and optionally \(q=2\) before collecting data; acquire coefficient intervals and a nonzero global \(\|E\|\) bound; record the independently justified preparation error; compare the measured means with the finite predictor using the displayed radii. A residual outside the interval falsifies at least one premise. Passing is only consistency on those observables. It does not establish that a material is exactly described by this Hamiltonian family. For a nature claim one must additionally acquire a defensible spin-model mapping, temperature, omitted-term bound, identical-copy preparation, local readout and replica coupling for purity. None was done here.

Prior-art boundary: the N90 predictor imports Chen–Liu's homogeneous log-concave counting FPRAS (Theorem 5.1 and rational Remark A.4); the N90 reduction and field lift remain conditional. The q-copy identity and SWAP/Rényi measurement are established mechanisms, including Hastings et al. (2010) and Abanin–Demler (2012). Local Pauli estimation is standard. This cycle claims no novelty for any of those mechanisms; the contribution here is the explicit finite calibration/preparation/sampling ledger and equal-copy-budget comparison for the N90 model interface. Priority is unknown.
