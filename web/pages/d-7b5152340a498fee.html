# Tracer-only conductivity non-identifiability: exact model proof

## Claim and scope

Fix dimension \(d\geq 1\), lattice spacing \(a_0>0\), inverse temperature
\(\beta=(k_B T)^{-1}\), and \(M\) independent labelled cation–anion pairs
in volume \(V\). The charges in each pair are \((+e,-e)\). For each pair use
coordinates \(u,v\in(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^d\), with odd \(L\), and physical
positions
\[
  x_+=u+v,\qquad x_-=u-v\pmod L.
\]
The odd-period condition makes this a bijection between pair coordinates and
the two physical ion positions. Lift paths to the lattice cover when measuring
displacements. In each coordinate and for each sign, let the pair make
transitions
\[
  u\mapsto u\pm1\quad\text{at rate }a,\qquad
  v\mapsto v\pm1\quad\text{at rate }b,
\]
where \(a,b>0\). Different coordinates and pairs use independent clocks.

At zero field, this is an irreducible, translation-invariant, bounded-jump,
reversible continuous-time jump process with the uniform equilibrium law. Its
equilibrium law does not depend on \(a,b\). The jumps move each ion by only one
lattice spacing, but the two-ion concerted event is allowed regardless of the
ions' separation; this is a kinetic toy model rather than a short-range
interacting electrolyte. For each individual ion, each
coordinate has exactly the same full path law for every split with fixed
\(a+b=\lambda\): a continuous-time symmetric nearest-neighbour walk with
right and left rates \(\lambda\). Thus every one-ion path marginal, not merely
its long-time diffusivity, is fixed by \(\lambda\).

Nevertheless, the conductivity changes with the split. Writing
\(D=a_0^2(a+b)\), the self-diffusion coefficient of either ion is \(D\),
while the cation–anion displacement cross coefficient is
\[
  C_{+-}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2t}
       \mathbb E[\Delta x_{+,k}(t)\Delta x_{-,k}(t)]
       =a_0^2(a-b)
\]
for each coordinate \(k\). Hence the charge-diffusion coefficient of one pair
is
\[
  D_{\rm ch}=D_++D_--2C_{+-}=4a_0^2b.
\]
With number density \(\nu=M/V\), the Einstein–Helfand conductivity and the
Nernst–Einstein value are respectively
\[
  \sigma=4\beta e^2 a_0^2 b\nu,\qquad
  \sigma_{\rm NE}=2\beta e^2 a_0^2(a+b)\nu.
\]
Therefore
\[
  \frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\rm NE}}=\frac{2b}{a+b}.
\]
Holding \(a+b\) fixed holds the equilibrium distribution, every individual
ion's complete path marginal, and \(\sigma_{\rm NE}\) fixed, while the true
conductivity ranges over \((0,2\sigma_{\rm NE})\) as \(a,b>0\) vary. The
closure \([0,2\sigma_{\rm NE}]\) is approached at the two degenerate endpoints.

## Proof

For a \(u\)-transition, both ions move by the same signed step. For a \(v\)-
transition, they move by opposite signed steps. The superposition seen by either
ion therefore has, in each coordinate, total rate \(a+b\) in each direction.
Poisson superposition gives the same entire single-ion path law for every
parameter split with fixed sum. The uniform law on \((u,v)\) is invariant and
reversible because every transition has an equal-rate reverse; the coordinate
map is bijective, so the same is true of the physical pair distribution.

In time \(t\), the expected number of positive and negative \(u\)-jumps in one
coordinate is \(at\) each, and of positive and negative \(v\)-jumps is \(bt\)
each. Same-sign \(u\)-jumps contribute positively to the cation–anion
displacement product; opposite-sign \(v\)-jumps contribute negatively. Thus
\(\mathbb E[\Delta x_+\Delta x_-]=2a_0^2(a-b)t\), proving the formula for
\(C_{+-}\). Each individual variance is
\(2a_0^2(a+b)t\), so \(D_+=D_-=a_0^2(a+b)\).

The pair's charge-weighted displacement is
\(e(\Delta x_+-\Delta x_-)=2e\Delta v\). Its variance in each coordinate is
\(8e^2a_0^2bt\). Summing over the \(d\) coordinates and \(M\) independent
pairs and applying
\[
  \sigma=\frac{\beta}{2dVt}
     \mathbb E\!\left[\left|e\sum_{p=1}^M
       (\Delta x_{p,+}-\Delta x_{p,-})\right|^2\right]
\]
gives \(\sigma=4\beta e^2a_0^2bM/V\). Substitution of the two self
diffusivities in
\(\sigma_{\rm NE}=\beta V^{-1}\sum_i q_i^2D_i\) gives
\(\sigma_{\rm NE}=2\beta e^2a_0^2(a+b)M/V\), and the ratio follows.

The process also obeys the usual local-detailed-balance response to a uniform
field \(E\) along one coordinate: \(u\)-jumps carry zero charge displacement,
while forward/backward \(v\)-jumps carry \(\pm2ea_0\). Bias their rates to
\(b\exp(\pm\beta ea_0E)\), whose ratio is
\(\exp(\pm2\beta ea_0E)\). The linear-response current gives the same
conductivity. This check is only a model consistency check; the zero-field
Einstein–Helfand derivation above is sufficient.

## General covariance envelope (separate from the jump construction)

For one cation and one anion in an equilibrium process where the long-time
limits exist, define the isotropic scalar self coefficients
\[
  D_\pm=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E[|\Delta x_\pm(t)|^2]}{2dt}
\]
and the symmetric cross coefficient
\[
  C=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2dt}
    \mathbb E[\Delta x_+(t)\cdot\Delta x_-(t)].
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz applied before taking the limit yields
\(|C|\leq\sqrt{D_+D_-}\). Consequently
\[
  (\sqrt{D_+}-\sqrt{D_-})^2
  \leq D_++D_--2C
  \leq(\sqrt{D_+}+\sqrt{D_-})^2.
\]
The covariance inequality is exact as a statement about second moments. A
constant-coefficient correlated Brownian diffusion on a two-particle torus
with diffusion block matrix
\(\begin{psmallmatrix}D_+I&CI\\CI&D_-I\end{psmallmatrix}\),
\(|C|\leq\sqrt{D_+D_-}\), is reversible under the uniform equilibrium law
and attains the entire interval while keeping each one-particle Brownian path
marginal fixed. This is a continuum diffusion model; it is not a claim that
every lattice electrolyte realizes every endpoint.

## Interface, assumptions, and what it does not prove

**Supplied:** exact one-ion equilibrium path marginals, charges, temperature,
volume, and density. These determine \(D_+,D_-\) and the NE estimate.

**Not supplied by those data:** synchronized cation–anion displacement
correlations or the total charge-current autocorrelation. Those determine the
off-diagonal terms in the exact conductivity.

The construction is a mathematical witness in a reversible, dilute,
noninteracting-pair jump model with concerted pair events. It has no Coulomb
interaction, exclusion, realistic lattice chemistry, electrode interface,
disorder, or fitted
activation barriers. It proves a model-class non-identifiability statement,
not a prediction that a particular battery electrolyte has a given Haven
ratio. It does not establish novelty or priority over the established
Green–Kubo/Haven-ratio literature.

## Measurement contract and decisive test

For a named material, inputs would need to be measured at the same temperature,
stoichiometry, phase, and sample state: \(D_i\) (for example PFG-NMR or
validated molecular dynamics), number densities, charges, and either
synchronized species-resolved cross displacement coefficients or the full
charge-current correlation. The strongest comparator is the exact collective
Einstein–Helfand/Green–Kubo value, checked against low-field impedance after
geometry, grain-boundary, electrode-polarization, and electronic-leakage
corrections. The NE result is the information-matched self-diffusion baseline;
cluster Nernst–Einstein is an additional approximation that must charge cluster
definition, lifetime, and independent-cluster assumptions.

The model predicts \(\sigma/\sigma_{\rm NE}=2b/(a+b)\), with
\(D=a_0^2(a+b)\) and \(C=a_0^2(a-b)\). One can infer the two mode rates from
\(a=(D+C)/(2a_0^2)\), \(b=(D-C)/(2a_0^2)\). A material trajectory with
non-Poisson single-ion jumps, non-negligible inter-pair interactions, or a
measured collective conductivity inconsistent with this relation rejects the
toy mechanism. For real-material relevance, a necessary next test is an
independent trajectory/impedance comparison where the cross term is estimated
with block uncertainty and the longest correlation times and finite-size
dependence are reported.
