## Primary source: macrophage immune-state heterogeneity

Redondo et al., “Heritable single-cell gene expression states shape functional variability in innate immune responses,” bioRxiv preprint v1, 2026-06-19, DOI [10.64898/2026.06.17.732820](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.17.732820). The PMC landing page states that this is a preprint and has not been peer reviewed: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13307960/>.

The preprint reports RNA-seq fluctuation assays in immortalised murine bone-marrow-derived macrophages, protein imaging of IL1β/CD36 across clones and mixed populations, and infection assays with *Listeria monocytogenes*. It reports >25-division RNA state persistence for a subset of TLR4-dependent genes; density-reinforced CD36 and transient IL1β; clone-size/local-neighbourhood associations; and a two-state branching-process model with constant, size-dependent, and clone-heterogeneous transition rates. These are source-reported findings, not independent validation by this lane.

Exact model interface given in the Methods: each cell changes OFF→ON at `k_on` and ON→OFF at `k_off`, and proliferates at shared rate `lambda`; `f_ON=k_on/(k_on+k_off)` and sister-pair odds ratio `OR=(k_on+lambda)(k_off+lambda)/(k_on*k_off)` are used to infer switching rates. The size-dependent model uses a Hill function for `k_on(N)`; its third model samples clone-level `k_on,max` from a Beta distribution. This lane does not certify that the experimental proximity-pairs are true tracked sisters or that clone size equals local density.

Methods specify the pair acquisition as scanning Euclidean distances between cell nuclear centroids in an endpoint image: a pair is two nuclei within 30 pixels, at least 120 pixels away from the nearest third cell; pixel size is 0.598 μm. The method does not report lineage tracking for these pairs. Therefore “sister pair” rate inference relies on a proximity proxy; the rate bias under independent-neighbor contamination is derived in `proof/proximity_pair_rate_bias.md`. This is a measurement assumption, not proof of misclassification.

## Primary source: T-cell receptor dynamic signalling (limited access)

Dunlock & Regot, “Antigen affinity modulates ERK pulsing frequency during T cell activation,” *Science Signaling* 19(920), eadw3709 (2026-01-13), DOI [10.1126/scisignal.adw3709](https://doi.org/10.1126/scisignal.adw3709). Primary abstract at [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41529098/). The abstract reports intermediate-affinity-induced ERK pulses, cumulative ERK association with activation, LCK-dependent pulse frequency, MEK-dependent amplitude, and distinct MLK/RAF contributions to LAT condensate promotion/sustainment. The publisher full text returned HTTP 403 during this audit; no methods, parameter values, raw data, or source code were reconstructed. Treat it only as a candidate mechanism until those are accessed.
