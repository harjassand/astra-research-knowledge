# What a post-write isotope cohort can identify

## 1. The physical question and the observable

Let A be acetylene, B ethylene, and C ethane. The useful task is to remove A while preserving B, including the much larger B cofeed. The proposed intervention asks whether a brief write pulse changes the chemical fate of **carbon first supplied after that pulse** under a common subsequent read condition.

This is stronger than fitting an average from two static endpoints. It is weaker than identifying an electronic state or an elementary rate constant. The supplied endpoint values contain neither this observable nor the premises that make it identifiable.

For each independently restored preparation, randomize z=0 (sham) or z=1 (write pulse). Admit no enriched tag during writing. After the write ends, admit a finite, chemically identical isotope read packet, with both A and B doubly carbon-labelled. Follow with the same unlabelled mixed feed and electrical read condition until the tag is recovered or its unrecovered quantity is bounded. Labels in old gas, adsorbates, support and membrane must be absent to an acquired bound, with natural-isotope backgrounds included.

For this first test, all incoming fresh A and B use the same tag. That distinguishes fresh from old carbon but deliberately does not assign ethane to the A or B source. Reciprocal source-specific labelling is a second experiment, described in `EXPERIMENT.md`.

Let M_A and M_B be admitted tagged moles, and A_z, B_z, C_z the eventual tagged outlet moles in each species. Count all other products in C2-carbon equivalents P_z and all unrecovered tag in R_z. Under intact C2 skeletons and corrected isotope purity/background,

`M_A + M_B = A_z + B_z + C_z + P_z + R_z`.

If C-C cleavage/recombination is not negligible, measure isotope atoms across all isotopomers. The same equation then holds for **fresh-carbon equivalents**, but a source-specific molecular-branch interpretation is not licensed without an additional atom-mapping model. Mixed labelled/unlabelled C2 products are an explicit diagnostic, not something to discard.

Define fresh-feed acetylene removal `a_z = M_A - A_z`, and fresh net ethylene gain `U_z = B_z - M_B`. These are final-cohort fates across the read and chase. They are not instantaneous rates during the 0.1-second write or a 10-second hold.

## 2. Exact fresh-feed identification statement

**Theorem, restricted old-material/output-transport null.** Suppose:

1. No enriched read tag is present before the read; all tag backgrounds and cross-contamination are zero or independently corrected.
2. The two arms admit identical tagged packets, and the chemically relevant exposure at the reacting specimen during read/chase is identical: reactant activities, actual interfacial potential, temperature, pressure, and other externally supplied fields. The treatment can change old inventories, but under the null those inventories have no influence on the chemical fate of newly admitted carbon.
3. The fresh-cohort chemical-fate operator is identical in the two arms. Treatment may release, retain, or prevent conversion of **old** material and may change downstream species-specific output transport.
4. Downstream transport and detection conserve each tagged species after its final chemical transformation, with known gain. All tails are collected. There are no unaccounted leaks, side products, fresh-tag inventories, isotope exchanges, or transformations in sampling lines.
5. Randomized arms are exchangeable after a validated preparation/reset, with no cross-trial carryover. For a stochastic reactor, statements refer to expected cohort outcomes, not an unobserved counterfactual for a particular molecule.

Then `E[A_0,B_0,C_0,P_0] = E[A_1,B_1,C_1,P_1]`. In particular, `E[C_0-C_1]=0` and `E[U_1-U_0]=0`.

**Proof.** Separate each material output into contributions of old carbon and freshly tagged carbon. By premise 1, old-carbon release contributes no enriched-tag signal. Premises 2 and 3 make the fresh-carbon fate distribution common to both arms. For a downstream linear transport example, write `y_z,j = h_z,j * f_z,j`, where `h_z,j >= 0` and `integral h_z,j = 1`. Tonelli's theorem gives `integral y_z,j = integral f_z,j`, even when the two kernels differ. More generally, premise 4 directly supplies this material-conservation equality without assuming a linear kernel. Complete integrated recovery therefore yields the same fresh-fate vector. Randomization and premise 5 identify its arm expectations. QED.

**What a violation establishes.** Once the background, recovery, exposure, and randomization premises are acquired, a positive fresh ethane deficit is incompatible with a model limited to direct removal of old product or a mass-preserving delay after reaction. It establishes changed subsequent processing of new feed in the tested system. The model is rejected even if its old support reservoir is much larger than the total Pd atom inventory.

**What a violation does not establish.** An old adsorbate can block a site, and a hydrogen reservoir can alter reaction probabilities of many fresh molecules. Those mechanisms violate premise 3 and constitute changes in subsequent turnover; they are not excluded by this theorem. Nor does it prove that the state lies on Pd rather than the support, membrane, gas volume, or electrical interface. A surface-state claim requires a second intervention or a state-specific measurement.

## 3. Finite-record conservative bounds

All intervals below must be simultaneous calibrated bounds at the stated confidence level. Closure is not itself a confidence interval, and common detector biases must not be treated as independent noise.

Let `C_z^- <= recovered fresh C_z <= C_z^+` after subtracting quantified old-tag/background contamination. Let `u_z` conservatively bound all unassigned fresh tagged C2 equivalents: unrecovered outlet tails, retained tag, unmeasured products and relevant material-balance uncertainty. Assume no incoming tagged ethane, and that ethane is terminal in the tested reaction/sampling system or that its subsequent transformations are included explicitly.

The eventual cohort ethane amount lies in `[C_z^-, C_z^+ + u_z]`: in the worst case all unassigned tag could ultimately emerge as ethane. Thus a lower bound on a fresh ethane deficit is

`D_C,lower = C_0^- - C_1^+ - u_1 - e - L_exposure`.

Here `e` bounds residual isotope assignment/scrambling/blank error not already included in the intervals, and `L_exposure` bounds a difference attainable by unchanged chemistry under the acquired exposure mismatch. A lower bound on useful ethylene gain is

`D_U,lower = B_1^- - B_0^+ - u_0 - e_B - L_B,exposure`.

The different arm indices on u are intentional. Missing ethane in the pulsed arm can fake suppression; missing ethylene in the sham arm can fake gain. If admitted M_B differs, subtract `M_B,1 - M_B,0` with its full interval. When M_A differs, compare conversion/fate fractions with interval denominators or replay matched input uncertainty through the null.

A complete C2-only balance with identical acetylene removal implies `U_1-U_0 = C_0-C_1`. If side products, retained carbon, or acetylene removal differ, use the full balance instead. Do not infer useful ethylene recovery from an ethane deficit alone.

There is no general value for `L_exposure`. It must come from same-electrode challenge controls over a bounded envelope of local compositions/potentials/temperatures, or a validated response model with acquired uncertainty. A global sensitivity bound `L*||u_1-u_0||` is legitimate only if its domain and constant L are measured or otherwise justified; an assumed small derivative does not close the gate.

The complete-cohort statistic avoids ill-conditioned deconvolution and does not require the detector to resolve the 0.1-second write. It sacrifices a phase-resolved rate/lifetime claim. To measure the hold-only kinetic response, acquire the species-specific transfer function or bounded inventories at each temporal boundary; a fast sampling clock alone is insufficient.

## 4. Exact countermodels and the limits of attribution

`countermodels.py` checks the following examples with rational arithmetic. Their numbers are illustrative, not a fitted Pd mechanism.

**Old inventory alone.** In a finite observation, the sham releases 8 units of old ethane plus 2 units of fresh ethane; the pulsed arm releases only the same 2 fresh units. The ordinary output appears to fall from 10 to 2. The fresh tag gives 2 in both arms and rejects no subsequent-turnover null. This is a finite-window countermodel. If rates and inventories are rigorously closed over a true periodic steady cycle, an additive old-output reservoir with unchanged fresh chemistry cannot indefinitely change cycle totals; the supplied package has no raw closure record establishing that stronger interface.

**Product-specific lag.** Both arms create 10 tagged units of ethane. The pulsed output kernel transmits one fifth immediately and delays the rest beyond the read window, while the inert tracer is transmitted immediately in both arms. A short-window product trace is 10 versus 2, and an inert-tracer check passes. Full chase yields 10 in both arms. Product-specific adsorption is not calibrated by a nonadsorbing tracer alone.

**Fresh product retained.** The pulsed arm generates the same 10 fresh ethane units but retains 8, so only 2 emerge before sampling stops. The carbon deficit is 8. The finite-record bound is exactly `10-2-8=0`; there is no false-positive certificate. An endpoint Pd-site inventory alone would miss support or membrane retention.

**Concentration versus molar flux.** Identical 10-unit ethane flows divided by total outlet flows 100 and 500 give measured fractions 0.10 and 0.02. Such a fivefold flow change would require a specific material/venting explanation under the actual apparatus and is not presented as likely. It demonstrates why an unmeasured concentration is not a molar rate. The modest ideal two-step gas-contraction sensitivity in the supplied package is not a bound on all flow/calibration errors.

**Electronic state versus finite hydrogen state.** In one model a retained electronic variable sets fresh B-to-C probabilities to 1/10 and 1/50. In another, an unchanged reaction law has the same probabilities under two finite internal H inventories or H activities created by the write. Each gives fresh outputs `(B,C)=(90,10)` and `(98,2)` for a 100-unit B cohort, complete carbon balance, the same terminal read potential, and zero read electrical current. The fresh-feed test detects a real future-fate change but cannot choose between these mechanisms. Neither current nor carbon closure measures hidden H by itself.

This equivalence also holds for a relaxing response, not just one endpoint. Let an electronic coordinate x and a normalized H occupancy theta each obey `theta_next=(theta+1)/2`, start at 1 in the sham and 1/5 after writing, and produce `C=10*theta` per 100-unit fresh B packet. Both descriptions give exactly the same carbon response at every packet. A finite unit-capacity H occupancy replenished by the common gas can produce a six-packet ethane deficit of 15.75 units despite only a 0.8-unit initial occupancy difference. Extra turnover and recharge hydrogen comes from gas; unmeasured H uptake differs between mechanisms. This shows why a finite inventory can act as a catalytic state and amplify across many turnovers. A bound on direct one-shot purge is not a universal bound on inventory-mediated kinetic influence. The code checks this exact rational example.

These are alternative causal models, not a claim that the real Pd electrode follows either formula. Subsurface H/C alternatives are nevertheless grounded in same-metal chemistry: Teschner and coauthors measured hydrogen/carbon-state effects and slower bulk/surface H exchange in Pd alkyne hydrogenation; their findings do not transfer a response kernel to this electrode. [Primary 2010 study](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp9103799).

**General finite-horizon nonidentification.** With no independently acquired capacity/dynamics restriction on hidden reservoirs, any finite sequence of observed branch probabilities can be generated by a hidden catalytic state or by a finite reservoir whose occupancy indexes the same sequence and whose occupancy-dependent reaction law reproduces it. Relabelling that hidden coordinate leaves every measured output law invariant. An output-only estimator cannot distinguish the labels. Measuring hydrogen balance, an independently bounded reservoir, a selective reset, or a state-specific intervention adds information; assigning a mechanistic name does not.

This is not an argument that all hypotheses are equally plausible. It identifies the additional premises needed for an exact causal claim.

## 5. Resolving the remaining equivalence, if the first test passes

The next measurement is selected by the surviving null, not by a fixed wish list:

- If only downstream delay survives, extend species-specific collection and account for retained fresh tag. Integrated recovery resolves it without a microscopic model.
- If reactive composition/transport survives, sample or bound the actual gas entering/leaving the productive electrode and perform sham composition perturbations spanning the possible mismatch. A blank-cell or inert-tracer match alone does not prove equal reactive exposures.
- If electrical lag survives, begin the fresh read after actual interfacial-potential convergence and use a closed-loop common read. Report a lost response if the effect disappears before convergence; that only excludes retained control at that delay.
- If H inventory survives, add same-regime hydrogen inlet/outlet and uptake accounting. A separately validated H/D replacement experiment can constrain its origin, but H/D switching changes kinetics and exchanges with support/membrane. D incorporation without those controls is not a unique state readout.
- If adsorbate coverage or minority Pd phases survive, acquire an operando signal under the productive mixed feed and manipulate that state selectively while holding the gas/electrical read boundary fixed. Absence of a detectable bulk XAS change does not bound a highly active minority population.

If no selective reset/state measurement or exposure bound is obtainable, stop the mechanism-specific claim. The system-level fresh-feed response can remain a useful empirical control observation, but its technological advantage must still beat the matched incumbent and include preparation/reset work.
