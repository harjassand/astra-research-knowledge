## B1. A regular net with no scalar square

Over a field (F), put

\[
f_1=x^2+yz,\qquad f_2=y^2+xz,\qquad f_3=z^2+xy.\tag{B1}
\]

For \(\operatorname{char}F\ne2\), these form a full regular sequence. An exact certificate is

\[
y^2f_1-yz f_2+(2z^2-xy)f_3=2z^4,
\]

and its cyclic rotations. Hence the ideal contains (x^4,y^4,z^4); it is primary to the homogeneous maximal ideal and has height3. The standard Cohen–Macaulay system-of-parameters theorem for polynomial rings implies regularity. Equivalently, the product of the three equations at a common geometric zero gives (2x^2y^2z^2=0), and then all variables vanish. In characteristic2, ((1,1,1)) is a nonzero common zero, so the regularity hypothesis fails.

Suppose a nonzero scalar linear form \(\ell=ux+vy+wz\) has square in the span of B1. Coefficient comparison gives

\[
u^2=2vw,\qquad v^2=2uw,\qquad w^2=2uv.\tag{B2}
\]

Multiplying gives (7u^2v^2w^2=0). If characteristic is not7, some coordinate vanishes, and B2 forces all three to vanish. This remains valid after **every** field extension. The polynomial certificate, for (e_1=u^2-2vw,e_2=v^2-2uw,e_3=w^2-2uv), is

\[
e_1v^2w^2+2vw e_2w^2+4uv w^2e_3=-7u^2v^2w^2.
\]

Characteristic7 is genuinely exceptional:

\[
(x+2y+2z)^2=f_1+4f_2+4f_3\quad\text{over }\mathbf F_7.
\]

The initial weaker principal-minor argument excluded characteristics2,3,7. Literal square comparison improves the exclusion to just7; the weaker communicated claim was safe, not a counterexample to this refinement.

## B2. Exact finite-field division port is impossible

In the last triangular row for this quadratic triple, there are no later forms, and homogeneity places (B_{31},B_{32}\in A_0=F). Thus

\[
t_3^2=c_3f_3+B_{31}f_1+B_{32}f_2,\quad c_3\ne0.\tag{B3}
\]

If \(\Delta\) is commutative, B1–B2 make B3 impossible in characteristic not7. This also rules out the interface over an algebraically closed center, where every finite-dimensional division algebra is a field.

If the center is **finite** and \(\Delta\) is finite-dimensional, then \(\Delta\) is a finite division ring. Wedderburn's theorem makes it a field (and if central over that center, it equals the center). Therefore the source's exact division/basis/row interface cannot be specialized to any finite field of characteristic outside \(\{2,7\}\), despite B1 remaining regular there. This applies to every finite extension of every such \(\mathbf F_p\), not just tested primes. A positive-characteristic *transcendental* center is a different interface and is not excluded by Wedderburn; its generic division property is not acquired here.

Named prerequisite: J.S.Milne, *Class Field Theory*, v4.03,6August2020, IV§4 Theorem4.1 (Wedderburn). His short proof uses conjugacy of maximal subfields and the fact that a finite group cannot be covered by conjugates of a proper subgroup. The latter follows because there are at most ([G:H]) conjugates, all sharing the identity, so their union has at most (1+[G:H](|H|-1)<|G|) elements. I read the relevant primary author-text theorem/proof excerpt and title/version, not the whole book.

This blocks a direct combination with cycle17–18 modular quiver/flag acquisition. It says nothing against modular verification of numerical Hilbert functions, characteristic-zero EGH, or a newly proved matrix-coefficient algorithm.

## B3. Matrix splitting loses the unit leading-exponent count

There is a concrete failure of the tempting replacement “split the division algebra into matrices and keep the same least-exponent extraction.” Work over any field, set (D=\operatorname{Mat}_2(F)), (P=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)),

\[
t_1=xI_2,\qquad t_2=Px+yI_2.
\]

These forms commute. Their ordered monomials form a full left-(D) module basis: in each homogeneous degree the change from (x^a y^b) is triangular with unit diagonal, since (t_2^b=y^b I_2+\) terms with positive (x)-degree.

Take the central homogeneous ideal (I=(y,x^2)\subset F[x,y]), which contains the full regular sequence (x^2,y^2). Degree-one elements of (DI) are (By), (B\in D), and

\[
By=B t_2-BP t_1.
\]

The source's reverse-variable lex comparison regards (t_1) as the smaller degree-one monomial. Choosing (B=E_{11}) gives leading exponent (t_1), whereas choosing (B=E_{12}) gives (BP=0\) and leading exponent (t_2). Thus both degree-one exponents are occupied, even though ((DI)_1\) has (F)-dimension4 and its normalized (D)-dimension is1. The resulting occupancy ideal contains both (x,y), giving quotient Hilbert coefficient0 in degree1; the true quotient (F[x,y]/I) has coefficient1.

This example meets the full ordered-module-basis and central regular-ideal conditions. It does **not** assert the source's triangular row identities for these particular (t_i); it isolates and disproves the proposed matrix replacement of the division-elimination step. No source theorem with a division hypothesis is contradicted.

More generally, an occupied leading coefficient subspace is a left ideal in \(\operatorname{Mat}_d(F)\) of dimension (d r_\alpha), (0\le r_\alpha\le d). The normalized counts are (r_\alpha/d), not merely0or1. Right multiplication by a commuting (t_j) preserves the leading coefficient and translates its exponent, so these ranks obey containment/monotonicity. Division precisely makes each nonzero coefficient ideal the whole algebra.

## B4. Containment and integer graded totals do not suffice for rounding

Even if one acquires all fractional ranks and their graded sums, the following squarefree-box control blocks an unqualified integral extraction lemma. On subsets of ({1,2,3}), define surviving weights

\[
s_\varnothing=1,\quad s_A=2/3\ (|A|=1\text{ or }2),\quad s_{123}=0.
\]

These lie in ([0,1]) and decrease under inclusion. Their integer degree totals are

\[
(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3)=(1,2,2,0).
\]

An actual squarefree monomial downset with (h_1=2) can use only two singleton variables, so it has at most their one pair: (h_2\le1). Thus no integral downset has these totals. This is not claimed to arise from the split source construction; it is a counterexample to rounding from **just** the proposed containment and degree-total data. Our quiver support/polytope preservation theorems do not supply a missing stronger integrality condition automatically.
