# Cycle 2: finite-window inference from repeated projected increments

**Status.** The local characteristic test can be made into a finite-data guarantee for a controlled, stopped, compact one-dimensional witness. This is an internally derived theorem under explicit diffusion, reset, calibration, and censoring assumptions. The concrete guarantee is mathematically finite but very expensive: 6 million independent starts at each of two setpoints. It does not beat a model-informed likelihood baseline; for this particular example the observed diffusion is a shifted CIR process with a closed-form transition law. No empirical run, chemical validation, external proof review, or novelty priority is claimed.

## 1. Repeated-start theorem

Let a diffusion on a compact operational set (K\subset\mathbb R^n) satisfy
\[
dX_t=b(X_t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t)\,dW_t,
\qquad q(x)=\sigma(x)\sigma(x)^T.
\]
The process is stopped and held at its first exit \(\tau=\inf\{t:X_t\notin K\}\). Fix a nominal reset (x\in K), sensor (H\in\mathbb R^{p\times n}), and window (h>0). On replicate (r), the actual reset (x_r) is set before an independent fresh diffusion run and obeys \(\|x_r-x\|\le\rho\). A calibrated sensor has actual gain \(\widetilde H\) with \(\|\widetilde H-H\|_{op}\le\epsilon_H\). The recorded datum is only the projected endpoint increment
\[
Y_r=\widetilde H(X_{h\wedge\tau}-X_0)+e_r,
\qquad \|e_r\|\le\nu.
\]
Here (e_r) is the residual difference of the final and initial readout errors; it may be adversarial, dependent across replicates, and dependent on the trajectory. If each endpoint readout error is bounded by \(\nu_s\), take \(\nu=2\nu_s\) after removal of a stable offset. Thus no random-noise model or zero-mean premise is hidden in the theorem. The complete list of reset choices is fixed before the runs (or independently randomized without using prior path data), so the latent increments are independent conditional on that list; their reset errors need not be identically distributed. For a fixed control/setpoint (u), if the realized value (u_r) differs from its nominal value by at most (\rho_u), take all coefficient bounds uniformly over that setpoint box and add (L_{a,u}\rho_u) and (L_{Q,u}\rho_u) to the drift and covariance biases, where these are response Lipschitz bounds in the control.

Assume on (K):

- \(\|b\|\le B\), \(\|\sigma\|_F\le S\), and the coefficients are (C^2) on a neighbourhood of (K);
- set \(A=\|\widetilde H\|B\), \(C=\|\widetilde H\|S\), and \(D=\sup_{z_1,z_2\in K}\|\widetilde H(z_1-z_2)\|\);
- \(\ell_a\) bounds the Lipschitz constants of both (Hb) and \(\widetilde Hb\), and \(\ell_Q\) bounds those of (HqH^T) and \(\widetilde Hq\widetilde H^T\);
- for each fixed control (u), (L_{a,u}) and (L_{Q,u}) bound the response Lipschitz constants in that setpoint.
- \(g_a=\sup_K\|\mathcal L(\widetilde Hb)\|\) and \(g_Q=\sup_K\|\mathcal L(\widetilde Hq\widetilde H^T)\|_F\), where \(\mathcal Lf=b\cdot\nabla f+\tfrac12q:\nabla^2 f\), componentwise.

All of (B,S,\ell_a,\ell_Q,L_{a,u},L_{Q,u},g_a,g_Q,\rho,\rho_u,\epsilon_H,\nu) are supplied bounds or certified from the candidate model/rate boxes. Let (r=\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial K)>\rho). A coordinate martingale maximal inequality and a union bound give the exit-probability bound
\[
p_h:=\Pr(\tau\le h)\le \min\!\left\{1,\ 2n\exp\!\left[-\frac{(r-\rho-Bh)_+^2}{2nS^2h}\right]\right\}.
\]
When (S=0), take (p_h=0) if (Bh<r-\rho), otherwise the trivial bound 1. The operational stop must actually be implementable: either the experiment has a compact invariant operating set, or a boundary interlock can stop the run before leaving (K). For hidden boundary coordinates, such an interlock is an additional supplied control/cost; the inference itself below consumes no hidden-coordinate readings.

Define (q_0=C^2) and the deterministic response-error budgets
\[
\begin{aligned}
\beta_a={}&\ell_a\rho+L_{a,u}\rho_u+\epsilon_H B+\tfrac12 g_a h+A p_h+\nu/h,\\
\beta_Q={}&\ell_Q\rho+L_{Q,u}\rho_u+\sqrt p\,\epsilon_H(2\|H\|+\epsilon_H)S^2+\tfrac12g_Qh+q_0p_h+A^2h\\
&+2\sqrt2\,\ell_a C\left(\tfrac25 Bh^{3/2}+\tfrac12Sh\right)
+\tfrac43AC\sqrt{hp_h}+(2D\nu+\nu^2)/h.
\end{aligned}
\]
For (R) replicates at this setpoint, compute only from increments
\[
\widehat a=\frac1{Rh}\sum_{r=1}^R Y_r,\qquad
\widehat Q=\frac1{Rh}\sum_{r=1}^R Y_rY_r^T.
\]
For any (0<\alpha<1), set
\[
v_1=(Ah+C\sqrt h)^2,\qquad v_2=8A^4h^4+128p^2C^4h^2,
\]
\[
L_1=\log(4p/\alpha),\quad L_2=\log(4p^2/\alpha),
\]
\[
s_a=\frac{\sqrt p}{h}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2v_1L_1}{R}}+\frac{4DL_1}{3R}\right),\qquad
s_Q=\frac p h\left(\sqrt{\frac{2v_2L_2}{R}}+\frac{4D^2L_2}{3R}\right).
\]
Then, with probability at least (1-\alpha),
\[
\|\widehat a-Hb(x)\|\le\beta_a+s_a,
\qquad
\|\widehat Q-Hq(x)H^T\|_F\le\beta_Q+s_Q.
\]
For (J) setpoints, apply this bound with per-point failure probability \(\alpha/J\) and take a union bound. If an unweighted `vech` response is used, convert the Frobenius bound to its Euclidean `vech` norm by at most a factor \(\sqrt2\).

### Proof sketch with the finite-window and stop terms exposed

Write the stopped projected increment as (V=U+M), where
\[
U=\int_0^{h\wedge\tau}\widetilde Hb(X_s)\,ds,
\qquad M=\int_0^{h\wedge\tau}\widetilde H\sigma(X_s)\,dW_s.
\]
Itô's formula for the stopped process, applied to \(\widetilde Hb\), gives
\[
\mathbb E[1_{s<\tau}\widetilde Hb(X_s)]-\widetilde Hb(x_r)
=\int_0^s\mathbb E[1_{u<\tau}\mathcal L(\widetilde Hb)(X_u)]du
-\mathbb E[1_{\tau\le s}\widetilde Hb(X_\tau)].
\]

The right side is bounded by (g_as+A p_h). Integrating over (s\in[0,h]) and dividing by (h), then charging state-reset, control-setpoint, and gain-calibration uncertainty, yields the first four terms of (\beta_a); the bounded readout increment contributes (\nu/h).

For the martingale part of the second moment,
\[
\mathbb E[MM^T]=\int_0^h\mathbb E[1_{s<\tau}\widetilde Hq(X_s)\widetilde H^T]ds.
\]
The same stopped-Itô calculation for \(\widetilde Hq\widetilde H^T\) contributes (g_Qh/2+q_0p_h) after division by (h); reset and control uncertainty add (\ell_Q\rho+L_{Q,u}\rho_u). Also \(\|UU^T\|_F/h\le A^2h\). For the cross term, use the product rule for (UM^T); the constant part \(\widetilde Hb(x_r)\) vanishes for the unstopped martingale and its stopped-survival correction is at most \((4/3)AC\sqrt{hp_h}\) after dividing by (h). The variable part is bounded by
\[
2\sqrt2\ell_aC\left(\tfrac25 Bh^{3/2}+\tfrac12Sh\right),
\]
using \(\mathbb E\|M_s\|^2\le C^2s\) and
\(\mathbb E\|X_{s\wedge\tau}-x_r\|^2\le2(B^2s^2+S^2s)\). Gain calibration contributes \(\sqrt p\,\epsilon_H(2\|H\|+\epsilon_H)S^2\) in Frobenius norm. Finally, for each replicate the matrix readout perturbation is bounded by
\(\|YY^T-VV^T\|_F\le2D\nu+\nu^2\).

For concentration, \(\|V\|\le D\), \(\mathbb E\|V\|^2\le(Ah+C\sqrt h)^2\), and each coordinate of (M) has quadratic variation at most (C^2h). The scalar martingale exponential tail, integrated to its fourth moment, gives \(\mathbb E\|M\|^4\le16p^2C^4h^2\); hence \(\mathbb E\|V\|^4\le v_2\). Bernstein's inequality applies to independent, not necessarily identically distributed, increments: a drift coordinate has variance at most (v_1) and centred bound (2D); a covariance entry has variance at most (v_2) and centred bound (2D^2). A union bound over the (p) drift and (p^2) covariance entries gives (s_a,s_Q). Adversarial (e_r) never enters this concentration step; its worst-case effect was already charged deterministically.

## 2. A finite test for the A/B rate-box witness

Use the two networks
\[
\mathcal N: B\xrightarrow{k}A+B,\ A\xrightarrow{\mu}0;
\qquad
\mathcal N': B\xrightarrow{\lambda}2A+B,\ A\xrightarrow{\mu'}0.
\]
Only (A) is measured, (H=(1,0)). (B) is a known controlled setpoint and does not change in either model. The one-dimensional observed Langevin equations at fixed (b\in\{1,2\}) are
\[
dA=(kb-\mu A)dt+\sqrt{kb+\mu A}\,dW,
\quad\text{or}\quad
 dA=(2\lambda b-\mu'A)dt+\sqrt{4\lambda b+\mu'A}\,dW.
\]
Set (a=1) at both interventions. ODE drift slopes match at (k=2\lambda), but the variance slopes are (k\) versus (4\lambda). With all four rates in ([1,2]), the two possible slope sets are ([1,2]) and ([4,8]), separated by 2. For each setpoint collect (R) stopped endpoint increments and set
\[
\widehat q_b=\frac1{Rh}\sum_{r=1}^R(\Delta A_{b,r}^{obs})^2,
\qquad \widehat s=\widehat q_2-\widehat q_1.
\]
Decide \(\mathcal N\) if \(\widehat s<3\), otherwise \(\mathcal N'\). If each \(|\widehat q_b-q_b(1,b)|<1/2\), the slope error is (<1), so the decision is correct for every rate tuple in either box.

Here is one deliberately conservative finite-data instantiation. Stop/hold the active state (A) at its first exit from (K=[0.5,2.5]); the safety interlock uses the same calibrated A channel, with any threshold error included in the A-reset bound. Use A-reset error (\rho=10^{-6}), B-setpoint error (\rho_u=10^{-6}), gain uncertainty (\epsilon_H=10^{-6}), endpoint residual errors at most (5\times10^{-7}) each (so (\nu=10^{-6})), (h=10^{-4}), and (\alpha=0.05). Bounds valid uniformly for both models, (b\in[1-\rho_u,2+\rho_u]), and rates in ([1,2]) are
\[
B=10,\ S=5,\ A=10.001,\ C=5.001,\ D=2.001,\ \ell_a=\ell_Q=2.001,\ L_{a,u}=4.001,\ L_{Q,u}=8.001,\ g_a=g_Q=20.001.
\]
The active diffusion state is A; B is a fixed control with the stated setpoint uncertainty. The primed variance is at most (4(2)(2.000001)+2(2.5)=21.000008<25=S^2), and its drift magnitude is below 10. The A-state derivatives of projected drift and variance are at most 2.001; the B-control derivatives are at most 4.001 and 8.001. Both fields are affine in A, so their generators are bounded by 20.001. D is the projected diameter of the A interval.

The scalar exit bound is
\[
p_h\le2\exp\!\left[-\frac{(0.5-10^{-6}-10^{-3})^2}{2(25)(10^{-4})}\right]<4.8\times10^{-22}.
\]
The fourth-moment bound is (v_2=8(10.001)^4h^4+128(5.001)^4h^2<8.007\times10^{-4}). Substitution into (\beta_Q) gives a systematic per-setting error below (0.05827); the dominant terms are (0.01001) finite-window drift-square bias, (0.00719) drift/diffusion cross bias, (0.040021) bounded readout perturbation, and the (8.001\times10^{-6}) B-setpoint uncertainty. At (R=6{,}000{,}000) per setpoint, scalar Bernstein with (L=\log(4/\alpha)=\log80), (V^2\in[0,D^2]), and centred bound (D^2), gives statistical error
\[
s_Q\le\frac1h\left[\sqrt{\frac{2v_2\log80}{R}}+\frac{2D^2\log80}{3R}\right]<0.36148.
\]
A union bound covers both setpoints with probability at least 95%. Therefore each (\widehat q_b) is within (0.41975) of its target, the slope error is below (0.83950<1), and the threshold-3 classifier is correct with probability at least 0.95 under either network and every rate tuple in the supplied box.

**Charged data and controls:** two known (B) setpoints; (12{,}000{,}000) fresh resets/trajectories in total; (h=10^{-4}) per trajectory; at least two endpoint reads per trajectory (24 million scalar endpoint reads), with the stated calibrated gain and worst-case endpoint noise; an (A)-channel safety monitor/interlock throughout each window; A-reset and B-setpoint errors each at most (10^{-6}); rate bounds supplied rather than learned; about 12 million scalar square/add updates for the two empirical second moments (streaming memory O(1)); and one final threshold comparison. The bound treats only the start/end increments as statistical input; continuous safety monitoring is an operational cost. Across all 12 million windows it is active for at most 1200 cumulative time units; if implemented with f digital safety reads per time unit, that adds 1200f scalar monitor reads. Continuous analog-stop power, reset time/energy, and the calibration procedure needed to certify the stated envelope remain instrument-specific cost terms (write these as C_monitor, C_reset, C_cal) and were not measured. If serialized, total diffusion-window time is at most 1200 model-time units; wall-clock throughput and parallel-well cost are unknown. If only the endpoints can be read and the physical diffusion is not confined, the bounded-increment premise fails. If a real instrument cannot provide the stated gain/noise/stop bounds, this finite-data guarantee does not apply. Millions of short starts and the (10^{-6})-scale endpoint-noise bound make this an existence proof of a finite test, not a claim of practical biological feasibility.

## 3. Equally informed finite-data baseline and novelty attack

The sourcewise method supplies a structural certificate and a robust rate-box margin. It does not improve the data statistic in this example: the actual classifier is a difference of two empirical second moments, and the sourcewise cone calculation explains why its target ranges are disjoint. An exact model-informed likelihood has at least as much information from the same increments.

In fact the fixed-(b) scalar diffusions above are shifted Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) processes. In \(\mathcal N\), put (Z=A+kb/\mu); then
\[
dZ=(2kb-\mu Z)dt+\sqrt{\mu Z}\,dW,
\]
a CIR process with \(\kappa=\mu,\theta=2kb/\mu,\sigma^2=\mu\). In \(\mathcal N'\), (Z'=A+4\lambda b/\mu'\) obeys
\[
dZ'=(6\lambda b-\mu' Z')dt+\sqrt{\mu' Z'}\,dW,
\]
with \(\kappa=\mu',\theta=6\lambda b/\mu',\sigma^2=\mu'\). Without the stop, the exact endpoint transition is a scaled noncentral chi-square: if (dZ=\kappa(\theta-Z)dt+\sigma\sqrt Z\,dW), then
\[
Z_h=c\,\chi'^2_{\delta}(\Lambda),\quad
c=\frac{\sigma^2(1-e^{-\kappa h})}{4\kappa},\quad
\delta=\frac{4\kappa\theta}{\sigma^2},\quad
\Lambda=\frac{4\kappa e^{-\kappa h}Z_0}{\sigma^2(1-e^{-\kappa h})}.
\]
Thus, with known stochastic model, setpoints, and the same increments, a generalized likelihood-ratio test can optimize over the two supplied rate boxes. For a known stochastic readout law this is an ordinary likelihood; with bounded adversarial errors, profile or robustify over the interval of compatible latent increments rather than treating the observed readout as an exact density sample. If the safety stopping rule is retained, use the corresponding one-dimensional stopped transition kernel (or retain the boundary atom/censoring information); its computation is an additional cost. The exit probability in this parameterization is below (5\times10^{-22}) per run, so a non-stopped CIR likelihood is also an essentially exact comparator at the displayed numerical precision, while the theorem itself remains valid for the stopped data.

This is not a claim that the exact likelihood has a proved smaller uniform finite-sample error for the composite rate boxes. It is a decisive control against any claim that the moment/cone procedure outperforms equally informed finite-data inference: under a known readout law, Neyman–Pearson makes the likelihood-ratio test optimal for two fixed parameter points on the same full endpoint data. Under the present adversarial-bounded readout model, the likelihood baseline must instead be robustified over compatible error intervals. For composite boxes, no head-to-head minimax comparison was done, and our moment bound is intentionally conservative. The sourcewise certificate may still help explain or prescreen a witness, but no statistical-efficiency gain is shown.

The primary literature further narrows novelty:

- Faul–Hoessly–Xia explicitly distinguish structural from practical identifiability and note that discrete-time transition densities are often unavailable; their own stated scope observes all species. Their theorem/proof quantifier conflict and the partial-local-characteristic extension are documented in the cycle-1 report. Cambridge's published page currently still prints “for every source” in Theorem 3.3(2), while Theorem 5.15 and §7.3 prove the confoundability criterion using nonempty intersection at every source. See [the published article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382) and [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07638v3). A targeted search did not surface an erratum; that is not proof none exists.
- Li, Barahona & Thomas, *Moment-based parameter inference with error guarantees for stochastic reaction networks*, J. Chem. Phys. 162, 135105 (2025), DOI [10.1063/5.0251744](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0251744), already give parameter outer-bounds from empirical moment intervals via convex/SDP constraints, including time-resolved data and unobserved species. Their scientific object is the chemical master equation (count process), not this Langevin diffusion, and their guarantee is conditional on the input intervals containing the true moments. This is a strong adjacent comparator, not a theorem dependency.
- Choi, Kang & Rempala, *Inferring structure and parameters of stochastic reaction networks with logistic regression*, PLoS ONE 21(2):e0341639 (2026), DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0341639](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341639), report partial-observation inference for synthetic SIR time series via Bayesian logistic regression plus an ODE approximation for hidden species, under a unique-stoichiometric-signature/pure-birth setup. It blocks broad claims that partial stochastic-network inference is untouched, though its model and data interface differ.
- Müller, Ramaswamy & Sbalzarini, [*Global parameter identification of stochastic reaction networks from single trajectories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4785), handle noisy partial-species time courses with stochastic simulation and Monte Carlo fitting; this is parameter fitting, not the source-cone structural criterion.

**Verdict:** the finite-window gate is passed conditionally for the specified stopped A-only diffusion and calibrated noise envelope. The generic field experiment is not passed: safety-stop implementation, realistic readout floor, reset cost, Langevin adequacy, and replicate throughput are unmeasured. The finite-data method has no demonstrated advantage over an equally informed CIR likelihood and no novelty/priority clearance. The scientifically defensible contribution is an explicit finite-sample bridge for one model witness plus a warning that the theorem is a conservative sufficiency certificate, not an inference improvement.

**Falsifiers and next gate.** The theorem fails if the stated uniform coefficient/generator bounds, reset/calibration limits, replicate independence, or stop behavior are violated. The A/B discrimination claim is falsified within the stated interface by any allowed rate pair for which (q(2)-q(1)) leaves its predicted interval, or by a measured readout/process law whose certified bias exceeds the assumed budget. The next useful comparison is a preregistered simulation using identical (R,h,H), reset/noise envelope, and rate boxes for (i) this variance-slope rule, (ii) exact stopped-CIR interval likelihood, and (iii) a matched ODE-only control; then report worst-case error and wall-clock/instrument cost. That comparison has not been run.
