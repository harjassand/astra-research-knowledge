The second term follows by taking Tr_C of the R_C recovery estimate.
Thus (1) is an ACTUAL local broadcaster with joint AB errors at most z.
This is the gate absent from mere k-extension or one-sided-CMI arguments.

4. EARLY FALSIFIER: ONE-SIDED CMI IS INSUFFICIENT EVEN AT BOUNDED I(A:B)

Let d=2m>=4, k=m+1, and let omega_{d,k}=P_wedge^k/binom(d,k), the
normalized projector onto the antisymmetric k-particle subspace of
(C^d)^(tensor k). Let A,B be two single particles and C the remaining
k-2 particles. Every r-particle marginal is P_wedge^r/binom(d,r).
This follows directly by tracing the uniform Slater-basis projector:
each r-subset has the same binom(d-r,k-r) completions and the marginal
is supported on the antisymmetric r-subspace. Thus its entropy is
ln binom(d,r). In particular
 omega_AB=(I-F)/[d(d-1)],
 I(A:B)=ln[2d/(d-1)]<=ln4.                           (7)

The C-conditioned CMI is
 delta_C=2ln binom(d,k-1)-ln binom(d,k-2)-ln binom(d,k)
        =ln[k(d-k+2)/((k-1)(d-k+1))]
        =2ln(1+2/d)<=4/d.                           (8)
It tends to zero, and universal recovery from the LARGE C block exists.
Nevertheless every AB separable state has antisymmetric-projector
expectation at most 1/2, whereas omega_AB has expectation 1. Therefore
 inf_{sep AB} T(omega_AB,sep)>=1/2,
 inf_{sep A:BC} T(omega_ABC,sep)>=1/2.                (9)

The omitted opposite-direction CMI is
 delta_B=ln[(d-1)k/(2(d-k+1))]
        =ln[(d-1)(d+2)/(2d)],                        (10)
which grows as ln(d/2). Thus small CMI conditioned on an arbitrary large
extension does not produce a B-local broadcaster. A decoder C->B can
be supplied by the C recovery, but B->BC recovery is precisely the
missing physical hypothesis. Many symmetric state extensions are not
an alternative to it. This is a fully explicit scoped obstruction,
not a falsifier of (O) or (D), and no novelty of the antisymmetric family
is claimed. Primary historical comparison remains to be recorded.
