CONSTRUCTIVE REFERENCE-EXACT POSITIVE RECONSTRUCTION
Rational graded hats, regular density paths, and dimension-uniform tensors
Date: 2026-10-07
Status: direct mathematical derivation, internally audited; not externally
reviewed or priority-cleared. No publishing performed.

MAIN OUTCOME
============

A concrete scalar finite-label compiler satisfies the spectral tensor
upper's two distinct requirements:

  ||(I-K_J)g_t||_2 <= C0 |t| J^-2 ||g_t||_2,
  <g_t,(I-K_J)g_t> <= C1 t^2 J^-2 ||g_t||_2^2.

It works for every known probability reference mu(dx)=rho(x)dx on [-1,1]
with 0<m<=rho<=M and rho Lipschitz with constant L, and every regular
normalized likelihood-ratio path specified below. It needs neither
Gaussian tails nor exponential-family structure. Endpoint grading is
essential for the claimed L2 rate with this positive operator.

The compiler uses J+1 scalar labels, exact rational knots, a two-outcome
encoder at each observation, and local reference-weighted hat decoders.
The decoded reference law is EXACTLY mu. The operator is positive,
self-adjoint, positive semidefinite and contractive in L2(mu).

The scalar result yields a dimension-uniform product upper for parameters
t_i=r_i v_i, sum v_i^2<=1, 0<=r_i<=1. For chosen label counts N_i>=1,

  sup_v TV(P_v, decoded(P_v)) <= C max_i r_i/N_i^2,
  D = product_i N_i,

where C depends only on common scalar regularity constants and not on the
number of coordinates. A non-exponential realization is g_t=1+t h.

Separately, a general s-dimensional, possibly nonproduct family with a
smooth likelihood ratio relative to a positive Lipschitz reference has a
reference-exact O(D^-2/s) compiler. Even a BV reference with common jumps
can give O(D^-2/s) in TV, despite raw-density smoothing giving only first
order. These are sufficient assumptions, not claims for arbitrary families.

1. SCALAR CONSTRUCTION AND OPERATOR IDENTITIES
==============================================

Assumptions: mu is a known probability on [-1,1] with density rho obeying
0<m<=rho(x)<=M<infinity and |rho(x)-rho(y)|<=L|x-y|.

For integer J>=1 choose the rational knots

  x_j = -1 + 6(j/J)^2 - 4(j/J)^3,    j=0,...,J.

They are strictly increasing. Let phi_j be the ordinary nonnegative
piecewise-linear nodal hat on this knot sequence, including the one-sided
endpoint hats. For x in [-1,1],

  sum_j phi_j(x)=1,
  sum_j phi_j(x) x_j=x.

Define m_j=int phi_j dmu>0 and nu_j(dx)=phi_j(x)mu(dx)/m_j.
The encoder draws label j with probability phi_j(x). The decoder, given
j, draws a fresh continuous observation from nu_j. Neither operation
depends on the unknown path parameter t.

For an input density g relative to mu, the decoded density relative to mu is

  K_J g(x)=sum_j phi_j(x) [int phi_j(y)g(y)mu(dy)]/m_j.

Thus K_J 1=1, int K_J g dmu=int g dmu, and K_J is positive. Its kernel
is symmetric relative to mu. For f,g in L2(mu),

  <f,K_J g> = sum_j <f,phi_j><g,phi_j>/m_j,
  <f,K_J f> >=0.

Jensen gives <f,K_J f><=||f||_2^2. Hence 0<=K_J<=I as a self-adjoint
operator. The reference is decoded exactly, including every factor rho;
the scheme approximates only the unknown likelihood ratio.

The exact Dirichlet identity is

  <f,(I-K_J)f> = sum_j m_j Var_{nu_j}(f).

There are J+1 labels, not J. A label dimension accounting that replaces
J+1 by J must explicitly absorb this constant or change notation.

2. RATIONAL GRID GEOMETRY
========================

Write F(u)=-1+6u^2-4u^3, u in [0,1]. Then
F'(u)=12u(1-u), 0<=F'<=3, and |F''|<=12.
With ell_j=x_{j+1}-x_j, these imply

  max ell_j <= 3/J,
  |ell_j-ell_{j-1}| <= 12/J^2,
  ell_0=ell_{J-1}=6/J^2-4/J^3 <=6/J^2.

At each node set a=x_j-x_{j-1}, b=x_{j+1}-x_j, assigning a=0 or b=0
at the corresponding endpoint. Uniformly,

  a,b<=d=3/J,    |b-a|<=12/J^2.

The knots have the exact integer representation

  x_j=(-J^3+6j^2 J-4j^3)/J^3.

Thus each knot needs O(log J) bits; cosine evaluation or irrational-node
rounding is unnecessary. The mesh has the same O(J) number of nodes as
the uniform grid, but its endpoint widths are O(J^-2).

3. EXACT FIRST-MOMENT AUDIT AND SCALAR APPROXIMATION
==================================================

Let c_j=int y nu_j(dy) be the reference conditional centroid. Direct
integration of an asymmetric hat gives

  int phi_j(y)dy=(a+b)/2,
  int phi_j(y)(y-x_j)dy=(b^2-a^2)/6,
  int phi_j(y)(y-x_j)^2dy=(a^3+b^3)/12.

Expand rho(y)=rho(x_j)+[rho(y)-rho(x_j)] in the first moment. Since
m_j>=m(a+b)/2 and rho(x_j)<=M,

  |c_j-x_j|
   <= (M/(3m))|b-a| + (L/(6m))(a^3+b^3)/(a+b)
   <= B/J^2,

  B := 4M/m + 3L/(2m).

This bound includes endpoints. There is no division by an asymptotically
small cell mass after cancelling the factor a+b.

For f in C^{1,1}([-1,1]) write H=Lip(f'); the following also holds for
f in C2 with H=||f''||_infinity. Taylor at node x_j gives

  |int f dnu_j-f(x_j)|
   <= ||f'||_infinity |c_j-x_j| + H d^2/2.

The ordinary nodal interpolant I_J f=sum phi_j f(x_j) has
||I_J f-f||_infinity<=H d^2/8, by the linear-interpolation remainder on
each cell. Combining the two estimates yields the explicit bound

  ||K_J f-f||_infinity
   <= J^-2 [B ||f'||_infinity + (45/8) H].                  (S1)

It therefore bounds Lp(mu) error for every 1<=p<=infinity, since mu is
a probability. The constant is convenient, not optimal.

For the energy bound, the support of each nu_j has diameter at most 2d.
The range of f there is at most 2d||f'||_infinity. Variance is at most
one quarter of squared range, so the exact identity in section 1 gives

  <f,(I-K_J)f> <=9 J^-2 ||f'||_infinity^2.                (S2)

The defect and energy estimates are different statements: S1 is linear
in a small likelihood perturbation, while S2 is quadratic. Both are
needed for the dimension-uniform tensor argument.

4. GENERAL REGULAR PATHS, WITH A NON-EXPONENTIAL REALIZATION
==========================================================

Let g_t>=0, int g_t dmu=1, |t|<=1, and assume common finite constants
A1,A2,Cchi such that

  ||g_t'||_infinity <= A1 |t| ||g_t||_2,
  Lip(g_t') <= A2 |t| ||g_t||_2,
  ||g_t||_2^2 <= exp(Cchi t^2).                           (P)

These are explicit regularity conditions, not an assumed reconstruction
theorem. Then S1-S2 give

  ||(I-K_J)g_t||_2 <= C0 |t| J^-2 ||g_t||_2,
  <g_t,(I-K_J)g_t> <= C1 t^2 J^-2 ||g_t||_2^2,

  C0=B A1+(45/8)A2,       C1=9 A1^2.                    (S3)

Example without exponential-family structure: let h in C^{1,1},
int h dmu=0, ||h||_infinity<=1/2, and set g_t=1+t h. Then g_t>=1/2,
||g_t||_2>=1, its first and second regularity quantities are exactly
linear in |t|, and

  ||g_t||_2^2=1+t^2||h||_2^2 <=exp(||h||_2^2 t^2).

Thus P holds with A1=||h'||_infinity, A2=Lip(h'),
Cchi=||h||_2^2. Arbitrary known positive Lipschitz references rho and
arbitrary centered smooth h are allowed. This is a family of regular
local statistical perturbations, not merely Gaussian or exponential
tilts in another notation.

Exponential realization: g_t(x)=exp(tx-psi(t)),
psi(t)=log int exp(tx)dmu, |t|<=1. Since X in [-1,1],
psi''(t)=Var_t(X)<=1 for every real t. Consequently

  psi(2t)-2psi(t)+psi(0)<=t^2,
  ||g_t||_2^2<=exp(t^2).

Also ||g_t||_infinity<=e^2 and ||g_t||_2>=1, while
g_t'=t g_t and g_t''=t^2 g_t. Hence A1=A2=e^2, Cchi=1 work, giving

  C0=e^2(B+45/8),       C1=9e^4.                         (S4)

Replacing x by a fixed bounded C^{1,1} statistic tau(x) also works, with
constants depending on its range and derivative bounds. No Gaussian
assumption is involved.

5. DIMENSION-UNIFORM PRODUCT COMPILER
====================================

Let mu=product_{i=1}^s mu_i, with uniform reference constants m,M,L and
paths g_{i,t} satisfying P with common A1,A2,Cchi. Let

  p_v relative to mu = product_i g_{i,t_i},
  t_i=r_i v_i, 0<=r_i<=1, sum_i v_i^2<=1.

Use K_i=K_{J_i} in coordinate i. Let T_i=I-K_i. These are commuting
positive semidefinite contractions on the product Hilbert space. Their
joint spectral representation gives

  0<=I-product_i K_i <=sum_i T_i,
  (I-product_i K_i)^2 <=(sum_i T_i)^2.

For f=product_i g_{i,t_i}, divide the expanded quadratic form by ||f||_2^2.
The diagonal terms are at most C0^2 sum_i t_i^2/J_i^4. Every cross term
factorizes into the two normalized coordinate energies. Thus

  ||(I-product K_i)f||_2^2 / ||f||_2^2
   <= C0^2 sum_i t_i^2/J_i^4
       + C1^2 (sum_i t_i^2/J_i^2)^2.

Put eta=max_i r_i/J_i^2. The ellipsoid condition implies

  sum_i t_i^2/J_i^4 <=eta^2,
  sum_i t_i^2/J_i^2
    =sum_i v_i^2 r_i(r_i/J_i^2)<=eta,
  ||f||_2^2<=exp(Cchi sum_i t_i^2)<=exp(Cchi).

Since TV is half the L1(mu) likelihood-ratio error and mu is a probability,

  TV <= (1/2) exp(Cchi/2) sqrt(C0^2+C1^2) eta.            (T1)

This constant is independent of s. Concatenating coordinate labels uses
exactly product_i (J_i+1) labels. The decoder independently samples the
coordinate local reference decoders. This is a concrete classical-memory
upper with no free per-parameter density description.

All label counts N_i>=1 can be included. For N_i>=2 take J_i=N_i-1,
so J_i^-2<=4N_i^-2. For N_i=1 let K_i be projection onto constants,
meaning that the observation coordinate is discarded and decoded from
mu_i. Since ||g_t-1||_infinity<=2||g_t'||_infinity,

  ||(I-K_i)g_t||_2 <=2 A1 |t| ||g_t||_2,
  <g_t,(I-K_i)g_t> <=4 A1^2 t^2 ||g_t||_2^2.

Thus T1 holds with eta=max r_i/N_i^2, after replacing C0 by
max(4C0,2A1) and C1 by max(4C1,4A1^2). This includes zero-radius and
unobserved coordinates without charging two labels to every mode.

T1 alone is an upper. Matching lower bounds or spectrum entropy laws
require the parent task's separately checked decision/regret converse.

6. A GENERAL s-DIMENSIONAL NONPRODUCT UPPER
==========================================

Let a known probability reference have density rho on [-1,1]^s satisfying
0<m<=rho<=M and coordinate Lipschitz bounds Li. Let p_theta=rho g_theta,
g_theta>=0 and int rho g_theta=1. Assume uniformly bounded gradient
coordinates and Hessian operator norm H. No parameterization assumption
or product structure on p_theta or rho is needed.

Use tensor products of the rational graded hats with J_i+1 knots in
coordinate i, and the same reference-posterior decoder

  nu_j(dx)=rho(x) Phi_j(x)dx / int rho Phi_j.

The scalar centroid proof can be integrated over all remaining coordinates
and remains valid, with Bi=4M/m+3Li/(2m). The tensor interpolant reproduces
affine functions. Taylor at tensor nodes gives an averaged remainder at
most (H/2) sum_i (3/J_i)^2; the interpolant remainder is at most
(H/8) sum_i (3/J_i)^2, using its barycentric second moment. Therefore

  ||K g_theta-g_theta||_infinity
   <=sum_i J_i^-2 [Bi||partial_i g_theta||_infinity+(45/8)H].

Half this bounds TV because the reference is a probability. With isotropic
J and D=(J+1)^s, this is O(D^-2/s), uniformly in theta for fixed s and
fixed stated constants. The rate assumption concerns the likelihood ratio
relative to a common known reference, not necessarily the raw density.

7. REFERENCE WITH COMMON JUMPS: BV IMPROVEMENT IN TV
==================================================

This result is weaker in norm than S1, but admits a much rougher reference.
Take a uniform tensor grid of spacing h on a bounded cube, ordinary
linear hats Phi_j, and any probability density r whose zero extension
r0 to R^s is BV. Do not assume r is bounded below, continuous, or bounded.
Let p_theta=r g_theta with smooth g_theta, uniform gradient Euclidean
bound G and Hessian operator norm H. Omit zero-mass labels.

For node z_j set u_{j,i}=int r Phi_j(x)(x_i-z_{j,i})dx. The scalar odd
hat moment has the compactly supported primitive

  B_h(t)=int_{-h}^t u(1-|u|/h)_+du,
  B_h(-h)=B_h(h)=0, B_h<=0,
  sum_{j in Z}|B_h(x-jh)|=h^2/6.

Integration by parts against the distributional derivative D_i r0 and
the tensor partition of unity in all other coordinates gives

  sum_j |u_{j,i}| <= (h^2/6)|D_i r0|(R^s).

The reference masses cancel when integrating the normalized first-moment
bias; this is why no lower bound on r is needed. Interpolation and node
Taylor remainders each contribute at most H s h^2/8 in L1(r). Hence

  TV <= h^2 [ (G/12) sum_i |D_i r0|(R^s) + Hs/8 ].       (BV)

This is a genuinely useful shared-reference improvement: common density
jumps are reproduced exactly and do not force first-order TV loss. In
one dimension with uniform r on the interval and affine g, the boundary
part of this bound is attained by the uniform-hat calculation below.

More generally, mollification of r0 at scale h gives
sum_j |u_{j,i}|<=C h omega_1(r0,h), where omega_1 is its L1 first
translation modulus (coordinatewise variants also work). If
omega_1(r0,h)<=A h^gamma, 0<gamma<=1, the reference-exact error is
O(h^{min(gamma+1,2)}). Here gamma is REFERENCE translation regularity,
not the moving-endpoint density power. Those two exponents must not be
conflated. For moving supports the likelihood ratio itself can be
nonsmooth and a separate modulus argument is required. The parent source
already establishes the exact tapered moving-endpoint law by reflection.

8. BOUNDARY COUNTEREXAMPLE AND OTHER LIMITS
==========================================

For the uniform reference on [0,1], uniform mesh h=1/J, ordinary endpoint
hats, and f(x)=x,

  K_h x-x=(h-x)/3 on [0,h],
          0 on [h,1-h],
          -(x-(1-h))/3 on [1-h,1].

Thus for 1<=p<infinity,

  ||K_h x-x||_p^p=2h^{p+1}/[3^p(p+1)],

and the infinity norm is h/3. The L2 error is order J^-3/2, not J^-2.
Taking likelihood ratios 1+a(x-1/2), |a|<2, turns this into a normalized
positive statistical family. It decisively refutes an ungraded-hat
J^-2 L2 claim even for smooth positive likelihood ratios. The rational
grading fixes this exact failure without signed coefficients.

No positive stationary nontrivial Markov kernel can reproduce all affine
coordinates EXACTLY on a finite-second-moment continuous reference:
stationarity and E[Y|X]=X imply E|Y-X|^2=0. Accordingly, K_J x need not
equal x; the theorem requires a controlled J^-2 drift instead.

The compact compiler does not automatically extend to arbitrary unbounded
references. Compactification can destroy the stated derivative bounds,
and tails need their own weighted/adaptive analysis. The outstanding lemma
for any claimed Gaussian/logistic/Laplace extension is an actual scalar
finite-label construction with both the J^-2 L2 defect and quadratic
Dirichlet estimates, uniformly over its admitted local parameter range.
It cannot be inferred merely from finite moments or reference reversibility.

Also unproved: a sharp converse for every regular density path; exact
runtime/finite-precision emulation under the continuous-output TV metric;
novelty or theorem-level priority for graded weighted splines.

9. IMPLEMENTATION AND COST BOUNDARY
==================================

Encoder: locate the knot interval in O(log J) comparisons using binary
search; only two hat weights are nonzero, so draw a Bernoulli and retain
one of J+1 labels. Endpoints and knot numerators are exact rationals with
O(log J) bits. A knot table has O(J log J) bits; formula evaluation avoids
the table. The decoder program/reference is supplied known side information.

Decoder: propose from the unweighted normalized local hat density
phi_j/int phi_j, then accept with probability rho(y)/M. The acceptance
probability is at least m/M, so at most M/m proposals are needed in
expectation. A local asymmetric triangular proposal can be sampled by
choosing the left/right side with probabilities a/(a+b), b/(a+b), then
using a square root of a uniform variate on that side. Computing m_j is
NOT required for this exact rejection sampler. Thus local reference
evaluation and sampling cost is bounded independently of J under a supplied
exact density-evaluation/continuous-randomness interface.

For a product family, cost is additive across retained coordinates and
retained alphabet size is multiplicative. Storing a joint label requires
ceil(log2 product N_i) bits in a mixed-radix code. This is a retained-label
claim, not an assertion that the whole decoder program or real sampler
has that many bits. A deterministic finite-bit real output is atomic and
has TV one against an absolutely continuous target; continuous random
output remains part of the stated Markov reconstruction interface.
