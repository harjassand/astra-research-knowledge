CYCLE 04 — EFFECTIVE PRIOR FOR FINITE-COST BROADCASTING / EB DEFICITS
Status: primary-source comparison; literature-derived finite-depth baseline, not a new theorem or novelty claim.
Date: 2026-10-09

DECISION

The closest exact prior mechanism is finite de Finetti for symmetric broadcast channels. It gives one common measure-and-prepare approximation to the channel marginal, in diamond norm, with error O(d^2/N) for N permutation-symmetric outputs. Recursively applying a supplied two-output broadcaster produces an N=2^m extension of Phi^m, so the result yields an additive-delta all-Hermitian quadratic-form deficit bound for one EB channel. The derived coefficient is m=O(log(d/delta)), not a universal 4 or 2. The theorem does not provide a bistochastic, Hilbert-Schmidt-self-adjoint canonical EB map, nor a polynomial gate-cost implementation of its collective measurement. It therefore does not establish the proposed fixed-C operator-order theorem.

EXACT FINITE-DEPTH CONSEQUENCE FROM CHIRIBELLA THEOREM 2.2

Let B: M_d -> M_d tensor M_d be a channel whose two marginals are the same bistochastic, Hilbert-Schmidt-self-adjoint channel Phi. Build the depth-m binary tree by applying B at every leaf. It has M=2^m leaves, M-1 uses of B, and every leaf marginal is Phi^m. Average the leaves over a uniformly random permutation; this makes the output channel permutation invariant without changing a leaf marginal. Chiribella's Theorem 2.2 then gives a measure-and-prepare channel whose one-leaf marginal Psi_m is EB and satisfies

    ||Psi_m - Phi^m||_diamond <= 2 d^2 / M.

This is one map for the entire input space and therefore one common measurement for all Hermitian observables; the diamond estimate is stronger than estimates tied to one chosen ensemble or one query. Mixed prepared states in the theorem can be spectrally refined into pure preparations while splitting the corresponding POVM effect. That refinement does not impose unitality or the canonical tracial barycenter.

On the real Hilbert space of Hermitian matrices, a unital trace-preserving CP map is an HS contraction. Self-adjointness gives real eigenvalues lambda in [-1,1], hence 1-lambda^m <= m(1-lambda), or I-Phi^m <= m(I-Phi). For any Hermitian A, the diamond estimate implies

    |tau[A (Phi^m-Psi_m)(A)]| <= sqrt(d) ||Phi^m-Psi_m||_diamond tau(A^2)
                                  <= 2 d^(5/2) 2^(-m) tau(A^2).

Therefore the following derived quadratic-form inequality holds for every Hermitian A:

    tau[A (I-Psi_m)(A)]
        <= m tau[A (I-Phi)(A)] + 2 d^(5/2) 2^(-m) tau(A^2).       (C4.1)

Here “quadratic-form inequality” means the inequality for every A; if Psi_m is not HS-self-adjoint it is the symmetric part of I-Psi_m that the inequality orders. For 0<delta<=1, taking

    m = max(2, ceil(log_2(2 d^(5/2)/delta)))

gives additive error at most delta and multiplicative loss m. The extension requires N=2^m leaves and N-1 calls to B, plus output permutation twirling. This derivation uses only the published diamond bound and elementary channel/HS estimates; C4.1 is not stated by Chiribella.

The output is not the target canonical comparator: the literature theorem guarantees an EB CPTP map, but not that it is unital, HS-self-adjoint, or has the exact tracial pure-state barycenter required by the canonical class. It also approximates Phi^m, not Phi. The error can be driven down at logarithmic loss in the deficit coefficient, but this route does not produce a delta-independent C=4 or C=2. No spectral gap is assumed; adding one would change the target and could bound the power loss by a gap-dependent coefficient.

RESOURCE ACCOUNTING / EXTRACTION LIMIT

The construction is finite in channel calls: M-1 recursive uses of B and a random output permutation. The explicit coherent-state M&P construction in the bosonic case measures all M outputs collectively; for a merely permutation-invariant channel, Theorem 2.2 passes through a Stinespring dilation and a symmetric purification with local dimension d^2. The paper does not give a gate-count or a polynomial-time procedure to compile this collective POVM from a rational Choi matrix, nor a procedure that corrects the output to exact bistochasticity while preserving the deficit inequality. Thus this is an explicit mathematical common-EB extraction theorem with a finite-copy cost, not a verified polynomial-resource instrument construction in the requested model.

NEARBY RESULTS THAT DO NOT CLOSE THE OPERATOR-ORDER GATE

1. Heinosaari–Miyadera, Proposition 12, gives the exact structural endpoint: a channel is N-self-compatible for every N>=2 iff it is EB. Its proof symmetrizes each N-output extension and invokes the finite de Finetti bound ||Lambda-Phi_N||_diamond <= 2d^2/N. This confirms the same dimension-dependent, norm-based extraction mechanism. Two-copy self-compatibility alone is only the premise; the theorem does not turn the supplied two-copy extension into an EB approximation of Phi itself. The binary tree is what changes the target to Phi^m and incurs the factor m.

2. Sutter–Scholz–Winter–Renner, Definition 3.7 and Proposition 3.6, define epsilon-antidegradability by diamond closeness of Phi to Xi composed with its complementary channel and show the optimum is an SDP. This is the closest efficient antidegradable-channel optimization result found. For the supplied self-compatible Phi the condition is exact (epsilon=0); the SDP concerns distance to antidegradability, not distance to EB, and it outputs no common measurement or HS Dirichlet comparison. It therefore cannot be used as an EB rounding theorem.

3. Ye–Bergh–Datta (arXiv:2609.40343v1, 2026-09-30) prove a channel de Finetti CP-order representation for permutation-covariant channels N^(n): A^n -> B^n, with a no-signalling condition on discarded input slots. The hypotheses (their Eqs. 60–63) use n replicated inputs and n outputs; a broadcaster here has one input and many outputs. Their conclusion (Eqs. 89–92) is CP domination / diamond approximation to a mixture of tensor-power channels. Even under its own hypotheses, CP order is a different cone from the real-HS quadratic-form order I-Psi <= C(I-Phi). The input geometry and the order conversion are both missing.

4. Jeronimo–Wu–Xu, “Optimal Quantum de Finetti Theorems via Argmax Rounding” (arXiv:2608.02590v1, 2026-08-03), give an especially close symmetric-extension comparison. Theorem 1.2 gives, for an arbitrary permutation-invariant N-site state on (C^d)^tensor N, a two-site tensor-power-mixture approximation in trace distance at most sqrt(d^2-1)/(2(N-1)); the paper states the matching Omega(d/N) lower-bound order. Theorem 6.1 improves the metric to dimension-free Hilbert–Schmidt error at most 8/sqrt(N-1), but only when the N-site state is supported on Sym^N(C^d); Theorem B.6 gives a matching Omega(N^-1/2) lower bound when d may grow. The paper explicitly says the bosonic Hilbert–Schmidt result does not extend to arbitrary exchangeable states by symmetric purification, because partial trace need not contract Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Our tree gives an output-permutation-invariant channel, not a bosonic-supported output state; its Choi operator also retains a reference input. Thus neither theorem supplies the desired channel superoperator/deficit comparison. The precise gate is bosonic support (or another reference-stable HS estimate) plus exact Choi marginal constraints and conversion from a state-distance estimate to one common EB quadratic-form lower bound. This is a direct geometric warning, not an obstruction to the weaker target inequality.

5. A separate norm-closeness shortcut is ruled out even inside the channel assumptions. For the qubit depolarizing map Phi_lambda(X)=lambda X+(1-lambda)tr(X)I/2 at lambda=2/3, the channel is unital, trace preserving, Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, and antidegradable (hence self-compatible); this is the boundary case in the unital-qubit classification of Cubitt–Ruskai–Smith. Its normalized Choi state has overlap 3/4 with the maximally entangled vector, whereas every separable state has overlap at most 1/2. Consequently every EB channel Psi satisfies ||Phi_lambda-Psi||_diamond >= 1/4, since the diamond norm dominates the trace norm on the normalized maximally entangled input. So self-compatibility cannot imply small diamond distance to EB, even in dimension two. This does not refute an all-Hermitian deficit inequality: the latter permits a large channel distance where I-Phi already has a fixed spectral gap.

BOTTOM LINE FOR THE CANDIDATE C=4 / C=2 CLAIM

There is strong prior art for finite common-EB extraction from large symmetric extensions, with a dimension-dependent diamond error. Combined with repeated broadcasting it gives (C4.1), a useful additive-delta baseline. Recent argmax-rounding de Finetti work gives a dimension-free Hilbert–Schmidt theorem under bosonic support, but its stated route loses control for general exchangeable channel outputs and does not preserve the input reference in the required form. The decisive open conversion in this audit is to remove the logarithmic power loss while preserving a single EB map, and to enforce the exact canonical/bistochastic constraints with an all-Hermitian HS deficit bound. The checked sources state neither C=4 nor C=2 nor the candidate positive-part order. This is a bounded source finding, not a priority or novelty claim.
