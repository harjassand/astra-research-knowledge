# Independent audit of r01 fractional annihilation inequality

Target read: `notes/r01_discovery/fractional_annihilation_derivation.md`, 2026-10-06. Auditor: r05_complexity. No r01 scripts were imported or changed.

Verdict: the mathematical proof appears correct for finite simple undirected graphs with no isolated vertices, with the intermediate fractional-degree inequality valid also when isolates exist. No counterexample or decisive proof gap found. This is an independent handwritten reconstruction and a distinct finite computation, not formal kernel validation or a bibliographic novelty certificate.

## Reconstruction

For any feasible fractional stable-set vector y (0≤y≤1 and y_u+y_v≤1 on edges), summing the edge constraints gives Σ_v d_v y_v≤m. If t=Σ_v y_v and h=floor(t), the cheapest fractional vector of cardinality t fills the h smallest degrees before any larger degree. Hence Σ_{i≤h}d_i≤m and a≥h. Fractional-cover/matching duality gives max Σy=n−ν_f, so a≥floor(n−ν_f). This argument handles tied and zero degrees: fill zero costs freely, then sort positive costs. No assertion of integral optimum is needed.

For isolate-free graphs, a maximum matching covers 2ν vertices; its unmatched vertices form an independent set, so adding one incident edge per unmatched vertex gives an edge cover of size n−ν. An inclusion-minimal edge cover has star components, since every edge must touch a degree-one vertex in the cover subgraph. Taking one edge per star proves the opposite inequality. Thus ec=n−ν and

    ec−a ≤ n−ν−floor(n−ν_f) = ceil(ν_f−ν).

To audit the last bound, take an extreme optimal fractional matching. Unit-weight edges are disjoint. In any connected remaining positive-edge component, positive columns of the tight-vertex incidence matrix must be independent, or a sufficiently small signed perturbation contradicts extremality. A tree has at least two slack leaves and fewer tight rows than edges; a component with at least two cycles has more edges than vertices; a unicyclic component must have all vertices tight and no leaves, hence is a cycle. An even cycle has an alternating kernel vector. An odd cycle forces all weights to 1/2. These statements exhaust connected simple support graphs. Rounding c vertex-disjoint odd cycles loses exactly c/2, giving ν_f−ν≤c/2. Any odd-cycle transversal meets all c support cycles, so c≤oct. The claimed ceiling inequality follows.

One useful sharpening of the downstream interpretation: ν_f is half-integral, so if t=ec−a>0, ceil(ν_f−ν)≥t implies ν_f−ν≥t−1/2, not merely ν_f−ν>t−1. Consequently oct≥2t−1. The displayed theorem already yields this; the explanatory sentence can be sharpened.

## Distinct finite test

`work/r05_complexity/audit_graph.py` computes fractional matching via the bipartite double cover B(G), not by r01's half-integral vertex-cover enumeration. Its maximum matching size is 2ν_f: place each original fractional edge weight on both directed lifted edges for one direction, and average the two lifted matching indicators per original edge for the reverse direction. Bipartite matching integrality completes the equality. The script uses augmenting paths in B(G), subset recursion for ordinary matching, exhaustive deletions/bipartition tests for OCT, and direct sorted-degree annihilation calculation.

All 1,100 labelled graphs of orders 0–5, including isolates, pass the intermediate lemma and the ν_f−ν≤oct/2 bound. Every isolate-free case passes the edge-cover chain. Another 1,000 random graphs of orders 6–10 pass; eight disjoint-triangle families give exact algebraic sharpness. Result: `results/r05_complexity/graph_independent_audit.json`. This is finite diagnostic evidence, not the universal proof.

## Closest verified prior art and scope

Ryan Pepper's four-page 2009 paper, *On the Annihilation Number of a Graph*, [publisher PDF](https://www.wseas.us/e-library/conferences/2009/houston/AAMCIS1/AAMCIS1-33.pdf), Theorem 3, proves α≤a using the original annihilation process. Its conclusion compares a against n−ν and spectral bounds; it does not state the fractional chain. The full primary PDF was read through the web tool. Direct snapshot download returned HTTP403, so no local PDF is claimed. The original 2004 thesis was not recovered or read.

Larson–Pepper, *Graphs with equal Independence and Annihilation Numbers*, published 9 September 2011, [publisher PDF](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v18i1p180/pdf), Definition 1.1/Theorem 1.2, records the equivalent smallest-degree characterization and α≤a. Only introduction/definitions inspected here; later characterization claims are outside this audit and later literature reports corrections.

Crucial prior-art caution for the proposed discovery experiment: Larson–Van Cleemput, *Automated Conjecturing I: Fajtlowicz's Dalmatian Heuristic Revisited (Extended Abstract)*, IJCAI2017, [primary PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/2017/0713.pdf), Section 4, explicitly lists both fractional independence number and annihilation number among the supplied invariant library. Its tables separately conjecture domination≤fractional independence and domination≤annihilation. The abstract identifies this as an extended summary of their 2016 Artificial Intelligence paper. Therefore supplying or using fractional independence in an automated graph-conjecturing system is established prior art. **Acquiring** the relaxation from a hidden-invariant interface could still be a discriminating experiment, but the basic explanatory variable is not historically new.
