LEAF 19 — NON-CUTOFF BOLTZMANN REGULARIZATION
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: BANKED SOURCE THEOREM + INTERNALLY PROVED FOURIER COROLLARY; no new theorem of global existence or hydrodynamic convergence.

Question and scope
For a specified three-dimensional non-cutoff kernel, what fractional coercivity follows from the primary linearized theorem, how do its constants scale with the angular amplitude, equilibrium density and temperature, and spatial torus length, and what is the strongest conclusion that can safely be drawn?

Kernel and conventions
Fix 0<s<1 and use the Maxwell-molecule radial factor. In the sigma representation set

  B(z,theta) = a theta^(-2-2s),  a>0, 0<theta<=pi,

with theta the deviation angle; the usual symmetrization may restrict collisions to theta<=pi/2. More generally, the proof below allows a kernel bounded below by a_- theta^(-2-2s) and above by a_+ theta^(-2-2s), with 0<a_-<=a_+<infinity. The radial factor can be generalized to Phi(r) comparable to r^gamma, -3<gamma<=1, but the Fourier-mode corollary below is stated for gamma=0. The angular measure in dimension three is sin(theta)dtheta, so its small-angle mass behaves as theta^(-1-2s)dtheta and is non-integrable for every s>0.

Linearize in the relative perturbation convention F=M(1+h), with M(v)=exp(-|v|^2) for the normalized source theorem. The Hilbert space is L^2(M dv), the collision-invariant projection P has range span{1,v_1,v_2,v_3,|v|^2}, and

  D(h) = -<L_B h,h>_{L^2(M)}
       = (1/4) integral B (h'+h_*'-h-h_*)^2 M M_* dv dv_* d sigma.

The result is stated for h in the operator domain. Complex Fourier modes are covered by applying the real estimate to real and imaginary parts.

Primary theorem and exact assumptions
Mouhot, “Explicit coercivity estimates for the linearized Boltzmann and Landau operators,” arXiv:math/0607538v1 (21 July 2006), published in Communications in Partial Differential Equations 31 (2006), 1321–1348, DOI 10.1080/03605300600635004, Theorem 1.2. In its notation B=Phi(|v-v_*|)b(cos theta), with N>=2, -N<gamma<=1, positive two-sided radial bounds C_Phi r^gamma <= Phi(r) <= C'_Phi r^gamma, and, for some 0<alpha<2, positive two-sided angular bounds c_b theta^(-(N-1)-alpha) <= b(cos theta) <= c'_b theta^(-(N-1)-alpha). For N=3 take alpha=2s. The theorem is for the linearized collision operator about a fixed Maxwellian, with no x or t variable in its statement.

The theorem gives both the weighted L^2 coercivity from its Theorem 1.1 and, for every h in D(L_B),

  D(h) >= C_{gamma,alpha} ||h-P h||^2_{H^(alpha/2)_loc(R^N)}.

For the selected kernel this is local H^s_v regularity. Its constant is constructive and depends on the radial and angular kernel bounds, gamma, alpha and N. “Local” matters: for each fixed ball B_R the proof yields a constant c_R>0 for the H^s(B_R) norm. It is not a single uniform bound over velocity balls of unbounded radius. The paper remarks that its explicit c_R may grow exponentially in R. The paper explicitly says it could not prove the stronger global weighted H^s estimate it describes as a natural conjecture.

Proof of the source estimate, in the relevant scope
The source proof first subtracts P h, which does not change D. It restricts the collision integral to a bounded velocity region using a smooth cutoff I_R. Expanding the square and applying pre/post-collisional changes of variables gives a positive jump term I_1^R and a cancellation remainder I_2^R. Carleman representation bounds the jump term below by

  I_1^R >= c_1(R,s) integral_{B_R x B_R}
          |h(v')-h(v)|^2 / |v-v'|^(3+2s) dv dv'
        >= c_2(R,s) ||h||^2_{H^s(B_R)}.

For the remainder, the cancellation change of variables averages over directions orthogonal to v-v_*. The first-order Taylor term vanishes by symmetry, leaving a second-order difference. Since the post-collisional displacement has size comparable to tan(theta/2)|v-v_*|, the angular error is controlled near zero by an integral proportional to

  integral_0^(pi/2) theta^(-2-2s) theta^2 sin(theta) dtheta
  ~ integral_0^1 theta^(1-2s) dtheta = 1/(2-2s),

which is finite exactly for s<1. Thus |I_2^R| <= C_4(R,s)||h||^2_{L^2(M)}. The theorem's spectral-gap estimate for Maxwell molecules gives ||h||^2_{L^2(M)} <= C_5 D(h) for h orthogonal to the invariants. Combining yields

  ||h||^2_{H^s(B_R)} <= c_2(R,s)^(-1)(1+C_4(R,s)C_5)D(h).

This reconstructs the estimate needed below. The angular integrability calculation also exposes a proof-constant loss of order at most (1-s)^(-1) in the cancellation error as s increases to 1; it does not establish optimal asymptotics for the best coercivity constant. The endpoint s=1 is excluded. The exponent s itself is the fractional derivative order, not a uniform-in-s constant.

Fourier-mode coercivity on a torus (proved corollary)
Let T_L^3=(R/LZ)^3 and suppose the reference Maxwellian and collision kernel are independent of x. For h(x,v) in L^2(T_L^3;D(L_B)), use normalized spatial measure and Fourier coefficients

  h_hat_m(v) = L^(-3) integral_{T_L^3} h(x,v) exp(-2 pi i m.x/L) dx,
  k_m = 2 pi m/L.

Because L_B acts only on v, Parseval gives

  L^(-3) integral_{T_L^3} D(h(x,.)) dx = sum_{m in Z^3} D(h_hat_m).

Apply the primary theorem to each coefficient (or separately to its real and imaginary parts), with the same velocity projection P. For every fixed R<infinity,

  L^(-3) integral_{T_L^3} D(h(x,.)) dx
    >= c_R(s,a) sum_{m in Z^3}
         ||(I-P)h_hat_m||^2_{H^s(B_R)}.                 (1)

The constant in (1) does not depend on L or m. The torus length changes only the frequency lattice k_m=2 pi m/L. The theorem controls microscopic velocity regularity; it provides no x derivative and no coercivity on the local hydrodynamic projection P h.

Constant dependence
1. Angular singularity and kernel amplitude. For the exact kernel b=a theta^(-2-2s), D_a=a D_1, so the best constant in (1) is exactly linear in a. For a general kernel with lower bound a_- theta^(-2-2s), and Phi(r)>=C_Phi r^gamma, positivity of the Dirichlet integrand gives D_B>=C_Phi a_- D_ref; hence one valid lower constant is C_Phi a_- times the reference-kernel constant. The upper bound a_+ is needed to specify the admissible class and to control cancellation terms in the source proof. The source gives constructive dependence on both angular comparison constants and on s, but no tabulated optimal formula. In the proof, the second-order angular remainder has size O((1-s)^(-1)); no uniform claim at s=1 follows. As s decreases to 0, the controlled order s decreases to zero.

2. Density, mean velocity and temperature. For a constant equilibrium

  M_{rho,u,T}(v)=rho(2 pi T)^(-3/2)exp(-|v-u|^2/(2T)),
  lambda=sqrt(2T), z=(v-u)/lambda,

the source's exact change of variables gives

  D_{rho,u,T}(h) = rho^2/pi^3 * lambda^gamma
                    D_base(h(u+lambda z)).             (2)

For gamma=0, if the H^s norm is measured on the normalized z-ball B_R, (1)'s coefficient is therefore rho^2/pi^3 times the normalized coefficient: it is independent of T and u. With rho in [rho_-,rho_+] and rho_->0, the uniform lower coefficient is proportional to rho_-^2. There can be no density-independent positive coefficient as rho approaches zero: for any fixed non-invariant h, (2) makes its dissipation proportional to rho^2.

If instead the local norm is written in physical v coordinates, the temperature dependence reappears through the patch and the Sobolev scaling. With the Gagliardo norm

  ||q||^2_{H^s(B)} = ||q||^2_{L^2(B)} + [q]^2_{H^s(B)},

one has exactly

  ||h(u+lambda .)||^2_{H^s(B_R)}
      = lambda^(-3)||h||^2_{L^2(B_{lambda R}(u))}
        +lambda^(2s-3)[h]^2_{H^s(B_{lambda R}(u))}.

Thus for gamma=0 the physical-patch estimate has coefficients rho^2 lambda^(-3) on the local L^2 term and rho^2 lambda^(2s-3) on the fractional seminorm, with the physical patch radius lambda R. Bounds 0<T_-<=T<=T_+ make these factors uniformly bounded above and below on the stated interval. For general gamma, multiply both factors by lambda^gamma as in (2). The source separately notes that the simple scaling formula for its local Sobolev norm is less direct than for its weighted L^2 estimate; the displayed formula is the direct Gagliardo change of variables used here.

3. Torus length. In the normalized spatial-average formulation, the coercivity constant in (1) has no L dependence. With unnormalized dx, both sides acquire the same L^3 factor. Any estimate that tries to control x variation or the macro part requires transport/hypocoercivity and can depend on L.

Explicit degenerating Fourier sequence for full kinetic coercivity
Let A_k=L_B+i v.k be the Fourier-mode generator in the relative-perturbation convention, and let k_L=(2 pi/L)e_1. Set h_L(x,v)=sin(2 pi x_1/L), independent of v. Its only nonzero spatial modes are collision invariants, so D(h_L)=0 and (I-P)h_hat=0; this is consistent with (1) and refutes any version of (1) that drops I-P or claims collisions directly damp density modes.

For the stronger proposed estimate ||A_k q||_{L^2(M)}>=c||q||_{L^2(M)} uniform over nonzero spatial modes and all L, take the single Fourier coefficient q(v)=1. Since L_B 1=0,

  ||A_{k_L}1||_{L^2(M)}/||1||_{L^2(M)}
     = |k_L| ||v_1||_{L^2(M)}/||1||_{L^2(M)}
     = |k_L|/sqrt(2) = sqrt(2) pi/L.

The ratio tends to zero as L tends to infinity. Hence no torus-uniform lower-bound coercivity estimate for the full streaming-collision operator can hold on all modes. This is an approximate-null-vector calculation, not a proof of a sharp semigroup decay rate; a separate hypocoercivity argument is required for such a rate. The familiar mean-zero torus Poincare constant also scales as (L/(2 pi))^2, matching the low-frequency obstruction.

Comparison and interpretation
The strongest same-interface baseline found is Gressman–Strain, “Global Classical Solutions of the Boltzmann Equation without Angular Cut-off,” arXiv:1011.5441v1 (24 November 2010), JAMS 24 (2011), DOI 10.1090/S0894-0347-2011-00697-8. Their angular assumption in dimension n=3 is sin(theta)b(cos theta) comparable to theta^(-1-2s), equivalent to b~theta^(-2-2s); their radial kernel is C_Phi|z|^gamma. Their Lemma 2.5 proves coercivity in the global anisotropic geometric fractional norm N^{s,gamma}, up to a compact-region L^2 remainder for velocity weight ell, and without that remainder when ell=0. This norm uses the lifted-paraboloid distance, so it is stronger and more geometrically adapted than the local isotropic H^s bound here. Their Theorem 1.1 separately proves global strong solutions near a Maxwellian on T^n for hard potentials gamma>=-2s, with conservation constraints, small initial perturbation, sufficient derivatives (in n=3 at least two spatial derivatives), and exponential decay. The selected gamma=0 kernel is included. These are established results, not consequences newly obtained by the Fourier summation above.

Hydrodynamic convergence is a further separate theorem. Cao–Carrapatoso, “Hydrodynamic limit for the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation,” arXiv:2304.06362v3 (19 March 2024), studies the epsilon-rescaled equation on normalized T^3 / R^3 with a perturbative global mild-solution theory. Its torus Theorem 2.3 additionally requires smallness, convergence of kinetic initial data to a well-prepared fluid datum, and the NSF compatibility conditions div u_0=0 and rho_0+theta_0=0; it proves strong convergence in L^infinity_t L^2_v L^1_x-Fourier. The linear local coercivity theorem alone supplies none of the nonlinear, uniform-in-epsilon, initial-data, or fluid-equation gates needed for that limit.

Supplied versus acquired; costs; comparator; falsifier
Supplied: exact kernel family, fixed homogeneous Maxwellian parameters, periodic spatial domain, and the input function h in the collision-operator domain. Acquired: the two primary theorem statements and their proof hypotheses from the cited papers. No oracle, sample, or external service is required. The functional input is infinite-dimensional; the proof has no finite-data or machine-precision cost. The constant is constructive at fixed s and R, but this note does not numerically extract it. The source proof reports exponential-in-R growth for local ball constants; the cancellation integral exhibits a (1-s)^(-1) endpoint loss in this proof. The direct Fourier lift adds no numerical conditioning or cost.

Comparator: the Gressman–Strain global anisotropic N^{s,gamma} coercivity estimate and their separate near-equilibrium global existence theorem. Our local H^s Fourier corollary is weaker and is recorded as a scoped reuse of the primary theorem.

Falsifier for (1): exhibit an admissible h and a fixed finite R for which D(h) / ||(I-P)h||^2_{H^s(B_R)} approaches zero, or show that the collision form fails to commute with spatial Fourier decomposition despite the assumed x-independent kernel and equilibrium. Falsifier for the L-scaling statement: violate the explicit k_L calculation above. A sequence rho_n->0 is instead a confirmation of the necessity of a positive density lower bound, since it makes the coefficient vanish like rho_n^2.

Unresolved gates and scientific status
The published local-regularity theorem and its assumptions are source-established. The Fourier-mode inequality, scaling conversion and degenerating sequence are elementary internal proofs from that source theorem. No novelty or priority claim is made. The best constant's optimal asymptotics in s, exact numerical extraction for a chosen R, spatially varying Maxwellians (which couple Fourier modes), a torus-uniform hypocoercive semigroup rate, and any extension from this coercivity statement to nonlinear global well-posedness or hydrodynamic convergence remain outside this result.
