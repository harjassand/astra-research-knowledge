N04 CELLULAR SENSING — OCCUPANCY-OPTIMIZED PREDICTION/WORK FRONTIER v4
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: exact derivation in a restricted two-state Markov sensor class; finite-tested by a standard-library script. Append-only extension to v1–v3. This is not a new universal thermodynamic law and no biological mechanism has been empirically confirmed.

Question and added result
The earlier records fixed a symmetric two-state reporter and derived an exact finite-horizon accuracy/work lower envelope. Here the reporter's mean occupancy is allowed to vary while the exogenous signal, fixed-input relaxation rate, and Bayesian forecast error stay fixed. This gives an exact one-parameter family linking three quantities that error alone does not determine: causal predictive information, stationary path irreversibility, and reporter occupancy.

For a symmetric telegraph environment with flip rate lambda and a binary reporter with fixed-input relaxation rate R, write Delta=u_+-u_->0 and m=P(M=1). At fixed Delta, the Bayes accuracy for predicting the environment tau time units ahead is independent of m. The occupancy parameter is feasible exactly when q<m<1-q, where q=Delta/(2R). Over this family, both the minimal chemical power and predictive mutual information are minimized at m=1/2 and strictly increase with |m-1/2| for every finite forecast horizon. The lower frontier is attained by a chemically consistent autonomous network. At m=1/2 it uses one ATP/ADP fuel pair; away from m=1/2 an attaining realization can use two state-conditioned fuel chemostats. Hidden parallel pathways can raise actual work arbitrarily without changing any observed path law, so this remains a sharp lower frontier, not an exact universal cost assignment.

Model and exact stationary law
Let E_t in {+,-} be an exogenous symmetric continuous-time telegraph signal. It flips in either direction at rate lambda>0, independent of M, with no work or chemical affinity on these flips. Let M_t in {0,1} be a reporter. Its conditional transitions are

    u_+ = R(m+q),       d_+ = R(1-m-q),
    u_- = R(m-q),       d_- = R(1-m+q),

where u_e is M:0->1, d_e is M:1->0, R=u_e+d_e is the fixed-input relaxation rate, Delta=2Rq=u_+-u_->0, and m=(u_++u_-)/(2R). Strictly positive rates require 0<q<1/2 and q<m<1-q. In particular, this family keeps the response relaxation time 1/R and the rate difference Delta fixed while changing the reporter's basal occupancy.

Set q_e=P(M=1|E=e), and c=q_+-q_-. The stationary conditional master equations give

    (lambda+R)q_+ - lambda q_- = u_+,
    -lambda q_+ + (lambda+R)q_- = u_- .

Their solution is

    q_+ = m+c/2,       q_- = m-c/2,
    c = Delta/(R+2 lambda) = 2Rq/(R+2 lambda).

Thus the stationary joint law in state order (+0,+1,-0,-1) is

    p = ((1-m)/2-c/4, m/2+c/4,
         (1-m)/2+c/4, m/2-c/4).

The environment is uniform, P(M=1)=m, and the four-state process has a single square cycle. All stationary probabilities are positive under the strict rate conditions above.

Finite-horizon error and predictive memory
The telegraph semigroup has E[E_(t+tau)|E_t]=exp(-2 lambda tau) E_t. For the optimal binary decision from M_t, the Bayes accuracy and error are

    A_tau = 1/2 + (c/2) exp(-2 lambda tau),
    epsilon_tau = 1/2 - (c/2) exp(-2 lambda tau).

They are independent of m. To see this directly, the difference between the two joint probabilities for the decision M=1 is c exp(-2 lambda tau)/2, and the complementary decision M=0 contributes the same difference; their sum fixes the Bayes accuracy. The analysis horizon tau is only used to score prediction. The network is autonomous, has no clock, and performs no tau-dependent operation.

For binary entropy h(x)=-x ln x-(1-x)ln(1-x), define w_tau=(c/2)exp(-2 lambda tau). Then

    I_tau(m) := I(M_t;E_(t+tau))
      = h(m) - [h(m+w_tau)+h(m-w_tau)]/2.

The Markov state E_t is a sufficient statistic for the entire future of E. Hence the causal predictive memory about the full future is

    I_causal(m) := I(M_t;E_(t:infinity)) = I(M_t;E_t)
      = h(m) - [h(m+c/2)+h(m-c/2)]/2.

At m=1/2, the reporter is a binary symmetric channel and

    I_tau(1/2) = ln 2 - h(epsilon_tau),

which saturates the binary Fano lower bound at fixed Bayes error. For general m, the same accuracy may correspond to more predictive mutual information. H(M)=h(m) is maximal at m=1/2; it is a distinct quantity from I_causal and I_tau.

Observed path irreversibility
The stationary circulation around the square is

    j = lambda*c/2.

The cycle affinity, using the orientation +0->+1->-1->-0->+0, is

    A_square(m) = ln[(u_+ d_-)/(d_+ u_-)]
      = ln[((m+q)(1-m+q))/((1-m-q)(m-q))].

Therefore the observed four-state path entropy-production rate is

    sigma_obs/kB = j A_square(m)
      = (lambda*c/2) ln[((m+q)(1-m+q))/((1-m-q)(m-q))].

This is a stationary path-space quantity, not the mutual-information rate and not a measurement/reset cost. If all unobserved chemical work channels are included and E-flips are zero-work, local detailed balance gives beta*P_chem >= sigma_obs/kB. The log-sum inequality proves this coarse-graining bound edge by edge. Equality holds when each observed edge has one thermodynamic channel, or more generally when all channels lumped onto that edge have the same forward/reverse traffic ratio.

Exact occupancy optimization at fixed accuracy and response rate
At fixed lambda,R,Delta (equivalently fixed c and tau-error), q and c are fixed while m ranges over (q,1-q). Differentiating the cycle affinity gives

    dA_square/dm = 2q[1/((1-m)^2-q^2) - 1/(m^2-q^2)].

Its sign is the sign of m-1/2. The affinity has a unique minimum at m=1/2 and diverges at both feasible endpoints. Thus the smallest observed irreversibility, and the smallest physically allowed chemical power, occur at balanced occupancy.

The information has the same ordering. For finite tau, w_tau>0, and

    dI_tau/dm = (1/2) ln[((1-m)^2(m^2-w_tau^2)) /
                         (m^2((1-m)^2-w_tau^2))].

The difference between the numerator and denominator inside the logarithm is w_tau^2(2m-1), so the derivative is negative below 1/2, zero at 1/2, and positive above 1/2. The same proof with w_0=c/2 applies to I_causal. Consequently, within this rate family and at fixed forecast accuracy, response rate, and environment law, both predictive mutual information and the sharp minimum work increase strictly with |m-1/2|. They are even in m-1/2, so they give a one-to-one parametric work/information curve on either occupancy branch. This statement is model-family-specific; it is not a general equality between work and predictive information.

At the optimizer m=1/2, define a=Delta/R=2q. Then

    A_square(1/2)=2 ln[(1+a)/(1-a)],
    beta*P_min = sigma_obs/kB
       = lambda*c ln[(1+a)/(1-a)],
    a = ((R+2 lambda)/R)c.

For a noncentral occupancy, the exact additional observed-path dissipation above the balanced realization is

    beta*P_occ(m) = (lambda*c/2)[A_square(m)-A_square(1/2)] >= 0.

This is a lower-bound penalty from the observed generator itself. A real multichannel reaction network adds the nonnegative hidden-channel term described in v2; that term cannot be inferred from the state path law.

For a finite-horizon error epsilon_tau, c=exp(2 lambda tau)(1-2 epsilon_tau), so the sharp global-in-m lower envelope is

    beta*P_min(epsilon_tau;lambda,R,tau)
      = lambda*c ln[(1+((R+2 lambda)/R)c) /
                     (1-((R+2 lambda)/R)c)],
    c = exp(2 lambda tau)(1-2 epsilon_tau),

for 0<c<R/(R+2 lambda). At c=0 the independent reporter has zero work; the upper endpoint of c is approached only as rates vanish and the required affinity/work diverges. The occupancy-optimized formula agrees with v3. This record adds the proof that it is the global minimum over all reporter means compatible with the same response rate and predictive accuracy.

Attainable autonomous chemical constructions
For m=1/2, the conditional rate ratios are reciprocal:

    u_+/d_+ = exp(A),       u_-/d_- = exp(-A),
    A = ln[(1+a)/(1-a)].

Use the same ATP/ADP/Pi reservoir on the two M-transition routes, with the E+ catalyst conformation coupling M:0->1 to ATP hydrolysis and the E- conformation coupling M:0->1 to the reverse ATP-synthesis reaction. Give the two routes equal endpoint standard free-energy differences (zero in this parameterization), choose their chemostat affinity to be A, and select positive reversible kinetic prefactors to set the specified rates. The E catalyst is unchanged by M transitions. E flips remain symmetric with rate lambda, so they exchange no work. The net fuel hydrolysis current is J_fuel=J_+-J_-=lambda*c, where J_+=lambda*c/2 and J_-=-lambda*c/2. Hence beta*P=J_fuel*A=lambda*c*A=sigma_obs/kB. This attains the global lower envelope with a finite-state, autonomous, locally detailed-balanced reaction network and one shared fuel couple.

For m other than 1/2, an equality construction uses one reversible route on each E-conditioned M edge, with separate fixed chemostat affinities A_+=ln(u_+/d_+) and A_-=ln(u_-/d_-). The stationary currents are J_+=j and J_-=-j, so total chemical power is J_+A_+ + J_-A_-=j(A_+-A_-)=j A_square. Both reservoirs and their opposite-direction fluxes are included. This is again autonomous and has no controller. A single shared ATP/ADP couple with unchanged reporter standard energy instead enforces the reciprocal ratios and therefore selects m=1/2; away from that point, a one-fuel implementation needs additional energetic couplings and their fluxes must be counted. The two-fuel construction makes the full parametric lower frontier attainable while making this boundary explicit.

The strict endpoints m=q and m=1-q are excluded because u_-=0 or d_+=0, respectively. They can be approached by positive-rate networks. The finite-horizon information approaches

    I_tau,max = h(q) - [h(q+w_tau)+h(q-w_tau)]/2,

while A_square and the minimal chemical power diverge. Thus no finite affinity realizes that endpoint. This is a limit statement, not a finite-work equality.

Hidden pathway counterexample and scope
The lower envelope does not identify actual work in a biochemical network. v1/v2 give two three-channel CRNs with exactly the same entire observed (E,M) path law, response rate, reporter entropy, finite-horizon error, and predictive information, but different ATP powers. In the base example the powers are (61/120)kBT ln 4 and (79/120)kBT ln 4, while a single-route realization of the same balanced observed law attains (1/5)kBT ln 2. v3 gives an unbounded-excess family when chemical affinities are allowed to grow. Therefore no estimator using only the observed input/reporter trajectories can recover total reaction-resolved ATP work across those network realizations. The exact result here is a sharp lower bound plus an attainable network, not an exact work assignment for all cells.

The prediction score is Bayesian accuracy and mutual information under a known telegraph input law. It is not a behavioral utility, an inference of a natural pathway, or an empirical measure of adaptation. The binary reporter is a sufficient state for the sensor's internal memory in this model; more complicated intracellular states can contain memory not represented by M.

Preparation, finite records, and observation boundary
All stationary probabilities and power rates above apply after the process has reached its stationary regime. The input can be prepared in its uniform stationary distribution. Reaching the joint stationary state from another initial distribution has a preparation transient whose work depends on the initial condition and network; it is not included in ongoing power. A finite experiment must add that burn-in cost and any readout energy. The formulas make a forecast at time t and score it using E_(t+tau); they do not require the cell or experimenter to reset M at t+tau. The final tau-long segment of a finite input record must be treated as unscored or censored unless its future is observed. No reset occurs in the network. A fluorescence readout, persistent memory, and physical reset each add their own energetic terms. If an external device drives E away from the assumed autonomous telegraph law, its work must also be counted.

- Barato, Hartich & Seifert, “Information-theoretic vs. thermodynamic entropy production in autonomous sensory networks,” New Journal of Physics 16, 103024 (2014), primary preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3186v3, Sections II–IV and Eqs. 3–4, 18, 21–23. Their Section II two-state-input/two-state-sensor toy model is exactly the balanced m=1/2 subfamily after identifying their correct/false rates with u_+,d_+ and u_-,d_-; Eqs. 3–4 give the same square-cycle stationary EPR. Their general bipartite model has an exogenous stochastic input changing internal rates, explicitly sums thermodynamically distinct channels, and finds no universal upper bound of information acquisition rate by dissipation. The symmetric architecture and its thermodynamic accounting are direct prior art.
- Marzen & Crutchfield, “Prediction and Power in Molecular Sensors: Uncertainty and Dissipation When Conditionally Markovian Channels Are Driven by Semi-Markov Environments,” arXiv:1707.03962v1 (2017), primary text https://arxiv.org/html/1707.03962v1, Sections II–IV and Eqs. 4–11. They derive predictive information and power for a broad conditionally Markovian sensor family and Markov/semi-Markov inputs. The telegraph sensor and information/power formulas here are a closed-form specialization and an added optimization over reporter occupancy, not a new general framework.
- Tjalma et al., “Trade-offs between cost and information in cellular prediction,” PNAS 120, e2303078120 (2023), https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2303078120, primary article text, especially Eqs. 2–7 and the Markov-signal analysis. Their Gaussian Markov input and linear-noise cellular-network model optimize predictive information against protein synthesis and operating costs. Those metrics and the resource boundary differ from the exact reaction-channel EPR optimization here, but the broad cost/prediction question and biological relevance are established.
- Freitas & Esposito, “Emergent second law for non-equilibrium steady states,” Nature Communications 13, 5084 (2022), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32700-7, primary article Eqs. 25–26. They show that distinct thermodynamic channels can generate the same observed master equation and that coarse-grained entropy production is a lower bound. This is the established hidden-work obstruction used here.

The exact binary telegraph specialization, its occupancy optimization, and the Fano equality point do not support a priority claim. A broader literature search could still uncover an exact prior derivation of this one-parameter family. No empirical article checked here validates the proposed ATP reaction architecture in a natural cell.
