1. Exact statistical and computational model

Let d>=1, x* in R^d, q=||x*||>0, and u=q^2. The m original clean pairs are iid and satisfy
  a_i~N(0,I_d), y_i=(a_i^T x*)^2+zeta_i,
  E[zeta_i|a_i]=0, E[zeta_i^2|a_i]<=sigma^2.
No noise fourth moment, symmetry, or independence from a_i is assumed. After seeing every clean pair, an adversary replaces at most floor(eps m) entire pairs with arbitrary finite real pairs. The received sample is fixed before new private learner seeds. The adversary may depend arbitrarily on the clean sample and may encode later or all clean observations in its replacements.

Supplied: eps>=0, R>=sigma/q^2, delta in (0,1/4), p in (0,1/10], and the one corrupted sample. The original bounded-fourth-moment input r_up>=K4/q^2 supplies R=r_up by conditional Cauchy--Schwarz. Acquired: h approximating q^2, the initial direction/amplitude, and every gradient estimate. Neither q nor sigma is supplied. The phase metric is dist(x,x*)=min(||x-x*||,||x+x*||).

The theorem counts real arithmetic operations in the model of its two imported algorithms, including the usual elementary square-root and private-random-draw access. It separately charges finding the acquired dyadic scale and rounding coordinates below. This is not a bounded-bit or implementation-performance theorem.

3. Initializer construction and full proof

Partition the first K_s floor(m/K_s) rows into K_s equal disjoint blocks. Set h=max(0,median of corrupted-response block averages). Var(y)<=2u^2+sigma^2<=(2+R^2)u^2. For each original clean block, Chebyshev gives bad probability <=1/8 at threshold sqrt(8(2+R^2)u^2/b), where b=floor(m/K_s)>=m/(2K_s). Hoeffding gives at most K_s/4 bad clean blocks, except with probability exp(-K_s/32)<=delta_s/2. At most eps m<=K_s/32 blocks can be modified under any allowed replacement. A strict majority of received block averages remain close to u. Consequently, simultaneously over every replacement,
  |h-u|<=4u sqrt((2+R^2)[32eps+(32log(2/delta_s)+1)/m]).
Condition (SC) implies h in [(1-nu)u,(1+nu)u]; in particular h>0 on this event. The partition is predetermined by indices. No independence between h and the remaining stages is required.

For L=A h define
  g_L(y)=L+clip(y,[-L,L]) in [0,2L],
  X_L=xi sqrt(g_L(y))a,
where xi is an optional independent learner sign. There is no norm cutoff on a. Signs center the underlying clean vector law but do not center a finite empirical sample; only raw outer products are used.

The intended PSD target is
  S_L=(L+u)I+2x*x*^T,
whose eigenvalues are L+3u and L+u, with gap2u. Conditional centering and Gaussian moments give E[y aa^T]=uI+2x*x*^T. Also
  E[y^2 aa^T] <= (3u^2+sigma^2)I+12u x*x*^T,
so its operator norm is at most 15u^2+sigma^2. Since |clip_L(y)-y|<=y^2/L,
  ||Q_L-S_L||op <=(15u^2+sigma^2)/L,
  Q_L=E[g_L(y)aa^T].                                    (3.1)
S_L, not Q_L, is the stability target. The bias is paid on the left of (IG); subtracting the bias again from lambda_min(S_L) would be unnecessary.

Uniform fractional deletion: for every k-subset I, the k by d_+ Gaussian design matrix obeys Pr(||A_I||op>sqrt(k)+sqrt(d_+)+sqrt(2t))<=exp(-t). A union bound over at most (em/k)^k subsets gives G_k with failure delta_D. For any fixed unit v, maximizing sum w_i(v^Ta_i)^2 subject to 0<=w_i<=1,sum w_i<=beta m uses at most k coordinates, with at most one fractional coefficient. Its maximum is at most the largest k-coordinate sum. Taking sup_v gives, simultaneously for every adaptive fractional deletion,
  ||m^(-1)sum w_i a_i a_i^T||op<=G_k.
Since 0<=g_L<=2L, deleted feature moments are <=2Lmax G_k for every L<=Lmax, where Lmin=A(1-nu)u,Lmax=A(1+nu)u. The weights may depend on every pair, every response, and the selected L.

Uniform full covariance: for fixed unit v and L, Z=g_L(y)(v^Ta)^2 is pointwise bounded by 2L(v^Ta)^2. For integers j>=2,
  E|Z-EZ|^j<=2^j E Z^j<=(8L)^j j!.
Bernstein's moment bound then gives scalar deviation 16L[sqrt(t/m)+t/m] with two-sided failure <=2exp(-t). A sphere 1/4-net of size <=9^(d_+) converts this to operator deviation
  E_m=32Lmax[sqrt(t_C/m)+t_C/m]
for J deterministic thresholds, with failure delta_C. The Gaussian full design event gives ||Ahat||op<=H_m with failure delta_A. As L -> g_L(y) is 2-Lipschitz pointwise, interpolating a threshold mesh of spacing Delta=Delta_rel u gives
  sup_{L in [Lmin,Lmax]} ||Qhat_L-Q_L||op
    <=E_m+2Delta(H_m+1).                                (3.2)
This mesh is only used in the proof. Its deterministic interval depends on the fixed true u, while its upper cardinality J is known. The clean-data event is uniform over the entire interval and hence covers adversarially/data-selected h.

For retention weights w_i in [0,1], let a=m^(-1)sum(1-w_i)<=beta and D=m^(-1)sum(1-w_i)g_L(y_i)a_i a_i^T. The normalized retained moment is (Qhat_L-D)/(1-a). Therefore
  ||(Qhat_L-D)/(1-a)-S_L||op
  <=[E_m+2Delta(H_m+1)+(15u^2+sigma^2)/Lmin
        +2Lmax G_k+a||S_L||op]/(1-a)
  <=gamma(Lmin+u)<=gamma lambda_min(S_L),                (3.3)
where (IG) is exactly a conservative version of this inequality after division by u and sigma^2/u^2<=R^2. This proves (1-gamma)S_L <= Q_w <= (1+gamma)S_L in Loewner order. It proves full-clean (21eps_alg,gamma) stability, including fractional deletion.

After the actual r<=eps<=eps_alg fraction of clean rows is replaced, deleting a further 20eps_alg fraction of the surviving empirical distribution costs at most r+(1-r)20eps_alg<=21eps_alg of the original clean mass. Thus the survivor law G is (20eps_alg,gamma)-stable relative to the SAME S_L. Write the received uniform empirical law as P=(1-r)G+rB. It also has the exact padding decomposition
  P=(1-eps_alg)G+eps_alg B',
  B'=(r/eps_alg)B+[(eps_alg-r)/eps_alg]G.
This establishes the source's positive-contamination mixture interface without requiring that eps_alg m be an integer or that extra original rows really be changed.

5. Population nonlinear geometry and finite-variance score covariance

Define the score P_x=((a^Tx)^2-y)(a^Tx)a. Gaussian fourth moments and conditional noise centering yield
  g(x)=E P_x=(3||x||^2-u)x-2<x,x*>x*,
  H(x)=Dg(x)=(3||x||^2-u)I+6xx^T-2x*x*^T.             (5.1)
These are the gradient and Hessian of one fourth of the population quadratic-measurement squared loss. The finite noise variance adds a constant to that loss.

Let z*=+x* or -x*, e=||x-z*||<=q/4. For a unit v write t=v^Tz*, z=v^T(x-z*). Completing the square gives
  6(t+z)^2-2t^2=4(t+3z/2)^2-3z^2>=-3e^2.
Hence H>=[3(q-e)^2-q^2-3e^2]I=(2q^2-6qe)I>=q^2 I/2. Dropping the negative rank-one term gives H<=(9||x||^2-q^2)I<14q^2 I. Thus
  q^2 I/2 <=H(x)<=14q^2 I                              (5.2)
on either q/4 ball. The line segment from x to z* remains in that ball.

Let U=a^Tz*, V=a^T(x-z*), W=U+V. The score is
  P_x=[2U^2V+3UV^2+V^3]a-zeta W a.
Conditional centering makes the signal/noise cross second moments zero. Holder and E[G^8]=105 imply, for every unit v and T_v=a^Tv,
  E[U^4V^2T_v^2]<=105q^4e^2,
  E[U^2V^4T_v^2]<=105q^2e^4,
  E[V^6T_v^2]<=105e^6.
Therefore the signal directional second moment is at most
  315(4q^4e^2+9q^2e^4+e^6)
  <=315(4+9/16+1/256)q^4e^2<1500q^4e^2.
The noise moment is at most sigma^2(||x||^2 I+2xx^T)<5sigma^2q^2I. Subtracting the signal mean can only reduce covariance. Consequently
  Cov(P_x)<=V_x I, tr Cov(P_x)<=dV_x,
  V_x=1500q^4e^2+5sigma^2q^2.                            (5.3)
Only the conditional second noise moment was used.

  sup_{x in C}||x-z*||
   <=q/9+sqrt(28/27)q/8<q/4.                            (7.1)
C lies strictly inside the lattice box: every coordinate is bounded by ||x||<1.24q, while 3s>1.47q. The chosen C may depend on every clean observation through the corruption; the origin master grids do not depend on its center.

9. Quantitative noise, sample, contamination, and full runtime consequence

Write
  D_p=dlog(3+60000sqrt(d)/p)+log(8/delta)+1.
Under K<=m/2, n>=m/2 and K=O(eps m+D_p), so
  B_m<=C_mean[sqrt(eps)+sqrt(D_p/m)].
Explicitly,
  K/n<=1200eps+360000log(2J_0/delta_stat)/m+2/m.
Thus (RG) is ensured by eps<=c_mean and m>=C_mean D_p, with very conservative absolute constants read from the displayed808,1200,180000 factors. It no longer requires an R^2 factor multiplying D_p.

The common-sample initializer tests are satisfied by the sufficient regime
  eps_alg log(e/eps_alg)<=c_init/(1+R^2),
  m>=C_init(1+R^2)^2[d_++log((1+R^2)/(delta eps_alg))],
with constants depending only on the fixed source-PCA constants and basin tolerances. This implicit formula is meaningful also at eps=0; its log contains eps_alg, never zero. For a purely explicit conservative sample selection, choose
  m>=C_init(1+R^2)^2[ d_++log((1+R^2)/(delta eps_alg)) ]
and check (SC),(IG),(RG) directly; no claimed asymptotic absorption replaces those exact gates. The Gaussian subset ceiling contributes log m/m when eps<1/m. The fixed constants are chosen so that the clipping, interpolation, deletion, normalization, and fluctuation terms in (IG) each fit a prescribed fraction of its RHS. In particular the clipping term is 1/[C_L(1-nu)], the interpolation term approaches 4alpha/1000, fluctuations are O(A sqrt((d_++log J/delta)/m)), and deletion is O(A[eps_alg log(e/eps_alg)+(d_++log1/delta)/m]). These bounds verify the displayed sufficient scaling after enlarging constants.

Combining the exact gates gives the full output consequence
  dist(x_T,x*)<=p q+C(sigma/q)[sqrt(eps)+sqrt(D_p/m)].     (9.1)
The optimized finite tolerance is explicit. At sigma=0 the guarantee is p q for every supplied p>0, not exact recovery after a finite number of iterations. At eps=0 the statistical sampling term persists. To make the statistical part contamination dominated when eps>0, take m>=C D_p/eps. Selecting p to match the unknown actual statistical floor is not automatic; the theorem states its supplied p term. The upper bound R can conservatively choose a tolerance but cannot certify the actual sigma from rare-tail data.

Let j=floor(log2 sqrt(h)). Finding this dyadic exponent by exponential bracketing and binary search, evaluating integer powers by repeated squaring, costs O(log^2(2+|j|)) real operations. This cost is not silently omitted for arbitrary unknown signal scale. Coordinate nearest-integer rounding over the bounded index range costs O(dlog(sqrt(d)/p)) comparisons/arithmetic per quantizer call; no unit-cost floor oracle is necessary. Selection logs can be replaced by upward integer binary-log bounds evaluated by power comparisons: for example log(2J_0/delta_stat) is bounded by d ceil(log2(3+6sqrt(d)/rho))+ceil(log2(4/delta_stat)). Increasing K,T,J_P,u_alg to such bounds preserves the proof. Their parameter-computation overhead is absorbed by the displayed iteration/confidence/polylogarithmic terms. If implemented with explicit integer arithmetic, its encoded parameter lengths must also be charged; this record makes no word-RAM transfer.

The complete arithmetic runtime, including the scalar pass, confidence amplification, score construction, mean solvers, projection, and rounding, is
  O( m d_+ gamma^(-2)log^4(d_+/eps_alg)log(1/delta)
     +d_+log^2(1/delta)+m+log^2(2+|j|)
     +T[ Õ(nd+u_alg Kd)+dlog(sqrt(d)/p) ] ),             (9.2)
where gamma^(-2)=Theta((1+R^2)^2), T=Theta(log(1/p)), u_alg=O(log(T/delta)), and the source mean solver's suppressed logs are its covering-SDP logs at fixed relative solver tolerance. Reusing the sample does not make the gradient work free: every call reads n dense pairs and constructs n d-vector scores in O(nd). For fixed R, contamination/confidence parameters and scale encoding, this is near-linear in dense input md up to the explicitly charged precision and dimension logs. 10. Limits, lower-bound comparison, and representation sharpness

The achievable sufficient high-noise contamination scale is approximately R^(-2)/log(1/eps), not proven maximal. A25 V6 independently provides a matching power-order finite-variance identifiability obstruction; the following reconstructs the decisive calculation and the fixed-fraction transfer.

Take two equal-norm parameters at angle theta in (0,pi/2]. For each a set mu_i=(a^Tx_i)^2, D=mu_0-mu_1, mid=(mu_0+mu_1)/2, p_a=D^2/(D^2+4sigma^2), A_a=|D|/(2p_a). Under model0 put Y=mid with probability1-p_a and Y=mid+sign(D)A_a with probabilityp_a. Under model1 use the opposite rare sign. For D=0, a null design event for distinct phase parameters, assign any identical centered variance-sigma^2 law. The conditional means are mu_i and variances are exactly p_a(1-p_a)A_a^2=sigma^2. Joint pair TV is E p_a, because their Gaussian design marginals coincide and their conditional response laws overlap only at the common atom. Gaussian quadratic-form moments give
  TV(P_0,P_1)<=E D^2/(4sigma^2)=q^4 sin^2(theta)/sigma^2.

Independently maximally couple m pairs from the two laws. The mismatch count is Binomial(m,TV). A randomized adversary in model0 samples the coupled model1 pairs conditional on its entire clean sample; if mismatches fit floor(eps m), it replaces all mismatches, otherwise it outputs its original sample. This always obeys the fixed budget. Its output law has TV distance from the model1 clean product law at most Pr(mismatches>floor(eps m)). If TV<=eps/4 and eps m>=2, a binomial exponential-moment bound yields an exponentially small bound exp(-c eps m). Since constant-angle phase balls of radius less than half dist(x_0,x_1) are disjoint, their output laws cannot both concentrate in the correct balls with probability arbitrarily close to1. Therefore for the finite-variance class, high-noise constant-angle recovery cannot be uniformly promised above an eps scale of order(q^2/sigma)^2. This obstruction does not establish that the logarithm in this construction is necessary. The two-point laws need not have a uniform bounded conditional fourth moment; this is a matched finite-variance comparison.

The sample dependence m~dR^4 of this PSD-shift construction is not proven optimal. In the Gaussian-noise subclass, equal-norm parameters have KL per pair=2sin^2(theta)/(sigma/q^2)^2. A constant-distance projective sphere packing has exp(c d) members; the usual packing/Fano argument gives the necessary high-noise order d(sigma/q^2)^2 for constant-relative directional accuracy. A25's signed-Rayleigh net estimator attains a better statistical scaling with exponential runtime. Thus a noise-factor sample gap remains; the present theorem does not close every statistical/computational tradeoff.

A25 V7 additionally exhibits an actual shifted-eigenspace failure at small constants: d=3,x*=q e3, conditional rare-tail sign sign(a1^2-a2^2), rare noise magnitude2L and its mean-zero compensator yield Q_L-S_L=-pL diag(2/pi,-2/pi,0)+a controlled remainder. When L=kappa sigma^2/q^2, kappa<1/(4pi), and sigma/q^2 is sufficiently large, the actual top population eigenvector is e2, perpendicular to the signal. This supports using a sufficiently large shift constant in this representation. It does not establish the R^4 sample cost as necessary. The earlier isotropic clipping-bias example alone did not establish eigenspace sharpness. Full finite bounds and runnable diagnostics remain in A25 V7; this example is not a premise of the recovery theorem.
