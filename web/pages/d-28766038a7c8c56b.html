## Covariance and the pair contribution

Let S_ab denote the real symmetrized expectation of J_a J_b, and let mu=Tr rho J. For a real vector u, `u^T(S-mu mu^T)u=Tr rho [u.(J-mu)]^2>=0`. Thus V=S-mu mu^T is a real positive semidefinite matrix. Permutation invariance gives `b=2mu/N`, real symmetric c_ab, and

`4S=N I+N(N-1)c`.

The local terms use `{sigma_a,sigma_b}/2=delta_ab I`; the N(N-1) ordered distinct-site terms all have the same expectation c_ab. There is no unsymmetrized imaginary collective covariance in this equation.

Put `X=4V/N` and `Z=X+b b^T`. Direct substitution yields

`Z=I+(N-1)(c-b b^T)=I+(N-1)C`.

Both X and Z are positive semidefinite. Exactly white weights imply `Tr rho J^2=3N/4`; this also follows from the normalized full Hilbert-space trace of J^2. Hence

`Tr X=3-N|b|^2`, `Tr Z=3-(N-1)|b|^2`, and `|b|^2<=3/N`.

Write s=Tr Z. It lies in [0,3]. Its nonnegative eigenvalues satisfy `Tr Z^2<=s^2`; therefore

`||Z-I||_F^2<=s^2-2s+3<=6`.

The last convex quadratic has endpoint values 3 and 6 on [0,3]. Consequently `T_2=||C||_F^2<=6/(N-1)^2`. This argument depends only on permutation invariance and that mean Casimir value. It does not by itself control higher centered tensors.

For N>=256, `t=|b|<=sqrt(3/N)<1/9`, so tau=(I+b.sigma)/2 and every tensor product reference have full support. In the correct conjugate-linear-first operator inner product `Tr tau U^dagger V`, the local Gram is

`G_ab=delta_ab-b_a b_b+i epsilon_abc b_c`.

It has eigenvalues 1-t,1+t,1-t^2. The centered Parseval identity previously audited is exact even when rho_r does not commute with tau^tensor r. It uses `R=(rho_r-tau^tensor r)(tau^tensor r)^(-1)`, zero constant and one-body coefficients, and inverse complex Gram matrices on each support. Thus

`chi_2=binomial(r,2) d_2^dagger(G^(-1) tensor G^(-1))d_2`

is at most `3r(r-1)/[(N-1)^2(1-t)^2]`. Both the factor 3 and r(r-1) come from multiplying the pair norm bound 6 by the number of unordered supports r(r-1)/2.

As an additional direct check, with `B_ab=epsilon_abc b_c`, one has `B^2=b b^T-t^2 I`, hence

`G^(-1)=(I-iB)/(1-t^2)`.

The imaginary antisymmetric part cannot be replaced by the inverse of the real covariance. For diagonal C and b=(t,0,0), the exact pair form is

`[C_xx^2+C_yy^2+C_zz^2-2t^2 C_yy C_zz]/(1-t^2)^2`.

The fresh odd-parity counterexample in `COUNTEREXAMPLES.md` verifies this identity using exact four-by-four rational matrices.

## Uniform higher-degree remainder

The preserved centered coefficient lemma is

`T_k<=36k^2 4^k(2k+1)!!/[k! binomial(N,k)]`.

Its full-white-weight transfer, polynomial-grid bound, and real symmetric sphere contraction were previously reconstructed in this auditor's `centered_audit` package. The present argument does not substitute a mean-spin or mean-Casimir promise for that lemma. Combining it with the exact centered Gram decomposition and `binomial(r,k)/binomial(N,k)<=(r/N)^k` gives, with `x=4(r/N)/(1-t)`,

`chi_(>=3)<=36 sum_(k>=3) k^2 (2k+1)!! x^k/k!`.

All coefficients are nonnegative and x<=9/256<1/2, so extending the finite support sum to the absolutely convergent power series is uniform over all legal states, N and r. Starting from `(1-2x)^(-3/2)` and applying `(x d/dx)^2` gives

`36 sum_(k>=3) k^2(2k+1)!!x^k/k! =108x[g(x)-1-10x]`,

where `g(x)=(1+3x)(1-2x)^(-7/2)`. The removed terms are exactly 108x and 1080x^2. Direct differentiation gives

`g'(x)=(10+15x)(1-2x)^(-9/2)`,

`g''(x)=105(1+x)(1-2x)^(-11/2)`.

On the full interval, `g''(x)<=(105*265/256)(128/119)^6<180`. The last comparison is rational. Integral Taylor remainder about zero now proves

`chi_(>=3)<=9720x^3<=9720(9/2)^3(r/N)^3=885735(r/N)^3`.

This is a finite uniform tail estimate, not an exchange of a state-dependent or N-dependent asymptotic expansion. For r=2 the actual tail is empty; the nonnegative cubic allowance is harmless.

## Legal lower family, both parities

Use the white weights and within each spin sector the equal mixture of extremal z weights ±j. For j=0 the two identical terms form the same normalized projector. For half-integer j, including j=1/2, the two states are distinct and the mixture is valid. Every sector mean is zero, so b=0 and G=I.

In a spin-j highest/lowest weight state, the second moments are `(j/2,j/2,j^2)`. Cross symmetrized moments vanish. For `a_N=sum p_j j`, the white Casimir identity gives `sum p_j j^2=3N/4-a_N`. Hence

`C=v_N diag(-1,-1,2)`, `v_N=(1-2a_N/N)/(N-1)`.

Its pair contribution is exactly `3r(r-1)v_N^2`. All higher support contributions are nonnegative, proving the stated lower bound on M. Cauchy-Schwarz gives `a_N<=sqrt(sum p_j j^2)<=sqrt(3N/4)`, so `1-2a_N/N>=1-h_N>0`. No parity-dependent approximation, distributional limit, or exchange of maximization and limit is needed.

The family is asymptotically extremizing for the two limits below. It is not an exact finite-N optimizer: there are explicit improvements at r=2 in both parities. The report only uses it for a lower bound, so those improvements do not invalidate the theorem.

## Finite remainder and limit quantifiers

Set `A0=3r(r-1)/N^2`, h=sqrt(3/N), q=r/N. The lower bound implies `M/A0>=(1-h)^2>=1-2h`. For the upper pair bound,

`(1-1/N)^(-2)(1-h)^(-2)<=(1-h)^(-4)<=1+8h`.

Here 1/N<=h, h<1/9, and the derivative `4(1-h)^(-5)` is at most `4(9/8)^5<8` on [0,1/9]. Thus

`M-A0<=24sqrt(3)q^2/sqrt(N)+885735q^3`,

while `A0-M<=6sqrt(3)q^2/sqrt(N)`. Since `24sqrt(3)<42`, their combination gives the proposed absolute remainder `42q^2/sqrt(N)+900000q^3` exactly as stated.

There is an explicit relative bound `|M/A0-1|<=28/sqrt(N)+600000q` for every r>=2 in the range. The large numerical constants make this bound loose at q=1/128; they do not affect its uniform validity or the two asymptotic conclusions.

For fixed r>=2, multiplying the remainder by N^2 gives terms of order `r^2/sqrt(N)` and `r^3/N`, both tending to zero. The unscaled remainder is O_r(N^(-5/2)). This holds along even and odd N.

For r_N->infinity and r_N=o(N), the sequence eventually satisfies 2<=r_N<=N/128. Divide by q_N^2. The remainder becomes at most `42/sqrt(N)+900000q_N`, tending to zero, while the leading coefficient is `3(1-1/r_N)->3`. The condition r_N->infinity is essential: at fixed r the coefficient of q^2 is `3(1-1/r)`, not 3. At r=1 rho_1 equals its own reference exactly and the centered divergence is zero, including its support-defined zero extension.

The class is compact for every fixed N, and the references have a uniform positive lower eigenvalue there, so the supremum actually has a maximizer. The proof establishes its leading constant by a uniform upper bound and a legal asymptotic lower family; it does not identify that finite maximizer. No sharp KL, trace-distance, testing, or certification constant follows merely from this sharp Petz bound.
