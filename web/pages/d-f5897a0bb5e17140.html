# Public time-lapse data audit: damage partition and repair

**Disposition: reject Panagopoulos/GSE288485 as a public-data route to a new aging or rejuvenation mechanism.** The paper is a useful assay and cell-cycle comparator, but its public releases do not contain the parent–daughter trajectories needed to separate damage partition at cytokinesis from post-division repair. The only surviving target-specific gap is a lesion-calibrated, fate-linked test in 2C-like mouse ESCs; that remains an experiment or data-access request, not a result supported by this release.

## What is actually public

Panagopoulos et al. (*Nature*, 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08986-0)) report live multigenerational U-2 OS tracking with endogenous 53BP1 and PCNA tags. The article’s data-availability statement says original high-content images, time-lapse microscopy and Live+QIBC raw data are “available on request.” Its [Supplementary Video 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08986-0) is an illustrative 36-hour PCNA-plus-track movie, not a numerical 53BP1 lineage table.

The specified accession, [GSE288485](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE288485), is bulk RNA-seq: six U-2 OS samples (three untreated, three collected 24 hours after 4 Gy IR). GEO’s processed files are Kallisto transcript tables; its sequencing reads are in SRA. Neither contains imaging, division, sister, time, or cell-cycle labels. The article’s separate [GSE288487](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE288487) is IR single-cell RNA-seq at 48 hours, also not lineage data. No raw FASTQs were downloaded.

I downloaded only the public processed [Nature source-data workbook](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-08986-0/MediaObjects/41586_2025_8986_MOESM9_ESM.xlsx) (957 KiB; local copy: `work/agents/c6_aging_rejuvenation/data/panagopoulos_2025_source_data.xlsx`). It has 34 figure-panel sheets. The relevant `ED8i` sheet is a 3-category summary (`High`, `Medium`, `Low`) by marker and condition. For 53BP1 sister-focus heterogeneity, it gives UT counts 1/9/22 and 4-Gy counts 7/28/19 (n=32 and 54 pairs). The paper’s legend says this score is between F1 sisters in G1; γH2AX, p53 and pRB are scored at the end of the 55-hour movie, with different pair counts. `ED5e` has similarly aggregated categories for APH/ATRi. These sheets retain neither pair IDs nor continuous paired sister measurements, exact event times, nor replicate/well labels.

Other workbook sheets do not close that gap. `S2c` and `ED2e` contain per-cell PCNA-foci counts with G1/S/G2 labels, so they can describe replication-pattern variation by phase, but they have no sister, time, or 53BP1-damage trajectory. `S2e` has ten numbered lineages per condition with aggregate counts of perturbed replication, cells with >10 53BP1 foci, and divisions; it is not a per-cell time series and contains no recorded lost-track/death denominator or loss reasons. Thus the workbook cannot test log sister-damage-ratio linearity, estimate a partition intercept separately from a repair slope, test phase-dependent sister ratios, or evaluate informative attrition.

As a limited reproduction check, [panagopoulos_table_check.py](panagopoulos_table_check.py) reads the complete 3×2 `ED8i` 53BP1 table and tests the equal-information null that UT and IR share one category distribution. The reproduced output is in [PANAG_CHECK_RUN.json](PANAG_CHECK_RUN.json): Pearson χ²(2, N=86)=9.468, p=0.008791, Cramér’s V=0.332, matching the paper’s reported p=0.0088. This verifies the released categorical contrast; it does not recover a physical damage ratio or add independent evidence about partition versus repair. The missing replicate labels also prevent a replicate-clustered reanalysis.

## Relevance and transfer boundary

The paper itself shows why a 53BP1 count cannot be read as a time-invariant lesion burden without calibration: inherited 53BP1 bodies are primarily G1-associated and clear on S-phase entry, replication stress changes their cell-cycle pattern, and nuclear 53BP1 concentration affects foci formation. Those observations support recording cell-cycle state and retaining a physical-lesion channel; they do not validate a linear 53BP1-to-lesion map for 2C-like divisions.

Transfer to mouse ESC aging is limited. U-2 OS is a human osteosarcoma line exposed to IR or replication-stress perturbations; this dataset has no MERVL/Zscan4 state, mouse ESCs, spontaneous aging trajectory, damaged-lineage death denominator, pluripotency recovery, or rejuvenation/function outcome. Ferrand et al. ([*Nature Communications*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-56090-8)) already directly measured CPD partition in irradiated U2OS and RPE-1 daughter pairs, so the broad proposition that physical DNA lesions can segregate asymmetrically is not a new candidate. The 2026 mouse-ESC study ([Wang et al., DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41422-026-01221-z)) is the relevant species/state/fate evidence; its GEO release GSE289323 is pooled positive/negative single-cell RNA-seq, not the sister-damage movie. The genuinely open and narrower empirical question is whether the ESC asymmetry is present at division in a physical lesion measure or develops through post-division repair/readout dynamics, with deaths and tracking losses retained. Panagopoulos’s public tables cannot answer it.
