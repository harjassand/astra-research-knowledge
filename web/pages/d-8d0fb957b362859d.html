# Scalar Euclidean finite index: prior audit and a sharper reduction

7 October 2026. Verdict: the exact target is **UNRESOLVED in this audit; historical priority UNKNOWN**. No source read here settles it. This is a scoped search, not evidence that the question is new or open in the literature.

## Exact question

For n>=3, let H=-Delta+V on L²(R^n), with real smooth bounded V(x)->0. Can a nonzero real zero eigenstate u satisfy e^(A|x|)u in L² for every A>0 while H has only finitely many negative eigenvalues (counting multiplicity)? The pointwise all-exponential version is equivalent for this purpose through local elliptic estimates with bounded V. The scalar identity principal matrix is essential. The already gated Davey–Jeznach variable-matrix cylinder counterexample does not answer this question.

## Closest primary theorems, with precise boundaries

1. **Baptiste Devyver, arXiv:1011.3390v1, 15 November 2010**, Theorem 1.2 and the Doob-transform proof. For a complete manifold and smooth potential with the Schrödinger operator bounded below, finite negative bound-state count is equivalent to existence of a positive smooth solution of Hψ=0 outside a compact set. The L² zero kernel is then finite dimensional. Lemma 1.1 records positive solution versus nonnegative Dirichlet form on a domain. Theorem 1.3 makes compact perturbations of weighted nonnegative Laplacians have finite index. The source uses the positive-Laplacian convention; our H has the same operator after translation. I read the introduction, these statements and the transform/compactness proof passages, not every auxiliary proof. This is a precise known reduction, not a lower decay bound for signed zero states. The arXiv page also lists v2, 11 March 2011; the saved PDF and statements used here are **v1**.

2. **Ujjal Das and Yehuda Pinchover, arXiv:2405.11695v1, 19 May 2024**, Theorem 6.1 and Remark 6.2. Their exterior nonnegativity extension additionally requires u to have constant sign near the artificial compact boundary. Remark 6.2 explicitly recognizes this condition as nontrivial. Their main comparison theorems on general domains use the ideal point of a one-point compactification: escape includes approach to the domain boundary, not just Euclidean infinity. Thus applying the main theorem on an exterior domain while only checking decay at infinity loses a hypothesis. Section 6 discusses limitations, but does not prove the desired unsigned finite-index implication.

3. **S. Chanillo, A. Logunov, E. Malinnikova, D. Mangoubi, arXiv:2008.00677v1, 3 August 2020**, §2.2 Lemmas 2.2–2.5 and §3. Local narrowness produces strong growth away from a nodal boundary. Their local nodal-count bounds also use separately proved doubling/Remez bounds for eigenfunctions on a fixed closed manifold. A finite count of global nodal domains supplies neither this local frequency bound nor a uniform thickness of an unbounded signed channel. I read these sections and the main interface; no Euclidean threshold transfer is asserted.

4. **Alexandre Eremenko, Dmitry Jakobson, Nikolai Nadirashvili, AIF 57(7), 2345–2360 (2007), DOI 10.5802/aif.2335**, §3 Theorem 3.2, pp.2356–2358. A bounded real planar Helmholtz solution can have exactly two global nodal domains despite endlessly repeated radial oscillations. They perturb J1(r) sin(theta) by a small translated copy to reconnect alternating rings. In our convention the potential is constant -1, so this operator has infinite negative index and is outside the target. It is a competent warning against treating global nodal count as local geometric complexity. I read §3, not the entire spherical-topology proof.

Classical Froese/Herbst and Agmon lower bounds remain important comparators, but full access to the relevant old Agmon1970 and FHHH1982 articles was not recovered here. The Agmon publisher page verifies December1970 and pp.1–25 but is subscription content. The accessible author publication list verifies bibliographic entries, not their exact assumptions. ## A complete sharper consequence of the known positive exterior solution

The previous bounded-log-gradient reduction can be strengthened when V->0: **grad log ψ->0**, and ψ has subexponential two-sided bounds. This is an elementary deduction from scale-invariant local estimates, not claimed as a new theorem.

Let R0 contain the exceptional compact set. Define

    m(s)=sup_{|y|>=s}|V(y)|,
    rho(x)=min(|x|/4, m(|x|/2)^(-1/2)),

where the second quantity is infinity if m=0. Then rho(x)->infinity. For sufficiently large x, B_(2rho)(x) is exterior and |rho² V(x+rho z)|<=1 for |z|<=2. The positive rescaled solution Ψ(z)=ψ(x+rho z) solves Delta Ψ=q(z) Ψ with |q|<=1. Harnack on B1 and interior W^(2,p) estimates for p>n, followed by Sobolev embedding, give

    |grad Ψ(0)| <= C_n sup_(B1) Ψ <= C'_n Ψ(0).

Consequently

    |grad log ψ(x)| <= C'_n/rho(x) ->0.             (1)

The constants depend on dimension and the fixed unit-ball potential bound, not on derivatives of V. Fix epsilon>0 and a radius R_epsilon beyond which the left side of (1) is <=epsilon. Integration along each radial segment, with the finite positive minimum and maximum of ψ on that sphere, gives

    c_epsilon exp(-epsilon |x|)
       <= ψ(x) <= C_epsilon exp(epsilon |x|).      (2)

Put w=u/ψ. Directly expanding Delta(ψw)=Vψw yields

    Delta w+b dot grad w=0,
    b=2 grad log ψ,
    b->0, curl b=0,
    div b=2V-|b|²/2 ->0.                          (3)

The two bounds (2) transfer all-exponential weighted L² decay in both directions between u and w; choose a slightly larger weight for the input state. The same statement holds pointwise. This retains signed boundary data; it does not remove them.

The exact remaining route is therefore much narrower than an arbitrary bounded drift equation: it is a **vanishing gradient drift with vanishing divergence** and an exterior signed solution. The scalar conductivity is ψ² I, which need not be globally bounded above and below. Multiplying the equation by a variable scalar does not produce the anisotropic cylinder principal matrix.

## Constructive use and decisive missing step

For an attempted finite-index counterexample, choose a globally positive smooth φ and write

    V0=Delta φ/φ,
    φ^(-1)(-Delta+V0+P)φ
      = -φ^(-2) div(φ² grad) + P,

with P smooth compactly supported. Provided V0+P is bounded and tends to zero, the positive exterior solution φ and Devyver's theorem certify finite index. A superexponential zero state would have to be φw, with w solving the weighted zero equation outside the support of P and satisfying a compatible compact-core equation. At every zero of w, the residual must vanish to the order required for a smooth bounded scalar P; simply dividing a chosen residual by w does not guarantee this. This is a known transform used as an exact construction interface, not a completed construction.

For a positive result, the missing assertion is an exterior lower decay bound for arbitrary signed boundary data under (3). Neither finite-dimensional kernel nor finite global nodal count proves it. The existing mixed-sign finite-index polynomial fixture and the anisotropic cylinder counterexample must remain controls.

Next falsifiable step: attempt a scalar, nodal-compatible weighted-conductivity mode transition satisfying (3), or prove a lower bound for that specific gradient-drift class. Check any proposed theorem against signed inner boundary data before testing its asymptotic decay. Scientific value would be a criterion separating arbitrarily fast zero-state localization from infinitely many negative bound states; an additional general positivity restatement would not resolve the target.
