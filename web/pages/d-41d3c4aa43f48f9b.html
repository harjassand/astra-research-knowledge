## Exact question and legal interface

For normalized nondecreasing integral submodular functions
\(r_1,r_2:2^E\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), supplied by exact rank-value oracles,
with \(|E|=n\) and \(r_1(E)=r_2(E)=R\), estimate

\[
 Z=\#\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^E:x(E)=R,\quad
                  x(A)\le r_i(A)\text{ for all }A\subseteq E,i=1,2\}.
\]

Each occupancy vector counts once. The encoded input contains binary \(R\)
and rational \(\varepsilon,\delta\); it does not contain a decomposition,
feasible sampler, explicit inequality list, or counting oracle. Query inputs
are subset indicators and answers are integers in \([0,R]\). The theorem
charges query writing, answer reading, fair random bits, arithmetic and
output length; the work performed *inside* the exact rank oracle is excluded.
The claimed uniform polynomial bounds hold on every random tape, including
bad numerical evaluations and aborted statistical runs.

Source: binary manuscript Theorem 1.1, `sections/introduction.tex`, lines
18–60; it is dated October 5, 2026 in `build/main.tex`.

## A reconstructed positive mechanism

The decisive imported interface is as follows. Pair \(2p\) labelled elements
into \(p\) pairs. Let \(\mathcal A\) be their transversals and
\(\mathcal A_{il}\) the degree-\(p\) states with pair \(i\) empty, pair
\(l\) full, all other pairs singly occupied. The two weights \(f,\widetilde f\)
are positive on **all** degree-\(p\) subsets. They agree on transversals.
The complement-set polynomial of \(f\) has the addition signature; the
polynomial of \(\widetilde f\) has it directly. On the actual state space,
\(q^{-1}f\le\widetilde f\le qf\).

The signature here means that every quadratic restriction after appropriate
inclusions or omissions has at most one positive eigenvalue. Positivity on
the entire degree domain matters: auxiliary configurations in the flow proof
may contain several original holes/full pairs and need not be chain states.
No comparison between the two weights is used on those auxiliary states.

### 1. Full-index cubic contraction

Let \(T_{iuv}\) be a symmetric tensor positive when all three indices are
distinct and zero on repetitions. Suppose each matrix slice \(T_i\) has at
most one positive eigenvalue. For a strictly positive vector \(a\), set
\(M=\sum_i a_iT_i\), and

\[
 D_{ii}=(Ma)_i/a_i>0.
\]

For a symmetric one-positive form \(Q\) and \(a^TQa>0\), decomposition
into a multiple of \(a\) and a \(Q\)-orthogonal vector gives
\(h^TQh\le(h^TQa)^2/(a^TQa)\). Tensor symmetry therefore implies

\[
 h^TMh\le\sum_i\frac{a_i(Mh)_i^2}{(Ma)_i}
          =h^TMD^{-1}Mh.
\]

Thus \(M'=D^{-1/2}MD^{-1/2}\preceq(M')^2\). The positive vector
\(u=D^{1/2}a\) obeys \(M'u=u\). The matrix
\(\operatorname{diag}(u)^{-1}M'\operatorname{diag}(u)\) is nonnegative,
row stochastic, and irreducible, since every off-diagonal entry is positive.
Its real eigenvalues lie in \([-1,1]\) and eigenvalue 1 is simple by the
maximum principle. The preceding quadratic inequality requires a positive
eigenvalue \(\lambda\) to obey \(\lambda\le\lambda^2\), hence
\(\lambda\ge1\). Therefore only the simple eigenvalue 1 is positive.
Congruence proves the assertion for \(M\). Replace nonnegative \(a\)
by \(a+\eta\mathbf1\) and take a matrix limit for a contraction on a pair.

The conclusion holds on the **full matrix**, before deleting either
contracted index. This is exactly the stronger fact needed by the next step.
It also proves closure of the addition/omission signatures under summing
disjoint pairs at occupancy one. Fixing inclusions and exclusions respectively
gives the same quadratic restriction or a principal submatrix.

### 2. Two- and three-pair inequalities

Fix a partial transversal assignment \(\sigma\). Let \(z_\sigma\) be its
transversal mass and \(\widetilde c_{il}^{\sigma}\) its defect masses under
\(\widetilde f\). Summing all other pairs leaves a quadratic on four elements.
Restrict to vectors constant on each pair. Its matrix is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}2\widetilde c_{li}^{\sigma}&z_\sigma\\
 z_\sigma&2\widetilde c_{il}^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its positive trace and positive index at most one imply
\(4\widetilde c_{il}^{\sigma}\widetilde c_{li}^{\sigma}\le z_\sigma^2\).

For three pairs, leave their six elements and form the cubic tensor.
Contract along both elements of pair \(k\), retaining their indices. On
vectors constant in each pair the resulting matrix \(N\) has

\[
 N_{ll}=2\widetilde c_{il}^{\sigma},\quad
 N_{ik}=2\widetilde c_{lk}^{\sigma},\quad
 N_{il}=z_\sigma,\quad N_{lk}=2\widetilde c_{ik}^{\sigma}.
\]

The Schur complement against its positive \(ll\) entry is nonpositive.
Since all entries and both remaining diagonal entries are nonnegative,

\[
 N_{ik}\le \frac{N_{il}N_{lk}}{N_{ll}}+
 \sqrt{\left(\frac{N_{il}^2}{N_{ll}}-N_{ii}\right)
       \left(\frac{N_{lk}^2}{N_{ll}}-N_{kk}\right)}
 \le\frac{2N_{il}N_{lk}}{N_{ll}}.
\]

Substitution gives
\(\widetilde c_{il}^{\sigma}\widetilde c_{lk}^{\sigma}
\le z_\sigma\widetilde c_{ik}^{\sigma}\). Deleting the contracted pair's
indices too early would leave no entries \(N_{ik},N_{lk}\), so that
shortcut cannot prove this inequality.

### 3. One-hole transport with exact cancellation

Fix included/excluded elements, two singleton slots \(P_0,P_1\), and
ordinary two-element slots. Let \(s_0,s_1\) be the two singleton-hole
masses under \(f\). A hole at slot \(i\) has multiplier \(W_i>0\),
and exchange-edge conductance is the minimum of its endpoint weights.
Let \(v_t\) count the configurations missing both singleton slots and
doubling ordinary slot \(t\). Then every real function \(g\) satisfies

\[
 (\bar g_0-\bar g_1)^2\le
 \left(\frac1{s_0W_0}+\frac1{s_1W_1}
       +\frac2{s_0s_1}\sum_t\frac{v_t}{W_t}\right)D_{\rm sl}(g).
\tag{T}
\]

Here is a complete independently reconstructed flow proof. Process ordinary
slots sequentially. At a recursion node the display \(R\) consists of the
two singleton elements and one choice from each processed slot. For every
displayed hole \(i\), let \(m_i\) be its mass after summing unprocessed
single choices. Assign coefficients \(h_i\) with \(\sum_i m_ih_i=0\),
starting at \(h_0=1/s_0,h_1=-1/s_1\). The potential is
\(\sum_i m_ih_i^2/W_i\).

Splitting the next slot \(\{a,b\}\), let \(m_i^a+m_i^b=m_i\), let \(u\)
be the common new-hole mass, and let \(X=\sum_i h_im_i^a\). Keep all old
coefficients; assign the new coefficient \(-X/u\) in the \(a\) child and
\(X/u\) in the \(b\) child. Both children remain balanced and their
total potential increase is \(2X^2/(uW_t)\).

The omission signature supplies the matrix

\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}V_t&(m_i^b)&(m_i^a)\\
 (m_i^b)^T&0&u\\(m_i^a)^T&u&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

with positive index at most one. Both \((h,0,0)\) and \(e_a-e_b\) are
\(Q\)-orthogonal to \(e_a+e_b\), whose square is \(2u>0\).
Their mutual product is \(-2X\) and the latter square is \(-2u\).
Cauchy–Schwarz on the negative form therefore gives
\(2X^2/u\le-h^TV_th\).

For a future slot \(t'\), the sum of \(h^TV_{t'}h\) over the two children
equals its parent value. Old–old entries add; old–new entries are equal
in both children and cancel through the opposite new coefficients;
the new diagonal vanishes. Therefore immediately before processing
\(t\), the sum over its level equals the root value
\(-2v_t/(s_0s_1)\). Summing the potential increases gives the coefficient
in (T) as an upper bound on total leaf potential.

At a leaf, the states \(S_i=F\cup(R\setminus\{i\})\) form a clique.
Choose a hub maximizing \(W_if(S_i)\). Send signed current
\(f(S_i)h_i\) from every nonhub state to the hub. Its energy is at most
the leaf potential, and its divergence is exactly \(f(S_i)h_i\) at
every state, including the hub. Distinct leaf flows use disjoint edges:
the union of an edge's endpoints recovers its unique display. A singleton
hole has one leaf representation and the desired source/sink divergence.
An ordinary hole has two representations. Their coefficients were created
with opposite signs at the same prefix and never altered, and both leaf
masses are \(f(S)\), so their divergences cancel. This proves the required
flow. Summation by parts followed by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz proves (T).

Nothing in this argument requires constructing the exponentially branching
recursion in the counting algorithm. It is an analytical certificate for
the directly simulated exchange chain.

### 4. From the two lifts to trace/observable control

Let \(C\) be the transversal mass, \(c_{il}\) the \(f\)-defect masses,
and choose good multipliers \(C/(4c_{il})\le w_{il}\le4C/c_{il}\).
The multiplied total mass satisfies \(\Lambda\le5p^2C\), hence
\(\pi(\mathcal A)\ge1/(5p^2)\). All defect classes also have probability
at least \(1/(20p^2)\).

For a partial assignment use
\(a_{il}=(\widetilde c_{il}^{\sigma}/z_\sigma)
(\widetilde c_{li}/C)\).
The two/three-pair bounds imply \(a_{il}a_{li}\le1/16\) and
\(a_{il}a_{lk}\le a_{ik}\). The graph of arcs \(a_{il}>1\) is acyclic:
a shortest directed cycle of length at least three can be shortened, and
a two-cycle violates the product bound. Thus it has a sink \(k\).
Using that pair as the two singleton slots gives
\(v_t/W_t\le4q^2 z_\sigma\). Multiply (T) by the between-child variance
factor \(s_0s_1/z_\sigma\). Charge the result over the conditioning tree;
edge restrictions at a fixed depth are disjoint. This proves

\[
 C\operatorname{Var}_\mu(g)\le C_p\mathfrak D(g),
 \qquad C_p=p(1+8pq^2).
\]

To control a defect mean, choose a joint transversal choice in its two
pairs with mass at least \(C/4\), and apply (T) using singleton elements
from **different original pairs**. Then
\(c_{it}c_{tl}\le q^3 Cc_{il}\) yields
\(C_d=8+32pq^3\). Let \(\mathcal Q\) retain each transversal separately
and replace each defect type by its conditional mean. Decomposing squared
deviation about the transversal mean gives

\[
 \operatorname{Var}_\pi(\mathcal Qg)
 \le (9C_p+2C_d)\mathfrak D(g)/C
 \le K_{\rm obs}\mathcal E_P(g),
 \quad K_{\rm obs}=10p^4(9C_p+2C_d).
\]

For \(G=\mathcal QG\), write \(G_c=G-\mathbb E_\pi G\). Taking
\(g=(I-P)^{-1}G_c\) on the mean-zero subspace gives
\(\langle G_c,(I-P)^{-1}G_c\rangle_\pi
\le K_{\rm obs}\operatorname{Var}_\pi(G)\). Laziness makes all stationary
covariances nonnegative. Therefore for an initial law \(\nu\le D\pi\),
whole-trajectory domination yields

\[
 \mathbb E_\nu\left[\left(m^{-1}\sum_{t<m}G(S_t)-\mathbb E_\pi G\right)^2\right]
 \le 2DK_{\rm obs}\operatorname{Var}_\pi(G)/m.
\]

This controls the particular observables even when no polynomial full
mixing estimate has been established for all fluctuations inside a defect
class. A harmonic extension from transversals gives
\(\mathfrak D(\widehat g)=2p^2C\mathcal E_{\rm tr}(g)\), hence inverse
trace gap at most \(2p^2C_p\). The excursion occupation measure started
from \(\mu\) is stationary and has mass \(1\) on transversals, so the
mean return cost is \(1/\pi(\mathcal A)\). For \(\nu\le D\mu\), the
mean cost of \(t\) deterministic trace steps is at most
\(tD/\pi(\mathcal A)\). This is an expectation before imposing any cap.

## Oracle acquisition and conditioning ledger

The retained partitions are acquired rather than supplied: at most
\(n(n-1)\) constrained submodular minimizations for each slack threshold
separate every distinguishable element pair. Rank queries on contractions
and unions of computed parts are still ordinary calls to the input oracle.
All rational modular terms are cleared by the product of denominators;
its *bit length* is the sum of their bit lengths. This does not substitute
polynomial dependence on the numerical denominators or on \(R\).

The binary reduction explicitly constructs only the paired port labels,
\(p\le2nN^{20021}\). Expansion matroids of size proportional to \(R\),
terminal slice subdivisions, conditioning trees and flows appear only in
the proof. The evaluator exposes a convex rational volume problem of
dimension \(f+6m+d_0\), with
\(d_0=\lceil1000E_0^2/\rho\rceil\), \(E_0=N^{51000}+\lceil\log_2(100/\rho)\rceil\).
It adds those coordinates explicitly and charges them. The large capacity
then affects a box endpoint and facet bit lengths, rather than the length
of a sampled unit-coordinate walk. Its fixed-degree dimension is legal
for an FPRAS, although unusable for computation at the displayed constants.

At phase \(j\), condition only on a past history whose stored multipliers
are good. Analyze a **virtual uncapped** restart and its observations.
The trace gap supplies a dominated endpoint law; domination and the
unconditional return-cost identity bound the expected excursion work.
Markov's inequality bounds the event that work exceeds its prescribed cap.
The implemented experiment agrees with the virtual experiment on paths
meeting the cap. Its failure is covered by the union of cap failure and
statistical estimation failure. No claim is made that the endpoint law
conditioned on cap success stays dominated. That invalid shortcut is absent.

Finite-bit implementation similarly exposes only the past before each
fresh evaluation. Conditional on that past its input is fixed; independent
new evaluator bits give the claimed failure probability. A union bound
over a deterministic action budget covers evaluator failures and rounded
transition decisions. Matching paths have exactly matching integer type
counts and therefore identical rational multiplier updates. Those rational
sizes are bounded on inaccurate histories by at most
\(H_o\log_2(N_{\rm av}+1)\) added numerator/denominator bits; zero counts
abort before division. The proof does not condition on all future evaluator
calls being successful.

There is no identified randomized oracle-information impossibility here.
The known single-matroid **deterministic** oracle lower bound does not apply
to randomized counting. An exponential number of rank inequalities also
does not itself prove a randomized query lower bound. The separate Chen–Liu
primary comparator directly claims an independence-oracle common-matroid
FPRAS. Neither its existence nor this source's argument is an independent
truth certificate; the interface is nevertheless compatible with these
known distinctions.

## Exact counterchecks and replay

The independent script uses only standard-library exact `Fraction`
arithmetic. A symmetric congruence elimination certifies inertia, including
two-by-two pivots when all remaining diagonals vanish. The flow test builds
actual signed currents, checks every vertex divergence, and verifies exact
energy ≤ summed leaf potential ≤ the claimed transport coefficient.

Across nine fixtures with capacities \((2,1),(2,2),(3,2)\), constant weights,
rank penalties, and curvature plus nonuniform rational external fields:

- 192 integer signature tests;
- 462 omission tests for \(f\) and 462 addition tests for \(\widetilde f\);
- 999 conditioned two-pair and 1,854 ordered three-pair inequalities;
- 309 full-index cubic contraction tests;
- 999 flow fixtures, 7,476 state appearances, and 1,593 local split inequalities;
- 64 independently enumerated coverage-polymatroid pairs, 5,768 positive
  root-direction lines with interval support, and 4,832 adjacent log-concavity
  inequalities.

All pass. These are bounded exact checks, not proofs for arbitrary oracles.

Two negative controls are preserved. First, the continuously log-concave
weight \(F(z)=2^{-(z_1-z_3)^2}\), on the total-two slice of
\(\{0,1,2\}^3\), has addition matrix at display zero

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1/16&1/2&1\\1/2&1&1/2\\1&1/2&1/16\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The antisymmetric first/third direction is negative, while restriction to
\((a,b,a)\) is \(17a^2/8+2ab+b^2\), positive definite with determinant
\(9/8\). It has two positive eigenvalues. Thus Prékopa log concavity alone
cannot furnish the discrete signature; the source's quantitative smoothing
and strict-curvature argument is load bearing. This is a counterexample to
a generic shortcut, not to the manuscript's corrected construction.

Second, with constant count weight and capacities \((3,2)\), \(f\)'s
addition restriction after fixing three first-port \(a\)-elements is
\(6a^2+12ab+8b^2\), whose two-by-two coefficient determinant is 12.
The exact full matrix has inertia \((2,4,1)\). Thus replacing the two lifts
by one would be false, even at constant weight. The source explicitly uses
\(f\) for omission/transport and \(\widetilde f\) for addition/defect masses.

## Prior art, significance and remaining decisive work

The primary comparator is Xiaoyu Chen and Kuikui Liu, *An FPRAS for Counting
Common Bases of Two Matroids*, arXiv:2610.06724v1, submitted October 5, 2026
at 17:15:12 UTC. The arXiv abstract/history and HTML were checked and archived.
Theorem 1.2 covers common bases from independence oracles; Remark 1.3 gives
\(\widetilde O(n^{13}/\varepsilon^2)\). Its Section C.9, Proposition C.20,
requires capacities represented in **unary**. Therefore it does not replace
the binary-capacity reduction of family 114. The comparator is contemporaneous
and discloses substantial GPT assistance. Its proof has not received an
independent audit here, and no priority, provenance or independence inference
is made from dates, author names or model disclosures alone.

The older source mechanisms are Lorentzian closure (Brändén–Huh), submodular
optimization and Edmonds' integral intersections, barycentric-spanner-type
rounding, Prékopa marginal concavity and rational convex-volume approximation.
Those pieces have distinct roles: integral intersection proves exact zero
detection; signatures prove transport; smoothing repairs a false implication
from ordinary concavity; volume approximation supplies an actual numerical
weight evaluator. The manuscript's potential substantive novelty is combining
these pieces to remove numerical-capacity dependence. Mere labelled expansion
would leave pseudo-polynomial size and the wrong multiplicities.

The displayed implementation chooses \(N\ge10^{10}\) and
\(H_o=10N^{61000}\). A nonaborting nontrivial run thus has at least
\(10^{610001}\) annealing phases. The volume lift can be still larger.
These are source operation counts, not hardware timing estimates. The theorem
would be a consequential theoretical feasibility result if adopted, but the
given construction is not a usable counting tool. A credible route to future
research is simplifying the signature-preserving binary reduction and using
the checked observable/trace mechanism with much smaller magnitude bounds.

The next high-value falsifiable gate is a fully independent audit of the
geometric-to-weight replacement, including the smooth Hessian error budget
and applicability of a concretely specified rational finite-bit volume
algorithm. A counterexample to its root-direction interval/support statement
or its homothetic cell inclusion would reject the binary extension even
though the transport mechanism above survives. After that gate, attempt to
replace the enormous fixed scales with explicit parameter-dependent bounds
and produce a moderate-size port sampler. Family 113 remains a separate
matching-energy problem; matroid transport does not validate it.
