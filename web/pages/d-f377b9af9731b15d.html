# Rounding/discrepancy scout — 7 October 2026

## Decision

**Best near-term target: an acquired, exact rational small-defect rounder with an explicit sparse law, first for cubes and partition bases.** I derived and executed this narrower mechanism. It removes N14's supplied signing oracle and its unexecuted ellipsoid extraction, at the cost of replacing `log(R/µ)` by `log R`. It does **not** validate P-round's general polytope construction or constitute a foundational discrepancy breakthrough. Binary coarsening, conditional expectations and cardinality rounding have substantial prior art; priority of this exact composition is unestablished.

**Higher-upside next step:** obtain the entropy-sensitive balanced signing bound by a certified finite-bit engine and plug it into this compiler. That would acquire the sharp N14 interface without relying on an exhaustive signing oracle. For unrestricted matroids, a genuinely new local exchange/rounding mechanism remains necessary; arbitrary exchanges do not provide the simultaneous exchange directions used below.

A quantitative negative-dependence obstruction is also proved below. It rules out a tempting cheap acquisition route, while leaving hard rounding itself easy on the example.

## Original construction A: explicit sparse cube law without signing oracle

**Theorem, internally derived; not externally reviewed.** Let rational `A∈[-1,1]^{R×n}`, `R≥1`, and rational `p∈[0,1]^n` be supplied. Set

`µ=Σ_j min(p_j,1-p_j)`, `r=ceil(log₂(2R))`.

A deterministic rational algorithm outputs a finite law with at most `dim F_cube(p)+1≤n+1` positive atoms `z∈{0,1}^n`, freezes every prescribed 0/1 coordinate, and has

`E Z=p`,

`||A(z-p)||∞≤1+µ≤2` if `µ≤1`,

`||A(z-p)||∞<1+12 sqrt(rµ)` if `µ>1`.

Thus `2+12 sqrt(rµ)` is a uniform bound. No row/column normalization beyond `|a_ij|≤1` is assumed. There is no approximation of the mean.

### 1. An elementary acquired balanced signing

For an active set of `s` coordinates, pair coordinates arbitrarily. If `s` is odd add one dummy zero column. A pair `(a,b)` receives opposite signs, so each original signing has total sign sum 0 for even `s` and absolute sum 1 for odd `s`.

Write the row's paired difference coefficients as `d_t=a_it-a_iu`, and `Y_i=Σ_t d_t η_t` for independent fair signs `η_t`. Since `|a_ij|≤1`,

`Σ_t d_t²≤2s`.

Coefficient comparison between `Π cosh(d_t z)` and `exp(z² Σd_t²/2)` gives

`E Y_i^{2r}≤(2r-1)!! (Σd_t²)^r≤(4rs)^r`.

Consequently

`E Σ_i Y_i^{2r}≤R(4rs)^r<(8rs)^r`.

Fix each `η_t` to the choice with the smaller conditional value of the polynomial `Σ_i Y_i^{2r}`. Conditional expectations never increase. The final result therefore satisfies

`|Y_i|<sqrt(8rs)` for every row.

All expectations are exact rational polynomials. They can be evaluated with suffix moment arrays through degree `2r`; odd suffix moments vanish. This costs `O(R s r²)` field operations and has polynomial bit complexity because the degree is `O(log R)`. No floating-point normal approximation, exponentials, eigenvectors, or signing search is used. The bound is weaker than Spencer's `sqrt(s log(R/s))`, which is exactly the remaining sharpness gap.

### 2. One shared uniform gives a small initialization law

Center around the nearest Boolean vertex: `b_j=1[p_j>1/2]`, `t_j=min(p_j,1-p_j)`, replace `A` by `A diag(1-2b)`. This reduces to `t∈[0,1/2]^n` with total mass `µ`.

For `µ≤1`, use zero with weight `1-µ` and `e_j` with weight `t_j`.

Otherwise set `J=ceil(log₂ n)`, `g=2^-J`, `l_j=g floor(t_j/g)`, and `α_j=(t_j-l_j)/g`. Take

`q_j(U)=l_j+g 1[U<α_j]`, one shared `U∼Unif[0,1]`.

Sorting the `n` rational thresholds gives at most `n+1` rational atoms. Their mean is exactly `t`; every atom has `||A(q-t)||∞≤ng≤1` and mass at most `µ+1`. Independent initialization is unnecessary.

### 3. Coarsen, branch and compress

At level `h=J,...,1`, the odd dyadic numerators form `S_h`. Use the acquired balanced signing and branch each current atom into its two fair orientations:

`q'_j=q_j±2^-h ξ_j` for `j∈S_h`.

Both outputs stay in the cube and lie on the next grid. Their average is the parent atom. Their total mass changes by at most `2^-h`, so throughout every retained trajectory mass is below `µ+2<3µ`. Also `s_h≤2^h(µ+2)` because each active coordinate is at least `2^-h`.

The cumulative row error is therefore less than

`1+sqrt(24rµ) Σ_{h≥1}2^-h/2 = 1+sqrt(24)(1+sqrt(2)) sqrt(rµ) < 1+12 sqrt(rµ)`.

After each level, merge duplicate atoms and find an exact affine dependence among the at most `2(n+1)` current atoms. Move the weights along that dependence until one becomes zero; repeat until the columns `[1;q]` are independent. This preserves the current pooled mean exactly and retains only existing atoms. Therefore the hard error/face/mass certificates survive. At the next level there are again at most `n+1` atoms. This is the point that removes the general ellipsoid extractor: compression happens before an exponential trajectory tree is ever formed.

### Costs and bit heights

There are at most `(n+1)J` signing calls. A straightforward implementation costs

`O(R n² J r²+n⁴ J)` rational field operations,

including repeated Gaussian elimination for compression. Faster exact dependence updates are possible but unneeded for this claim. Reading/retaining `A` costs `Θ(Rn)` entries.

At grid level `h`, integer dependence coefficients can be chosen as minors of `[1;2^h q]`, with `O(n(h+log n))` bits. Each deletion multiplies a common weight denominator by at most one such integer. At most `O(nJ)` deletions therefore give polynomial intermediate bit heights, rather than an unchecked growing-denominator recurrence. The initial common denominator has at most the total input bits of `p`. At the Boolean endpoint, affine independence and Cramer's rule bound reduced weight denominators by `Q exp(O(n log n))`, for `Q` a common denominator of `p`.

**Sharp-N14 option:** replace the moment engine with a certified Spencer/Lovett–Meka engine on pair-difference columns. Opposite signs within pairs automatically enforce the mass balance. The same sparse compiler then gives the sharper `C[1+sqrt(µ log(8R/µ))]` without any ellipsoid. This option is conditional until the signing engine is acquired. The elementary option matches the sharper order whenever `µ≤R^{1-ε}` for fixed `ε>0`, with an `ε^-1/2` factor.

## Original construction B: partition bases and arbitrary near-vertex defect

**Theorem, internally derived; not externally reviewed.** Additionally supply disjoint blocks `B_a` and integer quotas `k_a`, with `Σ_{j∈B_a}p_j=k_a`. There is a deterministic rational law with the same support/mean/face guarantees, every atom satisfying every block quota, and hard bound

`||A(z-p)||∞≤2+µ<3` when `µ<1`,

`||A(z-p)||∞<1+20 sqrt(rµ)` when `µ≥1`.

The operation/bit bounds above still apply. This is a partition-matroid base result, not an unrestricted matroid result.

**Initialization.** At mesh `g`, in each block the sum of fractional parts of `p_j/g` is an integer. Systematic sampling with one common uniform and cumulative fractional-part intervals rounds these parts to 0/1 while preserving their block sum exactly. Across all blocks, there are at most `n+1` breakpoints/atoms. Each coordinate changes by less than `g`, so feature error is at most 1 and defect relative to the fixed nearest vertex `b` is at most `µ+1`.

**Coarsening directions.** Every block has an even number of odd dyadic numerators. Pair active coordinates of the same baseline type first. Each same-type exchange `e_i-e_j` preserves both its quota and total defect. At most one mixed-type pair remains per block. Orient each as `(baseline 0)−(baseline 1)`, then pair these exchanges across blocks and use their difference. Each such four-coordinate direction preserves every quota and total defect. At most one mixed exchange remains unpaired, with defect derivative ±2.

The directions have disjoint supports, coefficients ±1, and each row's total squared direction coefficient is at most `4s`. The same even-moment procedure now gives row increment `<sqrt(16rs)`. Fair orientation preserves the mean; total defect changes by at most `2·2^-h`. Thus defect stays below `µ+3≤4µ`, active size satisfies `s_h≤2^h·4µ`, and summing gives `1+8(1+sqrt(2))sqrt(rµ)<1+20sqrt(rµ)`.

If `µ<1`, the nearest vertex `b` already satisfies every quota: its integer quota error has absolute value less than 1. Pair the deficit mass on baseline-1 coordinates with surplus mass on baseline-0 coordinates within each block by a rational transportation flow. Use the one-exchange vertices with those weights and `b` with weight `1-µ/2`. This is an exact-mean law; each atom changes at most two coordinates and has error at most `2+µ`.

**Why this does not silently extend to a general matroid.** Arbitrary pairings within active coordinates are feasible in partition bases. General matroid base exchange gives individual exchanges; it does not guarantee that every simultaneous subset/orientation of a family of exchanges remains a base. P-round's K4 complementary-exchange Hall failure is a valid gate here.

## Original obstruction: stable/negative-cylinder sampling can be exponentially wasteful

Consider a partition matroid whose blocks are disjoint pairs, exactly one element chosen from every pair, all prescribed coordinate means `1/2`. Let a law satisfy the upper negative-cylinder inequality

`Pr(all coordinates in T are 1)≤Π_{j∈T} E Z_j`.

For each complete pair choice, the all-ones event selecting its one chosen coordinate from every pair identifies exactly that output. Its probability is at most `2^-r`. There are `2^r` possible outputs and these upper bounds sum to one. Hence **every** such law must be the uniform independent product over pair choices. The same argument with arbitrary pair marginals forces their corresponding product law. Strongly Rayleigh/negatively associated laws have this upper-cylinder property.

Now divide the pairs into `q` feature blocks of an even number `t=2l` of pairs. In feature block `a`, put column `+e_a` on each left element and `−e_a` on each right element. Columns have Euclidean norm 1 and actual polytope edges change only two coordinates. The feature row error is a sum of `t` independent fair signs under the forced product law.

For a desired hard radius `B`, choose `l≥4(B+1)²`. There are at most `floor(B)+1≤B+1` central parity-compatible values. The elementary bound `binom(2l,l)/4^l≤1/sqrt(l+1)` gives

`Pr(|row error|≤B)<1/2`.

Feature blocks are independent, so the product law assigns at most `2^-q` probability to the all-rows hard-good event `G`. Any law supported on `G` is therefore at TV distance at least `1−2^-q` from it; rejection sampling from it takes expected at least `2^q` draws.

Meanwhile choose a fixed perfectly balanced alternating pattern in every feature block and its global complement, each with probability `1/2`. This **two-atom** law has all means `1/2` and zero feature error. Thus the example is an obstruction to acquiring a hard rounder from a stable/negative-cylinder reference by rejection, not to rounding, exact means, short edges, or arbitrary MCMC. It strengthens the prior disjoint-pair negative-correlation observation by quantifying TV and sampling cost.

## Optional spectral acquisition consequence (conditional on the July theorem)

Let `ν_p` be the rejective/maximum-entropy fixed-cardinality law with interior prescribed marginals `p`. The July bound implies, on the zero-sum tangent space,

`δᵀ C_p^† δ≤Σ δ_j²/[p_j(1-p_j)]`.

The Hessian of the convex negative maximum-entropy function in marginal coordinates is `C_p^†`. The Hessian of the sum of Bernoulli negative entropies is the diagonal matrix on the right. Integrating the Hessian inequality along a segment yields the independently derived consequence

`KL(ν_p || ν_q)≤Σ_j KL(Ber(p_j)||Ber(q_j))`.

Rational external weights give rational marginals/conditional probabilities by elementary-symmetric dynamic programming. The inequality certifies output-distribution error for approximate inversion, not exact means or hard atom bounds. The July theorem remains an unaudited dependency.
