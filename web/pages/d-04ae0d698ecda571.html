RESULT
The exact rank-r transfer survives an independent audit and admits a tighter
coefficient-specific bound than the initial 16^r envelope. For a requested
sector k, all exterior degrees above k can be discarded. Put

  s(r,k) = sum_{p=0}^{min(k,r)} binom(r,p)^2.

The reachable state count is at most s(r,k)^2, so one coefficient takes
O(n r^2 s(r,k)^2) exact field operations after factorization, with
O(s(r,k)^2) working state. For all coefficients in one pass the bound is
O(n r^2 binom(2r,r)^2), which is at most O(n r^2 16^r) and asymptotically
about O(n r 16^r) using the central-binomial estimate. This is a true FPT
bound, not n^{O(r)} subset enumeration. For small k, the coefficient-specific
bound can be much smaller. The prototype now implements and tests that pruning.

For orientation, state-count bounds (all sectors; target k=1; target k=2) are:
r=2: 36, 25, 36; r=3: 400, 100, 361; r=5: 63,504, 676, 15,876; r=10:
34,134,779,536, 10,201, 4,519,876. These are formula evaluations, not
measured resident states or runtimes. They show why the parameter is useful
for low rank and/or low target k while the worst-case base remains high.

EXACT FACTORIZATION AND TRANSFER
For exact F in Q(i)^{n x n} of rank r, choose r independent columns C and
then r independent rows R of F[:,C]. Let A=F[:,C], P=F[R,C], and
B=P^{-1}F[R,:]. Each column of F lies in the span of A; restricting to R
shows its unique coordinates are P^{-1}F[R,j]. Thus F=AB. This is an exact
polynomial-time acquisition from rational input. Rank zero is handled by the
empty factorization.

For each site i, write a_i=A[i,:] and b_i=B[:,i]. For selected disjoint
I,J of size k, the wedge coordinates satisfy
  det F[I,J] = <A_I,B_J>_bilinear,
where A_I is the wedge of rows a_i in increasing site order and B_J the wedge
of columns b_j in increasing site order. Expanding the squared absolute value
introduces two independent coordinate indices S,T. Therefore the sufficient
state consists of four exterior factors and the final linear functional keeps
only (S,S,T,T) with |S|=|T|=k. A site's three alternatives are empty, row/I,
and column/J, with the row or column update applied to its two replicas at
once. Since the alternatives are mutually exclusive, I intersect J is empty.
This proves the transfer identity by Cauchy–Binet and local multiplication;
there is no determinant enumeration in the algorithm.

In every reachable state, the two A-replica masks have equal degree p, and
the two B-replica masks have equal degree q. The number of such states is
  (sum_p binom(r,p)^2)^2 = binom(2r,r)^2.
For a single target k, degrees p,q>k cannot contribute to the terminal
contraction and can be pruned. This leaves s(r,k)^2 states. An occupied site
applies two coordinate insertions, each ranging over at most r choices, so
the direct transition costs O(r^2) per state. This justifies the refined
arithmetic bound. It does not claim the polynomial factor r^2 is optimal.

EXACT SAMPLING AND COST
For a fixed k, let V_i be the forward state after a fixed prefix and let W_{i+1}
be the backward covector of all suffixes that end in the k-sector. The three
next-site masses are
  m_s = <W_{i+1}, T_{i,s} V_i>,  s in {empty,row,col}.
They are nonnegative rationals, sum to the current completion mass, and their
conditional proportions are exactly the probabilities of the next local
occupancy under weight |det F[I,J]|^2. Thus sequential exact integer draws
produce the desired distribution whenever c_k(F)>0. No probability lower
bound is needed for correctness; a small branch can cost random bits only via
the bit length of the exact integer masses, not inverse probability. The
implemented suffix table costs O(n r^2 s(r,k)^2) field operations and
O(n s(r,k)^2) stored entries. Recomputing messages trades this for
O(s(r,k)^2) state memory at higher time. The implementation uses the
coefficient-pruned forward update and exact common-denominator draws.

TOTAL BIT COST AND CONDITIONING
Let L be the total binary encoding length of the rational-complex input. Clear
all input denominators by a product Q with log2 Q <= L and write G=QF. The
Gaussian-integer entries of G have O(L) bits (up to a fixed encoding factor).
The selected pivot determinant and cofactors have O(r(L+log r)) bits by the
determinant expansion/Hadamard bound. Thus A and B can be represented with
polynomial bit length O(r(L+log r)). A transfer term contains at most 2n
factor entries; the number of summands reaching one state is at most
(1+2r^2)^n. Consequently state numerators and denominators have a crude
polynomial bit bound O(nr(L+log r)+n log r). Exact arithmetic therefore gives
total bit complexity of the form
  O(n r^2 s(r,k)^2 * poly(n,r,L))
plus polynomial-time factorization and output. This is conservative and does
not rely on well-conditioned pivots: a near-singular exact pivot increases
bit lengths only as the determinant/cofactor bound, while floating-point
stability is not claimed.

For the output itself, c_k(F)=Q^{-2k}c_k(G); if nonzero, c_k(G) is a positive
integer, so c_k(F)>=Q^{-2k}>=2^{-2kL}. Hadamard and at most 3^n local
occupancy configurations give output bit length O(n+kL+k log n). This lower
bound can be exponentially small in n, so replacing exact arithmetic with a
floating low-rank approximation requires a sector-relative residual
certificate. More precisely, for ||E||_op<=1, if s_k(F) is the vector of
all disjoint k-minors, then
  ||s_k(F+E)-s_k(F)||_2
    <= k sqrt(binomial(n,k)) ||E||_op (||F||_op+1)^(k-1).
Hence relative error in c_k is at most 2x+x^2, where x is this bound divided
by sqrt(c_k(F)). Approximate rank by itself is not a safe certificate when
c_k is tiny; exact rank acquisition is a discrete property and may be
unstable under rounding.

ATTRIBUTED OVERLAP AND DIFFERENTIATION
The bounded-support Grassmann/treewidth construction in INITIAL duplicates
the exact support-graph-width route in c03_s01's frozen Sol report. I do not
claim that route as a separate contribution. c03_s03 independently gives an
exact ordered cut-rank MPS with bond D_p=2^(a_p+b_p), where a_p and b_p are the
two cross-block ranks, all-coefficient contractions O(n^2 D^3), and exact
sampling. If global rank(F)=r, then a_p,b_p<=r and c=max(a_p+b_p)<=2r.
Thus the MPS result already gives an exact rank-FPT algorithm with a
conservative O(n^2 2^(6r)) contraction bound. The direct four-factor norm
transfer is an independent contraction and has O(n r^2 binom(2r,r)^2),
approximately O(n r 2^(4r)), for global rank r. This may improve the
exponential dependence in the global-rank case under the stated direct
cost models, but is a cost optimization/alternative derivation, not a new
tractable class. For matrices whose cut-rank c is much smaller than r, the
MPS route can be substantially better. A practical exact solver should
estimate both state bounds and select the cheaper admissible contraction.
