## Verdict

This pass did not establish a new biological law. The strongest concrete result is a source-backed measurement/representation audit: the released CD36 rate workflow combines a daughter-pair odds ratio with an ON fraction from separate clone-level data. ## What is established in this lane

The primary source is a bioRxiv preprint dated 2026-06-19, explicitly marked not peer reviewed. It reports a combination of MemorySeq, single-cell RNA-seq, microscopy, and infection assays. The manuscript's model is a two-state branching process with equal state growth, ON/OFF transitions, optional population-size-dependent `k_on`, and a clone-random `k_on,max`. It reports long-lived CD36-like variation, transient IL1β-like response, and infection association in the tested macrophage system. Those are preprint claims, not new claims produced here.

The exact released Rmd selects `CD36_Mock` pairs from the pair-count workbook, computes their OR, then sets `FractionON` to the unweighted mean of a separate 1,108-clone workbook. In the selectively extracted public inputs, the pair table has 57 ON/ON, 471 OFF/OFF, and 38 mixed pairs: `f_pair=0.134276`, `OR=74.3684`; the script fraction is 0.216803. Holding OR fixed, `k_on+k_off` changes from 0.015706 to 0.019544 h^-1 under the same stated inversion. See `proof/rate_input_marginal_mismatch.md`. In addition, the Methods select endpoint pairs by centroid proximity (not reported lineage tracking), and the simulation uses clone size in place of directly measured local-neighbor histories while clone size also grows with time. The full 2.5-GB archive was not downloaded; only two small model workbooks were range-fetched and CRC-checked, so no full bootstrap/optimization or image-level reproduction was performed.

Two conditional model/data results are documented: a routine mean-fraction lag identity and the pair-contamination sensitivity. The strongest audit result is the observed workflow mismatch above; it is still not a biological discovery, and its interpretation depends on what the authors intended `f_ON` to estimate.
