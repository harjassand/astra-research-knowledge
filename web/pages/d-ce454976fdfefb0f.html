# Collision retention, removal of all pivots, and an exact repair barrier

## Exact question

After the all-degree constant-pivot HC4 route reduces to prior HC3/HC2 or the plane Keller problem, can an actual nonlinear reduction escape **every** constant Hessian pivot while retaining a known global gradient collision? If yes, what exact determinant obstruction remains and what repair degrees can be excluded universally? The target is a usable reduction interface, not another renaming of JC2.

## First-jet compatibility is required before a determinant search

Let \(f\) be any polynomial, \(\Phi\) a polynomial source automorphism, and \(p_i=\Phi(q_i)\) satisfy \(\nabla f(p_1)=\nabla f(p_2)=\lambda\). Then

\[
 \nabla(f\circ\Phi)(q_i)=J_\Phi(q_i)^T\lambda.
\]

Thus a known collision is retained exactly when

\[
 [J_\Phi(q_1)-J_\Phi(q_2)]^T\lambda=0.
\]

For a selected polynomial critical graph \(t=s(u)\), \(\partial_t(f\circ\Phi)=\sigma\), define \(\psi=(f\circ\Phi-\sigma t)|_{t=s(u)}\). On that graph,

\[
 \nabla_u\psi=\partial_u(f\circ\Phi).
\]

Therefore retaining the collision after reduction requires both graph membership and equality of the full pulled-back covectors, including the same critical level. Polynomial invertibility, determinant one, and a pointwise Schur complement alone do not imply it. The full Hessian chain rule supplies the second-jet determinant gate independently.

For the actual v2 construction, use the prior normalization

\[
 f_0=t^2+a(x,y)t+2(y\rho_0+2x\eta_0),\quad
 a=13+2\beta,\quad \beta=y^2(6x^2y^2+15xy+4).
\]

The original published pair is carried to

\[
 p_+=(1,-3/2,0,0,0),\quad p_-=(-1,3/2,0,0,0)
\]

in \((x,y,t,\rho_0,\eta_0)\). These are **not** a collision of \(\nabla f_0\): their full gradients are

\[
 (0,0,-19/2,c_+),\quad(0,0,-19/2,c_-),\qquad
 c_+=(-3,4)^T,\quad c_-=-c_+.
\]

Let an arbitrary fibre automorphism \(\Theta(x,y,t,R)\), \(R=(\rho,\eta)\), fix the zero section for all base points, and keep \((x,y,t)\) fixed. Let \(J_\pm=D_R\Theta(p_\pm)\). Its base and \(t\) derivatives vanish on the zero section. Hence the pair can be a full transformed collision only if

\[
 J_+^Tc_+=J_-^Tc_-,\qquad (J_++J_-)^Tc_+=0.
\]

All polynomial fibre automorphisms with the **same invertible linear first jet at these two points** are excluded, regardless of the number, degree or base dependence of their higher-order nonlinear terms. This includes every base-independent fibre automorphism fixing the origin, and every origin-fixing automorphism with a constant invertible linear part and arbitrary higher residual terms. This is an exact retained-witness obstruction, not a claim that those potentials have no other collision.

Since a fibre automorphism has a common nonzero constant Jacobian determinant, \(R_*=J_-J_+^{-1}\) has determinant one. The stronger necessary condition is

\[
 R_*^Tc_+=-c_+,\qquad \operatorname{tr}R_*=-2.
\]

The trace condition alone is not sufficient: the indicated covector must be the eigenvector. It provides a cheap exact first-jet gate for future nonlinear charts before large Hessian expansions.

## Explicit nonlinear construction passes collision and every-pivot removal

Set \(h=(1-x)/2\), and define

\[
 E_{12}(h)=\begin{pmatrix}1&h\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 E_{21}(-h)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-h&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 D(x)=[E_{12}(h)E_{21}(-h)E_{12}(h)]^2.
\]

Then \(D\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C[x])\), \(D(1)=I\), \(D(-1)=-I\). Explicitly in \(h\),

\[
 D=\begin{pmatrix}
 1-4h^2+2h^4&4h-6h^3+2h^5\\
 -2h+2h^3&1-4h^2+2h^4
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Use the genuinely nonlinear two-shear automorphism

\[
 T_1=\rho+\eta^2,\qquad T_2=\eta+T_1^2,
 \qquad (\rho_0,\eta_0)^T=D(x)(T_1,T_2)^T.
\]

Both fibre Jacobians have determinant one and both preserve the origin. The first jets at the chosen points are \(I\) and \(-I\), satisfying the required covector compatibility. This class differs from the base-cancelled class in the other cycle13 note: the actual critical-value base potential is retained, and the base matrix mixes the residual directions.

For \(\sigma=-19/2\),

\[
 t_*=(\sigma-a)/2=-45/4-\beta,
\]
\[
 \psi=2(y,2x)D(x)T(\rho,\eta)-(\sigma-a)^2/4.
\]

At the distinct reduced points \(q_+=(1,-3/2,0,0)\), \(q_-=(-1,3/2,0,0)\), \(t_*=0\), and

\[
 \nabla\psi(q_+)=\nabla\psi(q_-)=(0,0,-3,4).
\]

The full transformed gradients also coincide at \((q_\pm,t=0)\), with \(t\)-component \(-19/2\). Thus this is a genuine retained collision through the polynomial critical graph, not a reused collision of the original variables without the chain rule.

For every constant symmetric \(K\), the polynomial contraction \(\operatorname{tr}(K H\psi)\) can be constant only if \(K=0\). To certify this, list the ten Hessian entries in the order

\[
 (H_{xx},H_{xy},H_{x\rho},H_{x\eta},H_{yy},H_{y\rho},H_{y\eta},H_{\rho\rho},H_{\rho\eta},H_{\eta\eta}).
\]

Their positive-degree coefficient matrix has exact shape \(109\times10\) and rank10. A selected ten-by-ten minor on monomials

\[
 \eta,\eta^2,\eta^3,\eta^4,y,y\eta,y\eta^2,y\eta^4,y\rho,y^2
\]

has determinant \(15169518\ne0\). Off-diagonal factors of two in the trace pairing are invertible constants, so this proves the claimed absence of any nonzero constant symmetric contraction. In particular there is no nonzero constant direction \(v\) with \(D_v^2\psi\) constant. The property persists under all invertible affine source changes and quadratic potential additions.

However,

\[
 \det H\psi(0)=64,\quad
 \det H\psi(q_+)=429397/4,\quad
 \det H\psi(q_-)=-339587/4.
\]

Therefore the constant-Hessian gate fails. Collision retention and removal of every pivot are compatible; no general obstruction can infer one from the failure of the other. The degree14/HC5 determinant of the original published potential is not assumed invariant under this nonlinear source change.

## A universal obstruction to all low-degree potential repairs

Write \(\psi=h_0+f_{\mathrm{fib}}\), where

\[
 h_0=-(\sigma-a)^2/4,\quad
 f_{\mathrm{fib}}=2G_1T_1+2G_2T_2,\quad (G_1,G_2)=(y,2x)D(x).
\]

The Hessian of \(h_0\) is supported on its base two-by-two block and has maximal entry degree10. For the fibre Hessian, in source order \((x,y,\rho,\eta)\),

\[
 \deg (Hf_{\mathrm{fib}})_{ij}\le d_i+d_j,\qquad d=(4,4,3,4).
\]

Thus determinant terms with no base-only factor have degree at most30; those with one base-only factor have degree at most32. Only two base-only factors contribute at degree34. The exact highest components are

\[
 (\det H_{x,y}h_0)_{20}=-456192x^6y^{14},\quad
 (\det H_{\rho,\eta}f_{\mathrm{fib}})_{14}=x^{10}y^2\eta^2/8,
\]

and hence

\[
 (\det H\psi)_{34}=-57024x^{16}y^{16}\eta^2\ne0.
\]

Exact polynomial computation gives the adjugate entry degree matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 24&24&25&21\\24&24&25&21\\25&25&28&27\\21&21&27&26
 \end{pmatrix},
\]

with maximal two-by-two minor degree20 and maximal Hessian entry degree10. If a potential repair \(R\) has Hessian entry degree at most \(k\), determinant multilinearity bounds

\[
 \deg[\det(H\psi+HR)-\det H\psi]
 \le\max(28+k,20+2k,10+3k,4k).
\]

For **every** polynomial repair \(R\) of degree at most seven, \(k\le5\), the bound is33, so the nonzero degree34 term survives. No coefficient restriction, collision condition, support bound, finite search or external HC theorem is used in this universal repair obstruction. The same exclusion holds after an invertible affine reparametrization, since affine pullback preserves degree and multiplies the determinant by a nonzero constant.

### The degree-eight compatibility equation is impossible as well

For degree-eight repairs, products containing two repair Hessians have degree at most \(20+12=32\). Only the linear adjugate term can affect degrees34 or33. The only adjugate entry of degree28 is \(\rho\rho\), with

\[
 (\operatorname{adj}H\psi)_{\rho\rho,28}=684288x^{11}y^{15}\eta^2.
\]

Hence the degree34 equation forces the homogeneous degree-eight part to be exactly

\[
 R_8=x^5y\rho^2/24+\rho A_7(x,y,\eta)+B_8(x,y,\eta),
\]

where \(A_7,B_8\) are arbitrary homogeneous polynomials of the indicated degrees. This follows by dividing the nonzero polynomial factor in the degree34 identity and integrating \(\partial_{\rho\rho}R_8=x^5y/12\); it is not a sparse ansatz.

The forced term \(R_8^{\mathrm{forced}}=x^5y\rho^2/24\) preserves the known collision because its gradient vanishes at both sources. It cancels degree34, but the next degree33 component is

\[
 C_{33}=-38016x^{16}y^{16}\rho
 +57024x^{15}y^{15}\eta^2(4x+5y).
\]

The only free degree33 changes are

\[
 2(\operatorname{adj}H\psi)_{\rho\eta,27}\,\partial_\eta A_7
 +(\operatorname{adj}H\psi)_{\rho\rho,28}\,\partial_{\rho\rho}R_7,
\]

where \(R_7\) is the arbitrary homogeneous degree-seven part of the repair. The exact remaining cofactor is

\[
 (\operatorname{adj}H\psi)_{\rho\eta,27}=-228096x^{11}y^{15}\eta.
\]

Consequently the full degree33 compatibility equation is

\[
 C_{33}-456192x^{11}y^{15}\eta\,\partial_\eta A_7
 +684288x^{11}y^{15}\eta^2\,\partial_{\rho\rho}R_7=0.
\]

Setting \(\eta=0\) leaves \(-38016x^{16}y^{16}\rho=0\), an impossible polynomial identity. The arbitrary \(B_8\) and all lower repair degrees cannot enter this coefficient. Thus **no polynomial potential repair of degree at most eight can restore a constant Hessian determinant**, whether or not it preserves the collision. No model agreement is needed for the algebraic obstruction; an independently written checker provides additional internal reconstruction.

The specific forced repair gives exact determinant values \(1690363/16\) and \(-1359437/16\) at the two collision points, so it demonstrably fails beyond cancelling the leading term.

## Precise frontier and downstream use

This fixed nonlinear chart passes three independent gates: global polynomial critical solution, actual retained collision, and removal of every constant symmetric Hessian contraction. It fails the constant-determinant gate and cannot be rescued by any degree-at-most-eight potential repair, including repairs permitted to destroy the collision.

The first possible repair degree is therefore nine. This is a **necessary cutoff**, not an existence claim. At degree nine, products of two repair Hessians may enter degree34, so the linear degree-eight equation cannot be extrapolated. A next investigation would need to solve those higher graded compatibility equations together with \(\nabla R(q_+)=\nabla R(q_-)\), or identify a different nonlinear chart whose determinant obstruction already vanishes.
