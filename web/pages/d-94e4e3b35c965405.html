# Adversarial audit: prime-field descent and construction

Scope: Section 4 of `Q_GREEN_COMPACT_DETECTOR.md`, with the upstream nonvanishing and order bound treated as premises. Source checked: `pitp.txt`, equation (23), Section 5.2 and Lemma 5.1, and the explicit construction (28). The supplied files were read only.

Verdict: no gap or counterexample found in this section. This is a conditional validation of the descent step, not a validation of Sections 1–3 or a universal theorem certificate.

## Exact finite object to descend

Set `V_i = S tensor U_i`, where `S` is the standard nilpotent shift of size `m+1`. Because `S^(m+1)=0`, every recognizable-series evaluation at `V` is the evaluation of the finite noncommutative polynomial `g = sum_(|w|<=m) f_w w`. Consequently Lemma 5.1 applies directly to `g` and `V`; applying that polynomial lemma to an untruncated infinite expression is unnecessary. Each entry of `V_i` is regular at `t=0` and has prime-field rational coefficients.

## Denominator and numerator attacks

For a nonzero contribution to a length-`h` product of the `U_i`, the row indices form `q=q_0<q_1<...<q_h=r`. The denominator is a product of the indexed factors `(t^(q_j)-1)`, with each index appearing at most once. Its quotient into `Delta = product_(a=1)^(D-1) (t^a-1)` is the product of the unselected indexed factors and is therefore a polynomial in every characteristic. This argument makes no squarefreeness or pairwise-coprimality assumption. For example in characteristic 2, `(t^2-1)=(t-1)^2`, but selecting indices 1 and 2 consumes exactly the three corresponding copies of `(t-1)` already present in `Delta`.

The numerator exponent is at most `n[(r-q)-h]`; the letter order along the ascending path is the reverse of the written operator-product order, but this has no effect on the bound. Multiplication by `Delta` produces a polynomial of degree at most `binom(D,2)+n(D-1)=E-1`. The empty word contributes `Delta` times an identity entry. The `S` factors contribute only 0 or 1. Arbitrary coefficients in `F` may cancel these polynomials but cannot increase their degree.

## Taylor reduction and arbitrary coefficient fields

Every denominator `(t^r-1)` has constant term `-1`, including characteristic 2, and `Delta(0)=(-1)^(D-1)` is nonzero. Thus reduction is the unital algebra map from the ring of rational functions regular at zero into `F[t]/(t^E)`; it uses no derivatives, factorials, roots, or separability assumptions. A nonzero cleared numerator of degree below `E` cannot reduce to zero. This proves preservation of upstream nonvanishing.

The base-change identity `F tensor_(F_p) F_p[t]/(t^E) = F[t]/(t^E)` is valid for every field extension `F/F_p`. Multiplication on `F[t]/(t^E)` is injective because its action on the vector 1 returns the multiplying element. Thus the images of `1,t,...,t^(E-1)` stay linearly independent over `F`, and arbitrary extension-field word coefficients introduce no descent defect.

## Marker and order

The safe descended tuple is `W_i = S tensor rho(U_i mod t^E)`, equivalently the entrywise application of the matrix-ring embedding to `V_i`. Its dimension is `(m+1)DE`. This operation preserves multiplication order, scalar coefficients, and the identity. Nilpotence of `S` still kills all word lengths greater than `m`; the `m+1` shift size retains `S^m`. Nothing requires reducing the shift automorphism itself modulo `t^E`.

## Explicit cost witness

Let `B=n+m+ceil(log_2 p)+1`, with `n,m>=1` and prime `p` promised. Then `D=O(B^2)`, `E=O(B^4)`, and `d=O(B^7)`, more precisely `d=O(n^3 m^4)`.

For each entry with `q<r`, compute `(1+t)^(i(r-q-1)) mod t^E` by at most `n(D-1)` repeated multiplications by `1+t`; compute the denominator inverse as `-sum_(j=0)^floor((E-1)/r) t^(rj)`; multiply and truncate with ordinary coefficient-array arithmetic modulo `p`. A loose bound per entry is `O(nDE+E^2)` residue operations, for at most `nD^2` entries. Forming the multiplication blocks is Toeplitz copying, and tensoring with `S` inserts their positions and zeros. Every loop bound is polynomial in `B`; all residue operations have bit cost polynomial in `log p` because coefficients are reduced after each operation. No loop has length `p` and no polynomial factorization or extension-field acquisition is needed.

Dense output contains `n d^2` residues and has bit length `O(log(d+1)+n d^2(1+log p))`, hence a polynomial bound (for example `O(B^16)`, allowing standard separators). This is construction cost only. Evaluation of an arbitrary externally supplied series and representation of its arbitrary-field response are separate interface charges.
