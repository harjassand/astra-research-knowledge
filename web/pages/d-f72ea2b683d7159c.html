# Enhancer-memory kernel: schedule-dependent transcription at matched TF area

## Claim status

This is an internally derived consequence of a deliberately small model, not a biological finding or a novelty-cleared theorem. It supplies a falsifiable alternative to a fixed-gain/AUC model and a waveform-design calculation. The model is only a local, low-opening approximation and cannot establish cell-fate reprogramming.

## Model and derivation

Let `x(t) >= 0` be the measured active TF signal above baseline (for the proposed NF-κB assay, single-cell nuclear RelA); `c(t)` be excess effective activity of one target enhancer; and `y(t)` be target transcript deviation. For a low-opening regime, take

\[
\dot c=\alpha x-\beta c,\qquad
\dot y=\kappa(a_0+c)x-\delta y,
\]

with `c(0)=y(0)=0`, positive constants, an input of finite duration, and complete observation of the RNA decay tail. `c` is an effective transcriptional state, not ATAC signal, H3K4me3, or contact frequency. The term `c*x` is the minimal TF-dependent opening/readout interaction; if opening saturates, the equations must be replaced.

Since `c(t)=alpha int_0^t exp(-beta(t-s)) x(s) ds`, integration of the transcript equation gives

\[
\operatorname{AUC}(y)=\frac{\kappa}{\delta}\left(a_0\int x(t)dt+\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta|t-s|}x(t)x(s)\,ds\,dt\right).
\]

The first term depends only on TF area. The second depends on the full TF autocorrelation under an exponential lag kernel, so two waveforms with equal RelA AUC can produce different target RNA AUC even when the gain parameters are fixed. This is a direct schedule-dependence mechanism absent from the LTI sign law in `feedback_chromatin_sign_law.md`.

For a rectangular signal with area `A` and duration `T`, `x=A/T` on `[0,T]`, the opening-mediated term is

\[
Q(T)=\alpha A^2 f(\beta T),\qquad
f(z)=\frac{z-1+e^{-z}}{z^2}=\int_0^1(1-r)e^{-zr}dr,\quad f(0)=\tfrac12.
\]

The integral form proves `f` is strictly decreasing for `z>0`: within this model, at fixed measured TF area, concentrating an active TF trajectory in time increases the enhancer-memory contribution, with the effect controlled by the closure rate `beta`. It says nothing about how upstream signaling changes the TF waveform.

## Identifiability and decision boundary

With only one waveform, `a_0`, `alpha`, `kappa/delta`, and `beta` cannot be separated. With `n` matched-area waveforms `x_j`, the model has the form

\[
Y_j=D+C K_\beta[x_j],\qquad
K_\beta[x]=\tfrac12\int\!\int e^{-\beta|t-s|}x(t)x(s)dsdt,
\]

with unknown `D=kappa*a_0*A/delta` and `C=kappa*alpha/delta`. At least three distinct waveforms are needed even for local identification of the three quantities `(D,C,beta)`, and only when the sensitivity matrix with rows `[1,K_beta[x_j], C*dK_beta[x_j]/d beta]` has full rank. Therefore a schedule-design routine should evaluate this rank/condition number over the proposed waveform set *before* acquisition; if it is deficient or badly conditioned over the plausible `beta` range, report `UNKNOWN` and change the waveforms. A direct dynamic accessibility or functional-enhancer measurement can add information but must be validated as effective gain rather than assumed equivalent to it.

For equal-area rectangular waveforms, selecting durations so that `beta*T` is always either much smaller than one or much larger than one is poorly informative: respectively `f` is nearly `1/2` or nearly `1/(beta*T)`, and the profile is weakly sensitive to closure rate. A useful design spans the prior range of `beta*T` around order one. This is a design heuristic; no empirically grounded `beta` range or power calculation is currently available.

## Falsification test and costs

Use a randomized set of at least three microfluidic histories, chosen to give distinct measured same-cell RelA traces and matched RelA areas. Acquire full-tail nascent transcription for a predeclared NF-κB-dominant target, an enhancer-disabled or enhancer-perturbed condition, and an IRF/AP-1-dominant control. Fit on a subset of waveforms and predict held-out target AUCs from the exponential-kernel statistic. Compare, under the same data split and per-cell measurements, against the RelA-AUC-only model, a flexible per-condition model, and a model including measured co-TF covariates.

Reject this kernel if held-out outcomes miss preregistered noise bounds, the inferred closure parameter changes materially across waveform subsets, the enhancer perturbation does not attenuate the schedule-dependent component, or a co-TF/control signal accounts for it. The design pays for same-cell upstream/TF imaging, programmable stimulation, nascent-RNA time courses through the full tail, perturbation-efficiency and RNA-decay calibration, independent culture replicates, and a pilot to estimate noise and sample size. ATAC/CUT&Tag is a destructive association measurement and cannot by itself establish that `c` is a causal transcriptional state.

Even if this test succeeds, finite `beta>0` means the effective enhancer state decays after the stimulus is removed. No self-sustaining feedback or stable lineage marker is represented; this model predicts a transient, timescale-bounded memory rather than a programmed fate transition.
