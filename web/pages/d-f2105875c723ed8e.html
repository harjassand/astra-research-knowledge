## 1. Boolean geometry, face recovery, and endpoint bits

There exists an inequality/affine-equation description of `Q` with integer normal coefficients and constants bounded by `Cf=(2n)^n`. Here is a proof rather than an assumption about width. If `dim Q=d`, choose `d` coordinate projections that are an affine isomorphism on `aff(Q)`. The projected polytope is a full-dimensional signed Boolean polytope in `R^d`. A facet through `d` affinely independent projected vertices has normal and constant given by cofactors of the `d x (d+1)` matrix with rows `(v,1)`. Each cofactor is at most `d^(d/2)` by Hadamard. Lift the inequality using zero coefficients outside the chosen coordinates. For `d<n`, affine equations reconstructing other coordinates use cofactors of a `(d+1) x (d+1)` signed/one matrix; their coefficients and constants are at most `(d+1)^((d+1)/2)`. Both bounds are below `Cf`. The singleton case is immediate. This is an existence bound; it does not require listing exponentially many facets.

Suppose `x=X/Qx` has a common integer denominator and let `F=F(x)`. For every facet not containing `F`, its positive slack at `x` is at least `1/Qx`. For an integer normal `a` with `|a_j|<=Cf` and `h in [-1,1]^n`, `|a^T h|<=n Cf`. Consequently, with

`epsilon=1/(2 Qx n Cf)`,

`{h in [-1,1]^n : x+epsilon h in Q and x-epsilon h in Q}
 = lin(F-F) intersect [-1,1]^n`.

The tight inequalities force equality on `h`; all other inequalities retain positive slack. This proves the exact tangent-box identity. The relative-interior and integer-slack hypotheses are necessary.

To recover its span using exact LP, maintain an obtained span `W`, choose a rational basis of `W^perp`, and maximize/minimize these objectives on the box. If all values vanish, the box lies in `W`; otherwise add an optimizing vector outside `W`. At most `n` additions and `O(n^2)` LP calls suffice. Require a basic optimizing point (or exact vertex extraction), rather than an arbitrarily encoded optimum. Box vertices are solutions of integer face equations and coordinate bounds, and therefore have bits polynomial in `n log Cf`, independently of `Qx`. Rational elimination yields an integer equation matrix `E_F` for the tangent with polynomial bits. Its affine right-hand side `E_F x` is an integer bounded by `n ||E_F||_infinity`, since it also equals `E_F v` for a signed Boolean vertex `v` of `F`. Reduced affine equations therefore also lose dependence on the current denominator.

The face itself is `F = Q intersect aff(F)`. It can be used in slice LPs by adding these exact affine equations. This computational claim uses O1, not a weak or favorable-only optimization response.

For any nonzero rational direction `h=Hvec/Th` in the face tangent and cost kernel, an endpoint along `x+t h` is attained at an original facet. Its parameter has the form

`t = Th (Qx b-a^T X)/(Qx a^T Hvec)`.

The reduced denominator is at most `Qx n Cf Hmax`, with `Hmax=max |Hvec_j|`. At the endpoint,

`x+t h = [X(a^T Hvec)+(Qx b-a^T X)Hvec]/[Qx a^T Hvec]`.

Thus a potentially exponentially short step still has polynomial encoding in the current state. This is a bit-length statement, not a lower bound on step length. When `||h||>=1/2`, some coordinate has magnitude at least `1/(2sqrt(n))`, giving an endpoint search range below `4n`. Exact membership bisection to width `<1/(2B^2)`, `B=Qx n Cf Hmax`, separates all rationals with denominator at most `B`; rational reconstruction identifies the exact endpoint in polynomial calls/bits. Exact 1D LP is an alternative. A near endpoint cannot replace the exact endpoint in the face-drop proof.

## 2. Cost-slice anchor with history-independent bits

Let `F` be the current original face, `x in relint(F)`, and `S_F=F intersect {z:c^T z=gamma}`. Then `x in relint(S_F)` and `aff(S_F)=aff(F) intersect {c^T z=gamma}`: a small relative open ball in `F` around `x` intersects the hyperplane in a relative open ball of the intersection space.

Every vertex of `S_F` is either a vertex of `F` or a cost-hyperplane intersection with an edge of `F`. To prove this, take the minimal face `G` of `F` containing a purported slice vertex `z`. Locally at `z`, intersecting `G` with one hyperplane loses at most one dimension; if `dim G>=2`, it leaves positive dimension and `z` is not a slice vertex. If `dim G=1`, the hyperplane must cut the edge nontrivially. If `dim G=0`, `z` is an original Boolean vertex.

Clear the original cost denominators, writing `C=Tc c` integral and `C^T x0=g/Q0`, where `x0=X0/Q0` is an original common-denominator representation. On an edge `[u,v]`, the intersection is `u+lambda(v-u)` with

`lambda=(g-Q0 C^T u)/(Q0 C^T(v-u))`.

Its common coordinate denominator is at most `Bv=2 Q0 n max(1,||C||_infinity)`. This bound involves original `x0,c`; no current `Qx` occurs. A whole edge contained in the hyperplane does not create an interior slice vertex.

Choose `q+1` affinely independent slice vertices, `q=dim S_F`, and let `z` be their average. A full-dimensional simplex inside `S_F` makes `z in relint(S_F)`. Also `z in relint(F)`: any original facet not containing `F` is strict at `x`, so it is not identically tight on `S_F`; it is then strict at every point of `relint(S_F)`. The anchor's common denominator obeys

`Dz <= (q+1) Bv^(q+1)`.

This is an original-input bit bound even if the face is extremely narrow. Exact slice LPs plus affine-span expansion construct the vertices under O1. These are slice vertices, often fractional; requiring Boolean slice vertices would be incorrect.

## 3. An explicit same-face, exact-cost reset

Find a rational column basis `Z_1,...,Z_q` of `lin(S_F-S_F)` and normalize **each column** by a rational factor so that `||Z_l||_infinity<=1`. A particularly simple explicit choice uses the already obtained slice vertices `v0,...,vq`: set `Z_l=(v_l-v0)/2`. These columns are independent and every signed-cube coordinate difference has magnitude at most two; their bits have the original-input bound of section 2. RREF/free-coordinate bases followed by scaling also work. The normalization is an essential missing detail in a bare phrase such as “round in an exact slice basis”: an arbitrary rescaling of a basis can magnify a fixed coefficient mesh without bound. For a concrete failed implication, take `F=[-1,1]`, zero cost, anchor `z=0`, an allowed coefficient mesh `s`, and basis `Z=1/s`. An admissible mixed point `x'=1/2` has coefficient `s/2`; either nearest tie choice rounds its physical value to `0` or `1`, with error `1/2`, however small `s` is. Normalization repairs this failure; it is not a counterexample to the explicitly normalized routine.

Set `alpha=delta/(8n)` and `x'=(1-alpha)x+alpha z`. For every original inequality not tight on `F`, its slack at the rational anchor is at least `1/Dz`, and its slack at `x'` is at least `alpha/Dz`.

Express `x'-z = sum_l beta_l Z_l`. Choose a dyadic rational mesh

`s <= alpha/(16 Dz Cf n^3)`

and round each `beta_l` to the nearest multiple of `s`, giving `xhat=z+sum_l betahat_l Z_l`. Then

`||xhat-x'||_infinity <= q s/2 <= n s/2`,

and each facet perturbation has magnitude at most

`n^2 Cf s/2 <= alpha/(32 Dz n) < alpha/Dz`.

Every previously strict original inequality stays strict; every original face equation and the cost equation stay exactly equal. Hence `xhat in relint(F)`, with the **same original minimal face**. Frozen coordinates remain exactly frozen. Also

`||x'-x||_2 <= 2sqrt(n)alpha = delta/(4sqrt(n))`,

`||xhat-x'||_2 <= n^(3/2)s/2`,

which together imply `||xhat-x||_2<delta`. When `q=0`, the slice is a single rational anchor and the rounding part is unnecessary.

This proves that a reset after an exact face hit need not re-enlarge the original face. Using an anchor from the preceding, larger face would fail: in the rank-two parallel-pair fixture, the new point has Boolean-coordinate first pair `(1/2,1/2)`, whereas an old interior point can have `(3/10,3/10)`. Mixing toward that old anchor gives `z_1+z_2=1-(2/5)alpha<1` while still preserving cost `z_1-z_2=0`, undoing the just-counted rank-face drop. The construction must use the newly recovered face.

## 4. Row reset, barriers, potential, and tracking error

Perform cleanup before reset so that spread and scale inequalities hold; keep retained coefficients and scales fixed during the reset. Write `Delta=xhat-x`, `||Delta||<delta`. For a medium row update

`dhat = round_to_grid_{r delta}(d+b^T Delta)`.

Nearest rounding has error at most `r delta/2`; the following proof even allows error `r delta`. As `||b||_2=sqrt(q)<=r`,

`|(dhat-d)/r| <= 2delta`.

Because both endpoints are in the cube,

`|G(xhat)-G(x)| <= 2 ||p||_2 ||Delta||_2`,

and spread gives `||p||_2<=q/sqrt(K)<=r^2/sqrt(K)`. Therefore

`R |G(xhat)-G(x)|/(2r^2) <= R delta/sqrt(K) <= delta`.

Increasing `e` by `4delta` decreases **both** signed barriers by at least `delta`, independently of how small `r` is. With fixed `p,r`, each exponential Gram coefficient decreases, so this reset gives a genuine Loewner decrease of `W`; the active spectral dimension and backgrounds are unchanged. Hence `Lhat<=L`, and

`Phi(xhat)-Phi(x) <= eta | ||xhat||^2-||x||^2 | <= 3eta sqrt(n)delta`.

This Loewner conclusion belongs to reset with fixed retained vectors. It must not be attributed to arbitrary coefficient deletion: deletion is only entrywise monotone, as the spectral audit explains.

For large rows set `dhat=0`. Large-row motion between resets satisfies `b^T h=0`, and a large row can only lose mass, so its stored `d` is zero before this reset. Ignoring `b^T Delta` creates row-tracking error at most `sqrt(n)delta`. For medium rows the grid creates error at most `Rdelta`; coefficient deletion and scale changes keep `d` fixed and create no numerical tracking error. Define the tracked quantity as the sum of each coefficient's movements while it remains retained; conceptual offsets are held fixed on segments. Its difference from stored `d` therefore accumulates at most

`M(n+R+1)delta`

per row over `M` resets. This is distinct from the deliberate-deletion discrepancy later charged in section 7.

## 5. Full-step and arbitrarily short-step progress

Use S1: `||h|| in [1/2,1]`, `|grad Phi^T h|<=eps_g`, `h^T Hess Phi h<-3eta/16`, and remainder at most `B3 |t|^3/6`. The exact direction lies in the current original face tangent, cost kernel, large-row kernels, and protected-barrier kernels. The full segment is legal inside `Q`; barriers remain nonpositive by S2.

Let `t_+,t_->0` be the exact endpoints. Move length `s=min(tau,t_+,t_-)`. If `s<tau`, choose a sign attaining that endpoint, irrespective of which sign has a better approximate potential. Compactness, a nonzero tangent direction, and `x in relint(F)` make this a boundary point of `F`. Its minimal original face is a proper subface. Recompute that original face **before** the reset of section 3. All later resets preserve it. Thus the original faces are nested and at most `n` such short steps occur. No integral coordinate needs to appear at the hit.

For either sign and `0<s<=tau`, Taylor gives

`Delta Phi <= eps_g s - (3eta/32)s^2 +(B3/6)s^3`.

Since `B3 tau<=eta/8`, a full step satisfies

`Delta Phi <= eta tau^2 [1/(128n)-7/96]
 <= -(25/384)eta tau^2 = -(25/6)D`.

The reset charge is at most `3D/(2^12 n^(3/2))`, so a full move, including reset and cleanup, decreases potential by more than `4D`, hence certainly by `D`.

For a short step the quadratic and cubic terms sum to a nonpositive quantity, giving

`Delta Phi <= eps_g tau = D/(2n)`.

The same reset charge is `<D/(2n)`, so the post-reset increase is `<D/n`. Arbitrarily small steps need no positive lower bound: they are charged to strict face drops.

Initially `Phi<=0` by S2. With `f` full steps and `j<=n` short steps,

`-eta n <= Phi <= -f D+j D/n <= -fD+D`.

Thus `f<=eta n/D+1` and total moves `M<=eta n/D+n+1<S`. This also justifies a hard cap of `S`: up to that cap all allowance bounds below hold, and failing to terminate before the cap would contradict the potential bound. There is no circular assumption that a numerical allowance stays small for an unbounded run.

The cleaned-state spectral premise propagates. Since `Phi<=D`,

`(L-8)_+^3 <= eta n+D < 1`.

Indeed `eta n=1/K<=1/64` and `D<=eta/1024`. Therefore `L<9` at every cleaned state. The local spectral audit supplies its uniform segment bounds starting from such a state; the proof does not assume that `L(t)<9` throughout a segment.

Up to `M<S` resets,

`e=4Mdelta < 1/[2^10(n+R+1)] <= 1/1024`,

`tracking_error < M(n+R+1)delta < 1/4096`.

These bounds maintain the spectral cleanup assumption `e<=1/4` with substantial slack.

For these parameters `tau=1/(2^43 K^3 n^3)`, `D=1/(2^92 K^7 n^7)`, and `S=2^93 K^6 n^7+2n+4`. The count is polynomial in `n,k`, but its constant is enormous. This is a complexity construction, not evidence of practical running time.

## 6. Persistent bits: why this is not a growing-denominator recurrence

Let `B` be total original binary input size for this call, including `A,x0,c`, dimension parameters, and oracle query-cost encodings. The following components regenerate from that input rather than from the current denominator:

* Original face equations: integer bit bounds polynomial in `n log Cf`, by section 1. Their right-hand sides are bounded integers even if computed from a high-denominator current point.
* Slice vertices/anchor: section 2 bounds bits by `O(n(log Q0+log ||C||+log n))`; choosing a new face does not change that universal bound.
* Slice basis: rational elimination of the bounded face/cost equations and column normalization gives polynomial-bit `Z`. Any nonsingular `q x q` row minor has determinant magnitude at least `2^(-poly(B,n))`; Cramer's rule gives coordinate magnitude `|beta_l|<=2^poly(B,n)` because `x',z` stay in the cube. The **magnitude** bound controls the rounded integer grid indices, even if the unrounded `beta` denominator is huge.
* Mesh: choose `s=2^(-J)` with `J=ceil(log_2(16 Dz Cf n^3/alpha))`; `J` is polynomial in original bits. Rounded indices have bit length at most `poly(B,n)+J`, so `xhat=z+Z betahat` has original-input polynomial bits independent of current `Qx`.
* Row masks, coefficients and masses: retained coefficients are original `a_ij` or zero. Their exact sums of squares have polynomial bits. A nonzero mass is at least the square of the smallest nonzero encoded original coefficient, so the scale-halving count is `O(B+log n)`. Use `r=R 2^(-s_i)` with an integer `s_i`, halving until `q<=r^2<4q`. This scaled dyadic convention works for every integer `R`; literal unscaled powers of two need not satisfy `r<=R` (for example `R=11,q=100`).
* Stored displacement: barrier nonpositivity implies `|d|<H` since `G>=0` and `12R-e>0`. After grid reset, `d` lies on the rational grid `r delta` with a bounded integer index, so its bits depend only on original coefficient bits and parameter bits. Large `d=0`, and completed rows retain their last bounded encoding.
* Numerical allowance and counters: `e=4jdelta`, `j<S`, so their bits are polynomial (`log S`, not `S`, controls counter encoding).

Between two resets, S1's rational spectral implementation is polynomial in the current cleaned-state bits and requested precision. Exact face/cost/protected/large/approximate-gradient equations then have polynomial bits; exact rational kernel and symmetric-congruence operations give a polynomial-bit direction. Power-of-two physical normalization adds polynomial bits. One full update or one exact endpoint update has polynomial bits by section 1; tracked `d` is updated once and then immediately reset to its grid. Thus each cycle sees at most one polynomial expansion followed by regeneration to an original-input bound. It is not a recurrence of the form `B_(t+1)=poly(B_t)` without a reset.

This bit proof is conditional on using polynomial-bit **basic** outputs from O1 and the locally polynomial spectral formulas. An arbitrary exact LP implementation returning unnecessarily large rational optima would not satisfy the construction.

## 7. Terminal cost and full discrepancy telescoping

For a fixed row, deliberate deletion of an entry `p=a^2` occurs only if `p>q/K`. Since `|a|<=1`, this implies `q<K` before the first deliberate deletion. Let `q'=q-p`. Then

`|a| = p/sqrt(p) < sqrt(K) p/sqrt(q)
 <= 2sqrt(K)(sqrt(q)-sqrt(q'))`.

Summing deliberate deletions, allowing additional mass decreases from freezing between them, gives

`sum_deliberately_deleted |a| <= 2sqrt(K) sqrt(q_first) <= 2K`.

After an entry is deleted, its remaining signed coordinate movement is at most two. The total omitted signed row contribution is therefore at most `4K`. Freezing a coordinate creates no future omitted movement, since the same-face reset preserves that coordinate exactly thereafter.

When `N<=8k`, optimize `c` over the current original face `F`. Its signed Boolean optimizing vertex `v` has `c^T v<=c^T x=gamma`, hence the required favorable original cost. Original-face membership keeps `v` in `F(y)`. The current retained row's remaining movement is at most `2N<=16k` because every entry of a unit-Euclidean-norm column has magnitude at most one. Medium or completed stored `|d|<H`; large stored `d=0`. In fact full spread cleanup with `N<K` forces every retained mass to be zero: otherwise `max p>=q/N>q/K`. Thus the `16k` charge is conservative after a complete terminal cleanup; retaining it also covers a direct terminal path before cleanup and preserves the reported constant. Combining tracked movement, deliberate deletions, terminal movement, and numerical error gives

`|a_i^T(v-x0)| < H+4K+16k+1`.

Returning to Boolean coordinates divides this signed discrepancy by two:

`||A(b-y)||_infinity < (H+4K+16k+1)/2
 =16R^2+2K+8k+1/2 < B(k)`.
