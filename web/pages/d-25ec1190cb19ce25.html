# Independent exact marginal extraction gate

Worker: rounding_prior. Date: 2026-10-07 Australia/Brisbane. This note independently reconstructs the extraction reduction requested by root. It does **not** certify the candidate's favorable-cost rounding routine. That routine is the separate load-bearing scientific obligation.

## Legal interface and conclusion

Fix rational A, rational y=a/Q in P⊂[0,1]^n with integer Q≥1, and a hard radius B. A deterministic routine RESPONSE(c), on every rational cost c, returns a Boolean vertex b of P satisfying

    c·(b−y)≤0,       ||A(b−y)||∞≤B.

Its running time and its original strong-optimization/independence queries must be polynomial in the original input and the encoding of c. It need not minimize c over its range or over all good vertices. The response's hard bound must be centered at the **same fixed y** on every call. Approximate costs, a probability of a good response, or a bound centered at a changing intermediate point do not give this interface.

Under this interface, a deterministic polynomial-bit algorithm returns an explicit law with mean exactly y, at most dim F(y)+1≤n+1 positive atoms, and radius B on every atom. Its number of RESPONSE calls is O(n³(log Q+log n)). Extraction itself introduces no discrepancy inflation. The derivation below closes this mathematical gate; no full end-to-end implementation of D is claimed.

## 1. Uniform bounded separator for every finite collected set

For collected Boolean vertices S, define

    K_S={c∈R^n : c·(b−y)≥1 for every b∈S}.

The following equivalence is exact: K_S is nonempty if and only if y∉conv(S). One direction follows by averaging the inequalities. In the other direction, strict separation from the finite compact convex hull gives a positive gap, which can be scaled to one. With rational data, a rational solution follows as well.

If K_S is nonempty, minimize t under

    −t≤c_i≤t,   t≥0,   (Qb−a)·c≥Q  for b∈S.

The optimum exists: restricting t by the value of any feasible point makes c bounded. This polyhedron is pointed; its bounded optimum face has a vertex. Thus an optimum is obtained by n+1 linearly independent tight inequalities. Their integer coefficients and right-hand sides have absolute value at most Q, since 0≤a_i≤Q. Cramer's rule and the determinant expansion give

    ||c_0||∞≤R0,    R0=(n+1)! Q^(n+1).

The denominator determinant is a nonzero integer of absolute value at least one. Neither R0 nor its bit length depends on |S|, on a RESPONSE query, or on the geometry of P. A larger-than-necessary common input denominator Q is harmless; log Q is charged to the rational input.

Double c_0. Every separator inequality at 2c_0 has slack at least one. Every vector b−y has Euclidean norm at most √n. Therefore the Euclidean ball of radius r=1/(2n) around 2c_0 lies in K_S, because a perturbation changes any inequality by at most √n/(2n)≤1/2. This ball also lies in the box |c_i|≤4R0. Consequently the boxed K_S, whenever nonempty, is full dimensional regardless of the affine dimension of P or conv(S).

For a rational volume threshold, this ball contains the cube centered there of coordinate radius 1/(2n²), whose volume is n^(−2n). Take

    ε=(2n²)^(−n) < n^(−2n).

This is the positive volume certificate used below. Merely failing to locate a separator would not be enough.

## 2. Retrospective transcript containment

Begin with the ball E0 of radius R=4nR0 centered at zero; it contains the entire separator box. At each rational ellipsoid center z:

* If a box constraint is violated, take its central halfspace containing the box.
* Otherwise query RESPONSE(z), record b, and take the central halfspace

      (b−y)·(c−z)≥0.

  This halfspace contains the new separator constraint c·(b−y)≥1 because z·(b−y)≤0. If b−y=0, y is already that Boolean vertex and the one-atom law finishes the task.

For the final collected set S, the boxed K_S lies in **every** halfspace used, including the earlier ones. An outward central-cut update therefore ensures that this final boxed system lies in every maintained ellipsoid. The set need not be known in advance and the response map need not optimize over its range. No membership answer for a global set of good vertices is used.

After enough cuts, the ellipsoid volume is less than ε. If K_S were nonempty, section 1 would put a ball, hence a cube of volume strictly greater than ε, inside it. This contradicts containment. Therefore K_S is empty, and y∈conv(S).

The proof is the same substantive transcript argument as Cai–Daskalakis–Weinberg, Lemma 3.2 and its omitted proof; their Appendix G.1 proves a uniform rational separation gap and nonzero-volume guarantee independent of the transcript. This use is prior-derived, not a novel result of D. The exact constants R0 and r above are our specialized Boolean reconstruction.

## 3. A concrete finite-precision outward update

Here is a conservative explicit implementation lemma, independently derived to make the bit obligation reviewable. GLS Theorem 3.2.1 and Lemmas 3.2.8–3.2.10 already prove the corresponding standard finite-precision result, including containment, eigenvalue bounds, polynomial encodings, and a contraction at most exp(−1/(5n)). Their printed pages are 87–94 (PDF pages 99–106).

For n≥2 write E(M,z)={x:(x−z)^T M^(−1)(x−z)≤1}. Orient a nonzero rational cut g so that the retained halfspace is g·(x−z)≤0. Normalize ||g||∞=1 exactly. Set s=g^T M g and define the ideal central update

    z*=z−Mg/((n+1)√s),
    M*=n²/(n²−1) [M−(2/(n+1)) Mgg^T M/s].

The ideal shape M* is rational. In coordinates whitening M, let u be a point of the old unit ball and let t∈[−1,0] be its component along the cut. Its new ellipsoid quadratic form is at most

    1+[2(n+1)/n²] t(t+1)≤1.

Thus the ideal ellipsoid contains the retained half. Its shape eigenvalues relative to M are n²/(n+1)² along the cut and n²/(n²−1) in the other directions. In particular,

    (4/9) M ≼ M* ≼ (4/3) M.

The exact volume multiplier has logarithm

    −Σ_{j≥1} n^(−(2j−1))/[2j(2j−1)] ≤ −1/(2n).

Fix a maximum cut count N in advance. Let τ=1/(32n²), p=2N+ceil(log2(128n³)), and δ=2^(−p). The invariant below gives λ_min(M*)≥R²4^(−N), with R≥1. Approximate each coordinate of z* to absolute error at most δ, obtaining a dyadic center z'. Round the symmetric entries of M* to the nearest δ grid, obtaining Mbar, and put

    M'=(1+τ)² [Mbar+nδ I].

Symmetric entry rounding has operator error at most nδ. Hence Mbar+nδ I≽M*. Also

    Mbar+nδ I≼M*+2nδ I≼(1+τ)M*,

since 2nδ≤τ R²4^(−N). The center error measured in the M* inverse metric is at most

    √n δ / √λ_min(M*)≤τ.

The triangle inequality then gives E(M*,z*)⊂E((1+τ)²M*,z')⊂E(M',z'). Thus **every** retained point remains in the new rational ellipsoid. Conversely M'≼(1+τ)³M*, so volume inflation is at most (1+τ)^(3n/2)≤exp(3/(64n)). Combined with the ideal contraction, every update contracts volume by at most exp(−29/(64n))≤exp(−1/(4n)).

The same inequalities give λ_min(M')≥λ_min(M)/4 and λ_max(M')≤2λ_max(M). Starting from M0=R²I, induction yields

    R²4^(−j)≤λ_min(M_j)≤λ_max(M_j)≤R²2^j.

The center displacement is at most √λ_max(M_j)/(n+1)+√n δ. Summing gives center magnitude at most 2^(O(N))R, so all integer parts have O(N+log R+log n) bits. Stored center denominators are 2^p; stored shape denominators divide 2^p(32n²)². Rounding after each update prevents a denominator recurrence.

Only the reciprocal square root in z* requires approximation. Since ||g||∞=1, s≥λ_min(M)≥R²4^(−N), so 1/√s≤2^N/R. Rational bisection on [0,2^N/R], using the exact comparison q²s≤1, finds an interval narrow enough to make every center coordinate error at most δ/2; rounding to the nearest δ grid uses the remaining δ/2. The number of bisections and all temporary rational encodings are polynomial in N, log R, log Q and n. M*, s and the rational products/divisions have polynomial encodings before each rounding. No fixed-precision floating-point or exact irrational arithmetic is assumed.

For example, choose integers

    L=ceil(log2(2R)),
    H=n[1+2ceil(log2 n)],
    N=4n(nL+H+1).

Then vol(E_N)≤exp(−N/(4n))(2R)^n<ε. Since log R=O(n(log Q+log n)), N=O(n³(log Q+log n)). Every center used as a cost has polynomial encoding, and at most N calls to RESPONSE occur (box cuts need none). The inner oracle computations are therefore charged at polynomial-sized costs. Parameters are independent of the response's running-time details and its generated vertices, whose encoding is always n Boolean bits.

For n=1, use costs +1 and −1. A non-Boolean y forces the two Boolean responses 0 and 1, with weights 1−y and y; Boolean y is immediate. This avoids the n²−1 denominator in the central update. The zero-dimensional case is immediate.

The finite-precision construction is a polynomial-bit algorithmic proof. It is not evidence that an ordinary fixed-precision library, or an uninflated rounded update, satisfies the gate.

## 4. Exact weights, support, and their bits

The explicit system

    Σ_{b∈S} λ_b b=y,    Σ_{b∈S} λ_b=1,    λ_b≥0

is feasible by section 2. Solve it with an exact rational LP algorithm and obtain a basic feasible solution, or reduce affine dependencies exactly. GLS Theorem 6.4.9 and vertex extraction Lemma 6.5.1 supply a polynomial-bit exact LP interface; here all constraints are explicitly listed and S has polynomial size.

For affinely independent positive support of size r, choose an invertible r×r minor C of the augmented Boolean column matrix [b;1]. It has entries zero or one. Cramer's rule makes every weight denominator divide Q|det C|. Hadamard gives

    |det C|≤r^(r/2)≤(n+1)^((n+1)/2).

Thus a common weight denominator is at most Q(n+1)^((n+1)/2), and each weight has O(log Q+n log n) bits. The bound concerns final positive-support weights, rather than arbitrary redundant LP encodings.

Every supported vertex automatically belongs to the original minimal face F(y): an exposing linear inequality tight at y has nonnegative slack at every vertex of P; its exact weighted mean slack is zero, forcing each positive-weight slack to vanish. Hence affine reduction yields r≤dim F(y)+1. Explicit prior face access is unnecessary for this last implication.

All recorded b satisfy the identical hard bound centered at y, so extracting weights does not change that bound. Exact final checking consists of Boolean feasibility, nonnegative weights, their sum and mean, and rational feature inequalities. No norm bound is inflated to accommodate numerical checks.
