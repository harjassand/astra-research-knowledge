1. The deeper distinction: acceptable decisions overlap without identifying a model
The preceding sensing construction retained multiple models on a measurement fibre and certified one control for all of them. Its nonidentifiability result applied to the selected contrast; it explicitly did not exclude a stronger learner choosing other experiments.    research_note
The new route starts from a different mathematical object.
For model \(\theta\), define its acceptable controls by
\[
\mathcal G_\theta(\epsilon)
=
\left\{
u:
\max_v J(v,\theta)-J(u,\theta)\le\epsilon
\right\}.
\]
A decision requires an action in the true model’s set. It need not identify the model.
These sets need not define equivalence classes. Consider
\[
\mathcal G_0=\{1,2\},\qquad
\mathcal G_1=\{0,2\},\qquad
\mathcal G_2=\{0,1\}.
\]
Any two models share a good action, but all three do not. Identifying one model is sufficient to act, but ruling out one model is also sufficient: after excluding model \(a\), choose action \(a\).
A precise reduction
Suppose a finite family of good-control sets has empty intersection, while every proper subfamily has a nonempty intersection. For any common acquisition interface,
\[
\boxed{
\text{optimal worst-case decision-failure probability}
=
\text{optimal worst-case state-exclusion error}.
}
\]
The proof is constructive. Every action is unacceptable under at least one model; label that action by one such model. An action-valued measurement can therefore be converted into an exclusion measurement without increasing error. Conversely, for exclusion outcome \(i\), choose an action good under every model except \(i\). Minimality guarantees that such an action exists.
This does not invent quantum state exclusion or its semidefinite-programming formulation. Bandyopadhyay, Jain, Oppenheim and Perry established that framework, including optimality and worst-case variants. The contribution to this programme is using certified physical good-control sets to determine which exclusion experiment is actually useful. arXiv
There is a useful limitation. For convex acceptable sets in one real control dimension, a minimal conflict involves only two models. Zero-error exclusion of two nonorthogonal pure states is impossible, just like zero-error identification. The multiway overlap matters; it cannot be presumed for every design problem.
2. A physical task with a genuine decision conflict
I tested this operation on a driven four-site hard-core ring. Sites \(0\) and \(3\) are source and drain; sites \(1,2\) form two paths between them.
The Hamiltonian is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_\phi={}&
-\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in\{(0,1),(0,2),(2,3)\}}
(s_i^\dagger s_j+\mathrm{h.c.})\\
&-(e^{i\phi}s_1^\dagger s_3+\mathrm{h.c.})\\
&+U_Ln_0(n_1+n_2)
+U_Rn_3(n_1+n_2)
+Wn_1n_2+Zn_0n_3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Four interaction coefficients are independently uncertain:
\[
\boxed{
(U_L,U_R,W,Z)
\in
(4,3,1,2)+[-0.04,0.04]^4.
}
\]
The dynamics includes injection at rate \(2/5\), controlled drain rate \(\kappa\), and physical pure dephasing \(d=0.01\), with hopping set to one. The output is the stationary throughput
\[
J(\phi,\kappa;\xi)
=
\kappa\,\operatorname{Tr}(n_3\rho_{\mathrm{ss}}).
\]
This is not the earlier macroscopic coefficient \(D\). It is a finite-density nonequilibrium output, calculated with all occupation sectors present. No interaction is discarded. As a diagnostic, approximately 29.4% of the nominal stationary probability lies in sectors with at least two particles; those sectors contribute materially to the current.
Interference-sensitive transport in small quantum devices is established physics. The phenomenon of controlling current through phase-dependent interference is not itself a discovery of this run. arXiv
The uncertainty and controls
After common characterization, a fresh, persistent phase fault is promised to be one of
\[
\theta_j=\frac{2\pi j}{3},
\qquad j=0,1,2.
\]
The available commands are
\[
\alpha_a=\pi-\theta_a,
\qquad
\kappa\in\{2,4,8\}.
\]
This gives nine nominal controls. The actual flux is \(\phi=\theta_j+\alpha_a\).
When \(a=j\), the flux is \(\pi\). Otherwise it is \(+\pi/3\) or \(-\pi/3\). Arm-exchange symmetry and a gauge transformation prove that the two latter fluxes have the same throughput throughout the four-dimensional uncertainty box.
The exact physical certificates establish that the two nonmatching phase commands at drain rate \(4\) are optimal among the nine controls, for every admitted interaction model.
Writing \(g_4\) for the good-phase current and \(b_4\) for the matching, bad-phase current,
\[
\boxed{
0.04802918213
<
g_4(\xi)-b_4(\xi)
<
0.05046011754.
}
\]
The best competing good-phase drain setting, \(\kappa=8\), is worse by at least \(0.00320605179\). Thus the good-action overlap is a consequence of the interacting calculation, not a loss function chosen independently of the device.
The optimum is within this specified menu. Continuous-flux or unrestricted drain-rate optimality is not claimed.
3. Construct the experiment from the nuisance operators
The correct probe state is not supplied.
The compiler enumerates the occupation-state pairs connected by the unknown link and evaluates their eigenvalue differences under all four uncertain interaction templates. Only one connected pair has zero difference in every nuisance direction:
\[
|0100\rangle,\qquad |0001\rangle.
\]
This identifies a two-level probe space in which the interaction uncertainty disappears exactly.
Prepare \(|0100\rangle\), freeze the other hopping edges and reservoirs, retain the original dephasing, and expose the isolated link for
\[
T=\frac{\pi}{4}.
\]
The probe state under phase \(j\) is
\[
\rho_j
=
\frac12\left[
I+s\,\mathbf n_j\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma+zZ
\right],
\]
where
\[
\mathbf n_j=(\sin\theta_j,\cos\theta_j,0),
\]
\[
\Omega=\sqrt{4-d^2/4},
\qquad
s=\frac{2}{\Omega}e^{-dT/2}\sin(\Omega T).
\]
The expression for \(z\) is also calculated exactly in the note.
The interactions vanish during this deliberately prepared experiment, not during the stationary transport calculation. This is an experimental way to isolate relevant information, not a one-particle approximation to the physical objective.
The loss determines the readout
For drain rate \(4\), the only harmful outcome is reporting \(a=j\). The resulting optimal readout on this probe is
\[
\boxed{
E_a=\frac13\left[I-\mathbf n_a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\right].
}
\]
These three positive operators sum to the identity. On outcome \(a\), command \(\alpha_a\) and drain rate \(4\).
The measurement is designed to exclude, rather than identify, phase \(a\). Its ideal bad-action probability is
\[
p_{\mathrm{bad}}=\frac{1-s}{3}.
\]
Optimality is checked over all readouts of this probe
For the uniform phase prior, the exclusion problem has the explicit dual certificate
\[
Y=\frac16\left[(1-s)I+zZ\right],
\]
because
\[
\frac{\rho_a}{3}-Y
=
\frac{s}{6}
\left[I+\mathbf n_a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\right]
\succeq0.
\]
Thus every POVM has average exclusion error at least \((1-s)/3\), and the displayed readout attains it with equal error for all three phases.
The drain choice is also verified rather than presumed. Exact payoff-operator inequalities show that assigning any measurement effect to drain \(2\) or \(8\) cannot improve its average payoff over assigning it to drain \(4\), throughout the interaction box.
Consequently, the constructed readout is minimax-optimal over all nine-action POVMs on the prepared probe, not merely better than two selected quadratures.
Global optimality over every possible preparation and interrogation schedule is not claimed.

5. A fair bound against every phase-identification strategy
This is the main improvement over the previous contrast-only example.
The identification competitor may choose arbitrary preparations, ancillary systems, known controls, measurements, resets and feedback. It can also be given the exact four interaction coefficients, strengthening its access.
The common restrictions are:
- The unknown phase-sensitive link has hopping magnitude at most one.
- Total exposure to that unknown interaction, summed across specimens and all attempts, is at most \(\pi/4\).
- No free post-fault phase record, additional phase-sensitive coupling or amplified unknown interaction is available.
The exposure restriction is a hard total budget, not merely parallel wall time or an expected-time constraint. Known processing can be supplied free to the lower-bound competitor. The calculation therefore cannot win just by charging a competing learner more for classical computation.
For any pair of different phase hypotheses, and any common commanded phase,
\[
\|H_i-H_j\|=\sqrt3,
\]
with spectral range \(2\sqrt3\). This equality holds on the complete four-site Hilbert space.
An infinitesimal unknown-interaction exposure can increase the Bures angle between the two possible states by at most \(\sqrt3\,dt\). Known controls and common noise do not increase this bound. Ancillas, deferred measurements and adaptive query scheduling preserve it when total exposure is charged.
Therefore,
\[
\frac12\|\rho_i^{\mathrm{out}}-\rho_j^{\mathrm{out}}\|_1
\le
\sin(\sqrt3T).
\]
The binary discrimination error is consequently at least
\[
\frac{1-\sin(\sqrt3T)}2.
\]
At \(T=\pi/4\),
\[
\boxed{
p_{\mathrm{identification,error}}
\ge0.011031161767
}
\]
on at least one hypothesis. A uniformly successful three-label identifier would also distinguish any chosen pair, so uniform 99% phase identification is impossible within the common exposure budget.
This uses established Hamiltonian distinguishability and time–accuracy reasoning; the specific bound is reconstructed in the note rather than presented as a new quantum speed limit. arXiv
The result is a strict, finite-budget separation:
\[
\boxed{
\text{near-optimal physical action at }>99.819\%
\quad\text{while}\quad
99\%\text{ phase identification is impossible}.
}
\]
It is not an exponential sensing advantage. More exposure or stronger phase-sensitive access can change the conclusion.
Nor does it imply that all “learn-then-act” software performs poorly. A decision-aware learner that stops trying to identify the phase can implement the exclusion policy itself.

Readout is not an ideal oracle
The three-outcome readout has an explicit constant-size isometry and can be implemented using an empty occupation channel and known, phase-independent controls.
However, retaining a \(0.0005\) total-variation budget requires rapid, accurate readout. One conservative construction uses known control amplitude \(4096\) times the hopping scale; its readout duration is bounded by \(20\pi/4096\). Original dephasing then contributes less than \(0.000154\) to the error budget. The remaining preparation, switching, timing and calibration errors must fit the rest.
This is a demanding, untested hardware requirement, not an experimentally attained fidelity. The packaged timing command is rounded downward with a jitter guard so that the actual unknown-link exposure stays below \(\pi/4\).
Preparation, loading, reset and readout time remain additional resources. The result selects a control for stationary throughput; it does not bound the transient time needed to recover that throughput after switching.

8. Verification and the remaining scientific barrier
The executed checks include six exact certificate reconstructions, 462 independent full-state checks of the robust response polynomials, thirty phase-reflection checks, thirty-six direct physical probe calculations and full-Hamiltonian difference-norm checks.
An explicitly constructed readout isometry was verified. Random POVM comparisons passed, but optimality rests on the analytic dual certificate—not on those tests. A deliberately corrupted proposed inverse was rejected.
These checks support the finite algebra and implementation. They do not constitute independent mathematical review.
The strongest conclusion is:
A scientist may need to eliminate incompatible actions, not determine which microscopic model is true. The useful information object is the overlap structure of verified good-action sets.

In this example, that overlap is discovered from the interacting device calculation; the nuisance-insensitive probe is found from the Hamiltonian templates; the readout follows from the physical losses; and the advantage survives allowing competing identifiers other experiments.
The limitations remain substantial. The three-phase promise and finite action menu are restrictive. The fast readout is not demonstrated. The offline loss calculation still enumerates a small Hilbert space. Nothing here proves cheap construction of these objects for extended matter, continuous disorder or arbitrary controls.
The next consequential theorem would construct small, certifiable decision-conflict structures and physically realisable experiments for extended interacting systems, without first enumerating the models or solving the full many-body response. A small measurement SDP is not enough if obtaining its loss operators is itself intractable.
This run establishes a fair-access physical decision separation and a complete finite construction—not a civilisation-level control principle. Its contribution is to replace “this measurement cannot identify the model” with the stronger statement “within the common acquisition budget, identification is unnecessary work that no alternative identification strategy can complete to the same confidence.”
