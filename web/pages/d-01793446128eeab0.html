# Conditional resource-coupled reachability result

## Measured interface

Fix one engineered effector construct, one target-cell model, an effector-to-target ratio, and an assay horizon (H). Select (J) extracellular nutrient levels (c_1,ldots,c_J), including the zero-supplement control. For each level and each context (z) in a finite preregistered panel, measure two rates under the same CAR-T/tumor coculture. For each fixed context, the resource value is an upper bound and suppression value a lower bound across the full reachable target/effector-density and activation-state envelope; a rate measured only at the initial cell ratio is not assumed valid after target reduction:

- (r_{zj}): total nutrient input required per unit time to hold the extracellular concentration at (c_j). It includes effector uptake, non-effector uptake, chemical loss, and concentration-clamp overhead. It is measured in the coculture, so the energetic and expression burden of the importer is not free.
- (s_{zj}): the target-cell net suppression rate, defined as (-d\log U/dt) over the declared assay window, where (U) is the viable target-cell count. It includes target growth, CAR-mediated killing, and any other measured co-culture effect. Positive (s) means target count decreases.

The context index can include donor, target line, glucose background, oxygen, and other prespecified environmental conditions. Each context-specific resource and suppression bound must hold across the declared reachable density/activation envelope. Expression uncertainty is included by measuring the full engineered population, not by treating a linked fluorescent reporter as an activity oracle. For finite-noise work, replace the true rates by simultaneous upper confidence bounds (r^+_{zj}) and lower confidence bounds (s^-_{zj}).

The starting model assumes that, after concentration clamp settling, the measured rates are stationary over the assay horizon and depend on the current nutrient level and context, not the order of earlier levels. The falsifier protocol tests this assumption. If pulse order changes later response beyond the stated tolerance, the theorem below does not apply to the unaugmented interface.

## Theorem 1: robust budgeted reachability by dose occupation

Let a controller use levels (c_j) for time fractions (pi_j\ge 0), with (sum_j\pi_j=1). Under the stationary measured-rate model, the worst-context substrate requirement and guaranteed log suppression over horizon (H) are

\[
 B^+(\pi)=H\max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sum_{j=1}^J\pi_j r^+_{zj},\qquad
 S^-(\pi)=H\min_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sum_{j=1}^J\pi_j s^-_{zj}.
\]

For a desired target fraction (\rho\in(0,1)), with exact rates this finite-panel model reaches the target iff there is a mixture (\pi\) satisfying

\[
 B^+(\pi)\le B_{\max},\qquad S^-(\pi)\ge \log(1/\rho).
\]

The certificate is the linear feasibility system

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\pi_j\ge0,\qquad \sum_j\pi_j=1,\\
&H\sum_j\pi_j r^+_{zj}\le B_{\max} &&(z\in\mathcal Z),\\
&H\sum_j\pi_j s^-_{zj}\ge \log(1/\rho) &&(z\in\mathcal Z).
\end{aligned}
\]

With exact rates, the specified schedule yields (U(H)/U(0)\le\rho) in every measured context when the constraints are feasible. With finite-sample confidence bounds, feasibility is a conservative certificate. Infeasibility using only the conservative bounds is UNKNOWN, not biological impossibility; a separate optimistic relaxation using lower resource-use and upper suppression bounds would have to be infeasible to reject the finite dose library. This is not a claim about unmeasured contexts or nutrient levels.

### Proof

Let (\pi_j) be the fraction of the horizon spent at level (c_j). In context (z), the cumulative substrate input is the time integral of the measured resource rate, (H\sum_j\pi_j r^+_{zj}). The target log count satisfies

\[
\log\frac{U(H)}{U(0)}=-\int_0^H s(t)\,dt
\]

where s(t) is the realized net log-suppression rate. If the context-specific bounds hold throughout the reachable density/activation envelope, integration gives suppression of at least (H sum_j pi_j s^-_{zj}); the analogous upper integration bounds nutrient input. Requiring those bounds in every context gives the displayed linear certificate. Conversely, any feasible vector (\pi) is implemented by spending (\pi_jH) at each tested level. For exact stationary rates this is the exact finite-grid reachability criterion. With confidence intervals it is a sufficient certificate, and failure to certify remains UNKNOWN. Concentration-transition intervals must be included as measured levels/costs or be negligible by a tested clamp-settling criterion.

## Theorem 2: finite support and the single-context frontier

For one context with known stationary rates, the average resource/suppression pair generated by any dose schedule lies in

\[
\operatorname{conv}\{(r_j,s_j):j=1,\ldots,J\}.
\]

Every point in this two-dimensional convex hull has a representation using at most three levels by Carathéodory's theorem. A point on the upper suppression frontier at a fixed resource budget uses at most two levels: it lies on a vertex or edge of the upper hull. Thus an arbitrarily long concentration schedule cannot outperform the finite upper hull, and a frontier point can be implemented with one or two clamps (plus explicitly charged transitions). With finite-sample interval bounds, the hull of (r^+,s^-) pairs is an inner certificate region, not the exact biological reachable set.

For (M) contexts, the joint response vector has dimension (2M), so any point in the joint finite-panel hull has a representation using at most (2M+1) levels. This bound is a representation bound, not a statement that the sample size needed to acquire the (2M) response coordinates is small.

## Theorem 3: exclusivity removes competitor uptake from the invariant

Let (C(t)) be a private nutrient concentration, (E(t)\le E_{\max}(t)) engineered effector density, (U(t)\le U_{\max}(t)) target density, and

\[
\dot C(t)=u(t)-\ell(C,t)-E(t)v_E(C,t)-U(t)v_U(C,t),
\]

where (u) is the controlled supply and all terms are nonnegative. Suppose (C(0)\ge C_{\min}>0), and for all declared contexts and (C\ge C_{\min}),

\[
\ell(C,t)\le\bar\ell(C_{\min}),\quad v_E(C,t)\le\bar v_E(C_{\min}),\quad v_U(C,t)\le\bar v_U(C_{\min}).
\]

If, for every time (t),

\[
u(t)\ge\bar\ell(C_{\min})+E_{\max}(t)\bar v_E(C_{\min})+U_{\max}(t)\bar v_U(C_{\min}),
\]

then (C(t)\ge C_{\min}) for the whole horizon. If (\bar v_U=0) in the measured target panel, the target-cell density term disappears. If target-cell uptake is only bounded by (\bar v_U>0), its cost is explicit and grows with (U_{\max}).

### Proof

At the boundary (C=C_{\min}), the displayed supply condition makes (\dot C\ge0) for every allowed effector and target density. A scalar absolutely continuous trajectory cannot cross downward through a boundary at which its vector field is nonnegative. Hence ([C_{\min},\infty)) is forward invariant. This argument charges uptake by the engineered cells themselves. It does not assume a constant per-cell activity level, and uses an upper envelope measured in the full joint construct context.

**Shared-fuel no-go under an unbounded competitor.** If a target or another competing cell type has a positive uptake upper bound at the concentration floor, but no finite upper bound on its density is supplied, no finite nutrient supply can certify that floor uniformly: the barrier condition contains competitor density multiplied by a positive uptake term. Under a measured private-fuel gate with zero competitor uptake, that term vanishes. This is a worst-case model statement, not a claim that real tumor populations are unbounded.

## A concrete falsifiable threshold

For a 48-hour in-vitro co-culture and a prespecified tenfold target reduction, the required net suppression rate is

\[
\frac{\log 10}{48\ \mathrm h}=0.04797\ \mathrm h^{-1}.
\]

Thus, if the simultaneous lower confidence rate at a clamped cellobiose level is at least (0.04797\ \mathrm h^{-1}) in every context, and the simultaneous upper confidence input rate fits within the declared nutrient budget, the model predicts target counts no greater than 10% of their starting counts at 48 hours. A held-out paired coculture above that bound after all measurement gates pass falsifies the model-to-biology mapping. The 48-hour and tenfold values define a proposed assay target, not a clinical threshold.

## What is not proved

- No rate, concentration, expression distribution, tumor exclusivity, or killing coefficient has been estimated in this work.
- The cellobiose pathway's flux-to-function curve may be history-dependent; the recent early-methionine result makes this a live failure mode.
- A finite panel only certifies its named contexts and tested concentration grid.
- If kinetics depend on a latent state, either observe a validated state variable and augment the model or return `UNKNOWN`; do not condition on an unmeasured activity oracle.
- This theorem does not compare clinical outcomes or prove that cellobiose is safe, tumor-exclusive in all cancers, or superior to every other metabolic pathway.
