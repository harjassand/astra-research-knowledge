COMMUTING SCALAR-RESOLVENT PIT: POLYNOMIAL HITTING LIST AFTER THE SEPTEMBER 2026 PD RESULT
Date: 7 October 2026. Scope: characteristic-zero exact scalar point evaluation.

DECISIVE RESULT

The nilpotent polynomial subclass has an explicit deterministic poly(n,m) hitting set, as a direct corollary of Bhardwaj--Saptharishi (28 September 2026), not a new standalone PIT breakthrough. In fact arbitrary commuting m-state scalar resolvents have a poly(n,m) rational hitting list if poles can be recognized/skipped. Thus an argument that this particular commuting interface is blocked by currently quasipolynomial commutative-ROABP PIT is outdated. General commutative ROABPs remain a broader class and are not covered by this implication.

Primary load-bearing source: Shubham Bhardwaj and Ramprasad Saptharishi, Hitting Sets for Polynomials with Small Partial Derivative Spaces, arXiv:2609.34436v1, 28 September 2026.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34436
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34436

IMPORTED THEOREM AND AUDIT DEPTH

Their Theorem 1.1 gives, over any characteristic-zero field, G_i(t)=sum_{j=1}^B (it)^j/j with B=nr^2+r, preserving nonzeroness for every polynomial of partial-derivative dimension at most r. A total-degree-d polynomial then has a hitting set {G(t):t in T} for any dB+1 distinct field elements T. The stronger formal-series assertion is Theorem 3.1. I read its complete proof and the preceding load-bearing Lemmas 2.2, 2.6, 2.7, 2.9. The mechanism uses a derivation with trivial constants, an x-independent nonzero Wronskian, and degree versus vanishing-order contradiction. The derivative-closed basis must be chosen adapted to the degree filtration; this repairs an implicit basis-choice detail without changing the argument. This is a newly posted primary preprint with an AI disclosure; the present read is an internal reconstruction, not external certification.

DIRECT RESOLVENT REDUCTION (DERIVED HERE)

Let F have characteristic zero; let n,m>=1. Let A_1,...,A_n in F^{m x m} commute pairwise, lambda in F^{1 x m}, gamma in F^{m x 1}. Put X(x)=sum_i x_i A_i and
  f(x)=lambda (I-X(x))^{-1} gamma,
  p(x)=sum_{k=0}^{m-1} lambda X(x)^k gamma.
The matrix pencil is invertible formally at x=0. No representation information beyond n,m is needed to construct the points below.

Lemma 1: p has total degree <=m-1 and PD dimension <=m^2.
For a multiindex beta with q=|beta|, commutativity gives
  partial^beta p(x)
    =sum_{k=q}^{m-1} [k!/(k-q)!] lambda X(x)^{k-q} A^beta gamma.
For each fixed q in {0,...,m-1}, define a linear map
  L_q(v)=sum_{ell=0}^{m-1-q} [(ell+q)!/ell!] lambda X(x)^ell v,
from F^m to F[x]. Every order-q derivative is L_q(A^beta gamma), hence spans a space of dimension at most m. Taking the sum over the m possible derivative orders proves PDdim(p)<=m^2. Derivatives of larger order vanish. This is a proof, not a finite-test inference.

Lemma 2: f is the zero rational function iff p is the zero polynomial.
One implication follows from the formal expansion f=sum_{k>=0} lambda X^k gamma. Since the summands are homogeneous of distinct degrees, p=0 means each lambda X^k gamma=0 for 0<=k<m. Cayley--Hamilton for X over F[x] expresses every X^k, k>=m, as an F[x]-linear combination of lower powers; induction makes every subsequent scalar moment zero. Therefore f=0. This part needs no commutativity.

NILPOTENT POLYNOMIAL SUBCLASS: FULLY LEGAL ORDINARY HITTING SET

Assume the commuting matrices are nilpotent, equivalently here their generated ideal acts nilpotently; simultaneous triangularization over an algebraic closure gives X(x)^m=0. The identity descends to F. Then f=p and det(I-X)=1 for every scalar point, so every query is in the matrix-inverse domain.

Use r=m^2, B=nm^4+m^2, d=m-1,
  G_i(t)=sum_{j=1}^B i^j t^j/j,
  T={0,1,...,dB},
  H_nil={ (G_1(t),...,G_n(t)):t in T }.
By Lemma 1 and the imported theorem, this hits every nonzero f in the subclass. It uses at most (m-1)(nm^4+m^2)+1=O(nm^5) exact scalar evaluations. For m=1, the function is constant and T={0} suffices.

The nodes are rational and independent of all A_i, lambda, gamma. Their numerator/denominator bit sizes are polynomial in n,m: a common denominator B! suffices; numerator magnitudes require O(B log(n dB)+B log B) bits with harmless adjustments when d=0. Constructing the list takes polynomial field arithmetic and rational-bit time. This is an oracle-query statement; it does not bound the cost/height of an unknown oracle's returned values or promise stable numerical evaluation.

ARBITRARY COMMUTING RATIONAL RESOLVENT: DOMAIN-AWARE HITTING LIST

Retain the same B,G,T and let S={0,1,...,2m-1}. Define
  H_rat={ s G(t):t in T, s in S }.
If f!=0 then p!=0. Some t_0 in T has p(G(t_0))!=0. Write a=G(t_0) and Y=sum_i a_i A_i. Since p(a)=sum_{k=0}^{m-1}lambda Y^k gamma !=0, at least one of these moments is nonzero, so the univariate rational function f(sa) is nonzero. In the matrix domain,
  f(sa)=N_a(s)/Q_a(s),
  N_a(s)=lambda adj(I-sY) gamma,
  Q_a(s)=det(I-sY).
Here deg N_a<=m-1, deg Q_a<=m, and Q_a(0)=1. Consequently at most (m-1)+m=2m-1 values of s are numerator roots or domain failures. Among the 2m distinct values in S, at least one is a valid point with a nonzero scalar response. Thus H_rat contains a legal nonzero witness, with at most 2m[(m-1)B+1]=O(nm^6) candidates.

Algorithmic interface: the oracle must return a recognizable undefined/pole outcome, or otherwise permit skipping a failing matrix inverse. Then querying the entire list and returning NONZERO on any defined nonzero response is deterministic PIT. If the contract permits querying only points already guaranteed valid without any domain feedback, this algorithm is not such a protocol.

The latter domain issue is elementary: for any fixed nonzero a in F^n choose a linear form ell with ell(a)=1 and a scalar matrix block A_i=ell_i. Then det(I-sum a_i A_i)=0 at a. Hence only x=0 is common to every unknown pencil's domain. Querying that point alone cannot hit, for example, the 2-state nilpotent transfer f(x_1)=x_1. This is a domain-contract obstruction, not a superpolynomial PIT/query lower bound.

IMPORTANT CORRECTION: PD DIMENSION IS NOT GENERALLY <=m

The exponential transfer lambda exp(X) gamma has PD dimension at most m, but the raw resolvent polynomial has multinomial/factorial coefficients. An explicit counterexample to PDdim(f)<=m is m=5: let J be the regular strictly upper-triangular nilpotent Jordan block, A_i=J^i for i=1,...,4, lambda=e_1^T, gamma=e_5. Then
  f=x_4+2x_1x_3+x_2^2+3x_1^2x_2+x_1^4.
The polynomials partial_2 f=2x_2+3x_1^2 and partial_1^2 f=6x_2+12x_1^2 are independent. Together with f, partial_1 f, x_1,1 they span a six-dimensional derivative space (all remaining derivatives lie in that span). Thus PDdim(f)=6>5. The m^2 bound above handles this correctly.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ABOUT COMMUTATIVE ROABPS

Gurjar--Korwar--Saxena's journal Theorem 4.9 gives (ndw)^{O(log log w)} hits for degree-d, width-w ROABPs in every variable order. Their conference version names commuting ROABPs; the journal strengthens the class. This is polynomial for fixed width and quasipolynomial for variable width. I checked the journal theorem and its route through Lemmas 4.7--4.8; it is not poly(n,d,w).
Official primary full journal text:

Official primary conference landing page:

Bhargava--Tengse (2024) connects low PD dimension to small commutative-ROABP width (homogeneous case width<=PDdim). It is a representation theorem, not a polynomial general-width black-box PIT theorem.

Official primary conference landing page:

The reverse implication needed to apply the 2026 PD theorem to arbitrary commutative ROABPs fails: the width-1 polynomial prod_i(1+x_i) has PD dimension 2^n. Its mixed derivatives are prod_{i notin S}(1+x_i), S subset [n], an invertible triangular change of basis from all multilinear monomials. Thus small commuting-ROABP width does not imply polynomial PD dimension. I found no primary-source general-width polynomial commutative-ROABP PIT result in the narrow current search; that absence is a search finding, not a universal literature-proof claim.

FAILED/REJECTED ROUTES AND NEXT DECISIVE ACTION

Rejected: importing noncommutative black-box PIT or using the old general commutative-ROABP quasipolynomial bound as an obstruction for the narrower resolvent class. Rejected: PDdim<=m from exponential coefficients. Rejected: treating H_rat as an all-points-valid hitting set for arbitrary unknown rational domains.

For the parent lower-bound task, the decisive action is to remove any claimed poly-vs-quasipoly barrier for the nilpotent commuting resolvent; cite the 2026 theorem and the explicit m^2 derivative bound. Any remaining lower bound must address a different requirement (e.g. compact query count, safe-domain contract, reconstruction/acquisition, or coefficient/precision costs), because deterministic polynomial scalar hitting is already inherited. The elementary extension to all commuting resolvents is a direct conditional-interface corollary; its priority has not been independently established and should not be sold as novel.
