FULL IID QUTRIT LOWER FROM AN EXACT FLAGGED QUIBIT SUBORBIT
2026-10-08 | independent partial converse / explicit remaining gap

STATUS

INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE, conditional only on the critical-qubit
rank-Q score lemma reproduced below (whose separate proof is in
sol_collision_precision/quantum_branch.txt, §§2-7). This establishes a
nonzero quantum-branch lower bound for the actual positive-c iid qutrit
family. It allows arbitrary classical alphabets and arbitrary changes of
copy-count and Schur-spin sectors. It does not establish the proposed
joint law Q ~= epsilon^(-2) max{1,n^(-1/2)/epsilon}; in particular it does
not recover its cold-orientation epsilon^(-2) factor.

1. CONTRACT AND THEOREM

Fix 1/3<a<1/2 and 0<c0<=c1<infinity. For each sufficiently large n let

  p_n=(a+c/(2 sqrt(n)), a-c/(2 sqrt(n)), b),  b=1-2a,
  c in [c0,c1],
  rho_(U,n)=(U diag(p_n) U^dagger)^(tensor n),  U in U(3).

An archive may be any encoder and decoder factoring through an arbitrary
classical outcome and a quantum register of dimension at most Q on every
outcome. The classical outcome set is unrestricted in the converse. The
decoder reconstructs the full n-qutrit density and the worst-case error is
  e=sup_U (1/2)|| D E(rho_(U,n))-rho_(U,n)||_1.
No frame or plane, purifying reference, or entanglement assistance is
supplied to the archive.

There are constants C>0 and n0, depending only on a,c0,c1, such that every
such archive satisfies

  Q <= 1/(32 r_n)  =>  e >= C r_n/Q,
  r_n := c/(2a sqrt(n)).                                  (1.1)

Equivalently, for a requested error epsilon,

  Q >= min{ C r_n/epsilon, 1/(32 r_n) }.                  (1.2)

This is a lower for the full U(3) orbit because it is proved on a suborbit.
It is not a lower on total hybrid dimension D and does not match the global
joint-filter upper. All labels remain unrestricted, so no implementation
cost is inferred from (1.1).

2. EXACT CPTP EQUIVALENCE TO A VARIABLE-LENGTH QUIBIT EXPERIMENT

Use the fixed plane P=span{e1,e2} only to specify a subfamily for the lower
bound. Let v range over the Bloch sphere and identify the U(2) orbit in P
with

  rho_(v,n)=2a rho_(r_n,v) direct-sum b |e3><e3|,
  rho_(r,v)=(I+r v.sigma)/2.

Indeed the two eigenvalues of the first block are a(1+r_n)=a+c/(2sqrt(n))
and a(1-r_n)=a-c/(2sqrt(n)); the remaining eigenvalue is b. The plane and
its basis are fixed constants of this subfamily, not unknown information
provided to the original archive.

For M=0,...,n set pi_M=Bin(n,2a)(M). Define the variable-length qubit
experiment

  Omega_v = direct-sum_(M=0..n) pi_M rho_(r_n,v)^(tensor M),

where M is an orthogonal classical register. There are parameter-independent
CPTP maps A_n and B_n with

  A_n(Omega_v)=rho_(v,n),       B_n(rho_(v,n))=Omega_v.   (2.1)

To define A_n on an arbitrary input, read its orthogonal M register, sample
uniformly an M-element subset S of {1,...,n}, embed that block's M qubits
into the positions in S, and put |e3> into every other position. In
particular A_n does not resample M. To define B_n, measure each qutrit in the
orthogonal decomposition P plus span{e3}; output the resulting M and the
qubits in the flagged positions. The flag pattern has exactly the binomial
law pi_M, independently of v, so (2.1) is exact. No rare-event or
postselection error is present.

If an archive channel Phi factors through branches of dimension <=Q, then
Psi_n=B_n Phi A_n also factors through branches of dimension <=Q. Every
composite Kraus operator still factors through the same Q-dimensional
branch. By trace-distance contractivity and (2.1),

  sup_v T(Psi_n(Omega_v),Omega_v) <= e.                    (2.2)

The maps A_n,B_n are fixed preprocessing and postprocessing in the lower
proof; the original U(3) archive receives no v, frame, or plane estimate.

3. SPIN SCORE ON THE DIRECT-SUM SOURCE AND TARGET

Schur reduction of each length-M block gives

  rho_(r,v)^(tensor M)
    = direct-sum_(L=M mod 2) P_(M,L) tau_(L/2,v) tensor I_(mult)/f_(M,L),
  tau_(j,v)=exp(beta J_v)/Z_j,  beta=2 atanh(r),
  P_(M,L)=f_(M,L) (sqrt(1-r^2)/2)^M Z_(L/2),

with the usual exact binomial/Schur weights (P_(M,L) sum to one for each
M). Pinching and tracing the parameter-independent multiplicity, while
retaining the classical labels (M,L), is a CPTP map; a decoder can recreate
I/f and invert the Schur transform exactly. Composing these maps around
Psi_n therefore still gives a channel through dimension Q and does not
change the family or its error. Write j=L/2 and

  w_(M,j)=pi_M P_(M,2j).

For j>0 let R_j=Tr(tau_(j,z) J_z), a_j=R_j/(j+1), and set a_0=0. Then
0<=a_j<=1. On the output direct sum use the score

  F_v|_(M,j)=a_j (v.J^(j))/j - (a_j^2/2) I,  j>0;
  F_v|_(M,0)=0.                                           (3.1)

The operator interval is global and independent of M,j:
  -3/2 I <= F_v <= 1/2 I.
The target score is

  int Tr(F_v Omega_v) dv = sum_(M,j) w_(M,j) T_j,
  T_j=a_j^2/2+a_j^2/j,  T_0=0.                           (3.2)

The source and target of the variable-length reconstruction problem have
the same exact weights w_(M,j), so this baseline is identical on both
sides even if the reconstruction channel changes M or L arbitrarily.

4. ARBITRARY BLOCK-TRANSPORT SCORE DEFICIT

Refine a Kraus representation of the reduced channel by source and output
block projections. Refinement cannot increase Kraus rank; it does not alter
the score because the ensemble and F_v are block diagonal. Each resulting
block Kraus map is A:V_j -> V_k, for some source (M,j), output (M',k),
and has rank <=Q. Put

  w_A=||A||_HS^2/(2j+1),
  h_A= sum_i Tr(A J_i^(j) A^dagger J_i^(k))/||A||_HS^2.

Trace preservation gives sum_(M',k,A from fixed M,j) w_A=1. The exact
first-harmonic identity

  int v_i tau_(j,v) dv = R_j J_i/[ (2j+1) j(j+1)]

and (3.1) yield

  int Tr[F_(k,v) A tau_(j,v) A^dagger]dv
      =w_A [ a_j a_k h_A/(jk)-a_k^2/2 ],                 (4.1)

when j,k>0; zero-spin cases are handled below. This identity uses the
source thermal spin j and output score spin k; it imposes no sector-
marginal condition.

Vectorize A. Its normalized Choi vector has Schmidt rank rank(A)<=Q.
Time reversal on the input spin changes J_i^T to -J_i, so h_A is the
expectation of H=-sum_i J_i^(j) tensor J_i^(k). The total-spin decomposition
has maximum eigenvalue jk+min(j,k); write g_Q(j,k)>=0 for the rank-Q
deficit below this maximum. The rank-Q projector estimate gives, whenever
delta=|j-k|<=max(j,k)/(16Q) and max(j,k)>=16Q,

  g_Q(j,k) >= max(j,k)/(32Q).                            (4.2)

This follows by projecting onto eigenvalues within max(j,k)/(16Q) of the
top: that spectral subspace has dimension <=max(j,k)/(2Q), while a
Schmidt-rank-Q unit vector has squared overlap at most Q times that
dimension divided by 2max(j,k)+1. At least a fixed mass lies outside the
cutoff. For arbitrary pairs the same argument supplies a nonnegative
deficit.

Subtract (4.1) from the source-spin target score T_j. Using (4.2) gives

  Delta_(jk) >= (a_k-a_j)^2/2
       + a_j^2/j - a_j a_k/max(j,k)
       + a_j a_k g_Q(j,k)/(jk) >=0.                      (4.3)

The middle term is nonnegative because a_j increases with j and a_j/j
decreases with j; these facts follow from the thermal spin partition
function. If k=0, Delta_(j0)=T_j>=0. If j=0, the output score is
-w_A a_k^2/2<=0 while the source score is zero. Thus every pair of source
and output sectors has nonnegative deficit.

For constants B>=2 and sufficiently small r, the same pair estimate has
the uniform quantitative form

  Delta_(jk) >= c_B r/Q
       for every k>=0 whenever 1/r <= j <= B/(2r),
       and Q<=1/(32r).                                   (4.4)

For near outputs, |j-k|<=max(j,k)/(16Q), insert (4.2) into the last term
of (4.3); on the stated band a_j>=1/3 and a_k>=1/5, giving
Delta>=r/(240 B Q). For far outputs, the first term suffices: with
x=2rj in [2,B], a_j=A(x,r) converges in C^1 to Langevin
L(x)=coth(x)-1/x, whose derivative is strictly positive. The separation
condition implies |a_j-a_k|>=m_B/(16Q), even when k is outside the band,
by monotonicity; hence Delta>=m_B^2/(512Q^2)>=m_B^2 r/(16Q).
Set c_B=min{1/(240B),m_B^2/16}. These estimates are uniform over every
output copy-count M' and spin k.

5. POSITIVE CRITICAL-SPIN MASS FOR THE RANDOM LENGTH

The random length M is Bin(n,2a). Since a<1/2 and a>0, a Chernoff bound
gives Pr{M>=a n}>=1-exp(-c_a n). For every M in [a n,n],

  r sqrt(M) in [c0/(2sqrt(a)), c1/(2a)],

a fixed compact subset of (0,infinity). The critical-qubit Schur mass lemma
therefore supplies B and alpha>0 depending only on a,c0,c1 such that,
uniformly for these M and all sufficiently large n,

  Pr_(L|M){ 1/r <= L/2 <= B/(2r) } >= alpha.            (5.1)

For clarity, the lower half of (5.1) follows from L>=|S_M|, where S_M is
the sum of M independent +/-1 outcomes with mean r; its standardized
threshold at 2/r is uniformly bounded on the compact interval for
r sqrt(M). A uniform bounded-summand CLT gives positive mass above that
threshold. The upper tail follows from the exact Casimir moment
E[L(L+2)]=3M+M(M-1)r^2 and Markov's inequality after choosing B large.
Subtracting the upper tail leaves the displayed band mass.

Combining (5.1) with (4.4), the total source weight of the band in the
variable-length experiment is at least alpha/2 for large n. Since all
other pair deficits are nonnegative and transition weights sum to one,

  int Tr[F_v(Omega_v-Psi_n(Omega_v))]dv
       >= (alpha/2)c_B r/Q.                               (5.2)

For Hermitian F_v with interval width 2, trace duality gives the left side
<=2 sup_v T(Omega_v,Psi_n(Omega_v)). Together with (2.2), this proves

  e >= alpha c_B r/(4Q),

which is (1.1). Every binomial length, every Schur spin, all source-to-output
transitions, and the full output state are included. No typical-sector
replacement is made without retaining its probability in (5.1).

6. WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLOSE

This lower is only Q>=Omega(r/epsilon) below the branch saturation scale.
For epsilon>=r it is vacuous up to constants; for epsilon<r it is weaker
than the global joint-filter branch upper r epsilon^(-3). It does not
provide the independent cold CP2 rank factor epsilon^(-2), and multiplying
separate subgroup lower bounds would be invalid without a tensorized
rank-energy or score theorem. The same-state pure-to-warm counterexample
in IID_TRANSFER_GATE.txt remains a decisive obstruction to importing the
cold pure-carrier converse by a pure-input/warm-output retrieval argument.

The next mathematical gate is a joint rank-Q variational bound for the
actual two-scale U(3) thermal score: two cold complex transverse modes and
one hot critical spin mode must be counted together, with arbitrary
rectangular Schur-block Kraus operators. A plausible spectral-counting
target is low-energy multiplicity of order E^(-2) max{1,r/E}; this would
produce Q~epsilon^(-2)max{1,r/epsilon}, but it is only a heuristic until
the exact score operator, its all-sector transport inequalities, and the
likelihood/trace-distance normalization are proved.
