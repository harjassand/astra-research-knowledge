The following extension is derived here:

(A) Replace signs by independent uniform phases in {1,-1,i,-i}. Each sample
    remains nonnegative, unbiased, and computable in exact polynomial bit
    time. An elementary tensor-coefficient calculation proves

        E X_k^2 <= binom(2k,k) Z_k^2 <= 4^k Z_k^2.

    More generally, replace k by the maximum Johnson distance d between
    nonzero exterior coefficient supports. This improves the low-k guarantee;
    k=Theta(n) still gives exponential worst-case work. No dense-sector FPRAS.

(B) For a weighted monomial permutation matrix, let a_j,b_j be the squared
    alternating edge products on each even permutation cycle. Define

        rho_j=1+2a_j b_j/(a_j+b_j)^2,
        R=product_j rho_j.

    For every sector k with positive norm, E X_k^2/Z_k^2<=R. At full filling,
    if all cycles are even and nonzero, equality holds. Thus high density has
    polynomial variance whenever R<=poly(n), in particular O(log n) even
    cycles, or sum_j min(a_j,b_j)/max(a_j,b_j)=O(log n). One arbitrarily long
    balanced even cycle has relative variance exactly 1/2 at full filling.

(C) An explicit SYMMETRIC, dense, all-positive, uniformly well-conditioned
    rational family has full hard support, full off-diagonal cut ranks for
    EVERY order, yet E X^2/(E X)^2 >= (1/2)(3/2)^(n/2). For independent phase
    sample means/medians of batch means, constant-confidence relative error
    below 1/2 requires at least (3/4)(4/3)^(n/2) total samples. This is an
    estimator lower bound. The family admits an easy count approximation.

These are internally proved algorithmic/variance statements, checked on exact

already exact-countable by the frozen monomial/bounded-frontier Sol algorithms.
The contribution there is a variance classification, not a new tractable class.

1. GENERAL PAIRED FRAME, SAMPLE, AND EXACT BIT INTERFACE

Let V=[u_1,v_1,...,u_N,v_N] be m by 2N over Q(i). For a k-element site set S
write a_S=bigwedge_(j in S, increasing order)(u_j wedge v_j), a vector in the
Hilbert space H=Lambda^(2k) C^m. Its coordinates are det V[R,P(S)]. Set

    Z_k=sum_|S|=k ||a_S||^2 = sum_|S|=k det(V_S^* V_S).

For independent fourth roots z_j let

    A_z=sum_j z_j (u_j v_j^T-v_j u_j^T),
    p(z)=sum_|S|=k a_S z_S,
    X_k(z)=||p(z)||^2.

Expanding the kth exterior power of the two-form A_z gives p: repeated site
terms vanish because u_j wedge v_j is decomposable, distinct degree-two terms
commute, and division by k! removes their ordering multiplicity. Thus each
coordinate of p is Pf(A_z[R]). Character orthogonality E z_S conjugate(z_T)
=1[S=T] proves E X_k=Z_k. If Z_k=0, every a_S=0 and every sample is exactly0.

For the canonical frame u_j=e_j, v_j=(row_j F)^T, expanding the selected
identity columns gives

    Z_k=c_k(F)=sum_(I,J disjoint, |I|=|J|=k) |det F[I,J]|^2.

The transpose in A_z is ordinary transpose, not adjoint. Conjugation appears
in X and Gram norms. A_z=diag(z)F-F^T diag(z) in the canonical case.

As independently reconstructed in c03_l10/c03_l06, complex skew congruence
normal form and exterior norm invariance give

    sum_k X_k(A)t^k = sqrt(det(I+t A^*A)),

with constant term1. This uses the standard exact identity; no numerical
normal form must be acquired. If det(I+t A^*A)=sum d_r t^r, compute
q_0=1 and q_r=(d_r-sum_(j=1)^(r-1) q_j q_(r-j))/2. Coefficients d_0,...,d_k
can be computed by exact determinant interpolation or Newton traces.
The owned code implements Newton traces and this formal recurrence.

Fourth roots cost exactly2N fair bits and lie in Q(i). Exact rational input
denominators can be cleared once. All determinant coefficients, traces and
Pfaffian norms have polynomial bit length by expansion/Hadamard bounds;
there are exponentially many formal terms but only polynomial log term count.
The recurrence uses only polynomially many rational operations on such data.
For example its output height follows directly from a sum of binom(m,2k)
squared rational Pfaffians of degree k, not a numerical square-root condition.
A sample has polynomial bit work in m,N,k and input length. Averaging M
samples adds O(log M) bits after common denominator clearing.

This statement concerns the unweighted canonical F target, or explicit
rational-complex paired frames. Scaling a row by a supplied rational square
root can include corresponding square activities. Arbitrary rational row
activities must not be silently converted to uncharged independent algebraic
square roots. No arbitrary-activity annealing theorem is claimed here.

2. ELEMENTARY SECOND MOMENT, WITHOUT HYPERCONTRACTIVITY

Work in H tensor H. Since ||p||^4=||p tensor p||^2,

    p(z) tensor p(z)=sum_alpha b_alpha z^alpha,
    b_alpha=sum_(S,T:1_S+1_T=alpha) a_S tensor a_T.

Each alpha coordinate is0,1,or2; these monomials are mutually orthogonal on
the fourth-root product grid. If S intersect T has k-l elements, alpha has
2l singleton positions. There are exactly binom(2l,l) ordered decompositions
of alpha into k-sets. Cauchy--Schwarz in the Hilbert tensor product gives

    ||b_alpha||^2 <= binom(2l,l)
                      sum_(S,T:alpha) ||a_S||^2 ||a_T||^2.

Summing alpha proves E X_k^2<=binom(2k,k) Z_k^2. If all nonzero coefficient
sets have Johnson distance |S\T|<=d, every contributing pair has l<=d;
the same argument gives binom(2d,d). This is a mathematical parameter, not
an assumption that support diameter can be cheaply acquired for general V.
The uniform d=k bound requires no support or count oracle.

A batch mean of M>=4 binom(2k,k)/epsilon^2 samples has relative error at most
epsilon with probability at least3/4, by Chebyshev. Median of O(log(1/delta))
independent batches gives failure delta. Hence the sample bound is

    O(binom(2k,k) epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)).

This is a fully specified improvement of the peer's low-sector count bound.
Applying the same proof to the rational projected paired frames in c03_l06
changes its row-prefix/self-reduction sample factor9^k to binom(2k,k), with
the same charged TV and confidence budgets. That sampler consequence is a
derivation; a complete randomized end-to-end sampler is not implemented here.

An exact diagnostic variance identity is also available. For disjoint U,W
of equal cardinality l>0 define

    b_(U,W)=sum_(C disjoint U union W, |C|=k-l)
                <a_(C union W),a_(C union U)>.

Then X=Z+sum_(U,W) b_(U,W) z_U conjugate(z_W), and orthogonality implies

    Var X=sum_(U,W nonempty) |b_(U,W)|^2.                 (1)

Each inner product equals a cross-Gram determinant det(V_T^*V_S).
Equation(1) identifies interference channels exactly. Its generic acquisition
has exponentially many set pairs and is NOT a polynomial variance oracle.

3. HIGH-DENSITY CYCLE CLASSIFICATION

Suppose F[i,p(i)]=w_i, all other entries0, with p a permutation. Its undirected
support is a union of cycles; a two-cycle is one edge with two directed terms.
For an occupied site set R, a partial permutation cycle induces disjoint
paths. Its Pfaffian is zero if a path has odd size; otherwise the path has
one perfect matching, whose coefficient is a fixed product times a phase
monomial. Its squared modulus is deterministic. A fully occupied odd cycle
has zero Pfaffian. A fully occupied even cycle has exactly two alternating
perfect matchings (including the two directed choices on a two-cycle).
Their polynomial is

    p_j(z)=A_j z_(even j)+B_j z_(odd j),

where the signs are fixed Pfaffian signs and |A_j|^2=a_j, |B_j|^2=b_j.
The variable chi_j=z_even conjugate(z_odd) is uniform on fourth roots and
independent between cycles. Thus Y_j=|p_j|^2 is nonnegative and

    E Y_j=a_j+b_j=mu_j,
    E Y_j^2=mu_j^2+2a_j b_j=rho_j mu_j^2.

For arbitrary sector k, summing squared principal Pfaffians therefore has
the form

    X_k=sum_T h_T product_(j in T) Y_j,       h_T>=0,     (2)

where h_T collects all partial-path occupied sets and fixed products.
Odd cycles contribute only deterministic path terms. If mu_j=0 the terms
using that complete cycle vanish; omit it from the moment product.
For two summands T,T', independence gives

    E[(product_T Y)(product_T' Y)]
      =(product_T mu)(product_T' mu) product_(T intersect T') rho
      <=R (product_T mu)(product_T' mu).

The coefficients in (2) are nonnegative, so E X_k^2<=R(E X_k)^2. At full
filling with all cycles even, (2) contains only the product of all Y_j and
the bound is equality. Each rho_j is at most3/2. Therefore O(log n) cycles
give R<=poly(n) at arbitrarily high particle density.

Put r_j=min(a_j,b_j)/max(a_j,b_j) for a_j+b_j>0. Then
rho_j=1+2r_j/(1+r_j)^2 and log R<=2sum r_j. Many highly biased cycles can
therefore also have polynomial variance. These ratios, the cycle decomposition
and R are acquired directly from the zero pattern and edge products in
polynomial bit cost. An optimal-order/support-diameter oracle is unnecessary.

For m independent unit swap blocks, n=2m,k=m,

    p(z)=product_(j=1)^m(z_(2j-1)-z_(2j)),
    Z=2^m,   E X^2=6^m,   Var X/Z^2=(3/2)^m-1.          (3)

For the original Rademacher sampler the block factor is0 or4 equally, so
E X^2/Z^2=2^m. Complex phases improve this factor but do not remove it.
Exact monomial DP from the frozen Sol baseline counts/samples this entire
class in polynomial time. Those exact methods are the competent comparator.

4. DENSE SYMMETRIC FULL-SUPPORT COUNTEREXAMPLE

Let n=2m>=4. Let F0 be the direct sum of m swap matrices [[0,1],[1,0]]. Put

    D=(2n)!,       C[i,j]=D/(i+j+1) (indices0,...,n-1),
    H=D,          B=m!(1+H)^m,          N=binom(n,m),
    h=bit_length(128 N B),              eta=2^-h,
    F_eta=F0+eta C.

C is an integer symmetric all-positive scaled Cauchy matrix; F_eta is real
symmetric and every entry is positive. The description has polynomial bit
length: log D=O(n log n), h=O(n^2 log n), total dense encoding O(n^4 log n).

Every square C subminor is nonzero. Indeed, if columns with distinct y_j=j
were dependent on distinct x_i=i+1, multiply sum_j a_j/(x+y_j) by
product_j(x+y_j). This polynomial has degree at most q-1 and q distinct
roots x_i, so it vanishes identically. Substitution x=-y_j implies a_j=0.

For any square subminor of F_eta of size q<=m, its determinant polynomial
in eta has integer coefficients and sum of their absolute values at most
q!(1+H)^q<=B. Its top coefficient is the nonzero det C subminor, so the
polynomial is nonzero. Its lowest nonzero coefficient has absolute value
at least1. At eta<=1/(128NB), the remaining tail relative to that leading
eta power is at most B eta<=1/(128N)<1. Thus NO such minor vanishes.

Consequences: every full-basis hard configuration I has positive weight;
every F[L,R] and F[R,L] has full rectangular rank for every cut and every
ordering. At a half cut their rank sum is n. Hence the frozen small-cut-rank
and support-frontier exact algorithms do not certify a polynomial bound for
this dense family. A low-rank perturbation is not being hidden: C has full
rank. This statement is about those sufficient structural parameters.

Since ||C||_2<=nH and F0 is orthogonal, all singular values of F_eta lie in
[1-nH eta,1+nH eta]. For m>=2, B>=2H^2 and H>=n, whence nH<=B; thus
nH eta<=1/(128N)<1/32. In particular kappa_2(F_eta)<2, uniformly in n.

Let p_eta(z)=Pf(diag(z)F_eta-F_eta^T diag(z)). Its degree-m coefficients are,
up to fixed signs, the complementary minors det F_eta[I,I^c]. If d_I are
their differences from F0, expansion bounds give |d_I|<=B eta. Therefore

    ||p_eta-p0||_2 <=sqrt(N) B eta,
    |Z_eta-2^m| <=N[2B eta+(B eta)^2] <=3/128.            (4)

The second inequality uses that all F0 minors are0 or +/-1. By Section2,

    ||p_eta-p0||_4 <=binom(2m,m)^(1/4) sqrt(N) B eta
                   <=2^(m/2)/(128 sqrt(N)) <=1/128,

because N>=2^m. Equation(3) gives ||p0||_4=6^(m/4)>=1, so the reverse norm
triangle inequality and (4) imply

    E|p_eta|^4/Z_eta^2
      >= (127/128)^4 6^m / [(1+3/256)^2 4^m]
      >= (1/2)(3/2)^m.                                 (5)

No limiting or numerical argument is used. For T independent samples,
relative MSE of the mean is exactly (E X^2/Z^2-1)/T.

There is also a constant-confidence lower bound. The old polynomial p0 is
nonzero only if the two independent phases differ in EVERY swap block.
This event has probability (3/4)^m. Pointwise, the coefficient l1 bound gives

    |p_eta(z)-p0(z)|<=N B eta<=1/128.

Outside that rare event X_eta<=1/128^2, whereas Z_eta>=2^m-3/128>1.
If all T sampled phase strings avoid the rare event, every batch mean and
their median are below Z_eta/2. The union bound therefore gives

    Pr[relative error below1/2] <=T(3/4)^m.

Success probability3/4 requires T>=(3/4)(4/3)^m. This lower bound concerns
these independent-phase sample-mean/median routines. It does NOT cover a
different estimator, adaptive importance/coupled phases, or an algorithm
that reads and uses the explicit structure of F_eta.

In fact (4) gives an immediately computable excellent approximation 2^m
to this family's count. Thus conditioning, full support and positive entries
do not control this estimator's variance, but the example supplies no count
or sampling hardness theorem. Tiny determinant weights/phase-sample values
are represented exactly with charged polynomial input bits.
