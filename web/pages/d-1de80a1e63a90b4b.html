THEOREM

For each j=1,...,n let d_j be finite and let Phi_j:M_(d_j)->M_(d_j) be
CPTP, unital, TP, HS-self-adjoint and self-compatible, with its local
broadcaster supplied. Let
  V_j=span_R{I,G_(j,1),...,G_(j,k_j)},
where the G_(j,a) are supplied traceless Hermitian involutions with exact
pairwise anticommutation. They need not span M_(d_j), and V_j need NOT
be invariant under Phi_j.

Let D=product_j d_j, Phi=tensor_j Phi_j, and let V be the real linear span
in M_D of ALL product tensors of the local elements of V_j. Then one
canonical EB Psi on the full M_D satisfies
  Tr[A(I-Psi)(A)] <= 2 Tr[A(I-Phi)(A)]
for EVERY Hermitian A in V, including arbitrary entangled linear combinations.
The same Psi controls every A in V. No local or global positive HS spectrum
is assumed. For qubits with all three Pauli directions supplied, V is the
entire Hermitian matrix space, recovering the full signed-qubit theorem.

LOCAL CONSTRUCTION AND SIGNED STAR BOUND

Use tau_j(X)=Tr(X)/d_j. The basis I,G_(j,a) is tau_j-orthonormal. Define
the compressed score matrix
  T_(j,ab)=tau_j[G_(j,a) Phi_j(G_(j,b))].
Hermiticity preservation and HS-self-adjointness make T_j real symmetric.
It is a compression of an HS contraction, so its eigenvalues lambda_(j,a)
lie in [-1,1]. Orthogonal diagonalization rotates the supplied generators
to a new Clifford family G'_(j,a), with
  tau_j[G'_(j,a) Phi_j(G'_(j,b))]=lambda_(j,a)delta_ab.
This is a compressed correlation statement; Phi_j(G'_(j,a)) may still
have a nonzero component orthogonal to V_j.

Fix j and put B_a=sgn(lambda_(j,a))G'_(j,a), with either sign at zero.
The G'_a and B_a are separate Clifford families. On the normalized Choi
state of the supplied two-output broadcaster, their signed star expectation
for a subset S is 2 sum_(a in S)|lambda_(j,a)|. The representation-independent
star lemma bounds this expectation by |S|+1. Choosing S for eigenvalues of
absolute value greater than 1/2 gives
  sum_a mu_(j,a)<=1,  mu_(j,a)=(2|lambda_(j,a)|-1)_+.

Let D_(j,a) be the canonical channel of the two spectral effects
P_(j,a,+/-)=(I+/-G'_(j,a))/2. Explicitly,
  D_(j,a)(X)=tau_j(X)I+tau_j(G'_(j,a)X)G'_(j,a).
Let D_(j,0)(X)=tau_j(X)I and s_j=sum_a mu_(j,a). Set
  Psi_j=sum_a mu_(j,a)D_(j,a)+(1-s_j)D_(j,0).
It is canonical with at most 2k_j+1 effects, and its range lies in V_j.
On V_j its matrix in the rotated basis is
  Q_j=diag(1,mu_(j,1),...,mu_(j,k_j)).

IDENTITY CROSS TERMS AND COMPRESSED MATRIX

Unitality gives Phi_j(I)=I. TP gives
  tau_j[Phi_j(G'_(j,a))]=tau_j[G'_(j,a)]=0.
Therefore both identity/traceless cross entries vanish:
  tau_j[I Phi_j(G'_(j,a))]=0,
  tau_j[G'_(j,a) Phi_j(I)]=0.
The complete compression of Phi_j to V_j is consequently
  F_j=diag(1,lambda_(j,1),...,lambda_(j,k_j)).
The spectral positive absolute operator on the LOCAL TEST HILBERT SPACE V_j
is |F_j|=diag(1,|lambda_(j,1)|,...,|lambda_(j,k_j)|). This need not be a
quantum channel on M_(d_j); no such premise is needed. The local canonical
matrix Q_j obeys
  I-Q_j <= 2(I-|F_j|).

TENSOR COMPRESSION, INCLUDING ALL CROSS TERMS

Let E_(j,0)=I and E_(j,a)=G'_(j,a). The product basis
  E_alpha=tensor_j E_(j,alpha_j)
is orthonormal under tau_D=Tr/D and spans V. For EVERY pair alpha,beta,
the tensor product channel and trace factorization give
  tau_D[E_alpha Phi(E_beta)]
    =product_j tau_j[E_(j,alpha_j) Phi_j(E_(j,beta_j))]
    =product_j (F_j)_(alpha_j,beta_j).
Hence the compressed matrix of Phi on V is tensor_j F_j exactly.
Equivalently, if R_j is the orthogonal projection onto V_j and R=tensor_j
R_j, then R Phi|_V=tensor_j(R_j Phi_j|_(V_j)). Leakage outside one or more
local V_j is orthogonal to every product basis vector and creates no extra
quadratic cross terms. No invariant-subspace assumption enters this identity.

For an arbitrary A=sum_alpha c_alpha E_alpha in V, with real coefficients,
  tau_D[A Phi(A)]
    =sum_(alpha,beta) c_alpha c_beta product_j(F_j)_(alpha_j,beta_j)
    =sum_alpha c_alpha^2 product_j lambda_(j,alpha_j),
with lambda_(j,0)=1. Thus this diagonal compressed spectral calculation
applies to entangled linear combinations as well as simple tensor inputs.

PRODUCT COMPARATOR AND SCALAR BLOCK

Set Psi=tensor_j Psi_j. It is a single full-domain canonical EB map for the
product POVM, with at most product_j(2k_j+1) effects. Its restriction to V
is Q=tensor_j Q_j and it maps V into V. Lemma A of the qubit baseline,
applied solely as a theorem on the real test Hilbert spaces V_j, gives
  I-Q <= 2[I-(tensor_j |F_j|)].
Since |tensor_j F_j|=tensor_j |F_j| and tensor_j F_j<=|tensor_j F_j|,
  I-Q <= 2[I-(tensor_j F_j)].
By the exact compression identity this is the required Phi/Psi quadratic
comparison for every A in V.

Explicitly, on a product basis string put
  L_alpha=product_j lambda_(j,alpha_j),
  U_alpha=product_j mu_(j,alpha_j),  mu_(j,0)=1.
The positive tensor lemma yields U_alpha>=2|L_alpha|-1. Thus
  1-U_alpha<=2(1-|L_alpha|)<=2(1-L_alpha).
Multiplying by c_alpha^2 and summing proves the full simultaneous inequality.
This retains the identity string (L=U=1), zero eigenvalues, and negative
product eigenvalues without a gap or sign exception.

NONINVARIANT EXAMPLE FOR CHECKING THE INTERFACE

On a qubit, take supplied Clifford span V_j=span{I,X,Z} and let Phi_j be
dephasing along H=(X+Y+Z)/sqrt(3). It is canonical EB, self-adjoint, unital
and self-compatible. Its outputs on X and Z have nonzero Y components,
so the displayed local V_j is not invariant. The compressed two-direction
score matrix has all entries 1/3, hence eigenvalues 2/3 and 0, with rotated
generators G'_+=(X+Z)/sqrt(2), G'_-=(X-Z)/sqrt(2). The construction uses
mu_+=1/3 and mu_-=0. On any finite product of such spans the compressed
channel is the tensor product of these local matrices even though the
physical channel has Y leakage. This is an admitted concrete falsification
test of the no-invariance claim, not an assumption that leakage is absent.

AFFINE-FAMILY CONSEQUENCE

For a specified family rho_A=(I+A)/D with traceless A in V and
||A||_infinity<=theta<1, if the supplied product channel has uniformly
T(Phi(rho_A),rho_A)<=epsilon, the same Psi has uniformly
  T(Psi(rho_A),rho_A)<=sqrt(theta epsilon).
The proof is the previously audited full-domain canonical positivity argument:
delta_Phi(A)<=2theta epsilon, delta_Psi(A)<=2delta_Phi(A), and
(I-Psi)^2<=I-Psi. This consequence also includes entangled likelihoods A
inside the product test span; mere product likelihoods are not required.
