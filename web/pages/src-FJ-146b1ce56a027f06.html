# Branching correction sampler with a type-weighted work certificate

**Status:** theorem and exact two-type certificate frozen in this work folder; internal derivation, not externally reviewed. Historical novelty, continuum acquisition feasibility, and computational advantage are **UNKNOWN**.

## Result

The incoming nonlinear-correction theorem uses a constant tube radius and the global scalar condition \(\Gamma(h)<\lambda\). I derived a variable-envelope version whose continuation and nonlinear offspring are controlled by a positive mean-progeny operator \(M\). A positive vector \(w\) with \(Mw\le\alpha w\), \(\alpha<1\), certifies a finite expected tree even when an individual type has more than one expected child. The source, coefficient, and derivative envelopes must still cover all types.

The construction is event-first: at a root type \(x\), select a source/unary/nonlinear event using its resolvent mass, then draw the endpoint from that event's exact tilted resolvent law. Conditional disintegration preserves the centered fixed-point mean while bounding each returned sample by the local tube radius. This changes the sampling order and measure; it does not make the endpoint law or its normalizer free.

## Theorem contract

Let \(L\) generate a conservative Markov process, \(R_\Lambda=(\Lambda-L)^{-1}\) be positive, and \(K=\Lambda R_\Lambda\) be its probability kernel. For

\[
-Lu+F(x,u)=s,\qquad r=s+Lv-F(x,v),\quad V=F_u(x,v),\quad e=u-v,
\]

assume \(0\le V\le\Lambda\). On \(|e(x)|\le h(x)\), where \(0<h_{\min}\le h\le h_{\max}<\infty\), assume

\[
F(x,v+e)-F(x,v)-V(x)e=\sum_{m\ge2}c_m(x)e^m,\qquad |c_m(x)|\le B_m(x),
\]

with nonnegative \(B_m\) and a uniformly absolutely convergent series and derivative. Define \(a_1=1-V/\Lambda\),

\[
A_0=K(|r|/\Lambda),\quad A_1=K(a_1h),\quad A_m=K(B_mh^m/\Lambda).
\]

The envelope condition \(A_0+A_1+\sum_{m\ge2}A_m\le h\) makes the event probabilities valid and maps the centered fixed-point map into the tube. It is equivalently

\[
R_\Lambda\left[|r|+(\Lambda-V)h+\sum_{m\ge2}B_mh^m\right]\le h.
\]

A sufficient certificate is \((-L+V)h\ge |r|+\sum B_mh^m\), with the operator-domain and positivity assumptions stated in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

The mean number of children is described by

\[
(Mf)(x)=h(x)^{-1}K\left[a_1hf+\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{mB_mh^m}{\Lambda}f\right](x).
\]

If a bounded positive \(w\), with \(w_{\min}>0\), satisfies \(Mw\le\alpha w\) for \(\alpha<1\), then \(\rho(M)\le\alpha\) in the associated weighted sup norm. For a root at \(x\),

\[
\mathbb E_xN_n=M^n\mathbf1(x),\qquad
\mathbb E_xN_{\rm total}\le\frac{w(x)}{w_{\min}(1-\alpha)}.
\]

Finite expected total progeny proves almost-sure termination. The source, unary, and \(m\)-child event probabilities at type \(x\) are respectively \(A_0(x)/h(x)\), \(A_1(x)/h(x)\), and \(A_m(x)/h(x)\); the leftover probability returns zero. Each event's endpoint law is \(K(x,dy)g_j(y)/A_j(x)\), with \(g_0=|r|/\Lambda\), \(g_1=a_1h\), and \(g_m=B_mh^m/\Lambda\). The normalized returns are the source sign, a one-child copy, or \(-\operatorname{sgn}(c_m)(|c_m|/B_m)\) times the product of independent child returns. Thus \(|S_x|\le h(x)\), and first-event conditioning recovers the fixed point. The weighted contraction proves uniqueness in the tube. The complete proof is in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

## Exact separation case

The executable certificate checks the following two-state continuous-time Markov chain exactly over rational numbers:

\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}-3&3\\3&-3\end{pmatrix},\quad \Lambda=2,\quad
K=2(2I-L)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}5/8&3/8\\3/8&5/8\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Take \(v=0\), \(h=(2,1)\), \(r=(1,0)\), \(V=(1,2)\), and \(c_2=B_2=(1/2,0)\), so \(F_A(u)=u+u^2/2\), \(F_B(u)=2u\). The envelope totals are \((25/16,15/16)\le(2,1)\). The event probabilities (source, unary, binary, zero) are \((5/32,5/16,5/16,7/32)\) at A and \((3/16,3/8,3/8,1/16)\) at B. All nonzero tilted endpoint laws are point masses at A.

The mean-progeny matrix and positive vector are

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}15/16&0\\9/8&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
w=(1,6/5),\qquad Mw=(15/16)w.
\]

Thus \(\rho(M)=15/16\), although the ordinary sup-norm row bound is \(9/8\), and a B root has \(9/8\) expected children in its first generation. The exact expected total node counts, including the root, are 16 from A and 19 from B. The construction therefore exhibits subcritical long-run work without a pointwise subcritical offspring requirement.

For the incoming scalar certificate, \(R=1\), \(\lambda=\min V=1\), and \(P(t)=t^2/2\). Its best possible enclosure side is

\[
\max_{t\ge0}(\lambda t-P(t))=1/2<R,
\]

so no constant radius can meet the source enclosure, regardless of its work inequality. At the maximum spatial radius \(t=2\), \(\Gamma(t)=t=2\not<1\) as well. The exact fixed point is \(u_A=(-22+6\sqrt{19})/10\), \(u_B=3u_A/5\), inside the proposed tube. `verify_certificate.py` verifies the exact resolvent identity, event probabilities, envelope, positive-vector inequality, spectral radius, expected node counts, scalar-certificate failure for all radii, and the algebraic fixed-point identity. It performs no random simulation.

## N798/N804 transfer audit

The N804 retained-vector procedure suggests a legal way to *certify a new vector*: propose a positive vector for this sampler's own \(M\), then verify all rows exactly and compute \(\alpha=\max_i(Mw)_i/w_i\). Its stored vector and coercivity scalar belong to a different weighted kinetic operator and cannot be reused here. A retained-only inequality on a projection is insufficient without a bound on every omitted type.

N798's conditional source/form theorem and residual Gram identity concern a positive reversible weighted form and fully represented source/load columns. They do not supply this sampler's spatial tube \(h\), offspring operator \(M\), event masses, or exact tilted endpoint laws. An N798-style complete residual could be a candidate route to verifying a future representation of \(M\), but that bridge would need a new theorem that covers all omitted types and the nonlinear branch terms. No N798 or N804 theorem is composed into the present result.

This follows the repository's composition protocol: shared proof vocabulary or a retained vector is only a candidate connection until source and destination objects, domains, norms, precision, and costs match. The capital dossiers remain `source_derived_unreviewed`; their exact algebra and internal replays do not establish external review or physical capability.

## Prior art and conceptual scope

Positive supersolutions for branching-functional moments already occur in Agarwal and Claisse (2020, Proposition 3.4). They explicitly discuss nonconstant supersolutions as a case-by-case alternative to constant ones. Huang and Privault (2026, arXiv:2502.17853v2, Sections 6–7) analyze weighted progeny integrability using branching domination and Hamilton–Jacobi bounds. Marked branching representations and coefficient-dependent branching choices are also established (for example, Henry-Labordère et al., arXiv:1603.01727). The proposed event-first law is an exact conditional disintegration / change of measure of a marked resolvent kernel, not a new generic importance-sampling principle.

The present scoped contribution is the proof package that combines (i) tangent-resummed centered correction, (ii) a variable spatial envelope, (iii) event-first endpoint tilting that preserves a pathwise output bound, and (iv) a type-weighted mean-progeny certificate that permits some supercritical types. This is a structural extension of the incoming scalar criterion, but it is close to established supersolution and multitype branching methods. Historical novelty is **UNKNOWN**; it may be a useful specialization or recombination rather than a new principle.

## Acquisition and cost

The theorem is conditional on exact inputs and operations. Charge the predictor and its derivatives, residual and coefficient bounds, spatial envelope, a full-domain check of the envelope and vector inequalities, all event masses and normalizers, exact event-specific endpoint samplers, diffusion endpoint operations, precision/refinement, and support/complement coverage. If exact tilting uses rejection from K with envelope \(g_j\le C_j\), conditional on that event at root x, rejection costs \(C_j/A_j(x)\) expected proposals; after weighting by event probability its expected contribution is \(C_j/h(x)\) per node. Rare events can have high conditional latency, while expected cost depends on the envelope and tube scale; both tails and precision/refinement work still need measurement. The total includes one-time acquisition and verification plus per-query expected tree work; exact formulae and weighted-cost variants are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

The finite example's diffusion work is one exact two-state CTMC resolvent lookup with a rational table. It does not price a continuous Brownian endpoint or physical acquisition. Building h and w over a continuum, exact tilted sampling, and comparison with Newton/Krylov, deterministic discretization, multilevel Picard, or optimized branching are unmeasured. No efficiency advantage follows from finite expected work alone.
