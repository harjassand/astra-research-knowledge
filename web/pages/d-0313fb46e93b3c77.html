## Result

The October 5 construction has no hidden dependence on a particular rate vector in its slopes, offsets, active sets, plateau geometry, or square-root scale iteration. Rates first enter at trapping.tex:80–92 through

\[
h^H\sum_e k_e<\min_e k_e.
\]

For a fixed finite weakly reversible graph, replacing this by

\[
|R|K h^H<\kappa
\]

makes one nonempty compact convex absorbing set in each positive stoichiometric class work for every measurable rate schedule \(k_e(t)\in[\kappa,K]\). Absorption time can be bounded independently of the schedule for each fixed initial point. The proof extends to finite real embedded complexes provided the kinetic exponent of each reaction is its source complex.

There is also a stronger transfer: the same affine construction works for all endotactic networks, using minimum essential sources instead of weak-reversibility cut paths. The adjusted domination condition must include reaction-vector sizes. See the complete projected-flux proof below.

The precise feedback formulation is **every absolutely continuous solution of the admissible rate differential inclusion**. Arbitrary discontinuous feedback does not imply ordinary-solution existence or uniqueness; an exact reversible one-species counterexample is given below. Exogenous measurable bounded rates do give unique Carathéodory solutions.

## Dependency and quantifier audit

1. Define \(D=\{e_i\}\cup\{z-y:y,z\in C,z\ne y\}\) and
   \[
   E(r)=\min\bigl(\{1/2\}\cup\{|r\cdot v|/(2\|v\|_1):v\in D,r\cdot v\ne0\}\bigr).
   \]
   Both depend only on the embedded complexes (trapping.tex:20–39). All pairs of complexes, including sources in different linkage classes, are included. Using only reaction vectors here would be insufficient for the subsequent source comparison.
2. The full affine lemma supplies finite slopes \(r_\lambda\) and offsets \(b_\lambda(h)\), independent of rates and the class. Their activity approximation holds uniformly in \(p\in[-1,1]^d\) for sufficiently small \(h\) (affine.tex:288–308).
3. Finite slopes give
   \[
   H=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{|r_\lambda\cdot v|/2:r_\lambda\cdot v\ne0\}\bigr)>0.
   \]
   This is also independent of rates and the class (trapping.tex:55–77). For an active slope, any nonzero \(r\cdot v\) has the same sign as \(p\cdot v\), with \(|p\cdot v|\ge H\).
4. Choose class representative \(c\), then \(h_P\) below the activity threshold, such that \(\|\log c/\log h\|_\infty<H\) and \(|R|K h^H<\kappa\) for all \(0<h\le h_P\). This depends only on \(C,R,c,\kappa,K\), never on the rate schedule or the initial point.
5. Set \(M_h=F_h(c)\), \(K_h=\{F_h=M_h\}\). Every label active at \(c\) has zero slope. Hence a constant label attains the global maximum \(M_h\). The plateau is an intersection of finitely many closed halfspaces, contains \(c\), and lies in \(\operatorname{int}[\sqrt h,1/\sqrt h]^d\). No rates enter this argument (trapping.tex:96–137).
6. On \(P\setminus K_h\), no active slope lies in \(S^\perp\), since an \(S^\perp\) label has value at least \(M_h\) everywhere on \(c+S\) (trapping.tex:164–171). This is the load-bearing classwise strictness argument.
7. The square-root iteration uses only \(K_h\subset\operatorname{int}B_{\sqrt h}\), invariant sets, and finite entry. It assumes no nesting of plateaux and no autonomous dynamics (permanence.tex, complete proof).

## Complete affine-lemma audit

I attempted to break each of the nonstandard compactness steps. The following checks resolved the potential issues.

- Fixed-minimum construction: for a type \(k\) active label, comparison with \((t\mathbf1,0)\) forces each raised-coordinate limiting exponent to be at least \(\beta_k\). The lower-dimensional induction applies because its hypothesis explicitly allows approximating sequences outside the exponent cube. Boundary limits \(p_i=\beta_k\) or \(b\) therefore cause no missing case.
- The competitor of type \(k+1\) has offset of order \(-h^{\beta_{k+1}}\). Raised coordinates of the current label have limits at least \(\beta_k>\beta_{k+1}\), and an additional coordinate assumed above \(\beta_{k+1}\) supplies strict exponent separation. Thus the competitor wins. Only strict limiting exponent gaps are used; no unsupported inference from \(p_i^\nu\to p_i\) at equal exponents occurs.
- For type \(m-1\), the sole unraised coordinate must have limiting exponent exactly \(t\), because the minimum is \(t\) and all raised coordinates exceed \(t\). This closes the top induction type.
- Limit activity sets \(A_\lambda\) are closed by choosing one actual active point from each realizing sequence with \(h_n<1/n\) and distance less than \(1/n\). Continuity of the offsets in \(h\) is unnecessary.
- The bad-limit set uses \(E(r_\lambda)\) at a fixed slope, never \(E(p)\) at a varying point. It is compact even if \(E\) is discontinuous. Its image under \(\mu(p)=\min p_i\) has positive distance from \(t\), giving the neighborhood of minimum exponents.
- After pruning contained open intervals, both left and right endpoints are strictly ordered in the order of their centers. Consecutive intervals must overlap, since a gap would lie inside \([a,b]\) and be uncovered. Consequently the indicated cut interval is nonempty.
- The common shifts \(-\sum_{\ell<j}h^{q_\ell}\) preserve activity within a local family. Comparison with a neighboring constant-slope label excludes limiting minima above \(q_j\) or below \(q_{j-1}\). At an equality cut, the point remains strictly inside the original open interval, so the local guarantee applies.
- Uniform activity follows from finite labels plus compact \([-1,1]^d\). A violating sequence has one fixed label on a subsequence; its fixed tolerance passes to the limit.

There is no rate parameter anywhere in this induction. No mathematical gap was found. No Lean build was run, and the release's [149 Lean-scope documentation](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/149.md) must not be treated as formal certification of the October 5 classwise iteration.

## Uniform weak-reversibility flux bound

Let \(N=|R|\), \(M=\max_{y\in C}\|y\|_1\), and choose \(h\) so \(a_h:=\kappa-NK h^H>0\). For an active slope \(r\), order complex levels \(r\cdot y\). At every cut crossed by a downward edge, weak reversibility supplies an upward edge \(w\to w'\). Every downward source \(u\) is above every chosen upward source \(w\), hence

\[
x^u\le h^H x^w,\qquad
Q_j\ge(\kappa-NK h^H)x^w\ge a_h h^M.
\]

If no downward edge exists but the cut is crossed upward, \(Q_j\ge\kappa h^M\ge a_hh^M\). Distinct levels have gap at least \(2H\). Therefore, for each active \(r\notin S^\perp\),

\[
r\cdot f_k(x)\ge\delta_h:=2H(\kappa-NK h^H)h^M>0
\quad\text{for every }k\in[\kappa,K]^R.
\]

This also gives the source's compactness argument a direct schedule-uniform replacement. For real complexes with negative entries, \(x^w\ge h^{\|w\|_1}\ge h^M\) still holds on \(B_h\).

## Measurable controls, existence, finite absorption

For exogenous measurable bounded rates, \(f(t,x)\) is measurable in \(t\), continuous and uniformly locally Lipschitz in \(x\) on the positive orthant. Standard integral-equation local existence/uniqueness applies. A finite affine minimum is globally Lipschitz in \(x\), so \(F_h(x(t))\) is absolutely continuous on every finite interval of an AC trajectory. At almost every time where both \(x\) and the composition are differentiable,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}F_h(x(t))
=\min_{\lambda\in A_h(x(t))}r_\lambda\cdot\dot x(t).
\]

To justify this, discard inactive labels using their positive gap at the fixed time and expand the remaining finite set using differentiability of \(x\). Taking the right difference quotient gives the minimum. Integration gives monotonicity and the \(\delta_h\) lower bound outside the plateau. The source's pointwise right-derivative argument is unnecessary for measurable controls.

Starting in \(K_h\), a first exit from \(B_h\) is impossible: monotonicity plus \(F_h\le M_h\) keeps the trajectory in \(K_h\), which misses \(\partial B_h\). Its derivative is bounded there, giving a limit at any finite terminal time and local continuation. Thus the trajectory is global and positive.

For an initial point \(x^0\in P\), choose a small \(h_0\le h_P\) with \(\|\log x^0/\log h_0\|_\infty<H\). The same zero-slope argument puts it in \(K_{h_0}\). Iterate \(h_{j+1}=\min(\sqrt{h_j},h_P)\). There are

\[
J=\max\{0,\lceil\log_2(|\log h_0|/|\log h_P|)\rceil\}
\]

steps until \(h_P\). Define the finite excursion budget \(G_h=M_h-\min_{x\in B_h}F_h(x)\). At each step, entry occurs within \(G_{h_j}/\delta_{h_j}\). Hence one schedule-independent bound is

\[
T(x^0)\le\sum_{j=1}^{J}\frac{G_{h_j}}{\delta_{h_j}}.
\]

No useful size estimate for this bound or for the number of affine labels follows from the release.

For arbitrary feedback, state the differential inclusion

\[
\dot x\in\left\{\sum_e k_e x^{y_e}\nu_e:k\in[\kappa,K]^R\right\}.
\]

The value is a nonempty compact convex set and varies locally Lipschitzly with \(x\). It has local AC solutions; the plateau argument gives global continuation and absorption for every solution. Every feedback trajectory that actually satisfies the controlled ODE is covered. This does not assert ordinary-solution existence for arbitrary discontinuous feedback laws.

## Stronger all-endotactic deduction

The exact primary definitions are Gopalkrishnan–Miller–Shiu, [Definition 3.9 and Definition 3.14](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5303v2#S3.SS2). Local downloaded text: `work/agents/reaction_control_prior/sources/gms2013.txt`, essential-support definitions at 1455–1565 and endotactic definition at 1732–1805.

For a direction \(w\), essential reactions have \(w\cdot\nu_e\ne0\). The support is the set of maximal \(w\)-level sources among essential reactions. Endotacticity requires each essential reaction out of that support to have \(w\cdot\nu_e<0\).

Fix an active slope \(r\notin S^\perp\). Its essential set is nonempty. Apply endotacticity at \(w=-r\). Choose any source \(y_*\) minimizing \(r\cdot y\) among \(r\)-essential reactions, and an essential edge \(e_*\) out of it. Then

\[
r\cdot\nu_{e_*}>0,
\]

and **every** negative-projection edge \(e\) has \(r\cdot y_e>r\cdot y_*\). Equal-source-level negative edges are prohibited by the exact definition. Thus \(x^{y_e}\le h^H x^{y_*}\) for every negative edge. Unessential reactions at still-lower source levels contribute zero and cause no problem.

Put \(L=\sum_e\|\nu_e\|_1\). Since slopes are in \([-1,1]^d\), negative projection sizes satisfy \(|r\cdot\nu_e|\le\|\nu_e\|_1\). The selected positive projection is at least \(2H\), because \(\nu_{e_*}\in D\). Discard all other positive flux terms to obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
r\cdot f_k(x)
&\ge\kappa x^{y_*}(r\cdot\nu_{e_*})
 -K\sum_{r\cdot\nu_e<0}x^{y_e}|r\cdot\nu_e|\\
&\ge x^{y_*}(2H\kappa-KLh^H).
\end{aligned}
\]

Choose \(h_P\) with \(KLh^H\le H\kappa\) at all smaller scales. This yields

\[
r\cdot f_k(x)\ge H\kappa h^M>0.
\]

All the geometric, classwise, measurable-chain-rule, global-existence, and scale-iteration arguments above now apply without weak reversibility. If all reactions are self-loops, \(S=\{0\}\), every class is a singleton and permanence is immediate. Thus the proof candidate is a robust classwise permanence theorem for all finite endotactic source-matched power-law networks, including unbounded classes.

The adversarial-rate counterexample search is exact at this interface. For each fixed \((x,r)\), \(r\cdot f_k(x)\) is linear in the rate vector. Its minimum over the admissible cube is attained by setting \(k_e=\kappa\) when \(r\cdot\nu_e>0\), \(k_e=K\) when \(r\cdot\nu_e<0\), and arbitrary admissible values for zero projections. The displayed estimate lower-bounds precisely this worst corner. At a tie between active labels, the same estimate holds separately for every label, hence also for their minimum derivative. No numerical rate sampling is needed to exclude this attempted pointwise attack.

This is stronger than the release's stated theorem. The derivation is short once the arbitrary-tolerance affine lemma is available; the genuinely load-bearing supplied mechanism is still that lemma. The older primary literature proves the stronger robustness for **strongly** endotactic networks, so it must not be presented as a new feature in that subclass. General all-endotactic correctness/priority needs independent review.

## Decisive counterexamples and unsuccessful attacks

1. **Arbitrary feedback existence is false even for a reversible graph.** For \(0\rightleftarrows X\), set \((k_+,k_-)=(2,1)\) when \(x\le1\), and \((1,2)\) when \(x>1\). Then \(x'=2-x\ge1\) below/at 1 and \(x'=1-2x<-1\) above 1. No AC solution starts at 1. Any connected positive excursion above 1 has a strictly negative derivative and cannot leave 1 upward; any excursion below 1 has positive derivative and cannot leave 1 downward. Thus a solution would have to stay at 1, contradicting its specified derivative there. All feedback rates lie in \([1,2]\). The unrestricted rate differential inclusion itself does admit the stationary solution at 1.
2. **A lower rate bound cannot simply be omitted.** The same reversible graph with \(k_+(t)=e^{-t}\), \(k_-(t)=1\) has \(x(t)=e^{-t}(x^0+t)\to0\).
3. **An upper rate bound cannot simply be omitted.** Set \(k_+(t)=e^t\), \(k_-(t)=1\). Then \(x(t)=\tfrac12e^t+(x^0-\tfrac12)e^{-t}\to\infty\).
4. **Independent kinetic orders invalidate the transfer.** On the weakly reversible graph \(0\rightleftarrows X\), use forward monomial \(x^2\) and reverse monomial \(x\). Unit rates yield \(x'=x^2-x\), which blows up in finite time for \(x^0>1\). Matching exponent vectors to sources is essential.
5. **One compact set across all stoichiometric classes is impossible.** \(A\rightleftarrows B\) conserves \(a+b\); varying that total defeats every fixed compact concentration set. Class dependence is indispensable.
6. **Minimal invariant sets need not have finite-entry absorption.** For \(0\rightleftarrows X\) with rates in \([1,2]\), the equilibrium interval \([1/2,2]\) is invariant for every admissible rate vector. Constant rates \((1,2)\) from \(x^0<1/2\) approach its endpoint and never enter. Strict facet margins, as supplied by the affine construction, matter.
7. I tried to find an affine failure at equal exponent limits, at cut equalities, near discontinuities of \(E\), from nonnested plateaux, and from active \(S^\perp\) labels just outside the plateau. The source's explicit strengthened sequential hypothesis, finite fixed slopes, overlapping intervals, box rather than plateau iteration, and class representative rule resolve those attacks.

## Time-change extension

Absolute compact rate bounds can be weakened to \(\kappa a(t)\le k_e(t)\le Ka(t)\), with \(a\ge0\) locally integrable and \(\int_0^\infty a(t)dt=\infty\). The same geometry works; progress is \(F_h'\ge\delta_h a(t)\) outside the plateau. Each scale needs finite cumulative reaction time, so the finite scale iteration still finishes at a finite physical time. This allows rates tending to zero together, such as \(a(t)=1/(1+t)\), or unbounded but locally integrable common activity. If cumulative activity is finite, absorption generally fails. This is a time-change corollary, not a separate new mechanism.

## Prior art, acquisition, and limits

Relevant primary comparisons:

- Craciun's [toric differential inclusions paper](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~craciun/PAPERS_NEW/1_SIAG_Feb_2018_Revision_3.pdf), Section 2.2, states extended persistence/permanence for variable-rate weakly reversible embedded systems as conjectures.
- Gopalkrishnan–Miller–Shiu [2013/2014 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5303v2) proves permanence for strongly endotactic networks, including a differential-inclusion formulation.
- Boros–Hofbauer [2019/2020 single-linkage-class paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03071v2) permits bounded variable rates in its established subclass.
- Craciun [September 23, 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.02860v3) is identified by the exact October 5 source as proving bounded-trajectory persistence for general weakly reversible networks; its scope should be read separately rather than conflated with a common absorbing-set theorem.

Supplied inputs: exact finite embedded complexes and reactions; proof of the affine lemma; a class representative; compact rate bounds. Acquired in this run: exact-source reconstruction, dependency audit, schedule-uniform inward constant, AC-chain-rule transfer, exact counterexamples, and independent verification of the proposed endotactic flux argument.

No source-level quantitative bound on label count, bit heights, activity threshold, or proof-compilation runtime was acquired. The affine construction uses compactness and finite-subcover choices. Consequently this does not establish an efficient certificate acquisition algorithm, usable numerical concentration bounds for arbitrary networks, stochastic-noise robustness, uniqueness under arbitrary feedback, or independently mismatched kinetics.

The rational-certificate compiler proposed by unexplored_interfaces is plausible under rational inputs: choosing a sufficiently small scale uniformly over a compact initial polytope puts it strictly inside the plateau; proper relative-class facets have a uniform strict inward margin. Small rational perturbations of finite facet inequalities preserve that margin and positive compactness. Exhaustive enumeration plus exact real quantifier elimination can therefore search for a strict rational certificate. This is computability rather than an efficiency result and was not implemented here.
