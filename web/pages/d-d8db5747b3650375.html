## Universal obstruction, independently reconstructed

Assume finite `N_s`, finite real coefficients, and nonzero finite widths. Define `C=sum_l |theta_l|`. Since `0<s_l(u)<1`,

\[
|V(u)|\le C,\qquad e^{-C}\le e^{-V(u)}\le e^C.
\]

Hence for every `R>0`,

\[
Z_R=\int_{-R}^{R}e^{-V(u)}du\ge 2R e^{-C},
\qquad Z=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-V(u)}du=\infty.
\]

This holds for **all** finite fitted coefficient vectors, even with arbitrarily many but finitely many sigmoids, either sigmoid orientation, constants, and ridge regularization. With the paper's positive widths, additionally `V(-infinity)=0` and `V(+infinity)=sum theta_l`; both tails approach a nonzero density plateau. No parameter sign restriction within this finite family can fix the problem.

If all learned conditional kernels were proper, Tonelli's theorem and successive integration of the hierarchical product would give

\[
\int e^{-V(u)}\prod_j q_j(w_j\mid u_j)\,du\,dw_J\cdots dw_1
=\int e^{-V(u)}du=\infty.
\]

Thus conditional normalization and orthogonal reconstruction cannot rescue the coarsest factor. The exact factorization of the original proper Gibbs law remains valid; this defect enters with the finite learned ansatz.

## Minimal repairs, with training consequences

**One fixed confining baseline.** Replace `V_theta` by `B+V_theta`. For any bounded `V_theta`,

\[
e^{-C}\int e^{-B}du\le\int e^{-B-V_\theta}du\le e^C\int e^{-B}du.
\]

Therefore `exp(-B-V_theta)` is integrable iff `exp(-B)` is integrable. Choosing `B(u)=beta*u^2`, fixed `beta>0`, suffices for every finite coefficient vector and preserves linear fitting in theta. Its partition function obeys bounds `e^{±C} sqrt(pi/beta)`. Equivalently, declare a finite reference measure `mu(du)=e^{-B(u)}du` and explicitly include its score when fitting a Lebesgue density.

For scalar feature vector `Xi`, the empirical loss is

\[
\widehat L(\theta)=\tfrac12\theta^TM\theta+\theta^T c-\theta^T b+\text{constant},
\quad M=\langle\Xi'\Xi'^T\rangle,
\quad c=\langle B'\Xi'\rangle,
\quad b=\langle\Xi''\rangle.
\]

The ridge equation becomes `(M+lambda*I) theta=b-c`. Simply adding a Gaussian after training without acknowledging this term changes the score model.

**Finite support.** Define the density with indicator `1_[a,b]`. It is proper, but it models a truncated law. Sampling must explicitly enforce the support. The unweighted score objective in Eq. (87) has a boundary term on a finite interval:

\[
\tfrac12\int_a^b p\,(-V'-\partial_u\log p)^2du
=\int_a^b p\big(\tfrac12(V')^2-V''\big)du
+[pV']_a^b+\text{constant}.
\]

Dropping that boundary contribution needs justification. A smooth positive weight vanishing at endpoints permits generalized score matching with loss `E_p[.5*w*(V')^2-w'*V'-w*V'']`; this is a derived repair, not the paper's stated objective.

**Conditional tails.** For fixed coarse input the conditional Eq. (84) is a finite-dimensional quadratic form plus bounded sigmoid residual:

`E(w;u)=.5*w^T A w + b(u)^T w + c(u) + r(w;u)`, `|r|<=C(u)`.

Its real-space partition function is finite **iff A is positive definite on the wavelet subspace**. Sufficiency follows by completing the square. Necessity follows by comparing the integral to the quadratic exponential: a negative eigenvalue causes growth, while a zero eigenvalue gives either an exponential linear tail or a constant tail along its eigen-coordinate. Both diverge. The bounded residual cannot alter this conclusion. The ridge linear fit is not a positive-definiteness constraint. No trained matrices were available, so this is an audit requirement, not a claim that their fitted conditionals fail it. A fixed positive local quartic in reconstructed fields can instead dominate every finite quadratic and make conditional tails proper without a positivity assumption.

## What empirical score matching does and does not identify

Equation (87) and the same stated coarsest strategy use `mean[.5*(V')^2-V'']`, not Z. Logistic derivatives and second derivatives are bounded for nonzero widths, so this loss is finite for an everywhere-improper sigmoid-only model. Its finite optimum or ridge stability cannot certify normalization. This does not mean integration by parts is automatically invalid: under a rapidly decaying true law the formal derivative loss can still be defined. Its interpretation as the score of a learned probability law fails because that law does not exist.

A decisive finite-data example also survives after integrability is repaired. Put every observed datum inside `(-R,R)` and let

\[
B_\alpha(u)=\alpha(|u|-R)_+^4,\qquad\alpha>0.
\]

This is C3, integrable after exponentiation, and has first and second derivatives identically zero on the observed interval. For **any** bounded sigmoid potential V, every model `B_alpha+V` has exactly the same empirical score loss, while its probability mass in the observed interval tends to zero as alpha tends to zero. Proof: numerator is at most `2R e^C`, whereas denominator is at least

\[
e^{-C}\left[2R+\frac{\Gamma(1/4)}{2\alpha^{1/4}}\right]\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

For `V=0` this formula for Z is exact and the interval probability is `2R/Z`. This example is a **class of possible tail completions**, not a null direction inside the fixed finite logistic coefficient class. In the literal coefficient class, no normalizable model exists at all.

Do not overstate this as population nonidentifiability of all score learning. Two positive differentiable proper densities on the connected real line with equal scores everywhere are proportional, and normalization makes them equal. Hyvärinen's positive, correctly specified consistency theorem explicitly captures that distinction. The finite loss does not establish tails; tail assumptions, support design, a correct model class, or stronger sampling-based checks are still needed.

## Actual predecessor implementation: an important qualification

[Brossollet et al. (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02428-z), published 21 November 2025, is the paper's stated sigmoid source. Its Methods use a bounded scalar sigmoid residual **with quadratic interactions**. Its AMB+ dynamics conserves the spatial mean, selected as 0.6. A fixed-mean system is not evidence that a fluctuating full-support coarsest scalar can use only sigmoids.

That predecessor links [Elempereur/WCRG](https://github.com/Elempereur/WCRG), which identifies itself as code for the 2023 conditionally log-concave model paper. I retrieved a read-only snapshot at tree SHA `d81bac563fb3825472e807bff9ae4ded81585f4a`:

- `WCRG/Models/models_to_use.py:13–43`, `ANSATZ_NoCondi`, combines `SigmoidWindows` with `GaussianNonCondi`. The coarsest example `Learning_Models` notebook uses this ansatz at L=1.
- `Synthetizing` notebook's L=1 block uses first and last sigmoid centers as `window_min` and `window_max`.
- `WCRG/langevin.py`, `Mala`, lines 114–128, rejects reconstructed proposals outside these limits. These are state cutoffs, unlike bounding a proposal increment.

The upstream code supplies plausible repairs that the 2026 equations omit. It is **not** verified code for the 2026 results, and its fitted quadratic sign was not audited. No code was executed or trained, and no authors were contacted. The documented-density defect remains; the source-to-simulation discrepancy is unresolved.

## Additional normalization mismatch in free-energy reconstruction

Appendix D Eq. (96), TeX 2195–2211, correctly states

\[
\partial_u F_q(u)=E_{q(w\mid u)}[\partial_u E(w;u)].
\]

Then lines 2215–2218 explicitly use existing original Monte Carlo pairs without learned conditional samples, and Eq. (97) replaces that conditional expectation with original-data regression. In the population limit the fitted target is `E_{p(w|u)}[partial_u E]`. The difference is the expectation error `(E_p-E_q)[partial_u E]`; it is not generally removed by more original training pairs.

An exact smooth Gaussian counterexample: choose `E(w;u)=(w-a*u)^2/(2*sigma^2)`, so the learned conditional is `q=N(a*u,sigma^2)` and `F_q=-log sqrt(2*pi*sigma^2)` is constant. If original data have `p(w|u)=N(b*u,tau^2)`, then

\[
E_p[\partial_u E]=-a(b-a)u/\sigma^2,
\qquad F_{\rm fit}(u)=-a(b-a)u^2/(2\sigma^2)+\text{constant}.
\]

For `a=1,b=2,sigma^2=1`, the coarse sampler `p_J=N(0,1)` and learned q generate a joint law with coarse variance 1. The normalized energy constructed using fitted F instead has coarse variance 1/2. The KL of these coarse marginals is `(1-log 2)/2 = 0.1534264`. Therefore Eq. (105)'s product equality does not follow with fitted F substituted for induced F. This is a general counterexample to that substitution, not a reproduction of their fitted numbers. It disappears if p=q, if the relevant expectation errors vanish, or if F is learned under q. This issue affects reconstructed energy claims; sequential sampling does not use fitted F.
