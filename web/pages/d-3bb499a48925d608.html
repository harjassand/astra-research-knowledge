C03_L05 REVISION — LOW-RANK UPDATE OVER A BOUNDED-TREEWIDTH BASE
2026-10-07. This revision follows the immutable independent INITIAL and
root-authorized cross-phase exposure. INITIAL.txt is unchanged.

Refined claim
-------------

Let F in Q(i)^(n x n) be supplied together with an exact decomposition

  F = S + U V^T,     U,V in Q(i)^(n x r).

Let G_S be the undirected site-support graph of S: its vertices are [n] and
{i,j}, i!=j, is an edge if S_ij or S_ji is nonzero. A width-t tree-decomposition
of G_S is supplied. Then all

  c_k(F) = sum_{I,J subseteq[n], |I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty}
               |det F[I,J]|^2

are computable exactly in 2^{O(t+r)} poly(n,r,L,L_T) bit operations, with
polynomial-bit intermediate rationals. Here L includes the encodings of
F,S,U,V, and L_T is the decomposition size. The bound uses a four-generator
Grassmann elimination rather than the partition-state loop DP retained in
INITIAL.txt, improving the latter's Bell(t+r) state bound to one exponential.

The decomposition of G_S is part of the verifiable input certificate. The
contraction uses the elimination order obtained from this decomposition; the
order can itself be supplied as a certificate and its induced width checked
by exact fill simulation. Exact recognition checks F=S+UV^T, extracts G_S
by rational zero tests, and checks the decomposition or order certificate.
For fixed t, treewidth can be recognized exactly by Bodlaender's
constant-k algorithm. No efficient search for an arbitrary decomposition
F=S+UV^T from F alone is claimed. The rank factor is supplied and its
arithmetic/encoding cost is included.

This can have rank(F)=Theta(n): a sparse base may have full rank, while the
residual rank r is constant or logarithmic. It is distinct from a low-rank
matrix algorithm and from an algorithm whose width parameter is the support
of the full, typically dense F.

Proof, with the auxiliary local condition made explicit
-------------------------------------------------------

1. Add r auxiliary sites and form the (n+r)-square matrix

       M = [ S   U ]
           [-V^T I_r].

Let A denote the r auxiliary indices. For physical I,J with |I|=|J|=k,
Schur complementation through I_r gives

  det M[I union A, J union A]
      = det(S[I,J] + U[I,:] V[J,:]^T)
      = det F[I,J].

The equality is exact even when S[I,J] is singular. The block -V^T is
essential for the plus sign in S+UV^T.

2. Introduce diagonal indeterminates X=diag(x_1,...,x_N),
Y=diag(y_1,...,y_N), N=n+r, and the chiral determinant

  D_M(x,y) = det [ X   M ]
                 [-M*  Y ],

where M* is the conjugate transpose. Expanding along the diagonal entries
gives the formal identity

  D_M(x,y) = sum_{R,C subseteq[N], |R|=|C|}
              |det M[R,C]|^2
              product_{i notin R} x_i product_{j notin C} y_j.       (1)

For fixed selected rows R and columns C, the remaining determinant is
[[0,M[R,C]],[-M*[C,R],0]]. It vanishes when |R|!=|C|; at equal size q,
exchanging the two blocks contributes (-1)^q and the q negative signs
contribute another (-1)^q, leaving |det M[R,C]|^2. This proves (1), including
singular minors and complex entries.

3. Apply a tensor product of exact local linear functionals to (1). At every
physical site i<=n use

  T_i(1)=0,   T_i(x_i)=1,   T_i(y_i)=z,   T_i(x_i y_i)=1.

The constant monomial means both row and column selected and is rejected;
x_i means only the column is selected; y_i means only the row is selected
and contributes z; x_i y_i means neither is selected. Thus row/column
indices are disjoint at physical sites and z counts the number of selected
physical rows. At each auxiliary site a use

  T_a(1)=1,  T_a(x_a)=T_a(y_a)=T_a(x_a y_a)=0.

This forces both auxiliary row and column to be selected. Since (1) already
forces |R|=|C| and the same r auxiliary indices occur on both sides,
physical row and column counts coincide. Applying all T's to (1), then using
the Schur identity, yields exactly

  (product_i T_i)(D_M) = sum_{k=0}^n c_k(F) z^k.                    (2)

4. Equation (2) has a sparse tensor/Grassmann contraction on the site-support
graph of M. Write the 2N-by-2N determinant as the exterior coefficient of

  exp( bar-u X u + bar-u M d - bar-d M* u + bar-d Y d )

with the standard row-major four-generator order per site and full-volume
coefficient extraction. Every bilinear is even, so the exponential factors
over directed matrix entries. For i!=j the factors supported on sites i,j
are

  (1 + M_ij bar-u_i d_j)(1 - conjugate(M_ij) bar-d_j u_i).

For i=j, both terms are local. The diagonal selector factors are
  (1+x_i bar-u_i u_i)(1+y_i bar-d_i d_i).
Applying T_i replaces this diagonal selector by the local factor

  P_i = bar-u_i u_i + z bar-d_i d_i
                         + (bar-u_i u_i)(bar-d_i d_i)

at a physical site, and by P_a=1 at an auxiliary site. The entry M_aa=1
still contributes its local directed-entry factors; it must not be dropped.
Physical diagonal entries are also harmlessly kept as local factors. This
is the chiral convention used by the second implementation in
augmented_grassmann_dp.py. It avoids treating an auxiliary diagonal identity
as an off-diagonal graph edge.

5. For an elimination bag of w+1 sites, retain a table over subsets of the
4(w+1) Grassmann generators and z-degrees 0..n. Multiplying two tables uses
the exterior rule: overlapping generator subsets annihilate, disjoint
subsets combine with the sign needed to sort their union. Eliminating a site
keeps only monomials containing its full four-generator block and then
removes that block. Its block has even degree, so this extraction adds no
extra sign. All other factors are outside the bucket and cannot complete an
incomplete local block. This proves elimination correctness inductively.

The site-support graph of M is a subgraph of G_S plus the r auxiliary sites,
each adjacent to physical i whenever U_i,l or V_i,l is nonzero. Add all r
auxiliary sites to every bag of the supplied G_S decomposition; every new
edge is covered and width increases by at most r, so w<=t+r. The identity
block entries M_aa are local. A width-w tree-decomposition yields an
elimination order with at most w later neighbors per vertex (for example,
eliminate vertices exclusive to leaf bags and proceed inward). Each bag
table has at most 2^{4(w+1)} monomials; even a naive pairwise product is
2^{O(w)}. At most polynomially many edge and site factors occur, so the
arithmetic operation count is 2^{O(t+r)} poly(n,r). All c_k are extracted
at once.

6. For bit complexity, clear denominators of all rational-complex entries in
S,U,V by a common integer D with log D<=L. Every nonzero exterior monomial
selects a partial matching between the 2N row and 2N column generators. The
number of such terms is at most
sum_q binom(2N,q)^2 q! = 2^{O(N log N)}; the at-most 4^n local selector
choices add only O(N) to its logarithm. Each term has at most 2N matrix
entries, so denominator D^{2N} suffices for the full polynomial and every
partial contraction. Thus exact operand bit lengths are O(N(L+log N)),
apart from polynomial-size input verification. Checking F=S+UV^T uses
O(n^2r) rational operations. There is no small-minor division, approximate
rank threshold, or normalization oracle.

Planar result and Pfaffian limit
--------------------------------

If G_S is planar and r is fixed, planar separators give a tree-decomposition
of width O(sqrt(n)); a planar embedding or the recursively constructed
decomposition can be supplied/verified in polynomial time. Adding the r
auxiliary sites changes width by O(r). The exact contraction therefore costs

  2^{O(sqrt(n))} poly(n,L)

using the four-generator DP (with a provided planar embedding or a verified
planar decomposition construction). This is subexponential and does not
prove a polynomial-time planar Pfaffian algorithm.

The double-matching expansion has a precise obstruction to the direct local
Pfaffian route. Its four-port site constraint, on (red-row, blue-row,
red-column, blue-column), has signature

  g_0000=g_1100=g_0011=1, all other entries 0.

The nonzero patterns mean: neither role active; row role active in both
copies; column role active in both copies. A planar matchgate signature must
satisfy the four-port Grassmann-Plucker/matchgate identity. In one standard
ordering,

  g_0000 g_1111 = g_1100 g_0011 - g_1010 g_0101 + g_1001 g_0110.

Here the left side is zero and exactly one product on the right is nonzero,
so the identity fails (a port permutation only changes its sign). Thus no
planar perfect-matching gadget can realize this exact site-local constraint.
This rejects the direct site-local FKT reduction. It does not exclude a
nonlocal Pfaffian transform, a different planar algorithm, or approximation.

  sparse treewidth-t base admits path decompositions of width O(t log n),
