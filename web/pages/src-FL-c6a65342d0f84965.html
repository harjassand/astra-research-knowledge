# FL global modulation: primary-source prior-art and mechanism report

**Research date:** 14 October 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)  
**Scope:** global Jensen theorem for `phi(x)=r(x)V(k·x)` in `work/FL-sources/global/GLOBAL_COMPATIBILITY.md`; prior mechanisms, input classes, and proof-status comparison.  
**Status:** scoped mathematical comparison, not a priority opinion or independent proof certification.

## Finding

Astala–Iwaniec–Prause–Saksman (AIPS), Proposition 5.1, is a close and substantive antecedent: it proves a Jensen-type inequality for gradients of radial maps against every rank-one-concave integrand. Its actual input is `f(x)=Lambda(|x|)x` on a ball and its affine boundary datum is `Lambda(R) I`. The proof is a shell-by-shell monotonicity argument: a rank-one supporting inequality is integrated over each sphere, and the isotropic spherical second moment cancels the derivative of the affine comparison energy.

The FL theorem has a different structured family and a different identity. It permits an arbitrary real amplitude depending on every spatial coordinate, multiplying a prescribed periodic target loop with affine phase; its affine comparison matrix is arbitrary. The proof splits the gradient into two rank-one terms and uses `V''=-aV`, `a>=0`, to construct an exact divergence primitive. The resulting gap is the sum of a rank-one Bregman remainder and a nonnegative acoustic-curvature integral. The AIPS proof does not supply this identity by a direct specialization, while the FL proof does not reproduce AIPS's radial shell monotonicity. Both are positive Jensen constructions; the shared tangent inequality alone is not a novelty distinction.

The natural families are not equivalent by the obvious coordinate identifications. For a nonzero radial AIPS map, the target line is spanned by `x`; for a circle-loop FL map it is spanned by `V(k·x)`, fixed along affine-phase hyperplanes wherever the amplitude is nonzero. The FL example

```text
phi(x,y) = (1 + 0.5 cos x) (cos y, sin y)
```

has nonradial amplitude and affine phase, whereas a nontrivial radial map has angular phase `arg(x)`, which is not affine. This establishes a concrete mechanism/input distinction, not a theorem that the FL family is new among all laminate or Jensen constructions. No historical-priority claim follows from this comparison or from failure to find an exact search hit.

## Exact statements and mechanisms

### AIPS Proposition 5.1

In *A hunt for sharp L^p-estimates and Rank-One Convex Variational Integrals*, arXiv:1403.1095v1, §5 first introduces the wider radial-linear notation `f(x)=Lambda(|x|)x`, allowing matrix-valued `Lambda`. Proposition 5.1 itself restricts to scalar `Lambda:[0,R]->R`, with `f:B_R->R^n`, and assumes continuous rank-one-concave `E:R^(n×n)->R`. It concludes

```text
integral_{B_R} E(Df) <= |B_R| E(Lambda(R) I).
```

Equivalently, with `F=-E` and `u=f-Lambda(R)x`, this is the Jensen inequality for `Du` at affine datum `B=Lambda(R)I`. The radial profile is Lipschitz as set out in §5.1; the theorem is not for arbitrary matrix-valued `Lambda`.

The proof mollifies `E` while preserving rank-one concavity, then for each radius `t` defines the difference between the energy of `Df` over `B_t` and that of the affine map `x -> Lambda(t)x`. Since

```text
Df(x) = Lambda(|x|) I + Lambda'(|x|) x⊗x/|x|,
```

the increment from `Lambda(|x|)I` is rank one. Rank-one concavity gives its tangent upper bound. Integrating on `|x|=t` uses

```text
integral_{|x|=t} x⊗x = (|S^{n-1}|/n) t^(n+1) I,
```

which cancels the derivative of the reference affine energy and makes the shell-energy difference monotone. This is a clean global Jensen result for radial maps and implies their gradient distributions are laminates by the usual Jensen characterization. It is not an explicit laminate splitting tree.

### FL modulation theorem

The pinned source theorem assumes `F:R^(m×n)->R` is C² rank-one convex, `V:R->R^m` is C² periodic and satisfies `V''(theta)=-a(theta)V(theta)` with `a>=0`, and `r` is an arbitrary smooth real periodic scalar function of all `n` coordinates. With `theta=k·x`, `W=V'`, and

```text
phi = r V(theta),   P = r W⊗k,   Q = V⊗grad(r),
```

both `P` and `Q` are rank one. For every constant `B`, the source proves

```text
integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
 = integral R_F(B+P,Q)
   + integral a(theta) r^2 integral_0^1 t D^2F(B+tP)[V⊗k,V⊗k] dt >= 0.
```

Here `R_F(C,Q)=F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C)[Q]`. Rank-one convexity makes the first term nonnegative; the second is nonnegative because the Hessian in a rank-one direction is nonnegative. The equality comes from differentiating the source's vector primitive and integrating its divergence over the periodic cell. The argument extends in the source to smooth zero-boundary amplitudes; the stated quartic extension to `W^(1,4)` is by strong approximation.

The key difference from AIPS is not merely replacing a radial formula by a loop symbol. In AIPS, isotropy of the sphere integrates the rank-one radial increment into a scalar reference derivative. In FL, an ODE for the target loop turns phase differentiation into a scalar nonnegative curvature weight and yields an exact gap decomposition for arbitrary `B`. AIPS states Proposition 5.1 directly for continuous rank-one-concave integrands; FL's exact identity is derived for C² integrands, while its source separately extends the bounded-gradient Jensen inequality to continuous rank-one-convex tests by convolution. FL works for arbitrary `m,n`, arbitrary `B`, and full-coordinate amplitudes. The source's separate planar quartic corollary gives a uniform `mu_F/12` fourth-order gap for its modulation class; this is a quantitative strengthening on that class, not a general Morrey theorem.

### Convex-loop parameterization and limits of the comparison

The source also derives the oscillator condition for a smooth strictly convex closed planar curve enclosing the origin after reparameterizing at constant areal speed: `det(V,V')` is constant, so `V''` is parallel to `V`, and positive curvature gives `V''=-aV` with `a>0`. Thus the loop mechanism is broader than the circle/ellipse example, but still relies on a scalar spatial phase `k·x` and the special oscillator relation.

AIPS's nontrivial radial field has its target line varying around rays from a center; an FL field has a carrier line constant along parallel affine hyperplanes. A linear change of spatial coordinates does not turn radial angle into an affine scalar phase. Conversely, the displayed FL example has modulus varying in one Cartesian direction while the loop phase varies in another, so it is not a radial map. These examples rule out the direct identification of the two natural classes; they do not rule out an indirect representation through a more general laminate theorem.

## Other related primary sources

- **Laminate/Jensen characterization.** Guerra, *Extremal rank-one convex integrands and a conjecture of Šverák*, arXiv:1812.06689v2 (2019), §1, states Pedregal's characterization: a compactly supported probability measure is a laminate iff Jensen's inequality holds for every rank-one-convex integrand. It also identifies homogeneous gradient Young measures versus laminates as a dual formulation of Morrey's problem. Applied to the FL theorem, test `F_B(A)=F(B+A)`. The target's compactly supported periodic gradient distribution is therefore a laminate by this characterization (using local mollification to cover continuous rank-one-convex tests). This is a consequence of the all-background Jensen result, not an independent proof of it, and does not provide a finite splitting tree.
- **Spiral-like rank-one convex hull.** Kirchheim–Müller–Šverák, *Studying nonlinear PDE by geometry in matrix space*, MPI MIS Preprint 49 (2002), Proposition 23, treats a graph `K={(gamma(t),t)}` in a three-dimensional subspace under a tail-convex-hull separation condition and proves `K^rc=K`. The “spiral-like” construction is a set-hull/separate-convexity result. It is not a Jensen inequality for gradient measures of affine-phase loop modulations, nor a construction of the FL flux identity.
- **Compensated integrals.** Gutiérrez, *A Necessary Condition for the Quasiconvexity of Polynomials of Degree Four* (JCA 13, 2006), derives necessary weak-lower-semicontinuity/quasiconvexity constraints using compensated compactness. Its 2×2 discussion notes a condition also necessary under rank-one convexity, and it does not produce a violation of rank-one convexity for the competing fourth-order condition. This is relevant background on determinant/minor compensation, but is not a positive Jensen theorem for the FL family.
- **Numerical laminate/Morrey evidence.** Guerra–Teixeira da Costa, arXiv:2006.09063v1, report finite numerical searches as evidence toward Morrey's problem, not a proof. Sander–Kumar–Voss–Kochmann, arXiv:2201.06392v1, compare numerical quasiconvexity methods, finite-element/PINN approaches, and laminate/gradient-Young-measure representations. These sources confirm the standard testing framework; neither establishes the FL identity.
- **Recent restricted planar quartic result.** Bruno–Pasqualotto, arXiv:2608.06367v1 (6 Aug 2026), prove rank-one-convex/quasiconvex equivalence for the Dacorogna–Marcellini family and all homogeneous planar quartics invariant under both left and right `SO(2)`. This differs from the FL source's left-`SO(2)` invariant quartic cone and structured test fields. It does not state full quasiconvexity of the left-only cone.

## Broad Morrey/laminate preprint: status held open

Pedregal, *Morrey's conjecture: rank-one convexity implies quasi-convexity for two-dimensional, two-component maps*, arXiv:1905.06571v5 (last revised 18 June 2026), states that every homogeneous two-component planar gradient Young measure is a laminate and hence that rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity in `2×2`. If correct, its Jensen consequence subsumes the nonnegative FL Jensen inequality for planar periodic gradients; it does not by itself give the FL exact two-term identity or its explicit `mu_F/12` quantitative bound. It does not extend to the FL theorem's general `m×n` range.

The source's stated mechanism is triangulated piecewise-affine approximation, prescribed `(H_n)` rank-one decomposition directions (“molds”), and a set-valued fixed point synchronizing weights between the two scalar components. The load-bearing Proposition 7.1 asserts that each second-component gradient measure can be decomposed along the directions fixed by the first component. Its proof invokes Claim 7.3 (generic full range of a linear operation), then uses a local neighborhood/dimension-matching argument to conclude that the full relevant image fills the target space, followed by density and upper continuity. In the v5 text as reviewed, the generic-rank/surjectivity and dimension-to-full-image steps are asserted in prose; this report does not independently reconstruct the matrix ranks, prove the fixed-point hypotheses in full, or close those implications. This is a precise audit target, not a demonstrated flaw. Accordingly, the sweeping Morrey theorem is recorded here as **the authors' claim, not independently verified and not used as a premise**. No conclusion that it is false is warranted.

## Scope and conclusion for capital integration

The safe synthesis is:

1. AIPS Proposition 5.1 is established close prior art for a radial map class and demonstrates the broad Jensen/laminate strategy predates the FL source.
2. The FL source gives a rigorously stated different sufficient class with a loop-ODE flux identity and a stronger explicit quantitative result on a planar invariant quartic subfamily. The direct radial-to-affine-phase reduction does not work; this is a mechanism/input distinction, not a priority ruling.
3. Standard laminate characterization identifies the resulting compact gradient distributions as laminates, but does not furnish explicit finite lamination trees.
4. The Pedregal v5 Morrey claim could subsume planar Jensen nonnegativity if its proof is sound; it remains outside this report's verified theorem ledger pending independent closure of its load-bearing steps.
5. None of these comparisons closes full planar Morrey, creates a compatible negative quasiconvexity gap, or establishes historical novelty. The pinned global source itself disclaims novelty/priority certification.
