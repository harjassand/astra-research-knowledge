GENERAL ENVELOPE / LOCAL-DUAL INTERFACE
2026-10-09. Scoped internal derivation; no novelty or external validation claim.
Earlier frozen reports and source trees are preserved.

1. PURE ENVELOPE

For Hermitian Ai on C^d, f(psi)=sum_i <Ai>_psi^2, L=sum_i Ai^2,
V=tau(L), tau=Tr/d, and
k=max_{sum_y py pi_y=I/d} sum_y py f(pi_y), the exact envelope dual is

 k=min_G tau(G), subject to <psi|G|psi>>=f(psi) for every pure psi.   (I1)

One common pure ensemble is required. The feasible envelope G is PSD,
because f>=0. G=L is feasible by variance positivity. The feasible set
with trace bounded by Tr(L) is compact and closed, so a minimizer exists.
Equality of the two problems follows from compact moment duality, or
from a supergradient of the finite concave roof of f at the interior
state I/d. Thus an exact optimum G has tau(G)=k. G need not be unique.

2. THREE TRACIAL MARGINALS: NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT LOCAL DUAL

Set W=sum_i Ai^T(A)(Ai(B)+Ai(C)). The stronger, all-tracial-state problem is

 h(W)=max_{R>=0, R_A=R_B=R_C=I/d} Tr(RW)
     =min_{X,Y Hermitian} tau(X+2Y)
       subject to X_A^T+Y_B+Y_C-W>=0.                            (I2)

Output exchange permits equal B,C dual variables Y. The full-rank
product state is strictly feasible for the primal, and a large scalar
local dual is strictly feasible. Finite-dimensional SDP strong duality
therefore applies; the local-dual interface is exact for this stronger
three-marginal problem. Neither X nor Y must be positive individually.

For C>0, V-k<=C(V-s) follows from any such local dual with

 tau(X+2Y)<=2[(1-1/C)V+k/C],                                      (I3)

because legal equal-output Choi states have Tr(RW)=2s. For C=2 the
budget is V+k. A proof of (I2) within that budget is stronger than the
original legal-channel gate: no pair self-adjointness is then required.
Failure of a restricted dual ansatz is not failure of this SDP or gate.

3. SCALAR ALLOCATION IS A RESTRICTED ANSATZ

With K=L+G_opt, a natural trial is

 D_alpha=alpha K_A^T+(1-alpha)(K_B+K_C)/2-W.                       (I4)

Its trace budget is exactly V+k for every real alpha. It need not be
positive. Frame-dependent alpha and a non-diagonal optimal G are allowed
when testing whether this ansatz might be general.

If a smooth two-sided family has D_alpha(0)>=0 with kernel E, positivity
for both signs of an infinitesimal perturbation forces
P_E D_alpha'(0) P_E=0. A one-sided family instead requires the corresponding
compressed derivative to be positive. If alpha also varies, its derivative
contributes K_A^T-(K_B+K_C)/2; this contribution vanishes when K(0) is
scalar. Non-differentiable envelope branches must be kept separate.
These necessary conditions can determine an allocation before verification.

4. CHOSEN DECISIVE TEST

The test is a five-dimensional four-observable frame: a spin-one block
plus two classical levels and one traceless central observable separating
them. Its envelope is exact and decomposes explicitly. A spin equality
vector gives one necessary bound on alpha; a classical product vector
gives an incompatible one. Symmetry reduces every optimal envelope to
the unique diagonal optimum, preventing a non-diagonal escape.

This is the smallest extension in THIS block construction that supplies
two distinct same-sign classical eigenvalues: a single extra classical
level opposite to the scalar spin-block value cannot produce that
allocation constraint. No global minimality claim among all frames is made.
The frame is reducible; it is not an irreducible general-frame reduction.

C5_OBSTRUCTION_AND_GLUING.txt proves that the scalar ansatz fails while
a more general local dual certifies C=2 for this same frame. The general
arbitrary-frame finite-C question remains unresolved.
