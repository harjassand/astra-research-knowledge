## 2. A nonlinear quadratic-moment theorem

Let S:R^n→R^n be odd and C1, with for every x

    ||DS(x)||op<=a,
    ||DS(x)||F²<=rho a²,
    ||DS(x)||_*<=rho a, rho>=1.

For standard Gaussian G and |t|a<=1/4,

    E exp[t G·S(G)] <= exp[|t|rho a+3 rho t²a²].       (1)

Consequently, for p>=2,

    ||G·S(G)||p <= C a[rho+sqrt(rho p)+p].            (2)

This is an uncentered bound. The rho term is not the exact-linear gadget's much smaller rho a² trace mismatch. The theorem uses only first derivative budgets; no second derivative modulus or linearity is assumed.

### Complete proof

Oddness gives ES(G)=0. Componentwise Gaussian Poincare gives E||S(G)||²<=E||DS(G)||F²<=rho a². The function R(x)=||S(x)|| is a-Lipschitz, so Gaussian concentration gives

    E exp[u(R-ER)]<=exp(u²a²/2), u real.

Introduce an independent scalar standard Gaussian H and use exp(lambda R²)=E_H exp(sqrt(2lambda)H R). Conditional concentration and scalar Gaussian integration imply, for 2lambda a²<1,

    E exp(lambda R²)
      <= (1-2lambda a²)^(-1/2)
         exp[lambda(ER)²/(1-2lambda a²)].             (3)

For |t|a<1 the map F_t(x)=x-tS(x) is a global C1 diffeomorphism: injectivity and inverse Lipschitzness follow from ||F_t(x)-F_t(y)||>=(1-|t|a)||x-y||, and the inverse at y is the unique fixed point x=y+tS(x). Its Jacobian determinant is positive by the nonsingular homotopy from t=0. Let x=x_t(y). Lipschitzness gives

    ||S(x_t(y))|| <= ||S(y)||/(1-|t|a).

For D=DS(x), trace-log expansion is valid for |t|a<1, including nonsymmetric D. The first trace is bounded by rho a. For k>=2, |tr D^k|<=||D||F² a^(k-2)<=rho a^k. Hence

    -log det(I-tD) <= |t|rho a
                      +rho t²a²/[2(1-|t|a)].        (4)

The Gaussian density identity is exact:

    phi(x) exp[t x·S(x)-t²||S(x)||²/2]=phi(F_t(x)).

Change variables y=F_t(x) to obtain

    E exp[t G·S(G)]
      =E_y {exp[t²||S(x_t(y))||²/2]/det(I-tDS(x_t(y)))}.

Use (4), the inverse norm bound, and (3) with lambda=t²/[2(1-|t|a)²]. For |t|a<=1/4, lambda<=8t²/9 and 2lambda a²<=1/9. The log of (3) is at most 2rho t²a²; the remaining quadratic term in (4) is at most (2/3)rho t²a². This proves (1), with constant3. Both signs of t are covered. Chernoff and tail integration yield, for u>=0,

    Pr{|G·S(G)|>=rho a+sqrt(12rho)a sqrt(u)+4au}<=2e^-u,

which proves (2). Smooth convolution preserves oddness and the convex derivative norm bounds, so the result extends to Lipschitz maps with the stated almost-everywhere budgets using approximation/area formula and Fatou.

For a Gaussian covariance C with ||C||op<=c, whiten and apply the result to T(g)=C^1/2 S(C^1/2g). Its derivative budgets are multiplied by c. Thus (2) holds with an additional factor c. This will matter for signed covariance corrections, where C need not be <=I.

Nearest prior art: the Gaussian determinant identity is the finite-dimensional Ramer change-of-variables formula, explicitly present in Ustunel's author book arXiv:1003.1649v2, Chapter10 opening pp119–120/Theorem10.1.1. Poincare/concentration are classical. Our work is this elementary nuclear-budget corollary and its cloud-action application; no claim of new measure-change machinery.

## 3. The actual source secants can satisfy the stronger budgets

For the source's two-input primitive cloud

    Phi(u,g)=grad V(prox_eta V(x+sqrt(theta)u)+sqrt(eta)g),

SUPPLY a uniform primitive Hessian bound ||H_V(y)||F²<=rho and ||H_V(y)||op<=1. This is stronger than small energy of the final composite Phi. For every realized centered gradient secant, its derivative with respect to direction is the average of two primitive Hessians, so it retains those bounds. A proximal secant (including fixed-M approximation) has direction derivative operator norm<=1.

The actual forward action is J(u,v)=D_grad(D_prox(sqrt(theta)u)+sqrt(eta)v). Its directional derivative equals

    F [sqrt(theta)P, sqrt(eta)I],

where ||F||F<=sqrt(rho), ||F||op<=1, ||P||op<=1. The reverse action derivative is the vertical matrix

    [sqrt(theta)P F; sqrt(eta)F].

No symmetry of P is required for the pathwise bound; source mean-accuracy reasoning uses the exact symmetric comparison derivatives separately. Both realized derivatives have op<=L=sqrt(theta+eta) and HS<=sqrt(rho)L. Their composition S=J_forward o J_reverse has

    ||DS||op<=L², ||DS||F<=sqrt(rho)L²,
    ||DS||_*<=||DJ_forward||F ||DJ_reverse||F<=rho L².

Each fixed-seed action is odd. Nonlinear products S_k o ... o S_1 have the same budgets scaled to L^(2k). Conditional Gaussian moment bound (2), summation, and tilted covariance<=I give the source estimator clipping scale

    e_p <= C L²[rho+sqrt(rho p)+p]/(1-L²).           (5)

This is dimension independent under the SUPPLIED primitive bound. It is weaker than the exact-Jacobian scale L²[sqrt(rho p)+p]+rho L⁴ because we cannot cancel nonlinear divergence terms. Source bias control under both Q and P_K and B.4 caps remain necessary. Conditional relative bias still carries a dimension factor in its integrated scalar bound; choose derivative tolerance sufficiently small, paying the source's logarithmic precision cost.

Block selection and inverse-probability rescaling in Section4.4 preserve the budgets when the same rho promise holds for all selected blocks. Each action uses one selected block, not all blocks.

The source's unmodified well-conditioned core has kappa^-1 I<=H_V<=I and kappa<=kappa0 universal. Thus rho>=d/kappa0². The general-target proximal wrapper adds a known2I Hessian. Consequently (5) does NOT improve the dimension exponent of the actual existing core. To exploit low residual dimension one must separate the known quadratic throughout the relevant correction block. Appendix A.2 already performs known-quadratic integration for its improved RGO sampler, so this architectural idea is prior art in the very source.

## 4. Affine-baseline cloud correction without knowing the mean

A conditional extension addresses that known linear barrier directly. Supply

    Phi(g)=b+A g+psi(g), known A, ||A||op=a<1,

where b need not be evaluated separately. Supply realized odd forward/reverse residual actions with derivative op<=ell, HS<=sqrt(tau)ell, nuclear<=tau ell, tau>=1, and controlled conditional mean bias. The residual map satisfies the same true derivative bounds. No common derivative range/projector is assumed.

Set C0=I-AA^T and beta=(2a ell+ell²)/(1-a²)<=1/8. The base proposal is Phi(B1)+Z0, with Z0~N(0,C0). Thus a known full-rank Gaussian fluctuation is handled exactly while its unknown center stays hidden.

Let J_t be the residual Clark–Ocone integrand. The full integrand is A+J_t and

    Sigma=AA^T+Delta,
    Delta=int_0^1 [A J_t^T+J_t A^T+J_t J_t^T]dt.

The ideal conditional noise is C0-Delta. Define D=C0^-1/2 Delta C0^-1/2. Then ||D||op<=beta, ||D||F<=sqrt(tau)beta, ||D||_*<=tau beta. The matrix D can be indefinite. In whitened base-noise coordinates z, the exact conditional log tilt is

    w(B,z)=-1/2 logdet(I-D)
            -1/2 z^T[(I-D)^-1-I]z
          =1/2 sum_{k>=1}[tr D^k/k-z^T D^k z].      (6)

Its conditional normalizer is EXACTLY1; hence the Brownian marginal is unchanged. The ideal output is N(E Phi(G),I) by the original martingale completion. This requires neither b nor the mean of psi.

An unbiased correction action for D uses independent bridge endpoint and residual-action seeds:

    S(v)=C0^-1/2 {
       A J_reverse(C0^-1/2v)
       +J_forward(A^T C0^-1/2v)
       +J_forward_new(J_reverse_new(C0^-1/2v))}.

In the cross terms one bridge derivative sample suffices; the product uses two independent endpoint samples. Mean is Dv (or a quantitatively controlled approximation). The derivative has op<=beta, HS<=sqrt(tau)beta, nuclear<=tau beta, by triangle inequalities and the primitive budgets. Products retain the scaled budgets beta^k. Apply (2).

Truncate (6) at K. For signed eigenvalues |lambda|<=beta,

    C_K(lambda)=(1-lambda)/(1-lambda^(K+1)),
    ||C_K||op <= (1+beta)/(1-beta^(K+1)) <=2.

The covariance factor2, rather than <=I, must be included in tilted moment bounds. The nuclear tail gives |log Z_K(B)|<=Ctau beta^(K+1); conditional Gaussian discrepancy is at most Csqrt(tau)beta^(K+1). Consequently truncation TV<=Ctau beta^(K+1) for the usual small-normalizer condition. The nonlinear moment lemma controls W_K under both proposal and tilted laws at

    e_p^aff <= C beta[tau+sqrt(tau p)+p]/(1-beta).    (7)

Clipping/FORS then gives the conditional gadget bound

    TV(output,N(E Phi(G),I))
      <=C[tau beta^(K+1)+B(e_p^aff/B)^p
           +integrated_action_bias+value_error+cap_error].   (8)

This is a complete conditional correction mechanism. The mean bias, value accuracy and capped-tail terms cannot be omitted. Proof of clipping and FORS is the source's already independently reconstructed argument with (7) replacing its moment scale. All signed covariance identities in (6) and the Gaussian normalization are checked independently in our code.

### Legal gradient-only residual actions for a quadratic-plus-convex target

Let V(z)=q||z||²/2+U(z), q known>0, U convex, ||H_U||op<=h and tr H_U<=tau h uniformly. Gradient U is obtained from the same gradient oracle as grad V(z)-qz. Consider p=prox_eta V(x+sqrt(theta)u), y=p+sqrt(eta)g and c=qeta/(1+qeta). The exact proximal identity gives

    Phi(u,g)=grad V(y)=b+A(u,g)+psi(u,g),
    b=qx/(1+qeta),
    A=[q sqrt(theta)/(1+qeta) I, q sqrt(eta) I],
    psi=grad U(y)-c grad U(p).

The known affine value is q(x+sqrt(theta)u)/(1+qeta)+q sqrt(eta)g; it can be evaluated from the observed cloud point even if x,u are not separately known. b need not be supplied. Residual secants are built DIRECTLY from U and prox V, rather than subtracting independently randomized full-rank actions.

Let D_U,y and D_U,p denote source-centered secants of grad U at the two stored locations, and D_prox a source-centered proximal secant. Forward residual action:

    J(u,g)=D_U,y(D_prox(sqrt(theta)u)+sqrt(eta)g)
            -c D_U,p(D_prox(sqrt(theta)u)).

Reverse residual action:

    JT(w)=[sqrt(theta)D_prox(D_U,y(w)-cD_U,p(w)),
           sqrt(eta)D_U,y(w)].

Use fresh elementary seeds independent of incoming directions, as required by source B.3. Their directional derivatives have op<=ell, HS<=sqrt(tau)ell, nuclear<=tau ell, where

    ell=h[sqrt(theta+eta)+c sqrt(theta)].

This follows because each U secant derivative averages PSD Hessians, retaining trace<=tau h and HS<=sqrt(tau)h; multiplying by a proximal contraction and known scalar block matrices preserves the bounds. Triangle inequalities handle the two residual terms. No symmetry of the finite-M proximal derivative is needed. Exact mean approximation and finite-M costs follow the same source B.2/B.3 argument, with tolerances chosen explicitly for (8).

The nuclear residual promise is substantive. It is not inferred from a fitted low-rank covariance, nor from small derivative energy of the final composite. Models such as a log-sum-exp potential of bounded-norm features have a dimension-independent Hessian trace bound, even when their feature span is full dimensional, but the oracle cost and structural verification still need charging.

### Block-local implementation and fair costs

For Phi=b+sum_j A_j[N_j G_j+psi_j(G_j)], let C0=I-sum_j A_jN_jN_j^T A_j^T and B_j=C0^-1/2 A_j. Put

    q_j=||B_j||op²[2||N_j||op ell_j+ell_j²],
    beta_bar=sum_j q_j, p_j=q_j/beta_bar.

Sample block j with probability p_j and inverse-weight its three residual action terms. This gives realized budgets with beta_bar in place of beta and calls only that selected block. Use beta_bar<=1/8 and the same tau across blocks. This avoids an uncharged all-block derivative scan.

One FORS attempt uses J~Poisson(2B) estimators; acceptance is >=e^-2B. Expected attempts<=e^2B and expected estimator calls<=2B e^2B, including rejections. Each W estimator makes O(K) covariance actions; each affine covariance action uses O(1) forward/reverse residual routines. Each routine costs CS³ log* gradient queries at its REQUESTED precision, including proximal iterations. A proposal value costs the sum of the block value costs. Thus expected gradients are bounded by

    e^2B [C_value + C B K C_action].

The dependence of log* on dimension, radius and tolerance is retained. Computing/applying C0^-1/2 is an additional cost: arbitrary dense A can cost cubic preprocessing/quadratic application. In the paper's scalar-node tensor-I blocks, C0 is a small node matrix tensor I_d, enabling O(J³) preprocessing and O(J²d) application. Output and bridge vector work are at least linear in dimension and the number of revealed points. Exact-real theorem and floating-point replay are distinct; no interval certification is claimed.

Strong baseline: if A,psi are known linear, direct Gaussian sampling/one affine evaluation is superior. If a common intrinsic subspace is supplied, reduce to that subspace and sample its Gaussian complement exactly. Our interface supplies no common projector. Ordinary Monte Carlo mean estimation uses O(trace Var(Phi)/delta²) values; for residual-only values this can scale with tau, but recovering the hidden affine center cannot simply subtract an unavailable standardized cloud label. We do not claim a lower bound ruling out better competent baselines.

## 5. Alternate value-only operator handles true composite energy directly

c_nonlinear independently constructed central whole-map secants, with shared Gaussian direction and independent bridge endpoints, forming rank-one covariance actions. It needs only TRUE uniform differential energy of Phi plus value queries; it avoids the source's primitive cancellation failure. Heat smoothing after endpoint averaging removes any extra Hessian-modulus requirement. Its admitted scale is L²=O(1/[p(r+p)]), inferior to the exact-action O(r^-1/2); value precision depends on input dimension and secant step. Averaging r directions restores second moments only at r-fold query cost; nonlinear high moments remain open. I independently checked the PSD covariance, integrable heat bias, matrix-product moments, tilted moments, precision extension and rejected-attempt ledger. See `notes/c_nonlinear/cycle2/central_secant_cloud.md`.

## 6. Scientific status and next falsifiable step

The present results answer narrower questions: automatic derivative-energy transfer FAILS for actual source secants; dimension-sensitive behavior can be repaired using stronger nuclear budgets, whole-map shared-direction covariance estimates, or a known affine baseline with directly implemented residual actions. They do not establish an end-to-end rho exponent for Theorem1.1. Hidden RGO likelihoods and mean estimators retain ambient-dimensional terms; the well-conditioned core forbids rho<<d before a residual rewrite.

Next scientific test: implement the affine residual cloud for a full-span bounded-trace nonlinear gradient, with hidden center and no supplied projector; verify both proposal and tilted error budgets, all requested precision and cap tails; compare charged cost against value-only whole-map secants, source-gradient correction, low-subspace baseline when applicable, and direct Monte Carlo at equal TV accuracy. A full sampler theorem additionally needs a residual intrinsic-dimension version of the hidden-RGO and mean-estimation steps. Those are the load-bearing unresolved implications.
