# Independent audit: endotactic permanence proof mechanism

## Scope and interface reconstructed from the TeX

The state is (x\in\mathbb R^d_{>0}). A finite labelled edge set has sources and targets (y_e,y'_e\in C\subset\mathbb R^d), reaction vectors \(\nu_e=y'_e-y_e\), and real power-law monomials \(x^{y_e}=\prod_i x_i^{(y_e)_i}\), interpreted only for positive coordinates. The stoichiometric space is \(S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\nu_e\}\), and a positive class is \(P=(c+S)\cap\mathbb R^d_{>0}\) for a supplied \(c>0\). Rates are arbitrary measurable functions with the common bounds \(0<\kappa\le k_e(t)\le K<\infty\). Solutions are positive-domain Carathéodory solutions.

For each direction \(w\), the essential edges are exactly those with \(w\cdot\nu_e\ne0\); the essential source tier maximizes \(w\cdot y_e\) over those edges. Endotacticity says every essential edge on that tier has \(w\cdot\nu_e<0\). Consequently, in direction \(-r\), every essential edge at the minimum \(r\)-source tier has \(r\cdot\nu_e>0\). The argument permits noninteger and negative source coordinates; it requires the positive orthant domain.

## Reconstructed proof chain

1. An affine-minimum lemma supplies finitely many fixed slopes \(r_\lambda\in[-1,1]^d\) and small-scale offsets, so every active slope at \(x=X_h(p)=(h^{p_i})_i\), \(p\in[-1,1]^d\), is within a pointwise positive tolerance of \(p\). Taking a finite minimum over active-slope/vector products gives \(H>0\). Thus every nonzero comparison \(r\cdot v\), for coordinate directions or vertex differences, has the same sign as \(p\cdot v\), with magnitude at least \(H\).
2. For an active \(r\notin S^\perp\), choose an edge \(e_*:w\to w'\) at the minimum source level among edges with \(r\cdot\nu_e\ne0\). Endotacticity gives \(r\cdot\nu_{e_*}\ge2H\). Every edge with negative \(r\)-flux has source strictly above this tier, hence \(x^{y_e}\le h^H x^w\). Therefore
   \[
   r\cdot f(t,x)\ge x^w(2\kappa H-KLh^H)\ge\kappa Hh^M>0,
   \]
   when \(KLh^H\le\kappa H\), with \(L=\sum_e\|\nu_e\|_1\) and \(M=\max_e\|y_e\|_1\). If \(r\in S^\perp\), the product is zero. This is pointwise in all rate values, so measurable switching is handled a.e.
3. At the class representative \(c\), choosing \(h\) with \(\|\log c/\log h\|_\infty<H\) forces all active slopes to be zero. The least constant affine label gives a global ceiling \(M_h=F_h(c)\); its level set \(\mathcal K_h=\{F_h=M_h\}\) is an intersection of half-spaces. Activity of the constant label on \(\mathcal K_h\cap[h,h^{-1}]^d\) puts that intersection strictly inside \([\sqrt h,1/\sqrt h]^d\). Convexity and a segment-to-boundary argument then put the whole plateau there, making it compact.
4. Off the plateau inside the class, no active slope can lie in \(S^\perp\): such a label is constant on \(c+S\) and has value at least \(M_h\), while \(F_h<M_h\) off the plateau. The finite-minimum chain rule gives a.e. derivative equal to the minimum over *all* active labels, including ties. Thus \(F_h\) is nondecreasing in the box and increases at rate at least \(\kappa Hh^M\) off the plateau. Compact interior containment gives invariance, global continuation, and finite entry for any trajectory that stays in the box.
5. Every initial point has a sufficiently small initial scale at which it is in the initial plateau. Replacing \(h_j\) by \(h_{j+1}=\min(\sqrt{h_j},h_P)\) puts that plateau inside the next box; finite entry reaches the next plateau. Repeated square roots hit the fixed capped scale in finitely many steps. The plateaux need not be nested. Class invariance follows from \(f(t,x)\in S\) a.e.; intersecting the final plateau with \(c+S\) gives the classwise compact absorber.

This chain handles ties, real/negative exponents, an unbounded class, and measurable bounded rates, *conditional on the affine lemma and its uniform-activity corollary*. No separate problem was found in these later steps in this read-through. The two-sided common rate bound is load-bearing: the proof uses \(\kappa\) on the selected positive edge and \(K\) on the entire negative-flux sum.

## Reassessment of the affine-lemma neighborhood step

The displayed definition already reads \(A_\lambda\subset[a,b]^m\): the realizing sequence points \(p^n\) may lie in \(\mathbb R^m\), while only their limits in the cube are retained. The \(t=b\) singleton example therefore does not refute the proof; limits outside the cube are excluded. My earlier counterexample and alleged compactness gap were based on misreading that restriction and are withdrawn.

Closedness follows by the diagonal selection written in the source. If \(z_j\in A_\lambda\) and \(z_j\to z\), then \(z\in[a,b]^m\) because the cube is closed. For each \(j\), choose from a realizing sequence for \(z_j\) an actually active pair \((h_j,u_j)\) with \(0<h_j<1/j\) and \(\|u_j-z_j\|_\infty<1/j\). Then \(h_j\to0\), \(u_j\to z\), and the same label is active at every selected pair, so \(z\in A_\lambda\). Thus \(A_\lambda\) is closed in the compact cube and is compact. For each fixed label, the constraint \(\|p-r\|_\infty\ge E(r)\) is closed with fixed scalar \(E(r)>0\); the finite union \(B_t\) is compact. A point of \(B_t\) with \(\mu(p)=t\) would be realized by an active sequence with cube limit on the slice, contradicting the local-slice property. Continuity of \(\mu\) then gives a strictly positive minimum distance from \(t\) on nonempty \(B_t\), as used to choose \(\rho_t\). If \(B_t\) is empty, any initial positive radius works before the offset-decay shrinkage.

On rereading this step, I find no remaining gap in this compactness/neighborhood argument. The arbitrary outside-cube approximants are intentional and are allowed by the affine lemma; the limiting point is in the cube, exactly where the local guarantee applies.

## Exact dependency/gap ledger

- **Affine approximation / uniform activity:** finite \(D\), arbitrary pointwise-positive \(E(r)\), compactness of the bad-limit set \(B_t\subset[a,b]^m\), and finite-subcover gluing. The formerly reported bad-set domain error was not real: the source explicitly restricts limit points to the cube. I found no concrete defect in these sections on this pass.
- **Flux drift:** requires the corrected uniform-activity result; endotacticity with the essential-source convention; finite source/target set; nonzero \(\nu_e\in D\); and the common positive rate bounds. It does not require weak reversibility or nonnegative exponents.
- **Plateau and entry:** additionally require the affine offsets be defined on a common small-scale interval and satisfy their asserted little-o bounds; finite-minimum a.e. chain rule; Carathéodory continuation on compact subsets of the positive orthant; and class invariance from velocities in (S). The proof explicitly includes every minimizer at ties.
- **Nonnested scale iteration:** requires the fixed-scale entry result for a global trajectory staying in the next box and \(\mathcal K_h\subset\operatorname{int}B_{\sqrt h}\). The finite square-root count is correct; nesting is unnecessary.
- **Boundary and negative powers:** solutions never use vector-field values on the coordinate boundary. The compact positive plateau prevents boundary approach after the initial time, so negative exponents do not by themselves invalidate continuation. The class \(P\) may be unbounded; absorption is supplied by the scale chain, not boundedness of (P).
