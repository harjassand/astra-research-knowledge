## 1. Local equations and branch inverses

Fix a PD principal triple q. In independent auxiliary variables m_i,s_i, i=1,2,3, define

\[
U_i=1-2m_i s_i,\quad V_i=q_{ii}-s_i^2,\quad A_i=U_iV_i,
\]

\[
H_{ij}=q_{ij}(1-4m_i s_i)+s_j(-s_i-2q_{ii}m_i+6m_i s_i^2),\qquad
B=3s_1s_2s_3-q_{23}s_1-q_{13}s_2-q_{12}s_3.
\]

For each of the eight mass/root assignments choose P_i=U_i for a mass index and P_i=V_i for a root index. Define the degree-one branch inverse

\[
\ell_i=\begin{cases}2m_i&\text{mass},\\ s_i/q_{ii}&\text{root}.\end{cases}
\]

Then s_i ell_i=1 modulo the selected P_i. More explicitly, s_i ell_i=1−U_i in the mass case and 1−V_i/q_ii in the root case. The three selected quadratic relations have relatively prime leading monomials m_i s_i or s_i² under a graded term order. Reduction acts separately in each index and strictly lowers total degree by two; their polynomial division therefore yields an exact certificate without increasing total degree. This is a proved division property here, not an imported unrestricted Gröbner complexity bound.

Put

\[
d_i=q_{ii}\ell_i^2,\qquad r_{ij}=q_{ij}\ell_i\ell_j,\qquad \kappa_i=2-d_i.
\]

All have total degree at most two, and the exact polynomial identities

\[
r_{ij}^2=\alpha_{ij}d_i d_j,\qquad \alpha_{ij}=q_{ij}^2/(q_{ii}q_{jj})\in[0,1)
\tag{1}
\]

hold before taking any quotient. Direct selected-P reduction gives

\[
e_{ij}:=\kappa_i-r_{ij}\equiv H_{ij}\ell_i\ell_j\pmod{(P_1,P_2,P_3)}
\quad(i\text{ mass}),
\]

\[
e_B:=3-r_{12}-r_{13}-r_{23}\equiv B\ell_1\ell_2\ell_3
\pmod{(P_1,P_2,P_3)}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus every e polynomial has a certificate in (P_i,H_ij,B) with maximum degree of multiplier times generator at most six. A root index also has d_i−1=−P_i/q_ii with certificate degree two. These bounds include the lifts, not just equations in a localized quotient.

## 3. Three branches with one mass index: degree10
