GAMES / HAMILTONIANS — CYCLE 4: INVERSE-RESOLVENT ROW ACCESS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: an exact sparse history-image representation is constructed with constant raw gap and polynomial norm; the original-physical-basis constant-normalized-gap gate remains UNKNOWN.

HISTORY-AUGMENTED COMMON-IMAGE POSITIVE CONTROL

There is a distinct positive result when the representation may be enlarged
by a circuit history register. For a polynomial gate isometry V and a
one-sparse diagonal B with alpha H <= V*BV <= beta H, the binary-clock
construction in inverse_resolvent/13_history_common_image.md gives an exact
history-image kernel, a full-space enforcer gap 1/2, and exact terminal-test
compression. Its uniform complete row-list has constant degree, logarithmic
locality, polynomially many qubits, and polynomial norm. Scaling the enforcer
and terminal test yields a constant raw promise separation; normalization
reduces that separation by an inverse polynomial.

This is the standard Feynman--Kitaev sparse-QMA simulation. It resolves a
legal history-augmented representation interface but does not sparsify the
fixed physical C0/Cinc matrices, does not keep a constant normalized gap, and
does not exceed ordinary inverse-polynomial-gap sparse-QMA verification.
The original fixed-basis C0/Cinc access question remains open.

In the circuit-access model there is also no extra complexity consequence:
C0=UBU* is measured by applying U* and measuring diagonal B. A random choice
between this test and B/8 realizes K0=(C0+B/8)/(9/8), with YES energy at most
2e/9 and NO energy at least min(c,1)/18. The verifier uses polynomially many
gates on the physical witness register; it does not provide a classical
complete row list. The distinction between circuit access and row access is
the unresolved representation cost, not a new QMA verifier capability.

DECISION

The exact graph-incidence penalty

    Cinc = T* T / 16,       Tz = (z_a - K_a (I-H)^(-1) z_0)_(a>0)

is a positive contraction with kernel ran(V) and Cinc >= (I-VV*)/16 when
H <= 3I/4. On an explicit hardness-preserving BMVZ singleton-seed
augmentation, its exact rows are superpolynomially long: one argument gives
at least ceil((2^R-1)/(m-1)) entries for every real orthogonal PREPARE
completion; another gives 2^R entries for a specified completion and an
isolated flag sector. R is polynomially large and m is polynomial. This is a
representation-specific exact row-list obstruction, not an impossibility
for every positive enforcer.

The row/column l2 test weakens that obstruction for approximation. In the
displayed Cinc row the off-diagonal seed coefficients have total l2 norm
Theta(gamma 2^(-R/2)); for C0 the one-site diagonal entry of a selected
relative-reflection row tends to one and the remaining norm is
O((sqrt(3)/2)^R). Thus exact support counts alone do not rule out
constant- or inverse-polynomial-error sparse approximants. The selected
seed block of Cinc admits a sparse expander approximation, but this does not
approximate the entire Cinc matrix.

An operator-norm approximation with a uniform polynomial row-list would be
enough to preserve the QMA promise interval after a diagonal shift, with an
explicit error budget. The Neumann polynomial for (I-H)^(-1) has polynomial
circuit cost and controlled norm error, but no polynomial row-list follows:
the BMVZ clauses are tensor-OR terms with exponentially many candidate row
entries. The missing gate is support enumeration, not conditioning or
spectral precision.

One exact row-2 enforcer is available after an inverse-free polar rotation
and changing to the eigenbasis of H. Its spectral transformations and sorted
eigenvalue oracle are not uniformly accessible from the BMVZ term generator;
the direct dense fallback costs exponential dimension. This is an algebraic
positive control, not a sparse reduction. Separately, replacing each
tensor-OR clause by its sum of local violations yields a same-kernel,
polynomial-row local Hamiltonian with gap c/R. That is a valid
inverse-polynomial-gap sparse-QMA consequence, not constant-gap amplification
or a common-image enforcer.

EXACT INCIDENCE THEOREM AND COST

Let H=sum_j pi_j h_j be the normalized positive projection list including a
silent zero term, with H <= 3I/4. In the complement-LCU encoding K_0=Y=I-H,
Y>=I/4, and sum_a K_a* K_a=I. Set D_a=K_aY^(-1) for a>0 and
T=[-D; I]. Then

    D*D = Y^(-2)-I <= 15 I,
    TT* = I + D D* in [I,16 I].

Therefore Cinc=T*T/16 is a contraction. Tz=0 iff z=V(Y^(-1)z_0), so
ker Cinc=ran(V); all nonzero singular values of T are at least one and
Cinc >= (1/16)(I-VV*). This is a full-space statement, including all
physical label sectors. The formula provides exact kernel and gap but
contains Y^(-1) in its cross blocks.

For the all-completions access witness, append a fresh five-qubit BJSW seed
projection p_phi=|phi><phi|, phi=(|00010>-|10010>)/sqrt(2), as a positive
singleton layer with constant weight, scaling the old hard Hamiltonian and
the seed to H_out=(G tensor I + I tensor P_R)/2; add zero padding so
H=(3/8)G tensor I +(3/8)P_R <=3/4 I. Here P_R=I-(I-p_phi)^(tensor R)
is exactly the repeated singleton clause. For u=00010, v=10010,
x=u^(tensor R), the row of Q_R=(I-p_phi)^(tensor R) on {u,v}^R has all
entries 2^(-R), and P_R has off-diagonal entries -2^(-R).

Orthogonality of PREPARE columns gives

    sum_(a>0) A_(phi,a) D_a = chi_phi (R_phi Y^(-1)-I).

On the row (b,x) and columns (b,y), y in {u,v}^R minus {x}, the RHS entry is

    chi_phi 2^(-R) [<b|g_0(G)|b> + <b|g_1(G)|b>] > 0,
    g_0(z)=(1-3z/8)^(-1),  g_1(z)=(5/8-3z/8)^(-1).

Positivity follows from positive-definite functional calculus on 0<=G<=I.
Thus the union of the m-1 D_a row supports covers 2^R-1 distinct columns,
so some D_a has at least ceil((2^R-1)/(m-1)) entries. The corresponding
Cinc cross-label block is -D_a/16. This exact lower is independent of the
PREPARE completion but is scoped to this augmented legal family and the
displayed Cinc construction. BMVZ's singleton rule guarantees the repeated
seed; the augmentation preserves the source promise and constant layer
weight. It is not asserted to be the unmodified output of one fixed ABN
reduction.

There is also a simpler exact selected-completion calculation on an
F=01-in-every-copy reducing sector. A completion isolating the phi branch
gives K_b=2 kappa P_R; all old hard terms and the other seed flag clauses
vanish on this sector. With alpha=23/32, the cross block is
-gamma P_R, gamma=kappa/(8 alpha). A selected physical row has its same-label
diagonal 1/16 and exactly 2^R seed entries in label 0. This alternative
avoids the nontrivial g_i(G) factor but quantifies only one completion and
sector.

APPROXIMATION AND THE ROW-TAIL TEST

For Hermitian s-row-sparse A and Hermitian C, ||A-C|| >= ||(A-C)e_i||_2.
Since Hermiticity bounds both row and column support by s, the best s-entry
tail of a column is a necessary operator-norm error. For Cinc's displayed
selected row let N=2^R, a=1/16, d=gamma(1-1/N), q=gamma/N. For s>=2 its exact
best retained squared mass is a^2+d^2+min(s-2,N-1)q^2, giving

    tau_inc(s) = gamma sqrt(max(0,N+1-s))/N.

For polynomial s=o(N), this is gamma 2^(-R/2)(1-o(1)); gamma is at most a
constant and can be inverse polynomial due to the selected term weight.
Therefore the one-column test is exponentially small and gives no
constant-error or inverse-polynomial-error sparse-approximation lower bound.

For C0, conjugating the physical output-label register into the term frame
and selecting the phi-to-psi block exposes a row of R_phi R_psi. With counts
(a,b,c) of outputs in the common, phi-only, and psi-only seed alphabets, its
entry is

    r_R=4(3/8)^a(1/2)^b(1/8)^c
         -2*1[c=0]*(1/2)^(a+b)
         -2*1[b=0]*(3/4)^a(1/4)^c + 1[b=c=0],

with multiplicity R!/(a!b!c!)*3^c. The squared entries sum to one. The
diagonal coefficient

    r_0(R)=1+4(3/8)^R-2(1/2)^R-2(3/4)^R

tends to one, so the row's best one-coordinate residual is at most
(2+o(1))(sqrt(3)/2)^R. For an s-row-sparse approximation to C0, label
conjugation increases support by at most a factor m in the selected block
row, giving the necessary condition eps >= (1/8) tau_R(ms). This also
decays exponentially for s>=1. The exact 5^R nonzero count remains true;
the row-mass test simply cannot make it robust at useful error.

These estimates are necessary one-row tests, not sufficient approximations.
In particular, a sparse expander can approximate the isolated seed block
-gamma(I-J/N) in operator norm, but that does not treat the other Cinc
blocks or provide a uniform global enforcer. Global sparse approximation
for C0/Cinc and alternative exact positive enforcers remain UNKNOWN.

GAP TRANSFER, WITH COSTS

Suppose H has YES minimum energy <=e and NO minimum energy >=c, and an exact
image enforcer C obeys C>=gamma(I-VV*) and 0<=C<=I. If
alpha H<=V*BV<=beta H for a positive contraction B, set
r=min(alpha c,1), tau=gamma/8. The energy of C+tau B is at most tau beta e
in YES and at least gamma r/16 in NO. The proof decomposes an arbitrary
physical vector y=Vx+z: either ||z||^2>=r/16 and C supplies the gap, or
the encoded component is large enough that B supplies it. The unnormalized
separation is Delta=gamma r/16 - tau beta e.

If a Hermitian row-list approximation Ctilde has certified error eta, then
Ctilde+eta I>=C and is positive, adds only a diagonal row entry, and has YES
threshold at most tau beta e+2 eta and NO threshold at least gamma r/16.
Thus 2eta<Delta suffices; eta<=Delta/4 keeps at least half the raw gap.
Symmetric entry rounding by mu on s entries per row costs at most s mu in
operator norm, so requested fractional precision b>=ceil(log_2(8s/Delta))
is sufficient for an eta budget Delta/8. This transfers a gap only after
the uniform row-list oracle has been supplied.

For Cinc, Neumann truncation G_L=sum_(k=0)^L H^k gives
||Y^(-1)-G_L||<=4(3/4)^(L+1). Substitution into T and taking T_L*T_L/16
gives a positive polynomial-circuit approximation at error
<=((8+sigma_L)sigma_L)/16, sigma_L=4(3/4)^(L+1). The degree L is
O(log(1/eta)); however, the BMVZ tensor-OR terms have row support up to
2^(5R), and no cancellations or row-enumeration algorithm for this Gram
polynomial are proved. It is operator-norm approximability, not sparse
access.

OTHER CONSTRUCTIONS AND CONSEQUENCES

1. Inverse-free polar/spectral route. The failure block Z of V satisfies
Z*Z=F=2H-H^2. A polar rotation within the failure subspace maps
Z to an inclusion times sqrt(F), preserving B. In the eigenbasis of H,
Y=I-H and sqrt(F) are diagonal; each logical coordinate gives a 2x2
rank-one image projector. The complementary projector is exact, gap one,
and row degree at most two. But constructing the polar rotation, the global
eigenbasis, sorted eigenvalue labels, and coefficient oracle costs
exponential dimension in the generic dense fallback. A uniform sorted
coefficient oracle would reveal lambda_min through 2 lambda_min-lambda_min^2,
so would place this QMA-hard promise in P. This is a conditional diagnostic,
not an unconditional impossibility result.
2. Direct-sum incidence. For any isometry V, the map T(z0,z1)=z1-Vz0
has TT*=I+VV* in [I,2I]. Thus C=T*T/2 has kernel ran((V,I)/sqrt(2)),
contraction and gap 1/2 without Y^(-1). The test B'=B direct-sum 0 has
compression (1/2)V*BV, retaining comparison H/2<=...<=H. For the selected
BMVZ completion, the cross block contains V and inherits its seed row
support; this removes the inverse but does not close row access.
3. Clock route. Local Feynman-Kitaev propagation has explicit sparse rows
and a path gap Theta(T^-2); a terminal test has history weight 1/(T+1).
Biasing a birth-death clock can give a constant clock gap, but a Rayleigh
test f(t)=t/T forces gap <=1/(2T^2 pi_0 pi_T). Constant root mass therefore
forces terminal mass O(T^-2). Making the test invariant on a suffix instead
returns to an uncontrolled exact image anchor. No constant-gap sparse
history encoding with the desired test compression was found.
4. Sparse-QMA fallback. For each OR clause h_j, let S_j=sum_r P_{jr}.
The commuting joint spectrum gives h_j<=S_j<=R h_j and equal kernels.
Then Hhat=(1/R)sum_j w_j S_j is k-local, polynomial-row, a contraction,
and H/R<=Hhat<=H. The source promise becomes e versus c/R. This is a
derived inverse-polynomial-gap sparse local QMA instance, not a constant-gap
PCP result and not a full-image enforcer.
5. Prior-art boundary. Detectability preserves a supplied gap (up to
constant coloring losses); ordinary graph sparsification needs an explicit
graph and preserves rather than creates the gap; HHL outputs inverse states,
not classical row lists. None provides BMVZ physical-row access.

SEPARATE L02 REPETITION BRANCH

The operator-repetition theorem remains a separate game-theoretic result.
The zero-spectrum Jensen step and mixed exposure law were independently
reconstructed in `cycle2/operator_exposure_referee.txt`: positive resolvent
regularization handles continuous spectrum at zero, vectorwise L2 transport
uses strong closure where appropriate, and exposure probabilities retain
the unnormalized vector-norm mass. The tail is
exp[-sigma delta^3 k/(2048(1+ln d))], or denominator 1024 for connected
support. This does not supply a finite tensor strategy, Hamiltonian, sparse
row oracle, or common positive encoding. No gap is inferred from an
efficient question sampler.

DECISION / NEXT BOTTLENECK

The fixed-C0 exact row obstruction, fixed-Cinc exact row obstruction, and
conditional norm-to-gap argument are now separated cleanly. The selected
row tails explain why neither exact-support count proves an approximate
no-go. The strongest positive controlled result is an abstract 2-sparse
spectral re-encoding whose access would solve the hard source promise; the
strongest directly generated sparse Hamiltonian has gap c/R. The actual
high-ceiling open gate is a uniform construction, from the BMVZ term/path
generator in its original physical representation, of an exact or
sufficiently accurate positive full-space image enforcer with polynomial
complete row lists, constant normalized gap, and the common compression.
Alternatively, a global impossibility theorem for that interface would
resolve it. Neither is established. Status: UNKNOWN.
