# Polynomial squarefree constraints as recurrence weights

Status: complete elementary transfer and a conditional cross-field consequence. The new input range comes from the release's claimed density theorem; this investigation does not treat that theorem as externally validated. The general ergodic ingredients are prior results, not new claims.

## Exact supplied interface

Let b:N→{0,1} indicate simultaneous k-power-freeness of a fixed finite polynomial family F₁,…,F_s, with k≥2. Let Ω_p be its forbidden residue set modulo p^k, and let

b_R(n)=1{n avoids Ω_p for every p≤R}, M_R=∏_{p≤R}p^k,

c_R=∏_{p≤R}(1−#Ω_p/p^k), c=∏_p(1−#Ω_p/p^k)>0.

Assume the exact density statement (1/N)Σ_{n≤N}b(n)→c. This is what family020 Corollary1.3 supplies for admissible fixed separable polynomial families whose irreducible factors have degree at most k+2, conditional on its proof and prior higher-degree inputs.

Since b_R≥b and b_R is periodic of mean c_R,

lim_{N→∞}(1/N)Σ_{n≤N}|b_R(n)−b(n)|=c_R−c→0.

Thus b is rationally almost periodic in the Besicovitch/Cesaro L1 metric. This conclusion needs the **exact Euler product density**, not merely some positive density. It can also be obtained directly from the large-prime tail. The order of limits is R fixed, N→∞, then R→∞.

For every bounded sequence a(n), the weighted averages with b and b_R differ by at most ||a||∞ times the displayed L1 error in the limit superior. The same estimate holds in a Banach space when each a(n) has uniformly bounded norm. No pointwise bound on the discarded large-prime inputs is needed.

## Irrational polynomial phases: independent proof

Fix a vector of real polynomials P=(P₁,…,P_m). Assume every nonzero integer combination v·P has at least one irrational nonconstant coefficient. For every fixed arithmetic progression n=Mh+a, that combination still has an irrational nonconstant coefficient: its highest irrational coefficient is multiplied by an integer power of M, while all higher coefficients contributing to that degree are rational. Weyl's polynomial equidistribution theorem therefore applies on every fixed progression.

For a nonzero v, expand b_R into its finite residue classes modulo M_R. Weyl gives

lim_N (1/N)Σ_{n≤N}b_R(n)e(v·P(n))=0.

The L1 approximation gives the same limit with b. The zero Fourier mode tends to c. Trigonometric approximation and the Weyl criterion consequently yield, for every Jordan measurable B⊆T^m,

# {n≤N: b(n)=1 and P(n) mod1∈B}=c·Leb(B)·N+o(N).

In particular, squarefree values of n^4+1 or n^4+2 can coexist in the predicted density with any fixed irrational polynomial Bohr condition. The argument supplies no uniformity in growing phase degree, frequency, number of polynomials, or shrinking B. Rational phase components retain their CRT obstructions and must be included in the finite local calculation.

## A sharp recurrence criterion

For a single fixed polynomial f in the release's admissible range, put R_f={n≥1:f(n) is k-power-free}. Assume its exact density theorem, including for every fixed substitution f(ut), u≥1. Irreducibility is preserved by this affine substitution; for a separable family, factor degrees and separability are preserved as well.

Then R_f is divisible, meaning d(R_f∩uN)>0 for every fixed u≥1, **if and only if f(0) is k-power-free**.

Necessity: if p^k divides f(0), every n divisible by p^k has f(n)≡f(0)≡0 mod p^k. Hence R_f∩p^kN is empty. This includes f(0)=0, which is excluded by the power-free convention.

Sufficiency: if f(0) is k-power-free, then for every u and p, the value f(u·0)=f(0) shows that p^k cannot divide every value of f(ut). The substituted polynomial is locally admissible. Its exact positive density gives

d(R_f∩uN)=(1/u)c_{f(u·),k}>0.

For a simultaneous family, the same argument gives divisibility exactly when **every** F_i(0) is k-power-free; local conditions are imposed jointly, as in the manuscript's Corollary1.3.

Bergelson–Kułaga-Przymus–Lemańczyk–Richter, arXiv:1611.08392v2, Theorem1.4 (PDF p4; version9 February2018), states that a rational positive-density set is an averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence exactly when it is divisible. Thus, conditional on family020, this gives an explicit necessary and sufficient arithmetic criterion for R_f to be a polynomial multiple-recurrence averaging set.

Concretely, f(n)=n^4+1 and f(n)=n^4+2 satisfy it. Given an invertible probability-preserving system (X,μ,T), a measurable A with μ(A)>0, and fixed integer-valued rational polynomials q_i with q_i(0)=0,

lim_N (1/|R_f∩[1,N]|) Σ_{n≤N,n∈R_f} μ(A∩T^(−q₁(n))A∩…∩T^(−q_ℓ(n))A)>0.

The theorem also transfers to polynomial configurations in positive-upper-density subsets of N, via its stated combinatorial consequence. This opens squarefree-value restrictions on recurrence parameters. It does not claim prime parameters or a new multiple-recurrence theorem.

An obstruction example is f(n)=n^4+12, Eisenstein at3 and locally squarefree-admissible (the value at0 handles every odd prime, and the value at1 handles2). The proposed density theorem applies, but f(0)=12 is not squarefree, and every even n has a value divisible by4. Its positive-density squarefree set cannot average even the recurrence test given by rotation on two points. Positive density alone is insufficient.

For general bounded functions in polynomial multiple ergodic averages, RAP gives L2 convergence by Proposition2.3 of the same prior paper (PDF pp7–8). The passage from b_R to b has norm error at most Π_i||f_i||∞·(c_R−c). Positivity needs the divisibility argument above; it does not follow merely from this norm approximation.

## Representation and computational limits

Outside finitely many primes depending on discriminants, leading coefficients, contents, and resultants, #Ω_p is at most the fixed sum D of factor degrees. Consequently c_R−c=O_F,k(R^(1−k)); the finitely many exceptional primes must already be included. This is an asymptotic Cesaro error bound, not an effective finite-prefix threshold in N.

The literal period has log M_R∼kR, so an expanded period table costs exp(Θ(R)) entries. A factored representation by forbidden roots costs O(DR) bits, plus the fixed polynomial data, because Σ_{p≤R}log p=O(R). Computing prime lists and local roots, and testing every local condition on a new input, are additional charged costs. For fixed degree one can use exhaustive finite-field root search followed by Hensel lifting at the good primes; this is executable but not a free oracle. Nothing here establishes a short exact predictor for all large n or an effective recurrence search time.
