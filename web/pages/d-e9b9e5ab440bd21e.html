 A=a_1^dagger ... a_l^dagger|0>, B=b_1^dagger ... b_l^dagger|0>,

with a fixed canonical ordering and the overall determinant signs absorbed in the definition of A/B. Define p_A=<A|rho_local|A>, p_B=<B|rho_local|B>, t=p_A+p_B, q=1-t, c=<A|rho_local|B>. For spectators these are local corner probabilities and coherence, not one particular global matrix element. ## Theorem and complete proof

For l>=2, every convex mixture of Slaters satisfies

 |c| <= min(t/2, (1-t)/(2^l-2)).                         (1)

The second branch is the substantive convex-Slater witness; the first is density-matrix positivity. In particular |c|<=2^-l, but the population-dependent inequality is stronger near the cat subspace. A fixed phase phi gives the linear witness

 W_phi=(I-|A><A|-|B><B|)/(2^l-2)
       -(exp(i phi)|B><A|+exp(-i phi)|A><B|)/2.

It is nonnegative on the entire null. Measuring both quadratures permits a valid data-dependent modulus test with the simultaneous confidence region below; this is not the invalid affine-cumulant argument.

First take a pure l-particle Slater on precisely the selected 2l modes. Its occupied frame U is a 2l×l isometry. Split its rows into A and B blocks U_A,U_B. Put L=U_A^dagger U_A. Then U_B^dagger U_B=I-L. If its eigenvalues are lambda_i in[0,1], determinant amplitudes give

 p_A=prod_i lambda_i, p_B=prod_i(1-lambda_i),
 |c|=sqrt(p_A p_B).

Expand 1=prod_i[lambda_i+(1-lambda_i)] as a sum over all subsets S. The two extreme terms are p_A and p_B. Each of the 2^l-2 proper subset terms w_S has w_S w_(S^c)=p_A p_B. Pairing proper subsets with complements and applying AM–GM gives

 1-p_A-p_B = sum_proper S w_S >= (2^l-2)sqrt(p_A p_B).

The proof includes lambda_i=0/1 by continuity (or directly). Pure Slaters in a different local number sector have p_A=p_B=c=0 and satisfy (1). For a convex mixture, t and c are affine; apply the pure inequality componentwise and the triangle inequality. Positivity separately gives |c|<=sqrt(p_Ap_B)<=t/2.

For a global Slater with spectator modes, restriction to the selected modes is a gauge-invariant quasifree state. Its local one-body contraction C diagonalizes by a one-body unitary; the local state is the classical mixture of local occupation Slaters with independent eigenmode occupations of probabilities nu_j. One elementary proof is Wick uniqueness on the finite CAR algebra: the mixture has the same determinant moments as the restricted global Slater and hence the same density operator. Thus it is a convex mixture of local Slaters across local number sectors, already covered above. Restricting a convex global ensemble preserves this property. This argument does not require spectator measurements or their hidden ensemble labels.

The null is basis independent: every global Slater in every orbital basis is included. The statistic c and the named measurement circuit depend on the chosen basis; calling the statistic itself basis invariant would be wrong.

## Sharpness, not just a loose null bound

The exact projection of the null onto the two scalar observables (t,|c|) is

 0<=t<=1, 0<=|c|<=min(t/2,(1-t)/(2^l-2)).              (2)

This statement does not assert feasibility for every separately specified p_A,p_B.

Write t_0=2^(1-l). A balanced Slater with occupied orbitals (a_i+b_i)/sqrt(2) has t=t_0 and |c|=2^-l, saturating both branches. For t>=t_0, mix it with the 50/50 incoherent A/B mixture using weight w=(1-t)/(1-t_0). The resulting t is the desired value and |c|=(1-t)/(2^l-2). For 0<=t<=t_0, take occupied principal angles lambda=(x,1-x,1/2,...,1/2), where x(1-x)=t 2^(l-3). This is solvable because the right side is <=1/4. It yields p_A=p_B=t/2, |c|=t/2. Randomizing the relative phase lowers |c| continuously without changing t. Therefore every point in (2) is attainable. No test using only these two scalars can improve this null envelope.

At q=0, nonzero c rules out every Slater mixture. A dephased cat rho_eta with populations1/2 and c=eta/2 is detected for every eta>0 at infinite precision. It is not confused with the incoherent mixture eta=0, despite their identical lower RDMs for l>=3.

## Explicit finite measurement protocol

Order modes pairwise so each pair's four local Fock states are 0,a,b,ab. On the single-particle subspace implement

 U_theta=1/sqrt(2) [[1,exp(-i theta)],[-exp(i theta),1]].

The vacuum/double-occupied states are unchanged, since det U_theta=1. Let R_theta be the second-quantized product over all pairs. After applying it, read the full selected occupation string, compute parity y=(-1)^(number in A), and multiply by the **unrenormalized** indicator z=1[number on selected2l modes=l]. Record yz in{-1,0,1}. This does not discard or divide by the accepted-sector probability.

For a single occupied pair, U_theta^dagger diag(-1,1) U_theta has off-diagonal entries -exp(±i theta). Empty/double pairs contribute phase-independent factors. Within local number l, there are m singly occupied pairs and D doubles with m+2D=l; therefore parity signal f(theta)=E(yz) has harmonics -l,-l+2,...,l. Its unique highest-harmonic term is

 (-1)^l[c exp(i l theta)+c^* exp(-i l theta)].

Consequently for theta_j=pi j/(l+1), j=0,...,l,

 c=(-1)^l/(l+1) sum_j exp(-i l theta_j) f(theta_j).     (3)

All lower harmonics cancel because their difference from l is a nonzero even integer of magnitude<=2l. The half-circle grid is essential: a naive l+1-point full-circle grid aliases harmonics when l is odd. Without the sector indicator, use 2l+1 uniformly spaced phases over the full circle; this also works and costs more settings. One additional unrotated occupation setting gives p_A,p_B and q. Thus l+2 settings suffice under this interface. A random uniform phase choice per copy estimates the same discrete coefficient without assigning a fixed number of copies to every setting.

Per measured copy: l specified mixers (parallel depth1 if the hardware supports them), 2l resolved occupations, O(l) classical parity/sector work. The unrotated population sample has no mixers. A general materials spectroscopy experiment need not support these controls; the theorem supplies no free experimental access to arbitrary orbitals.

## Finite-copy certificate and systematic costs

For each phase sample, v=(-1)^l exp(-i l theta_j)yz has |v|<=1 and E v=c. With n independent phase samples and m independent population samples, Hoeffding plus a union bound gives, simultaneously with probability>=1-delta,

 |c_hat-c|<=epsilon_c=sqrt(4 log(8/delta)/n),
 |q_hat-q|<=epsilon_q=sqrt(log(4/delta)/(2m)).

A sound rejection is

 max(0,|c_hat|-epsilon_c) > min(1,q_hat+epsilon_q)/(2^l-2).  (4)

The confidence statement includes choosing the phase of c from these same data. Absolute gap Delta requires O(Delta^-2 log(1/delta)) copies; exponentially small coherences require exponentially many copies. The favorable cat gap does not make arbitrary coherence estimation cheap. The l+2 settings count is not the number of copies.

If calibration establishes phase-uniform bounded expectation bias beta_c and population bias beta_q, add them to the respective errors in (4). A common phase error magnitude h gives at most l h parity expectation bias: the phase generator has norm l/2, so the conjugated observable derivative has norm<=l. If each lifted pair mixer differs in operator norm by nu, a telescoping product gives at most2l nu observable bias. Detector errors require their own calibrated bound; they cannot be assumed absent. For an encoded sector with independent parity-bit errors e, contrast decays as (1-2e)^l, and correcting this known attenuation can inflate copy variance exponentially. This is an obstruction to scaling, not a free error-mitigation step. All statements assume i.i.d. copies; adversarial drift needs a different protocol.

## Coherence-order lower bound and depth limitation

For the two complementary l-particle determinants, every number-conserving monomial of body order r<l has zero A–B matrix element: it can replace at most r occupied orbitals. Thus rho_eta and rho_0 have the same entire rRDM for r<l. Any POVM whose effects lie in the linear span of such lower-order monomials has identical probabilities on these states. Known one-body unitaries preserve that span, so arbitrary orbital rotations before **lower-correlation-order** measurements do not repair the blindness. Adaptive choices based only on such probabilities cannot help, by induction on the classical transcript.

This is a correlation-order lower bound, not a statement that high-order Hamiltonian gates are necessary. Simultaneous single-mode detectors followed by parity retain an l-body correlation and therefore escape it. Full occupation-string POVMs are not lower-order POVMs. Likewise interacting time evolution can increase body order and is not covered. Corner populations outside a promised fixed local-l sector can involve up to2l occupation factors; the protocol explicitly reads them.

A high Fourier harmonic alone is not an entanglement-depth certificate: the balanced product Slater has a nonzero l-th harmonic, c=2^-l, for every l. Inequality(1) safely certifies failure of the convex-Slater null, hence particle depth at least2 under the source definition. It does **not** prove depth l, a k>1 threshold, or mode-GME for arbitrary partitions. Standard encoded-qubit separability inequalities cannot silently be applied to basis-independent fermionic k-producibility. A robust k>1 extension remains a load-bearing open gap.
