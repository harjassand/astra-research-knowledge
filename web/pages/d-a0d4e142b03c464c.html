# Exact simplex covers by a two-point dictionary

Date: 2026-10-06. Worker: r06_combinatorics.
Status: complete elementary proof reconstructed here; independently reconstructed audit by r09_algebra passed, including fractional steps; novelty unverified. This is a scoped combinatorial result, not a breakthrough claim. The audit is an additional model reconstruction and exact computation, not external validation.

## Interface and statement

Let `S={s_1,...,s_n}` be a basis of `R^n`, `n>=2`, and `k>=1` an integer. Repetitions are allowed in

`kS={sum c_i s_i : c_i nonnegative integers, sum c_i=k}`.

The sets `A,B` may contain arbitrary real vectors; they need not lie in the nonnegative cone, the lattice generated by S, or kS. Among all finite A,B with `|A|=2` and `kS subset A+B`, the minimum of |B| is

`Q(n,k,2)=[z^k] 1/((1-z)^(n-1)(1-z^2))`

`=sum_{j=0}^{floor(k/2)} binom(n+k-2j-2,k-2j)`.

More generally, restricting A to a translate of a nonconstant r-term arithmetic progression (`r>=2`), the exact minimum is

`Q(n,k,r)=[z^k] 1/((1-z)^(n-1)(1-z^r))`

`=sum_{j=0}^{floor(k/r)} binom(n+k-rj-2,k-rj)`.

Only the r=2 statement treats **all** A of cardinality r. The progression statement does not characterize optimal unrestricted dictionaries with r>=3.

## Proof

Apply the invertible linear map taking S to the standard basis. Translate A by minus its first element and B by the opposite translation. First take `A={0,delta}`. Write `T=T_{n,k}={x in Z^n_{>=0}:sum x_i=k}`, and `N_n(u)=binom(n+u-1,u)` for `u>=0`, zero otherwise.

Each element b of B covers at most two targets, b and b+delta. If no two targets differ by delta then |B|>=|T|. In particular this holds unless delta is a nonzero integer vector of coordinate sum zero. This case cannot improve on the claimed bound, which is at most |T|.

For integer zero-sum delta, put `L=sum_i max(delta_i,0)=sum_i max(-delta_i,0)>=1`. The graph with vertex set T and edges `{x,x+delta}` is a disjoint union of finite paths. It has no cycles because moving along delta is strictly monotone under a linear functional nonzero on delta. A component with ell vertices can be covered by exactly `ceil(ell/2)` choices of b: use alternate vertices along its path. Conversely any b covers at most two consecutive vertices in a single component, so at least that many choices are necessary. Choices b outside T cannot improve this bound.

Let `I_j` count target x for which `x,x+delta,...,x+j delta` all belong to T. For positive delta coordinates the first vertex imposes the strongest nonnegativity constraint; for negative coordinates the last vertex does. Therefore `x_i>=j max(-delta_i,0)`, and translation by `j delta_-` yields

`I_j=N_n(k-jL)`.

A path with ell vertices contributes `max(ell-j,0)` to I_j. Its maximum matching size is

`floor(ell/2)=sum_{j>=1}(-1)^(j+1) max(ell-j,0)`.

Consequently the exact minimum for this fixed delta is

`Q_L(n,k,2)=sum_{j>=0}(-1)^j N_n(k-jL)`

`=[z^k](1-z)^(-n)/(1+z^L)`.

It remains to show this is minimized at L=1. For n=2, T is an interval with k+1 points. Every matching on any graph with k+1 vertices has size at most `floor((k+1)/2)`. The unit-step path attains this bound, so `Q_L(2,k,2)>=Q_1(2,k,2)` for **every** k. Equivalently the formal power series

`F_L(z)=(1-z)^(-2)(1/(1+z^L)-1/(1+z))`

has nonnegative coefficients. Multiplication by `(1-z)^(-(n-2))`, whose coefficients are nonnegative, gives the same inequality for every n>=2. For n=2 this factor is 1. Thus all delta give at least Q_1. Choosing `delta=e_1-e_2` and alternating vertices in each path achieves Q_1. Finally

`(1-z)^(-n)/(1+z)=(1-z)^(-(n-1))/(1-z^2)`.

This proves the unrestricted two-point theorem.

### Progression extension, including fractional real steps

For a nonzero integer zero-sum step Delta and a dictionary of h consecutive multiples of Delta, a path of ell vertices requires exactly `ceil(ell/h)` dictionary elements. Each chosen element covers at most h consecutive path vertices, and blocks of h achieve this. Since

`ceil(ell/h)=sum_{j>=0}(max(ell-jh,0)-max(ell-jh-1,0))`,

the exact value is

`Q_L(n,k,h)=sum_{j>=0}(N_n(k-jhL)-N_n(k-(jh+1)L))`

`=[z^k](1-z)^(-n)(1-z^L)/(1-z^(hL))`.

In dimension two, the paths partition the interval of k+1 points, and the sum of their ceiling counts is at least `ceil((k+1)/h)`. The unit-step path achieves this. The same formal-series convolution used above gives `Q_L(n,k,h)>=Q_1(n,k,h)` for all n>=2.

For a general real step delta, take `A={0,delta,...,(r-1)delta}`. If no positive integer j<=r-1 has j delta integral of sum zero, each b covers at most one target. Otherwise all coordinates of delta are rational and sum zero. Let q be the smallest positive integer with `q delta in Z^n`; q divides every j with j delta integral. Put `Delta=q delta`, positive mass L. For a fixed b, the indices j for which `b+j delta` is a target all lie in one residue class modulo q. There are at most `h=ceil(r/q)` such indices, and their targets lie consecutively on a Delta-path. Thus |B|>=Q_L(n,k,h)>=Q_1(n,k,h)>=Q_1(n,k,r), where the last inequality follows directly from allowing larger covering blocks. A unit integral step achieves Q_1(n,k,r), proving the progression statement. In fact for a fixed fractional step the bound Q_L(n,k,h) is attained using the longest index residue class, with suitable translation of b.

## Relation to source and prior art

Xu, arXiv:2605.10771v1 (11 May 2026), Theorem 1.1 proves the complete asymmetric two-fold cover theorem: `S+S subset A+B`, `|A|<=n-t` implies `|B|>=n+binom(t+1,2)`. Its p=2 slice equals `binom(n,2)+1`, exactly the k=2 specialization above. Xu does not state the k>2, p=2 theorem in the five-page source read in full.

Bukh, van Hintum and Keevash, arXiv:2409.07442v1 (11 September 2024), Section 2 gives a vector model and a coordinate-complement argument. Their higher-order domain-change model has k residue dictionaries and carry offsets, not just an arbitrary two-dictionary cover of kS. Therefore the result above does **not** settle their higher-order domain-change problem.

Targeted searches for two-element additive dictionary covers and higher-order vector models located no exact formula above. This is not an exhaustive prior-art check. The path-cover calculation is elementary and may well be known as a consequence of covering a discrete simplex by translates of a short segment. Novelty remains unverified.

## Decisive obstruction to unrestricted r-point extrapolation

For n=4,k=2,r=3, the progression minimum is 6. However Xu's sharp construction gives unrestricted |A|=3,|B|=5: `A={e1,e2,e3}` and `B={e1,e2,e3,e4,2e4-e1}`. Every target e_i+e_j is covered, so the progression formula cannot extend to arbitrary r-element A. The independent-directions geometry already helps at r=3.

## Consequence and next falsifiable step

This supplies an exact small-dictionary baseline and lower-bound certificate for compressing a complete degree-k independent feature dictionary into two additive lookups. The assumptions require exact sums and truly independent generators; this says nothing by itself about approximate learned representations or general model compression.
