ADVERSARIAL FREE-RADIUS AUDIT — F_64 x F_64, K=256
2026-10-08

Verdict and scope

For the exact regular-representation score

  Gamma_(2,64) = sup { || sum_(s,t in S) a_st lambda(s^-1 t) || :
                    A=A*, Tr A=0, ||A||_HS=1 },

with S=S_1 disjoint union S_2, |S_j|=2d=128 and K=256, I found no
counterexample to C^2=50. The upper-bound sector proof matches this
interface. An explicit family of admissible coefficient matrices gives
Gamma_(2,64)^2 >= 3,203,583/65,024 = 49.26770115649607..., so 50 is a
near-sharp upper bound, not the exact radius claim. Exact maximization at
this finite d remains open in this audit.

Sector upper bound, reconstructed

The diagonal terms sum to (Tr A)I=0. For j=1,2, define x_j^2 as the sum
of |a_st|^2 over s,t in S_j, s!=t; define y^2 over the two ordered
cross-factor blocks (S_1 x S_2) union (S_2 x S_1). The sectors are disjoint,
so x_1^2+x_2^2+y^2<=1.

For a same-factor pair s!=t, s^-1 t is a reduced length-two word; its first
letter recovers s and its second recovers t. The map is injective, so the
collected coefficient l2 norm is x_j. Tensorized Haagerup at multidegree
(2,0) or (0,2) contributes 2+1=3, giving ||Q_j||<=3x_j.

For a cross-factor relative operation, commutation identifies exactly the
two preimages (s,t) and (t^-1,s^-1). Unique coordinates in F_64 x F_64
rule out further preimages. Its collected coefficient is
c_g=a_st+a_(t^-1,s^-1), so summing |z+w|^2<=2(|z|^2+|w|^2) over disjoint
fibres gives ||c||_2<=sqrt(2)y. Its multidegree is (1,1), whose Haagerup
factor is (1+1)(1+1)=4; hence ||Q_12||<=4sqrt(2)y.

Therefore

  ||P_A|| <= 3x_1+3x_2+4sqrt(2)y
           <= sqrt(9+9+32) sqrt(x_1^2+x_2^2+y^2)
           <= sqrt(50).

The constants check as stated: 3 is the same-factor length-two factor;
4 is the mixed multidegree factor; sqrt(2) is only the two-preimage
coefficient-collection loss; and C^2=2*3^2+(4sqrt(2))^2=50. Hermiticity
is compatible with these bounds, and the argument in fact bounds the
collected polynomial for arbitrary coefficients with the same sector
norms. Nonzero diagonal entries do not cause a problem: they cancel by
Tr A=0 and consume, rather than add to, the available HS norm.

Adversarial lower witness / sector interaction

The three sector bounds can be approached together, so a proposed objection
that triangle/Cauchy loses alignment is not borne out. Put k=128 and
L_j=sum_(s in S_j)lambda(s), a self-adjoint adjacency operator on the
jth free-group factor. Define coefficient matrices E_j and F_12 as follows:
E_j has value 1/sqrt(k(k-1)) on off-diagonal pairs in S_j x S_j; F_12 has
value 1/(sqrt(2)k) on both cross blocks; all other entries are zero. They
are real symmetric, traceless, HS-orthonormal, with disjoint supports. Direct
expansion gives

  Q(E_j)=(L_j^2-kI)/sqrt(k(k-1)),
  Q(F_12)=sqrt(2)L_1 L_2/k.

The k-regular tree adjacency norm is t=2sqrt(k-1). Finitely supported
radial shell vectors xi_n can be chosen with
||(L-tI)xi_n||^2=(4k-3)/n -> 0. On the same product vectors xi_n tensor xi_n,
the E_j sector expectations tend to

  alpha=(t^2-k)/sqrt(k(k-1))=(3k-4)/sqrt(k(k-1)),

and the cross expectation tends to

  beta=sqrt(2)t^2/k=4sqrt(2)(1-1/k).

Thus the explicit admissible matrix

  A0=(alpha(E_1+E_2)+beta F_12)/sqrt(2alpha^2+beta^2)

has Tr A0=0, ||A0||_HS=1, and its operator norm is at least the limiting
product-vector Rayleigh quotient sqrt(2alpha^2+beta^2). At k=128 this
squares to exactly

  2*(380)^2/(128*127) + 32*(127/128)^2
    = 3,203,583/65,024 = 49.26770115649607....

Hence the remaining upper/lower gap in Gamma^2 is at most
47617/65024=0.73229884350394. Numerically sqrt(lower)=7.01909546569,
versus sqrt(50)=7.07106781187. These are exact symbolic witness formulas
plus decimal evaluations, not a finite-matrix approximation. They are
consistent with the source's stronger general estimate
0<=16r^2-7r-Gamma_(r,d)^2<=16r^2/d, which gives gap<=1 here.

Falsifier search and status

The strongest possible failure points were (i) an overlooked extra
cross-factor fibre, (ii) a failure of same-factor injectivity at inverse
letters, or (iii) inability to align the sector norms on a common vector.
Unique reduced words and unique direct-product coordinates settle (i)-(ii);
the product radial witness settles (iii) in the near-saturating direction.
No explicit A violating 50 was found. Exact finite-d maximization is not
proved. Status: the interface match and sector algebra are
INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE conditional on the cited tensorized Haagerup
inequality; that inequality is SOURCE-REPORTED here, not reproved from
first principles. The source's Proposition 5.1 states the upper bound and
Theorem 5.2 gives the lower witness/asymptotic saturation. The cycle4 use of
C^2=50 is therefore a correct source-conditional upper-bound premise for
this abstract free regular radius. It is not by itself a finite-U(N) score
transfer or a complete entropy-gap proof.

Cost: analytic sector reconstruction, primary-source check, and exact
rational evaluation only; no heavy computation, full regular-representation
matrix, random sample, or finite seed. The radial test vectors have finite
support for every n, but the limiting lower-bound statement uses n->infinity.
