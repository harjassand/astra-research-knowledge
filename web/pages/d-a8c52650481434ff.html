# Passive finite-state learning: a rare-context barrier

**Verdict.** Without a lower bound on state/context mass (or an active mechanism that can force the context), there is no uniform finite-sample guarantee for worst-case conditional prediction, even for stationary two-state HMMs with a known state count, full-rank emissions, full-rank transitions, and a constant hidden-chain spectral gap. Adaptive choice of observables after seeing the same passive data cannot repair a transcript that never contains the informative context. This is an information limit shared by spectral, Baum–Welch/EM, and every other estimator given the same observations.

This does not obstruct stationary-average prediction or fixed-horizon total-variation approximation: the difficult context can have arbitrarily small probability. The error criterion and the conditioning/margin promise therefore have to be named before claiming “representation acquisition.”

## Interface and theorem

The learner knows the alphabet \(\{0,1\}\), state count 2, and the following model family, but not which value of \(\theta\) is used. It receives at most \(B\) symbols from one or more independent stationary reset episodes. It may compute any statistics, select any bounded trajectory observables adaptively from the data, and randomize its output. A reset starts the hidden chain in its stationary law; all symbols from all episodes count toward \(B\). No action changes the hidden transition law, and there is no separate conditional-probability oracle.

For \(0<\varepsilon\le 1/8\), define two transition matrices, with rows indexed by the current state \(A,B\), by

\[
T_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}1-\varepsilon&\varepsilon\\1-\theta&\theta\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \theta\in\{1/4,3/4\}.
\]

The emission is deterministic, \(Y_t=0\) in state \(A\) and \(Y_t=1\) in state \(B\). Initialize at stationarity, so

\[
\pi_\theta(B)=\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\theta+\varepsilon},\qquad
\pi_\theta(A)=1-\pi_\theta(B).
\]

The two targets have identical \(A\to B\) transition probability \(\varepsilon\), but their conditional predictions at the informative context differ:

\[
\Pr_\theta(Y_{t+1}=1\mid Y_t=1)=\theta,
\qquad |3/4-1/4|=1/2.
\]

**Theorem (uniform passive conditional recovery fails).** For any estimator \(\widehat\theta\) based on at most \(B\) observed symbols, including one that chooses its trajectory observables adaptively after inspecting those symbols,

\[
\max_{\theta\in\{1/4,3/4\}}
\mathbb E_\theta\left|\widehat\theta-\theta\right|
\;\ge\;\frac{1-4B\varepsilon}{4}
\quad\text{whenever }4B\varepsilon\le 1.
\]

Thus for every proposed uniform sample budget \(B\), choosing \(\varepsilon=1/(100B)\) leaves worst-case expected absolute error at least \(0.24\). No finite sample bound uniform over this model class can guarantee conditional error below (say) \(0.2\). The conclusion remains true if \(\varepsilon\) is known to the learner.

**Proof.** Since the chain is stationary, every observed position has probability \(\pi_\theta(B)\) of being 1. For both values of \(\theta\), \(\pi_\theta(B)\le 4\varepsilon\). A union bound over the \(B\) observed symbols gives

\[
q_\theta:=\Pr_\theta(D=0^B)\ge 1-4B\varepsilon.
\]

Conditional on the all-zero transcript \(D=0^B\), the estimator's output law is the same under both models: its input data are identical, and any internal randomization is target-independent. Let \(Q\) denote that common output law. Its two conditional losses satisfy the triangle inequality:

\[
\mathbb E_{Q}|\widehat\theta-1/4|
+\mathbb E_{Q}|\widehat\theta-3/4|\ge 1/2.
\]

Writing \(q=1-4B\varepsilon\), each model's total expected loss is at least \(q\) times its loss on this transcript. Hence their maximum is at least

\[
\frac{q}{2}\left(\mathbb E_Q|\widehat\theta-1/4|
+\mathbb E_Q|\widehat\theta-3/4|\right)\ge q/4.
\]

This proof allows arbitrary post-hoc computations and adaptive test selection: on the all-zero transcript every selected feature and every computed response is the same. It also covers any method that uses newly sampled passive trajectories, by counting their symbols in \(B\). A test oracle that returns a conditional probability without drawing the rare conditioning event, or an intervention that drives the chain into \(B\), is a strictly richer information interface.

## Regularity and conditioning check

Both models are ordinary two-state HMMs. Their emission matrix is \(O=I_2\), so \(\sigma_{\min}(O)=1\). The determinant of \(T_\theta\) is \(\theta-\varepsilon\), hence the transition matrix has rank 2. Both stationary initial probabilities are positive. The nontrivial eigenvalue is \(\theta-\varepsilon\), so the absolute spectral gap is \(1-|\theta-\varepsilon|\ge 1/4\) for \(\varepsilon\le1/8\). The example therefore keeps state count fixed, emissions perfectly separating, and hidden-chain relaxation bounded away from zero while the stationary mass of state \(B\) tends to zero.

The pair-probability matrix used by standard spectral HMM learning is nevertheless ill-conditioned. With rows for \(Y_2\) and columns for \(Y_1\),

\[
P_{2,1}=\begin{pmatrix}
\pi_A(1-\varepsilon)&\pi_B(1-\theta)\\
\pi_A\varepsilon&\pi_B\theta
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad |\det(P_{2,1})|=\pi_A\pi_B|\theta-\varepsilon|.
\]

Its largest singular value tends to 1 and its smallest is \(\Theta(\varepsilon)\). Thus this example is consistent with Hsu–Kakade–Zhang's theorem: their spectral guarantee assumes positive initial mass and full-rank transition/emission matrices, and its sample complexity depends polynomially on inverse singular values of \(O\) and \(P_{2,1}\), including a fourth power for \(\sigma_2(P_{2,1})^{-1}\). The theorem does not promise margin-free conditional recovery. Their separate all-time conditional-KL result averages over histories and adds a strict positivity condition on symbol operators; our deterministic-emission family does not satisfy that additional condition.

## Objective boundary

The theorem concerns the worst-case conditional target after observing \(Y_t=1\), not the stationary-average score. The informative context has probability \(\pi_B=\Theta(\varepsilon)\); its contribution to stationary-average one-step loss can therefore be \(O(\varepsilon)\), even when its conditional prediction is wrong by a constant. Likewise, for any fixed data horizon \(B\), both output strings equal \(0^B\) with probability at least \(1-4B\varepsilon\). Their length-\(B\) laws therefore share at least that much mass on the all-zero string, so their total-variation distance is at most \(4B\varepsilon\). An average-risk guarantee may correctly ignore that context. It cannot be re-labelled as a guarantee for the conditional law on every history.

This is also distinct from the parity-clock/fixed-dictionary conditioning obstruction in the prior workspace. That obstruction rules out a uniformly stable fixed polynomial bank while explicitly leaving open arbitrary computation on raw paths. The rare-context argument is an information-theoretic lower bound for the stated passive, worst-case conditional objective, so arbitrary raw-data feature discovery cannot escape it without additional data, a mass/margin promise, or active control.

## Equal-information comparison

The comparison budget is the same \(B\) raw symbols, with the same reset convention and no intervention. The lower bound is estimator-agnostic, so it applies to the following methods under that budget:

* **Spectral HMM / observable-operator methods.** Hsu, Kakade and Zhang estimate one-, two-, and three-observation tables from reset samples and apply an SVD. Their Conditions 1–2 and Theorem 6 give finite-horizon guarantees with explicit dependence on \(\sigma_m(O)\) and \(\sigma_m(P_{2,1})\). Our family satisfies their rank condition but has \(\sigma_2(P_{2,1})=\Theta(\varepsilon)\), exactly the missing margin. The construction exposes why an adaptive choice of features from the same data cannot replace a coverage or singular-value condition.
* **Baum–Welch / EM.** Hsu–Kakade–Zhang describe EM-style hidden-state imputation and parameter maximization as heuristics with local-optimum and initialization issues. Regardless of optimization quality or initialization, an estimator receiving only the same all-zero data has the lower bound above. This says nothing against EM's practical performance on well-covered models or average prediction objectives.
* **System identification.** Passive HMM data have no chosen input. Controlled system-identification guarantees add an input-generation/controllability interface and quantitative excitation or stability assumptions. For example, Sattar et al. analyze partially observed bilinear systems with i.i.d. isotropic inputs, uniform stability under the input set, and persistence of excitation; their learned history features have dimension growing exponentially in history length. Such input information may escape this example if it can reliably move or excite the hidden state, but it is not information contained in the same passive output sample. A fair comparison must charge the input protocol and any reset/actuation budget.

The passively observed two-state example is already fully observable from its emissions; what is missing is *visitation*, not a better representation basis. In a richer active interface, a positive acquisition theorem should name an excitation/coverage condition and charge the number and cost of actions that establish it.

## Relation to existing finite-state acquisition results

The prior workspace contains a stronger growing-state passive lower bound for exact model acquisition: a fixed-rational, irreducible and aperiodic binary \(m\)-state family has hidden spectral gap bounded below by a positive constant, while the only distinguishing emission occurs with probability at most \(2^{-(m-1)}\) per observed time. It implies \(\Omega(2^m)\) passive symbols for exact identification; a full-rank-emission variant uses a larger alphabet. See `/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-07/you-have-access-to-over-100-5/work/algebra_acquisition/prior/ACQUISITION_BOUNDARY.md`, Section 4. That result strengthens state-complexity scaling; the present two-state theorem isolates the conditional-loss and missing-context-margin issue in the smallest model and directly meets the usual HMM rank assumptions.

The algebraic universal-moment construction in the same workspace gives a fixed polynomial-size bank that is population-complete for every bounded-rank process, but its robust statistical theorem takes a positive lower bound on the smallest nonzero Hankel singular value as input. The parity-clock example proves that this conditioning cannot be removed for every universal bounded fixed dictionary. Neither result contradicts the lower bound here: exact population rank and finite-sample, worst-history conditional accuracy are different guarantees.

## Evidence status and next gate

The two-state lower bound above is an internally reconstructed theorem with a complete proof in the stated interface. The construction is elementary; no novelty or priority claim is made. Literature statements are source-reported from the primary papers identified above. No experiment or external proof review was performed or needed for the elementary bound.

The decisive next positive theorem, if pursuing acquisition, needs at least one of: (i) a uniform lower bound on relevant history/state mass; (ii) a lower bound on the observable Hankel singular value plus a stability estimate for conditional normalization; or (iii) an active, costed policy whose inputs guarantee adequate excitation. Its loss must distinguish worst-history conditional accuracy from distribution-weighted risk. A same-data benchmark should feed identical reset trajectories to a spectral/SVD learner, Baum–Welch with specified initialization, and adaptive feature discovery, while scoring both unconditional finite-horizon TV and conditional error on a predeclared rare history.
