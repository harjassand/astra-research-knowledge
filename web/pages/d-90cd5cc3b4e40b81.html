## G2. Domain and convergent Gaussian representations

At fixed finite size, a linear oscillator coordinate is infinitesimally relatively bounded with respect to the free oscillator Hamiltonian. Thus (H\) is self-adjoint on its free operator domain, and its quadratic form is bounded below. Completing each oscillator square in a fixed spin-X sector gives the shift (-g_q^2\sum_\ell c_{q\ell}^2/\omega_{q\ell}=-g_q^2\chi_q/2\). Alternatively, for every (0<a<1\), the interaction is form-bounded by (aH_B+C_a\). Consequently (H\ge(1-a)H_B-C_a'\), with finite spin multiplicity, so its compact spectrum has a trace-class heat kernel at every positive inverse temperature. No finite norm of (H\) or (\phi_q\) is assumed.

A direct way to justify the representation, without treating an unbounded interaction as a bounded Dyson term, is to diagonalize the spin-X operators. Put all oscillator forces, X checks and X fields in a diagonal spin Hamiltonian (D\); the remaining Z-check spin flips form a bounded operator (B\). The ordinary bounded-perturbation Dyson expansion in (B\) is trace-norm convergent. For a time simplex (s_0+\cdots+s_m=\beta\), Schatten Hölder bounds a term by

\[
\|e^{-s_0D}B\cdots Be^{-s_mD}\|_1
 \le\|B\|^m\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta D},
\]

after harmless scalar shifts, and the simplex volume gives (\beta^m/m!\). Every spin word is a finite piecewise-constant source (x_q(s)=\pm1\) for the oscillator coordinate. A free oscillator thermal Gaussian trace, or its coordinate heat-kernel composition, gives exactly

\[
\exp\left\{\frac12\sum_qg_q^2
 \int_0^\beta\!\int_0^\beta C_q(s-u)x_q(s)x_q(u)\,ds\,du\right\},
\quad
C_q(s)=\sum_\ell c_{q\ell}^2
 \frac{\cosh[\omega_{q\ell}(\beta/2-s)]}{\sinh(\beta\omega_{q\ell}/2)}.
\]

The source has a bounded quadratic form because (|x_q|\le1\), (C_q\ge0\) and (\int_0^\beta C_q=\chi_q\). This supplies dominated convergence for its Gaussian expansion at fixed finite size. The X-basis spin flips and coordinate heat kernels are nonnegative. The Z/Fock expansion is also nonnegative: X check permutations, direct X insertions and each ladder matrix element have nonnegative coefficients with the stated signs. Its Taylor/Duhamel coefficients coincide with the Gaussian expression; a Wick majorant is

\[

 \sum_{m\ge0}\frac{[\beta^2\sum_qg_q^2v_q/2]^m}{m!}.

The factor ((2m-1)!!/(2m)!=1/(2^m m!)\) accounts for pairings and the time simplex. This extensive expression proves finite-size convergence only. It is not a practical bound, an N-uniform preparation estimate, or a free-Poisson law.
