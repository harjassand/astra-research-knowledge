# An explicit receiving contract for bounded-support proposal compilers

16 October 2026. Independent receiving-interface derivation. The mathematical statements below are conditional on the preserved completed-pair memory construction and its named LSS/HT imports. They do not establish a useful threshold, physical implementation, historical priority or correctness accepted by an external specialist.

## 1. Declare boundary boxes before assigning rates

Fine owner cells are indexed by `x in Z^4`. A proposal stream `e` has a fixed rate `lambda_e >= 0`, a fixed owner `x_e`, and a fixed finite physical-qubit patch `K_e`. All rates are independent of input states, Kraus outcomes and storage time. There are finitely many stream types per owner, uniformly in system size. For each CSS sector declare a box containing the incident syndrome vertices of every Pauli supported on `K_e`; declare one box `B_e` containing both sector boxes in their respective integer owner coordinates. Require

`B_e subset x_e + {-q,...,q}^4`                                                   (G1)

for one fixed finite integer `q`, independent of `L`. This is a boundary-reset radius, not a body count or an unspecified physical patch radius. A pair compiler must use the union of the two operator supports before forming its box. Its product operator stays within that union, but that union can be much larger in diameter than either support.

Define the total owner intensity

`nu(x) = sum_{e: x_e=x} lambda_e`, `nu_* = sup_x nu(x)`.                          (G2)

This is a sufficient intensity interface. A relabelling of a large collection as one "patch" does not change the sum.

## 2. Exact guard-incident bound

For coarse side `b`, put `C_z = bz + {0,...,b-1}^4`. The birth guard used by the forcing proof is

`G_z = union_{||w-z||_infinity <= R+2} C_w`.

It is an integer box of side `V=(2R+5)b`. The exact intensity of proposals whose reset boxes meet this guard is

`I_z = sum_e lambda_e 1{B_e intersects G_z}`.                                   (G3)

By (G1), an incident proposal has owner in `G_z + {-q,...,q}^4`. Therefore

`I_z <= sum_{x in G_z+[-q,q]^4} nu(x) <= nu_* [ (2R+5)b + 2q ]^4`.               (G4)

All boxes in this formula are integer boxes, so the displayed side count has no missing `+1`. Independent fixed-rate Poisson streams give, on a slab of length `Delta=Cb/kappa`,

`P(birth-bad at z) = 1-exp(-Delta I_z)`

`                         <= (nu_*/kappa) Cb [ (2R+5)b + 2q ]^4`.                (G5)

The equality is valid for the declared "any incident proposal" flag; it does not depend on whether the proposal actually changes a state. If stream-specific radii are useful, retain the exact sum in (G3), or use `sum_e lambda_e 1{x_e in G_z+[-q_e,q_e]^4}` rather than replacing all `q_e` by a maximum.

The bound remains valid on an injectively lifted torus guard. Counting periodic owners can only reduce the crude volume bound if the displayed box wraps, although the stopped-replay argument separately requires an injective patch.

## 3. The literal finite primitive guard survives enlargement

Choose `b>=q`. Every possible incident owner is then in a coarse cell at infinity distance at most `R+3` from `z`. Thus the birth flag reads only primitive streams in the same fixed radius-`R+3` guard as the correction-chain and certificate flags. The full slab flag depends on a fixed primitive region, independent of the actual initial configuration and exterior history.

Consequently two flag primitive supports are disjoint if their targets are separated by more than `2(R+3)`, and a flag is independent of the **joint sigma field** of all flags that far away. This verifies the neighborhood-conditional LSS hypothesis, rather than merely pairwise independence. The dependence degree is at most

`D = [4(R+3)+1]^4-1`,

independent of `b,L,T`. Fix the support catalog first; choose `C,R` and the eroder; choose HT and LSS tolerances; then choose `b>=q` large enough; finally choose the local rate budget. An error support that grows with `L` or a time-dependent expanding catalog does not satisfy this contract.

For the ticket source's conservative constants, let

`delta_service(b) = 2b^4 exp((5 log(16384)-C/2)b)`,

`delta_fast(b) = b^4 (512 Cb)^m/m!`, `m=floor(Rb/2)`.

Take `C>10 log(16384)` and `R>=4096 e C+10`. For `Rb>=4`, Stirling's elementary lower bound `m! >= (m/e)^m` and `m>=Rb/4` give `delta_fast <= b^4 2^-m`. Both terms tend to zero as `b` increases. If the LSS admissible marginal is `delta_*`, choose fixed `b>=q` so that their sum is at most `2delta_*/3`, then the explicit sufficient admission condition is

`nu_*/kappa <= delta_* / {3 Cb [ (2R+5)b + 2q ]^4}`.                            (G6)

This supplies an actual symbolic sufficient budget after the qualitative HT/LSS tolerances are chosen. It is not a numerical operational threshold; those tolerances and the coarse scales can be exceptionally poor.

## 4. Pauli coefficient compiler: exact multiplicities

For the compiler in `originals/compiler/STRUCTURED_COMPILER.md`, assign each diagonal coefficient, unordered correlated edge, and Hamiltonian coefficient an owner and its complete boundary box. The fixed proposal rates are respectively

`2 M_aa`, `4 M_ab` for `a<b`, and `2 u_p`.

Hence define

`w(x) = sum_{diagonal e owned by x} M_aa`

`       + 2 sum_{unordered edges e owned by x} M_ab`

`       + sum_{Hamiltonian e owned by x} u_p`.

Then `nu(x)=2w(x)` and (G5) becomes

`P(birth-bad) <= 2 (w_*/kappa) Cb [ (2R+5)b+2q ]^4`.                            (G7)

The factor two on unordered edges is essential: it is exactly the compiler's ordered coefficient sum. Bounds that count each edge once and then use the ordered-sum formula undercharge by two.

The diagonal floor, the two original labels of every edge, its Hermitian product label, and every Hamiltonian floor must all be physically installed. The same fixed rates apply to every admitted signed coefficient matrix, including singular endpoints. Signed differences of correction generators cannot be treated as positive coefficient matrices.

An alternative owner-free bound is possible. If each reset box is contained in the radius-`r` neighborhood of its declared support-cell set `S_e`, define `j(y)=sum_e lambda_e 1{y in S_e}`. Any incident proposal has some support cell in `G_z+[-r,r]^4`, so `I_z<=sum_{y in G_z+[-r,r]^4}j(y)`. This can be useful for irregular catalog assignments, but `r` must charge any filled box between separated supports.

## 5. General fixed known-operator dictionaries

The receiving theorem does not require fault channels, their Kraus operators, or installed floors to be Pauli. Suppose a separately proved exact local compiler gives

`F_e+E_e = sum_j lambda_{e,j}(Phi_{e,j}-Id)`,

where every `Phi_{e,j}` is CPTP on a fixed bounded union patch and the `lambda_{e,j}` are fixed before the unknown scalar error coefficients are selected. Use those actual rates and union-support boundary boxes in (G1)-(G6). This is sufficient for exactly the same completed-pair proof.

Proof: condition on correction and fault proposal clocks and locations. A composition of CPTP maps remains CPTP. Every local fault Kraus operator has a finite Pauli expansion on its union patch, regardless of the dictionary in which it was specified. The ticket event is simultaneous over **all** such Pauli histories. On a good schedule, each recovered Pauli term is scalar on the entire code; hence each recovered full Kraus operator is scalar as well. Trace preservation supplies the scalar normalization. No sum of absolute Pauli coefficients enters the probability or diamond bound. A dense Pauli expansion in this last proof step therefore does not impose a `4^q` proposal-rate penalty. It also does not supply a missing exact compiler, physical floor, support bound or input-independent schedule.

For example, if a proven known-direction compiler for `D[L+delta]-D[L]` gives rate at most `4||L|| eta+2eta^2`, with `delta=sum_j z_j A_j`, fixed supplied operators `A_j`, fixed scalar coefficient bounds and `sum_j |z_j| ||A_j||<=eta`, this receiving result uses that rate directly. The conditional conclusion is independent of how many Paulis occur when the `A_j` are expanded. A rate formula alone is insufficient: the exact CPTP decomposition, installed floor and bounded union patch remain required. This document does not independently certify the parent investigation's general unitary-pair compiler identity.

The static scope is a memoryless Lindblad generator with scalar coefficients fixed in time, possibly spatially inhomogeneous. It does not admit unknown quantum-bath operator coefficients or continuous memory-bath Hamiltonians. Deterministic time-varying channels with one fixed rate envelope can be treated by the same schedule proof as a separate time-ordered evolution statement; that extension is not silently identified with `exp(TG)` for one static `G`.

## 6. Precise resulting construction

Keep the ideal **completed pair** generator

`G_ideal = kappa sum_(v,a,sector) (C_(v,a)-Id)`.

Add the fixed implemented floors and admitted static local signed errors. If the exact proposal compilers, (G1), and (G6) hold, the preserved probability/topology argument gives

`||Dec_L exp(TG) Enc_L - Id||_diamond`

`    <= min(2, A L^4 (1+kappa T) exp(-cL))`

for a compatible family `L=bM`, sufficiently large `M`, all finite `T>=0`, all six logical qubits and arbitrary untouched references. The constants depend on the declared catalog and qualitative imports.

This statement applies to a new, explicitly regularized generator. It neither transfers the same robustness to original active-only pair jumps nor identifies it with the 24-qubit whole-vertex/depolarized construction. The original active pair theorem is restricted to its stated independent Pauli noise.
