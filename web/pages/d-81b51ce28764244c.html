# Frozen independent derivation: spectral-volume ceiling for slow compact-link ends

Frozen 2026-10-08 before receiving any independent audit derivation.
Work status: internally reconstructed; no formal replay, specialist validation, or priority clearance.

## Exact theorem

Let a smooth complete connected three-manifold have, outside a smooth compact core, one end
\[
g=dr^2+f(r)^2H(\log r),\quad r>R_0.
\]
Assume f>0 smooth, f(r)/r -> a>0; H(t) is smooth and contained in a C-infinity compact family K of metrics on one fixed connected closed surface; every H in K has the SAME POINTWISE area form mu, mu(Sigma)=4pi; and ||H_t||_C0 -> 0 in a fixed background metric. The compact core plus r<=R exhausts M. Polynomial growth in r agrees with geodesic polynomial growth since distance from a fixed point in the core is r+O(1).

For the real smooth harmonic functions of pointwise growth at most k, write h_k=dim H_k. Then h_k is finite for every k>=0 and
\[
\boxed{\limsup_{k\to\infty} h_k/k^2\le9a^2/4.}
\]
The normalized asymptotic volume ratio is a^2. Nonnegative Ricci curvature is not needed. No conclusion for arbitrary Ricci-nonnegative manifolds is asserted.

More precisely, any p independent members of H_k obey
\[
pk\ge C_p:=\inf_{H\in K}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\alpha_j(H),
\qquad
\alpha_j(H)=\frac{\sqrt{1+4\lambda_j(H)/a^2}-1}{2}. \tag{1}
\]
Eigenvalues include multiplicity and lambda_0=0. This finite-dimensional inequality is load-bearing.

## Finite collar comparison

Set t=log r, b(t)=f(e^t)/e^t -> a. The metric and end energy are
\[
g=e^{2t}(dt^2+b(t)^2H(t)),\quad
E(u;[t_1,t_2])=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}e^s
 \bigl(b(s)^2|u_s|^2+|\nabla_{H(s)}u|^2\bigr)d\mu\,ds. \tag{2}
\]
Let N_t v = partial_t u|_t for the harmonic extension into the enclosed compact domain Omega_{e^t}. Green's identity gives
\[
\langle N_tv,v\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}
=e^{-t}b(t)^{-2}E(u;\Omega_{e^t}). \tag{3}
\]
Thus N_t is nonnegative self-adjoint on the fixed Hilbert space. It is the radius-normalized Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, with compact resolvent.

Fix L>0. Discard energy inside t-L and free the inner boundary trace. This gives, for every outer trace v,
\[
\langle N_tv,v\rangle \ge
\inf_{w(0)=v}b(t)^{-2}\int_{-L}^{0}e^s
 \bigl(b(t+s)^2|w_s|^2+
 |\nabla_{H(t+s)}w|^2\bigr)d\mu\,ds. \tag{4}
\]
The free inner trace imposes the natural Neumann boundary condition. The inequality direction is correct: discarding the core and freeing the inner boundary decrease the minimal energy.

For this fixed L, b(t+s)->a uniformly. Also
\[
\sup_{-L\le s\le0}\|H(t+s)-H(t)\|_{C^0}
\le L\sup_{\tau\ge t-L}\|H_t(\tau)\|_{C^0}\to0.
\]
Uniform ellipticity from K therefore yields delta_L(t)->0 such that (4) is at least
\[
(1-\delta_L(t))\inf_{w(0)=v}
\int_{-L}^{0}e^s
 \bigl(|w_s|^2+a^{-2}|\nabla_{H(t)}w|^2\bigr)d\mu\,ds. \tag{5}
\]
This is a quadratic-form comparison for EVERY trace and spatial frequency, not a fixed-cutoff approximation.

In the frozen Laplace eigenbasis, let alpha(alpha+1)=lambda/a^2, alpha>=0. The scalar minimizer solves
\[
w''+w'-\alpha(\alpha+1)w=0,\quad w(0)=1,\quad w'(-L)=0.
\]
For alpha>0 and z=exp(-(2alpha+1)L), it is
\[
w(s)=\frac{e^{\alpha s}+
 [\alpha/(\alpha+1)]z e^{-(\alpha+1)s}}
 {1+[\alpha/(\alpha+1)]z}.
\]
Integration by parts makes the minimum energy the derivative at zero:
\[
q_L(\alpha)=w'(0)=
 \alpha\frac{1-e^{-(2\alpha+1)L}}
 {1+[\alpha/(\alpha+1)]e^{-(2\alpha+1)L}}. \tag{6}
\]
For alpha=0, w=1 and q_L(0)=0. The multiplier increases with lambda by its variational characterization. Direct algebra gives
\[
0\le\alpha-q_L(\alpha)\le
2\alpha e^{-(2\alpha+1)L}. \tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
N_t\ge(1-\delta_L(t))q_L(\alpha(-\Delta_{H(t)})) \tag{8}
\]
as quadratic forms.

For any p-dimensional boundary subspace W_t, min-max and the Ky Fan trace principle give
\[
\operatorname{tr}(N_t|_{W_t})\ge
(1-\delta_L(t))\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}q_L(\alpha_j(H(t))). \tag{9}
\]
Here p and L are held fixed before t->infinity. Compactness bounds the first p roots uniformly over H. Equation (7) then lets L->infinity AFTERWARD to obtain the trace floor C_p in (1). No growing-cutoff freezing estimate is used.

## Gram determinant

Choose independent u_1,...,u_p in H_k and set
\[
G_{ij}(t)=\int_\Sigma u_i(e^t,x)u_j(e^t,x)d\mu(x).
\]
G is positive definite: a linear combination vanishing on the boundary is zero in the enclosed compact domain by harmonic Dirichlet uniqueness and zero on M by smooth elliptic unique continuation.

Fixed pointwise mu gives G'_ij=2<N_t u_i,u_j>. Therefore
\[
\tfrac12(\log\det G)'=
 \sum_{i=1}^{p}\langle N_tv_i,v_i\rangle, \tag{10}
\]
where v_i is an orthonormal basis of the boundary span. This follows directly from tr(G^{-1}G').

Given epsilon>0, first choose L large so all sums of the q_L roots are >=C_p-epsilon; then choose t late so the factor 1-delta_L(t) loses at most another epsilon. Equations (9)-(10) yield
\[
\tfrac12(\log\det G)'\ge C_p-2\epsilon
\]
for all sufficiently late t. Pointwise growth and Hadamard give
\[
\det G(t)\le\prod_iG_{ii}(t)\le C e^{2pk t}.
\]
Integrate the lower derivative, compare this upper rate, then epsilon->0. This proves (1).

## Uniform Weyl and the constant

For each fixed smooth H of area 4pi, ordinary Weyl gives lambda_j(H)/j->1. The convergence is uniform over K, as follows. Choose a finite C0 metric net for K. Common pointwise area form makes all Rayleigh denominators identical. In a sufficiently small net neighborhood, inverse-metric Dirichlet forms compare within factors 1+-epsilon, hence ALL eigenvalues compare by min-max. Apply individual Weyl to finitely many net centers and take the largest onset index. Then epsilon->0. No unproved uniform-degree spectral assumption is needed.

Since
\[
\sqrt\lambda/a-\tfrac12\le
 [\sqrt{1+4\lambda/a^2}-1]/2
 \le\sqrt\lambda/a,
\]
uniform Weyl and sum_{j<p}sqrt(j)=(2/3)p^{3/2}+O(sqrt(p)) imply
\[
C_p=\frac{2}{3a}p^{3/2}+o(p^{3/2}). \tag{11}
\]
For fixed k, (1) precludes arbitrarily large finite independent subsets, proving h_k finite. With p=h_k, divide (1) by p and use (11):
\[
k\ge(2/(3a)+o(1))\sqrt p.
\]
This gives the advertised coefficient 9a^2/4; subsequences with bounded p contribute zero. Finally Vol(Omega_r) ~ (4pi/3)a^2r^3, giving normalized AVR=a^2.

## What is and is not closed

The upper derivation closes under its exact premises using standard smooth elliptic, min-max, unique continuation and Weyl facts. Those facts are imported; this is not a formal-kernel proof.

Matching optimality requires verifying that the repository N175 candidate satisfies these end premises and that its countable harmonic transfer is valid. That lower construction remains source-derived and externally unverified.
