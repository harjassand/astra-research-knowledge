# Cycle 2: actual gradient secants lose a true differential-energy budget

Worker c_gaussian, 2026-10-06. Old artifacts are unchanged. The source is Chen–Chewi–Lu–Zhang, arXiv:2609.38710v1, Appendix B.2–B.3 and Algorithm 3.4. I re-read the actual forward/reverse recursions, independently checked the formulas, and exchanged them with r10 and team C. Agreement is an internal cross-check, not external validation.

**Verdict:** a specific smooth composition permitted by Appendix B.3 has Φ≡0 and a uniformly tiny smoothed-comparison Jacobian, while the source's realized secants have full output-coordinate differential energy. Their actual first likelihood estimator has L² norm asymptotic to L²α² sqrt(n)/8. Thus the exact-Jacobian r-only moment proof cannot transfer on the basis of the true/comparison differential budget alone. This example does not establish a nonzero final-output sampling bias: its near-symmetric noise may be protected by clipping cancellation. It is a structured implementation obstruction, stronger than the previous abstract robust-noise example but narrower in its conclusion.

## 1. Fully specified legal composition

Let n≥256, m=n, 0<α≤1/2, 0<L≤1/2. For a frequency ω>0 define

V(x)=sum_{j=1}^n [x_j²/4−α cos(ωx_j)/ω²],
f(x)=∇V(x)=x/2+(α/ω)sin(ωx), componentwise.

The Hessian is diagonal with entries 1/2+αcos(ωx_j), so V is globally convex, f is 1-Lipschitz and C∞, and f(0)=0. No frequency bound or Hessian-continuity modulus occurs in B.3's hypotheses.

Use exactly three steps:

z_0=g, z_1=f(g), z_2=f(g), z_3=(L/2)(z_1−z_2), Φ(g)=z_3.

The third primitive is the identity, and all offsets are zero. The supplied coefficient bound (B.5) holds: for arbitrary B_i with ||B_i||op≤1,

T_1=B_1, T_2=B_2, T_3=(L/2)B_3(B_1−B_2), ||T_3||op≤L.

The graph has S=3, m_0=n, and W=2 bounds all coefficients and f(0). The actual value implementation gives exactly zero, so Φ=hatΦ=0 and ||DΦ||F=0. A compiler could coalesce the duplicate branches and avoid this noise; the source's stated recursion does not perform that optimization. This observation limits the inference from the true map to this implementation, rather than proving that every implementation must fail.

## 2. Use the source's actual radius relations

Appendix B.3 prescribes

B_graph=(8W)^{4(S+1)}m_0(1+R),
t=ε min(1,L)/(C B_graph²),
s_i=t^{S−i+1}, r_i=t s_i.

Here s_1=t³, s_2=t², r_1=t⁴, r_2=t³. The final identity is unaffected by smoothing. One may take R_n=4sqrt(n), fixed ε∈(0,1/2), and any sufficiently large fixed C. Choose

q_n=100sqrt(2log n)/L,
ω=q_n/s_1.

This is allowed because ω is absent from the source coefficient/gradient-norm bounds. Then

ωs_1=q_n, ωs_2=q_n/t,
ωr_1=a_n=tq_n→0, ωr_2=b_n=q_n,
b_n L→∞.

The source primitive remains 1-smooth for every ω. This is a sequence of increasingly oscillatory but uniformly smooth gradients; third and higher derivative bounds diverge, and the source explicitly supplies none. Evaluating the enormous frequency at finite precision can be costly. The mathematical oracle is exact-real gradient access, matching the source model.

## 3. Even the comparison map has a uniform low-energy promise

Gaussian convolution gives

(P_s f)(g)=g/2+(α/ω)e^{−ω²s²/2}sin(ωg).

Consequently

Φ°(g)=(Lα/(2ω))[e^{−q_n²/2}−e^{−q_n²/(2t²)}]sin(ωg),

sup_g ||DΦ°(g)||F²
≤(nL²α²/4)e^{−q_n²}
≤L²α²/(4n).

Thus both the original map and the source's actual smooth comparison map meet r=1 uniformly. The means of Φ, hatΦ, and Φ° under the standard Gaussian are all zero by oddness. The value discrepancy is at most Lαsqrt(n)e^{−q_n²/2}/(2ω), exponentially tiny. This avoids the loophole that smoothing might destroy the original low-energy promise.

## 4. Exact forward and reverse routines

For a branch with radii r_i,s_i and independent ξ_i, the centered secant is

D_i(v)=v/2+α C_i h_i(v),
C_i=cos[ω(g+s_i ξ_i)], h_i(v)=sin(ωr_i v)/(ωr_i),

componentwise. The source draws fresh independent branch seeds. Its forward action is

J_g(v)=(Lα/2)[C_1 h_{a_n}(v)−C_2 h_{b_n}(v)].

Its reverse action is different away from zero:

J_g^T(w)=α[C_1 h_{a_n}(Lw/2)−C_2 h_{b_n}(Lw/2)].

This follows by starting w_3=w, propagating w_1=Lw/2 and w_2=−Lw/2, then adding their branch contributions to w_0. Each routine is odd and globally L-Lipschitz, as required by the source. The notation J^T denotes its approximate reverse routine; it is not literally the transpose of a nonlinear map.

At direction zero both have Jacobian

D_v J_g(0)=D_w J_g^T(0)=(Lα/2)diag(C_1−C_2).

For each coordinate and any fixed input g_j,

E C_i=e^{−(ωs_i)²/2}cos(ωg_j),
E C_i²=½[1+e^{−2(ωs_i)²}cos(2ωg_j)].

Therefore, uniformly in g,

E ||D J_g(0)||F²=(1+o(1)) nL²α²/4.

This contradicts deriving an implemented r=1 Frobenius budget from the true map's zero energy. It is not a finite rounding artifact. The reverse routine obeys the same statement.

For the actual covariance action hatΣ(v)=J_{g_1}(J_{g_2}^T(v)) with fresh forward/reverse seeds, the direction derivative at zero is a diagonal product. Uniformly in g_1,g_2,

E ||D hatΣ(0)||F²=(1+o(1)) nL⁴α⁴/16,

instead of the exact-Jacobian budget rL⁴. This remains true when g_1,g_2 are the Gaussian inputs sampled by the cloud gadget, since the lower bounds are uniform in those inputs.

## 5. Large energy at Gaussian directions, not just at zero

For Z standard, put F(a)=E[sin(aZ)/a]²=(1−e^{−2a²})/(2a²). The mixed moment is

G(a,b)=E[sin(aZ)sin(bZ)/(ab)]
=[e^{−(a−b)²/2}−e^{−(a+b)²/2}]/(2ab).

At g=0 the exact forward action energy is

E||J_0(Z)||²=n(Lα/2)²[q_1F(a_n)+q_2F(b_n)−2μ_1μ_2G(a_n,b_n)],

where q_i=E C_i², μ_i=E C_i. As a_n→0, b_n→∞, and phases become uniform,

E||J_0(Z)||²∼nL²α²/8.

A simple uniform lower bound is nL²α²/16 for sufficiently large n. The first secant branch is essentially a random diagonal linear action. The second branch has bounded output ≤1/b_n and vanishes on ordinary Gaussian directions. Averaging their means is accurate, but their realized energy does not cancel.

## 6. Actual first likelihood estimator loses the r-only moment bound

Let A=Lα/2, and use independent branch seeds in forward and reverse routines. Both maps are coordinatewise, with the same realization reused on Z and Z′ as Algorithm 3.4 requires. On one coordinate,

J_g^T(z)=A C_{r,1}z+e_r(z),
hatΣ(z)=A² C_{f,1}C_{r,1}z+e_S(z).

Using |sin u−u|≤|u|³/6, |h_b(v)|≤1/b, and the global L-Lipschitz bound on the reverse map gives

||z e_S(z)||L²(N(0,1))
≤C_α A²[a_n²L²+(b_nL)^−1]=o(A²).

The cosine phases are independent across branches and coordinates. Their second moments tend to 1/2 uniformly in the input, so E(C_{f,1}C_{r,1})²→1/4. For the k=1 summand

W_j=½[Z_j′ hatΣ_j(Z_j′)−Z_j hatΣ_j(Z_j)],

the leading L² norm is A²/2, because E(Z′²−Z²)²=4. The preceding error is o(A²). Every W_j has conditional mean zero given the action realization, since Z_j,Z_j′ are identically distributed. Hence cross-coordinate covariance is exactly zero even after mixing over Brownian inputs, and

||W_1||L²(Q)∼A²sqrt(n)/2=L²α²sqrt(n)/8.

Choosing L_n=n^{−1/8} gives L_n²=n^{−1/4} and

||W_1||2∼(α²/8)n^{1/4}→∞,

while the would-be r=1 clipping scale at p=2 is

L_n²(sqrt(2)+2)+L_n⁴=O(n^{−1/4})→0.

Thus the proposed exact-Jacobian moment refinement is false for this actual secant implementation, despite a uniformly tiny comparison Jacobian. This is a failure of a transfer lemma. Unlike the prior asymmetric scalar-I noise example, no final-output TV lower bound has been established here; the approximate uniform cosine symmetry could make clipped conditional means small despite huge estimator moments.

## 7. Independent audit of team C's nonlinear replacement bound

Team C and r10 proposed a weaker sufficient lemma. Suppose S:R^n→R^n is odd, C¹, a-Lipschitz, and uniformly

||DS(x)||F²≤ρa², ||DS(x)||_*≤ρa, ρ≥1.

I independently verified

E exp[tG·S(G)]≤exp[|t|ρa+C t²ρa²], |t|a≤c,

and thus ||G·S(G)||p≤Ca[ρ+sqrt(ρp)+p]. This is uncentered and has a leading ρ term; it does not reproduce the sharper exact-matrix sqrt(ρ) cancellation.

Proof: y=x−tS(x) is a global diffeomorphism for |t|a<1, by the contraction mapping argument, and its determinant is positive. The exponent −||x||²/2+t x·S(x) becomes −||y||²/2+t²||S(x(y))||²/2. The log determinant power series gives

−log det(I−tDS)≤|t|ρa+C t²ρa²,

using |tr(DS)^j|≤||DS||F² a^{j−2} for j≥2. Also ||S(x(y))||≤||S(y)||/(1−|t|a). Oddness plus Gaussian Poincaré gives E||S(G)||²≤ρa². For R=||S(G)||, Gaussian concentration and an independent scalar H~N(0,1) give

Eexp(λR²)=E_H E_Gexp(sqrt(2λ)HR)
≤(1−2λa²)^{−1/2}exp[λ(ER)²/(1−2λa²)].

For λa²≤c this is ≤exp(Cλρa²). Substitute λ=t²/[2(1−|t|a)²], then apply Chernoff and tail integration. The proof extends to Lipschitz S with justified area/change-of-variables approximation; C¹ suffices for the source's smooth constructed maps. I found no fatal gap.

A supplied realized-action derivative budget can therefore support a weaker nonlinear correction bound. It must control the implementation throughout the direction domain, not just DΦ. A robust coefficient-graph promise, or primitive Hessian/action-energy bounds propagated through the graph, is a possible sufficient interface. The cancelling example shows that true-map energy alone is insufficient. Acquisition and verification of such a uniform promise remain separate costs.

## 8. Finite reconstruction, limitations, next step

Run `python3 work/c_gaussian/cycle2/structured_secants.py`. It uses exactly the source forward/reverse formulas in scaled coordinates and saves results/c_gaussian/cycle2/structured_secants.json. It imports no other worker code. For n=65,536, L=.25, α=.25, the true derivative energy is zero, the comparison-energy log upper bound is below −3.5 million, the expected forward direction-Jacobian energy is 64 against budget .0625, and Gaussian-direction action energy is 32.0000. The covariance-action direction-Jacobian energy is approximately .0625 against budget .00390625. The empirical W_1 L² norm is .1346 versus asymptotic .125; finite simulation is illustrative, and the analytic limit proves failure of the moment transfer.

To avoid inaccurate floating-point reduction of the enormous exact-real ω, Monte Carlo uses uniform wrapped phases. A wrapped N(0,σ²) phase differs from uniform phase in TV by at most sum_{k≥1}e^{−k²σ²/2}. Here σ≥q_n; all 4n phases jointly differ by at most 4n e^{−q_n²/2}/(1−e^{−q_n²/2}), with log bound below −1.7 million in the final case. Exact closed-form moments retain the true Gaussian phases. Finite precision and gradient-function evaluation costs are not measured.

No novelty claim is made for branch cancellation, Gaussian convolution, or the weaker nonlinear lemma. The consequence is a precise missing hypothesis for future low-r samplers: a true differential-energy promise does not certify the realized source secants. The next falsifiable step is to add shared-seed/common-branch reuse or a structural coefficient budget, derive the resulting action energy and nonlinear quadratic bound, and compare charged work against simplifying this graph to the identically zero map. No whole-sampler improvement is claimed.
