# Family 028: exact periodic certificates and the shared-data mechanism

All substantive build TeX was read and saved in `sources/frontier_number3/cycle19/028/`. ## Exact question and author claims

For a fixed D, put an edge between distinct Gaussian primes at Euclidean distance at most D. The source's `thm:main` claims an eventual finite bound B_D on every component, uniformly over its starting prime, including the coordinate axes. Its B_D is nonexplicit. The stronger load-bearing `thm:sieve` claims that for every D >= 1, some finite set P_D of rational primes p=1 mod 4 makes the periodic avoiding set

`A(P) = {(x,y) in Z^2 : x^2+y^2 != 0 mod p for every p in P}`

contain no infinite self-avoiding D-step walk. Every Gaussian prime outside the associates of selected Gaussian factors belongs to A(P).

For Q=product(P), the avoiding set is periodic under Q Z^2. The source's `prop:periodic`, in `periodicity.tex`, turns an obstruction into the bound

`max(Q^2, |E|+(K_D-1)|E| Q^2)`,

where E is the finite set of associates of selected Gaussian factors and K_D counts lattice vectors of length at most D, including zero. We reconstruct a sharper exact finite interface below: replace Q^2 by the largest component size M of the lifted periodic graph when no winding cycle exists.

## 1. Complete finite decision interface

Input: a finite set P of distinct split rational primes, and an integer d2. Permitted steps are the nonzero lattice vectors s with squared length <=d2. This represents D=sqrt(d2) exactly, without floating-point comparisons. More generally the argument works in Z^d with any finite periodic vertex set and any finite symmetric step alphabet.

Construct the quotient graph on surviving residues modulo Q in each coordinate. Retain each edge's physical step vector, even when different physical steps have the same quotient endpoints. Choose a spanning tree in each component and assign a potential L(v) in Z^2 by summing physical steps from its root. For every edge u --s--> v, test `L(v)=L(u)+s`.

If every edge passes, each tree path from the root to v reaches exactly root+L(v); every other path has the same displacement because all edge equations telescope. Thus each lifted component is a translate of this finite potential set. Distinct quotient vertices have distinct residues, hence distinct lifted points. The component has exactly the quotient component's cardinality. This gives a locally verifiable obstruction certificate, consisting of one integer potential per quotient vertex.

If an edge fails, the tree path and this edge contain a simple quotient cycle with nonzero displacement delta. Its displacement belongs to Q Z^2 because the quotient cycle closes. Repeating the physical cycle gives an infinite injective avoiding walk: points at different places within one cycle have different quotient residues, and repetitions at the same place differ by a nonzero integer multiple of delta. This is an explicit infinite-walk certificate for this fixed sieve.

These two cases are exhaustive. They decide whether a specified finite periodic sieve percolates. They do not choose the sieve promised by the source's universal theorem. A percolating small sieve therefore does not refute `for every D there exists a finite P_D`; its avoiding vertices need not be Gaussian primes.

The own implementation is `work/frontier_number3/cycle19/periodic_sieve.py`. No release scripts were executed. It uses only the Python standard library. Vertex generation uses the two roots of -1 modulo each split p; direct verifiers instead recompute the norm predicate. The obstruction verifier checks the complete vertex set, every local edge equation, and component sizes. The cycle verifier checks the arithmetic predicates, all step lengths, simple quotient cycle, and nonzero period displacement.

Costs for Q: O(Q^2 * |step alphabet|) time and O(Q^2) memory; obstruction certificates also have O(Q^2) entries. Q grows as a product of primes, so this is often intractable in input bit length. A certificate for one chosen finite sieve is scoped evidence, not validation of the asymptotic source theorem.

The search uses signed 64-bit arrays and explicitly rejects inputs whose Q^2 times the maximum step coordinate exceeds that range. Direct certificate verification uses Python integer arithmetic. Hardware memory and time can prevent acquiring an otherwise finite certificate; no bounded-resource promise is made.

## 3. Abstract shared-data coverage transfer, independently reconstructed

The strongest broadly reusable source mechanism is `thm:transfer` in `coverage-transfer.tex`. Its interface is finite abelian groups, rather than Gaussian primes specifically.

For signed coordinate homomorphisms phi_c with group sizes p_c in [T,2T], sample an auxiliary balanced set S of s coordinates independently of all measured variables. Let A be a finite mixing variable, Y=y(A), and conditional on exact A=a let h_1,...,h_n be independent with one displacement law nu_a. Put H=(h_1,...,h_n) and Z=Y+h_1. Coordinates are (tau,beta)-covered when fewer than p^(1-beta) residues have mass below tau/p.

The exact source assumptions are:

- `0<tau<=3/8`, `0<delta<=1/64`, `tau'=tau+4delta`, `0<beta'<=beta<1`, `0<eta<=1`.
- At each exact a, all but an eta' fraction of coordinates have (tau',beta') coverage for Y+h_1 under the conditional law.
- `E_S H(F_S(Y)) >= (1-epsilon) s Lbar`, and `H(H) <= kappa s Lbar`, with `Lbar=mean log p`.
- `eta' <= eta delta/100`, `epsilon+kappa <= eta delta beta'/16`.
- For every p: `p exp(-delta^2 n p^(-beta)/2) <= delta/100`, `delta^(-1)<p^(beta'/2)`, and `log(2e/delta)<=(beta'/4)log T`.

The conclusion is that all but an eta fraction of coordinates have (tau,beta) coverage for the mixture Z. It improves the exceptional-set exponent while reducing the probability floor. It does not promise full support or independence of coordinates, and independence of continuations is required only after the exact mixing checkpoint is fixed.

Reconstruction. If too many coordinates are deficient, choose a set K of ceil(p^(1-beta)) deficient residues. Using the one common H, list candidate Y residues whose n translated trials hit K at most n(tau+delta)|K|/p times. The true residue is on this list with probability at least delta by Markov's inequality and the identical one-trial marginal. At a conditionally covered checkpoint, averaging translations of its small exceptional set eliminates all but fewer than delta^(-1)p^(1-beta') candidate starts. Conditional independence and a binomial lower-tail bound eliminate all remaining candidates except on probability delta/100. After averaging exact checkpoints, the true residue appears on a list of size <=p^(1-beta'/2) with mean probability >=delta/2 over deficient coordinates.

This implies conditional entropy deficit at least delta beta' log T/8 per deficient coordinate (binary success entropy is absorbed by the logarithmic assumption). But subadditivity and the chain rule bound the mean deficit by (epsilon+kappa)Lbar: the entire shared vector H costs H(H) only once in the joint entropy of s residues. Since Lbar<=2log T, the displayed budget makes the two statements incompatible if the deficient fraction exceeds eta. This completes the abstract transfer implication under its exact assumptions.

The cost condition is substantial. The trials need roughly `n >= 2 delta^(-2) p^beta log(100p/delta)`, while shared-data entropy must fit the s-coordinate budget. Unconditional independence of h_i would be the wrong interface; checkpoint mixtures can correlate every trial. This finite abstract implication is independently reconstructed, but its applicability to the source's enormous schedule still needs a full end-to-end audit.

## 4. What is still load-bearing in the universal theorem

The source's route to a finite P_D combines:

1. `lem:enrichment` in `geometry.tex`: uniform sampling from distinct differences of a forward self-avoiding segment, plus a fair endpoint choice, improves normalized joint residue entropy from h to approximately (1+h)/2.
2. `lem:rectangle`: signed Gaussian-factor products almost surely separate a thin rectangle. Determinant divisibility and Boolean-cube expansion avoid a separate enumeration of directions.
3. The backward coverage induction and common terminal time law in `schedule.tex`. Its parameters are extremely separated, including windows with log T around 100^m, a middle g=exp(-M^3), and a bottom g=exp(-100M^20).
4. `prop:information-rate` in `information.tex`: zero avoidance forces >=c/log T information per increment, after testing candidate checkpoint residues with independent posterior continuation packages.
5. `lem:telescope`: nested residue batches and dyadic increment words at one approximately shift-invariant time law charge the total to the bounded entropy of a step, while the selected batch costs diverge.

We read all of these source proofs but have not independently verified their complete combination, the thin-rectangle lemma, or the global schedule inequalities. Neither small finite computations nor a Lean-labelled main wrapper resolves these gates. No decisive counterexample was found during this cycle.

## Prior art, novelty, downstream use, next step

Vardi's *Prime Percolation* (1998), Section 6 and Proposition 6.2, explicitly studies periodic sieve graphs and passage from no infinite walk to a uniform component bound. Printed pp.283-284 and p.286 were inspected as rendered pages from the saved PDF. The finite periodic idea is old; the voltage-potential presentation and own code are independently constructed tools, with no novelty claim for the mathematical criterion. Gethner-Stark (1997) is cited by the release for periodic obstructions at sqrt(2) and 2, but our Project Euclid retrieval returned an access challenge, so we do not assert that paper was read.

Downstream use: exact positive and negative certificates for any proposed periodic sieve, and a coverage transfer principle for correlated continuation mixtures in additive finite groups. The next discriminating allocation should reconstruct the source's rectangle/enrichment mechanism and schedule independently, then give the finite engine a genuinely nontrivial larger-D proposed sieve. Searching longer arbitrary small-prime prefixes without analytic guidance has poor product-of-primes cost and is not the main research bottleneck.
