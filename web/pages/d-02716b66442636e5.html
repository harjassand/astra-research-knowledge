CYCLE 3 — RANK-FREE HANKEL SMOOTHING FOR FINITE-HORIZON TASK RISK
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: SELF-CONTAINED FINITE-SAMPLE THEOREM; raw-path interface and costs explicit

1. RESULT AND BOUNDARY

For a fixed stationary-history horizon m and prediction horizon h, add-one smoothing of the raw empirical Hankel block gives an explicit finite-H conditional predictor. Its expected task loss is the stationary-context-weighted KL divergence, not a worst-case loss over histories. With M=a^m contexts, K=a^h continuations, and n independent raw paths of length m+h, the bound is

  E R_{m,h} <= (K-1)/(n+1) sum_{u:p_u>0} [1-(1-p_u)^(n+1)]
             <= M(K-1)/(n+1),

where p_u is the true stationary probability of context u. No observable-rank estimate, singular-value gap, or process-specific dictionary is used. The bound charges rare contexts by their actual stationary mass and exposes the exponential horizon dependence through M and K. A task-weighted conditional L1 norm is controlled by Pinsker's inequality.

This is an affirmative rank-free bridge in a task-weighted norm. It is not a theorem that an unweighted singular-value tail, Frobenius residual, or operator residual controls prediction risk. The procedure directly regularizes and estimates the task rows. It does not learn a finite-state realization, predict beyond h, or exploit low rank for sample savings.

2. PROCESS AND RAW-DATA CONTRACT

Let Sigma be a known alphabet with |Sigma|=a>=2. The process is a stationary HMM with an unknown number s<=R of hidden states, where R is an optional supplied state cap. The observable minimal Hankel rank is unknown and is not supplied. For the stationary version of the theorem, each training episode is an independent length-(m+h) observation path from the stationary HMM. No hidden state, transition, emission, word-event query, or model-specific basis is observed. The known task universe consists only of all words of the specified context and continuation lengths:

  U=Sigma^m,  V=Sigma^h,  M=|U|=a^m,  K=|V|=a^h.

This is the universal task index set, not a supplied process-dependent dictionary. The finite Hankel block is

  H[u,v]=P(X_1...X_m=u, X_(m+1)...X_(m+h)=v),
  p_u=sum_v H[u,v],  q_u(v)=H[u,v]/p_u  when p_u>0.

The task is prediction of the next h observations after a stationary length-m history. Its finite-H KL risk for a predictor qhat is

  R_{m,h}(qhat)=sum_{u:p_u>0} p_u KL(q_u || qhat_u),

with natural logarithms. The corresponding task-weighted conditional L1 norm is

  ||qhat-q||_(1,p)=sum_{u:p_u>0} p_u ||qhat_u-q_u||_1.

The test history is distributed according to the stationary HMM law. The displayed risk is its expected conditional KL; the future observation itself has already been integrated out in q_u.

For a concrete substantive HMM class, one may additionally promise T_ij>=alpha>0 and emission probabilities e_i(x)>=nu>0, for every state i, destination j, and x in Sigma. These assumptions give a one-time mixing bound for a nonstationary reset and ensure p_u>=nu^m for every context. They are not needed for the iid-stationary-path risk theorem below. In particular, neither alpha nor nu appears in its sample bound.

3. ESTIMATOR: SOFT EMPIRICAL HANKEL ROWS

For each raw episode i, read its full length-(m+h) string and record its one pair (U_i,V_i). Let

  C_uv = #{i: U_i=u,V_i=v},  C_u=sum_v C_uv.

The raw empirical Hankel block is Hhat[u,v]=C_uv/n. Smooth it as

  Htilde[u,v]=(C_uv+1)/(n+MK)
            = n/(n+MK) Hhat[u,v] + (MK)/(n+MK) * 1/(MK).

Thus Htilde is the convex mixture of the empirical joint table and the uniform joint table, with weight n/(n+MK) on the empirical table. Normalize each row:

  qhat_u(v)=Htilde[u,v]/sum_z Htilde[u,z]
           =(C_uv+1)/(C_u+K).

Every row is a probability distribution, including an unseen context, for which it is uniform. The construction is rank-free, finite, and fully specified by the alphabet, task horizons, and raw path counts. It does not estimate a rank, truncate an SVD, or assume the smoothed table is a globally consistent infinite Hankel realization.

4. THEOREM

Theorem (stationary task-risk and task-norm bounds). Let n>=1 independent stationary raw paths of length m+h be drawn from any stationary finite-alphabet process (hence in particular from the positive finite-state HMM class above). Apply the add-one estimator. Then

  E_train R_{m,h}(qhat)
    <= (K-1)/(n+1) sum_{u:p_u>0} [1-(1-p_u)^(n+1)]
    <= M(K-1)/(n+1).

Consequently,

  E_train ||qhat-q||_(1,p) <= sqrt(2 M(K-1)/(n+1)).

For any epsilon,delta>0, Markov's inequality gives the explicit confidence statement

  P_train{R_{m,h}(qhat)>epsilon}
    <= M(K-1)/((n+1)epsilon).

Thus a sufficient (conservative) sample size for risk at most epsilon with probability at least 1-delta is

  n >= M(K-1)/(delta epsilon) - 1.

Equivalently, a sufficient sample size for ||qhat-q||_(1,p)<=zeta with probability at least 1-delta is

  n >= 2 M(K-1)/(delta zeta^2) - 1.

These confidence bounds use Markov, so their 1/delta dependence is not asserted optimal. All bounds are independent of the hidden-state count, observable rank, and every nonzero singular value of H.

The first bound also gives a distribution-dependent rare-context charge in an explicit formula. Each context contributes at most

  (K-1) min{p_u,1/(n+1)}

to the expected risk bound. A context with n p_u << 1 contributes at most approximately (K-1)p_u; a frequently observed context contributes at most (K-1)/(n+1). Therefore the average task does not require a uniform minimum context probability. If the task is changed to a worst-case guarantee over every history, a rare-context penalty returns: under e_i(x)>=nu, p_u>=nu^m, but per-history learning can require context coverage on a scale proportional to nu^(-m), along with additional continuation-accuracy costs. This theorem makes no per-history claim.

5. PROOF

Fix u with p_u>0 and condition on C_u=t. Given which paths have context u, their continuations are independent with common law q_u; hence the row counts (C_uv)_v are multinomial(t,q_u). The add-one row is r_v=(C_uv+1)/(t+K), and r_v>=1/(t+K). Since log x<=x-1,

  KL(q_u||r) <= sum_v q_u(v)^2/r_v - 1.

For C~Binomial(t,q), direct binomial expansion (or the identity 1/(c+1)=integral_0^1 x^c dx) gives

  E[1/(C+1)] = [1-(1-q)^(t+1)]/((t+1)q) <= 1/((t+1)q),

when q>0; a zero-probability symbol contributes zero and follows by continuity. Taking expectation of the chi-square upper bound,

  E[KL(q_u||r) | C_u=t]
    <= (t+K) sum_v q_u(v)^2 E[1/(C_uv+1)] - 1
    <= (t+K)/(t+1) - 1
    = (K-1)/(t+1).

Now C_u~Binomial(n,p_u). The same integral identity gives

  p_u E[1/(C_u+1)]
    = [1-(1-p_u)^(n+1)]/(n+1).

Multiply the row bound by p_u and average over C_u, then sum over u. This proves the first inequality. There are at most M positive-probability contexts and each bracket is at most one, giving M(K-1)/(n+1). Also 1-(1-p)^(n+1)<=min{1,(n+1)p}, proving the stated rare-context contribution.

Pinsker in nats gives ||qhat_u-q_u||_1<=sqrt(2 KL(q_u||qhat_u)). Weighted Jensen then yields

  ||qhat-q||_(1,p)
    <= sum_u p_u sqrt(2 KL(q_u||qhat_u))
    <= sqrt(2 R_{m,h}(qhat)).

Take expectation and use Jensen once more to obtain E||qhat-q||_(1,p)<=sqrt(2 E R). Markov applied to R gives the confidence statement and its sample-size corollaries. This completes the proof.

6. NONSTATIONARY RESET COST

If the environment supplies an exact stationary reset, the stationary theorem costs n resets and n(m+h) observed symbols, with no burn-in. Otherwise suppose each independent reset begins at an arbitrary hidden-state law and T_ij>=alpha. For s hidden states, any two rows of T overlap in probability mass at least s alpha, so the Dobrushin coefficient is at most 1-s alpha<=1-alpha. Put rho=1-alpha. After discarding B observations/transitions, the hidden-state law is within rho^B in total variation of stationarity; applying the same emission and path kernels cannot increase total variation. Couple each retained episode to a stationary length-(m+h) path with mismatch probability at most rho^B. For n independent episodes, a union bound gives a joint coupling mismatch at most n rho^B.

For every fixed training dataset, qhat_u(v)>=1/(n+K), so 0<=R_{m,h}(qhat)<=log(n+K). The risk is a bounded function of the training dataset. Comparing the burn-in data law with n stationary paths under the coupling therefore gives

  E_reset R_{m,h}(qhat)
    <= M(K-1)/(n+1) + n rho^B log(n+K).

The corresponding probability bound is this right-hand side divided by epsilon. To make the initialization term at most tau, choose B with n rho^B log(n+K)<=tau. For 0<rho<1, it suffices to take

  B >= max{0, ceil(log(n log(n+K)/tau)/(-log rho))}.

If rho=0, B=1 suffices (or B=0 for an exact stationary reset). This explicitly charges the mixing burn-in. Emission positivity gives p_u>=nu^m but does not enter average-risk sample count; it only supplies a worst-case context lower bound if the task asks for one.

7. ACQUISITION, COMPUTATION, PRECISION, AND FAIR BASELINE

Raw data and information cost. Each episode supplies one context/continuation pair from its full length-(m+h) observed path. The theorem uses n independent paths, n reset operations, and n(m+h) retained symbol observations, plus nB discarded burn-in symbols when stationary reset is unavailable. If an emitted symbol is encoded in fixed-width bits, retained communication is n(m+h) ceil(log2 a) bits. There is no separate event query and no cost-free access to any H entry.

Count storage and arithmetic. Encoding one observed pair costs O(m+h) symbol inspections (or O(1) arithmetic after base-a word encoding). It causes one increment of C_uv and one of C_u. A sparse hash/table implementation stores at most min{n,MK} nonzero pair counts and min{n,M} row counts, each with O(log(n+1)) count bits and O((m+h)log a) key bits; it need not allocate M K cells. Expected hash update time is O(n) after reading paths, or deterministic direct-index updates use the corresponding integer word encoding. A prediction query costs O(1) count lookups and O(1) rational arithmetic once the row and continuation indices are encoded. Materializing all entries requires MK output writes and MK storage. The estimator has no SVD, pseudoinverse, or rank threshold.

Precision. Counts and add-one probabilities can be returned exactly as rational numbers: numerators are at most n+1 and row denominators are at most n+K, so O(log(n+K)) bits per numerator/denominator suffice. The theorem assumes exact count arithmetic. A fixed-precision implementation must separately account for output-rounding error; no floating-point spectral step is hidden in this guarantee.

Same-data full-path baseline. The competent baseline retains every length-(m+h) raw path (or all its prefix statistics), builds the full empirical Hankel/count representation, and can apply exactly this row smoothing. It has the identical n paths, contexts, continuation counts, and risk guarantee. By retaining raw data it can also run any other predictor or an SVD-based method on these same paths. Thus this result gives no trajectory/sample-information advantage over the full-path baseline. A dense empirical-Hankel implementation pays O(MK) materialization/storage and, for a dense SVD of an M-by-K block, O(MK min{M,K}) arithmetic; the sparse row estimator avoids those downstream matrix costs when only this task is requested. That is a computational/storage saving, not an acquisition saving. This note does not show that a low-rank HMM method cannot use the same paths to improve sample efficiency under additional structure.

8. LIMITS, STATUS, AND SOURCES

The theorem proves finite-horizon prediction after length-m observed histories for h future symbols. It does not cover infinite-history prediction, an unknown/unbounded horizon, arbitrary continuation policies, model recovery, hidden-state identification, or worst-case conditional risk. Its sample bound is exponential in the stated horizons through M K=a^(m+h); that cost is displayed rather than hidden. If raw paths are drawn from one uninterrupted dependent trajectory rather than independent resets, this theorem does not apply directly; a separate dependence analysis is needed. The simple proof does not turn a Frobenius/operator Hankel tail into a task guarantee and does not compare low-rank estimator minimax rates.

The smoothing and risk argument above is proved here from elementary binomial identities, chi-square domination of KL, Pinsker, and Markov's inequality. The positive-HMM mixing step is the direct overlap/coupling consequence of the Dobrushin contraction coefficient. For context, Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang's primary spectral HMM result uses a rank condition and a nonzero singular-value separation to recover an observable realization; the present theorem needs neither because it estimates only the requested finite-horizon conditional task. See https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413. Dobrushin's original paper is R. L. Dobrushin, “Central Limit Theorem for Nonstationary Markov Chains I,” Theory of Probability and its Applications 1 (1956), 65–80, DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/1101006; the overlap contraction used here is stated and derived explicitly above.

Status is internal mathematical proof, not external review, novelty validation, or a claim of superiority to structured spectral learning. No experiment is needed for the analytic theorem. A future stronger result would need to connect a specified approximation/tail certificate to this task-weighted loss or give a decisive obstruction; neither is claimed here.
