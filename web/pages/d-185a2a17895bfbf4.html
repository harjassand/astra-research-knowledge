  c_k(F) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty} |det F[I,J]|^2,

with a nonnegative unbiased sign sample X_k, E X_k=c_k, and

CLOSEST PRIMARY-SOURCE RESULTS

1. Pfaffian Cauchy-Binet and minor summation are established identities.
Okada, “Pfaffian Formulas and Schur Q-Function Identities,” arXiv:1706.01029,
Theorem 3.2 and Theorem 3.4, gives Pfaffian analogues of Cauchy-Binet and the
Ishikawa-Wakayama minor-summation formula. The displayed formulas sum products
of sub-Pfaffians into one Pfaffian. This is direct prior art for the
algebraic expansion used to represent Pf(A_s[R]) as a sum over selected
two-column blocks, but it does not state the Rademacher estimator, the
specific disjoint-minor coefficient c_k(F), the formal-square-root sample
routine, or its 9^k relative-variance guarantee.

2. A closer “squared minors plus randomization” analogue is Cohen,
“Ramanujan Graphs in Polynomial Time,” FOCS 2016. The paper notes that
characteristic-polynomial coefficients of Z^T Z are sums of squares of
k-by-k minors (pp. 278-279), and computes expected characteristic
polynomials for a random-permutation construction by expanding minors and
then encoding the required averages in a multivariate determinant
(pp. 279-280). That is a consequential nearby precedent for expectation
identities involving squared minors and determinant coefficients. Its input
distribution is a structured random permutation perturbation, its objective
is a deterministic expected-characteristic-polynomial computation for an
interlacing-family graph construction, and it does not give the current
all-or-none paired-column disjoint-minor estimator or its hypercontractive
sample bound. This source makes a broad “new use of expected squared minors”
or “first determinant-coefficient randomization” claim unsafe without a more
complete search.

3. Matoya and Oki, “Pfaffian Pairs and Parities,” arXiv:1912.00620, is an
exact counting result for specified Pfaffian classes. Their Pfaffian parity
definition requires a nonzero constant c such that det A[B]=c for every
feasible parity base; this makes the number of parity bases extractable from
a Pfaffian. They also give polynomial-time counts of minimum-weight bases
for such Pfaffian pairs/parities. This is adjacent parity/Pfaffian counting
prior art, but its constant-determinant structure differs from the general
complex positive sum of squared disjoint minors here. It neither implies nor
disproves the estimator for arbitrary F.

4. Celis, Deshpande, Kathuria, Straszak, and Vishnoi, “On the Complexity of
Constrained Determinantal Point Processes,” arXiv:1608.00554v3, provides the
closest constrained-DPP interface. Their real PSD Gram kernel gives weights
det(L[S,S]); their framework studies restricting this DPP to a family C.
The c03_l10 Cauchy-Binet formula makes c_k a DPP-like partition function on
the 2n columns [e_i, (row_i F)^T], with Hermitian PSD kernel V^*V,
constrained to choose both columns of exactly k site-pairs and no columns
from the other sites. This is an all-or-none two-element-block constraint.
The complex Hermitian input is not literally the real PSD input in their
formal statement; a bridge would be needed for a theorem transfer. Even
setting that field distinction aside, Celis et al. give exact
polynomial-time algorithms for one budget when cost magnitude is measured
unarily and for partition families with a constant number of parts. Their
hardness result concerns binary-encoded budget instances; their mixed-
discriminant reduction is for the associated exact-budget constrained DPP
problem. The pair-block constraint here has n simultaneous sitewise choices
of 0 or 2, plus a global pair-count, and I found no direct reduction from
their hard family to this exact constraint, nor an encoding in their
constant-number-of-part tractable case. Thus that paper is a concrete
reduction target, not an AP-hardness theorem for c_k.

5. Iwata-Kobayashi, “A Weighted Linear Matroid Parity Algorithm,”
arXiv:1905.13371, is polynomial-time weighted optimization, not a general
partition/counting or approximation algorithm. It is not direct prior art
for the c_k estimator.

INDEPENDENT INTERFACE CHECK AND LIMITS

I reconstructed the representation boundary used in the literature
comparison. Put v_i=(row_i F)^T and V=[e_1,v_1,...,e_n,v_n]. For a selected
site set S of size k, the Gram determinant det(V[:,P(S)]^*V[:,P(S)]) expands
by Cauchy-Binet over row sets R of size 2k. It vanishes unless R contains
every selected identity coordinate. Writing R=S union J, |J|=k and J
disjoint from S, the remaining determinant is det F[S,J] up to an irrelevant
ordering sign. Thus

  c_k(F) = sum_{|S|=k} det(V[:,P(S)]^* V[:,P(S)]),

which is exactly the Hermitian-Gram, DPP-like block-constrained sum stated
above. This explains the Celis comparison without asserting literal equality
of their real-kernel interface or importing its hardness conclusion.

The low-k runtime boundary matters for priority and significance. Directly
enumerating all disjoint (I,J) costs n^{O(k)}, which is polynomial for fixed
k; the estimator's more consequential parameter regime is k=O(log n),
where it remains polynomial while that naive enumeration can be
quasi-polynomial. At k=Theta(n), the proved 9^k sample factor is exponential.

SOL BASELINE DISTINCTION

outputs/round6/SOL_BASELINE.txt is explicitly frozen as a Sol-origin
baseline. Its leading exact c_k route is a bounded-frontier contraction for
an acquired low-width support ordering, including the c01_s01 subclass with
all but t rows monomial relative to a permutation and t=O(log n); it obtains
exact counts/prefixes on that structural subclass. The baseline also records
that general determinant-parity counting/sampling remains open. This is a
different algorithmic interface and origin from c03_l10's arbitrary-F,
low-k randomized estimator. The two can coexist in a portfolio on their
respective domains; the Sol exact subclass neither supplies the estimator's
variance proof nor turns its exponential dense-k bound into a general
polynomial algorithm.

AUDIT VERDICT

No exact match for the full Rademacher/Pfaffian-square-root/9^k estimator was
identified in this targeted primary-source check. The main components are
standard Pfaffian minor summation, Gram/Cauchy-Binet, a skew-symmetric normal
form, and Boolean-cube hypercontractivity, assembled into a particular
estimator. Cohen 2016 is a meaningful nearby squared-minor/randomized-
expectation precedent and must be cited in any novelty discussion. The
currently supportable status is: internal mathematical claim; finite exact
diagnostics; targeted adjacent prior art identified; exact priority and
novelty unresolved; no dense-sector FPRAS or hardness transfer established.
