12 October 2026. **The circular shrink-ring mechanism is existing experimental prior art.** The current corrected baseline is Cui et al., [*Dendrite initiation and deflection in biaxially stressed solid electrolytes*, Nature 655, 631–637 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10734-x). Its global balance, annular stress, radial interference, strength/fill inequalities, laminate energy, and reported numerical illustrations contain no substantive algebraic error. All numerical material choices below are declared illustrative, with no candidate reinforcement material selected or validated.

## 1. Equilibrium and manufacture

For a bounded body at static equilibrium, with no body force and zero external traction, integration of `div(x_i sigma_j)=sigma_ji` gives

\[
\int_V\sigma_{ij}\,dV=\int_{\partial V}x_i t_j\,dA=0.
\]

Interfacial tractions cancel in the composite integral. Consequently, a ceramic fraction `f` under `sigma_xx,sigma_yy <= -P` requires balancing tensile material. If all other load-bearing material has tensile normal stress at most `S`, then `fP <= (1-f)S`. This exact necessary condition does not supply an allowable strength, a stable interface, or a uniform local stress field. Voids cannot supply the balance. A free homogeneous body given uniform thermal expansion has no residual stress.

A physically complete manufacture must supply differential stress-free dimensions: prestretch and bond/release, thermal mismatch with the correct sign, irreversible transformation, or a controlled differential chemical strain. In an elastic cooling process the relevant mismatch is the **integral** of the CTE difference over temperature, not a high-temperature CTE multiplied by an arbitrary interval. Plastic flow, cracking and phase change during processing alter the stress-free dimensions. The elastic energy below is a resource held in the state; an exothermic transformation may supply it. It is not a universal external-work bound and does not determine the factory's heat, tooling or yield cost.

## 2. Symmetric skins: a valid interior solution with a transport price

Let the core thickness be `t_c`, combined thickness of two identical skins be `t_r`, and `r=t_r/t_c`. Assume isotropic plane stress, perfect bonding, small strain, and a flat symmetric laminate far from edges. Let `M_i=E_i/(1-nu_i)` be the biaxial modulus. If the core's free in-plane eigenstrain exceeds the skins' by `Delta epsilon*`, force balance and strain compatibility give

\[
S=P/r,\qquad
\Delta\epsilon^*=P\left(\frac1{M_c}+\frac1{rM_r}\right),\qquad
P=\frac{\Delta\epsilon^*}{1/M_c+1/(rM_r)}.
\]

These are exact within this uniform laminate model. Thinner balancing skins need higher tension and larger elastic mismatch. On cooling from a stress-free bonding temperature, **higher-CTE skins** provide the desired sign; lower-CTE skins put the core in tension. Demonstrating a compressed protective coating is therefore not automatically a demonstration of a compressed core.

For `P=330 MPa`, `t_c=20 micrometres`, `E_c=150 GPa`, `nu_c=0.25`, `M_r=250 GPa`, and `r=0.33`, the skins must sustain `1.00 GPa` equibiaxial tension and the eigenstrain mismatch must be `0.565%`. An assumed CTE difference `5e-6/K` would require an elastic cooling interval of `1,130 K`. That number is a sensitivity example, not a proposed thermal process. A phase-transforming alloy may obtain mismatch differently; its transformation stress, hysteresis, electrical insulation and cycling stability must then be measured.

The stored elastic energy per separator area is

\[
U_A=P^2t_c\left(\frac1{M_c}+\frac1{rM_r}\right).
\]

There is no extra factor `1/2`: there are two equal in-plane stress/strain components. The example stores `37.29 J/m^2`. This energy can drive relaxation or debonding, but it is **not** the interfacial energy-release rate of an unspecified delamination. A crack geometry and boundary conditions are needed to calculate that rate.

Continuous skins sit in series with through-plane ion transport. In the linear, uniform one-dimensional model,

\[
\mathrm{ASR}=t_c/\kappa_c+t_r/\kappa_r+\sum R_{\rm interface}.
\]

The added bulk resistance relative to the bare core is `r kappa_c/kappa_r`. At `r=0.33`, keeping that increase below `20%` requires `kappa_r >= 1.65 kappa_c`, before interface resistance. Dense ion-blocking skins fail the transport contract. Perforating a blocking skin leaves an active-area fraction `phi`; the average current density in the conducting apertures is at least `j_device/phi`, before spreading resistance and current crowding. Holes also alter the load transfer and local stress. One cannot retain both the uniform-skin prestress solution and its full conducting area after perforation without re-solving the problem.

The closest measured coating example is Cai et al., [*Thermo-mechanical characterization and stress engineering of Lipon solid electrolyte*, J. Mater. Chem. A 12, 3943–3953 (2024)](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2024/ta/d3ta06386k). They measured a CTE around `4.1e-6/K`, yield stresses around `60–100 MPa` in their temperature/rate conditions, viscoplastic relaxation, and loss of ductility after annealing above `140 C`. These measurements are not a sustained `1 GPa` tensile-skin qualification. They are direct evidence that coating prestress can depend on time, temperature and processing history.

At a free vertical outer edge, an unwrapped core has zero normal traction; its compression normal to that edge cannot stay `-P` right up to the boundary. Interface shear must transfer the core's membrane force over an edge region. If both skins transfer `P t_c` per unit width over length `ell`, then the maximum magnitude of at least one interface shear stress is at least `P t_c/(2 ell)`. This is a force-transfer bound, not a general solution for the boundary layer. Protected inactive margins, edge wraps or a frame can address the edge, but their area and failure routes are additional costs. Asymmetric skins generally generate curvature and a through-thickness stress gradient, rather than the claimed uniform compressed core.

## 3. Circular rings and tiled meshes

For a disk radius `a` and same-height ring outer radius `b`, ideal frictionless shrink contact at pressure `P` yields the classical plane-stress solution

\[
\sigma_{rr}^{c}=\sigma_{\theta\theta}^{c}=-P,\quad
\sigma_{rr}^{r}=A-B/\rho^2,\quad
\sigma_{\theta\theta}^{r}=A+B/\rho^2,\quad
A=\frac{Pa^2}{b^2-a^2},\quad B=Ab^2.
\]

With `f=a^2/b^2`, peak ring hoop tension is `H=P(1+f)/(1-f)`. A **principal-tension** allowable `S` gives `f <= (S-P)/(S+P)`, requiring `S>P`. The radial interference must satisfy

\[
\delta/a=P\left[\frac{1-\nu_c}{E_c}+
\frac{(1+f)/(1-f)+\nu_r}{E_r}\right].
\]

This compatibility is exact for the stated plane-stress/frictionless model. A fully bonded radial interface must reconcile different core/ring Poisson thickness strains and introduces a three-dimensional correction. A shape-memory ring has a nonlinear constitutive law; inserting an illustrative constant `E_r` is not a calibrated model of the published NiTiNb ring.

At `P=0.330 GPa`, principal allowables `S=1,2,5 GPa` permit core fractions at most `0.504,0.717,0.876`. Even ideal hexagonal packing of separate outer circles multiplies those fractions by `pi/(2 sqrt(3))=0.907`, before margins/seams. An ion-blocking ring therefore trades active area for retained load. Shrinking all tiles at fixed shape/strength does not remove this fraction penalty. Taller rings can trade in-plane dead area for stack volume and introduce three-dimensional load transfer.

A uniaxial ductile yield stress must not be substituted for `S`. The inner ring has principal stresses `(-P,H,0)`, hence `sigma_VM=sqrt(H^2+PH+P^2)`. If the yield allowable is `Y`, the hoop allowable becomes `(sqrt(4Y^2-3P^2)-P)/2`; a positive core fraction needs `Y>sqrt(3)P`. For illustrative `Y=2 GPa`, the fraction limit is about `0.692`, rather than `0.717` obtained by treating `2 GPa` as an allowed principal tension.

**A counterexample to an inexpensive polygonal tensile mesh:** take a flexible, frictionless, constant-tension loop around a square tile. Local force balance gives normal line load `q=T curvature`. Straight faces have zero curvature, so the loop supplies no distributed normal pressure there; forces concentrate at the corners. Uniform contact pressure requires constant curvature in this model. A square/hexagonal grid must therefore use bending-stiff members, distributed bonding, or a load-spreading interphase to approximate the circular pressure field. Those changes require their own material fraction, local stress and creep calculation. The counterexample excludes the flexible-loop shortcut; it does not exclude every rigid-frame design.

Tiling also creates a connected network of seams. A well-compressed tile interior does not certify an unstressed seam, ring/core interface, seal or electrode-facing coating against lithium penetration. Electronically conducting reinforcement that contacts both electrodes creates a short even if the ceramic is perfect. An insulating coating must remain intact under assembly strain, radial contact, chemistry and cycling; it cannot be priced as an ideal zero-thickness boundary.

## 4. Buckling, delamination and relaxation

An open compressed tile must resist out-of-plane instability. For an isotropic **simply supported square** of side `L` and thickness `t_c`, equal biaxial membrane compression gives

\[
P_{\rm cr}=\frac{\pi^2E_c}{6(1-\nu_c^2)}(t_c/L)^2.
\]

This follows from the exact first sinusoidal mode of the Kirchhoff plate equation. With the illustrative core above, `P_cr=330 MPa` at `L=0.565 mm`; a `1 mm` square has `P_cr=105 MPa`, and a `10 mm` square `1.05 MPa`. These numbers are not disk/ring buckling predictions. Frictionless radial ring contact supplies no transverse shear support, so a simply supported or clamped plate edge cannot be assumed merely from shrink fitting. Bonding or electrode support may change the result and must be specified.

For an infinite compressed plate on a static **bilateral** Winkler foundation, `N_cr=min_q(D q^2+k/q^2)=2 sqrt(Dk)`, where `D=E_c t_c^3/[12(1-nu_c^2)]`. The example would require `k >= 1.02e11 N/m^3` to stabilize `330 MPa`. Unilateral electrode contact, separation and time-dependent flow cannot automatically be credited with this bilateral stiffness. This calculation supplies a testable support requirement, not a statement that real lithium contacts meet or fail it.

Conversely, applying the compressed-core Euler load to a perfectly bonded **symmetric laminate** is incorrect. When all layers follow the same transverse displacement, the leading geometric stiffness depends on the total membrane resultant; the tensile skins balance the compressed core, giving `N_total=0`. The uniform laminate therefore lacks that whole-plate Euler term in classical plate theory. This does not certify local skin wrinkling, three-dimensional instability, a debonded compressed patch, or interface fracture. A debond can remove exactly the stabilizing tensile-layer constraint.

Relaxation is a separate gate even if initial stress and stability pass. For example, model the skins as a biaxial Maxwell material with elastic modulus `M_r` and viscosity `eta_r`, so their creep rate is `S/eta_r`, while the core is elastic. At fixed manufactured mismatch,

\[
\dot P=-P/[\eta_r(r/M_c+1/M_r)],\quad
P(t)=P(0)e^{-t/\tau},\quad
\tau=\eta_r(r/M_c+1/M_r).
\]

Retaining `90%` of the initial compression over service time `t_s` requires `tau >= 9.49 t_s` in this model. This is an exact result of the stated constitutive example, not a prediction for LLZO, NiTiNb or any selected skin. Interface creep, corrosion, fatigue and thermal cycling can instead control retention. Stress must be mapped after assembly and during relevant cycling, rather than inferred permanently from the initial interference.

## 5. Chemistry, comparison and falsifiers

No reinforcement or bond has yet been selected with simultaneously qualified tensile stress/strain, ionic transport where required, electron blocking, lithium compatibility and lifetime. Mechanically useful compression does not itself establish chemical stability. The companion [Fincher et al. primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10279-z), as reconstructed in `scientific_obstruction/OBSTRUCTION.md`, reports current-dependent corrosion-assisted crack growth. Its implication here is limited: a mechanics-only threshold cannot be transferred across cycling regimes without checking crack-tip chemistry.

The strongest baseline is the implemented retained-ring Cui cell, not an unsupported bare pellet. It reports a short-capacity sweep to `100 mA/cm^2` and separate long cycling at `10 mA/cm^2`, `1 mAh/cm^2`. An improved manufacturing claim must match thickness, chemistry, device active area, current, areal capacity, cycle count and operating temperature, while charging reinforcement volume, insulation, seals, interface resistance, processing, discarded units and retained stress. Existing related operations also include [thermally induced compression, Joule (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2025.102232) and [ion-exchanged LLZTO surface compression, Advanced Energy Materials (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.71296). Publisher search excerpts establish these as relevant comparators; this worker did not acquire their full methods.

Broad prestressed mesh/skin novelty is also unsupported: [US9954213B2 (2018)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9954213B2/en), description around its prestressed-layer/mesh embodiments, expressly discloses prestressed high-strength and shape-memory layers, meshes, and ionic material in apertures. This is a primary disclosure, not proof that its examples meet the present biaxial LLZTO contract, and no patent-validity or freedom-to-operate conclusion is made.

Decisive gates for a narrower improvement are:

1. Map both in-plane principal stresses throughout active core, edges and seams after assembly, temperature excursions and the specified life. Failing the minimum retained compression rejects the proposed transferred operation even if the mean stress passes.
2. Measure through-plane ASR and electronic leakage on the **complete assembly**, at matched active area. Passing a ceramic-only conductivity measurement is insufficient.
3. Resolve actual transverse supports and interface fracture/creep. An instability, seam short, coating breach, or load-path failure rejects the architecture before a ceramic-interior crack-steering benefit can establish device improvement.
4. Cycle the complete assembly against Cui's retained-ring baseline under matched capacity and duration, then at the claimed practical regime. No such comparison or new device result was obtained here.

**Outcome:** no new transformative separator mechanism survives this branch. A laminate or tiled frame remains a conditional engineering variant; the calculations identify expensive simultaneous requirements rather than a demonstrated advantage.
