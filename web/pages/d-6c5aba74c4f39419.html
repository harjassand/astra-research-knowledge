# Cycle 3: PGCC reservoirs, resistant-daughter bursts, and model discrimination

## Decision

The primary study establishes a plausible induced reservoir and directly visualizes noncanonical daughter production, but it does not identify a PGCC burst rate, a daughter-count distribution, or the probability that one imaged daughter becomes a durable resistant founder. Its reported culture-level enrichment cannot be substituted for any of those hazards. A discriminating lineage assay is feasible in vitro, but the published lineage experiment is too sparsely denominated and too short for estimating the needed rates.

The strongest test is two linked, predeclared comparisons:

1. Count independently clonogenic, rechallenge-resistant daughters per individually tracked PGCC and test a tail of the offspring law against a mean-and-variance-matched non-burst law.
2. From one confirmed resistant daughter founder at a time, compare fixed-horizon lineage survival under an exact birth-death branching process and its moment-matched nonnegative Feller diffusion.

Both comparisons are conditional on a defined cell line, induction schedule, recovery window, drug challenge, and resistance assay. Neither is a clinical protocol or a fitted prediction about patients.

## What the primary study measured

Zhang et al. induced Hey ovarian-cancer PGCCs with 50 μM olaparib for seven days and withdrew drug for up to ten days. PGCCs were identified by DNA content greater than 4C. The reported Hey-cell PGCC fraction was 1.05% without treatment and 34.4% after seven days of olaparib; the fraction fell during drug-free recovery. This is a prevalence time course, not an induction, survival, or burst hazard: transitions, deaths, cell divisions, and changing denominators all affect the fraction.

For lineage imaging, Hey cells were labeled with FUCCI and, in separate movies, H2B-mCherry and EGFP-tubulin. Fluorescent Hey cells or Hey PGCCs were plated at 1,000 cells per well on a glass-bottom plate; a Lionheart FX system imaged every 15 minutes at 10×, 37°C and 5% CO₂, for up to one week. Movies show endoreplication during exposure and multiple post-withdrawal outcomes. One example shows a tripolar event producing three daughters, two of which re-fuse; another shows divisions at movie time codes 1:30, 36:30, and 64:45. These are examples, not event-time estimates or a burst-size sample.

The resistant-daughter experiment is suggestive but not a source-rate assay. The authors established four daughter subclones from a single PGCC. All four had varying resistance to olaparib-induced cell death compared with parental Hey cells. The assay exposed cells to 25–400 μM olaparib for seven days and read propidium-iodide (PI) death. The four subclones are not four independent PGCC mothers; PI death response is not equivalent to durable clonogenic outgrowth. The study does not report a denominator of independently followed PGCC mothers, the number of PGCCs that burst, the distribution of daughter counts per mother, or the fraction of daughters that both clonally establish and retain resistance after a standardized washout/rechallenge.

The imaging horizon is also shorter than the recovery horizon: the culture recovery was followed up to day 10, but the stated live-imaging window was up to one week. Events after imaging stops are right-censored, not non-bursts. Plating 1,000 cells or PGCCs per well does not establish 1,000 independent, continuously tracked founder lineages.

## Minimal state-reservoir model and bookkeeping

Use separate cell types for ordinary drug-sensitive cells S, transient polyploid persisters P, and stable resistant lineages R. Treatment can move S into P at rate a(t); P can die at rate dP, revert to S, or undergo a delayed reproductive event. At a P reproductive event, the parent is replaced by a random joint offspring vector (KS, KP, KR) with PGF

    fP(zS,zP,zR) = E[zS^KS zP^KP zR^KR].

This allows daughters to differ in state and retains their dependence through the joint offspring vector. A P burst is an additional source of R daughters only to the extent that its KR component is positive and those daughters pass an operational resistance assay.

Stable resistance should be represented as the distinct heritable type R, with its own drug-dependent birth, death, and state-transition rates. Pre-existing R at treatment start, new S-to-R mutation/transition, and P-derived R daughters are distinct source terms. Do not fold them into a single “persister survival” coefficient or count a P-derived R daughter again as an ordinary S birth. If P produces transiently tolerant but not stably resistant daughters, those are separate offspring types and must not be called R.

For a homogeneous, independent-lineage continuous-time branching model, event timing and the complete event-specific offspring PGFs are sufficient inputs. The PGCC interpretation is an expanded state model, not a new branching-process equation. A shared environment or a maternal latent factor coupling sibling fates would require that factor in the type/state or an explicit dependence model.

## Exact, moment-matched offspring-tail prediction

Let Y be the number of daughter lineages from one image-verified PGCC that (i) are tracked to independent founders, (ii) pass a prespecified clonogenic outgrowth criterion, and (iii) retain a prespecified olaparib-resistance phenotype after the same washout/rechallenge protocol. A visible bud or tripolar daughter alone does not count as Y.

Two illustrative offspring laws, chosen for an assay calculation and not estimated from Zhang et al., are:

| Law | P(Y=0) | P(Y=1) | P(Y=2) | P(Y=3) | E[Y] | Var(Y) | P(Y≥2) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Burst A | 0.95 | 0 | 0.05 | 0 | 0.10 | 0.19 | 0.0500 |
| Dispersed B | 0.93333 | 0.0500 | 0 | 0.01667 | 0.10 | 0.19 | 0.01667 |

The laws match exactly on the mean and variance of resistant daughter counts but differ threefold in the prespecified upper-tail probability. Their PGFs are

    hA(s) = 0.95 + 0.05 s²,
    hB(s) = 14/15 + (1/20)s + (1/60)s³.

This is a mathematical test fixture, not a claim that either law is biologically present. It shows what event-resolved founder counts add beyond moment-only summaries. For 157 independent, completely observed PGCC mothers, an exact one-sided binomial test rejecting for at least six Y≥2 mothers has type-I error 0.04878 under B and power 0.80168 under A. This is an optimistic minimum for this large prespecified difference: it assumes known frozen probabilities, perfect classification, independent mothers, no censoring, and no well-level clustering. It is not enough to estimate a flexible burst-size distribution. Targeting 300–500 independent mothers would yield about 15–25 Burst-A events at p=0.05 and support basic timing/heterogeneity checks; actual enrollment must be inflated by the measured complete-track fraction.

The more immediate source-rate endpoint is the event probability per viable PGCC by a fixed recovery day, with death and administrative censoring retained as competing outcomes. If no burst is observed across E fully observed viable-PGCC-days, a constant-hazard Poisson model gives one-sided 95% rate upper bound approximately 3/E. For example, 3,000 viable PGCC-days only bounds the burst hazard below about 0.001 per PGCC-day after zero events; a 10-day follow-up of 300 mothers reaches that exposure only if all 300 remain observed and at risk throughout. It says nothing about the daughter-resistance probability unless daughters are also followed.

## Exact birth-death branching versus Feller diffusion

After a daughter has passed the fixed resistance assay, define its post-challenge lineage as one founder with per-cell birth rate bR and death rate dR under the same fixed in-vitro exposure. Let N(T)>0 mean at least one viable descendant remains at horizon T. The exact linear birth-death branching extinction probability from one founder is

    qBD(T) = dR(1 - exp[-rT]) / (bR - dR exp[-rT]),  r=bR-dR,

with critical-case limit qBD(T)=bR T/(1+bR T) when r=0. From K independent daughter founders, exact extinction is qBD(T)^K.

The nonnegative Feller diffusion with the same infinitesimal drift and variance,

    dX = r X dt + sqrt((bR+dR) X) dW,

has extinction mass by T, starting at X(0)=K,

    qF(T) = exp{-2 r K / [(bR+dR)(1-exp(-rT))]},

with critical-case limit exp{-2K/[(bR+dR)T]}. This is the absorbing Feller branching diffusion, not an Euler approximation that can wander below zero.

At the illustrative, explicitly assumed rates bR=dR=0.05 per day and T=10 days, one founder has exact branching survival 2/3=0.6667 but Feller survival 1-exp(-2)=0.8647. Starting from two independent founders, survival is 8/9=0.8889 under branching and 1-exp(-4)=0.9817 under Feller. These values are not paper-reported rates. The comparison is a concrete falsifiable prediction once bR and dR have been estimated from independent, event-coded daughter-lineage data and frozen before a held-out survival cohort. Under the illustrative one-founder case, a one-sided exact binomial test of the frozen predictions first reaches 5% size and 80% power at 29 independent founders, rejecting the branching survival probability 2/3 if at least 24 survive; its size is 0.04513 and power 0.80946 under Feller. This idealized n ignores rate-estimation uncertainty, assay error, clustering, and informative censoring. Those require a larger validation cohort or a joint likelihood/parametric-bootstrap comparison.

The relevant comparison is not “a branching model versus all diffusion models.” A fully specified branching process computes the correct discrete result, while this Feller diffusion is its first-two-rate-moment continuous-state approximation. If held-out single-founder survival agrees with the branching prediction and excludes the Feller prediction, the diffusion approximation fails at that low-count endpoint under the measured rates. If rates are not calibrated independently, or survival calls are not complete, neither model has a fair predictive test.

## Matched assay, controls, and full burden

Use one independently induced, image-verified PGCC mother per isolated microchamber/lineage position. Extend live imaging through the entire predeclared recovery horizon (10 days if testing the stated prevalence/recovery window); do not code the final three days as event-free when imaging only for the paper’s stated seven-day window. Record mother state, divisions/budding/fusion, viable mother-days, burst time, complete joint daughter counts, daughter tracking loss, and whether each lineage survives the outgrowth/rechallenge assay. Use unique lineage labels and retain death, frame gaps, field exit, and segmentation failure as competing or censored outcomes.

Controls and matching:

- Untreated, fluorescently labeled parental Hey cells establish ordinary mitotic timing and imaging-associated loss.
- Vehicle and olaparib-induced, sorted/verified non-PGCC survivors distinguish a PGCC-specific outcome from ordinary surviving cells; stratify by DNA content and cell-cycle state instead of comparing a selected PGCC cohort with an unselected bulk.
- Maintain one induced PGCC arm in continued drug and one after withdrawal to check whether daughter production depends on withdrawal; both arms use the same imaging cadence, density, and medium.
- Challenge parental Hey cells, PGCC-derived daughters, and non-PGCC survivor daughters with the same prespecified concentration and duration, then score both immediate death and clonogenic regrowth after washout. A PI assay alone cannot certify a viable resistant founder.
- Treat independently induced mothers, not multiple daughters or subclones from one mother, as independent units. The four Zhang daughter subclones from a single PGCC cannot estimate mother-to-mother burst probability.
- Freeze event definitions, resistance threshold, event-call sensitivity/specificity, state strata, candidate laws, bR/dR estimates, and the held-out test before examining validation cohorts.

The burden is substantial but technically plausible in culture: seven days of induction, up to ten days of continuous recovery imaging, daughter isolation/expansion, and a seven-day rechallenge/outgrowth assay. Fifteen-minute sampling gives 672 frames per imaging position over seven days and 960 over ten days. For 157 single-mother positions at ten days that is 150,720 position-timepoints before fluorescence channels and focus/QC; 300–500 positions imply 288,000–480,000 position-timepoints. Many mothers can be imaged per field only if individual tracks remain unambiguous. The actual study’s 1,000 cells per well is not a demonstrated one-mother-per-position throughput or tracking-success rate. Include separately derived parent and daughter cultures, plate/well replicates, positive/negative call controls, and outgrowth validation; no monetary cost is inferred.

## Feasibility judgment and falsifiers

Qualitative replication of endoreplication, multipolar division, and daughter formation is supported by the primary movies and appears feasible in the reported cell-line setting. Quantitative risk calibration is currently no-go from the publication because the PGCC-mother denominator and mother-level event records are missing, the resistant subclones share one PGCC mother, daughter resistance is measured by PI death rather than durable founder establishment, and the stated live imaging ends before the maximum recovery horizon.

The assay is feasible as a new, event-resolved culture study if it can enroll hundreds of independent mothers, maintain unambiguous tracks through recovery, and measure clonogenic resistance in each daughter lineage. The 157-mother number only supports the specified, large moment-matched tail contrast under ideal assumptions; the controlled estimate of burst hazard and resistance establishment likely needs the larger cohort and explicit competing-risk/censoring analysis. If PGCCs are too rare after induction, or track completion falls sharply during the giant-cell stage, the correct conclusion is a precision/censoring limit, not zero burst risk.

Predeclared falsifiers:

1. The burst mechanism is not quantitatively distinguishable from the dispersed offspring alternative if the held-out, mother-level P(Y≥2) interval excludes the planned threefold difference, after assay sensitivity and missingness are accounted for.
2. The resistance-source interpretation fails if visible daughters do not produce independently clonogenic, rechallenge-resistant lineages at a rate above matched non-PGCC survivors.
3. The Feller approximation is not rejected if held-out survival is compatible with both predictions; it is rejected at this endpoint only if calibrated exact branching survival is supported and the Feller survival prediction is excluded under independently estimated rates.
4. Zero observed events do not establish zero risk. With event-dependent dropout, report a worst-case interval that treats unresolved mothers/daughters as possible bursts/resistant founders, or retain the rate as unidentified.
