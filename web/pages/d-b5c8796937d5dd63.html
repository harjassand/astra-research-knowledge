# Result: a negative answer to a published positive-map problem

I redirected away from the previous bound-entangled state family. The strongest outcome is a **complete probabilistic proof of a negative answer to a published open question**, together with a general theorem on the decomposition complexity of PPT maps:

\[
\boxed{\text{For every finite }r,\text{ there exist indecomposable }r\text{-entanglement-breaking maps.}}
\]

The case \(r=2\) answers the question posed by Devendra, Mallick and Sumesh in *Mapping cone of \(k\)-entanglement breaking maps*: whether every \(2\)-entanglement-breaking map is decomposable. Their definition does **not** assume complete positivity, which is essential here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.14991v2)

The proof is unconditional on the earlier Astra candidates and on the October release. **It is an internally derived mathematical result, not externally verified or historically priority-cleared.** I have not established a historic-scale consequence, and neither PPT-cubed nor NPT bound entanglement is resolved.

## 1. Why I abandoned the previous family as the main route

The earlier construction’s audited algebraic structure survived: the double-factorial spin weights, singleton-separability constraints, and singlet-pair decomposition remain consistent with the reconstructed arguments. Its arbitrary-channel theorem also has a carefully restricted conclusion: eventual entanglement survival, not necessarily a nonvanishing operational advantage under general nonunital noise. The original artifact explicitly retained that distinction. UNIVERSAL_BOUND_ENTANGLEMENT_PR…

However, the audit produced two decisive obstructions to the hoped-for larger consequences.

### A larger grouped cut is explicitly distillable

For every even \(N\ge4\), group two qubits into laboratory \(A\) and the remaining \(N-2\) into laboratory \(B\). Local projections onto spin-one sectors produce the same conditional \(3\times3\) state:

\[
B=\frac37P_0+\frac17P_1+\frac1{35}P_2,
\]

where \(P_j\) denotes the total-spin-\(j\) projector for two spin-one systems.

The exact partial-transpose spectrum is

\[
\operatorname{spec}(B^\Gamma)
=
\left\{
\frac1{21}\;(1\text{ time}),
-\frac1{21}\;(3\text{ times}),
\frac{23}{105}\;(5\text{ times})
\right\}.
\]

More importantly, the Schmidt-rank-two vector

\[
v=\frac{|+1,0\rangle+|0,-1\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\]

satisfies

\[
\langle v,B^\Gamma v\rangle=-\frac1{21}.
\]

Thus one copy can be locally filtered to an entangled two-qubit state across that grouped cut. This is *one-copy distillability* in the standard sense—not production of a pure Bell pair from one copy.

This does not contradict singleton separability. It does show that this natural larger cut does **not** provide bipartite NPT bound entanglement.

### Two-producibility blocks the higher-tensor argument

There is also a general obstruction independent of the coefficients.

Suppose a map \(Q\) and \(Q\otimes Q\) are positive. Then applying \(Q\) to every site of **any two-producible state** gives a positive operator: apply positivity to each one- or two-site block, then tensor and sum.

Consequently, the previous family cannot test failures of tensor positivity that require genuinely larger clusters once one- and two-factor positivity are known. Its universal noise-survival property does not remove the unrestricted-input obstacle in tensor-stable positivity.

A separate exact calculation also blocks naive regularization of its single-copy entropy argument. At \(N=4\), a product across laboratories of local inter-copy Bell states gives

\[
\langle P_0\otimes P_0\rangle=\frac18,
\]

while the squared single-copy separable maximum is

\[
\left(\frac13\right)^2=\frac19.
\]

The relevant single-copy bound therefore does not multiply against the allowed cross-copy alternatives.

**Decision:** the previous family remains mathematically interesting, but its established mechanisms did not supply a credible direct path to the much larger problems being targeted. Continuing to improve its noise thresholds would not address these obstructions.

## 2. The new general theorem

The successful redirect concerns the geometry of positive maps.

A linear map \(P:M_d(\mathbb C)\to M_d(\mathbb C)\) is **\(r\)-entanglement breaking** when

\[
(\operatorname{id}_r\otimes P)(X)
\]

is separable for **every** positive semidefinite operator \(X\) on \(\mathbb C^r\otimes\mathbb C^d\). This is stronger than merely requiring that output to be positive or PPT. It is the finite-reference definition used in the published question. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.14991v2)

A map is **decomposable** when

\[
P=P_1+T\circ P_2
\]

for completely positive maps \(P_1,P_2\), with \(T\) the ordinary matrix transpose.

### Theorem A — finite-reference entanglement breaking does not imply decomposability

For every integer \(r\ge1\) and every

\[
d\ge 2^{23}r^3,
\]

there exists a complex-linear map \(P:M_d\to M_d\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
P\text{ is unital and trace preserving},\\
P\circ T=P=T\circ P,\\
P\text{ and }P^*\text{ are }r\text{-entanglement breaking},\\
P\text{ is indecomposable}.
\end{gathered}}
\]

Here \(P^*\) is the Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint.

The separation is quantitative:

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{D\ \mathrm{decomposable}}
\|P-D\|_\diamond
\ge\frac6{55}.
}
\]

Thus these maps are not merely infinitesimally outside the decomposable cone.

**These \(P\) are not physical quantum channels.** They are positive but not completely positive. The diamond norm is being used as a mathematical norm on linear maps, not as an implementable channel-discrimination experiment.

For \(r=2\), the conservative sufficient dimension is

\[
d=2^{26}=67{,}108{,}864.
\]

No matrix of that size was generated. The construction specifies a Gaussian ensemble and proves a positive probability of satisfying all the required universal inequalities. Rational examples also exist by strictness of the inequalities and density of rational coefficients.

The order of quantifiers matters: there is a suitable map for every finite reference dimension. This is not one fixed map that is entanglement breaking for references of every dimension.

## 3. The decisive proof

The full proof contains explicit probability bounds and normalization checks. Its central mechanism is:

> Construct a matrix that is weak on every low-Schmidt-rank direction, but still has enough total quadratic mass to separate a PPT state. Then strengthen positivity of its small-reference compressions to actual separability.

### A. Build partial-transpose symmetry into the random matrix

Let \(V_d\) be the real vector space of traceless real symmetric \(d\times d\) matrices. Its dimension is

\[
h=\frac{d(d+1)}2-1.
\]

Choose a Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal basis \(S_1,\ldots,S_h\), let \(g_{ab}\) be independent standard real Gaussian variables, and define

\[
G=\frac1d\sum_{a,b=1}^{h}g_{ab}S_a\otimes S_b.
\]

This construction gives exact identities:

\[
G^\Gamma=G,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}_A G=\operatorname{Tr}_B G=0.
\]

It is also invariant under partial transpose on the other factor.

Define the restricted bilinear norm

\[
q_r(G)=
\sup_{\substack{\|x\|=\|y\|=1\\
\operatorname{SR}(x),\operatorname{SR}(y)\le r}}
|\langle x,Gy\rangle|.
\]

The vectors here are **complex**, even though the coefficients of \(G\) are real.

The proof establishes

\[
\Pr\{q_r(G)>\eta\}
\le
4\exp\left(36dr-\frac{d^2\eta^2}{16}\right).
\]

Together with a sphere-net estimate and a chi-square concentration bound, this proves that, when \(d\ge2^{23}r^3\), there are realizations satisfying simultaneously

\[
\boxed{
\|G\|_\infty<5,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(G^2)>\frac9{40}d^2,\qquad
q_r(G)<\frac1{64r}.
}
\]

The restricted-norm argument controls **all** low-rank vectors, not sampled ones. Its crucial extension step uses singular-value decompositions to prove

\[
q_{\min(2r,d)}(G)\le2q_r(G),
\]

allowing a finite rank-constrained net to control the entire continuum without a dimension-dependent logarithmic loss.

### B. Construct a PPT state and an indecomposable witness

Set

\[
\rho=\frac{I+G/6}{d^2},
\qquad
W=I-32G.
\]

The global norm bound gives

\[
\frac{I}{6d^2}\preceq\rho\preceq\frac{11I}{6d^2}.
\]

The exact symmetries give

\[
\rho^\Gamma=\rho,\qquad
\rho_A=\rho_B=\frac{I_d}{d}.
\]

Thus \(\rho\) is a full-rank PPT density matrix with maximally mixed marginals.

Yet

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(W\rho)
=
1-\frac{32}{6d^2}\operatorname{Tr}(G^2)
<
-\frac15.
\]

Define \(P\) by its input-first Choi matrix:

\[
J(P)=\frac Wd.
\]

Every decomposable Choi matrix has nonnegative pairing with every PPT state. The negative pairing above therefore proves that \(P\) is indecomposable.

The zero partial traces of \(G\) make \(P\) exactly unital and trace preserving.

This does not yet prove the strongest claim. We must establish **separability of every bounded-reference output**, not just positivity.

### C. The separable-compression lemma

The missing step is an elementary dimension-asymmetric separable ball.

**Lemma.** If \(E=E^*\) acts on \(\mathbb C^s\otimes\mathbb C^b\) and

\[
\|E\|_\infty\le\frac1{2s-1},
\]

then \(I+E\) is separable.

The important feature is that the second dimension \(b\) does not enter the bound.

The proof is explicit. Write \(E\) as an \(s\times s\) block matrix. For every off-diagonal block, separate its Hermitian and skew-Hermitian parts. Each can be absorbed into a sum of positive tensor products by allocating a controlled amount of diagonal identity. The remaining diagonal blocks are positive because

\[
1-(2s-1)\|E\|_\infty\ge0.
\]

No commutativity of the blocks is assumed. The radius is not claimed optimal or historically new.

Now take any complex isometry \(U:\mathbb C^s\to\mathbb C^d\), with \(s\le r\). Every vector in the range of \(U\otimes I\) has Schmidt rank at most \(r\). Therefore

\[
\left\|(U^*\otimes I)G(U\otimes I)\right\|_\infty
\le q_r(G).
\]

The corresponding compression of \(W\) is

\[
(U^*\otimes I)W(U\otimes I)
=
I+E_U,
\]

with

\[
\|E_U\|_\infty
\le32q_r(G)
<
\frac1{2r}
\le\frac1{2s-1}.
\]

Hence **every such compression is separable**.

Every pure input on \(\mathbb C^r\otimes\mathbb C^d\) is a local-filtered maximally entangled vector supported on one of these subspaces. The Choi identity then expresses its output under \(\operatorname{id}_r\otimes P\) as a local filtering of the corresponding separable compression. Mixed positive inputs follow by summation.

This proves \(r\)-entanglement breaking. Swapping the two tensor factors gives the same property for \(P^*\).

### D. Robust indecomposability

The witness calculation also gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho J(P)]\le-\frac1{5d},
\qquad
\|\rho\|_\infty\le\frac{11}{6d^2}.
\]

For any decomposable \(D\),

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho J(D)]\ge0.
\]

Trace-norm duality therefore yields

\[
\|J(P)-J(D)\|_1\ge\frac{6d}{55}.
\]

Evaluating the stabilized map norm on a normalized maximally entangled input gives the stated diamond-norm gap \(6/55\).

## 4. A second theorem: PPT decomposition complexity grows linearly

The same construction resolves the asymptotic scale of a related question.

Let \(\mathcal S_k\) be the cone of positive operators with Schmidt number at most \(k\), and define

\[
\mathcal D_k=\mathcal S_k+\Gamma(\mathcal S_k).
\]

This allows a low-Schmidt positive term plus the partial transpose of another such term.

Its trace-one base is

\[
\mathcal C_k=
\left\{
A+B^\Gamma:
A,B\in\mathcal S_k,\ 
\operatorname{Tr}A+\operatorname{Tr}B=1
\right\}.
\]

Some elements of this base are not positive; the following is therefore a **half-trace-norm distance to a cone base**, not a claim that all comparison operators are physical states.

### Theorem B

For every \(d\ge2^{23}k\), there exists a full-rank PPT state \(\rho\), invariant under partial transpose and with maximally mixed marginals, such that

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal C_k}
\frac12\|\rho-\tau\|_1
\ge\frac7{3200}.
}
\]

Only the weaker restricted bound \(q_k(G)<1/64\) is needed here.

In map language, let

\[
K(d)=
\min\left\{
k:\mathrm{PPT}(d,d)\subseteq
\mathrm{SP}_k+T\circ\mathrm{SP}_k
\right\},
\]

where \(\mathrm{SP}_k\) consists of completely positive maps admitting Kraus operators of rank at most \(k\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\left\lfloor\frac d{2^{23}}\right\rfloor+1
\le K(d)\le d
}
\]

for \(d\ge2^{23}\). In particular,

\[
\boxed{K(d)=\Theta(d).}
\]

Park’s August 2026 manuscript asks for the smallest such \(k\), and identifies the corresponding dual simultaneous-positivity problem. This result determines the asymptotic order, **not the exact answer at each dimension**. In particular, it does not settle the separate \(d=4,k=2\) case. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13551v2)

The distinction from ordinary high Schmidt number is substantive. Park supplies examples of PPT maps with high Schmidt number that nevertheless belong to the low-level decomposable cone. A witness must control both summands simultaneously; high Schmidt number alone does not do that. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13551v2)

## 5. The strongest attempted consequence—and a proved obstruction

The route to a much larger theorem was PPT-cubed.

One proposed sufficient condition is that every PPT map belong to \(\mathrm{SP}_2+T\circ\mathrm{SP}_2\). The new theorem disproves that universal inclusion. But **disproving a sufficient condition does not disprove PPT-cubed**. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13551v2)

I examined this rather than leaving it as a verbal caveat.

Let \(\Lambda\) be the actual physical channel with

\[
J(\Lambda)=d\rho.
\]

Additional Gaussian estimates allow the construction to satisfy

\[
\|g\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le8d,
\qquad
\|g\|_F\le d^2.
\]

A direct composition calculation then gives

\[
dJ(\Lambda^2)=I+E,
\qquad
\|E\|_F\le\frac29.
\]

Gurvits and Barnum’s established bipartite separable-ball theorem states that \(I+E\) is separable whenever \(E\) is Hermitian and \(\|E\|_F\le1\). Consequently, \(\Lambda^2\) is entanglement breaking. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0204159v2)

Since \(\Lambda\) itself is not entanglement breaking, these examples have **entanglement-breaking index exactly two**.

This proves an instructive separation:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Large decomposable-Schmidt complexity can coexist with complete
entanglement loss after two iterations.}
}
\]

It also blocks the most immediate attempt to market the Gaussian construction as a PPT-cubed counterexample.

The tensor-stability route has a different unresolved gap. What is proved is

\[
\forall r\;\exists d,P:
(\operatorname{id}_r\otimes P)(X)\text{ is separable for every }X\ge0.
\]

A relevant tensor-stability breakthrough would require a **fixed nontrivial map** and positivity of every tensor power on every positive input. The maps and dimensions here change with \(r\). No passage between those quantifiers has been established.

## 6. What is new, and what is inherited

The proof uses a method with clear antecedents.

Huber, Lami, Lancien and Müller-Hermes already used identity-plus-Gaussian states and restricted Schmidt witnesses to prove high-dimensional entanglement in PPT states. Their paper also studies partial-transpose-invariant constructions. **Gaussian high-Schmidt geometry is not a discovery of this investigation.** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04975)

The proposed contribution is the stronger combination:

\[
\text{uniform complex low-rank control}
\ \Longrightarrow\
\text{separability of every small Choi compression}
\ \Longrightarrow\
r\text{-EB indecomposability},
\]

with exact transpose invariance, unital/trace-preserving normalization, and quantitative separation.

The second result applies the same simultaneous witness to the **sum** of two low-Schmidt cones, rather than only to the ordinary Schmidt-number cone.

The literature search located the named questions and the major antecedents. It did not locate these exact conclusions. However, several exact-string searches returned irrelevant results, and I did not complete an exhaustive citation-graph review. A specialist could recognize the existence theorem as an unstated consequence of existing Gaussian geometry, or locate a subsequent resolution of the published question.

Thus the defensible claim is:

**A complete proof answering the stated mathematical question has been produced here. Whether that answer is historically new remains unestablished.**

## 7. Verification and reproducibility

The verification was written and executed during this investigation. No finite numerical test is used in place of the universal Gaussian-net proof.

| Check | Executed scope |
|---|---|
| Gaussian subspace and normalization | 8 basis dimensions; 32 comparisons of two projection formulas; 24 Gaussian fixtures |
| Restricted-rank diagnostics | 2,760 sampled complex bilinear pairs—explicitly lower bounds, not certificates |
| Explicit separable-ball decomposition | 100 fixtures with varying reference and output dimensions |
| Choi and local-filter identities | 20 fixtures, including complex isometries |
| Rank-net extension algebra | 120 SVD rank-splitting checks; exact rational constant checks |
| Prior-family audit | Dense \(N=2,4,6,8\); exact four-qubit spectra; exact spin-one branch; cross-copy counterexamples |
| Channel-composition obstruction | 24 direct composition/Choi checks |

The largest recorded algebraic discrepancy across the main Gaussian tests was below \(7\times10^{-13}\). The direct channel-composition discrepancies were below \(2\times10^{-15}\). The designated old-family certificates and numerical constants were checked with exact symbolic or rational arithmetic.

**No theorem-sized matrix was produced.** In particular, the small-dimensional sampled-rank tests do not certify an indecomposable \(2\)-EB example at those small dimensions. The existence assertion comes from the analytic probability bounds.

## 8. Final classification and research decision

| Category | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Historic-scale or major foundational problem resolved | **Not established.** PPT-cubed, NPT bound entanglement, and nontrivial all-tensor positivity remain unresolved here. |
| Specific published open question | **Negative answer supplied by the complete internal proof:** not every \(2\)-EB map is decomposable. |
| General theorem | Indecomposable \(r\)-EB maps for every finite \(r\), with robust norm separation; linear-order PPT decomposition complexity. |
| Conditional on unverified earlier work | **No.** The main proof does not assume an Astra candidate or an October-release theorem. |
| External correctness and historical novelty | Still require specialist checking and priority review. |
| Practical advantage | None proved. The sufficient dimensions are enormous, and the principal positive maps are not physical channels. |

The central decision is therefore **not to keep treating the original state family as the main path to a 10/10 discovery**. The redirect produced a substantially different theorem and a proposed solution to a named open question, but its immediate bridges to the larger problems encounter explicit, now-proved obstructions.

The highest-value continuation is not another constant improvement. It is a mechanism that controls a **fixed** map under all tensor powers, or constructs an actual non-entanglement-breaking composition of three physical PPT maps. The present work supplies neither, and the index-two calculation explains why its physical Gaussian channels are not ready-made examples.
