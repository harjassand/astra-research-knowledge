## Exact interface and parameters

Input integers are `T>=1` and `B>=0`. The precision cost is charged in B, rather than in the binary encoding length of B: writing B-bit coordinates may require that much work. Put

    s=T+1,  q=600s,  N=2q²=720000s²,  h=12/q,
    L=ceil(log_2 s),
    I_0=20+10L,
    I=I_0+ceil(log_2(B+1)),
    A_I=1+I+(16/3)sI,
    delta=min{2^-24 s^-8,  2^-B/(32s),  h/(10000 A_I)}.   (B1)

The ceilings are integer operations; delta is an explicitly given positive rational. A dyadic implementation may replace it by `2^-ceil(log_2(1/delta))`, which lies in `[delta/2,delta]`, and use that smaller value throughout. The ceilings can be obtained by integer bit lengths and rational comparisons without transcendental oracles.

Use precisely the real shifted initializer (P3), including both moved first poles. Acquire its initial distinct rational unit reset within geodesic distance delta of those real nodes. Execute I exact minimum-Euclidean tangent corrections, exact rational Cayley retractions, and distinct rational unit resets, each within geodesic distance delta of the Cayley output. The initialization acquisition error and any initial distinctness perturbation together must fit in its one delta budget.

The output is N distinct rational points on S², with normalized harmonic mean vector through T satisfying

    |N^-1 sum_i e(x_i)| < 2^-B/4 < 2^-B,
    N^-1 sum_i e(x_i)e(x_i)^t >= (13/19) I_M.           (B2)

It also retains the old mean bound `beta_T/16`, since `I>=I_0` and delta is at most the old tolerance. No claim that the rational points are exact spherical designs is made.

## Basin and travel for every precision input

These are the full discrete normal-matrix inequalities (P22)–(P24); no continuum substitution and no structural assumption on later residuals is used.

The same linear envelope remains valid for an arbitrary finite number of steps:

    zeta_j <= 4^-j Z_0 + (8/3)delta.                  (B4)

Indeed `Z_0+(8/3)delta < 1/(8s²)`, so the quadratic term in (B3) is at most `zeta_j/4`. The sum of all Cayley lengths and all resets, including the real prescribed polar shift from the true old centroid anchors, is at most

By the third term in (B1), this is strictly below

To avoid circularity, use joint induction: the partial travel bound is no larger than the displayed full bound, so the current sampled normal matrix has the required positive definiteness; (B3) bounds its next displacement and residual; the next partial travel stays inside h. ## Quadratic precision estimate and exact correction count

The linear envelope (B4) supplies the travel estimate. A separate sharper envelope supplies the accuracy with only logarithmically many additional corrections.

Set

    a=2s²,  u_j=a zeta_j,  d=a delta,
    t_0=1/15000,
    t_(j+1)=2t_j²,
    t_j=(1/2)(1/7500)^(2^j).                          (B6)

Then (B3) is `u_(j+1)<=u_j²+2d`. Since `s>=2` and delta is at most the old tolerance,

    d<=2^-23 s^-6<=2^-29,
    8t_0+16d+2 < 4.                                  (B7)

We claim `u_j<=t_j+4d` for all j. At j=0 the stronger `u_0<=t_0` holds. If the claim holds, `t_j<=t_0`, and

    u_(j+1) <= (t_j+4d)²+2d
             = t_j²+(8t_j+16d+2)d
             <= 2t_j²+4d = t_(j+1)+4d.

Hence the actual residual, including every reset, satisfies

    zeta_j <= (1/(4s²))(1/7500)^(2^j)+4delta.         (B8)

The mean-vector conversion is `|b_j|<=sqrt(T(T+1))zeta_j<s zeta_j`. Because `7500>2^12` and `2^I>=B+1`,

    s zeta_I <= (1/(4s)) 2^(-12*2^I)+4s delta
              <= (1/(4s)) 2^[-12(B+1)] + 2^-B/8
              < 2^-B/4.                             (B9)

This proves the claimed accuracy. The surplus `I_0` preserves the already accepted fixed-beta interface without optimizing constants. The exact number I is `O(log(T+1)+log(B+1))`; this is a consequence of the quadratic reset envelope, not of finite numerical descent.

The Gram estimate in (B2) follows from the same signed sampling bound for P² of degree at most 2T, since the final nodes remain in their old anchor balls. Rational Cayley updates give exact unit length; the deterministic reset handles distinctness.

## Rational acquisition and complete cost boundary

For (B1),

    log_2(1/delta)
       <= C + B + 8 log_2 s + log_2(I+1)
       = O(B+log(T+1)).                              (B10)

This follows by taking the maximum of the logarithms of the three reciprocals; `A_I<=1+7s(I+1)` and `1/h=50s`. With `I=I_0+ceil(log_2(B+1))`, the term `log(I+1)` is also at most a constant plus `B+log s`. Thus each reset chart/grid coordinate has `O(log(N/delta))=O(B+log(T+1))` bits. The smaller dyadic tolerance has the same height order.

The existing certified centroid series, Machin pi calculation, trigonometric intervals, and square-root guards acquire the shifted initializer at `O(B+log T)` requested precision, with O(log q) additional guard bits. The lower bounds on north chart denominators and transverse radii in the full proof still hold, independently of B. Every real acquisition error is paid inside the initial delta budget.

In the rational orthogonal harmonic basis, degree-T evaluations and gradients at reset nodes have height `O(T[B+log(T+1)])`, plus the existing `O(T log(T+1))` basis coefficients. Summing over N potentially differing denominators is charged up to `O(NT[B+log(T+1)])` input height. Each exact normal matrix uses `O(NM²)` rational arithmetic operations, and each exact solve `O(M³)`, where `M=T(T+2)`. Clearing denominators and determinant bounds keep all transient matrix, solve and Cayley heights polynomial in T and B; their cost is not replaced by output-coordinate height. Every reset restores the small node input height for the next solve.

The specified distinct grid spacing `delta/(16N)` and at most N+1 candidate shifts incur at most O(N³) naive rational equality comparisons per reset. All candidate comparisons and initializer acquisition are charged. There are the explicitly given I corrections and I+1 resets. Therefore the total deterministic bit time is polynomial in `(T,B)`, and the final point coordinates have `O(B+log(T+1))` bits. The theorem does not assert a practical polynomial exponent, compact normal matrices or an executed universal-cardinality loop.
