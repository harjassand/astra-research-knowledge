Question: does the release's common resolvent purification and exact answer transport require finite-dimensional tensor-product strategies, or can it be used for commuting-operator strategies? The latter allow an arbitrary Hilbert space H with vector psi and Alice/Bob POVMs in commuting operator algebras. Passing to their von Neumann closures preserves commutation and enlarges the allowed measurements within the same commuting model. Let these closures be A and B, so A subset B'.

## 1. Common purification in an arbitrary Hilbert space

For every bounded positive effect T on H define

  F_T:H -> L2((0,infinity),ds) tensor H,
  (F_T h)(s)=T(T+sI)^-1 h.

The integral of the squared scalar spectral function is t, so F_T*F_T=T as bounded operators. The map has norm <=1. No trace or finite-rank spectral decomposition is needed.

If T belongs to A, then every matrix coefficient of F_T relative to an orthonormal basis of L2 belongs to A: it is the bounded functional-calculus integral of scalar kernels times T(T+sI)^-1. This is a rectangular column over A. The row/column operators obtained from it by bounded strong limits retain that membership.

For epsilon>0, because T and T+epsilon I commute,

  ||F_(T+epsilon I)-F_T||^2
  =sup_(t in spec T) epsilon^2 int_0^infinity
       s^2/[(t+epsilon+s)^2(t+s)^2] ds
  <=epsilon^2 int_0^infinity (epsilon+s)^-2 ds=epsilon.        (1)

At t=0 the original zero map gives the same bound directly. The integral inequality uses (t+s)>=s and (t+epsilon+s)>=(epsilon+s).

For finite positive ensembles A_l and B=sum_l q_l A_l, the released Jensen inequality

  sum_l q_l(F_(A_l)-F_B)*(F_(A_l)-F_B)
      <=sum_l q_l f(A_l)-f(B), f(t)=t log t

extends to arbitrary bounded effects. First replace A_l by A_l+epsilon I and B by B+epsilon I. The proof's second resolvent identity and spectral inequality are bounded-operator identities; B+epsilon I is invertible. Both integral expressions converge in operator norm at fixed epsilon. Then use (1), the continuity of f on a compact interval including zero, and continuous functional calculus to pass to zero in operator norm. The shifted operators need not be contractions, but the purification/Jensen identities only require bounded positivity. The effect bound is retained after the limit.

## 2. Exact bounded POVM transport without an unbounded inverse

Let 0<=G_a and sum_a G_a=T. The formal expression T^-1/2 G_a T^-1/2 can be unbounded at an accumulating zero spectrum if written naively. The correct bounded object is a form contraction.

For u=T^1/2 h and v=T^1/2 k, define

  q_a(u,v)=<h,G_a k>.

This is well defined because G_a<=T kills ker T, and its absolute value is at most ||u||||v|| by Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive form G_a. It therefore extends uniquely to a bounded positive contraction K_a on closure(ran T^1/2)=P_T H, and is set to zero on ker T. It satisfies

  G_a=T^1/2 K_a T^1/2, 0<=K_a<=P_T, sum_a K_a=P_T.           (2)

Put f_epsilon(T)=(T/(T+epsilon I))^1/2. Then

  (T+epsilon I)^-1/2 G_a(T+epsilon I)^-1/2
     = f_epsilon(T) K_a f_epsilon(T) -> K_a strongly.          (3)

Since every left-hand operator belongs to A and A is strongly closed, K_a belongs to A. This also proves that the indicated strong limit actually exists.

Let F_T=V_T T^1/2 be its polar decomposition. The partial isometry can be obtained as the bounded strong limit

  V_T = SOT-lim_(epsilon->0) F_T(T+epsilon I)^-1/2.

It is a rectangular column over A, V_T*V_T=P_T. Hence V_T V_T* and all V_T K_a V_T* belong to B(L2_s) tensor_vN A. For a fixed default answer a_*, define

  Gtilde_a=V_T K_a V_T*
            +1_(a=a_*)[I-V_T V_T*].                          (4)

These are bounded positive operators summing to I in the same enlarged algebra. Equations (2)--(4) give exactly

  F_T* Gtilde_a F_T=G_a.                                    (5)

No lower spectral bound on T is assumed or charged. All zero-effect cases are covered by the default measurement.

## 3. Two parties remain commuting

Use separate scalar ancillas L2_s and L2_t for Alice and Bob. For T in A and S in B, set

  Psi_(T,S)(s,t)=T(T+sI)^-1 S(S+tI)^-1 psi

in L2_s tensor L2_t tensor H. Every entry of the first factor belongs to A, every entry of the second to B, so the factors commute. Alice's transported measurement (4) acts in B(L2_s) tensor I_t tensor_vN A. Bob's analogous measurement acts in I_s tensor B(L2_t) tensor_vN B. These two enlarged algebras commute.

With finer effects G_a in A summing to T and H_b in B summing to S, transport gives

  <Psi_(T,S),Gtilde_a Htilde_b Psi_(T,S)>
      =<psi,G_a H_b psi>.                                   (6)

To verify it, commute all Bob factors past Alice's rectangular columns and measurements, apply the Alice pullback (5), then apply the Bob pullback. Also ||Psi_(T,S)||^2=<psi,TS psi>. Both products are positive because the factors commute. This is an exact joint measurement identity, not only two separate marginal identities.

The finite common-span reduction in the release is unnecessary and generally unavailable here. The commuting strategy model admits the full enlarged Hilbert space. An arbitrary Hilbert space may be reduced to the cyclic subspace generated by finitely many bounded measurements if separability is desired.

## 4. The mixed exposure telescope has no finite-dimensional trace requirement

Every expected effect remains a bounded effect in its original algebra, because questions and answers are finite. Write chi(F,H)=<psi,FH psi>. For fixed positive H in B, F->chi(F,H) is the normal positive functional <H^1/2 psi,F H^1/2 psi>. Its mass is g<=1. Applying scalar Jensen to the spectral measure of F gives, for h=chi(F,H),

  -chi(f(F),H)<=h log(g/h)<=-h log h.

The convention is zero at h=0. Summing finite answer histories and applying the original finite-probability Jensen inequality bounds each entropy potential in [-p(log(1/p)+|C|log d),0]. The operator Jensen inequality from section 1 controls the squared differences of the double-resolvent vectors, since its operator belongs to A and commutes with H. Thus the two entropy telescopes reproduce the exact input (3) in SPECTRAL_BARYCENTER_REPETITION.md, with common Hilbert-space vectors and commuting transported POVMs.

At fixed finite questions/repetitions the flags are finite, though H and the scalar ancillas may be infinite. Inserting correlated finite flags in two separate classical registers preserves the commuting algebra model. A barycenter is a vector in the same common Hilbert space, and hence a legal shared resource, independent of the fresh questions. It need not be acquired from the original state.
