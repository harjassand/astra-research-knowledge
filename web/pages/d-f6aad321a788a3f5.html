# Acquiring a rational noncommutative witness from one exact black-box query

## Result

A useful access-level corollary follows from the pinned OpenAI construction. Given only unary bounds (n,s), one can draw a single rational matrix tuple of dimension (D=O((n+s)^{10})), submit it to an exact evaluation oracle for an unknown admissible noncommutative rational formula of size at most (s), and obtain a defined invertible witness with probability at least (1/2) whenever the formula is nonzero in the paper's operational sense. Independent trials reduce the false-negative probability to (2^{-r}) after (r) trials. A successful oracle response is itself a certificate: the formula's original syntax tree is defined there and its exact output has full rank.

This is a black-box witness-acquisition protocol, not a new universal-list theorem. It is an internally reconstructed corollary of the pinned proof's determinant-degree estimate and the finite-grid Schwartz–Zippel bound. It does not establish priority or improve known randomized polynomial-time rational identity testing. Its practical value is a precise, coefficient-height-independent query interface: one bounded-height point per attempt, with a concrete response and cost contract.

## Exact interface and statement

Use the paper's formula convention: a rooted tree with variable or rational-constant leaves, ordered addition/multiplication gates, and inverse gates; size counts every tree vertex. A formula is admissible if it has some defined evaluation by rational square matrices of a positive dimension. It is nonzero if some such defined evaluation has nonzero output. The input to the protocol is promised admissible and supplied only through a bound (n,s). The protocol does not see the tree, coefficients, inverse depth, or constant heights.

The oracle accepts an (n)-tuple (X) of rational (D\times D) matrices and returns either UNDEFINED, when some inverse occurrence in the original tree has a singular operand, or the complete exact rational matrix Φ(X). The protocol accepts (X) exactly when the returned matrix has nonzero determinant. Exact arithmetic is required. Equivalently, the oracle may return the one-bit predicate “defined and invertible,” but its computation cost must then be charged to that stronger service.

For every nonzero admissible Φ of size at most (s), one trial accepts with probability at least (1/2); for every operationally zero formula, acceptance probability is zero. After (r) independent trials, a nonzero formula is accepted with probability at least (1-2^{-r}), while an operationally zero formula is never accepted. If a trial's normalizing matrix is singular, the protocol skips the oracle query for that trial.

## Why the random point works

The pinned paper first translates the actual formula tree into at most (s+1) feasible affine-pencil constraints: one pencil for each inverse-gate occurrence and one root augmentation. Their simultaneous invertibility is equivalent to definedness of the original tree and invertibility of its final value. It adds the constant pencil (1), so the family has at most (s+2) members, all of order at most (B=2s+1). The formula's admissibility and nonzeroness imply a common feasible witness over an extension field; this is what makes each pencil determinant below a nonzero polynomial.

Set (w=n+1), Δ (=2w^3B), let (M) be the least power of two strictly greater than (BΔ), let ℓ (=M/2), and let (D=Mℓ=M^2/2). The proof constructs polynomial matrices ḼR_i(t,u,v) over the rational algebra (R=\mathbb Q[\xi]/(\xi^ℓ+1)), then maps multiplication in (R) to rational matrices (Z_i=\rho_M(\tilde R_i(t,u,v))\in\mathrm{Mat}_D(\mathbb Q)). If (Z_0) is nonsingular, normalize by (X_i=Z_0^{-1}Z_i).

For a feasible certificate pencil (L(x)=A_0+\sum_{i=1}^n A_ix_i) and every complex root ω of ξ^ℓ+1, the pinned proof establishes that

\[
P_{L,\omega}(t,u,v)=\det\!\left(\sum_{i=0}^n A_i\otimes R_i^{*,\omega}(t,u,v)\right)
\]

is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most (3BM^2). Multiply these factors over the at-most-(s+2) formula certificates and all ℓ (=M/2) roots, obtaining a nonzero (P_\Phi\in\mathbb C[t,u,v]) with

\[
d_\Phi:=\deg P_\Phi\le (s+2)(M/2)(3BM^2)=\tfrac32(s+2)BM^3.
\]

The source grid length is (G=1+3(s+2)BM^3), so (d_\Phi<(G/2)). For independent uniform coordinates in ({1,\dots,G\}), Schwartz–Zippel gives

\[
\Pr[P_\Phi(t,u,v)\ne0]\ge 1-d_\Phi/G>1/2.
\]

At any such triple, the constant-pencil factor forces (Z_0) to be nonsingular. Splitting the rational algebra over its complex roots shows every certificate pencil is nonsingular on the normalized rational tuple (X); nonsingularity over the complex extension implies the rational determinant is nonzero. The domain certificates then show that the original expression, including inverse gates in branches that later cancel, is defined and has an invertible value over ℚ. The oracle check accepts. This proves the lower bound on per-trial success without knowing (P_\Phi), any certificate, or the formula.

For exactly uniform random bits, replace (G) by (G'=2^{\lceil\log_2 G\rceil}<2G) and sample (t,u,v\) from ({1,\dots,G'\}). Since (d_\Phi<G/2\le G'/2), the same success bound holds, and each attempt uses exactly (3\log_2 G'=O(\log(n+s+1))) random bits. This samples from the parameterized construction directly; it does not enumerate or store the full list.

## Charged construction, query, and response costs

Let (m=n+s+1). The pinned bit analysis gives (M\le16m^5), (D=O(m^{10})), and (G=O(m^{17})). It bounds the integer entries of (U_i=MZ_i) by (b=O(m^5\log m)) bits. By Cramer's rule and the source's minor bound, every rational entry of (X_i=U_0^{-1}U_i) can be encoded with

\[
\Lambda=O(D(b+\log(D+1)))=O(m^{15}\log m)
\]

bits per numerator/denominator pair. Thus one query tuple has (O(nD^2\Lambda)=O(m^{36}\log m)) input bits, including all matrix entries and delimiters. These point-height and input-length bounds depend only on (n,s), not on formula-constant heights.

The paper proves polynomial-time construction of the complete (G^3)-tuple list. A single sampled tuple can be produced by running its explicit arithmetic for one grid triple. One direct loop implementation expands each R_i entry across its O(M) Taylor indices, forms the M^2 Fourier coefficients with M signed coefficient updates each, accumulates the lifted M-by-M matrix over M^2 pairs and M diagonals, and writes its D-by-D multiplication representation. This takes at most O(nM^5) coefficient operations, each on O(b)-bit integers, hence O(nM^5 b^2) bit operations with schoolbook arithmetic. To normalize, compute U_0^{-1}U_i entrywise by Cramer's rule: at most nD^2+1 determinants of order D, each with O(D^3) exact elimination updates. The source bounds all intermediate reduced fractions by Lambda bits. Charging each rational update including gcd reduction by O(Lambda^3) elementary bit operations gives a safe per-candidate upper bound

\[
O(nM^5b^2+nD^5\Lambda^3)=O(m^{96}\log^3m)
\]

bit operations; this is a deliberately coarse bound, not an optimized implementation. The dense candidate output is (O(m^{36}\log m)) bits. A more efficient exact linear solve can improve this arithmetic bound, but is unnecessary for polynomiality.

For a standard exact value oracle, let (L_j) be the actual encoded response length on the (j)-th defined query and let (C_\Phi(X_j)) be the oracle's internal evaluation cost. The protocol's total work over (r) attempts is at most the candidate-generation work above per attempt, plus \(\sum_j C_\Phi(X_j)\), plus exact rank/determinant processing polynomial in (D+L_j), plus the bits written and read. It makes at most (r) oracle calls (at most (2) in expectation for a nonzero formula under an unbounded repeat-until-hit policy). The oracle's response length and internal cost are not bounded by (n,s): even a size-one constant can have an arbitrarily long rational encoding. No such cost is hidden in the construction bound.

The protocol requires exact definedness and exact nonsingularity. There is no finite precision bound in the theorem. In particular, a size-one scalar constant (2^{-K}) and the zero constant cannot be uniformly distinguished by an absolute-error oracle with tolerance ε when (2^{-K}\le2ε). Thus no precision independent of coefficient height suffices, even before considering matrix conditioning.

## Relation to prior and companion sources

The pinned paper at commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a` proves a deterministic polynomial-bit universal **list** over ℚ for rational formulas; it does not charge evaluation of an unknown formula with arbitrary-height constants. The present protocol uses the same internal construction but does not materialize that list: the degree argument makes one random parameter triple good with constant probability and black-box output verifies it.

The sibling release paper “One Rational Matrix Hitting Point for Noncommutative Formulas” gives one deterministic tuple for division-free formulas, with dimension at most (2ns^2). Its evaluation is everywhere defined, so its argument does not discharge inverse-gate domain conditions. “Uniform Matrix Hitting Points in Every Positive Characteristic” gives one tuple over ℝ_p for division-free formulas, including characteristic two, but also has no inverse gates. Neither source supplies a singleton for rational formulas.

The earlier Arvind–Chatterjee–Mukhopadhyay work constructs deterministic quasipolynomial hitting sets for rational formulas of arbitrary inverse height; the pinned paper itself cites the 2025 ECCC revision and 2026 SIAM publication. Randomized black-box rational identity testing is also prior art: Derksen–Makam's matrix semi-invariant degree bounds yield randomized polynomial-time RIT, as the later ECCC paper states. So the acquisition corollary is not a new randomized-PIT breakthrough. Separately, Astra's N51 card describes an internally audited but externally unverified candidate all-field integer strong list via folded (q)-Newton reversal and prime patching; it says the full generator was not executed and that formula evaluation remains a separate interface. That record is relevant prior capital, not independent confirmation of this corollary.

## Verdict and next gate

The strongest missing interface lemma is resolved conditionally by the pinned proof: under a supplied unary size bound and an exact matrix-value oracle that respects the original expression's domain, a nonzero rational formula yields an acquired, exactly certified invertible evaluation after an expected two queries, with polynomially bounded rational query height independent of the hidden constants. The algebraic guarantee is internally reconstructed from the release PDF; correctness and novelty have not received independent specialist review here, and the candidate generator was not executed. This does not solve acquisition under noisy/rounded values, scalar-only access, absent size bounds, nonadmissible expressions, or evaluation-cost bounds depending only on (n,s).

The decisive next check is an independent implementation of the one-triple sampler plus a held-out black-box harness for formulas with nested inverses, dead inverse branches, high-height constants, and adversarial near-singular outputs. It should record each exact query tuple, bit height, undefined status, determinant certificate, response length, and oracle runtime. Before implementation is presented as progress, compare the required exact oracle and query volume to existing randomized RIT interfaces and inspect whether the all-field N51 construction admits a corresponding constant-fraction parameter sampler.
