# Foundational theorem amplification: quantum broadcasting beyond reversible, tracial channels

Outcome: I have derived a broader quantum-to-classical comparison theorem from Astra's broadcast-tree construction. The argument extends the comparison to arbitrary faithful stationary quantum states and removes the reversibility assumption, with dimension-independent constants.

The derivation uses a sharp bipartite correlation inequality obtained from Lieb's concavity theorem. It also yields quantitative entanglement-breaking approximation results.

This is substantive mathematical progress relative to the theorem recorded in Astra. It is not an established 10/10 historic breakthrough. The argument below is mathematically complete subject to the cited standard theorem, but historical novelty and independent verification remain outstanding.

## 1. Main theorem

Let \\(M_d=M_d(\mathbb C)\\), and let \\(\sigma\\) be any faithful density matrix. Equip Hermitian matrices with the KMS inner product

\\[ \langle A,B\rangle\_\sigma =\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma^{1/2}A\sigma^{1/2}B). \\]

Suppose \\(T:M_d\to M_d\\) is a unital completely positive map satisfying

\\[ T\_\*(\sigma)=\sigma, \\]

and is self-compatible: there exists a unital completely positive map

\\[ \mathcal B^\*:M_d\otimes M_d\longrightarrow M_d \\]

such that

\\[ \mathcal B^\*(A\otimes I) =\mathcal B^\*(I\otimes A)=T(A). \\]

Write \\(T^\sharp\\) for the adjoint in the KMS inner product.

### Theorem A — Faithful-state classical comparison

If \\(T=T^\sharp\\), there exists a single finite-outcome entanglement-breaking channel \\(\Psi\\), preserving \\(\sigma\\) and self-adjoint in the KMS inner product, satisfying

\\[ \boxed{\Psi\succeq(2T^2-I)\_+} \\]

and

\\[ \boxed{I-\Psi\preceq4(I-T).} \tag{A} \\]

All inequalities are quadratic-form inequalities on the complete Hermitian matrix space. The positive-part operation is spectral, not completely positive order.

The constant is independent of \\(d\\), the spectrum of \\(\sigma\\), and the number of observables being compared.

### Theorem B — Removal of reversibility

For an arbitrary stationary, self-compatible \\(T\\), without KMS self-adjointness or normality, there exists a single \\(\sigma\\)-preserving KMS-self-adjoint entanglement-breaking channel \\(\Psi\\) such that

\\[ \boxed{ I-\Psi\preceq 8\left(I-\frac{T+T^\sharp}{2}\right). } \tag{B} \\]

Equivalently, every Hermitian \\(A\\) satisfies

\\[ \boxed{ \langle A,(I-\Psi)A\rangle\_\sigma \le8\bigl(\\|A\\|\_\sigma^2-\langle A,T(A)\rangle\_\sigma\bigr). } \\]

In particular, taking \\(\sigma=I/d\\) gives a dimension-independent common classical comparator for every bistochastic self-compatible quantum channel, without imposing Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjointness.

These statements use the channel and its compatible extension as supplied mathematical objects; they do not assert efficient construction or acquisition.

## 2. Proof: the new mathematical bridge

The original Astra proof works in the tracial inner product, where a basic variance calculation bounds correlations between broadcaster outputs.

That calculation cannot simply be reused for nontracial states. The essential amplification is a different correlation inequality.

### Lemma — Sharp KMS correlation bound

Let \\(\omega\_{AB}\\) be any bipartite density matrix with marginals \\(\rho_A,\rho_B\\). For Hermitian \\(X,Y\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \left|\operatorname{Tr}\omega\_{AB}(X\otimes Y)\right| \le \sqrt{ \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^{1/2}X\rho_A^{1/2}X) \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_B^{1/2}Y\rho_B^{1/2}Y) }. } \tag{1} \\]

This bound holds for arbitrary bipartite quantum states, including entangled states. The constant one is optimal.

Proof.

First suppose \\(\omega\_{AB}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\\) is pure. Choose a Schmidt decomposition

\\[ |\psi\rangle=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}|e_i\rangle|f_i\rangle. \\]

Writing \\(X\_{ij}=\langle e_i|X|e_j\rangle\\), and similarly for \\(Y\\), gives

\\[ \langle\psi|X\otimes Y|\psi\rangle = \sum\_{i,j}\sqrt{p_ip_j}X\_{ij}Y\_{ij}. \\]

Cauchy–Schwarz therefore yields (1), since

\\[ \sum\_{i,j}\sqrt{p_ip_j}|X\_{ij}|^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^{1/2}X\rho_A^{1/2}X). \\]

For a mixed state, write

\\[ \omega\_{AB}=\sum_k q_k|\psi_k\rangle\langle\psi_k|. \\]

Apply the pure-state inequality to each summand, followed by scalar Cauchy–Schwarz.

The final step uses concavity of

\\[ \rho\longmapsto \operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{1/2}X\rho^{1/2}X), \\]

which follows from Elliott Lieb’s 1973 concavity theorem.&#x20;

It gives

\\[ \sum_kq_k \operatorname{Tr}(\rho\_{A,k}^{1/2}X\rho\_{A,k}^{1/2}X) \le \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^{1/2}X\rho_A^{1/2}X), \\]

and likewise for subsystem \\(B\\). This proves (1). \\(\square\\)

The inequality is sharp. For

\\[ \sigma= \begin{pmatrix} p&0\\\0&1-p \end{pmatrix}, \quad |\Omega\_\sigma\rangle =\sqrt p\\,|00\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}\\,|11\rangle, \\]

and \\(X=Y=\sigma_x\\),

\\[ \langle\Omega\_\sigma| X\otimes X |\Omega\_\sigma\rangle = \\|X\\|\_\sigma^2 =2\sqrt{p(1-p)}. \\]

This also exposes why the ordinary variance estimate is inadequate: \\(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma X^2)=1\\), whereas the KMS quantity becomes arbitrarily small as \\(p\\) approaches zero.

### Reconstructing the broadcast-tree proof

We now prove Theorem A without assuming Astra's conclusion.

A useful preliminary fact is that every unital, \\(\sigma\\)-preserving completely positive map is a contraction in the KMS norm:

\\[ \\|T(A)\\|\_\sigma\le\\|A\\|\_\sigma. \tag{2} \\]

To see this, apply Lemma (1) to the bipartite state obtained by applying \\(T\_\*\\) to one half of a canonical purification of \\(\sigma\\). The resulting correlation is

\\[ \langle X,T(Y)\rangle\_\sigma, \\]

up to transpose on the purifying register. Lemma (1) bounds it by \\(\\|X\\|\_\sigma\\|Y\\|\_\sigma\\). Taking the supremum over \\(X\\) proves (2).

Because \\(T=T^\sharp\\), it has a KMS-orthonormal Hermitian eigenbasis, with eigenvalues in \\([-1,1]\\).

Choose its \\(\sigma\\)-centered eigenobservables \\(A_1,\ldots,A_m\\) satisfying

\\[ T(A_i)=\lambda_iA_i,\qquad |\lambda_i|>\frac1{\sqrt2}. \\]

If there are no such observables, the replacement channel \\(A\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma A)I\\) already proves the theorem.

Otherwise, start with the randomized spectral measurement

\\[ E^{(0)}\_{i,a}=\frac1mP\_{i,a}, \\]

where \\(P\_{i,a}\\) is the eigenprojection of \\(A_i\\) for eigenvalue \\(a\\). Set

\\[ z_j^{(0)}(i,a)=ma\\,\mathbf1\_{\\{i=j\\}}. \\]

This gives exactly unbiased operator moments:

\\[ \sum_y z_i^{(0)}(y)E_y^{(0)}=A_i. \\]

Recursively define

\\[ E^{(n+1)}\_{y,z} =\mathcal B^\*(E_y^{(n)}\otimes E_z^{(n)}) \\]

and

\\[ z_i^{(n+1)}(y,z) =\frac{z_i^{(n)}(y)+z_i^{(n)}(z)} {2\lambda_i}. \\]

Since both marginals of the broadcaster equal \\(T\\), induction gives

\\[ \boxed{ \sum_y z_i^{(n)}(y)E_y^{(n)}=A_i } \tag{3} \\]

at every depth.

The first moments are preserved exactly even though different \\(A_i\\) need not commute.

### The decisive covariance recursion

Define

\\[ C\_{n,ij} = \sum_y\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma E_y^{(n)}) z_i^{(n)}(y)z_j^{(n)}(y). \\]

After symmetrizing the broadcaster, let

\\[ \omega=\mathcal B\_\*(\sigma),\qquad R\_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}\omega(A_i\otimes A_j). \\]

Both marginals of \\(\omega\\) equal \\(\sigma\\).

For \\(A(v)=\sum_i v_iA_i\\), Lemma (1) implies

\\[ v^{\mathsf T}Rv = \operatorname{Tr}\omega(A(v)\otimes A(v)) \le \\|A(v)\\|\_\sigma^2 =\\|v\\|^2. \\]

Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{R\preceq I_m.} \tag{4} \\]

This is precisely the nontracial inequality required by the original branching argument.

Writing \\(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)\\), expansion of the estimator recursion gives

\\[ \boxed{ C\_{n+1} =\frac12D^{-1}(C_n+R)D^{-1}. } \tag{5} \\]

Define

\\[ H=(2D^2-I_m)^{-1}. \\]

The congruence map

\\[ \mathcal L(M)=\frac12D^{-1}MD^{-1} \\]

preserves positive-semidefinite order, even when some eigenvalues are negative.

Since \\(R\preceq I_m\\),

\\[ C_n\preceq H+\mathcal L^n(C_0-H). \\]

Every selected pair satisfies \\(2|\lambda_i\lambda_j|>1\\), so the second term converges to zero.

Consequently,

\\[ \limsup\_{n\to\infty} C_n\preceq H. \tag{6} \\]

No traciality, spectral gap, commutativity or output independence has been assumed.

### Regression and physical measurement reconstruction

For each measurement effect define

\\[ p_y=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma E_y),\qquad \rho_y= \frac{\sigma^{1/2}E_y\sigma^{1/2}}{p_y}. \\]

The associated Heisenberg measure-and-prepare map is

\\[ \Psi_n(A)=\sum_y\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_y A)E_y. \\]

It is completely positive, unital, entanglement breaking, \\(\sigma\\)-preserving and KMS-self-adjoint.

Moreover,

\\[ \langle A,\Psi_n(A)\rangle\_\sigma = \sum_yp_y\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_y A)^2. \\]

For any Hermitian \\(A\\), put

\\[ u_A(y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_y A), \qquad \beta_i=\langle A,A_i\rangle\_\sigma. \\]

Equation (3) gives

\\[ \sum_yp_yu_A(y)z_i^{(n)}(y)=\beta_i. \\]

Classical regression therefore gives, for every \\(v\in\mathbb R^m\\),

\\[ \langle A,\Psi_n(A)\rangle\_\sigma \ge 2v^{\mathsf T}\beta-v^{\mathsf T}C_nv. \tag{7} \\]

The measures \\(\sum_y p_y\delta\_{\rho_y}\\) have barycenter \\(\sigma\\). Compactness of the finite-dimensional state space yields a convergent subsequence of their first and second moments. Carathéodory's theorem supplies a finite ensemble realizing those limiting moments.

Passing to this limiting finite-outcome channel \\(\Psi\\), using (6), and choosing \\(v=H^{-1}\beta\\), produces

\\[ \langle A,\Psi(A)\rangle\_\sigma \ge \sum\_{i=1}^m (2\lambda_i^2-1)\langle A,A_i\rangle\_\sigma^2. \tag{8} \\]

The identity direction is fixed by both \\(T\\) and \\(\Psi\\); their stationarity removes cross terms between it and the centered subspace.

Thus (8) is exactly

\\[ \Psi\succeq(2T^2-I)\_+. \\]

Finally, for \\(t\in[-1,1]\\),

\\[ 1-(2t^2-1)\_+ \le2(1-t^2)\le4(1-t). \\]

Spectral calculus proves Theorem A. \\(\square\\)

## 3. Proving the nonreversible theorem

The second amplification uses the KMS adjoint rather than assuming it is itself broadcastable.

Define

\\[ \Omega\_\sigma(A)=\sigma^{1/2}A\sigma^{1/2}, \qquad T^\sharp=\Omega\_\sigma^{-1}T\_\*\Omega\_\sigma. \\]

Because \\(\sigma\\) is faithful and stationary, \\(T^\sharp\\) is unital, completely positive and \\(\sigma\\)-preserving.

Consider the multiplicative reversibilization

\\[ \Gamma=T^\sharp T. \\]

It is KMS-self-adjoint, positive and contractive.

It is also self-compatible. Indeed,

\\[ \mathcal B\_\Gamma^\*=T^\sharp\circ\mathcal B^\* \\]

is completely positive and unital, and both of its marginal channels equal \\(\Gamma\\).

Theorem A applies to \\(\Gamma\\). Hence there is a physical EB channel \\(\Psi\\) satisfying

\\[ I-\Psi\preceq4(I-\Gamma). \tag{9} \\]

Set \\(H_T=(T+T^\sharp)/2\\). The exact identity

\\[ 2(I-H_T)-(I-\Gamma) =(I-T)^\sharp(I-T)\succeq0 \tag{10} \\]

gives

\\[ I-\Gamma\preceq2(I-H_T). \\]

Combining (9) and (10),

\\[ \boxed{ I-\Psi\preceq8(I-H_T). } \\]

This proves Theorem B. \\(\square\\)

The important point is that no claim that \\(T^\sharp\\) is self-compatible is needed. Only the composite \\(T^\sharp T\\) must be self-compatible, and its broadcaster is explicit.

## 4. Strongest consequences

### A. Dimension-independent classical approximation of channel powers

For KMS-symmetric \\(T\\), use the same comparator \\(\Psi\\) and define

\\[ \Lambda_n=\Psi T^n. \\]

Each \\(\Lambda_n\\) is entanglement breaking.

Since \\(0\preceq\Psi\preceq I\\),

\\[ (I-\Psi)^2\preceq I-\Psi. \\]

Using Theorem A,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \\|(T^n-\Lambda_n)A\\|\_\sigma^2 &\le2\langle T^nA,(I-T^2)T^nA\rangle\_\sigma\\\ &\le2\max\_{0\le x\le1}x^n(1-x)\\|A\\|\_\sigma^2. \end{aligned} \\]

Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{ \\|T^n-\Lambda_n\\|\_{2,\sigma\to2,\sigma} \le \sqrt{\frac{2n^n}{(n+1)^{n+1}}} \le\sqrt{\frac2{n+1}}. } \tag{11} \\]

This extends Astra's dimension-independent power approximation from the tracial setting to arbitrary faithful stationary states.

If \\(T\\) is KMS-normal, not necessarily self-adjoint, applying Theorem A to \\(T^\sharp T\\) similarly gives

\\[ \boxed{ \inf\_{\Lambda\ {\rm EB}} \\|T^n-\Lambda\\|\_{2,\sigma\to2,\sigma} \le 2\sqrt{\frac{n^n}{(n+1)^{n+1}}}. } \tag{12} \\]

These rates do not require spectral gaps.

### B. Classicalization of Cesàro powers without normality

The arbitrary nonnormal case admits another unconditional conclusion.

Let

\\[ F_N=\frac1N\sum\_{k=0}^{N-1}T^k. \\]

Using Theorem B, set \\(\Lambda_N=\Psi F_N\\), which is EB.

For any Hermitian \\(A\\),

\\[ \begin{aligned} \\|(F_N-\Lambda_N)A\\|\_\sigma^2 &\le8\langle F_NA,(I-H_T)F_NA\rangle\_\sigma\\\ &=8\langle F_NA,(I-T)F_NA\rangle\_\sigma. \end{aligned} \\]

The telescoping identity

\\[ (I-T)F_N=\frac{I-T^N}{N} \\]

and KMS contractivity give

\\[ \boxed{ \\|F_N-\Lambda_N\\|\_{2,\sigma\to2,\sigma} \le\frac4{\sqrt N}. } \tag{13} \\]

Thus even for an arbitrarily nonnormal, nonreversible self-compatible channel, its Cesàro iterates admit dimension-independent entanglement-breaking approximation.

This is not a pointwise estimate on \\(T^N\\) itself.

### C. One classical ensemble for arbitrarily many observables

For any finite Hermitian family \\(\mathcal A=(A_1,\ldots,A_q)\\), set

\\[ V\_\sigma(\mathcal A)=\sum_j\\|A_j\\|\_\sigma^2, \\]

\\[ S_T(\mathcal A)=\sum_j\langle A_j,T(A_j)\rangle\_\sigma, \\]

and let

\\[ K\_\sigma(\mathcal A)= \max\_{\sum_y p_y\rho_y=\sigma} \sum\_{y,j}p_y\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_y A_j)^2. \\]

The maximizing ensemble may be taken finite and pure.

Theorem A gives

\\[ \boxed{ V\_\sigma-K\_\sigma \le4(V\_\sigma-S_T) } \tag{14} \\]

for KMS-reversible channels. Theorem B gives the same result with constant eight for arbitrary stationary self-compatible channels.

The same ensemble works across the entire frame. There is no additional factor depending on \\(q\\).

This is a general quantum-to-classical comparison principle, although its KMS quadratic information metric must not be confused with operational Holevo information or trace-norm distinguishability.

## 5. Adversarial tests and limitations

Resolved

The sharp correlation lemma replaces the tracial variance argument without dependence on the minimum eigenvalue of \\(\sigma\\).

Noncommuting observables

Resolved

Full covariance matrices and regression preserve arbitrary slow/fast cross terms.

Nonnormal channels

Resolved

Unresolved

There is also a simple example demonstrating that the nontracial extension applies beyond EB channels.

Take any faithful qubit state \\(\sigma\\), and define

\\[ T\_\*(\rho)=\tfrac12\rho+\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(\rho)\sigma. \\]

It admits the symmetric broadcaster

\\[ \mathcal B\_\*(\rho) = \tfrac12(\rho\otimes\sigma+\sigma\otimes\rho). \\]

The channel preserves \\(\sigma\\) and is KMS-symmetric. Yet its Choi matrix has a negative partial-transpose eigenvalue for every faithful diagonal qubit \\(\sigma\\), so it is not entanglement breaking. The theorem therefore applies to genuinely quantum, nontracial examples.

## 6. Novelty and significance assessment

The original mechanism was the binary broadcast tree with covariance control and simultaneous regression, preserved in Astra's N330 record. The new connecting argument is the KMS correlation bound, which makes the construction compatible with nontracial states. Petz/KMS adjoint reversal then extends the result to nonnormal channels.

The covariance lemma itself is a consequence of established mathematics, not a new fundamental inequality. The compositions with the KMS adjoint are also standard operations. What goes beyond the archived result is their combination with the full-space broadcast-tree comparator.

I did not find the precise combined theorem in the primary literature checked, but this search does not establish publication priority. Existing literature includes quantum channel compatibility and KMS-symmetric quantum Markov processes, so careful specialist review remains necessary.&#x20;
