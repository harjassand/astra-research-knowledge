# A folded-gradient-excursion rejection test, with a full first-order collar coefficient

7 October 2026, cycle 9. **Exact general obstruction lemma; actual-profile application UNKNOWN.** This is a second approach, retained alongside the compatible ridge calculation in `SAFE_RIDGE_TRANSITION.md`. It targets a gradient trajectory that leaves an inner radial section through a negative phase, turns outward in a nodal collar, and returns to the same section through a positive phase. Such a fold can force a large transverse derivative even when every cocycle value is small.

## Exact obstruction, without a PDE approximation

Let F be smooth on an exterior region and let γ_H be a C¹ one-parameter family of its oriented gradient trajectories with endpoints p(H),q(H). Assume the family is regular enough to differentiate its Laplacian integral. Define the exact conductivity cocycle

    C(H)=−∫_(γ_H) ΔF/|DF| dl,

with the orientation supplied by DF. Any C¹ solution of DF·Dh=−ΔF satisfies

    C(H)=h(q(H))−h(p(H)),
    |C'(H)|≤M_H[|q'(H)|+|p'(H)|],                (1)

where M_H bounds |Dh| at the two endpoints. Equation (1) is the chain rule applied to the exact transport identity; it does not require control of Dh in the excursion interior.

Consequently, if escaping patches admit heights H_j comparable to their macro length L_j and constants c,C>0 such that

    |p_j'(H_j)|+|q_j'(H_j)|≤C exp(−c a_j L_j),
    |C_j'(H_j)|≥c L_j/R_j²,                       (2)

then any global h must have

    M_j≥c (L_j/R_j²) exp(c a_jL_j)→∞.           (3)

This rules out |Dh|→0 and even a bounded gradient. The exponential dominates every algebraic prefactor because a_jL_j=sqrt(a_jR_j) and a_j is subpower. More general endpoint and cocycle scales can be inserted directly into (1). Unlike a fixed-time flow-volume estimate, this test explicitly includes movement of BOTH variable-time endpoints.

For the exactly harmonic flat wave e^(−as)cos(ay), the first integral e^(−as)sin(ay) explains the fold. At an inner section s=s_0, a trajectory whose nodal turning point has s=s_0+H has endpoints with phase ζ near π and near 0, satisfying sin ζ=e^(−aH). Their H-derivatives have physical size O(e^(−aH)). The exact harmonic cocycle is zero. The exponential endpoint sensitivity by itself therefore proves no obstruction.

## A repair coefficient that cannot be obtained by deleting the collar

The single-wave actual construction, at tangential macro position t_0=T L with fixed nonzero T, has first small parameter

    μ=t_0/R = η T,   η=(aR)^(−1/2).

Freezing the slow coefficients at a turning point, the radial envelope contributes the first residual 2μ sin ζ after division by a²E. The first harmonic-repair corrector must be included. Let b=arcsin τ<π/2 and extend the following function by zero outside |ζ−π/2|<b:

    p(ζ)=b tan b cos u−u sin u,  u=ζ−π/2.

It satisfies p''+p=−2 sin ζ in the collar, p=0 at both endpoints and p≥0. The zero extension has derivative jumps. Thus the first residual is the DISTRIBUTION

    R_1=2 sin ζ+(∂_ζ²+1)p.                       (4)

It is zero in the open collar and equals 2 sin ζ outside, but it also contains the endpoint measures. Deleting those measures changes the transport coefficient.

Retain the actual fixed radial mollifier. The conjugated smoothing operator on the frozen correction is

    P_σ(ζ)=∫ψ(u)e^(σu_1)p(ζ−σu_2)du.

It is nonnegative, supported away from ζ=0,π when b+σ<π/2. Smoothing commutes with ∂_ζ²+1, and radial averaging preserves e^(−as)sin ζ. Hence the smoothed first residual is

    R_(1,σ)=2 sin ζ+(∂_ζ²+1)P_σ.

For the frozen smooth family obtained by radially averaging e^(−as)e^(−μζ)[cos ζ+μp(ζ)], the first variation at μ=0 of a fixed finite excursion has

    d h/dζ = μ R_(1,σ)(ζ)/sin ζ,
    C_frozen = −μ B_σ(H)+O_H(μ²).

Here its unperturbed inner endpoint phase is ζ_0=arcsin(e^(−aH)). Once both endpoints lie outside the smoothed collar,

    B_σ(H)=2(π−2ζ_0)+2∫_0^π P_σ(ζ)/sin³ζ dζ,
    B_σ(H)→B_σ=∫_0^π R_(1,σ)(ζ)/sin ζ dζ as aH→∞.

The remainder is allowed to depend on H; no uniform large-H nonlinear expansion is asserted. Endpoint movements do not contribute at first order because the unperturbed harmonic cocycle is identically zero. The limiting coefficient is useful only after the differentiated actual-profile gate below is acquired.

Integrating the distribution by parts twice, with no boundary term because P_σ has compact interior support, gives the EXACT coefficient

    B_σ=2π+2∫_0^π P_σ(ζ)/sin³ζ dζ > 2π.         (5)

Here (∂_ζ²+1)csc ζ=2 csc³ζ. Equation (5) includes the entire Dirichlet collar corrector, its endpoint measures and the fixed smoothing. At σ=0 it becomes explicitly

    B_0=2π+2[b tan²b+tan b−b].                   (6)

For (6), integrate p/cos³u on [−b,b]; the value is b tan²b+tan b−b. Both terms are positive for b>0. The harmonic smoothing correction to the leading raw wave is a multiple of sin ζ at this order and does not alter R_(1,σ) or (5).

This is an exact calculation in the frozen first-order collar problem. It is not a replacement for the actual global W. It explains why the raw estimate obtained by merely skipping the collar, 2(π−2b), has the wrong coefficient. The omitted endpoint currents more than compensate the deleted open collar.

## What is and is not acquired for the actual F

If the actual folded cocycle had the uniform differentiated expansion

    C_actual(H)=−B_σ t_0/R_turn(H)+E(H),
    |E'(H)|=o(|t_0|/R_turn(H)²),                 (7)

then C_actual'(H)=B_σ t_0/R_turn²+o(t_0/R_turn²). For |t_0| comparable to L, this supplies the polynomial lower bound in (2). Together with the actual exponentially small endpoint derivatives it would reject global small-gradient conductivity for that specific repaired member, while leaving the original-potential index logically separate.

Neither ingredient of this implication has yet been proved for the literal F. Needed are a stable turning-point parametrization through the actual repaired collar, simultaneous control of both inner-section endpoint derivatives, and a C¹ expansion of the exact Laplacian integral with the margin in (7). An undifferentiated O(η²) remainder, or an O(κ) coefficient estimate, does not give (7). Naively differentiating a kernel gives a factor a; differentiating an unstable trajectory can be much worse. These costs must be removed by a stable phase parametrization or overcome by a sufficiently high-order controlled expansion.

The spectrum worker's localized stripe expansion controls its potential to o(a/R) for a different variational purpose. The worker explicitly confirmed that this does not acquire (7); no implication is imported from that proof. The compatible two-wave ridge has no fold and does not resolve this test. These differing local mechanisms are both retained.
