\begin{abstract}
We study parallel zero-false-alarm detection with a known, strictly positive
miss effect \(E\), a finite-dimensional input Hamiltonian \(H\), and a unique
zero-energy ray fixed by \(E\). The entire input support, rather than its
expected energy, must lie below a spectral ceiling. An energy-ordered
Cholesky factorization \(E=T^\dagger T\) converts the constrained minimum
miss into a constrained maximum tensor norm. For \(B=T^{-1}\), the exact
exponent with unrestricted numbers of parallel uses is
\(\inf\{s\geq0:B^\dagger B\leq e^{sH}\}\).
The complete fixed-use reliability curve is a one-dimensional convex
variational formula, with a logarithmic finite-use remainder uniform over
all energy densities. The fully separable comparator is an exact finite
allocation problem, whose limiting curve is the concave envelope of
single-input spectral-cutoff points. The supremum entanglement gain equals
the number of distinct positive energy levels, independent of their
degeneracies. A qutrit example has exact hard exponent
\(\log((1+\sqrt{17})/2)\), strictly between its separable hard exponent
\(\log2\) and its expected-energy exponent \((5/3)\log2\).
An explicit Dicke-state family supplies finite legal probes and polynomial
prefactors. The resource constrained here is accepted channel-input support;
acquisition, preparation, driving, and reference costs are separate.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact input contract}
All logarithms are natural. Let \(\mathcal H\) have dimension \(D\geq2\), and
let the known operators satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contract}
 H\geq0,\qquad \ker H=\mathbb C\ket0,\qquad
 0<E\leq I,\qquad E\ket0=\ket0.
\end{equation}
Write \(\mathcal H=\mathbb C\ket0\oplus\mathcal H_+\).
Hermiticity implies \(E=1\oplus\ep\), while \(H=0\oplus\hp\) with
\(\hp>0\). Let \(h_{\min},h_{\max}\) be its smallest and largest eigenvalues.
For \(n\) parallel inputs define
\[
 H^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^n H_i,\qquad
 P_{n,Q}=\mathbf1_{[0,Q]}(H^{(n)}).
\]
An input density operator is legal precisely when
\(\rho=P_{n,Q}\rho P_{n,Q}\). Its miss probability is
\(\Tr(\rho E^{\otimes n})\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:missdef}
 p_n(Q)=\min_{\rho=P_{n,Q}\rho P_{n,Q}}\Tr(\rho E^{\otimes n}),\qquad
 \popt(Q)=\inf_{n\geq0}p_n(Q),
\end{equation}
where \(n=0\) means the vacuum strategy with miss one.

One channel realization is a memoryless map \(\Phi\) with pure null output
\(\Phi(\ket0\bra0)=\ket v\bra v\) and
\(E=\Phi^*(\ket v\bra v)\). The largest zero-false-alarm rejection effect
on \(n\) outputs is \(I-(\ket v\bra v)^{\otimes n}\), giving the stated miss.
Equivalently, every effect satisfying \eqref{eq:contract} can be realized by
a binary measure-and-prepare channel with orthogonal no-click and click
outputs. We optimize the channel inputs and this output test, with no
retained ancillary system and no adaptive intervention between channel uses.

Both \(E^{\otimes n}\) and \(H^{(n)}\) preserve each pattern of ground and
excited sites. A legal pattern has at most
\(\lfloor Q/h_{\min}\rfloor\) excited sites. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:groundreduction}
 \popt(Q)=\min_{0\leq r\leq\lfloor Q/h_{\min}\rfloor}
 \lambda_{\min}\left(
  P^+_{r,Q}\ep^{\otimes r}P^+_{r,Q}\big|_{\Ran P^+_{r,Q}}\right),
\end{equation}
omitting empty positive-energy subspaces and including the value one at
\(r=0\). Thus arbitrary additional vacuum slots do not hide an unlimited
input resource, and \(\popt(Q)>0\) for every finite \(Q\).

The hard-support condition is stronger than
\(\Tr(\rho H^{(n)})\leq Q\). In particular, a typical projection for
\(-\log E\) need not produce a legal hard-energy input, and an arbitrarily
small discarded norm can be exponentially relevant to a miss probability.

\section{An exact finite-dimensional reduction}
Choose an orthonormal energy basis in nondecreasing energy order and take
the upper Cholesky factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cholesky}
 E=T^\dagger T,\qquad B=T^{-1},\qquad
 A=B^\dagger B=(TT^\dagger)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The ground blocks of \(T,B,A\) are all one. An upper triangular matrix in
this ordering maps a basis vector only to vectors with no greater energy.
It preserves cumulative lower-energy subspaces; it need not preserve an
individual energy eigenspace.

\begin{lemma}[Compressed Gram identity]\label{lem:gram}
For every \(n\geq1\) and \(Q\geq0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 p_n(Q)=\norm{B^{\otimes n}P_{n,Q}}^{-2}.
\end{equation}
If \(y\) is a legal unit vector, then
\(\chi=B^{\otimes n}y/\norm{B^{\otimes n}y}\) is legal and has miss
\(\norm{B^{\otimes n}y}^{-2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both \(T^{\otimes n}\) and \(B^{\otimes n}\) preserve \(\Ran P_{n,Q}\).
Their restrictions \(T_P,B_P\) are inverses. The compressed effect is
\(T_P^\dagger T_P\), whose smallest eigenvalue is
\(\norm{T_P^{-1}}^{-2}\). Minimizing a linear functional over density
operators gives this smallest eigenvalue. Finally,
\(T^{\otimes n}\chi=y/\norm{B^{\otimes n}y}\), which proves the explicit
miss identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Energy-basis invariance]\label{lem:invariance}
The tilted norms and order thresholds formed from \(A\) below are
independent of choices within degenerate energy eigenspaces.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Allow factors that are upper triangular in energy blocks. Two such factors
\(T_1,T_2\) of the same \(E\) obey \(T_2=UT_1\), where
\(U=T_2T_1^{-1}\) is unitary and upper in energy blocks. A finite
block-upper unitary is block diagonal: orthogonality of its first block
column forces its other first-row blocks to vanish, and induction completes
the argument. Hence \(U\) commutes with \(H\).
Then \(B_2=B_1U^\dagger\) and \(A_2=UA_1U^\dagger\).
Conjugating an energy basis inside its degenerate blocks reduces to the
same argument. All the relevant norms and order inequalities are unchanged.
\end{proof}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilts}
 C_s=e^{-sH/2}Ae^{-sH/2},\quad
 \phi(s)=\log\norm{C_s}=2\log\norm{Be^{-sH/2}},\quad
 \sh=\inf\{s\geq0:A\leq e^{sH}\}.
\end{equation}
The order test can be restricted to \(\mathcal H_+\).
If \(E=I\), all rates are zero. Otherwise \(\sh>0\) is the unique solution
of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
 \norm{e^{-\sh\hp/2}A_+e^{-\sh\hp/2}}=1.
\end{equation}
Indeed the excited norm starts at \(1/\lambda_{\min}(\ep)>1\), tends to
zero, and decreases by at least a factor \(e^{-h_{\min}t}\) when \(s\)
is increased by \(t>0\). The full norm retains its ground contribution one.

\section{The complete fixed-use reliability curve}
Put \(P_{n,c}=P_{n,nc}\) and
\[
 F_n(c)=\log\norm{B^{\otimes n}P_{n,c}}^2=-\log p_n(nc),\qquad
 g(c)=\inf_{s\geq0}\{\phi(s)+sc\}.
\]
Thus \(c\) is a ceiling per available parallel use, not an average
expectation per use.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform fixed-use theorem]\label{thm:fixed}
Under \eqref{eq:contract}, let
\[
 K=(D-1)h_{\max},\qquad C_D=(D-1)+D(D-1)^2.
\]
For all \(n\geq1\) and \(c\geq0\), simultaneously,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform}
 ng(c)-2\sh K-D\log(n+1)-C_D
 \ \leq F_n(c)\ \leq ng(c).
\end{equation}
In particular
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac1n\log p_n(nc)=g(c)
 =\inf_{s\geq0}\left\{sc+
   \log\norm{e^{-sH/2}Ae^{-sH/2}}\right\}.
\]
The curve is nondecreasing, concave, and globally \(\sh\)-Lipschitz.
It is zero at \(c=0\), and eventually equals
\(\log\norm A=-\log\lambda_{\min}(E)\).
\end{theorem}

We give the proof including degeneracies and a uniform finite-\(n\)
construction. The familiar log-convexity and type estimates also appear in
more general standard forms in \cite{BourinLee,CsiszarShields}; the argument
here does not require an imported detection theorem.

\begin{lemma}[Tilt geometry]\label{lem:geometry}
The function \(\phi\) is convex, nonincreasing, nonnegative, and zero for
\(s\geq\sh\). If \(W_s\) is the top eigenspace of \(C_s\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subgrad}
 \partial\phi(s)=[-h_{\max}(W_s),-h_{\min}(W_s)],
\end{equation}
where the extrema are those of the compression of \(H\) to \(W_s\).
At any positive minimizer of \(\phi(s)+sc\), there is a unit top vector
\(w\) with \(\bra wH\ket w=c\). At a minimizer \(s=0\), one can choose
such a vector with mean energy at most \(c\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The matrix-valued function \(z\mapsto Be^{-zH/2}\) is analytic on every
finite vertical strip and its norm is constant along each boundary line,
because the imaginary exponential is a unitary right factor.
Applying the three-lines theorem to scalar matrix elements and taking
the norm proves log-convexity. Monotonicity follows by right multiplication
by the contraction \(e^{-tH/2}\). The ground block gives nonnegativity.

If the top eigenvalue of \(C_s\) is \(\lambda=e^{\phi(s)}\), then
\[
 P_{W_s}C_s'P_{W_s}=-\lambda P_{W_s}HP_{W_s}.
\]
The left and right derivatives of the top eigenvalue are the minimum
and maximum eigenvalues of this derivative compression, respectively.
Dividing by \(\lambda\) yields \eqref{eq:subgrad}.
The numerical range of a Hermitian compression is the interval between
its extremal eigenvalues. The subgradient optimality condition therefore
gives a top vector of the required mean. At the endpoint \(s=0\), the
right derivative condition is \(c\geq h_{\min}(W_0)\).
For \(c>0\), a minimizer exists in \([0,\sh]\); a positive minimizer at
\(\sh\) is an interior point of the original domain \([0,\infty)\).
At \(c=0\), the ground vector suffices.
Finally, restricting the infimum to slopes \(s\in[0,\sh]\) gives the
claimed monotonicity, concavity, and Lipschitz bound for \(g\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniform rounded-type estimate]\label{lem:type}
For a probability vector \(p\) on at most \(D\) coordinates and any \(n\),
there is a count vector \(k\) with \(\sum_jk_j=n\) such that its exact
multinomial probability obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typeprob}
 p_{\mathrm{type}}=\frac{n!}{\prod_jk_j!}\prod_jp_j^{k_j}
 \ \geq e^{-C_D}(n+1)^{-D}.
\end{equation}
For any energies \(0\leq h_j\leq h_{\max}\), this choice also satisfies
\[
 \left|\sum_jk_jh_j-n\sum_jp_jh_j\right|\leq K.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose \(j_*\) with \(p_{j_*}=\max_jp_j\geq1/D\).
Round \(np_j\) down for \(j\ne j_*\), and assign the remaining counts to
\(j_*\). Zero-probability coordinates receive no counts.
Writing \(a=k_{j_*}-np_{j_*}\), we have \(0\leq a\leq D-1\).
For \(q_j=k_j/n\), all terms other than \(j_*\) in
\(n\KL(q\Vert p)\) are nonpositive, whence
\[
 n\KL(q\Vert p)
 \leq (np_{j_*}+a)\log\left(1+\frac a{np_{j_*}}\right)
 \leq a+\frac{a^2}{np_{j_*}}\leq C_D.
\]
The vector \(k\) is a mode of the multinomial law with parameter \(q\):
the ratio of the probability of another count vector \(\ell\) to that of
\(k\) is the product of factors
\(k_j!k_j^{\ell_j-k_j}/\ell_j!\), each at most one. A zero \(k_j\)
forces zero probability when \(\ell_j>0\).
There are at most \((n+1)^D\) count vectors, so the probability of \(k\)
under \(q\) is at least \((n+1)^{-D}\). Changing the parameter from \(q\)
to \(p\) multiplies it by \(e^{-n\KL(q\Vert p)}\), proving
\eqref{eq:typeprob}. The increases and decreases of counts each sum to
\(a\), so their energy difference has absolute value at most
\(ah_{\max}\leq K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed}]
The order bound \(A\leq e^{\phi(s)}e^{sH}\) tensors to
\[
 A^{\otimes n}\leq e^{n\phi(s)}e^{sH^{(n)}}.
\]
Compression below \(nc\) proves \(F_n(c)\leq n(\phi(s)+sc)\) for every
\(s\geq0\), hence the upper bound.

If \(c\leq K/n\), the ground strategy gives \(F_n(c)\geq0\), while
\(ng(c)\leq n\sh c\leq\sh K\); the lower bound follows.
Otherwise set \(c'=c-K/n>0\). Choose a dual minimizer \(s\in[0,\sh]\)
and a unit top vector \(w\) from Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry}. Its mean \(h\)
is \(c'\) when \(s>0\), and at most \(c'\) when \(s=0\).
In an energy basis let \(p_j=|\langle j,w\rangle|^2\), and form the rounded
type from Lemma~\ref{lem:type}. If \(\Pi_k\) is its tensor-basis projector,
the unit vector
\[
 y=\Pi_k w^{\otimes n}/\sqrt{p_{\mathrm{type}}}
\]
has exactly one total energy \(q_n=\sum_jk_jh_j\leq nh+K\leq nc\).
Since \(C_s\geq\lambda\ket w\bra w\), tensor order gives
\[
 \bra yC_s^{\otimes n}\ket y\geq\lambda^np_{\mathrm{type}}.
\]
The exact energy of \(y\) now yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typelower}
 \norm{B^{\otimes n}y}^2
 =e^{sq_n}\bra yC_s^{\otimes n}\ket y
 \geq e^{n\phi(s)+sq_n}p_{\mathrm{type}}.
\end{equation}
For \(s>0\), use \(q_n\geq nc'-K\); for \(s=0\), the energy term vanishes.
In either case,
\[
 F_n(c)\geq ng(c')-\sh K-D\log(n+1)-C_D.
\]
As \(g(c)-g(c')\leq\sh K/n\), this is \eqref{eq:uniform}.
The convergence and plateau statements follow. At \(c=0\), the legal
subspace is the ground ray and \(F_n(0)=g(0)=0\) exactly.
\end{proof}

This proof works for nonlattice energy eigenvalues: an occupation type
always has one exact total energy. No assumption of a differentiable
top eigenvalue, nondegenerate \(H\), or constant lower bound on the
coordinates of \(w\) was used.

\section{Unrestricted uses and the fully separable comparator}
\begin{theorem}[Exact hard exponent]\label{thm:free}
For \(E\ne I\), there is a constant \(C>0\), depending on the fixed
\((E,H)\), such that for all \(Q\geq0\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:freebounds}
 e^{-\sh Q}\leq\popt(Q)\leq C(Q+1)^D e^{-\sh Q}.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
 \lim_{Q\to\infty}\frac{-\log\popt(Q)}Q=\sh,\qquad
 Q_\epsilon=\frac{\log(1/\epsilon)}{\sh}
              +O(\log\log(1/\epsilon)),
\]
where \(Q_\epsilon\) is the infimum hard ceiling permitting miss at most
\(\epsilon\). For \(E=I\), the miss is always one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The tensor order \(A\leq e^{\sh H}\) and Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} give
\(p_n(Q)\geq e^{-\sh Q}\) for every \(n,Q\).
At the excited threshold \eqref{eq:root}, choose a unit saturated vector
\(w\) with \(h=\bra wH\ket w>0\). The full tilted matrix has top
eigenvalue one and \(g(h)=\sh h\), by dual optimality.
Take \(n=\lfloor Q/h\rfloor\). Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed} at ceiling \(nh\)
produces a legal input with
\[
 -\log p_n(nh)\geq\sh nh-2\sh K-D\log(n+1)-C_D.
\]
Since \(nh\leq Q<nh+h\), this proves \eqref{eq:freebounds} after enlarging
\(C\) to cover \(n=0\). Inverting its two sides gives the budget claim.
\end{proof}

Let \(0=h_0<h_1<\cdots<h_k\) be the distinct energy levels of \(H\).
For \(j\geq1\), define the single-input cutoff effect and log miss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoffpoints}
 E_j=P_{\leq h_j}EP_{\leq h_j}\big|_{\Ran P_{\leq h_j}},\qquad
 \ell_j=-\log\lambda_{\min}(E_j),\qquad \ell_0=0.
\end{equation}
The comparator below allows arbitrary unequal product factors and arbitrary
correlated separable mixtures, supplied with the same \(E,H\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact separable allocation and full curve]\label{thm:sep}
The minimum \(n\)-use legal separable miss is \(e^{-K_n(c)}\), where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integersep}
 K_n(c)=\max\left\{\sum_{j=1}^k n_j\ell_j:
 n_j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\
 \sum_jn_j\leq n,\
 \sum_jn_jh_j\leq nc\right\}.
\end{equation}
Its limiting curve and an equivalent dual formula are
\begin{align}
 \gs(c)&=\max\left\{\sum_{j=0}^k\pi_j\ell_j:
 \pi_j\geq0,\ \sum_j\pi_j=1,\ \sum_j\pi_jh_j\leq c\right\}\label{eq:sephull}\\
 &=\inf_{s\geq0}\left\{sc+\max_{0\leq j\leq k}(\ell_j-sh_j)\right\}.
 \nonumber
\end{align}
Uniformly in \(n,c\),
\[
 n\gs(c)-\sum_{j=1}^k\ell_j\leq K_n(c)\leq n\gs(c).
\]
With unrestricted uses the exact separable exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 \etah=\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\frac{\ell_j}{h_j},
\end{equation}
and its least budget is \(\log(1/\epsilon)/\etah+O(1)\) when \(E\ne I\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If a positive mixture has no support outside a subspace, neither does
any of its positive-weight components: their outside-projector traces
are nonnegative and sum to zero. Thus every product component in a legal
separable decomposition is legal. For a local factor let \(h_j\) be its
highest occupied energy. It is supported below \(h_j\) and its miss is at
least \(e^{-\ell_j}\). The product of the highest-energy diagonal
probabilities of all factors is positive. Its total energy is the sum
of these local maxima, so legality forces their sum to be at most \(nc\).
This yields \eqref{eq:integersep} as a lower bound on miss for each product
and hence for every mixture. Conversely, assign a minimum-eigenvalue
vector of \(E_j\) to each of the \(n_j\) sites and ground to unused sites.
This is legal and attains its allocated miss exactly.

The continuous problem \eqref{eq:sephull} is a finite linear program;
its dual is the displayed formula. Flooring \(n\pi_j\) at positive
levels and filling the remainder with ground reduces energy and loses
at most \(\sum_j\ell_j\) in log miss, proving the uniform remainder.
Finally every legal product has miss at least \(e^{-\etah Q}\).
Repeating a maximizing single-input cutoff vector \(\lfloor Q/h_j\rfloor\)
times attains \(e^{-\etah Q+O(1)}\). These statements also give the
separable budget asymptotics.
\end{proof}

The curve \(\gs\) is the nondecreasing upper concave envelope of the cutoff
points \((h_j,\ell_j)\), with the budget interpreted as an inequality.
Operational inclusion gives \(g(c)\geq\gs(c)\).
The unrestricted entanglement advantage has the exact criterion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:criterion}
 \sh>\etah\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_+\not\leq e^{\etah\hp}.
\end{equation}
This criterion concerns entanglement between channel inputs, not
entanglement with a retained external ancilla.

\section{A sharp bound from the number of energy levels}
\begin{theorem}[Sharp structural gain bound]\label{thm:gain}
For \(E\ne I\), with \(k\) distinct positive energy levels,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
 1\leq\frac{\sh}{\etah}
 \leq\min\left\{k,\frac{h_{\max}}{h_{\min}}\right\}.
\end{equation}
If \(k>1\), the bound \(\sh<k\etah\) is strict for every fixed model.
Its supremum over models with \(k\) positive energy levels is nevertheless
exactly \(k\), including prescribed nonzero degeneracies.
For \(k=1\), or more generally \([E,H]=0\), the two exponents coincide.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From \(T^\dagger T\leq I\), also \(TT^\dagger\leq I\), so \(A\geq I\).
Let \(J=A_+-I\geq0\). A leading energy-prefix block of \(A\) is
\(B_j^\dagger B_j\), where \(B_j\) is the inverse Cholesky factor of
\(E_j\). Its norm is \(e^{\ell_j}\leq e^{\etah h_j}\).
Thus each diagonal energy block satisfies
\[
 J_{jj}\leq(e^{\etah h_j}-1)I_j.
\]
For any positive \(k\)-block matrix, Cauchy--Schwarz for its block
quadratic forms gives \(J\leq k\bigoplus_jJ_{jj}\). Therefore
\[
 J\leq k(e^{\etah\hp}-I)\leq e^{k\etah\hp}-I,
\]
using \(k(t-1)\leq t^k-1\) for \(t\geq1\).
This proves \(\sh\leq k\etah\). For \(k>1\), the last inequality is
strict on every positive energy block; continuity then gives
\(\sh<k\etah\). The lower bound is operational inclusion.

For the other bound set \(L=-\log E\) and
\(\alpha=\norm{\hp^{-1/2}L_+\hp^{-1/2}}\).
Scalar Jensen in the spectral distribution of \(L^{(n)}\) gives, for
every input,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jensen}
 -\log\Tr(\rho E^{\otimes n})
 \leq\Tr(\rho L^{(n)})\leq\alpha\Tr(\rho H^{(n)}).
\end{equation}
Thus \(\sh\leq\alpha\leq\norm{L_+}/h_{\min}\), whereas the full
single-input cutoff gives \(\etah\geq\norm{L_+}/h_{\max}\).

For sharpness, take one vector from each positive energy level, with
\[
 h_j=R^{j-1},\quad h_0=0,\quad
 |v_j|^2=\frac{h_j-h_{j-1}}{h_k},\quad
 \ep=e^{-\tau\ket v\bra v}\quad(R>1,\ \tau>0).
\]
Set \(E=I\) on any additional degenerate excited directions.
The cutoff eigenvalues are
\(1-(1-e^{-\tau})h_j/h_k\).
Since \(-\log(1-a x)/x\) increases on \(0<x\leq1\), the exact
separable exponent is \(\etah=\tau/h_k\).
Differentiating the Cholesky factor at \(E=I\) gives
\[
 A_+(\tau)=I+\tau\ket v\bra v+O(\tau^2).
\]
Indeed if \(T=I+\tau U+O(\tau^2)\), then
\(U^\dagger+U=-\ket v\bra v\) and
\(B^\dagger B=I-\tau(U^\dagger+U)+O(\tau^2)\).
Comparing \(e^{t\tau\hp}\) with this expansion for \(t\) above or below
the largest generalized eigenvalue yields
\[
 \frac{\sh(\tau)}{\tau}\longrightarrow
 \sum_j\frac{|v_j|^2}{h_j}
 =\frac{k-(k-1)/R}{h_k}.
\]
Hence \(\sh/\etah\to k-(k-1)/R\) as \(\tau\downarrow0\), and then
approaches \(k\) as \(R\to\infty\).
If \(E,H\) commute, one may use an energy-block-diagonal square-root
factor; then \(A=E^{-1}\), and single-input eigenvectors attain the
order threshold. This proves equality in that case.
\end{proof}

The effects in the sharp family approach \(I\); the construction does not
require an unbounded effect dynamic range. Its positive energy hierarchy
does grow. At fixed total input dimension \(D\), the supremum gain is
\(D-1\). This concerns the hard-support contract; an expected-energy
contract has a different possible gain.

\section{Exact qutrit separation and explicit probes}
Consider the ground block one and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qutrit}
 \hp=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \ep=\begin{pmatrix}1/2&\sqrt2/4\\\sqrt2/4&3/4\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The effect and the two-use witness below were supplied in the unreviewed
summary \emph{N32-hard-energy-detection}, source key
\texttt{d-c8be4a4922031676}, \texttt{updates/O/summary.txt}.
Its linked full proof was not available in the supplied packet.
All mathematical statements used here are reconstructed explicitly below;
this provenance does not provide external validation or an authorship claim.
The excited basis vectors are denoted \(\ket l,\ket h\).
Their effects have eigenvalues \(1/4,1\). The factors and transformed
matrix are
\[
 T_+=\begin{pmatrix}1/\sqrt2&1/2\\0&1/\sqrt2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B_+=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt2&-1\\0&\sqrt2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_+=\begin{pmatrix}2&-\sqrt2\\-\sqrt2&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(x=e^s\), the condition \(e^{s\hp}-A_+\geq0\) first requires
\(x\geq2\), and its determinant is
\[
 (x-2)(x^2-3)-2=(x-1)(x^2-x-4).
\]
Writing \(R=(1+\sqrt{17})/2\), the exact exponents are therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qutritrates}
 \sh=\log R=0.9406136421\ldots,\qquad
 \etah=\log2,\qquad
 \frac{\sh}{\etah}=1.3570186369\ldots .
\end{equation}
For every real \(Q\), the separable optimum is
\(2^{-\lfloor Q\rfloor}\): every occupied local unit of maximum energy
contributes at most \(\log2\) to log miss, and low-level inputs attain
every integer budget.

\subsection{The full fixed-use separation}
The three separable points are \((0,0),(1,\log2),(2,\log4)\), hence
\[
 \gs(c)=\min\{c\log2,\log4\}.
\]
For the entangled curve, \(\phi(s)=\log\max\{1,\lambda_+(s)\}\), where
\[
 \lambda_+(s)=\frac{2e^{-s}+3e^{-2s}
 +\sqrt{(2e^{-s}-3e^{-2s})^2+8e^{-3s}}}{2}.
\]
Its value at each \(c\) is the exact scalar minimization in
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed}. The excited threshold top vector has high-level
probability \((7-\sqrt{17})/10\), and mean
\[
 h_*=\frac{17-\sqrt{17}}{10}=1.2876894374\ldots .
\]
The full top space at \(s=\sh\) also contains ground. Therefore
\(g(c)=\sh c\) for \(0\leq c\leq h_*\).
At \(s=0\), \(A_+\) has top eigenvalue four and vector
\((-1/\sqrt3,\sqrt{2/3})\), of mean energy \(5/3\).
Thus \(g(c)=\log4\) for \(c\geq5/3\).

Concavity and the point \(g(h_*)>\!h_*\log2\), together with
\(g(0)=0\) and \(g(2)=\log4\), imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qcurvegap}
 g(c)>\gs(c)\quad\text{for every }0<c<2,
\end{equation}
with equality at \(c=0\) and all \(c\geq2\).
For each fixed interior density the miss-ratio logarithm is therefore
\(n[g(c)-\gs(c)]+O(\log n)\), a strict exponential advantage under an
exact hard-support ceiling.

\subsection{A small witness and a scalable explicit family}
For two uses at \(Q=3\), the basis
\(\{\ket{ll},(\ket{lh}+\ket{hl})/\sqrt2\}\) has compressed effect
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its lower eigenvalue is \((3-\sqrt5)/8<1/8\), with normalized eigenvector
proportional to
\(\ket{ll}-\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}(\ket{lh}+\ket{hl})/\sqrt2\).
The antisymmetric excited vector has miss \(1/4\); three low inputs have
miss \(1/8\); and the one-active-input optimum is \(1/4\).
Ground-pattern reduction thus also shows that this is the global
unrestricted-use optimum at \(Q=3\).
Repeating this block attains a rate
\(-\frac13\log((3-\sqrt5)/8)\); it does not attain \(\sh\).

For \(0\leq i\leq r\), let \(\ket{D_{r,i}}\) be the normalized Dicke
state with \(i\) high sites among \(r\) excited sites.
The explicit legal probe
\[
 \chi_{r,i}=\frac{B_+^{\otimes r}\ket{D_{r,i}}}
                  {\norm{B_+^{\otimes r}\ket{D_{r,i}}}}
\]
has maximum energy \(r+i\). In the Dicke basis its amplitudes are
proportional to
\[
 (-1)^j\binom ij\,\frac{2^{-(i-j)/2}}{\sqrt{\binom rj}},
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq i,
\]
up to an irrelevant common phase. Define the positive integer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
 W_{r,i}=2^{r-i}S_{r,i},\qquad
 S_{r,i}=\sum_{t=\max(0,2i-r)}^i
                 \binom it\binom{r-i}{i-t}3^t .
\end{equation}
Directly counting intersections of the two high-site sets gives
\[
 \bra{D_{r,i}}A_+^{\otimes r}\ket{D_{r,i}}=W_{r,i}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} then gives the exact finite miss
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicitmiss}
 p(\chi_{r,i})=\frac1{W_{r,i}}=\frac{2^{i-r}}{S_{r,i}}.
\end{equation}
For integer \(Q\), maximize this integer score over
\(\lceil Q/2\rceil\leq r\leq Q\), \(i=Q-r\).
This defines a deterministic family without a minimum-eigenvalue solver.
The chosen family need not attain the finite-\(Q\) optimum.

For completeness, a generating function already proves its asymptotic
rate. Put \(S_Q=\sum_{r+i=Q,\ 0\leq i\leq r}W_{r,i}\).
The symmetric-power trace identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gen}
 \sum_{Q\geq0}S_Qu^Q
 =\frac1{\det(I-uA_+\operatorname{diag}(1,u))}
 =\frac1{(1-u)(1-u-4u^2)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed the trace of \(\Sym^r(A_+\operatorname{diag}(1,u))\) is
\(\sum_i u^iW_{r,i}\).
The identity \(\sum_{r\geq0}\Tr\Sym^r(M)=1/\det(I-M)\) follows by
geometric series on its two eigenvalues; polynomial coefficient identities
extend it to every \(2\times2\) matrix.
The coefficients \(F_Q\) of \(1/(1-u-4u^2)\) obey
\[
 F_0=F_1=1,\quad F_Q=F_{Q-1}+4F_{Q-2},\quad
 F_Q=\frac{R^{Q+1}-R_-^{Q+1}}{\sqrt{17}},
 \quad R_-=\frac{1-\sqrt{17}}2.
\]
Thus \(S_Q=\sum_{j\leq Q}F_j=\Theta(R^Q)\).
One of at most \(Q+1\) scores is at least \(S_Q/(Q+1)\), proving an
explicit miss bound \(C(Q+1)R^{-Q}\).

\begin{proposition}[One-sided explicit prefactor]\label{prop:prefactor}
There are choices of the same probes with
\[
 p(\chi_{r,i})\leq C\sqrt{Q+1}\,R^{-Q},\qquad r+i=Q,
\]
and hence an attainable budget
\[
 \frac{\log(1/\epsilon)+\tfrac12\log\log(1/\epsilon)+O(1)}{\log R}.
\]
This does not assert optimality of the second-order coefficient \(1/2\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d=\sqrt{17}\),
\(p_*=(7-d)/10\), and \(q_*=3(5-d)/20\).
For a summand of \eqref{eq:score}, with \(p=i/r\), \(q=t/r\), define
\[
 f(p,q)=(1-p)\log2+
 p\,b(q/p)+(1-p)b((p-q)/(1-p))+q\log3,
 \quad b(x)=-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x).
\]
The pair \((p_*,q_*)\) is strictly inside the feasible region.
Differentiation and \(d^2=17\) give
\[
 \partial_qf(p_*,q_*)=0,\quad
 f(p_*,q_*)=(1+p_*)\log R,\quad
 \partial_pf(p_*,q_*)=\log R,
\]
while
\(\partial_{qq}f=-1/q-2/(p-q)-1/(1-2p+q)<0\).
Choose \(r\) nearest \(Q/(1+p_*)\) and \(i=Q-r\).
The \(q\)-saddle continues smoothly for \(p=i/r\) near \(p_*\).
The identities above show that its exponent \(rf(p,q(p))\) is
\(Q\log R+O(1)\).
Every integer \(t\) within a sufficiently small fixed multiple of
\(\sqrt r\) of this saddle changes that exponent by at most a constant.
Uniform elementary Stirling bounds in this interior neighborhood give
each corresponding product of binomials a lower bound
\(c r^{-1}R^Q\). There are \(\Theta(\sqrt r)\) such terms.
Their positive sum proves \(W_{r,i}\geq c'R^Q/\sqrt{Q+1}\).
Inverting gives the probe bound and the displayed budget.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The expected-energy comparison}
The constant \(\alpha\) in \eqref{eq:jensen} is the optimum exponent for
the different contract constraining only total expected input energy.
To see attainability, take a unit generalized maximizing vector \(w\)
with \(\ell=\bra wL\ket w=\alpha\bra wH\ket w\).
Project \(w^{\otimes n}\) onto an \(L^{(n)}\)-window
\([n(\ell-\delta_n),n(\ell+\delta_n)]\), with
\(\delta_n\to0\) and \(n\delta_n^2\to\infty\).
Scalar concentration makes the normalized projected state approach the
product state in norm. Its expected energy is
\(n\bra wH\ket w+o(n)\), and its miss is at most
\(e^{-n(\ell-\delta_n)}\). Together with \eqref{eq:jensen}, this proves
the expected-energy exponent \(\alpha\).
This projection has no hard-energy legality guarantee.
For \eqref{eq:qutrit},
\[
 L_+=(\log4)\ket v\bra v,\qquad
 v=(\sqrt{2/3},-\sqrt{1/3}),\qquad
 \alpha=\frac56\log4=\frac53\log2>\sh .
\]
The strict difference is therefore a difference of resource contracts,
not an inconsistency between exponents.

\section{Calibration, preparation costs, and scope}
\begin{proposition}[Calibration continuity under a preserved pure null]
\label{prop:calibration}
Suppose \(E'\) also satisfies \eqref{eq:contract} for the same \(H\) and
\((1-\delta)\ep\leq E'_+\leq(1+\delta)\ep\), \(0\leq\delta<1\).
Then
\[
 |\sh(E')-\sh(E)|,\quad
 |\etah(E')-\etah(E)|
 \ \leq\frac{-\log(1-\delta)}{h_{\min}} .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a pattern of \(r\) excited inputs, tensor order changes its miss by
factors between \((1-\delta)^r\) and \((1+\delta)^r\).
Every legal pattern has \(r\leq Q/h_{\min}\).
Taking minima over patterns, then logarithms divided by \(Q\) and limits,
proves the first bound. The same product argument proves the second.
\end{proof}
If \(\norm{E'-E}\leq\varepsilon<\lambda_{\min}(\ep)\), one may take
\(\delta=\varepsilon/\lambda_{\min}(\ep)\).
For the qutrit, a sufficient advantage-preserving margin is
\[
 \varepsilon<
 \frac14\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{\log R-\log2}{2}\right)\right]
 =0.0290961173\ldots ,
\]
provided \(E'\) remains a full-rank effect fixing the ground.
This is not robustness to a mixed null or nonzero false alarms.

The proofs specify accepted input states, rather than an uncharged
deterministic preparation process. In the type construction at tilt \(s\),
let \(\lambda=\norm{C_s}\) and
\[
 M=Be^{-sH/2}/\sqrt{\lambda}.
\]
Then \(M^\dagger M=C_s/\lambda\leq I\), so \(M\) is a valid local
postselected filter. On the exact-energy type vector \(y\), its
\(n\)-fold success probability is at least
\(p_{\mathrm{type}}\geq e^{-C_D}(n+1)^{-D}\), and its accepted output
is \(B^{\otimes n}y/\norm{B^{\otimes n}y}\).
If \(y\) is itself prepared by projecting \(w^{\otimes n}\) onto its
type, that projection succeeds with probability \(p_{\mathrm{type}}\);
the combined attempt succeeds with probability at least
\(p_{\mathrm{type}}^2\). Repeated attempts, coherent reference resources,
driving work, and the precision of amplitudes must be charged in a
preparation-cost contract. The operator \(B\) lowers the energy support,
so the accepted probe meets the declared hard ceiling exactly.

The one-dimensional order test and convex minimization involve fixed
finite matrices, but no bit-complexity bound or scalable physical
preparation bound is asserted. The results do not cover singular \(E\),
infinite-dimensional inputs, nonunique ground spaces, interacting input
Hamiltonians, \(n\)-dependent effects, unknown-channel acquisition,
ancilla-assisted tests, or adaptive stopping. These cases require their
own contracts and proofs.

Neighboring primary work includes mean-energy constrained channel
discrimination and its amortized converse framework
\cite{WildeEtAl}, mean-energy unitary discrimination
\cite{BeckerEtAl}, and optical coding under asymptotic photon-number
occupation constraints \cite{BardhanEtAl}.
The latter permits vanishing leakage and averages codewords; it is not
the exact support condition in \eqref{eq:missdef}.
Standard matrix log-convexity and type-probability machinery are recorded
in \cite{BourinLee,CsiszarShields}. These references delimit nearby
interfaces and support established ingredients. The complete exact-contract
priority question and external proof review remain open.
