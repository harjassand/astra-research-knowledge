# Global modulation moments and the nine-moment dual

## Result

The global modulation identity gives more than nonnegativity: for every left-
SO(2)-invariant quartic and every affine background, it yields an explicit
positive-semidefinite completion of the N794 nine-moment functional. The
completion is an (8\times8) integral of acoustic rank-one atoms. An exact
rational example verifies all nine dual equations.

There is also a useful cone statement. In either the nine-dimensional
left-SO(2) coefficient space or the full 35-dimensional planar quartic space,
the **closed conic hull** of moment vectors from the positive modulation family
is the full dual cone of rank-one-convex quartics. This follows by polarizing
the family and taking the small-amplitude limit of a single rank-one wave.
It says the positive profiles generate every dual moment functional after
closure. It does not say that the moment vector of every compatible gradient
belongs to that cone; proving that inclusion is the unresolved global
quasiconvexity step.

I tested profile generation separately. The exact periodic potential below has
a connected primitive triad, full-rank gradient covariance, and an exact
nine-moment vector, but it cannot be a convex-loop modulation for any affine
carrier and cannot be a one-coordinate complex envelope. Its connected
frequency classes cannot be split by the current resonance-separated,
support-partition packet rule. This is an exact obstruction to that direct
profile-generation scheme. It is not a separating functional for its
nine-moment vector and is not a negative Jensen gap.

## Explicit dual completion from the flux identity

Use the N794 basis

\[
f=(u^2,v^2,uv,w^2,uw,vw,qu,qv,qw),
\quad
u=|A_{\cdot1}|^2,\ v=|A_{\cdot2}|^2,\ w=A_{\cdot1}\cdot A_{\cdot2},\ q=\det A.
\]
For a periodic modulation \(\varphi=rV(k\cdot x)\), an affine background
\(B\), and normalized torus average, let

\[
m_i(B,\varphi)=\mathbb E f_i(B+D\varphi)-f_i(B).
\]
Write \(W=V'\), \(P=rW\otimes k\), and \(Q=V\otimes\nabla r\). The
global identity gives

\[
m\cdot p=
\mathbb E\,\mathcal R_F(B+P,Q)
+\mathbb E\,a(k\cdot x)r^2\int_0^1t\,
D^2F(B+tP)[V\otimes k,V\otimes k]dt,
\]
where \(F=\sum_i p_i f_i\). For a quartic,

\[
\mathcal R_F(C,Q)=\int_0^1(1-s)D^2F(C+sQ)[Q,Q]ds.
\]

For each point, rotate output coordinates by \(U\in SO(2)\) with
\(Ue_1=V/|V|\), and set \(x(A)=\operatorname{vec}(U^TA)\) in N794's
row-major order. For a spatial vector \(b=(b_1,b_2)\), set

\[
z(A,b)=\begin{pmatrix}b_1x(A)\\b_2x(A)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^8.
\]

If \(Q_F(K)\) is the N794 acoustic Gram matrix for any skew completion
\(K\), its defining identity is

\[
D^2F(A)[V\otimes b,V\otimes b]
=|V|^2 z(A,b)^TQ_F(K)z(A,b).
\]

Consequently the following matrix is an explicit completion:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_B={}&\mathbb E\int_0^1(1-s)|V|^2\,
 z(B+P+sQ,\nabla r)z(B+P+sQ,\nabla r)^Tds\\
&+\mathbb E\,a(k\cdot x)r^2\int_0^1t|V|^2\,
 z(B+tP,k)z(B+tP,k)^Tdt.
\end{aligned}
\]

The weights are nonnegative, so \(Y_B\succeq0\). Each outer product has
off-diagonal block \(b_1b_2xx^T\), which is symmetric. Therefore it
annihilates all six skew-block constraints \(\langle C_j,Y_B\rangle=0\).
Expanding \(Q_F(K)=\sum_i p_iB_i+\sum_j k_jC_j\) and comparing the
coefficients of the arbitrary \(p_i,k_j\) gives

\[
\langle B_i,Y_B\rangle=m_i(B,\varphi),\qquad
\langle C_j,Y_B\rangle=0.
\]

Thus the N794 dual completion is explicit for this family, rather than only
existential by finite SDP duality. The formula is compatible with every
affine background. For each fixed quartic \(F_p\), if its complete affine
quadratic has a minimizer \(B_*(p)\), then \(Y_{B_*(p)}\) certifies the moment
vector at that quartic's optimizer and its pairing equals the optimized gap.
The optimizer can depend on \(p\), so these are background-indexed
completions, not one common background selected for every coefficient vector.
Nonnegativity for all \(B\) makes each quadratic bounded below; its Hessian
is positive semidefinite for compatible gradients, so its minimum is
attained (with the usual pseudoinverse when singular).

An exact check uses \(r=1+\tfrac12\cos x\), \(V=(\cos y,\sin y)\),
\(k=e_2\), and \(B=0\). Its moment vector and one completion are

\[
m=\left(\frac3{128},\frac{227}{128},\frac{17}{128},0,0,0,0,0,0\right),
\qquad
Y=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac1{512},0,0,\frac{17}{256},0,0,0,\frac{227}{512}\right).
\]

The nine exact pairings \(\langle B_i,Y\rangle\) equal the displayed \(m_i\);
all six skew constraints vanish. The check is in
[`verify_global_moment_bridge.py`](verify_global_moment_bridge.py), with
machine-readable output in
[`verification_results.json`](verification_results.json).

## Closed-cone generation and its limit

Let \(C_d\) be the cone of rank-one-convex homogeneous planar quartics in a
fixed coefficient basis of dimension \(d=9\) (left-SO(2) invariant) or
\(d=35\) (unrestricted). Let \(S_d\) be all moment vectors

\[
m(B,\varphi)_i=\mathbb E f_i(B+D\varphi)-f_i(B)
\]
from the global modulation family, with arbitrary affine \(B\). The proved
identity gives \(S_d\subseteq C_d^*\). Conversely, if a coefficient vector
\(p\) is nonnegative on every element of \(S_d\), take the allowed
zero-carrier ellipse modulation

\[
\varphi_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon a\sin(b\cdot x),\qquad
D\varphi_\varepsilon=\varepsilon(a\otimes b)\cos(b\cdot x).
\]

For quartic \(F_p\), exact Taylor expansion gives

\[
m(B,\varphi_\varepsilon)\cdot p
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}D^2F_p(B)[a\otimes b,a\otimes b]
+\frac{3\varepsilon^4}{8}F_p(a\otimes b).
\]

Divide by \(\varepsilon^2\) and let \(\varepsilon\to0\). Nonnegativity on
all modulation moments forces every acoustic Hessian to be nonnegative, so
\(p\in C_d\). For any \(a\ne0\), choose a nondegenerate ellipse with
\(V(0)=a\); rational spatial directions are dense and the acoustic form is
continuous. Hence

\[
(\operatorname{cone}S_d)^*=C_d,
\qquad
\overline{\operatorname{cone}S_d}=C_d^*.
\]

In the nine-dimensional case N794 identifies \(C_9^*\) with the set of
moment vectors admitting its (8\times8\) PSD completion. In dimension 35,
the same polar statement is valid, but no N794 nine-moment/8-by-8 interface is
being asserted.

The inclusion still needed for global closure is

\[
\{m(B,D\psi):D\psi\text{ any compatible periodic gradient}\}
\subseteq C_d^*.
\]

That is the rank-one-convex-to-quasiconvex implication itself. The polar
generation result characterizes the target cone; it does not put arbitrary
compatible gradients into it. The exact negative interaction increment
\(-95/256\) for determinant-square in the source packet independently rules
out adding positive block margins without cross-resonance control.

## Exact profile obstruction to direct generation

Consider the smooth periodic potential

\[
\begin{aligned}
\psi_1&=\cos x-\sin x+\cos y-\sin y,\\
\psi_2&=\cos x+\sin x+\cos y-\sin y+\cos(x+y)+\sin(x+y).
\end{aligned}
\]

Its three frequency classes \(e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2\) form a primitive connected
triad. Its exact zero-background nine-moment vector is

\[
\left(\frac{27}{2},\frac{31}{2},\frac{19}{2},
\frac{11}{2},\frac{11}{2},\frac{11}{2},0,-2,-1\right).
\]

The gradient covariance \(C=\mathbb E[D\psi(D\psi)^T]\) in row-major
coordinates is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\0&1&0&1\\0&0&2&1\\0&1&1&2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det C=1,
\]
and \(\mathbb E|D\psi|^4=48\). Thus this is a genuinely multidirectional,
orientation-sensitive moment profile, not a rank-one or reflection-even
example.

For any proposed affine carrier \(k=(p,q)\), a representation
\(\psi=rV(px+qy)\) would force

\[
\det(\psi,\partial_{(q,-p)}\psi)=0
\]
pointwise: the carrier is constant along \((q,-p)\), leaving only the scalar
amplitude to vary. Let \(D_x=\det(\psi,\psi_x)\) and
\(D_y=\det(\psi,\psi_y)\). Direct exact Fourier calculation gives

\[
\widehat D_x(1,2)=i/2,\quad \widehat D_y(1,2)=0;
\qquad
\widehat D_x(0,1)=1,\quad \widehat D_y(0,1)=1/2.
\]

The first coefficient of \(qD_x-pD_y\) forces \(q=0\); the second then
forces \(p=0\). So no nonzero affine carrier works, for any loop shape,
including after an invertible linear change of input coordinates. At \(k=0\)
the image would lie on one fixed line, but \(\psi(0,0)=(2,3)\) and
\(\psi(\pi,0)=(0,-1)\) are not collinear.

The companion class \(z(x)e^{iny}\) also cannot contain this profile: the
complex field \(\psi_1+i\psi_2\) has nonzero vertical Fourier frequencies
\(-1,0,1\), while that class has a single vertical frequency \(n\). Under
the exact packet rule, the primitive triad must be kept in one packet, so it
cannot be split into several positive blocks. This refutes a direct
support-partition generation of all compatible profiles by the current
modulation and one-coordinate-envelope families. The exact moments above are
reported to make the obstruction auditable; I have not proved that this
moment vector lies outside the conic hull of family moment vectors, nor that
it lacks a PSD completion. It is not a Morrey counterexample.

## Scope against N794, N806 and N809

- **N794 / left-SO(2), nine coefficients:** the explicit (8\times8) matrix
  above is in exactly this dual basis and closes the completion for the
  modulation family. It is not a completion theorem for all compatible
  gradients. N794's full nine-moment compatibility gate remains open.
- **Full35 quartics:** the global modulation identity itself applies without
  symmetry and the closed-cone polar argument extends to 35 coefficients.
  The explicit small dual matrix above does not extend by this derivation,
  because N794's (8\times8) SOS interface uses left-SO(2) reduction.
- **N806/N809 local results:** N806 handles fixed-contact cubic/cross-Hessian
  control in the nine-coefficient class; N809 upgrades all-background
  stability to full35 at simple rank-three transverse contacts, for
  determinant-balanced laws relatively close in (L^2/L^4) to a fixed
  rank-one direction. Rank-two tangent contacts, large-transverse profiles,
  and global Morrey closure remain open. Those local hypotheses do not cover
  the connected-triad profile above.
- **Affine optimization:** the flux identity and its (Y_B) hold separately
  for every (B), so its conclusion survives complete affine minimization.
  This is stronger than a fixed-background evaluation. It does not permit
  independently optimizing spatial packets without the exact cross terms.
