SU(3) WALL COUNT: ALL-MATRIX ADVERSARIAL FILTER AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: the proposed weighted low-spectrum count for a>=b>=1, with
arbitrary complex matrices on End(V_(a,b)); focus on whether near-wall
stable multiplicities or nonnormal rank-Q filters refute it.
Status: no weighted-count counterexample established. An exact SVD
identity rules out a specifically nonnormal improvement at every rank.
The stable L_T counterexample survives only as a diagnostic: it does not
control the required small eigenvalues of L_S inside multiplicity blocks.

1. TARGET AND EXACT BLOCK TEST

Put N=a+b, B=b+1, d=(a+1)B(N+2)/2, and

  L_w=L_T/N^2+L_S/(N sqrt(C_S)),
  L_T=sum_A ad(T_A)^2,  L_S=sum_A ad(S_A)^2.

For interior carriers, decompose

  End(V_(a,b))=direct_sum_(p,q) V_(p,q) tensor M_(p,q).

On each isotypic block the two terms commute and

  L_w|_(p,q)= c2(p,q)/N^2 I + K_(p,q)/(N sqrt(C_S)),
  K_(p,q)>=0,  dim M_(p,q)=m_(p,q).

Thus, writing kappa_(p,q,j) for the eigenvalues of K_(p,q), the exact
weighted count is

  D_w(T/N^2)=sum_(p,q)!=(0,0):c2(p,q)<=T dim(V_(p,q))
       # {j: kappa_(p,q,j)<=sqrt(C_S) T/N-c2(p,q)sqrt(C_S)/N}.
                                                               (1.1)

In particular, the stable ordinary-Casimir multiplicity contributes to
D_w only through eigenvalues of K below the stated threshold. The exact
SU(3) normalization gives sqrt(C_S) comparable to B for a>=b>=1.

2. ADVERSARIAL NEAR-WALL SCALE AND WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY REFUTE THE COUNT

The exact stable branch range recorded in
`../branching/weighted_branching_audit.txt` is

  m_(p,q)=min(p,q)+1,  p congruent q (mod 3),  p+q<=b.

At cutoff T=B, choose p,q in an interior cone of size sqrt(B). The
identity Weyl term is the full multiplicity there, and summing gives
the known exact lower bound D_T(B)>=c B^3. Along a sequence with
B->infinity and B/sqrt(N)->0, the proposed weighted right side at T=B
is only O(B^2), because (B/N)B^3=o(B^2). This proves that dropping L_S
is an invalid proof shortcut.

It does not refute D_w. For these labels, (1.1) requires

  kappa_(p,q,j) <= O(B^2/N)                              (2.1)

even at the largest admissible Casimir. To violate the O(B^2) target,
one would need enough multiplicity eigenvalues below (2.1). The stable
ordinary count supplies about B^3 weighted modes, but supplies no
information about their kappa values. Even a hypothetical *one* soft
mode per stable block would yield only about B^(5/2) weighted modes
(there are order B labels and each dim(V_(p,q)) is order B^(3/2)); this
would already exceed B^2 when B=o(sqrt(N)). This is a useful concrete
adversarial test for future block calculations, not a constructed
family: only the adjoint block currently has a proved soft mode, and a
single fixed block has constant total dimension.

The exact GT labels explain the gap. In the stable range, the
multiplicity is in bijection with spins

  j=|p-q|/3, |p-q|/3+1, ..., (2 min(p,q)+max(p,q))/3.

This is a cardinality/branching statement; it does not diagonalize
K_(p,q). No argument here puts one eigenvalue below (2.1) in every
stable block, nor proves that all but a controlled number exceed it.
The exact adjoint two-copy calculation in
`../subelliptic/adjoint_block_and_count_obstruction.txt` has one
T-aligned eigenvalue kappa~B^2/N^2 and one hard eigenvalue of order one
near the wall. This is consistent with one soft fiber channel but does
not extend by itself to growing (p,q).

3. EXACT NONNORMAL-TO-PSD REDUCTION FOR EVERY RANK BUDGET

Let G be any Hermitian generator and write an arbitrary complex matrix
in SVD form A=U diag(s_1,...,s_d) V*, s_i>=0. Put H=U*GU,
K=V*GV, A_L=|A|=U diag(s)U*, and A_R=|A*|=V diag(s)V*. Directly
expanding commutators and pairing (i,j) with (j,i) gives

  e_G(A)-[e_G(A_L)+e_G(A_R)]/2
       =sum_(i,j) s_i s_j |H_(ij)-K_(ij)|^2 >=0,
  e_G(X)=||[G,X]||_HS^2.                                  (3.1)

Equivalently,

  2e_G(A)-e_G(A_L)-e_G(A_R)
   =2 sum_i s_i^2 |H_(ii)-K_(ii)|^2
       +4 sum_(i<j) s_i s_j |H_(ij)-K_(ij)|^2.             (3.2)

Both identities are exact for complex/non-normal A, repeated singular
values, and zero singular values. Apply (3.1) to every T_A and S_A and
weight by the strictly positive coefficients N^-2 and
(N sqrt(C_S))^-1. Then

  E_w(A)>=(E_w(|A|)+E_w(|A*|))/2.                         (3.3)

The two moduli have the same rank and Hilbert-Schmidt norm as A. At
least one therefore has energy no greater than A, proving for each
1<=Q<=d

  inf_(rank A<=Q, ||A||_HS=1) E_w(A)
    =inf_(F>=0, rank F<=Q, ||F||_HS=1) E_w(F).             (3.4)

So an adversary that relies on phases, unequal left/right singular
vectors, or nonnormality cannot improve the rank-Q optimum. This is the
precise sense in which that kind of rank-Q filter counterexample fails.
Equation (3.4) does not rule out a positive semidefinite counterexample:
it proves no diagonal, Cartan, fiber-preserving, or coherent-state
ansatz is optimal. The positive-filter search remains the real issue.

An independent finite checker was saved as
`svd_weighted_check.py`. It builds the exact finite harmonic carrier
generators and S_A, samples complex rank-Q SVD matrices, computes both
weighted commutator energies, and compares the observed gap with the
right side of (3.1) summed over T and S. For (a,b)=(3,1),(4,1),(4,2),
and (5,2), it checks 16 seeded samples per carrier over ranks 1,2,
floor(d/3),floor(d/2). The largest absolute identity residual was
1.8e-14; all observed gaps were positive. These are floating-point
finite checks only; (3.1)-(3.4) are the proof.

4. ALL-MATRIX LOWER CONTROL THAT IS AVAILABLE WITHOUT THE GATE

Since L_w>=L_T/N^2 as positive operators, every complex rank-Q matrix
satisfies

  Lambda_w(Q)>=Lambda_T(Q)/N^2.                            (4.1)

Schur partial trace and the Schmidt-rank-Q bound apply to arbitrary
complex matrices, so the global SU(3) Casimir count yields the
unconditional estimate recorded in the companion audit
`../adversarial/weighted_complex_rank_audit.txt`:

  Lambda_w(Q)>=c N^-2 (d/Q)^(1/3), 1<=Q<=d/2.              (4.2)

For rank one, the exact all-matrix lower is Lambda_w(1)>=2/N; the
highest-weight projector gives a matching O(1/N) upper. Near full rank,
the exact right-kernel argument gives

  Lambda_w(d-k)>=3k/(N^2 d), 1<=k<d.                       (4.3)

These controls cover arbitrary complex matrices, but (4.2)-(4.3) are
weaker than the proposed 3-to-2 profile in the growing intermediate
regimes. The SVD reduction does not strengthen them.

5. FINITE WEIGHTED SPECTRUM CHECK AND BOUNDARY

I ran the existing direct superoperator checker
`../adversarial/finite_spectrum_check.py`. It forms the full d^2 by d^2
matrix L_w and diagonalizes it for (a,b)=(2,1),(3,1),(4,1),(2,2),(3,2).
At t=x/N^2 for integer x<=N, the largest reported ratio

  (D_w(t)-1) / {d[(N^2/B)t^2+N^3 t^3]}

was 0.275, at (a,b,x)=(4,1,4); all five cases remain finite small
diagnostics. They do not reach a growing near-wall regime and cannot
establish a uniform constant.

Verdict. The proposed weighted count is neither proved nor refuted by
this adversarial audit. The known stable multiplicity refutes only the
ordinary-Casimir shortcut. The exact all-matrix SVD identity eliminates
nonnormal rank-Q filters as a separate mechanism, while leaving PSD
filters unrestricted. A decisive counterexample to the count would
require a growing family of (p,q) blocks with many K_(p,q)-eigenvalues
at or below O(BT/N), starting with the sharper O(B^2/N) threshold at
T=B. A decisive proof would instead need a uniform sublevel-count
bound on those exact multiplicity matrices. Neither is supplied by
zero-weight multiplicities or the current adjoint calculation.
