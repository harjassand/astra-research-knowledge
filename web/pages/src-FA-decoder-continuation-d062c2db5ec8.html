VERDICT

The finite-horizon compiler in RESULT.txt is mathematically sound for its
stated exact coordinate-block class, conditional on the inherited
all-schedules radius theorem in mathematical_operation/PROOF. Its exact
rounding, scalar-noise alphabet, lattice embedding, and blockwise causal
composition close. The claimed radius remains O((1-gamma)^-3 sqrt(d)); for
fixed block rank r, exhaustive dynamic programming has runtime polynomial in
explicit horizon N, rational input bit length, and 1/(1-gamma), with a degree
depending on r. This is not polynomial in log(1/(1-gamma)).

The current proof/compiler statement is specifically about disjoint
coordinate-support blocks. A general *rational orthogonal-subspace* version
is also valid by the weighted-basis construction below, but is not what the
current finite-horizon code executes. discover_orthogonal_blocks.py supplies
an exact dense example and useful basis machinery; it is not a general
finite-horizon compiler implementation. block_controller.py and
periodic_block_lift.py instead establish an executable product for the
particular inherited 2D periodic controller. Keep these three evidence scopes
separate.

1. COORDINATE-BLOCK COMPILER CHECK

For a local block of dimension r>=1 and length n>=1, let M=ceil(8nr),
round each coordinate of Mv to a nearest integer z, and set
vbar=2z/(2M+r). Then ||z-Mv||_2<=sqrt(r)/2. Therefore

  ||vbar|| <= (2M+sqrt(r))/(2M+r) <= 1,
  ||vbar-v|| <= (sqrt(r)+r)/(2M+r) <= r/M <= 1/(8n).

All inequalities are exact; the middle bounds use sqrt(r)<=r. The extra r in
the denominator is the norm-restoring normalization, not an approximation.

Let m=1-gamma, K=ceil(max(8n,8/m)), and delta=1/K. If the acquired scalar
reading zeta_hat obeys |zeta_hat-zeta|<=delta/8, nearest-grid rounding
zeta_bar=delta*round(zeta_hat/delta) gives |zeta_bar-zeta|<=5delta/8. Its
alphabet can be indexed by integers k with |k|<=ceil(K gamma)+1; the
alphabet size is A=2ceil(K gamma)+3=O(K). Every represented value obeys
|zeta_bar|<=gamma+2delta, since ceil(K gamma)+1<=K gamma+2. As
K>=8/m, delta<=m/8, so the virtual game has positive margin
m'=1-(gamma+2delta)>=3m/4.

Apply the inherited arbitrary-schedule theorem to the rational schedule
vbar_t and margin m'. Its bound is at most C(m')^-3 sqrt(r), hence at most
C(4/3)^3 m^-3 sqrt(r). The theorem's policy need not be extracted: every
state reachable from the origin in this rational virtual game lies on the
lattice with denominator L=K(2M+r). In integer lattice coordinates p, the
transition is exactly

  p' = p + 2z(K epsilon + k),   epsilon in {-1,+1}, k in the finite alphabet.

The finite predecessor recursion over lattice states in the radius-B ball
therefore contains the theorem's winning policy restricted to reachable
states. Exact backward induction decides whether the origin wins. Search
integer radii by doubling and stop at the first winning radius. The inherited
bound implies termination; it does not need to be known by the running
search. If R is the inherited upper bound, the first winning tested radius
is O(R), including integer rounding.

A state in the radius-B ball has each integer lattice coordinate bounded by
BL, so S<= (1+2BL)^r states suffice. The drift alphabet has A=O(K) values.
At one time/state, checking both signs and all alphabet values costs O(Ar)
integer operations. One radius trial costs O(n S A r) operations and
O(nS) policy/winning-mask bits; the geometrically increasing trials are
bounded by the final trial up to a constant depending on fixed r. Here
L=K(2M+r)=O(r n(n+1/m)), and B=O(m^-3 sqrt(r)). Thus S and total work are
polynomial in n and 1/m for fixed r. The exponent grows with r.

Exact rational cost: z is found by integer division on the input rationals;
its coordinates have O(log M) bits because ||v||<=1. L, transition shifts,
and grid indices have bit length polynomial in log n+log(1/m) and the input
bit length. Safe-ball comparisons are integer/rational squared-norm tests,
with no floating-point tolerance. If gamma or m is an input, the interface
must supply an exact rational gamma, or a certified rational upper bound on
it with positive known margin; an unknown real margin cannot be used to form
K. The number of *bits* in a scalar reading is O(log n+log(1/m)), but its
required absolute error is delta/8=Theta(min(1/n,m)). The lattice denominator
and number of states scale polynomially in n and 1/m, not merely in
log(1/m). Therefore the result is pseudo-polynomial in the margin when the
margin is encoded in binary and can be exponentially small in its bit length.

Transfer to the physical block is bounded per step by

  ||(epsilon+zeta)v-(epsilon+zeta_bar)vbar||
    <= |epsilon+zeta| ||v-vbar|| + |zeta-zeta_bar| ||vbar||
    <= 2/(8n) + 5delta/8.

Summing n steps and using K>=8n gives at most 1/4+5/64=21/64<1. Thus the
physical radius is at most B+1. Since m<=1 and r>=1, the additive 1 is
absorbed into the same O(m^-3 sqrt(r)) radius. This comparison uses the
same postdecision scalar zeta and does not replace its causal choices by a
global covariance.

2. RATIONAL ORTHOGONAL SUBSPACES (PROOF EXTENSION, NOT CURRENT EXECUTABLE
   GENERAL COMPILER)

The same claim extends from coordinate blocks to mutually orthogonal
rational subspaces U_j of rank r_j<=r, provided the exact decomposition can
be supplied or recovered and each input direction lies in one U_j. One
exact recovery method for an explicit rational direction list is to connect
two nonzero directions when their rational dot product is nonzero. Different
connected components then have pairwise zero cross-dot-products, so their
spans are orthogonal. Exact rank tests reject components above r. This costs
O(N^2 d) rational dot-product work plus polynomial-bit exact linear algebra.

For each component, construct a rational orthogonal basis b_1,...,b_q by
Gram-Schmidt and rescale each basis vector by a power of two so
1<=w_i=||b_i||^2<4. This is possible with exact rational comparisons and
polynomial bit cost. Write each direction as v=sum_i c_i b_i; the
coefficients and weights w_i are rational. Use the weighted norm
||c||_G^2=sum_i w_i c_i^2, which is exactly the physical Euclidean norm.
Round z_i=nearest(M c_i), use M=ceil(16 n q), and set
vbar=sum_i [z_i/(M+q)] b_i. If e=z-Mc, then ||e||_G<=sqrt(q), because
|e_i|<=1/2 and w_i<4. Also ||c||_G<=1. Consequently

  ||vbar|| = ||z||_G/(M+q) <= (M+sqrt(q))/(M+q) <= 1,
  ||vbar-v|| = ||e-qc||_G/(M+q)
                 <= (sqrt(q)+q)/(M+q) <= 2q/M <= 1/(8n).

This is the exact rational-basis analogue of the coordinate normalization;
using the coordinate formula unchanged for a non-unit basis would not be
justified. Keep the same K and scalar rounding. In basis coefficient lattice
coordinates, take L=K(M+q); transitions are p'=p+z(K epsilon+k). The
weighted ball is tested exactly after clearing the rational denominators of
w_i. Since w_i>=1, each coordinate satisfies |p_i|<=BL, so the state count
is at most (1+2BL)^q. The inherited theorem applies in the physical
q-dimensional Euclidean subspace and yields the same radius. For fixed q,
basis computation, coefficient conversion, common-denominator construction,
state tests, and table generation all have polynomial bit cost. Orthogonal
composition then gives squared global radius at most
C'^2 m^-6 sum_j r_j <= C'^2 m^-6 d.

This extension is a proof-level/compiler-design result, not an executed
finite-horizon implementation in the reviewed files. The existing
coordinate-support compiler needs no weighted metric. The dense discovery
script's one Hadamard-style fixture is evidence for a rotated example, not a
benchmark or a universal certificate.

3. CAUSALITY, INTERLEAVING, AND EVIDENCE SCOPE

The predecessor recursion is existential in the current sign and universal
in every allowed k. By induction it therefore handles a scalar drift chosen
adaptively after the sign and after the complete prior history; it is not a
fixed/open-loop-noise argument. Each orthogonal component has its own local
clock and local virtual state. A global adaptive adversary induces, on each
component's activations, one of the local adaptive scalar sequences already
covered by that universal quantifier. Inactive components do not move.
Consequently arbitrary interleaving preserves every local radius and
orthogonality sums their squared radii. This requires that each component's
local direction subsequence is the prescribed/compiled one; choosing when
to activate a component may be adaptive, but changing its local direction
order is outside the compiled schedule interface.

The concrete 2D product is a different, stronger-running-time artifact for
its specific schedule family: it reuses the inherited local period-2 table,
keeps one phase per block, and has an all-time invariant. The small exact
finite certificate in block_controller.py is another local finite-game
receipt. Neither demonstrates that arbitrary fixed-r schedules have been
run through the general DP. verify_block_certificate.py's reported
51,195,620-transition total is retained as prior evidence; it was not
recomputed during this review.

4. OPEN SCOPED CAVEATS

- The radius constant C comes from the inherited theorem and is not numerically
  extracted in the reviewed compiler note. Radius doubling still halts by
  that theorem and gives an asymptotic polynomial bound with an unspecified
  absolute hidden constant; it does not provide an a priori practical time
  cap or a concrete numerical radius without extracting C.
- The current general compiler claim is established for exact coordinate
  blocks. The weighted rational-basis argument above closes the mathematical
  extension to rational orthogonal subspaces, but the reviewed files do not
  implement or benchmark that general weighted-metric DP.
- Complexity is polynomial in 1/m, not in the bit length log(1/m), and
  exponential in the block rank if r is allowed to grow.
- An executable local 2D controller, a proof-theoretic finite-r compiler,
  and a supplied rational decomposition are distinct deliverables and must
  not be conflated.
