1. EXACT INTERFACE AND CONVENTIONS

H is any finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, d=dim H. States rho_0,
rho_1 are arbitrary positive semidefinite trace-one matrices; they may be
singular. B:B(H)->B(H tensor H) is ONE Schrödinger CPTP channel supplied
independently of the unknown label. Let B_a=Tr_other B for a=0,1 and assume

  epsilon=max_{x=0,1; a=0,1} T(B_a rho_x,rho_x),
  T(rho,sigma)=||rho-sigma||_1/2.

No output independence, permutation symmetry, entropic bound, dimension
bound, common reference, domination, faithfulness, or spectral gap is used.
The root fidelity and affinity are UNSQUARED quantities

  F(rho,sigma)=||sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma)||_1,
  A(rho,sigma)=Tr[sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma)],
  Delta=F-A.

Theorem. For every integer L>=0 there is one full-domain EB channel E_L
that works for both promised states and obeys

  max_x T(E_L rho_x,rho_x)
    <= eta_L := L epsilon + sqrt(ln(2)/(2*2^L)),                 (1)
  Delta(rho_0,rho_1)<=2 sqrt(2 eta_L).                         (2)

All information logarithms in the proof are natural. Thus, with
c=sqrt(ln(2)/2),

  v(epsilon)=min{1, inf_{L>=0 integer} 2 sqrt(2[L epsilon+c 2^(-L/2)])}

is a uniform modulus. Define v(0)=0. For 0<epsilon<=1, taking
L=ceil(2 log_2(1/epsilon)) gives

  Delta<=min{1,2 sqrt(2 epsilon
               [2 log_2(1/epsilon)+1+c])}.                   (3)

Here Delta<=1 follows from 0<=A<=F<=1. If the broadcasting error is instead
the FULL trace norm beta=2 epsilon, the exact corresponding statements are

  max_x ||E_L rho_x-rho_x||_1
    <= L beta+sqrt(2 ln(2)/2^L),                             (4)
  Delta<=2 sqrt(L beta+sqrt(2 ln(2)/2^L)).                    (5)

These conversions contain no additional hidden factor of two.

2. ITERATION OF THE SAME BROADCASTER

Let C_0=id and C_(L+1)=B^(tensor 2^L) composed with C_L. This applies B
once to each current receiver and produces K=2^L receivers. A receiver's
channel is a composition of L maps selected from B_0,B_1. For a promised
rho_x, if a partial path output is omega, then trace contraction gives

  T(B_a omega,rho_x)
    <=T(B_a omega,B_a rho_x)+T(B_a rho_x,rho_x)
    <=T(omega,rho_x)+epsilon.

Induction proves T(C_L(rho_x)_j,rho_x)<=L epsilon for every x,j. This
uses the one-step premise only on rho_x, not on all intermediate states.
Joint C_L(rho_x) may be arbitrarily correlated or entangled.

3. FINITE BINARY EXTRACTION, INCLUDING PER-STATE ERROR

We prove a lemma for arbitrary joint states Omega_0,Omega_1 on
Q_1 tensor ... tensor Q_K. A uniform BINARY DESIGN PRIOR P(X=x)=1/2
is used to build a common protocol. It places no prior assumption on the
actual unknown input, whose error is bounded separately for each label.

Define a sequential adaptive two-outcome measurement on receivers 1..K.
At stage j the preceding binary record is h. Put

  p_h=P(H_(j-1)=h), q_x(h)=P(X=x|h),
  tau_x^h=conditional state of Q_j under Omega_x after prefix h.

For p_h=0 assign any states; for q_x(h)=0 its tau_x^h can be arbitrary.
These values are computed from the known pair, not read from the unknown
label. Measure on Q_j the projector onto the positive spectral subspace
of tau_0^h-tau_1^h and its complement. Let Y_j be the outcome. The
conditional outcome laws r_0,r_1 satisfy

  TV(r_0,r_1)=T(tau_0^h,tau_1^h)=:D_h.

Indeed the difference of the two states has trace zero, and the positive
spectral projector captures exactly its positive trace, ||difference||_1/2.
For binary X a direct calculation gives

  TV(P_(X,Y_j|h),P_(X|h) P_(Y_j|h))=2 q_0(h)q_1(h)D_h=:d_h.   (6)

The two label blocks of the joint-minus-product law are opposite, each
with coefficient q_0 q_1 times r_0-r_1. This proves (6), including q_x=0.

Classical Pinsker in natural logarithms is D(P||Q)>=2 TV(P,Q)^2. One
elementary proof first coarse-grains to the event {P>=Q}, using log-sum.
For the resulting Bernoulli parameters a,b, relative entropy as a function
of a has second derivative 1/[a(1-a)]>=4 and value/first derivative zero
at a=b. Hence it is >=2(a-b)^2. Boundary cases follow by limits.

Consequently

  g_j:=I(X:Y_j|H_(j-1))>=2 sum_h p_h d_h^2.                  (7)

The same complete sequential protocol determines all g_j. The classical
chain rule and nonnegative conditional entropy imply

  sum_(j=1)^K g_j=I(X:Y_1,...,Y_K)<=H(X)=ln(2).

Choose one fixed j with g_j<=ln(2)/K. The final recovery protocol performs
ONLY its prefix stages 1..j-1; the j-th Helstrom measurement is used only
to analyze error. For each prefix record h prepare the quantum state

  xi_h=q_0(h)tau_0^h+q_1(h)tau_1^h.

The prefix measurement acts on factors other than Q_j. Its nonselective
action therefore preserves the original Q_j marginal. Let p(h|x) be the
prefix outcome law under Omega_x. Bayes' rule for the design prior gives
p(h|x)=2 p_h q_x(h), and

  Omega_(x,j)=sum_h p(h|x)tau_x^h,
  R(Omega_x)=sum_h p(h|x)xi_h.

Trace convexity, EXACT homogeneity of T for this two-state mixture, and
the cancellation specific to the balanced binary prior yield, for EACH x,

  T(Omega_(x,j),R(Omega_x))
    <=sum_h p(h|x) T(tau_x^h,xi_h)
     =sum_h 2 p_h q_x(h)q_(1-x)(h)D_h
     =sum_h p_h d_h
    <=sqrt(sum_h p_h d_h^2)
    <=sqrt(g_j/2)<=sqrt(ln(2)/(2K)).                         (8)

There is no conversion from mean error to worst error. The two per-label
upper bounds are literally equal. Actual errors need not be equal.

The prefix is a genuine finite POVM: for a record h the Kraus operator is
the tensor product of the adaptive projectors selected along h, with
identities on the unmeasured factors. Its effect W_h=K_h* K_h is positive;
summing over the records gives I by sequential completeness. At j=1 the
prefix is empty, W=I, and xi is the averaged Q_1 state. Thus K=1 is legal.

Apply this lemma to Omega_x=C_L(rho_x). The input effects
M_h=C_L^*(W_h) are positive and sum to I. Therefore

  E_L(Z)=sum_h Tr(M_h Z)xi_h

is an actual full-domain CPTP EB channel, fixed for both unknown labels.
Combining (8) with the path estimate in Section 2 proves (1).

4. EB RECONSTRUCTION CONTROLS ROOT-FIDELITY MINUS AFFINITY

Let E(Z)=sum_h Tr(M_h Z)xi_h be any EB measure-and-prepare map. Set
p_h=Tr(M_h rho_0), r_h=Tr(M_h rho_1). The trace-norm unitary variational
formula, insertion sum_h M_h=I between the roots, and Hilbert-Schmidt
Cauchy-Schwarz give

  F(rho_0,rho_1)
    =max_U |sum_h Tr[U sqrt(rho_0)M_h sqrt(rho_1)]|
    <=sum_h ||U sqrt(rho_0)sqrt(M_h)||_2
                ||sqrt(M_h)sqrt(rho_1)||_2
     =sum_h sqrt(p_h r_h).                                 (9)

Affinity is jointly concave and homogeneous of degree one on pairs of
positive matrices. Its superadditive form therefore gives

  A(E rho_0,E rho_1)
    >=sum_h A(p_h xi_h,r_h xi_h)=sum_h sqrt(p_h r_h).         (10)

For clarity, Appendix A reconstructs the needed exponent-one-half
concavity from a finite-dimensional parallel-sum variational formula;
no source card's assertion is treated as a premise. It is established
prior mathematics, also contained in Lieb's Corollary 1.1, p=r=1/2.

The finite-dimensional square-root continuity inequality is

  ||sqrt(R)-sqrt(S)||_2^2<=||R-S||_1                       (11)

for arbitrary positive R,S. Here is a direct proof, including singular
matrices. Put H=sqrt(R)-sqrt(S), H=H_+-H_-, and J=sign(H). Then

  ||R-S||_1>=Tr[J(R-S)]
    =Tr[|H|(sqrt(R)+sqrt(S))]
    >=Tr[H_+^2+H_-^2]=||H||_2^2.

The last inequality follows by evaluating separately on H_+,H_-:
sqrt(R)+sqrt(S)-H=2sqrt(S)>=0 and
sqrt(R)+sqrt(S)+H=2sqrt(R)>=0, so the surplus is
2Tr[H_+ sqrt(S)]+2Tr[H_- sqrt(R)]>=0. This is the finite matrix
Powers-Størmer inequality, not a new root-continuity result.

For states rho_x,rho'_x with T(rho_x,rho'_x)<=eta, expansion of affinity
and Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz, using ||sqrt(state)||_2=1, now give

  |A(rho'_0,rho'_1)-A(rho_0,rho_1)|
    <=||sqrt(rho'_0)-sqrt(rho_0)||_2
       +||sqrt(rho'_1)-sqrt(rho_1)||_2<=2 sqrt(2 eta).        (12)

Equations (9)-(10) imply Delta<=A(E rho_0,E rho_1)-A(rho_0,rho_1).
Applying (12) proves (2). In particular every simultaneous pair EB
reconstruction error is >=Delta^2/8 in half trace norm.

6. LEGAL SMALL-ERROR CONTROL AND CHARGED COSTS

Let 0<c<1, s=sqrt(1-c^2), and consider the pure qubit states

  rho_0=(1/2)[[1+s,c],[c,1-s]],
  rho_1=(1/2)[[1-s,c],[c,1+s]].

They obey F=c, A=c^2, Delta=c-c^2. The common CPTP broadcaster

  B(Z)=Z_00 rho_0 tensor rho_0+Z_11 rho_1 tensor rho_1

has both marginals E(Z)=Z_00 rho_0+Z_11 rho_1. Each wrong Helstrom
outcome has probability p=(1-s)/2, so its worst half-trace marginal
error is epsilon=p s=s(1-s)/2. As c decreases to zero,

  epsilon=(1+o(1))c^2/4, Delta=(1+o(1))c,
  Delta/sqrt(epsilon)->2.

Therefore a universal O(epsilon) affinity-gap bound is impossible; a
square-root lower order is genuine. This does NOT refute the proved
sqrt(epsilon log(1/epsilon)) upper bound. It is an explicit legal finite
family/control, not an unrestricted broadcasting-to-EB counterexample.

For fixed L the construction uses K=2^L receivers, K-1 calls to supplied
B, up to K-1 measured receivers, and at most 2^(K-1) prefix records.
Conditional states, their spectral sign projectors, and the good index j
require exact information about the two joint outputs (potentially

It reconstructs marginal density matrices, not purifications or joint

APPENDIX A. ELEMENTARY EXPONENT-ONE-HALF AFFINITY CONCAVITY

This optional independent derivation removes reliance on a cited concavity
claim. Let L,R be strictly positive linear operators on any finite complex
Hilbert space and t>0. The quadratic parallel-sum identity is

  <v,(L^(-1)+tR^(-1))^(-1)v>
    =inf_(x+y=v){<x,Lx>+<y,(R/t)y>}.

The minimizer obeys Lx=(R/t)y=w, and v=(L^(-1)+tR^(-1))w;
substitution proves the identity. Its right side is an infimum of affine
functions of L,R, so this parallel sum is jointly operator concave.

On the Hilbert-Schmidt space of matrices, define the commuting positive
operators L_X(Z)=XZ and R_Y(Z)=ZY for X,Y>0. Their simultaneous spectral
calculus and the scalar integral int_0^infinity u^(-1/2)/(1+u)du=pi give

  L_X^(1/2)R_Y^(1/2)
    =(1/pi)int_0^infinity t^(-1/2)
               (L_X^(-1)+tR_Y^(-1))^(-1)dt.

Both L_X and R_Y depend linearly on X,Y. Taking the quadratic form at
the identity matrix proves that

  A(X,Y)=Tr[sqrt(X)sqrt(Y)]

is jointly concave. All integrals converge for strictly positive finite
operators. Replacing each singular input by input+delta I and taking
delta down to zero extends the inequality by matrix-root continuity.
Homogeneity A(aX,aY)=a A(X,Y) gives the finite-sum superadditivity used
in (10). This is standard parallel-sum/geometric-mean mathematics;
no novelty claim is made for this proof or the affinity concavity.
