# Cycle 2: robust thermoelectric PF–ZT frontier

## Verdict

There is an exact robust extension of the earlier bounded-transport interval reduction, but it is a monotonic-corner corollary rather than a new thermoelectric mechanism. Under profile-independent rectangular error radii for the first three transport moments and lattice leak, the worst-case power factor and ZT are attained at explicit box corners. Preserving the center values of the first two moments and reducing the third moment still makes a saturated energy interval optimal. Thus the robust maximum-ZT subject to a robust PF floor remains a two-endpoint search.

This result survives as a conditional theorem inside the stated moment model. It does not yield a material-specific robust design or a unique measurement policy. Those require acquired covariance/error bounds, instrument and sample costs, fabrication response, and a measured relation between electronic filtering and lattice heat flow. The standard boxcar results of Maassen (2021) and Ding et al. (2023), plus existing robust thermoelectric system-design work, leave no defensible novelty claim here. The productive conclusion is a rigorous stopping point: the route is exhausted until a named material and its acquisition/fabrication interface are supplied.

## Interface and exact robust certificate

Keep the cycle-1 interface. At fixed temperature, in one transport direction and under Boltzmann transport/RTA, write the normalized profile as $0\leq\phi(x)\leq1$, with $w(x)=1/[4\cosh^2(x/2)]$ and

\[
A=\int\phi w,\qquad B=\int x\phi w,\qquad C=\int x^2\phi w,
\qquad a=\frac{\kappa_l}{\Sigma_{\max}k_B^2T}>0.
\]

Then normalized $PF=B^2/A$ and $ZT=B^2/[A(C+a)-B^2]$. The current result treats the physical branch with a fixed sign of $B$; reflect $x\mapsto-x$ if needed so $B>0$.

For one candidate design, suppose a supplied rectangular uncertainty set is
\[
A\in[A_-,A_+],\quad B\in[B_-,B_+],\quad C\in[C_-,C_+],\quad a\in[a_-,a_+],
\]
with $A_->0$, $a_->0$, and $B_->0$. Interpret the rectangle literally as the adversarial set; it may be conservative if the moment errors are correlated. Since
\[
\partial_A PF<0,\quad \partial_B PF>0,\quad
\partial_A ZT<0,\quad\partial_C ZT<0,\quad\partial_a ZT<0,\quad\partial_B ZT>0
\]
on this branch, the exact worst cases over this box are
\[
PF^- = \frac{B_-^2}{A_+},\qquad
ZT^- = \frac{B_-^2}{A_+(C_++a_+)-B_-^2}.
\]
The denominator is positive whenever the box contains at least one physically admissible moment tuple: $AC\geq B^2$, and the worst corner only increases $A,C,a$ and lowers positive $B$. If the $B$-interval crosses zero, its least absolute value is zero, so no positive robust PF floor is certifiable. For a negative-sign interval, use the endpoint nearest zero in place of $B_-$.

**Robust interval theorem.** Assume the same center lattice-leak parameter $a$ and absolute uncertainty radii are used for every admissible profile, $B_-=B-\epsilon_B>0$, $A_+=A+\epsilon_A>0$, and $a_+=a+\epsilon_a>0$. For every feasible robust PF floor $p_0>0$,
\[
\sup_{0\leq\phi\leq1:\; PF^-(\phi)\geq p_0}ZT^-(\phi)
=
\sup_{\ell<r:\; PF^-(1_{[\ell,r]})\geq p_0}ZT^-(1_{[\ell,r]}),
\]
including limiting half-lines. The same statement holds if the error radii depend on $(A,B)$ alone, because the replacement preserves those two center moments.

**Proof.** The cycle-1 bathtub/rearrangement argument supplies an interval profile $\psi=1_{[\ell,r]}$ with the same $A,B$ as any $\phi$, and $C_\psi\leq C_\phi$. The robust PF uses only $A,B$, so it is unchanged. Its robust-ZT denominator changes by exactly
\[
(A+\epsilon_A)(C_\phi-C_\psi)\geq0,
\]
while its numerator is unchanged. Hence the replacement is robustly feasible and its worst-case ZT is no smaller. This proves the reduction over all profiles. The corner formula follows from the displayed derivative signs. \(\square\)

The equivalent threshold tests for simultaneous robust requirements $PF^-\geq p_0$ and $ZT^-\geq z_0$ are
\[
(B-\epsilon_B)^2\geq p_0(A+\epsilon_A),
\]
\[
(1+z_0)(B-\epsilon_B)^2\geq z_0(A+\epsilon_A)(C+a+\epsilon_C+\epsilon_a).
\]
They provide an exact acceptance certificate for fixed interval bounds. They are not a new material law.

Two limits matter. First, a true joint confidence region or covariance ellipsoid need not have a corner minimizer, and error radii depending on profile shape or C can spoil equal-$A,B$ dominance. Second, physical $A,B,C$ are correlated by the common spectrum and satisfy the moment cone. An independent rectangle can include impossible tuples and understate the usable robust frontier. A measured joint region could sharpen the bound, but no such data were available.

## Measurement policy and complete costs

There is no data-independent optimal measurement allocation. For example, consider $n_i$ replicate acquisitions of $i\in\{A,B,C,a\}$, a simultaneous coverage factor $q_i$, and a validated noise scale $s_i$, giving radii $\epsilon_i=q_i s_i/\sqrt{n_i}$. If a replicate costs $u_i$ joules of instrument energy (or $c_i$ units of time/money), the exact conditional design problem for prescribed robust floors is
\[
\min_{n_i\in\mathbb{N},\,n_i\geq1}\sum_i u_i n_i
\quad\text{subject to the two robust threshold inequalities above, with }
\epsilon_i=q_i s_i/\sqrt{n_i}.
\]
For a simultaneous confidence guarantee, the $q_i$'s must come from a joint procedure or a stated multiplicity correction; treating correlated fitted moments as independent does not automatically give that guarantee. If a systematic bias floor $b_i$ exists, use $\epsilon_i=b_i+q_i s_i/\sqrt{n_i}$; no number of repeats can certify a target below $b_i$. Calibration, sample-to-sample heterogeneity, temperature control, energy resolution, and all acquisition energy belong in these inputs.

For orientation only, linearize the ZT target margin
\[
M_Z=(1+z_0)B^2-z_0A(C+a)
\]
at small radii. Its first-order uncertainty loss is
\[
g_A\epsilon_A+g_B\epsilon_B+g_C\epsilon_C+g_a\epsilon_a,
\quad(g_A,g_B,g_C,g_a)=
\big(z_0(C+a),\;2(1+z_0)B,\;z_0A,\;z_0A\big).
\]
If PF is slack, the continuous local relaxation \(\min\sum_i\alpha_i/\epsilon_i^2\) subject to \(\sum_i g_i\epsilon_i\leq\tau\) has the interior solution
\[
\epsilon_i=\frac{\tau(\alpha_i/g_i)^{1/3}}
{\sum_j\alpha_j^{1/3}g_j^{2/3}},\qquad
C_{\min}=\frac{(\sum_j\alpha_j^{1/3}g_j^{2/3})^3}{\tau^2}.
\]
Here $\alpha_i$ packages replicate energy and squared noise/coverage scales. If the PF constraint is active as well, its local gradient is $(p_0,2B,0,0)$; the local KKT rule replaces $g_i$ by $\lambda_Zg_i+\lambda_Pg_i^{PF}$. This is an internally derived small-error allocation rule, not a global solution to the nonlinear program. Rounded sample counts must be checked again against both exact inequalities. No $q_i,s_i,u_i,b_i$, joint covariance, precision target, or confidence level was supplied, so no numerical sample allocation is defensible.

The thermodynamic device formula also excludes laboratory and fabrication work. Net output would have to charge filter/gate/control work and all contacts and parasitic heat paths; any life-cycle energy claim would separately charge $E_{\rm acquisition}$, $E_{\rm fabrication}$, and operating control energy against integrated delivered electrical work. The ideal boxcar profile itself carries a fabrication requirement: finite band/mode resolution, interface resistance, yield, profile drift with temperature, and the possibility that changing the electronic profile also changes $\kappa_l$. The fixed-$\kappa_l$, uniform-cap theorem says nothing about those coupled costs.

## Prior art, Astra routing, and status

The shape result is heavily prefigured. Maassen’s primary bounded-transport result proves that a boxcar optimizes ZT under a pointwise transport cap, while a Heaviside profile optimizes PF. Ding et al.’s primary article text optimizes Landauer transmission under an arbitrary density-of-modes upper-bound function $M(x)$ and lattice heat contribution $\beta$; it reports a boxcar transmission as optimal even for arbitrary $M(x)$ and various lattice conductances. Their article’s core equations (8)–(13) and arbitrary-$M(x)$ discussion make the same interval shape even less novel than the 2023 abstract alone suggests. Their stated objective is maximum ZT; the source section does not state the present robust PF-floor problem with moment intervals. The robust PF-floor extension above is an equal-moment rearrangement plus monotonic interval arithmetic; it is likely an incremental corollary, not a new thermoelectric principle. Park, Xia, Ozoliņš and Jain also optimize thermoelectric bands under more realistic scattering/band constraints, emphasizing how restrictive a free profile is. A primary 2020 system-level robust-design paper already uses uncertain TEG inputs and a mean/variance Pareto search; it is adjacent rather than the same intrinsic-moment objective.

Measurement uncertainty is experimentally material. Mackey, Dynys and Sehirlioglu report resistivity uncertainty of ±7% and, at high temperature, asymmetric uncertainties of +1.0%/−13.1% in Seebeck coefficient and +7.3%/−27.0% in PF for a four-probe setup. Alleno et al.’s multi-laboratory Co$_{0.97}$Ni$_{0.03}$Sb$_3$ study reports temperature-averaged relative standard uncertainties (68% confidence) of 6% in $S$, 8% in resistivity, 11% in thermal conductivity, and 19% in $ZT$. A 2026 module round-robin identifies heat-flux measurement as the key uncertainty for module efficiency and reports substantial variation in maximum electrical power. These data support treating uncertainty and device boundaries seriously; they do not supply moment error bars or costs for the present profile model.

## Falsifier and restart gate

An algebraic counterexample inside the stated positive-denominator box, or a noninterval profile with the same first two moments and profile-independent radii that achieves a strictly larger worst-case ZT, would falsify the mathematical theorem. Treating profile-dependent uncertainty radii as fixed violates its hypotheses instead. A physical test of the theorem's application should measure energy-resolved $\Sigma(E)$ and its cap on one sample, $S,\sigma,\kappa_{\rm total}$ on the same direction/temperature range, an independently supported interval for $\kappa_l$, plus complete-device contact and heat-flux channels. From a predeclared joint confidence set for $A,B,C,a$, calculate the robust envelope and then compare a fabricated interval-filter candidate with a matched profile having the same $A,B$. A repeatable noninterval device that exceeds the claimed robust bound at the same supplied cap, PF floor, temperature, contacts, and heat boundary is a decisive failure of the model application; first test whether profile-dependent $\kappa_l$, mode cap, or hidden heat paths explain it.

The next research turn should begin only after acquiring: (i) one named material/device profile with joint moment uncertainty and uncertainty dependence on candidate design, (ii) a measured time/energy/precision curve for each channel, and (iii) a fabrication/filter process with matched controls and net operating work. Without these inputs, there is no evidence-based optimal measurement policy, fabrication recommendation, or nature-level gain.
