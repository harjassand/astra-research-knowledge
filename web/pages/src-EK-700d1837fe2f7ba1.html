# C4 — When marginal evidence erases the phenomenon

**Status:** strongest current obstruction candidate. The core GHZ marginal fact is established prior work, so this is not a novelty claim. The useful result here is a task-level impossibility statement, an explicit data-interface reduction, and the readout-noise cost of the smallest representation that escapes it.

## 1. North Star

Build an information audit for experimental science that detects when a data representation has quotiented out the very interaction or coherence the experiment is supposed to establish, then identifies the cheapest additional observable that restores the target claim.

## 2. Exact question

Can the phase of an $n$-partite coherent superposition be identified, even with exact and unlimited data, if the evidence interface supplies every proper-subsystem reduced state separately but discards synchronized whole-system outcomes? What is the least extra record needed to recover the phase, and how does its precision cost scale under independent detector error?

## 3. Importance

The target is a concrete version of a broad scientific overclaim: inferring global coherence, a genuine $n$-way interaction, or an $n$-party correlation from increasingly precise lower-order summaries. A local table can be exactly correct and still contain zero information about the global claim. More samples of that same summary do not repair the problem.

The practical redirection is to replace “reconstruct the whole state from marginal reports” with “identify the particular global observable needed for the claim, and preserve the per-trial evidence for that observable.” This limits the measurement burden while making the inference identifiable.

## 4. Obstruction

For $n\ge2$, define
\[
 |G_\theta\rangle = (|0^n\rangle+e^{i\theta}|1^n\rangle)/\sqrt2,
 \qquad \rho_\theta=|G_\theta\rangle\langle G_\theta|.
\]
If $S\subsetneq[n]$, tracing out any nonempty complement kills the off-diagonal terms because $\operatorname{tr}(|0\rangle\langle1|)=0$:
\[
 \rho_{\theta,S}=\operatorname{tr}_{[n]\setminus S}(\rho_\theta)
 =\tfrac12(|0^S\rangle\langle0^S|+|1^S\rangle\langle1^S|),
\]
which is independent of $\theta$. In particular, $|G_0\rangle$ and $|G_\pi\rangle$ are orthogonal while **every proper-subsystem density matrix is identical**.

Let the evidence map be $M_{n-1}(\rho)=(\rho_S)_{S\subsetneq[n]}$. Then $M_{n-1}(\rho_0)=M_{n-1}(\rho_\pi)$. Any estimator or classifier whose entire input is this map has the same output law on both cases. Under an equal prior on the two phases, its best possible classification success is $1/2$, even if all marginals are supplied exactly and without sampling error. The target $T(\rho)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho X^{\otimes n})$ equals $+1$ and $-1$ on the pair, so it is not a function of the marginal evidence.

Equivalent classical reduction: let $b\in\{-1,+1\}$ and draw $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ with
\[
 p_b(x)=2^{-n}\bigl(1+b(-1)^{x_1+\cdots+x_n}\bigr).
\]
Every proper coordinate marginal is uniform, since summing over any omitted coordinate cancels the parity term; the full row reveals $b=(-1)^{\sum_i x_i}$ exactly. Measuring all qubits of $|G_0\rangle$ or $|G_\pi\rangle$ in the $X$ basis gives these two distributions. Thus this is both a state-identification obstruction and a database join/coarsening obstruction.

**Scope condition:** the no-go applies when the interface releases only subsystem states or separately aggregated marginal tables. Synchronized local measurements of all $n$ sites followed by retaining their joint per-shot outcomes are already richer evidence; they are precisely the escape below. This distinction prevents the false claim that local detectors themselves cannot reveal a global correlation.

## 5. Missing capability

What is absent is not local measurement precision. The representation has dropped the cross-site pairing that carries the $n$-way statistic. Once the row identity is discarded, no downstream learner can recreate it from exact marginal tables. This blocks universal global-state recovery and, more narrowly, certification of a phase-sensitive global coherence from proper marginals alone. Structural assumptions can choose a phase, but the data do not identify it.

## 6. Actual first investigation

I derived the partial-trace identity and the classical parity construction independently, then checked the novelty boundary against the quantum marginal literature and an experimental GHZ parity protocol. The formal reduction above is the concrete counterexample: it yields exactly equal proper marginals, orthogonal global states, and a one-bit global sufficient statistic.

I compared this with two other obstruction patterns. Observational causal direction can be reversed by factorizing the same joint law in either direction, with interventions as the escape; it is important but less focused on a lossy representation. A rare-event construction makes finite-sample claims unstable, but its barrier is finite precision rather than exact erasure. The parity pair is the stronger C4 candidate because it survives exact, unlimited marginal evidence and has a minimal, experimentally demonstrated escape.

## 7. Prior work plus acquisition, precision, and output costs

The marginal obstruction itself is known. Walck and Lyons prove that generalized GHZ states are the pure $n$-qubit states not determined by their $(n-1)$-qubit reduced states ([PRL 100, 050501 (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.050501)); their later result treats uniqueness against arbitrary competing states ([PRA 79, 032326 (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.79.032326)). In contrast, Linden and Wootters show that generic pure multipartite states are determined by reduced states on a fraction of the parties ([PRL 89, 277906 (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.89.277906)). The exact obstruction is therefore exceptional but fatal to a universal guarantee unless GHZ-type states are ruled out by an explicit promise.

The escape is also established experimentally. In a 10-qubit superconducting GHZ experiment, Song et al. recorded synchronized outcomes from all qubits and extracted coherence from global parity oscillations; the paper reports repeated state-preparation/readout and the parity operator as a product over all qubits ([PRL 119, 180511 (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.119.180511)). The gap is not a missing measurement technique. It is whether an intended scientific workflow actually acquires and preserves the synchronized record and charges its noise and calibration costs.

**Minimal escape representation.** In each trial $j$, measure every qubit locally in the $X$ basis, record signs $s_{ij}\in\{-1,+1\}$, and retain
\[
 r_j=\prod_{i=1}^n s_{ij}.
\]
Then $\mathbb E[r_j]=\operatorname{tr}(\rho X^{\otimes n})$, equal to $\cos\theta$ on $|G_\theta\rangle$. For the promised $\theta\in\{0,\pi\}$ pair, an ideal parity result identifies the phase in one shot. To recover an arbitrary phase, add one orthogonal equatorial setting, giving the sine quadrature as well as the cosine quadrature. The per-trial parity is sufficient for the binary promise; keeping the full synchronized row permits later audit and other analyses.

For an additive-$\epsilon$ estimate of a bounded parity expectation with ideal readout, Hoeffding gives
\[
 N\ge 2\epsilon^{-2}\log(2/\delta)
\]
shots for error probability at most $\delta$. If each site's recorded sign independently flips with probability $q<1/2$, write $\lambda=1-2q$. The observed parity mean is attenuated by $\lambda^n$. Dividing by this calibrated factor gives the simple bound
\[
 N\ge 2\epsilon^{-2}\lambda^{-2n}\log(2/\delta).
\]
For the binary GHZ promise, the noisy parity distributions are Bernoulli with means $\pm\lambda^n$; their per-shot Chernoff information is $-\tfrac12\log(1-\lambda^{2n})$, asymptotic to $\lambda^{2n}/2$ at fixed $q\in(0,1/2)$. So the exact marginal no-go can be escaped, but independent fixed-rate readout noise can make the rescue exponentially expensive in $n$. These are costs for this stated noise model, not an optimality claim for all hardware or fault-tolerant protocols.

Acquisition needs $n$ synchronized local outcomes per shot. Retaining raw data costs $nN$ outcome bits plus shot identifiers; retaining only parity costs $N$ bits or an $O(\log N)$-bit count, but gives less auditability. Separate histograms with no common shot identity erase the parity. Preparation, basis rotation, phase calibration, loss, and correlated errors must be charged separately; unknown $q$ also requires an independent calibration or a joint identifiable model.

## 8. Decisive next step

Run a preregistered data-interface audit on a real target with an $n$-way phase/coherence claim. Construct the two GHZ/parity cases, give the current marginal-only pipeline exact proper-marginal tables, and verify that its outputs are identical. Then ingest synchronized per-shot outcomes, estimate the parity witness, and repeat under the measured per-site error model. The go/no-go result should report whether the real workflow can acquire shared shot IDs, whether a specific global witness actually supports the scientific claim, and whether its measured noise leaves a tractable shot budget. Stop if the only apparent success depends on a pure-state/GHZ promise that the application cannot justify or on uncharged perfect readout.
