Abstract
We prove that the existential theory of the reals lies in the counting hierarchy. More
generally, we show that the truth of existential–universal sentences over the reals can be
decided in a fixed level of the counting hierarchy, even when the integer polynomials are
given by arithmetic circuits.

1.1 History and significance
Tarski proved decidability of the first-order theory of real closed fields [15]. Collins’s cylindrical
algebraic decomposition gave a geometric method for eliminating quantifiers [8], and Canny
established the polynomial-space upper bound for the existential fragment [6]. For each
fixed number of real quantifier blocks, Renegar’s decision bounds yield a polynomial-space
algorithm [10, Theorem 1.1]. This applies to the circuit encoding above: the quartic reduction
in Lemma 6.1 preserves the two quantifier blocks. Our theorem places this two-block language
in a fixed level of the counting hierarchy. Real quantifier hierarchies distinguish the number
and order of blocks; Schaefer and Štefankovič develop their finite-encoding formulation [11].
The existential class already captures many geometric realization and continuous feasibility
problems; Schaefer, Cardinal, and Miltzow survey these connections [12].
The counting hierarchy gives a way to decide some exact algebraic questions without
constructing their enormous intermediate integers. Allender, Bürgisser, Kjeldgaard-Pedersen,
and Miltersen proved thatPosSLP—positivity of the integer output of a division-free straight-
line program—belongs toCH [1]. Their modular-product and approximate Chinese-remainder
techniques are the counting tools behind our treatment of implicit polynomial families. Every

implicitly represented families that need not have short coefficient lists or arithmetic circuits.

that preserve this description and recover the sign of an integer from it. The modular-product
and approximate Chinese-remainder methods follow the arithmetic approach of Allender et
al. [1, Section 4]; we prove the forms, including the oracle bounds, that we need.

5 Existential tests for controlled families
The polynomial operations developed above allow us to construct much larger polynomials
