# N02 addendum v4 — general finite-beam seed delay from the Lax eigenfunction

This append-only note extends v1's three-beam delay calculation to any finite discrete angular spectrum with one isolated fastest unstable Lax root. It leaves all earlier versions unchanged. The exact one-pulse solution remains source-reported; this addition identifies its unstable-tail normalization and the resulting leading seed-to-peak time for a generic finite-beam seed.

## General tail coefficient

Let \(v_i\ne0\) be finitely many beam velocities with real nonzero \(G_i\), \(G_1=\sum_i v_iG_i\ne0\), and homogeneous equations
\[
 \dot{\mathbf D}_i=(\mathbf D_0-v_i\mathbf D_1)\times\mathbf D_i,
 \qquad \mathbf D_n=\sum_i v_i^n\mathbf D_i.
\]
Suppose the diagonal equilibrium \(\mathbf D_i=G_i\hat z\) has one simple isolated fastest unstable Lax zero \(u=a+ib\) (choose the conjugate with sign convention giving growth), with \(b\ne0\), \(\Gamma=|G_1b|\), \(\omega=-G_1a\), and \(\lambda^2=G_1^2(a^2+b^2)\). Define the unnormalized transverse unstable eigenfunction
\[
 Q_i=\frac{G_i w_i}{w_i+\omega-i\Gamma},\qquad w_i=v_iG_1.
\]
An overall conjugation is used if the opposite convention for \(D_i^x+iD_i^y\) is chosen; its norm and all conclusions below are unchanged. The denominator is nonzero because \(\Gamma>0\). Put \(M=\max_i|Q_i|\), so \(\widehat Q_i=Q_i/M\) has \(\max_i|\widehat Q_i|=1\).

The exact soliton reconstruction from v1 gives the transverse vector
\[
 \mathbf D_i^\perp(t)=G_i\frac{w_i\mathbf J_\perp(t)+w_i^2\mathbf r_\perp(t)}
 {\lambda^2+w_iS+w_i^2}.
\]
Along the incoming separatrix tail, \(\mathbf r_\perp(t)\sim(4\Gamma/\lambda)e^{\Gamma(t-t_p)}e^{i\omega t}\), and the linearized pendulum relation is \(\mathbf J_\perp\sim(\omega+i\Gamma)\mathbf r_\perp\). The denominator factors as
\[
 \lambda^2+w_iS+w_i^2=(w_i+\omega+i\Gamma)(w_i+\omega-i\Gamma).
\]
After cancellation,
\[
 q_i^{\rm sol}(t):=D_i^x+iD_i^y
 \sim \frac{4\Gamma}{\lambda}Q_i e^{\Gamma(t-t_p)}e^{i\omega t}.
\]
Therefore the maximum beam-amplitude tail coefficient relative to \(\widehat Q\) is
\[
 K(u,G)=\frac{4\Gamma}{\lambda}\max_i|Q_i|.
\]

If the initial seed's unstable projection is \(\epsilon\alpha\widehat Q+o(\epsilon)\), \(\alpha\ne0\), while all other seed components are \(O(\epsilon)\), matching the incoming tail gives
\[
 t_p=\frac1\Gamma\log\frac{K(u,G)}{\epsilon|\alpha|}+o(1),
 \qquad \epsilon\to0.
\]
The coefficient \(K\) is fixed by the initial ELN distribution and the selected Lax root; the seed contributes only its complex projection magnitude to the logarithmic delay and its phase to a removable global azimuth. This is exact for the one-root separatrix matching and, for a finite beam set with a unique simple fastest unstable pair, the standard finite-dimensional unstable-manifold theorem gives convergence to that orbit on compact pulse-time windows after the shift. Slower unstable modes vanish relative to it as \(\epsilon^{1-\Gamma_j/\Gamma}\); stable modes decay; semisimple neutral modes remain \(O(\epsilon)\). A resonance or a second root with growth \(\Gamma\) invalidates the one-root conclusion; v3 gives an explicit equal-growth obstruction.

For v1's three-beam example, \(Q=(-9/4+i\sqrt7/4,\ 5/4-i3\sqrt7/4,\ 1+i\sqrt7/2)\), up to the same conjugation/phase convention. Here \(M=\sqrt{11/2}\), \(4\Gamma/\lambda=8/\sqrt{11}\), and \(K=4\sqrt2\), recovering v1's exact additive delay \(\Gamma^{-1}\log(4\sqrt2)\).

## Scope

This finite-dimensional theorem does not require an angular moment closure: the selected complex Lax root and full beam weights determine the angular pulse, its peak conversion, and the leading seed delay. For a continuous spectrum the same tail coefficient can be written with an essential supremum after choosing a function-space norm, but the nonlinear logarithmic-time convergence remains unproved here; a continuum semigroup/unstable-manifold estimate is still required. Equal fastest roots require a vector of seed projections, and the relative phases or magnitudes can affect the first excursion, as explicitly shown in v3. Spatial transport, collision terms, and realistic supernova/merger dynamics remain outside this theorem.
