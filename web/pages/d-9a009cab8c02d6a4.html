## 1. Exact question, interface and source boundary

Let `n,m>=1`, and let `F` be any characteristic-zero field. An acyclic coefficient representation is

\[
 f=\sum_w (uB_wv)x_w,\qquad
 B_{i_1\ldots i_k}=B_{i_1}\cdots B_{i_k},\quad B_\epsilon=I_m,
\]

where `u` is a constant row, `v` a constant column, and all `B_i` are strictly upper triangular `m`-by-`m` matrices. In particular, all products of length at least `m` vanish. The generator receives only `n,m`; the representation, coefficients and support are used only to prove correctness. The oracle returns the complete exact evaluated matrix. An approximate, scalar, iid-sample, internal-gate or fixed-seed oracle is a different interface.

The proposed tuple is the source's explicit integration-operator matrix at a smaller dimension:

\[
 (T_i^{(d)})_{r,q}=
 \begin{cases}
 (-1)^{r-q-1}/(r i^{r-q}),&0\le q<r<d,\\
 0,&0\le r\le q<d.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{1}
\]

The source, *One Rational Matrix Hitting Point for Noncommutative Formulas*, dated 24 September 2026 and authored OpenAI, is pinned at commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`. I read all mathematical sections, including its full word-independence proof, formula conversion, scalar multiplicity argument and jet construction. Its original Proposition 5.1 uses

\[
 d_{\rm old}=(n-1)\binom m2+m
\]

and proves nonzero **first column**. Its formula theorem uses `m<=2s` and the coarser `2ns^2`. The new proof below does not invoke the source's scalar multiplicity theorem. It uses finite-word faithfulness and controls a bivariate integral kernel instead.

## 2. No hidden increase in ABP states

An acyclic ABP on `m` vertices with affine labels `c_0+sum_i c_i x_i` has this representation without adding vertices. Topologically order the vertices. Let `E` collect constant edge coefficients and `N_i` the `x_i` coefficients. These matrices are strictly upper triangular. Set

\[
 Q=(I-E)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}E^j,\qquad
 u=e_\sigma^TQ,\quad v=e_\rho,\quad B_i=N_iQ.
\]

Every path contributing word `i_1...i_k` has a unique expansion into constant subpaths and those `k` variable choices, giving

\[
 f_{i_1\ldots i_k}
 =e_\sigma^T QN_{i_1}Q\cdots N_{i_k}Qe_\rho=uB_wv.
\]

The empty coefficient is `e_sigma^T Q e_rho`. Strict upper triangular times upper triangular remains strictly upper triangular. Scalar elimination changes edge coefficients, not the number of states. Edge count and coefficient bit lengths are separate resources.

For an ordered fan-in-two formula tree, a leaf uses two vertices; addition joins disjoint child graphs with a new source and sink; multiplication concatenates disjoint children by a scalar edge and adds no vertices. With `L` leaves and `a` addition gates, `m=2L+2a<=2s`. This is a tree argument. Shared circuits cannot reuse the same vertex count.

## 3. Reconstructed finite-word faithfulness

Define the **linear operators**

\[
 J_i a(z)=\int_0^z\frac{a(t)}{t+i}\,dt,\qquad
 h_\epsilon=1,\quad h_{iw}=J_i h_w.                         \tag{2}
\]

Formal integration exists in characteristic zero, and each `J_i` raises order by at least one. The map `x_w -> h_w` is linear and is not multiplication of commuting functions. The word `i_1...i_k` means operator composition `J_i1...J_ik`; the rightmost operator acts first.

Here is a reconstruction of the needed lemma, including its field transfer. Fix a finite length `D`. Over the complex numbers let `E_D` be the free associative algebra truncated above word length `D`, with nonempty-word ideal `a` satisfying `a^(D+1)=0`. Put

\[
 U(z)=\sum_{|w|\le D}h_w(z)X_w,\qquad
 U'=\left(\sum_i\frac{X_i}{z+i}\right)U,\quad U(0)=1.
\]

This finite-dimensional equation continues along paths avoiding the finitely many poles. Existence can be constructed by word-length induction using ordinary holomorphic primitives. A loop based at zero has a transport value `Q_gamma` in `1+a`. A solution with initial value `b` equals the initial-one solution times `b` on the right, so loop concatenation gives products in reverse chronological order. Every ordered product is still a loop value; this reversal does not identify different free words.

A based loop winding once around `-i` and zero times around other poles satisfies

\[
 Q_i=1+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\,X_i\pmod{a^2}.
\]

This follows directly from the degree-one periods; the access path and return path cancel. For each word define `C_w=prod_j(Q_ij-1)`, and `C_empty=1`. Every `C_w` lies in the linear span of loop values. Its least word-length part is exactly `(2 pi sqrt(-1))^|w| X_w`. In length order these vectors form a triangular basis of the truncated free algebra.

If a linear functional `L` annihilates the germ `U(z)`, continuation makes it annihilate all loop values and hence all the `C_w`. Thus it is zero, proving complex linear independence of the finitely many `h_w`.

Every Taylor coefficient of every `h_w` is rational. Complex independence implies some finite Taylor-row prefix has full column rank: the descending kernels live in a fixed finite-dimensional space and have zero intersection. A nonzero rational square minor then remains nonzero under `Q -> F` for every characteristic-zero field. This proves independence over `F` without assuming `F` embeds in the complex numbers.

Consequently, for every nonzero **finite polynomial**, `f(J)1=sum f_w h_w` is nonzero. This conclusion is not asserted for an unmarked infinite recognizable series; Section 8 gives an explicit counterexample even for the complete operator.

## 4. The ordered input-output kernel

Set

\[
 q(z)=\prod_{i=1}^n(z+i),\qquad
 A(z)=\sum_i\frac{B_i}{z+i}=P(z)/q(z),\quad\deg P\le n-1.
\]

Since `q(0)=n! !=0`, all entries are regular at zero. The finite sum

\[
 G(z)=\sum_{|w|<m}B_wh_w(z)
\]

has `G(0)=I` and `G'=AG`; its inverse exists over `F[[z]]`. If `f_empty=uv` is nonzero, every nonempty product of the strictly lower triangular `T_i` has zero diagonal, so the output diagonal already detects `f`. Assume henceforth `uv=0` and `f!=0`.

For arbitrary `a in F[[z]]`, define

\[
 H_a(z)=\sum_{1\le|w|<m}B_w J_w a(z).
\]

Termwise differentiation peels the **leftmost** letter, giving

\[
 H_a'=A H_a+A aI,\qquad H_a(0)=0.
\]

This is not `H_a'=H_a A+...`; that would reverse the representation. Formal variation of constants gives

\[
 f(J)a(z)=\int_0^z K(z,t)a(t)\,dt,\qquad
 K(z,t)=uG(z)G(t)^{-1}A(t)v.                              \tag{3}
\]

The same kernel is obtained without differential systems: for a single letter `K_i(z,t)=1/(t+i)`, and for a nonempty suffix `w`,

\[
 K_{iw}(z,t)=\int_t^z\frac{K_w(s,t)}{s+i}\,ds.
\]

This ordered-simplex identity is independently implemented in the checker. It is useful for catching a mistaken word-order convention.

All operations in (3) are legitimate in `F[[z,t]]`. The integral takes a monomial `z^a t^b` to `z^(a+b+1)/(b+1)`, so it is well-defined in the total-degree completion. Finite-word faithfulness says `f(J)1!=0`; therefore `K!=0`. The converse is not needed.

## 5. Short rational row closure, with poles handled correctly

Define rational rows

\[
 R_0=u,\qquad R_{j+1}=R_j'+R_jA.
\]

Induction proves `(uG)^(j)=R_jG`. Writing `R_j=Z_j/q^j` gives

\[
 Z_0=u,\qquad
 Z_{j+1}=qZ_j'-jq'Z_j+Z_jP,\qquad
 \deg_z Z_j\le j(n-1).                                  \tag{4}
\]

For `n=1`, all `Z_j` are constant, so their derivative term is zero. For larger `n`, each term has degree at most `(j+1)(n-1)`. The rational rows themselves are regular at zero.

Because `u!=0`, choose the first dependence `R_k in span_F(z)(R_0,...,R_(k-1))`. It exists with `1<=k<=m`. Let `V` be the span of the preceding independent rows. The operator `D_A R=R'+RA` obeys

\[
 D_A(cR)=c'R+cD_A(R).
\]

It sends each basis row into `V`: the last is sent to the dependent `R_k`. It therefore preserves all of `V`, and every later `R_j` lies in `V`.

The dependence coefficients may have poles at zero. No evaluation of those coefficients at zero occurs. The closure identity is over the rational function field. The individual `R_j` and the following scalar rational functions are regular at zero, and an identically zero rational combination is also zero as a formal germ.

Differentiating (3) in the first variable and then setting `z=t` yields

\[
 b_j(t):=\left.\partial_z^jK(z,t)\right|_{z=t}
       =R_j(t)A(t)v
       =Z_j(t)P(t)v/q(t)^{j+1}.                           \tag{5}
\]

If `b_j=0` for `j<k`, rational row closure forces every later `b_j` to vanish as well. But

\[
 K(t+x,t)=\sum_{j\ge0}b_j(t)x^j/j!
\]

is its formal Taylor expansion. The substitution `z=t+x` is an automorphism of the two-variable power-series ring, inverse `x=z-t`; at any fixed total degree only finitely many terms contribute. Hence all `b_j=0` would imply `K=0`, a contradiction. Some `j<k<=m` has `b_j!=0`.

Its numerator is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most `(j+1)(n-1)`. Its denominator is a unit at zero. Thus

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_0 b_j\le(j+1)(n-1),\qquad
 \operatorname{ord}_{\rm total}K
 \le j+\operatorname{ord}_0 b_j
 \le n(j+1)-1\le nm-1.                                 \tag{6}
\]

The selected monomial in `(x,t)` cannot cancel with a different `x` degree. An invertible linear substitution preserves the first nonzero homogeneous total degree: it is invertible on each finite homogeneous polynomial space. This supplies the advertised **bivariate** bound. A bound on the scalar output `f(J)1` does not follow from (6), because integration against one seed can cancel the entire first kernel part.

### One state can be discarded in the strictly triangular case

Let `U=span(e_0,...,e_(m-2))`. Strict upper triangular `A` preserves `U`, and `A(t)v` lies in `U` because the last row of `A` is zero. Both `G` and `G^-1` preserve `U`. Consequently the kernel in (3) equals

\[
 K(z,t)=u_U G_U(z)G_U(t)^{-1}(A(t)v)_U,
\]

where `G_U'=A_U G_U` has dimension `m-1`. The final column is still a rational vector with denominator `q` and numerator degree at most `n-1`; it need not equal `A_U` times a constant column, and the proof never requires that extra property. A nonconstant `f` forces `u_U!=0` and `K!=0`. Applying the same closure proof to this smaller system gives

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\rm total}K\le n(m-1)-1.             \tag{7}
\]

For `m=1`, all nonempty coefficients vanish and the class consists of constants. This sharpened count does not apply to arbitrary cyclic transitions.

The total-degree bound (7) is attained. Choose

\[
 c_i=\frac{(-i)^{n-1}}{\prod_{j\ne i}(j-i)},\qquad L=\sum_i c_i x_i.
\]

Partial fractions give `sum_i c_i/(t+i)=g(t)=t^(n-1)/q(t)`. The polynomial `L^k` has an acyclic `m=k+1` state chain. With `tau(z)=integral_0^z g(s)ds`, repeated ordered integration gives

\[
 K_{L^k}(z,t)=\frac{g(t)}{(k-1)!}(\tau(z)-\tau(t))^{k-1}.
\]

Since `g(t)=t^(n-1)/n!+...` and `tau(z)=z^n/(n n!)+...`, its first nonzero total degree is exactly `nk-1=n(m-1)-1`. This proves sharpness of the **kernel-order estimate**, not optimality of the factor two in the eventual matrix dimension. The checker verifies nine such cancelling examples.

## 6. Full output recovers the first homogeneous kernel part

Suppose the first nonzero kernel part has total degree `ell-1`. Write it

\[
 K_{\ell-1}(z,t)=\sum_{b=0}^{\ell-1}c_bz^{\ell-1-b}t^b,\qquad c\ne0.
\]

For the input `a(t)=t^q`, direct integration gives

\[
 [z^{q+\ell}]f(J)z^q
 =\sum_{b=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{c_b}{q+b+1}.                 \tag{8}
\]

Only this homogeneous kernel degree contributes to this output coefficient. The `J_i` preserve the ideal `(z^d)` because they raise order, so their induced maps on the quotient are exactly (1). Quotienting before any composition cannot change a retained coefficient. Thus (8) equals entry `(q+ell,q)` of `f(T^(d))` whenever `q+ell<d`.

Take `q=0,...,ell-1`. These entries are the Hilbert/Cauchy matrix `C_(q,b)=1/(q+b+1)` times `c`. To prove invertibility directly, suppose `sum_b c_b/(X+b+1)` vanishes at those `ell` distinct nonnegative integers. Its numerator after a common denominator has degree at most `ell-1`, and those integers are not poles, so that numerator is zero. At `X=-b-1` its only surviving term is `c_b` times a product of nonzero integer differences. Therefore all `c_b` vanish, a contradiction. This proof works over every characteristic-zero field.

Some selected entry is nonzero, and its largest row is `2ell-1`. From (6), `ell<=nm`, so all these entries fit in `d=2nm`. From (7), `d=2n(m-1)` suffices when `m>=2`. Constants use the diagonal, and a dimension-one tuple is enough when `m=1`.

This finishes the universal acyclic proof. The result is an improvement in order as `m` grows; it need not improve the original dimension at every small `m`. One may use the smaller of the proved old and new dimension bounds. For `n=1`, the direct degree bound already gives `d=m`, smaller than the new Cauchy bound.

For size-`s` formulas, `d=4ns` is the immediate conservative consequence of `m<=2s`. The sharpened count gives `4ns-2n` for the same upper bound and `s>=1`. These are full-matrix guarantees, not first-column ones.

For a base dimension `d`, a nonzero matrix entry has numerator `+/-1` and denominator bit length at most

\[
 B=3+\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil+d\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil.
\]

There are `n d(d-1)/2` nonzero entries. Repeated integer powers and denominator formation use `O(n d^2 B^2)` schoolbook bit operations and `O(n d^2 B)` output bits. A complete oracle response contains `d^2` field elements; its computation and encoding are additional costs. For the scaled list, numerators have at most an additional `O(log m)` bits and the list has `m` times the base nonzero records. A dense single direct-sum input or output still occupies the square of its full dimension unless a compressed interface is explicitly granted.

## 7. Why commuting auxiliary variables do not destroy NC word order

The free word multiplication occurs in `B_w` and `J_w`, and the finite quotient evaluates the same ordered operator products. The variables `z,t` encode output and integration positions; their being commuting formal variables says nothing about commuting the operators. Faithfulness is proved in a truncated **free associative** algebra before field transfer, and the kernel is formed after the ordered action is established.

An easy falsifier of a commutative-substitution error is `x_1x_2-x_2x_1`: its first-column series begins `-z^3/24`, while scalar substitution gives zero. The independent checker verifies all 30 binary words of lengths one through four against the ordered-simplex kernels and retained matrix entries. It also verifies cancellation through the invertible substitution `z=x+t` and exact Hilbert determinants through size 12.

There is no hidden `m^2` state space in the derivative proof: `G(z)G(t)^-1` factors between an `m`-component row and column. The covariant derivative acts on the row in the first variable, keeping that column as a parameter. Vectorizing a two-variable PDE would introduce an unnecessary tensor square.

## 8. Decisive cyclic obstruction

Let `A=J_1`, `B=J_2` and consider the proper rational series

\[
 R=x_1x_2-x_2(1+x_1)^{-1}x_1(1+x_2).                   \tag{9}
\]

Its degree-two part is `x_1x_2-x_2x_1`, so it is nonzero. Yet `R(J_1,J_2)` is zero as a **complete operator**, not just on the constant seed.

Set `Q a=integral_0^z a(t)dt` and `C=(I+J_1)^-1J_1`. If `y=C a`, differentiation of `y+J_1y=J_1a` gives `((z+1)y)'=a` with zero initial value. Thus

\[
 C a=Q a/(z+1).
\]

Also `Q(a+J_2a)=(z+2)J_2a`, since both sides vanish at zero and have derivative `a+J_2a`. Therefore

\[
 (J_2 C(I+J_2)a)'=\frac{Q(a+J_2a)}{(z+1)(z+2)}
 =\frac{J_2a}{z+1}=(J_1J_2a)'.
\]

Both sides vanish at zero, proving the operator identity. Inverses `I+J_1` and `I+T_1` are legal: the former is invertible in the order-raising operator completion, the latter has a finite nilpotent inverse. Hence every finite unscaled tuple (1) gives a complete zero matrix for (9), at **any** dimension.

A five-state recognizable realization makes the scope explicit. With states `0,...,4`, letter edges are `0->1:x1`, `1->3:x2`, `0->2:x2`, `2->4:-x1`, `4->4:-x1`, `4->3:x2`; take initial row `e_0^T` and final column `e_3+e_4`. The self-loop produces the series for the inverse. It is cyclic and does not satisfy strict upper triangularity.

The checker independently replays the complete identity for dimensions 2 through 14 and then evaluates that five-state series at the scaled tuples below. It preserves zero at scale one and nonzero output at other scales. These are exact implementation facts; the operator derivation proves the obstruction at every dimension.

## 9. Repaired proper-series hitting: a short fixed list of scales

This section reconstructs root's sharpening. The input is a **proper recognizable series**

\[
 R=\sum_w(uB_wv)x_w
\]

with arbitrary constant `m`-state transitions. Matrix evaluation on jointly nilpotent `X_i` means this series, equivalently

\[
 R(X)=(u\otimes I)\left(I-\sum_i B_i\otimes X_i\right)^{-1}(v\otimes I).
\]

The inverse is a finite nilpotent sum. An arbitrary syntactic rational expression may be undefined at zero or at these tuples even when it represents the same function; that broader oracle is not imported.

A nonzero constant coefficient is detected on the diagonal of every scaled tuple. The remaining proof concerns zero constant coefficient.

Work over `F(lambda)`, with an indeterminate `lambda`, and define

\[
 G_\lambda(z)=\sum_w\lambda^{|w|}B_wh_w(z),\qquad
 A_\lambda(z)=\lambda P_0(z)/q(z).
\]

The sum is well-defined in `Mat_m(F[lambda][[z]])`: a coefficient of `z^a` involves only words of length at most `a`. It satisfies `G_lambda'=A_lambda G_lambda`, `G_lambda(0)=I`. For zero constant coefficient use the input-output kernel

\[
 K_\lambda(z,t)=uG_\lambda(z)G_\lambda(t)^{-1}
                 \lambda P_0(t)v/q(t).
\]

This also lies in `F[lambda][[z,t]]`, degreewise. A nonzero word-length slice of `R` is a finite nonzero polynomial; finite-word faithfulness makes its `h` image nonzero. Distinct `lambda` powers separate slices, so `R(lambda J)1!=0` and `K_lambda!=0`. This uses no false unmarked cyclic faithfulness premise.

Repeat the row-closure proof over `F(lambda,z)`. Some `j<m` has

\[
 b_j(\lambda,t)=\frac{\lambda Z_j(\lambda,t)P_0(t)v}{q(t)^{j+1}}\ne0,
\]

and recurrence (4) with `P=lambda P_0` proves

\[
 \deg_z Z_j\le j(n-1),\qquad \deg_\lambda Z_j\le j.
\]

The nonzero numerator after dividing by the explicit factor `lambda` is a polynomial in `lambda` over `F[z]` of degree at most `j<=m-1`. It cannot vanish identically in `z` at every one of the distinct nonzero integers `1,...,m`: regard it as a polynomial over `F(z)`. Thus some fixed scale `a in {1,...,m}` has `b_j(a,t)!=0`. Its numerator degree in `t` is still at most `(j+1)(n-1)`. Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{\rm total} K_a\le nm-1,
\]

and the same Cauchy argument detects `R(aT_1^(2nm),...,aT_n^(2nm))`.

**Proved construction:** the `m` predetermined scaled tuples, each of dimension `d=2nm`, hit every nonzero proper series of state dimension at most `m`. A single direct-sum tuple has dimension `md=2nm^2`. It is jointly nilpotent of index at most `d`, even though its full matrix dimension is larger.

One can instead retain `lambda` by multiplication with a nilpotent shift `S_(m+1)`: the selected detector has some coefficient of `lambda` degree between one and `m`, and Cauchy recovery preserves that same degree. Hence `S_(m+1) tensor T_i` of dimension `2nm(m+1)` also hits with full output. The proof bounds the degree of **a selected nonzero detector**, not the degree of the complete evaluated polynomial `R(lambda T)`. The latter can have much larger degree. Confusing these claims would be a real gap.

Neither construction asserts a nonzero fixed seed column. No claim of minimal cyclic dimension or historical novelty is made.

## 10. Legal output-only residual reconstruction and complete costs

Assume an oracle returns only the final full matrix `R(X)` for any rational jointly nilpotent tuple of the bounded dimensions used below. The learner knows `n,m` and uses exact field arithmetic. It has no internal gates, transition matrices, coefficient membership queries or equivalence oracle.

Let `r<=m` be the dimension of the left residual span. For prefix `w`, write `R_w=sum_v R_(wv)x_v`. Every residual combination has the same transitions and a changed initial row. In the acyclic case, use the full feature

\[
 \phi(g)=g(C_1,\ldots,C_n),\qquad C_i=(T_i^{(d)})^T,
 \quad d=2nm.
\]

It is injective on that span. Indeed `g(C)=rev(g)(T)^T`; the reversed coefficient representation has transitions `B_i^T`, and a simultaneous anti-diagonal permutation makes them strictly upper triangular again. This proves the needed class membership. It is generally **false** that `g(T^T)=g(T)^T` without word reversal.

For arbitrary proper series use the tuple of features

\[
 \phi(g)=\bigl(g(aC_1,\ldots,aC_n)\bigr)_{a=1}^m.
\]

Reversal has the same state count with transposed transitions, and the scaled-set theorem makes this feature injective on the residual span. The learner may retain `m d^2` field entries without receiving one large direct-sum response and its redundant off-block zeros.

### Exact extractor with wide prefix layers

For `w=i_1...i_k`, prepend `k` layers of width `d` to a suffix block `C`. At the edge between consecutive prefix layers put `I_d` in the requested letter's matrix and zero in the others. The last prefix edge is `I_d` into the suffix block. Only that final block has internal transitions `C_i`. The tuple is strictly upper triangular in layer order and has dimension `(k+1)d`. Its first-layer-row / suffix-column output block is exactly `R_w(C)`:

\[
 [X_{j_1}\cdots X_{j_b}]_{\rm first,suffix}
 =\begin{cases}C_v,&j_1\cdots j_b=wv,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]

This identity remains valid for proper infinite series because the query is jointly nilpotent. At `k=0`, just query the suffix tuple. For the scaled feature do this separately at each of the `m` scales.

### Exact extractor with scalar prefixes and several seeds

Alternatively, use `k` scalar prefix states, ending with the edge into coordinate `e_q` of the suffix. The output source-row / suffix-column is row `q` of `R_w(C)`. Run this for all `d` seeds to recover the whole feature. Each query dimension is `d+k`. A single seed recovers only one row and cannot be presumed injective at `d=2nm`.

### Reconstruction proof

Query the empty feature. Select a prefix whenever its feature is independent of the selected features, and query every selected prefix's `n` children. A feature dependence is a true residual dependence by injectivity. Taking a further left derivative preserves it, so no descendants of a discarded prefix are needed. At most `r` prefixes are selected, their children complete an invariant residual span, and only `1+nr` distinct prefixes are needed. Every selected prefix has selected ancestors, so its length is at most `r-1` and each queried child has length at most `r`.

Solve `R_(pi)=sum_q A_i[p,q]R_q` from the features. Let `alpha` be the coordinates of the empty residual and let `beta_q` be the constant term of residual `R_q`, read from any feature's `(0,0)` diagonal entry. Induction on word length gives the exact all-word identity

\[
 [x_w]R=\alpha A_w\beta.
\]

The acquired realization has `r` states and is minimal because its residual basis spans the entire left Hankel space. Its weights need not be nonnegative or stochastic. This is the established residual/Hankel learning principle, compiled to the stated matrix oracle.

Put `M=1` for acyclic targets and `M=m` for the proper scaled list; keep `d=2nm` for a uniform comparison. Straightforward legal bounds are:

| Extractor | Full output oracle calls | Largest matrix dimension | Total returned field entries, upper bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Width-`d` prefix layers | `M(1+nr)` | `(r+1)d` | `M(1+nr)(r+1)^2d^2` |
| Scalar prefixes, all seeds | `M(1+nrd)` | `d+r` | `M d^2+M nrd(d+r)^2` |

These include complete oracle responses even though most entries are discarded. Query construction and encoding are also charged. The feature width is `M d^2`; conservative repeated exact coordinate solves use `O(n r^3 M d^2)` field operations. The output realization contains `nr^2+2r` field entries.

For acyclic targets, the source's old first-column feature already gives `1+nr` calls at dimension `d_old+r=O(nm^2)`. Wide extraction at the smaller base dimension still has maximum `O(nmr)`, hence the same `O(nm^2)` worst case. Therefore **a smaller one-shot hitting dimension does not automatically give a smaller same-call learning dimension**. The all-seed alternative exchanges calls for smaller matrices. For proper targets, the scaled construction improves the earlier large length-marker base, but the same prefix/response accounting is still required.

This does not prove reconstruction from one fixed tuple value, nor from an oracle restricted to just one seed or one fixed dimension. Over the rationals, runtime must include the total encoded response length. A bounded-height hidden realization supplies polynomial response and elimination bit bounds through finite nilpotent truncation and determinant bounds; without such a height promise, no runtime independent of hidden coefficient heights is asserted.

## 11. Lower bounds, stability and prior-art status

For fixed binary alphabet, every homogeneous polynomial of degree `2t` has an acyclic affine-edge ABP with `m_t=3*2^t-2` vertices. Use left prefix nodes of lengths `0,...,t-1` and right suffix nodes of lengths `0,...,t`. A left leaf `a` connects to suffix node `b` of length `t` with label `c_(a1b)x1+c_(a2b)x2`; the right nodes emit their suffix towards the empty sink. This realizes all `4^t` coefficients independently.

For any fixed tuple and any fixed seed, evaluation is a linear map from this entire `4^t`-dimensional coefficient space into `F^d`. Universal detection requires `d>=4^t=(m_t+2)^2/9`. A complete response lies in `Mat_d(F)` and only requires `d^2>=4^t`, hence `d>=2^t=(m_t+2)/3`. Thus linear order in `m` is optimal at fixed `n` for the full-response model, while quadratic order is necessary for fixed-seed features. This proves why replacing the old residual row by a smaller single row is invalid. It does not prove optimal dependence on `n` or on cyclic state count.

Every `T_i` is nilpotent: `x_1^d` vanishes. A shared circuit can construct exponentially large degree by repeated squaring. This obstructs extending the same formula-size dimension rule to arbitrary shared circuits; such a polynomial cannot have the asserted small acyclic representation.

Exact injectivity supplies no precision guarantee. Multiplying a nonzero rational target by an arbitrarily small nonzero rational scales all responses below any fixed absolute error threshold while preserving the state count. Normalization and a charged height/separation bound would be additional assumptions. Hilbert-matrix inversion is also potentially ill-conditioned; no claim of practical numerical stability follows from its exact determinant.

Nearest mechanisms are classical hyperlogarithm independence, standard formula/ABP path conversion, covariant derivative/observability closure and residual/Hankel reconstruction. The release explicitly cites Chen (1977), Deneufchatel et al. (2011), Nisan (1991), Raz–Shpilka (2005), and Forbes–Shpilka (2013). Its original scalar multiplicity mechanism is attributed to Moura (2003), but the new kernel bound bypasses that quadratic Wronskian bound. This audit verified the release text and its dependency proof; it did not complete an independent historical search of all these earlier papers. Companion worker's directly read primary prior-art snapshots show deterministic quasipolynomial black-box ABP hitting/reconstruction and polynomial white-box or internal-gate methods. They are not evidence of priority for our sharper full-response dimensions.

The potentially consequential deduction is a universal, explicit, polynomial-size exact matrix feature with linear acyclic state dimension, plus the short scaled repair for proper cyclic models. It can serve as a deterministic symbolic identity/equivalence primitive and compile a residual acquisition oracle into a reusable minimal representation. It is not a resolution of general commutative PIT, arbitrary rational-expression domain acquisition, statistical HMM identification or finite precision learning.

For equivalence of two unrelated promised representations, the difference has a block direct-sum representation with state bound `m_1+m_2`, so the tester must use that sum. The sharper common-transition bound used for residual combinations does not apply automatically to two unrelated models. A supplied-model baseline already has polynomial reachable-space linear algebra; the additional capability here concerns the specified final-matrix-output oracle.

## 12. Audit gates and next falsifiable step

All following universal proof gates survived reconstruction: affine-edge elimination without new states; finite-word independence and arbitrary-field transfer; left-action Volterra identity; first rational dependence and closure despite coefficient poles; numerator degrees including `n=1`; bivariate total-degree invariance; retained matrix entry order; characteristic-zero Cauchy invertibility; separate constant handling; and proper scaled repair with selected `lambda` degree rather than a global degree assertion.

The implementation check covers 14 small acyclic representations, nine sharp cancelling kernels, 30 ordered words, 12 exact Cauchy determinants, 13 complete cyclic collisions, the scaled escape of the five-state collision, and six residual prefixes under both legal extractors. It finishes in approximately half a second locally. These finite cases do not strengthen the theorem quantifiers; their role is to catch orientation and dimension mistakes in code.

No algebraic gap was found in the conservative `2nm` acyclic construction or in the proper scaled list. The largest unresolved scientific claims are historical novelty, a precision-stable variant under explicit normalization/height assumptions, and a realistic cost advantage with a realizable matrix oracle. The next proof attack should seek an exact nonzero `m`-state acyclic `f` with `f(T^(2nm))=0`, or a proper `m`-state series simultaneously zero on all `m` scaled tuples; any such witness would identify a failed gate above. The next acquisition attack should enforce the final-output-only oracle and verify every retained feature relation against the all-word residual span, while charging every response entry.
