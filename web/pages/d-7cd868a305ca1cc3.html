# Quantum thermodynamic costs of physically acquired measurement archives

## Early blocker and best scoped lemma

The detection records N18, N32, and N62 price the quantum probes by an input-energy observable. They do not price the physical measurement instrument, the memory cells that retain a result, its retention window or refresh schedule, or resetting the archive for reuse. A number of classical output bits alone therefore does not determine the work or heat paid to acquire and retain them.

A useful, fully specified post-measurement model is available for qubit memory cells undergoing independent amplitude damping. Let \(X\) be a uniform label in \([M]\), already acquired by a measurement and then encoded into \(m\) cells. Each cell has Hamiltonian \(H_M=\Delta|1\rangle\langle1|\), and one retention interval applies the independent channel with Kraus operators \(A_0=|0\rangle\langle0|+\sqrt{1-p}|1\rangle\langle1|\) and \(A_1=\sqrt p|0\rangle\langle1|\) to each cell. A collective decoder estimates \(X\) with average error \(\epsilon\). Write

\[
g_p(q)=h_2\!\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4p(1-p)q^2}}{2}\right),\qquad
F_p(q)=h_2((1-p)q)-g_p(q),
\]

where \(q\) is the mean excited-state population per cell and \(h_2\) is binary entropy in bits. The recent amplitude-damping capacity preprint proves the finite-block bound \(\chi(\mathcal A_p^{\otimes m}(\bar\rho))\leq\sum_iF_p(q_i)\) for arbitrary correlated/entangled block inputs and proves strict concavity of \(F_p\) for \(p<1\). Combining this with Jensen and the decoder's binary success test gives the finite-reliability archive bound

\[
d_{\rm bin}^{(2)}(1-\epsilon\Vert 1/M)
\;\leq\; \chi(X:B^m)
\;\leq\; m\max_{0\le q\le \min\{E,1\}}F_p(q),
\tag{1}
\]

when the message-averaged input excitation is at most \(mE\). Here \(d_{\rm bin}^{(2)}(a\Vert b)=a\log_2(a/b)+(1-a)\log_2((1-a)/(1-b))\); its left side equals \(\log_2 M-h_2(\epsilon)-\epsilon\log_2(M-1)\). Thus (1) charges the number of cells, their average stored energy \(\Delta mE\), and output reliability separately. It is a conditional, source-derived corollary, not an externally reviewed theorem and not a bound on the work of writing the result.

There is also a direct reliability-versus-energy converse in this model. Let \(Q=M^{-1}\sum_x\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_xN)\) be the average total excitation number across the encoded block, where \(N=\sum_i|1\rangle\langle1|_i\). The probability that the memory output is the all-ground state for message \(x\) is \(v_x=\operatorname{Tr}(p^N\rho_x)\ge p^{\operatorname{Tr}(N\rho_x)}\), by Jensen. The all-ground output is identical for all messages. For any decoder, the contribution of this common output to average error is at least \(M^{-1}\sum_{x\ne x_*}v_x\), where \(x_*\) indexes the largest \(v_x\). Applying Jensen to the remaining \(M-1\) messages and using the average-energy constraint gives

\[
\epsilon\;\geq\;\frac{M-1}{M}\,p^{MQ/(M-1)},\qquad 0<p<1.
\tag{2}
\]

For \(\epsilon<(M-1)/M\), rearranging (2) gives \(Q\ge\frac{M-1}{M\ln(1/p)}\ln\frac{M-1}{M\epsilon}\). For fixed target error as \(M\to\infty\), this has scale \(\ln(1/\epsilon)/\ln(1/p)\). This is sharp in order: arrange \(r\) banks of \(M\) cells; to encode label \(x\), excite cell \(x\) in every bank. If any excitation survives, decode \(x\); if all are lost, guess uniformly. This uses exactly \(r\) excitations, \(m=rM\) cells, and error \(p^r(1-1/M)\). The construction shows that, at fixed error tolerance, a fixed total memory excitation budget can store arbitrarily many labels if the number of zero-energy blank cells is allowed to grow. It does not permit error to vanish at fixed energy.

Equation (2) is reconstructed here from the amplitude-damping Kraus action and has not been independently reviewed. The proof is included to make its scope auditable. It is a simple sparse-code boundary result, closely related to the pure-loss pulse-position direction already recorded in the inherited noisy-archive capital; it is not claimed as a new general memory theorem.

## Exact acquisition and storage contract

The acquisition interface is a finite source ensemble \(X\sim\pi\), specimens \(\rho_x^{\otimes n}\), a supplied physical POVM \(\{M_y\}\) with \(P(y|x)=\operatorname{Tr}(M_y\rho_x^{\otimes n})\), followed by an encoder that maps the actually observed record \(y\) to a memory input state. The relevant end-to-end protocol is

\[
X\sim\pi,\quad \rho_x^{\otimes n}
\xrightarrow{\{M_y\}}
Y
\xrightarrow{y\mapsto\sigma_y^{(m)}}
\sigma_Y^{(m)}
\xrightarrow{\mathcal A_p^{\otimes m}\text{ for one hold interval}}
B^m
\xrightarrow{\text{decoder}}
\hat X.
\]

For a uniform \(M\)-hypothesis task with error \(\epsilon\), standard data processing and the Holevo bound imply

\[
d_{\rm bin}^{(2)}(1-\epsilon\Vert 1/M)\leq
\min\left\{\chi\bigl(\{\pi_x,\rho_x^{\otimes n}\}\bigr),
m\max_{0\le q\le\min\{E,1\}}F_p(q)\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Equation (3) is for reconstructing the source hypothesis \(X\) after measurement and storage. For archiving the raw measurement transcript \(Y\), use \(Y\) itself as the storage label in (1) (uniform case); for a nonuniform transcript, replace the uniform Fano term by \(H(Y)-h_2(\epsilon)-\epsilon\log_2(M-1)\). The quantum Holevo information about \(X\) is not an upper bound on the entropy of a random transcript, since the POVM can produce randomness independent of \(X\). Neither bound prices the work used by the POVM, cooling, control, readout electronics, preparation of clean memory, memory access, or refresh. If only a final decision or sufficient statistic is needed, that is a different storage label and can have far smaller cost. This boundary must be fixed before comparing resource totals.

Energy \(\Delta Q\) in (1)–(2) means energy above the all-ground memory state injected into the retention channel. It is not heat dissipated by the writing procedure. The channel is a model for a noisy retention interval; multiple intervals, correlated errors, refresh, or energy spent controlling the memory require a different channel and cost accounting.

## Thermodynamic boundary and counterexample to a universal write cost

There is no universal \(k_BT\ln2\) work bill for every bit written at the instant of measurement. In an ideal implementation with a pure initialized pointer register, premeasurement can be a reversible unitary correlation/copying operation. Faist et al. derive the work cost of a logical process from the conditional entropy of discarded information and explicitly identify ideal measurement with an initialized register as zero-work in principle; the cost of restoring that register is paid when resetting it. Sagawa–Ueda and later quantum measurement experiments likewise distinguish measurement work, meter free-energy change, outcome information, post-measurement state, and reset. The familiar \(k_BT\ln2\) applies to resetting an unbiased bit in a symmetric degenerate memory under the stated thermodynamic setup, not to every data-acquisition event.

There is a useful lower bound for a full reuse cycle once the model is specified. If the decoded archive has error \(\epsilon\), then its average physical memory state \(\bar\rho_B\) satisfies \(S_2(\bar\rho_B)\ge\chi(X:B)\ge d_{\rm bin}^{(2)}(1-\epsilon\Vert1/M)\). For a degenerate memory subsequently reset to a fixed pure blank state at temperature \(T\), with no side information or correlations available to the resetter, the reversible reset work is at least

\[
W_{\rm reset}\ge k_BT\ln2\;d_{\rm bin}^{(2)}(1-\epsilon\Vert1/M).
\tag{4}
\]

For nondegenerate cells, correlated reset side information, or a resetter that can use the source/post-measurement system, the relevant free-energy and conditional-entropy expression changes. Equation (4) is a reset bound under explicit assumptions; it cannot be transferred to the write step.

Holding/reliability energy is also material-specific. A primary model-and-experiment paper on a micromechanical bit reports that, in its model, the energy used to preserve a fixed error probability over a fixed duration can be made arbitrarily small by refreshing more frequently, with a separate lifetime ceiling from the uncertainty principle. This is not a universal architecture result; it is evidence that lifetime, refresh count, and the definition of counted energy must be explicit.

## Final verdict and next gate

No new universal thermodynamic law follows from N18/N32/N62 or W–Z. The sharp blocker is that the requested object is not a single channel until the source ensemble, measurement instrument, physical memory Hamiltonian, retention dynamics/time, allowed refresh, blank-state preparation, reset protocol, and side information are specified. Landauer does not fill in those missing choices.

The strongest concrete result here is the conditional finite-error archive frontier (1)–(3) for independent amplitude-damping qubit cells, plus the explicit zero-temperature sparse-code boundary (2). The finite-block capacity converse follows from the recent arXiv preprint’s Theorem 5.5/Proposition 5.6 and standard Fano/data processing; equation (2) is internally reconstructed. The result is not a proved measurement-work lower bound and is likely an incremental model-specific specialization of known noisy-memory and pulse-position ideas. Equation (4) separately prices reset under a degenerate-memory/no-side-information model.

To pursue a theorem that adds more than this, the next gate is a specified experimental memory architecture and one concrete acquisition task: name the POVM/instrument and raw-vs-sufficient-statistic archive, measure/calibrate its retention channel versus time and refresh, fix per-cell Hamiltonian and clean-blank resource accounting, and choose whether the objective is write work, stored energy, heat, total wall-plug energy, or reset work. Then compare optimal entangled encoding against an equally informed product-state/Pulse-position baseline under the same acquisition and reliability contract. Without that interface, a scalar “thermodynamic cost of a physically acquired archive” is undefined.
