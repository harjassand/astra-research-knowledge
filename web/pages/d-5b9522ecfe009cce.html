## Three planes, all subspaces accounted for

Let S1,S2,S3 be planes in K^3 with independent normal covectors, where K is any
field. Their pairwise intersections are lines, none contained in the third
plane. For the packing polytope

    P={x>=0: sum_i xi dim(Si intersect W)<=dim W for every W},

one has exactly P={x>=0: xi+xj<=1 for i!=j}. Necessity follows from each
pair-intersection line. For sufficiency, enumerate only the POSSIBLE DIMENSIONS
of W in the proof, not all its points or coordinates. A line belongs to at most
two planes, so its constraint follows from a pair inequality (or xi<=1). A
plane equal to Si has coefficient pattern(2,1,1); its inequality follows by
adding the two pair inequalities incident to i. Any other plane has pattern
(1,1,1), hence sum xi<=3/2<=2. The ambient constraint 2sum xi<=3 follows by
adding all three pair inequalities. W=0 is vacuous. This proof covers every
subspace over K; it is not a finite field enumeration.

With cost2 and rational caps 0<=ui<=1, let a<=b<=c be the sorted caps. Then

    tau=2 min(a+b+c,1+a,3/2).

Each displayed term is an upper bound: cap constraints; the pair inequality
opposite the smallest cap plus that cap; and the sum of the three pair
inequalities. Each bound has a feasible attaining vector in its applicable
case. If a+b+c is minimal, b+c<=1, so the cap vector itself is feasible. If
3/2 is minimal, a>=1/2, so (1/2,1/2,1/2) is feasible. In the remaining case
1+a is minimal, a<=1/2 and b+c>=1. In sorted coordinates choose

    x=(a,max(a,1-c),1-max(a,1-c)).

The second coordinate is at most b because a<=b and 1-c<=b. It is at most
1-a because a<=1/2 and c>=a. The last coordinate is therefore at least a,
at most c, and at most1-a. All three pair inequalities hold and the sum is
1+a. Ties may be assigned to any applicable attaining case.

The dual certificates are also explicit: cap penalties yi=2 for the cap-only
case; uniform building weights p=(1,1,1) for the3/2 case; or coefficient2 on
the pair-intersection line opposite the smallest cap, and cap penalty2 on that
smallest coordinate. The kernel does not receive a precomputed LP solution.

## Two decompositions in arbitrary even dimension

Let n=2q and let S1,...,Sq and S(q+1),...,S(2q) each be direct decompositions
of K^n into2-planes. Each decomposition indicator is in P: its intersections
with any W are pairwise disjoint subspaces of W. Therefore, for t in[0,1],

    x=(t,...,t,1-t,...,1-t)

is a feasible packing vector. If the interval

    [1-min_(i>q) ui, min_(i<=q) ui]

is nonempty, such a vector meets all caps. With all costs2 it attains n.
The ambient constraint bounds every packing objective by n, and uniform
building weights p=(1,...,1) provide a matching dual. The theorem includes
q=1 and every field, subject to acquired directness; it does not cover an
arbitrary arrangement of2-planes.

For the frozen n4 input the two concatenated determinants are1 and12, and
the feasible mixing interval is the singleton{1/3}. The controller obtains
the exact value4 and primal(1/3,1/3,2/3,2/3). Computing the two determinants
uses O(n^3) integer arithmetic with polynomial bit costs in n and entry height
via fraction-free elimination/Hadamard minor bounds; the cap interval uses
linear-many exact rational comparisons. This is a family acquisition cost,
not the complexity of a generic packing oracle.
