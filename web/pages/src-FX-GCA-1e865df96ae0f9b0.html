## Statement with exact supplied inputs

Let G be any real homogeneous quartic on R^(2x2), invariant under left
SO(2). Write G(X)=T(X,X,X,X), with T symmetric four-linear. Suppose a real
scalar lambda is supplied such that

\[
Q(X)=\tfrac12D^2G(I)[X,X]-\lambda\det X\ge0
\quad\hbox{for every }X. \tag{1}
\]

Let K=ker Q. Impose the exact cubic-flatness condition

\[
T(I,Z_1,Z_2,E)=0
\quad\hbox{for all }Z_1,Z_2\in K,\ E\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}. \tag{2}
\]

When Q is nonzero, let kappa>0 be any supplied or exactly computed lower
bound on Q restricted to K-perp. Let M>0 bound the four-linear norm of T;
the sum of the absolute coefficients of the expanded polynomial G is one
explicit choice. With

\[
H(A)=((a-d)^2+(b+c)^2)^2,
\qquad
\alpha_*=401296M^2/\kappa+(9391/4)M,
\]

the quartic G+alpha H is quasiconvex for every alpha>=alpha_*. For every
bounded planar Lipschitz Omega, every affine B, every W_0^(1,4) perturbation,
and every nu>=0, alpha>=alpha_*+nu implies

\[
\int_\Omega[(G+\alpha H)(B+D\phi)-(G+\alpha H)(B)]
\ge(4\nu/1875)\int_\Omega|D\phi|^4. \tag{3}
\]

The periodic analogue holds. These are sufficient conditions in the full
nine-dimensional invariant space. Failure of (1) or (2) is not a
counterexample to quasiconvexity or a proof that finite completion fails.
No equivalence for the full eight-dimensional projected cone is asserted.

## Proof

Put S(U,V,W)=T(I,U,V,W), and J0=sqrt(2)M. Decompose every increment X=Z+Y
orthogonally with Z in K and Y in K-perp. Equation (2) gives

\[
|S(E,X,X)|\le3J_0|E||X||Y|,\qquad
|S(X,X,X)|\le4J_0|X||Y|^2. \tag{4}
\]

Indeed the first expansion has only 2S(E,Z,Y)+S(E,Y,Y), and the second
has only 3S(Z,Y,Y)+S(Y,Y,Y). Bound each surviving term by J0 times the
product of the norms and use |Z|,|Y|<=|X|. Also Q(X)=Q(Y)>=kappa|Y|^2.

For an arbitrary background, rotate its target by SO(2) to write
B=sI+E with s>=0 and E anticonformal. Left invariance, compatibility,
norms and zero boundary values are preserved. Then |C B|^2=2|E|^2.
The exact quartic Taylor remainder is

\[
6T(B,B,X,X)+4T(B,X,X,X)+T(X,X,X,X).
\]

For X=Dphi, integrate (1); the constant-coefficient minor term
lambda s^2 integral det X vanishes. The three Young inequalities used in
REPORT.md Section 9 then give, using J0^2=2M^2,

\[
\int[G(B+X)-G(B)-DG(B):X]
\ge (\kappa/2)s^2\int|Y|^2
 -(2592M^2/\kappa+8M)|E|^2\int|X|^2
 -(512M^2/\kappa+3M)\int|X|^4. \tag{5}
\]

For clarity, the cross quadratic term is bounded by
36J0 s|E||X||Y|, costing kappa s^2|Y|^2/4 and
1296J0^2 |E|^2|X|^2/kappa. The conformal cubic term is bounded by
16J0 s|X||Y|^2, costing another kappa s^2|Y|^2/4 and
256J0^2 |X|^4/kappa. The remaining terms cost
8M|E|^2|X|^2+3M|X|^4. Thus no uniform ellipticity on K was assumed.

The H Bregman gap obeys

\[
H(B+X)-H(B)-DH(B):X
\ge |C B|^2|C X|^2+|C X|^4/5.
\]

For zero-trace gradients, integral |C X|^2=integral |X|^2 and
integral |X|^4<=(625/4) integral |C X|^4. The latter is the classical
complex-valued Beurling estimate described and sourced in REPORT.md.
Taking alpha at least half the coefficient of |E|^2|X|^2 in (5), plus
5(625/4) times the quartic coefficient, cancels both negative terms.
This is exactly alpha_* above. Linear terms integrate to zero, proving
quasiconvexity. Strong W^(1,4) approximation and the periodic cutoff
argument extend the result. A further nu H supplies (3).

## The zero-rank case

If Q=0 and (2) holds, then T(I,U,V,W)=0 for all U,V,W. By left invariance,
T(Q_theta I,U,V,W)=0 for every rotation Q_theta as well. The matrices
Q_theta I span the conformal plane, so T annihilates that plane in every
slot. Consequently G depends only on the anticonformal projection of A.
Left SO(2) acts as ordinary rotations on that two-dimensional projection;
an invariant homogeneous quartic there is a multiple of its squared norm
squared. Thus G=cH. The exact checker also regenerates the twenty symmetric
third-derivative equations in the nine-coefficient basis: their rank is
eight and their kernel is span{H}. A coefficient alpha>=max(0,-c) is a
convex completion; any additional nu H gives (3). This handles Q=0
without introducing an inverse or a nonexistent positive kappa.

## Exact finite compiler and charged inputs

For rational coefficients and rational lambda, the compiler does the
following fixed-dimensional exact operations:

1. Constructs the 4x4 symmetric matrix of (1), checks all principal minors
   for positive semidefiniteness, and computes its exact rank and a kernel
   basis by rational linear algebra.
2. Checks (2) on every pair of kernel basis vectors and the four coordinate
   vectors. Bilinearity then gives the full quantified condition.
3. If rank(Q)=r>=1, computes elementary symmetric functions e_r,e_(r-1) of
   its positive eigenvalues as coefficients of det(I+tQ), and sets

\[
\kappa=e_r/e_{r-1}
=\left(\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i^{-1}\right)^{-1}
\le\min_i\lambda_i.
\]

4. Uses M equal to the sum of absolute polynomial coefficients and returns
   alpha_*, the requested nonnegative H reserve, and the margin (3).

No eigenvalue approximation or support-feasibility oracle is hidden.
Lambda is a supplied certificate parameter. Finding such a lambda from
arbitrary coefficients is a separate task. A failed check returns
`UNRESOLVED_BY_THIS_COMPILER`, not `NOT_QC`. Kernel projection is used only
in the proof, so exact orthonormalization is unnecessary in the compiler.
All matrices have order at most four and the polynomial has at most 35
monomials. With fixed dimension, rational bit sizes grow O(n) for n-bit
input coefficients under the fixed arithmetic expressions.

The admission predicate is independent of adding any multiple of H to G,
because D^2H(I)=D^3H(I)=0. It therefore gives a well-defined sufficient
region in the full eight-dimensional H projection. That region is a
convex cone: for admitted G1,G2 with their supplied lambdas, Q1+Q2 is PSD,
ker(Q1+Q2)=ker Q1 intersect ker Q2, and both cubic tensors vanish on pairs
from that intersection. Positive scaling also preserves admission. No
closedness claim is made.

## Rank-one contacts and the limitation

If K=span{R} for a nonzero rank-one R and some finite H completion is
rank-one convex, then (2) follows automatically: the nonnegative acoustic
polynomial vanishes at (I,R), so its derivative in every background
direction E is zero. Since D^3H(I)=0, this is D^3G(I)[R,R,E]=0. Polarizing
gives the stated flatness on K. The same reasoning works when every member
of K is rank one. It does not justify flatness for a kernel containing
nonrank-one matrices.

The contact-stratum theorem in REPORT.md is an exact slice of this
compiler. There lambda is forced to be 3ell, and the admitted nontrivial
kernel is span{e11}. At either excluded equality boundary, the enlarged
kernel violates cubic flatness; the report proves actual acoustic
obstructions rather than merely reporting compiler failure.

The compiler is complementary to the supplied heat proof. For the sharp
inherited J_r, the contact forces lambda=r+2 and its quadratic kernel
contains K_r=diag(1,-r-1). The exact value

\[
T(I,K_r,K_r,K_r)=-r(r+2)/12
\]

is nonzero for r<-2 (and r>0), so (2) fails. The negative branch is
nevertheless quasiconvex by the separately supplied heat theorem. This is
a concrete reason not to call the new predicate necessary in the full
space.

## Primary comparison

The publisher PDF of Iwaniec-Kristensen (2005),
https://www.rivmat.unipr.it/fulltext/2005-4s/pdf/03.pdf , was acquired and
visually read at printed pp.77-80. Its Theorem 3.1 assumes globally uniform
strict Legendre-Hadamard positivity; its Lemma 4.1 provides the familiar
strong quasiconvexity of powers of elliptic linear operators. The
Taylor/Young and elliptic absorption method is credited to that classical
framework. The persistent rank-one contacts considered here violate the
strict hypothesis. No matching degenerate-flat classification was acquired
in this bounded comparison; historical novelty remains UNKNOWN.
