NONSIMPLY-LACED ROOT NORMALIZATION AND SHELL-CONDUCTANCE AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: independent check of root-plane normalization, exact shell traces,
and the simple-to-all-root conductance comparison for the fixed regular
cone. This does not replace the global upper-profile proof or its Cartan
patch.

RESULT

No root-length obstruction was found for fixed irreducible B,C,F4,G2
systems (or simply-laced types). The shell recurrence is length-free when
root vectors are normalized as an sl2 triple; root lengths enter exactly
once in the Casimir energy through kappa_alpha=(alpha,alpha)/2. Coroot
heights, rather than ordinary heights, control the leading shell trace.
The formulas below preserve these two distinct roles.

1. NORMALIZATION, INCLUDING SHORT ROOTS

Let B be the invariant form normalized so long roots have squared length
2. Choose root vectors E_alpha^*=E_-alpha and
[E_alpha,E_-alpha]=H_alpha, where H_alpha is the coroot alpha^vee. Put
b_alpha=B(E_alpha,E_-alpha). Invariance gives

  B(H_alpha,H)=B(E_alpha,[E_-alpha,H])
              =alpha(H) B(E_alpha,E_-alpha).

As B(H_alpha,H)=2 alpha(H)/(alpha,alpha), this forces

  b_alpha=2/(alpha,alpha),   kappa_alpha=b_alpha^(-1)=(alpha,alpha)/2.

Set

  T_alpha,x=(E_alpha+E_-alpha)/(2 sqrt(b_alpha)),
  T_alpha,y=(E_alpha-E_-alpha)/(2 i sqrt(b_alpha)).

Then B(T_alpha,x,T_alpha,x)=B(T_alpha,y,T_alpha,y)=1/2. Together with a
B-orthogonal Cartan basis of B-norm 1/2, these are the Hermitian-generator
normalization whose Casimir eigenvalue is
c_lambda=1/2 (lambda,lambda+2 rho), for long-root squared length 2.
For Hermitian X, [E_-alpha,X]=-[E_alpha,X]^dagger, and direct expansion
therefore gives the exact root-pair identity

  ||[T_(alpha,x),X]||_HS^2+||[T_(alpha,y),X]||_HS^2
       = kappa_alpha ||[E_alpha,X]||_HS^2.             (A)

For X=f(H0), H0=rho^vee, alpha(H0)=ell_alpha=ht(alpha), so

  ||[E_alpha,X]||_HS^2
       = sum_q c_alpha(q)|f(q)-f(q-ell_alpha)|^2,
  c_alpha(q)=Tr_(W_q)(E_-alpha E_alpha).

Thus the contribution is kappa_alpha c_alpha(q), with no extra factor 2.
For short roots kappa is 1/2 in lacing number 2 and 1/3 in lacing
number 3; the recurrence below does not absorb these factors.

2. EXACT TRACE AND CONDUCTANCE IDENTITIES

Write positive roots gamma and let

  F_Phi(z)=prod_(gamma>0)(1-z^ht(gamma))^(-1)=sum_q g_q z^q,
  V_q=sum_(u=0)^q g_u,
  h_alpha^vee=ht(alpha^vee)=<rho,alpha^vee>,
  L_alpha=<lambda,alpha^vee>,
  A_(gamma,alpha)=<gamma,alpha^vee>.

For a regular weight with every simple Dynkin label in [N,2N],
N h_alpha^vee<=L_alpha<=2N h_alpha^vee. The Kostant multiplicity
formula has only its identity Weyl term for depths q<=N: every nonidentity
shift lambda+rho-w(lambda+rho) is a positive-root combination of height
at least N+1. Hence the shell multiplicity is exactly g_q in that range,
and occupation marking in the PBW product gives

  s_alpha(q):=Tr_(W_q) H_alpha
    =[z^q] F_Phi(z) [ L_alpha
             - sum_(gamma>0) A_(gamma,alpha) z^ht(gamma)/(1-z^ht(gamma)) ].   (B)

This formula uses coroot pairings; substituting ordinary root coefficients
for A_(gamma,alpha) or h_alpha^vee would be incorrect outside the simply-
laced normalization.

Since E_alpha maps W_q to W_(q-ell_alpha), unitarity and
[E_alpha,E_-alpha]=H_alpha imply, by taking the W_q trace,

  c_alpha(q+ell_alpha)-c_alpha(q)=s_alpha(q),
  c_alpha(q)=0 for q<ell_alpha,
  c_alpha(q)=sum_(t>=1, q-t ell_alpha>=0) s_alpha(q-t ell_alpha).              (C)

(C) is exact throughout the representation and has no root-length
multiplier. The sign is fixed by [E_alpha,E_-alpha]=H_alpha.

Let A_Phi=max_(gamma,alpha>0)|<gamma,alpha^vee>|. A PBW monomial
beta=sum n_gamma gamma of depth q has sum n_gamma<=q, so
|<beta,alpha^vee>|<=A_Phi q. For q<=M=floor(N/(2 A_Phi)), each stable
weight in W_q has H_alpha eigenvalue between
N h_alpha^vee-N/2 and 2N h_alpha^vee+N/2. As h_alpha^vee>=1,

  (N/2) g_q <= s_alpha(q) <= (5N/2) h_alpha^vee g_q.             (D)

These bounds are for individual roots and correctly retain long/short
coroot data.

For the simple-root combined conductance define

  K1=sum_i kappa_(alpha_i),
  c_s(q)=sum_i kappa_(alpha_i)c_(alpha_i)(q).

A simple root has ht=ht(alpha_i^vee)=1, so (C)-(D) yield, for q<=M,

  (N/2) K1 V_(q-1) <= c_s(q)
                     <= (5N/2) K1 V_(q-1).                (E)

For any positive alpha and q>=ell_alpha with q-ell_alpha<=M-1,
(C)-(D) give

  kappa_alpha c_alpha(q)
       <= (5N/2) kappa_alpha h_alpha^vee V_(q-ell_alpha).  (F)

If j is an active simple increment in the alpha-edge difference, then
q-ell_alpha+1<=j<=q, hence q-ell_alpha<=j-1. Combining (E)-(F), whenever
j<=M,

  kappa_alpha c_alpha(q)
       <= 5 (kappa_alpha h_alpha^vee/K1) c_s(j).           (G)

The stated proof's looser fixed-volume comparison with Gamma_Phi also
follows; (G) shows Gamma_Phi is unnecessary if (F) is kept in its sharper
V_(q-ell_alpha) form. For a cap ending at shell M, all nonzero increments
have j<=M and all source shells satisfy q<=M+ell_alpha-1, so (F)-(G) use
stable trace shells only through q-ell_alpha<=M-1. This endpoint check is
important: one must not assert the positive trace lower bound (D) at
shells above M.

The low-rank linear ramp consequently has

  E(F_R) <= (5N/(2R)) V_(R-1)
             sum_(alpha>0) kappa_alpha h_alpha^vee ell_alpha^2,

because exactly R source shells cross a root edge and its difference is
at most ell_alpha/R. The near-full harmonic cap uses (G) on active simple
increments; Cauchy across ell_alpha one-step differences and at most
ell_alpha occurrences per increment give a fixed-root-system all-root
energy bound. Any larger ell-power constant, such as the inherited
ell_alpha^3 bound, is safe but not sharp.

3. LENGTH DATA FOR B2 AND G2

The following standard simple-root conventions make the factors explicit.
Coroot heights are listed separately from ordinary heights.

Type | positive-root coefficients | ht | ht(coroot) | kappa
B2, alpha1 short | (1,0) | 1 | 1 | 1/2
B2, alpha2 long  | (0,1) | 1 | 1 | 1
B2               | (1,1) | 2 | 3 | 1/2
B2               | (2,1) | 3 | 2 | 1
G2, alpha1 short | (1,0) | 1 | 1 | 1/3
G2, alpha2 long  | (0,1) | 1 | 1 | 1
G2               | (1,1) | 2 | 4 | 1/3
G2               | (2,1) | 3 | 5 | 1/3
G2               | (3,1) | 4 | 2 | 1
G2               | (3,2) | 5 | 3 | 1

Here A_B2=2, K1=3/2 and A_G2=3, K1=4/3. These data show why
ordinary height and coroot height cannot be conflated: for example the
B2 root (1,1) has heights 2 and 3; the G2 root (2,1) has heights 3 and
5. The conductance leading term uses the latter, whereas the taper step
uses the former.

4. FINITE DIAGNOSTICS AND SCOPE

Ran [root_length_audit.py](root_length_audit.py). Its B2 coefficient check
used N=180, M=45; its G2 check used N=360, M=60. For every listed positive
root it verified integer coroot coefficients/pairings, the exact PBW
marked-occupation shell trace formula (B), trace bounds (D) on all tested
stable shells, the recurrence (C), the conductance upper (F) through
source depth M+ht(alpha)-1, and the weighted simple comparison. All passed.
It also checked (A) on a four-dimensional su2 string for squared root
lengths 2, 1, and 2/3; maximum absolute discrepancies were 0, 0, and
1.8e-15.

These finite checks confirm the formulas on the specified coefficient
ranges and length conventions only. The proof is the invariant-form/sl2
calculation and PBW/Kostant argument above; no finite representation
simulation was used to establish the theorem. The global Cartan patch and
the full all-Q endpoint bookkeeping remain separate from this audit.
