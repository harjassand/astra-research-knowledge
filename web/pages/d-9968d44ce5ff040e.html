Finite-regime overlap audit: codimension Green lower vs uniform taper upper
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: algebraic comparison of the two stated results; no new optimizer or leading-constant claim.

Objects and common domain

Put p=m−1, N≥max(4p,16), n=binom(N+p,p), and

  Λ_{N,p}(k)=inf{ E(X): ||X||_HS=1, rank(X)≤n−k }.

The constructive_uniform_upper theorem applies to integers
1≤k≤k_max:=floor(32^(−p)n). The interval is nonempty exactly when
n≥32^p. In particular for p=1, n=N+1 and this requires N≥31 (the upper
theorem's nominal N≥16 condition alone does not ensure any admissible k).
For p≥2 the same integer nonemptiness condition applies; e.g. for p=2 the
first N satisfying it is N=44. Throughout, p is fixed: neither theorem
claims constants uniform as p grows.

The lower result ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt has an exact finite
spectral form for every 1≤k<n. In the present SU(m) notation, let

  λ_j=j(j+p),
  b_j=binom(j+p,p)^2−binom(j+p−1,p)^2,   1≤j≤N,
  λ_{N+1}=∞,

and define

  B_K(k)=(k²/n) sum_{j=1}^K b_j/λ_j + k/λ_{K+1},
  B(k)=min_{0≤K≤N} B_K(k).

Then for all 1≤k<n,

  Λ_{N,p}(k) ≥ [ n B(k)/k² + 1/(p+1) ]^(−1).          (G)

This finite bound is for arbitrary, possibly nonnormal matrices and every
right-kernel orientation. The taper upper is a feasible construction for the
same rank-at-most constraint. Its actual rank is n−V_R, where
V_R=binom(R+p,p)≥k; this rounding may remove more than k dimensions. Thus
the inequalities below genuinely overlap on the whole common integer domain,
but they need not identify a minimizer or exact codimension-k rank.

1. Critical case p=1 (m=2): logarithmic overlap

ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND gives the explicit finite lower bound

  Λ_{N,1}(k) ≥ 1/[3.5+log(n/k)].                       (1)

The uniform construction gives

  Λ_{N,1}(k) ≤ 12/[1+log(n/k)].                        (2)

Let x=log(n/k). On the full allowed range x≥log 32. The ratio of the
displayed upper envelope to the lower envelope is

  12(3.5+x)/(1+x),

which decreases from about 18.72 at k/n=1/32 to 12 as k/n→0. Therefore
(1)–(2) certify a uniform inverse-logarithmic order throughout the finite
range, including its proportional-codimension endpoint. They do not match
leading constants: for k=o(n), the lower envelope is (1+o(1))/log(n/k),
while the stated upper envelope is (12+o(1))/log(n/k). For k∼θn with
0<θ≤1/32, they give the fixed positive bracket

  1/[3.5+log(1/θ)]  ≲ Λ_{N,1}(k)
       ≲ 12/[1+log(1/θ)],

with the floor causing only an O(1/n) change in θ at the upper endpoint.
This is comparability, not a limiting value for Λ at fixed θ.

Regime readout for p=1:

* Fixed k (including k=1): both are Θ(1/log n); the displayed constants
  leave a factor tending to 12 as n→∞.
* k→∞ but k=o(n): both are Θ(1/log(n/k)); again the displayed upper is
  twelve times the lower asymptotically, not a leading-constant match.
* k∼θn in the allowed interval: both are Θ_θ(1), with the finite envelope
  ratio at most 18.72; no vanishing relative gap follows.

There is a separate existing refinement: heat_spectral_profile.txt, its
r=1 specialization to the same spin-N/2 carrier, proves
Λ_{N,1}(k)=(1+o(1))/log(n/k) for k=o(n). Thus the actual leading constant
is one in that sublinear regime. That sharp constant is not delivered by
the two displayed constants (1)–(2); it uses the sharper upper analysis in
that separate memo. It does not settle the proportional regime k∼θn.

2. Dimensions p≥2: power-law overlap and constants

The root bound states, for each fixed p≥2, a finite lower of the form

  Λ_{N,p}(k) ≥ c_p (k/n)^a,   a=(p−1)/p,                 (3)

with c_p>0 depending only on p. The file does not give a numerical value
for c_p. The uniform taper supplies the fully explicit upper

  Λ_{N,p}(k) ≤ U_p (k/n)^a,
  U_p=2(p−1) C_p B_p^(p−1)(p!)^(1/p)/η_p,
  C_p=p^p/(p−1)!,  B_p=1+(p!)^(1/p),  η_p=1−(3/4)^p.   (4)

For clarity about finite constants, one can extract an explicit (loose)
c_p directly from (G). Write

  A_p=2p^(2p−1)/[p!(p−1)!]
       +p^(2p−2)/[(p−1)!]^2.

For j≥1, set u=binom(j+p−1,p), v=binom(j+p−1,p−1). Then
b_j=2uv+v², u≤(pj)^p/p!, and v≤(pj)^(p−1)/(p−1)!, so
b_j≤A_p j^(2p−1). Since λ_j≥j², for K≤R with
R=(n/k)^(1/(2p)),

  sum_{j=1}^K b_j/λ_j ≤ A_p R^(2p−2).

Choose K=min(N,floor R). If K<N, then K+1>R and
k/λ_{K+1}≤k/R²; if K=N, the tail term is zero. Hence

  B(k) ≤ (A_p+1) k^(1+1/p)n^(−1/p).

Substitution into (G), using (n/k)^a≥1, gives the explicit valid finite
constant

  c_p^explicit = 1/[A_p+1+1/(p+1)]

in (3), for every 1≤k<n. This crude extraction is included to make the
finite overlap numerically interpretable; it is not asserted to be the
best constant in ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.

For p=2, this gives A_2=12 and c_2^explicit=3/40=0.075. Meanwhile

  U_2=128(2+sqrt(2))/7 ≈ 62.43.

So this completely explicit pair brackets the capacity by the same power
with a factor U_2/c_2^explicit≈832.4. The finite theorem is order-matched,
but these constants do not amount to a close numerical estimate. For
general p the same conclusion holds with the explicit expressions above;
the ratio is p-dependent and no uniform-in-p control is claimed.

Regime readout for every fixed p≥2 in the common interval:

* Fixed k, especially k=1: both sides scale as n^(−a)k^a. For k=1 the
  actual codimension is exact in the lower constraint and the upper uses
  R=0, V_R=1, so no rank overshoot occurs.
* k=o(n), whether k stays bounded or grows: both sides are comparable to
  (k/n)^a with p-dependent constants. This is genuine order matching; the
  leading coefficient and optimal kernel orientation remain unidentified.
* k∼θn with 0<θ≤32^(−p): both sides are fixed-order quantities bounded
  between c_p^explicit θ^a and U_p θ^a (up to floor effects). This extends
  comparability to the proportional regime for each fixed p, beyond the
  k=o(n) hypothesis of the asymptotic presentation in
  alternative_high_ceiling.txt. It does not give an asymptotic equality.
* At the largest allowed fraction θ=32^(−p), the common power factor is
  θ^a=32^(−(p−1)). The interval itself becomes exponentially narrow in p;
  the constants also depend on p.

3. What is matched, and what is not

Matched across all common finite k:

* p=1: inverse-logarithmic dependence, with explicit finite brackets (1)
  and (2); for k=o(n), a separate result sharpens the true leading
  constant to one.
* p≥2: the power exponent (p−1)/p, including fixed k, sublinear k, and
  proportional k within the construction's small-density range. Exact
  finite rank rounding is respected by the upper witness.

Not matched:

* No finite-k optimizer or equality of lower and upper functions is shown.
* For p≥2, the root file's c_p is unnamed numerically, and the explicit
  extracted c_p versus U_p can leave a very large factor. No leading
  coefficient is established, even as k/n→0, by this pair alone.
* For p=1, the pair's constants differ by a factor tending to 12 in the
  sublinear regime; the separate spin result, not the displayed taper
  constant 12, supplies the leading-one asymptotic.
* For k proportional to n, neither pair gives a sharp limiting profile.
  The stated upper is confined to k/n≤32^(−p); conclusions outside it
  would require another upper construction.

All comparisons are finite algebraic consequences of the two stated
inequalities and the displayed explicit extraction from (G). The finite
diagnostics recorded elsewhere are not used as proof. No novelty, external
validation, or operational-memory transfer is claimed.
