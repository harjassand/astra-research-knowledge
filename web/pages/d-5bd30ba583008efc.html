# Track A — Exact Bernoulli Collapse

Foundational conjugacy not established

The investigation has produced a rigorous structural result about the residual tail \\(D\\), together with a different approach to absorption using measurable cocycles.

The central conclusion so far is that the residual tail is not merely a small ambiguity left over by the infinite defect coding. It is itself an infinite, mixing \\(G\\)-system. Consequently, the remaining problem is more naturally formulated as preserving the extension's cocycle while redistributing Bernoulli randomness.

## 1. New structural theorem: the residual tail is mixing

Let

\\[ R=\mathbb F_2[G],\qquad D=\bigcap\_{n\ge0}B^nX. \\]

Assume \\(G\\) is countably infinite and torsion-free, with \\(ab=1\\) and \\(ac=0\ne c\\).

Proposition. The Haar action \\(G\curvearrowright D\\) is mixing and \\(D\\) is infinite. Moreover, for every nonidentity \\(t\in G\\), the coordinate restriction

\\[ \boxed{D\longrightarrow \mathbb F_2^{\langle t\rangle}} \\]

is a surjective, \\(\langle t\rangle\\)-equivariant compact-group homomorphism that pushes Haar measure to fair Bernoulli measure.

### Proof

Use the right-convolution convention

\\[ (T_r x)(g)=\sum_h r_hx(gh). \\]

Identifying the Pontryagin dual of \\(X\\) with \\(R\\), the dual of \\(B=T_b\\) is right multiplication by \\(b\\).

The annihilator of the residual tail is therefore

\\[ D^\perp=N =\bigcup\_{n\ge0}\\{r\in R:rb^n=0\\}, \\]

and

\\[ \widehat D=R/N. \\]

Suppose a nonzero character \\(r+N\\) has a nonidentity stabilizer \\(g\in G\\). Then

\\[ (g-1)r\in N, \\]

so for some \\(n\\),

\\[ (g-1)rb^n=0. \\]

Since \\(g\\) has infinite order, left multiplication by \\(g-1\\) is injective on \\(\mathbb F_2[G]\\): a nonzero finitely supported vector cannot be invariant under translation by \\(g\\).

It follows that \\(rb^n=0\\), contradicting \\(r+N\ne0\\).

Thus every nonzero character of \\(D\\) has trivial \\(G\\)-stabilizer. The Fourier-character criterion for mixing of compact algebraic actions proves that \\(G\curvearrowright D\\) is mixing.

Furthermore, applying augmentation to \\(ab=1\\) gives \\(\varepsilon(b)=1\\). Hence \\(B\\) fixes the constant-one configuration, which belongs to \\(D\\). Therefore \\(D\ne\\{0\\}\\). A nontrivial finite compact \\(G\\)-system cannot be mixing when \\(G\\) is infinite, so \\(D\\) is infinite.

Finally, for \\(H=\langle t\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\\), the group ring \\(R\\) is free as a left \\(\mathbb F_2[H]\\)-module. Because \\(\mathbb F_2[H]\\) is a domain, a nonzero \\(f\in\mathbb F_2[H]\\) cannot satisfy \\(fb^n=0\\), since \\(b^n\ne0\\).

Therefore

\\[ N\cap\mathbb F_2[H]=\\{0\\}. \\]

Duality gives the claimed surjection onto the full binary \\(H\\)-shift. \\(\square\\)

Significance: This strengthens the inherited statement \\(D\ne0\\). It identifies unavoidable residual randomness, although it does not establish a new Bernoulli conjugacy or the historical novelty of the proposition.

## 2. Strongest new mechanism: cocycle-preserving Bernoulli insertion

Rather than splitting \\(D\\) off as an independent factor, retain its entire extension structure.

Write

\\[ Y=K^{\mathbb N},\qquad \Phi:X\twoheadrightarrow Y,\qquad \ker\Phi=D. \\]

Choose a Borel section \\(s:Y\to X\\). Such a section exists because \\(\Phi\\) is a continuous surjection between compact metrizable spaces.

Define the \\(D\\)-valued cocycle

\\[ \alpha(g,y)=g s(y)-s(gy). \\]

The original action becomes the skew product

\\[ g\cdot(y,d)= \bigl(gy,\\;gd+\alpha(g,y)\bigr). \\]

This representation is exact. Its measure is Haar\\((Y)\otimes\\)Haar\\((D)\\), and it does not assume the extension splits equivariantly.

### A sufficient theorem for genuine entropy collapse

Let \\(B\_\varepsilon\\) be a nontrivial Bernoulli \\(G\\)-shift of base entropy \\(\varepsilon>0\\). Suppose there is a p.m.p. \\(G\\)-isomorphism

\\[ Q=(j,z):Y\longrightarrow Y\times B\_\varepsilon. \\]

If there exists a measurable \\(t:Y\to D\\) such that, for every \\(g\in G\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \alpha(g,j(y))-\alpha(g,y) =t(gy)-g t(y) } \tag{\*} \\]

almost everywhere, then

\\[ \boxed{X\cong_G X\times B\_\varepsilon.} \\]

This implies an exact conjugacy between Bernoulli shifts of unequal base entropy.

### Explicit conjugacy and inverse

Express \\(x=s(y)+d\\), where \\(y=\Phi(x)\\) and \\(d\in D\\). Define

\\[ F(x)= \left( s(j(y))+d+t(y),\ z(y) \right). \\]

Its inverse is explicit. Given \\((x',v)\in X\times B\_\varepsilon\\), first recover

\\[ y=Q^{-1}\bigl(\Phi(x'),v\bigr). \\]

Then set

\\[ d=x'-s(j(y))-t(y) \\]

and return

\\[ F^{-1}(x',v)=s(y)+d. \\]

The identity \\((\*)\\) proves equivariance by direct substitution. Fiberwise translation preserves Haar measure on \\(D\\), while \\(Q\\) preserves the base measure, establishing measure preservation. The two formulas are inverse almost everywhere.

Thus this is a complete conditional absorption theorem, not an approximate-coding argument.

## 3. Why this route is potentially useful

Assume, additionally, that the inherited proposed decomposition of \\(K\\) is correct for some \\(\varepsilon>0\\):

\\[ K\cong_G B\_\varepsilon\times R\_\varepsilon. \\]

Then

\\[ Y=K^{\mathbb N} \cong_G B\_\varepsilon^{\mathbb N}\times R\_\varepsilon^{\mathbb N}. \\]

By shifting the countably many \\(B\_\varepsilon\\) coordinates, one obtains isomorphisms

\\[ Q_N=(j_N,z_N):Y\cong_G Y\times B\_\varepsilon \\]

that leave the first \\(N\\) \\(K\\)-coordinates unchanged.

Consequently,

\\[ j_N(y)\longrightarrow y \\]

in the product topology for every \\(y\\), and for each fixed \\(g\in G\\),

\\[ \alpha(g,j_N(y))-\alpha(g,y) \longrightarrow0 \\]

in probability.

This follows by approximating the measurable map \\(\alpha(g,\cdot)\\) by continuous maps, using that \\(j_N\\) preserves Haar measure.

We therefore obtain a sequence of Bernoulli-inserting transformations whose effect on the residual cocycle tends to zero in measure.

The exact remaining problem is:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Does some }j_N\text{ preserve }[\alpha] \text{ in measurable twisted }H^1(G\curvearrowright Y;D)? } \\]

Here \\(D\\) retains its nontrivial \\(G\\)-action. Small cocycle perturbations need not be coboundaries, so convergence alone does not establish the required equality.

This mechanism is distinct from splitting \\(D\\), cancelling \\(R\_\varepsilon\\), or obtaining an inverse limit of linear automorphisms.

## 4. Reduction to one measurable binary function

The cocycle equation admits a more concrete formulation.

Set

\\[ \delta(y)=j(y)-y\in Y. \\]

Suppose there is a measurable \\(G\\)-equivariant map

\\[ \Lambda:Y\longrightarrow X \\]

satisfying

\\[ \boxed{\Phi(\Lambda(y))=\delta(y).} \tag{L} \\]

Then the entire conjugacy becomes

\\[ \boxed{ F(x)=\bigl(x+\Lambda(\Phi x),\ z(\Phi x)\bigr). } \\]

This automatically intertwines the \\(G\\)-actions. Given \\((x',v)\\), its inverse is

\\[ y=Q^{-1}(\Phi x',v),\qquad x=x'-\Lambda(y). \\]

The inverse is measurable, and conditional Haar measure on the fibers of \\(\Phi\\) is preserved by translation. Hence \\(F\\) is a p.m.p. conjugacy.

Condition (L) is equivalent to the cocycle equation \\((\*)\\).

### An explicit infinite system of XOR equations

Every measurable equivariant map \\(\Lambda:Y\to\mathbb F_2^G\\) is determined by a single binary observable \\(f:Y\to\mathbb F_2\\):

\\[ \Lambda_f(y)(g)=f(g^{-1}y). \\]

Write \\(p=1-ba\\), and let \\(\delta_n(y)\in K\\) be the \\(n\\)-th component of \\(\delta(y)\\).

Then (L) is equivalent to

\\[ \boxed{ \sum\_{h\in G}(pa^n)\_h f(h^{-1}y) = \delta_n(y)(e) \pmod2, \qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots } \tag{E} \\]

almost everywhere.

Every sum is finite because \\(pa^n\in\mathbb F_2[G]\\).

Thus the required nonlinear measurable conjugacy can be obtained by solving countably many finite-support parity constraints in one measurable Boolean observable.

### Every finite truncation is solvable

For any integer \\(m\ge1\\), define

\\[ \Lambda^{(m)}(y) =\sum\_{i=0}^{m-1}B^i\delta_i(y). \\]

This is a measurable, \\(G\\)-equivariant map \\(Y\to X\\), and the algebraic relations \\(AB=I\\), \\(AP=PB=0\\) give

\\[ \Phi(\Lambda^{(m)}(y)) = (\delta_0(y),\ldots,\delta\_{m-1}(y),0,0,\ldots). \\]

Therefore the function

\\[ f_m(y)=\Lambda^{(m)}(y)(e) \\]

solves the first \\(m\\) equations of (E) exactly, with no approximation and no additional hypothesis on \\(\delta\\).

The obstruction is now sharply isolated:

> Does the special displacement \\(\delta=j-\mathrm{id}\\), arising from a Bernoulli-coordinate insertion on \\(K^{\mathbb N}\\), admit a measurable equivariant lift despite the failure of convergence of its canonical finite-level lifts?

This is a measurable infinite-system problem, not a finite-rank problem.

## 5. Adversarial checks and obstructions

### A. Finite-level linear corrections cannot converge measurably

There is a general discreteness phenomenon.

Let \\(U,V:X\to X\\) be distinct continuous \\(G\\)-equivariant group homomorphisms. Their difference has a nonzero, shift-invariant compact-group image in \\(X\\). Haar measure on that image has a fair binary marginal at every group coordinate.

Consequently,

\\[ \boxed{ \Pr\_{x\sim\mathrm{Haar}(X)} [(Ux)(e)\ne(Vx)(e)]=\tfrac12. } \\]

Thus distinct continuous equivariant linear maps are separated by a fixed positive distance in convergence in measure.

In particular, consider the finite-level shear

\\[ S_n(x)=x+B^nPA^{n+1}x. \\]

It replaces the \\(n\\)-th defect coordinate \\(k_n\\) by \\(k_n+k\_{n+1}\\), leaving the other defect coordinates unchanged. It is an involutive, equivariant, Haar-preserving automorphism.

Yet

\\[ \Pr[(S_nx)(e)\ne x(e)]=\tfrac12 \\]

for every \\(n\\).

Conclusion: Sending a linear correction deeper into the defect tail does not make that correction small in the original Bernoulli coordinates. An infinite Hilbert-hotel construction needs genuinely nonlinear measurable control, not merely a limit of continuous linear shears.

### B. Algebraic augmentation obstructs linear Bernoulli absorption

Pontryagin duality identifies

\\[ \widehat K\cong Rp \\]

as a left \\(R=\mathbb F_2[G]\\)-module.

The augmentation homomorphism \\(\varepsilon:R\to\mathbb F_2\\) satisfies

\\[ \varepsilon(a)=\varepsilon(b)=1, \qquad \varepsilon(p)=0. \\]

Hence

\\[ \mathbb F_2\otimes_R Rp=0. \\]

Were there a continuous equivariant compact-group isomorphism

\\[ K^m\cong K^m\times2^G \\]

for finite \\(m\\), duality would imply

\\[ (Rp)^m\cong(Rp)^m\oplus R. \\]

Tensoring with the augmentation module would give

\\[ 0\cong\mathbb F_2, \\]

a contradiction.

This rules out algebraic absorption of an independent binary Bernoulli factor by finite powers of \\(K\\). It does not rule out a nonlinear measurable conjugacy.

### C. The original extension is relatively weakly mixing

The relatively independent self-joining of \\(X\\) over \\(Y\\) identifies with \\(X\times D\\) through

\\[ (x_1,x_2)\longmapsto(x_1,x_2-x_1). \\]

The induced action is the diagonal action on \\(X\times D\\). Both are mixing, by the Bernoulli property of \\(X\\) and Proposition 1.

Therefore

\\[ \boxed{\Phi:X\to Y\text{ is relatively weakly mixing}.} \\]

The extension has compact group fibers, but their \\(G\\)-action is nontrivial and mixing. It is not an ordinary relatively compact isometric extension.

### D. Two tempting literature shortcuts do not establish cancellation

The Bowen–Hoff–Ioana relative-isomorphism argument distinguishes a relative isomorphism for an ergodic transformation from one for equivalence-relation Bernoulli extensions. Neither statement alone establishes the required equivariant decomposition of an arbitrary algebraic \\(G\\)-action. The inherited assertion that \\(K\\) has a direct Bernoulli factor must therefore remain conditional until its separate proof is checked.&#x20;

### E. A useful enlargement of the mechanism

There is no reason to insist that the conjugacy act identically on the residual fiber.

Let \\(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\_G(D)\\). It is enough to solve

\\[ \boxed{ \alpha(g,jy)-\sigma\alpha(g,y) =t(gy)-gt(y). } \tag{C} \\]

Then

\\[ F(s(y)+d)= \bigl(s(jy)+\sigma(d)+t(y),z(y)\bigr) \\]

is an exact p.m.p. conjugacy, with the evident inverse using \\(\sigma^{-1}\\).

This enlargement is substantive. The existing splitting \\(X\cong_GX\times K\\) already satisfies (C): take \\(j\\) to be the left shift on \\(K^{\mathbb N}\\), \\(z(y)=y_0\\), and

\\[ \sigma=A|\_D,\qquad t(y)=As(y)-s(jy). \\]

Here \\(A|\_D\\) is an automorphism with inverse \\(B|\_D\\).

So the residual extension does possess nontrivial self-similarities that preserve its cocycle class up to a fiber automorphism. The missing step is finding one that extracts an independent Bernoulli coordinate without extracting an entire \\(K\\)-coordinate.

### Strongest continuation target

The decisive lemma is now:

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} Y=K^{\mathbb N},\quad Q=(j,z):Y\cong_GY\times B\_\varepsilon,\\\ \exists\\,\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\_G(D),\ t:Y\to D\text{ measurable}\\\ \text{such that equation (C) holds for every }g\in G. \end{gathered} } \\]

The most concrete attack is first to try \\(\sigma=I\\), solving (E) with a measurable binary observable; if that fails, identify the subgroup of residual automorphisms that can stabilize the extension class.

Final assessment: No exact conjugacy \\(2^G\cong_G4^G\\), or other unequal-base-entropy conjugacy, has been proved. The strongest advance is a reduction to an explicit cocycle-stabilization problem with fully solvable finite truncations and a precise obstruction to the naive infinite limit. Whether the structural propositions are novel remains unverified. They are not, by themselves, a 10/10 historical breakthrough.
