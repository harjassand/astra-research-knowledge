RAW-TRAJECTORY LOWER FRONTIER FOR RARE PREDICTIVE DIRECTIONS
8 October 2026

STATUS

SCOPED, INTERNALLY DERIVED THEOREM. A rational two-hidden-state HMM family gives a Le Cam/Fano lower bound for rank and predictive-basis acquisition from one stationary raw trajectory. The learner is told the whole finite family and all parameters except the active category J. Rare active events carry the basis signal; geometric idle runs make the raw mixing time tunable. A count-based estimator matches the basis-identification order. The proof charges every raw symbol, including idle symbols. No scalar-query oracle, dense sensing interface, hidden-state labels, or supplied one-hot WFA is used.

The lower is for this explicit family, not a universal minimax theorem for finite-state processes. Standard testing inequalities and finite-state spectral learning are prior tools; no novelty or priority claim is made. An exact-rational verifier checks the finite algebra in `work/programmes/acquisition_learning/cycle2/gap_precision_lower/verify_exact.py`.

1. FAMILY, PARAMETERS, AND LEGAL DATA

Choose integers q>=4 and m>=2, let s=1/m, choose rational p in (0,1/(2q)] and rational delta in (0,1/4]. Put p0=1-qp>=1/2. There are q candidate trigger labels 1,...,q and one common label 0. The observable alphabet is

  Sigma = {idle} union ({0,...,q} x {0,1}),  |Sigma|=2q+3.

For a candidate J in {1,...,q}, a hidden state S_t in {0,1} records whether the most recent active label was J. Initialize S_1~Bernoulli(p), which is stationary. At each raw time t, independently:

* with probability 1-s, emit `idle` and keep S_{t+1}=S_t;
* with probability s, draw X_t with P(X_t=i)=p for each i in {1,...,q} and P(X_t=0)=p0; draw B_t conditional on S_t with P(B_t=1|S_t)=1/2+delta S_t; emit (X_t,B_t); then set S_{t+1}=1{X_t=J}.

The training data is exactly one stationary raw string Y_1,...,Y_T. The learner sees all alphabet symbols and knows q,m,p,delta and this family, but not J and not whether the process is the rank-one null P_0 (delta=0) or a rank-two alternative P_J. It is not given hidden states, a realization, a basis, or conditional probabilities. All transition/emission probabilities can be rational. The null output law is independent of J.

The hidden transition matrix, with rows/columns ordered 0,1, is

  P_S = [[1-sp, sp], [s(1-p), 1-s(1-p)]].

Its stationary distribution is (1-p,p), and its nontrivial eigenvalue is 1-s. Thus

  ||Law(S_{t+k}|S_t=x)-Bernoulli(p)||_TV <= (1-s)^k,
  tau_mix(1/4) = ceil(log(4)/(-log(1-s))) = Theta(1/s)=Theta(m).

The observed process is a hidden Markov output of this chain, so its beta-mixing time is at most this scale, up to a one-step indexing shift. Write tau=1/s for the dwell-time parameter and tau_mix for the conventional 1/4 mixing time.

2. RANK, RARE HISTORY MASS, AND THE SINGULAR WITNESS

At delta=0 every raw symbol is iid: the output distribution does not depend on S. Its minimal predictive/Hankel rank is one. For delta>0 the HMM gives Hankel rank at most two. It is exactly two because histories ending in an active label J and in label 0 leave hidden states 1 and 0, respectively, and these states have different future laws. Thus the rank-one and rank-two alternatives have state cap two.

The stationary event that the current output is active with category i has mass s p for each candidate i, and mass s p0 for category 0. Define

  rho := s p = p/tau.

This is the smallest selected basis-event mass; the common reference event has mass at least s/2. Each atomic one-symbol history (i,b), i>=1, has probability rho(1/2 +/- p delta), between 15rho/32 and 17rho/32 under the stated range, so using the atomic-history minimum changes rho only by absolute constants.

Here is a finite event-Hankel witness, rather than an assumed query matrix. Let L=ceil(1/s) and alpha=1-(1-s)^L. Use the two observable history events

  h_J = {current output is (J,0) or (J,1)},
  h_0 = {current output is (0,0) or (0,1)},

and two future events over the next L raw symbols: f_1 (respectively f_0) means that a first non-idle output occurs within L steps and its bit is 1 (respectively 0). These are unions of ordinary finite words, so their 2x2 matrix is a row/column aggregation of a finite block of the ordinary word Hankel matrix. It is observable from raw symbols. The block is

  G_J = alpha * [[rho(1/2+delta), rho(1/2-delta)],
                  [s p0/2,         s p0/2        ]].

Its determinant is alpha^2 rho (s p0) delta. Let gamma=sigma_2(G_J), the smallest nonzero singular value of this two-dimensional predictive witness. Because q>=4, p/p0<=1/q<=1/4; because delta<=1/4, the squared norm of the first unscaled row is at most 5rho^2/8. Hence ||G_J||_F < alpha s p0, while its second row has norm alpha s p0/sqrt(2). Using sigma_1<=||G_J||_F and sigma_1>=||row_2|| gives

  alpha rho delta <= gamma <= sqrt(2) alpha rho delta,
  (1-e^(-1)) rho delta <= gamma <= 2 rho delta.

In particular gamma=Theta(rho delta). The event block has rank one at delta=0 and rank two at delta>0. This gamma is explicitly the singular gap of this acquired finite event-feature witness; it is not asserted to equal the unweighted singular value of an arbitrarily truncated all-word Hankel matrix.

3. RAW-TRAJECTORY KL BUDGET

Let d(delta)=KL(Ber(1/2+delta)||Ber(1/2)). For delta<=1/4, the Bernoulli chi-square bound gives d(delta)<=4delta^2. If the previous active label is known, comparing a biased bit to a fair bit in either KL direction costs at most 6delta^2: the denominator q(1-q) in the chi-square bound is at least 3/16 for the relevant Bernoulli probabilities.

For the KL calculation, reveal extra data W: the active/idle flags, active labels and bits, plus the last active label before the trajectory starts. This only makes testing easier. Under P_J versus P_0 the flag/label law is the same; at a raw time the current step is active with probability s and the hidden trigger state is 1 with stationary probability p. Therefore the chain rule gives

  KL(W_J || W_0) = T s p d(delta) = T rho d(delta)
                    <= 4 T rho delta^2.

By data processing this also upper-bounds KL of the complete observed raw trajectory law P_J^(T) against P_0^(T). No symbol is silently discarded in T: an idle output consumes one raw symbol while contributing no trigger-conditioned bit information.

For J!=K, the revealed active/idle and label sequence is common. The bit laws differ only when the previous active label is J or K, each of stationary probability p, and an active bit is emitted with probability s. Each directional bit KL is at most 6delta^2, so

  KL(P_J^(T) || P_K^(T)) <= KL(W_J || W_K)
                            <= 12 T rho delta^2.

These bounds hold for any estimator of the raw observations, including full-likelihood, full-Hankel, or arbitrary-computation procedures.

4. LOWER BOUNDS: RANK DECISION VERSUS BASIS IDENTIFICATION

Let eta<=1/4 and set

  kl_bin(eta) = KL(Ber(1-eta)||Ber(eta))
              = (1-2eta) log((1-eta)/eta).

Rank decision. Fix any J. A procedure that distinguishes P_0 (rank one) from P_J (rank two) with both errors at most eta induces a binary test with those errors. Data processing through the test requires KL(P_J^(T)||P_0^(T))>=kl_bin(eta), so

  T >= kl_bin(eta)/(4 rho delta^2).

This is an exact-rank lower bound even though both models have two-state-or-less realizations and mixing time Theta(tau). It shows why any uniform finite-sample rank claim needs a positive observable gap: for fixed T, rational delta can be taken arbitrarily small, making the two rank classes arbitrarily close.

Basis acquisition. Suppose an estimator must return the correct J with probability at least 1-eta for every rank-two alternative. With J uniform, Fano and the reference P_0 give

  I(J;Y_1:T) <= q^(-1) sum_j KL(P_j^(T)||P_0^(T))
               <= 4 T rho delta^2,
  I(J;Y_1:T) >= (1-eta)log q - log 2 >= (1/4)log q.

Thus T>=log(q)/(16 rho delta^2). Also, turning the q-way estimator into a test between any two indices J,K and applying the pairwise KL bound gives T>=kl_bin(eta)/(12 rho delta^2). Combining the two lower bounds,

  T >= [log q + kl_bin(eta)]/(32 rho delta^2).

This is also a target-error lower for estimation of the finite witness. Form all q event blocks G_i, using h_i and h_0 as their rows. Under P_J, G_J has second singular value gamma and every G_i for i!=J has rank one; under P_0 all q blocks have rank one. If an estimator returns all blocks with operator-norm error at most gamma/4, Weyl's inequality and a gamma/2 cutoff identify both the rank and J. Therefore the same Fano/pairwise lower applies to any such uniform block estimator. The count estimator below can meet this stronger block-error target by tightening the conditional-mean tolerance from delta/4 to delta/16, changing only the absolute sample constant.

Equivalently, since gamma is within absolute factors of rho delta,

  T >= c * (rho/gamma^2) * [log q + kl_bin(eta)]
    = c * (tau/(p delta^2)) * [log q + kl_bin(eta)],

for an absolute c>0. The first display is the direct parameter statement; the second uses the selected event-Hankel gap. Since |Sigma|=2q+3, the multi-hypothesis term is log((|Sigma|-3)/2). The hidden-state and predictive-rank cap is only two.

This charges raw trajectory length T. At the lower-bound scale, the expected number of active outputs is sT, and the expected number of appearances of the rare trigger event is rho T. If cost is instead charged only for active outputs, the corresponding order is 1/(p delta^2) times the logarithmic factor. In the requested raw-symbol metric, idle runs multiply that order by tau=1/s. A single stream start is used; no independent resets are assumed.

5. MATCHED EQUALLY INFORMED BASELINE

The strongest information-matched baseline is any minimax-optimal decision rule over the known family {P_0,P_1,...,P_q}, using the whole raw trajectory; unrestricted likelihood is an implementation route. The preceding lower bounds apply to every such rule. There is also a simple explicit estimator with the same order, so the lower is not an artifact of an inefficient spectral method.

Discard idle outputs and write the active subsequence as (X_1,B_1),...,(X_N,B_N). The flags give N~Binomial(T,s). For r=1,...,M=floor((N-1)/2), form disjoint pairs (X_(2r),B_(2r+1)). These pairs are iid, and

  Law(B_(2r+1)=1 | X_(2r)=i) = 1/2 + delta*1{i=J}

under P_J, while it is 1/2 for every i under P_0. For each candidate i, estimate this conditional mean from the pairs with X_(2r)=i. With probability at least 1-eta, all candidate counts are at least M p/2 and all means are within delta/4 of their expectations whenever

  M p delta^2 >= 16 log(4q/eta).

The rank test thresholds max_i p_hat_i at 1/2+delta/2; on the same event it selects rank one under P_0 and rank two plus the correct J under P_J. Binomial concentration for N then gives, for example,

  T >= ceil(128/(rho delta^2) * log(8q/eta))

as a sufficient raw-symbol budget. Thus basis/rank acquisition on this family has the matched order

  T = Theta((rho delta^2)^(-1) [log q + log(1/eta)])
    = Theta((rho/gamma^2) [log q + log(1/eta)]).

The estimator scans T symbols, uses O(T+q) arithmetic operations, O(q) counters of O(log T) bits, and returns J in ceil(log2 q) bits plus the rank bit. A fixed-width coding of the acquired stream costs at most T ceil(log2(2q+3)) bits. The lower bound counts symbols, not the compression cost of a particular encoding.

The learner is highly informed: it knows the entire candidate construction and p,s,delta. It still does not know J or receive a hidden basis. This makes the lower stronger as an obstruction to a less informed teacher-free learner. A dense empirical Hankel/SVD or the exact MLE has no extra data and cannot beat the lower. No scalar responses, matrix probes, one-hot state labels, or off-trajectory conditional answers are charged or supplied.

6. PREDICTION IS A DIFFERENT TARGET

Under P_J, the state S_t=1 has stationary probability p. The next raw-symbol law given state 1 differs in total variation by exactly s delta from the fair-bit rank-one law; given state 0 it agrees. Therefore the rank-one predictor that always outputs the iid fair-bit null kernel has expected stationary one-step TV prediction error

  E_PJ[ TV(Q_0, Law(Y_(t+1)|Y_1:t)) ] = p s delta = rho delta = Theta(gamma).

Consequently, for a prediction target epsilon >= rho delta, this rank-one predictor already meets the target although the true Hankel rank is two. Exact rank/basis recovery is strictly more demanding than prediction at that tolerance. The basis Fano lower above is not automatically a prediction lower for every loss/tolerance; converting it to such a result requires a loss-specific decoder argument. That stronger claim is UNKNOWN here.

7. COUNTEREXAMPLE, SCOPE, AND UNKNOWN

For any fixed raw-symbol budget T, choose rational delta>0 sufficiently small. P_0 has Hankel rank one and P_J has rank two, but

  KL(P_J^(T)||P_0^(T)) <= 4 T rho delta^2 -> 0,
  TV(P_J^(T),P_0^(T)) <= sqrt(2 T rho) delta -> 0.

Thus no finite T can uniformly recover exact rank across this family when gamma may approach zero. This is a concrete counterexample to any overbroad claim that bounded state count, positive transition probabilities, or a finite witness horizon alone guarantees rank acquisition. The slow version also shows that a bound stated only in informative active samples undercharges the raw-symbol interface by the mixing/dwell factor tau.

Known limitations and UNKNOWN:

* The result is a finite rational HMM family with two hidden states and a known alphabet/family; it is not a minimax lower for all unknown finite-state HMMs or arbitrary unstructured predictive bases.
* The q possible trigger labels are visible in the alphabet and known to the learner; what is hidden is which event is predictive. This is not a lower for discovering an unknown event dictionary.
* The singular value gamma is for the explicitly named 2x2 event-feature Hankel witness. No equality to the smallest singular value of every standard unweighted prefix/suffix block is claimed.
* Rank-only testing against an unknown J may have a different minimax rate from basis identification; the theorem gives a fixed-J Le Cam lower, a q-way Fano basis lower, and a matched scan for joint rank+basis acquisition.
* Prediction below epsilon=Theta(gamma) is not lower-bounded by the basis Fano statement without an additional risk-to-index reduction.
* General tau dependence for arbitrary processes, non-geometric renewal laws, unknown alphabet, and finite-horizon model selection are open here.
* Exact finite-state rank is not a prediction metric. Our rank-one predictor is an explicit example where task error can be small while rank recovery remains impossible.
