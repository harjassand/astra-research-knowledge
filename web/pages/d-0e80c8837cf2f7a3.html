# Exact acquisition: matched prior, constructions, and boundaries

## Interface and status

Input: the expanded rational coefficients of a homogeneous polynomial, with a promise

\[
 f(x)=H((Lx)_1^2,\ldots,(Lx)_n^2),\qquad L\in GL_n(\mathbb Q).
\]

The main subclass here is quartic, with \(H(m)=m^TQm\), \(Q\in\operatorname{Sym}_n(\mathbb Q)\), \(Q\succ0\). The metric and frame are absent. The requested output is a rational SPD metric aligned with some valid rational frame, or the frame itself with an exact evenness/PD certificate. Numerical whitening, an already supplied Euclidean metric, and algebraic rather than rational output are different interfaces.

A rational aligned metric can be multiplied by a common positive denominator to give an integral aligned SPD metric; this does not provide unimodular or integral orthonormal coordinates. Exact normalization to determinant one may itself introduce algebraic entries. Integer output up to scale and an exact rational frame are the relevant compatible interfaces.

Three concrete conclusions survive:

1. **All binary promised quartics admit an exact polynomial bit coefficient-only acquisition using the classical sextic covariant.** This is a reconstructed application of established invariant theory, not a new covariant. The parent worker's Hessian extactic specializes to exactly this sextic.
2. **All promised quartics in arbitrary dimension admit an acquired, unique, algebraic aligned metric by ordinary tensor norm balancing.** The proof below is independently reconstructed for this class. This is an established paradigm, but it does not meet ordinary rational-matrix output or give a variable-dimension polynomial bit algorithm.
3. **The polar reflection certificate is directly in recent primary literature:** Miura–Taki, arXiv:2608.25445v1, Proposition 3.5. A separate note reconstructs its all-dimension linear-reflection form and an exhaustive constant-degree baseline. This rejects novelty of metric-free quartic reflection certification. It does not reject an efficient variable-dimension procedure that acquires the rational reflection directions from coefficients.

The parent workers' generic higher-dimensional acquisition construction is audited elsewhere. This note establishes prerequisites and boundaries; it does not prove their extactic nonvanishing on every PD \(Q\). Historical novelty of that full composition remains **UNKNOWN**. A later independently reconstructed structural result is saved in `COMPLEX_MONOMIAL_AUTOMORPHISMS.md`: every complex automorphism of the QPD quartic permutes its hidden axes, with at most \(2n^2-n\) complex and \(n^2\) real reflections. This gives an all-input final point-count bound but not a polynomial solver or a nonzero-extactic theorem.

## Complete binary construction from a classical covariant

For a binary quartic define

\[
 h=\det\operatorname{Hess}f,\qquad
 G=f_xh_y-f_yh_x.
\]

Olver–Shakiban, *Graph Theory and Classical Invariant Theory* (1989), Example 3, printed p.216/PDF p.5, equations (8)–(9) and the following Jacobian formula, defines the Hessian and sextic covariants of the binary quartic. Their scalar normalization is immaterial. The same geometric sextic is discussed by Milne, *An easy geometrical representation of the sextic covariant of a binary quartic*, pp.65–66. Olver's publication year is verified on the first page. Milne's first page records receipt/read dates in October/November 1911; an inconsistent publisher search date was not used as chronology evidence.

In hidden even coordinates write

\[
 f=a x^4+2b x^2y^2+c y^4,\qquad a>0,\ c>0,\ ac-b^2>0.
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
 h=48\{abx^4+(3ac-b^2)x^2y^2+bcy^4\},
\]

\[
 \boxed{G=1152xy(ac-b^2)(ax^4-cy^4).}
\]

Hence \(G\ne0\), and both hidden coordinate hyperplanes divide it. Under a rational invertible change of variables, Hessian determinant and Jacobian acquire only determinant scalar weights. Thus, in the input coordinates, the two rational hidden linear forms divide the rational degree-six polynomial \(G\).

Algorithm:

1. Compute \(G\) from the expanded input coefficients.
2. Factor \(G\) over \(\mathbb Q\), retain its homogeneous linear factors, and normalize each coefficient row to a primitive integral row. There are at most six.
3. For each independent pair, form the rational row matrix \(A\); substitute \(x=A^{-1}u\).
4. Reject any odd exponent. Otherwise read the coefficients as \(a'u_1^4+2b'u_1^2u_2^2+c'u_2^4\), and require \(a'>0\), \(a'c'-(b')^2>0\).
5. Return any passing \(A\), its coefficient matrix \(Q'\), \(M=A^TA\), and axis columns \(A^{-1}\).

At least the original two hidden rows pass, up to rational scaling and permutation. Their rescaling acts on \(Q\) by a positive diagonal congruence, preserving PD. The returned decomposition is checked by exact substitution, and

\[
 (A^{-1})^T M A^{-1}=I.
\]

This covers every binary promised input, including symmetric extra sextic factors. Extra factors cause candidate pairs, not silent acceptance: the evenness and PD checks decide. The boundary \(ac=b^2\) can annihilate the sextic and is outside the promise. Exact near-boundary inputs do not require a floating point separation margin; their bit height is charged.

Degree, number of variables, and candidate count are constant. Differentiation, fixed-degree rational factorization, rational inverses, substitutions, and sign decisions therefore have deterministic polynomial bit complexity in the input coefficient length. This is not a measured SymPy runtime bound.

### Exact identification with the parent's Hessian extactic

Let \(f\) be any homogeneous binary polynomial of degree \(d\), \(K=\operatorname{Hess}f\), \(h=\det K\), and \(F=\operatorname{adj}(K)\nabla h\). Euler differentiation gives \(Kx=(d-1)\nabla f\). Consequently

\[
 \det(x,F)=\det(Kx,\nabla h)=(d-1)\operatorname{Jac}(f,h).
\]

This is a polynomial identity even where \(K\) is singular. For quartics the degree-one extactic is exactly \(3G\). Thus the binary construction is the parent's chosen vector-field/extactic construction in classical terminology. A novelty claim for its binary covariant is rejected.

## An all-input algebraic metric from tensor balancing

This construction is a specialization of norm minimization/Kempf–Ness theory. Recent primary anchors include Hirai–Nieuwboer–Walter, *Interior-Point Methods on Manifolds: Theory and Applications*, published 2026-07-07, §6.1 Definition 6.1, Proposition 6.2, Theorem 6.9; and Bagherian, *Algebraic formulation of simultaneous tensor-metric learning*, published 2026-05-25, §§2.1.1–2.1.2, Theorems 5–6. The latter's applications learn metrics numerically from tensor/data problems; they do not state this exact hidden-frame interface. We independently prove the requisite existence/uniqueness here rather than assume arbitrary norm orbits are closed.

Let \(T\) be the rational symmetric 4-tensor with \(f(x)=T_{ijkl}x_ix_jx_kx_l\). For \(P\succ0\), \(\det P=1\), define

\[
 N_f(P)=T_{ijkl}T_{abcd}P_{ia}P_{jb}P_{kc}P_{ld}.
\]

Repeated indices are summed. If \(P=gg^T\), then \(N_f(P)=\|f(g\,\cdot)\|_B^2\), where the Bombieri norm is the ordinary induced tensor norm. This definition uses only input coefficients and unknown entries of \(P\).

### Finite stabilizer and strict geodesic convexity

The infinitesimal linear stabilizer of every promised quartic is zero. In hidden coordinates, suppose the directional action \(\nabla f(y)^TAy\) vanishes identically. The \(y_i^4\) coefficient gives \(Q_{ii}A_{ii}=0\), hence \(A_{ii}=0\). For every \(i\ne j\), the coefficients of \(y_i^3y_j\) and \(y_iy_j^3\) give

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}Q_{ii}&Q_{ij}\\Q_{ij}&Q_{jj}\end{pmatrix}
 \binom{A_{ij}}{A_{ji}}=0.
\]

Every such principal submatrix is nonsingular because \(Q\succ0\), so \(A=0\). This works over \(\mathbb C\) too. The algebraic linear automorphism group is therefore zero dimensional and finite.

For a determinant-one SPD geodesic

\[
 P(t)=P_0^{1/2}\exp(tB)P_0^{1/2},\qquad B=B^T,\quad\operatorname{tr}B=0,
\]

diagonalize \(B\) orthogonally and expand the transformed tensor in its orthogonal monomial basis. Its squared norm has the form

\[
 N_f(P(t))=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha^2 w_\alpha\exp(t\,\alpha\cdot b),
\]

with \(w_\alpha>0\). The second derivative is positive unless every supported monomial has \(\alpha\cdot b=0\); that exceptional case is precisely a nonzero infinitesimal stabilizer of the transformed quartic. The zero-stabilizer proof excludes it. Thus \(N_f\) is strictly convex along every nonconstant SPD geodesic.

### Coercivity and existence

Since \(Q\succ0\) and \(L\) is invertible, \(f(x)>0\) for every real nonzero \(x\). Put \(\alpha=\min_{\|x\|=1}f(x)>0\). If \(P=gg^T\), tensor Cauchy–Schwarz and evaluation at a largest singular-vector direction give

\[
 \sqrt{N_f(P)}\ \ge\ \max_{\|x\|=1} f(gx)
 \ \ge\ \alpha\|g\|_{op}^4,
\]

so \(N_f(P)\ge\alpha^2\lambda_{max}(P)^4\). Any determinant-one SPD sequence leaving every compact set has \(\lambda_{max}\to\infty\) along a subsequence. Thus the objective is coercive. It has exactly one minimizer \(P_f\).

### Alignment and exact algebraicity

For any \(f\)-automorphism \(S\), \(|\det S|=1\): positivity makes the real automorphism group compact, and the determinant homomorphism of a compact group has absolute value one. Tensor contraction gives

\[
 N_f(SPS^T)=N_f(P).
\]

Uniqueness therefore gives \(SP_fS^T=P_f\). For hidden coordinate flips \(S_i=L^{-1}D_iL\), this forces \(LP_fL^T\) to be diagonal. Consequently \(M_f=P_f^{-1}\) is an aligned SPD metric.

Define the cubic symmetric matrix polynomial

\[
 C_{ia}(P)=T_{ijkl}T_{abcd}P_{jb}P_{kc}P_{ld}.
\]

Differentiating gives \(dN=4\operatorname{tr}(C\,dP)\), and stationarity under \(\det P=1\) gives

\[
 \boxed{nC(P)P=N_f(P)I,\qquad\det P=1,\qquad P\succ0.}
\]

These are rational polynomial equations and strict rational inequalities, with exactly one real SPD solution. Each entry of this isolated semialgebraic solution is real algebraic. General exact real algebraic elimination can return it. At fixed dimension this is an exact finite algebraic acquisition procedure. Nothing here supplies a polynomial dependence on variable \(n\), or an ordinary rational output matrix.

### An explicit hidden-coordinate normal form

Once hidden coordinates are used solely for analysis, sign invariance makes \(P=\operatorname{diag}(p_i)\). Put \(r_i=p_i^2\). The Bombieri tensor norm is

\[
 N=\sum_iQ_{ii}^2r_i^2+\frac23\sum_{i<j}Q_{ij}^2r_ir_j=r^TSr,
\quad S_{ii}=Q_{ii}^2,\quad S_{ij}=Q_{ij}^2/3.
\]

Stationarity is \(r_i(Sr)_i=N/n\), with \(\prod_i r_i=1\). This explicit system exhibits the algebraic rather than rational nature of the metric. The off-diagonal coefficient is obtained from the six symmetric tensor entries \(T_{iijj}=Q_{ij}/3\); it is not \(Q_{ij}^2\) without the factor \(1/3\).

### Complexity of recent numerical balancing is a different claim

Hirai–Nieuwboer–Walter optimize a Kempf–Ness log norm to additive objective error over a prescribed radius. Theorem 1.8 states an iteration bound of order \((1+R_0)N(\pi)\log(N(\pi)R_0/\varepsilon)\); Theorem 6.9 supplies the detailed barrier formulation. This is approximate objective minimization, with radius and representation parameters charged. It does not produce exact rational axes. Approximate balancing followed by rational reconstruction needs explicit condition, separation, and height bounds; these have not been supplied for all promised inputs here.

## Rational canonical selection obstruction

Take

\[
 f=a x^4+2b x^2y^2+c y^4,\qquad a,c>0,\quad ac-b^2>0.
\]

Sign flips force every symmetry-invariant SPD metric to be diagonal. The real scaled swap

\[
 S(x,y)=((c/a)^{1/4}y,(a/c)^{1/4}x)
\]

also preserves \(f\), and \(S^TMS=M\) forces \(M_{22}/M_{11}=\sqrt{c/a}\). For \(f=x^4+2y^4\), no rational such SPD metric exists. Yet \(I\) is a valid rational aligned metric.

Therefore a completely GL-natural, full-real-symmetry-invariant **rational** metric selector cannot exist on all promised inputs. A selector may choose a rational aligned frame while breaking nonrational symmetries. It may also output the invariant metric space, a real/algebraic metric, or a rational metric invariant only under recovered rational automorphisms. This obstruction does not reject those outputs.

The balancing metric makes the issue explicit: for \(x^4+2y^4\), \(P_f=\operatorname{diag}(2^{1/4},2^{-1/4})\), so the inverse metric has the irrational projective ratio above. For \(\sum_i p_i x_i^4\) with distinct positive primes, projective canonical metric ratios are \(\sqrt{p_i/p_1}\). Their common field has degree \(2^{n-1}\). This warns against silently treating a common-number-field representation as a polynomial sized rational matrix. Separate radical or arithmetic-circuit representations can remain compact; no lower bound for every algebraic encoding or for every rational-axis algorithm follows.

## Scientific applications with actual compatible formulas

### Quantum mechanical quartic potentials

Keren's recent primary preprint arXiv:2608.07047v3 (submitted v1 Aug7; v3 Sep9), §II.B symmetry reduction and §III Eq7, uses

\[
 V(q)=20[(q_1^2-1)^2+(q_2^2-1)^2]-q_1^2q_2^2.
\]

Its leading homogeneous quartic has

\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}20&-1/2\\-1/2&20\end{pmatrix}\succ0.
\]

This is a genuine rational coupled instance of the exact main subclass. The paper uses sign symmetries to organize moment blocks. Under a rational invertible coordinate calibration, the leading quartic is an expanded hidden-frame input; the binary construction above recovers a certified rational frame. The rest of the Hamiltonian must also be checked before using that reflection group to reduce moment matrices. In particular, the kinetic quadratic form is an independent supplied physical metric. A potential-only aligned metric is not a derivation of that kinetic metric.

This is a concrete coordinate-normalization/certification use, not evidence that the source's original visibly even benchmark needed missing-metric acquisition. The preprint's larger bootstrap/counterexample claims were not adopted or independently verified by this worker.

### Smooth quartic K3 surfaces and projection symmetries

For \(n=4\), a promised QPD quartic defines a smooth quartic surface over \(\mathbb C\), even though its real zero set is empty. Hidden rational axis points are outer quasi-Galois points and hidden flips act as projective involutions. Miura–Taki Prop3.9 identifies the relevant non-Galois involution's K3 type as \((8,8,1)\). Its quotient/projection model comes from the exact even normal form, rather than a numerical Euclidean symmetry fit.

The Fermat input \(\sum_i y_i^4\) meets QPD, and their Thm5.4 counts 28 complex quasi-Galois points. Four are coordinate Galois points; 24 are pair directions with fourth roots of unity. Only 16 directions are rational/real. A rational GL mixing preserves the hidden-input promise and makes recovering those rational projective symmetries a concrete symbolic-geometry task. Coupling the squared coordinates generically removes order4 symmetries while retaining order2 flips. This application is mathematically real and exact, although the source begins in visible normal coordinates and does not demonstrate a need for a new polynomial-dimensional acquisition algorithm.

### Elasticity boundary and a matched subclass

For an orthotropic elasticity tensor \(C\), evaluation on a rank-one longitudinal strain gives

\[
 f(v)=C_{ijkl}v_iv_jv_kv_l
 =\sum_i C_{iiii}v_i^4+2\sum_{i<j}(C_{iijj}+2C_{ijij})v_i^2v_j^2.
\]

Thus \(Q_{ii}=C_{iiii}\) and \(Q_{ij}=C_{iijj}+2C_{ijij}\). A rational exact orthotropic tensor with normal stiffness \(\operatorname{diag}(2,3,4)\), zero normal cross coupling, and shear entries \(C_{1212}=C_{1313}=C_{2323}=1/8\) is physically SPD and induces PD \(Q\) with off-diagonal entries \(1/4\). Rational orthogonal mixing or rational coordinate calibration gives compatible expanded exact examples. The physical Euclidean metric is ordinarily supplied in elasticity algorithms; unknown calibration gives the genuinely missing-metric variant.

General stable elasticity is broader than this promise. Isotropic elasticity produces \(f=c\|v\|^4\), hence \(Q=c\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\), rank one. Noise, transverse elastic information, or a non-PD induced \(Q\) invalidates direct use of this theorem. A recovered symmetry of the longitudinal quartic also need not preserve the entire elasticity tensor; verify it against \(C\) before material classification.
