THEOREM
For all real x,y define
 k(x)=x sinh(x)−cosh(x)+1,
 T(x,y)=x sinh(y)+y sinh(x)−4 sinh(x/2)sinh(y/2).
Then
 T(x,y)^2 <= 4 cosh((x−y)/2)^2 k(x)k(y).
Equality holds exactly when x=y or xy=0.
The proof establishes the pair inequality, not full PSD of the proposed
kernel K(x,y)=T(x,y)/(2cosh((x−y)/2)). Pairwise Cauchy bounds do not in general
imply PSD of every finite kernel matrix, so that stronger statement is left
unclaimed.

PROOF
1. Preliminary identities and monotonicities.

k is even, k(0)=0, and k'(t)=t cosh(t). Thus for t>=0,
 k(t)=integral_0^t v cosh(v) dv
     =t^2 integral_0^1 u cosh(ut) du,
and k(t)>0 for t>0. Put
 F(t)=k(t)/cosh(t)=t tanh(t)−1+sech(t),
 phi(t)=k(t)/(t^2 cosh(t)), with phi(0)=1/2.
For t>=0,
 F'(t)=t sech(t)^2+(1−sech(t))tanh(t)>=0,
strictly >0 for t>0. Also
 phi(t)=integral_0^1 u cosh(ut)/cosh(t) du.
For each 0<=u<=1 the integrand's cosh ratio is nonincreasing in t>=0, because
 its logarithmic derivative is u tanh(ut)−tanh(t)<=0.
Indeed tanh(ut)<=tanh(t), both are nonnegative, and u<=1. Therefore phi is
nonincreasing on [0,infinity), and strictly decreasing when t increases
between distinct nonnegative points (the integrand is strict for 0<u<1).

2. Exact midpoint identity and a dominating kernel.

First take x,y>=0, and put m=(x+y)/2, z=|x−y|/2, so 0<=z<=m. If m=0 the
theorem is immediate. Hyperbolic addition identities give exactly
 T(x,y)=2m sinh(m)cosh(z)−2z cosh(m)sinh(z)
         −2cosh(m)+2cosh(z).
Hence
 K(x,y)=m sinh(m)−z cosh(m)tanh(z)−cosh(m)sech(z)+1
       =k(m)−cosh(m) k(z)/cosh(z)
       =cosh(m)[F(m)−F(z)].
By monotonicity of F, K(x,y)>=0. By monotonicity of phi,
 cosh(m) k(z)/cosh(z)
   =cosh(m) z^2 phi(z) >= z^2 k(m)/m^2.
Consequently
 0<=K(x,y)<=H(x,y):=xy k(m)/m^2,
using m^2−z^2=xy. For positive unequal x,y, z>0 and m>z, so K<H strictly.
When x=y, z=0 and K=H=k(x). When xy=0, m=z and K=H=0.

3. The dominating kernel is an explicit Gram kernel.

For every real x introduce a vector in
 L2([0,1],u du) direct-sum L2([0,1],u du):
 v_x(u)=(x cosh(ux/2), x sinh(ux/2)).
Its squared norm is
 ||v_x||^2=x^2 integral_0^1 u cosh(ux)du=k(x),
including x=0 by continuity. Its inner product with v_y is
 <v_x,v_y>=xy integral_0^1 u cosh(u(x+y)/2)du.
For the nonnegative pair above this is precisely H(x,y). Cauchy–Schwarz
therefore gives H(x,y)^2<=k(x)k(y). Combining 0<=K<=H proves the desired
inequality for nonnegative x,y. It also proves equality only when x=y or
xy=0: every other positive pair has the strict first bound K<H.

4. Signs and zero endpoints.

T is odd separately in each variable and k is even. Simultaneously negating
x and y leaves K unchanged. Thus two negative variables reduce exactly to
the nonnegative case. For opposite signs let a=|x|, b=|y|>0. Then
 |T(x,y)|=T(a,b),
 cosh((x−y)/2)=cosh((a+b)/2) > cosh((a−b)/2).
The proved nonnegative bound and the larger denominator give the inequality,
strictly when ab>0. If either variable is zero, T=0 and k(x)k(y)=0 exactly.
This covers every real pair and proves the equality characterization. QED.
