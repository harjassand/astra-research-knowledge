CYCLE06 INDEPENDENT BASELINE: THE GLOBAL QUBIT WEAK-REGULARITY ENDPOINT

Status and scope
----------------
This is a model-assisted, internally derived candidate theorem with a complete
analytic proof below. It is not externally reviewed, formally verified, or a
claim of novelty. Numerical diagnostics are separate evidence and are not used
as proof. No sibling Cycle06 proof was read before this baseline was frozen.

THEOREM (claimed and proved below). Let sigma and rho be faithful density
matrices on C^2. Let L be the positive Heisenberg generator of a finite KMS-
symmetric quantum Markov semigroup: exp(-t L) is unital completely positive,
sigma is stationary, and L is selfadjoint for the sigma-KMS inner product.
No primitivity or modular covariance is assumed. Put

  Gamma(X) = sigma^(1/2) X sigma^(1/2),
  H = Gamma^(1/2) L Gamma^(-1/2),
  J(rho) = Tr[L_*(rho) (log rho - log sigma)],
  E(rho) = Tr[sqrt(rho) H(sqrt(rho))].

Then J(rho) >= 2 E(rho).

The same conclusion holds with an untouched classical reference, meaning a
joint rho that is block diagonal in some reference basis. The fully quantum
reference extension and the endpoint in dimensions >= 3 remain UNRESOLVED.
The proof does not promote the existing finite searches to either conclusion.

1. Every finite KMS generator has a Hermitian-noise Lyapunov representation
------------------------------------------------------------------------
This part holds in every finite dimension m. Set

  s = sqrt(sigma),   d = sigma^(1/4),
  ell_s(V) = (sV + Vs)/2.

The map S_t = exp(-tH) is completely positive, Hilbert-Schmidt selfadjoint,
and S_t(s)=s. Indeed it is obtained from exp(-tL) by the two completely positive
invertible sandwiches X -> d X d and X -> d^(-1) X d^(-1). KMS symmetry gives
Hilbert-Schmidt selfadjointness after this conjugation. Unitality gives H(s)=0.

Every Hilbert-Schmidt selfadjoint CP map S admits Hermitian Kraus operators.
To see this, write S(X)=sum_j W_j^* X W_j. Its Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint is
S^*(X)=sum_j W_j X W_j^*. Since S=S^*, their average equals S. Writing
W_j=F_j+iG_j with F_j,G_j Hermitian gives

  (W_j^* X W_j + W_j X W_j^*)/2 = F_j X F_j + G_j X G_j.

Fix a Hilbert-Schmidt orthonormal Hermitian basis F_0,...,F_(m^2-1), with
F_0=I/sqrt(m) and F_a traceless for a>0. Therefore S_t has a coefficient
representation

  S_t(X)=sum_(a,b) C_ab(t) F_a X F_b,

where C(t) is real symmetric positive semidefinite. The coefficient matrix is
unique: the left-right multiplication maps X -> F_a X F_b are a complex basis
of the space of superoperators. Consequently C(t) is analytic in t, irrespective
of any choice or regularity of individual Kraus operators. At t=0,
C(0)=m e_0 e_0^T. For any real vector v with v_0=0, positivity of C(t) for t>=0
and v^T C(0)v=0 imply v^T C'(0)v>=0. Thus the traceless block of C'(0) is a
real positive semidefinite matrix. Diagonalize it and obtain traceless Hermitian
operators B_1,...,B_k for which

  -H(X) = gamma X + WX + XW + sum_j B_j X B_j,

with gamma real and W Hermitian. Explicitly, gamma=C'_(00)(0)/m and
W=sum_(a>0) C'_(0a)(0) F_a/sqrt(m). Hence

  H(X) = (VX+XV)/2 - sum_j B_j X B_j,
  V = -gamma I - 2W.

There is no residual Hilbert-Schmidt skew term. This statement concerns H;
the original L may include a Hamiltonian correction after the nonunitary
similarity, and none is discarded. The constraint H(s)=0 is exactly

  ell_s(V) = sum_j B_j s B_j.

Since s>0, ell_s is invertible on all matrices: in an eigenbasis of s its
inverse multiplies the (i,j) entry by 2/(s_i+s_j). Thus

  H = sum_j H_(B_j),
  V_B = ell_s^(-1)(B s B),
  H_B(X) = (V_B X + X V_B)/2 - B X B.                 (1)

Conversely, every Hermitian B in (1) defines a legal finite KMS generator.
The drift semigroup X -> exp(-t V_B/2) X exp(-t V_B/2) is CP. Its Dyson
expansion with the CP noise X -> BXB gives a CP semigroup exp(-tH_B).
H_B(s)=0, and transport by the two sandwiches gives a unital CP semigroup.
H_B is Hilbert-Schmidt selfadjoint. Transport therefore gives KMS symmetry,
and L_*(sigma)=d H_B(d^(-1) sigma d^(-1)) d=d H_B(s)d=0. Finally H_B>=0:
the transported unital positive semigroup has operator norm and spectral radius
at most one; similarity preserves the spectrum; selfadjointness of H_B forces
all its eigenvalues to be nonnegative. This also verifies E_B>=0.

Adding a scalar to a noise operator has no effect:

  V_(B+cI) = V_B + 2cB + c^2 I,
  H_(B+cI) = H_B.                                    (2)

This follows from ell_s(2cB+c^2 I)=c(Bs+sB)+c^2s. It justifies using traceless
noise operators throughout the qubit calculation. The representation above
therefore covers ALL KMS-symmetric finite qubit generators, not just a selected
noise family or a modular-covariant subclass.

2. Exact quadratic gate in any dimension, including a quantum reference
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For any faithful rho, write

  R=sqrt(rho),
  X=d^(-1) rho d^(-1),
  Y=d (log rho-log sigma) d,
  C=(XY+YX)/2-2rho,
  Z=ell_s^(-1)(C).

Since L_* = Gamma^(1/2) H Gamma^(-1/2), cyclicity of trace gives

  J_B = Tr[H_B(X)Y],
  E_B = Tr[V_B rho] - Tr[B R B R].

The map ell_s is Hilbert-Schmidt selfadjoint, hence so is its inverse. Inserting
(1) yields the exact identity

  J_B - 2E_B
    = Tr[B s B Z] - Tr[B X B Y] + 2 Tr[B R B R].       (3)

Thus the universal all-dimensional endpoint is equivalent to nonnegativity of
this quadratic form for every faithful sigma,rho and Hermitian B. Necessity
uses the converse legality in section 1; sufficiency sums over the represented
noise operators. This is an exact reduction, not a proof of that endpoint.

For an untouched finite quantum reference with product stationary state
sigma_A tensor tau_R, H_total=H_A tensor id_R. Neither J nor E depends on tau_R:
the term involving I_A tensor log tau_R vanishes because L_A(I_A)=0. Therefore
one may choose tau_R=I_R/dim(R). Formula (3) remains valid with B=B_A tensor I_R
and V=V_(B_A) tensor I_R. Nonnegativity for these joint rho remains UNRESOLVED.

3. Exact Bloch formulas for one qubit noise operator
--------------------------------------------------
Unitary diagonalization of sigma and a diagonal unitary rotation of rho reduce
the qubit states to

  sigma = (I + tanh(alpha) sigma_z)/2,
  rho   = (I + r [sqrt(1-c^2) sigma_x + c sigma_z])/2,
  r=tanh(beta),

where alpha>=0, beta>=0 are finite and -1<=c<=1. Interchanging the sigma
eigenvectors makes alpha nonnegative. At beta=0 the angle is immaterial.
Use the abbreviations

  C_a=cosh(alpha),  S_a=sinh(alpha),  A=cosh(alpha/2),
  k_a=tanh(alpha/2), q=sech(beta), h=1-q,
  G=beta tanh(beta), K=G-h,
  k(x)=x sinh(x)-cosh(x)+1.

For beta>0, K=k(beta)/cosh(beta)>0, because k(0)=0 and k'(x)=x cosh(x)>0
for x>0. The function k is even and nonnegative on the whole real line.

For B=sigma_x+a sigma_z, direct insertion of (1) gives

  E_B=E_0+a E_1+a^2 E_2,
  J_B=J_0+a J_1+a^2 J_2,

  E_0=C_a-1+h c^2-S_a r c,
  E_1=2 sqrt(1-c^2)(k_a r-hc),
  E_2=h(1-c^2),

  J_0=G[(C_a-1)+(C_a+1)c^2]
      -2c[alpha r C_a+beta S_a]+2alpha S_a,
  J_1=2 sqrt(1-c^2)[(beta S_a+alpha r)/A-2A G c],
  J_2=2G(1-c^2).                                      (4)

For clarity, these are finite matrix identities, not fits. They can be derived
using log rho = beta [sqrt(1-c^2)sigma_x+c sigma_z]
                  -log(2cosh beta)I,
log sigma = alpha sigma_z-log(2cosh alpha)I,
and sqrt(rho)=uI+v[sqrt(1-c^2)sigma_x+c sigma_z], where
u=(sqrt((1+r)/2)+sqrt((1-r)/2))/2,
v=(sqrt((1+r)/2)-sqrt((1-r)/2))/2 and 4v^2=h. In the sigma eigenbasis,
(V_B)_(ij)=2(BsB)_(ij)/(s_i+s_j); this computes every entry of (4).

Define

  U=(beta S_a+alpha r)/A-2k_a r,
  W=2(h-A G),
  F_0=J_0-2E_0.

Then F(a,c)=J_B-2E_B is

  F(a,c)=F_0(c)+2a sqrt(1-c^2)(U+Wc)
                       +2a^2 K(1-c^2).              (5)

For -1<c<1, completing the square shows

  F(a,c)>=Q(c):=F_0(c)-(U+Wc)^2/(2K).                (6)

Q is a quadratic polynomial in c. Its quadratic coefficient is exactly

  (C_a+1)G-2h-W^2/(2K) = -2Gh(A-1)^2/K <=0.          (7)

Here C_a+1=2A^2. After multiplying (7) by K/2, the cancellation is
(A^2G-h)(G-h)-(h-AG)^2=-Gh(A-1)^2. Thus Q is concave, and
Q(c)>=min(Q(-1),Q(1)). The endpoint quantities in Q are optimized limiting
values; the minimizer a can diverge as c tends to an endpoint. They are not
replaced by the smaller calculation with a fixed at zero at that endpoint.

4. Exact endpoint factorization
-------------------------------
Fix eta in {-1,+1}, put b=eta beta and delta=b-alpha, and set

  T(x,y)=x sinh(y)+y sinh(x)-4 sinh(x/2)sinh(y/2).

At c=eta, the diagonal-state identities from (4) simplify to

  E_0=(cosh(delta)-1)/cosh(beta),
  J_0=2delta sinh(delta)/cosh(beta),
  F_0=2k(delta)/cosh(beta).                           (8)

The remaining linear coefficient obeys

  A cosh(b) eta(U+eta W) = -T(b,delta).               (9)

An explicit verification of (9), avoiding numerical factorization, is

  A cosh(b) eta(U+eta W)
   = b[S_a cosh(b)-(C_a+1)sinh(b)]
       +alpha sinh(b)-2sinh(alpha/2)sinh(b)
       +2A(cosh(b)-1)
   = -b sinh(delta)-delta sinh(b)
       +2[cosh(b-alpha/2)-cosh(alpha/2)]
   = -b sinh(delta)-delta sinh(b)
       +4sinh(b/2)sinh(delta/2).

Using K=k(beta)/cosh(beta) in (6), (8), and (9) gives

  Q(eta)
   = [4A^2 k(beta)k(delta)-T(b,delta)^2]
                         /[2A^2 cosh(beta)k(beta)].  (10)

Since b-delta=alpha and k(b)=k(beta), the scalar theorem in section 5 makes
the numerator of (10) nonnegative. Hence both endpoints and all of (6) are
nonnegative. Formula (5) therefore proves J_B>=2E_B for every real-plane noise
B=sigma_x+a sigma_z and every interior angle. Continuity gives c=+-1. Scaling
covers b_x sigma_x+b_z sigma_z for b_x !=0; b_x=0 follows by continuity, or
directly from F=2 b_z^2 K(1-c^2). The beta=0 case follows by continuity in rho;
directly it has F=2 b_x^2 k(alpha) in the real plane. No division by K is used
at beta=0.

5. A positive semidefinite hyperbolic kernel (independent Hardy proof)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
SCALAR THEOREM. On the whole real line the kernel

  mathcal K(x,y)=T(x,y)/[2cosh((x-y)/2)]                (11)

is positive semidefinite, with mathcal K(x,x)=k(x). In particular,

  T(x,y)^2 <= 4cosh((x-y)/2)^2 k(x)k(y).              (12)

Here positive semidefinite means every finite real-point Gram matrix is PSD.
This full-kernel statement is stronger than the two-point inequality needed
in (10).

Proof. In the open complex unit disk define analytic functions

  a(z)=(1-z^2)atanh(z)/z,  a(0)=1,
  b(z)=sqrt(1-z^2),       b(0)=1,

with the analytic branches obtained from the values at zero. Both are bounded
analytic functions and extend continuously to the closed disk: the logarithmic
singularities of atanh at +-1 are killed by 1-z^2, and the boundary zeroes of
b have continuous limits. For z=e^(i theta), away from the two boundary points
theta=0,pi,

  atanh(e^(i theta))
    = (1/2)log|cot(theta/2)|
                       +(i pi/4)sign(sin theta),
  (1-z^2)/z=-2i sin theta.

Consequently

  Re a(e^(i theta))=(pi/2)|sin theta|,
  |b(e^(i theta))|^2=2|sin theta|,
  2 Re a=(pi/2)|b|^2                                (13)

almost everywhere on the circle. Let H^2 be the scalar Hardy space with
reproducing kernel k_z(w)=1/(1-conj(z)w). Let M_b be multiplication by b, P_+
the orthogonal projection from L^2 of the circle onto H^2, and T_phi the
Toeplitz operator P_+ M_phi restricted to H^2. The kernel

  C(z,w)=[a(z)+conj(a(w))-b(z)conj(b(w))]
                                      /(1-z conj(w)) (14)

is the reproducing-kernel matrix of

  M_a+M_a^*-M_b M_b^*
    = T_(2Re a)-M_b M_b^*
    = (pi/2) M_b^* M_b-M_b M_b^*.                    (15)

For completeness, evaluation of M_a k_w gives the a(z) term, and
M_a^* k_w=conj(a(w))k_w gives its conjugate term. Similarly M_b M_b^* gives
the b(z)conj(b(w)) term. The Toeplitz identity in (15) uses (13) and
T_(|b|^2)=M_b^* M_b.

An analytic multiplier is hyponormal by an elementary projection inequality:
for every f in H^2,

  ||M_b^* f||=||P_+(conj(b)f)|| <= ||conj(b)f||
                                      =||b f||=||M_b f||.

Thus M_b^* M_b-M_b M_b^*>=0. Since pi/2>1, the last operator in (15) equals

  (pi/2-1)M_b^* M_b+(M_b^* M_b-M_b M_b^*)>=0.

Therefore C is a PSD kernel on the disk. Set t=tanh(x/2) and u=tanh(y/2),
which are real points in (-1,1). Elementary hyperbolic algebra gives

  mathcal K(x,y)=2sinh(x/2)sinh(y/2) C(t,u).            (16)

For example, 1-tu=cosh((x-y)/2)/(cosh(x/2)cosh(y/2)) and
a(t)=x/[2sinh(x/2)cosh(x/2)], with the removable limit at x=0.
Multiplication of a Gram matrix by a real diagonal matrix preserves PSD even
when the diagonal entries have different signs. Equations (14)-(16) therefore
prove (11) on all real points. Its diagonal is
[2x sinh(x)-4sinh(x/2)^2]/2=k(x). The 2-by-2 principal minor inequality gives
(12); zero arguments follow directly or by continuity. This finishes the
scalar theorem and the real-plane noise proof.

6. Imaginary perpendicular noise and summation over all generators
----------------------------------------------------------------
In the chosen basis sigma and rho are real. Complex conjugation preserves J_B
and E_B. As quadratic functions of Hermitian B, they therefore have no mixed
term between a real Hermitian noise and the purely imaginary sigma_y sector.
For B_y=sigma_y and B_x=sigma_x, their Lyapunov V's are identical since
B_y s B_y=B_x s B_x. The root-rho identities in section 3 and the off-diagonal
entries of X,Y give separately

  E_y-E_x=h(1-c^2),
  J_y-J_x=2G(1-c^2).

It follows that

  (J_y-2E_y)-(J_x-2E_x)=2K(1-c^2)>=0.                 (17)

The real-plane proof includes B_x, so it includes B_y as well. Every traceless
Hermitian qubit noise is b_x sigma_x+b_y sigma_y+b_z sigma_z. Conjugation
separates its real-plane and perpendicular terms, and the two nonnegative
quadratic forms prove J_B-2E_B>=0 for that entire noise space. Identity shifts
cancel by (2). The representation (1) expresses every allowed qubit generator
as a sum of these elementary legal noises. Linearity of J,E in H sums the
inequalities and proves the stated global qubit theorem.

7. Classical-reference consequence and remaining exact gates
------------------------------------------------------------
If rho_AR=sum_j p_j rho_j tensor |j><j| is block diagonal in a reference basis,
then sqrt(rho_AR) has blocks sqrt(p_j)sqrt(rho_j), while H_total=H_A tensor id.
Therefore E_total=sum_j p_j E_A(rho_j). The log p_j terms in J vanish by
trace preservation; the log tau_R term vanishes as explained in section 2;
hence J_total=sum_j p_j J_A(rho_j). The qubit theorem proves J_total>=2E_total
for every such classical reference. It imposes no bound on the reference
alphabet size.

For a general quantum reference, block reduction is unavailable. Formula (3)
with B_A tensor I_R is the exact remaining gate. For dimensions >=3, (3) with
arbitrary Hermitian noise B is the exact remaining gate. The scalar kernel
theorem is potentially useful there but no valid dimension lift is asserted.
The conclusions do not establish universal complete weak regularity.
