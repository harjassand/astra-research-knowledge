If each key appears at most twice per path, all paths are either already balanced or belong to a small family of literal signatures, then independent rare flips give many occurrences of both colours on every path.

The source's norm-band key bound sharpens from four to **two**, using the exact half-open upper endpoint. Selecting one representative per key makes the structured-family lower-tail argument independent despite shared keys. At the unchanged family160 parameters, this gives the candidate strengthening

`N >= k^(10^-5 k)`, with each colour occurring at least `k^(19/20)/4` times on every nonzero-step cyclic k-progression.

For every fixed `0<epsilon<1/10`, adjusting only the fixed size coefficient and flip probability gives

`N >= k^((epsilon/1600) k)`, with each colour occurring at least `k^(1-epsilon)/4` times.

These colourings withstand fewer than that many edits on each progression. The universal sparse-repair lemma and sharpened line-band lemma are proved in the note. The superexponential application depends on the source's Theorem5.5 dichotomy and Lemma6.2 literal-key entropy, which were internally reconstructed but need a separate adversarial check before programme-level adoption. No explicit useful threshold or efficient colouring-acquisition algorithm is supplied. At the original probability, the simple condition `p<=2 gamma` already needs `k>=50^20`.

The closest verified prior mechanism is Fox–Hunter's robust hitting sets and robust colourings in [arXiv:2606.02541v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02541v1), submitted 1 June 2026; robust hitting is not a new idea. Their Lemmas2.8–2.9 concern exponential-size settings. The specific two-colour/superexponential/near-linear-resistance tradeoff was not stated in the read pinned manuscript; global novelty is unverified. ## Second reusable deduction

[NONLINEAR_ROUGH_STEPS.md](</Users/harjas/Documents/ChatGPT/Literature Digestion/notes/frontier_combin1/cycle19/NONLINEAR_ROUGH_STEPS.md>) proves an arithmetic generalization of family164 Lemma6.2: replace the linear divisor `t0+mz` by an integer polynomial f(z) with smooth leading coefficient and rough comparable sampled values. Interpolation gives a rational approximate numerator; a superpolynomially small error forces its bounded-denominator polynomial remainder to vanish exactly. The quotient is integer-valued at the sampled indices by coprimality.

Combined with the corrected quantitative Leibman input and the source's dimension induction, this gives a conditional comparison of unrestricted nilsequence averages with averages on most nonlinear steps `Q(t)` under exact fixed-degree and smooth-leading hypotheses. A concrete case is `Q(t)=t^2+1`, sampled in a CRT class avoiding all small prime roots. The note gives the complete induction interface and both exact obstructions:

- Dropping smooth leading coefficient permits `Q(t)=p_w t`, `p_w>w`, and the circle phase `P_t(x)=x/p_w`; every fine progression is biased although the unrestricted average tends to zero.
- Dropping joint polynomial dependence permits `P_t(x)=x/t`; again every step is biased.

This nonlinear interface has no quantitative rate, does not permit model dimension/degree/Lipschitz norm to grow for free, and does not establish the full finite sums/products theorem. The standalone division lemma survives even if a future nonabelian audit rejects the extension's analytic induction.
