# An exact resource boundary between sequent DAGs and occurrence trees

Status: independently proved at the stated interface; finite exact-kernel evidence
is supporting evidence. Historical priority and the intended convention of the
recent paper's word "tree-like" remain unresolved. No claim that its main
separation theorem is false.

## Question and primary interface

Can ordinary tree proofs line-simulate DAG proofs in every structural logic when
"lines" count formula occurrences, with no conclusion-size term?

The primary starting point is Amirhossein Akbar Tabatabai,
[arXiv:2609.00422v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00422v1), submitted
31 August 2026 21:59:35 UTC. Its pp5–6 define proof size as symbols and sequent
lines as the number of formulas appearing in the proof. Theorem8, p10, asserts
line- and size-equivalence of structural sequent systems, their tree versions,
and Frege. Table1, p6, is the exact calculus used below. The printed rules and
statements were visually checked. These are authors' claims, not ours.

Work below concerns **base FL_e in L_u={0,1,*,→,∧,∨}**, including cut and implicit
exchange by multiset equality. No weakening, contraction, exponentials, additive
top/bottom, extension axioms, or proof assumptions are admitted. Identity may
contain any compound formula; arbitrary intermediate formulas and cuts are
allowed. An **occurrence tree** means each premise occurrence has its own proof
subtree: an earlier endpoint cannot fill two premise slots without two copies.

## Universal potential induction

Define integer weights by

    w(p)=w(0)=0, w(1)=1,
    w(A*B)=w(A)+w(B),       w(A→B)=w(B)−w(A),
    w(A∧B)=max(w(A),w(B)), w(A∨B)=min(w(A),w(B)).

For a sequent S=(Γ⇒Δ), put

    d(S)=w(Δ)−Σ_{F in Γ}w(F),

where empty Δ has weight0 and every antecedent multiplicity is retained. Let
u(P) be the number of primitive ⇒1 leaves of an occurrence-tree proof P.
Then **d(end(P))≤u(P)**. This follows by induction over the actual tree,
with all Table1 rules covered:

This is a proof-cost potential, not an algebraic truth model. Its w(1)=1 charges
the primitive resource-producing unit axiom; it is not an interpretation that
treats the monoidal unit as an additive neutral element.

| Exact rule | Relation between conclusion deficit d and premise deficits |
|---|---|
| identity A⇒A | d=0; no unit leaf |
| primitive ⇒1 | d=1; one unit leaf |
| primitive 0⇒empty | d=0; no unit leaf |
| L1 | d=d_p−1 |
| R0, L*, R→ | d=d_p |
| L∧i, R∨i | d≤d_p |
| R∧, L∨ | d=max(d_1,d_2) |
| R*, L→, cut | d=d_1+d_2 |

For R∧ and L∨, max(d1,d2)≤u1+u2 because each ui is nonnegative. For the last
three rules, use u(P)=u1+u2. This argument does not assume cut elimination,
positivity of formula weights, subformula property, or bounded formula size.

## Sharp family and exact minima

Let U0=1 and U(n+1)=Un*Un. The closed DAG proof is

    ⇒1;  ⇒1*1;  ⇒(1*1)*(1*1); ...; ⇒Un,

where every R* fills both slots from the same earlier closed endpoint. It has
n+1 rows and n+1 formula occurrences; this is legal DAG proof reuse, not a
formula contraction rule. The common syntax DAG also has n+1 constructor nodes.

The potential gives d(⇒Un)=2^n, so every occurrence-tree proof contains at least
2^n primitive unit leaves. All FL_e rules have at most two premises. Therefore
such a proof has at least 2^(n+1)−1 nodes. Every node carries at least one formula:
the only zero-formula sequent is empty⇒empty, excluded by Boolean soundness of
all Table1 rules (interpret *=∧,1=true,0=false). Hence

    minimum occurrence-tree formula occurrences for ⇒Un = 2^(n+1)−1.

The pure unit/R* tree attains this number.

For the DAG lower bound, every axiom has deficit at most1. Each unary rule
cannot raise it; every binary rule either adds two deficits or takes their
maximum. Consequently row i (one-based) has deficit at most 2^(i−1), by
induction using the largest nonnegative earlier deficit. A proof of ⇒Un needs
at least n+1 rows, each charged at least one formula. The displayed DAG attains:

    minimum DAG formula occurrences for ⇒Un = n+1.

Thus there is an exact exponential gap under this metric, even with unrestricted
cuts and unrestricted compound identity instances in both systems.

## The important convention dichotomy

The recent paper's p5 wording restricts each sequent to being a premise of at
most one **rule**. If this means at most one *distinct consuming inference*, but
permits two slots of that inference to cite the same row, our n+1 sequence
satisfies the displayed definition. Such a graph is not an occurrence tree:
its unfolding has 2^n unit leaves. Under that weaker graph convention the family
does **not** refute its Theorem8. Under the usual premise-occurrence convention,
the sharp theorem above refutes the literal line-equivalence assertion.

The paper must therefore be read with this explicit dichotomy. Its later
recursive proof does not get a small occurrence tree from the weaker graph
definition; it can instead be repaired directly on DAGs. That constructive
repair is recorded separately in DAG_FDP_REPAIR_AUDIT.md.

No contradiction to **symbol-size equivalence** follows. Counting formula
constructor occurrences (before parentheses), the displayed DAG costs
2^(n+2)−n−3, while its pure unit tree costs n*2^(n+1)+1. The conclusion already
has 2^(n+1)−1 constructors. This family supports a polynomial symbol-size
relationship and disproves only a bound polynomial in formula-occurrence lines
alone for actual trees. A goal-size term can remove this obstruction.

## Evidence, controls, and use

work/logic_gate14/cycle18/fl_e_resource_boundary.py reconstructs exact Table1
rules with antecedent Counters, arbitrary formula DAGs, both premise slots,
chronology, and the two separate tree predicates. It imports no producer code.
TREE_RESOURCE_CHECKS.json checks five bounded family members, all16 primitive
rule cases, exact charged counts, and rejects a DAG mislabeled as an occurrence
tree, a dropped tensor premise, and an unlicensed left resource. Finite rule
instances are not represented as closed theorems. The universal proof is the
induction above.

This diagnoses when a line-sensitive argument may serialize DAGs for free. It
also supplies a primitive regression/control family for future proof-complexity
compilers: proof sharing and formula sharing are distinct, and a large final
formula can make a correct size simulation fail as a line simulation.

Raheleh Jalali arXiv:2006.09705v2, Definition3.11 p21, uses the more occurrence-
sensitive phrasing that each step is used at most once as a hypothesis. Its
Theorem6.2 pp35–36 is a sequent-to-Frege interpretation; it is not the numbered
published Theorem6.3 cited in the recent paper. The published version must be
acquired before attributing the disputed tree claim to that older source.
Its unavailable published proof is not inferred from the preprint numbering.
