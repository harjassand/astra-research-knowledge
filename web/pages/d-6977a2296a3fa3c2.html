SEPARATE AUDIT OF OPTIONAL HIGH-FREQUENCY SPLITTING

Status: analytic audit and explicit distributional reconstruction of the
optional frozen module. It is NOT used by the main equal-radius bootstrap
or its sigma-finite/rectifiable consequence. The whole-Z stopping-cover

assumed. The source comparison is separately recorded.

1. EXACT LOCAL SCOPE AND COMPACTNESS

The affine (n-2)-plane alternative is stated for n>=2. For n=1, the main
PDE theorem already gives at most one good center, and no negative-
dimensional plane convention is needed. Take sufficiently small r, e.g.
r<1/64, with original centers in Z subset B1 and the exact tuple on B4.
This supplies every enlarged ball and frequency radius used below.
The global good-center premise supplies .5<=P<=N at all physical radii
below 2. The frozen phrase 'common interior room' must include this
fixed enlarged-ball room, not just the displayed radius 3r.

Suppose the optional alternative fails along eta_j down to zero. If
E_j<=.5+gamma, its first branch holds immediately by P>=.5. Otherwise
E_j>.5+gamma. The nonempty high set forces E_j<=N+eta_j. Extract
E_j->E>=.5+gamma, so k=2E>1. Failure of the affine-plane branch permits
n high centers in B_r(x) with n-1 quantitatively independent differences:
choose greedily outside the rho r neighborhood of each existing span,
extending lower-dimensional spans to an affine (n-2)-plane as needed.
Their scaled diameter is <=2 and the lower simplex thickness survives
subsequence extraction. Translate the first to 0, dilate by r, and use
a SINGLE normalization H(0,.5)=1 for the whole tuple.

The established bounded-frequency local mass lemma applies after this
normalization on every fixed enlarged ball needed here. To move its
normalizing radius from .5 to 1, use the same estimate
  dot P >= q_rad/2 -(1+2N)^2/2,
where q_rad=R_rad/H and n_rad=D/H, n_rad^2<=q_rad. On each fixed
positive radius interval it bounds integral q_rad; Cauchy bounds
integral n_rad and hence the ratio of the H values. No inverse scale
depending on eta_j is needed for this fixed-radius compactness.

At each selected moving center, H(z,3) is bounded below by the normalized
positive height in B_{.5}(0): |z|<=2 leaves a strictly positive kernel
there at radius 3. The same fixed-interval ratio estimate bounds H(z,s)
away from zero for EACH fixed s>0. Thus weak tuple limits preserve the
frequency quotients at fixed radii; small denominators are not assumed
away without proof. Bounded local mass and the smooth compactly supported
kernels yield convergence under the moving center translations. The
limit retains PSD, all distributional PDE, 2mu<=d, and |mu|<=C d.

For every fixed s in (0,3), high-set membership and monotonicity give
  E_j-eta_j <= P(z_i^j,s) <= P(z_i^j,3) <= E_j
eventually. Thus every selected limit center has constant P=E on (0,3).

2. FULL NULL IDENTITIES AND THE NORMAL REDUCTION

The established defect bound Q(2P)/H<=dot P forces Q(k)=0 at every
radius in (0,3). PSD then annihilates the FULL vector (-k,y-z_i) on
each such interior ball. Exhausting the radii gives
  M(y)(-k,y-z_i)=0 on B3(z_i),
where M=[nu f^t;f beta]. All selected centers have |z_i|<=2, so these
balls share B1(0). Subtracting the full identities on that common ball
annihilates (0,z_i-z_1). Their n-1 independent differences span an affine
plane through z_1=0. After an orthogonal change of coordinates, its normal
is e_n. Thus f=f_n e_n and beta=d e_n tensor e_n as MEASURES; no beta
block is substituted by a square or a covariance approximation.

Writing t=y_n, the first-center identity gives BOTH equations
  t f_n = k nu,          t d = k f_n.                         (S.1)
The second of these is an important part of the full PSD null relation.

3. SHORT DISTRIBUTIONAL CONTRADICTION

On the common ball, grad nu=2f and div f=d give
  partial_t nu=2f_n,     partial_t f_n=d.
Differentiate the first equation in (S.1), in distributions:
  f_n+t d = k partial_t nu=2k f_n.
Substitute the second equation in (S.1):
  (k+1) f_n=2k f_n, hence (k-1)f_n=0.
Since k>1, f_n=0 and then k nu=t f_n=0. This contradicts the height
normalization in B_{.5}(0). Products by t and their distributional product
rule are legitimate for Radon measures; division by t is never used.
This shorter argument also rules out every possible measure on t=0.
The stress equation and |mu| domination are not needed for this final
contradiction, although they are retained in the original compact class.

4. INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE FROZEN PROFILE ARGUMENT

Tangential grad nu=0 implies, locally, Lebesgue constancy in the tangent
variables. The remaining scalar Radon measure alpha(dt) satisfies
  t partial_t alpha = 2k alpha.
Away from 0, the first-order distributional ODE gives the smooth densities
c_+ t^{2k} for t>0 and c_- |t|^{2k} for t<0, with c_+,c_->=0.
A residual Radon measure at t=0 can only be an atom A delta_0. The
distribution identity t delta'_0=-delta_0 gives
  -A delta_0=2k A delta_0,
so A=0. Derivatives of the displayed profiles create no plane delta
because 2k>2. Thus, on each connected common ball,
  nu=c_+/- |t|^{2k} dy,
  f_n=k c_+/- sign(t)|t|^{2k-1} dy,
  d=k(2k-1)c_+/- |t|^{2k-2} dy.                             (S.2)
At least one coefficient is positive by H(0,.5)=1. Local descriptions
agree on overlaps, so coefficients are constant on each connected half.

Rank-one beta and the tangent-independent density d imply
  div beta=grad d.
The stress equation is therefore grad(mu-d)=0. A distribution with zero
gradient on a connected ball is a CONSTANT LEBESGUE DENSITY, not an
arbitrary plane atom and not an automatically zero constant. Write
  mu=d+c dy.
Since k>1, d tends to zero at the interior plane t=0. Domination gives
|c|<= (C+1)d almost everywhere near it, hence c=0. Only then may one
conclude mu=d; 2mu<=d forces d=0 and contradicts (S.2) and the positive
height. This proves the frozen longer contradiction with its constant
correctly charged. For k=1, d need not tend to zero, so this step cannot
be reused; a nonzero additive constant in mu-d is real and admissible.
The sharper short proof likewise does not give a contradiction at k=1.
