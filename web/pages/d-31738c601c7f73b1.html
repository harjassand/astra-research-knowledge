QUESTION

Does the alternative free-electron route based on local charge measurements
already give a compiler into the target's static opposite-spin pair matrix F
followed by one terminal hard one-particle-per-site projection? The answer
from the checked material is no: the cited universality result supplies a
sequential circuit with interleaved charge measurements and spin operations,
not the required static-F compiler. A new exact check pins down two specific
obstacles in the proposed direct conversion. It does not rule out a larger
teleportation or ancilla construction.

PRIMARY-SOURCE RECONSTRUCTION

Beenakker, DiVincenzo, Emary, and Kindermann, arXiv:quant-ph/0401066v1,
define a spatial-mode charge measurement Q_i with outcomes 0,1,2 and its
charge-one projector P_i. Their parity detector outcome p=1 is precisely
charge one in each of the two arms; outcome p=0 is charge 0 or 2. Figure 2
uses this detector between two polarizing beam splitters. Appendix A gives
the two-encoder CNOT branch. In the paper's notation, the first selected
parity p1 maps the initialized ancilla to a=x+p1+1, and the second selected
parity p2 plus ancilla result z maps

  |x>|y> -> (-1)^((p2+1)(x+z+p1+1)) |x>|x+y+z+p1+1>.

The paper suppresses normalization in the displayed equations. Restoring it,
the fixed branch p1=p2=1,z=0 is exactly CNOT with amplitude 1/(2 sqrt(2))
on every computational basis input, hence branch probability 1/8 independent
of the input. The amplitude factors are 1/sqrt(2) from selecting one term
of the initialized ancilla |+>, and 1/2 from the normalized H--charge-one--H
branch. This is a useful positive gate-level fact: the selected charge
outcomes are both q=1, and no Pauli correction is needed in this branch.

EXACT FOCK-SPACE DEFERRAL CHECK

Modes are ordered (a_up,a_down,b_up,b_down). Let U be the polarizing
beam-splitter permutation that swaps a_down and b_down, and let
Q=P1(a)P1(b) project onto one electron in each arm. Applying U, Q, then U
again (U is an involution) to the four logical states with one electron per
arm gives the exact branch matrix

                 |00> |01> |10> |11>
  U Q U :        |00>   0    0   |11>.

Here 0/1 denote up/down. In other words this named p=1 charge branch is the
same-spin parity projector. By contrast, a terminal Q after the inverse
beam splitter is the identity on the entire one-electron-per-arm input
space. For example, |up_a,down_b> becomes a double occupation in arm a and
an empty arm b at the detector, so the actual intermediate p=1 branch kills
it; applying the inverse splitter first restores one particle to each arm,
and a terminal-only Q then accepts it. The two maps are exactly unequal.

This checks the candidate route in its own detector basis, rather than
assuming that all charge measurements are ordinary mode-resolved outcomes.
It is an exact obstruction to the simplest deferral (PBS -> charge-one
projection -> inverse PBS, with only a final projection after the inverse).
The circuit explicitly reuses the first encoder's ancilla in the second
encoder, so this is a live issue for the stated CNOT branch. It is not a
no-go for replacing that reuse by fresh ancillas, teleportation, a changed
encoding, or a larger static gadget.

EXACT PAIR-FAMILY CLOSURE CHECK

The target Gaussian source has only opposite-spin pair terms
sum_ij F_ij a_i^dagger b_j^dagger. A two-site singlet is generated by the
symmetric matrix F=[[0,1],[1,0]], whose k=1 term in up-then-down order is

  u1^dagger d2^dagger + u2^dagger d1^dagger
  = u1^dagger d2^dagger - d1^dagger u2^dagger

in site-ordered notation. An exact rational site-local spin rotation

  u1^dagger -> (3/5)u1^dagger + (4/5)d1^dagger,
  d1^dagger -> -(4/5)u1^dagger + (3/5)d1^dagger

maps this state, in canonical up-then-down mode order, to

  (4/5)u1^dagger u2^dagger
  + (3/5)u1^dagger d2^dagger
  + (3/5)u2^dagger d1^dagger
  + (4/5)d1^dagger d2^dagger.

The nonzero up-up and down-down components lie outside the opposite-spin
pair-source family. Thus local spin rotations used in the source circuit
cannot in general be absorbed by simply updating F. A common global spin
rotation of a singlet does preserve it; this example only refutes closure
under the local operations needed for an unconstrained circuit encoding.
It does not preclude a symmetry-preserving code or gadget compilation.

BOUNDARIES, COST, AND NEXT GATE

No explicit map from an arbitrary circuit to a rational F' and terminal
hard-site norm/prefix is established. In particular, the checked Beenakker
route has sequential P1 outcomes, uses mode transformations after its first
measurement, and contains spin rotations; the target F family alone does not
absorb those steps by the simple substitutions tested here. A serious next
gate is an explicit teleportation/ancilla gadget whose intermediate
charge-one outcomes are terminal constraints and whose source/output remain
in the opposite-spin F interface, or a proof that a precisely named gadget
cannot exist under stated locality and mode-count restrictions. Any reduction
must keep the branch success probability, output postselection, bit lengths,
and the target's conditional-vs-unnormalized norm interface explicit.

The check is tiny and exact; only a four-mode Fock sector and a two-site
pair example were evaluated. No solver, randomized experiment, or large
compute was run. Source: Beenakker et al., “Charge detection enables
free-electron quantum computation,” https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0401066v1.
Their reported universality is a circuit result with intermediate charge
measurements and feed-forward/fixed-branch corrections. No PP-hardness or
AP-hardness theorem for the static hard-projected BCS target is inferred.
