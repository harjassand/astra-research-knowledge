## Exact counterexample: fixed small local coupling does not give global product closeness

Consider N independent spin/oscillator pairs, each with

\[
H_q=\omega a_q^*a_q-gX_q(a_q+a_q^*),\qquad \omega>0,
\]

and no spin penalty. This is a finite-mode Gaussian model, not an actual encoded-memory counterexample. Let (\alpha=g/\omega>0\), (v=\coth(\beta\omega/2)\). Completing the square shows that the interacting Gibbs state has equal X-spin blocks whose oscillator thermal states are displaced by (\pm\alpha\). In the uncoupled product resource, the spin is uniformly mixed and the oscillator is undisplaced thermal.

Measure the commuting spin X and oscillator coordinate (\phi=a+a^*\), and multiply their outcomes. The result (Y=X\phi\) is Gaussian (N(2\alpha,v)\) under the interacting state, and (N(0,v)\) under the product state. Across the independent pairs, the sample average has variance (v/N\). The test “average Y at least (\alpha\)” has an error at most (\exp[-N\alpha^2/(2v)]\) in either resource, by the elementary Gaussian tail bound. Operational trace distance therefore satisfies

\[
\frac12\|\tau_{\rm int}^{\otimes N}-\tau_{\rm prod}^{\otimes N}\|_1
 \ge 1-2\exp[-N\alpha^2/(2v)]\longrightarrow1.
\]

Both (\chi=2/\omega\) and (v\) are fixed finite constants. The intensive g may be arbitrarily small but fixed. Thus those local bounds and small g alone cannot supply an N-uniform global trace-distance replacement of the interacting resource by a product initialization. No assertion about the actual code's logical error follows from this example. It is a decisive obstruction to a generic initialization substitution, and it does not contradict a possible more local dynamical comparison under additional hypotheses.
