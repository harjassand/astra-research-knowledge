# A local discrete trace-sandwich consequence

Status: independently derived finite-dimensional lemma, prompted by the all-q Lipschitz-sandwich lemma in the pinned positive-temperature hard-sphere manuscript, `build/sections/slab.tex`, `lem:sandwich`. This is a local imaginary-time/filter comparison. It does not acquire a product-initialized memory theorem, a Gibbs sampler, or the original physical bath. It is not a novelty claim.

## 1. Exact interface and bound

Take an arbitrary Hermitian n×n matrix H in a specified orthonormal basis, and A=diag(a_z), with a_z real. Define a nonnegative diagonal matrix

    W_A(z)=Σ_{z'≠z}|H_{zz'}|[cosh(a_z−a_{z'})−1].           (S1)

For every δ>0 and every real q>0,

    Tr[(e^A e^(−δH) e^A)^q]
       ≤Tr exp{−qδ[H−2A/δ−W_A]}.                          (S2)

No commutation or stoquasticity is assumed. Matrix sparsity/locality becomes relevant only when bounding W_A. If H itself is supplied as a dense matrix, construction of W_A costs O(n²) arithmetic and transcendental evaluations, plus their charged precision; n is exponentially large for a generic many-spin system. A sparse local representation may construct W_A symbolically, but does not evaluate the trace efficiently.

## 2. Complete proof

For |u|≤1, the Hermitian part of e^{uA}He^{−uA} has entry H_{zz'} cosh[u(a_z−a_{z'})]. Its difference from H has zero diagonal. The absolute off-diagonal quadratic form is at most

    Σ_{z,z'} |H_{zz'}|[cosh(a_z−a_{z'})−1]|f_z||f_{z'}|
       ≤Σ_z W_A(z)|f_z|²,                                 (S3)

because coefficients are symmetric in z,z', cosh(uΔ)−1≤cosh(Δ)−1, and 2|f_z||f_{z'}|≤|f_z|²+|f_{z'}|². Hence the Hermitian part is at least H−W_A in quadratic-form order.

Let u(s)=−1+2s/δ and U(s)=e^{u(s)A}e^{−sH}e^A, 0≤s≤δ. U(0)=I and U(δ)=X:=e^Ae^{−δH}e^A. Differentiation gives

    U'=[2A/δ−e^{uA}He^{−uA}]U.

Thus, with K=H−2A/δ−W_A,

    ½ d||U(s)f||²/ds ≤−〈U(s)f,K U(s)f〉.                (S4)

Apply the same differential inequality to k tensor copies and restrict to their alternating subspace. If E_1≤…≤E_n are the eigenvalues of K, the copy-sum of K on that subspace has lowest eigenvalue Σ_{i≤k}E_i. Gronwall yields

    ||∧^k X||≤exp[−δΣ_{i≤k}E_i],   k=1,…,n.              (S5)

X is positive definite. If x_1≥…≥x_n are its eigenvalues, (S5) means Σ_{i≤k}log x_i≤−δΣ_{i≤k}E_i. For every h∈R, the sum of positive parts of log x_i−h is the largest shifted partial sum, including k=0. Therefore

    Σ_i(log x_i−h)_+≤Σ_i(−δE_i−h)_+.

For q>0 use the exact scalar identity e^{qy}=∫_R q²e^{qh}(y−h)_+dh. Integrating the last inequality proves (S2), including noninteger q. The proof never replaces exponential form order by operator monotonicity.

## 3. Concrete CSS specialization

Suppose the old CSS Hamiltonian is a weighted sum of positive check penalties, each original check has support at most z_c, and each qubit is incident to at most z_c original Z checks. Add finite-support Pauli terms V=Σ_F v_F P_F. In the computational Z basis, each P_F flips a subset F_flip and multiplies by a unit-modulus phase; terms that do not flip are diagonal. Assume each perturbation term has weight at most w and per-site coefficient intensity

    J:=sup_b Σ_{F:b∈supp P_F}|v_F| <∞.

Choose a set T of original Z checks and put N_T(z)=Σ_{a∈T}(1−C_a(z))/2 and A=λN_T, λ real. All original X checks commute with N_T, so their matrix entries connect equal a_z and contribute **zero** to W_A. The diagonal original penalties likewise contribute zero. A perturbation can change N_T only if its flip set meets Q_T:=∪_{a∈T}supp C_a. It changes at most z_c w checks. Using the triangle bound for aggregate matrix entries,

    0≤W_A≤w_T I,
    w_T=|Q_T|J[cosh(z_c w|λ|)−1]
        ≤z_c|T|J[cosh(z_c w|λ|)−1].                      (S6)

For the actual inherited recursive-code incidence/support bound z_c=7, this is 7|T|J[cosh(7w|λ|)−1]. Positive single-site X fields have w=1 and J=max ε_b. The finite trace lemma also permits Y or other Pauli perturbations with the displayed coefficient-intensity accounting; the **positive path/affinity** arguments from the source do not thereby transfer to them.

Writing β=qδ, (S2) gives the local modified-Hamiltonian comparison

    Tr X^q/Z_β(H)
      ≤Z_β(H−2λN_T/δ−W_A)/Z_β(H)
      ≤e^{βw_T} Z_β(H−2λN_T/δ)/Z_β(H).                 (S7)

The last inequality is scalar eigenvalue min–max, not exponential operator order. The required field/free-energy comparison concerns only checks in T and nearby perturbation supports; an extensive ||V|| bound has not been inserted.

## 4. Competent controls and unresolved step

The exact trace on the right remains an acquired/supplied interface. Replacing it by the crude operator-norm bound loses the scientific benefit: it gives e^{2q|λ||T|+βw_T}, which need not improve on the elementary congruence norm bound e^{2q||A||}. In particular, for A≤0 the contraction e^A already implies eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue domination of X by e^{−δH}, hence Tr X^q≤Z_β(H); the scalar e^{βw_T} estimate alone is weaker. No improvement is claimed for that case.

The useful content is (S7) with its **retained local field partition function**, for positive local marks/filters where a uniform local free-energy response is separately available. This is precisely how the source's colored path-marker construction uses its analogous continuum bound. To change the old product-bath bottleneck one would still need a justified local comparison for the actual time-evolved initialized resource, or a charged way to obtain the necessary correlated resource. An imaginary-time trace estimate by itself supplies neither.

For a finite-mode microscopic spin–boson Hamiltonian the coefficients X_b φ_b are unbounded and signed in oscillator coordinates. The finite-spin bounded-intensity specialization is not an automatic extension to that model. Nor does it control exact Bohr-frequency pairing or a Davies generator.

Failure of the common positive-measure or time-evolved outside-marginal interface would block that transfer even though (S2) remains valid.
