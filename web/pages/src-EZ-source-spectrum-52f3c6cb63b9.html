# Curvature sources, operator iteration, and sharp spectral complexity

**Astra Primitive Genesis — research record, 13 October 2026**

**Status.** This record contains explicit theorem statements, proofs, constructions, sharpness examples, and executable finite diagnostics. Historical novelty and independent correctness review are unresolved. The operator-curvature comparison used below is prior research, not a new discovery of this run. No major open problem, general many-body compression theorem, or civilisation-changing capability is claimed to have been established.

## 0. What is being preserved

The original goal was to find a mathematical operation that permits consequential spectral or computational conclusions without acquiring an exponentially large representation first. The preserved mechanism separates three quantities that cannot be conflated:

1. the norm of a source;
2. the Hilbert-space rank or singular-value summability of a source;
3. the dimension of the spaces generated by repeatedly applying a supplied operator algebra to that source.

The results are a constructive positive-operator recursion with a uniform error bound; an exact source-rank classification; an optimal Schatten-class transfer law; a finite-precision obstruction; and an explicit replacement of an inadequate single-operator representation by a commuting row representation. The last construction acquires its representation from formulas, not from an assumed spectral oracle. It also retains the large multiplicities rather than making them disappear.

These results do **not** acquire suitable curvature certificates for arbitrary mathematical-physics or complexity-theory targets. Treating such acquisition as automatic would relocate the original obstruction. In particular, the product-degree example is a structural test, not an algorithmic speedup: its diagonal formula is simpler than the iterative method.

## 1. Setup, provenance, and contract

All Hilbert spaces are separable and complex. All operators appearing before a compactness conclusion are bounded. The symbol `<=` in operator formulas means the Loewner order. For `a>0`, set

\[
 g_a(t)=\frac{t}{1+at},\qquad t\ge0,
 \qquad
 f_a(t)=\frac{t}{1-at},\qquad 0\le t<a^{-1}.
\]

These functions are applied to self-adjoint operators by continuous functional calculus. The identity

\[
g_a(X)=a^{-1}\big[I-(I+aX)^{-1}\big]
\]

makes its order monotonicity immediate.

The starting single-operator comparison is

\[
 A=T^*T\preceq g_a(TT^*+BB^*)=g_a(C+P),
 \quad C=TT^*,\quad P=BB^* . \tag{1.1}
\]

A comparison of exactly this form occurs in Balasubramanian–Kasiviswanathan, arXiv:2610.07728v2, Section 7, Lemma 7.1 [1]. Their source-response/spectral-radius argument is **not** claimed as a contribution here. Their claimed KLS theorem is not a premise of any proof in this note and was not independently validated in this run.

The candidate contribution is the collection of statements below, with their exact scope. It is not a claim that the component ideas of operator monotonicity, spectral interlacing, trace concavity, fixed-point iteration, weighted shifts, or displacement structure are new. In particular, classical matrix subadditivity [2] and existing displacement-rank/singular-value analysis [3] are relevant prior art. A limited search is not historical-priority clearance.

**Input/output contract for the constructive part.** Given `a`, finitely many bounded operators `V_i` satisfying `sum_i V_i V_i^* <= I`, and a bounded source `B`, construct the unique positive solution of a specified operator recursion, with norm error at most `epsilon`. The theorem supplies an iteration count and a representation-space bound. It does not promise inexpensive application of arbitrary input operators, inexpensive word-space Gram matrices, or a small representation when the word space grows rapidly.

## 2. Constructive positive recursion

Let

\[
 \Phi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_iXV_i^*,\qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^{d}V_iV_i^*\preceq I,
 \qquad P=BB^*.
\]

Define

\[
 \mathcal F(X)=g_a(\Phi(X)+P),\qquad X\succeq0. \tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — explicit acquisition and norm convergence

There is a unique bounded positive solution

\[
 X_* = \mathcal F(X_*).
\]

Starting from `X_0=0`, the iterates `X_{k+1}=F(X_k)` increase to `X_*` in operator norm and satisfy

\[
 \boxed{\quad 0\preceq X_*-X_k\preceq\frac{1}{a(k+1)}I.\quad} \tag{2.2}
\]

More precisely, for positive `X,Y`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|\mathcal F(X)-\mathcal F(Y)\|
 \le g_a(\|X-Y\|).
 \quad} \tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently, for any positive initial value `Y_0`,

\[
 \|Y_k-X_*\|
 \le\frac{\|Y_0-X_*\|}{1+ak\|Y_0-X_*\|}.
\]

If a bounded positive target `A` is only a subsolution,

\[
 A\preceq\mathcal F(A),
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{\quad A\preceq X_*\preceq X_k+\frac{1}{a(k+1)}I.\quad} \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Put `delta=||X-Y||`. Positivity and subunitality of `Phi` give

\[
 \Phi(X)+P\preceq\Phi(Y)+P+\delta I.
\]

For every positive `Z`, functional calculus gives

\[
 g_a(Z+\delta I)-g_a(Z)
 =\delta(I+aZ)^{-1}(I+aZ+a\delta I)^{-1}
 \preceq\frac{\delta}{1+a\delta}I.
\]

Apply monotonicity, then exchange `X` and `Y`, proving (2.3). The scalar iterate is

\[
 g_a^{\circ k}(t)=\frac{t}{1+akt}.
\]

Every image of `F` is between zero and `a^{-1}I`. The sequence starting at zero is monotone. For `m>k`, its tail obeys

\[
 \|X_m-X_k\|
 \le g_a^{\circ k}(\|X_{m-k}\|)
 \le g_a^{\circ k}(a^{-1})
 =\frac1{a(k+1)}.
\]

It is therefore norm-Cauchy. Norm-continuity of `F` shows that its limit is a fixed point. Two positive fixed points at distance `delta` would obey `delta<=g_a(delta)`, forcing `delta=0`. Monotonicity and the tail estimate prove (2.2). Iterating (2.3) proves the general convergence estimate. Finally, a positive subsolution increases under iteration and all its iterates converge to the same fixed point. Thus `A<=X_*`, proving (2.4). ∎

### Theorem 2.2 — the representation is generated by source words

Suppose `rank B=r<infinity`. Let

\[
 W_k=\operatorname{span}\{V_{i_\ell}\cdots V_{i_1}u:
 u\in\operatorname{ran}B,\ 0\le\ell<k\},
 \qquad W_0=\{0\},\quad D_k=\dim W_k.
\]

Then `ran X_k` is contained in `W_k`; in particular `rank X_k<=D_k`. The fixed point is compact, and for every positive subsolution `A`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \lambda_{D_k+1}(A)\le\frac1{a(k+1)}.
 \quad} \tag{2.5}
\]

Eigenvalues are ordered nonincreasingly and padded with zeros. Such a positive `A` is also compact.

#### Proof

The support inclusion follows inductively because `Phi(X_k)+P` is supported on `W_{k+1}`, and `g_a(0)=0`. Equation (2.2) approximates `X_*` by finite-rank operators in norm, proving compactness. A positive operator dominated by a compact positive operator is compact: for finite-rank projections onto the leading eigenvectors of `X_*`, the norm of `(I-Q)A^{1/2}` tends to zero, so `A^{1/2}` is compact. Apply min–max to (2.4), using `rank X_k<=D_k`, to obtain (2.5). ∎

If the `V_i` commute, the number of distinct monomials of degree less than `k` is `binomial(d+k-1,d)`, so

\[
 D_k\le r\binom{d+k-1}{d}.
\]

Without relations, only the generally much larger bound

\[
 D_k\le r(1+d+\cdots+d^{k-1})
\]

is automatic. More generally, an actually proved estimate `D_k<=C k^s` yields eigenvalue counting of order at most `t^{-s}` through (2.5). This is a consequence of an acquired source algebra, not an assumption on the unknown target eigenvectors.

**Computational costs.** Achieving the uniform norm error in (2.2) needs `k=O((a epsilon)^{-1})` iterations. This is not logarithmic dependence on accuracy. Each step must apply the `V_i`, acquire the generated span, and evaluate a finite-dimensional inverse on it. The size, conditioning, coefficient precision, and cost of acquiring that span remain charged. For dense `N x N` inputs, ordinary dense matrix operations provide an implementation, but do not remove `N` from the cost. In an infinite-dimensional representation, computability and cost of source-word inner products must be established from that representation; they are not free oracles.

If positive approximate iterates incur norm errors `eta_j` per step, nonexpansiveness gives the safe bound

\[
 \|\widetilde X_k-X_*\|
 \le\frac1{a(k+1)}+\sum_{j=1}^k\eta_j.
\]

Thus both truncation and rounding must be paid. The supplied floating-point script is a diagnostic, not an interval-certified implementation.

### Source perturbations

For fixed `Phi`, let `X_P` and `X_Q` be the fixed points for positive sources `P,Q`. If `||P-Q||<=eta`, the same modulus argument gives, with `delta=||X_P-X_Q||`,

\[
 \delta\le g_a(\delta+\eta),
 \qquad
 \boxed{\quad
 \delta\le\frac{\sqrt{\eta^2+4\eta/a}-\eta}{2}.
 \quad} \tag{2.6}
\]

This estimate is attained by `Phi(X)=X`, `P=eta I`, and `Q=0`, with scalar fixed points. It applies to perturbations **inside** `g_a`. Section 7 treats a different error, added **outside** `g_a`; the signs of the two formulas differ.

## 3. An exact scalar-source index

Return to (1.1). Boundedness gives

\[
 a\|A\|\le a g_a(\|C+P\|)<1.
\]

Order reversal of inversion gives the exact equivalence

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 A\preceq g_a(C+P)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 f_a(A)\preceq C+P.
 \quad} \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed, the first inequality is equivalent to
`I-aA >= (I+a(C+P))^{-1}`; invert it, subtract `I`, and divide by `a`. The argument is reversible.

The polar decomposition `T=U A^{1/2}` makes `C=UAU^*`, so this is a special case of the source recursion with one contraction `U`. This is a mathematical identification, **not** a cheap procedure to acquire `U` from an implicit `T`; constructing the polar factor may itself require difficult spectral information.

### Lemma 3.1 — compact source forces compact target

If `B` is compact and (1.1) holds, `T` is compact.

#### Proof

In the quotient by compact operators, `P` vanishes. Denote the quotient map by `pi`, and put `m=||pi(T)||^2`. The positive operators `pi(A)` and `pi(C)` both have norm `m`. Functional calculus and the order comparison imply

\[
 m\le g_a(m)=\frac{m}{1+am}.
\]

Hence `m=0`, which is exactly compactness of `T`. ∎

For a prescribed finite or countably infinite positive sequence

\[
 a^{-1}>\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots>0,
 \qquad \lambda_j\longrightarrow0\text{ in the infinite case},
\]

define `r_a(lambda)` to be the least possible rank of a source `B` in (1.1), over all bounded operators whose positive singular-value squares are the prescribed list. Arbitrary zero singular values and enlargement of the Hilbert space are permitted. For the empty list set `r_a=0`.

### Theorem 3.2 — exact reciprocal-window formula

With `xi_j=1/lambda_j`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 r_a(\lambda)=\sup_{x>0}\#\{j:x\le\xi_j<x+a\}.
 \quad} \tag{3.2}
\]

The answer may be infinite. In the finite-rank case it is equivalently the least integer `r` satisfying

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \lambda_{j+r}\le\frac{\lambda_j}{1+a\lambda_j}
 \quad\text{for every applicable }j.
 \quad} \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Necessity

If `rank P=r`, min–max applied on a codimension-`r` kernel yields

\[
 \lambda_{j+r}(C+P)\le\lambda_j(C)=\lambda_j.
\]

Equation (3.1) then implies `f_a(lambda_{j+r})<=lambda_j`, precisely (3.3). Taking reciprocals gives `xi_{j+r}>=xi_j+a`. This is equivalent to having no more than `r` reciprocal eigenvalues in any half-open interval `[x,x+a)`. The half-open endpoint is important when equality occurs.

#### Sufficiency and explicit realisation

Suppose the interval count is finite, equal to `r`. On `ell^2(N)`, padding a finite list by zeros, define

\[
 Te_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,e_{j+r},
 \qquad
 Bu_j=\sqrt{f_a(\lambda_j)}\,e_j,
 \quad 1\le j\le r.
\]

Then `T^*T` has the prescribed diagonal. In the first `r` coordinates, `C+P` equals `f_a(lambda_j)`; in later coordinates it equals `lambda_{j-r}`. Equation (3.3) therefore proves (3.1) coordinatewise. The source has exactly rank `r`.

If the interval supremum is infinite, necessity excludes all finite-rank sources. An infinite-rank source still exists: use diagonal `T` and `BB^*=f_a(A)-A`, as in Section 5.1. ∎

**What this realisation preserves.** It preserves singular values, including their multiplicities. It need not preserve the eigenvalues of a pre-existing nonnormal operator, locality, tensor-product structure, or any differential interpretation. No claim for a fixed ambient dimension with prescribed kernel dimensions is made.

### Spectral counting and heat-trace consequences

For any actual rank-`r` certificate, repeated use of (3.3) gives

\[
 \lambda_{j+kr}\le\frac1{\lambda_j^{-1}+ka},\qquad 1\le j\le r.
\]

Thus `T` is in weak Schatten class `S_{2,infinity}`: in elementary terms its singular values obey a `j^{-1/2}` bound after grouping by `r`.

For `H=A^{-1}` on the support of `A`,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}
 \le\frac{\sum_{j=1}^r e^{-\beta/\lambda_j}}{1-e^{-a\beta}}
 \le\frac{r e^{-\beta/\|T\|^2}}{1-e^{-a\beta}},
 \qquad\beta>0.
\]

These are genuine consequences of the certificate. They do not supply a cheap certificate for an arbitrary inverse operator.

## 4. Optimal Schatten transfer

For `0<p<infinity`, the Schatten quantity is

\[
 \|X\|_{S_p}=\left(\sum_j s_j(X)^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]

It is a quasi-norm when `p<1`; the statements below do not require a triangle inequality for it.

### Theorem 4.1 — sharp exponent and constants

Under (1.1), if `B in S_p`, then

\[
 \boxed{\quad T\in S_{q(p)},\qquad q(p)=\max\{p+2,2p\}.\quad} \tag{4.1}
\]

More precisely,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|T\|_{S_{p+2}}^{p+2}\le\frac{2}{pa}\|B\|_{S_p}^{p},
 \qquad0<p\le2,
 \quad} \tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|T\|_{S_{2p}}^{4}\le\frac1a\|B\|_{S_p}^{2},
 \qquad2\le p<\infty.
 \quad} \tag{4.3}
\]

Both meet at `a||T||_{S_4}^4<=||B||_{S_2}^2`. The exponents and displayed constants cannot be improved universally.

### 4.1 Proof for `0<p<=2`

Put `alpha=p/2`. We use the classical trace inequality

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(X+Y)^\alpha
 \le\operatorname{Tr}X^\alpha+\operatorname{Tr}Y^\alpha,
 \quad X,Y\succeq0,\quad0<\alpha\le1. \tag{4.4}
\]

For completeness, if `0<alpha<1`, integrate the scalar identity

\[
 x^\alpha=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}
 \int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}\frac{x}{t+x}\,dt.
\]

For positive matrices, order reversal of inversion implies

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\big[X(t+X+Y)^{-1}\big]
 \le\operatorname{Tr}\big[X(t+X)^{-1}\big],
\]

and likewise for `Y`. Add and integrate to obtain (4.4). At `alpha=1` it is an equality. This classical ingredient is not claimed as new; [2] contains a broader matrix subadditivity result.

Compactness follows from Lemma 3.1. Write `lambda_j=s_j(T)^2`, let `mu_j` be the ordered eigenvalues of `C+P`, and let `Q_N` project onto its leading `N` eigenvectors. Apply (4.4) on this finite-dimensional subspace, then eigenvalue domination under compression:

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^N\mu_j^\alpha
 \le\operatorname{Tr}(Q_NCQ_N)^\alpha
   +\operatorname{Tr}(Q_NPQ_N)^\alpha
 \le\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^\alpha+\|B\|_{S_p}^p.
\]

Equation (3.1) implies `f_a(lambda_j)<=mu_j`. Consequently,

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^N\left[f_a(\lambda_j)^\alpha-\lambda_j^\alpha\right]
 \le\|B\|_{S_p}^p.
\]

The scalar inequality `(1-u)^(-alpha)>=1+alpha u` gives

\[
 f_a(\lambda)^\alpha-\lambda^\alpha
 \ge\alpha a\lambda^{\alpha+1}.
\]

Letting `N` increase proves (4.2). All trace operations before this limit are finite-dimensional; no subtraction of two infinite traces is used. ∎

### 4.2 Proof for `p>=2`

Let `P_N` be the leading `N` spectral projection of `A`, and set

\[
 W_N=A^{p-2}P_N.
\]

At `p=2`, this means `W_N=P_N`, including any zero-eigenvalue issue. Spectral rearrangement gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_NC)\le\operatorname{Tr}(W_NA).
\]

One direct justification is to write `W_N` as a nonnegative linear combination of nested spectral projections and apply the min–max upper bound for the trace of `C` on each projection. `A` and `C` have the same nonzero eigenvalues with multiplicities.

Since

\[
 f_a(A)-A=aA^2(I-aA)^{-1}\succeq aA^2,
\]

(3.1) implies

\[
 a\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^p
 \le\operatorname{Tr}(W_NP).
\]

For `p>2`, finite-rank trace Hölder with exponents `p/2` and `p/(p-2)` gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_NP)
 \le\left(\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^p\right)^{(p-2)/p}
       \|B\|_{S_p}^{2}.
\]

For `p=2`, directly use `Tr(P_NP)<=Tr P`. Rearranging and taking `N` to infinity proves (4.3). ∎

## 5. Two mechanisms force two different exponents

### 5.1 Normal diagonal example: the `2p` obstruction

Let `Te_j=t_j e_j`, where `0<=a t_j^2<1`, and take

\[
 Be_j=\frac{\sqrt a\,t_j^2}{\sqrt{1-at_j^2}}e_j.
\]

Then `C=A` and `P=f_a(A)-A`, so (1.1) is an equality. As `t_j` tends to zero, the source singular values are asymptotic to `sqrt(a) t_j^2`. Hence

\[
 B\in S_p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\in S_{2p}.
\]

Sequences at the summability threshold rule out every universally smaller output exponent. Uniformly scaling a finite diagonal example to zero makes the ratio in (4.3) tend to one, proving sharpness of its constant.

### 5.2 Harmonic weighted shift: repeated nonnormal propagation

On `ell^2(N_0)`, fix `b>0` and define

\[
 \lambda_k=\frac{b^2}{1+ab^2(k+1)},\qquad
 Te_k=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,e_{k+1},\qquad
 Bz=bz e_0.
\]

Now

\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\ldots),
 \qquad
 C+P=\operatorname{diag}(b^2,\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\ldots).
\]

The recurrence `lambda_k=g_a(lambda_{k-1})`, with initial source `b^2`, proves equality in (1.1). The source has rank one, while

\[
 s_k(T)\asymp k^{-1/2}.
\]

Thus `T` is compact but not Hilbert–Schmidt. Finite source rank alone does not imply trace-class or Hilbert–Schmidt response.

This operator also has spectral radius zero. Its weights decrease, so

\[
 \|T^m\|^2=\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda_k,
\]

whose `m`-th root tends to zero. A small eigenvalue spectrum is therefore not the same thing as a small singular-value spectrum.

### 5.3 Direct sums force the `p+2` obstruction

Take an orthogonal sum of the preceding shifts with parameters `b_i` tending to zero, and sources on their root vectors. The singular values of `B` are `b_i`. For every `q>2`, write `alpha=q/2`. Monotone integral comparison gives

\[
 \frac{b_i^{q-2}(1+ab_i^2)^{1-\alpha}}{a(\alpha-1)}
 \le\sum_{k\ge0}
 \left[\frac{b_i^2}{1+ab_i^2(k+1)}\right]^\alpha
 \le\frac{b_i^{q-2}}{a(\alpha-1)}.
\]

Hence

\[
 T\in S_q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (b_i)\in\ell^{q-2}.
\]

For example, a suitably scaled sequence

\[
 b_i=(i+1)^{-1/p}[\log(i+2)]^{-2/p}
\]

belongs to `ell^p` but to no `ell^s` with `s<p`. It rules out any universally valid `q<p+2`. For `q<=2`, one block already fails. At `q=p+2`, a single block with `b` tending to zero makes the ratio in (4.2) tend to one. This proves sharpness of that constant.

Together the normal and nonnormal examples prove the full exponent `max(p+2,2p)` is optimal, not just an artifact of the proof.

## 6. A representation obstruction and its explicit replacement

### 6.1 Finite scalar-source rank fails on a simple product spectrum

On a Hilbert basis indexed by `alpha in N_0^n`, define

\[
 Ae_\alpha=\frac1{|\alpha|+2}e_\alpha.
\]

This definition is sufficient; no physical model or unknown spectral theorem is required. At total degree `m`, the eigenvalue multiplicity is

\[
 M_{n,m}=\binom{n+m-1}{m}.
\]

The reciprocal eigenvalues are the integers `m+2`. For `a=1`, a half-open window of length one contains one degree level. On the cutoff `|alpha|<=m`, Theorem 3.2 therefore gives exactly

\[
 \boxed{\quad r_1=\binom{n+m-1}{m}.\quad} \tag{6.1}
\]

For the full space and `n>=2`, this tends to infinity, despite the fixed operator norm `||A||=1/2`. At `n=m=20`, the minimum scalar-source rank is `68,923,264,410` and the full cutoff dimension is `137,846,528,820`.

Thus a dimension-free operator norm does not imply a small-rank source representation. This is an exact obstruction to a proposed route, not evidence against a general concentration theorem or a general computational class separation.

### 6.2 The same operator has an explicit finite-channel source recursion

The operators in this construction are the standard symmetric multivariable shifts, not newly invented operators; see the established multivariable operator theory in [6]. The calculation below places them in the present recursion and checks the resulting costs. Define

\[
 V_i e_\alpha=
 \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i+1}{|\alpha|+1}}e_{\alpha+e_i},
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]

These operators commute. A direct coefficient calculation shows

\[
 \sum_i V_iV_i^*=I-|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|.
\]

Set `P=|e_0><e_0|` and `Phi(X)=sum_i V_i X V_i^*`. For degree `m>=1`,

\[
 \Phi(A)e_\alpha
 =\sum_i\frac{\alpha_i}{m}\frac1{m+1}e_\alpha
 =\frac1{m+1}e_\alpha.
\]

At the vacuum, the source equals one. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\quad A=g_1(\Phi(A)+P).\quad} \tag{6.2}
\]

A rank-one source and `n` explicitly given propagation operators suffice. There is no contradiction with (6.1), which constrains one endomorphism `T` with `T^*T=A` and the paired operator `TT^*`, not a general multi-channel map.

The generated space `W_k` is exactly the degrees less than `k`, with

\[
 D_k=\binom{n+k-1}{n}.
\]

The iterates reproduce `A` exactly on those degrees and vanish on the others; their norm error is exactly

\[
 \|A-X_k\|=\frac1{k+2}.
\]

This also proves the universal `1/k` convergence order in Theorem 2.1 is sharp. The representation has changed from a huge scalar source to a small list of formulas, but the multiplicities survive in the generated spaces. For large `n` and `k`, those spaces remain large. The example does not establish a general tensor-compression theorem or a speedup over the explicit diagonal baseline.

### 6.3 Why a small number of channels is still not a spectral-compression guarantee

For the product example, the multiplicity formula immediately gives

\[
 A^{1/2}\in S_q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>2n.
\]

Thus the single-operator Schatten law of Section 4 cannot be transferred to arbitrary row maps merely because the source has rank one.

There is an even sharper example. On the Hilbert basis of finite words over `d>=2` letters, let `V_i` prepend letter `i`, and let `P` project onto the empty word. The `V_i` have mutually orthogonal ranges and satisfy `sum V_i V_i^*=I-P`. Define

\[
 Ae_w=\frac1{|w|+2}e_w.
\]

Again `A=g_1(Phi(A)+P)`, by the same one-step scalar recurrence. Each degree has `d^m` vectors. The operator is compact, but `Tr(A^s)=infinity` for every finite `s>0`, because `sum_m d^m/(m+2)^s` diverges. Therefore compactness and a rank-one source coexist with failure of every finite Schatten bound in the multi-channel setting. The missing information is precisely the growth of generated spaces, not the number of initially supplied formulas.

## 7. An unavoidable finite-precision floor

Suppose the single-operator comparison is only known with an additive residual:

\[
 A\preceq g_a(C+BB^*)+\varepsilon I,
 \qquad B\text{ compact},\quad\varepsilon\ge0. \tag{7.1}
\]

Let `||T||_ess` denote the norm modulo compact operators. In the quotient algebra, set `m=||T||_ess^2`. Taking norms gives

\[
 m\le\frac{m}{1+am}+\varepsilon.
\]

Solving the scalar quadratic yields

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|T\|_{\mathrm{ess}}^2
 \le\frac{\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+4\varepsilon/a}}2.
 \quad} \tag{7.2}
\]

The bound is attained on an infinite-dimensional space by `B=0` and

\[
 T=\sqrt{m_*}\,I,
 \qquad
 m_*=\frac{\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+4\varepsilon/a}}2.
\]

Thus the unresolved singular-value scale is asymptotically `(epsilon/a)^(1/4)`. An arbitrarily small nonzero residual does not certify compactness. In finite dimensions, the same identity block can have arbitrarily large multiplicity. This is a precision obstruction, not merely a poor bound from one numerical implementation.

For a rank-`r` source, the finite-dimensional interlacing recurrence becomes

\[
 \lambda_{j+r}\le g_a(\lambda_j)+\varepsilon,
\]

whose scalar limiting floor is the same `m_*`.

## 8. What the mathematics changes, and what it does not

The following consequences are proved rather than assumed: exact source-rank requirements; attainable rank-optimal realisations; optimal source-to-spectrum summability exponents; explicit source-generated approximation spaces; a dimension-free iteration error with the representation cost retained; and sharp precision floors. These make a source-based compression proposal falsifiable before attributing computational power to it.

The substantive representation change is in Section 6: a scalar-source obstruction is not treated as the end of the problem. A multi-channel source calculus is explicitly constructed and proved. It removes the huge initial source rank, but not the large number of generated modes. This distinction matters for any attempted transfer to scientific computation.

There is no proof here that a general interacting Hamiltonian, arbitrary log-concave measure, or computational search problem admits an economically acquired comparison of the required form. There is also no proof that its source-word dimensions, coefficient precision, or source-word Gram calculations remain economical under interactions. Supplying these as assumptions would not meet the original mission.

An exceptional application would require a target-specific construction of the operators and source, a proof of the comparison from the original input, and an end-to-end cost smaller than the strongest relevant specialised method. No such application has been established in this run. The current record is therefore a candidate mathematical mechanism with precise boundaries, not a declaration of a historic breakthrough.

## 9. Evidence, limited comparison, and repository scope

The executable `verify.py` records exact rational/integer checks separately from floating-point stress tests. It checks reciprocal-window rank against the necessary interlacing condition; rank-optimal constructions; exact harmonic source recursions; product multiplicities and commuting row formulas; precision-floor identities; noncommuting finite-matrix Schatten inequalities; and positive-map contraction/bracketing diagnostics. The actual counts and numerical residuals are in `verification_results.json`.

Finite checks do not prove infinite-dimensional statements. The analytic proofs above are the basis of those statements. Numerical results are not interval-certified. No external reviewer or formal proof assistant checked this note, and no historical-priority audit was completed.

Selective repository reading used Astra revision `4ae225b0bf2a07e1ced2952a3e1399d4b45e039c`, including its entry material and N764 dossier. That dossier retains an unresolved interacting inverse-rank issue, but its tensor-train bond rank is not the source rank used here. No transport gate is claimed closed. The OpenAI mathematics repository's README was consulted as a catalogue/status entry; its full manuscript collection was not audited. No repository files were changed.

Initial directions included commutative identity detection, concentration, and many-body gap criteria. No constructive commutative identity-testing breakthrough was obtained. Existing noncommutative identity-testing results [5] do not automatically eliminate commutative cancellation. Inverse-square finite-size frustration-free gap scaling is prior work [4], not a contribution of this run. Current KLS preprint claims were treated as research claims requiring their own scrutiny; their full conclusions are not imported into this note.
