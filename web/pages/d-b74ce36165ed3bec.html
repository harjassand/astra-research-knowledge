## Exact adopted theorem

The input is one fixed integer condensed sum
P(X)=sum f_j(X)exp(g_j(X)/h_j(X)), degree<=d, width<=k, nonzero h_j,
distinct rational exponent fractions, and height w bounding f_j,h_j and
cross differences D_jt. It supplies n,k,d,w and the exact oracle
Eval(p,q,a,u,v), with changing prime characteristics and order-q a.
Bounds/condensation are certified promises or separately charged work.
Individual g_j coefficients may exceed w and must still be read/evaluated.
Rational quotient circuits need separate denominator handling.

For prime q, support(C)<q, degree(C)<=q-1 and fixed integer norm<=W,
modular nullity gives p^{r_p}|Res(Phi_q,C). The product norm bound yields
sum r_p log p<=(q-1)log W. Sampling distinct progression primes with
floor-log weights and then primitive roots gives error<=ceil(log2 W)/T_p.
A second capacity budget bounds bad q characteristics. Extracting a fixed
f coefficient vector, before random point evaluation, keeps W<=kw.

Writing L=k+binom(k,2), the exact four-event bound is

    L ceil(log2 w)/T_q + dk^2/q_min + d/p_min
       + ceil(log2(kw))/min_q T_p(q).

Every q>max{k,6d(k^2+1)}, T_q>=6Lceil(log2 w), and each
T_p(q)>=6ceil(log2(kw)) give one-query false acceptance<1/2 and perfect
completeness. Fresh independent repetitions on the same fixed input give
error<2^-t. Adaptive targets, duplicate prime capacity, composite q and
unbounded/evaluation-dependent integer lifts are excluded explicitly.

An elementary central-binomial supply bound constructs Q. Effective uniform
Linnik, pinned Xylouris0906.2749v1 Theorem1.1, plus distinct CRT classes
construct enough p=1 mod q. Deterministic enumeration, rejected candidates,
trial-division certification and order construction cost polynomial in
**numerical** d,k,log w. Field widths are asymptotically logarithmic in
those parameters. The effective absolute constant is not numerically
instantiated. This is not deterministic PIT, poly(log q) acquisition,
general circuit-size polynomial time, a fixed-field sparse-Descartes result
or a concrete useful Linnik runtime bound. Add input/expansion, n/sampling,
