# Morphogenesis control: a conditional range–speed law for the Fog mechanogen front

**Continuation note (cycle 9, 2026-10-08):** This file preserves the original C8 passive-pool analysis. The current assessment is [`cycle9/FINAL_REPORT.md`](cycle9/FINAL_REPORT.md), with its claim ledger at [`cycle9/CLAIMS.json`](cycle9/CLAIMS.json), exact model derivation at [`cycle9/proof/robust_detachment_margin.md`](cycle9/proof/robust_detachment_margin.md), and restart/failure gates at [`cycle9/FAILURES_AND_GATES.md`](cycle9/FAILURES_AND_GATES.md). C8’s transport lemma remains valid for its stated PDE, but the C9 source audit does not identify it as the tissue speed law.

## Finding

The strongest defensible result from this lane is a **conditional control law and a decisive missing-measurement test**, not a new established biological mechanism. In a moving epithelial front, a passive, front-relative surface-ligand excess with lateral diffusivity `D`, clearance `k≥0`, and material-row front speed `v_rel` has exact decay length

\[
\ell=\frac{2D}{v_{rel}+\sqrt{v_{rel}^{2}+4Dk}},\qquad v_{rel}\ell\le D.
\]

The inequality says how far this type of passive signal can extend at a chosen front speed; the equality `k=D/ell²−v_rel/ell` gives the clearance compatible with a target footprint. The formula is classical moving-frame advection–diffusion–clearance mathematics. Its useful application here is the model comparison across a 2026 Fog surface-pool measurement and an older Drosophila contractility-wave speed. It suggests that the measured slow Fog pool could span tens of microns while the fast extracellular pool would be effectively uniform.

The evidence does **not** identify this transport model as the tissue mechanism. The reported MyoII exponential length is not the Fog-slow length, the FCS slow component is only presumed to be membrane-associated/receptor-bound, and the wave speed is imported from a different study/condition as a proxy for speed through material cell rows. A cell-local wave-timed response with no lateral ligand transport can reproduce the same spatial exponential. Therefore the biological claim remains `UNKNOWN`; the model is a candidate design law to test.

## Current biological mechanism reconstructed

The 2026 primary study by Mundhe et al. in *Nature Communications* ([DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-68418-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-68418-z)) extends two earlier results in the same Drosophila posterior-endoderm system. The 2019 *Nature* study by Bailles et al. ([DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1492-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1492-9)) described a self-replicating contact/adhesion/deformation relay: local invagination moves anterior cells against the vitelline membrane, integrins anchor the cortex, MyoII is activated, and contraction/detachment relays the cycle. Its 1D model showed that bistable local mechanochemical feedback plus finite spatial coupling can support a constant-speed wave. The 2024 *Developmental Cell* study by Collinet et al. ([DOI 10.1016/j.devcel.2023.11.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2023.11.022)) resolved sequential integrin–MyoII feedbacks and found that prolonged contact strengthens adhesion and slows detachment/wave propagation.

The 2026 work separates initiation from propagation-zone signaling. Restoring Gα only in the primordium gives near-normal initial invagination but reduces MyoII propagation from 7.1±0.3 to 2.5±0.06 rows on average. A diffusible, primordium-only Fog rescue restores about 5.3±0.2 rows, while a membrane-tethered Fog source gives only 1.7±0.1 despite similar primordium invagination. Thus dispersed Fog and propagation-zone GPCR signaling are necessary in those tested embryos; initiation alone or a purely mechanical relay is insufficient as a complete account.

The signal is not a simple free-ligand concentration gradient. The study measures near-uniform extracellular Fog::YFP (`D≈55 μm²/s`) and a small, much slower apical Fog::YFP population (`16%`, `D≈1.6 μm²/s`) whose fraction varies across the propagation zone. The authors regard the slow population as membrane-associated or receptor-bound, but do not chemically identify it. Gprk2 RNAi raises this fraction to `30±15%`, flattens the slow-pool spatial slope, and broadens the MyoII response. Integrins are required for the increase/broadening under Gprk2 depletion, yet do not directly increase GPCR clustering; the source interprets integrins as signaling cofactors. The precise integrin–GPCR link and possible ligand post-translational modifications remain unresolved.

This is a multicomponent mechanism: Fog source and diffusion, receptor oligomerization/endocytosis, tissue contact with the vitelline membrane, integrin activation, MyoII feedback, cell motion, and detachment. The 2026 article reports that greater Fog/MyoII amplitude and range slow wave progression through increased adhesion/reduced de-adhesion. That makes joint signal range and tissue speed the relevant control objective; maximizing contractility alone is a poor design target.

The adjacent 2025 Hydra paper, “Mechanochemical patterning localizes the organizer of a luminal epithelium” ([DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adu2286](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adu2286)), is a distinct stronger baseline for mechanical steering of regeneration. It couples Wnt softening and strain-dependent Wnt production in a pressure-cycled shell and experimentally biases organizer location with an agarose-window constraint. Its authors describe the shell model as qualitative because key mechanical/chemical parameters are uncertain. I do not claim that form of mechanical organizer control as a new result here.

## Conditional model and reconstructed proof

In the cell-material coordinate, write `x` for distance into the signal field ahead of the front. In the front frame, assume the background-subtracted mobile surface pool `b` has constant lateral diffusivity `D>0`, front-relative drift `v_rel>0`, first-order clearance `k≥0`, no distributed source in the measured tail, and no local amplification there. Its steady tail solves

\[
0=D b''+v_{rel}b'-kb.
\]

For `b=b_0e^{-x/ell}`, the characteristic equation is `D/ell²−v_rel/ell−k=0`. The positive decay exponent is `(v_rel+sqrt(v_rel²+4Dk))/(2D)`, yielding the formula in `proof/moving_frame_bound_pool.md`. `ell` decreases with both `v_rel` and `k`; at zero clearance its maximum is `D/v_rel`. A target passive footprint is feasible iff `v_rel ell_target≤D`.

This derivation is exact for the stated PDE and nowhere proves that Fog, MyoII, or tissue mechanics obey it. The full 2026 biology has an integrin-dependent source/gain and geometry-dependent contact, so it can violate the source-free, linear assumptions. The formula is a proposed prediction target for a reduced model, not a replacement for the active wave model in Bailles et al. or for the measured feedback loop in Mundhe et al.

## Quantitative audit

Bailles et al. report a wave speed proxy of `2.2±0.2 μm/min` and about one row per three minutes. Mundhe et al. report `D_slow=1.6±0.2 μm²/s`, `D_fast≈55 μm²/s`, and a Fog-slow profile measured across a region reaching 70 μm. Directly combining those **cross-paper means** gives:

- `D_slow/v ≈ 43.6 μm` (35–54 μm when taking corners of the reported ± values; these are not confidence limits).
- `D_fast/v ≈ 1,500 μm`.
- Across 70 μm, the front sweep time at the proxy speed is about 1,909 s; the characteristic diffusion times `L²/(2D)` are about 1,531 s for the slow pool and 44.5 s for free Fog. The corresponding Peclet numbers `vL/D` are about 1.60 and 0.047.

This scale separation is consistent with free Fog equilibrating across the propagation zone much faster than the front moves, while the slow pool moves on a comparable timescale. The measured uniform free Fog agrees with the first part. It does not verify the second part because FCS diffusivity is not yet established as the moving material-frame mobility of an active Fog-bound pool.

As a deliberately conditional inversion, if the **MyoII** e-fold length were the same field length, then the slow-pool model would require `k≈0.147 s⁻¹` in WT (`ell=3.18 μm`), versus `0.047 s⁻¹` under gprk2 RNAi (`ell=5.48 μm`), a 3.15-fold change. Two extra Fog copies give `k≈0.051 s⁻¹` (`ell=5.26 μm`). This direction matches slower receptor clearance broadening the profile. But MyoII is shaped by integrin co-signaling and its reported length is much shorter than the tens-of-microns threshold range; no such effective clearance rate was measured. Treat those values as a hypothesis inversion only. Reproduction of all calculations is in `code/front_length_audit.py` and `code/front_length_audit.json`.

## Adversarial baselines and what survives

**Free Fog reaction–diffusion.** If one tried to identify the WT MyoII e-fold length (`3.18 μm`) with a free-Fog moving-frame tail at `D_fast≈55 μm²/s`, the same proxy speed would require an effective first-order sink near `5.43 s⁻¹`. That is only a conditional mismatch calculation: the 2026 live Fog::YFP experiment found uniform extracellular Fog, so a simple free concentration gradient cannot explain the measured MyoII profile under those conditions. Classic morphogen papers already analyze receptor binding, endocytosis, transport, and clearance; this report makes no novelty claim for that model class (`sources/NOTES.md`).

**Cell-local wave-timed response.** For a moving front, a per-cell kinetic waveform `M(s)` indexed by contact/activation time is observed in space as `M(x)=M(s(x))`. An exponential time response with `s=x/v_rel` gives the same spatial exponential with `ell=v_rel tau` and requires no lateral ligand transport. The data imply `tau≈87 s` for the WT MyoII length and `≈149 s` for the gprk2 value when using the 2019 speed proxy. These are not excluded; the primary study itself notes the equivalence between spatial front profiles and temporal signaling maturation in each cell's frame. Same-condition single-cell movies are needed to select between these explanations.

More generally, any single profile `P(x)` can be reproduced by the no-transport waveform `M(a)=P(v_rel a)` over its observed age window. The spatial snapshot therefore has no mechanistic identifying power by itself; discrimination requires independently measured cell-age kinetics or held-out perturbations.

**Known integrin–MyoII relay.** The 2019/2024 studies establish this stronger mechanical baseline. The 2026 primordium-only rescue and tethered-Fog controls show it is not sufficient alone for the observed Drosophila wave. The new candidate model must improve held-out predictions over that already informed relay, not compare only against no signaling.

**Maximize Fog/MyoII amplitude.** The 2026 study reports that expanding the gradient slows the wave, consistent with the 2024 contact/detachment mechanism. This is an observed counterexample to treating maximal signal amplitude as maximal tissue propagation. The useful objective is a calibrated range–speed frontier.

The current study's parameter sets are not matched across papers, and the FCS component's molecular identity is uncertain. No novelty or priority claim is made for the advection–diffusion equation; its derivation is standard. The potentially useful new link is the quantitative application of that law to the measured surface-slow Fog pool and the output-speed tradeoff, which remains a candidate until directly tested.

## Proposed design algorithm and decisive experiment

For a target activation footprint `ell_target`, acquire the effective surface-pool mobility `D`, cell-row-relative front speed `v_rel`, and first-order clearance `k` in one condition. Compute the feasibility test `v_rel ell_target≤D`; if it passes, calculate the required `k=D/ell_target²−v_rel/ell_target`. Then map the measured `k` to manipulable Fog-source dose/Gprk2 perturbation, and map `v_rel` to contact/de-adhesion mechanics. The selection objective should maximize front travel over a fixed developmental window subject to a minimum signaling footprint and detachment/adhesion constraints. Falsify the model if same-condition data violate the range bound or if a no-transport cell-time model predicts held-out profiles equally well.

The decisive test is a matched 3-factor perturbation: graded Fog source level × graded Gprk2/endocytosis × integrin contact intact/depleted. Match initial primordium invagination depth. The already published diffusible-versus-membrane-tethered Fog rescue is a necessary control for source dispersion; the published Gprk2 × integrin comparison is a necessary control for integrin dependence. Add same-embryo or carefully matched-cohort measurements of (i) cell-row-relative front speed and cell trajectories, (ii) Fog-slow profile, mobility, and pulse-chase clearance, (iii) βPS/contact geometry via dextran exclusion, and (iv) MyoII amplitude, e-fold length, threshold range, contact duration, and detachment time. At least one Gprk2 dose and one integrin condition must be held out from parameter fitting.

Existing methods offer practical acquisition hooks: the 2026 study reports FCS traces of 30–60 s with multiple spots per embryo, live z-stacks at 4–20 s intervals for marker imaging, and endogenous Fog/βPS and MyoII reporters; cell-row speed can be obtained from tracked contact-to-detachment trajectories. These require custom genotypes, injections/knockdowns, confocal/FCS time, and embryo-level replication. The published studies report small embryo-level samples for several profiles; cells/measurement spots nested within embryos are not independent biological replicates. Sample size must be powered from a pilot embryo-level variance estimate; the source papers do not supply enough raw data here to compute a defensible target number. Mechanical detachment force is not directly measured in this proposed profile assay, so use detachment time/contact geometry as proxies unless an independent force assay is added. Energy/resource expenditure is not quantified.
