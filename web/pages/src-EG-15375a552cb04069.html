Internally prestressed separator: explicit construction and resource bounds
Root invention and continuum derivation, 12 October 2026

Status: independently reconstructed existing architecture, rejected as a new
mechanism. The materials worker's subsequent full methods/supplement retrieval
found that Cui et al. already use a contracting NiTiNb shape-memory alloy ring
around the LLZO pellet. This corrects the scout's earlier implication of a
removable external fixture. Root's circular tensile-ring load path is therefore
directly anticipated by the strongest relevant demonstration. Numerical material
values below are declared illustrative assumptions, not acquired measurements.

Desired operation
------------------
Maintain approximately 330 MPa biaxial in-plane compression in an ion-conducting
garnet separator through a self-contained load path. Recent
experiments support stress steering of lithium cracks in particular LLZTO
geometries. They do not establish that 330 MPa is sufficient for all chemistry,
defects, current, capacity or cycle life. Root's proposed implementation is a
tiling of compressed ion-conducting cores, each surrounded by a closed tensile
ring, rather than prescribing a favorable stress field without its load path.
The basic ring operation is already the published experimental method. Tiling
does not establish a new operation or an improvement over that method.

Exact necessary balance
-----------------------
Let a bounded composite body be at static equilibrium, div(sigma)=0, with zero
external traction and no body force. Assume the Cauchy stress is symmetric and
interfaces transmit traction. For each i,j, the divergence theorem gives

  integral_Omega sigma_ij dV
    = integral_boundary x_i * traction_j dS = 0.

Thus an entire free-standing single-phase separator cannot have sigma_xx<0
everywhere (or sigma_yy<0 everywhere). Residual surface compression is balanced
by tension elsewhere. This does not prohibit useful local compression.

If ceramic volume fraction f has sigma_xx,sigma_yy<=-P, and all remaining
load-bearing material has tensile normal stress <=S, then necessarily

  f*P <= (1-f)*S,      f <= S/(P+S).                         (1)

Void fractions cannot carry the balancing stress. Equation (1) is necessary,
not sufficient: maximum principal stress, fracture, fatigue, interfaces and
electrochemical stability may impose stricter conditions. Uniform thermal
expansion in a free homogeneous body supplies no residual stress.

Constructive circular cell
--------------------------
Take a ceramic disk of radius a and a same-thickness elastic annular ring with
outer radius b. Work in small-strain isotropic plane stress, with no body force,
outer radial traction zero, and ideal frictionless interference contact giving
pressure P at a. Perfectly bonded phases with different thickness strains require
a three-dimensional interface correction; that model is not asserted here.
The exact classical axisymmetric solution is

  ceramic: sigma_rr=sigma_theta_theta=-P,
  ring:    sigma_rr=A-B/r^2, sigma_theta_theta=A+B/r^2,
  A=P*a^2/(b^2-a^2), B=A*b^2.

The ceramic therefore has the desired uniform in-plane compression in this
ideal model; the tensile ring closes its force path. With f=a^2/b^2,

  max ring hoop stress = P*(1+f)/(1-f).

A ring tensile limit S requires S>P and

  f <= (S-P)/(S+P),                                        (2)

which is stricter than the general balance bound. S is an allowable principal
tensile stress, not automatically a measured uniaxial yield stress. At the ring's
inner edge the principal stresses are (-P,S,0); a ductile von Mises criterion
would instead test sqrt(S^2+P^2+P*S). Plane-stress displacement
compatibility requires an initial radial interference delta satisfying

  delta/a = P*[(1-nu_c)/E_c
                + ((1+f)/(1-f)+nu_r)/E_r].                 (3)

Indeed the disk contracts by P*a*(1-nu_c)/E_c and the inner ring expands by
P*a*[((1+f)/(1-f)+nu_r)/E_r]. Their sum is the required interference.
Creating this interference, then retaining it under cycling, is a paid
manufacturing operation. Thermal mismatch can generate it only with the correct
sign and sufficient integrated expansion mismatch; relaxation changes P.

Illustration only: P=0.330 GPa, E_c=150 GPa,nu_c=0.25,E_r=250 GPa,nu_r=0.25.
If an admissible sustained ring strength were S=2 GPa, the maximal core fraction
from (2) would be 0.71674 and (3) requires about 1.00% interference. A choice
f=0.85 would instead require peak hoop stress 4.07 GPa and 1.83% interference.
These are substantial requirements, not material-property or durability claims.

Transport and failure interfaces
--------------------------------
For insulated rings with through-plane active ceramic disks, the most favorable
parallel-path ionic conductivity is f_device*kappa_c. Separate circular cells
in hexagonal packing have f_device <= pi/(2*sqrt(3))*f, before dead margins,
seals and interfacial losses. At the illustrative S=2 GPa limit this geometric
factor is about 0.650. Shrinking tiles does not remove this strength/fill penalty.
A dense ion-blocking tensile skin in series instead blocks through-plane ionic
transport. Electronic isolation of reinforcement is an independent requirement;
a conductive ring contacting both electrodes would short the separator.

The stress solution does not certify out-of-plane stability. A compressed open
disk can buckle; the critical stress scales as E_c*(thickness/a)^2 with a factor
set by its actual edge restraint. A frictionless ring supplies radial pressure
but no transverse support by itself; even a simply supported boundary cannot be
assumed. A compliant ring is not a clamped boundary.
In a perfectly bonded symmetric laminate the total in-plane resultant can be
zero, so treating core compression alone as whole-laminate Euler loading is
incorrect. Delamination and local compressed-core buckling still require checks.

Internal stress also stores elastic energy. For a uniform isostrain two-phase
laminate with biaxial moduli M_i=E_i/(1-nu_i), core stress -P and balancing
reinforcement stress fP/(1-f), its stored energy per composite volume is

  U=f*P^2/M_c + f^2*P^2/[(1-f)*M_r].                        (4)

This expression is for the laminate, not the annular stress solution. It shows
why debonding can release significant energy; it is not itself an interfacial
fracture calculation. At f=0.85,M_c=200 GPa,M_r=300 GPa the value is about
2.21 MJ/m^3, or 111 J/m^2 for 50 micrometre total thickness. Actual energy
release depends on crack geometry and constraints.

The engineering gate is therefore explicit: a chemically compatible, ionically
acceptable, electronically insulating tensile load path must sustain the required
stress and interference, transfer it uniformly into the active ceramic, avoid
buckling/debonding and relax negligibly. Grain-boundary and crack-tip reactions
remain relevant even if the elastic construction works. Lateral lithium growth
must not create a new interface route through the separator.

Prior-art and matched comparison boundary
-----------------------------------------
Shrink-fit rings, residual stress and composite prestressing are established
mechanics. Residual compression is already a solid-electrolyte strategy, including
ion exchange and thermal gradients. The candidate must beat these and Cui's
direct self-contained ring demonstration at matched chemistry, thickness, active area,
current, areal capacity and cycle count; root has established no such result.
Root's independent synthesis is the load-path calculation plus exact
strength/fill/interference gates in the battery task; its architecture was found
to be existing prior art. Worker reports contain the targeted prior-art comparison
and the primary-method correction. This route does not survive as a discovery.
