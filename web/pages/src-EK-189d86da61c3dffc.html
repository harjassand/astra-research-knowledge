# C1 — primitive genesis: quotient-first decoder certificates

**Status key:** *known* means established literature; *internal reconstruction* means derived here without a novelty claim; *candidate* means a capability proposal; *falsified* means an explicit counterexample defeats the tempting claim; *open* means unresolved.

## 1. North Star

**[Candidate]** Give an exact, inspectable answer to a device-replacement question: for two supplied finite-dimensional channels, either construct a physical decoder that turns the available channel into the target, or return a finite operational test proving every decoder fails. Diagnose whether failure comes from information erased by the source or from the impossibility of extending the induced map to a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) operation. Keep supplied mathematical data, acquired data, numerical gap, and physical test cost as separate claims.

This is a small reverse-inference primitive: turn a global “for every input” simulation assertion into a decoder or an explicit experiment.

## 2. Exact question

Let \(N:A\to B\) and \(M:A\to C\) be finite-dimensional CPTP maps. Decide whether a CPTP map \(D:B\to C\) exists with
\[
M=D\circ N.
\]
If yes, return \(D\); if no, return a finite witness separating \(M\) from every \(D\circ N\).

The derivations assume exact supplied channel maps, such as Kraus operators or Choi matrices. They do not assume exact reconstruction from finitely many laboratory queries.

## 3. Importance

A decoder is a direct replacement guarantee: every later task using \(M\) can instead use \(N\) followed by \(D\). A no-go certificate identifies a test that exposes the missing capability. This can support channel compilation, interface compatibility, and resource-conversion audits.

The certificate can diagnose two different failures. A kernel witness says the source erased a direction the target still uses. An extension witness says the target is linearly consistent with the source’s information, but no physical decoder realizes that rule.

## 4. Current obstruction

For supplied channel descriptions, exact factorization is an SDP. A bare solver status is hard to inspect and gives no readable physical witness. A dual certificate may need an entangled reference and a joint measurement; existence does not imply a low-depth measurement or cheap channel acquisition.

For black-box channels, exact equality has no uniform finite-query test without a promised separation margin. The explicit family in §6 shows this. A query-based system needs an UNKNOWN region or a stated noise/model assumption.

This predicate is narrower than universal information or capacity order. Failure of postprocessing simulation does not settle a capacity or divergence comparison.

## 6. Actual investigation

### Internal reconstruction: quotient, then physical extension

If \(M=D\circ N\), necessarily
\[
\ker N\subseteq\ker M.
\]
When this holds, define \(\overline M:\operatorname{ran}N\to\mathcal L(C)\) by
\[
\overline M(N(X))=M(X).
\]
The kernel inclusion makes this well-defined. Since the original maps preserve trace, \(\overline M\) preserves trace on \(\operatorname{ran}N\). The remaining question is whether this partially defined map has a CPTP extension to all of \(\mathcal L(B)\). That extension question is an SDP. The reverse-inference route therefore has two failure points: descent to the quotient, then physical extension.

If inclusion fails, choose a Hermitian \(X\in\ker N\) with \(M(X)\ne0\). Trace preservation gives \(\operatorname{Tr}X=0\), so its positive and negative parts have the same trace \(s>0\). The input states \(\rho_+=X_+/s\), \(\rho_-=X_-/s\) obey \(N(\rho_+)=N(\rho_-)\) but \(M(\rho_+)\ne M(\rho_-)\). A Helstrom measurement distinguishes the target outputs with a strict advantage, while every decoder sees identical source outputs. This gives a no-reference witness directly from the kernel computation.

Kernel inclusion is sufficient only for the first point. Let \(N(\rho)=\tfrac12\rho+\tfrac14 I\) be the qubit depolarizing channel and \(M=\operatorname{id}\). In the Pauli basis, \(N\) fixes \(I\) and scales each traceless Pauli by \(1/2\); it is linearly invertible, so \(\ker N=\{0\}\) and the kernel test passes. But any decoder with \(D\circ N=\operatorname{id}\) must equal \(N^{-1}\), and
\[
N^{-1}(|0\rangle\langle0|)=
\begin{pmatrix}3/2&0\\0&-1/2\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is not positive. No CPTP decoder exists.

A direct operational witness confirms the extension failure. On equal-prior inputs \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\), \(M\) outputs orthogonal states and succeeds with probability 1. The outputs of \(N\) are \(\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1/4)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(1/4,3/4)\), with trace-norm difference 1. Every CPTP decoder contracts trace distance, so its discrimination success is at most
\[
\tfrac12+\tfrac14\cdot1=\tfrac34.
\]

### Internal reconstruction: one Choi-state binary test

Use unnormalized Choi matrices \(J_T\), with \(\operatorname{Tr}J_T=d_A\), and normalized Choi states \(\rho_T=J_T/d_A\). The set
\[
\mathcal S_N=\{\rho_{D\circ N}:D:B\to C\text{ CPTP}\}
\]
is compact and convex: the decoder set is compact and convex, and \(D\mapsto(D\otimes\mathrm{id})(\rho_N)\) is affine.

For an effect \(0\le E\le I_{C\otimes A}\), define
\[
g(E)=\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho_M)-\max_D\operatorname{Tr}(E\rho_{D\circ N}).
\]
Finite-dimensional minimax gives
\[
\max_{0\le E\le I}g(E)
=\min_D\tfrac12\lVert\rho_M-\rho_{D\circ N}\rVert_1.
\]
Interchange the max over the compact convex effect set with the min over the compact convex decoder set; for each decoder, the optimal effect is the positive spectral projector of the trace-zero state difference. The gap is zero exactly when a decoder exists and positive otherwise.

Operationally, prepare a normalized maximally entangled state on \(A\otimes A\), send one half through the channel, then measure \(E\) jointly on the output and reference. A strict gap means the target passes this binary test better than every postprocessing of the source. This is an internal derivation from Choi encoding, convexity, and minimax, not a novelty claim. The gap is Choi-state trace distance to the simulation set, not the diamond-norm postprocessing deficiency; it lower-bounds that stronger deficiency, and no dimension-independent reverse bound is claimed.

**Worked exact example:** let \(N=\Delta_Z\) and \(M=\Delta_X\), qubit dephasing in the \(Z\) and \(X\) bases. For \(X=\sigma_x\), \(N(X)=0\) while \(M(X)=X\). Equivalently, \(N\) sends both \(|+\rangle\) and \(|-\rangle\) to \(I/2\), but \(M\) sends them to orthogonal states. Every postprocessing of \(N\) has equal-prior success \(1/2\), while \(M\) succeeds with probability 1.

The normalized Choi state of \(M\) is \(\rho_M=(I+X\otimes X)/4\). Every \(D\circ\Delta_Z\) has zero \(X\otimes X\) correlation because the reference remains block-diagonal in the \(Z\) basis. The effect
\[
E=(I+X\otimes X)/2
\]
accepts \(M\) with probability 1 and every postprocessing of \(N\) with probability \(1/2\). The gap is exactly \(1/2\): a constant decoder gives \(\rho_{D\circ N}=I/4\), whose trace distance from \(\rho_M\) is \(1/2\), and the displayed effect proves no decoder does better.

### Falsified acquisition claim

Let \(N=\Delta_Z\) and \(M_t=(1-t)R_{I/2}+t\Delta_X\), where \(R_{I/2}\) is the constant replacer channel and \(0\le t\le1\). At \(t=0\), \(M_0\) is simulated by a constant decoder after \(N\). For every \(t>0\), \(N(X)=0\) but \(M_t(X)=tX\), so kernel inclusion fails and no decoder exists. Yet \(\lVert M_t-M_0\rVert_\diamond\le2t\). A standard hybrid bound limits any fixed \(k\)-query adaptive experiment’s equal-prior distinguishing advantage by \(kt/2\). Since \(t>0\) can be arbitrarily small, no fixed finite query budget uniformly decides exact factorability for unknown channels. A promised gap or an UNKNOWN region is required.
