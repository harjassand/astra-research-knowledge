1. Exact polar definitions
----------------------------
Let A=Gamma_sigma^(1/p)f=U|A| be its polar decomposition and p'=p/(p-1).
Define, for a>=1,

 I_a,p^polar(f)=Gamma_sigma^(-1/a)[U|A|^(p/a)],
 E_p^polar(f)=p/[2(p-1)] Re<I_p',p^polar(f),L(f)>_sigma,
 E_2(G)=<G,L(G)>_sigma,
 <G,H>_sigma=Tr[G^*Gamma_sigma(H)].

For KMS L, the quadratic E2 is real nonnegative for every complex G. At p2
the polar map gives I2,2(f)=f. On Hermitian/positive inputs these definitions
agree with the explicitly signed/positive conventions in the frozen baseline.
Partial polar isometries at singular inputs cause no ambiguity because every
power exponent used here is positive; extend by continuity if desired.

2. Weighted dilation power transport
--------------------------------------
Use stationary Sigma=(I2/2)tensor sigma, generator Lhat=id_M2 tensor L, and

 D_f=[[0,f],[f^*,0]].

This is Hermitian. The lifted generator is KMS and has a faithful stationary
state even though it need not be primitive. For any real r,

 Gamma_Sigma^r(D_f)=2^(-r)D_(Gamma_sigma^r f).

Hence the weighted Hermitian matrix at exponent1/p is
Xhat=2^(-1/p)D_A. Since
|D_A|=diag(|A^*|,|A|), functional calculus gives, for every alpha>0,

 sgn(D_A)|D_A|^alpha
             =[[0,U|A|^alpha],[|A|^alpha U^*,0]].

For the signed map I_a,p on the dilation, the power contributes
2^(-alpha/p)=2^(-1/a), exactly canceled by Gamma_Sigma^(-1/a).
Consequently the full matrix identity is

 I_a,p^signed,Sigma(D_f)=D_(I_a,p^polar,sigma(f)).       (D1)

This holds in particular for a=p' and a2. There is no missing dimension
factor in the transported profile maps.

3. Exact real-pairing factors
-------------------------------
For arbitrary complex G,H, the stationary weighting gives

 <D_G,D_H>_Sigma
   =[Tr(G Gamma_sigma(H^*))+Tr(G^*Gamma_sigma(H))]/2
   =Re<G,H>_sigma.                                    (D2)

The two terms are conjugates because Gamma is HS selfadjoint and preserves
adjoints. Also Lhat(D_f)=D_(L(f)). Equations(D1),(D2) therefore imply exactly

 E_p^signed,Sigma(D_f)=E_p^polar,sigma(f),
 E_2,Sigma(I_2,p^signed,Sigma(D_f))
                      =E_2,sigma(I_2,p^polar,sigma(f)). (D3)

Apply the already frozen signed-Hermitian theorem to D_f. This proves

 E_p^polar(f)>=C_p E_2(I_2,p^polar(f))

for every complex f and finite1<p<infinity. The reference weighting and
2x2 block trace factor have both been charged exactly. The proof needs the
signed-Hermitian extension; a positive-input theorem alone would not suffice
because D_f is generally indefinite.

4. ROOT-supplied absolute-vs-signed comparison, separately checked
------------------------------------------------------------------
For Hermitian amplitude Q with eigenvalues epsilon_a q_a, q_a>=0, a legal
Hermitian noise H_B satisfies exactly

 Tr[QH_B(Q)]-Tr[|Q|H_B(|Q|)]
     =sum_{a,b}q_aq_b(1-epsilon_a epsilon_b)|B_ab|^2>=0.

The drift terms agree since Q^2=|Q|^2; only the B(.)B term changes. Summing
the legal noises proves E2(unsigned I2,p f)<=E2(signed I2,p f) for Hermitian
f. Thus the signed gradient profile also bounds the unsigned root energy if
one intentionally adopts that mixed convention. It still cannot rescue the
literal unsigned gradient E_p on negative inputs, which may be negative.
No absolute-vs-polar comparison for arbitrary non-Hermitian f is claimed.
