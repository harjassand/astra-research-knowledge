## Supplied data

Let ell be an odd prime, K a finite characteristic-two field containing
a primitive ell-th root zeta, kappa=ell². A finite incidence structure has
t points, exactly ell+1 incident lines at each point, and incidence girth
at least12. Supply columns x_v∈K^m with

    0<m<t<=m ell²,
    sum_v x_v x_v^T=-I_m,
    sum_(v in f) x_v x_v^T=0 for every line f.

The source's polynomial-pairing proposition constructs these columns from
its punctured affine structure. Its interpolation basis and line-sum
identities were read. Summing line identities does not force the total sum
to vanish: the regularity ell+1 is zero in characteristic two. The full
existence of the required punctured regular high-girth structure remains
a load-bearing prerequisite, not inferred from these algebraic identities.

## Embedded local groups and the planar count

At each point glue a copy of the additive group F_ell²; at each incident
line glue its prescribed one-dimensional subgroup. There are exactly
ell+1 distinct one-dimensional directions. The resulting group N has a
finite presentation. It is essential to prove these prescribed subgroups
inject, rather than assume all vertex groups survive arbitrary amalgamation.

I reconstructed the supplied planar proof. A hypothetical collapsed local
word yields a weighted bipartite planar graph with one exceptional white
vertex whose balance is nonzero. Minimality removes parallel edges and
merges consecutive equal-labelled neighbors using adjacent planar arcs.
At black vertices the balance and distinct local directions force degree
at least3; ordinary white vertices have degree at least2, while the one
exceptional vertex has degree at least1. Nonexceptional face boundaries
project to nonbacktracking incidence walks, hence have length at least12.
At most one exceptional face is discarded. If E is the edge count, these
bounds give

    n_black<=E/3,
    n_white<=(E+1)/2,
    n_faces<=E/6+1.

Consequently V+F<=E+3/2, incompatible with the connected planar Euler
identity V+F=E+2. The reduction rules and exceptional endpoint matter; a
bare appeal to high girth without them would not establish injection.
This proves the local injectivity used by the conditional algebra. It
does not claim additional intersections of unrelated vertex groups.

## Rectangular identity and scalar packing

In K[N] let P_v be the normalized subgroup average and P=diag(P_v).
The local F_ell² decomposition into its one-dimensional subgroups, together
with the supplied line sums, yields matrices

    U=(-kappa x_v P_v)_v,       T=(P_v x_v^T)_v,
    UT=I_m,                   UP=U, PT=T.

The central twist construction adjoins an ell-order element z and produces
H=N×<z> inside a finitely presented group L. Let e be the central-character
idempotent corresponding to zeta. The slot choices (s_v,alpha_v) are
distinct and exist because t<=m kappa. The exact character orthogonality
then gives packed matrices C,J0 with

    J0 C=eP.

Define a=U J0+(1-e)I_m and b=C T+(1-e)I_m in Mat_m(K[L]). All cross terms
vanish because the packed maps live in the e corner, so ab=I_m. Furthermore

    J0(ba-I_m)C=e(TU-P).

This defect is nonzero. The augmentation/central-character homomorphism
used to see it is defined only on K[H], sending N to1 and z to zeta.
It need not, and generally does not, extend to K[L]. Under it the defect
becomes a t×t scalar rank-m matrix minus I_t; since t>m it cannot vanish.
The embedding K[H]⊂K[L] preserves that already established nonzero element.

Failure in Mat_m(K[L]) alone would not be a scalar direct-finiteness
counterexample. The source supplies the extra finite group

    F=(Z/ell)^m semidirect S_m.

Distinct character idempotents and coordinate permutations provide matrix
units and a copy of Mat_m(K[L]) in an idempotent corner of K[L×F]. Add the
complementary idempotent 1-f to each packed element. Their scalar products
are then AB=1 and BA≠1. This construction averages only odd-order factors;
it does not divide by |S_m| in characteristic two.

Finiteness of every displayed presentation and the surviving ell-order
local group give a finitely presented output WITH odd-prime torsion. The
selected Lean endpoint matches exactly this conclusion. A fixed fieldF2,
torsion-free output, or nonsoficity has not been obtained by this argument.
