N04 CELLULAR SENSING — ASYMMETRIC INPUT ACCURACY PLATEAU v5
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: exact finite-state counterexample and internally verified CRN parameterization. Append-only companion to v1–v4. This strengthens the warning that prediction accuracy alone cannot specify information or work when the input prior is biased; it is not a new general thermodynamic law.

Purpose
The balanced telegraph input in v1–v4 made the Bayes accuracy invariant under changes of reporter occupancy. This note checks the alternative of an asymmetric but still autonomous, exogenous, temporally correlated environment. A nonuniform prior introduces an additional obstruction: the Bayes-optimal classifier can always guess the more common input state over a whole open set of sensing parameters. Accuracy then stays at the prior baseline while predictive information and dissipation vary.

Autonomous equilibrium source and sensor
Let E be a two-state equilibrium source with transition rates alpha=1 from + to - and beta=3 from - to +. Its stationary prior is pi=P(E=+)=beta/(alpha+beta)=3/4, and its temporal correlation decays as exp[-(alpha+beta)tau]=exp(-4tau). The source is exogenous: both transition rates are independent of reporter state M. It can be realized with a state free-energy difference E_+ - E_- = -kBT ln 3 and detailed-balanced transitions; no external controller or source work is included.

Let M in {0,1} be the reporter. For response rate R=3, rate difference Delta=1/2, and mean occupancy m=P(M=1), choose

    u_+ = R m + (1-pi) Delta,
    u_- = R m - pi Delta,
    d_+ = R-u_+,
    d_- = R-u_-,

where u_e is M:0->1 and d_e is M:1->0. The two explicit positive-rate choices are

    sensor L, m=1/4: (u_+,d_+)=(7/8,17/8), (u_-,d_-)=(3/8,21/8),
    sensor C, m=1/2: (u_+,d_+)=(13/8,11/8), (u_-,d_-)=(9/8,15/8).

Both have u_e+d_e=3 for each E state and u_+-u_-=1/2. They share the same exogenous signal law, conditional response relaxation rate, and rate contrast. Their input-conditioned chemical routes may use fixed E+- and E--gated chemostats with affinities ln(u_+/d_+) and ln(u_-/d_-), respectively. The E state is catalytic and unchanged by M transitions. The reporter standard energy is independent of E; the two chemostat affinities are explicitly part of the resource boundary.

Stationary law
For a general two-state input with rates alpha, beta, s=alpha+beta, and prior pi=beta/s, the stationary conditional reporter occupancies obey

    q_+ = P(M=1|E=+) = m+(1-pi)c,
    q_- = P(M=1|E=-) = m-pi*c,
    c = Delta/(R+s).

This follows by solving the two conditional stationary equations; multiplying them by pi and 1-pi cancels the E fluxes and gives Rm=pi*u_+ +(1-pi)*u_-. The rational joint laws in state order (+0,+1,-0,-1) are

    sensor L: (123/224,45/224,45/224,11/224),
    sensor C: (81/224,87/224,31/224,25/224).

Both are strictly positive and normalized. Here c=1/14.

Exact Bayes-accuracy plateau
At horizon tau let z=c exp(-s tau). The conditional distributions of M given the future signal are

    P(M=1|E_(t+tau)=+) = m+(1-pi)z,
    P(M=1|E_(t+tau)=-) = m-pi*z.

Define the joint-decision differences

    D_1 = pi P(M=1|E_future=+) - (1-pi)P(M=1|E_future=-)
        = (2pi-1)m+2pi(1-pi)z,
    D_0 = pi P(M=0|E_future=+) - (1-pi)P(M=0|E_future=-)
        = (2pi-1)(1-m)-2pi(1-pi)z.

The Bayes classifier chooses + for reporter value 1 if D_1>0, and chooses + for value 0 if D_0>0. For both sensors, D_1>0 and D_0>0 at tau=0; since z decreases with tau, these inequalities hold for every tau>=0. Thus the optimal forecast always guesses the more common state +, and

    A_tau = pi = 3/4,     epsilon_tau = 1/4

for both sensors at every horizon. Their state entropies differ, H(M)=h(1/4) versus h(1/2), and so the equality of classification accuracy does not mean their internal memory states have equal entropy.

Their predictive mutual information is also different. For any horizon,

    I_tau(m) = h(m)-pi*h(m+(1-pi)z)-(1-pi)*h(m-pi*z).

For tau=0, the exact-rate calculation gives I_tau=0.002645432878 nats for sensor L and 0.001916125164 nats for sensor C; these positive values differ even though both sensors make exactly the same optimal classification decision at every horizon. The equality A_tau=pi is a plateau, not perfect ignorance: each reporter can carry likelihood information that is insufficient to change the majority-class decision.

Thermodynamic cost and exact comparison
The stationary cycle current is

    j = pi*alpha*c = 3/56.

The square-cycle affinity is

    A_square = ln[(u_+ d_-)/(d_+ u_-)].

The observed path EPR and an attainable one-route-per-edge chemical power are

    sigma_obs/kB = beta*P_min = j A_square.

For sensor L, A_square=ln(49/17), giving beta*P_min=0.056711086824 per time unit. For sensor C, A_square=ln(65/33), giving beta*P_min=0.036314984380 per time unit. Thus the same input law, response rate, rate contrast, and Bayes forecast accuracy across every horizon coexist with different predictive mutual information and different minimum chemical power.

An autonomous locally detailed-balanced realization couples the + reporter edge to one fixed fuel chemostat with affinity A_+=ln(u_+/d_+) and the - edge to a second fixed chemostat with affinity A_-=ln(u_-/d_-). At stationarity their net edge currents are +j and -j. Counting both chemostat free-energy fluxes gives beta*P=j(A_+-A_-)=j A_square, exactly the full path EPR. The source transitions obey detailed balance with the stated energy difference. No controller, signal holding, or time-dependent protocol is used. Both networks are therefore physically realizable within the declared multi-chemostat boundary. A specific cellular pathway is not claimed.

The equality construction includes only ongoing stationary chemical power. As in v3/v4, reaching the stationary distribution from another preparation has a transient work cost; it is not in beta*P. No reporter reset is performed between forecasts. Fluorescence readout, persistent storage, and physical reset each add costs outside the boundary. Finite-record endpoints must be censored unless the future signal is observed. If a laboratory apparatus drives the signal away from the autonomous detailed-balanced source, its work must also be included.

Interpretation, counterexamples, and literature status
For a biased prior, a raw Bayes accuracy can ignore all sensing over an interval where the best decision is simply to guess the majority class. In that interval, accuracy is not a sufficient predictor of either I(M;future) or energy use. Balanced accuracy, log loss, mutual information, or a score subtracting the prior-only baseline can distinguish the sensors. The exact plateau condition for pi>1/2 is

    m <= 1 - [2 pi(1-pi)/(2pi-1)] z,

provided D_1>0, which holds here. When it holds, both reporter values predict the majority state. At the equality boundary the classifier is indifferent for one reporter value. The pi<1/2 case follows by relabeling the signal states.

This is a model-specific metric obstruction rather than a new thermodynamic bound. Barato, Hartich & Seifert (2014), primary preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.3186v3, Sections II–IV, already analyze an autonomous bipartite two-state input/two-state sensor and identify distinct information and entropy-production metrics. Marzen & Crutchfield (2017), https://arxiv.org/html/1707.03962v1, define total predictable information from the sensor state to the whole input future and derive power expressions for conditionally Markovian sensors. Freitas & Esposito (2022), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32700-7, Eqs. 25–26, show that different hidden channel decompositions can preserve the master equation while changing EPR. The present asymmetric example is an elementary exact counterexample to using Bayes accuracy alone as an energetic or informational summary. No novelty or priority is asserted.

The derivation above establishes the all-tau plateau because the decision inequalities are strict at tau=0 and z decreases thereafter.
