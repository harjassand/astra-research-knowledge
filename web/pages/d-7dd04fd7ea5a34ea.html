1. JOINT RETURN OF A QUANTUM ENERGY REFERENCE
==========================================================================

COORDINATOR DERIVATION: JOINT RETURN OF A FINITE QUANTUM ENERGY REFERENCE
2026-10-10. Internal mathematics; foundational/novelty gate NOT PASSED.
Motivated by round2/05.txt; independently derived below before opening the
2021 Chiribella--Yang--Renner paper. This is a restricted resource calculation,
not an assertion of new physics or a general computing breakthrough.

Model. n>=1 qubits have H_S=sum_i |1><1|_i. The reference R has
H_R=sum_{r>=0} r|r><r| and initial density operator rho with finite mean mu.
Initial joint state is |0^n><0^n| tensor rho. An arbitrary joint unitary U
commutes with H_S+H_R. All outputs are retained. Target is
tau=|+^n><+^n| tensor rho, including the original reference uncorrelated with
all outputs. Write D=1/2||U(initial)U* - tau||_1. There is no discarded energy
reservoir, externally driven nonconserving operation, or uncharged catalyst.
The constructed upper bound takes rho as a supplied resource, not acquired
coherence from an incoherent input. No bound on implementing arbitrary U is
implied; the explicit upper uses n specified two-sector qubit interactions.

1. A lower bound against ALL conserving protocols, not just translated ladders.
For any s>0 the bounded effect exp[-s(H_S+H_R)] is invariant under U. Put
L_rho(s)=Tr(rho exp(-sH_R)), b_n(s)=((1+exp(-s))/2)^n.
Its expectation on the actual output is L_rho(s), while on the target it is
L_rho(s)b_n(s). The variational trace-distance characterization therefore gives

 D >= L_rho(s)(1-b_n(s)) >= exp(-s mu)(1-b_n(s)).                 (1)

The second inequality is scalar Jensen applied to the energy measurement
distribution of rho. It is valid for mixed states and unlimited energy support.
Taking s=1/(mu+n), which is <=1, uses 1-exp(-s)>=s/2 and
 b_n(s) <= exp[-n(1-exp(-s))/2] <= exp(-ns/4).
Since ns/4<=1/4, 1-exp(-ns/4)>=ns/8; also exp(-s mu)>=exp(-1).
Consequently

 D >= n/[8 e (mu+n)].                                         (2)

In particular D<=epsilon implies mu >= n/(8 e epsilon)-n.
For 0<epsilon<=1/(16e), mu>= n/(16e epsilon). Heavy tails do not
evade the linear-in-n, inverse-error mean-energy requirement in this model.
This argument uses a bounded energy witness, avoiding the invalid step of
bounding a difference of unbounded mean energies by trace distance.

Finite support variant. If rho is supported on r in [a,a+L-1], both actual
and ideal total energies lie in [a,a+L-1+n]. Their means differ by n/2.
On that common support the affine-rescaled total energy is a bounded effect.
Thus

 D >= n/[2(L+n-1)].                                           (3)

The effect can be clipped outside the common support to obtain an actual
bounded effect on the full Hilbert space. Again rho may be mixed, and U
may be arbitrary subject to exact total-energy conservation.

2. Uniform constructive upper bound for a supplied sine reference.
For L>=1 take eta=sum_{k=0}^{L-1} c_k |n+k>,
 c_k=sqrt(2/(L+1)) sin(pi(k+1)/(L+1)).
Its mean energy is mu=n+(L-1)/2. Use a Hadamard in each conserving sector
span{|0,r>,|1,r-1>} for r>=1 and identity on |0,0>. Reuse R on n fresh
zero-state qubits. The n-level offset avoids the lower boundary even on the
all-one branch. The final pure state is exactly

 Psi=2^(-n/2) sum_x |x> T^{|x|}eta,

where T shifts the ladder down by one on the encountered support. For norm
calculations extend to the bilateral ladder, where T is unitary; all physical
states occurring here have nonnegative energy. Let theta=pi/(L+1).
The path-adjacency eigenvector identity gives <eta,T eta>=cos(theta), so
 ||T eta-eta||^2=2(1-cos(theta)).
Telescoping gives ||T^w eta-eta||<=w||T eta-eta||. Orthogonality of the
system basis and W~Bin(n,1/2) yield

 ||Psi-|+^n>eta||^2
   = E ||T^W eta-eta||^2
   <= 2(1-cos(theta)) E W^2
   = (1-cos(theta)) n(n+1)/2.

For unit vectors, their trace distance is at most their Euclidean distance.
Hence, uniformly in ALL integers n,L>=1,

 D <= min(1, sqrt[(1-cos(theta)) n(n+1)/2])
   <= min(1, pi sqrt[n(n+1)]/[2(L+1)]).                        (4)

Taking L >= pi sqrt[n(n+1)]/(2 epsilon) suffices. The mean energy is
O(n/epsilon). Together with (2), the optimum supplied mean-energy scaling
for this precise state-preparation-and-joint-return task is Theta(n/epsilon)
for uniformly small epsilon. This does not construct the coherent reference
from free inputs, assert a universal gate diamond-norm optimum, or give an
experimentally verified protocol. Exact sine amplitudes are idealized: an
initial reference trace-distance error delta contributes at most 2 delta
to this same-reference-return comparison by unitary invariance and triangle
inequality, so state preparation precision must be charged.

3. Exact best possible overlap for a fixed PURE resource distribution.
Let eta=sum_r sqrt(p_r) exp(i phi_r)|r>. Decompose the ideal |+^n>eta into
total-energy sectors. Its squared norm in sector E is
 q_E=sum_{w=0}^n binom(n,w)2^(-n) p_{E-w}.
The initial vector has squared sector norm p_E. Each conserving U can rotate
the nonzero initial vector arbitrarily inside its fixed-energy sector. By
Cauchy--Schwarz, and by choosing each sector's rotation to align the vectors,

 max_U |<+^n|<eta| U |0^n>|eta>| = sum_E sqrt(p_E q_E).          (5)

The arbitrary-sector rotations need not have an efficient gate synthesis.
Equation (5) is an exact optimization identity, not a charged algorithm.
It gives an independent finite diagnostic against overstating the sequential
shift protocol's optimality at finite n,L. It is not used for (4).

4. Corrections to the investigator's version.
The flat profile formula A=1-n/(2L) applies only when n<=L; at n>>L use
 A=E[(1-W/L)_+]. In particular the proposed example L=sqrt(n) has A->0,
but this conclusion requires the truncated-overlap formula, not continuation
of 1-n/(2L) into negative values. A Chebyshev bound or binomial tail suffices.
The sine-state uniform bound above replaces the unproved asymptotic remainder.
Preparing a shared reference requires one coherent resource preparation,
while preparing n separate banks requires n; preparation cost is still
unresolved, not equal by assertion. One fixed input error is a lower bound
on diamond error for the JOINT output/reference channel only; it is not a
lower bound on the usual reduced system channel after tracing out R.

5. Foundational and prior-art gate.
This mechanism is smooth boundary tapering plus total-energy conservation.
It does not solve acquisition of coherent resources, fault tolerance,
universal classical simulation, or any requested landmark. Literature search
found Chiribella, Yang and Renner, Fundamental Energy Requirement of Reversible
Quantum Operations, Phys. Rev. X 11, 021014 (2021), DOI
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.11.021014 . Their stronger gate/computation
results already establish matched energy bounds and shared-battery reuse.
Its error conventions and reference-return requirement must be read before
claiming direct equivalence to (1)--(5). No novelty is claimed. The earlier
finite-phase-reference sine profile and Aberg's catalytic coherence are
also established antecedents. This calculation is preserved as useful
internally derived mathematics, with FAIL at the mission's selection gate.

2. EXACT GAUSSIAN NORM EVALUATION AND PERMANENT
==========================================================================

AUDIT: EXACT STANDARD-GAUSSIAN NORM REDUCTION TO PERMANENT
Investigator 04 | 2026-10-10

RESULT

The proposed reduction is correct as an exact polynomial-time Turing reduction. I found no flaw in the factor 2^N, the Gram construction, interpolation, or square-root recovery. It proves exact real standard-Gaussian squared-norm evaluation for integer-coefficient circuits is #P-hard (using the standard #P-completeness of 0/1 permanent). It does not imply hardness of approximate norm evaluation, randomized PIT, or identity testing from one norm query.

1. GRAM CONSTRUCTION AND ITS COST

Let M∈{0,1}^{n×n}, N=2n, and
  A = [[0,M],[M^T,0]].
A is symmetric, zero-diagonal, and 0/1. Its graph has E≤N(N−1)/2 edges and degree d_i≤N−1. For integer t=N,...,2N, form B_t with one column e_i+e_j for every unordered edge {i,j} of A, and t−d_i copies of e_i for each i. Since t≥N>d_i, all multiplicities are nonnegative. Directly:

- The diagonal (i,i) of B_t B_t^T gets d_i from incident edge columns and t−d_i from singleton columns, totaling t.
- For i≠j, the (i,j) entry is 1 exactly when {i,j} is an edge, otherwise 0.

Hence B_t B_t^T=tI+A. Its number K_t of columns is
  K_t=E+Σ_i(t−d_i)=Nt−E≤2N².
The sparse column list is explicitly constructible in O(N²) space/time up to ordinary integer-index costs; a dense N×K matrix would use O(N³) cells. All entries are 0/1.

2. COMPLEX-WICK IDENTITY WITH THE NORMALIZATION PROVED

Let X_1,...,X_K,Y_1,...,Y_K be independent real N(0,1), and z_k=X_k+iY_k. For formal s_k,u_k,
  E exp(Σ_k s_k z_k + u_k conjugate(z_k)) = exp(2Σ_k s_k u_k).
For one coordinate, this follows from the ordinary Gaussian moment-generating function:
  E exp(sz+u conjugate(z))
  = E exp((s+u)X+i(s−u)Y)
  = exp(((s+u)^2−(s−u)^2)/2)=exp(2su).
Independence gives the product over k. Comparing coefficients (equivalently differentiating at zero) yields the exact pairing identity
  E[∏_{r=1}^N z_{k_r} ∏_{s=1}^N conjugate(z_{l_s})]
    =2^N Σ_{σ∈S_N} ∏_{r=1}^N 1_{k_r=l_{σ(r)}}.
There are no pairings between two z's or two conjugates; each matched pair contributes 2.

For an integer B∈Z^{N×K}, set L_i=Σ_k B_ik z_k and P=∏_{i=1}^N L_i. Expand P conjugate(P) and apply the pairing identity:
  E|P|²
   =2^N Σ_{σ∈S_N} ∏_i (Σ_k B_ik B_{σ(i),k})
   =2^N perm(BB^T).
Writing P=R+iI with real integer-coefficient circuits R,I gives pointwise |P|²=R²+I², and therefore
  E[R²]+E[I²]=2^N perm(BB^T).
This confirms the normalization. For N=1,B=[1], the identity reads E[X²]+E[Y²]=2, consistent with the formula.

3. RECOVERING PERM(M)

For each t=N,...,2N, use B_t and the circuit
  P_t=∏_{i=1}^N Σ_{k=1}^{K_t}(B_t)_{ik}(X_k+iY_k).
No complex primitive is needed in the oracle input: build R_t,I_t by the recurrence (R_0,I_0)=(1,0),
  R_i=R_{i−1}a_i−I_{i−1}b_i,
  I_i=R_{i−1}b_i+I_{i−1}a_i,
where a_i=Σ_k(B_t)_{ik}X_k and b_i=Σ_k(B_t)_{ik}Y_k. Each is an integer arithmetic circuit of size O(NK_t)=O(N³), with 2K_t=O(N²) real Gaussian inputs and degree N. Two exact norm queries give
  q(t):=perm(tI+A) = (E[R_t²]+E[I_t²])/2^N.
The N+1 distinct query points determine the degree-at-most-N polynomial q(t), including q(0). Lagrange interpolation uses polynomially many rational operations; the interpolation weights at 0 have O(N log N) bits each (even a crude product bound is polynomial), and query values have polynomial bit length.

For a 0/1 M, every nonzero permutation term of A must match top-block rows to right-block columns and bottom-block rows to left-block columns. These choices are independent and each contributes a permutation term of M or M^T. Thus
  perm(A)=perm(M) perm(M^T)=perm(M)^2.
Since perm(M) is a nonnegative integer, exact integer square root of q(0) recovers it. This is a polynomial-time Turing reduction from 0/1 permanent to exact Gaussian norm evaluation.

4. BIT-COST ACCOUNTING

For t≤2N, entries of tI+A are at most 2N. Therefore
  0≤q(t)≤N!(2N)^N,
so q(t), 2^Nq(t), and each nonnegative norm E[R_t²],E[I_t²] have O(N log N) bits. The circuit has O(N³) arithmetic gates and O(N²) Gaussian variables, all integer constants of O(1) bits. The interpolation has N+1 points and polynomial bit complexity; integer square root is polynomial in the O(N log N)-bit result. No coefficient expansion of P_t is needed by the reduction. The only black box is exact evaluation of the specified standard-Gaussian squared norm.

The norm outputs are integers: R_t,I_t have integer coefficients, and Gaussian moments of integer powers are integers ((2r−1)!! for even powers and zero for odd powers). More importantly, the derived identity itself gives the stronger polynomial bit bound above.

5. CHECK AND SCOPE

A finite exact sanity checker is at work/tests/R3_04_gaussian_reduction_check.py. It expands the real/imaginary circuit and evaluates monomial-pair Gaussian moments using integers, then checks B_tB_t^T=tI+A and the Wick identity for every 1×1 0/1 matrix and t=2,3,4 (N=2). It passes. This only checks small instances; the general proof is the pairing calculation and interpolation above.

Potential failure points audited: (i) the complex-Gaussian variance is 2, producing 2^N, not 1; (ii) Re and Im norms must be added, not either one alone; (iii) edge columns contribute to the diagonal as well as the desired off-diagonal, exactly canceled/filled by t−d_i singleton columns; (iv) the query values lie away from zero, but N+1 samples still recover the constant of a degree-N polynomial; (v) the final permanent is a square, so integer square root is exact.

Conclusion: exact standard-Gaussian norm evaluation for rational/integer arithmetic circuits is #P-hard under polynomial-time Turing reductions, assuming the standard 0/1 permanent completeness theorem. No claim follows for FPRAS/additive approximation, norm-zero testing, or general PIT: interpolation needs exact values with all bits, and the reduction uses multiple circuits/queries. The earlier Boolean-distribution argument was weaker in its relevance to Gaussian norm; this construction closes that exact-norm gap.

COORDINATOR DERIVATION: AN INVERTIBLE-LOCAL-GAUGE OBSTRUCTION
2026-10-10. Internally derived, not externally certified; novelty UNKNOWN.
Mission gate: FAIL. This rules out a specified computational shortcut, not
general approximate counting. It is not the requested foundational discovery.

The source-informed starting tensor is
 F = [[4,0,0,1], [0,9,4,0], [0,4,9,0], [1,0,0,4]],
flattened by ports (1,2)|(3,4), each port dimension two. It is a legal critical
EPR tensor with (a,b,c,d)=(4,9,4,1) and b=a+c+d. The original source's
determinant representation and this parameter contract are in the pinned N190
proof (Astra 19e5c78ddcf0ad95816ad766dcc08839b2578b39). The argument below
requires only this displayed matrix, not the correctness of a general sampler.

Claim. There are no A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4 in GL(2,C) for which
 M=(A_1 tensor A_2) F (A_3 tensor A_4)^T
is the nonnegative signature of a four-port weighted perfect-matching gadget
in the sense of Cai--Liu (general, not necessarily planar matchgates). The
same holds with F replaced by F^k for every integer k>=1, corresponding to
identical serial gates with unchanged physical port pairing.

Imported necessary condition. Cai--Liu, Counting perfect matchings and the
eight-vertex model, arXiv:1904.10493v1, Theorem 4.1, says a nonnegative
four-ary matchgate signature has one parity and its four products of
complementary entries P,Q,R,S obey each product <= sum of the other three.
Only necessity is used. We do not import any counting algorithm, nor infer
that the converse to the spectral condition below is true.

1. Invariant under the FULL complex invertible local group.
Let e=[[0,1],[-1,0]], J=e tensor e, and K(M)=M J M^T J. All transposes here
are ordinary transposes, not conjugate transposes. For every complex 2x2
matrix A, A^T e A=det(A)e. Hence for L=A_1 tensor A_2 and R=A_3 tensor A_4,
 L^T J L=d_L J, R^T J R=d_R J,
where d_L=det(A_1)det(A_2), d_R=det(A_3)det(A_4).
Using L^T J=d_L J L^{-1}, direct multiplication yields

 K(L M R^T) = d_L d_R L K(M) L^{-1}.                     (1)

Thus the unordered spectrum of K is invariant up to a common nonzero scalar.
For the displayed F, parity ordering gives blocks [[4,1],[1,4]] and
[[9,4],[4,9]]. F is symmetric and commutes with J. Consequently

 spec K(F) = {25,9,169,25}.                              (2)

This avoids the false use of singular values under nonorthogonal gauges.
The construction belongs to the established invariant-theoretic framework
for four binary tensor factors. No claim that this framework is new is made.

2. What a nonnegative matchgate would require of the invariant.
First suppose M has even parity. After the same parity reordering it consists
of blocks X=[[p,q],[r,s]] and Y=[[u,v],[w,z]], with nonnegative entries.
J has blocks swap and minus-swap, respectively. Directly,
 X swap X^T swap = [[ps+qr,2pq],[2rs,ps+qr]],
whose eigenvalues are (sqrt(ps)+sqrt(qr))^2 and
(sqrt(ps)-sqrt(qr))^2. The Y block has the analogous eigenvalues from uz,vw.
All four invariant eigenvalues are therefore real and nonnegative.
If M is invertibly equivalent to F, they are nonzero, and (1)--(2) force
d_L d_R to be a positive real number even if the A_i are complex.

After removing its positive square root, the invariant root multiset is
{13,5,5,3}. Partition it into the two parity blocks, say (a>=b),(c>=d).
The complementary products for the first block, up to a common positive
scale and exchanging P with Q, must be
 P=(a+b)^2/4, Q=(a-b)^2/4.
The other two products are R=(c+d)^2/4, S=(c-d)^2/4, up to exchange.
The imported product inequalities imply

 ab = P-Q <= R+S = (c^2+d^2)/2,                        (3)
 cd <= (a^2+b^2)/2.                                    (4)

There are only three pair partitions of {13,5,5,3}. Pairing 13 with a 5
requires 65<=(25+9)/2=17, false (two equivalent partitions). Pairing 13
with 3 requires 39<=(25+25)/2=25, false. Every possible pairing violates a
necessary inequality. This proves the contradiction for even parity.

For odd parity, exchange the two basis labels on one external port. This
permutates nonnegative entries and realizes the corresponding odd/even
parity conversion of a matching signature (one pendant edge of unit weight
at that port). Equivalently its complementary products still satisfy the
same imported inequalities. It is another invertible local transform, so
the even-parity contradiction applies. Hence odd parity cannot evade it.

3. Identical serial grouping does not remove this obstruction.
F^k remains symmetric, commutes with J, and has invariant root multiset
{13^k,5^k,5^k,3^k}. The two types of necessary inequality become
 65^k <= (25^k+9^k)/2, or 39^k <= 25^k.
Both are false for every integer k>=1. The same proof excludes all such
serial powers without enumerating a finite set of k. An irrelevant positive
normalization of F or F^k changes only the common invariant scale.

4. Exact scope.
Independent GL(2,C) transformations on each port form a larger class than
edge-consistent holographic gauges on a network, where neighboring transforms
must cancel. Local impossibility therefore also blocks those consistent
gauges for a network containing this ungrouped gate. The serial-power result
blocks the specified identical two-wire grouping too.
It does NOT exclude nonlocal representations, sums of gadgets, approximate
representations, singular transformations with altered interfaces, larger
port dimensions, general heterogeneous or multiwire groupings, or a different
sampler. It proves no #P hardness, absence of an FPRAS, or quantum/classical
complexity separation. The general EPR normalization problem remains open.

4. EXACT TEST AND COMPRESSION OF RANK-COLLAPSING KRAUS HISTORIES
==========================================================================

EXACT TEST AND COMPRESSION OF RANK-COLLAPSING KRAUS HISTORIES
10 October 2026 — internally derived scoped theorem; novelty not established

This result does not pass the foundational selection gate. It uses standard
exterior algebra and conic Caratheodory elimination. It neither recognizes
all entanglement-breaking channels nor simulates general many-qubit systems
in polynomial time. Complexity below is polynomial in the dense Hilbert
dimension d, not in the number of qubits log_2(d).

THEOREM AND INTERFACE

Input: T explicit trace-preserving channels on C^d, each given by Gaussian-
rational Kraus matrices K_i^(t), with each real/imaginary numerator and
denominator at most B bits. Outcome counts r_t are included in input cost.
For a history w=(i_1,...,i_T), let B_w=K_iT^(T)...K_i1^(1).

(a) In polynomial bit complexity, decide whether EVERY B_w has rank <=1,
without listing the histories. For a repeated fixed instrument, eventual
all-history rank collapse occurs iff it has occurred by D=binom(d,2) steps.

(b) In polynomial bit complexity, construct the exact forgotten-outcome
composition in the form Phi(X)=sum_j c_j B_j X B_j*, with at most d^4 terms,
c_j positive rationals and each B_j an actual length-T branch product.
The c_j need not equal its original trajectory weight. If (a) holds, every
retained B_j is rank one and this gives an exact measure-prepare description.

The input is the supplied Kraus instrument. The output preserves the terminal
channel after forgetting outcomes. It need not preserve recorded histories,
their probabilities, or responses to interventions between stages.

PROOF OF (a)

Let E=Lambda^2 C^d, dim(E)=D, and A_i^(t)=Lambda^2 K_i^(t). Starting with
V_0=E, compute V_t=sum_i A_i^(t)V_(t-1). By induction V_t is the span of
the images of all exterior branch products A_w. Thus V_T=0 iff all A_w=0.
Functoriality gives A_w=Lambda^2 B_w, and Lambda^2 B=0 iff rank(B)<=1.
This proves the equivalence. For d=1 the exterior space is already zero.

Store a basis of at most D vectors of length D, generating r_t D candidates
at each step. Select a maximal independent subset of the actual candidates
by rational elimination, rather than repeatedly normalizing their basis.
Every retained vector then remains A_w e_j for a genuine history w.

For a fixed instrument, F(V)=sum_i A_i V is monotone. Since V_1 subset V_0,
induction gives V_(t+1) subset V_t. Equality at any step persists forever.
A descending chain in dimension D either reaches zero by step D or stabilizes
at a nonzero space. This cutoff is not asserted for time-varying instruments.

For bit costs, clear all O(d^2) rational denominators in each dense input
matrix. Their common denominator has O(d^2 B) bits. Entries of a raw length-t
product have length L_t=O(t d^2 B+t log d). The same polynomial bound applies
to exterior product-image columns (their entries are minors of raw products).
Exact independence tests have polynomial-size minors. A straightforward
algorithm uses O(sum_t r_t D^3) field operations, on polynomial-bit numbers.
The retained basis uses O(D^2) scalar entries, in addition to input and
temporary candidate storage. This proves polynomial bit complexity.

PROOF OF (b)

For any matrix B write P(B)=vec(B)vec(B)*. These lie in the real vector space
Herm_(d^2), of dimension m=d^4. Begin with B=I and coefficient c=1.
If sum_j c_j P(B_j) is the exact Choi matrix at stage t-1, composing with
the next instrument gives the exact candidate list K_i^(t)B_j with unchanged
c_j. There are at most r_t m candidates. Delete zero matrices.

Whenever there are more than m candidates, exact rational elimination finds
a nonzero integer relation sum_j h_j P(B_j)=0. Nonzero P(B_j) are PSD, so h
has both signs: taking trace rules out a nonzero relation of one sign.
Set theta=min_(h_j>0) c_j/h_j and replace c_j by c_j-theta h_j.
All coefficients stay nonnegative, at least one becomes zero, and the Choi
matrix is unchanged. Delete zero coefficients and repeat. Only weights
change; every remaining B_j is still an actual branch product. Induction
proves exact channel equality and the d^4 support bound at every stage.

This procedure never materializes the exponential history list. There are
only polynomially many candidates, dependency calculations and eliminations.
The remaining issue is coefficient length, which a unit-cost argument would
miss. The coordinates of P(B_j) have O(L_t) bits. Clearing their denominators
and using maximal minors gives a dependence h of polynomial bit length in
m, r_t and L_t. This bound depends on raw branch products, not on the current
coefficient denominators.

Maintain every c_i=n_i/Q with a COMMON positive integer denominator Q. If j
attains the minimum and h_j>0, an exact update is

  n_i' = n_i h_j - n_j h_i,       Q' = Q h_j.

Thus log Q grows additively by at most poly(m,r_t,L_t) per elimination, over
a polynomial number of eliminations. To bound numerators, trace preservation
gives sum_i c_i ||B_i||_F^2=d. Every nonzero raw rational B_i has a nonzero
entry of magnitude at least 2^-O(L_t), so c_i<=d/||B_i||_F^2<=d 2^O(L_t).
Consequently log n_i<=log Q+O(L_t+log d), also polynomial. This completes
the exact bit-complexity proof, including positive coefficient updates.

If every branch is rank <=1, all retained nonzero B_j have rank one. Define

  E_j=c_j B_j* B_j,
  sigma_j=B_j B_j*/Tr(B_j B_j*).

Then sum_j E_j=I and c_j B_j X B_j*=Tr(E_j X) sigma_j for every X. All output
entries are rational; no square-root encoding is needed. These equalities
give the claimed exact measure-prepare form.

BOUNDARIES AND COMPETENT BASELINE

An arbitrary dense channel already has a Choi representation computable in
polynomial time and a Kraus representation with at most d^2 terms if arbitrary
linear combinations/spectral factorization are allowed. Our d^4 construction
does not improve generic channel simulation. Its point is retaining actual
branch products with positive reweighting, hence retaining the rank-one
property when that branchwise condition holds.

The completely depolarizing qubit channel illustrates the acquisition gap.
Its supplied Pauli Kraus matrices I/2,X/2,Y/2,Z/2 are all invertible. Every
history product is invertible, so (a) fails at every time. Nevertheless the
channel is entanglement breaking, as its output is always Tr(X)I/2. Thus no
representation-independent criterion follows, and tensoring many qubits
still makes the dense dimension exponential. Low-dimensional PPT theory does
not remove this general acquisition obstacle.

Rank-one Kraus/measure-prepare equivalence is established prior knowledge:
Horodecki, Shor, Ruskai, General Entanglement Breaking Channels (2003),
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302031 . Constructive conic Caratheodory and
invariant-subspace closure are standard mechanisms. A bounded literature
search has not established novelty of the exact combined formulation.

5. CONNECTED PHYSICAL COUNTEREXAMPLE TO IID LOOP SAMPLING
==========================================================================

CONNECTED PHYSICAL COUNTEREXAMPLE TO IID LOOP-SELECTOR SAMPLING
10 October 2026 — internally derived; estimator-specific, not hardness

Let F(a,c,d)=aI+cC+dD on two qubits, with positive a,c,d,
C=2|psi+><psi+| and D=(X tensor X)SWAP. In the computational basis,

  F=[[a,0,0,d],[0,b,c,0],[0,c,b,0],[d,0,0,a]],  b=a+c+d.

For a chronological gateword on a closed register, Z=Tr(product_e F_e).
One natural unbiased estimator independently chooses I,C,D at gate e with
probabilities a_e/b_e,c_e/b_e,d_e/b_e, and returns

  Y=(product_e b_e) Tr(product_e P_e).

The trace of a selector word is computed exactly by binary equality/parity
constraints along its strands. Each consistent connected loop contributes
2; an odd-parity loop contributes 0. Dropping this parity check on a general
schedule is invalid. All selectors have nonnegative entries, so Y>=0.

THEOREM

For every k>=1 there is a connected, legal, 2k-qubit path instance, with
2k-1 gates and strictly positive polynomial-bit rational weights, such that

  E[Y^2]/Z^2 >= exp(-0.02) (189/169)^k.

Nevertheless its Z is computable exactly by O(k) rational arithmetic with
polynomial bit lengths. Therefore the variance obstruction concerns this
estimator alone. It is not evidence of counting hardness or a universal
barrier to relative approximation.

PROOF

Put k pair gates F(4,4,1) on edges (0,1),(2,3),...,(2k-2,2k-1).
Put k-1 bridge gates F(1,epsilon,epsilon) on (1,2),(3,4),...,(2k-3,2k-2),
where epsilon=1/(100k). Apply each edge once, in any chronological order.
The interaction graph is a connected path.

The partial trace over either endpoint of F is (2a+c+d)I: the corresponding
partial traces of I,C,D are respectively 2I,I,I. On ANY interaction tree
with one gate per edge, a leaf qubit participates in only one gate. Tracing
it out replaces that gate by a scalar identity on its neighbor, independent
of the chronological interleaving. Iterate leaf removal. The final qubit
has trace 2, giving the exact identity

  Z=2 product_edges (2a_e+c_e+d_e).

Our path therefore has Z=26^k(1+epsilon)^(k-1).

For one pair gate, Tr(I)=4 and Tr(C)=Tr(D)=2, so the estimator is 36 with
probability 4/9 and 18 with probability 5/9. Its mean is 26 and its second
moment is 756, hence its relative second moment is 756/676=189/169.

Let A be the event that every bridge selects I. Its probability is
(1+2epsilon)^-(k-1). Conditional on A, bridge operators are identities,
the pair traces are independent, and Y equals (1+2epsilon)^(k-1) times
the product of the k pair estimators. Keeping just this nonnegative
contribution to E[Y^2] yields

  E[Y^2]/Z^2 >= (189/169)^k
                 [(1+2epsilon)/(1+epsilon)^2]^(k-1).

Since log(1+epsilon)<=epsilon and 1+2epsilon>=1, the bracketed factor to
power k-1 is at least exp(-2epsilon(k-1))>=exp(-0.02). This proves the bound.
Relative variance equals the relative second moment minus one.

The exponentially growing second moment defeats the usual variance-based
FPRAS argument for this iid estimator. A second moment alone is not a proof
that every possible estimator or even every differently analyzed procedure
requires exponential time. The exact tree baseline explicitly succeeds.

WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED

An estimator using exact local conditional sums may remove this witness.
But on arbitrary networks no polynomial acquisition/normalization/variance
bound has been proved. A physical interaction tree must also be distinguished
from an acyclic factor graph on individual binary wire variables: periodic
wires can create factor-graph cycles even when the interaction graph is a
path. The leaf-partial-trace proof above is an operator proof and does not
need the latter graph to be acyclic.

work/tests/R4_24_connected_loop_check.py verifies the exact formulas by all
3^(2k-1) selector assignments for k=1,2,3,4, and their reversed chronological
orders. It also compares the parity calculation with dense matrix traces
on all 27 selector words around a nonbipartite triangle: four traces are
zero, confirming that the parity indicator cannot simply be omitted.
These are finite diagnostics supplementing the all-k proof. Novelty has
not been established, and the result does not pass the foundational gate.

6. COMPLEX LEE-YANG SPECTRAL AND FINITE-PRODUCT BOUNDS
==========================================================================

COMPLEX LEE–YANG TRANSFER: SPECTRAL SEPARATION AND FINITE-PRODUCT COLLAPSE
10 October 2026. Internally reconstructed scoped result; novelty unestablished.
This does NOT pass the foundational-discovery gate.

Provenance and imports
----------------------
The PSD spectral-ratio theorem and its squeezing/Schwarz mechanism are due to
Bravyi, Gosset, Liu and Wong, arXiv:2607.14401v1 (15 July 2026):
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14401v1
Their quantum ground-energy algorithm is prior work, not our discovery.
We extend the stated PSD theorem to complex matrices, and give a quantitative
finite-product norm consequence. The central squeezing mechanism is imported
and adapted, not claimed as a new general principle. Complex projective
contraction has older antecedents, including Rugh (2010),
https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2010.171.1707 .

For a tensor v with n binary indices set f_v(z)=sum_x v_x z^x. Write LY(r)
if f_v has no zeros when every |z_i|<r. A matrix K on n qubits is flattened
with f_K(z,w)=sum_xy K_xy z^x w^y. No conjugation is used in contractions.
Here D_a denotes the open n-dimensional polydisk of radius a.

We import two standard facts, stated precisely as Facts 1–2 in the source:
(i) contracting two binary tensor indices preserves zero-freeness in the
remaining radii when the product of the contracted radii exceeds 1;
(ii) a nonzero coefficientwise limit of tensors in LY(r) is in LY(r).
Tensor products and nonzero scalar multiples preserve LY. Thus K in LY(r)
maps any vector in LY(s) to a NONZERO vector in LY(r) if rs>1. Products of
matrices in LY(r), r>1, remain in LY(r); their (0,0) entries are nonzero.
These facts include cancellation control; positivity is not being assumed.

THEOREM A — arbitrary complex spectrum
---------------------------------------
Let n>=1, r>1, and let K be ANY complex 2^n by 2^n matrix in LY(r).
It has a unique eigenvalue lambda_* of largest modulus, that eigenvalue is
nonzero and algebraically simple, and every other eigenvalue satisfies

                  |lambda/lambda_*| <= r^(-2).

Both factors of its rank-one spectral projector are LY(r), with the left
factor interpreted as a bilinear covector. No PSD, normality, diagonalizability,
or sign condition on K is needed. The ratio is sharp already for n=1:
diag(1,r^(-2)) has polynomial 1+r^(-2)zw and lies in LY(r).

Proof, conditional comparison. Suppose first K psi=lambda_* psi with
psi in LY(r) and lambda_*!=0. For an independent eigenvector phi, put
alpha=lambda/lambda_* and g=f_phi/f_psi, a nonconstant holomorphic function
on D_r. For alpha!=0 and 1/r<s<u<r, every value alpha*g(v), v in D_u,
belongs to g(D_s). Otherwise eta=phi-alpha*g(v)*psi is in LY(s), while
K eta=lambda_* alpha*(phi-g(v)*psi) vanishes at v, contradicting the
nonzero LY(r) contraction property. Consequently, writing Delta(a) for
the diameter of g(D_a), |alpha| Delta(u)<=Delta(s).

For any holomorphic h on D_r and 0<s<u<r,

                    diam h(D_s) <= (s/u) diam h(D_u).

To prove this, take any z,w in the closed radius-s polydisk and apply the
one-variable Schwarz lemma to
  F(zeta)=[h((u/s)z zeta)-h((u/s)w zeta)] / diam h(D_u).
It maps the open unit disk into the closed unit disk and F(0)=0; evaluate
at zeta=s/u. Diameter zero is immediate. Diameters are finite because
u<r. This gives |alpha|<=s/u; send s down to 1/r and u up to r. Eigenvalue
zero is trivial. This proves the comparison once the LY eigenvector exists.

Acquisition without PSD. Fix 1<R<r. The polynomial f_K has a positive
minimum modulus on the compact closed radius-R polydisk. Hence a coefficient
neighborhood of K lies in LY(R). Within it choose K_j tending to K with
simple spectrum and pairwise distinct eigenvalue moduli. Such matrices are
dense: perturb to simple spectrum, then perturb the diagonal eigenvalues
in a diagonalizing basis to remove the finitely many modulus equalities.

Each K_j has a unique simple modulus-leading eigenvalue lambda_j!=0.
Indeed trace K_j!=0: contract all input-output pairs, whose radii multiply
to R^2>1. Therefore lambda_j^(-m) K_j^m tends to a nonzero rank-one spectral
projector P_j. Each normalized power is in LY(R); hence so is P_j.
Writing P_j=v_j w_j^T, its polynomial factors as f_vj(z)f_wj(w).
The nonvanishing of this product implies v_j,w_j in LY(R).
The conditional argument applies and bounds the second eigenvalue modulus,
counting algebraic multiplicities, by R^(-2) times the first.

Eigenvalue multisets, and their sorted moduli, vary continuously with matrix
entries. Passing to K gives rho_2(K)<=R^(-2)rho_1(K). Closing K itself to
its trace gives trace K!=0, so rho_1(K)>0. Let R increase to r. The strict
ratio excludes both a tie and any leading Jordan block. Normalized powers
of K now converge to its nonzero rank-one projector P. Limit closure puts
P in LY(r), and factorization gives both LY factors. QED.

THEOREM B — uniform finite-product norm estimate
-------------------------------------------------
Let K_1,...,K_T be arbitrary complex n-qubit matrices, EACH in LY(r) for
the same r>1. Put M=K_T...K_1, e=|0^n>, a=e^T M e!=0, and

                        B=(M e)(e^T M)/a.

Then B has rank one, and, with ||.||_F the Frobenius norm and ||.||_2 the
operator norm,

 ||M-B||_F <= 2(1+r^2)^(n/2) r^(-2T) ||M e||_2
           <= 2(1+r^2)^(n/2) r^(-2T) ||M||_2.             (B)

In particular relative operator-norm error at most epsilon is ensured by

 T >= [log(2/epsilon)+(n/2)log(1+r^2)]/[2 log r].

For r=1+delta near 1 this is O((n+log(1/epsilon))/delta), not exponential
mixing time. The exponential prefactor is paid inside a logarithm. There is
no assumption of a common eigenbasis, a common limiting direction, or a
fixed operator; B depends on the entire product.

Proof. Let psi_0=e, phi_0 be arbitrary, and propagate both under K_t.
All psi_t are LY(r); phi_t may vanish and need NOT be LY. Set
g_t=f_phi_t/f_psi_t, holomorphic on D_r. Choose 1/r<s<u<r with u>1.
For any v in D_u put c=g_t(v). If c is not in g_(t-1)(D_s), then
eta=phi_(t-1)-c psi_(t-1) has nonvanishing polynomial
f_psi_(t-1)*(g_(t-1)-c), so is LY(s). Its image under K_t must be LY(r),
but vanishes at v. Contradiction. This includes c=0 and constant ratios.
Thus g_t(D_u) is contained in g_(t-1)(D_s). The Schwarz diameter bound
above implies Delta_t(u)<=(s/u) Delta_(t-1)(u).

Apply this separately for each phi_0=e_y. Then g_0(z)=z^y and
Delta_0(u)<=2u^|y| (the y=0 diameter is actually zero). Hence

 |g_T(z)-g_T(0)| <= 2u^|y| (s/u)^T,          z in D_u.

The polynomial of the yth column of M-B equals
f_(M e)(z) [g_T(z)-g_T(0)]. On the unit torus (inside D_u), multiply the
uniform ratio bound by |f_(M e)|, square, and integrate. Parseval gives

 ||(M-B)e_y||_2 <= 2u^|y| (s/u)^T ||M e||_2.

Square and sum over y. Since sum_y u^(2|y|)=(1+u^2)^n, this yields
||M-B||_F <= 2(1+u^2)^(n/2)(s/u)^T||M e||_2.
Now send s down to 1/r and u up to r. All right-side constants converge;
the fixed left side is independent of the radii, proving (B). QED.

A second, shorter proof of Theorem A from Theorem B
---------------------------------------------------
This provides a route that avoids generic perturbation entirely. Apply (B)
to m repeated copies of K. The best rank-one approximation inequality gives

 sigma_2(K^m)/sigma_1(K^m) <= C r^(-2m),
                  C=2(1+r^2)^(n/2).

Here m is a positive integer power, not a transpose. Trace contraction gives
rho(K)>0. The exterior-square operator satisfies
||exterior^2(K^m)||_2=sigma_1(K^m)sigma_2(K^m) and
exterior^2(K^m)=(exterior^2 K)^m. By the matrix spectral-radius formula,

 lim_m [sigma_2(K^m)/sigma_1(K^m)]^(1/m)
     = rho(exterior^2 K)/rho(K)^2
     = |lambda_2|/|lambda_1|.

The eigenvalues of exterior^2 K are lambda_i lambda_j, i<j, with algebraic
multiplicity; this also holds for non-diagonalizable matrices by triangular
form. Thus the limit is valid even if the exterior-square spectral radius is
zero. Taking mth roots of the bound proves the ratio <=r^(-2). Counting
algebraic multiplicity again excludes a leading tie or Jordan block. The
projector/eigenvector conclusions then follow from normalized powers and LY
limit closure as above. This second proof uses standard singular-value and
exterior-algebra identities in addition to the stated tensor-closure facts.

COMPUTATIONAL LIMIT AND GATE VERDICT
-------------------------------------
Theorems A–B assume zero-freeness of the whole n-qubit matrix. They do not
recognize it from arbitrary local input, compute M e or e^T M, extract a
partition function, or implement a nonunitary product at polynomial cost.
Even a normalized one-qubit rank-one projector onto
chi=(|0>+a|1>)/sqrt(1+a^2), 0<a<1/r, has flattened polynomial
(1+az)(1+aw)/(1+a^2). Its n-fold tensor power lies in LY(r), is already
rank one, yet a direct successful application to |0^n> occurs with probability
(1+a^2)^(-n). The state itself is easy to prepare by rotations: this is a
counterexample to inferring efficient generic postselection, NOT a hardness
claim for every implementation or an unknown state-preparation problem.

Identity on an idle qubit has polynomial 1+zw and LY radius exactly 1.
Padding a local biased gate with idle identities therefore does not satisfy
the whole-layer r>1 hypothesis. Costs/conditions must be proved on every wire.
Low global operator rank also gives no low spatial entanglement of its factors.

Three Luna audits support these derivations internally. The ingredients
closely extend the source's existing mechanism. The exact scope may be a
useful corollary, but priority, novelty, and a consequential new algorithm
remain unestablished. No Sol depth team is justified by this result alone.

ALL-COPY LOCAL MINIMA AND TWO OBSTRUCTIONS TO GLOBAL EXTENSION

Independent internal audits: work/round9/21_alln_audit.txt and 23_alln.txt.

DEFINITIONS AND GLOBAL TARGET

For C in M_(3^n)(C), let
  q_n(C) = sum_(S subset [n]) (-1/2)^|S| ||Tr_S C||_F^2.
Write L(A)=A-(Tr A)I_3/2. Then q_n(C)=<C,L^tensor n(C)>_HS.
The unresolved global target is q_n(C)>=0 for every n and every rank(C)<=2.
Two-copy positivity is already a July 2026 source theorem, not our discovery:

1. COMPLETE LOCAL THEOREM

For every fixed n>=1, set D=2^n, x=0^n, y=1^n, s=(-1)^(n+1), and
  C0=|x><x|+s|y><y|.
There is a neighborhood of C0 within the complex rank-at-most-two matrices
on which q_n is nonnegative. Its zero set in a sufficiently small such
neighborhood is a smooth real manifold of dimension 4D-6+4n. All of these
zeroes are critical points of q_n restricted to rank two. The real Hessian
has that nullity and is strictly positive in normal directions.

The radius is not uniform in n; the theorem says nothing about matrices
outside these neighborhoods. We prove it on rank exactly two. This suffices
because C0 has two nonzero singular values and is separated from rank<=1.

Binary zero manifold.
Embed B=(C^2)^tensor n as the 0/1 coordinates. For C supported on B, let
F=Pi^tensor n, where Pi(A)=A-(Tr A)I_2/2 is the orthogonal projection onto
traceless 2-by-2 matrices. Compression of L to this block equals Pi, hence
  q_n(C)=<C,F(C)>=||F(C)||_F^2.
Put epsilon=[[0,1],[-1,0]], J=epsilon^tensor n, and theta(C)=J C^T J^(-1).
This is a complex-linear Hilbert-Schmidt isometric involution. Locally it
fixes I_2 and negates the traceless subspace, so
  F theta = (-1)^n F.
Since J^T=(-1)^n J, theta(C)=sC is equivalent to (CJ)^T=-CJ.
Every rank-two matrix in this spin-flip eigenspace therefore has q_n=0.
Nonzero rank-two complex skew matrices are the decomposable bivectors
uv^T-vu^T. They form a smooth complex manifold of dimension
2(D-2)+1=2D-3. Right multiplication by J^(-1) preserves rank. This gives
a smooth binary zero manifold through C0 of real dimension 4D-6.

At each site also vary the common two-plane in C^3. Local unitary conjugacy
preserves q_n, so isometric embeddings of the binary zero manifold remain
zeroes. Gr(2,3) has real dimension 4, giving another 4n parameters.
Fix smooth local orthonormal frames for these planes to remove frame gauge.
At C0, binary fiber variations stay in B-by-B, whereas plane variations
have a single 2 in a row or column next to the corresponding x or y endpoint.
These 4n real variations are independent and disjoint from the binary ones.
Thus the parameterization is an immersion and is an embedded manifold on
a sufficiently small patch, of dimension 4D-6+4n.

Stationarity of the entire zero manifold.
For any binary-supported rank-two C with F(C)=0, the B-by-B compression of
L^tensor n(C) vanishes. Every other nonzero entry has a 2 in BOTH its row
and column at some common site: L introduces 2 only through a diagonal
term proportional to E_22. Thus L^tensor n(C) has no row or column in B.
Every rank-two tangent has the form A V*+U B* when C=U V*, and hence has
its column support or its row support in B. Its inner product with
L^tensor n(C) is zero. The derivative of q_n in every tangent direction
is consequently zero. Local unitary conjugacy proves the same for every
member of the moving-plane family. This full stationarity statement is
needed: a positive semidefinite Hessian at C0 alone would not suffice.

Full Hessian computation.
Order W=(x,y) first and its complement second. Set Gamma=diag(1,s).
A complete rank-two chart near C0 is
  C(X,Y,Z) = [[Gamma+X, Y], [Z, Z(Gamma+X)^(-1)Y]],
with X 2-by-2, Y 2-by-(3^n-2), and Z (3^n-2)-by-2, all complex.
Its tangent H has all entries in a W row or W column. There are 4*3^n-4
complex coordinates. Let h_z=(L^tensor n(C0))_(z,z). The quadratic Taylor
coefficient (half the Hessian quadratic form) is exactly
  Q(H)=<H,L^tensor n(H)>
       +2 Re sum_(z outside W,w in W) (h_z/gamma_w) Z_(z,w)Y_(w,z),
where gamma_x=1, gamma_y=s. This follows by expanding the lower-right
block to second order and polarizing the ambient quadratic form.

If z has j digits equal to 1 and ell digits equal to 2, direct application
of L to E_xx and E_yy gives
  h_z=2^(-n)(-1)^(j+ell) [1-(-1)^ell].
In particular h_z=0 for binary z and for even ell.

On the binary tangent block Q=||F(H)||^2. Its kernel has complex dimension
2D-3. Here is an explicit verification, not just a dimension assumption.
For a binary matrix unit the local images are Z_Pauli/2, -Z_Pauli/2,
E_01, E_10. Among units having a row or column in {x,y}, the resulting
tensor labels coincide exactly in spin-flip orbits. The two diagonal units
form one orbit. E_xy and E_yx are each singleton orbits. The other 4(D-2)
units form 2(D-2) paired orbits under (r,c)->(complement c,complement r).
Distinct orbit images are orthogonal. On each paired orbit the Gram block
is a positive multiple of [[1,+/-1],[+/-1,1]], with one complex kernel
direction; each singleton has positive norm. The kernel dimension is thus
1+2(D-2)=2D-3, and the kernel is precisely the theta=s tangent eigenspace.

For nonbinary z, the two coordinates b=H_(w,z), d=H_(z,w) form an isolated
block
  Q_(w,z)=g_w(z)(|b|^2+|d|^2)+2 Re[(h_z/gamma_w)bd],
  g_w(z)=2^(-m_w(z)), m_w(z)=number of digits z_i equal to w_i.
There are no cross terms between different outside pairs or with the binary
block. A site carrying 2 gives an off-diagonal unit whose ordered pair L
fixes; this also fixes its endpoint and orientation. All remaining diagonal
positions must agree with the fixed endpoint, which gives the stated g.

For odd ell the ratios are
  |h_z|/g_x(z)=2^(1-ell-j)<=1,
  |h_z|/g_y(z)=2^(1-n+j)<=1;
for even ell they vanish. The real and imaginary two-variable blocks have
eigenvalues g_w(z)+/-h_z/gamma_w. All are nonnegative. Equality occurs only
when z has exactly one 2 and all other digits equal to the endpoint w.
There are 2n such pairs, each with real kernel dimension 2. Hence the
outside kernel has real dimension 4n. The full Hessian nullity is
  2(2D-3)+4n=4D-6+4n,
and all its other eigenvalues are positive.

Completion of the local proof, including all higher orders.
The constructed smooth manifold consists of critical zeroes. Its tangent
therefore lies in the Hessian kernel. The dimensions just computed agree,
so the tangent equals the kernel and the Hessian is positive on a normal
complement. Choose smooth adapted local coordinates (p,z) with the zero
manifold z=0. For f=q_n in these coordinates, f(p,0)=0 and df(p,0)=0.
At the origin the z-Hessian is positive definite. By continuity it remains
bounded below by c I for some c>0 on a smaller coordinate neighborhood.
Taylor's integral formula in z gives f(p,z)>=c||z||^2/2 there. Thus local
nonnegativity and the precise local zero set follow, with no unproved
inference from Hessian positivity or tests of individual kernel rays.

Spin-flip/antistate cancellation of full odd-qubit correlations is prior
work, e.g. Tran et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03385 . The operator
calculation here is provided explicitly; it is not claimed as an invented
spin-flip mechanism. Prior Hessian approaches also occur in section VI of
https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18037v1 (a different rank-one parametrization of
a two-copy Cauchy inequality). Nonproduct equality cases and earlier local
minimum work are discussed in https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.2613 , section III.
No exhaustive novelty comparison is claimed.

2. EXACT LIFT: WHY UNIFORM LOCAL STABILITY CAN HIDE THE ORIGINAL PROBLEM

Let X have rank<=2 on m qutrits. Choose vectors a,v,u,b such that
X=a v*+u b*, and put R=u v*. For real t define the three-by-three block matrix
  C(t)=[[R,tX,0],[0,R,0],[0,0,0]].
It has rank at most two for every t, because it factors as
  [[u,t a],[0,u],[0,0]] times [[v*,t b*,0],[0,v*,0]].
Its first-site diagonal blocks sum to 2R. Expanding only that site's L gives
  q_(m+1)(C(t)) = 2 q_m(R)+t^2 q_m(X) - (1/2)q_m(2R)
                = t^2 q_m(X).
For a nontrivial rank-two X the representation may be chosen with u,v nonzero,
so C(0)=diag(R,R,0) is a rank-two zero. Consequently nonnegative Hessian
at ALL such zeroes, for arbitrary entangled u,v, already implies the full
lower-copy target. This proposed local-to-global route is not a free lemma.
The identity is exact and does not presume positivity of q_m.

Nor do local unitary orbits of the binary family cover all critical zeroes.
For n=3 take R=|Phi><Phi| with Phi=(|00>+|11>+|22>)/sqrt(3), and C=P tensor R,
P=diag(1,1,0). Then rank C=2, q_3(C)=q_1(P)q_2(R)=0, and
L^tensor3(C)=-E_22 tensor L^tensor2(R), orthogonal to every rank-two tangent
at C. This is a critical zero with local range support dimensions (2,3,3).
Every member of the moving-binary-plane family has local supports at most 2.
Local unitaries preserve these dimensions, so an orbit reduction cannot
cover this example. It is not a saddle certificate.

3. FAILURE OF A DIRECT MULTICOPY SWAP-SECTOR EXTENSION

For n>=2 let u=0^n, v=1^n in the binary subspace, and let F_i swap the two
copies of the ith site. Put Pplus=product_i(I+F_i)/2 and
Peven=(I+product_i F_i)/2. Let
  z0=u tensor v+v tensor u,
  z=(Peven-Pplus)z0, b=2^(1-n).
All terms of Pplus z0 have the same coefficient b and complementary binary
strings in the two tensor factors. The flattened symmetric matrix Z thus
has antidiagonal entries 1-b on u<->v and -b on every other complement pair.
Its two largest squared singular values sum to 2(1-b)^2, while
  ||Z||_F^2=2(1-b)^2+(2^n-2)b^2=2(1-b).
The ratio is 1-b. For n>=3 this is greater than 1/2 and tends to 1.
Thus the two-copy half-concentration estimate used for the double-
antisymmetric sector cannot hold for the union of all nontrivial even
swap sectors. This Z has high rank; it is NOT a negative rank-two witness
for q_n. A different certificate might still succeed.

MISSION GATE

The local theorem resolves all orders near one explicitly described family.
It supplies neither global control of the rank-two variety nor a negative
rank-two matrix. The exact lift and sector example expose two tempting
extensions as insufficient. No foundational candidate has passed; do not
deploy the five-Sol depth team on this scoped result alone.
