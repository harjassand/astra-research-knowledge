## Exact interface and theorem

Let n>=1, source coordinates be `(B,tau)` with n−1 base coordinates, and let

`G inC[B,tau]^n`, `detJG=kappa inC*`.

G may be ANY Keller map, including the published F after any global polynomial source automorphism or any verified Keller target transformation. Let `Q inC[B,tau]` be an arbitrary polynomial repair. Introduce n dual variables Z and the potential

`L(B,tau,Z)=Z·G(B,tau)+Q(B,tau)`.              (1)

For every Q, the full2n-dimensional Hessian is

`[[Z·HessG+HessQ, JG^T],[JG,0]]`,

so `detHessL=(-1)^n kappa²` is a nonzero constant. This load-bearing source premise is automatically satisfied in the whole repair class, including nonquadratic and base-dependent repairs. It does not by itself give a polynomial elimination.

**Theorem.** For a fixed scalar level sigma, there exists a global polynomial section `tau=s(B,Z)` of

`Z·G_tau(B,s)+Q_tau(B,s)=sigma`                (2)

if and only if

`G(B,tau)=h(B)+tau g(B)`,
`Q_tau(B,tau)=q1 tau+q0(B)` with `q1 inC*`.    (3)

In that case the unique section is

`s=(sigma−Z·g−q0(B))/q1`.                     (4)

Thus a section polynomial at even ONE fixed level forces a constant nonzero scalar pivot and a section jointly polynomial in all levels. No higher-degree repair in the source coordinates can compensate for nonlinear dependence of a Keller map on the eliminated coordinate.

The normalization `2Z·G` used for the published potential replaces Z·g by2Z·g and multiplies the full determinant by2^(2n); it changes no degree argument or necessity. These statements are about repairs independent of the dual variables. General dual-dependent repairs, direct odd-dimensional charts and cancellation between nonlinear stages are outside the theorem. Rational or algebraic sections whose ACTUAL selected critical value is polynomial are treated in the additional criteria below; rational or algebraic sections alone are not excluded.

## One-dimensional case and exclusion of dual-independent sections

When n=1, the Keller condition immediately gives G_tau=kappa, so G is affine. If s were independent of Z, equation(2) would have a nonzero Z coefficientkappa, impossible. For m=deg_Z s>=1 and e=deg_tau Q_tau, the identity `kappa Z+Q_tau(s)=sigma` has degrees1 and em; it forces e=m=1. Both coefficients are scalars, and q1!=0, giving(3)–(4). This handles n1 without a transverse-variable argument.

Now let n>=2 and work over the base fraction field K=C(B). If s is independent of Z, comparing coefficients in(2) forces `G_tau(B,s(B))=0`. Evaluating the unit Keller determinant at the same section would give a zero column and zero determinant. Hence

`m=deg_Z s>=1`.                              (5)

This comparison is valid over K as well, and the original section lies in the global polynomial ring C[B,Z].

## Nonlinear dependence cannot be repaired

Suppose for contradiction that `d=deg_tau G_tau>=1`. Its top coefficient is a nonzero vector a_d(B) over K. If Q_tau is zero or has degreee<=d, the highest total Z degree in(2) cannot cancel. In general its two possible leading degrees are

`md+1` for `Z·G_tau(B,s)`,
`me` for `Q_tau(B,s)`.

The leading terms are nonzero: `(Z·a_d)s_m^d` and the nonzero top coefficient of Q_tau times`s_m^e`, in the polynomial domain K[Z]. Lower tau terms have strictly smaller degrees. Therefore cancellation in(2) necessarily gives

`m(e−d)=1`.

The degrees are integers, so

`m=1`, `e=d+1`.                              (6)

Write the affine section `s=s0+ell·Z`, where ell is a nonzero vector over K. Use an invertible K-linear change of dual coordinates whose first coordinate is `U=ell·Z`, and whose other n−1 directions span the kernel of ell. The right side `Q_tau(B,s0+U)` depends only on U. Comparing the coefficients of every transverse dual coordinate forces

`G_tau(B,tau)=r(tau) ell`                     (7)

for some r inK[tau]. This is a polynomial identity because U is an independent variable and tau=s0+U ranges symbolically over K[tau]. It is not inferred from a single critical point or fibre.

Since ell is independent of tau, deg_tau r=d>=1. But the tau column of JG inK[tau] has the nonconstant common factor r. Thus r divides detJG=kappa, which is a nonzero unit of K[tau]. This contradiction excludes d>=1. Consequently G_tau=g(B), and G is affine in tau. Its derivative vector is not zero because the Keller determinant is a unit.

## Global polynomiality forces constant repair curvature

With d0, the first summand of(2) has Z degree1. The repair must have e>=1, and its degree after substituting s is em. Therefore `em=1`, so e=m=1. Write

`Q_tau=q1(B)tau+q0(B)`, with q1!=0.

The section over K is precisely(4). For it to belong to C[B,Z], each coefficient of Z_i implies that q1 divides g_i in C[B]. The column g is UNIMODULAR over this base ring: evaluate the cofactor identity for the unit Jacobian JG at tau=0 to obtain a polynomial row r0(B) satisfying `r0·g=1`. Hence q1 divides1 and is a nonzero constant. The scalar constant/base-term division in(4) creates no further restriction once q1 is a unit.

Conversely conditions(3) give the explicit global polynomial section(4). This proves necessity and sufficiency, including global divisibility rather than only a fraction-field inverse.

## Rational sections with polynomial selected critical values

**Additional theorem.** Suppose a rational section `s inC(B,Z)` satisfies(2), and its actual selected critical value

`E(B,Z)=L(B,s(B,Z),Z)−sigma s(B,Z)`

belongs to the GLOBAL polynomial ring C[B,Z]. Then conditions(3) hold and s is the global polynomial section(4). Thus rational envelopes with polynomial critical values cannot escape the criterion.

Differentiate the rational identity using the critical equation. The exact envelope derivative is

`E_(Z_i)=G_i(B,s)`.

All these compositions are polynomial in B,Z because E is polynomial. Over K=C(B), at least one G_i is a positive-degree polynomial in tau; otherwise the Keller tau column would vanish. Write s=A/D in lowest terms in K[Z]. If an irreducible polynomialp divides D, its p-valuation on s is negative. The leading tau term of that G_i evaluated at s has the uniquely most negative valuation, since all its nonzero coefficients belong to K* and have p-valuation0. It cannot cancel with a lower tau term. Hence G_i(B,s) has a pole along p, contradicting polynomiality. Therefore D is a unit and `s inK[Z]`.

The preceding section proof over K now applies before its final global divisibility step: it forces G affine in tau and `Q_tau=q1(B)tau+q0(B)` with q1 a nonzero polynomial in C[B]. At this point q1 need not yet be a unit, and s is only known to be polynomial over K. Write `Q=q1 tau²/2+q0 tau+Q0(B)`. Direct stationary substitution gives

`E=Z·h+Q0−(Z·g+q0−sigma)²/(2q1)`.             (9)

Its coefficient of Z_i² is `−g_i²/(2q1)`. Since E is globally polynomial, q1 divides every g_i² in the UFD C[B]. If q1 had an irreducible factor, that factor would divide every g_i, contradicting the already proved unimodularity of the Keller tau column. Thus q1 is a unit, necessarily a nonzero constant. Formula(4) is then globally polynomial. This argument also works for n1, where the base ring is simply C.

The polynomial critical-value premise is essential. A sharp control is `G(B,tau)=(B,tau)`, `Q=B tau²/2`. Its doubled full Hessian determinant is1, and at level sigma it has the rational section `s=(sigma−Z2)/B`. But its selected critical value is `BZ1−(Z2−sigma)²/(2B)`, which is not polynomial; its reduced rational Hessian determinant is1/B. This retains the rational possibility outside the theorem rather than claiming rational sections never exist. The control is a Keller automorphism and has no retained collision; it is not an HC4 counterexample.

## Polynomial curve fields over a nonclosed base field

The algebraic-branch extension needs the following OLD prerequisite, not a new theorem of this programme: an intermediate field of K(T) containing a nonconstant polynomial admits a polynomial generator. Ordinary Lüroth over arbitrary K is stated in de Bondt arXiv1501.06046v4, Theorem1.1; the polynomial-generator refinement is explicitly discussed and proved in Gutierrez–Rubio–Sevilla arXiv0805.2338v1, Section2 Algorithm2 and its correctness proof, printedpp6–7. Both have arbitrary-field conventions. Only the univariate form is needed here. Algorithmic runtime claims are not adopted.

For completeness, the necessary refinement has an independent pole proof which preserves the nonclosed-K issue. Let `K subset E subset K(T)`, with E containing a nonconstant polynomial f(T). Ordinary Lüroth gives `E=K(r(T))` for rational r over K. Write f=F(r), with nonconstant rational F over K. Pass to an algebraic closure ONLY to discuss geometric poles and fibres. The rational map r:P¹_T→P¹_r is nonconstant, and every geometric point has a nonempty fibre. At a pole beta of F, every point in r^(-1)(beta) is a pole of f, with pole order multiplied by the positive local mapping degree. Since f is polynomial, that entire fibre is contained in the single point T=infinity. Therefore every pole of F is the same point

`beta=r(infinity) inK union {infinity}`.

The pole is K-rational because r has coefficients in K and infinity is a K-rational point. Choose a Möbius transformation over K sending beta to infinity, and put h=M(r). The only pole of h is at T=infinity, so `h inK[T]`. Moreover `E=K(h)`.

If any polynomial g(T) in E is written `g=H(h)` with H rational over K, H cannot have a finite geometric pole: its fibre under the nonconstant polynomial h is nonempty and lies at finite T, where g cannot have a pole. Thus `H inK[U]`. This proves both a polynomial generator and POLYNOMIAL outer compositions over the actual K, without assuming K algebraically closed or importing a multivariate field-generation theorem. The Möbius change is a fraction-field proof device; it is not an asserted global polynomial source chart.

Apply this to `E=K(G_1(T),...,G_n(T))`, with K=C(B). The Keller cofactor identity makes the derivative column `(G_1'(T),...,G_n'(T))` unimodular in K[T]. The lemma gives `G_i=P_i(h)` with h polynomial. Its derivative h' divides every G_i', so h' is a unit of K[T]. In characteristic zero h is affine and nonconstant. Consequently

`K(G_1(T),...,G_n(T))=K(T)`.                   (10)

This is a birational statement for the ONE-dimensional curve with B fixed generically. It does not assert a rational or polynomial inverse for the full n-dimensional Keller map G.

## Algebraic branches with polynomial selected critical values

**Further theorem, independently reconstructed.** Let s be ANY algebraic element over K(Z), satisfying(2), and suppose its actual selected critical value E belongs to C[B,Z]. Then s is the global polynomial section(4), and conditions(3) hold. Thus allowing an algebraic critical branch does not enlarge the polynomial critical-value class.

First s cannot be algebraic over K. If it were, the free dual variables Z would still be algebraically independent over K(s). Coefficient comparison in(2) would force every `G_i'(s)=0`, contradicting the Keller cofactor identity evaluated at s. Therefore s is transcendental over K.

By(10), there is rational recovery

`T=A(G_1(T),...,G_n(T))/D(G_1(T),...,G_n(T))`,

with A,D polynomials over K and `D(G(T))` a NONZERO polynomial in T. Because s is transcendental over K, substitution T→s is injective on K[T]; its recovery denominator `D(G(s))` is therefore nonzero. This explicitly handles the possible bad inverse chart instead of assuming its denominator survives on every target fibre.

Characteristic zero makes the algebraic extension separable, so each dual derivation extends to K(Z,s). Differentiating the critical-value identity and using(2) gives the same envelope formula

`G_i(B,s)=E_(Z_i) inK[Z]`.

Substitution into the rational recovery places s in K(Z). The already proved rational-section criterion then makes q1 a global constant unit and s globally polynomial. Since the remaining scalar equation is linear with nonzero constant coefficient, this canonical section is unique; an algebraic branch cannot hide another polynomial envelope within this interface.

No assumption about connected fibres, generic injectivity of the entire Keller map, or a globally polynomial source inverse was used. The only field-generation prerequisite is the old univariate polynomial Lüroth refinement, explicitly sourced and independently reconstructed above. An algebraic section with NONPOLYNOMIAL critical value remains outside this conclusion.

## Reduced Hessian and collision obligations

Under(3), the eliminated corner is `L_tau_tau=q1 inC*`. The full Hessian determinant is already constant for every Q. The exact Hessian chain rule for the critical value and the Schur identity therefore give the reduced determinant

`detHess(critical value)=(-1)^n kappa²/q1`.

At a nonzero level sigma, the reduced critical value is that of `L−sigma tau`; subtracting the level term is necessary for the envelope derivative. The theorem does not discard this distinction.

The actual map G has a collision after any source automorphism, but transporting it to a gradient collision of the critical value requires its points to lie on the SAME selected graph and requires the repaired source-gradient outputs to agree. For arbitrary Q neither condition is automatic. For the published F, the cycle17 unique-ruling theorem additionally forces any global three-dimensional source automorphism satisfying(3) to preserve the old base and scale/translate the old pivot. Thus general source-only repairs cannot obtain a new polynomial ruling; base-dependent repair terms still have their own collision-gradient condition.
