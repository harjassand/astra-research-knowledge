## 1. Exact geometric access and original-face identity

Use `Q=2P-1`, `x0=2y-1`, fixed rational `c`, and
`gamma=c^T x0`. All "original input" bounds refer to this favorable-cost
call, including its queried cost. A later extraction query may use another
cost with its own charged bit length.

A Boolean polytope has an integer inequality/affine-equation description
with every coefficient and constant bounded by `Cf=(2n)^n`. One independent
proof chooses `d=dim Q` coordinate projections forming an affine
isomorphism on `aff Q`. Projected vertices are still signed Boolean.
Facet normals/constants are cofactors of the signed-vertex/ones matrices;
Hadamard bounds them by `d^(d/2)`. Affine equations for remaining
coordinates come from a `(d+1)`-square signed-vertex/ones matrix and have
bound `(d+1)^((d+1)/2)`. These are below `Cf`. The singleton case is
trivial. No facet enumeration is required by this existence bound.

For a current point `x=X/Qx`, each original inequality strict at `x` has
slack at least `1/Qx`. With `epsilon=1/(2Qx n Cf)`, its slack survives
both displacements `+/-epsilon h` whenever `||h||_infinity<=1`.
Tight inequalities force `a^T h=0`. Therefore

`{h in [-1,1]^n : x+epsilon h in Q, x-epsilon h in Q}`

`= lin(F(x)-F(x)) intersect [-1,1]^n`.

This independently certifies the tangent-box identity used in the ledger.
Exact span expansion via maximizing/minimizing rational objectives
orthogonal to the currently obtained span takes at most `n` additions and
`O(n^2)` LPs. Require vertex/basic outputs with bounded bits, rather than
an arbitrary gratuitously large rational optimum. The actual tangent box
has bounded integer tangent equations and cube inequalities, so its
vertices have polynomial bits independent of `Qx`. Integer normals obtained
by exact elimination also have polynomial bits; their affine right sides
are integers because they agree with their value at a signed Boolean
vertex of `F`. Thus face equations regenerate from bounded original data.

For a nonzero tangent direction `h=Hvec/Th`, an original facet endpoint has

`t = Th (Qx b-a^T X)/(Qx a^T Hvec)`.

Its reduced denominator is at most `Qx n Cf Hmax`. Its endpoint point has
the exact representation

`[X(a^T Hvec)+(Qx b-a^T X)Hvec]/[Qx a^T Hvec]`.

There is no lower-bound-on-distance premise here. An exact 1D LP realizes
the endpoint under the geometric oracle below. Membership bisection plus
bounded-denominator rational reconstruction is another valid realization:
a positive endpoint lies below `4n` when `||h||>=1/2`; bisection to width
below `1/(2B^2)` for `B=Qx n Cf Hmax` isolates its rational value. A
merely approximate endpoint cannot be used as a face-drop certificate.

## 3. Cost-slice anchor and same-face normalized reset

Let `F` be the newly recovered minimal original face at a post-move point
`x`, and let `S_F=F intersect {c^T z=gamma}`. Since `x in relint F`, a
small relative open ball around it in `F` intersects the cost hyperplane
in a relative ball. Thus

`aff S_F=aff F intersect {c^T z=gamma}`, `x in relint S_F`.

A slice vertex can have original minimal-face dimension at most one:
intersecting a local original face of dimension at least two with one
hyperplane leaves a nontrivial local segment. It is consequently either
a signed Boolean vertex or a hyperplane intersection with an original
edge. Write `C=Tc c` integral and `C^T x0=g/Q0`; an edge intersection has

`lambda=(g-Q0 C^T u)/(Q0 C^T(v-u))`.

Its common coordinate denominator is at most
`Bv=2Q0 n max(1,||C||_infinity)`, independent of current `Qx`.
An original edge contained wholly in the cost hyperplane supplies only
its original endpoints as slice vertices.

Using exact slice LPs, select `q+1` affinely independent spanning vertices,
`q=dim S_F`, and average them to form `z`. Their full-dimensional simplex
is inside `S_F`, so `z in relint S_F`. Moreover `z in relint F`: an
original inequality not identically tight on `F` is strict at `x`, hence
not identically tight on `S_F`, hence strict at its relative interior.
An anchor common denominator satisfies

`Dz <= (q+1) Bv^(q+1)`.

Choose a rational basis `Z_1,...,Z_q` of the slice tangent, normalized
columnwise to `||Z_l||_infinity<=1`. For instance RREF plus division by
the maximum coordinate magnitude suffices; alternatively, normalized
differences of the already obtained slice vertices give bounded columns.
All basis bits depend on the bounded face/cost/vertex encodings.

Set `alpha=delta/(8n)`, mix to `x'=(1-alpha)x+alpha z`, and write
`x'-z=sum beta_l Z_l`. Round the coefficients to nearest multiples of
a dyadic mesh

`s <= alpha/(16Dz Cf n^3)`.

At `x'`, every original inequality not tight on `F` has slack at least
`alpha/Dz`. The rounding displacement has infinity norm at most `ns/2`,
so its facet effect is at most `n^2 Cf s/2 <= alpha/(32Dz n)`, strictly
less than this slack. All original face and cost equations hold exactly
because the basis lies in their kernel. Thus the reset point is in
`relint F`, with the same original face and exact cost; frozen signed
coordinates stay fixed. Its total Euclidean displacement is less than
`2sqrt(n)alpha+n^(3/2)s/2 < delta`.

This construction independently verifies the peer ledger's normalized
reset. Without normalization a column can be scaled arbitrarily and
amplify the coefficient grid. Using a preceding larger face's anchor
could also undo a rank-facet hit. Neither defective version is adopted.

## 4. Barrier/reset and numerical tracking gate

Before reset perform cleanup, and keep `b,p,r` fixed during reset.
For `Delta=xhat-x`, `||Delta||<delta`, update a medium displacement to a
nearest `r delta` grid point after adding `b^T Delta`. Even allowing
grid error `r delta` gives

`|(dhat-d)/r| <= ||b|| ||Delta||/r+delta <= 2delta`.

Inside the cube,

`|G(xhat)-G(x)| <= 2||p|| ||Delta|| <= 2r^2 delta/sqrt(K)`.

The normalized `G` contribution therefore increases by at most `delta`.
Incrementing `e` by `4delta` makes each signed medium barrier decrease
by at least `delta`. Both signs and arbitrarily small scales are covered.
Fixed retained vectors and lower weights give genuine Loewner domination
of `W`; hence the spectral part cannot increase. The all-ambient
quadratic gives potential increase at most
`2eta sqrt(n)delta`, so the reported `3eta sqrt(n)delta` is safe.

Large rows keep `d=0`; normal steps annihilate their retained row. Reset
movement need not annihilate it, and this omitted displacement is at most
`sqrt(n)delta` per reset. A medium row's only untracked reset movement is
its grid error, at most `Rdelta`. Changes of retained coefficients keep
stored `d` fixed and account for deliberate omissions separately. Thus
each row's numerical tracking error after `M` resets is below
`M(n+R+1)delta`. This explicitly accounts for resetting a large row to
zero before it later becomes medium; no uncharged large-row motion is
silently discarded.

## 6. Original-input bit regeneration and spectral-oracle contract

The reset point's original-bit bound does not follow merely from being
rational. It follows from regenerating a bounded face/cost basis, anchor,
and grid. Each basis entry has original-input polynomial bits. Any
nonsingular `q`-row minor has determinant at least
`2^(-poly(original_bits,n))` after clearing its bounded denominators.
Its inverse therefore has size at most `2^poly(original_bits,n)`.
Since `x'-z` is bounded in the cube, the coefficient magnitudes `|beta_l|`
obey the same bound, irrespective of their unrounded denominators.

The mesh exponent is `O(log Dz+log Cf+log n+log delta^(-1))`.
Thus the rounded grid indices, and then `xhat=z+Z betahat`, have
original-input polynomial bits. This is the load-bearing distinction
between bounded coefficient magnitude and a possibly enormous current
denominator. The latter is discarded by the rounding.

Retained coefficients are original `A` entries or zero. Their nonzero
masses have a lower bound from the smallest nonzero encoded original
coefficient. The convention `r=R 2^(-s_i)` therefore needs only
polynomially many bits and halvings; it works when `R` is not itself a
power of two. Barrier nonpositivity gives `|d|<H`; grid indices on
`r delta` consequently have polynomial bits. `e=4jdelta`, `j<S`,
also has bounded bits. Completed rows keep their last bounded `d`.

My local spectral routine is polynomial in current rational state bits,
`m,n,K`, and the logarithm of requested precision. Here `K<=64n`,
`eta^(-1)=Kn`, and all `tau,D,eps_g,delta,S` have polynomial bits.
The reset regeneration turns that contract into a bound in original
input bits. Rational constraints, kernel bases, shifted congruence
elimination, and dyadic physical normalization then produce an
original-bit-bounded direction. An exact endpoint update has bounded
bits by section 1; a full update has bounded bits directly. Temporary
tracked row updates receive this bounded expansion once and are
immediately reset.

Thus each cycle consists of one polynomial-size temporary expansion
followed by regeneration to a uniform original-input bound. It is not an
iterated `B_next=poly(B_current)` argument. Exact geometric outputs must
be extracted with the primary-source bounded vertex/basic procedure;
arbitrary unnecessarily long exact optima would not meet this contract.

The iteration bound is polynomial but enormous:
`tau=1/(2^43K^3n^3)`, `D=1/(2^92K^7n^7)`, and
`S=2^93K^6n^7+2n+4`. This establishes a theoretical complexity
bound, without evidence of practical usability.

## 7. Independent terminal discrepancy/cost telescope

Every original matrix entry satisfies `|a_ij|<=1`. A deliberate entry
deletion requires `p=a^2>q/K`, so its first occurrence in a row has
`q<K`. With `q'=q-p`,

`|a| < sqrt(K) p/sqrt(q)`

`    <= 2sqrt(K)(sqrt(q)-sqrt(q'))`.

Summing over deleted entries, with any extra decreases from frozen
coordinates only shortening the mass path, yields total removed
absolute coefficient at most `2K`. Each removed coordinate subsequently
moves by at most two in signed space, so this contributes at most `4K`
to the row. A coordinate frozen on a nested original face never moves
again and creates no such omission.

When `N<=8k<K`, full spread cleanup actually empties every retained
row: a nonzero row supported on at most `N` coordinates would have
`max p>=q/N>q/K`, contradicting spread. Thus all rows are completed
and retain their last stored `|d|<H` (or zero if they were emptied
while large). This observation was pointed out by the trajectory worker
and independently checked here. The source's terminal `16k` charge is
conservative and also covers a terminal shortcut before full cleanup.
Optimize the cost on the current original face and extract a
Boolean vertex `v`. It satisfies `c^T v<=c^T x=gamma` and remains in
the initial face. Retained terminal movement is zero after full cleanup;
the weaker bound `2N<=16k` suffices for the reported constant. The
numerical tracking charge is below `1/4096`. Combining gives

`|a_i^T(v-x0)| < H+4K+16k+1`.

The original unsigned discrepancy is half this signed value:

`||A(b-y)||_infinity <16R^2+2K+8k+1/2 < B(k)`.
