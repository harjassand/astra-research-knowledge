INDEPENDENT CONDITIONAL THRESHOLD WRAPPER FROM H_B
================================================

Scope and status
----------------
This is an independent scalar proof from the parent-supplied named premise H_B.
No Cycle10 Luna threshold-wrapper body has been read. H_B is a conditional
premise here; this file does not acquire H_B for any quantum strategy class.
The conclusion applies to finite commuting repeated-game strategies precisely
when H_B holds for every such strategy, every repetition length, and the legal
tensor-product strategies constructed in Section 6. Logarithms are natural.

1. Exact game and conditional premise
------------------------------------
Let G be a finite binary-win two-player game, with finite nonempty answer sets
A and B, a question distribution pi, and commuting value v in [0,1]. Repetition
G^n uses n independent samples from pi. A strategy for the whole repeated game
can correlate all n answers on either player's side. Let W_i in {0,1} be its
actual win indicators and W=sum_i W_i. We assume no independence of these W_i.
Set d=|A||B| and ell=ln d. The game's binary predicate may equivalently include
independent evaluator randomness in the question sample.

For C subset [n], let E_C={W_i=1 for every i in C}, p_C=Pr(E_C), and, only when
p_C>0 and |C|<n, set

  q_C= E[(W-|C|)/(n-|C|) | E_C].

The permitted premise is the following uniform statement, with a fixed B>=1:

  (H_B) q_C <= v+B*((ln(1/p_C)+|C|ell)/(n-|C|))^(1/4).

It holds for every finite commuting repeated-game strategy, every integer n>=1,
and every proper C with p_C>0. In particular C=empty has p_C=1 and q_C=EW/n<=v.
No convention for q_C is required when p_C=0. The premise is required for all
strategies, not just a particular strategy or its own conditional extensions.

2. Exact all-n theorem
---------------------
Under H_B, for every legal strategy, every n>=1, and every

  0<delta<=1-v,

one has, with the explicit positive constant c_B=1/(1024 B^4),

  Pr[W >= ceil((v+delta)n)]
    <= exp[-c_B*delta^5*n/(delta+ell)].                  (2.1)

For d=1 the denominator is delta and (2.1) means exp[-c_B delta^4 n].
The theorem includes v=0, all-win threshold v+delta=1, zero-probability
conditioning events, and arbitrary nonintegral (v+delta)n. If v=1 there are no
allowed positive deltas, so the stated assertion is vacuous. No conclusion for
n=0 is needed; if it is included, both sides at the empty threshold are 1.

3. Symmetric success moments and their entropy recurrence
--------------------------------------------------------
For 0<=j<=n define

  p_j = binom(n,j)^(-1) sum_{|C|=j} p_C
      = E[(W)_j/(n)_j],                               (3.1)

where (u)_j=u(u-1)...(u-j+1) and (u)_0=1. Thus p_0=1. Whenever p_j>0 and j<n,
put a probability distribution on j-subsets by

  w_C=p_C/[binom(n,j)p_j].

Terms p_C=0 have weight zero and are omitted. Double counting pairs (C,i),
where |C|=j and i notin C, gives

  p_{j+1}/p_j = sum_C w_C q_C.                         (3.2)

Indeed p_C q_C=(n-j)^(-1)sum_{i notin C}p_{C union {i}}, and each (j+1)-subset
is counted j+1 times. This is an identity for arbitrary correlated indicators.

Let u_C=1/binom(n,j) be the uniform law on j-subsets. The exact identity

  sum_C w_C ln(1/p_C)=ln(1/p_j)-D(w||u)<=ln(1/p_j)      (3.3)

uses ordinary classical relative entropy on this finite index set. Applying
H_B in (3.2), Jensen concavity of t^(1/4), and (3.3) gives

  p_{j+1}/p_j
    <= v+B*((ln(1/p_j)+j ell)/(n-j))^(1/4).            (3.4)

Thus the cost of averaging conditional strategies is controlled by the single
success moment p_j. No quantum relative-entropy production or conditional
strategy construction is asserted in this scalar step.

4. Entropy barrier for sufficiently large repetition length; d>=2
----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix 0<delta<=1-v, and define

  a=delta^4/(16 B^4),
  s=v+3delta/4,
  L=ell+ln(1/s)+a.                                    (4.1)

Here 0<s<1, a>0, and ell>=ln2, so L>0. Suppose for now that

  n>=8L/a.

Let

  k=floor(a n/(4L)).                                 (4.2)

Then k>=a n/(8L)>=1. Moreover

  k/n<=a/(4L)<=delta^4/(64 B^4 ell)<=delta/4,

since ell>=ln2>1/2 and 0<delta<=1, B>=1. Consequently k<n and k is a legal
proper-subset size. Set t=ceil((v+delta)n), which lies in {1,...,n}, and
P=Pr(W>=t). We may assume P>0. For every 0<=j<=k, (3.1) implies

  p_j>=P (t)_j/(n)_j>=P s^j.                         (4.3)

For the second inequality, each factor, indexed by 0<=u<j, satisfies
(t-u)/(n-u)>=(t-u)/n>=v+delta-k/n>=s. In particular all p_j through k are
positive, so the logs and recurrence used below are finite.

Call an index j<=k good when

  ln(1/p_j)+j ell <= a(n-j).                          (4.4)

Index 0 is good. At a good j<k, (3.4) gives

  p_{j+1}/p_j<=v+B a^(1/4)=v+delta/2.                (4.5)

There are two exhaustive possibilities.

(a) Some j in {1,...,k} is not good. By (4.3),

  ln(1/P)>=ln(1/p_j)-j ln(1/s)
          >a n-j[a+ell+ln(1/s)]
          =a n-jL >=3a n/4.                          (4.6)

(b) All indices 0,...,k are good. Multiplying (4.5) for j=0,...,k-1 gives
p_k<=(v+delta/2)^k. Combining it with (4.3),

  ln(1/P)>= k ln[s/(v+delta/2)] >= k delta/(4s)
           >= a delta n/(32 sL).                    (4.7)

The middle bound is ln(1+x)>=x/(1+x), applied with
x=delta/[4(v+delta/2)]. It remains valid at the all-win endpoint.

The denominator sL has a uniform bound with no hidden log(1/delta):

  sL=s ell+s ln(1/s)+s a
     <=ell+1/e+1/16<=2ell<=2(delta+ell).              (4.8)

For the penultimate inequality one can use 1/e<1/2 and ln2>=2/3>9/16.
Combining (4.7) and (4.8) proves

  ln(1/P)>=a delta n/[64(delta+ell)]
           =delta^5 n/[1024 B^4(delta+ell)].          (4.9)

In case (a), (4.6) is stronger than (4.9), since delta/(delta+ell)<=1.
Therefore (2.1) holds for every strategy as soon as n>=8L/a. Importantly the
large-n cutoff depends only on v, delta, ell, and B, and not on the strategy.

5. Degenerate answer alphabet d=1
--------------------------------
If d=1, each player's answer set has one element. Every measurement with a
single outcome is the identity. Hence the winning indicator on each coordinate
is the same fixed function of that coordinate's question sample, regardless of
the strategy, its Hilbert space, or any attempted intercoordinate correlation.
The product question distribution therefore gives the exact generating function

  E exp(lambda W)=(1-v+v exp(lambda))^n.              (5.1)

Here v is the success probability of the unique-answer base game. This is not
an assumption of independence for general strategies: it is forced in the
special d=1 case by the game definition and the singleton output algebra.

For F(lambda)=ln(1-v+v exp(lambda)), F(0)=0, F'(0)=v, and
F''(lambda)=v_lambda(1-v_lambda)<=1/4. Integrating twice for lambda>=0 yields
F(lambda)<=v lambda+lambda^2/8. Exponential Markov with lambda=4delta gives

  Pr[W>=ceil((v+delta)n)]<=exp(-2delta^2 n)
                         <=exp(-c_B delta^4 n).     (5.2)

This proves (2.1) for ell=0 at every n. The argument uses the standard repeated
GAME product-question structure; H_B with a free abstract parameter ell=0,
without this game structure, is not being silently treated as the same premise.

6. Legal tensor-product amplification removes every short-n loss
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For d>=2, start with any legal original n-copy strategy. For concreteness write
its commuting realization as a unit vector psi on a Hilbert space H and POVM
families {M_a^x} and {N_b^y}, indexed by full n-tuples of inputs and outputs,
with [M_a^x,N_b^y]=0 for all x,y,a,b. There is no requirement that the individual
coordinate outputs within one player's original POVM commute or be independent.

For any integer m>=1 use H^(tensor m), vector psi^(tensor m), and the product
POVMs over m consecutive blocks of n coordinates:

  M_{a^(1),...,a^(m)}^{x^(1),...,x^(m)}
       = tensor_{u=1}^m M_{a^(u)}^{x^(u)},
  N_{b^(1),...,b^(m)}^{y^(1),...,y^(m)}
       = tensor_{u=1}^m N_{b^(u)}^{y^(u)}.            (6.1)

They are positive, sum to identity, and commute between the two players:
within each tensor factor the original Alice/Bob effects commute, and across
factors they act on distinct factors. Thus (6.1) is a legal strategy for G^(mn).
Finite-dimensional realizations remain finite-dimensional. More generally a
commuting Hilbert-space realization also remains a commuting realization; the
required uniform H_B must apply to the strategy class actually under study.
The base game's v and per-coordinate ell=ln(|A||B|) are unchanged by blocking.

The product state and product question law make the m BLOCK experiments
independent with exactly the original block distribution. This deliberately
constructed block independence is unrelated to any independence of the n
win indicators in the original strategy. If each block wins at least
ceil((v+delta)n) coordinates, their total wins at least

  m ceil((v+delta)n)>=ceil((v+delta)mn).               (6.2)

Writing P for the original threshold probability, the mn-copy threshold
probability is therefore at least P^m. For all sufficiently large m, the
uniform large-length estimate (4.9) applies to this product strategy, giving

  P^m<=exp[-c_B delta^5 mn/(delta+ell)].               (6.3)

If P=0 the conclusion is immediate; otherwise taking the m-th root proves
(2.1) for the original n. No asymptotic limit or compactness is needed: choose
any integer m for which mn>=8L/a. The ceiling inequality (6.2) is exact, so
there is no uncharged loss in delta and no restriction on the short original n.

7. Zeros, v=0, and complete logical dependencies
------------------------------------------------
If v=0, H_B at C=empty gives EW=0. Since W>=0, W=0 almost surely. Every positive
delta has t>=1, so its threshold probability is zero. If a success moment p_j
vanishes, (4.3) shows any positive tail with j<=k would be impossible; the proof
never conditions on a zero-probability event. When v+delta=1, t=n and (6.2)
is exactly the all-win event in every block; all the same bounds hold.

The sole analytic conditional input for d>=2 is H_B. The remaining inputs are
binary scores, the product question distribution of repetition, the legality
and scope closure of tensor products, and elementary scalar probability.
For d=1 the product-question/single-output structure supplies the exact (5.1).

The proof establishes an all-n conditional theorem, not an unconditional
parallel-repetition theorem or an acquisition of H_B. It also does not assert
optimality of the exponent 5 or of c_B. Any proposed stronger losing-event
premise from the parent will be analyzed in a separate append-only addendum.
