# Relative characteristic-p reconstruction from a pure-symbol zero predicate

Date: 2026-10-07. Worker: frontier_number1, cycle19. Status: independently reconstructed handwritten theorem candidate, derived from the pinned family009 mechanism and strengthened jointly with root. Not formalized; novelty not established. Root is conducting a separate adversarial proof check.

## Exact question and interface

Let p be prime. Consider pairs (K,H) of characteristic-p fields satisfying K^p ⊆ H ⊆ K and [K:H] ≥ p²; degrees may be infinite. Define the elementary abelian p-group, written additively,

    V(K,H) = K*/H*,  [f]+[g]=[fg].

Define W(K,H) as V⊗_{Fp}V divided by the Fp-span of [f]⊗[1−f] for f≠0,1. Only the binary predicate

    N([f],[g]) : image([f]⊗[g]) = 0 in W(K,H)

is supplied, together with V's vector-space structure. The entire group W, nonzero product values, field addition, and the base H are not supplied separately. This is a relative Milnor-symbol quotient: the usual K2/p is additionally quotiented by tensors involving H*.

**Candidate theorem R.** For pairs (K,H),(L,C) as above, every Fp-linear bijection Θ:V(K,H)→V(L,C) preserving N in both directions has a unique expression

    Θ = r α_*,  r∈Fp*,

where α:K→L is a field isomorphism with α(H)=C. Conversely such maps preserve N. Equivalently, the field-pair isomorphisms correspond bijectively to predicate-preserving linear isomorphisms modulo global Fp* scaling.

This is a full reconstruction/rigidity statement, not an efficient acquisition algorithm. The input is an entire, generally infinite, group plus an exact relation-preserving isomorphism. A finite sample or a finite transcript does not supply that input.

## Complete proof reconstruction

### 1. Relative p-bases and derivations

Write F/B for an exponent-one extension: F^p⊆B⊆F. For s∉M with B⊆M⊆F, the minimal polynomial divides T^p−s^p. If its degree e were strictly between 1 and p, it would be (T−s)^e and the coefficient −es would put s in M. Thus every strict adjunction has degree p.

Take a maximal p-independent set by Zorn: every finite sublist has degree p to the power of its length. The union of an increasing chain is still p-independent because each condition involves only finitely many elements. Maximality makes the set generate F over B. Its finite-support monomials, with exponents in {0,…,p−1}, form a B-basis. Each element has a finite monomial expansion: it lies in the field generated by a finite subset, whose p-monomials are already a basis. This proves the needed facts for infinite degrees as well as finite ones.

For basis variable s_i define B-linear Euler derivation D_i by multiplying a basis monomial by its i-th exponent modulo p. Reducing an exponent by p removes an element of B and does not change that exponent in Fp, so the product rule holds. The D_i commute. On B(s_i), the eigenvalues are 0,…,p−1; the eigenvectors are exactly b s_i^j. On B(X,Y), the common kernel of D_X,D_Y is B. Also B(X)∩B(Y)=B for p-independent X,Y.

### 2. A zero symbol forces relative p-dependence

If s∉B and t∉B(s), extend s,t to a relative p-basis. The pairing

    β([f],[g]) = (D_s f/f)(D_t g/g) − (D_t f/f)(D_s g/g)

is Fp-bilinear, is unchanged by factors in B*, and kills each Steinberg tensor: the two derivatives of 1−f are the negatives of those of f. It therefore descends to W(F,B). It sends the image of [s]⊗[t] to 1. Hence

    s∉B and N([s],[t])  ⇒  t∈B(s).                 (Z)

The reverse implication is not needed anywhere in theoremR. In particular, theoremR does not require the deep injectivity of the absolute dlog symbol.

For a,b∈B*, the Steinberg tensor applied to −bs/a gives

    N([s],[a+bs]),  whenever both entries are nonzero.       (A)

The quotient kills the B factors, so this is an exact relative identity.

### 3. Local intersection classification

Let X,Y be p-independent over B. Let U∈B(X)* and V∈B(Y)* have [U]∉Fp[X], [V]∉Fp[Y]. Define the B-vector subspaces

    P = X B(Y/X),       Q = U B(V/U).

**Lemma I.** P∩Q≠0 iff there is a unique n∈{1,…,p−1} such that

    U^n=a+bX^n,   V^n=c+dY^n,   a,b,c,d∈B*.

In this case, choose r∈{1,…,p−1} with nr≡1 modulo p. Then

    P∩Q = B·Z,       Z=(cU^n−aV^n)^r
                         =(bcX^n−adY^n)^r ≠0.       (I)

**Necessity.** Choose nonzero Z∈P∩Q, and use Euler derivations on B(X,Y). The nonmonomial hypotheses ensure D_XU,D_YV≠0. Put

    B_X=U/(D_XU),  A_X=B_X−1;
    B_Y=V/(D_YV),  A_Y=B_Y−1.

A_X∈B(X)\B and A_Y∈B(Y)\B: otherwise the corresponding U or V is an Euler eigenvector, hence a monomial. In particular neither A vanishes. For δ=D_X+D_Y and δ′=B_XD_X+B_YD_Y, the two expressions for Z give δZ=Z=δ′Z. With q_X=D_XZ/Z and q_Y=D_YZ/Z,

    q_X+q_Y=1,      A_Xq_X+A_Yq_Y=0.                (1)

Both q's are nonzero. Since δ commutes with D_X,D_Y, it kills both q's. Equation(1) therefore implies δ(A_X/A_Y)=0, hence

    D_XA_X/A_X = D_YA_Y/A_Y ∈ B(X)∩B(Y)=B.

Both A's are nonconstant Euler eigenvectors, so

    A_X=h_X X^m,   A_Y=h_Y Y^m,

for h_X,h_Y∈B* and the same m∈{1,…,p−1}. Put n=p−m. The logarithmic derivatives of U^n and X^n+h_XX^p are equal, because both equal n/(1+h_XX^m). Their quotient is in the kernel of D_X on B(X), hence in B*. This gives U^n=a+bX^n with both coefficients nonzero. The calculation for V is identical.

If U^j=a′+b′X^j for 1≤j<p, both coefficients must be nonzero (else [U] is a monomial). Taking D_X shows A_X=a′/(b′X^j), an eigenvector of eigenvalue p−j. Distinct eigenvalues force j=n. Thus either of the two inclusions determines n.

**Sufficiency and the witness.** The two expressions for W=cU^n−aV^n=bcX^n−adY^n are nonzero by p-independence. Write nr=1+kp. Then

    W^r = X^{nr}(bc−ad(Y/X)^n)^r ∈ X B(Y/X),
    W^r = U^{nr}(c−a(V/U)^n)^r ∈ U B(V/U),

since X^p,U^p∈B. This proves existence and formula(I).

**Dimension one.** As A_X−A_Y≠0, equation(1) uniquely fixes q_X and q_Y. Two nonzero intersection elements therefore have identical X and Y logarithmic derivatives. Their quotient lies in B(X,Y) and is killed by both derivatives, so is in B*. Thus every witness is a B multiple of Z.

All this uses only the two-variable exponent-one field B(X,Y), of degree p² over B; ambient relative p-rank is irrelevant.

### 4. One scalar aligns every projective line

The set V(F,B) is also the point set P_B(F); keep this projective B-scalar structure distinct from V's Fp-vector-space structure.

Given Θ:V(K,H)→V(L,C), join two nonzero source points [x],[y] when C(X)≠C(Y), where [X]=Θ[x],[Y]=Θ[y]. Each C(X) has degree p. Different such fields mean a p-independent pair. The graph is a complete multipartite graph, with at least two parts since [L:C]≥p² and Θ is onto. It is connected and has diameter at most two.

On an edge, 1,x,y are H-linearly independent. Otherwise y=a+bx with b≠0; if a=0 the points coincide, and if a≠0 identity(A), followed by preservation of N and implication(Z), would put Y in C(X), contradicting the edge.

For any a,b∈H*, put

    u=x+a,  v=y+b,  z=bx−ay=bu−av.

All three are nonzero. The points [u],[v] are not Fp-multiples of [x],[y], by independence of the p monomials in H(x),H(y). Four applications of(A) give

    N([x],[u]), N([y],[v]),
    N([y/x],[z/x]), N([v/u],[z/u]).

Preservation of N and(Z) transfer this into the hypotheses of lemmaI:

    U∈C(X), V∈C(Y),
    Z∈X C(Y/X)∩U C(V/U),

where [U]=Θ[u], [V]=Θ[v], [Z]=Θ[z]. The denominating points are nonzero in V: Y/X∉C by p-independence, while V/U∈C would put U in C(X)∩C(Y)=C.

LemmaI gives one exponent n for both U and V. Fix [x], one neighbor [y], and b. V stays fixed as a varies, so uniqueness forces the same n for every a. Taking a=1 fixes U, and uniqueness now makes n independent of the neighbor and b. Adjacent vertices have the same exponent. Connectedness gives one global n.

Consequently Ψ=nΘ maps the line through [1],[x] into the line through [1],[X^n]. All lines are multiplicative translates of these lines, and Ψ is a group homomorphism, so Ψ maps every line into a line.

For an exponent s∈Fp*, the power map [f]↦s[f] can send all projective lines into lines only when s=1. Indeed take x∉H. The collinear points [1],[x],[x+1] would require (x+1)^s∈H+Hx^s. For 1<s<p, its binomial coefficient s at x^{s−1} is nonzero, contradicting p-monomial independence. The case p=2 has no nontrivial scalar.

Apply the construction to Ψ^−1; obtain s such that sΨ^−1 maps lines into lines. Compose with Ψ and the preceding paragraph forces s=1. Thus Ψ and its inverse map lines into lines. If Ψ(ℓ)⊆ℓ′, the inverse maps ℓ′ into some line ℓ″, giving ℓ⊆Ψ^−1(ℓ′)⊆ℓ″. Two distinct lines cannot contain one another, so the inclusions are equalities. Therefore Ψ maps lines onto lines. The same scalar argument makes n unique.

### 5. Projective lifting, including infinite dimension

Choose an H-basis e_0,e_i (i in an arbitrary index set I), with |I|≥2. A line-preserving bijection preserves all finite spans, hence maps basis points to a C-projective basis. Choose representatives e′_i; scale them so that the images of [e_0+e_i] are [e′_0+e′_i].

The affine complement of the hyperplane spanned by the e_i, i≠0, has finite-support coordinates (t_i). Its bijection preserves parallel coordinate lines. On axis i it gives a bijection σ_i:H→C fixing 0,1. Varying a single coordinate changes only that corresponding output coordinate, so the map is coordinatewise. Every vector has finite support, making this conclusion valid for infinitely many axes.

In every two-axis plane, the diagonal through (0,0),(1,1) forces σ_i=σ_j. There is one σ. Lines (t,t+a) and (t,at) respectively give σ(t+a)=σ(t)+σ(a) and σ(at)=σ(t)σ(a). Thus σ is a field isomorphism. Define

    S(Σ t_i e_i)=Σ σ(t_i)e′_i,

with finite sums. This additive, σ-semilinear bijection induces Ψ on the affine chart and on its points at infinity, which are directions of affine lines. Any other semilinear lift T satisfies T(v)=c_vS(v). Additivity at two independent vectors forces their c's equal; dependent vectors can be compared to an independent one. Thus T=cS and the coefficient-field isomorphism is unique.

### 6. Multiplicativity recovers the field pair

Since Ψ[1]=[1], normalize S(1)=1. For f≠0, the two semilinear maps w↦S(fw) and w↦S(f)S(w) induce the same projective map because Ψ is a homomorphism of multiplicative quotients. They agree at 1, so uniqueness of the lift makes them equal everywhere. S is multiplicative, hence a field isomorphism α. Semilinearity and S(1)=1 give S(h)=σ(h), so α(H)=C.

Then nΘ=α_*, or Θ=n^−1α_*. If a second field isomorphism induces a scalar multiple of α_*, the scalar power map would preserve all lines, forcing scalar1; lift uniqueness and normalization at1 force the field isomorphisms equal. This completes theoremR.

## Concrete consequences and exact limitations

**Absolute reconstruction.** Set H=K^p,C=L^p. Every characteristic-p field of p-rank at least two is recovered from K1/p plus its pure degree-two zero predicate; finite or infinite p-rank is allowed. Any resulting field isomorphism also identifies the intrinsic perfect core ⋂_{m≥1}K^{p^m}. No separately named constant field is necessary for this intrinsic statement.

**Finite and other perfect bases.** If K/k is finitely generated regular over any perfect k, then the perfect core is k: a nonconstant f is transcendental, and T^{p^m}−f over k(t_2,…,t_d)(f) is Eisenstein, so p^m-th roots of f cannot all fit in one finite extension. Thus the same mod-characteristic predicate recovers both the field and its finite or infinite perfect constant field for transcendence degree≥2. This strengthens the named-base hypothesis beyond algebraically closed constants.

**Height-one inseparable geometry.** The relative version recovers any exponent-one field extension K/H of relative p-rank≥2, including its actual base H. Such pairs are generic field data for height-one purely inseparable dominant morphisms. The theorem supplies a much smaller invariant interface for classifying these pairs and testing whether invariant isomorphisms are geometric. It does not recover a chosen integral model or a variety's topology from the field pair.

**Local computation.** With coordinate access to C and U∈C(X),V∈C(Y), testing n=1,…,p−1 reduces lemmaI to two univariate p-coefficient support tests; each positive case yields the explicit Z in(I). Incremental powering costs O(p³) C-arithmetic operations with classical length-p multiplication; storage for the checks is O(p), and the explicit two-variable witness may use O(p²) coefficients. This is a stated coordinate oracle, not a complexity claim in bit operations or a way to obtain that oracle from V.

**Rank-one failure is real.** For F=F_2(t), H=F^2, the absolute K2(F)/2 is zero by the differential-symbol injectivity theorem of Bloch–Gabber–Kato (1986, Theorem2.1), since Ω_F^2=0. V is the free F2-vector space on monic irreducible polynomials. Swap [t+1] with [t²+t+1] and fix all other basis elements; this preserves all zero symbols. It is not induced by a field automorphism: Aut(F_2(t))=PGL_2(F_2), and the only members fixing [t] are t↦t and t↦1/t. They send [t+1] to [t+1] or [t+1]+[t], not [t²+t+1]. Thus the relative rank≥2 condition cannot simply be removed. Perfect fields give the still stronger degeneration V=0.

**Acquisition remains an additional problem.** The proof uses global bijectivity, exact two-sided predicate preservation, a connected graph over all points, and a projective lift. None is certified by the finite checks below. Without an effective enumeration, a bound on p-rank, a presentation of the group, and a promised globally compatible map, there is no finite acquisition guarantee. A finite witness proves the local lemma's configuration, not global compatibility or efficient recovery of the field.
