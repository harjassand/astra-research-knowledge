# Electrical series construction with explicit feed and product interfaces

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: circuit construction and conditional resource bounds; not a demonstrated iron-extraction process. All numerical geometry below is illustrative. Material conductivity, contact resistivity, membrane stability, reduction chemistry, atom supply, and selective transport are not validated here.

## Main result

A high projected current density does **not**, by itself, impose the same enormous current at the power-supply terminals. Equal-area membrane cells can be electrically connected in series while receiving feed and collecting product in parallel. This trades current for voltage at unchanged ideal power.

The escape has three mandatory costs: every local contact still carries the original current density, the feed/product compartments must remain electrically isolated while powered, and the conductors must physically cross or bypass the material-access spaces. A common conducting metal-product collector invalidates the series circuit. A batch bipolar cartridge, de-energized before common collection, is a concrete way to meet isolation. Its required storage gaps expose a substantial conductor-volume/heat tradeoff. It does not solve the acquisition of Fe atoms from ore.

## Stipulated transport law and electrical notation

Take as the hypothetical local membrane contract

    atom flux per projected area = 2 f D_s c_s Z* e E / (r k_B T),
    conducting area fraction g = 2 f t/r,
    projected current density j = sigma_Fe g E,
    local active voltage v = E L.

The analysis assumes the resulting atom flux and j are linear in the same E. It does not establish that contract. Let A be the total active membrane area *online*, and let m be the corresponding iron mass flux per projected area. The root agent owns atom/electron accounting.

## Construction: electrically series, materially parallel

1. Divide active area A into N equal cells of projected area a=A/N.
2. Each cell has an electrically conducting Fe transport membrane between distinct source and destination current collectors. Feed and product chambers have insulating walls and their own ports.
3. Connect the destination collector of cell k electrically to the source collector of cell k+1. A bipolar plate may perform this connection. It must block material transfer between the clean product face and the dirty feed face while conducting electrons. This requires a chemically compatible, validated Fe diffusion barrier; it is an additional material interface, not free wiring.
4. Put the cells in stacked columns to let current cross most collectors normally. Connect columns in a serpentine electrical series. Feed and product remain parallel material streams, rather than passing the same atoms through every electrical cell.
5. During powered operation, seal the individual feed/product chambers from shared conducting manifolds. Accumulate product in the clean chambers.
6. Disconnect the supply, discharge/equalize the internal electrical nodes, then open the chambers for common product removal and gangue/feed service. Clear all shared conducting paths before re-energizing. A second identical cartridge can keep the process online while the first is serviced, at the cost of doubling installed membrane and collector area if the service time does not exceed the run time.

The exact ideal terminal quantities are

    I_terminal = j A/N,
    V_terminal = N v,
    P_ideal = j A v,
    mass output = m A.

The product sum is mA only because **each** cell receives fresh feed and exposes a product outlet. If the atoms instead pass through N channels materially in series and are harvested only at the last, the throughput is ma=mA/N. A long connected Fe snake cannot claim both the electrical series benefit and N-fold independent product output.

This construction needs N membranes, roughly N bipolar collectors, N feed cavities, N clean product cavities, insulating separators and seals, and at least two material service ports per cell or an equivalent batch opening mechanism. Their areas, volumes, fabrication, service frequency, contamination, and replacement costs must be charged.

## Exact local contact lower bound

Suppose a contact has specific resistivity rho_c (ohm m^2), real conducting area A_c, and total current I. Its heat obeys

    P_c = integral rho_c J_n(x)^2 dA >= rho_c I^2/A_c

by Cauchy--Schwarz. If A_c<=kappa a for each membrane cell, then summing all N cells gives

    P_c,total/A >= rho_c j^2/kappa.

This is independent of N. If the contact is restricted to the Fe shell fraction, kappa=g. If the shell flares into a broader collector, kappa can exceed g, but the flare, spreading loss, extra Fe area, and transport selectivity must be included explicitly.

For several contacts per cell, a necessary condition for contact heat at most fraction eta_c of the ideal membrane heat is

    sum_contacts rho_c/kappa <= eta_c v/j.

No strictly positive model-independent contact penalty follows if one permits an ideal monolithic zero-resistance junction. The bound is conditional on the actual measured or proved specific resistivity and contact geometry. Neither is established for the proposed ore/Fe/CNT device.

Illustration: j=6e7 A/m^2, v=30 microvolt, g=0.02. Two equal contacts restricted to the shell fraction require

    rho_c <= eta_c * 5e-15 ohm m^2.

For total contact overhead eta_c=0.1, each contact must therefore have rho_c<=5e-16 ohm m^2. Series connection does not relax this requirement. This is a target for measurement, not a claim that a suitable contact exists.

## Collector geometry and conductor-volume cost

A face collector cannot be assumed equipotential for free. Consider a square cell with side b, a sheet collector of thickness h and conductivity sigma_c, and a full-width bus along one edge. Uniform injected projected current j gives sheet current K(x)=j(b-x). Therefore one collector dissipates

    P_sheet = j^2 b^4/(3 sigma_c h).

For two such face collectors over total membrane area A,

    P_two_sheets/A = 2 j^2 b^2/(3 sigma_c h).

For j=6e7 A/m^2, b=1 mm, h=1 micrometre, and assumed sigma_c=1e7 S/m, this is 2.4e8 W/m^2. A planar millimetre tile with thin edge-fed electrodes is therefore incompatible with the illustrative 1.8e3 W/m^2 active heat budget. Merely drawing a series wire at the tile edge hides this loss.

Normal-current bipolar stacking avoids the long lateral sheet path **only for the compact collector itself**. Even an ideal homogeneous full-area plate of thickness h_p has

    P_plate/A >= j^2 h_p/sigma_p.

For h_p=1 micrometre and assumed sigma_p=1e7 S/m, this lower bound is 360 W/m^2 per effective plate per cell. Current constriction, porosity, imperfect interfaces, and mechanical supports add cost. If current remains restricted to fraction g throughout the plate, the bound is larger by 1/g. The best-case full-area value must not be used without a spreading construction. In particular, a one-micrometre plate does **not** electrically connect nodes on opposite sides of a 100-micrometre feed or product cavity; the next bound charges that missing path.

Column-to-column leads also have a finite material cost. For a conductor of length ell, conductivity sigma, volume V, and current I,

    R = integral ds/(sigma S(s)) >= ell^2/(sigma V),
    P >= I^2 ell^2/(sigma V).

For prescribed separate leads indexed by l, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    P_leads V_leads >= (sum_l |I_l| ell_l/sqrt(sigma_l))^2.

This is a conditional but exact resistance/volume bound. It charges internal leads even when terminal current is modest.

## Storage-gap conductor bound: the stack's decisive cost

Suppose the batch stack places successive electrically connected membrane nodes at least H apart to make room for feed, product, and separators. Current must cross or bypass each such space. For a connection of current I, conductivity at most sigma, and conductor volume V, the bound

    P >= I^2 H^2/(sigma V)

also holds for arbitrary branching conductors: every plane between the two node planes carries net current I; Cauchy--Schwarz first over that plane and then over the separation gives the inequality. A conductor detour cannot eliminate it. Enlarging the conductor outside the active footprint can reduce loss, but that extra volume is counted.

For N equal cells carrying I=jA/N, N_gap such connections, and a total connection-heat allowance eta_g jAv, the exact summed bound is

    V_gap_conductors/A >= [j H^2/(sigma eta_g v)] (N_gap/N)^2.

With N_gap=N-1 internal gaps, the factor is (1-1/N)^2 and tends to one; including N required periods gives factor one exactly. Thus increasing N does not solve the internal current/collection-gap cost. A conductor whose cross-section is limited to the active footprint has V/A<=(N_gap/N)H and necessarily

    P_gap/A >= (N_gap/N) j^2 H/sigma,
    H <= (N/N_gap) eta_g sigma v/j  if its heat is to meet the allowance.

At j=6e7 A/m^2, v=30 microvolt, assumed sigma=1e7 S/m, and eta_g=0.1, the footprint-limited separation cannot exceed approximately 0.5 micrometre. Taking H=200 micrometre instead requires approximately **80 litres of conductor per online m^2** to keep this added heat at 180 W/m^2. For a single N=300,000 stack with N_gap=N-1, the exact volume lower bound is 80(299999/300000)^2 litres, exceeding 79.9994 litres. For the 300-column layout below, N_gap=300(1000-1)=299,700 internal column gaps gives 79.84008 litres before intercolumn leads. A full-area conductor across those internal gaps would dissipate 71.928 kW/m^2. These bounds exclude contacts, current constriction, supports, and terminal or intercolumn wiring.

This is a lower bound for the stated batch stack geometry, not a theorem against every three-dimensional architecture. A proposed escape must construct material access that avoids this minimum electrical separation, or provide the larger conductor volume, or accept more heat. Continuously removing electrically disconnected product packets could reduce storage height, but packet formation, source isolation, and break-before-make transport are then additional unproved operations.

## Why a common conducting product manifold fails

In an ideal series chain the output nodes have distinct potentials V_k=kv, k=1,...,N. A perfectly conducting common product rail imposes equal potentials and shorts the cells. A common connected Fe product sheet is such a rail.

Suppose instead every output connects to a common floating rail through identical resistance R. The rail chooses the mean node potential, which minimizes leakage. The exact minimum shunt power is

    P_shunt = (v^2/R) sum_k (k-(N+1)/2)^2
            = v^2 N(N^2-1)/(12R).

Thus even a floating collector requires high branch resistances if N is large. In the numerical example below, keeping shunt loss below 180 W requires R>11,250 ohm per product branch. A connected metal collection rail does not provide that isolation.

Allowed escapes are explicit batch collection after de-energization, truly insulating separated transport packets, or break-before-make collection hardware. A conducting liquid or partly reduced ore network in a shared feed manifold creates the same problem. It must be isolated as well; treating only the clean product side is insufficient.

## Feed, product, and batch-volume accounting

For run time tau, iron density rho_Fe, and usable product void fraction phi_p, the product chamber height H_p must satisfy

    phi_p H_p >= m tau/rho_Fe.

If the packed feed contains C_Fe kilograms of recoverable Fe per cubic metre, the feed chamber height must satisfy

    H_f >= m tau/C_Fe.

These imply the material-storage lower bound

    V_feed + V_product >= m A tau (1/C_Fe + 1/rho_Fe)

before inaccessible void, gangue retention, fixtures, gas handling, manifolds, and reduction reagents are counted. This bound does not improve with electrical series count.

For the normal batch stack, H is at least H_f+H_p. Combining storage with the conductor bound gives

    V_gap_conductors/A >= [j m^2 tau^2/(sigma eta_g v)]
                           * (1/C_Fe + 1/rho_Fe)^2 (N_gap/N)^2.

Thus the conductor volume needed at fixed extra-heat allowance grows quadratically with the batch run interval. Faster servicing reduces this cost but raises the required collection/refill frequency; that service is not free. Ore with lower recoverable Fe concentration makes the bound worse than pure-metal feed.

For m=10 kg/(m^2 hour) and an illustrative rho_Fe=7870 kg/m^3, output grows by 0.353 micrometre of equivalent dense iron per second. A 100 micrometre clean product gap holds at most 0.787 kg/m^2 and fills within 283.32 seconds. A one-micrometre gap fills within 2.8332 seconds. Feed volume generally exceeds product volume because ore contains gangue and void; its Fe concentration must be measured and included.

If a fixed installed area operates for duty fraction D and the stipulated linear transport law remains valid, maintaining the same average output requires j_on=j_average/D. Contact heat averaged over time is at least the DC heat divided by D. The active membrane's ohmic heat has the same penalty because E also increases by 1/D. Alternatively, install A/D membrane area and run the online fraction at the original local flux. Neither pulsing nor batch downtime is a free current reduction.

## Illustrative circuit layout, with unresolved fabrication gates exposed

Choose online A=1 m^2, j=6e7 A/m^2, v=30 microvolt, and N=300,000 electrical cells. Then

    cell area = 3.333e-6 m^2; square side = 1.826 mm,
    cell and terminal current = 200 A,
    terminal voltage = 9 V,
    ideal online power = 1.8 kW,
    output = 10 kg/hour if the stipulated atomic transport is sustained.

Use 300 columns with 1,000 cells each. With 100 micrometre feed gaps, 100 micrometre product gaps, and one 1 micrometre collector plate per cell, the columns are already approximately 0.201 m high before supports, membrane thickness, seals, and manifolds. The two channel volumes total 0.2 litre across the online active area. The feed gap may have to be much larger to hold enough Fe-bearing ore for the same cycle; the formula above determines it.

There are 300,000 feed/product compartment pairs. At the 100-micrometre product-capacity limit, each cell produces about 2.62 mg per cycle. Common collection is permitted only after electrical shutdown and equalization. The 0.2-litre channel-volume figure excludes the **approximately 80 litres of gap-spanning conductor** required to keep its additional heat below ten percent in the stated geometry. The geometric contradiction in a naive sketch is precisely to retain the 0.2-litre channel volume while pretending that one-micrometre plates span those spaces. No manufacturing or harvesting cost is claimed acceptable.

For 299 equal column links of length 2 mm, current 200 A, assumed conductivity 1e7 S/m, and a total link-heat allowance of 180 W, the conductor-volume inequality requires at least 7.947 millilitres of ideal conductor. Each uniform link then needs approximately 13.29 mm^2 cross-section. These are additional leads and end contacts; the thin active plates do not supply that cross-section for free. Link length and packing must be recomputed for an actual layout.

A second full cartridge can hide service downtime only if its service finishes within one run interval. Installed membrane area then becomes 2 m^2, with twice the cells, collectors, chambers, and their fabrication cost. The 9 V, 200 A power-supply specification applies to the cartridge that is online.

The numbers show that terminal current can be reduced by a valid series topology. They also show that the batch normal-stack version pays a substantial internal conductor cost. They do not show that local contacts, 300,000 sealed material compartments, nanomembrane manufacture, feed access, or cyclic product stripping are viable.

## The already-pure-iron versus ore boundary

Coh et al. demonstrated current-driven movement and surface rearrangement of an **already existing iron nanocrystal** through a carbon-nanotube constriction. That establishes neither reduction of iron oxide nor continuous delivery of fresh Fe atoms from gangue-bearing ore to a transport shell. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5709 ; DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.185901.

The electrical construction above must receive Fe atoms at each source interface at rate ma. It cannot count transport of the original membrane, seed crystal, collector foil, or preloaded pure Fe inventory as extraction from ore. Required upstream steps include whatever reduction, liberation, interface renewal, and impurity rejection actually establish that supply. Their energy, reagent, area, throughput, and contamination costs remain separate and mandatory.

If the source is only unreduced iron oxide and no reaction interface delivers Fe^0 to the shell, this circuit transports no newly extracted iron regardless of how favorable the electromigration law is. No feed-to-shell chemical bridge was supplied or proved here.

## Scientific disposition

The constructive escape is real at the circuit level: electrically series, materially parallel bipolar cells, with batch isolation and explicitly charged internal conductors. It defeats an argument based solely on total terminal current. The completed normal-stack construction does **not** evade internal current-contact and collection cost: the storage-gap volume/heat inequality exposes it directly.

It does not defeat local contact heating, current constriction, compartment/manifold costs, or the missing ore-to-Fe supply interface. The decisive electrical measurement is the attainable combined specific contact resistance and spreading resistance at operating conditions. The decisive systems experiment is a complete cell fed with gangue-bearing reduced ore whose recovered product is measured separately from every preloaded Fe component. No such demonstration or economically credible scaled architecture is established by this note.
