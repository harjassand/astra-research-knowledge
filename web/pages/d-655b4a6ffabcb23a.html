has a parameter-dependent mixture sigma of identical one-qubit product states

FULL Duhamel RECONSTRUCTION

Write s=N^(3/4), C=A+MI>=0, t0=2/N-M/s^2, and diagonalize
C=sum_i lambda_i n_i n_i^T with 0<=lambda_i<=2M. Set F_i=n_i.J,
c_i=lambda_i/s^2, v=b/s,

  H = Q_C(J)/s^2 + v.J = sum_i c_i F_i^2 + v.J,
  K = t0 J^2 + H.

The scalar shift MI is rotationally invariant, and J^2 commutes with H and
each F_i. On every finite SU(2) representation the exact positive heat identity
is

  exp(t J^2) = integral exp(x.J) nu_t(dx),
  nu_t(dx)=(4 pi t)^(-3/2) exp(-t/4-|x|^2/(4t))
           sinh(|x|/2)/(|x|/2) dx.

Rotation invariance makes the integral scalar on each spin-j irrep. Its scalar
is the normalized character integral; differentiating
int_0^infty exp(-r^2/(4t))cosh((j+1/2)r)dr
gives exp(t j(j+1)). The j=0 case gives mass one. Since
exp(x.J)=[exp(x.sigma/2)]^(tensor N), this seed is an iid product mixture.

Define M_H(X)={H,X}/2 and

  L(X)=M_H(X) - (1/4) sum_i c_i [F_i,[F_i,X]].

The sign is important. Because H contains c_i F_i^2, combining its Jordan
term with the double commutator gives the real-filter generator

  L(X)=M_(v.J)(X) + (1/4)sum_i c_i
         (F_i^2 X + 2 F_i X F_i + X F_i^2).

Indeed, with commuting left/right multiplication by F_i,

  exp(delta L_i)(X)
    = E_Z exp(sqrt(c_i delta/2) Z F_i) X
                  exp(sqrt(c_i delta/2) Z F_i), Z~N(0,1).

Each exponential filter is a tensor power of a single-qubit matrix. The field
factor is also a tensor-power sandwich. Finite Lie-Trotter products thus map
positive iid-product mixtures to positive iid-product mixtures; their finite-
dimensional limit does too. Therefore W=exp(L)(exp(t0 J^2)) is positive and
sigma=W/Tr W is fully separable, in fact an iid-product mixture. This argument
does not discard the trace weights of the filters.

On the spin-j block let Y_j=exp(L_j)I_j. Duhamel relative to M_H gives

  Y_j-exp(H_j)
   = -(1/4)sum_i c_i int_0^1 exp((1-q)L_j)
                    [F_i,[F_i,exp(qH_j)]] dq.                 (1)

For positive X, Tr L_j(X)=Tr H_j X and exp(uL_j) is CP, hence
Tr exp(uL_j)X<=exp(u||H_j||)Tr X by Gronwall. Jordan decomposition extends
this induced trace-norm bound to Hermitian X.

To bound the integrand, set H(theta)=exp(i theta F)H exp(-i theta F).
Then exp(q H(theta))=exp(i theta F)exp(qH)exp(-i theta F), whose second
theta derivative at zero is -[F,[F,exp(qH)]]. The ordered-simplex formula for
the first and second Fréchet derivatives of exp yields

  ||[F,[F,exp(qH)]]||_1
   <= d exp(q||H||)
       (q ||[F,[F,H]]|| + q^2 ||[F,H]||^2).                (2)

The q and q^2 coefficients are exact: the two ordered second-derivative
simplexes have total volume one. Integrating them over q in [0,1] gives 1/2
and 1/3. No operator monotonicity of exp is used.

For x=j(j+1)/s^2, ell=j/s, and r=(j+1/2)/s, rotation derivatives annihilate
MI and give

  ||H_j|| <= 2M x+B ell,
  ||[F_i,H_j]|| <= 2M x+B ell,
  ||[F_i,[F_i,H_j]]|| <= 4M x+B ell,
  sum_i c_i=Tr(C)/s^2<=6M/s^2.

Thus (1)-(2) imply

  ||Y_j-exp(H_j)||_1
   <= d_j exp(2Mx+B ell) (3M/(2s^2))
      [2Mx+(B/2)ell+(2Mx+B ell)^2/3].                    (3)

The multiplicity of spin j is
m_j=(2j+1)/(N/2+j+1) binom(N+1,N/2-j). Put
a_j=(2j+1)m_j exp(2j(j+1)/N). Since
t0 j(j+1)=2j(j+1)/N-Mx, multiplying (3) by the heat weight and using
x<=r^2, ell<=r gives the numerator U_N. For the target normalization, directly
use ||Q_A/s^2+b.J/s||<=Mx+B ell and x<=r^2, ell<=r, obtaining

  Tr exp(K) >= D_N,
  D_N=sum_j a_j exp(-M r_j^2-B r_j),
  U_N=sum_j a_j exp(M r_j^2+B r_j)
       [2M r_j^2+(B/2)r_j+(2M r_j^2+B r_j)^2/3].

For positive W,G, T(W/TrW,G/TrG)<=||W-G||_1/TrG. Combining block bounds
proves the displayed finite-N certificate. The critical binomial entropy
quadratic cancels exp(2j(j+1)/N); the scaled spin radius r=j/N^(3/4) has
quartic tail exp(-4r^4/3). Its radial weight is proportional to
r^2 exp(-4r^4/3), up to uniform integrable bounds after every fixed
exp(Cr^2+Dr) tilt. The denominator has positive mass on a compact radial
interval, so U_N/D_N remains bounded for fixed M,B. The certificate has O(N)
public scalar terms and no rare-sector denominator.

SCALAR PRIOR REMAINDER

For r=|x|, z=2N^(-1/4)r and d0=1-M/(2sqrt(N)), the exact unnormalized
scaled seed density is

  f_N,M(r)=sinh(z)/z * exp[N logcosh(z)-2sqrt(N)r^2/d0],
  f_inf,M(r)=exp[-4r^4/3-Mr^2].

For 0<=z<=1, Taylor's theorem and
|(logcosh)^(6)(z)|<=16+136+240+120=512 give
|logcosh(z)-z^2/2+z^4/12|<=512 z^6/720<z^6.
Coefficient comparison using (2k+1)!>=6^k k! gives
0<=log(sinh(z)/z)<=z^2/6. The quadratic terms combine exactly as

  Nz^2/2 - 2sqrt(N)r^2/d0 = -M r^2/d0,

and |M r^2/d0-Mr^2|<=M^2 r^2/sqrt(N) when N>=M^2. Therefore

  |log f_N,M(r)-log f_inf,M(r)|
   <=N^(-1/2)[(2/3+M^2)r^2+64r^6].                        (4)

Both interior densities have a common quartic envelope; for example
f_N,M,f_inf,M <=exp(-r^4/3+2r^2) for N>=M^2. Using
|e^a-e^b|<=|a-b|(e^a+e^b), (4) integrates to O(N^-1/2) in weighted L1
under any fixed tilt exp(Lr^2+Kr), since a quartic exponential dominates every
such tilt and the polynomial r^2+r^6.

For z>=1, logcosh(z)/z^2 is decreasing, so with
delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0 the exact density after a fixed tilt is bounded by a
polynomial times exp(-4 delta sqrt(N) r^2+Lr^2+Kr). This exterior starts at
r=N^(1/4)/2; its integral is exp(-cN). The limiting quartic density has the
same exp(-cN) tilted tail. Normalizers are bounded below on a fixed compact
interval and converge at rate O(N^-1/2), so the normalized tilted prior also
has weighted L1 remainder R_prior,N=O_(M,B)(N^-1/2). This is the scalar term
in the universal product decoder's error bound. It is subdominant to its
separate O(N^-1/4) quantum/product approximation term; it does not transfer the
known-parameter O(N^-3/2) cone-distance rate to a universal channel.
