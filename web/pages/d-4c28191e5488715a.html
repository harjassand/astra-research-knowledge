# Independent rational central-cut localization lemma

7 October2026. stat_exec15, parent-verified gpt-6.1-sol/max; no nested workers. The lemma below is independently reconstructed from the elementary central-cut ellipsoid formula. It does not supply the SOS optimizer, hidden inner center, frame or metric. It is an algorithmic primitive to gate the producer's forthcoming complete reduction. The existing ellipsoid method receives method credit; novelty is not assessed.

## Exact contract

Let q>=2 and E(c,H)={c+H^(1/2)u:||u||<=1}, H rational SPD. Start c=0,H=R²I with known positive rational R. For at most T cuts, each nonzero rational g specifies the retained halfspace g·(x−c)<=0. The intended retained set must be contained in that halfspace: retaining only one unknown optimal point is insufficient for the volume proof. The algorithm will produce rational enclosing ellipsoids, using bit sizes polynomial in q,T,log R and the cut encodings.

Write h=g^THg>0, z=Hg/sqrt(h). The ideal central-cut enclosure is

    c*=c−z/(q+1),
    H*=q²/(q²−1)[H−(2/(q+1))Hgg^TH/h].

Direct conjugation by H^(-1/2) proves H* positive definite and

    (q/(q+1))² H <= H* <= [q²/(q²−1)] H.

The usual cut-ball enclosure follows by rotating g to the first coordinate and completing the square. Its volume ratio is

    [q²/(q²−1)]^(q/2) sqrt((q−1)/(q+1))
        <= exp[−1/(2(q+1))] <= exp[−1/(3q)].

For completeness, the first inequality follows from log(1+x)<=x and log((1−u)/(1+u))<=−2u with u=1/q: log ratio <=q/[2(q²−1)]−1/q=−(q²−2)/[2q(q²−1)] <=−1/[2(q+1)] for q>=2. The exact enclosure/volume formula is a standard premise of the ellipsoid method, not a new theorem about this programme.

## Rational center and shape rounding

Set e=1/(100q²), a=R2^(−T), theta=qe. Approximate 1/sqrt(h) by a positive rational t with |t sqrt(h)−1|<=theta, using rational squared comparisons and dyadic bracketing. Its bit cost is polynomial in the rational h encoding and log(1/theta). Put cu=c−tHg/(q+1). Because ||z||_(H^-1)=1 and H*>=(q/(q+1))²H,

    ||cu−c*||_(H*^-1) <=theta/q=e.

Round cu componentwise on a rational grid of step dc=e a/q. Every rounding error has Euclidean norm at most sqrt(q)dc, hence H* inverse norm at most e, provided lambda_min(H*)>=a². With the rounded center cr, the displacement from c* is at most2e in that norm. Thus

    E(c*,H*) subset E(cr,F), F=(1+3e)² H*.

Round each independent entry of F to a grid of step dh=e a²/(4q), symmetrically, and add q dh I. If the rounded matrix is F0, ||F0−F||op<=q dh (even without using the half-step nearest-rounding gain). Therefore

    F <= Hr:=F0+q dh I <=F+2q dh I <=(1+e)F.

All shapes are rational, contain the intended halfspace intersection, and remain SPD. Their extra volume inflation is at most

    [(1+e)(1+3e)²]^(q/2)
        <=exp[(7/2)qe]=exp[0.035/q].

Combined with the ideal reduction, each actual rounded step shrinks volume by at least exp[−1/(4q)]. This proof accounts for BOTH center and shape rounding; exact rational shape arithmetic alone would not suffice because the ideal center contains sqrt(h).

## Why the fixed grids are legal, and their heights

Every rounded shape satisfies Hr>=H*>=(q/(q+1))²H>=H/4. Induction gives lambda_min(H_j)>=R²4^(−j)>=a² for j<=T, exactly the bound used above. Conversely Hr<=2H, so lambda_max(H_j)<=R²2^j. The centers move by at most a constant times the current longest axis per cut, hence their norms are at most a constant times R2^(T/2) plus the fixed grid errors.

The fixed rational grids have logarithmic denominator size O(T+log q+encoding(R)). All rounded entries and centers are polynomial-bit numbers in those parameters. A temporary exact update uses rational matrix arithmetic on the current shape/cut, and the reciprocal square-root approximation has polynomial-bit encoding in those inputs. The grids prevent recursive denominator growth across T iterations; the claim is not inferred merely from having T polynomially many arithmetic operations. Exact inverse/LDL and rational input-gradient costs must still be included by the caller.

For q=1 use an interval: retain the appropriate half-interval and choose its rational midpoint. The q²−1 formula above is undefined there. A direct n1 quartic branch can instead avoid SDP/spectral acquisition entirely when composing the hidden-QPD learner.

## Unknown-center feasibility and smooth optimization

Suppose an unknown-center ball of radius r>0 is contained in the feasible body, and the body is in the initial radius-R ball. If every center is infeasible and its separation cut retains the entire body, every ellipsoid retains that ball. But after

    T>4q² ln(R/r)

the volume upper is smaller than the ball's volume, a contradiction. A rational computable horizon is8q²[1+ceil(log2(max(1,R/r)))]. Therefore a feasible center MUST be acquired before that horizon. The center of the inner ball need not be given. A known feasible rational center, however, cannot be assumed when invoking a source theorem that explicitly requires one.

For a smooth concave objective Phi with interior maximizer x*, suppose an acquired public-margin proof supplies a radius-r ball around x* where Phi>=Phi(x*)−kappa. If a feasible center c has objective gap>kappa, concavity implies for every x in that ball

    gradient Phi(c)·(x−c) >=Phi(x)−Phi(c)>0.

The opposite-oriented central gradient cut retains the whole near-optimal ball. Infeasible-center cuts also retain it. The same volume argument therefore forces a center with gap<=kappa. Approximate objective evaluation must choose among all feasible centers with an explicit extra error budget; it does not affect the exact rational gradient cuts. If the objective is sigma-strongly concave in the affine coordinates, a gap b gives distance <=sqrt(2b/sigma), after charging all evaluation error.

For the SOS objective logdet M+mu logdet B, exact gradients use rational inverses at rational feasible centers; log values require a separate finite-bit approximation. On a public positive-margin ball, Hessian bounds are obtained directly from tr(X^-1 dX X^-1 dX), and stationarity at x* gives a quadratic near-optimal ball. With ||dB||F>=||dx|| and ||B||op<=3Sf+1, the objective is at least mu/(3Sf+1)² strongly concave. These facts support the proposed reduction, but its precise margin/coordinate-error/dual-certificate constants and log routine remain to be gated separately. This note does NOT equate central-point existence with optimization.
