## What a Comparator run would establish

The repository's `lean/ComparatorChallenges/README.md` gives a setup command and links per-paper scope notes. Comparator's official README says a successful run checks that the named solution theorem proves the same statement as the trusted challenge, uses only configured axioms, and is accepted by Lean's kernel. Its trust boundary includes the challenge and its transitive imports/Lake files, `landrun`, `lean4export`, the Lean kernel, and the operating system/hardware/sandbox; it also requires an unprivileged run and warns against compiling a potentially adversarial solution before the challenge. All selected configs inspected here (003's four targets, 197's four targets, 277, and 287) permit only `propext`, `Quot.sound`, and `Classical.choice`, and set `enable_nanoda: false` (no second kernel is configured). This is a formal statement check, not an audit that the challenge captures every claim or hypothesis in a paper, not external mathematical review, and not novelty/priority validation.

No Comparator run was performed. Lean/Lake shims and the repository's Lean 4.34.1 toolchain are present; `comparator`, `landrun`, and `lean4export` are absent from PATH. I did not build the corpus. The formalization catalogue ends with `review.status: unchecked`.

## Family 003 — quasi-Riemann hypothesis

Paper claim: all Dirichlet L-functions and finite-order Hecke L-functions over `Q(sqrt(-3))` have no zeros for `Re(s)>7/8`, allowing the principal pole at 1; this implies the same zero-free region for zeta and resolves the quasi-Riemann hypothesis (`preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/build/paper.tex:37-43, 77-86`).

Formal scope note: Lean documents zeta, every Dirichlet character at every positive modulus, and finite-order Hecke characters over that field, with the principal-character point `s=1` omitted. It also documents a uniform real-zero exclusion `1-beta >= c/log(q)` for primitive nonprincipal real characters, `q>=3`, both parities, one unspecified `c>0`; it does not exclude real zeros outside that interval. Later paper applications are outside the formal scope (`lean/docs/003.md:8-25`). The four Comparator mappings (zeta, Dirichlet, Hecke, Siegel-zero gap) appear in `lean/formalization.yaml` and their JSON configs allow only the three core axioms above.

Status: the repository has formal targets for the claims in the scope note, but none was replayed here. The formalization is narrower than every application in the paper and gives no explicit Siegel-gap constant. After a targeted Comparator run, the decisive remaining check is a mathematical source-to-formalization audit of the exact theorem and imported definitions; neither the catalogue nor Comparator establishes the truth of the paper's prose by itself.

## Family 197 — direct finiteness, determinant, and cellular automata

Current family headline: the October 4 paper claims a finitely presented torsion-free group with a direct-finiteness counterexample over `F_2`; its abstract also claims a finite 2-dimensional classifying complex (`CONTENTS.md:4491-4500`; its source abstract is `preprints/A-Torsion-Free-Group-Algebra-That-Is-Not-Directly-Finite-October-4-2026/build/paper.tex:36-40`).

That is not what the linked Lean scope note says it formalizes. For the earlier characteristic-two construction, the selected finitely-presented theorem explicitly includes an element of odd prime order in the group; it therefore does not establish torsion-freeness. The docs say nonsoficity is outside the formalized statements. The separate determinant formalization proves an invertible group-ring matrix with Fuglede–Kadison determinant in `(0,1)`, but omits the paper's spectral-measure integral formulation. The odd-characteristic result uses a group with torsion (`lean/docs/197.md:9-27`). The current `KaplanskyFinitelyPresented` challenge and solution theorem both state the odd-prime torsion witness (`lean/ComparatorChallenges/KaplanskyFinitelyPresented.lean:7-17, 33-40`; `OAI/RingTheory/DirectFiniteness/FinitelyPresented.lean:33-40`).

Status: the October 4 torsion-free result is present in the current catalogue but has no matching entry in `lean/docs/197.md`, `lean/formalization.yaml`, or a Comparator theorem. The older characteristic-two formalization cannot certify that new result because its witness is required to have torsion. The family page also states all-configurations cellular-automaton claims; distinguish the individual formalized scopes before treating those as covered. Next decisive check: specify and prove the October 4 theorem with torsion-freeness in the formal statement, then compare its full statement against the PDF, finite presentation, and finite 2-complex claims. Do not substitute the existing torsionful target.

## Family 277 — threshold parallel repetition

Paper claim: for every finite two-player game with arbitrary correlated questions, value `v<1`, and `0<delta<1-v`, the paper proves universal exponential decay with rate proportional to `delta^5/(1+log(|A||B|))`; it separately claims an explicit cubic rate for each fixed question distribution (`CONTENTS.md:6963-6972`; the proof of the universal rate is `build/sections/04-threshold.tex:214-230`).

Formal scope: arbitrary correlated questions and every finite-dimensional joint strategy, including the supremum without attainment, but the exponent is `delta^13`, not the paper's `delta^5`; the distribution-dependent cubic bound is explicitly outside scope (`lean/docs/277.md:7-15`; target `lean/OAI/Probability/EntangledGames/Main.lean:9-18`). Since `0<delta<1`, the `delta^13` estimate is weaker. The current Comparator config targets this weaker theorem and the YAML maps it.

Status: the formal result supports qualitative exponential threshold repetition under its formal game/strategy definitions, but does not formalize the published quantitative exponent or the cubic refinement. Next check: either accept and clearly label the weaker `delta^13` formal theorem, or add a challenge for the `delta^5` claim and a separate challenge for the fixed-distribution cubic result; then replay only those configs.

## Family 287 — free group factors

Paper claim: a normal, unital, trace-preserving isomorphism between `L(F_2)` and `L(F_3)`, from which the paper invokes the classical alternative to conclude all interpolated free group factors, including the infinite-rank factor, are isomorphic and their common fundamental group is `R_{>0}` (`CONTENTS.md:7159-7168`; paper `build/main.tex:44-76`).

Formal scope note and target: all parameters `r,s>1`, including infinity, with a normal trace-preserving equivalence; the fundamental-group conclusion is not the selected Comparator statement (`lean/docs/287.md:7-15`). The standalone config `ComparatorChallenges/InterpolatedFactors.json` targets `OAI.FreeGroupFactorMain.Interpolation.allInterpolatedIsomorphic` and allows only the three core axioms. Its challenge and solution encode group von Neumann algebras, normalized corners, traces, and ultraweak topologies in their Lean definitions (`ComparatorChallenges/InterpolatedFactors.lean`; source proof at `OAI/Analysis/InterpolatedFactors/Interpolation.lean:33-37, 101-110`).

Status: the theorem and config exist, but a search of `formalization.yaml` finds no `InterpolatedFactors`, `allInterpolatedIsomorphic`, or free-group-factor record; do not count it as a registered/replayed Comparator result from this snapshot. No run was performed. Next check: audit the challenge's encoded factor/corner definitions against the paper's standard `L(F_r)` objects, then run this single Comparator config in the trusted environment. ## Recent removals and repairs

The October 7 history says 3 of the earlier 722 manuscripts were withdrawn, bringing the current catalogue to 719. The load-bearing correction is concrete: in the withdrawn split-abelian-eightfold proof, `I(f_1)=-m` but each reverse stabilization trace has sign `-1` after accounting for the two cusp-branch orientations, so the claimed zero signed count is actually `I_new=-2m != 0`. The invoked Eliashberg–Murphy cancellation hypothesis then fails. The K3 Kuga–Satake and products-of-K3 Hodge papers adapt that flawed construction and are also withdrawn. The notices expressly say this withdraws the proofs and does not establish the statements are false (`history.md:7-15`; split-eightfold notice `README.md:4-14`; K3 notices at `README.md:4-8`).

The history also records 14 revised manuscripts: Ashkin–Teller/Lipschitz repairs, Kähler MMP/abundance hypothesis and dependency clarifications, a corrected cone-equality claim and removed cone-comparison dependency, revised torus-projection/common-clock estimates, and an obsolete citation removed from the low-Selmer-corank BSD paper; 13 additional companion references were updated (`history.md:17-31`). It does not name a direct correction to families 003, 197, 277, or 287. Family 197 does include the newer October 4 torsion-free claim, which the current Lean scope does not cover.

The repository retains other K3/Weil manuscripts distinct from the three withdrawal notices. Their presence does not restore the withdrawn conclusions; any proposed route using them needs an independent dependency audit against the sign error and its stated hypotheses.
