# Independent gate: the symmetric-slot estimate in 093

This reconstructs manuscript Lemma 6.2 (`lem:matrix-copied-slots`) from finite-dimensional linear algebra and the three-lines theorem. It is an internal proof check of a scoped lemma, not certification of the full main theorem. In particular it does not establish the later weighted clipping, frozen diffusion limit, or scalar information extraction. No finite numerical test is used as a proof.

Let V be a finite-dimensional real inner-product space. Let `psi=direct_sum_d psi_d`, where each psi_d is a symmetric tensor in V^{tensor d}, every d>=k>=2, and only finitely many d occur. The tensors need not be normalized. Let rho be the sum of their one-slot reductions, let R=sqrt(rho), and let H be self-adjoint. On each component let H_av be the average of H acting on the d existing slots. The claim is

\[
\|H_{\rm av}\psi\|^2
\le \operatorname{tr}(RHRH)+\frac{2}{k}\operatorname{tr}(\rho H^2).
\tag{1}
\]

For each component put `a_d=<psi_d,H_(1)^2 psi_d>` and `c_d=<psi_d,H_(1)H_(2) psi_d>`. Slot symmetry and Cauchy–Schwarz give |c_d|<=a_d. Expansion of the squared average gives

\[
\|H_{\rm av}\psi\|^2
=\sum_d \left(c_d+\frac{a_d-c_d}{d}\right)
\le\sum_d c_d+\frac2k\sum_d a_d.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus it suffices to show `|sum c_d|<=tr(RHRH)`. The arithmetic quantity `tr(rho H^2)` is not an adequate replacement if the later sharp saturation argument is to work.

Define the complex-linear correlation map C by

\[
\operatorname{tr}(X C(Y))
=\sum_d\langle\psi_d,X_{(1)}Y_{(2)}\psi_d\rangle.
\tag{3}
\]

After fixing all remaining tensor indices, the two exposed indices give matrices A_alpha. Direct contraction gives

\[
C(Y)=\sum_\alpha A_\alpha Y^{\mathsf T}A_\alpha^*,
\qquad C(I)=C^*(I)=\rho.
\tag{4}
\]

The transpose is complex-linear and preserves positivity. Symmetry of the first two slots supplies the common marginal in (4); independence of the remaining slots is not assumed. Consequently C and C* are positive, with identical marginals rho.

First restrict all correlation expressions to the support S of rho. For v in its kernel, the sum of the nonnegative squared contractions `<v,rho v>` is zero, so each tensor has zero contraction against v on every slot. Thus each tensor lies entirely in S^{tensor d}. Compressing H to S leaves c_d and `tr(RHRH)` unchanged. The original `tr(rho H^2)` can only exceed its compressed counterpart, because the omitted term is `tr(rho H(I-P_S)H)>=0`. This makes (1) valid even when H sends a supported vector into the kernel. No inverse of a zero eigenvalue is taken.

On S, set `U(Y)=rho^{-1/2}C(Y)rho^{-1/2}`. It is unital and positive, and

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\rho U(Y))=\operatorname{tr}(\rho Y).
\tag{5}
\]

Its operator-norm contraction can be seen directly, without requiring complete positivity of the transpose. The row of matrices `B_alpha=rho^{-1/2}A_alpha` obeys `sum B_alpha B_alpha*=I`. Accordingly U is an isometric compression of a block diagonal array of copies of Y^T. Since transpose preserves the operator norm, `||U(Y)||_op<=||Y||_op` for arbitrary complex Y. The adjoint of every normalized map below has the analogous positive compression representation.

For the strip 0<=Re z<=1 define

\[
U_z(Y)=\rho^{z/2}
 U(\rho^{-z/2}Y\rho^{-z/2})\rho^{z/2}.
\tag{6}
\]

On Re z=0 the imaginary powers on the left and right are unitary, so (6) is an operator-norm contraction. At z=1,

\[
U_1(Y)=C(\rho^{-1/2}Y\rho^{-1/2}),
\qquad U_1^*(I)=I.
\tag{7}
\]

The adjoint is unital with the same transpose/compression form, hence contractive in operator norm. Trace duality makes U_1 contractive in trace norm. Imaginary powers preserve this conclusion on Re z=1. Normalization of rho to trace one is unnecessary.

For completeness, let A and B have Hilbert–Schmidt norm one. Choose fixed singular-vector factors and continue A's singular values by powers 2z, giving an analytic A_z, and continue the singular values of B* by powers 2(1−z), giving an analytic paired matrix B_z. Then A_{1/2}=A and B_{1/2}=B*. On Re z=0, A_z has operator norm one and B_z has trace norm one; on Re z=1 their roles are reversed. The analytic scalar function `tr(B_z U_z(A_z))` is therefore bounded by one on both boundary lines. Three-lines gives

\[
|\operatorname{tr}(B^*U_{1/2}(A))|\le1.
\]

The finite-dimensional strip functions are bounded on the strip; imaginary powers do not cause growth. Approximation handles zero singular values of A and B. Taking the supremum over B proves

\[
\|U_{1/2}(A)\|_{\rm HS}\le\|A\|_{\rm HS}.
\tag{8}
\]

Apply (8) to `A=rho^{1/4}Hrho^{1/4}`. Its squared HS norm is tr(RHRH), and cyclicity of trace gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A U_{1/2}(A))=\operatorname{tr}(H C(H))=\sum_d c_d.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz and (8) prove the desired correlation bound. Equations (2) and `sum a_d=tr(rho H^2)` prove (1). Every estimate has constant one or the stated 2/k; no dimension enters.

An exact adversarial example shows why the finite-slot error matters. In dimension two take `psi_k=e_0^{tensor k}`, `rho=e_0e_0*`, and `H=e_0e_1*+e_1e_0*`. The k tensors obtained by flipping a single slot are orthonormal, hence

\[
\|H_{\rm av}\psi_k\|^2=1/k,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(RHRH)=0,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(\rho H^2)=1.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus the correction cannot be removed, or replaced by a uniform o(1/k) coefficient. A nonsingular version is `psi_k=sqrt(1-p)e_0^{tensor k}+sqrt(p)e_1^{tensor k}` for k>=3 and 0<p<1. It has `rho=diag(1-p,p)`, squared average 1/k, and geometric term `2sqrt(p(1-p))`, which vanishes as p→0. This exposes both the kernel and the small-eigenvalue boundary analytically.

The source's next energy step also checks algebraically. Writing
`e=E||DR||²`, `a=sum_i E tr(R²H_i²)`, `c=sum_i E||[H_i,R]||²`, and `d=E||Dpsi||²`, the Sylvester relation gives

\[
e=a-c/4,\qquad
\sum_iE\operatorname{tr}(RH_iRH_i)=e-c/4.
\]

The tensor derivative identity and (1) give `e²<=d(e−c/4+2a/k)`, while the explicit Sylvester solution gives a<=4e. If d→1, k→infinity and liminf e>=1, then `e<=d(1+8/k)` forces e→1; the same inequality forces c→0, and a→1. This completes the scoped saturation implication. The genuinely remaining checks begin when this initially unbounded matrix column is modified by weighted tail clipping and logarithmic spectral blocks.
