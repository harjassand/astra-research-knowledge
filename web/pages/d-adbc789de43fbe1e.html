# Annealing ratio-estimator cost clarification

The INITIAL checkpoint's shorthand ratio-cost line should be read as the cost per stage only under an additional choice of variance accounting; the safe total count for estimating the full product is as follows.

For one adjacent pair, put R_t=w_t/w_{t-1} and r_t=E_{π_{t-1}}R_t=Z_t/Z_{t-1}. The normalized variable X_t=R_t/r_t equals π_t/π_{t-1}, so if this density ratio is at most 2 pointwise, E X_t=1 and E X_t²<=2. An average of n independent X_t samples therefore has relative variance at most 1/n.

Take independent stage estimates and their product. Its mean is one after normalization and its relative variance is

  product_t (1+CV_t²) - 1 <= (1+1/n)^T - 1 <= exp(T/n)-1.

Thus n=Theta(T/epsilon²) samples per stage make the product's relative variance O(epsilon²). A constant number of independent product repetitions and a median increase success to a constant; O(log(1/delta)) repetitions give failure at most delta. This gives O(T² epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) independent samples total. Each sample must be fresh or sufficiently separated from the preceding one; treating correlated Markov-chain states as independent is invalid without an autocorrelation/spectral bound. If one instead estimates every ratio to relative error epsilon/T and unions stage failures, the simpler bound is O(T³ epsilon^-2 log(T/delta)) samples total.

The product-chain gap itself gives a warm-start-free implementation: for each sample from π_{t-1}, mix the replicated chain over the truncated path 0,…,t−1 from any known state, with TV error budget divided across all sample calls. If H=max_j log(1/min π_j), then the slot-product minimum mass obeys Λ<=q(T+1)H. With q=32L and M=Tq²+q, the displayed gap 3/(8M) gives O(M(Λ+log(S/delta))) steps per call for S total calls. Multiplying by the sample count above, plus per-step bit cost and any approximate-refresh error, is the full conditional charge. This remains polynomial when L,T,H and all exact transition bit costs are polynomial.

The exact finite importance-variance example remains a counterexample only to a direct large jump; it does not establish a lower bound when an overlap-controlled path is inserted. No determinant-specific path/kernel has yet been acquired.
