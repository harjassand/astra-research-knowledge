# Cycle 2: numerical compilation at the exact/noisy oracle boundary

## Result and scope

For a rational recognizable series with a *supplied* bounded-height realization, every response to a jointly strictly upper-triangular rational matrix query has an explicit common denominator bound. This gives a deterministic rounded-exact compiler: `2 h_*+2` fractional bits suffice to reconstruct each exact entry, after charging integer range, all returned entries, rational query descriptions, reconstruction time, and the learner's own adaptive query-generation cost. It does not give a polynomial-sample Gaussian compiler: the direct uniform averaging method is sufficient at `O(σ² Q_*^4)` samples per scalar entry, and the separate scaled-word testing family below gives a genuine `Θ(Q^4)` lower bound for a particular coefficient-resolution problem. The lower bound comes from the Gaussian KL/excitation interface, not from rational denominator separation alone.

These are internal theorems for the stated rational subclass and channel contracts. They neither validate L01's universal exact learner nor settle priority. The prior-art correction and pinned-source caveats are in [the first-cycle boundary audit](FINAL_REPORT.md); the formulas and proofs are in [the proof sheet](COMPILER_PROOF.md).

## Rational-response compiler

Let `f(w)=λᵀA_{w₁}⋯A_{w_k}γ` be a rational minimal realization of dimension `r≤m` over an `n`-symbol alphabet, with `f(ε)=0`. Suppose every reduced realization numerator and denominator is at most `2^H` in magnitude. Let each query be a rational tuple `X_1,…,X_n` of dimension `D`, jointly strictly upper triangular, with each reduced entry numerator and denominator at most `2^B`. Let `q_λ,q_γ,q_A,q_X` be products of the denominators of all entries in the respective parameter blocks. Then

`h_* := ceil(log₂(q_λ q_γ (q_A q_X)^(D−1))) ≤ 2rH +(D−1)(nr²H+nD²B)`.

Writing `S=Σ_i A_i⊗X_i`, strict triangularity gives `S^D=0` and

`f(X)=(λᵀ⊗I_D)(I+S+⋯+S^(D−1))(γ⊗I_D)`.

Every output denominator divides `Q_*=q_λq_γ(q_Aq_X)^(D−1)`. The response magnitude is at most `2^hmag`, where

`hmag = ceil(2H+log₂(rD)+(D−1)(H+B+log₂(nrD)))`.

Thus an exact dense response has `D²` entries and at most `O(D²(h_*+hmag+1))` rational payload bits.

For a rounded-exact service, define `p` to be the number of binary places after the radix point, with nearest-grid absolute error at most `2^(−p−1)`. The condition `p≥2h_*+2` puts every returned value within `1/(2Q_*²)` of its exact rational. Bounded-denominator rational reconstruction by continued fractions then uniquely recovers every entry. Per query: one call, dimension `D`, `D²` returned entries, query-description length `L_X=O(nD²(B+1))` in dense encoding, and `D²(p+hmag+O(1))` returned bits. Schoolbook continued fractions cost `O(D²(p+hmag+h_*)²)` bit operations and `O(D²(p+hmag+h_*))` working bits. An adaptive exact learner is simulated transcript-for-transcript if it computes each next rational query from the already reconstructed history; its own query generation and arithmetic are additional costs. No height/dimension/query-length bound is inferred by the compiler.

An optional local evaluator, when the realization is available rather than hidden, forms `S` in `O(nr²D²)` rational operations and evaluates the nilpotent sum using `D−1` dense `(rD)×(rD)` multiplications, `O(r³D⁴)` rational operations. Intermediate bit lengths are `O(D(h_A+h_X+H+B+log(nrD))+h_λ+h_γ)`, with `h_A≤nr²H`, `h_X≤nD²B`, `h_λ,h_γ≤rH`. This is a local arithmetic bound, not an acquisition guarantee.

For at most `T` adaptive exact queries of dimension at most `D`, use each round's actual `h_*` (or the uniform maximum) and reconstruct before issuing the next query. This makes the transcript simulation deterministic under the stated rounding guarantee; no union bound is needed.

## Gaussian channel: sufficient compiler and separate lower bounds

Suppose each scalar response is observed with independent `N(0,σ²)` noise and query repetition is allowed. A uniform compiler can estimate each response entry to `ε=1/(4Q_*²)`. Repeating each exact-learner query

`s ≥ 32 σ² Q_*^4 ln(4TD²/δ)`

times makes every entrywise sample mean accurate to `ε` with probability at least `1−δ/2` over `T D²` entries; continued fractions then reproduce the exact adaptive transcript. **This is a sufficient worst-case simulation cost, not a necessary cost inferred from denominator separation.** For an ideal real Gaussian channel no finite output-bit count exists. For a digital adapter, quantize each sample at spacing `η=1/(4Q_*²)`, clip at

`R=2^hmag + σ sqrt(2 ln(4N/δ))`, `N=T s D²`,

and encode each clipped sample in `b_G=ceil(log₂(2+2R/η))` bits. Clipping fails with probability at most `δ/2`; averaging plus quantization error is at most `3/(8Q_*²)`, so reconstruction succeeds with total failure at most `δ`. Per simulated query the complete cost is `s` calls of dimension `D`, `sD²` scalar samples/returned entries, `sD²b_G` returned bits, and `sD` total query dimension. Query descriptions cost `sL_X` bits if retransmitted, or `L_X` plus protocol overhead if cached. Accumulation takes `O(sD²b_G)` bit operations and reconstruction `O(D²(b_G+h_*+hmag)²)`. A per-call excitation cap `Σ_iX_iᵀX_i⪯EI` is paid on every repeat: cumulative excitation opportunity is `sE`; repetition is not free. Multiply by `T` for totals.

Two independent lower bounds explain why the stochastic cost can be large.

1. For `f_w(u)=1[u=w]`, `w∈[n]^d`, minimal Hankel rank is `d+1`, with an acyclic `0/1` realization. Under the per-call excitation cap and adaptive Gaussian observations, the reconstructed energy/KL proof gives

   `C₀ := E₀ Σ_t D_t ≥ 2σ² a^(−2)(n/E)^d kl(1−δ,δ)`

   for testing zero against every scaled target `a f_w`, `a≠0`. Since each full response has `D_t²≥D_t` entries, returned-entry/sample cost obeys the same lower bound. The `a^−2` factor follows by multiplying every hidden-word mean by `a`; the `(n/E)^d` factor follows from `Σ_w||X_w||_F²≤D E^d` and the adaptive KL chain rule. This is a source-card-scoped internal reconstruction, not an external verification of N25.

2. To obtain a direct `Q^4` lower bound without confusing it with the sufficient averaging bound, fix one word `w` and compare `g_0=(1/q)f_w` against `g_1=(1/(q+1))f_w`. Both are rational rank-`d+1` series of entry height `H=ceil(log₂(q+1))`. An integer, strictly upper-triangular dimension-`d+1` query places a unit edge at each position of `w`, obeys `Σ_iX_iᵀX_i⪯I`, and returns the coefficient as its `(1,d+1)` entry. The exact response gap is `Δ=1/[q(q+1)]`; the denominators of this distinguishing response are at most `Q=q+1`. For any adaptive Gaussian strategy, `||X_w||_F²≤D E^d`, so the KL chain rule under `g_0` and binary data processing imply

   `E_{g0} Σ_t D_t ≥ 2σ²q²(q+1)² E^(−d) kl(1−δ,δ)`.

   At `E=1`, this is `Θ(σ²Q^4)` for fixed `δ`, with at least as many returned scalar entries. Here the quartic order is established by an actual same-class two-target testing lower bound; it is not deduced from denominator separation. The learner may use any admissible adaptive query, not just the explicit exact query.

For fixed-point rounding with grid spacing `2^(−b)`, the hidden-word pair `0,f_w` also gives a direct information-loss bound: if `C₀=E₀Σ_tD_t`, then

`C₀ ≥ (1−2δ)2^(−2b−2)(n/E)^d`.

Along the null transcript, total word-output energy below `2^(−2b−2)` forces every entry to round to zero and prevents transcript divergence; Markov plus the word-energy lemma proves the inequality. This is a fixed absolute-grid statement and does not cover floating point with unbounded exponent.
