# Finite calibration certifies a robust comparison with confidence, not an exact unknown law

## Exact interface and claim boundary

Let there be H labelled scientific states, A available source actions, one target channel, finite signal alphabets of size at most K, positive known action costs c_a, and fresh conditionally independent observations. Calibration can prepare each labelled state and obtain n independent samples of each channel row. Label preparation, resets, target measurements and source measurements must all be charged. The row supports, finite state class, stationarity and a probability floor beta>0 are supplied modelling restrictions. Finite data do not establish those restrictions exactly.

Write d^a_ij=KL(F^a_i||F^a_j), g_ij=KL(G_i||G_j). The known-law controlled-menu rate is

    alpha_i = min sum_a c_a w_a
              subject to w_a>=0, sum_a w_a d^a_ij >= g_ij for every j!=i.

Team D's exact-conversion claim gives this rate an arbitrary finite-output operational interpretation conditional on known true laws. This worker has not independently reconstructed that whole controlled-menu conversion proof. The calibration comparison below is a complete independent implication about these coefficients. Fixed-source [Li-Liu v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19897v2), Theorems 1-2, supplies the recent known-law context; source dates and interface were independently read. Equality cases are not treated as strict superiority.

## Theorem 1: robust finite-menu comparison from simultaneous intervals

Suppose a confidence region C covers every true channel row with probability at least 1-delta, and compute valid directed KL bounds d^-<=d<=d^+, g^-<=g<=g^+ for every model in C. Define

    U_i = min sum_a c_a w_a, sum_a w_a d^-a_ij >= g^+_ij, w>=0;
    L_i = min sum_a c_a w_a, sum_a w_a d^+a_ij >= g^-_ij, w>=0.

On the common coverage event, L_i<=alpha_i<=U_i simultaneously for every i. Proof: every feasible vector for U_i is feasible for the true programme; every vector feasible for the true programme is feasible for L_i. This also covers infinite/infeasible values with the usual conventions. Correlations between different KL interval endpoints cause no soundness problem: the rectangular relaxation is conservative.

Consequently U_i<=1-gamma for every i certifies a statewise positive rate margin with probability at least 1-delta. If L_i>1 for some i, source observations cost more than target observations in that state for long full target transcripts, by stopped-transcript KL and data processing. Neither conclusion is an unconditional exact law statement. Any data-dependent selection among the finite menu preserves the guarantee because the coverage event is simultaneous. Fixed-n confidence intervals cannot be reused at arbitrary data-dependent calibration stopping times without a confidence sequence or a predeclared check schedule.

This statistical certificate says that the true supplied-class model has a coefficient property. It does not output the true model's exact simulator, discover a correct state class, or evaluate exact policy kernels.

## A constructive confidence region and explicit budget

Let empirical row probabilities be p_hat. There are at most R=(A+1)HK categorical coordinates. Coordinate Hoeffding plus a union bound gives simultaneous error at most

    e = sqrt(log(2R/delta)/(2n)).

The derivation follows from Pr(|p_hat-p|>e)<=2exp(-2ne^2), summed over coordinates. Assume e<=beta/2. True coordinates are at least beta and empirical coordinates then at least beta/2. Along the segment between true and empirical rows, differentiate KL(p||q): its p derivative is log(p/q)+1 and q derivative is -p/q. Their absolute values are bounded by 1+log(2/beta) and 2/beta respectively. Therefore

    |KL(p||q)-KL(p_hat||q_hat)| <= L e,
    L=K[1+log(2/beta)+2/beta].

Use estimate plus/minus Le, truncate the lower endpoint at zero. All row intervals/normalizations may also be optimized directly to tighten this coarse bound. If intervals' numerical LP/log calculations are advertised as proofs, outward rounding or exact lower/upper certificates must be used.

For a prospective guarantee suppose feasible true-law witness vectors w_i have cost<=1-gamma, sum weights<=W, and every constraint has positive slack at least sigma. On coverage d^- >= d-2Le and g^+ <= g+2Le. Thus robust slack is at least sigma-2Le(W+1). It stays at least sigma/2 when e<=sigma/[4L(W+1)]. A sufficient calibration count **per channel row** is

    n >= max{2/beta^2, 8 L^2 (W+1)^2/sigma^2} log(2R/delta).

There are (A+1)H rows, so measurements total at most (A+1)H ceil(n), before preparation/reset costs. This is conservative and can be impractical near small probability floors or vanishing margins. A true cost margin often lets a feasible vector be slightly inflated to obtain KL constraint slack, but this observation is not a free known witness or a sharp bound.

## Theorem 2: zero-error finite calibration certification is impossible across a KL boundary

Take binary symmetric source F_p=(Bern(p),Bern(1-p)) with p in an open interval around known q>1/2, and target G_q. The two directed source KL rates are

    D(p)=(2p-1) log[p/(1-p)].

D is strictly increasing for p>1/2. Thus p>q has strict KL dominance; p<q fails it. The failure follows directly by asking for the full n-observation target transcript: exact simulation requires E_i N D(p)>=nD(q), so E_i N<=n is impossible when p<q. The positive known-law dominance implication invokes Li-Liu's theorem, rather than being reproved here.

Consider any calibration-only procedure that emits a dominance certificate after finitely many observations. Its calibration actions and independent randomization may be adaptive. Every finite transcript has positive likelihood under every p in this class; on a fixed finite transcript, the external randomness law is the same under each p. Hence for every n the laws restricted to observations/randomness through time n are mutually absolutely continuous. If the certificate has positive probability under some p_good>q and is emitted at a finite time, its event intersects {tau<=n} with positive probability for some n, and that same finite-time event has positive probability under any p_bad<q. Therefore it cannot have zero false-positive probability throughout the class.

This remains true for an unbounded calibration stopping time whose certificate is emitted after finitely many observations on a positive-probability event. It is not merely a fixed-n lower bound. Abstention can keep error zero only by never issuing a nontrivial finite certificate. Waiting for an infinite sequence is outside a finite-latency calibration interface.

For delta-sound certification with power>=1-delta at p_good=q+h and false-positive probability<=delta at p_bad=q-h, data processing for the certificate bit gives

    E_good tau >= [(1-2delta)log((1-delta)/delta)] / KL(Bern(q+h)||Bern(q-h)).

The stopped KL identity holds for integrable tau because increments are bounded on this full-support pair; complementary state labels do not change this per-measurement KL. At small h, the denominator is 2h^2/[q(1-q)]+O(h^3). Thus calibration needs order h^-2 log(1/delta) measurements, and no uniform finite guarantee spans arbitrarily small positive margins. Numerically q=.8,h=.01,delta=.05 requires at least 2145.40 expected calibration samples for these power/error constraints.

This does **not** prove that exact simulation with unknown channels and continued sampling is impossible. Bernoulli factories can realize nontrivial known functions of an unknown coin bias, sometimes with integrable unbounded stopping. [Holtz-Nazarov-Peres v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1936v3) explicitly studies this interface; only its abstract was independently read here, while r08_information inspected the relevant theorem. Such simulation need not reveal a true parameter or issue a zero-error dominance certificate. Finite separated model catalogues can be selected with probability>=1-delta, yielding an exactly known channel conditional on correct selection; that remains a confidence event, not zero-error calibration.

## Theorem 3: arbitrary future stopping policies amplify tiny channel mismatch

Let fitted one-step law be Bern(q), actual law Bern(q-e), q away from the endpoints. Their one-step total variation distance is e. A target policy takes exactly N measurements and reports the binary event that the sample average is at least q-e/2. Hoeffding gives fitted event probability at least 1-exp(-Ne^2/2), actual event probability at most exp(-Ne^2/2). Hence output TV is at least

    1-2exp(-Ne^2/2).

For every e>0 this approaches one as N grows. The output remains binary, and the stopping time is deterministic and integrable. Thus even bounded stopping policies of unrestricted horizon defeat any law-independent uniform output approximation bound tending to zero with one-step mismatch. Adaptive stopping is not needed for this obstruction; allowing it cannot repair the missing horizon assumption. At q=.8,e=.01,N=105967, the output TV is at least .9900003.

For a **fixed** policy with integrable reference stopping time T and uniform one-step discrepancy e across all state/action pairs, maximal coupling until the first disagreement gives

    TV(output_actual,output_reference) <= min{1,e E_reference T}.

Proof: the chance of first disagreement on step t is at most e times the probability that the reference policy has not stopped before t. Summation gives e E T. Actions and stopping decisions coincide while coupled histories agree. This bound concerns output distributions; it does not automatically transfer expected cost.

## Theorem 4: fitted finite expected cost alone does not control actual cost

Fitted coin p=1/2, actual coin r=51/100. A policy samples k times, then makes H=2^k additional requests only when every one of the first k samples is one. Its fitted expected cost is k+1, while its actual expected cost is k+(51/50)^k. Every instance has a deterministic finite maximum k+2^k and finite expectation under both laws. Nevertheless the cost ratio is unbounded as k grows, despite one-step TV=.01. At k=1000 the respective expectations are 1001 and about 398265651.66.

Therefore an expected fitted-sample bound does not give robust actual-cost control without a hard cap, quantitative tail certificate, or a suitable likelihood/moment bound. r08_information supplies a stronger infinite-mean counterexample and a hard-cap repair; this independent finite construction establishes failure even when actual expectation remains finite.

## Illustrative scientific finite-menu budget

Use Team D's three-state menu. Action a returns a Bernoulli signal with success p in state a and 1-p otherwise; target returns the correct state with probability q and each wrong state with (1-q)/2. Common symmetry and the parameters' shared action/state dependence are **supplied class restrictions**. One known-label scalar calibration stream identifies p; another correct/incorrect target stream identifies q. Under this supplied symmetry, samples can be pooled; otherwise each row requires separate calibration.

Here d(p)=(2p-1)log[p/(1-p)], g(q)=(3q-1)log[2q/(1-q)]/2. For each state, the adaptive occupation programme has alpha=g/d: its matching action distinguishes that state from both alternatives simultaneously. Every frozen iid action mixture has worst-state coefficient at least 3g/(2d), attained by the uniform mixture. For a given mixture lambda, at least one pair has lambda_i+lambda_j<=2/3, proving the lower bound. These coefficient facts do not by themselves construct a sampled unknown-law exact converter.

With illustrative empirical estimates p_hat=.9,q_hat=.8, simultaneous confidence radius .01, and delta=.05, Hoeffding requires n=21911 samples for each scalar parameter, total 43822. On the common coverage event p in [.89,.91], q in [.79,.81]. Monotonicity yields

    adaptive alpha <= g(.81)/d(.89) < .939649 <1,
    every frozen mixture worst alpha >= 3g(.79)/[2d(.91)] >1.092974 >1.

The script certifies these strict inequalities using exact rational log-series bounds, not floating-point assertions alone. The log expansion uses z=(x-1)/(x+1), log x=2 sum z^(2j+1)/(2j+1), with tail bounded by 2z^(2m+1)/[(2m+1)(1-z^2)]. Hundred-term rational bounds leave ample margin.

The displayed estimates are illustrative, not actual measurements. A confidence procedure has at least .95 simultaneous coverage over its repeated sampling distribution; this is not a posterior .95 probability conditional on this exact displayed dataset. Its certificate can be wrong on the failure event.

## Consequence, novelty, and falsifiable continuation

These are standard confidence, absolute-continuity, KL/data-processing and coupling principles specialized to the programme's interface. No foundational novelty is claimed. The consequential distinction is operational: robust coefficient certification with controlled failure probability is attainable; unconditional exact reproduction from a fitted continuous law is not supplied by calibration; arbitrary later horizons make small law errors material.

A credible scientific route requires a preregistered sensor/state family, physically justified support/symmetry/stationarity assumptions, labelled calibration access and costs, independent held-out measurements, explicit report horizons or a separated finite catalogue, and a simulator with charged runtime/tails. Next falsifiable experiment: calibrate a declared actual three-state instrument at the derived measurement budget, compute simultaneous robust LP bounds, and compare both arms on held-out finite reports under a predeclared horizon. Reject the claimed advantage if interval widths eliminate the rate margin or calibration/amortization costs dominate. No real instrument has yet met that admission test.
