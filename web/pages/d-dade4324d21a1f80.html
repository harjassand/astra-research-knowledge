## Exact interface and deductions

Use Dong–Leng's weighted Sylvester score, `-i[A,rho]=(1-lambda)rho S+lambda S rho`. Appendix A.1 defines this for every lambda in[0,1], gives the copy-expectation loss in Lemma14 Eq56, the filtered-commutant criterion in Theorem15, and endpoint identification in Lemma16. Those are author results. The choice of a random weight, finite exceptional set, finite-list obstruction, and finite model design below are our deductions or applications. Drawing a weight changes the same whole-score population loss interface; it does not require explicitly measuring the unknown score matrix.

Fix sigma with eigenvalues p_i>0. The dressed matrix entries are

`K_lambda(A)_ij=sqrt(p_i p_j) A_ij / ((1-lambda)p_i+lambda p_j)`.

On the real vector space V of traceless Hermitian matrices define T_lambda(X) to be all commutators with K_lambda(A_a) and their adjoints. Its Frobenius Gram G_lambda is positive semidefinite and rational in lambda. The score fiber is singleton iff its kernel is zero: any nonscalar Hermitian common commutant element gives a sufficiently small positive normalized perturbation of sigma, and any faithful competitor gives such a commutant element by the fiber reduction. This is why the good-weight conclusion is simultaneous over all faithful competitors to the fixed sigma; it is not a union bound over uncountably many pairs.

At lambda=0 every K is the same similarity D^{-1} A D, D=diag(sqrt(p_i)), so scalar ordinary commutant implies T_0 injective. Thus det G_0>0. All denominators stay positive on[0,1], and the rational determinant is not identically zero; its zero set is finite. One absolutely continuous draw in any nonempty interior interval therefore identifies EACH fixed target/frame almost surely against ALL faithful competitors. No event valid simultaneously for every faithful target has been proved by this argument.

For a real-analytic p-parameter faithful state model on a neighborhood of a compact semianalytic parameter set, flattening real and imaginary parts of every whole score gives a finite vector-valued real-analytic response beta(theta,lambda). Matrix-exponential Gibbs parameterization is analytic; the weighted Sylvester superoperator is invertible throughout the faithful neighborhood, hence its inverse and scores are analytic. Any distinct state pair is distinguished by some lambda in every open interior interval: otherwise analytic continuation gives equality at endpoints, contradicting the known endpoint theorem. Redundant parameters with equal states are indistinguishable and are intentionally retained as equivalent.

For m sampled weights, the pair/weight equality incidence has dimension at most2p: each distinct-state pair has a finite zero-weight set, so the weight fiber has dimension zero. Its projection into the m-dimensional weight space is null when m>2p. Thus2p+1 absolutely continuously sampled weights identify the ENTIRE specified model simultaneously, in the sense of states. Compact semianalytic assumptions supply a direct subanalytic proof; alternatively the following existing analytic-response theorem applies on analytic parameter neighborhoods/chart covers.

## Finite-list collision and novelty boundary

The complete construction/proof is in `notes/r06_combinatorics/cycle3/finite_weight_cauchy_krylov.md`; independent audit is in `notes/team_b_quantum/cycle3/finite_interior_weights_audit.md`. Its interface is a nonempty finite prescribed list Lambda⊂(0,1), closed under complements, with k distinct values. It constructs d=k+2, three fixed real-symmetric observables with scalar ordinary complex commutant, and ONE faithful pair with equal entire scores at every listed weight. Target condition number can be≤2 and trace distance≥1/6. The frame is chosen after the list; it is not the fixed local Pauli frame.

The standard ingredients are polynomial Sylvester/Krylov annihilation and Vandermonde circuits. The candidate contribution is their exact use to enforce simultaneous weighted-score collision while retaining ordinary irreducibility and a macroscopic state difference. General claims that finite spectral samples can fail or that sufficiently many samples restore rigidity already have the priors above. The positive d(d−1)-distinct-h theorem in `notes/b_math/cycle3/FINITE_WEIGHT_BOUNDARY.md` likewise uses old rational-interpolation machinery; its score-specific sample count/assumptions require their own audit rather than generic novelty rhetoric.

There is no contradiction among the results: a finite list can be defeated by subsequently choosing dimension/frame/target; each fixed target/frame has a finite exceptional set; a fixed analytic p-parameter model admits one design with2p+1 generic weights. These quantifiers are the useful research consequence.

## Costs, unresolved mechanism, and next falsifiable step

An ideal continuous draw is not a finite-precision implementation. A finite grid supports a pointwise failure bound from an explicitly bounded polynomial commutator minor degree, as independently audited by b_math; it supplies no distance from exceptional roots. A successful weight can still have arbitrarily small commutator gap. Neither theorem supplies a measured-score calibration, copy bound, model-time-evolution cost, efficient optimizer, or scalable representation of the whole score. The known endpoint option is already universally identifying, and any practical reason to prefer interior weights must charge their implementation costs.

Concrete next step: for a specified structured Gibbs model and supplied physical frame, bound the measure of weights with commutator gap below eta, then relate this same metric to the author's copy-loss and model-side circuit estimator. This can distinguish an accessible random-interior repair from an exact population identification theorem with poor calibration. The full-localXYZ SLD blind construction in our other cycle3 audit makes this question consequential, but it does not establish bad-gap behavior at every asymmetric weight.
