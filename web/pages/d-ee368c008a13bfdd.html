1.2 Motivation
The equilibrium-counting problem lies at the intersection of potential theory, Morse theory,
real algebraic geometry, and the geometry of harmonic functions. Even its qualitative aspects
require care. The ﬁeld equations contain algebraic radicals; signed conﬁgurations can have
positive-dimensional critical sets, even with only three charges [13]; and critical points produced
by diﬀerent asymptotic models must remain separated when those models are combined into
one Coulomb potential.
For positive Morse conﬁgurations, classical Morse-theoretic arguments give at least n− 1
equilibria; see, for example [17, 5]. Positivity also conﬁnes every equilibrium to the convex hull
of the sites: at an equilibrium, the ﬁeld equation expresses x as the convex combination of
the ai with weights proportional to qi/|x−ai|3. Allowing signed charges removes this general
conﬁnement principle and permits cancellations between multipole moments. The construction
below exploits those cancellations to realize a long alternating zonal jet in a rescaled limit and to
3
make each pair of consecutive degrees contribute quadratically many critical points. Separating
these contributions over L− 1 spatial scales is what raises the total count from quadratic to
cubic.
1.3 Historical and related work
Maxwell discussed electrostatic equilibrium counting in Article 113 of his treatise [16]. Gabrielov,
Novikov, and Shapiro explained how the modern nondegenerate-equilibrium bound ( n− 1)2 is
extracted from that discussion [7]. Early Morse-theoretic work on nondegenerate Newtonian
potentials includes Kiang [11]. Morse and Cairns later proved that, with the strengths and all
but one site ﬁxed, nondegeneracy holds for almost every position of the remaining site [17, The-
orem 6.2]. Abanov, Hayford, Khavinson, and Teodorescu give a broader account of equilibrium
questions in classical electrostatics [1].
The ﬁrst general ﬁnite upper bound for nondegenerate conﬁgurations was obtained by
Gabrielov, Novikov, and Shapiro using Khovanskii’s fewnomial theory; their bound is indepen-
dent of the ambient dimension, and their specialized bound for three charges is 12 [7]. Zolotov
subsequently used the Thom–Milnor theorem to obtain the exponential bound 5 · 9n+3 [25].
Edelsbrunner, Fillmore, and Oliveira proved the B´ ezout bound 2n(3n− 2)3 when the strengths
and all but one site are ﬁxed and the remaining site is chosen generically; they also disproved
a Voronoi-theoretic upper bound proposed in [7]; see [5]. For three positive charges, Gabrielov,
Dm. Novikov, T. Novikov, and Shapiro improved the upper bound for nondegenerate equilibria
from 12 to 6 in 2026 [8]; a sharp bound of 4 for arbitrary nonzero real strengths, assuming only
that the critical set is ﬁnite, is proved in [13].
Several sharper or more explicit results are known under planar, symmetric, or few-charge
hypotheses. Killian obtained an improved upper bound for equilibria in the plane of copla-
nar charges [12], and Peretz treated the three-charge problem by the argument principle [18].
Bilogliadov analyzed equal positive charges at roots of unity for a family of Riesz potentials [4].
Tsai proved the proposed bound for equilateral and isosceles-right three-site geometries [22] and
later treated three charges of equal absolute magnitude [23]. Uteshev and Yashina developed
exact algebraic and bifurcation methods for three and four positive planar charges [24], while
Erd´ elyi–Rosenblatt–Rosenblatt studied the structure and ﬁniteness of ﬁeld zero sets, including
collinear planar sources [6]. Lee and Tsai exhibited four coplanar positive charges with nine pla-
nar equilibria, attaining (4− 1)2 in that restricted setting [15]. Ghosh, Goldberg, and Hollender
studied the computational complexity of ﬁnding approximate equilibria [9].
Recent work has substantially improved the lower bounds. Edelsbrunner, Fillmore, and
Oliveira constructed, for every integer l≥ 1, a conﬁguration of nl = 8l unit positive charges
with exactly 25
7 (nl−1) equilibria [5]. Arathoon, Ball, and Kvalheim then constructed ﬁve positive
charges with at least 24 nondegenerate equilibria and showed that a small perturbation gives
a ﬁnite Morse conﬁguration. Their iteration yields, for every integer m≥ 0 and n = 3 + 2m,
at least 4 + 20m = 10n− 26 equilibria [2]. A separate positive cubature construction gives the
superlinear asymptotic bound M+(n)≥ (3.596× 10−4−o(1))n3/2 for positive charges [14]. The
present result uses signed charges and a diﬀerent mechanism to obtain the larger exponent 3.
