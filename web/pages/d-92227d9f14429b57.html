## 1. Three active polar coefficients annihilate a quadratic ideal

For exact coefficient conventions, let T be normalized by f(x)=T(x,x,x,x), F=f(v), g(x)=T(v,v,v,x), and P=F²T(v,x,x,x)−3Fg(x)T(v,v,x,x)+2g(x)³. The symbols [x_i³]P and [x_i²x_j]P denote ordinary polynomial coefficients with no factorial division. If m_i=v_i² and s_i=(Qv²)_i, the cleared cofactors are

\[
\widetilde A_i=(F-2m_is_i)(q_{ii}F-s_i^2),
\]

\[
\widetilde H_{ij}=q_{ij}F^2-Fs_j(s_i+2q_{ii}m_i)-4Fq_{ij}m_is_i+6m_is_i^2s_j,
\]

and [x_i³]P=v_i A_i, [x_i²x_j]P=v_j H_ij exactly. These conventions fix the factor three in the mixed identity below. The outside [x_k²x_i]P uses the same H_ki formula; setting v_k=0 gives v_i F(q_ikF−s_i s_k).

Fix i,j and work modulo K3 **after multiplication by v_i v_j**. Set

\[
a=q_{ii},\quad b=q_{ij},\quad c=q_{jj},\quad \Delta=ac-b^2>0,
\quad m=v_i^2,\quad n=v_j^2,
\]

\[
F=a m^2+2bmn+c n^2,\qquad p=a m^2-c n^2,\qquad G=v_iv_jp,
\quad s_i=am+bn,\quad s_j=bm+cn.
\]

Here n in the displayed binary formulas is a variable, not the ambient dimension. The notation is local to this section.

The cycle10 diagonal cleared cofactors satisfy

\[
\widetilde A_i=-\Delta n^2p,\qquad \widetilde A_j=\Delta m^2p.
\]

The additional exact active cofactor identity is

\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_{ij}=3\Delta mn p.}
\]

To reconstruct it directly, substitute F,s_i,s_j into

\[
bF^2-Fs_j(s_i+2am)-4Fbms_i+6ms_i^2s_j.
\]

The result factors as 3mn(ac−b²)(am²−cn²). The raw polar coefficients are P_i=[x_i³]P=v_i A_i, P_j=[x_j³]P=v_j A_j and P_ij=[x_i²x_j]P=v_j H_ij. Multiplication by the missing direction coordinate consequently gives

\[

Equivalently (m,n)²G⊂I+K3, and in particular FG∈I+K3. An explicit degree-ten syzygy is

\[
\Delta FG=a v_iP_j+\frac{2b}{3}v_iP_{ij}-c v_jP_i\pmod{K3}.

This improves the earlier f²G membership to fG. It holds also for b=0; the active mixed cofactor is nonzero as a polynomial because Δ>0.

## 2. Outside coefficients give linear annihilators for every pair constraint

For an outside index k let

\[
u=q_{ik},\quad w=q_{jk},\quad s_k=um+wn,\quad c_k=um-wn,\quad C_k=v_iv_jc_k.
\]

The two outside raw coefficients, after multiplication by the missing pair coordinate, give

\[
D_i=v_j[x_k^2x_i]P=F(s_iC_k-uG),
\]

\[
D_j=v_i[x_k^2x_j]P=F(-s_jC_k+wG)\pmod{K3}.
\]

These follow from the exact binary identities F u−s_i s_k=s_i c_k−up and F w−s_j s_k=−s_j c_k+wp. Since FG is already in I+K3, we obtain Fs_iC_k,Fs_jC_k∈I+K3. Inverting the fixed nonsingular two-by-two principal matrix gives

\[
\Delta FmC_k=cD_i+bD_j+(cu-bw)FG,

\[
\Delta FnC_k=-bD_i-aD_j+(aw-bu)FG\pmod{K3}.
\]

Thus F(m,n)C_k⊂I+K3. In particular F²C_k∈I+K3. The inverse principal matrix is a proof tool only; the observed-coordinate algorithm does not need it.

## 3. Lift the gcd in the even polynomial ring

Let g=p_ij=gcd(p,c_k:k outside), up to a nonzero rational scalar. Its degree in the even variables m,n is k=0,1 or2. The required binary membership is

\[
Fg\in(p,c_k:k\ne i,j).
\]

The proof is elementary in Q[m,n], where F,p have degree two and each c_k has degree one.

- All c_k vanish: g=p and Fg=Fp.
- Their span has dimension two: it contains m,n, hence F; here g=1.
- Their span has dimension one, generated by c_0=um−wn. If c_0 divides p, then g is proportional to c_0 and Fg is already in (c_0).
- Otherwise p,c_0 are coprime, g=1, and every homogeneous degree-two form belongs to their ideal. This can be checked without an imported complete-intersection bound: let δ=aw²−cu²≠0 and N=aw²+2buw+cu². Then the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{\delta F=Np-2c_0\big[a(bw+cu)m+c(aw+bu)n\big].}
\]

It works also when u=0 or w=0. If δ=0 and c_0 is nonzero, both u,w are nonzero and c_0 divides p; this is the previous case. Thus no denominator is used at its zero.

Choose homogeneous coefficients in the membership Fg=λp+Ση_kc_k. Homogeneity gives deg(λ)=k and deg(η_k)=k+1≥1. Consequently every η_k belongs to (m,n). This detail is what makes the next power sufficient; replacing it by a generic binary-form regularity bound would lose powers.

Multiply by v_i v_j and then by F:

\[
F^2 v_iv_jg=\lambda FG+\sum_k F\eta_kC_k\in I+K3
\]

by §§1–2. Therefore every pair generator of J satisfies f²v_i v_jp_ij∈I+K3. Terms from restoring the full f and full s_i lie in K3 after multiplying by v_i v_j, so the binary identities lift correctly to the ambient hidden ring.
