INDEPENDENT ALGEBRA CHECK

For F=n.J, u=n.m, v=n-u m, and r=n cross m, local spin-1/2 multiplication
gives {F,k}=Bk and [F,k]=iRk, where B=Nu+v.grad and R=r.grad. Since B,R
commute with constant left/right matrix multiplication,

  B^2 k = F^2 k + 2 FkF + kF^2,
  R^2 k = -(F^2 k - 2 FkF + kF^2),

so B^2-R^2=2(F^2 k+kF^2) and S2 follows with the stated sign. Applying
this to H=sum_i lambda_i(n_i.J)^2/s^2+b.J/s gives S3. Expanding the square
of B gives the potential N^2 u_i^2+N(1-u_i^2); R contributes no scalar
potential. The Hessian, first-order and zero-order terms in S4 therefore have
the displayed factors. In particular, V_N=Tr(H k_N) and the angular term
must retain its minus sign. The peer's exact small-matrix fixtures are useful
transcription checks but are not the basis of this all-N derivation.

EXACT GLOBAL COUNTEREXAMPLE; STOPPED CLAIM SURVIVES IT

Take M=1, Lambda=2, A=diag(-1,0,-1), b=0, so C=A+Lambda I=diag(1,2,1)
is admissible and Lc=3. At m=r e_3, direct multiplication of the two
diagonal terms in D_C gives

  D_C(r e_3)=diag(1-2r^2, 2-r^2, (1-r^2)^2).

At r=3/4 this is diag(-1/8,23/16,49/256), already indefinite strictly
inside the unit Bloch ball. At r=1 it is diag(-1,1,0). Thus any statement
that the un-stopped generator is a global positive diffusion for all compact
admitted anisotropies is false. This is not a counterexample to revision S1:
its prescribed stop has r0^2=1/64, r0=1/8. The general S5 lower margin there
is (63/64)^2-3/64=3777/4096>3/4. The exact script independently checks these
matrices with rational arithmetic. Attribution: counterexample and check by
c07_l04; candidate and stop design by c07_s02.

TAIL AUDIT AND CONSTANT REPAIR

The initial-seed tail S7 can be made fully explicit from the stated radial
unnormalized density. Put a0=2 atanh(r0/2), delta=1/2-logcosh(1),
Lbase=(7/3) exp(-Lambda/2-1/12), and use sinhc(1)=sinh(1). For r in
[a0,2], f(r/2)>=(r/2)^4/48, hence

  integral_[a0,2] q(r)dr
    <= (8/3)sinhc(1) exp(-N a0^4/768).

For r>=2, f(r/2)>=delta r^2/4 and, if N delta>=2,
r/2<=N delta r^2/8. Therefore the integrand is at most
r^2 exp(-N delta r^2/8). With x=N delta/8, integration by parts bounds its
integral by exp(-N delta/2)[8/(N delta)+4/(N delta)^2], which is at most
6 exp(-N delta/2) for N delta>=2, and hence at most 6 exp(-N delta/4).
(Using 8/(N delta)^2 instead of 4/(N delta)^2 is also valid.) On the
normalizer, set r=N^-1/4 R for 1<=R<=2. For N>=Lambda^2,
a=Lambda/(2 sqrt(N))<=1/2 and

  (1/(4t0)-N/8)r^2 = Lambda R^2/[16(1-a)] <= Lambda/2,
  N f(r/2) <= R^4/192 <= 1/12,

while sinhc(r/2)>=1. Integrating R^2 gives the lower bound
N^-3/4 Lbase. Consequently the following concrete S7 constants are valid:

  Cseed = ((8/3)sinhc(1)+6)/Lbase,
  kappa0 = min(a0^4/768, delta/4),
  pbad <= Cseed N^3/4 exp(-kappa0 N),

provided N>=Lambda^2 and N delta>=2. The displayed candidate leaves these
constants implicit; that is a repair/clarification, not a failure of S7.

For the exit estimate, coordinate quadratic variation is at most
Tc/(2s^2). After the S8 drift allowance, vector martingale displacement
3r0/8 forces one of three coordinates to cross 3r0/(8 sqrt(3)). The
two-sided exponential-martingale maximal bound and a union bound give

  P(tau<=1) <= 6 exp(-3 r0^2 s^2/(64 Tc)).

This is four times stronger in the exponent than S9's displayed
6 exp(-3 r0^2 s^2/(256 Tc)); the displayed bound is valid. With Tc<=3Lc,
the weaker form implies kappa1=r0^2/(256 Lc), as stated. The bound is uniform
over starting branches in the inner half-ball. The Feynman-Kac weight costs
at most exp(hN); the additional semigroup and M_H factors in S10 cost
exp(hN) and hN, respectively. Thus the exp(2hN) in S10 is charged correctly.

DUHAMEL / NORMALIZATION GATES

The stopped Ito formula yields W'_t=E[1_(t<tau)w_t M_H k_N(m_t)]. Freezing
the state and weight after tau gives M_H W_t-W'_t equal to the positive
boundary term E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_H k_N(m_tau)]. Duhamel against the finite
matrix semigroup then gives S10 with trace-norm integrand bound
||M_H k||_1<=||H||<=hN. Seed clipping contributes <=2pbad before propagation,
and at most 2 exp(hN)pbad after propagation, as in S11.

Because C>=I, the normalized trace of H in each spin-j block is
Tr(H_j)/(2j+1)=Tr(C)j(j+1)/(3s^2)>=0; the linear b term has zero block
trace. Scalar trace Jensen gives Tr exp(H_j)>=2j+1, proving the target
unnormalized trace is at least one relative to the heat seed. For positive
G,W the normalization inequality used in S12 follows by adding and
subtracting W/Tr(G): ||G/TrG-W/TrW||_1<=2||G-W||_1/TrG, and the trace
distance convention contributes the factor 1/2. This supports S12.

The local SDE is well-defined up to the stop from S5's strict positive
margin; the square root is smooth on a neighborhood of the stopped ball.
The stopped mixture is positive and lies in the identical-product cone.
These checks support the mathematical certificate, conditional on the exact
heat-seed representation and the stated elementary radial estimates. No
finite-bit Brownian sampler, stopping-time discretization, weight estimation,
or error-budget implementation was run. Accordingly S1 is not established
as an executable preparation algorithm.

FIXED-BOUND VERSUS GROWING-BOUNDARY

For fixed M,B, hN=O_(M,B)(sqrt(N)); hence the linear-in-N seed exponent in
S7 dominates propagation costs, and the N^(3/2) stop exponent dominates
them by a wider margin. The constants degenerate if the admitted bound grows:
r0^2=Theta(1/Lc), a0^4=Theta(1/Lc^2), kappa0=Theta(1/Lc^2),
kappa1=Theta(1/Lc^2), while hN=Theta(Lc sqrt(N)+B N^1/4). For example,
with B fixed and M=N^alpha, the displayed S11 seed bound's crude exponents
require alpha<1/6 for kappa0 N to dominate hN, whereas the stopped tail only
requires alpha<1/3. At/above that boundary these particular estimates no
longer imply an exponentially small error. This is a nonuniformity of the
proof constants, not a physical lower bound or a refutation of the stated
fixed-M,B quantifiers.
