1. INPUT, OUTPUT AND THE PRE-ROUNDING INTERFACE

Let N>=1 physical blocks have integer sizes m_i=2j_i>=0 and dimensions
d_i=m_i+1. M=sum_i m_i; spin-zero blocks may be skipped after one scan.
Let U_i identify the normalized m_i-qubit symmetric Dicke basis with the
physical spin-j_i basis. U=tensor_i U_i and P=UU^dagger. M=0 is trivial.

The compiler input is a FINITE, ACQUIRED, positive structured virtual state

 Gamma=sum_(lambda=1)^K w_lambda T_(k_lambda,u_lambda,x_lambda,phi_lambda), (D1)

where weights are exact nonnegative rationals summing to one, x is rational
in[0,1], k,u>=0 with k+u<=M, and T is the UNIFORM mixture over assignments
of exactly k copies of the same normalized pure qubit

 psi(x,phi)=sqrt(1-x)|0>+sqrt(x)e^(i phi)|1>,

u computational |1> factors and M-k-u computational |0> factors. Its
assignment count is H_(M,k,u)=C(M,k)C(M-k,u). The phases have exact unit-
modulus descriptors with charged polynomial-time precision acquisition;
the intended front end uses roots of unity of order at most M+1. K and all
input descriptor lengths are charged. Uniform assignments are part of D1,
not an arbitrary permutation-invariant separable-state recognition promise.

The output is a compact finite classical sampling program. It samples an
outer label, local block counts and exactly positive rational trace-one
physical local density matrices; their tensor product is one output sample.
The expected physical state approximates normalized U^dagger Gamma U.
Its expanded mixture may have exponentially many count profiles. We claim
polynomial-size DATA and polynomial bit work per sample, not polynomially
many expanded physical product terms, a dense global matrix or hardware cost.
The projected local physical pure states are generally NOT spin-coherent.
They are arbitrary vectors in the spin-j_i site space. Site separability
does not imply all-virtual-qubit separability after projection; SU(2) rotations
alone need not prepare these vectors. Hardware implementation is uncharged
only because it is NOT claimed by this classical descriptor compiler.

IMPORTANT: D1 must be acquired BEFORE the virtual compiler rounds pure
coherent local factors into general rational mixed densities. A rational
mixed factor does not automatically retain the count/norm formula below.
We project the exact algebraic pure factors in D1, then round the PHYSICAL
vectors/densities as in Section7. This fixes the interface identified by root.

The projection stage is unconditional given D1. Applying it to sharp-domain
Gibbs states additionally needs the front-end composition in Section9.
Existence of a separable representation alone is not an acquisition algorithm.

2. EXACT LOCAL PROJECTION AMPLITUDES AND NORM

In a block of size m, let r coherent factors, b computational ones and
a=m-r-b computational zeros occur. For t=0,...,r, the total one count is
l=b+t. There are C(r,t) virtual bit strings with that count and nonzero
amplitude. The normalized Dicke vector is the sum of ALL C(m,l) such bit
strings divided by sqrt(C(m,l)). Consequently the physical unnormalized
projected vector v_(m,r,b) has exact coefficients

 v_l = C(r,l-b)/sqrt(C(m,l))
       x^((l-b)/2)(1-x)^((r-l+b)/2)e^(i(l-b)phi),          (D2)

for b<=l<=b+r, and zero otherwise. Endpoints use the continuous convention
0^0=1. Permuting the input factors within the block has no effect, because
the symmetric projection and its Dicke coordinates are invariant.

Its squared norm is the exact rational number

 z_(m,r,b)(x)=sum_(t=0)^r [C(r,t)^2/C(m,b+t)]
                         x^t(1-x)^(r-t).                 (D3)

It is independent of phi. Since C(m,l)<=2^m and C(r,t)^2>=C(r,t),

 z >= 2^-m sum_t C(r,t)x^t(1-x)^(r-t)=2^-m.              (D4)

Also z<=1, since it is the squared norm of an orthogonal projection of
a normalized vector. Thus 2^-m<=z<=1, including m=0, r=0 and x=0,1.
Every projected component is nonzero. The exact physical pure density is
v v^dagger/z. This norm floor is specific to D1; we do not apply it to
arbitrary virtual product states with unrelated coherent directions.

3. GLOBAL PROJECTION MASS AND ITS DYNAMIC PROGRAM

For block i, the number of assignments with given(r_i,b_i) is

 c_i(r_i,b_i)=C(m_i,r_i)C(m_i-r_i,b_i).                    (D5)

Let F_0(0,0)=1 and F_0(k,u)=0 otherwise. Process the blocks by

 F_i(k,u)=sum_(r,b:r+b<=m_i) c_i(r,b)z_(m_i,r,b)(x)
                                     F_(i-1)(k-r,u-b).   (D6)

Terms with negative totals are zero. Retain only k,u>=0 and k+u no more
than the processed block size. This is an exact positive rational DP.
The unweighted analogous sum is H_(M,k,u), because
 product_i (1+v+w)^(m_i)=(1+v+w)^M.
Hence the actual conditional projection mass of one D1 label is

 s_lambda=F_N(k_lambda,u_lambda)/H_(M,k_lambda,u_lambda). (D7)

Equivalently, F_N is the sum over all legal count profiles of
product_i c_i(r_i,b_i)z_i(r_i,b_i). D7 therefore includes the true uniform
assignment normalization. D4 gives 2^-M<=s_lambda<=1 for EVERY label.

Projection reweights the label law to

 pi_lambda=w_lambda s_lambda / S, S=sum_lambda w_lambda s_lambda,
                   2^-M<=S<=1.                          (D8)

All masses and pi are acquired by exact rational arithmetic. In particular
one need not estimate exponentially small success by Monte Carlo or retry
physical projections. The field or phase law is already in the outer D1
weights. Since D7 is phase-independent, repeated phase labels may share the
same table; phase may be sampled later only if its specified conditional
law is retained. No independence of an arbitrary outer phase law is assumed.

4. EXACT POSTERIOR SAMPLING OF LOCAL COUNTS

After choosing lambda, start at(k,u)=(k_lambda,u_lambda). Process blocks
backwards. At block i the exact transition law is

 Pr[(r_i,b_i)=(r,b) | remaining(k,u)]
    =c_i(r,b)z_i(r,b)F_(i-1)(k-r,u-b)/F_i(k,u).            (D9)

The denominator is positive along every reached state; D6 shows that these
probabilities sum to one. Replace totals by(k-r,u-b) and continue. Products
of D9 telescope to the exact profile posterior product_i(c_i z_i)/F_N.
For the sampled counts output local pure states from D2, normalized by D3.
All assignments within a count profile have these SAME projected local
states, so discarding individual virtual arrangements loses nothing.
Combining D8 and D9 gives expected state exactly

             Gamma_phys = U^dagger Gamma U / S.          (D10)

This proves the no-projection-rejection mechanism. It is posterior classical
sampling, not an assertion that the physical projection succeeds often.

5. RATIONAL OPERATION AND BIT COST

For one distinct x, there are O(M^2) DP states per layer and O(m_i^2+1)
local transitions. After skipping m_i=0, D6 therefore needs
 O(M^2 sum_(m_i>0) m_i^2)<=O(M^4)
exact arithmetic operations. Computing all local z values needs
O(sum_i(m_i+1)^3) operations, also polynomial; identical block sizes may
share local tables. Reading spin-zero blocks costs O(N). If m_i<=p is fixed,
sum m_i^2<=pM and the DP cost is O(pM^3+N), hence O(M^3+N) for fixed spins.
These are upper bounds, not a practical scaling benchmark. Multiple labels
reuse a DP whenever x is the same. A crude K-fold bound remains polynomial.

There is also a direct common-denominator BIT bound; counting arithmetic
operations alone would be insufficient. Write x=A/B in lowest terms and
let L_x bound its input bit length. For block i choose

 Q_i=B^(m_i) product_(l=0)^(m_i) C(m_i,l).

Then W_i(r,b)=Q_i c_i(r,b)z_i(r,b) is a nonnegative INTEGER. Evaluate D6
with integer DP numerators and common prefix denominator product_(j<=i)Q_j.
The prefix numerators are at most 3^(sum_(j<=i)m_j) product_(j<=i)Q_j,
since z<=1 and the sum of assignment multiplicities is 3^prefix-size.
Thus every prefix integer/denominator has

 O(M L_x + sum_i m_i^2 + M)=O(M L_x+M^2)

bits. D7 adds only O(M) bits for H<=3^M. Exact rational transition laws
therefore have polynomial bit lengths too. Computing powers, binomials,
products and integer DP with elementary integer arithmetic is polynomial
bit work, with no exponentially long denominator acquisition.

If each rational w has at most L_w bits, summing K label masses using the
product of their denominators gives a crude O(K(L_w+M L_x+M^2)) bit bound.
All label posterior probabilities can accordingly be obtained in polynomial
bit work. K is explicitly charged; an exponentially large supplied list is
not called a polynomial compiler. Numerical M and the explicit spin list
are charged: if a spin is binary encoded and enormous, polynomial in M
need not be polynomial in log M or the compressed input length alone.

6. ERROR AMPLIFICATION FROM THE VIRTUAL TARGET

Use T(A,B)=||A-B||_1/2. Let rho_v be a virtual target state, with
||rho_v-Gamma||_1<=eta_f. Compression by U is a completely positive
trace-nonincreasing map and contracts the Hermitian trace norm. Put
Omega=U^dagger rho_v U, Sigma=U^dagger Gamma U, t=Tr Omega>0 and S=Tr Sigma.
Then ||Omega-Sigma||_1<=eta_f and |t-S|<=eta_f. Expanding with denominator S,

 ||Omega/t-Sigma/S||_1
 <=||Omega-Sigma||_1/S+||Omega||_1 |1/t-1/S|
 <=2 eta_f/S <=2^(M+1)eta_f.                             (D11)

Hence T after projection is at most 2^M eta_f. This uses the KNOWN D1
success floor of Sigma, not an unacquired lower success bound for rho_v.
If input error is expressed in half trace norm eta_T instead, the bound
is 2^(M+1)eta_T. For requested physical error epsilon, it suffices to acquire

 eta_f<=epsilon 2^-M/16,
 or eta_T<=epsilon 2^-M/32.                              (D12)

The projected target error is then <=epsilon/16. The required extra accuracy
is M+O(log(1/epsilon)) BITS; it is not an exponential precision-bit cost.
This does not mean acquiring a representation is free. It is a requested
precision to the separately charged virtual front end.

7. PHYSICAL LOCAL ROUNDING: EXACT PSD OUTPUTS

D2 has finite algebraic descriptors. Its norm is >=2^(-m/2). Approximate each
unnormalized complex vector by a complex dyadic vector f with

 ||f-v||_2 <= (epsilon/(128N)) 2^(-m/2).

It is nonzero. The normalization inequality
 ||f/||f||_2-v/||v||_2||_2 <=2||f-v||_2/||v||_2
gives <=epsilon/(64N). Consequently the local pure-density trace distance
is <=epsilon/(64N). Output the EXACT rational matrix

                  R_i = f f^dagger/(f^dagger f).         (D13)

Its entries are Gaussian rationals, it is positive semidefinite by its
outer-product representation and its trace is exactly one. Positivity is
not inferred from entrywise rounding or a numerical eigenvalue test.
Product telescoping bounds the expected state's local-rounding trace
distance by sum_i epsilon/(64N)<=epsilon/64.

A sufficient coefficient precision is O(m+log(N/epsilon)+log(m+1)) bits,
in addition to charged rational input lengths. One can compute magnitudes
as square roots of the exact rational nonnegative terms in D3, which lie
in[0,1]. Integer isqrt or rational bisection encloses each square root with
the requested dyadic accuracy in polynomial bit work. This avoids a rare-
amplitude relative-accuracy requirement. The factor 2^(-m/2) is charged in
the precision even at endpoint x=0 or1.

For phase exp(2pi i a/q), q<=M+1, acquire its sine/cosine by rational intervals:
pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239) has geometrically convergent alternating
rational series, and sine/cosine on a bounded argument have factorially
convergent Taylor intervals. O(R) terms suffice for R precision bits;
phase powers t<=M need O(log M) extra error bits. Thus the phase precision
is explicitly polynomial and the exact root-of-unity descriptor is not a
free real oracle. Other supplied phases require an equally charged evaluator.
No exact algebraic quantum gate synthesis or hardware preparation is claimed.

8. BOUNDED FAIR-BIT SAMPLING AND FINAL ERROR

The exact posterior distributions D8/D9 may be sampled approximately using
a FIXED number of fair bits at each stage. For any t-entry rational law,
floor its first t-1 probabilities on a denominator 2^B and assign the residual
to the last entry. This gives an exactly nonnegative dyadic law with total
variation <=(t-1)2^-B; B fair bits and integer CDF comparisons sample it.
This is bounded sampling and has no unbounded projection/rejection loop.

At the label stage choose 2^B>=64K/epsilon. At each block stage choose
2^B>=64N (m_i+1)(m_i+2)/epsilon. The sum of the conditional TV errors is
<=epsilon/32 by the standard sequential coupling/telescoping inequality,
regardless of which DP states are reached. The effect on expected trace-one
output states is at most the same TV error. Bits per sample are polynomial
in N,M,log K and log(1/epsilon); DP and state-vector data remain polynomial.

Combining D12, posterior rounding and D13, the delivered expected physical
state differs from normalized U^dagger rho_v U by at most

 epsilon/16 + epsilon/32 + epsilon/64 =7epsilon/64<epsilon.

One may allocate larger slack to front-end subroutines without changing
the polynomial bit result. The fully separable delivered expected state
is exact as a positive mixture of valid rational local states; only its
approximation to the Gibbs target has an error tolerance.

9. WHERE THE STRUCTURED VIRTUAL ENSEMBLE COMES FROM

For a sharp-domain virtual Gibbs target with numerical M, rational known
alpha_q>=0, 0<=delta_q<=2log2 (promised domain), rational finite h and
target virtual accuracy eta_T, the direct filtered sector construction of
c01_s03 supplies polynomially many(k,u) branches. Each branch has u fixed
ones in the complement of a uniform coherent k-subset; its symmetric Dicke
law has diagonal weights proportional to

 exp[-delta_q(l+u-M/2)^2/M+h(l+u-M/2)], l=0,...,k.

The intrinsic symmetric parameter is delta'=delta_q k/M, within the imported
sharp coherent cone for k>=1, and the shifted field is finite. The k=0
branch has only computational factors. Rational maximum-exponent shifting
and clipping acquire the normalized branch probabilities at polynomial
bit cost even for enormous binary-encoded alpha_q or h. The rational sector
LP and positive outer probability construction are charged front-end steps.

Each conditional branch must then be compiled to PURE rational-population
coherent nodes with its exact(k+1)-phase grid, keeping the finite algebraic
factors and the rational nonnegative weights. Those rules, combined with
the acquired rational(k,u) probabilities and uniform virtual placements,
are EXACTLY D1. Summing O(M^2) branches each with O(k^2) quadrature labels
gives a crude K=O(M^4). Do not insert final mixed one-qubit rounding here.
Only the physical output vectors are rounded in Section7.
