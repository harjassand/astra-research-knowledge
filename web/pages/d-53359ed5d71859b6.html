## Lumping of reaction networks: Generic and critical parameters

Eilertsen, Romanovski, Schnell and Walcher, arXiv:2606.28895v2, revised 2 July 2026; v1 27 June 2026. Read introductory mechanism, Section 3, Proposition 3/4, Section 4 Lemma 1, and column examples. Proposition 3 fixes a parameter-independent map valid across an open parameter set; it does not deny parameter-dependent eigenvector reductions. Section 4, Lemma 1 gives T DF B=0 for a specified map and kernel basis, explicitly credited there to Li–Rabitz. This is the relevant exceptional-equality interface.

Our contribution is the independently solved pulse/testing fixture in `reaction_target_laws.md`; its second-order versus first-order sensitivity comparison is not attributed to the authors. A local PDF manuscript header says July 7, while the recoverable arXiv version history says July 2; metadata uses the arXiv version timestamp. HTML access for v2 failed; the full PDF was saved and extracted, with the stated sections deeply read rather than every example audited.

## Approximate Constrained Lumping of Chemical Reaction Networks

Leguizamon-Robayo, Jiménez-Pastor, Tribastone, Tschaikowski and Vandin, arXiv:2411.14242v1, 21 November 2024, older prerequisite. The saved primary author field spells Tribastone's given name “Micro”; metadata preserves that spelling without silently correcting it. Read introduction, exact-lumping characterization and Theorem 3.1 with its hypotheses. That error bound fixes a finite horizon and bounded initial/reachable sets, requires an analytic vector field on a compact set containing both original and projected states, and scales with the declared lumping tolerance. It is a reduction of a supplied ODE, not acquisition of the real kinetic template.

## ALDER: Discovering the Laws of a World by Acting in It

Cao, Deng, Delfosse and Kersting, arXiv:2609.33728v1, 27 September 2026. Read main architecture, fitting/admission mechanism, Section 3.2 Equation (6), and Section 5 limitations. Equation (6) selects feasible experiments by weighted candidate disagreement, input novelty and cost; the authors explicitly call disagreement an information-gain proxy. Their given representation/typed grammar and largely short simulator interactions are stated limitations. The supplied candidate set need not contain the true equation. Protected held-out admission is useful empirical evidence, not universal identification.

Our discriminating experiment is a proposed mathematically specified test of target-dependent acquisition; it is not an ALDER replication or demonstrated defect. An equally informed information-design baseline can exploit the same asymmetric preparation. No special engine advantage or bibliographic novelty is established.
