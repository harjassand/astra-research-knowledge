## Exact statistical/acquisition interface

Let n≥2, d=n(n+1)/2, and let the unknown A range over the Frobenius ball K={A∈Sym(n):||A||F≤R}. Fix τ>0 with τR<1. Real H=Y=ℓ2(N;R^n). Fix known weights 1=w1≥w2≥…>0 tending to0; optionally Σw_l²<∞ for a Hilbert–Schmidt family. Define

F_A=⊕_{l≥1} w_l(I_n+τA).

The input f=(f_l) has charged squared energy ||f||H²=Σ||f_l||². For a finite family of currents f_i, require Σ_i||f_i||²≤E. A scalar detector is a known unit-norm linear output functional. For each current, the retained detector collection is orthonormal; noise is independent N(0,σ²) in the resulting scalar coordinates. Thus the detector operator P_i:Y→R^{b_i} is a contraction/partial isometry and the data are

y_i=P_iF_Af_i+σz_i, z_i iid standard real Gaussian vectors.

All currents, detectors, E, weights and parameter metric are fixed in advance and known. A deterministic design may optimize using the known family but cannot depend on unknown A. Detector construction cost is not declared free. The total acquired scalar count is Σb_i. Repeating arbitrary nonorthogonal tests changes the information/noise normalization and is outside this particular comparison unless the covariance is explicitly charged.

## Operator and continuum properties

Every F_A is bounded, self-adjoint, compact and strictly positive in quadratic-form sense: each block I+τA has smallest eigenvalue≥1−τR>0 and each w_l>0. Compactness follows by truncating blocks, with operator-norm tail bound (1+τR)w_{L+1}. It is not uniformly coercive on the infinite-dimensional H; that is appropriate for this NtD-like compact structural class but is not a PDE realization theorem.

For A≠B, F_A−F_B=τ⊕w_l(A−B). Every nonzero finite matrix A−B has positive rank and appears in infinitely many nonzero blocks, so this secant has infinite rank. Likewise every nonzero parameter tangent D has infinite-rank operator τ⊕w_lD. Hence the Cycle1 vector-rank theorem applies.

More directly, a full-support Gaussian input has (f1,…,fn) forming an invertible n×n column matrix almost surely. Its finite block marginal has nonsingular real Gaussian covariance, so the determinant polynomial is nonzero and its zero set is null. The first n output blocks then determine A exactly by linear algebra. Normalizing the sampled input to fixed energy E preserves the spanning property and does not change this probability-one noiseless result.

This does not settle finite/noisy performance: reading output block l divides its sensitivity by w_l, and normalization shares energy across input blocks.

## Finite top-block detector obstruction

If detectors read only the first output block, one current identifies only Af1. If f1 spans a one-dimensional subspace S, every symmetric D vanishing on S is invisible. Such D are exactly the arbitrary symmetric matrices on S⊥, of dimension (n−1)n/2. Small perturbations ±hD are both in K and give identical acquired data, with or without noise.

More generally k currents with first-block vectors spanning s dimensions leave an invisible symmetric space of dimension (n−s)(n−s+1)/2. Thus all continuum secant/tangent ranks can be infinite while a fixed small detector architecture destroys one-current identifiability exactly. With one current and top L blocks the same formula holds with s=dim span(f1,…,fL)≤L. In particular L<n leaves invisible directions for every input.

Independently of block structure, fewer than d total scalar measurements cannot identify all A in an open ball for this linear parameter family: the finite linear measurement map has rank at most that scalar count. An arbitrary nonlinear continuum theorem cannot be substituted for this elementary finite-detector constraint.

## Fisher information with explicit parameter metric

Subtract the known response ⊕w_l f_l. For one input the linear mean derivative in tangent D is L_f(D)=τ(w_lDf_l)_l. With full sensitivity norm the information quadratic is

I_f(D,D)=(τ²/σ²)Σ_l w_l²||Df_l||²
             =(τ²/σ²)tr(D² C_f),
C_f=Σ_l w_l² f_l f_l^T.

Any finite orthonormal detector design has Fisher information no larger in Loewner order, since ||P L_f(D)||≤||L_f(D)||. This full sensitivity norm is an upper benchmark, not an assumption that infinitely many noisy scalars were acquired. The tangent image is finite dimensional and a projection onto it would preserve the benchmark at the cost of constructing that detector map.

For every PSD C,

min_{D∈Sym(n),||D||F=1} tr(D²C)=λmin(C).

The lower bound follows from C≥λmin(C)I and tr(D²)=||D||F². Equality holds at D=vv^T for a unit eigenvector v of the smallest eigenvalue. Thus E-optimal Fisher design in Frobenius parameter geometry is exactly maximizing λmin(C_f).

## Sharp optimized one-current bound

Claim:

sup_{Σ||f_l||²≤E} λmin(C_f)=E/(Σ_{l=1}^n w_l^{-2}).

Proof. Let α=λmin C_f. If α=0 the upper bound is immediate. Otherwise form columns b_l=w_l f_l and x_l=C_f^{-1/2}b_l. Then Σ_l x_lx_l^T=I_n. The operator X with columns x_l is a coisometry, so X*X is a rank-n orthogonal projection. Therefore q_l=||x_l||² satisfies 0≤q_l≤1 and Σq_l=n. Since b_l=C_f^{1/2}x_l and C_f≥αI,

E≥Σ_l ||f_l||²
  =Σ_l w_l^{-2} x_l^T C_f x_l
  ≥α Σ_l w_l^{-2}q_l
  ≥α Σ_{l=1}^n w_l^{-2}.

The last inequality is elementary: the increasing costs w_l^{-2} are minimized by filling n unit-capacity bins with q_l=1 in the first n bins. For clarity, its difference from Σfirstn costs is bounded below by w_n^{-2}[Σ_{l>n}q_l−Σ_{l≤n}(1−q_l)]=0. Infinite sums are legitimate because terms are nonnegative and input energy is finite.

Equality is achieved by setting α=E/(Σfirstn w_l^{-2}), f_l=(sqrtα/w_l)e_l for l≤n, and all later f_l=0. Then C_f=αI and total input energy equals E. Reading the first n output blocks uses n² acquired scalars and captures the entire parameter-dependent response of this input. Thus the upper bound is attained within a finite detector design.

The sharp minimum Fisher eigenvalue is consequently

γ_single=(τ²E/σ²)/(Σ_{l=1}^n w_l^{-2}).

This is an optimized deterministic single-current conclusion. Averaging Fisher information over random input realizations is a different design objective and must not be silently substituted for a lower bound valid for the one realized current.

## Sharp optimized multi-current bound at the same energy and scalar count

For any number of inputs, C_multi=Σ_i,l w_l² f_{i,l}f_{i,l}^T has trace≤Σ_i,l||f_{i,l}||²≤E because w_l≤1. Hence λmin(C_multi)≤E/n. Any finite detector restriction can only reduce information.

This bound is attained by n currents, each supported in block1, with f_{j,1}=sqrt(E/n)e_j. Read all n first-block outputs for each current. Total energy is E and acquired scalar count is n². The information covariance is C_multi=(E/n)I and

γ_multi=τ²E/(nσ²).

Both compared strategies therefore have the same total input energy, scalar acquisition count, detector normalization, additive noise variance, known model and parameter metric. The exact E-optimal Fisher advantage is

γ_multi/γ_single=(1/n)Σ_{l=1}^n w_l^{-2}.

It can be arbitrarily large despite infinite-rank every secant/tangent and almost-sure one-current exact recovery. For example n=2 gives a three-dimensional symmetric parameter family and ratio (1+w2^{-2})/2, tending to infinity as w2→0. A smaller detector budget or inaccessible detectors can make both strategies worse; the continuum property alone chooses neither budget nor design.

The unbounded separation is robust if the single-current strategy may choose arbitrary, even repeated, unit-norm detectors with independent equal-variance scalar noise. For B acquired scalar tests the detector frame satisfies ||P||op²≤B, so single-current minimum Fisher is at most Bγ_single. The displayed multi-current schedule at B=n² still achieves γ_multi, giving advantage at least (Σfirstn w_l^{-2})/(nB), which remains unbounded as w2→0. The exact ratio above assumes orthonormal detector sets; arbitrary nonorthogonal tests need their actual frame/noise covariance and need not obey that exact ratio. Uncharged detector gain or arbitrarily many repetitions would change the acquisition interface.

This is a statistical sensitivity comparison, not a full global minimax theorem. For a fixed design, opposite parameters ±hvv^T along its weakest sensitivity direction have Gaussian KL divergence 2h²γ, provided h≤R. Standard two-point testing bounds then turn small γ into a noise-resolution obstruction. For randomized designs the direction must not be chosen after observing the random input when claiming minimax risk; a design-independent prior or other valid averaging argument is needed.

## Independent audit of coordinator's randomized minimax upgrade

The Team A coordinator subsequently supplied a valid global minimax argument; the preceding Fisher paragraph retains the original theorem's narrower scope. This upgrade allows one known randomized input independent of the unknown A, with energy at most E in every realization. Detectors may depend on the known input but are a fixed finite orthonormal collection conditional on it. Use the uniform prior on the Frobenius sphere ||A||F=R⊂K, independent of the input design.

Grant the one-current estimator exact Af1. For nonzero f1, the linear observation D↦Df1 has rank n on Sym(n), so its noiseless uniform-sphere Bayes squared-error risk is R²(d−n)/d: conditional expectation retains the observed orthogonal parameter projection and the unobserved component has mean zero by sphere symmetry. If f1=0, the risk is R² and the same lower bound holds.

After granting Af1 and subtracting the known baseline and the observed first-block contribution, the original finite detector data can be simulated from σz+τP_tail(w_lAf_l)_{l≥2}. Their remaining mean norm is bounded by τR w2 sqrtE, since ||A||op≤R, ||P_tail||≤1 and Σtail||f_l||²≤E. Compare the joint prior/granted-observation/noisy-tail law against the same prior and granted observation with pure tail noise. Gaussian conditional KL is at most τ²R²w2²E/(2σ²), hence total variation at most τR w2 sqrtE/(2σ). Clip estimators to the radius-R ball; squared loss is at most4R². Thus Bayes risk, and therefore minimax risk, for any such one-current design is at least

R²(d−n)/d − 2τR³w2 sqrtE/σ.

The bound is uniform in each conditional input realization, so averaging over a known random input independent of the prior is legitimate. No parameter is chosen after seeing that random input. Nonorthogonal B-test frames replace the tail norm bound by sqrtB times the displayed bound and still allow a separation with sufficiently small w2.

For n first-block currents sqrt(E/n)e_j, divide their noisy columns by τsqrt(E/n), subtract the known identity contribution, and symmetrize. Each raw matrix entry has noise variance σ²n/(τ²E); diagonal variances are unchanged and off-diagonal averages have half that variance. Frobenius MSE is exactly σ² n d/(τ²E), uniformly over A. Clipping to K can only reduce it. Taking σ small and w2 much smaller than σ gives a strictly positive one-current risk lower bound while the multi-current upper bound is small, at the same E and n² acquired scalar count.
