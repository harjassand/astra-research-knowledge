## Exact question and interface

Can the new superexponential two-colour progression construction be strengthened so that **every** tested progression contains many witnesses of both colours, making the construction resistant to adversarial recolouring?

For an integer `k`, the objects are all cyclic sequences `a,a+d,...,a+(k-1)d` in `Z/NZ`, with `d != 0`. In the source construction `N=q^D`, where `q` is a power of a prime `P>k`; therefore all these sequences have distinct terms. A resistant colouring asks for at least `rho(k)` positions of each colour on every one of these progressions. An edit set `E` with `|E intersect P|<rho(k)` for every progression must then leave every progression nonmonochromatic.

## New elementary refinement: a key occurs at most twice

Let distinct points of a line be separated by Euclidean distance at least one. Write them in unit-speed coordinates as `z0+t e`, where `z0` is orthogonal to `e`. Their squared norms are `C+t^2`, with `C>=0`.

For a fixed integer `m`, the half-open band is

`m <= C+t^2 < m+1`.

On `t>=0`, it is empty or an interval with lower endpoint `sqrt(max(0,m-C))` and **excluded** upper endpoint `sqrt(m+1-C)`. The interval length is at most one, because `(sqrt(v)-sqrt(u))^2<=v-u<=1`. It contains at most one member of a 1-separated set: two would need distance at least one, and equality would require the excluded endpoint. On `t<=0` the reflected interval likewise contains at most one point. Thus the band contains at most two points. Counting `t=0` twice can only enlarge this bound.

Apply this to the line and separation already reconstructed in the source's Lemma 6.1. Every actual key has multiplicity at most **two**. This conclusion uses the half-open integer band exactly. It is false for the closed band `[m,m+1]`: on a line through the origin, `t=-1,0,1` all belong to `[0,1]` in squared norm.

## Universal finite sparse-repair lemma

Let `V` be a finite set, `P` a finite family of ordered `k`-tuples of distinct elements, `c0:V->{0,1}`, and `kappa:V->K` a key map. Assume each key occurs at most twice on any tuple. Assume every tuple is either:

- rich: each outer colour occurs at least `gamma k` times; or
- structured: its literal signature `((c0(v_j),kappa(v_j)))_(j<k)` belongs to a family of size at most `T`.

For `0<p<=min(1/2,2 gamma)`, suppose

`2 |P| 2^k p^(gamma k/4) + 2 T exp(-p k/16) < 1`.  (SR)

Then there is a colouring of `V` with at least `p k/4` positions of **each** colour on every tuple. Integer counts therefore reach `ceil(p k/4)`.

**Proof.** Give each key an independent Bernoulli(p) bit `E_K` and set `c(v)=c0(v) xor E_(kappa(v))`.

For a rich tuple and a target bit `b`, fewer than `gamma k/2` final positions of `b` require flipping at least `gamma k/2` of its initial `b` positions. At most two positions share a key, so at least `gamma k/4` distinct keys must be flipped. There are at most `k` keys; a union bound over their possible subsets bounds the probability by `2^k p^(gamma k/4)`. This conservative bound also covers ceilings. A union bound over the two colours and all tuples gives the first term in (SR). Since `p<=2 gamma`, retaining `gamma k/2` positions is enough for the promised `p k/4`.

For a structured tuple, choose one position per distinct key. There are `s>=k/2` such positions. For any target bit `b`, their final indicators are independent, and each has success probability either `p` or `1-p`, hence at least `p`. The count stochastically dominates `Binomial(s,p)`; its lower tail below `p k/4<=p s/2` is at most `exp(-p s/8)<=exp(-p k/16)`. One elementary derivation is to apply Markov to `2^(-X)` and use `E 2^(-X)=(1-p/2)^s<=exp(-p s/2)` and `(log2)/2-1/2<-1/8`. The selected representatives give a deterministic lower bound on the count on the entire tuple, regardless of the second positions sharing keys.

Literal signatures with identical bit and key identities impose the same event on the same random variables. Consequently only `T` events, rather than all structured tuples, need to be counted for each target colour. Renaming keys is forbidden. The second union bound gives the second term of (SR). A positive-probability choice avoids both failure families. QED.

This is a theorem for arbitrary finite keyed set systems. Geometry is needed only to establish the multiplicity, dichotomy and signature-entropy inputs in an application.

## Consequence at the source parameters

Keep `c=10^-5`, `gamma=1/100` and `p=k^(-1/20)`. Use the sharpened multiplicity two, the family160 dichotomy and `|P|<N^2=q^(2D)`.

The logarithm of the rich-family bound is at most

`(2c - gamma/80) k log k + k log2 + O(k^(1/10) log k)`.

Here `2c-gamma/80 = -21/200000 < 0`. This tends to minus infinity. The structured-family logarithm is at most

`log(2 T_aff) - k^(19/20)/16 = -k^(19/20)/16 + o(k^(19/20))`,

which also tends to minus infinity. Eventually `p<=2 gamma`; this alone needs `k>=50^20`, so the assertion does not promise a practically small threshold.

**Derived candidate application.** For every sufficiently large `k`, the same cyclic size `N>=k^(10^-5 k)` admits a two-colouring in which every nonzero-step cyclic `k`-progression contains at least `k^(19/20)/4` occurrences of each colour. Restriction to `[N]` gives the same property for ordinary progressions there. Any edit set meeting each tested progression in fewer than `ceil(k^(19/20)/4)` positions preserves avoidance of monochromatic `k`-progressions.

The universal sparse-repair lemma and norm-band refinement are proved above. The application is flagged as depending on the source's internally audited structural interfaces, pending a separate adversarial audit of those interfaces.

## Near-linear resistance with an explicit size tradeoff

The two used structural interfaces persist when the setup's fixed constant `c` is changed: `q`, the cutoff `alpha=q^(-D-1)`, and the adaptive mesh change, but the derivations require only fixed positive `c`, `log q=O(k^(9/10) log k)`, and the displayed bound for `D log q`. All other scales and the local pattern counting argument are unchanged in their hypotheses. The old final union bound is replaced entirely by (SR).

Fix `0<epsilon<1/10`, set `p=k^(-epsilon)` and `c_epsilon=epsilon/1600=gamma epsilon/16`. Then

`2c_epsilon - gamma epsilon/4 = -gamma epsilon/8 < 0`,

and `log T_aff=O(k^(9/10) log k)=o(k^(1-epsilon))`. Therefore the same argument gives the candidate strengthening

`N >= k^((epsilon/1600) k)` and `rho(k) >= k^(1-epsilon)/4`.

The threshold may depend on epsilon. This trades a smaller size coefficient for resistance approaching a linear number of positions on every progression. It gives no polynomial-time algorithm to acquire the colouring and no fixed positive density independent of k.
