## Exact question and interface

Input is only the expanded rational homogeneous degree-four polynomial

    f(x) = ((Lx)^{circ 2})^T Q ((Lx)^{circ 2}),

in n variables. Unknown L is rational invertible; unknown Q is rational symmetric positive definite. No lower-degree term, metric, axes, eigenvalue gap, latent bit bound or condition number is supplied. Let B be the expanded input bit length. f is coercive: f(x)>0 for real x!=0. Its Hessian need not be positive definite, and Q may have negative or zero off-diagonal entries.

The target is an exact rational frame V giving f(Vz)=(z^{circ2})^T Q' z^{circ2}, Q'>0, together with a positive rational weighted-square certificate. A good trial of the construction below recovers the originally promised primal rays L^{-1}e_i. Every returned representation is checked unconditionally. The independently gated uniqueness theorem in `AXIS_IDENTIFIABILITY.md` proves that every even QPD representation has the same unordered primal rays, so the final verifier also certifies the original rays under the full promise, including successful bad-line trials. Latent scales and ordering remain arbitrary.

**Additional promise for the variable-dimensional theorem proved here:** the extactic E_F for the explicitly computed field below is not identically zero. This is a proper Zariski-open restriction, proved to include *every diagonal positive-definite Q*. It does not currently cover every coupled Q>0. A nonzero line evaluation is an original-input certificate that E_F!=0; a zero evaluation does not certify E_F=0. Bounded executions may return UNKNOWN. Under the extra promise, independent uniform trials have expected polynomial bit cost in n and B.

This restriction is absent for n=1,2: E_F is universally nonzero there. It is also proved automatically for every Q=D(aI+bJ)D>0 with positive diagonal D, including negative coupling and arbitrary dimension; the complete hereditary proof is `EXCHANGEABLE_COUPLING_NONVANISHING.md` and its independent gate is PASS. The binary algorithm can instead use the known classical sextic with a deterministic constant-sized search, as described below.

## 1. A polynomial field from the quartic coefficients

Put

    H_f = Hess f,   h_f = det H_f,
    F_f = adj(H_f) grad h_f,   D_f = F_f dot grad.

No polynomial matrix inverse is taken. F_f is homogeneous of degree r=4n-3; n=1 gives r=1. The determinant h_f has degree2n. The multivariate determinant, field and extactic need not be expanded.

Under g(x)=f(Lx), direct differentiation gives

    H_g(x)=L^T H_f(Lx)L,
    h_g(x)=det(L)^2 h_f(Lx),
    adj H_g(x)=det(L)^2 L^{-1} adj H_f(Lx)L^{-T},
    F_g(x)=det(L)^4 L^{-1}F_f(Lx).                 (1)

These are polynomial identities; adjugates retain meaning on singular Hessian loci. Thus the field is GL-natural up to a constant nonzero multiplier. Multiplying a derivation by a nonzero *constant* cannot change whether its coordinate extactic vanishes.

In hidden y coordinates the quartic is invariant under every sign flip S_i. Equation(1), with S_i^T=S_i and det(S_i)^4=1, gives F(S_i y)=S_i F(y). Therefore F_i is odd in y_i and even in all other y_j, so y_i divides F_i. Each unknown rational covector ell_i(x)=(Lx)_i is a Darboux polynomial. All D iterates of ell_i stay divisible by ell_i.

Define

    E_F(x)=det[D_F^k x_i]_(0<=k<n, 1<=i<=n).

Every hidden ell_i divides E_F by changing the coordinate-column basis to include ell_i. If nonzero, its degree is

    d = n+(r-1)n(n-1)/2 = n+2n(n-1)^2.            (2)

Extactic divisibility is established prior (Pereira/Chèze), not our discovery. The new open obligation is universal nonvanishing for this specific metric-free field.

## 2. Proved nonvanishing for every diagonal Q, hence generically

Assume f(y)=sum_i q_i y_i^4, q_i>0, and let c=12^n product_i q_i. Then

    H_f=diag(12q_i y_i^2),   h_f=c product_i y_i^2,
    F_i=(c^2/(6q_i)) y_i product_(j!=i) y_j^4.    (3)

Take an initial point with y_i>0 and pairwise distinct a_i=q_i y_i^4. Define z_i=q_i y_i^4. Along the polynomial flow,

    D z_i = (2c^2/(3 product_j q_j)) product_j z_j,

the same positive value for every i. Reparametrize time locally by ds/dt equal to this value. Then

    z_i(s)=a_i+s,   y_i(s)=q_i^{-1/4}(a_i+s)^{1/4}.

The ordinary s-Wronskian of these n functions has rows

    d^k y_i/ds^k = y_i(s) (1/4)_(falling k) (a_i+s)^{-k}.

Its determinant is a product of nonzero y_i, the nonzero numbers (1/4)_(falling k), and the Vandermonde determinant of pairwise distinct (a_i+s)^{-1}. Hence it is nonzero at s=0. Replacing d/ds by D=(ds/dt)d/ds changes the Wronskian by the nonzero triangular row determinant (ds/dt)^[n(n-1)/2]. This proves E_F is nonzero for every positive diagonal Q.

For fixed n, F and E_F have coefficients polynomial in the entries of Q. The locus where *all* coefficients of E_F vanish is algebraic. Equation(3) supplies a point outside it, so the exceptional locus is proper. Its complement intersects the PD cone in a Zariski-open, Euclidean-dense set; a nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on a nonempty real open set. Equation(1) transports nonvanishing through every rational invertible L.

This does **not** prove the exceptional locus avoids the PD cone. It supplies neither a lower bound on distance to that locus nor an acquisition method for a promised input on it. No conditioning bound is needed for the exact algorithm on the nonzero-extactic subclass.

The prior attracting-axis proof does not apply to(3): in these trajectories the z_i increase at the same rate, so projective flow approaches an interior ray. Nonvanishing here comes from a Vandermonde Wronskian, not an axis attractor.

## 3. Polynomially many hidden-hyperplane candidates from one line

Choose integer u,v from a grid with K>8nd values per coordinate. Evaluate E_F(u+tv) and every ambient derivative partial_i E_F(u+tv) by formal flow jets, as described in Section7. Factor the resulting rational univariate polynomial. For each rational root rho of multiplicity e, form

    c(rho,e)_i = (d/dt)^(e-1) partial_i E_F(u+tv)|_(t=rho).

If E_F=ell^e R, ell(v)!=0 and R(u+rho v)!=0, then

    c(rho,e) = e! ell(v)^(e-1) R(u+rho v) grad ell.

Repeated factors must therefore be retained. Zero vectors are discarded. The same good-line proof as the supplied-metric theorem applies: the product of the n relevant transversality/no-collision polynomials in(u,v) has degree below2nd. Schwartz–Zippel gives bad-line probability below1/4. One good line produces at most d rational normals containing all unknown ell_i. Additional candidates are harmless; no combinatorial search over all n-tuples is needed.

## 4. Acquire and verify a reflection from each candidate normal

For a nonzero rational candidate c, construct a rational basis W of the hyperplane c^T x=0. Solve the *linear* system in v

    grad f(Wz)^T v = 0 identically in z.            (4)

If its nullspace is not one-dimensional, discard this candidate. If it is spanned by v with c^T v!=0, normalize v so c^T v=1. Construct

    S = I-2v c^T,

and verify the full polynomial identity f(Sx)=f(x). S is automatically a rank-one involution: S^2=I, its fixed hyperplane is ker c^T, and its negative eigendirection is v. We can also check the odd t^3 coefficient in f(Wz+tv), but the full identity is the final verifier.

For a true hidden normal c proportional to ell_i, use hidden coordinates to analyze(4). On y_i=0, the coefficient of y_j^3 in the directional derivative is4v_j Q_jj for j!=i. Since Q_jj>0, all v_j with j!=i vanish. The kernel is exactly the primal i-axis; its pairing with ell_i is nonzero. Its reflection is the true hidden sign flip and passes the identity test. Thus a good line acquires all n hidden reflections. Extra candidates can add only exactly verified rational f-symmetries.

The first derivative condition alone does not certify reflection symmetry: in dimension2 it holds for a continuum of Birkhoff-normal hyperplanes of any coercive quartic. The full identity test is essential. It is fixed-degree polynomial substitution, with polynomial size and bit cost.

## 5. Rational invariant-metric acquisition by an exact linear projector

This primitive is independently reconstructed and gated by root. Let S_1,...,S_k be any acquired rational f-automorphisms and define on rational symmetric matrices

    T(A)=(1/k)sum_i S_i^T A S_i.                   (5)

The real f-stabilizer is compact: positive constants alpha,beta satisfy alpha||x||^4<=f(x)<=beta||x||^4, so every g preserving f and its inverse have uniformly bounded operator norm. These constants are used only in the existence proof and are not algorithm inputs.

A common real invariant SPD metric K exists, for example by integrating xx^T over the compact body f<=1 and inverting the covariance. Every g has |det g|=1 since it preserves that body's positive finite volume. The pullbacks A->S_i^T A S_i are isometries for the real inner product

    <A,B>_K = tr(K^{-1} A K^{-1} B).

For involutions they are selfadjoint, so T has real eigenvalues in[-1,1]. Alternatively bounded powers plus the norm equality argument also covers arbitrary acquired generators. An average fixed vector is fixed by each generator: equality in the squared-norm convexity inequality forces every S_i^T A S_i=A. Thus ker(I-T) is exactly the common invariant-form space.

Eigenvalue1 is semisimple, and

    Sym_n = ker(I-T) direct_sum im(I-T).

Compute rational bases for these two spaces, invert their concatenated basis matrix, and project I onto ker(I-T) along im(I-T). Call the result M. All of this is ordinary rational linear algebra in N=n(n+1)/2 dimensions.

This exact rational projector equals the Cesaro limit

    M=lim_(m->infinity) (1/m)sum_(a=0)^(m-1)T^a(I).

Each T^a(I) is an average of word pullbacks g^T g. Compactness gives a common strictly positive lower eigenvalue bound for all such word matrices, so M is SPD. No numeric average, Haar integral, group enumeration, SPD feasibility oracle or spectral-gap estimate is computed. We verify rational SPD and S_i^T M S_i=M after computing M.

On a good line every hidden sign flip belongs to the acquired set. In hidden coordinates its invariant forms are diagonal. Consequently

    M=L^T diag(b_i)L,   b_i positive rational.      (6)

Rationality follows both from the projector computation and from rational L. This selects a metric invariant under the *acquired rational subgroup*. It need not respect every nonrational real symmetry, and that distinction avoids the obstruction in Section8.

## 6. Axis selection and unconditional certificate

Convert every original line-candidate c to w=M^{-1}c. On a good line, (6) puts the quartic in normalized squared coordinates with PD contrast G=diag(b)^{-1}Q diag(b)^{-1}. The observable score

    f(w)/(w^T M w)^2

is m^T Gm on the simplex. The identity

    sum_i m_i G_ii - m^T Gm
      = sum_(i<j) m_i m_j (G_ii+G_jj-2G_ij)

is strictly positive off its vertices, since each pairwise contrast is positive under G>0. Therefore a candidate pool containing every true axis has a true axis as its highest-score member. Select it, filter the original pool by exact M-orthogonality, and continue. Every principal face has a PD contrast, so all n original primal rays are recovered on a good line.

The prototype also applies the exact restricted-stationarity/negative-Hessian tests of the prior supplied-metric theorem at every stage. For a bad line, M may lack alignment because some hidden reflections may be missing; those tests by themselves do not have the original axis-identification guarantee. The final verifier is therefore mandatory: check V invertible, V^T M V diagonal, f(Vz) exactly coordinatewise even and equal to (z^2)^T Q' z^2, and Q'>0 by rational LDL. Rational LDL yields positive rational weights and rational quadratic factors whose weighted squares are re-expanded to f in original coordinates.

This final certificate is sound outside the promise as well. It certifies a valid quartic decomposition, while membership in the generic subclass is a separate question. Under QPD the independent uniqueness theorem identifies its unordered primal rays with the original promised ones. A good-line trial is guaranteed to pass, so under E_F!=0 the expected number of trials is at most4/3 and failure after A independent trials is below4^{-A}.

## 7. Complete polynomial-bit accounting and executable circuit

The input has O(n^4) rational coefficients. Hessian entries have degree2, third derivatives degree1. A polynomial-sized determinant/adjugate circuit is available by Newton identities and Cayley–Hamilton:

    e_0=1, e_k=(1/k)sum_(i=1)^k(-1)^(i-1)e_(k-i)tr(H^i),
    adj H=sum_(k=0)^(n-1)(-1)^k e_(n-1-k)H^k,
    (grad h)_j=tr(adj H partial_j H).

Divisions are only by known nonzero integers k; no evaluated polynomial is inverted. This circuit computes F without expanding its multivariate coefficients.

Solve X'=F(X), X(0)=u+tv, through formal-time order n-1. Differentiate the recurrence in each initial coordinate using first-order dual numbers. Coordinate flow coefficient X_k has univariate t-degree at most1+(r-1)k=O(n^2), and each dual derivative has at most that bound. Evaluate the same determinant/matrix-power circuit in the truncated formal-time/dual polynomial ring at every recurrence stage. Ring arithmetic is polynomial in n,d and coefficient size. The prototype implements exactly this circuit: it never expands det(Hess f), F, or E_F in all original variables.

For height, put Hessian entries over a common integer denominator q_0, with coefficient one-norm at most A. Then det H and adj H have denominator divisors q_0^n and q_0^(n-1); F has denominator divisor q_0^(2n-1). Their logarithmic one-norm bounds are O(n(log A+log n)), using determinant/cofactor expansion bounds and the degree factor from differentiating det H. These bounds hold even though the symbolic expansion is not executed. Common-denominator formation already has polynomial bits in the expanded input.

Write F=P/q and C=max(1,max_i||P_i||_1). For a homogeneous g of degree s, ||(P dot grad)g||_1<=s C||g||_1. Hence

    ||(P dot grad)^k x_i||_1
      <= C^k product_(a<k)[1+(r-1)a],

and denominators divide q^k. Line substitution with coordinates bounded by S multiplies this bound by at most (2max(1,S))^[1+(r-1)k]. The determinant has denominator divisor q^[n(n-1)/2], and numerator one-norm at most n! times the product of the row bounds. With r=O(n), d=O(n^3), k<n and log S=O(log n), all coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in n,B. Dual derivatives add only a degree factor. Constant divisions in the Newton circuit and factorial divisions in the ODE recurrence preserve polynomial intermediate heights; evaluated degrees and number of nested products are polynomial.

Rational univariate factorization is standard polynomial bit time (LLL and subsequent algorithms). Root numerators/denominators have polynomial bits from the rational-root theorem. Multiplicity derivatives and acquired normals therefore have polynomial height. Equation(4) is a rational linear system after a degree-three substitution into O(n^3) coefficients; its kernel and every rational reflection have polynomial bit size. k<=d. Building the N-square matrix of(5), its two rational subspaces and the projector is polynomial bit time with polynomial output height. No bound on hidden L is presumed: the recovered coefficient vectors inherit their height bound from the evaluated polynomial and rational linear systems.

Score comparisons, restricted Hessian checks, fixed-degree coordinate substitution, rational LDL and the final exact identity check are polynomial bit time. ## 8. Exact obstructions and complete binary boundary

**Canonical rational metric obstruction (prior worker deduction).** For f=a x^4+2b x^2y^2+c y^4, a,c>0 and ac-b^2>0, sign flips force any metric invariant under every real f-symmetry to be diagonal. The real symmetry

    S(x,y)=((c/a)^{1/4}y,(a/c)^{1/4}x)

then forces M_22/M_11=sqrt(c/a). With a=1,c=2,b=0 this is irrational, so no rational SPD metric respects the full real stabilizer. Rational aligned metrics nevertheless exist (I is one in these coordinates). Thus a rational fully equivariant canonical selector cannot solve the promised interface on all inputs. This is an obstruction to that selector, not to rational axis acquisition. Our method uses the rational acquired subgroup and breaks the nonrational symmetry.

**Binary universal nonvanishing.** Let H=Hess f, h=det H. Euler gives H(x,y)^T=3 grad f. Therefore

    E_F=x F_2-y F_1=3 Jac(f,h).

Direct reconstruction gives

    h=48[ab x^4+(3ac-b^2)x^2y^2+bc y^4],
    Jac(f,h)=1152 xy(ac-b^2)(a x^4-c y^4).         (7)

Under QPD, (7) is nonzero and includes the two hidden rational factors after any rational invertible L. This is the classical quartic sextic, not a newly invented invariant; prior worker located Olver–Shakiban1989 Example3 and Milne1911/1912. Primary source inspection/recoverable files are in `sources/metric_prior/`; our direct formula is independent arithmetic reconstruction.

A deterministic binary coefficient-only comparator factors this fixed-degree homogeneous sextic over Q (ordinary univariate dehomogenization plus factors at infinity), considers its at most15 unordered pairs of rational linear factors, and verifies the resulting even QPD identity.
