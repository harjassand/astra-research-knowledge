CONTACT FACE: INDEPENDENT STRUCTURAL AUDIT
15 October 2026. Internal exact mathematics; historical priority UNKNOWN.

Let V be the real vector space of left-SO(2)-invariant homogeneous quartic
polynomials on real 2 by 2 matrices, and let K be its rank-one-convex cone.
This ambient space is essential throughout. Nothing below claims extremality
in the cone of all integrands or even all homogeneous quartics.

Write A=[[a,b],[c,d]], u=a^2+c^2, v=b^2+d^2, w=ab+cd, j=ad-bc,
H=(u+v-2j)^2=((a-d)^2+(b+c)^2)^2.

1. THE CONTACT IS AN EXPOSED FACE, AND ITS SPAN HAS DIMENSION FOUR

Every member of V has the unique nine-coefficient representation

 f0 u^2 + f1 v^2 + f2 uv + f3 w^2 + f4 uw + f5 vw
 + f6 uj + f7 vj + f8 wj.

The quadratic invariant identity is uv=w^2+j^2; the displayed nine monomials
are independent as quartic polynomials in a,b,c,d.

For F in K, the acoustic polynomial
P_F(A,eta,xi)=D^2F(A)[eta tensor xi,eta tensor xi] is nonnegative everywhere.
The functional ell0(F)=P_F(I,e1,e2) is linear and nonnegative on K. Hence
C=K intersect ker ell0 is an exposed face of K.

At the contact (I,e1,e2), nonnegativity forces P_F and all its first
derivatives in A,eta,xi to vanish. Exact linear elimination gives

 f1=-f7/4, f2+f3=-f7/2, f4=f5=f8=0.

Consequently C lies in the four-dimensional space
F=alpha H+beta u^2+gamma j^2+delta uj. The parameter conversion is

 f0=alpha+beta, f1=alpha, f2=6alpha+gamma,
 f3=-4alpha-gamma, f6=-4alpha+delta, f7=-4alpha,
 f4=f5=f8=0.

2. THE TWO LMI CONDITIONS ARE NECESSARY

Let M1=[[6beta+delta,delta],[delta,gamma]] and
M2=[[8alpha,delta],[delta,2beta+delta]].

For any real k, take A_t=[[1,-kt],[kt,1]] and R_t=e1 tensor (t,1).
The exact acoustic identity is

 (1/2)D^2F(A_t)[R_t,R_t]
 =t^2[6beta+delta+2delta(1-k)+gamma(1-k)^2]
  +t^4 k^2(2beta+delta).

Letting t tend to zero forces the quadratic bracket to be nonnegative
for every k; this is exactly M1 >= 0, including gamma=0.

For A=[[0,b],[c,0]] and R=e11, put X=b+c and Y=c. Then

 (1/2)D^2F(A)[R,R]
 =2alpha X^2-delta X Y+(2beta+delta)Y^2
 =(X/2,-Y) M2 (X/2,-Y)^T.

Thus M2 >= 0 is also necessary. These tests do not divide by gamma,
alpha, delta, or an acoustic determinant.

3. EXTREME RAYS OF THE TWO-LMI CONE

Let S={coefficients: M1>=0,M2>=0}. It is a closed pointed cone. The map
from coefficients to the block diagonal matrix diag(M1,M2) is injective.
The functional trace(M1)+trace(M2) is strictly positive on S except at 0,
and its unit section is compact.

For delta=0, S reduces to alpha,beta,gamma>=0. The axis rays H,u^2,j^2
are extreme even with respect to possible decompositions having nonzero
delta: the zero block or diagonal entries of each axis force the same
zero block/entries in every positive-semidefinite summand.

For delta!=0, both blocks are nonzero, alpha>0 and gamma>0. If either
block is positive definite, the constraints preserving the kernel of the
other block impose at most two linear equations on the four coefficient
directions. A nonproportional direction can therefore be perturbed with
both signs by a sufficiently small amount inside S. Such a point is not
extreme. Consequently a non-axis extreme ray requires both blocks to
have rank one.

Normalize gamma=1 and write r=delta. Their determinant equations give

 beta=r(r-1)/6, alpha=3r/[8(r+2)].

The second block has lower diagonal r(r+2)/3. Thus its positivity requires
r>0 or r<-2. For these ranges put

 J_r = 3r/[8(r+2)] H + r(r-1)/6 u^2 + j^2 + r uj.

Its block kernels are span(1,-r) and span(r+2,-3). Requiring a perturbation
to preserve both kernels gives three independent linear equations in four
unknowns. The resulting line is exactly span J_r, proving extremality.
This exhausts the extreme rays of S.

4. INDEPENDENT SUFFICIENCY CHECK FOR EVERY SHARP RAY

By left invariance it suffices to test target direction e1. Write the
spatial direction (p,q), x=(a,b,c,d)^T, and define M_r by

 (1/2)D^2J_r(A)[e1 tensor (p,q),e1 tensor (p,q)]=x^T M_r(p,q) x.

Its first three leading principal minors are

 L1 = r/([4(r+2)]) [(2r+1)^2 p^2+3q^2],

 L2 = 3r^2/[16(r+2)^2] *
      {[(2r+1)p^2-3q^2]^2+12r(r+2)p^2q^2},

 L3 = 3q^2 r^2/[16(r+2)^2] *
      {[(r+1)(2r+1)p^2-3q^2]^2+3r(5r+4)p^2q^2}.

The full determinant is

 det M_r(p,q)=9p^4 q^4 r^4/16.

For r>0 or r<-2, r/(r+2), r(r+2), r(5r+4) are positive. If pq!=0,
all four leading minors are positive, so Sylvester's criterion gives
positive definiteness. The two coordinate directions are checked directly:

 At p=0,q=1, M_r is positive semidefinite with kernel span I.

 At q=0,p=1, the (b,c) block is
 r/[12(r+2)] * (3,-2r-1)^T (3,-2r-1),
 while the (a,d) block has positive first diagonal and positive determinant
 r(2r+1)^2/[4(r+2)]. The full kernel is therefore
 span A_r, where A_r=[[0,(2r+1)/3],[1,0]].

This proves J_r is rank-one convex for every allowed r. H and u^2 are
convex; j^2 is polyconvex. By the compact-base extreme-ray description,
S is their closed conic hull together with the J_r rays. The rank-one
convex cone is closed. Hence S is contained in K. Together with Section 2,
this proves the full two-LMI equivalence on the contact span, and C=S.
It also proves the listed rays are extreme in K, because C is a face of K.

5. EVERY NONTRIVIAL SHARP RAY IS NONPOLYCONVEX AT I

This is a local obstruction to a polyconvex supporting hyperplane, not
an inference from failure of a particular Gram certificate.

If a finite-valued F on all matrices is polyconvex, it has a convex
minor potential g(A,z) with F(A)=g(A,det A). This potential can be taken
finite everywhere: given any (A,z), choose B with det B=z-det A; then
(A,z) is the midpoint of (A+B,det(A+B)) and (A-B,det(A-B)). Thus a
supporting hyperplane exists at (I,1).

For smooth F its pullback must have the form

 F(A) >= F(I)+DF(I):(A-I)
         +lambda[det A-1-tr(A-I)].

For J_r, its Hessian in the e12 direction is zero. A nonnegative support
gap must have a positive-semidefinite Hessian, so the e12-e21 cross
entry of D^2(J_r-lambda det)(I) must vanish. That entry is
-(r+2)+lambda. Therefore lambda=r+2 is forced.

The resulting support gap is

 G_r(A)=J_r(A)-(r+2)j-(2/3)r(r+2)a+(r+2)(r+1)/2.

The Hessian of this gap has null direction K_r=diag(1,-r-1). Along it,
the exact polynomial identity is

 G_r(I+t K_r)
 = -r(r+2)t^3/3
   +(r+2)(9r^3+36r^2+40r+12)t^4/24.

For every allowed r, the cubic coefficient is nonzero (indeed negative).
Thus the gap is negative for sufficiently small positive t. This refutes
polyconvexity at I for every J_r, r>0 or r<-2.

The same cubic argument applies when alpha is increased while beta,
gamma=1,delta=r stay at this first-LMI boundary. H contributes only
fourth-order terms near I. Thus all these first-boundary members are
nonpolyconvex at I as well.

6. SHARP RAYS ARE NONEXPOSED IN THE FULL NINE-DIMENSIONAL CONE K

They ARE exposed inside C: the nonnegative functional obtained by adding
the two block-kernel quadratic forms vanishes only on their ray. Exposure
does not automatically pass from an exposed face to its ambient cone.
For these rays it actually fails.

Let T be the compact set of acoustic evaluation functionals
ell_(A,eta,xi)(F)=D^2F(A)[eta tensor xi,eta tensor xi], with
|A|=|eta|=|xi|=1. The invariant quartic Q0(A)=|A|^4 satisfies

 ell_(A,eta,xi)(Q0)
 =8[A:(eta tensor xi)]^2+4|A|^2|eta tensor xi|^2 >= 4.

Consequently cone(T) is closed: evaluation at Q0 bounds the total mass
of any convergent nonnegative conic combination, and conv(T) is compact
in finite dimension. Bipolar duality gives K*=cone(T). Caratheodory's
theorem permits finite conic combinations; no distributional derivative
of an acoustic evaluation is being included as a positive functional.

Suppose L>=0 on K and L(J_r)=0. Write L as a nonnegative finite sum of
normalized acoustic evaluations. Every evaluation used with positive
weight must vanish on J_r. After rotating its target vector to e1, the
determinant and kernels in Section 4 show its only possible contacts are

 p=0, A proportional to I; or q=0, A proportional to A_r.

For j^2, the acoustic form in this target normalization is 2(dp-cq)^2.
It vanishes at both contact families. Hence every term of L annihilates
j^2 and L(j^2)=0. Since j^2 and J_r are independent, L cannot expose the
ray J_r in K.

In fact its smallest exposed face in K is exactly cone{J_r,j^2}. It is
exposed by ell0+ell_r, where ell_r(F)=D^2F(A_r)[e11,e11]. The first zero
puts F in C. For z=(r+2,-3), the second zero is

 (1/2)ell_r(F)=(1/9) z M2 z^T=0.

Since M2 is positive semidefinite, M2 z^T=0, so

 alpha=3delta/[8(r+2)], beta=(r-1)delta/6.

The first LMI now forces delta=t r and gamma=t+s for t,s>=0. Thus
F=t J_r+s j^2, as claimed. The preceding dual argument proves that no
smaller exposed face contains J_r.

7. LINEARLY TRANSFORMED ISOTROPIC QUARTICS DO NOT EXPLAIN THESE RAYS

Consider any two-sided SO(2)-invariant rank-one-convex quartic
Q(A)=a0|A|^4+b0|A|^2 det A+c0(det A)^2, and invertible real P,D.
The operation Q(P A D) preserves rank-one convexity and quasiconvexity
when Q is quasiconvex. Average this function under left SO(2), obtaining E.
On rank-one matrices A=x tensor n,

 E(x tensor n)=a0 k_P |x|^4 [n^T D D^T n]^2,

where k_P>0. Rank-one convexity forces a0>=0; if a0=0, evaluating the
e11 acoustic form at diagonal matrices forces b0=0, so E is a multiple
of j^2.

For J_r, the same rank-one restriction is

 |x|^4 {alpha(n1^2+n2^2)^2+beta n1^4}.

With alpha>0 this is a square of a real quadratic form in n only when
beta=0. Indeed the odd coefficients force its mixed term to vanish,
and the n1^2 n2^2 coefficient then forces its two diagonal coefficients
to be equal. In the allowed sharp ranges beta=0 occurs only at r=1.

At r=1 the restriction is radial, forcing D D^T to be scalar. Then
Q(P A D) and its left average are right-SO(2)-invariant. J_1 is not:
its expansion contains (u-v)j/2. Thus E cannot be proportional to J_1.

Since J_r is extreme in K, a positive finite sum of these left averages
could equal J_r only if every nonzero average were proportional to J_r.
The preceding obstruction rules this out for every allowed r. Therefore
neither a single two-sided-invariant quartic under P A D nor positive
finite combinations of such transformed quartics prove its QC by simple
inheritance. Adding arbitrary convex quartics does not rescue that finite
decomposition: after left averaging, extremality would force a convex
multiple of J_r, contradicting Section 5.

This is a precise exclusion of these standard linear-transform and
positive-combination routes. It does not exclude a different analytic
consequence of the literature, nonpolynomial constructions, nonlinear
changes of variables, or a future direct QC proof.

Transposition must not be treated as a general QC-preserving operation.
For this isotropic source class specifically Q(B^T)=Q(B), so
Q(P A^T D)=Q(D^T A P^T), reducing it to the already covered operation.

8. SCOPE OF THE AUDIT

The structural identities above are independently checked by
verify_structural.py and its exact receipt. They establish ROC,
extremality with the stated ambient cone, nonpolyconvexity, and the
nonexposure distinction. They do not themselves establish QC of any
nonpolyconvex J_r. Other workers' heat certificates have separate
provenance and validation. No historical-first claim is made here.
