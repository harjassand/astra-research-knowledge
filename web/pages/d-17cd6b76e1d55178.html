# No-cloning / compatibility obstruction for common diagonal compression

Date: 2026-10-08  
Status: **INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE** for a scoped per-test theorem; **REFUTED** as a general obstruction to aggregate common-B encodings.  
Scope: arbitrary finite-dimensional logical spaces and arbitrary common isometries; no claim about implementing the isometry or a sparse positive enforcer.

## Question and interface

Can noncommuting rank-one tests from a hard family be compressed with constant
strength into one common diagonal algebra, and can no-cloning or
information-disturbance prohibit it? The answer depends on whether one asks
for each test separately or only their weighted aggregate. The exact theorem
below rules out sufficiently strong *simultaneous individual* tests. A
Naimark-style construction below shows that no such argument rules out the
aggregate test, even with a single common diagonal projection and exact
compression.

Let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal K\) be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces,
\(V:\mathcal H\to\mathcal K\) an isometry, and \(D_1,D_2\) commuting
diagonal effects (in particular, diagonal projections are allowed). Put
\(A=V^*D_1V\), \(B=V^*D_2V\). The four diagonal effects

\[
E_{11}=D_1D_2,\quad E_{10}=D_1(I-D_2),\quad
E_{01}=(I-D_1)D_2,\quad E_{00}=(I-D_1)(I-D_2)
\]
are a POVM. Their compressions \(G_{ab}=V^*E_{ab}V\) form a joint POVM
for \(A,B\). Thus compressed effects from the common diagonal algebra are
compatible, regardless of the isometry, workspace size, or use of active
ancillas. More generally, the same conclusion holds for any two commuting
effects in place of diagonal effects.

## Exact two-seed theorem (sharp)

Let unit vectors \(\phi,\psi\in\mathcal H\) satisfy
\(c=|\langle\phi,\psi\rangle|\in(0,1)\), and let
\(P=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|\), \(Q=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\). Suppose
compatible effects \(A,B\) obey, as operator inequalities,

\[
0\le A,B\le I,\qquad \alpha P\le A\le \beta P,
\qquad \alpha Q\le B\le \beta Q,
\]
for constants \(0<\alpha\le\beta\). Necessarily

\[
\boxed{\alpha\le (1+c)^{-1}.}
\]

Proof. The upper sandwiches imply \(\operatorname{supp}(A)\subseteq
\operatorname{ran}P\) and \(\operatorname{supp}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}Q\),
so \(A=aP,B=bQ\) with \(a,b\ge\alpha\). For a joint POVM \(G_{ab}\),
\(0\le G_{11}\le A,B\). Since the one-dimensional supports of \(P,Q\) have
zero intersection, \(G_{11}=0\). Hence \(A+B\le I\), while
\(A+B\ge\alpha(P+Q)\). The top eigenvalue of \(P+Q\) is \(1+c\), proving
the bound.

The threshold is sharp: if \(0\le\alpha\le(1+c)^{-1}\), the four effects
\(G_{10}=\alpha P\), \(G_{01}=\alpha Q\), \(G_{11}=0\),
\(G_{00}=I-\alpha(P+Q)\) are a joint POVM. Naimark dilation realizes them
as compressions of commuting diagonal projections. Thus beta contributes no
additional obstruction (take \(A=\alpha P,B=\alpha Q\)); values of alpha
below threshold genuinely evade this route.

For the BJSW five-qubit seed pair recorded in
`outputs/research/games_hamiltonians/cycle2/positive_encoding_cycle2.md`,
\(c=1/(2\sqrt2)\), so the sharp threshold is
\(\alpha_*=2\sqrt2/(2\sqrt2+1)\simeq0.738796125\). In particular,
alpha=1 is impossible for both sharp individual tests; alpha=1/2 is
compatible. The BMVZ-style weighted terms of weight 1/4 are well below this
threshold. No conclusion follows for a single aggregate \(B\).

### Amplified OR tests

The same witness is available for the repeated-block tests
\[
P_{\phi,T}=I-(I-P)^{\otimes T},\qquad
P_{\psi,T}=I-(I-Q)^{\otimes T}.
\]
Let \(S=\operatorname{span}\{\phi,\psi\}\) and
\(L=\phi^\perp\cap\psi^\perp\). The subspace
\(\mathcal R=S\otimes L^{\otimes(T-1)}\) is reducing for both OR tests. On
\(\mathcal R\), each product kernel is a one-dimensional line in the first
factor (tensored with \(L^{\otimes(T-1)}\)); the OR projector is the
rank-one projection onto \(\phi\), respectively \(\psi\), in \(S\), tensored
with the identity on the other factors. Their overlap is still c. Compressing
the compatible tests to \(\mathcal R\) preserves compatibility and the order
inequalities, so the same threshold holds, independently of T. This is a
per-clause statement only.

## Additive perturbation version

Suppose instead that compatible effects satisfy
\[
\|A-\alpha P\|\le\epsilon,\qquad
\|B-\alpha Q\|\le\epsilon,
\]
where \(\epsilon\ge0\). Let \(G=G_{11}\) be the joint effect. Then
\(0\le G\le A,B\) and \(I-A-B+G\ge0\). For \(x\in\ker P\),
\(\langle x,Gx\rangle\le\langle x,Ax\rangle\le\epsilon\|x\|^2\);
the analogous bound holds on \(\ker Q\). In \(S=\operatorname{span}\{\phi,\psi\}\),
choose unit \(u\perp\phi\), \(v\perp\psi\). Their overlap magnitude is c,
so if \(x=au+bv\),
\[
\|x\|^2\ge(1-c)(|a|^2+|b|^2),\qquad
\|G^{1/2}x\|\le\sqrt{2\epsilon/(1-c)}\,\|x\|.
\]
On \(S^\perp\), the bound is \(\|G^{1/2}x\|\le\sqrt\epsilon\|x\|\).
Decomposing any vector into these two orthogonal subspaces and using the
triangle inequality yields
\[
\|G\|\le\epsilon\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-c}}\right)^2.
\]
Evaluate \(I-A-B+G\ge0\) on a top eigenvector of \(P+Q\), and use
\(\|(A+B)-\alpha(P+Q)\|\le2\epsilon\). The result is
\[
\boxed{\alpha(1+c)\le1+\epsilon K(c)},\qquad
K(c)=2+\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-c}}\right)^2.
\]
This is a sufficient quantitative obstruction, not claimed optimal. For the
BJSW value, \(K(c)\simeq9.6117\); alpha=1 is therefore impossible whenever
\(\epsilon<c/K(c)\simeq0.03678\).

If the supplied seed vectors are approximate, with unit-vector errors at most
eta, their overlap magnitude lies in
\([c_-,c_+]\), where
\(c_- = \max(0,c_0-2\eta)\) and \(c_+=\min(1,c_0+2\eta)\). Consequently the
exact order theorem is uniform if \(\alpha>(1+c_-)^{-1}\); the additive
version is uniform if \(c_+<1\) and
\(\alpha(1+c_-)>1+\epsilon K(c_+)\). An exact isometry with an active
ancilla preserves inner products, so adding a workspace cannot turn
nonorthogonal seeds into orthogonal labels. If the encoding is only an
approximate isometry, its Gram-matrix distortion must be included in eta;
the exact-isometry theorem does not silently cover it.

## Counterexample: aggregate compression has no no-cloning obstruction

Take any finite family of positive contractions \(0\le H_j\le I\), weights
\(w_j\ge0\) with \(\sum_{j=1}^M w_j=1\), and aggregate
\(H=\sum_jw_jH_j\) (thus \(0\le H\le I\)). On
\(\mathcal K=\mathbb C^{M+1}\otimes\mathcal H\), define
\[
Vx=|0\rangle\sqrt{I-H}\,x+
\sum_{j=1}^M|j\rangle\sqrt{w_jH_j}\,x.
\]
Then \(V^*V=I-H+\sum_jw_jH_j=I\), so V is an isometry. Put
\(D_j=|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes I\) and
\(B=\sum_{j=1}^M D_j\). These are common computational-basis diagonal
projections (B is one projection), and
\[
V^*D_jV=w_jH_j,\qquad V^*BV=H.
\]
Thus the entire possibly noncommuting weighted family has exact aggregate
compression, alpha=beta=1 for H, and a single common diagonal B. It is not
cloning: each per-clause effect is weighted/unsharp. In particular, for
uniform M the individual coefficient is 1/M and tends to zero, while the
aggregate remains exact. The construction is valid even if the \(H_j\) are
rank-one tests with the BJSW overlap.

An exact image enforcer is \(C=I-VV^*\), a positive contraction with
\(\ker C=\operatorname{ran}V\) and gap 1. This algebraic counterexample
does **not** meet a sparse/local implementation interface in general:
\(C\) can be dense, and the isometry requires square roots of global
operators (including \(\sqrt{I-H}\)). The workspace dimension is
\((M+1)\dim\mathcal H\); B and each D_j are row-1-sparse, but these facts
give no polynomial circuit or entry-access guarantee for V or C. If \(H_j\)
are projections, \(\sqrt{w_jH_j}=\sqrt{w_j}H_j\), but \(\sqrt{I-H}\) may
still be global. Therefore no-cloning/compatibility alone cannot disprove a
common-B aggregate encoding; any valid impossibility theorem must use further
interface conditions, such as sparse C, efficient V, or strong separate
per-clause compression. This is not a solution to the positive sparse
constant-gap encoding problem.

## Constants and boundary

* Exact per-seed threshold: alpha at most \((1+c)^{-1}\), sharp; beta is
  immaterial except for containing alpha. This gives a genuine obstruction
  only above threshold.
* Approximate per-seed threshold: the explicit additive bound above; for
  \(c=1/(2\sqrt2)\), alpha=1 tolerates at most about 3.68% operator-norm
  error under this proof.
* Active ancillas: preserve the compatibility theorem and seed Gram matrix;
  they do not evade the individual-test bound. They do allow Naimark dilation
  and hence the aggregate counterexample.
* Constant-factor aggregate compression: not obstructed, even exactly, by
  noncommutativity. No alphabet, communication, or oracle cost is established
  for the displayed V/C; the only unconditional resource statements are the
  algebraic workspace dimension and the row-1-sparse diagonal tests.
* Novelty boundary: this note claims only the elementary compatibility
  theorem, its scoped perturbative estimate, and the explicit aggregate
  escape as an internally derived obstruction test. It makes no priority or
  literature-novelty claim.

## Validation gate

The proof is finite-dimensional and uses only POVM compatibility, rank-one
support, and a two-dimensional Gram-matrix estimate. A source-level/external
review should check the intended encoding definition: whether the target
requires separate per-clause comparisons or only a common aggregate B; what
operator-norm approximation is charged; and whether C and V must have sparse
row access, local terms, or polynomial circuits. Until those interface
conditions are fixed, the aggregate no-cloning obstruction is **UNKNOWN** and
the displayed construction is an algebraic counterexample to its
unqualified form. Relevant local interface context: `positive_encoding_cycle2.md`
and `seed_hypercube_tradeoff.md` in this directory.
