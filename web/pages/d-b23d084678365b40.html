# An exact one-term prism criterion

Status, 7 October 2026: family 317 was read in full (35-page pinned PDF).
The chain identity below was independently derived. Its transfer to strict
Gray cylinders uses the published strong-Steiner/Gray tensor comparison
adopted by the manuscript. We did not compile the Lean solution or validate
the complete model-structure dependency graph. No novelty claim is made for
the refined criterion.

## Question, interface, and author claims

Can a positive chain formula be recognized locally and then extended across
an arbitrary algebraic attachment, with stationarity on the attached part?
The supplied inputs are a small poset E, monotone maps f,g:E->E with f<=g,
and an old full subposet A on which f=g. They are actual poset maps; acquiring
them and proving their hypotheses are input costs.

OpenAI, *Thomason Model Structures in Every Strict Higher Dimension*, dated
25 September 2026, claims in Theorem 1.1 (page 2) proper combinatorial model
structures on small strict globular n-categories for n>=1 and n=omega, with
c_n Sd^2 left adjoint to Ex^2 N_n giving a Quillen equivalence with simplicial
sets. The advance over prior homotopy-category equivalences is control of
arbitrary pushouts, permitting both weak factorization systems. It does not
give a finite algorithm to compute arbitrary homotopy types.

The manuscript's Lemma 3.1 (pages 10-13) assumes A is a sieve and g is
constant on E minus A. For a nondecreasing word w=(w_0,...,w_p), it uses

    C(w tensor e_0)=F(w)=[f(w_0),...,f(w_p)],
    C(w tensor e_1)=G(w)=[g(w_0),...,g(w_p)],
    C(w tensor u)=h(w)=[f(w_0),...,f(w_p),g(w_p)],

with normalized repeated vertices equal to zero. The right tensor interval
has du=e_1-e_0, so the required identity is

    d h(w) = h(d w) + (-1)^p (G(w)-F(w)).

Theorem 5.2 (pages 18-20) then contracts every attachment of c_n N A -> c_n N E
when A is a sieve with a right-adjoint retraction. Lemma 4.6 and finite sieve
covers glue the local contractions without requiring compatible cylinders on
overlaps. Proposition 6.1 gives the actual nerve-pushout comparison; Section 7
transfers the model structure.

## Our exact criterion and proof

Define condition P on monotone f<=g by

    for every comparable x<y in E,
        f(x)=g(x) OR g(x)=g(y).                       (P)

Claim: the displayed one-term prescription C is a positive,
augmentation-preserving chain map on every simplex precisely when P holds.
Here the chain-map assertion is an identity in the free normalized chain
complex of E. Necessity and sufficiency are independent of a model structure.

Necessity. Take a one-simplex (x,y). Its chain-map residual is

    d h(x,y) - h(y) + h(x) + G(x,y) - F(x,y)
       = -[f(x),g(y)] + [f(x),g(x)] + [g(x),g(y)].

If f(x)<g(x)<g(y), these are three distinct basis edges; the long edge has
coefficient -1, so the residual is nonzero. Since f(x)<=g(x)<=g(y), the only
remaining possibilities are exactly the alternatives in P. This also handles
normalized degeneracies.

Sufficiency and positivity. For a nondecreasing word w, any g(w_j) which
differs from the final value g(w_p) must equal f(w_j), by P. Consequently all
vertices in the two endpoint words lie in the totally ordered set comprising
the f-image word and the one final value g(w_p). Every image in C is therefore
a positive basis word or zero. The endpoint maps are normalized chain maps.

For p>0 put

    B =[f(w_0),...,f(w_(p-1)),g(w_p)],
    B'=[f(w_0),...,f(w_(p-1)),g(w_(p-1))].

If g(w_(p-1))=g(w_p), then B=B' and G(w) has equal final vertices, so
B-B'=G(w)=0. If the two values differ, then for every j<p monotonicity gives
g(w_j)<=g(w_(p-1))<g(w_p), and P gives f(w_j)=g(w_j). Hence B=G(w), while B'
has repeated final vertices and is zero. Thus B-B'=G(w) in both cases.

Expanding d h(w), all other deletion terms cancel with h(d w), leaving
exactly (-1)^p(B-B'-F(w))=(-1)^p(G(w)-F(w)). For p=0 this is the boundary of
[f(w_0),g(w_0)]. The augmentation at both interval endpoints is one.

Simplex compatibility. Precomposition with an injective monotone ordinal map
selects the same words. A noninjective ordinal map produces repeated f-images
in h and repeated images in both endpoint formulas; all are zero in normalized
chains. Thus C is compatible with faces and degeneracies. If a simplex lies
in any full subposet A on which f=g, the complete chain map factors through
the augmentation of the interval. This proves stationarity on all lower
boundary data, not merely vanishing of the top-dimensional image.

By the published comparison between tensors of strong Steiner complexes and
the biclosed Gray tensor, these compatible chain maps assemble over simplex
colimits to a strict right cylinder U_E tensor D_1 -> U_E, where U_E=c_n N E.
Finite-dimensional descent uses the manuscript's Lemma 2.1 or its cited
published internal-hom truncation theorem. The cylinder is stationary along
U_A whenever f=g on A. In this refined construction A need not be a sieve;
the criterion itself supplies the necessary ordered image sets. Arbitrary
attachment then uses the same strict internal-hom pushout argument as the
paper's Lemma 3.2, provided both endpoint maps fix the old subcategory.

The paper's assumptions imply P. If x belongs to A, the first alternative
holds. If x does not belong to A and x<y, then y also does not belong to A
because A is a sieve, and the constant-complement assumption gives the second.
Thus this is a recognition/generalization of that formula, not a disproof of
Lemma 3.1.

## Concrete extension and decisive obstruction

Take the fork poset E={a<b,a<c}, A={a}, f constant at a and g the identity.
The map g is not constant on E minus A; that complement has no greatest
element. Nevertheless P holds: every moving vertex is maximal. The criterion
provides a single strict right cylinder from the retraction to the identity,
stationary on U_A. For every attachment U_A->X it extends to the pushout and
gives a direct homotopy retraction. The manuscript's general couniversality
theorem also covers this example, but gets it via cover gluing. The refined
criterion gives a shorter explicit certificate.

More generally, a right-adjoint retraction r:E->A with A a sieve and antichain
complement has f=ir<=id_E=g satisfying P. Thus the arbitrary-target attachment
has a single stationary contraction cylinder without cover induction.

Dropping P fails already for E={0<1<2}, f=id and g=(1,2,2). On w=(0,1),
h(w)=[0,1,2], its boundary is [1,2]-[0,2]+[0,1], but the required right side
is zero. This is an obstruction to this one-term strict positive formula,
not an obstruction to every possible nerve homotopy or Gray cylinder.

## Evidence, source distinction, costs, and next step

Run `python3 work/frontier_topology/cycle19/prism_criterion.py`. It independently
represents normalized words as integer formal sums and checks all basis
chain identities for all monotone pairs f<=g on chains with one to five
vertices: 5,839 pairs, of which 1,158 satisfy every identity. In every tested
pair, identity satisfaction is exactly P. It also checks the fork example
and records the explicit three-vertex failure. PRISM_CHECKS.json preserves
the counts and residual. These finite checks do not establish the universal
claim; the all-degree proof above does that at the chain level.

On a supplied finite poset with m vertices and an order matrix, checking P
costs O(m^2) equality/order tests. Checking f,g are monotone has the same
order. Each simplex's chain prescription uses O(p) vertex accesses. Constructing
every simplex explicitly can cost exponentially many words. In the Thomason
adjunction, a d-simplex acquires ((d+1)!)^2 top-dimensional simplices after two
barycentric subdivisions. Existence of the transfer therefore provides no
automatic improvement in time or representation size for arbitrary inputs.

Nearest inspected primary prior art is Gagna, arXiv:1612.04450v2 (15 February
2018; initial submission 14 December 2016), Sections 6.3-6.8. Those sections
construct nerve homotopies through positive concatenation at a chosen
simplicial cut; they do not assert the one-term right-tensor chain prescription
above for arbitrary f<=g. Theorem 5.6 already gives homotopy types via strict
n-categories. Ara-Maltsiniotis arXiv:1305.5086v3 (18 April 2014), abstract,
establishes the n=2 transfer and isolates conditions for n>2. These facts
bound the release's novelty to arbitrary strict globular pushout/model
control, rather than first representation of spaces by strict categories.

Concrete downstream use: a syntactic recognizer for a stationary contraction
can output exact directed homotopy certificates on simple finite poset
attachments. This can reduce proof search for couniversal attachments and
formalization of directed rewriting constructions. It does not solve the
arbitrary homotopy-equivalence recognition problem.

Best next falsifiable step: formalize the refined chain criterion and its
necessity on arbitrary posets; independently check the strong-Steiner/Gray
assembly and finite-reflection bridge. Then compare the resulting criterion
against already published stationary-cylinder variants before claiming any
novelty. A proof relying only on top-chain vanishing would be insufficient:
stationarity must hold on the complete lower cell tables.
