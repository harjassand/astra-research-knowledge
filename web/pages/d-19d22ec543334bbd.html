# Independent audit: directed guarded switching theorem

**Audit target:** `work/agents/engineering_control/directed_guarded_switching/PROOF.md` as inspected 2026-10-08, including its explicit same-time switch/jump convention in §Guarded switching theorem.  
**Verdict:** no counterexample found to the stopped-barrier inequality under the theorem's stated CTMC/compensator interpretation. The numerical constants and switch-at-jump convention are consistent. The main theorem is conditional on an important operational convention: a segment uses its fixed old mode through the jump at its stopping time, and an outside-box post-jump state is recorded as failure before any update. If that convention is omitted, a post-jump mode update can change the segment's potential and invalidate a naive one-function Dynkin argument. The present proof now states the convention explicitly.

## 1. Interface and assumptions checked

The process is a pure-jump molecular CTMC at fixed volume (V), with concentrations (x_{i,s}=Z_{i,s}/(\alpha_iV)). In mode (j), site-local reaction (r) has intensity (\alpha_iV k_{jir}(t)\phi_{y_{jir}}^{\alpha_iV}(x_i)), with predictable (0\le k\le K); directed transfer (i\to k) of species (s) has intensity (Vc_{j,ik,s}x_{i,s}). While active, each mode is fixed, the state has no reset at a mode change, and an accepted switch starts the next segment only in that target mode's guarded core. A first post-jump state outside the current box is failure before a switch can take effect.

The pathwise proof needs the mode and reaction-rate values that determine a jump to be predictable from the pre-jump history. The current PROOF.md now makes the simultaneous-event convention explicit: at a switch/jump tie, the jump is generated by (J_{t-}); only subsequent intensities use the selected new mode. The deterministic/predictable face-margin hypotheses must hold for every allowed reaction-rate choice. (N_T) must count all safe-prefix segment starts, and its finite expectation is needed to sum restart terms. For a noisy sensor, the target-core erosion makes the post-switch state safe for the next restart; the source-box condition is already guaranteed pathwise while the old segment remains active, provided failure on a true box exit is immediate as stated.

## 2. Generator and constant audit

For a face gap (g_q), write a mark's scaled increment as \(\xi=V\Delta g_q\). Then
\[
\sum_r\lambda_r\xi_r=V\,\mathcal L_tg_q,\qquad
\sum_r\lambda_r\xi_r^2\le VB_q,
\]
because the reaction contribution to the latter is
\(\sum_r(\alpha_iVk_r\phi_y)(\nu_s/\alpha_i)^2/V\le
\alpha_i^{-1}\sum_rK_r\nu_s^2U^y\), and incoming/outgoing transfers contribute respectively
\(\alpha_i^{-2}\sum_kc_{ki,s}b_{k,s}\) and
\(\alpha_i^{-2}C_i^{out}b_{i,s}\). This matches the stated (B_q). The jump-size bound is
\(J_q=\alpha_i^{-1}\max(|\nu_{r,s}|,1\text{ for incident transfers})\), as stated. Total activity is bounded by (V\Lambda_j): reaction marks contribute (V\sum_i\alpha_i\sum_rK_rU_i^{y_r}), and directed transfers contribute (V\sum_{i\to k,s}c_{ik,s}b_{i,s}). This matches the stated \(\Lambda_j\).

The falling-factorial comparison gives \(0\le x^y-\phi_y^W(x)\le E_y(U)/W\), including insufficient-reactant states. Multiplying by the deterministic jump and rate-envelope bounds gives inward-drift error \(D_q/V\). Thus \(V\ge\lceil2D_q/\bar\gamma_q\rceil\) yields finite-volume inward drift at least \(\mu_q=\bar\gamma_q/2\).

For \(\Phi_q=e^{-\theta_qVg_q}\),
\[
\frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_q}{\Phi_q}
=\sum_r\lambda_r(e^{-\theta_q\xi_r}-1)
\le V\{-\theta_q\mu_q+(e/2)\theta_q^2B_q\}
\le V\{-\theta_q\mu_q+(3/2)\theta_q^2B_q\}\le0
\]
in the collar, using \(\theta_qJ_q\le1\), (e<3), and \(\theta_q\le\mu_q/(3B_q)\). Outside the collar, positive terms satisfy
\(e^{-\theta_q\xi}-1\le e^{\theta_qJ_q}-1\le e-1<2\), hence
\(\mathcal L_t\Phi_q\le2V\Lambda_j e^{-\theta_qV\ell_q}\le2V\Lambda_je^{-c_*V}\). Summing the (M_j) faces gives exactly the residual used in the theorem and (C_*=2\max_jM_j\Lambda_j); no missing factor of (V), (\alpha_i), or (1/2) was found.

At a first exit, at least one face (q) has (g_q(X_\tau)<0), so \(\Phi_j(X_\tau)\ge\Phi_q(X_\tau)\ge1\). Since (X_{\tau-}\in Q_j), every crossed face overshoots by at most (J_q/V), giving \(\Phi_q(X_\tau)\le e^{\theta_qJ_q}\le e\); each un-crossed face has potential at most 1. Thus \(\Phi_j(X_\tau)\le eM_j<3M_j\), which is sufficient for stopped Dynkin integrability. The probability bound itself only needs nonnegativity and the lower bound \(\Phi_j\ge1\) on exit.

## 3. Stopping times, switch-at-jump, and restart sum

For segment (n), hold the old mode (j_n) fixed and stop at the minimum of (T), the first post-jump exit of (Q_{j_n}), and the next accepted switch. The stopped potential is bounded by (3M_{j_n}), and total rate before stopping is bounded by (V\Lambda_{j_n}), so the compensated jump formula is integrable at this bounded stopping time. Conditional Dynkin gives
\[
\Pr(E_n\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_n})
\le \Phi_{j_n}(X_{\sigma_n})
+C_*Ve^{-c_*V}\,\mathbb E[\tau_n-\sigma_n\mid\mathcal F_{\sigma_n}],
\]
where (E_n) is exit during segment (n), and (\sigma_n,\tau_n) are its start and stop. A guard start gives \(\Phi_{j_n}(X_{\sigma_n})\le A_V\). On the safe prefix, the segment intervals are disjoint, so their lengths sum to at most (T); their start indicators sum to at most (N_T). Summing the conditional inequalities gives
\(\Pr(\cup_nE_n)\le A_V\mathbb E[N_T]+C_*VTe^{-c_*V}\).

If an exit and switch request share a time, evaluate the old-mode segment at the post-jump state before the update. An outside-(Q_j) post-jump state is then exit; an inside-(Q_j\cap Q_k^{d_k}) state terminates the old segment and starts a new one with \(\Phi_k\le A_V\). The potential can jump because its mode index changes, but it is not used across that mode update: each side of the switch is handled by its own stopped segment and its own start bound. This closes the usual switch-at-jump gap. Standard CTMC jump ties between distinct reaction/transfer marks have probability zero under locally bounded intensities; if the model permits simultaneous batch marks, (J_q), (B_q), and the overshoot proof must bound the aggregate mark instead.

The bound counts exits from the *active box*; since (Q_j\subseteq S), any exit from prescribed (S) during a segment is included in this event. It does not claim the active box is invariant, and it does not cover a controller with delayed failure detection or switches that reset the molecular state.

## 5. Comparator: does p-safety/PDMP subsume this CTMC interface?

Primary source checked: Wisniewski, Bujorianu, and Sloth, “p-Safe Analysis of Stochastic Hybrid Processes,” IEEE TAC 65(12), 5220–5235 (2020), [author manuscript](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/72932/1/Wisniewski_etal_IEEE_TAC_2020_p_Safe_analysis_of_stochastic_hybrid_processes.pdf), [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2020.2972789).

The paper explicitly treats PDMPs with spontaneous and guard-forced jumps and defines a hybrid process through a deterministic flow, state-dependent jump rate, and transition kernel (Definition 6; Theorem 4). Its numerical section states that zero flow with unit upward jumps is p-safety verification of a Poisson process. Any fixed finite-\(V\) countable-state CTMC embeds at the process-class level: index each lattice count (and fixed mode) as a discrete hybrid state, attach a frozen dummy continuous coordinate if required by the PDMP state-space convention, set flow to zero, take total outgoing rate \(\lambda(z)=\sum_{z'\ne z}q(z,z')\), and set \(K(z'|z)=q(z,z')/\lambda(z)\). In this sense, the fixed-coefficient CTMC safety problem is within the paper's generic p-safety/PDMP model, including absorbing exit states.

That is not an exact subsumption of the present robust interface. The paper formulates a fixed strong-Markov process and its p-safety optimization; it does not directly state the uniform bound over arbitrary predictable, history-dependent reaction-rate choices or an externally selected stopping-time mode controller that updates after an observed jump. Its introduction says control is not treated explicitly, then remarks that an occupation-measure/control extension could characterize existence of a safe control. That remark is not a proof for all predictable controls. A finite-state Markov feedback controller (or another controller augmentation yielding a fixed Markov PDMP) could be handled by the generic framework, but its occupation-measure/moment computation is a different route and does not supply the explicit rational \(e^{-cV}\) constants, finite-volume factorial correction, directed heterogeneous-volume face margins, or restart cost (A_V\mathbb E[N_T]\).

The paper's abstract p-safety model does not acquire reaction rates, stoichiometry, transport coefficients, uncertainty sets, or a controller. Those remain supplied inputs in any CTMC/PDMP embedding.

## 6. Residual issue / falsifier

No theorem-breaking issue is identified under the stated immediate-exit and predictable old-mode semantics. Clarify in any downstream interface that: (i) mode/rate values at a molecular jump are the predictable pre-jump values; (ii) after a switch at that jump, only later hazards use the new mode; (iii) simultaneous distinct jump marks are excluded (standard CTMC convention) or their aggregate jump size/activity is separately bounded; and (iv) the counted switch segments on the safe prefix start in the claimed target cores. A direct falsifier is a simulated or constructed path in which a jump is generated under the new mode at the same time the switch is decided, or a post-jump out-of-box state is allowed to switch before being counted; either violates the interface rather than the algebra.
