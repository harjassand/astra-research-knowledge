# Proof: safe dither and the state-regressor Gramian

## Interface

Consider the fully observed stochastic linear system

\[
x_{t+1}=Ax_t+Bu_t+w_t,\qquad
u_t=K_0x_t+e_t,
\]

where \(w_t\sim\mathcal N(0,W)\) and \(e_t\sim\mathcal N(0,U)\) are mutually independent and independent across time. Assume \(F=A+BK_0\) is Schur stable and \(x_0=0\). The baseline controller and \(W\) are known in the chance-constrained formulation; \(A,B\) are the unknown plant matrices. Write \(z_t=(x_t,u_t)\).

## Covariance identity

The dynamics reduce to

\[
x_{t+1}=Fx_t+Be_t+w_t.
\]

Since the two innovations have zero mean, are independent of \(x_t\), and have covariance \(U,W\), respectively,

\[
P_{t+1}=FP_tF^T+BUB^T+W,\qquad P_0=0.
\]

Iteration gives

\[
P_t=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}F^k(BUB^T+W)(F^k)^T.
\]

Schur stability yields the convergent limit

\[
P_\infty=\sum_{k=0}^\infty F^k(BUB^T+W)(F^k)^T,
\]

and

\[
P_\infty-P_t=F^tP_\infty(F^t)^T\succeq0.
\]

Let \(C=\begin{bmatrix}I\\K_0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(D_U=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&U\end{bmatrix}\). Because current \(e_t\) is independent of \(x_t\),

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(z_t)=CP_tC^T+D_U,
\qquad
\Gamma_\infty=CP_\infty C^T+D_U.
\]

For a vector \(q=(a,b)\) of state/input feature coefficients,

\[
q^T\Gamma_\infty q
=(a+K_0^Tb)^TP_\infty(a+K_0^Tb)+b^TUb.
\]

If \(P_\infty\succ0\) and \(U\succ0\), the right side can vanish only for \(b=0,a=0\), so \(\Gamma_\infty\succ0\). Conversely, if \(P_\infty\) is singular, take nonzero \(a\in\ker P_\infty\), \(b=0\). If \(U\) is singular, take nonzero \(b\in\ker U\) and \(a=-K_0^Tb\). Either choice gives a nonzero null vector of \(\Gamma_\infty\). Therefore

\[
\Gamma_\infty\succ0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_\infty\succ0\text{ and }U\succ0.
\]

## Reachability criterion

For \(U\succ0\), the state covariance is the controllability Gramian of \((F,G)\), where \(GG^T=W+BUB^T\). A finite-dimensional controllability Gramian is positive definite exactly when the reachable span

\[
\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{range}(G),\operatorname{range}(FG),\ldots,
\operatorname{range}(F^{n-1}G)\}
\]

is all of \(\mathbb R^n\). Since \(\operatorname{range}(G)=\operatorname{range}([W^{1/2},BU^{1/2}])\), this is equivalent to controllability of \((F,[W^{1/2},BU^{1/2}])\). This proves the exact rank criterion.

The excitation margin relevant to finite-sample least squares is not just \(\lambda_{\min}(W)\). It is \(\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma_\infty)\), which also depends on the controllability geometry, the injected covariance \(U\), and the feedback coordinate map \(K_0\). This identity alone gives no concentration rate for the adaptive finite-sample Gram matrix.

## Block-horizon covariance contraction

The full-rank-noise assumption in the 2026 safe-LQR proof also supplies one-step contraction of covariance tracking. A finite-horizon Gramian gives a direct static replacement and suggests the corresponding time-varying condition.

For a fixed controller, let \(P\succ0\) solve the exact Lyapunov equation

\[
P=FPF^T+Q,\qquad Q\succeq0,
\]

and assume \(P\preceq\nu I\). For any block length \(L\), telescoping yields

\[
P-F^LP(F^T)^L=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}F^kQ(F^T)^k.
\]

If this \(L\)-step Gramian is at least \(\mu I\), then

\[
F^LP(F^T)^L\preceq P-\mu I
\preceq(1-\mu/\nu)P.
\]

Thus the covariance error contracts by a factor \(1-\mu/\nu\) every \(L\) steps, even when \(Q\) is singular at each individual step. For the controlled system, \(Q=W+BUB^T\), and the Gramian is positive definite exactly under finite-horizon joint reachability.

For a time-varying sequence, define \(\Phi_{t+L:s+1}=F_{t+L-1}\cdots F_{s+1}\), with the empty product equal to identity, and

\[
G_{t,L}=\sum_{s=t}^{t+L-1}\Phi_{t+L:s+1}Q_s\Phi_{t+L:s+1}^T.
\]

Suppose target covariances satisfy the approximate recursion

\[
P_{s+1}=F_sP_sF_s^T+Q_s+D_s,
\]

with \(0\preceq P_s\preceq\nu I\), \(\|P_{s+1}-P_s\|\le\zeta\), and \(\|D_s\|\le\varepsilon_s\). Iterating and rearranging gives

\[
\Phi_{t+L:t}P_t\Phi_{t+L:t}^T
=P_{t+L}-G_{t,L}-\sum_{s=t}^{t+L-1}
\Phi_{t+L:s+1}D_s\Phi_{t+L:s+1}^T.
\]

If \(G_{t,L}\succeq\mu I\), then

\[
\Phi P_t\Phi^T\preceq P_t-r_{t,L}I
\preceq (1-r_{t,L}/\nu)P_t,
\]

where

\[
r_{t,L}=\mu-L\zeta-
\sum_{s=t}^{t+L-1}\|\Phi_{t+L:s+1}\|^2\varepsilon_s.
\]

This is useful only when \(r_{t,L}>0\) uniformly. It is an exact algebraic lemma under the displayed recursion, but the full adaptive-LQR proof has not been rewritten to establish such a uniform block Gramian from its estimated, safety-scaled covariances. In particular, the lower bound must survive model uncertainty and policy changes; the existing proof's one-step lower covariance bound does not automatically imply it.

## Chance-constraint budget

For a scalar \(\alpha^Tz_t\), zero-mean Gaussianity gives

\[
\Pr(\alpha^Tz_t\le\beta)=
\Phi\!\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^T\operatorname{Cov}(z_t)\alpha}}\right)
\]

when \(\beta>0\). Therefore the chance constraint at level \(1-\delta\), \(0<\delta<1/2\), is equivalent to

\[
\alpha^T\operatorname{Cov}(z_t)\alpha
\le\beta^2/\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)^2.
\]

Set \(U=\eta I\), and define the dither-induced state Gramian

\[
H=\sum_{k\ge0}F^kBB^T(F^k)^T.
\]

Then

\[
\Gamma_\infty(\eta)=\Gamma_\infty(0)+\eta
\left(CHC^T+\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&I\end{bmatrix}\right).
\]

For each constraint, write its baseline slack as

\[
m_j=\beta^2/\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)^2-
\alpha_j^T\Gamma_\infty(0)\alpha_j.
\]

Strict feasibility means \(m_j>0\). If

\[
M_j=\alpha_j^T\left(CHC^T+\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&I\end{bmatrix}\right)\alpha_j,
\]

then \(M_j\ge0\) and the dithered covariance satisfies the constraint whenever \(\eta M_j\le m_j\). This gives the stated common bound \(\eta\le\min_{j:M_j>0}m_j/M_j\); if \(M_j=0\), that constraint places no restriction on \(\eta\).

For \(x_0=0\), \(P_t\preceq P_\infty\), and hence \(\operatorname{Cov}(z_t)\preceq\Gamma_\infty\). The scalar Gaussian variance under each linear functional is therefore no larger at finite times than its stationary value. The stationary dither budget guarantees every-time chance feasibility.

## Rank-deficient-noise fixture

Take \(A=0\), \(K_0=0\), \(B=(0,1)^T\), \(W=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)\), and scalar \(U=\eta>0\). Then \(F=0\), so

\[
P_\infty=W+BUB^T=\operatorname{diag}(1,\eta).
\]

The current action noise is independent of the state, yielding

\[
\Gamma_\infty=\operatorname{diag}(1,\eta,\eta)
\]

for \(z=(x_1,x_2,u)\). The process covariance has rank one, while the full feature Gramian is positive definite. With separate two-sided state and input constraints \(|x_i|\le c\), \(|u|\le c\), each encoded as two one-sided Gaussian constraints, the baseline has variances \((1,0,0)\). If \(c>\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)\), it has positive chance slack on every coordinate. Taking \(0<\eta\le c^2/\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)^2\) preserves the chance constraints; if a stricter action/state cap is present, reduce \(\eta\) to the smallest coordinate slack. This is an exact algebraic fixture, not an experiment.

## Failure boundary and falsifier

If \(U=0\) and \(W\) is rank deficient, the state Gramian may be singular; the full \([A\ B]\) matrix is then not identifiable from the stationary regressor distribution. If both disturbance and safe input reachability miss a state subspace, a global model-recovery claim is false without more initial-condition coverage or prior structure. For a single trajectory, such unreachable directions might be irrelevant to the realized objective; for a global all-state certificate, they are not.

An exact hidden-mode example is \(B=e_1\), \(W=e_1e_1^T\), and \(A_\theta=\operatorname{diag}(0,\theta)\) with \(\theta\in\{-1/2,+1/2\}\). Start at \(x_0=0\), and apply any controls through the first input coordinate. The second coordinate remains zero, so the full observed trajectory has the same law for both plants. Yet for the global safe set \(\mathcal S=\{x:x_2\ge0\}\), the plant with \(\theta=+1/2\) preserves nonnegative second coordinates while the plant with \(\theta=-1/2\) maps every strictly positive second coordinate to a negative one in one step. No learner restricted to this trajectory can certify the same global invariant claim for both plants. The joint controllability condition fails exactly in the hidden coordinate.

The lemma is falsified if a finite-dimensional instance violates any exact identity above: the Lyapunov covariance recursion, the equivalence \(\Gamma_\infty\succ0\iff(P_\infty\succ0,U\succ0)\), the controllability criterion, or the chance-constraint threshold. The theorem-level extension has a different falsifier: exhibit a plant satisfying the stated safe baseline, slack, and joint controllability lower bound where the modified initialization fails to obtain the claimed confidence set or the adaptive controller breaks the per-time safety/regret guarantee.

## Literature status

The algebraic covariance and controllability claims are classical. The application target is a conditional weakening of the full-rank disturbance assumption in Hutchinson, Jiang, and Alizadeh (2026), with a safe-dither constraint. This note has not established that the resulting adaptive theorem is new or that prior work lacks the same reduction. It does not assert theorem priority.
