## Coordinate changes and top reduction

For constant T invertible,

`grad(f o T)(x)=T^T grad(f)(Tx)` and
`Hess(f o T)(x)=T^T Hess(f)(Tx) T`.

Thus determinant constancy and gradient invertibility are preserved; source
translations have the analogous property. This does not extend to arbitrary
nonlinear source changes.

Write f=f4+f3+f2+f1+f0. Hess(f4) has homogeneous entries of degree 2; the
degree 8 component of det Hess(f) is det Hess(f4), hence zero. Homogeneous Hesse
in four variables provides a constant coordinate direction omitted by f4.
Apply homogeneous Hesse again if its ternary representative has zero ternary
Hessian. If the resulting binary Hessian vanishes, the binary form is a fourth
power: writing h=Y^d p(X/Y), vanishing reduces to
`d p p''-(d-1)(p')^2=0`; with q=p'/p this is `d q'+q^2=0`, giving a power of
one affine linear polynomial. This independently recovers the three exhaustive
essential-variable cases: ternary, binary, unary. f4!=0 because degree(f)=4.

The degree-at-most-four scope also includes the lower degrees. For degree3,
the top determinant component is det Hess(f3)=0. Homogeneous Hesse in four
variables makes f3 independent of a constant direction u; the remaining
quadratic terms then give the common form with degree(Q)<=1. The same
uniform proposition applies. For degree at most2, the nonzero constant
Hessian is already an invertible constant matrix, so the gradient is an
affine automorphism. Thus no lower-degree branch is omitted.

## Ternary branch

Let f4=phi(x1,x2,x3), A=Hess3(phi), det A!=0. In four-variable block form
Hess(f4)=diag(A,0). Its adjugate is zero except the (4,4) entry det A.
The degree 7 determinant component contains three degree 2 entries and one
degree 1 entry, so it is

`det A * (f3)44`.

The polynomial ring is a domain: `(f3)44=0`. Therefore f3=g(x)+u h(x), h
quadratic, and the remaining parts give f=P(x)+uQ(x)+a u², degree(Q)<=2.

The degree 6 component is

`2a det A - grad(h)^T adj(A) grad(h)`.

Both summands are fixed and may cancel. No separation or inference a=0 is
valid or needed. This equation is retained but not used to add an unwarranted
restriction in the inversion proposition.

## Binary branch

Let f4=phi(x1,x2) with nonsingular polynomial binary Hessian A. A degree 6
term in the four-dimensional determinant uses two degree 2 entries and two
degree 1 entries. Three top entries are impossible because the top is
supported in two rows/columns. Thus this homogeneous component is

`det A * det Hess_(x3,x4)(f3)`.

It vanishes, so the second determinant is zero. Write its symmetric matrix
as [[alpha,beta],[beta,delta]], with all three entries homogeneous linear
forms in all four variables. If alpha!=0, primality of alpha and
alpha delta=beta² give beta=mu alpha, delta=mu² alpha with mu constant.
If alpha=0, beta=0, while delta may be arbitrary. Consequently the matrix is
lambda rr^T for a constant r, including the zero case. A nonzero constant
w perpendicular to r satisfies D_w² f3=0. Making w the fourth coordinate
direction preserves the top form and produces the same common structure.

This needs a constant quadratic null direction, not a constant kernel of the
entire four-variable Hessian; those are different conditions.

## Unary branch and all degeneracies of Lemma 5.2

Let f4=c x1^4, c!=0, and y=(x2,x3,x4). The only degree 5 determinant
contribution is `12c x1² det Hess_y(f3)`, so the latter determinant is zero.

For a homogeneous cubic F write

`F=g3(y)+x1 q2(y)+x1² ell1(y)+gamma x1³`,
`Hess_y(F)=A(y)+x1 M`, M constant symmetric.

At x1=0, det A=0. Homogeneous Hesse reduces g3 to at most two essential
y-variables. Its essential-variable cases are exhaustive:

* Two: A=diag(A2,0), det A2!=0 by the binary power characterization. With
  M=[[B,m],[m^T,mu]], the coefficient of t in det(A+tM) is mu det A2.
  Hence mu=0 and D_e3² F=0. Mixed entries m need not vanish.
* One: A=diag(6k y1,0,0), k!=0. If N is the trailing2×2 block of M,
  `det(A+tM)=6k y1 t² det N + t³ det M`. Therefore det N=0. Any nonzero
  v in ker N, supported in coordinates2,3, obeys v^TAv=v^TMv=0.
* Zero: A=0 and det M=0. A nonzero v in ker M works.

The x1² ell1 term contributes no y-Hessian. In each case a constant
coordinate change in y gives D_u² f3=0 while f4 remains independent of u.
No degenerate case is missing.

## Uniform inversion: a!=0

The Hessian is [[B,v],[v^T,2a]],
`B=Hess(P)+u Hess(Q)`, `v=grad Q`. This remains valid for arbitrary-degree Q.
Let

`z=u+Q/(2a)`, `S_z=P-Q²/(4a)+zQ`.

The gradient outputs are `Y4=2az`, `Yx=grad_x S_z`. Differentiating at fixed
z and only then substituting z=u+Q/(2a) gives

`Hess_x S_z=B-vv^T/(2a)`.

The Schur determinant identity supplies `det Hess_x S_z=c/(2a)`. Over
C(z), de Bondt Theorem 2.1 gives a polynomial inverse in the three x-variables.
The formal-inverse descent below puts its coefficients in C[z]. Recover
z=Y4/(2a), x=H(z,Yx), u=z-Q(x)/(2a). This is a two-sided polynomial inverse.

## Uniform inversion: a=0 and degree(Q)<=2

Let M=Hess(Q), ell=grad Q(0), v=Mx+ell. The determinant is

`c=-v^T adj(Hess P+uM) v`.

The coefficient of u² is `-v^T adj(M) v`, hence zero. Rank analysis uses
congruence over C and translations; arbitrary P remains arbitrary.

A useful simplification of the author's exclusions is that **Q cannot have
a critical point**: at any point where grad Q=0 the last Hessian row and
column vanish, contradicting c!=0.

* Rank3 M is therefore immediately impossible, since Mx+ell has a zero.
* Rank2: normalize M=diag(1,1,0). The u² coefficient is -ell3², so ell3=0.
  Then v has a zero after translating the first two variables, impossible.
  This directly excludes rank 2 without using the author's additional
  t33=0/square argument. That argument was also checked symbolically and
  has no defect.
* Rank1: normalize Q=x²/2+alpha y, removing a constant and the image part
  of ell. If alpha=0, Q has a critical point, impossible. If alpha!=0,
  the coefficient of u in the determinant is `-alpha² Pzz`, so P=R(x,y)
  +z S(x,y). The constant determinant is
  `(x Sy-alpha Sx)²=c`. Since the ring is a domain, DS=kappa for constant
  kappa!=0, kappa²=c, D=x d/dy-alpha d/dx. Polynomial coordinates
  `q=x²/2+alpha y`, `s=-x/alpha` give Dq=0, Ds=1. Thus S=kappa s+F(q).
  Outputs Y4=q and Y3=S recover x,y. The two remaining outputs satisfy
  `[Y1-Rx,Y2-Ry]^T=[[Sx,x],[Sy,alpha]][z,u]^T`; determinant -kappa is a
  unit. Hence z,u are polynomially recovered. R and F are arbitrary.
* Rank0: Q affine. Its linear part must be nonzero, so normalize
  Q=lambda x3+q0, lambda!=0. Then
  `det Hess_(x1,x2) P=-c/lambda²`. Apply HC2 over C(x3) and the descent
  lemma to obtain a polynomial inverse over C[x3]. Recover x3 from Y4,
  x1,x2 from Y1,Y2, and u from Y3. All possibilities are covered.

## Polynomial coefficient descent, independently reconstructed

More generally, let R be a domain, K its fraction field, F in R[x]^n,
and det JF a unit of R. Suppose F has a polynomial inverse over K. Subtract
F(0) and normalize by JF(0); its inverse matrix has entries in R because its
determinant is a unit. The normalized map is x+H(x), order(H)>=2.

The homogeneous-degree recursion for the unique formal inverse x+sum G_d
uses additions, multiplications and substitution, with no division by
nonunits. Thus every G_d lies in R[y]. The K-polynomial inverse equals this
formal inverse and has only finitely many nonzero G_d, so descends to R[y].
Undo the affine normalization. This proves the exact lemma needed over
C[z] and C[x3]. The unit assumption is essential; F=t x over C[t] has a
K-inverse y/t but no inverse over C[t].

Similarly an arbitrary characteristic-zero field coefficient problem can be
restricted to a finitely generated subfield k/Q and embedded in C. The same
unique formal recursion shows that a polynomial inverse supplied over C has
coefficients in k. Thus Ni's transfer argument is correct. No uniform bound
on inverse degree across specializations is assumed; polynomiality over the
single fraction field is enough for coefficient descent.
