Cycle 2 near-full-rank audit: capacity, Green functions, and kernels

Scope. Primary-source audit of whether a rank-deficiency constraint on a matrix Casimir energy is covered by a capacity or Green-function theorem. The conclusion is deliberately narrow: there is an exact noncommutative-capacity identity for projection witnesses and an elementary sharp Poincare bound for arbitrary matrices. The sources checked do not give a variational profile for arbitrary Hilbert-Schmidt matrices constrained by their kernel. This is a bounded search, not a priority certification.

1. Interface and exact near-full-rank bound

Let H be a complex Hilbert space of dimension d. Let L >= 0 be self-adjoint on HS(H), with ker L = C I, and write E(X)=<X,LX>_HS. Let lambda_1>0 be the smallest eigenvalue of L on the traceless subspace. For ||X||_HS=1 and rank(X) <= d-k, k>=1,

  E(X) >= lambda_1 ||X - (Tr X/d) I||_HS^2
       = lambda_1 (1 - |Tr X|^2/d)
       >= lambda_1 k/d.                                      (1)

The last step is |Tr X| <= ||X||_1 <= sqrt(rank X)||X||_HS <= sqrt(d-k). This argument applies to non-Hermitian X as well. It is a derived Poincare/rank inequality, not a theorem attributed to one of the sources below. For a conjugation Casimir of an irreducible compact-group representation, Schur's lemma gives ker L=C I, so (1) applies with lambda_1 the first nonzero Casimir eigenvalue.

The packet's integrated rank-spectral bound reduces to exactly lambda_1 k/d when k<=d/2. Indeed, if D(t)=Tr 1_[0,t](L), then D(t)=1 for 0<=t<lambda_1 and D(t)>=2 for t>=lambda_1. For Q=d-k>=d/2, [1-QD(t)/d]_+ vanishes for t>=lambda_1, leaving integral_0^infty [1-QD(t)/d]_+ dt=lambda_1 k/d. Thus the existing rank-spectral theorem supplies the gap-only bound in the near-full-rank regime; it does not yield a stronger multi-band estimate there.

This universal dependence on lambda_1,d,k is sharp over positive invariant operators: take L=lambda_1(I-P_scalar), where P_scalar is the HS projection onto C I, and X=(I-P_K)/sqrt(d-k) for any k-dimensional K. Then E(X)=lambda_1 k/d. This L is conjugation-invariant, though generally not the quadratic Casimir of a specified representation. There is also equality in the smallest genuine Casimir example: for the fundamental SU(2) representation, d=2, the conjugation Casimir is lambda_1 on traceless matrices, and a normalized rank-one projection has energy lambda_1/2. Consequently no stronger bound follows from only the spectral gap, dimension, and rank constraint; fixed higher representations may have additional structure, which is not resolved by this observation.

For a prescribed kernel K, there is an exact finite-dimensional Green-operator reformulation. Let P_K be its orthogonal projection and R_K(X)=X(I-P_K), the HS-orthogonal projection onto M_K={X:XP_K=0}. Define

  L_K = (R_K L R_K)|_{M_K}.

If k=dim K>=1 and ker L=C I, then L_K is positive definite: a zero-energy vector in M_K would be scalar and also annihilate K, hence zero. Therefore G_K=L_K^{-1} exists and

  inf_{XP_K=0, ||X||_HS=1} E(X) = lambda_min(L_K) = 1/||G_K||_op.   (2)

The at-most-(d-k)-rank infimum is inf_{dim K=k} lambda_min(L_K), since every such X has a k-dimensional subspace in its kernel. Equation (2) is an exact compressed-Green characterization, but by itself it is only a reformulation. A useful theorem would need uniform control of ||G_K|| over all K, or a tractable characterization of its worst subspace. No such general bound was found in the sources checked.

2. Closest noncommutative capacity theorem: exact for projections

Voiculescu, “The condenser quasicentral modulus,” Quantum Topology 15 (2024), 419–448, DOI 10.4171/QT/208, defines a noncommutative condenser quantity for orthogonal projections P,Q in a semifinite von Neumann algebra and a tuple T=(T_j):

  k_J(T;P,Q) = inf { max_j ||[T_j,A]||_J :
                      0<=A<=I, AP=P, AQ=0 },

with the paper's finite-trace/ideal hypotheses. The paper also gives a combined-commutator variant, replacing the maximum by ||(sum_j [T_j,A]^*[T_j,A])^(1/2)||_J. This is explicitly motivated as a noncommutative analogue of nonlinear condenser capacity; it is not the same object as a rank-constrained Rayleigh quotient.

There is an exact overlap for projection witnesses. In finite dimension take M=B(H), J=S_2, let T_j be self-adjoint Lie generators, and set L(X)=sum_j [T_j,[T_j,X]], so E(X)=sum_j ||[T_j,X]||_2^2. Given any k-dimensional kernel K, set P=I-P_K and Q=P_K. They are complementary, and AP=P, AQ=0 force A=P. Hence the combined-commutator variant satisfies

  (combined condenser modulus for T;P,Q)^2
       = sum_j ||[T_j,P]||_2^2 = E(P).                       (3)

Thus the noncommutative condenser construction evaluates the Casimir energy of I-P_K for every orientation of K, and X=P/sqrt(d-k) gives the exact rank-(d-k) upper witness E(P)/(d-k). This is the closest direct theorem found for arbitrary kernel subspaces. But it only covers this projection witness: its admissible variable A is positive and fixed to P in the complementary-boundary case. It does not optimize over all normalized X with XP_K=0, so it does not establish the infimum in (2), much less its optimization over K. For non-complementary P,Q, A may interpolate on the remaining subspace, but that remains a positive-contraction commutator problem, not the arbitrary-matrix quadratic problem.

On coherent-state orbits, P_g=pi(g)P_pi(psi)pi(g)^* has E(P_g)=E(P_pi(psi)) because the Casimir is conjugation-invariant. Consequently (3) gives a constant condenser value along the orbit, and (I-P_g)/sqrt(d-1) is an exact rank-(d-1) trial family. This is an upper construction only. A capacity of the orbit as a subset of projective space would use functions on that space and a different Dirichlet form; no theorem found identifies that capacity with the matrix Casimir infimum for arbitrary kernel lines, and coherent-state lines need not exhaust all lines in H.

3. Exact scalar graph results, and why the transfer is only an analogy

For a finite connected weighted graph (V,c), the scalar Dirichlet form is

  E_G(f)=1/2 sum_{x,y in V} c(x,y)|f(x)-f(y)|^2.

With nonempty Dirichlet boundary and prescribed values there, the killed graph Laplacian is positive definite on functions vanishing at the boundary; its inverse is the Dirichlet Green matrix. In the condenser case, the minimum of E_G over f=1 on the source and f=0 on the sink is the effective conductance/capacity, and the minimizer is harmonic off the boundary. Bendito–Carmona–Encinas–Gesto, “Potential theory for boundary value problems on finite networks,” Applicable Analysis and Discrete Mathematics 1 (2007), 299–310, Proposition 3.3 gives the variational Dirichlet principle for their self-adjoint network boundary-value problems under the stated positive-semidefinite-energy condition; Section 4 gives the condenser/effective-conductance formulation. These are exact theorems for scalar functions with vertex boundary conditions.

The matrix constraint XP_K=0 is a linear-subspace boundary condition on a matrix-valued unknown, not generally a set of scalar coordinate vertices. It becomes a standard graph Dirichlet problem only under additional structure: a chosen basis must make the admissible coordinates a pinned vertex set and L's quadratic form must have the graph-Laplacian form (or a separately justified block/operator-valued analogue). A general compact-group Casimir need not have a scalar graph-Laplacian matrix in the matrix-unit basis, and arbitrary K rotates the constrained subspace. The finite-network condenser theorem therefore supplies the variational analogy and a possible model after extra hypotheses, not a theorem for arbitrary matrix kernels. Its Green inverse is the inverse of a scalar killed Laplacian, whereas (2) is a compressed operator on HS(H).

4. A matrix-stratum capacity theorem with a different object of study

Larsson, “Matrix-valued Bessel processes,” Electronic Journal of Probability 20 (2015), no. 60, 1–29, arXiv:1212.4986v4, studies real d-by-d matrices as points of an ambient state space with determinant-weighted measure m(dx)=|det x|^(delta-1) dx, delta>0. His capacity is a Sobolev capacity of subsets A of that state space:

  Cap(A)=inf int (|f|^2+||Df||^2) dm,

over W^{1,2} test functions f>=1 on a neighborhood of A. Theorem 5.1 says Cap(M_k)=0 for the stratum M_k={x:rank x=k}, k=0,...,d-2, for every delta>0; it also gives Cap(M_{d-1})=0 when delta>=2. This is an exact rank-stratum capacity result, including all kernel orientations, but the points are matrices x and the variables in the energy are scalar functions f(x) on matrix space. It concerns polar sets and Sobolev density for the determinant-weighted diffusion. It does not bound <X,LX> for one fixed X, does not use a compact-group Casimir, and does not imply low energy for a rank-deficient X. Treating this as a rank-constrained Casimir theorem would conflate the state-space variable with the function being tested.

5. Conclusions and novelty boundary

Exact results located:

* Arbitrary-matrix rank deficiency plus a scalar kernel gives the sharp universal gap inequality (1), derived here. In the packet's spectral-profile theorem, this is all the lower profile detects when rank Q>=d/2.
* The Voiculescu combined condenser functional exactly equals Casimir energy on the complement projection for every kernel subspace K, equation (3). It provides a legitimate noncommutative capacity connection for projection witnesses.
* Finite weighted-graph capacity/Green theorems give exact minimization and inverse-Laplacian formulas for scalar vertex boundary data.
* Larsson proves zero Sobolev capacity of low-rank strata in determinant-weighted matrix space, under the parameter ranges stated above.

No primary source checked gives a capacity/Green bound for inf_{rank X<=d-k, ||X||_HS=1}<X,LX> over arbitrary, possibly non-Hermitian matrices in an irreducible compact-group carrier, uniformly over arbitrary kernel orientation. The projection case is covered in the specific sense of (3), and the gap-only lower bound is elementary; a sharper general near-full-rank theorem remains open in this bounded audit. Novelty/priority for that narrower arbitrary-matrix interface is UNKNOWN, not established by non-detection. Avoid claiming a new capacity principle without stating the projection restriction and the distinction between the two energy spaces.
