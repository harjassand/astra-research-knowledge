Let Omega be a bounded connected Lipschitz domain in R^2. Let g_1,g_2 be
fixed affine functions. Put X=W_0^(1,4)(Omega), with norm ||h||_X=||grad h||_4.
Poincare and the planar Sobolev extension/Morrey theorem give
||h||_(W1,4)+||h||_infinity<=C||h||_X. A bounded weakly convergent sequence
has a uniformly convergent subsequence. All integrals are ordinary Lebesgue
integrals; averaging instead changes only fixed normalizations.

Let F:R^(2x2)->R be a real homogeneous quartic polynomial, rank-one convex,
with

  mu_1=min_{|xi|=1} F(e_1 tensor xi)>0.

Matrices below are written by OUTPUT ROWS. Fix v in g_2+X, and define

  A[a]=[grad a;grad v],
  I_0(a)=integral F(A[a]),                  a in g_1+X.

Let a_* be its unique scalar minimizer. For a prescribed mean
m=integral a_ref, where a_ref in g_1+X, use a_*^m and I_*^m for the unique

  q_epsilon=epsilon v/(1+epsilon g_1),

The admissible amplitude condition is epsilon^(-1)+a>=0. Boundary values

2. SCALAR FOURTH-CONVEXITY AND RESIDUAL CERTIFICATES

For c=mu_1/3, rank-one convexity and the globally convex quartic line
restriction give

  I_0(a)>=I_0(b)+DI_0(b)[a-b]+c||a-b||_X^4.                (2.1)

For completeness, if f(t)=F(C+tQ)=At^4+Lt^3+C_2t^2+Dt+E and Q is rank
one, global convexity of f implies C_2>=3L^2/(8A) when A>0. Completing
the square gives f(1)-f(0)-f'(0)>=A/3; when A=0 convexity forces L=0
and C_2>=0. Use Q=e_1 tensor (grad a-grad b) and integrate. This proves
(2.1) with the stated constant without uniform quadratic ellipticity.

Summing (2.1) in the two directions gives fourth monotonicity:

  (DI_0(a)-DI_0(b))[a-b]>=2c||a-b||_X^4.                  (2.2)

Quartic growth and Young imply scalar coercivity
I_0(a)>=c_0||a-g_1||_X^4-C(v,g_1). Convex weak lower semicontinuity gives
attainment. The scalar Euler equation and (2.1) give uniqueness and the
source's global energy remainder. On the mean constraint the Euler equation
is

  DI_0(a_*^m)[eta]=lambda_* integral eta,                  (2.3)

for a unique real mean multiplier lambda_*; this is not the unscaled
fourth-mass multiplier from the phase variables. Choose any fixed
zeta in C-infinity_c with integral zeta=1 to evaluate lambda_*.

For a feasible trial a in the relevant affine scalar class, let d be the
dual norm of its Euler residual on the feasible tangent space T, where
T=X without mean constraint and T={eta in X: integral eta=0} with one:

  d=sup_{eta in T, ||eta||_X<=1}|DI_0(a)[eta]|.

Then

  ||a-a_*||_X <= (3d/(2mu_1))^(1/3),
  0<=I_0(a)-I_0(a_*)
       <=(3/4)(3/(4mu_1))^(1/3)d^(4/3).                  (2.4)

The first bound follows from (2.2) and stationarity of a_* on T. The
second maximizes d t-c t^4 over t>=0 after (2.1). These are continuum
residual bounds; d is not a sampled pointwise norm.

Exact flux characterization. Put S_1=F_1(A[a]) in L^(4/3), and define

  N_0={sigma in L^(4/3)(Omega;R^2): div sigma=0 in distributions},
  N_m={sigma in L^(4/3): -div sigma is a spatial constant}.

Then, in the corresponding tangent class,

  d=inf_{sigma in N_0 or N_m} ||S_1-sigma||_(4/3).         (2.5)

Proof. The gradient image of T is a closed subspace of L4 by Poincare.
Its annihilator in L^(4/3) is N_0, or N_m for zero-mean tests. In the
latter case, a distribution vanishing on all mean-zero compact tests is
a constant: subtract its action on zeta times the integral of a test.
The norm of the restricted linear functional is the distance to this
annihilator, by the Hahn-Banach extension theorem. Both annihilators are
closed. Reflexivity gives attainment of the distance; strict convexity
of L^(4/3) gives a unique closest flux. In particular ANY supplied exactly
equilibrated sigma yields a rigorous upper bound on d and hence (2.4).
Constructing or integrating such a flux remains an acquisition cost; no
efficient universal optimizer is asserted.

3. UNIFORM C1 TRANSLATION EXPANSION

For a in a bounded subset of g_1+X define

  B_1[a]=[0;grad((a-g_1)v)],
  B_2[a]=[0;-grad(g_1(a-g_1)v)],
  J_1(a)=integral DF(A[a]):B_1[a]
        =integral F_2(A[a]).grad((a-g_1)v).                 (3.1)

For every fixed bound B on ||a-g_1||_X and ||v||_(W1,4), there are
epsilon_B>0,C_B<infinity such that, uniformly on this set,

  ||A_epsilon[a]-A[a]-epsilon B_1[a]||_4 <= C_B epsilon^2,
  |I_epsilon(a)-I_0(a)-epsilon J_1(a)| <= C_B epsilon^2,
  ||DI_epsilon(a)-DI_0(a)-epsilon DJ_1(a)||_(X*)
                                                   <= C_B epsilon^2. (3.2)

In particular ||DI_epsilon-DI_0||_(X*)<=C_B epsilon.

Proof. The exact multiplier epsilon v/(1+epsilon g_1) has the expansion
epsilon v-epsilon^2g_1v+O(epsilon^3) in W^(1,4) and in L-infinity, uniformly
for bounded v. Products with h or an X variation are bounded by planar
Sobolev multiplication. Thus

  A_epsilon[a]=A[a]+epsilon B_1[a]+epsilon^2 B_2[a]+O(epsilon^3)

in L4, with the same expansion for its affine derivative in a. A quartic
polynomial defines a C-infinity map from L4 to L1. Its Taylor expansion
and Holder give (3.2), including the X* derivative estimate. For example
|DF(A+E)-DF(A)|<=C(|A|+|E|)^2|E| and
|D^2F(A)|<=C|A|^2 give the derivative bound directly. J_1 and DJ_1 are
bounded and Lipschitz on each such bounded set. No minimizer regularity
or differentiation of a minimizing branch is used here.

4. NEW SHARP UPPER RATE AND FIRST-ORDER STRESS DEVELOPMENT, NO MASS

Theorem 4.1. The supplied unconstrained translation minimum is attained
for all sufficiently small epsilon. Every minimizer a_epsilon is positive
in the amplitude chart and satisfies

  ||a_epsilon-a_*||_(W1,4) <= C epsilon^(1/3),
  0<=I_0(a_epsilon)-I_0(a_*) <= C epsilon^(4/3),
  m_epsilon=I_0(a_*)+epsilon J_1(a_*)+O(epsilon^(4/3)).    (4.1)

These estimates are locally uniform for bounded supplied v in W^(1,4).

Proof of attainment and inactive positivity. The source comparison,
also immediate from (3.2) before bounding a, is

  |I_epsilon(a)-I_0(a)|
       <=C epsilon(1+||a-g_1||_X^4).

For small epsilon, scalar coercivity absorbs this perturbation uniformly.
The feasible comparison a=g_1 bounds the minima, so all minimizing sequences
and minimizers are uniformly bounded in X. For fixed epsilon, weakly
convergent sequences have h converging uniformly along a subsequence;
h grad q_epsilon then converges strongly in L4. At frozen h, changing
grad a changes A_epsilon by (1,q_epsilon) tensor delta grad a, a rank-one
matrix. Scalar convexity in the gradient and quartic growth give weak
lower semicontinuity. The boundary and positivity constraint are closed,
so the direct method gives attainment. The uniform L-infinity bound on
a_epsilon makes epsilon^(-1)+a_epsilon>0 for small epsilon. Positivity
is inactive in an X neighborhood, and therefore

  DI_epsilon(a_epsilon)=0 in X*.

By (3.2), the Cartesian residual has norm at most C epsilon. Apply (2.4)
to obtain the first two estimates of (4.1). Finally J_1 is Lipschitz on
the bounded set, so

  J_1(a_epsilon)=J_1(a_*)+O(epsilon^(1/3)).

Insert this and the Cartesian excess estimate into the value expansion
in (3.2). The O(epsilon^2) error is smaller than O(epsilon^(4/3)), proving
the last statement. This improves the supplied R^(-1/4) rate by using
stationarity rather than only a value comparison. QED.

Optional uniqueness: for sufficiently small epsilon, the entire direction
n_epsilon is uniformly close to e_1. Continuity of F on the unit rank-one
set gives F(n_epsilon tensor xi)>=mu_1|xi|^4/2. Two positive global radial
minimizers have equal energy under their density interpolation. The
strict rank-one quartic remainder then makes their logarithmic amplitude
gradients identical, so their ratio is constant. Their common strictly
positive boundary trace fixes the ratio to one. Thus the global minimum
in fact has a unique amplitude; no smoothness of this optimizer follows.

5. NEW EXACT-MASS EXPANSION AND THE MEAN-MULTIPLIER TERM

Keep the supplied fourth mass exactly, using a_ref in g_1+X:

  M_epsilon(a)=integral (epsilon^(-1)+a)^4(1+q_epsilon^2)^2,
  C_epsilon(a)=[M_epsilon(a)-M_epsilon(a_ref)]/(4epsilon^(-3)).

Define

  L(eta)=integral eta,
  H_1(a)=(3/2)integral(a^2-a_ref^2).

Uniformly on bounded X sets, including first derivatives,

  C_epsilon(a)=L(a-a_ref)+epsilon H_1(a)+O(epsilon^2),
  DC_epsilon(a)=L+epsilon DH_1(a)+O(epsilon^2).            (5.1)

The actual pointwise normalized mass is
(1+epsilon a)^4[1+epsilon^2v^2/(1+epsilon g_1)^2]^2.
Its common v^2 term cancels on subtraction; the first nonzero correction
is exactly (3/2)(a^2-a_ref^2). Also, exactly,

  DC_epsilon(a)[eta]
     =integral (1+epsilon a)^3(1+q_epsilon^2)^2 eta.        (5.2)

Theorem 5.1. Let a_*^m minimize I_0 at mean m=L(a_ref), and let lambda_*
be its multiplier as in (2.3). Every exact-mass translation minimizer
a_epsilon^m exists for small epsilon and has

  ||a_epsilon^m-a_*^m||_(W1,4) <=C epsilon^(1/3),
  m_epsilon^mass=I_0(a_*^m)
       +epsilon[J_1(a_*^m)-lambda_* H_1(a_*^m)]
       +O(epsilon^(4/3)).                                 (5.3)

Its normalized mass multiplier lambda_epsilon obeys
lambda_epsilon=lambda_*+O(epsilon^(1/3)). The constants are locally
uniform for bounded v,a_ref. This retains the supplied mass exactly.

Proof. The comparison a_ref is feasible and gives uniform bounds, just
as in Section 4. Uniform convergence makes the mass constraint closed;
the direct method gives attainment and the same eventual inactive
positivity. At a bounded minimizer, DC_epsilon(a)[zeta]=1+O(epsilon), so
the derivative of the constraint is onto R. The Banach Lagrange theorem
gives

  DI_epsilon(a_epsilon^m)=lambda_epsilon DC_epsilon(a_epsilon^m). (5.4)

Testing with zeta bounds lambda_epsilon uniformly. Equations (3.2) and
(5.1) imply

  ||DI_0(a_epsilon^m)-lambda_epsilon L||_(X*)<=C epsilon.  (5.5)

Put delta=a_epsilon^m-a_*^m and t=||delta||_X. The exact constraint gives
|L(delta)|<=C epsilon. Testing (5.4) with zeta and using the local Lipschitz
bound for DI_0 gives

  |lambda_epsilon-lambda_*|<=C(t+epsilon).

The fourth monotonicity (2.2), (2.3), and (5.5) now give

  2c t^4
    <=(lambda_epsilon-lambda_*)L(delta)+C epsilon t
    <=C epsilon t+C epsilon^2.

Young absorbs the epsilon t term, proving t<=C epsilon^(1/3). It also
proves the asserted multiplier convergence. The Cartesian Bregman excess

  G_epsilon=I_0(a_epsilon^m)-I_0(a_*^m)-lambda_*L(delta)

is nonnegative by (2.1). Applying (2.1) in the reverse direction gives
G_epsilon<=(DI_0(a_epsilon^m)-DI_0(a_*^m))[delta]-c t^4,
so G_epsilon<=C epsilon t+C epsilon^2=O(epsilon^(4/3)).

Finally, Lipschitz continuity of H_1 and the exact constraint imply

  L(delta)=-epsilon H_1(a_*^m)+O(epsilon^(4/3)).

Combine this with the Bregman excess, J_1(a_epsilon^m)=J_1(a_*^m)+O(t),
and (3.2). This proves (5.3). No smooth optimizer branch, implicit-
function theorem, or omitted boundary contribution was used. QED.

The factor/sign of the correction depends on the declared normalization.

6. GLOBAL C1 ENVELOPE AND A WEAK STRESS IDENTITY

Theorem 6.1. On the affine Banach space v in g_2+X, the fully scalar-
eliminated value R_F(v)=I_v(a_v) is Frechet C1. Its derivative is

  DR_F(v)[w]=integral F_2([grad a_v;grad v]).grad w.        (6.1)

The optimizer map v->a_v is locally Holder-1/3 in W^(1,4), and DR_F is
locally Holder-1/3 in X*. In particular the Taylor remainder is locally
O(||w||_X^(4/3)). The same result holds at fixed mean m, with a_v^m.

Proof. Scalar coercivity bounds a_v uniformly on bounded v sets. Let
a_v,a_z be the minimizers at v,z. The scalar Euler equation at z and
quartic derivative growth give

  ||DI_v(a_z)||_(X*)<=C||z-v||_X,

on the relevant tangent space. Apply (2.2) against a_z-a_v to obtain
||a_z-a_v||_X<=C||z-v||_X^(1/3). Polynomial derivative growth then yields
the same Holder modulus for F_2([grad a_v;grad v]) in L^(4/3).

For the envelope derivative compare the value at v+w first with the
competitor a_v, and then compare the value at v with a_(v+w). Taylor
expansion in the second row gives the same candidate linear functional
from both inequalities; changing a_(v+w) to a_v in it costs at most
C||w||_X^(4/3). This proves Frechet differentiability and (6.1).
No derivative of a_v is taken. At fixed mean the optimizer difference
lies in the mean-zero tangent and the proof is unchanged. QED.

Consequently the first translation coefficient has the exact stress form

  J_1(a_v)=DR_F(v)[(a_v-g_1)v].                            (6.2)

The variation is in X because a_v-g_1 has zero trace and both factors
are bounded W^(1,4) functions. Equivalently it is the distributional
transverse Euler residual -div F_2(A_v) paired with (a_v-g_1)v.
For a mean constraint replace R_F by its fixed-mean version; the additional
term in (5.3) accounts for drift of that mean.

Without a fixed mean, critical points of this C1 reduced functional are
exactly the weak vectorial Euler critical points whose first row has
been minimized: the first-row equation holds by definition, and (6.1)
is the second-row equation. At fixed first-row mean, reduced criticality
instead gives -div F_1(A_v)=lambda_* and -div F_2(A_v)=0; the first-row
constant multiplier must be retained. This supplies the first envelope
equation at arbitrary weak minima, extending the source's smooth-branch
statement. It does not produce a global minimum or justify a second
derivative.

The source's local positive-carrier implicit-function calculation can
be valid because it explicitly has an invertible linearized elliptic
operator. Its general Schur Hessian is conditional on such a branch.
Fourth-convexity alone does NOT give an invertible quadratic Hessian.
The next examples explicitly have non-C1 optimizer response and non-C2
minimum values in the translated epsilon parameter at its degenerate
endpoint, despite smooth polynomial input functionals. They do not prove
optimality of the Holder exponent for the map v->a_v or for DR_F(v).
Indeed their unperturbed optimizer a_v=cv depends linearly on v.

7. SHARPNESS AND A FRACTIONAL VARIATIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Use Omega=(0,1)^2, g_1=g_2=0, and the smooth nonnegative zero-boundary bump

  v(x,y)=x(1-x)y(1-y).

For any fixed real c define the convex homogeneous quartic

  F_c([xi;eta])=|xi-c eta|^4+|eta|^4.                      (7.1)

It is rank-one convex, and mu_1=1 exactly. It is also coercive in the full
matrix, but is not left-SO(2)-invariant. The Cartesian row minimum is

  a_*=c v,          E_0=integral |grad v|^4.

Here q_epsilon=epsilon v and

  I_epsilon(a)
    =integral |grad a-c grad(v+epsilon va)|^4
               +|grad(v+epsilon va)|^4.                  (7.2)

Define the bounded nonzero linear functional on X

  ell_v(b)=4 integral |grad v|^2 grad v.grad(vb),
  B=ell_v(v)=8 integral v|grad v|^4>0,
  H(b)=integral |grad b|^4+ell_v(b).                      (7.3)

H is coercive and strictly fourth-convex, so it has a unique minimizer
b_*; it is nonzero and min H<0 because ell_v is nonzero and a suitable
small signed multiple of v has negative H. Homogeneity at its critical
point gives ell_v(b_*)=-4||grad b_*||_4^4 and min H=-3||grad b_*||_4^4.

The exact rescaling a=cv+epsilon^(1/3)b gives, uniformly for bounded b,

 [I_epsilon(cv+epsilon^(1/3)b)-E_0-c epsilon B]
                         /epsilon^(4/3) = H(b)+O(epsilon^(2/3)). (7.4)

Indeed its first transformed row is
epsilon^(1/3)grad b-c^2epsilon grad(v^2)-c epsilon^(4/3)grad(vb),
and its second row is
grad v+c epsilon grad(v^2)+epsilon^(4/3)grad(vb).
Expanding their fourth powers proves (7.4). If c=0 the remainder even
improves to O(epsilon^(4/3)), but the displayed common bound suffices.

Theorem 7.1. Let a_epsilon be the exact global translated minimizer for
(7.2). Then

  epsilon^(-1/3)(a_epsilon-cv) -> b_* strongly in W^(1,4),
  m_epsilon=E_0+c epsilon B+epsilon^(4/3)min H
                                    +o(epsilon^(4/3)).    (7.5)

Proof. Section 4 already bounds the scaled minimizers in X. Positivity
is inactive for all bounded scaled competitors. Uniform convergence in
(7.4), weak lower semicontinuity of ||grad b||_4^4, and weak continuity of
ell_v give the liminf bound. Every fixed b is a recovery sequence in the
same chart. Thus every weak optimizer limit minimizes H and equals b_*.
The minimum values converge as well. The fourth-convexity of H upgrades
optimizer convergence to strong X, hence strong W^(1,4). This is also an
explicit weak-W^(1,4) Gamma-development, not merely a formal scaling.
No blanket equicoercivity of arbitrary rescaled sublevels is needed for
this optimizer statement; boundedness of the actual scaled optimizers
was proved independently. QED.

Because b_* is nonzero, ||a_epsilon-cv|| is asymptotic to a nonzero
constant times epsilon^(1/3). Thus the improved minimizer exponent is
sharp in the general class. For c=1, B>0 proves that the unrenormalized
O(epsilon) value order is sharp, and min H<0 proves that the first-order
renormalized O(epsilon^(4/3)) order is sharp. For c=0 the leading value
correction itself is a strictly negative epsilon^(4/3) term. All original
energies remain nonnegative because F_c is convex; no quasiconvexity
counterexample is produced by these value corrections.

8. THE SHARPNESS PERSISTS WITH THE EXACT SUPPLIED FOURTH MASS

Use the same example and choose a_ref=cv. The limiting Cartesian mean
constraint is integral a=c integral v; a_*=cv is its minimizer and its
mean multiplier lambda_*=0. Let

  T={b in X: integral b=0},
  H_T=H restricted to T.

The functional ell_v is nonzero on T. An explicit mean-zero polynomial
test is b_0=v^2-v/25: since integral v=1/36 and integral v^2=1/900, its
integral is zero, and the companion checker finds ell_v(b_0) !=0 exactly.
Therefore H_T has a unique nonzero minimizer b_*^T and min H_T<0.

The exact mass-constrained translated minima satisfy

  epsilon^(-1/3)(a_epsilon^mass-cv)->b_*^T strongly in W^(1,4),
  m_epsilon^mass=E_0+c epsilon B+epsilon^(4/3)min H_T
                                            +o(epsilon^(4/3)). (8.1)

Proof. Section 5 bounds the scaled minimizers. The exact normalized mass
constraint, expanded about cv, forces the mean of the scaled variable
to tend to zero (in fact it is O(epsilon) on bounded sets). This gives
the liminf constraint. For a fixed b in T, C_epsilon(cv+epsilon^(1/3)b)
is O(epsilon^(4/3)), and its derivative along zeta tends to one. Hence
a correction t_epsilon zeta with t_epsilon=O(epsilon^(4/3)) restores the
ACTUAL fourth mass exactly. After dividing by epsilon^(1/3), this
correction tends strongly to zero. On the bounded scaled set, direct
differentiation of (7.2) gives ||DI_epsilon||_(X*)=O(epsilon): the first
transformed row is O(epsilon^(1/3)), and the second-row variation carries
an epsilon factor. The correcting energy cost is therefore
O(epsilon^(7/3)), negligible in (7.4). This proves recovery. To apply the
constrained fourth-convexity remainder to the actual scaled minimizers
b_epsilon, first project them into T by
b_tilde_epsilon=b_epsilon-L(b_epsilon)zeta. Their mean is O(epsilon),
so this projection changes their X norm and H value by o(1). Then
H_T(b_tilde_epsilon)->min H_T, and its fourth-convexity on T gives
b_tilde_epsilon->b_*^T strongly in X. The small projection error yields
the same conclusion for b_epsilon. QED.

Thus neither retaining exact fourth mass nor passing to its mean limit
improves the general 1/3 and 4/3 exponents. The nontrivial limiting force
is the transverse stress, projected onto the correct mean-zero tangent.

9. PRIOR ART, VERIFICATION SCOPE, AND THE PRECISE REMAINING OBLIGATION

Scalar convexity, quotient-norm flux representations, envelope arguments,
and variational asymptotic developments are established machinery. These
proofs are explicit applications and sharper conclusions for the supplied
translation chart, not claims to have invented those general tools.

Primary literature checked selectively:
  Braides and Truskinovsky, Asymptotic expansions by Gamma-convergence,
  Continuum Mechanics and Thermodynamics 20 (2008), 21-62.
  Author-deposited record: https://cvgmt.sns.it/paper/727/
  The paper studies successive variational approximations and singular
  parameter blowups. It supplies historical context, not a black-box
  theorem used to infer (7.5) or (8.1), whose compactness/recovery arguments
  are supplied above. A full priority search for the chart-specific
  coefficients and sharp examples has not been completed.

The bounded exact checker reconstructs polynomial translation derivatives,
the normalized mass coefficients, the quartic response powers and sharp
convex family, and exact polynomial bump integrals. It does not formalize
Sobolev compactness, the Banach multiplier theorem, Hahn-Banach, weak lower
semicontinuity, or any analytic Gamma-development proof.

The global sign comparison R_F(v)>=|Omega|F(B), with the correct traces
and mean contract when present, remains unproved for arbitrary frozen
components. The stress expansion identifies its gradient rather than
removing the coupled sign obstruction. No defeated alternating search,
numerical negative-cycle claim, hardware claim, upload, or repository
mutation was performed. Higher regularity/nondegenerate Hessian assumptions
can yield better local perturbation expansions, but are not consequences
of mu_1>0 and were not silently added. Sharpness inside the narrower
left-SO(2) invariant cone and full quasiconvexity remain open here.
