# A negative macro test for legal exclusive cores of the actual repaired state

## Exact result and retained interface

Fix n>=3 and a positive loss L(r)->infinity. In the prescribed-loss diagonal construction of `notes/r15_geometric_analysis/cycle6/ADMISSIBLE_SLOW_ENVELOPES.md`, choose the step gaps at least 2 in log R. The resulting admissible a has infinitely many constant plateaus, with plateau values A_j->infinity. There are legal choices of the macro partition, tangent directions and the once-fixed smoothing radius sigma for which the ACTUAL resulting smooth real pair (F,V), including its global Dirichlet repair W, has

    (-Delta+V)F=0,  every exponential times F in L2,
    |D^m V| <= C_m a^(m+3/2)/sqrt(R),
    |V| <= C r^(-1/2)L(r) eventually,

and H=-Delta+V has infinitely many negative eigenvalues. The new conclusion is only the last line. The source construction and arbitrary-loss theorem retain their existing provenance and assumptions. No historical novelty or independent external validation is claimed.

The legal tests are ordinary compact macro cutoffs, without the zeros of F. They have support diameter a fixed multiple of ell=sqrt(R/a), and their negative Rayleigh quotient has order a/R. Keeping the repair's zero-extension boundary jumps is essential to the calculation.

## 1. Reserving actual single-wave cores

Choose B=4*pi*sqrt(n) before constructing the cover. Choose escaping centers x_j=(r_j,0,...,0), with R_j=sqrt(K^2+r_j^2), well inside successive constant plateaus. Put A_j=a(R_j), ell_j=sqrt(R_j/A_j). Because the plateaus have a fixed positive logarithmic width and ell_j/R_j->0, the cubes |x-x_j|_infinity<4B ell_j fit inside the plateaus for all large j. Choose the centers so those cubes and their fixed enlargements are disjoint.

The following is a legal modification of the audited covering argument, not an assumed property of its arbitrary partition. Start with an ordinary bounded-overlap slow-radius partition chi_i^0. Add disjoint smooth bumps b_j equal to one on |x-x_j|_infinity<=3B ell_j and zero for |x-x_j|_infinity>=4B ell_j. Define

    chi_j^reserved=b_j,
    chi_i^other=(1-sum_j b_j) chi_i^0.

The new nonnegative partition sums to one. Local comparability of ell and bounded overlap give the same all-order derivative bounds C_m ell^(-m), with constants depending on the fixed B. Every support remains in a fixed multiple of its macro ball. Packing separately in the original and reserved families bounds the overlap graph degree; the union need not have mutually disjoint cores across the two types. Give each reserved center direction t_j=e_2, perpendicular to x_j; they never overlap each other. Greedily assign the other tangent directions with the usual finite forbidden-cap argument, now also avoiding any neighboring reserved direction. The n>=3 tangent sphere still has positive dimension. Thus every hypothesis used by the sparse-repair theorem remains valid.

In each reserved inner cube the ACTUAL q is exactly

    q=cos(A_j t),  t=x_2,

Choose the sparse threshold smaller if needed so 0<tau<=sin(1/10). Decreasing tau preserves sparsity. It can be done in the original theta,rho,tau,K parameter order. ## 2. The local geometry through second order

Suppress j, write A=A_j, R_0=R_j, ell=sqrt(R_0/A), eta=1/(A ell), mu=A/R_0=A^2 eta^2, and let

    s=x_1-r_j,  t=x_2,  z=(x_3,...,x_n),
    S=s/ell, T=t/ell, Z=z/ell, zeta=A t,
    d=n-1, U=T^2+|Z|^2, b_0=exp(-U/2), c=cos(zeta).

On this plateau Phi(R)=A R+constant. Relative to E(x_j) exp(-A s), its envelope b_eta satisfies, uniformly with the finitely many macro derivatives needed on fixed cubes,

    b_eta=b_0[1+eta S U/2+eta^2 b_2(Y)+o(eta^2)].       (1)

The unknown smooth b_2 is immaterial: it has no zeta dependence and (partial_zeta^2+1)(b_2 c)=0. The fixed origin regularization contributes o(eta^2) here. Indeed its relative size on a macro cube is O(K^2 sqrt(A)/R_0^(3/2)), and its ratio to eta^2 is O(K^2 A^(3/2)/sqrt(R_0))=O(K^2 kappa)->0. At the operator level its residual O(A^2 K^2/R_0^2) is o(mu), since A K^2/R_0->0.

After removing exp(-A s), the exact Cartesian Laplacian divided by A^2 is

    L_0+eta D_1+eta^2 D_2,
    L_0=partial_zeta^2+1,
    D_1=2(partial_zeta partial_T-partial_S),
    D_2=Delta_(S,T,Z).                                  (2)

Applying it to b_eta c gives, through the second order,

    eta b_0 2T sin(zeta)
    +eta^2 b_0[-d c-2ST(1-U/2)sin(zeta)].                (3)

For example D_2(b_0 c)=(U-d)b_0 c, whereas the radial term -2 partial_S(b_0 S U c/2)=-U b_0 c. They cancel U exactly. Retaining a transverse Gaussian but freezing this radial term would produce the wrong spectral compensation.

## 3. Exact stripe correctors, including boundary jumps

Put delta=arcsin(tau)<=1/10. On each stripe |zeta-(pi/2+k*pi)|<delta solve three Dirichlet ODEs and extend their solutions by zero outside the stripes:

    L_0 p=2sin(zeta),  L_0 r=c,  L_0 k=-2p'.             (4)

Extend them antiperiodically with period pi. In the stripe about pi/2, put h=zeta-pi/2 and C_delta=(1+2delta tan(delta))/2. The complete solutions are

    p(h)=h sin(h)-delta tan(delta) cos(h),
    r(h)=[h cos(h)-delta cot(delta) sin(h)]/2,
    k(h)=C_delta h cos(h)-(h^2/2)sin(h)
            +[delta^2/2-C_delta delta cot(delta)]sin(h).  (5)

They vanish at h=+-delta. Globally they are continuous but their first derivatives jump at stripe boundaries. Every L_0 in the remainder of the proof is DISTRIBUTIONAL, including those jumps.

The local repair through second order is

    w_app=E(x_j)e^(-A s)b_0 { -eta T p
        +eta^2[d r+(T^2-1)k+ST(1-U/2)p] }.              (6)

Inside the stripes, equations (2)-(5) give Delta(f+w_app)=o(mu)E(x_j)e^(-A s), with O(A^2 eta^3) error apart from the stated origin error. It has exactly zero Dirichlet trace on the stripe walls. This approximates the actual GLOBAL w; the justification is in Section 5.

Define the antiperiodic distributions

    K_cell=L_0 p-2sin(zeta),
    A_cell=L_0 r-c,
    B_cell=L_0 k+2p'.                                   (7)

They vanish inside every stripe; outside, their regular parts are respectively -2sin(zeta), -c, and 0. They also contain the repair's boundary measures. Dividing them by c is safe at those boundaries, where |c|=tau. Set their quotient to zero in the inner stripe neighborhood containing the zeros of c.

Their means, normalized on any interval of length pi, are

    M_r=mean(A_cell/c)
       =[-pi+2delta+cot(delta)-delta csc(delta)^2]/pi,
    M_k=mean(B_cell/c)=2k'(delta)/(pi sin(delta)),
    k'(delta)=C_delta[cos(delta)-delta csc(delta)]
                 -delta sin(delta).                    (8)

To check the boundary signs: r'(delta)=[cos(delta)-delta csc(delta)]/2, and the two derivative jumps divided by c each contribute r'(delta)/sin(delta). Likewise the two k jumps contribute k'(delta)/sin(delta). Omitting these terms changes (8).

K_cell/c is odd and pi-periodic, so has mean zero. Also p(K_cell/c)/c has mean zero as a distribution: p=0 on its boundary support and K_cell=0 in the open support of p. No product of two singular distributions occurs.

Let D_delta=cos(delta)-delta csc(delta)<0; the inequality follows from sin(delta)cos(delta)<delta. Therefore M_k<0. For delta<=1/10,

    M_r<=-1+2delta/pi,
    |M_k|<=8delta/pi,
    d M_r-M_k <= -2+12delta/pi < -3/2.                 (9)

For the middle bound use sin(delta)>=delta/2, delta/sin(delta)-cos(delta)<=delta^2, and 1+2delta tan(delta)<=2. These elementary inequalities hold throughout [0,1/10]. The last bound uses d>=2 and M_r<0. Thus the unsmoothed weak mean is already strictly attractive.

## 4. The actual radial smoothing, with a fixed small sigma

Take the original nonnegative normalized radial C-infinity kernel psi, supported in the unit ball. On the plateau epsilon=sigma/A is exactly constant, so smoothing commutes with Delta. Define its fast-coordinate operator

    S_sigma h(zeta)=integral psi(v)e^(sigma v_1)
                                  h(zeta-sigma v_2)dv.

Radial harmonic averaging gives S_sigma cos=cos and S_sigma sin=sin exactly. Introduce the moment operators

    C_sigma=integral psi(v)e^(sigma v_1)v_2 c(zeta-sigma v_2)dv,
    K_sigma=integral psi(v)e^(sigma v_1)v_2 K_cell(zeta-sigma v_2)dv,
    P_sigma=S_sigma p-sigma C_sigma.

The distributions are smoothed by these operators. Choose sigma<delta/4. Their nonzero residuals are then confined away from c=0. Define smooth pi-periodic coefficients, extended by zero on the remaining inner collar,

    v_sigma=(S_sigma K_cell)/c,
    alpha_sigma=(S_sigma A_cell)/c,
    beta_sigma=(S_sigma B_cell-sigma K_sigma)/c,
    n_sigma=(P_sigma/c)v_sigma.                          (10)

The additional moment terms are REQUIRED; radial averaging is not just ordinary convolution in t when exp(-A s) is present. Expanding translation of the slow variables gives the actual smoothed potential uniformly on compact macro subsets:

    V=A^2 { -eta T v_sigma
       +eta^2[d alpha_sigma+(T^2-1)beta_sigma
                         +ST v_sigma-T^2 n_sigma]
       +o(eta^2) }.                                     (11)

Here is a direct derivation. Before smoothing, Delta(f+w_app)/A^2 has first coefficient -T b_0 K_cell and second coefficient

    b_0[d A_cell+(T^2-1)B_cell+ST(1-U/2)K_cell].

Translating -T b_0 adds -sigma(T^2-1)b_0 K_sigma at second order. Translating the leading denominator b_0 c and smoothing its first correction gives

    F/[E(x_j)e^(-A s)b_0]
       =c+eta[SU c/2-T P_sigma]+O(eta^2).

Dividing on the safe-denominator region yields (11); throughout the inner collar both the actual potential and every coefficient in (11) are zero by harmonic averaging. No quotient is taken at a moving or unresolved zero.

Parity gives mean(v_sigma)=0. As sigma decreases to zero,

    mean(alpha_sigma)->M_r,
    mean(beta_sigma)->M_k,
    mean(n_sigma)->0.                                  (12)

These are limits of smooth functions against finite measures, not a discarded boundary layer. All residuals in (7) are smooth off the stripe endpoints plus finitely many endpoint measures per period. Their denominators are uniformly separated from zero on the smoothed support for sigma<delta/4. The first two limits follow from weak convergence of S_sigma to the identity and sigma K_sigma->0. For the last, P_sigma->p uniformly, and p vanishes at both endpoints; the regular part of K_cell is supported where p=0. Hence the product's weak integral tends to zero. This also explains why one cannot compute these means by simply erasing the collars and their boundaries.

Choose sigma ONCE small enough that, with m_r=mean(alpha_sigma), m_k=mean(beta_sigma), m_n=mean(n_sigma),

    d m_r-m_k<-1,  m_k-m_n<0.                           (13)

Existence follows from (9), M_k<0 and (12). This is a legitimate extra smallness condition within the source smoothing construction. No dependence on j is allowed or needed. If an already fixed sigma has not been checked against (13), this argument does not classify it. Constants may diverge as this chosen sigma becomes small; all are fixed before j tends to infinity.

## 5. Why the local calculation controls the global repair

On the outer cube |Y|_infinity<3B, actual Omega consists of literal flat stripes. The global w belongs to H_0^1(Omega), solves -Delta w=Delta f, and has the audited bound |w|<=C eta E. Its restriction to each stripe segment has zero trace on the flat side walls. The local w_app has the same side-wall trace and the residual stated after (6). The two functions may differ on the outer macro-cube boundary, where the same bound and envelope comparability give

    |w-w_app|<=C_B eta E(x_j)e^(-A s).

Here is an explicit localization bound independent of all data beyond that cube. In each stripe let h=A(t-t_node), lambda=pi/(4delta) and b(h)=cos(lambda h)>=1/sqrt(2). The positive function E(x_j)e^(-A s)b(h) obeys

    -Delta[e^(-A s)b(h)]=A^2(lambda^2-1)e^(-A s)b(h).

It is a forcing barrier. For each cube face multiply it by exp(-A times distance to that face). For an s face, the Laplacian coefficient is lambda^2-(1+-1)^2>0. For a z face it is lambda^2-2>0. For a t face its lower bound is lambda^2-2-2lambda>0, since |b'/b|<=lambda and lambda>=pi/(0.4)>7.8. Sum the face barriers. On their own faces they dominate the boundary data after multiplying by a fixed C_B; on stripe walls the error trace is zero. The weak maximum principle on these bounded Lipschitz stripe segments yields

    |w-w_app| <= C_B E(x_j)e^(-A s)
       [eta^3+K^2/R_0^2+eta exp(-c_B A ell)]             (14)

on |Y|_infinity<=2B. The forcing estimate has used (1)-(6); the origin contribution is separately retained. The barriers may be rescaled for the fixed margin B. On regions outside Omega both zero extensions are zero, so (14) also holds there.

After actual smoothing, every derivative of order m costs only C_(m,sigma) A^m on this pointwise error. This follows by differentiating the kernel, without differentiating the global W. For m=2, the potential error on safe denominators is

    O(A^2 eta^3)+O(A^2 K^2/R_0^2)
                     +O(A^2 eta exp(-c_B/eta))=o(mu).   (15)

The denominator is bounded below by a fixed multiple of E(x_j)e^(-A s) on the coefficient support. In the inner collar everything is exactly harmonic. Equation (15) proves that (11) concerns the full actual potential and includes all global repair effects. It is not an assumption of local Dirichlet independence.

## 6. A legal macro test and infinite negative index

Let chi(Y)=product_(i=1)^n cos(pi Y_i/(2B)) on the cube |Y|_infinity<B, extended by zero. It is a compact H^1 trial, and

    integral |D_Y chi|^2 / integral chi^2 = n pi^2/(4B^2)=1/64.

Use chi_j(x)=chi((x-x_j)/ell_j). Periodic averaging of (11) gives

    q_H(chi_j)/||chi_j||^2
       =mu_j[1/64+d m_r-m_k
                 +(m_k-m_n)<T^2>_(chi^2)+o(1)] <0      (16)

eventually, by (13). The first-order term has mean zero and contributes o(mu), rather than merely o(kappa): two integrations by parts against bounded periodic primitives give O(eta^2) for the macro integral of T v_sigma(T/eta). The compact weight chi^2 is C^1 with integrable second weak derivative, and its value and first derivative vanish at the endpoints. Multiplying this O(eta^2) by A^2 eta gives O(A^2 eta^3)=o(mu). The second-order coefficient averages follow by one such integration (or ordinary periodic weak convergence). The mean of ST v_sigma is zero. Uniform error (15) also contributes o(mu).

All these supports are disjoint and escape to infinity. Their finite spans have diagonal negative form. Min-max therefore gives infinite negative index. Since the actual V is bounded and tends to zero, its negative spectrum is discrete, hence infinitely many negative eigenvalues accumulating only at zero. Smooth compact approximations can replace each H^1 test while preserving negativity.
