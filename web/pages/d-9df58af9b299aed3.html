## Reconstruction without a supplied two-mode bound

Write a=mu1, c=mu2, d=mu3, r=c-a²>0. In L²(nu), multiplication by q is a positive contraction. Compress it to the orthonormal vectors 1 and (q-a)/sqrt(r). Its matrix is

    M = [[a,sqrt(r)],[sqrt(r),e]],
    e=(d-2ac+a³)/r.

The trace is T=a+e=(d-ac)/r, determinant D=ae-r=aT-c. Its characteristic polynomial p(q)=q²-Tq+D obeys

    integral p = c-Ta+D = 0,
    integral q p = d-Tc+Da = 0.

Therefore, by removing the terms orthogonal to p,

    integral p² = integral q²p = mu4-Td+Dc = rho >= 0.

The spectral measure nu2 of M at its first coordinate is a positive probability measure on two distinct nodes in [0,1]. Distinctness follows from sqrt(r)>0. Its moments muhat_h obey the characteristic recurrence, agree with mu0=1, mu1=a, mu2=c, and mu3=d, and satisfy

    muhat4 = c²+rT² = Td-Dc.

Thus rho is exactly mu4-muhat4, not a squared parameter-fit error or an unexplained norm surrogate. If rho=0, p=0 nu-almost everywhere, so nu is supported on its two roots and all future moments follow the recurrence. This inference genuinely requires positivity of nu; dimension need not be supplied.

For f(q)=q^h, let L be its cubic Hermite interpolant at the two nodes, matching values and derivatives. The double-node interpolation remainder is f''''(xi)p(q)²/24. On [0,1], for h>=4,

    0 <= f''''(xi)/24 <= binom(h,4).

Integrating the remainder and using cubic moment matching gives

    0 <= mu_h-muhat_h <= binom(h,4)rho.

The error is zero for h<=3 and equal to rho for h=4. This is standard Gaussian quadrature reconstructed from orthogonality and Hermite interpolation; it is not claimed as a new theorem.

## Noisy certificate audit

Suppose clipped estimates of mu1,...,mu4 have error at most w and rL>0 as defined in the peer note. Both r and rhat=chat-ahat² are at least rL, and |r-rhat|<=3w. Hence the stated loose bound |sqrt(r)-sqrt(rhat)|<=3w/sqrt(rL) is valid. The numerator N=d-2ac+a³ has perturbation at most (1+4+3)w=8w. Since 0<=e<=1,

    |Nhat-e rhat| <= 8w+3w = 11w.

Division by rhat>=rL and clipping the estimate of e to [0,1] proves the stated 11w/rL error. The raw symmetric matrix differs from M in Frobenius norm by at most

    kappa = sqrt(w²+18w²/rL+121w²/rL²).

Spectral clipping into 0<=X<=I is the Euclidean projection onto a closed convex subset of symmetric matrices, so it cannot increase this Frobenius distance to M. The resulting matrix Mhat and M are contractions; the telescoping identity for powers bounds their moment difference by h*kappa.

Consequently rho <= max(0,mu4hat+w-<e1,Mhat^4 e1>+4kappa)=rhoU, and for h>=4 the predicted error is at most h*kappa+binom(h,4)rhoU. For h<=3 it is at most h*kappa. These bounds are sound on the one joint confidence event; they do not introduce a fresh failure budget for each reused query.

The rL dependence is essential. At rL<=0 these formulas cannot be used; a one-mode certificate or UNKNOWN is the correct fallback. This audit does not establish that the conservative four-assay certificate is cost efficient or that floating-point spectral clipping is rigorously rounded.
