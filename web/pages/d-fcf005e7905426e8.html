Section 3 defines degree as the ordinary degree of projective closure, and irreducibility over C. Theorem 3.1 takes fixed N,r≤N,e0,C, positive weights ω_l, and δ_1,…,δ_r; S⊂Z^N is integral, lies in |z_l|≤CX^{ω_l}, and is contained in a degree-≤e0 algebraic set Z of dimension ≤r. For every fixed e, every C-irreducible A⊂Z defined over Q, deg A≤e, dim A=h>0 and A∩S≠∅ must satisfy, uniformly in X,Z,S,A, either

  |S∩A| ≪_{e,α} X^α for every fixed α>0,

or

  H_A(T) ≥ T^h/(h! δ_h^h) − C_e T^(h−1)  (T≥1),

where H_A is the C-dimension of restrictions of ambient coordinate monomials of ω-weight ≤T. Degree-zero-dimensional pieces contribute at most their degree. The theorem requires the alternatives for every fixed degree allowance e, not only for the initial cut components.

COMMON INPUT AND DESCENDANT SCOPE
In §11.2, for each paired box the initial sets from Prop. 5.2 have degree, number, and dimension bounded independently of X; their dimension is at most d, except for the stated mixed-cut components of dimension d. The ambient integer coordinates after Prop. 6.2 are (x′,y′)∈Z^(2d), with weights (U repeated d times,V repeated d times) and |x′_i|≪_f X^U, |y′_i|≪_f X^V. The block transformations are integral unimodular and may vary. Their bounds preserve the weighted polynomial filtration. Thus Theorem 3.1 is used with N=2d and r=d, on each bounded-degree initial set / box-group separately. The initial varieties lie in the rational-coordinate arithmetic variety

  a_i^k b_i = n − θ_i  (1≤i≤d),

with a_i,b_i linear forms over the fixed number field in x′,y′ and n the rational-coordinate integer input. Every selected point has all a_i≠0. Therefore any irreducible descendant A meeting selected S is not contained in the excluded boundary and meets this open set. The Section 3 definition makes the relevant irreducible descendants C-irreducible; this matches the hypotheses in §§8–9.

Selected-set facts used by the small-count branches are: within a fixed interval/box group, at most one selected point has a prescribed n, and at most O_f(1) selected points have a prescribed x′. These are the source's Lemma 4.1 bounds; they remain true after restricting S to A because S∩A⊂S. The inputs are selected from the prime-divisibility set before the geometric counting, not all integer points of the ambient variety. This is sufficient: Theorem 3.1 permits an arbitrary selected subset S.

NUMBER-FIELD RANGE (§11.2): DESCENDANT-BY-DIMENSION MAP
Use the source thresholds δ_h(U,V) in (10.3). With

  Ψ_h(U,V;D) = UV / ((kU+V)^((h−1)/h) D^(1/h)),

Prop. 7.1 gives H_A(T) ≥ c_h T^h/h! − O_e(T^(h−1)+1), where c_h=(kU+V)^(h−1)D/(U^hV^h). Thus δ=Ψ_h(U,V;D) is exactly the threshold for this coefficient. The cited source claims the error uniform for fixed weights and degree bound, independently of moving equations and blockwise integral changes.

1. Dimension h=d. By Prop. 7.4, a d-dimensional descendant meeting S can only be a mixed affine component from Prop. 5.2. Its equation has the stated bidegree/saturation form, yielding D=λU+s_0V≥U+V. Hence c_d is at least the coefficient corresponding to δ_d=Ψ_d(U,V;U+V). This is the only top-dimensional case; second-cut sets have smaller dimension.

2. Dimensions 2≤h≤d−1, outside the quartic strengthening. Prop. 7.1 and (7.6) give D≥min(U,V), hence δ_h=Ψ_h(U,V;min(U,V)). For d=4 in quartic mode Q, Lemma 6.3 first removes the infinite-a-fiber base. Its remaining-set conclusion says n is algebraic over the a-functions on every irreducible subvariety meeting S. Then h algebraically independent a-coordinates exist. The source’s strengthened pair-image bound gives D≥V: for h=3 this is precisely δ_3=Ψ_3(U,V;V). For h=2, the stronger Prop. 8.1 gives

  H_A(T) ≥ (T²/2) min{ (2U+V)D_0/(U²V²), 3/U² } − O_e(T),
  D_0=min(3U,2V,U+V),

which matches δ_2=max{Ψ_2(U,V;D_0), U/√3} in (10.3). The bounded-degree and moving-coordinate uniformity is stated in Props. 7.1 and 8.1.

3. Dimension h=1. Prop. 9.2 supplies δ_1=max{U/2,min(U,V/g)} and H_A(T)≥T/δ_1−O_{f,e}(1), exactly the h=1 alternative. Its branches cover every geometrically irreducible Q-defined curve meeting S:
   - Constant n or constant a-image (equivalently fixed x′) has O_f(1) selected points.
   - Nonlinear a-image, or line image with function-field degree at least two over its parameter, gives two independent monomial families and H_A(T)≥2T/U−O_e(1). Since δ_1≥U/2, this implies the required bound.
   - For a line image with degree-one function field, the rational coordinates y′_i and n are bounded-degree functions in Q(s). If a y′_i is nonpolynomial, Lemma 9.1 gives O_{f,e,α}(X^α) selected points: its coefficient-uniform divisor/resultant bound applies to integral s,y′_i in the transformed boxes, and fixed s fixes x′ with O_f(1) selected points.
   - If all y′_i are polynomials in s, then (s−τ_i)^k divides n(s)−θ_i, the τ_i are distinct, deg n≥1+d(k−1), and deg b_i=deg n−k. For d>4, this gives deg b_i≥(d−1)(k−1); for the quartic d=4,k=2 equality deg n=5 is excluded by the one global exceptional-value deletion of Prop. 8.2, so surviving curves have deg b_i≥4. Writing g=(d−1)(k−1) for d>4 and g=4 for d=4, the distinct-degree functions s^j b_i^a (0≤j<g) give H_A(T)≥gT/V−O_e(1), while powers of s give H_A(T)≥T/U−O(1). Together these imply the stated δ_1 Hilbert bound. The source's E_f deletion is O_f(X^(1/5)) on n∈[−2X,2X] and is performed once before curves, boxes, and prime intervals; it is not charged per descendant.

4. Dimension h=0. Section 3's degree bound gives at most deg A points, so no alternative is required.

The separate quartic infinite-fiber deletion in Lemma 6.3 is also an upfront cost, not a descendant alternative. For each initial set its base has dimension ≤2, and the selected-point cost is O_f(X^(2U)). After deleting it, its conclusion is exactly what makes n algebraic over a on every remaining A meeting S. Section 11 and the already-banked parameter inequalities pay this per-box cost. No further point deletion is needed in the Hilbert alternatives.

DIRECT SURFACE RANGE (§11.1): SEPARATE THEOREM 3.1 INPUT
This range uses a different ambient set: Z={f(x)=y z^k}⊂A³_Q, irreducible, dimension r=2, with weights (1,b′,η′). S is the selected integer triples (x,y,z), one chosen prime z=p>X per qualifying input x∈(X,2X], satisfying f(x)=y p^k and the displayed coordinate bounds.   H_Z(T)=D T²/(2 b′η′)+O(T),  D=max{d,b′+kη′},

so δ_2=√(b′η′/D)<0.4999 gives exactly the h=2 alternative. Zero-dimensional descendants are degree-bounded. These are precisely the alternatives used for Theorem 3.1 with r=2; the finite list of (b′,η′) choices makes the constants uniform.
