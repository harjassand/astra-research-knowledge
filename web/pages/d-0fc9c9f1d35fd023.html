Cycle 3 leaf: structural multi-catalyst small-gain certificate
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the stated CTMC family and certificate; UNKNOWN for necessity for recurrence or for any broader WR class.

Outcome

The one-catalyst boundary discount extends to r catalysts and s substrates by discounting B_i's Lyapunov weight exactly when at least one catalyst that interacts with B_i is present. This creates a finite strict LP. Its first inequalities control catalyst linear drift (the catalyst death budget must cover substrate births); its edge inequalities control substrate/catalyst cross-tier drift (substrate death must cover autocatalytic catalyst production, including a last-catalyst boundary surcharge). A final positive discount epsilon_i makes immigration of a missing neighbor catalyst generate negative drift proportional to a large, currently frozen B_i.

For positive finite rate intervals, rational endpoints, and integer k_ji, this LP has an exact rational primal/dual certificate with polynomial bit complexity in the input size. Feasibility is complete for this particular weighted boundary-corrected linear Foster template, not for recurrence. In special interaction graphs it is exactly a scalar/diagonal M-matrix or a max-times cycle condition. For a general shared-substrate graph, an ordinary summed matrix spectral-radius condition is sufficient but not necessary; an explicit rational counterexample is given below.

Model and assumptions

There are catalyst species A_j (1<=j<=r) and substrate species B_i (1<=i<=s). Let E be the set of interacting pairs. Assume every substrate has at least one neighbor: N(i)={j:(j,i) in E} is nonempty. A catalyst with no interactions is allowed. For j in [r] the network has 0 <-> A_j. For each (j,i) in E it has

  A_j <-> A_j+B_i,
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i,

where k_ji is a positive integer. All six rate families are fixed positive constants for the CTMC; for robust certification, each lies in a supplied finite interval with rational endpoints strictly between zero and infinity:

  0 -> A_j: alpha_j;           A_j -> 0: delta_j;
  A_j -> A_j+B_i: beta^+_ji;   A_j+B_i -> A_j: beta^-_ji;
  A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i: gamma^+_ji;
  (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i -> A_j+B_i: gamma^-_ji.

Use standard falling-factorial mass-action propensities: alpha_j, delta_j A_j, beta^+_ji A_j, beta^-_ji A_j B_i, gamma^+_ji A_j B_i, and gamma^-_ji (A_j)_(k_ji+1) B_i. All reactions are paired with their reverses, hence the graph is weakly reversible. Every rate interval is an input, not learned or inferred. The fixed-rate recurrence theorem below applies separately to every fixed choice inside the box; a time-varying rate path has a uniform pointwise generator bound but is not assigned a positive-recurrence conclusion.

Boundary-corrected potential

For each substrate define the interaction-support indicator

  I_i(a)=1{sum_(j in N(i)) a_j > 0}.

Choose positive catalyst weights lambda_j, active substrate weights w_i, and discounts epsilon_i. When substrate i is inactive, its total coefficient is w_i+epsilon_i. Define

  Phi(a,b)=sum_j lambda_j a_j
           +sum_i [w_i+epsilon_i(1-I_i(a))] b_i.

Thus B_i has weight w_i+epsilon_i when none of its neighbor catalysts is present, and weight w_i once at least one such catalyst is present. This support-specific correction is needed for sparse E: a catalyst not adjacent to i must not activate or deactivate B_i's discount. Since every w_i and lambda_j is positive, Phi is nonnegative and proper; Phi(a,b)>=c sum_j a_j+c sum_i b_i for c=min_j,i{lambda_j,w_i}>0. Use 1+Phi for a strictly positive Foster function.

Exact jump changes

The catalyst immigration jump 0->A_j changes Phi by lambda_j, less epsilon_i b_i for each i in N(j) with I_i(a)=0. It activates those discounts. Catalyst death A_j->0 changes Phi by -lambda_j, plus epsilon_i b_i for each i in N(j) for which a_j=1 and every other catalyst in N(i) is zero. This is the last-neighbor boundary surcharge.

The substrate pair changes Phi by +w_i and -w_i: whenever A_j>=1 for (j,i) in E, I_i=1, and the substrate weight is w_i. The autocatalytic pair changes Phi by +k_ji lambda_j and -k_ji lambda_j. Its reverse starts with A_j>=k_ji+1 and therefore does not deactivate any I_i. The reverse autocatalytic drift is nonpositive and will only help.

Let the rational rate endpoints be denoted alpha_j in [alpha_j^-,alpha_j^+], delta_j in [delta_j^-,delta_j^+], beta^+_ji in [(beta^+_ji)^-,(beta^+_ji)^+], beta^-_ji in [(beta^-_ji)^-,(beta^-_ji)^+], and similarly for gamma. Sufficient robust strict inequalities are

  p_j := delta_j^- lambda_j
         - sum_(i:(j,i) in E) (beta^+_ji)^+ w_i > 0,                     (C_j)

  q_ji := (beta^-_ji)^- w_i
          - k_ji (gamma^+_ji)^+ lambda_j
          - delta_j^+ epsilon_i > 0,                                    (S_ji)

  lambda_j>0, w_i>0, epsilon_i>0.                                      (P)

The surcharge uses the largest possible catalyst death rate delta_j^+; the negative linear catalyst drift uses delta_j^-; substrate births and autocatalytic production use upper endpoints; substrate removal uses its lower endpoint. No lower bound on gamma^- is needed in these inequalities because the reverse term has the favorable sign. Positive lower bounds alpha_j^- are needed for the boundary activation drift, and finite upper bounds alpha_j^+ bound the constant immigration term.

Generator calculation and independent sufficiency proof

Fix any allowed rate vector and state (a,b). Put C_0=sum_j alpha_j^+ lambda_j. Apart from the support-toggle terms, the generator is exactly

  sum_j [alpha_j lambda_j-delta_j lambda_j a_j]
  +sum_(j,i in E) w_i[beta^+_ji a_j-beta^-_ji a_j b_i]
  +sum_(j,i in E) k_ji lambda_j[gamma^+_ji a_j b_i
                                      -gamma^-_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i].

The support-toggle terms are

  -sum_(i) epsilon_i b_i 1{I_i(a)=0}
      sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j
  +sum_(i) epsilon_i b_i
      sum_(j in N(i)) delta_j
       1{a_j=1, sum_(h in N(i)\{j})a_h=0}.

For each edge, the last-neighbor surcharge is at most delta_j^+ epsilon_i a_j b_i, since its indicator forces a_j=1. The reverse autocatalytic term is nonpositive. Hence (C_j),(S_ji) give the pointwise bound

  L Phi(a,b) <= C_0
      -sum_j p_j a_j
      -sum_(j,i in E) q_ji a_j b_i
      -sum_i epsilon_i b_i 1{I_i(a)=0}
                       sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j^- .                       (1)

For a fixed i, if I_i=0, all neighboring a_j vanish and the last term in (1) is at most -epsilon_i b_i sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j^-. If I_i=1, at least one neighboring a_j>=1, and the edge sum in (1) is at most -b_i min_(j in N(i)) q_ji. Define

  chi_i=min{ epsilon_i sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j^-,
             min_(j in N(i)) q_ji } > 0.

Then

  L Phi(a,b) <= C_0 -sum_j p_j a_j -sum_i chi_i b_i.                    (2)

This is a direct weighted Foster inequality. It does not claim each catalyst coordinate drift L A_j is separately negative: when A_j is small and a neighboring B_i is huge, autocatalysis can make L A_j positive. The certificate controls that cross drift jointly with the B_i removal and boundary discount.

By (2), L Phi<=-1 outside a finite level set. Every sublevel set of Phi is finite, and every state has finite total rate. Also L Phi<=C_0 globally. Stopped Dynkin gives P(tau_R<=T)<= (Phi(x)+C_0 T)/R for tau_R=inf{t:Phi(X_t)>=R}; explosion while Phi remains bounded is impossible because a finite state set has bounded total rate. Thus the minimal CTMC is nonexplosive. If every substrate has a neighbor, the chain is irreducible: create a neighboring catalyst via 0->A_j, use A_j+B_i->A_j to reduce any positive B_i one molecule at a time, then drain catalysts; conversely create target catalysts and use A_j->A_j+B_i to build target substrates, suppressing other jumps along the finite path. All desired finite paths have positive probability because the relevant rates are positive and finite at each visited state. The standard irreducible CTMC Foster criterion therefore proves positive recurrence for each fixed rate vector in the interval box.

Strict-LP feasibility, exact compilation, and certificates

The boundary correction can be chosen after the main small-gain LP. Define

  P_ji=(beta^+_ji)^+ / delta_j^-,
  Q_ji=k_ji (gamma^+_ji)^+ / (beta^-_ji)^-  for (j,i) in E.

The base strict LP is

  lambda_j > sum_(i:(j,i) in E) P_ji w_i       for each j,
  w_i > Q_ji lambda_j                         for each (j,i) in E,
  lambda_j>0, w_i>0.

Feasibility of this base LP is equivalent to feasibility of (C_j),(S_ji),(P) for some epsilon_i>0. Necessity follows because (S_ji) is stronger than w_i>Q_ji lambda_j. Conversely, if the base system is strict, let s_ji=(beta^-_ji)^- w_i-k_ji(gamma^+_ji)^+lambda_j>0. For each nonisolated substrate choose a rational

  0 < epsilon_i < min{w_i/2, min_(j in N(i)) s_ji/(2 delta_j^+)}.

Then q_ji>s_ji/2>0. This establishes completeness for the specified weighted inequalities; it does not establish necessity for recurrence or for arbitrary Foster functions.

Normalize the base LP by sum_j lambda_j+sum_i w_i=1 and introduce a common margin t. The exact rational LP is

  maximize t
  subject to
    delta_j^- lambda_j - sum_i (beta^+_ji)^+ w_i >= t,       all j;
    (beta^-_ji)^- w_i - k_ji(gamma^+_ji)^+ lambda_j >= t,   all (j,i) in E;
    lambda_j>=t, w_i>=t, t>=0;
    sum_j lambda_j+sum_i w_i=1.

Its optimum is positive iff the strict small-gain LP is feasible. A positive optimum returns rational lambda,w,t; choosing epsilon by the displayed formula returns rational boundary weights w_i+epsilon_i when inactive. Exact dual/Farkas output handles the negative case. For an explicit dual convention, let z=(lambda,w,t), let Gz>=0 stack the catalyst-margin rows, edge-margin rows, and positivity rows lambda_j-t>=0,w_i-t>=0, and let h^Tz=1 be the normalization. The primal is max e_t^Tz subject to Gz>=0,h^Tz=1,z>=0. A rational dual (u,pi) with u>=0 and

  -G^T u + h pi >= e_t

has objective pi and certifies t<=pi. In particular, pi=0 certifies that no strict weights exist. If the weak normalized primal Gz>=0,h^Tz=1,z>=0 is infeasible, a Farkas pair u>=0, pi<0 with -G^T u+h pi>=0 certifies infeasibility by contradiction after multiplying by z. All checks are exact rational dot products.

Small examples show the certificate format. Feasible two-catalyst/one-substrate instance: delta_j=1, beta^+_j1=3/4, beta^-_j1=1, gamma^+_j1=3/4, k_j1=1 for j=1,2, and alpha_j=gamma^-_j1=1. Take lambda_1=lambda_2=1, w_1=4/5, epsilon_1=1/100; the inactive B_1 coefficient is 81/100. Then p_j=2/5 and q_j1=1/25 exactly. Infeasible one-catalyst/one-substrate instance: delta=beta^+=beta^-=gamma^+=1,k=1. The normalized margin rows are lambda-w-t>=0 and w-lambda-t>=0; their half-sum is -t>=0. The dual multipliers u=(1/2,1/2,0,0) on those two rows and the two positivity rows, with pi=0, certify t<=0. The boundary surcharge can only make the strict system harder.

The displayed primal slacks, comparison-matrix eigenvalue, and zero-margin dual are checked with exact fractions by `python3 outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle3/evidence/verify_small_gain_examples.py`. This finite check verifies only those fixtures; the general LP soundness and Foster proof are analytic above, and the script does not implement an LP solver.

Bit and cost model: input is r,s,E; positive integer k_ji in binary; and finite positive rational interval endpoints for alpha/delta and beta/gamma in numerator/denominator binary. Let N=r+s+|E| and L be total input bit length. Constructing the matrix uses O(N) variables/rows (more precisely O(r+s+|E|)) and rational coefficients of bit length polynomial in L; forming k gamma endpoints adds O(log k) bits. A general exact rational LP algorithm (ellipsoid or another polynomial-bit LP method) solves the margin LP in time polynomial in N and L. Rational basic primal and dual solutions have polynomial bit length by determinant bounds; epsilon_i, selected by minima of finitely many rational slacks/endpoints, also has polynomial bit length. Strict feasibility may have an exponentially small margin, so floating tolerances are not sound; exact sign/dual checking is essential. This is not a strongly-polynomial claim, nor a runtime for acquiring the input rate intervals. If only floating estimates or point queries for unknown rates are available, they must first be converted to certified rational enclosures; that acquisition cost is additional.

Spectral-radius and interaction-graph tests

Eliminating w_i gives the exact monotone homogeneous map

  F_j(lambda)=sum_(i:(j,i) in E) P_ji max_(h in N(i)) Q_hi lambda_h.

The base LP is feasible iff some lambda>0 has F(lambda)<lambda componentwise. Equivalently, with the nonlinear max-spectral radius

  rho_max(F)=inf_(lambda>0) max_j F_j(lambda)/lambda_j,

the LP is feasible iff rho_max(F)<1. This is a precise max-linear characterization, not generally an ordinary matrix spectral-radius test.

Special case 1: every substrate i has exactly one catalyst neighbor sigma(i). Then

  F_j(lambda)=g_j lambda_j,
  g_j=sum_(i:sigma(i)=j) P_ji Q_ji.

The LP is feasible iff max_j g_j<1, exactly the nonsingular M-matrix condition for I-diag(g); when r=1 this is the familiar one-catalyst scalar small-gain sum.

Special case 2: every catalyst j interacts with exactly one substrate. The LP inequalities become edge constraints w_i>Q_ji lambda_j and lambda_j>P_ji w_i. Make a directed bipartite gain graph with edge lambda_j->w_i of gain Q_ji and w_i->lambda_j of gain P_ji. Positive weights exist iff every directed cycle has gain product <1 (equivalently, the maximum cycle geometric-mean/max-times spectral radius is <1). Taking logarithms converts this to strict difference constraints; summing around a cycle proves necessity, and the no-nonnegative-cycle difference-constraint theorem proves sufficiency.

For general shared-substrate graphs the ordinary matrix M with entries M_jh=sum_{i: (j,i),(h,i) in E} P_ji Q_hi is only a sufficient comparison: F(lambda)<=M lambda, so rho(M)<1 implies feasibility. It is not necessary. In the feasible rational example above, P_11=P_21=Q_11=Q_21=3/4, hence M is the 2x2 all-(9/16) matrix with rho(M)=9/8>1, while lambda=(1,1),w=4/5 satisfies the LP. Thus replacing the exact max in F by a sum loses feasible networks.

Limits and status

The proof is for the named stochastic mass-action network, positive finite fixed-rate box, standard falling-factorial propensities, and the support-dependent piecewise-linear potential above. It gives a sufficient, exact, efficiently verifiable recurrence certificate when feasible. LP infeasibility means only that this certificate template fails; it is not transience, explosion, or failure of every Lyapunov method. The reverse autocatalytic rates are not needed for drift but positivity of all rates is used for the stated irreducibility. If a substrate has no catalyst neighbor, its count is frozen and full-state irreducibility fails; remove such coordinates or state a communicating-class result separately. No deterministic-to-stochastic transfer is used.
