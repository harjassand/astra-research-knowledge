# Multiscale observable certificates: prior-art audit

Worker: c_priorart. Retrieval and audit: 2026-10-06. This is an excerpt-level primary-source audit, not a complete literature survey. Metadata and recoverable copies are in `sources/c_priorart/metadata.json`. The two supplied recent files actually end `.txt`, not `.html.txt`.

## Exact question and interface

Given a target Gibbs law P on an N-site lattice, an approximately learned coarse-to-fine generator Q, a specified bounded local or scale observable f, and computable discrepancy information, certify |Pf-Qf| without a constant growing with N. Separate approximation error, finite conditional sampling error, coarse-law mismatch, thermal/relevant-direction mismatch, and calibration/data uncertainty. A coefficient that hides divergence as the mass vanishes or correlation length grows is not a critical-regime certificate.

## Established interfaces that must be beaten

1. **Local/block influence comparison.** Rebeschini and van Handel, [Comparison Theorems for Gibbs Measures](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4117), submitted 2013-08-19; journal reference J. Stat. Phys. 157, 234-281 (2014). Corollary 2.6 (PDF p8) bounds |Pf-Qf| by sum_ij delta_i(f) D_ij b_j, D=sum_n C^n, with C the Wasserstein sensitivity matrix of P's single-site updates and b_j the Q-averaged Wasserstein discrepancy between P and Q updates at the same boundary. Theorem 2.4 (p7) extends this to overlapping block kernels using positive weights, diagonal W, block influence R, D=sum_n(W^-1 R)^n, and defect W^-1 a. It requires quasilocality and a stated decay condition; it does not assume that a block approximation automatically contracts. Uniformly bounded resolvent rows already give volume-independent local-observable bounds. Merely renaming sites as scales or wavelet blocks does not create a novel comparison theorem.

2. **Conditional KL/Fisher hierarchy.** Guth, Lempereur, Bruna and Mallat, [Conditionally Strongly Log-Concave Generative Models](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/guth23a.html), ICML 2023, PMLR 202, 12224-12251; dates 23-29 July 2023. Proposition 2.1 (p3, proof Appendix E.1 p20) already proves TV(P,P_hat) <= [sqrt(KL(P||P_theta))+sqrt(KL(P_hat||P_theta))]/sqrt(2), with each KL exactly a sum of conditional errors plus coarse error. Equation (8) (p4) gives conditional KL(P||P_theta) <= loss/alpha for loss = FI/2 and uniform lower Hessian alpha for the learned conditional energy. Theorem 2.1 addresses finite-data excess risk in exponential models under explicit conditioning/moment/regularity assumptions. Proposition 3.1 (p6) supplies conditional strong log-concavity on sufficiently high Fourier bands of scalar-potential energies. This already covers the obvious conditional-score-to-KL construction.

3. **Stein/Poisson sensitivity.** Mackey and Gorham, [Multivariate Stein Factors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.07392v6), initial 2015-12-23, inspected v6 dated 2016-11-23: Theorem 2.1 (p2) gives the Langevin Poisson solution u_h and M1(u_h) <= 2 M1(h)/k for their half-speed generator, with higher derivative bounds using k, L3, L4. Gorham, Duncan, Vollmer and Mackey, [Measuring Sample Quality with Diffusions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06972v6), initial 2016-11-21, inspected v6 dated 2018-11-13: Definition 4 (p7) uses integrable Wasserstein decay r(t); Theorem 5 (p8) gives M1(u_h) <= M1(h) integral r and a Stein solution. Theorems 6-7 turn Stein discrepancy into Wasserstein control under additional coefficient assumptions. A fast-mixing constant is supplied or proved; small empirical residual alone does not establish it. A multiscale Stein certificate needs a constructive improvement to these sensitivities, not only a residual diagnostic.

4. **Wavelet conditioning/covariance.** Marchand, Ozawa, Biroli and Mallat, [Wavelet Conditional Renormalization Group](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04941v1), 2022-07-11; journal reference Phys. Rev. X 13, 041038 (2023). Theorem IV.1 gives scale-uniform upper/lower eigenvalue bounds for normalized wavelet-field covariance for stationary fields with spectral density c|k|^-zeta and sufficiently many wavelet vanishing moments. This is already wavelet preconditioning at critical covariance scaling. It is not a theorem that every non-Gaussian conditional has a uniform log-Sobolev constant, nor a theorem of rapid mixing for arbitrary frustrated dynamics.

## Recent author claims and scope

**Bandini et al., [2608.31114v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31114v1), submitted 2026-08-31.** Sections III, IV.2 and IV.3 implement WCRG for frustrated soft-spin fields. The reported O(log L) is conditional *sweep count*, based on observed O(1) conditional decorrelation at each scale. Generating N output coordinates still costs at least O(N); training/equilibrium source data have separate costs. Section IV.3 reports residual microscopic-energy histogram mismatch under the finite conditional ansatz. The learned hierarchy samples its own approximate law. The paper does not provide a uniform computable local-observable error certificate for the true target.

**Hasenfratz et al., [2608.28580v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28580v1), submitted 2026-08-28.** Section IV distinguishes blocked-coarse mismatch from detail-conditional mismatch. Section V.1 uses the conditional flow as an initializer, with target-invariant fine updates. A deliberately mistuned thermal coupling slows long-distance relaxation; a substantially different microscopic action on the critical surface is repaired quickly. These are controlled empirical tests, not a bound uniform in volume or criticality. Global independence MH suffers extensive residual density-ratio variation; sufficient rethermalization restores the target, but the required finite stopping time is not certified.

**Singha et al., [2604.10209v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10209v1), submitted 2026-04-11.** Section III builds conditional Gaussian mixtures plus masked continuous flows and preserves coarse coordinates, enabling cheap exact restriction of the *model* sample. This is a close architectural baseline for learned coarse-to-fine generation plus multilevel variance reduction. Sections III.F and Appendix E use self-normalized importance weights. Their finite-sample 'unbiased' language needs correction: with one sample the weight cancels, giving E_Q f rather than E_P f. Exact restriction/telescoping does not remove this ratio-estimator bias. The PDF body says April 14, 2026 while same-v1 HTML body says August 24, 2026; arXiv submission record says April 11. All three are preserved; source-version drift is unresolved.

## Independently reconstructed deduction

The finite-state Dobrushin certificate is proved in `DERIVATIONS.md` without relying on the cited proof. Exact enumeration in `work/c_priorart/local_certificate_check.py` supplies adversarial checks only. For a ring with beta=.18, fields .03 versus .14, the one-site indicator bias/certificate at N=12 are .077197883/.077668146, while global KL=.102707884. The same coefficient matrix has row sum .348511780 for every tested ring N=4,6,8,10,12; the proof, not the finite list, explains its local scaling.

The decisive obstruction is a two-phase family: conditional defects tend to zero as epsilon tends to zero, but a local phase-sensitive bias tends to .4. The resolvent grows as 1/(2 epsilon), cancelling the small defect. Thus even local conditional accuracy cannot replace a stability/mixing assumption. At criticality the corresponding influence or response coefficient is exactly the load-bearing quantity to prove.

## Assumptions and novelty constraints

- Global hierarchy KL is generally extensive in N even for products. No volume-independent global-TV conclusion follows from a fixed per-site error. Local observables can remain accurate despite global distinguishability.
- TV certifies bounded observables. Unbounded phi^2 or phi^4 need explicit tails/moments or a stronger weighted transport metric; Pinsker alone is insufficient.
- Conditional score matching must use the correct conditioning law. In a cascade, sampling-error KL averages use the law of the actual sampled coarse prefix. Training averages under the equilibrium prefix cannot silently replace it.
- Strong lower curvature is enough for the cited Fisher-to-KL step. The cited MALA cost additionally assumes a uniform upper Hessian beta. Raw quartic energies have unbounded positive curvature, so that particular finite-condition-number guarantee cannot be imported unchanged. The paper itself notes delta=infinity for phi^4.
- Projecting onto high frequencies can establish first-level lower curvature. Iterating requires controlling the *exact marginalized energies*, including generated couplings. An RG scalar-potential ansatz is not a proof for all coarse levels.
- Gaussian mass-only MLE plus spectral rescaling is a one-parameter exponential-family calibration construction. Direct FFT sampling of the supplied target covariance is the stronger baseline; no foundational novelty or computational advantage is established.

## Concrete use and next falsifiable step

Use the existing block comparison theorem as the initial certificate engine. The meaningful new target is an explicitly computable block/scale sensitivity matrix for a non-Gaussian frustrated model, with a bounded relevant-mode correction and constants remaining controlled over a declared critical window. The next falsifiable step is to compute or rigorously bound those sensitivities for the actual conditioned Hamiltonian, compare its certificate against direct local/block Gibbs and CSLC baselines, and test a thermal perturbation separately from irrelevant perturbations. If no resolvent/Poisson sensitivity can be bounded, the certificate stays conditional and should not be labelled a discovered critical sampler theorem.

Unexplored alternatives: observable-specific response/covariance bounds with thermal mode projected out; Stein control variates with verified local Poisson solves; genuinely unbiased randomized multilevel estimators; boundary-condition/phase-sensitive certificates. None is established by this audit.
