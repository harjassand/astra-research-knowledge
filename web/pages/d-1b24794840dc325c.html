CYCLE THREE: A SHARP HANKEL-GAP / FUTURE-KL MODULUS UNDER FAST MIXING
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the explicit two-state family and task below; novelty and external validation UNKNOWN.

Question and result
-------------------
Does a weak Hankel direction that is safely droppable for a short task remain
safe for H-step or full-future prediction when H grows, even for a fixed-size
positive HMM with uniform fast mixing and fixed emission floors? No. In a
two-state symmetric HMM, the population rank-one SVD truncation of the
one-symbol context/future block is exactly the iid fair predictor. If the
second Hankel singular value is gamma, its expected full-past H-step KL risk
is between 2 H gamma^2 and (512/15) H gamma^2. This gives a matching horizon
scale H=Theta(gamma^-2): below it the truncation loss is small; at that scale
it is order one; for every nonzero gamma the expected full-future KL is
infinite. State count, mixing time, transition-entry floor, emission-entry
floor, emission singular value, and one-symbol context mass all remain fixed.
Only the transition's nonstationary singular value and the Hankel gap vanish.

This is a population approximation theorem, not a claim that a learner can
select rank without data. The expectation defining R_H is over an independent
fresh realization of the stationary process, with its full two-sided past
revealed as test context and its next H observations scored. Equivalently, by
stationarity, it is the average fixed-truncation bias at a stationary time
after any amount of same-source training. It is not a same-path acquisition
lower bound: the truncated predictor is fixed in advance and does not depend
on a training transcript. A same-stream, nontruncating estimator recovers the
weak direction at the matching gamma^-2 sample scale; its explicit risk
upper is included below. The construction differs from the Cycle-2
short-reset lower and the growing-K/K^2 parameter family: it uses a single
stationary raw stream, fixed K=2, and a horizon-dependent truncation bias.

Model and interface
-------------------
The hidden state S_t and observed symbol Y_t lie in {-1,+1}. For a parameter
rho in (0,1/4], the stationary hidden chain has uniform stationary law and

    P_rho(S_(t+1)=s' | S_t=s) = (1 + rho s s')/2.

Emission noise is fixed and known, with eta=1/2:

    P(Y_t=y | S_t=s) = (1 + eta y s)/2.

The process is generated from its stationary law and an actual raw trajectory
is observed; no hidden states, resets between time points, or conditional
probability oracle are used. For the approximation-bias functional, draw a
fresh stationary test trajectory independently of any training data, reveal
its full observed past F_0 = sigma(Y_t:t<=0), and predict its next H raw
symbols. The reference supplied representation is the population rank-one
truncation described below. KL is in nats. Define

    R_H(rho) = E_rho[ D_KL( P_rho(Y_1,...,Y_H | F_0) || U^H ) ],

where U is the fair Bernoulli law and U^H its iid H-fold product. This is the
expected task risk of the truncated predictor, not a hidden-state loss.
The same rate holds if F_0 is replaced by any finite observed context of
length at least one. By stationarity this also describes the expected
approximation bias at a fixed terminal time after a raw training trajectory,
if that trajectory's terminal observed history is used as context and the
truncated predictor remains fixed in advance. It does not lower-bound the
risk of a predictor selected from that training trajectory.

The H-step objective is cumulative in the ordinary fixed-horizon sense: by
the KL chain rule its expectation is the sum of the H one-step predictive KL
terms, each at a deterministic time. The theorem does not cover a random
horizon or stopping time, an anytime bound, a worst-case pathwise cumulative
loss, or a random number of repeated tasks.

All charged margins
-------------------
The hidden transition matrix, in state order +1,-1, is

    P_rho = 1/2 [[1+rho,1-rho],[1-rho,1+rho]].

Its entries are at least 3/8, its singular values are 1 and rho, and its
Dobrushin coefficient is rho<=1/4. From a point mass, distance to stationarity
after t steps is rho^t/2, so the 1/4 mixing time is one transition. The
stationary hidden-state mass is 1/2 per state.

The emission matrix, with rows indexed by observations, is

    E = [[3/4,1/4],[1/4,3/4]].

It is strictly positive, has entrywise floor 1/4, and singular values 1 and
1/2. Observed symbols are stationary fair bits, so each one-symbol context
has mass exactly 1/2. For every finite word w of length l>=1,

    P_rho(Y_1...Y_l=w) >= (1/2)(1/4)^(l-1),

because every next observation, conditionally on any past, has probability at
least 1/4. Thus finite contexts are never excluded by a zero-probability
issue; the bound for long contexts decays exponentially and is not used by
the expectation-risk proof. The Hankel witness uses prefix length one and
future length one. The task horizon H is separately charged.

The only collapsing algebraic margin is sigma_min(P_rho)=rho. This is not
hidden inside “fast mixing”: the chain mixes faster as rho decreases, but its
transition operator approaches rank one. Its effect on observable rank is
quantified exactly by gamma below.

Theorem: exact rank gap and matching task-risk modulus
-----------------------------------------------------
Let H_1 be the raw two-symbol Hankel block with rows indexed by the one-symbol
prefix y in {+1,-1}, columns by the one-symbol continuation z, and entries

    H_1[y,z] = P_rho(Y_0=y,Y_1=z).

Then, with c=eta^2 rho=rho/4,

    H_1 = 1/4 [[1+c,1-c],[1-c,1+c]],
    sigma_1(H_1)=1/2,   sigma_2(H_1)=c/2=rho/8=:gamma.

For every 0<rho<=1/4, the observable Hankel rank is exactly two; at rho=0
the process is iid fair and its rank is one. The rank-one SVD truncation of
H_1 is the all-1/4 matrix and is exactly the pair block of the iid fair
predictor U. For every integer H>=1,

    2 H gamma^2 <= R_H(rho) <= (512/15) H gamma^2.       (1)

Both constants are universal for this fixed emission channel and
0<rho<=1/4. Consequently the sharp risk modulus is Theta(H gamma^2): to
keep the truncation KL below epsilon it is sufficient that
gamma^2 <= 15 epsilon/(512 H), while gamma^2 >= epsilon/(2H) forces risk at
least epsilon. For any fixed gamma>0, R_H tends to infinity linearly as
H grows, so the full-future KL is infinite. Low-rank truncation is therefore
valid only relative to a finite horizon and a declared risk tolerance.

Proof
-----
1. Observable block and rank-one truncation.

The hidden chain has E[S_t S_(t+1)]=rho. Conditional emission means are
E[Y_t|S_t]=eta S_t, so E[Y_t Y_(t+1)]=eta^2 rho=c. By sign symmetry both
one-symbol marginals are fair. Thus

    P(Y_0=y,Y_1=z)=(1+c yz)/4,

which gives the displayed H_1. Its orthonormal eigenvectors are the constant
and alternating sign vectors, with eigenvalues 1/2 and c/2. Since c>0,
rank(H_1)=2. An HMM with two hidden states has Hankel rank at most two, so the
infinite observable rank is exactly two. At rho=0 the hidden states are iid,
and independent emissions through the fixed channel produce iid fair
observations, hence rank one. Deleting the alternating singular direction
from H_1 leaves 1/4 times the all-ones matrix, which is precisely the
one-step block of the valid rank-one iid fair process U.

2. Expected full-past future KL equals H times one-step predictive
information.

Let F_(t-1)=sigma(Y_j:j<=t-1), and define

    I_* = I(Y_0;F_-1).

The KL chain rule and stationarity give

    R_H(rho)
      = sum_(t=1)^H E[ D_KL(P(Y_t | F_(t-1)) || U) ]
      = H I_* .

The second equality uses the fact that each Y_t is marginally fair: its
expected conditional KL to U is exactly its mutual information with the
entire preceding observation past.

The past contains Y_-1, so data processing gives

    I_* >= I(Y_0;Y_-1).

The pair (Y_-1,Y_0) is a symmetric binary pair with correlation c. For

    g(x)=1/2[(1+x)log(1+x)+(1-x)log(1-x)],

its mutual information is g(c). On the other hand, by the HMM Markov
structure, F_-1 -> S_-1 -> S_0 -> Y_0 is a Markov chain, so

    I_* <= I(S_0;S_-1)=g(rho).

Now g(0)=g'(0)=0 and g''(x)=1/(1-x^2). For 0<=x<=1/4,

    x^2/2 <= g(x) <= x^2/[2(1-x^2)].

Use c=rho/4=2gamma for the lower bound, obtaining
g(c)>=c^2/2=2gamma^2. For the upper bound,

    g(rho) <= rho^2/[2(1-rho^2)]
             <= (8/15)rho^2 = (512/15)gamma^2,

since rho^2<=1/16. Multiplication by H proves (1).

The same proof works for a finite context of any length at least one. In the
chain-rule expansion, the available past contains the immediately preceding
symbol, giving the pair-mutual-information lower; the full hidden Markov
past gives the same upper by data processing.

3. Same-data model-retaining baseline.

The truncation risk is approximation bias even when H_1 is known exactly.
It is not an unavoidable risk from raw acquisition. Let a baseline observe
one stationary raw training path Y_1,...,Y_n, know the fixed two-state family
and eta, and retain the weak rank-two direction. Its risk bound applies to a
fresh continuation after this training path, uniformly over the continuation
history (and hence also to an independent stationary test context). Write
D_t=S_t S_(t+1) and
Z_t=Y_t S_t. The symmetric HMM gives iid transition signs D_t of mean rho;
the emission signs Z_t are iid of mean eta and independent of the hidden
chain. Hence

    W_t=Y_tY_(t+1)=Z_t Z_(t+1) D_t,
    E W_t=c=rho/4.

The sequence W_t is 1-dependent: W_t uses only Z_t,Z_(t+1),D_t, so W_t and
W_(t+d) are independent for d>=2. With N=n-1, the raw moment

    chat=N^(-1) sum_(t=1)^N W_t,
    rhohat=clip_[0,1/4](4 chat)

satisfies E[(rhohat-rho)^2] <= 48/N. Indeed Var(chat)<=3/N, and clipping to
an interval containing rho cannot increase absolute error. This uses no
reset or hidden-state label.

For completeness, the model-retaining plug-in predictor has an explicit
full-history risk bound. Let ell(h) be the hidden log-odds after observed
history h, initialized at ell(empty)=0. Its update and next-symbol
probability are

    ell(hy)=2 atanh(rho tanh(ell(h)/2)) + 2y atanh(eta),
    p_rho(+1|h)=[1+eta rho tanh(ell(h)/2)]/2.

For rho,rhohat in [0,1/4], the update's derivative in ell is at most 1/4
and its derivative in rho is at most 32/15. Thus, on every history,

    |ell_rho(h)-ell_rhohat(h)| <= (128/45)|rho-rhohat|.

The next-symbol probabilities differ by at most
(61/180)|rho-rhohat|, and both lie in [7/16,9/16]. Since
D(Ber(p)||Ber(q)) <= (p-q)^2/[q(1-q)], the one-step KL is at most
5|rho-rhohat|^2. Applying the KL chain rule to the H future symbols yields
uniformly over histories

    E[ future-KL risk of plug-in model ] <= 240 H/N.       (2)

The full raw-history likelihood is the strongest same-data baseline; it can
emulate this estimator and may improve constants. This baseline is an upper
bound on acquisition risk, not a comparison establishing a same-path lower
bound for all acquisition rules. Equation (2) is a concrete
moment-based implementation. If
N gamma^2 is large, (2) is smaller than the truncation bias in (1), and it
retains the weak direction rather than deleting it. The estimator can set
rhohat=0 at some finite samples; that is appropriate uncertainty near the
rank boundary, not a guarantee of rank-two selection.

Costs, supplied information, and precision
------------------------------------------
The approximation theorem is population-level and uses the exact H_1 SVD;
it acquires no data. Its costs are K=2 hidden states, alphabet size two,
one-symbol witness horizon, future task horizon H, and no oracle response.
Its charged conditioning is sigma_2(H_1)=gamma and sigma_min(P_rho)=8gamma.
The transition and emission entry floors remain 3/8 and 1/4; emission
singular value is 1/2; state and one-symbol context masses are 1/2. For
history words of length l, a simple uniform lower mass is
(1/2)4^{-(l-1)}; the proof integrates over histories and pays no reciprocal
rare-word factor.

The matched raw baseline acquires n binary symbols from one stationary
trajectory, no resets, and one lag-product sum. It uses O(n) arithmetic
updates and O(log n) counter bits, then stores one parameter; full-path
retention costs n bits if desired. The clipped moment parameter is rational
on a dyadic grid after output rounding. To represent rho to absolute
precision b bits costs O(b) output bits. The H-step forecast can be
computed by the two-state filter in O(H) scalar operations and O(1) filter
memory. All transition/emission model structure and eta are supplied in this
baseline; rho is acquired from the path. No claim is made for an unknown
HMM family or unknown emission channel.

The exact predictive-operator oracle boundary is clear: supplying rho (or
the entire numerical rank-two observable representation) makes the
rank-two predictor exact and removes the raw-sample estimation cost. Supplying
only the state count or the singular-vector basis does not remove the
truncation bias if the weak coefficient is still discarded. The constant
and alternating singular vectors are known in this family, so basis
discovery is not the mechanism here.

Falsifiers and claim boundary
-----------------------------
A direct falsifier is a sign/indexing error in the pair Hankel matrix or in
the identity R_H=H I_*. The rational checker enumerates finite observed laws
and finite-context risks at several rho values; it is not a substitute for
the all-H information proof. The checker computes raw word probabilities
exactly with rational arithmetic for rho=1/4,1/8,1/16, and the rank-one
rho=0 boundary; its finite-context KL diagnostics use
floating-point logarithms and are only convention checks. A falsifier for the matching risk order would
violate either I(Y_0;Y_<0)>=I(Y_0;Y_-1) or the Markov-chain data-processing
upper I(Y_0;Y_<0)<=I(S_0;S_-1).

What is established: exact population rank and rank-one SVD truncation;
matching all-fixed-deterministic-H task-KL approximation-bias modulus for the
specified symmetric HMM, evaluated on a fresh stationary full-past context;
and a raw same-data model-retaining baseline with O(H/n) expected
continuation-KL risk. The fixed-H objective is an expected cumulative loss,
not a random-time or pathwise cumulative guarantee.
What is not established: a finite-sample rank-selection theorem uniform as
gamma->0; optimal minimax sample constants; a lower bound on every
rank-preserving estimator; or the same modulus for arbitrary positive HMMs,
nonstationary sources, other task losses, or tasks conditioned on hidden
states. External validation, novelty, and priority are UNKNOWN.
