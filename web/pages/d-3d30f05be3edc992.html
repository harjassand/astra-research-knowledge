RADIAL TRANSPORT AUDIT — 7 October 2026

Verdict
The claimed two-way uniform total-variation approximation is valid under the
stated exponential-slack hypothesis. No density, nonatomicity, or total-variation
convergence of the base radial laws is required. The transport kernels are fixed
by the base laws and do not use the unknown tilt parameter. This is an existence
and analytic convergence result; weak convergence alone supplies no rate or
efficient procedure for obtaining the limiting law or evaluating its quantiles.

Protocol and source scope
Read work/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.txt, work/repository/00_START_HERE.txt, and
work/repository/01_CORE.txt. This note is a direct proof audit of the explicit
classical experiment posed by the parent, rather than a literature or quantum
bridge certification. The scale a_N and sector label j are used only through
the known pushed-forward radius law q_N of r=j/a_N.

Exact assumptions and notation
Let q_N and q be probability Borel measures on [0,infinity), and assume q_N
converges weakly to q. Let sigma be normalized uniform probability on S^2.
Let M,B be fixed nonnegative constants and let delta>0. Assume

 C := sup_N integral exp[(M+delta)r^2+(B+delta)r] q_N(dr) < infinity.

For theta=(A,b), A is a real 3-by-3 matrix with operator norm at most M,
and b is real with Euclidean norm at most B. Only the symmetric part of A
affects the tilt, so one may instead require A symmetric. Define

 h_theta(r,u)=r^2 u^T A u+r b.u,
 w_theta(r,u)=exp[h_theta(r,u)],
 Z_N,theta=integral w_theta dq_N dsigma,
 Z_theta=integral w_theta dq dsigma.

The probability experiments are P_N,theta=(w_theta/Z_N,theta)(q_N x sigma)
and P_theta=(w_theta/Z_theta)(q x sigma). A complex-valued h would not define
the stated probability experiment; reality is implicit in the tilt setup.

1. Parameter-independent quantile coupling, including atoms
For a probability law nu, let F_nu be its right-continuous CDF and

 Q_nu(t)=inf{x>=0:F_nu(x)>=t}, 0<t<1.

Let T be uniform on (0,1). Set R_N=Q_qN(T), R=Q_q(T), and let pi_N be
the joint law of (R_N,R). Weak convergence implies Q_qN(t)->Q_q(t) at
every continuity point of the monotone quantile Q_q, hence for Lebesgue-a.e.
t. Thus R_N->R almost surely under this common quantile coupling. In
particular d_N=E[min(1,|R_N-R|)]->0.

For clarity, the coupling can be implemented in either direction by the
randomized probability-integral transform. Given X=x with base law nu,
sample T from the kernel

 Uniform((F_nu(x-),F_nu(x)]) if nu({x})>0,
 delta_{F_nu(x)}             if nu({x})=0.

Then T is uniform and Q_nu(T)=X almost surely. Values at t=0,1 and
nu-null exceptional input points can be assigned arbitrarily.

The forward kernel K_N takes (r_N,u), obtains T by this transform for
q_N, and returns (Q_q(T),u). The reverse kernel L_N takes (r,u), obtains
T by the transform for q, and returns (Q_qN(T),u). Equivalently these
are the regular conditional laws of pi_N in the two directions, retaining
the same sphere direction. Standard Borel disintegration ensures existence.
All their randomness and laws are independent of theta.

Atoms require randomization when a source atom must be split. In particular
one must not write an inverse CDF composition as a deterministic radial
map on atomic laws without the conditional-uniform step. If the original
input labels are j rather than r, the known injective map j/a_N converts
them to radii and recovers j from output support exactly.

2. Uniform unnormalized-density convergence
Put H(r)=Mr^2+Br, J(r)=(M+delta)r^2+(B+delta)r, and G(r)=exp[H(r)].
The hypothesis and Portmanteau applied to the nonnegative continuous
exp[J] give integral exp[J] dq<=C. Also |h_theta(r,u)|<=H(r).

On 0<=s,t<=R,

 |h_theta(s,u)-h_theta(t,u)| <= (2MR+B)|s-t|,
 |w_theta(s,u)-w_theta(t,u)| <= L_R|s-t|,
 L_R=exp[MR^2+BR](2MR+B).

If S=max(R_N,R)>R, monotonicity gives

 |w_theta(R_N,u)-w_theta(R,u)| <= 2exp[H(S)]
 <= 2exp[-delta(R^2+R)] exp[J(S)].

Since exp[J(S)]<=exp[J(R_N)]+exp[J(R)], its expectation is <=2C.
Therefore, for R>=1,

 Delta_N := E_{pi_N x sigma}[sup_theta |w_theta(R_N,U)-w_theta(R,U)|]
 <= L_R R d_N + 4C exp[-delta(R^2+R)].

First fix R and take N->infinity, then take R->infinity. This proves
Delta_N->0. It also implies sup_theta |Z_N,theta-Z_theta|<=Delta_N.
No convergence of q_N to q in total variation has entered the argument.

3. Normalization and two-way TV bound
Let c=integral exp[-H(r)] q(dr)>0. The function exp[-H] is bounded and
continuous, so c_N=integral exp[-H] dq_N -> c. Thus, for all sufficiently
large N and all theta,

 Z_N,theta>=c_N>=c/2,   Z_theta>=c.

On the common space with base pi_N x sigma, define two probability laws
with densities w_theta(R_N,U)/Z_N,theta and w_theta(R,U)/Z_theta.
Their respective output marginals in the target (R,U) coordinates are
K_N P_N,theta and P_theta. Their respective output marginals in the
source (R_N,U) coordinates are P_N,theta and L_N P_theta. Marginalization
contracts total variation (TV is one half of L1 here). Consequently both
directional errors are bounded by

 (1/2) E |w_N/Z_N-w/Z|
 <= [E|w_N-w|+|Z_N-Z|]/(2Z_N)
 <= Delta_N/Z_N
 <= 2Delta_N/c.

Hence

 sup_theta TV(K_N P_N,theta,P_theta) -> 0,
 sup_theta TV(L_N P_theta,P_N,theta) -> 0.

This certifies the exact classical radial-transport step posed to the audit.
It does not certify a preceding quantum-to-classical reduction or any rate
for q_N's weak convergence.

4. Independent tail-audit strengthening
The delegated independent attack found no counterexample under the stated
hypothesis. It also found a strengthening of this CLASSICAL TRANSPORT STEP
ONLY: if B>0, positive slack can be removed entirely provided the exact
boundary-envelope moment is bounded:

 C0=sup_N integral exp[Mr^2+Br] dq_N < infinity.

For each fixed theta, integration over the sphere gives

 integral w_theta(r,u) dsigma(u)
 <= G_B(r):=exp[Mr^2] sinh(Br)/(Br), with G_B(0)=1,
 G_B(r)/exp[Mr^2+Br]=(1-exp[-2Br])/(2Br)<=1/(2Br).

G_B is nondecreasing. On S=max(R_N,R)>R, the angular integral of
|w_N-w| is at most 2G_B(S). Portmanteau bounds the q boundary moment
by C0, so the expected tail is at most 2C0/(BR). Compact-radius
equicontinuity is unchanged. Therefore sup_theta E|w_N-w|->0 and the
same TV proof works. Here sup_theta must remain outside the angular
integral: E sup_theta |w_N-w| need not enjoy this angular improvement.
This does not weaken any whole quantum theorem: Casimir or upper-symbol
linear corrections proportional to r/a can overwhelm the angular 1/r gain
on escaping radii much larger than a log r. Such corrections require their
own domination argument before a zero-slack transfer is justified.

If B=0 and M>0, a bounded exact-boundary moment is insufficient in general.
Choose R_N->infinity, p_N=exp[-MR_N^2],

 q_N=(1-p_N)delta_0+p_N delta_RN,   q=delta_0.

Then q_N weakly converges to q and integral exp[Mr^2] dq_N=2-p_N<=2.
At theta0=(0,0) the large-radius probability is p_N->0. At
theta1=(MI,0) it is 1/(2-p_N)->1/2. Both limiting experiments based
on q are exactly the same law delta_0 x sigma. A parameter-independent
reverse kernel returns the same law for theta0 and theta1. By the triangle
inequality its worst-case TV error is at least half their separation,
which tends to 1/4. Thus weak convergence plus a bounded boundary moment
does not imply the claimed equivalence across all allowed M,B.

The failure uses the allowed isotropic matrix MI. If a separate model
restricts A to traceless matrices, this particular counterexample is not
available and a separate boundary-tail audit is needed. If M=B=0, all
tilts are constant and the base-law coupling yields exact equivalence.

Failure ledger and strongest next attack
No failure in the stated transport lemma. Invalid shortcuts are deterministic
atom-splitting, replacing weak convergence by TV convergence without a
kernel, and dropping all tail control while retaining isotropic positive
quadratic tilts. The next load-bearing audit belongs upstream: whether a
quantum-to-classical reduction is uniform over the relevant sectors and
parameter set, including its normalization and tails. The radial step alone
adds no computational acquisition guarantee.
