# Space-enabling technology: sealed foil radiator cells

**Result: a credible, unbuilt engineering architecture; the claimed frontier-level improvement does not survive the present full-system accounting.** The strongest defensible opportunity is cheaper manufacture of fault-contained radiating cells. It is not a new heat-rejection principle. With explicit coolant plumbing, fluid, deployment, armor, pointing reserve and mass margin, the nominal model yields **609 W/kg installed**, or **462 W/kg with a redundant primary loop**, at a 350 K emitter and 200 K effective sink. Strong existing modules already demonstrated about **727 W/kg at different, hotter conditions**. An approximate temperature normalization leaves only a 7% advantage over that module-only comparator; it is not a matched test or an established gain.

All numbers for the proposed architecture are **conditional calculations**, not device measurements. No working radiator, manufacturing process, lifetime result, novelty result, or economic advantage was established. No source repositories or existing outputs were changed.

## Architecture and the concrete target

A 1 m² module has a one-metre central coolant spine and two half-metre radiating wings. Each wing is a welded titanium foil sheet partitioned into 10 mm wide, independently sealed water heat-pipe cells. Two embossed liquid-return arteries run under a fine capillary screen in each cell. The fine screen provides capillary pressure while the larger arteries carry most of the longitudinal return flow. This avoids making the whole radiating surface a thick, liquid-saturated wick. Each cell's evaporator is thermally clamped to the central spine, with **no fluid connection between a cell and the primary coolant loop**. A cell puncture therefore cannot drain the primary loop. A hit on the common spine remains a system failure unless separately protected or backed up.

The proposal deliberately uses known components: saturated water, titanium foil, composite wicks, independently sealed heat pipes, a pumped single-phase heat-input loop, and deployable membranes. The unverified engineering hypothesis is that a continuous forming/welding/filling line can produce the thin, artery-containing cells cheaply and with adequate yield, while preserving capillary priming and hermeticity after deployment and freeze cycles.

The originally attractive target was ≤1.2 kg/m² of **installed projected footprint**, ≥1 kW/m² useful heat rejection, and <5% power loss after five separated 1 mm punctures per m². The fully charged model misses the mass target: 1.39 kg/m² before reserve, 1.73 kg/m² with 25% reserve, or 2.28 kg/m² with a redundant primary loop. The meaningful performance gate is now **at least twice the installed W/kg of the best available panel in the same test, at the same source temperature, sink, area convention, pointing and puncture dose**. The current model does not predict that result.

The useful domain is an uncrewed orbital platform with a cold two-sided view, a radiator orientation within about 5° of edge-on to sunlight, and slow maneuvers. This is not yet a lunar-surface radiator design: surface support loads, dust, hot-ground view, anchoring and lunar-night survival would require a different, separately charged system.

## Governing equations and numerical point design

The runnable arithmetic is in `model.py`; `python3 work/genesis_space/model.py` recreates `model_results.json` without third-party packages. Approximate water properties near 350 K and all unmeasured design inputs are exposed in the file. This is a lumped screening model, not CFD or finite-element validation.

For projected footprint A, two faces, emissivity ε, geometric escape/view factor F, emitter T, effective radiative background Ts, absorptivity α and grazing sunlight angle θ:

\[
Q_{\rm payload}+P_{\rm pump}+Q_{\rm sun}
=2A\epsilon\sigma F(T^4-T_s^4),\qquad
Q_{\rm sun}=A\alpha S\sin\theta.
\]

At T=350 K, Ts=200 K, ε=0.85, F=0.90, α=0.90, S=1361 W/m² and θ=5°, gross rejection is 1163 W/m²; absorbed sunlight is 107 W/m². The remaining 1056 W/m² must also carry pump dissipation. For the modelled 100 kW loop, that subtraction is about 3 W/m². A low-emissivity foil is unusable without a suitable coating; the coating's mass, outgassing, adhesion and environmental durability remain development requirements.

For each 0.5 m × 0.01 m cell, Q≈5.28 W and phase-change mass flow is

\[
\dot m=Q/h_{fg}=2.28\times10^{-6}\ {\rm kg/s}.
\]

Capillary operation requires

\[
\Delta p_{\rm cap}=\frac{2\gamma\cos\beta}{r_p}
>\Delta p_{\rm artery}+\Delta p_{\rm fine\ wick}
+\Delta p_{\rm vapor}+\rho g\Delta z.
\]

Use two 0.4×0.3 mm liquid arteries, a 50 µm fine wick, effective pore radius 20 µm, contact angle ≤15°, and required local-wick permeability ≥10⁻¹³ m². With a rectangular laminar-duct approximation,

\[
\Delta p_{\rm artery}\simeq
\frac{12\mu L\dot V}{b h^3(1-0.63h/b)}.
\]

The model conservatively puts full collected flow through the whole artery length and all condensate through half a cell pitch of fine wick. It gives capillary head 6.11 kPa, artery loss 0.45 kPa, lateral fine-wick loss 1.71 kPa and vapor loss ~0.016 kPa. The microgravity capillary ratio is 2.81. An adverse half-metre lunar head reduces it to 2.06. A vertical Earth test would fail this point design (ratio 0.88); horizontal tests and an 0.08 m elevation impose the intended orbital/lunar capillary conditions without falsely demanding a one-g vertical device.

The vapor core is assumed to be 9.4 mm² per cell. Saturated vapor density is ~0.258 kg/m³ and speed ~0.94 m/s, comfortably below a sonic limit in this simple calculation. Entrainment, priming failures, bubbles and nonlinear boiling oscillations are not excluded by that speed estimate.

The two-scale wick is essential. A single fine, liquid-filled layer thick enough to return fluid over half a metre costs substantially more wick and water mass. It is also known heat-pipe engineering: using an artery is not a newly discovered pumping effect. [Walker, Tarau and Anderson's 2013 primary paper](https://www.1-act.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/ACT-TechPaper_GroovedAndSelf-VentingArterialHeatPipesForSpaceFissionPowerSystems.pdf) identifies trapped-vapor/noncondensable-gas de-priming as a failure of conventional arteries and demonstrates self-venting designs. The proposed thin foil artery must prove its own re-priming; the steady pressure balance alone does not supply it.

Water pressure near 350 K is ~41.7 kPa. A 25 µm wall with an assumed 4 mm local curvature radius has approximate membrane stress

\[
\sigma_{\theta}\simeq pR/t=6.67\ {\rm MPa}.
\]

This modest nominal stress does **not** validate thin seams. Welding defects, crease damage, proof pressure, local peel stress, hot excursions and trapped freezing water may dominate. At 400 K water pressure is several times greater; the 350 K point design cannot be silently extrapolated into the 500–600 K reactor-radiator regime. If manufacturing needs 50 µm walls, installed mass with the same reserve rises by 0.281 kg/m² and performance falls to ~524 W/kg.

For a 100 kW single-phase water heat-input loop with a 10 K temperature drop,

\[
\dot m_{\rm loop}=Q/(c_p\Delta T)=2.39\ {\rm kg/s},\qquad
P_{\rm pump}=\Delta p\dot m/(\rho\eta_p).
\]

At 60 kPa total pressure drop and 50% efficiency, pumping is ~295 W. Pump energy is small; plumbing and coolant mass are not. A 35 mm bore, 20 m main supply/return allocation alone contains about 19 kg of water; local 12 mm bore spines add roughly 11 kg per 100 m². The spine-to-cell interface must convey ~1 kW per metre with less than a 10 K source-to-emitter drop. Achieving a low-resistance, compliant interface at very low mass is an unclosed experiment.

For constant heat input and fixed geometry, dQrad/dT=8AεσFT³>0, giving a locally stabilizing thermal feedback. This scalar sign check does not establish fluid or spacecraft structural stability. An approximate ten-metre thin coolant rail can have a first bending mode around 0.06 Hz with these allocations; slow maneuvers and isolated reaction-wheel forcing are required until a full structural model exists. A pressure-supported membrane has little out-of-plane stiffness when cold or depressurized.

The cell membrane alone is only ~0.36 kg/m². Reporting its W/kg as system performance would inflate the figure by almost five times. For 100 kW, the full single-loop prediction corresponds to roughly 165 kg; the redundant-loop prediction is roughly 217 kg, before other spacecraft power, load, communication, propulsion or shielding hardware.

As a **screening sensitivity only**, apply the radiation ratio

\[
\frac{350^4-200^4}{(400-19.1)^4-250^4}=0.782
\]

to the existing 380 W / 0.523 kg module. This yields ~568 W/kg at the nominal proposed thermal boundary, versus 609 W/kg for the proposed installed array. Temperature-dependent transport, emitter temperature maps, geometry, orientation and omitted competitor infrastructure prevent this from proving either equality or superiority. It shows why an order-of-magnitude claim is unsupported and why a matched comparison is mandatory.

## Fault containment, freezing and manufacture

There are 200 cells/m² and 20,000 cells in a 100 m² array. If five separated punctures destroy exactly one cell each, radiating power falls 2.5% and total released water is about 1.44 g per m². The premise must be tested: impact spall can damage adjacent cells, seams can unzip, and released water can contaminate neighboring surfaces. A common-rail hit is not local. Moving the rails behind appropriately tested bumpers or duplicating them costs mass already acknowledged above.

Cells contain liquid only in the wick and arteries, leaving most vapor volume empty. This offers expansion space during freezing, but does not guarantee survival: ice can locally block the arteries or trap a liquid pocket. The source must bootstrap a melt/condensation front without auxiliary heaters silently placed across the whole radiator. NASA's earlier RDU cold-start experiments are direct evidence that sunlight alone need not thaw a water radiator ([NASA/TM-2012-217205](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120002929/downloads/20120002929.pdf)).

The proposed production sequence is form arteries, lay treated wick, weld longitudinal cell lanes, evacuate and degas, charge, partition-seal the filling edge, apply the emissive surface and inspect. Metre-scale 25 µm titanium welding is a development requirement. Fine-wick wetting must remain adequate after storage, vacuum bake and cycling; initial clean-coupon contact angle is insufficient.

At 10 mm cell pitch, the design has about 105 m of hermetic seams per m². A 100 m² array therefore needs about 10.5 km of seam, taking ~292 machine-hours at an assumed 10 mm/s, before cleaning, charging, inspection, rework or handling. Even ten seconds of inspection per sealed cell is another 56 hours. These process counts are more informative than calling foil inexpensive. Charge tolerance near ±10% is a starting experimental variable; noncondensable gas, residual cleaning products and artery priming may set much tighter practical yield limits.

No defensible production or launch-price advantage follows from the calculation. Required infrastructure includes an inert-atmosphere precision weld/roll-forming line, wick surface treatment, vacuum filling, leak metrology and a cold-shroud thermal-vacuum facility. A transparent cost model is

\[
C=C_{\rm tooling}+C_{\rm foil/wick/coating}+r_{\rm weld}t_{\rm weld}
+r_{\rm inspection}t_{\rm inspection}+C_{\rm rejected\ cells}
+C_{\rm qualification}+\lambda M_{\rm delivered}.
\]

The rates, yields, qualification costs and delivered-launch price λ are **UNKNOWN** here. Any commercial claim needs actual quotations and production trials. The full-space-platform capital—power generation, electronics, robotic assembly, communications, attitude control and disposal—is additional, not free because the radiator is light.

## Falsifiable terrestrial experiment and next milestone

First build a **0.5×0.1 m, ten-cell coupon** at the proposed 25 µm foil gauge, artery geometry and wick thickness. It should transport roughly 50 W from its short root into a cold shroud at source 360 K without a forced-convection cold plate on the condenser. Measure the dry and charged mass, two-sided calorimetric rejection, IR temperature map, root contact resistance, leak rate, capillary operation versus tilt and vapor charge. Run both horizontal and ~0.08 m adverse head conditions. Demonstrate repeatable priming after one freeze/thaw and one stow/deploy cycle. A persistent dry region, failed weld, inability to re-prime, or required wall/artery reinforcement that destroys the mass advantage stops the thin-cell path before a large array is built.

If the coupon passes, build a **1 m² integrated module** containing the flight-like coolant spine, support, coatings, deployment hardware, armor and harness. The mass of required array-level pump, main pipes and control hardware must be allocated using a real design. Test in ≤10⁻⁴ Pa vacuum with ≤200 K cold shrouds, 360 K source, matched Sun input, and a baseline radiator in the same chamber. Correct heater-block and cable heat losses by calibration; measure flow×enthalpy and shroud/calorimetric power independently to a stated uncertainty target of ≤3%.

Predeclare all of the following gates:

1. ≥1 kW useful rejection per projected m², including solar absorption and pumping heat, with source-to-emitter drop ≤10 K.
2. At least 2× the **installed** W/kg of the best selected conventional comparator at matched conditions. Target mass originally ≤1.2 kg/m²; current charged model predicts a miss. Do not substitute membrane mass or compare a 500 K emitter with a 350 K competitor.
3. After five separated 1 mm penetrations per m², ≤5% power loss, no loss of primary-loop coolant and no seam-propagated failure. Then test a rail-threatening impact against the actual protection/redundancy design.
4. Start from a 200 K cold soak using only the declared source interface and declared start-up power; no uncounted condenser-wide heater tape. Demonstrate 100 freeze/start cycles and deployment after storage, with no progressive gas blockage or leak.
5. Report production yield, actual seam speed, rework rate and leak/charge inspection time. A hand-built successful coupon is not evidence of cheap 20,000-cell manufacture.

Passing these tests would establish an integrated terrestrial radiator advance. It would still leave multi-year environmental exposure, debris risk, microgravity fluid behavior and launch/deployment qualification open. The next space milestone would be a small instrumented free-flying panel after those engineering tests, not a claimed operational 100 kW platform.

## What survives as an enabling interface

The central issue is the **heat-input geometry**. A lightweight emitting sheet does not provide the capability to acquire 100 kW from a compact source at only a 10 K temperature drop. A long thermal bus must pay fluid, conductance, protection and deployment costs. One way to remove much of that bus is to distribute dissipative loads over independent radiator tiles and distribute high-voltage electricity/data instead of low-temperature heat. For scale, a 10 m, 100 kW, 1 kV aluminum DC feed can have a conductor mass below 1 kg at a 1% resistive-loss allowance. Insulation, arc protection, connectors, converters, local chip-to-radiator spreading and shielding can easily dominate that conductor mass.

That load-distribution choice is a plausible systems interface, but distributed spacecraft loads and modular orbital compute already exist as concepts. It cannot be promoted into a discovery merely by renaming the layout. Concentrated reactors, habitats and manufacturing heat sources often cannot accept the interface. It is a useful requirement to take into a different architecture, not a verified new primitive from this round.

**Recommendation to synthesis:** keep the foil-cell architecture as a falsifiable, limited manufacturing hypothesis. Do not select it as a landmark technology on current evidence. The decisive obstruction is the combination of strong existing module performance and the supporting mass/thermal interface needed to turn a thin emitter into a usable heat-rejection system.
