# Closest prior for the smaller cyclic scalar grid

Root's independently reconstructed new result is `notes/root_cycle19/CYCLIC_SCALAR_GRID_HITTING.md`: an unknown proper n-letter series with at most m states is detected by one of the **m tuples ℓT, ℓ=1,…,m, each of dimension2nm**. All tuples are jointly nilpotent, so proper realizations are defined. The proof combines the release's finite-word faithfulness with a bivariate Volterra kernel, covariant row closure, Cauchy moments, and a nonzero degree≤m−1 scalar polynomial. It uses neither a new Moura estimate nor a supplied hidden realization. Root/peer mathematical gates, not this comparator note, support that theorem. The longer first-column marker derived by this worker remains a correct separately gated construction; it is no longer the smallest cycle19 cyclic detector.

**The closest old route already applies to proper cyclic series in quasipolynomial time.** Arvind–Chatterjee–Ghosal–Mukhopadhyay–Ramya, *On Identity Testing and Noncommutative Rank Computation over the Free Skew Field*, ITCS2023, primary publication1February2023, §2.4/Theorem17/PDFpp10–11, states the classical Schützenberger truncation result: an m-state recognizable series is nonzero iff its degree<m truncation is nonzero. The source explicitly reduces that truncated series to a small NC-ABP. Section2.4 was read, as were the introduction and Claim26/PDFp14, which uses a scalar parameter to move a polynomial witness to a defined nonzero rational-series value. Claim26 itself is under an additional hardness premise in that paper; it is not an unconditional polynomial hitlist.

The truncation fact also follows directly. Let V_k be the span of uB_w for |w|≤k. If V_k=V_(k+1), it is invariant under every B_i and contains all reachable rows. Since V_0 has dimension at most1 and the ambient dimension is m, stabilization occurs by k=m−1 (the u0 case is immediately zero). If all those reachable rows annihilate v, the whole series is zero. Hence a nonzero series has a nonzero word of length<m. This proves a truncation bound, not a compact deterministic observation map by itself: enumerating all such words uses exponentially many queries when the alphabet is not unary.

Unroll that degree<m truncation into an acyclic width-O(m), depth-O(m), O(m²)-state ABP. Forbes–Shpilka arXiv1209.2408v2, Construction4.1 and Theorem4.6/Corollary4.7/PDFpp23–26, construct a deterministic **quasipolynomial** hitlist on strictly upper triangular m-square matrices. The underlying evaluated variable matrix has only its first superdiagonal populated; products of m variables vanish. Consequently the **actual proper-series oracle** evaluated on any old hitlist tuple returns exactly its degree<m truncation, with no higher-degree contamination or inverse-domain failure. The older list therefore already gives legal proper cyclic identity testing in (nm)^O(log m) generation/list cost. The m1 case uses only the constant coefficient and zero tuple. Characteristic zero satisfies the primary field-size requirement.

This is a composition of established primitives, rather than a quoted theorem that happens to use precisely our oracle terminology. It also gives the competent old reconstruction control: concatenate all full-matrix responses from the old hitlist as a feature vector, use prefix-chain output queries to extract residual features, then apply Hankel/residual-span synthesis. Every linear combination of left residuals has the same m-state transitions, so the old hitlist is injective on that entire span. Thus the peer's residual-extraction/BFS proof works with the old list too, at quasipolynomial list/feature/query cost and polynomial individual matrix dimensions. The exact matrix service is equally supplied to both methods. The root grid improves this charged deterministic list/feature cost relative to that known route; it does not first establish cyclic reconstruction capability.

Forbes–Shpilka Theorem1.5/PDFp6 already explicitly states output-only NC-ABP reconstruction in randomized polynomial or deterministic quasipolynomial time after implementing membership/equivalence observations. The minimum-state/Hankel synthesis principle is older still. Beimel–Bergadano–Bshouty–Kushilevitz–Varricchio, JACM2000, Theorem3.3/PDFp12 in the retained author manuscript, gives polynomial cyclic multiplicity-automaton learning with **word-value membership and counterexample equivalence queries**. Balle–Mohri's primary author snapshot, *Learning Weighted Automata*, §4.1/PDFpp13–14, states these two oracles explicitly and reconstructs the established basis-growth argument. Its retrieved20-page snapshot has no verified date in its header; no date is inferred from its URL/internal filename. Its §3.5/Theorem2/PDFp9 also requires a complete Hankel basis. A promised existence of some finite basis does not supply that basis.

Neither ordinary polynomial white-box equivalence nor a supplied equivalence-query teacher establishes a hidden-target polynomial matrix-oracle learner. With both linear representations supplied, finite reachable/observable span computations decide equality; here the target transitions are unknown. Word membership is easily compiled into a path-matrix query, but compiling complete equivalence or a faithful residual observation remains the load-bearing step. Random generic matrix evaluations give a randomized polynomial control; derandomizing that compact observation is what the new root grid addresses. An exact counterexample-EQ teacher is a stronger service than an ordinary matrix output and must be charged separately.

A useful corroborating primary boundary is Arvind–Chatterjee–Datta–Mukhopadhyay, *Equivalence Testing of Weighted Automata over Partially Commutative Monoids*, MFCS2021, primary publication18August2021. §2/Theorem2.1/PDFp5 quotes the older quasipolynomial NC-ABP hitlist, and §3/PDFp6 gives the same length≤m−1 recognizable-series criterion. Its main results concern explicitly supplied automata over partially commuting monoids; they are not a hidden single-tape polynomial oracle learner. We read those excerpts and the introduction, not its whole main proof.

The nearest still-missing comparator is an older deterministic polynomial universal **matrix-output** hitlist or learning algorithm for arbitrary hidden proper finite-state noncommutative series, with dimensions, state bound, domain, and exact response costs matching this interface. This check did not find one; it does not rule one out. Stronger membership-plus-counterexample teachers, white-box transitions, sparse word support, large symbolic coefficient fields treated as unit-cost, or a list with quasipolynomial size are not the same cost model.
