Equality is a ratio tending to one in a coupling/resolution double scaling, not an exact finite-E identity of quantum channels. ## Exact locality counterexample to the hard-mode proxy

A literal sharp spatial-momentum restriction is nonlocal. This can be proved without interactions, gauge fixing, eikonal approximations or finite numerical checks.

Take a real free massless scalar in 3+1 Minkowski space, standard canonical normalization, and retain only |p|>E. Write φ_E and π_E=∂_tφ_E for the projected fields. At equal time,

    [φ_E(0,x),π_E(0,y)]
       =i[δ³(x−y)−K_E(|x−y|)],
    K_E(r)=∫_(|p|<E) d³p/(2π)³ e^(ip·(x−y))
          =[sin(Er)−Er cos(Er)]/(2π²r³).             (L1)

Derivation: the canonical oscillator commutator leaves the Fourier kernel of the admitted momenta. Angular integration gives K_E=(1/(2π²r))∫_0^E p sin(pr)dp, yielding (L1). The low-frequency kernel is an ordinary continuous function, with K_E(0)=E³/(6π²). For 0<Er<π it is strictly positive because d(sin u−u cos u)/du=u sin u>0 there.

Choose nonzero nonnegative C_c^∞ spatial smears f,h whose supports are disjoint and whose pairwise separations lie in (0,π/E). Then the delta term vanishes but

    [φ_E(f),π_E(h)]=−i∫f(x)h(y)K_E(|x−y|)d³x d³y ≠0. (L2)

The points are equal-time and spacelike separated. In the original local field theory the same commutator is zero. Smearing avoids all ambiguities about point distributions.

This is also nonuniform on apparatus-sized supports. Fix disjoint dimensionless supports with separations in (0,π), and define

    f_E(x)=E² f(Ex), h_E(x)=E h(Ex).

Then the right side of (L2) is exactly E-independent: the two measures contribute E^−6, the smears E³, and K_E=E³K_1. These are natural dimensionless smears: vacuum φφ covariance scales as E² and ππ as E⁴, so the corresponding smeared variances remain finite and E-independent. The obstruction survives on spatial scales 1/E; it is not an artifact of comparing unnormalized point functions. For fixed undilated supports, instead, the commutator is O(E³) and vanishes as E→0. Those are different limits.

For completeness, away from the light cone, with r>|t|,

    [φ_E(t,x),φ_E(0,0)]
       = i/(4π²r)[sin(E(r−t))/(r−t)−sin(E(r+t))/(r+t)]
       = i E³t/(6π²)+O(E⁵)                          (L3)

at fixed t,r. This follows by subtracting the low-momentum integral from the causal full Pauli–Jordan distribution. It provides a time-separated version of the same obstruction.

Gauge relevance: check_causal independently reconstructed the stronger free-Maxwell test using gauge-invariant magnetic fields. At spacelike separation,

    Σ_i[B_i,E(t,x),B_i,E(0,0)]
       = i/(π²r)∫_0^E k² sin(kt)sin(kr)dk
       = i t E⁵/(5π²)+O(E⁷).                        (L4)

Thus this is not a spurious use of the nonlocal Coulomb-gauge vector potential. Its full local Maxwell counterpart is causal, whereas its strictly hard projected observable is not. The independent worker's saved derivation is a separate source of internal verification; it is not external scientific validation.

Source §4.1's statement that inserting θ(|k|−E) does not alter causality/locality cannot mean exact microcausality of the hard-only theory. Hermitian evolution and algebraic cutting-rule unitarity can still hold. The counterexample **does not refute** analytic inheritance of M_E from the full amplitude, nor prove that the scaling matching (4.2)/(4.10) fails for the exact functionals admitted by the paper. It does prove the failed implication

    exact unitary sharp-mode hard proxy ⇒ exact causal localized finite detector.

The leading scaling result may use only fixed physical supports, in which case (L3)'s violation vanishes. Export to literal detector supports growing like 1/E needs its own uniform theorem.

## Why strict resolution cannot be an exactly localized coupling

Let f∈C_c^∞(R³) describe a localized equal-time linear detector coupling O_f=∫f(x)φ(x)dx. If this operator contains only |p|>E modes, then its Fourier transform f̂(p) vanishes on the open ball |p|<E. Compact support makes f̂ an entire function of the complex momentum components. An entire function vanishing on a real open ball is identically zero: fix the other two real coordinates in a small box and use the one-variable identity theorem, then iterate over the other coordinates. Fourier injectivity gives f=0.

Therefore no nonzero exactly compact localized linear coupling has an exact forbidden open low-momentum band. This is the standard Fourier analyticity/Paley–Wiener obstruction, not a novelty claim. It identifies the physical modeling choice: a local detector can have a response window that strongly suppresses low frequencies, but an exactly sharp band restriction is a nonlocal projection. A cutoff on emitted energy in an outcome POVM can of course be operationally legitimate; it does not require excising low-energy **virtual** modes from the theory.

## Positivity, analytic crossing, and the legal alternative

For a fully specified experiment retain all soft modes and use local apparatus couplings. Let V be the full scattering/detector isometry and C a specified reference isometry, C†C=I. Define V=C+iT and B=C†T. Direct algebra gives

    2 Im_H B=T†T.                                    (P1)

For any detector effect 0≤F_E≤I,

    Q_E=T†F_E T≥0,  Q_E≤2 Im_H B.                    (P2)

This is r03's exact coherent-input optical interface. It needs neither a one-amplitude representation of a reduced channel nor the hard-mode proxy. Actual raw detector counts are V†F_E V; these equal Q_E only when F_E C=0, or when the reference/interference contribution is separately and operationally handled. In long-range scattering C need not be supported exclusively at the forward angle; subtracting a reference is not automatically the detector's dark outcome.

The analytical task is now explicit: construct B as boundary values of a genuinely analytic crossing-compatible reference-relative amplitude, show the detector/test effect transforms into an admitted positive cone under crossing, and bound the full complex contour. Neither (P1), complete positivity of a channel, nor matching diagonal inclusive probabilities gives any of these properties. The cut could contain Q_E plus other nonnegative contributions, enabling a lower-bound dispersion theorem if those analytic assumptions are proved. This is a better route than silently treating δsubsoft or dynamical radiation as a scalar BV correction.

Lippstreu 2505.04702v2 §5 derives reference-relative unitarity for exact Coulomb-background amplitudes with modified disconnected structure, illustrating why the reference matters. Its §6 explains that Coulomb phase and real radiation are branches of a single analytic expression, and that crossing of the infrared-finite full object is a substantive assumption. The source's exactly solvable model omits soft-photon production; it does not by itself close the real-radiation gate. Plestid–Quílez 2606.19432v1 makes a different asymptotic matching construction; its nonrelativistic/static anchors do not establish a uniform full gravity radiation theorem.

A physical coherent-state check also rules out a tempting shortcut. For two distinguishable soft currents, their unresolved-state overlap behaves as exp[−G B log(E/λ)] with B>0 and infrared regulator λ. Removing λ first at any G>0 gives zero overlap; taking G→0 afterward does not restore coherence. The opposite order gives one. Thus the diagonal equality in source (2.6) is not an all-coherent-input channel equality, even when detector scaling is later imposed. This agrees with the source's own footnote17 and established soft-decoherence work. It does not invalidate correctly prepared dressed full-Hilbert-space amplitudes.

Independent analytical reconstruction of r07's proposed stronger shock-fixture obstruction follows. Let U(g)=Γ(1−ig)/Γ(1+ig), a=2log(q/E), y=2e^(a/2)=2q/E, and

    K(a)=∫_0^∞ Im[U(g)e^(iag)]dg/g.

    M_J0(s)y^−s=−U(g)e^(iag)/(2ig).

Pairing the ±g pieces produces −K/π. Moving the Mellin line to Re s=0 contributes half the pole at s=0, yielding

    J0(y)=1/2−K(a)/π,
    K(a)=π/2−πJ0(2q/E).                              (A1)

The integral is a genuinely truncated-limit oscillatory integral; it does not have an absolutely convergent Mellin endpoint integral at g=0. Its imaginary part divided by g has a finite limit at zero. In a Mellin-line approach, taking 0<c<c0<1 permits a uniform oscillatory tail O(R^(c0−1)/logR), while the simple pole supplies the half-residue.
