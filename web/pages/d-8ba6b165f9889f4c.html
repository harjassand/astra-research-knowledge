# Loss and herald complexity: interface audit and verdict

## Verdict

The evidence supports a **principled rejection of the generic transfer** “vanishing loss certificate + tiny unconditional error + bounded conditional energy implies an efficient conditional sampler.” It does not establish a general inverse-herald-probability runtime lower bound or a complexity separation. The strongest candidate sampling route in this family remains N42: an inverse-success-free sampler for finite-energy Gaussian photon heralds whose Bargmann kernel is entrywise nonnegative, conditional on an exact-zero and relative-counting FPRAS for unweighted perfect matchings in general graphs. The imported counting theorem is a new, highly consequential preprint claim; the associated reduction and package have not received independent mathematical review. N64 improves a different resource—the conditional photon-number tail—and cannot supply that counting primitive.

N81–83 give a sharper obstruction to broadening the sampler promise. Under a specified known-rational pure-Gaussian, uniform-vacuum-loss, binary-herald interface, a constant-TV conditional sampler with polynomial bit runtime would imply `PP = BPP` (and the stated quantum analogue `PP = BQP`). This is a conditional implication, not a separation, and it does not prove that runtime must scale as `1/p_herald`. N100–108 concern fermionic loss/resource behavior and do not transfer to bosonic photon-herald sampling.

For N45, fixed photon-number conditioning remains a real reduction gap. Ordinary perfect-matching counts and the constant-fibre size-k matching reduction do not encode “exactly k selected vertices” in the doubled-graph construction: there this is a prescribed number of designated vertical edges, i.e. a colored-edge count. A newer all-graph variable-cardinality graph-GBS sampler is not a fix: its theorem has arithmetic-time rather than finite-bit guarantees and does not sample after exact cardinality conditioning or resolve rare pins.

### N42’s positive-kernel counting route

The proposed route represents the conditional count law through nonnegative coefficients of a Gaussian generating kernel. Positive marginals preserve the nonnegative-coefficient structure. Hafnian/loop-hafnian weights are converted to unweighted perfect-matching counts with gadgets; exact zero detection avoids treating an impossible event as a relative-approximation instance. The claimed work is polynomial in the finite-bit input and the numerical `h,E,ε⁻¹`, not in `1/p_herald`.

The load-bearing external input is the pinned OpenAI release family 113 manuscript, which claims an FPRAS for the number of perfect matchings in arbitrary simple graphs with relative error, exact-zero handling, and worst-case polynomial bit complexity. Its §10.1 also gives size-`k` uncolored matching/f-factor reductions. This would close an old major gap if correct: the 2017 Štefankovič–Vigoda–Wilmes paper explicitly says an FPRAS for general-graph perfect matchings remained open. The contrast makes the claim important; it does not validate it. The release's Lean scope documentation is not a substitute for an independent proof audit or a build/replay here. N42 remains conditional and unverified, not an established sampler.

### N82 filter lemma and what it does not buy

For a normalized Fock-space state/channel, let `p₀>0` be the probability of a specified loss-free herald sector of total photon number `h`. Apply the exact number filter `F_g=g^{N_H}` and then pure loss `η` on the herald modes. N82's stated bound is trace distance at most `2g²/p₀` between the conditional retained state and the normalized no-loss `k`-sector state; the dependence on `p₀` is sharp in general. A supplied lower bound such as `p₀≥2⁻q` permits choosing a rational `g` so the conditional approximation is small with polynomial representation length. This accounts for a **known** lower bound on the target branch before filtering; it does not derive a useful lower bound on an unknown final herald.

The physical success bound still contains `ηʰ g^(2h) p₀/Z_g`. Thus an efficient ideal conditional sampler plus this filter is not an efficient physical sampler. The lemma lets the N81 reduction control conditional-state error despite small loss; it does not make the postselected event common. N83 also correctly isolates a separate issue: trace distance alone cannot bound an unbounded photon-number observable, so the energy estimates need direct moment/tail work such as N64.

## Strongest obstruction and decisive failed transfer

N81 re-encodes a PostBQP decision bit in an `R`-photon two-rail state. Fanout copies the *classical decision basis bit*, not an arbitrary unknown quantum state. Balanced bunchers map the rails into `|R,0⟩` or `|0,R⟩`; the readout probability has a constant bias proportional to the PostBQP gap. The KLM/postselection and loss-filter budgets are selected so fixed-TV sampler error cannot erase this gap. This yields the implication in the table. It remains conditional on the reduction's correctness and the usual consequence is “such a sampler would collapse PP to BPP,” not “such a sampler provably cannot exist.”

That is enough to reject the proposed *transfer argument*: none of (a) an arbitrarily small one-particle loss certificate, (b) exponentially small unconditional TV distance to vacuum, or (c) constant bounded conditional energy, supplies an algorithm for the conditional distribution. The constructed family has all three properties and still places the assumed sampler behind a PP-collapse implication. The qualifier matters: unless `PP ≠ BPP` is assumed, it is not a theorem that the sampler is hard; and no inverse-success-probability lower bound is proved.

N64 should not be overread in the opposite direction. It proves or claims a useful probability-free conditional *energy* bound for a broad physical class. It does not change amplitudes' phase cancellation, reveal the exact normalizer/conditional weights, or eliminate the counting problem. N44 supplies a narrow bridge for small, gauge-referenced phases in count-TV with quadratic-`θ` dependence. N64's separate state-level result allows displacement and controls subsequent channels, but its linear-`θ` bound still needs the specified positive pure reference and `θ[h+6(h+1)(1+E)]` to be small; neither result reaches arbitrary phases.

## N45: multiplicative estimation is not sampling

For an even-vertex graph with adjacency `Q`, the reported construction uses `B=Q/(n+1)` and gives

`Pr(all n modes contain one photon) = sqrt(det(I−B²)) (n+1)^(−n) #PM(Q)²`.

With the prefactor known and controlled, multiplicative estimation of this probability is equivalent to relative PM-count estimation on the family. This is a rare-event estimation equivalence, not an ordinary-TV sampler theorem: TV error `ε` places no useful relative guarantee on an event with probability far below `ε`.

For the doubled graph, pins on collision-free inclusion/exclusion events can encode a variable-total event. If one conditions on exactly `|S|=k`, however, the matching construction must prescribe how many vertical edges are selected. OA113 §10.1 concerns the total number of edges in an uncolored matching and does not provide a color-count constraint. Equating these would silently change the problem. The gap is an interface obstruction, not a hardness proof.

Anand et al. (arXiv:2511.16558) later state an all-graph sampler for the variable-cardinality graph-GBS distribution `μ(S) ∝ c^(2|S|) PM(G[S])²` in arithmetic time. This is useful primary-source context but does not provide bit-complexity guarantees for the present reduction, an exact fixed-`|S|` conditional sampler, or cost control for a rare pinned event.

## Final gate and next decisive test

The central open question is whether there is an algorithm for bosonic photon-count sampling with arbitrary complex phases and rare heralds under a finite-bit interface that avoids the N81 conditional PP-collapse candidate, or a stronger promise that blocks its reduction. The best bounded directions are: preserve nonnegative/gauge-positive Bargmann structure (N42); give a nonnegligible-herald or certified conditional-weight promise and charge it; or prove a quantitative bounded-phase theorem with its phase-description cost included (N44/N64 direction). N64's energy control can bound a photon cutoff once the energy scale is charged, but cannot substitute for any of these acquisition promises.

The next decisive validation of current candidates is two-part: independently check OA113's theorem and then audit the N42 hafnian/log-weight gadgets, zero handling, and bit-cost composition against that exact theorem interface. For N81, independently review the reduction and its postselection, source-rounding, filter, and retained-state TV budgets. Re-running the finite scripts alone would not resolve either gate.
