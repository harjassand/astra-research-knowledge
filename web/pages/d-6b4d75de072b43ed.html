SPARSE PREDICTIVE-OBSERVABLE DISCOVERY BY ADAPTIVE GROUP TESTS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: established group-testing bounds specialized to a restrictive predictive-correlation interface; finite-sample realization derived here; no novelty or broad teacher-free acquisition claim.

RESULT

For a known finite dictionary of N centered orthonormal predictive observables, suppose the target conditional mean has exactly s nonzero coefficients, all with known positive orientation and magnitude at least gamma. A scalar pooled-correlation query on a subset A has population mean zero exactly when A misses the support. Thresholding it therefore gives a legal group-test bit from ordinary observed (context, next-outcome) samples; no teacher supplies feature labels or the support.

In the exact one-bit oracle, an elementary adaptive splitting tree identifies every s-subset in Q <= 1 + 2 sum_{d=0}^{ceil(log2 N)-1} min(2^d,s) = O(s(1+log(N/s))) queries. Fano/counting gives Q >= (1-delta) log2 binom(N,s)-h2(delta) for success probability 1-delta. For 1 <= s <= N/2, these match up to constants because log2 binom(N,s) is between s log2(N/s) and s log2(eN/s). This is the standard adaptive group-testing order, not a new group-testing theorem.

For the empirical-correlation realization, one test on a pool A needs O(B^2 |A| gamma^-2 log(Q/delta)) independent transitions when |Y|<=B. Across the splitting tree this gives

  M_train = O((B^2/gamma^2) [N(1+log(s+1)) + s(1+log(N/s))] log(Q/delta))

independent transitions, with probability at least 1-delta. This is an upper bound for fresh batches per adaptive test, not a minimax sample bound. A raw-trajectory learner can estimate all N singleton correlations from the same batch in O((B^2/gamma^2) log(N/delta)) transitions, at O(N) feature-evaluation work per transition. Therefore the group-test query-count saving is not itself a sample-efficiency or end-to-end acquisition advantage when all raw features are accessible.

1. ACCESS CONTRACT AND PREDICTIVE MEANING

Let X be the observed context and Y a naturally observed next-symbol indicator, reward, or other scalar future target. The finite dictionary phi_1,...,phi_N is supplied as executable functions of X. It is centered and orthonormal under the sampling distribution:

  E[phi_i(X)] = 0,       E[phi_i(X) phi_j(X)] = 1{i=j}.

Assume the conditional mean is exactly in its span,

  E[Y | X] = a_0 + sum_{j=1}^N theta_j phi_j(X),

with unknown support S={j: theta_j != 0}, |S|=s, and a known sign orientation such that theta_j >= gamma > 0 for j in S; theta_j=0 off S. Also assume |Y|<=B. The sign/margin promise is essential. If signs are unknown, pooled sums can cancel; e.g. theta_i=gamma and theta_j=-gamma make the pool {i,j} look empty. Orthonormality makes the coefficients meaningful conditional-mean coordinates; without it, a nonzero covariance is only association and correlated dictionary elements can alias.

For any chosen subset A, form the scalar observable

  Z_A(X,Y) = Y sum_{j in A} phi_j(X),       mu(A)=E[Z_A]=sum_{j in A} theta_j.

The mean identity follows by orthonormality. Thus mu(A)=0 iff A misses S, and mu(A)>=gamma otherwise. The legal measurement is the scalar empirical mean from fresh observed transitions. It asks no human or teacher for the answer and never requests S. It does assume access to a named finite dictionary and a stationary/i.i.d. sampling source for fresh transitions. It does not discover an unenumerated feature, nor does it solve arbitrary HMM/trajectory prediction.

The exact group-test abstraction is the population-bit map b(A)=1{mu(A)>0}. It is a useful query model for deriving the combinatorial bound. An exact population mean is not free in a sampled environment; the finite-batch implementation and its sample bill appear below. If the deployment exposes only arbitrary passive trajectory strings and does not permit pooled feature evaluation, even this query interface is unavailable.

2. ADAPTIVE QUERY UPPER BOUND AND INFORMATION LOWER BOUND

Assume N>=2 and 1<=s<=N/2. Split each candidate set into two nearly equal children. Query the left child of every known-positive internal node. If it is negative, the right child is known positive from the parent; if it is positive, query the right child too. Recurse on positive children and stop at singletons. For an unknown-size model, query the root once; if s is known positive, its outcome is already known and that first query can be omitted.

At depth d there are at most min(2^d,s) positive nodes, and at most two child queries per such node. With D=ceil(log2 N),

  Q <= 1 + 2 sum_{d=0}^{D-1} min(2^d,s).

Writing m=ceil(log2 s), the sum through depth m is O(s); each later level contributes at most s, for D-m=O(1+log2(N/s)) levels. Hence Q=O(s(1+log2(N/s))). This tree is a simple worst-case guarantee; Hwang's generalized binary splitting gives the sharper established bound log2 binom(N,s)+s for its stated known-s setting.

For a lower bound, choose S uniformly from the M=binom(N,s) possible supports. Each noiseless group-test answer is one bit, so Q answers have mutual information at most Q bits, even when each query depends on all earlier answers. If the decoder errs with probability at most delta, Fano gives

  Q >= [log2 M - h2(delta) - delta log2(M-1)]
       >= (1-delta) log2 M - h2(delta).

At zero error, the decision tree must have at least M leaves and Q>=ceil(log2 M). Since

  s log2(N/s) <= log2 binom(N,s) <= s log2(eN/s),

the adaptive query count is optimal to constant factors for sparse support. This is an information/query lower bound for binary OR responses, not a sample lower bound for raw trajectories or for arbitrary real-valued linear measurements.

Noisy one-bit version. If each true bit is independently flipped with probability rho<1/2, a query conveys at most 1-h2(rho) bits. Fano gives

  Q >= [log2 M - h2(delta) - delta log2(M-1)]/[1-h2(rho)].

A simple finite-confidence upper is to repeat each of the Q0 noiseless-tree decisions r times and take majority, with

  r >= log(Q0/delta) / [2(1/2-rho)^2],
  Q_noisy <= Q0 r.

This has an extra log(Q0/delta) reliability factor. It is not the sharp noisy adaptive result. Scarlett's noisy adaptive paper gives stronger two/three-stage achievability and converse results; exact limits depend on sparsity scaling and noise channel. Do not claim the elementary repetition bound matches the noisy information lower bound.

3. FINITE-PRECISION POOLED-CORRELATION IMPLEMENTATION

For every A, orthonormality and bounded Y give

  Var(Z_A) <= E[Z_A^2]
             <= B^2 E[(sum_{j in A} phi_j(X))^2]
             = B^2 |A|.

Use a median-of-means estimate from a fresh batch for each adaptively selected pool, and report positive iff the estimate exceeds gamma/2. For a universal constant C, a batch of

  n_A = ceil(C B^2 |A| gamma^-2 log(2Q/delta))

transitions makes the conditional error probability at that query at most delta/Q. Fresh samples make this statement valid after conditioning on all earlier adaptive choices; a union bound makes every test bit correct with probability at least 1-delta.

For the balanced split tree, let W=sum over queried pools |A|. At depth d, each queried child has size at most ceil(N/2^(d+1)); there are at most 2 min(2^d,s) such queries. Summing and splitting at d=ceil(log2 s) yields

  W = O(N(1+log(s+1)) + s(1+log(N/s))).

The ceiling in n_A adds O(Q) samples, already covered by this expression. Therefore

  M_train = sum_A n_A
          = O((B^2/gamma^2) [N(1+log(s+1)) + s(1+log(N/s))]
             log(2Q/delta)).

The logarithms in this sample expression can use any fixed base; constants change. The precision dependence gamma^-2 is unavoidable for estimating a small mean gap under ordinary finite-variance noise in standard two-point settings, but this note does not prove a matching sample lower bound for the pooled adaptive trajectory model. The support entropy lower bound above counts one-bit responses, not independent trajectories. The increasing |A| variance is exactly why query count and trajectory count must be kept separate.

Further costs:

* A naive implementation evaluates each phi_j in every queried pool on each batch example. Its feature-evaluation count is proportional to (B^2/gamma^2) log(Q/delta) sum_A |A|^2, besides the cost to obtain the transitions. Computing a pool sum once does not remove the need to evaluate its constituent features unless the dictionary has exploitable structure.
* If coefficient values are also needed for prediction, after support discovery estimate the s selected moments on fresh data. To get coefficient-vector Euclidean error at most epsilon with coordinatewise median-of-means bounds, O(B^2 s epsilon^-2 log(s/delta)) transitions suffice; selected-feature evaluation costs O(s) per transition. This is separate from support discovery.
* Independent held-out validation of the selected support can estimate all N correlations from one raw batch: O(B^2 gamma^-2 log(N/delta)) transitions, by median-of-means and a union bound. This certifies the support only under the stated sparse, orthonormal, sign/margin model. For a held-out predictive-risk comparison, clip predictions/losses to a known bound L and use O(L^2 Delta^-2 log(1/delta)) fresh target-task examples to distinguish a risk gap Delta. Validation data and feature evaluations are charged; passing an in-class correlation check is not a general process-equivalence proof.

4. ADAPTIVE VERSUS FIXED QUERY DESIGNS

For noiseless OR queries, adaptive designs can use each result to skip empty subtrees. Their worst-case query count above is within a constant factor of support entropy. Fixed/nonadaptive designs commit every pool before seeing results and enable parallel execution. The information lower bound applies to both, but the best fixed query count depends on the sparsity regime and whether one asks for worst-case or high-probability recovery.

A primary nonadaptive result with a uniformly random fixed-size support and vanishing error gives the threshold, up to lower-order terms,

  min{ max[s log2(N/s), s log2(s)/ln 2], N }.

Bay, Price and Scarlett prove matching achievability and converse, with polynomial-time decoding. When s<=N^(1-c) for fixed c>0, this is order s log N, the same order as adaptive s log(N/s). In mildly sublinear regimes, the fixed threshold can be order N while adaptive generalized binary splitting remains order s log(N/s). For example, s=N/log N gives adaptive O(N loglog N/log N) versus fixed order N. Near-dense supports also erase the pooling benefit; one-by-one testing becomes optimal in the cited nonadaptive model above its stated density threshold. These are source-model query-count comparisons, not episode-cost comparisons for empirical correlations.

For symmetric BSC noise, both adaptive and nonadaptive methods pay reliability overhead; capacity-style lower bounds divide support entropy by 1-h2(rho). Nonadaptive noisy methods are easier to run in parallel, while adaptive methods can exploit feedback and sometimes improve constants substantially at low noise. Scarlett (2019) gives explicit two/three-stage adaptive schemes, notes regimes where nonadaptive methods already attain the converse, and proves an additional Omega(s log s) converse for symmetric noise. This does not establish one universal adaptivity gap for every sparsity/noise scaling.

A raw-sample comparator is stronger than an OR-test matrix in the proposed process interface. Given each raw (X,Y), one can compute Y phi_j(X) for every j, then estimate all N moments from the same sample batch. With the same bounded-variance and margin assumptions, O(B^2 gamma^-2 log(N/delta)) transitions identify support by simultaneous singleton confidence intervals, at O(N) feature evaluations per transition. For s=1 this can use logarithmically many transitions, while the fresh-pool splitting realization above uses O(N) transitions. The costs differ: the scan emits/retains N statistics per batch and performs O(N) feature work; the adaptive scalar method serializes pools and can incur repeated sampling. Therefore no efficiency advantage follows from the adaptive query count alone. A fair experiment must compare the same raw data, dictionary, transition budget, computation, memory, and validation set against this retaining/coordinate-scan baseline.

5. LATER-TASK TRANSFER EXAMPLE AND FULL COST LEDGER

Example: A controlled process has a fixed transition kernel and a supplied finite bank of centered context/action observables. The target is the probability of the next observation symbol. From prior transitions, the learner discovers a sparse set of context features and estimates their coefficients. A later task changes the reward or goal condition but keeps the same observation/action encoding and transition law. The stored support and transition predictor can seed next-observation prediction immediately; the new reward coefficients or policy still need acquisition. Transfer is valid only under this explicit invariance promise. If the later task changes the dynamics, support, context distribution, or feature semantics, revalidate there and permit rejection/relearning.

Store and charge:

* dictionary program/feature definitions: L(D) bits, unless supplied as common input;
* support: ceil(log2 binom(N,s)) bits for an enumerative code, or s ceil(log2 N) bits for a simple index list;
* coefficient grid: O(s log2(1+Gamma/epsilon)) bits for coefficients in [gamma,Gamma] to resolution epsilon; rational exact encoding charges numerator/denominator bit lengths;
* query audit: Q outcomes plus queried node addresses, at most O(Q log N) bits in a literal transcript, or a smaller implicit tree if reproducible from code and outcomes;
* learner/decoder code and hyperparameters, training and held-out transitions, feature evaluation, coefficient estimation, and later-task validation;
* retained raw trajectories if the baseline stores them, and any future execution cost to evaluate the predictor.

If a reusable acquired package costs C_acq (all discovery, coding and source validation) and later tasks each cost C_base from scratch versus C_reuse to load/evaluate/revalidate, the nominal break-even task count is C_acq/(C_base-C_reuse) when C_base>C_reuse. This arithmetic is meaningful only after the same-data retaining baseline and task-shift validation costs are included. No amortized gain is established here.

6. FAILURE CONDITIONS AND CLAIM STATUS

The group-test reduction fails if active coefficient signs can cancel, the minimum nonzero correlation has no known positive margin, the observable functions are not supplied/enumerable, the target is not represented by the declared dictionary, or the sampling distribution drifts so the support/covariance changes. The finite-sample result assumes fresh independent transitions; mixing-time or dependent-trajectory guarantees need additional concentration hypotheses. A support of correlations may also miss predictive effects that are nonlinear or conditionally useful but have zero marginal covariance.

Established results: adaptive generalized binary splitting, group-testing entropy converses, nonadaptive threshold results, and noisy group-testing bounds are prior art. The tree bound and finite-batch pooled-correlation reduction are elementary derivations in this note. The predictive-observable interpretation and transfer example are a scoped application proposal, not a demonstrated representation-acquisition advantage. External correctness review, novelty/priority search in predictive-system identification, and empirical matched-baseline results remain UNKNOWN.
