EXACT PROOF AND CERTIFICATE INTERPRETATION

1. Contract and exact convolution kernel

Write Z=sigma_z, X=sigma_x. For a,b in {-1,+1}, put

  W_ab = (I+aZ+bX)/4.

Then J(s,t)=sum_ab exp(i(as+bt)) W_ab. Consequently the only finite
operator-valued measure with Fourier characteristic nu_hat J is

  E(B) = (1/4) sum_ab nu(B-(a,b)) (I+aZ+bX).                 (1)

Its total mass is I. Thus the full contract is exactly positivity of
(1) on every Borel set, equivalently positive definiteness of its full
Fourier characteristic. No pointwise Hermitian-positivity interpretation
of the complex matrix J is being used.

On one noise lattice coset, translate the noise to 2Z^2 and let
q_ij=nu({(2i,2j)}). At output (2i+1,2j+1), set

  a=q_ij, b=q_(i+1,j), c=q_(i,j+1), d=q_(i+1,j+1),
  S=a+b+c+d,
  Zc=a-b+c-d,
  Xc=a+b-c-d.

The corresponding matrix in (1) is (S I+Zc Z+Xc X)/4. Orthogonality of
the two Pauli matrices gives its eigenvalues (S +/- sqrt(Zc^2+Xc^2))/4.
The exact lattice condition is therefore

  q_ij>=0, sum_ij q_ij=1,
  S>=sqrt(Zc^2+Xc^2) for every (i,j) in Z^2.                (2)

This is an infinite family of local second-order cone inequalities.

2. Exact finite dual certificates imply bounds on the infinite lattice

For any exact rational unit vector u=(u_z,u_x), condition (2) implies

  A_(i,j,u)(q)=u_z Zc+u_x Xc-S <= 0.                       (3)

Let finitely many nonnegative rational multipliers y_(i,j,u) be given.
For a prescribed center c=(c_x,c_y), define at every noise node n=(i,j)

  d_n = |(2i,2j)-c|^2
        + sum_(cells,u) y_(cells,u) [coefficient of q_n in A_(cells,u)].

If d_n>=L at every n, multiplying by q_n and summing proves

  E_q |(2i,2j)-c|^2 >= L,                                 (4)

because the summed correction is sum y A(q)<=0. This implication uses
only (2), and it applies to every infinite-support feasible q with a
finite second moment.

The adaptive_dual_K*_c*.json files supply the actual multipliers. Each
row has the form

  [i, j, u_z, u_x, y].

Every coefficient is an exact rational string. The verifier checks
u_z^2+u_x^2=1 and y>=0, reconstructs all four coefficients in (3), and
checks d_n>=L with Fraction arithmetic.

For a file with parameter K the multipliers are supported on cells
-K-1<=i,j<=K. Thus their correction can only touch noise nodes
-K-1<=i,j<=K+1. The verifier checks every such node. Outside this box the
correction is identically zero, and

  |(2i,2j)-c|^2
    >= min{(2K+4-|c_x|)^2, (2K+4-|c_y|)^2} >= L.

This last inequality handles all omitted nodes, rather than truncating
their probabilities. The multiplier inequalities are the proof; the
floating-point optimization that suggested the multipliers is not used
by the verifier.

4. All continuous and singular noise measures are covered

Take the measurable quotient map pi:R^2 -> R^2/(2Z^2), represented by
r in [0,2)^2. Every probability measure nu admits a regular conditional
disintegration

  nu(B)=integral sum_(i,j) q_ij(r) 1_B(r+(2i,2j)) tau(dr),

where q(r) is a probability law on Z^2 for tau-almost every r. The four
shifts (a,b) all send the same input coset to the same output coset,
because their pairwise differences lie in 2Z^2. Disintegrating (1)
therefore gives exactly the matrices of Section 1 on each coset.

Positivity of (1) implies (2) for tau-almost every r. This follows by
testing positivity on the measurable output coset fibers and on a
countable dense family of vectors in C^2; there are only countably many
lattice cells. Conversely, conditional positivity implies positivity
of (1) by integration. No density with respect to Lebesgue measure is
required anywhere.

Finite covariance of nu implies finite conditional second moments for
almost every r. Apply (6) to each conditional law; translating a coset
does not change its covariance. The law of total covariance gives

  trace Cov(nu)
    = integral trace Cov(nu|r) tau(dr)
      + integral |E[N|r]-E[N]|^2 tau(dr)
    >= L.                                                 (7)

This proves the stated lower bound under the complete quantifiers of
the assignment.

5. Explicit infinite dependent upper law

For an integer N and a rational 0<rho<1, let p_ab=p_ba>=0, 0<=a,b<=N,
and define unnormalized lattice masses

  q_mn = rho^(|m|+|n|)
         p_(min(|m|,N),min(|n|,N)).                        (8)

Normalize by Zq=sum q_mn, and put the noise at the physical points
(2m,2n). Reflection symmetry makes the mean and cross-covariance zero.
The geometric tail makes all moments finite.

For positive-output cells i,j>=0, factor rho^(i+j) out of the four
corner masses. The remaining four values are

  a=p_(clamp i,clamp j),
  b=rho p_(clamp(i+1),clamp j),
  c=rho p_(clamp i,clamp(j+1)),
  d=rho^2 p_(clamp(i+1),clamp(j+1)),

where clamp k=min(k,N). If i>N or j>N, the clamped pattern is already
represented at i=N or j=N. Reflections and exchange cover the remaining
cells. Therefore every infinite positivity condition is equivalent to
the finite types 0<=j<=i<=N. Exact rational S^2-Zc^2-Xc^2>=0 and S>=0
checks on those types prove full positivity of the actual infinite law.

The file certified_upper_N10_mixture.json gives N=10, rho=1/5 and all
66 rational entries of p. It was formed by a rational convex mixture of
two guide tables; its validity is determined by the final exact checks,
not by the feasibility of either guide table. The exact checker also
verifies p00*p11 != p10^2, so its two coordinates are dependent.

For completeness, exact normalization and moment formulas are as
follows. For 0<=i<=N define

  D_0=1, D_i=2 for 0<i<N, D_N=2/(1-rho),
  T_0=0, T_i=2i^2 for 0<i<N,
  T_N=2[N^2/(1-rho)+2N rho/(1-rho)^2
          +rho(1+rho)/(1-rho)^3].

Then, summing i,j over 0,...,N,

  Zq = sum rho^(i+j) p_ij D_i D_j,
  Cq = 4 sum rho^(i+j) p_ij (T_i D_j+D_i T_j),
  trace Cov(nu) = Cq/Zq.

The saved table gives exactly

  Cq/Zq = 3911259954946061065911894562429
          /1231364609261368746876606814187
        = 3.1763621639996797... < 3.1763622.                (9)

Combining (7) and (9) proves the reported interval.

6. Compact handwritten dependence counterexample to the bound 4

This simpler law does not give the narrow upper bound, but its six
weights make the strict improvement over 4 directly checkable. Let
N=2 and rho=1/5. For m,n in Z define

  q_mn = w_(min(|m|,2),min(|n|,2))
         rho^((|m|-2)_+ + (|n|-2)_+),

with symmetric table

  w00=1, w10=3/20, w11=1/20,
  w20=1/500, w21=9/1000, w22=1/500.

The same tail-pattern argument reduces all positivity tests to six
types. Their exact determinants S^2-Zc^2-Xc^2 are

  (0,0): 7/400,
  (1,0): 151/1000000,
  (1,1): 73/250000,
  (2,0): 41/1562500,
  (2,1): 41/1562500,
  (2,2): 9/9765625.

They are strictly positive. Its normalizing mass is 153/80 and its
unnormalized physical trace second moment is 2819/400. Thus

  trace Cov(nu) = 2819/765 = 3.6849673202614377... < 4.

Also w00*w11 != w10^2, proving dependence directly.

7. Optional entirely analytic universal lower bound without a table

Let N be mean-zero noise with law nu and let S be independent uniform
on {(+/-1,+/-1)}. Put V=N+S and r(V)=E[S|V]. Formula (1) is positive
exactly when |r(V)|<=1 almost surely. Consequently

  2=E[V dot r(V)] <= E|V| = E g(N),
  g(n)=(1/4)sum_s |n+s|.

Writing t=|n|^2, concavity of

  H_K(w)=sqrt(2K+2sqrt(K^2-4w)), K=t+2,

and (n_x+n_y)^2+(n_x-n_y)^2=2t gives

  g(n) <= f(t)=sqrt((t+2+sqrt(t^2+4))/2).

For z=f(t)>=sqrt(2),

  t=z^2(z^2-2)/(z^2-1),
  (f(t)-sqrt(2))/t=(z^2-1)/(z^2(z+sqrt(2))).

The last ratio has its unique maximum at w>sqrt(2) satisfying
w^3-3w-2sqrt(2)=0. Explicitly

  w=(sqrt(2)+1)^(1/3)+(sqrt(2)-1)^(1/3),
  beta=max ratio=2/w^3.

Therefore g(n)<=sqrt(2)+beta |n|^2 and

  trace Cov(nu) >= (2-sqrt(2))*w^3/2
                 approximately 2.66217523319253.

This weaker bound and the compact upper example require no search
certificate. The much stronger main interval uses exact finite rational
certificates together with the analytic infinite-lattice, all-mean,
and all-coset extensions above.

8. Existence and unfinished sharp value

The infimum defining C_* is attained. Center a minimizing sequence.
Its bounded second moments imply tightness; along a weakly convergent
subsequence the first moments remain zero and the second moment is
lower semicontinuous. Positivity of (1) is closed under weak convergence
because every nonnegative continuous compactly supported scalar test
function and every vector in C^2 have nonnegative expectation under the
approximating positive measures. The weak limit is therefore feasible
and attains the infimum.

Neither an exact symbolic value of that minimum nor an exact optimizer
is established by these certificates. The interval is the proved claim.
