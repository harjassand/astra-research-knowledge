# An inverse-catalecticant metric, and an exact counting obstruction

Worker `metric_geometry10`, 7 October 2026. Assignment: an independent coefficient-only alternative to the cycle9 Hessian-flow detector. **Partial advance, with a proved obstruction to this particular route.** No universal polynomial-bit hidden-axis algorithm is established here. No historical priority claim is made.

The surviving conclusions are:

* On every odd-dimensional QPD input with all hidden off-diagonal entries nonzero and of a common sign, the inverse middle catalecticant acquires a rational aligned SPD metric by an exterior double-form power. No metric, axes, or hidden height/condition bound is supplied.
* Exact evaluation of this specified metric map is #P-hard in variable dimension, even if its positive scalar normalization is free. This hardness holds on visible-coordinate positive-coupling QPD inputs. It is **not** hardness of acquiring any aligned metric or frame; on those visible inputs the identity metric is already aligned.
* In every even dimension at least two there are rational QPD inputs with every off-diagonal entry positive and an irrational real symmetry that forbids any rational SPD metric invariant under the full real stabilizer. Thus a universally natural rational metric selector cannot simply replace symmetry-breaking acquisition, even when the catalecticant and its curvature tensor are nonsingular and definite.
* The simplest inverse-catalecticant endomorphism is identically scalar for every nonsingular middle catalecticant, and therefore supplies no spectral axes.

## Exact interface and assumptions

Input: the expanded rational coefficients of a homogeneous quartic

\[
f(x)=((Lx)^2)^TQ((Lx)^2),\qquad L\in GL_n(\mathbb Q),\quad Q=Q^T\succ0,
\]

with variable dimension n. Neither L, Q, hidden axes nor an aligned metric is input. Our positive metric theorem additionally assumes odd n at least three and either Q_ij>0 for every i!=j, or Q_ij<0 for every i!=j. The algorithm detects that additional condition from the coefficient-derived curvature form, under the QPD promise. Failure is UNKNOWN for this route, not nonmembership in the full promise.

Output: a rational SPD matrix M satisfying L^{-T}ML^{-1}=positive diagonal. This is an aligned metric, rather than a recovered frame. An existing correctly charged supplied-aligned-metric axis procedure can be composed afterwards. The present executable acquires and verifies the metric; it does not silently receive or reuse fixture axes at its learner interface.

## 1. Normalization of the quartic and its middle catalecticant

Let T be the fully symmetric rational four-tensor with f(x)=T_ijkl x_i x_j x_k x_l, summing ordered indices. Each polynomial coefficient is divided by its multinomial multiplicity. In hidden y=Lx coordinates,

\[
T_{iiii}=Q_{ii},\qquad T_{iijj}=Q_{ij}/3\quad(i\ne j),
\]

and all other parity patterns vanish.

Define the middle catalecticant C as the bilinear form on symmetric contravariant matrices,

\[
C(A,B)=T_{ijkl}A_{ij}B_{kl}.
\]

In an orthonormal symmetric-matrix basis E_ii and (E_ij+E_ji)/sqrt(2), its hidden-coordinate blocks are

\[
B=\frac{2\operatorname{diag}(Q_{ii})+Q}{3}\quad\text{on the diagonal matrices},
\qquad 2Q_{ij}/3\quad\text{on each off-diagonal direction}.
\]

B is positive definite. Therefore C is nonsingular **if and only if every Q_ij with i!=j is nonzero**. This equivalence is invariant under arbitrary invertible L. Building C and deciding its nonsingularity are polynomial-bit rational linear algebra in dimension n(n+1)/2.

If C is nonsingular, let U^{ijkl} be its inverse tensor, normalized by

\[
T_{ijab}U^{abkl}=\tfrac12(\delta_i^k\delta_j^l+\delta_i^l\delta_j^k).
\]

In hidden coordinates its nonzero entries include

\[
U^{iijj}=(B^{-1})_{ij},\qquad
U^{ijij}=U^{ijji}=3/(4Q_{ij})\quad(i\ne j).
\]

These two sorts of entries must not be identified: U is symmetric within its two pairs and between the pairs, but generally is not a fully symmetric four-tensor.

## 2. Why the first endomorphism is identically scalar

Let U_sym be the full symmetrization of U. The natural candidate endomorphism is

\[
A_i{}^j=T_{iabc}(U_{\rm sym})^{jabc}.
\]

Because T is fully symmetric in a,b,c, symmetrizing U does not change that contraction. The defining inverse identity, with its two free pair indices contracted, gives

\[
A_i{}^j=\sum_c T_{icab}U^{jcab}
=\tfrac12\sum_c(\delta_i^j\delta_c^c+\delta_i^c\delta_c^j)
=\boxed{\tfrac{n+1}{2}\delta_i^j}.
\]

This holds for every fully symmetric quartic tensor with nonsingular C, regardless of QPD. Computing this contraction and hoping for a simple spectrum cannot acquire an axis. Higher tensor networks are not excluded by this identity.

## 3. A coefficient-derived curvature form whose signs are controlled by QPD

Antisymmetrize the inverse tensor:

\[
R^{ijkl}=U^{ikjl}-U^{iljk}.
\]

It is a symmetric bilinear form on the second exterior power, with the usual algebraic-curvature pair symmetries. All of its hidden-coordinate off-diagonal entries on the basis e_i wedge e_j vanish, and

\[
R^{ijij}=k_{ij}:=(B^{-1})_{ij}-\frac{3}{4Q_{ij}}.
\]

Every k_ij is nonzero, and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{sign}(k_{ij})=-\operatorname{sign}(Q_{ij})}.\tag{1}
\]

Proof with strict inequalities: put D=diag(Q_ii), G=D^{-1/2}QD^{-1/2}. Then G>0 has diagonal one, so |G_ij|<1 for i!=j. Let

\[
E=D^{1/2}B^{-1}D^{1/2}=3(2I+G)^{-1}.
\]

We have 0<E<(3/2)I. The compression to coordinates i,j has eigenvalues in (0,3/2), so its spectral gap is less than 3/2, implying |E_ij|<3/4. Consequently

\[
|Q_{ij}(B^{-1})_{ij}|=|G_{ij}E_{ij}|<3/4.
\]

Now factor

\[
k_{ij}=-\frac{3}{4Q_{ij}}\left(1-\frac43Q_{ij}(B^{-1})_{ij}\right).
\]

The parenthesis is strictly positive, proving (1). No numerical eigenvalue bound is computed or supplied; this inequality is a completeness proof for exact rational sign/definiteness tests.

Thus, under the QPD promise, R is definite precisely when all hidden off-diagonal Q_ij have one common sign. It is negative definite for positive couplings, positive definite for negative couplings, and indefinite for mixed signs. C is singular when any coupling is zero. These are explicit boundaries of this route.

## 4. Odd dimension: a rational aligned metric without reflections

Let n=2p+1. Choose R_+=R or -R by exact rational definiteness so that R_+ is positive definite. Consider its exterior double-form power

\[
W=\frac1{p!}R_+^{\wedge p}
\in\operatorname{Sym}^2(\Lambda^{n-1}V).
\]

No supplied metric is used for this exterior multiplication. In the input coordinate basis define c_i=(-1)^i e_0 wedge ... wedge e_{i-1} wedge e_{i+1} wedge ... wedge e_{n-1}. Read W's coefficient matrix on c_i tensor c_j as M. Equivalently this uses the coordinate volume to identify Lambda^{n-1}V with V* tensor det(V), producing a quadratic form with a determinant-density weight.

In hidden coordinates R_+ is diagonal with strictly positive coefficients r_ij=|k_ij|. Its power remains diagonal. Repeated edges vanish under exterior multiplication, and orientation signs occur twice and cancel. Hence

\[
\boxed{M_{ii}=\sum_{\mathcal M\text{ perfect matching of }[n]\setminus\{i\}}
\ \prod_{\{a,b\}\in\mathcal M}r_{ab}>0,\qquad M_{ij}=0\ (i\ne j).}\tag{2}
\]

The factor p! from ordering the matching edges is exactly canceled by our normalization. These diagonal entries are hafnians, not determinants or Pfaffians; the two orientation signs remove determinant-style cancellation.

For f_x=f_y composed with L, the tensor U and R transform by four copies of L^{-1}. The cofactor representation on Lambda^{n-1}V is det(L)^{-1}L^T in the c_i basis. Therefore the resulting metric satisfies

\[
\boxed{M_x=\det(L)^{-2}L^TM_yL}.\tag{3}
\]

It is rational and positive definite, and its hidden-coordinate diagonalization is exactly the required alignment. Equation (3), including its determinant factor, is checked independently against expanded-coefficient fixtures. Rationality follows directly from inversion, exterior multiplication and rational volume coefficients, not from a rationality assertion about a numerical minimizer.

This is the top exterior double-form adjugate construction familiar from Einstein-Lovelock tensors; the new application here begins with a quartic inverse catalecticant instead of a supplied Riemannian curvature/metric. The prior construction itself is not new.

## 5. Exact cost accounting: small output is insufficient

The matrix C has polynomial dimension and can be inverted in polynomial bit complexity. R has O(n^4) entries of polynomial bit height. Equation (2) also has polynomial output bit height: each summand uses p=O(n) rational entries, and there are at most (n-2)!! terms. The logarithm of that term count is O(n log n).

The straightforward exterior computation, however, traverses spaces with binomial(n,2k)^2 coefficients; this is exponential in n. Polynomial output height and small final n-by-n output do not remove that arithmetic cost. The following reduction shows that exact evaluation of this particular map has an actual counting obstruction, including when a scalar multiple of the output is acceptable.

### 5a. Absolute normalization reduces positive hafnians

Let Z be any symmetric matrix with strictly positive rational off-diagonal weights z_ij on n=2p+1 vertices. Choose

\[
Q(t)_{ii}=1,\qquad Q(t)_{ij}=\frac{3t}{4z_{ij}}>0\quad(i\ne j).
\]

For all sufficiently small positive rational t, Q(t) is strictly diagonally dominant and PD. An explicit admissible range follows by computing the maximum off-diagonal row sum of the rational matrix A with A_ij=3/(4z_ij): any t<1/max_i sum_j A_ij works. Thus every oracle query is a legal positive-coupling QPD input with L=I.

Write B(t)=I+(t/3)A, h(t)=det B(t), and b(t)=B(t)^{-1}. The positive curvature coefficients are

\[
r_{ij}(t)=z_{ij}/t-b_{ij}(t).
\]

For any deleted vertex i,

\[
P_i(t):=t^ph(t)^pM_{ii}(t)
=\operatorname{haf}\left([z_{ab}h(t)-t\operatorname{adj}(B(t))_{ab}]_{a,b\ne i}\right).\tag{4}
\]

Every edge polynomial inside the hafnian has degree at most n; hence P_i has degree at most pn. Since h(0)=1, the off-diagonal adjugate at zero vanishes, and

\[
P_i(0)=\operatorname{haf}(Z_{[n]\setminus\{i\}}).
\]

Query an exact metric evaluator at pn+1 distinct admissible rational t values, multiply by the explicitly computable t^ph(t)^p, and interpolate at zero. This is a polynomial-time Turing reduction from exact positive weighted hafnians to evaluation of the fully normalized metric map. All query and interpolation bits are polynomial in n and the weight encoding.

### 5b. The obstruction survives arbitrary positive output scaling

Suppose instead the evaluator returns any positive multiple of the exterior metric. Entry ratios M_oo/M_jj cancel that scale. By (4) each such ratio is P_o(t)/P_j(t), with both numerator and denominator of degree at most pn. Use 2pn+1 admissible samples to reconstruct this rational function by rational linear algebra and polynomial gcd, then take its finite limit at t=0. A pair of degree-at-most-pn polynomials satisfying all those samples represents the unique rational function: the cross-difference of two representations has degree at most 2pn and vanishes at more points than its degree.

Now let Z be any strictly positive weighted matrix on an even set of 2p original vertices. Add a dummy o. Fix distinct original i,j, set its dummy edge z_oi=1, and every other dummy edge z_ok=s>0. The just-recovered limit is

\[
\frac{\operatorname{haf}(Z)}{
\operatorname{haf}(Z_{[2p]\setminus\{i,j\}})
+s\sum_{k\ne i,j}\operatorname{haf}(Z_{[2p]\setminus\{j,k\}})}.
\]

This follows by expanding the denominator's perfect matching at the dummy vertex. Its s->0 limit is haf(Z)/haf(Z without i,j), finite and positive. It can be reconstructed from a constant number of positive s queries, or from a conservative degree-p rational-interpolation bound. All those queries again use small positive t and legal PD contrasts.

Recursively delete i,j. The empty hafnian equals one, so multiplying these p recovered ratios evaluates haf(Z) exactly in polynomially many queries. This proves a reduction to the **projective** metric map, not just to its absolute scalar.

Positive rational hafnians are #P-hard: for a 0/1 matrix A of order p, the hafnian of the symmetric bipartite block matrix [[0,A],[A^T,0]] equals perm(A). Replace every zero off-diagonal weight by s>0. The resulting positive hafnian is a polynomial of degree at most p in s. Evaluate at p+1 positive integers and interpolate its value at zero. Valiant's Theorem1 proves the 0/1 permanent is #P-complete. Thus polynomial-bit exact evaluation of our exterior metric map, even up to positive scale, would put every #P function in FP.

This is a route-specific statement. It does not show exponential lower bounds without a complexity assumption, and it does not prove hardness of any aligned metric acquisition. In fact the reduction uses L=I, where I is trivially a valid aligned metric. A coefficient-only algorithm is free to avoid the expensive canonical exterior metric and choose a different rational metric or recover axes first.

## 6. Even dimension: full natural rational selectors remain impossible with positive coupling

Let n=2m and split the indices into equally sized sets A and B. Let epsilon=1/(10n). Define rational Q by

\[
Q_{ii}=2\ (i\in A),\quad Q_{ii}=1\ (i\in B),\quad
Q_{ij}=\begin{cases}2\epsilon&i,j\in A,\ i\ne j,\\
\epsilon&\text{otherwise},\ i\ne j.\end{cases}
\]

All off-diagonal entries are positive, and Q is strictly diagonally dominant, hence PD. Choose a fixed bijection between A and B, with partner map pi. The real involution

\[
(Sy)_i=2^{-1/4}y_{\pi(i)}\ (i\in A),\qquad
(Sy)_i=2^{1/4}y_{\pi(i)}\ (i\in B)
\]

preserves f(y)=(y^2)^TQ(y^2). Squared-coordinate weights are 1/sqrt(2) on A and sqrt(2) on B. Their products turn the B-B coupling epsilon into the A-A coupling 2epsilon, while mixed couplings are unchanged. The quartic diagonal entries likewise interchange correctly. The exact squared-coordinate identity is checked in the n=4 fixture.

Every metric invariant under the coordinate sign flips is diagonal. S^TMS=M then imposes

\[
M_{ii}=\sqrt2\,M_{\pi(i)\pi(i)}\qquad(i\in A).
\]

No rational SPD diagonal metric satisfies these equations. Hence this f has no rational SPD metric invariant under its full real stabilizer. A fully GL-natural rational metric, including one with a determinant-density covariance law, would be invariant under S because |det S|=1; it therefore cannot exist at this input.

The identity is nonetheless a rational aligned SPD metric in these visible coordinates. The obstruction applies to full real-symmetry invariance, not to an arbitrary rational aligned metric, nor to the rational-generator projector of cycle9. The latter can break an irrational symmetry by averaging only acquired rational automorphisms.
