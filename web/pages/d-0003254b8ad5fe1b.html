CYCLE 2 SYNTHESIS — ACQUISITION AND LEARNING OF PREDICTIVE STRUCTURE
8 October 2026

VERDICT

Cycle 2 repaired the raw-data interface and produced several serious scoped results, including a matched all-history prediction theorem with unknown transition and emission parameters. It did not resolve teacher-free acquisition for general unknown finite-state predictive processes.

The strongest positive results remain conditional in different ways:
- finite-Hankel recovery requires a supplied state cap plus a singular gap, and CUR/skeleton methods use coherence or a finite witness promise;
- the raw spectral HMM rank pilot requires a supplied lower bound on the pair-moment gap and an independent fitting batch;
- the strongest all-history future-prediction theorem is for one explicit two-state symmetric family with fixed mixing, emission, and rank margins;
- sparse dictionary discovery succeeds for fully observable Markov chains with anchor and convex-hull margins, not general hidden-state processes.

The strongest obstruction is a fast-mixing K-state family with K^2/4 unknown transition coordinates. Its one-context expected-TV minimax rate is Theta(min{1,K/sqrt(N)}), despite constant mixing and constant normalized singular values. The hidden-HMM version charges its context mass and unnormalized Hankel singular value at order 1/K. Thus mixing and conditionalized observability alone do not provide dimension-free acquisition.

No result establishes a statistical advantage for adaptive Hankel/CUR basis selection over the strongest learner retaining the same raw observations. CUR can lower downstream storage or arithmetic in its conditional regime, but every raw trajectory already supplies all observed-prefix indicators; the full-data baseline can reproduce the selection and retain the path.

LEAD THEOREMS

A. Raw Hankel rank and realization under explicit gaps.
For an HMM with at most R hidden states, full observable Hankel rank r is witnessed by prefixes and suffixes of length at most R−1. Probabilities through length 2R−1 determine a minimal real linear realization up to similarity. The raw-string finite-sample wrappers estimate the needed word probabilities from actual trajectories, not an entry oracle. They can select unknown rank only when the relevant singular gap, and for CUR also coherence/conditioning, are charged. The more source-specific HKZ reconstruction uses independent raw length-three triples and retains its emission/transition singularity, positivity, and tail-mass terms. Its split-sample unknown-rank pilot certifies m under lambda=sigma_m(P_21)>=4R_r, then spends an independent fitting batch. It is not gap-free rank selection, and finite-precision SVD/pseudoinverse costs remain open. Full details: algebraic_rank, spectral_raw, spectral_source_reconstruction, raw_hankel_concentration, noisy_realization, raw_witness_hitting, rank_free_stop.

B. Full-history prediction with both transition and emission unknown.
For the stationary symmetric two-state HMM with lambda,m in [1/2,3/4], two lag moments r1=m^2 lambda and r2=m^2 lambda^2 identify both parameters. From one legal stationary raw path of T observations, a clipped two-moment estimator has E[(lambdahat-lambda)^2+(mhat-m)^2] <= 65863/(4T). A log-odds filter contracts by at most 3/4 uniformly over every finite history. The returned predictor therefore has expected worst-history one-step TV <385/sqrt(T), one-step KL <=1,354,896/T, and H-step conditional future KL <=1,354,896 H/T. A full raw-path local Le Cam pair gives TV >= (25/1024)sqrt(7/(32(T-1))) and KL >= 4375/(16777216(T-1)) for T>=15. These match the one-step rates. The variance proof is special: Y_t=S_t E_t and D_t=S_tS_(t+1) yield iid flips and emission signs, making each lag-k product finite-dependent. It is not a generic mixing-HMM argument. Full proof, precision, and cost: full_predictor_unknown_transition/RESULT.txt.

C. Fast-mixing growing-state prediction obstruction.
For K even, a family with K hidden/output states and K^2/4 free transition signs has exact uniform stationary marginals, Dobrushin contraction <=5/8, transition/emission normalized singular values bounded below by constants, and a one-symbol predictive task. Its expected one-context TV minimax risk obeys explicit lower and upper bounds of order min{1,K/sqrt(N)}. For the full-support HMM channel, minimum context mass, transition/emission floors, and raw pair-block singular values scale as 1/K; those costs are stated rather than suppressed. At the deterministic-emission endpoint, the one-symbol context is the full predictive state and the process is a K-state Markov source. This is a dimension-dependent statistical obstruction, not a rare-switch or hidden-basis nonidentifiability example. Full proof: mixing_counterexample/RESULT.txt.

D. A conditional teacher-free dictionary result.
For stationary fully observable finite Markov chains with unknown sparse stochastic factorization P=Theta Q, pure anchor rows, no-near-pure and convex-hull margins, state coverage, and beta-mixing bounds, raw transition pairs recover the unknown predictive atom dictionary and sparse coordinates up to permutation. The sample, mixing, validation, support, and retained-raw-data baseline costs are explicit. This is true dictionary acquisition without a supplied feature list, but it does not cover hidden-state HMMs and does not beat the full-data baseline in information. Full result: sparse_observable_raw/RESULT.txt.

OTHER RESULTS AND BOUNDARIES

- Population horizon: two finite-state HMMs with s and t hidden states that agree on every word through length s+t−1 agree at all lengths. This is identifiability, not a noisy sample bound. A separate fast-mixing two-state pair aliases at length one and differs at the next prediction. The independent-episode test count is exact for that pair, not universal.
- No-gap failures: exact observable rank cannot be selected uniformly from finite noisy data when a nonzero Hankel direction can approach zero. Word-horizon tables can grow exponentially in R; rare witness mass and singular gap enter both sample and precision costs. Rank-free stopping can certify rank with a promised gap and mixing/mass bounds, but the leaf does not recover predictive coefficients or a smaller adaptive basis.
- Matched raw-data baseline: any CUR, adaptive basis, or summary computed from a trajectory is a randomized/postprocessed function of that trajectory. A learner retaining the complete path can simulate it; no information-theoretic trajectory-sample advantage follows. Fresh holdout data are needed if the selected representation itself is to receive an honest post-selection certificate.
- Controlled policies: exact finite examples show active cue-conditioned actions can beat matched precommitted policies in the named task; another known-kernel finite example needs two adaptive actions versus three fixed actions. These are small-class task-specific separations. Two cue-alignment examples share their core mechanism and are not counted as independent evidence for general unknown-basis learning. The causal-prefix theorem gives population operator identifiability with complete action support through 2K−1, at an exponential exhaustive schedule; it is sufficient, not query-minimal.
- Precision and rare events: a two-state family with q rare basis directions has a matching raw-trajectory count rate involving witness mass and gap; a separate coupon example needs Theta(K^2 log K) symbols to see all rare witness cells. Fast mixing does not eliminate low-mass acquisition costs.
- Parameter-rate control: the iid length-three two-state statistical-rate leaf overlaps the lead theorem's moment inversion. It proves parameter estimation at Theta(epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) in its fixed-margin iid-episode interface; it is not independent evidence for a separate mechanism. The fixed-transition passive leaf is a special case and adds an all-history filtering bound. The lead theorem consolidates these moment/filter ideas while broadening to unknown transition and emission parameters and full future KL.
- Amortization: exhaustive finite-state refitting can be compared against a restricted independent-refit accounting model, but a raw-retaining strategy can simulate acquired summaries at no extra sample cost. A reusable representation may save priced storage or arithmetic; a net advantage against the strongest equal-information retainer remains UNKNOWN.

PROGRAM STATUS, STRONGEST NEXT ATTACK

Status: PARTIAL PROGRESS; broad teacher-free predictive-structure acquisition remains UNKNOWN.

The next decisive target is a risk-certified, rank-free representation learner on actual raw HMM trajectories. It should estimate task-relevant future laws without deciding whether a tiny singular direction is exactly zero, and it should return a certificate in a declared loss (uniform-history TV, expected conditional KL, or finite-horizon joint KL). The difficult interface is to avoid supplied dictionaries, queried conditional laws, and context floors that are exponential in horizon. Keep hidden state cap, mixing, emission floors, witness mass, horizon, precision, reset cost, and model dimension explicit. Compare against full raw-path empirical/HMM model selection on the same trajectories, with fresh validation after adaptive selection.

Two possible routes deserve the next cycle:
1. Extend the explicit filter-stability argument beyond the symmetric two-state model to a substantive unknown-transition/emission class, then determine which contraction/positivity constants suffice for all-history or discounted future KL.
2. Pair the rare-witness lower bounds with a constructive adaptive raw-trajectory basis search, and prove its dependence on candidate count, witness mass and singular gap against an equally informed full-data learner.

A useful success criterion is a theorem that either (a) achieves a task-risk certificate without exact rank selection when weak directions are prediction-irrelevant, or (b) proves a matching lower bound showing that a particular gap/mass/horizon term cannot be removed. The current work has not reached either result for general HMMs.
