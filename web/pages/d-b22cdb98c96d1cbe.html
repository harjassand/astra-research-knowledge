3. **Fu et al., “Transient Behavior of the Metal Interface in Lithium Metal–Garnet Batteries,” Angewandte Chemie (2017), DOI [10.1002/ange.201708637](https://doi.org/10.1002/ange.201708637).** The official Wiley publisher page and abstract were inspected. It reports Mg as a model metal coating that dissolves in molten Li and diffuses into bulk Li, leaving low, stable interfacial resistance. It is included only as a phase/processing-specific counterexample to inferring Mg immobility/insulation from the elemental label. It does not test LaMg3 or LiMgLa.

## Transfer ledger

- Ji's and Vema's electrolytes are both garnet-family ceramics, so the interface analogy has a physical basis. Ji uses Ta-doped LLZTO; Vema uses Al-substituted LLZO. Their Li composition, impurity population, particle chemistry, and mechanical/electrochemical protocols differ.
- Vema's oxygen EDS result does not identify its residue as a specific oxide. Its source model assumes insulating particles; it does not measure Mg/La phase conductivity.
- Ji's DFT interface models and computed Wulff shape support LaMg3 as a candidate phase and orientation, not its volume fraction, stability/solubility under stripping, or Li-ion transport.
- Neither source measures the fraction of a deliberate inoculant that is captured as a contact-blocking interface layer (`eta`), or tests the coupled capacity tradeoff in the same material.
- Parent's main VSC/local-current derivation is intentionally not repeated here. This lane covers the phase, capture, geometry, transport, and prior-evidence boundary.
