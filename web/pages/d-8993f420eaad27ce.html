# Independent audit: one random current for known finite conductivity spaces

2026-10-06. Independent reconstruction by r07_analysis of root/rank_pde route. Conditional mathematical conclusion passes the checks below. External validation, practical measurement gain and publication novelty remain unestablished.

## Exact scope and quantifiers

Use a bounded connected smooth domain Ω⊂R^n, n≥2, and nonempty relatively open boundary patch Σ. H=L²_◇(Σ) is the real, separable mean-zero current space. Flux is g on Σ and zero on its complement; voltages are normalized by mean zero on Σ. This normalization is necessary for a self-adjoint H→H ND map and can be changed from the source's boundary-mean convention by subtracting a constant, which does not affect pairings with mean-zero tests.

Fix one finite-dimensional vector space W⊂L∞(Ω), every element of which is piecewise analytic in Harrach's exact sense. Set σ_a=σ₀+Σ_j a_jφ_j with linearly independent φ_j and σ₀∈W. Let U={a:ess inf σ_a>0}; U is open. The same W contains both all differences σ_a−σ_b and all directional contrasts Σv_jφ_j. This is essential: individually piecewise analytic conductivities with unrelated partitions do not automatically meet this closure assumption. Unknown moving interfaces and anisotropic tensors are outside the proved scope.

The good-input event is: with probability one, ONE draw g simultaneously identifies ALL a∈U, and its differential separates ALL nonzero parameter directions at ALL a∈U. The probability space/model/covariance is fixed first. It is not an uncountable union of pointwise probability-one statements. The same good realization has an inverse Lipschitz bound on EACH compact K⊂U; the constant depends on K and g. Neither one constant over the entire positive cone nor one event simultaneously valid for every conceivable model space W is asserted.

## Independent rank reconstruction

The external facts read directly in Harrach1808.04737v3 §§2.1–2.3 are the derivative identity, two monotonicity inequalities, ordinary gradient localized potentials, and an accessible definite-sign patch for nonzero piecewise analytic contrasts (Lemmas2.5–2.8). The source displayed outer-hull equation omits the avoided-set condition; the proof uses its intended accessible complement meaning, independently clarified by rank_pde against Harrach–Ullrich2013 Definition3(d). That prerequisite correction must remain explicit. His existing derivative injectivity is insufficient by itself: a nonzero compact operator could have rank one.

Here is my independent finite-codimension reconstruction. Suppose κ≥0 outside a closed excluded set D₂, and κ≥c>0 on an accessible open patch D₁. For any codimension-N current constraint V=E⊥, choose N+1 mutually separated closed balls in a smaller ball inside D₁\D₂. In n≥2 removal of the other balls does not obstruct a path to Σ: detour inside disjoint thin annuli whose closures lie in the smaller ball. Apply the source localized-potential theorem separately to each target ball with D₂ and all the other balls as the low-energy region. Scale each current to have target gradient energy one, while the total gradient norm on the low-energy region is at most ε.

Choose l²-unit coefficients c_j solving the N homogeneous E constraints. On the union of balls the diagonal gradient map has norm one; the off-diagonal map is at most √(N+1)ε. On D₂ the combined norm is at most √(N+1)ε. Therefore the signed quadratic integral is at least

c(1−√(N+1)ε)²−||κ||∞(N+1)ε²>0

for sufficiently small ε. If the visible contrast is negative, change sign. These estimates are uniform over the selected coefficient vector, so coefficients depending adversarially on ε do not invalidate the argument. The combined current cannot be zero because its target energy is positive. No bound on its boundary-current norm is needed or obtained.

If a compact self-adjoint derivative operator had finite rank, its kernel would be the orthogonal complement of its finite-dimensional range. The construction gives a strictly nonzero form in that kernel, a contradiction. For T=Λ(σ₁)−Λ(σ₂), put κ=σ₂−σ₁. A positive visible κ gives the LOWER monotonicity bound using background σ₂; a negative visible κ gives the UPPER bound using background σ₁. Again the kernel contradiction proves infinite rank. Choosing the wrong inequality/background would fail; the current rank_pde proof chooses correctly.

This proves more than rank: infinitely many eigenvalues of the visible sign exist, because otherwise removing the finite-dimensional eigenspace of that sign makes the quadratic form sign-incompatible with the construction.

## Gaussian and incidence reconstruction

A Gaussian on H must have trace-class positive injective covariance C. White noise with identity covariance is not H-valued and cannot be substituted. In a covariance eigenbasis, g=Σ√λ_j ξ_j e_j converges in H almost surely because Σλ_j<∞. Every λ_j>0; the support closure is H even if λ_j decay very quickly. Smooth samples can be arranged by using a dense smooth basis and appropriately fast covariance decay; this does not make the covariance nondegenerate on a finite truncation of H's complement.

For any off-diagonal parameter pair, infinite rank selects r=2d+1 independent images from finitely many covariance eigenvectors and r bounded output functionals. Nonvanishing of a rank minor persists on an open pair neighborhood. Countably many neighborhoods cover the second-countable pair space. On one neighborhood, condition on the independent Gaussian tail and express the r projected response-equality equations in the finite Gaussian head. The derivative of those equations with respect to the head is onto R^r. Their incidence manifold has dimension 2d+N−r<N. Projection to the head is null. This holds for every fixed tail. Countable union and Fubini give the uniform good-input event.

For derivative failure, local projective tangent charts have dimension 2d−1; choose r=2d to get the same strict dimension deficit. Analyticity of a↦Λ(σ_a) follows directly from coercive affine weak operators and analytic inversion, so the required C² regularity is available. Event measurability follows from continuous equations over separable Polish parameter/input spaces: projected collision sets are analytic/Suslin, hence universally measurable; complete the Gaussian probability space before applying Fubini. Merely taking probability zero for each fixed pair would not be enough.

## Compact lower-Lipschitz reconstruction

Let G_g(a)=Λ(σ_a)g. On the good event it is injective and its derivative is injective throughout U. If compact K lacked a lower Lipschitz bound, select pairs whose normalized output secants tend to zero. Compactness gives limiting endpoints. Distinct limits violate injection. Coincident limits use the segment Taylor expansion in a sufficiently small convex parameter neighborhood: normalized coordinate differences have a convergent unit direction, and the limiting output is DG_g(a)v≠0. This contradiction proves a positive κ(g,K). Equivalence of coefficient Euclidean norms with conductivity L∞ norms follows from the fixed independent finite basis. κ(g,K) remains noncomputable and can be tiny.

## Additional proved refinement: fixed finite input and output spaces on K

The continuum result does not logically force infinite input/output complexity once K is fixed. A compactness argument yields finite spaces selected BEFORE the random experiment; their dimensions have no effective bound here.

Let F:U→L(H,H) be C². On compact K define C as the operator-norm closure of all normalized secants [F(a)−F(b)]/|a−b|, together with unit directional derivatives DF(a)v, a∈K, |v|=1. C is compact: distinct endpoint limits give ordinary secants; coalescing endpoints give unit directional derivatives. Every T∈C has infinite rank by the PDE rank results.

Fix r=2d+1. For each T select r current vectors x_j and r bounded voltage functionals ℓ_i with det[ℓ_i(Tx_j)]≠0. This minor remains nonzero in an operator-norm neighborhood of T. A finite subcover of compact C supplies finitely many x_j and ℓ_i. Let E be their finite span and Q collect all those functionals into R^M. Then

rank(QT|E)≥r for EVERY T∈C.

Consequently every off-diagonal pair in K has rank≥r after the SAME finite input/output restriction, and every nonzero tangent direction over K does too. Draw one current g∈E with any nondegenerate Gaussian, or more generally any absolutely continuous law on E. The finite rank-incidence proof gives injection and immersion on K almost surely, and compact lower-Lipschitz stability for a↦QΛ(σ_a)g.

The incidence proof can be applied to pair/tangent neighborhoods covering K: each retained nonzero minor persists locally. One need not falsely assert the rank condition everywhere outside K. The finite E and Q depend on the supplied model and K, not on the unknown coefficient or the random draw. Only the lower stability constant depends on that draw. E can be chosen from a prescribed dense smooth current basis, and Q from a dense mean-zero voltage-test basis, by approximating each selected finite minor before taking the finite union.

Thus a safe claim is: a sufficiently rich finite current-design space and a sufficiently rich finite voltage-testing space exist for which one random current works uniformly on fixed K. This is not yet a finite-electrode Complete Electrode Model theorem: driving basis functions and testing voltage functionals are continuum boundary operations, and the theorem does not show their CEM approximation preserves the rank condition. Neither minimal current-space dimension, detector count, precision nor practical gain is known.

## Prior-art checks and what is unresolved

Sontag2002 §6.9 explicitly discusses gains from higher codimension of vector-response coincidences. The rank-sensitive finite-dimensional incidence principle therefore sits close to old prior art; do not market it as a wholly new generic embedding principle.

Alessandrini1999, 'Generic uniqueness and size estimates in the inverse conductivity problem with one measurement', primary journal PDF §§2–3, concerns unknown inclusion geometry and shows nonuniqueness imposes analytic-curve structure on the inclusion boundary. Its genericity concerns shapes, not a full-support Gaussian current simultaneously identifying an arbitrary fixed finite-dimensional scalar coefficient space. The primary publisher page establishes publication1999-11-01; recent search-crawl labels are not source dates.

Boullé–Halikias–Otto–Townsend2607.15832v1 Theorem1 is pointwise in a fixed true coefficient vector and observes a full state u on which a known dictionary can be evaluated. A boundary-only EIT voltage does not provide that interior state/dictionary, and its statement is not uniform over all coefficient vectors. Thus its zero-one law cannot directly prove this route. Supplementary proofs were not read in this audit.

Harrach2019 proves finite-Galerkin and finite-electrode whole-map uniqueness/stability. That collects multiple input responses; it is a stronger experimentally specified discretization result in some respects, but does not itself assert one random current suffices. Exact novelty of the one-random-current fixed-W theorem remains unresolved after targeted searches. No claim that the general Calderón problem, unknown interfaces or noise-calibrated practical recovery has been solved.

Next decisive work: prove an effective rank/singular-value margin for compact normalized operator secants, certify finite E/Q sizes and a useful random-current stability tail, then compare costs with classical multi-current measurement under matched scalar voltage count and current energy. The present proof is a qualitative experiment-count result only.

## Explicit finite refinement in the radial two-layer witness

I independently checked rank_pde's separation-of-variables formula on the unit disk with known interface radius R, inner conductivity c₁>0 and outer conductivity c₂>0:

μ_n=[1−tρ^n]/[nc₂(1+tρ^n)], t=(c₁−c₂)/(c₁+c₂), ρ=R²∈(0,1).

One need not use an infinite-frequency limit to recover the two coefficients. Take one current g=α₁cosθ+α₂cos2θ with both α_i nonzero and measure its two cosine voltage coefficients β_i. Then μ_i=β_i/α_i. Put q_i=iμ_i. The observable ratio is

q₂/q₁=J(t)=[(1−tρ²)(1+tρ)]/[(1+tρ²)(1−tρ)].

It is strictly increasing for −1<t<1, since

J'(t)/J(t)=2ρ/(1−t²ρ²)−2ρ²/(1−t²ρ⁴)>0.

Indeed ρ>ρ² and 1−t²ρ²≤1−t²ρ⁴, so the first positive fraction is strictly larger. Thus the ratio determines t uniquely. Then c₂=(1−tρ)/(q₁(1+tρ)) and c₁=c₂(1+t)/(1−t). The map (c₁,c₂)↦(q₁,q₂) has nonzero Jacobian: (c₁,c₂)↦(t,c₂) is a diffeomorphism; (t,c₂)↦(J(t),q₁) has J'(t)>0 and ∂q₁/∂c₂≠0. On compact positive parameter boxes, two output coefficients from this single two-frequency current therefore give inverse Lipschitz recovery. A nondegenerate Gaussian in the span of the two current modes has α₁α₂≠0 almost surely.

This is an elementary simultaneous-diagonalization witness, not a claimed new EIT result. Its two scalar observations for two unknowns make the actual information cost transparent. It illustrates that the one-current qualitative theorem can have efficient finite instances, while it does not establish efficient truncation for the general fixed-partition model.
