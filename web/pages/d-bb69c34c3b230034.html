# Conditional sign law for chromatin gain versus transcriptional feedback

## Scope

This is an internally reconstructed local linear model result. It is not a biological discovery, global cell-state controllability theorem, or claim that measured accessibility equals effective transcriptional gain. Its intended use is to define a falsifiable and identifiable interface for a dynamic cell-programming experiment.

## Model and interface

Let `u(t)` be a measured upstream IKK/NEMO input deviation; `x(t)` be nuclear RelA deviation; `m(t)` and `z(t)` be NFKBIA/IκBα mRNA and protein deviations; and `y_g(t)` be the deviation of one target-gene transcript. Around a fixed baseline, use

\[
\dot x=b u-\lambda x-\gamma z,\qquad
\dot m=k_m a_f x-\delta_m m,\qquad
\dot z=k_p m-\delta_p z,\qquad
\dot y_g=k_g a_g x-\delta_g y_g.
\]

All decay and gain constants are positive. `a_f` and `a_g` are *effective transcription gains* at the feedback and target regulatory regions. They are not ATAC counts, H3K4me3, enhancer-contact frequency, or reporter brightness. The model assumes a stable local linearization, no other feedback route in the tested range, a fixed state of each effective enhancer over one challenge, and a challenge/readout interval long enough that the perturbation trajectories return to baseline. A transcript-specific RNA decay constant is paid for.

## Derivation

With zero initial deviations, Laplace transformation gives

\[
X(s)/U(s)=\frac{b(s+\delta_m)(s+\delta_p)}{(s+\lambda)(s+\delta_m)(s+\delta_p)+\Gamma a_f},\quad \Gamma=\gamma k_p k_m,
\]

and

\[
Y_g(s)/X(s)=\frac{k_g a_g}{s+\delta_g}.
\]

For stable, fully observed responses returning to baseline, trajectory area equals the transform at `s=0` times input area. Therefore

\[
\frac{\int x\,dt}{\int u\,dt}=\frac{b}{\lambda+\Gamma a_f/(\delta_m\delta_p)},\qquad
\frac{\int y_g\,dt}{\int x\,dt}=\frac{k_g a_g}{\delta_g}.
\]

Compare a primed condition with effective gains `a_f'=r_f a_f` and `a_g'=r_g a_g`. Let `r_u` be the ratio of measured upstream input AUC in the primed and baseline arms. Let

\[
L=\frac{\Gamma a_f}{\lambda\delta_m\delta_p}
\]

be the baseline low-frequency negative-feedback loop gain. Then the target transcript AUC ratio is exactly

\[
R_g=\frac{\operatorname{AUC}(y_g)' }{\operatorname{AUC}(y_g)}=r_u r_g\frac{1+L}{1+L r_f}.
\]

For an input-matched challenge, `r_u=1`. If upstream input AUCs differ, their measured ratio must remain in the expression; extracellular cytokine dose is not a substitute for the same-cell NEMO/IKK input.

Within this model, the target is primed (`R_g>1`) iff

\[
r_g>\frac{1+Lr_f}{1+L};
\]

tolerance (`R_g<1`) holds below this boundary. Thus chromatin/accessibility changes at a target enhancer and at a shared negative-feedback enhancer have different roles: the former is a numerator gain, the latter changes the global signal denominator.

A numerical illustration only: if `L=4`, `r_g=1.5`, `r_f=2`, then `R_g=5/6≈0.8333` (tolerance despite a 50% target-region gain). If the feedback enhancer remains at baseline (`r_f=1`), the same target gain gives `R_g=1.5`. These are model values, not measurements.

## Exact identifiability boundary and conditioning

If only the target AUC ratio `R_g` is observed, the equation gives a continuum of explanations: for any `L>0` and `r_f>0`, choose `r_g=R_g(1+Lr_f)/(1+L)`. Endpoint target data alone therefore do not identify locus gain versus feedback gain under this interface.

The two channels become separable if (i) upstream input is recorded, (ii) nuclear RelA AUC is recorded, and (iii) effective feedback gain is calibrated with at least one nonzero NFKBIA-enhancer perturbation `r_f≠1`. Write `q=[AUC(x)'/AUC(x)]/r_u=(1+L)/(1+Lr_f)`. One obtains

\[
L=\frac{1-q}{q r_f-1},\qquad r_g=R_g/q.
\]

The local conditioning is

\[
\left|\frac{dL}{dq}\right|=\frac{|1-r_f|}{|q r_f-1|^2}.
\]

Thus the experiment is uninformative as `r_f→1`, and becomes ill-conditioned in the high-feedback limit `q r_f→1`. For `L=4,r_f=2`, `q=5/9` and `|dL/dq|=81`; a 0.01 absolute error in `q` is already about 0.81 first-order error in `L`. Strong feedback requires larger enhancer perturbations, replicate acquisition, or a direct IκBα measurement; the reporter count is not free.

The same two readouts also distinguish shared feedback from locus-specific target gain: after correcting for `k_g/δ_g`, the target/RelA AUC ratio estimates `a_g`, while RelA/input AUC estimates the common feedback denominator. With only bulk endpoint RNA and accessibility, this separation is unavailable.

## Robust decision rule with acquired intervals

If acquired uncertainty gives intervals `L∈[L-,L+]`, `r_g∈[g-,g+]`, `r_f∈[f-,f+]`, and measured input ratio `r_u∈[u-,u+]`, compute the minimum and maximum of

\[
R_g(r_u,r_g,L,r_f)=r_u r_g(1+L)/(1+Lr_f)
\]

over their interval corners. This is exact: for fixed values of the other coordinates, the ratio is monotone in `r_u`, `r_g`, and `r_f`; it is monotone in `L` with derivative `r_u r_g(1-r_f)/(1+Lr_f)^2`, so each one-dimensional extremum is at an endpoint. Return `CERTIFIED_PRIMING` only if the lower bound exceeds `1+ε`; return `CERTIFIED_TOLERANCE` only if the upper bound is below `1−ε`; otherwise return `UNKNOWN`. The accompanying code tests the corner rule on synthetic intervals. This is a model-level robust classification rule, not a nature-level controller.

## What the result does not prove

1. The dynamic pulse-train response in Wray et al. cannot be explained by this *linear time-invariant AUC law*: full-response AUC depends only on the zero-frequency transfer. Their equal-exposure schedule effect requires state-dependent, nonlinear or finite-window dynamics. Using this equation to explain that result would be a category error.
2. `a_f` and `a_g` are effective local gains, not direct chromatin measurements. H3K4me3 may be a consequence of TF residence rather than its cause.
3. Other TFs (IRF, AP-1, C/EBP, STAT) can change target transcription independently of RelA. Genes with such co-regulation need a multivariate model or are outside this theorem.
4. Cell growth, death, state mixing, nonlinearity, feedback delays outside this linearization, saturation, changing receptor input, hidden input variation, and finite observation windows can break the AUC relation. It is not a model of lineage transitions or a nature-level capability.

## Falsifier and decisive experiment

In one cell context, predefine an upstream input and select a target whose expression is dominantly NF-κB-driven plus an IRF-dominant negative control. Randomize (a) NFKBIA feedback-enhancer CRISPRi/a, (b) target-enhancer CRISPRi/a, and their combination. Use identical, microfluidically delivered challenge waveforms and record NEMO/IKK input and nuclear RelA in the same living cells; acquire nascent target and NFKBIA transcription over the same time course. Measure effective `r_f,r_g` with calibrated enhancer perturbation/targeted nascent reporters and measure RNA decay (or use nascent output). ATAC/CUT&Tag is a separate destructive association readout; it must not be substituted for effective gain without validation. Fit `L` on one randomized enhancer level and predict held-out levels and pulse schedules.

The model is rejected for the tested genes if RelA/input ratios fail the common feedback-gain law across feedback-enhancer levels, target/RelA ratios change systematically with feedback-enhancer perturbation, or held-out target-AUC ratios fall outside a preregistered noise/measurement interval. Systematic dependence on pulse shape after the response tail is fully captured also rejects this linear AUC interface and triggers a nonlinear dynamic model. Strongest equal-information controls are (i) target-only (no RelA) regression, (ii) RelA-only (no enhancer) prediction, (iii) a model with unconstrained per-condition coefficients, and (iv) the full dynamic model with explicit IRF/AP-1 covariates. All see the same randomized conditions and cell-level records.

Costs: construct and validate two targeted enhancer perturbations and controls; live proximal/RelA reporters; microfluidic stimulus histories; nascent RNA and transcript-decay measurements; targeted chromatin profiling; enough independent culture/animal replicates to estimate between-preparation variance; and the calibration needed to convert editing into effective gains. Exact currency, imaging throughput and sample size remain unknown until pilot noise/perturbation efficiencies are measured.
