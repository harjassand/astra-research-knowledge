CYCLE06 PRIMARY-SOURCE AUDIT: BIDIRECTIONAL APPROXIMATE MARKOV INTERSECTION
Date: 2026-10-09
Scope: primary-source check of the coordinator-supplied interface only. No sibling proof or Cycle06 Sol report was read.

TARGET INTERFACE

For a tripartite state tau_ABC, define

  delta_B = I(A:C|B)_tau,   delta_C = I(A:B|C)_tau.

The supplied target asks whether, under I(A:B)_tau <= C, delta_B+delta_C -> 0 forces a dimension-free trace-distance approach to Sep(A:BC), with the stronger possibility of one B-local EB reconstruction followed by a recovery map. B and C need not have equal dimensions, and no marginal symmetry is assumed. The exact proposed modulus/channel interface has not been supplied in this literature memo; this report assesses the implication and its closest source results, not its proof.

THE EXACT INTERSECTION IS ALREADY A THEOREM

Hayashi and Zhao, “Double Markovity for quantum systems,” arXiv:2601.09995v1 (submitted 2026-01-15), explicitly work with finite-dimensional systems (p. 2). Their Theorem 1, p. 3, characterizes the simultaneous exact conditions

  I(A:C|B)_tau = 0 and I(A:B|C)_tau = 0.

They prove these are equivalent to the existence of PVMs {E_B,j} on B and {E_C,j} on C such that the joint block measurement leaves tau_ABC invariant and the resulting classical label J satisfies

  I(A:BC|J) = 0.

This is an exact quantum double-Markov/common-label theorem, not just two independent recovery statements. It has no I(A:B) bound and no equal-dimension or symmetric-marginal assumption. Since J is classical and screens off A from BC, the theorem implies

  tau_ABC = sum_j p_j tau_A^j tensor tau_BC^j,

so tau is separable across A:BC. Since J is also readable in B, tracing out C gives tau_AB=sum_j p_j tau_A^j tensor tau_B^j with orthogonal B supports. Thus an EB channel on B that measures j and prepares tau_B^j fixes tau_AB. A B-to-BC measure-and-prepare recovery can read j and prepare tau_BC^j, recovering tau_ABC from tau_AB. This establishes the exact endpoint, including the stronger common-label structure.

WHAT THE APPROXIMATE RECOVERY THEOREM DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE

Fawzi and Renner, “Quantum conditional mutual information and approximate Markov chains,” arXiv:1410.0664v3 (2015), Theorem 5.1, pp. 15-16, give for each state a channel R_{B->BC} such that

  F(tau_ABC, (id_A tensor R_{B->BC})(tau_AB)) >= 2^{-I(A:C|B)_tau/2}

in their bits convention. Applying the same theorem with B and C exchanged gives a second, independently chosen recovery channel from C. The theorem does not make these two maps compatible, produce the common PVM label of Hayashi-Zhao, or make either recovered state separable across A:BC. Its trace estimate compares tau_ABC with a recovered state; it is not itself proximity to the set of exact Markov states or to Sep(A:BC).

This distinction is the load-bearing issue in the candidate. Two high-fidelity reconstructions from different input marginals are not yet one common classical sufficient statistic, a B-local EB approximation, or a nearby separable state. The exact intersection theorem handles compatibility only at zero CMI; the checked source statements do not supply its dimension-free stability under a small sum of the two CMIs.

THE ONE-SIDED ROBUSTNESS OBSTRUCTION IS NOT A COUNTEREXAMPLE HERE

Ibinson, Linden, and Winter, “Robustness of quantum Markov chains,” arXiv:quant-ph/0611057v1 (2006), Theorem 4, p. 10, lower-bound the minimum relative-entropy distance to an exact Markov state by one CMI, and Examples 6-7 show that the ratio can grow with dimension. This is about distance to a Markov-state set in relative entropy, not trace distance to Sep(A:BC), and it tests only one Markov orientation. It does not settle the current two-CMI premise.

In particular, their Example 7 uses a purification with rho_AC maximally mixed on the symmetric subspace, rank r=d(d+1)/2. Direct entropy evaluation gives delta_B=I(A:C|B)=log_2(2d/(d+1))<1, while delta_C=I(A:B|C)=log_2 r and I(A:B)=log_2 r. Thus this family has neither delta_B+delta_C -> 0 nor a dimension-independent I(A:B) cap. It must not be presented as a refutation of the supplied bidirectional implication. Their Example 6 is a qubit family; its small-one-CMI discussion likewise does not establish a counterexample to the two-CMI conclusion.

FIXED-DIMENSION CONSEQUENCE VERSUS THE REQUESTED UNIFORM RESULT

For fixed finite dimensions d_A,d_B,d_C, exact Hayashi-Zhao plus compactness gives a qualitative modulus: the state space is compact, both CMIs are continuous, the zero set is contained in the closed separable set, and therefore the maximum trace distance to Sep(A:BC) over states with delta_B+delta_C <= epsilon tends to zero as epsilon -> 0. This argument does not give a dimension-independent modulus, does not use or explain the I(A:B)<=C condition, and does not construct an EB channel or recovery map. The substantive proposed advance is uniformity as dimensions vary, controlled only by the information cap, plus any channel-level EB/recovery realization.

HISTORICAL STATUS AND DECISIVE MISSING LEMMA

The zero-error core is now explicitly known as quantum double Markovity with a common locally readable classical label (Hayashi-Zhao Theorem 1). The candidate would be a quantitative, dimension-uniform stability theorem for that exact intersection under the sum of the two directional CMIs and a cap on I(A:B). It is not an immediate corollary of Fawzi-Renner, whose two directions give separate maps, nor of Ibinson-Linden-Winter, whose one-CMI relative-entropy obstruction is logically different. A bounded primary-source pass found no statement matching the full approximate implication; priority remains UNKNOWN.

The decisive missing lemma is a dimension-free robust common-label/intersection theorem: from delta_B+delta_C <= epsilon and I(A:B)<=C, construct one nearby state with a B/C-readable common classical label screening off A from BC, or directly prove trace distance to Sep(A:BC) is at most g_C(epsilon)->0. For the stronger channel claim, additionally construct a single B-local EB map whose output is close to tau_AB and a compatible recovery that reconstructs tau_ABC. A one-sided recovery estimate alone is insufficient. Infinite-dimensional normal extensions are outside Hayashi-Zhao’s stated finite-dimensional theorem and need separate source/proof treatment.

This is consequential for redundant recoverable records and approximate sufficient statistics: it would turn two-way near-sufficiency of B and C for A into an approximately classical common statistic without assuming B=C or a symmetric extension. It is distinct from full spectrum-broadcast objectivity, which further requires explicit multi-fragment record distinguishability, outcome agreement, and non-disturbance.
