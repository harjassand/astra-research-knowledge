## R1. Original equations and exact ports

Every nonzero arrow must have rank ONE. Write its map as u_a phi_a times a nonzero scalar, with u_a a target column and phi_a a source row. Zero arrows impose no condition and are deleted. The scalar is immaterial. For every arrow keep distinct outgoing and incoming ports, even if their vectors repeat. Add directed edges out_a->in_a, and in_a->out_b whenever the ports meet at the same vertex and phi_b(u_a)!=0. Call X forward closed if every edge starting in X ends in X.

At vertex i put L_i(X)=span of selected incoming columns and H_i(X)=intersection of kernels of UNselected outgoing rows. Forward closure implies L_i(X) is contained in H_i(X). With signed integer weights sigma_i, set W_i(X)=H_i(X) for sigma_i>=0 and L_i(X) for sigma_i<0. If phi_a is nonzero on W_tail, its outgoing port must be selected; hence its incoming port is selected and u_a belongs to W_head. Thus W(X) satisfies every ORIGINAL arrow equation. No acyclicity, semistability balance, genericity or real/complex-field hypothesis entered the proof. Loops and directed cycles are allowed.

## R2. Full unbalanced support equality

Write sigma_i^+=max(sigma_i,0), sigma_i^-=max(-sigma_i,0), and C=sum sigma_i^+ alpha_i. Define on every port subset

    F(X)=sum_i [sigma_i^+ rank(outgoing rows NOT in X)
                +sigma_i^- rank(incoming columns IN X)].

Then score(W(X))=C-F(X) for closed X. Conversely, given any original subrepresentation W, select in_a iff u_a belongs to W_head, and out_a iff phi_a is nonzero on W_tail. Literal arrow closure and the local pairing implication make X(W) closed. Also L_i(X(W)) is contained in W_i, which is contained in H_i(X(W)). Positive weights favor the latter space, negative weights the former, and zero weights do not change the score. Therefore score(W(X(W)))>=score(W). Taking extrema in both directions proves

    max_W sigma.dim W = C - min_closed X F(X).

Each rank term, including a rank on a complement, is submodular; all coefficients are nonnegative. Closed subsets are closed under union and intersection. Hence their F-minimizers are a lattice with a unique largest member X*. Both L_i and H_i grow with X. For an optimal W, its X(W) is an F-minimizer, and score equality forces W_i(X(W))=W_i at every nonzero-weight vertex. At zero weight our chosen H_i contains W_i. Thus W(X*) contains every optimizer and is precisely the unique largest optimizer. Zero-weight handling is necessary; choosing the smaller endpoint there would not give this conclusion.

## R3. An ordinary integer SFM oracle, without a free ring-family service

Let s be the number of ports, R=sum_i |sigma_i| alpha_i, and M=(s+1)R+s+1. Let cut_D(X) count directed edges leaving X. Minimize on ALL subsets

    G(X)=(s+1)F(X)-|X|+M cut_D(X).

Directed cut is submodular. Every nonclosed subset has G>=M-s=(s+1)R+1, whereas the closed empty subset has G<= (s+1)R. Hence every minimizer is closed. Among closed subsets, one unit of F dominates every cardinality change, so minimizing G selects X*. A largest set-minimizer exists, and is the only cardinality-largest one. The formula removes any hidden closed-set optimizer call. Its scalar integer bit lengths are polynomial in the original binary weights, dimensions and port count; it builds no weight copies.

Evaluating G performs ranks of submatrices of fixed input columns/rows and a directed-cut count, plus charged integer weighted sums. This is a valid polynomial-time exact evaluation oracle over Q or a caller-certified prime field. Composing it with an established SFM algorithm is a theorem-level support/canonical acquisition algorithm. An executable SFM backend is **NOT ACQUIRED** in this cycle; zero generic SFM calls were made. Metric's Jiang2007.01445v5 Section6 composition uses a randomized high-probability meta algorithm. ## R4. Why rational output height is polynomial for this subclass

For an integer rank-one matrix A choose a nonzero entry A_ab, take its column b as u and row a as g. Then A=u g/A_ab. Use u,g as the port vectors: their nonzero scalar normalization changes no closure implication. They have original input height. Every returned L_i is a span of a subset of these fixed columns, and every H_i is a kernel of a subset of these fixed rows. With active total dimension A0 and original entry height H, Hadamard/Cramer bounds give O(A0(H+log(A0+1))) scalar numerator/denominator bits, up to standard rational-input denominator clearing. Numerical weights affect the selected subset, not this coordinate bound. Dense active dimensions are at most twice the dense arrow-entry count. Isolated vertices instead return a full/zero descriptor, or their explicit basis allocation must be charged separately.

This is distinct from the iterated meet/join compiler, whose identity arrows have rank3 and whose acquired matrices depend on previous operations. Splitting a rank>=2 arrow into separate rank-one arrows is NOT an equivalent reduction: I2 maps span(1,1) to itself, while replacing it by E11 and E22 forces the target to contain both coordinate lines. The rank-one hypothesis cannot be silently dropped.

## R5. Fixed rank-one gadgets cannot couple arbitrary boundary lines

Take a finite fixed gadget with rank-at-most-one arrows and two boundary spaces identified with K^2. Restrict each boundary subspace to a line. At a boundary line, an incoming rank-one column tests whether its one fixed target line is contained; an outgoing row tests whether the line is its one fixed kernel. Each nonzero boundary port therefore has at most one exceptional projective line. Loops and arrows touching both boundaries count both ports.

Outside the union of these finitely many exceptions, all line signatures are identical. For any FIXED choice of auxiliary subspaces, every arrow-closure truth value depends on the boundary lines only through these signatures. If the gadget admits a generic diagonal pair (L,L), replacing one boundary by another common generic M keeps the SAME auxiliary choice feasible, and preserves all dimension-weight scores. It consequently admits (L,M) with L!=M. Over an infinite field there are two such common generic lines, so no finite gadget of this literal kind can realize exactly the free I2 inclusion relation. This also prevents objective weights alone from repairing the same projection interface.

Over F_q, let e be the number of distinct exceptions in the UNION of the two boundary lists. There are q+1 projective lines. If e<q, at least two common generic lines remain, and the same argument applies. Thus a gadget realizing ALL free boundary identity inclusions needs e>=q and at least q incident nonzero boundary PORTS. The size is Omega(q), rather than polynomial in log q, under this exact interface. This is a communicated deduction of ours, independently reconstructed by metric, and applies also to extension fields. It is not a lower bound for a different encoding or a general finite-field support algorithm. Input-dependent/nonlinear boundary changes and numerical-field-size gadgets are outside this theorem.
