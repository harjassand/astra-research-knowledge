## Exact primary prior boundary

Jorge Vitorio Pereira, *Vector Fields, Invariant Varieties and Linear Systems*, arXiv math/0011205v1, 24 November 2000; journal Annales de l'Institut Fourier51(5),1385–1405(2001). Downloaded primary preprint; Part1 Theorems1–2 and Part2 §6 Propositions5–7, Theorem3 and proof were read.

- Proposition5 (PDF p10): an invariant hypersurface defined by an element of a finite-dimensional linear system divides/is supported in its extactic divisor. The usual differentiation/divisibility argument is elementary and applies in any dimension.
- Part1 Theorems1–2 (PDF p5) concern P². There, vanishing of the degree-d extactic determinant gives a meromorphic first integral of degree at most d.
- Part2 Theorem3 (PDF pp12–13) applies in arbitrary dimension and gives a first integral on a dense open set, meromorphic on projective varieties. Its proof maps a point to the subspace of sections with infinite contact, a Grassmannian-valued map. It does NOT identify a ratio of two fixed linear forms or retain the planar degree bound.

Therefore excluding fixed linear-ratio first integrals alone would not prove our higher-dimensional determinant nonzero. The argument below avoids that false transfer and even avoids relying on the abstract meromorphic-integrability theorem.

## Theorem

Let G be any real symmetric positive-definite n by n matrix. On R[y_1,...,y_n] define

    D=sum_i y_i (Gm)_i partial_i, m_i=y_i²,
    E_G(y)=det[ D^k y_i ]_(0<=k<=n-1,1<=i<=n).

Then E_G is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree n². Each y_i divides E_G, since all D^k y_i are divisible by y_i. Neither G^-1 1>0 nor any separation of column entries is needed. A positive constant multiplying D only rescales the determinant by a nonzero factor, so the actual gradient-field convention is immaterial.

### Step1: induction and one nonzero minor

For n=1, E_G=y_1. Suppose the theorem holds in n−1 variables. Choose j with maximal diagonal G_jj. Let

    W=det[ D^k y_i ]_(0<=k<=n-2, i!=j).

The ideal (y_j) is D-invariant. Restricting W to y_j=0 gives exactly the extactic determinant for the positive-definite principal submatrix G_{-j,-j}, because differentiation and restriction commute modulo this invariant ideal. By induction W is not the zero polynomial.

### Step2: if E_G vanished, construct conserved rational coefficients

Assume E_G is identically zero. Over R(y), the invertible matrix defining W gives unique rational functions c_i, i!=j, satisfying

    D^k y_j = sum_(i!=j) c_i D^k y_i, k=0,...,n−2.

The zero full determinant extends this same relation to k=n−1. Differentiate each of the first n−1 identities and subtract the next identity. This gives

    sum_(i!=j) (D c_i) D^k y_i=0, k=0,...,n−2.

Since W is invertible over R(y), D c_i=0 for every i. Cramer's determinants all have homogeneous degree sum_(k=0)^(n−2)(2k+1)=(n−1)². Thus every c_i has degree0 and the Euler derivation R=sum y_i partial_i also satisfies R c_i=0.

On the unit sphere, put h=m^T Gm and Y=D−hR. Then Y is tangent to the sphere and Y c_i=0 wherever the rational functions are defined. The vector field Y is polynomial and its flow on the compact sphere exists for all nonnegative time and is real analytic.

### Step3: there is an open attracting cone to the j-axis

Positive definiteness and maximality give, for i!=j,

    G_ij < sqrt(G_ii G_jj) <= G_jj.

Set delta=min_(i!=j)(G_jj−G_ij)>0. Near e_j, use r_i=y_i/y_j and y_j>0. Along Y,

    r_i'=r_i[(Gm)_i−(Gm)_j].

At e_j every bracket is at most −delta. By continuity choose an open cone sum_(i!=j) r_i²<eta on which all brackets are at most −delta/2. Then

    (sum r_i²)' <= −delta sum r_i².

The cone is forward invariant, ratios converge exponentially to0, and the sphere trajectory converges to e_j. This proves the needed attracting basin directly, without a saddle-escape theorem or a quantitative complexity assumption.

### Step4: contradiction without any pole assumption at the limit

Choose an initial point u in that open spherical cone with W(u)!=0. Such a point exists: a nonzero homogeneous polynomial cannot vanish on an open spherical patch. Every c_i is finite near u and is locally conserved. Hence, for a nonempty initial time interval,

    y_j(t)=sum_(i!=j) c_i(u)y_i(t).

Both sides are real analytic along the entire sphere trajectory. Their equality therefore extends to every nonnegative finite time, even if a Cramer denominator later vanishes. Taking t to infinity gives 1=0, since y_j(t)→1 and all other coordinates tend to0. Contradiction. Therefore E_G is not identically zero.

Degree: D raises homogeneous degree by2, so row k has degree2k+1 and det has degree sum_(k=0)^(n−1)(2k+1)=n².
