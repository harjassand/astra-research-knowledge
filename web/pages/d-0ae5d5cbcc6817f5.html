**Exact question/interface:** For faithful finite-dimensional states, with known Hermitian generators and unitary-orbit SLDs `−i[A,ρ]={ρ,L}/2`, when does equality of all scores uniquely determine the normalized state?

**Author claim:** Dong–Leng arXiv:2609.28391v1, Lemma 6 / Theorem 15, proves sufficiency of a scalar commutant for the **dressed** frame `K=T_σ(A)`. **Our deductions:** The complete fiber is `ρ=√σ Q√σ` for `Q>0` in `{K_a}'` with `TrσQ=1`. This makes scalar dressed commutant necessary and sufficient over all faithful states, and fiber dimension equals commutant dimension minus one. Ordinary irreducibility fails in dimension three; it cannot fail in dimension two. Exact `d=3` and `d=4` counterexamples share identical SLDs and have original commutant dimension one but dressed dimensions three/four.

**Usable repair/proof:** A generic additional dense Hermitian generator makes the dressed frame irreducible almost surely for every **fixed** target, via a nonzero polynomial commutator-rank minor. This does not prove simultaneous uniqueness for all targets, conditioning, local implementability, or economical score acquisition. A known fiber requires exactly `m−1` independent scalar moment anchors; a positive conditional-expectation Gram operator proves sufficiency. A commutator-gap perturbation inequality gives a finite condition-number certificate.

**Assumptions/obstruction:** Faithful states and exact full matrix scores. Identifiability is not sample complexity.
