# Cycle 2: exact interacting marginal certificate and closure obstruction

Decision: **do not retain this as a frontier independent programme on current evidence**. The source-tail defect from cycle 1 remains a concrete audit. ## Interface and assumptions

A supplied finite-dimensional C² Gibbs energy E(c,d), with integral exp(-E) finite, is split into retained coordinates c and details d by an orthogonal map. Assume differentiation under the conditional integral is valid (it is for the polynomial examples below), and a constant symmetric lower Hessian bound H(x)>=H0. Partition H0 into cc,cd,dd blocks and assume H0dd is strictly positive definite. The retained block and the full H0 need not be positive. Define the exact marginal energy U(c)=-log integral exp[-E(c,d)] dd. This is an exact marginal, not a fitted local ansatz.

## Constructive theorem: the lower bound evolves by a Schur complement

Let S(H0)=H0cc−H0cd H0dd^-1 H0dc. Then, for every c,

\[
 \nabla_c^2 U(c)\succeq S(H_0).
\]

Proof. Conditional differentiation gives

\[
 U_{cc}=\mathbb E[E_{cc}\mid c]-\operatorname{Cov}(E_c\mid c).
\]

The detail conditional is uniformly convex, so the Brascamp–Lieb covariance inequality, applied to t^T E_c for any constant vector t, gives

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(E_c\mid c)
 \preceq \mathbb E[E_{cd}E_{dd}^{-1}E_{dc}\mid c].
\]

Thus Ucc>=E[S(H(x))|c]. Schur monotonicity here does not require H0cc or H0 to be positive: for fixed t,

\[
 t^TS(H)t=\inf_z (t,z)^T H(t,z)
 \geq\inf_z(t,z)^TH_0(t,z)=t^TS(H_0)t,
\]

because both detail blocks are positive. Combining the inequalities proves the claim. Quartic confinement and uniform detail convexity justify all integrals and differentiation in the explicit construction.

Repeated elimination has an honest certificate: update H0 by the appropriate Schur complement, then test the next proposed detail block. Schur associativity means the final constant bound equals direct elimination of the combined detail blocks, assuming their principal block is positive. This provides a complete rule for the lower bound without assuming that the exact marginal retains the original functional form.

At any stage with detail lower bound alpha I, alpha>0, Bakry–Emery gives conditional LSI at least alpha in the usual Euclidean normalization. Conditional continuous Langevin dynamics contracts relative entropy at rate at least 2alpha. This is not a discrete-time work bound: upper smoothness, stepsize, evaluation cost, initial entropy and discretization must be charged separately. Unbounded quartic Hessians do not meet a finite global upper-smoothness assumption.

## Explicit interacting counterexample to quadratic-plus-local closure

Consider the three-site, locally coupled energy

\[
 E(c_1,c_2,d)=\frac b2(c_1^2+c_2^2)+\frac a2d^2
 +\kappa(c_1+c_2)d+\frac14(c_1^4+c_2^4+d^4),
 \quad a>0,\quad\kappa\ne0.
\]

It normalizes for every finite b and kappa because the positive quartic dominates all quadratics. Its detail Hessian is a+3d²>=a for every retained field: uniform conditional convexity, LSI and continuous Langevin contraction are certified. Yet the exact marginal is

\[
 U(c)=\frac b2(c_1^2+c_2^2)+\frac14(c_1^4+c_2^4)
 -\log Z_a(\kappa(c_1+c_2)),\qquad
 Z_a(t)=\int e^{-ad^2/2-d^4/4-td}\,dd.
\]

Let C4(a) be the fourth cumulant of the even law proportional to exp(-ad²/2−d⁴/4). Then

\[
 \partial_{c_1}^2\partial_{c_2}^2 U(0)=-\kappa^4 C_4(a).
\]

Every energy of the form quadratic(c)+v1(c1)+v2(c2) has this mixed fourth derivative equal to zero. A nonzero C4 therefore proves exact nonclosure even though the eliminated conditional is uniformly convex. This is a genuine interacting example, not another Gaussian-gap calculation.

### Exact constructive choice guaranteeing nonzero fourth cumulant

For a=0 the pure-quartic moments are

\[
 M_2=2\frac{\Gamma(3/4)}{\Gamma(1/4)},\quad M_4=1,
 \quad M_6=3M_2,\quad C_4(0)=1-3M_2^2<0.
\]

The strict inequality needs no numerical Gamma evaluation: strict log-convexity gives Gamma(5/4)²<Gamma(3/4)Gamma(7/4); the Gamma recurrence implies [Gamma(3/4)/Gamma(1/4)]²>1/12. Strict log-convexity itself follows by Holder on the Gamma integral, with strictness because different powers of the integration variable are not proportional.

Set C=M6+M4*M2+6*M2*(M4+M2²) and

\[
 a=\min\{1/(2M_2),\;-C_4(0)/(2C)\}>0.
\]

To verify this choice, view the a-law as the pure quartic law tilted by w=exp(-a d²/2). Its tilt normalization is at least 1−a M2/2>=1/2, using exp(-z)>=1−z. For k=2,4,

\[
 |M_k(a)-M_k(0)|\leq a[M_{k+2}(0)+M_k(0)M_2(0)].
\]

Also M2(a)<=M2(0), since its derivative is -Var_a(d²)/2. Consequently

\[
 |C_4(a)-C_4(0)|\leq aC\leq -C_4(0)/2,
 \qquad C_4(a)\leq C_4(0)/2<0.
\]

This is an explicit normalizable model with a positive conditional gap and a provably generated mixed quartic interaction. The analytic constants give a approximately .0215277. With b=-.1 and kappa=.5, quadrature gives mixed derivative approximately .0226128. The displayed analytic argument establishes nonzero; the finite quadrature is only a cross-check.

## Why this certificate does not yet solve critical sampling

For E(x)=.5 x^T Kx+g/4 sum_i x_i^4−beta/2 ||x||², the supplied generic lower Hessian is H0=K−beta I. Spectral elimination can certify a fixed alpha only on modes with K-eigenvalue at least beta+alpha. The spectral root therefore retains all lower modes.

For the nearest-neighbor periodic d-dimensional Laplacian, K(k)=4 sum_mu sin²(k_mu/2). At every fixed positive threshold beta+alpha, the sublevel set has positive Fourier volume. Its lattice mode count is Theta(N) as N=L^d grows. Exact Schur elimination of a constant microscopic bound preserves its inertia: eliminating positive principal blocks does not remove the negative directions. Thus this particular microscopic-curvature certificate cannot collapse the interacting critical root to one scalar.

This is a limitation of the lower-bound method, not a lower bound on all effective-theory constructions. Nonlinear integration can substantially improve the true curvature, as the three-site example shows: its constant Schur bound is much more negative than its actual coarse Hessian. Proving such improvements at all scales is precisely the substantive interacting RG input that cannot be silently replaced by scalar-potential closure or typical-field Hessian checks.

Details alone also never certify the root dynamics. For example, E_h(c,d)=h(c²−1)²+a||d||²/2 gives every detail an exact gap a, while the root's Euclidean Langevin spectral gap tends exponentially to zero. A direct bound follows from f(c)=clip(2c,-1,1): if p_h=P_h(|c|<=1/2), the Rayleigh quotient is at most 4p_h/(1−p_h). For h>=1, the central interval has potential at least 9h/16, while two intervals of total length 1/sqrt(h) around ±1 have potential at most 81/256. Hence p_h<=exp(81/256)sqrt(h)exp(-9h/16). This only bounds that local root dynamics. A supplied one-dimensional root law can instead be sampled by inverse CDF, so this is not a general sampling hardness claim; learning or evaluating the root law remains a separate charged task.

## Strongest prior baseline and novelty disposition

* Brascamp–Lieb, JFA22(4),366–389, August1976, DOI10.1016/0022-1236(76)90004-5, is the original source for the covariance and strengthened marginal inequalities. Publisher metadata/abstract was checked; full original text was not obtained/read in this cycle.
* Kuntz–Lim–Johansen, arXiv:2204.12965v5 (18Feb2023), AppendixB.3 Theorem4 proof, explicitly derives the covariance/Schur marginal-Hessian argument. We read that proof, not their whole paper. Our indefinite retained lower block is a transparent extension of the same argument, not a substantial novelty claim.
* Bauerschmidt–Bodineau, arXiv:1809.02075v2 (10Jul2019), Section2 Theorem2.1 and Corollary2.2, recursively transfer Brascamp–Lieb bounds using verified renormalized nonconvexity and scale-coupling conditions. Their critical hierarchical phi4 analysis goes beyond the constant microscopic bound.
* Bauerschmidt–Bodineau, arXiv:1907.12308v2 (31Mar2020), Theorem1.2, gives a multiscale LSI criterion using the Polchinski-flow Hessian and an integrated scale bound; Theorem1.6 applies to massive sine-Gordon. Negative effective curvature is allowed. Their rigorous criterion is a stronger baseline than merely certifying every coarse conditional as globally convex.

These papers have different exact assumptions; neither supplies an acquired critical certificate for arbitrary frustrated WCRG training. Nonetheless the reconstructed Schur theorem and the closure warning do not meet the programme's novelty threshold. The older work already identifies and tackles the missing renormalized-curvature problem.
