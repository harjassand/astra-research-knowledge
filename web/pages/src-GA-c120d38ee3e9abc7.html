# Periodic suppression does not bound the response to one preparation pulse

Status: root-derived analytic countermodel, internally unreviewed at drafting. This is an identifiability theorem about a stated observation interface. It is not a fitted palladium mechanism, acquired material response, or technological invention. Historical originality is UNKNOWN. Its conservation and positivity assumptions are deliberately explicit.

## Observation contract and statement

Let a positive voltage be held for h>0 and a negative voltage for d>0 in each period P=h+d. Normalize the static positive ethane rate to one and call the static negative rate beta>=0. Suppose the observed periodic mean ethane rate is rho and

    D = h + d beta - P rho,      0 < D < h.

No time-resolved common-hold output, convergence rate to the periodic regime, or independently bounded hidden-state dynamics is supplied. Static endpoint rates and the periodic average alone are the observations in the theorem. Additional measured transient records must be checked separately, rather than declared reproduced.

**Theorem.** There are bounded one-state, positive, exponentially stable switched kinetic models with exactly those static endpoints and exactly that periodic average for which both (i) the integrated response to one pulse from the static positive equilibrium and (ii) the integrated response to executing versus omitting one scheduled pulse after identical periodic conditioning tend to zero over every fixed subsequent common positive hold. The number of cycles needed to condition the model tends to infinity. Other members of the same model family give a large isolated-pulse effect. Consequently, a measured periodic suppression area supplies neither a positive lower bound on a single-pulse common-hold effect nor a finite upper bound on conditioning/washout time under this observation contract.

## Exact construction

Take z in [0,1]. During a positive hold, set z'=-mu z and b=1-z. During a negative hold, set z'=lambda(1-z) and b=beta. All rates are nonnegative, and [0,1] is invariant. These two coefficients describe an abstract hidden kinetic state, not measured electrochemical or hydrogen parameters.

Choose any mu>0 small enough that

    L(mu) = (1-exp(-mu h))/mu > D.

Put v=exp(-mu h), z*=D/L(mu), u=(1-z*)/(1-z* v), and lambda=-log(u)/d. Then 0<z*<1 and 0<u<1, so both relaxation rates are finite and positive. At the start of the positive portion of a periodic steady cycle,

    z* = 1-u + u v z*.

The return map has contraction u v<1 and a unique globally attracting fixed point. The positive-phase ethane total is h-z*L=h-D; the negative-phase total is d beta. Thus the mean is exactly (h-D+d beta)/P=rho. Static positive equilibrium gives b=1; static negative equilibrium gives b=beta. This holds for every selected mu, not just a numerical approximation.

## Two different interventions, with different histories

For the isolated intervention, start at the static positive equilibrium z=0. A negative pulse writes z=1-u, whereas a duration-matched positive sham keeps z=0. Over a subsequent common positive hold of duration T, the ethane deficit is

    Delta_iso(T) = (1-u) (1-exp(-mu T))/mu.

For the conditioned intervention, begin immediately before a scheduled negative pulse, after both arms share identical periodic history. The state is z* v. Executing the pulse produces z*, whereas omitting it and maintaining positive voltage for d produces z* v exp(-mu d). Over the following common positive hold,

    Delta_cond(T) = z* (1-exp(-mu P)) (1-exp(-mu T))/mu.

This duration-matched sham is part of the definition. Comparing different observation clocks would change the estimand. Both expressions are positive; neither is the periodic-vs-static suppression D.

As mu tends to zero, L tends to h and z* tends to D/h. Moreover,

    1-u = z* (1-v)/(1-z* v) = O(mu),
    1-u v = (1-v)/(1-z* v) = O(mu).

For any fixed T, both contrasts tend to zero. The 1/e conditioning count is -1/log(u v), which diverges. Conversely, L decreases continuously from h to zero. As mu approaches the unique positive solution of L(mu)=D from below, z* tends to one and u tends to zero. Hence Delta_iso(h) tends to D. Every member before that endpoint has finite lambda. Continuity supplies intermediate isolated effects; no infinite-rate model is required for the zero-effect limit.

## Molecular balances and scope

This scalar construction can be embedded in a carbon-balanced two-reaction bookkeeping model. For example, choose positive-phase acetylene consumption a=a_plus+k z and negative-phase consumption a_minus. Its periodic integral is h a_plus+k D+d a_minus, independent of mu. Choose k from any desired periodic acetylene total, provided 0<=a_plus+k z<=the acetylene feed over z in [0,1]. Ethylene net gain is a-b; ethane consumes carbon from the abundant ethylene cofeed when b>a. Check feed and nonnegative outlet constraints for a proposed numerical embedding. This is an optional admissible subclass, not an assertion that every possible published rate triple fits those bounds.

The construction does not reproduce native chromatograms, potential/current records, spectroscopy, unreported conditioning histories, or the actual catalyst's coupled energy balance. It does not show the physical experiment is erroneous. It excludes a logical inference from static endpoints and steady averages to a minimum isolated-pulse response. Slow inventory-mediated or other bounded catalytic-state alternatives cannot be eliminated merely by calling the pulse short or the average effect large.

## Consequence for Astra's acquisition decision

The source's separate isolated-pulse and periodically conditioned interventions are both useful. A null result in a fixed 10-second window in both is still compatible with a slowly accumulated state, unless the measured conditioning/recovery trajectory excludes that state class. Record conditioning cycles, randomized withdrawal of the pulse train, recovery under a common hold over increasing horizons, and state/reset reproducibility. A sufficiently long observation horizon must be acquired from dynamics or a justified state bound; cycle averages do not provide it.

These measurements establish preparation-dependent chemistry before identifying its microscopic carrier. Fresh-carbon tagging with complete chase, already developed in FZ/N884, can distinguish new turnover from old-material release. It does not by itself identify electronic control or bound slow conditioning. Close 2021 pulse-sweeping prior art separately stops the generic prepare-then-react novelty claim.
