UNKNOWN-MIXING PREDICTIVE-KL CERTIFICATION: A FULL-RETENTION LOWER BOUND
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the stated candidate-risk certification interface. Novelty, external review, and implications for every possible learner are UNKNOWN.

1. ANSWER AND SCOPE

There is no distribution-free, finite-time confidence certificate for the
stationary-history predictive KL risk of an arbitrary supplied predictor from
one stationary raw HMM path when the class has no mixing-rate promise. The
obstruction survives retention of the entire path and computation by an
unbounded randomized algorithm. If reducible stationary HMMs are admitted,
two models remain statistically indistinguishable even from the infinite
path, while the same fixed predictor has low risk in one and high risk in the
other. If the class is restricted to irreducible HMMs with at most two states, exact
infinite-path indistinguishability disappears, but every almost-surely finite
adaptive stopping rule can still be defeated by choosing an unknown slow
transition rate after the rule's stopping horizon on the easy model.

This is a post-hoc risk-auditing theorem for a supplied predictor q. It is not
a theorem that every learner fails to find a good predictor. In particular,
the cited Markov-chain literature proves useful expected prediction bounds
without a known spectral gap. That distinction is part of the result, not a
qualification to omit.

2. LEGAL INTERFACE AND RISK

Fix an output alphabet A={0,1} and a hidden-state cap D>=2. A stationary HMM
has a stationary two-sided extension (Z_t,Y_t)_{t in Z}; the data oracle
reveals only Y_1,Y_2,... from one stationary path. At each request it returns
the next symbol. There is no reset/restart, hidden-state query, entry query,
feature dictionary, mixing-rate, context-mass, rank, or spectral-gap input.
The algorithm may use internal randomness, retain every symbol, stop at an
arbitrary stopping time tau, and return PASS/NO for a finite-description
predictor q supplied before the path. The comparator also knows q and D.

For a predictor q(.|h) defined on the whole observed past h=Y_{-infty:t},
define the stationary-history risk

  R_P(q) = E_P[ D( P(Y_{t+1}|Y_{-infty:t}) || q(.|Y_{-infty:t}) ) ].

All witnesses below use an order-one q, so q(.|h)=q(.|Y_t). On the witnesses,
the infinite-past conditional law is explicit. A (epsilon,delta) certificate
must satisfy both:

  completeness: if R_P(q)<=epsilon/2, then P_P(PASS with tau<infty)>=1-delta;
  soundness:    if R_P(q)>=2epsilon, then P_P(PASS)<=delta.

This formulation is a precise post-hoc “certify q” task. It does not give the
algorithm an oracle for R_P(q). The candidate q itself is a legal supplied
input, not a hidden feature basis.

3. THE CANDIDATE PREDICTOR

Let epsilon>0 and delta in (0,1/2). Choose a rational p in

  0 < p < (1-2delta)/(1-delta).

Choose integers L,B>=1 and set zeta_0=2^(-L), zeta_1=2^(-B). Define the
full-support binary predictor

  q(1|0)=zeta_0,  q(0|0)=1-zeta_0,
  q(1|1)=zeta_1,  q(0|1)=1-zeta_1.

Choose L so that r_0=-log(1-zeta_0)<=epsilon/2, and choose B so that

  p (B log(2)-r_0) >= 2epsilon,                 (A)
  p (B/2-1) log(2) >= 2epsilon.                (B)

Such finite L,B exist. For small epsilon, L=O(log(1/epsilon)); B=O(1+epsilon/p).
Using full-support q avoids an infinite-KL artifact. Its two dyadic rows take
O(L+B) coefficient bits.

4. EXACT INFINITE-PATH IMPOSSIBILITY (REDUCIBLE STATIONARY HMM)

Let P_0 be a one-hidden-state HMM that emits 0 deterministically. Let P_mix
have two hidden states {0,1}, transition matrix I_2, deterministic emission
Y_t=Z_t, and stationary initial law pi=(1-p,p). Both are stationary HMMs with
at most two hidden states. P_mix is reducible; stationarity is promised, not
ergodicity.

The infinite observed path law is

  P_mix^Y = (1-p) delta_(0,0,...) + p delta_(1,1,...),
  P_0^Y   = delta_(0,0,...).

Thus TV(P_mix^Y,P_0^Y)=p. The stationary-history conditional next-symbol laws
are point masses at the current symbol. Hence

  R_{P_0}(q) = r_0 <= epsilon/2,
  R_{P_mix}(q) = (1-p)r_0 + p B log(2),
  R_{P_mix}(q)-R_{P_0}(q) = p(B log(2)-r_0) >= 2epsilon.

The risk gap is not an algebraic-rank statement: it is an explicit gap in the
target expected conditional KL risk for the same fixed q.

Theorem 1 (no full-path certificate). For every epsilon>0, delta in (0,1/2),
and D>=2, the q above admits no randomized procedure satisfying completeness
and soundness uniformly over stationary HMMs with at most D states, even if
the procedure observes the entire infinite raw path. Indeed, by the choice of
p, (1-p)(1-delta)>delta. On the common all-zero component, any procedure has
the same transcript law under P_0 and P_mix. Therefore

  P_mix(PASS) >= (1-p) P_0(PASS) >= (1-p)(1-delta) > delta,

contradicting soundness. This argument includes arbitrary adaptive stopping,
lossless storage of all observations, exact arithmetic, and unbounded
computation. Equivalently, any risk estimator with additive error less than
3epsilon/4 and failure probability delta under both models would induce a
test separating risks <=epsilon/2 and >=2epsilon; Le Cam's inequality gives
sum of test errors >=1-p, so such estimation is impossible when
2delta<1-p.

The conclusion persists for every finite prefix length T: its full-data law
has exactly the same TV distance p. Any empirical Hankel matrix, SVD, fitted
HMM, stopping transcript, or compressed statistic is a postprocessing of this
path and cannot improve that testing bound. On the event the path is all
zeros, full retention sees exactly the same data under the two models.

Proof. Conditional on the latent component, the output is constant forever.
The zero component of P_mix has weight 1-p and is exactly P_0's complete
infinite observation law. The risk expressions follow by conditioning on the
two stationary histories 0^infty and 1^infty. The certificate inequality is
the mixture identity above; no algorithmic restriction is used. For risk
estimation, threshold an estimate at the midpoint of the two risk ranges and
apply the standard two-point testing bound. QED.

5. IRREDUCIBLE FINITE-STOPPING VERSION: SLOW TRANSITION / MISSED HORIZON

The exact infinite-path result uses reducibility. The following variant shows
what remains if “HMM” is implicitly restricted to irreducible hidden chains.
Keep the same fixed q,p,L,B. For any a>0 define a two-state Markov chain whose
state is also its deterministic output, with transitions

  P_a(1|0)=a,       P_a(0|0)=1-a,
  P_a(0|1)=b,       P_a(1|1)=1-b,
  b = a(1-p)/p.

For 0<a<=p/(2(1-p)), this chain is irreducible and b<=1/2. Its stationary
law is exactly (1-p,p), because (1-p)a=pb. Given the infinite observed past,
the next-symbol law is its row at the current state. Write
K_b=D(Ber(1-b)||Ber(2^(-B))), where the Bernoulli parameter is the
probability of output 1. Direct expansion gives

  K_b=(1-b)B log(2)-h(b)-b log(1-2^(-B))
      >= (1-b)B log(2)-h(b)
      >= (B/2-1)log(2),

where h is binary entropy, b<=1/2 and h(b)<=log(2). Thus

  R_{P_a}(q) = (1-p)D(Ber(a)||Ber(2^(-L))) + p K_b >= 2epsilon.

The first term is nonnegative. This lower bound is uniform as a tends to
zero: while the chain becomes arbitrarily slow, its stationary mass p on
state 1 stays fixed and q continues to assign its next 1 probability 2^(-B).

The first T outputs are all zero under P_a with probability

  m_T=(1-p)(1-a)^(T-1).

This event has the same observation prefix as P_0, so for any PASS event based
on a stopping time tau<=T,

  P_a(PASS) >= m_T P_0(PASS and tau<=T).

Theorem 2 (no uniformly finite adaptive stop without a rate). No procedure
can meet the above completeness/soundness requirements over irreducible
HMMs with at most two hidden states if it must stop almost surely on every
low-risk instance. To
see this, suppose it does. Under P_0, completeness implies that for every
eta>0 some finite T has

  P_0(PASS and tau<=T) >= 1-delta-eta.

If “two-state irreducible” is interpreted as exactly two hidden states for
both hypotheses, represent P_0 with transition rows (1/2,1/2) and let both
hidden states emit 0 deterministically. That hidden chain is irreducible and
its observed path law is still 0^infty. Thus the theorem does not rely on a
one-state null or on a reducible null; it does rely on no emission-rank or
observability promise.

Choose eta>0 small enough that (1-p)(1-delta-eta)>delta. Then choose a>0
small enough that b<=1/2 and

  (1-p)(1-a)^(T-1)(1-delta-eta)>delta.

The alternative P_a is irreducible, has risk at least 2epsilon, and has the
all-zero T-prefix with probability m_T. Consequently P_a(PASS)>delta,
contradicting soundness. The stopping algorithm may be randomized and may use
all symbols up to tau; the lower bound couples its internal random seed on
the shared all-zero prefix.

For a fixed horizon T, the total-variation distance between the full T-symbol
laws is exactly 1-m_T, since P_0 is concentrated on 0^T. The event of missing
the first 0-to-1 transition after starting in state 0 has probability
(1-a)^(T-1). Its expected waiting time is 1/a and its tail is exponential
only on the unknown scale a. Here the two-state spectral gap is a+b=a/p,
which can be arbitrarily small. Thus this is a horizon/missed-transition
obstruction, not an infinite-path nonidentifiability claim for irreducible
chains: with unlimited time, an irreducible P_a eventually leaves 0 almost
surely.

6. COSTS, VALIDATION, AND EQUALLY INFORMED BASELINES

Oracle/query cost. One oracle call reveals one binary observation (one bit).
A T-symbol prefix costs T queries and at most T retained bits. The adaptive
procedure may stop at tau and store every returned bit. The lower bound holds
for the entire raw path, so no sublinear memory or restricted-query premise
drives it. Hidden states, conditional-probability entries, restarts, and
independent validation samples are not available in this interface.

Description and coefficient bits. The candidate q has L+B+O(1) bits in a
dyadic encoding; B=O(1+epsilon/p), and for small epsilon L=O(log(1/epsilon)).
P_0 and P_mix have constant transition/emission tables; representing the
stationary mixture weight p=r/s costs O(log r+log s) bits. The irreducible
finite-horizon witness can choose a=2^(-M), with M large enough for the
required m_T overlap; b=a(1-p)/p then has O(M+log s) bits. The argument does
not conceal an infinite-precision transition coefficient. The coefficient
precision cost is charged to the alternative's description, and may grow
with the stopping horizon.

Arithmetic and time. The theorem is information-theoretic and applies to any
randomized decision function of the complete data, without a computational
bound. A practical empirical Hankel/SVD or full-data likelihood routine is
weaker only in computation, not stronger in information. There is no useful
arithmetic upper bound to report for the impossible certificate. Risk
formulas and the finite check below use logarithms; the proof itself is
symbolic apart from inequalities (A),(B).

Validation exposure. With M independent stationary restarts of P_mix and one
observed symbol per restart, the probability of missing the 1-component is
(1-p)^M. To reduce this miss probability to at most delta requires

  M >= log(delta)/log(1-p) = Theta(p^(-1) log(1/delta))

for small p, with one bit per restart. A single non-ergodic path has only one
component draw; extending it in time never supplies a second draw. For the
irreducible alternative, a long enough run can expose transitions, but a
uniform validation length requires a lower bound on a (or a rate estimate
backed by additional assumptions). Independent starts and a known rate are
different resources from retaining the same path.

Strongest same-data baseline. The strongest baseline is the full raw path
with arbitrary postprocessing, including a full empirical Hankel block,
full-block SVD, or maximum-likelihood HMM fit with D and q known. The exact
TV/overlap calculations already apply to it. The empirical one-symbol
context basis {0,1} is available for free in the witness; the missing object
is evidence that context 1 has stationary mass p, not a feature basis. A
basis-supplied oracle therefore does not close this gap. Supplying the true
stationary law and transition/emission tables would allow direct evaluation
of R_P(q), but is a stronger, model-specific oracle, not validation from the
path.

Literature comparator. Han, Jana, and Wu prove minimax expected KL prediction
bounds for fully observed stationary finite-state Markov chains without a
spectral-gap condition, using an average of add-one predictors; for fixed
k>=3 their risk is of order (log n)/n up to constants. Their theorem is an
important counterweight: no-gap prediction can be useful, so the present
result must not be described as impossibility of learning all predictors.
Their target is expected risk of a designed predictor in the known-state
Markov model. The theorem here is a high-confidence, post-hoc certificate for
a supplied q against the stationary-history conditional KL target from one
path. The two interfaces are not interchangeable.

Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang's spectral HMM learner assumes positive stationary
mass and full-rank transition/emission operators, and its baseline analysis
uses independent HMM sequences; the paper notes that one long sequence can
be used with effective sample size discounted by the mixing rate. It does
not furnish a rank-free, no-rate post-hoc risk certificate for this input.
The lower bound is stronger than a comparison to either algorithm because
it grants the auditor the whole raw path and unbounded computation.

7. FAILURE BOUNDARY AND UNKNOWN

• Exact indistinguishability from the infinite path uses a stationary but
  reducible HMM. If the task silently requires irreducibility, use Theorem 2:
  it rules out a uniform almost-sure finite stopping certificate with no
  rate, but not eventual discrimination on any fixed irreducible model.
• This is a theorem about auditing a fixed supplied q. It does not show that
  every training algorithm fails to return a low-risk predictor. A learner
  may output a structurally robust q, and a theorem may bound its expected
  risk without examining a particular fitted q. A matching impossibility
  for all data-dependent output predictors is UNKNOWN.
• If the user asks only for expected risk averaged over both training data
  and future histories, a priori universal prediction bounds may apply; that
  is not the post-hoc certificate defined here.
• A known positive context floor and a known mixing/rate bound restore finite
  validation schedules in standard concentration approaches. This package
  does not derive a sharp sufficient certificate under those promises.
• No novelty, priority, or external proof review is claimed. The model
  calculations are elementary and internally checked. The local prior
  cards reviewed below are source-reported records, not independent proof
  validation.
