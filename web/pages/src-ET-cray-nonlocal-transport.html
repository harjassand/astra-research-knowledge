1. What changed: preserve the defect, then complete it
The earlier no-go theorem remains intact: a finite-range, exactly conserved legal current cannot carry positive transport in box-closed cooperative dynamics. Increasing its dictionary cannot fix that obstruction.    cray_genesis_report
The new construction does not search for such a current.
Instead, its finite object is a legal current with a precisely specified source–sink defect:
\[
\partial H
=
(\text{left port occupation}-\text{right port occupation})
\times \text{reservoir gates}.
\]
A separate, genuinely nonlocal spatial current connects these defects. Their cancellation is proved after assembly, rather than imposed on each finite module.
I examined three alternatives before developing this construction. Direct projection of a conservation defect transfers the difficulty into an uncontrolled inverse: small residuals do not imply small correction energy without a quantitative spectral estimate. Weak-limit constructions likewise require a bounded-energy sequence that still has to be built. Unbounded routing is more concrete, but it is already established prior art, and forward path length alone does not control inverse congestion. Shapira’s recent Thomson-principle paper explicitly identifies unbounded paths as necessary for cooperative models. arXiv
The selected operation separates the difficulties:
Construct legal source–sink transport inside finite blocks. Construct global spatial conservation around arbitrarily large obstacles. Join them through coefficients that remain invariant during every microscopic module transition.

The last requirement is essential. A naive multiplication of the two currents would generally fail.
2. The finite object: a source–sink module for hard KA
The model remains the square-lattice hard KA rule: an exchange requires a vacant neighbor other than the exchange partner at both endpoints. No forbidden transition is softened.
Write \(p\) for particle density, \(q=1-p\), and \(\chi=pq\). The target is the infinite-volume bulk variational diffusion coefficient
\[
D(p)=\frac{1}{2\chi}
\inf_{f\ \mathrm{local}}
\mathbb E_p\sum_{i=1}^{2}c_i(a_i+G_i f)^2,
\]
with horizontal displacement field
\[
a_i=(\eta_0-\eta_{e_i})\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}.
\]
This is collective diffusion, not tagged-particle self-diffusion. The underlying variational duality is not the invention; a Thomson-type lower-bound formulation is already available. arXiv
Ports and reservoir gates
Partition the lattice into \(3\times3\) blocks. Each block’s center is its port; its other eight sites form its reservoir.
A reservoir is declared good when there is at least one vacancy on each of its four outer sides, and at least one vacancy among the four neighbors of the center. This rule never reads the port occupation.
Exactly 154 of the 256 reservoir patterns are good. Their probability is the explicit polynomial
\[
\gamma(p)
=
q^8+8pq^7+28p^2q^6+52p^3q^5+49p^4q^4+16p^5q^3.
\tag{1}
\]
Because the reservoirs are disjoint, their gate values are independent under the known Bernoulli equilibrium.
For two adjacent blocks \(u,v\), I constructed a finite current \(H\) on their \(6\times3\) union satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{b\subset Q_{uv}}H_b(\zeta)
=
(\zeta_{s_u}-\zeta_{s_v})g(\omega_u)g(\omega_v).
}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(H_b(\zeta)\) is the outgoing current along a configuration-graph edge. Thus the sum is a configuration-space divergence, not a spatial divergence.
Every transition used by \(H\) is legal even with the entire exterior occupied. It is consequently legal in any actual exterior configuration: additional vacancies cannot disable a KA move.
Importantly, \(H\) can pass through intermediate states whose gates are bad. Only its source–sink defect has the gated form in (2).

This is an explicitly evaluable rational function. Its coefficients are recomputed by the checker from the stored currents—not accepted from a numerical optimizer.
3. The nonlocal operation: make a stream potential constant across obstacles
On the coarse lattice, call an edge open when both endpoint blocks are good. Let
\[
b=1-\gamma(p)
\]
be the bad-block probability.
Draw the dual edges crossing closed coarse edges. Each bad block produces a small closed-dual square; neighboring bad blocks join these into larger components.
For a dual vertex \(z\), let \(C_z\) be its closed-dual component. Define
\[
\Psi(z)=\frac{1}{|C_z|}\sum_{w\in C_z}w_2.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the affine height \(z_2\) is replaced by its average over the entire obstacle component.
Take the rotated discrete gradient:
\[
k_h(x,y)=\Psi(x,y)-\Psi(x,y-1),
\]
\[
k_v(x,y)=\Psi(x-1,y)-\Psi(x,y).
\tag{5}
\]
Three properties follow directly.
Spatial conservation. The discrete divergence of \(k\) vanishes by cancellation of the four potential differences.
Legality. A closed edge has both dual endpoints in the same component, so its current is zero.
Nonzero mean transport. The correction
\[
\beta(z)=\Psi(z)-z_2
\]
is stationary. Once its integrability is established,
\[
\mathbb E k_h=1,\qquad \mathbb E k_v=0.
\]
The current is genuinely nonlocal: an obstacle component can be arbitrarily large. The next step controls that nonlocality explicitly.
A finite matrix certifies the infinite tail
Let
\[
R_z=\max_{w\in C_z}\|w-z\|_\infty.
\]
A component reaching distance \(r\) contains a path of at least \(r\) distinct bad sites. Choose a shortest such path. It cannot have a shortcut between nonconsecutive vertices.
A necessary local restriction is particularly useful. After a cardinal step, there are one cardinal and two diagonal continuations without a two-step shortcut. After a diagonal step, there are two cardinal and three diagonal continuations.
These counts give the matrix
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
2&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]
With \(A=bM\) and \(u=(1,1)^T\),
\[
\mathbb P(R_z\ge1)\le4b,
\]
\[
\mathbb P(R_z\ge r)
\le16b^2u^TA^{r-2}u,\qquad r\ge2.
\tag{7}
\]
This overcounts the relevant self-avoiding paths, which is safe. It does not assume independence for repeated-site walks.
The tail is summable whenever
\[
\boxed{1-4b-b^2>0.}
\tag{8}
\]
No fitted percolation threshold or unspecified constant is used.
Summing (7) gives
\[
\mathbb E R_z^2
\le
m_2(b)
:=
4b+
\frac{32b^2(3-4b+b^2)}
{(1-4b-b^2)^2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(|\beta(z)|\le R_z\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(k_h^2+k_v^2)
\le
\mathcal K(b):=1+8m_2(b).
}
\tag{10}
\]
This supplies an explicit energy budget for the nonlocal spatial current.
4. The crucial lifting step: freeze the reservoirs inside each module
Simply multiplying \(H\) by \(k\) evaluated on the actual gate field would be wrong. A microscopic transition inside \(H\) can change that field and therefore change the coefficient multiplying the current.
The repair is to evaluate the coefficient on a modified gate field.
For a coarse edge \(e=(u,v)\), let \(\Gamma^{e+}\) be the gate configuration with both endpoint blocks forced good, leaving all other gates unchanged. Define
\[
F_e=k_e(\Gamma^{e+}).
\tag{11}
\]
This coefficient depends only on reservoirs outside the two-block module. It is therefore unchanged by every transition inside that module.
Assemble
\[
J_b^{\mathrm{tile}}
=
\sum_{e:\,b\subset Q_e}F_eH_b^e,
\tag{12}
\]
then average over the nine translated block partitions.
This produces a current covariant under every unit lattice translation, rather than only block translations.
Why the defects cancel exactly
On a nonzero source in (2), both endpoint reservoirs are good. Therefore
\[
F_eg_ug_v=k_e(\Gamma).
\tag{13}
\]
After substituting the finite source identity, the global conservation pairing reduces to
\[
\sum_{e=(u,v)}
\mathbb E\!\left[
k_e(\Gamma)(\sigma_u-\sigma_v)f
\right],
\tag{14}
\]
where \(\sigma_u\) denotes the port occupation.
Condition on all reservoirs. The current \(k\) is now fixed, and the ports remain independent Bernoulli variables.
For a local test function \(f\), put
\[
m_u=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{ports}}[(\sigma_u-p)f].
\]
Only finitely many \(m_u\) are nonzero. Equation (14) becomes
\[
\sum_{e=(u,v)}k_e(m_u-m_v)
=
\sum_um_u\,\operatorname{div}k(u)
=0.
\]
This proves admissibility against every local corrector. It does not assert that an uncontrolled infinite pointwise sum telescopes.
Resulting certificate theorem
Finite-module integration by parts gives the response
\[
\langle a,J\rangle=\frac{2\chi}{L},
\qquad L=3.
\]
The overlap weights in (3), independence of the forced coefficients from their modules, and the nonlocal energy bound give
\[
\|J\|^2
\le
\frac{2\chi C(p)}{L^2}\mathcal K(b).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
D(p)\ge
\frac{1}{C(p)\mathcal K(1-\gamma(p))}.
}
\tag{15}
\]
Everything on the right is determined by the finite witness and explicit rational formulas.
The lower-bound calculation does not need access to an infinite configuration. It needs the density \(p\), the finite certificate, and the proved tail formula.

6. The nonlocal contribution is controlled—not assumed
The construction also supplies finite-radius approximations.
Set \(\beta_R(z)=\beta(z)\) when the entire component \(C_z\) is resolved within radius \(R\), and zero otherwise. Form its rotated gradient and clip it to open edges.
The resulting \(k_R\) is legal but generally not conserved. The tests exhibit a nonzero truncated divergence. Those defects are deliberately retained.
For \(R\ge1\), let \(S=(I-A)^{-1}\). An explicit tail bound is
\[
T_R(b)
=
16b^2u^TA^{R-1}
\left[
(R+1)^2I+(2R+3)AS+2A^2S^2
\right]u.
\tag{16}
\]
It satisfies
\[
T_R(b)\ge
\mathbb E\!\left[R_z^2\mathbf1_{\{R_z>R\}}\right].
\]
Only a \(2\times2\) rational matrix inverse and power are needed.
Applying the same lifting operation to \(k_R\) gives a finite-range legal current \(J_R\), with
\[
\boxed{
\|J-J_R\|^2
\le
\frac{16\chi C(p)}{L^2}T_R(b).
}
\tag{17}
\]
The previously missing completion-error bound is now derived from the construction itself.
For the normalized current \(F=3J\), sufficient coarse radii for
\[
\|F-F_R\|\le10^{-6}
\]
include \(R=18\) at \(p=0.20\), \(R=46\) at \(p=0.30\), and \(R=1280\) at \(p=0.40\).
The deterioration near the explicit counting threshold is substantial. These are ensemble \(L^2\) bounds, not uniform pointwise bounds for arbitrary configurations.
Finite evaluations of \(\|J_R\|\), combined with (17), can improve the energy estimate for this witness. That does not prove convergence of the lower bounds to the true diffusion coefficient.
Complete cost accounting
For fixed block size, evaluating a radius-\(R\) current at one configuration requires \(O(R^2)\) coarse gate queries. But exact expectation of an arbitrary truncated current can require
\[
2^{O(R^2)}
\]
gate configurations.
Likewise, a complete two-block configuration graph for general block size \(L\) has
\[
2^{2L^2}
\]
states.
The current implementation pays that cost at \(L=3\). It establishes neither polynomial scaling in larger block size nor polynomial accuracy complexity. The headline bounds avoid the large-radius expectation calculation because the explicit majorant (10) already completes their proof.
7. The broader mathematical operation
The construction provides more than a single current.
For each gate field, component averaging
\[
(\mathcal A_\Gamma\psi)(z)
=
\frac1{|C_z|}\sum_{w\in C_z}\psi(w)
\]
is a linear, idempotent operation that makes a potential constant across closed dual edges.
It can act on
\[
\psi(z)=z_2+\phi_z,
\]
where \(\phi\) is a bounded local stationary field, not only on the bare affine height. The resulting rotated gradient remains globally conserved, legal and of unit mean horizontal flux. A corresponding completion tail follows from the same component-radius estimate.
This gives a concrete route for completing finite local stream modifications before evaluating or optimizing them.
The associated microscopic lifting identity is equally important: a finite configuration-space defect of the form
\[
(\sigma_u-\sigma_v)g_ug_v
\]
can inherit spatial conservation through reservoir-frozen coefficients.
This is not a universal theorem that every approximate conservation law has an affordable completion. It identifies a specific, verifiable interface through which completion is constructive.
8. What remains decisive for consequential dense transport
The current small-block gate becomes too rare at high density. At \(p=0.80\),
\[
\gamma(p)=0.08380672,
\]
far outside the proved completion regime. As vacancy density tends to zero,
\[
\gamma(1-q)=16q^3+O(q^4).
\]
Thus improving the same finite optimizer cannot turn this into a dense-regime result.
The decisive next theorem is a compressed cooperative module construction: larger-scale, port-independent reservoir gates with sufficiently high probability, together with legal source–sink currents satisfying (2), an explicit weighted energy bound, and a compact construction/checker that avoids enumerating \(2^{2L^2}\) configurations.
Good-box, frameability and port-independent coarse-connectivity mechanisms already provide relevant geometric ingredients. They must be treated as prior art, not rediscovered and renamed. arXiv
The requirement is more specific than “find a better corrector.” It is to produce a compact, exact certificate for the finite module’s source balance and energy, including bad intermediate states and congestion. Once that input is supplied, the nonlocal conservation, response normalization and completion error follow through the construction above.
