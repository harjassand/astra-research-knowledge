# Biomolecular design frontier audit

## Verdict

The strongest line from this pass is a conditional, experimentally addressable result for kinetic RNA switches: under irreversible ligand capture, every hidden cotranscriptional folding network reduces for response prediction to the distribution of cumulative ligand-competent exposure. Exact dose-response measurements at finitely many concentrations do not identify that distribution or certify off-grid response without additional assumptions. Complete single-molecule exposure trajectories instead give a uniform finite-sample dose-response certificate with explicit sequence-screening cost.

This is a defensible theorem and acquisition protocol inside a narrow model. Its mathematical ingredients are standard and current literature already covers kinetic riboswitch models, single-molecule trajectories, and expression-platform engineering. I found no basis to claim scientific novelty or frontier status from this bounded audit. The main advance here is a clean performance-relevant interface, exact finite-panel non-identifiability, and a distribution-free acquisition guarantee; external priority review remains open.

## Exact kinetic mechanism and prediction

Fix the sequence, ionic/temperature conditions, and RNAP decision window `[0,T]`. Let `X(t)` be a transcript's ligand-free cotranscriptional path and let `r(X(t))` be the ligand-association rate per unit concentration in its current conformation. If ligand capture is conditionally Poisson, irreversible until the transcription decision, and determines the output, define `A_T = integral_0^T r(X(t)) dt`. The capture probability at concentration `c` is exactly

`R(c) = 1 - E[exp(-c A_T)]`.

This is the Laplace transform of the exposure distribution. Hidden folding states and transition rates do not need to be separately reconstructed if the sole outcome of interest is first capture. In fact, on bounded exposure support, the exact curve for every concentration uniquely determines the exposure distribution, though it still cannot identify the microstate network. Conversely, equilibrium populations or mean accessibility alone do not determine the curve. For example, with mean active occupancy `1/2` throughout the window and `c k_on T=2`, a half-active/half-trapped population captures ligand with probability `0.4323`; a rapidly mixing population with the same one-time active fraction approaches `0.6321`. The approximately 0.20 difference is a quantitative prediction from path heterogeneity.

For any fixed finite panel of positive concentrations, there are distinct exposure distributions on `[0,k_on T]` matching every exact point on the panel but disagreeing at a selected unassayed dose. The proof is a finite-moment nullspace construction for distinct exponentials. Thus off-grid curve certification from a finite reporter panel must state and defend its kinetic/shape assumptions.

If instead one measures `n` independent, complete single-molecule exposure paths per construct and computes `R_hat(c)=1-n^{-1}sum_i exp(-c A_i)`, DKW plus monotonicity yields

`Pr(sup_{c>=0}|R_hat(c)-R(c)| > eps) <= 2 exp(-2 n eps^2)`.

For `K` candidate sequences, `n >= ceil(log(2K/delta)/(2 eps^2))` trajectories per sequence gives a simultaneous confidence band with probability at least `1-delta`. Maximizing a prespecified response score then has regret at most `2 eps`. As sufficient counts, `K=16`, `delta=.05`, `eps=.1` requires 324 complete trajectories per sequence (5,184 total); `eps=.05` requires 1,293 per sequence (20,688 total). These bounds are conservative, not claimed optimal.

The corresponding experiment is a matched expression-platform panel sharing the same ligand-binding aptamer. Select variants predicted to have similar ensemble mean competent exposure but different dwell-time correlations; record cotranscriptional single-molecule trajectories through a fixed RNAP decision window; predict the dose curves without a Hill fit; and independently measure a withheld ligand concentration and transcription readthrough. A failure of the Laplace prediction after verifying irreversible commitment, calibrated association rates, and state assignment rejects the interface. If binding can dissociate before the decision or bound and unbound trajectories differ in output in ways not captured by the exposure variable, the model must be expanded and this certificate does not apply.

## Literature boundary

Prior primary work establishes each neighboring biological fact. Wickiser et al. measured adenine-riboswitch ligand kinetics and distinguished equilibrium from kinetic control ([Biochemistry, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1021/bi051008u)). Frieda and Block directly followed single pbuE transcripts during transcription ([Science, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225722)). Beisel and Smolke formulated kinetic rate models and response descriptors for riboswitches ([PLoS Computational Biology, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000363)); Sun et al. modeled cotranscriptional RNA kinetics with a helix-based transition network ([J. Phys. Chem. B, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcb.8b04249)). Bushhouse, Fu, and Lucks tuned sensitivity and dynamic range via expression-platform kinetics across multiple riboswitch classes ([Nature Communications, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55601-3)). That study functionally characterized 3,289 expression-platform loop variants and reports nine biological replicates at each of eight ligand concentrations for its dose curves. These papers make a generic claim that kinetics matter untenable as novelty. The bounded search did not establish that the exposure-law reduction, finite-dose indistinguishability result, or DKW all-dose certificate are absent elsewhere; all novelty claims remain unresolved.

The primary-source comparison suggests the strongest equally informed controls for the proposed experiment are: (1) the current Hill-fit workflow, (2) a prespecified finite-state CTMC fitted to single-molecule dwell times, and (3) the exposure-law estimator. They must receive the same ligand concentrations, transcription window, sequence panel, and calibration data. A fair comparison reports curve error at withheld concentrations and separately charges trajectory acquisition and reporter replicates.

## Protein-folding route

The folding-landscape child reconstructed the exact Boltzmann condition: target occupancy at least `1-delta` iff the total target-vs-off-target free-energy gap is at least `kBT log((1-delta)/delta)`. At 298 K, 95% occupancy requires 1.74 kcal/mol of basin free-energy advantage. A target-versus-one-decoy minimum-energy gap does not account for multiplicity or unsampled partition mass. Without a bound on omitted mass, any finite decoy transcript is compatible with arbitrarily low target occupancy. At one target microstate versus 100 equal-mass off-target states, the per-state gap must be about 4.46 kcal/mol (6.46 if both basin free-energy estimates carry 1 kcal/mol error).

This is a useful certificate boundary, not a new statistical-mechanics result. Norn et al. analyzed 4,204 designs and found target probability more predictive than a target-only energy score, but their finite decoy ensembles are not an exhaustive physical partition function ([PNAS, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2017228118)). Experimental work on Top7 found nonnative equilibrium states despite a successful designed target structure ([Cell, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2006.12.042)); SspB redesign showed positive-only stability optimization can retain competing oligomer states while explicit negative design changes specificity ([PNAS, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0506124102)). Full supporting details are in [the folding-landscape memo](children/folding_landscape/RESULT.md).

## Binder specificity and negative-design scope

A second, experimentally concrete result is about the scope of binder specificity claims. For a finite tested off-target panel, there are indistinguishable binding worlds that agree on every measured construct but bind an untested target. Without a justified distribution over off-target contexts and an assay-sensitivity floor, the only sound certificate is panel-scoped. Under iid contexts drawn from a declared distribution `Q`, prevalence `p` of true cross-binding, and sensitivity at least `s`, zero detections among `n` distinct contexts give the exact one-sided bound `p <= min(1,(1-alpha^(1/n))/s)` at confidence `1-alpha`. At 95% confidence with `s=.9`, 332 independent negative contexts bound `p` below 1%. Replicate wells on one construct do not count as new draws from `Q`.

The motivating contemporary case is Odin-Multi, a September 2026 bioRxiv preprint ([primary source](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.08.749745v1)). The method explicitly optimizes shared binder sequences against named on- and off-target structures. On its near-identical pMHC specificity test, counterselection raised a computational score-passing fraction from 6.0% to 14.2%; in vitro screening identified one follow-up clone, S2, with stronger tetramer discrimination against a one-residue M4A peptide variant than the comparison binder. The test used a named panel and did not establish absence of cross-reactivity across the human immunopeptidome. Counterselection also increased trajectory acquisition substantially: the M4A campaign ran 1,700 trajectories versus 203 target-only. Multiplying these counts by reported median V100 trajectory times gives about 1,681 versus 68 V100 GPU-hours, excluding AF3 re-evaluation, failed orchestration, and experimental costs. The toxin campaign also had an assay-identity limitation: one tested target was a candidate toxin-containing venom fraction, not a sequence-confirmed pure off-target. The full source audit and staged assay accounting are in [the specificity note](children/generative_robustness/research_note.md).

This supports a practical three-valued output for negative design: `PASS(panel, assay, threshold)`, `FAIL(panel, assay, threshold)`, or `UNKNOWN(outside panel / detection limit / construct identity)`. The design experiment should compare target-only and explicit-counterselection at equal sequence-synthesis and panel-assay budgets, then report verified specific-hit yield against a held-out panel. A model score is candidate prioritization, not the binding discriminator.

## Sequence-function acquisition boundary

With arbitrary labels over `N` candidates and no sequence prior, finding a unique active sequence after `B` adaptive queries has exact minimax success `(B+1)/N`; this is a hidden-hit lower bound, not a model of typical protein/RNA landscapes. If one instead assumes a full degree-`d` interaction model across multi-allelic sites, exact landscape identification needs and is achieved by `D_d = sum_{|S|<=d} product_{i in S}(q_i-1)` distinct genotype labels. For an eight-binary-edit local search, 93 labels through triples identify the cubic model. A proposed 16-sequence blind challenge at Hamming weights 4–8 would test whether that assumption transfers; 109 total unique genotypes and two replicates require 218 genotype-replicate outcomes per condition. That 16-member holdout is a pilot choice, not a theorem-derived power guarantee. The primary literature includes full four-site TrpB maps (160,000 amino-acid variants) and complete eight-site RNA libraries (65,536 sequences), showing the importance of assay context and higher-order effects; low-label designs rely on additional evolutionary/structural priors. See [the sample-complexity memo](children/sample_complexity/RESULT.md) for proof, assay accounting, and sources.

## Allostery: energetic coupling is measurable; its microscopic path is not

For a fixed protein construct, ligand, and equilibrium assay, the mutation–extension coupling is exactly the four-condition thermodynamic-cycle contrast `η = [G_mut,ext−G_WT,ext]−[G_mut,core−G_WT,core]`. It establishes context-dependent energetic coupling, but does not identify whether that coupling came from a direct contact, hidden conformational selection, or fast dynamics. A binary equilibrium table cannot distinguish these mechanisms: any positive 2×2 Boltzmann weight matrix has a two-state latent-conformation factorization as well as a direct-interaction representation. State-resolving or time-resolved data are needed to distinguish specified mechanism classes.

The recent PSD-95 PDZ3 extension map makes the design consequence concrete. It reports that deleting the C-terminal αC extension shifts wild-type ligand-binding free energy by +0.97 kcal/mol; a predictor given only the invariant core must incur at least 0.485 kcal/mol worst-context error on this two-context absolute target, because it has one input for two different values. At 298 K, the reported shift corresponds to about a 5.2-fold dissociation-constant change within that study’s thermodynamic model. A focused discrimination experiment would compare wild type, the distal I341 mutation, direct-contact control F337, and a neutral control with αC present/absent and a canonical terminal tetrapeptide versus the full 9-residue CRIPT peptide; ITC or fluorescence anisotropy plus NMR relaxation separates the specified direct-extension and distributed-dynamics predictions. The exact I341 substitution-level effect must first be frozen from Supplementary Data 3; the main text does not supply its numerical coupling. See [the full assessment](children/allostery/assessment.md). These are standard identifiability/cycle arguments applied to recent data, not a novel theory of allostery.

## Scope, costs, and open gate

The kinetic certificate is about first ligand capture under an absorbing-capture model, not all-cellular gene expression, and it does not control concentration-dependent ligand effects on unbound structure, photobleaching/censoring, or force-field accuracy. Complete trajectories, binding calibration, and a separate readthrough assay are distinct costs. Temporal resolution and competence-state assignment must be calibrated; if each exposure has a known absolute measurement error `eta_A`, prediction error on `[0,c_max]` increases by at most `c_max eta_A`.
