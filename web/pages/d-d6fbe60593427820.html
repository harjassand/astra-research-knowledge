# Reasoning mechanisms: tested transfer and exact reading record

Date: 2026-10-07. Source snapshots remained read-only. Official release pin: `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`.

**New result in this run:** the stable-law mechanism in family 271 cannot supply exact-marginal sampling for arbitrary Boolean polytopes with short edges. A constant-cardinality, four-vertex polytope has edge support four and an easily sampled exact-mean law, but every real-stable law on its legal vertices is a point mass. The exact-mean law also violates the source's joint-presence inequality. Cartesian products keep edge support four in arbitrary dimension. This is an obstruction to a proposed transfer, not a refutation of the Heisenberg source or the existing ROUND objective.

## 1. The mechanism and the target interface

The attached A–F state includes ROUND: for a Boolean polytope \(P=\operatorname{conv}V\subset[0,1]^n\), with actual maximum edge support \(k(P)\), produce a law supported on legal vertices, preserve the supplied mean \(y\in P\) exactly, and control every supported atom's discrepancy for a matrix with unit Euclidean columns. Its support bound is \(\dim F(y)+1\le n+1\). The attachment seeks a constructive, costed implementation; it does not demand negative association or stability. Its exact source is `/Users/harjas/.codex/attachments/2f87408a-6b4c-49c5-b868-d29f20d801d1/Pasted text.txt`, particularly its ROUND section. Later inherited results keep the implementation/acquisition gap open.

The [271 summary](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/reasoning_traces/spontaneous-magnetization-quantum-heisenberg-ferromagnet.pdf), §§1,3–5, describes a stable polynomial for the full, unexposed pin-output law. The manuscript's `02-pin-test.tex`, lines 1–370, gives the concrete algebra: begin with

\[
 F_\pi(x,y)=\prod_i(x_i+y_{\pi(i)}),
\]

apply independent partial symmetrizations for Bernoulli transpositions, then splice an unpinned coordinate by setting \(x_q=y_q=t\) and taking the coefficient of \(t\). Splicing a closed cycle contributes the required factor two. Stability is retained along these **specific** operations. The resulting law has the joint-presence bound

\[
 \Pr(J\subseteq A)\leq\prod_{j\in J}\Pr(j\in A).
\]

The useful transfer hypothesis to test is therefore concrete: *can every short-edge Boolean polytope admit a real-stable legal-vertex law with a prescribed mean?* A positive answer would provide a source of concentration, but it adds a substantive premise beyond ROUND. The following test rejects it.

## 2. Counterexample and universal stable-support lemma

Start with

\[
 V=\{000,110,101,011\},\qquad y=(1/2,1/2,1/2).
\]

The four points are affinely independent, so \(P=\operatorname{conv}V\) is a tetrahedron. Every pair is an edge and differs in two coordinates; hence \(k(P)=2\). Writing the three mean equations and total mass equation forces each atom's probability to be \(1/4\). Its generating polynomial is

\[
 G(z)=\tfrac14(1+z_1z_2+z_1z_3+z_2z_3).
\]

At \(z_1=z_2=z_3=i/\sqrt3\), every variable lies in the open upper half-plane and \(G=0\), so the law is not real stable. All three pairwise coordinate covariances nevertheless vanish. The stronger diagnostic gives

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_1,\mathbf1\{X_2\lor X_3\})=1/8>0,
\]

so pairwise covariance tests alone miss the failure of negative association.

To retain constant cardinality, lift each atom to \((v,1-v)\):

\[
 V'=\{000111,110001,101010,011100\},\qquad y'=(1/2)^6.
\]

Again this is an affine three-dimensional simplex. Every atom has cardinality three, every edge changes four coordinates, and \(k(P')=4\). The first three mean equations still force the unique law \(U\), uniform on the four atoms. With one-based coordinates \(J=\{2,3,4\}\), precisely the atom \(011100\) contains \(J\). Thus

\[
 \Pr_U(J\subseteq X)=1/4>1/8=\prod_{j\in J}\Pr_U(X_j=1).
\]

This directly violates the proposed transferred presence bound. In fact all non-complement coordinate pairs are independent; complement pairs have negative covariance. Even constant cardinality together with nonpositive pairwise covariances is insufficient.

**Lemma. Every real-stable probability law supported on \(V'\) is a point mass.**

Proof. The four supports are

\[
 S_0=\{4,5,6\},\quad S_1=\{1,2,6\},\quad
 S_2=\{1,3,5\},\quad S_3=\{2,3,4\}.
\]

Suppose two distinct atoms \(S_a,S_b\) have positive weights \(p,q\). They share exactly one coordinate \(c\). The other two supports share a coordinate \(d\) absent from \(S_a\cup S_b\). Set \(z_d=0\). This kills precisely the two other monomials and leaves

\[
 z_c(pz_uz_v+qz_rz_s),
\]

where the exclusive pairs \(\{u,v\}\) and \(\{r,s\}\) are disjoint. Give these variables the values

\[
 z_c=i,\quad z_u=1+i,\quad z_v=q(1+i),\quad
 z_r=-1+i,\quad z_s=p(-1+i).
\]

All five remaining variables have positive imaginary part, and the two terms cancel because \((1+i)^2=2i\), \((-1+i)^2=-2i\). This contradicts stability under real specialization.

For an elementary proof avoiding that closure theorem, restore \(z_d=i\varepsilon\). The full generating polynomial is affine in \(z_u\), say \(A(\varepsilon)z_u+B(\varepsilon)\). At zero its coefficient is \(ipq(1+i)\neq0\), and its root is \(1+i\). For sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), the root \(-B(\varepsilon)/A(\varepsilon)\) still has positive imaginary part by continuity. All six variables now lie in the open upper half-plane, producing an actual zero of the full polynomial. Arbitrary weights on the two killed atoms do not affect the argument. Therefore two positive atom weights are impossible. Every point-mass polynomial is a nonzero monomial and is stable. ∎

Consequently, among stable laws on these legal vertices,

\[
 \min\|\mathbb E X-y'\|_\infty=1/2,\qquad
 \min d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(X),U)=3/4.
\]

Both are sharp because every stable law is a point mass. Thus allowing a small mean error or a small distributional repair cannot rescue this transfer while retaining the legal support.

For every \(r\ge1\), use \(P_r=(P')^r\subset[0,1]^{6r}\). An edge of a Cartesian product changes one factor, so \(k(P_r)=4\), while every atom has cardinality \(3r\). Any exact-mean law at \((1/2)^{6r}\) has the uniform marginal in each block and inherits the same triple-presence violation. Moreover a stable joint law would have stable block marginals under specialization of other variables to one. The lemma then forces every block, hence the whole joint law, to be deterministic. The elementary continuity proof above extends directly: specialize outer variables to one, create the isolated block root, then replace all outer variables by \(1+i\varepsilon\) and the deleted variable by \(i\varepsilon\).

This family satisfies the rounding interface in a particularly benign way. With \(A=I_{6r}\), every column has Euclidean norm one and every atom has discrepancy \(\|A(X-y)\|_\infty=1/2\). Uniformly sample one of the four lifted vertices and repeat it in every block. This legal law has exact marginals, four atoms (within the required support limit \(\dim F(y)+1=3r+1\)), rational weights \(1/4\), two fair bits total, and \(O(r)\) output time. A supplied linear objective over the full product polytope can be optimized by evaluating four block scores per factor; the rational arithmetic has polynomial bit cost in the original objective input. Short edges, efficient optimization, small discrepancy, and exact mean coexist with the stability obstruction.

## 3. What was tested, and which alternatives failed

The exact test is `work/stability_rounding_counterexample.py`, with output `work/stability_rounding_counterexample.json`. It uses rational arithmetic and \(\mathbb Q(i)\), with no optimization solver, numerical root finder, or simulation. `python3 work/stability_rounding_counterexample.py` exited zero. It checked both affine ranks, the unique \(1/4\) weights, all simplex edge supports, cardinalities, identity-feature discrepancy, the covariance and presence inequalities, the base algebraic root relation, and all six two-atom isolation patterns at three positive rational weight pairs (18 exact cancellations). It also checked the four-atom repeated-block control for 1, 2, and 5 blocks. The universal lemma follows from the displayed algebra and continuity argument, not from those 18 examples.

Three plausible repairs were deliberately tested:

1. **Check only pairwise covariances.** The base law has zero pairwise covariances and still fails negative association. The lift retains nonpositive pairwise covariances and fails the actual source-style triple inequality.
2. **Restrict to a fixed-cardinality support.** The complement lift makes every atom cardinality three while preserving the obstruction and giving the stronger point-mass classification.
3. **Repair or approximate within legal support.** The classification gives exact lower bounds \(1/2\) on marginal error and \(3/4\) on total variation. An unrestricted positive-polynomial repair could add illegal vertices, which changes ROUND's interface. Existing cycle19 work had already identified that support problem for the Fourier moment repair; it is not a new positive result here.

The uniformly sampled four-atom law is the equally informed control: it already performs the target mean-preserving task at constant cost. The stable mechanism cannot outperform it on this family because the stable mechanism cannot meet its feasibility condition.

The support also fails basis exchange: from \(S_0=\{4,5,6\}\) and \(S_1=\{1,2,6\}\), exchanging element 4 for either 1 or 2 gives an absent support. This identifies a concrete additional structural premise to investigate in a genuine application. The next useful observation is whether that application's legal support admits the exchange/partial-symmetrization/splicing operations; edge support alone cannot establish it. This report makes no claim that a stable representation is necessary for ROUND, or that all matroid rounding mechanisms fail.

The closest established framework is the relation between stable generating polynomials and negative dependence, described in [Borcea–Brändén–Liggett, *Negative dependence and the geometry of polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2340v2), and restrictions on stable-polynomial supports in [Brändén, *Polynomials with the half-plane property and matroid theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605678v4). Only their abstracts/metadata were read in this run; no result from their full proofs is required above. The elementary counterexample is a new tested application in this run, not a claim of historical novelty in stable-polynomial theory.
