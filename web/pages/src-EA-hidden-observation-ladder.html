Let \(J\) be uniform averaging on a finite state space. The generator has the form
\[ \boxed{ Q_r=J-I+\varepsilon A, \qquad \varepsilon=\frac1{16r}, \qquad A^3=0. } \tag{2} \]
The central issue is to construct \(A\) so that it simultaneously produces positive transition rates, long observational concealment, and a short controlled response.
Use a uniform Boolean state space \(\Omega=\mathbb F_2^d\), with Walsh characters
\[ \chi_a(x)=(-1)^{a\cdot x}. \]
These form an orthonormal basis. Choose a character \(f\), put \(\ell=2r-1\), and choose characters \(c_1,\ldots,c_\ell\) such that
\[ 1,\ f,\ c_1,\ldots,c_\ell,\ d_1,\ldots,d_\ell, \qquad d_j=fc_j, \]
are all distinct.
Define \(A\) on this basis by
\[ Af=d_1, \qquad Ac_j=d_{j+1}\quad(1\le j<\ell), \qquad Ac_\ell=f, \tag{3} \]
and set it to zero on every other basis character.
Then
\[ A1=A^*1=0,\qquad \|A\|_{2\to2}=1,\qquad A^3=0. \]
In particular, the hidden propagator is unusually simple:
\[ \boxed{ e^{tQ_r} = J+e^{-t} \left[ I-J+\varepsilon tA+\frac{\varepsilon^2t^2}{2}A^2 \right]. } \tag{4} \]
But observation introduces another operator:
\[ Dg=fg. \]
It acts by
\[ D1=f,\quad Df=1,\quad Dc_j=d_j,\quad Dd_j=c_j. \]
Interleaving evolution and observation therefore creates the long sequence
\[ f \xrightarrow{A}d_1 \xrightarrow{D}c_1 \xrightarrow{A}d_2 \xrightarrow{D}\cdots \xrightarrow{D}c_\ell \xrightarrow{A}f. \tag{5} \]
Every closed scalar observation word containing \(A\) must use at least \(2r\) copies of \(A\). Repeated observation cannot skip a rung.
This is the relevant distinction: simple hidden time evolution does not imply shallow observable structure.
There are \(2r\) nonzero Walsh entries in \(A\). In the ordinary state basis,
\[ A_{xy} = \frac1n \sum_{\text{ladder edges }a\to b} \chi_b(x)\chi_a(y), \]

\[ |A_{xy}|\le\frac{2r}{n}, \qquad K:=\max_x\sum_y|A_{xy}|\le2r. \]

\[ \frac{7}{8n}\le q_{xy}\le\frac{9}{8n} \]
