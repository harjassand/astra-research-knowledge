TWO-DIMENSIONAL CRITICAL DIRICHLET BOSONS: UNIFORM POSITIVE-PENALTY
GIBBS ROBUSTNESS FROM A LOGARITHMIC COLLISION FILTER
8 October 2026. Sol independent derivation; internally proved candidate.
No external proof validation or novelty/priority claim. Natural logarithms.

1. THEOREM AND EXACT DOMAIN

Let L>=2 be an integer, Lambda_L={1,...,L-1}^2, and S=(L-1)^2. Work on
the entire boson Fock space over ell^2(Lambda_L), with total number M.
Let h_L be 2J times the Dirichlet nearest-neighbor graph Laplacian of the
square, J>0. Its diagonal entries are 8J and its nearest-neighbor entries
are -2J. Thus its product-sine eigenvalues are

 epsilon_k=4J sum_{a=1}^2(1-cos(pi k_a/L)),  1<=k_a<=L-1.

Set K=dGamma(h_L), beta=tau L^2, tau>0, and
 H0=K-lambda M/L^2,   -B<=lambda<=0,   B>=0 fixed.
Let rho0=exp(-beta H0)/Z0 be the free grand-canonical state.

Fix an integer exclusion radius R>=1, independent of L. P_R denotes the
configuration-space projection onto occupations having no two particles
at sup-norm distance <=R, including no two at one site. P_R includes the
vacuum and all one-particle states. It has finite rank: M<=S on its range.

Let w be ANY closed nonnegative quadratic form such that ran P_R is in
its domain and w[v]=0 for every v in ran P_R. Require q0+w to be densely
defined, where q0 is the closed form of H0. Its closed sum defines H_W.
The coefficients/strength of w may depend arbitrarily on L and lambda.
No locality, diagonality, boundedness, or number conservation of w is
required. This includes every positive finite-range pair penalty of
arbitrary strength that vanishes on P_R. For an operator W>=0 the kernel
condition is W^{1/2} P_R=0; it implies WP_R=0 when read on this finite
subspace. A smaller physical forbidden displacement set can be enclosed
in the square of radius R; using the more restrictive P_R is permitted.

Then rhoW=exp(-beta H_W)/ZW exists on the whole Fock space and, uniformly
over all such forms and lambda in [-B,0],

 D(rhoW || rho0) <= G_L = O_{J,tau,B,R}(1/log L),
 ||rhoW-rho0||_1 <= min(2,sqrt(2 G_L)).                    (1.1)

An explicit public G_L is given below. Nothing on its right depends on
w's strength, its interacting spectrum, or an interacting Gibbs oracle.
Here D is quantum relative entropy with natural logarithms, and the norm
is the full trace norm (not half the trace norm).

The identical estimate holds for the exact hard restriction H_P=P_R H0
P_R on ran P_R, whose Gibbs state is embedded by zero into the full Fock
space. It also holds for a larger finite-dimensional physical allowed
subspace containing P_R, such as the no-double-occupation subspace with
no nearest-neighbor exclusion: the same P_R trial remains legal. These
are direct finite-dimensional compression statements, not an
unstated convergence assertion about every possible singular form limit.
Any specified penalty limit for which the states converge in trace norm
inherits (1.1) by lower semicontinuity of relative entropy.

2. PUBLIC CONSTANTS AND ALL-NUMBER FREE MOMENTS

Put theta=8 tau J, d_theta=1-exp(-theta), and define convergent public sums
over positive integer pairs k=(k1,k2) in N^2:

 N0=d_theta^{-1} sum_k exp(-theta |k|^2),
 Ntheta=d_theta^{-1} sum_k exp(-(theta/2)|k|^2),
 E_K=(2J pi^2/d_theta) sum_k |k|^2 exp(-theta |k|^2),
 Ctheta=exp(Ntheta).

Choose ell=floor(sqrt L). If ell>=3R, put t=log(ell/R)>1 and

 a_L=144 N0^2/L,
 b_L=144 Ctheta Ntheta^2/(theta L),
 H_L=512 Ctheta Ntheta^2/t.

For ell>=3R and a_L<=1/2, an explicit admissible bound is

 G_L = [ h2(a_L)+b_L log(S+1)+a_L
          +tau a_L(E_K+B N0)+H_L ]/(1-a_L),              (2.1)

where h2(a)=-a log a-(1-a)log(1-a), h2(0)=0. If either cutoff condition
fails, set G_L=N0, a valid KL bound by the vacuum comparison in Section 7.
The trace bound is always capped by 2. In the large-L regime
(2.1)=O(1/log L): all terms except
H_L are O(log L/L), and t=(1/2)log L+O_R(1).

For 0<=x<=pi, 2x^2/pi^2<=1-cos x<=x^2/2. Consequently

 8J|k|^2 <= L^2 epsilon_k <= 2J pi^2 |k|^2,
 0<=K<=16J M.

The normalized eigenvectors are
 phi_k(x)=(2/L) sin(pi k1 x1/L) sin(pi k2 x2/L),
so |phi_k(x)|^2<=4/L^2. In the free state, the independent mode occupations
are geometric with parameters exp(-q_k), q_k=beta epsilon_k-tau lambda.
Thus q_k>=theta |k|^2 and
 n0,k=(exp(q_k)-1)^{-1}<=d_theta^{-1}exp(-theta |k|^2).
In particular E0 M<=N0 and E0(L^2 K)<=E_K, uniformly in L and lambda.

Use the single fixed fugacity tilt
 rho_theta=exp(theta M) rho0 / E0 exp(theta M).
It is again a stable free Gaussian state: q_k-theta>=theta(|k|^2-1)
>=theta|k|^2/2>=theta. Its occupations satisfy
 ntheta,k<=d_theta^{-1} exp(-(theta/2)|k|^2).
Since log Ztheta=sum_k -log(1-exp(-(q_k-theta)))<=sum_k ntheta,k,
and Z0>=1,

 E0 exp(theta M)=Ztheta/Z0<=Ctheta.                       (2.2)

Therefore E0 M^r<=r! theta^{-r} Ctheta for every fixed integer r>=0.
The r! is a moment-order constant; no particle-sector m! is hidden here.
For any nonnegative diagonal observable A commuting with M,

 E0[(M-1)A] <= (Ctheta/theta) Etheta A                    (2.3)

whenever the left is used on M>=1; our pair observables vanish for M<2.
The same bound holds with M instead of M-1, since M<=exp(theta M)/theta.
This one tilt will absorb the degree factor in the many-particle gradient.

Let gamma_s(x,y)=Tr rho_s b_y^* b_x, s=0 or theta. Mode expansion gives
 gamma_s(x,x)<=4 Ns/L^2 and |gamma_s(x,y)|^2<=gamma_s(x,x)gamma_s(y,y).
Independent geometric modes give the exact normal-ordered Wick identity

 Gamma_s^{(2)}(x,y):=Tr rho_s b_x^* b_y^* b_y b_x
                  =gamma_s(x,x)gamma_s(y,y)+|gamma_s(x,y)|^2
                  <=32 Ns^2/L^4.                      (2.4)

At x=y this is E_s[n_x(n_x-1)], not E_s[n_x^2]. One can derive the Wick
identity directly by expanding in modes: the fourth moment is the sum of
the two pairings, and a geometric mode has E[N(N-1)]=2(E N)^2. No
independence assumption about particle positions is made.

3. THE COLLISION FILTER AND ITS EXACT SUPPORT

Sections 3-6 work in the cutoff regime ell>=3R and a_L<=1/2. Section 7
also supplies the all-L bound when either condition fails.

For z in Z^2, r=|z|_infinity, define

 f(z)=0,                        r<=R,
 f(z)=log(r/R)/t,                R<r<ell,
 f(z)=1,                        r>=ell.

On a labeled m-particle configuration X=(x1,...,xm), set
 F_m(X)=product_{a<b} f(x_a-x_b), with F_0=F_1=1.
This symmetric multiplier defines a diagonal operator F on boson Fock
space. It has 0<=F<=I, [F,M]=0, and finite rank because a configuration
with m>S has a duplicated site and hence F_m=0. Moreover ran F is inside
ran P_R, and w vanishes exactly on its range. In fact F is strictly
positive on every allowed basis configuration for ell>=3R, so ran F=P_R.

Put p=Tr rho0 F^2=1-delta, qM=Tr rho0 M(I-F^2), and sigma=F rho0 F/p.
The vacuum ensures p>0. The scalar product inequality

 1-product_{a<b} f(x_a-x_b)^2
       <=sum_{a<b}(1-f(x_a-x_b)^2)
       <=sum_{a<b} 1_{|x_a-x_b|_infinity<ell}

and (2.4) give

 delta <=(1/2) sum_{x,y in Lambda} Gamma_0^{(2)}(x,y)
                                  1_{|x-y|_infinity<ell}
       <=16 N0^2 S(2ell+1)^2/L^4 <= a_L.                 (3.1)

Similarly use M<=exp(theta M)/theta and (2.2), then the same tilted
Wick bound:

 qM <=(Ctheta/theta) 16 Ntheta^2 S(2ell+1)^2/L^4 <= b_L. (3.2)

Here S<=L^2 and (2ell+1)^2<=9L. The pair density is an exact Fock
observable; no truncation of particle number has been used.

4. A DISCRETE CAPACITY BOUND WITH AN EXPLICIT CONSTANT

Let E={+e1,-e1,+e2,-e2}. Define the directed lattice energy
 C_f=sum_{z in Z^2} sum_{e in E} [f(z+e)-f(z)]^2.
The sup radius changes by at most 1 on a lattice edge. A nonzero
difference crosses shells k to k+1, R<=k<=ell-1, and has magnitude
 log(1+1/k)/t<=1/(kt).
Shell k has exactly 8k points. There are at most 32k edges directed
from that shell to shell k+1 and the same number in reverse; hence

 C_f <=(64/t^2) sum_{k=R}^{ell-1} 1/k
     <=64(t+1/R)/t^2 <=128/t.                           (4.1)

This is a crude explicit test-function energy bound. It does not assume
that the logarithm is an exact discrete harmonic function, and it does
not approximate a continuum capacity without an error estimate.

5. EXACT THERMAL COMMUTATION AND THE GRADIENT COST

Since [rho0,K]=0,

 Tr rho0 F K F = Tr rho0 F^2 K
                 -(1/2) Tr rho0 [F,[F,K]].              (5.1)

All F expressions are finite-rank, so there is no unbounded cyclic-trace
problem. Denote the last contribution by Dir. We now bound it with the
correct labeled normalization; this is the place at which a spurious
m! or occupation-dependent hopping factor could otherwise enter.

On ell^2(Lambda^m), let K_m=sum_a h_L^{(a)}, and let P_sym be the
orthogonal symmetrizer. The lifted physical m-sector block of rho0 is

 r_m=Z0^{-1} exp(tau lambda m) P_sym exp(-beta K_m).

It is a positive matrix, with nonnegative real entries. Indeed the
one-particle heat kernel is nonnegative because -h_L has nonnegative
off-diagonal entries, its tensor product is nonnegative, and P_sym is
the average of permutation matrices and commutes with K_m. Its trace on
the full labeled space equals the physical weight of that m-sector.
Symmetric multipliers and K_m restrict to the physical sector, so their
traces with r_m are exactly the physical traces.

The matrix entry of [F,[F,K_m]] at X,Y is
 (F_m(X)-F_m(Y))^2 K_m(X,Y).
For a one-label nearest-neighbor hop X->X^{a,e}, K_m(X,X^{a,e})=-2J.
It follows exactly that

 Dir=J sum_{m,X,a,e: x_a+e in Lambda}
       r_m(X^{a,e},X) [F_m(X^{a,e})-F_m(X)]^2 >=0.       (5.2)

Positive semidefiniteness implies
 0<=r_m(Y,X)<=sqrt(r_m(X,X)r_m(Y,Y))
             <=(r_m(X,X)+r_m(Y,Y))/2.
The reverse-hop bijection exchanges X and Y. Thus (5.2) is at most

 J E0^{lab} sum_{a,e: x_a+e in Lambda} (Delta_{a,e} F_m)^2. (5.3)

Only m-1 factors of the pair product change when coordinate a moves.
Telescoping a product of numbers in [0,1] and Cauchy-Schwarz give

 |Delta_{a,e} F_m| <=sum_{b!=a}|f(x_a+e-x_b)-f(x_a-x_b)|,
 (Delta_{a,e} F_m)^2 <=(m-1) sum_{b!=a}
                           [f(x_a+e-x_b)-f(x_a-x_b)]^2. (5.4)

For m=0,1 the gradient is zero. Absorb m-1 with (2.3). The ordered
labeled-pair expectation in the tilted state is exactly

 Etheta^{lab} sum_{a!=b} g(x_a,x_b)
       =sum_{u,v in Lambda} Gamma_theta^{(2)}(u,v) g(u,v). (5.5)

To check the normalization in (5.5), an occupation vector n has
 w(n)=m!/product_x n_x! labeled representatives. The lifted normalized
occupation vector has squared coefficient 1/w(n) at each such tuple.
If the physical diagonal occupation weight is p(n), then r_m(X,X)
equals p(n)/w(n). Summing the w(n) copies cancels that factor, and the
ordered-pair count is n_u(n_v-delta_uv). This proves (5.5) exactly,
also for repeated sites, without an m! estimate or sector cutoff.

For each u, summing over v and permitted hops of u is at most C_f:

 sum_{u,v in Lambda} sum_{e: u+e in Lambda}
               [f(u+e-v)-f(u-v)]^2 <= S C_f <= L^2 C_f.

Combining (5.3)-(5.5), (2.4), and (4.1) yields

 Dir <= [32J Ctheta Ntheta^2/(theta L^2)] C_f,
 beta Dir <=[4096 tau J Ctheta Ntheta^2/(theta t)]
           =512 Ctheta Ntheta^2/t = H_L.                (5.6)

There is no derivative of a many-particle marginal and no low-number
approximation in this bound. The geometric-mode tilt controls all
particle sectors at once. The boundary has no omitted edge penalty:
K_m has only the actual in-box off-diagonal hops, and all Dirichlet
boundary killing appears on its diagonal, where the commutator is zero.

Since rho0 K is positive and F^2<=I, Tr rho0 F^2 K<=E0 K. Normalize
(5.1), use (5.6), and obtain

 beta(Tr sigma K-E0 K) <=[tau delta E_K+H_L]/p.          (5.7)

The number expression is exact:
 Tr sigma M-E0 M=(delta E0 M-qM)/p.
As lambda<=0, its chemical contribution satisfies

 beta Tr(sigma-rho0)(-lambda M/L^2)
       =-tau lambda (delta E0 M-qM)/p
       <=tau B delta N0/p.                              (5.8)

The use of lambda<=0 is explicit. Absolute-value estimates would cover a
stable bounded positive chemical range with modified constants, but that
extension is not needed or asserted in the theorem.

6. ENTROPY OF THE TWO-OUTCOME FILTER

Let G=sqrt(I-F^2), and omega=G rho0 G/delta when delta>0. The map
 v -> Fv tensor |0> + Gv tensor |1>
is an isometry. Dephasing the two-dimensional outcome factor increases
entropy, so

 S(rho0)<=h2(delta)+p S(sigma)+delta S(omega).
Therefore, even without division by p,
 S(rho0)-S(sigma)<=h2(delta)+delta S(omega),              (6.1)

where the dropped term is -delta S(sigma)<=0. This is an unconditioned
trial-state argument; no heralded operation is required in the archive.

For completeness the infinite-dimensional entropy step is legitimate.
rho0, F, and G commute with M. Each M-sector is finite dimensional,
and the outcome map preserves its weight. Finite-sector entropy
monotonicity can be summed over these sectors. The number distribution
has an exponential moment by (2.2), so its Shannon entropy is finite;
the total output also has finite mean M and its entropy is bounded by
the number Gibbs reference with a two-dimensional outcome register.
In particular Tr omega M=qM/delta<infinity, S(sigma)<infinity, and
S(omega)<infinity. This supplies a direct finite-block justification
instead of using entropy continuity on an uncontrolled Fock dimension.

For s=log(S+1), the full boson number reference has

 Z_M(s)=Tr exp(-sM)=(1-exp(-s))^{-S}=(1+1/S)^S<=e.

The Gibbs maximum-entropy inequality then implies
 delta S(omega)<=s qM+delta log Z_M(s)<=s qM+delta.
Together with (3.1)-(3.2) and monotonicity of h2 on [0,1/2],

 S(rho0)-S(sigma)<=h2(a_L)+b_L log(S+1)+a_L.            (6.2)

If delta=0 the same conclusion is read with no failure state. From
(5.7)-(5.8) and the free Gibbs identity,

 D(sigma||rho0)=S(rho0)-S(sigma)+beta Tr(sigma-rho0)H0
       <= h2(a_L)+b_L log(S+1)+a_L
           +[tau a_L(E_K+B N0)+H_L]/(1-a_L)
       <=G_L.                                         (6.3)

The common denominator in (2.1) is a convenient looser bound. The
entropy estimate itself does not need that denominator.

7. PARTITION COMPARISON AND THE UNBOUNDED FORM ENERGY IDENTITY

H0>=epsilon_min M, epsilon_min>0 for each fixed L. It has compact
resolvent and finite partition at every positive inverse temperature.
The closed form sum H_W>=H0 also has compact resolvent: its bounded form
balls embed compactly via the H0 form domain. Eigenvalue minmax therefore
gives ZW(beta')<=Z0(beta')<infinity for every beta'>0. Vacuum energy is
zero for both H0 and w, so ZW>=1. This proves existence of rhoW.

Let H_P be the finite-dimensional form compression of H0 to ran P_R,
and ZP=Tr_{P_R} exp(-beta H_P). For every trial subspace inside P_R the
H_W form equals the H0 form. Hence the first dim(P_R) minmax eigenvalues
of H_W are no larger than the corresponding H_P eigenvalues. Thus

 ZW>=ZP.                                                (7.1)

In the cutoff regime, the variational principle on P_R, applied to the
sigma of Section 3 supported there,
gives

 log(Z0/ZP)<=D(sigma||rho0)<=G_L.                        (7.2)

Only eigenvalue/variational comparison is used. Exponentiation of a
Loewner order is not assumed to be operator monotone.

For the unbounded penalty, the energy identity is justified as follows.
By the bound at beta/2, sum_j E_j exp(-beta E_j)<infinity, since for
E_j>=0, E_j exp(-beta E_j)<=[2/(e beta)]exp(-beta E_j/2).
Every eigenvector of H_W is in its form domain; its nonnegative H0 and
w form values sum to E_j. Taking the Gibbs-weighted sum gives finite
values of both expectations and

 Tr rhoW H_W = Tr rhoW H0 + E_{rhoW} w.

The Gibbs entropy is finite and equals beta Tr rhoW H_W+log ZW. Also
Tr rhoW H0 is finite, so both terms defining D(rhoW||rho0) are finite.
Consequently the exact form-sense identity is

D(rhoW||rho0)=log(Z0/ZW)-beta E_{rhoW} w
             <=log(Z0/ZP)<=G_L.                         (7.3)

This proves (1.1) by quantum Pinsker. It also shows the stronger
simultaneous conclusions
 0<=log(Z0/ZW)<=G_L,  beta E_{rhoW} w<=G_L,
 |F_W-F_0|=beta^{-1}log(Z0/ZW)<=G_L/(tau L^2),
where F=-beta^{-1}log Z is grand potential. The lower bound on the
partition log ratio follows from H_W>=H0 by minmax. These are form
expectations of w; no operator-domain expectation is silently assumed.

The vacuum also gives the all-L fallback
 D(rhoW||rho0)<=log Z0<=sum_k n0,k<=N0.
The same bound holds for the hard restriction, since ZP>=1. This
justifies the definition of G_L outside the cutoff regime in Section 2.
One may optionally improve G_L inside that regime by taking its minimum
with N0; the variational trial itself need not satisfy that smaller bound.

For the exact hard restriction, apply (7.2) and the finite-dimensional
Gibbs identity directly. Its embedded state satisfies
 D(rhoP||rho0)=log(Z0/ZP)<=G_L.
Thus the fully hard exclusion, not merely every finite penalty size,
is included without exchanging a large-L and infinite-strength limit.
If P_phys is a larger finite-dimensional allowed subspace containing P_R,
minmax gives Z_{P_phys}>=ZP. Its embedded Gibbs state has relative entropy
log(Z0/Z_{P_phys}), so the same conclusion includes exact onsite hard
core and other less restrictive physical collision projections.

8. COLLECTIVE CLASSICAL SUFFICIENCY AND CHARGED RESOURCES

Let n copies share the unknown lambda in [-B,0]. Their admissible
positive forms w_j may differ. Put rho_prod=tensor_j rhoW_j(lambda).
Relative entropy additivity yields

 D(rho_prod||rho0(lambda)^{tensor n})<=n G_L,
 ||rho_prod-rho0(lambda)^{tensor n}||_1
       <=min(2,sqrt(2nG_L)).                            (8.1)

Let K^(n)=sum_j K_j and T=sum_j M_j. The free n-copy state is
 proportional to exp(-beta K^(n)+tau lambda T).
For each integer t>=0 let sigma^0_{n,t} be the normalized restriction of
exp(-beta K^(n)) to the T=t sector. This finite-dimensional canonical
state depends only on public L,J,tau,n and t. The fixed channel

 Phi_n(X)=sum_{t>=0} Tr(P_{T=t}X) sigma^0_{n,t}

is measure-prepare, hence entanglement breaking, and fixes every free
n-copy state for every unknown lambda. It uses ONE total-number
statistic, not n separate counts. Contractivity and (8.1) give

 ||Phi_n(rho_prod)-rho_prod||_1
       <=min(2,2sqrt(2nG_L)).                            (8.2)

This remains true when the w_j fail to commute with particle number:
only the nearby free family must have the total-number sufficiency.
It is a statement about the entire density matrix. It does not preserve
a purification/reference system and does not claim energy accuracy.

The free total-number tail is bounded using (2.2):
 Pr_0(T>t_*)<=exp(n Ntheta-theta(t_*+1)).
Keep t=0,...,t_* plus one overflow symbol, preparing vacuum for overflow.
The resulting finite-outcome measure-prepare map Phi_{n,t_*} obeys

 ||Phi_{n,t_*}(rho_prod)-rho_prod||_1
 <=2sqrt(2nG_L)+2exp(n Ntheta-theta(t_*+1)),             (8.3)

again capped by 2. Only a FREE tail is needed. For tail probability at
most epsilon_tail, choose
 t_*=max(0,ceil[(n Ntheta+log(1/epsilon_tail))/theta]).
The persistent classical archive is at most ceil(log2(t_*+2)) bits,
with no persistent quantum register. At fixed public J,tau it costs
O(log(n+1)+loglog(1/epsilon_tail)) classical bits. For fixed n and error
it is independent of L and the strengths of all w_j. From (2.1), every
n(L)=o(log L) gives vanishing interacting-to-free trace error, and a
vanishing EB error if epsilon_tail also tends to zero.

Costs and supplied/acquired boundary:
 - The model geometry, J,tau,B,R,L,n and stable free Hamiltonian are
   public. lambda and all w_j are unknown; no oracle for them is used.
 - The F Jastrow filter is a variational witness, not an encoder that
   must be implemented or whose successful branch must be acquired.
 - The encoder is the specified total-number measurement. Its physical
   realization and error costs are charged separately; no efficient
   hardware implementation is claimed just by giving the CPTP map.
 - The finite cutoff and certified G_L use convergent public Gaussian
   sums and elementary arithmetic. The one-dimensional Gaussian series
   factorize, and positive decreasing tails are bounded by their first
   omitted term plus a Gaussian integral. Hence constants are acquired
   with stated truncation/rounding margins, without a capacity oracle,
   when the public scalar parameters have effective representations and
   a known positive lower bound on theta. An arbitrary noncomputable real
   parameter is not assigned a numerical compiler by this theorem. The
   exponential Ctheta bound is deliberately crude and may make certified
   small-error lattice-size thresholds extremely large.
 - Canonical preparation is an actual decoder task. A straightforward
   enumerator uses the nS free sine modes and all occupation multisets
   of total t, whose count is binomial(nS+t-1,t). Evaluating their free
   Boltzmann weights and preparing that mixture is finite and explicit;
   its time, storage, sampling precision, and physical output cost are
   not suppressed. At fixed n,t it is polynomial in S, but no efficient
   bound uniform in n and error is asserted. The canonical output need
   not be generated by querying an interacting eigenstate oracle.
 - Gate-probability and sine-mode preparation errors add trace errors
   to (8.3); they need their own precision budget in an implementation.
 - Input Gibbs-state generation is not acquired here. This result is a
   comparison/archival theorem for supplied states from the admitted
   family, not an efficient solver for all positive many-body forms.
 - Changing unknown J or tau changes the free canonical decoder; total
   number alone is not claimed sufficient for those enlarged families.
 - Copies with different unknown lambda_j are outside the ONE-statistic
   corollary; separate counts remain possible with different cost.

The strongest matched control is the exact known free family, for which
the same total-number channel is exactly sufficient. The estimate shows
all admitted positive penalties are uniformly invisible in the critical
limit relative to that control. It does not prove a quantum-memory
lower bound, a universality statement for negative interactions, or an
optimal finite-size/copy-number threshold.

9. WHAT WAS NEEDED AND WHAT IS STILL OPEN

All load-bearing steps for this theorem are proved above: sine bounds,
stable tilted free moments, normal-ordered collision density, discrete
logarithmic capacity, exact commutator with correct boson normalization,
entropy, form-domain partition comparison, and the collective decoder.
There is no unproved interacting regularity/coercivity input and no
conditional asymptotic limit in the main finite-L bound.

The kernel hypothesis is substantive. A positive one-body potential
nonzero on allowed one-particle states is not admitted. A form that only
has a small expectation on P_R instead of annihilating it needs an extra
trial w-energy term; arbitrary-strength uniformity then cannot follow
from this proof. Negative or attractive interactions are not admitted.
Growing R, closing free-gap/fugacity regimes, unknown K or beta, and
reference-preserving reconstruction require fresh estimates and are not
silently obtained by replacing a constant in (2.1).

Scientific status is INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. An independent human
or formal proof audit is still needed. Established 2D logarithmic
scattering/Jastrow, Wick, entropy, and Gibbs-variational techniques are
prior; the precise lattice all-positive-form wholetrace theorem and
collective archive interface are the output being banked. External
priority and optimality are UNKNOWN.
