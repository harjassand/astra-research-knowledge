CYCLE 10 — SQUARE NEGATIVE-CORNER HOOKUP GATE
Luna/max independent obstruction audit, 2026-10-09

Status
------
The full-field geometry exposes a necessary gate in any square hookup proof:
high Q and finite asymmetric side-mean ranges do not themselves certify that
the two walk tips lie on the same side of the completed oriented crosscut. I
give a positive-volume negative-corner square witness with both Q values at
1/100, a nonempty all-negative T transcript, and walk tips on opposite sides
when the complete crosscut is fixed. Conditional on that full crosscut, every
bridge hits it before the landing.

This is not yet a counterexample to the G1 conditioning on only
(Phi_(R'),Theta_R): that pair leaves the annulus unexposed, and other
annular crosscut completions can change which side contains a walk tip. I do
not claim that the partial-record conditional hookup probability is zero.
The remaining gate is a uniform positive probability of a same-side-
compatible annular completion under the exact ordered
(Gamma_R,T(Gamma_R)) square law. The side-mean range is used only as the
supplied finite input identified below; it is not rederived here.

Explicit square witness
-----------------------
Consider the finite square NN graph on

    V_M = { (x,y) in Z^2 : -M <= x,y <= M },

with M=12R and R a sufficiently large multiple of 6. Pin the right boundary
arc to +1/2 and the complementary left boundary arc to -1/2, with transitions
on the top and bottom edges between columns 0 and 1. These pins lie in the
requested asymmetric intervals [c,1/2] and [-1,-c] for every c<=1/2.

On free vertices take the sign pattern

    h(x,y) = +1/4  if x>=1 and (x,y)!=(1,y0),
             -1/4  otherwise,
    y0=R/3.

The free-edge differences are at most 1/2, and every free-to-boundary
difference is strictly less than 1. Only the two fixed transition edges can
be saturated, at difference 1. Thus the sign event has positive relative
Lebesgue volume: an open neighborhood of the displayed free-height vector
preserves all signs and the exact interface. The positive region is connected
and the negative region is connected. Their negative-corner dual-NN interface
is one simple boundary-to-boundary crosscut, vertical except for the one-site
negative notch at (1,y0). It crosses the primal edge from (0,0) to (1,0); let
that dual edge be e_sigma^*. Its positive side is east when the path is
oriented from bottom to top.

The notch explicitly keeps the T-observations. In the local corner order
(NW,NE,SE,SW), the face with lower-left corner (0,y0-1) has signs
(-,-,+,-); the negative-corner rule traverses bottom-to-right and queries
T=(0,y0)<0. The face with lower-left corner (0,y0) has signs (-,+,-,-); the
rule traverses right-to-top and again queries T=(0,y0)<0. No other turn of
this crosscut queries an off-cut site. Hence the exact transcript includes
the deduplicated set T(Gamma_R)={(0,y0)} with its required negative sign; it
does not discard a turn query or add an unused checkerboard flag.

The finite local check at `local_witness_check.py` builds the face-pairing
graph for a 25-by-25 vertex square with y0=4. It finds one simple bottom-to-top
crosscut of 26 dual edges and exactly the two listed turn queries, both at
(0,4)<0. Its retained output is `LOCAL_WITNESS_CHECK.json`. This is a bounded
diagnostic of the explicit notch geometry; the general side-separation
argument is the planar crosscut fact stated below.

Take R'=2R and observer v=(10R,0). The boundary and observer are outside
B_(6R). The outer interface tips are at (1/2,+2R)+O(1) and
(1/2,-2R)+O(1); the inner tips are at (1/2,+R)+O(1) and
(1/2,-R)+O(1). The notch is at height R/3, away from the tips. Set the outer
walk prefix to the horizontal path from v to q_out=(2R,0), and set the
reversed inner walk piece to the horizontal path from 0 to
q_in=(-R,0). The prescribed root contact at 0 is allowed; after its first
step the inner piece stays on the west side and has no forbidden contact.

For all sufficiently large R, every distance appearing in Q(Phi_(2R)) and
Q(Theta_R), before the cap 1/100, is at least R/2. In particular, each strand
tip is order R from the corresponding walk piece, each walk tip is order R
from the observed strand piece, and the two strand tips are order R apart.
The notch changes these bounds by O(1). Thus

    Q(Phi_(2R)) = Q(Theta_R) = 1/100

for the capped definition, and the records satisfy any threshold
0<a<=1/200. Rounded-disk conventions only change the estimates by O(1).

The pair of records has positive probability. The sign event above has
positive volume, and the independent walk can follow any prescribed finite
nearest-neighbor route with positive probability. For example, after reaching
q_out, route through (2R,-3R/2), then (-R,-3R/2), then q_in, and finally 0.
This route crosses the unobserved annulus between radii R and 2R and reaches
0 for the first time only at its last step. Its probability is 4^{-ell_R}
for a finite ell_R=O(R), which is positive. The outer and inner records expose
none of this bridge.

Full-crosscut conditional bridge obstruction
---------------------------------------------
Fix the complete vertical-with-notch crosscut from the field witness, not
just its partial record. The ordered port pair makes its positive bank east.
The outer walk tip q_out is on that positive side; the reversed inner-walk
tip q_in is on the negative side. Any full walk from q_out to q_in must
cross the crosscut before reaching 0. The square dual-NN crosscut separates
the two primal sides: every NN path between them traverses an edge crossed
by the crosscut and hence visits a vertex adjacent to it. Thus

    P(Z^sigma | complete Gamma, the two walk pieces) = 0

for this fixed full field/interface, despite both partial-record Q values
being 1/100. The finite walk path described above witnesses that the same
partial-record values occur with positive probability in the full
field/walk experiment; it necessarily has an earlier stopping contact.

This does not imply

    P(Z^sigma | Phi_(2R),Theta_R) = 0.

The unobserved annulus can route the completed crosscut around q_out or
q_in. The local port order alone does not rule out those alternatives, and
the partial record conditions do not freeze the full crosscut. A valid
counterexample to the partial-record G1 lower bound would need to show that
all such alternative annular completions have zero or vanishing conditional
mass; this packet does not do that. The exact conclusion is a falsifier for
the shortcut that fixes a full crosscut first and then asserts a uniform
point-versus-continuum lower bound for every high-Q pair of walk pieces.

The supplied finite actual-Gamma mean-range theorem states, for every
positive-probability complete ordered path under square +/-1/2 Dobrushin
pins, that E[h_x|Gamma] lies in [c,1/2] on the positive side and in
[-1,-c] on the negative side. The displayed complete Gamma is such a path,
and the construction uses the full negative T constraint. Thus assuming that
supplied finite range input, these records are also in the finite asymmetric
side-gap regime. No inference from geometric clearance to a height gap is
used. The finite range input is internal/supplied, not independently
validated by this audit. These ranges constrain conditional means, not the
realized heights on an attachment: a variable uniform on [0,1] has mean 1/2
and has no deterministic positive lower pin margin. Thus the source barrier
proposition's pure-exterior hypothesis on prescribed heights cannot be
filled by substituting the mean c without an additional attachment argument.

Why this is a hookup obstruction
-------------------------------
The source candidate defines the landing event by both first-contact and
edge-membership, Z^sigma={S_tau=0, e_sigma^* subset gamma}, while Phi and
Theta reveal only the outer and inner pieces. Its Q quantities test local
separation from the pieces shown in each record; they do not test whether
the two walk tips occupy the same component of the complement of a compatible
completed crosscut. The hidden annulus can contain the first walk/crosscut
contact. In the square ordered record, the inner orientation cannot be
reversed after conditioning to make the sides match.

The proof must retain the annular completion as a random object until side
compatibility is established. A sufficient record-level route would show
uniform positive mass for same-side-compatible completions, then prove
barrier avoidance and a 1/log R walk bridge lower bound on that mass.
Alternatively, one could condition on a completed interface and use a
same-side test there, but would still need a lower bound for that test under
the partial-record law. Same-side compatibility is necessary, not
sufficient. The asymmetric mean intervals do not encode the annular side
label, and they are means rather than prescribed attachment pins.
