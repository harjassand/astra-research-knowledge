# Cycle 2: clone-level safety under hidden turnover

## Outcome and status

This cycle moved from endpoint-only CTMC aliasing to a different interface: branching populations observed through expected state counts. The lead result is a fully specified decision reversal. Two continuous-time branching models have the same expected S/R/U population trajectories under each of two fixed interventions, but their founder-clone target-and-safety probabilities rank the interventions in opposite orders. Thus even an exact, continuously measured **mean** time series does not determine this clone-level decision when birth and death are hidden behind the same net growth rate.

This is a conditional branching-process result, not a new general identifiability theorem or a biological discovery. The branching PGF and the net-birth-rate confounding are standard and are covered by established work. The Cycle 2 value is the explicit connection from that ambiguity to a safe intervention choice, an exact one-snapshot moment interface, and a finite-founder decision rule. No novelty claim is made.

The two-state CTMC issue from the parent message remains separate. With no branching, constant two-state coefficients, and an accurately observed nonconstant full trajectory, switching rates and growth difference can be identified; a single endpoint or arbitrary time-varying rates are different interfaces. This report does not use endpoint aliasing to challenge that result.

## Model and decision interface

Start one verified founder in proliferating state S. During one fixed intervention and context, each S cell independently:

- divides to two S cells at constant rate b;
- dies at constant rate d;
- switches to a persistent target mark R at rate a;
- switches to a persistent harm mark U at rate q.

R and U do not proliferate, die, or revert before the single terminal observation at T. This deliberately simple state ontology makes `U_T > 0` equivalent to “the clone ever entered U” and `R_T > 0, U_T = 0` the success event. The assumptions can be replaced, but the formulas then must be rederived. In particular, a transient/reversible harm marker, state-specific growth of R/U, cell interaction, or incomplete clone recovery invalidates this exact model.

There are two candidate fixed interventions. Both act through their state-switching rates (a,q); birth and death rates are assumed intervention-invariant within the frozen context. The selected decision metric is

\[
J_u=\Pr_u(R_T>0,\ U_T=0),
\]

with an upper bound \(\Pr_u(U_T>0)\le\delta\) if the application requires a separate risk threshold. `Target` and `harm` need biological definitions fixed in advance; the examples below are synthetic and carry no schedule recommendation.

The observation unit is an independently randomized founder clone, not a descendant cell. The experiment begins with a known roster of pure S founders. At one terminal time, recover all descendants, their founder identity, and their persistent R/U state; record clones with no descendants as zero. This is one timepoint, but it is a complete clone census. If barcode dropout, subsampling, reporter error, cross-founder mixing, or spatial loss is unbounded, return `UNKNOWN`; counting sampled descendants as independent founders is invalid.

## Exact mean-trajectory alias and action reversal

For a fixed action \(u=(a,q)\), the mean cell counts solve

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}m_S\\m_R\\m_U\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}b-d-a-q&0&0\\a&0&0\\q&0&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}m_S\\m_R\\m_U\end{pmatrix},
\qquad (m_S,m_R,m_U)(0)=(1,0,0).
\]

Writing \(r=b-d-a-q\), this gives \(m_S(t)=e^{rt}\), \(m_R(t)=a\int_0^t e^{rs}\,ds\), and \(m_U(t)=q\int_0^t e^{rs}\,ds\). Every mean curve depends on b,d only through \(b-d\).

Take two worlds with the same net growth:

\[
W_L:(b,d)=(1,0.5),\qquad W_H:(b,d)=(10.5,10),\qquad b-d=0.5.
\]

Each world offers two arms at T=1:

- A: \((a,q)=(0.1,0)\);
- B: \((a,q)=(0.2,0.3)\).

For each arm, all three expected state-count curves are exactly equal between \(W_L\) and \(W_H\), at every time. At T=1 the synthetic predictions are:

| Arm | Mean (S,R,U) in either world | \(J_u\) in \(W_L\) | \(J_u\) in \(W_H\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | (1.491825, 0.122956, 0) | 0.118170 | 0.087765 |
| B | (1.000000, 0.200000, 0.300000) | 0.157318 | 0.054211 |

So B is better in \(W_L\), while A is better in \(W_H\). The gaps are 0.039148 and 0.033554. This is stronger than saying clone-risk levels differ: the intervention ranking reverses although the full expected state-count time series in every arm is unchanged. No sample size of observations that contain only those expected means can identify the correct ranking.

The load-bearing calculation is the branching PGF. Define

\[
H(x,t)=\mathbb E[x^{R_t}\mathbf 1\{U_t=0\}\mid S_0=1].
\]

Conditioning on the first event in an interval dt yields

\[
\partial_t H=bH^2-(b+d+a+q)H+d+ax,\qquad H(x,0)=1.
\]

The \(bH^2\) term is the two independent daughter clones after division; death contributes d; a target switch contributes ax; and a harm switch contributes zero. Therefore

\[
J_u=H(1,T)-H(0,T),\qquad \Pr(U_T>0)=1-H(1,T).
\]

The executable calculator solves this Riccati equation in closed form and independently checks all four decision-reversal values with RK4. The deterministic formula, mean-curve equality, endpoint-moment inversions, numerical values, and finite-data arithmetic are in [branching_safety_controller.py](branching_safety_controller.py) and [branching_safety_controller_results.json](branching_safety_controller_results.json).

For the two aliased worlds and arms, any policy based only on the common mean-curve input faces minimax regret at least 0.018068, even if randomized optimally between A and B. The equalizer chooses B with probability 0.53847. Choosing B deterministically has worst-case regret 0.033554. These are model-level decision bounds, not natural-system predictions.

## What one founder-resolved endpoint adds

The full expected trajectories above are insufficient because they retain only net growth. In this restricted model, a single clone-resolved terminal census can identify the branching rates at the population-law level. Let \(m_S,m_R,m_U\) be unconditional means per seeded founder and \(v_S=\operatorname{Var}(S_T)\). Let \(r=b-d-a-q\). Direct moment equations give

\[
m_S=e^{rT},\quad m_R=a\frac{e^{rT}-1}{r},\quad
m_U=q\frac{e^{rT}-1}{r},
\]

with the continuous limits \(m_R=aT,m_U=qT\) at r=0. Since the safe-cell count is a linear birth-death process with birth rate b and total removal rate d+a+q,

\[
v_S=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{b+d+a+q}{r}\,m_S(m_S-1),&r\ne0,\\
(b+d+a+q)T,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\]

For r≠0, recover

\[
r=\frac{\log m_S}{T},\qquad
a=\frac{r m_R}{m_S-1},\qquad q=\frac{r m_U}{m_S-1},\qquad
s=\frac{r v_S}{m_S(m_S-1)},
\]

where s=b+d+a+q; for r=0, use \(a=m_R/T\), \(q=m_U/T\), and \(s=v_S/T\). Then \(b=(s+r)/2\) and \(d=(s-r)/2-a-q\). This separates intervention-dependent switching (a,q) from the intervention-invariant birth and death hazards (b,d). At the exact r=0 examples above, \(v_S=2\) in \(W_L\) and 21 in \(W_H\) for arm B, despite identical means.

This is a sufficiency statement for exact population moments, not a finite-sample rate guarantee. It requires unconditional moments across founder clones, including extinction zeros. Conditional moments among surviving clones do not obey this inverse. It also does not establish an assay-minimality theorem: other lineage, covariance, or event-history observations can be sufficient.

## Finite-data robust choice rule

There are m fixed actions and n independent founder clones randomized per action. For clone \(\ell\) in action u record

\[
Y_{u\ell}=\mathbf 1\{R_{u\ell}(T)>0,\ U_{u\ell}(T)=0\},\qquad
Z_{u\ell}=\mathbf 1\{U_{u\ell}(T)>0\}.
\]

These are binary founder outcomes. Descendant count does not increase n. For known endpoint classification and familywise error \(\alpha\), use

\[
h_n=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4m/\alpha)}{2n}},\quad
L_u=\bar Y_u-h_n,\quad U^Z_u=\bar Z_u+h_n.
\]

Hoeffding plus a union bound over the 2m Bernoulli means gives simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\). Given a safety threshold \(\delta\) and a measured action-cost cap, define

\[
\mathcal A_{\rm cert}=\{u:U^Z_u\le\delta,\ c_u\le C\}.
\]

Select \(\arg\max_{u\in\mathcal A_{\rm cert}} L_u\). If this set is empty, output `UNKNOWN / no certified action`; do not force a selection. On the coverage event, the selected action's true harm probability is at most \(\delta\). If an optimal action has true harm probability at most \(\delta-2h_n\), it is certified, and the chosen target probability is within \(2h_n\) of the best target probability among such margin-feasible actions. If the best option lies exactly on the risk boundary, finite data may correctly return unknown.

For m=2, \(\alpha=0.05\), and a desired interval half-width of 0.05, the formula requires 1,016 independent founders per arm. This guarantee is conditional on founder independence, fixed endpoints, complete clone recovery, and correct event classification. If target/harm markers have known nondifferential Youden index J, the separation in observed means is reduced by J; if calibration uncertainty is estimated, include those calibration parameters in the same familywise confidence set. If founders cluster within donors/animals, the independent experimental unit is the donor/animal or the analysis must include the clustering; thousands of related descendants are not thousands of trials.

For just ranking A versus B in the explicit alias pair, the smaller true gap is 0.033554. The difference-of-two-Bernoulli-means Hoeffding bound gives error at most \(e^{-n\Delta^2}\); 2,661 founders per arm suffice for at most 5% ranking error at that supplied gap. This bound is conditional on the two-arm model and does not replace a pilot. The controller must separately charge barcode/library construction, endpoint assays, all descendants recovered per founder, marker calibration, culture/intervention resources, and independent donor replication. There is no supplied dataset from which to estimate the biological n or assay cost.

## Intervention-invariant rates and acquisition gate

The example assumes b,d are common across interventions while a,q can change by intervention. Do not pool b,d merely because a test fails to reject equality. Before pooling, set biologically meaningful equivalence margins \(\eta_b,\eta_d\), estimate each arm's rates from clone-resolved data, and require simultaneous confidence intervals for between-arm differences to lie inside both margins. If they do not, retain arm-specific \((b_u,d_u)\) and optimize with their separate uncertainty sets. Rate rescaling, treatment-specific toxicity, or factor exposure changing division/death invalidates the common-rate assumption.

The controller's safety guarantee uses the direct founder-level Y/Z outcomes and therefore does not need the common-rate assumption. The rates are used to explain the mean-data alias and support model extrapolation; the robust decision itself stays valid under arbitrary rate noninvariance if each action's observed outcomes are representative of its future randomized clones. This separation avoids silently relying on an untested invariant-rate premise.

If a mechanistic confidence set is desired, the same endpoint clone census supplies a finite data likelihood from full founder-clone count vectors; alternatively, complete event histories supply cause-specific counts and at-risk cell-time. In an event-history design with cause count C_j, exposure A_j, predictable intensity \(\lambda_jY_j(t)\), a prespecified bound \(0\le\lambda_j\le\Lambda\), and a capped exposure \(A_j\le A_{\max}\), the unit-jump Freedman radius

\[
\beta=\sqrt{2\Lambda A_{\max}x}+\frac{2x}{3},\qquad
x=\log(2J/\alpha),
\]

gives simultaneous \(|C_j-\lambda_jA_j|\le\beta\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\) across J cause rates, by the counting-process martingale bound and a union bound. When A_j>0 this yields \([\max(0,C_j-\beta)/A_j,\min(\Lambda,(C_j+\beta)/A_j)]\). If exposure is too small, the interval is vacuous and the controller returns unknown. The calculator's illustrative \(\Lambda=12\), \(A_{\max}=200\), J=6, \(\alpha=0.05\) radius is 165.85 events: this is intentionally a warning that rate separation may cost far more imaging/cell-time than direct founder-outcome certification. These rate intervals are not required for the direct Hoeffding safety rule.

## Strongest baseline and literature boundary

The closest statistical baseline is Gunnarsson, Foo & Leder, “Statistical inference of the rates of cell proliferation and phenotypic switching in cancer,” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 568 (2023), [DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111497](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111497), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.08096). Their multitype branching process explicitly separates division, death and switching. Its mean matrix depends on birth/death only through the net rate b−d; the paper proves pure-start fraction slopes identify switching rates and discusses recovering birth rates from initial covariance slopes after switching/net rates are known. Therefore neither our net-growth alias nor the use of count variance to separate turnover is new. Their likelihood-based estimator on equally informed replicated count data is the strongest parameter-inference control for a mechanistic-rate objective. Our direct Y/Z selector should be compared with that method at equal numbers of independent founders, cell-count burden, assay error, and model misspecification.

The closest lineage trajectory baseline is Ventre et al., “Trajectory inference for a branching SDE model of cell differentiation via lineage tracing,” *Journal of Mathematical Biology* 92 (2026), [DOI 10.1007/s00285-026-02375-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-026-02375-5), [open article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00285-026-02375-5). Their theorem uses observable generation numbers and all leaves of complete lineage trees; exact trajectory recovery is proved for zero death. They explicitly characterize bias with death and/or subsampling, with correction requiring added lineage information. This is a stronger baseline than means and must be included when pedigrees are available. It also cautions that a terminal assay missing descendants cannot carry the proposed coverage guarantee.

Other adjacent prior art includes Hormoz et al.'s KCA inference from lineage trees and endpoint single-cell measurements ([Cell Systems 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015)) and time-series composition/growth fitting in Transcompp ([Jagannathan et al., Bioinformatics 2020, DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021)). They address richer transition/rate inference than endpoint aggregate fractions. Neither should be presented as defeated by this model. Their information contracts differ from an absolute per-founder target-and-ever-harm outcome.

## Falsifiable test and costs

The model predicts that when two contexts/treatments have matched net growth and the same switching rates, their exact absolute expected S/R/U curves match while clone-level target-safe success may differ because turnover differs. In the explicit synthetic pair, one founder endpoint census predicts a much higher safe-target probability for B in the low-turnover world and for A in the high-turnover world. A falsifier is a well-measured branching dataset whose observed founder-level joint outcomes and clone count moments fall outside the model's prespecified confidence region, including non-exponential event times, state-dependent rates, clone interaction, reporter reversal, or incomplete lineage recovery.

A practical blinded assay, if a biological question is later chosen, would randomize the same number of independently established founder clones to each candidate exposure in one fixed cell/donor/injury context; record founder barcodes at baseline; use a permanent target and harm recorder or a direct lineage image that captures all descendants; collect all clone-level S/R/U counts at a preregistered T; and reserve independent founders/donors for validation. Include vehicle, each candidate treatment, reporter-calibration controls, barcode-dropout controls, and the strongest equally informed GFL likelihood and Ventre lineage-reweighting comparators. Compare all arms on equal founders, lineage reads, sequencing/imaging burden, missingness, and total treatment cost. A treatment only passes if its held-out founder-level lower target bound and upper harm bound meet prespecified thresholds. This is a design to test a model, not a recommendation to use a cell intervention.

- Remaining gates: test whether complete permanent fate marks and founder-level clone census are feasible for a specified system; acquire rates/outcome data; preregister treatment invariance margins and an unsafe-risk threshold; quantify reporter and dropout uncertainty; compare against the full Gunnarsson likelihood and the applicable Ventre lineage method; obtain an independent mathematical review if a theorem claim is advanced.
