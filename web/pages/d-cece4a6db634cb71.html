## Family 290: conditional absence of a uniform modular mean rate

Proposer: `rounding_prior`; stable proposal:
`notes/rounding_prior/cycle19/MODULAR_RECOVERY_COMPILER_AND_RATE_BOUNDARY.md`.
Source: *Bounded recovery for modular spectral averages*, dated September 23,
2026, pinned release commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`.
Read scope: Section 3 in full, bounded-recovery Theorem 4.1 and its initial
proof setup as an excerpt. The implication below uses the theorem statement,
not an independent audit of its whole proof.

Assume the theorem's faithful normal state `φ` and scalar centralizer,
with `H=L²(M)`, unit `ξ=φ^{1/2}`, `D=log Δ`, `U_s=e^{isD}`. Additionally
assume unit vectors `h_n⊥ξ` supported in `[-δ_n,δ_n]`, with `δ_n↓0`.
This additional spectral premise is not proved from the scalar centralizer.

Fix `T=I−P_ξ`. Since `Dξ=0`, this projection commutes with `U_s` and
`TU_s h_n=U_s h_n`. The averaged squared response is exactly 1. Theorem
4.1 therefore yields, along a subsequence, `v_n∈M` with constants
`C_*<∞, η>0`, `‖v_n‖≤C_*`, `‖Tv_nξ‖≥η`, and spectral support of
`v_nξ` in `[-4δ_n,4δ_n]`.

Center `w_n=v_n−φ(v_n)1`. The identity
`φ(v_n)=⟨v_nξ,ξ⟩` gives `w_nξ=Tv_nξ`; centering preserves the
lower bound exactly. It also preserves the band because `Dξ=0`.
The state bound `|φ(v_n)|≤‖v_n‖` gives `‖w_n‖≤2C_*`.
Set `x_n=w_n/(2C_*)`; then `‖x_n‖≤1`, `φ(x_n)=0`, and
`‖x_nξ‖≥η/(2C_*)`.

For fixed `R>0`, define
`A_R(x)=(2R)^{-1}∫_{−R}^{R}σ_s(x) ds`. The spectral theorem and
`σ_s(x)ξ=U_sxξ` imply

`‖(A_R(x_n)−x_n)ξ‖≤4Rδ_n‖x_nξ‖`.

Choose a member of the same sequence with `δ_n≤1/(8R)`. Then
`‖A_R(x_n)ξ‖≥η/(4C_*)`. All constants were acquired with the single
fixed `T`, before choosing `R`. Thus, conditionally on the extra spectral
premise, the centered operator unit ball has no uniform mean-ergodic decay
rate in the Hilbert-vector norm.

Verdict: **PASS for the stated conditional implication**. The quantifier is
`∀R ∃x_R`, not one operator failing its pointwise ergodic limit. This is
compatible with the source's ordinary Hilbert and predual norm convergence
for each fixed input. It gives no type-III acquisition theorem, physical
mixing bound, or noncommutative trace-moment theorem.

## Family 251: priority correction, commutation and infinite-dimensional norm

Proposer: `frontier_groups`; source:
`sources/frontier_groups/cycle19/release/preprints/Strong-Ulam-Stability-Characterizes-Amenability-October-5-2026/build/strong-ulam-stability.tex`.
Read scope: sparse-flag properties (F1)–(F3), the prescribed-subspace transfer
and exact-fixing lemmas, Hilbert/circuit construction, and Proposition 5.1's
comparison proof. This is an excerpt audit; the boundary/detection argument
and entire theorem were not independently reconstructed here.

At level `k`, `G_k` is identity on compressed `X_k` and commutes with
coordinate sectors `X_{k+1}` and `Y_k`. A current arrival projection selects
one compressed `X_k` line and `Y_{k−1}`; the next departure projection
`Q_{k+1}` selects `X_{k+1},Y_k`. Consequently they commute as Hilbert
orthogonal projections. The smaller priority includes every active next
departure at its location. The nested transfer estimate therefore gives
`‖(K_k−I)Q_{k+1}‖≤Chη` on each orthogonal arrival block. Off those
blocks `K_k=I`; an orthogonal direct sum preserves the bound without any
edge/label count.

The exact-fixing lemma supplies a unitary `K̃_k` fixing `Q_{k+1}` pointwise
and satisfying `‖K̃_k−K_k‖≤Chη`, `‖K̃_k−I‖≤Ch`. A unitary fixing
a closed subspace pointwise reduces its orthogonal complement. After
extension by identity outside level `k`, `K̃_k` commutes with the next
exchange: that exchange acts only on `Q_{k+1}H_k` and an arrival subspace
in `H_{k+1}`, on both of which `K̃_k` is identity. All later layers have
disjoint levels.

The corrected factors act on distinct orthogonal levels; hence
`‖∏_kK̃_k−I‖=sup_k‖K̃_k−I‖≤Ch`. Replacing the original factors costs
`CNhη` by ordinary finite product telescoping; changing arrival frames costs
`CNη`. Thus the stated `Ch+CNη+CNhη` circuit comparison is justified.
It does not follow from orthogonal initial supports alone: exact reduction
of the next departure subspace is the essential commutation condition.

Infinite group locations cause no additional difficulty here. Each layer is
an orthogonal sum of uniformly bounded finite-fiber blocks, so its norm is
the supremum of the block norms. The circuit has finitely many layers.
These are full operator-norm estimates, rather than strong-operator limits
or finite-support approximations.

In the exact-fixing lemma, `‖U−I‖<1/2` is load-bearing in infinite dimension:
the compression `d` on the complementary subspace satisfies `‖d−I‖<1`,
so it is invertible; its polar factor is an onto unitary. Without this norm
condition a polar factor can be only a partial isometry. The source meets
the condition by taking its numerical `h_0` sufficiently small.

Verdict: **PASS for the targeted comparison and full-norm scope**, conditional
on the source's sparse/adjusted-frame estimates. The proposer has separately
extracted the heterogeneous layered correction lemma in
`notes/frontier_groups/cycle19/ULAM_TOOLS.md`; this gate does not assert a new
similarity theorem or the complete strong-Ulam characterization.
