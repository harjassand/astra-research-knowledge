Result: a matching information bound for recovering coherent dynamics
The strongest result is a quantitative obstruction to reconstructing real-time dynamics from imaginary-time measurements, together with a reconstruction algorithm attaining the same exponential order.
The obstruction survives several conditions that might otherwise be expected to make the problem tractable: a known complete spectrum, a fixed ground-state gap, substantial overlap with every eigenmode, nonnegative imaginary-time propagation, and a one-dimensional nearest-neighbour realization.
For an imaginary-time measurement window \(B\) and a requested real-time prediction horizon \(T\), the derived sample requirement is
\[
\boxed{
N_*(T,B)
=
\exp\!\left[\Theta\!\left(T\log\!\left(2+\frac{T}{B}\right)\right)\right].
}
\]
This is a worst-case, constant-accuracy result under the measurement models specified below. The constants in the exponent are not optimized.
This is not a new fast simulator. General ill-posedness of analytic continuation is established mathematics. The candidate contribution is the matching quantitative law and its realization under the strong physical restrictions above. Historical priority for that combination remains unresolved. DOI
1. The selected obstruction and exact access model
Let an unknown probability measure \(\mu\) be supported on \([0,1]\). Define
\[
G_\mu(s)=\int e^{-sx}\,d\mu(x),
\]
\[
A_\mu(t)=\int e^{-itx}\,d\mu(x),
\qquad
p_\mu(t)=|A_\mu(t)|^2.
\]
For a Hamiltonian \(H\) with spectrum in \([0,1]\) and a prepared state \(\psi\), these are
\[
G_\mu(s)=\langle\psi,e^{-sH}\psi\rangle,
\qquad
p_\mu(t)=|\langle\psi,e^{-itH}\psi\rangle|^2.
\]
Thus the target can be an ordinary return probability, not merely an inaccessible complex phase.
A measurement chooses \(s\in[0,B]\). I considered two separate noise models:
\[
\text{additive data:}\qquad G_\mu(s)+Z,
\]
\[
\text{relative-precision data:}\qquad \log G_\mu(s)+Z,
\]
where every call has independent standard Gaussian noise \(Z\). The second model is deliberately generous: it permits constant-precision measurements of the logarithm even when \(G_\mu(s)\) is small.
Query locations may be chosen adaptively. The algorithm may use unlimited processing. It is not given the Hamiltonian’s matrix entries or access to coherent real-time evolution.
Main theorem
Let \(N_*(T,B)\) be the minimum worst-case number of calls sufficient to produce a finite description of \(p_\mu(t)\) satisfying
\[
\Pr\!\left[
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
|\widehat p(t)-p_\mu(t)|
\le\frac1{16}
\right]\ge\frac23
\]
for every \(\mu\).
There are universal constants \(c,C,T_0>0\) such that, for \(T\ge T_0\) and every \(B>0\), in either observation model,
\[
\boxed{
cT\log\!\left(2+\frac TB\right)
\le
\log(1+N_*(T,B))
\le
CT\log\!\left(2+\frac TB\right).
}
\tag{1}
\]
The analogous statement holds for uniform approximation of \(A_\mu(t)\).
Two consequences follow immediately:
\[
\boxed{
B\text{ fixed}:
\quad
T_{\mathrm{recoverable}}
=
\Theta\!\left(\frac{\log N}{\log\log N}\right),
}
\]
whereas even an unlimited imaginary-time window gives only
\[
\boxed{
B=\infty:
\quad
T_{\mathrm{recoverable}}=\Theta(\log N).
}
\]
These are minimax orders—not assertions that every individual spectrum is difficult.
The representational issue is specific: compressing an imaginary-time function accurately does not establish that its real-time continuation is accurately determined. Recent work on compressed imaginary-time representations explicitly distinguishes these tasks; changing the representation of the forward data does not by itself remove inverse instability. 2. Positive spectra with concealed real-time differences
The lower bound begins with a finite-difference construction, but adds a common positive background to remove an important loophole.
Let \(m\) be an odd positive integer and \(n=m+1\). Put
\[
x_j=\frac38+\frac{j}{4m},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
On these nodes, let \(u\) be the uniform distribution, \(u_{\mathrm{even}}\) the uniform distribution on even-indexed nodes, and \(b_+,b_-\) the binomial \(\operatorname{Bin}(m,\tfrac12)\) distribution conditioned on even and odd index.
Define
\[
\nu_\pm
=
\frac14u+\frac14u_{\mathrm{even}}+\frac12b_\pm,
\]
then add a known reference level:
\[
\boxed{
\mu_\pm=\frac14\delta_0+\frac34\nu_\pm.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Both measures have the same complete support. Every nonzero-energy weight is at least
\[
\boxed{\frac{3}{16n}.}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, concealment does not depend on an exponentially small overlap with some eigenmode.
The signed difference of the bulk weights is
\[
\nu_+(x_j)-\nu_-(x_j)
=
\frac{(-1)^j}{2^m}\binom mj.
\]
Finite differences annihilate polynomials of degree below \(m\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\int x^q\,d\mu_+
=
\int x^q\,d\mu_-
\qquad(0\le q<m).
}
\tag{4}
\]
These exact local sum rules may be supplied to the estimator for free.
Imaginary-time difference
The binomial theorem gives the complete difference:
\[
\boxed{
G_+(s)-G_-(s)
=
\frac34e^{-3s/8}
\left(\frac{1-e^{-s/(4m)}}2\right)^m.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Let
\[
a=\min\!\left\{\frac{B}{8m},\frac12\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\sup_{0\le s\le B}|G_+(s)-G_-(s)|
\le\frac34a^m.
\tag{6}
\]
The reference level ensures
\[
\frac14\le G_\pm(s)\le1
\qquad\text{for every }s\ge0.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le s\le B}
|\log G_+(s)-\log G_-(s)|
\le3a^m.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Neither normalization nor access to relative precision removes the difference’s smallness. The measured signal itself stays above one quarter.
Real-time difference
Take
\[
t_m=4\pi m,
\qquad
\xi_m=e^{-3\pi im/2}\in\{i,-i\}.
\]
Since \(e^{-it_mx_j}=\xi_m(-1)^j\),
\[
A_+(t_m)=\frac14+\frac9{16}\xi_m,
\]
\[
A_-(t_m)=\frac14-\frac3{16}\xi_m.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
p_+(t_m)=\frac{97}{256},
\qquad
p_-(t_m)=\frac{25}{256}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
The return probabilities differ by \(9/32\), despite the tiny difference in the entire available imaginary-time record.
3. Why adaptive measurements cannot bypass the bound
Suppose two Gaussian query models have conditional means differing by at most \(D\), uniformly over all allowed query locations.
The relative-entropy chain rule gives, for the complete transcript of \(N\) adaptive measurements,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathsf P_+\|\mathsf P_-)
\le\frac{ND^2}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Adaptivity does not invalidate this calculation: at a common transcript argument, the policy chooses the same next query, and the uniform mean-difference bound applies.
Pinsker’s inequality yields
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathsf P_+,\mathsf P_-)
\le\frac{\sqrt N\,D}{2}.
\]
Equal-prior testing success of at least \(2/3\) requires total variation at least \(1/3\). Using the more permissive logarithmic measurement model and (7),
\[
\boxed{
N
\ge
\frac4{81}
\left[
\max\!\left\{2,\frac{8m}{B}\right\}
\right]^{2m}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Predicting the return probability to error \(1/16\) distinguishes the values in (8), so this is also a prediction lower bound.
Choose the largest odd \(m\) with \(4\pi m\le T\). Then \(m=\Theta(T)\), proving the lower half of (1).
The argument covers randomized estimators, nonlinear reconstruction, adaptive temperature selection, and arbitrary subsequent processing. It is a two-point information bound, not an assumption that a particular reconstruction method performs badly. The general relationship between such bounds and functional estimation is established in optimal-recovery and statistical decision theory; that proof strategy is not claimed as new here. arXiv
A small-dimensional example
For \(m=7\), the total system has only nine levels, including the reference. Its real-time target is
\[
t_7=28\pi\approx87.96.
\]
With \(B=1\), equation (10) requires
\[
\boxed{N>10^{23}}
\]
even for the logarithmic observation model.
These are dimensionless units with spectral scale bounded by one, not laboratory seconds. The example shows that large Hilbert-space dimension is not the source of the obstruction.
4. Realization by local, gapped Hamiltonians
The measures above are not merely abstract probability distributions.
For either bulk measure \(\nu_\pm\), orthonormalize
\[
1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}
\]
in \(L^2(\nu_\pm)\). Multiplication by \(x\) becomes a real symmetric tridiagonal matrix \(H_\pm\).
Tridiagonality follows because \(xp_j\) is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree below \(j-1\). Choosing positive leading coefficients makes neighbouring couplings nonzero. A diagonal sign change makes all off-diagonal entries negative.
The first basis vector represents the constant polynomial. Therefore,
\[
\langle e_0,e^{-sH_\pm}e_0\rangle=G_{\nu_\pm}(s),
\]
\[
\langle e_0,e^{-itH_\pm}e_0\rangle=A_{\nu_\pm}(t).
\]
Use the total Hamiltonian and fixed preparation
\[
\mathcal H_\pm=0\oplus H_\pm,
\qquad
\psi=\frac12e_{\mathrm{ref}}+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}e_0.
\tag{11}
\]
This is a nearest-neighbour chain plus one known isolated reference level. Preparation requires a fixed two-site superposition, not an unknown eigenstate.
Engineered nearest-neighbour coherent-transfer systems are established prior art. The local realization itself is not the proposed new capability. APS Journals
What is well conditioned
The two nominal Hamiltonians have exactly the same spectrum, so every partition function
\[
\operatorname{tr}e^{-s\mathcal H_\pm}
\]
is identical. The full spectrum can therefore be supplied without helping to distinguish the pair.
The ground state is unique, its energy is zero, and the gap above it is \(3/8\). Its preparation weight is exactly \(1/4\). The remaining energies lie in \([3/8,5/8]\), and their preparation weights satisfy (3).
There is also a polynomial lower bound on every chain coupling.
Lemma. If a Jacobi matrix has eigenvalue spacing at least \(\Delta\) and every endpoint spectral weight is at least \(\alpha\), then every neighbouring coupling \(b\) satisfies
\[
|b|\ge\frac{\Delta\sqrt\alpha}{3}.
\tag{12}
\]
To prove it, split the chain at \(b\). The left principal block has fewer eigenvalues than the complete matrix. Since the full eigenvalues are \(\Delta\)-separated, some full eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is at distance at least \(\Delta/3\) from the left block’s spectrum. For its normalized eigenvector,
\[
\|v_{\mathrm{left}}\|\le\frac{3|b|}{\Delta}.
\]
Its endpoint component has squared magnitude at least \(\alpha\), proving (12).
Here,
\[
\boxed{
|b|\ge\frac1{24m\sqrt{m+1}}.
}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus neither exponentially weak hopping nor exponentially small spectral weights are needed.
The excited-state spacings are \(O(1/m)\); they are not claimed uniformly separated. The ground-state gap is uniform. Distinguishing these two kinds of gap is important.
5. Robustness: locality replaces exact coefficient tuning
Moment matching also has a spatial interpretation.
For \(m=2r-1\), the two Jacobi matrices have identical diagonal coefficients through index \(r-2\) and identical bonds through distance \(r-1\). Their near-boundary dynamics agree.
A closed walk starting at the observed endpoint needs at least \(m\) matrix factors to encounter the first potentially different diagonal coefficient. This gives a robustness statement that does not depend on the exact binomial identities.
Perturb the bulk matrices by real symmetric tridiagonal matrices \(E_\pm\) satisfying
\[
\|E_\pm\|\le\frac1{64t_m},
\tag{14}
\]
while preserving their common near-boundary coefficients. A common perturbation \(E_+=E_-\) is sufficient. Keep the reference unchanged.
The perturbed bulk spectra remain in \([1/3,2/3]\), and their endpoint moments still agree through degree \(m-1\).
Uniform imaginary-time bound
Taylor expansion at zero gives a bulk difference bounded by \(2B^m/m!\).
For a bound uniform over all imaginary times, expand \(e^{-sx}\) around \(x=1/2\). On \([1/3,2/3]\), its degree-\((m-1)\) remainder is at most
\[
e^{-s/3}\frac{(s/6)^m}{m!}.
\]
Its supremum over \(s\ge0\) is at most \(2^{-m}\). Consequently, including the reference,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le s\le B}
|G_+^{\mathrm{pert}}(s)-G_-^{\mathrm{pert}}(s)|
\le
\frac32
\min\!\left\{\frac{B^m}{m!},2^{-m}\right\}.
}
\tag{15}
\]
The reference still guarantees \(G\ge1/4\), so the logarithmic difference is at most four times this.
Real-time separation remains
Duhamel’s formula gives
\[
\|e^{-it(H+E)}-e^{-itH}\|\le t\|E\|.
\]
Under (14), each return probability changes by at most \(1/32\). The difference between the two hypotheses therefore remains at least
\[
\boxed{\frac7{32}.}
\tag{16}
\]
The same exponential-order lower bound follows in both noise models.
This is a meaningful but structured robustness theorem. It is not a full unrestricted perturbation neighbourhood. A change at the observed endpoint can create a first-order imaginary-time difference and make detection much easier.
Also, arbitrary perturbations need not preserve the nominal common spectrum or the spectral-weight floor. Free complete-spectrum side information is asserted for the nominal isospectral family, not simultaneously for every perturbed pair.
6. A reconstruction attaining the same exponential order
The lower bound does not leave an unexplained gap: there is an explicit upper construction for every probability measure on \([0,1]\).
It reconstructs the desired response directly, without first recovering a spectral density. Direct functional recovery is a classical approach; the task here is to obtain the appropriate resource bound. arXiv
Fix amplitude accuracy \(\epsilon>0\), and set
\[
M=
\left\lceil
\max\!\left\{16T,\log_2\frac4\epsilon\right\}
\right\rceil,
\qquad
a=\min\!\left\{\frac BM,\frac1{16}\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Measure only at
\[
s_\ell=\ell a,\qquad \ell=0,\ldots,M.
\]
For \(0\le x\le1\), put
\[
z=1-e^{-ax}.
\]
Then
\[
e^{-itx}=(1-z)^{it/a}.
\]
Truncate the binomial expansion:
\[
P_t(x)
=
\sum_{j=0}^{M}
(-1)^j\binom{it/a}{j}
(1-e^{-ax})^j.
\tag{18}
\]
Uniform approximation error
For \(j>M\), uniformly over \(0\le t\le T\),
\[
\left|\binom{it/a}{j}\right|z^j
\le
\left(\frac{eT}{j}+ea\right)^j
\le
\left(\frac e8\right)^j
<2^{-j}.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\substack{0\le t\le T\\0\le x\le1}}
|P_t(x)-e^{-itx}|
\le2^{-M}\le\frac\epsilon4.
}
\tag{19}
\]
Expanding the powers in (18) gives
\[
P_t(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}w_\ell(t)e^{-s_\ell x}.
\]
The resulting coefficient norm satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le T}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}|w_\ell(t)|
\le
S:=
2\left[
2e\left(2+\frac TB\right)
\right]^M.
}
\tag{20}
\]
For completeness, this follows by bounding the coefficients with the rising factorial \((T/a)_j/j!\). The sequence \(2^j(T/a)_j/j!\) grows by at least a factor two, so its finite sum is at most twice its last term. Applying \(j!\ge(j/e)^j\) gives (20).
A usable error certificate
If every measured value obeys
\[
|\widehat G(s_\ell)-G(s_\ell)|\le\delta,
\]
then the entire reconstructed curve satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\left|
\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}w_\ell(t)\widehat G(s_\ell)-A_\mu(t)
\right|
\le
2^{-M}+S\delta.
}
\tag{21}
\]
Choose \(\delta=\epsilon/(4S)\). Repeating each Gaussian query sufficiently often yields the simultaneous bound with probability \(1-\zeta\), using
\[
\boxed{
N
=
O\!\left(
(M+1)S^2\epsilon^{-2}
\log\frac{M+1}{\zeta}
\right).
}
\tag{22}
\]
For logarithmic measurements, estimate \(\log G\), clip the estimate above at zero, and exponentiate. The map
\[
y\mapsto e^{\min(y,0)}
\]
is \(1\)-Lipschitz, so the same raw accuracy suffices.
The output amplitude is a polynomial in \(t\) of degree \(M\). Squaring its modulus and clipping to \([0,1]\) yields the return-probability predictor. Amplitude error \(e_A\) gives probability error at most \(2e_A+e_A^2\).
For fixed accuracy and confidence,
\[
M=O(T),
\qquad
\log S=O\!\left(T\log\!\left(2+\frac TB\right)\right),
\]
which proves the upper half of (1).
This is matching exponential order, not matching practical constants. The displayed construction is conservative.
7. Computational resources and failed escape routes
The distinction between statistical samples and numerical precision is decisive.
The reconstruction needs roughly
\[
O\!\left(T\log\!\left(2+\frac TB\right)\right)
\]
precision bits at constant target accuracy—not exponentially many bits. Generating and evaluating its coefficients has polynomial bit cost in the degree, input encoding and requested working precision.
But acquiring that precision from independent noisy samples requires the exponential count in (1).
Therefore, the theorem does not rule out efficient continuation from a deterministic oracle that supplies arbitrary-precision \(G(s)\) at cost polynomial in the requested number of bits. That is a stronger access model.
It also does not establish computational hardness for these Hamiltonians when their complete matrices are given. They have only \(O(T)\) levels and can be simulated by ordinary matrix methods. The obstruction is specifically the information delivered through the specified measurements.
Several proposed shortcuts fail within that interface:
Positivity, a known spectrum and sum rules are insufficient. The two hypotheses satisfy them simultaneously. Their difference lies in spectral weights, not unknown eigenvalues.
Removing small signal amplitude is insufficient. The known reference keeps \(G(s)\ge1/4\), including at arbitrarily large \(s\). Logarithmic measurements retain the lower bound.
Unlimited imaginary time is insufficient. Increasing \(B\) changes the superexponential \(e^{\Theta(T\log T)}\) regime into an exponential \(e^{\Theta(T)}\) regime; it does not produce polynomial sample complexity.
Finite numerical output does not mean cheap acquisition. The returned curve has a compact description, but the data required to certify it are expensive.
The upper bound is an oracle sampling theorem, not a laboratory implementation. Preparation, decorrelation and per-query physical costs remain separate. It uses times no larger than \(\min(B,M/16)\). Quantization and independent systematic errors must fit the tolerance in (21). Since both \(G\) and \(\log G\) are \(1\)-Lipschitz in \(s\), sufficiently small absolute timing errors can be charged directly—but the required instrumental accuracy may itself be severe.
8. Assessment and evidence
The completed consequence is a matching information-resource law for coherent prediction, with a physically restricted lower-bound family and a constructive upper certificate.
It identifies a precise obstruction: short imaginary-time data can conceal an alternating redistribution of substantial positive spectral weights. Those changes cancel many imaginary-time moments while adding coherently in real time. Locality and a common near-boundary region preserve the obstruction under the stated perturbations.
A method that promises substantially better worst-case prediction must therefore add information or impose a restriction excluding these spectral-weight variations. A different basis or reconstruction objective, by itself, does not do so.
