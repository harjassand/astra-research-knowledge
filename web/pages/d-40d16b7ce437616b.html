## Reconstruction of the load-bearing argument

For every original coordinate, sort its distinct input levels. Each successive nonzero gap belongs to one nested upper cut `B`. Group all gaps belonging to the same `B` into a nonnegative ℓ1 vector `b_B`; let `v(k)=min_i x_i(k)` and `w_B=‖b_B‖₁`. There are finitely many subsets `B⊂[n]`, even with infinitely many original coordinates. Integrability follows from `|v|,b_B≤2Σ_i|x_i|`. With `z_i(B)=1_{i∈B}` and `T(u)=v+Σ_Bb_Bu_B`, direct telescoping gives

`Tz_i=x_i`, `‖z_i−z_j‖_H²=‖x_i−x_j‖₁`, and `‖Tu−Tu'‖₁≤Σ_Bw_B|u_B−u'_B|`, where `‖u‖_H²=Σ_Bw_Bu_B²`.

This reconstruction is ambient: it is not a projection into the span or convex hull of the original data. Such extra target restrictions are not part of the proved interface.

Write `φ(r)=3r²−2r³`, `a=u−e`, `b=u'−e`, `δ=b−a`, `U=‖a‖_H²`, `V=‖δ‖_H²`, `c=⟨a,δ⟩_H` for binary `e`. The exact quartic Bregman remainder is

`R=‖b‖_H⁴−‖a‖_H⁴−4Uc=2UV+(2c+V)²`.

Each pair `a_B,b_B` has the same sign because `e_B` is an endpoint of `[0,1]`. Integrating `|φ'(r)|≤6|r−e_B|` gives `|φ(u'_B)−φ(u_B)|≤3|δ_B||a_B+b_B|`. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and

`2R−V‖a+b‖_H²=8(c+V/4)²+V²/2≥0`

give `‖Φ(u')−Φ(u)‖_{1,w}²≤18R`. The same-sign requirement is essential. Taking `u=(r,r)`, `u'=(r+ε,r−ε)`, `e=0`, then `ε/r→0` and `r→0`, makes the ratio tend to 18, establishing sharpness of this local inequality, not of the full cotype constant.

Attach `h_i=(Gz)_i` to an alive state of a killed walk, and `z_i` to its absorbing dead state. The equation `h_i=pz_i+qΣ_ja_ijh_j` makes this a bounded Hilbert martingale. Centering its potential at the time-zero-measurable binary vector `z_{X₀}` makes each linear Bregman term vanish conditionally. Telescoping controls the total squared transformed jumps by `18B_G`, where

`B_G=Σ_{ij}π_iG_ij‖x_i−x_j‖₁²`.

The death jump contributes the fidelity cost once. Stationarity gives the same live edge distribution at each time; summing the survival probabilities contributes `q/p=t` times the edge cost. Thus `J_t(y)≤18B_G`.

For the geometric comparison, `D(s)=E‖x_{X_s}−x_{X₀}‖₁²` has subadditive square root, and `D(t)≤4W`. At `t=1`, `ED(S)≤3W`. At `t≥2`, with `S=tJ+R₀` and `α=q^t≤4/9`, the exact block law yields

`EJ²=α(1+α)/(1−α)²≤52/25`, `P(R₀=r)≤9/(5t)`, and `ED(R₀)≤9W/5`.

Minkowski gives `B_G≤[(4√13/5)+(3/√5)]²W=((253+24√65)/25)W<18W`. The comparison is valid at periodic and nonreversible chains, since only stationarity is used. Consequently `J_t(y)≤324W`.

The same finite cut construction works in a real `L1(μ)` space using fixed measurable representatives. A global Hilbert-to-arbitrary-`L1` extension claim would still require the prior extension theorem's target hypotheses; it is not inferred from this finite construction alone.

## Compiler and hostile checks of the proposed consequences

For finite rational coordinate input `x∈Q^{n×d}`, solve once for `G`. For each original coordinate, sort the `n` values `α₁≤⋯≤α_n` and their indices. For row `i`, the median expectation is

`y_i(k)=α₁+Σ_{r=1}^{n−1}(α_{r+1}−α_r)φ(Σ_{s>r}G_{i,j_s})`.

Ties contribute zero gaps. This is exactly the direct triple expectation and avoids exponential cut enumeration. Dense arithmetic counts are `O(n³+n²d+nd log n)`, with `O(n²+nd)` storage. Exact rational bit growth is polynomial after common-denominator scaling and determinant bounds; the ordinary Python fractions implementation is not a claim of optimal bit complexity or measured hardware performance. All `nd` data coordinates and full output acquisition remain charged.

For the probability simplex `Δ={z≥0:Σ_kz_k=1}`, raw medians generally lose mass: for uniform basis input, the mass is `nφ(1/n)=3/n−2/n²`. Unconditional normalization is therefore not a globally stable repair. For any fixed reference probability `r∈Δ`, define `R_r(z)=z⁺+(1−s)r` when `s=‖z⁺‖₁≤1`, and `R_r(z)=z⁺/s` when `s≥1`.

The positive part is 1-Lipschitz. Each nonnegative branch is 2-Lipschitz. Across branches, split the straight nonnegative segment at mass one: its two ℓ1 lengths add to the original distance, so the global constant is also 2. The repair fixes every simplex point, hence `J_t(R_ry)≤4J_t(y)≤1296W`. The constant 2 is attained for `r=e₁`, `z=0`, `z'=εe₂`. Choosing `r=x₁` instead of a new anchor coordinate preserves any common support face already containing all inputs. Choosing one shared reference is essential to the simple edge-cost argument; arbitrary row-specific repairs are not covered.

The [Mendel–Naor prior](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3963v2), Theorem 1.11 and Corollary 1.13, was independently read at the exact locations. Its barycentric and global target hypotheses are satisfied by ℓ1, a dual Banach space. The source's Hilbert→ℓ1 extension followed by `R_r` therefore gives Hilbert→Δ extension with at most an additional factor 2. This is an existence consequence; an efficient arbitrary-domain extension algorithm is a separate unsolved acquisition step here.

For the hypercube simple walk and data `x_v=e_v∈ℓ1^{2^d}`, the character `χ_k(v)=(−1)^{v_k}` has eigenvalue `1−2/d` and `Gχ_k=(1+2t/d)⁻¹χ_k`. Testing neighboring resolvent rows against this bounded character gives `‖G_v−G_w‖₁≥2/(1+2t/d)`. Since `W≤4`, the prescribed linear-resolvent cost ratio is at least `t/(1+2t/d)²`, hence at least `d/9` at `t=d`.

This proves failure of that linear smoother's universal bound. It is not an advantage over every competent smoothing procedure: the admissible constant map `y_v=0` has `J_t=1`, and therefore ratio approaching 1/4 on these data. Stationary-mean output is another linear constant comparator. The independently computed full-state ratios at `d=2,3,4` are respectively linear `0.67556,0.86082,1.09845`, median `0.64666,0.51521,0.42764`, and constant-zero `0.33333,0.28125,0.27826`. Thus this diagnostic isolates why the fixed resolvent needs a nonlinear postprocessing; it does not certify a general optimization or learning advantage.

## New acquisition deduction: first-moment control of sampled medians

For any three real numbers,

`|med(a,b,c)|²≤|ab|+|ac|+|bc|`.

At least two of their magnitudes are at least the magnitude of their signed median; the product of these two is one summand on the right. Thus, for three independent copies of a scalar variable `X` with finite `E|X|`,

`E med(X₁,X₂,X₃)²≤3(E|X|)²`.                                      (A)

This needs no second moment of `X`. The constant 3 is sharp: Bernoulli(`p`) has ratio `3−2p`, tending to 3 as `p↓0`. More generally, an odd median of `2k−1` independent copies obeys `E|M|^k≤binom(2k−1,k)(E|X|)^k` by the analogous sum of products over `k`-element subsets. This higher-moment fact is a scalar/coordinate theorem, not a noncommutative trace or unrestricted vector-moment assertion.

For a row `i`, draw `L` independent triples of `G_i` endpoints, form their coordinatewise medians `Z_{iℓ}`, and set `ŷ_i=L⁻¹Σ_ℓZ_{iℓ}`. Subtracting the deterministic center `x_i` commutes with medians. Let `μ_ik=E_{G_i}|x_J(k)−x_i(k)|` and `D_i=Σ_kμ_ik=E_{G_i}‖x_J−x_i‖₁`.

By (A), each centered median coordinate has variance at most `3μ_ik²`. The `L` triples are independent, so Minkowski in `L2` of the sampling probability space gives

`(E‖ŷ_i−y_i‖₁²)^{1/2}≤Σ_k(E|ŷ_i(k)−y_i(k)|²)^{1/2}≤√(3/L)D_i`. (B)

For countably many coordinates use finite partial sums and monotone convergence. The `L1(μ)` version uses the integral form of Minkowski. Dependencies among coordinates and among different rows are allowed; only the triples within a row must be independent.

Jensen gives `Σ_iπ_iD_i²≤B_G≤18W`. Write `e_i=ŷ_i−y_i`. Stationarity and `‖e_i−e_j‖₁²≤2‖e_i‖₁²+2‖e_j‖₁²` imply

`J_t(e;0)≤(1+4t)Σ_iπ_i‖e_i‖₁²`.

Minkowski for the weighted fidelity/edge direct sum therefore gives the actual randomized compiler guarantee

`E J_t(ŷ)≤[18+√(54(1+4t)/L)]² W`.                              (C)

This is an expected objective bound. For probability input followed by a single fixed `R_r`, multiply the right side by 4. With confidence at least `1−δ`, Markov's inequality on the nonnegative error energy also gives

`J_t(ŷ)≤[18+√(54(1+4t)/(Lδ))]² W`.                             (D)

Thus `L≥54(1+4t)/η²` yields the expected factor `(18+η)²`; the direct confidence version requires `L≥54(1+4t)/(η²δ)`. We have not replaced `1/δ` by a logarithm for this relative global objective guarantee.

## Sparse probability output and an implicit representation interface

There is a stronger absolute per-row confidence statement for probability input. Center at zero in (A), so `Σ_k E_{G_i}x_J(k)=1` and `(E‖ŷ_i−y_i‖₁²)^{1/2}≤√(3/L)`. Every sample median has mass at most 3/2 because its absolute value is coordinatewise at most half the sum of the three input absolute values. Replacing one independent triple therefore changes the sample average by at most `3/L` in ℓ1. Bounded differences gives, with probability at least `1−δ`,

`‖R_rŷ_i−R_ry_i‖₁≤[2√3+3√2√log(1/δ)]/√L`.                    (E)

A union bound replaces `log(1/δ)` by `log(n/δ)` for simultaneous accuracy at all finite `n` states. The same random tape can be reused across rows: within-row triples remain independent, and neither (C) nor the union bound requires independence between rows. Using the same tape again makes row queries consistent. This describes a random implicit map; fresh independent randomness on every repeated query would not define one fixed map.

If inputs are supplied as sparse probability vectors with at most `s` entries and the chain has a supplied transition sampler, the map can be queried without constructing `G`, enumerating the state space, sorting every coordinate, or listing the ambient dimension. One query uses `3L` endpoint walks, expected `3Lt` transitions, `3L` data-vector reads, `O(sL)` sparse median/aggregation work and at most `O(sL)` nonzero raw output entries. The repaired sparse representation adds the reference's support and one mass computation. The full explicit output still costs `n` queries and its actual output size.

The global objective version costs expected `3nLt` transitions. With its relative expected guarantee above, `L=O(t/η²)`, hence transition count `O(nt²/η²)`. Sampling geometric lengths directly or by survival trials also costs randomness; a stored shared tape has expected `O(Lt)` step draws. Oracle setup, exact/specified transition sampling, reading each vector representation, arithmetic precision, reference acquisition, and output storage are assumptions/costs. Arbitrary black-box infinite-dimensional vectors do not come with a free coordinatewise median or integration oracle.

The result is dimension-free **sample complexity for this median functional**, not dimension-free explicit arithmetic and not estimation of the original endpoint mean. A generic endpoint empirical mean on the uniform `n` basis vectors has at most `L` observed support entries and ℓ1 error at least `2(1−L/n)` for `L≤n`. Median smoothing changes the sanctioned output functional: its rare-coordinate suppression is precisely what makes (A) possible. It cannot be used to claim that unknown arbitrary probability distributions can now be learned in ℓ1 with dimension-free sample counts.

## Prior art, downstream use, and remaining decisive gates

The verified nearest primary mechanism is Cheng–Wang–Xiang, [arXiv:2609.08749v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08749v2), revised 9 September 2026, Sections 2.1 and 2.3, especially Lemma 2.4: binary cut representations and the same cubic already occur in their sharp torus metric-cotype proof. The new source mechanism is its stopped-martingale quartic estimate and ambient reconstruction for Markov cotype. Those invariants are distinct. The prior full snapshot is preserved; only the stated sections were read here.

The additional scalar moment observation (A), its sampled-functional application (B), and the oracle/compiler consequences (C)–(E) were reconstructed here. A narrow phrase search did not locate an exact prior sampling theorem, but that is weak evidence; no literature-first or publication claim is made. The scalar moment inequality itself is elementary and may be old.

Concrete use: a certified finite nonlinear smoothing operator can now be queried on an implicit Markov state space with sparse distribution-valued data, with sample precision specified independently of ambient coordinate count. It supplies feasible graph-denoising or representation outputs with a long-walk budget guarantee, including after simplex repair. It can be a warm start or comparison point for a convex objective; no constant-factor approximation to `min_y J_t(y)` was proved.

The strongest next falsifiable step is to remove or prove necessary the `t` factor in the sampling error's edge energy using economical joint endpoint couplings. With independent row estimates, nearly equal smoothed rows can still have edge sampling variance `Θ(1/L)`, so multiplying by `t` really requires `L=Ω(t)` for that estimator. Common random numbers do not automatically fix this: on a two-state deterministic flip walk they can yield opposite row errors. A useful coupling must be constructed and charged, with a universal graph-energy error bound. Efficient global Lipschitz extension from arbitrary supplied samples, extra target linear constraints, and a noncommutative Schatten-1 reconstruction remain distinct open interfaces in this scoped work.
