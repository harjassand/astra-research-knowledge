## 1. Scientific question and scope

The investigated output is the incremental voltage at the end of a prescribed current pulse, rather than a reconstructed relaxation spectrum. In the linear-response model below, write

\[
Q_T=\Delta V(T)/I.
\]

This is a transfer-response quantity in ohms, evaluated just before the end of a constant pulse of duration T. It assumes stationarity or subtraction of the unforced trajectory from the same initial state. It does not, without further information, equal the voltage change of an arbitrarily prepared, nonlinear battery under a finite operating current.

The numerical study uses the first cell's 70 EIS frequency samples from Zenodo record 8369275. The retained CSV is a transcription of the first row of each of three upstream EIS CSVs. It is not a full independently checksummed download of the original dataset. Source paths, authors, and all acquisition limitations are in sources/provenance.json.

The paired pulse files were located, but a complete aligned current/time/voltage trace was not recovered into the executable environment. Therefore no held-out experimental pulse prediction is reported. Other cells, pseudo-open-circuit curves, and pulse-derived constraints were not used. An EIS-only obstruction is not an obstruction for the full characterization package.

## 3. Mathematical model

Assume a positive distributed-RC response, plus series resistance, capacitance, and inductance:

\[
Z(i\omega)=R_\infty+i\omega L+\frac{\kappa}{i\omega}
 +\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{d\mu(\tau)}{1+i\omega\tau},
\qquad R_\infty,\kappa\geq0,\quad\mu\geq0.
\]

The physical inductance is nonnegative. For the outer bounds we allow any real L, a conservative enlargement of this model class. The four explicit feasible witnesses nevertheless have positive inductance.

The integrals at measured frequencies must exist. Total relaxation resistance need not be bounded, and there is no assumed maximum relaxation time. This is a restricted RC model class, not the class of all passive positive-real systems. Resonant or inductive distributed dynamics, nonlinearity, state dependence, and unknown initial relaxation are not automatically included.

For T>0, the non-impulsive step response is

\[
Q_T=R_\infty+\kappa T+
\int_{(0,\infty)}(1-e^{-T/\tau})\,d\mu(\tau).
\]

The ideal inductor contributes only at the discontinuous switching instant, not to this T>0 expression. A finite-ramp experimental input requires its actual waveform instead of silently using an ideal step.

## 4. A positive normalization derived from measurements

Choose two measured angular frequencies 0<a<b and let r=b/a. Define

\[
M=\operatorname{Re}Z(ia)-\operatorname{Im}Z(ia)
 +\frac{a}{b}\operatorname{Im}Z(ib).
\]

The series-inductance contribution cancels exactly: -aL+(a/b)bL=0. Put x=a tau. The positive relaxation weight is

\[
w(x)=\frac{1+x}{1+x^2}-\frac{x}{1+r^2x^2}.
\]

For x>=0,

\[
w(x)=\frac1{1+x^2}+x\left(\frac1{1+x^2}-\frac1{1+r^2x^2}\right)>0.
\]

Consequently,

\[
M=R_\infty+\frac{\kappa}{a}(1-r^{-2})
 +\int w(a\tau)\,d\mu(\tau).
\]

This is a measured finite mass, not an assumed microscopic norm. In this experiment, a=2 pi times 0.01 Hz and b=2 pi times 1 Hz, so r=100. With an assumed componentwise observation budget epsilon=0.1 mOhm,

\[
M\leq 58.47-(-12.77)+0.01(-1.42)+2.01(0.1)
=71.4268\;\mathrm{mOhm}.
\]

This number has no claimed confidence level: epsilon is a deterministic exploratory assumption.

### Compactification

Let s=x/(1+x), q=1-s, D_lambda=q^2+lambda^2 s^2, and

\[
B=q^3+s^2(r^2-s)>0.
\]

Reweight the relaxation measure by w and include two endpoint atoms: mass R_infinity at s=0 and mass kappa(1-r^-2)/a at s=1. This gives a nonnegative measure nu on [0,1] with total mass M. Every measured RC kernel, after division by w, is continuous on this compact interval:

\[
F_\lambda(s)=\frac{D_1D_r}{D_\lambda B}
 [q-i\lambda s],\qquad\lambda=\omega/a.
\]

For alpha=aT, the target kernel is

\[
H_T(s)=\frac{1-e^{-\alpha q/s}}{q}\frac{D_1D_r}{B},
\]

with continuous endpoint values

\[
H_T(0)=1,\qquad H_T(1)=\frac{aT}{1-r^{-2}}.
\]

Thus Q_T=integral H_T dnu. The slow endpoint is a genuine capacitive limit, not an arbitrary tail cutoff.

### Bounded-observable consequence

Every finite-time Q_T is bounded under bounded measurements because H_T is continuous on a compact interval and nu has bounded mass. No bound on total relaxation resistance is needed.

More generally, any nonnegative relaxation observable with kernel g(tau) is bounded whenever sup g(tau)/w(a tau) is finite. Under a strictly feasible observation box, the converse holds: if this ratio is unbounded, add an atom of mass delta/w(a tau_n) at locations where the ratio diverges. All measurement changes are bounded by delta times fixed constants, since every measured kernel divided by w is bounded. Choose delta small enough to preserve the observation box. The target contribution is delta g(tau_n)/w(a tau_n), which diverges. This is an elementary positive-moment argument, not a claim of a new general duality theorem.

In particular, the DC relaxation resistance has g=1 and is not uniformly bounded. A transparent construction takes resistance sqrt(tau) at an increasingly slow mode: the finite-frequency perturbation vanishes while its DC resistance diverges. Finite-time boundedness does not imply uniqueness or accurate long-horizon prediction.

## 5. Continuum certificates rather than a finite-grid claim

The optimization grid supplies candidate dual coefficients only. A finite-grid optimum is not by itself a continuum bound.

Stack the observed real and imaginary components into y. Let F(s) be their normalized kernel vector. Suppose a coefficient vector v annihilates series inductance exactly. If

\[
H_T(s)-v^TF(s)\geq-\delta\quad\text{for every }s\in[0,1],
\]

then, for every admissible model,

\[
Q_T\geq v^Ty-\epsilon\lVert v\rVert_1-\delta M_{\max}.
\]

An upper bound follows from v^T F-H_T>=-delta with the signs reversed. This follows simply by integrating the pointwise inequality against nu>=0 and bounding the observation error. It does not require strong duality or trusting the optimizer's termination flag.

Implementation details:

- Dual coefficients are converted to exact rational numbers. One existing imaginary coefficient is adjusted so that sum v_im,j f_j=0 exactly.
- The complete interval [0,1] is covered, including a separately bounded fast endpoint interval. No untested relaxation tail is omitted.
- mpmath interval arithmetic with 35 decimal digits bounds the residual and its first two derivatives. Adaptive Taylor bounds certify whole subintervals, not sampled points.
- Near the slow endpoint the implementation evaluates E(u)=(1-exp(-u))/u through its integral representation and a Taylor series with an explicit remainder. This avoids division of two nearly vanishing quantities.
- Scalar output bounds use exact rational arithmetic. Displayed decimal intervals must be rounded outwards.

The derivation is explicit and the executable checks pass. The implementation has not been independently audited or formalized in Lean. Interval-library correctness and the provided model assumptions remain premises.

### The full spectrum rejects the tighter model

The positive-RC model has a nonincreasing real part as frequency increases. In the retained input,

- Re Z at 10,000 Hz is 38.03 mOhm;
- Re Z at 5,484 Hz is 37.00 mOhm.

Their 1.03 mOhm ordering violation forces any uniform real-component error budget to be at least 0.515 mOhm, irrespective of the number of relaxation modes. Series inductance and capacitance do not alter this real-part condition. An exploratory full-band grid fit required approximately 0.543 mOhm, but that latter optimum is not asserted as an exact continuum minimum.

Thus the narrow lower-frequency certificate is not a certificate for the entire measured spectrum, nor evidence that the actual cell belongs to the assumed RC class. The frequency restriction is an exploratory modeling choice, not a physically established remedy for the rejected full-band model.

## 7. A different operation: design the measurements for the target

After the reconstruction route failed the novelty and scientific-capability tests, a separate experiment investigated a change in the information interface. It constructs a six-frequency linear estimator of Q_10 directly, instead of fitting a relaxation distribution.

For a positive single-mode target kernel k_T, seek coefficients satisfying

\[
(1-\eta)k_T(\tau)\leq\sum_j v_j K_j(\tau)
\leq(1+\eta)k_T(\tau)
\]

for every relaxation time, together with exact inductance cancellation. Positivity extends this modewise bound to any mixture. With componentwise noise epsilon, the estimate qhat=v^T y implies

\[
\frac{\widehat q-\epsilon\lVert v\rVert_1}{1+\eta}
\leq Q_T\leq
\frac{\widehat q+\epsilon\lVert v\rVert_1}{1-\eta}
\]

when eta<1. The output bounds were checked by the same continuum method without a mass-repair term, so unused low-frequency observations are not covertly needed.

The optimization used a declared response scale of 62.5 mOhm to balance approximation error against noise amplification. That scale was selected during exploration of cell 001; this is not a blinded prospective design. The resulting kernel inequalities themselves are uniform over the stated positive-RC class.

## 8. The nonlinear scientific obstruction remains

Even perfect knowledge of the linearized impedance does not determine unrestricted finite-amplitude response. A stronger elementary counterexample also applies to bounded-amplitude EIS experiments.

Suppose all characterization currents satisfy |I|<=a. Add the memoryless voltage contribution

\[
V_{\mathrm{extra}}(I)=c\,\operatorname{sign}(I)(|I|-a)_+^3,
\qquad c\geq0.
\]

It is zero for every such characterization input. It is monotone, and I V_extra(I)>=0, so it is passive. Nevertheless, at I_p>a it adds c(I_p-a)^3 to the voltage. Identical small-amplitude data and passivity alone therefore cannot give a uniform high-amplitude voltage guarantee over this broader class.

This is an information-theoretic counterexample, not a claim that the tested cell literally contains that element, and not a historical-novelty claim. Additional material restrictions or nonlinear measurements may rule it out. Those restrictions and measurements must be supplied and charged.

Unknown initial relaxation, different rest histories, state-of-charge motion, temperature variation, frequency calibration, and nonlinear response are separate from floating-point error. Neither an exact dual certificate nor an attractive fitted curve bounds those physical effects automatically. The published pseudo-open-circuit and pulse information may resolve part of the ambiguity; established competitors are entitled to use it under a matched acquisition contract.

Matrix formation uses O(mn) work/storage. The recorded complete local replay took approximately 16.09 seconds on the supplied container CPU, with a largest child-process peak resident set of 178,680 KiB. This is not aggregate process-tree peak memory and is not a strongest-incumbent benchmark. No GPU or upstream implementation was executed. Instrument acquisition, original data collection, literature retrieval, model-inference cost, and failed download attempts are outside that replay timing and were not measured as a unified total.

The retained numerical evidence distinguishes deterministic input-box uncertainty, numerical certification, and unbounded/unmeasured physical-model discrepancy. ## 10. Assessment and promotion gate

The useful retained construction reweights unobserved modes using an actually measured positive quantity and can certify a target observable without selecting a microscopic spectrum. The exact countermodels also prevent a narrow point fit from being mistaken for identified long-horizon behavior.

However, the core mathematics overlaps existing moment/duality machinery; the pilot does not reveal a compute barrier that strong existing methods cannot handle; the physical voltage target was not validated; and the apparent EIS-only obstruction may be removable with information already available to incumbent joint-domain methods. The sparse-probe experiment does not establish a net acquisition advantage.

**Retain this packet as a verification and research-selection tool. Do not promote it as a foundational scientific-computational breakthrough or continue merely optimizing the RC solver.** The next promotion gate is an experimentally validated, target-specific information operation that improves a measured nonlinear decision under a matched total acquisition and validation budget against the strongest relevant incumbent. Without that evidence, the flagship search should move to a different obstruction rather than enlarging this certificate architecture.
