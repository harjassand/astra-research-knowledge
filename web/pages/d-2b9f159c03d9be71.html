## Exact acquisition contract

Dimension d≥2; n=d+1 physical terminals. Use the finite response family

F_0=n⁻¹I, F_v=n⁻¹I+τP_v,

where τ=1/(2n²), P_v=vvᵀ, and v ranges over e_i and (e_i±e_j)/√2. These d²+1 response operators are realized by the same complete resistor graph with positive conductances c_ij(v)>3/4. Input and output coordinates are fixed orthonormal coordinates of the zero-sum terminal-current space, so their norms match physical boundary Euclidean norms.

Each retained scalar detector is a known unit-norm voltage functional. Noise is independent N(0,σ²) in each acquired scalar. The single-current arm may jointly optimize its current and **arbitrary d unit detectors**, including nonorthogonal or repeated readings. Every reading is charged separately. Across distinct current settings, detector noise is likewise independent. Currents and detectors are deterministic and fixed before learning the unknown v. This is a nonadaptive design contract; adaptive sensor selection is not included.

Compare:

- Single current: one vector g of norm one, with any d jointly optimized unit response detectors. Total current squared norm one, d acquired scalars, one source setting.
- Multiple currents: d vectors g_j=e_j/√d, each measured by exactly one unit detector h_j specified below. Total current squared norm one, d acquired scalars, d source settings.

Scalar count and input norm are equal; source switching count differs explicitly. This measures squared current-amplitude budget, not model-dependent Joule dissipation. Analog detector implementation is required: acquiring a full vector and discarding coordinates later would instead incur d² raw scalar samples and is not this contract.

## Full-response single-current raw pair margin

Let D_d=Σ_{i=0}^{d−1}(1+√2i)². Then

max_{||g||=1} min_{a≠b} ||(F_a−F_b)g||²=τ²/D_d.

This equality concerns d orthonormal detectors retaining the whole vector response. It is not the optimum over arbitrary repeated unit readings; that stronger comparator is bounded in the next section. This raw, unnormalized equality needs its own proof; it is not automatic from normalized secants. The upper bound uses **only base pairs**, whose differences are τP_v and whose Frobenius norms are all τ. If the raw minimum is τ²t, coordinate pairs require |g_i|≥√t, and both root signs require ||g_i|−|g_j||≥√(2t). Sorting gives 1≥tD_d for every possible current. Thus no arbitrary current exceeds τ²/D_d under full-vector detection.

The arithmetic current g_i=(1+√2(i−1))/√D_d attains this upper bound on base pairs. The independently proved all-pair normalized lower bound in the companion note gives at least 1/D_d on every nonbase pair. For distinct projectors in this particular finite family, |vᵀw|≤1/√2, so

||P_v−P_w||_F²=2[1−(vᵀw)²]≥1.

Therefore each raw nonbase pair also has sensitivity at least τ²/D_d. This proves raw equality without overlooking the varying secant norms.

## Competent single-current arm: jointly optimize all unit detectors

Let h_1,…,h_d be any unit detectors, and define M=Σ_j h_jh_jᵀ. Then M≥0 and tr M=d, with no orthogonality assumption. For a fixed current g, the raw squared mean separation for a base-direction pair is

τ² (vᵀg)² vᵀMv.

Allowing every trace-d PSD M can only strengthen this comparator, so it is safe for an upper bound. Let τ²t be the minimum separation over all pairs. Sort the magnitudes a_i=|g_i| and permute the diagonal weights m_i=M_ii into the same order. Sign-root directions remain in the family, so for each adjacent pair choose the sign giving the magnitude difference. PSD implies |M_ij|≤√(m_i m_j), and hence vᵀMv≤m_i+m_j for either root sign. It follows that

t≤½(a_{i+1}−a_i)²(m_i+m_{i+1}).

If t>0 then these adjacent weights and gaps are positive. For any q=1,…,d−1, sum the first q gaps and use convexity of x↦x⁻¹/²:

a_{q+1}≥Σ_{i=1}^q(a_{i+1}−a_i)
 ≥√(2t) Σ_{i=1}^q(m_i+m_{i+1})⁻¹/²
 ≥√(2t) q^{3/2}/√[Σ_{i=1}^q(m_i+m_{i+1})]
 ≥√(t/d) q^{3/2}.

The final inequality uses Σ_{i=1}^q(m_i+m_{i+1})≤2 tr M=2d. Since the remaining d−q coordinates all have magnitude at least a_{q+1}, unit input norm gives a_{q+1}≤1/√(d−q). Therefore

t≤d/[(d−q)q³] for every q.

Define K_d=max_{1≤q≤d−1}(d−q)q³. Then the strongest single-current design, including arbitrary repeated unit detectors, satisfies

sup_{g,h_1,…,h_d} min_{a≠b} squared mean separation ≤τ²d/K_d,

K_d~27d⁴/256.

The t=0 case is immediate. This is a universal upper bound, not an asserted exact joint optimum. It is proved using base pairs alone, so the other pair constraints cannot invalidate it. Repeated detectors do not erase the asymptotic gap: their total amplification trace is charged as d and spread across the many adjacent-root constraints.

## Designed one-scalar-per-current experiment

Let 1=(1,…,1), and

s²=(d+24)/16,
h_j=(e_j+¼·1)/s.

Each h_j is unit norm. Use y_j=h_jᵀF_a g_j+σz_j, z_j independent standard Gaussian, for j=1,…,d. Subtracting the known baseline is permitted and does not change noise.

For any projector difference R=P_v−P_w, or R=P_v−0, the d-dimensional mean difference is

m(R)=τ/[s√d] [diag(R)+¼R1].

Here diag(R) is the vector of diagonal entries. This identity follows directly from g_j=e_j/√d and symmetry of R; the jth entry is τ[h_jᵀR e_j]/√d.

**All-pair minimum theorem:**

min_{a≠b} ||m(P_a−P_b)||²=τ²/(8s²d)
                            =2τ²/[d(d+24)].

Proof: Each allowed projector has diagonal entries in {0,1/2,1}. Every projector's row-sum vector has entries in {0,1}: coordinate directions have a single one, plus roots have two ones, and minus roots have zero row sums. Thus every row-sum difference has magnitude at most one in every coordinate.

If two nonbase models have different diagonal vectors, their diagonal difference has sum zero and entries in {0,±1/2,±1}. Thus at least two entries have magnitude at least 1/2. At each such coordinate the mean numerator has magnitude at least 1/2−1/4=1/4. Its squared norm is therefore at least 2/16=1/8. If one model is the base, choose the difference orientation R=P_v; both its diagonal and row-sum vectors are nonnegative, so they cannot cancel, and the numerator squared norm is at least ||diag(P_v)||²≥1/2. Thus all different-diagonal pairs exceed or meet the required lower bound.

If two distinct models have the same diagonal vector, they must be the plus and minus roots on the same coordinate pair. Their difference has zero diagonal and two unit-magnitude row sums. Its squared mean difference is exactly

[τ²/(s²d)] [2/16]=τ²/(8s²d).

These pairs exist for all d≥2 and attain the lower bound. This covers every base and nonbase pair and proves the exact minimum.

## Consequence and parameter scales

Relative to the best whole-vector single current, the designed d-current experiment has factor

G_d=2D_d/[d(d+24)]~4d/3.

Against the stronger single-current comparator that jointly optimizes arbitrary d unit detectors, its guaranteed improvement factor is at least

H_d=2K_d/[d²(d+24)]~27d/128.

These are achieved or guaranteed factors, not a claim that the d-current/one-detector design is globally optimal among all matched-budget experiments. Both exceed one for sufficiently large d. Since full-vector single detection is admissible and the joint upper bound has the same asymptotic order, the competent single-current optimum is Θ(τ²/d³). Because τ=Θ(d⁻²), its absolute margin scales as Θ(d⁻⁷), versus Θ(d⁻⁶) for this matched-count multi-current design; more energy or lower noise is still required as dimension grows.

With the specified Gaussian noise, KL between any two parameter alternatives is their squared mean separation divided by 2σ². Therefore the same factor compares worst pairwise KL. The single-current raw optimum is positive in each finite dimension.

### Deterministic-design classification corollary

Let N_d=d²+1 be the number of models, S_multi=2τ²/[d(d+24)], and choose

σ²=S_multi/[16 log(2N_d)].

For the explicit multi-current design, nearest-neighbor classification among its known mean vectors has worst error at most

(N_d−1)exp[−S_multi/(8σ²)]
 =(N_d−1)/(4N_d²)≤1/(4N_d).

The first bound follows by a union bound over pairwise Gaussian comparison errors. In contrast, every fixed single-current/current-detector design has two models with KL at most

[τ²d/K_d]/(2σ²)=8 log(2N_d)/H_d.

Le Cam's two-point bound and Pinsker's inequality therefore give single-current worst error at least

max{0,½[1−√(4 log(2N_d)/H_d)]}→1/2.

This is a full minimax separation over **deterministic, fixed, known-family designs and all estimators** at the specified dimension-dependent noise level. The hard pair may depend on the selected design, as permitted by those minimax quantifiers. It does not provide a lower bound for randomized or adaptive designs without an additional prior/averaging argument. Nor does it claim a useful finite-d bound when the displayed lower bound is zero. The multi arm's source switching remains charged as d settings; the single arm has one.

Resource scaling: d²+1 known candidate networks, d+1 terminals, d(d+1)/2 edges in each, d retained scalars, total input norm one. The multi-current schedule has d source settings and d separately implemented unit voltage detectors. The family has a succinct formula; explicitly materializing every candidate's conductance table costs O(d⁴) numbers. Its support is supplied, not acquired from unrestricted experimental data. The base construction and schedule are explicit polynomial-size objects; they are not a handpicked optimization instance or a hidden continuum of measurements.
