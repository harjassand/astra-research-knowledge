# Result: a sharp noise boundary for collective-thermal bound entanglement

I derived a theorem that closes the post-preparation noise-threshold gap recorded in Astra’s N135 card and extends the result from depolarizing noise to **every fixed unital qubit channel applied identically and independently**.

For depolarizing noise, the exact asymptotic boundary is

\[
\boxed{\lambda_c=\frac1{\sqrt3}\approx0.577350269.}
\]

The pinned repository previously established entanglement only for \(\lambda>\sqrt{\pi/8}\approx0.626657069\), explicitly leaving the exact threshold unresolved. 

The stronger finding is not the numerical constant alone. The derivation shows that this family of **multipartite bound-entangled states reaches the same identical-unital-noise boundary as a Bell pair**, despite no Bell pair being distillable by the individual, separated laboratories. Moreover, the surviving entanglement remains distinguishable from full separability using only **three local spin-measurement settings**.

**Status:** this is a self-contained mathematical derivation with newly executed checks, not an externally reviewed or historically priority-cleared discovery. I have not established that it is comparable in scientific significance to an important October-release result family.

## 2. Strongest exact theorem

### Model

For \(N\) qubits, define

\[
J_a=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_a^{(i)}.
\]

Let \(\rho_{N,\nu}\) be the stationary state selected from the maximally mixed input \(I/2^N\) by the collective thermal generator

\[
\mathcal L_\nu
=
(\nu+1)\mathcal D[J_-]+\nu\mathcal D[J_+],
\]

where \(\mathcal D[L](\rho)=L\rho L^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}\).

The assumptions are uniform collective coupling, unnormalized collective jumps, and no additional noise during preparation. This is the stationary state selected by that input—not a claim that the generator has a unique stationary state for arbitrary inputs. The selected-state formula and hidden-pair representation were already present in Astra’s original thermal checkpoint; I reconstructed the ingredients needed here. 

After preparation, apply the same unital qubit channel \(\Lambda\) independently to every qubit. Write its action on Bloch vectors as \(r\mapsto Tr\), and put

\[
\omega_N=\Lambda^{\otimes N}(\rho_{N,\nu_N}),
\qquad
s=\operatorname{tr}(TT^{\mathsf T})=\|T\|_F^2.
\]

Here \(\mathrm{SEP}_N\) means full separability across the \(N\) individual qubits.

### Theorem — internally derived, not externally reviewed

**At or below the boundary, \(s\le1\):**

\[
\boxed{\omega_N\in\mathrm{SEP}_N}
\]

for every \(N\) and every finite bath occupation \(\nu_N\ge0\).

**Above the boundary, \(s>1\):** provided

\[
\nu_N=o(\sqrt N),
\]

the output is entangled for all sufficiently large \(N\). More strongly,

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\inf_{\sigma\in\mathrm{SEP}_N}
\frac12\|\omega_N-\sigma\|_1>0.
}
\]

The positive gap may depend on \(T\). This does not assert that the distance approaches one or give a uniform gap as \(s\downarrow1\).

**The separation survives a fixed local measurement.** Choose one of three orthogonal axes uniformly, measure every qubit along that axis, and retain the axis and the summed outcome. If \(\mathcal M_N\) denotes this measurement, then

\[
\boxed{
\liminf_{N\to\infty}
\inf_{\sigma\in\mathrm{SEP}_N}
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\mathcal M_N(\omega_N),\mathcal M_N(\sigma)
\right)>0.
}
\]

Thus an entangling measurement across the whole system is not necessary for the separation.

### Bound-entangled regime

When additionally

\[
\nu_N\ge
\nu_*=\frac{\sqrt{15}-3}{6}
\approx0.145497224
\]

eventually, the entangled outputs remain separable across **every one-qubit-versus-rest cut** and have entanglement depth exactly two.

The singleton-separability calculation is reconstructed in the proof packet, rather than accepted solely from its repository card. The original checkpoint records the same threshold and distinguishes it from full separability. 

There is no contradiction: different bipartitions have different separable decompositions. They need not combine into a decomposition into products of all \(N\) individual qubits.

In particular, the fixed choice \(\nu=1\) supplies a family that is eventually **bound entangled throughout the entire above-boundary channel region**.

### Two concrete consequences

For depolarization,

\[
\Lambda_\lambda(\rho)
=
\lambda\rho+(1-\lambda)\frac I2,
\qquad 0\le\lambda\le1,
\]

we have \(T=\lambda I\), hence \(s=3\lambda^2\). Therefore

\[
\lambda\le1/\sqrt3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{fully separable for every }N,
\]

whereas

\[
\lambda>1/\sqrt3,\quad \nu_N=o(\sqrt N)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{entangled for all sufficiently large }N.
\]

For phase damping, \(T=\operatorname{diag}(\eta,\eta,1)\), every fixed residual coherence \(\eta\ne0\) preserves entanglement for sufficiently large \(N\); complete dephasing, \(\eta=0\), gives full separability.

The sphere \(s=1\) is **already known** as the identical-unital two-qubit entanglement-annihilation boundary. The proposed advance is that this particular many-body thermal bound-entangled family attains it, together with the local-measurement separation theorem—not a new classification of the channels. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.3757)

## 3. Essential proof

The full note supplies the stationary-state derivation, limiting estimates, singleton-cut calculation, and a separate filtered-moment proof. The decisive argument has four parts.

### A. An exact decomposition proves the separable side

The stationary state is a mixture of permutation-averaged configurations consisting of

\[
\frac{N-M}{2}\text{ singlet pairs}
\quad+\quad
M\text{ qubits in a separable symmetric thermal state}.
\]

Here \(M=2j\) is twice the total-spin label. The symmetric thermal factor has a positive coherent-product decomposition, obtained by product-filtering the coherent-state resolution of the symmetric projector.

Let

\[
K=TT^{\mathsf T}.
\]

The noisy singlet is

\[
(\Lambda\otimes\Lambda)(|\psi^-\rangle\langle\psi^-|)
=
\frac14\left(
I\otimes I-\sum_{a,b}K_{ab}\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_b
\right).
\]

After a common rotation diagonalizes \(K\), with eigenvalues \(k_a\ge0\), this becomes

\[
(1-s)\frac{I_4}{4}
+
\sum_{a=1}^3
k_a\frac{I_4-\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_a}{4}.
\]

When \(s\le1\), this is an explicit convex combination of separable states. Every hidden pair becomes separable; the unpaired factor was already separable. Consequently the whole mixture is fully separable.

This direction is finite-\(N\) and exact. It agrees with the established two-qubit result. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.3757)

**The converse needs new work:** an unrecorded mixture of entangled pair configurations can still be separable. Looking at the pairs individually cannot settle it.

### B. The thermal family has a Gaussian-plus-one-sided-displacement limit

The spin-sector weights imply

\[
\frac{M}{\sqrt N}\Rightarrow X,
\]

where \(X\) has the Maxwell density

\[
f_X(x)=
\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\,x^2e^{-x^2/2},
\qquad x>0.
\]

Within a spin sector, the mean thermal excitation number is at most \(\nu_N\). Therefore, when \(\nu_N=o(\sqrt N)\), the typical unpaired coherent spins approach the same downward direction.

Define

\[
\Sigma=\frac{I-TT^{\mathsf T}}4,
\qquad
v=Te_z.
\]

Directly multiplying the pair and unpaired-spin characteristic functions gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}
\left(\omega_Ne^{it\cdot J/\sqrt N}\right)
\longrightarrow
e^{-t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma t/2}
\,\mathbb E e^{-iX\,t\cdot v/2}.
\]

The limiting expression is the classical characteristic function of

\[
\boxed{Z=G_\Sigma-\frac12vX,}
\]

where \(G_\Sigma\) is a centered Gaussian independent of \(X\).

The important feature is not merely Gaussian noise. It is that the additional displacement is **one-sided**, because \(X\ge0\).

This is a characteristic-function statement. It does not assume that the three noncommuting spin components can be measured simultaneously.

### C. Every separable competitor has a constrained Gaussian-mixture limit

Consider an arbitrary product component with Bloch vectors \(r_1,\ldots,r_N\). Define

\[
\mu_N=\frac1{2\sqrt N}\sum_i r_i,
\qquad
Q_N=\frac1N\sum_i r_ir_i^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
C_N=\frac{I-Q_N}{4}.
\]

Because \(\|r_i\|\le1\),

\[
0\preceq C_N\preceq I/4,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}C_N\ge\frac12.
\]

A uniform Taylor expansion gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
\rho_{\mathrm{product}}e^{it\cdot J/\sqrt N}
\right)
=
e^{it\cdot\mu_N-t^{\mathsf T}C_Nt/2}
+O_t(N^{-1/2}).
\]

Crucially, the error is uniform over heterogeneous product inputs. Integrating over **any** separable decomposition produces a mixture of Gaussians whose component covariance matrices satisfy the same trace constraint.

For the three-setting result, only the three marginal variances are needed. If the measured laws converge, tightness forces a common limiting mixture with

\[
0\le c_a\le\frac14,
\qquad
c_1+c_2+c_3\ge\frac12
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]

The means and variances remain jointly mixed. Replacing the competitor by one Gaussian—or choosing unrelated mixtures for the different axes—would be invalid.

### D. One-sided tails force a contradiction above the boundary

Reverse an axis’s outcome labels when necessary so that its projection of \(v\) is nonnegative. The corresponding target limit has the form

\[
Z_a=G_a-b_aX,
\qquad b_a\ge0,
\]

with Gaussian variance

\[
\sigma_a^2=e_a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma e_a.
\]

Since \(X\ge0\),

\[
\Pr(Z_a>R)
\le
\Pr\!\left(\mathcal N(0,\sigma_a^2)>R\right).
\]

Now use the following elementary tail lemma:

> A Gaussian mixture whose upper tail is bounded by that of a Gaussian of variance \(\sigma^2\) cannot assign positive mixing mass to component variances greater than \(\sigma^2\).

Otherwise, some positive mass has both a strictly larger variance and a bounded mean. Its upper tail eventually decays more slowly than the claimed Gaussian upper bound, giving a contradiction.

Applying this separately to the three axes forces

\[
c_a\le\sigma_a^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]

But then

\[
\frac12
\le
\sum_a c_a
\le
\sum_a\sigma_a^2
=
\frac{3-s}{4}.
\]

This is impossible when \(s>1\).

The same argument proves the nonvanishing measurement-distance gap. Were that gap to approach zero along a subsequence, separable alternatives would have the same three limiting marginal laws as the target, producing precisely the impossible common mixture above.

**The reusable mechanism is therefore a separability obstruction based on one-sided fluctuation tails, not just a better second-moment estimate.**

## 4. A second derivation explains the old threshold’s slack

A separate route uses a weak product filter,

\[
F_{N,t}
=
\exp\!\left(\frac{tJ_n}{2\sqrt N}\right),
\qquad
n=\frac{Te_z}{\|Te_z\|}.
\]

For depolarization, the normalized filtered state has limiting total spin variance

\[
V_\lambda(t)
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\frac1N\sum_a\operatorname{Var}(J_a)
=
\frac{3(1-\lambda^2)}4
+
\frac{\lambda^2}{4}
\operatorname{Var}(X_{\lambda t/2}),
\]

where \(X_b\) has density proportional to

\[
x^2e^{-x^2/2-bx},\qquad x>0.
\]

Every fully separable state obeys

\[
\sum_a\operatorname{Var}(J_a)\ge N/2.
\]

This inequality follows directly from single-qubit Bloch-vector bounds; collective-spin criteria and their application to thermal bound entanglement have established antecedents in Tóth and collaborators’ work. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0702219)

At \(t=0\),

\[
\operatorname{Var}(X)=3-\frac8\pi,
\]

which reproduces the repository’s older sufficient threshold \(\sqrt{\pi/8}\). As \(t\) increases, the radius variance tends to zero, exposing the exact boundary \(1/\sqrt3\).

There is also an exact rational example, requiring no numerical integration:

\[
\lambda=\frac{29}{50}=0.58,\qquad \nu=1,\qquad t=100.
\]

The derived bound gives

\[
V_{29/50}(100)
\le
\frac{3(1-(29/50)^2)}4+\frac{12}{100^2}
=
\frac{4989}{10000}
<
\frac12.
\]

Thus eventual entanglement at \(\lambda=0.58\), inside the old unresolved interval, follows from an exact inequality.

**The filter is not an efficient experimental shortcut.** Rescaling its local factors to valid norm-one Kraus operators gives success probability

\[
p_{N,t}
=
\exp\!\left[-\frac{t\sqrt N}{2}+O(1)\right].
\]

That vanishing success probability is why I did not infer the unfiltered distance gap from the filtering argument. The three-setting tail proof establishes that gap separately, without postselection.

## 5. Computational verification actually executed

The packet contains two newly written scripts using different formulas for filtered moments and characteristic functions. They share basic matrix constructors and spin weights; this is internal verification, not independent external review.

The executed checks include **216** comparisons between the finite-\(N\) filtered-moment formula and explicit density matrices for \(N=2,\ldots,7\). The maximum absolute discrepancy in the variance sum divided by \(N\) was

\[
4.44\times10^{-16}.
\]

A separate set of **90** full-matrix characteristic-function comparisons, including generic measurement directions, had maximum discrepancy

\[
1.01\times10^{-15}.
\]

There were also 500 noisy-singlet partial-transpose checks and 90 exact symbolic Bernstein-generator identities. The latter check a background thermal-preservation mechanism, not the asymptotic theorem itself.

Large-\(N\) sector calculations produced, for \(\nu=1\):

| \(\lambda\) | Filter \(t\) | Variance sum/\(N\), \(N=10^8\) | Derived limiting value |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(1/\sqrt3\) | 50 | 0.5011464085 | 0.5011354788 |
| 0.58 | 50 | 0.4988470197 | 0.4988360285 |
| 0.60 | 18 | 0.4867260621 | 0.4866991317 |
| 0.62 | 12 | 0.4735302983 | 0.4734923148 |

Values below \(0.5\) violate the separable variance bound after filtering. The boundary case remains on the separable side, as required.

These large-\(N\) calculations evaluate spin-sector formulas—not \(2^N\)-dimensional matrices or physical experiments. Their decimals are floating-point diagnostics, not interval-certified finite-size theorems. The code records truncation bounds, including a separate reweighted bound where postselection is involved.

## 6. Novelty and significance

Several important ingredients are explicitly **not new**.

Collective thermal reservoirs generating or enhancing entanglement were studied by Li and Xu, including the role of singlet components. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505216) Collective-spin detection of thermal bound entanglement also predates this investigation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0702219) The distinctions among full separability, partition-specific separability, and entanglement depth under local noise are part of the established multipartite-noise literature. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.4790) Most importantly, the channel boundary \(\|T\|_F^2=1\) was already identified for two qubits by Filippov, Rybár and Ziman. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.3757)

**The proposed new contribution is the many-body converse:** above that known pair-annihilation boundary, this unrecorded thermal mixture still remains globally entangled, with a nonzero separation visible in three local measurement settings. The proof also extends that conclusion throughout the bath regime \(\nu_N=o(\sqrt N)\).

Relative to the pinned Astra state, this closes a specific open gap. Relative to the full historical literature, priority remains unresolved. The primary-source comparison located the major antecedents, but it was not an exhaustive specialist audit of fluctuation-limit separability criteria or all filtered collective witnesses.

The theoretical significance is the coexistence of three properties:

**Singleton separability, exact depth two, and asymptotically optimal identical-unital-noise survival for that depth class.**

That separates *robustness of entanglement* from *availability of distillable Bell pairs*. It does not establish a computational, sensing, thermodynamic, or cryptographic advantage.

There is an operational implication: the entanglement does not require inaccessible global measurements to distinguish it from full separability. However, the proof currently gives existence of bounded three-axis scores rather than an explicit optimized score table with a useful sample-complexity bound. The gap could be very small near the channel boundary.

## 7. Highest-value continuation and preserved research state

The highest-value continuation is to turn the three-axis separation into an explicit, certified witness:

\[
\text{bounded score functions}
\;+\;
\text{finite-}N\text{ error bounds}
\;+\;
\text{quantified dependence on }\|T\|_F^2-1.
\]

That would convert the existence theorem into an evaluable test without the product filter’s exponentially small success probability.

The present theorem does not cover nonunital channels, different noise maps at different sites, symmetry-breaking noise during preparation, critical baths \(\nu_N\asymp\sqrt N\), or channel parameters approaching the boundary with \(N\). Those changes alter actual proof interfaces and are recorded as unresolved—not treated as automatic extensions.
