## 1. Exact criterion: decision identification is weaker than mechanism identification

Let `theta` denote an admissible physical world. A declared action `a` has utility `J_theta(a)`, complete resource cost `c_theta(a)`, and feasibility `c_theta(a)<=B`. An experiment supplies a response record `y=h(theta)`. Let `A*(theta)` be the set of optimal feasible actions. For a finite action class, an exact decision based only on `y` exists if and only if

    intersection_{theta:h(theta)=y} A*(theta) is nonempty

for every response fiber that can occur.

Necessity: the decision rule must choose one action that is optimal in every world yielding that same record. Sufficiency: choose a common optimizer in each fiber. With finite actions, a fixed tie-breaking order gives an explicit rule. A unique mechanism need not be identified; all worlds in a fiber may have different microscopic descriptions and the same optimal action.

For a strict superiority claim between an illuminated action and a declared dark comparator class, define

    Delta(theta)=J_theta(light)-sup_{a in dark,c_theta(a)<=B}J_theta(a).

An exact response record certifies strict superiority uniformly over its compatible worlds precisely when `inf_{theta:h(theta)=y}Delta(theta)>0`. With uncertainty, replace the response fiber by a valid simultaneous confidence set. The same idea supplies a sufficient finite-precision margin; no particular inference terminology closes a fiber that still contains opposite decisions.

This criterion is about a specified action class, budget, horizon and population. A mechanism claim, a universal thermal-only exclusion, an optimum over untested control histories and a decision over a measured finite library are different questions.

## 2. Minimal useful measurement need not be a full mechanism or full spectrum

Suppose the detector is a fixed known affine map `y=A r+b`, while the decision uses the linear chemical score `J=w.r`. On an unrestricted response space, `J` is exactly determined by `y` if and only if `w` belongs to the row space of `A`.

If `w=A^T v`, then `J=v.(y-b)`; no full inversion is necessary. Conversely, if `w` does not annihilate `ker A`, choose `z in ker A` with `w.z!=0`. Perturb an interior nonnegative product vector by `+/-epsilon z`. Both responses have the same detector output but different utilities; choose a comparator between them and the decision reverses. The physical nonnegativity constraints are respected by taking epsilon small. On a restricted physical response set, the general fiber criterion above is the exact version; row-space membership is a transparent sufficient condition and a necessity when an open response region is admissible.

This identifies a minimal additional sensor obligation: observe the decision-relevant unresolved projection, rather than reconstruct every product or every hidden mechanism. It does not make a detector coefficient or common background free. A detector gain or offset allowed to change between dark and light can create apparent improvement with identical chemistry. Joint calibration uncertainty must be carried through the forward model.

## 3. A decision witness can close a finite experiment without site identification

For independently controllable modules, let a measured mode have utility `J_ij` and cost vector `c_ij`. Let budgets be `B` and let `beta>=0` componentwise. If valid bounds cover every mode in the declared class, acquire numbers `alpha_i` satisfying

    J_ij-beta.c_ij <= alpha_i for every i,j.

Then every schedule in the source's normalized-mode relaxation obeys

    total utility <= beta.B+sum_i alpha_i.

This is simply summing the mode inequalities with nonnegative weights and applying resource feasibility. It remains true for deterministic choices. Thus a strict lower confidence bound for illuminated utility above this upper certificate, together with an upper confidence bound certifying illuminated cost feasibility, closes that **declared decision** without identifying microscopic sites or the light mechanism.

The sufficient calibration can target the penalized scalar `J-beta.c`, rather than all output coordinates. However, every admitted mode must be covered, directly or by a justified envelope. Choosing beta from data is safe if the original joint confidence set covers all measured response/cost coordinates; every resulting algebraic witness is then valid simultaneously. Choosing a score w from scalar confidence intervals that were only valid for one prespecified score is not safe.

The converse direction is asymmetric. Being below a dark **upper relaxation** does not establish a feasible dark emulator. To close a decision in favor of dark, replay an actually feasible dark configuration and obtain a lower utility confidence bound that exceeds the illuminated upper bound at matched cost. An LP mixture can require unavailable time sharing, incur transitions or be fractionally infeasible. Overlapping bounds remain UNKNOWN.

## 4. Coverage is necessary; finite calibration cannot silently cover arbitrary controls

If an unmeasured admitted action has no valid response upper bound, two worlds can agree on every calibration and differ only at that action: assign it low utility in one world and utility above the illuminated candidate in the other. Their response fibers contain opposite decisions. No decision rule using those calibrations can certify the same winner in both worlds.

This obstruction persists for finitely sampled continuous controls even when the response is required to be smooth: a nonnegative smooth bump supported between calibration points leaves every sampled response unchanged and raises the unsampled optimum. A measured grid is not a universal temperature or pulse-history envelope.

A sufficient repair is an acquired modulus. If each module's utility and cost obey

    |J_i(x)-J_i(xj)|<=L_J,i d(x,xj),
    |c_i(x)-c_i(xj)|<=L_c,i d(x,xj),

and the calibration grid has fill distance `h_i`, then

    J_i(x)-beta c_i(x)
       <= max_j(J_ij^upper-beta c_ij^lower)
                           +(L_J,i+beta L_c,i)h_i.

The resulting sum is a valid continuum dark-control upper certificate. The modulus is a supplied/acquired structural input, including every physical history variable the control class admits. A smooth recorded temperature or an Arrhenius fit over some range does not establish it for unknown populations, pulses, transient states or catalyst aging.

The incoming heterogeneous Arrhenius counterexample is stronger than mere finite-grid failure: even a complete common-temperature bulk response curve may not identify the local-population hull under population-correlated heating. A same-module operational library remains valid for its directly calibrated controls because it measures the internal heterogeneity's aggregate response. It does not become a complete library of all spatially selective thermal states.

## 5. Factorization and hidden interactions are paid information

The module model reduces an exponential configuration class to local responses only under additive output and cost closure. Without that structure, calibrating modules separately cannot exclude a response term supported on one untested joint configuration. For example, add

    H(a_1,...,a_n)=K product_i 1{a_i=a_i*}

at an untested configuration `a*`. It is zero on every tested configuration and can change the best dark utility arbitrarily. A smooth control-space bump gives the same issue for continuous modes. A finite held-out additivity test can falsify factorization; it does not prove a uniform bound over all untested configurations without further assumptions.

A justified uniform residual `|J_joint-sum_i J_i|<=kappa` repairs the utility bound by adding `kappa`; resource residuals need their own conservative sign treatment. Shared gas inventories, pressure, heating and detector saturation can change both sides. The fact that modules have separate names is not a physical independence certificate.

## 6. Varying costs, durations, and uncertainty

The source's upper-score/lower-cost substitution is conservative: optimistic cost admits more dark actions and optimistic utility raises their bound. A sharper version uses the joint calibrated uncertainty set `U_ij` and

    alpha_i(beta)=max_j sup_{(J,c) in U_ij}(J-beta.c),

rather than independently maximizing utility and minimizing cost. Retaining their correlation can materially sharpen a decision. Common uncertain latent parameters across modules should also be retained where possible; replacing them by independently favorable values is an outer bound, not an acquired physical configuration.

Budget feasibility is itself a decision boundary. If a high-reward action has unknown cost `B+/-epsilon`, worlds arbitrarily close in cost response can require different choices even when their reward gap is large. No uniform finite precision closes exact feasibility at every budget boundary. Require a resource margin, an explicit tolerance, or a risk/expected-cost decision with its own declared semantics.

An expected cost is not a hard per-trial budget certificate: an action costing `0` or `2B` with equal probability has mean `B` but violates a pathwise budget half the time. The optimization must match the intended resource guarantee.

The normalization `sum_j lambda_ij=1` also fixes an exposure horizon. Mixing rate responses uses time weights. Totals measured over unequal-duration blocks must be divided by their durations, or the duration must appear explicitly as a resource. Settling, start-up, memory and idle modes cannot be silently assigned zero cost. A comparator class should contain a feasible baseline or idle mode; apparent superiority to an empty feasible dark class is vacuous.

## 7. Two-port physical response: what the minimal experiment identifies

For the explicitly assumed steady linear reciprocal passive two-port,

    Q=L Delta P, L=[[L11,L12],[L12,L22]] positive semidefinite,

two unloaded directional flow ratios identify only

    q^2=L12^2/(L11 L22).

This determines the stated maximum efficiency within that model. It does not identify absolute power. Scaling `L` to `sL` leaves both ratios and q^2 unchanged while scaling every flow and the maximum output at a fixed source pressure:

    P_out,max=L12^2 (Delta P1)^2/(4L22).

Thus an efficiency-only response can leave a fixed-power or throughput decision unresolved by an arbitrarily large factor. An absolute flow/pressure calibration or a direct loaded-work test closes the missing scale. Two linearly independent pressure excitations with their two measured flows identify the entire linear matrix; they do not certify that a real specimen remains linear, passive, reciprocal and stationary outside the tested domain.

A direct same-task loaded experiment can decide between two actually implemented policies by their measured net useful work and complete input cost, without identifying electronic versus mechanical transmission. That is a useful decision closure. It does not establish a mechanism, isolation, material lifetime, untested optimized load or superiority over all conventional implementations. Matched controls must deliver the same required work, throughput, isolation and retention endpoint.

## 8. Minimal confirmatory protocol and matched incumbent

Choose the actual utility, horizon, resource guarantee and finite comparator policies before confirmation. Acquire enough detector response to identify that utility; acquire cost feasibility with a margin; build a simultaneous uncertainty set covering calibration, drift and any structural closure residuals. Use the dark upper witness if available and replay the best attainable dark configuration. Measure the candidate independently at the same endpoint and include all auxiliary input costs. Stop with a declared winner only when the two-sided decision intervals separate; otherwise retain UNKNOWN.

This can be smaller than mechanism identification, but it is ordinary operational decision design. A direct randomized comparison using the same measured policies and scores is the mandatory matched incumbent. Any claimed economy from local calibration must pay its additivity/coverage assumptions and compare against that incumbent, not against full hidden-state tomography. No sample-efficiency, algorithmic novelty or physical superiority is established by the formulation itself.
