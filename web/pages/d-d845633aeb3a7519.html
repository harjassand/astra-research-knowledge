# Finite dose and hidden-dose counterexamples to blanket adaptive robustness

## Scoped conclusion

In the published one-cluster, finite-`β` Gillespie model, the running-maximum force rule is not dose invariant. Its ranking advantage depends on the distribution used for ligand quantity and on whether affinity comparisons have independently drawn or matched quantities. The exact hard-threshold argument only proves reachability in the `β=∞` limit; it cannot certify the finite-`β=5` simulations or a biological mechanism.

## Independent redraws: nuisance-dose ranking

For each affinity pair we independently drew `L` for the low- and high-affinity receptor, as the source Fig. 5c code does. Both controllers were treated as knowing the prior. Model parameters were `Eb=13.3 kBT`, `ΔEb=.5 kBT`, `Ea=12.6 kBT`, `kon=.05/s`, `k0=1126.92/s`, `F0=800 pN`, `β=5`, `tmin=1 s`, and `tmax=1800 s`. The adaptive threshold came from a finite scan (`mc=60,50,30` for the three priors); the time-only policy came from a 3×3 scan (`tc=60,60,240 s`). These are finite-grid comparators, not global optima.

Fresh 5000-pair reruns yielded:

| Dose prior | Adaptive `F(mmax)` AUC (95% MC interval) | Time-only AUC (95% MC interval) | Adaptive fraction censored | Mean contact duration: adaptive / time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `max(5, round Normal(100,30))` | .762 [.750,.773] | .642 [.629,.655] | .034 / .038 | 123 / 67 s |
| Uniform integers 5–195 | .690 [.678,.703] | .584 [.570,.597] | .150 / 0 | 315 / 67 s |
| Uniform integers 5–60 | .631 [.618,.644] | .595 [.582,.609] | .202 / .214 | 435 / 175 s |

The wide-dose adaptive AUC remains above the time-only AUC even under the worst completion of every `tmax`-censored score consistent with its current score and finite antigen pool: [.650,.761] versus .584. In the low-dose prior its corresponding interval is [.542,.761], which crosses the time-only result. These are outcome-completion bounds, not confidence intervals. The low-dose result therefore does not establish an adaptive advantage after accounting for contact cutoff.

Adaptive gains on independently varying dose come with longer simulated contacts. In the source-normal prior, adaptive contact duration is about 1.8× the time control; in the wide prior it is about 4.7×. The model's Bell-barrier proxy is lower for adaptive runs in these samples, but that proxy omits force displacement and cannot be called physical work or ATP cost.

## Matched-dose comparisons: conditional affinity ranking

Repeating the same comparisons with each low-/high-affinity pair at the same drawn `L` removes between-cell dose as a ranking nuisance. AUCs became:

| Dose prior | Adaptive `F(mmax)` AUC (95% MC interval) | Time-only AUC (95% MC interval) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `Normal(100,30)` | .806 [.795,.816] | .876 [.867,.884] |
| Uniform 5–195 | .735 [.723,.747] | .860 [.851,.869] |
| Uniform 5–60 | .660 [.648,.672] | .730 [.719,.742] |

The time-only comparator had no `tmax` censoring in these runs. For this matched-dose diagnostic we used the same prior-tuned time settings from the initial scan, not a separate time-schedule optimization for the matched-dose objective; time-only already wins without that extra tuning. This does not invalidate the source's independently redrawn ranking metric: it shows what that metric asks. Adaptive force is useful as a partial compensator when different encounters contain different hidden ligand doses, while not being a general improvement in affinity discrimination conditional on a shared dose.

## Local versus global diagnostic

At the source baseline `L=100`, `mc=tc=60`, `F0=800 pN`, `β=5`, the source-style one-sided finite difference gives:

| Policy | `αE` for `ΔEb=.5 kBT` | `αL` per added antigen (`ΔL=24`) | `αL(ΔL=24)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `F(mmax)` | 2.771 | 0.00878 | 0.211 |
| `F(t)` | 3.516 | 0.08784 | 2.108 |

The normalized affinity-to-dose sensitivity ratio improves from about 40 to 316, a 7.9× shift. But affinity sensitivity itself falls (2.77 vs 3.52), and the matched-dose global AUC favors time-only control. A small local dose derivative is therefore not synonymous with higher receptor selectivity or robustness to a prior shift.

## Exact boundary and finite-β counterexample

In the hard step-force (`β=∞`) model, `m<=L`, so `L<mc` makes the threshold unreachable. For `L>=mc`, the exact reach probability is in `../proof/finite_dose_threshold.md`. With `mc=60`, this gives `h1(59)=0` and `h1(60)=0.998068` at source pre-force rates. This is an idealized reachability result only.

At finite `β=5`, `F(mmax)` is positive below the threshold. If `L=30`, the maximum attainable force is bounded by `800·30^5/(30^5+60^5)=24.24 pN`; it is not identically zero. The threshold scan's prior-optimal `mc` changes 60→50→30 from the normal prior to the wide and low uniform priors. At `mc=80` on the low prior, 99.8% of runs hit `tmax`; adaptive score AUC falls to about .493. A fixed threshold is thus not a universal finite-dose controller.
