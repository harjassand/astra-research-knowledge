RESTART: CYCLE THREE HANKEL-GAP / HORIZON MODULUS

Read RESULT.txt first. For the stationary two-state symmetric HMM with
transition persistence rho in (0,1/4] and fixed BSC emission eta=1/2, the
length-one context/future Hankel block has second singular value
gamma=rho/8. Its rank-one SVD truncation is iid fair. The expected full-past
H-step KL approximation bias, evaluated on an independent fresh stationary
test trajectory with its full observed past as context, is exactly
H I(Y_0;Y_<0), bounded by

    2 H gamma^2 <= risk <= (512/15) H gamma^2.

The state count, transition-entry floor, mixing time, emission floor and
emission singular value are constant. The charged collapsing margin is
sigma_min(P)=rho=8 gamma. The rank truncation is useful only relative to a
fixed deterministic H and declared tolerance; full-future KL diverges for
gamma>0. This is an approximation-bias theorem, not a same-path acquisition
lower bound for predictors selected using a training transcript. The
expected fixed-H sum is not a random-stopping-time, anytime, or pathwise
cumulative guarantee.

Run the finite fixture:

    python3 outputs/research/acquisition_learning/cycle3/horizon_gap_modulus/check.py

The script computes observed word laws with fractions exactly for
rho=1/4,1/8,1/16 and the rho=0 rank-one boundary, and checks finite-context KL bounds using floating-point
logarithms for history lengths 1-3 and future horizons 1-3. It does not
verify the infinite-past information proof; that proof is in RESULT.txt.

Strong same-data comparator: a raw one-stream lag-product moment estimates
rho with mean squared error <=48/(n-1). Its full-history plug-in predictor
has expected H-step continuation KL <=240H/(n-1), uniformly over
continuation histories, so sufficiently many raw symbols let a learner
retain the weak mode rather than truncate it. A full raw-path likelihood is
at least as informed. Supplying the numerical rank-two representation would
remove acquisition cost; supplying only its basis would not.
