## Exact interface and pointwise theorem

Fix a dimension d, a faithful normalized state σ, and a finite known Hermitian frame `{A_a}` with ordinary complex commutant `CI`. For every λ in `[0,1]`, define the entire operator-valued score by

\[
-i[A_a,\tau]=(1-\lambda)\tau S_a^{\tau,\lambda}
+\lambda S_a^{\tau,\lambda}\tau.
\]

The state and frame are fixed before drawing λ. The target is not supplied as a spectrum oracle to an estimator: its spectral coordinates below are used solely to prove the theorem.

**Theorem.** There are finitely many exceptional weights outside which the score tuple identifies σ among **all** faithful competitors. Consequently one weight drawn independently from an absolutely continuous distribution on a nondegenerate interior interval identifies this fixed σ almost surely.

Let `σ=U diag(p_i)U†`, `D=√σ`. The exact score fiber, obtained by rearranging both score equations, is

\[
\rho=DQD,\qquad Q>0,\quad\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma Q)=1,
\quad[Q,K_{\lambda,a}^{\sigma}]=0,
\tag{1}
\]

where in the σ eigenbasis

\[
(K_{\lambda,a}^{\sigma})_{ij}
=\frac{\sqrt{p_i p_j}}{(1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j}(A_a)_{ij}.
\tag{2}
\]

For clarity, if `L=S_a^{σ,λ}`, the rearranged matrices are
`B=A−iλL`, `C=A+i(1−λ)L`; the equations are `Bσ=σC` and `Bρ=ρC`. They are equivalent to (1) with `K=DCD⁻¹`. A Hermitian Q commuting with K also commutes with K†, by taking adjoints. A nonscalar Hermitian commutant element gives a positive normalized perturbation of Q=I, so a scalar Hermitian joint commutant is necessary and sufficient for a singleton faithful fiber.

One can use the Gram matrix of the real map on traceless Hermitian X,
`X↦([X,K_a],[X,K_a†])_a`. Its entries are rational in λ, it is positive definite at λ=0, and its determinant is not the zero rational function. Its zero set is finite. This proves the theorem with the precise physical fiber condition.

There is a cleaner sufficient rank test with a sharper degree count: use the complex map

\[
\mathcal M_\lambda:\{X:\operatorname{Tr}X=0\}\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_a M_d(\mathbb C),\qquad X\longmapsto([X,K_{\lambda,a}])_a.
\tag{3}
\]

Its domain has dimension `N=d²−1`. At λ=0, `K_0=D⁻¹AD` is a single common similarity of the original frame. Therefore (3) is injective. Choose one N-by-N output-coordinate minor nonzero at λ=0. Its entries are rational in λ with no poles on `[0,1]`; the selected minor is nonzero except at finitely many real roots. At every nonzero-minor weight the complex commutant is scalar, and hence (1) gives uniqueness. A singular complex commutant is a stronger possible failure than physical blindness, so this argument does not claim that every zero of the selected minor causes a collision.

The theorem says `for every fixed σ, probability one over λ, for every faithful ρ`. It does **not** exchange these quantifiers to `probability one over λ, for every σ and ρ`. An adversary choosing a target/frame after a prescribed finite set of weights can use the finite-weight Krylov obstruction. No contradiction follows.

## Finite randomness: an explicit root count

Let `q=d(d−1)` and clear denominators using

\[
P_\sigma(\lambda)=\prod_{i\ne j}[(1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j].
\]

This polynomial has degree at most q and is strictly positive on `[0,1]`. Diagonal entries of K equal those of A, while each offdiagonal entry has one of these factors as its denominator. Thus every entry of `P_σ K` is a polynomial of degree at most q, also after conjugating back to any fixed physical basis. Every entry of `P_σ M_λ` has the same degree bound. The selected N-by-N minor is therefore a nonzero polynomial of degree at most

\[
B=qN=d(d-1)(d^2-1).
\tag{4}
\]

Every physically blind weight makes that minor zero. There are at most B bad weights. This improves the twice-as-large bound obtained by clearing a Gram determinant.

Draw uniformly from M distinct interior grid points, fixed independently of the target/frame. The probability of failed exact identification for each fixed target/frame is at most `min(1,B/M)`. For failure probability ε, take `M=2^b>B/ε`; a uniform draw uses b unbiased bits. If δ is a fixed rational in `(0,1/2)`, the exact rational grid `δ+(1−2δ)(j+1)/(M+1)`, `j=0,…,M−1`, stays interior. This is finite random design with a uniform **pointwise** failure bound over targets. It is not a simultaneous-all-target event, and it gives no lower bound on distance to a bad root or on the smallest commutator singular value.

For `d=2^n`, `B<d⁴`, so the random-bit count is `O(n+log(1/ε))`. This counts design randomness only; exact score operators, measurement compilation, acquisition and candidate evaluation are not free.

## Weighted metric and copy-loss audit

Dong–Leng arXiv:2609.28391v1 Appendix A.1, Lemma 12 Eqs. (41)–(45), defines the positive Hilbert–Schmidt superoperator

\[
\mathscr I_{\sigma,\lambda}=(1-\lambda)L_\sigma+\lambda R_\sigma.
\]

Both multiplication superoperators are positive selfadjoint and commute. If λ lies in `[δ,1−δ]`, then

\[
2\delta\mathscr I_{\sigma,1/2}
\preceq\mathscr I_{\sigma,\lambda}
\preceq2(1-\delta)\mathscr I_{\sigma,1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]

This is relative metric regularity. It does not remove a target eigenvalue floor in an absolute Hilbert–Schmidt comparison: `λ_min(σ)||X||_F²≤||X||²_{σ,λ}≤λ_max(σ)||X||_F²`.

The population objective is exactly

\[
J_\lambda(\rho;\sigma)=\frac12\sum_a
\|S_a^{\rho,\lambda}-S_a^{\sigma,\lambda}\|_{\sigma,\lambda}^2.
\]

Strict positivity makes zero objective equivalent to matching every complete score. Lemma 14 Eq. (56) gives

\[
J_\lambda(\rho;\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma O_\lambda(\rho))+C_\lambda,
\]

\[
O_\lambda(\rho)=\frac12\sum_a\left[
\lambda S_a^\dagger S_a+(1-\lambda)S_aS_a^\dagger
+\partial_a(S_a+S_a^\dagger)\right],
\qquad\partial_a(X)=-i[A_a,X].
\tag{6}
\]

The observable is Hermitian and depends on the candidate and known frame, while the constant is independent of the candidate. The source integration-by-parts identity checks: the two cross terms sum to `−Tr σ∂(S+S†)`. At λ=1/2, S is Hermitian, recovering `.5 S²+∂S` per direction. In a well-specified model, σ achieves zero; at a good draw it is the unique state achieving zero. This does not assert that a numerical optimizer finds it.

A coarse cost bound is possible without a target spectrum oracle, but it exposes dimension dependence. In a candidate eigenbasis,

\[
S_{ij}=i\frac{p_i-p_j}{(1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j}A_{ij},
\qquad\|S\|_F\le\delta^{-1}\|A\|_F.
\]

Consequently `||S||op≤√d ||A||op/δ` and the norm of the summand observable in (6) is at most

\[
R_a=\|A_a\|_{\rm op}^2\left(\frac{d}{2\delta^2}
+\frac{2\sqrt d}{\delta}\right).
\tag{7}
\]

If a projective measurement of the supplied total candidate observable is already implementable, a Hoeffding bound needs at most `2(ΣR_a)² log(2/α)/η²` copies to estimate its expectation within η with failure probability α. The construction/compilation of that observable, repeated optimization queries, and converting loss error to state error are additional charged tasks. The elementary entrywise multiplier bound establishes the Frobenius estimate; it must not be misread as a dimension-free operator-norm or circuit-cost bound. No efficient quantum advantage is established.

## One tuple works over a specified analytic model

Let Θ be a compact semianalytic subset of `R^p`. Suppose `θ↦τ_θ` is real analytic and faithful on an open neighborhood of Θ; a compact analytic parameter manifold with finitely many such charts also suffices. The known Hermitian frame is fixed and ordinarily irreducible. Choose m weights whose joint distribution is absolutely continuous on `I^m`, where `I=[δ,1−δ]`. Independence is sufficient, but joint absolute continuity is the needed property.

**Theorem.** If `m>2p`, the joint complete score tuple identifies every state in the model simultaneously almost surely. Redundant parameters are allowed: the conclusion is injectivity in τ, not necessarily in θ.

Define E_m as the triples `(θ,θ′,λ_1,…,λ_m)` with `τ_θ≠τ_θ′` and all scores equal at each chosen weight. The state inequality is the positivity of a real-analytic squared Frobenius difference. Score equality is the vanishing of squared real/imaginary entry differences. The weighted Sylvester matrix is invertible on a neighborhood of `Θ×I`, so its inverse and scores are real analytic there. Compactness gives a uniform faithful eigenvalue floor automatically. Thus E_m is relatively compact semianalytic; it need not be closed, because state inequality excludes the diagonal.

For every fixed distinct-state pair, the bad weight set is finite: if the pair had infinitely many score-equality weights in I, rationality would imply equality at all weights, contradicting endpoint identification. Alternatively the pointwise theorem already places this pair's bad set inside a finite exceptional set. Hence each fiber of E_m over `(θ,θ′)` has dimension zero (a finite product of finite sets).

The analytic fiber-dimension inequality gives `dim E_m≤2p`. Projection into `I^m` cannot increase dimension; relative compactness makes that projection subanalytic. For `m>2p` it has Lebesgue measure zero. This projection is precisely the set of weight tuples that fail to identify at least one distinct model-state pair. Absolute continuity avoids it almost surely. This justifies the simultaneous-all-model-states quantifier; a union of pairwise probability-zero events alone would not justify it.

Nonlinear Sci. 12 (2002), Theorem 2 and Lemma 2.1, pp. 563–564, supplies the same analytic experiment-design argument; §4 extends to vector-valued responses. Its precursor *Critical points for least-squares problems involving certain analytic functions, with applications to sigmoidal nets* (1996), Appendix A Proposition A.2, pp. 263–264, supplies the fiber/projection dimension facts. We read these theorem/proof excerpts from recoverable primary-author PDFs. The model theorem is a direct specialization of this existing method. The quantum pointwise one-weight result is stronger than a general fixed-target analytic experiment bound because the dressed commutator rank certificate excludes all competitors together; historical novelty of that specialization remains unverified.

The result promises exact model identification, not a uniform linear inverse estimate. For each fixed η>0, compactness gives a positive loss separation between model states at least η apart after a good tuple is chosen; the theorem neither computes that number nor controls its deterioration near the diagonal. Analyticity, a prescribed model independent of the draws, and complete score access are consequential assumptions. An arbitrary smooth model or a state/model chosen after the draws is outside this theorem.

## Consequence and next falsifiable step

The physical local X/Z and X/Y/Z SLD counterexamples show that a deterministic symmetric weight can be blind outside high temperature. They do not imply that an independently randomized interior weight is blind for the same fixed target. Pointwise randomization preserves the paper's weighted copy-loss interface and changes the identification quantifier. For a compact p-parameter Gibbs family with a fixed ordinary-irreducible frame, the existing analytic design method supplies a simultaneous model repair using at most `2p+1` weights.

The decisive missing implication is efficient calibration and acquisition: bound the measure of `{λ: smallest commutator singular value≤t}` for a specified implementable frame and target/model, then prove candidate score-observable compilation and copy complexity with an equally informed direct-measurement baseline. A nonzero polynomial and root count alone do not establish those bounds.
