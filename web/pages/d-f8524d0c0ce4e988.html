# Independent vector experiment theorem and adversarial checks

## Interface and surviving theorem

Let M be a second-countable C1 real manifold of dimension d, H a separable real Hilbert space, Y a real Banach space, and F:M→L(H,Y) a C1 map in operator norm. Let μ be a Gaussian Borel probability on H with full support. For k independent samples f_i from μ define E_f(a)=(F(a)f_1,…,F(a)f_k) in Y^k.

If rank(F(a)−F(b))≥R for every a≠b and kR>2d, E_f is injective almost surely, simultaneously for all a,b. If F is C2 and rank(dF(a)[v])≥T for every nonzero tangent v, and kT>2d−1, E_f is an immersion almost surely. Both conditions give an injective immersion on the same probability-one set. In particular infinite rank of every secant and every nonzero tangent operator implies that ONE Gaussian input suffices.

Analyticity is unnecessary for this uniform rank statement. It is useful for finite-matrix rank stratification and often available for PDE forward maps. The derivative hypothesis is independent of the secant hypothesis. Counts refer to physical inputs with entire Y-valued responses, not a finite scalar sensor budget.

## Independently reconstructed Hilbert incidence lemma

Let P be a second-countable C1 manifold of dimension b, and let A:P→L(H,Y) be C1, with rank A(p)≥R everywhere. Then kR>b implies

μ^k{(f_i): there exists p∈P with A(p)f_i=0 for all i}=0.

Proof. Center the Gaussian first; a deterministic mean will only shift affine terms below. Its covariance Q is positive, trace class and injective because of full support. In a covariance eigenbasis e_j, Qe_j=λ_je_j, λ_j>0, Gaussian samples have expansions f_i=Σ_j sqrt(λ_j)ξ_ij e_j with independent standard real normal ξ_ij. Finite-dimensional H is handled by taking all its coordinates.

Fix p_0. Since rank A(p_0)≥R, there are h_1,…,h_R with independent images A(p_0)h_j. Finite KL spans H_N=span(e_1,…,e_N) are dense, so approximate these vectors sufficiently closely that their images remain independent. Hence some N gives rank A(p_0)|H_N≥R. There are continuous linear functionals ℓ_1,…,ℓ_R on Y for which L=A(p_0)|H_N followed by ℓ=(ℓ_j) has rank R: choose coordinate functionals on the finite-dimensional image span and extend by Hahn–Banach. Some R×R minor of this R×N matrix is nonzero, and remains nonzero on an open neighborhood U of p_0. Thus on U, L(p)=ℓ A(p)|H_N has rank exactly R. Second countability provides a countable collection of such U,N,ℓ covering P.

Fix one U and split each sample into its finite head x_i∈H_N and tail z_i∈H_N⊥. Condition on all tails. The necessary equations for a bad input are

G_i(p,x_i)=ℓ A(p)(x_i+z_i)=0 in R^R.

For every fixed tail tuple, G=(G_i):U×(H_N)^k→(R^R)^k is C1. Its derivative in head variables has block diagonal form diag(L(p),…,L(p)), of rank kR. Therefore its zero set is a C1 submanifold, or empty, of dimension b+kN−kR. This number is smaller than kN. Its projection into the head space has Lebesgue measure zero: cover by countably many C1 charts and compact coordinate cubes, whose projected parametrizations are Lipschitz maps from lower-dimensional Euclidean sets. Finite heads have a nonsingular Gaussian density and are independent of tails, so the conditional probability of any bad head is zero for every fixed tail.

Measurability is not being assumed from an arbitrary uncountable union. Exhaust U by countably many compact sets K_j⊂U. For each compact K_j the ORIGINAL bad event

B_K={f∈H^k: there exists p∈K with A(p)f_i=0 for all i}

is closed: a convergent sequence of input tuples admits a convergent subsequence of witnessing parameters by compactness, and operator-norm continuity passes equations to the limit. Its head-tail pullback is Borel and its sections are contained in the null projections just proved. Fubini applies to this Borel event. A countable union over compact pieces and covering neighborhoods proves the lemma. This also repairs the usual informal gap of projecting a noncompact infinite-dimensional incidence and assuming its projection is measurable.

Apply the lemma to P=(M×M)\Δ, b=2d, A(a,b)=F(a)−F(b). For immersion use countably many coordinate charts of real projective tangent bundle P(TM), b=2d−1, with local C1 representatives v of each projective direction; A(a,[v])=dF(a)[v] is C1 when F is C2. Changing representatives by a nonzero scalar changes neither rank nor kernel. No compactness of M is needed for these probability-one conclusions.

## Rank strata and field conventions

For a FINITE MATRIX real-analytic F, rank sets D_r={(a,b):a≠b,rank(F(a)−F(b))=r} are locally semianalytic. A countable smooth stratification permits the lemma on each stratum. If d_r is their real dimension, sufficient inequalities are kr>d_r for every nonempty D_r. This is a sufficient criterion; failure of the inequality is not impossibility. A tangent rank-stratum refinement is identical. One must either state finite matrices or supply a genuine stratification for infinite-operator rank loci; finite-rank truncations alone do not automatically prove such a stratification.

All dimensions/ranks in the above proof are REAL. If H,Y are complex, F(a) is complex linear, and the Gaussian has full support on the REALIFICATION of H, complex rank r means real rank2r. Thus sufficient secant count is 2kr>2d. Positive complex variance of every complex-linear scalar observation alone is not a substitute for full real support: a real Gaussian supported on the real axis of C has positive complex variance but no density in R2.

In finite-dimensional H, Gaussian sampling can be replaced by any joint input law absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on H^k. In Hilbert space it suffices to have independent dense-basis finite heads with conditional densities; the KL decomposition is the clean Gaussian instance.

## Compact inverse Lipschitz proof, including irregular K

Let M be a C1 embedded manifold in a Banach space B, and let K⊂M be ANY compact subset. Fix an injective C1 immersion E:M→Y^k. K need not itself be a manifold, and its boundary need not be regular.

In an embedded chart φ:U⊂R^d→B and at z0, J=D(E∘φ)(z0) is injective, so s=min_{|v|=1}||Jv||>0. On a sufficiently small convex coordinate ball, ||D(E∘φ)(z)−J||≤s/2 and ||Dφ(z)||≤L. Integrating along a segment and using a fixed J gives

||E(φ(z))−E(φ(w))||≥(s/2)|z−w|,
||φ(z)−φ(w)||_B≤L|z−w|.

For the upper estimate, choose bounded R:B→R^d with RDφ(z0)=Id by extending finite-dimensional coordinate functionals. Shrink so ||RDφ(z)−Id||≤1/2. Integration gives |z−w|≤2||R||·||φ(z)−φ(w)||_B. Bounded derivative of E∘φ now gives a local upper Lipschitz constant as well.

Choose finitely many smaller chart neighborhoods covering K. Pairs in at least one common such neighborhood satisfy local lower/upper inequalities. This union of pair neighborhoods is an open neighborhood of the diagonal in K×K. Its complement C is compact and contains no diagonal pairs. On C the ratio ||E(a)−E(b)||/||a−b||_B is continuous, finite, and positive; its minimum is positive and maximum finite. Combine these with the finitely many local constants. Thus E|K is bi-Lipschitz with sample-dependent constants. If F is defined only on K, one must instead provide a suitable smooth ambient model extension; the theorem does not derive a differentiable chart from an arbitrary compact set.

## Equality obstruction and uniform nonlinear zero-one failure

Take M=R, H=R3, Y=R2, and the polynomial operator family

F(t)=[[t,t²,t³],[0,t,t²]].

For t≠s, (F(t)−F(s))/(t−s)=[[1,t+s,t²+ts+s²],[0,1,t+s]], so every secant has rank2. Moreover F'(t)=[[1,2t,3t²],[0,1,2t]] has rank2 for every t.

For input f=(a,b,c), c≠0, collision of distinct t,s is equivalent to t+s=−b/c and ts=a/c. Hence a real collision exists exactly when Δ=b²−4ac>0. If Δ<0 the sampled map is injective. Both sets are nonempty open subsets of R3, so any full-support nonsingular Gaussian has positive probability of success and positive probability of failure. Equality kR=2d=2 does not suffice for a universal almost-sure result. The sampled map is nevertheless immersive almost surely: a zero derivative requires b=−2tc and a=t²c, hence Δ=0, a Gaussian-null algebraic surface. For the compact interval K=[−1,1], failure still has positive probability: choose f=(−1/4,0,1), with collisions ±1/2 that persist under small input perturbation. The good event Δ<0 remains open and positive probability.

Quantifier warning: this 2×3 example concerns UNIFORM injectivity over every parameter. For any fixed t*, failure to identify that t* imposes a hyperplane a+t*b+(t*)²c=0 and has probability zero. Therefore this example alone is not a counterexample to a fixed-truth zero-one theorem.

## Separate fixed-truth nonlinear zero-one failure

Take M=(−1,1), H=R2, Y=R, F(t)=[t,t²], and fix truth t*=0. For f=(a,b), the response is at+bt². A distinct parameter s has the same response as0 exactly when s=−a/b lies in (−1,1)\{0}. Except for Gaussian-null coordinate lines this means |a|<|b|. Under independent standard real Gaussian a,b this has probability1/2 by exchange symmetry. Thus fixed-truth identification succeeds with probability1/2 and fails with probability1/2. Both possibilities occur on open sets. This refutes an unrestricted nonlinear extension of the fixed-coefficient zero-one claim, while leaving the original linearly parameterized dictionary theorem intact. Uniform injectivity also has mixed probability: |a|>2|b| gives a strictly monotone map on (−1,1), while the preceding failure set is globally noninjective.

The same scalar quadratic family shows strictness of the tangent bound. Every nonzero tangent operator v[1,2t] has rank1, but at kT=1=2d−1 the sampled derivative a+2bt vanishes inside M whenever |a|<2|b|, a positive-probability open event. Thus equality cannot replace the strict tangent inequality in a general theorem.

## Independent PDE rank extension check

Harrach1808.04737v3 Lemmas2.7–2.8 supply localized potentials and a definite-sign visible region for nonzero piecewise-analytic contrast κ. For any finite-codimension current subspace of codimension N, pick N+1 disjoint closed balls strictly inside one small ball in the visible open region outside the shield D2. For n≥2, removing the other interior disjoint balls does not prevent access from Σ: detour the existing path in the punctured small ball. Apply ordinary localized potentials targeting each ball and suppressing the shield plus all other balls, then normalize target energy to1. The cross-target gradient columns have norms≤ε; their combined norm is≤sqrt(N+1)ε, so the larger bound(N+1)ε is safe. Choose unit coefficients killing the N constraints. Target-union gradient norm is≥1−(N+1)ε and shield gradient norm≤sqrt(N+1)ε. Thus the κ-weighted energy is≥c(1−(N+1)ε)²−||κ||∞(N+1)ε²>0 for ε small. This contradicts vanishing of a derivative quadratic form on a finite-codimension kernel, proving infinite derivative rank. Monotonicity with the correctly chosen background yields the same contradiction for each nonzero secant operator. This check depends on the exact LP and visible-sign hypotheses; it does not prove them for broader coefficients.

Crucial regularity boundary: Harrach Definition2.1 warns that sums of arbitrary piecewise-analytic functions are not known automatically to remain piecewise analytic. Definition2.2 deliberately assumes the ENTIRE finite-dimensional span consists of piecewise-analytic functions. Use a fixed admissible finite-dimensional V (e.g. fixed regular partition with bounded-degree polynomials), so all secants and tangents satisfy the LP contrast hypotheses. Merely stating that each individual model member is piecewise analytic is insufficient.

## Downstream use and next falsifiable step

There is a qualitative finite-detector corollary after selecting a good input. Suppose the parameter manifold is embedded in finite-dimensional V, K is compact, Y is a Hilbert space, and P_n are finite-dimensional orthogonal projections converging strongly to Id. For a fixed injective immersion E, the normalized secant set {(E(a)−E(b))/||a−b||_V:a,b∈K,a≠b} has compact closure: away from the diagonal use compactness; along the diagonal pass to normalized source secants in the compact unit sphere of V and use continuous derivatives in local charts. Its norms are bounded below by the compact inverse Lipschitz constant c>0. Strong convergence is uniform on this compact closure, so some finite n gives ||(Id−P_n)z||≤c/2 on every normalized secant. Hence P_nE|K retains a lower Lipschitz bound c/2. This n depends on the Gaussian realization and model; no count or conditioning probability is certified. This corollary is an existence result, not a sensor-design algorithm.

If the independently checked infinite-rank PDE lemma applies on a fixed finite-dimensional C2 conductivity model in such V, the theorem gives one Gaussian applied boundary current with the whole observed voltage on Σ, simultaneously identifying every model conductivity and yielding compact-set inverse Lipschitz recovery. This consumes a function-valued response; finite sensors/noise conditioning remain additional acquisition questions. The immediate falsifier is an admissible nonzero contrast/tangent with finite-rank local NtD secant/derivative, or a failure of the shield/access assumptions in the exact model class. A practical follow-up should certify a finite voltage sensor approximation on a compact model for a selected good current and quantify its probability of acceptable conditioning. No deterministic noise constant follows from the rank argument alone.
