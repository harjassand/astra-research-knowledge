## 2. Actual global repair supplies the C3 margin

Writing zeta=at independently of the slow physical variables gives the exact conjugated operator

    (a^2 E)^(-1)Delta[E U]|_(zeta=at)=L0 U+Aop U,
    L0=partial_zeta^2+1,
    Aop=-2(t/R)partial_zeta+(2/a)partial_t^slow partial_zeta
      -(2/a)(x/R) dot grad_slow+(1/a^2)Delta_slow-d_R,
    d_R=K^2/R^2+(n-1)/(aR)+K^2/(aR^3).               (T1)

This keeps the origin and curvature terms exactly. The fixed stripe inverse G_D of L0 is coercive because 2b<pi. Define U0=cos(zeta), U_(m+1)=-G_D Aop U_m for m=0,1,2. Every U1,U2,U3 has zero trace on the SAME stripe walls as W. The exact interior telescope is

    (L0+Aop)(U0+U1+U2+U3)=Aop U3.

The coefficient hierarchy has one eta gain per inverse step. Specifically, -2(t/R)partial_zeta and (2/a)partial_t^slow partial_zeta each cost one eta, since 1/(aL)=eta. The other slow terms gain at least eta^2; 1/(aR)=eta^2, transverse x_i/R=O(eta), and the origin coefficient is smaller eventually. Thus

    U_m=O(eta^m), Aop U3=O(eta^4),
    partial_r^j partial_(t,z)^alpha U_m
          =O(eta^m R_j^(-j)L^(-|alpha|)),              (T2)

for the finite derivatives used here. Fast stripe derivatives also have fixed bounds. G_D commutes with slow differentiation. This reconstruction corrects the earlier prose saying that *every* slow-differentiation term gained eta^2: the fast/slow t cross term gains only eta. The actual recurrence and its eta^4 remainder remain valid; the author corrected the wording.

Let w_app=E(U1+U2+U3). On a bounded stripe segment in the 3B cube, e=W-w_app has regular interior forcing O(a^2 eta^4 E), zero stripe trace and artificial radial/z face data O(eta E). The positive stripe barrier

    H0=E(x_j)e^(-a(r-r_j))cos(pi h/(4b)),
    h=zeta-pi/2,

has -Delta H0=a^2[(pi/(4b))^2-1]H0 and is bounded below by H0's exponential factor times 1/sqrt(2). Multiplying it by exp(-a d_face) preserves strict supersolution signs because (pi/(4b))^2>4. Weak comparison on each bounded stripe segment gives, in the smaller 2B cube,

    |W-w_app|<=C E[eta^4+eta exp(-B/eta)].             (T3)

Artificial data from outside the core have been paid for; W has not been replaced by an independently solved local repair. Both zero extensions vanish outside the actual stripes. There is no derivative of global W in (T3).

Differentiating the fixed local kernel up to order three yields

    |D^m(F-F_app)|<=C_m a^m E[eta^4+eta exp(-B/eta)],
    m<=3.                                            (T4)

Reflection in each inactive z coordinate gives a stronger exponential estimate for the odd part. The source and stripe geometry are exactly even there; the reflected difference of W is harmonic with zero stripe trace and O(eta E) face data. The same face barriers and fixed kernel show that F_z and F_rz on z=0 are exponentially small after polynomial derivative costs. This is derived from actual global W, without assuming that the rest of its cover is symmetric.

## 3. The regular neck and the full first-order collar coefficient

On a fixed phase interval rho<=theta<=pi-rho, F_t=-aE sin(theta)[1+O_rho(eta)]. Therefore phase is a regular parameter for the actual gradient curves even through F_r=0. The equations are

    dr/dtheta=F_r/(aF_t), dz/dtheta=F_z/(aF_t).

The zeroth radial solution is r=r_H+log(sin theta)/a. Smooth normalized coefficients of the parametrix have soft r derivatives, while (T4) bounds the nonsoft actual error. Differentiating the finite-interval integral equation consequently gives

    h_+-r_H=h_--r_H=log(sin rho)/a+O_rho(eta/a),
    h_+'=h_-'=1+O_rho(eta/(aR_j))+O_rho(eta^4),        (T5)

where h_+ and h_- are the radial gate heights at theta=rho and pi-rho. The z gate data and their H derivatives are exponential. The equalities in (T5) mean separate estimates, not exactly equal actual heights.

The first stripe correction is (t/R)p+d_R r_D, with

    L0p=-2sin(theta), p=0 at the stripe endpoints,
    p=b tan(b)cos(h)-h sin(h)>=0.

Zero extension retains both endpoint measures. After the fixed tilted radial smoothing define P_sigma=S_sigma p. The first residual is exactly

    R_(1,sigma)=2sin(theta)+L0P_sigma.

The raw first averaging correction is a multiple of sin(theta), annihilated by L0; it cannot alter this residual. Since L0(csc theta)=2csc^3(theta) and P_sigma has compact support between the phase gates, distributional integration by parts gives

    B_sigma=2pi+2 integral_0^pi P_sigma/sin^3(theta)dtheta,
    2pi<B_sigma<3pi,
    B_(sigma,rho)=B_sigma-4rho.                       (T6)

For completeness, the excess is at most

    4(b+sigma)e^sigma b tan(b)/cos^3(b+sigma)<0.006.

The oriented neck runs from theta=pi-rho to rho. Its exact cocycle therefore has

    C_neck=-B_(sigma,rho)t0/r_H+O_rho(eta^2),
    C_neck'=B_(sigma,rho)t0/r_H^2+O_rho(a eta^4)
                                           +polynomial*exp(-c/eta). (T7)

The derivative bound is substantive: the order>=2 parametrix coefficients have r derivative O(eta^2/R_j)=O(a eta^4), and (T4) bounds the derivative of the normalized actual-error integrand by the same order. The phase map (T5) introduces no unstable factor. No long-excursion remainder is differentiated by assertion.

## 4. Exact tail coefficients and soft nominal-crest differentiation

In the two safe peak tails every mollifying ball misses the collar, so W contributes EXACTLY ZERO. Write the actual raw smoothing as

    G/E=C(r,t,z)cos(at)+D(r,t,z)sin(at).

C,D are the exact compact-kernel integrals in the author note, with no fast physical t dependence. Introduce a virtual nominal peak t_c by replacing at by a(t-t_c), while retaining the SAME C,D,E. At the two actual peaks this is G up to its overall sign, which cancels in the drift and cocycle ratios.

Let theta=a(t-t_c), mu_c=t_c/r, delta=1/(ar), q_K=K^2/r^2. At z=0 the exact normalized kernel exponent is

    -(1/delta)[sqrt(1+q_K+(mu_c+delta theta)^2
       -2sigma delta[u1+(mu_c+delta theta)u2]
       +sigma^2 delta^2|u|^2)
       -sqrt(1+q_K+(mu_c+delta theta)^2)].              (T8)

The apparent division by delta is a smooth integral difference quotient at delta=0. On the fixed compact kernel support the coefficients, normalized derivatives and quotients are uniformly smooth in these variables. At fixed relative phase,

    partial_(t_c)=r^(-1)partial_(mu_c).

There is NO factor a from differentiating the carrier phase. This is the load-bearing acquisition boundary: neighboring actual peaks are compared through the nominal-peak family, not by subtracting unrelated pointwise remainders.

Set h_sigma=sigma integral psi(u)e^(sigma u1)u2 sin(sigma u2)du and d=n-1. Reflection gives C even in the soft physical t variable and D odd. Expanding the smooth exact coefficients yields

    C=1+O(eta^2), D=h_sigma (t/r)+O_soft(eta^3),
    Delta G/(a^2 E)=2(t/r)sin(at)
      +[(-d+2h_sigma)delta-2h_sigma(t/r)^2-q_K]cos(at)
      +O_soft(eta^3).                                (T9)

These coefficients can be derived without differentiating a truncated amplitude. Convolve the exact raw residual 2(t/R)sin(at)-d_R cos(at). The micro shift of t/R contributes +2h_sigma delta cos(at); its tilt correction contributes -2h_sigma(t/r)^2 cos(at). Radial harmonic averaging fixes the leading cosine and sine multipliers at one. All remainder derivatives are soft at fixed relative phase. Here q_K=o(eta^3) for the fixed origin and subpower a; its common leading contribution is retained explicitly.

The instantaneous crest root has

    theta_crit=-(1-h_sigma)mu_c+e_theta,
    |partial_r^j partial_(t_c)^l e_theta|
       <=C eta^3 R_j^(-j)L^(-l), j<=2,l<=1.           (T10)

To see the derivative as well as the value, write the normalized G_t coefficients at fixed phase: its cosine coefficient is -(1-h_sigma)(t/r)+O_soft(eta^3), and its sine coefficient is -1+O_soft(eta^2). The implicit root is smooth and odd under (mu_c,theta)->(-mu_c,-theta). Its residual monomials have weighted order at least three (mu_c^3, mu_c delta and smaller origin products). Applying r^(-1)partial_mu_c to those terms gives exactly the bound in (T10).

The invariant crest is the backward solution from the instantaneous root at r_*=r_j+L. If w=theta_c-theta_crit, its exact equation and terminal data have the form

    w'=q(r,w,t_c)w-theta_crit', w(r_*)=0,
    q+w q_w>=c a.

The backward integral equation gives |w|<=C eta/(aR_j)=C eta^3. Differentiation in t_c has forcing q_tc w-partial_tc theta_crit', with

    |q_tc|<=C a/L, |partial_tc theta_crit'|<=C/R_j^2,
    |forcing|<=C eta/(R_j L).

Thus stability gives

    |partial_tc w|<=C eta^3/L.                        (T11)

A soft r derivative is also available on the working interval: differentiating the w equation gives stable coefficient q+wq_w and forcing q_r w-theta_crit''. This is O(eta/R_j^2), with terminal derivative O(eta/R_j). A fixed macro gap from r_* suppresses that terminal derivative exponentially, so |w_r|<=C eta^3/R_j there. This ensures that the crest coefficients used at unequal radial gates have ordinary soft r control as well.

Evaluate g_c=-Delta G/G_r on this invariant crest. In (T9), the leading sin phase becomes -(1-h_sigma)mu_c. Therefore its quadratic contribution is

    -2(1-h_sigma)mu_c^2-2h_sigma mu_c^2=-2mu_c^2.

The kernel dependence cancels. The invariant-crest change costs O(a eta |w|)=O(a eta^4), with t_c derivative O(a eta^4/L) by (T11). Consequently

    g_c=M(r)-2a t_c^2/r^2+R_c(r,t_c),
    M(r)=(-d+2h_sigma)/r-aK^2/r^2,
    |R_c|<=C a eta^3, |partial_tc R_c|<=C a eta^3/L.  (T12)

For the actual peaks t_-=t0+pi/(2a) and t_+=t0-pi/(2a), the mean value theorem and (T12) now give

    g_-(r)-g_+(r)=-4pi t0/r^2+O(a eta^4).             (T13)

This is a derivative theorem, not an inference that a small remainder at each peak has a small difference. It is the missing term that reverses the earlier collar-only sign.

## 5. Uniform branch-defect derivative

The exact safe-tail radial equation is y'=b(r,y)=G_t/G_r. Near either invariant crest G_r has fixed nonzero sign, and b_y>=c a uniformly in a small fixed peak phase tube. For v=a(y-y_c(r)), subtract the exact invariant-crest equation to obtain

    v'=a beta(r,v)v,
    beta+v beta_v=b_y/a>=c,
    |beta_r|<=C eta/R_j.                              (T14)

The constants hold uniformly for 0<rho<=rho0 and |v|<=2rho+O(eta). The ratio coefficients are smooth on this fixed tube and their r derivatives are soft; no denominator degenerates as rho decreases. The leading eta=0 beta is tan(v)/v, independent of r, making the eta/R_j perturbation bound explicit.

With u=a(h-r) the pullback V satisfies V_u=-beta(h-u/a,V)V and initial datum a(y_gate-y_c(h)). Since y_gate is a fixed phase location, its h derivative is -a y_c'(h)=O(eta/R_j). Differentiating this stable equation gives

    |V|<=C(rho+eta)e^(-cu),
    |V_h|<=C[eta/R_j+(rho+eta)eta u/R_j]e^(-cu).       (T15)

Let f_c(r,v)=-Delta G/G_r on y=y_c(r)+v/a and Phi_c=f_c(r,v)-f_c(r,0). The exact tail coefficients give the uniform difference bounds

    |Phi_c|<=C a eta |v|, |Phi_v|<=C a eta,
    |Phi_r|_(v fixed)<=C a eta/R_j |v|.                (T16)

These follow by integrating f_v, whose leading term is 2a(t/r)sec^2(theta_c+v), and its soft r derivative. The normalized tail numerator is O(eta) on the fixed tube, while the denominator is bounded away from zero. Crucially Phi and Phi_r vanish at v=0; subtracting unrelated Taylor-error bounds would not give (T16).

For D_c(h)=integral_(r_-)^h Phi_c(r,v(r;h))dr, the exact pullback formula is

    D_c(h)=(1/a)integral_0^(a(h-r_-))
                    Phi_c(h-u/a,V(u;h))du.

Leibniz differentiation produces the endpoint term Phi_c(r_-,V_end) plus

    (1/a)integral[Phi_r+Phi_v V_h]du.

Apply (T15)-(T16) and integrate e^(-cu) and u e^(-cu). This gives

    |D_c'(h)|<=C rho eta/R_j+C eta^2/R_j
                          +C a eta e^(-c a(h-r_-)).   (T17)

The coefficient C of rho is genuinely uniform as rho decreases in the same fixed safe tube. This permits choosing rho ONCE before j grows. Inactive z gate data and H derivatives are exponential; the exact raw tail is even in z, its z drift is O(z/R_j), and the y-z cross terms vanish at z=0. Backward evolution over O(L) keeps these effects polynomial times the same exponential, below the differentiated margin.

## 6. Unequal gates, actual endpoint derivatives and the final sign

The oriented negative branch runs from r_- to h_-(H); the positive branch runs from h_+(H) back to r_-. Therefore

    C_tail=integral_(r_-)^(h_-) g_-dr
      -integral_(r_-)^(h_+) g_+dr+D_-(h_-)-D_+(h_+)
      +exponential error.

Differentiation is at the actual unequal gates. Write h_+-r_H=h_--r_H=log(sin rho)/a+O_rho(eta/a), and retain their separate derivatives from (T5). The common crest term M has size O(a eta^2) and derivative O(a eta^2/R_j). Its uncancelled pieces have the bounds

    |M(h_-)-M(h_+)|<=C_rho a eta^5,
    |M(h_+)(h_-'-h_+')|<=C_rho(a eta^5+a eta^6).

The same unequal-height correction to the quadratic crest parts is O_rho(a eta^5). The common log(sin rho)/a shift changes their neighboring difference by O_rho(a eta^5). The remainder comparison in (T12) costs O(a eta^4), already below the a eta^3 margin. Thus no surviving common endpoint term has the decisive order. Together with (T13),(T17),

    C_tail'=-4pi t0/r_H^2+O(rho eta/R_j)
                +O_rho(a eta^4)+polynomial*exp(-c/eta). (T18)

The endpoints at the fixed section are also stable. The scalar backward flow derivative is exp[-integral_(r_-)^h b_y dr]. Differentiation with respect to radial start time at fixed y_gate is exactly -D_y(flow) b(h,y_gate). Multiplying by h'(H) from (T5) retains exponential smallness. Gate z data and their H derivatives contribute only polynomial times exponentials. Since h-r_->=L/2 eventually,

    |p_j'(H)|+|q_j'(H)|<=C exp(-c/eta_j).              (T19)

The maps exist on an ordinary neighborhood because the neck F_t and tail F_r stay nonzero there. At z0=0 the H column and t_return/z0 cross column are exponential; the z_return/z0 block is bounded. Off-axis H variation can contain O(|z0|/R_j), so exponential contraction in every transverse direction is NOT asserted. This is the corrected precise neighborhood statement. For the actual z0=0 family, (T19) is sufficient.

Combine (T7) and (T18):

    C_actual'=(B_sigma-4rho-4pi)t0/r_H^2
      +O(rho eta/R_j)+O_rho(a eta^4)
      +polynomial*exp(-c/eta).                        (T20)

By (T6), B_sigma-4pi<-pi. Choose one rho>0 small enough to absorb the uniform C rho error from (T17); then hold rho fixed and let j grow. Since t0/L->1, r_H/R_j->1 and a eta^4/(a eta^3)=eta->0,

    C_actual'(H)<=-c L/R_j^2                          (T21)

uniformly for 0<=H<=L/2. There is no interchange of a shrinking rho with the j limit.

For any exterior C1 h solving the transport equation, integration along each regular actual gradient curve gives C_actual=h(q_j)-h(p_j). Differentiating this exact identity and using (T19)-(T21) forces

    max(|grad h(p_j)|,|grad h(q_j)|)
       >=c (L_j/R_j^2)exp(c/eta_j)->infinity.

The curves and endpoints escape to infinity. Hence bounded exterior gradient is impossible, and in particular a gradient tending to zero is impossible. This conclusion does not use the original operator's spectral index.
