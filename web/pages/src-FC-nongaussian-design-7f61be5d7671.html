1. The scientific decision selected
I narrowed the target to a local defect-charging or trap-design threshold in a correlated host:
How much independently specified reference cost can be offset by lowering a defect level or changing its on-site interaction, after allowing the surrounding interacting system to re-equilibrate?

For a spinful defect, this includes interventions such as
\[
V=\delta\epsilon(n_\uparrow+n_\downarrow)
+\delta U\,n_\uparrow n_\downarrow.
\]
The target is the full-system free-energy change, not the energy of an isolated defect. The environment may respond nonlinearly, rearrange its charge and correlations, and remain computationally inaccessible.
I did not retain CO/Cu(111) as a claimed application. FEMION already combines local auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo with global screening and addresses CO site preference and hydrogen desorption on that surface. I have not established that changing those adsorption geometries satisfies the intervention assumptions proved below. arXiv
The selection also changes the comparison baseline. Correlated-sampling AFQMC already exploits energy-difference cancellation; avoiding two independently calculated total energies is not itself an invention. arXiv
The change in information acquisition
The earlier counterexample used observations of the uncoupled environment. Its one-particle spectrum could conceal interactions that became decisive after coupling.
Here the observation is made in the actual coupled reference system. It measures a local variable after the surrounding system has already responded to the defect.
That distinction is essential. The theorem does not say that a few bare-bath correlators determine arbitrary chemistry. It identifies a different observation that can suffice for a restricted but nontrivial family of decisions.
2. Exact model and information required
Fix one global conserved sector, such as specified total particle numbers \(N_\uparrow,N_\downarrow\). All traces below remain in that same sector.
Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\) be projectors onto a finite local label—for example, the four occupation configurations of a spinful defect. They commute with the conserved charges and sum to the identity within the chosen sector.
For the arbitrary interacting reference Hamiltonian \(H\), define
\[
D=\sum_iP_iHP_i,\qquad T=H-D.
\]
Here \(D\) contains everything that preserves the label. It can contain the entire strongly interacting environment and strong label-dependent interactions with it. Only \(T\) changes the label.
The candidate designs are
\[
H(v)=H+\sum_i v_iP_i.
\]
At known inverse temperature \(\beta\), the required output is
\[
\Delta F(v)=F(H(v))-F(H),
\qquad
F(H)=-\beta^{-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}.
\]
Scalar formation or implementation costs must be included in the \(v_i\). They are not supplied by the theorem.
The principal measurement is
\[
\boxed{
p_i=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(P_i\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z_H}\right).
}
\]
This is a local histogram, not a reconstructed quantum state. The additional requirement is a certified number \(C\ge1\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{1\le \frac{Z_H}{Z_D}\le C.}
\]
The next section constructs \(C\) without calculating either partition function.
3. The missing bound is local quantum mixing—not bath Gaussianity
From calibrated microscopic interactions
Suppose
\[
g_{ij}\ge\|P_iTP_j\|,\qquad g_{ii}=0,
\]
with \(g\) symmetric and nonnegative. Then
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{norm}}=\max_i[e^{\beta g}]_{ii}
}
\]
is valid.
For a binary occupation label,
\[
\boxed{
C_{\mathrm{norm}}=\cosh(\beta\kappa),
\qquad
\kappa\ge\|P_0HP_1\|.
}
\]
Thus, when \(\beta\kappa\) is small,
\[
1-\frac1C
=\frac{(\beta\kappa)^2}{2}
+O((\beta\kappa)^4).
\]
This does not require weak environment interactions. It restricts only the processes that change the measured label.
For a local fermion coupled by
\[
\sum_j(t_jd^\dagger c_j+\mathrm{h.c.}),
\]
a valid full-Fock-space bound is
\[
\kappa=\sqrt{\sum_j|t_j|^2}.
\]
Restricting to a conserved sector cannot increase that norm.
A practical qualification matters: a narrow measured linewidth is not automatically a bound on this total coupling norm. Unresolved high-energy modes can make the latter much larger.
A stronger option: measure boundary kinetic energy
Let
\[
\tau=\langle T\rangle_H.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\log\frac{Z_H}{Z_D}\le-\beta\tau,
\qquad \tau\le0.
}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
C=
\exp\!\left[
\min\{\log C_{\mathrm{norm}},-\beta\tau\}
\right]
}
\]
is valid. With uncertain observations, use a certified lower bound on \(\tau\).
This follows directly from the Gibbs variational principle:
\[
F(D)
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_HD)-\beta^{-1}S(\rho_H)
=
F(H)-\tau.
\]
The significance is operational: a measured local energetic quantity can replace a pessimistic worst-case norm without reconstructing non-Gaussian environmental response.
For finite-range hopping, \(T\) involves only nearby bonds. In a platform permitting calibrated two-mode rotations, a hopping quadrature can be converted into a number-difference measurement. The required controls, measurement errors and preparation costs must still be established for the chosen platform.
4. Main theorem: turn the histogram into a two-sided design certificate
Set
\[
u_i=e^{-\beta v_i},
\qquad
A(v)=\sum_i p_i u_i.
\]
Define
\[
L(v)=
\min_{\substack{0\le w_i\le p_i\\
                 \sum_i w_i=1/C}}
\sum_i w_i u_i.
\]
Then, for an arbitrary interacting reference system,
\[
\boxed{
-\frac1\beta\log A(v)
\;\le\;
\Delta F(v)
\;\le\;
-\frac1\beta\log L(v).
}
\]
The interval can also be intersected with
\[
[\min_i v_i,\max_i v_i].
\]
What the operation actually computes
To obtain \(L(v)\), sort the \(u_i\) and retain probability mass \(1/C\) in the least favorably reweighted bins.
Equivalently:
Remove at most \(\eta=1-1/C\) of the measured probability mass from the configurations that would most strongly favor the proposed design.

This takes \(O(k\log k)\) comparisons and \(O(k)\) subsequent arithmetic. It requires no interacting reference solver or target-state preparation once the physical observations and calibration are available.
The familiar expression \(-\beta^{-1}\log A\) is generally not an exact quantum free-energy formula. It is one side of the interval. The probability-removal operation supplies the other.
Proof
Let
\[
z_i=\operatorname{Tr}_{P_i}e^{-\beta P_iHP_i},
\qquad
w_i^{\mathrm{true}}=\frac{z_i}{Z_H}.
\]
Scalar Jensen’s inequality, applied in an eigenbasis of each compressed block, gives
\[
z_i\le\operatorname{Tr}(P_i e^{-\beta H}).
\]
Therefore
\[
0\le w_i^{\mathrm{true}}\le p_i,
\qquad
\sum_iw_i^{\mathrm{true}}=\frac{Z_D}{Z_H}\ge\frac1C.
\]
Applying the same block-trace inequality to the target Hamiltonian yields
\[
\frac{Z_{H(v)}}{Z_H}
\ge
\sum_iw_i^{\mathrm{true}}e^{-\beta v_i}
\ge L(v).
\]
Golden–Thompson, applied at the actual reference \(H\), gives
\[
\frac{Z_{H(v)}}{Z_H}
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_H e^{-\beta V})
=
A(v).
\]
Taking negative logarithms proves the result. Golden–Thompson is an established ingredient, not a new inequality claimed here. arXiv
Why this is not a finite-response approximation
Suppose every relevant measured probability satisfies
\[
p_i\ge p_*> \eta.
\]
Then, simultaneously for every vector of local shifts \(v\),
\[
\boxed{
\text{interval width}
\le
\frac1\beta
\log\frac1{1-\eta/p_*}.
}
\]
The bound does not grow with the magnitude of the target shifts.
Indeed,
\[
A(v)\ge p_*\,\max_i u_i,
\]
while removal of mass \(\eta\) loses at most \(\eta\max_i u_i\). Hence
\[
L(v)\ge(1-\eta/p_*)A(v).
\]
This is the strongest consequence: one sufficiently informative reference histogram can certify an entire family of nonperturbatively large local interventions. It does not expand the bath response to second, fourth or any finite order.
The limitation is visible in the data. Initially rare configurations and a large coherence budget can make the result uninformative. More samples reduce statistical error; they do not automatically reduce that physical uncertainty.
5. A sharp supporting comparison for interacting blocks
I also derived a stronger block-partition theorem.
Let
\[
c_i=\operatorname{Tr}(P_i e^{-\beta H}),
\]
and form the \(k\times k\) scalar matrix
\[
M_{ii}=\log z_i,\qquad M_{ij}=\beta g_{ij}\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
z_i\le c_i\le[e^M]_{ii},
\qquad
\sum_i z_i\le Z_H\le\operatorname{Tr}e^M.
}
\]
The upper bound is attained, for every positive \(z_i\) and nonnegative symmetric \(g\), by one-dimensional blocks with energies
\[
-\beta^{-1}\log z_i
\]
and off-diagonal couplings \(-g_{ij}\). The lower bound is attained by zero off-diagonal coupling when \(g\) specifies upper bounds.
Thus this is a complete worst-case partition comparison for that block-information contract.
The constructive proof expands the quantum exponential into closed sequences of block transitions. Schatten Hölder bounds every term by the product of its transition norms and block partition weights. Those dominating terms sum exactly to the scalar exponential \(e^M\).
No environment dimension appears in the bound. However, this sharpness does not make the final histogram certificate minimax sharp; the histogram supplies less information than exact block partition weights.
Norm-compression inequalities have substantial prior literature. I have not established historical priority for this characterization or for its combination with the acquisition operation. arXiv

I tested the operation on an interacting, number-conserving fermionic control—not a quadratic reservoir.
The model is an eight-site, open \(2\times4\) Hubbard rectangle with

Its conserved-sector Hilbert space has dimension \(3{,}136\).
The measured label is the up-spin occupation of one defect site. Its two incident up-spin hoppings are \(0.025t\); the remaining hopping amplitudes are \(t\). The reference local potential is \(-2t\).

7. Acquisition, precision and the costs that remain
What must actually be acquired
The basic physical inputs are the temperature, local label readout, candidate intervention coefficients, and calibrated label-changing interactions. A kinetic-energy measurement is optional.
For \(k\) labels, independent samples give simultaneous per-bin error at most \(a\) with failure probability at most \(\alpha\) when
\[
N\ge\frac{\log(2k/\alpha)}{2a^2}.
\]
With a confidence region \(\mathcal P\) for the histogram, the rigorous statistical operation becomes
\[
A^+(v)=\max_{p\in\mathcal P}p\cdot u,
\]
\[
L^-(v)=
\min_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P,\;0\le w\le p\\
                 \sum_iw_i\ge1/C}}
w\cdot u.
\]
These are linear programs with \(k\) and \(2k\) variables. A simultaneous histogram confidence region validates all subsequent choices of \(v\).
Local thermodynamic readout is experimentally established—for example, few-electron entropy measurements using a charge-sensing mesoscopic circuit. That is evidence for the measurement category, not evidence that this complete calibrated protocol has been demonstrated for an interacting defect substrate. arXiv
The important unresolved physical cost: equilibrium
The experiment cost includes repeated preparation or equilibration, readout, calibration, and any kinetic-energy measurements.
No universal inexpensive equilibration procedure is established here. A stationary-looking local histogram does not prove that a hidden metastable sector has been sampled. Correlated frames cannot be counted as independent observations without a justified dependence bound.
Weakening tunnelling may improve \(C\) while making charge exchange slower. This trade-off must be measured in a proposed implementation; it is not removed by the theorem.
Classical and numerical costs
After acquisition, the exact-histogram calculation uses \(O(k\log k)\) sorting work and \(O(k)\) weight arithmetic. Constructing the general norm budget requires a small \(k\times k\) matrix exponential. There is no full many-body matrix in this stage.
That does not make all parameters harmless. The label count can itself grow exponentially with the number of resolved local degrees of freedom. Large \(\beta\|g\|\) can make the certificate useless. General numerical implementations require outward rounding of coefficients and optimization errors; only the supplied special-case sign certificate was checked with exact rational arithmetic.
Model and reference errors
The reference histogram incorporates the actual surrounding interactions. Those interactions need not be reconstructed merely because they are non-Gaussian.
But the intervention model must be correct. If the actual target is
\[
H+V(v)+R_v,\qquad\|R_v\|\le\epsilon_v,
\]
the free-energy interval must be expanded by \(\epsilon_v\) on both sides.
A geometry change, modified hopping network, unaccounted charge sector or unknown chemical reference cannot be silently represented as \(V(v)\).
All reported results are finite-temperature comparisons in a fixed ensemble. They do not automatically become ground-energy statements as \(\beta\to\infty\).
8. Comparison with the strongest relevant approaches
The result should not be interpreted as a new general solution of quantum impurity equilibrium.
Two preprints submitted on 1 October 2026 give polynomial-time classical equilibrium algorithms for constant-size interacting impurities in noninteracting fermion baths; the second was revised on 5 October 2026. Those results already change the theoretical baseline. arXiv
Here the environment can be interacting, but the cost is paid through a different input contract: physical equilibrium observations, a specified local intervention family, and an informative coherence budget. Without the physical observations, this is not a polynomial algorithm for a supplied interacting Hamiltonian.
Continuous-hybridization impurity methods also do not necessarily require finite-bath fitting. This continuation therefore does not count bath compression as its advantage. arXiv
Certified many-body bootstrap approaches provide model-based ground-energy bounds through positivity constraints. They address a different task from this finite-temperature, measurement-assisted comparison. No matched hard-instance advantage over those methods was demonstrated. arXiv
The narrower improvement established here is concrete: from one reference histogram and local mixing information, obtain a two-sided quantum design certificate that ordinary reference-state variational and Golden–Thompson bounds can leave indeterminate.
9. What information is genuinely necessary?
The histogram alone is insufficient.
At \(\beta=1\), consider
\[
H_J=J\sigma_x,
\qquad
V=\frac15I+\sigma_z.
\]
Every reference has the same occupation histogram,
\[
p=(1/2,1/2).
\]
But
\[
\Delta F_J
=
\frac15
-\log\cosh\sqrt{J^2+1}
+\log\cosh J
\]
is negative at \(J=0\) and positive at \(J=10\).
Thus even perfect local populations can give opposite design preferences when label-changing quantum dynamics are uncontrolled. The kinetic or microscopic mixing certificate is substantive information, not bookkeeping.
Conversely, the positive theorem establishes that a complete environmental influence functional is unnecessary for the specified family. What suffices is:
the local label distribution in the loaded equilibrium system, a bound on how much quantum mixing can distort its reweighting, and a calibrated description of the proposed intervention.
That is the change from the previous quadratic-bath programme. Non-Gaussian response need not be reconstructed or truncated; it can remain inside the physically sampled reference system.
This removes the non-Gaussian reconstruction requirement within an explicit, testable regime. It does not remove equilibrium preparation, arbitrary chemical-model acquisition, or strong-coherence limitations. Establishing those conditions for a consequential real defect—and demonstrating an advantage under matched inputs and accuracy—remains necessary before calling this a transformative materials capability.
