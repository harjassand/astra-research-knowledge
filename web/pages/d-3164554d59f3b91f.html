# Source020: dynamical boundary of the quartic k=2 density claim

**Leaf:** geometry_number / source020 dynamics attack  
**Checked:** 2026-10-08  
**Status:** Conditional transfer proved from the stated arithmetic theorem. Exact density is sufficient in the squarefree-at-zero case, but is not necessary for the dynamical transfer. The recurrence property alone does not validate the exact density claim. A zero-density APMR example is proved below. The arithmetic theorem remains conditional here, not independently certified.

## Exact interfaces

For a fixed irreducible quartic $f\in\mathbb Z[x]$, write
\[
 \rho_f(q)=\#\{a\pmod q:f(a)\equiv0\pmod q\},\qquad
 R_f=\{n\ge1:f(n)\text{ is squarefree}\}.
\]
The squarefree convention is that no prime square divides the value. Source020's Theorem 1.1 has range irreducible fixed $f$ of degree $4\le d\le8$, $k=d-2$, and local admissibility $\rho_f(p^k)<p^k$ for every prime. It claims
\[
 |\{n\le X:f(n)\text{ is }k\text{-power-free}\}|=c_{f,k}X+o_f(X),\quad
 c_{f,k}=\prod_p(1-\rho_f(p^k)/p^k)>0.
\]
For a quartic, $k=2$. The claim is pointwise in the fixed polynomial; no uniformity in $f$ is stated.

BKLPR's exact Definition 1.2 defines “averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence” by requiring, for every invertible probability-preserving system $(X,\mathcal B,\mu,T)$, every measurable $B$ with $\mu(B)>0$, every $\ell\ge1$, and all $P_i\in\mathbb Q[t]$ with $P_i(\mathbb Z)\subset\mathbb Z$ and $P_i(0)=0$, that the limit
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|R\cap[1,N]|}\sum_{n=1}^N1_R(n)\,
 \mu\!\left(B\cap T^{-P_1(n)}B\cap\cdots\cap T^{-P_\ell(n)}B\right)
\tag{BKLPR-1.2}
\]
exists and is positive. The denominator is $|R\cap[1,N]|$, not $N$. BKLPR Theorem 1.4 has the narrower interface: if $R$ is rational and has $d(R)>0$, then it is an averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence iff it is divisible, meaning $d(R\cap u\mathbb N)>0$ for each $u\ge1$. Thus the theorem classifies only the positive-natural-density rational sets, while Definition 1.2 itself also admits zero-density sets.

## Conditional quartic transfer: iff $f(0)$ is squarefree

**Theorem.** Assume source020 Theorem 1.1 for every fixed irreducible quartic satisfying its local condition. For each such $f$,
\[
 R_f\text{ is an averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(0)\text{ is squarefree}.
\]

**Proof.** Write $c_f=\prod_p(1-\rho_f(p^2)/p^2)>0$. For $Y\ge2$, put
\[
 S_Y=\{n\ge1:p^2\nmid f(n)\text{ for every prime }p\le Y\},
 \qquad M_Y=\prod_{p\le Y}p^2.
\]
The set $S_Y$ is periodic modulo $M_Y$ and has density $c_Y=\prod_{p\le Y}(1-\rho_f(p^2)/p^2)$. Since $R_f\subset S_Y$, the exact density claim gives
\[
 d(S_Y\setminus R_f)=c_Y-c_f\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently $1_{R_f}$ is a Cesàro $L^1$ limit of periodic sequences, so $R_f$ is rational in the BKLPR sense; also $d(R_f)=c_f>0$.

Suppose that $f(0)$ is squarefree. Fix $u\ge1$ and set $g_u(t)=f(ut)$. Substitution by a nonzero rational linear map preserves irreducibility and degree, so $g_u$ is an irreducible quartic. It satisfies source020's local hypothesis:

* If $p\nmid u$, multiplication by $u$ permutes residues modulo $p^2$, so $\rho_{g_u}(p^2)=\rho_f(p^2)<p^2$.
* If $p\mid u$ and $p\nmid f(0)$, then $f(ut)\not\equiv0\pmod p$ for every $t$, so there are no roots modulo $p^2$.
* If $p\mid u$ and $p\mid f(0)$, squarefreeness gives $v_p(f(0))=1$. Modulo $p^2$, $f(ut)\equiv f(0)+ut f'(0)$ because all terms of degree at least two in $ut$ are divisible by $p^2$. If $v_p(u)\ge2$, this is the nonzero residue $f(0)\pmod{p^2}$, so there are no roots. If $v_p(u)=1$, dividing the congruence by $p$ gives a linear congruence in $t\pmod p$; it has at most one solution modulo $p$, hence at most $p<p^2$ roots modulo $p^2$.

Apply the family-wide source020 theorem to each $g_u$. It gives positive density $c_{g_u,2}$ for $R_{g_u}=\{m:f(um)\text{ is squarefree}\}$. As $R_f\cap u\mathbb N=uR_{g_u}$,
\[
 d(R_f\cap u\mathbb N)=c_{g_u,2}/u>0.
\]
Therefore $R_f$ is divisible, and BKLPR Theorem 1.4 gives the stated recurrence property.

Conversely, if $f(0)$ is not squarefree, choose $p$ with $p^2\mid f(0)$. For every $n\in p^2\mathbb N$, $f(n)\equiv f(0)\equiv0\pmod{p^2}$. Hence $R_f\cap p^2\mathbb N=\varnothing$, so $R_f$ is not divisible. Since the arithmetic theorem gives $R_f$ rational and positive-density, BKLPR Theorem 1.4 implies it cannot be an averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence. This proves the conditional equivalence. ∎

**Interface caveat.** An asymptotic for just one polynomial $f$ provides $d(R_f)>0$ and (through the sieve) rationality, but it does not establish all progression densities. The positive-divisibility proof applies the family theorem to every transformed polynomial $f(u\,\cdot)$. The family-wide quantifier in source020 is essential to this deduction.

## Positive density alone is not enough for source020's transfer

Take $f(x)=x^4+12$. It is irreducible by Eisenstein at 3. It is locally admissible: for $p\ge5$, $p^2\nmid f(0)=12$; for $p=3$, $9\nmid12$; for $p=2$, $4\nmid f(1)=13$. Thus $\rho_f(p^2)<p^2$ for every prime. The claimed source020 theorem supplies $d(R_f)=c_f>0$. But every even $n$ satisfies $n^4+12\equiv0\pmod4$, so $R_f\cap2\mathbb N=\varnothing$. This set fails divisibility, and by BKLPR Theorem 1.4 it is not an averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence. The exact obstruction is the fixed progression $2\mathbb N$; positivity of the global quartic density cannot replace progression positivity.

## Zero-density APMR set: the squares

Let $Q=\{m^2:m\ge1\}$. Its natural density is zero since $|Q\cap[1,N]|=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor/N\to0$. Nevertheless, $Q$ is an averaging set of polynomial multiple recurrence under the exact BKLPR Definition 1.2.

Fix an arbitrary system, $B$ with $\mu(B)>0$, and admissible polynomials $P_1,\ldots,P_\ell$. Put $M=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and $Q_i(m)=P_i(m^2)$. Each $Q_i\in\mathbb Q[m]$ is integer-valued on $\mathbb Z$ and has zero constant term. Since the squares are distinct,
\[
 \frac{1}{|Q\cap[1,N]|}\sum_{n=1}^N1_Q(n)
 \mu\!\left(B\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}T^{-P_i(n)}B\right)
 =\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M
 \mu\!\left(B\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}T^{-Q_i(m)}B\right).
\]
The right side has a limit by A. Leibman's polynomial multiple-average convergence theorem (Theorem 1, 2005): the averages of $\prod_{i=0}^{\ell}T^{Q_i(m)}1_B$ converge in $L^1$, using $Q_0=0$; integrating gives the correlation limit. The limit is strictly positive by Bergelson–Leibman's polynomial multiple recurrence theorem (Theorem A0, 1996), applied to the same $Q_i$ and the single transformation $T$ (take every commuting transformation there equal to $T$). BKLPR themselves state this combined existence-and-positivity fact as equation (1.1), and explicitly identify the convergence inputs and polynomial recurrence input. Since $M=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor\to\infty$, this proves that the limit in Definition 1.2 exists and is positive for every choice of system, event, and polynomials. So $Q$ is APMR despite $d(Q)=0$.

This is not an application of BKLPR Theorem 1.4: its hypothesis $d(R)>0$ fails. It shows that the full Definition 1.2 is not equivalent to the positive-density rational classification outside that theorem's stated range.

## Necessity and what recurrence cannot recover

The exact Euler product is **not necessary** for the recurrence transfer. The source020 application needs enough arithmetic to establish (i) rationality of $R_f$, (ii) positive density, and (iii) positive density on every fixed progression; BKLPR Theorem 1.4 then applies. A weaker sufficient package is periodic supersets $S_Y\supset R_f$ satisfying $\overline d(S_Y\setminus R_f)\to0$ (which gives rationality) together with the individual statements $d(R_f\cap u\mathbb N)>0$ for all $u$. This does not require identifying $d(R_f)$ with a particular Euler product. Alternatively, an independent proof of rationality and divisibility bypasses the Euler-product theorem entirely.

The exact natural-density identity has a specific arithmetic role in the source-derived proof: it evaluates the sieve approximation error as
\[
 d(S_Y\setminus R_f)=c_Y-c_f\to0,
\]
thereby proving that $1_{R_f}$ is RAP/rational. The APMR conclusion does not recover that identity or this sieve-tail estimate. The squares show that APMR does not even force positive natural density under Definition 1.2. Even among periodic divisible sets, $2\mathbb N$ and $3\mathbb N$ have different densities $1/2$ and $1/3$ and are both APMR. Thus the qualitative recurrence predicate cannot certify the exact quartic Euler factor. An explicit numerical value for the normalized average on the trivial system would encode $d(R)$ when it exists, but the APMR theorem only asserts positivity, not that value.

## Precise load-bearing original lemma

BKLPR Lemma 2.9 (v2, p. 13) states that if $R\subset\mathbb N$ is rational and $T_g$ is a totally ergodic nilrotation on a nilmanifold $X=G/\Gamma$, then for all $x\in X$ and $F\in C(X)$,
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N1_R(n)F(T_g^n x)
 =d(R)\int_XF\,d\mu_X.
\]
This is the decorrelation step in their proof of Theorem 2.6 (rational + divisible implies APMR). They replace a multiple-correlation sequence by a nilsequence (Theorem 2.7), pass to a component on which some power $T_g^u$ is totally ergodic, then apply Lemma 2.9 with $R/u=\{n:un\in R\}$. Divisibility makes $d(R/u)=u\,d(R\cap u\mathbb N)>0$; the unweighted positive average yields a positive Haar integral. The lemma supplies a positive $R/u$-weighted average. If this limit formula failed, their stated proof would lose its positivity step; that would invalidate this proof route, not automatically disprove Theorem 2.6. Lemma 2.9 is cited as the primary recurrence-proof dependency, not as a result of source020.

## Baseline, costs, falsifiers

**Baseline.** Without the quartic theorem, BKLPR Theorem 1.4 remains the exact positive-density rational interface; Definition 1.2 also permits sets outside its range, as $Q$ demonstrates. Source020 is an arithmetic route to the inputs for its positive-density application.

**Costs.** The finite sieve approximant has period $M_Y=\prod_{p\le Y}p^2$; a fully expanded period table has $M_Y$ entries, while factored local-root data avoids expansion. Source020's $o_f(X)$ is pointwise and gives no effective $Y(\varepsilon)$ or uniform estimate over $f$. For recurrence, positivity for each fixed $u$ suffices; no uniform lower bound over $u$ is used. The square-set proof has no arithmetic computation: it reindexes by $m\le\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and invokes the established polynomial-average convergence and recurrence theorems.

**Falsifiers.** The conditional quartic equivalence would fail if a locally admissible irreducible quartic with squarefree $f(0)$ had $d(R_f\cap u\mathbb N)=0$ for some $u$, assuming source020's theorem for $f(u\,\cdot)$. Source020's arithmetic premise is falsified by any admissible quartic whose actual squarefree-value density differs from its claimed Euler product. The squares claim would fail only if either the polynomial multiple-average convergence theorem or the polynomial multiple recurrence theorem failed for the composed integer-valued zero-constant polynomials $Q_i(m)=P_i(m^2)$; both are directly covered by the cited primary results. The Lemma 2.9 dependency is falsified by a counterexample to its displayed limit. No such counterexample was found.
