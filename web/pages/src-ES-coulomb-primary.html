WORKING DERIVATION — CONSERVATION-COMPATIBLE FINITE MEMORY
13 October 2026. Internally derived candidate; priority UNKNOWN.

Original target remains deterministic 3D point-Coulomb relaxation on the
collisional scale. The construction below does NOT derive that limit. It
addresses a concrete sub-obstruction: a positive finite-time resonance
replacement need not conserve energy. Sections 2-8 supply screening in a
radial diagnostic; Section 9 removes that supplied length for an exact
continuum Fourier calculation. Neither construction acquires screening
from particles. No molecular simulation advantage is claimed.

1. FAILURE OF POSITIVITY ALONE

Use Fourier measure dk (no (2pi)^-3 prefactor; this convention is local).
For u=v-v*, define the raw finite-time tensor

  B_T(u)=int dk k k^T |Vhat(k)|^2 F_T(k.u),
  F_T(omega)=(1-cos(T omega))/(pi T omega^2).

F_T is nonnegative, normalized, and is the transform of a triangular time
window. B_T is positive. But B_T(u)u is nonzero at finite T.
For Q_B(f)=div_v int B(v-v*)[f* grad f-f grad_* f*]dv*,
the weak form is

  <psi,Q_B(f)> = -1/2 int ff* [grad psi-grad_*psi*]^T
                         B(u)[grad log f-grad_*log f*] dv dv*.

For a Maxwellian with covariance theta I, its kinetic-energy production is

  dE/dt = 2/(theta T) int_0^K k^2 |Vhat(k)|^2
                              [1-exp(-theta T^2 k^2)] dk > 0.

Derivation: rotational symmetry splits B=a P_u+b e_u e_u^T. Its longitudinal
coefficient obeys r^2 b = (4/T) int k^2 Vhat^2[1-sinc(Tkr)]dk.
For u~N(0,2 theta I), E sinc(Tk|u|)=exp(-theta T^2 k^2).
This is a failure of using B_T as an instantaneous velocity-only collision
generator, not a contradiction of the actual memory dynamics: microscopic
potential/field energy can exchange with kinetic energy.

2. TRANSVERSE COMPLETION

Let phi be an even probability density on R, phi>=0, and
F_T(omega)=T phi(T omega). Put P_u=I-u u^T/|u|^2 for u!=0 and

  A_T(u)=int dk (P_u k)(P_u k)^T |Vhat(k)|^2 F_T(k.u).

At u=0 the value can be chosen arbitrarily for the weak formula (a null set
for absolutely continuous f). The tensor is symmetric, even, positive and
A_T(u)u=0. Its collision operator therefore conserves mass, momentum and
kinetic energy exactly, and dissipates int f log f. All Maxwellians are
stationary. These structural identities use the standard Landau weak form;
positivity/projection and the H-theorem mechanism are known mathematics.

For radial Vhat and a finite ultraviolet cutoff K, direct angular integration
gives, with r=|u|,

  A_T(u)=a_T(r) P_u,
  a_T(r)=pi/r int_0^K k^3 Vhat(k)^2 R_phi(Tkr) dk,
  R_phi(a)=int_{-a}^a (1-x^2/a^2) phi(x) dx.

The infinite-time resonance tensor has
  a_inf(r)=pi/r int_0^K k^3 Vhat(k)^2 dk.

If mu2=int x^2 phi(x)dx<infinity, then

  0<=1-R_phi(a)
    =int_|x|>a phi(x)dx + a^-2 int_|x|<=a x^2 phi(x)dx
    <=mu2/a^2.

Moreover a^2[1-R_phi(a)] -> mu2. The second term tends to mu2;
the first multiplied by a^2 is bounded by the vanishing second-moment tail.
Thus smoothing the positive window cannot improve the leading T^-2
transverse deficit within this operation, unless its frequency variance is zero.

For Vhat(k)=1/(k^2+kappa^2),
  int_0^infinity k Vhat(k)^2 dk = 1/(2 kappa^2),
and hence, UNIFORMLY IN THE FINITE CUTOFF K,

  0<=a_inf(r)-a_T(r)<= pi mu2/(2 T^2 kappa^2 r^3).

The tensors themselves diverge logarithmically as K->infinity. This bound
does not define an uncut finite collision operator. It controls their difference
uniformly and preserves, rather than removes, the Coulomb ultraviolet problem.

3. OPTIMAL WINDOW AT FIXED MEMORY

Let w in H_0^1([-1,1]) be real and even, nonzero, and
  phi(x)=|what(x)|^2/(2pi ||w||_2^2),
where what(x)=int_-1^1 w(s)exp(i x s)ds.
Parseval gives mu2=||w'||_2^2/||w||_2^2. The inverse transform of phi is
the autocorrelation of w and has support [-2,2]. Thus F_T has maximum
time lag 2T. The Dirichlet Poincare inequality gives

  mu2>=pi^2/4,

with equality for w(s)=cos(pi s/2). Its transform is
  what(x)=pi cos(x)/[(pi/2)^2-x^2],
with removable values what(+-pi/2)=1; ||w||_2^2=1.

For maximum lag L=2T the sharp minimum variance is pi^2/L^2. Therefore
the cosine window's transverse completion satisfies

  0<=a_inf(r)-a_L(r)<=pi^3/(2 L^2 kappa^2 r^3),

uniformly in K. This is optimal for the leading angular deficit over
nonnegative squared-Fourier windows generated on [-L/2,L/2]. It is NOT
an optimality assertion over all collision closures or signed kernels.

Comparator: the rectangular amplitude gives Fejer phi=(1-cos x)/(pi x^2),
which has infinite variance and only T^-1 angular convergence. Exactly,

  R_Fejer(a)=(2/pi)[Si(a)+(cos a-2)/a+sin a/a^2],
  1-R_Fejer(a)=4/(pi a)+O(a^-3).

A triangular amplitude instead gives phi=3 sinc^4(x/2)/(4pi), mu2=3,
with the same support [-2,2] in time. Cosine's pi^2/4 is smaller.
For an equal maximum time lag L, compare cosine at T=L/2 with Fejer at T=L.
The order improvement survives this accounting; comparing equal T alone
would give cosine twice the temporal access.
Window optimization is the classical Poincare/Rayleigh operation. The
candidate composite is its use with exact collision transversality and a
cutoff-uniform weak error certificate. Historical novelty has not been cleared.

4. WEAK OPERATOR ERROR

Suppose f>0 has mass M, bounded density and bounded Hessian of log f,
||Hess log f||<=H_f, and psi has ||Hess psi||<=H_psi.
These assumptions include an anisotropic Gaussian. Then

 |<psi,Q_L^K(f)-Q_inf^K(f)>|
 <= pi^3 H_f H_psi/(4 L^2 kappa^2)
                         int ff*/|v-v*| dv dv*.

Proof: both gradient differences in the weak form are bounded by their
Hessian bounds times |u|; insert the tensor bound and integrate.
The remaining integral is finite. For example its crude upper bound is
M[2pi ||f||_infinity+M], by splitting |u| at 1.
This error estimate is uniform in K, retains nonlinear f in the weak form,
and needs neither an acquired spectral gap nor a supplied chaos bound.
It still does not control the microscopic-to-kinetic approximation.

The Hessian assumption on log f can in fact be removed in this screened
weak estimate. Suppose only f>0 has bounded density B, mass M, and finite
Fisher information I(f)=int f|grad log f|^2. For every R>0 put
  C2(f;R)=4pi B R+M/R^2.
Splitting the convolution at R gives
  sup_v int f(v*)/|v-v*|^2 dv* <= C2(f;R).
Using |grad log f-grad_*log f*|<=|grad log f|+|grad_*log f*|,
symmetry, and Cauchy-Schwarz gives instead

 |<psi,Q_L^K(f)-Q_inf^K(f)>|
 <= pi^3 H_psi/(2L^2 kappa^2) C2(f;R) sqrt(M I(f)).

Both versions are retained: the first has a sharper integrand for smooth
scores; the second uses a weaker and explicitly stated regularity interface.
Neither acquires a uniform Fisher bound from microscopic dynamics.

5. WHAT WOULD CLOSE THE ORIGINAL TARGET

The radial Yukawa potential is a diagnostic, not a substitute for point
Coulomb. A Balescu-Lenard completion must use a symmetric off-resonant
dielectric weight that agrees with |epsilon_f(k,k.v)|^-2 on k.u=0.
One candidate is 1/(|epsilon_f(k,k.v)| |epsilon_f(k,k.v*)|).
It preserves pair symmetry and positivity but agreement on resonance alone
does not give controlled off-resonant or microscopic error. It requires
uniform response estimates and a derivation of the dielectric from dynamics.

The decisive obligations are: nonlinear collisional-time cumulant/energy-flux
control from iid data; dynamically generated screening, not fitted Yukawa;
strong-collision matching/ultraviolet renormalization; uniform near-u=0 and
dielectric derivative estimates; and stability allowing generator error to
control the evolved law. No one of these is established by the theorem above.

The kinetic cell's close-pair counterexample independently shows why small
unweighted fixed-order cumulants cannot supply the missing microscopic bound.
An energy-weighted pair/history state is necessary; its cheap closure is open.

6. DECISIVE FAILURE OF THE NEXT EXTENSION: NARROW RESPONSE RIDGES

The radial estimate cannot be promoted to arbitrary dynamically screened
weights merely because those weights are smooth and positive. Consider the
normalized, strictly stable Lorentzian response ridge
  h_eta(omega)=eta/[pi(omega^2+eta^2)], eta>0.
Its exact resonance value is h_eta(0)=1/(pi eta). Any normalized positive
window F_T=T phi(T omega), with finite sup phi, obeys

  int F_T(omega)h_eta(omega)domega / h_eta(0)
          <=pi eta T sup phi.

For cosine, sup phi=phi(0)=8/pi^3: w is nonnegative so |what(x)|<=what(0).
At fixed maximum lag L=2T, the ratio is <=4 eta L/pi^2. A relative
approximation to the ridge therefore needs L=Omega(1/eta). Window
optimization and transverse projection do not remove weak damping.
This is an exact filter counterexample; it is not asserted to be a
standalone derivation of a Coulomb dielectric.

Physical relevance: the real-frequency Gaussian plasma susceptibility has
Re chi_a(u)=-u^-2-3a u^-4+O(u^-6), while Im chi_a(u) is proportional to
u exp[-u^2/(2a)]. Thus at k~1/u the dielectric can be arbitrarily close
to zero with exponentially weak damping. Turning this asymptotic into
uniform nonlinear collision bounds requires the weighted resonance and
velocity-density cancellation, not a uniform lower bound on |epsilon|.
The kinetic cell retains the causal response as a dynamical state for this
reason. A short, positive resonance compiler alone cannot close the target.

7. A BRIDGE THAT DOES SURVIVE: FIXED MEMORY AT LEADING LOGARITHM

The cutoff-uniform error has a useful consequence stronger than a fixed-cutoff
approximation. Let ell=log(K/kappa)>0. For Yukawa,

  a_inf^K(r)=pi/(2r)[log(1+z)+1/(1+z)-1], z=(K/kappa)^2,

and for K>=kappa,
  |a_inf^K(r)-pi ell/r|<=pi/(2r).
Therefore the normalized finite-memory tensor A_L^K/ell converges to the
Landau tensor pi P_u/r as K->infinity for EVERY FIXED L>0. Under the
conditions of Section4, with finite first velocity moment, the explicit weak
bound is

 |<psi, Q_L^K(f)/ell-Q_Landau,pi(f)>|
 <= pi H_f H_psi/(4ell) int ff*|v-v*|dv dv*
   + pi^3 H_f H_psi/(4L^2 kappa^2 ell) int ff*/|v-v*|dv dv*.

Proof: apply the weak form separately to the radial logarithm error and
the finite-memory tensor error. No time limit and cutoff limit are interchanged.
This keeps exact conservation while extracting the singular leading-log
generator with a fixed maximum lag. It explains a possible leverage point:
short-distance grazing interactions dominate the Coulomb logarithm, whereas
long collective memory can affect subleading screened terms. It does not
justify discarding that memory in the full N-body process. Establishing this
separation FROM the microscopic dynamics is still the original unresolved gate.

Under the weaker bounded-density/finite-Fisher interface of Section 4, the
same normalized weak error is bounded by
  pi H_psi M sqrt(M I(f))/(2ell)
  +pi^3 H_psi C2(f;R) sqrt(M I(f))/(2L^2 kappa^2 ell).
This version needs no first velocity moment: the Lipschitz gradient factor
cancels the radial 1/r error, and score integrability follows from Fisher.

The known Landau grazing/leading-log mechanism is credited. The candidate
increment is the explicit fixed-memory positive completion with its uniform
weak estimate and matched-window sharpness. Novelty remains UNKNOWN.

9. REPRESENTATION MUTATION: UNSCREENED COULOMB WITH FINITE MEMORY

This section uses the continuum Fourier measure of Section 1, not an
unproved replacement of a periodic particle system by a continuum spectrum.
Take Vhat(k)=|k|^-2, retain the ultraviolet cutoff K, and fix T=L/2>0.
The projected coefficient now exists at k=0 without any screening parameter:

  a_T^K(r)=pi/r J_phi(A),  A=KTr,
  J_phi(A)=int_0^A R_phi(a) da/a.

For cosine phi is bounded near zero, so R_phi(a)=O(a) there. Tonelli gives
the exact acquired formula

  J_phi(A)=int_|x|<=A phi(x)
                 [log(A/|x|)-1/2+x^2/(2A^2)] dx.

Suppose additionally int phi(x)|log|x|| dx<infinity, as for cosine. Define
  c_phi=1/2+int phi(x)log|x|dx.
Then

  J_phi(A)=log A-c_phi+e_phi(A),
  0<=e_phi(A)<=mu2/(2A^2).

Proof: the exact error is
  int_|x|>A phi(x)[log(|x|/A)+1/2]dx
   +(1/(2A^2))int_|x|<=A x^2 phi(x)dx.
It is positive. On y>=1, log y+1/2<=y^2/2. This bounds the first
term by the second-moment tail divided by 2A^2, proving the bound.
Also A^2 e_phi(A)->mu2/2: the scaled tail is bounded by a vanishing
second-moment tail. This sharp asymptotic uses no dielectric oracle.

Independent check supplied the cosine constant in closed form:
  c_cos=1/2+log(pi/2)-Ci(pi)-Si(pi)/pi
       =0.2884249210069453... .
Here Si and Ci are the usual real sine and cosine integrals. One derivation
uses the characteristic function of phi,
  C(t)=(1-t/2)cos(pi t/2)+sin(pi t/2)/pi, 0<=t<=2,
and C(t)=0 for t>2. Integrating
  E log|X|=-gamma-int_0^2 [C(t)-1_{t<=1}]/t dt
gives the stated constant. Its numerical value is diagnostic; the integral
definition and exact error proof do not rely on floating quadrature.

Thus the unscreened finite-memory operation extracts the leading Coulomb
logarithm with fixed L and with no supplied infrared length. For ell=log K>0,
under Section 4 assumptions and the additional integrability displayed below,

 |<psi,Q_T^K(f)/ell-Q_Landau,pi(f)>|
 <= pi H_f H_psi/(2ell) int ff* r |log(Tr)-c_phi| dv dv*
  + pi H_f H_psi mu2/(4ell K^2 T^2) int ff*/r dv dv*.

The first integral is finite for bounded f with finite
int f(v)|v|log(2+|v|)dv; the near-r=0 factor r|log r| is bounded.
This is a weak generator statement with an explicit finite cutoff, not an
N-body limit or an evolved-law error bound. A lower Fourier cutoff k0>0
changes J(KTr) into J(KTr)-J(k0Tr); the leading log K is unchanged at fixed
k0,T. The actual discrete torus spectrum still requires its own estimates.

The same computation diagnoses precisely what transverse projection removes.
For the UNPROJECTED tensor B_T=a_T P_u+b_T e_u e_u^T,

  b_T^K(r)=pi R_phi(KTr)/r,  0<=b_T^K(r)<=pi/r.

Indeed b_T=(2pi/r)int_0^A a^-3 int_-a^a x^2 phi(x)dx da;
Tonelli evaluates this to pi R_phi(A)/r. Hence projecting changes the
log-normalized weak operator by at most
  pi H_f H_psi/(2ell) int ff* r dv dv*.
Projection is therefore quantitatively subleading in this diagnostic.
It is still an imposed operation, not a derivation of field-energy exchange.

A crucial nonuniformity remains: at k0=0 and fixed K,r, b_T->pi/r as
T->infinity. Thus the UNNORMALIZED energy defect does not vanish with
longer memory. For unit-mass Maxwellian covariance theta I, its limiting
kinetic-energy production is 2sqrt(pi)/sqrt(theta). With a fixed positive
k0 the longitudinal coefficient is instead pi[R(KTr)-R(k0Tr)]/r and tends
to zero. Infrared and long-memory limits cannot be exchanged. Dynamic
screening/collective response remains necessary for the subleading theory.

Finite duration as an infrared regulator and the leading-log Landau
mechanism have prior physical uses; no priority is claimed for either idea.
The composite exact coefficient, weak error, projection comparison and

10. A MATCHED COMPARATOR THAT LIMITS THE WINDOW CLAIM

The leading-log limit is NOT specific to finite frequency variance. If phi
is any even probability density with finite absolute logarithmic moment,
the exact error formula in Section 9 still applies. Its tail tends to zero
by logarithmic integrability, and its truncated second-moment term tends to
zero by dominated convergence. Thus J_phi(A)=log A-c_phi+o(1) without a
finite mu2. Finite variance buys the explicit A^-2 rate, not existence of
the leading-log generator. This distinction prevents overvaluing the
cosine optimization as if it created the Landau limit.

For the Fejer comparator, its characteristic function is 1-|t| on [-1,1].
The same logarithm identity gives
  E log|X|=1-gamma,  c_Fejer=3/2-gamma=0.9227843350984671... .
At EQUAL maximum lag L, its large-K radial coefficient is
  a_Fejer=pi/r[log(KLr)-c_Fejer+o(1)],
whereas cosine uses T=L/2 and gives
  a_cos=pi/r[log(KLr)-(log 2+c_cos)+O((KLr)^-2)].
The cosine offset log 2+c_cos=0.9815721015668906... is actually larger
than c_Fejer by 0.0587877664684235... . Hence cosine is not uniformly
superior even among these finite-lag Coulomb coefficients. Neither offset
is the physically derived subleading screened constant. The valid advantage
is its sharp finite-variance angular/error control in the stated class;
the universal leading-log leverage belongs to finite-memory radial
elimination itself. The next decisive task remains microscopic acquisition,
not optimization of this subleading number.
