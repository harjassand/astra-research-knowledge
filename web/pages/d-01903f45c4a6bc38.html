1. Target and boundary
---------------------
Target: obtain a nonlocal equilibrium move from finite, checkable local
interaction data, prove detailed balance and a mixing statement, and account for
energy-query, learning, rejection, memory, precision, and free-energy costs.
No basin labels or transition paths are supplied.

The strongest complete positive statement below is an exact symmetry-refresh
theorem. It removes mixing barriers caused by an exact finite symmetry group,
provided the quotient chain already mixes. A factor-table procedure can certify
such symmetries from a known bounded-arity interaction hypergraph. This is a
real acquisition interface, but rigid-fragment, pivot, permutation, and cluster
moves are established prior art. The quotient-mixing premise also leaves
non-symmetry metastability unresolved. I therefore do not claim novelty or a
general molecular capability.

2. Exact group-refresh theorem
-----------------------------
Let Ω be a finite or standard probability space with Gibbs measure π, and let
G be a finite group of measurable bijections preserving π. Let P0 be a
π-reversible Markov kernel that is G-equivariant:

    P0(gx,gy)=P0(x,y).

The G-orbits form a measurable quotient. The quotient kernel is

    Pbar([x],[y]) = sum_{z∈[y]} P0(x,z),

which is independent of the representative x by equivariance. Write γbar for
its Poincaré spectral gap. Define the orbit-refresh kernel

    RG(x,A) = |G|^{-1} #{g∈G : gx∈A},

or, when Ω is finite, RG(x,y)=|G|^{-1} #{g∈G : gx=y}.

For finite G, RG(x,·) is uniform on the finite orbit Gx. Thus RG is conditional
expectation onto orbit-invariant functions, is π-reversible, and has no energy
or force rejection. For 0<ρ<1, set

    P = (1-ρ) P0 + ρ RG.

Then P is π-reversible and

    gap(P) >= min{ (1-ρ) γbar, ρ }.                 (G1)

Proof. In L²(π), let Πf=Eπ[f | orbit]. G-equivariance and reversibility imply
P0Π=ΠP0, so the orbit-constant and orbit-mean-zero subspaces are orthogonal
invariant subspaces for P0. Also RG=Π and

    Varπ(f)=Varπ(Πf)+Eπ[Varπ(f | orbit)],
    <f,(I-RG)f>π=Eπ[Varπ(f | orbit)].

On the first subspace the Dirichlet form of P0 is that of Pbar, at least
γbar Varπ(Πf). On the second subspace its Dirichlet form is nonnegative. Hence

    <f,(I-P)f>π >= (1-ρ)γbar Varπ(Πf)
                            + ρ Eπ[Varπ(f | orbit)],

which proves (G1). If P0 is lazy and Ω is finite, the usual finite-state bound gives

    t_mix(ε) <= gap(P)^{-1} log(1/(ε π_min)).

For continuous Ω, (G1) remains an L² spectral-gap statement. A conversion to
worst-case total-variation mixing additionally needs regularity or a warm-start
condition; no such conversion is claimed here.

The barrier removal in (G1) is precise: any bottleneck separating states only
by their G-orbit label is removed in one refresh. It does not remove a bottleneck
between quotient states. Worst-case total-variation mixing also retains the
standard log(1/π_min) dependence.

3. Interaction-local acquisition and its complete cost
------------------------------------------------------
Consider q-state variables x_1,...,x_n and an input interaction hypergraph
whose scopes S_a have size at most k. Assume the energy is an unknown function
of the form

    H(x)=sum_a ψ_a(x_{S_a}),

and the scope list is supplied. Only the total-energy oracle H(x) is queried;
force calls are not needed. The family D of all subsets of supplied scopes has
size at most m 2^k, where m is the number of scopes. Fix a reference state 0 at
each variable. For each S∈D, acquire the values

    H_T(x_T) = H(x_T,0_{-T}),   T⊆S,

and form the canonical interaction tables

    φ_S(x_S) = sum_{T⊆S} (-1)^{|S|-|T|} H_T(x_T).

Möbius inversion gives H(x)=sum_{S∈D} φ_S(x_S) on all q^n states. It takes at
most

    Q_acq <= sum_{S∈D} q^{|S|} <= m 2^k q^k

total-energy queries and stores O(m 2^k q^k) table entries. The factor tables
are not individually identifiable without a gauge convention, but this
canonical decomposition is sufficient to evaluate H and verify symmetries.
Each oracle call still costs the molecular force field's full energy-evaluation
cost C_E unless the source itself exposes local terms; total acquisition work
is at most Q_acq C_E. No force evaluations, trajectory equilibration, or
rejection trials are hidden in this count.

Given a candidate permutation or finite group action g from a typed interaction
graph, compare the transformed factor tables. If the scopes are permuted among
themselves and every factor table agrees after the induced variable
permutation (up to constants whose total is independent of x), this certifies
H(gx)=H(x) exactly. Checking one generator costs O(m q^k) table comparisons.
The candidate generator list is an input to this procedure; no polynomial
bound is asserted for finding all graph automorphisms. This is the supplied
structure boundary.

At runtime, sample g uniformly from G, return gx, and cache H(x). The move
costs O(n) state transformation plus group-sampling cost C_G. If all energy
terms are stored locally, the acceptance check is unnecessary for a certified
symmetry and no energy/force call is needed. If a group element has a bounded
symmetry defect D_g(x)=H(gx)-H(x), use the symmetric group proposal and
Metropolis probability

    a(x,gx)=min{1, exp[-β D_g(x)]}.

This satisfies detailed balance because the group proposal is symmetric. If a
certified bound |D_g(x)|<=Δ holds for all x and all proposed g, then every
proposal is accepted with probability at least exp(-βΔ). Within a single orbit,
the uniform-proposal Metropolis kernel minorizes the conditional Gibbs law by
exp(-βΔ): for w(x)=exp(-βV(x)) with oscillation at most Δ,

    K(x,y)/π(y|orbit)
      = (|orbit|^{-1} sum_z w(z)) / max{w(x),w(y)}
      >= exp(-βΔ).

So the conditional orbit mixing time is at most exp(βΔ) log(1/ε), independent
of any barrier in the G-invariant part H0 of H=H0+V. This is only a conditional
orbit statement; a quotient mixing bound is still needed for global mixing.

For a cut-supported transformation, only factors changed by g enter D_g. A
conservative, locally checkable certificate is the sum of the oscillations of
those factors. If at most s factors change and each oscillation is at most J,
then Δ<=sJ, giving a conditional orbit cost exp(βsJ), with no dependence on
the number of variables moved. Long-range electrostatics, changing contact
lists, or an interface with s growing with molecule size remove this guarantee
unless a tighter defect bound is proved.

Finite-precision boundary: if every acquired energy value has absolute error
at most u, each φ_S has error at most 2^k u, and reconstruction gives the
conservative uniform bound ζ<=m 2^{2k}u. An approximate target based on Hhat
with ||H-Hhat||∞<=ζ differs in total variation by at most
0.5(exp(2βζ)-1), capped at 1. A certified defect table must widen its bound
by 2ζ. Exact detailed balance for the exact physical target requires exact
energy ratios; a finite implementation can instead use interval-certified
accept/reject decisions and charge a per-step transition-kernel error δ. Over
T updates a coupling bound adds at most Tδ to total-variation error. Exact
transcendental Bernoulli costs and implementation-level interval code were not
implemented here.

This factor-acquisition route applies to finite rotamer or fragment-state
models with fixed bounded-arity interactions. It does not directly acquire
continuous all-atom potentials, because discretization bias must also be
bounded and moving one torsion can change an input-dependent nonbonded contact
graph. A continuous torsion discretization with per-bin energy oscillation at
most η/β incurs a target error that must be charged; no continuous-model
guarantee is claimed in this record.

4. Molecular interpretation and exact free-energy scope
------------------------------------------------------
The structurally checkable positive case is a finite-state molecule with
identical fragments or domains whose typed local interaction tables are
permuted by a symmetry. This includes exchange-equivalent subunits and
symmetry-related rotamer states. A nonlocal group refresh samples among those
states without a transition-path oracle. For exact symmetry, symmetry-related
basins A and gA have

    Z_A = sum_{x∈A} exp(-βH(x))
        = sum_{x∈A} exp(-βH(gx)) = Z_{gA},
    F_A-F_{gA}=0.

A direct continuous-coordinate instance is parity refresh for an achiral
molecule in an achiral, isotropic environment. For atom coordinates
r=(r_1,...,r_N) in R^{3N}, apply r_i↦Q r_i for one fixed orthogonal Q with
det(Q)=-1. This map has absolute Jacobian one, preserves every pair distance
and ordinary angle, and reverses each signed dihedral. Therefore a force field
made from pair-distance, angle, and mirror-even torsion terms has H(Qr)=H(r).
The algorithm reflects the whole conformation with probability 1/2. It needs
O(N) coordinate operations, no energy or force calls if the term list
certifies parity, and accepts every move. Any two disjoint mirror basins A and
QA have exactly equal restricted partition functions, however high the path
barrier between them. A termwise defect bound Δ for chiral or external-field
terms gives the exp(-βΔ) acceptance floor instead. The promise is physically
checkable from the energy terms and environment; it fails for a chiral
environment, a chiral molecule whose force field breaks parity, or a
non-reflection-symmetric boundary condition. This recovers a narrow but
practical symmetry-bridging capability. Mirror inversion moves for
enantiomer sampling and mirror-rotation transformations for molecular structure
searches already appear in the literature; this is an application of known
symmetry Monte Carlo moves, not an originality claim.

Thus this mechanism supplies exact equality, not a new estimator for arbitrary
unequal basin free energies. For an approximate bijection g:A→B with
|H(gx)-H(x)|<=Δ pointwise and a measure-preserving Jacobian, it gives only

    exp(-βΔ) <= Z_B/Z_A <= exp(βΔ),  |F_A-F_B|<=Δ.

To estimate a nonzero difference from samples, if both basins have probability
at least p_min under π, multiplicative Chernoff estimates of their indicator
means need

    N = O((p_min δ²)^-1 log(1/α))

effectively independent samples for relative error δ and failure probability
α. Taking δ<=β ε_F/4 controls the free-energy error by ε_F when δ<=1/2.
Generating those samples costs the mixing time between samples. If p_min is
exponentially small, this estimator remains exponentially costly. Rare-basin
free energies require additional structure or a validated path/overlap method.

5. Why a broader energy/force-oracle claim is impossible
--------------------------------------------------------
There is an explicit smooth hidden-well lower bound for an unrestricted
potential oracle. Let Ω=[0,1]^d. Choose a binary code C⊂{0,1}^d of size
N>=exp(c d) and minimum Hamming distance at least d/4. Place centers
s_b=(1/4,...,1/4)+(1/2)b. For fixed r=0.1 and sufficiently large d, their
Euclidean separation exceeds 2r and all radius-r balls lie in Ω. Let φ_d be a
C∞ bump supported on the unit ball, equal to 1 on the radius-1/2 ball, with
gradient and Hessian operator norms bounded by constants independent of d.
For an unknown center s, define

    U_s(x) = -A φ_d((x-s)/r).

The exact energy-and-force oracle returns (0,0) outside B(s,r). The Gibbs
measure at inverse temperature β assigns mass at least 1-η to B(s,r) if

    βA >= log(1 + (1-η)/(η v_d (r/2)^d)),

where v_d is the unit-ball volume. For fixed r and η, Stirling's formula gives
A=O(d log d). The gradient and Hessian bounds are therefore polynomial in d.
Yet an adaptive algorithm that makes Q energy/force queries can hit at most Q
of the N disjoint supports along its all-zero transcript. Its no-hit output
can lie in at most one additional support. Averaged over uniform s, its
probability of outputting into the correct support is at most (Q+1)/N. A
sampler within total variation ε of every π_s must put at least 1-η-ε mass in
the correct support, and hence

    Q >= N(1-η-ε)-1 = exp(Ω(d)).

This lower bound is internally derived for a global d-body bump. It does not
apply to known bounded-arity molecular interaction graphs; it establishes why
local smoothness/force bounds alone cannot certify discovery of a collective
coordinate.

A separate computational boundary remains even when all local factors and the
graph are known. Barahona proves NP-hardness of finding a ground state for
three-dimensional Ising spin glasses with integer couplings. This implies a
precise conditional obstruction to a uniform approximate sampler: if one
algorithm sampled every such instance to TV error at most 1/4 in time polynomial
in the instance size and β, then choose β>=n log 2+log 8. Since energies are
integers, every non-ground state is at least one energy unit above the ground
state, so its Gibbs probability is at most 2^n exp(-β)<=1/8. The approximate
sampler therefore returns a ground state with probability at least 5/8.
Repeating O(log(1/δ)) times and returning the lowest-energy output solves the
NP-hard ground-state problem in randomized polynomial time. Unless NP⊆BPP,
there is no such uniform polynomial sampler for this unrestricted local class.
This does not rule out approximate samplers at a fixed bounded β or on a
restricted physical subclass; it rules out the all-β, arbitrary-frustration
promise. Barahona also gives NP-hard partition-function cases. The exact
sampling reduction above is the claim used here.

6. Baseline comparison at equal structural information
------------------------------------------------------
Local Metropolis. It needs one energy difference per proposal (one force call
only if the proposal construction uses forces). In the Curie-Weiss check

    H(x)=-J m(x)^2/(2n),  m=sum_i x_i,  x_i∈{-1,+1},  βJ=2,

the low-temperature target has positive and negative magnetization basins. For
even n, the positive-cut conductance of single-spin Metropolis is exactly

    Φ_loc = [π(m=2)/π(m>0)] [(n+2)/(2n)] exp(-2βJ/n).

By Stirling, the first ratio is exp(-c n+O(log n)) for some c>0. The Rayleigh
quotient of the cut indicator gives gap <=Φ_loc/[1-π(m>0)], so local mixing is
exponential. A global spin-flip symmetry refresh has acceptance one and
positive-cut conductance at least 1/4 when mixed into the local kernel with
probability 1/2. The supplied finite script checks detailed balance of the
magnetization-lumped chain and the conductance values through n=128. This is a
symmetry example, not a molecular validation.

Replica exchange. For inverse temperatures β_i,β_j, swapping configurations
x_i,x_j has acceptance

    min{1, exp[(β_i-β_j)(H(x_i)-H(x_j))]}.

It pays for K replicas and their energy/force evaluations; effective transport
depends on overlap along the temperature ladder. It is a strong equally
informed baseline when temperature makes the barrier traversable, but it does
not remove the need to choose a ladder. In a certified exact-symmetry case,
group refresh crosses the symmetry mode using one configuration and no ladder.
Primary source: Hukushima and Nemoto, 1996,
https://doi.org/10.1143/JPSJ.65.1604.

Umbrella sampling. A bias over a supplied or acquired collective coordinate
can bridge separated windows, but the input includes the coordinate and window
schedule; estimating ratios needs overlap among windows. Its query/trajectory
cost is the sum over windows, including equilibration and overlap uncertainty.
The present exact group action is a coordinate discovered from typed local
symmetry data, but it only handles symmetry-related basins. Primary source:
Torrie and Valleau, 1977,
https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991(77)90121-8.

Splitting. It requires a nested sequence of score sets; if a score has
conditional survival probability p at each of L levels, the number of
trajectories needed for fixed relative error scales at least with the inverse
level survival variance and its resampling cost. Without an acquired score,
the group theorem supplies no splitting levels. If both basins are rare, the
symmetry refresh still needs an orbit map and bounded defect.

Nonlocal MCMC. Pivot, rigid-body, crankshaft, and segment moves are established
molecular Monte Carlo methods. Their detailed-balance ratios include the
energy difference and any Jacobian/proposal-density ratio; acceptance often
decreases with the number of moved residues or changed contacts. The exact
symmetry update is a special case with zero energy difference; for a bounded
cut defect it has a quantitative acceptance floor. This is a useful
certificate for an existing move family, not a claim that the family is new.
See Betancourt, 2011, https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3515960, and the protein Monte
Carlo methods review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2860296/.

Metadynamics. History-dependent bias can discover and fill minima along a
chosen low-dimensional collective-coordinate set. It charges coordinate
acquisition and bias deposition; its original dynamics is non-Markovian in the
physical coordinate alone, so equilibrium reweighting/error claims need their
own conditions. The exact symmetry kernel is a stationary MH/Gibbs kernel but
cannot discover basins outside its orbit. Primary source: Laio and Parrinello,
2002, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.202427399.

The theorem's input is stronger than a generic nonlocal proposal: it supplies
or acquires an exact group action. Its advantage is limited to orbit
metastability. It does not beat an equally informed cluster method on a model
where that method already mixes rapidly, and it does not yield a generic
barrier-free sampler.

7. Falsification, source status, and next gate
---------------------------------------------
Counterexample to overgeneralization: add any non-invariant field of total
oscillation Δ across the group orbit. The best generic acceptance guarantee
falls to exp(-βΔ); if Δ=Θ(n), the group proposal can again be exponentially
rare. If a basin split is not a union of orbits, the orbit update may not cross
it. If the quotient chain has exponentially small conductance, (G1) leaves
that bottleneck untouched. If factor arity k grows with n, acquisition costs
q^k and are no longer polynomial. If a continuous torsion well is narrower
than the discretization scale, no finite-grid conclusion follows without a
derivative/regularity bound.
