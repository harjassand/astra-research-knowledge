SECTION 6: VANDERMONDE FRAME AND MULTIPLICITY

Let ell_i=sum_{k=i}^{m-1} a_k, ell_m=0, and eta_i=(ell_i-mean(ell))/(2N).
Then eta_i-eta_{i+1}=a_i/(2N)>0. With V_{i,s}=eta_i^s, s=1,...,p,
H=2 V^T(I-11^T/m)V. The augmented matrix [1,V] is the ordinary square
Vandermonde evaluation matrix for 1,t,...,t^(m-1), so

  det(H)=(2^p/m) prod_{i<j}(eta_i-eta_j)^2 > 0.

This proves strict positive definiteness for every regular integral
highest weight, including m=2 (p=1). The report's lower determinant
estimate follows pairwise from eta_i-eta_j >= (j-i)c/2 >= c/2. Its trace
bound follows from H positive semidefinite and
Tr(H)<=2m sum_{s=1}^p b^(2s), when |eta_i|<=b. Finally, for positive H,
lambda_min(H) >= det(H)/(Tr H)^(p-1), since each other eigenvalue is at
most Tr(H). The p=1 convention is denominator 1. These steps validate
the conditioning implication used before the finite Gram perturbation.

For the multiplicity, represent lambda by the partition ell. Tensoring
with the fundamental adds one box. If every Dynkin label a_i>=1, every
one of the m rows is addable: for row i>1 the preceding row exceeds it
by a_{i-1}>=1, and row 1 is always addable. The resulting m partitions
are distinct as SU(m) weights after removing their common trace shift.
Thus V_lambda tensor C^m is multiplicity-free with m summands. Tensor
adjunction gives

  dim Hom_G(V_lambda,V_lambda tensor C^m tensor (C^m)*)
       = dim End_G(V_lambda tensor C^m) = m.

Since C^m tensor (C^m)* = 1 plus ad and V_lambda occurs once in itself,
the adjoint multiplicity in End(V_lambda) is m-1. This establishes the
dimension needed by the frame argument; it does not assume the families
are independent. Their independence follows separately from the positive
Gram bound for N>=N_0, giving the full adjoint isotypic component there.
No completeness assertion for the polynomial frame at N<N_0 is needed.

An independent Weyl-alternant check gives the same multiplicity. In
chi_lambda chi_ad, the alternant terms are indexed by adjoint weights
mu=0 (multiplicity m-1) and roots e_i-e_j. A term can contribute to
chi_lambda only if lambda+rho+mu=w(lambda+rho). For regular lambda, the
coordinates of lambda+rho have pairwise gaps at least 2. Any nonidentity
permutation moves some coordinate by absolute amount at least 2, whereas
a root has coordinates only 0,+/-1. Thus only the identity/zero-weight
term contributes, with coefficient m-1. This checks the argument at
arbitrary regular highest weights, not just the finite grid below.

SECTION 7: ALL-N RESET PATCH

Use the generator metric in the source, with long roots of squared
length 1 and fundamental Casimir C_F=(m^2-1)/(2m). In partition
coordinates the Casimir is

  C2(a)=1/2 [sum_i ell_i(ell_i+m+1-2i) - (sum_i ell_i)^2/m].

Equivalently, for lambda=sum_i a_i omega_i,
C2(lambda)=1/2 (lambda,lambda+2rho). All fundamental-weight inner
products (omega_i,omega_j) are positive. The fundamental values are
i(m-i)(m+1)/(2m), minimized at i=1,m-1, with value C_F. Consequently
every nonzero dominant integral weight has C2>=C_F. Decomposing End(V)
into irreducibles, its scalar summand is the full kernel of the
commutator Laplacian and every traceless summand has Casimir >=C_F.
Hence, for arbitrary (possibly nonnormal) A,

  E(A)>=C_F (||A||_HS^2-|Tr A|^2/d).

The singular-value inequality |Tr A|^2<=rank(A)||A||_HS^2 yields, for
||A||_HS=1 and rank(A)<=Q<d,
Lambda_a(Q)>=C_F(1-Q/d)>=C_F/d. No equality at rank d is claimed; the
identity has zero energy there, as required.

The Weyl dimension product in Dynkin labels has factors
1+(a_i+...+a_{j-1})/(j-i). Under a_i<=CN, each factor is at most
1+CN, hence d<=(CN+1)^M. For N<N_0 and Q<d, the reset channel has risk
at most 1 and

  C_small Lambda_a(Q)/N^2
    >= (N_0/N)^2 (CN_0+1)^M/d >= 1,

using the stated C_small and N<N_0. For Q=d, the identity channel gives
zero risk. Thus the small-scale patch is valid without assuming a
small-N Gram lower bound. This checks the stated patch logic; it does not
validate other parts of the transfer theorem.

FINITE EXACT SEARCH

Runnable command (Python standard library only):

  python3 outputs/research/combinatorics_optimization/cycle3/regular_su_m_audit/audit_multiplicity_smallN.py

The script uses Fraction arithmetic throughout. It exhaustively checked:

* 3,279 regular label vectors, m=2,...,8 and each a_i in {1,2,3};
  fundamental one-box additions were all valid and distinct.
* 16,395 (m,a,N) points from that grid with N=1,...,5; exact Gram
  determinants matched the Vandermonde product, all leading principal
  minors were positive, the report's determinant lower bound held, the
  trace bound held with b=max_i|eta_i|, and the exact Weyl dimension
  satisfied d<=(CN+1)^M with pointwise C=max_i a_i/N.
* 126 regular label vectors, m=2,...,7 and each a_i in {1,2}; direct
  signed Weyl-alternant enumeration returned adjoint multiplicity m-1.
* 335,915 nonzero dominant label vectors, m=2,...,8 and each a_i in
  {0,...,5}; the exact Casimir formula was always >=C_F, with the minimum
  attained at a fundamental endpoint weight for each m.

Observed output: all assertions passed; minima C_F were 3/4, 4/3, 15/8,
12/5, 35/12, 24/7, 63/16 for m=2,...,8 respectively. These are finite
checks. The uniform positivity, rank-trace inequality, and Weyl product
bounds above are established by the accompanying algebraic derivations,
not by extrapolating the grid. No direct operator-matrix enumeration of
the Y_{s,A} was run.
