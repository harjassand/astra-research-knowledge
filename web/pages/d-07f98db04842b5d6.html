\section{A large kernel forced by growing divisibility}

For a uniformly discrete proper bounded-geometry space $Z$, use complex,
integral locally finite analytic $K$-homology and put
\[
 \KX_1(Z)=\varinjlim_{r\to\infty}\KK_1(C_0(P_r(Z)),\C).
\]
Here $P_r$ is the Rips complex whose simplices have diameter at most $r$.
Let $A_Z=C^*_{\max}(Z)$ be the maximal completion of the algebra of bounded,
locally compact finite-propagation operators on
$H_Z=\ell^2(Z)\otimes\ell^2(\N)$. Compact entries need not have uniformly
bounded ranks. The assembly map is the canonical maximal coarse index
\[
 \mu_Z^{\max}:\KX_1(Z)\longrightarrow K_1(A_Z).
\]
The corresponding reduced map is obtained by the maximal-to-reduced quotient.

Write
\[
 \mathcal B=\ell^\infty(\N,\Z)/\Z^{(\N)},\qquad \co=2^{\aleph_0},
\]
where $\Z^{(\N)}$ denotes finite-support sequences. Bounded integer sequences
have finite range; this quotient is an abelian group, not a Banach space.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:large}
For the bounded-degree graph union $X$ constructed in Section~\ref{sec:graph},
there is an injective homomorphism
\[
 \mathcal B\hookrightarrow\ker\mu_X^{\max}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \dim_{\Q}\ker(\mu_X^{\max}\otimes\Q)=\co.
\]
The reduced assembly kernel also has rational dimension $\co$.
\end{theorem}

The new connection is that the source's analytic vanishing holds for every
base class, while its divisibility obstruction detects every nonzero bounded
integer sequence, rather than only the constant sequence used there.
We first isolate this connection so its quantifiers are explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Amplification by bounded sequences]\label{lem:amplify}
Suppose finite components $X_j$ become isolated at every fixed Rips scale.
Suppose $d_j\in K_1(P_{r_0}(X_j))$ define a homomorphism
\[
 D:\prod_j\Z\longrightarrow\KX_1(X),\quad
 a\longmapsto[(a_jd_j)_j],
\]
with $\mu D=0$. Assume that for each fixed scale $r\geq r_0$ there are
integers $M_{j,r}\to\infty$ such that, for all sufficiently large $j$,
\[
 n(d_j)_r=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_{j,r}\mid n
 \qquad(n\in\Z).
\]
Finally assume $D$ kills every finite-support sequence. Then $D$ restricts to
an injection $\mathcal B\hookrightarrow\ker\mu$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $D(a)=0$, its representing class is zero at some finite later scale $r$.
Restriction to each isolated large component gives $a_j(d_j)_r=0$.
Thus $M_{j,r}\mid a_j$ eventually. For bounded $a$, eventually
$M_{j,r}>\sup_j|a_j|$, so $a_j=0$. Hence a bounded sequence belongs to the
kernel of $D$ exactly when it has finite support. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rank}
$\dim_{\Q}(\mathcal B\otimes\Q)=\co$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Encode the nonempty finite binary strings injectively by natural numbers.
For each infinite binary string $s$, let $E_s$ be the codes of its successive
nonempty prefixes. Distinct $E_s$ have finite intersection. If
$\sum_{k=1}^m n_k1_{E_{s_k}}$ has finite support, choose a nonzero $n_k$.
Infinitely many elements of $E_{s_k}$ avoid all other $E_{s_l}$, where the
sum equals $n_k$, a contradiction. Thus their classes in $\mathcal B$ are
$\Z$-linearly independent. The group $\mathcal B$ is torsion-free, since
$na$ has finite support for $n\neq0$ only if $a$ does. Clearing denominators
therefore preserves this independence after tensoring with $\Q$. There are
$\co$ strings, giving the lower bound. The cardinality of $\mathcal B$ is at
most $\co$, giving the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\section{The source construction and its topological obstruction}
\label{sec:graph}

We reconstruct the construction of \cite{OAIcoarse}, rather than assuming
its headline assembly claim. Its two essential inputs are congruence
divisibility and a maximal-norm analytic transfer.

Fix a finite symmetric generating set $S$ of $\Gamma=\SL_3(\Z)$.
The established property~(T) of this group is used in the analytic argument.
Let $Q_k$ be its image modulo $2^k$, with $Q_0=\{1\}$.
Reduction $Q_{k+1}\to Q_k$ is surjective with fibers of size at most $2^9$.
Moreover, reduction is injective on any fixed $S$-word ball for sufficiently
large $k$: a finite set of distinct integral matrices stays distinct modulo
all sufficiently large powers of two.

Set $m_j=2^j$, $l_j=100(j+1)$, and
\[
 U_j=1+m_jE_{12},\quad V_j=1+m_jE_{23},\quad
 [U_j,V_j]=1+m_j^2E_{13}.
\]
Thus $U_j,V_j$ commute modulo $2^{2j}$, but their commutator survives modulo
$2^{3j}$. The base graph is $B_j=(\Z/l_j\Z)^3$ with axis-neighbor edges.
For an oriented base edge, let $a,b$ be its signed seam crossings in the first
two coordinates. For $k\leq2j$ define $Y_{j,k}$ on $B_j\times Q_k$ by
fiber edges $q\leftrightarrow sq$ and horizontal matching edges
\[
 (u,q)\longleftrightarrow(v,qV_j^bU_j^a).
\]
Left and right actions commute. Put $Y_j=Y_{j,2j}$.

The graph $X_j$ has slices $Y_{j,k_j(t)}$, $-3j\leq t\leq3j$, where
\[
 k_j(t)=\begin{cases}2j,&|t|\leq j,\\3j-|t|,&j\leq|t|\leq3j.\end{cases}
\]
Consecutive slices are connected by reduction of the fiber coordinate or
by identity when the levels agree. Its endpoints are two copies of $B_j$;
its central collar is $Y_j\times[-j,j]$. All graph edges have length one.
These graphs are connected with degree at most $|S|+6+2^{10}$.
Projection to $B_j$ is $1$-Lipschitz, and each endpoint is isometric to $B_j$.

Form $B=\bigsqcup B_j$, $Y=\bigsqcup Y_j$, $X=\bigsqcup X_j$. Choose the
zero base vertex, identity fiber coordinate, and central height as roots.
Join roots of successive components by auxiliary metric edges of length
$100+j^2$, without adding vertices. The intrinsic component metrics are
unchanged. At each fixed radius cross-component pairs occur in only
finitely many finite components. A bounded-degree weighted graph with all
edge lengths at least one has uniformly finite balls, so these spaces are
proper, uniformly discrete, and of bounded geometry. The projection
$f:Y\to B$ and endpoint maps $i_\pm:B\to X$ are proper and bornologous.

Let $T_j=(\R/l_j\Z)^3$. A periodic triangulation of its unit cubes embeds
it in $P_3(B_j)$. Let $u_1,u_2,u_3$ be its integral coordinate classes, let
$L_j$ have $c_1(L_j)=u_1u_2$, and let $e_3\in K^1(T_j)$ be the third circle
generator. The class
\[
 \theta_j=([L_j]-1)e_3
\]
is primitive in $K^1(T_j)$. The exterior-algebra description of torus
$K$-theory and its torsion-free universal coefficient theorem give
$\tau_j\in K_1(T_j)$ with $\langle\theta_j,\tau_j\rangle=1$.
For each integer sequence $a$ let $\beta(a)$ be the coarse class represented
at scale three by the product of $a_j\tau_j$ pushed into $P_3(B_j)$, and set
\[
 D(a)=(i_+)_*\beta(a)-(i_-)_*\beta(a).
\]
This is legitimate for arbitrary sequences, because
$\KK_1(C_0(\bigsqcup P_3(B_j)),\C)=\prod_j K_1(P_3(B_j))$.

Fix an integer scale $r\geq3$. If $l_j>4r$, the small coordinate differences
between vertices of a Rips simplex lift uniquely to $\Z^3$, compatibly on
faces. Affine interpolation defines a retraction
$p_{B,j}:P_r(B_j)\to T_j$. Compose it with graph projections to get
$p_{Y,j},p_{X,j}$.

\begin{lemma}[The integral obstruction]\label{lem:div}
For each fixed $r$ there is a finite integer $c_r$ such that, for all
sufficiently large $j$,
\[
 p_{Y,j}^*\theta_j\in2^{j-c_r}K^1(P_r(Y_j)).
\]
If $d_j$ is the difference of the two endpoint images of $\tau_j$, then
\[
 n(d_j)_r=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2^{j-c_r}\mid n.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $j$ such that reduction to $Q_{2j}$ is injective on the word ball
of radius $2r$. Suppress $j$, and write $m=2^j$.
The seam numbers $a_{xy},b_{xy}$ of oriented Rips edges are integer
cocycles representing $p_Y^*u_1,p_Y^*u_2$. Choose a length-at-most-$r$ path
on each ordered edge $x<y$, with fiber coordinates $q_x,q_y$.
Its Cayley steps give a word $w_{xy}$ satisfying
\[
 q_y=\overline w_{xy}q_x\,\overline g(a_{xy},b_{xy}),\qquad
 g(a,b)=V^bU^a.
\]
On an ordered triangle, additivity of seams and commutation modulo $m^2$
give $\overline w_{02}=\overline w_{12}\overline w_{01}$. Word-ball
injectivity then gives $w_{02}=w_{12}w_{01}$ in $\Gamma$.

In $N=\ker(Q_{3j}\to Q_{2j})$, each element is $1+m^2D$ modulo $m^3$.
This is an abelian group, and $d(1+m^2D)=D_{13}\bmod m$ is a homomorphism.
Its elements commute with matrices equal to one modulo $m$, including $g$.
Putting $C=1+m^2E_{13}$, elementary matrix multiplication gives
\[
 g(a,b)g(a',b')=g(a+a',b+b')C^{ab'},\qquad d(C)=1.
\]
Choose lifts $\widetilde q_x\in Q_{3j}$ and define $n_{xy}\in N$ by
\[
 \widetilde q_y=\widetilde w_{xy}\widetilde q_x
                 g(a_{xy},b_{xy})n_{xy}.
\]
Substituting along edges $01,12$, and moving $n_{01}$ only past $g_{12}$,
gives $n_{02}=C^{a_{01}b_{12}}n_{01}n_{12}$. Thus the cochain
$c_{xy}=d(n_{xy})$ satisfies $\delta c=-a\smile b$ modulo $m$.
The coefficient exact sequence $0\to\Z\xrightarrow{m}\Z\to\Z/m\to0$
therefore shows that $p_Y^*(u_1u_2)$ is divisible by $m$ in integral
$H^2$. No torsion-freeness of this cohomology group is required.
Line-bundle classification gives a root $p_Y^*L_j\cong(L')^{\otimes2^j}$.

Bounded geometry supplies a dimension bound $D_r\geq1$ for $P_r(Y_j)$,
uniform in $j$. Put $z=[L']-1$. Its classifying map lands in
$\mathbb{CP}^{D_r}$ by cellular approximation, so $z^{D_r+1}=0$.
Consequently
\[
 [p_Y^*L_j]-1=\sum_{h=1}^{D_r}\binom{2^j}{h}z^h.
\]
For $1\leq h\leq D_r<2^j$,
$h\binom{2^j}{h}=2^j\binom{2^j-1}{h-1}$ shows that every coefficient is
divisible by $2^{j-\lfloor\log_2D_r\rfloor}$.
Multiplication by $p_Y^*e_3$ proves the first assertion with
$c_r=\lfloor\log_2D_r\rfloor$.

For the second, take $j>2r$. Cover $P_r(X_j)$ by its induced subcomplexes
$M^+$ on heights $t\geq-r$ and $M^-$ on heights $t\leq r$.
Their intersection $I$ lies in the central collar. A length-$r$ path
between vertices of $I$ stays within heights $[-2r,2r]$; hence forgetting
height induces $h:I\to P_r(Y_j)$ and $p_X|_I=p_Yh$.
If $n(d_j)_r=0$, Mayer--Vietoris gives $\eta\in K_1(I)$ whose images in
$K_1(M^\pm)$ are the two endpoint classes $n\tau_j$.
Pairing its $M^+$ image with $(p_X|_{M^+})^*\theta_j$ gives
\[
 n=\langle h^*p_Y^*\theta_j,\eta\rangle.
\]
The first assertion makes this integer divisible by $2^{j-c_r}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Why every endpoint difference has zero maximal index}

We give the analytic argument, including the universal-norm issues.
The standard identification of the maximal coarse index and its naturality
are those of \cite{OOY,SS}; this is not an arbitrary map declared to be
assembly.

\begin{lemma}[Norm and covering control]\label{lem:norm}
For each $R$ there is $C_Z(R)<\infty$ such that
\[
 \|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\|T\|_{\max}
 \leq C_Z(R)\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}},\quad T\in\C[Z],\ \prop(T)\leq R.
\]
Bounded finite-propagation operators with possibly noncompact entries
define multipliers of $A_Z$, with the analogous upper bound.
The finite-vertex compact ideal is $\mathcal K(H_Z)$ with its ordinary norm.
Exact covering isometries for proper coarse maps define maximal Roe
homomorphisms, independent on $K$-theory of the isometry chosen.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $N_R=\sup_z|B(z,R)|$. Partition the matrix support into at most
$2N_R-1$ row-column matchings by greedy edge coloring. Each matching
operator $T_a$ has at most one entry in a row and column and has norm at
most $\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. Its square $T_a^*T_a$ belongs to the diagonal
$C^*$-algebra $\prod_z\mathcal K$. Every algebraic $*$-representation is
contractive on that algebra, so $\|T_a\|_{\max}\leq\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.
Summing proves the estimate. For a matching multiplier $D$ of norm at most
one, use the diagonal operator $W=(1-D^*D)^{1/2}$ and the identity
\[
 T^*T-(DT)^*(DT)=(WT)^*(WT)
\]
to obtain contractive left multiplication in every representation; use
adjoints for right multiplication. Sum over matchings for general $D$.
Finite-vertex corners are $C^*$-algebras of compact operators, so their
maximal and concrete norms agree; their closure is the stated ideal.

For a proper coarse map, choose a covering isometry using orthogonal
infinite-dimensional subspaces for its finite vertex fibers. Conjugation
preserves propagation control and compact entries, and universality extends
it to the maximal algebras. Two covering isometries $V_0,V_1$ are compared
in a doubled module by $(\cos\lambda V_0,\sin\lambda V_1)$.
For a fixed algebraic $T$ the conjugates have one common propagation bound
and vary continuously in operator norm. The proved estimate gives maximal
norm continuity; contractivity and density extend the homotopy to $A_Z$.
This also proves functoriality and that a cover of the identity induces the
identity on $K$-theory.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Transfer and sliding]\label{lem:analytic}
The projection $f$ induces a surjection $K_1(A_Y)\to K_1(A_B)$, and
\[
 (i_{+,A}f_A)_*=(i_{-,A}f_A)_*:K_1(A_Y)\to K_1(A_X).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a base vertex $u$, let $F_u=f^{-1}(u)$ and
$\xi_u=|F_u|^{-1/2}\sum_{y\in F_u}\delta_y$.
Let $V:H_B\to H_Y$ send $\delta_u\otimes h$ to $\xi_u\otimes h$.
Left multiplication by $\Gamma$ on all fibers gives finite-propagation
unitary multipliers of $A_Y$. Property~(T) gives a projection $P$ in this
maximal multiplier algebra onto invariant vectors, and finite-propagation
fiber-preserving multipliers $P_n\to P$ in multiplier norm.
Indeed, for the symmetric generators the Laplacian
$\Delta=1-|S|^{-1}\sum_{s\in S}s$ has spectrum
$\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,2]$ in the maximal group algebra for some
$\varepsilon>0$; $(1-\Delta/2)^n$ converges to its kernel projection.
Concretely, $P$ is $VV^*$.

Choose shortest horizontal transport $O_{uv}:\ell^2(F_v)\to\ell^2(F_u)$
within each $B_j$, reversing the choice for the reversed pair. Across
components use $O_{uv}=|\xi_u\rangle\langle\xi_v|$.
These maps intertwine left $\Gamma$ and preserve constants.
Define $L(T)_{uv}=O_{uv}\otimes T_{uv}$ for $T\in\C[B]$.
Matching decomposition bounds this operator; within components its
propagation is bounded by that of $T$. The finitely many cross-component
entries at any fixed propagation bound give another finite bound.
Thus $L(T)\in\C[Y]$, preserves adjoints, and commutes with $P$.

For fixed $T,T'$, the defect $L(T)L(T')-L(TT')$ has finite vertex support:
in large tori, paths of uniformly bounded length with the same endpoints
have the same seam totals, and their right transports compose because
$U_j,V_j$ commute in $Q_{2j}$. The defect kills constants. On its finite
support the maximal norm is the concrete norm, so $P_n$ times the defect
converges to zero in maximal norm. Multiplier convergence also gives the
limit $P$ times the defect; hence that product is zero. It follows that
\[
 \Phi(T)=PL(T)
\]
is a $*$-homomorphism $\C[B]\to A_Y$, extending to $A_B$ by universality.
If $J:H_Y\to H_B$ covers $f$, the operators $JP_nL(T)J^*$ have one common
base propagation bound and converge concretely to $JVT V^*J^*$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:norm} makes the convergence maximal. Therefore
$f_A\Phi=\Ad(JV)$; $JV$ covers the identity. This proves the surjection.

For sliding, put $s_{j,t}(u,q)=(u,\bar q,t)$ in $X_j$ and choose covering
isometries $D_{j,t}:H_{Y_j}\to H_{X_j}$. These maps are $1$-Lipschitz,
successive vertex images are distance one apart, and the isometries for
different heights have orthogonal ranges. If $T_j$ is the compression of
$T\in\C[Y]$ to $Y_j$, define
\[
 S_0(T)=\bigoplus_j\sum_{t=-3j}^{3j-1}D_{j,t}T_jD_{j,t}^*,\quad
 S_1(T)=\bigoplus_j\sum_{t=-3j}^{3j-1}D_{j,t+1}T_jD_{j,t+1}^*.
\]
Despite the unbounded number of copies, orthogonality bounds the operator
norm by $\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. Entries remain compact because each
component is finite; propagation is bounded by $\prop(T)$.
Their multiplicative defects are compact: compression fails to respect
products only in the finitely many components met by cross-component
entries. Thus $qS_0,qS_1$ are homomorphisms to
$A_X/\mathcal K(H_X)$.

Rotate each copy by
$W_{\lambda,j,t}=(\cos\lambda D_{j,t},\sin\lambda D_{j,t+1})$ in a doubled
target. All ranges are orthogonal. The rotation homomorphisms modulo
compacts have propagation at most $\prop(T)+1$ and satisfy
\[
 \|F_\lambda(T)-F_\nu(T)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq2\|T\|_{\mathrm{op}}|\lambda-\nu|.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:norm} and density make this a point-norm homotopy in the
maximal quotient. Hence $(qS_0)_*=(qS_1)_*$.
The two stacks are the same interior stack plus, respectively,
$q i_{-,A}f_A$ and $q i_{+,A}f_A$, modulo compacts. Orthogonal sums add
on $K_1$, so canceling the interior stack gives equality after $q_*$.
The six-term sequence and $K_1(\mathcal K)=0$ make $q_*$ injective on
$K_1(A_X)$, proving the required equality before quotienting.
\end{proof}

For clarity, the naturality used here applies to every $\beta(a)$.
On the Rips complex with its proper Wasserstein metric, regrouping an ample
dense-set module by support vertices identifies its Roe algebra with the
vertex Roe algebra, with propagation changed by at most $2r$.
For an odd Fredholm cycle $F$, barycentric localization
$F'=\sum_v\varphi_v F\varphi_v$ (where $\sum_v\varphi_v^2=1$) has finite
propagation and differs from $F$ by a locally compact operator.
It represents the same class. With $p=(1+F')/2$, the canonical odd maximal
index is $[\exp(2\pi i p)]$. Maximal Paschke duality and the boundary-map
description are established in \cite[Propositions 2.22, 2.27--2.28 and
Corollary 2.29]{SS}; finite-propagation index compatibility is also
\cite[Corollary 2.18]{OOY}.
For a proper continuous coarse inclusion covered by $V$, the pushed cycle
is $VF'V^*-(1-VV^*)$. Its exponential is
$1+V(\exp(2\pi i p)-1)V^*$, proving naturality in the maximal algebra.
All endpoint inclusions here are such simplicial maps at scale three.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:large}]
Given any $a$, use transfer to lift $\mu_B^{\max}(\beta(a))$ to
$b\in K_1(A_Y)$. Naturality and sliding give
\[
 \mu_X^{\max}D(a)
 =(i_{+,A}f_A)_*b-(i_{-,A}f_A)_*b=0.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:div} supplies the hypothesis of Lemma~\ref{lem:amplify}
with $M_{j,r}=2^{j-c_r}$; its thresholds depend on $r$, not on the integer
coefficient. Finite-support differences vanish after taking a scale at
least the diameters of their finitely many components: each such Rips
complex is a simplex and has zero odd $K$-homology. Thus the amplification
lemma gives the asserted injection. Tensoring this injection with $\Q$
preserves it by flatness, and Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} gives kernel dimension
at least $\co$.

For the upper bound, each $C_0(P_r(X))$ is separable. Fredholm cycles on
separable Hilbert spaces form a set of cardinality at most $\co$: a
representation is determined on a countable dense set by countably many
operators, each determined by countably many complex matrix entries.
The same bound holds for the operators defining the cycle and for their
quotient into $\KK$ classes. Integer scales are cofinal, so
$|\KX_1(X)|\leq\co$ and its rationalization has cardinality at most $\co$.
This proves exact dimension. The reduced quotient kills the same injected
classes and has the same domain, proving its assertion as well.
\end{proof}

\section{An elementary consequence for all stationary chains}

The following result has a proof independent of the coarse-assembly
construction. It improves and extends the mechanism of \cite{OAImarkov}.
All $L_1$ spaces in this section are real.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:stationary}
Let $P$ be any finite row-stochastic matrix, and let $\pi$ be any stationary
probability vector: $\pi P=\pi$. For every integer $t\geq1$ and
$x_1,\ldots,x_n\in L_1(\Omega,\mu)$ there exist $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ in that
space with
\begin{align*}
 &\sum_i\pi_i\|x_i-y_i\|_1^2
  +t\sum_{i,j}\pi_iP_{ij}\|y_i-y_j\|_1^2\\
 &\hspace{10mm}\leq\frac{224}{t}\sum_{s=1}^t
      \sum_{i,j}\pi_i(P^s)_{ij}\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2.
\end{align*}
No reversibility, irreducibility, aperiodicity, or positive lower bound on
$\pi_i$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[A sharp cube inequality]\label{lem:sharp}
On a finite weighted Hilbert space $H=\ell_2(w)$, put $Q=[0,1]^N$ and
\[
 \psi(r)=\begin{cases}2r^2,&0\leq r\leq\tfrac12,\\
                   1-2(1-r)^2,&\tfrac12\leq r\leq1.\end{cases}
\]
Let $\Psi$ act coordinatewise. For $e\in\{0,1\}^N$ and $u,u'\in Q$, define
\[
 R_e(u,u')=\|u'-e\|_H^4-\|u-e\|_H^4
       -4\|u-e\|_H^2\langle u-e,u'-u\rangle_H.
\]
Then
\[
 \|\Psi(u')-\Psi(u)\|_{\ell_1(w)}^2\leq8R_e(u,u').
\]
The constant $8$ is optimal among all $C^1$ scalar functions fixing zero
and one that are applied coordinatewise with a universal constant in this
inequality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $a=u-e$, $b=u'-e$, $\delta=b-a$, and
$A=\|a\|_H^2$, $d=\|\delta\|_H^2$, $r=\langle a,\delta\rangle_H$.
In every coordinate $a,b$ have the same sign. Since
$|\psi'(s)|=4\min(s,1-s)\leq4|s-e_k|$,
\[
 |\psi(u'_k)-\psi(u_k)|\leq2|b_k^2-a_k^2|
                        =2|\delta_k||a_k+b_k|.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the squared weighted $\ell_1$ norm at most
$4d\|a+b\|_H^2$. Direct expansion gives
\[
 R_e=2Ad+(2r+d)^2,
\quad
 2R_e-d\|a+b\|_H^2
 =8(r+d/4)^2+d^2/2\geq0.
\]
This proves the bound.

For optimality, suppose a $C^1$ scalar function $\eta$ fixing endpoints
satisfies the bound with constant $C$. In the unweighted two-dimensional
cube use $u=(s,s)$, $u'=(s+\varepsilon,s-\varepsilon)$.
For $e=(0,0)$, division by $\varepsilon^2$ and the limit
$\varepsilon\to0$ give $\eta'(s)^2\leq2Cs^2$.
For $e=(1,1)$ the same argument gives
$\eta'(s)^2\leq2C(1-s)^2$. Hence
\[
 1\leq\int_0^1|\eta'(s)|\,ds
 \leq\sqrt{2C}\int_0^1\min(s,1-s)\,ds=\frac{\sqrt{2C}}4.
\]
Thus $C\geq8$, attained by $\psi$. This optimality concerns this pointwise
quartic inequality, not the final constant $224$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:stationary}]
First realize the finite data exactly by weighted cuts. For each nonempty
proper $B\subset[n]$, set, pointwise on $\Omega$,
\[
 v=\min_i x_i,\quad
 b_B=\left(\min_{i\in B}x_i-\max_{i\notin B}x_i\right)_+,
 \quad w_B=\int b_B\,d\mu.
\]
All these functions are integrable; discard the cuts with $w_B=0$.
With $z_i(B)=1_{i\in B}$ and $T(u)=v+\sum_Bu_Bb_B$, ordering the finitely
many scalar values pointwise gives
\[
 T(z_i)=x_i,\qquad
 \|x_i-x_j\|_1=\|z_i-z_j\|_{\ell_1(w)}=\|z_i-z_j\|_H^2,
\quad
 \|T(u)-T(u')\|_1\leq\|u-u'\|_{\ell_1(w)}.
\]
There are at most $2^n-2$ cuts. If every weight is zero, take $y_i=x_i$.

Put $p=1/(t+1)$, $q=t/(t+1)$, and
\[
 h=p(I-qP)^{-1}z,\qquad y_i=T(\Psi(h_i)).
\]
The resolvent is a convex combination of powers of $P$, so $h_i\in Q$ and
$h_i=pz_i+q\sum_jP_{ij}h_j$.
Start a stationary $P$-chain $X_0,X_1,\ldots$. While alive at $i$, stop with
probability $p$, assigning terminal value $z_i$, or move to $j$ with
probability $qP_{ij}$, assigning value $h_j$. Once stopped keep the value
fixed. Its initial value is $h_{X_0}$ and this is a bounded martingale $M$.
Its terminal value is $z_{X_S}$, where $\Pr(S=s)=pq^s$ for $s\geq0$.
The center $e=z_{X_0}$ is measurable at time zero.

For $F_e(u)=\|u-e\|_H^4$, the derivative term in each martingale increment
has expectation zero. Summing Lemma~\ref{lem:sharp}, telescoping, and then
using bounded convergence and monotone convergence gives
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{m\geq1}\|\Psi(M_m)-\Psi(M_{m-1})\|_{\ell_1(w)}^2
 \leq8\mathbb E\|z_{X_S}-z_{X_0}\|_H^4.
\]
The omitted initial potential is nonnegative. Stationarity computes the
left side exactly as
\[
 \sum_i\pi_i\|z_i-\Psi(h_i)\|_{\ell_1(w)}^2
 +t\sum_{i,j}\pi_iP_{ij}\|\Psi(h_i)-\Psi(h_j)\|_{\ell_1(w)}^2,
\]
because $\sum_{m\geq0}pq^m=1$ and $\sum_{m\geq0}q^{m+1}=t$.
Endpoint fixing and contractivity of $T$ therefore prove the stronger
geometric-resolvent bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric}
 B+tE\leq8\sum_{s\geq0}pq^s D(s),\quad
 D(s)=\mathbb E\|x_{X_s}-x_{X_0}\|_1^2,
\end{equation}
where $B,E$ are the two sums in the theorem.

We finish with a stationary-chain comparison valid in any metric.
The triangle inequality, Minkowski, and stationarity give
$\sqrt{D(r+s)}\leq\sqrt{D(r)}+\sqrt{D(s)}$.
Writing $W=t^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^t D(s)$ and averaging
$D(t)\leq2D(s)+2D(t-s)$ gives $D(t)\leq4W$.
For $s=jt+r$ with $0\leq r<t$,
\[
 D(s)\leq2j^2D(t)+2D(r).
\]
For the geometric $S$, $J=\lfloor S/t\rfloor$ satisfies
$\mathbb EJ^2\leq\mathbb ES^2/t^2=2+1/t\leq3$.
Its residue has probabilities $pq^r/(1-q^t)\leq2/t$:
$q^t\leq1/2$ follows from $(1+1/t)^t\geq2$.
Thus $\mathbb ED(S)\leq2\cdot3\cdot4W+2\cdot2W=28W$.
Combining with \eqref{eq:geometric} proves $B+tE\leq224W$.
Reversibility is not used in any step.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Directed nonlinear spectral calculus]
For a stationary pair $(P,\pi)$ let $\gamma(P)$ be the least constant in
\[
 \sum_{i,j}\pi_i\pi_j\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2
 \leq\gamma(P)\sum_{i,j}\pi_iP_{ij}\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2
\]
for all finite data in the real $L_1$ space. If $\gamma(P)<\infty$, then
\[
 \gamma\left(\frac1t\sum_{s=1}^tP^s\right)
 \leq224\left(4+\frac{\gamma(P)}t\right).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the constructed $y$, write $V(x)=\sum\pi_i\pi_j\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2$.
Minkowski gives $\sqrt{V(x)}\leq2\sqrt B+\sqrt{V(y)}$
$\leq2\sqrt B+\sqrt{\gamma(P)E}$.
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $V(x)\leq(4+\gamma(P)/t)(B+tE)$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:stationary}. This is the one-bank Poincar\'e constant;
no two-bank absolute spectral statement is asserted.
\end{proof}

For rational data in $\ell_1^m$ the construction is exact and effective.
Compute $R_t=((t+1)I-tP)^{-1}$. In each coordinate $k$ sort the distinct
values $\alpha_1<\cdots<\alpha_r$, set
$B_l=\{j:x_j(k)\geq\alpha_{l+1}\}$, and use
\[
 y_i(k)=\alpha_1+\sum_{l=1}^{r-1}(\alpha_{l+1}-\alpha_l)
                   \psi\left(\sum_{j\in B_l}(R_t)_{ij}\right).
\]
The arithmetic cost is $O(n^3+mn^2+mn\log n)$, with polynomial rational
bit complexity. No finite algorithm is claimed for unspecified infinite
coordinates. The accompanying diagnostic script uses exact rational
arithmetic; its finite checks are separate from the proofs.

\section{Historical and verification limits}

The graph construction, its primitive endpoint class, congruence root,
maximal transfer, and sliding argument come from the model-generated
preprint \cite{OAIcoarse}. The additional results here are the injection
of $\mathcal B$, its exact rational kernel dimension, the stationary
extension, and Lemma~\ref{lem:sharp}. These were derived in this investigation;
that statement of provenance does not certify priority.

The traditional coarse-assembly problem appears in \cite{HR}.
Published maximal-norm and index foundations are \cite{OOY,SS}.
Earlier locally finite kernel examples in \cite{DFW} have a different
domain issue: their classes disappear under coarsening, as discussed in
that paper's Remark~6.2. The source's Rips-colimit obstruction is the
essential distinction. Theorem~\ref{thm:large} strengthens a kernel
already claimed in the source; it does not independently create its
historical counterexample.

No homomorphism preserving both this topological kernel and its index has
been constructed into a coefficient-free group assembly problem.
For example, graph containment in a Cayley graph would not establish
injectivity of the map on Rips-colimit $K$-homology.
There is also a precise obstruction to one proposed transfer:
Hanke--Schick \cite{HS} prove that a rational group $K$-homology class
pairing nontrivially with the subring generated by $H^1,H^2$ has nonzero
maximal group index. Thus a map preserving a nonzero such pairing and the
zero index cannot exist. This observation does not rule out all other
group transfers. Neither the group Novikov conjecture nor a higher-signature
counterexample is resolved here.

The release's Lean material for family~307 concerns its reduced companion
and a constructed assembly map, not the October~5 maximal result or
Theorem~\ref{thm:large}. Selected code was read, not rebuilt.
In particular, \texttt{separatedIndexFamily} has a zero-map fallback if
scale compatibility fails, but \texttt{SeparatedCoarseAssembly.lean}
proves \texttt{separatedIndexFamily\_atScale}, eliminating that fallback
for its finite-component setup using \texttt{separatedRipsIndex\_coarsen}.
The fallback alone therefore supplies no refutation. Identification of
the formal objects with the stated canonical theorem and the full
dependency proof remain outside this audit. No formal validation is
borrowed for the new claims.

For the second result, the source uses cubic cut flattening and asserts
metric Markov cotype two for $\ell_1$. The pointwise piecewise quadratic
bound above and its optimality have an independent elementary proof.
The original cut-smoothing literature \cite{CWX} also uses a cubic.
Metric Markov cotype and Lipschitz extension were connected in \cite{MN}.
However, Ball's 1992 $\ell_2$-to-$L_1$ extension question and the associated
$O(\sqrt{\log k})$ cut-sparsifier bound were already answered by Naor's
September~2026 preprint \cite{Naor}; they are not new consequences here.
Those priority checks eliminate an initially high-upside route.

Bounded primary-literature checks did not establish prior publication of
the two precise additions above. Absence from those checks is not proof
of novelty. External specialist review, formal replay, and a complete
priority investigation have not occurred. The stated 10/10 objective
therefore remains unmet; the complete smaller arguments are retained
without being promoted to a foundational resolution.
