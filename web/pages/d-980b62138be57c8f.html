## D1. Exact channel and primitive

Let Eη be the one-mode pure-loss channel, with vacuum environment. Its complement, up to an output unitary, is E(1−η). For η≥1/2 it is degradable:

\[
\widetilde E_\eta=E_{(1-\eta)/\eta}\circ E_\eta.
\tag{D1}
\]

For all finite n and finite-energy n-mode ρ, EPnI with the independent vacuum port gives

\[
S(E_\lambda^{\otimes n}\rho)\ge n f_\lambda(S(\rho)/n),
\quad f_\lambda(u)=g(\lambda g^{-1}(u)).\tag{D2}
\]

For 0<λ≤1, fλ is increasing, continuous and onto [0,∞). For 0<λ<1 it is convex. Writing h(r)=g'(r)=log(1+1/r), direct differentiation gives

\[
f_\lambda''(g(r))=
\frac{\lambda[(1+\lambda r)h(\lambda r)-(1+r)h(r)]}
{r(1+r)(1+\lambda r)h(r)^3}\ge0.
\tag{D3}
\]

Indeed t↦(1+t)h(t) has derivative h(t)−1/t<0. Continuity covers u=0, f1(u)=u and f0=0. This convexity, not just a bound on each one-mode marginal, is what permits an arbitrary entangled block code and nonuniform classical conditioning.

## D2. Four bounds on every block ensemble

Fix an arbitrary finite ensemble {pi,ρi} on n modes, with ρ=Σpiρi and total mean input photons≤nE. Every positive-probability member has finite energy. Let B_i=Eη^⊗n(ρi), F_i=E(1−η)^⊗n(ρi). Maximum entropy at fixed total energy and entropy concavity give

\[
S(E_\eta^{\otimes n}\rho)\le ng(\eta E),
\quad\sum_i p_i S(B_i)\le ng(\eta E).
\]

There is therefore a βn∈[0,1] with

\[
\sum_i p_iS(B_i)=ng(\beta_n\eta E).\tag{D4}
\]

The zero-energy case follows by continuity and consists only of vacuum input. Apply D2 to each input and Jensen to fη. Its monotone inverse gives

\[
\frac1n\sum_i p_iS(\rho_i)
\le f_\eta^{-1}(g(\beta_n\eta E))=g(\beta_n E).
\tag{D5}
\]

Apply D2 to each Bi through D1 and Jensen to f(1−η)/η:

\[
\frac1n\sum_i p_iS(F_i)
\ge f_{(1-\eta)/\eta}(g(\beta_n\eta E))
=g((1-\eta)\beta_n E).\tag{D6}
\]

Both bounds use the same βn, regardless of entanglement within ρi, block length or nonuniform probabilities. For countable conditioning with finite mean energy, truncate/condition and pass using the finite-energy entropy bound and Jensen; finite ensembles already suffice for the inspected regularized interface.

## D3. Classical/quantum/entanglement region

The named dynamic-coding service bounds each normalized block region by

\[
\begin{aligned}
n(C+2Q)&\le S(B)+\sum_i p_i[S(\rho_i)-S(F_i)],\\
n(Q+G)&\le\sum_i p_i[S(B_i)-S(F_i)],\\
n(C+Q+G)&\le S(B)-\sum_i p_iS(F_i).
\end{aligned}\tag{D7}
\]

Here C,Q≥0; G is signed net entanglement generation, so consumption is negative. Insert D4–D6 and the average-output entropy bound. For some common β∈[0,1], every achievable triple must satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
C+2Q&\le g(\beta E)+g(\eta E)-g((1-\eta)\beta E),\\
Q+G&\le g(\eta\beta E)-g((1-\eta)\beta E),\\
C+Q+G&\le g(\eta E)-g((1-\eta)\beta E).
\end{aligned}\tag{D8}
\]

For a sequence of blocks βn has a convergent subsequence in [0,1]. The inequalities survive rate limits by continuity. The same holds for closure points of the regularized union. There is no hidden replacement by product-state inputs in this step.

## D4. Public/private/key region

The inspected private-dynamic service uses pure inner ensemble states ρij, mixed conditionals ρi and mean ρ. Its first upper bound is S(B) minus a nonnegative average output entropy; its second and third bounds have the same differences as D7 with P,K in place of Q,G. Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
C+P&\le g(\eta E),\\
P+K&\le g(\eta\beta E)-g((1-\eta)\beta E),\\
C+P+K&\le g(\eta E)-g((1-\eta)\beta E)
\end{aligned}\tag{D9}
\]

for one β∈[0,1], with C,P≥0 and signed key generation K. The code's extra inner conditioning gives no loophole because D4–D6 apply to the mixed ρi. This is exactly De Palma's Theorem11 specialization/Corollary12 and the earlier Wilde–Hayden–Guha conditional optimality question.

## D5. Competent achievable control and what was acquired

The 2012 achievable construction takes a two-mode squeezed purification of a thermal state of mean βE and displaces its transmitted half by an isotropic Gaussian amplitude of variance (1−β)E. The average transmitted state is thermal of mean E. The four entropies required by D7 are exactly

\[
g(\eta E),\quad g(\beta E),\quad
g(\eta\beta E),\quad g((1-\eta)\beta E).
\tag{D10}
\]

Displacement covariance and thermal attenuation give these identities directly. The inspected prior coding theorem promotes them to the achievable D8 region and the companion private coding theorem to D9. Those quantum-coding services, finite-alphabet/Fock approximation, and energy-constrained limiting conventions remain the named prior resources. This audit does not provide an explicit finite block encoder/decoder, error exponent, gate count or rational amplitude compiler.

Thus the new theoretical capability is an exact all-block converse closing the previously conditional tradeoff regions, including classical–quantum and public–private slices. It is not a practical communication protocol newly created by us. The older linear qEPI gives weaker curved outer bounds in this application; it already gives the plain p=1 thermal relative-entropy contraction in our other note. Keeping those two comparisons separate avoids overstating the new entropy result.

## D6. Physical and computational nontransfer
