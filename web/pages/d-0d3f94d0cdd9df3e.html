An explicit failure of complex affine-space cancellation
OpenAI
September 23, 2026
Abstract
We construct an explicit integral complex affine fourfoldX with X×A1∼= A5 but
X̸∼= A4. This gives a negative answer to Zariski’s affine-space cancellation problem over
C in dimension four. The same construction disproves the Stable Coordinate Conjecture
in ambient dimension five and yields smoothA3-fibrations over A1 and A2 that are not
Zariski-locally trivial. These fibrations disprove the Dolgachev–Weisfeiler affine-fibration
conjecture over these bases.
1 Introduction
The Zariski cancellation problem asks whether an affine variety is determined by its cylinder
when that cylinder is affine space. Over the complex numbers, its algebraic formulation is the
implication
A[w]≃C[n+1] =⇒A≃C[n],(1.1)
where A is a finitely generated commutative unital complex algebra,w is an independent
polynomial variable, and all isomorphisms fix the complex scalars. HereC[r] denotes a polynomial
ring inr variables over a ringC. The hypothesis says that adjoining one variable turnsA into a
polynomial ring; the question is whether that variable can be cancelled. An isomorphism of
cylinders need not respect the given projection to the affine line, so its existence alone does not
identify polynomial coordinates onA.
Cancellation for the affine line follows from the work of Abhyankar, Heinzer, and Eakin [1,
Corollary (2.8)]. Cancellation for the complex affine plane was established through the work of
Fujita, Miyanishi, and Sugie [10, 21], using the structure of affine surfaces and cylinderlike open
sets. In positive characteristic, Asanuma’s work on polynomial fiber rings supplied threefolds
whose one-variable cylinders are affine four-space [2]. Gupta proved that suitable members of
this family are not affine three-space over their ground field [12], then established failure of
cancellation in every dimension at least three over every field of positive characteristic [13]. The
latter result uses a separate family of examples: adjoining variables to a threefold whose first
cylinder is already polynomial would not produce higher-dimensional counterexamples.
The characteristic-zero problem in dimensions at least three is still listed as open in Gaifullin–
Petrov’s July 2026 preprint [11, p. 1]. Gupta’s survey [14] explains its history and its connections
with coordinates, polynomial fibrations, and the recognition of affine space. We give a negative
answer to(1.1) over C for n = 4. The defining polynomial also gives a negative answer to the
Stable Coordinate Conjecture overC in ambient dimension five, already after adjoining one
variable.
