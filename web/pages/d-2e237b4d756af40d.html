1. LEGAL INTERFACE AND EXACT CONCLUSION

Let G be the original nearest-neighbor graph on
  V = {0,...,m} x {0,...,n}, m,n >= 1.
Every outer-boundary vertex is pinned. The pins are +1/2 on one nonempty
boundary arc and -1/2 on its complementary nonempty arc. There are exactly
two boundary edges whose endpoint pins have opposite signs, called a and b.
The free heights have uniform Lebesgue law on
  K = {h : |h_u-h_v| <= 1 for every original NN edge uv,
            h_boundary = the specified pins}.
There are no diagonal constraints and no constraints coming from a chosen
affine interpolation. The free polytope is bounded and full dimensional:
free heights all zero make every constraint incident to a free vertex
strict. Thus free heights are nonzero almost surely. Empty free sets are
interpreted as a trivial zero-dimensional case.

There is a deterministic sign-only contour Gamma with these properties.

(i) Gamma is a simple noncrossing embedded crosscut of the rectangle, from
a's midpoint to b's midpoint. It crosses only original NN edges, at their
midpoints, and uses a fixed deterministic resolution of a checkerboard face.
It is a simple path in the PORT-RESOLVED dual graph. On contracting each
face to the ordinary square-dual center it is a NN dual trail; that trail
need not be vertex-simple. The resolution convention is part of the theorem.

(ii) Its geometric sides V_+(Gamma), V_-(Gamma) are determined by Gamma and
the pinned arcs, independently of unrevealed signs. Every crossed edge has
one positive-sign endpoint on V_+ and one negative-sign endpoint on V_-.
The set of crossed edges is exactly the original NN edge cut between these
two vertex sets. Deleting it separates the sides. Each side can contain
several primal graph components, and its bulk signs need not be constant.

(iii) For each admissible completed path gamma, there are sets C_+(gamma),
C_-(gamma) of its positive/negative cut endpoints, and a set T_-(gamma)
contained in V_-(gamma), such that its complete event is exactly
  {Gamma=gamma} = {h_p>0 for p in C_+,
                  h_q<0 for q in C_- union T_-}.                 (1.1)
Pins in these sets have their prescribed signs already. In particular there
is NO extra path-induced sign restriction on a positive-side free vertex
outside C_+. The path observation does not include the unused contour
loops, whether a visited face is checkerboard, or extra height magnitudes.

(iv) After conditioning on gamma and ALL negative-side heights, the positive
law factors exactly into uniform component polytopes for the original NN
constraints, positive boundary pins +1/2, and the cut walls
  0 <= h_p <= alpha_p <= 1, p in C_+ free.                         (1.2)
Only the cut-adjacent positive free vertices get these walls. All other
positive-side free vertices have no path-induced sign wall. Every such cut
vertex p has an opposite-sign original NN cut partner. Factorization is
conditional on the outside heights; it is generally false given only gamma.

(v) For every x in V_+(gamma),
  E[h_x | Gamma=gamma] <= 1/2.                                   (1.3)
This includes all free vertices of that geometric side, even vertices in
a primal component disconnected from the positive pinned arc after cutting.
The comparison raises only the upper endpoints in (1.2) to 1, leaves their
lower endpoints 0 unchanged, and leaves all other walls unchanged. It does
not use a generic assertion that arbitrary interval enlargement is monotone.

2. THE CANONICAL NEGATIVE-CORNER RESOLUTION

Put a port at the midpoint of each original edge. In each square face count
its sign-changing edges. The number is 0, 2, or 4.
  0: no face arc.
  2: join their two ports by the straight chord in that face.
  4: signs alternate. Pair the two ports incident to each NEGATIVE corner,
     using the two disjoint straight chords that cut off those corners.
For a given adjacent port pair its geometric chord is identical whether
the face has two or four sign changes. Observing a chord therefore does
not itself reveal the presence of the unused checkerboard chord.

Across an interior edge, the matching chords on its two incident faces join
at the port. On a boundary edge there is one incident face. The port graph
has degree two at every interior used port; its only degree-one ports are
a and b, because those are the only sign-changing boundary edges. It has
no crossing inside a face: the two checkerboard chords are disjoint.

A finite graph with all degrees two except these two degree-one vertices
has a unique path component joining them, and all its other components are
cycles. Take Gamma to be precisely that open component, oriented so that
the positive pinned arc is on its left. A traversal from a never repeats
a port: a repeat would enter a cycle whose vertices already have degree
two, leaving no attachment for the degree-one starting vertex. The face
arcs are disjoint apart from their joined port endpoints. Hence the open
component is a simple embedded piecewise-linear curve. It meets the outer
boundary only at a and b, so is a proper Jordan crosscut.

This is a canonical deterministic rule once the sign field and marked
arcs are specified. Reversing the traversal changes no unparametrized path.
Its positive-side locality is asymmetric under exchanging signs; the
negative-corner choice is essential for the result we seek.

One can explore just this open strand without revealing a loop collection.
At an incoming edge AB, label its positive endpoint A and its negative
endpoint B; list face corners cyclically as A,B,C,D. Query C first.
  C positive: exit across BC, regardless of D.
  C negative: query D; if D positive, exit across CD;
             if D negative, exit across DA.
Thus all positive signs queried by this rule occur at used cut endpoints.
A fourth-corner query not incident to the used ports is negative and is on
the negative side. The following path-event proof does not require that an
exploration history be retained as part of the completed-path observation.

3. GEOMETRIC SIDES AND THE EXACT PATH EVENT

Within each traversed chord the cut endpoints labelled positive lie on one
local side and those labelled negative on the other. This orientation is
consistent when arcs join across a port, since the shared original edge has
the same endpoint signs in both faces. It agrees at the marks with the two
pinned boundary arcs. The Jordan crosscut separates the rectangle into
two geometric sides; every original vertex belongs to one of them.
Use the positive pinned boundary arc to name V_+, and the other arc to
name V_-. The local endpoint labels just described coincide with those
global geometric sides.

An original edge not used by Gamma does not meet it and stays in one side.
An interior used edge meets it once at its midpoint, crossing from one side
to the other. A used boundary transition edge has endpoints on the two
different boundary arcs. It follows that
  CUT(gamma) = {uv in E(G): u in V_+(gamma), v in V_-(gamma)}.       (3.1)
In particular no remaining edge joins the two sides, and no primal route
can go around the marked endpoints. Side labels are geometric, not a
claim that every height in V_+ is positive or every height in V_- negative.

For a path gamma that occurs for at least one sign field, let C_+ and C_-
be its cut endpoints in their respective geometric sides. This definition
uses gamma alone. It is not a conditional label taken from a new sign field.
Define T_- as follows: whenever gamma turns between two adjacent ports
whose common original corner belongs to C_+, include the opposite, fourth
corner of that face. These are its positive-corner turns. The chord cuts
off the positive common corner, so the fourth corner is on V_-. A face
can be visited twice; then all its four ports and all four original corners
are cut-adjacent, and both passages are around negative corners. Repeated
faces require no fourth-corner test outside the cut endpoints.

Here is the complete local verification of (1.1).

A. Opposite-port passage. Its two used edges have four distinct endpoints.
Their C_+/C_- signs force exactly two sign changes in the face. The prescribed
chord is the unique pairing of those two ports.

B. Turn around a negative common corner. The three used-edge endpoints
have signs +,-,+. If the fourth corner is positive there are exactly two
sign changes and their ports are paired. If that corner is negative the
face is checkerboard, and the negative-corner rule pairs the same two ports.
Thus this local passage imposes no sign test on its fourth corner. If gamma
has only this one passage in the face, that corner is on the geometric
positive side. A possible unused chord cutting off a negative island there
is not part of gamma. If gamma also has the second passage, that fourth
corner is itself in C_-, and its sign was already a cut-endpoint condition.

C. Turn around a positive common corner. The three used-edge endpoints
have signs -,+,-. The fourth corner MUST be negative. A negative fourth
corner gives the two required sign changes and this turn. A positive fourth
corner gives a checkerboard whose negative-corner pairing does not contain
this turn. This is exactly the T_- sign condition, on the negative side.

D. Two passages in one face. Every corner is a cut endpoint. The four cut
signs are the checkerboard signs and their negative-corner pairing is forced.

Necessity of (1.1) follows from this table and the cut signs. For sufficiency,
take ANY other sign field obeying the right side of (1.1). All the pairings
used by gamma, in every visited face, are preserved by A--D. Every gamma
port therefore has exactly the same neighbors along gamma as before.
The starting and terminal marked ports remain the only degree-one ports.
Unobserved arcs or unvisited faces can change, but cannot attach a new arc
to the already saturated gamma ports. Thus the unique open component is
still precisely gamma. This proves the equivalence for all sign fields,
not merely for the sampled rectangles.

An immediate counterfactual check is that changing any single sign of an
uncut positive-side free vertex, holding all other signs fixed, preserves
gamma. This assertion concerns the sign contour; after fixing actual outside
heights, the original Lipschitz inequalities can of course restrict the
feasible heights. The claim is absence of an extra path-event indicator,
not removal of the original physical constraints.

4. EXACT CONDITIONAL DENSITY AND THE FACTORIZATION QUALIFICATION

Fix an admissible gamma. Each such path has positive probability: choose
free heights +1/10 or -1/10 according to one producing sign field. All edges
incident to a free vertex then have strict Lipschitz inequalities, and all
these signs persist in an open free-coordinate neighborhood.

Condition further on all free heights in V_-(gamma), denoting them z; original
pins are constants. Fubini disintegration of the finite Lebesgue density
is enough; no abstract spatial-Markov assumption is needed. For almost
every z under this conditional marginal, the positive free-coordinate fiber
has positive volume. In the path event (1.1) all conditions on C_- and T_-
become fixed indicators. The only positive-coordinate sign conditions are
h_p>0 for p in C_+ free.

For a cut partner q in C_- we have z_q<0, including z_q=-1/2 when it is a pin.
The original cut constraint is z_q-1 <= h_p <= z_q+1. Under h_p>0 its lower
inequality is automatic. At each free p in C_+ the remaining upper bound is
  alpha_p(z) = min_{q: pq in CUT(gamma)} (1+z_q).                 (4.1)
It is <=1. It is >0 on every positive-volume fiber. The weak form 0<=h_p
differs from the strict sign inequality only on a null coordinate hyperplane.
Constraints on cut edges with a pinned positive endpoint are constants in z.

Consequently the conditional positive density is exactly proportional to
  product_{uv internal to V_+} 1{|h_u-h_v|<=1}
  product_{p in C_+ free} 1{0<=h_p<=alpha_p(z)},                 (4.2)
with positive boundary pins +1/2. There is no factor 1{h_x>=0} at an uncut
positive-side vertex, no extra diagonal edge, and no revealed-loop factor.

The factors in (4.2) split over components of the positive free-vertex graph
(pinned boundary values can be included as fixed incident boundary data).
Their reference measures are products of Lebesgue measures, and their
normalization is a product of component volumes. This proves exact conditional
independence and uniform component kernels for almost every z. It also covers
several positive primal components created by passages through a resolved
checkerboard face. No connectivity to the original positive pinned arc is
assumed for every one of these components.

For completeness, after freezing all cut-endpoint heights A=C_+ union C_-,
both sides factor over remaining free components, but negative components
can still have the local sign walls on T_- not already in A. If pure ordinary
pinned kernels on BOTH sides are wanted, freeze also the heights of T_-.
Then every indicator in (1.1) is fixed, every crossed edge has both endpoints
fixed, and the remaining density is precisely the product of the original
NN Gibbs component densities. This is an exact DLR formulation with all
revealed data accounted for. Freezing A alone and silently omitting a residual
negative T_- wall would be incorrect for this particular square routing rule.

Conditioning on gamma ALONE generally does not factor positive and negative
heights: the unfrozen cut inequalities still couple the sides. An explicit
positive-volume counterexample to that stronger interpretation is in Section 7.

5. THE REQUIRED ONE-SIDED WALL COMPARISON, PROVED IN FINITE DIMENSION

We need only the following finite wall fact. On a finite original NN graph,
fix some pins at +1/2. Some free vertices have walls [0,alpha_p], 0<alpha_p<=1.
Other free vertices have no such walls. Suppose every free component has
an incident pin or one of these walled vertices, and the old feasible set
has positive volume in all its free coordinates. The uniform feasible law
with these walls is stochastically dominated, for coordinate expectations,
by the law obtained by replacing ONLY alpha_p with 1. The lower wall 0 stays
the same, as do all pins and internal unit-Lipschitz constraints.

Here is an elementary proof, including the ergodicity issue.

Every component is bounded by an anchor: a pin has value 1/2; a walled vertex
has value in [0,1]. Traveling along at most |V|-1 internal edges bounds all
heights in a common interval [-M,1+M], with an integer M>=|V|. Add that same
box to both polytopes; it does not affect either feasible set. It also is
invariant under h -> 1-h.

Use a lattice mesh delta=1/N with N even, so all fixed pins are lattice points.
For sufficiently large N the old full-dimensional polytope contains a lattice
point. Its lattice feasible set is contained in the new one. A one-site
conditional law on either finite lattice set is uniform on a consecutive
integer interval. The lower endpoint is the maximum of its fixed lower walls
and all neighbor heights minus 1; its upper endpoint is the minimum of its
fixed upper walls and all neighbor heights plus 1. Rounding to the lattice
preserves the monotonicity of both endpoint formulas.

At ordered states h<=g, the old conditional lower endpoint is <= the new
one, and the old conditional upper endpoint is <= the new one. Raising the
specified upper walls is used for the latter comparison; it is not used to
lower a lower wall. Uniform distributions on integer intervals whose two
endpoints are ordered have ordered inverse-CDF quantiles. Updating the same
random coordinate using the same uniform number thus preserves h<=g. Start
the two random-site heat-bath chains from the same feasible old lattice point.

Each such finite chain is irreducible. The coordinatewise minimum m of all
its feasible lattice states is itself feasible: coordinatewise minimum
preserves every interval wall, pin, and unit-Lipschitz edge constraint. Given
a state h!=m, choose i with h_i>m_i and try lowering h_i by delta. Fixed lower
walls or incident pins cannot block the move because h_i-delta>=m_i. Upper
walls and the upper direction of each edge constraint get easier. If a free
neighbor j blocks the lower edge inequality, the lattice spacing and the
unit edge capacity imply h_j=h_i+1. Feasibility of m gives
  m_j <= m_i+1 <= h_i+1-delta = h_j-delta.
Thus j also lies above m_j. Follow a blocking neighbor. Its height increases
strictly by 1 at each step, so this process cannot cycle in the finite graph.
It ends at a coordinate that can be lowered by delta. Repeating decreases
the nonnegative integer N sum_i(h_i-m_i) to zero. Every state reaches m by
allowed single-coordinate moves; their reverses are allowed as well. Each
heat-bath move has positive probability and every state has a positive
holding probability. The chain is therefore irreducible and aperiodic.

Uniform measure on its finite feasible set is stationary: each one-site
update resamples the exact uniform conditional interval. Hence the coupled
chains converge in both marginals to their uniform lattice laws, and their
ordered coupling gives the claimed expectation inequality on the lattice.

Normalized lattice sums converge to normalized Lebesgue integrals as N tends
to infinity through even integers: the polytopes are bounded, full dimensional,
and their boundaries are contained in finitely many affine hyperplanes.
Coordinate functions are continuous and bounded on the common box. Passing
to this limit proves the continuous expectation comparison. Closing strict
cut sign walls at zero changes no continuous law.

6. APPLICATION OF THE COMPARISON AND THE MEAN BOUND

Every positive free component in (4.2) has an anchor of the type in Section 5.
Indeed, if it had neither a positive boundary-pin neighbor nor a cut wall,
it would have no edge to its complement in the original connected graph,
contrary to the nonempty pinned boundary. An edge from this component to
the negative side is a cut edge by (3.1), so its positive endpoint is walled.

For almost every fixed z, raise each alpha_p(z) in (4.1) to 1 and leave its
lower endpoint at 0. Leave uncut bulk vertices without a sign wall. The new
component polytope has fixed pins +1/2, cut walls [0,1], and the original NN
constraints. The simultaneous reflection
  h_v -> 1-h_v for every vertex in that component
preserves its density and pins. Thus every coordinate in the reference
component has mean 1/2, including vertices not themselves cut-adjacent.
The wall comparison gives
  E[h_x | Gamma=gamma, h_{V_-}=z] <= 1/2
for every positive-side free x. Pins on that side equal 1/2 already.
Integrating over the conditional negative-side heights proves (1.3).

The reference law has cut walls [0,1], not a [0,1] wall at every bulk vertex.
Such global bulk walls would change the law and are unnecessary. Conditioning
on positive cut heights individually would also not justify a 1/2 bound at
each such fixed coordinate; the comparison keeps those coordinates free.

7. EXACT COUNTERCONTROLS TO STRONGER INTERPRETATIONS

7A. Path-only factorization is false even for a straight interface.
Take m=n=4. Pin the right boundary arc positive and the left arc negative:
on top and bottom, x>=3 is +1/2 and x<=2 is -1/2; right/left edges have the
corresponding constant signs. Give all free vertices x=3 height +1/10 and
all free vertices x<=2 height -1/10. Gamma is the straight vertical crosscut
through the edges ((2,y),(3,y)), y=0,...,4.

Let p=(3,2), q=(2,2). In configuration A replace h_p by 17/20, leave h_q=-1/10,
and keep all other free heights as above. In configuration B replace h_q
by -17/20, leave h_p=1/10, and keep the other free heights as above. Both
configurations obey every original constraint, with every constraint incident
to a free vertex strict; the largest such difference is 19/20. Their signs
and their Gamma are identical.

Consequently under the law conditioned on this Gamma,
  P(h_p in (4/5,9/10) | Gamma)>0,
  P(h_q in (-9/10,-4/5) | Gamma)>0.
Both have open positive-volume feasible neighborhoods. But the joint event
has probability zero, since it would imply h_p-h_q>8/5>1 on their NN edge.
The positive and negative marginal laws cannot be independent. Deleting
an edge in a conditional-density description requires first accounting for
its outside endpoint; it does not erase an unfrozen physical inequality.

7B. The unresolved dual-center trail need not be vertex-simple.
The retained exact 3x3-face sample has free order
  (1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(2,2)
and signs +,-,-,-, with the right-arc pin convention of the finite protocol.
Its open resolved strand uses two disjoint passages of face (1,0), so the
ordinary dual-center contraction visits that center twice. Full ordered
crossed edges, face passages, sides, and physical realizability are retained
in repeated_center_control.json and the complete sign catalogue.
This falsifies vertex simplicity for OUR canonical rule after contraction.
It does not prove that every other rule with such a stronger property fails.
The strict ordinary-dual vertex-simple strengthened gate remains UNKNOWN.

7C. Observing extra contour information can destroy the desired locality.
In a passage around a negative common corner, its fourth corner lies on the
positive geometric side if this is the only Gamma passage in that face.
That fourth sign is free in (1.1). Revealing that this face is checkerboard,
or revealing its unused loop arc, would force that corner negative. This
extra observation is deliberately absent from the completed-path variable.
Likewise pairing checkerboard ports around POSITIVE corners instead would
make the same negative-corner turn require a positive fourth sign. The local
table is a diagnostic of that incompatible choice, not a claim about a
separately specified global exploration or a physical interpolation law.

binary interior sign assignment was examined in the declared increasing-y,

This check is only a bounded combinatorial falsification control. The general
