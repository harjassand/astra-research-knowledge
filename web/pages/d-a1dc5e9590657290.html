Abstract
We give an explicit characterisation of the quantum states which saturate the
strong subadditivity inequality for the von Neumann entropy.
By combining a result of Petz characterising the equality case for the
monotonicity of relative entropy with a recent theorem by Koashi and Imoto,
we show that such states will have the form of a so–called
short quantum Markov chain, which in turn implies that two of the systems
are independent conditioned on the third, in a physically meaningful sense.
This characterisation simultaneously generalises known necessary and
sufficient entropic conditions for quantum error correction as well as
the conditions for the achievability of the Holevo bound on accessible
information.
Keywords: Entropy, strong subadditivity, invariant algebras, quantum Markov state

I Introduction
The von Neumann entropy [13]
S⁡(ρ)=−Tr⁡ρ​log⁡ρ,S(\rho)=-{\operatorname{Tr}}\rho\log\rho,
of a density operator
ρ\rho on a finite dimensional Hilbert space ℋ{\cal H}
shares many properties with its classical counterpart,
the Shannon entropy
H(P)=−∑x∈𝒳P(x)logP(x)H(P)=-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P(x)\log P(x)
of a probability distribution PP on a discrete set 𝒳{\mathcal{X}}.
(All logarithms in this work are understood to be to base 22. Also, we will use
the terms “state” and “density operator” interchangeably.)
For example, both are nonnegative, and equal to 00 if and only if the
state (distribution) is an extreme point in the set of all
states (distributions), i.e. if ρ\rho is pure (PP is a point mass).
Both are concave and, moreover, both are subadditive: for a state
ρA​B\rho_{AB} on a composite system ℋA⊗ℋB{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}
with reduced states
ρA=TrB⁡(ρA​B),ρB=TrA⁡(ρA​B),\rho_{A}={\operatorname{Tr}}_{B}\bigl(\rho_{AB}\bigr),\quad\rho_{B}={\operatorname{Tr}}_{A}\bigl(\rho_{AB}\bigr),
it holds that
S⁡(ρA​B)≤S⁡(ρA)+S⁡(ρB).S(\rho_{AB})\leq S(\rho_{A})+S(\rho_{B}).
A directly analogous inequality holds for a distribution over a
product set and its marginals.
(Many more properties of SS are collected in the review by
Wehrl [26] and in the monograph [14].)
We shall view von Neumann entropy as a generalisation of Shannon
entropy [19]
in the following precise way: if the set 𝒳{\mathcal{X}} labels
an orthonormal basis {|x⟩:x∈𝒳}\bigl\{{|{x}\rangle}:x\in{\mathcal{X}}\bigr\} of ℋX{\cal H}_{X}
we can construct the state
ρP=∑xP⁡(x)​|x⟩​⟨x|\rho_{P}=\sum_{x}P(x){{|{x}\rangle}\!{\langle{x}|}}
corresponding to the distribution PP. This clearly defines an affine linear
map from distributions into states.
It is then straightforward to check that
S⁡(ρP)=H⁡(P),S(\rho_{P})=H(P),
so all properties of von Neumann entropy of a single system also
hold for Shannon entropy of a single distribution.
Similarly, for a distribution PP on a cartesian product 𝒳×𝒴{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}},
we use the tensor product basis
{|xy⟩=|x⟩⊗|y⟩:x∈𝒳,y∈𝒴}\bigl\{{|{xy}\rangle}={|{x}\rangle}\otimes{|{y}\rangle}:x\in{\mathcal{X}},y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\bigr\}
to define the state ρP\rho_{P} on ℋX⊗ℋY{\cal H}_{X}\otimes{\cal H}_{Y}.
Again, it is straightforward to check that reduced states correspond to
taking marginals:
TrY⁡(ρP)=ρP|𝒳,TrX⁡(ρP)=ρP|𝒴.{\operatorname{Tr}}_{Y}\bigl(\rho_{P}\bigr)=\rho_{P|_{{\mathcal{X}}}},\quad{\operatorname{Tr}}_{X}\bigl(\rho_{P}\bigr)=\rho_{P|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}}.
Hence all entropy relations for bipartite states also hold
for bipartite distributions.
In [9] Lieb and Ruskai proved the remarkable relation
S⁡(ρA​B)+S⁡(ρB​C)≥S⁡(ρA​B​C)+S⁡(ρB),S(\rho_{AB})+S(\rho_{BC})\geq S(\rho_{ABC})+S(\rho_{B}),
(1)
with a tripartite state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} on the system
ℋA⊗ℋB⊗ℋC{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}\otimes{\cal H}_{C}. It clearly generalises
the previous subadditivity relation, which is recovered for a
trivial system BB: ℋB=ℂ{\cal H}_{B}={\mathbb{C}}. In fact, this inequality plays a
crucial role in nearly every nontrivial
insight in quantum information theory, from the famous
Holevo bound [5] and the properties of the coherent
information [3, 18] to the recently proved additivity
of capacity for entanglement–breaking channels [20].
The present investigation aims to resolve the problem of characterising
the states which satisfy this relation with equality: the main result
is theorem 6.
Roughly speaking, the strong subadditivity inequality expresses the fact
that discarding a subsystem of a quantum system is a dissipative operation,
in the sense that it can only destroy correlations with the rest of the world.
Our work, therefore, can be interpreted as providing a detailed description
of the conditions under which the act of discarding a quantum system can be
locally reversed on a particular input.
We restrict ourselves to finite dimensional systems in
this paper. The question of whether a similar result holds in infinite
dimension is left open.
The rest of the paper is organised as follows.
In section II we will review the
case of probability distributions: there the solution to our problem
is easy to obtain, and in fact well–known. This will provide the
intuitive basis for understanding our main result.
After that, in section III we review quantum relative
entropy and the relation of its monotonicity property
to the strong subadditivity inequality.
Section IV presents a condition given by Petz for
equality in the monotonicity of relative entropy, while
section V presents and proves our main result,
a structure theorem for states which satisfy strong subadditivity
with equality. An essential step is the application of a recent
result of Koashi and Imoto [7] for which we give a
short but non–constructive algebraic proof in the appendix.
In section VI, we show how the entropic conditions
for quantum error correction as well as the conditions for saturation
in the Holevo bound follow as easy corollaries from our structure theorem.
The question of characterising the equality case of strong subadditivity
as well as of the monotonicity of relative entropy was considered in
earlier work by Petz [15],
where it was related to the existence of quantum operations with
certain properties. Ruskai [17] has given a
characterisation in terms of an operator equality, which can be
used to show that the states described in our main theorem 6
are indeed equality cases (as she has informed us, this was pointed out to
her by M. A. Nielsen after [17] appeared).
Neither of these results is as explicit as one could wish for, however, because
while both give algebraic criteria which one can check on any given state,
they do not yield a simple description of all the states that satisfy equality.
This simple description is exactly what our theorem 6 provides.
II The classical case
Let us first look at the classical case of probability distributions
and their Shannon entropies. The exposition is most conveniently
phrased in terms of random variables denoted A,B,CA,B,C, taking
values in 𝒜,ℬ,𝒞{\cal A,B,C}, respectively, with a joint
distribution
PA​B​C​(a,b,c)=Pr⁡{A=a,B=b,C=c}.P_{ABC}(a,b,c)=\Pr\{A=a,B=b,C=c\}.
The distribution of AA is the marginal PA=PA​B​C|𝒜P_{A}=P_{ABC}|_{\cal A}
of the joint distribution to 𝒜{\cal A} and similarly for the other variables.
Shannon [19] defined the mutual information
I⁡(A:B)=H⁡(A)+H⁡(B)−H⁡(A​B),I(A:B)=H(A)+H(B)-H(AB),
with H⁡(A)=H⁡(PA)H(A)=H(P_{A}) and so on. It is not hard to show
that I⁡(A:B)≥0I(A:B)\geq 0 with equality if and only
if AA and BB are independent.
Conditional mutual information is defined as
I(A:C|B)=∑b∈ℬPB(b)I(A:C|B=b),I(A:C|B)=\sum_{b\in{\cal B}}P_{B}(b)I(A:C|B=b),
where I(A:C|B=b)I(A:C|B=b) is the mutual information between the
variables AA and CC conditional on the event “B=bB=b”,
i.e. I⁡(A|B=b:C|B=b)I(A|_{B=b}:C|_{B=b}), with
Pr⁡{A|B=b=a,C|B=b=c}=PA​B​C​(a,b,c)PB​(b)=:PA​C|B​(a,c|b).\begin{split}\Pr\bigl\{A|_{B=b}=a,C|_{B=b}=c\bigr\}&=\frac{P_{ABC}(a,b,c)}{P_{B}(b)}\\
&=:P_{AC|B}(a,c|b).\end{split}
It is straightforward to check that with these definitions one has the
chain rule
I(A:BC)=I(A:B)+I(A:C|B).I(A:BC)=I(A:B)+I(A:C|B).
(2)
This implies the formula
I(A:C|B)=H(AB)+H(BC)−H(ABC)−H(B).I(A:C|B)=H(AB)+H(BC)-H(ABC)-H(B).
Because the left hand side is by definition a convex combination
of mutual informations, each of which is always nonnegative, we obtain strong
subadditivity for classical distributions.
Theorem 1
I(A:C|B)=0I(A:C|B)=0 if and only if AA and CC are conditionally
independent given BB, meaning
∀b​ s.t. ​PB​(b)≠0A|B=b,C|B=b​ are independent.\forall b\text{ s.t. }P_{B}(b)\neq 0\quad A|_{B=b},C|_{B=b}\text{ are independent.}
This is the case if and only if
PA​B​C​(a,b,c)=PB​(b)​PA|B​(a|b)​PC|B​(c|b)=PA​(a)​PB|A​(b|a)​PC|B​(c|b),\begin{split}P_{ABC}(a,b,c)&=P_{B}(b)P_{A|B}(a|b)P_{C|B}(c|b)\\
&=P_{A}(a)P_{B|A}(b|a)P_{C|B}(c|b),\end{split}
(3)
i.e. iff AA—BB—CC is a Markov chain in this order.
Proof . Clearly, the conditions are sufficient. Assume conversely that
I(A:C|B)=0I(A:C|B)=0. By definition of the latter quantity, this implies
that for all bb with PB​(b)≠0P_{B}(b)\neq 0, I(A:C|B=b)=0I(A:C|B=b)=0.
But this implies independence of A|B=bA|_{B=b} and C|B=bC|_{B=b}.
Hence, eq. (3) follows:
PA​B​C​(a,b,c)=PB​(b)​PA​C|B​(a,c|b)=PB​(b)​PA|B​(a|b)​PC|B​(c|b)=PA​(a)​PB|A​(b|a)​PC|B​(c|b).\begin{split}P_{ABC}(a,b,c)&=P_{B}(b)P_{AC|B}(a,c|b)\\
&=P_{B}(b)P_{A|B}(a|b)P_{C|B}(c|b)\\
&=P_{A}(a)P_{B|A}(b|a)P_{C|B}(c|b).\end{split}
□\Box
The remainder of the paper is devoted to describing the
quantum mechanical generalisation of this
equivalence between zero conditional mutual information, conditional
independence,
the Markov property and the factorization of the joint distribution given
in eq. (3).
III Relative entropy
Our approach to saturation of the strong subaddivity inequality will
be via the quantum relative entropy;
this quantity was defined by Umegaki [25] for two
quantum states ρ\rho and σ\sigma as
S(ρ∥σ)=Tr(ρ(logρ−logσ))S(\rho\|\sigma)={\operatorname{Tr}}\bigl(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\bigr)
if the support of ρ\rho is contained in the support of σ\sigma,
and +∞+\infty otherwise. We note that this definition generalises
the familiar Kullback–Leibler divergence [8]
of two probability distributions,
just as von Neumann entropy generalises Shannon entropy.
For a bipartite state ρA​B\rho_{AB} it is straightforward to check that
S(ρA​B∥ρA⊗ρB)=S(ρA)+S(ρB)−S(ρA​B),S(\rho_{AB}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=S(\rho_{A})+S(\rho_{B})-S(\rho_{AB}),
(4)
and the latter quantity is abbreviated I⁡(A:B)I(A:B), in formal extension
of the definition of Shannon’s mutual information [19] to
quantum states.
Example 2
Let {p⁡(x),ρx}\{p(x),\rho_{x}\} be an ensemble of quantum states
on ℋ{\cal H}. The Holevo quantity χ\chi is defined as
χ⁡({p⁡(x),ρx})=S⁡(∑xp⁡(x)​ρx)−∑xp⁡(x)​S​(ρx).\chi\bigl(\{p(x),\rho_{x}\}\bigr)=S\left(\sum_{x}p(x)\rho_{x}\right)-\sum_{x}p(x)S(\rho_{x}).
Holevo [5] showed that this quantity is an upper bound
to the mutual information between xx and the outcomes yy of any
particular measurement performed on the states ρx\rho_{x}.
It is easily seen that
χ⁡({p⁡(x),ρx})=I⁡(A:B),\chi\bigl(\{p(x),\rho_{x}\}\bigr)=I(A:B),
with the bipartite state
ρA​B=∑xp⁡(x)​|x⟩​⟨x|A⊗(ρx)B.\rho_{AB}=\sum_{x}p(x){{|{x}\rangle}\!{\langle{x}|}}_{A}\otimes(\rho_{x})_{B}.
For a tripartite state ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} we can also consider the information
I⁡(A:B​C)I(A:BC), which can be written as
S(ρA​B​C∥ρA⊗ρB​C)=S(ρA)+S(ρB​C)−S(ρA​B​C).S(\rho_{ABC}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC})=S(\rho_{A})+S(\rho_{BC})-S(\rho_{ABC}).
(5)
The difference between eqs. (5)
and (4), which by virtue of the classical
chain rule eq. (2) we might call the quantum conditional
mutual information I(A:C|B)I(A:C|B) is, therefore,
S(ρA​B​C∥ρA⊗ρB​C)−S(ρA​B∥ρA⊗ρB)=S⁡(ρA​B)+S⁡(ρB​C)−S⁡(ρA​B​C)−S⁡(ρB).\begin{split}S(\rho_{ABC}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC})&-S(\rho_{AB}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=S(\rho_{AB})+S(\rho_{BC})-S(\rho_{ABC})-S(\rho_{B}).\end{split}
(6)
The right hand side here is nonnegative by strong subadditivity.
(Note that this is an important theorem in the quantum case despite being
an almost trivial observation classically.) The left hand
side, however, can be rewritten as S(ρ∥σ)−S(Tρ∥Tσ)S(\rho\|\sigma)-S(T\rho\|T\sigma),
with the states ρ=ρA​B​C\rho=\rho_{ABC} and σ=ρA⊗ρB​C\sigma=\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC},
and the quantum operation T=TrCT={\operatorname{Tr}}_{C}, the partial trace over ℋC{\cal H}_{C},
which as a linear map can be written as T=idA​B⊗TrT={\operatorname{id}}_{AB}\otimes{\operatorname{Tr}}.
Now a theorem of Uhlmann [24] (proved earlier by
Lindblad [10]
for the finite–dimensional case of interest here)
says that for all states ρ\rho and
σ\sigma on a space ℋ{\cal H}, and all quantum operations
T:ℬ⁡(ℋ)→ℬ⁡(𝒦)T:{\cal B}({\cal H})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal K}),
S(ρ∥σ)≥S(Tρ∥Tσ),S(\rho\|\sigma)\geq S(T\rho\|T\sigma),
(7)
so Uhlmann’s theorem implies strong subadditivity and we have equality in the
latter if and only if there is equality in the former.
IV The equality condition
for relative entropy
The formulation in the previous section
of strong subadditivity as a relative entropy
monotonicity under a partial trace operation
transforms the question for the equality conditions for the
former into the same question for the latter.
Note that by the very monotonicity relation, there is a “trivial”
case of equality in eq. (7),
namely if there exists a quantum operation
T^\widehat{T} mapping T​ρT\rho to ρ\rho and T​σT\sigma to σ\sigma.
In fact, this is the only case of equality:
Theorem 3 (Petz [15])
For states ρ\rho and σ\sigma,
S(ρ∥σ)=S(Tρ∥Tσ)S(\rho\|\sigma)=S(T\rho\|T\sigma)
if and only if there exists a quantum operation T^\widehat{T}
such that
T^​T​ρ=ρ,T^​T​σ=σ.\widehat{T}T\rho=\rho,\quad\widehat{T}T\sigma=\sigma.
Furthermore, on the support of T​σT\sigma, T^\widehat{T} can be
given explicitly by the formula
T^​α=σ12​T∗​((T​σ)−12​α​(T​σ)−12)​σ12,\widehat{T}\alpha=\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{*}\left((T\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha(T\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}},
(8)
with the adjoint map T∗T^{*} of TT:
T∗​(X)=∑iAi∗​X​Ai, if ​T​(α)=∑iAi​α​Ai∗.T^{*}(X)=\sum_{i}A_{i}^{*}XA_{i},\text{ if }T(\alpha)=\sum_{i}A_{i}\alpha A_{i}^{*}.
□\Box
Observe that the definition of T^\widehat{T} in
eq. (8) depends on σ\sigma, thereby
automatically ensuring that T^​T​σ=σ\widehat{T}T\sigma=\sigma. Sometimes, we
add the subscript σ\sigma to T^\widehat{T} to emphasize the dependence.

As in example 2, let {p⁡(x),ρx}\{p(x),\rho_{x}\} be an ensemble
of states on ℋ{\cal H}
and define

Remark 5
In [2] the “transpose channel” T^\widehat{T} of

makes an appearance in a slightly different context:
there a set of states is subjected to a quantum channel, and

fidelity criterion for the original states and the images of the
channel output states. It was shown that the error using
T^\widehat{T} is always at most twice as the minimum error under the
optimal recovery map.
V Structure of states with equality
Let ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} be a state on ℋA⊗ℋB⊗ℋC{\cal H}_{A}\otimes{\cal H}_{B}\otimes{\cal H}_{C}.
As we observed earlier, Uhlmann’s theorem specialized to
the states ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC} and σA​B​C=ρA⊗ρB​C\sigma_{ABC}=\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC},
along with the map T=TrCT={\operatorname{Tr}}_{C}, states that
S(ρA​B​C∥ρA⊗ρB​C)≥S(ρA​B∥ρA⊗ρB).S(\rho_{ABC}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC})\geq S(\rho_{AB}\|\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}).
Consequently, theorem 3 provides the condition for equality here:
T^​T​ρ=ρ\widehat{T}T\rho=\rho. Now, because T=idA⊗RB​CT={\operatorname{id}}_{A}\otimes R_{BC}, with
the restriction map RB​C=idB⊗TrCR_{BC}={\operatorname{id}}_{B}\otimes{\operatorname{Tr}}_{C},
and σ\sigma is a tensor product, we obtain (compare
example 4):
T^=idA⊗R^,\widehat{T}={\operatorname{id}}_{A}\otimes\widehat{R},
(10)
with R^=R^ρB​C\widehat{R}=\widehat{R}_{\rho_{BC}}.
Summarising, in the above monotonicity and hence in strong subadditivity
we have equality if and only if
ρA​B​C=(id⊗R^)​ρA​B.\rho_{ABC}=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\widehat{R})\rho_{AB}.
(11)
We are now in a position to prove our main result:
Theorem 6

satisfies strong subadditivity (eq. (1)) with equality if and only
if there is a decomposition of system BB as
ℋB=⨁jℋbjL⊗ℋbjR{\cal H}_{B}=\bigoplus_{j}{\cal H}_{b^{L}_{j}}\otimes{\cal H}_{b^{R}_{j}}

come from analysing the quantum Markov chain condition,
eq. (11).
After defining the quantum operation φ=TrC∘R^\varphi={\operatorname{Tr}}_{C}\circ\widehat{R},
the Markov condition gives us

𝐌{\bf M} of states on ℋA{\cal H}_{A} invariant under φ\varphi.
To this we can apply theorem 10 from the

ℋB=⨁jℋbjL⊗ℋbjR,{\cal H}_{B}=\bigoplus_{j}{\cal H}_{b^{L}_{j}}\otimes{\cal H}_{b^{R}_{j}},

such that every μ∈𝐌\mu\in{\bf M} can be written

VII Discussion
We have exhibited the explicit structure of the tripartite states ρA​B​C\rho_{ABC}
which satisfy strong subadditivity with equality. Not only are they short
quantum Markov chains in the sense of [1],
it is even the case that the AA and CC systems are conditionally
independent given BB, in a physically meaningful sense:
there is information in the BB system which can be obtained by
a non–demolition measurement, conditioned upon which the quantum state
factorises.
By specialising our result to particular types of states, we can easily
recover the entropic conditions for quantum error correction and the
conditions for saturation in the Holevo bound. In the general case, our
theorem characterises exactly when a quantum operation preserves correlations,
whether they be classical, in the form of pure entanglement, or more exotic,
such as combinations of the two or even bound
entanglement [6].
We left open the problem of a similar characterisation in infinite dimension
(of system BB — infinite AA and CC are covered by our result):
we only note that our method will certainly not work, as it relies ultimately
on the classification of finite dimensional operator algebras.
A further interesting problem could be to address the approximate
case: if a state almost satisfies strong subadditivity, does it
mean that its structure is close in some sense to the form of
theorem 6? There might be a relation to [2]
(see remark 5), where an approximate fidelity condition
was studied.
