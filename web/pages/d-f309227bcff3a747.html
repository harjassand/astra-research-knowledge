## Exact interface and strongest claim

Work first in a finite-dimensional tracial algebra (the central-reference reduction and its hypotheses are inherited from the supplied cycle-3/4 baseline, not newly proved here). Let \(\tau\) be normalized trace, \(\Phi\) a positive, unital, trace-preserving, trace-selfadjoint channel with a compatible two-output broadcaster, and let likelihoods be \(\rho_i=(I+H_i)/d\), where \(H_i=H_i^*\), \(\tau(H_i)=0\), and \(\|H_i\|_\infty\leq\theta<1\). Write \(r=\max_i\|(I-\Phi)H_i\|_{1,\tau}\).

The supplied internal commutator estimate (cycle-3 sandwich report, Eq. (14)) gives, for \(A_i=H_i/\theta\),

\[
 \max_{i,j}\|[A_i,A_j]\|_{2,\tau}
 \leq C_0\sqrt{r/\theta},\qquad C_0=4+2\sqrt2.
\]

For any *fixed* number \(N\) of likelihoods, Bansil--Kachkovskiy's Corollary 1.4 gives commuting selfadjoint contractions \(A'_1,\ldots,A'_N\) with

\[
 \sum_i\|A_i-A'_i\|_{2,\tau}^2
 \leq C(N)\big(C_0\sqrt{r/\theta}\big)^{2/3^{N-1}}.
\]

Let \(B=W^*(A'_1,\ldots,A'_N)\), an abelian subalgebra, and let \(E_B\) be the trace-preserving conditional expectation. Since it is the \(L^2(\tau)\) orthogonal projection and \(A'_i\in B\),
\[
 \|A_i-E_BA_i\|_{1,\tau}
 \leq\|A_i-E_BA_i\|_{2,\tau}
 \leq\|A_i-A'_i\|_{2,\tau}.
\]
The Schrödinger channel dual to \(E_B\) is entanglement breaking: if \(P_z\) are the minimal projections of \(B\), then \(E_B(X)=\sum_z \operatorname{Tr}(P_zX)P_z/\operatorname{rank}(P_z)\), a measure-and-prepare map. Therefore the *same* EB channel reconstructs all N states with
\[
 \max_i T(\rho_i,E_B(\rho_i))
 \leq {\theta\over2}\sqrt{C(N)}
      \big(C_0\sqrt{r/\theta}\big)^{1/3^{N-1}}.
\]
If the indexed family has an \(a\)-net of N states in trace distance and the same residual bound holds on its centers, trace-distance contractivity gives for every family member
\[
 \sup_{\rho} T(\rho,E_B(\rho))
 \leq 2a+{\theta\over2}\sqrt{C(N)}
      \big(C_0\sqrt{r/\theta}\big)^{1/3^{N-1}}. \tag{*}
\]
This is the strongest result in this checkpoint. It is useful only where the covering cardinality is controlled: for each fixed \(a\), N must stay bounded independently of matrix dimension/sequence, or a quantitative relation among \(N(a), C(N(a)), r\) must make (*) vanish. The theorem supplies neither such relation nor a uniform bound for growing families. The result is an application of an imported almost-commuting theorem to the supplied commutator estimate, not a new proof of either input.

## Why this does not settle the target

The decisive target in the supplied baseline is map-level. For a tracial channel \(\Phi\), define
\[
 \beta_m=\sup_{(\mathcal A,\tau,\Phi)}\ \inf_{E\in EB_\tau}\ \sup_{A=A^*,\|A\|_\infty\leq1}
       \|E(A)-\Phi^m(A)\|_{1,\tau}.
\]
A dimension-independent proof that \(\beta_m\to0\) would suffice, since the baseline gives for a \(\theta\)-bounded likelihood with one-step residual at most r
\[
 T(E(\rho),\rho)\leq\tfrac12\big((1+\theta)\beta_m+mr\big),
\]
then choose m growing slowly enough that \(mr\to0\). The whole range \(\Phi^m(\{A:\|A\|_\infty\leq1\})\) has supplied uniform pair-commutator bound \(2/\sqrt{m+1}+4/(m+1)\) in normalized tracial \(L^2\). What is missing is converting that uniform approximate commutativity of the entire CP range to a single finite-dimensional EB map in normalized \(L^\infty\!\to L^1\) norm.

The finite-tuple theorem cannot directly do this: its exponent is \(2/3^{N-1}\) in squared total error and its constant is only specified as \(C(N)\). Both deteriorate with N. A pointwise abelian/amenable limit or approximating each finite set does not control the supremum over the growing operator-norm unit ball. Exact fixed-point projection is also not a substitute: the supplied two-point slow classical chains retain slow modes while projection deletes them. I have not proved an injectivity/amenability lifting lemma that fixes this uniformity issue, nor found a counterexample to the full target.
