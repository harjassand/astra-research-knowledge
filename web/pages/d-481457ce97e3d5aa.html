CYCLE 3 LEAF 15 — EXACT SMALL-GAIN CERTIFICATES AND THE COMPLEX-BALANCE BOUNDARY
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the strict-LP decision boundary and
the displayed family-specific criteria; FINITE-EVIDENCE for the Fraction
checker fixtures. Product-form and nonexplosion implications are
EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED under the cited source theorems. Necessity of small gain
for recurrence is REFUTED by an exact complex-balanced fixture. No claim is
made that LP failure implies transience or explosion.

RESULT
------
For the supplied complete catalyst/substrate family, with fixed strictly
positive rational rates and positive integer k_ji, the named strict small-gain
condition is an exact rational linear-feasibility question. It has short,
exactly checkable certificates on both sides: a normalized rational positive
weight vector for feasibility, and a normalized nonnegative Farkas/Gordan
multiplier for infeasibility. General rational LP algorithms decide it in
polynomial bit complexity in the encoded LP size. The supplied Python checker
verifies witnesses; it is not an optimizer.

This decides whether the specified weighted Foster template passes. It does
not decide positive recurrence for every input network. Small gain is not
necessary: a three-species instance is complex balanced and positive
recurrent while its small-gain LP has an explicit Farkas witness of
infeasibility. Conversely, a three-species instance passes the strict
small-gain test but is not complex balanced. The two sufficient criteria are
incomparable on this family. The supplied Foster implication uses the
continuous-time generator drift, so the result concerns physical-time
recurrence; the LP is not an embedded jump-chain/event-count test.

1. INPUT INTERFACE AND MATRIX
----------------------------
Fix finite r,s >= 1 and the complete family from
outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle3/leaves/foster_multicatalyst.txt:

  0 <-> A_j                         rates alpha_j, delta_j
  A_j <-> A_j+B_i                   rates p_ji, q_ji
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i        rates u_ji, v_ji.

The CTMC uses standard falling-factorial mass-action propensities. For this
leaf's exact decision result, alpha_j,delta_j,p_ji,q_ji,u_ji,v_ji are
positive binary-encoded rationals and k_ji are positive binary-encoded
integers. No rate oracle, rounded estimate, unknown real, time-varying rate,
interval box, or volume parameter is part of the input. The exact LP concerns
the strict condition from the supplied Foster theorem:

  delta_j lambda_j - sum_i p_ji w_i > 0                    for every j,
  q_ji w_i - k_ji u_ji lambda_j > 0                         for every j,i,
  lambda_j > 0, w_i > 0.

Put x=(lambda_1,...,lambda_r,w_1,...,w_s)^T and construct the rational
matrix A with r+rs+(r+s) rows:

  catalyst row j: [delta_j at lambda_j; -p_ji at each w_i],
  pair row (j,i): [-k_ji u_ji at lambda_j; q_ji at w_i],
  positivity rows: the (r+s) by (r+s) identity matrix.

Then the exact condition is simply Ax>0 coordinatewise. The alpha_j and
reverse rates v_ji do not enter this LP: immigration is handled by the finite
boundary-barrier choice in the supplied Foster proof, while each v_ji term
has favorable nonpositive drift. Their strict positivity remains among the
Foster theorem's assumptions.

2. DECISION EQUIVALENCE AND CERTIFICATE FORMS
---------------------------------------------
Let n=r+s and normalize by 1^T x=1. Homogeneity gives the margin LP

  t* = max t
       subject to 1^T x=1, x>=0, Ax>=t 1,
       with t a free real variable.

This LP is feasible for every instance (take any x in the simplex and t
sufficiently negative); its optimum is finite and attained. The identity
rows imply t<=min_l x_l<=1/n. Moreover,

  strict small gain is feasible  iff  t*>0.

Forward: if Ax>0 and x>0, normalize x; the finite minimum of its entries and
of Ax is positive, so t>0 is feasible. Reverse: if t>0 is feasible, then
each coordinate x_l>=t>0 and each required drift margin is at least t.
Because this is a rational LP, a positive optimum admits a rational optimal
basic solution.

The exact positive certificate is x in Q^n satisfying
1^T x=1, x_l>0, and every row of Ax>0. A checker needs only exact rational
additions/multiplications and sign tests.

The dual/minimax form gives the exact negative certificate:

  y>=0,  1^T y=1,  A^T y<=0.

Soundness is immediate. If a strict feasible x existed, then
0 < y^T A x = (A^T y)^T x <= 0, since x>=0, contradiction. Conversely, if
strict small gain fails, rational LP duality (equivalently the strict
Gordan alternative) supplies such a y. To see the dual boundary, the dual
margin objective is min_y max_l (A^T y)_l over y in the row simplex; its
optimum equals t*. If t*<=0, an optimal y has A^T y<=t*1<=0. This witness
certifies failure of this strict template, and nothing stronger about the
CTMC recurrence classification.

The included checker uses exact fractions to verify either supplied
certificate form. It does not search for certificates. A rational LP solver
is the decision algorithm; a found candidate is checked independently by
the small verifier.

3. BIT COST AND WHAT IS DECIDABLE
--------------------------------
Let L be the total binary input length, including the rational numerators and
denominators and the integer k_ji. There are n=r+s variables and
m=2r+s+rs constraints/rows in A. A sparse representation has
O(r+s+rs) nonzero entries. Forming the products k_ji u_ji increases
coefficient bit length by at most the sum of the two operand bit lengths.
Thus the constructed rational LP has size polynomial in L. Exact rational
LP (for example, the ellipsoid method) decides the sign of t* in time
polynomial in the dimension and total LP encoding length. This is a
bit-complexity statement, not a strongly-polynomial bound.

The primal or dual certificate can be chosen with polynomially many bits:
rational LP optima have basic rational solutions, with numerator and
denominator sizes bounded polynomially by determinant bounds for the
input coefficient matrix. Verification of x takes O(nnz(A)) rational
multiply-adds; verification of y takes O(nnz(A)) such operations; all
intermediate integer bit lengths remain polynomial in input plus witness
length. Floating point alone is not a decision method because a positive
strict margin may be exponentially small in L. No time for acquiring or
validating externally supplied rate constants is counted; the interface
assumes exact rational data. The checker uses only Python's standard-library
Fraction and verifies two finite fixtures, not worst-case runtime.

For a feasible rational weight vector, the supplied Foster proof's auxiliary
choices can also be made rational: for example fix rho=1, choose a rational K
strictly above max{lambda_max,(H+1)/alpha_sum}, and compute the integer
boundary cutoff B_1 by an exact ceiling. Their bit lengths are polynomial in
the rate/weight and margin bit lengths. This does not mean that the finite
exceptional state set is polynomial in input length: its cardinality can be
large when exact positive margins are small. The LP recognition/check cost
does not enumerate that set.

The decision boundary is therefore:

  fixed rational rates + named strict weighted condition -> DECIDABLE in P;
  supplied primal/Farkas witness -> exact finite verification;
  “is this CTMC positive recurrent?” for arbitrary rates -> not decided by
       this LP result;
  LP failure -> recurrence status UNKNOWN from small gain alone.

This is a bounded recognizer for one sufficient Foster certificate family,
not a completeness theorem for stochastic recurrence. No undecidability claim
is asserted; no reduction establishing one was found or attempted here.

4. EXACT THREE-SPECIES TWO-WAY SEPARATION
-----------------------------------------
Both fixtures use r=1,s=2,k_11=k_12=1, so each has three species and lies
outside a two-species-only conclusion. All rates not shown separately below
are set for i=1,2 identically.

(A) Complex balance and product form, but small gain is infeasible:

  alpha=delta=1; p_i=1, q_i=2, u_i=v_i=1.

The strict inequalities require
  lambda-w_1-w_2>0, 2w_1-lambda>0, 2w_2-lambda>0.
The exact row matrix (coordinates lambda,w_1,w_2, followed by positivity
rows) is
  A = [[ 1,-1,-1],[-1,2,0],[-1,0,2],[1,0,0],[0,1,0],[0,0,1]].
The exact Farkas multiplier
  y=(1/2,1/4,1/4,0,0,0)^T
has 1^T y=1, y>=0, and A^T y=(0,0,0)^T. Therefore no strict small-gain
weights exist. This is aggregate failure: both individual pair comparisons
delta*q_i > p_i*k_i*u_i hold (2>1), but two substrate production loads share
one catalyst-loss budget.

At the same time c_A=1 and c_B1=c_B2=1/2 satisfy detailed balance on every
edge: alpha=delta*c_A; p_i*c_A=q_i*c_A*c_Bi; and
u_i*c_A*c_Bi=v_i*c_A^2*c_Bi. The complex graph is a tree, so edgewise zero
flux is also equivalent to complex balance. The normalized product-Poisson
law with means (1,1/2,1/2) is stationary by Anderson–Craciun–Kurtz, Theorem
4.1. The family is irreducible on N_0^3 under its positive rates (as checked
in the supplied Foster-family report); Anderson–Cappelletti–Koyama–Kurtz,
Theorem 3, gives nonexplosion for complex-balanced mass-action systems.
Consequently this instance is positive recurrent. Thus small gain is not
necessary for recurrence, and its Farkas certificate must not be read as
transience or explosion.

(B) Small gain feasible, but the network is not complex balanced:

  alpha=1, delta=3; p_i=1, q_i=2, u_i=v_i=1.

Choose the normalized candidate x=(1/3,1/3,1/3). The catalyst margin is
3(1/3)-1/3-1/3=1/3, each pair margin is 2(1/3)-1/3=1/3, and each positivity
row is 1/3. This exact primal certificate passes. But 0<->A forces
c_A=alpha/delta=1/3, while the amplifier edges force c_A=u_i/v_i=1, so no
complex-balanced equilibrium exists. The strict Foster theorem still proves
nonexplosion and positive recurrence in this fixture. Therefore the two
criteria are incomparable; the product-form criterion is a sufficient
alternative, not a substitute characterization of the weighted LP.

5. GENERAL COMPLEX-BALANCE/PRODUCT-FORM COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------
For this complete family, the complex graph has vertices 0, A_j,
U_ji=A_j+B_i, and V_ji=(k_ji+1)A_j+B_i. Since k_ji>=1, these are distinct;
the undirected graph is a tree with reversible edges. At a complex-balanced
equilibrium, the net edge-flux vector has zero divergence at every vertex.
The incidence matrix of a tree has no nonzero circulation, so every edge's
net flux is zero. Dividing those detailed-balance equations by positive
coordinates yields the following exact necessary-and-sufficient rate test:

  c_Aj = alpha_j/delta_j;
  for each i, p_ji/q_ji is independent of j, and c_Bi is that common ratio;
  u_ji/v_ji = (alpha_j/delta_j)^(k_ji) for all j,i.

If these equalities hold, the positive vector c is a complex-balanced point;
then the product of Poisson distributions with means c is stationary on the
whole irreducible state space. Complex-balance equations are exact rational
equalities for rational rates and integer exponents. Their exact test can
also be polynomial in bit length even when a binary-encoded k is huge: reduce
both positive rational sides; for a base integer at least 2, if k exceeds
the target integer's bit length equality is impossible, and otherwise exact
exponentiation builds only polynomially many bits. The base-1 case is
immediate. The included fixture checker uses this guarded comparison.
This direct edge test should not be confused with applying the
deficiency-zero theorem to every
weakly reversible rate choice: here n_c=1+r+2rs, there is one linkage class,
and the reaction vectors span r+s dimensions, so deficiency is
  n_c-1-(r+s)=s(2r-1)>0.
Positive deficiency does not preclude complex balance at special rates; it
only means the deficiency-zero all-rates shortcut is unavailable.

The external product-form implication used here is exactly the
complex-balanced -> stationary Poisson product direction of ACK Theorem
4.1. No broad converse about every possible product-form invariant law is
needed for the two fixtures or claimed here. For this specific family,
complex balance itself is exactly characterized by the tree edge equations
above.
