1. MODEL AND CLAIMED MECHANISM
==============================

There are two patches. x_1 in [0,1] is focal prey density in patch 1; x_2 in
[0,1] is alternate prey density in patch 2; q is effective per-capita attack
pressure by a shared generalist consumer on focal prey; s is a candidate
short-term memory of recent net alternate-prey loss. The controlled stress
u(t) acts on patch 2. The deterministic candidate is

  x_1' = x_1 [ r_1 (x_1 - theta)(1 - x_1) - c q ],
  x_2' = x_2 [ r_2 (1 - x_2) - u(t) ],
  L(t) = [-x_2'(t)]_+,
  s'   = L - kappa s,
  q'   = gamma [ alpha (1 - x_2)_+ + beta s - q ].                 (1)

Here [z]_+ = max(z,0), 0 < theta < 1, and all parameters are nonnegative.
The finite example uses r_1=1.2, r_2=1, theta=.22, c=1.5, alpha=1,
gamma=.8, beta=3, kappa=.3. Densities are normalized; time is in units fixed
by r_2=1. The beta*s term is a hypothesis, not a fitted empirical relation:
loss of alternate prey is proposed to leave a decaying foraging/search memory
that temporarily raises attack pressure on the focal prey. The signal L is a
state-derived loss flux, not an instruction to provide future u(t). q can be
measured operationally as focal-prey kill hazard per focal individual,
including consumer abundance and allocation.

The state space x_i in [0,1], q,s >= 0 is forward invariant for deterministic
initial states in that set and u>=0. x_1=0 is invariant; below theta its
per-capita drift is negative. This is an Allee basin boundary, not merely an
imposed threshold. In the finite-population version, n_1=0 is absorbing.

2. STATIC FOLD, BASIN BOUNDARY, AND B-TIPPING
============================================

For constant 0 <= u < r_2, the patch-2 equilibrium is x_2*=1-u/r_2. At a
constant state L*=0, s*=0 and q*=alpha*u/r_2. Positive focal-prey equilibria
therefore solve

  r_1 (x - theta)(1 - x) = c alpha u/r_2.                         (2)

The left side is a concave quadratic on [theta,1], with maximum
r_1(1-theta)^2/4 at x_m=(1+theta)/2. Thus the positive branch exists iff

  u <= u_B := r_2 r_1 (1-theta)^2 / (4 c alpha).                  (3)

For u<u_B, its roots are

  x_-(u), x_+(u) = (1+theta +/- sqrt((1-theta)^2
                            -4 c alpha u/(r_1 r_2)))/2,            (4)

where x_- is the lower root and x_+ the upper root. At a positive equilibrium
the focal eigenvalue is

  lambda_x = r_1 x (1+theta-2x),                                  (5)

so x_- is unstable and x_+ stable; x_1=0 is stable because its per-capita
growth at zero is -r_1 theta-cq<0. For u>u_B held constant, q tends to
alpha*u/r_2 > r_1(1-theta)^2/(4c), so x_1' / x_1 is eventually bounded
strictly below zero for every x_1 in [0,1]; extinction follows. This is a
genuine loss of the positive equilibrium (B-tipping) when the super-fold
endpoint is held. A finite supercritical pulse can instead end before a basin
crossing, so crossing u_B alone is not a pathwise extinction theorem.

For the numeric values above, u_B=q_fold=.12168. At endpoint U=.09 the two
positive equilibria are x_-=.4110025126 and x_+=.8089974874. At U=.14 no
positive equilibrium exists.

3. CORRECTED INVARIANT: THE ORIGINAL RESPONSE CANNOT R-TIP FROM THE HEALTHY
   ATTRACTOR UNDER A MONOTONE SUB-FOLD RAMP
==========================================================================

Set beta=0 in (1), take x_2(0)=1, q(0)=0, and suppose
0<=u(t)<=U<r_2. Let K=1-U/r_2. The scalar x_2 vector field points into
[K,1]: at K,

  x_2' = K[U-u(t)] >= 0,

and at 1, x_2'=-u(t)<=0. Hence x_2(t)>=K and
alpha(1-x_2(t))<=alpha U/r_2. The stable linear filter for q gives

  0 <= q(t) <= alpha U/r_2.                                      (6)

If U<=u_B, x_+(U) exists. At the lower boundary x_1=x_+(U),

  x_1' = x_+(U)[r_1(x_+-theta)(1-x_+) - c q(t)] >= 0,

by (2) and (6); at x_1=1, x_1'=-c q<=0. Therefore [x_+(U),1] is positively
invariant. A trajectory starting at the healthy attractor x_1=1 cannot cross
the final high-root barrier under any such monotone or nonmonotone bounded
input in this beta=0 model. The earlier fast/slow comparison from x_1=.225
is a basin-state recovery contrast, not an attractor-based R-tip. In the
finite script, beta=0 from x_1=1 stays above x_+=.809; from x_1=.225 the fast
case crosses theta while the slow case recovers. This explicitly preserves
the correction raised by the director.

4. FALSIFIABLE LOSS-MEMORY EXTENSION AND RATE-INDUCED ESCAPE
===========================================================

For beta>0, L integrates recent alternate-prey loss:

  s(t) = integral_0^t exp[-kappa(t-v)] L(v) dv,

and q is a second low-pass response to the present deficit plus that memory:

  q(t) = exp(-gamma t)q(0)
       + gamma integral_0^t exp[-gamma(t-v)]
                         [alpha(1-x_2(v))+beta s(v)] dv.           (7)

At a constant endpoint L*=s*=0, so the fold (3) and roots (4) are unchanged.
Under slow ramps, loss flux is small (order 1/ramp duration after scaling)
and s decays; under fast alternate-prey decline, s can transiently increase q
above its endpoint value and above q_fold. Unlike the beta=0 response, no
invariant based on the endpoint stress bounds q by alpha*U/r_2.

For u(t)=U min(t/T,1), initial state (x_1,x_2,q,s)=(1,1,0,0), and U=.09,
the finite RK4 run gives:

  T=.1:  peak q=.203191, cross x_-(U) at t=10.71, cross theta at t=20.76;
  T=3:   peak q=.195944, cross x_-(U) at t=15.95, cross theta at t=31.34;
  T=4:   peak q=.191059, minimum x_1=.445479, no crossing of x_-=.4110;
  T=100: peak q=.097527, minimum x_1=.788528, no Allee crossing.

The final high equilibrium persists at U=.09 for every run; the fast case
starts exactly at the healthy attractor of u=0. This is a candidate
attractor-based rate-induced tip in the sense of failure to track the moving
high equilibrium, followed by crossing of the final basin separator. The
numbers are illustrative model evidence only. The rate threshold is only
bracketed between T=3 and T=4 for these parameters; no exact threshold is
claimed. The loss-memory law and beta value are not empirically established.

The frozen high-branch one-sided eigenvalues at U=0 are
(-.936,-1,-.8,-.3); at U=.09 they are approximately
(-.386372,-.91,-.8,-.3). No eigenvalue tends to zero on this sub-fold
continuation. Thus a local critical-slowing-down eigenvalue criterion does not
diagnose this simulated rate tip. However, the one-sided Jacobian is triangular
and nonnormal. At U=.09, with a=r_2 x_2*=.91 and state order
(x_1,x_2,q,s), the positive-loss-side matrix is

  J_+ = [[lambda_x, 0, -c x_+, 0],
         [0, -a, 0, 0],
         [0, -gamma alpha, -gamma, gamma beta],
         [0, a, 0, -kappa]].

Its eigenvalues are still (-.386372,-.91,-.8,-.3). A linear tangent run from
delta x_2(0)=1, all other perturbations zero, gives peak delta q=+1.299 per
unit delta x_2 at t=3.652 and minimum delta x_1=-2.984 at t=6.082 when beta=3.
The otherwise identical beta=0 tangent has delta q in [-.344,0] and delta
x_1 in [0,.570]: the proposed memory term reverses the sign of the delayed
focal response in this linearized test. These are derivatives per unit
alternate-prey density, not finite displacements or empirical evidence. The
positive-part loss law makes this a one-sided Jacobian. This calculation shows
why stable eigenvalues alone do not rule out large transient or time-series
responses. It does not prove that every statistical early-warning indicator
fails; it only establishes no eigenvalue approaches zero along the tested
endpoint branch and a finite transient gain in the candidate model.

5. EXACT NONLINEAR ATTACK-DEBT IDENTITY
======================================

Write p(x)=r_1(x-theta)(1-x). For every deterministic solution with x_1>0,

  d(log x_1)/dt = p(x_1)-c q_eff(t),

where q_eff=q with the access gate open and q_eff=0 with it closed. Integrating
gives the exact identity

  log(x_1(t)/x_1(0))
      = r_1 integral_0^t (x_1-theta)(1-x_1) ds
        - c integral_0^t q_eff(s) ds.                              (8)

Define the attack debt

  D(t) = c integral_0^t q_eff(s) ds
         - r_1 integral_0^t (x_1-theta)(1-x_1) ds.                 (9)

Equation (8) says log(x_1(0)/x_1(t))=D(t). At the first Allee-boundary hit,

  D(t_theta) = log(x_1(0)/theta).                                 (10)

The second integral is the full nonlinear compensatory-growth remainder; it
must not be discarded or replaced by a local Jacobian. For the fast R run,
the right side is log(1/.22)=1.5141277 and the numerically integrated debt
at the first theta crossing is 1.514328 (RK4 step .01; absolute residual
about 2e-4 at the threshold crossing, with the largest pre-crossing log
identity residual 1.02e-12). The slight threshold-level discrepancy is due to
reporting the first discrete integration step after the continuous crossing.

The operationally measured variables are x_1 and focal-prey attack hazard
q_eff. The identity classifies realized paths without requiring the future
stress trajectory. It is not by itself a forecast: a future u(t) can change
the future attack exposure. Its capability is a pathwise certificate and a
real-time control margin, not a guarantee about unobserved future inputs.

6. REALIZABLE GATE INTERVENTION CERTIFICATE
===========================================

Suppose an experimental patch can physically close a consumer-access gate so
that q_eff becomes zero. This manipulates the interaction itself; it does not
freeze the consumer's hidden adaptive state. Assume x_1 is observed, the
gate's worst-case actuation delay is tau_g, and an empirical upper bound
q_eff<=Q holds during that delay. While theta<=x_1<=1, p(x_1)>=0, hence

  d(log(x_1/theta))/dt >= -c Q.                                    (11)

If at the trigger time t the observed true margin
m(t)=log(x_1(t)/theta) satisfies m(t)>c Q tau_g, then x_1 cannot hit theta
before the gate closes. Once closed, q_eff=0 and x_1'=x_1 p(x_1)>=0 for
theta<=x_1<=1, so deterministic recovery is guaranteed in this model. This
uses a current-state margin and a measured/physical bound on attack rate; no
future u(t) is needed. With discrete sampling interval Delta and absolute
margin error epsilon, a conservative trigger guard must include at least
cQ(tau_g+Delta)+epsilon, plus any known upper bound on additional direct
focal-prey mortality during delay. If unmodeled direct mortality is
unbounded, or if the access intervention does not actually block the attack,
there is no such guarantee. Under demographic noise, gate closure improves
the drift but cannot guarantee survival at finite N; the certificate is
deterministic and needs a probabilistic margin extension for a CTMC.

7. DEMOGRAPHIC NOISE AND WHAT THE FINITE RUN SHOWS
=================================================

For an individual-count interpretation, let n=Nx_1 and define, while n>=1,
birth and death rates

  lambda_+(n)=n b(x_1,q),  lambda_-(n)=n d(x_1,q),
  b=nu+[g]_+, d=nu+[-g]_+,
  g=p(x_1)-c q_eff, nu>0.                                          (12)

Then lambda_+-lambda_-=N x_1 g, so the deterministic drift is (1). At n=0
both rates vanish and extinction is absorbing. Before the process reaches a
small cutoff n>=2, the exact log-count Doob decomposition is

  log(n(t)/N)-log(n(0)/N) = A_N(t)+M_N(t),                         (13)

  A_N'(t) = n b log(1+1/n) + n d log(1-1/n),
  <M_N>'(t) = n b log^2(1+1/n) + n d log^2(1-1/n),                (14)

where M_N is a martingale. For n large,

  A_N' = g - (b+d)/(2n) + O(n^-2),
  <M_N>' = (b+d)/n + O(n^-2).                                      (15)

Thus, away from the absorbing boundary and with bounded per-capita rates,
demographic log-noise variance is order 1/N over a fixed horizon. A common
environmental shock is not suppressed by this scaling. Equations (13)-(15)
are exact/asymptotic claims for the CTMC defined in (12), not for arbitrary
ecological noise processes.

The runnable diagnostic uses a tau-leap approximation. With zero imposed
stress, deterministic initial state x_1=x_2=1, and adaptive consumer access
open, demographic fluctuations in x_2 feed through the rectified loss-memory
state into q. In seed 1509, N=80/320/1280, 120 replicates, horizon 10, the
Allee-crossing counts were 95/120, 47/120, and 7/120 (risks .792, .392,
.058; Wilson 95% intervals [.711,.855], [.309,.481], [.029,.116]). True
absorbing extinctions were 49/120, 4/120, and 0/120. In the matched model
control with the access gate closed, no replicate crossed theta. These are
finite-model diagnostics, not field estimates. A second gate-closed control
starting at x_1=.30 gave crossing risks .517, .117, 0 across the same N values,
showing the expected high demographic sensitivity near an Allee separator.

A coarse time-step check for the no-stress/open-gate simulation used 120
replicates at dt=.02,.01,.005,.0025. Crossing risks ranged .792-.825 at N=80,
.275-.425 at N=320, and .025-.083 at N=1280. The ordering was stable, but
finite-sample uncertainty remains material. The simulation's q/s update uses
the expected stress-driven loss flux computed from current noisy x_2; it does
not explicitly sample predator learning or the consumer population. It also
omits shared environmental noise, dispersal and observation error.

8. CAUSAL EXPERIMENT AND MECHANISM CLASSIFICATION
================================================

A feasible first test is a replicated two-patch mesocosm with focal prey,
alternate prey and a shared generalist consumer. Independent tanks are drawn
from the same stock, blocked on measured starting densities and randomized;
the design does not assume that hidden whole-ecosystem states can be cloned.

Randomize a controlled alternate-prey removal schedule with the same total
removal and final x_2 across fast and slow schedules. Use high-frequency
nonlethal focal/alternate-prey census and video or individual tracking to
estimate focal attack hazard q_eff, consumer movement and the decline flux.
Factorially randomize consumer access to the focal patch with a removable
barrier and sham barrier. This is a physical interaction intervention, not an
attempt to freeze diet adaptation. Fit beta,kappa on a training block, then
test held-out ramps. The candidate predicts an intermediate q/s pulse after
fast loss, attack-debt accumulation through (9), and a fast-only collapse
from the healthy initial state at a sub-fold endpoint when access is open;
closing access or slowing the ramp should prevent deterministic basin
crossing. Failure to observe a loss-rate memory term or failure of the gate
effect rejects this proposed mechanism.

In separate long step-and-hold blocks, vary the final stress slowly to locate
the static fold and hysteresis. A held endpoint above u_B with loss of the
positive equilibrium is B-tipping. A randomized same-endpoint fast/slow
contrast below a measured fold, from a healthy high state, supported by the
q-history and open/closed gate contrast, is evidence for R-tipping within the
tested mesocosm. A no-stress loss that scales with census size and disappears
under consumer exclusion supports demographic-noise transmission through
adaptive attack. If patch size is changed to vary N, per-capita rates and
spatial geometry must be checked; if they cannot be held comparable, N-scaling
does not isolate demographic noise. Common shocks and hidden drivers must be
blocked, measured or left as an unresolved confounder.

There is no guaranteed passive classification of an ecosystem's future from
one present state when future forcing is unrestricted: the same
(x_1,x_2,q,s)=(1,1,0,0) remains healthy under u=0 and eventually loses the
positive branch under u>u_B held. A current-state-only predictor cannot know
which future input occurs. The experiment identifies causal response to
assigned schedules, and the gate rule gives a conditional intervention under
a measured attack-rate envelope; neither supplies a natural future forecast.

9. ALTERNATIVES, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND LIMITATIONS
================================================

* The beta=0 model fails the healthy-attractor R-tip test by the exact
  invariant in section 3. Its old x_1=.225 example was near the Allee
  separator and is retained only as a basin-state contrast.
* A stable triangular/non-normal response can create transient attack, but
  without a true Allee boundary it does not establish irreversible ecosystem
  tipping. Here the Allee saddle, extinction state, and exact basin crossing
  are explicit.
* Standard prey switching and alternate-prey effects are established
  ecological topics. The specific memory of the *rate* of alternate-prey
  loss, its sign and gain beta are not established by the literature checked.
  They are the central empirical gap, not a conclusion.
* q is an effective trophic state. The construction omits predator abundance
  dynamics, prey refuges, migration, spatial gradients inside each patch,
  environmental forcing directly on x_1, and species-specific observation
  error. Any of these can change the basin or mimic the proposed memory.
* The positive-part operator is nonsmooth at zero prey-loss flux. Frozen
  spectra and J_+ are one-sided local diagnostics; the tangent response does
  not prove global basin geometry. Numerical trajectories and tau leaps are
  finite tests only.
* The no-forecast claim is an impossibility under unrestricted future input,
  not a limitation specific to this model. The gate theorem assumes the
  focal prey is harmed through the gated attack term only, and q is bounded
  during gate latency.
* At finite N the deterministic debt threshold is perturbed by the martingale
  and finite-size compensator. A crossing may be N-tipping even if the mean
  trajectory does not cross; an R/N mixture is possible. A single extinction
  record cannot identify the cause.

10. PRIMARY LITERATURE COMPARISON
=================================

Ashwin, Wieczorek, Vitolo & Cox (2012), “Tipping points in open systems:
bifurcation, noise-induced and rate-dependent examples in the climate
system,” Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 370:1166-1184, DOI
10.1098/rsta.2011.0306. Their definitions distinguish B-, N- and R-tipping;
R-tipping need not accompany a bifurcation or noise. This is the classification
baseline, not a novel contribution here.

Panahi, Do, Hastings & Lai (2023), “Rate-induced tipping in complex
high-dimensional ecological networks,” PNAS 120(51):e2308820120, DOI
10.1073/pnas.2308820120. They compute global R-tipping probabilities from
ensembles of initial conditions for ten empirical mutualistic networks and
derive a rate-probability scaling law. Therefore generic ecological R-tipping
and global network susceptibility are already published. The present
candidate is narrower: a two-patch Allee model, a measurable loss-memory
hypothesis, attack-debt path identity and an access-gate protocol. No priority
claim is made.

Boerlijst, Oudman & de Roos (2013), “Catastrophic Collapse Can Occur without
Early Warning: Examples of Silent Catastrophes in Structured Ecological
Models,” PLoS ONE 8(4):e62033, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0062033. Their
stage-structured models show that variance/autocorrelation indicators can be
silent when noise excites directions away from the critical eigenvector. This
is theoretical/model evidence for limits of CSD, not field confirmation of
(1).

Gsell et al. (2016), “Evaluating early-warning indicators of critical
transitions in natural aquatic ecosystems,” PNAS 113:E8089-E8095, DOI
10.1073/pnas.1608242113. They analyze long records from five freshwater
ecosystems with documented persistent transitions; indicators sometimes
precede transitions, but agreement and reliability are low. This is relevant
observational evidence that generic warnings are inconsistent.

Carpenter et al. (2011), “Early warnings of regime shifts: a whole-ecosystem
experiment,” Science 332(6033):1079-1082, DOI 10.1126/science.1203672. Gradual
top-predator addition in a manipulated lake produced warning signals more
than a year before the food-web transition completed. It is a positive
experimental counterexample to any claim that CSD always fails.

Murdoch (1969), “Switching in General Predators: Experiments on Predator
Specificity and Stability of Prey Populations,” Ecological Monographs
39:335-354, DOI 10.2307/1942352, experimentally studied generalist predator
switching and training with snails feeding on mussels/barnacles. Hossie, Chan
& Murray (2021), “Increasing availability of palatable prey induces
predator-dependence and increases predation on unpalatable prey,” Scientific
Reports 11:6763, DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-86080-x, experimentally manipulated
dragonfly-nymph/tadpole prey mixes and found alternate-prey context could
change kill-rate relationships. These support the feasibility and context
sensitivity of measuring multi-prey attack. Neither validates a response to
the *rate of prey loss* or the s equation in (1).
