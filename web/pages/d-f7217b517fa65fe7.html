STRONGEST RESULT
For fixed public M,B<infinity, A=A^T real with ||A||op<=M and |b|<=B, put
  J_i=(1/2)sum_(a=1)^N sigma_i^(a), s=N^(3/4),
  K=2J^2/N+Q_A(J)/N^(3/2)+b.J/s,
  rho=exp(K)/Tr exp(K), T(rho,sigma)=||rho-sigma||_1/2.
For every N with t0=2/N-M/N^(3/2)>0 there is an explicit state sigma,
which is a convex mixture of IDENTICAL single-qubit products tau^(tensor N),
such that
  sup_(A,b) T(rho,sigma) <= min{1, E_N(M,B)},
  E_N(M,B) = [3M/(2N^(3/2))] U_N(M,B)/D_N(M,B),                 (1)
where entirely public finite binomial sums define the quantities on the right:
  j=j_min,j_min+1,...,N/2; j_min=0 even N, 1/2 odd N,
  d_j=2j+1,
  m_N(j)=d_j/(N+1) binom(N+1,N/2-j),
  a_N(j)=d_j m_N(j) exp[2j(j+1)/N],
  r_j=(j+1/2)/s,
  D_N=sum_j a_N(j) exp[-M r_j^2-B r_j],
  P(r)=4M r^2+(B/2)r+(4M r^2+B r)^2/3,
  U_N=sum_j a_N(j) exp[M r_j^2+B r_j] P(r_j).
The quotient U_N/D_N is bounded for all sufficiently large N, for each fixed
M,B. Consequently the approximation error is O_(M,B)(N^(-3/2)). For M=0
the construction is exact. This is a fully separable whole-density result,
stronger than a statement concerning two-spin reductions or collective moments.

CONSTRUCTION AND PROOF

1. Exact separable invariant base, proved rather than imported.
For t>0 define the positive measure
  nu_t(dx)=(4pi t)^(-3/2) exp(-t/4) exp[-|x|^2/(4t)]
              [sinh(|x|/2)/(|x|/2)] d^3x.
Then, on EVERY finite-dimensional SU(2) representation,
  exp(tJ^2) = integral exp(x.J) nu_t(dx).                       (2)
Proof: rotation invariance makes the integral scalar on each spin-j irrep.
Its scalar is the trace divided by d_j. Write k=j+1/2 and r=|x|. The character
of exp(rJ_z) is sinh(kr)/sinh(r/2), so the scalar integral equals
  (4pi t)^(-3/2)exp(-t/4) [8pi/d_j]
       integral_0^infty r exp[-r^2/(4t)] sinh(kr) dr.
Differentiate the elementary integral
  integral_0^infty exp[-r^2/(4t)] cosh(kr) dr
       =sqrt(pi t)exp(tk^2).
This gives exp[t(k^2-1/4)]=exp[tj(j+1)], proving (2).
Since exp(x.J)=[exp(x.sigma/2)]^(tensor N), (2) is an iid product mixture.
nu_t itself has total mass one, by the j=0 case. A normalized base is sampled
from the trace-weighted density nu_t(dx)[2cosh(|x|/2)]^N.

2. Shift the anisotropy into a positive quadratic form.
Set C=A+MI>=0, diagonalize C=sum_i lambda_i n_i n_i^T, 0<=lambda_i<=2M,
and let c_i=lambda_i/N^(3/2), F_i=n_i.J, v=b/s. Then
  H=sum_i c_i F_i^2+v.J,
  K=t0 J^2+H,
with t0 as above. The Casimir commutes with H and all F_i.

3. A separability-preserving semigroup.
Define the linear map on N-spin operators
  L(X)={H,X}/2 - (1/4)sum_i c_i[F_i,[F_i,X]].                  (3)
For c_i>=0 its quadratic summand is
  L_i(X)=c_i/4 (F_i^2 X+2F_i X F_i+X F_i^2).
This is the infinitesimal Gaussian local-filter map:
  exp(delta L_i)(X)
    =E_Z exp(sqrt(c_i delta/2) Z F_i) X
               exp(sqrt(c_i delta/2) Z F_i), Z~Normal(0,1).
The exact equality follows by using commuting left/right multiplication by F_i
and the scalar Gaussian mgf; not just by a truncated stochastic expansion.
The field map is exp(delta v.J/2) X exp(delta v.J/2).
Every filter is g^(tensor N) for a two-by-two g. Lie-Trotter products therefore
preserve the cone of positive iid-product mixtures; its closure is closed in
finite dimension. Thus exp(L) preserves that cone. Equivalently, Stratonovich
  dG=sum_i sqrt(c_i/2) F_i G o dW_i +(v.J/2)G dt
has G=g^(tensor N) trajectorywise and E[G X G^*]=exp(tL)X.

Define the unnormalized positive operator
  W=exp(L)(exp(t0 J^2)), sigma=W/Tr W.                        (4)
Equation (2) plus the filter argument proves sigma fully separable and even an
iid-product mixture. The maps are not trace preserving; the final trace weights
are part of the classical mixing law. This is not a claim of free postselection.

4. Finite-dimensional double-commutator estimate.
In spin-j dimension d_j define M_H(X)={H_j,X}/2. Because the Casimir is central,
  W_j=exp[t0 j(j+1)] Y_j, Y_j=exp(L_j)I_j,
whereas the target block is exp[t0 j(j+1)]exp(H_j).
For positive X, complete positivity and Tr L_j(X)=Tr H_j X give
  Tr exp(uL_j)X <= exp(u||H_j||op)Tr X.
The same induced trace-norm bound holds on Hermitian X by its Jordan decomposition.
Duhamel gives
  Y_j-exp(H_j)= -1/4 sum_i c_i integral_0^1
      exp[(1-q)L_j] [F_i,[F_i,exp(qH_j)]] dq.                (5)

For any Hermitian F,H and q>=0, differentiating
exp(q exp(i theta F)H exp(-i theta F)) twice at theta=0 yields
  ||[F,[F,exp(qH)]]||_1
    <=d exp(q||H||op)
      {q||[F,[F,H]]||op+q^2||[F,H]||op^2}.                 (6)
To verify the factors: the first derivative of the exponential is a simplex
integral with total coefficient q; the two ordered terms in the second derivative
have simplex volume 1/2 each and total coefficient q^2. The inserted operators
have total exponential length q. Applying submultiplicativity with
||X||_1<=d||X||op proves (6). No operator monotonicity of exp is invoked.
Equations (5)-(6) imply
  ||Y_j-exp(H_j)||_1
   <= [d_j exp(||H_j||op)/4] sum_i c_i
        {D2_i/2+D1_i^2/3},                                 (7)
where D1_i=||[F_i,H_j]||op, D2_i=||[F_i,[F_i,H_j]]||op.

5. Rotation derivative bounds and normalization.
Conjugation by exp(i theta F_i) rotates the symmetric matrix C and vector b.
For the skew rotation matrix Omega, ||Omega||op=1, so first/second matrix
derivatives have norms <=2||C||op and <=4||C||op. The field derivatives have
norms <=|b|. Diagonalizing a real symmetric D and using sum J_i^2=j(j+1)I
shows ||Q_D||op<=||D||op j(j+1). Thus uniformly
  ||H_j||op <= 2M j(j+1)/s^2+B j/s <=2M r_j^2+B r_j,
  D1_i <=4M r_j^2+B r_j,
  D2_i <=8M r_j^2+B r_j,
  sum_i c_i<=6M/N^(3/2).
Multiplying (7) by multiplicity and the scalar base, and using the FIRST,
sharper bound on ||H_j|| in the preceding display before rounding radii,
t0 j(j+1)+||H_j||op<=2j(j+1)/N+M r_j^2+B r_j, gives
  ||W-exp(K)||_1 <= [3M/(2N^(3/2))] U_N.                    (8)
For the ORIGINAL A, the sector nonscalar generator norm is <=M r_j^2+B r_j.
Therefore Z=Tr exp(K)>=D_N. For positive W,G=exp(K),
  T(W/Tr W,G/Tr G)<=||W-G||_1/Tr G,
by the triangle inequality and |Tr(W-G)|<=||W-G||_1. This proves (1).

6. Why the public moment quotient is uniformly bounded.
The supplied independent N20 audit (local handle d-e52dcc61c4754c0b.txt, Section 5)
proves that a_N(j)/[2^N N^(3/4)] converges as a radial Riemann-sum measure to
  c0 r^2 exp[-4r^4/3] dr, c0=8sqrt(2/pi),
and has integrable envelopes after ANY fixed tilt exp(K1 r^2+K2 r), including
any fixed polynomial factor. Its proof is elementary binomial entropy plus
Stirling: in k=j+1/2 <=(N+1)/4,
  a_N(j)/2^N <=C r_j^2 exp(-r_j^4/6),
and in the exterior it is <=2(N+1)exp[1-(N+1)/192]. Exterior tilts grow only
exp(O(sqrt N)), so do not defeat this suppression. Compact radii 1<=r_j<=2
bound D_N/[2^N N^(3/4)] below uniformly. Hence
  U_N/D_N ->
    [integral_0^infty r^2 exp(-4r^4/3+Mr^2+Br)P(r)dr]
    /[integral_0^infty r^2 exp(-4r^4/3-Mr^2-Br)dr] <infinity.
This suffices for the asserted O(N^(-3/2)); the finite-N certificate (1) does
not require an unspecified asymptotic constant or a radial convergence rate.

INTERFACE AND LIMITS
The construction (3)-(4) DEPENDS on A,b. It establishes proximity to the
fully separable cone and gives a known-parameter preparation representation.
It does not itself furnish a parameter-independent reverse channel from ONE
quartic observation. The existing N20 reverse prepares coherent spin sectors
with maximally mixed multiplicities; those states generally contain singlets
and are only two-producible by the separate permutation-decoding construction.
The O(N^(-3/2)) theorem cannot be inserted into the operational recovery argument
as a universal decoder without an additional proof.

A finite Lie-Trotter iid-mixture preparation bound and a universal separable
decoder are proved below. Physical filter normalization/success and the
complexity of sampling trace-weighted Brownian trajectories must be charged.
The parent worker is separately auditing an importance-resampling compiler;
that algorithm is not counted as executed or certified by this file.

ADDENDUM C — A PARAMETER-INDEPENDENT FULLY SEPARABLE DECODER

There is also a universal cq preparation map, independent of A,b:
  tau_N(x)=exp(2N^(-1/4) x.sigma)/Tr exp(2N^(-1/4) x.sigma),
  Ssep_N(x)=tau_N(x)^(tensor N).                              (11)
For p_(A,b)(x) proportional to exp[-4|x|^4/3+x^T A x+b.x],
  sup_(A,b) T(Ssep_N p_(A,b),rho_N(A,b)) ->0.                 (12)
This supplies a fully separable reverse channel for the N20 statistical experiment.
Together with its already audited forward channel T_N it gives uniform whole-state
recovery using a measurement followed by N local single-qubit preparations.
The O(N^(-3/2)) rate in (9) is NOT asserted for (12). A public bound below has
an O(N^(-1/4)) term and a weighted scalar Laplace remainder tending to zero.

C1. The exact scaled base prior.
Set z=2N^(-1/4)r and d0=1-M/(2sqrt N), r=|x|. After the change of variables
xfield=4N^(-1/4)x, the normalized iid-mixture prior in (2), with t=t0, has
Lebesgue density q_N,M(x) proportional to
  f_N,M(r)=sinch(z)exp[N logcosh(z)-2sqrt(N)r^2/d0],
where sinch(z)=sinh(z)/z, sinch(0)=1. The factor 2^N cancels on normalization.
Its limit q_inf,M(x) is proportional to exp[-4r^4/3-Mr^2].
For EVERY fixed L,K>=0,
  integral exp(Lr^2+Kr)|q_N,M-q_inf,M| d^3x ->0,             (13)
and all corresponding weighted polynomial moments remain uniformly bounded.
Proof: on compact r, Taylor expansion gives
  Nlogcosh(z)-2sqrt(N)r^2/d0
       =-4r^4/3-Mr^2+O_(M,R)(N^(-1/2)); sinch(z)=1+O_R(N^(-1/2)).
For 0<=z<=1, tanh(z)>=z/2, hence sech(z)^2<=1-z^2/4. Integrating twice yields
logcosh(z)<=z^2/2-z^4/48. Since sinch(z)<=exp(z), or more sharply <=exp(z^2/2),
the interior is dominated by exp[-r^4/3+(L+2)r^2+Kr] for N>=1.
For z>=1, logcosh(z)/z^2 is decreasing: its derivative has sign
z tanh(z)-2logcosh(z)<0, as the negative derivative of that numerator follows
from tanh(z)-z sech(z)^2>0. Thus logcosh(z)<=logcosh(1)z^2, and with
delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0 the exterior is bounded by
exp[-4delta sqrt(N)r^2+Lr^2+(K+2)r]. For sufficiently large N this is bounded
by an integrable Gaussian envelope. The exterior starts at r>=N^(1/4)/2,
so its mass vanishes. A fixed compact radial interval bounds normalization below.
These bounds prove (13) by dominated convergence, with the ordinary r^2 Jacobian.

C2. Uniform weight moments without physical postselection.
Write g for the two-by-two random filter at time one from Section 3 and set
  w(x,g)=[Tr(g tau_N(x) g^*)]^N,
  mu=E_(q_N,M,g) w =Tr W/Zbase.
The exact L is selfadjoint for the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product. Scalar Jensen
on its spectral measure in the vector I_j gives
  Tr exp(L_j)(I_j)/d_j >=exp[Tr H_j/d_j]>=1.
Hence mu>=1; this argument does not claim every individual trajectory has w>=1.

For an integer p>=1, w^p is the trace weight of the same local filter on pN
replicas. With J now the total spin on n=pN qubits, the generator is still (3),
with c_i,v defined from ORIGINAL N. For any kappa>0,
  H_n<=aJ^2+K I, a=(2M+kappa)/N^(3/2), K=B^2/(4kappa).
This uses Q_C<=2M J^2 and the scalar inequality B sqrt(J^2)/s<=kappa J^2/s^2+K.
The upper bound is central, so its product with H_n is commuting. The positive
central curve F_t=exp[t(aJ^2+K)] satisfies
  (d/dt-L_n)F_t=(aJ^2+K-H_n)F_t>=0.
CP comparison via Duhamel gives exp(L_n)I<=exp(aJ^2+K). Selfadjointness then
implies E_g w^p<=exp(K)Tr[tau_N(x)^(tensor n) exp(aJ^2)].

An elementary bound for this last quantity follows directly from (2). If m is
the Bloch vector of tau, scalar Hoeffding for its +/-|y|/2 spectral probabilities
gives Tr(tau exp(y.sigma/2))<=exp(m.y/2+|y|^2/8). Also sinch(|y|/2)<=exp(|y|^2/8)
by Hoeffding for a uniform variable on [-|y|/2,|y|/2]. Gaussian integration gives
  Tr[tau^(tensor n) exp(aJ^2)]
    <=exp(-a/4)d^(-3/2)exp[a n^2|m|^2/(4d)],
  d=1-a(n+1)/2>0.                                          (14)
For tau_N(x), |m|=tanh(2N^(-1/4)r)<=2N^(-1/4)r. Take kappa=1 and N large
enough that d>=1/2. For p=2 this gives the PUBLIC bound
  E_g w(x,g)^2 <=2^(3/2)exp(B^2/4)exp[8(2M+1)r^2],
  K2_N:=2^(3/2)exp(B^2/4) integral q_N,M exp[8(2M+1)r^2]d^3x,
  E_(q_N,M,g) w^2<=K2_N=O_(M,B)(1).                         (15)
For definiteness N>=[4(2M+1)]^2 suffices for every p=2 denominator used here
and for the exponential-moment bound in C4. Higher p needs its own threshold.
The trace moment is part of the classical mixture law; no quantum success
probability is discarded.

C4. Identify the weight law using product-state concentration.
Let E=exp(L)I-exp(H), an Hermitian collective operator. The base integral gives
  integral q_N,M |Tr[tau_N(x)^(tensor N)E]|d^3x
      <=Tr[exp(t0J^2)|E|]/Zbase

  Lerr_N=[3M/(2s^2)] Usharp/Zbase=O_(M,B)(N^(-3/2)).         (17)

For fixed x, write the Bloch vector as m=tanh(2N^(-1/4)r)u and mu0=Nm/2.
Let Y=J-mu0 I, C=A+MI, and
  h0=mu0^T C mu0/s^2+b.mu0/s,
  h=x^T C x+b.x.
Then H-h0=(2Cmu0/s^2+b/s).Y+Q_C(Y)/s^2. Under tau_N(x)^(tensor N),
  Tr tau^(tensor N)(H-h0)^2 <=Vvar_N(r),
  Vvar_N(r)=(4Mr+B)^2/(2sqrt N)+18M^2/N.                   (18)
To verify: ||mu0||<=s r, the SYMMETRIZED covariance of J is N(I-mm^T)/4, and a centered
single-qubit component has range one, variance <=1/4 and absolute size <=1.
Thus the fourth moment of each centered collective component is <=N^2/4.
Writing Q_C(Y)=sum lambda_i(n_i.Y)^2 and using trace Cauchy-Schwarz bounds its
second moment by (sum lambda_i)^2 N^2/4<=9M^2N^2. The inequality
(X+Y)^2<=2X^2+2Y^2 then gives (18).
Also r-[N^(1/4)/2]tanh(2N^(-1/4)r)<=4r^3/(3sqrt N), so
  |h0-h| <=[(16M/3)r^4+(4B/3)r^3]/sqrt N.                 (19)

Because aJ^2+K commutes with H, the bound on H in C2 implies

C5. The limiting tilted prior and a fully public recovery remainder.

ADDENDUM D — KNOWN-PARAMETER CLASSICAL SAMPLER AND LOCAL PREPARATION

This construction applies when A,b are known and supplied to the preparation
algorithm. It uses finite Trotter products, classical importance resampling,
and N single-qubit preparations. It uses no physical filtering/postselection.
Its cost statement is in an explicitly declared real-arithmetic/random-normal
model; a complete finite-bit compiler has not been written or executed.

D1. A seed rejection sampler using only normal/uniform draws.
Use the ORIGINAL HS field radius rfield, and set R=N^(1/4)rfield,
y=R/(2N^(1/4)), f(y)=y^2/2-logcosh(y), d0=1-M/(2sqrt N). The exact normalized
seed radius has unnormalized density
  Q_N(R)=R^2 sinch(y)exp[-M R^2/(16d0)-N f(y)], R>=0.       (22)
For N>=M^2, on 1<=R<=2, f(y)<=y^4/12 and sinch(y)>=1 give
  Zseed=integral Q_N(R)dR >=L0:=(7/3)exp[-M/2-1/12].
The bound f(y)<=y^4/12 is global: tanh(y)<=y implies
y-tanh(y)=integral_0^y tanh(t)^2dt<=y^3/3, then integrate once more.

Let delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0. For y<=1, C1 gives f(y)>=y^4/48 and
sinch(y)<=exp(y^2/2)<=exp(1/2), hence Q_N(R)<=exp(1/2)R^2exp(-R^4/768).
For y>1, f(y)>=delta y^2 and sinch(y)<=exp(y). If Ndelta>=2,
exp[y-Ndelta y^2]<=exp[-Ndelta y^2/2]. If also sqrt N>=8/delta, this gives
Q_N(R)<=R^2exp(-R^2). Therefore the SUM of the two displayed envelopes works
globally. Completing the scalar square -u^2+u<=1/4 with u=R^2/sqrt(768)
gives the simpler global envelope
  Q_N(R)<=h(R):=exp(3/4)R^2exp[-R^2/sqrt(768)]+R^2exp(-R^2),
  Cg:=integral h(R)dR=(sqrt(pi)/4)[exp(3/4)768^(3/4)+1].
Sample a mixture of the two Maxwell laws with probabilities proportional to
their two terms in Cg. Draw Z~Normal(0,I_3); in the first component take
R=768^(1/4)|Z|/sqrt(2), and in the second take R=|Z|/sqrt(2). The orientation
u=Z/|Z| is uniform on the sphere and independent of the radius. Accept with
probability Q_N(R)/h(R). Its expected attempts are <=Cg/L0, a fixed public
constant depending on M only. Set xfield=R N^(-1/4)u and prepare the seed
one-qubit tau=exp(xfield.sigma/2)/[2cosh(|xfield|/2)].
All envelope inequalities have been proved here; no mode/root/Gamma oracle
is needed. The finitely many N below the stated thresholds are outside this
asymptotic runtime statement, but (9)-(10) still give the mathematical approximation.

D2. Weight second moments, uniform in the number of Trotter steps.
For a finite Trotter trajectory g_m, every two-by-two factor has determinant one,
so g_m is in SL(2,C). For each fixed g_m,
  E_seed [Tr(g_m tau g_m^*)]^N
      =Tr rho_base (g_m^*g_m)^(tensor N)>=1.
Indeed det(g_m^*g_m)=1, so its tensor power is a collective exponential
exp(h.J_u); rho_base is SU(2)-invariant and the J_u spectrum is paired +/-m.
Scalar Jensen in each spin sector gives expectation >=1. Thus the total mean
weight mu_m is >=1 EXACTLY at every finite m.

On n=pN replicas, each quadratic factor has a central upper comparison
exp(c_i J^2/m), and the field factor has exp[(J^2/s^2+B^2/4)/m]. These
comparisons are the positive central supersolution argument in C2, with kappa=1.
Every factor commutes with multiplication by central functions, so the product
comparison survives composition in either order. Consequently
  Phi_m,n^*(I)<=exp[(6M+1)J^2/s^2+B^2/4],
uniformly m. For p=2, (14) gives, when its d>=1/2,
  E_g w_m(R,g)^2 <=3exp(B^2/4)exp(D R^2), D=(6M+1)/2.

This uses only normals/uniforms for sampling; Gamma appears only in a fixed

D3. Importance resampling gives an actual averaged output state.
Draw Rprop iid proposals (seed xfield and m-step local-filter paths) and compute
their positive weights w_l=[Tr(g_l tau_l g_l^*)]^N using two-by-two matrices.
Select one index with probabilities w_l/sum_k w_k. Prepare N copies of the
normalized qubit g_l tau_l g_l^*/Tr(g_l tau_l g_l^*).
For any test f in [0,1], let mu=Ew, nu=Ewf, and let mu_hat,nu_hat be sample
averages. The selected output expectation is E(nu_hat/mu_hat). Since
E nu_hat=nu and 0<=nu_hat/mu_hat<=1,
  |E(nu_hat/mu_hat)-nu/mu|
    =|E[(nu_hat/mu_hat)(1-mu_hat/mu)]|
    <=E|mu_hat-mu|/mu

Taking the supremum over quantum effects after product-state preparation yields
the trace-distance bound sqrt(K2star/Rprop). This is a bound on the AVERAGED
algorithmic output law/state, not an assertion that a finite empirical measure
is TV-close to a continuous target (it is not).
