## 1. Independently reconstructed sphere derivatives

Let y=Oz, ||y||=1, m=y∘y, g=Gm, and q=mᵀGm. Symmetry of G gives

    grad_R q=4 diag(y)g,
    Hess_R q=8 diag(y)G diag(y)+4 diag(g),
    yᵀ grad_R q=4q.

Projection onto the tangent space therefore gives grad_S q=4y∘(g-q1). For any unit tangent vector v, the unit-speed sphere geodesic is y(t)=y cos(t)+v sin(t). Direct second differentiation, including y''(0)=-y, gives

    q''(0)=8(y∘v)ᵀG(y∘v)+4 sum_i v_i²(g_i-q).

Thus the draft uses the full Riemannian Hessian quadratic form, not the ambient Hessian or an unconstrained second derivative. An orthogonal change by the unknown O preserves norms, tangency, gradient norm and sphere-Hessian eigenvalues. Their numerical values can be obtained directly from the observed quartic without constructing O for this argument.

## 2. Exact stationary-point exclusion

At a sphere-stationary y, each nonzero coordinate has g_i=q. Suppose i and j are active. The vector

    v=(y_j e_i-y_i e_j)/sqrt(m_i+m_j)

is unit, tangent and supported in the active coordinates. Its second derivative is exactly

    8[m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j)](e_i-e_j)ᵀG(e_i-e_j).

Since ||e_i-e_j||²=2, it is at least 16 mu m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j)>0, where mu=lambda_min(G)>0. Therefore every non-axis stationary point has a strict ascent direction and cannot be a local maximum. Every local maximum is an axis. No assertion that every axis is a local maximum is justified or needed.

The draft's counterexample is correct: G=[[1,2],[2,5]] is positive definite, and at e_1 the tangent curvature toward e_2 is 4(G_21-G_11)=4>0. Conversely the continuous quartic on the compact sphere has a global maximum, so there is at least one usable axis maximum at every exact deflation stage. This is an existence statement, not a finite-cost search procedure.

The strict-saddle terminology is objective-oriented: for maximization, the proved obstruction is positive curvature at non-axis stationary points. Such a point could be a local minimum rather than a saddle with both signs; the precise ascent statement avoids ambiguity.

## 3. Quantitative approximate-stationary exclusion

Suppose ||grad_S q||₂<=epsilon. For each active coordinate,

    |g_i-q|<=epsilon/(4 sqrt(m_i)).

For the same pair tangent direction the first positive term is bounded below exactly as above. The two other terms have lower bound

    -epsilon [m_j/sqrt(m_i)+m_i/sqrt(m_j)]/(m_i+m_j).

If m_i,m_j>=delta>0, the bracket divided by m_i+m_j is a convex combination of 1/sqrt(m_i) and 1/sqrt(m_j), so it is at most 1/sqrt(delta). Also

    m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j)>=delta/2.

Consequently

    q''(0)>=8 mu delta-epsilon/sqrt(delta).

Under epsilon<=4 mu delta^(3/2), this is at least 4 mu delta. Hence the simultaneous observable conditions

    ||grad_S q||₂<=4 mu delta^(3/2),
    lambda_max(Hess_S q)<4 mu delta

force the second-largest squared hidden coordinate to be <delta. For n>=2, let i be a largest coordinate. Then 1-m_i=sum_(j!=i)m_j<(n-1)delta and

    min_(sign) ||y-sign e_i||²
       =2(1-sqrt(m_i))<=2(1-m_i)<2(n-1)delta.

The strict Hessian threshold is appropriate: the proof excludes >=delta, including equality, and therefore produces a strict distance bound. The draft should explicitly treat n=1 separately because its displayed strict distance bound would read 0<0. In dimension 1 the axis is already determined up to sign, so no acquisition or curvature test is required. This is a harmless endpoint amendment, not a failure of the argument.

The estimate uses the actual latent mu. It does not furnish the learner with mu or an input-derived lower bound. No implementation may treat those conditional thresholds as supplied oracle data. The property also does not assert uniformly negative curvature at every acceptable axis maximum; that stronger bound is unnecessary for the proven proximity test and is not established.

## 4. Exact deflation preserves the promise

Suppose an exact unit axis u=sign Oᵀe_i is obtained in observed z coordinates. Choose any exact orthonormal basis B for u-perp, with B an n by n-1 matrix. Then e_iᵀOB=0. Removing this zero row from OB gives a square orthogonal matrix O', because BᵀB=I and O is orthogonal. For z=Bx,

    q(Bx)=((O'x)∘(O'x))ᵀ G_(i removed) ((O'x)∘(O'x)).

The retained matrix is a principal submatrix of G and is positive definite. Its least eigenvalue is at least mu by the Rayleigh quotient applied to vectors with zero i-th coordinate. Thus the same structural result applies inductively to exact restrictions. The n=1 endpoint terminates with the final axis. An exactly acquired frame yields the promised factor directions for subsequent exact algebraic processing; their representation and recovery in the original rational coordinates remain separate tasks.

This reduction uses exact axes and exact orthogonality. If u is only approximately an axis, the deleted hidden coordinate does not vanish identically on u-perp, and the restricted quartic need not retain the exact diagonal-square promise. A finite-precision algorithm therefore needs a perturbation analysis, not an unmodified invocation of the exact lemma at each stage.

## 5. What this gate authorizes and what remains unresolved

The valid downstream change is to investigate sphere-based sequential acquisition without waiting for a full weighted O(n) stationary-landscape theorem. The root's conclusion that this is an alternative qualitative route is justified. The gate does not establish the requested polynomial-bit coefficient-only synthesis algorithm.

Still missing are: an acquired conditioning/separation bound from the compressed rational input; a charged finite-precision procedure producing the stated approximate first/second-order conditions; stability and accumulated error control for approximate restrictions; and exact reconstruction of a rational factor subspace/certificate from the approximate frame, including whitening and algebraic-number costs. The latent factorization may not be handed to any of these procedures. Exact final expansion and positive-definiteness checks would verify a candidate certificate but do not prove its efficient acquisition.

No prior-art claim was audited in this bounded assignment. The structure is consistent with the simple explanation that a strictly convex quadratic on the probability simplex has no nonvertex local maxima, while the squared-coordinate sphere parameterization makes each positive-support face accessible.
