## 1. Exact contract

Let F be a homogeneous quartic polynomial on R^(2x2), and let U be a
neighborhood of zero. The evolution is in nondivergence form

    v_t = sum_(i,j) C^(ij)(Dv) v_(ij),

where the output matrices are continuous at zero, C^(ij)=C^(ji), and C(0)
is strongly parabolic: the symmetric output principal symbol is bounded below
by kappa |n|^2 I for some kappa>0. Let N(A,z) be homogeneous quadratic in the
six compatible Hessian entries z, with C2 coefficients in A near zero.

The required nullity can be local: for every constant B and compact smooth
phi with B+Dphi contained in U,

    integral N(B+Dphi,D2phi) = 0.

The pointwise hypothesis is P_C(A,z)+N(A,z)>=0 for every A near zero and
every compatible Hessian z, where

    P_C(A,z) = sum F_(alpha k,beta l)(A)
                       z_(beta,lk) [sum C^(ij)(A) z_(ij)]_alpha.

This all-jet hypothesis is stronger than an inequality only along one family
of solutions. No existence, global regularity, affine relaxation, or
quasiconvexity conclusion is supplied by the reduction itself.

## 2. The canonical density is the correct derivative

For a smooth trajectory for which integrations by parts are justified,
use relative energy integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)] on the whole plane, so
the affine background does not make the energy infinite. Its derivative is

    d/dt integral [F(Dv)-F(B)]
      = integral F_(alpha k)(Dv) partial_k v_(t,alpha)
      = - integral F_(alpha k,beta l)(Dv) v_(beta,lk) v_(t,alpha)
      = - integral P_C(Dv,D2v).

This calculation integrates onto DF before substituting the evolution. It
does not differentiate C. Continuity of C at zero is all the reduction later
uses; stronger regularity may be needed for a particular PDE solution theory.

## 3. Homogenization, including preservation of nullity

Write the coefficientwise Taylor expansion

    N(tA,z) = N0(z)+t N1(A,z)+t^2 N2(A,z)+o(t^2),
    N2(A,z) = (1/2) D_A^2 N(0,z)[A,A].

The remainders are uniform on compact sets of A and z, since there are only
21 coefficient functions.

At A=0, P_C(0,z)=0, so N0 is a nonnegative quadratic form. Substitute
epsilon phi in the nullity identity at B=0 and divide by epsilon^2.
Taking epsilon to zero shows that N0(D2phi) has zero integral for every
compact smooth phi. Nonnegativity and continuity imply that it is zero
pointwise on every such phi. A cutoff quadratic potential realizes any
prescribed compatible Hessian at an interior point, so N0(z)=0 for all z.

Equivalently, constant-coefficient symbol testing shows that N0 vanishes
on all pure jets a tensor n tensor n. Those jets span the six-dimensional
Hessian space, and a positive semidefinite quadratic form vanishing on a
spanning set is zero. Both routes justify the proposed zeroth-order step.

For fixed A,z, quartic homogeneity gives P_C(tA,z)=O(t^2). Positivity for
both signs of t forces N1(A,z)=0. Dividing positivity by t^2 and taking
t to zero now gives

    P_(C(0))(A,z)+N2(A,z) >= 0

for every A,z. The arbitrary A are legitimate because tA is in U for small
t, and D2F(tA)=t^2 D2F(A).

Nullity of N2 needs its own scaling argument. For arbitrary B0,psi, use
B=tB0 and phi=t psi in local nullity, and use quadratic homogeneity in z:

    0 = t^2 integral N(t(B0+Dpsi),D2psi)
      = t^4 integral N2(B0+Dpsi,D2psi)+o(t^4).

Uniform Taylor remainder on the bounded gradient image permits division by
t^4 and passage to the limit. Thus N2 is globally null in the polynomial
space of A-degree two and Hessian-degree two. FV's exact null-space
classification applies: N2=omega2(B1,B2), with omega2 a unique closed
two-form having homogeneous quadratic coefficients.

This argument does not need the full C2 null-correction classification in
the incoming report. It uses only its elementary local scaling idea and the
previous FV polynomial classification, which was replayed below. It also
avoids any unjustified claim that a variable-coefficient heat density is
integrally equivalent to P_C.

## 4. Explicit constant-coefficient bridge

Put q_k=(F_(alpha k))_alpha and denote the two columns of A by A_1,A_2.
For constant output/spatial coefficients C define the one-form

    eta_C = [(C21)^T q1-(C11)^T q2] dot dA_1
            +[(C22)^T q1-(C12)^T q2] dot dA_2.

Let B_i=partial_i Dv. Then, pointwise on compatible Hessians,

    P_C(A,z)-E_C(A,z) = (d eta_C)_A(B1,B2),
    E_C = sum_(i,j) D2F(A)[B_i,C^(ij) B_j].

To derive this, subtract the two divergences

    partial_k(F_(alpha k) C^(ij)_(alpha gamma) v_(gamma,ij))
      -partial_i(F_(alpha k) C^(ij)_(alpha gamma) v_(gamma,jk)).

All third jets cancel by commutation, leaving exactly P_C-E_C. Its first
flux component is eta_C(B2), and its second is -eta_C(B1), which proves
the displayed pullback identity. Target matrices need not be symmetric.

For a quartic polynomial F, eta_C is cubic and d eta_C has homogeneous
quadratic coefficients. Consequently

    P_(C(0))+N2 = E_(C(0))+(omega2+d eta_(C(0)))(B1,B2)

is precisely a constant strongly parabolic corrected-heat certificate in
the FV class. The total two-form is closed and quadratic. FV's no-go for
its exposed quartic F, and for F-gamma det(A)^2 for gamma>=0, therefore
contradicts the proposed quasilinear certificate.

The independent script checks this bridge for an arbitrary symmetric 4x4
Hessian and all 12 real constant output/spatial coefficients. It also
reconstructs the 15-by-21 pure-Hessian symbol rank and the four independent
third-jet coefficients forcing closedness: -T012,-T013,T023,T123.

## 5. A real failure of the variable-coefficient substitution

Use F(A)=a^4 in row-major coordinates, C11=I, C12=0, and
C22=I+b E12, where E12 sends the second output component to the first.
This system is smooth. For |b|<=1 its symmetric principal symbol is at
least (1/2)|n|^2 I.

The algebraic difference is

    P_C-E_C = 12 a^2 [z0 z2-z1^2+b(z0 z5-z1 z4)].

On the 2pi-periodic square take

    v1=t(sin x+sin y),    v2=t sin x cos y.

For 0<t<=1 the gradient stays in the strongly parabolic region, yet

    integral_(0,2pi)^2 (P_C-E_C) = 3 pi^2 t^5 != 0.

This exact integral is checked independently. Hence substituting E_C for
the true energy derivative before freezing C at zero would invalidate a
proof. The frozen argument above does not make that substitution.

## 6. Useful extensions and essential limits

* The reduction applies to every homogeneous quartic polynomial. Thus the
  certificate cone with continuous gradient-dependent coefficients and C2
  local quadratic-Hessian null corrections equals the constant-coefficient,
  closed homogeneous-quadratic-form certificate cone. The converse inclusion
  is immediate by using constant C and a polynomial correction.
* The FV invariant positive-decomposition obstruction also transfers when
  each heat summand satisfies this enlarged smooth local certificate contract.
  Its unrestricted polyconvexity and quasiconvexity questions remain UNKNOWN.
* Lower-order additions b(Dv) contribute only terms linear in the Hessian
  to the canonical derivative. If a full all-jet positive density uses the
  same quadratic-null correction, replacing z by s z and dividing by s^2
  as s tends to infinity extracts the same forbidden quadratic density.
* Gradient-only divergence-form quasilinear systems are included when their
  expansion as C(Dv)D2v has strongly parabolic C(0). Spatial indices may be
  symmetrized because mixed Hessians agree.
* Continuity of C at zero, rather than C2 regularity of C, is sufficient.
  N only needs a coefficientwise second-order Taylor expansion with a uniform
  little-o remainder at zero for this proof; C2 is a convenient sufficient
  condition.
* Strong parabolicity at zero is essential. The formal gradient flow
  v_t=div DF(Dv) has the nonnegative canonical density |div DF(Dv)|^2 and
  zero null correction, but its coefficients vanish at zero for every
  homogeneous quartic. It escapes this theorem and supplies no automatic
  affine-relaxation or Jensen argument.
* Singular corrections at zero, nonlocal operations, higher jets, and
  positivity only along restricted solutions remain outside this argument.
