1. What the heated standards actually determine

Use a matched receiver input and power-wave normalization in kelvin. Its
power for a uniform passive one-port at T with reflection gamma is

 P(T,gamma)=G[T(1-|gamma|^2)+Tu|gamma|^2+T0
                           +2 Re(gamma Q)].                 (1)

Q is a complex receiver input/output noise correlation; its sign convention
can be conjugated without changing any bound below. The inherited exact
receiver model is calibration_budget.py:21-37. For a nonzero receiver input
reflection, the feedback denominator must also be acquired/deembedded;
the simple equation (1) is not valid without that condition.

If gamma, G, Tu, T0 and Q remain fixed in a hot/cold pair, the difference is
G*DeltaT*(1-|gamma|^2). Correlated receiver noise cancels exactly here;
discarding Q from the physical instrument model does not make it cancel
when gamma changes with heating or switching.

Suppose the simultaneously reported reflection is g=d+c*gamma, c!=0.
Then z=(Ph-Pc)/DeltaT satisfies

 z=q0-q1|g|^2+2q2 Re(g)+2q3 Im(g),
 q1=G/|c|^2, q2=q1 Re(d), q3=q1 Im(d),
 q0=G-q1|d|^2.                                             (2)

A rank-four quadratic design determines q, hence d, G and |c|. It does not
determine phase(c) when true standard phases have not independently been
supplied. Indeed, c'=exp(i phi)c and gamma'=exp(-i phi)gamma preserve both
the reported g and every thermal contrast. Receiver correlation Q can be
rotated consistently, preserving its magnitude and positive covariance.
True impedance standards of independently known complex reflection can
fix this phase; their uncertainty and reference-plane placement are then
part of the coherent acquisition contract, not information from heating.

This restates the inherited partial-repair algebra, then adds a finite
contract. It does not repair general non-affine VNA errors or certify global
injectivity of the inherited 40-parameter cable template.

2. Deterministic joint calibration bounds and dual certificates

Let Ahat_i=[1,-|ghat_i|^2,2 Re(ghat_i),2 Im(ghat_i)]. If
|g_i-ghat_i|<=eGamma, |ghat_i|<=1 and |q_j|<=Q_j, then

 |(A_i-Ahat_i)q| <= (2 eGamma+eGamma^2)Q1
                             +2 eGamma(Q2+Q3).              (3)

This charges coherent shared error. No independence across standards or
frequency bins is assumed for the deterministic bound. The envelope Q_j
is a supplied/acquired parameter bound and cannot be silently invented.

Let eT bound each standard's local temperature departure from its stated
value, including all lossy delay cable and termination components. If all
their local temperatures obey that bound, positivity of local dissipation
gives a total hot/cold power error <=2 Gmax eT. A single thermometer on one
component does not establish this uniformity bound.

If the hot/cold reflection changes by at most ehc, |gamma|<=1,
|Tu-Th|<=Rmax and |Q|<=Qmax, (1) gives an additional hot-state power error

 Gmax[(2 ehc+ehc^2)Rmax+2 Qmax ehc].                         (4)

Thus actual gamma at both temperatures matters even if all thermal
standards share one VNA error. The G,Tu,Q envelopes must cover the whole
sequence. Any remaining receiver-parameter or switch-transfer change
requires an additional differential power bound.

Use ABBA with cold and hot at center times -3h/2,-h/2,h/2,3h/2 and
weights -1/2,+1/2,+1/2,-1/2. Constant and linear common drift cancel. For
|b''(t)|<=M, the residual is at most M h^2. To prove this, set
F(a)=(b(a)+b(-a))/2. The second-derivative kernel of F(h/2)-F(3h/2)
has one sign; its absolute integral equals [(3h/2)^2-(h/2)^2]/2=h^2.
Equal finite averaging windows preserve the second-derivative bound.

Longer individual dwells can make curvature drift larger. Repeating fixed
subdwell ABBA cycles keeps the worst coherent residual at M h^2; it does
not average that bound down. The script uses subdwell 1 s and deadtime
.01 s, h=1.01 s. Each hot and cold state receives tau seconds aggregated
over tau/2 cycles. Fast switching requires TWO already equilibrated copies
of each nominal impedance at different temperatures. Their actual mismatch
is charged by (4). Heating one physical source every second is not assumed.
Thermal equilibration and the ten-source commissioning cost remain UNKNOWN.

For a simultaneous bound eps_i on z_i-Ahat_i*q, any rational b with
Ahat^T b=l is a dual certificate

 |l^T q-b^T z| <= sum_i |b_i| eps_i.                        (5)

The code uses the exact rational left inverse
B=(Ahat^T Ahat)^(-1) Ahat^T and verifies B*Ahat=I exactly. Each row is a
certificate for one component of q. A general scalar functional can use
another dual b, including a weighted L1-optimal one; arbitrary matrix
conditioning or iid Fisher noise is not substituted for (5).

values define the constructed receipt; the inverse uses only reported g

3. Finite-sample simultaneous coverage and wallclock

For m independent proper complex Gaussian modes, sample power has Gamma
shape m and mean P. The chi-square Chernoff bound gives

 Pr(|Phat-P| > P[sqrt(2x/m)+x/m]) <=2 exp(-x).               (6)

Replacing P by an independently acquired common upper bound Pcap preserves
coverage. Exact rational upper square roots are checked by integer squares.
For N power estimates, a union bound needs 2N exp(-x)<=alpha and does not
require independence BETWEEN estimates. It does require the stated
within-record mode law, count and power cap. Effective B*tau is not free
when filtering, RFI or temporal correlation reduce the independent count.

For five hot/cold pairs, 490 frequency bins and alpha=.05, x=13 is enough;
the script certifies exp(13)>196000 using a positive rational series.
Pcap=1000 and B=100 kHz are hypothetical acquired inputs. At tau=100 s
per hot or cold state, the joint q1 error bound is .526235 rather than a
claimed precise calibration. The sequence costs 3030 s at one-third duty
including stated switch deadtime, plus 1800 s inherited VNA warmup. At
tau=1000 s it costs 30300 s and q1 error remains .173750.

Pooling a single flat 50 MHz interface is much cheaper but needs independently
acquired flatness of the calibration quantities and source geometry. The
script separately labels that stronger assumption; it is not an upgrade
obtained by averaging unknown frequency-dependent receiver functions.
Actual VNA acquisition, thermal equilibration, correlated-mode qualification,
commissioning and independent validation are excluded UNKNOWN costs.

These simultaneous affine-calibration intervals do not identify a useful

and a small fit residual at one fitted model do not supply eK.

4. Exact quantitative residual spatial ambiguity

Consider the physical passive distortionless line

 z(x,s)/Z0=Z0*y(x,s)=a(x)+s/v0,
 R(x)=Z0*a(x), G(x)=a(x)/Z0,
 L=Z0/v0, C=1/(Z0*v0).                                    (7)

L,C and velocity are known constants. Because Z0 is constant, no internal
loss interface reflects; all propagation generators commute. With
u(x)=integral_0^x a and A=u(L), full boundary transmission is
exp[-A-sL/v0], and the local equilibrium covariance is diagonal. Independent
sources give, in kB units,

 CL=2 integral_0^A T(u)exp(-2u)du,
 CR=2 integral_0^A T(u)exp[-2(A-u)]du, CLR=0.                (8)

Therefore any two a(x) maps with the same A,L and same T(u) give exactly
the same full S and full emitted covariance at EVERY frequency. Any remote
termination and receiver measurement is a function of those boundary data,
so arbitrary heated standards, perfect absolute phase and infinite data
do not distinguish the pair. A common positive-real dispersion factor h(s)
on a(x) extends this identity, but is unnecessary for the counterexample.

Even grant exact T(u), which the interface need not actually identify.
Suppose independent local calibration supplies amin<=a(x)<=amax. Write
v(u)=dx/du. Then 1/amax<=v<=1/amin and integral_0^A v=L. The physical
uniform spatial mean is (1/L) integral T(u)v(u)du. Its sharp interval is a
continuous linear program. For any scalar lambda, dual bounds are

 max integral T v <=lambda L+
     integral max_{v in [1/amax,1/amin]} (T-lambda)v du,
 min integral T v >=lambda L+
     integral min_{v in [1/amax,1/amin]} (T-lambda)v du.       (9)

Take L=100 m, A=1/5, amin=1/1000, amax=3/1000 per metre. In loss coordinate,
T=350 K on [.09,.11] and 300 K elsewhere. A hot physical length of 20 m
uses a_hot=.001 and a_cold=.00225. A hot length of 20/3 m uses a_hot=.003
and a_cold=27/14000. Both obey every stated local loss bound and length.

The physical means are 310 and 910/3 K. Set lambda=300 in (9); the two
primal models attain both dual bounds exactly. Thus the complete feasible
mean interval is [910/3,310] K even with exact T(u). Any point estimator
based on the complete boundary data has worst absolute error at least
10/3 K, since it returns the same result on the two models.

The step is not essential. Set
T(u)=300+50 max(0,1-|u-.1|/.01).
Both physical profiles are continuous and Lipschitz with bound 15 K/m.
The same maps give physical means 905/3 and 305 K, a diameter 10/3 K and
minimax absolute error at least 5/3 K. All temperatures remain 300-350 K.

This is a new sharp quantitative use of the inherited spatial gauge, not
a claim that the gauge itself was newly discovered here. It rules out
generic intact-cable physical-mean identification. It does NOT prove
nonidentifiability inside the inherited narrower 20-section, 40-scale,
known sqrt(f)/f R/G law template. That conditional model still needs global
injectivity, acquired laws and an uncertainty enclosure; these remain open.

Active TDR and fully coherent VNA controls agree exactly on the gauge pair.
Endpoint thermometers see 300 K in both. Internal temperature probes or
known-position loss measurements can break the gauge, but change the access
and capital contract. Their installation cannot be charged only to controls.

5. A stronger geometry-free operation: certified overtemperature

For independent local equilibrium noise in a linear passive network, write

 C=integral T(x)W(x)dx, W(x)>=0, A=integral W=I-S*Sdagger.    (10)

The normalization follows from thermal equilibrium: matched incoming waves
at T0 have covariance T0 I, outgoing equilibrium waves also have T0 I, so
C_uniform=T0(I-S*Sdagger). This is established passive-network theory;
Wedge/Rutledge 1991 is a primary reference. Local temperature linearity and
positive covariances also follow directly from the independent-source
integral in Bucher/Molnar 2024, equations (10)-(13).

If every local dissipative region has T<=Tlim, then

 C-Tlim A <=0.                                             (11)

Consequently ANY certified positive quadratic form vdagger(C-Tlim A)v
certifies some region above Tlim. No spatial coordinate, positive localized
kernel, or resistance/temperature constitutive law is needed. It does not
identify the region, nor does failure to find a violation certify safety.
An entirely invisible hot region remains possible if its W coupling is zero.
External interference, active/DC excess noise and long-range correlated
sources are outside (10), and cannot be relabeled local temperature.

There is a receiver-basis sign comparison which can avoid absolute coherent
phase calibration. Arrange an acquired matched upstream noise bath at Tlim,
uncorrelated with forward receiver noise. For the SAME stable receiver gain
H and additive covariance N,

 Ydut=H(C+Tlim S*Sdagger)Hdagger+N,
 Yref=H(Tlim I)Hdagger+N,
 Ydut-Yref=H(C-Tlim A)Hdagger.                              (12)

Any fixed H preserves the implication of a positive observed quadratic
form. Unknown receiver phase therefore does not create a spatial inversion
gate for this SIGN certificate. Real receivers emit reflected noise: if
upstream U=Tlim I+deltaU and input/output correlation is Q, the uncorrected
extra term has norm at most hmax^2||deltaU||+2 hmax||Q|| for ||S||<=1.
Without those acquired bounds, (12) is not a valid alarm. Isolation or full
noise-wave calibration, its temperature and loss, switch transfer, gain
drift and reference thermometry must all be included.

An exact passive example uses a lossless hybrid connecting two equally
absorbing orthogonal modes, one at 360 K and one at 300 K:

 A=(1/2)I, C=[[165,15],[15,165]], Tlim=340,
 C-Tlim A=[[-5,15],[15,-5]].                               (13)

Each physical port has noise temperature 330 K. The coherent plus mode
has 360 K and a threshold-power margin of 10 K. With v=(1,1), the exact
quadratic form is 20. Simultaneous uncertainty .5 in each diagonal and
.5 in real off-diagonal contributes at most 2, leaving a certified 18>0.

A separate held-out plus-mode validation with constructed power centers
450 and 440 K counts, Pcap=500, B=1 MHz, tau=1 s each, and x=5 gives a
joint finite-sample difference error <=3.167277661. Hypothetical systematic
budgets summing to 1.9 K leave a positive margin 4.932722339 K counts.
Integration costs 6 s at one-third duty. Reference equilibration, isolation,
commissioning and validation costs are UNKNOWN; six seconds is not an
end-to-end device claim. An additionally acquired mode-absorption upper
bound .51 would convert the margin to a local temperature lower bound
349.672 K. Without it, the valid conclusion is only some T>340 K.

6. Strongest comparator and decision

A conventional calibrated lossless hybrid followed by one radiometer
measures exactly the same coherent plus mode. With the same threshold
reference, system temperature, bandwidth, switching and drift bounds it
matches the digital two-receiver alarm. Comparing only with uncombined port
powers or endpoint sensors would manufacture an advantage.

Bosma's 1967 thesis already defines invariant characteristic noise
temperatures and diagonalizes noisy multiports into independent modes.
Printed page 81 (PDF page 91), equations (3.65)-(3.66), and printed pages
123-124 (PDF pages 133-134), equation (4.72), directly establish this prior
core. The page 81 scan was rendered and visually inspected. Therefore the
alarm is an explicit finite-error application of established theory, not a
new civilization-enabling operation. Its robust acquisition might be useful,
but no physical advantage over competent Johnson-noise controls is shown.
