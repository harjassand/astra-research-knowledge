CYCLE03 CONSEQUENCES: BOUNDED-HOLEVO APPROXIMATE BROADCASTING PRIOR ART
Prepared 2026-10-09. Bounded primary-source pass; no priority or novelty claim.

QUESTION AND INTERFACE

For a finite family E={rho_i} of states on a finite-dimensional H, use
T(rho,sigma)=||rho-sigma||_1/2. Entropies and relative entropies in the
task interface are in nats; the substate theorem's observational divergence
is in bits and is converted explicitly below. Define

  C_chi(E)=sup_{p on labels} [S(sum_i p_i rho_i)-sum_i p_i S(rho_i)],
  b(E)=inf_B sup_i max_{a=1,2} T(Tr_other-a B(rho_i),rho_i),
  e(E)=inf_{EB Lambda} sup_i T(Lambda(rho_i),rho_i).

Here one CPTP broadcaster B serves every label, output correlations are
allowed, and one full-domain entanglement-breaking channel Lambda serves
every label. The question is whether, for each fixed M, b(E_n)->0 and
C_chi(E_n)<=M force e(E_n)->0 uniformly over growing Hilbert dimension and
label count. This is the single-use Holevo capacity of the state family,
maximized over every prior; it is not the regularized classical capacity of
an arbitrary quantum channel. A bound for one selected prior is weaker.

RESULT OF THE LITERATURE PASS

The closest direct primary theorem is Lemm-Wilde's Theorem 14 on a fixed
pair of mixed states. It treats a single common channel that simultaneously
approximately broadcasts both states and bounds relative-entropy loss by a
channel-dependent Petz-recovery residual. This is closer than the exact
no-broadcasting theorem, but it does not give the requested dimension-free
affinity-gap modulus or a common EB reconstruction for a many-label family.
The authors explicitly note that their Petz residual vanishing alone does
not imply commutation; a different residual has that exact-zero property,
but the theorem does not bound it in terms of b(E).

The exact zero-error endpoint is established: Barnum-Caves-Fuchs-Jozsa-
Schumacher prove the two-state iff-commuting result for invertible states,
and Kalev-Hen give the arbitrary-density-matrix extension. Pairwise
application yields the finite-family iff-commuting criterion. Horodecki-
Horodecki-Horodecki-Piani show that a fixed-prior Holevo-information
increase from one copy to two copies vanishes iff the ensemble is
broadcastable (on its positive-prior support). Neither result gives an
approximate, dimension-independent route from small marginal error to one
uniform EB channel. At zero error, pairwise commutation yields one common
eigenbasis and therefore one common dephasing/EB channel. Approximate
pairwise commutation or one recovery map per pair would not by itself supply
the task's single max-label EB channel; the N93 encoder/decoder transfer
explicitly preserves that common-channel quantifier.

The useful capacity import is Schumacher-Westmoreland's information-radius
theorem: a maximizing ensemble average sigma_* for a finite-dimensional
available set A obeys D(rho||sigma_*)<=chi* for every rho in A, and chi*
is the minimum of max_{rho in A}D(rho||sigma) over average states sigma
of ensembles supported on A. For A={rho_i}, this is precisely
C_chi(E)=sup_p chi(E,p), so a fixed M supplies one center with
D(rho_i||sigma_*)<=M for every label. A bound on chi for one selected
prior cannot be substituted. Jain-Nayak's quantum substate theorem then
supplies nearby states dominated by that center, but it does not itself
give a broadcaster or EB decoder.

Historical significance, if the proposed chain survives independent proof
review, would be a uniform stability theorem: bounded Holevo capacity plus
vanishing two-output marginal error would force one common EB channel to
reconstruct every promised state, with no dependence on dimension or label
count. This would quantitatively strengthen the exact commuting-state
endpoint and fill a gap left by the fixed-prior exact information quantity
and the pair recovery inequality. The literature pass does not establish
that priority claim; the N46 pair bridge and the internal reductions remain
conditional, and C4 is an internally reconstructed candidate rather than
external validation.

EXACT SOURCE STATEMENTS

1. Exact no-broadcasting endpoint, with the rank history made explicit.
   Barnum, Caves, Fuchs, Jozsa, and Schumacher, “Noncommuting Mixed States
   Cannot Be Broadcast,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 76, 2818 (1996), DOI
   10.1103/PhysRevLett.76.2818; arXiv:quant-ph/9511010v1 (1995-11-08),
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9511010 . The formal two-state problem
   and correlated-output definition are PDF p.1, Eq. (1); their iff
   commuting statement is PDF p.1, and the converse proof explicitly
   assumes invertible states at PDF p.2. Thus this is the seminal exact
   full-rank result, not by itself the arbitrary-rank proof.

   The full-rank-free exact extension is Kalev and Hen, “The No-Broadcasting
   Theorem and its Classical Counterpart,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 100, 210502
   (2008), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.210502; arXiv:0704.1754v3
   (2008-05-02), https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.1754v3 . Their PDF p.1,
   Eqs. (1)-(2), defines one physical process whose two marginals equal
   each arbitrary input state; the relative-entropy proof and treatment
   of infinite relative entropy are PDF pp.1-2. They state arbitrary
   density matrices at p.1. In finite dimension, applying the pair result
   to every pair gives: a finite family is broadcastable by one common
   channel iff its members commute pairwise. The commuting direction is
   implemented by measuring their common eigenbasis and preparing the
   corresponding duplicated basis state, which is also EB. Thus at exact
   zero error, common broadcasting and one common EB reconstruction
   coincide. Neither paper gives a dimension- and family-size-independent
   approximate modulus from b(E) to e(E).

2. Holevo quantumness of an ensemble: M. Horodecki, P. Horodecki,
   R. Horodecki, M. Piani, “Quantumness of ensemble from no-broadcasting
   principle,” Int. J. Quantum Inf. 4, 105 (2006), DOI
   10.1142/S0219749906001748; use the exact arXiv:quant-ph/0506174v2
   (2007-05-31) at https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0506174 .
   Source location: PDF pp.3-4, Eqs. (6)-(12), Proposition 1, and its
   finite-ensemble extension. For a fixed prior ensemble E, they define
   chi_q(E)=inf_{extensions E_AB with both marginals E} chi(E_AB)-chi(E).
   Proposition 1 says chi_q is zero iff the pair is broadcastable; the
   extension to n labels says chi_q^(n)(E)=0 iff every pair in the family
   is broadcastable, equivalently the states commute pairwise. This applies
   to the positive-prior support; labels with zero prior are not controlled.
   The Petz equality argument constructs one common map in the exact-zero
   case when the prior has full support on the family.
   This is a fixed-prior Holevo quantity, not C_chi(E)=sup_p chi(E,p).
   It is an exact-zero characterization, not a dimension-free stability
   theorem from small b(E), nor a uniform max-label EB bound.

3. Quantitative simultaneous two-state approximate broadcasting:
   M. Lemm and M. M. Wilde, “Information-theoretic limitations on
   approximate quantum cloning and broadcasting,” Phys. Rev. A 96, 012304
   (2017), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.96.012304; arXiv:1608.07569 (2016),
   https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevA.96.012304 and
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.07569 .
   Source location: Appendix C, Theorem 14, Eqs. (C1)-(C4), PDF p.10;
   limitation discussion Eqs. (C5)-(C7), PDF pp.10-11. For fixed states
   sigma_1,sigma_2 with finite D(sigma_1||sigma_2), and one channel Lambda
   whose two outputs for each input have identical marginals
   tilde-sigma_i, they prove

     D(sigma_1||sigma_2)-D(tilde-sigma_1||tilde-sigma_2)
       >= Delta_R(tilde-sigma_1,tilde-sigma_2),

   where Delta_R is one eighth the beta(t)-average of the squared trace
   distance between two rotated-Petz recoveries of the first output
   marginal, using the second joint output as reference. The support
   condition is ker(tilde-sigma_2) subset ker(tilde-sigma_1), or the
   approximation discussed in their Sec. II. This interface is compatible
   with the task's broadcaster after symmetrizing B by
   (B+Swap o B)/2: the two marginals then agree, and if the original
   marginal errors are <=b, their common marginal remains within b of each
   promised input. The theorem's residual is channel-dependent. The authors
   explicitly say Delta_R=0 only yields a projected equality and need not
   imply that the states commute (PDF pp.10-11); their alternate
   Carlen-Lieb residual Delta_CL has the implication Delta_CL=0 => commute,
   but no dimension-free estimate Delta_CL<=g(b) is provided there. This
   is the nearest exact approximate theorem found in this bounded pass;
   it does not produce a single EB channel for all labels.

CAPACITY AND SMOOTHING IMPORTS USED BY THE N93 ROUTE

4. Information radius: B. Schumacher and M. D. Westmoreland, “Optimal
   signal ensembles,” Phys. Rev. A 63, 022308 (2001), DOI
   10.1103/PhysRevA.63.022308; exact arXiv:quant-ph/9912122v1
   (1999-12-31), https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9912122 .
   Source location: “Properties of optimal ensembles,” PDF pp.7-9, maximal
   distance property Eq. (17) and min-max formula Eq. (19). For a
   finite-dimensional available set A, their maximizing ensemble average
   sigma_* satisfies D(rho||sigma_*)<=chi* for every rho in A; Eq. (19)
   minimizes max_{rho in A}D(rho||sigma) over average states sigma of
   ensembles from A. With A the finite promised family, ensembles are exactly
   priors on the labels. The supremum over all priors is what gives one
   center controlling every label. A one-prior chi bound does not give this
   all-state radius. This is a center theorem, not a reconstruction result.

5. Substate theorem: R. Jain and A. Nayak, “Short proofs of the Quantum
   Substate Theorem,” arXiv:1103.6067v2 (2012-01-12),
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.6067v2 . Their Theorem 1 uses observational
   divergence, not relative entropy. Definition 1, PDF p.2, is
   D_obs(rho||sigma)=sup_{0<=M<=I, Tr(M sigma)>0}
   Tr(M rho) log_2[Tr(M rho)/Tr(M sigma)]; they state
   D_obs(rho||sigma)<=D_rel,bits(rho||sigma)+1. Theorem 1, PDF p.3,
   assumes supp(rho) subset supp(sigma) and gives, for eta in (0,1), a
   state rho' with squared fidelity F(rho',rho)>=1-eta and
   rho'<=[(1-eta)^(-1) 2^{D_obs(rho||sigma)/eta}] sigma. Hence a relative
   entropy bound D_rel,nats<=M gives
   rho'<=[(1-eta)^(-1) exp((M+ln 2)/eta)] sigma.
   The +ln(2) term is required by the observational-to-relative divergence
   conversion. Fixed M and eta make this factor dimension- and
   label-independent. This gives smoothing into a common sandwich; it does
   not provide the compatible preservation estimate or an EB map. Those
   steps are separate N93 derivations, not claims of this theorem.

PRIORITY VERDICT AND NEXT GATE

The strongest prior theorem found in this bounded pass remains Lemm-Wilde
Theorem 14 with its exact interface above. It is a pairwise relative-entropy
loss/recovery theorem, not a uniform dimension-free modulus from two-output
marginal error to a pair gap, and not a construction of one max-label EB
channel. The exact no-broadcast endpoint and the fixed-prior Holevo
quantumness functional are established antecedents, but neither states the
capacity-bounded uniform conclusion. If the internally reported C4,
weighted-trace, smoothing/Petz, centralization, pair-bridge and transfer
proofs all survive fresh review, the substantive new theorem would be
  for every fixed M, b(E_n)->0 and C_chi(E_n)<=M
  imply e(E_n)->0 uniformly in dimension and label count,
  with one full-domain EB channel per family serving all labels.
This is a candidate contribution, not a priority claim. The primary-source
pass is bounded; failure to find an exact statement is not evidence of
novelty. External priority remains UNKNOWN. The conclusion does not extend
to unbounded-capacity families, for which N61 is an internal obstruction.
