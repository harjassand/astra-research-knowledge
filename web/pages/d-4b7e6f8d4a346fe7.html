Primary-literature audit: nonlinear catalyst/substrate WR family
Date checked: 2026-10-08
Status: bounded source audit; no novelty or priority claim

Scope and family fingerprint
----------------------------
Audited the family in foster_multicatalyst.txt: finite species A_1,...,A_r,
B_1,...,B_s; immigration/degradation 0 <-> A_j; substrate production/removal
A_j <-> A_j+B_i; and catalyst-dependent autocatalysis
A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i, k_ji >= 1, with strictly positive rates.
The state is the well-mixed molecule-count vector in N_0^(r+s), with standard
stochastic mass-action propensities. The report in this directory supplies the
conditional strict-small-gain Foster argument; this file audits external
coverage and does not re-prove that argument.

The complex graph is a bidirected tree with nodes
  0; A_j; A_j+B_i; (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i.
It has 1+r+2rs complexes, one linkage class, and stoichiometric rank r+s:
0 <-> A_j spans each A_j direction, and A_j <-> A_j+B_i spans each B_i
direction. Thus d=r+s is both species dimension and rank, and the deficiency
is s(2r-1)>0. Every autocatalytic high complex has molecularity k_ji+2 >= 3.
The lattice is irreducible: births/deaths move A counts to/from zero, and
while any A_j is present, A_j <-> A_j+B_i can move each B_i count both ways.

Main finding
------------
For d=2 (necessarily r=s=1), an author slide and multiple conference/seminar
abstracts report a theorem that every two-species weakly reversible stochastic
mass-action CTMC is positive recurrent on each closed irreducible class. This
family is within the announced class, including its higher-order complexes.
However, as of this audit date, the authors' project publication page lists
the proof as “in preparation”; the ECMTB 2026 listing has no presentation
materials; and an exact-title arXiv API query returned zero records. I found
no manuscript or proof to verify and therefore classify this as a
source-reported 2D theorem, not as a published/proof-audited theorem.

For d>=3, the published general weakly reversible single-linkage stochastic
result located requires binary complexes and a pure multiple of every
individual species. The family violates both the binary requirement and the
pure-complex condition for every B_i. One-dimensional weakly reversible
results do not apply because its stoichiometric rank is r+s>=2. The published
deterministic permanence theorem does apply in every d, but does not imply
stochastic nonexplosion or positive recurrence. The directly established
stochastic results for this exact family are therefore: (a) the internal
conditional Foster result when its strict small-gain inequalities are feasible;
and (b) the published complex-balance case described below. For the remaining
rate choices in d>=3, this audit locates no all-rate positive-recurrence result
covering the family. This is a bounded search statement, not evidence of
novelty and not a claim that no such result exists.

Applicability: if r=s=1, the target has exactly two species, is WR, uses
stochastic mass action, and is irreducible on N_0^2. Thus it lies within the

2. Published stochastic results and exact non-coverage
------------------------------------------------------

Anderson, Cappelletti, Kim (2020), “Stochastically modeled weakly reversible
reaction networks with a single linkage class,” Journal of Applied
Probability 57(3), 792–810. arXiv:1904.08967, v2 revised 16 Jan 2020:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08967
https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08967v2
Theorem 4.1: a weakly reversible binary mass-action system with one linkage
class is positive recurrent if for each species S, at least one of S or 2S is
a complex. The proof uses an n-step embedded chain and concludes CTMC
positive recurrence; Theorem 3.1 supplies nonexplosion when the embedded
chain is positive recurrent. The candidate has one linkage class but has
complexes of order k_ji+2>=3, so is not binary; moreover no B_i-only complex
(B_i or 2B_i) occurs. Theorem 4.1 does not apply. Its assumptions must not be
reduced to “WR and one linkage class.”

Wiuf and Xu (2023 version), “Classification and threshold dynamics of
stochastic reaction networks,” arXiv:2012.07954, v3 revised 24 Jan 2023:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07954
https://arxiv.org/html/2012.07954v3
Theorem 4.8 establishes nonexplosion and exponential ergodicity for endotactic
mass-action SRNs under H2–H5, where H2 is dim(S)=1, including the
one-dimensional WR case; Corollary 4.10 states WR positive recurrence on
every positive irreducible class under H2–H3. The target's reaction vectors
span all r+s species directions, hence rank at least two; it is outside that
result's 1D conclusion. The paper studies stochastic compatibility classes,
so rank—not an informal count of “independent reactions”—is the relevant
comparison.

Xu (2026 preprint), “On the Regulary of Reaction Systems,” arXiv:2409.05340,
v2 revised 9 May 2026:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05340
https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05340v2
Theorem 4.6(v) says every second-order weakly reversible stochastic
mass-action system is regular (nonexplosive stochastic model and global
deterministic solution). Theorem 4.13 says for every bimolecular WR system,
positive recurrence in every communicating class is equivalent to existence
of a stationary distribution in every class; it does not prove that such a
distribution exists. This target has order at least three, so neither theorem
covers it. This source is an arXiv preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication
in the version checked.

Complex balance, product form, and a parameter-restricted exact result:

Anderson, Craciun, Kurtz (2010), “Product-form stationary distributions for
deficiency zero chemical reaction networks,” Bull. Math. Biol. 72 (2010),
1947–1970. arXiv:0803.3042, v4 revised 31 Jan 2010:
https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3042
https://arxiv.org/html/0803.3042v4
Theorem 4.1: for any stochastic mass-action network whose deterministic
system has a positive complex-balanced equilibrium c, the stochastic system
has product-Poisson stationary distribution; on an irreducible full lattice
it is unique. Theorem 4.2 gives the all-rate consequence for WR deficiency
zero networks. The target deficiency is s(2r-1)>0, so the latter does not
apply automatically.

Anderson, Cappelletti, Koyama, Kurtz (2018), “Non-explosivity of stochastically
modeled reaction networks that are complex balanced,” Bull. Math. Biol. 80
(2018), 2561–2579. arXiv:1708.09356, v2 revised 18 May 2018:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09356
https://arxiv.org/html/1708.09356v2
Theorem 3: any complex-balanced stochastic mass-action system is
nonexplosive for any initial distribution. Combined with the ACK product form
and irreducibility, this yields positive recurrence for the target whenever
its rates satisfy the exact complex-balance conditions below.

Exact complex-balance subfamily. Since every edge of the complex graph is a
bridge and each edge is present in both directions, a divergence-free
complex-flux field must have zero net flux on each edge. Thus complex balance
here is equivalent to detailed balance edge-by-edge. A positive c exists iff
for all j,i:
  c_Aj = alpha_j/delta_j,
  c_Bi = p_ji/q_ji,
  c_Aj^(k_ji) = u_ji/v_ji.
In particular p_ji/q_ji must be independent of j for fixed i, and
u_ji/v_ji must agree with (alpha_j/delta_j)^k_ji for every i. These are
restrictive equalities, not consequences of WR. For example r=s=k=1 and
alpha=delta=p=1, q=2, u=1, v=3 satisfy the target report's small-gain
inequality delta*q>p*k*u but are not complex balanced: the first edge forces
c_A=1 while the high edge forces c_A=u/v=1/3. This illustrates that the
published complex-balance theorem covers only a strict subfamily of the
candidate Foster result.

3. Close stochastic analogues: autocatalysis, catalysts, and space
------------------------------------------------------------------

Bibbona, Kim, Wiuf (2020), “Stationary distributions of systems with
Discreteness Induced Transitions,” J. R. Soc. Interface 17 (2020), 20200243,
DOI 10.1098/rsif.2020.0243. arXiv:2001.04917, v3 revised 28 Mar 2020:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04917
https://arxiv.org/html/2001.04917v3
Theorem 2 applies in any number d of species to their specified model (14):
pairwise conversion reactions A_i+A_j -> 2A_i and A_i+A_j -> 2A_j, together
with immigration 0 -> A_i and linear degradation A_i -> 0, with arbitrary
nonnegative conversion rates and positive immigration/degradation rates.
It proves positive recurrence on N^d, a unique stationary distribution,
finite moments, and exponential convergence. The competition conversions
preserve total molecule count; the immigration/degradation terms give the
total-count birth-death control. In the target, A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i
increases total count by k_ji>=1, its reverse is higher-order and can be
disabled at the boundary, and the B_i reactions are catalyst-conditioned.
Thus Theorem 2 is a valuable autocatalytic analogue but not a theorem for the
target's stoichiometry or propensities. Its introduction also mentions
reaction-diffusion/spatial DIT models as related phenomena; its Theorem 2
itself is for a finite-dimensional well-mixed CTMC, not a spatial lattice
process.

Gopalkrishnan (2011), “Catalysis in Reaction Networks,” Bull. Math. Biol. 73
(2011), 2962–2982, DOI 10.1007/s11538-011-9655-3. arXiv:1006.3627, v3
revised 11 May 2011:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.3627
https://arxiv.org/html/1006.3627
Theorem 4.1 is a reaction-graph/algebraic statement: weakly reversible
networks with critical siphons are catalytic (in the paper's pathway sense).
The paper relates this to deterministic persistence/event systems and treats
the network level across dynamics. It does not establish CTMC nonexplosion
or positive recurrence for stochastic mass-action models; it is a structural
catalysis analogue, not coverage of the current stochastic claim.

Spatial scope: the audited target has no spatial coordinate, migration, or
site-indexed species; its state has r+s counts. None of the finite-dimensional
well-mixed theorems above can be transferred to an infinite-lattice or
reaction-diffusion version without a separately specified process and
assumptions. The spatial/autocatalytic literature mentioned by Bibbona–Kim–
Wiuf is contextual model analogy, not a general recurrence theorem.

4. Deterministic permanence does apply, but is not stochastic stability
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Boros and Hofbauer (2020), “Permanence of Weakly Reversible Mass-Action
Systems with a Single Linkage Class,” SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 19(1) (2020),
352–365, DOI 10.1137/19M1248431. arXiv:1903.03071, v2 revised 13 Mar 2019:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03071
https://arxiv.org/html/1903.03071v2
Theorem 2: a weakly reversible, single-linkage-class mass-action ODE with
bounded kinetics, restricted to any positive stoichiometric class, has a
compact forward-invariant subset entered in finite time by every trajectory;
the system is permanent. Constant positive rate parameters satisfy their
bounded-kinetics hypothesis, and the target is one linkage class, so this
deterministic result applies in all r,s,k. Permanence bounds deterministic
positive trajectories; it is not a statement about the CTMC clock, explosion,
or invariant probabilities.

That distinction is essential. The 2018 Anderson–Cappelletti–Koyama–Kurtz
paper above includes a mass-action example whose deterministic trajectory
converges to a positive equilibrium while the stochastic model explodes
almost surely from specified initial states. Thus deterministic stability
alone cannot substitute for a stochastic proof. The target's nonexplosion
argument must use its stochastic generator / propensity structure.

5. Gap and applicability matrix
-------------------------------

Case d=2, any positive rates:
  Announced 2D WR theorem: yes, if the reported theorem is verified from its
  forthcoming manuscript. Target is irreducible, so the class qualifier
  causes no gap. Public proof/published status: UNVERIFIED as of 2026-10-08.
  Published external theorem found: complex-balance subset only.

Case d>=3, complex-balanced rates:
  Published ACK Theorem 4.1 plus ACKK Theorem 3 give a Poisson stationary
  measure, nonexplosion, and (by irreducibility) positive recurrence. Exact
  parameter equalities above are necessary and sufficient for this tree.

Case d>=3, arbitrary positive rates:
  The published one-linkage stochastic theorem misses the target because it
  is binary and requires a pure species multiple for each species. The
  one-dimensional theorem misses because rank=r+s>=3. The bimolecular Xu
  preprint misses because the target order is >=3. Deterministic permanence
  applies but does not bridge to stochastic recurrence. The 2026 research
  group page itself reports the full arbitrary-dimensional chemical
  recurrence conjecture as open. No exact all-rate d>=3 source located in
  this bounded audit; no novelty inference follows.

Case any d, rates satisfying the target's strict small-gain LP:
  The sibling Foster report gives a direct conditional nonexplosion and
  positive-recurrence proof for this family. It is stronger in exact-family
  applicability than the external structural theorems, but it is conditional
  on feasible strict margins and is not an all-rate WR theorem.

Outstanding source gate:
  Recheck the authors' project page and exact-title arXiv query for a posted
  manuscript, then audit its theorem/proof, stated state-space assumptions,
  propensity conventions, treatment of boundary states, and positivity/
  irreducibility hypotheses before upgrading the 2D result's status.
  Separately, the d>=3 non-complex-balanced parameter region outside the
  small-gain certificate remains unresolved by sources identified here.

Search limits and claim discipline
----------------------------------
This is an exact-family literature audit, not a comprehensive bibliography
of every stochastic CRN, catalyst, reaction-diffusion, or interacting-particle
paper. Negative findings above are restricted to the primary sources and
searches listed; they are not novelty, priority, or nonexistence claims.
Conference abstracts and slides record reported results but do not expose a
checkable proof. Deterministic permanence, graphical catalysis, recurrence of
the underlying discrete reaction graph, nonexplosion, and positive recurrence
are kept as distinct conclusions.
