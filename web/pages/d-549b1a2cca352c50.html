An isomorphism of the free group factors

Abstract
We solve the free group factor isomorphism problem affirmatively by proving thatL(F2)and
L(F3)are isomorphic as tracial von Neumann algebras. The classical free group factor alternative
then implies that all interpolated free group factors, includingL(F∞), are isomorphic and have
fundamental groupR >0.
1 Introduction
For a discrete groupG, its group von Neumann algebra is
L(G) ={λ(g) :g∈G}′′,(λ(g)ξ)(h) =ξ(g−1h), ξ∈ℓ2(G),
with canonical traceτ(x) =⟨xδe,δe⟩. Write Fn for the free group onn generators and F∞for the
free group on countably infinitely many generators. The free group factors arose in Murray and von
Neumann’s foundational work [9]. Their nonhyperfiniteness distinguished them from the hyperfinite
factor, but did not distinguish their ranks; see the historical account in [10, p. 518]. The question
whether L(Fm)and L(Fn)are isomorphic for distinctm,n≥2became a central problem in the
classification of finite factors. Dykema’s introduction [3, p. 123] records the early history and credits
Kadison with raising the rank-isomorphism question.
Free probability supplied a way to study these factors through their generating distributions.
Voiculescu developed the reduced-free-product framework [14] and its semicircular and circular
models [15]. His compression results showed that the fundamental group ofL(F∞)contains every
positive rational, and Rădulescu extended this to every positive real number [10]. Dykema and
Rădulescu independently constructed the interpolated factorsL(Fr)for real r> 1and established
their amplification calculus [3, 11]. These results relate changes of rank to normalized corners. They
also give a dichotomy: the interpolated free group factors are either all isomorphic or pairwise
nonisomorphic. The version including the infinite parameter is due to Rădulescu [11, Corollary 4.7];
see also the precise restatement in [5, Theorem 1.1].
The amplification calculus thus links rank to corner size, while the dichotomy reduces the
classification to two alternatives. To choose the isomorphic alternative, it suffices to construct
an isomorphism between two distinct finite ranks. Our constructive result is the following rank
comparison.
Theorem 1.1(Isomorphism of consecutive ranks).For every integer n≥3there is a unital
trace-preserving normal∗-isomorphism
L(Fn)∼=L(Fn+1).
The construction gives a freely generating Haarn-tuple insideL(Fn+1). Applying it at ranks
three and four, and then using the established amplification formula, gives the headline conclusion.
