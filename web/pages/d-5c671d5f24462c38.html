MAIN RESULT
===========
The global-denominator repair is refuted as a Lyapunov template by an exact
mixed-face ray: changing one low substrate count changes the denominator of
a different high inactive face and can make LV tend to +infinity. A local
per-substrate inverse-polynomial correction survives the corresponding
mixed-face attack and gives a fixed-class positive-recurrence theorem for a
finite higher-degree extension of the sparse paired graph.

Exact model. Let J,I be finite, E subset J x I, and I_+={i:N(i) is
nonempty}, N(i)={j:(j,i) in E}. Counts are a_j for catalysts A_j and b_i
for substrates B_i. For each j include

    0 <-> A_j                                  alpha_j, delta_j a_j,
    A_j <-> A_j+B_i                            p_ji a_j, q_ji a_j b_i,
    A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i              u_ji a_j b_i,
                                                v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i,

for each (j,i) in E, with fixed k_ji>=1 and all six rate constants positive
and finite. Propensities use stochastic mass action and falling factorials.
Add any finite list of reversible channel pairs

    A_j+z_B <-> A_l+z_B,       A_j+z_B <-> z_B,

where z_B contains substrates only and |z_B|<=m. Catalyst conversion has
total catalyst count unchanged; deactivation/activation changes it by one.
Every catalyst-containing source is first order in catalysts. All added
channels preserve every B coordinate. Fix any isolated-substrate count
vector (i.e. b_i for i not in I_+); all constants below may depend on this
label vector.

For any integer D>=m, any finite epsilon_i>0, K>=1, and R_i>1 satisfying

    delta_j - sum_{i:(j,i) in E} p_ji (R_i-1) > 0   for every j,

the potential

    V(a,b)=1+A+sum_{i in I_+} R_i^(b_i)
                [1+epsilon_i h_i(a)/(1+b_i)^D] + K 1_{A=0},
    A=sum_j a_j,   h_i(a)=1_{sum_{j in N(i)} a_j=0},

is norm-like on the fixed-label active class and has finite constants C,c>0
such that, for every state in that class,

    LV <= C - c [ A + sum_{i in I_+} R_i^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D ].         (1)

Since R_i^(b_i) is comparable to the corresponding summand in V and
b_i=O(log V), (1) implies

    LV <= C' - c' V/(1+log V)^D.                                   (2)

The base sparse network gives one irreducible class on the active
coordinates: clear each B_i with a temporary neighbor catalyst, drain the
catalysts, then build a target by immigration and A_j->A_j+B_i. Added
reversible channels do not remove these paths. Localized physical-time
Dynkin plus (1) proves nonexplosion; (2) gives LV<=-1 off a finite set, so
the continuous-time Foster criterion yields positive recurrence in every
fixed-label irreducible class. This is a stochastic result for the stated
grammar. It does not infer jump-process recurrence from deterministic ODE
permanence and does not cover arbitrary weakly reversible networks.

Proof spine
-----------
1. The exact base generator is the cycle-4 sparse paired-edge formula,
   with each local support correction W_i=R_i^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D. A substrate
   jump of B_i requires an adjacent catalyst, so h_i=0 before and after;
   it changes no other b_l. Thus it creates no cross-face correction.
2. Choose R_i close enough to one to leave every catalyst with positive
   linear margin when all its neighboring substrate counts are zero. For
   high b_i, its edge bracket
   p(R_i-1)R_i^b-q(1-R_i^-1)bR_i^b+k u b
   is at most -c bR_i^b. If the neighborhood is active, this paired q-sink
   also pays the last-neighbor-death correction; if inactive, catalyst
   immigration contributes -alpha(N(i)) epsilon_i W_i. The remaining
   catalyst tail is controlled by the same sparse proof as Cycle 4: either
   the zero-neighbor linear margin applies, or a bounded positive neighbor
   supplies a reverse falling-factorial sink of degree k+1>=2; a high
   active neighbor pays for that catalyst through its q-block. Low-face
   switch and K-boundary terms are bounded.
3. For an added catalyst switch, any positive h_i:0->1 correction requires
   that its source catalyst be the unique molecule in N(i), with count
   exactly one. This enables the base q-removal on that same B_i. Let
   E*=max_{i in I_+} b_i log R_i. The added substrate monomial of degree m
   is at most a constant times (1+E*)^m after fixed labels are frozen.
   If b_i log R_i>=E*/2, the local denominator D>=m reduces the positive
   switch to O(R_i^(b_i)); the same-state q-sink is order b_i R_i^(b_i).
   If b_i log R_i<E*/2, a maximizing substrate t has a negative block at
   least a positive multiple of W_t, while the switch is smaller by an
   exponential exp(-E*/2) times a polynomial. This controls arbitrary
   multi-substrate z_B, heterogeneous R_i, and simultaneous switches.
   Reverse activations and positive K-boundary increments are at most
   polynomial in active substrate counts and are absorbed by the largest
   face's exponential-over-polynomial sink. Finite low-count leftovers go
   into C.
4. Because V>=1+A+sum_i R_i^(b_i), V-sublevel sets and their one-jump target
   sets are finite. On every such set exit rates are bounded. From LV<=C,
   stopped Dynkin gives P_x(tau_L<=t)<=(V(x)+Ct)/L. Infinite jumps cannot
   occur inside a finite set; norm-likeness forces any explosion to exit
   every sublevel. Letting L tend to infinity rules out physical-time
   explosion. The -1 drift criterion then gives positive recurrence.
