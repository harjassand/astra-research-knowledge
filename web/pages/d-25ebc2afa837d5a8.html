# Positive-hazard and factorial tomography for sparse mass-action reaction networks

**Independent research note — 10 October 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)**

**Verification status:** Conditional exact mathematical derivations, elementary proof supplied below, reproducible numerical tests. Neither original historical priority, external peer review, formal verification, physical experimentation, nor a civilization-level breakthrough has been established. The mathematical ingredients include established Markov generator identities, the binomial/Newton basis, positive sparse polynomial learning, and weighted-hypergraph reconstruction.

## 0. Capability objective and what was actually attained

Target barrier: infer a previously unknown stochastic molecular mechanism, including its distinct elementary transformations and all reaction rates, from *unpaired* population snapshots, without guessing an exhaustive candidate interaction graph in advance.

Under an explicit *active-query interface* (precise controlled starting molecule counts and complete destructive endpoint readouts), the entire non-null bounded-order stochastic mass-action reaction network is identifiable. For fixed reaction order, a constructive procedure uses a number of experimental batches polynomial in species count and number of reaction channels, with explicit statistical dependence on small rates and observation time. The final result is restricted; no general cell-scale network inference or historic leap is claimed.

The most useful conceptual separation is:

* A binary **departure observable** is sufficient to determine the set of reactant *supports*, and can be processed by a nonnegative hypergraph oracle.
* Full short-time **endpoint jump distributions** are sufficient to identify complete reactant counts, product counts, and kinetic parameters by triangular Newton inversion.

The first problem is easier than the second. The exact observation interface remains the major obstacle to general application.

## 1. Model and information contract

Let there be `d >= 1` labeled species and at most `R` non-null directed reactions `r` with input complex `alpha_r in N^d`, output complex `beta_r in N^d`, and fixed positive rate `kappa_r`. Their reactant and product molecular orders satisfy `|alpha_r|_1 <= k` and `|beta_r|_1 <= ell`. Assume `kappa_min <= kappa_r <= kappa_max`, `kappa_min > 0`, `k,ell` known. Reaction propensities are stochastic mass action in the binomial normalization

    lambda_r(x) = kappa_r * prod_i binom(x_i, alpha_{ri}).

The nonexplosive CTMC has generator

    L f(x) = sum_r lambda_r(x) [f(x+beta_r-alpha_r)-f(x)].

Only channels with `beta_r != alpha_r` are observable; duplicate pairs `(alpha,beta)` are aggregated by adding rate constants. Reverse channels are distinct. This defines the maximal identification target, without pretending to recover invisible reactions or physically indistinguishable duplicates.

**Permitted experimental action:** for each run, choose and initialize exactly a deterministic `x in N^d`; let the CTMC evolve for a controlled time `t`; destructively observe the complete endpoint `X_t`. Runs are independent. There are no matched time trajectories or measured molecular reaction events. The initial condition is known because it was prepared, not because a trajectory was tracked. When inferring supports use `x_i = M 1[i in S]` with `M = max(k,1)` for user-selected mask `S`.

**Hard resource obligations:** preparation fidelity, molecule counting, species naming, product-order knowledge, sampling at short times, precision, random-mask resets, computation, and the tiny positive-rate threshold are all required and charged. In most realistic cell experiments these assumptions are not satisfied.

## 2. Selected direction and competing opportunities

Exploratory opportunities considered before consulting Astra's catalogue:

1. Deterministic black-box polynomial identity testing: potentially foundational but no decisive new derandomization or circuit lower-bound argument found.
2. Recovery of nonlinear latent stochastic dynamics under changing diffusion regimes: potentially important, but a June 2026 primary paper already proves a close identifiability result, so this route was not claimed as new.
3. Generic Hamiltonian learning from product-state short-time entropy: a candidate cut-average invariant has an aliasing obstruction. Existing Hamiltonian-learning algorithms are also strong.
4. Stochastic generator identification from unpaired population snapshots: an explicit positive-hazard interface enabled a constructive support algorithm and, independently, a full factorial inversion of reaction channels.

The fourth was selected because the exact algebraic obstacle was tractable, not because it was already proved to have 9–10/10 impact. The decisive *unsolved* general capability is information-efficient chemical mechanism inference with realistic sensing and perturbation.

## 3. Exact support encoding theorem

For each subset `S subseteq [d]`, set `x(S)_i = M` for `i in S` and `0` otherwise, with `M >= k`. Let

    q(S) = lim_{t downarrow 0} Pr_{x(S)}[X_t != x(S)] / t.

Then

    q(S) = sum_{A subseteq S} theta_A,

where

    theta_A = sum_{r: supp(alpha_r)=A}
                 kappa_r * prod_{i in A} binom(M,alpha_{ri}).

Every present `theta_A > 0`; if every reaction rate is at least `kappa_min`, then `theta_A >= kappa_min`. Every support has `|A| <= k`, and there are at most `R` different supports. The function `q` is therefore a nonnegative, sparse multilinear polynomial in the occupancy bits `b_i=1[i in S]`:

    q(b) = sum_A theta_A prod_{i in A} b_i.

**Proof.** At deterministic `x`, CTMC transition theory gives `Pr_x(X_t != x) = Lambda(x)t + O_x(t^2)` where `Lambda(x)=sum_r lambda_r(x)` over non-null channels; this follows immediately by separating one- and multiple-jump histories. For `x(S)`, `lambda_r(x(S))` vanishes unless all consumed species in `supp(alpha_r)` lie in `S`, since `binom(0,a)=0` for any `a>=1`. Otherwise the factor is exactly `kappa_r prod_i binom(M,alpha_{ri})`, independent of the other members of `S`. Group reactions by reactant support. Strict positivity comes from mass-action positivity and `M>=k>=alpha_{ri}`. QED.

**Why positivity matters.** Mean population drift `E[L X_i]` contains signed increments `nu_{ri}`, so opposing reactions can cancel identically. The exit hazard is a *positive sum*, preserving a nonzero witness for every enabled support. This is an established class of monotone-additive encoding, not proof of a new mathematical primitive.

## 4. Constructive positive-Fourier support peeling

Define, for `B subseteq [d]`, with `b_i` independent unbiased bits:

    C(B) = E_b [ q(b) * prod_{i in B}(2b_i-1) ]
         = sum_{A superseteq B} theta_A * 2^{-|A|}.

If `H` is disjoint from `B`, let `C_0(B;H)` be the same expectation with `b_i=0` for `i in H` (the other bits independent and unbiased). Then

    C_0(B;H) = sum_{A superseteq B, A intersect H = empty}
                     theta_A * 2^{-|A|}.

Consequently,

    D(B,H) = C(B)-C_0(B;H)
           = sum_{A superseteq B, A intersect H != empty}
                     theta_A * 2^{-|A|}.

This difference is strictly positive **if and only if** there is a still-unrecovered reactant support that contains `B` and at least one element of `H`. Its smallest nonzero value is at least `kappa_min * 2^{-k}`.

**Proof of Fourier identity.** Each monomial indexed by `A` factorizes. If `B` is not a subset of `A`, an independent factor `(2b_i-1)` has zero expectation. If `B subseteq A`, then `E[b_i(2b_i-1)]=1/2` for each `i in B` and `E[b_i]=1/2` for each `i in A\B`, yielding `2^{-|A|}`. Forcing `H` absent kills exactly the monomials meeting `H`. QED.

**Algorithm (`PositivePeel`).**

```
found = {}
while residual C(empty) > 0:
    B = empty
    while D(B, [d] \ B) > 0:
        H = [d] \ B
        binary-search subsets of H with D(B, candidate) > 0
        add the discovered coordinate to B
    theta_B = sum_{T subseteq B} (-1)^(|B|-|T|) q(T)
    found[B] = theta_B
    subtract theta_B * prod_{i in B} b_i from all future correlations
return found
```

Each `D` uses two correlation evaluations; a binary search identifies a new necessary coordinate in `O(log d)` queries. Each support contains at most `k` coordinates. The inclusion-exclusion expression for its weight is precisely Boolean Möbius inversion and costs `2^{|B|} <= 2^k` additional ordinary exit queries.

**Correctness.** A positive `D(B,H)` means an extension support exists; a positive sub-half exists whenever a positive full group exists. Inductively `B` remains contained in at least one true support until no further extension is possible. Then `B` itself is a maximal true support, because otherwise `D(B,[d]\B)>0`. Möbius inversion extracts its exact coefficient even in the presence of nested supports. Removing that monomial preserves nonnegativity of the remaining representation. Each iteration removes a distinct positive support. If the only remaining support is `empty`, the method returns it (the extension condition is immediately false). Thus it terminates after at most `R` iterations and finds every support exactly, in

    O(R [ (k+1) log(d+1) + 2^k ])

exact *correlation/point-query types*. Each correlation is an expectation, not a cost-free primitive: physical sampling cost is analyzed in Section 6.

**Important predecessor.** Additive weighted hypergraph reconstruction is a well-established problem, with Bshouty--Mazzawi (STACS 2010) obtaining `O(m log n / log m)` *exact additive oracle* queries for constant-rank weighted hypergraphs. `PositivePeel` is an elementary constructive decoder, **not** a claim to optimality or priority in hypergraph algorithms.

## 5. Exact full-reaction tomography via Newton differences

For every nonzero net jump `nu in Z^d`, define the infinitesimal destination intensity

    j_nu(x) = lim_{t downarrow 0} Pr_x[X_t = x+nu]/t.

Then

    j_nu(x) = sum_{alpha}
                  kappa_{alpha,alpha+nu} prod_i binom(x_i,alpha_i),

with zero coefficients for absent or invalid channels. For a function `f` on `N^d`, define `Delta_i f(x)=f(x+e_i)-f(x)` and `Delta^alpha=prod_i Delta_i^{alpha_i}`.

**Full inversion theorem.** For any candidate input complex `alpha` with `|alpha|_1<=k` and any nonzero `nu` such that `alpha+nu>=0` coordinatewise,

    kappa_{alpha,alpha+nu}
      = (Delta^alpha j_nu)(0)
      = sum_{0<=gamma<=alpha}
            (-1)^{|alpha|-|gamma|} binom(alpha,gamma) j_nu(gamma).

Here `binom(alpha,gamma)=prod_i binom(alpha_i,gamma_i)`. The expression reconstructs the aggregate rate for *each* directed reaction, not just a species interaction or mean drift. Each nonzero recovered coefficient yields `beta=alpha+nu` and kinetic constant `kappa`.

**Proof.** Use the identity `Delta_i binom(x_i,a_i)=binom(x_i,a_i-1)`. Thus

    (Delta^alpha prod_i binom(x_i,beta_i))|_{x=0}
       = prod_i binom(0,beta_i-alpha_i)
       = 1 if beta=alpha, else 0,

interpreting binomial coefficients with negative second index as zero. Applying this to the finite polynomial expansion of `j_nu` isolates exactly its coefficient at `alpha`. Expanding the finite differences yields the displayed alternating sum. QED.

The recovered supports from Section 4 restrict candidates to `alpha` with `supp(alpha)` among known supports and `|alpha|<=k`. There are at most `binom(k,|A|)<=2^k` positive input multiplicity vectors per support `A`, and at most `(k+1)^k` deterministic seed states `gamma<=alpha` for all such `alpha` per support. Short-time endpoint histograms from these states determine all `j_nu(gamma)`. A true non-null net jump `nu` appears from seed `x=alpha` with instantaneous intensity at least `kappa_min`, so net jumps can be discovered rather than supplied by an oracle.

**Identification limitations.** Different elementary physical mechanisms that have the same `alpha`, `beta`, and summed propensity are observationally equivalent. Reactions with `alpha=beta` make no observable jumps and cannot be reconstructed by endpoint laws. Time-resolved passive snapshots may have additional nonidentifiability; the theorem only covers the stated active interface.

## 6. Finite-sample and full resource accounting

Let

    B = R * kappa_max * max(1,k+ell)^k,
    C = 3*B^2,
    Delta = kappa_min * 2^{-k}.

For every prepared initial state used above (each coordinate <= `max(k,1)`), the *initial* and any *once-jumped* state's total rates are at most `B` (if `k=0`, there are only zeroth-order reactants). A second jump by time `t` has probability at most `B^2 t^2/2`, regardless of later instantaneous rates. Comparing the one-jump probability with its first-order term gives the uniform **short-time bias bound**

    | Pr_x[X_t != x]/t - Lambda(x) | <= C*t,
    | Pr_x[X_t = x+nu]/t - j_nu(x) | <= C*t

for `nu != 0`. No upper bound on states reached after two or more jumps is needed for this comparison; assume the CTMC is nonexplosive. The conservative constant `C=3 B^2` covers both cases.

> **Boundary on `k=0`:** use `M=max(k,1)` as above, so all prepared states still have coordinates at most `M`. Rates for zeroth-order reactions are independent of the initial state, and `B=R kappa_max` suffices. For `k>=1`, `M=k`.

Let `m` be the number of distinct true supports, `m<=R`. For finite-sample support recovery choose a target accuracy `eta` satisfying

    eta <= Delta/[8(1+R*2^k)].

For simultaneous channel-rate recovery to error at most `epsilon` and identification by the known floor `kappa_min`, also choose

    eta <= epsilon/2^k,
    eta <= kappa_min/(4*2^k).

Set `t<=min(1,eta/(2 C))`. Every randomized correlation is measured by drawing an independent random mask, preparing the associated state, and observing

    Y = chi_B(mask) * 1[X_t != X_0] / t,

where `chi_B=prod_{i in B}(2b_i-1)`. For `C_0`, additionally force `H` absent. For point queries and jump histograms use analogous indicator variables without a character sign. They lie in `[-1/t,1/t]`. Hoeffding's inequality shows that

    N >= (8/(t^2*eta^2)) * log(2 Q D / delta)

independent endpoints *per sampled query or deterministic seed-state batch* suffice to keep each raw estimate within `eta/2` of its finite-time expectation with probability at least `1-delta`, by a union bound. Here `Q` upper-bounds the polynomially many correlation/point and deterministic seed batches; `D` upper-bounds possible distinct single-step jump labels (one may take

    D <= [sum_{a=0}^k binom(d+a-1,a)]
         [sum_{b=0}^ell binom(d+b-1,b)]

as a conservative bound, ignoring duplicates). Counting every candidate label separately in the union bound does NOT mean an independent experiment is needed for each label: a complete state endpoint yields a single multinomial histogram.

**Proof of robustness.** Finite-time bias is at most `Ct<=eta/2`; sampling error is at most `eta/2`, hence each raw query/transition estimate has error at most `eta`. A recovered weight obtained by at most `2^k` terms has error at most `2^k eta`. After at most `R` recovered weights, a residual `C` estimate has error at most `eta+R*2^k eta`; a difference `D` at most twice this. The true `C` (for existence) or `D` is either zero or at least `Delta`. The chosen `eta` makes the error no more than `Delta/4`. A fixed threshold `Delta/2` therefore distinguishes all true zero and nonzero tests, ensuring correct support peeling. Each Newton inversion of any `j_nu` contains at most `2^k` transition-rate estimates with total absolute error at most `2^k eta<=epsilon`. Since true individual rate coefficients are at least `kappa_min`, thresholding at `kappa_min/2` recovers precisely the present reaction pairs. QED.

**Resource scale:** `Q = O(R[(k+1)log(d+1)+2^k+(k+1)^k])` sampled query/seed batches. The total number of independent endpoint preparations is at most `Q*N`, and each full-state readout handles `d` counts. Evaluation of residual correlations requires additional `O(Rk)` arithmetic per sampled mask in a simple implementation. One conservative total computational bound is polynomial in `Q,N,d,R,log D` for fixed `k,ell`, allowing sparse histogram lookup instead of materializing a `D`-element dense array. Memory can be `O(R k + Q + number-of-distinct-observed-jumps)` in streaming form, excluding retained raw biological data. Randomness is charged to independent mask generation.

**Improved but still expensive sampling:** Since `Pr[X_t != x]<=Bt`, each correlation sample has variance at most `B/t`. Bernstein's inequality can improve the `N` dependence to `O([B/(t eta^2)+1/(t eta)] log(QD/delta))`. Neither bound is practical for arbitrarily small `kappa_min`, large `k`, or large `R`; physical reset and assay overhead can dominate everything. Exact real-valued rate reconstruction from finitely many snapshots is mathematically impossible without finite precision / distributional assumptions: the finite-sample statement certifies only errors <= `epsilon`.

**Known precision assumptions:** The rates and initial molecular counts are not estimated jointly. Time is calibrated. There is no unmodeled observation noise, perturbation bias, reaction-induced species aliasing, or hidden species. To certify identification without a known floor `kappa_min`, one would need an additional stopping rule with statistical guarantees or accept an unknown missed weak-reaction risk.

## 7. An alternative support-only experimental interface (derived extension)

A potentially less restrictive but still *strong* intervention/sensor arrangement can recover supports without exact starting molecular counts. Assume a single random baseline molecule-count vector `Y~mu`. For every mask `S`, one can perform a species-selective deletion `X_0^S=Y*1_S` **without changing the distribution of retained coordinates**, and one has a universal irreversible detector reporting whether *any actual reaction event* occurred before `t`. The detector must distinguish a genuine event even if the net state subsequently returns, and it must not alter the original reaction rates.

Then (provided `E_mu Lambda(Y)<infinity`)

    q_mu(S) = lim_{t downarrow 0}
                 Pr[at least one reported event | S]/t
            = sum_{A subseteq S} Theta_A,

    Theta_A = sum_{r:supp(alpha_r)=A}
                 kappa_r E_mu[prod_{i in A}binom(Y_i,alpha_ri)].

**Proof.** Conditional on `Y`, the first-event probability is `1-exp(-Lambda(Y*1_S)t)`. Divide by `t`, apply dominated convergence using `Lambda(Y*1_S)` as bound, then factor each propensity by support. The expectation of its enabling factorial monomial does not depend on which other coordinates have been deleted, by the intervention assumption. QED.

If every true reactant complex has positive joint enabling factorial moment, each present `Theta_A>0` and the same support decoder applies. This removes *known exact molecule counts* for the support stage **but introduces an equally explicit universal event detector and nonperturbing deletion condition**. It does *not* recover products, rates, or stoichiometric multiplicities. No general physical device achieving these requirements was supplied. This is a conditional interface substitution, not a general escape from the acquisition barrier.

## 8. Counterexamples and impossibility boundaries

1. **Rates arbitrarily close to zero.** Compare a no-reaction process to a single reaction with rate `kappa` at its enabling seed. Short-time snapshots exhibit an event with probability `~kappa*t` in the latter. Distinguishing the two at constant confidence requires at least order `1/(kappa*t)` independent experiments in the relevant rare-event regime. Thus no uniform rate-free sample bound can hold.
2. **Inaccessible reactants.** If the only initial state ever prepared is `0`, no reaction creates species `B`, and a hidden reaction consuming `B` is added, the added reaction is forever inactive. Every obtainable trajectory and snapshot is identical. Some form of excitation is indispensable.
3. **Inadequate first two moments.** A one-dimensional compound-Poisson process jumping `+1` at rate `1` and `+3` at rate `1` has `E[X_t]=4t`, `Var(X_t)=10t` from zero. A different process jumping `+1` at rate `1`, `+2` at rate `3/4`, and `+4` at rate `3/8` has *exactly the same mean and variance for every time*, but a different jump law. Mean/variance fitting alone cannot distinguish these mechanisms.
4. **Invisible self-loops and channel merging.** `alpha->alpha` never changes an endpoint; duplicate channels sharing identical `alpha->beta` appear as one aggregated rate. No observation of species count snapshots can distinguish them.
5. **Partial readout and latent species.** If relevant species or jump destinations are not observed, different networks can have identical observed marginal laws. The factorial inversion requires `j_nu` on complete labeled states.
6. **Perturbed interventions.** If turning off species `i` changes rates of reactions not consuming `i`, the positive polynomial representation generally fails. The alternative random-baseline theorem explicitly prohibits this.
7. **Finite-time contamination.** Two or more reactions in a short interval may mimic a nonexistent direct jump; an endpoint-difference estimator at fixed nonzero `t` is not an exact generator without an error analysis. The bias and resource floor are charged in Section 6.
8. **High reaction order.** The factors `2^k`, `(k+1)^k`, `max(k+ell)^k` and the signal floor `2^{-k}` prevent a uniform scalable result when `k` grows substantially. Species-dimension polynomiality is for *bounded order* only.

## 9. Executable checks and their scope

`verify.py` runs exact synthetic `q`/`j` mathematical identity and peeling tests on four random low-order networks with `8-11` species and `6-12` reaction channels. It uses complete enumeration of all `2^d` masks for each *ideal exact* correlation, so **this identity-check implementation itself is exponential in `d`**. For these small instances every support and individual rate coefficient is recovered within floating-point rounding.

`randomized_oracle_test.py` uses *Monte Carlo sampled masks* instead of `2^d` enumeration. It recovered all six support hyperedges from a synthetic `d=48`, `k=2` weighted additive oracle using `137` estimated correlation types and `10,960,019` noiseless synthetic `q` evaluations, with exact Möbius weights. **This verifies the randomized query mechanism, not a physically acquired continuous-time derivative or optimal sample performance.**

`verify.py` additionally simulates a one-species Gillespie CTMC with independent, unpaired endpoint experiments for three deterministic starting counts. Total `1,050,000` endpoints at `t=0.02`, four true reactions, numerical recovered rate max absolute error `0.0341428571`, and a false second-order birth coefficient of `0.0187142857` due to statistical and finite-time effects. This illustrates why positivity/gap thresholds and large sample budgets are necessary; the simulation does not establish a credible biological experiment.

## 12. Highest-value unresolved gates, not minor extensions

**Gate A: The acquisition barrier.** Develop and validate a practical experiment providing consistent support masks and a positive event signal (or equivalent observables) *without exact bulk reinitialization and full high-dimensional state readout*. Prove identifiability including the intervention perturbations, assay noise and censored species. This is the gate that would turn support reconstruction from a query-logic result into a potentially consequential scientific method.

**Gate B: Net stoichiometry recovery with weak measurement.** Prove full reaction recovery from realistically accessible sparse/partial observations or exhibit a sharp identifiability obstruction under the exact intended interface. Do not repackage full-state readout as a free oracle.

**Gate C: Resource-realistic benchmarks.** Implement a matched full acquisition pipeline on curated ground-truth reaction networks, with actual sample counts, preparation throughput, measurement errors and cost per recovered channel; compare to 2016 Reactionet Lasso, Cinquemani's methods, modern stochastic system ID, and direct finite-generator inference.

**Gate D: A general principle.** Determine whether the nonnegative hazard-support representation admits a *much broader* theorem for experimentally accessible jump processes, dynamical systems, or scientific models, under verifiable rather than stipulated observability. Without such a result, this remains specialist mathematical machinery.

**Bottom line:** The exact active-interface inverse theorem and its error accounting are useful, but they are not a historic capability revolution. The difficulty that remains is *creating an informative, affordable information channel from the physical system*, not decoding an already ideal channel.
