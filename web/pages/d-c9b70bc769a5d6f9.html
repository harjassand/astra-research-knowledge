## Degree35 is linear; degree34 already is not

The original Hessian has maximal entry degree10, maximal two-by-two minor degree20, and maximal cofactor degree28. A degree-nine repair has Hessian entry degree at most7. Terms with two repair Hessians can reach degree34 but cannot reach degree35; terms with three or four have bounds31 and28. The sole degree28 cofactor is

\[
 (\operatorname{adj}H\psi_0)_{\rho\rho,28}
       =684288x^{11}y^{15}\eta^2.
\]

Thus degree35 forces \(\partial_{\rho\rho}R_9=0\), hence

\[
 R_9=\rho A_8(x,y,\eta)+B_9(x,y,\eta).
\]

After this condition, the non-base Hessian entries, including repairs, retain the cycle13 bounds \(\deg H_{ij}\le d_i+d_j\), \(d=(4,4,3,4)\). Terms with zero or one base-only Hessian factor have degree at most30 or32. Therefore the degree34 and33 equations come entirely from the base determinant times the residual two-by-two determinant. Its nonzero degree20 factor is

\[
 (\det H_{x,y}h_0)_{20}=-456192x^6y^{14}.
\]

At degree34 define \(a=\partial_\eta A_8\), \(b=\partial_{\rho\rho}R_8\). The residual determinant has degree14 part exactly

\[
 \left(-\frac p4+b\right)\left(-\frac32p\eta^2\right)
       -\left(-\frac12p\eta+a\right)^2
 =\frac18p^2\eta^2-\frac32p\eta^2b+p\eta a-a^2.
\]

The last term is the essential quadratic repair-Hessian contribution. Setting \(\eta=0\) gives \(a(x,y,0)^2=0\), so \(a=\eta W\). The equation then yields

\[
 b=\frac p{12}+\frac23W-\frac{2W^2}{3p}.
\]

Because \(b\) is polynomial, \(p\mid W^2\). Unique factorization for \(p=x^5y\) implies \(x^3y\mid W\). Since \(W\) is homogeneous of degree6,

\[
 W=x^3yV_2,\qquad
 b=\frac p{12}+\frac23x^3yV_2-\frac23xyV_2^2,
\]

with arbitrary homogeneous quadratic \(V_2(x,y,\eta)\). These are polynomial necessities, not a finite coefficient search.

## Degree33 synchronizes the free quadratic form

Put \(W_0=W(x,y,0)\). At \(\eta=0\), the degree13 residual determinant is

\[
 -\frac{2}{3p}(W_0-p/2)^2
       \left[\rho(W_0-p/2)+(B_9)_{\eta\eta}(x,y,0)\right].
\]

The coefficient of \(\rho\) forces \((W_0-p/2)^3=0\). Thus

\[
 V_2=\frac{x^2}{2}+\eta\ell_1,
 \qquad \ell_1=\alpha x+\beta_1y+\gamma\eta.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 a=\eta\left(\frac p2+x^3y\eta\ell_1\right),\qquad
 b=\frac p4-\frac23xy\eta^2\ell_1^2.
\]

For the **full** degree33 equation, not only the restriction, set

\[
 u_6=-p/4+b,\quad v_7=-p\eta/2+a,\quad w_8=-3p\eta^2/2,
\]
\[
 u_5=2q_5+(R_7)_{\rho\rho},\qquad
 v_6=4q_5\eta+\rho b_\eta+(A_7)_\eta,
\]
\[
 w_7=\rho[W-p/2+\eta W_\eta]+12q_5\eta^2+(B_9)_{\eta\eta}.
\]

Here \(R_8=(b/2)\rho^2+\rho A_7+B_8\); all displayed free polynomials have the indicated homogeneous degree and are independent of \(\rho\). The equation is exactly

\[
 u_6w_7+u_5w_8-2v_7v_6=0.
\]

Solving it over the fraction field for \((R_7)_{\rho\rho}\), the coefficient of \(\rho\) is

\[
 \frac{4y\eta}{9x}\ell_1^2(2\ell_1+3\gamma\eta).
\]

Neither \(A_7\) nor \(B_9\) can affect that coefficient. Polynomiality at \(x=0\) forces

\[
 y\eta(\beta_1y+\gamma\eta)^2(2\beta_1y+5\gamma\eta)=0,

which, in the polynomial domain, implies \(\beta_1=\gamma=0\). Therefore **only the scalar \(\alpha\) remains** in this quadratic form.

The exact remaining condition is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (R_7)_{\rho\rho}={}&\frac89\alpha^3x^2y\eta\rho-2q_5
 -\frac{16}3\alpha\frac{q_5}{x}\eta
 -\frac{16}3\alpha^2\frac{q_5}{x^2}\eta^2\\
 &-\frac43\alpha\frac{(A_7)_\eta}{x}
 -\frac49\alpha^2\frac{(B_9)_{\eta\eta}}{x^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Because \(q_5\) is divisible by \(x^4\), the only remaining polynomiality restriction is

\[
 \boxed{x^2\mid\alpha^2(B_9)_{\eta\eta}
                 +3\alpha x(A_7)_\eta.}
\]

This is automatic when \(\alpha=0\). When it holds, integration in \(\rho\) gives a polynomial homogeneous \(R_7\), with free terms \(\rho A_6+B_7\). Moreover \(A_8\) has an arbitrary integration term depending only on \(x,y\). Thus these three leading faces leave substantial free data. They do **not** establish a degree-nine impossibility or determinant solution.
