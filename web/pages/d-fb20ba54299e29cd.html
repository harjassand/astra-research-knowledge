RANGE-2 REPULSION: NUMBER ARCHIVE SURVIVES, AND A FREE-SECTOR DECODER IS ASYMPTOTICALLY OPERATIONAL
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the number-tail and perturbative decoder bounds below; FINITE-EVIDENCE for the N=2,...,8 numerical replay; SOURCE-REPORTED for the periodic-bulk local-charge classification and hard-core-boson finite-size studies. The exact open-chain nonintegrability classification remains outside these sources. No external proof review, full finite-bit compiler execution, or hardware preparation was performed.

Decision
========

For the open path with fixed J,tau,V>0, N=L-1, beta=tau L^2, and unknown lambda in a supplied interval [-B,B], the range-2 repulsion W=V sum_i n_i n_(i+2) does not remove the exact total-number sufficient statistic. Its sector archive has the same uniform cubic low-temperature overflow upper bound as the free XX chain. This follows directly from positivity of W and sectorwise min-max; it does not assume Bethe integrability.

Sibling scope: cycle2 leaf01 proves a broader qualitative whole-Fock limit for arbitrary nonnegative fixed-range density interactions. This leaf's added result is quantitative for the homogeneous fixed-V range-2 case: an explicit finite-L relative-entropy/Pinsker rate and a candidate operational free-sector decoder.

The exact sector decoder is not automatically an efficient preparation algorithm: it must prepare the interacting canonical state in each retained number sector. There is, however, a separate asymptotically efficient approximation. The entire interacting Gibbs state is within O_(J,tau,B)(sqrt(V/L)) trace distance of the free XX Gibbs state. A decoder that replaces each retained interacting sector by the matching free canonical sector therefore has error at most the archive tail plus this explicit perturbation term. A stable finite-bit mode-subset sampler and an O(N^2)-Givens Slater circuit give a polynomial-in-N decoder for fixed precision and fixed model constants once N is large enough. The sampler/circuit construction is derived here but not implemented as a certified finite-bit compiler.

1. Interface and exact sector archive
=====================================

Assume an open chain P_N, L=N+1, n_i=b_i^dagger b_i in {0,1}, and M=sum_i n_i. Set

  K_0 = -2J sum_(i=1)^(N-1) (b_i^dagger b_(i+1)+h.c.) + 4J M,
  W   = V sum_(i=1)^(N-2) n_i n_(i+2),
  H_lambda = K_0 + W - lambda M/L^2,
  beta = tau L^2.

J,tau,V are fixed positive supplied parameters; B<infinity is supplied and lambda is unknown in [-B,B]. The result is for the path boundary condition and fixed V; it does not silently cover periodic boundaries or a V that grows with N. Since [K_0,M]=[W,M]=0, with P_m the projector onto M=m,

  omega_m^V = P_m exp[-beta(K_0+W)]P_m / Z_m^V,
  Z_m^V = Tr(P_m exp[-beta(K_0+W)]),
  rho_lambda^V = sum_(m=0)^N q_lambda^V(m) omega_m^V,
  q_lambda^V(m) = exp(tau lambda m) Z_m^V / Z_lambda^V.

Thus omega_m^V is exactly independent of lambda. A nondestructive measurement of M and a classical record of m is a sufficient-statistic archive, whether or not the fixed-sector Hamiltonian is Bethe-solvable.

For a cutoff d>=1, store each of m=0,...,d-1 exactly and use one overflow label for m>=d. On overflow the decoder outputs the vacuum |0...0>. If it can prepare omega_m^V on the retained labels, then the reconstructed state differs from rho_lambda^V by exactly P_lambda^V(M>=d): the true overflow block is supported on m>=d, orthogonal to the added vacuum mass. The archive uses d+1 labels, or ceil(log2(d+1)) bits. For d>=N+1 the representation is exact. The error statement is an upper bound, not an optimality claim.

2. Repulsive min-max preserves the cubic tail
=============================================

In the m-particle sector K_0+W is a positive perturbation of K_0. By the min-max principle, its eigenvalues in sorted order are each at least the corresponding free eigenvalue; therefore Z_m^V<=Z_m^0 for every m and every beta>0. The vacuum term in Z_lambda^V is 1. The open-chain one-particle free energies and their beta-scaled values are

  epsilon_(L,k)=4J(1-cos(pi k/L)),
  a_(L,k)=L^2 epsilon_(L,k) >= 8J k^2,  1<=k<=N.

For any s>0, uniformly in N and lambda in [-B,B],

  P_lambda^V(M>=d)
    <= exp(-s d) sum_(m>=d) exp(tau B m) Z_m^V
    <= exp(-s d) product_(k=1)^N [1+exp(s+tau B-tau a_(L,k))]
    <= exp(-s d) product_(k>=1) [1+exp(s+tau B-8 tau J k^2)].

Taking s=2 tau J d^2 (equivalently s=tau c d^2/4 with c=8J) and splitting the product at k approximately sqrt(d^2/4+B/(8J)) gives

  log P_lambda^V(M>=d) <= -(4/3) tau J d^3 + O_(J,tau,B)(d^2).

The constants are independent of N, lambda, and V>=0. The proof charges no acquisition of q_lambda^V or lambda: the encoder measures the supplied quantum input's total number. The archive dimension d+1 is sufficient for tail error at most epsilon when d=O_(J,tau,B)(1+(log(1/epsilon))^(1/3)); equivalently its required retained-bit count is O(log d). This is a one-sided upper bound and no matching tail lower bound is claimed.

The proof does not need a free-fermion solution for the interacting sector. It needs only W>=0 and the free dispersion as a comparison Hamiltonian. If W is attractive, or if the field ceases to be a scalar on each M sector, this argument no longer applies as written.

3. A free conditional-sector decoder with explicit error
========================================================

Let rho_(s,lambda) be the Gibbs state of K_0+sW-lambda M/L^2, s in [0,1]. Relative entropy gives

  D(rho_(1,lambda)||rho_(0,lambda))
     = -beta <W>_(1,lambda) + log Z_0(lambda)-log Z_1(lambda)
     <= log Z_0(lambda)-log Z_1(lambda)
     = beta integral_0^1 <W>_(s,lambda) ds
     <= beta <W>_(0,lambda).

The final inequality follows because s -> log Z_s is convex: its second derivative is the nonnegative Kubo-Mori variance of W, so <W>_s is nonincreasing. The free state is a number-conserving fermionic Gaussian state under Jordan-Wigner. With sine modes phi_k(i)=sqrt(2/L) sin(pi k i/L) and occupancies p_k=(1+exp(tau[a_(L,k)-lambda]))^(-1), Wick's rule gives

  <n_i n_(i+2)>_0
    = sum_(k<ell) p_k p_ell |phi_k(i)phi_ell(i+2)-phi_ell(i)phi_k(i+2)|^2.

The determinant is at most 4 pi (k+ell)/L^2 in absolute value: subtract phi_k(i)phi_ell(i) from the first term and use |phi_k(i+2)-phi_k(i)|<=2 sqrt(2) pi k/L^(3/2). Since p_k<=exp(tau B-8 tau J k^2), summing the O(L) range-2 bonds yields

  <W>_0 <= V C_*(J,tau,B)/L^3,
  C_* = 32 pi^2 exp(2 tau B) sum_(1<=k<ell<infinity)
                    (k^2+ell^2) exp[-8 tau J(k^2+ell^2)] < infinity.

An explicit computable upper bound is available. For gamma=8 tau J, put
S_0=sqrt(pi)/(2 sqrt(gamma)) and
S_2=sqrt(pi)/(4 gamma^(3/2))+1/gamma+sqrt(pi)/(2 sqrt(gamma)). Integral comparison gives C_*<=32 pi^2 exp(2 tau B) S_0 S_2. This makes the N>=const/epsilon^2 threshold explicit; it also displays its potentially large dependence on small tau J or large tau B.

Pinsker therefore gives the whole-state bound

  T(rho_(1,lambda),rho_(0,lambda)) <= delta_L,
  delta_L = sqrt(tau V C_*/(2L)).

There is a sharper way to apply this to the archive than a triangle inequality on the whole state. Both states are block diagonal in M, so the relative-entropy chain rule implies

  sum_m q_lambda^V(m) D(omega_m^V || omega_m^0)
     <= D(rho_(1,lambda)||rho_(0,lambda)) <= tau V C_*/L.

Quantum Pinsker and Jensen then give sum_m q_lambda^V(m) T(omega_m^V,omega_m^0)<=delta_L. Replacing each retained omega_m^V by the free conditional state omega_m^0, while still mapping overflow to vacuum, has total trace error

  <= P_lambda^V(M>=d) + delta_L.

The decoder uses no lambda and no interacting partition function. It does use the measured m, N, J, and tau. V and B enter its certified error guarantee and the size threshold, though not the free conditional-state circuit itself. The bound is uniform in lambda. For V=V_N, the displayed guarantee tends to zero whenever V_N/L -> 0; the theorem here assumes fixed V>0.

4. Finite-bit and gate accounting for the free decoder
=====================================================

For a retained m, the exact free conditional state is the mixture of Slater determinants indexed by mode subsets S of size m, with probabilities proportional to exp[-tau sum_(k in S) a_(L,k)]. Set a_(L,k) in increasing order. A shifted elementary-symmetric-polynomial table avoids tiny unshifted partition weights:

  F_(i,j) = sum_(S subset {i,...,N}, |S|=j)
            exp[-tau(sum_(k in S) a_(L,k) - sum_(k=i)^(i+j-1) a_(L,k))],
  F_(i,j) = F_(i+1,j-1) + exp[-tau(a_(L,i+j)-a_(L,i))] F_(i+1,j).

For valid i,j, 1<=F_(i,j)<=binom(N-i+1,j). At step (i,j), include mode i with probability F_(i+1,j-1)/F_(i,j); otherwise skip with the complementary shifted term. All exponents are nonpositive. For all m<d the table takes O(Nd) arithmetic operations and intermediate values have O(d log N) integer-bit height. For one supplied m it takes O(Nm).

One finite-bit realization (candidate construction, not executed here) is:
* Compute the sine-mode energies to p-bit certified intervals. Since arguments pi k/L lie in [0,pi], range-reduced rational Taylor/binary-splitting trigonometry is polynomial in the bit length of N and p.
* Evaluate each exp(-x), x>=0, by rational intervals; if a certified lower bound has x>p ln 2, replace it by zero with absolute error below 2^-p. F values are bounded by N^d and bounded below by 1, so p=O(d log N+log(Nd/eta)+ell_in) suffices for accumulated DP/ratio error eta after increasing the constant, where ell_in is the encoded length of J,tau,N.
* Round each conditional probability to an r-bit dyadic and draw it with r fair bits. Taking r=ceil(log2(4N/eta)) bounds the union of N threshold-rounding errors by eta/4; allocate the remaining eta to the interval-DP error. This costs O(N log(N/eta)) fair random bits per prepared Slater state.
* The subset S determines a computational mode-occupation Fock state. Apply the second-quantized discrete sine transform. A real N-by-N orthogonal matrix decomposes into O(N^2) Givens rotations, implementable as nearest-neighbor fermionic two-mode gates. With a fixed universal gate set, compiling total trace error eta_g by per-gate synthesis error O(eta_g/N^2) costs O(N^2 polylog(N/eta_g)) two-qubit gates and O(N^2 log(N/eta_g)) description bits. The output is N qubits; the classical subset label is not the quantum output.

The QND encoder can reversibly count the N occupation bits into a saturating min(M,d) register using O(N log N) elementary reversible gates and O(log N) ancillas, then measure the register. Measuring every site separately would destroy within-sector coherences and is not the specified encoder. This gate count assumes an abstract gate set with nonlocal access to the counter; a strictly nearest-neighbor count circuit must additionally route the ancilla/data and its depth is not charged here.

If lambda is known and one instead prepares from scratch, the free Gibbs reference is simpler: sample the independent mode Bernoulli variables p_k(lambda) with O(N log(N/eta)) fair bits after certified probability rounding, and apply the same sine-transform circuit. Its trace error from the interacting target is at most delta_L+eta. This is a distinct interface: preparing from scratch requires lambda or an acquisition procedure; the archive decoder takes its measured m and never estimates lambda.

For fixed J,tau,V,B and target epsilon, choosing d to make the tail <=epsilon/3, L>=9 tau V C_*/(2 epsilon^2) to make delta_L<=epsilon/3, and DP/gate errors <=epsilon/3 yields a polynomial-in-N,1/epsilon implementation for this large-L regime: O(Nd poly(ell_in+d log N+log(N/epsilon))) preprocessing, O(N log(N/epsilon)) random bits, O(N^2 polylog(N/epsilon)) state-preparation gates, plus O(N log N) QND-count gates and N output qubits. The constant C_* can be bounded explicitly by Gaussian integrals; it can be very large when tau J is small or tau B large, so it is not hidden as a practical constant. If J,tau,V,B themselves vary as input, the displayed constant's numerical dependence must be charged; this is not a uniform polynomial solely in their binary input lengths.

The exact interaction-aware decoder has an explicit dense fallback, but it does not give a uniform joint poly(N,1/epsilon) guarantee. In sector m, D_m=binom(N,m), and a dense matrix-exponential/eigendecomposition representation costs roughly O(D_m^3 poly(beta||H_m||, input bits, log(1/eta))) classical arithmetic and generic purification preparation O(D_m^2 polylog(D_m/eta)) gates once a certified density matrix is obtained. Since ||K_0+W||_m<=8Jm+VN and beta=tau(N+1)^2, the required energy-scale precision is polynomial in N for fixed parameters. Since D_(d-1)<=N^(d-1), this is polynomial in N for a fixed cutoff d, with a degree that grows with d. With d=Theta((log(1/epsilon))^(1/3)), it is not a joint polynomial bound. This is a cost of that fallback, not a hardness result. No general low-temperature, finite-bit Gibbs circuit for the interacting target was located in this leaf.

There is also an exact small-cutoff simplification specific to this two-body W: W vanishes identically in the m=0 and m=1 sectors. Thus a cutoff d=2 decoder prepares the true retained sectors using only vacuum and the free one-particle sine-mode Gibbs state; its error is exactly P_V(M>=2), with no delta_L term. For larger fixed d, dense sector methods are polynomial in N at fixed d, though their degree grows with d. This can be operationally useful whenever the explicit finite-N tail already falls below the target epsilon.

5. Equally informed baseline and what is not established
========================================================

Strongest same-interface baseline: the exact M-sector archive with decoder omega_m^V. It has the same unknown lambda, no lambda estimation, and exact error P(M>=d), but its sector preparation is uncharged unless a concrete solver is supplied. The present free-sector decoder improves the operational side by paying an explicit global perturbation error and providing a finite-bit/circuit construction in the large-L regime. The Chernoff tail is an upper bound, not an optimal memory law, and there is no lower bound proving a benefit over another encoder.

Acquisition and preparation are separate. The archive encoder consumes an already prepared input Gibbs state and performs a QND measurement. It does not certify the cost of producing that input state or infer lambda from data. The free approximation can prepare a new state without interacting solver cost only if lambda is supplied; an unknown-lambda-from-scratch task needs a separate acquisition protocol. This report contains no physical-hardware preparation, gate-duration measurement, or N-dependent wall-clock benchmark. Its N=2,...,8 run below is a dense numerical theorem check, not execution of the asymptotic sampler/circuit.

6. Integrability source check
=============================

For the periodic, translation-invariant bulk there is now a stronger primary result than the usual numerical-chaos citation. Shiraishi, J. Stat. Phys. 192, 170 (published 25 November 2025; [arXiv:2501.15506v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15506), 10 April 2026; [journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-025-03551-5)), Theorem 2.1 classifies symmetric periodic spin-1/2 chains with both NN and NNN coupling matrices nonzero. It proves there is no k-support conserved quantity for 4<=k<=L/2 except when a global spin rotation maps the model to a classical ZZ-only family or to a specified Bethe-solvable cross-coupling family. The periodic bulk of the present model maps to J2_ZZ=V/4 !=0 and J1=diag(-J,-J,0), plus a longitudinal field. For J,V>0, J1 has rank two with eigenvalues (-J,-J,0): it is neither the rank-one classical matrix nor the (a,-a,0) cross-coupling matrix in the Bethe exception, and an orthogonal global spin rotation preserves these eigenvalues. Thus this periodic bulk is outside both integrable exceptions in Shiraishi's theorem. This is a rigorous nonintegrability classification in that paper's local-conserved-charge sense, not a statement that no conceivable nonlocal conserved operator exists.

The exact open chain used by the Gibbs proof is not periodic, so Theorem 2.1 does not directly prove nonintegrability of each finite open chain. Rigol and Santos, Phys. Rev. A 82, 011604(R), published 9 July 2010, directly identify NNN repulsive interactions as breaking integrability in finite 1D hard-core bosons using full exact diagonalization. Santos and Rigol, arXiv:1006.0729 (submitted 3 June 2010; journal Phys. Rev. E 82, 031130), specify NN hopping/interaction plus NNN interaction and report integrability breaking via half-filled finite-size exact diagonalization. Santos and Rigol, Phys. Rev. E 81, 036206 (published 5 March 2010), identify NN-only models as integrable and NNN terms as a chaos-inducing perturbation. These are finite-size evidence for corresponding hard-core-boson models, not an all-coupling theorem for every open boundary or the low-density scaling sector.

The archive and closeness proofs above are independent of integrability and remain true even if an open-chain special case had additional conserved quantities.

7. Finite numerical replay
==========================

`exact_numerical_check.py` constructs the open-chain sector matrices for N=2,...,8 at J=1, tau=1/5, V=1, B=1/2, lambda=1/4, diagonalizes them in double precision, and compares the repulsive and free canonical blocks. It checks sector min-max ordering, the relative-entropy/free-W inequality, the relative-entropy block chain rule, the conditional Pinsker bound, the exact archive-tail identity, the Chernoff product upper bound, and the shifted DP against brute-force elementary symmetric sums. All seven finite checks pass. The worst observed min-max violation is only -7.11e-15 (roundoff); the largest DP absolute discrepancy is below 3.5e-18. At N=8 the computed T(rho_V,rho_0)=5.25e-9, the analytic Pinsker bound is 0.0849, and P_V(M>=1)=0.0207. These numbers illustrate that the analytic perturbation constant is conservative at tiny N.

The script reports about 0.10 s for its internal N=2,...,8 calculations on Python 3.14.3/NumPy 2.5.3 (process startup and JSON serialization are outside that counter). This is not the runtime of the finite-bit DP/Givens decoder, and it does not test N=64, a larger-N asymptotic, certified interval arithmetic, or physical gate execution. The full per-sector values and checks are in `exact_numerical_check.json`.

Falsifiers and open gates
========================

The exact archive tail proof would fail if M did not commute with the Hamiltonian, if W were not positive semidefinite, or if the open-chain reference dispersion bound were wrong. The efficient free decoder would fail if the Wick determinant estimate or the relative-entropy monotonicity step failed; the small-N checks test examples only. A next useful gate is to implement certified interval DP and a gate-synthesis error simulator at moderate N, then compare with dense sector matrices on held-out fixtures. External review of the Kubo-Mori bound and the uniform finite-N constants remains open. Strict nonintegrability of the exact open chain and the physical preparation apparatus remain outside this evidence.
