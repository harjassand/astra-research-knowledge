## Exact prior interface

For a finite Hilbert space suppose Hermitian Xbar,Zbar satisfy
Xbar^2=Zbar^2=I, Xbar Zbar=-Zbar Xbar, and commute with the full H.
Let P commute with H and this algebra. Let

    L(rho)=sum_a [S_a rho S_a* - {S_a*S_a,rho}/2],
    L(rho_beta)=0, rho_beta=e^(-beta H)/Z,
    [S_a,Xbar]P=[S_a,Zbar]P=0.                       (1)

Chesi's norm is the induced trace norm restricted to Hermitian inputs,
not a local interaction norm or a diamond norm:

    ||L||_1=max_{F=F*,||F||_1<=1} ||L(F)||_1.         (2)

No detailed balance is required for the theorem itself. The algebra
generated by Xbar,Zbar is a full two-dimensional matrix factor. Thus
H=I_logical tensor H_aux on the **entire Hilbert space**, not just its
ground band. Gibbs likewise factors as I/2 tensor eta_aux. Set
Enc(tau)=tau tensor eta_aux and Dec=Tr_aux. They are CPTP and
Dec Enc=id. They are thermal encodings; this supplies no efficient
preparation algorithm and no statement about a ground-state encoding.

For Enc(tau)=O rho_beta, O belongs to the logical algebra, is Hermitian,
commutes with P,rho_beta and obeys ||O||<=2. Split L(O rho_beta) into
left P/Pperp and input P/Pperp blocks. The two input-Pperp blocks are
each bounded by 2||L||_1 Tr(Pperp rho_beta). The input-P blocks satisfy

    P L(OP rho_beta)=O P L(P rho_beta)
                    =-O P L(Pperp rho_beta),
    Pperp L(OP rho_beta)=Pperp L(P rho_beta)O
                       =-Pperp L(Pperp rho_beta)O.    (3)

The identities use [S_a,O]P=0 and its adjoint P[S_a*,O]=0, plus
stationarity. Each is bounded by the same quantity. The semigroup is
trace-norm contracting on Hermitian inputs, and [H,Enc(tau)]=0, so

    ||Phi_t Enc(tau)-Enc(tau)||_1
        <=8 t ||L||_1 epsilon_P,
    epsilon_P=Tr(Pperp rho_beta).                    (4)

Decoding cannot increase this bound. This independently reconstructs
the prior's quantitative theorem. Its conclusions concern supplied
thermal encoding states throughout the full Gibbs support.

## Jump-rate dependence without a frequency count

For a direct GKSL representation, norm inequalities give

    ||L||_1<=2 sum_a ||S_a||^2.                      (5)

For an independent-channel Davies generator with Hermitian physical
couplings A_alpha and positive rates gamma_alpha(omega), put
g_alpha=sup_omega gamma_alpha(omega). Assume those suprema are finite.
The spectral components are

    A_alpha,omega=sum_{E'-E=omega} P_E A_alpha P_E'.

The unweighted frequency sums are infinite-time averages:

    sum_omega A_omega*A_omega
       =time_average A(t)*A(t),
    sum_omega A_omega F A_omega*
       =time_average A(t)F A(t)*.                  (6)

For positive F the weighted CP part has trace at most
g_alpha||A_alpha||^2 Tr F. Split a Hermitian F into its positive and
negative parts; the same bound holds for its trace norm. The weighted
anticommutator is bounded by the same constant. Hence

    ||L_Davies||_1<=2 sum_alpha g_alpha||A_alpha||^2.  (7)

This holds for **any H**, even with exponentially many Bohr frequencies;
there is no factor for their number. It is Chesi Proposition1 with
explicit nonunit operator norms. Uniform bath intensity

    r_bath=sup_q sum_{alpha:q in supp A_alpha}
                     g_alpha||A_alpha||^2

gives ||L||_1<=2N r_bath for nonempty physical supports. Scalar coupling
parts give no dissipator and can be removed. Independent channels are
an exact interface. For correlated channels one instead needs a bound
on the positive rate matrices, not an uncharged entrywise claim.

If [H,P]=[H,O]=0, then

    [A_alpha(t),O]P=e^(iHt)[A_alpha,O]P e^(-iHt).

Thus bare-coupling protection passes exactly to each spectral component
and then to linear channel combinations. Their geometrical supports
need not be estimated at this step. For commuting local H one can also
bound that support by the checks touching A, but it is not needed for
(7) or this protection transfer. KMS gives Gibbs stationarity for the
standard Hermitian-channel Davies construction; it does not supply
the protection or full-spectrum logical factorization.

## Larger support is an erosion problem, not automatic multiplication

Fix a deterministic syndrome decoder C(s) and its single-Pauli good
set G1. Both decoder-dressed logicals are protected there. If E is a
product of m single-site Pauli factors, starting at s is safe only if
every pre-factor syndrome lies in G1. Define a safe set G_q by requiring

    s+s_Q in G1 for every admitted Pauli prefix Q,
    prefix length at most q-1.                       (8)

Then every Pauli of support size at most q in the admitted support
family is protected on G_q, by inductively applying the single-factor
commutators along its syndrome path. The final syndrome need not be
in G1. The same set protects arbitrary non-Pauli A on such a support:
expand A in its Pauli basis and use exact linearity. No Pauli
coefficient l1 factor is needed for exact protection or the rate
bound (7).

Protecting all single-site errors on P does not alone protect their
products. For a distance-three code take a weight-three logical
L=E1 E2 E3 and a decoder correcting each single factor. On the code
projector all three singles are protected. E1 E2 has the same syndrome
as E3, so its correction is E3 up to a stabilizer and its decoded
action is the nontrivial logical L. This algebraic counterexample
refutes the unqualified product implication; it does not refute the
actual source after the safe set is enlarged as in (8).

Let M_prefix be the number of admitted distinct prefix Pauli shifts,
including identity, and let a valid shift Q change syndrome energy by
at most E_flip |Q|. Syndrome shifts are bijections on the valid
syndrome image. Gibbs change of variables gives

    mu_beta(G_q^c)
        <=M_prefix exp(beta E_flip(q-1)) mu_beta(G1^c). (9)

For the normalized H0=sum_a (I-C_a)/2, one may take
E_flip=max_qubit sum_{a:q in supp C_a} penalty_a.
For unit penalties E_flip<=7 in the actual reachable family. For the
source's H0=-sum C_a, excitation energies are doubled and the same
physical-beta formula has E_flip<=14. This normalization must not be
lost when the source writes a syndrome weight |s|.

Crude global inventory for all weight-q jumps is
M_prefix<=sum_{j=0}^{q-1} 3^j binomial(N,j). For physically local
support sets one can instead choose input support anchors q0 and
balls covering every support. If each anchor ball has at most v_R
qubits, taking all Paulis in those N balls gives
M_prefix<=N 4^(v_R). The choice charges density/range and avoids
assuming a bound on the number of tiny duplicate bath channels.
For fixed range, q, v_R and beta this loses only a fixed factor and
a power of N; a stretched-exponential source tail survives.

For q growing with N, the honest sufficient condition is

    log M_prefix + beta E_flip(q-1) = o(source barrier exponent).

Support merely smaller than code distance does not by itself imply
this quantitative Gibbs statement. Local filling/distance can certify
that a *closed* local correction difference is a stabilizer, but
cannot replace the thermal probability control in (9).

## Finite-depth dressing: exact positive interface and its boundary

For H'=U H U*, with U of fixed gate depth d, arity a and physical
range rho, fixed physical bath operators S_alpha pull back to
A_alpha=U* S_alpha U. Their norms are unchanged, their weight is at
most a^d times the old weight, and their physical range increases by
at most the fixed light-cone distance. Apply (8)–(9) to that enlarged
family. O'=U O U*,P'=U P U*,Enc'=Ad_U Enc and
Dec'=Dec Ad_U* give exact full-spectrum commutation and Gibbs
conjugation. Davies frequencies are recomputed for H', but spectral
components pull back exactly to those of H. Thus this fixed-bath
finite-depth **isospectral** transfer is certified when the erosion
tail and bounded intensity hypotheses are paid.

A ground-band quasi-local dressing from a spectral stability theorem
does not give H'=U H U* on the full spectrum. It supplies neither
exact [H',O']=0 throughout Gibbs support nor the conjugated thermal
auxiliary state. Small low-band width/distance alone therefore cannot
license (4) at positive temperature. An approximate version would
need explicit Gibbs-weighted commutator and stationary-state errors,
uniform at the claimed exponentially long times.
