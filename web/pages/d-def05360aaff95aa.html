## Exact interface

For a supplied integer m ≥ 1, a < b, and a supplied arbitrary function E : [a,b]^m → (0,∞), the conclusion is existence of one finite, nonempty family of labels (r,b_r) with r ∈ [a,b]^m independent of h and b_r(h) = o(h^a). For

`F_h(x) = min_r (r · x + b_r(h))`, `X_h(p) = (h^{p_1},...,h^{p_m})`,

the sequential conclusion is: if h_n → 0, p^n → p ∈ [a,b]^m, and the same label is minimizing at X_{h_n}(p^n) for every n, then ||p-r||∞ < E(r). The p^n may be outside the cube. Every tied minimizer counts. The family is fixed before the eventual uniform h bound is chosen. The uniform corollary concerns all p inside the cube, all active labels, and all sufficiently small h.

E is evaluated at the fixed output slope r, not at the moving point p^n. This distinction is load-bearing. Neither a positive global infimum of E nor continuity, measurability, or a modulus of E is assumed or used.

## Reconstruction of the decisive argument

Write μ(p) = min_i p_i. Induct on dimension. At each fixed t ∈ [a,b], construct a finite local family containing the baseline (t1,0), all of whose slopes have minimum t. Type k labels raise exactly k coordinates above t, for k = 0,...,m−1. The base local family is a singleton when m=1 or t=b.

Choose β_k ∈ (t,b) strictly decreasing with k and so close to t that β_k−t < E(r) for every already constructed type k−1 slope r. This uses finitely many positive E values. For each k-coordinate subset U, apply the lower-dimensional lemma on [β_k,b]^U with tolerance s ↦ E(r(s)), where r(s) is s on U and t elsewhere. Append the local offset −h^{β_k}. Thus a type k label has offset −h^{β_k}+d(h), with d=o(h^{β_k}).

Now assume a type k label stays active along h_n → 0 and p^n → p with μ(p)=t.

1. Comparison with the baseline gives

   `Σ_{i∈U} (r_i−t) h^{p_i^n} ≤ (1+o(1)) h^{β_k}`.

   Every coefficient is bounded below by β_k−t>0. If any limiting p_i<β_k, that term/h^{β_k} tends to infinity. Hence p_i≥β_k on U. No claim about the ratio is made at equality.

2. Comparing only with labels having this same U cancels every off-U term and the appended −h^{β_k}. The original lower-dimensional label is therefore active in its inductive family. Its limiting exponent vector lies in the inductive cube, even if the approximating vectors approached its faces from outside. Induction gives the strict error bound on U.

3. If k<m−1 and an off-U coordinate p_j exceeds β_{k+1}, take any type k+1 competitor on U∪{j}. All those limiting exponents strictly exceed β_{k+1}: the existing U coordinates are at least β_k>β_{k+1}, and the added coordinate was chosen strictly above β_{k+1}. Its difference from the baseline is −h^{β_{k+1}}+o(h^{β_{k+1}}). The supposed active type k difference is at least −h^{β_k}+o(h^{β_k}) (or exactly zero for k=0). Since β_k>β_{k+1}, the competitor is strictly smaller. Thus every off-U p_i≤β_{k+1}, and its error from t is ≤β_{k+1}−t<E(r). When k=m−1, the sole unraised coordinate has limiting exponent exactly t, because μ(p)=t and all raised coordinates exceed t.

This proves a strict bound at every local limiting active point with μ(p)=t, without making a strict comparison at an equal limiting exponent.

For each fixed local label, let A_r be the subset of the cube consisting of all its asymptotic active limits, using actual active sequences with unrestricted p^n ∈ R^m. A_r is closed: given z_n ∈ A_r → z, choose a realizing active point u_n with h_n<1/n and ||u_n−z_n||∞<1/n. Then u_n→z realizes z∈A_r. This proof needs no continuity of the offsets.

The bad set

`B_t = union over local labels r of {p∈A_r : ||p−r||∞ ≥ E(r)}`

is compact. For each label E(r) is one constant; the union is finite. By the preceding fixed-minimum proof, no point of B_t has μ(p)=t. Therefore `min_{p∈B_t}|μ(p)−t| > 0` when B_t is nonempty. Choose a positive ρ_t smaller than this number. The family is valid whenever the limiting minimum lies in I_t=(t−ρ_t,t+ρ_t). Shrink ρ_t further so t+ρ_t<min β_k for a nonsingleton family. This ensures every local offset c=o(h^{t+ρ_t}). A singleton has zero offset and needs no exponent restriction.

Select a finite subcover of [a,b] by the intervals I_t, discard contained intervals, and order surviving centers t_1<...<t_N. The endpoints of the surviving intervals are strictly ordered in the same direction. Consecutive intervals overlap, since otherwise the closed gap between their endpoints would contain an uncovered point inside [a,b]. Choose

`q_j ∈ (max(t_j,L_{j+1}), min(t_{j+1},R_j))`.

The interval is nonempty by the overlap and the two intervals containing their own centers. With q_0=a and q_N=b, each closed range [q_{j−1},q_j] lies strictly inside I_{t_j}.

Shift every label in local family j by the common offset `−Σ_{ℓ<j} h^{q_ℓ}`. These shifts preserve activity inside each local family. If a globally active label comes from family j, comparison with the next baseline rules out μ(p)>q_j: its value minus the shifted next baseline is `h^{q_j}+o(h^{q_j})>0`. Comparison with the previous baseline rules out μ(p)<q_{j−1}: all coefficients of the slope difference are ≥t_j−t_{j−1}>0, one coordinate dominates h^{q_{j−1}}, and the local offset is o(h^{q_{j−1}}). Therefore its limiting minimum belongs to [q_{j−1},q_j], which is inside the local validity interval. This proves the lemma.

Finally, failure of the uniform corollary would give h_n→0, p^n in the cube, and violating active labels. Finiteness freezes the label on a subsequence and compactness supplies a limiting p in the cube. The nonstrict violating inequality passes to the limit with the fixed number E(r), contradicting the lemma's strict conclusion.

## Specific adversarial points and outcomes

| Attack | Outcome and exact reason |
|---|---|
| Arbitrarily small E values and inf E=0 | The β choice uses a finite existing set of slopes. The nearby-minimum step uses a finite set of fixed E(r) values. A global positive infimum is never inferred. |
| E discontinuous at every point | No passage E(r_n)→E(r) occurs. Labels are frozen before limit arguments. |
| p^n outside the cube, including approach to p_i=β_k or p_i=b | The inductive interface explicitly permits this. Only the limiting vector must be in the inductive cube. |
| Equality p_i=β_k | The baseline comparison proves only ≥. The lower-dimensional lemma handles equality. The competitor comparison uses β_k>β_{k+1}, so it has a strict gap anyway. |
| Equality p_i=β_{k+1} off U | The desired bound is ≤β_{k+1}−t<E(r), so equality is harmless. |
| Noncontinuous or oscillatory offsets | Closedness of A_r uses actual active points and diagonal selection, not continuity in h. The constructed offsets can in fact be continuous finite sums. |
| Strict error bounds lost under compactness | The compact set is the closed set of violations `error≥E(r)`. It excludes μ=t completely. This is the correct direction for the strict conclusion. |
| Ties inside a local family or between families | Every minimizing label is compared independently. At μ=q_j, either neighboring family is valid because the cut is strictly inside both open validity intervals. |
| Nonadjacent family domination | Only adjacent baseline competitors are required to exclude an active label outside its assigned range. No unproved nearest-family assumption is used. |
| Signs of slope differences at the lower cut | Every local slope coordinate is ≥t_j, so comparison with t_{j−1}1 has a uniform positive coefficient gap. Cancellation is impossible. |
| Offset asymptotics near a cut | q_j<R_j=t_j+ρ_j for the upper comparison, and q_{j−1}<t_j for the lower comparison, so local offsets are little-o of the cut scale. |
| Cover pruning creates a gap | Removing only contained intervals preserves the cover. Strict endpoint ordering plus centers inside [a,b] proves each remaining adjacent overlap. |

No decisive falsification survived these attacks.

## A stronger offset statement

The construction proves more than stated. For every label, one may choose

`b_r(h) = −Σ_{ℓ=1}^{M_r} h^{γ_{r,ℓ}}`, with every γ_{r,ℓ}>a,

allowing M_r=0. Repeated exponents can instead be collected with positive integer coefficients. Proof: the one-dimensional local offsets are zero; a local raised-coordinate construction appends one −h^{β_k}, with β_k>t≥a; its inductive offsets have exponents strictly above β_k; and global gluing appends −h^{q_j}, with q_j>t_j≥a. This strengthening is preserved through the full dimension induction.

Thus all offsets may be nonpositive, smooth for 0<h<1, and defined on the entire interval 0<h<1. Their little-o bound follows termwise. The lemma's robustness to arbitrary offset continuity is not an essential ingredient of the construction.

## Hostile tolerance examples

The network tolerance is a particularly meaningful stress test:

`E(r)=min({1/2} union {|r·v|/(2||v||_1) : v∈D, r·v≠0})`.

It has arbitrarily small values near comparison hyperplanes and jumps to positive values on them. For an active slope, `|(p−r)·v| < |r·v|/2` whenever r·v≠0. If p·v=0, this forces r·v=0. Consequently the lemma really does supply slopes exactly on the appropriate hyperplanes when such points require them; replacing this E with a continuous positive lower-bound tolerance would lose that property. This is a valid consequence, not a contradiction.

A simple hostile one-dimensional tolerance is E(0)=1/2 and E(r)=|r|/2 for r≠0 on [−1,1]. Positive slope neighborhoods shrink geometrically toward zero, but the slope r=0 covers a whole neighborhood of zero. For example the slopes −1, −0.6, 0, 0.6, 1 with limiting cuts −0.75, −0.4, 0.4, 0.75 have their entire closed limiting activity ranges strictly inside the corresponding tolerance intervals. The offsets `−Σ_{ℓ<j}h^{q_ℓ}` realize these limiting ranges. Finite-h transition exponents move by O(1/|log h|), so the strict margins give the uniform estimate for sufficiently small h. This illustrates why an attempted counterexample based only on shrinking E near a jump fails.

Other attempted pathologies included E made tiny away from an arbitrary closed exceptional set Σ and large on Σ. Such E can force active slopes to lie exactly on Σ at points in Σ, but compactness still permits finitely many neighborhoods centered at points of Σ. Dense exceptional sets do not create an immediate obstruction: positivity prevents a radius from being bounded above by distance to a dense set unless it is zero.

## Downstream use and remaining scope

The affine lemma is independent of any reaction network assumption. Its application in `trapping.tex` is mathematically compatible with the exact interface: a finite comparison-direction set is supplied; E is positive everywhere by omitting zero products; the slope family is fixed; then the nonzero comparison magnitudes have one positive minimum H. The sign and monomial-gap deduction on B_h follows correctly.

The affine lemma alone does **not** prove endotactic flux positivity. That needs a separate domination argument under the exact endotactic definition. The source's proof in `trapping.tex` lines 195–220 invokes a directed return path and therefore only establishes its stated weakly reversible cut-flux conclusion. For the wider endotactic claim, this section cannot be copied unchanged. A different proof can plausibly use a positive reaction at a strictly lower source than the lowest-source negative reaction, but reaction projection magnitudes and upper/lower rate bounds must be included in the domination constant. This is the remaining mathematical load-bearing transfer, not a defect found in the affine construction.

The plateau construction and scale stepping use only the fixed slope family and eventual activity/sign bounds. They do not need h-continuity of offsets, nesting of plateaux, or an effective bound for h. The same affine family is used throughout all sufficiently small scales.

## Supplied versus acquired data and costs

Supplied: m, a, b, the whole positive function E (or a mathematically specified formula for it), and the exact source proof. Acquired in this audit: a complete internal reconstruction, the attack outcomes above, and the stronger finite-power offset statement. No numerical search or formal prover was used as proof evidence.

The existence proof has no usable bound on |Λ|, on bit heights or description complexity of the chosen real slopes/exponents, or on the threshold h_0. It uses compactness twice and selects a finite subcover after considering families for all t. An arbitrary function E has no stated effective representation or evaluation cost, so an efficient construction from finite oracle access is not established.

There cannot be a cardinality bound depending only on m and [a,b], uniformly over positive E. For constant E≡ε, the uniform corollary forces the slope-centered open infinity-norm ε-balls to cover [a,b]^m, because every p has at least one active label. Volume therefore gives

`|Λ| ≥ ((b−a)/(2ε))^m`.

Thus even the elementary approximation interface permits exponentially many slopes in dimension and arbitrarily many as ε decreases. Any practical controller or resource claim must charge the retained slopes, power exponents, offsets, and the precision/smallness needed for the selected scale. This limitation does not invalidate the qualitative permanence argument.

The separate transfer to endotactic systems must be assessed against the exact published interface. [Johnston–Pantea–Donnell, arXiv:1412.4662](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4662) describes general endotactic permanence as conjectural in its 2014 abstract. These sources establish a meaningful prior-art distinction, but do not certify current novelty of the root's wider theorem.
