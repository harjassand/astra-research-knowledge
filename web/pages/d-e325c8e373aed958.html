# Number theory / arithmetic geometry / logic outcome

## Strongest constructive outcome

Conditional on the released family032 algebraic exact Kuga–Satake and ordinary Hodge results, we reconstructed a generalized Hodge theorem for arbitrary mixed products of independent rank-three RM K3 surfaces over one real quadratic field with a common even-Clifford quaternion class.

The new local mechanism is a pairwise auxiliary K3 surface of rational transcendental rank eight. Its fourth exterior power carries a shared-field cross tensor using only one return from H² of an abelian square. Matching highest-weight tokens across the two real embeddings makes the number of returns equal to half the actual Hodge level. Composition with a mixed-K3 ordinary-HC quotient map gives exactly the required support codimension.

This mechanism repairs an exact counterexample to the naive idea that independent self-power GHC automatically extends to mixed products. The conditional proof was independently reconstructed by hodge_escalation. Priority and foundational release correctness are unverified; this is not an unrestricted GHC proof or a historical-calibre claim.

## Exact next frontier

Two independent rank-three RM K3 surfaces over the same real quadratic field but with distinct quaternion Brauer classes are the smallest remaining test. The spin-tensor representation has nonsplit Tits algebra of index two, forcing multiplicity two and h^(2,0)=2 for its E-rational realization; the auxiliary K3 pairing above therefore fails. One must prove an algebraic crossed-spin shuffle/return or another mixed carrier. The source's auxiliary metric tensor and its tensor-group conclusion restricted to the original V_i do not establish that transport.

## Analytic-number-theory negative result

`CHOWLA_MULTILINEAR_GATE.md` gives an exact quadratic-phase counterexample: each factor passes the released averaged fixed-frequency short Fourier input at strength D^(−1/2), but their three-point rough-divisor average remains ≍B^(−1+ε), whereas the proposed centering extension needs B^(−1−ε). The example is not multiplicative; it refutes the sufficiency of the available Fourier input, not Chowla. A centered divisibility hypergraph estimate is also absent.
