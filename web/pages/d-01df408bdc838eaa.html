# C7: every feasible EB comparator has a canonical POVM majorant

## Claim and scope

Work on `$M_d$` with normalized trace `$τ=\operatorname{Tr}/d$` and the real Hilbert space of Hermitian matrices. Let `$Φ$` be unital, trace-preserving, and Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, so `$K=2Φ-I$` is self-adjoint and `$K(I)=I$`. For an EB channel `$Λ$`, interpret `$Λ\succeq K$` either as Loewner order (which requires the difference to be self-adjoint), or more weakly as the quadratic-form inequality

\[
  \langle A,\Lambda(A)\rangle_\tau\ge \langle A,K(A)\rangle_\tau
  \quad\text{for every Hermitian }A.
\tag{1}
\]

Then (1) holds for some trace-preserving EB channel if and only if it holds for a **canonical** EB channel generated by one POVM. In particular, the canonical POVM support gate used in the C7 moment-cone note is necessary and sufficient for the original unrestricted-EB factor-two problem. This removes the possible gap between a canonical-support deficit and a counterexample against all EB comparators.

The result is an exact finite-dimensional algebraic lemma. It neither proves the universal factor-two inequality nor supplies a counterexample.

## Proof

For a POVM `$M=(M_y)_y$`, define its canonical channel in Schrödinger form by

\[
  \Gamma_M(X)=\sum_y \operatorname{Tr}(M_yX)\,
                 \frac{M_y}{\operatorname{Tr}M_y},
\tag{2}
\]

omitting zero effects. This is the same-effect/same-preparation measure-and-prepare channel. It is trace-preserving, unital, and self-adjoint in normalized Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.

Suppose a trace-preserving EB channel `$Λ$` satisfies (1). Its Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint `$Λ^*$` is unital because `$Λ$` is trace-preserving. Set

\[
  T=\frac{\Lambda+\Lambda^*}{2}.
\]

Since `$K$` is self-adjoint, (1) says `$T-K\succeq0$`. Also

\[
  \langle I,(T-K)I\rangle_\tau
   =\tau(T(I)-I)=0,
\]

because `$K(I)=I$`, `$Λ$` is trace-preserving, and `$Λ^*(I)=I$`. For a positive operator, a zero quadratic form at `$I$` implies `$(T-K)I=0$`; hence `$T(I)=I$`. But `$Λ^*(I)=I$`, so `$T(I)=(\Lambda(I)+I)/2=I$`, and therefore `$Λ(I)=I$`. Thus `$Λ$` is bistochastic, and its adjoint is also a channel.

Use a finite Holevo form for this EB channel:

\[
  \Lambda(X)=\sum_y \operatorname{Tr}(M_yX)\sigma_y,
  \qquad \sum_y M_y=I,\quad \sigma_y\succeq0,\quad
  \operatorname{Tr}\sigma_y=1.
\tag{3}
\]

Unitality gives the second normalization

\[
  \sum_y \operatorname{Tr}(M_y)\sigma_y=I.
\tag{4}
\]

Write `$p_y=\operatorname{Tr}(M_y)/d>0$`, `$ρ_y=M_y/\operatorname{Tr}(M_y)$`, and `$N_y=d p_y\sigma_y=\operatorname{Tr}(M_y)\sigma_y$`. Equations (3)–(4) show that both `$M=(M_y)_y$` and `$N=(N_y)_y$` are POVMs. The canonical channels `$Γ_M$` and `$Γ_N$` prepare `$ρ_y$` and `$σ_y$`, respectively.

In the normalized-τ Hilbert space, define vectors

\[
  u_y=d\sqrt{p_y}\,\rho_y,\qquad
  v_y=d\sqrt{p_y}\,\sigma_y.
\]

Direct substitution into (2)–(3) gives the Hilbert–Schmidt operators

\[
  \Lambda=\sum_y |v_y\rangle\langle u_y|,\quad
  \Lambda^*=\sum_y |u_y\rangle\langle v_y|,\quad
  \Gamma_M=\sum_y |u_y\rangle\langle u_y|,\quad
  \Gamma_N=\sum_y |v_y\rangle\langle v_y|.
\]

Consequently

\[
  \frac{\Gamma_M+\Gamma_N}{2}-T
   =\frac12\sum_y |u_y-v_y\rangle\langle u_y-v_y|\succeq0.
\tag{5}
\]

The channel `$Γ=(\Gamma_M+\Gamma_N)/2$` is itself canonical: it is generated by the single POVM consisting of the effects `$M_y/2$` and `$N_y/2$`, with a retained coin identifying which half was used. Equation (5) implies `$Γ\succeq T\succeq K$`. This proves the nontrivial direction; the reverse follows because canonical maps are EB channels.

If one starts with the standard Loewner formulation `$(\Lambda\succeq K)$`, the same proof applies. If the convention requires `$Λ$` to be Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint from the outset, then `$T=\Lambda$`; the quadratic-form version above is slightly stronger because it permits any TP EB map initially.

## Quantifiers and costs

This is a per-channel existence reduction. It preserves the entire Hermitian operator domain and the exact Loewner constant; the constructed POVM does not depend on a test frame or state family. Given an `$r$`-outcome Holevo form (3), it produces a canonical POVM with at most `$2r$` effects. A finite Holevo form exists in finite dimension by the separable-Choi characterization of EB maps; a Choi-space Carathéodory representation uses at most `$d^4$` product terms, hence at most `$2d^4$` effects after the construction. No efficient optimization, channel acquisition, finite-precision compilation, or hardware cost is claimed.

## Consequence for the all-Q attack

Let the canonical-map set be $\mathcal{C}_d$ and define $S_d(Q)=\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{C}_d}\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Psi)$. The C7 moment-cone report proves

\[
  \exists\Psi\in\mathcal{C}_d:\ \Psi\succeq 2\Phi-I
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  S_d(Q)\ge 2\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Phi)-\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q
  \quad\forall Q\succeq0.
\]

By (5), this is also equivalent to existence of any EB comparator satisfying the factor-two order. Thus any exact `$Q$` and rigorous moment-dual envelope `$Z$` violating that support inequality would refute factor two against **all** EB channels, not just canonical channels. Conversely, proving the all-Q support inequality settles the unrestricted canonical and full-EB comparison.

This bridge does not fix the main unresolved step: the marginal-feasible star/Choi correlations must still be bounded by the pure-state ensemble moment support for every mixed positive `$Q$`. The unconstrained star maximum is invalid when its top state violates Choi marginals. See `eb_moments/REPORT.md` for the exact primal/dual and its qutrit route obstruction, and `markov_form/MARKOV_FORM_GATE.md` for the independent Markov-form attack.
