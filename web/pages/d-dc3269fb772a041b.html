## 1. Why proof reuse is legal, and what it does not permit

The source's Section 2.1.1 defines a proof as a sequence of sequents inferred
from earlier sequents. Only **tree-like** proofs restrict each prior sequent to
one use. Its `l(proof)` is the total number of formula occurrences in the written
sequents, not merely the number of rows and not the number of syntax symbols.

For example, reusing `p => p` twice in right fusion produces
`p,p => p*p`. It does not produce `p => p*p`: the two premise contexts are combined
as a multiset. Conversely, right additive conjunction permits the same context
in its two premises. Reusing a cached proof through this rule is precisely the
permitted additive rule, not contraction.

Store one exact proof endpoint `Pair(A): A^t,A^s => bottom` for every input DAG
node A. Topologically process each node once. For `A=B->C`, identity on `C^s`
and `Pair(B)` give, by left implication,

    B^t,C^s,(C^s->B^s) => bottom.

Left conjunction selects the second conjunct of A^t; left fusion joins B^t
and C^s into A^s. All contexts in these rows have at most three antecedent
occurrences. The cache never supplies an extra hypothesis inside a sequent.

The remaining cases are equally bounded:

* A=B and C: use the two child pairs, select one projection in each branch,
  and left-disjoin the negative component.
* A=B or C: select the corresponding negative projection in each branch and
  left-disjoin the positive component.
* A=B*C: select `C^t->B^s` from A^s; identity on C^t and Pair(B), then left
  fusion, yield the endpoint.
* A=!B: identity on !B^t and bottom, followed by left implication, gives the
  endpoint. No recursive obligation is needed.
* Atoms and constants: bounded constant/identity/unit cases.

Thus Pair cache construction contributes O(V) rows and O(V) **formula
occurrences**, when V is the common syntax-node count. Negation-duality requires
only a constant number of extra rows referring to Pair(A); double negation can
be handled by fixed wrappers. There is no recursive family of new substituted
operands generated by these obligations.

## 3. Independently reconstructed finite Frege skeleton compression

**Lemma.** Fix any finite propositional inference system of fixed-arity formula
schemata, with no uniform-substitution inference rule or other unbounded
syntactic side conditions. If it has an L-line proof of an ordinary formula A,
then it has an L-line proof of that same A whose lines and instantiated schema
operands have a common syntax DAG with O(L+|A|) nodes. New auxiliary atoms may be
replaced by one fresh atom; atoms occurring in A stay rigid.

**Construction and proof.** Freeze the actual rule used at each source line and
its earlier-line references. Introduce a term variable X_i for the unknown
formula at each line i. At every rule use, make a fresh copy of its fixed schema
terms and schematic variables. Equate each copied premise root with its X_j,
and its conclusion root with X_i. Finally equate X_L with the actual rigid term
tree of A. The total number of constructor occurrences and variables is
N=O(L+|A|): every rule is fixed and has bounded size.

The original proof supplies a finite ground solution to these equations.
Form a quotient of this finite term graph by required equalities. When two
constructor nodes become equal, their labels must match and their corresponding
children must become equal. A mismatch is impossible because the original
solution solves the equations. Each quotient class therefore has either a
well-defined constructor and ordered child classes, or no constructor.

The quotient cannot have a directed constructor cycle. Along each constructor
edge the original ground solution's formula height strictly decreases. A cycle
would require a finite ground term to be its own proper subterm. Label each
unconstrained class by one fresh atomic formula. The resulting acyclic graph
has at most N+1 nodes; unfold its X_i roots to define replacement formulas B_i.
The schema equations ensure every B_i is inferred from the specified earlier
B_j by that same schema. The rigid last-root equation ensures B_L=A exactly.

This is a change of the **whole proof certificate** justified by syntactic
unification, not an object-level substitution inference applied to an earlier
theorem. It does not add a logical rule or a contraction principle. It also
does not assume that the original enormous proof formulas can be read in time
polynomial in L: the lower-bound argument needs existence, and the frozen
skeleton itself is a separate compact input.

## 5. Definitional EF: valid elimination and a concrete invalid alternative

An EF definition has the form `(e->F) and (F->e)`, with e absent from F, all
earlier lines/hypotheses, and the final target. Consequently F can use only
previous extension variables: a future extension variable could not be fresh
after already appearing in F. Expand definitions globally in chronological
order. The expansion has a compact syntax DAG by redirecting each e leaf to
the expanded root of F; it can have an enormous ordinary formula tree.

Every original ordinary rule remains a substitution instance after this global
expansion. Every definition becomes `(F_exp->F_exp) and (F_exp->F_exp)`, which
has a fixed number of ordinary Frege lines by identity and derived adjunction.
The final target stays the same. Thus an EF proof of symbol size S yields a
circuit Frege proof with O(S) nodes/steps. Moreover, an EF proof with E lines
yields an ordinary, possibly exponentially expanded Frege proof with O(E)
lines. The established Frege-line lower bound therefore gives both an EF size
lower bound and an EF line lower bound under exactly these definitions.

**Actual obstruction to raw Chu translation.** In the standard affine
residuated algebra on [0,1], put

    x*y=max(0,x+y-1), x->y=min(1,1-x+y),
    x and y=min(x,y), x or y=max(x,y), bottom=0.

The ordinary unit and top are 1; linear 0 and bounded bottom are both 0. The
crisp exponential `!x=1` iff x=1, and 0 otherwise, validates the exponential
rules as well: !x<=x, !x*!x=!x, and !x<=1; for promotion either some bang
antecedent is 0 (so the antecedent product is 0), or every antecedent is 1,
in which case validity of the premise forces its conclusion to be 1 and hence
its bang also to be 1. Fusion/implication satisfy residuation directly from
their displayed formulas, and negation x->0 equals 1-x, so involution holds.
Let p=3/4, F=p*p=1/2, e=1/2. The source definition e equivalent
to F has value 1. But for D=N=bottom,

    e^s=0,
    F^s=(p->0) and (p->0)=1/4,
    (e->F)^t=(e->F^t) and (F^s->e^s)=1 and 3/4=3/4.

Hence the raw translated definition is not valid even when the source
definition holds. Naming a formula atomically discards its negative component.
This refutes a direct extension-translation rule and explains the necessity of
unfolding before Chu compilation. It does **not** refute the expansion proof.
The independent rational calculation and finite term-quotient discriminators
are executable in `work/logic_prior14/cycle14/independent_obstructions.py`;
their results are in `results/logic_prior14/cycle14/independent_checks.json`.
