# Local stabilizer generation under recursive coarse graining

no nested workers. This is an exact conditional mathematical derivation,
not a proof that the new three-dimensional memory has TQO-2.

## Question, importance and primary premises

Balasubramanian, Davydova and Lin, arXiv2605.10943v1 (11 May 2026),
construct geometrically local, bounded-density CSS Hamiltonians with a
stretched-exponential memory lifetime at fixed sufficiently low positive
temperature. Their §1.1 expressly leaves TQO-2 and arbitrary-local-
Hamiltonian spectral stability for later work. Code distance supplies
TQO-1. The memory theorem uses thermal encoding; neither a ground-space
initialization theorem nor efficient passive thermal encoding is inferred.

This lead asks whether local generator support can be transferred through
their recursive replacement/coarse-graining maps, closing an actual
premise for robustness under implementation errors. We must establish
the maps' supports, a small-region anchor and the required geometric
collar. A first-cohomology isomorphism alone does not supply them.

## Exact stabilizer criterion (prior, reconstructed)

Let S be a signed abelian Pauli stabilizer group with -I absent, generated
by commuting local checks. For A inside B, write S(A) for all global
stabilizers supported entirely in A and S_B for the group generated by
checks supported entirely in B. Let P and P_B be the global and local
ground-space projectors, respectively.

The normalized reduced states on A are proportional to the Pauli sums
over S(A) and S_B(A). They are uniform states on their respective
stabilizer subspaces. Therefore their kernels agree exactly iff

    S(A) = S_B(A).

One direction follows immediately from the same subgroup. Conversely,
equal kernels imply equal normalized support projectors; Pauli
orthogonality identifies their complete signed subgroup coefficients.
Since S_B is a subgroup of S, it suffices to show every global stabilizer
supported in A is generated by checks within B. This criterion is prior
in Bravyi–Hastings–Michalakis, arXiv1001.0344, §2.3; it is not a new
definition or novelty claim.

For CSS codes this splits into two exact binary filling problems. In the
paper's cochain convention, X stabilizers are im δ0 and Z stabilizers
are im(δ1)^T. A mixed X/Z stabilizer has support equal to the union of
its two component supports, so there is no hidden cancellation outside A.
For either sector, every global boundary e supported in A must admit a
generator coefficient vector b with e=δb whose selected generators are
supported in B. The support of a generator means its whole physical
check support, not just a check's chosen center point.

## One-level support transfer lemma (our conditional deduction)

Consider fine and coarse binary cochain complexes with incoming maps δ0
and next maps δ1. Suppose maps P,I and a qubit-to-check map K satisfy

    P1 δ0_f = δ0_c P0,     δ0_f I0 = I1 δ0_c,
    e + I1 P1 e = δ0_f K e whenever δ1_f e=0.

The last identity is the degree-one part of a controlled deformation
retract; a complete equivalence in every degree is unnecessary. In
particular it may coexist with extra H0/H2 metacheck spaces. All maps
must be explicit and linear. No error-decoder nonlinear choice is silently
substituted for them.

Assume physical coordinate maps between levels have dilation λ>1, and
for every fine e supported in B(x,r):

* P1 e is supported in the coarse ball B(x/λ,r/λ+a).
* K e uses fine checks whose whole support lies in B(x,r+b).
* I0 sends coarse generator support B(x/λ,s) to fine whole-check
  support B(x,λs+b).

Here a,b are uniform constants after fixing construction parameters;
they may depend on its fixed geometric dilation, but not the recursion
depth. Let f_i(r) bound the radius needed to generate any global
boundary supported in a radius-r ball at level i. Then

    f_(i+1)(r) <= max{r+b, λ f_i(r/λ+a)+b}.              (R)

Proof: write e=δ0_f u globally. Then P1 e=δ0_c P0 u is a coarse global
boundary and admits a filling v of radius f_i(r/λ+a). The identity gives

    e = δ0_f [K e + I0 v].

The two summands have exactly the radii in (R). No bound on the unknown
global u was used. Apply the same proof to the dual chain complex for
the Z sector. A one-sector proof is insufficient for TQO-2.

## Explicit missing anchor and recurrence closure

After j projections the boundary radius is exactly bounded by

    r_j = r/λ^j + [aλ/(λ-1)](1-λ^(-j)).

The fixed support floor r_*=aλ/(λ-1) does not vanish with recursion.
It is therefore not legitimate to claim that a small boundary eventually
becomes empty. Blindly recursing to the finite seed gives a filling radius
of order λ^i, the complete system scale, even for a fixed small region.
That estimate does not close a local-generation theorem.

A sufficient additional premise is a uniform small-ball anchor:
at every level, all global boundaries supported in balls of radius
r_*+1 have fillings of whole-check radius at most M, where M is a
constant independent of the level. Also let the finite seed satisfy
f_0(s)<=s+M, which is elementary from its fixed diameter.

Stop after j=ceil(log_λ max{r,1}) projections, or at the seed if reached
first. In the first case r_j<=r_*+1. Each injection expands a radius by
λ and adds b. Unrolling (R) gives the maximum of the lifted anchor

    λ^j M + b(λ^j-1)/(λ-1)

and intermediate local homotopy terms

    λ^s(r_s+b) + b(λ^s-1)/(λ-1),   0<=s<j.

For r>=1, λ^j<λr except at the harmless r=1 endpoint. Substituting r_s
shows all terms are at most C r for a constant depending only on
λ,a,b,M. For example

    C = 2 + λ[M+b+(aλ+2b)/(λ-1)]

is safe. The seed-first case uses f_0(r_j)<=r_j+M and the same estimate.
Thus the stated premises establish proportional-ball local generation
uniformly through arbitrarily many levels.

## Canonical TQO-2 collar remains a separate issue

The conclusion just proved generates a radius-r stabilizer inside a
radius-Cr ball. It is not automatically the one-unit-neighborhood TQO-2
condition in BHM §2.2. In particular it gives neither a constant-thickness
collar nor the full stability theorem by merely citing its name. One
must sharpen the support localization, or independently adapt a stability
proof/appropriate local-topological-order theorem to this collar.

The new paper's H1-isomorphism proofs and syndrome-local-computability
definitions do not by themselves assert our three geometric map bounds,
the uniform anchor, or the required collar. Its finite patch homotopies
and nilpotent gluing perturbation are promising inputs to the one-level
map bounds; inspecting them is the next substantive step. A proof of
the actual anchor is likely to require local patch/relative exactness,
not only global code distance.

## Prior, controls, acquisition and falsifiable next step

BHM already isolates TQO-2 and gives stabilizer criteria plus an unstable
toric-code modification. A redundant local check can be omitted without
changing a global code yet destroy local generator completeness: this
will be reconstructed as a control before adopting an inference from
distance/H1-equivalence. No novelty is asserted for that obstruction.

The actual memory construction, not an arbitrary synthetic complex, is
the target. Input acquisition is a public versioned source/geometry
description; constructing its code and finite patch matrices costs at
least their size. Its enormous fixed random-embedding constants and
thermal-encoding premise must be charged rather than hidden behind an
existence proof. We have run no physical-memory or finite-code simulation.

Next: explicitly obtain P,I,K for each local replacement block, verify
their whole-check geometric supports, and decide whether a level-uniform
anchor holds. A valid failure of the anchor in an actual recursively
reachable patch would change the decision; failure on an unrelated
complex would only refute an unjustified transfer principle.
