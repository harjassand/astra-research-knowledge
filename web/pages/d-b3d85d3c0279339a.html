interpreted as the completed ordered port-pair strand, or with a fixed compatible
embedding that uses the SAME arc for a given port pair in two-crossing and
checkerboard faces. This interpretation agrees with the sign-only algorithm
and code. The proof does not condition on unused loops, a checkerboard flag,
or a magnitude-dependent affine zero contour. A richer observation would
require a new conditional-kernel proof and is not covered by this verdict.

primal components on a geometric side, and additional negative-side sign
queries. Conditioning on ALL negative-side heights, rather than just a path

No counterexample to the origin's one-sided finite theorem was found. The
independent path-only-factorization and ordinary-center controls remain valid
and preserved; they do not falsify the explicitly scoped origin theorem.

2. DOMAIN AND CONTOUR OBSERVATION

Origin lines 22--33 specify the 1-skeleton of a finite square-face cellulation
of a disk with simple boundary, complementary +/-1/2 boundary arcs, exactly
two transition edges, and uniform continuous NN-Lipschitz measure. The parent
gate and independent baseline restrict to rectangles. That restriction is
fully covered by the origin's statement.

The argument also extends to the origin's square-face disk domain: its used
ports have degree two in the interior and degree one precisely at the two
marked boundary edges; the original graph is connected; the embedded region
is a disk with simple boundary; each original edge has one or two incident
square faces. These are all the domain properties used in the proof. Free
heights all zero still make all free-incident constraints strict, and graph
distance to the pinned boundary bounds the polytope. No edge through an
excluded part of the plane or an extra diagonal constraint is being added.

Origin lines 35--43 and 56--94 specify negative-corner checkerboard pairing
and the sign-only p,q,r,s decision tree. This is the independent baseline's
rule with the orientation reversed: the origin puts positive on the right,
the baseline names it on the left. The unparametrized resolved strand and
its two geometric vertex sides are unchanged.

The finite Gamma variable must be the ordered crossed edges and face port
pairings, as used by the origin's sign-only successor code. Equivalently choose
the same fixed geometric chord for any given port pair whether that chord
comes from two or four crossings in that face. This convention is explicit
in the independent baseline Section 2 and is essential for exact locality.

Origin lines 36--38 describe joining two-crossing arcs and drawing checkerboard
pairs as small corner arcs. Read these as a topological drawing convention,
not a separate observable that discloses whether an unused checkerboard arc
exists. If the literal drawing instead uses a recognizably different shape

The discrete path interpretation used by the actual rule needs no such extra

3. LOCAL RULE, GLOBAL SIMPLE CURVE, AND ORDINARY DUAL CENTERS

Origin lines 58--94 agree with the independent local table. At an incoming
edge with p positive and q negative, r opposite p and s opposite q:
  r positive -> qr, with no query of s;
  r negative, s positive -> rs;
  r negative, s negative -> sp, with the extra uncut r negative.
The extra negative r is on the negative geometric side. In the last branch
the uncut edge qr connects it to the negative cut endpoint q. In a checkerboard
the two successor pairings cut off the negative corners and are disjoint.

Origin lines 97--105 provide the finite degree argument for the unique open
component. Every used interior port has degree two; only the two boundary
marks have degree one. The pairings are bijective and noncrossing. Consequently
the open component is simple after resolution; all other components are loops.
Neither visited-face simplicity nor ordinary dual-center simplicity follows.

Origin lines 110--122 and 238--250 explicitly acknowledge multiple primal
components and repeated ordinary centers. They do not make the strengthened
claim falsified by the independent 3x3 control. Independent enumeration found
201 such center-revisit samples among the declared 786 configurations, but the
general simple-resolved-curve proof is the degree/noncrossing proof above.

The independent baseline's Jordan argument yields the exact equality
  CUT(gamma) = all original NN edges joining V_+(gamma) to V_-(gamma).
Each uncut original edge lies within one side. Each cut edge crosses the
proper simple arc exactly once, and the two boundary transition edges join
the two boundary arcs. This excludes routes around the marked endpoints.
It also proves that each positive cut endpoint has an opposite-sign original
NN partner, as stated in origin lines 124--128.

4. COMPLETED-PATH EVENT: NECESSITY AND SUFFICIENCY

Origin lines 130--138 identify the queried signs and omit unvisited loops.
The independent baseline supplied the stronger explicit equivalence, now
usable as the precise interface contract:
  {Gamma=gamma} = {h>0 on C_+(gamma),
                  h<0 on C_-(gamma) union T_-(gamma)}.             (4.1)
Here C_+ and C_- are the geometric positive/negative cut endpoints. T_- contains
the opposite fourth corner whenever a gamma passage turns around a positive
common corner. It is contained in the negative geometric side and can overlap
C_-. No positive-side vertex outside C_+ belongs to this sign specification.

The only single-passage face with an unspecified fourth sign is a turn around
a negative corner. Its three cut-corner signs are +,-,+; a positive fourth
corner gives the same turn with two crossings, and a negative fourth corner
gives the same turn under checkerboard negative-corner pairing. In the latter
case its unused second arc is unobserved. A turn around a positive corner has
cut signs -,+,- and forces the fourth sign negative. A straight passage and
a double visit use all four corners as cut endpoints. These cases exhaust
all possible gamma passages.

These tests are not merely necessary. Any sign field meeting (4.1) preserves
every pairing used along gamma. Each gamma port already has its full degree
within this open component, so changed unobserved arcs cannot attach to it.
The same two marks remain the only degree-one ports. Hence the completed
open strand is exactly gamma. This establishes path-only locality without
equating a completed path observation to the full exploration history or
the complete level-loop configuration.

Origin line 136 literally says no sign of a free vertex in a right-side
component is revealed. The next line expressly retains positive cut sign
walls, so the intended statement is no sign of an UNCUT free interior vertex.
Cut positives are often themselves free variables and their positive signs
are revealed. This wording must not be used as a claim about all free vertices;
the exact statement (4.1) eliminates the ambiguity. No proof step in the
displayed upper-half argument needs the broader literal reading.

5. DLR: ALL NEGATIVE HEIGHTS VERSUS PATH OR WALL HEIGHTS ONLY

Origin lines 142--162 correctly condition on gamma AND all left/negative-side
heights. For almost every such height fiber, negative tests in (4.1) are fixed
and the only remaining positive-coordinate sign tests are those on C_+.
For a free p in C_+ and fixed negative partners q,
  alpha_p = min_{q:pq in CUT}(1+h_q) <= 1,
  0 <= h_p <= alpha_p.                                           (5.1)
The original lower edge bounds h_q-1<0 are redundant. Positive-volume fibers
have alpha_p>0. Cut inequalities at pinned positive endpoints are constants.
Internal original NN edges and original positive pins +1/2 remain in place.

The density is exactly the product of those internal-edge indicators and
cut-wall indicators. It factors over positive free components, with pinned
values treated as fixed incident boundary data. Each free component has a
pin anchor or cut-wall anchor; otherwise it would have no original edge to
its complement in the connected pinned graph. An isolated positive component
in a checkerboard resolution is therefore covered, not discarded.

No positive-negative product given only gamma is claimed in origin lines
142--162. It would be false: the independent Section 7A exhibits two open
positive-volume events at opposite cut endpoints, each with positive marginal
probability but whose joint is excluded by the original unit edge constraint.
That control remains an essential guard against dropping the outside-height
conditioning when reusing this theorem.

If one instead freezes only the full cut heights C_+ union C_-, the extra
negative tests on T_- may still constrain negative-side free vertices. Exact
factorization then includes those local sign walls. Freezing also T_- heights
gives ordinary sign-free pinned Gibbs kernels over all remaining components.
The origin's one-sided proof does not make that stronger cut-height-only
ordinary-kernel assertion. Its use of ALL negative heights correctly removes
this issue on the positive side.

6. WALL COMPARISON, POSITIVE VOLUME, AND STATIONARY LAW

Origin lines 151--159 explicitly keep the cut lower endpoint 0 and raise only
its upper ceiling from alpha_p to 1. This is the required ordered comparison;
it does not rely on the unsafe generic statement that arbitrary wall enlargement
is monotone. No [0,1] wall is introduced at an uncut bulk vertex.

The reference component has cut walls [0,1], pins +1/2, and unchanged NN edges.
It is invariant under simultaneous h->1-h, so each coordinate has mean 1/2.
The constant-c interior test at origin lines 174--177 is valid on a positive
fiber: choose 0<c<min(1/2, all free alpha_p). Every cut wall and every internal
or free-pin edge inequality is then strict. If there is no free cut wall,
c=1/4 works. A component with no free vertices is trivial.

The one-site endpoint order at origin lines 166--174 is also correct. The
passage to invariant laws at lines 178--180 compresses the ergodicity argument.
Positive interior alone is not a sufficient general theorem about arbitrary
convex heat-bath state spaces. For this NN problem a full justification was
independently given in frozen baseline Section 5: use mesh 1/N with N even,
round the ordered interval walls, and couple random-site updates by the same
uniform quantile. The coordinatewise minimum feasible lattice state exists.
A blocked downward step propagates to a free neighbor exactly one unit higher;
following such blockers cannot cycle in a finite graph. Thus every state
reaches that minimum by allowed moves. The finite chains are irreducible and
aperiodic with uniform invariant laws. Couple from the same old feasible state,
pass to stationary marginals, and then use bounded-polytope Riemann sums to
obtain the continuous expectation comparison. This supplies the analytic
completion without assuming an unproved continuous boundary-start property.

It follows for almost every fixed negative fiber that E[h_x|gamma,z]<=1/2
at every positive-side coordinate. Integrating those heights, as in origin
lines 181--183, gives E[h_x|gamma]<=1/2. Fixing positive cut coordinates instead
and trying to bound their fixed values individually would not justify this
argument; they remain part of the compared positive kernel.
