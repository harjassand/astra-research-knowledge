N03 TURBULENT TRANSPORT — FROZEN RECORD v1
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: internally derived exact shear result; finite-time numerical check; no claim of turbulent universality or novelty priority.

TARGET

Find a quantitative way to connect vanishing molecular diffusivity, Lagrangian pair randomness, and macroscopic scalar dissipation while keeping scalar geometry explicit. A second target is to ask what fixed kinetic-energy and kinetic-dissipation budgets force in a tractable Navier–Stokes family. The result below is exact for periodic incompressible shears. It does not solve the fixed large-scale velocity-forcing problem.

1. EXACT FORCED NAVIER–STOKES SHEAR FAMILY

Work on the normalized flat torus T², with spatial average denoted by angle brackets. Let

    u_n(x,y) = (U_n(y), 0),     U_n(y) = A sin(n y),     n in N.

This field is divergence-free and stationary. Its nonlinear term vanishes, u_n·∇u_n=0. It is an exact solution of forced incompressible Navier–Stokes with pressure zero and body force

    f_n = -nu_n Δu_n = nu_n n² u_n.

The mean kinetic energy and viscous dissipation per unit mass are

    E = (1/2)<|u_n|²> = A²/4,
    epsilon = nu_n<|∇u_n|²> = nu_n A² n²/2 = 2 nu_n E n².

The power input <f_n·u_n> equals epsilon. Thus, for any prescribed positive E and epsilon, choose A=2√E and nu_n=epsilon/(2 E n²). With fixed Prandtl number Pr=nu_n/kappa_n, set kappa_n=nu_n/Pr. Then E, epsilon, and Pr are exactly independent of n, while nu_n,kappa_n tend to zero and

    alpha := kappa_n n² = epsilon/(2 E Pr)

is fixed. The forcing amplitude is fixed, but its wavenumber is n and therefore moves to small spatial scales.

2. EXACT FINITE-TIME SCALAR ANOMALY IN THIS SEQUENCE

Take an unforced passive scalar with fixed large-scale initial datum theta_n(x,y,0)=cos(kx), where k is a fixed positive integer. The advection-diffusion equation is

    ∂t theta_n + U_n(y) ∂x theta_n = kappa_n Δtheta_n.

Writing theta_n=Re(e^{ikx} Phi_n(y,t)), then changing variable z=n y gives the exact reduction

    Phi_n(y,t) = exp(-kappa_n k² t) Psi(z,t),
    ∂t Psi = alpha ∂zz Psi - i q sin(z) Psi,
    Psi(z,0)=1,                    q := k A.

The cell equation is independent of n. The scalar energy and cumulative molecular dissipation per unit area are

    E_theta,n(t) := (1/2)<theta_n(t)²>
                  = (1/4) exp(-2 kappa_n k²t) <|Psi(t)|²>_z,

    chi_n(t) := kappa_n ∫_0^t <|∇theta_n(s)|²> ds
              = E_theta,n(0)-E_theta,n(t)
              = (1/4)[1-exp(-2 kappa_n k²t)<|Psi(t)|²>_z].

Consequently, for each fixed t,

    lim_(n→∞) chi_n(t) = chi_alpha(t) := (1/4)[1-<|Psi(t)|²>_z].

The imaginary potential does not directly change cell energy. Integration by parts gives

    d/dt <|Psi|²>_z = -2 alpha <|∂z Psi|²>_z.

For q≠0 and alpha>0, ∂z Psi is nonzero for all sufficiently small positive times, since ∂t Psi(z,0)=-i q sin(z). The norm is nonincreasing, so <|Psi(t)|²><1 for every t>0. Thus chi_alpha(t)>0 at every fixed positive time even though kappa_n→0. This is a genuine finite-time scalar dissipation anomaly for this sequence and this fixed scalar mode.

Feynman–Kac also gives Psi(z,t)=E_z[exp(-i q∫_0^t sin(Z_s)ds)] for dZ_s=√(2 alpha)dW_s on the circle. Squaring its modulus uses two independent Z histories with the same starting z, so chi_alpha=(1/4)E[1-cos(k ΔX)] where ΔX is their longitudinal displacement difference. This is the exact phase-sensitive pair-separation observable for the constructed mode.

The small-time expansion is explicit. Since Psi=1-iq t sin(z)+O(t²),

    <|∂z Psi|²> = q² t²/2 + O(t³),
    1-<|Psi(t)|²> = alpha q² t³/3 + O(t⁴),
    chi_alpha(t) = alpha q² t³/12 + O(t⁴)
                 = epsilon k² t³/(6 Pr) + O(t⁴).

The last equality uses A²=4E and alpha=epsilon/(2E Pr). This is an internal mathematical prediction for the constructed shear sequence, not empirical evidence.

Lagrangian mechanism: in backward time the cross-stream coordinate has Brownian diffusivity kappa_n. Its fast phase Z=nY therefore has diffusivity alpha, not zero. The longitudinal displacement contains A∫sin(Z_s)ds, a non-degenerate random additive functional. Two independent backward paths from the same endpoint acquire O(1) longitudinal separation as n grows. The scalar cos(kx) detects this separation through its phase. The cell equation is also the conditional characteristic-function equation for this additive functional. In the small-time limit the pair displacement obeys E[(ΔX)²]=(2/3)A² alpha t³+O(t⁴); averaging the scalar phase gives chi= k²E[(ΔX)²]/8+O(t⁴), agreeing with the PDE coefficient above.

3. SHARP TAYLOR-DISPERSION ENVELOPE UNDER SHEAR ENERGY AND DISSIPATION BUDGETS

For a general smooth, mean-zero periodic shear U(y), write U=Σ_(m≠0) Uhat_m exp(i m y), and set

    E = (1/2)<U²> = (1/2)Σ |Uhat_m|²,
    Z = <(U')²> = Σ m² |Uhat_m|²,
    epsilon = nu Z.

The long-time longitudinal Taylor/Green–Kubo coefficient for a passive particle is

    K_T = kappa + (1/kappa) Σ_(m≠0) |Uhat_m|²/m².

Indeed the cross-stream particle is Brownian with generator kappa∂yy, so its stationary shear-velocity correlation is <U, exp(kappa s Δ)U>; integrating that correlation over s≥0 gives the inverse-Laplacian expression. This coefficient refers to long-time effective particle/scalar spreading for a fixed shear and fixed positive kappa; it is distinct from the finite-time cell limit in §2.

Define p_m=|Uhat_m|²/(2E), so Σp_m=1, and s=Σp_m m²=Z/(2E)=epsilon/(2 nu E). Then

    K_T-kappa = (2E/kappa) Σ p_m/m².

The direct Jensen bound is

    K_T-kappa ≥ 4 Pr E²/epsilon.

It is sharp when all nonzero Fourier energy is on one frequency shell. A sharper exact bound uses the integer spectrum. If j²≤s≤(j+1)², define

    g(s) = ((j+1)²-s)/((j+1)²-j²) * 1/j²
         + (s-j²)/((j+1)²-j²) * 1/(j+1)².

Then

    K_T-kappa ≥ (2E/kappa) g(s).

Proof: on the support q=m²∈{1,4,9,...}, the piecewise-linear interpolation g through (j²,1/j²) is convex. Its successive slopes are -1/[j²(j+1)²], which increase with j. Jensen applied to g gives Σp_m/m²=Σp_m g(m²)≥g(Σp_m m²)=g(s). Equality is attained by placing the energy only on adjacent shells j and j+1 with weights chosen to give mean s. Thus this is the exact minimum over all periodic shears with the prescribed E and Z. Also K_T-kappa≤2E/kappa by m²≥1; for s>1 this upper bound is a sharp supremum, approached by mixing the fundamental mode with a vanishing energy fraction at an arbitrarily high frequency.

As nu→0 with E, epsilon and Pr fixed, s→∞ and (2E/kappa)g(s) is asymptotic to 4 Pr E²/epsilon. The single-shell sine family in §1 attains the continuous bound exactly when s=n². Its Taylor coefficient is

    K_T-kappa = A²/(2 kappa_n n²) = 4 Pr E²/epsilon.

The budget meaning is explicit: for shears, E fixes total velocity variance and epsilon/nu fixes its mean squared Fourier wavenumber. Taylor spreading weights each mode by the inverse squared wavenumber. At fixed budgets the least spreading is attained by concentrating velocity energy at one scale (or, for a non-square s, two adjacent scales).

4. OBSERVABLE-DEPENDENT PAIR-COUPLING ENVELOPE

The previous construction shows why trajectory randomness alone does not determine dissipation of a specified scalar. Use the normalized L² inner product ||f||²=(1/|Omega|)∫|f|². Let u be any smooth divergence-free velocity on a compact boundaryless domain, kappa>0, and let theta solve ∂t theta+u·∇theta=kappaΔtheta with initial scalar f. Let P_x(da) be the law of the backward stochastic trajectory's initial point a when it ends at x at time t. The stochastic representation is theta(x,t)=Tf(x):=∫f(a)P_x(da). Define the pair coupling

    Pi_t(da,db) := (1/|Omega|)∫_Omega P_x(da)P_x(db) dx,

where the two histories are independent conditional on the common endpoint x. Incompressibility and volume preservation make Pi a symmetric coupling with uniform marginals. Jensen's identity and the scalar energy balance give

    ||f||²-||Tf||² = (1/2)∫[f(a)-f(b)]² Pi_t(da,db),
    chi_f(t) := kappa∫_0^t <|∇theta|²> ds
              = (1/2)(||f||²-||Tf||²)
              = (1/4)∫[f(a)-f(b)]² Pi_t(da,db).

Thus a measured pair kernel gives a sharp finite-time dissipation envelope for every fixed scalar class. If T* denotes the L² adjoint, Q:=I-T*T is a positive contraction and chi_f=(1/2)<f,Qf>. For a fixed band-limited, zero-mean scalar space H_K, the exact smallest and largest possible fractions of initial scalar energy dissipated are the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of the finite Hermitian matrix P_K Q P_K. For a spatially translation-invariant pair coupling with separation R=b-a, Fourier modes diagonalize Q:

    Q exp(i k·x) = [1-Re Phi(k)] exp(i k·x),
    Phi(k) := E exp(i k·R),
    chi_cos(k)/E_theta,0 = E[1-cos(k·R)].

The last quantity depends on orientation and phase separation modulo the scalar wavelength, not only on E|R|². In particular, a kernel can be broad in directions perpendicular to k, or concentrated at phases k·R≈2πZ, and still dissipate little of that scalar mode. Conversely, if Pi assigns mass at least p to a set on which [f(a)-f(b)]²≥s0, then chi_f≥p s0/4. This is an exact observable-dependent lower bound with no universality assumption.

There is also a general no-go bound for uniformly smooth velocities. Lift the backward paths to R^d from a common terminal point and let L(s)=||∇u(s)||_infinity and Λ=∫_0^T L(s)ds. For their relative displacement R, the independent Brownian noises give

    d E|R|²/dτ ≤ 2 L(T-τ) E|R|² + 4 d kappa,    E|R(0)|²=0.

Gronwall yields E|R(T)|²≤4d kappa T exp(2Λ). If the fixed initial scalar is L_f-Lipschitz, the pair formula therefore gives

    chi_f(T) ≤ d kappa T L_f² exp(2Λ).

Thus bounded integrated Lipschitz strain rules out a finite-time anomaly for a fixed smooth observable. Conversely, if chi_f(T)≥c>0, then Λ≥(1/2)log(c/(d kappa T L_f²)); integrated strain must diverge at least logarithmically as kappa→0. This bound does not follow from finite kinetic dissipation alone: the latter controls nu∫||∇u||², not the unweighted L-infinity strain integral. The shrinking-scale shear escapes the no-go through its diverging strain; a fixed smooth velocity with uniformly bounded Λ does not.

This pair-coupling form is a derived reformulation of an established Lagrangian fluctuation–dissipation relation, not claimed here as a new general theorem. Its use in this record is to make the scalar mode dependence and the correct finite-band envelope explicit. It tells what extra information a universal roughness-only claim omits: the joint backward pair kernel relative to the chosen scalar increments.

5. EXTREMAL AND DEGENERATE CHECKS

• No shear: A=0 gives Psi=1 and chi_alpha=0. For theta0=cos(kx), the exact finite-n loss is (1/4)(1-exp(-2 kappa_n k²t)), which tends to zero.
• Scalar aligned with shear layers: with u_n=(A sin(ny),0) and theta0=cos(my), u·∇theta=0. Then theta=exp(-kappa_n m²t)cos(my) and chi_n=(1/4)(1-exp(-2 kappa_n m²t))→0. The velocity family has the same E, epsilon and Pr as in §2. Therefore these budgets and nontrivial velocity shear do not force anomaly for every scalar.
• Fixed smooth shear scale: if n is fixed as kappa→0, then alpha=kappa n²→0. The cell equation tends to Psi=exp(-i q t sin z), whose modulus is one, so the finite-time scalar dissipation tends to zero. The anomaly in §2 relies on a matched sequence of shrinking velocity scales.
• Fixed shear support: if U has Fourier support |m|≤m_f and E stays bounded, then epsilon=nu Z≤2 nu E m_f²→0. Thus a positive finite kinetic-dissipation limit at bounded E cannot be realized by stationary shears with fixed velocity-forcing support. The constructed positive-anomaly sequence moves the forcing support to n~nu^(-1/2).
• Scalar mode geometry: the pair formula vanishes when f is constant on paired points, even if the pair kernel is nontrivial. This is the exact failure mode of treating spontaneous stochasticity as sufficient for every prescribed scalar.

8. CLAIM STATUS AND EXACT GAP

• Source-established: stochastic Lagrangian FDR and scalar-dissipation relation under Drivas–Eyink hypotheses; classical Taylor dispersion for fixed shear profiles.
• Internally derived: exact matched-scale shear reduction, positive fixed-time scalar loss, small-time coefficient epsilon k²/(6 Pr), and sharp discrete Taylor-coefficient lower envelope at fixed shear energy and enstrophy.
• Finite-tested: Fourier solver agrees with the cell energy identity and the displayed asymptotic coefficients within the recorded resolutions.
• Counterexample established: identical velocity energy/dissipation/Pr budgets can yield zero anomaly for a scalar aligned with the shear and positive anomaly for a streamwise scalar.
• Unresolved: whether an equally decisive exact mechanism or observable envelope can be derived for Navier–Stokes sequences with fixed large-scale velocity forcing, bounded physical initial energy, and vanishing viscosity; whether finite kinetic dissipation alone constrains the limiting pair coupling; and whether the shear budget envelope has a useful analogue for non-shear turbulent flows. Any extension must account for forcing-scale transfer and the full pair coupling, not only a second moment of separation.
