## Exact candidate interface

The algorithmic claim audited here is the c01_s01 fixed-variable optimizer in
`work/cycle6/c01_s01/spin_bridge/FIXED_VARIABLE_ORACLE_COMPILER.txt`. For fixed
local dimension d, it takes a rational Hermitian trace-one permutation-invariant
operator R, supplied in a polynomial-size commutant representation, and
approximates

    v_theta(R) = min_{S in conv{ sigma^tensorN : sigma >= theta I, Tr sigma=1 }}
                 (1/2)||R-S||_1

to additive epsilon. It proposes an output mixture of at most
D=binom(N+d^2-1,d^2-1) rational one-site states with dyadic weights and a
Turing running time polynomial in N, input bit length L, and log(1/epsilon),
for each fixed d. Its current proof route imports fixed-variable real
quantifier elimination, rational ellipsoid optimization, a transcript-to-finite
primal SDP argument, and a polynomial-bit SDP theorem. This is an iid-cone
optimizer for a *whole N-body* PI input, not a general separability decision
algorithm. It does not claim the nearest point is the input state, or that the
target has an exact iid decomposition.

The separate root derivation
`work/cycle6/control/SYMMETRIC_WHITEBALL_COMPILER.txt` proposes a correction
from an exponentially accurate iid approximation to a fully separable
decomposition. Its trace-norm sufficient condition is
`||R-S||_1 <= (zeta/d)^N`, with every local factor of S at least `zeta I`.
After removing the all-white branch of S, the residual about the maximally
mixed state has trace-norm radius `d^(-2N)`. The proposed sampler groups
operator words by their fixed-d count vector (D types), then samples a product
word by permutation and local signs. The resulting full-separable product
factors can differ from site to site. These two pieces must be assessed
separately: a known efficient construction of a tiny identity-centered ball
would bear on the correction, but would not subsume the iid optimizer or the
model-specific all-N Gibbs estimate.

## Strongest exact algorithmic prior art found

### A. Whole-state product-mixture balls and sampling

**Schack and Caves (1999), “Explicit product ensembles for separable quantum
states,” arXiv:quant-ph/9904109.** Their canonical qubit expansion (Sec. 2.2,
Eqs. 7–17) uses the six pure projectors
`P_{j,mu}=(I+(-1)^mu sigma_j)/2` and dual operators
`Q_{j,mu}=(I+3(-1)^mu sigma_j)/6`. For every N-qubit state it gives

    rho = sum_{a_1,...,a_N} Tr[rho (Q_{a_1} tensor ... tensor Q_{a_N})]
                            (P_{a_1} tensor ... tensor P_{a_N}).

If all coefficients are nonnegative, this is an explicit finite fully
separable ensemble (the listed support may have 6^N terms). The paper proves
that for `rho=(1-epsilon)I/2^N+epsilon rho_1`, every coefficient is
nonnegative whenever `epsilon <= 1/(1+2^(2N-1))`.

There is a direct trace-ball corollary particularly close to the residual
correction. Each Q has eigenvalues `2/3,-1/3`, so
`||Q_{a_1} tensor ... tensor Q_{a_N}||_op=(2/3)^N`. The coefficient of
`I/2^N` is `6^-N`. For any trace-zero Hermitian Delta,

    |Tr[Delta (tensor_i Q_{a_i})]| <= ||Delta||_1 (2/3)^N.

Thus `||Delta||_1 <= 4^-N` implies every canonical coefficient of
`I/2^N+Delta` is nonnegative, because
`4^-N(2/3)^N=6^-N`. This exact l1-ball corollary is an algebraic consequence
of their displayed expansion; it is stronger as a trace-ball statement than
their separately stated white-noise mixing-parameter bound.

For a permutation-invariant input, the canonical coefficient is constant on
the site-permutation orbit of a six-label string. There are only
`binom(N+5,5)` label-count types. Summing the coefficient over each orbit
gives a probability on polynomially many types; after drawing a type, a
uniform random permutation samples an atom from its orbit. This is a derived
type-compression sampler, not an algorithm stated in Schack–Caves. Its
per-orbit coefficients can be evaluated from a fixed-dimension commutant
representation by symmetrizing the tensor product of the local Q operators.
The number of count vectors is polynomial for fixed d. The actual mixture
still has exponentially many possible pure product atoms; its succinctness is
in its type list and sampler, not in the expanded support.

The direct overlap is substantive for the d=2 correction: its residual radius
is exactly `4^-N`. It means a “qubit identity-ball sampler for PI targets” is
not by itself a new conceptual result. A sharper type count (the proposed
`binom(N+3,3)` Pauli-orbit basis), rational/dyadic local-factor output, and a
certified bit-complexity implementation could still be a useful refinement;
they need comparison at the algorithm/interface level.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9904109, especially Sec. 2.2,
Eqs. (7)–(17). The preceding Braunstein–Caves–Jozsa–Linden–Popescu–Schack
paper, “Separability of very noisy mixed states and implications for NMR
quantum computing,” arXiv:quant-ph/9811018, gives a constructive finite
product decomposition in an overcomplete discrete product basis and derives
an all-qubit white-noise neighborhood of order `4^-N`; its Sec. 3 continuous
representation gives the same coefficientwise positivity argument. Primary
source: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9811018, especially Sec. 3, Eqs.
(10)–(13).

**Rungta et al. (2000), “Qudit Entanglement,” arXiv:quant-ph/0001075.** This
is the close arbitrary-local-dimension analogue. Their Sec. 4.1–4.2 constructs
the canonical continuous product-projector representation for any N-qudit
operator. If D is the local dimension and V the volume of pure-state
projective space, the local dual is

    Q_psi = (D/V)((D+1)|psi><psi| - I),

with eigenvalues `D^2/V` and `-D/V`; the maximally mixed coefficient density
is `V^-N`. Therefore, for a trace-zero Hermitian Delta,

    ||Delta||_1 <= D^(-2N)
       => |Tr[Delta (tensor_i Q_{psi_i})]| <= V^(-N),

so `I/D^N+Delta` has a nonnegative product-projector density. This is again a
direct trace-ball corollary of their kernel, not their stated threshold: their
paper separately proves nonnegativity for the white-noise mixing coefficient
`epsilon <= 1/(1+D^(2N-1))`.

The same kernel also yields an exact constant-acceptance *continuous-randomness*
sampler for that ball. Put `Qtilde_psi=V Q_psi` and
`p(psi_1,...,psi_N)=Tr[(I/D^N+Delta) tensor_i Qtilde_{psi_i}]`. Then
`||Qtilde_psi||_op=D^2`, the identity baseline gives p=1, and the trace-ball
condition gives `0<=p<=2`. Draw the local pure states independently from Haar
measure and accept with probability `p/2`; accepted product states have
exactly the positive canonical product-mixture law, and the acceptance
probability is 1/2. For a PI density supplied in the commutant basis, evaluating
p can be reduced to a contraction with the permutation-twirl of the product
Q operator, whose count-vector expansion has polynomial size at fixed D.
That compressed evaluation and the rejection algorithm are deductions from
their representation; the paper does not give their running-time or finite-bit
analysis. In particular, this route does not yet establish the candidate's
exact rational finite-bit output interface. It does show that the mathematical
idea of sampling a product decomposition throughout the candidate's tiny
arbitrary-D correction ball has a short primary-source route.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0001075, Secs. 4.1–4.2, Eqs.
(73)–(86).

**Gurvits–Barnum (2003), “Separable balls around the maximally mixed
multipartite quantum states.”** They prove a generic Frobenius-norm
identity-centered separability ball with radius `2^(-(N/2-1))` for
unnormalized matrices, and explicitly characterize their proof as
nonconstructive with respect to the product decomposition. This subsumes
priority for existence of a general separable ball, but not the sampler.
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0302102, abstract and lines
41–47 of its introduction.

### B. Optimizing separable or coherent mixtures

**Koßmann–Zeiss (2026), “Fixed points in de Finetti hierarchies,”
arXiv:2607.23689v1.** This is the closest recent computational prior art found.
For fixed local dimensions and a permutation-invariant extension in compressed
Schur form, Theorem 6.9 computes, in polynomial time in extension length n,
explicit separable mixtures for a retained bipartite marginal `AB`; at least
one candidate is within
`c(M)*sqrt(2 ln(2) log(d_A)/n)` in trace norm. The candidate has polynomially
many terms. Their method uses Schur–Weyl blocks and type-coarse-grained
measurements; their paper explicitly combines it with outer hierarchies into
poly(1/epsilon) algorithms for SEP/cSEP optimization.

The interface is different in four material ways: their target is one
bipartite marginal, not the whole `N`-body state; the input is an assumed
extension; the output is a general bipartite separable mixture, not a mixture
of `sigma^tensorN`; and the accuracy dependence is polynomial in
`1/epsilon`, not in `log(1/epsilon)`. It is close prior art for compressed
permutation-invariant processing and polynomial-size separable inner
certificates, but does not directly subsume the c01_s01 optimizer.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23689, abstract, §§2.1 and 6,
Theorem 6.9. In particular the theorem states the retained-marginal trace
bound and computes candidates for all m in `0,...,n-1`.

**Malavolta (2026), “Quantum Separability in Polynomial Time,”
arXiv:2607.23773v1.** This gives randomized polynomial-time weak membership
and an additive-accuracy separable support-function algorithm for bipartite
`d x d` states at a fixed Frobenius gap. It also returns approximate
product-vector optimizers. Its variables are two local pure vectors and its
runtime is polynomial in the local dimension for constant gap. This does not
give an N-partite PI state mixture, an iid-cone projection, or a
poly(log(1/epsilon)) finite-bit algorithm for exponentially small trace error.
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23773, abstract and §1.1.

**Giraud–Braun–Braun (2008), “Classicality of spin states,”
arXiv:0805.2592.** For a *single spin-j system*, “classical” means a convex
mixture of angular-momentum coherent projectors. The paper characterizes
spin-1/2 and spin-1 exactly and gives a linear-programming approach; by
Carathéodory, a single-spin representation needs at most `(2j+1)^2` coherent
projectors. For a top-spin symmetric subspace, these projectors correspond to
identical pure products across sites. This is relevant to the symmetric-sector
interface, but it does not cover generic permutation-invariant mixed states
with lower Schur sectors or the larger mixed-local iid cone
`conv{sigma^tensorN}`.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/0805.2592, abstract and §II.3.

**Wolfe–Yelin (2013/2014), “Certifying Separability in Symmetric Mixed States,
and Superradiance,” arXiv:1307.5779v3.** For N-qubit states diagonal in the
top-spin Dicke basis, they parameterize a fully separable ansatz as finite
mixtures of phase-twirled identical pure-qubit products and reduce membership
to polynomial moment equations in the Dicke populations. They prove the
ansatz characterization for N=4 and conjecture its completeness for general
N; the paper is explicit that their condition is sufficient generally while
the all-N necessity remains conjectural. It is narrower than all PI operators
and does not furnish the c01_s01 general full-state compiler.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1307.5779, Eqs. (4)–(17), especially
the caveat and conjecture around Eq. (17).

**Tura et al. (2017), “Separability of diagonal symmetric states: a quadratic
conic optimization problem,” arXiv:1706.09423v2.** This is an important
algorithmic-scope boundary. For bipartite diagonal-symmetric states on
`C^d tensor C^d`, Theorem 3.1 characterizes separability exactly by complete
positivity of an associated `d x d` matrix. The paper notes that this
completely-positive-matrix membership problem is NP-hard when `d` is part of
the input (and gives PPT/separability equivalence only in the stated low
dimensions). It also treats the narrower N-qubit diagonal-symmetric family.
This is not a hardness result for the candidate's fixed-d, general
permutation-invariant N-body iid-cone projection: the state classes and
optimization interfaces differ, and variable-d hardness does not imply
hardness at fixed d. It does show why symmetry alone should not be described
as making unrestricted separability easy, and it is relevant prior art for
special symmetric separability tests and conic formulations.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1706.09423, abstract and §§1–3,
especially Theorem 3.1 and the complete-positive-cone discussion.

**Mathé–Usui–Gühne–Vitagliano (2026), “Estimating the best separable
approximation of non-pure spin-squeezed states,” Quantum 10, 2078.** This is
a close physical comparator for all-sector fully connected qubit XXZ thermal
states. Its method combines lower bounds from spin-squeezing inequalities
with iterative/numerical separable ansatz approximations; it is not a
poly(log(1/epsilon)) optimizer returning a certified whole-state closest
mixture. The previously saved exact scope discussion is in
`PRIORITY_EXACT_SEPARABILITY_OPEN_BALL.md`.

Primary article and full text: https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-04-21-2078/
and https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07814.

### C. Fixed-variable polynomial optimization

The support function of the iid cone has the form

    h(W) = max_{sigma >= theta I, Tr sigma=1} Tr(W sigma^tensorN).

For fixed local d, sigma has p=d^2-1 real coordinates; positivity is a fixed
finite family of principal-minor polynomial inequalities; and the objective is
a rational polynomial of degree at most N. Thus an exact support-threshold
query is an existential real-algebraic formula in a fixed number p of
variables. Basu–Pollack–Roy and Renegar provide general quantifier-elimination
and decision algorithms whose dependence on degree and coefficient bit size
is polynomial when the variable count is fixed. These sources make the
fixed-variable oracle a standard generic algorithmic ingredient after the
reduction is written down. They do not by themselves provide the c01_s01
trace-norm projection, ellipsoid weak-oracle tolerances, transcript recovery,
positive rational primal weights, support reduction to D atoms, or final
finite-bit sampler. I found no inspected primary source that states this
complete quantum interface with its `poly(N,L,log(1/epsilon))` guarantee.

Primary sources: Basu–Pollack–Roy, “On the combinatorial and algebraic
complexity of quantifier elimination,” JACM 43(6), 1002–1045 (1996),
https://doi.org/10.1145/235809.235813; Renegar, “On the Computational
Complexity and Geometry of the First-Order Theory of the Reals, Part II,”
J. Symbolic Comput. 13 (1992), https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(10)80004-5;
author Saugata Basu’s survey, Theorem 2.18,
https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf.

## Exact novelty boundary and gates

The literature inspected supports these negative/positive distinctions:

* Generic identity-centered separable balls are old. Explicit finite product
  ensembles for qubit balls are old. Positive continuous product densities for
  qudit identity balls are old. The corresponding qubit type-compressed and
  qudit constant-acceptance samplers follow by short symmetry/dual-kernel
  arguments, but their polynomial-bit implementations were not found stated
  in those papers.
* Polynomially many terms in efficient separable approximations of
  permutation-invariant *marginals* are now known. Coherent mixtures for a
  single spin and product-moment tests on special symmetric-supported families
  are also known.
* The candidate remains distinguishable, in the inspected sources, at the
  exact combined interface: full N-body PI input in commutant encoding; additive
  trace-norm projection onto the iid cone; polynomially many *identical-site*
  atoms; finite rational/dyadic output; and poly(N,L,log(1/epsilon)) bit time.
  The symmetry-compressed white-ball bridge then seeks an exact whole-state
  fully separable sampler for the special Gibbs targets, which is distinct
  from general separability membership.

This is not a novelty certification. It remains **UNKNOWN** whether the full
optimizer interface is already implicit in the real-algebra/convex-optimization
literature or in an uninspected symmetric-quantum source. The fixed-variable QE
oracle is standard; novelty, if any, lies in proving and implementing the exact
commutant/trace-norm/projection-to-primal pipeline and combining it with the
model-specific all-N approximation. The continuous Rungta sampler gives a
particularly sharp test: show whether the finite-bit rational parity compiler
materially improves the expected-time continuous sampler at the same
compressed-input and output-accuracy interface.

For the Gibbs all-N theorem priority, retain the separate open-ball report
`PRIORITY_EXACT_SEPARABILITY_OPEN_BALL.md`: Gurvits–Barnum and broad
high-temperature results are nearby prior art, while FKV 1991 full text was
