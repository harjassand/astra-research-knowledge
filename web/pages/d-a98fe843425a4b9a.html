# Hydrodynamic control: same drift, different fluctuation cost

## Result

For a closed population of (N) binary agents, a neutral mean-field copying interaction can be added without changing the fluid ODE. It does change the Hamiltonian, the controlled path entropy, the central-limit variance, the stationary large-deviation rate, and an observable jump-activity statistic. This is a concrete counterexample to any control or rare-event prediction that uses only the hydrodynamic drift.

The calculation is exact for the finite-(N) model below. The general density-dependent LLN/LDP framework is established prior art; this report makes no novelty or priority claim for the example or for the variational formula.

## Model and scaling

There are (N) agents in states (A) and (B), with (K_N(t)) agents in state (A) and density (X_N=K_N/N\in\{0,1/N,\ldots,1\}). A (B\to A) spontaneous flip has per-agent rate \(\kappa>0\), and a (B\to A) copying event has per-agent rate \(c X_N\). The reverse spontaneous and copying rates are \(\kappa\) and \(c(1-X_N)\). Thus the aggregate count rates are

\[
\lambda_k=(N-k)(\kappa+c k/N)=N b_c(k/N),\qquad
\mu_k=k(\kappa+c(N-k)/N)=N d_c(k/N),
\]
where
\[
b_c(x)=(1-x)(\kappa+cx),\qquad d_c(x)=x(\kappa+c(1-x)).
\]

Equivalently, this is the mass-action network (B\to A, A\to B, A+B\to2A, A+B\to2B), with density-scaled propensities \(N\kappa(1-x),N\kappa x,Nc x(1-x),Nc x(1-x)\). Total population is conserved. (c=0) is the independent-flip reference; any (c>0) is interacting.

## Fluid limit, with a direct bound

The drift is independent of the interaction parameter:
\[
f_c(x)=b_c(x)-d_c(x)=\kappa(1-2x),\qquad \dot x=\kappa(1-2x).
\]
The generator martingale decomposition is
\[
X_N(t)=X_N(0)+\int_0^t\kappa(1-2X_N(s))\,ds+M_N(t),
\]
with predictable quadratic variation
\[
\langle M_N\rangle_t=\frac1N\int_0^t[b_c(X_N(s))+d_c(X_N(s))]ds
\le \frac{(\kappa+c/2)t}{N},
\]
because (b_c+d_c=\kappa+2cx(1-x)\le\kappa+c/2\). Doob's (L^2) inequality and Grönwall give, for the ODE solution (x(t)),
\[
\mathbb E\sup_{s\le T}|X_N(s)-x(s)|^2
\le 2e^{4\kappa T}\left(|X_N(0)-x(0)|^2+\frac{4(\kappa+c/2)T}{N}\right).
\]
Thus (X_N\to x) uniformly in probability on every fixed time interval when the initial densities converge. All (c\ge0) share the same fluid law.

## Load-bearing fluctuation/control derivation

The exponential generator has the exact density-scaled Hamiltonian
\[
H_c(x,p)=b_c(x)(e^p-1)+d_c(x)(e^{-p}-1).
\]
In particular \(\partial_pH_c(x,0)=f_c(x)\), but
\[
\partial_{pp}H_c(x,0)=b_c(x)+d_c(x)=\kappa+2cx(1-x).
\]
At the common stable equilibrium \(x_*=1/2\), set \(g_c=\kappa+c/2\). Then
\[
H_c(1/2,p)=g_c(\cosh p-1),\quad
L_c(1/2,v)=\sup_p\{pv-H_c(1/2,p)\}
=v\,\operatorname{arsinh}(v/g_c)-g_c\left(\sqrt{1+(v/g_c)^2}-1\right).
\]
Consequently \(L_c(1/2,v)=v^2/(2g_c)+O(v^4)\) for small (v). In the two genuinely interacting cases (c=\kappa) and (c=2\kappa), this cost is respectively \(v^2/(3\kappa)+O(v^4)\) and \(v^2/(4\kappa)+O(v^4)\), despite identical ODEs.

The control interpretation follows at finite (N), not by analogy. Change the (+/-) reaction intensities from \(Nb_c(X_N),Nd_c(X_N)\) to \(Nu_+,Nu_-\). For a common initial law, the path-space relative entropy is
\[
D(Q_N\Vert P_N)=N\,\mathbb E_{Q_N}\int_0^T
\left[\ell(u_+;b_c(X_N))+\ell(u_-;d_c(X_N))\right]dt,
\quad \ell(q;\lambda)=q\log(q/\lambda)-q+\lambda.
\]
If a limiting interior path \(\phi\) is imposed through fluxes \(u_+-u_-=\dot\phi\), minimizing pointwise cost over nonnegative fluxes gives
\[
L_c(\phi,\dot\phi)=\inf_{q_+-q_-=\dot\phi}
\{\ell(q_+;b_c(\phi))+\ell(q_-;d_c(\phi))\}.
\]
For (b,d>0), the minimizing fluxes are
\[
q_+=\frac{\dot\phi+\sqrt{\dot\phi^2+4bd}}2,
\qquad q_- =\frac{-\dot\phi+\sqrt{\dot\phi^2+4bd}}2,
\]
which yields the Legendre transform above. Hence the asymptotic controlled entropy cost is (N\int_0^T L_c(\phi,\dot\phi)dt+o(N)) for regular interior paths under these intensity controls. A drift-only controller cannot recover this cost: the omitted symmetric reaction flux (c x(1-x)) cancels from (b-d) but remains in both the entropy and Hamiltonian.

## Independent exact discriminator: stationary tails and activity

For (c>0), detailed balance gives
\[
\frac{\pi_N(k+1)}{\pi_N(k)}
=\frac{N-k}{k+1}\frac{k+\alpha_N}{N-k-1+\alpha_N},
\qquad \alpha_N=\frac{N\kappa}{c}.
\]
Thus \(K_N\) has the symmetric beta-binomial law with parameters \((N,\alpha_N,\alpha_N)\). For (c=0), it is \(\operatorname{Binomial}(N,1/2)\). The exact scaled variance for (c>0) is
\[
N\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_N}(X_N)
=\frac{1+2\kappa/c}{4(1+2N\kappa/c)}N
\longrightarrow \frac{\kappa+c/2}{4\kappa}.
\]
For (c=0), the limit is (1/4); for (c=2\kappa), it is (1/2). This agrees with the linear-noise/OU variance computed from drift derivative (-2\kappa) and noise coefficient (g_c).

The exact stationary ratios also give the speed-(N) local LDP rate
\[
I_c(x)=\int_{1/2}^x\log\!\left(
\frac{y(\kappa+c(1-y))}{(1-y)(\kappa+cy)}
\right)dy,
\]
with the continuous endpoint extension; (I_0(x)=x\log(2x)+(1-x)\log(2(1-x))\). At (x=3/4\),
\[
I_0(3/4)=\tfrac34\log(3/2)+\tfrac14\log(1/2)\approx0.130812,
\quad I_{2\kappa}(3/4)=3\log2-\tfrac54\log5\approx0.067645.
\]
For two interacting choices, (I_\kappa(3/4)=\tfrac{15}{4}\log3+2\log2-\tfrac54\log5-\tfrac74\log7\approx0.08895\), still different from (I_{2\kappa}). The local stationary probability near (3/4) therefore has exponentially different costs under identical hydrodynamics.

A directly observable discriminator avoids rare-event sampling. Let (J_N(T)) count all flips. Its predictable rate is
\[
N[b_c(X_N)+d_c(X_N)]=N[\kappa+2cX_N(1-X_N)].
\]
Starting at (X_N(0)\to1/2), the common ODE remains at (1/2). The compensated-count martingale has variance (O(N)), so for each fixed (T>0),
\[
\frac{J_N(T)}{NT}\xrightarrow{P}\kappa+c/2.
\]
This distinguishes (c=\kappa) from (c=2\kappa) with a fixed limiting gap \(\kappa/2\), using ordinary trajectories rather than the exponentially rare (X_N\simeq3/4) event. The statistic observes the total jump count, so the measurement interface includes resolved flip events; density snapshots alone do not provide it.

## Failed routes, caveats, and next step

1. **Drift-only LDP/control transfer fails.** The mapping ((b,d)\mapsto b-d) is many-to-one; adding the same nonnegative (c x(1-x)) to each channel preserves drift and changes (H''(x,0)) and the entropy action. This is the central counterexample, not a failed theorem proof.
2. **The LLN alone is not a discriminator.** Any statistic of the limiting density path has exactly the same limit for every (c); the discriminator must include jump activity, (N^{-1/2}) fluctuations, or exponentially rare occupancy data.
3. **Stationary tails are not needed to identify (c) operationally.** They give a sharp asymptotic comparison but estimating them requires exponential sample effort; the ordinary event-count statistic has a fixed gap and variance of order (1/N) for fixed (T).
4. **This does not settle general interacting hydrodynamic control.** The system is one-dimensional, complete-graph, and has only two unit jumps; it does not handle spatial correlations, hidden fast variables, control constraints, or non-Markov control policies. The LDP/control theorem invoked for arbitrary CRNs remains a standard cited dependency. No simulation, external independent proof check, or empirical-data test was performed.

The next useful extension is a spatial two-type voter/contact process in which the empirical density drift is unchanged by a neutral interaction but the quadratic-variation *operator* (not just scalar activity) changes. That would test whether the same discriminator survives when local correlations and finite-range structure matter.
