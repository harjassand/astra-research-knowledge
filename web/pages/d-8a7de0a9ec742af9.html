\subsection*{The leading noisy-memory law}
For a fixed generalized amplitude-damping channel and expected input
excitation at most $E$ per cell, let $C_E>0$ be the constrained classical
capacity calculated below. For unknown $|b|\le R_n$, with $R_n\to0$ and
$\kappa_n=nR_n^2\to\infty$, the result is
\begin{align*}
 \liminf_n\frac{m_n}{\log_2\kappa_n}&\ge\frac{3}{2C_E}
 &&\text{for every vanishing-error archive},\\
 m_n&=\left(\frac{3}{2C_E}+o(1)\right)\log_2\kappa_n
 &&\text{for a constructed sequence}.
\end{align*}
For a known shrinking radius and unknown direction, replace $3/2$ by $1$.
These statements count every retained input-dependent register and concern
reconstruction of density states, not their purifications. They describe
the optimal leading coefficient; they do not promise reliability for
every sequence at the exact first-order boundary.

\clearpage\section{Independent completion of the unknown-ball archive}




Write $\rho_b=(I+b\cdot\sigma)/2$ for a qubit with Bloch vector
$b\in\mathbb R^3$, and $\T(P,Q)=\|P-Q\|_1/2$ for half trace distance.
The input is $n$ identically prepared copies of the same unknown state.
The compressor is not given $b$. A classical archive of dimension $D$
means a single retained label in a fixed alphabet of at most $D$ values.
The encoder and decoder may use temporary quantum computation, fresh
randomness, and fresh output qubits. Every input-dependent register that
survives the storage interval must be included in this alphabet.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:archive}
Let $n\ge4$, $0<R\le1/2$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/4$, and $\kappa=nR^2$.
There are an encoder into a classical alphabet of size $D$ and a decoder
to $n$ qubits, depending only on $(n,R,\varepsilon)$, such that
\begin{align}
 D&\le10^{12}(1+\kappa)^{3/2}\varepsilon^{-6},\label{eq:D}\\
 \sup_{|b|\le R}\T\bigl(\operatorname{Dec}\operatorname{Enc}
        (\rho_b^{\otimes n}),\rho_b^{\otimes n}\bigr)
 &\le\frac{2R}{1+R}+\varepsilon.\label{eq:error}
\end{align}
This is a density-state reconstruction statement. It makes no assertion
about preserving a purification or arbitrary correlated inputs.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses an exact continuous measurement only to define a finite
POVM and finite decoder states. A continuous outcome is never retained.
The decoder resamples from a known conditional reference law. It does
not prepare $\rho_{\widehat b}^{\otimes n}$ from a point estimate.

\section*{The fine measurement and its likelihood}

The Schur decomposition of $n$ qubits has blocks
\[
 (\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}\simeq
 \bigoplus_{L\in\mathcal L_n}\mathcal V_L\otimes\mathbb C^{m_L},
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_n=\{n\bmod2,n\bmod2+2,\ldots,n\},
\]
where $\dim\mathcal V_L=L+1$ and
\[
 m_L={n\choose(n-L)/2}-{n\choose(n-L)/2-1}.
\]
A binomial coefficient with a negative lower index is zero.
Fix a Schur unitary $W$ in this decomposition. For $u\in S^2$, let
$\ket{L,u}$ be the spin coherent vector, identified with
$\ket u^{\otimes L}$ in the symmetric $L$-qubit space.
For normalized sphere area measure $\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolution}
 (L+1)\int_{S^2}\proj{L,u}\dd\mu(u)=I_{\mathcal V_L}.
\end{equation}
For example, rotation covariance makes the integral scalar on the
irreducible spin space, and its trace fixes the scalar. This also covers
$L=0$. Measure the block $L$ and use this coherent POVM in the block.
The corresponding fine decoder state is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Omega}
 \Omega_{L,u}=
 W^*\left(\proj{L,u}\otimes\frac{I_{m_L}}{m_L}
          \text{ in block }L\right)W.
\end{equation}

Here is the record law, including its normalization and parameter
convention. If $\delta=|b|$, the Schur block of $\rho_b^{\otimes n}$ is
\[
 \left(\frac{1-\delta^2}{4}\right)^{(n-L)/2}
    \left.\rho_b^{\otimes L}\right|_{\mathrm{sym}}
       \otimes I_{m_L}.
\]
This is the standard two-row Schur representation: the determinant
representation has exponent $(n-L)/2$, and the remaining factor is the
$L$th symmetric power. Taking its trace against the coherent effect
gives the probability measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q_b(L,\dd u)=
 m_L(L+1)2^{-n}(1-|b|^2)^{(n-L)/2}(1+b\cdot u)^L\dd\mu(u).
\end{equation}
It is a probability measure because the effects in
\eqref{eq:resolution}, summed over blocks, form a POVM. In particular,
\[
 q_0(L,\dd u)=m_L(L+1)2^{-n}\dd\mu(u),\qquad
 \sum_{L\in\mathcal L_n}m_L(L+1)=2^n,
\]
and the exact likelihood is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likelihood}
 \frac{\dd q_b}{\dd q_0}(L,u)
 = (1-|b|^2)^{(n-L)/2}(1+b\cdot u)^L.
\end{equation}
For $|b|\le1/2$, it is strictly positive everywhere on the fine record
space. Thus every fine event of positive $q_0$ measure has positive
$q_b$ measure for every reference parameter used below.

\section*{The coherent reconstruction error}

We prove the error of the unbinned map
$\rho_b^{\otimes n}\mapsto\int\Omega_{L,u}\dd q_b(L,u)$ directly.
For $\delta>0$, put
\[
 r=\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta},\qquad a=1-r,\qquad
 Z_L=\sum_{k=0}^Lr^k.
\]
In a spin basis aligned with $b$, the normalized input block is
\[
 \tau_L=Z_L^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^L r^k\proj k.
\]
Let
\[
 B_L(P)=(L+1)\int\bra{L,u}P\ket{L,u}\proj{L,u}\dd\mu(u).
\]
Phase covariance makes $B_L(\tau_L)$ diagonal in this basis. With
$t=(1+u\cdot b/\delta)/2$, its $k$th diagonal entry is
\begin{align*}
 \beta_k
 &=\frac{L+1}{Z_L}{L\choose k}
   \int_0^1 t^{L-k}(1-t)^k(1-a(1-t))^L\dd t\\
 &=Z_L^{-1}\mathbb E(1-aU)^L,
 \qquad U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k+1,L-k+1).
\end{align*}
Weighted arithmetic--geometric mean gives
$1-aU=(1-U)+rU\ge r^U$. Jensen's inequality for the exponential gives
\[
 \beta_k\ge Z_L^{-1}r^{L\mathbb EU}
 =Z_L^{-1}r^{L(k+1)/(L+2)}
 \ge Z_L^{-1}r^{k+1}.
\]
Consequently $B_L(\tau_L)\ge r\tau_L$. Both matrices have trace one,
so $B_L(\tau_L)=r\tau_L+(1-r)\xi_L$ for a state $\xi_L$, and
\[
 \T(B_L(\tau_L),\tau_L)\le1-r=\frac{2\delta}{1+\delta}.
\]
At $\delta=0$, the block is $I/(L+1)$ and the map fixes it by
\eqref{eq:resolution}; the $L=0$ block is also fixed. The map preserves
the Schur block weights and the maximally mixed multiplicity factors.
Taking the direct-sum weighted trace distance therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fine-error}
 \T\left(\int\Omega_{L,u}\dd q_b,\rho_b^{\otimes n}\right)
 \le\frac{2|b|}{1+|b|}\le\frac{2R}{1+R}.
\end{equation}

\section*{A uniform second moment of the record}

The partition will use $X=Lu\in\mathbb R^3$, rather than $(L+2)u$.
We first calculate a moment of $X'=(L+2)u$.
Let $J_i$ denote spin $L/2$ generators in block $L$. The coherent
first moment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstmoment}
 (L+1)\int u_i\proj{L,u}\dd\mu(u)=\frac{2J_i}{L+2}.
\end{equation}
For $i=z$, the $k$th diagonal element of the left side is the mean of
$2t-1$ for $t\sim\operatorname{Beta}(L-k+1,k+1)$, namely
$(L-2k)/(L+2)$; azimuthal integration removes off-diagonal elements.
The other coordinates follow by rotations. Thus the law $q_b$ obeys
$\mathbb EX'=nb$, since $2J$ is the total Pauli observable.

The total-spin Casimir gives another exact identity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir}
 \mathbb E L(L+2)=3n+n(n-1)|b|^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $4J_{\mathrm{tot}}^2$ has eigenvalue $L(L+2)$ in block $L$,
and its expectation on $\rho_b^{\otimes n}$ is the displayed value
by expanding $J_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n\sigma^{(j)}$.
It follows that
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E|X'-nb|^2
 &=3n-n|b|^2+2\mathbb EL+4\le5n+4\le9n,\label{eq:momentprime}\\
 \mathbb E|X-nb|^2
 &\le2\mathbb E|X'-nb|^2+2\mathbb E|2u|^2
 \le18n+8\le20n\quad(n\ge4).\label{eq:moment}
\end{align}
The law of $u$ at $L=0$ is included in these formulas. For $X=Lu$,
all such records map to $0$.

\section*{The likelihood bound used inside a cell}

Define a continuous function on $\mathbb R^3$ by
\[
 F_b(x)=|x|\left[\ln(1+b\cdot x/|x|)
                    -\frac12\ln(1-|b|^2)\right]\quad(x\ne0),
 \qquad F_b(0)=0.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:likelihood} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loglikelihood}
 \ln\frac{\dd q_b}{\dd q_0}(L,u)
 =\frac n2\ln(1-|b|^2)+F_b(Lu).
\end{equation}
For $x\ne0$, write $v=x/|x|$ and
$\phi_b(v)=\ln(1+b\cdot v)-\frac12\ln(1-|b|^2)$. Then
\[
 \nabla_xF_b(x)=\phi_b(v)v+
          (I-vv^{\mathsf T})\frac b{1+b\cdot v}.
\]
For $|b|\le1/2$, the derivative of $\phi_b$ with respect to $b$ has
norm at most $2+2/3=8/3$. The derivative of $b/(1+b\cdot v)$ has
operator norm at most $2+2=4$. Integrating these derivatives along
the parameter segment gives, for $|b|,|c|\le1/2$,
\[
 |\nabla_x(F_b-F_c)(x)|\le\frac{20}3|b-c|<7|b-c|.
\]
Since $F_b-F_c$ is continuous at zero, integration along a segment,
split at zero if necessary, proves the global bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lipschitz}
 |(F_b-F_c)(x)-(F_b-F_c)(y)|\le7|b-c||x-y|.
\end{equation}
In particular, there is no singular small-$L$ remainder. Collisions
of the map $(L,u)\mapsto Lu$ at $L=0$ have exactly the same likelihood
in \eqref{eq:loglikelihood}.

We will also use the following elementary conditional replacement
bound. If $P,Q$ are mutually absolutely continuous probability measures
on an event $A$, and the oscillation of $\ln(\dd P/\dd Q)$ over $A$ is
at most $\lambda$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionalTV}
 \TV(P(\cdot\mid A),Q(\cdot\mid A))\le e^\lambda-1.
\end{equation}
To prove it, the normalizing mean of $\dd P/\dd Q$ lies between its
infimum and supremum. The conditional density ratio therefore lies
in $[e^{-\lambda},e^\lambda]$. Integrating its deviation from one
proves \eqref{eq:conditionalTV}. The weaker bound displayed here is
sufficient; the definition of total variation includes a factor $1/2$.

\section*{The finite encoder and honest decoder}

Set
\[
 A=\sqrt{80/\varepsilon},\qquad
 h=\frac{\sqrt n\,\varepsilon^{3/2}}{500},\qquad
 B=nR+A\sqrt n.
\]
Partition $\mathbb R^3$ into half-open cubes $C_k$ of side $h$, centered
at $z_k=hk$ for $k\in\mathbb Z^3$. Retain cubes whose closures meet
the ball $\{|x|\le B\}$ and whose fine preimages have positive $q_0$
measure. All other records receive one tail label. Write
\[
 A_k=\{(L,u):Lu\in C_k\}.
\]
The actual encoding effect for a retained label is the finite POVM
effect
\[
 E_k=W^*\left(\bigoplus_{L\in\mathcal L_n}
  (L+1)\int_{\{u:Lu\in C_k\}}\proj{L,u}\dd\mu(u)
                       \otimes I_{m_L}\right)W.
\]
The tail effect is $I-\sum_kE_k\ge0$. Thus the construction is a
single finite POVM, with no measurement history stored after it.

For each retained cube choose the fixed reference
\[
 c_k=\operatorname{proj}_{\{|c|\le R\}}(z_k/n),
\]
the Euclidean radial projection onto the source ball. It obeys
$|c_k|\le R\le1/2$. Strict positivity in \eqref{eq:likelihood}
implies $q_{c_k}(A_k)>0$, so the decoder state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cellstate}
 \omega_k=\frac1{q_{c_k}(A_k)}
            \int_{A_k}\Omega_{L,u}\dd q_{c_k}(L,u)
\end{equation}
is well-defined, positive, and normalized. The decoder prepares it
given label $k$. Equivalently, it uses fresh randomness to draw a fine
record from $q_{c_k}(\cdot\mid A_k)$ and then prepares
$\Omega_{L,u}$. This conditional distribution depends only on the
known label and fixed reference, not on the unknown input parameter.
The tail decoder prepares any fixed state, for example
$(I/2)^{\otimes n}$.

For even $n$, a cube containing $0$ may receive all $L=0$ records.
Equation \eqref{eq:cellstate} integrates over their full preimage, and
$\Omega_{0,u}$ is independent of $u$. The decoder does not attempt to
invert $X=Lu$. This removes the latent-collision ambiguity explicitly.

\section*{Error and alphabet size}

Fix any $|b|\le R$, and define its good fine event
\[
 G_b=\{(L,u):|Lu-nb|\le A\sqrt n\}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:moment} and Markov's inequality,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 q_b(G_b^{\mathsf c})\le\frac{20}{A^2}=\frac\varepsilon4.
\end{equation}
Every good record is in a retained cube, apart from fine preimages of
$q_0$ measure zero, which also have $q_b$ measure zero:
$|Lu|\le nR+A\sqrt n=B$.
Call a retained cube good if its preimage intersects $G_b$.
For such a cube choose $x_0=Lu\in C_k$ with
$|x_0-nb|\le A\sqrt n$. Since $b$ is in the projection ball,
$|b-c_k|\le|b-z_k/n|$. One can verify this projection inequality
directly: if $w=z_k/n$ lies outside the ball, subtracting
$|b-Rw/|w||^2$ from $|b-w|^2$ gives
$(|w|-R)(|w|+R-2b\cdot w/|w|)\ge0$. Hence
\[
 |b-c_k|\le\frac A{\sqrt n}+\frac{\sqrt3h}{2n}.
\]
By \eqref{eq:loglikelihood}--\eqref{eq:Lipschitz}, the oscillation of
$\ln(\dd q_b/\dd q_{c_k})$ on $A_k$ is at most
\begin{align*}
 \lambda
 &\le7\sqrt3h\left(\frac A{\sqrt n}+\frac{\sqrt3h}{2n}\right)\\
 &=\frac{7\sqrt{240}}{500}\varepsilon
          +\frac{21}{500000}\varepsilon^3\le\frac\varepsilon4.
\end{align*}
For the last inequality use $\sqrt{240}<16$ and
$\varepsilon^3\le\varepsilon/16$.
Also $\lambda\le1/16$, so the power series gives
$e^\lambda-1\le\lambda/(1-\lambda)\le16\lambda/15$.
Thus every good cube has conditional replacement error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:goodTV}
 \TV(q_b(\cdot\mid A_k),q_{c_k}(\cdot\mid A_k))
 \le\frac{4\varepsilon}{15}.
\end{equation}

Mixtures of the states $\Omega_{L,u}$ contract total variation into
half trace distance, since
$\T(\int\Omega\dd P,\int\Omega\dd Q)\le\TV(P,Q)$.
Weight \eqref{eq:goodTV} by $q_b(A_k)$ over good cubes. Bad retained
cubes and the tail are disjoint subsets of $G_b^{\mathsf c}$, so their
combined contribution is at most $\varepsilon/4$, not one new tail
allowance per label. The error added by all finite-cell replacements is
therefore at most
\[
 \frac\varepsilon4+\frac{4\varepsilon}{15}
 =\frac{31\varepsilon}{60}<\varepsilon.
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:fine-error} proves \eqref{eq:error}.

For the count, any retained cube center has each coordinate of magnitude
at most $B+h/2$, so there are at most $(2B/h+3)^3$ retained cubes.
Let $Q=\sqrt{1+\kappa}\,\varepsilon^{-2}\ge1$. Because
$\sqrt\varepsilon\le1/2$ and $\sqrt{80}<9$,
\[
 \frac Bh=500\varepsilon^{-2}
                  (\sqrt{\varepsilon\kappa}+\sqrt{80})
 \le4750Q.
\]
Including one tail label gives
\[
 D\le1+(9500Q+3)^3
 \le(9503^3+1)Q^3<9504^3Q^3<10^{12}Q^3.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:D}, and completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:archive}.

\paragraph{The physical memory model.}
Let
\[
 \rho_b=(I+b\cdot\sigma)/2,\qquad b\in\mathbb R^3,\quad |b|\le1,
\]
and suppose the supplied input is $\rho_b^{\otimes n}$, with one fixed but
unknown $b$. The archive consists of a completely positive trace-preserving
encoder $\mathcal E_n$ to $m_n$ physical qubits, a fixed memoryless channel
$\mathcal A_{\gamma,\nu}^{\otimes m_n}$, and a completely positive
trace-preserving decoder $\mathcal D_n$ back to $n$ qubits. The encoder may
produce arbitrary mixed states and arbitrary entanglement across the memory
qubits. All input-dependent retained information passes through these
$m_n$ channel uses. There is no noiseless retained side register, feedback,
or preshared entanglement. Fixed ancillas and discarded processing workspace
are permitted. Define
\[
 H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m |1\rangle\langle1|_j.
\]
For a fixed $E\in[0,1]$, impose the expected energy condition on \emph{each
original source state}:
\begin{equation}\label{nm:energy}
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[H_{m_n}\,
       \mathcal E_n(\rho_b^{\otimes n})\right]\le m_n E
 \quad\text{for every allowed }b.
\end{equation}
This condition averages the encoder's internal randomness; it does not
require each realization to have a definite excitation number. Let
\begin{equation}\label{nm:error}
 e_n=\sup_b \frac12\left\|
 \mathcal D_n\mathcal A_{\gamma,\nu}^{\otimes m_n}
       \mathcal E_n(\rho_b^{\otimes n})-\rho_b^{\otimes n}
 \right\|_1.
\end{equation}
The parameters $\gamma,\nu,E$ are fixed as $n\to\infty$.

\subsection{The constrained channel theorem, with a heterogeneous extension}

Use entropy in nats temporarily. Set
\[
 h(x)=-x\ln x-(1-x)\ln(1-x),\qquad
 g(u)=h\!\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4u}}2\right),
\]
with the continuous endpoint conventions, and define
\begin{align}
 t_{\gamma,\nu}(p)&=(1-\gamma)p+\gamma\nu,\\
 v_{\gamma,\nu}(p)&=\gamma\nu(1-\nu)
                 +\gamma(1-\gamma)(p-\nu)^2,\\
 e_{\gamma,\nu}(p)&=g(v_{\gamma,\nu}(p)),\\
 F_{\gamma,\nu}(p)&=
    \frac{h(t_{\gamma,\nu}(p))-e_{\gamma,\nu}(p)}{\ln2}.
\end{align}
The channel acts by
\[
 \mathcal A_{\gamma,\nu}
 \begin{pmatrix}1-p&z\\\bar z&p\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}1-t_{\gamma,\nu}(p)&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\,z\\
       \sqrt{1-\gamma}\,\bar z&t_{\gamma,\nu}(p)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The input population $p$ is measured in the displayed computational basis.

\paragraph{Exact external ingredient from family 276.}
For every finite-dimensional channel $\mathcal N$ and every unit vector
\[
 \psi=\sqrt{1-p}|0\rangle\psi_0+\sqrt p|1\rangle\psi_1,
\]
where the normalized branch vectors need not be orthogonal, the source's
Lemma \texttt{partner-branch} states
\begin{equation}\label{nm:branch}
 S[(\mathcal A_{\gamma,\nu}\otimes\mathcal N)(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)]
 \ge e_{\gamma,\nu}(p)
       +(1-p)S[\mathcal N(|\psi_0\rangle\langle\psi_0|)]
       +pS[\mathcal N(|\psi_1\rangle\langle\psi_1|)].
\end{equation}
The source also proves convexity of $e_{\gamma,\nu}$ on $[0,1]$.
These are stronger inputs than unconstrained additivity of minimum output
entropy. They retain the input population needed for an energy constraint.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous finite-block consequence.}
For $\mathcal N_m=\bigotimes_{j=1}^m\mathcal A_{\gamma_j,\nu_j}$ and an
arbitrary density operator $\tau$ on the entire input block, let
$p_j=\operatorname{Tr}(|1\rangle\langle1|_j\tau)$.
Induction using \eqref{nm:branch} gives
\begin{equation}\label{nm:hetero-entropy}
 S[\mathcal N_m(\tau)]\ge\sum_{j=1}^m e_{\gamma_j,\nu_j}(p_j).
\end{equation}
For a pure state, at the induction step the later-site populations obey
$p_j=(1-p_1)p_{j|0}+p_1p_{j|1}$, irrespective of branch orthogonality.
Convexity of each site-specific $e_j$ combines the two branch bounds.
For mixed states, entropy concavity followed by convexity of $e_j$ gives
the same conclusion from a pure decomposition. Nothing requires the
parameters of different channel factors to agree.

For any finite ensemble $\{q_a,\tau_a\}$, let
$\bar p_j=\sum_a q_a\operatorname{Tr}(|1\rangle\langle1|_j\tau_a)$.
The entropy of the average channel output is at most
$\sum_j h(t_{\gamma_j,\nu_j}(\bar p_j))$, by dephasing in the
computational basis and classical subadditivity. Equation
\eqref{nm:hetero-entropy} and convexity bound the average individual
output entropy below by $\sum_j e_{\gamma_j,\nu_j}(\bar p_j)$.
Hence its Holevo information, in bits, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{nm:hetero-holevo}
 \chi(\{q_a,\mathcal N_m(\tau_a)\})
       \le\sum_j F_{\gamma_j,\nu_j}(\bar p_j).
\end{equation}
Equality for any prescribed $p_1,\ldots,p_m$ is attained by the uniform
ensemble of products of
\[
 \phi_{j,\pm}=\sqrt{1-p_j}|0\rangle\pm\sqrt{p_j}|1\rangle.
\]
Each codeword in that product ensemble has expected excitation $p_j$
at site $j$. Therefore, under a mean excitation cost
$\sum_j c_j\bar p_j\le B$, $c_j\ge0$, the exact constrained block
Holevo optimum is
\begin{equation}\label{nm:hetero-opt}
 \max_{\substack{0\le p_j\le1\\\sum_j c_jp_j\le B}}
                 \sum_j F_{\gamma_j,\nu_j}(p_j).
\end{equation}
Every $F_j$ is concave, as $h\circ t_j$ is concave and $e_j$ convex.
Thus this is a separable concave allocation problem. No unrestricted
additivity theorem alone would justify this constrained formula.

\paragraph{Identical channel and operational capacity.}
For fixed $\gamma,\nu,E$, define
\begin{equation}\label{nm:CE}
 C_E=\max_{0\le p\le E}F_{\gamma,\nu}(p).
\end{equation}
If the average input ensemble satisfies
$\sum_j\bar p_j\le mE$, concavity and Jensen imply
\[
 \chi\le\sum_j F(\bar p_j)
       \le m F\!\left(m^{-1}\sum_j\bar p_j\right)\le mC_E.
\]
The uniform product phase-pair ensemble at a maximizing $p_E\le E$
attains $mC_E$. Thus the constrained Holevo information is exactly
$mC_E$ at every block length, including arbitrary entangled block inputs.

The finite-alphabet classical--quantum HSW coding theorem applied to
$\{\phi_{+,p_E},\phi_{-,p_E}\}$ achieves every rate below $C_E$.
Every product codeword has expectation $mp_E\le mE$, \emph{individually},
so no average-over-messages relaxation of the energy constraint is needed.
A code with average error $\epsilon_m$ can be expurgated to at least half
its messages, each with error at most $2\epsilon_m$, without changing any
remaining codeword's energy. Hence the same limiting rate is achievable
with maximal error tending to zero and per-codeword expected energy.
The converse follows from the Holevo bound and Fano. Consequently $C_E$
is the energy-constrained unassisted classical capacity in this model.
This invocation is existential: no polynomial-time decoder is asserted.

For checks, if $\gamma=0$, then
$C_E=h_2(\min\{E,1/2\})$; if $\gamma=1$ or $E=0$, then $C_E=0$.
When $\gamma<1$ and $E>0$, a phase pair with $0<p<\min\{E,1\}$ has
distinct output states and positive Holevo information, so $C_E>0$.

\subsection{Converse for any noisy archive}

All logarithms in the remainder are base two.
For $n\ge6$, divide $n$ input copies into three groups of
$\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ and measure Pauli $X,Y,Z$ respectively.
The vector $\widehat b$ of sample means satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{nm:tomography}
 \mathbb E_b\|\widehat b-b\|_2^2\le 18/n.
\end{equation}
This bound needs no asymptotic normality or quantum LAN theorem.
For hypotheses separated by $d_n=s_n/\sqrt n$, nearest-neighbor
decoding is correct whenever $\|\widehat b-b\|<d_n/2$.
Markov's inequality bounds its failure probability by $72/s_n^2$.
Measuring the reconstructed state in \eqref{nm:error} changes the
entire outcome law in total variation by at most $e_n$.
Thus an archive followed by this tomography measurement transmits the
packing index with maximal error at most
\begin{equation}\label{nm:eta}
 \eta_n=e_n+72/s_n^2.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Packing constants.}
On the sphere $|b|=\delta_n$, choose a maximal $d_n$-separated set,
where $d_n\le2\delta_n$. Its chord-radius $d_n$ caps cover the sphere.
A cap of that chord radius has exact area $\pi d_n^2$; hence its
cardinality $N_n$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{nm:shell-packing}
 N_n\ge4\delta_n^2/d_n^2=4\kappa_n/s_n^2,
 \qquad\kappa_n=n\delta_n^2.
\end{equation}
For the ball $|b|\le R_n$, a maximal $d_n$-separated set covers the
ball by Euclidean radius-$d_n$ balls. Comparing volumes, even though
covering balls can protrude outside the allowed ball, gives
\begin{equation}\label{nm:ball-packing}
 N_n\ge(R_n/d_n)^3=\kappa_n^{3/2}/s_n^3,
 \qquad\kappa_n=nR_n^2.
\end{equation}
A finite maximal packing exists by compactness. Distinct points on the
packing are actual allowed source states, with no requirement that
the archive acquire a classical parameter supplied separately.

\paragraph{Fano through the physical channel.}
Give the packing index $I$ the uniform distribution on $[N_n]$ and
form the input ensemble
$\tau_i=\mathcal E_n(\rho_{b_i}^{\otimes n})$.
These states may be entangled across all $m_n$ channel inputs.
Condition \eqref{nm:energy} applies to every $\tau_i$, so also to
their average. Any decoded packing-index estimate is a measurement of
the channel output, incorporating the archive decoder. If its actual
average error is $r_n$, the Holevo bound, data processing, and Fano give
\begin{equation}\label{nm:fano-exact}
 (1-r_n)\log N_n\le m_nC_E+h_2(r_n),\qquad r_n\le\eta_n.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $\eta_n\le1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{nm:fano-bound}
 m_nC_E\ge(1-\eta_n)\log N_n-h_2(\eta_n)
            \ge(1-\eta_n)\log N_n-1.
\end{equation}
The first inequality uses monotonicity of $h_2$ on $[0,1/2]$; the
second form avoids a delicate small-error entropy estimate.
No upper bound on the encoder's circuit size, workspace, or input
preparation complexity was used. Entangling the physical memory inputs
is already included in the channel Holevo converse.

Take $\kappa_n\to\infty$, $e_n\to0$, and choose, for example,
$s_n=\ln(e+\kappa_n)$. Then $s_n\to\infty$,
$\log s_n=o(\log\kappa_n)$, $s_n=o(\sqrt{\kappa_n})$, and
$\eta_n\to0$. Equations \eqref{nm:shell-packing}--\eqref{nm:fano-bound}
give
\begin{align}
 m_n C_E&\ge(1-o(1))\log\kappa_n
                   &&\text{on a known-radius sphere},\label{nm:shell-lower}\\
 m_n C_E&\ge(3/2-o(1))\log\kappa_n
                   &&\text{on the entire unknown-radius ball}.
                   \label{nm:ball-lower}
\end{align}
These lower bounds require only the original iid source, the trace
error condition, the source-wise expected energy condition, and the
channel entropy ingredient. In particular they do not require the
full-ball archive theorem.
If $C_E=0$, \eqref{nm:fano-bound} contradicts diverging $N_n$ and
$\eta_n\to0$, so no such vanishing-error archive exists in either regime.

 D_{n,\delta,\epsilon}

Its input is the quantum source itself; the parameter $\delta$ is known,

Choose $\epsilon_n=1/\ln(e+\kappa_n)$ for sufficiently large $n$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{nm:QK-rate}
 \log D_n=\log\kappa_n+O(\log\ln\kappa_n),
 \qquad e_{\rm archive,n}\to0.
\end{equation}
Encode each classical archive label as a product phase-pair codeword
for the fixed physical channel, and decode that label by a collective
measurement before applying the classical archive's state-preparation

of its archive label. Indeed, under the joint law of the true and decoded

 m_n\le\left\lceil\frac{\log D_n+1}{r_n}\right\rceil

for an attainable sequence, conditional on the family-276 entropy theorem

This is a matching leading coefficient, not a reliability criterion for

including entangled physical memory inputs; the upper construction uses

\subsection{Total expected excitation with an unbounded number of cells}

The preceding result fixes expected excitation per cell $E$. A different
resource question permits any finite number $m_n$ of physical cells and
counts only the total expected input excitation, subject to
\begin{equation}\label{nm:total-cost}
 \operatorname{Tr}[H_{m_n}\mathcal E_n(\rho_b^{\otimes n})]
       \le\mathcal Q_n\quad\text{for every allowed }b.
\end{equation}
No cell-count bound is imposed in this subsection. The noise parameters
remain fixed, and the channel environment is not counted as input excitation.

Suppose $0<\gamma<1$ and $0<\nu\le1$. Put $a=\gamma\nu\in(0,1)$.
Since $v(0)=a(1-a)$, $t(0)=a$, and $g(a(1-a))=h(a)$,
$F(0)=0$. For $a\ne1/2$,
\[
 g'(a(1-a))=
 \frac{\ln((1-a)/a)}{1-2a},\qquad
 v'(0)=-2a(1-\gamma),\qquad t'(0)=1-\gamma.
\]
Differentiation therefore gives the finite positive slope
\begin{equation}\label{nm:c0}
 c_0:=F'(0)=
 \frac{1-\gamma}{1-2a}\log_2\!\frac{1-a}{a}.
\end{equation}
The continuous value at $a=1/2$ is
\begin{equation}\label{nm:c0-half}
 c_0=\frac{2(1-\gamma)}{\ln2}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $\ln((1-a)/a)/(1-2a)\to2$ there. The numerator and denominator
in \eqref{nm:c0} have the same sign on either side of $1/2$.

Concavity of $F$ and $F(0)=0$ imply the supporting-line inequality
$F(p)\le c_0p$ for every $p\in[0,1]$. Thus
\eqref{nm:hetero-holevo} in the identical-channel case gives
\begin{equation}\label{nm:cost-holevo}
 \chi\le c_0\sum_{j=1}^{m_n}\bar p_j\le c_0\mathcal Q_n,
\end{equation}
for arbitrary entangled input ensembles obeying \eqref{nm:total-cost}.
The packing/Fano argument now applies with $m_nC_E$ replaced by
$c_0\mathcal Q_n$. Consequently every vanishing-error archive sequence
obeys
\begin{align}
 \liminf_n\frac{\mathcal Q_n}{\log_2(n\delta_n^2)}
       &\ge\frac1{c_0} &&\text{on the known-radius sphere},
       \label{nm:shell-cost-lower}\\
 \liminf_n\frac{\mathcal Q_n}{\log_2(nR_n^2)}
       &\ge\frac3{2c_0} &&\text{on the entire ball}.
       \label{nm:ball-cost-lower}
\end{align}
No fixed-$E$ limit or unproved uniform coding theorem is needed for this
converse: the supporting-line inequality is valid at every finite block.

For attainability, first fix $p\in(0,1)$, so the binary phase-pair
alphabet is fixed. Provided the corresponding classical source archive
upper bound is accepted, HSW with maximal-error expurgation gives
\[
 \mathcal Q_n=m_np
       =\left(\frac{r p}{F(p)}+o(1)\right)\log_2\kappa_n,
 \qquad r=1\text{ for the sphere},\quad r=3/2\text{ for the ball}.
\]
Here $\kappa_n$ has its respective definition, and $F(p)>0$ for
$0<p<1$. Send the source archive label by such a code; the energy bound
holds for each label and hence for each original source state.
Only after establishing this statement for each fixed $p$ do we take
$p\downarrow0$. Since $F(p)/p\to c_0$, the infimum of the displayed
asymptotic coefficient is $r/c_0$.

A matching single sequence can be obtained by a diagonal choice: choose
fixed $p_\ell\downarrow0$ and, for each $\ell$, choose a sufficiently
large source-size threshold so that its fixed-alphabet codes fit the
archive labels at rate at least $(1-1/\ell)F(p_\ell)$ and maximal error
at most $1/\ell$. Such thresholds exist because $\log\kappa_n\to\infty$.
Choose the thresholds increasingly and let $\ell(n)\to\infty$ slowly.
This produces vanishing source reconstruction error and
\begin{equation}\label{nm:cost-match}
 \mathcal Q_n=\left(\frac r{c_0}+o(1)\right)\log_2\kappa_n
\end{equation}
with unrestricted, possibly very large, cell count. This is the ordinary
capacity-per-unit-cost deduction with a newly explicit slope from the
inspected thermal source lemma. It is not an efficient decoder or a
uniform finite-block statement for an arbitrarily prescribed $p_n\to0$.

\paragraph{Pure zero-cost output at zero temperature.}
When $\nu=0$ and $0<\gamma<1$, the preceding finite-slope formula does
not apply. Instead
\[
 F(p)=\frac{h((1-\gamma)p)-g(\gamma(1-\gamma)p^2)}{\ln2}
     =(1-\gamma)p\log_2(1/p)+O(p)\quad(p\downarrow0).
\]
The second entropy term is $O(p^2\log(1/p))$, so $F(p)/p\to\infty$.
The same fixed-$p$ coding argument, followed by a slow diagonal limit,
therefore supplies archive sequences with
\begin{equation}\label{nm:zero-temp-cost}
 \mathcal Q_n/\log_2\kappa_n\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
if the appropriate source archive upper bound is accepted and cell count
is unrestricted. The noiseless endpoint $\gamma=0$ has the analogous
infinite slope $h_2(p)/p\to\infty$. The replacement endpoint $\gamma=1$
has $F\equiv0$ and supports no such archive.

Equation \eqref{nm:zero-temp-cost} concerns excitation per growing
information requirement; it asserts neither zero total excitation nor
uniform reliability at fixed cell count. In fact ordinary amplitude
damping, with $0<\gamma<1$, forces total excitation to diverge whenever
a growing number of messages is transmitted with vanishing error.
To see this, write $P=|0^m\rangle\langle0^m|$ and let
$\omega_i=\mathcal A_{\gamma,0}^{\otimes m}(\tau_i)$, where
$\operatorname{Tr}(H_m\tau_i)\le\mathcal Q$. The vacuum effect pulled
back through amplitude damping is $\gamma^{H_m}$. Therefore, by
Jensen's inequality for the convex function $x\mapsto\gamma^x$,
\[
 q_i:=\operatorname{Tr}(P\omega_i)
     =\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^{H_m}\tau_i)
     \ge\gamma^{\operatorname{Tr}(H_m\tau_i)}\ge\gamma^{\mathcal Q}.
\]
For any $N$-outcome discrimination POVM $\{M_i\}$, put
$a_i=\langle0^m|M_i|0^m\rangle$, so $\sum_i a_i=1$.
Purify $\omega_i$ and split the purification by $P$ and $I-P$.
Cauchy--Schwarz for $M_i^{1/2}$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(M_i\omega_i)
       \le\bigl(\sqrt{q_i a_i}+\sqrt{1-q_i}\bigr)^2.
\]
Sum the two squared terms over equiprobable messages, and bound their
cross term by Cauchy--Schwarz again. The first average is at most $1/N$
and the second is at most $1-\gamma^{\mathcal Q}$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{nm:vacuum-success}
 P_{\rm correct}\le
     \left(N^{-1/2}+\sqrt{1-\gamma^{\mathcal Q}}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Any archive decoder and subsequent measurement can be pulled back to
such a POVM. Thus $N_n\to\infty$ and $P_{\rm correct}\to1$ imply
$\gamma^{\mathcal Q_n}\to0$, equivalently $\mathcal Q_n\to\infty$.
This remains compatible with \eqref{nm:zero-temp-cost}.
For the noiseless endpoint $\gamma=0$, the elementary input-vacuum
bound instead gives $P_{\rm correct}\le1/N+\sqrt{\mathcal Q}$ for
$\mathcal Q<1$, and only the lower conclusion
$\mathcal Q_n\ge1-o(1)$ from that argument.

\section{Total excitation at zero temperature, with unrestricted cell count}

Fix $0<\gamma<1$ and let $\mathcal A_{\gamma,0}$ be the qubit
amplitude-damping channel, with loss probability $\gamma$:
\[
 K_0=|0\rangle\langle0|+\sqrt{1-\gamma}|1\rangle\langle1|,
 \qquad K_1=\sqrt\gamma|0\rangle\langle1|.
\]
For $m$ physical cells let
$H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m|1\rangle\langle1|_j$ and
$\Pi_m=|0^m\rangle\langle0^m|$.
The cell count $m$ is finite and fixed independently of the transmitted
message, but it is not bounded as a resource in this section. No retained
input-dependent noiseless register or receiver-side entanglement assistance
is available. The counted cost is expected \emph{input} excitation.
All logarithms denoted $\ln$ are natural.

\subsection{A finite-message converse allowing arbitrary quantum coherence}

For $q\ge0$, write $k=\lfloor q\rfloor$ and $\theta=q-k$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{pe:integer-envelope}
 G_\gamma(q)=(1-\theta)\gamma^k+\theta\gamma^{k+1}.
\end{equation}
This is the decreasing, convex, piecewise-linear interpolation of
$\gamma^k$ on the nonnegative integers. In particular
$G_\gamma(q)\ge\gamma^q$.

Let $\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N$ be arbitrary $m$-qubit density operators,
with equiprobable messages and $N\ge2$. Put
\[
 \sigma_i=\mathcal A_{\gamma,0}^{\otimes m}(\tau_i),\qquad
 \overline Q=\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(H_m\tau_i),\qquad
 a_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_m\sigma_i),\quad
 \overline a=\frac1N\sum_i a_i.
\]
For every decoding POVM $M_1,\ldots,M_N$,
\begin{equation}\label{pe:success}
 P_{\rm s}:=\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(M_i\sigma_i)
 \le\left(\sqrt{1-\overline a}+\frac1{\sqrt N}\right)^2
 \le\left(\sqrt{1-G_\gamma(\overline Q)}
                  +\frac1{\sqrt N}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
These bounds can of course be capped at one. In particular, the average
decoding error satisfies
\begin{align}
 P_{\rm e}
 &\ge G_\gamma(\overline Q)
        -2\sqrt{\frac{1-G_\gamma(\overline Q)}N}-\frac1N,
       \label{pe:error-sharp}\\
 &\ge\gamma^{\overline Q}-\frac2{\sqrt N}-\frac1N.
       \label{pe:error-simple}
\end{align}
Negative lower bounds are vacuous. The same conclusions hold with a budget
$Q\ge\overline Q$ in place of $\overline Q$.

\paragraph{The vacuum identity and the integer correction.}
The adjoint one-cell channel maps $|0\rangle\langle0|$ to
$|0\rangle\langle0|+\gamma|1\rangle\langle1|$. Tensoring gives the exact
identity
\begin{equation}\label{pe:vacuum-identity}
 a_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^{H_m}\tau_i).
\end{equation}
It holds for coherent and entangled inputs. Since the spectrum of $H_m$
is integer-valued, $\gamma^{H_m}=G_\gamma(H_m)$. Applying the ordinary
scalar Jensen inequality to the spectral probability distribution of
$H_m$ in the average input state proves
\[
 \overline a\ge G_\gamma(\overline Q)\ge\gamma^{\overline Q}.
\]
Convexity of $G_\gamma$ follows because its slope on $[k,k+1]$ is
$-(1-\gamma)\gamma^k$, an increasing sequence of negative numbers.

\paragraph{Why off-diagonal blocks do not invalidate the converse.}
Write $P_m=I-\Pi_m$ and set
\[
 x_i=\operatorname{Tr}(M_i\Pi_m\sigma_i\Pi_m),\qquad
 y_i=\operatorname{Tr}(M_iP_m\sigma_iP_m),\qquad
 z_i=\operatorname{Tr}(M_i\Pi_m\sigma_iP_m).
\]
Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz, applied to
$M_i^{1/2}\Pi_m\sigma_i^{1/2}$ and
$M_i^{1/2}P_m\sigma_i^{1/2}$, gives
$|z_i|\le\sqrt{x_i y_i}$.
Let $x=N^{-1}\sum_i x_i$ and $y=N^{-1}\sum_i y_i$.
The POVM completeness relation and $a_i\le1$ imply
\[
 x=\frac1N\sum_i a_i\langle0^m|M_i|0^m\rangle\le\frac1N,
 \qquad y\le\frac1N\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}(P_m\sigma_iP_m)
                =1-\overline a.
\]
One more scalar Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
$P_{\rm s}\le x+y+2\sqrt{xy}$, proving \eqref{pe:success}.
Any CPTP processing before the final decision is absorbed into the POVM
by its adjoint. No Holevo additivity or capacity theorem is needed.

\paragraph{A caution against treating the vacuum as a classical erasure.}
In general $\sigma_i\not\ge a_i\Pi_m$: a nonzero vacuum off-diagonal
block makes that purported operator inequality false. For example, the
equiprobable one-qubit inputs
$\sqrt{1-p}|0\rangle\pm\sqrt p|1\rangle$ have energy $p$, vacuum
probability $1-(1-\gamma)p$, and optimal success probability
\[
 \frac12+\sqrt{(1-\gamma)p(1-p)}.
\]
At $\gamma=p=1/2$, this is $(1+1/\sqrt2)/2>3/4$, whereas the bound
$P_{\rm s}\le1-\overline a+1/N$ would give $3/4$.
The cross term in \eqref{pe:success} is therefore essential at finite $N$.

\subsection{Pulse-position attainability and an additive asymptotic match}

For message $i\in[N]$, let $|e_i\rangle$ denote the computational basis
vector on $N$ cells with one excitation at position $i$. The codeword
$|e_i\rangle^{\otimes k}$ uses $m=kN$ cells and exactly $k$ excitations.
Measure the output in the computational basis. Any surviving excitation
identifies $i$ without error. On the all-vacuum outcome, guess uniformly
in $[N]$. The average and maximal error are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{pe:ppm-integer}
 P_{\rm e}=(1-1/N)\gamma^k.
\end{equation}
The probability that all $k$ excitations are lost is $\gamma^k$.
This is an elementary pulse-position erasure code, with no collective
quantum receiver required.

For a real budget $q=k+\theta$, use $m=(k+1)N$ cells and the mixed
codeword
\begin{equation}\label{pe:ppm-mixed}
 \tau_i=(1-\theta)
    (|e_i\rangle\langle e_i|)^{\otimes k}\otimes\Pi_N
  +\theta(|e_i\rangle\langle e_i|)^{\otimes(k+1)}.
\end{equation}
Every codeword has expected excitation $q$. The same measurement gives
average and maximal error
\begin{equation}\label{pe:ppm-real}
 P_{\rm e}=(1-1/N)G_\gamma(q).
\end{equation}
The random choice is made by the encoder; its outcome is not retained in
any side register. If $\theta=0$, the unused final block can be removed.

Let $Q_{\rm av}(N,\eta)$ be the infimum of average expected excitation
over all finite cell counts and all codes with average error at most
$\eta$. Let $Q_{\rm max}(N,\eta)$ be the corresponding infimum of the
maximum codeword expected excitation, requiring maximal error at most
$\eta$. For $0<\eta<1-1/N$, define
\begin{align}
 A_N(\eta)&=1-\left(\sqrt{1-\eta}-N^{-1/2}\right)^2
           =\eta+2\sqrt{(1-\eta)/N}-1/N,\label{pe:A}\\
 B_N(\eta)&=\frac\eta{1-1/N}.\label{pe:B}
\end{align}
Then \eqref{pe:success} and \eqref{pe:ppm-real} give the finite bracket
\begin{equation}\label{pe:finite-bracket}
 G_\gamma^{-1}(A_N(\eta))
 \le Q_{\rm av}(N,\eta)
 \le Q_{\rm max}(N,\eta)
 \le G_\gamma^{-1}(B_N(\eta)).
\end{equation}
Here $G_\gamma^{-1}:(0,1]\to[0,\infty)$ is the ordinary decreasing
inverse. For $t\in(0,1]$, it has the explicit form
\begin{equation}\label{pe:G-inverse}
 G_\gamma^{-1}(t)
 =k+\frac{1-t/\gamma^k}{1-\gamma},\qquad
 k=\left\lfloor\frac{\ln(1/t)}{\ln(1/\gamma)}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}

The upper and lower inverse bounds differ by at most
\begin{equation}\label{pe:bracket-gap}
 G_\gamma^{-1}(B_N(\eta))-G_\gamma^{-1}(A_N(\eta))
 \le\frac{2}{(1-\gamma)\eta\sqrt N}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the magnitude of $G_\gamma'$ is at least
$(1-\gamma)G_\gamma$ on every linear piece. Thus the inverse difference
is at most $\ln(A_N/B_N)/(1-\gamma)$, and
$0\le A_N-B_N\le2/\sqrt N$, $B_N\ge\eta$.
The nonnegativity follows either directly from \eqref{pe:A}--\eqref{pe:B}
or from the feasibility of the pulse-position upper bound.

Consequently, if $N\to\infty$ and $\eta_N\sqrt N\to\infty$, with
$0<\eta_N<1-1/N$, then
\begin{equation}\label{pe:additive-match}
 Q_{\rm av}(N,\eta_N)=Q_{\rm max}(N,\eta_N)
       =G_\gamma^{-1}(\eta_N)+o(1).
\end{equation}
The equality denotes equality of the asymptotic expressions, not an
exact finite-$N$ equality of the two optimization problems.
If additionally $\eta_N\to0$, the integer correction is bounded by one,
so
\begin{equation}\label{pe:log-law}
 Q_{\rm av}(N,\eta_N)\sim Q_{\rm max}(N,\eta_N)
       \sim\frac{\ln(1/\eta_N)}{\ln(1/\gamma)}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $\eta\in(0,1)$, the limit is instead the explicit constant
$G_\gamma^{-1}(\eta)$; dropping the integer correction would not give
the exact limiting constant. In all cases, vanishing error for
$N\to\infty$ requires $Q\to\infty$, by \eqref{pe:error-simple}.

\subsection{A pure-reference channel principle with binary excitation cost}

The same energy law holds for a broader finite-dimensional channel class.
Let $\Phi:A\to B$ be any CPTP map, with $\dim A\ge2$, and let $|0\rangle$
and $|v\rangle$ be unit vectors satisfying
\[
 \Phi(|0\rangle\langle0|)=|v\rangle\langle v|.
\]
Give each cell the binary excitation observable
$H=I_A-|0\rangle\langle0|$, and put $H_m=\sum_jH_j$.
The input and output reference vectors may belong to different spaces.
Define the effect $E_\Phi=\Phi^*(|v\rangle\langle v|)$.
Since $0\le E_\Phi\le I_A$ and
$\langle0|E_\Phi|0\rangle=1$, positivity of $I_A-E_\Phi$ implies
$(I_A-E_\Phi)|0\rangle=0$. Hermiticity therefore gives the block form
\[
 E_\Phi=|0\rangle\langle0|\oplus B_\Phi
       \quad\text{on }\mathbb C|0\rangle\oplus|0\rangle^\perp.
\]
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{pe:general-channel-gamma}
 \gamma_\Phi:=\lambda_{\min}(B_\Phi)\in(0,1).
\end{equation}
Then $E_\Phi\ge|0\rangle\langle0|+
\gamma_\Phi(I_A-|0\rangle\langle0|)=\gamma_\Phi^H$.
Tensor products preserve this positive-operator order, so every $m$-cell
input $\tau$ has reference-output probability
\begin{equation}\label{pe:general-channel-vacuum}
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[(|v\rangle\langle v|)^{\otimes m}
                           \Phi^{\otimes m}(\tau)\right]
 =\operatorname{Tr}(E_\Phi^{\otimes m}\tau)
 \ge\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma_\Phi^{H_m}\tau).
\end{equation}
For an equiprobable ensemble of average cost $Q$, the right side averages
to at least $G_{\gamma_\Phi}(Q)$. The reference-output projector is rank one,
so the block Cauchy--Schwarz proof of \eqref{pe:success} applies verbatim.

To attain the same pulse-position upper bound, choose a unit eigenvector
$|w\rangle\perp|0\rangle$ of $B_\Phi$ with eigenvalue $\gamma_\Phi$.
Put $|w\rangle$ at the active position and $|0\rangle$ at all inactive
positions. The independent output tests
$\{|v\rangle\langle v|,I_B-|v\rangle\langle v|\}$ have exactly zero
false-positive probability on inactive cells. An active cell clicks with
probability $1-\gamma_\Phi$, even if its output has coherence about $|v\rangle$.
Thus $k$ repetitions and uniform guessing on no clicks give maximal error
$(1-1/N)\gamma_\Phi^k$; mixing adjacent repetition counts gives
$(1-1/N)G_{\gamma_\Phi}(Q)$ at cost $Q$ for every message.

Consequently \eqref{pe:finite-bracket}--\eqref{pe:log-law} hold for this
channel with $\gamma$ replaced by $\gamma_\Phi$, including arbitrary
entangled codewords in the converse. In particular, with unrestricted
cell count, $N\to\infty$, and $\eta_N\sqrt N\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{pe:general-channel-match}
 Q_{\rm av}^{\Phi}(N,\eta_N)=Q_{\rm max}^{\Phi}(N,\eta_N)
        =G_{\gamma_\Phi}^{-1}(\eta_N)+o(1).
\end{equation}
If also $\eta_N\to0$, its leading value is
$\ln(1/\eta_N)/\ln(1/\gamma_\Phi)$.
This principle requires a unique zero-cost input ray and equal unit cost
on its entire orthogonal complement. It does not cover unequal higher-level
energies, a multidimensional zero-cost subspace, or the endpoint cases
$\gamma_\Phi=0,1$. Its proof is direct; the pulse-position method is the
standard one cited below, and priority of this exact finite-error
universality statement has not been established.

\subsection{Consequences for iid qubit archives}

Let $\rho_b=(I+b\cdot\sigma)/2$, and consider either the known-radius
sphere $|b|=r_n$ or the full ball $|b|\le r_n$.
An archive consists of CPTP maps $\mathcal E_n,\mathcal D_n$ around
$\mathcal A_{\gamma,0}^{\otimes m_n}$, with source-wise energy
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[H_{m_n}\mathcal E_n(\rho_b^{\otimes n})]
       \le Q_n
\]
and worst-case half trace-distance reconstruction error at most $e_n$.
Put $\kappa_n=nr_n^2$.

\paragraph{An exponential tomography bound.}
For $n\ge6$, measure Pauli $X,Y,Z$ on three disjoint groups of
$t=\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ copies. Each sample is in $[-1,1]$.
Hoeffding's inequality and a coordinate union bound give, for every
allowed $b$ and every $s>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{pe:tomography-tail}
 \Pr_b\{\|\widehat b-b\|_2\ge s/(2\sqrt n)\}
 \le6\exp\left(-\frac{ts^2}{24n}\right)
 \le6e^{-s^2/144}.
\end{equation}
The final inequality uses $t\ge n/6$. Therefore, a nearest-neighbor
decision for a packing with spacing $d=s/\sqrt n$ has error at most
$6e^{-s^2/144}$ on the original source. Applying it after the archive
adds at most $e_n$ to each hypothesis error, by trace-distance
contraction under a measurement.

A maximal $d$-separated sphere packing has
$N\ge4\kappa_n/s^2$ whenever $s\le2\sqrt{\kappa_n}$: its chord-radius
$d$ caps cover the sphere and each cap has area $\pi d^2$.
A maximal ball packing has
$N\ge\kappa_n^{3/2}/s^3$, by the covering volume argument.
Every packing point is an actual source state; the encoder is not
supplied the packing index. Its image ensemble inherits the source-wise
energy constraint. Hence \eqref{pe:error-simple} implies the unconditional
source converse
\begin{equation}\label{pe:source-finite-converse}
 \gamma^{Q_n}
 \le e_n+6e^{-s^2/144}+\frac2{\sqrt N}+\frac1N.
\end{equation}
This uses only the amplitude-damping Kraus operators and elementary
source discrimination, independently of family 276 or either source
archive upper theorem.

\paragraph{Qualitative energy threshold.}
If $\kappa_n\to\infty$ and $e_n\to0$, choose
$s_n=\kappa_n^{1/4}$. The tomography error tends to zero and both packing
cardinalities tend to infinity. Equation
\eqref{pe:source-finite-converse} then forces $Q_n\to\infty$.

For the converse implication, assume $r_n\to0$ and accept a finite
classical source archive with error at most
$2r_n/(1+r_n)+\varepsilon_n$ and finite alphabet $D_n$ for every
$\varepsilon_n\downarrow0$. Both the supplied Q.K bound and the repaired
Q.U bound have this property. Every prescribed budget $Q_n\to\infty$
then suffices: for large $n$ use $k_n=\lfloor Q_n\rfloor$ pulse-position
repetitions per archive label, with $m_n=k_nD_n$. Each label has exactly
$k_n$ excitations, so every original source satisfies the energy bound.
The combined worst-case trace error is at most
\begin{equation}\label{pe:threshold-upper}
 \frac{2r_n}{1+r_n}+\varepsilon_n+\gamma^{k_n}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Thus, in the shrinking source regimes with $\kappa_n\to\infty$, divergence
of total excitation is necessary and, conditional only on the accepted
classical source archive, sufficient for vanishing-error reconstruction
with unrestricted cell count. No claim that the construction minimizes
cell count is made.

\paragraph{A prescribed-accuracy leading law.}
Let $e_n\to0$, and suppose eventually $r_n\le e_n/16$.
For the sphere impose
\begin{equation}\label{pe:sphere-precision-regime}
 \frac{e_n\sqrt{\kappa_n}}{\sqrt{\ln(6/e_n)}}\longrightarrow\infty;
\end{equation}
for the full ball impose
\begin{equation}\label{pe:ball-precision-regime}
 \frac{e_n\kappa_n^{3/4}}{(\ln(6/e_n))^{3/4}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
Set $s_n^2=144\ln(6/e_n)$ in \eqref{pe:tomography-tail}.
Its error is at most $e_n$, and the relevant regime gives
$N^{-1/2}=o(e_n)$ as well as the required sphere spacing condition.
Thus \eqref{pe:source-finite-converse} yields
\begin{equation}\label{pe:source-accuracy-lower}
 Q_n\ge\frac{\ln(1/e_n)-\ln2+o(1)}{\ln(1/\gamma)}.
\end{equation}
More explicitly, before taking the regime limits, the right side of
\eqref{pe:source-finite-converse} is at most
\[
 2e_n+s_n/\sqrt{\kappa_n}+s_n^2/(4\kappa_n)
 \quad\text{on the sphere},
\]
and at most
\[
 2e_n+2s_n^{3/2}/\kappa_n^{3/4}+s_n^3/\kappa_n^{3/2}
 \quad\text{on the full ball}.
\]
These expressions expose the packing/coherence accuracy floor.

For attainability choose source accuracy $\varepsilon_n=e_n/4$.
Its error is at most $2r_n+e_n/4\le3e_n/8$.
Choose
\begin{equation}\label{pe:source-k}
 k_n=\left\lceil\frac{\ln(2/e_n)}{\ln(1/\gamma)}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Pulse-position transmission of the source archive label has maximal error
at most $e_n/2$. Under a coupling of the true and decoded labels, the
correctly decoded cases reconstruct the same state and the incorrect
cases contribute half trace distance at most one. Therefore the combined
source reconstruction error is at most $7e_n/8<e_n$. Each original source
has expected excitation exactly $k_n$, independently of its label law.
The physical cell count is explicitly
\begin{equation}\label{pe:source-m}
 m_n=k_nD_n.
\end{equation}
Using the source alphabet bounds quoted in the earlier noisy-memory
derivation, one can take
\begin{align}
 D_n&\le1+2^{30}(1+\kappa_n)e_n^{-3}\ln(1+288/e_n)
                       &&\text{on the sphere},\label{pe:sphere-D}\\
 D_n&\le4096\cdot10^{12}(1+\kappa_n)^{3/2}e_n^{-6}
                       &&\text{on the full ball}.\label{pe:ball-D}
\end{align}
These are bounds on a chosen finite source alphabet; harmless integer
rounding can be absorbed in their constants.

It follows that the infimum source-wise expected excitation among all
archives at reconstruction tolerance $e_n$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{pe:source-accuracy-match}
 Q_{\rm source}^{*}(n,r_n,e_n)
       \sim\frac{\ln(1/e_n)}{\ln(1/\gamma)}
\end{equation}
under the stated sphere or ball regime, conditional on the corresponding
source archive upper theorem. The lower bound is unconditional relative
to those source constructions. This result concerns iid density-state
reconstruction, not purification preservation or correlated inputs.

\begin{proposition}[A girth-preserving extension of the released repair]
Fix an integer $d\geq3$, put $q=d-1$ and $a=10^{-4}$, and assume the
pairing, cleanup, completion, and exact-repair results in the released
manuscript \emph{Deterministic nonbipartite Ramanujan graphs in every fixed
degree}, September 23, 2026, with the input and bit-complexity guarantees
stated there. There is then a deterministic algorithm which, for every
sufficiently large even $n$, outputs a simple $d$-regular graph $G'_n$ on
$n$ vertices such that
\[
 \operatorname{girth}(G'_n)\geq
 g_n:=\left\lfloor10^{-7}\log_q n\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 -2\sqrt q<\lambda_i(G'_n)<2\sqrt q\quad(2\leq i\leq n).
\]
The graph is connected and nonbipartite. The algorithm takes polynomially
many bit operations in $n$, with an exponent depending only on $d$.
The smaller coefficient $10^{-7}=.001a$ is used to avoid dependence on
the manuscript's integer rounding convention for its radius $g_0$;
the same argument preserves $g_0$ itself when that exact radius is used.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We identify the source-dependent inputs before the new argument.
The source chooses $q^{g_0}\asymp n^{.002a}$. Its early pairing rejects
pairs at actual graph distance at most $g_0$; its cleanup obeys the same
distance exclusion; its boundary-completion proposition states that every
added edge preserves the girth exclusion. Hence the graph $G$ entering
repair has girth at least $g_0$, and, for sufficiently large $n$,
$g_0\geq g_n$. These are assertions of the released construction, not
claims established afresh here.

The repair theorem uses
\[
 M=\lceil n^{1/3+10a}\rceil
\]
draws. Each draw consists of two oriented edges of $G$, with probability
law $\mu$. Its proof, Step 3, supplies the pointwise bound
\[
 \mu(g,j)\leq\frac{C_d}{(dn)^2}.
 \tag{G1}
\]
The law already incorporates restriction to typical edges, numerical
approximation, and prime-denominator rounding. It need not have exactly
uniform marginals. Step 4 uses only pairwise independence between draws.
Step 7 realizes this by a finite prime-field sample space: for a prime
$P>M$, a uniform seed $(\gamma,\beta)\in\mathbb F_P^2$ selects draw $r$
from the residue $\gamma r+\beta$, using the source's fixed residue-to-edge-
pair map. The prime $P$ and the number $P^2$ of seeds are polynomial in
$n$. The source proves that a fraction $1-o(1)$ of these seeds meets its
spectral fluctuation, bulk-error, and simplicity requirements.

Regard the $2M$ oriented old edges as distinct \emph{edge slots}.
The two endpoints within one edge slot are allowed to be adjacent.
Introduce one additional event $\mathcal A$: every pair of endpoints
belonging to distinct edge slots has distance greater than $g_n$ in the
fixed graph $G$ before any switch. This event concerns both slots in one
draw and slots belonging to different draws. It also rules out repeated
old edges and coincident endpoints.

A ball of radius $g_n$ in a graph of maximum degree $d$ has at most
\[
 1+d\sum_{h=0}^{g_n-1}q^h\leq C_d q^{g_n}
 \tag{G2}
\]
vertices. For the two slots of a single draw, (G1) dominates the joint
law by a constant multiple of two independent uniform oriented edges.
The prescribed endpoints of those uniform edges are independent uniform
vertices. Thus their probability of being within distance $g_n$ is at
most $C_d q^{g_n}/n$. For slots in distinct draws, summing (G1) over
the other slot bounds each marginal by $C_d/(dn)$; pairwise independence
of draws gives the same joint domination and proximity bound. A union
bound over all pairs of slots and their four pairs of endpoints gives
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal A^c)
 \leq C_d\frac{M^2q^{g_n}}n
 \leq n^{-1/3+(20+.001)a+o(1)}=o(1).
 \tag{G3}
\]
The exponent is negative for $a=10^{-4}$. Replacing $g_n$ by the source's
$g_0$ gives the equally negative exponent
$-1/3+(20+.002)a+o(1)$.
No conditioning or change to the repair law is made: we simply intersect
$\mathcal A$ with the source's success event. Since both have probability
$1-o(1)$ under the same uniform prime-field seed, their intersection is
nonempty for sufficiently large $n$.

We now prove the deterministic girth assertion for every successful seed
in this intersection. A switch deletes old edges $\{u,v\}$ and
$\{x,y\}$ and inserts $\{u,y\}$ and $\{x,v\}$. The source's successful
collections have distinct endpoints across all $M$ switches, so their
inserted edges form a matching. Write $G^-$ for $G$ with every selected
old edge deleted and $G'$ for the graph after insertion.
Consider a simple cycle $C$ of $G'$. If it uses no inserted edge, it is
a cycle of $G$ and has length at least $g_0$. Otherwise the matching
property means that between consecutive inserted edges along $C$ there
is a nonempty path in $G^-$. Fix one such path with endpoints $p,p'$.
If they belong to distinct old edge slots, $\mathcal A$ forces its length
to exceed $g_n$. If they belong to the same slot, they are the distinct
endpoints of one deleted old edge; adjoining that edge to the simple path
forms a simple cycle of $G$. Consequently the path has length at least
$g_0-1\geq g_n-1$. In either case $C$, which also contains an inserted
edge, has length at least $g_n$. This covers cycles containing arbitrarily
many switches; checking switches individually would not suffice.

For the algorithm, retain the source's pairing and repair law and enumerate
exactly its same seeds. Precompute truncated graph distances to depth $g_n$
in $G$ using breadth-first search from each vertex, then reject a seed
unless $\mathcal A$ holds. There are $O(M^2)$ required distance comparisons
per seed. Reject other invalid switches as in the source and apply its
exact spectral test
\[
 B^T(4qI-A_{G'}^2)B\succ0,
 \qquad B=[e_1-e_n,\ldots,e_{n-1}-e_n].
 \tag{G4}
\]
The intersection argument proves that some seed passes both rejection and
(G4). Computing $g_n$ requires only the integer comparison
$q^{10^7g_n}\leq n<q^{10^7(g_n+1)}$; $10^7$ is a fixed constant.
Breadth-first distances take $O_d(n^2)$ time and space, the additional
per-seed comparisons take polynomial time, and all existing rational
spectral and seed computations retain the source's polynomial bit bound.
Connectedness and nonbipartiteness follow from (G4), since
$d>2\sqrt{d-1}$ and a disconnected or bipartite regular graph has a
nonconstant eigenvalue of magnitude $d$.
\end{proof}

 t_{\rm mix}(\epsilon)\le\log_{d-1}n+
