Let CP-HC4 mean: every f in C[x1,x2,x3,x4] with det Hess f a nonzero
constant, and with some nonzero constant v satisfying D_v²f constant,
has a two-sided polynomial gradient inverse. The constant may be zero.

**Theorem, with named established prerequisites:** CP-HC4 is equivalent
to JC2. The forward structural proof uses Lin2609.06465v1 Theorem1 and
the established HC2/HC3 theorems, not any proposed general HC4 theorem.
Lin's proof was reconstructed in ZERO_PIVOT_GATE, retaining Bertini--Krull
spectrum finiteness and Piontkowski's rank-one affine cylinder theorem.

## Nonzero pivot: prior all-degree Schur argument and coefficient descent

A constant invertible linear coordinate change makes v the last
coordinate direction. For a=D_v²f!=0, integration gives

`f=P(u)+w Q(u)+(a/2)w²`, u=(x,y,z), P and Q arbitrary polynomials.

The fourth gradient output is sigma=Q(u)+a w. Therefore

`w=(sigma-Q(u))/a`,
`R_sigma(u)=P(u)-(sigma-Q(u))²/(2a)`.

The three other gradient outputs are exactly grad_u R_sigma, and the
Schur identity, after this polynomial triangular substitution, gives

`det Hess_u R_sigma=(det Hess f)/a=c/a`.

This is a nonzero constant unit of C[sigma]; it places no degree bound
on Q. The established de Bondt1203.6605v3 Theorem2.1, whose ground field
is any characteristic-zero field, applies over C(sigma). It yields a
polynomial inverse in the three gradient outputs over C(sigma).

Here is the complete descent needed for a global inverse. For any domain
R and polynomial F in R[u]^n with Jacobian determinant a unit of R,
assume its inverse is polynomial over Frac(R). Translate the target by
F(0); the linear Jacobian at source0 is invertible over R. Recursive
homogeneous comparison constructs the unique formal inverse in R[[Y]].
The normalized polynomial inverse over Frac(R) is that same formal
inverse by uniqueness, so every one of its finitely many coefficients
lies in R. Undoing the target translation keeps it polynomial over R.
Both composition identities hold over R by their identities over the
fraction field. No coefficient integrality, chosen parameter value,
or rational-to-polynomial upgrade is left implicit.

Apply this with R=C[sigma]. Recover u by the descended inverse, then w
by the displayed formula. This proves CP-HC4 unconditionally in the
nonzero-pivot branch, in all degrees. It is the existing Schur/HC3
mechanism used in Ni §6; the arbitrary-Q scope follows directly from
that prior proof even though Ni's Proposition6.1 states degQ<=2.

## Zero pivot: exact surviving plane interface

When a=0, write f=P(u)+wQ(u). The last Hessian row makes grad Q nowhere
zero. The coefficient of w² in the bordered determinant is -U(Q), so
Lin's theorem gives a constant direction annihilating Q. After a
constant linear coordinate change in u, Q depends on x,y only.

The independent binary dichotomy in ZERO_PIVOT_GATE then gives either
an unconditional polynomial inverse, or

`f=R(x,y)+z S(x,y)+w Q(x,y)`,
`det D(S,Q)` a nonzero constant.

The gradient of this latter potential is polynomially invertible if
and only if the plane Keller map G=(S,Q) is. JC2 therefore implies
CP-HC4. A potential with a zero pivot need not have a globally affine
Q: its plane lift can contain any plane Keller map. Neither the real
Lorentzian lightlike argument nor a pointwise Schur inverse can remove
this remaining plane interface.

## Converse: every plane Keller map is already in the pivot class

For any plane Keller map G=(G1,G2), take

`f(x,y,z,w)=z G1(x,y)+w G2(x,y)`.

It has D_z²f=0 and det Hess f=(det DG)²!=0. Its gradient is
`(DG(x,y)^T(z,w),G(x,y))`. If CP-HC4 supplies a polynomial inverse H,
then `h(V)=pi_(x,y) H(0,0,V1,V2)` is polynomial and G(h(V))=V.
Because h is dominant, the identity h(G(h(V)))=h(V) implies h∘G is
the identity on a dense set and hence identically. Thus h is a two-sided
polynomial inverse of G. This proves CP-HC4 implies JC2.

The constant source coordinate changes above are global polynomial
automorphisms and act on the gradient by an invertible constant target
matrix. No nonlinear source Hessian congruence is presumed. Divisions
occur only by the nonzero constants a, c and fixed linear determinants.
The a=0 branch is treated separately, so its specialization does not
inherit a denominator a.

## Downstream use and next discriminating step

A candidate HC4 descent retaining any constant directional second
derivative has no mechanism beyond a plane Keller obstruction. Nonzero
pivots are already invertible; zero pivots reduce to the exact plane
lift or an invertible branch. A new descent must either solve that
plane interface or produce a polynomial potential outside this pivot
class while preserving its determinant and an actual global collision.
The next falsifiable step is to certify absence of every constant
pivot of a specific legal reduced candidate, and then verify the
collision and determinant independently. A failed finite search for
pivots alone does not establish the required absence.
