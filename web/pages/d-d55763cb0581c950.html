## Exact interface and quantifiers

Work on S^2 with normalized surface measure sigma. Let e be any real orthonormal basis of all nonconstant spherical harmonics of degrees 1 through T, so dim(e)=T(T+2). For N distinct rational unit directions the accepted interface is

    b=(1/N)sum_i e(omega_i), ||b||<=beta_T,
    S=(1/N)sum_i e(omega_i)e(omega_i)^T > I/2,
    beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5].

The obstruction proves an upper bound for the weaker real-input event ||b||<=2 beta_T, using no Gram or distinctness condition. For each fixed C>0 and every integer T>=8 and N>=1 with N<=C T^2,

    Pr(||b||<=2 beta_T)
       <=6 exp[-T^2 exp(-64C)/128].                    (G1)

The exponential-in-T^2 wording requires that C is a fixed cardinality constant. The coefficient exp(-64C)/128 is positive but can be tiny; the bound is not a statement about practical moderate-degree behavior. Its right side may exceed one at small T, which merely makes that instance trivial. If the acceptance probability is zero, the expected retry count is infinite. Otherwise independent whole-set retry has expected count at least the reciprocal in (G1).

No claim about all randomized acquisition algorithms, correlated draws, position optimization, or a supplied design follows. A strict positive outward boundary pairing gives existence but no contraction/monotonicity guarantee; the polynomial rotating-map counterexample in the note is also correct.

## 1. The cap-forcing polynomial

Let n=floor((T-1)/2), q=n+2, s=1-16/q^2. For T>=8, q>=5 and 0<s<1. A uniform sphere point has axial coordinate z=omega dot u uniform on [-1,1]. Let P_l be Legendre polynomials normalized by P_l(1)=1 and put phi_l=sqrt(2l+1)P_l. They are orthonormal for dz/2.

The universal recurrence is not inferred from checking degrees up to 20. Orthogonality and parity show zP_l has only P_(l+1) and P_(l-1) components. The leading coefficient of P_l is 2^(-l) binom(2l,l), giving the first coefficient (l+1)/(2l+1); evaluation at z=1 gives the second l/(2l+1). Thus the off-diagonal coefficient in the orthonormal basis is exactly

    a_l=(l+1)/sqrt[(2l+1)(2l+3)]>1/2,

because a_l^2-1/4=1/[4(2l+1)(2l+3)].

The index range matters. There are n+1=q-1 coordinates

    v_l=sqrt(2/q)sin(pi(l+1)/q), l=0,...,n.

The q-th-root identity gives sum_(j=1)^(q-1) sin^2(pi j/q)=q/2. Therefore ||v||=1. The path matrix of size q-1 with off-diagonals 1/2 has v as eigenvector of eigenvalue cos(pi/q); the two omitted endpoint coordinates are sin(0)=sin(pi)=0. Every v_l is positive. For p=sum_l v_l phi_l,

    integral z p(z)^2 dz/2
       =2 sum_(l=0)^(n-1) a_l v_l v_(l+1)
       >cos(pi/q)>=1-pi^2/(2q^2)>1-8/q^2.

Set Q_u=(z-s)p(z)^2. Its degree 2n+1<=T, it is nonpositive off the open cap z>s, and its uniform mean is

    mu_u>8/q^2.                                       (G2)

The bound |P_l(z)|<=1 follows directly from the spherical harmonic addition kernel and Cauchy-Schwarz: its diagonal is 2l+1 and its zonal value is (2l+1)P_l(z). Consequently

    ||p||_infinity<=sqrt(sum_(l=0)^n (2l+1))=n+1,
    ||p||_2=1,
    ||Q_u||_2<=2||p^2||_2<=2(n+1).                   (G3)

The degree-T spherical harmonic expansion of Q_u is finite. Its nonconstant coefficient norm is at most (G3). If ||b||<=2 beta_T, Cauchy-Schwarz gives

    (1/N)sum_i Q_u(omega_i)
       >=mu_u-2(n+1)||b||
       >8/q^2-4(n+1)beta_T>0.                        (G4)

Indeed n+1<= (T+1)/2 and q<=T+1, so the negative correction is at most 1/[1024(T+1)^4], whereas the positive term is at least 8/(T+1)^2. If the open cap contained no point, every summand would be nonpositive. Therefore every cap z>s must contain a point. This necessity is independent of N and does not use the Gram test.

## 2. Packing constants

Put r=arccos(s)<pi/2. The normalized radius-r cap area is

    p_cap=(1-s)/2=8/q^2.

A maximal finite 2r-separated center set exists: successive centers have disjoint positive-area radius-r caps, so the process cannot continue indefinitely. Maximality means its open radius-2r caps cover the sphere. The latter area is

    (1-cos(2r))/2=sin^2 r=1-s^2<=32/q^2.

For the number K of centers,

    K>=q^2/32>=T^2/128.                               (G5)

The open radius-r caps are disjoint; their boundaries have sigma measure zero. A passing configuration must occupy every one of these fixed caps. The cap centers may be selected once, independently of the random experiment. No algorithm for finding a design is assumed by this geometric packing argument.

## 3. Poissonization direction and retry costs

Let Z have Poisson mean 2N, independently of the iid directions. Cap counts are independent Poisson variables of mean 2N p_cap: summing the multinomial formula against the Poisson law proves this directly. Thus, for f(j)=Pr(all K caps occupied by j iid uniform points),

    E f(Z)=(1-exp(-2N p_cap))^K
        <=exp[-K exp(-2N p_cap)].

The function f is nondecreasing. Paley-Zygmund applied to Z with threshold half its mean gives

    Pr(Z>=N)>=N^2/(4N^2+2N)>=1/6.

Hence E f(Z)>=f(N)/6, so f(N)<=6 E f(Z). The direction is correct; it is not an assertion that fixed-N cap occupancies are independent. Since q>=T/2 and N<=CT^2,

    2N p_cap=16N/q^2<=64C.

Combining with (G5) proves (G1). The acceptance event for the full rational interface is a subset of the mean event after the rounding argument below.

## 4. Independent harmonic rounding bound

The addition identity gives sum_m e_lm(omega)^2=2l+1. Applying the spherical Laplacian to this constant and using its degree-l eigenvalue yields

    sum_m |grad_S e_lm|^2=l(l+1)(2l+1).

Summing degrees,

    sum_(l=1)^T sum_m |grad_S e_lm|^2
        =T(T+1)^2(T+2)/2<(T+1)^4/2.

For any unit-speed geodesic, the Euclidean norm of the derivative of the full harmonic vector is at most (T+1)^2/sqrt(2). The geodesic distance between two sphere points is at most pi/2 times their Euclidean chord distance. Thus a chord perturbation <=delta changes e by less than

    [pi/(2sqrt(2))](T+1)^2 delta
         <2(T+1)^2 delta.                            (G6)

At delta_T=2^(-14)(T+1)^(-7), this is <=beta_T/4. If a rounded configuration passes ||b_rounded||<=beta_T, its underlying real configuration has ||b_real||<=5 beta_T/4<2 beta_T. Rounding can even be coupled across the points to avoid collisions; the proof uses only the pointwise displacement bound. Distinctness and the exact Gram test can only reject more configurations.
