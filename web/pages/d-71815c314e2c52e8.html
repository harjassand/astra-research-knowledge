Independent reconstruction of all-size parity exchange and sharpness
Recorded: 2026-10-07 12:53 UTC

STATUS

The assigned all-size exchange claim is proved from the stated stability
interface. For every nonzero multiaffine polynomial

    h(x) = sum_{U subset E} f(U) x^U,

with f(U) >= 0, support only on even-cardinality sets, and h nonvanishing
whenever every Re(x_i)>0, all even S,T and every a in D=S triangle T satisfy

    sqrt(f(S) f(T)) <= sum_{j in D minus {a}}
        sqrt(f(S triangle {a,j}) f(T triangle {a,j})).              (EX)

No homogeneity, full-support, determinant representation, or shared-core
assumption is needed. The constant one is sharp for every even |D|, including
inside the hard-BCS coefficient class. A full-support equality condition is
also obtained below. I do not claim a complete structural classification of
equality for all one-sided zero patterns.

FULL RECONSTRUCTION

If f(S) f(T)=0, (EX) is immediate. Otherwise put D=S triangle T,
m=|D|. Since both endpoints are even, m is even; since a is specified, m>=2.
Twist by every element of T:

    h_T(x) = (product_{i in T} x_i) h(y),
    y_i = 1/x_i for i in T, and y_i=x_i otherwise.

For every monomial U this replaces its support by T triangle U, so
[x^W]h_T=f(T triangle W). The map x -> 1/x preserves the open right
half-plane because Re(1/x)=Re(x)/|x|^2>0, and the prefactor is nonzero there.
Thus h_T is still right-half-plane stable. Its coefficients are nonnegative
and it has even support. Set every coordinate outside D to zero. This boundary
specialization remains stable or is identically zero by multivariate Hurwitz;
it is not zero because its constant coefficient is f(T)>0. Call the result
g(x_D). This full-T twist is important: its coefficients still refer to the
original f on E, so a common occupied core or common holes have not been
removed from the data.

Fix arbitrary positive weights w_j for j in J=D minus {a}, let
W=product_{j in J}w_j, and restrict

    p_w(z)=g(x_a=1, x_j=w_j z for j in J).

If Re z>0, every argument of g has positive real part, so p_w is a real
right-half-plane-stable polynomial. Its degree is exactly d=m-1, which is odd:
the coefficient of z^d is f(S)W>0. Its constant coefficient is f(T)>0.
Write

    A_j = f(T triangle {a,j}),
    B_j = f(S triangle {a,j}).

Even support and multiaffinity give the four coefficients

    a_0       = f(T),
    a_d       = f(S) W,
    a_1       = sum_j A_j w_j,
    a_(d-1)   = W sum_j B_j/w_j.                                  (1)

For a_1, the only possible degree-one supports are {a,j}; a singleton is
forbidden by parity. For a_(d-1), the selected variables are J with one j
omitted; the support is D minus {a,j}, whose original coefficient is B_j.
These formulas also hold for m=2, where d=1 and the endpoint and neighboring
coefficients overlap.

Elementary odd-degree lemma. If p(z)=sum_{k=0}^d a_k z^k has odd degree,
a_0,a_d>0 and no roots in Re z>0, then a_1 a_(d-1)>=a_0 a_d. Every real root
is -r_l with r_l>0; every nonreal conjugate pair gives a factor
z^2+2s_k z+t_k with s_k>=0 and t_k>s_k^2 (allowing s_k=0). Therefore

    a_1/a_0       = sum_l 1/r_l + sum_k 2s_k/t_k,
    a_(d-1)/a_d   = sum_l r_l   + sum_k 2s_k.

The number q of real linear factors, counted with multiplicity, is odd and at
least one. The extra pair terms are nonnegative, and
(sum_l r_l)(sum_l 1/r_l)>=q^2>=1. This proves the lemma, including boundary
imaginary roots and repeated roots. The odd degree is essential: 1+z^2 is
right-half-plane stable but violates the analogous even-degree inequality.

Applying the lemma to (1) and cancelling W yields, for every positive w,

    f(S)f(T) <= (sum_j A_j w_j)(sum_j B_j/w_j).                     (2)

By Cauchy-Schwarz, with the reverse bound approached by choosing weights,

    inf_{w_j>0} (sum_j A_j w_j)(sum_j B_j/w_j)
        = (sum_j sqrt(A_j B_j))^2.                                 (3)

If A_j,B_j>0, take w_j=sqrt(B_j/A_j); if A_j>0,B_j=0 take w_j down to
zero; if A_j=0,B_j>0 take w_j to infinity; if both are zero that coordinate
is irrelevant. Thus (3) includes all zero patterns without perturbing f or
assuming genericity. Taking the infimum in (2), then square roots, proves
(EX).

APPLICATION TO THE ACTUAL HARD-BCS INTERFACE

For even n and complex F, set

    f(U)=Z_{[n]\U}(F),
    Z_R(F)=sum_{I subset R, |I|=|R|/2} |det F[I,R\I]|^2,

and f(U)=0 for odd U. The signed-hole polynomial

    Q_F(x)=sum_{U even} (-1)^((n-|U|)/2) f(U) x^U

is real stable in the upper half-plane. The exact source derivation uses the
Hermitian chiral matrix H=[[0,2F],[2F*,0]], the stable determinant
det(diag(u,v)-H), then the averaged directional derivative
2^(-n) product_i (partial_{u_i}+partial_{v_i}) and identification u_i=v_i.
The selected balanced chiral minors are (-1)^k 2^(2k)|det F[I,J]|^2; the
averaging factor cancels 2^(2k). Rotating variables gives

    h_F(x)=(-1)^(n/2) Q_F(i x)=sum_U f(U)x^U,

which is right-half-plane stable and even. Hence (EX) applies to the actual
ambient-hole coefficients with the shared core retained. This stability
bridge is established in the prior cycle-4 report cited below; my independent
reconstruction here begins at its exact output interface and does not infer
stability of the distinct pair-label polynomial.

EQUALITY AND SHARPNESS

For the univariate lemma, equality is completely classified: it holds iff

    p(z)=C (z+r) product_{k=1}^{(d-1)/2}(z^2+tau_k^2),

for C,r,tau_k>0. Indeed equality in the root proof forces q=1 (otherwise
q^2>1) and forces every pair contribution 2s_k to vanish, so each remaining
root pair is purely imaginary. Conversely this factorization gives equality.

For the exchange inequality, if there are no one-sided exchange coefficients
(A_j=0 iff B_j=0 for all j) and the common-positive index set is nonempty,
the infimum (3) is attained. Then (EX) is equality iff the balanced slice
p_w, with w_j proportional to sqrt(B_j/A_j) on A_j,B_j>0, has exactly the
root form above; weights for A_j=B_j=0 do not matter. A common rescaling of
all w_j only rescales z and preserves this root condition. If one-sided zero
indices occur, (3) is only approached at a boundary of positive weight space;
the exact criterion available from this proof is that the endpoint-minor
ratio

    [sum_j A_j w_j][sum_j B_j/w_j]/[f(S)f(T)]

has infimum one. I do not promote this limiting condition to a complete
support-decomposition theorem.

The sharp family is h(x)=product_i(1+lambda_i x_{2i}x_{2i+1}), lambda_i>0.
Its coefficients are supported exactly on unions of fixed pairs. Let S,T be
such unions and let D be any union of the pairs on which they differ. For
a in one differing pair, only its partner j contributes to the right side:
every other {a,j} breaks a pair on both endpoints. The partner move swaps the
occupancy of that pair between S and T, so
f(S triangle {a,j})f(T triangle {a,j})=f(S)f(T). Thus equality holds for
all |D|=2r, while every other exchange term is zero. Multiplying by a positive
common scalar leaves the inequality unchanged. This family is realized in the
hard-BCS class by F with one nonzero directed entry per fixed pair; its hole
coefficients are a positive scalar times a pair product with reciprocal
activities. The constant one therefore cannot be improved, even for six-hole
instances and even with an arbitrary common core.

STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCE AND LIMIT

The all-size inequality supplies a macro-level symmetric-exchange bound with
leading constant one, so a structural relocation argument need not multiply
an avoidable C>1 at every exchange. It retains the original aggregate hole
partition f(U), not an acquired microscopic transition probability. When the
sum has one nonzero term, the fixed-pair example shows why equality can
identify a forced partner relation. For a general positive sum the theorem
only guarantees some j with sqrt(A_jB_j)>=sqrt(f(S)f(T))/(|D|-1); it does not
give a uniformly chosen good partner, a polynomial point-query algorithm for
f, a microscopic two-replica energy bound, a sampler, or an FPRAS. Equality
classification for all degenerate one-sided support patterns remains a
useful structural follow-up.
