# The strongest result: optimal tomography without identical copies

I derived a theorem that goes beyond the original storage problem:

> **The average of arbitrary independent, non-identical quantum states can be learned with the same optimal sample-count scaling as an identically prepared state—including the optimal dependence on its rank.**

The proof also identifies an explicit correlation penalty, allowing some correlated and entangled inputs. Separately, the construction that led to it extends the qubit collision archive to higher dimensions and gives a memory law that distinguishes eigenvalue gaps at different scales.

These are **written research derivations with reproducible checks**, not externally certified results. The measurement and one important estimator are prior art; the proposed advances are the non-identical-input theorem, its correlation-controlled extension, and the uniform collision-storage construction. Priority remains provisional.

The complete deliverable is the NonIID_Tomography_and_Collision_Archives.zip[research and evidence pack](sandbox:/mnt/data/NonIID_Tomography_and_Collision_Archives.zip), containing both proofs, runnable tests, their outputs, a preserved failure log, and an append-only compact-state update.

## 1. Why this became the strongest target

Your compact state supplied the key starting point: approximate the **distribution of a measurement record**, then reconstruct by conditional resampling, rather than prepare a product state at a point estimate. That distinction is essential to its qubit archive. Pasted text

The frontier refresh exposed a more immediate opening. Visnevskyi and Mančinska’s paper, **revised 5 October 2026**, establishes robustness for spectrum estimation but leaves the eigenbasis stage of full tomography open. It also repeats the question of learning the average of arbitrary non-identical product inputs at the usual sample complexity. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03582v2)

De Palma, Fanizza, Mowry and O’Donnell state that average-state tomography question explicitly: can one learn the average with \(O(d^2/\varepsilon^2)\) samples, rather than the straightforward \(O(d^3/\varepsilon^2)\) route? Their result concerns hypothesis testing, not full tomography. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06147v1)

The relevant June work already establishes optimality of Keyl’s *covariant large-deviation rate function* in its stated setting. I therefore did not treat another iid optimality argument as a new target. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16948v1)

The useful connection was instead this:

**A collective measurement can have simple, low-order operator moments even when its full outcome distribution is complicated. Those moments can survive loss of the iid assumption.**

That observation turns out to be enough.

# 2. Main theorem

Let the input be

\[
\omega=\rho_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_n,
\]

where each \(\rho_i\) is an arbitrary \(d\)-dimensional density matrix. There is **one copy of each state**, not \(n\) copies of each.

Define

\[
\bar\rho=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\rho_i,
\qquad
r=\operatorname{rank}(\bar\rho).
\]

The states need not be close, commute, or share an eigenbasis.

Apply the ordinary Keyl measurement. Its outcome consists of a partition

\[
\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d\ge0,
\qquad \sum_i\lambda_i=n,
\]

and an estimated eigenbasis \(U\). Return the ordinary physical estimator

\[
\widehat\rho
=
U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda/n)U^\dagger.
\]

### Derived bound

Writing \(T(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,T(\widehat\rho,\bar\rho)^2
\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{4rd}{n}\right\}.
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,T(\widehat\rho,\bar\rho)
\le
\min\left\{1,2\sqrt{\frac{rd}{n}}\right\}.
}
\]

The estimator does not require \(r\) as an input.

For failure probability \(\beta\),

\[
\Pr\!\left[T(\widehat\rho,\bar\rho)>\varepsilon\right]
\le
\frac{4rd}{n\varepsilon^2}.
\]

Thus \(n\ge 4rd/(\beta\varepsilon^2)\) suffices. At constant confidence, the sample complexity is

\[
\boxed{\Theta(rd/\varepsilon^2).}
\]

The lower bound is inherited from the iid subclass, using the rank-sensitive tomography lower bound of Scharnhorst, Spilecki and Wright. The new part here is the upper bound for arbitrary independent, non-identical inputs—not a new iid lower bound. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07699v1)

## 3. Proof: the collective measurement has a simple variance structure

The complete argument is in the TOMOGRAPHY.md[tomography proof](sandbox:/mnt/data/flag_collision_research/TOMOGRAPHY.md). Its central steps are short enough to give here.

### 3.1 Use a known unbiased statistic—but return the ordinary estimator

Write

\[
Z=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)U^\dagger,
\]

and introduce

\[
y_i
=
\lambda_i
+\#\{j:\lambda_i>\lambda_j\}
-\#\{j:\lambda_j>\lambda_i\},
\qquad
Y=U\operatorname{diag}(y)U^\dagger.
\]

This “donation” statistic and its iid unbiasedness are due to Pelecanos, Spilecki and Wright. **They are not discoveries of this run.** Their result gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\rho^{\otimes n}}Y=n\rho.
\]

\(Y\) can have negative eigenvalues; it is only an analytical auxiliary. The actual output remains \(Z/n\), which is always a density matrix. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07788)

### 3.2 Lift iid unbiasedness to an operator identity

For a Hermitian observable \(H\), let

\[
S_H=\sum_{i=1}^n H^{(i)}
\]

and let \(A_H\) denote the first-moment operator of the measurement statistic \(\operatorname{Tr}(HY)\).

Both \(A_H\) and \(S_H\) are invariant under permutations of the input registers. Iid unbiasedness says

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
(A_H-S_H)\rho^{\otimes n}
\right]=0
\]

for every density matrix \(\rho\).

Homogeneity extends this to all positive-definite Hermitian matrices. Polynomial polarization then determines every permutation-invariant coefficient, giving

\[
\boxed{A_H=S_H.}
\]

Therefore, for **every input state** \(\omega\), including correlated inputs,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\omega Y=\sum_{i=1}^n\omega_i,
}
\]

where \(\omega_i\) is the \(i\)-th marginal.

This is the first important removal of the iid assumption. It does not compare \(\omega\) with \(\bar\rho^{\otimes n}\), whose global trace distance could be large.

### 3.3 The scalar second moment is a Casimir

Put

\[
W_i=d+1-2i,
\qquad
w_i=y_i-\lambda_i,
\]

and define

\[
c_\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i(\lambda_i+d+1-2i),
\qquad
b_\lambda=\lambda\cdot W+\|w\|_2^2.
\]

The pointwise identity is

\[
\|Y\|_F^2=c_\lambda+b_\lambda.
\]

Moreover, if \(\ell(\lambda)\) is the number of nonzero parts,

\[
0\le \lambda\cdot W\le(d-1)n,
\qquad
\|w\|_2^2\le d^2\ell(\lambda).
\]

The first bound follows from

\[
\lambda\cdot W
=
\sum_{k<d}(\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1})k(d-k).
\]

For the second, \(w\) is the average of \(W\) within each equal-part block. Averaging cannot increase squared norm; keeping the zero block intact gives the stated bound.

Now take a Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal Hermitian basis \(\{H_a\}\). Its collective quadratic operator is

\[
\sum_a S_{H_a}^{\,2}
=
nd\,I+2\sum_{i<j}F_{ij},
\]

where \(F_{ij}\) swaps registers \(i,j\). On Schur sector \(\lambda\), this operator has eigenvalue \(c_\lambda\).

Combining this fact with the first-moment identity gives, for arbitrary \(\omega\),

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\omega\left\|Y-\sum_i\omega_i\right\|_F^2
={}&
\sum_i\bigl(d-\operatorname{Tr}\omega_i^2\bigr)\\
&+\mathcal C(\omega)
+\mathbb E_\omega b_\lambda,
\end{aligned}
}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C(\omega)
=
2\sum_{i<j}
\left[
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_{ij}F_{ij})
-
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_i\omega_j)
\right].
}
\]

That is the exact correlation contribution. It can be negative; it is not a norm of all correlations.

### 3.4 Independence eliminates the correlation term

For a product input,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_{ij}F_{ij})
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_i\rho_j),
\]

so \(\mathcal C(\omega)=0\), regardless of how different the \(\rho_i\) are.

Positivity implies every \(\rho_i\) is supported inside \(\operatorname{supp}\bar\rho\). Thus the input is supported on an \(r\)-dimensional single-site subspace tensor-powered \(n\) times, and

\[
\ell(\lambda)\le r
\]

with probability one. Therefore,

\[
\mathbb E\|Y-n\bar\rho\|_F^2
\le
(2d-1)n+d^2r.
\]

Also,

\[
\|Y-Z\|_F\le d\sqrt r,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(Z/n-\bar\rho)\le2r.
\]

Minkowski’s inequality and the rank-to-trace-norm inequality yield

\[
\sqrt{\mathbb E\,T(Z/n,\bar\rho)^2}
\le
\frac{\sqrt{2r}}{2n}
\left[
\sqrt{(2d-1)n+d^2r}+d\sqrt r
\right].
\]

When \(n\ge rd\), this is at most

\[
\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
\sqrt{\frac{rd}{n}}
<
2\sqrt{\frac{rd}{n}}.
\]

When \(n<rd\), use \(T\le1\). This completes the claimed bound.

**The key simplification is that full non-iid tomography does not require the entire non-iid measurement distribution. Its first moment and one scalar second moment suffice.**

# 4. What this immediately enables

## Preparation drift without an \(n\)-fold error penalty

Suppose the independent preparations are intended to approximate a target \(\rho_\star\), and define

\[
\bar\zeta
=
\frac1n\sum_iT(\rho_i,\rho_\star).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\,T(\widehat\rho,\rho_\star)
\le
\bar\zeta+2\sqrt{\frac{rd}{n}}
\le
\bar\zeta+\frac{2d}{\sqrt n}.
}
\]

The error from preparation bias appears with coefficient one, rather than accumulating as \(n\bar\zeta\).

This is a full-density-matrix statement, not only a spectrum statement. It also separates two questions that are easy to conflate: learning the source’s actual average and recovering an intended state from biased preparations.

The rank here is that of the **actual average**. Noise that increases its rank does not preserve the smaller rank of an ideal target for free.

The practical limitation is equally important: the theorem assumes an ideal collective measurement. It does not establish a practical apparatus-noise threshold or a low-cost implementation of that measurement.

## Some correlated—and entangled—inputs retain the same rate

For arbitrary \(\omega\), let

\[
\bar\rho=\frac1n\sum_i\omega_i,
\qquad
r=\operatorname{rank}\bar\rho,
\qquad
\mathcal C_+=\max\{\mathcal C(\omega),0\}.
\]

The same argument gives

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{\mathbb E\,T(\widehat\rho,\bar\rho)^2}
\le
\min\left\{
1,\;
2\sqrt{\frac{rd}{n}}
+
\sqrt{\frac{r\mathcal C_+}{2n^2}}
\right\}.
}
\]

Consequently, **pairwise product marginals suffice**:

\[
\omega_{ij}=\omega_i\otimes\omega_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal C=0.
\]

Higher-order correlations or entanglement need not invalidate the optimal rate.

More generally, if

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}
\left|
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_{ij}F_{ij})
-\operatorname{Tr}(\omega_i\omega_j)
\right|
\le\chi
\]

for every \(i\), the extra term is at most

\[
\sqrt{\frac{r\chi}{2n}}.
\]

Thus a bounded aggregate of the relevant two-body correlations preserves the square-root rate. This is a conditional theorem, not an assumption that arbitrary laboratory noise obeys such a bound.

### A counterexample fixes the boundary

Consider

\[
\omega=
\frac12|0^n\rangle\langle0^n|
+
\frac12|1^n\rangle\langle1^n|.
\]

Every single-site marginal is \(I/2\), but

\[
\mathcal C(\omega)=\frac{n(n-1)}2.
\]

The state lies entirely in the symmetric sector. The ordinary Keyl estimate is therefore pure on every outcome, so

\[
T(\widehat\rho,I/2)=\frac12
\]

exactly.

This rules out replacing the correlation condition with a promise about one-site marginals alone.

# 5. The storage mechanism that led to the theorem

The higher-dimensional extension remains valuable in its own right. Its full derivation is in the STORAGE.md[storage proof](sandbox:/mnt/data/flag_collision_research/STORAGE.md).

## 5.1 A finite-\(n\) classicalization inequality for qudits

For a positive spectrum

\[
a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_d>0,
\]

define

\[
\Gamma(\rho)=\sum_{i<j}\log\frac{a_i}{a_j}.
\]

I derived one universal Schur coherent measure-and-prepare channel \(\mathcal E_{n,d}\), independent of \(\rho\), satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{n,d}(\rho^{\otimes n})
\ge
e^{-\Gamma(\rho)}\rho^{\otimes n}.
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
T\!\left(
\mathcal E_{n,d}(\rho^{\otimes n}),
\rho^{\otimes n}
\right)
\le1-e^{-\Gamma(\rho)}.
}
\]

The bound is uniform in \(n\) and the Schur sector. For qubits, it reduces exactly to the supplied \(2\delta/(1+\delta)\) bound.

The mechanism is an exact **positive flag-integral representation**. If \(A>0\), \(m_k=\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}\), and \(U_k\) is the leading \(k\)-frame, define

\[
C_{\lambda,A}(U)
=
(\det A)^{\lambda_d-d+1}
\prod_{k<d}
\det(U_k^\dagger A^{-1}U_k)^{-(m_k+2)}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\omega C_{\lambda,A}(U)
=
\operatorname{Tr}(\omega A^{\otimes n}).
}
\]

Differentiating at \(A=e^{tH}\) produces the moment identities used above. Comparing the positive representation with the coherent measurement likelihood produces the channel inequality.

For fixed \(d\), \(\rho_n\to I/d\) makes the classicalization error vanish, even when

\[
n\|\rho_n-I/d\|_F^2\to\infty.
\]

Thus the source can retain a growing amount of distinguishable information while requiring no retained quantum coherence.

This concerns the complete density matrix \(\rho^{\otimes n}\), **not preservation of an external purification**.

## 5.2 A finite archive governed by covering number

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a compact family in

\[
\frac{I}{2d}\le\rho\le\frac{3I}{2d},
\]

and let \(N(\mathcal F,t)\) denote its Frobenius covering number. Put \(p=d^2-1\).

The derived finite classical code satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D_{\mathrm{cl}}
\le
1+C_d
N(\mathcal F,n^{-1/2})
\varepsilon^{-p}
[\log(C_d/\varepsilon)]^p,
}
\]

with reconstruction error

\[
e\le
1-e^{-\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal F}\Gamma(\rho)}
+\varepsilon.
\]

Conversely, every classical, quantum, or hybrid code with uniform reconstruction error at most \(1/8\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D\ge c_dN(\mathcal F,n^{-1/2}).
}
\]

All retained input-dependent registers are counted.

The decoder does something specific: it resamples the fine measurement record under a nearby reference model **conditioned on the stored cell**, then prepares the corresponding mixture. It does not prepare a tensor power at the cell’s point estimate.

A load-bearing regularity lemma makes this possible without assuming spectral gaps. Eigenvector derivatives introduce denominators \((\lambda_i-\lambda_j)^{-1}\), but the likelihood coefficients telescope:

\[
\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}(\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1})
=
\lambda_i-\lambda_j.
\]

The apparent singularity cancels.

For fixed \(d\), families shrinking uniformly to \(I/d\), and diverging covering entropy, the resulting optimal vanishing-error memory is

\[
\boxed{
m=(1+o(1))
\log_2N(\mathcal F_n,n^{-1/2}),
}
\]

entirely classically.

This is an information-theoretic finite construction. Arbitrary model nets and conditional integrations may be computationally difficult; no general efficient gate compiler is supplied.

## 5.3 Different eigenvalue gaps contribute separately

For a fixed-dimensional unitary orbit,

\[
N(\mathcal O_a,t)
\asymp_d
\prod_{i<j}
\left(1+\frac{|a_i-a_j|}{t}\right)^2.
\]

The tube-volume argument remains valid through degeneracies.

Suppose all \(a_{i,n}\to1/d\), and define

\[
S_n=
\sum_{i<j}
\log_2\!\left(1+n(a_{i,n}-a_{j,n})^2\right).
\]

When \(S_n\to\infty\),

\[
\boxed{
m_{\mathrm{opt}}=(1+o(1))S_n,
}
\]

with vanishing global reconstruction error and entirely classical storage.

For example, take the qutrit spectrum

\[
\left(
\frac13+n^{-1/4}+n^{-3/4},
\frac13+n^{-1/4}-n^{-3/4},
\frac13-2n^{-1/4}
\right).
\]

Two gaps are of order \(n^{-1/4}\); the third is below the \(n^{-1/2}\) resolution scale. The leading memory is

\[
(1+o(1))\log_2n.
\]

For a full Frobenius ball of radius \(R_n\) around \(I/d\), with \(R_n\to0\) and \(nR_n^2\to\infty\), the corresponding result is

\[
m_{\mathrm{opt}}
=
\left(\frac{d^2-1}{2}+o(1)\right)
\log_2(nR_n^2).
\]

Orbit-volume connections themselves are not new: the October minimum-description paper already discusses them. Its theorem explicitly fixes the spectrum, however, and disclaims uniformity through moving collisions. The proposed addition here is the uniform classical construction and its collision-dependent consequences—not discovery of the general volume–memory connection. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06742v1)

# 6. An important failed transfer: tomography is not a heterogeneous archive

The non-iid tomography theorem does **not** imply that arbitrary non-identical products admit the logarithmic archive.

A sharp separation comes from commuting states

\[
\rho_s=
\bigotimes_{i=1}^n
\frac{I+\delta s_iZ}{2},
\qquad
s_i\in\{-1,+1\},
\qquad
\sum_i s_i=0.
\]

Every member has exactly the same average single-site state \(I/2\).

Nevertheless, choose balanced sign strings from a Hamming packing. Measuring \(Z\) distinguishes

\[
K=\exp\!\bigl(\Theta(n\delta^2)\bigr)
\]

such joint states with constant success. Reconstruction preserves that discrimination, while a \(D\)-dimensional memory can distinguish \(K\) equiprobable hypotheses with success at most \(D/K\).

The explicit construction in the note yields

\[
\boxed{
\log_2D
\ge
\frac{n\delta^2}{64\ln2}-O(1).
}
\]

So the average can be known exactly while preserving the whole heterogeneous experiment requires \(\Omega(n\delta^2)\) bits.

This obstruction is worth retaining: it prevents the new tomography theorem from being turned into an invalid universal compression claim.

## Assessment

**The tomography theorem is the most consequential result of this run.** Its derivation answers the specific trace-distance average-state question identified in the retrieved literature, gives the rank-optimal rate, and extends beyond independent inputs through an exact correlation quantity.

The reusable mechanism is more important than a constant improvement:

> **Analyze the operator moments of a collective measurement, rather than approximating its entire input by an iid product. A one-body first moment and a two-body Casimir can preserve statistical efficiency even when the full input distributions are very different.**

The storage results provide a second consequence: near the maximally mixed state, retained memory follows distinguishability at the statistical resolution scale, with separate contributions from different eigenvalue gaps.

That is a substantial, concrete research outcome. **Scientific priority and independent correctness review remain unresolved**, and the results do not establish universal noise tolerance, practical large-scale quantum hardware, or a solution to the unrelated major conjectures in the compact state.
