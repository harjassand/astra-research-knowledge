C03_L05 REVISIONS — FINITE EXCHANGEABILITY / COUNT-GAUSSIAN BRIDGE
8 October 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)

Status
------
This is a primary-literature and exact-necessary-condition bridge for the
specific family

  p_k^(N) = Z_N(delta)^(-1) exp[-delta (k-N/2)^2/N],
  q_k^(N) = p_k^(N) / binom(N,k),  0 <= k <= N.

It does not prove (or refute) that a fixed delta in (0,2 log 2] works for
every N. The central unresolved item remains a uniform positive certificate
for the full truncated Stieltjes moment matrices, or one counterexample N.
The old INITIAL.txt was not modified.

Exact interface and reduction
-----------------------------
For fixed N, the exchangeable law on {0,1}^N that assigns each string with k
ones probability q_k is a mixture of iid Bernoulli laws iff there is a
probability measure mu_N on [0,1] such that

  q_k = integral_0^1 x^k (1-x)^(N-k) mu_N(dx),  k=0,...,N.

The measure may depend on N. “Uniformly all N” here means this finite moment
feasibility holds for each N at one common delta; it does not require a single
coherent mu to generate the whole family as N varies. With odds t=x/(1-x),
reweighting by (1-x)^N turns these q_k into the first N moments of a positive
measure on [0,infinity). Conversely a finite positive measure with moments
q_0,...,q_N gives mu_N by multiplying it by (1+t)^N and mapping t to
x=t/(1+t); normalization follows from

  integral (1+t)^N dnu(t) = sum_k binom(N,k) q_k = sum_k p_k = 1.

The p=1 endpoint can be handled separately; a finite-odds representing
measure suffices for this strictly positive target. Thus the exact task is a
truncated Stieltjes moment problem. Curto–Fialkow's finite-order criterion
makes the precise PSD-plus-range interface explicit. If N=2m, define
H_m=(q_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<=m), B_(m-1)=(q_(i+j+1))_(0<=i,j<=m-1), and
v=(q_(m+1),...,q_(2m)). A representing measure exists iff H_m and B_(m-1)
are PSD and v lies in Range(B_(m-1)). If N=2m+1, define H_m and
B_m=(q_(i+j+1))_(0<=i,j<=m), and v=(q_(m+1),...,q_(2m+1)); the criterion
is H_m,B_m PSD and v in Range(H_m). Thus, in strict-PD cases the range
condition is automatic; in singular cases bare PSD is not sufficient. This
is the exact finite criterion behind the root's H0/H1 formulation.

Independent exact necessary bounds
----------------------------------
1. The N=2 bound is exact and simple. Up to a common positive normalization,
   q_0=q_2=exp(-delta/2) and q_1=1/2. Positivity of the 2x2 moment matrix
   [[q_0,q_1],[q_1,q_2]] requires

     q_0 q_2 - q_1^2 = exp(-delta) - 1/4 >= 0,

   hence delta <= log(4)=2 log(2). If equality holds at N=2, the only
   representing Bernoulli parameter is x=1/2. This is an obstruction for
   the “all N” question because N=2 is among its instances; it does not force
   mu_N at N>2 to be that same measure.

2. All adjacent 2x2 Hankel minors give precisely the same uniform cutoff,
   and no stronger one. For 1<=k<N,

     log(q_(k-1) q_(k+1) / q_k^2)
       = -2 delta/N + log(((k+1)(N-k+1))/(k(N-k))).

   A Stieltjes moment sequence is log-convex, so necessarily

     delta <= (N/2) log(1 + (N+1)/(k(N-k))).

   The most restrictive k is central. If N=2m this bound is
   2m log(1+1/m); if N=2m+1 it is
   (2m+1)/2 log(1+2/m). Both increase with m (for the odd case, use
   log(1+x)>2x/(2+x) and 2/(m+1)>=(2m+1)/(m(m+2)) for m>=1).
   Their global minimum is N=2, namely 2 log 2. Therefore every adjacent
   2x2 minor is nonnegative for every N whenever delta<=2 log 2. This is
   only log-convexity; it does not imply positivity of the larger Hankel
   matrices.

3. A separate macroscopic necessary condition comes from exchangeable
   variance. Under any Bernoulli mixture with random parameter P and
   E[P]=1/2,

     Var(K) = N/4 + N(N-1) Var(P) >= N/4.

   For the normalized discrete-Gaussian count law above, Riemann sums on
   (K-N/2)/sqrt(N), with Gaussian tail domination, give

     Var(K)/N -> 1/(2 delta).

   Consequently delta>2 is impossible for all sufficiently large N. This is
   weaker than N=2's bound but independently marks the asymptotic variance
   boundary. It leaves the candidate delta=2 log 2 in the feasible-variance
   regime; variance is not a sufficiency argument.

Primary literature and exact applicability
-------------------------------------------
A. Diaconis and Freedman, “Finite Exchangeable Sequences,” Annals of
   Probability 8 (1980), 745–764, DOI 10.1214/aop/1176994663,
   https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-8/issue-4/Finite-exchangeable-sequences/10.1214/aop/1176994663.full .
   Their finite de Finetti theorem is an approximation: for a k-coordinate
   marginal of an n-coordinate exchangeable law on a c-point space, the
   variation-distance error to a mixture of iid laws is bounded at order
   c k/n (the paper's stated bound is 2 c k/n). At k=n it does not certify
   exact representation of the full N-bit law. It cannot close the present
   moment-cone membership question.

B. G. R. Wood, “Binomial Mixtures and Finite Exchangeability,” Annals of
   Probability 20 (1992), 1167–1173, DOI 10.1214/aop/1176989684,
   https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-20/issue-3/Binomial-mixtures-and-finite-exchangeability/10.1214/aop/1176989684.full .
   This is directly about binomial-mixture membership and finite
   exchangeability (including the probability that a randomly chosen law
   lies in the binomial-mixture cone), but it gives no family-specific
   certificate for the present q_k. It is the right exact-cone background,
   not a proof that this one-parameter curve stays in the cone.

C. T. M. Liggett, J. E. Steif, and B. Toth, “Statistical mechanical systems
   on complete graphs, infinite exchangeability, finite extensions and a
   discrete finite moment problem,” Annals of Probability 35 (2007),
   867–914, DOI 10.1214/009117906000001033,
   https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-35/issue-3/Statistical-mechanical-systems-on-complete-graphs-infinite-exchangeability-finite/10.1214/009117906000001033.full ; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0512191 .
   Their ferromagnetic Curie–Weiss Ising theorem concerns configuration
   weights proportional to a^k b^(k(N-k)) with b<=1 (equivalently
   nonnegative pair coupling), and proves infinite extendibility for that
   family. They also show the antiferromagnetic Curie–Weiss law is not
   infinitely extendible and derive sharp finite-l-extendibility statements
   near zero negative coupling. The latter regime changes b with extension
   length l and is not our fixed-N count-Gaussian family. Here the count
   masses p_k are Gaussian but the per-string weights include
   1/binom(N,k), i.e. an extra -log binom(N,k) term in log q_k; they are not
   the quadratic Curie–Weiss configuration weights. Thus neither the
   ferromagnetic representation theorem nor the antiferromagnetic
   obstruction transfers as stated.

D. T. Konstantopoulos and L. Yuan, “On the extendibility of finitely
   exchangeable probability measures,” Transactions of the AMS 371 (2019),
   7067–7092, DOI 10.1090/tran/7701,
   https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3081322/ ; preprint
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06188 . Their theorem gives a general necessary
   and sufficient functional-analytic criterion for extending a finitely
   exchangeable law. For binary exchangeability this is compatible with the
   binomial-mixture criterion, but the abstract functional-norm condition
   does not supply a uniform delta certificate for this sequence.

E. R. E. Curto and L. A. Fialkow, “Recursiveness, Positivity, and Truncated
   Moment Problems,” Houston Journal of Mathematics 17 (1991), 603–635,
   https://www.math.uh.edu/~hjm/v017n4/0603CURTO.pdf . Their truncated
   Stieltjes results are the relevant exact finite-order framework. Positive
   Hankel/localizing matrices give the moment positivity tests; singular
   data require the theorem's range/extension hypotheses. No uniform
   eigenvalue lower bound for the present N-dependent matrices is supplied
   by that general theorem.

F. Y. Barhoumi-Andréani, M. Butzek, and P. Eichelsbacher, “A surrogate by
   exchangeability approach to the Curie-Weiss model,” arXiv:2305.06872,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06872 . The paper gives an explicit finite
   de Finetti density for the ordinary ferromagnetic Curie-Weiss law. It
   again uses the usual quadratic per-configuration Gibbs weight, so it is
   useful structural context but does not identify a mixing density for
   q_k^(N).

Net result and decisive next gate
---------------------------------
The primary literature supplies exact membership frameworks and strong
nearby Curie-Weiss results, but no theorem located in this targeted search
proves or refutes the count-Gaussian family for any fixed positive delta
uniformly in N. The false shortcut is to transfer ferromagnetic Curie-Weiss
infinite extendibility from a quadratic per-string Gibbs weight; the inverse
binomial factor changes the family. The useful bounds are: uniform
log-convexity survives all N exactly through delta=2 log 2, the N=2
obstruction makes that cutoff necessary, and the variance obstruction only
appears above delta=2 asymptotically. The decisive unresolved step is to
prove H0/H1 PSD (with singular range conditions) for every N and, ideally,
produce an explicit representing measure mu_N, or find a higher-order
negative minor. The tests reported by root through N=64 are finite evidence,
not this theorem.

Cost/boundary: no numerical or symbolic computation was used for the
claims above; all displayed inequalities are closed-form. For each fixed N,
exact feasibility can be checked by a finite semidefinite/Hankel test, but
that incurs matrices of order Theta(N), and checking finitely many N has no
uniform-in-N force. No source-priority or novelty claim is made.

Targeted literature result on the construction mechanism
--------------------------------------------------------
After exposure I ran a second primary-source search specifically for a
probability construction combining (i) a finite imaginary-Gaussian / inverse-
heat shift of the beta-logit or binomial kernel, (ii) a uniform strictly
positive density margin, and (iii) an exact finite-degree moment correction.
I found no direct analogue in the primary probability literature surveyed.
This is a bounded search result, not a novelty or priority claim.

The closest exact probability references remain the Curto–Fialkow truncated
Stieltjes criteria and the Liggett–Steif–Toth Curie–Weiss / finite-extension
paper above. The latter's standard antiferromagnetic Curie–Weiss law has no
positive iid-mixture representation, and its near-zero finite-l extension
formula is not the c07 positive-density correction for the reciprocal-
binomial count-Gaussian law. The Barhoumi-Andréani et al. finite Curie-Weiss
de Finetti density is likewise for the ordinary ferromagnetic Gibbs family.
There is a method-level but non-probability neighbor: Farouki, “Legendre–Bernstein basis transformations,” Journal of
Computational and Applied Mathematics 119 (2000), 145–160,
https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-0427(00)00376-9, develops the basis
conversion used for the correction coefficients. A related condition-number
paper is Farouki and Lyche, “On the p-norm condition number of the
multivariate triangular Bernstein basis,” J. Comput. Appl. Math. 119 (2000),
259–273, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0377-0427(00)00383-6. These are method-level
approximation-theory ingredients, not probability-mixture constructions. That makes the correction
algebra a standard approximation-theory ingredient; it does not provide a
positive mixing density, imaginary-heat contour, or uniform all-N
probabilistic completion.

No change to the primary conclusion: before this authorized cross-exposure,
my independent lane established exact necessary bounds but no positive
certificate. The c07 result is a separate attributed proof route and should
be credited as such; no merged proof or independent replication is claimed
