C01_L03 FINAL — EXTERIOR ALGEBRA / PLÜCKER ATTACK
7 October 2026, 13:43:59 UTC

The universal one-column Grassmann–Plücker identity and its Cauchy–Schwarz consequence remain the strongest positive result. For rank-r minors p_A,p_B and any a∈A\B, with d=|B\A|,

  |p_A p_B|^2 ≤ d Σ_{b∈B\A}|p_{A-a+b} p_{B-b+a}|^2.

The proof is the alternating cofactor dependence of r+1 columns, wedged with A\{a}; shared columns vanish and Cauchy–Schwarz gives the bound. It covers zero minors, arbitrary common cores, and complex matrices. It provides a pointwise O(r)-term cross-replica certificate and a local mass/acceptance bound. Exact seeded tests covered 17,940 ordered GP instances, 11,586 nonzero endpoints, and all ordered F4 pair-closed instances; see the immutable initial checkpoint and `plucker_replica_sos_result.txt`.

The deeper attack found the exact boundary of projecting this identity back to pair-closed bases. For F the cyclic permutation matrix on n=2q sites, precisely the two alternating pair bases are supported. Every proper partial pair replacement has zero determinant. In the ordinary-column Plücker expansion between the two supported endpoints, the unique nonzero exchange term is split across the two replicas. Thus no finite-factor inequality using only pair-closed one-step destinations can follow from this expansion.

Retaining split-column defects can restore connectivity in this example: there is an explicit n-step symmetric cross-swap path between the endpoints, and every intermediate determinant product has modulus one. This path is shortest because all n columns must change replicas. Exact Bareiss enumeration for n=4,6 verifies the GP support, path, and zero pair-exchange destinations. It is a special-case path, not a general congestion argument.

A character-register formulation makes the lifted-state obstacle precise. The group (Z2)^n that multiplies both columns of each pair decomposes ordinary Plücker basis states by their split-label set. Pair-closed states are the trivial character sector. One-column cross-swaps toggle the same two character bits in both replicas, preserving their relative character. On q independent two-site swap blocks, the ordinary DPP volume partition is 4^q and the pair-closed partition 2^q, so conditioning the unrestricted volume sample on pair closure costs exactly 2^-q. The 2^q split-character sectors have equal mass; a natural shared-character diagonal projection also retains only fraction 2^-q of the independent tensor norm. Exact checks for q=1,2,3 are in `revisions/plucker_pair_closure_audit_result.json`.

Consequently the obvious alternatives fail in opposite ways: restrict to pair-closed states and the Plücker exchange graph can disconnect; allow the natural determinant-weighted defect sectors and the target sector can be exponentially rare. A successful construction must retain independent mismatch registers while assigning defect weights so that the paired endpoint marginal is exact and the reversible energy/congestion remains polynomial. The pointwise Plücker inequality alone does not give this reweighting, global connectivity, or a Poincaré inequality. The cross-swap walk also preserves the replicas’ column multiset, so it cannot by itself compare different union/intersection fibers.

External scope check: Larson’s 2026 paper gives a rank-9 binary matroid counterexample to general basis-pair symmetric-exchange connectivity ([primary arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02208)); the specific binary obstruction did not survive the separately tested rational lift, so it is only a caution against inferring global connectivity from the local identity. The regular-matroid result ([Bérczi–Mátravölgyi–Schwarcz](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07130)) is restricted to regular matroids and does not cover arbitrary rational-complex instances. Neither source is load-bearing for the Plücker proof.
