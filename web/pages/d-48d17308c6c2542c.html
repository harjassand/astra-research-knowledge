A25 PHASE IV — SECOND-MOMENT-ONLY SPECTRAL INITIALIZATION
Version 3, 2026-10-09 (current; preserves v1-v2 history)
Status: signed-moment route internally derived; finite diagnostics run; near-linear robust signed-matrix algorithm unresolved. No discovery or priority claim.

1. EXACT TARGET AND CONTRACT

Clean observations are iid (a,Y), with a~N(0,I_d), fixed x!=0, q=||x||,

    S=(a^T x)^2,    Y=S+zeta,
    E[zeta|a]=0,    E[zeta^2|a]=sigma^2.

There is no fourth-moment or tail assumption on zeta. After drawing all m clean pairs, a strong adversary may inspect them and replace at most epsilon m whole pairs. The initializer target is a unit vector close to +/-x/q. Put s=sigma/q^2. The response and signal are on the same scale as y; sigma^2 is the conditional noise variance, so sigma is the response standard deviation.

The request is to remove the extra factor 1+s^2 in the PSD-shifted initializer, aim at m~d(1+s^2)/epsilon with near-linear runtime, test sharpness of the clipping bias and any information-theoretic barrier, and charge scale acquisition, robust threshold selection, PCA, precision, sample and runtime costs. No fourth-moment premise or adaptively acquired fresh-data oracle is silently added.

2. POPULATION IDENTITIES

Let M=E[Y aa^T]. Gaussian fourth moments and conditional mean zero give

    E[S aa^T]=q^2 I+2xx^T,
    M=q^2 I+2xx^T.                                                   (2.1)

Thus lambda_1(M)=3q^2, lambda_2(M)=q^2, and the top eigengap is 2q^2. This moment is available under A25's second-moment model and is the natural signed initializer.

A different second moment is

    Q0=E[Y^2 aa^T]=(3q^4+sigma^2)I+12q^2 xx^T.                       (2.2)

The cross term vanishes after conditioning on a. Its eigenvalues are 15q^4+sigma^2 along x and 3q^4+sigma^2 perpendicular to x. In particular, the operator scale of Q0 is 15q^4+sigma^2. Equation (2.2) uses only the conditional second moment of zeta; it does not require E[zeta^4|a].

3. WHY THE SHIFTED PSD REPRESENTATION COSTS AN EXTRA NOISE FACTOR

For c_L(y)=clip(y,[-L,L]), define

    X_L = sqrt(L+c_L(Y)) a,       0 <= L+c_L(Y) <= 2L.

Its population covariance is

    E[X_L X_L^T]= L I + E[c_L(Y)aa^T].                                (3.1)

Since |Y-c_L(Y)|<=Y^2/L, for any unit v,

    |v^T E[(Y-c_L(Y))aa^T]v|
       <= L^{-1} E[Y^2(a^T v)^2]
       <= (15q^4+sigma^2)/L.                                         (3.2)

Choose L=C(q^2+sigma^2/q^2)=Cq^2(1+s^2), with a sufficiently large constant, so (3.2) is a small fraction of q^2 and the gap in (3.1) stays constant order q^2. This uses no fourth moment.

For the PSD summand T_L=X_LX_L^T, ||T_L||<=2L||a||^2 and

    T_L^2=(L+c_L(Y))^2 ||a||^2 aa^T.

Using 0<=L+c_L(Y)<=2L and Gaussian design moments gives a matrix-variance scale of order dL^2 (up to log factors after design truncation). Matrix Bernstein therefore needs

    m_shift = O(d L^2/q^4 * logs)
             = O(d(1+s^2)^2 * logs).                                 (3.3)

A near-linear robust-PCA call additionally needs the inlier stability and its own stated corruption/sample conditions. The factor (1+s^2) in (3.3) comes from the artificial isotropic shift L: it multiplies every Gaussian row even when the response noise is zero-mean. Equation (3.2) alone does not prove this is a minimax cost.

4. SIGNED MOMENT: CLEAN FINITE-VARIANCE INITIALIZER

A direct signed empirical mean avoids the artificial shift. To make matrix summands bounded while preserving a response-bias bound, set

    h_R(a)=min(1,R^2/||a||^2),       P_R(a)=h_R(a)aa^T,
    Z_{L,R}=c_L(Y)P_R(a).

At a=0, define h_R=1. Let M_{L,R}=E[Z_{L,R}]. The exact difference splits as

    M_{L,R}-M
      = E[(c_L(Y)-Y)P_R(a)] + E[Y(P_R(a)-aa^T)].                     (4.1)

For the first term, P_R is PSD and P_R<=aa^T. Thus

    ||E[(c_L(Y)-Y)P_R(a)]||op <= (15q^4+sigma^2)/L.                    (4.2)

For the second term, P_R-aa^T depends only on a. The noise cancels exactly:

    E[zeta(P_R-aa^T)]=E[E[zeta|a](P_R-aa^T)]=0.

The remaining design-winsorization term has a minus sign:

    E[S(P_R-aa^T)] = -E[S(1-h_R(a))aa^T] <= 0.                        (4.3)

For unit v, the absolute value of its quadratic form is bounded by

    E[(a^T x)^2(a^T v)^2 1{||a||>R}]
      <= sqrt(E[(a^T x)^4(a^T v)^4)] sqrt(P{chi_d^2>R^2})
      <= sqrt(105)q^2 sqrt(P{chi_d^2>R^2}).                            (4.4)

Taking R^2=C(d+log(1/beta)) makes (4.4)<=beta q^2 for a universal sufficiently large C. Taking L>=C(q^4+sigma^2)/q^2 makes (4.2)<=beta q^2. Hence ||M_{L,R}-M||<=2 beta q^2, and Weyl gives a top gap at least 2q^2-4 beta q^2. For beta<1/4 this stays positive and constant order q^2.

For each sample, ||Z_{L,R}||op<=LR^2. Also

    Z_{L,R}^2=c_L(Y)^2 h_R(a)^2 ||a||^2 aa^T
                <= R^2 Y^2 P_R(a)
                <= R^2 Y^2 aa^T.

Consequently the matrix variance is at most R^2 Q0 in Loewner order, with operator norm at most R^2(15q^4+sigma^2). For iid clean samples, matrix Bernstein gives, with probability at least 1-delta,

    ||m^{-1} sum Z_i - M_{L,R}||op
      <= C sqrt(R^2(15q^4+sigma^2) log(2d/delta)/m)
        + C L R^2 log(2d/delta)/m.                                   (4.5)

With R^2=Theta(d+log(1/beta)) and L=Theta(q^2(1+s^2)), each term is <=c q^2 for

    m_clean = O((d+log(1/beta))(1+s^2) log(2d/delta)).                (4.6)

This is a fully derived clean signed-matrix concentration result under only conditional second moments. A top eigenvector of the clean signed empirical matrix is constant-angle accurate by Davis–Kahan once the bias and (4.5) are each a sufficiently small fraction of q^2. It removes one factor 1+s^2 from (3.3). It is not yet a robust algorithm: the ordinary signed empirical matrix can be changed arbitrarily by a single replacement.

5. STRONG-CONTAMINATION STABILITY OF THE SIGNED EMPIRICAL MATRIX

The bounded response and radial design map make a precise inlier deletion bound possible. Let w_i in [0,1], alpha=m^{-1}sum_i(1-w_i)<=epsilon, and define

    Mhat_w = [m^{-1} sum_i w_i Z_i]/(1-alpha),
    Mhat   = m^{-1} sum_i Z_i,
    D      = m^{-1} sum_i (1-w_i)Z_i.

Then exactly

    Mhat_w-M_{L,R}=[(Mhat-M_{L,R})-D+alpha M_{L,R}]/(1-alpha).        (5.1)

Since |c_L(Y_i)|<=L and P_R(a_i)<=a_i a_i^T,

    ||D||op <= L ||m^{-1}sum_i(1-w_i)a_i a_i^T||op.                  (5.2)

For iid standard Gaussian rows, a standard subset-net argument gives simultaneously for all q_i in [0,1] with sum q_i<=epsilon m,

    ||m^{-1}sum_i q_i a_i a_i^T||op
      <= C[d/m + epsilon log(e/epsilon) + log(1/delta)/m],             (5.3)

with probability 1-delta (for epsilon m<1, retain the exact d/m+log(1/delta)/m form). To see (5.3), for a fixed subset I of k=ceil(epsilon m) rows, ||A_I||op^2 is O(d+k+k log(em/k)+log(1/delta)); union-bound over at most (em/k)^k subsets. For fractional weights, maximize each directional quadratic form by filling the largest k row scores, so the subset bound applies. This event is adaptive to the full clean dataset and therefore supports an adversary that chooses rows after seeing all observations.

Combining (4.5), (5.1)-(5.3) gives the sufficient additive stability bound

    ||Mhat_w-M_{L,R}||op
      <= [e_M + C L(d/m+epsilon log(e/epsilon)+log(1/delta)/m)
                 + epsilon ||M_{L,R}||op]/(1-epsilon),               (5.4)

where e_M is the right side of (4.5). Since ||M_{L,R}||op=O(q^2) after the bias choice, (5.4) is a small fraction of q^2 if

    epsilon log(e/epsilon) <= c/(1+s^2),
    m >= C(d+log(1/beta))(1+s^2)log(d/delta).                         (5.5)

Thus the *clean inlier signed empirical matrix* is stable at the desired eigengap scale under the same second-moment contamination tradeoff. But (5.4) is a stability certificate, not an algorithm that identifies the inlier weighted mean from arbitrary replacements. The cited near-linear robust-PCA interface is for PSD covariance samples and cannot be invoked on the signed matrices Z_i by merely relabeling them.

6. RAYLEIGH-QUERY ROUTE AND A STATISTICAL ESTIMATOR

For any unit u, the scalar query

    W_u=Y(a^T u)^2

has the correct mean u^T M u and finite variance. Conditional mean zero eliminates the cross term and E[zeta^2|a]=sigma^2 yields

    E[W_u^2]
      = E[(a^T x)^4(a^T u)^4] + 3sigma^2
      <= sqrt(E[(a^T x)^8]E[(a^T u)^8])+3sigma^2
      = 105q^4+3sigma^2.                                               (6.1)

No fourth moment of zeta occurs. This is a sharper noise dependence than the variance of the shifted PSD samples.

Here is an explicit, but exponential-time, strong-contamination estimator establishing a scale-free statistical upper bound without response clipping. Take a Euclidean 1/4-net N of the unit sphere with |N|<=9^d. For 0<epsilon<c0/2, choose a small universal c0 and request exactly m=kb observations in k blocks of size b=floor(c0/epsilon). For each u in N, compute the average of W_u in each block and take the median theta_u. For a clean block, Chebyshev and (6.1) give

    P(|blockmean-u^TMu|>alpha) <= (105q^4+3sigma^2)/(b alpha^2).

Take alpha=q^2/64 for the proof. If epsilon(1+s^2)<=c1 for a sufficiently small universal c1 relative to c0, then b>=c0/(2epsilon) and the right side is at most 1/16. A Chernoff bound and union bound over N show that, with probability 1-delta, fewer than k/4 clean block means are bad simultaneously for all u, provided k>=C(d+log(1/delta)).

Each replaced observation can spoil at most one block. With b=floor(c0/epsilon), the adversary can affect at most epsilon k b <=c0 k blocks. Choose c0<1/8. Therefore more than half of the block estimates for every u remain within alpha, even when the adversary chooses replacements after seeing all clean observations. Hence |theta_u-u^TMu|<=alpha simultaneously on N.

Output the net direction maximizing theta_u. If v=x/q and u0 in N has ||u0-v||<=1/4, then v^T M v-u0^T M u0=2q^2(1-(u0^Tv)^2)<=0.124q^2. Uniform error alpha means the output uhat satisfies 3q^2-uhat^T M uhat<=0.124q^2+2alpha<0.16q^2. Since this Rayleigh deficit is exactly 2q^2 sin^2(angle(uhat,x)), the output has constant angle. The required costs for epsilon>0 are

    b = Theta(1/epsilon),
    k = Theta(d+log(1/delta)),
    m = kb = Theta((d+log(1/delta))/epsilon),
    epsilon <= c/(1+s^2).

This uses epsilon, dimension and confidence to form blocks; it does not require q, sigma, a response threshold, fourth moments, random repartitioning, or causally acquired batches. For epsilon=0, use ordinary clean block means; the sample bound is m=O((d+log(1/delta))(1+s^2)). The fixed-sample guarantee assumes epsilon is supplied, as in the AISTATS strong-contamination interface; if epsilon is unknown, selecting b needs a separate grid/selection argument. The estimator's guarantees are instance-wise in s, so deciding whether a fixed m suffices still needs a known ratio bound or a separately justified stopping rule.

This statistical sample bound is no larger than the requested O(d(1+s^2)/epsilon) target and can be smaller by a factor 1+s^2 in the admissible contamination regime. It is not near-linear: N is exponential in d, and evaluating all W_u costs O(m d 9^d) arithmetic. The estimator is a valid statistical witness that the extra noise factor in the shifted initializer is not an information-theoretic necessity for direction estimation. It does not close the requested computational objective.

A plausible algorithmic direction is an efficient robust operator-mean estimator for rank-one symmetric observations y aa^T, or a robust optimization method that maximizes the uniform median-of-means Rayleigh functional. The standard PSD robust-PCA theorem does not provide this. Matrix-vector products of the unrobust signed mean cost O(md), but robustifying the mean separately for adaptive directions does not produce one fixed linear operator, and exponential-net maximization cannot be counted as near-linear runtime.

7. THE RESPONSE-CLIPPING BIAS IS WORST-CASE SHARP

The upper bound in (3.2) has a sigma^2/L component that cannot be improved in order under only conditional mean and variance. Fix L>=sigma and define, conditionally independently of a,

    A=2L,       p=sigma^2/(A^2+sigma^2),       b=sigma^2/A,
    zeta=A       with probability p,
    zeta=-b       with probability 1-p.

Then E[zeta|a]=0 and

    E[zeta^2|a]=pA^2+(1-p)b^2=sigma^2.

Also b<=L, so for any S=(a^Tx)^2>=0, the common response S-b is never below -L. The rare response S+A is always above L. Thus both clipping residuals Y-c_L(Y) are nonnegative and, conditionally on a,

    E[Y-c_L(Y)|a] >= p(A-L)=pL
      = sigma^2 L/(4L^2+sigma^2).

Since E[aa^T]=I,

    E[(Y-c_L(Y))aa^T] >= [sigma^2 L/(4L^2+sigma^2)] I.              (7.1)

For L/sigma large, the coefficient is (1/4+o(1))sigma^2/L. Therefore the conditional noise class itself attains the sigma^2/L clipping-bias order. The signal's own Gaussian tail adds a nonnegative term in this construction; it does not cancel the lower bound. This refutes attempts to improve (3.2) uniformly while retaining scalar response clipping as the only tail control. It does not refute robust signed-mean or median-of-means methods, which need not clip the response.

Finite diagnostic: a 2,000,000-row simulation at d=4,q=1,sigma=1,L=20 observed a perpendicular clipping-bias diagonal of 0.01249, against the exact lower floor pL=0.01249 and sigma^2/L=0.05. The rare-event count was 1,219 versus expectation 1,249. The complete output and code are `work/applied/a25_shifted_initializer/a25_finite_checks.py`; numerical agreement is only a finite diagnostic, while (7.1) is the exact proof.

8. CONTAMINATION LOWER BOUND: EPSILON OF ORDER 1/S^2 IS NECESSARY

The constant-angle contamination threshold in (5.5) is also unavoidable over the second-moment-only noise class. Fix two equal-norm parameters x,x' with angle theta, and for each a put

    mu0=(a^T x)^2,  mu1=(a^T x')^2,  Delta=mu0-mu1,
    mid=(mu0+mu1)/2,
    p=Delta^2/(Delta^2+4sigma^2),
    A=|Delta|/(2p).

For Delta>0, define conditional response laws

    P0=(1-p)delta_mid+p delta_(mid+A),
    P1=(1-p)delta_mid+p delta_(mid-A),

and reverse the rare signs when Delta<0. Their means are mu0 and mu1. Their conditional variances are

    p(1-p)A^2 = sigma^2,

so the corresponding noises have conditional mean zero and the required constant conditional variance. The conditional total variation is exactly p, since the common atom has mass 1-p. The design marginal is identical under both models, hence

    TV(P_x,P_x')=E_a p
      <= E_a[Delta^2]/(4sigma^2).

For equal q, tr(xx^T-x'x'^T)=0 and Gaussian quadratic-form moments give

    E_a[Delta^2]=2||xx^T-x'x'^T||_F^2=4q^4 sin^2(theta).

Consequently

    TV(P_x,P_x') <= sin^2(theta)/s^2.                                  (8.1)

Two Huber epsilon-contamination neighborhoods overlap whenever TV<=epsilon/(1-epsilon). The same order applies to the strong fixed-fraction model: maximal coupling of m clean pairs mismatches a TV fraction, and when TV is a sufficiently small fraction of epsilon, a randomized adversary can replace all mismatches within budget with high probability. Therefore constant-angle recovery is impossible uniformly over this noise class when epsilon is at least c/s^2. This is an identifiability obstruction, not a computational lower bound. It matches the leading epsilon scale from (5.5) up to the log(e/epsilon) stability loss.

Finite diagnostic: at d=8,q=1,sigma=5,theta=0.3, the script estimated E[Delta^2]=0.348895 versus exact 0.349329 and the constructed pair-law TV=0.003387 versus upper bound 0.003493. The asymptotic contamination floor is sin^2(theta)/s^2=0.003493. See the output of `a25_finite_checks.py`.

9. SAMPLE LOWER BOUND FOR THE NOISE SCALE

For the allowed special case zeta|a~N(0,sigma^2), conditional response laws are Gaussian with equal variance. For equal-norm x,x', their one-sample KL divergence is

    KL(P_x||P_x')=E[Delta^2]/(2sigma^2)
                   =2 sin^2(theta)/s^2.                              (9.1)

A constant-angle packing of the q-sphere modulo sign has exp(c d) points. Fano applied to (9.1) implies m=Omega(d s^2) for constant-relative directional recovery when s>=1. This matches the high-noise factor in (4.6) up to logarithms. This gives the high-noise lower bound only; A25 does not claim a separate lower bound for the signal-dominated regime. The contamination lower bound (8.1) is separate and persists as m tends to infinity.

10. UNKNOWN SCALE AND THRESHOLD ACCOUNTING

The signed Rayleigh/net estimator does not require q or sigma to compute its output; for epsilon>0 its block size uses the known corruption level only. Its sample count is sufficient when m is at least C(d+log(1/delta))/epsilon and epsilon<=c/(1+s^2). The actual scale still determines whether the guarantee applies, so choosing a sample budget without a supplied ratio bound needs a separately justified stopping rule.

For the clipped signed matrix (Section 4), the response threshold L does require an upper bound on sigma^2/q^2, and R requires a dimension/confidence choice. If a valid supplied sigma upper bound is sigma_bar, use L=C(q_hat^2+sigma_bar^2/q_hat^2) after obtaining q_hat. The response mean satisfies E[Y]=q^2 and Var(Y)=2q^4+sigma^2. A one-dimensional robust mean/median-of-means scale estimate to constant relative accuracy has the corresponding order (1+s^2)log(1/delta) samples when epsilon<=c/(1+s^2), with constants depending on the robust mean guarantee. The `q_hat` computation itself can be scale-free as a median-of-means point estimate, but proving/choosing a same-batch threshold requires uniformization over its candidate values.

There is no uniform finite-sample upper-confidence estimator of sigma^2 under only the stated conditional second moment: for any sample size m and confidence delta, a noise law that is zero except on a symmetric rare event of probability p<<delta/m, with amplitude sigma/sqrt(p), has variance sigma^2 but produces an all-zero observed noise sample with probability at least 1-delta. Increasing sigma while reducing p leaves this event probability unchanged. This does not prove that a parameter-free signed estimator fails; it means one cannot silently select L from an empirical variance estimate and claim a certified upper bound. A Lepski/grid threshold selector with contaminated data would need a separate uniform proof and is not supplied here.

11. RESOURCE AND CLAIM ACCOUNTING

* Shifted PSD candidate (known valid upper scales): sample O(d(1+s^2)^2 log factors); vector input dimension d; robust-PCA runtime from the A07/DKKPPI path is near-linear in its input times polynomial factors in the stability/corruption parameters; no finite-precision implementation checked here.
* Clean signed clipped estimator: sample O(d(1+s^2)log(d/delta)); each matrix-vector multiply costs O(md); forming the full matrix explicitly costs O(md^2), so a near-linear eigenvector routine must use implicit products.
* Signed adaptive-deletion stability: proved at scale q^2 when epsilon log(e/epsilon)≲1/(1+s^2), with the exact finite-sample terms in (5.4). It is a data property, not an estimator guarantee against arbitrary replacements.
* Rayleigh median estimator: for known epsilon>0, sample O((d+log(1/delta))/epsilon) under epsilon≲1/(1+s^2); for epsilon=0, sample O((d+log(1/delta))(1+s^2)); exponential-net runtime O(md 9^d). It is a valid statistical witness that no fourth moment is needed for direction estimation.
* Replacement handling for a near-linear signed-matrix mean, with a single globally strong adversary, remains the decisive missing algorithmic step. Generic vectorizing of d-by-d matrices has dimension d^2 and loses the target near-linear cost. Separate fresh-batch adaptive-query claims are not used.
* Norm q and noise sigma acquisition are charged above. The task's desired m~d(1+s^2)/epsilon is not established by a near-linear algorithm here. The proved statistical net route has no 1/epsilon factor in the clean sample term, but is exponential-time; the contamination floor remains.
* Arithmetic/precision: exact real arithmetic is assumed for displayed spectral calculations. In a finite-precision implementation, all matrix-vector and top-eigenvector residuals would need total operator error below a fixed c q^2 gap margin. No bit-complexity or numerical residual bound is proved.

Unresolved: a near-linear, single-batch robust signed-matrix mean/top-eigenvector algorithm under the stated strong corruption model; whether the A07 stability-based robust-PCA filtering proof can be adapted to signed rank-one matrix means without d^2 cost; a same-data robust threshold selector if clipping is retained; externally independent proof review; finite precision; novelty/priority.

The target near-linear robust initializer is not achieved in this finite phase. No breakthrough claim is made.
