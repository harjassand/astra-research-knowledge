N23 — SHARP SINGLE-LAG BINARY ENTROPY-PRODUCTION FLOOR
Version v1; 9 October 2026
Status: derived universal strengthening and finite construction; exact all-dimension infimum unresolved. Internal arithmetic check supplied. No novelty or external-validation claim.

1. Frozen target and quantifiers

Let X be a stationary finite-state time-homogeneous CTMC with all microscopic states even under reversal, positive stationary law pi on its recurrent class, and finite local-detailed-balance entropy production rate sigma (nats per time, k_B=1). Observe Y=g(X) in {0,1}. Fix t>0 and p=P(Y_0=1), and write C=Cov(Y_0,Y_t)<0 and delta=-C. The problem is the infimum of sigma over every such finite CTMC that exactly realizes this one pair table, with no hidden-state or activity cap. This is a pair-only constraint; matching a full observed path law is a stronger and different problem. The result below also applies to physical channel-resolved EP since it is at least the state-path EP.

Stationarity makes the pair table symmetric: P(1,1)=p^2-delta and P(1,0)=P(0,1)=p(1-p)+delta. Feasibility requires 0<delta<=p(1-p), with strict endpoint restrictions for nondegenerate finite models as appropriate.

2. Midpoint factorization and nonlinear lower bound

Put s=t/2. Conditional on X_0=i, let U_i=P(Y_{-s}=1|X_0=i) and V_i=P(Y_s=1|X_0=i). By conditional independence of past and future given X_0,

  E U=E V=p,       E[UV]=p^2-delta.

Write m=(U+V)/2 and tau=|U-V|. Then

  -delta = Cov(U,V) = Var(m)-E[(U-V)^2]/4,
  hence E[tau^2]>=4 delta.                                      (A)

The average conditional path Jeffreys divergence over the two half paths is t sigma. Data processing to their observed binary endpoints gives

  t sigma >= E J_B(U,V),
  J_B(u,v)=(u-v)(logit(u)-logit(v)).                         (B)

For fixed tau=|u-v|, J_B is minimized when (u+v)/2=1/2: indeed the logit difference is the integral over an interval of length tau of the symmetric convex function 1/[x(1-x)], minimized by centering the interval at 1/2. The minimum equals 4 tau atanh(tau). Thus, using g(z)=sqrt(z) atanh(sqrt(z))=sum_{k>=0} z^{k+1}/(2k+1), which is increasing and convex on [0,1), Jensen and (A) yield

  t sigma >= 4 E[tau atanh(tau)]
           = 4 E[g(tau^2)]
          >= 4 g(E[tau^2])
          >= 4 g(4 delta)
           = 8 sqrt(delta) atanh(2 sqrt(delta)).               (C)

The bound is strict relative to 16 delta for every delta>0, since atanh(x)>x for x>0. Its small-delta expansion is 16 delta +(64/3)delta^2+O(delta^3). This improves the linear covariance floor in the root v2 and the 4I/t pair-information floor when the latter is weaker. No claim is made that (C) is the exact physical infimum.

3. Exact abstract midpoint cost at p=1/2

For p=1/2, the binary midpoint-factorization convex roof K(P) from root v2 is exactly the right side of (C). Set r=sqrt(delta), put equal atom weights on

  (U,V)=(1/2+r,1/2-r), (1/2-r,1/2+r).

Their mixture has the target pair table, and each atom has Jeffreys cost 8r atanh(2r); the lower bound (C) proves optimality over all such factorizations. Thus K(P)=8sqrt(delta)atanh(2sqrt(delta)) for this symmetric pair. For p!=1/2, (C) remains valid but this note does not solve K(P).

Distinguish three statements: (i) this factorization is feasible in the abstract latent-midpoint relaxation; (ii) below it is realized by a finite CTMC for a nontrivial interval of delta; (iii) its Jeffreys objective equals the CTMC EP. Statements (i) and (ii) hold for the displayed family. Statement (iii) is impossible for any finite positive-EP CTMC, by the strict data-processing argument below. It remains open here whether unbounded finite generators can approach K(P)/t.

4. Finite CTMC that realizes the optimal conditional factors

Take the four-state ring j mod 4 with uniform stationary law, clockwise rate a and counterclockwise rate b, both positive. Let S=a+b, J=a-b, and observe 1 on states {0,1}, 0 on {2,3}. For centered f=1_{Y=1}-1/2, the n=4 Fourier mode gives

  Cov(Y_0,Y_t)= (1/4) exp(-S t) cos(J t).

Choose Jt=pi and St>pi (equivalently a=(S+J)/2>b=(S-J)/2). Then

  delta=(1/4)exp(-St) in (0,exp(-pi)/4),
  U_i,V_i are complementary 1/2 +/- (1/2)exp(-Ss) for every i,
  and E J_B(U_i,V_i)=8sqrt(delta)atanh(2sqrt(delta))=K(P).

The state-level EP is

  sigma=J log(a/b)=2J atanh(J/S),
  sigma t=2 pi atanh(pi/(St)) > K(P),

where the strict inequality also follows from the path data-processing argument in §5. The four-cycle is a physical finite LDB realization by coupling its clockwise/counterclockwise jumps to a chemical reservoir with cycle affinity 4 log(a/b); equal state energies are compatible with the uniform stationary law.

5. Why no finite chain attains midpoint data-processing equality

For a finite irreducible CTMC, compare the conditional half-path laws from midpoint state i under Q and its stationary adjoint Q*. Their likelihood ratio on a path is the sum of edge affinities log(pi_x k_xy/(pi_y k_yx)); the escape-rate terms cancel because Q and Q* have equal statewise exit rates. If sigma>0, some recurrent cycle has nonzero affinity A. At a base state i on that cycle, the no-jump path and a positive-probability path that traverses the cycle and returns to i both end at hidden state i, hence have the same observed binary endpoint, but their path log-likelihood ratios are 0 and A. The likelihood ratio is therefore not measurable with respect to the endpoint observation. Strictness in KL data processing gives strict inequality in (B) on that midpoint state, hence t sigma > E J_B(U,V). Negative pair covariance rules out detailed balance: for reversible Q, <f,e^{tQ}f>_pi>=0. Thus every finite compatible irreducible chain has sigma>0 and the midpoint projection is strictly lossy.

This is finite nonattainment only. It does not provide a uniform gap as hidden dimension grows, and so it cannot decide whether inf sigma=K(P)/t. No approaching sequence for K(P) was found.

7. Primary-source audit and scope

A. Baseline additive reversibilization is independently present in the 3 Oct 2026 Evidence Press v1.1.0-candidate release “Hidden dissipation without a known architecture,” whose PAPER.tex is linked at https://github.com/ipitchford/hidden-dissipation-floor/blob/main/PAPER.tex and release page at https://evidencepress.org/releases/hidden-dissipation-floor/. Its § additive reversibilization establishes the same stationary path-KL comparison D(P_Q^[0,T]||P_R^[0,T])<=T sigma/4. Its two-clock theorem uses conditional past/future persistence tests and the fact their reversible comparison has nonnegative covariance. That method and the factor-1/4 comparison are established candidate prior art for the current work; they are not claimed as N23 discoveries.

B. The Evidence Press release claims a zero-cost classification for a different, stronger data constraint: the entire stationary ergodic two-label alternating-renewal path law P is fixed exactly, and all finite deterministic-observation CTMC realizations are allowed. Its Theorem in § dichotomy states V_infty(P)=0 iff both complete dwell densities are finite positive mixtures of exponentials iff a finite reversible exact realization exists. For its 3-state ring family P_q it gives a positive all-dimension lower certificate and a ring upper bound, while explicitly leaving the positive optimum and finite-dimensional attainment open. It is an unrefereed mathematical candidate with internal replay, not external human review or independent validation. Its full-path renewal theorem does not settle the pair-only infimum considered here; the pair table forgets the waiting-time law.

C. The linked earlier preprint Murad Aznagulov, arXiv:2609.14623v1 (13 Sep 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14623, proves fixed-hidden-dimension attainment even with unrestricted rates and treats a priced-architecture problem. For its two-block renewal family it proves positive V_n for every feasible finite n but explicitly leaves open whether V_n tends to positive V_infty or to zero as n grows. The 3 Oct Evidence Press v1.1 candidate presents a subsequent zero-limit theorem for this renewal subclass. Neither result gives the exact optimum from a single observed binary pair table.

D. Aznagulov, arXiv:2609.37205v1, “The Spectral Cost of Detecting Nonequilibrium at Finite Temporal Resolution,” https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37205, studies the number of sampled transitions needed to detect an irreversible generator against arbitrary hidden reversible nulls when gaps have a hard lower bound. Its objective is a sampling/detection cost; it does not claim a sharp EP minimization for one negative-covariance pair.

E. For complete dwell laws, the release credits the reversible spectral-mixture restriction to earlier work by Skinner and Dunkel and Tu. This is not new background. The source page labels its own result unrefereed and states that no priority certification or external validation is provided. Therefore this audit records a direct overlap and scope boundary, not an absence-of-prior-art claim.

8. Exact unresolved gate

For fixed (p,delta,t), define V_pair(p,delta,t)=inf sigma over all finite-state, time-homogeneous, all-even LDB CTMCs realizing exactly the stationary binary pair table. Established here is

  V_pair >= [8 sqrt(delta) atanh(2sqrt(delta))]/t,

with exact equality for the abstract midpoint factorization at p=1/2, and a finite 4-cycle that realizes those conditional factors for 0<delta<e^{-pi}/4 while paying strictly larger path EP. Unknown: whether V_pair equals this relaxation as an infimum, whether a stronger dimension-free gap holds, what the exact V_pair is for any nontrivial parameter set, and whether a finite generator can ever attain V_pair. A decisive next proof must either construct a sequence of finite generators with EP t approaching K(P), or derive a stronger obstruction from time-homogeneous semigroup compatibility that survives unbounded dimension and activity. The current strict-cycle argument alone does neither.
