Frozen 2026-10-09. This is a source-dependent theorem, not a priority claim.

Precise conclusion. Assuming the stated curve construction in BSSZ,
arXiv:2605.28781v1, Section 7.3, there are infinitely many finite fields
E_i=F_(1024^m_i), m_i prime, and subsets R_i of E_i^* with n_i=|R_i|
such that, eventually,

  |E_i|^.16 <= n_i <= |E_i|^.17,
  |R_i+R_i| <= n_i^1.89,  |R_i R_i| <= n_i^1.91,
  |R_i intersect (a+xi K)| <= 1024

for every proper subfield K, every a in E_i, and every nonzero xi in E_i.
Moreover |R_i intersect cK| <= sqrt(|K|) for every subfield K INCLUDING
E_i itself and every c in E_i. The latter is precisely the hypothesis
of Li--Roche-Newton Theorem 1.4, and does not contradict its lower bound.

The indispensable new step is a simultaneous exclusion of all nonconstant
three-point ratios that evaluate into any proper non-base subfield.
This step is proved below independently of BSSZ's additive construction.

1. Abstract evaluation lemma.

Let C/F_q be smooth, projective, geometrically connected, with genus g.
Let D be an effective divisor of degree d and A subset L(D), |A|=N.
Let m>d be prime. For all sufficiently large m relative to g, the set V
of closed points of degree m obeys |V| >= q^m/(2m). More precisely this
holds whenever

 q^m-q-2g(q^(m/2)+sqrt(q)) >= q^m/2.

Proof: every point over F_(q^m) has degree 1 or m, so
 m|V|=#C(F_(q^m)) - #C(F_q).
The two Weil bounds imply the displayed inequality. This is an exact
closed-point count, not a heuristic density estimate.

Every v in V avoids D: otherwise d >= deg(v)=m. Thus evaluation is
defined on L(D). It is injective there: a nonzero h in L(D) has total
zero degree equal to total pole degree <=d; it cannot vanish at a degree
m>d point. In particular f2-f1 never vanishes at v for distinct f1,f2
in A. No bad denominator points have been omitted from the argument.

For each ordered triple with f2 != f1 define
 r=(f3-f1)/(f2-f1).
If r is nonconstant, its rational-map degree is at most 2d. Indeed its
pole divisor is bounded by the poles of f3-f1 plus the zeros of f2-f1.
Each has degree <=d. This deliberately loose bound avoids assumptions
about common base divisors. For each finite set S in an algebraic closure,
the number of geometric points mapping under r into S is at most 2d|S|.
The assertion includes inseparable maps: fiber multiplicities sum to
the map degree, so the number of distinct points is no larger.

Let K be any subfield of F_(q^m). It is invariant under q-Frobenius.
If a degree-m point has r(v) in K under one residue-field embedding,
all m geometric conjugates have values in K. Therefore

 #{v in V: r(v) in K} <= 2d|K|/m.

This is independent of the chosen residue-field isomorphism. For any
finite list of subfields K_j let Z(v) count ordered nonconstant-ratio
triples with r(v) in at least one K_j. Summing, then dividing by |V|,
gives the simultaneous bound

 E_v Z(v) <= 4d N^3 (sum_j |K_j|)/q^m.                 (A)

2. From ratios to affine-subfield concentration.

Let B=ev_v A and assume Z(v)=0. For K among the selected fields put
h=|K intersect F_q|. If B intersect (a+xi K) contains more than h points,
choose distinct x1,x2 there. At most h points x3 satisfy
(x3-x1)/(x2-x1) in K intersect F_q. Every remaining x3 gives a ratio in
K outside F_q and lifts to a unique triple in A. Its rational ratio
cannot be constant: the full constant field of F_q(C) is F_q, a
consequence of geometric connectedness. Such a triple is counted by Z,
a contradiction. Thus the affine intersection is at most h.

More generally an intersection of size t contributes at least
t(t-1)(t-h) distinct triples whenever t>=h. This also proves the
balanced regime below. For t close to a threshold one must use the
asymptotic (1-o(1)) factor, not incorrectly replace this product by t^3.

3. Exact subfield lattice and parameters.

Set q=2^10 and take m prime >5. All proper subfields of E=F_(2^(10m))
are indexed by proper divisors of 10m. Those not contained in F_q are
exactly K_1=F_(2^m), K_2=F_(2^(2m)), K_5=F_(2^(5m)). Their respective
intersections with F_q have sizes 2,4,32 because the intersection
degree is gcd(jm,10)=j. Their sizes sum to at most 3q^(m/2).
Consequently (A) is at most 12d N^3 q^(-m/2).

4. Removing the base-field scalar orbit obstruction.

Choose one representative of each F_q^*-orbit in B_i and call it R_i.
The action is free because no element is zero, so |R_i|=N_i/1023.
For K contained in F_q, any two nonzero points of cK are in the same
F_q^*-orbit; hence |R_i intersect cK|<=1. For K=K_j the cap is at most
2,4,32 respectively, each <=sqrt(|K_j|) eventually. For K=E use
|R_i|<|E|^.17<sqrt(|E|). The case c=0 has empty intersection.
These cases exhaust all subfields. The affine cap is inherited.

The fixed thinning factor is absorbed by the preselected exponent slack:
|R_i+R_i| <= 1023^1.889 |R_i|^1.889 <= |R_i|^1.89 eventually, and
|R_i R_i| <= 1023^1.909 |R_i|^1.909 <= |R_i|^1.91 eventually.
Similarly it preserves the strict .16/.17 density bounds.

5. Balanced companion.

Choosing m_i/g_i ->85 instead gives
E Z <= q^((3rho_+-42.5+o(1))g_i)=o(N_i^2), because
3rho_+-42.5-2rho_- < -0.43449. Some place has this same bound.
An affine K_j intersection t>N_i^(2/3) would give
t(t-1)(t-h) >= (1-o(1))N_i^2, a contradiction. Base-subfield cosets
again have bounded size. Thus all proper affine cosets have at most
N_i^(2/3) points, with |E_i|^.459 <= N_i <= |E_i|^.472 eventually.
The exact square-root multiplicative-coset condition is asserted for
the sparse regime in Section 4; no such implication is made here.

6. Primary comparison and limits.

Source pin: Bloom--Sawin--Schildkraut--Zhelezov, arXiv:2605.28781v1,
https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28781v1, Section 7.2 and Theorem 7.8.
The authors already supply the foundational low-growth mechanism and
suggest specializing their function-field example to finite extensions,
explicitly raising the subfield issue. This record supplies a quantitative
specialization argument; it cannot claim discovery of sum-product
conjecture failure. The underlying curve/Picard/torsion estimates have
not been fully reconstructed in this campaign.

Li--Roche-Newton, arXiv:1106.1148v1, Theorem 1.4,
https://arxiv.org/html/1106.1148v1, gives lower exponent 12/11 (up to
logarithms) under the exact condition verified here. Its quoted Katz--Shen
affine criterion is also met: proper-field caps are bounded and the full
field is handled by N<sqrt(|E|). There is no contradiction with either.

Mohammadi--Stevens, arXiv:2103.08252v3,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08252v3, proves exponent 5/4 in prime fields
for sets smaller than about sqrt(p). Its field and characteristic
hypotheses do not apply to the fixed-characteristic extension sequence.
Search did not establish an exhaustive strongest-current-bound or
priority result for these exact extension-field hypotheses. Novelty is
therefore UNKNOWN, not inferred from failure to find a duplicate.

The construction is existential. One can finitely enumerate places and
ratio triples if the source curve/functions are explicitly supplied,
but the source acquisition and field-operation costs have not been
accounted for. No efficient algorithm or technological capability follows.
