# r09_algebra — approximate recovery at the exact-support boundary

Status: **proved restricted consequence and exact obstruction; general positive-scattering robustness unresolved.** This is a modest theorem, not a claimed foundational breakthrough. Primary recent lead: Wang–Yuan, *Identifiability of Nonnegative Tensor Decompositions via Positive Scattering*, arXiv:2609.11606v1, submitted 10 September 2026. Relevant inspected locations: Theorem 1, Lemmas 6–7, Proposition 3, Theorems 3–4, Example 1. Versioned HTML and readable extraction are in `sources/r09_algebra/`.

## Question and interface

Given a finite Cartesian state space X=X_1×…×X_d, d≥2, a known support C⊆X with pairwise Hamming distance at least two, and a probability tensor P supported on C, can approximate nonnegative CP decompositions recover its coordinate components despite losing every exact zero?

Input promise: |C|=R, P(c)=w_c≥α>0, Σ_c w_c=1. A competitor is Q=Σ_{s=1}^R λ_s q_s, where λ_s>0, Σ_s λ_s=1, and q_s=⊗_j q_{sj} is an arbitrary product probability distribution. Factors of Q may be strictly positive; their support is not supplied or constrained. Observation metric is total variation TV(P,Q)=½||P−Q||_1≤η. This is a represented-class recovery statement for an explicit, known support promise. It does not acquire a latent support from unrestricted data.

## Author claims versus our work

Wang–Yuan augment the subset factor-dimension budget by a support-dependent scattering cost, obtaining sufficient conditions for minimality and uniqueness of exact nonnegative decompositions. Their support argument forces competing rectangular supports into rook-connected components; Example 1 applies this to the W tensor. Our independent results below are (a) a quantitative approximate-recovery theorem for the coordinate-code special case and (b) an explicit nearby family with nonunique positive decompositions. Neither result uses the unaudited general scattering theorem as a premise.

The older Lovitz–Petrov paper, arXiv:2103.15633v2 (15 September 2021; journal 2023), Theorem 4, supplies the splitting theorem used in the recent paper. Its statement was verified against the primary version, not merely the recent quotation. The full Lovitz–Petrov proof was not independently reconstructed here.

## Independent lemma: leakage localizes a product distribution

For a product distribution q, let ℓ=q(X\C). For each mode j, fixing all coordinates except j leaves at most one point of C: two such points would have Hamming distance one. Therefore

q(C)≤max_a q_j(a).

Indeed, condition on the other d−1 coordinates; the conditional probability of C is either zero or one entry of q_j, hence at most its largest entry. Choose a modal coordinate m_j of q_j and let m=(m_1,…,m_d). Each q_j(m_j)≥1−ℓ. Consequently the union bound gives

q(m)≥1−dℓ.

If ℓ<1/(d+1), then q(m)>ℓ. Thus m∈C, since otherwise q(X\C)≥q(m)>ℓ. In this case TV(q,δ_m)=1−q(m)≤dℓ.

For ℓ≥1/(d+1), choose any c∈C; TV(q,δ_c)≤1≤(d+1)ℓ. Combining the two cases: every product q has a assigned c(q)∈C with

TV(q,δ_{c(q)})≤(d+1)q(X\C).

This proof is universal over finite alphabets and all product distributions. The code checks are only adversarial finite evidence for possible transcription mistakes.

## Restricted robust-identifiability theorem

Assume η<α/(2d+3). Then there is a permutation of the R competitor components indexed by C such that

Σ_c λ_c TV(q_c,δ_c)≤dη,

½Σ_c|λ_c−w_c|≤(d+1)η,

λ_c≥α−(d+1)η,

TV(q_c,δ_c)≤dη/[α−(d+1)η].

Complete derivation: Put ℓ_s=q_s(X\C). Since P(X\C)=0, Σ_s λ_sℓ_s=Q(X\C)≤η. Apply the preceding lemma to every component, and form P'=Σ_s λ_sδ_{c(q_s)}. Convexity of TV gives TV(Q,P')≤(d+1)η, and hence TV(P,P')≤(d+2)η. Because (d+2)η<α, no point of C can be omitted by P': an omitted point c would give TV(P,P')≥P(c)≥α. There are R components and R points, so their assignments are bijective. In particular λ_s≥α−(d+2)η.

Suppose a component had ℓ_s≥1/(d+1). Its leakage contribution would be at least [α−(d+2)η]/(d+1)>η, where the strict inequality is exactly α>(2d+3)η. This contradicts the total-leakage bound. Thus every component is in the low-leakage case. Its assigned point is its modal cell, and Σ_s λ_s TV(q_s,δ_{c(q_s)})≤dΣ_s λ_sℓ_s≤dη. Now repeat the triangle inequality to obtain TV(P,P')≤(d+1)η and λ_s≥α−(d+1)η. Finally λ_sℓ_s≤η and TV(q_s,δ_c)≤dℓ_s give the individual bound.

At η=0 this proves nonnegative rank R and exact uniqueness directly: any product with zero leakage is a point mass; fewer than R components cannot cover C. No singular-value condition is needed. The theorem assumes exactly R competing components. Overfitting permits arbitrary splitting of a component, so component-level identifiability would require a different quotient.

## Exact obstruction: uniqueness is not an open property

Write a=(1,ε), b=(ε,1), with 0<ε<1, and let

T_ε=a⊗a⊗b+a⊗b⊗a+b⊗a⊗a.

For any 0<t<ε,

T_ε=a⊗a⊗(b−ta)+a⊗(b+ta)⊗a+b⊗a⊗a.

Expanding the two t terms shows their exact cancellation. Every factor on both sides is strictly positive because b−ta=(ε−t,1−tε)>0. The multisets of rank-one terms differ. The construction is an elementary matrix-factor mixing inside the shared first-mode factor, not a numerical solver artifact.

The matrix A_ε with columns a,b is invertible. Thus T_ε=A_ε^{⊗3}W has real rank three, since W has real rank three. An independent real-rank proof for W uses its mode-1 matrix pencil: slices [[0,1],[1,0]] and [[1,0],[0,0]] span a pencil whose determinant is −x², so its rank-one locus spans only one dimension; a two-term decomposition would force two independent rank-one generators. Hence both displayed nonnegative decompositions are minimal.

Normalize T_ε by its entry sum 3(1+ε)^3. It tends to P=W/3 in TV. Explicitly, each W coordinate has mass (1+2ε²)/[3(1+ε)^3]≤1/3, so

TV(P,T_ε/[3(1+ε)^3])=(3ε+ε²+ε³)/(1+ε)^3→0.

Therefore exact nonnegative uniqueness is destroyed arbitrarily close to an exactly support-certified tensor. This does **not** contradict the robust theorem: the alternative components become close to the same coordinate cells. It distinguishes fragile exact uniqueness from stable approximate recovery.

## Validation and decisive limits

Run `python3 work/r09_algebra/check_support_stability.py`. Exact rational checks verify three positive-fill examples, equality of both decompositions and their inequivalence. Exhaustion over 34 nonempty distance-two codes in the binary three-cube and 1,331 rational product distributions checks 45,254 code/measure pairs with no failure. A further 1,000 seeded rational perturbed W mixtures satisfy the quantitative recovery bounds. Results are saved in `results/r09_algebra/support_stability_checks.json`.

The α dependence is necessary for component recovery: assigning weight α to a cell and moving that component to a distant product distribution changes the observable mixture by TV at most α while changing that normalized component by order one. The d constants are sufficient and not claimed sharp. Known coordinate supports are much narrower than arbitrary intrinsic facet supports. Extending the proof to general positive scattering requires quantitative control of facet maps, approximate rank/dimension, and mass allocation across near-disconnected support boxes; exact combinatorial connectivity alone supplies no such margin.

## Prior art and novelty status

Exact code-support uniqueness is elementary and sits inside Wang–Yuan's examples; it is not claimed new. The perturbation mixing is standard nonnegative-factorization geometry. The quantitative theorem was independently derived during this run, but novelty has not been established: searches for robust support-based nonnegative tensor identifiability and product-measure distance-two concentration found related approximate-rank and separable-factorization literature, without a matching theorem. This is a usable restricted theorem and a candidate research direction, not a publication-ready novelty assertion.

De las Cuevas–Klingler–Netzer, arXiv:2004.10219v2 (2021), studies approximate rank separations for ℓ_p, principally p>1. Only its primary abstract was read; it should not be treated as establishing or excluding the TV component-recovery theorem here. The p=1 and normalized component interface matters.

A second recent advance is Bhargava–Schulman–Sivan, ECCC TR26-073 (verified primary page publication 11 May 2026), ICALP 2026 Theorem 1: randomized exact decomposition under Kruskal's promise in n^{O(k)}, where k=2r+2−max pairwise Kruskal-rank sums. Its runtime uses exact Real RAM arithmetic; general noisy recovery and bit complexity do not follow. We inspected the theorem and mechanism, not its full proof. Combining observed rook-support splitting with this algorithm may give exact recovery beyond global Kruskal conditions, but the decomposition into support blocks itself is old and that combination has not been investigated enough to claim novelty.

## Concrete downstream use and next falsifiable steps

In a sparse latent-class model with known mutually separated outcome cells, the theorem certifies that any R-component nonnegative fit close in TV has individually localized components, even when fitted factors erase all exact zeros. This converts an exact support certificate into an explicit error tolerance η<α/(2d+3).

Next decisive steps: (1) seek a dimension-free replacement for the dℓ localization bound or produce product-measure/code counterexamples; (2) prove a counterpart for non-singleton rectangular support blocks separated in at least two modes; (3) search older robust nonnegative CP and weighted independent-set literature for the same inequality before making any novelty claim; (4) only after a quantitative facet margin is defined, test whether finite positive scattering yields a genuinely general stability theorem. Unexplored alternatives include algorithmic Kruskal near the (r,r−1,3) regime and tensor-based causal intervention design.
