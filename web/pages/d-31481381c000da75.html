## Interface being compared

Input is an integer T>=1. With normalized area sigma on S², let e be an orthonormal basis of all nonconstant harmonics through degree T, of dimension M=T(T+2). The accepted programme theorem acquires distinct rational unit nodes with

    N=720000(T+1)²,
    b=N^-1 sum e(x_i),  |b|<=1/[2048(T+1)^5],

## Exact matching generic primary source

Boris Polyak and Andrey Tremba, *New versions of Newton method: step-size choice, convergence domain and under-determined equations*, [arXiv1703.07810v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07810v2), revised 26 August 2019; v1 was submitted 22 March 2017. The archived PDF has 35 pages. I read Sections 1–4.1, the pure-Newton discussion and source references, and visually checked PDF pages5–6. Remaining application sections were not audited.

Section2, PDF page5, explicitly gives the Newton subproblem

    minimize ||z||_infinity subject to P'(x_k)z=P(x_k),

and reduces it to linear programming. Domain and residual norms can be chosen independently. Lemma3.1, PDF pages6–7, states that

    ||A* p||_(domain dual) >= mu ||p||_(residual dual)

implies solvability of Az=b with a minimum-norm solution satisfying ||z||<=||b||/mu. Its proof uses compact convex images of norm balls and strict separation. This is the same convex argument as the programme's tangent correction argument, after the spherical sampled-gradient estimate supplies mu.

Assumptions A′ and B′, PDF page9, require a Lipschitz derivative throughout a ball and a uniform adjoint lower bound there. Equation13 proves

    u_(k+1) <= |1-alpha_k|u_k + [L/(2mu²)]alpha_k²u_k².

Algorithm1 (equation15, PDF page10) uses minimum-norm corrections with damped or full Newton steps. Theorem4.1 (PDF pages10–11) gives a quantified ball condition, convergence, and double-exponential bounds after reaching the pure-Newton stage. Thus minimum-coordinate correction, the separation proof of its bound, and semilocal quadratic convergence cannot be described as a new generic solver.

The source itself traces the surjectivity lemma to Banach and the underdetermined local framework to Graves, Newton–Mysovskikh and metric regularity. I have not independently read those older original proofs and do not elevate their precise statements beyond this attribution.

## Independent reconstruction of the relationship

Let V be the mean-zero polynomial space with norm G, Y=V* with its dual norm, and X_X the tangent product space with norm max_i |v_i|. Write F(X)(P)=N^-1 sum P(x_i). Its differential A_X obeys

    ||A_X*P||_(X_X*)=N^-1 sum |grad_S P(x_i)| >=G(P)/2.

Polyak–Tremba Lemma3.1 therefore yields a tangent solution of A_Xv=-F(X) of product norm at most2||F(X)||_Y. Replacing product Euclidean balls by ambient coordinate boxes, then projecting tangentially, costs at most sqrt(3), giving the programme's bound <4||F(X)||_Y. Optimizing the box radius is one implementation; a feasible box with the same bound is sufficient for the proof.

The uniform average neighborhood Hessian bound <=6T G(P), together with the average neighborhood gradient bound <2G(P), supplies the same O(T) derivative/remainder scale as the published semilocal theorem. A product of small stereographic/Cayley charts converts the manifold problem into ordinary underdetermined equations. On a chart of radius O(h), chart differential singular values are bounded above and below by constants and chart second derivatives are bounded. The adjoint bound stays positive, and the derivative is Lipschitz with constant O(T) in the G-dual norm. Alternatively, the programme directly proves the invariant retracted recurrence

    epsilon_(k+1) <=64T epsilon_k²

before rounding. This avoids any need to assert that a changing tangent space literally is the fixed Euclidean domain in Polyak–Tremba's theorem.

The centroid error epsilon_0<=6Th² has L epsilon_0/mu²=O(T²h²), which is small for the chosen fixed constant in h. The cumulative corrections are O(Th²)<h. These are exactly the two quantities a standard semilocal argument needs. The sphere-specific work is therefore supplying a uniformly usable norm/basin and constructing its initializer, rather than discovering a new Newton principle.

Neither the generic theorem nor a mere full-row-rank Jacobian supplies that initializer. Arbitrary approximate designs may have small Euclidean mean while their derivative is ill-conditioned in maximum-displacement norm. A theorem about numerical local convergence also does not by itself give rational initialization, polynomial input heights, exact LP output verification, bounded-height resets, or a polynomial bit budget uniform in T. Those are the remaining complete-interface comparisons.
