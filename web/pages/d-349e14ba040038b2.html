# Nonlinear observability for feedback control

## Verdict

I reconstructed an exact quantitative result for active observation through a square-law sensor and a one-cycle output-feedback stabilizer. For a scalar integrator, the best worst-case state error achievable by a precommitted probe with duration \(T\), squared-input budget \(E\), and adversarial per-sample sensor error \(\delta\) is exactly \(\delta/\sqrt{ET}\), provided the initial-state interval contains the adversarial pair specified in the proof. A constant input pulse and two endpoint readings attain the lower bound, and an analytic correction drives the state to that radius.

This is a complete model theorem, but I did **not** establish a genuinely new advance beyond current state-estimation/control baselines. Output-set observability metrics and observability-aware trajectory planning are established, and robust MHE/output-feedback stability results are much more general. The exact minimax law is a sharp candidate benchmark for that literature; its novelty and priority remain open. Do not promote it as a new general observability theorem.

## Interface and protocol

The plant is \(\dot x=u\), \(x(0)=x_0\), with supplied sensor law \(y=x^2+v\), \(|v|\leq\delta\). The control-to-state map, sensor origin, and control trace are known exactly; model and sensor acquisition are not charged because they are supplied. The probe is chosen before any measurements and satisfies \(\int_0^T u^2\,dt\leq E\). A full-trace lower bound is proved, while the attaining protocol uses only \(y(0)\) and \(y(T)\).

Choose \(D=\sqrt{ET}\) and hold \(u=D/T\). The estimator

\[
\widehat{x}_0=\frac{y(T)-y(0)-D^2}{2D}
\]

satisfies \(|\widehat{x}_0-x_0|\leq\delta/D\). The matching lower bound uses two initial states centered at minus the midpoint of the probe’s displacement range; their full noiseless output traces remain within \(2\delta\), so admissible bounded-noise sets overlap. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds every open-loop displacement range by \(\sqrt{ET}\), yielding the minimax floor. The complete proof and one-cycle control construction are in [quadratic_sensor_minimax.md](proofs/quadratic_sensor_minimax.md).

For output-feedback settling, apply \(u_c=-(\widehat{x}_0+D)/T_c\) for duration \(T_c\). With exact integrator dynamics, the final state is \(x_0-\widehat{x}_0\). Probe effort is \(D^2/T\); correction effort is at most \((R+D+\delta/D)^2/T_c\), and correction speed is at most \((R+D+\delta/D)/T_c\). State safety on \([-S,S]\) is guaranteed by \(R+D\leq S\) and \(\delta/D\leq S\). To meet error target \(\varepsilon\), require \(D\geq\delta/\varepsilon\), plus probe-amplitude, safety, and correction-amplitude limits.

The protocol uses two measurements at accuracy \(\delta\), scalar arithmetic with one division, and time \(T+T_c\). If sensor error is \(\delta_s\) and uniform quantization step is \(\Delta\), use \(\delta=\delta_s+\Delta/2\); the report gives an explicit two-word bit bound from the output range. Bounded control/displacement error is charged separately with an explicit enlarged final-state bound. Process disturbances are zero in the exact minimax claim.

## Baselines and novelty boundary

Hermann–Krener’s observability rank condition is qualitative and local; for weakly controllable analytic systems, Theorem 3.12 equates it with weak observability. It does not quantify noise amplification or actuator effort. Hausman et al. optimize local observability Gramians over nonlinear robot trajectories. Alaeddini et al. add a decaying oscillatory observability probe to stabilizing feedback and analyze a bearing-only example where ordinary LQR creates an unobservable path. These works already establish the mechanism “excite the plant to make its nonlinear output informative.”

For estimation guarantees, Knüfer–Müller prove RGAS for nonlinear FIE and MHE under i-IOSS with process and measurement disturbances. Guo–Streif’s 2025 IQC result gives closed-loop ISS for MHE-based output feedback under robust detectability and an already stabilizing true-state controller. Deole–Mesbahi’s 2025 set-valued output framework is the closest to the exact bounded-noise calculation: it defines finite-horizon output-tube observability and optimizes a concave observability lower bound over trajectories. The present theorem makes the scalar square-law case exact and charges \(L^2\) excitation, but I found no evidence that this is beyond their general framework or standard minimax set-membership control.

The theorem is weaker than general MHE in model scope, process-noise coverage, and state dimension. Its possible contribution is a closed-form minimax certificate in a plant where excitation must be purchased, not a new observer class or general separability theorem. No downstream application or practical advantage over an equally informed MHE has been demonstrated.

## Counterexample and decisive next gate

At zero input, \(+a\) and \(-a\) produce the identical output \(a^2\) forever. No estimator can resolve that symmetry, regardless of computation. If an unknown additive process disturbance can cancel the probe, the same obstruction returns; for a probe velocity \(U\), the admissible disturbance \(w=-U\) holds the state fixed whenever the disturbance set contains \(-U\).

A direct falsifier for the exact formula is a calibrated integrator/square-law sensor with certified endpoint error at most \(\delta\) and known displacement \(D\), where the endpoint estimator’s error exceeds \(\delta/D\). For actuator or process displacement error \(\zeta_p,\zeta_c\), use the proof’s larger bound rather than the exact one.

The next useful research gate is a prior-art attack on whether the exact (\delta/\sqrt{ET}\) open-loop minimax law is already a corollary of finite-horizon set-valued observability/metric regularity, followed only if it survives by extending to vector states or state-dependent sensor uncertainty while retaining sharp energy and excitation costs. A route that merely re-labels an observability Gramian is not enough.
