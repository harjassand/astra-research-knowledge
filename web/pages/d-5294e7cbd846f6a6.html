# Learning, statistics, and observable acquisition

**Verdict.** This branch produced no historically validated new general learning algorithm or unconditional lower bound. Its strongest result is a precise access-boundary theorem: complete-law KL closeness to an exact teacher does not buy uniform prediction at exponentially rare prefixes, even though the same example is easy for the canonical average-path loss. Separately, the algebraic-oracle investigation makes a concrete exact-versus-Gaussian distinction with full dimension, bit, and precision charges. Positive feature acquisition was demonstrated only on supplied, structured process classes. Across these results, the main durable contribution is scope discipline: exact matrix answers, Gaussian matrix observations, source-conditional suffix samples, teacher conditionals, and passive paths are different acquisition interfaces.

No result was tested on biological measurements or a physical process. Every theorem below concerns an explicitly stated mathematical source and access service. Historical novelty is unverified; no priority claim is made.

## Lead result: teacher access does not reveal a source-only rare conditional

The pinned Astra A06 target gives the learner passive complete trajectories from an unknown finite-state law P, plus exact rational evaluation access to a fixed teacher Q satisfying `KL(P || Q) <= zeta`. Deployment requires a compact finite-precision recurrence with bounded retained state, bounded future-task readouts, no later teacher calls, and actual-path MSE `O(zeta) + epsilon^2`. The source card's Hellinger argument controls squared conditional-TV error averaged on source-generated prefixes. It does not claim uniform error over every prefix.

For `n >= 2`, let X be uniform on `{0,1}^n`. Under P_b, set Y=b deterministically when X=0^n, and make Y fair otherwise. Let Q be uniform on all `n+1` bits, identical for both b in `{0,1}`. With \(\rho=2^{-n}\), the source is an O(n)-state clock-and-flag HMM with probabilities in \(\{0,1/2,1\}\), the teacher is positive and exactly evaluable, and

\[
\mathrm{KL}(P_b\Vert Q)=\rho\ln2,\quad
\mathrm{TV}(P_b,Q)=\rho/2,\quad
\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)=\rho.
\]

For `m` passive trajectories, `TV(P0^m, P1^m) = 1 - (1-rho)^m <= m rho`. Thus for `m <= 2^(n-2)`, an equal-prior test succeeds with probability at most 5/8, even with unlimited exact Q queries, because every teacher answer is the same. Uniform 1/3-TV prediction at the supplied prefix 0^n would identify b with success at least 2/3. Therefore that strengthened target needs `Omega(2^n) = Omega(1/zeta)` passive trajectories, or `Omega(n 2^n)` observed symbols. A chosen-prefix conditional sample from the unknown P_b resolves the bit in one call; a teacher conditional sample does not.

At the same time, at cut `t <= n`, the only differing history is `0^t`, of source mass `2^-t`, and its conditional TV to Q is `2^-(n-t+1)`. Hence

\[
\mathbb E_{h\sim P_b}\mathrm{TV}(P_b(\cdot\mid h),Q(\cdot\mid h))^2
=2^{-2n+t-2}\le 2^{-n}/4=\zeta/(4\ln2).
\]

The one-state teacher recurrence therefore already meets this pair's average actual-path target for a suitable universal constant. This lower bound **does not refute A06's canonical objective** and does not establish that its recurrence compiler is impossible. It rules out only the tempting upgrade from a KL promise to mass-free all-prefix accuracy. The self-contained argument and interface table are in [CYCLE3_REPORT.md](CYCLE3_REPORT.md); the detailed proof and machine-readable boundary are in [the child report](children/teacher_free_hmm/CYCLE3_REPORT.md) and [cycle-3 claim ledger](children/teacher_free_hmm/CYCLE3_claims.json).

## Algebraic acquisition: exact answers and noisy observations are distinct

The hidden-word series `f_w(u)=1[u=w]`, for `w in [n]^d`, has minimal Hankel rank `d+1`. An explicitly constructed exact rational full-matrix query of dimension `d+1`, satisfying `sum_i X_i^T X_i <= I` (the E=1 excitation cap), decodes w from one response. Its dense input has `n(d+1)^2` scalar slots and `O(nd log(nd))` nonzero rational payload bits; a fully dense encoding also pays the per-slot description overhead. It returns `(d+1)^2` rational entries, with `O(d^2 + d log(nd))` total bits under the stated dense-output convention. This is an explicitly known one-word class, not discovery of a general learner.

Under the paired independent-entry Gaussian matrix oracle with variance `sigma^2` and per-query excitation `E=1`, the reconstructed adaptive KL argument gives expected total query dimension at least

\[
2\sigma^2 n^d\,\mathrm{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)
\]

for any test distinguishing zero from every hidden word with both errors at most `delta < 1/2`; returned matrix entries are at least this many because `D^2 >= D`. An adjacent-amplitude pair also proves an actual `Q^4`-order Gaussian lower bound from the signal gap, separately from rational denominator separation. These are statements about two specified services, not a contradiction of Astra L01's exact-oracle theorem. L01 and N25 cards remain source-derived/unreviewed here, and the indexed longer proofs were unavailable at the pinned commit.

For a rational recognizable series with supplied realization dimension `r`, entry height `H`, and a jointly strictly upper-triangular query tuple of dimension `D` and entry height `B`, the bounded-height compiler gives a common response denominator `Q_*` with

\[
\log_2 Q_*\le 2rH+(D-1)(nr^2H+nD^2B).
\]

At least `2 log2(Q_*) + 2` fractional bits suffice for exact continued-fraction reconstruction. For `T` adaptive calls this preserves the exact transcript only when query generation, all `T D^2` returned entries, integer ranges, and bit arithmetic are charged. Under Gaussian observations, averaging each entry to tolerance `1/(4Q_*^2)` gives a **sufficient** cost of order `sigma^2 Q_*^4 log(T D^2/delta)` repeats; this generic compiler bound is not itself a lower bound. Full denominator derivation and bit accounting are in [COMPILER_PROOF.md](children/exact_noisy_oracles/COMPILER_PROOF.md) and [Cycle 2 report](children/exact_noisy_oracles/CYCLE2_REPORT.md).

## Prior art and novelty check

The active structure of finite-state learning is established. Angluin's (L^*), weighted-automata observation-table methods, and active weighted-automata algorithms over fields already use adaptive membership/output queries and linear-algebraic realization. In particular, Kaznatcheev and Panangaden's [weighted-automata learning result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10498) gives a linear-algebraic generalization of the Angluin–Schapire approach. This does not directly identify their query service with a full matrix-valued oracle, but blocks any claim that active finite-state/weighted-automaton acquisition itself is new.

Chosen-prefix conditional-sample learning is also established. Mahajan et al. (COLT 2023) define conditional probability and conditional future-sample services; Liu and Moitra (STOC 2025) prove arbitrary finite-rank distribution learning in total variation from chosen-prefix conditional samples. This is stronger than passive paths and distinct from A06's teacher Q, which is only close in complete-law KL and does not reveal `P(. | h)`. Passive noisy parity is an established conditional hardness mechanism in Kearns et al. (STOC 1994). Spectral HMM/PSR learning gives positive results under supplied finite-rank bases or positivity/full-rank conditions, often with explicit inverse singular-value dependence. Rational reconstruction is classical. These source boundaries are cited in the child reports and prohibit priority claims for the corresponding routine mechanisms.
