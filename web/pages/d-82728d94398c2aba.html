# Primary-source notes: indiscriminate photoreduction / downstream selection

## Candidate

Edgecomb, J. M.; Sau, A.; Manoj, N.; et al. “Selectivity emerges from indiscriminate photoreduction.” *Nature* 656, 630–637 (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7). Official full article page: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10897-7. Official Supplementary Information: https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10897-7/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10897_MOESM1_ESM.pdf.

Nature abstract reports that super-potent photoreductants can make forward outer-sphere SET diffusion-limited and alter selectivity away from substrate redox potentials; downstream reaction and BET determine competition. Authors demonstrate formal [3+2] annulation of cyclopropyl ketones with more readily reduced alkenes despite up to about 1 V redox mismatch. The abstract and SI are available; main text is subscription preview.

## Mechanism and evidence locators in the official SI

- **Substrates/product:** Ket-1 is 1-(2,2-dimethylcyclopropyl)propan-1-one; styrene is the easier-to-reduce partner. Ket-1 radical anion β-scission yields an open-chain ketyl radical, which adds styrene and annulates. Styrene radical anion undergoes BET to photocatalyst before protonation/hydrogenation. This returns the easy partner while the fragmented ketyl proceeds to product. SI S25–S30; kinetic scheme/model S81–S86.
- **Catalytic activation evidence:** Per•− generated at the cathode is not sufficiently reducing in the dark; under 390-nm illumination the cell supports reduction of both substrates. SI S10 Table S1.3: under constant potential/light 49% A and 2% B; in dark 0% A/B with 92% ketone and 93% styrene remaining. SI S11 Table S1.4: dark electrolysis/direct reduction controls show no annulation and preferential styrene hydrogenation. SI S14–S19 compare sodium/naphthalide thermal reduction, classical photoredox, and other conditions. These are source-reported controls, not independently repeated.
- **Observed product split:** SI S7–S8 Table S1.1, perylene/390-nm row: 70% annulation product A, 7% styrene-hydrogenation product B, 1% ketone remaining, 8% styrene remaining (GC yields). Thus observed A:B is 10:1; do not treat this endpoint GC ratio as a rate constant or isolated yield.
- **Capture kinetics:** SI S25 reports diffusion-limited styrene reduction `kSET=3×10^10 M−1 s−1`. SI S30 prose reports Ket-1 reduction `kSET=4×10^9 M−1 s−1`. SI S2.18 caption / S73 text for the Ket-1 quencher instead gives `3×10^10 M−1 s−1` (its caption sentence appears copied: it says capture “by pyrene” despite Ket-1 being named as quencher). SI S80 calculates at relevant substrate concentrations (80 mM vs ≤6 mM perylene) relative solvated-electron capture of styrene vs neutral perylene as 50 and Ket-1 vs perylene as 7. Those statements cannot resolve the direct styrene/Ket-1 rate conflict; preserve `g=1` caption-literal and `g=2/15` prose alternatives. Details and gates are in `GATE_DERIVATION.md`.
- **BET/protonation:** SI S27 reports that reversible styrene-radical-anion/perylene behavior is consistent with BET faster than protonation; computed protonation barrier is 8 kcal/mol above BET. SI S30 supports fast Ket-1 radical-anion β-scission outrunning BET. Computed barriers are mechanistic support, not measured elementary rates.
- **Paper's own kinetic model:** SI S81–S86 gives excess/constant substrate and redox-partner assumptions, Marcus SET, BET, ketone fragmentation, styrene protonation, coupling and back reactions. Eq. S9 is the two-channel hazard ratio reconstructed in `GATE_DERIVATION.md`. Under diffusion-limited equal forward SET, Eqs. S10–S13 discuss equal-product, fast target trap, and slow-BET regimes. Fig. 5c illustration (code in SI S97) uses `kdiff=10^10 M−1s−1`, acceptor `[A]=5.6×10−5 M`, competitor sink `k2=10^3 s−1`, assumed 0.75-V substrate redox gap, and sweeps `k1/k2`; these are illustrative model settings, not experimental fitted rates.
- **Mechanistic scope caveats in SI:** The optimal constant-current cell may include multiple reducing species. SI S13 establishes that perylene radical anion at fixed potential/light can produce the selectivity but does not prove it is the only active species under the optimized current protocol. The model assumes constant concentrations/excess; actual reaction has depletion and multiple intermediates. The paper tests chain propagation and a Marcus-inverted-region explanation in SI S85–S92 but those remain source analyses.

## Physical input and uncharged costs

SI S6 describes divided-cell preparation under N2, DMF, cathodic TEAPF6, anodic TEABr, Zn-rod sacrificial anode, reticulated vitreous carbon cathode, simultaneous 390-nm Kessil irradiation and 1-mA electrolysis to 1 F/mol. Screening runs use 0.3 mmol substrate and about 7.75 h; charge from 1 F/mol on 0.3 mmol is 28.9 C (8.0 h at 1 mA), consistent at the protocol level with “about 8 h” rather than an exact duration for every experiment. Perylene loading is 7.5 mol%; substrate concentration is 0.075 M; styrene is 1 equiv in SI screen or 1.2 equiv in some reaction protocols. The source identifies wavelength, current and charge but does not report wall-plug electrical energy, cell voltage across the run, absorbed photon count, quantum yield, or joules/mol product. It also consumes Zn metal and electrolytes. These costs prevent a full energetic-efficiency claim.
