## Mathematical check

For permutation-invariant N-qubit states with exactly white Schur weights and arbitrary density operators within each sector, the frozen statement is Petz chi-square and relative entropy at most `2e9(r/N)^2` against the product of the exact one-site marginal, for `1<=r<=N/1024`. The small-r case N<1024 is empty. Every r_N=o(N) is eventually covered.

The centered Gram matrix is correct, including the imaginary antisymmetric term. Its eigenvalues are `1-|b|,1+|b|,1-|b|^2`; the Casimir bound gives `|b|<=sqrt(3/N)`. For `L=rho T^-1`, the weighted norm is `Tr rho^2 T^-1`, and cyclicity makes its centered-basis coefficients exactly centered expectations. Orthogonality by support and finite-dimensional Parseval are legitimate even though rho and T need not commute. The support-wise inverse-Gram bound pays at most2^w for support size w. Weight-one coefficients vanish exactly. This is the decisive cancellation; bounding all-reference conditioning by an exponential in r would not prove the mesoscopic claim.

The complex two-by-two Schur norm, determinant bound, and weighted-sector MGF estimate lead to `|F_N^b(z)|<=6 e^(3/2)(1+4Nu^2)e^(13Nu^2/4)`. The chosen radius gives a coefficient square of `36e^3(1+w)^2e^w(39N/(2w))^w`. The support2^w factor cancels the radius denominator2^w. Exact permutation multiplicities give `(r)_w/(N)_w^2 sum_n (product n_a!) |coefficient_n|^2`. Count and factorial bounds yield q=110r/N and the sum begins at w=2. The geometric-series and final constant are conservative and consistent. Jensen in the eigenbases proves `D<=log Tr rho^2 T^-1<=chi-square` without commutation.

No fatal mathematical gap was found in these exposed checks. The exact Schur weights are essential; the theorem must not be relaxed to a mere mean-Casimir promise. The loose constant is asymptotic evidence, not a practical finite-N sample estimate.

## Unrestricted separable null

If soundness is required against every globally fully separable N-qubit state, the product state `tau^tensor N` is a legal alternative matching all one-site data. With at most r distinct sites on each fresh independent preparation, exchangeability and classical adaptive label choice reduce the accessible data to a channel of m copies of rho_r. Joint quantum memory and final measurements do not bypass data processing. A constant testing gap needs `m=Omega(N^2/r^2)`. For r=1, accessible states agree exactly, so no number of such preparations can certify entanglement in this model.

This is a lower bound only. A protocol distinguishing the target from its product-marginal comparator does not by itself certify entanglement against all separable alternatives. The lead reports a sound random-pair O(N^2)-preparation test, matching the order for each fixed r>=2; the growing-r matching upper is unresolved at this checkpoint.

Direct physical preparation cost is `mN=Omega(N^3/r^2)` qubit instances. The claimed `Omega(N^2/r)` readout cost needs either full r-site access on every copy or a separate variable-budget information bound. For deterministic budgets r_i<=r, additivity would give `sum r_i^2=Omega(N^2)`, hence `sum r_i>=Omega(N^2/r)`. I notified the lead of this distinction; the lead agreed to add the deterministic-budget version and label saturated-budget counting separately. Expected adaptive stopping/readout costs require additional justification.

## Trusted global spin promise

If the null is supplied with the same trusted `E J^2=3N/4`, the product-marginal alternative is generally excluded. For any mixture of product states, let v_i be each component's Bloch vectors. Then

`4 E J^2=3N+E|sum_i v_i|^2-E sum_i |v_i|^2`.

At the trusted value, `E|sum_i v_i|^2=E sum_i |v_i|^2<=N`. Jensen gives `|sum_i E v_i|<=sqrt N`, or mean Bloch norm at most `1/sqrt N`. The polarized target has norm asymptotic `sqrt(8/pi)/sqrt N`, a strict constant-factor violation. A single-site mean estimate on O(N) copies can therefore infer entanglement with this supplied promise.

The r-site resource refinement is also explicit. For a uniformly random size-r subset and a fixed z axis,

`E S_r^2=r+r(r-1)/(N(N-1)) (4 E J_z^2-N)`

`<=r+2r(r-1)/(N-1)`.

This bound uses `J_z^2<=J^2` and works for arbitrary nulls with the promise, with no permutation assumption if the subset is randomized. The target and null mean-gap is of order r/sqrt N. For r=o(N), the subset-sum variance is O(r), so a constant-error promised test uses O(N/r) preparations. At r=1 this is O(N). The exact target mean remains tied to the specified polarized regime or an explicitly constructed polarized sector state; acquiring the promise experimentally is not free.

The unrestricted quadratic certification lower bound and the trusted-promise mean test are both meaningful. They answer different legal null interfaces. No universal preparation barrier across both interfaces is established.
