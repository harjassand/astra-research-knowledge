INTERFACE AND CLAIM

J_a=(1/2)sum_i sigma_a^(i) on the WHOLE N-qubit tensor product.
Consider rho_(N,alpha,delta) proportional to exp[alpha J^2/N-delta J_z^2/N],
alpha>0 and delta>=0. Full SEP permits different local factors. No symmetry-
sector projection is part of the input state. Let P_sym,k be the projector onto
the k-qubit maximum-spin space and define the unnormalized symmetric state
 Omega_k(u)=P_sym,k exp[-u J_z,k^2/k], k>=1.

If normalized Omega_k(delta0) is fully separable for EVERY k>=1, then
rho_(N,alpha,delta) is exactly fully separable for EVERY N>=1, every finite
alpha>0, and every 0<=delta<=delta0. The same holds with any axial field hJ_z,
with no bound on finite h. Conversely, all-alpha/all-N full separability at a
given delta implies full separability of Omega_N(delta) for every N, by taking
alpha to infinity at fixed N. Thus the uniform-in-alpha radius question is
exactly the symmetric Gaussian-Dicke moment gate, not merely an analogy with
the limiting sector.

1. SEPARABILITY IS MONOTONE AS DELTA DECREASES

If 0<=u1<=u2 and Omega_k(u2) is separable, put t=(u2-u1)/k. For a scalar
Gaussian g with variance2t,
 E[exp(g J_z,k/2) Omega_k(u2) exp(g J_z,k/2)]
   =exp(t J_z,k^2)Omega_k(u2)=Omega_k(u1).
The equality uses commutation with J_z,k and the scalar Gaussian MGF on each
magnetic eigenvalue. Every filtering operator is a tensor product of positive
one-qubit diagonal operators. Thus every integrand is an unnormalized fully
separable state, and normalization preserves SEP. At t=0 this is the identity.
Only positivity of the integral is used; no physical postselection is free.
Consequently it suffices to prove the moment condition at the one endpoint
delta0 for every k, rather than separately at all u in [0,delta0].

2. EXACT ISOTROPIC HEAT REPRESENTATION

For every positive alpha and finite N, normalized exp(alpha J^2/N) has a
positive radial mixture of tau(r u)^tensorN, where
 tau(r u)=((I+r u.sigma)/2), 0<=r<1, u uniform on the sphere.
This is the elementary SU(2) heat identity, derived in this worker's
03_spin_growing_window_and_exact_boundary.txt PartC by evaluating the radial
Gaussian integral on each spin-j representation. Set t=alpha/N there; no
critical scaling or small-alpha premise is needed. Its radial law is explicitly
 q_t(R) proportional to R^2 sinch(R/2)
       exp[-R^2/(4t)+N logcosh(R/2)], r=tanh(R/2).
The radial integral is finite for all t>0.

At each fixed radius r,
 tau(r u)=r P_u+(1-r)I/2, P_u=(I+u.sigma)/2.
Expand this tensor product over all subsets S of {1,...,N}, |S|=k. Spherical
averaging uses integral P_u^tensor k du=P_sym,k/(k+1), by SU(2) invariance
on its irreducible symmetric support and trace one. It gives EXACTLY
 integral tau(r u)^tensor N du
  =sum_S r^k(1-r)^(N-k)
       [P_sym,S/(k+1)] tensor [(I/2)^tensor S^c].        (R1)
Every coefficient is positive, and traces sum to one by the binomial theorem.
The k=0 convention is a scalar projector and dimension1.

3. CONDITION ON THE WHITE SITES AFTER THE AXIAL FILTER

Since J^2 commutes with J_z^2, put F=exp[-delta J_z^2/(2N)]. The unnormalized
target is F exp(alpha J^2/N)F. Apply F to each positive term in (R1), then
expand the white factors in the computational z basis. For a complement
string z on S^c let m_z be its total magnetization, and G_z=exp[-delta m_z
J_z,S/N]. Since the complement is a J_z eigenstate, the filtered term on S is
 P_sym,S exp[-delta(J_z,S+m_z)^2/N]
  =exp[-delta m_z^2/N] G_z Omega_k(delta k/N) G_z.        (R2)
Here G_z= tensor_(i in S) exp[-delta m_z sigma_z^(i)/(2N)] is a local
invertible filter. For k=0 the term is just a positive scalar times the
complement product string. For k>=1, delta k/N<=delta<=delta0, so Section1
and the assumed endpoint certificate make Omega_k(delta k/N) separable.
Local filtering then proves every term of (R2) fully separable.

The exact positive radial integral, subset sum and complement-string sum
therefore yield an unnormalized separable decomposition of the WHOLE target.
Renormalize its positive total trace. A common axial field hJ_z is a further
filter exp(h J_z/2)=tensor_i exp(h sigma_z^(i)/4), giving the field statement.
All lower-spin sectors have been retained by the white-site expansion; they
were not discarded or assumed separately separable.

4. NECESSITY OF THE SYMMETRIC SECTOR GATE

At fixed N, the largest eigenvalue of J^2 is (N/2)(N/2+1), and the gap to
the next spin is N. The alpha/N exponent thus suppresses every lower sector
by at least exp(-alpha). Normalized rho converges in trace norm to normalized
Omega_N(delta) as alpha tends to infinity. SEP is closed in finite dimension,
so all finite-alpha separability forces this limiting state separable.
An explicit crude bound is
 T(rho_(N,alpha,delta),Omega_N(delta)/Tr Omega_N(delta))
 <=[2^N/(N+1)]exp[-alpha+delta N/4].                    (R3)
The upper uses the total lower-sector dimension, the minimum Gaussian weight
exp(-delta N/4) in the top block, and the spin gap. It is only a sufficient
finite-alpha closeness bound, not the exact lower-sector mass.

A product vector in the symmetric subspace must have all its one-site vectors
collinear. In any full-separable decomposition of a state supported there,
positivity forces every pure-product summand into that support. Therefore its
full SEP decomposition consists of pure identical products. Averaging phase
around z makes their Dicke populations binomial. With
 p_k proportional to exp[-delta(k-N/2)^2/N],
 full SEP of the symmetric state is equivalent to a positive measure mu on
[0,1] satisfying p_k=binom(N,k)integral p^k(1-p)^(N-k)dmu(p).

5. EXACT FINITE MOMENT CRITERION, NOT ONLY LOG-CONVEXITY

Put q_k=p_k/binom(N,k), or use the unnormalized version; positive scalar
normalization does not affect this condition. Let
 H0=(q_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<=floor(N/2)),
 H1=(q_(i+j+1))_(0<=i,j<=floor((N-1)/2)).
Then the symmetric state is fully SEP iff H0,H1 are PSD. This is the known
qubit diagonal-symmetric criterion; primary source checked: Nengkun Yu,
Separability of Bosonic Systems, arXiv:1501.02957, Theorems1-2. Its preprint
uses unnormalized Dicke vectors, so binomial normalization must be kept here.

For clarity, a self-contained convex-dual proof avoids a PPT-sufficiency
assumption for general states. A diagonal bosonic witness corresponds to a
polynomial g(t)=sum a_k t^k nonnegative on t>=0; the endpoint p=1 gives the
nonnegative leading coefficient. Its expectation is sum a_k q_k. Every
nonnegative real polynomial on [0,infinity) of degree<=N is a sum of squares
of polynomials of degree<=floor(N/2), plus t times squares of degree
<=floor((N-1)/2), by real-root factorization. Nonnegativity of all those
expectations is exactly H0,H1 PSD. Separation from the compact convex cone
of homogeneous binomial moment vectors proves sufficiency. This is standard
moment-cone geometry and is not claimed as new priority.

Log-convex adjacent q is only a2-by-2 minor test. Higher minors may fail first;
spin variance is also only a low-order test. Neither is a full certificate.

6. COUNTERS TRANSFER BACK TO FINITE ALPHA

If H0 or H1 has a negative quadratic form, the maximum-spin Gaussian state
has a negative partial-transpose block in the most balanced split. A normalized

||W||op<=1. If its expectation on the normalized top state is -nu<0, then
the full finite-alpha state is entangled whenever twice the right side of
(R3) is <nu. This supplies an actual finite-alpha counter after a negative
moment certificate; a bosonic-only witness is not naively extended outside
the symmetric subspace.
