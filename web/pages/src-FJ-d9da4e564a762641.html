# General contact compensation beyond the invariant quartic cone

**Result.** The incoming cubic-compensation argument extends to any planar compatible gradient whose local contact data have a simple Hodge projection, a mixed-derivative symbol factorization, and a cubic in a specific differential ideal. No nine-coefficient or rotational-invariance assumption is needed after those local hypotheses are verified. I prove the criterion in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

In coordinates $X=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}$, contact direction $R=e_{12}$, and defect $e=(c,d-\lambda a)$, assume a pointwise quadratic energy $Q_0\ge\nu|e|^2$. If $C|_{c=0,d=\lambda a}=\kappa a^3$ and $D^2C[R,R]=\partial_b^2C=0$, then

\[
|\fint C(D\phi)|\le K(\fint Q_0(D\phi))^{1/2}\|D\phi\|_4\|D\phi-(D\phi:R)R\|_4.
\]

The proof has two reusable pieces: the polynomial remainder belongs to the ideal $(c,d-\lambda a)$ and gains one transverse factor because the cubic is at most linear in $b$; a Hodge correction reduces the remaining $a^3$ term to the established mixed-derivative interpolation

\[
\|\psi_{12}\|_{8/3}^2\lesssim\|\psi_{11}\|_2\|\psi_{22}\|_4.
\]

The same mechanism extends to constant-coefficient compatibility systems when the projected operator symbols obey the corresponding geometric-mean factorization and the defect projection is bounded in $L^2$ and $L^4$. This is a sufficient criterion, not an automatic consequence of the wave cone.

Two exact examples locate its reach. First, $C(A)=\det(A)A_{11}$ vanishes on every rank-one matrix but can have nonzero average on a resonant compatible gradient: for $\phi=(\sin x+\sin y,\sin(x+y))$, $\fint C(D\phi)=-1/4$. This cubic satisfies the criterion with $Q_0=c^2+(d-a)^2$, so the theorem controls a nonzero resonance instead of claiming cancellation. Second, dropping $\partial_b^2C=0$ fails even when $\ker Q_0\cap\Lambda=\mathbb R e_{12}$ and $C$ vanishes on $\ker Q_0$: the exact compatible sequence in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) has $C=b^2c$, $\fint Q_0=n^{-4}$, $\fint C=-n^{-2}$, total $L^4=O(1)$, and transverse $L^4=O(n^{-1})$. Thus its compensation ratio diverges linearly in $n$.

Concentration shows the field exponents are sharp for this factorized form. A compactly supported rank-one profile with cubic average $\asymp\rho^{1/2}$ has defect $L^2\asymp\rho^{1/2}$ and both field $L^4$ norms bounded above and below. Lowering either field exponent from 4 to $p<4$ makes that slot scale as $\rho^{2/p-1/2}$, breaking the estimate; lowering the defect exponent from 2 to $q<2$ also breaks it. Higher exponents are not excluded by this scaling argument.

**Prior-art boundary.** The mixed-derivative inequality, imaginary-power multipliers, Hodge projection, and canceling-operator theory are established tools; this report claims none of them as new. Gutiérrez's primary 2006 paper develops compensated-compactness conditions for quartic weak lower semicontinuity and, in the gradient case, explicit resonant three-wave cubic conditions. Its three-wave test is close in subject but has a different form and quantifier from the present all-gradient (L^2\times L^4\times L^4) estimate. Van Schaftingen's primary canceling-operator theorem characterizes critical (L^1\)-Sobolev estimates, not this cubic contact estimate. The narrower contribution here is the reusable contact Hodge-symbol-ideal criterion, its (b^2c) compatible obstruction, and the sharp scaling boundary. Historical priority remains **UNKNOWN**; the mixed-smoothness chapter was identified bibliographically but not read in full. Exact source passages and read depth are in [READ_LEDGER.md](READ_LEDGER.md).

This result does not prove full quasiconvexity, classify all quartic contacts, establish necessity of the sufficient criterion, or make a historical-novelty claim. Its source-specific application is the Type-II contact step of the incoming report; it supplies no extra global conclusion beyond that local cubic inequality and existing contact-plane consequences.
