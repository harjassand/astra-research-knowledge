# Finite-copy coverage and a supplied finite-complexity closure

Status: routine bounds supporting the predictive-quotient result; not a discovery claim. All accuracies below use Kolmogorov distance between CDFs. Exact real observations and known Gaussian dose shifts are granted to both learner and comparator. No decoder/noise paired observations are supplied. The recent perturbation paper 2504.18522v3 already acknowledges finite coverage and low overlap as limitations; these bounds do not expose a hidden claim of uniform finite-sample extrapolation.

## Smooth decoders: unavoidable exponential coverage cost

Take source doses mu in [-R,R], target L>R+1, Z_mu~N(mu,1), and unknown increasing C-infinity decoder f. Define the standard smooth step psi(t)=0 for t<=0, psi(t)=1 for t>=1, and psi(t)=exp(-1/t)/(exp(-1/t)+exp(-1/(1-t))) otherwise. Compare

    f_0(u)=u,  f_1(u)=u+4 psi(2(u-(L-1))).

Both agree below L-1, and 1<=f_1'<=65 globally. For the derivative bound, when t<=1/2, psi(1-psi)<=exp(2-1/t), so psi'<=exp(2-v)(v^2+4)<=8 for v=1/t>=2; use symmetry for t>=1/2. Thus the alternatives have uniform smooth bi-Lipschitz bounds independent of target distance L, although no analytic/degree restriction is imposed.

At target threshold x=L+2, F_0(x)=Phi(2), while f_1(L-1/2)=L+7/2>x gives F_1(x)<=Phi(-1/2). Their target CDF separation is at least Delta=Phi(2)-Phi(-1/2)>0.6687. Couple the latent samples in the two experiments. For **any adaptive source-dose choices**, the next observations coincide unless Z_mu>L-1, with probability at most q=Q(L-1-R). Hence N-copy transcript TV<=Nq. The usual coupling/two-point argument gives worst expected CDF error >=Delta(1-Nq)/2. Error below Delta/4 requires N>1/(2q); error <=Delta/3 with probability .9 in both models requires N>=.8/q (otherwise a test induced by the estimator contradicts the TV bound).

For R=1,L=8 the latter requirement exceeds 810 million copies; at L=10 it exceeds 1.286 quadrillion. This is a tail-coverage consequence, not an architecture-specific lower bound. Granting exact shifts strengthens the lower bound's interface. The decoder class varies with L; each member retains the same derivative bounds. Fixed bounded extrapolation distance is a different problem.

## Competent unrestricted control matches the leading exponential rate

Use samples at the strongest permitted source dose R and set delta=L-R. Since f is increasing,

    F_L(x)=G_delta(F_R(x)), G_delta(v)=Phi(Phi^{-1}(v)-delta).

Thus a direct retaining predictor is Fhat_L=G_delta(Fhat_R), with no representation reconstruction. This is a valid CDF; its atoms do not supply a TV approximation to a continuous law.

Here is an explicit uniform CDF bound. Fix epsilon in (0,1), failure probability zeta, choose a with Q(a)<=epsilon/4, and suppose delta>=a. Put kappa=epsilon/8, eta=kappa/3, r_min=Q(delta+a). Use a geometric grid of survival probabilities from r_min to 1/2 with neighboring ratio <=1+eta; its size K<=ceil(log((1/2)/r_min)/log(1+eta))+2. Multiplicative Chernoff and a union bound give simultaneous relative error eta at all grid points if

    N >= [27/(kappa^2 r_min)] log(2K/zeta).

Monotonicity then gives relative error <=kappa throughout [r_min,1/2], since the bracketing ratios lie between (1-eta)/(1+eta) and (1+eta)^2. For the inverse-normal survival quantile tau(r)=Q^{-1}(r), the Gaussian hazard is at least 1/4 when tau>=-1/2. Both r and rhat are <=5/8, so |tau(rhat)-tau(r)|<=4|log(rhat/r)|. Therefore the transformed CDF error is <=2|log(rhat/r)|<=8kappa/3<=epsilon/3 on the central target interval [L-a,L+a] in latent coordinates. Monotonicity and the endpoint tail probabilities extend this to a global error <epsilon.

At fixed epsilon,zeta this gives log N <= delta^2/2+O(delta+log delta). The smooth-decoder lower pair gives log N >=delta^2/2-O(delta). These match the leading Gaussian exponential coefficient. A naive absolute DKW estimate would instead give a weaker rate and is an inadequate comparator. The upper bound here is conservative and analytic; the huge sample counts printed by the code were not executed.

## Supplied polynomial closure changes exponential to polynomial dependence

Now **supply** the class f(u)=sum_{j=0}^D b_j u^j with |b_j|<=B and f'(u)>=a_0>0 globally. D,B,a_0, orientation, and the Gaussian shift law are known model assumptions. They are not discovered or validated by the algorithm.

Use D+1 fixed nodes u_j=-1+2j/D and unpaired source-dose-zero samples X=f(N(0,1)). At empirical ranks ceil(N Phi(u_j)), read yhat_j and interpolate the degree-D polynomial through (u_j,yhat_j). Let V be the Vandermonde matrix, K_0=||V^{-1}||_infinity, M=B sum_{j=1}^D j 2^{j-1}. Choose gamma<=1. Since phi>=phi(2) on [-2,2], a Hoeffding/union bound ensures every latent empirical quantile is within gamma of its prescribed node, with probability >=1-zeta, provided

    N >= max{ 2/[phi(2)gamma], [2/(phi(2)^2 gamma^2)] log[2(D+1)/zeta] }.

The first term handles the ceiling in each order-statistic rank. This is a proof device; the estimator receives only X, never its paired latent draw. On this event |yhat_j-f(u_j)|<=M gamma and max coefficient error <=K_0 M gamma.

For target V_L~N(L,1), let A=sqrt(2 log(8/epsilon)); its probability outside |V_L|<=|L|+A is <=epsilon/4. On the central event, decoder error is at most

    K_0 M gamma (D+1)(1+|L|+A)^D.

The true output density is bounded by 1/(a_0 sqrt(2pi)). Choose gamma also <=a_0 sqrt(2pi) epsilon/[2 K_0 M(D+1)(1+|L|+A)^D]. Coupling f(V_L) and fhat(V_L), plus this density bound and the tail event, yields CDF error <=epsilon. The learned polynomial need not be globally monotone for that coupling argument: define its prediction as the law of fhat(V_L). Thus the charged number of unpaired observed samples is

    O_{D,B,a_0}((1+|L|+sqrt(log(1/epsilon)))^{2D} epsilon^{-2} log(D/zeta)).

Sorting costs O(N log N), interpolation O(D^3) by a direct solve, and evaluation O(D) per generated prediction. This is an exact-real access model; a finite-bit implementation would need its own precision analysis. Constants depend on the conditioning of the fixed Vandermonde matrix; fixed D is essential. The result is a retaining polynomial/quantile baseline, not a neural architecture advantage.

The replay code uses an increasing cubic, unpaired samples, and reuses each learned fit at three target doses. It charges 222,000 source observations in total. The N=2,000,L=10 fixture fails badly (CDF grid error 0.984), while N=200,000,L=10 gives 0.00546. All nine results, including the failure, are preserved. The finite grid check verifies that example, not the universal sample bound.

## What remains hard

The smooth lower pair has one polynomial member and one distant smooth modification. Consequently short in-distribution agreement cannot cheaply certify that the decoder belongs to the globally polynomial class. The class restriction makes extrapolation affordable **only if supplied**, or if acquired through a separate scientifically justified observation/regularity mechanism. This is the next meaningful acquisition problem. Merely fitting a polynomial, an analytic network, or a finite representation does not establish true-world closure.
