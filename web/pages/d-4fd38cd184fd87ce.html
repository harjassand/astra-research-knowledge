Date: 2026-10-08

DECISION

The lead proof directly approximates the explicit Gaussian-sector weights
f_k=exp(-(k-N/2)^2/N) by positive rationals using range reduction and odd
Taylor partial sums. The relative error yields a rational Dicke-diagonal MPO
of bond (N+1)^2 and, by a generic CP-preserved Loewner sandwich, a
postselection-stable conditional law. A supplied rational bounded-bond MPO
Kraus operator admits exact contractions and finite-bit sequential
computational-basis sampling in polynomial bit time without rejection or a
1/P factor. This construction needs no N125/Cycle-3 premise and no
separability promise. It refutes an N125-specific algorithmic gain for this
consumer; N125 may still matter if an independently required interface asks
for an explicit positive product-state ensemble or separability certificate.
The sampler is not an arbitrary-circuit simulator, and strict advantage over
source-reported compilers remains unknown.

Two exact research mechanisms from the prior algebra-complexity frontier were
also pushed in independent directions: a model-specific optimality theorem
for pair-local selective matrix multiplication, and scoped transfers and

1. Lead result: postselection-stable positive MPO consumer

For target sector weights f_k>0 and rational g_k satisfying
a f_k<=g_k<=b f_k for 0<a<=b, let |D_k> denote the normalized N-qubit
Dicke vector with k excitations. Put

    F=sum_k f_k, G=sum_k g_k, p_k=f_k/F, q_k=g_k/G,
    rho=sum_k p_k |D_k><D_k|, sigma=sum_k q_k |D_k><D_k|.

    L^-1 rho <= sigma <= L rho,       L=b/a,

on the entire Hilbert space: rho and sigma are both zero outside the same
symmetric subspace. For any CP trace-nonincreasing map Phi, put
A=Phi(rho), B=Phi(sigma), P=Tr A, Q=Tr B. Order implies P=0 iff Q=0;
when positive set tau=A/P and upsilon=B/Q. The success masses are
comparable by L, and the normalized outputs satisfy

    L^-2 tau <= upsilon <= L^2 tau,
    D(tau,upsilon) <= L^2-1.

For the direct lower Taylor weights, a=1-delta and b=1, so the bound is
(1-delta)^(-2)-1. With rational delta=epsilon/16 and epsilon<=1/2 this is at most
128 epsilon/961. Adding finite-bit branch rounding eps/8 gives total output
error below 0.259 epsilon for every N. The direct rational weights have
polynomial bit size: r=O(N), m_*=max_k m_k=O(1+log(N/delta)), and the
normalized MPO entry height is O(N^2 m_*(log N+log(m_*+1))+N). They are not
asserted to be separable.

This is independent of success probability. By comparison, an additive
trace-distance error Delta only yields the generic conditional bound
3 Delta/(2P) when target event mass is P; a two-qubit example attains
conditional TV 1 at input TV and event mass both eta.

The Cycle-3/N125 construction is optional here. Its finite approximant also
has a ket/bra Hamming-count MPO of bond (N+1)^2 and a finite product-state
phase-twirl representation, but that extra property is not needed by the
classical MPO consumer. Its source theorem remains an internal candidate.

Given a supplied open-boundary rational MPO Kraus operator K of length N,
bond chi and local bit height B, promise K*K<=I. With
M=(N+1)^2 chi^2, K sigma K* is an exact rational MPO of bond at most M.
Exact suffix/prefix contractions decide zero success and give conditional
computational-basis probabilities. Dyadic branch thresholds add at most
eps/8 TV, so the direct-construction sampled-output error is below 0.259
eps. A common-denominator contraction has bit height
H=O(N(B_loc+log(2M))) for local common-denominator height
B_loc=O(M^2(B+B_sigma+1)); the O(NM^2)
rational operation schedule has conservative schoolbook bit cost
O(NM^2 H^2), plus polynomial denominator bookkeeping. Random bits are
O(N log(N/eps)); the numerical success probability P does not enter, though
its exact numerator and denominator bit lengths are charged in H.

An exact diagnostic builds the N=4 Dicke-projector mixture densely and checks
the count-pair entry rule and prefix-zero/CNOT filter. An N=20 synthetic
sector law uses a prefix-zero projector followed by a bond-2 CNOT; its
nonzero event is below 2^-20 and its four-outcome conditional law obeys the
Loewner bound. This is a formula/fixture replay, not a generic MPO contractor
implementation or benchmark. Full proof, claims, cost ledger, and checker:
`consumer_gate/`.

The matched direct construction removes the N125-specific algorithmic delta
for this consumer. The generic relative-order proof is a stability lemma,
not itself a new algorithmic capability. N118's whole-Gibbs input is not this
maximal-spin-supported state. N123's true-separability promise is met by the
N125 approximant but separability of the directly constructed rational
approximant and target rho is not established here; its source card does not
state this relative-order conditional guarantee. Both interfaces remain
source-reported/unvalidated, so strict comparative advantage is UNKNOWN.
For the explicit f_k in this example, scalar target weights can be computed

2. Independent direction: selective matrix-product arithmetic

In the exact pair-local bilinear model, each output (i,j) must be obtained
from products of a linear form in row i of X and a linear form in column j
of Y; arbitrary linear preprocessing and decoding are free, but mixed-row or
mixed-column product gates are excluded. Projecting a decoded output onto
the monomial space {x_ik y_lj} gives coefficient matrix I_D. Each assigned
gate has rank at most one there, so every requested pair needs D gates.
For a supplied mask W of m pairs, the exact minimum is mD over every field.
At D=N^(1/4), m=Theta(N^(15/8)) this is Theta(N^(17/8)), but it is not a
lower bound for unrestricted bilinear circuits: the exact seven-product
Strassen identity is the escape witness. Thus the arbitrary-mask target
remains UNKNOWN. Full theorem and exact witness checker:
`mask_algorithms/`.

3. Independent direction: noncommutative evaluation versus commutative PIT

For an NC polynomial with at most s supplied free words, the N68 full-response
acquisition recovers the sparse word list; aggregating coefficients by
Parikh vectors then exactly gives the commutative image, with all response
and expanded-word bits charged. This is an interface-specific transfer, not
an improvement over known sparse commutative PIT.

For one coefficient-independent tuple in M_d(Q)^n, the response map on
degree-<=D commutative polynomials is linear into a d^2-dimensional space.
When binom(n+D,D)>d^2 it has a nonzero kernel, so even a full matrix response
cannot PIT the entire polynomial space. The leaf also proves that a supplied
finite-state realization abelianizes to a bordered affine-pencil
determinant, while xy-yx shows why a free NC nonzero hit does not test the
commutative quotient. No general commutative determinant PIT follows. Full
proofs, source bounds, and exact kernel/commutator diagnostics:
`evaluation_to_pit/`.

4. Independent direction: witness computability versus effective tensor use

Conditional on the prime-subfield form of the L03/leaf04 9/4 exponent
premise, exhaustive exact enumeration gives a total-computable search for a
finite rational/residue rank witness at each encoded characteristic and
rational exponent slack. The search has no useful bound in the supplied
inputs; its worst-case acquisition cost is tied to the unknown first
witness size. Separately, a supplied exact Laurent degeneration yields a
rank(T^tensor m)<=r^m(2md+1)^2 decomposition by constant-coefficient
extraction, with explicit coefficient/output bit bounds. The W tensor shows
border rank two need not give exact rank two for the limit, and its standard
specialization has route-specific exponential cancellation. Neither result
gives a compact arbitrary-mask selective algorithm. Full conditional proof,
counterexamples, exact checkers, and source boundary:
`border_rank_bits/`.
