# A fixed irreducible frame can be blind at every weight in a prescribed finite interior set

Status: **general exact construction and complete proof**, our deduction. Exact rational replay independently tests all whole-score equations and ordinary commutants in four small instances. External novelty is unverified. The frame is an arbitrary real symmetric frame constructed below; it is not the fixed physical local Pauli frame. The state dimension depends on the number of prescribed weights.

## Interface and conclusion

For a faithful density matrix omega and a Hermitian observable A, define the generally non-Hermitian score by

\[
-i[A,\omega]=(1-\lambda)\omega S_A^{\omega,\lambda}
+\lambda S_A^{\omega,\lambda}\omega.
\]

The positive entrywise denominators make this equation uniquely solvable. Let Lambda be a nonempty finite subset of (0,1), closed under lambda -> 1-lambda, with k distinct elements. Choose any d >= k+2 distinct positive numbers p_i, and let D=diag(p_i), e=(1,...,1)^T. There is a fixed finite real symmetric frame with scalar complex ordinary commutant, and distinct faithful states sigma,rho, whose entire scores coincide for every observable in that frame at every lambda in Lambda.

For an original list of r interior weights, first adjoining complements gives k <= 2r. Thus d <= 2r+2 suffices. The construction can use d=k+2 and **three observables**, independent of k. It proves that finitely many interior weights, together with ordinary frame irreducibility alone, cannot ensure identification uniformly across dimensions. It does not say that every frame or every fixed dimension fails.

## Polynomial annihilation of a Krylov space

Write L_lambda(Z)=(1-lambda)DZ+lambda ZD. These commuting linear operators multiply entry ij by (1-lambda)p_i+lambda p_j. Define

\[
W=\operatorname{span}\{e,De,\ldots,D^{k-1}e\},\qquad
H=\prod_{\mu\in\Lambda}L_\mu(Z),\qquad A=D^{-1/2}HD^{-1/2},
\]

where Z is any real symmetric matrix annihilating W. Since the p_i are distinct, dim W=k. Complement closure implies H=H^T, hence A=A^T. For each lambda in Lambda,

\[
K_\lambda=L_\lambda^{-1}(H)=\prod_{\mu\ne\lambda}L_\mu(Z),\qquad K_\lambda e=0.
\]

Indeed the remaining product expands as a linear combination of D^a Z D^b with a+b=k-1; every term kills e because Z D^b e=0. Also K_lambda^T=K_(1-lambda), so e^T K_lambda=0. Consequently P=ee^T/d is a common reducing rank-one projector for all these filtered matrices at all prescribed weights. No imaginary score condition is discarded.

This gives an injective family of admissible observables of dimension (d-k)(d-k+1)/2: every L_lambda and the diagonal congruence are invertible. Adding scalar I yields a direct extra dimension, because the filtered scalar I does not annihilate e. The exact converse below proves equality.

Conversely suppose a real symmetric H has [P,L_lambda^(-1)H]=0 for all listed lambda. Then K_lambda e=c_lambda e and e^T K_lambda=c_lambda e^T. Since H=L_lambda K_lambda, e^THe=c_lambda e^TDe, so all c_lambda equal c. Subtract cD and let Z=(product L_lambda)^(-1)(H-cD). The row constraints are (product_(mu!=lambda) L_mu) Z e=0. For each fixed positive p_i, the k polynomials in y,

\[
\prod_{\mu\ne\lambda}((1-\mu)p_i+\mu y),\quad\lambda\in\Lambda,
\]

form a basis of polynomials of degree at most k-1: their distinct missing roots -(1-lambda)p_i/lambda give the Lagrange evaluation argument. Thus the row constraints imply sum_j Z_(ij)p_j^b=0 for every b<k, or ZW=0. Complement closure makes Z symmetric. This reconstructs r03's independently derived converse. Hence the full admitted symmetric observable space has dimension

\[
1+\frac{(d-k)(d-k+1)}2\quad(k<d),
\]

and consists only of scalar I if k>=d. At k=d-1 there is only one non-scalar observable direction; no frame in this **fixed rank-one-projector ansatz** can be irreducible. Thus d=k+2 is sharp for this ansatz. This is not a general positive identification theorem in dimension d.

## Exact equality of the entire scores

Let T=tr D, and choose any t>0. Set

\[
\sigma=D/T,\qquad
\rho=\frac{D^{1/2}(I+tP)D^{1/2}}{T(1+t/d)}.
\]

Both states are faithful and have trace one, since tr(DP)=T/d. They differ because P is non-scalar. At sigma the score is S=-iB, with

\[
B_{ij}=\frac{p_j-p_i}{(1-\lambda)p_i+\lambda p_j}A_{ij}.
\]

Put u=D^(1/2)e. Entrywise calculation gives

\[
(A-\lambda B)u=D^{1/2}K_\lambda e=0,\qquad
u^T(A+(1-\lambda)B)=e^T K_\lambda D^{1/2}=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
[A,uu^T]-(1-\lambda)uu^TB-\lambda Buu^T
=(A-\lambda B)uu^T-uu^T(A+(1-\lambda)B)=0.
\]

The same score equation holds for the rank-one perturbation D^(1/2)PD^(1/2)=uu^T/d. It holds for D by the definition of B, and hence for the positive normalized sum defining rho. Uniqueness proves equality of the entire generally non-Hermitian scores at sigma and rho, simultaneously for every listed lambda.

## Vandermonde circuits force ordinary irreducibility

For every subset S of {1,...,d} of size k+1, define the circuit vector

\[
(z_S)_i=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j\in S\setminus\{i\}}(p_i-p_j)\right)^{-1},&i\in S,\\
0,&i\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]

The Lagrange interpolation leading-coefficient identity gives sum_i p_i^a(z_S)_i=0 for a=0,...,k-1. Thus Z_S=z_S z_S^T annihilates W. Every supported coordinate is nonzero. The constructed observable has entries

\[
(A_S)_{ij}=\frac{(z_S)_i(z_S)_j}{\sqrt{p_i p_j}}G(p_i,p_j),\qquad
G(x,y)=\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y).
\]

All off-diagonal entries within S are nonzero. More strongly its principal S block is invertible. Expand G(x,y)=sum_(a=0)^k c_a x^a y^(k-a). Every c_a>0 because every lambda is strictly interior. On S, the kernel matrix equals V C V^T, where V_(i,a)=p_i^a is a square Vandermonde matrix and C has anti-diagonal C_(a,k-a)=c_a. Hence

\[
\det G_S=(-1)^{k(k+1)/2}\left(\prod_{a=0}^k c_a\right)
\prod_{i<j,\ i,j\in S}(p_j-p_i)^2\ne0.
\]

The diagonal factors z_i/sqrt(p_i) are also invertible on S. Consequently ker A_S is exactly the coordinate space outside S, and im A_S is exactly the coordinate space on S.

If a complex matrix M commutes with every A_S, it preserves every such coordinate image and kernel. Given i != j, d>=k+2 permits a subset S containing i and excluding j; invariance makes M_(j,i)=0. Thus M is diagonal. Given any pair i,j, a subset S containing both exists, and its nonzero A_(i,j) implies M_(i,i)=M_(j,j). Therefore M is scalar. At d=k+2 the d subsets omitting one coordinate give the complete required frame.

For general d, the full subset family can be reduced to d cyclic windows S_a={a,a+1,...,a+k} modulo d (r03's independently reconstructed improvement). Their coordinate membership codes are all distinct because the nonempty proper cyclic block has a unique 0-to-1 boundary. Preservation of these coordinate subspaces forces M diagonal. Every cyclic adjacent pair occurs in a window, so its nonzero off-diagonal entry forces all diagonal values equal. This permits any d>=k+2, including a power-of-two Hilbert-space dimension, with d observables; these observables still are not the fixed local Pauli frame.

At minimal dimension d=k+2, W-perp has dimension two. Three distinct omit-one circuit vectors are pairwise nonparallel because each has a different unique zero coordinate. If the first two are a basis, the third has two nonzero basis coefficients. Their three outer products therefore span all symmetric matrices on W-perp: the third supplies the cross term. Every other circuit observable lies in their linear span. Consequently **any three distinct omit-one circuit observables already have scalar joint commutant**. This proves the constant frame-size refinement without assuming a generic pair is sufficient.

The endpoint exclusion is load-bearing: a zero or one weight eliminates a coefficient c_a, making the displayed determinant vanish. At an endpoint the source proves ordinary irreducibility sufficient for identification; this construction is consistent with that result.

## A well-conditioned target and a macroscopic collision

Root's quantitative refinement is exact. Choose distinct p_i in [1,2] and t=d. Then

\[
\rho=\tfrac12\sigma+\tfrac12|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|,
\qquad \psi_i=\sqrt{p_i/T}.
\]

Thus kappa(sigma)<=2, lambda_min(rho)>=1/(4d), and kappa(rho)<=4d. Measuring the projector onto psi gives the trace-distance lower bound

\[
\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1\ge
\tfrac12\left(1-\frac{\sum_i p_i^2}{T^2}\right)
\ge\tfrac12(1-2/d)\ge1/6,
\]

since d>=3, sum p_i^2<=2T and T>=d. The entire prescribed tuple of scores is still exactly equal. Taking sqrt(p_i)=(3d+i)/(3d) for i=0,...,d-1 makes the construction rational for rational weights while keeping every p_i<16/9<2. This is the replay's choice. Frame normalization preserves the collision and irreducibility; a uniformly large ordinary commutator gap is not established as the number or locations of weights vary.

## Evidence, prior art, and consequence

The source supplies the filtered-commutant sufficient condition and the endpoint identification theorem. It does **not** claim this finite simultaneous interior-weight obstruction. Our independent proof above verifies the score equations directly, so it does not reverse an author sufficient condition without justification.

Polynomial Krylov annihilation and Vandermonde circuits are standard linear-algebra mechanisms. Their combination here constructs a score-learning obstruction. External novelty, especially in weighted logarithmic derivative identification, remains unverified. Previously reconstructed cycle2 examples allowed the frame to vary with one weight; this construction holds one frame and one state pair fixed across the entire prescribed finite list.

Downstream use: a proposal to restore arbitrary-state quantum score identification by using two or any fixed finite number of interior weights needs an additional target/frame/dimension hypothesis.
