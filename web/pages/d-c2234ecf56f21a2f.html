# Passive acquisition fails on low-mass predictive contexts

## Claim and interface

Consider the stationary two-state HMM with states A,B, deterministic emissions Y=0 on A and Y=1 on B, and transition probabilities

- A→B with probability ε, A→A with probability 1−ε;
- B→B with probability θ, B→A with probability 1−θ.

Compare θ₀=1/4 and θ₁=3/4. The one-step target is the conditional prediction after context Y_t=1:

    pθ := Pθ(Y_{t+1}=1 | Y_t=1) = θ.

Thus the two targets differ by 1/2. The stationary mass of B is

    πθ(B) = ε / (ε + 1 − θ).

For any passive transcript containing at most B observation symbols from this process (one stream or independent stationary resets, with reset cost included in B), let Rθ be any randomized estimator of pθ and evaluate absolute loss. For ε≤1/8,

    maxθ∈{1/4,3/4} Eθ |Rθ − θ| ≥ (1 − 4Bε)/4.

The bound is informative whenever Bε≤1/4. It applies to every post-hoc adaptive choice of feature, test, or predictor because the event used in the proof makes the complete observed data identical under both models.

## Proof

The probability of the all-zero length-B transcript is

    Pθ(0^B) = (1 − πθ(B))(1 − ε)^(B−1).

Since πθ(B)≤4ε for both θ values and (1−ε)^(B−1)≥1−(B−1)ε, we have

    Pθ(0^B) ≥ 1 − (B+3)ε ≥ 1 − 4Bε.

On the all-zero transcript, the estimator has the same output law under both models. For every real q,

    |q−1/4| + |q−3/4| ≥ 1/2.

Writing m=minθ Pθ(0^B), the sum of the two expected risks is therefore at least m/2. Their maximum is at least m/4, which yields the claim. The argument also covers randomized learners by averaging over their common conditional output law on the all-zero record.

## Scope, controls, and interpretation

This is a worst-history conditional-prediction obstruction. It is not a lower bound on stationary-average prediction error: the rare context has mass Θ(ε), so a predictor may be excellent on average while wrong by a constant after Y_t=1. It is also not a fixed-horizon total-variation lower bound. Increasing observation length until Bε is large, or adding an active reset/conditional-sampling service, changes the interface and pays for visits to B.

The family has full-rank emission matrix O=I and a transition-matrix spectral gap bounded below by 1/4. The small conditioning parameter is instead the probability mass carried by B, reflected in σ_min(P(Y_t,Y_{t+1}))=Θ(ε). This is consistent with Hsu–Kakade–Zhang spectral learning, whose guarantees depend on singular values; it does not refute that algorithm or its assumptions. Primary source: Hsu, Kakade, Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models,” author manuscript / JCSS 2012, https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~djhsu/papers/hmm.pdf.

An inherited prior-work note in the user memory describes a stronger m-state rare-output passive lower bound, but that note was not reopened or independently verified here. The present two-state calculation is an auditable local derivation, not a novelty claim.

## Why this matters for acquisition claims

No procedure can use an unseen rare context to choose an adaptive predictive feature from the same passive transcript. A uniform conditional-prediction theorem therefore needs at least one of: a lower bound on context/state mass, a lower bound on a relevant Hankel singular value, a costed active intervention/reset that places the process in the context, or a target restricted to average risk. These conditions are alternatives in the theorem statement; they are not equivalent in physical experiments.

The competing acquired structure must be stated explicitly. A fixed test bank and a post-hoc-selected test are both functions of the same transcript on the all-zero event. A resettable conditional sampler can evade the event, but the service supplies reachability that passive acquisition did not have; its interventions and failures must be charged.
