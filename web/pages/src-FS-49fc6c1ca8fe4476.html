# Global compatibility from transverse density averaging

Research date: 15 October 2026. Worker: global_compatibility.

## Result and scope

For a left-SO(2)-invariant rank-one-convex homogeneous planar quartic F, define

    mu=min{F(R): rank R=1, |R|=1} >=0.

Let x,y each have period 2pi, let k be a nonzero integer, let g(x) be an arbitrary smooth periodic real function, and put

    theta(x,y)=ky+g(x),  n=(cos theta,sin theta),
    phi(x,y)=r(x,y)n(x,y),

where r is an arbitrary real periodic W^(1,4) amplitude. There is no curvature cancellation, smallness, Fourier cutoff, amplitude positivity or nonconcentration hypothesis. Then

    E F(Dphi) >= c_* mu E|Dphi|^4,
    c_*=(1+2*3^(1/3))^(-3) > 1/60.                       (1)

If in addition

    r(x,y+pi/|k|)=r(x,y),                                (2)

then for every constant affine background B,

    E[F(B+Dphi)-F(B)] >= c_* mu E|Dphi|^4.               (3)

The zero-background theorem allows every amplitude, without (2). The all-background theorem explicitly requires (2). Negative k is allowed. For k=0, phi depends on r(x,y) and a phase g(x); the same zero-background proof works, and the all-background assertion follows if any separate exact domain symmetry makes Dphi antipodal. No generic all-background claim is made at k=0.

This removes both weighted curvature-cancellation assumptions from the earlier N812 non-affine sheared family. Its retained half-turn hypothesis is sufficient for the background transfer; it is not used to prove zero-background positivity. It covers resonant transverse amplitudes and zeros. It does not prove arbitrary-profile quasiconvexity, and no negative compatible test is obtained.

For the uncertified non-polyconvex G from N819, mu_G=3. Thus (3) supplies the simpler rational all-background bound

    E[G(B+Dphi)-G(B)] >= (1/20)E|Dphi|^4.                (4)

Originality and external correctness remain UNKNOWN. The argument combines the incoming density convexification with the older one-coordinate envelope reduction; it does not claim invention of either ingredient.

## 1. Why averaging the density preserves complete compatibility

Work first with smooth strictly positive r. Put rho=r^4 and

    sigma(x)=E_y rho(x,y),   R(x)=sigma(x)^(1/4).

Output invariance removes theta itself. With a=grad rho/(4rho) and K=(g'(x),k), the complete energy is

    E F(D(rn))=E rho f_K(grad rho/(4rho)),
    f_K(a)=F([[a1,a2],[K1,K2]]).

For fixed K, f_K is convex in the full vector a: any difference of its two matrix arguments has only the first row and therefore rank at most one. Its perspective

    h_K(rho,z)=rho f_K(z/(4rho))

is convex jointly in rho>0 and z.

At each fixed x the coefficient K is independent of y. Moreover

    E_y rho=sigma, E_y rho_x=sigma', E_y rho_y=0.

Jensen's inequality gives

    E_y h_K(rho,grad rho) >= h_K(sigma,(sigma',0)).        (5)

The right side is exactly the energy density of the actual compatible map

    phi_bar(x,y)=R(x)exp(i(g(x)+ky)).

This is not averaging incompatible gradients or choosing gradient moments independently. It replaces the amplitude by a concrete scalar function and keeps the phase unchanged.

## 2. Keeping the quantitative Picone remainder

Let A_R=[[R'/R,0],[g',k]]. The rank-one Bregman bound for a convex homogeneous quartic along a rank-one line is

    F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C):Q >= F(Q)/3 >= (mu/3)|Q|^4.        (6)

For completeness, write F(C+tQ)=At^4+Lt^3+C2t^2+Dt+E. If A>0, nonnegativity of its second derivative gives C2>=3L^2/(8A), and

    A+L+C2-A/3
      =3(L+4A/3)^2/(8A)+C2-3L^2/(8A)>=0.

If A=0, the globally nonnegative linear second derivative forces L=0 and C2>=0.

Apply (6) to the first-row increment grad log(r/R). The tangent terms of the perspective at (sigma,(sigma',0)) average to zero in y because all three averaged differences in Section 1 vanish. Therefore

    E F(Dphi) >= E F(Dphi_bar)+(mu/3)C,
    C=E |grad r-(r/R)(R',0)|^4.                         (7)

The comparison has no unknown Euler multiplier or mass minimizer. It uses the explicit conditional fourth moment sigma.

## 3. One-coordinate reduction, reconstructed with its constant

For phi_bar, remove its output rotation and put a=R'/R, b=g'. Let

    M=E_x R^4,
    b_bar=(E_x R^4 b)/M.

If M=0 the map is zero and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise use the probability weight R^4/M. Periodicity gives E_x R^4 a=E_x(R^4)'/4=0. The first matrix column has weighted mean (0,b_bar), while the second column is the constant (0,k). Convexity in the first column and (6) yield

    E F(Dphi_bar) >= mu A0+(mu/3)A1,
    A0=M(b_bar^2+k^2)^2,
    A1=E_x R^4[a^2+(b-b_bar)^2]^2.                      (8)

The mean matrix has only the second row, so its value is bounded below by its rank-one value mu(b_bar^2+k^2)^2.

Minkowski's L4 inequality applied to the mean matrix and first-column deviation gives

    (E|Dphi_bar|^4)^(1/4) <= A0^(1/4)+A1^(1/4).

Weighted Holder gives

    A0+A1/3
      >= (A0^(1/4)+A1^(1/4))^4/(1+3^(1/3))^3.

Consequently

    E F(Dphi_bar)
      >= mu/(1+3^(1/3))^3 E|Dphi_bar|^4.                (9)

The older N812 proof used a coarser factor8 and obtained mu/24. Equation (9) is the same reduction with a sharper elementary norm comparison. Improving the constant is not the main result.

## 4. Recovering the complete original gradient

There is an exact decomposition

    Dphi=(r/R)Dphi_bar+n tensor [grad r-(r/R)(R',0)].

The norm |Dphi_bar(x,y)| is independent of y, since its y dependence is only an output rotation. Because E_y(r/R)^4=1,

    E |(r/R)Dphi_bar|^4 = E|Dphi_bar|^4=:A.

Thus

    (E|Dphi|^4)^(1/4) <= A^(1/4)+C^(1/4).

Combine (7), (9) and weighted Holder once more:

    E F(Dphi)
      >= mu[A/(1+3^(1/3))^3+C/3]
      >= mu (A^(1/4)+C^(1/4))^4/(1+2*3^(1/3))^3.

This proves (1). Since 3^(1/3)<29/20,

    (1+2*3^(1/3))^3 < (39/10)^3=59319/1000<60.

Therefore the clean rational coefficient mu/60 is valid.

## 5. Zeros, signs, weak amplitudes and concentration

For a real homogeneous quartic, replacing r by |r| leaves F(D(rn)) and |D(rn)| unchanged almost everywhere: the two gradients differ only by a target sign where r is nonzero, and the weak derivative of r vanishes almost everywhere on its zero set.

For nonnegative r in W^(1,4), regularize

    r_epsilon=(r^4+epsilon^4)^(1/4).

Then r_epsilon tends strongly to r in W^(1,4): its gradient is

    [r^3/(r^4+epsilon^4)^(3/4)] grad r,

with multiplier bounded by one and converging to one off the zero set. On the zero set grad r=0 almost everywhere. Also |r_epsilon-r|<=epsilon.

The corresponding averaged reference is exactly

    R_epsilon=(E_y r^4+epsilon^4)^(1/4)>0.

Prove the positive-reference inequalities for r_epsilon and pass directly in the final bound (1), using strong W^(1,4) continuity of quartic energy. No division by a limiting zero R is needed. Smooth approximation supplies the same argument for weak amplitudes.

Every constant in (1) is independent of frequency, minimum amplitude, concentration scale and g's curvature. Thus the theorem includes concentrated amplitude sequences whenever they remain legitimate W^(1,4) maps. It does not infer a compactness property of all phase fields.

## 6. All affine backgrounds under the explicit antipodal symmetry

Condition (2) gives

    phi(x,y+pi/|k|)=-phi(x,y),
    Dphi(x,y+pi/|k|)=-Dphi(x,y).

For a homogeneous quartic, the odd terms in its expansion around B consequently have zero mean:

    E[F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
      =E F(Dphi)+(1/2)E D^2F(B)[Dphi,Dphi].              (10)

For fixed B the latter Hessian is a constant quadratic form nonnegative on rank-one matrices. Fourier/Parseval on the periodic gradient gives

    E D^2F(B)[Dphi,Dphi]>=0,

since every sine/cosine Fourier gradient coefficient is an output vector tensored with its spatial wave vector. W^(1,4) is contained in W^(1,2), so the identity applies to the actual weak gradient by approximation. Equations (1) and (10) prove (3).

No minimization over B is needed; the bound holds before taking the affine infimum.

## 7. An exact example outside the older curvature hypotheses

Take

    r(x,y)=(1+(1/4)sin x)(1+(1/3)cos(2y)),
    g(x)=(1/5)sin x,  k=1.

The amplitude is positive and satisfies r(x,y+pi)=r(x,y). Its conditional density is

    sigma(x)=(289/216)(1+(1/4)sin x)^4.

One of the two earlier weighted curvature cancellations fails:

    E_x sigma g''=-19363/138240 !=0.                    (11)

The new all-background theorem still applies. This is a precise scope enlargement, not an example already covered by the earlier cancellation assumptions.

Multiplication by exp(ig(x)) generally introduces infinitely many x Fourier modes. The amplitude's cos(2y) factor supplies transverse frequencies -1,1,3 in phi, and more general amplitudes supply arbitrary interacting odd frequencies. A one-frequency storage cannot simply be added to certify this family.
