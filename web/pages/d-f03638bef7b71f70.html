## U1. Legal oracle and controlled interface

Theorem 1.1 supplies a target-independent circuit generator from unary n. For every n-qubit unitary U, there exists a Boolean function f_U on polynomially many address bits such that the circuit, with bit oracle `|x,b> -> |x,b xor f_U(x)>`, approximates the entire unitary channel in diamond norm by at most 1/2. Width, ordinary gates, queries, address length and uniform generation time are polynomial in n. The alphabet is H, T, T-dagger, CNOT. Workspace is clean in the ideal operator comparison and discarded at the end.

The oracle can have an exponentially large truth table, and no efficient procedure that computes f_U from U is asserted. Coherent labels are also allowed: one common skeleton works for an arbitrary family U_lambda, keeps every label, and preserves its relative scalar phase. This matters at every recursive call and in compilation.

## U2. Programmable slots and their hidden data

A slot applies an arbitrary table-controlled U(2) gate on one target bit. A unit vector on u bits can be prepared in u+1 slots by successive conditional prefix masses and one phase slot. A basis permutation uses 5u slots and u clean temporary bits, writing its image, erasing the original with the inverse, then swapping. A diagonal unitary uses one slot. A positive Fourier transform on b bits uses O(b) slots: Hadamard then diagonal conditional phase for each processed bit, followed by bit reversal.

These are exact statements in the slot model. They do not charge acquisition of all prefix masses, phases, permutation values or their inverses. At U8 these data become finite gate words stored in f_U. No table is replaced by a polynomial-time arithmetic oracle without proof.

## U3. A contracting principal block

Set `M=4096(n+1)^2`, `r=3/4`. For valid order `2<=d<=2^n`, let q be the largest power of two at most d, `s=max(1,floor(q/M))`, `k=d-s`. By target-dependent permutations, diagonal signs and Walsh transforms, put U into

\[
V=P_LUP_R=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad\|D\|\le3/4.
\tag{U1}
\]

If s=1, a row permutation places a modulus-at-most `1/sqrt(d)` entry in the required corner. Otherwise one independent diagonal sign choice bounds all q s entries of `M_0 E_2 W` by `4sqrt((n+1)/q)` using a sub-Gaussian union bound. A second sign choice uses Hermitian dilation of `h_i^*c_i`, variance at most `16s(n+1)/q`, and the Gaussian moment estimate

\[
\mathbb E\|\sum_i\epsilon_iL_i\|
\le2h^{1/(2a)}\sqrt{(2a-1)\|\sum_iL_i^2\|}.
\]

For h=2s,a=n+1 this is at most `16(n+1)sqrt(s/q)<=1/4`. The estimate is independently derived by Gaussian integration by parts, pairwise weighted AM–GM in an eigenbasis, trace moment recursion, and conditional Jensen from Gaussian absolute values to signs. Thus an admissible sign table exists. It is not efficiently found by this argument.

The transformations use O(n) slots and fresh bits with fixed structure. Since `s>=d/(4M)`, repeated reduction to k has fewer than `J=8M(n+1)=32768(n+1)^3` steps. The many label choices can be stored in slot tables without enumerating the labels in the circuit generator.

## U4. Exact feedback and matched adjoint fields

For any diagonal internal unitary Z, set

\[
X=(I-DZ)^{-1}C,\quad F=A+BZX,
\quad E=\binom I{ZX},\quad O=\binom F X.
\]

`VE=O` and norm conservation give `F^*F=I`; F is square, hence unitary. With `R=OF^*=(I,Y)^T`, the adjoint relation gives

\[
FE^*=R^*V,\qquad Y=(I-Z^*D^*)^{-1}Z^*B^*.
\tag{U2}
\]

The inverse is legal because `||D||<1`. Truncate both geometric series to L terms. Each pointwise error is at most `tau=r^L/(1-r)`.

Use `G=(Z/2^b)^(L+1)`, `b=nL+ceil(log2(L+1))`, and phases labelled by `v_i=(1,i,...,i^L)`. Define the field maps with the **adjoints** E_L(z)^*, R_L(z)^*, averaged with factor `|G|^(-1/2)`; the input dimension is d and the output is phase-label times port. Averaging the first phase coordinate separates lengths 0 through L. The Gram internal block telescopes via `CC^*=I-DD^*` or `B^*B=I-D^*D`, giving

\[
(1-r^{2L})I\le\mathcal E_L^*\mathcal E_L,
\mathcal R_L^*\mathcal R_L\le I,
\qquad
\|\widetilde F\mathcal E_L-\mathcal R_LV\|\le2\tau.
\tag{U3}
\]

Both lower and upper inequalities hold for each of the two maps. The common Fourier output layout is retained for reversing the second encoder.

## U5. Actual row sparsity and its exact limits

In Fourier coordinates a frequency records the multiset of at most L visited internal indices. Ordinary sums of all recorded powers are at most `L 2^(nL)<2^b`, so no modular alias occurs. Length plus the first m power sums determine the length-m multiset by Newton identities, including repeated roots. Every internal monomial includes the input's rightmost diagonal phase. Thus a nonzero Fourier row can involve only indices in that multiset, at most L of them. At zero frequency there is exactly the matching port input. Negative frequencies on the E side are negated before decoding.

This proves sparsity in the **rows of the Fourier coefficient map**. Its columns can be dense. The subsequent Fourier transform converts coefficients to labels and need not preserve row sparsity. The theorem does not claim sparsity in the original computational coordinates or a sparse positive image-enforcement Hamiltonian. Frontier_quantum_bridge is independently writing an explicit obstruction to that latter transfer.

## U6. Nearly isometric row-sparse matrix encoding

Suppose `T:C^d -> C^U` has at most L nonzero entries per row and `(1-epsilon)I<=T^*T<=I`. Add a row register, `h=ceil log2(L+1)` high input-index bits and two flags. For each column, prepare its entries in the first-flag-one part; the leftover norm goes into flag zero. For each row, choose L distinct extended indices containing its actual support and padded indices as needed, and apply the inverse preparation of their uniform superposition. Each relevant entry gets `1/sqrt(L)`. Rotate the second flag by amplitude `t_0 sqrt(L)`, where `t_0=sin(pi/(4L+2))`. The exact block is t_0 T.

Alternate the two reflections about the prepared input subspace and the common good output subspace for L rounds. In each singular plane, the initial angle is `beta_j=arcsin(t_0 sigma_j)` and the final angle is `(2L+1)beta_j`. Since `(2L+1)theta=pi/2`, its vector differs from `sigma_j e_j` by at most

\[
(2L+1)2t_0(1-\sigma_j)+(1-\sigma_j)
\le(\pi+1)(1-\sigma_j)\le5\epsilon.
\]

The planes are mutually orthogonal, so this is a genuine operator-norm bound, not only a columnwise bound. The exact-slot cost is O(L(n+w+h)), with w+h+2 new bits; arbitrary labels merely control the same slots. Applying it to U3 followed by Fourier transforms gives both field encoders with error `5r^(2L)` and shared embedding.

## U7. One recursive call and clean error

Suppose the smaller controlled F(z) synthesis has clean error delta'. Encode E, apply that circuit **once**, reverse R. The four-term operator comparison, using only ideal field output for the recursive error service and contracting field maps, gives

\[
\delta\le\delta'+\frac{2r^L}{1-r}+10r^{2L}
\le\delta'+18r^L.
\tag{U4}
\]

Leakage outside the good subspace is already charged by the encoder error and is not amplified by a unitary recursive circuit. Each label branch has the same bound; hence coherent label superpositions are legal. The recursive circuit never appears inside the local field amplification loops. All fresh bits return to zero in the ideal comparison; reused actual scratch need not be exactly zero at intermediate times.

The source chooses L=10000J and obtains error below1/100 by `r^L<=3/L`. Its Q=O((n+1)^10), S=O((n+1)^13) bounds follow by summing the encoder costs across fewer than J levels. The generator computes only the sequence of valid dimensions and register/control lists, not any target-dependent table content.

## U8. Boolean compilation preserves relative phases

For each table entry `W_y=e^(i gamma_y) S_y`, S_y in SU(2), approximate S_y and `diag(e^(i gamma_y),e^(-i gamma_y))` by determinant-one words. Apply the latter to a zero phase scratch bit. This retains the relative phase under coherent y, with clean error at most eta for the whole slot. Store all word symbols in one Boolean truth table addressed by slot, padded controls, word, position and symbol bit. Two read and two erase queries per word position give exactly restored lookup bits. H,T,T-dagger,CNOT implement conditional symbols exactly, including controlled H via the explicit conjugation formula.

Compilation's telescoping identity places `(actual-ideal)` only after ideal prefixes; thus the per-slot zero-scratch error bound suffices even if actual phase scratch leaks. The source's elementary SU(2) density argument and commutator refinement are valid with matrix phases, not projective equivalence. Diamond error is at most twice the clean operator error, after arbitrary reference extension and partial trace.

## U9. Stronger deduction: gate words cost polynomially in accuracy bits

The paper weakens its own refinement to halving error and records a length bound O(xi^-3). Its actual proof gives the stronger recurrence

\[
\epsilon_{j+1}\le C\epsilon_j^{3/2},\qquad \ell_{j+1}\le5\ell_j,
\tag{U5}
\]

with an absolute C and a fixed finite initial determinant-one net. Choose the fixed initial net so `q_0=C^2 epsilon_0<=1/4`. Then `q_j<=q_0^((3/2)^j)` and length at most `5^j ell_0`. Therefore

\[
\ell(\xi)\le K[\log_2(2/\xi)]^4,
\tag{U6}
\]

for some absolute integer K, because `log_(3/2)5<4`; enlarge K for the constant-accuracy range. This is the established Solovay–Kitaev mechanism, directly present in the manuscript proof. Its fixed K can be hardcoded exactly as the author's weaker constant can. The generator still need not find a net word or calculate a gate table: those choices are supplied by the oracle. This is an existential-oracle result with charged circuit cost, not an oracle-acquisition algorithm.

## U10. Explicit accuracy-bit theorem and complete circuit cost

Given unary n>=1 and B>=1, retain M,J from U3 and set

\[
c=\lceil\log_2(36J)\rceil,\qquad L=4(B+c),
\quad b=nL+\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil,
\quad K_G=b(L+1).
\tag{U7}
\]

Since `(3/4)^4<1/2`, `18J r^L<=2^(-B)/2`. The same recursive skeleton therefore has clean error at most half the requested error. For its actual number S of slots, choose the integer

\[
h=2^{B+1}(S+1),\qquad\eta=1/h.
\]

Approximate the two words in each slot to eta/2, with the fixed common padding length

\[
\ell=\left\lceil K[\log_2(4h)]^4\right\rceil.
\tag{U8}
\]

Use the Boolean compilation of U8. Total added clean error `S/h<2^(-B)/2`, so the final clean error is less than `2^(-B)` and full diamond distance less than `2^(1-B)`.

For `N=n+1`, `L=O(B+log N)`, `b=O(NL)`, `K_G=O(NL^2)`, `h_index=O(log L)`. Because L dominates log N and is at least a constant, summing the exact per-level costs gives

\[
Q=O(N^4L^2),\qquad S=O(N^4L^3).
\tag{U9}
\]

Here Q includes all retained phase/port/work registers; any initial coherent-label width t is added to Q. The count does not multiply the J-level recursion by its number of possible label strings.

The compiling address has length `O(Q+log(S+1)+log(ell+1))`; preparing it costs O(Q) reversible gates per word symbol. Two words per slot, four bit-oracle calls per symbol give at most `8S ell` calls. Ordinary gate count is `O(S ell[Q+log S+log ell])`, width is `O(Q+log S+log ell)`, and all lists/loops are generated in time polynomial in n+B (or n+B+t). The exponential integer h has only O(B+log S) bits. Formula U8 uses that bit length, not an h-step loop. Thus this is polynomial in requested **accuracy bits**, rather than polynomial in inverse error. Constants remain enormous; no finite implementation or practical speed evidence is provided.

The exact target-dependent Boolean table may still contain `2^{poly(n+B)}` entries, and the generator has not acquired a way to fill or evaluate it. In our finite-bath preparation setting `n=log_2 D` is at least the spin count; the earlier dense matrix/filter controls and heralded success cost are not removed by U10. In a setting that independently supplies efficient coherent table access, U10 is a genuine synthesis resource; without that service it is not a construction of the physical controller.
