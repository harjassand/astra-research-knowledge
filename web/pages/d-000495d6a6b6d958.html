## 1. Exact exported interface

Input: integer T>=1; N rational unit anchors x_i; a rational orthogonal harmonic basis q_j of degrees1,...,T with exact squared norms nu_j>0; the exact moment vector b_j=N^-1 sum_i q_j(x_i); and

    J_(j,ia)=N^-1 [grad_S q_j(x_i)]_a.

Supply any rational matrix R in Q^(3N x M), M=T(T+2), whose column block at each node is tangent:

    x_i^T R_i=0.

A rounded numerical right inverse may be exactly tangent-projected before use. Exact right inversion is optional. For residual vectors use ||z||_nu^2=sum_j z_j^2/nu_j. Compute the rational scalars

    b2=sum_j b_j^2/nu_j,
    c2=max_i sum_(a=1)^3 sum_j R_(ia,j)^2 nu_j,
    a2=sum_(j,k) [I-JR]_(jk)^2 nu_k/nu_j.           (C1)

Thus ||Rz||_(max node Euclidean)<=C||z||_nu when C>=sqrt(c2); ||(I-JR)z||_nu<=a||z||_nu when a>=sqrt(a2). These are Frobenius upper bounds of the relevant whitened operators, all checkable by rational arithmetic. They deliberately need not be the best operator bounds.

Also certify an initial Gram lower bound lambda>1/2:

    H-lambda diag(nu_j) is positive semidefinite,
    H_(jk)=N^-1 sum_i q_j(x_i)q_k(x_i).             (C2)

An exact rational LDL check may use positive definiteness instead. Arbitrary unweighted polynomial bases require their full rational norm matrix rather than silently assuming diagonal nu; this note uses the established orthogonal associated-Legendre basis.

## 2. Explicit global differential bounds

Let e_j=q_j/sqrt(nu_j). Set lambda_l=l(l+1) and

    A2=sum_(l=1)^T lambda_l(2l+1)/2,
    B2=sum_(l=1)^T lambda_l(3lambda_l-2)(2l+1)/8.   (C3)

For any point and unit tangent direction v,

    ||d e[v]||_2=sqrt(A2),
    ||Hess_S e[v,v]||_2=sqrt(B2).                  (C4)

Here is an independent proof of the second formula. The addition kernel for degree l is (2l+1)P_l(x dot y). On a great circle gamma(t), its two-variable restriction is (2l+1)P_l(cos(t-s)). Differentiate twice in t and twice in s at zero. The result is (2l+1)[P_l'(1)+3P_l''(1)]. Legendre's differential equation gives P_l'(1)=lambda_l/2 and, after one differentiation, P_l''(1)=lambda_l(lambda_l-2)/8. This is exactly (C3). Differentiating once in each variable proves the first formula. Mixed unit directions obey the same B bound by polarization: the sum of the squared norms |u+v|^2+|u-v|^2 is4, so the triangle bound for the vector-valued symmetric Hessian is B. Rescaling gives ||Hess e[u,v]||<=B|u||v|.

For fixed rational unit x use the rational Cayley chart on its tangent plane,

    Q_x(u)=[(1-|u|^2/4)x+u]/[1+|u|^2/4].          (C5)

Put d=1+|u|^2/4. Its induced metric is d^-2 times the Euclidean tangent metric, so its differential norm is1/d<=1. For a straight parameter curve u+t w, the Christoffel expression is

    Gamma(u;w,w)=-(u dot w)w/d+|w|^2 u/(2d).

The vector in brackets is a reflection of u times |w|^2/(2d), hence has Euclidean norm |u||w|^2/(2d). Applying dQ gives covariant acceleration norm |u||w|^2/(2d^2). This is a factor3 smaller than a naive triangle estimate. Therefore on |u|<=r,

    ||D^2(e composed Q_x)(u)[w,w]||_2
         <=K(r)|w|^2,  K(r)=B+(r/2)A,             (C6)

for rational upper bounds A>=sqrt(A2), B>=sqrt(B2). Polarization gives the same mixed bound. The exact unit identity for (C5) follows by expanding its numerator squared norm.

Useful constants: at T=2, A2=18 and B2=63; at T=3, A2=60 and B2=420. These bounds have no N factor because the moments are averaged by1/N.

## 3. The finite basin inequalities

Choose rational nonnegative upper bounds a,C,A,B as above, a positive rational radius rho, and an optional final distinctness perturbation distance delta_f. Let

    r=C rho,
    kappa=K(r) C^2 rho.

The following exact rational tests are sufficient:

    b2 <=rho^2/16,
    a+kappa <=1/2,
    r<=1,
    lambda >1/2+2E A(r+delta_f),  E>=sqrt(M).     (C7)

The radius bound r<=1 is convenient, not load bearing for the global chart. If direct final Gram verification is preferred, the last inequality can be omitted from the *root-finding* certificate and the Gram interface verified exactly after continuation; in that version final acceptance is conditional on that separate finite test. A weaker unchecked Gram at the initializer is never presented as final acceptance.

The a/kappa inequalities also certify derivative surjectivity: a<1 implies JR is invertible and J has full row rank. A true right inverse is R(JR)^-1 with maximum-node bound C/(1-a). We do not need to form it to iterate.

## 4. Frozen-preconditioner continuation

Define rational moment map F(z) at nodes Q_(x_i)((Rz)_i), with outputs in the q basis. Its derivative at0 is JR and F(0)=b. Iterate

    Phi(z)=z-F(z),     z_0=0.                     (C8)

Every iterate represents equal-weight rational unit directions. On ||z||_nu<=rho, (C6) and averaging give

    ||D F(z)-J R||_(nu->nu)<=K(r)C^2||z||_nu,
    ||D Phi(z)||<=a+kappa<=1/2.                  (C9)

Taylor with F(0)=b yields

    ||Phi(z)||_nu<=||b||_nu+a rho+(kappa/2)rho
       <=rho[1/4+a+(1/2-a)/2]<=3rho/4.          (C10)

Thus the closed ball maps into its interior and the actual contraction is proved. Banach supplies a unique z* in this ball with F(z*)=0. No convexity of a design potential, degree theorem, supplied exact design, or uncharged nonlinear root oracle is assumed.

To control bits, reset z after every iteration to a rational coordinate grid so the weighted reset error is<=sigma, with

    sigma<=min(beta_T/128,rho/16),
    beta_T=1/[2048(T+1)^5].

For example set V=max(1,max_j 1/nu_j) and coordinate mesh chi<=sigma/(2MV). Nearest rounding gives ||Delta z||_nu<=sigma/2; the looser charged sigma is safe. All nu have explicitly charged encoded bit lengths. Because (C10) leaves rho/4 slack, every rounded iterate remains in the ball. The error recursion is

    ||z_k-z*||_nu<=2^-k rho+2sigma.               (C11)

The map F is at most3/2 Lipschitz there, since ||JR||<=1+a and its derivative perturbation adds at most kappa. Choose I with 2^-I rho<=beta_T/128. Then

    ||F(z_I)||_nu <=(3/2)(beta_T/128+2sigma)
       <=9 beta_T/256<beta_T/4.                 (C12)

If an input already has ||b||<=beta_T/4, continuation is unnecessary; exact final mean/Gram and unit/distinct checks suffice. Otherwise the specified rounding/stopping parameters are nondegenerate. R, basis coefficients and rounded z have fixed polynomial bit lengths; exact evaluations and chart operations have polynomial height at each iteration. The number of steps is O(log(rho/beta_T)), charged against the certificate encoding. There is no recursive uncontrolled exact-denominator growth.

## 5. Gram and distinctness

The feature norm is identically sqrt(M)<=E by the addition theorem. A geodesic displacement g changes e by at most A g, and its empirical outer-product Gram by at most2E A g. Each Q_x(u) has geodesic distance2atan(|u|/2)<=r. Thus (C2) and (C7) retain Gram>I/2 along the full continuation and after perturbation distance delta_f.

Choose delta_f<=beta_T/(16A), plus the Gram margin in (C7). Deterministic local stereographic candidate shifts make final rational directions distinct, moving each by at most delta_f. The previous cycle's N+1 injective candidates/node argument works without any initial or intermediate separation. It adds mean<=A delta_f<=beta_T/16, so (C12) leaves ample slack for the accepted tiny-mean interface. Naive comparisons cost O(N^3), or use an ordered dictionary. Unit norms and rational coordinates are exact.

Final acceptance is directly verifiable from the emitted rational directions: sum b_qj^2/nu_j<=beta_T^2 and empirical H-(1/2)diag(nu) positive definite. The certificate may be conservative and fail even when this final test passes; such a failure is not an impossibility conclusion.

## 6. Runtime, comparison, and downstream use

The input certificate is all rational and checkable in polynomial bit time in T,N and its encoded height. Building a candidate R and entering (C7) may use finite numerical initialization/LP corrections followed by exact reconstruction; that acquisition cost and each rejected proposal must be reported. The theorem is a polynomial continuation guarantee **once the certificate is acquired**. It does not hide candidate acquisition or claim that N=18/32 always works at arbitrary T.

The frozen-preconditioner continuation uses rational matrix products and moment evaluations, with no repeated exact LP after certification. Its fixed-height z reset can be implemented with exact integer division. Final coordinate height depends polynomially on the certificate height rather than O(log T) uniformly; this is the price of practical a-posteriori input.
