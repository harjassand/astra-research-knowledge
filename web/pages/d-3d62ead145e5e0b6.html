# Fixed critical data and a global exceptional set

Status: an independently reconstructed characteristic-zero lemma, and a derived extension of family020's Proposition8.2. No novelty claim: finiteness of polynomials with prescribed critical data belongs to Lyashko–Looijenga/Hurwitz theory. The extension below is a useful way to carry the release's global exceptional-set argument into other determinant problems. It does not improve the release's final exponents in the tested parameter schedule.

## Exact interface

Fix integers d,k≥2, put r=k−1 and M=dr+1, and fix distinct algebraic numbers θ₁,…,θ_d in a fixed number field L. Consider polynomials Φ∈L[t] of degree exactly M with distinct labeled points z₁=0,z₂=1,z₃,…,z_d∈L satisfying

Φ(z_i)=θ_i, and Φ^(j)(z_i)=0 for 1≤j≤r.

There are finitely many such normalized polynomials and point tuples. Write their polynomial family as F. Define one global set

E={n∈Z: n=Φ(z) for some Φ∈F and z∈L}.

Then # (E∩[−X,X])=O_{L,F}(X^(1/M)). The constants depend on the fixed field and fixed critical values, and no uniformity in those inputs is asserted.

For any N∈L[s] of degree M with distinct τ_i∈L and (s−τ_i)^k dividing N(s)−θ_i, every integer value N(s) at s∈L belongs to this same E. The coefficients, slopes, and heights of N may vary; the field and θ_i may not vary.

## Proof of finiteness, reconstructed with tangent vectors

The polynomial-and-critical-point constraints define a finite-type algebraic locus over C, restricted to the open set where the leading coefficient and every z_i−z_j are nonzero. At a solution, Φ' has degree dr and has a root of multiplicity at least r at each z_i. Thus these multiplicities are exactly r and exhaust all its roots; Φ^(r+1)(z_i)≠0.

Let Q be an infinitesimal coefficient variation of Φ, and let v_i be the infinitesimal variation of z_i. Differentiating the constraints gives

Q(z_i)=0,

Q^(j)(z_i)+Φ^(j+1)(z_i)v_i=0 (1≤j≤r).

For 0≤j≤r−1, the second term is zero. Consequently ∏_i(t−z_i)^r divides Q. This product is a nonzero scalar multiple of Φ', so

Q=AΦ', deg A≤1.

At derivative order r, the product rule reduces to Q^(r)(z_i)=A(z_i)Φ^(r+1)(z_i). Hence v_i=−A(z_i). The fixed normalizations impose v₁=v₂=0, so A(0)=A(1)=0. Since A has degree at most one, A=0, Q=0, and all v_i=0.

Every solution has zero Zariski tangent space. In particular every irreducible component of the finite-type locus is zero-dimensional, and there are only finitely many points. This proof also establishes that the normalized solution locus is reduced at every solution. If it is empty, the conclusion is immediate.

## Proof of the integer-value bound

Fix Φ∈F. Choose an integer c>0 such that cΦ(t)=A_Mt^M+…+A₀ with A_i∈O_L and A_M≠0. If Φ(z)=n∈Z, then w=A_Mz satisfies the monic equation

w^M+A_{M−1}w^(M−1)+A_{M−2}A_Mw^(M−2)+…+(A₀−cn)A_M^(M−1)=0.

Thus w∈O_L and z lies in the fixed fractional lattice A_M^(−1)O_L. For every embedding σ:L→C, σ(Φ)(σ(z))=n. The leading term of each fixed polynomial σ(Φ) bounds every |σ(z)| by O(X^(1/M)) when |n|≤X. In a fixed Z-basis of the fractional lattice, all integer coordinates of z therefore have that same bound.

The locus where Φ(z) is rational is a fixed algebraic set of dimension one: after base change to C it has coordinate ring

C[v,(w_σ)_σ]/(σ(Φ)(w_σ)−v : σ:L→C).

Each equation is monic, after division by its leading coefficient, in its own w_σ. The ring is finite free over C[v], of rank M^[L:Q]. Hence the locus has bounded degree and dimension one. A bounded-degree affine curve contains O(R) integer points in a box of radius R: choose a nonconstant coordinate on each curve component, slice at its O(R) integer values, and use the degree bound on every finite fiber. Zero-dimensional components are harmless. With R=O(X^(1/M)) this counts O(X^(1/M)) possible inputs, hence values. Sum over the finite family.

Finally normalize N by Φ(t)=N(τ₁+(τ₂−τ₁)t). Its critical points and values satisfy the fixed system, and its normalized input still lies in L. This proves the global-value assertion.

## Consequence for the release's polynomial line curves

In family020 Section9, a polynomial line curve has a_i(s)=r_i(s−τ_i), r_i≠0, and a_i(s)^k b_i(s)=n(s)−θ_i. Distinct θ_i force distinct τ_i. Therefore

deg n≥M=d(k−1)+1, deg b_i=deg n−k≥(d−1)(k−1).

The release globally deletes the equality case only for d=4,k=2, where M=5. The same construction works for every fixed d,k. After deleting one E of size O(X^(1/M)) before any boxes or curves are chosen, every surviving polynomial-line curve has

deg b_i≥(d−1)(k−1)+1.

The curve Hilbert threshold may accordingly use g=(d−1)(k−1)+1 for all d,k, in place of the release's g=(d−1)(k−1)+1_{d=4}. The other curve branches still give the threshold U/2, so the resulting δ₁ is max{U/2,min(U,V/g)}.

This is a genuine local extension, but it is not a stronger global density theorem. Independently reconstructed exact arithmetic over all 3,989 parameter cells in degrees4–8 found zero changed cumulative exponent sums after this substitution. Higher-dimensional cumulative maxima or the U/2 branch dominate. Results: `results/frontier_number2/cycle19/PARAMETER_SCAN.json`.

## Adversarial boundaries

* The field L must be fixed. For Φ(t)=3t²−2t³, every n∈Z has a preimage z_n in some number field of degree at most three. Allowing that field to vary with n makes the set of values all of Z, contradicting the asserted O(X^(1/3)) bound. A bounded field degree alone is insufficient.
* All prescribed critical values are necessary. Removing a value constraint permits a nonzero tangent direction. The finite exact tests delete the final value constraint and obtain rank one less than the full Jacobian rank.
* The minimal degree M is essential for exhausting Φ'. At higher degree, Q need not be divisible by all of Φ', so the two affine normalizations no longer force rigidity by this argument.
* The global deletion must occur once. Charging O(X^(1/M)) separately to a growing number of boxes can erase a determinant saving.
* This lemma cannot repair a deficient higher-dimensional Hilbert bound. The exact scan is a concrete falsifier for that optimistic interpretation.
