## 2. Original exact bridge: no symmetry/commutation requirement

Let `B` be a unital trace-preserving completely positive map on a finite matrix algebra, `K=K*`, and `tau=exp(K)/Z`. Operator concavity of log gives

```text
log B(exp K) >= B(K).
D(tau || B(tau))
  = Tr tau [K - log B(exp K)]
  <= Tr tau [K - B(K)].                         (1)
```

Consequently,

```text
T(tau, B(tau)) <= sqrt(Tr tau [K-B(K)] / 2)
               <= sqrt(||K-B(K)|| / 2).          (2)
```

Here `T=||.||_1/2`, entropy uses natural logarithms, and the expectation in (1) is necessarily nonnegative. This is an elementary deduction from operator Jensen and quantum Pinsker; no priority is claimed. Unlike the stronger linear minorant in N01, it does not need `[B(exp K),K]=0`. Exponentiation of a noncommuting operator inequality is never used.

For a spin-j coherent channel

```text
B_j(X) = (2j+1) int_S2 <u|X|u> |u><u| dmu(u),
```

the exact eigenvalues on tensor ranks 1 and 2 are

```text
lambda_1 = j/(j+1),
lambda_2 = j(2j-1)/[(j+1)(2j+3)].                (3)
```

Rank 0 is preserved. Write `A_0=A-(tr A)I/3` and

```text
Q_A(J) = sum_ab A_ab (J_a J_b + J_b J_a)/2.
```

Then `Q_A0` has rank 2 and

```text
||Q_A0|| <= ||A_0||op j(j+1),
(1-lambda_2)||Q_A0|| <= 3 ||A_0||op j.           (4)
```

For `j=0,1/2` the absent rank-2 component is zero. These identities are a useful reusable route for collective Gibbs families whose Hamiltonians are not rotationally invariant. Large scalar functions of `J^2` cause no defect.

## 3. A concrete critical quantum experiment

Take N spin-half sites and `J=sum_i sigma_i/2`. Public data are N, the spin decomposition, and fixed bounds `||A||op<=A_*`, `||b||<=B_*`. The unknown parameters are a real symmetric 3-by-3 matrix A and vector b. One full density matrix is supplied:

```text
rho_(N,A,b) = exp[ 2 J^2/N
                   + Q_A(J)/N^(3/2)
                   + b.J/N^(3/4) ] / Z_(N,A,b).  (5)
```

The scalar part of A includes an unknown temperature displacement in the critical window `beta_N=2+lambda/sqrt(N)`. Its traceless part is an unknown anisotropy tensor. The quadratic term and linear term need not commute. All-to-all interaction and permutation symmetry remain explicit assumptions. This is neither an arbitrary unknown local Hamiltonian nor a supplied arbitrary invariant reference.

**Operational statement, independently audited in qualitative form:** fixed parameter-independent channels, one in each direction, approximate (5) uniformly by a single observation

```text
X in R^3,
p_(A,b)(x) = Z_(A,b)^(-1)
             exp[-4|x|^4/3 + x^T A x + b.x].     (6)
```

The error tends to zero in full trace distance/TV. The reverse channel prepares the entire N-spin mixed density, including its multiplicity state, rather than only matching magnetization moments.

### 3.1 Critical sector calculation

For even N, the spin-j multiplicity is

```text
m_N(j) = (2j+1)/(N+1) binom(N+1, N/2-j).
```

At A=b=0 the sector mass is proportional to

```text
(2j+1) m_N(j) exp[2j(j+1)/N].                   (7)
```

Set `k=j+1/2`, `L=N+1`. The combinatorial factor equals

```text
4k^2/L * binom(L,L/2-k).
```

The entropy expansion gives

```text
log binom(L,L/2-k)
 = L log2 - .5 log(pi L/2)
   - 2k^2/L - (4/3)k^4/L^3
   + O(k^2/L^2 + k^6/L^5 + 1/L).              (8)
```

The Gibbs quadratic term cancels the leading `-2k^2/L`; the residual quadratic term is `2k^2/[N(N+1)]`. Therefore `k/N^(3/4)` has limiting density proportional to

```text
r^2 exp[-4r^4/3],  r>=0.                       (9)
```

For a rigorous qualitative TV limit, use compact-uniform Stirling, radial-cell jitter of width `N^(-3/4)`, and the elementary entropy inequality

```text
I(z) >= z^2/2 + z^4/12, |z|<=1,
```

to control tails. For `k<=L/4` the Stirling prefactor is uniformly bounded; beyond this range its extra polynomial cost is dominated by an exponential in N. Tilting by bounded A,b only multiplies the radial bound by `exp[C(r^2+r)]`, so all needed moments are uniformly integrable. The same derivation works on the odd-N half-integer lattice.

Finite sanity check, **not proof**: TV between the exact zero-field sector law and the normalized quartic grid law was `0.05125, 0.02388, 0.01147, 0.00563, 0.00279` for `N=64,256,1024,4096,16384`. The values of `sqrt(N)*TV` decreased from `0.410` to `0.357`. This supports the coefficient and Weyl shift, not a certified convergence rate. Exact check output is `work/scouts/gibbs_criticality_checks.json`; its script is adjacent.

### 3.2 Whole-state classicalization and parameter-independent channels

Schur decomposition gives a spin Gibbs factor tensor a maximally mixed multiplicity state. On each spin sector the scalar `2j(j+1)/N` is preserved by B_j. Equations (1)–(4) yield the uniform global bound

```text
D(rho || B rho)
 <= (3/2)||A_0||op N^(-1/2) + B_* N^(-3/4).
T(rho, B rho) = O(N^(-1/4)).                    (10)
```

The entropy expectation also gives `O(N^(-3/8))` trace error after using the uniform tilted moment `E j=O(N^(3/4))` derived from the quartic tails. The conservative global bound (10) is already enough to establish vanishing error.

The encoder measures j and its coherent direction u. Its unnormalized joint density is the known critical sector weight times `<u|exp(F_j)|u>`, where F_j contains only the A,b terms. Its lower symbol is

```text
g_j(u) = [j^2 u^T A u + (j/2)(tr A-u^T A u)]/N^(3/2)
         + (j/N^(3/4)) b.u.                    (11)
```

Put `a=tr A/3`. An exact upper symbol is

```text
h_j^+(u) = a j(j+1)/N^(3/2)
           +(j+1)(j+3/2) u^T A_0 u/N^(3/2)
           +(j+1)b.u/N^(3/4).
```

Its coherent quantization is F_j. The difference from (11) is at most

```text
3(j+1/2)||A_0||op/N^(3/2) + B_*/N^(3/4).
```

Scalar Jensen supplies `<u|exp(F_j)|u> >= exp(g_j(u))`. For the integrated upper bound, diagonalize F_j. For each normalized eigenvector psi, `(2j+1)|<psi|j,u>|^2 dmu(u)` is a probability measure, and its expectation of `h_j^+` is the eigenvalue. Scalar Jensen followed by summation gives

```text
Tr exp(F_j)/(2j+1) <= int exp(h_j^+) dmu
                   <= exp(delta_j) int exp(g_j) dmu.
```

This remains valid at `j=0,1/2`: the rank-two *function* in the upper symbol need not vanish, but its quantization does. Pointwise lower domination and the integrated mass bound give TV at most `1-exp(-max_j delta_j)` after normalization. Thus the actual encoded distribution is within `O(N^(-1/2))` TV of the discrete classical density proportional to

```text
q_N^0(j) exp[(j/N^(3/4))^2 u^T A u
                     + (j/N^(3/4)) b.u].       (12)
```

Radial jitter turns (12) into (6) in uniform TV. The reverse channel rounds the radius to a sector, prepares its coherent spin state at the supplied direction, attaches the public maximally mixed multiplicity, and undoes the decomposition. Comparing it with `B rho` plus (10) completes the proposed bidirectional limit.

No decoder step uses the unknown A,b or the actual tilted sector law. The mean-field formula supplies the public family; this is not acquisition of a Hamiltonian from data. The next section defines an explicit computable additive remainder; no unproved joint N,D power law is needed.

### 3.3 Exact cells and a public finite-N error certificate

Write `M=A_*`, `B=B_*`, `s=N^(3/4)`, `Delta=1/s`, `k_j=j+1/2`, and `r_j=k_j/s`. The allowed spins are `j_min,j_min+1,...,N/2`, with `j_min=0` for even N and `1/2` for odd N. Use the cells

```text
I_(j_min) = [0,(k_(j_min)+1/2)/s),
I_j = [(k_j-1/2)/s,(k_j+1/2)/s) for j>j_min.
R_N = (N+2)/(2s).
```

Their union is `[0,R_N)`; the first odd-N cell has width `3Delta/2`, and all other cells have width Delta. The forward channel draws r uniformly in its measured sector's cell and outputs x=ru. The reverse rounds to these same cells and clamps `r>=R_N` to `j=N/2`. Its preparation is `P_(j,u) tensor I_mult/m_N(j)` followed by the inverse Schur transform. At x=0 fix any direction. Thus reverse-after-forward is exactly B_N. Multiplicity reset does not break unitality: applied to identity, the measurement density is `(2j+1)m_N(j)`, and the prepared block integrates to `I_(V_j) tensor I_mult`.

Define entirely public probability densities on radial Lebesgue measure:

```text
a_N(j) = (2j+1)m_N(j) exp[2j(j+1)/N],
q_N^0(j) = a_N(j)/sum_l a_N(l),
w_N(r) = q_N^0(j)/|I_j| for r in I_j; 0 for r>=R_N,
w_0(r) = c_r r^2 exp[-4r^4/3], r>=0,
c_r = 4(4/3)^(3/4)/Gamma(3/4).
```

The multiplicity formula in Section 3.1 holds for both parities because `N/2-j` is always an integer. The following are finite, explicit one-dimensional integrals and a finite binomial sum, involving M,B,N only:

```text
V_N = int_0^infty exp[Mr^2+Br] |w_N(r)-w_0(r)| dr,
eta_N(r) = Delta [M(2r+Delta)+B],
U_N = int_0^infty w_N(r) exp[Mr^2+Br]
                         (exp[eta_N(r)]-1) dr,
z_N = sum_j q_N^0(j) exp[-M r_j^2-B r_j],
z_0 = int_0^infty w_0(r) exp[-Mr^2-Br] dr,
gamma_N = min{1,(V_N+U_N)/min(z_N,z_0)}.          (13a)
```

Every point in I_j obeys `|r-r_j|<=Delta`, including the exceptional odd-N first cell. For `f(r,u)=r^2 u^T A u+r b.u`, this gives `|f(r_j,u)-f(r,u)|<=eta_N(r)`. Consequently the L1 difference of the two *unnormalized* tilted densities `w_N(r)e^(f(r_j,u))` and `w_0(r)e^(f(r,u))` is at most `V_N+U_N`. Their normalizers are at least z_N,z_0; normalizing gives the TV bound gamma_N in (13a). The compact Stirling limit and quartic tilted-tail proof in the independent audit show `V_N,U_N -> 0`, `z_N -> z_0>0`, hence `gamma_N -> 0`. No rate of these integrals is asserted.

Because `||A_0||op<=4M/3`, conservative global symbol and generator constants are

```text
delta_N = 2M(N+1)/N^(3/2)+B Delta,
kappa_N = M(N+1/4)/N^(3/2)+(B/2)Delta,
sigma_N = (1-exp[-delta_N])+(1-exp[-2kappa_N]),
beta_N = sqrt[M N^(-1/2)+(B/2)Delta],
alpha_N = min{1,sigma_N+gamma_N}.                (13b)
```

Here delta_N bounds upper-minus-lower symbols, and kappa_N bounds the lower symbol minus `f(r_j,u)`; changing a log density by at most kappa_N costs at most `1-e^(-2kappa_N)` in normalized TV. The generator estimate gives beta_N directly by Pinsker. These quantities certify

```text
sup_Theta TV(T_N rho_(N,A,b),p_(A,b)) <= alpha_N,
sup_Theta T(S_N p_(A,b),rho_(N,A,b)) <= alpha_N+beta_N.  (13c)
```

All constants and finite-size remainders in (13a)–(13c) are now defined explicitly and independently of the unknown parameters and archive dimension. The radial certificate can be evaluated to any declared numerical quadrature precision; an ordinary floating-point evaluation alone is not a certified enclosure. The audit proves its vanishing qualitatively and does not certify additional Robbins-rate constants.

## 4. Original non-Gaussian memory law

For one observation from (6), define e_D as the infimum uniform TV reconstruction error through a memory of total dimension D. Arbitrary quantum/hybrid memories and independent shared classical seeds are allowed; every input-dependent retained register is charged. The matching lower bound below requires `B_*>0`; a single known state has zero reconstruction error and cannot obey a positive lower law.

### 4.1 Positive classical upper: O(D^(-2/3)), without a tail logarithm

Map `y_i=tanh(x_i)`. The transformed densities on `[-1,1]^3` extend by zero to smooth functions at the boundary: the quartic exponential beats every inverse power of the boundary distance. On a fixed compact A,b set, their C^2 norms are uniformly bounded.

Use tensor-product piecewise-linear nonnegative hats `phi_k` on a uniform grid of mesh h, with `sum_k phi_k=1`. On input y retain label k with probability `phi_k(y)`. Decode k by sampling the fixed density `phi_k/int phi_k`. The resulting operator is

```text
K_h p = sum_k [int p phi_k / int phi_k] phi_k.
```

Interior hats have zero first moment about their node; C^2-flatness at the boundary controls the boundary hats. Therefore `||K_h p-p||_1<=C h^2` uniformly, while `D=O(h^(-3))`. Every decoder is parameter independent. This proves `e_D<=C D^(-2/3)` with no truncation/tail factor.

An explicit constant is available. Let H bound the operator norm of the Hessians of the transformed densities. For mesh `h=2/m`, each normalized hat has coordinate second moment `h^2/6`; the difference between its average and the nodal density is at most `Hh^2/4`. Multilinear interpolation costs at most `3Hh^2/8`. Integrating over the cube of volume 8 gives TV at most `5Hh^2/2=10H/m^2`. Taking `m=floor(D^(1/3))-1` for `D>=64`, and using one fixed reconstruction for smaller D, gives

```text
e_D <= C_* D^(-2/3),  C_*=max{16,40H}.          (14a)
```

H itself can be made a public one-dimensional bound. Put

```text
Z_- = 4pi int_0^infty r^2 exp[-4r^4/3-Mr^2-Br] dr,
P(r) = (16/3)r^3+2Mr+B+2,
H = (3/Z_-) sup_(r>=0) exp[-4r^4/3+Mr^2+(B+2sqrt(3)+4)r]
                          [P(r)^2+16r^2+2M+2+2P(r)].
```

To verify this, differentiate the log of the transformed density in x coordinates: its first derivatives are bounded by P(r), its second derivative entries by `16r^2+2M+2`, and `d/dy_i=cosh(x_i)^2 d/dx_i`. The displayed exponential dominates the Jacobian and derivative factors. Quartic decay makes H finite and makes the transformed density and its first two derivatives vanish on every boundary face. No unknown parameter enters this constant.

### 4.2 Quantum lower: Omega(D^(-2/3))

Restrict to A=0 and b in the nondegenerate cube `[-a,a]^3`, where `a=B_*/sqrt(3)>0`, contained in the declared field ball. Choose a prior with density proportional to `Z_(0,b)` on that cube. Its posterior mean given X=x factorizes:

```text
m_i(x) = a coth(a x_i) - 1/x_i,
m_i(0)=0.                                     (13)
```

Each coordinate is strictly increasing from -a to a, and `m_i'(x_i)` is comparable to `1/(1+x_i^2)`. The predictive density of X is proportional to

```text
exp[-4|x|^4/3] product_i [sinh(a x_i)/(a x_i)].
```

Consequently Y=m(X) has a globally bounded Lebesgue density L: the inverse-Jacobian grows only polynomially while the predictive tail decays quartically.

Any reconstruction kernel with uniform TV error e obeys

```text
E ||m(X)-m(X')||^2 <= 18 a^2 e.                (14)
```

To see this, compare the original and reconstructed joint laws of b,X using the bounded test `b.m(X)`, compare the two predictive laws using `||m(X)||^2`, and use `E[b|X]=m(X)`. Expanding squared distance gives (14).

If the memory encoding of x is omega_x and the decoder POVM is M(dx'), then `omega_x<=I` and `int Tr M=D` imply

```text
Pr(||m(X)-m(X')||<=r) <= D L (4pi/3) r^3.     (15)
```

Taking the RHS to be 1/2 forces squared distortion at least `c D^(-2/3)`. With (14), `e>=c' D^(-2/3)`. Conditioning on an independent common seed leaves (15) valid. There is no classical-memory restriction. This is unassisted storage: a free pre-shared entangled decoder system is outside the interface, and any input-dependent persistent register must be charged.

The constants can be specified without an unspecified density supremum. Define

```text
c_a = min{a^2 exp(-2)/3, 1-sinh(1)^(-2)},
Q_0 = pi (3/4)^(3/4) Gamma(3/4),
L_* = [Q_0 c_a^3]^(-1)
      sup_(r>=0) (1+r^2)^3 exp[-4r^4/3+sqrt(3)a r].
```

For `|at|<=1`, the posterior on `[-a,a]` has density at least `exp(-2)/(2a)`, so its variance `m_i'(t)` is at least `a^2 exp(-2)/3`. For `|at|>=1`, monotonicity of `s/sinh(s)` gives `m_i'(t)>= [1-sinh(1)^(-2)]/t^2`. Thus `m_i'(t)>=c_a/(1+t^2)` everywhere. The predictive normalizer is at least Q_0, and `sinh(at)/(at)<=exp(a|t|)`. Dividing by the Jacobian of m yields an induced density bounded by L_*. With `v_3=4pi/3`, set `r=(2DL_*v_3)^(-1/3)` in (15); the squared distortion is at least `r^2/2`. Hence

```text
e_D >= c_* D^(-2/3),
c_* = (1/(36a^2)) [2L_* v_3]^(-2/3)>0.         (15a)
```

For completeness, (14) uses two bounded tests only. Uniform reconstruction TV at most e gives joint prior/output TV at most e and predictive TV at most e. Comparing `b.m(X')` with `b.m(X)` costs at most `6a^2e`; comparing `|m(X')|^2` with `|m(X)|^2` costs at most `6a^2e`. Conditional independence of b and the archive output given X gives `E[b.m(X')]=E[m(X).m(X')]`. Expanding squared distance gives the stated factor 18. The volume argument integrates the predictive density over the preimage under m of a ball and uses `omega_x<=I`, then `int Tr M=D`; it does not assume classical labels.

This posterior-map proof is reusable for non-Gaussian full-dimensional exponential families whenever the pushed-forward predictive density is bounded. It is not tied to a Gaussian integration-by-parts identity.
