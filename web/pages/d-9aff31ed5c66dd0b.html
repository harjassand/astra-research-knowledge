## Reusable obstruction, including average errors

Fix finite incidence/wiring data, r≥2, prime q>r, N labels and m points, exceptional set E. Set S_inc = sum of regular-point degrees and

\[
\Delta=Nr/4-2m-r|E|>0.
\]

Let Y_i be the r-by-r block array of D-dimensional permutation matrices X_iuv. For each triangle t let δ_t be its actual Hamming defect. Let λ_i be the average fixed-point fraction over all r²(r−1)² nondegenerate rectangles of label i. Then **every** assignment, in **every** positive dimension D, satisfies

\[
\boxed{\Delta\le \sum_t\delta_t+\frac{r^2}{8}\sum_i\lambda_i.}
\tag{1}
\]

This uses three independent facts:

- Real trace expansion gives rank_R(Y_i) ≥ r²D/[2r−1+(r−1)²λ_i].
- An integer minor with row norm ≤sqrt(r) has determinant absolute value ≤r^(d/2). If its rank modulo q is d', q^(d−d') divides that determinant. Hence rank_Fq(Y_i) ≥ half its real rank. No condition on q dividing D occurs.
- A permutation difference has rank at most its support size over every field. Each failed triangle therefore costs at most Dδ_t in the Cartesian factorization. Rank-one scalar interpolation weights then bound the aggregate rank by D(2m+r|E|+sum δ_t).

The elementary lower bound

\[
\frac{r^2}{2[2r-1+(r-1)^2\lambda]}
\ge r/4-r^2\lambda/8
\]

proves (1) without a maximum-error assumption. Its difference multiplied by the positive denominator 8[2r−1+(r−1)²λ] is

\[
2r+(3r^2-2r)\lambda+r^2(r-1)^2\lambda^2\ge0.
\]

Writing barδ for the mean over T=r²S_inc triangles, and barλ for the mean over all rectangles, yields

\[
\Delta\le T\bar\delta+(Nr^2/8)\bar\lambda.
\]

Thus a finite local-law test can choose a uniform triangle with probability T/(T+Nr²/8), otherwise a uniform rectangle, and a uniform model point. It rejects a triangle when its word moves the point, and a rectangle when its word fixes it. Its rejection probability is at least

\[
\Delta/(T+Nr^2/8)>0.
\]

Each evaluation uses at most four permutation queries. This is a useful averaged certificate, not a practical acquired tester: its test distribution and generator set still depend on the compiled astronomical presentation. Novelty of this routine averaging extension is unverified.

## Original attempt: replace random-line alteration by D(5,q)

The primary [LUW 1995 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9501231), §2, Proposition 2.1, gives a q-regular bipartite D(5,q), with q^5 vertices on each side and girth at least 10. The explicit equations, rather than merely that theorem's abstract, are decisive. A local copy is work/scouts/groups_sources/LUW1995.pdf. The older [full construction paper](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.udel.edu/dist/3/7714/files/2022/02/Fv22new.pdf) supplies the girth argument; [Gupta–Taranchuk](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.04629v2) confirms the D(5,q) specialization. I additionally reconstructed the short-cycle exclusion below.

Take P=F_q^5. Points are (t,y,z,w,v), line labels [a,b,c,d,e], with incidences

\[
b-y=at,\quad c-z=bt,\quad d-w=ay,\quad e-v=az.
\tag{2}
\]

For a fixed line, its points are

\[
(t,\ b-at,\ c-bt,\ d-ab+a^2t,\ e-ac+abt),
\tag{3}
\]

an actual affine line with direction (1,−a,−b,a²,ab). Distinct labels give distinct geometric lines. For a fixed point and first line coordinate a there is exactly one incident label.

**Explicit perfect matching:** match a point to the incident label with a=t. Conversely every label has a unique incident point with t=a. This is a bijection between all points and all line labels.

**Independent girth derivation:** in a simple cycle of 2s edges, let a_j,b_j be the successive line parameters, and let τ_j≠0 be the change in point first-coordinate along line j. Adjacent a_j are distinct, since an incident line with specified a is unique. Closure in the five coordinates gives

\[
\sum\tau_j=\sum a_j\tau_j=\sum a_j^2\tau_j
=\sum b_j\tau_j=\sum a_jb_j\tau_j=0.
\]

Two distinct affine lines cannot give a 4-cycle. For s=3, the three a_j are distinct and Vandermonde invertibility contradicts nonzero τ_j. For s=4 with three distinct a-values, group the repeated value in the first three equations; the two singleton coefficients must vanish. With four distinct a-values the last two equations force b_j=A+B a_j: their two-dimensional kernel has basis 1,a. With two a-values alternating, the same equations force equal b-values at opposite positions, again giving b_j=A+B a_j. Every adjacent-line intersection then has t=B, so both endpoints on each line coincide. Thus no 6- or 8-cycle exists, in any characteristic.

### Grid-selection lemma

Let S={0,...,floor(q/10)−1}. Put m=q^5, N=|S|^5, and choose an affine bijection U of F_q^5 uniformly. Select all marks in B=U(S^5). Assign each mark its matching line from (2), keep those N labels, and puncture each at its own mark.

At any point x, the q incident labels have q different matching marks; one is x itself. After puncturing, its retained degree is the number of marks in B among a fixed set A_x of exactly q−1 distinct points.

The affine group is 2-transitive on P. With ρ=N/m, for distinct a,a',

\[
\Pr(a\in B)=\rho,\qquad
\Pr(a,a'\in B)=\frac{N(N-1)}{m(m-1)}.
\]

Their membership covariance is −ρ(1−ρ)/(m−1). Therefore the degree d_x has

\[
\mu=(q-1)\rho,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(d_x)\le\mu.
\tag{4}
\]

This proof does not assume independent selection of labels or uniform random line directions. It applies to any regular affine-line incidence graph with a fixed perfect matching.

Let E={x:d_x<4r²}. For μ≥8r², Chebyshev and then Markov give

\[
\mathbb E|E|\le4m/\mu,\qquad
\Pr(|E|>m/(2r))\le8r/\mu.
\tag{5}
\]

All retained punctured incidences already have girth at least 10, since they form a subgraph of D(5,q). No cycle enumeration or deletion is required.

Interpolation survives the moved grid: use ordinary product Lagrange polynomials in the coordinates U^−1(X). Each f_i has total degree ≤5(|S|−1), so entries of H=f f^T have line degree ≤10(|S|−1)<q−1. The characteristic-q sum over each full affine line vanishes, while deleting its mark leaves −E_ii. This supplies exactly the rank-one certificate required by (1).

### Geometry and resulting candidate

Use the source's two Cartesian blocks at each regular point and its cyclic shifted triangle wiring, unchanged. Give every triangle angles

\[
(21\pi/40,\ 9\pi/40,\ 9\pi/40)
\]

at O,V,W. The sum is 39π/40<π. At O the simple complete-bipartite link has cycles of at least four edges, of length at least 2.1π. At V,W the wiring gives a locally injective projection to the punctured incidence graph: fixing a label determines the shifted array coordinate uniquely, and a label belongs to one block at a given point. Link cycles have at least ten edges, of length at least 2.25π. A path between distinct same-label x-directions projects to an internally reduced closed walk and contains a cycle, so every rectangle turn has link distance at least 2.25π.

The standard CAT(−1) link criterion therefore makes the universal cover CAT(−1); finite shapes and a finite base complex give a proper cocompact action. It is contractible, deck torsion is impossible, and the rectangle paths are nontrivial closed local geodesics. The internal conclusion is a torsion-free nonsofic hyperbolic group with a finite two-dimensional classifying complex. This reconstructs the algebra and geometry for the replacement rather than adopting OA252's global theorem. A strict cell-intersection convention can be handled by subdivision without changing the metric.

## Decisive failed transfers

**Normalized HS or operator norm to rank:** exp(it)I_D approaches I_D in either norm while its difference has rank D for every nonzero sufficiently small t. Consequently the triangle rank-error charge fails for general unitary microstates. Finite-field power-sum cancellation has no automatic characteristic-zero lift.

**Rectangle rank separation to block-array rank:** over C take X_uv=(a_u+b_v)I_D, with distinct positive a_u,b_v. Every rectangle ratio differs from one, because the cross-product difference is (a_u−a_u')(b_v'−b_v)≠0. Thus rank(I−rectangle)=D, yet rank((X_uv))=2D for r≥2. This kills the replacement lower-rank lemma even at full separation. It is a local counterexample, not a hyperlinear or linear-sofic model of the whole group.

**Exact linear kernel to approximate obstruction:** exact finite-dimensional representations do not control finite-relator microstates without a metric-specific stability theorem. Supremum-over-all-pairs Ulam assumptions cannot be silently substituted for a finite-presentation approximation.

No nonhyperlinearity, failure of linear soficity, property-(T) conclusion, or group-ring/surjunctivity counterexample is established here.
