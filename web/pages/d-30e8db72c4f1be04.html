DETERMINISTIC INWARD POLYTOPE DOES NOT IMPLY STOCHASTIC SAFE STATIONARITY

Status: explicit mathematical counterexample with complete proof. This is a
derived construction, not a novelty claim. The literature precedent is cited
at the end. No stochastic simulation is needed for the conclusion.

1. Exact density-dependent reaction network

For integer volume V >= 1, use species A,B and reactions

    empty -> A       propensity c V
    A -> empty       propensity d_A n_A
    A+B -> 2B        propensity k n_A n_B / V
    B -> empty       propensity d_B n_B.

All c,d_A,d_B,k are positive. A+B -> 2B converts one A to B; it does
not increase total molecule count. Distinct reactants mean there is no
falling-factorial correction. With density X^V=Z^V/V, these rates are
exactly V q_r(x), where q=(c,d_A a,k ab,d_B b). Thus this is exact
density dependence, not merely an asymptotic approximation.

The deterministic mass-action drift is

    a' = c - d_A a - k ab,
    b' = k ab - d_B b.

The stoichiometric vectors span R^2, so the example has no conservation
constraint and its safe polytope is full-dimensional in the ambient space.

2. A full-dimensional strict inward certificate

First set c=2 and d_A=d_B=k=1. The positive equilibrium is (1,1).
The Jacobian there is [[-2,-1],[1,0]], with both eigenvalues -1, so the
equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable. Define

    P = {(a,b): 7/4 <= a+b <= 9/4, 1/2 <= b <= 3/2}.

This is a bounded full-dimensional parallelogram with a >= 1/4 and b >= 1/2.
Write s=a+b. Then s'=2-s and b'=b(s-b-1). Its four outward facet normals
and maximum outward normal drifts are

    s=7/4: normal (-1,-1),   normal dot F = -1/4;
    s=9/4: normal ( 1, 1),   normal dot F = -1/4;
    b=1/2: normal ( 0,-1),   normal dot F <= -1/8;
    b=3/2: normal ( 0, 1),   normal dot F <= -3/8.

For Euclidean unit normals, the first two margins are 1/(4 sqrt(2));
the other two are 1/8 and 3/8. Hence every facet has inward margin at
least 1/8. The deterministic flow leaves P invariant and remains away
from both coordinate-zero boundaries.

3. The certificate survives independent parameter uncertainty

Keep exactly the same P and allow independently

    c in [39/20,41/20],
    d_A,d_B,k in [49/50,51/50].

On total-density facets, s'=c-d_A a-d_B b. Since a,b are positive in P,

    at s=7/4: s' >= 39/20 - (51/50)(7/4) = 33/200;
    at s=9/4: s' <= 41/20 - (49/50)(9/4) = -31/200.

On the b facets, b'=b(k a-d_B), and a=s-b. Therefore

    at b=1/2: b' >= (1/2)[(49/50)(5/4)-51/50] = 41/400;
    at b=3/2: b' <= (3/2)[(51/50)(3/4)-49/50] = -129/400.

After normalizing the total-density normals by sqrt(2), every facet
still has inward margin at least 41/400. This is a nondegenerate robust
parameter box, not a singleton-parameter artifact.

Each parameter choice in the box also has a positive asymptotically
stable equilibrium:

    a*=d_B/k,   b*=c/d_B-d_A/k.

Positivity follows from c k > d_A d_B, including at the worst corners.
At that equilibrium the Jacobian has negative trace -(d_A+k b*) and
positive determinant k^2 a* b*, so both eigenvalues have negative real
parts. The inward certificate alone is enough for the counterexample.

4. Nonexplosion

Let N=n_A+n_B. Arrivals occur at constant rate cV. Infection preserves N,
and deaths reduce it. Thus on every finite interval [0,T],

    N_t <= N_0 + J_T,

where J_T is the finite Poisson(cVT) arrival count. Conditional on this
finite bound, infection propensity is bounded by k (N_0+J_T)^2/(4V),
and all other propensities are bounded as well. No infinite number of
reaction jumps can occur in finite time. The CTMC is nonexplosive.

5. Extinction with probability one, for every finite V

At the point parameters, N is itself an immigration-death chain:

    n -> n+1 at rate 2V;   n -> n-1 at rate n.

It hits zero almost surely. Here is an explicit proof, also valid with
immigration rate C>0 and per-capita death rate d>0. Put rho=C/d and

    S_m = sum_{j=1}^m (j-1)! / rho^(j-1),   S_0=0.

For integers 0<n<K, the probability of reaching K before zero is

    P_n(T_K<T_0) = S_n/S_K.

Indeed, the harmonic recursion C(u_{n+1}-u_n)=dn(u_n-u_{n-1}) gives
successive increments proportional to (n-1)!/rho^(n-1), and the endpoint
values are u_0=0,u_K=1. In the finite set {0,...,K}, hitting either
endpoint takes finite time almost surely. Since S_K diverges,

    P_n(T_0<infinity) >= 1-S_n/S_K -> 1.

For unequal positive death rates, set d=min(d_A,d_B). Couple the actual
total population below an immigration-death chain Y with immigration
cV and per-capita death d. One construction labels every original and
arriving molecule. Give each label a common death clock of rate d and
retain in Y any label removed early by the actual excess death rates.
Infection only changes a label's type. Actual labels always form a
subset of Y's labels, so N_t <= Y_t. Y hits zero in finite time almost
surely by the argument above; at that time the actual chain has no B.

Once n_B=0, it remains zero forever: the only reaction producing B
requires a B reactant. Consequently the B-extinction time is finite
almost surely, for all positive parameter choices, including the entire
uncertainty box.

6. Unique invariant law and zero safe stationary mass

On the closed class n_B=0, A is an immigration-death chain with
immigration cV and per-capita death d_A. Its invariant distribution is

    pi^V(n_A,n_B) = 1_{n_B=0} exp(-cV/d_A) (cV/d_A)^n_A/n_A!.

Detailed balance follows from pi(n)cV=pi(n+1)d_A(n+1).

This is the unique invariant probability law of the full chain. To see
why there is no other stationary law, let mu be any invariant law. For
each finite initial state z, extinction gives P_z(n_B(t)>0)->0. By
dominated convergence and invariance,

    mu{n_B>0} = integral P_z(n_B(t)>0) mu(dz) -> 0.

Thus mu is supported on n_B=0, where the immigration-death chain has
the unique Poisson invariant law displayed above.

Because P requires b>=1/2, the density pushforward of pi^V satisfies

    pi^V{Z^V/V in P} = 0

for every finite V. As V tends to infinity, the stationary densities
converge to the boundary point (c/d_A,0), which at the point parameters
is (2,0), rather than the deterministic positive equilibrium (1,1).

This example has a stationary law and a positive recurrent boundary
class. The failure is stationary concentration in the safe positive
region, not failure of stationary-law existence.

Scope caveat: this four-reaction network is not strongly endotactic.
For w=(0,1), A+B is a w-maximal source complex and its reaction to 2B
has positive w-drift. Therefore this example directly defeats assertions
based only on density dependence, nonexplosion, a positive stable
equilibrium, and a robust full-dimensional inward polytope. If strong
endotacticity is an additional hypothesis, use the separately audited
ACKN Example 3.1 instead; see ACKN_NUMERICAL_AUDIT.txt.

7. The order-of-limits obstruction

At the point parameters and initial density converging to (1,1), the
finite-time density-dependent law of large numbers gives convergence
to the deterministic trajectory, here constant at (1,1). Hence taking
V->infinity first and then t->infinity gives delta_(1,1).

For each finite V, the chain converges instead to pi^V. One direct
proof uses N_t distributed as Binomial(N_0,e^-t) plus an independent
Poisson(2V(1-e^-t)), together with P(n_B(t)>0)->0. Thus taking
t->infinity first and then V->infinity gives delta_(2,0).

Finite-time ODE approximation and deterministic strict inward drift do
not justify interchanging these limits. Local deterministic certificates
control neither stochastic accessibility of the extinction boundary nor
the long-time closed communicating classes.

9. Supplementary minimal examples and their exact scope

(a) Finite conserved exact mass-action variant:
    A+B->2B at rate 2 n_A n_B/V, B->A at rate n_B, n_A+n_B=V.
    In density z=n_B/V, z'=z(1-2z) has stable equilibrium 1/2.
    The interval z in [1/4,3/4] is strictly inward and excludes both
    coordinate-zero endpoints, but is only relative to the conserved
    stoichiometric class. The unique invariant law is delta_(V,0).
    Finite-state absorption is immediate: at every positive state a
    sequence of at most V consecutive deaths has positive probability.

(b) One-species full-dimensional stochastic mass-action variant:
    A->2A at rate lambda n, A->empty at rate delta n,
    2A->A at rate k n(n-1)/V, lambda>delta>0.
    Limiting ODE x'=(lambda-delta)x-kx^2 has stable positive equilibrium
    and strictly inward intervals around it. The finite-V drift differs
    by kx/V, so this is asymptotic rather than exact density dependence.
    The four-reaction example above avoids this convention issue.
