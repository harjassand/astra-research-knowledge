# Observable cumulants do not inherit pure-state bounds by mixing

Status: independently derived exact counterexample to a specific mixed-state implication in a source. Not a disproof of its pure-state calculations, not a disproof of the recent transport QFI bound, and not a claim that the general cumulant nonlinearity is new.

## Source interface and verified versions

Recent starting source: Ding et al., *Transport-Noise Witnesses of Electronic Multipartite Entanglement*, [arXiv:2608.16028v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16028v1), submitted 2026-08-17. Main Sec I Eq (1) defines basis-independent fermionic k-producibility, extends it via convex mixtures, and Eq (3) measures QFI from equilibrium noise. Sec II uses 2CRDM as a benchmark. Its current-noise/QFI witness has actual convexity; the obstruction below does not invalidate it.

Prerequisite: Liu et al., *Entanglement Witness for Indistinguishable Electrons Using Solid-State Spectroscopy*, [arXiv:2408.04876v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04876v2), submitted v1 2024-08-09, v2 2025-03-25; Phys Rev X 15, 011056 (2025). PDF dated 2025-03-27. HTML rendering shows 2026-08-24 despite its v2 metadata; the preserved versioned PDF is the decisive object. Sources are preserved under sources/r12_geometry/. PDF p15 visually inspected; Eq (68) is unambiguously an ensemble average of component cumulants.

The source's measured cumulant is Eq (32), with CAR conventions:

\[
H_{ik}(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho c_i c_k^\dagger,
\quad K_{ijkl}(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho c_i c_j(c_kc_l)^\dagger-H_{ik}(\rho)H_{jl}(\rho)+H_{il}(\rho)H_{jk}(\rho).
\]

For a number-conserving Slater state K=0. Sec III.3 gives mu(1)=0 and claims mu(k)=(2k-1)/4 for k>1. Sec III.4 Eq (67) defines mixed-state k-separability as the convex hull of pure k-producible states. Eq (68) then replaces K(rho) by sum p_tau K(rho_tau), and Eq (69) uses this asserted additivity to extend the eigenvalue upper bound to mixtures.

Our question is exact: does the observable K defined in Eq (32) equal the right side of Eq (68), and therefore obey the asserted bound for all such convex mixtures?

## Exact correction to Eq (68)

Write rho=sum p_tau rho_tau, Hbar=sum p_tau Htau, and delta Htau=Htau-Hbar. The raw four-point term is affine, while the two products are quadratic. Direct expansion gives

\[
K_{ijkl}(\rho)=\sum_\tau p_\tau K_{ijkl}(\rho_\tau)
+\sum_\tau p_\tau\big[\delta H^\tau_{ik}\delta H^\tau_{jl}-\delta H^\tau_{il}\delta H^\tau_{jk}\big].
\]

The second line is a classical ensemble covariance term. It is generically nonzero, even when every component is a Slater determinant. All products here use the antinormal ordering of the paper; no exchange-sign convention has been changed.

An ensemble-averaged cumulant, defined to omit the covariance term, is a different quantity. It depends on a chosen decomposition and is not generally determined by rho or by the measured one-/two-particle RDM. In particular an unlabelled mixture experiment does not give component one-particle RDMs separately.

## Counterexample family, all particle numbers N>=2

On d=2N fermionic modes, set

\[
|A_N\rangle=c_1^\dagger\cdots c_N^\dagger|0\rangle,
\quad |B_N\rangle=c_{N+1}^\dagger\cdots c_{2N}^\dagger|0\rangle,
\quad \rho_N=\tfrac12|A_N\rangle\langle A_N|+\tfrac12|B_N\rangle\langle B_N|.
\]

Each component is a pure Slater and hence 1-producible in the source's convention. Thus rho_N is 1-separable by its Eq (67), without any need to optimize a single-particle basis.

The antinormal one-particle matrix is H=I/2. Let s_i=+1 for i<=N and -1 otherwise. For i!=j, the hole occupations have covariance

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(1-n_i,1-n_j)=\tfrac14s_is_j.
\]

There is no off-diagonal one-particle coherence. The only nonzero fourth-order entries are therefore

\[
K_{ijij}=\tfrac14s_is_j,\qquad K_{ijji}=-\tfrac14s_is_j\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Repeated equal pairs vanish by CAR. In the flattened matrix M_(ik),(jl)=K_ijkl, the subspace indexed by diagonal pairs (i,i) has the block

\[
M_D=\tfrac14(ss^T-I_{2N}).
\]

It has eigenvalue (2N-1)/4 along s and eigenvalue -1/4 on s-perp. Each remaining pair of indices (i,j),(j,i) forms a 2x2 block with eigenvalues +/-1/4. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{max}(M(\rho_N))=(2N-1)/4.}
\]

This is positive for every N>=2 although each component has K=0. It violates mu(1)=0; it even equals the source's claimed pure GHZ_N maximum mu(N). For N>=3 it exceeds mu(N-1), so the mixed-state extension would falsely infer at least N-particle entanglement from an explicitly 1-separable state. This conclusion does not rely on accepting the source's general pure-state optimization proof: the mu(1)=0 counterexample alone suffices.

## Measurement-level impossibility

Let |GHZ_N>=(|A_N>+|B_N>)/sqrt(2). For N>=3, every number-conserving operator containing at most two creation and two annihilation operators has zero matrix element between A_N and B_N: it can replace at most two occupied modes, whereas these determinants differ in N occupied modes. Thus GHZ_N and rho_N have identical one- and two-particle RDMs, including the entire K tensor.

It follows that no function of those equal-time RDMs alone can distinguish these two states under an arbitrary mixed-state interface. This is the fermionic version of the familiar GHZ lower-marginal blindness; it is not claimed as a new general fact. For N=2 the full two-particle RDM does distinguish coherence, but the source's maximum eigenvalue is still fooled by rho_2.

This does not assert equality of complete frequency-resolved spectra under a fixed Hamiltonian. Time evolution, additional observables, a purity promise, or a restricted equilibrium state class may restore distinguishability. The obstruction concerns the asserted state-functional mixed-state theorem and equal-time 2CRDM measurement interface.

## Novelty, limitations, and next step

Nearest foundations include Wick cumulants, convex-Gaussian/Slater mixture resource theories, GHZ marginal indistinguishability, and QFI convexity. The potentially useful contribution is an exact source-specific obstruction with its correct covariance term and an operational boundary for spectroscopy; there is no asserted new foundational theorem yet. Targeted searches for the arXiv identifier/title/PRX article number with erratum, correction and mixed-state terms found no fix in the retrieved results; this is not an exhaustive erratum audit. The general pure-state mu(k) optimization is also a separate open audit: the source itself notes a GHZ-maximality assumption near Eq (66).

Concrete downstream use: prevent thermal or classically fluctuating mean-field ensembles from being misclassified as intrinsic fermionic entanglement by a nonlinear cumulant diagnostic. A replacement witness must optimize over the convex hull of admissible states, use a genuinely convex measure such as QFI, or state an experimentally defensible pure-state/purity assumption.
