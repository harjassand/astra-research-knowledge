What Faster Means?
LetP⊆Qbe calculi in the diagram. Then:
Definition
We sayQis exponentially faster thanPwhen there is a sequence{S n}n∈N
of sequents such that:
(Provability) IMALL⊢S n.
(Upper bound)S n has aQ-proof of sizen O(1), and
(Lower bound) the shortestP-proof ofSn has size 2Ω(n).
Usually, the first two are easy. The hard work is to show the third part, i.e.,
the feasible unprovability, a complexity-theoretic variant of unpovability.

Feasible Extraction: Computational Data
LetPbe a proof system. To prove a lower bound forP, a natural
adaptation of the interpretation method is the following:
To any sequentS, assign a setDS of computational objects.
From anyP-proofπofS, feasibly extract a computational deviceC π
that computes an element ofDS.
Find a sequence{Sn}n∈N of short provable sequents for which the
objects inD Sn are hard to compute.
Since a short proof ofSn would yield a small computational device for an
object inD Sn, the computational hardness ofDSn implies thatS n must
have longP-proofs.
The method reduces proof complexity to computational complexity.
