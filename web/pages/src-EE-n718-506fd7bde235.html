# Intervention-product completion: acquiring hidden control information without microscopic gain calibration

## Status and exact claim

This is a research proof candidate developed in this conversation. The statements below have self-contained mathematical arguments and exact finite falsification tests. They have not received independent review, formal verification, or historical-priority clearance. No physical experiment has been conducted.

The proposed contribution is an operational combination: product controls make a hidden transition-space inner product observable; this inner product certifies synthesis of the response needed to measure a positive predictive residual; a mixed-time identity makes the construction valid away from stationarity; and the acquired residual controls finite-horizon feedback prediction. The construction allows arbitrary heterogeneous, unknown gains on individual hidden transitions. It does not assume a known geometric field law.

The following are established mathematics, not claimed inventions: generator response identities, strong lumpability, Gram matrices and Hilbert-space projection, polynomial approximation, bounded-variable concentration, convex quadratic optimization, coupling, and Markov-chain time changes. Novelty of the operational theorem and its combination of consequences remains unresolved.

Two structural access assumptions remain load-bearing: a known composition law for controls, and either unchanged within-label transitions or a counter for all microscopic jump events. Neither is implied by thermodynamic rate ratios. They are not claimed inferable from arbitrary observed data. The source-clock counterexample proves why eliminating all such access information is impossible.

All errors below concern finite horizons or a specified preparation distribution; no nonzero residual is asserted to yield a uniform infinite-horizon path approximation.

## 1. Model and target

First use a finite hidden state space with a row generator Q. The hidden size need not be known to the experimenter. Let ell(i) in {1,...,k} be the visible label, e_a its indicator, E the matrix of these indicators, and D_a=diag(e_a). A repeatable preparation produces an unknown distribution nu. For an all-hidden-start exact statement assume nu_i>0. For approximate prediction the appropriate condition is domination of the deployed distributions by nu, stated below. No stationarity of nu is assumed.

Write R=diag(nu), mu=nu E, M=diag(mu), and assume mu_a>=rho>0. Put

    A = E^T R Q E,
    L = M^{-1} A,
    B = Q E - E L,
    Gamma_nu(Q) = B^T R B.

L is a valid visible generator. Gamma is positive semidefinite and measures variation, within a visible label, of aggregate microscopic transition rates. Assume a supplied bound Lambda on all baseline microscopic exit rates. Then ||Q||_infty<=2 Lambda and ||Qe_a||_infty<=Lambda.

Gamma=0 is equivalent to strong lumpability when nu has full support. It is not equivalent to Markovianity of one stationary observed record, and is not equivalent to equilibrium or zero dissipation.

Define a *mathematical target*, not an assumed available field:

    V_a = (Q D_a - D_a Q - diag(Qe_a))/2.

Off the diagonal,

    (V_a)_ij = q_ij v_a(i,j),
    v_a(i,j) = (e_a(j)-e_a(i))/2.

V_a has zero row sums and zero within-label off-diagonal entries, but may have signed off-diagonal entries. It will be synthesized only in data analysis. No signed-rate physical generator is requested.

## 2. The weaker intervention interface

Available reproducible settings u=0,...,p-1 have generators K_u with

    (K_u)_ij = q_ij g_u(i,j),  i!=j,
    g_0=1,

and zero row sums. All g_u are nonnegative and unknown, and may vary arbitrarily between hidden transitions with the same visible source and destination.

For each selected pair u,v the experimenter can apply a *product setting* K_{u odot v} with

    (K_{u odot v})_ij = q_ij g_u(i,j) g_v(i,j),  i!=j.

This is a law of the actuator, not a temporal sequence of pulses. Running K_u and then K_v does not implement K_{u odot v}. A log-linear model q_ij(h)=q_ij exp(theta_ij dot h), with unknown theta_ij, has this product setting at parameter h_u+h_v. The theorem does not assume that every physical actuator is log-linear.

Two versions of event access are allowed.

**S: selective access.** Every g_u(i,j)=1 when ell(i)=ell(j). No microscopic event counter is needed. Syntheses must satisfy sum_u w_u=0, so their within-label contributions cancel exactly.

**A: activity-complete access.** A detector reports whether at least one microscopic jump occurred in a short interval; microscopic source/destination identities are not needed. The g_u may also change within-label transitions. There is no zero-sum constraint on w.

A counter must count actual events of the modeled process, not merely visible label changes. It is an additional sensor, not a free consequence of label observations.

All used generators, including product settings, must have supplied finite exit-rate bounds. A large product rate is charged in time resolution and sampling costs.

## 3. The hidden transition-space norm is observable

Define directed baseline flux weights

    omega(i,j)=nu_i q_ij,  i!=j.

In version S restrict omega to cross-label transitions. In version A retain all transitions. In either case its total mass is at most Lambda.

For each setting let

    A^u_cd = derivative at t=0 of E_nu[e_c(Y_0)e_d(Y_t)] under K_u.

For c!=d,

    A^u_cd = sum_{i in c, j in d} omega(i,j) g_u(i,j).

These are observed short-time label statistics, with no hidden-state conditioning.

Define H, b_a and c_a by

    H_uv = sum_{c!=d} A^{u odot v}_cd,                         (S)
    H_uv = initial total microscopic-jump intensity under K_{u odot v}, (A)
    (b_a)_u = sum_{c!=d} [(1_{d=a}-1_{c=a})/2] A^u_cd,
    c_a = sum_{c!=d} [(1_{d=a}-1_{c=a})^2/4] A^0_cd.

Equivalently, b_a is one half of the initial derivative of the unconditioned probability of label a, and c_a is one quarter of the initial boundary-crossing activity for label a.

For a real coefficient vector w define

    E_a(w)=w^T H w-2 b_a^T w+c_a.                           (3.1)

### Theorem 1: observable positive synthesis certificate

For every w (with sum w=0 in version S),

    E_a(w)=sum_{i!=j} nu_i q_ij
             [sum_u w_u g_u(i,j)-v_a(i,j)]^2 >=0.           (3.2)

In S the omitted internal terms are exactly zero. In A every term is measured through the activity matrix. In particular H is positive semidefinite.

**Proof.** Expand the square. Product interventions supply exactly the quadratic cross terms; ordinary interventions supply the linear terms; the baseline supplies the constant. In S, internal gains are all one and the target is zero there, so the zero-sum constraint removes the unobserved terms. This proves the identity. No assumption of independent effects across hidden edges is made.

Thus product settings provide inner products of unknown intervention gains. This replaces microscopic gain calibration with an experimentally accessible positive quadratic form.

### Consequence for response synthesis

Put W_a=sum_u w_u K_u. Then

    |nu (W_a-V_a) Qe_b| <= 2 Lambda^(3/2) sqrt(E_a(w)).      (3.3)

Indeed, for any bounded g,

    nu(W_a-V_a)g = sum omega(i,j) delta(i,j)[g(j)-g(i)],

where delta=sum w g_u-v_a. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds this by sqrt(E_a) times the square root of sum omega(g(j)-g(i))^2. For g=Qe_b, |g|<=Lambda and total omega<=Lambda, giving (3.3).

A small fitted norm is therefore not merely an encoding or rank condition: it bounds the actual hidden operator error needed in the next theorem.

## 4. A nonstationary response-completion identity

Define observable derivatives for the target generator Q:

    C_ab(t)=E_nu[e_a(Y_0)e_b(Y_t)],
    A_ab=C'_ab(0),
    C2_ab=C''_ab(0),
    T_ab=partial_s partial_t E_nu[e_a(Y_s)e_b(Y_{s+t})]|_(0,0).

For each physical setting u, run K_u for time s and then Q for time t, and set

    Z_ub=partial_s partial_t E_nu[e_b(Y_{s+t})]|_(0,0).

The corresponding operator expressions are

    T_ab=nu Q D_a Qe_b,
    C2_ab=nu D_a Q^2 e_b,
    Z_ub=nu K_u Qe_b.

### Theorem 2: acquisition of the predictive Gram matrix

The exact identity is

    <Qe_a,Qe_b>_nu
       = T_ab-C2_ab-2 nu V_a Qe_b.                         (4.1)

Consequently the experimentally synthesized matrix

    Gtilde_ab = T_ab-C2_ab-2 sum_u w_au Z_ub,
    Gammatilde = Gtilde-A^T M^{-1} A

satisfies

    ||sym(Gammatilde)-Gamma_nu(Q)||_op
       <= 4 k Lambda^(3/2) max_a sqrt(E_a(w_a)).           (4.2)

**Proof.** Rearrange the definition of V_a:

    2 V_a Qe_b = Q D_a Qe_b-D_a Q^2e_b-(Qe_a)(Qe_b).

Integrate against nu. This proves (4.1) without an invariant measure. The Gram matrix of the conditional averages is A^T M^{-1}A. Equation (3.3), the factor two in (4.1), and the bound ||X||_op<=k max_ab |X_ab| prove (4.2). Symmetrizing does not increase distance to the true symmetric Gamma.

The term T is essential. It vanishes when nu Q=0, but generally does not vanish under a reproducible nonstationary preparation. The exact verifier includes an example where omitting it produces a nonsymmetric, incorrect matrix.

This theorem does not assume that the geometric perturbation is ever physically implemented. Only the actual settings K_u and their products are used.

## 5. Observable control sufficiency, including statistical conditioning

For a coefficient budget W>=1 define

    I_a(W)=min E_a(w), subject to ||w||_1<=W,

with the zero-sum constraint in version S. This is a finite convex quadratic program in p coefficients. Its dimension depends on the number of selected controls, not on hidden-state count.

Both I_a(W) and a minimizing combination can be acquired from observed moments. A small value gives (4.2). A large value means this chosen control span and budget do not synthesize the desired completion accurately. It does NOT prove that no other experiment or nonlinear identification method could succeed.

Exact identifiability and a usable finite budget are distinct. Nearly dependent gains can produce very large coefficients and large sample cost. The l1 budget makes this conditioning an explicit resource.

### Noisy positive certificate

Suppose all entries of Hhat-H, bhat-b and chat-c have errors at most tau. Symmetrize Hhat and put

    Hplus = sym(Hhat)+p tau I.

On the confidence event Hplus-H is positive semidefinite. For any ||w||_1<=W,

    E_a(w) <= U_a(w)
       := w^T Hplus w-2 bhat_a^T w+chat_a+2tau W+tau.       (5.1)

Minimize this convex upper certificate on the budgeted feasible set. If Hplus is not positive semidefinite in an actual execution, return inconclusive; on the asserted confidence event this cannot happen. An optimization tolerance is added to the returned bound.

For comparison, for any feasible w,

    U_a(w)-E_a(w)
       <= (p+1)tau W^2+4tau W+2tau.                       (5.2)

No inversion of a nearly singular moment matrix is required for a valid upper certificate. No exact zero is inferred from rounding. A significantly negative empirical norm or matrix eigenvalue is a model/noise inconsistency, not evidence for autonomy.

## 6. Exact characterization for a multiplicative control family

This section characterizes the completion operation, not all conceivable identification methods.

Suppose basic unknown gain signatures are Z(e)=(z_1(e),...,z_d(e)) in a compact subset K of (0,infinity)^d. The experimenter can apply every finite nonnegative integer monomial product. Thus finite data combinations produce polynomials p(Z). In version S, Z=1 on internal transitions and p(1)=0 is imposed.

Let the finite flux measure omega on the relevant edge set have positive total mass m. Normalize it to a probability measure, let T_a(e)=v_a(e), and write conditional moments of T_a given Z under that probability measure.

### Theorem 3: overlap is the exact completion obstruction

For activity-complete access,

    inf_polynomials E_a(p) = m E[Var(T_a | Z)].             (6.1)

For selective access,

    inf_{p(1)=0} E_a(p)
       = m E[Var(T_a | Z) 1_{Z!=1}]
         + m E[T_a^2 1_{Z=1}].                            (6.2)

**Proof.** Conditional expectation is the orthogonal projection onto functions of Z. Polynomials are dense in L2 of any finite Borel measure on a compact subset of Euclidean space, by Stone-Weierstrass and density of continuous functions. Under p(1)=0 their closure is the subspace of functions of Z vanishing at the atom Z=1, if that atom has mass; if it has no mass the restriction does not change the L2 closure. To see the latter statement, first cut off a continuous function in a small neighborhood of 1, whose measure tends to zero, then approximate it uniformly by a polynomial and subtract the polynomial's value at 1. Orthogonal projection gives the displayed errors.

For all labels simultaneously, version S can have zero infimum precisely when the gain signature determines the ordered visible cross-transition, up to flux-null sets, and no cross-transition has signature 1. Version A requires only that the signature determine the target vector (e_destination-e_source)/2; all internal transitions share target zero.

Unknown heterogeneous gains are allowed. Disjoint gain distributions need not be known in advance: finite product moments directly evaluate any proposed polynomial residual. Finite-data upper certificates exist without proving or estimating the entire support.

If two positive-flux transition classes with different targets have exactly the same gain signature, their conditional variance gives a strictly positive irreducible completion error. No increase in polynomial degree removes that collision.

### An explicit finite guarantee for separated gain bands

For one target a, suppose a scalar gain z lies in [1/2,2] and three ordered closed bands S_-, S_0, S_+ separated by gaps at least g>0 carry respectively targets -1/2,0,+1/2. Let 1 belong to S_0. These assumptions can be a sufficient physical class, but are not needed for the a posteriori certificate.

Let r_-,r_+ be the midpoints of the gaps and set

    y_-(z)=(z-r_-)/2,   y_+(z)=(z-r_+)/2.

On the allowed bands, each relevant y has magnitude at least a=g/4 and lies in [-1,1]. Define

    P_m(x)=x sum_{j=0}^m [binom(2j,j)/4^j] (1-x^2)^j.

For a<=|x|<=1,

    |P_m(x)-sign(x)| <= a^(-2)(1-a^2)^(m+1).

This follows by truncating (1-z)^(-1/2)=sum binom(2j,j)z^j/4^j and bounding the tail geometrically.

Put

    p(z)=[P_m(y_-(z))+P_m(y_+(z))]/4
          -[P_m(y_-(1))+P_m(y_+(1))]/4.

Then p(1)=0, and if the displayed sign-approximation error is at most delta, |p(z)-T_a|<=delta on every allowed band. Degree D=2m+1 with

    D=O(g^(-2) log(1/(g delta)))

suffices. Its coefficient l1 norm is bounded by 5^(m+1): ||(z-r)/2||_coefficient,1<=2, ||1-((z-r)/2)^2||_1<=5, and the geometric sum plus the constant subtraction give this bound.

Therefore E_a(p)<=Lambda delta^2. Moments require powers through 2D, whose maximum exit rate is at most Lambda 2^(2D).

For FIXED band separation, coefficient size, product-rate bound, sample cost and time resolution are polynomial in delta^(-1). The exponents depend on the band gap. This is not a joint fully polynomial guarantee in g^(-1), and the worst-case constants can be impractical. The data-fitted quadratic program may find a far shorter filter, as in the exact example below. No optimum-rate claim is made.

## 7. Finite samples, physical time and precision

The population derivatives in the theorem are replaced by finite differences; there is no derivative oracle.

Normalize time using a supplied common maximum exit rate Rmax for all target, primitive and product settings used. Thus every normalized generator has row-operator norm at most two. Restore Gamma units by multiplying by Rmax^2.

Assume corrected visible indicators phi_a(O) obey E[phi_a(O)|Y=b]=1_{a=b}, |phi_a|<=kappa. This can be obtained from a known full-rank sensor confusion matrix. Noise on distinct readouts is conditionally independent. Repeated-label products at the same time are replaced by the corresponding single indicator, rather than assuming two independent copies of one corrupted reading. A total-activity bit, if used, must have an analogous calibrated readout. Unknown or singular sensor channels are not covered.

For a normalized operator error eta in (0,1], a coefficient budget W, and failure probability zeta, a conservative common scalar tolerance is

    tau = eta^2 rho^2 / [10^6 (p+1) k^4 (1+W)^2],
    h = tau/(100k),
    beta = tau h^2/(100k).

Estimate the primitive raw means to beta using independent preparation trials. Observables use at most two label indicators, or one activity bit. There are O(p^2 k^2) conservative scalar settings for one target generator, with additional polynomial factors for a finite library of targets.

Use

    first derivative: [f(h)-f(0)]/h,
    second derivative: [f(2h)-2f(h)+f(0)]/h^2,
    mixed derivative: [F(h,h)-F(h,0)-F(0,h)+F(0,0)]/h^2.

Bounded generators give bias at most 2h for the first derivative and 8h for the second or mixed derivative of a single indicator/product statistic. The mixed bounds use partial derivatives of total order three. For aggregated first-order moments use the bounded events of any visible change or of crossing a specified label boundary; their bias bounds do not grow as k^2. Reconstructing them from corrected label-pair means multiplies raw statistical error by at most k^2, which the stated beta covers. An activity bit Pr(N_h>=1) has the same or a smaller first-derivative bound. Raw mean errors are amplified by at most 4/h^2 in second/mixed differences.

A simple Hoeffding bound therefore gives

    N_total = O(p^2 k^2 kappa^4 beta^(-2)
                 log(p k/(zeta beta))).                   (7.1)

This is polynomial in the displayed parameters. Its accuracy dependence is deliberately conservative (roughly eta^(-12), with other polynomial factors). It is not a claim of optimized, practically modest, or minimax sample complexity. No hidden-state count or minimum microscopic preparation mass enters the statistical budget.

On the simultaneous confidence event, (5.1) applies to coefficients chosen using the same data: the error bounds are uniform on the l1 ball. If each returned U_a is at most eta^2/(256k^2), the synthesis contribution to operator error is at most eta/4. The residual projection and mixed-jet errors fit within the remaining eta budget with the stated tau. More generally report the actual a posteriori bound from (4.2) plus the propagated statistical errors instead of issuing a binary claim.

A sufficient condition for this success is that, for every target a, a coefficient vector of norm at most W has true E_a<=eta^2/(1024k^2), allowing a small charged optimization tolerance. Equation (5.2) supplies the margin. If the controls or budget are insufficient, the procedure returns an upper bound too large to certify the desired accuracy; it does not manufacture a result.

A finite number of samples tests separated hypotheses, for example Gamma=0 versus tr Gamma>=g. It cannot establish exact equality from a numerical zero.

### Preparation and time

The preparation nu need not be stationary but it must be repeatable. Its preparation time Tprep is charged once per trial. If instead stationary starts are acquired by a supplied mixing inequality M0 exp(-gamma t), waiting gamma^(-1) log(4 M0 N_total/zeta) before each trial couples the experiment to independent stationary starts with total error at most zeta/4. No mixing bound is assumed inferable from a finite visible record.

If the actual starting law in each raw experiment is within alpha in total variation of nu, its bias is included in the raw-mean error budget. A safe requirement is alpha<=beta/(4 kappa^2). This is more demanding as time differences shrink; it is not omitted.

All pulse durations are at most 2h/Rmax in the simple scheme. Timing errors of order beta/Rmax, and corresponding bounded-generator switching/setting errors, fit the raw-mean budget. The binary precision requirement is logarithmic in the reciprocals of these tolerances. Large Rmax or W increases those requirements explicitly.

The robust quadratic programs have dimension p, plus standard variables for the l1 constraint, and can be solved to charged additive accuracy with polynomial-time convex optimization. The exact verifier below solves rational normal equations for selected examples; it is not an implementation of the full statistical or budgeted experimental procedure.

## 8. Approximate composition and unobserved internal effects

The basic primitive controls can be heterogeneous without error. What matters is a controlled error in the product law.

Suppose the measured product setting differs from q_ij g_u g_v by a signed off-diagonal perturbation with nu-weighted absolute flux at most xi, uniformly over the used pairs. Then each true/observed H entry differs by at most xi. Add

    xi ||w||_1^2

to the squared synthesis-error certificate. Primitive and baseline moments are measured for their actual settings. An analogous bound for nonzero b/c model errors is added linearly. This avoids assuming unknown gains are individually close to the geometric ones.

In selective mode, suppose internal neutrality is approximate, with

    sum_{internal i!=j} nu_i q_ij (g_u(i,j)-1)^2 <= eps_int^2

for each primitive control. For sum w=0, Minkowski gives an unobserved internal residual at most W^2 eps_int^2. Add it to the squared certificate. It must be physically bounded or measured using activity-complete access. It cannot simply be ignored.

These bounds show robustness to a meaningful class of composition errors and internal nuisance errors. They also identify the structural information that observed endpoint data alone do not certify.

## 9. From acquired residuals to future controlled prediction

Let a finite library of actual deployment actions have arbitrary generators Q^j, j=1,...,s. They need not be known label multipliers of one baseline. Apply the preceding acquisition to each Q^j as target, using product probes relative to it and a common preparation nu. Define L^j and Gamma^j with that nu.

If all Gamma^j=0 and nu has full support, every Q^j is strongly lumpable. Therefore any well-posed predictable policy selecting these actions based on visible history has exactly the visible intensities L^{j_t}_{Y_t,b}. Hidden reconstruction is unnecessary. Convex randomized mixtures of the tested actions are also covered. Unprobed actions are not covered automatically.

For approximate prediction, assume every actual controlled microscopic marginal through time T is bounded by C nu. Put d_j=tr Gamma^j. Then

    TV(P_true^{policy,[0,T]}, P_reduced^{policy,[0,T]})
       <= C T sqrt((k-1) sum_j d_j).                       (9.1)

The right side can of course be clipped at one. It concerns the whole continuous observed path, including jump times, uniformly over the policy class with that domination bound. No positive visible-rate floor is needed.

**Proof.** For each action and hidden state, retain the within-label transitions and rescale the rates toward each other label so their total equals L^j_ab. When the true total is zero but L^j_ab positive, distribute the comparison rate within the destination label arbitrarily. The resulting comparison generator is strongly lumpable. Couple the two hidden chains until the first discrepant jump. At a shared state i, action j, the mismatch intensity is at most sum_{b!=ell(i)} |r^j_ib-L^j_ell(i),b|. Bound the maximum over actions by the square root of (k-1) times the sum of squared residuals over actions and destinations; integrate using domination by C nu and Cauchy-Schwarz. The comparison visible law is precisely the proposed reduced feedback model. This proves (9.1).

For one finite-dimensional sufficient condition, suppose

    lambda nu_l <= q^j_il <= Lambda nu_l, i!=l,

uniformly over deployed actions. If the deployment start satisfies rho0<=D0 nu, then all policies have C=max(D0,Lambda/lambda). The proof is the scalar inequality p_l'<=Lambda nu_l-lambda p_l. This is a real coverage/mixing assumption and is not inferred from a small Gamma.

Estimated reduced rates with maximum row off-diagonal l1 error delta_L add T delta_L; observed initial-law error adds its total variation. If the residual estimate has operator error eta_j, use max(0,tr Gammahat^j+k eta_j) as an upper bound for d_j. Thus the final prediction bound remains data-certified.

For any common observable path reward in [0,1], let epsilon be the resulting uniform path-error bound. The optimal values over all admissible visible-history policies differ by at most epsilon. A policy within beta of optimal in the reduced model has regret at most 2epsilon+beta in the hidden model. This follows by applying the uniform expectation bound to the two policies and taking suprema. The computation of a reduced-model policy is a separate charged task; no efficient solver for arbitrary path-dependent objectives is asserted. For conventional finite-state, finite-action Markov rewards, this provides the usual small-state control problem with an experimentally acquired performance certificate, not a general solution of arbitrary partially observed control.

For a standard Borel hidden space with bounded pure-jump kernels, all preceding algebra and statistical acquisition carry over by replacing sums by integrals. The conclusion Gamma=0 is nu-almost-everywhere closure, not a statement about nu-null preparations. The path theorem remains valid for deployed marginals dominated by nu; the required comparison kernels are obtained by normalizing each destination-label kernel, with arbitrary fixed fallback distributions where needed. Diffusions with unbounded generators and rate explosions are not covered by this extension.

## 10. Exact evidence and counterexamples

### Heterogeneous, nonstationary recovery

Use the rational four-state generator

    Q=(1/16)[[-11,3,5,3], [3,-11,3,5],
              [3,5,-13,5], [5,3,5,-13]],
    labels=(0,1,0,1),
    nu=(1,2,3,4)/10.

The preparation is not stationary. One physical actuator has multiplier matrix

    z=[[1,1/2,1,2/3], [3/2,1,2,1],
       [1,2/3,1,1/2], [2,1,3/2,1]].

It is not a known geometric field and its gains differ within each visible direction. Apply settings whose rates are q_ij z_ij^j. Using only the moment matrices for powers through 8, the exact constrained normal equations recover

    p(z)=14/5-(175/12)z+(85/4)z^2-(35/3)z^3+(11/5)z^4.

The sum of coefficients is zero and their absolute sum is 105/2. The measured quadratic synthesis residual is exactly zero. The other label uses -p. The mixed-pulse formula recovers

    Gamma=(5/384)[[1,-1],[-1,1]].

The verifier independently checks the actual hidden operator equality, which is NOT input to the fitting calculation. This is exact algebra on an explicit model, not a physical data claim or a finite-data assertion of exact equality.

Omitting the nonstationary correction instead gives

    [[73/1920,-73/1920], [119/1920,-119/1920]],

which is wrong and nonsymmetric.

### Gain collision

If every cross-label edge in this example has the same scalar gain 2, all polynomial control combinations are constant on cross edges. The best synthesis residual for label 0 is exactly 117/880, equal to the flux-weighted conditional variance from (6.2). Increasing degree cannot remove this collision.

### Source clocks: product composition is not enough

For the same Q with uniform preparation, take gain 2 on every rate leaving label 0 and 1/2 on every rate leaving label 1, INCLUDING internal transitions. Product settings obey the multiplication law.

If internal neutrality is falsely assumed, cross-only moments give zero fitted synthesis error and the apparent Gamma is zero. The actual Gamma is

    (1/64)[[1,-1],[-1,1]].

An activity-complete calibration instead gives a strictly positive minimum residual, exactly 1/24 in this fixture. It does not issue the false zero certificate.

More generally, source-clock controls q_ij^u(t)=c_{ell(i)}(t,visible history)q_ij transform an unforced observed path by an observable time change. They cannot distinguish two models with exactly the same passive observed law, even adaptively and even though their rate ratios change according to the same prescribed conservative potential difference as a geometric field. This is the previous source-clock obstruction, preserved here. The observed path is formed by solving tau'(t)=c_{Y_tau(t)} and applying the common time-change map; total variation contracts under this map. No finite or infinite observed-only dataset can universally decide the hidden closure property over a class containing such matched models.

Thus the theorem does not magically derive a microscopic exclusion restriction from visible data. It identifies an operational substitute for detailed calibration and an exact reason why completely unrestricted substitution is impossible.
