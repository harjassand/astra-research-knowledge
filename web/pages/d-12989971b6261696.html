# Cycle3: target-only calibration, approximate conversion, and the cost of adaptive reuse

2026-10-06. Complete elementary upper/lower propositions and executed fixture. No novelty claim. This route reduces the full-matrix requirement, then identifies which fresh-law guarantee genuinely costs calibration.

## Supplied structure and acquired information

Source: unknown symmetric binary channel F_1=Bern(p), F_0=Bern(1-p), with supplied promise p>=3/4. Target: G_1=Bern(q), G_0=Bern(1-q), unknown q in[1/4,3/4], independent of p. Symmetry, state labels, the source gap, the target parameter range and the iid channel template are supplied. The learner has labelled target-row1 calibration samples, then only source observations from an unknown deployment state theta. Deployment source observations contain no information about q beyond what is retained from calibration.

The target is every report of a fresh n-observation target trajectory, including a report chosen after inspecting retained calibration data D. Precisely, with probability at least1-beta over D, the conditional generated law Q_D^theta must satisfy TV(Q_D^theta,G_theta^n)<=epsilon for both states. This is a **conditional fresh-law contract**, stronger than one predeclared report averaged over calibration randomness. It is useful for adaptive downstream report selection; it is not automatically the appropriate contract for every application.

All q-dependent retained calibration, including raw samples, is part of D. The remaining randomness comes from fresh source requests and auxiliary coins. A private reserve whose contents are excluded from the information given to downstream report selection is a different interface; it can act as a finite stream of unexposed fresh target samples and must be charged by its size. Both comparator and proposed method may retain all allowed data and use the same source/target access.

## Upper bound: learn only q, classify source orientation, simulate internally

From m labelled target-row1 samples let qhat be their mean clipped to[1/4,3/4]. For confidence1-beta, Hoeffding gives

|qhat-q|<=r=sqrt(log(2/beta)/(2m)).

Use an odd k fresh source samples and majority to guess theta. The source gap promise gives error eta<=exp(-k/8), uniformly over unknown p>=3/4. Thus k>=8log(1/eta), rounded up to an odd integer, is sufficient. This does not learn a full source matrix or demand precise p calibration.

Generate n independent auxiliary bits from Bern(qhat) if the guess is1 and Bern(1-qhat) if it is0, then run the requested report program. On a correct orientation, the whole generated trajectory differs from the true target by at most

TV(Bern(qhat)^n,Bern(q)^n)
 <= sqrt[n kl(qhat||q)/2]
 <= |qhat-q| sqrt[n/(2q(1-q))]
 <= r sqrt(8n/3).

The first inequality is product KL plus Pinsker. The second follows from kl(a||b)<=chi-square(Bern(a)||Bern(b))=(a-b)^2/[b(1-b)], using log x<=x-1. The last uses q in[1/4,3/4]. A wrong orientation contributes at most eta in TV, so every report chosen after D has error at most

eta + sqrt[4n log(2/beta)/(3m)].

In particular, choosing model-law budget e and orientation budget eta yields

m>=4n log(2/beta)/(3e^2), k>=8log(1/eta), total epsilon=e+eta.

Calibration therefore scales as n/e^2 rather than the n^2/e^2 obtained by adding one-step TV errors. There is no full source calibration; physical source requests per deployment are O(log(1/eta)), independent of n, while auxiliary generation/report computation takes O(n). The retained fitted parameter requires O(log m) bits. The compiler uses ordinary estimation, testing and sampling; a competent baseline can run the same algorithm.

This also explains why Li–Liu's exact pairwise-KL conversion rate cannot be transferred directly to fixed additive-TV approximation: an epsilon state-classification mistake is allowed here, whereas exact reproduction must preserve exponentially rare terminal probabilities. Once the state is approximately identified and the target model is known, arbitrarily long target trajectories can be simulated internally. The target-law calibration burden is the remaining resource bottleneck in this independent-q class.

## Lower bound for every conditional generator, not just a plug-in

The following theorem allows arbitrary retaining state, computation and randomization; source observations may be arbitrarily numerous, since they are q-independent. Calibration may stop adaptively, with an integrable number M of labelled q-row or reflected(1-q)-row observations. No target observations are available after calibration.

**Theorem.** Let n=2l+1>=3 be odd, epsilon<=1/16, beta<1/2. Any generator satisfying the conditional fresh-law contract uniformly over the target class has, for at least one q,

E_q M >= n kl(1-beta||beta)/(512 epsilon^2).

Thus the upper and lower bounds match in their n, epsilon and high-confidence orders. Constants are conservative; this is not an exact minimax constant.

Proof. Set d=4sqrt2 epsilon/sqrtn and q_+=1/2+d, q_-=1/2-d. These lie in[1/4,3/4]. Let A be the trajectory event with more ones than zeros. The binomial majority probability f(q) has derivative

f'(q)=n binom(2l,l) q^l(1-q)^l.

This follows by differentiating its binomial sum and cancelling consecutive terms. The central coefficient obeys binom(2l,l)/4^l>=1/(2sqrtl): equality at l=1, and the inductive ratio (2l+1)/(2l+2)>=sqrt(l/(l+1)) follows by squaring, with a positive remainder1.

For q between q_- and q_+, q(1-q)>=1/4-d^2. Also d^2=32epsilon^2/n<=1/(8n), so (1-4d^2)^l>=1-4ld^2>=3/4 by Bernoulli's inequality. Integrating f' across the interval gives

TV(G_1(q_+)^n,G_1(q_-)^n)
 >= f(q_+)-f(q_-)
 >= (3/4)d n/sqrtl
 >= d sqrt(n/2)=4epsilon.

Consequently the epsilon-TV balls around the two target laws are disjoint. From calibration D, define a theoretical test accepting q_+ when Q_D^1 lies within epsilon of its target law. The conditional guarantee implies acceptance probability at least1-beta under q_+ and at most beta under q_-. This test may be computationally expensive; it is used only for an information lower bound.

By data processing, the calibration transcript KL is at least kl(1-beta||beta). Each labelled target request contributes the same directed KL

kl(q_+||q_-)=2d log[(1/2+d)/(1/2-d)]<=16d^2.

The coarse bound follows from log(1+x)<=x and d<=1/4. Reflected rows have the same KL; fresh source observations and state-independent coins contribute zero conditional KL. Stopped KL accounting therefore gives transcript KL<=16d^2 E_(q_+) M. Combining yields

E_(q_+) M>=kl(1-beta||beta)/(16d^2)
 =n kl(1-beta||beta)/(512epsilon^2).

QED. A fixed maximum m is a special case. This is a lower bound on **all conditional generative procedures** under the declared information interface. If q is a known function of p, or an ongoing q-coin oracle exists, source/factory information can invalidate the q-independent premise; see cycle2's Bernoulli-factory exception.

The obstruction also holds inside an informative target interval around q=.8; it is not an artifact of near-uninformative q=.5. check_causal independently derived this extension: take n-1 divisible by5, the integer mode l=.8(n-1), and d=8epsilon/sqrtn with epsilon<=1/80. Chebyshev gives mode probability at least1/[4sqrt(n-1)]. On |q-.8|<=d<=.1/sqrtn, its log-density has zero derivative at.8 and second derivative at least-100(n-1), so its value stays at least exp(-1/2) times the central value. Integrating the corresponding binomial-tail derivative gives target-law TV at least4exp(-1/2)epsilon>2epsilon. One-sample calibration KL is at most25d^2, yielding E M>=n kl(1-beta||beta)/(1600epsilon^2). For a target interval[.7,.9], the preceding upper proof uses q(1-q)>=.09 instead of3/16. See `notes/check_causal/cycle3/acquisition_cost.md` for the independently reconstructed interior calculation.

## The stronger retaining baseline and the contract boundary

For an unconditional predeclared report, retain n actual target-row1 bits during calibration. Classify theta from source observations and output those bits or their reflection. Because the classification stream is independent of the retained bits, the marginal trajectory law is within eta of the true target. Its cost is n target calibration requests plus k source requests, with no q estimation. This is a valid, stronger baseline for that weaker objective.

It does not satisfy the conditional fresh-law contract. For a retained word D, the output is D deterministically on a correct orientation; conditional target-law TV is at least1-max(q,1-q)^n. A report selected after seeing D can simply ask whether the output equals D. Cached output says yes almost surely; a fresh target trajectory says yes with probability q^(number ones)(1-q)^(number zeros), at most(3/4)^n.

Reusing the same word for two predeclared trajectory requests is also detectable without seeing D: their equality has probability1 for cache reuse, versus (q^2+(1-q)^2)^n for independent fresh trajectories. Disjoint unused cache blocks remain a legitimate finite resource: R trajectory draws require Rn retained target samples. A private unexposed reserve can preserve freshness relative to the exposed transcript for that finite supply. These alternatives are retained, not prohibited by an artificial baseline restriction.

For the fitted generator, a conditional whole-session guarantee over R trajectories instead has total horizon Rn, so calibration grows as Rn/epsilon^2. A finite calibration batch cannot guarantee a fixed additive error for arbitrarily long independent-fresh transcripts in the independent-q class. Per-report guarantees and a joint session guarantee are distinct.

## A report quotient that avoids fine q precision

There is a positive alternative if the admitted target class consists of reports that factor through the majority bit of n iid target observations, with n>=1024 chosen before those observations (possibly after calibration). Any postprocessing of that bit is allowed. The target parameter range can now be any full-support interval containing q=.8. Source p remains unknown with the same p>=3/4 promise.

Use231 labelled target samples and accept when qhat>=.7. The one-sided confidence bound q>=qhat-.1 has failure probability at most exp(-2*231*.1^2)<.01. Thus the probability of accepting an invalid q>=.6 certificate is below.01. This is an unconditional bad-acceptance guarantee; it is not claimed to be.99 coverage conditional on acceptance for every q. If true q=.8, acceptance probability is at least1-exp(-4.62)>.99; elsewhere the mechanism may return UNKNOWN.

On a valid acceptance, every admitted target majority bit has statewise error at most exp(-2*1024*.1^2)=exp(-20.48). A source majority using31 fresh observations has statewise error at most exp(-31/8). Both binary laws are close to the same invariant point mass at theta, so triangle inequality gives report TV at most

exp(-31/8)+exp(-20.48)<.020755.

This is uniform over all admitted n and all postprocessing/report choices after calibration, because q>=.6 is a single shared certificate and the target majority error bound only improves with n. It costs231 target calibration plus31 source deployment requests, total262 for one accepted report, and O(31) generation work; no n-bit auxiliary transcript is generated. Calibration can be retained for further reports in this fixed law class. A joint R-report bound may still accumulate R times the report error.

The mechanism acquires only a coarse lower bound on q, then uses a target-specific approximate quotient that removes the noise parameter. It preserves reports of the majority bit, not the entire target transcript or its calibration-sensitive fluctuations. A same-information retaining baseline performs exactly the same source majority; there is no architecture advantage. Actual preparation and per-request costs must be added before a physical comparison. check_causal's complementary version assumes known p=.9 and uses12 source requests, for243 total; our31-request version uses only the coarser supplied source-gap promise.

## Executed calculation and cost ledger

`work/r08_information/cycle3/target_only_calibration.py` uses n=1025, beta=.05, e=.05, eta=.01. It charges **2,016,588 target calibration requests**, zero full-source matrix calibration requests, and37 source requests per deployment. Each deployment also makes1025 auxiliary target-bit draws and runs the report program. In the synthetic fixture the conditional whole-trajectory bound is.06 and its measured full-history TV is.018077. The latter is a floating binomial diagnostic; proofs above provide the guarantees. The actual orientation error was independently evaluated as an exact rational binomial sum, about2.137e-5.

For this contract the conservative universal calibration lower bound is1473.66 expected target requests for some q. It is not attained by the implementation. The upper/lower match the rate rather than constants. All q-dependent calibration requests count even though they occur before deployment; this burden can exceed the source requests saved across many future reports. Exact sampled-source arithmetic and probability-generator costs remain distinct from physical source-request savings.

The learned scalar q is a reusable sufficient law parameter only within the supplied symmetric iid target class. New target families, correlations, changed p/label conventions or unknown noise models require new calibration or a different theorem. No broader model discovery or representation-acquisition result follows.

Concrete consequence: distinguish target-only law estimation, cheap source orientation and finite raw-sample retention before claiming acquisition gains. The next falsifiable direction is a target law with a physically acquired invariant that cancels q, or a lawful factory that draws new q-information from the source. Merely moving calibration earlier does not create an unlimited fresh-data resource.
