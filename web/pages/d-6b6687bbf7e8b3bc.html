## Unconditional stopping tail and expected costs

Let kstar be the first index beta_k<=b/2. Then beta_kstar is in(b/4,b/2], with the stated endpoint exception b=1. For j>=1, reaching kstar+j requires continuation at its preceding index. The global conditional error bound gives

Pr(reach kstar+j)<= (delta/4)^(2^(j+1)).

The j>=1 qualification matters: reaching kstar itself is bounded only by1. The tail does not condition on a good confidence event and therefore includes incorrect earlier scale decisions. It proves a.s. termination and controls costs even on calibration-failure paths.

Each stage's full expected reads, conditional on its starting history, are <=ceil(1/t_k)/eta+A m_k, with A=1+2(R+1)/eta. This is the charged no-reset source-label bound. A complete-return implementation instead gives M/eta after initial hitting; either is enough. Thus stage cost is bounded by a baseline constant times beta_k^(-p)[log(4/delta)+2log(k+1)], p=L/(L-1). Its physical time is this count times t_k, of order beta_k^-1 times the same logarithm. Geometric summation before kstar is dominated by the last cost. Afterward, the doubly exponential reach tail dominates 2^(pj) growth and log(j+1). Therefore

E_b N_scale=O_Q(b^-p[log(1/delta)+loglog(e²/b)]),

E_b T_scale=O_Q(b^-1[log(1/delta)+loglog(e²/b)]).

Necessity of the loglog factor is not established. Constants are not uniform in graph size/rates/conditioning. The expectation includes failed search paths, which is essential for an expected-cost claim.

## EP refinement: a second cost gap

The optional EP paragraph proposes intersecting successive confidence sets and doubling until the EP enclosure is both nonempty and narrow. On a positive-probability confidence-failure event, an intersection can become empty; subsequent intersections remain empty. That literal rule then never stops and may have infinite unconditional expected cost. Good-event accuracy/termination does not establish an unconditional expected-cost bound. This does not affect the fixed final relative-rate block above.

Repair the specified rule by terminating and returning EMPTY/INCONSISTENT when an intersection becomes empty, or supply a restart/last-set rule with an independently proved finite cost tail. The fixed validity union budget can cover the failure report, but one must still prove accuracy-success and runtime separately. Even after that repair, the acknowledged effective bound on b|sigma'(b)| and certified stationary/interval arithmetic cost remains needed for an EP expected-complexity theorem. Mere analytic existence of a finite derivative bound is not its acquisition or computational certificate.

This hazard was communicated to r17 and root. r17 subsequently repaired its note to terminate INCONSISTENT/EMPTY on an empty intersection. That closes this particular failure-event nontermination path; it does not supply the remaining EP derivative/arithmetic expected-cost certificate. The present PASS is for relative-rate acquisition, not an implemented finite-cost arbitrary-graph EP estimator. Unknown-baseline acquisition is being investigated separately by r10; fixed relative-rate precision and fixed additive EP precision need different calibration accounting because reverse-flux uncertainty can multiply log(1/b).
