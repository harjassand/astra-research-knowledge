# Independent audit of the weighted real Landis construction

7 October 2026. Audited proposal: `notes/r15_geometric_analysis/cycle6/WEIGHTED_REAL_LANDIS_CONSTRUCTION.md`, Sections 1–7. **Verdict: PASS for the weighted sparse-domain inverse, global exhaustion, regularized origin, parameter order, variable-radius smoothing, and smooth potential gluing. No load-bearing analytic gap found.** The bounded-overlap/tangent-wave construction is reconstructed below and has a separate r12 audit. This is a universal analytic argument, not numerical evidence or external validation.

## Exact interface and source boundary

For each fixed n≥3 and 0<β<1/2, the proposed construction supplies nonzero real u∈C∞(Rⁿ) and real V∈C∞(Rⁿ) satisfying −Δu+Vu=0,

    |V(x)|≤C(1+|x|)^−β,
    |u(x)|≤C exp(−c|x|^p),  p=(4−2β)/3.

C,c are finite existential constants depending on the construction; no prescribed potential norm, sign, derivative control, positive energy, explicit algorithm, or uniform limit β↑1/2 is claimed. Both u and V are designed together.

Frank–Ivanisvili, arXiv:2608.00802v1, submitted 1 August 2026: Theorem 1 proves the bounded real-potential exponent 4/3 in every n≥3. Their Lemmas 22–27 prove the sparse-domain inversion mechanism for their specific envelope/frequency and weights A^−k E. Their Sections 8–9 prove variable smoothing by differentiating the kernel on the nonsmooth correction. Appendix A explains the two bounded-error terms a/ℓ and a²ℓ/r and the tangent sphere dimension restriction. **Appendix A does not state or prove the weighted real theorem.** The changed envelope/frequency and arbitrary locally comparable weight therefore need the reconstruction below; citing Appendix A alone is insufficient.

Cruz-Sampedro, Proceedings Edinburgh Math. Soc. 42(1999),143–153, Theorem 1 gives the continuation restriction for general complex power-decaying potentials in ambient dimensions covering n≥3. Its Theorem 2 is explicitly a **planar complex** construction on R², with continuous V and C² solution. It is not a real construction and does not itself furnish an odd-dimensional example. FI's introduction explicitly distinguishes the previously unsettled odd-dimensional complex case. I sent this source-quantifier correction to r15/root; r15 corrected its descriptions. Historical novelty of the weighted real extension is still UNKNOWN.

## 1. Independent exponent and local-comparability calculation

Put α=(1−2β)/3∈(0,1/3), d=(1+α)/2, s=(1−α)/2, and

    R=(K²+|x|²)^1/2, a=R^α, ℓ=R^s, κ=R^−β,
    Φ=(R^(1+α)−K^(1+α))/(1+α), E=e^−Φ.

The essential equalities follow from 2β+3α=1:

    a/ℓ = a²ℓ/R = R^−β,
    ℓ/R = 1/(aℓ) = κ/a² = R^−d.

All remaining smooth errors fit: (a/R)/κ=R^−d, ℓ^−2/κ=R^−d, and R^−2/κ=R^(β−2)≤1. On macro balls of radius Cℓ, |R(y)−R(x)|≤Cℓ(x), so power weights are comparable and |a(y)²−a(x)²|≤C a²ℓ/R=Cκ. This comparison must not be applied to E on those balls.

On micro balls B(x,ρ/a(x)), 0<ρ≤1, the relative R change is at most Cρ/(aR). Consequently a,κ,R^(2α−2), and a^−2 have uniformly bounded ratios, independently of large K and of ρ≤1. Since ∇Φ=xR^(α−1) and |∇Φ|≤a, integrating along a segment gives E(y)/E(x) between exp(−Cρ) and exp(Cρ). These are exactly the comparability constants needed for the weighted inverse; no macroscopic exponential comparison enters.

The micro radius b=ρ/a has |∇b|≤Cρ/(aR), and the macro radius ℓ has |∇ℓ|≤Cℓ/R. Thus both admit the usual comparable-radius, bounded-overlap cover once K is large. Its constants do not grow with K.

## 2. Complete weighted sparse-domain inverse reconstruction

Assume an open U satisfies |U∩B(x,b(x))|≤θ|B(x,b(x))| for every x, b(x)=ρ/a(x), a≥a_min>0. Assume the comparable-radius cover above. Let h>0 be smooth with hE∈L² and with h,a,E locally comparable on these balls by a fixed constant L. For θ sufficiently small depending only on n,L and the cover constants, the weak Dirichlet solution

    −Δz=hE on U, z∈H¹_0(U)

satisfies the universal bound

    0≤z(x)≤Cρ²h(x)a(x)^−2 E(x).                    (1)

Here is the proof with the global and boundary steps explicit.

(i) Extend v∈H¹_0(U) by zero. On each covering ball the zero set occupies fraction at least 1−θ. Ordinary Poincaré for v−v_B, together with |v_B|²|B\U|≤∫_B|v−v_B|², gives ∫_B|v|²≤C b_B²∫_B|∇v|². Multiply by a_B², compare a locally, and sum over the bounded-overlap cover:

    ∫_U a²|v|²≤Cρ²∫_U|∇v|².                     (2)

Since a≥a_min, (2) is coercivity for the Dirichlet form on H¹_0(U). Lax–Milgram gives existence and uniqueness for every L² right-hand side. Testing with the negative part gives z≥0. This works with infinitely many unbounded components; no boundary regularity of U is assumed.

(ii) If Z is the zero extension, −ΔZ≤(hE)1_U in distributions. Test the weak equation with φ min(1,z/ε), φ≥0. The extra term φ1_(0<z<ε)|∇z|²/ε is nonnegative. Let ε↓0 using ∇z=0 a.e. on {z=0}; this gives ∫∇Z·∇φ≤∫_U hE φ. No smoothness of ∂U is used.

(iii) First solve on a bounded smooth subdomain D compactly contained in U. Its right-hand side is bounded there and its solution continuous up to the boundary. Subtract the ball Dirichlet Green potential of (hE)1_D from the zero extension. The difference is distributionally subharmonic, and the ball Green function in n≥3 is bounded by C|x−y|^(2−n). The mean-value inequality on a concentric half-ball yields

    z_D(x)≤C∫_(D∩B(x,b)) h(y)E(y)|x−y|^(2−n)dy
             +Cb^−n∫_(D∩B(x,b))z_D(y)dy.          (3)

Define Ψ=ρ²ha^−2E and M_D=sup_D z_D/Ψ, finite because D is bounded, Ψ positive, and z_D continuous. Local comparability and ∫_B|x−y|^(2−n)dy=C b² bound the first term by CΨ(x). The second is at most Cθ M_D Ψ(x), because D⊂U and Ψ is locally comparable. Choose θ with Cθ≤1/2. Taking the supremum gives M_D≤2C, uniformly over D. The decisive gain is b²=ρ²/a²; a global unweighted estimate would not replace this step.

(iv) Exhaust U by nested bounded smooth domains D_m, each a finite union of smooth components. Zero-extend their solutions to U. Equation (2) supplies a common H¹_0(U) bound depending on ||hE||₂ and a_min, not on m. A weak subsequential limit solves the equation on every compactly supported test; density and uniqueness identify it with z. The set {v∈L²:0≤v≤2CΨ} is closed and convex, hence weakly closed. Here Ψ∈L² follows from hE∈L² and a≥a_min. Thus the pointwise bound passes almost everywhere; local smooth elliptic regularity then gives it everywhere in U. This proves (1).

For h₁=κ and h₂=R^(2α−2), all local constants above are uniform in K sufficiently large and ρ≤1. The weights are smooth, positive, and h_iE∈L² for each fixed K. No hidden K-dependence is lost when θ and then ρ are selected.

## 3. Origin defect and noncircular repair

The envelope obeys |∇Φ|²−a²=−K²R^(2α−2). The approximate tangent-wave construction therefore gives

    |Δf|≤CE[κ+K²R^(2α−2)|q|], f=Eq.              (4)

At R=K the origin error divided by κ is K^d; it cannot be absorbed in a K-independent Cκ bound. The proposal correctly retains it until the repair step.

On Ω={|q|<τ}, apply (1) separately to h₁,h₂ and use comparison for the signed right-hand side in −Δw=Δf. This gives

    |w|≤Cρ²E[R^−d+τK²/R²].                       (5)

Choose θ using the inverse lemma's uniform local constants. Then choose ρ so Cρ² is small. Only then obtain τ from local trigonometric sparsity; finally choose K so Cρ²K^−d≤τ/40. The second term in (5) is already small because K²/R²≤1. These choices give |w|≤τE/20 without requiring ρ to be chosen after τ. Thus the parameter selection is not circular.

For smoothing one also needs the global weighted bound

    |W|≤C_K κ a^−2E,                              (6)

where W is the zero extension. From (5), the ratio of its second term to κa^−2 is bounded by K²R^(d−2)≤K^d, since d<2. So (6) holds with a finite K-dependent constant. The original theorem permits exactly that dependence.

## 4. Variable radius: full derivative ledger

Set ε=σ/a after τ,K are fixed. Then |∇ε|≤C/(Ra) and |D²ε|≤C/(R²a). Smooth f and W separately. For the smooth part G(x)=∫ψ(z)f(x−ε(x)z)dz, the exact chain rule produces the average of Δf and three error families:

    |∇ε||D²f|≤C(a/R)E,
    |∇ε|²|D²f|≤CR^−2E,
    |Δε||∇f|≤CR^−2E.

All fit CκE by Section 1. The average of (4) fits C_KκE because K²R^(2α−2)/κ≤K^d. Thus |ΔG|≤C_KκE.

For the repair part H(x)=∫K_x(y)W(y)dy, put t=a/σ, ζ=t(x−y), K_x=tⁿψ(ζ). On its support |x−y|≤1/t. We have |∇t|≤Ct/R, |D²t|≤Ct/R², |∇ζ|≤Ct, and |D²ζ|≤C(t/R+R^−2). Product and chain rules therefore give |Δ_xK_x|≤C_σ a^(n+2), with support inside B(x,ε(x)). Integrate against (6), using micro comparability and volume O(a^−n):

    |ΔH|≤C_σ a^(n+2)a^−n[C_Kκa^−2E]≤C_KκE.

There is no derivative of W or any presumed smoothness across ∂Ω in this calculation. Smoothness of the final mollified function follows by differentiating the kernel on compact x sets, where its support and all derivatives are uniformly bounded.

## 5. Division/gluing and nontriviality

Choose σ so |q(y)−q(x)|≤τ/8 throughout the mollifying ball. If |q(x)|<τ/2, that ball lies in Ω. Radial normalized averaging preserves its harmonic f*=f+W exactly, even though ε varies with x; equality holds as functions throughout the open set O={|q|<τ/2}, hence ΔF=0 there.

Outside O the ball has a fixed q sign and |q|≥3τ/8. Equation (5) leaves f* with that sign and magnitude at least (3/8−1/20)τE almost everywhere. Nonnegative averaging and micro E comparison give |F|≥cτE there. Thus O∪{F≠0}=Rⁿ. The formulas V=0 on O and V=ΔF/F on {F≠0} coincide on their open overlap and glue smoothly. All zeros of F are in the harmonic region; no division at a zero is performed. The upper Laplacian bound yields |V|≤C_Kκ globally.

Sparsity gives some |q|≥τ point in every micro ball, where the safe lower bound makes F≠0. Finally E≤C_K exp(−c|x|^(1+α)), so F is the required nonzero globally decaying solution. Realness is retained in every step.

## 6. Cover/sparsity prerequisites and sharpness

The geometric cover proof uses only slow variation of ℓ and packing of disjoint cores; its enlarged intersection graph has bounded degree. A micro ball sees a bounded number of these patches because (aℓ)^−1→0 uniformly as K grows. Greedy tangent directions in S^(n−2) avoid finitely many small ambient caps for n≥3. On overlapping patches, frequency pairs are uniformly separated.

Freezing the partition at a micro-ball center leaves a finite family of cosine sums with nonnegative coefficients summing to one. Frequencies have bounded nonzero modulus and separated ± pairs. Distinct exponentials restricted to a generic line are independent by a Vandermonde determinant, so none of these sums is identically zero. Compactness plus the analytic zero-set theorem gives a uniform small-sublevel measure bound. The freezing error is O(ρ/(aℓ))=O(ρK^−d) and can be made smaller than its threshold after τ is chosen. This recovers the sparsity premise; n=2 fails at the frequency-selection step. No quantitative transversality or nodal-boundary regularity is assumed.

For the sharpness comparison, a hypothetical solution decaying with power p+δ would satisfy exp(λr^p)u∈L² for every λ>0. Since V is smooth bounded and u,Δu=Vu∈L², Fourier elliptic regularity gives u∈H²(Rⁿ), meeting the older upper theorem's regularity. Cruz-Sampedro Theorem 1 then makes u compactly supported, and local unique continuation for smooth bounded V makes it zero. Thus the proposed exponent is sharp in power in the declared n≥3 class. This uses the **upper theorem**, not the planar complex example as a same-dimensional construction.

## Consequence and remaining uncertainty

The mathematical extension passes this audit: slowly power-decaying smooth real potentials can sustain superexponentially localized zero-energy states in n≥3 under these unrestricted pointwise assumptions. Reality plus weak pointwise decay does not improve the sharp power. Finite constants and no derivative restrictions remain important. Novelty and practical significance must not be certified by our internal agreement. My limited primary search found no distinct real weighted construction; that absence is not a novelty proof. FI supplies the new real mechanism; the weight balance is close to its Appendix A.
