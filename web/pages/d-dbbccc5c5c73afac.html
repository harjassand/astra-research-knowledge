## Focal primary study

Simon Andersson et al., “Old mitochondria regulate niche renewal via α-ketoglutarate metabolism in stem cells,” *Nature Metabolism* 7, 1344–1357 (2025), DOI [10.1038/s42255-025-01325-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-025-01325-7), PMID 40659911, PMCID PMC12286850. Open-access full text: [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12286850/); publisher page: [Nature Metabolism](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-025-01325-7).

Relevant source spans/figure references in the PMC full text:

- Abstract and main text, lines 203–219: old-mitochondria-enriched ISCs, elevated αKG/TET-linked epigenetic change and PC fate bias; mitochondrial age labels were operationally ≥48 h versus ≤8 h; approximately 9% of Lgr5-high ISCs were classed ISC-mito-O and most crypts had 1–2.
- Figure 1 legend and main text, lines 228–257: age-selective mitochondrial apportioning observed in 15% of divisions at +3/+4 positions; the in-vitro PC-containing-organoid contrast was transient (about threefold at the 48 h bottleneck, no first-division PC, and comparable mature-organoid PC number at day 10). Organoid outcomes are proxies for in-vivo tissue repair.
- Main text, lines 319–334: 7-day dm-αKG raised epithelial αKG/ISC 5hmC and EdU-reported Paneth renewal, with no change in total ISCs or PCs in Extended Data Fig. 6. This is a cell-label/count result, not a directly measured crypt repair response.
- Main text and Figure 4 legend, lines 367–378: 3-week dm-αKG increased EdU-positive PC fraction in aged mice (PBS n=3, dm-αKG n=4), lowered Notum transcript in the sorted renewed EdU-positive PC population but not the non-renewed population (paired n=4), and separate 2-week prophylaxis before a single 100 mg/kg 5-FU challenge reduced day-5 weight loss and restored ileal villus-cell density (injury/recovery groups generally n=6–7). These readouts are not reported as same-crypt joint trajectories.
- Main text, lines 380–387: the authors themselves state that deterministic PC differentiation from >10% asymmetric events would exceed the observed/known slow PC-turnover rate, so they infer bias rather than fate determination. They discuss neutral stem-cell competition and the unknown step from metabolic state to symmetry breaking.
- Methods, lines 393–402: aged mice were ≥18 months; dm-αKG was 600 mg/kg intraperitoneally twice daily unless a figure legend specified otherwise; 5-FU was 100 mg/kg i.p.; the aging-recovery caption specifies once-daily dm-αKG for two weeks before 5-FU. For the in-vivo EdU label, drinking water contained 0.2 mg/ml; EdU reports DNA synthesis/lineage renewal subject to labeling assumptions.
- Linked source-data files are available from lines 329, 366, 376; they were not downloaded or independently reanalyzed here. The model and counterexamples below use no digitized effect sizes from these files.

## Primary prior art and interpretation boundary

1. **Aged Paneth Notum and Wnt:** Nalle Pentinmikko et al., “Paneth cell produced Notum attenuates regeneration of aged intestinal epithelium,” *Nature* 571, 398–402 (2019), DOI [10.1038/s41586-019-1383-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1383-0); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8151802/). The primary paper reports elevated Notum in aged Paneth cells, Wnt suppression in ISCs, and improved aged-epithelium regeneration after genetic/chemical Notum targeting or Wnt support, including the same general 5-FU mucositis endpoint. Thus Paneth-Notum causality and Notum-inhibition regeneration predate the focal paper. This does not establish that dm-αKG’s benefit is mediated by its renewal route.
2. **Paneth as niche:** Toshiro Sato et al., “Paneth cells constitute the niche for Lgr5 stem cells in intestinal crypts,” *Nature* 469, 415–418 (2011), DOI [10.1038/nature09637](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09637); [PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3547360/). This supports the relevant cell–niche connection, not the new rate model.
3. **Neutral clonal drift:** Hugo J. Snippert et al., “Intestinal crypt homeostasis results from neutral competition between symmetrically dividing Lgr5 stem cells,” *Cell* 143, 134–144 (2010), DOI [10.1016/j.cell.2010.09.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2010.09.016); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20887898/). Their lineage-tracing/model analysis is prior art against treating all stem-cell outputs as deterministic asymmetric division.
4. **Paneth residence-time uncertainty:** Heather Ireland et al., “Cellular inheritance of a Cre-activated reporter gene to determine Paneth cell longevity in the murine small intestine,” *Developmental Dynamics* 233, 1332–1336 (2005), DOI [10.1002/dvdy.20446](https://doi.org/10.1002/dvdy.20446); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15937933/). The primary abstract reports up to 57 days for reporter inheritance and argues that thymidine-based estimates underestimated Paneth longevity. The “up to 57 days” value is not assumed here to be an exponential mean residence time. Earlier nucleotide-label estimates were about 2–3 weeks, so the residence distribution and measurement definition must be acquired for any numeric event-budget bound.
5. **Organelle/tissue context:** Hien Bui et al., “Glucose-6-phosphate-dehydrogenase on old peroxisomes maintains self-renewal of epithelial stem cells after asymmetric cell division,” *Nature Communications* 16, 3932 (2025), DOI [10.1038/s41467-025-58752-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-58752-z); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12033372/). In mammary and epidermal stem-cell models, old peroxisomes and peroxisomal G6PD support self-renewal. This is a scope warning: organelle-age effects are cell/organelle/context-specific; it does not establish an intestinal ISC trade-off.
