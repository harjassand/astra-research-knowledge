# Cycle 2: exact application paths and their remaining bottlenecks

## Lead conclusion

The second pass did more than show where combinatorial guarantees fail to model chemistry. It found three exact capability paths under narrow, measurable models: (1) family 139’s oracle guarantee applies to a genuinely coupled logistic binding posterior under a sparse spectral design condition; (2) family 115’s exact uniform table sampler gives an exact conditional randomization test for independent geometric counts with separable odds; and (3) family 131’s degree-switch chain supports conditional graph tests when the labeled degree sequence is the intended null. Each application retains a concrete physical or statistical caveat, and each OpenAI/math theorem remains source-reported rather than independently proof-verified.

The highest-ceiling representation result from this lane is a positive stochastic-kernel embedding of the N51/family-116 rational matrix hitting tuple. It removes the two-state polynomial-identity obstruction by adding hidden Markov states, while exposing a quantitative readout bottleneck: the source controls binary encoding size, not a polynomially resolvable signal or a manageable hitting-list search. This is a real mathematical bridge, but not yet a physical detection capability.

No result in this pass supports a claim of new biological behavior, a validated source theorem, or historical priority.

## 1. A family-139 posterior that really satisfies the oracle hypotheses

Consider a two-state equilibrium occupancy assay with binary readout and a log-affinity parameter vector $z\in\mathbb R^d$. For calibrated design rows $b_j$, use the logistic response model $\Pr(Y=1\mid z,b_j)=\sigma(b_j^Tz)$ and standard Gaussian prior $N(0,I)$. At each row collect one bound and one unbound observation. Up to an additive constant, the negative log posterior is
\[
 V(z)=\tfrac12\|z\|^2+2\sum_j\log\cosh(b_j^Tz/2),
\]
so the exact minimizer is supplied at $z=0$. Its Hessian is
\[
 \nabla^2V(z)=I+\tfrac12B^T\operatorname{diag}(\operatorname{sech}^2(Bz/2))B.
\]
Therefore $B^TB\preceq2I$ proves the global sandwich $I\preceq\nabla^2V(z)\preceq2I$. A concrete sparse coupled design is $B_d=[I_d;C_d/2]$, where $C_d$ has rows $e_k+e_{k+1}$. Since $\|C_d\|_2\le2$, $B_d^TB_d=I+C_d^TC_d/4\preceq2I$ for every $d$. This is curved and coupled, rather than a Gaussian posterior that the oracle sampler would make trivial.

The family-139 statement promises at most $C_\epsilon d^\epsilon$ exact value-and-gradient oracle calls, total variation error at most $0.1$, for each fixed $\epsilon>0$, with unrestricted computation between calls. The positive construction therefore verifies a real model interface. Its limitation is sharp: the balanced event has prior-predictive probability at most $2^{-(2d-1)}$ for $2d-1$ rows, so it is an eligibility construction rather than typical-data performance. Exact oracle calls also do not count assay acquisition, bit time, or the cost of evaluating a stored likelihood. A separate Michaelis–Menten log-parameter posterior in the child report has negative curvature $-4109/1600$ at a specified point; because affine changes preserve Hessian inertia, that posterior cannot be moved into the family-139 class by whitening alone.

Full derivation, finite design check, counterexample, and claims: [family-139 report](../sparse_response_metrology/FINAL_REPORT.md), [claims](../sparse_response_metrology/CLAIMS.json). Evidence is source-derived and conditional; no experiment or source-proof audit was performed.

## 2. Exact conditional randomization for geometric cell counts

Let $X_{gc}$ be independent geometric random variables on $\{0,1,\ldots\}$ with probability mass $(1-\rho_{gc})\rho_{gc}^{x}$, where $\rho_{gc}=a_gb_c<1$. Their joint mass at a table $x$ is proportional to
\[
 \prod_{g,c}\rho_{gc}^{x_{gc}}=\prod_g a_g^{r_g}\prod_c b_c^{s_c}
\]
after conditioning on row totals $r_g$ and column totals $s_c$. Thus the conditional law is exactly uniform over the fixed-margin fiber, and the row/column nuisance factors cancel. This is a positive model-scoped transfer: the family-115 exact sampler supplies independent conditional null tables, and for a preregistered statistic $T$ the plus-one Monte Carlo value
\[
 p=\frac{1+\#\{b:T(X^{(b)})\ge T(X_{\rm obs})\}}{M+1}
\]
is super-uniform under the null by exchangeability of the observed and sampled tables. The exact sampler is almost-surely terminating with expected polynomial bit time for unrestricted nonnegative cells and binary-encoded margins. $M$ exact draws cost $M$ times that expectation; the source’s every-run result is an almost-uniform sampler with accuracy parameter, not exact polynomial time.

The distribution family matters. With margins $(2,2)$ by $(2,2)$, the three possible 2-by-2 tables have conditional weights $(1/3,1/3,1/3)$ under geometric shape 1, $(1/6,2/3,1/6)$ under Poisson, and $(9/34,16/34,9/34)$ under negative-binomial shape 2, even with separable main-effect parameters. The uniform sampler is therefore an exact null only for the stated geometric model. Structural zeros and cell caps are not covered by the exact uniform-sampling theorem; the companion result counts bounded tables with an FPRAS.

Full model, test, costs, and exact rational check: [family-115 report](../stationary_escape_math/FINAL_REPORT.md), [claims](../stationary_escape_math/CLAIMS.json). The geometric conditioning identity is elementary prior art, not a novelty claim; the cross-field capability is the precise sampler/test interface.

## 3. Degree-conditioned graph tests

For an explicit simple graph host equal to the complete graph and a graphical labeled degree sequence, family 131’s stated lazy four-vertex switch chain reaches total-variation distance at most $1/4$ within $2n^8$ steps. Its exact sampler terminates almost surely in expected polynomial bit time, but has a rare exponential-enumeration branch. A positive statistical use is a prespecified test of a graph statistic under a degree-conditioned null. Under a null that fixes labeled compartments and degrees and independently samples uniform realizations within each compartment, the plus-one rank test is conditionally valid; Hoeffding’s inequality gives $B\ge\log(2/\beta)/(2\varepsilon^2)$ draws for tail-probability precision $\varepsilon$ with failure probability at most $\beta$.

The exact or approximate sampler’s graph distribution must match the null. A degree-only null can generate forbidden cross-compartment contacts, while a compartment-stratified null still ignores within-compartment geometry. The child report gives both a two-compartment falsifier and a matched geometry control, and charges the source’s mixing and approximate-sampling errors. This is a conditional statistical workflow, not a new network theorem.

Full analysis: [family-131 report](../block_cover_assay/FINAL_REPORT.md), [claims](../block_cover_assay/CLAIMS.json). Its proof and sampler are source-reported, unverified.

## 4. Rational formula hitting sets as positive stochastic kernels

The N51 first pass gave a two-state obstruction: the nonzero free rational formula $\Phi=s_4(X_1,\ldots,X_4)(I+X_5)^{-1}$ vanishes on every 2-by-2 substitution by the standard polynomial identity, although order-dependent physical outputs need not vanish. A new representation removes that dimension-specific obstruction.

For a rational $d$-by-$d$ tuple $A_i$, set $m=d+1$, $u=\mathbf1$, $E=[e_1-e_m,\ldots,e_d-e_m]$, $F=[I_d\ 0]-(1/m)\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_m^T$, and $J=uu^T/m$. Then $FE=I_d$, $EF=I_d-J$, $F u=0$, and $\mathbf1^TE=0$. With $c=1/2$ and sufficiently small rational $t>0$, the matrices
\[
 P_i=J+E(cI+tA_i)F
\]
are strictly positive doubly stochastic kernels. For $S=[u\ E]$, one has $S^{-1}P_iS=\operatorname{diag}(1,cI+tA_i)$ and the exact decoder $A_i=t^{-1}(FP_iE-cI)$. Expanding the alternating degree-four polynomial shows that terms containing a scalar identity cancel, so $s_4(cI+tA_1,\ldots,cI+tA_4)=t^4s_4(A_1,\ldots,A_4)$. The inverse block is $((1+c)I+tA_5)^{-1}$, which is nonsingular for the chosen small $t$. Hence a nonzero source hit remains nonzero on positive Markov kernels of one additional state.

This result changes the conclusion from “two-state no-go” to “dimension can restore representation.” It does not yet provide a physically efficient hit. If the largest encoded entry of $A_i$ has $L$ bits, the positivity scale may be $t=2^{-O(L)}$, and the evaluated signal scales as $t^4$ even when the source polynomial evaluation is a nonzero integer. Resolving a signal $\gamma$ by transition sampling costs at least order $\gamma^{-2}$ observations per estimated matrix entry. The source provides polynomial bit bounds, but no polynomial signal-margin bound, usable hitting-list search, or end-to-end assay cost. The exact embedding is therefore a candidate representation lemma with an explicit physical-readout failure gate, not a validated experiment.

The original two-state proof and source provenance are in [N51 report](../nc_formula_bio_design/FINAL_REPORT.md) and [N51 claims](../nc_formula_bio_design/CLAIMS.json). The expanded positive embedding and precision audit are in [N51 cycle-two note](../nc_formula_bio_design/CYCLE2_NOTE.md), [full derivation](../nc_formula_bio_design/CYCLE2.md), and [exact finite checker](../nc_formula_bio_design/cycle2_embedding_check.py). The checker fixture is not claimed to appear in the source hitting list. Family 116/N51 source theorem verification and historical priority remain open.
