Result: certified design without identifying the physical model
I constructed a sensing-to-control procedure that selects a near-optimal interaction for an uncertain, strongly interacting quantum chain—using a measurement that cannot identify either the model or its absolute transport coefficient.
The new task allows two unknown physical parameters,
\[
U\in[3.9,4.1],\qquad d\in[1.9,2.1],
\]
while choosing the next-nearest-neighbour interaction \(V\in[0,3]\). The hopping remains \(t=1\), and \(d\) is the original physical dephasing rate, not artificial noise.
One local interference-measurement setting, repeated on independent preparations, supplies the control adjustment. The resulting uniform guarantees are:
Measurement repetitions	Certified loss from the actual model’s global optimum
\(262{,}144\)	Less than \(8.781\times10^{-5}\)
\(1{,}048{,}576\)	Less than \(4.343\times10^{-5}\)


These hold with probability greater than 99%, conditional on the stated parameter range, physical model and explicitly budgeted measurement and actuation errors.
The offline construction and checking stages totalled 252.14 seconds, with approximately 1.88 GiB peak memory, using one BLAS thread. The laboratory protocol was not executed; the measurement tests were synthetic.

1. What becomes obtainable
The retained Hamiltonian is
\[
H(U,V)=
-\frac12\sum_i(X_iX_{i+1}+Y_iY_{i+1})
+\frac U4\sum_iZ_iZ_{i+1}
+\frac V4\sum_iZ_iZ_{i+2},
\]
with dynamics
\[
\mathcal L O
=
i[H,O]
+\frac d4\sum_i(Z_iOZ_i-O).
\]
The output remains the infinite-temperature density-response second-moment transport coefficient \(D(U,V,d)\). Neither interaction is discarded or treated through a weak-interaction expansion.
Previously, the model parameters were known, and the calculation certified an optimum of \(D(V)\). The new procedure must choose a good setting for the actual unknown \(U,d\), without first determining them.
It constructs a rule \(V_{\mathrm{chosen}}(\text{observations})\) satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\max_{0\le V\le3}D(U,V,d)
-
D(U,V_{\mathrm{chosen}},d)
\le \text{reported error}.
}
\]
This is a model-wise guarantee throughout the uncertainty box—not an average over a favourable prior distribution.
The calculation also proves that every fixed control chosen without observations has worst-case loss at least
\[
\boxed{0.00012390875}
\]
over that same box. Both measurement-based guarantees improve on this lower bound.
That comparison does not exclude better active experiments. It establishes that the observations provide decision-relevant information that no fixed setting can replace.
2. The necessary object is a decision on an observational equivalence class
Suppose a measurement has mean \(m(\eta)\), where \(\eta\) contains uncertain physical parameters. All models satisfying
\[
m(\eta)=y
\]
belong to the same measurement fibre: repeated use of that measurement cannot distinguish them.
Full identification requires shrinking such a fibre to one model. A good decision requires something weaker:
Find one control that is near-optimal for every model still compatible with the observation.

With finite statistical error, the fibre is thickened to \(|m(\eta)-y|\le h\). The computation then certifies the control over that entire set.
How the relevant measurement direction is found
For a performance objective \(F(u,\eta)\), let
\[
A=F_{uu},\qquad B=F_{u\eta}
\]
at a nominal control and model. The first-order compensating control is
\[
\boxed{u(\eta)\approx u_0-A^{-1}B\,\eta.}
\]
The matrix \(-A^{-1}B\) has only as many rows as there are controls. It describes how the best decision moves, rather than how every physical output changes.
This implicit-function and cross-Hessian reasoning is not itself new. Tan and Frazier already derive decision-risk bounds involving cross-Hessian parameter errors and a fourth-order nuisance remainder. The new obligation here is to acquire the needed quantities from the interacting quantum problem, realise the relevant measurement physically, and certify the complete nonlinear decision rule rather than assume its validity. arXiv
For the present chain, the numerical response derivatives suggested
\[
\Delta V\approx0.60955\,\Delta U-0.07682\,\Delta d.
\]
A finite rational search selected the implementable direction
\[
\boxed{
\Delta V=
\frac{39}{64}\Delta U-\frac{13}{170}\Delta d.
}
\]
That choice is only a proposal. Its final guarantee comes from checking the full physical residuals over the uncertainty box, not from trusting those nominal derivatives.
Why this reduces the information requirement
Along an accurately compensated control, the first-order change in the optimality condition cancels. Unmeasured nuisance variation then enters the gradient at second order.
If curvature is certified,
\[
F_{uu}\preceq-\mu I,
\]
a gradient bound gives
\[
\boxed{
\max_uF(u,\eta)-F(u_0,\eta)
\le\frac{\|\nabla_uF(u_0,\eta)\|^2}{2\mu}.
}
\]
Thus second-order uncertainty in the optimality condition can become fourth-order decision loss. Absolute response values need not exhibit the same cancellation.
The curvature and global optimizer region must be proved. A small gradient at one point is insufficient.

5. The certificate includes uncertain physical noise and the thermodynamic limit
The computational extension is not merely multivariate curve fitting.
Write
\[
B_\theta=S(d)+A(U,V).
\]
For a finite-ring proof, normalize using the fixed reference
\[
S_{\mathrm{ref}}=S(2)+\lambda I,\qquad \lambda>0,
\]
rather than changing the normalization with the unknown \(d\):
\[
T_{\theta,\lambda}
=
S_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}
(B_\theta+\lambda I)
S_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1/2}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{Re}T_{\theta,\lambda}\succeq\beta I,
\qquad
\beta=\min(1,d/2),
\]
and
\[
\|T_{\theta,\lambda}^{-1}\|\le\beta^{-1}.
\]
The normalized perturbation norms in the axes \((V,U,d)\) are bounded by
\[
(1,1,1/2).
\]
These are calculated from local interaction strengths and the known dephasing structure. No many-body relaxation gap is supplied.
For finite trial fields \(f,g\), with exact population balance \(PA_0f=0\), the corrected output obeys

\kappa-\mathcal F
=
\langle r,R T^{-1}r\rangle
\]
before taking the controlled limits. Here \(r\) is the complete normalized residual, and \(R\) is the parity involution.
Differentiating this identity gives computable bounds. In particular, with \(q_0,q_V,q_{VV}\) the complete residual derivative norms,

The deeper result is:
The computational target can be a near-optimal action shared by all models compatible with an observation—not a uniquely identified model, an absolute response curve, or a compact full inverse.

Here that target was explicitly constructed and verified for a nonperturbative uncertainty family. The control law remains useful across observationally indistinguishable models whose transport values differ substantially.
The general open problem is now a constructive decision-complexity theorem: given a broader interacting model and a realistic set of available experiments, acquire sufficiently accurate conservation-safe decision residuals and a realizable low-dimensional measurement map at controlled total cost. A low-rank decision Jacobian alone does not provide that theorem. Competing distant optima, nonlinear nuisance effects, inaccessible measurements and dictionary growth can still defeat the reduction.
This is a concrete uncertainty-aware scientific decision capability, not a civilisation-level materials predictor. Its advance is that model identification has been removed from the deployed decision procedure—and that omission is certified rather than assumed harmless.
