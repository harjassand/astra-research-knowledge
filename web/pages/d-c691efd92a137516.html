# Row-ℓ2 tails for the exact C0 and Cinc seed gadgets

This note strengthens the exact support counts in
`cycle3/complement_lcu/03_decisive_counterexample.md` and
`cycle4/inverse_resolvent/01_incidence_access.md` by calculating the best
possible rowwise ℓ2 truncation. A row-tail bound is a necessary operator-norm
condition for a sparse approximation, but it need not be a useful constant-gap
obstruction: both gadgets have exponentially many entries whose aggregate row
mass is small.

## C0: isolated relative-reflection row

Use the full-list label transform from the C0 support note. In the
term-label basis, the selected φ-to-ψ data block is exactly

\[
  -\chi_\phi\overline{\chi_\psi}R_\phi R_\psi,
  \qquad |\chi_\phi\chi_\psi|=1/8.
\]

For the source string `z^R`, let `a,b,c` count output symbols in
`{z}`, `{u}`, and `{v1,v2,v3}`, with `a+b+c=R`. The unscaled row entry is

\[
r_R(a,b,c)=4(3/8)^a(1/2)^b(1/8)^c
 -2\mathbf1_{c=0}(1/2)^{a+b}
 -2\mathbf1_{b=0}(3/4)^a(1/4)^c
 +\mathbf1_{b=c=0}.
\]

It occurs with multiplicity
\[
  \mu_R(a,b,c)=\frac{R!}{a!b!c!}3^c.
\]
Let \(\mathcal M_R\) be the multiset containing
\(|r_R(a,b,c)|^2\) with these multiplicities, including zeros. Define
\(M_R(k)\) to be the sum of the `k` largest members of \(\mathcal M_R\),
or all members when `k >= 5^R`. This is the exact largest squared ℓ2 mass
that any `k` retained coordinates can capture. Since `R_phi R_psi` is
unitary, \(\sum\mathcal M_R=1\), and the optimal residual row norm is

\[
  \tau_R(k)=\sqrt{1-M_R(k)}.
\]

The formula is an exact finite sorting prescription over rational numbers;
it is not the term-support union. For example, the largest-entry diagnostic
from exact evaluation is:

| R | exact support | largest absolute entry | residual after retaining that entry |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 25 | 1/2 | 0.866025 |
| 3 | 125 | 1/4 | 0.968246 |
| 4 | 625 | 0.321289 | 0.946981 |
| 5 | 3125 | 0.492554 | 0.870282 |
| 6 | 15625 | 0.623917 | 0.781491 |
| 7 | 78125 | 0.721579 | 0.692333 |
| 8 | 390625 | 0.793526 | 0.608536 |

The diagonal coordinate has exact value
\[
 r_0(R)=1+4(3/8)^R-2(1/2)^R-2(3/4)^R.
\]
For every `k >= 1`, retaining this coordinate gives
\[
 \tau_R(k)\le \sqrt{1-r_0(R)^2}
   = (2+o(1))(\sqrt3/2)^R.
\]
Thus the row has support `5^R` for `R>=2`, but a single coordinate captures
`1-o(1)` of its squared norm. The exact best tail \(\tau_R(k)\) can only be
smaller than the displayed bound.

Now let \(\widetilde C_0\) be any Hermitian matrix with at most `s` nonzeros
per physical row, and suppose \(\|\widetilde C_0-C_0\|\le\epsilon\). Apply
the same unitary label transform used to isolate the seed pair. A transformed
row is a combination of `D` physical rows, so it has at most `D s` distinct
data-output coordinates in the fixed `\psi` output-label block. The selected
row of the transformed error has ℓ2 norm at most its operator norm. Hence the
necessary condition is

\[
  \boxed{\epsilon\ \ge\ \tfrac18\,\tau_R(Ds)}. \tag{C0-tail}
\]

This holds for the complete label list and every exact PREPARE completion:
the pair coefficient is isolated after all labels and terms have been
combined. For `s>=1`, its right side is at most
\(\frac18(2+o(1))(\sqrt3/2)^R\). Therefore the row/column norm test rules out
the approximation only when `epsilon` is below the exact tail threshold; it
does **not** rule out constant or inverse-polynomial `epsilon` as `R` grows.
It does rule out exact equality with a polynomial-row-sparse approximation,
recovering the support obstruction. This is a statement about this C0
canonical projector on the augmented full list, not all positive exact
enforcers with the same kernel.

## Cinc: complete incidence row on the reducing flag sector

Use the legal augmented BMVZ instance and exact PREPARE completion in
`cycle4/inverse_resolvent/01_incidence_access.md`. On the all-`flag=01`
reducing sector, write \(N=2^R\),
\(\alpha=23/32\),
\(\kappa=\sqrt{\pi/(2(1+\pi))}\), and
\[
  \gamma=\frac{\kappa}{8\alpha}
     =\frac{4\kappa}{23},
\]
where `pi` is the positive LCU probability of the selected repeated
\(\phi\)-clause label. The selected physical row `(b,x)` has exactly these
nonzero values:

* same-label diagonal `(b,x)`: \(a=1/16\);
* label-0 coordinate `(0,x)`: magnitude \(d=\gamma(1-1/N)\);
* the other `N-1` label-0 coordinates in \(\{00010,10010\}^R\): magnitude
  \(q=\gamma/N\) each.

All remaining entries vanish. Since `pi<=1`, \(\gamma\le2/23\), so
\(q\le1/23<1/16=a\). Also `d>=q`. Thus after the two diagonal coordinates,
the off-diagonal gadget entries are the largest remaining entries. The exact
row norm and best retained squared mass are

\[
 \|C_{(b,x),*}\|_2^2=a^2+\gamma^2(1-1/N),
\]
and, for integer `s>=2`,
\[
 M_{\rm inc}(s)=a^2+d^2+
   \min\{s-2,N-1\}\,q^2.
\]
For `s=1`, \(M_{\rm inc}(1)=\max\{a^2,d^2\}\); for `s>=N+1`, it is the
full row mass. Consequently the exact optimal residual is

\[
 \tau_{\rm inc}(s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \sqrt{a^2+\gamma^2(1-1/N)-\max\{a^2,d^2\}},&s=1,\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\gamma\frac{\sqrt{\max\{0,N+1-s\}}}{N},&s\ge2.
 \end{cases} \tag{Cinc-tail}
\]

In particular, for polynomial `s` and `R` growing as a positive power of the
source size,
\[
 \tau_{\rm inc}(s)=\gamma\,2^{-R/2}(1-o(1))
\]
provided `s=o(2^R)`. The selected clause can be chosen with
\(\pi\ge(3/32)/M_\phi\), where `M_phi` is the polynomial number of output
labels carrying the aggregate \(\phi\)-weight `3/32`; hence `gamma` is at
least inverse-polynomial in the output size. The row-tail threshold is still
exponentially small in `R`.

For any Hermitian \(s\)-row-sparse \(\widetilde C_{\rm inc}\),
\[
 \boxed{\|\widetilde C_{\rm inc}-C_{\rm inc}\|
        \ge \tau_{\rm inc}(s).} \tag{Cinc-op}
\]
Indeed, applying the error to the selected basis vector gives a column whose
unmatched coordinates have at least the best-`s` row tail; Hermiticity makes
the same bound a row bound. Thus `epsilon < tau_inc(s)` is impossible. At
constant or inverse-polynomial `epsilon`, this particular row/column test
does not prohibit polynomial `s`, despite the exact `2^R` support count.

This full-list statement is scoped to the displayed hardness-preserving
singleton augmentation, the displayed exact PREPARE completion, and the
displayed reducing sector. The hard-base terms and other seed terms remain in
the full list; they vanish or act as the stated scalar on this sector, so the
formula is exact there. It is not a bound for every PREPARE completion or for
the unmodified canonical BMVZ output.

There is a further reason not to read the row tail as an operator-norm
no-go. On the `N`-dimensional gadget coordinates, the cross block is exactly
\(-\gamma I+\gamma J/N\), where \(J/N\) is the rank-one all-ones
projection. A normalized adjacency matrix `P` of a `d`-regular undirected
expander has `P 1=1`; hence \(\|P-J/N\|\) is its second-eigenvalue ratio.
Replacing this rank-one summand by `P` gives a `d`-row-sparse block with
operator error \(\gamma\|P-J/N\|\), while the physical adjoint block can be
replaced by \(P^*\) to preserve Hermiticity. Any fixed relative accuracy
`eta<1` (absolute error `eta*gamma`) on this compressed block can therefore
be obtained with a degree depending on `eta`, but not on `R`. This is only a
block-local illustration, **not** a sparse
approximation of the entire Cinc matrix; other blocks remain unaddressed.

## Interpretation

Both support arguments prove exact row-list lower bounds, but neither yields
a constant-gap approximation lower bound through a single row or column.
The exact necessary thresholds are (C0-tail) and (Cinc-tail). A stronger
negative result would need a global spectral argument that uses many coupled
rows or another invariant; entry counts alone are insufficient. Conversely,
the expander observation only handles the displayed Cinc compression and is
not a global sparse-enforcer construction.
