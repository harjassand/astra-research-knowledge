# Cycle 3: prior-art attack on hidden-state tails and lineage discrimination

**Verdict as of 2026-10-08.** The broad biological mechanism and the proposed lineage-resolved discriminator are already established for both candidate branches. ecDNA copy-number segregation is a demonstrated source of population heterogeneity and rapid treatment adaptation; a 2026 primary study directly combines clonal barcoding, single-cell DNA sequencing, sister-daughter segregation measurements, and drug perturbation to distinguish static clone selection from active ecDNA reconfiguration. Stress-induced polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs) that later bud or burst into viable, resistant progeny have likewise been directly captured by time-lapse imaging and single-cell cloning. A “higher-cumulant” offspring model is not new branching mathematics: the standard offspring PGF already retains the full offspring distribution. I found no directly matched paper that gives a prospective, finite-sample-calibrated *risk prediction* from such a hidden-state model against an equally informed moment-matched baseline. That narrower endpoint remains a possible project, but the mechanism plus assay is not a novelty claim, and the quantitative bridge remains unvalidated.

The 2026 ecDNA paper is the closest predecessor to the proposed **finite-data discriminator**: it observes clone-linked ecDNA distributions before and after perturbation and directly tests daughter segregation, rather than inferring adaptation from a bulk mean alone. The PGCC papers are the closest predecessor to the **burst discriminator**: they isolate or track single giant cells and record daughter release and colony formation. Those assays already resolve the hidden-state event more directly than an endpoint third-cumulant measurement.

## Exact matched-moment construction: what a cumulant can and cannot buy

Here is a small, exact illustration, not a fit to any tumor. Let $K$ be the number of viable offspring from one predeclared reproduction episode. Compare

\[
P_0(K=0)=\tfrac14,\quad P_0(K=2)=\tfrac34,
\]

with

\[
P_1(K=0)=\tfrac1{20},\quad P_1(K=1)=\tfrac35,\quad
P_1(K=2)=\tfrac3{20},\quad P_1(K=3)=\tfrac15.
\]

Both laws have $\mathbb E[K]=3/2$ and $\mathrm{Var}(K)=3/4$. Their third central cumulants differ: $\kappa_3(P_0)=-3/4$, while $\kappa_3(P_1)=9/20$. Yet their one-founder extinction probabilities differ substantially because the branching PGFs are

\[
f_0(s)=\tfrac14+\tfrac34s^2,\qquad
f_1(s)=\tfrac1{20}+\tfrac35s+\tfrac3{20}s^2+\tfrac15s^3.
\]

The smallest roots of $f_i(q)=q$ are $q_0=1/3$ and

\[
q_1=\frac{\sqrt{65}-7}{8}\approx0.13278.
\]

Thus the survival probabilities are about $0.667$ versus $0.867$ despite identical mean and variance. This confirms that a second-moment-only model can miss a consequential tail difference. It does not make a third cumulant sufficient: the extinction root depends on the whole PGF, and many different hidden-state mixtures can share a chosen finite list of cumulants.

Under the *fully specified toy laws* and perfect episode classification, there is also a finite exact discriminator. $P_0$ never produces odd $K$, while $P_1$ does with probability $0.8$. For $n$ independent, completely observed founder episodes, the test “reject $P_0$ if any $K\in\{1,3\}$” has type-I error zero under this null and power $1-(0.2)^n$ under this alternative; only $n=2$ episodes give 96% power. This is a support test, not a new cumulant method. It depends on a predeclared event window, independent founder episodes, exact daughter counts, and a literal no-odd-offspring null. Missed frames, merged lineages, ordinary multi-division windows, or model uncertainty destroy the zero-error claim. For PGCC biology, direct imaging/isolated-cell cloning is precisely the observation that identifies the event; for ecDNA, barcoded single-cell and paired-daughter assays are the direct observation. A third-moment test on unlabeled bulk counts is weaker and cannot identify which mechanism caused the skew.

## Novelty ruling and defensible remainder

The broad claim—“a stress-hidden tumor state changes offspring tails/cumulants, and a lineage-resolved finite-data assay separates it from ordinary clonal evolution”—should be **rejected as a novelty claim**. Its candidate realizations are not hypothetical: current ecDNA work measures clone-specific reconfiguration under stress, and PGCC work visibly records bursting, delayed release, colony formation, and recurrence-relevant progeny. Standard branching PGFs already convert offspring distributions into extinction probabilities.

What I did not find in this bounded primary-source pass is a paper that simultaneously (a) prospectively freezes a confidence set for the complete clone-state offspring PGF, (b) predicts a defined rebound/establishment endpoint on blinded held-out tumor lineages, (c) gives finite-sample coverage under event-detection and culture heterogeneity, and (d) beats a same-information full-distribution or lineage-tracing baseline at matched sampling and treatment cost. That is a narrow candidate *validation/decision contribution*, not novelty of ecDNA segregation, PGCC bursts, higher moments, lineage tracing, or branching extinction. Priority remains `UNKNOWN`; this is not a clearance search.

A viable discrimination study would preregister either an ecDNA state (`copy-number distribution plus sister-daughter segregation`) or a PGCC state (`ploidy, stress history, and direct daughter-release event`), rather than combining both under one unidentified “hidden-state” label. Estimate the complete event distribution on training lineages, freeze the offspring PGF and a risk endpoint, and compare blinded holdout calibration against an equally informed clone-selection-only model and an unrestricted empirical/full-PGF baseline. Match cell count, effective independent lineages, sequencing depth, assay sensitivity, time-lapse frame rate, stress and washout schedule, culture and organoid replicates, and treatment exposure. Report cell-days, independent founder lineages, failed/censored tracks, FISH/scDNA-seq or ploidy-state calls, barcode reads/molecules, assay latency, and follow-up time separately. A falsifier is a held-out rebound rate outside the frozen prediction interval, disappearance of the cumulant advantage after conditioning on measured ecDNA/PGCC state, or no calibration/prediction gain over the full-information matched baseline. No experiment or risk-model validation was run for this audit.
