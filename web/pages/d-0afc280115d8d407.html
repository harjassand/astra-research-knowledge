PRIMARY-SOURCE / PRIORITY COMPARISON: ALL-MODE SU(2) COMPATIBLE-CHANNEL RESULT
8 October 2026. Focused check requested by /root/classicality.

Target under comparison
-----------------------
`outputs/research/sol_classicality/ALL_MODE_RACAH_PROOF.txt` claims an
internally proved candidate: for n=2J, each nonconstant mode l has

  eta_(n,l)=(-1)^n(n+1){J J l; J J l},
  mu_(n,l)=n!(n+1)!/[(n-l)!(n+l+1)!],
  inf_(n>=1, 1<=l<=n) (1-eta_(n,l))/(1-mu_(n,l))^2 = a_*>0.

Together with the candidate's common-marginal Stinespring inequality
lambda_l^4(1-eta_(n,l)) <= 2(1-lambda_l^2), this gives a dimension-free
squared comparison with one fixed coherent measure-and-prepare channel
Psi_coh for every actually 1-to-2 compatible SU(2)-covariant bistochastic
channel on the same spin space. Its constant is finite but non-effective.
For nonnegative-L2 spectrum the file further claims
||Psi_coh Phi^m-Phi^m||_(infinity->L1,tau) <= sqrt(C/(2m+1)); the EB
approximant is Psi_coh Phi^m, not necessarily Phi^m itself. These are the
candidate file's claims, not independently established literature facts.

1. All SU(2)-covariant channels and Berezin approximations: exact overlap,
   but a different asymptotic interface
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Aschieri, Ruba, Solovej (ARS), “SU(2)-equivariant quantum channels:
semiclassical analysis,” arXiv:2404.04133 (2024; published CMP 405, 298
(2024)), https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04133.

ARS define all equivariant CPTP maps B(H_J)->B(H_K); these form a simplex
with extreme channels Phi_(J,K)^M, M=|K-J|,...,K+J, and every channel is a
unique convex mixture (eq. (1.37), pp. 7-8). Theorem 5, eqs. (1.38)-(1.41),
approximates each extreme channel Phi_(J,K)^(K+i) by
(2J+1)/(2K+1) Op_K H_J^(-i), and every convex-mixture channel by
(2J+1)/(2K+1) Op_K H_J^(lambda). For arbitrary mixtures the stated bound
requires 2J<=K and is proportional to (2J+1)^2/(2K+1); ARS explicitly
interpret this as accurate when J^2<<K (eqs. (1.40)-(1.41) and the
paragraph immediately following). The norm estimate is for every
1<=p<=infinity and rho in B(H_J), with the dimension normalization printed
in the theorem. This is a real all-channel semiclassical/Berezin result,
but K is a large output spin relative to the input J.

The exact edge identity (1.42) is
Phi_(J,K)^(K+J)=(2J+1)/(2K+1) Op_K H_J. At K=J it identifies one simplex
vertex with Op_J H_J, the canonical coherent POVM followed by coherent
state preparation, hence an EB channel. ARS's maps H_J^(-i) are covariant
POVMs and Op_K is positivity-preserving quantization, so the displayed
approximants are measure-and-prepare/EB. Theorem 5 approximants generally
depend on i or on the mixture lambda; the result does not choose a single
fixed coherent decoder for all compatible channels.

Theorem 1, especially eqs. (1.17)-(1.22), gives Berezin-transform
convergence with Laplacian/Casimir regularity on the input. The exact
Berezin eigenvalue in ARS eq. (3.18) is

  (2J)!(2J+1)!/[(2J-l)!(2J+l+1)!],

which is exactly mu_(n,l) after n=2J. This is the sharpest literal
coefficient overlap found. ARS does not define the candidate's eta (the
eigenvalue of its normalized multipole aggregate D_l), does not impose a
two-output common-marginal extension, and states no lower bound on
(1-eta)/(1-mu)^2. Its Sobolev/Casimir smoothing bounds do not imply that
uniform scalar gap. In particular, Theorem 5's all-mixture hypothesis
2J<=K cannot cover a same-spin channel K=J (except the trivial J=0 case).

2. Compatibility/broadcasting and EB reconstruction: no two-copy theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ARS does not formulate channel compatibility or a broadcasting map; the
arXiv paper contains no compatibility/broadcasting result. Its channel
approximation is a one-channel, large-output-spin statement. The fact that
its approximant is EB is not an implication from broadcastability.

Two direct sources cited by ARS clarify the boundary:

* Al Nuwairan, “SU(2)-irreducibly covariant and EPOSIC channels,”
  arXiv:1306.5321, https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.5321. Corollary 3.17 and
  Proposition 4.1 identify the convex simplex/extreme points for channels
  between irreducible SU(2) representations; Corollary 4.2 says a CP
  equivariant map is a scalar multiple of an equivariant channel. These are
  structural channel results only; no common-output extension, coherent
  reconstruction from compatibility, Racah gap, or channel-power claim is
  stated there.
* Christandl, Koenig, Mitchison, Renner, “One-and-a-half quantum de Finetti
  theorems,” arXiv:quant-ph/0602130, https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0602130.
  Theorem II.7' applies to a state on a reference A and n output systems
  (C^d)^tensor n that is permutation-invariant relative to A. After keeping
  k outputs, it is within trace-distance 2 d^2 k/n of a mixture
  integral tau_A^sigma tensor sigma^tensor k dm(sigma); this is separable
  across reference/output. Applied to the Choi state of a many-output
  symmetric channel extension, it gives a dimension- and output-count-
  dependent approximation by separable Choi states. The theorem itself is a
  state-level approximation; it does not provide a trace-preserving EB
  channel approximation in a channel norm. It is not a
  two-copy, dimension-free result: at n=2, k=1 the stated upper bound is
  vacuous for general d. Nor does it select
  the fixed spin-coherent POVM of the target theorem. This is related prior
  machinery, not an implication of ARS or an exact match to the candidate.

3. Uniform diagonal Racah/Berezin gap: only the Berezin half appears
-------------------------------------------------------------------
ARS eq. (3.18) supplies the candidate's mu exactly. Neither ARS Theorem 1
nor Theorem 5 supplies eta, a 6j/Racah coefficient comparison, or the
uniform infimum a_*>0. Searching the full arXiv PDF for “Racah” and “6j”
returns no occurrences. Al Nuwairan's simplex characterization also does
not state this gap. Thus the source overlap does not settle, estimate, or
imply the all-mode scalar gate. The candidate's exact Sturm/frozen-energy
argument is a distinct internal proof route, subject to the target file's
own audit and non-effective-constant limitations.

4. Powers of covariant channels and EB: distinguish one-step from iterates
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
ARS Theorem 5 concerns a single channel as K grows; eq. (1.42) is one
exact EB extreme channel. Theorem 1 concerns Berezin transforms. Neither
states that powers Phi^m become EB or approach EB. Al Nuwairan and the
van Haastrecht source cited by ARS likewise give channel-simplex or
functional-trace/Berezin-limit statements, not a power theorem. Van
Haastrecht, “Limit formulas for the trace of the functional calculus of
quantum channels for SU(2),” arXiv:2402.06054, Theorem 1.1, treats a fixed
input representation and growing auxiliary/output representation, and
proves a trace-functional limit; it does not address compatible marginals
or EB powers.

The target file's (4) is therefore the exact potentially overlapping
claim: under actual compatibility plus nonnegative L2 spectrum, the
iterates are within O(m^-1/2), in its stated infinity-to-normalized-L1
norm, of the EB channels Psi_coh Phi^m. It does not assert Phi^m is itself
EB, and its norm is not a diamond-norm claim. No source checked here gives
that same dimension-free rate and interface.

Priority boundary
-----------------
This was a focused comparison of ARS and its most directly relevant cited
primary sources [Al Nuwairan; Christandl et al.; van Haastrecht], not a
comprehensive novelty search or external proof review. ARS is prior art for
the coherent/Berezin channel approximant and for semiclassical channel
output control; its exact Berezin multiplier matches mu. The sources
checked do not state the compatibility-to-one-fixed-coherent-EB result or
the uniform diagonal Racah ratio needed here. Historical priority of that
bridge and of the target all-mode theorem remains UNKNOWN. This comparison
does not certify novelty.
