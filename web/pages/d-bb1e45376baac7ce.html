# Finite Morse index versus a superexponential threshold state

7 October 2026. Independent reconstruction and obstruction, **not a proof that the negative spectrum is infinite**. The question remains **UNKNOWN** on the exact interface below. The threshold nodal bound and the explicit index-one fixture are proved here; the fixture has polynomial decay and does not answer the superexponential question negatively.

Subsequent same-day gate: CYLINDER_FINITE_INDEX_GATE.md verifies a genuine finite-index superexponential zero state for smooth real symmetric variable coefficients on T²×R, from Davey–Jeznach2607.07639v1. ## Interface and established boundary

Let V be real, smooth, bounded on R^n, with V(x)→0. Let H=−Δ+V, and let a nonzero real smooth u satisfy Hu=0, u,Δu∈L². Thus u∈H². The question is whether the additional bound |u(x)|≤C exp(−c|x|^p), p>1, or C exp(−c|x|log|x|), forces infinitely many negative eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity. These bounds imply decay faster than every fixed exponential. The r15 construction is taken as an independently audited input; this note does not re-audit it.

The essential spectrum is [0,∞). The global nonnegative-form argument already in r15's SPECTRAL_STRUCTURE_FILTER proves at least one negative eigenvalue. It does not prove infinitely many. If the negative spectral subspace has finite dimension m, the quadratic form must be nonnegative outside some compact set: otherwise recursively choose negative compactly supported tests with disjoint supports escaping every previous support; their span is negative definite in every finite dimension.

For smooth bounded V, exterior form nonnegativity is equivalent to the existence of a positive exterior H-harmonic function ψ. This equivalence, and finite Morse index versus positive solution outside a compact set, are classical: Devyver arXiv:1011.3390v2, Lemma1.1, Theorems1.1–1.2. Its Laplacian convention is positive, so its Δ+V is our −Δ+V. The version is 11 March 2011; the retrieved PDF's dynamically rendered front date says May24,2022 and is not a revision date.

The recent Sirakov–Souplet arXiv:2609.15821v1 (14 September 2026), §2.3 Theorem2.10, assumes nonnegativity on the **whole space**. Specializing to constant leading coefficients and no drift gives an exponential sphere-integral lower bound, so the known superexponential states force the bottom of H to be negative. It supplies no finite-index conclusion.

## A threshold Courant bound, with no zero-eigenspace multiplicity loophole

**Claim.** If m<∞, every nonzero real L² zero eigenstate on this interface has at most m+1 nodal domains.

For a nodal domain D, define u_D=u on D and zero elsewhere. The usual Sobolev nodal truncation property gives u_D∈H¹ and ∇u_D=1_D∇u almost everywhere. This holds without assuming smooth nodal boundary: approximate the positive or negative component by truncations away from level zero, then pass in H¹. Weak Hu=0 extends from compact smooth tests to H¹ tests because u∈H¹ and V is bounded. Consequently

q(u_D)=q(u,u_D)=0.

Different nodal pieces have disjoint supports and zero cross-form terms. Suppose there are m+2 domains D_1,…,D_{m+2}. Among the first m+1 pieces choose a nonzero linear combination w orthogonal in L² to the m-dimensional negative spectral subspace. Then q(w)=0. On that spectral complement H is nonnegative; its spectral form representation shows that a zero-form vector is supported at spectral value zero. Hence w lies in ker H, belongs to H², and is a smooth solution. It vanishes throughout D_{m+2}, a nonempty open set. Local unique continuation for bounded smooth V forces w=0 everywhere, contradicting the independent nonzero nodal pieces. Thus there cannot be m+2 domains.

This is a standard Courant/min–max mechanism reconstructed at the threshold, not a novelty claim. It bypasses possible multiplicity of the zero eigenspace by leaving one open domain unused; no 'm plus kernel dimension' estimate is needed.

**What would finish one route:** prove that every superexponentially decaying real threshold eigenstate has infinitely many nodal domains. No such assertion is established here. High local oscillation or infinitely many nodal-set components is not the same as infinitely many globally connected sign domains in n≥3.

## Exact finite-index obstruction to eventual sign

In R³ set

V(x)=−15/(1+|x|²)²,
u(x)=x_1/(1+|x|²)^(3/2),
ψ(x)=(|x|²−1)/(1+|x|²)^(3/2).

Direct differentiation gives Hu=Hψ=0. The state u has ||u||²_L²=π²/4 and lies in H². The potential is smooth, bounded, real and tends to zero. ψ>0 outside the unit ball, giving exterior positivity explicitly. The two nodal domains of u are precisely x_1>0 and x_1<0, and both are unbounded. Every Euclidean sphere centered at zero sees both signs. Thus exterior positivity and finite Morse index do not imply eventual sign or constant-sign inner boundary data for the actual eigenstate.

The Morse index is **exactly one**, as follows without a numerical eigenvalue calculation or CLR constant. Under inverse stereographic projection, the round metric on S³ is g=4(1+|x|²)^−2δ. Write ω=[2/(1+|x|²)]^(1/2), φ=ωf. The conformal Laplacian identity and a direct potential substitution give, for compactly supported smooth φ,

q(φ)=∫_(S³)(|∇_g f|²−3f²) dvol_g.

Indeed the Euclidean Dirichlet term transforms to ∫(|∇_g f|²+3f²/4), while Vω^−4=−15/4. The sharp sphere Poincaré inequality is ∫|∇_g f|²≥3∫f² when ∫f=0. Therefore a subspace on which q is negative definite has dimension at most one: any two-dimensional subspace has a nonzero mean-zero f. Conversely constants can be approximated in H¹(S³) by smooth functions vanishing near the omitted north pole; the point has zero H¹ capacity in dimension three. A cutoff at geodesic radius δ has gradient energy O(δ) and lost volume O(δ³), so its q converges to −3Vol(S³)<0. These cutoffs correspond to compactly supported Euclidean tests. Thus the index is one. Since σess=[0,∞), this is one negative eigenvalue counted with multiplicity.

Under this transformation u/ω is a constant multiple of the first sphere coordinate, whose eigenvalue is three. The two nodal domains attain the preceding m+1 bound. This is the familiar linearized conformal-bubble operator; no novelty is asserted.

The tail of u is polynomial, not exponential or superexponential. This fixture rejects a proposed intermediate implication, **not the universal spectral conjecture**.

## Why the exterior ground-state transform does not close the gap

With a positive exterior solution ψ and v=u/ψ,

div(ψ²∇v)=0,
or Δv+2∇logψ·∇v=0.

Uniform unit-ball Harnack bounds for ψ, together with interior gradient estimates for bounded V, yield exponential upper/lower bounds for ψ along ordinary exterior ball chains and a bounded logarithmic gradient on a smaller exterior domain. Hence v still decays faster than every fixed exponential. This is an exact reduction, but v has arbitrary signed inner-boundary data. It is not a nonnegative solution, and it does not satisfy an exterior zero-Dirichlet condition. In the explicit fixture v=x_1/(|x|²−1), illustrating the signed boundary interface even with exact exterior positivity.

The global argument q(|u|)=0⇒H|u|=0 cannot be repeated on an exterior domain: u restricted there is not in H¹_0, and an inner-boundary form term is present. Kato gives H|u|≤0, a subsolution, not a positive supersolution to which Harnack applies. Finite many global nodal domains also does not supply a unit-radius Harnack chain within any one of them.

A precise conditional geometric statement is available. If an unbounded sign domain contains a chain from a fixed point to points x_j→∞, with overlapping balls of some fixed radius ρ>0, doubled balls staying in that domain, and at most C(1+|x_j|) links, positive-solution Harnack gives |u(x_j)|≥c exp(−C'|x_j|). Thus a superexponential state rules out every such chain. Inferring the existence of one from finite index is the missing geometric lemma, not a consequence of the form calculation.

## Primary-source boundary and next discriminator

Das–Pinchover arXiv:2405.11695v1 (19 May 2024), §6 Theorem6.1 and Remark6.2, explicitly extend their Landis argument to an operator nonnegative outside a compact set **with a constant-sign condition near the inner boundary**. They identify that condition as nontrivial. In their one-point compactification of an exterior domain, approaching the inner boundary also approaches the ideal point; a decay assumption only as Euclidean |x|→∞ is not automatically a boundary decay assumption. These results must not be cited as an unconditional theorem for our signed state.

Current disposition: at least one negative eigenvalue is proved; infinitely many remains UNKNOWN. No exterior-signed theorem, infinite-negative fixture for the actual construction, or finite-index superexponential counterexample was obtained. Search failure is not evidence that the conjecture is open in the literature.

The decisive next step should be one of: (i) construction-specific compact negative-form witnesses at arbitrarily large radii, which immediately prove infinite negative spectrum by the disjoint-support argument; (ii) a proof that the constructed state has infinitely many global nodal domains; or (iii) an exterior signed-solution Landis theorem under finite index that actually addresses the inner-boundary issue. The spectral distinction would tell future unique-continuation research whether real superlocalization inherently requires infinitely many unstable bound states. Until one of these succeeds, do not use finite Morse index as an exclusion hypothesis claimed to be proved.
