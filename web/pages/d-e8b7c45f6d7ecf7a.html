# Reaction inference: what data can certify global or stochastic safety?

## Verdict

Finite low-copy observations cannot certify global permanence or all-time stochastic safety over reaction networks of unbounded order. A one-species counterexample uses the same three positive rate constants in both models: the immigration–death network

\[
\varnothing\xrightarrow{1}X,\qquad X\xrightarrow{4}\varnothing
\]

and that network plus $mX\xrightarrow{1}(m+1)X$, for an unknown $m\ge3$. Below count $m$, their full CTMC generators coincide exactly. The baseline has globally attracting equilibrium $x=1/4$, a $\mathrm{Pois}(1/4)$ stationary count law, and no explosion. The augmented ODE still has a locally asymptotically stable equilibrium near $1/4$, but has finite-time blow-up at high concentrations; its CTMC has positive probability of explosion. Starting at $n_0<m$, $q$ complete trajectory records of duration $T$ have

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_{\mathsf B}^{q,T},P_{\mathsf H_m}^{q,T})\le q\Pr\{\mathrm{Pois}(T)\ge m-n_0\}.
\]

For any finite passive design, choose $m$ large enough that the two cases remain statistically indistinguishable. Exact finite precision cannot overcome exact equality on observed states.

The positive route is to supply a maximum reaction order $N$ and acquire exact transition rates for every transition vector on the count simplex $\mathbb S_N$; an established theorem then gives unique structural recovery. That recovers a generator under its mass-action assumptions; it does not itself decide global deterministic permanence or stochastic all-time safety. Those require separate global certificates.

## Distinguishing observation requirements

For an unknown-order model, one must either (i) justify a finite upper order and obtain the full generator/rate map over enough states, or (ii) run a controlled high-copy intervention that crosses candidate source thresholds and measures event-specific hazards. For the counterexample, preparing/resetting at $n=m$ predicts $+1$ intensity $1$ under the baseline and $1+m!$ under the augmented model. An ideal fixed at-risk exposure $\tau$ gives Poisson counts; exposure

\[
\tau\ge\frac{2\log(1/(2\epsilon))}{(\sqrt{1+m!}-1)^2}
\]

suffices for equal-prior test error \(\le\epsilon\) in that idealized threshold test. A measured excess falsifies the baseline. Failing to see an excess at any finite maximum count cannot rule out a higher hidden order.

For known order $N$ in $d$ species, the exact-data identifiability theorem uses $\binom{N+d}{d}$ states. Laboratory use must pay for each state’s visits or active preparation, event-direction resolution, and intensity estimation precision. The theorem itself is exact-rate-map identifiability; it supplies no universal finite-sample precision bound.

The proof builds a weighted essential-source drift and finite scale-entry chain. It is deterministic; it does not infer the diagram and does not cover jump processes. Exact source: `sources/proofs/d-326696af15b5e02b.html.txt`, especially lines 400–508 and 682–741. The source itself records that no useful size, precision, cutoff, or waiting-time bounds are obtained (lines 1093–1113).

See its card and the exact certificate section at lines 1117–1154 of the N33 source.
- **N35**, molecular-jump safety: supplied integer stoichiometry, rate caps, strict inward polytope/collar, and buffered lattice start yield an explicit exit bound
  \[
  \Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le\sum_i e^{-\theta_iVg_i(X_0)}+2mV\Lambda T e^{-c_*V},
  \]
  hence exponentially small exit over an exponentially long but finite horizon. The proof is in the N33 source, lines 1163–1314. A supplied example shows exit occurs almost surely eventually for every even $V\ge6$ despite that bound (the expanded C04 page, lines 203–205). Thus finite-horizon safety is not infinite-time persistence.
- **N50**, global reaction recognizer: conditional candidate computability result for a promised rational endotactic network. Exact real-closed-field obligations are described, but the general quantifier-elimination compiler is unimplemented and has no complexity bound. Exact source: `sources/proofs/d-70992bc828901f0a.html.txt`, lines 1–80. This is a certificate-acquisition route after the actual model is supplied, not a data-inference theorem.
- **N76/N77**, stochastic recurrence boundary: only a supplied summary was available. N76 source-reports an event-chain upward-potential inequality but explicitly lacks negative physical-time drift/nonexplosion/recurrence. N77 source-reports a deterministic-class attractor with escaping positive-recurrent stationary laws across infinitely many components. Neither original proof/code packet was available, so both remain source-reported and historically unverified; see `sources/proofs/d-14513a2bef6ac3df.html.txt`, lines 150–224.

The hidden-tail construction is deliberately non-endotactic. It does not refute N33. Instead, it identifies the inference boundary: if the measured network is actually endotactic and its finite diagram and global rate box are supplied, N33 provides a deterministic permanence candidate; if graph/order and rate bounds are inferred only from finite observations, the hidden-tail family blocks a uniform conclusion. Even an acquired deterministic absorber does not by itself prove stochastic nonexplosion, positive recurrence, or uniform tightness.

## Primary identifiability result

Enciso, Erban, and Kim, “Identifiability of Stochastically Modelled Reaction Networks,” Theorem 3.1, gives exact generator identifiability under a supplied finite order bound. Its triangular Newton/falling-factorial algorithm reconstructs an order-\(\le N\) network from complete exact rate values on \(\mathbb S_N\), with \(|\mathbb S_N|=\binom{N+d}{d}\). This is a positive exact-data theorem, not a finite noisy-trajectory sample bound. The paper also documents non-identifiability on conservation-restricted state sets.

Primary source: [Enciso–Erban–Kim, arXiv:2006.02272v2, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.02272v2). In particular, Theorem 3.1 and its Newton algorithm appear in §3.1; the known-order identifiability consequence is Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.1; conservation-law restrictions are handled in §3.3.

## Decision-changing falsifier

The clean experimental falsifier is a copy-number ramp beyond a predeclared maximum source order, with direct count and event-direction observation. At a candidate threshold $m$, the hidden model predicts the excess $+1$ hazard $m!$ over the immigration baseline. Observe the hazard conditioned on count $m$, account for volume scaling, and compare against the baseline’s prediction. A statistically resolved excess rejects the baseline mechanism and its global-safety extrapolation. If no high-count intervention or credible order bound is available, finite low-copy data leave the global claim unidentified.
