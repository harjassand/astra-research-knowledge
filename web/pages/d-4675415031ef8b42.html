1. ACTUAL-WEIGHT LOGARITHMIC-CAP INFORMATION UPPER

Let B=B(Y) be any measurable d-by-d real symmetric positive-semidefinite
matrix with E tr B<infinity. No log-concavity, PI, LSI, field-gradient condition,
commutation, or strict positivity is required. Let L>0 be fixed, f_L(x)=Lx/(L+x),
and, conditionally on Y, let Z_T be centered Gaussian with covariance
A_T(B)=I+f_L(TB). Define Var_F U=E||U-EU||_F^2. Then

  integral_0^infinity I(Y;Z_T) dT/T^2
    <=min{G_L E tr B, 2 H_L Var_F(sqrt B)},             (A0)

where G_L=[((1+L)/L)log(1+L)-1]/2<=L/4 and G_L/log L->1/2.
The first branch improves the H_L energy-only corollary below; the variance
branch remains useful for nearly constant fields. Both are included to give
the fixed-reference comparator the same information.

  integral_0^infinity I(Y;Z_T) dT/T^2
    <= 2 H_L Var_F(sqrt B)
    <= 2 H_L E tr B
    <= 2 log(1+L) E tr B,                              (A)

where

  H_L=L integral_0^infinity dt/[(1+t)^3(1+(1+L)t)]
     =((1+L)^2/L^2) log(1+L)-(1+L)/L-1/2.

In particular H_L/log L ->1 as L->infinity. This is an upper theorem for
the original scale weight, and includes changing nullspaces. The previous
dT/T nullspace obstruction must not be imported into this regime.

Proof of the G_L branch. For Q=N(0,I), the mixture information identity gives
I(Y;Z_T)<=E KL(N(0,A_T(B))||Q). The Gaussian divergence is
(1/2)sum_i [f_L(Tb_i)-log(1+f_L(Tb_i))]. For b=0 this summand vanishes. For
b>0, put u=Tb/L and q=1+L. Writing g(u)=Lu/(1+u)-log(1+Lu/(1+u)), integration
by parts (g(u)/u vanishes at zero and infinity) gives

  (b/(2L)) integral g(u) du/u^2
    =(Lb/2) integral du/[(1+u)^2(1+qu)]
    =(b/2)[(q/L)log q-1]=b G_L.

The last integral uses z=(1+u)^(-1) and integral_0^1 z/(q-Lz) dz.
Nonnegative Fubini now proves the first branch of (A0). The elementary bound
f-log(1+f)<=f^2/2 gives G_L<=L/4, so this exact baseline strengthens the
coarser L E tr B/4 reference-divergence bound. It costs no field eigenvectors.

Proof of the variance branch. Let B' be an independent copy. Jensen applied to the mixture density
gives I(Y;Z_T)<=E KL(N(0,A_T(B))||N(0,A_T(B'))). Symmetrize the expectation
and use the elementary Gaussian KL formula:

  I(Y;Z_T)<= (1/4) E tr(A A'^{-1}+A' A^{-1}-2I).

Choose orthonormal eigenbases u_i,v_j for B,B', with eigenvalues b_i,c_j.
Write w_ij=|<u_i,v_j>|^2, a_T(b)=1+f_L(Tb). The trace is exactly

  sum_ij w_ij (a_T(b_i)-a_T(c_j))^2/[a_T(b_i)a_T(c_j)].

This identity is valid without simultaneous diagonalization. Since a_T>=1,
its scalar summand is bounded by
4(sqrt(a_T(b_i))-sqrt(a_T(c_j)))^2. Put b=r^2,c=s^2, r,s>=0.
Cauchy--Schwarz along u between r and s, followed by nonnegative Fubini, gives

  integral (sqrt(a_T(r^2))-sqrt(a_T(s^2)))^2 dT/T^2
    <= |r-s| integral_min(r,s)^max(r,s)
            [integral |partial_u sqrt(a_T(u^2))|^2 dT/T^2] du.

For every u>0, direct substitution t=Tu^2/L evaluates the bracket as H_L.
The single endpoint u=0 has measure zero and causes no problem. Thus this
integral is at most H_L(r-s)^2. The eigenbasis sum of (sqrt(b_i)-sqrt(c_j))^2
is ||sqrt B-sqrt B'||_F^2. Combining the factor 4 with the factor 1/4 and
E||U-U'||^2=2 Var_F U proves the first inequality in (A).

The exact H_L formula follows by z=(1+t)^(-1), r=L/(1+L):
H_L=r integral_0^1 z^2/(1-rz) dz
   =[log(1+L)-r-r^2/2]/r^2.
Dropping two powers of 1+t in the denominator yields
H_L<=L integral dt/[(1+t)(1+(1+L)t)]=log(1+L).
Finally E||sqrt B||_F^2=E tr B and subtracting the mean decreases this energy.
All steps can be first performed on finite intervals and then passed by
monotone convergence, so no prior integrability of mutual information is used.

The frozen c09_s01 rare-field construction has E tr B=1 and lower integrated

2. COVARIANCE-WHITENED SUSPENSION REFINEMENT

Fix d>=2 and assume that every isotropic log-concave nu in every finite
dimension satisfies ||kappa_(d+1)^nu(u,.,...,.)||_HS^2<=b||u||^2.
For an isotropic regular mu with dmu=e^-V dx and Hess V>=aI, a>0,
the root refinement is valid:

  ||T_d f||_HS^2 <= b Var_mu(f)/(d!)^2                  (B)

for every f in L2(mu). Here T_d f=(1/d!) nabla_z^d E_mu_z f at z=0.
The factor 2 obtained by splitting linear and orthogonal parts is unnecessary.
This conclusion is conditional on the cumulant hypothesis, not a KLS proof.

Independent audit. First take smooth f with bounded Hessian, Ef=0, Ef^2=1,
without assuming E[Xf]=0. Set sigma^2=1+2/beta^2 and
S=(N^(-1/2) sum_i f(X_i)+eta_beta)/sigma, where Xi are iid mu and eta_beta
is independent centered Laplace noise of variance 2/beta^2. The joint negative
log density is the maximum of two functions with X-block Hessians
Hess V plus/minus beta Hess f/sqrt N and affine S dependence. It is convex
when N>=(beta||Hess f||_infinity/a)^2. This argument does not use E[Xf]=0.

Let Y=(S,X_1,...,X_N), v=E[Xf], c=(v,...,v)/(sigma sqrt N). Its covariance is

  C=[[1,c^T],[c,I_(Nn)]],    ||c||^2=||v||^2/sigma^2<1.

The inequality ||v||<=1 follows from isotropy and the L2 norm of f. Strict
inequality follows from the positive independent noise. Hence C is positive
definite and Z=C^(-1/2)Y is isotropic log-concave. For each X-coordinate basis
vector e_j, set h_j=C^(1/2)e_j; these form an orthonormal family because their
Gram matrix is the X-block of C, exactly identity. Also h_0=C^(1/2)e_S has
norm one. The vector h_0 need not be orthogonal to the h_j: the universal
slice bound permits any first vector and does not require that orthogonality.

Multilinear cumulant covariance gives
kappa_Y(e_S,e_j1,...,e_jd)=kappa_Z(h_0,h_j1,...,h_jd).
Restricting the last d inputs to an orthonormal family cannot increase the
Hilbert--Schmidt norm: extend it to a full orthonormal basis and drop only
nonnegative squared coordinates. Thus their entire squared sum is <=b.

Direct differentiation of the joint log-Laplace transform in the S coordinate
gives (1/(sigma sqrt N)) sum_i F_f(z_i). The mixed derivative with d X
indices is zero unless all d copies coincide, and then equals
(d!/(sigma sqrt N))(T_d f)_(j1,...,jd). The N copies cancel the 1/N, giving

  (d!)^2 ||T_d f||_HS^2/sigma^2 <= b.

For every beta choose a finite qualifying N. Let beta tend to infinity;
the left tensor and mu are fixed, so no infinite-dimensional convergence
or limiting whitened measure is needed. This proves (B) for smooth f.

Extension detail: centered smooth compact-support functions are dense in
centered L2(mu); they have bounded Hessians and may be normalized. For each
fixed tensor coefficient, T_d f is the inner product of f with a fixed
degree-d polynomial (obtained by differentiating normalized tilts). Regular
strong log-concavity supplies finite moments of all orders, so that polynomial
lies in L2. Coefficients therefore converge under L2 approximation. The
finite-dimensional tensor inequality passes to the limit. Subtracting a
constant leaves T_d unchanged, yielding Var f. This spells out the density
and continuity step rather than appealing to a tensor-compiler oracle.

For linear f=<u,x>, T_d f=kappa_(d+1)^mu(u)/(d!). Thus the reverse comparison
of optimal universal constants holds on the same class. To equate constants
over all isotropic log-concave measures, the class-extension can be explicit.
Restrict the density to a ball of radius R and normalize. Its fixed-order
moments converge as R grows, since log-concave probability laws have all fixed
moments. For fixed R convolve with N(0,tI), then multiply the positive density
by exp(-epsilon ||x||^2/2) and normalize. The convolved negative log-density
has Hessian between zero and t^-1 I: differentiating the Gaussian convolution
gives Hess V=t^-1 I-t^-2 Cov(X|Y), and log-concavity gives the lower bound.
The added quadratic supplies lower bound epsilon I and upper bound
(t^-1+epsilon)I. Higher density derivatives are controlled by conditional
moments of the bounded input. Let t,epsilon tend to zero for each R and take
a diagonal sequence in R. Center and multiply by the covariance inverse
square root, which tends to identity; all fixed-order moments and cumulants
converge. Applying the regular linear-function bound then passing to the limit
gives the cumulant bound for each general isotropic log-concave law. Thus
the optimal universal constants agree after the factorial, subject to the
same standard definition of full-dimensional log-concave probability measure.
This is an analytic approximation, not a numerically acquired regularizer.

3. COSTS AND LEARNING BOUNDARY

Write H=||Hess f||_infinity. To get sigma^2<=1+delta, choose beta^2>=2/delta;
then choose N>=max(1,ceiling(2H^2/(a^2 delta))). The suspension draws Nn scalars
and evaluates f N times; it does not acquire f. Dimension is Nn+1. If
||E Xf||=1, lambda_min(C)=1-1/sigma is Theta(beta^-2), so whitening condition
number is Theta(beta^2) and ||C^-1/2|| is Theta(beta). An explicit whitening
also requires the vector E Xf to its chosen precision; no sampling guarantee
for that vector is supplied by the purely analytic proof. A rank-two block
formula makes application O(Nn), but does not make moment acquisition free.

T_d has n^d ordered entries, or binomial(n+d-1,d) independent symmetric
coefficients. A dimension-free operator norm does not bound this multiplicity.
The Gaussian quadratic example below makes the acquisition cost quantitative.
It is established quadratic regression structure, not a newly discovered
learning method. It audits what cannot be obtained for free from (B).

For X~N(0,I_n), unknown symmetric A with ||A||_F<=1, and actual observed label
Y=tr(A(XX^T-I))+eta, eta~N(0,sigma_eta^2), define f_A(X)=tr(A(XX^T-I)).
Then Ef_A=0, Var f_A=2||A||_F^2, Hess f_A=2A, and T_2 f_A=A. Thus the
dimension-free Gaussian operator bound coexists with M=n(n+1)/2 response modes.
Given K iid labeled samples, the moment estimator

  Ahat=(1/(2K)) sum_r Y_r (X_r X_r^T-I)

is unbiased and has exact mean squared Frobenius error

 [(2n^2+18n+28)||A||_F^2+8(tr A)^2+sigma_eta^2 n(n+1)]/(4K).  (C)

To check (C), orthogonally diagonalize A, set U=sum_i(X_i^2-1), and use
E Z_i^2=2, E Z_i^3=8, E Z_i^4=60. The matrix squared norm is
n^2-n+(2n-2)U+U^2. For f=sum a_i Z_i,
Ef^2=2||A||^2, Ef^2U=8||A||^2, and
Ef^2U^2=(4n+48)||A||^2+8(tr A)^2. Subtracting the squared estimator mean
contributes -4||A||^2, giving the numerator. Noise contributes n(n+1).

For unit noise, any estimator on the Frobenius unit ball has minimax error
at least c min(1,M/K). Use an orthonormal symmetric-matrix basis E_i and
the hypercube A_theta=a sum_i theta_i E_i with
a=min(M^-1/2,(4 sqrt K)^-1). Neighbors have KL=4Ka^2 and TV<=a sqrt(2K)
<=sqrt(2)/4. Any estimated coefficient of the wrong sign has squared error
at least a^2. Pairing neighbors therefore lower-bounds the prior error by
M a^2(1-sqrt(2)/4)/2, which is at least a positive constant times min(1,M/K).
The model and noise interface are supplied and actual labels are charged;
a sample/evaluation oracle has not been inferred from the KLS theorem.

An estimator reads K(n+1) real observations, uses O(Kn^2) arithmetic and
O(n^2) working storage. Its theorem is ideal-real; finite precision, Gaussian
digitization and unknown-model discovery are not certified by the formula.
The separate finite-digital Gibbs compiler closes an actual conditional-
sampler gate for an admitted known-model class, with all such costs explicit.
