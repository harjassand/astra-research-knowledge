0. Exact target and result
==========================
The question is whether a cosmological linear metric response attributed to modified gravity can instead be produced by Einstein gravity plus an actual local healthy finite-field dark action, while holding fixed the physical metric seen by known matter and the full tensor response. An effective definition T_dark=M^2 G-T_known is not an action realization.

This record declares a broad but specific action class. It proves an obstruction for every finite field count in that class, and gives a fully specified stable causal f(R) response that violates it. The theorem is not a classification of all possible local dark-sector actions. Its essential restriction is a first-derivative scalar Lagrangian, with internal rotational singlets/triplets and positive spatial principal energy.

The exclusion is stronger than the previous single-solid closure: arbitrary cross-coupled clocks, entropy variables, multiple material-coordinate triplets, and all their scalar initial modes are allowed. No scalar response pole analysis, quasistatic limit, positive lower sound speed, or removal of hidden initial data is needed for the exact exclusion. The central identity is

  mu_T = C_z(b_x,b_x) >= 0,
  Pi_dark = 2 i p C_z(b_x,w_x),
  |Pi_dark|^2 <= 4 p^2 mu_T C_z(w_x^*,w_x).                 (0.1)

Here C_z is the spatial principal energy form, b_i are background material gradients, w_z is the scalar field displacement with w_x its rotated transverse copy, p=k/a, and Pi multiplies Q^i_j=n^i n_j-delta^i_j/3. Therefore mu_T=0 implies Pi_dark=0 for every scalar perturbation. The tensor equation is

  h_ij''+2 Hcal h_ij'+(k^2+2 a^2 mu_T/M^2)h_ij
                   =2 a^2 Pi_known,ij^TT/M^2.             (0.2)

Full GR tensor matching, including constant kinetic normalization M^2 and its coupling to a calibrated known tensor source, requires mu_T=0. Merely observing c_GW=1 does not do so.

1. Declared healthy local action class
====================================
Take

 S = integral sqrt(-g) [M^2 R/2+P(alpha^r,B^{AB})] d^4x
                                                  +S_known[g,known],
 B^{AB}=g^{mu nu} partial_mu phi^A partial_nu phi^B,
 phi^A=(alpha^r,phi^{aI}), I=1,2,3,

where r=1,...,s and a=1,...,R, with arbitrary finite s,R. All fields are spacetime scalars. The alpha^r are internal rotational singlets. Each phi^{aI} is an internal rotational triplet; a common internal O(3) acts on all I. P is an arbitrary smooth invariant function, may depend explicitly on the singlets, and is independent of the material-coordinate phi^{aI} themselves (independent shifts preserve background homogeneity). All cross-couplings through B are allowed. The physical known matter is minimally coupled to the same g as in both compared descriptions. No direct dark-known matter coupling, derivative of the metric, curvature in P, higher scalar derivatives, auxiliary constraints with singular kinetic matrices, spin-2 internal field multiplets, or kinetic-theory continuum is included.

On a flat FRW background,

  alpha^r=alpha^r(t),  phi^{aI}=lambda_a x^I,

where lambda_a are constant, and t is cosmic time. The background is isotropic. In an instantaneous orthonormal frame at any event,

  q^A=partial_0 phi^A,  b_i^A=partial_i phi^A,
  q^{aI}=0, b_i^r=0, b_i^{aI}=beta_a delta_i^I,
  beta_a=lambda_a/a.

Let P_AB=dP/dB^{AB} and H_AB,CD=d^2P/(dB^{AB}dB^{CD}), using the symmetric-matrix differential convention dP=P_AB dB^{AB}; repeated symmetric entries are summed over all ordered A,B. Write P(u,v)=P_AB u^A v^B and H(U,V)=H_AB,CD U^{AB} V^{CD}. For any spatial unit vector n, define

 S_n(w)=b_n tensor w+w tensor b_n,
 C_n(u,v)=-2P(u,v)-H(S_n(u),S_n(v)).                     (1.1)

The fixed-metric principal quadratic Lagrangian is

 L_2=1/2 dot(pi)^T K dot(pi)+dot(pi)^T D_i partial_i pi
                                  -1/2 partial_i pi^T C_ij partial_j pi,
 K(u,v)=-2P(u,v)+H(S_0(u),S_0(v)),
 S_0(w)=q tensor w+w tensor q,
 D_n(u,v)=-H(S_0(u),S_n(v)).                            (1.2)

The restrictions called healthy in this theorem are K>0 and C_n>=0 for every spatial direction, with a hyperbolic causal principal system whose characteristic speeds are real and at most one, and no instability in the remaining lower-order terms in the admitted background/scale range. The proof below only needs C_n>=0, so it remains valid in a larger algebraic class. Zero-speed endpoints are allowed at this mathematical level; they may require an additional strong-coupling audit before physical admission. No claim about a UV completion is made.

The positive-energy condition is explicit: with canonical momentum p_pi=K dot(pi)+D_i partial_i pi, the principal Hamiltonian is

 H_2=1/2 (p_pi-D_i partial_i pi)^T K^{-1}
                         (p_pi-D_j partial_j pi)
                         +1/2 partial_i pi^T C_ij partial_j pi.

Thus K>0 and C_n>=0 ensure a nonnegative principal energy even in the presence of clock/material derivative mixing. A stable but indefinite-Hamiltonian exotic system is not covered by this hypothesis. All calculations concern the original local fields before integrating out any of them; an effective nonlocal scalar kernel cannot conceal a negative principal energy.

2. Derive the tensor contact coefficient
=======================================
Use signature (-,+,+,+). Variation of the action gives the mixed stress

  T^i_j=P delta^i_j-2P_AB g^{i mu} partial_mu phi^A partial_j phi^B. (2.1)

Let h_ij be a dimensionless traceless spatial metric perturbation in the orthonormal frame. A cross component h_xz is sufficient to identify the isotropic shear coefficient. It can be a transverse tensor polarization for a wave propagating along y; the contact coefficient is independent of the wave direction. Its first variation is

  delta B = -h_xz S_z(b_x),
  delta(g^{i mu} partial_mu phi^A partial_j phi^B)|_xz
                                                    =-h_xz b_x^A b_x^B.

Therefore, keeping the fields fixed,

  delta T^x_z
    = [2P(b_x,b_x)+H(S_z(b_x),S_z(b_x))] h_xz
    = -C_z(b_x,b_x) h_xz.                                (2.2)

The factor in the Hessian follows because
 H(b_x tensor b_z,S_z(b_x))=H(S_z(b_x),S_z(b_x))/2.
The isotropic background pressure term in the covariant stress is already removed by using mixed T^i_j; a cosmological constant has zero mixed traceless stress and is not a graviton mass.

No singlet/triplet field fluctuation carries a tensor helicity at linear order. Equivalently, a TT metric produces delta B in the spin-2 part of the triplet-pair space, whereas the linear field source in the triplet EOM contains a divergence partial_i h_ij and in the singlet EOM a trace. Both vanish. There is no hidden tensor oscillator available to cancel (2.2). It follows that the complete dark tensor response is the algebraic contact law

  (delta T^i_j)_TT=-mu_T h^i_j,  mu_T=C_z(b_x,b_x).         (2.3)

Rotational invariance makes the same mu_T apply to both TT polarizations and every direction. The Einstein kinetic action has coefficient M^2/8, unchanged by P; the tensor field equation is (0.2), and m_T^2=2mu_T/M^2. C_z>=0 implies mu_T>=0.

For a single standard solid, C_z(b_x,b_x)=mu and this reduces to the known shear-modulus mass term. The derivation here does not add independent-component shear moduli; it uses the full cross-coupled matrix and therefore includes possible attempted cancellations between species.

3. Derive scalar anisotropic stress and the exact invariant
==========================================================
Work in Poisson gauge,

 ds^2=a^2[-(1+2Psi)d tau^2+(1-2Phi)delta_ij dx^i dx^j].

For a Fourier mode with physical momentum p along z, a scalar triplet fluctuation is

  pi^{aI}=delta_z^I w_a,
  w_z=(0,...,0;w_a delta_z^I),
  w_x=(0,...,0;w_a delta_x^I).

The singlet scalar fluctuations are allowed independently. They do not contribute to traceless spatial stress: their explicit P variations and time derivatives are rotational singlets, as are the lapse and spatial trace metric perturbations. The spatial derivative of the longitudinal triplets gives delta B=i p S_z(w_z). Direct variation of (2.1) yields

  Pi_dark := delta T^z_z-delta T^x_x
     =-4 i p P(b_z,w_z)
      -2 i p H(b_z tensor b_z-b_x tensor b_x,S_z(w_z)).     (3.1)

There is no division by a transfer amplitude, enthalpy, background time derivative, modulus, or displacement. The k=0 mode is excluded because Q^i_j is then undefined.

The required rotational identities are

 P(b_z,w_z)=P(b_x,w_x),
 H(b_z tensor b_z-b_x tensor b_x,S_z(w_z))
                                      =H(S_z(b_x),S_z(w_x)). (3.2)

For completeness, the second identity is a finite-dimensional representation argument. On the triplet-pair space, H preserves the decomposition into spatial spin 0,1,2. The first argument on the left is spin 2. The spin-2 projection of S_z(w_z) has species factor beta_a w_b+w_a beta_b and spatial tensor e_zz-I/3. The spin-2 projection of S_z(w_x) has the same species factor and spatial tensor (e_xz+e_zx)/2. S_z(b_x) has species factor beta_a beta_b and spatial tensor e_xz+e_zx. The invariant bilinear on each spin-2 multiplicity is the spatial Frobenius inner product times an arbitrary species matrix. Its spatial contractions are both one:

 tr[(e_zz-e_xx)(e_zz-I/3)]=1,
 tr[(e_xz+e_zx)(e_xz+e_zx)/2]=1.

Spin-0 and spin-1 projections cannot couple to the first spin-2 argument. This proves (3.2) for any number of triplets and arbitrary invariant cross-couplings. Singlet-triplet terms cannot contribute a spin-2 tensor. The same identities hold if the invariant species matrices are singular.

Insert (3.2) into (3.1):

  Pi_dark=2 i p C_z(b_x,w_x).                              (3.3)

For a positive semidefinite real form C, C(u,u)=0 implies C(u,v)=0 for every v: otherwise the polynomial C(u+t v,u+t v)=2t C(u,v)+t^2 C(v,v) is negative for one sufficiently small sign of t. Applied to (2.3),(3.3), this proves

  EXACT EXCLUSION THEOREM.
  For every admitted finite action, background, k!=0, and scalar solution,
  full GR tensor response with the same M^2 and known-matter coupling
  implies Pi_dark=0.                                     (3.4)

No choice of adiabatic/isocurvature displacement, entropy, initial momentum, primordial mixture, retarded pole, or field count defeats (3.4). All scalar solutions obey the same pointwise algebra. If the transverse spatial form is strictly positive, mu_T=0 also forces b_x=0; hence every nonzero material gradient requires a nonzero tensor mass term in that stricter class. If transverse zero speeds are allowed, a perfect-fluid limit remains possible but still has zero scalar anisotropic stress.

The positive-form Cauchy-Schwarz inequality also gives

 |Pi_dark|^2 <=4 p^2 mu_T C_z(w_x^*,w_x).                  (3.5)

This is a sharp quantitative coefficient-level inequality; saturation occurs when the two vectors are collinear modulo null directions. It says an attempted small tensor response with fixed scalar shear requires a large transverse deformation energy. It is not, by itself, an observation-only finite-error bound because C_z(w_x^*,w_x) is a hidden-field quantity.

After known scalar anisotropic stress is removed, the traceless Einstein equation is

 -M^2 p^2 (Phi-Psi)=Pi_dark.                              (3.6)

Consequently exact GR tensor response requires zero residual scalar slip. Known photons/neutrinos/free-streaming species must be included in the subtraction; ordinary pressureless dust has Pi_known=0. Phi and Psi are the Bardeen potentials fixed by Poisson gauge. The result is a stress/action consistency invariant, not the assertion that every nonzero slip establishes modified gravity.

 S_f= M_0^2/2 integral sqrt(-g) f(R) d^4x+S_known[g,known],

 f(R_*)=18H_*^2,

Set M^2=M_0^2 F_*=3M_0^2 in the comparison Einstein action. This matches the measured tensor kinetic normalization, rather than merely dividing its equation by a constant.  h_ij''+2Hcal h_ij'+k^2 h_ij
                              =2a^2 Pi_known,ij^TT/M^2.  (4.3)

This matches both GR propagation and its linear source coupling, with m_T=0.

 S_f= M_0^2/2 integral sqrt(-g)[F R-U(F)] +S_known,

5. Conserved physical source and retarded scalar response
========================================================
Take a weak positive comoving distribution of ordinary dust as initial data at a finite conformal time tau_i<0. Write its source strength epsilon explicitly:

 T_known^{mu nu}=epsilon rho_s(x) a^{-3} ubar^mu ubar^nu
                                                    +O(epsilon^2),
 ubar^mu=(1/a,0,0,0) in conformal coordinates.

On the de Sitter background this is separately conserved at O(epsilon): the energy equation is rho'+3Hcal rho=0 and its momentum is zero. The dust acceleration caused by its O(epsilon) metric enters its stress only at O(epsilon^2), since the unperturbed dust density vanishes. There is no artificial spacetime creation step or unconserved imposed force. The evolution domain begins at the initial Cauchy slice; the dust is already present there. A positive compact distribution has a k=0 perturbation as well as nonzero Fourier modes. Linear Fourier sectors decouple, so the homogeneous O(epsilon) correction does not affect the nonzero-mode calculation. This is perturbation theory about de Sitter, not a claim that the exact sourced nonlinear geometry stays de Sitter.

For any nonzero Fourier mode write delta rho(t,k)=epsilon rho_s(k)a^{-3}. The f(R) trace equation is

 3 Box F+F R-2f=T_known/M_0^2,
 (Box-m_s^2)delta F=T_known/(3M_0^2),
 delta F''+2Hcal delta F'+(k^2+2a^2H_*^2)delta F
                                      =a^2 delta rho/(3M_0^2). (5.1)

In exact de Sitter a=-1/(H_* tau) and a''/a=2a^2 H_*^2. Therefore chi=a delta F satisfies the constant-coefficient forced oscillator

 chi''+k^2 chi=epsilon rho_s(k)/(3M_0^2).                  (5.2)

Specify zero dark scalar Cauchy data delta F(tau_i)=delta F'(tau_i)=0. Equivalently chi(tau_i)=chi'(tau_i)=0. The retarded solution is exactly

 delta F(tau,k)=epsilon rho_s(k)/(3M_0^2 k^2 a)
                         [1-cos k(tau-tau_i)].           (5.3)

Both homogeneous scalar constants have been fixed, rather than silently fitted. If nonzero initial scalar data are allowed, add a^{-1}[A_k cos k(tau-tau_i)+B_k sin k(tau-tau_i)]; the source response (5.3) is the zero-initial part. The P-class exclusion (3.4) covers all of those possible initial modes and does not depend on identifying (5.3) in passive cosmological data.

The metric scalar potentials can be obtained exactly using g_E=(F/F_*)g. The Einstein-frame background scalar is constant at an extremum of its potential, so its scalar stress has no linear perturbation. Because the known dust has zero background, the change of its stress under the perturbative conformal factor is O(epsilon^2). With normalization M^2=3M_0^2, its linear Einstein-frame potentials satisfy

 V:=Phi_E=Psi_E=-epsilon rho_s(k)/(2M^2 k^2 a),
 V'+Hcal V=0,
 k^2 V=-a^2 delta rho/(2M^2).                            (5.4)

The conformal transformation of the metric gives

 Phi=V+delta F/(2F_*), Psi=V-delta F/(2F_*),
 Phi-Psi=delta F/F_*,
 Phi+Psi=2V.                                            (5.5)

Thus, letting c=cos k(tau-tau_i),

 Psi=V (4-c)/3,
 Phi=V (2+c)/3,
 eta:=Phi/Psi=(2+c)/(4-c).                               (5.6)

These are exact first-order de Sitter source-transfer formulas, without a quasistatic approximation. At tau=tau_i+pi/(2k), if this lies below zero,

 eta=1/2,  Psi=(4/3)V, Phi=(2/3)V, Phi+Psi=2V,
 m_T=0 and tensor source coupling=1/M^2.                 (5.7)

The unscaled source amplitude cancels in eta. All perturbations can be made arbitrarily weak by reducing epsilon, so this effect does not require leaving linear theory. Choose k/a below the EFT cutoff throughout the finite interval and k|tau_i|>pi/2; a subhorizon finite experiment can satisfy both conditions. A full conformal-period average of aPhi and aPsi, if the entire period fits in the domain, gives the same 2/3 and 4/3 factors exactly because the cosine averages to zero. Averaging the ratio eta itself does not give 1/2 and is not claimed.

Equations (4.3),(5.6) violate (3.4) whenever c!=1 and rho_s(k)!=0. Hence there exists a stable causal, finite-field local modified-gravity source response with exactly GR-normalized tensors and known-matter coupling that no healthy member of the declared P class can reproduce, regardless of its number of clocks/solids, constitutive cross-couplings, retarded scalar poles, or scalar initial data. This is an action-class exclusion, not an effective-T rewrite.

6. Actual operational content and limitations
============================================
An ideal comparison requires the physical background H, tensor kinetic normalization and coupling to a calibrated tensor source, full tensor propagation including its algebraic mass/contact term, and the two scalar Bardeen potentials with known scalar anisotropic stress subtracted. Measuring the tensor speed alone cannot determine mu_T. The target example's finite scalar slip can in principle be accessed by ordinary test-particle acceleration (Psi) and light deflection/lensing (Phi+Psi). Physical tracers respond geodesically to the same g; an arbitrarily dilute separate tracer population can read the O(epsilon) force without affecting the source at that order.

For passive cosmology, the invariant Pi_dark=0 is easier to identify than a general retarded kernel: it holds for every solution, so unidentified primordial mixtures cannot create a nonzero slip within the class. There is still a real inverse problem in reconstructing both potentials, their matched Fourier/time modes, ordinary relativistic anisotropic stress, and the tensor mass response. RSD, lensing projections, source calibration, selection effects and noise are not eliminated by this theorem. No observed data, finite-error forecast, or present experimental exclusion has been supplied.

An exact tensor zero is an ideal response statement. A finite upper bound mu_T<=mu_max does not by itself give a model-independent finite upper bound on scalar slip: (3.5) also needs an upper bound on the hidden deformation energy. If independently one supplies C_z(w_x^*,w_x)<=E_max, then

 |Phi-Psi|<=2 sqrt(mu_max E_max)/(M^2 p),                  (6.1)

after known stress subtraction. If one instead supplies a lower scalar gradient bound C_scalar>=c_min^2 K on the longitudinal triplet sector, an upper transverse speed bound C_transverse<=K, and a principal scalar perturbation energy budget epsilon_pert, then

 C_z(w_x^*,w_x)<=2 epsilon_pert/(p^2 c_min^2),
 |Pi_dark|^2<=8 mu_max epsilon_pert/c_min^2,
 |Phi-Psi|<=sqrt(8mu_max epsilon_pert)/(c_min M^2 p^2).    (6.2)

These additional inputs are not contained in distances, lensing, growth identities or tensor-speed data. They must not be advertised as observations. The exact zero theorem needs none of them.

The restriction to singlet/triplet matter is deliberate. Internal spin-2 scalar multiplets can carry tensor helicities and a dynamical tensor stress kernel; vectors/tensors, curvature couplings, constrained higher-derivative theories, dissipation and loop continua require a new proof. In particular kinetic-gravity braiding is a healthy curvature-free second-derivative matter action with EH tensors and unsuppressed scalar Poisson feedback, so the earlier first-derivative high-k transparency idea is not a theorem about all local dark sectors. KGB alone has zero linear anisotropic stress on a homogeneous clock background and does not contradict (3.4), but it makes the ontology line beyond this action class ambiguous.

The exact theorem concerns quadratic/linear health and the admitted EFT range, not a nonlinear stability proof or UV completion. It does not prove modified gravity from nonzero slip unless the declared dark-sector class is independently justified. Its supported claim is that the broad finite P media class is not closed under all healthy GR-tensor scalar responses.
