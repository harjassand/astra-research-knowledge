# Identifiability of a hidden positive-definite biquadratic frame

7 October2026. **Independent root reconstruction PASS** in the universal-identifiability section of `notes/root_cycle9/HOMOGENEOUS_ACQUISITION_GATE.md`. A rational metric is not supplied or computed for this uniqueness argument. No historical novelty claimed or external validation asserted.

Suppose the same real quartic f has two representations

    f(x)=((Lx)^2)^T Q((Lx)^2)
        =((Kx)^2)^T R((Kx)^2),

with real invertible L,K and symmetric Q,R positive definite. Then their unordered primal coordinate rays agree, so K differs from L by invertible monomial row action. This is identifiability up to permutation and nonzero coordinate scaling, not numerical stability or polynomial-time acquisition.

Each representation supplies a coordinate sign-reflection group preserving f. Since f>0 away from zero and is homogeneous degree4, its full real linear stabilizer is compact. Averaging any SPD form over the compact subgroup generated by both sign groups yields a common SPD metric M invariant under both. Equivalently, the invariant-metric existence argument via the covariance of the body f<=1 supplies such an M. M is used only in the proof.

Sign invariance forces

    M=L^T B L=K^T C K,

for positive diagonal B,C. Therefore both primal frames are mutually orthogonal in M. Whitening M gives two orthonormal frames in which f=H(m) with a PD squared contrast. Diagonal positive normalization preserves positive definiteness of the contrasts.

For a PD contrast G and simplex point m,

    sum_i m_i G_ii-m^T Gm
      =sum_(i<j)m_i m_j(G_ii+G_jj-2G_ij)>0

whenever m is not a vertex. Thus every global maximizer of f on the M-unit sphere belongs to the axis set of either representation. Such a maximizer exists, so at least one ray is common to both frames.

Restrict f to the M-orthogonal complement of a common ray. Both representations retain the remaining orthogonal axes and their PD principal contrast matrices. Repeat in dimension n-1. The base n=1 is immediate. Induction proves all rays agree.

Consequently an unconditional exact verifier of *some* even QPD representation also certifies its axes are the original promised axes under QPD. This removes the previous original-frame uniqueness caveat from the metric-free algorithm. It does not certify hidden scales, ordering or any particular Q before normalization.

The coercivity/PD hypotheses are load-bearing. Under merely strictly convex restriction to the simplex, f=(x^2-y^2)^2 has Q=[[1,-1],[-1,1]] with positive pairwise contrast4, but rational Lorentz matrices

    L_t=[[(1+t^2)/(1-t^2),2t/(1-t^2)],
         [2t/(1-t^2),(1+t^2)/(1-t^2)]],

for rational t!=+/-1 preserve x^2-y^2 and give infinitely many distinct rational axis frames producing exactly the same f with the same strict simplex contrast. Thus recovering an *originally chosen* hidden frame in this wider class without additional supplied structure is information-theoretically impossible. Finding one valid representation is a different, still meaningful task. The full real stabilizer there is noncompact, so the common-SPD argument does not apply.
