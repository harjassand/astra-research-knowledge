## Reconstruction: robust nearest-neighbor molecular theorem

Fix m>=1, even V>=2m, and 0<kappa<=K. Consider

    mA -> (m-1)A+B,       mB -> A+(m-1)B,

with A+B=V, initial A=V/2, and arbitrary predictable (including
history-dependent) controls k_A,k_B in [kappa,K]. Write J=A. The exact
factorial intensities are

    b_j=k_B V^(1-m) (V-j)_m,    d_j=k_A V^(1-m) (j)_m,

where (x)_m=x(x-1)...(x-m+1). The closed communicating interval is
I={m-1,...,V-m+1}. The total rate is bounded by a constant times KV, so
the finite controlled chain cannot explode.

The extremal static chains are J^-=(k_A=K,k_B=kappa) and
J^+=(k_A=kappa,k_B=K). Common-clock thinning gives a pathwise coupling
J^-<=J<=J^+: at a meeting state, births of the lower chain can be embedded
among controlled births, and controlled deaths among deaths of the lower
chain; use the reversed inclusions for the upper chain. Nearest-neighbor
jumps cannot cross an order gap without meeting. This remains valid for
predictable controls because the thinning rates are chosen from the current
joint history and preserve each marginal.

For J^-, detailed balance gives

    pi^-(j) proportional W_j,
    W_j = (kappa/K)^j product_{r=0}^{m-1} binom(V-2r,j-r),
    W_{j+1}/W_j = (kappa/K) (V-j)_m/(j+1)_m.

For m<=L<V/2, Q={L,...,V-L}, and

    R_V = sum_{j=m-1}^L W_j / sum_{j=m-1}^{V/2} W_j,

the exact uniform-time lower-tail bound follows by starting a stationary J^-
chain conditioned on Y_0<=V/2. This conditioned state is always <=V/2;
order coupling yields

    P_{V/2}(J^-_t<=L)
      <= P(Y_t<=L | Y_0<=V/2)
      <= pi^-([m-1,L])/pi^-([m-1,V/2]) = R_V.

Reflection gives the matching upper-tail bound for J^+. Hence, for every
admissible controller and t>=0, P(J_t notin Q)<=2R_V. An exit must cross
from L or V-L, where the intensity is at most K V^(1-m)(L)_m. Integrating
the source-state probabilities gives

    P(tau_Q<=T) <= 2 K V^(1-m)(L)_m T R_V.

No mixing-time estimate is used. For fixed m, rho=kappa/K, and L/V->q<p,
Stirling gives log W_j=C_V+V[m H(j/V)+(j/V)log rho]+O(log V), where
p=rho^(1/m)/(1+rho^(1/m))=1/[1+(K/kappa)^(1/m)]. Thus
R_V=exp[-V m D(q||p)+O(log V)]. The exponent is sharp for the lower static
controller: its exact mean hitting time is

    E_i tau_{L-1} = sum_{k=L}^i [sum_{j=k}^{V-m+1} W_j]/[d_k W_k],
    i=V/2,

obtained by substituting consecutive differences into the birth-death
Poisson equation. Its logarithmic rate is V m D(q||p)+O(log V), matching the
finite-horizon upper bound. This result is a sharp controlled safety
certificate for this conserved scalar family; it does not extend by itself
to multidimensional recovery.

## N76: independently derived event-chain extension

Let C be a finite set of complexes, all of which are reactants of at least
one reaction. For x in N^d define

    G(x)=min_{y in C, y<=x} (x-y)!,
    M(x)=max_{y in C, y<=x} x_under_y,

where factorials and falling factorials are coordinatewise products. Then
G(x)=x!/M(x). Every enabled reaction r:y->y' has y' in C and
x'=x-y+y' satisfies x'-y'=x-y. Therefore

    G(x') <= (x-y)! = x!/x_under_y,
    G(x')/G(x) <= M(x)/x_under_y.                 (A)

At a fixed state x, one available complex y_* attains M(x); because y_*
is a reactant, at least one outgoing reaction is enabled with propensity
at least kappa M(x). For each time before the next event, (A) gives

    sum_r k_r(t) x_under_{y_r} G(x+nu_r)/G(x)
        <= |R| K M(x),
    sum_r k_r(t) x_under_{y_r} >= kappa M(x).

Apply the marked counting-process compensator up to the next event time:
the conditional expected potential ratio at that event is at most C.
This argument does not freeze rates during a waiting interval, so it covers
predictable time variation and history dependence. Thus
Z_j=G(X_j)/(C^j G(X_0)) is a nonnegative supermartingale. Ville's maximal
inequality proves the displayed checkpoint claim. The proof requires positive
lower bounds for every reaction edge and source-completeness; if a maximizing
complex is not a source, the denominator lower bound fails. For rational
kappa,K the constant C and G(x) are exactly representable; logarithms are
not needed to check the equivalent rational threshold
G(X_j)/(C^j G(X_0))>=a.

This is an event-indexed upper-growth result only. Since it permits linear
growth of log G in j and log G(x) is roughly |x|log|x| away from fixed-size
complex corrections, the implied population envelope is about j/log j.
For bimolecular rates this still allows an upper rate scale of order
j^2/log^2(j), whose reciprocal is summable. It cannot establish nonexplosion
or recurrence in physical time, much less negative Foster drift. It is a
portable lemma to combine with an independently proved clock or return
estimate, not a substitute for one.

For each n>=0, the exact closed class is

    Gamma_n={(n,0,2,d),(n+1,1,1,d),(n+3,2,0,d): d>=0}.

I=A-B(B+1)/2=n, which is nonlinear and not a stoichiometric conservation

    w_0=1/2,  w_1=1/(n+1),
