4. Exact uniform rank obstruction

Let T_a (a=0,...,35) be the original integer Kraus matrices of A0. The Kraus
operators of M_t have the form
  T_a U0(t) d T_b^T K T_c.
There are four independent exact integer linear combinations L_r of the T_a
which annihilate columns 0 and 4. Therefore L_r U0(t)=L_r for every real t.
Consequently the 60-vectors flatten(L_r d T_b^T K T_c) lie in the Kraus span
of M_t and are parameter independent.

phase_d_rank_certificate.json records the original Kraus basis indices, every
integer combination, and 60 selected products. The stdlib replay reconstructs
each L_r and checks its two zero columns, reconstructs every product, and checks
the determinant of the resulting 60-by-60 integer matrix modulo the prime1009.
The determinant modulo1009 is495. Thus these products span all complex6-by-10 matrices,
so J(M_t), a positive sum of Kraus-vector projectors, is strictly positive and
has rank60 for every real t. The partial transpose is equal to the Choi matrix,
since both outer A0 factors have the exact transpose invariances.
The replay additionally proves the original Kraus space has dimension15, the
two-column constraint has rank11, and its exact annihilator space has dimension4:
the modular ranks give lower bounds and the four independent exact null rows
give matching upper bounds.

The proof actually covers ANY complex2-by-2 block filter on coordinates0,4,
identity on the other eight: the equation L_r filter=L_r still holds. It does
not require invertibility of that block. Such general filters need not define
TP Phi_U; the physical TP assertion above is for unitary rotations.

There is also an explicit uniform positivity margin. Embed the four annihilator
coefficient rows in the original36 Kraus coordinates; their Frobenius norm
squared is42. Let Q be the Gram sum of the60 selected constant Kraus vectors,
ordered in the reference-first Choi convention. Cauchy-Schwarz gives
  Q <= 42 J(M_filter)
for every such complex block filter: sum first over selected products, enlarge
to all four combinations at each (b,c), then use coefficient-matrix norm<=42.
Q is a positive integer Gram matrix, has nonzero determinant, and its exact
trace is8974168593985584. Its determinant is a positive integer, hence>=1, and
each eigenvalue is at most its trace. Therefore
  J(M_filter) >= I60/[42*(8974168593985584)^59].
This deliberately conservative bound is an exact inequality, not a floating
point spectral estimate. It does not give an EB margin.

A full-rank Choi matrix has the whole tensor space as its range, including every
product vector. Hence no extension of the source argument "the nonzero Choi
range contains no product vector" is possible anywhere in this chosen family.
This is a uniform structural obstruction to that mechanism, not a proof that
these cubes are EB or that another entanglement witness cannot work.

5. A valid complex-product witness test and complete null evidence

For even n use H_n=direct sum of [[0,1],[-1,0]], and
  Lambda_n(X)=Tr(X)I-X-H_n X^T H_n^T.
This positive map is the Breuer-Hall criterion, primary source:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0605036v2, equations(1)-(4), page2.
Its validity over complex matrices is reconstructed here. For a unit vector z,
z and H_n conjugate(z) are orthonormal, because H_n is skew symmetric. Therefore
Lambda_n(zz*)=I-zz*-(H_n conjugate(z))(H_n conjugate(z))* is a positive projector.
Spectral decomposition extends positivity to every complex PSD matrix.

For any q, W_q=(id tensor Lambda_n*)[qq*] is block positive on every complex
product vector, since Lambda_n sends PSD factors to PSD. A negative expectation
q*[(id tensor Lambda_n)(J)]q is therefore a valid entanglement witness. The
same statement holds for reference-factor application and for invertible local
filters. This differs fundamentally from CCNR, already proved blind in Phase B.
