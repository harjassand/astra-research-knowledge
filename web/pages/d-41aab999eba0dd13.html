# Aging/regeneration control branch — final report

**Verdict: no new theoretical capability or biological discovery survived the novelty and evidence gates.** The branch retained three scoped research cards, two independent subagent audits, and a source-data reanalysis of the 5 October 2026 CMA–senescence paper. Each useful result is either conditional standard mathematics or a descriptive reconstruction of a published experiment. No organism-level rejuvenation, clinical claim, or priority claim is made.

The most informative current lead is a specific proteostasis-to-clearance mechanism: CMA-dependent lysosomal turnover in macrophages limits SIRPα/SHP-1 phagocytosis inhibition, while CMA-deficient senescent-cell secretomes suppress macrophage uptake and survival. Sereda et al. directly report these effects in cultured cells, macrophage-Lamp2a conditional mice, wound repair, aged mice, and a bleomycin lung model ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01240-w)). Yet the key experiments do not measure the same outcome at the same biological level: KFERQ-Dendra puncta are a CMA-delivery reporter; SA-β-gal, p21, HMGB1 and transcript scores are senescence proxies; IgG-bead fluorescence is bead uptake; and the wound/organ readouts are tissue-level outcomes. No per-cell, in-vivo rate of senescent-cell birth, macrophage death, or senescent-cell removal is jointly acquired.

## Source-data reanalysis and decisive limit

I downloaded the article’s Fig. 4 source-data workbook and wrote a small reproducible extractor. In its Fig. 4e assay, the source means for efferocytosis of staurosporine-killed targets are 53.47% versus 2.94% with untreated control fibroblasts, and 45.13% versus 2.58% with palbociclib-senescent fibroblasts, comparing control macrophages with CMA-deficient macrophages. The paper’s two-way ANOVA assigns 98.22% of total variation to macrophage genotype, 0.8562% to fibroblast state, and 0.7214% to interaction (three independent experiments). This is a large macrophage-genotype effect in that assay; it is not a measurement of living senescent-cell clearance in tissue.

In Fig. 4h, descriptive means of bead-uptake fluorescence are 14.63 for control secretome/no CA, 2.61 for CMA-deficient secretome/no CA, and 6.39 for CMA-deficient secretome/CA. The CA condition closes 31.4% of the no-CA mean gap and leaves 68.6% of that gap. These are 10–11 fields per group nested within three independent experiments; the fields are not independent animals, so this package does not calculate field-level confidence intervals or claim a quantified population effect. Bead signal is also not direct senescent-cell fate. This partial in-vitro rescue is a sharper observation than “CMA activation helps,” but it is a descriptive reanalysis of the paper’s own data, not a new theory or validated intervention rule.

The obvious macrophage SIRPα-bypass direction is preempted. Schloesser et al. already show that senescent cells upregulate the CD47–QPCT/L axis, suppress bystander efferocytosis through contact, and that blocking CD47/SIRPα or QPCT/L can reverse that suppression; CD47 loss alone did not make senescent cells engulfable ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.202207097)). A new claim would require a narrower mechanism and a genuinely new measured property, not renaming this checkpoint route.

The in-vivo CMA experiment also does not isolate a compartment-specific drug effect: CA77.1 was given systemically at 30 mg/kg, five days/week, for five months to mice starting at 18 months of age. The paper’s LysM-Cre driver may affect monocytes and granulocytes as well as macrophages; human lung evidence is correlational, and a transcriptional CMA score is a surrogate. A source-matched test would compare compartment-restricted manipulation while acquiring macrophage survival, per-cell uptake of labelled senescent targets, source-cell birth/transition, and tissue repair in the same animals. This is a prospective experiment gate, not a result. It needs aged cohorts, longitudinal dosing, flow/imaging or lineage assays, and tissue-specific pharmacodynamic measures; animal number, acquisition throughput and cost cannot be priced from the source.

## Other directions and failure decisions

- **Mitochondrial turnover / mtDNA damage:** the independent exact finite-copy model gives the neutral Moran threshold probability (k/m), calendar-time rescaling under genotype-blind turnover, and a mutation-supply/threshold accounting corollary. Neutral heteroplasmy variance, network/time-rescaling, human turnover estimates, and exact Moran threshold analysis are prior art. Selective clearance can reverse the direction. **Rejected as a new advance.** See [mitochondrial result card](children/mito_turnover/RESULT_CARD.md), proof, model, counterexamples, and source notes.
- **Intestinal Paneth-niche repair:** the independent source reconstruction derives the conditional mass-balance bound (0.15Dpc\leq P/\tau_P) and a sharp crypt-coverage interval from a pooled renewal fraction. Missing joint rates prevent a numerical fate bound; pooled EdU/Notum do not determine which crypts cross a local functional threshold. The inequalities are standard and the αKG–Paneth mechanism is published primary work. **Retained only as a source-specific measurement gate; not a theoretical advance.** See [tissue-repair result card](children/tissue_repair/RESULT_CARD.md), proof, counterexamples, executable model, and source notes.
- **Muscle-stem-cell CMA/proteostasis:** Ramírez-Pardo et al. report age-related MuSC CMA loss and rescue with CMA/glycolysis interventions ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-025-01411-w)). A newer source reports GLS-dependent oxidative and reductive glutamine flux, lipid synthesis and aged-MuSC rescue ([Lee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-026-01120-3)). The newer study supplies M+5 citrate/DNL labeling, acute proliferation, transplantation and function readouts, but no repeated-injury reserve trajectory or joint single-cell fate/lineage rates. A generic reserve-versus-output branching model would be standard and is not promoted as a result.
- **Senescence source–clearance feedback:** broad production/clearance decline, SASP feedback and age-dependent turnover models already appear in Ovadya et al. (2019) ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glz181)); wound senescence has a published spatial model ([primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012298)). A fresh two-compartment or bistability model without acquired rates would not remove the obstruction that source production, macrophage survival and direct senescent-cell clearance were not jointly measured. This endpoint was rejected.
