# Independent audit of arXiv:1905.06571v5

**Result: an exact counterexample to the nonempty-image claim for a mold explicitly permitted by Section 4.** For the periodic piecewise affine maps below and a permitted mold, the map in Definition 6.1 satisfies

\[
\mathcal T(t)=\varnothing\qquad\text{for every }t\in\Theta_1.
\]

This falsifies Corollary 7.2 with the paper's stated arbitrary-labeling scope and prevents its Kakutani argument from proving Theorem 1.1. It does **not** refute the mathematical statement that planar rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity. No conclusion about historical acceptance or another possible proof is made.

The audit was conducted independently from the primary proof; no other worker's critical findings were read. It is internal same-team proof checking, not external verification. ## 1. Triangulation and two legitimate scalar maps

Take \(l=1\), so the periodic unit square is divided into four squares of side \(1/2\). Split each small square along its anti-diagonal, whose normal is \((1,1)\). Nodal arrays are indexed by row \(y\), column \(x\), with both indices interpreted modulo two. Let

\[
u=\begin{pmatrix}0&4\\2&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]

These arrays define continuous periodic piecewise affine functions in \(P_1\) by nodal interpolation. In cell \((i,j)\), number the lower triangle \(T_{2(2j+i)}\) and the upper triangle \(T_{2(2j+i)+1}\). If its lower-left, lower-right, upper-left, and upper-right nodal values are \(a,b,c,d\), its gradients are

\[
\nabla w_{\mathrm{lower}}=2(b-a,c-a),\qquad
\nabla w_{\mathrm{upper}}=2(d-c,d-b).
\]

The resulting exact gradients are:

| Triangle | \(\nabla u\) | \(\nabla v\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(T_0\) | \((8,4)\) | \((-2,-2)\) |
| \(T_1\) | \((2,-2)\) | \((4,4)\) |
| \(T_2\) | \((-8,-2)\) | \((2,4)\) |
| \(T_3\) | \((-2,4)\) | \((-4,-2)\) |
| \(T_4\) | \((2,-4)\) | \((4,2)\) |
| \(T_5\) | \((8,2)\) | \((-2,-4)\) |
| \(T_6\) | \((-2,2)\) | \((-4,-4)\) |
| \(T_7\) | \((-8,-4)\) | \((2,2)\) |

Each component has eight distinct gradients. Both gradient averages are zero. Compatibility across every interface holds, including periodic boundary interfaces, because these are genuine nodal interpolants. The verifier additionally checks each gradient jump is parallel to its interface normal.

## 2. A permitted mold and a strictly positive feasible input

Use Section 4's \(j=1\) normal set \(\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\). Label its four vertical interfaces first and its four horizontal interfaces next, both in row-major order. The eight adjacent-triangle pairs, using the zero-based triangle numbering above, are

\[
(T_0,T_3),(T_2,T_1),(T_4,T_7),(T_6,T_5),
(T_0,T_5),(T_2,T_7),(T_4,T_1),(T_6,T_3).
\]

Give each pair successive leaf labels \((2i-1,2i)\), and at all subsequent dyadic levels combine consecutive pairs, as required by the paper. Thus the last two displayed interface pairs are combined with each other at the next level. In the one-based leaf indexing of this sub-mold, the relevant subtree is

\[
[[13,14],[15,16]].
\]

Section 4, PDF page 12, expressly allows arbitrary interface tagging and treats all such taggings as valid. This order obeys its required interface adjacency and dyadic pairing rules. Complete the other two sub-molds by listing vertical then diagonal interfaces for \(j=2\), and horizontal then diagonal interfaces for \(j=3\), in row-major order. Each triangle then appears twice in each sub-mold and six times overall, exactly as required for \(C_s\). The final joining of the three sub-molds can be any permitted fixed order; the obstruction below arises earlier.

The uniform leaf vector

\[
t_k=1/48\quad(1\leq k\leq48)
\]

belongs to \(\Theta_1\): every initial interface pair has mass \(1/24\), and each triangle has total mass \(6/48=1/8\). This vector is strictly positive and lies in the relative interior of the weight polytope. The argument below is stronger: it applies to every \(t\in\Theta_1\).

## 3. Exact obstruction for every possible output weight vector

Consider the last two horizontal interfaces in the \(j=1\) list. Both lie at \(y=1/2\); one has \(0\leq x\leq1/2\), the other \(1/2\leq x\leq1\). Their adjacent triangle pairs are \((T_4,T_1)\) and \((T_6,T_3)\).

For any input \(t\in\Theta_1\), divide the first member's leaf mass in these two pairs by the fixed positive pair mass \(1/24\), and denote the resulting fractions by \(\alpha,\beta\in[0,1]\). The two first-level \(u\) barycenters are

\[
A_u=\alpha(2,-4)+(1-\alpha)(2,-2)=(2,-2-2\alpha),
\]
\[
B_u=\beta(-2,2)+(1-\beta)(-2,4)=(-2,4-2\beta).
\]

The next dyadic mixture has decomposition difference, by (3.5),

\[
U=A_u-B_u=(4,-6-2\alpha+2\beta).
\]

In particular, its second coordinate is in \([-8,-4]\).

For any proposed output \(r\in\Theta_1\), denote the corresponding fractions by \(\gamma,\delta\in[0,1]\). The two \(v\) barycenters are

\[
A_v=\gamma(4,2)+(1-\gamma)(4,4)=(4,4-2\gamma),
\]
\[
B_v=\delta(-4,-4)+(1-\delta)(-4,-2)=(-4,-2-2\delta).
\]

Their required decomposition difference is

\[
V=A_v-B_v=(8,6-2\gamma+2\delta),
\]

whose second coordinate is in \([4,8]\). Consequently

\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(U,V)
&=72+16\alpha-16\beta-8\gamma+8\delta\\
&\geq72-16-8=48>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is an inequality on the entire four-dimensional box \([0,1]^4\), a larger set than the fractions permitted jointly by the triangle mass equations. Those additional equations cannot restore feasibility. Both vectors are nonzero, so no convention for parallelism involving a zero vector is relevant.

Definition 6.1 requires these two decomposition differences to be parallel. They cannot be parallel for any feasible \(t,r\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T(t)=\varnothing\text{ for every }t\in\Theta_1}
\]

for this permitted mold. The uniform strictly positive input already furnishes the requested counterexample. The lower bound is strict, so the obstruction also persists under sufficiently small perturbations of the nodal arrays; degeneracy or a dense/generic-pair argument does not remove it.

More explicitly, independent nodal sup perturbations of at most \(\varepsilon\) in each component move each triangle-gradient coordinate by at most \(4\varepsilon\), and each paired decomposition-difference coordinate by at most \(8\varepsilon\). For \(\varepsilon\leq1/10\), this gives \(U_x\geq16/5\), \(U_y\leq-16/5\), \(V_x\geq36/5\), and \(V_y\geq16/5\), uniformly over all input and output fractions. Therefore

\[
\det(U,V)\geq(16/5)^2+(16/5)(36/5)=832/25>0.
\]

The failure holds throughout this open neighborhood of the primary fixture.

## 4. Where Claim 7.4 does and does not help

Claim 7.4's local geometric assertion is correct under its intended interpretation. Given the two interface barycenter vectors incident to a triangle, draw lines through them parallel to their two distinct normals. Their unique intersection is the only possible value of that triangle's gradient.

That uniqueness statement does not show that arbitrary fixed decomposition directions can produce the required interface barycenters for every function \(w\in P_l\). The explicit example above shows they cannot: at a mandatory upper mixture the candidate \(v\) interface differences have positive second coordinate, while the supplied \(u\) differences have negative second coordinate and positive first coordinate. No nonnegative interface weights can align them.

The Section 7 proof also needs more than a dimension count to justify its stated range and convexity conclusions. Independence of the imposed constraints, surjectivity onto gradients of periodic functions, and legitimacy of every last-level convex decomposition are separate requirements. A neighborhood in the feasible image does not by linearity alone make all inequality constraints automatic over the full linear span. The present counterexample directly falsifies the claimed universal nonemptiness, so these further observations are not needed to establish the failure.

Step 3's translation averaging also requires an equivariant choice or another argument showing the constructed measures transform under translations; fixed \(u,t\), interface labels, and arbitrarily chosen representations do not give that invariance by themselves. This is an additional proof obligation, not a second asserted counterexample in this audit.

## 5. What survives and what must stop

- The nonempty, compact, convex nature of \(\Theta_l\) is consistent with the reconstruction.
- The fixed-point implication in Proposition 6.1 remains a valid conditional route: if a common weight vector makes the paired row differences parallel at every node, it produces the requested joint rank-one tree.
- For fixed input directions, the parallelism equations are affine linear in output weights, so convexity/closedness of each image is plausible and is not the failed property here.
- Nonemptiness fails for the allowed mold above. Kakutani cannot be applied to this map, and Section 7 does not establish the claimed theorem with its stated arbitrary-mold scope.
- Restricting to a special mold, allowing a function-dependent mold, or changing the map would require a new construction and proof. The counterexample does not prove that every possible alternative mold fails.
- No downstream foundational consequence is promoted from this source proof. The truth of planar ROC=>QC and its historical acceptance remain outside the conclusion of this audit.
