# Binary linear support dispersion with explicit costs and a recent hardness consequence

Status (6 October 2026): independently reconstructed mathematical derivation; not externally validated. The existence/order of the support-dispersion result is established prior art (Alon et al., 1999, Theorem 7). Candidate novelty is the explicitly quantified, algebraic-degree-preserving application to the weak demi-hardness interface of Li–Ren–Zhong (19 September 2026). Novelty UNKNOWN; this is not an unconditional circuit lower bound or an algorithm for general Avoid.

## Exact question and interfaces

Can a barely nontrivial set of strings whose nonmembership in a circuit's image is hard to refute be compressed into a circuit with no easily refutable missing output, while preserving GF(2) algebraic degree?

Input to the mathematical construction: integers N >= k >= m >= 1, error 0 < epsilon < 1, and a fixed set S subset F_2^N with |S| >= 2^k. The sampler receives only N and m; it does not receive S, membership access to S, or certificates about S. It samples a uniformly random surjective GF(2)-linear map L:F_2^N -> F_2^m. The claim is that L(S)=F_2^m with high probability. It is a seeded, per-source support guarantee; it does not require one L to work for all S simultaneously.

In the proof-complexity application, G:F_2^n -> F_2^N is supplied as an explicit polynomial-size circuit. S is a mathematically defined hard-string set, not an acquired or efficiently recognizable set. The existence of a sufficiently large S remains an explicit assumption. Randomly sampling L is efficient; recognizing that the sampled L is secure is not supplied or established.

## Proposition A: explicit finite-parameter dispersion

Write s=ceil(log_2 m) and a=ceil(log_2(32/epsilon^2)). If

    k >= m + s + a,

then for every fixed S with |S| >= 2^k, a uniformly random surjective L:F_2^N -> F_2^m satisfies

    Pr[L(S)=F_2^m] >= 1-epsilon.

This has entropy loss log m + 2 log(1/epsilon)+O(1). Its order is established by Alon et al. The explicit proof below uses elementary second moments and controlled random doubling, rather than importing their technical tail lemma. No novelty is claimed for the asymptotic result or the elementary ingredients.

### Step 1: grow a random span until few points are uncovered

Let p=|S|/2^N >= 2^(k-N), d0=N-k+a, and choose d0 independent uniform vectors v_1,...,v_d0 in F_2^N. Put K0=span(v_1,...,v_d0) and q0=1-|S+K0|/2^N. They need not be linearly independent.

We claim E[q0] <= 2^(N-k-d0) = 2^-a <= epsilon^2/32.

To prove it, introduce an auxiliary independent uniform r in F_2^N and, for fixed z, count the coefficient vectors b in F_2^d0 for which z+r+sum_j b_j v_j belongs to S. Each indicator has mean p, and the indicators for any two distinct coefficient vectors are independent. This follows because the two distinct affine linear forms in (r,v_1,...,v_d0) have rank 2. Consequently the count X has mean mu=2^d0 p and variance mu(1-p). Chebyshev gives Pr[X=0] <= 1/mu. Averaging over z and noting that translating by r does not alter the uncovered density proves the claim. This argument counts coefficient vectors with multiplicity, correctly allowing a rank-deficient sampled span.

Markov now gives

    Pr[q0 > epsilon/16] <= epsilon/2.

### Step 2: random doubling with summable failure probabilities

Choose s further independent uniform vectors w_i. Put K_i=K_(i-1)+span(w_i) and q_i=1-|S+K_i|/2^N. Conditional on every past vector, elementary double counting gives

    E[q_i | K_(i-1)] = q_(i-1)^2.

Indeed the uncovered set becomes U intersect (U+w_i), and each ordered pair of U has exactly one difference w_i.

For i=1,...,s define A_i=2^(i+1)/epsilon. When q_(i-1)>0, conditional Markov gives

    Pr[q_i > A_i q_(i-1)^2 | past] <= epsilon/2^(i+1).

When q_(i-1)=0 the bound holds automatically. The probability of any such failure is strictly below epsilon/2. On their complement, starting from q0 <= epsilon/16, induction gives

    q_i <= epsilon * 2^(-i-3) * 2^(-2^i).

For i=0 the displayed expression is epsilon/16. If it holds at i-1, multiplication by A_i after squaring reproduces exactly the expression at i. Since 2^s >= m, q_s < 2^-m, including m=1 where s=0.

### Step 3: uniformly complete the kernel; use missing cosets, not a false independence assumption

There are d0+s <= N-m sampled vectors. Conditional on their span K_s, independently choose a uniformly random (N-m)-dimensional subspace K containing it. The resulting marginal K is uniform among all subspaces of this dimension: the procedure is invariant under every invertible linear transformation, and that group acts transitively on such subspaces. Choose a uniformly random isomorphism F_2^N/K -> F_2^m to obtain L. Thus L is exactly a uniformly random surjective linear map.

If some fiber of L misses S, that entire coset of K, of cardinality 2^(N-m), misses S+K_s because K_s subset K. This would force q_s >= 2^-m. Therefore q_s < 2^-m implies L(S)=F_2^m. Combining the two failure bounds proves Proposition A.

The proof does not make the generally false step E[q_i] <= E[q_(i-1)]^2. Alon et al. explicitly warn about this: if S is a hyperplane, uncovered density stays 1/2 until one sampled vector leaves that hyperplane, and E[q_i]=2^(-i-1), not 2^(-2^i).

### Fixed-randomness, bounded-time variant (independent reconstruction by r06)

Exactly the same parameter inequality also suffices when L is a uniformly random **arbitrary** m by N binary matrix, with no rank test. Keep the slack in the proof above: conditional on full row rank, failure probability is at most epsilon/2 + sum_(i=1)^s epsilon/2^(i+1) = epsilon - epsilon/2^(s+1). For the unconditioned matrix, the rank-deficiency probability is at most sum_(i=0)^(m-1)2^(i-N) < 2^(m-N) <= 2^(-s-a) <= epsilon^2/(32*2^s). The combined failure probability is therefore strictly less than epsilon. In particular, a worst-case polynomial-time zero-error support-disperser family uses exactly mN random bits, with every seed legal; non-surjective seeds are simply part of the charged failure probability. This old all-linear family has established prior art; this bookkeeping repairs the bounded-time sampler interface without claiming novelty.

## Proposition B: finite proof-complexity transfer

Fix a proof system P and a Tseitin CNF encoding tau_z(G) of the equation G(x)=z; a refutation certifies that the equation has no solution. Require a polynomial-time proof transformation with a monotone polynomial length bound b: for every surjective linear L and every z, a P-refutation of tau_(Lz)(L composed with G) of length <= ell yields a P-refutation of tau_z(G) of length <= b(ell,N,m,|G|). The usual Tseitin gate encoding plus closure under simple parity reductions suffices (Ren–Wang–Zhong, ITCS 2026, Definition 3 and Claim 4). Semantic implication alone is insufficient.

Let H be the strings z in F_2^N for which no P-refutation of tau_z(G) has length <= b(ell,N,m,|G|). If |H| >= 2^k and Proposition A's inequality holds, then with probability at least 1-epsilon over L, **for every** y in F_2^m there is no P-refutation of tau_y(L composed with G) of length <= ell.

Proof: with that probability L(H)=F_2^m. For any y pick z in H with Lz=y. A short refutation for y would transfer to a refutation for z forbidden by the definition of H. This is a statement for a fixed P, threshold ell, and finite instance; it does not silently interchange uniform proof systems, thresholds, or lengths.

For an asymptotic generator, weak hardness must be quantified at every polynomial threshold (or at an explicit stronger bound) before deriving superpolynomial refutation size. A fixed-threshold sampler may produce different maps at different thresholds. A single family hard for all thresholds requires a further diagonal/selection argument or suitably growing thresholds; it is not asserted here.

## Proposition C: uniform SearchNP avoidance transfer

Assume a supplied uniformly polynomial-time constructible circuit family G_n:F_2^n -> F_2^N(n) is weakly demi-hard in the following precise sense: every uniform polynomial-time nondeterministic B that rejects all strings in Range(G_n) leaves at least 2^k strings rejected for all sufficiently large n. Require N polynomial in n. Alternatively one may adopt the source paper's uniform-adversary interface where the circuit description of nonuniform G_n is included as input; the hardness premise must then include that same description. Let m>n satisfy Proposition A's inequality, and let C_L=L composed with G_n.

Then every uniform polynomial-time errorless SearchNP heuristic A for Avoid succeeds on at most an epsilon fraction of uniformly random surjective L.

Proof: suppose success probability exceeds epsilon. Define B(z) to accept iff it can guess a surjective m by N matrix L and an accepting nondeterministic branch of A(C_L) whose output is Lz. All these descriptions and computations have polynomial size, so B is a legal nondeterministic polynomial-time machine. It rejects Range(G): Lz belongs to Range(C_L) whenever z=G(x), contradicting errorless avoidance if A outputs it. Let R be B's rejected strings. Weak demi-hardness supplies |R| >= 2^k. Proposition A says at least 1-epsilon of maps cover R. Hence there is an L which both covers R and permits A to output some y. Some z in R has Lz=y; that same branch then makes B accept z, a contradiction. This independently reconstructs the zero-error-disperser reduction in Li–Ren–Zhong Theorem 4.1.

The epsilon in this result is a bound on solver success, not a sampler's probability of producing a successful avoidance witness. No deterministic Avoid algorithm is constructed.

## Degree, size, depth, sampling and verification costs

If each output of G is a GF(2) polynomial of degree at most d, each output of L composed with G is an XOR of outputs of G and still has degree at most d. The same applies when G is NC0_d by expanding each constant-local function in algebraic normal form. Thus the resulting functions belong to XOR composed with AND_(<=d), after polynomial-size expansion for fixed d. The sampler preserves algebraic degree, but generally destroys constant locality: a dense row can depend on every input.

An m by N binary matrix needs mN seed bits, O(mN) description bits and at most m(N-1) two-input XOR gates. A balanced implementation adds O(log N) depth; an unbounded fan-in parity layer adds one layer in the appropriate circuit model. Dense multiplication uses O(mN) bit operations or O(m ceil(N/w)) word parity operations with a declared word size w. Gaussian rank checking is polynomial time (a simple bit implementation O(m^2 N)); accepting only full-row-rank uniformly sampled matrices gives the exact uniform-surjective distribution. The acceptance probability is product_(i=0)^(m-1)(1-2^(i-N)) >= product_(j=1)^infty(1-2^-j) > .288, so expected trials <3.5. No membership tests of H or any test of security are charged because none are performed or provided. Certifying full dispersion given an arbitrary explicit support by enumeration would require reading that support; certifying the hard-string assumption is outside this construction.

If exact uniform surjectivity is unnecessary, the bounded-time variant uses one uniform mN-bit seed and no rank test/retries, with the same total dispersion error. Propositions B/C extend to this family by defining the parity proof transformation for all matrices and letting B guess any matrix; the coverage argument itself excludes non-surjective successful seeds. The original uniform-surjective versions remain separately valid.

Taking m=n+1 and epsilon=1/2, it suffices to assume k >= n+1+ceil(log_2(n+1))+7. A sufficient easy-to-state count is |H| >= 1024 n 2^n. This is only O(n) above the unavoidable <=2^n actual-image strings, the same order as the September paper's barely nontrivial notion, while using XOR postprocessing. The constant 1024 is conservative; the source's 100 is not claimed here.

## Prior art and novelty status

- The support-coverage order |S| >= c_epsilon m 2^m is Alon–Dietzfelbinger–Miltersen–Petrank–Tardos (1999), Theorem 7, pages 7–8 of saved preprint. Proposition 2.2 also shows that an order m*2^m threshold is necessary for arbitrary sets for **any** binary linear map, so eliminating the log m entropy loss universally is not available.
- Li–Ren–Zhong (arXiv:2609.23228v1, 19 September 2026), Definition 3.1, Theorems 3.3/3.5/3.7/4.1, provides the general transfer interface and contrasts zero-overhead projections with high-independence hashing. Their Section 3.2 analyzes the latter for barely nontrivial demi-hardness. Its listed bibliography does not contain Alon et al.'s linear-coverage theorem or Dhar–Dvir; this observation is not a proof of novelty.
- Ren–Wang–Zhong (ITCS 2026), Theorem 2, uses pairwise hashing; its stated parameter cost is N>=2m+1. Definition 3 and Claim 4 establish the proof-encoding reduction needed here. Linear maps plus leftover hashing were already used, so XOR postprocessing itself is established.
- Dhar–Dvir (journal 2024), Theorems 2.2/2.4, gives substantially stronger l_infinity near-uniformity for linear hashes with O(log entropy) loss. This also implies full coverage for relative error <1; it is an alternative imported route, with different constants.

The candidate contribution is an economical application/explicit quantitative reconstruction, not a claim that a foundational open problem is solved. Its importance is that one need not introduce higher algebraic degree merely to compress a small hard set into an expanding hard range-avoidance instance. Whether this consequence is already recorded elsewhere remains UNKNOWN.

## Decisive limitations and next falsifiable step

1. No supplied G is proved to satisfy the weak demi-hardness premise; acquisition of such hardness is the load-bearing unresolved obligation.
2. Dense XOR rows do not remain NC0. This result cannot remove the NC0 locality/stretch barrier in the September paper.
3. Binary support dispersion does not imply metric/Lipschitz dispersion with a good normalized Hamming constant. Flipping one input bit can flip all m outputs in a dense linear map. Hence this transfer cannot be silently substituted into the remote-point theorem, which has a separate Lipschitz requirement.
4. The hard-set cardinality condition counts false nonmembership statements too. Since |Range(G)|<=2^n, the O(n) factor makes the premise nonvacuous, but does not establish it.
5. The fixed-P finite proof statement above and the uniform SearchNP hardness statement have separate legal interfaces. Hardness against all proof systems and one explicit uniform secure generator are not derived.

Next falsifiable step: audit all primary-paper versions cited by Li–Ren–Zhong for linear-support dispersion and explicit degree-preserving barely-hardness consequences. If already established, classify this as a reusable synthesis. Independently inspect the explicit Proposition A proof (especially uniform kernel completion and threshold ceilings). For a genuine new result, pursue sparse binary linear families with the same nearoptimal support threshold while preserving a useful locality/Lipschitz bound; current proof uses fully random dense vectors, and does not support that leap.

## Proposition D: why arbitrary ambient stretch blocks a constant-local universal family

This is an elementary obstruction, reconstructed here; novelty is not claimed. Let F be any distribution of m-output Boolean maps on N input bits, with each output depending on at most w source coordinates. Assume that for every k-coordinate bit-fixing support S, Pr_(f~F)[f(S)=F_2^m] >= 1-epsilon. Necessarily

    w >= (1-epsilon) N/k.

Proof: choose the free coordinate set I uniformly among k-subsets of [N]. For any fixed f, let U(f) be the union of all source coordinates used by its outputs; |U(f)|<=mw. With all nonfree coordinates fixed to zero, f(S_I) depends on at most |I intersect U(f)| independent bits. Full support of m output bits requires |I intersect U(f)|>=m. Markov and E_I[|I intersect U(f)|]=k|U(f)|/N imply

    Pr_I[f(S_I)=F_2^m] <= k |U(f)|/(mN) <= kw/N.

Average over f and exchange the finite expectations. The universal per-source success requirement gives the claimed inequality. This argument does not assume row independence, linearity, or a symmetric sampler. Thus when k=m+O(log m) and N/m grows without bound, constant output locality is incompatible with this universal support-dispersion interface. It does not rule out sparse families with locality proportional to N/k, source-dependent sampling, or weaker restricted-source promises. Extractor-locality lower bounds and sparse extractor constructions are established related literature (screened primary ECCC TR16-018, introduction and cited Bogdanov–Guo 2013); those use additional distributional guarantees, and their hypotheses are not imported here.


## Proposition E: explicit infinite obstruction to zero-loss linear support dispersion

Let A={0,1,2,3,4,5,8,10} in F_2^4 (integers encode four binary coordinates). Directly certified pairs in `results/r14_computation/difference_full_certificate.json` show A+A=F_2^4. For any integer m>=3 set S_m=A times F_2^(m-3), a size-2^m support in F_2^(m+1). Then S_m+S_m=F_2^(m+1). Every linear L:F_2^(m+1)->F_2^m has a nonzero kernel vector v. Since v=a+b for distinct a,b in S_m, L collides on S_m. Thus |L(S_m)|<2^m and L(S_m) cannot cover the codomain. This is a universal-in-m construction whose finite seed certificate is supplied. It is an explicit illustration of the established need for entropy loss; no novelty is claimed. It does not contradict Proposition A, whose hypotheses demand extra entropy.

For the separate remote-point obstruction, the onto matrix with rows e_0+e_i (i=1,...,m) maps a one-bit change in coordinate zero to a change in every output coordinate. Its normalized Hamming Lipschitz constant is at least N=m+1. Therefore full coverage and low GF(2) degree alone supply no uniform small Lipschitz constant.
