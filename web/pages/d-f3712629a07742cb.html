# Independent all-degree contracted-jet gate

Worker hessian_check12, cycle14, 7 October 2026. Lead proposed by
charges_design9; complete proof independently reconstructed here.
No degree bound is imposed on the fibre automorphism. Historical
priority and external validation are UNKNOWN.

Let Theta be a polynomial fibre automorphism over C[x,y], fixing
R=0, and let D=D_RTheta(x,y,0), det D=delta in C*. Set
`psi=h(x,y)+(2y,4x)Theta(x,y,R)` with arbitrary polynomial h.
Require the contracted quadratic fibre jet of the actual reduced
potential to vanish:

`sum_i (2y,4x)_i Hess_R Theta_i(x,y,0)=0`.

Individual component jets need not vanish. At the zero section,
with G=(2y,4x)D,

`Hess psi=[[Hess h,DG^T],[DG,0]]`,
`det Hess psi=(det DG)²`.

Thus a constant nonzero full Hessian determinant forces G to be
a plane Keller map. Its component ideal is(x,y), because D is
unimodular. For arbitrary two-base D(x,y), no general inverse
follows; this is the exact surviving plane interface.

For D depending only on x, write D=[[a,b],[c,d]]. Differentiation
gives

`Jac G=4[y(a'b-ab')-2delta+2x(c'b-ad')]`.

Constancy forces a'b-ab'=0. Since det D is a unit, gcd(a,b)=1.
If both are nonzero, a/b has zero derivative over C(x), so their
ratio is constant and coprimality makes both constant. If one
vanishes, the determinant identity makes the other a nonzero
constant. This exhausts the degenerate cases. Differentiating
det D then gives c'b-ad'=0, hence Jac G=-8delta.

The second row has form(c0,d0)+r(x)(a,b), with polynomial r:
choose whichever nonzero constant a or b to solve it. Therefore

`G=2(y+2xr)(a,b)+4x(c0,d0)`.

Its explicit global inverse is

`x=(-bG1+aG2)/(4delta)`,
`y=(d0G1-c0G2)/(2delta)-2x r(x)`.

The last two gradient coordinates already distinguish every pair
of distinct bases on the zero section. Therefore no such one-base
first jet with vanishing contracted quadratic jet can retain the
known collision and have nonzero constant Hessian determinant,
irrespective of higher fibre terms or h. This does not exclude
collisions away from the zero section or prove every potential in
the class globally invertible.

The reduced-jet hypothesis matters. If a precritical chart depends
on t, eliminating t can add a residual Schur term. Vanishing of its
precritical contracted quadratic jet is not by itself sufficient.
Apply the statement to the actual reduced Hessian block.

The contracted condition is strictly weaker than individual jet
vanishing. For example Theta=R+(2x,-y)^T(y rho+2x eta)² has
nonzero individual quadratic jets, is a global polynomial shear,
and leaves(2y,4x)Theta=(2y,4x)R identically. Its inverse subtracts
the same term because the linear form is invariant.

The minimum total degree of any compatible first jet at the
opposite witness bases is two: every matrix D(x,y) of total
degree<=1 has D(b+)+D(b-)=2D(0), an invertible matrix, so the
opposite nonzero covectors cannot agree. The degree-two D2=PMP^-1
in DEGREE6_LEGAL_REPAIR_GATE.md attains the bound. It is minimal
only for this base-fixed origin-fixing first-jet interface, not
for every possible source chart or potential.

The quadratic two-shear D2 chart has a nonzero contracted second
jet and escapes this particular hypothesis. The odd-cubic chart
has zero second jets, so its determinant already fails on the zero
section; no global constant-pivot assumption is needed to reject
that all-degree branch. The independently replayable free-jet and
inverse checker is `work/hessian_check12/cycle14/zero_quadratic_jet_checks.py`,
with saved JSON. The downstream use is a universal pre-expansion
filter: retained collisions in a one-base first-jet chart require
a nonzero reduced contracted quadratic jet. All general two-base
or different-coordinate alternatives remain explicit.
