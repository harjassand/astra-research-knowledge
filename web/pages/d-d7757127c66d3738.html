## Attribution and result

The starting result is peer-originated: `work/cycle6/c03_l10/low_sector_pfaffian_fpras.txt`
claims a relative approximation for the general rational-complex coefficient
`c_k(F)` in `O(9^k eps^-2 log(1/delta))` exact polynomial-bit sample work.
I independently reconstructed its identities and complex variance argument.
No interface defect was found. Exact exhaustive-sign checks on two `n=4`
matrices verify the coefficient identity, square-root computation, nonnegative
samples, the second-moment bound, and the exact-zero case. The assertions are
not a proof of the all-dimension theorem; that proof is the derivation below.

Deeper consequence derived here: the estimator extends to all row-prefix
marginals by projecting pair columns away from the forced span. This yields a
polynomial-time approximate TV sampler for the complete canonical hard-BCS
configuration law when `k=O(log n)`: sample the row set by approximate
self-reduction, then sample the column set exactly by volume-sampling
self-reduction. This bridges relative counts to a sampler without using a
normalized sampler to recover its normalizer. It remains a low-sector result;
the cost is exponential for `k=Theta(n)`. The constrained projection identity
and exact column-prefix masses were checked on a dense complex rational
`n=4, k=2` fixture. No end-to-end randomized sampler was implemented.

## 1. Generic paired-volume estimator

Let `V` be an `m x 2N` matrix over `Q(i)`, with columns grouped as
`(u_i,v_i)`. For a set `S` of group labels, let `V_S` contain both columns of
each group, and put

    Z_r(V) = sum_{|S|=r} det(V_S^* V_S),       0 <= 2r <= m.

For independent uniform signs `s_i`, define the skew matrix

    A_s = sum_i s_i (u_i v_i^T - v_i u_i^T),
    X_r(A_s) = sum_{R subseteq [m], |R|=2r} |Pf(A_s[R])|^2.

The transpose in this skew lift is an ordinary transpose; conjugation enters
only in the squared absolute values and Gram matrices. The Pfaffian
minor-summation identity gives

    Pf(A_s[R]) = sum_{|S|=r} (prod_{i in S} s_i) det(V[R,P(S)]),

where `P(S)` selects the two columns of each group in `S`. Rademacher
character orthogonality removes all cross terms between distinct `S`. Summing
over `R` and applying Cauchy--Binet therefore proves

    E_s X_r(A_s) = sum_{|S|=r} det(V_S^* V_S) = Z_r(V).       (1)

For a complex skew-symmetric `A`, unitary congruence puts it into `2 x 2`
blocks with singular values `sigma_j`, plus at most one zero coordinate.
The degree-`r` sum of squared principal `2r`-Pfaffians is invariant under
unitary congruence: it is the squared norm of the `r`th exterior power of the
associated two-form. On the block form it is `e_r(sigma_1^2,...,sigma_q^2)`.
Consequently

    sum_{|R|=2r} |Pf(A[R])|^2
      = [t^r] sqrt(det(I + t A^* A)),

where the square root is the formal series with constant term one. If
`det(I+t A^*A)=sum d_j t^j` and the square root is `sum q_j t^j`, exact
coefficient extraction uses

    q_0=1,  q_r=(d_r-sum_{j=1}^{r-1} q_j q_{r-j})/2.

No floating eigenvalue or square-root computation is required. The matrix
`A_s` has rational-complex entries, the polynomial determinant has polynomial
degree and bit complexity, and its exact coefficients through degree `r`
have polynomial bit length. The last bound also follows directly from the
principal-Pfaffian representation: each sample is a sum of at most
`binom(m,2r)` squared rational Pfaffians of order `2r`.

For fixed `R`, the function `p_R(s)=Pf(A_s[R])` is a degree-`r` multilinear
polynomial on the Boolean cube. Applying scalar Bonami--Beckner
hypercontractivity separately to its real and imaginary parts gives

    ||p_R||_4^2 <= 2 * 3^r E|p_R|^2.

Then Minkowski in `L2` yields

    ||X_r||_2
      <= sum_R || |p_R|^2 ||_2
      <= 2 * 3^r sum_R E|p_R|^2
      = 2 * 3^r Z_r(V),

so `E X_r^2 <= 4 * 9^r Z_r(V)^2`. A batch of
`ceil(16 * 9^r / eta^2)` independent sign samples has relative error at most
`eta` with probability at least `3/4`, by Chebyshev. A median of
`O(log(1/delta))` independent batch means gives confidence `1-delta`.
If `Z_r=0`, (1) and `X_r>=0` imply every sign sample is exactly zero. When
`Z_r>0`, a zero sample is possible; the FPRAS guarantee, rather than a single
zero observation, controls false-zero output probability.

For `k=O(log n)`, `9^k=n^{O(1)}`. For `k=Theta(n)` this estimator remains
exponential; it gives no hardness lower bound and no unrestricted FPRAS.

## 2. Connection to the hard-BCS norm and exact normalizer

For `F in Q(i)^(n x n)`, define the paired columns

    u_i=e_i,   v_i=(row_i(F))^T,   V=[I_n,F^T].

For each `I` with `|I|=k`, let `V_I` select both columns at every site in `I`.
Any nonzero `2k`-row minor of `V_I` must include all identity rows indexed by
`I`; its remaining `k` rows form a set `J disjoint from I`. Expanding the
identity block leaves `det F[I,J]` up to sign. Cauchy--Binet gives

    det(V_I^*V_I) = sum_{|J|=k, J intersect I=empty} |det F[I,J]|^2,
    c_k(F) = sum_{|I|=k} det(V_I^*V_I).

Thus (1) estimates the *unnormalized* coefficient itself. There is no
normalizer ambiguity or multiplicative conversion from a normalized sample.
The estimator is for arbitrary rational-complex `F`, including exact-zero
sectors, but its polynomial bound only covers low pair number `k=O(log n)`.

## 3. Forced and excluded row sets preserve the estimator interface

Let `A,B` be disjoint row-site sets to be forced into and excluded from the
selected row set, respectively. Define

    W_k(A,B) = sum_{|I|=k, A subseteq I, I intersect B=empty}
                 det(V_I^* V_I).

If `|A|>k`, or the `2|A|` columns `C=V_A` are dependent, then `W_k(A,B)=0`
exactly. Otherwise let

    G=C^*C,    d=det(G)>0,    P=I-C G^{-1} C^*,
    E=[n] minus (A union B),    r=k-|A|.

For every `T subseteq E` of size `r`, the block determinant / Schur
complement identity gives

    det(V_{A union T}^* V_{A union T})
      = d * det((P V_T)^*(P V_T)).

Therefore

    W_k(A,B) = d * Z_r(P V_E),

where the columns of `P V_E` remain grouped into their original site pairs.
The generic estimator applies unchanged to these rational-complex pair
vectors. Multiplication by the known positive factor `d` preserves relative
error. Its sample values are nonnegative, and all samples are zero whenever
`W_k(A,B)=0`. The forced projection has polynomial bit length: `G` is a
`2|A| x 2|A|` rational-complex Gram matrix and is inverted exactly; all
minors and adjugate entries have bit lengths polynomial in `n` and the input
encoding length. No inverse of the possibly tiny `d` is used in the estimate.

This is the key interface repair over naive inclusion-exclusion: it expresses
the positive constrained mass itself as one projected paired-volume sum, so
no large positive terms are subtracted to reveal a small prefix mass.

## 4. Approximate canonical-sector sampler with charged TV error

The canonical law is

    Pr[I,J] = |det F[I,J]|^2 / c_k(F),
    |I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty.

Its row marginal is `Pr[I]=det(V_I^*V_I)/c_k(F)`. Sample `I` by scanning row
sites. Given the current selected prefix `A` and excluded prefix `B`, compute
relative estimates of the two nonnegative child masses
`W_k(A union {i},B)` and `W_k(A,B union {i})`, then choose the branch in
proportion to those estimates. The exact child masses sum to the parent mass.
If both estimates have relative error at most `eta`, the Bernoulli branch
probability changes by at most `eta/(2(1-eta))`; a zero child is exactly
zero on every estimator sample. Dyadic rounding with denominator `D` adds at
most `1/D` total variation per branch. For `eta=epsilon/(4n)` and
`D>=4n/epsilon`, the good-estimator path has row-law TV error below
`epsilon/2`. At most `2n` FPRAS calls are made. Assigning each confidence
failure at most `epsilon/(4n)` makes their union probability at most
`epsilon/2`, so the overall row sampler is within `epsilon` TV.

Each constrained FPRAS costs
`O(9^k n^2 epsilon^-2 log(n/epsilon))` samples under these parameters; at
most `2n` calls give
`O(9^k n^3 epsilon^-2 log(n/epsilon))` exact polynomial-bit sample work.
For `k=O(log n)` this is polynomial in `n`, input bit length, and
`epsilon^-1`. The confidence union bound remains polynomial. This sampler
does not require knowing `c_k` exactly and does not invert any branch or
sector probability.

Now condition on a supported row set `I`. Put

    M=F[I,[n] minus I],

which has `k` rows and `n-k` columns. The allowed column-set weights are
`|det M[:,J]|^2`, and `det(M M^*)=det(V_I^*V_I)>0`. This is a rank-`k`
volume-sampling law and can be sampled exactly with polynomially many exact
determinant calculations. For a selected column prefix `S` of size `a` and
excluded prefix `B`, let `C=M[:,S]`, `q=k-a`, and `R` be the remaining
columns. If `C` is independent, set `P=I-C(C^*C)^{-1}C^*`. The total
completion mass is

    Z(S,B) = det(C^*C) * [t^q] det(I_k + t P M_R M_R^* P).

For `S=empty`, the determinant factor is one. If `C` is dependent, or there
are too few remaining columns, the mass is zero. The identity follows by
Schur complement for each completion and Cauchy--Binet for the sum of all
`q`-column minors. Each branch mass is a polynomial-bit rational computable
in polynomial time. Sequential exact rational branch sampling produces the
conditional `J` law in expected polynomial bit time; capping the random-bit
procedure adds an explicitly chosen abort/TV error. Applying this exact
conditional kernel to the approximate row law preserves its TV distance,
so the resulting joint `(I,J)` sampler is within the same stated bound.

This is a randomized low-sector TV sampler derived from the peer estimator;
the end-to-end implementation is not present. It is stated for the promise
`c_k(F)>0`, since a normalized sector law is undefined at zero. Exact
zero-sector handling can be joined to the deterministic support/zero route
from the frozen baseline; a bounded-error root FPRAS alone has a small
false-zero probability on positive instances. On the good row-prefix
estimation event, no unsupported row set is sampled. Any bad event is charged
to the stated confidence failure. It does not extend to `k=Theta(n)` under
the current `9^k` bound.

## 6. Attributed c03 peer scan

I read the frozen c03 Sol initial reports and the available c03 Luna
checkpoints after `ALL30_SOL_INITIALS_FROZEN`. This scan is a claim-interface
comparison, not independent verification of every peer proof or implementation.

- `c03_s01/INITIAL.txt`: exact Grassmann coefficient/prefix counter for
  bounded support-graph treewidth, with F-only logarithmic-treewidth
  acquisition claimed. It covers all sectors on that structural class and is
  complementary to low-`k` arbitrary-`F` approximation.
- `c03_s02/INITIAL.txt`: dense full-support positive family with exponentially
  slow stationary local pair-replacement chains, but its own report gives an
  easy two-endpoint approximation; it explicitly does not establish
  approximation hardness.
- `c03_s03/INITIAL.txt`: exact rational MPS/Grassmann algorithm for ordered
  matrices whose maximum cut-rank sum is logarithmic, including coefficients,
  prefixes, zero detection, and sampling. This is another structural class,
  not an all-`F` approximation theorem.
- `c03_l01/INITIAL.txt`: identifies the weighted Gram-volume versus constant-
  determinant Pfaffian-counting interface gap; no general FPRAS or hardness
  transfer is claimed.
- `c03_l02/INITIAL.txt` and `c03_l07/INITIAL.txt`: route-specific failures of
  literal bosonic KLM/bunching substitutions into fermionic FLO. These do not
  refute the low-sector estimator, and they do not transfer the N81 PP
  implication to the hard-parity model.
- `c03_l05/INITIAL.txt`: exact bounded `(sparse support treewidth + low-rank
  update)` counting claim; the general connectivity DP is not implemented in
  that checkpoint. It does not imply a generic approximation theorem.
- `c03_l09/INITIAL.txt`: exact polynomial-bit recurrence for at-most-one
  nonzero per row, with an executable finite check. It is a strict
  functional-digraph extension of the monomial class, still a structural
  subclass.
- `c03_l10/low_sector_pfaffian_fpras.txt`: source of the directly audited
  paired-volume estimator. The peer report itself limits the result to low
  `k`, discloses no end-to-end FPRAS implementation, and makes no hardness or
  novelty claim. The row-prefix projection and TV sampler above are my
  attributed derivation from that estimator.

These reports are mutually compatible: they close or propose distinct
tractable subclasses and one local-mixing obstruction, while none supplies a
general `k=Theta(n)` FPRAS or an approximation-preserving hardness reduction.
The l10 low-sector result is the strongest new downstream consequence for my
assigned normalization/TV interface.
