# Decisive retaining baseline: recursive known-support GCD interpolation

2026-10-07. Primary: Demin–van der Hoeven, `2312.17380v2.pdf`, §§2.3–2.4,2.7,3.1–3.2, particularly Theorem3.3 and Remark3.4. v2 dated25Feb2025 by the arXiv record. Read named sections directly. This reconstruction is ours; author theorem already gives the main algebraic cost in a supplied interpolation model. Independent mathematical audit PENDING. It substantially weakens any claim that the normalized-shadow candidate introduces a new characteristic-independent nearlinear GCD capability.

## Author claim and exact point of comparison

Theorem3.3: recursive GCD cost O(n((sbar/s+delta_degree) S(s)+s M(delta_degree) log(delta_degree))) with s=#G, sbar=#A+#B+#G, S sparse interpolation cost, and M dense multiplication cost. The proof acquires univariate GCDs and normalizes their value at the next coordinate's fixed point to the previously acquired projected GCD. Remark3.4 explicitly observes that exponents of the first k variables are known when reconstructing the next variable. §2.4 Remark2.4 abstracts away exponent-vector costs and uses derivatives as a char0 justification for S(s) depending only on sparsity. Hence the author statement itself should not be blindly treated as an explicit arbitrary-characteristic bit theorem. But that DOES NOT mean our shadow method gives a new capability: known-support interpolation eliminates unknown-exponent discrete logs or derivatives, as reconstructed below.

## Reconstruction without an unknown-support interpolation oracle

Let A,B be explicit sparse input polynomials, D numeric total degree, G their GCD with supplied upper bound T, over a sufficiently large finite field. Pick c_i, alpha_i in K* uniformly. Define A_j=A(x_1,...,x_j,c_{j+1},...,c_n), and B_j similarly. On the generic coprime-cofactor event, their GCD is G_j=G(x_1,...,x_j,c_{j+1},...,c_n), up to a field unit. Normalize G_0=1 (provided G(c)!=0).

Inductively G_{j-1} is known explicitly. Let E be its support in j-1 variables. Every monomial of G_j projects onto an element of E provided the nonzero coefficient polynomial in x_j of each projected monomial does not vanish at c_j. This is the critical no-cancellation event. It can be charged for every projection of G, at most nT coefficient polynomials of degree <=D, giving failure <=nTD/(Q-1). E contains no supplied target support: it was recovered at the preceding stage.

For e in E, put z_e=product_{i<j} alpha_i^{e_i}. Choose alpha so all z_e are distinct at every stage. Each pair difference is a nonzero polynomial of total degree <=D; at most nT^2 pairs suffice, so alias failure <=nT^2 D/(Q-1). Let t=|E|<=T. For r=1,...,t, acquire

  h_r(u)=gcd(A_j(alpha_1^r,...,alpha_{j-1}^r,u),
             B_j(alpha_1^r,...,alpha_{j-1}^r,u))

and normalize it so h_r(c_j)=G_{j-1}(alpha_1^r,...,alpha_{j-1}^r). On the generic specialization and nonvanishing events, this is exactly the correctly scaled G_j evaluated at the geometric sequence. Weight-dependent monomial offsets do not occur: the retained variable u is an original coordinate, so the univariate GCD keeps its proper monomial factor.

For each u degree k<=D, its coefficient in G_j has the form

  C_k(x_1,...,x_{j-1})=sum_{e in E} a_{k,e} x^e.

The acquired coefficients satisfy h_{r,k}=sum_e a_{k,e} z_e^r for r=1,...,t. The resulting matrix is the usual r=0,...,t-1 Vandermonde matrix multiplied by the invertible diagonal matrix diag(z_e). Thus a_{k,e} is found by solving the SAME known-node transposed Vandermonde system for each k. Its determinant is nonzero because z_e are distinct. No root extraction, primitive element, discrete logarithm, ordinary derivative, or large-characteristic assumption appears. Read the exponent of u directly from k. The sparse reconstructed G_j becomes the input support for the next stage. At j=n normalize by lex leading coefficient to return G.

## Cost reconstruction, including the missing input/exponent work

Each stage requires t<=T univariate GCDs of degree <=D, costing soft-O(TD) field operations with fast GCD. Each of D+1 coefficient vectors uses known-node Vandermonde inversion in soft-O(T) field operations (standard fast evaluation/interpolation/transposed evaluation; arbitrary characteristic with distinct nodes). Thus reconstruction costs soft-O(TD) per stage.

Acquiring the t univariate input images can ALSO be batched at soft-O(t_A+t_B+DT) field operations per stage, rather than t separate sparse evaluations. Group input terms by the exponent k of retained u. Their r-th coefficient is a power sum sum_l a_l z_l^r. The generating function of these coefficients is sum_l a_l/(1-z_l z), computed by balanced rational-function summation and truncated to order t. A group with s_k monomials costs soft-O(s_k+t); summing over <=D+1 groups costs soft-O(t_A+t_B+DT). Coincident input bases need only be combined; no distinct-node assumption is needed for power-sum generation. Dense materialization has the same DT cost. This is the explicit fast sparse evaluation mechanism hidden in the author's S(s) notation.

All original-input alpha products and c scaling products can be maintained by prefix/suffix products per term, with binary powers: O(n(t_A+t_B) log(D+1)) field operations total. For newly recovered projected GCD terms, update bases using the existing prefix basis times alpha_j^k. This costs O(nT log(D+1)) total. Exponent vectors contain n coordinates and are charged when emitted or projected. Stage-by-stage copying can be soft-O(n^2 T) integer-label bit work in a naive representation; a persistent prefix tree or parent pointer plus new coordinate avoids quadratic copy and emits the final nT logD bits once. Whether the full pipeline can implement that without extra overhead should be checked, but it does not impose a characteristic condition or a new support oracle.

Consequently the natural reconstruction costs

  soft-O(n((t_A+t_B)+TD)+n(t_A+t_B+T) log(D+1))

field operations, with field extension and bit representation separately charged. This is at least as competent an algebraic baseline as the proposed shadow method and usually has no R repetition factor. A conservative generic-field budget may be larger than the source theorem's compact statement because individual geometric specializations involve degrees up to rD; that enlarges log field size, not the numeric arithmetic cost. A full explicit error-budget proof and executable baseline are still needed before adopting the reconstruction as an independent theorem. Crucially, the mechanism is strongly indicated by the 2025 author's algorithm and remark, so novelty cannot be awarded to our broad consequence by ignoring it.

## Conservative explicit error-budget completion

This completion is our deduction, not the exact probability expression stated by the primary source. Define

  C_base=n^2 D^2+nTD+nT^2D+nT[2(T+1)D^2+4(T+1)D]+D.

Requiring Q-1>=C_base/delta suffices by the following conservative union bound (constant factors deliberately not optimized).

- The successive c projections keep cofactor coprimality with failure at most n^2D^2/(Q-1), using the author's resultant argument stage by stage. This event also fixes ideal projected G_j independent of alpha.
- Each nonempty projected G coefficient polynomial in suffix c variables has degree <=D; at most nT such support buckets exist. Exclude their zeros: nTD/(Q-1). Thus actual support at each stage is exactly the projected ideal support needed for known-node interpolation.
- Conditional on these c events, for every pair of distinct known projected exponents e,f, alpha^e-alpha^f is nonzero degree<=D. At most nT^2 pairs: nT^2D/(Q-1).
- For EVERY individual stage j and r=1,...,t<=T, coprime projected cofactors U_j,V_j over the prefix polynomial ring have a nonzero resultant in prior coordinates after retaining u. Its degree is <=2D^2. On replacing prior coordinates by alpha_i^r, the resultant remains nonzero because r>=1 makes this substitution injective on polynomials; its degree is <=2rD^2. Excluding leading/nonzero constant coefficients and the normalization polynomial G_{j-1}(alpha^r) adds at most4(r+1)D. The total is bounded by nT[2(T+1)D^2+4(T+1)D]/(Q-1). If both cofactors have u degreezero, use their nonzero values instead of a resultant; covered by the same allowance.
- Exclude G(c)=0, costing D/(Q-1), so the normalization G_0=1 corresponds to G/G(c).

On their simultaneous good event the induction and Vandermonde recovery above are exact. Each step accesses only previously acquired G support. Exclude excessive projected term counts or input degree violations before emitting output labels; bounded bad-event work is retained in the claimed algebraic cost.

r06 identified why the fourth event cannot be omitted: U=x_1-a,V=u-d,G=1, alpha^r=a, c_j!=d has a correct previous projected GCD and a nonzero normalization value but the acquired univariate gcd is u-d. Thus those intermediate conditions ALONE do not imply correct acquisition. This does not refute the 2025 theorem; it requires a separate alpha-specialized resultant exclusion in our reconstruction. We do not adopt the paper's shorter displayed failure expression without its own audit. r06 independently confirmed the corrected r>=1 injectivity/Vandermonde mechanism, but has not certified every constant in C_base.
