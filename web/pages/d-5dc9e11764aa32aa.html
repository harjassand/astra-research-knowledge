# Causal experimental design under confounding and assay cost

## Verdict

I reconstructed a sharp fixed-confidence cost law for one binary causal-mechanism contrast with perfect confounding, an unknown intercept, tunable measurement precision, and explicit intervention/measurement costs. The theorem is intentionally scoped. It does not yield a general adaptive causal-discovery result or a novelty claim.

## Result

The mechanisms

\[
H_0:\ Y=\alpha+(\Delta/2)U+\epsilon,
\qquad
H_1:\ Y=\alpha+\Delta X-(\Delta/2)U+\epsilon,
\]

with passive \(X=U\), have exactly the same observational law. Under \(\operatorname{do}(x)\), their endpoint means differ by \(\Delta x\). Because \(\alpha\) is unrestricted, one fixed-dose arm is not uniformly identifying. Fresh-unit balanced \(\operatorname{do}(+a),\operatorname{do}(-a)\) pairs cancel it; \(D=(Z_+-Z_-)/2\) has mean zero under \(H_0\) and \(\Delta a\) under \(H_1\).

For sub-Gaussian endpoint disturbance proxy \(B>0\), precision \(\tau\), intervention cost \(c_I(a)=c_0+\kappa_Ia^2\), and measurement cost \(c_M(\tau)=m_0+\gamma\tau\), define

\[
\eta(a,\tau)=
\frac{\Delta^2a^2}{2(B+1/\tau)(c_0+m_0+\kappa_Ia^2+\gamma\tau)},
\qquad \eta_*=\sup_{a,\tau}\eta(a,\tau).
\]

Every adaptive, sequential procedure with both error probabilities at most \(\delta<1/2\) has expected total cost at least \(\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)/\eta_*\) under each mechanism at the equal-intercept least-favourable Gaussian pair. The transcript KL chain rule proves this because each outcome's KL per cost is at most \(\eta_*\), regardless of how its action was selected from past data. The matching static action design repeats balanced endpoints at

\[
a_*=A,\qquad
\tau_*=
\sqrt{\frac{c_0+m_0+\kappa_IA^2}{B\gamma}}.
\]

An anytime paired e-process gives uniform error control over the stated nuisance class. At the least-favourable Gaussian point it is the SPRT and achieves expected cost \(\eta_*^{-1}\log(1/\delta)(1+o(1))\), matching the lower-bound leading constant. Thus adaptation in action choice does not improve the minimax rate in this binary model; adaptive stopping is shared by both controls.

## Prior-art and source status

The closest current adaptive-design source is [FOCUS, arXiv:2609.32165v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32165), which gives fixed-confidence adaptive recovery for fully observed linear-Gaussian DAGs with a max-min interventional-KL lower bound and a tracked design. [Hou, Park, and Li (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28856) directly study causal discovery with latent variables and selection; they identify a system-subgraph/MAG from single-target interventional regimes and bound constraint-query counts, but do not optimize intervention money or measurement precision. [Hauser and Bühlmann (2012)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/hauser12a.html) establish that interventions refine observational DAG equivalence classes under their assumptions. These sources rule out general adaptive-design novelty; the narrow candidate here is the exact latent-confounding pair plus separate precision/intervention charges and its no-action-adaptation law. Broader prior art and priority remain unresolved.
