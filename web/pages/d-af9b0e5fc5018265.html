# Positive exterior factor: a precise conductivity route and its unclosed global gate

7 October 2026, cycle 8. **The universal Euclidean scalar finite-index question remains UNKNOWN.** No positive exterior solution for the actual repaired potential was constructed, and no signed-boundary exclusion was proved. This note acquires a different possible construction interface and proves its local compatibility. It carefully distinguishes changing the pair from classifying the existing pair.

## Question and authoritative input

The actual pair is -Delta F+VF=0 from `notes/r15_geometric_analysis/cycle6/ADMISSIBLE_SLOW_ENVELOPES.md`. Its frequency a tends to infinity arbitrarily slowly, kappa=a^(3/2)/sqrt(R), E=exp(-integral a), and V=O(kappa) with every fixed derivative tending to zero. Every exponential times F is square integrable. The cycle-7 nodal proof establishes, for this same fixed construction,

    O={|q|<tau/2}, Delta F=0 on O,
    |partial_r F| >= c aE >0 outside O on a tail,
    |D^m F| <= C_m a^m E,
    |D^m Delta F| <= C_m kappa a^m E.

Consequently **every sufficiently distant critical point of F has an open harmonic neighborhood**. This is stronger than merely Delta F=0 at the critical point. It follows directly from the radial derivative lower bound, without changing the smoothing radius or any construction parameter.

Finite negative spectrum would provide a positive exterior solution phi of the ORIGINAL equation -Delta phi+Vphi=0. For such phi the already proved reduction gives |D log phi|->0 and v=F/phi solving div(phi^2 Dv)=0. The Rellich term J/H-r eta Q/H has no controlled sign. No argument below removes that term for the original pair.

## Alternative construction, with changed potential

Instead, try to realize the already supplied fast profile F itself as

    div(exp(h) DF)=0.                                      (1)

If a smooth real h solving (1) on an exterior domain also satisfies

    |Dh| ->0,  Delta h ->0,                                (2)

then define

    phi_tilde=exp(h/2),
    u_tilde=phi_tilde F,
    V_tilde=Delta(phi_tilde)/phi_tilde
             =(1/2)Delta h+(1/4)|Dh|^2.                    (3)

Direct differentiation yields

    Delta(phi_tilde F)-V_tilde(phi_tilde F)
       =phi_tilde[Delta F+Dh dot DF]=0.

Thus (1)-(2) would produce a real scalar exterior pair with V_tilde->0 and a positive exterior comparison solution phi_tilde. Since |Dh|->0 uniformly, radial integration gives |h(x)|<=epsilon|x|+C_epsilon for every epsilon>0. Hence u_tilde keeps every exponential L2 weight of F. On compact tests supported in that exterior,

    q_Vtilde(phi_tilde chi)=integral phi_tilde^2 |Dchi|^2 >=0.

This is a sufficient exterior construction certificate. It does NOT show V_tilde=V, and therefore does NOT classify the index of the original repaired potential. A whole-space counterexample additionally needs a smooth, bounded, nodally compatible compact completion of (u_tilde,V_tilde). A generic cutoff of h introduces the term Dh dot DF/F at zeros and is not a valid completion. The established exterior-positivity/finite-index theorem can be used only after such a global scalar pair exists.

## Local realization is already available everywhere in the tail

At a regular point DF!=0, the smooth flow-box theorem gives a transverse section Sigma and coordinates x=X(t,z) for the gradient flow X'=DF(X). For arbitrary smooth initial h0 on Sigma, set

    h(X(t,z))=h0(z)-integral_0^t Delta F(X(s,z)) ds.       (4)

Then Dh dot DF=-Delta F, proving (1) in that flow box. At a critical point in the tail, F is harmonic on an open neighborhood, so h=constant solves (1) there. Thus there is no remaining LOCAL isotropic-realizability obstruction for this specific profile. The statement supplies neither a common h on overlapping neighborhoods nor the bounds (2).

The transport problem can be normalized to expose the scales without dividing by DF. Put

    X=DF/(aE),  f=-Delta F/(aE).

These are globally smooth on the tail because aE>0; equation (1) is X dot Dh=f. The actual ledger gives

    |X|<=C, |DX|<=Ca,
    |f|<=C kappa/a ->0, |Df|<=C kappa ->0.

Outside O, |X|>=c, whereas f=0 throughout O. Therefore |f|/|X|<=C kappa/a at every noncritical point, interpreting it as zero inside O. The forcing per unit gradient-trajectory length is small. This is compatible with (2), but it only controls the directional derivative of h along the flow.

For any flow segment of the normalized X,

    h(X(t,z))-h(X(0,z))=integral_0^t f(X(s,z)) ds.       (5)

Differentiating the transport equation gives, along that trajectory,

    d(Dh)/dt = Df-(DX)^T Dh.                           (6)

Although f and Df tend to zero, DX may have size a->infinity. The transverse initial data and the integral in (5) must be chosen consistently when trajectories approach critical sets, escape to infinity, or approach separatrices. No uniform control of the fundamental matrix in (6), no globally compatible transverse data, and no bound on Delta h have been acquired. Small forcing along each curve is not a bound on transverse derivatives of its integral.

There is a particularly concrete global rejection test. Let K be any fixed compact subset of the exterior and let gamma_j(t), 0<=t<=T_j, be X-flow segments whose two endpoints lie in K, although the intermediate segments may escape arbitrarily far. A continuous solution h forces

    |integral_0^T_j f(gamma_j(t))dt| <= 2 sup_K |h|.       (7)

Thus unbounded excursion integrals rule out ANY globally continuous positive conductivity exp(h) on that exterior, before asking for derivative decay. More sharply, if the two endpoint sequences converge to p and q, their integrals must converge to the single value h(q)-h(p). Two incompatible limits reject realization even if all integrals remain bounded. These statements follow directly from (5); they are not inferred for the actual F. A gradient flow has no nonconstant periodic orbit, but that fact does not remove these compact-endpoint excursion constraints.

The same quantity has a flow-Jacobian interpretation. Since

    div X=-f-X dot D log(aE),

Liouville's determinant formula yields, on a regular flow segment,

    integral_0^T f(X(t,z))dt
      =-log det D_z X(T,z)-log[(aE)(X(T,z))/(aE)(z)].     (8)

Here D_z X(T,z) is the full spatial derivative of the time-T flow map, with T held fixed while differentiating. It is NOT the derivative of a variable-time boundary return map. Determinants are positive while the smooth flow exists. For compact endpoints the second term is uniformly bounded; a collapsing or expanding fixed-time flow volume supplies the same obstruction. Replacing the flow determinant by a return-map determinant without the normal-flux factor would be incorrect. Equations (7)-(8) provide a direct discriminating quantity for a future implementation of the actual macro transitions.

## Primary prior and a tempting invalid shortcut

This is the established isotropic-realizability problem, not a new general method. Briane-Milton-Treibergs, arXiv:1301.1613v1, submitted 8 January 2013, section 2.2 and Theorem 2.14, construct conductivity through the gradient flow. The displayed theorem retains assumptions (2.2)-(2.3): the gradient is periodic and nowhere zero. Its proof on PDF page 11 explicitly obtains a positive uniform lower bound on |Du|^2 and uses it to make trajectories cross a common equipotential. We visually checked that page. The abstract's brief phrase about global realization cannot be applied after silently dropping this hypothesis. Our DF tends to zero and may vanish.

Briane, arXiv:1306.0236v1, submitted 2 June 2013, introduction and Theorem 2.1, studies the critical-point problem. It formulates the Laplacian integral along the gradient flow as the relevant boundedness condition near saddle points. Its introduction gives inf|Du|>0 as the earlier global interface. The necessary condition Delta u(x*)=0 alone is not sufficient for local isotropic realization in its examples. Our open harmonic neighborhood bypasses that particular local issue, but not global transport and derivative control.
