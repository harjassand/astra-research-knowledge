# A conservative density inflation for the literal replacement template

7 October 2026. Independent reconstruction by pde_spectrum9, verified
GPT-6.1 Sol/max, no nested workers. This is an analytic count. No template
at the astronomical construction size was generated. The count concerns
the original Tanner 2-cells after removal of the auxiliary diagonals;
counting their auxiliary pieces can only increase the upper bound.

## Exact interface and verdict

Use Balasubramanian–Davydova–Lin, arXiv:2605.10943v1, the local template
in paper.tex lines1450–1498 and the primary replacement-flatten,
replacement-fold, replacement-full and replacement-full-RZ figures.
Each parent square uses the X–Z auxiliary diagonal. The flattening has
two in-plane square-grid layers and a perpendicular subcomplex of
thickness two. Its zero-area cells are precisely those perpendicular
strip cells; coincident abstract layers remain distinct.

Suppose the perturbed parent formal triangles have unit-ball density
at most t', side lower bound l, width lower bound w, and the actual
template grid has denominator ell. Assume

    ell/lambda < 2003, l >= (135/2)lambda,
    w >= (2/5)lambda, lambda >= 10^24.                 (1)

Then the original fine Tanner 2-cell density is at most F t', with the
safe integer

    F = 2^26.                                         (2)

The stronger geometry in rounding_spectral's readmission variant
(l>=78lambda,w>lambda) satisfies (1). Neither the source's literal
64pi coefficient nor its illustrative t/lambda pair is used. This closes
the template-count input to that peer's separate cap/LLL admission.

## The grid and unit edges

Put a flat parent square at z=(0,0), q=(0,1), x=(1,1), q'=(1,0).
The affine maps on the two sides of the X–Z diagonal are

    Phi(u,v)=z+u(q'-z)+v(x-q')       for u>=v,
    Phi(u,v)=z+u(x-q)+v(q-z)        for u<=v.           (3)

Each coordinate derivative is a parent Tanner side, of length strictly
less than ell. A fine unit axis step in the flat integer grid is a step
1/ell in (u,v), so its physical length is at most one. A step crossing
the diagonal has two such pieces and the same bound by their lengths.
Edges in the flattened transverse direction have zero length. Thus every
original fine Tanner edge has length at most one. This calculation pays
an auxiliary parent diagonal of length up to two before dilation; it
never assumes that diagonal is a unit Tanner edge.

A fine Tanner square has boundary paths of length at most two between
any two of its four vertices. Its image is the union of the affine
images of its diagonal pieces, all lying in the convex hull of those
vertices. Its whole image therefore has diameter at most two. This also
holds for a collapsed strip square. Every fine piece meeting a unit ball
is consequently contained in the radius-three ball about the same
center.

## Positive-area cells

Every positive piece is a triangle or square of the integer grid in one
of the two in-plane layers. The diagonal u=v meets the grid at integer
corners, so cutting along it produces whole elementary right triangles,
not an arbitrarily short sliver. The same holds after the X/Z color
swap in U_Z. An elementary triangle occupies the fraction 1/ell^2 of
its parent formal triangle. The parent area is at least lw/2: take its
longest side, whose perpendicular foot is on that side; its altitude is
at least the assumed width w, and its side length is at least l.
Hence each positive elementary piece has area at least

    delta = lw/(2ell^2) >= 27/(2*2003^2).              (4)

Within a fixed parent formal triangle and a fixed in-plane layer the
piece interiors are disjoint. If a positive fine Tanner face meets a
unit ball, assign it to one of its pieces meeting the ball. That piece
is contained in the parent plane's radius-three disk. Its area is at
least delta. Assigning a face only once can only reduce the count.
The two layers therefore contribute at most

    2*9pi/delta < 72/delta                            (5)

faces per parent formal triangle meeting the ball, using pi<4. The
argument counts the two coincident layers separately. Identifications
between different parent templates decrease the quotient count and do
not invalidate the estimate.

## Collapsed strip cells

The actual primary flattening figure is needed here. It displays one
L-shaped perpendicular strip of thickness two. In the flat U_X grid its
two arms project onto the inset axis lines u=1/ell and v=1/ell; in U_Z
the mirrored lines are u=1-1/ell and v=1-1/ell. End and corner portions
are included. Splitting either arm at u=v gives at most two affine
segments. Keeping both arm labels, both possible layer boundary labels,
and the two diagonal sides gives the loose bound of eight straight
arrays per parent formal triangle. This is an upper bound on the
literal template, not a bound for an arbitrary added folded strip.

Along each such array a flat longitudinal unit step maps to a parent
Tanner-side vector divided by ell. Its physical length is at least

    h = l/ell.                                        (6)

Successive noncorner intervals have disjoint interiors on their straight
carrier line. A unit ball cuts out an interval of length at most two
on that line; no more than 2/h+4 longitudinal intervals can meet it.
The extra four cover the two ends and any diagonal cut. The strip has
two transverse grid rows, and the source doubles only the two outer
layers. Even if one counts both auxiliary pieces and retains every
boundary label before quotient gluing, there are at most sixteen
2-cell occurrences per longitudinal interval. This deliberately pays
more than the literal two-row strip requires.

At an L-junction or endpoint the remaining cells occupy the fixed
integer corner boxes, of side at most four in the two in-plane
directions and thickness two in the transverse direction. Counting
both layer faces and both strip directions gives fewer than 256
additional occurrences per parent formal triangle. Thus a safe bound is

    Z <= 8*16*(2ell/l+4)+256 < 2^14.                  (7)

Every such occurrence maps inside its parent formal triangle; a parent
triangle whose strip contributes to the ball itself meets that ball.
No bigger parent ball and no bound on the number of unrelated coincident
sheets have been inserted for free. Accidental physical overlap cannot
increase the count beyond summing the abstract arrays; identified
boundary cells are already overcounted by that sum.

Combining (5) and (7), the per-triangle count is strictly below

    72*2*2003^2/27 + 2^14 < 2^25 < F.                (8)

Multiplying by the number, at most t', of parent formal triangles
meeting the ball proves (2). The finite seed density is also below F
and can be absorbed in that same enlarged constant. The counting proof
works for both alternating replacements because U_Z is the literal
color-swapped mirror of the same grids and collapsed strip.

## Admission and limits

Equation (2) supplies an actual fixed F, independent of lambda, depth
and code size, to GEOMETRIC_READMISSION.md. That note chooses t'=100k,
k=300,t=Ft' and a finite integer lambda satisfying its displayed
symmetric LLL inequality, while preserving (1). Its fixed-plane hitting
bound and finite dependency count are separate derivations; their
readmission must be cited rather than replaced by (2).

The conclusion is mathematical existence of a legal constant-density
family once the cap/LLL argument is adopted. It is not an execution of
geometric acquisition, an exact finite-bit event oracle, an improved
practical density, a generic perturbation theorem, or a perturbed
thermal lifetime result. The source's abstract unperturbed thermal
analysis still needs its own hypotheses and charges.

The decisive falsifier here would be a literal extra transverse sheet
whose number or thickness grows with ell, a diagonal not aligned with
the flat integer grid, or a nonzero elementary piece smaller than (4).
Those do not occur in the inspected X–Z source realization; this note
does not claim a density count for another replacement template.
