## Exact theorem and acquisition interface

Let Q be real symmetric. Normalize its positive diagonal by a proof-only positive real diagonal congruence, and suppose the resulting matrix has the form

    Q = I - B,  B_ii=0, B_ij=B_ji>=0,
    beta = max_i sum_(j!=i) B_ij <= 3/16.                 (1)

Equivalently, before normalization, Q has nonpositive off-diagonal entries and

    max_i sum_(j!=i) |Q_ij| / sqrt(Q_ii Q_jj) <= 3/16.

The spectral norm of B is at most beta, so (1) implies Q>0. This is an explicit weak-coupling promise: the normalized core has eigenvalues in[13/16,19/16] and condition number at most19/13. It is not an all-conditioned-Stieltjes theorem. The original diagonal scaling and L may have arbitrarily large condition number, and no numerical condition parameter, connectedness, rank-one structure, nonzero off-diagonal assumption, permutation, block partition, metric, attracting point or rational normalizing diagonal is supplied. In particular, arbitrary connected nonclique graphs are allowed. The constant is sufficient, not asserted optimal.

For any unknown real invertible L, put f(x)=((Lx)^2)^T Q((Lx)^2). From f alone define

    H=Hess f, h=det H, F=adj(H) grad h,
    D_F=F dot grad,
    E_F=det[x,D_F x,...,D_F^(n-1)x].                     (2)

Then E_F is a nonzero polynomial, and every hidden coordinate hyperplane divides it. This real/algebraic nonvanishing statement does not presume rational hidden axes.

For the exact learner, separately impose the established rational-hidden interface: only the expanded rational quartic is given, and it admits an unknown rational invertible L with rational Q>0 satisfying the structural promise. Under that interface, the unchanged flow-line, rational-reflection, acquired invariant-SPD, axis-selection and final positive weighted-square certificate procedure is an expected polynomial-bit acquisition algorithm. Hidden data may have arbitrarily large condition number; neither the data nor a conditioning estimate is consulted. Degree remains

    deg F=4n-3,
    deg E_F=n+2n(n-1)^2.                                (3)

## 1. Log masses turn the Hessian determinant into a concave function

Work in the normalized hidden real coordinates y. Set

    m_i=y_i^2>0, z_i=log m_i,
    Y=diag(sqrt(m)), P=diag(m), f=m^T Qm.

Direct differentiation of the quartic gives

    H=4diag(Qm)+8YQY,
    K(z)=Y^-1 H Y^-1
        =12I-8B-4diag(sum_j B_ij exp(z_j-z_i)),
    h=(product_i m_i) det K(z).                         (4)

K is Loewner-concave: in every direction u its second derivative is the diagonal matrix

    K''_ii=-4 sum_j B_ij exp(z_j-z_i)(u_j-u_i)^2 <=0.

Therefore the domain K>0 is convex. On it, along any line,

    (log det K)''
       =tr(K^-1 K'')-tr(K^-1 K' K^-1 K') <=0.          (5)

The second term is the squared Frobenius norm of K^-1/2 K' K^-1/2, and the first is nonpositive. Hence log h(e^z)=sum_i z_i+log det K(z) is concave. This is a log-mass statement, not the false general-QPD assertion that h(m)^(1/n) is concave in linear masses.

## 2. On Qm>0, log f is strictly convex modulo radial scaling

For any positive definite Q, including our Stieltjes matrix, set

    C=PQP>0, c=C1, c_i=m_i(Qm)_i, f=1^T C1=sum_i c_i.

If Qm>0, every c_i is positive. Direct differentiation gives

    grad_z log f=2c/f,
    Hess_z log f=(2/f)(C+diag(c))-(4/f^2)cc^T.         (6)

For every u, both Cauchy inequalities

    (c^T u)^2 <= f u^T C u,
    (c^T u)^2 <= f sum_i c_i u_i^2

hold, the first in the C inner product and the second in the positive diagonal(c) inner product. Summing proves that (6) is positive semidefinite. Equality in the first inequality already forces u to be a constant vector because C>0. Thus its kernel is exactly span{1}.

The projective function

    G(z)=log h(e^z)-(n/2)log f(e^z)                    (7)

consequently has a negative-definite Hessian on 1-perpendicular wherever Qm>0 and K>0. It is invariant under z -> z+a1. No claim is made that log f remains convex on every positive-Hessian mass point outside Qm>0.

## 3. A compact ratio box stays strictly inside both positive domains

Let R=3/2 and consider

    Z_R={z: sum_i z_i=0, max_i z_i-min_i z_i<=log R}.

This is compact and convex. Projective quantities are unchanged by rescaling m, so for estimates choose min_i m_i=1, max_i m_i<=R. Then

    (Qm)_i=m_i-(Bm)_i >=1-R beta >=23/32>0.

Also H=4diag(Qm)+8YQY>0. Thus G is smooth on a neighborhood of the entire box and has strictly negative Hessian on its tangent hyperplane when n>=2. The n=1 projective problem is trivial.

## 4. Exact determinant derivatives force every boundary point inward

Define

    A=Y H Y=P K P=12P(I-E)P,
    E=(1/3)diag(Bm/m)+(2/3)B,
    xi_i=(A^-1)_ii=(K^-1)_ii/m_i^2,
    alpha_i=partial_(z_i) log h.

Differentiating (4) entry by entry yields the exact identity

    alpha_i=1+4 sum_j B_ij m_i m_j(xi_i-xi_j).          (8)

For clarity, the positive term in the trace derivative is 4(K^-1)_ii sum_j B_ij m_j/m_i and the negative terms are 4 sum_j(K^-1)_jj B_ij m_i/m_j; substituting xi gives (8).

E is symmetric entrywise nonnegative. Its row sums are at most

    (R+2)beta/3 <=7/32.

The Neumann series gives nonnegative (I-E)^-1 and its row norm at most32/25. With our proof-only minimum mass1,

    0<xi_i<=1/(12m_i^2) *32/25 <=8/75.

In (8), the absolute difference of two positive xi's is bounded by their common upper bound, without an extra factor2. Consequently

    |alpha_i-1| <=4 R^2 beta*(8/75) <=9/50.            (9)

At any boundary point, let i be a maximum mass coordinate and j a minimum, so m_i=R and m_j=1 in this scale. Then

    c_i=m_i^2-m_i(Bm)_i >=R^2(1-beta)>=117/64,
    c_j<=1,
    c_i-c_j>=53/64,
    f=sum c_i<=sum m_i^2<=n R^2,
    n/f>=4/9.

Using G_i=alpha_i-n c_i/f gives the strict estimate

    G_i-G_j <=9/25-(4/9)(53/64)=-29/3600<0.           (10)

When extrema are repeated, define a tangent direction which lowers every maximum coordinate by1/number(maxima), raises every minimum coordinate by1/number(minima), and leaves the remaining coordinates fixed. Its coordinate sum is zero; a sufficiently small positive displacement enters the box strictly. Its G derivative is the average minimum G_j minus the average maximum G_i, which is positive by (10). Therefore no boundary point maximizes G.

G attains its unique maximum z_* in the box interior. At that point its gradient in 1-perpendicular vanishes; its total gradient vanishes too because shift invariance gives sum_i G_i=0. Its transverse Hessian is negative definite. Thus it is a full-support nondegenerate projective maximum of h/f^(n/2). This is an existence proof; the learner does not optimize G or receive z_*.

## 5. The coefficient-derived field has an open attracting projective basin

Choose the positive representative p_i=sqrt(exp(z_*i)), and rescale it to f(p)=1. The positive-orthant mass/log maps are smooth local projective charts. They transfer the negative transverse Hessian of G to a negative-definite constrained Hessian of log h on f=1.

Where H>0, define V=H^-1 grad log h=F/h^2. Homogeneity gives

    Hy=3 grad f, grad f dot V=2n/3.

On f=1, the tangent field

    X=V-(n/6)y                                         (11)

is the gradient of log h for the induced positive Hessian metric. Its equilibrium p satisfies V(p)=(n/6)p. The negative-definite constrained Hessian makes the linearized tangent field have only negative real eigenvalues. Hence p has an open attracting basin on f=1.

The cone over this basin is open in the full ambient space. For an unnormalized V orbit x=r y with f(y)=1,

    r_dot=(n/6)r^-3, dtheta/dt=r^-4,
    r^4=r(0)^4+(2n/3)t.

The projective clock theta tends to infinity. Thus generic points in that open cone converge projectively to the same ray p. Multiplication by h^2>0 to obtain F preserves the orbits; possible finite-time radial escape of the polynomial field does not remove their limiting direction.

## 6. The full-support basin proves E_F is nonzero

Here is the cycle10 sign-orbit argument, reproduced to expose the exact analytic step. Suppose E_F vanished. Let r<n be the first rational dependence index of y,D_F y,... . The first r vectors span a rational rank-r space W(y). Differentiating the dependence shows that every subsequent derivative belongs to W.

At a generic regular point, choose a rational basis for W. Its derivative lies in W, so along a local analytic trajectory W is a fixed ambient subspace; equivalently, every fixed normal to its initial span annihilates the whole local trajectory. That scalar identity extends along the analytic trajectory through poles of the chosen rational chart. The fixed initial W therefore contains the projective limiting ray p for generic points in the open basin. All augmented rank minors vanish on an open set, hence p belongs to W(y) as a rational identity globally.

The hidden sign group commutes with F and gives W(sy)=sW(y). Applying the preceding global identity at sy shows that every s p belongs to generic W(y). Since p has no zero coordinate, its sign orbit spans R^n, contradicting rank r<n. Thus E_F is nonzero. No continuum-of-maxima convergence assertion or all-axis-attractor premise is used.

Every F_i is divisible by y_i because F is sign-equivariant. All D_F^k y_i are therefore divisible by y_i. This proves hidden-hyperplane divisibility of E_F. If f_x(x)=f_y(Lx), direct chain rules give F_x(x)=(det L)^4 L^-1 F_y(Lx), and hence

    E_(f_x)(x)=(det L)^[2n(n-1)-1] E_(f_y)(Lx).

Thus both divisibility and nonvanishing transport through the unknown L and the proof-only normalization, over R or C.
