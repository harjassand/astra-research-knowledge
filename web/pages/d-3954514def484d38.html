RESULT

I reconstructed the two admitted canonical algorithms in the baseline and
proved a strict exact extension of the monomial/permutation class. For every
explicit rational-complex pair matrix with at most one nonzero entry in each
row, arbitrary column collisions, arbitrary rational onsite squared weights
0 <= t_j (including hard zeros and strong attraction), and any requested
pair number k, all norm coefficients and all row/column-prefix counts are
computable exactly by a deterministic polynomial-bit dynamic program. The
support graph is a partial functional digraph: each component is a rooted
in-tree ending at a zero row, or an in-tree feeding one directed cycle. The
cycle transfer matrix from the permutation case survives; a two-state tree
message handles arbitrary repeated column images. No log-concavity or imported
FPRAS is used for this extension.

This is a costed tractable subclass of the determinant-weighted parity / BCS
counting problem, not a solution to general parity. It strictly extends the
reported permutation class and includes non-bipartite supports with repeated
columns (a tested example has self-cycles and in-trees). I do not claim
literature novelty, external review, physical usefulness, or a general
approximation boundary.

RECONSTRUCTION OF THE EXISTING BIPARTITE CANONICAL ALGORITHM

Input is an explicit F in Q(i)^(n x n), a supplied/acquired site bipartition
A union B with F_AA=F_BB=0, rational t_i in [0,2], and requested pair sector
k. The physical amplitude on |U|=|D|=k is
  (-1)^(k(k-1)/2) det F[U,D] product_(i in U intersect D) sqrt(t_i).
Particle-hole transformation on both spins at every B site takes the state to
a rank-2|B| graph Slater matrix G=[M;I], with
  M=[[0,F_AB],[-F_BA^T,0]].
The transformed local squared-filter tables are (1,1,t_i) on A and
(t_i,1,1) on B. Total transformed occupation is 2|B|, and the physical pair
number equals occupation in the A-color modes. Thus the exact sector norm is
the coefficient [x^E] f(x)g_A(x_A)g_B(x_B), where
  f(x)=det(G* diag(x) G)
  g_A=sum_(|T_A|=2|A|-k) prod_(i in A) b_i(2-|T_A intersect E_i|) x^T_A
  g_B=sum_(|T_B|=k)      prod_(i in B) b_i(2-|T_B intersect E_i|) x^T_B.
The two filter polynomials use disjoint variables. Their local factorial
complement inequality is 2 b_i(1)^2 >= b_i(0)b_i(2), exactly t_i<=2;
zero entries have interval support, so hard projection is included. Together
with determinant-source log-concavity this fits the imported homogeneous
log-concave subset-convolution FPRAS, provided positive support witnesses,
coefficient-range bounds, and value oracles are supplied. The internal
reports also charge the exact support/prefix programs and inherited N63
reversible compiler. The count gate and compiler were not executed. The
reported per-count inherited bound is O(m^10 B^2 eps^-2 log^2(2B/eps))
value-oracle/arithmetic work, m=2n and B=m+ln(R_f R_g), plus acquisition,
bit arithmetic, prefixes, reversible histories, phases, and rotations.
There is no inverse original filter or sector probability in that conditional
bound. This route is for bipartite PAIR-MATRIX support, not a bipartite lattice
or Hamiltonian alone.

RECONSTRUCTION OF THE EXISTING PERMUTATION DYNAMIC PROGRAM

For F[i,p(i)]=f_i with p a permutation, a row set I has one possible
nonzero column set J=p(I). Its minor magnitude squared is prod_(i in I)w_i,
w_i=|f_i|^2. Therefore
  Z(s)=sum_I s^|I| prod_(i in I)w_i prod_(j in I intersect p(I))t_j.
Writing x_i=1[i in I], the only interactions are along the disjoint cycles of
p. In cycle order the transition from previous bit a= x_(p^-1(i)) to b=x_i
is
  T_i[a,b]=(s w_i)^b t_i^(a b),   a,b in {0,1},
and Z is the product over cycles of tr(product_i T_i). These are degree-one
polynomial matrix entries; multiplying them computes all coefficients in
O(n^2) rational operations. A fixed row bit deletes one matrix column; a
fixed down-site bit is a fixed row bit at p^-1(j). Thus exact canonical
coefficients and every prefix count are available, with O(n^2) work per
query and O(n^3) for a straightforward n-query Born self-reduction. This is
the baseline's exact transfer algorithm, not the Chen-Liu count route.

NEW FUNCTIONAL-DIGRAPH EXTENSION: CLAIM AND FULL RECURRENCE

Let each nonzero row i have its unique supported column p(i) and entry f_i;
zero rows have p(i)=None and w_i=0. No column-degree restriction is imposed.
For any selected row set I and column set J, F[I,J] can be nonsingular only
if J=p(I) and p is injective on I. In that case the submatrix is a monomial
matrix and |det F[I,p(I)]|^2=prod_(i in I)w_i. Thus the exact BCS sector norm
is
  c_k = sum_(|I|=k, p|_I injective)
          prod_(i in I) w_i prod_(j in I intersect p(I)) t_j.
With x_i=1[i in I] and y_j=sum_(i:p(i)=j)x_i, injectivity is exactly
y_j in {0,1}. Hence
  Z(s)=sum_(x in {0,1}^n) 1[y_j<=1 for all j]
       s^(sum_i x_i) prod_i w_i^x_i prod_j t_j^(x_j y_j),
with c_k=[s^k]Z(s). A forced column bit is y_j; a forced row bit is x_i.

Every weak component of the directed graph i -> p(i) has either one sink or
one directed cycle, with in-trees feeding it. For a noncycle node j, let C_j
be its children (rows mapping to j), and let W_i(b) be the polynomial for
the subtree below child i conditional on x_i=b. After enforcing child
capacity, the two possibilities at j are
  A_0 = product_(i in C_j) W_i(0),
  A_1 = sum_(c in C_j) W_c(1) product_(i in C_j\{c}) W_i(0).
They represent zero or exactly one selected child. Let eta_j in {0,1,bot}
be a forced column bit or no constraint, and let rho_j in {0,1,bot} be a
forced row bit or no constraint. The subtree messages are
  W_j(x) = 1[rho_j is bot or rho_j=x] (s w_j)^x
           ( 1[eta_j in {bot,0}] A_0
             + 1[eta_j in {bot,1}] t_j^x A_1 ),  x in {0,1}.
At a sink, sum W_j(0)+W_j(1) to obtain its component polynomial. If p_j=None,
then w_j=0, so selecting that zero row contributes no positive term.

For a cycle vertex j, compute A_0,A_1 from only its off-cycle tree children.
Let r be the selected bit of its cycle predecessor and x the bit of j. Its
2-by-2 polynomial transition is
  M_j[r,x] = 1[rho_j is bot or rho_j=x] (s w_j)^x
     * ( 1[eta_j is bot or eta_j=r] t_j^(r x) A_0
         + 1[r=0 and eta_j in {bot,1}] t_j^x A_1 ).
The first term has no selected off-cycle child, so total y_j=r. The second
term is possible only at r=0 and has exactly one selected tree child, so
y_j=1. For a directed cycle v_0,...,v_(ell-1) with p(v_i)=v_(i+1), sum
cyclic predecessor/current bits to obtain
  Z_cycle(s)=tr(M_v0 M_v1 ... M_v(ell-1)).
Multiply all sink and cycle component polynomials. This proves the formula
for all coefficients and for every simultaneous row/column prefix: tree
messages enforce each local incoming capacity once, and the trace sums the
remaining cycle bits once. Self-loops are cycles of length one; their
predecessor bit is the vertex's own bit, giving the expected t_j^x factor.
When p is a permutation there are no off-cycle tree children and these M_j
are exactly the baseline T_j. Thus the earlier algorithm is a special case.

COST, ACQUISITION, AND DOWNSTREAM USE

The support map p and rational weights are directly read from F; each row
support is checked by exact zero tests. Build children, peel trees, and find
cycles in O(n) graph operations. Maintain A_0 and A_1 incrementally over
children. Naive polynomial multiplication is exact. Across tree merges,
each pair of input vertices from different child subtrees is combined at
its unique branch point; cycle matrix products and component products combine
each cross-component pair at most once. There are O(n^2) such pairs, so all
coefficients or one prefix-restricted polynomial take O(n^2) rational
arithmetic operations. The code uses naive coefficient convolution.

Each nonzero term uses each w_i and each t_j at most once. If L is the total
rational input encoding length, a common denominator formed from all input
denominators has O(L) bits; each coefficient sums at most 2^n terms, so its
numerator/denominator lengths are O(L+n). Exact rational arithmetic is
therefore polynomial-bit. One coefficient c_k is an exact canonical norm;
there is no approximation ratio or rarity penalty. A straightforward
self-reduction uses n prefix queries, hence O(n^3) rational operations per
Born sample (plus random-bit/precision work). An exact random-bit sampler has
expected polynomial work; with a capped bit procedure one must report the
small abort probability explicitly. A coherent basis loader can reversibly
simulate the same prefix-count circuit and apply the monomial determinant
phase, but no reversible circuit or gate synthesis was implemented here.

The target state phases are explicit: the selected f_i phases, the sign of
the permutation p|_I, and (-1)^(k(k-1)/2). The y_j bits give D=p(I). This
allows the standard conditional-amplitude construction from exact prefix
norms in polynomial arithmetic size, with precision for square roots and
phase rotations charged separately. I count exact norms and the classical
self-reduction as proved; I leave coherent execution as a construction
blueprint pending implementation.

BOUNDARIES / REMAINING GATE

- At most one nonzero per row is load-bearing. With two entries in a row,
  multiple column sets can contribute and determinant cancellations return;
  the functional-digraph recurrence no longer applies. No hardness follows.
- This does not close weighted matroid-parity counting for general F. The
  audited alternating-cycle and tensor counterexamples refute particular
  bounded-block FLC walks and positive-overlay encodings, not every sampler.
- The bipartite Chen-Liu/N63 result is a separate conditional route and its
  imported count/compiler implementations remain unrun.
- Hamada-Iwata's deterministic linear-matroid-parity theorem supplies an
  unweighted support witness only; it is not a weighted partition-function
  algorithm.
- The next decisive step for this work is a targeted priority search for the
  exact one-row-functional weighted BCS model, followed by a reversible
  implementation/resource audit and a falsification attempt at n>8. General
  parity remains open.
