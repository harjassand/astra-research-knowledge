## Exact promise and distinction from general proof search

The input is an expanded rational homogeneous polynomial f in n variables, of degree 2h with h>=2, and a rational positive-definite matrix M. There exist an unknown rational invertible L and an unknown positive diagonal B such that

    M=L^T B L,   y=B^(1/2)Lx,
    f(x)=H(y_1^2,...,y_n^2),

where H is a homogeneous degree-h polynomial whose restriction to the probability simplex is strictly convex. No H,L,B, hidden axes, eigenvalue gap, attraction neighborhood, or condition number is input. M is supplied in this broader theorem. Since M and L are rational, B=L^(-T) M L^(-1) is rational, although its square roots need not be.

There is a randomized exact algorithm recovering all n rational primal coordinate rays L^(-1)e_i up to scale and permutation in expected bit cost polynomial in n, h, and the rational input length. The bound is polynomial in the **numerical degree h**, not log h for a sparse binary-exponent representation. Fixed-degree inputs, including h=2, have ordinary polynomial bit complexity. A finite trial budget gives a scoped failure probability under the promise; unconditional soundness of a nonnegativity certificate requires the final exact verifier described below.

This is not an algorithm for recognizing arbitrary hidden convexity, acquiring an arbitrary missing metric, deciding general nonnegativity, or synthesizing all mathematical proofs. The original quartic problem acquires its M directly from the lower-degree coefficients; that separate fact is why the broader supplied-metric theorem solves its original coefficient-only interface.

## 1. Rational vector field and polynomial degree

Compute F=(1/2)M^(-1) grad f and D=F dot grad, all from rational input. In the hidden coordinates,

    D y_i = y_i H_i(m),   m_i=y_i^2,   H_i=partial_i H.

The unknown rational form ell_i(x)=(Lx)_i divides D ell_i and all its subsequent D iterates. Set

    E(x)=det[D^k x_i]_(0<=k<n,1<=i<=n).

Each row k is homogeneous of degree 1+(2h-2)k. If E is nonzero, its degree is

    d=n+(h-1)n(n-1).

Changing the linear coordinate basis changes E by a nonzero constant and transports D. Choosing a column basis containing ell_i shows ell_i divides E. This divisibility mechanism is classical extactic/Darboux theory. The next step is the essential coverage argument: E cannot vanish identically under the promised hidden convexity.

## 2. A maximum vertex supplies a strict attracting direction

Choose j maximizing H(e_j). For i!=j, put phi(t)=H((1-t)e_j+t e_i), 0<=t<=1. Strict convexity and differentiability imply

    phi'(0) < phi(1)-phi(0) <= 0.

Indeed equality between the initial derivative and endpoint secant, together with the supporting-line inequality for a differentiable convex function, would force phi to equal that line throughout the interval. Thus

    alpha_i=H_i(e_j)-H_j(e_j)<0.

Write r_i=y_i/y_j with y_j>0, and change time by ds/dt=y_j^(2h-2). Homogeneity yields

    dr_i/ds=r_i[H_i(r_1^2,...,1,...,r_n^2)
                   -H_j(r_1^2,...,1,...,r_n^2)],
    d(log y_j)/ds=H_j(r_1^2,...,1,...,r_n^2).

A sufficiently small cube about r=0 makes every bracket at most alpha_i/2<0. It is forward invariant and every r_i decays exponentially in s. The coefficient in the second equation is bounded there, so y_j stays positive and finite for every finite s, whether it ultimately grows or shrinks. Consequently an open real cone of trajectories approaches the j-axis in projective coordinates. No quantitative rate from the unknown H is required by the algorithm.

## 3. Hereditary attracting directions force a nonzero extactic

Induct on n. For n=1, E=x_1 is nonzero. Restricting to y_j=0 commutes with D because that coordinate ideal is invariant. The restriction of H to the remaining simplex is strictly convex. By induction, the Wronskian minor W with columns y_i, i!=j, and rows D^k, 0<=k<=n-2, is not identically zero: its restriction to y_j=0 is the lower-dimensional extactic.

If E were zero, Cramer's rule over the rational-function field would give coefficients c_i(y) satisfying

    D^k y_j=sum_(i!=j)c_i D^k y_i,  0<=k<=n-2.

Vanishing of E supplies the same relation at k=n-1. Differentiate each of the first n-1 identities and subtract its next identity. Invertibility of W then gives D c_i=0 for all i. In particular y_j=sum c_i y_i with rational first-integral coefficients.

Choose an initial point in the attracting cone with det W!=0. Near that point the c_i are constant along its trajectory. Therefore the analytic identity

    y_j(s)=sum_(i!=j)c_i(initial)y_i(s)

holds for all finite s by analytic continuation of the polynomial ODE in rescaled time. This formulation crosses any later pole of a rational representative without evaluating it there. Dividing by y_j(s) and sending s to infinity gives 1=0. This contradiction proves E!=0.

Only the polynomial minor W was restricted to y_j=0; the possibly pole-bearing rational c_i were never restricted. The argument needs neither distinct decay exponents nor a theorem identifying higher-dimensional rational first integrals with ratios of fixed linear forms. Those tempting shortcuts are invalid in this generality.

## 4. One rational line recovers a complete candidate pool

Choose integer vectors u,v independently and uniformly from a finite grid. Compute the univariate polynomials f_E(t)=E(u+tv) and g_i(t)=(partial_i E)(u+tv), without expanding E in all its variables. Factor f_E over the rationals. For each rational root r of multiplicity e, form

    c(r,e)=[(d/dt)^(e-1)g_i(t)|_(t=r)]_i.

If E=ell^e R, ell(v)!=0, and R(u+rv)!=0 at r=-ell(u)/ell(v), then

    c(r,e)=e! ell(v)^(e-1) R(u+rv) grad ell.

Thus repeated factors are retained. A zero vector is discarded; additional rational roots may give additional nonzero candidates and cause no problem.

For each of the n hidden forms, failure is avoided by ell_i(v)!=0 and R_i(ell_i(v)u-ell_i(u)v)!=0. The latter polynomial is nonzero because R_i is not divisible by ell_i, and its argument ranges over the entire hyperplane ell_i=0. The product of all bad-event polynomials has total degree at most n+2n(d-1)<2nd. A grid of K>8nd values makes failure probability below 1/4. Hence one good line yields at most d rational covectors containing every hidden coordinate normal.

Convert each covector c to w=M^(-1)c. Its observable rational score is

    score(w)=f(w)/(w^T M w)^h.

On the unit ellipsoid this equals H(m) on the simplex. A strictly convex function on a simplex has only vertices as possible global maxima: a nonvertex is a nontrivial convex combination of vertices and has value strictly less than their weighted average. A pool containing all hidden rays therefore has a hidden axis as its maximum-score member. Filter the **original** pool by exact M-orthogonality to that ray and repeat. Restriction to a principal face preserves strict convexity, so all n axes are recovered. No repeated approximate deflation or repeated transformed-input acquisition occurs.

**Acceptance on possibly bad lines.** Before accepting each selected ray w, verify exact constrained stationarity and negative definiteness of the constrained Hessian on the current tangent space. These are rational checks: put s=w^T M w, lambda=2h f(w)/s, form a rational basis T for the vectors M-orthogonal to w and all previously accepted rays, check T^T grad f(w)=0, and require

    T^T [Hess f(w)-lambda M] T < 0.

In dimension one the tangent space is zero-dimensional and the remaining ray is the only axis. A failed check or exhausted candidate pool restarts the trial. Sylvester/LDL sign tests require no numerical eigenvalue margin.

Under the promise these checks can accept only a hidden axis. In normalized hidden coordinates, a stationary point with at least two active coordinates has an active-support tangent v. Writing a=y circ v, one has sum a_i=0, and the great-circle second derivative is

    4 a^T Hess H(m) a + 2 sum_i v_i^2[H_i(m)-sum_j m_j H_j(m)].

Stationarity makes the second term vanish on the active support; convexity of H restricted to that face makes the first term nonnegative. Hence such a point cannot have negative-definite tangent Hessian. Conversely the maximum vertex chosen on a good line has strictly negative transverse Hessian entries 2[H_i(e_j)-H_j(e_j)], so every good-line stage passes. Induction over accepted exact axes makes the face promise available at the next stage. An unlucky line therefore either yields a valid full axis frame or restarts. This is the missing acceptance step that turns a high-probability candidate generator into promised-class Las Vegas recovery.

The final rational matrix V can also be checked for invertibility and M-orthogonality. Under the promise it is exactly L^(-1) times a nonsingular monomial matrix. Merely finding an even polynomial in some coordinates would not suffice: (x^2-y^2)^2 is even in both its original and 45-degree rotated coordinates, but only the original contrast is strictly convex on its simplex. No general convexity or nonnegativity recognition is asserted outside the promise.

## 5. Charged bit complexity

Formal flow jets compute E and its ambient gradient on the chosen line. Solve X'=F(X), X(0)=u+tv, through order n-1 in formal time s; differentiate that recurrence in each initial coordinate. Row k of the extactic is k! times the k-th flow coefficient. Its univariate degree is at most 1+(2h-2)k. Truncated series arithmetic and repeated polynomial products avoid enumeration of exponentially many multivariate monomials.

Here is an explicit denominator check. Put all coefficients of F over a common integer denominator q. Inductively the coefficients of X_k(t) have denominators dividing q^k k!: products contributing to index k have component indices summing k-1, and (k-1)! divided by their factorials is an integer. The same bound holds for initial-point derivatives. Determinant denominators can thus be bounded by q^(n(n-1)/2) times the product of k!, 0<=k<n. Numerator heights are polynomially bounded in n,h,input bits and log K by the cubic-or-higher homogeneous recurrence: each differentiated iterate has degree O(hn), at most n nested differentiations, and its coefficient one-norm grows by products of the input coefficient one-norm and these degrees. Logarithms of those products are polynomial. The determinant adds n! terms, contributing only O(n log n) extra logarithmic height.

All evaluated univariate degrees are at most d=O(hn^2), with polynomial coefficient heights. Standard rational univariate factorization has polynomial bit complexity. Rational roots have polynomial numerator and denominator length by the rational-root theorem; gradient evaluation and candidate normalization therefore also have polynomial height. Exact rational score comparison, M-orthogonality, restricted Hessian checks and basis inversion preserve polynomial bit bounds. The general algorithm outputs the acquired frame; it need not expand f(Vz). Such an expansion can be exponentially larger than a sparse high-degree input and is not included in this complexity claim. If a transformed expression is wanted, retaining the substitution as an arithmetic circuit suffices. Full coefficient substitution is charged only in the fixed-degree quartic corollary below. The proof invokes polynomial-time implementations of these arithmetic primitives, not an asymptotic guarantee inferred from SymPy timings.

Using a single original candidate pool is important: no repeated hidden-coordinate transformations can inflate the input description between stages. Uniform random draws require actual independent random bits in the mathematical algorithm; a fixed seeded fixture is a reproducibility diagnostic, not a worst-case probability guarantee.

## 6. Concrete downstream use: coefficient-only quartic SOS synthesis

For p=((Lx)^2-a)^T Q((Lx)^2-a), with Q rational PD, a>0 and Qa>0, acquire M=-Hess(p_2)/4. The quartic part has h=2 and H(m)=m^T Gm with G PD. The theorem acquires V. Substitute x=Vz, read Q' and b' from quartic and quadratic coefficients, solve a'=Q'^(-1)b', and verify Q'>0, a'>0 and the exact full identity. Rational LDL then supplies positive rational weighted squares in the original input coordinates.

These final checks are unconditional: a certificate returned for an out-of-promise input remains valid. Under the promise, good-line probability is above 3/4, so expected retries are at most 4/3 and failure after T independent tries is below 4^(-T). This closes the earlier latent-conditioning and approximate-deflation gaps for this exact rational class. It does not solve the separate full-orthogonal landscape question; that question has ceased to be a prerequisite for this acquisition method.

## Additional quartic scope: positive pairwise contrasts suffice

The prior investigator observed that the quartic acquisition theorem needs less than G>0, or even convexity of H on the simplex. Suppose instead that the hidden symmetric G satisfies only

    w_ij=G_ii+G_jj-2G_ij>0 for every i!=j.

For a vertex j of maximum diagonal, G_ij<(G_ii+G_jj)/2<=G_jj. Thus the projective attracting-cone proof and face induction remain valid. The exact score identity

    sum_i m_i G_ii-m^T Gm=sum_(i<j)m_i m_j w_ij

keeps every nonvertex strictly below the largest vertex score. At a non-axis sphere stationary point, take a tangent supported on two active coordinates i,j. Its second variation is

    8 m_i m_j/(m_i+m_j) w_ij>0,

so the same strict-Hessian acceptance test is sound under this weaker promise. The original PD quartic SOS theorem is unchanged: PD remains necessary there for the particular positive weighted-square certificate construction.

For example G=[[1,1/2,1/2],[1/2,1,-7/2],[1/2,-7/2,1]] has pairwise contrasts 1,1,9, but (-2,1,1)^T G(-2,1,1)=-5. Hence its restriction to the simplex is not convex. Axis acquisition with a supplied rational metric still applies, while no nonnegativity claim follows. This explicitly distinguishes the wider algebraic acquisition mechanism from convex proof synthesis. Its historical novelty has not been assessed separately.
