1. FINITE REDUCTION AND RECIPROCITY

Ground patterns decouple, so choose k excited sites and at most b highs,
where k+b<=Q. Let P_b be this Hamming-weight downset. Set
 L=[[1,0],[1/sqrt2,1]], E+=(1/2)LL^T,
 T=P_b L^tensor k P_b, A=P_b E+^tensor k P_b.
L only raises weight, hence P_b L^k=P_b L^k P_b, and
 A=2^(-k) T T^T.
J=(-1)^weight satisfies T^(-1)=J T J. Therefore
 J A^(-1) J=2^k T^T T,
 spec(A^(-1))=4^k spec(A),
 lambda_min(A)=4^(-k)/lambda_max(A).

All computational entries of A are strictly positive. Its unique positive
top eigenvector is invariant under every site permutation. Thus its top
eigenvalue equals the top eigenvalue of the symmetric Dicke block. The
same reciprocity identity holds within that block and yields its minimum.
This proves that the global minimum also lies in the symmetric block.
No exponentially large full-space trace is used below.

The exact Schur decomposition and weight shift r+j are independently
verified in ../schur_cutoff_check/REPORT.txt. Their r>0 sectors cannot
improve the minimum. The elementary permutation/reciprocity argument
already suffices for this particular qutrit effect.

2. EXACT DICKE DIAGONALS AND CONSTRUCTIVE PROBES

For 0<=i<=k let |D_{k,i}> be the normalized symmetric i-high state and
a_{k,i}=<D_{k,i}|E+^tensor k|D_{k,i}>. Direct counting gives

 a_{k,i}=2^(-(k+i)) S_{k,i},
 S_{k,i}=sum_{t=max(0,2i-k)}^i C(i,t) C(k-i,i-t) 3^t.

For cutoff i, the symmetric triangular matrix is
 T_{ab}=1[a>=b] C(a,b) sqrt(C(k,a)/C(k,b)) 2^(-(a-b)/2).
The legal normalized vector chi=J T^T e_i / ||T^T e_i|| has amplitudes

 chi_j proportional to
 (-1)^j C(i,j) 2^(-(i-j)/2) / sqrt(C(k,j)),  0<=j<=i.

Its maximum energy is k+i, and T^T J T^T=J gives EXACTLY

 p_chi=2^(-k)/||T^T e_i||^2=4^(-k)/a_{k,i}
      =2^(i-k)/S_{k,i}.

The reciprocal miss W_{k,i}=2^(k-i)S_{k,i} is a positive integer.
For each Q the explicit family can be chosen by maximizing that integer
over ceil(Q/2)<=k<=Q, i=Q-k. This avoids an eigensolver entirely.

3. EXACT GENERATING FUNCTION PROVES THE LIMIT

Define S_Q=sum_{k+i=Q,0<=i<=k} W_{k,i}. The symmetric-power trace identity
sum_{k>=0} Tr Sym^k(M)=1/det(I-M), interpreted coefficientwise, gives

 sum_{Q>=0}S_Q u^Q
 =1/det(I-4u E+ diag(1,u))
 =1/(1-2u-3u^2+4u^3)
 =1/[(1-u)(1-u-4u^2)].

One may prove the trace identity first for a diagonalizable 2x2 matrix:
Tr Sym^k(M)=sum_{j=0}^k lambda_1^j lambda_2^(k-j); summing geometric
series gives the determinant. Polynomial coefficient identities extend
it to every M, so no approximation/imported asymptotic theorem is used.

The coefficients F_Q of 1/(1-u-4u^2) satisfy
F_0=F_1=1, F_Q=F_(Q-1)+4F_(Q-2), and
F_Q=(R^(Q+1)-R_-^(Q+1))/sqrt17,
R_-=(1-sqrt17)/2. Thus S_Q=sum_{j<=Q}F_j=Theta(R^Q).

The largest of at most Q+1 explicit integer scores is >=S_Q/(Q+1).
For the converse via this independent route, in each symmetric cutoff
lambda_max<=trace, hence 1/p_ent(Q)<=sum_{q<=Q}S_q=O(R^Q).
Together these prove the exact limiting exponent ln R. An independent
general triangular converse strengthens the lower bound on p to R^-Q.

4. BETTER CONSTRUCTIVE PREFACOR BY STIRLING, NOT A FIT

Put d=sqrt17, p*=(7-d)/10 and q*=3(5-d)/20. Choose k nearest to
Q/(1+p*) and i=Q-k. The integer score summand has logarithmic rate

 g(p,q)=(1-p)ln2
 +p h(q/p)+(1-p)h((p-q)/(1-p))+q ln3,
 h(x)=-x ln x-(1-x)ln(1-x).

At (p*,q*) both binomial fractions are strictly inside (0,1),
partial_q g=0, and direct algebra using d^2=17 gives
 g(p*,q*)=(1+p*)ln R,
 partial_p g(p*,q*)=ln R.
The second identity means the derivative of g/(1+p) vanishes at p*.
With exact Q=k+i, rounding k and i changes k g(i/k,q*(i/k)) from Q ln R
by O(1); smoothness is valid in a fixed neighborhood of these interior
fractions. The q saddle smoothly continues by the implicit-function
theorem because partial_qq g=-1/q-2/(p-q)-1/(1-2p+q)<0.

For each integer t within a sufficiently small fixed multiple of sqrt k
of that saddle, Taylor's theorem changes the exponent by at most O(1).
Uniform elementary Stirling bounds give each product of binomials in
that window >=c k^-1 exp[Q ln R]. There are Theta(sqrt k) such legal t.
Their sum is >=c' k^-1/2 R^Q. Since k=Theta(Q), this proves
W_{k,i}>=c'' R^Q/sqrt(Q+1), hence the stated explicit miss upper bound.
No global saddle uniqueness or unproved optimal second-order bound is
needed for this one-sided constructive conclusion.

5. GENERAL TRIANGULAR THEOREM: HOSTILE AUDIT

Hard_energy derived the following theorem; this worker checked each
invariance, normalization and typical-window step independently.
For finite-dimensional H with positive excited block, 0<E<=I, E ground
1, write E=T^dag T in an energy-ordered basis with upper Cholesky T,
and B=T^-1. Both T and B map every hard-energy downset into itself.
Therefore for n slots the exact minimum is
 p*_n(Q)=||B^tensor n P_Q||^-2.

Let s be the least s>=0 with B^dag B<=exp(sH), D=exp(-sH/2).
Then ||BD||<=1 and
||B^n P_Q||=||(BD)^n exp(sH_sum/2)P_Q||<=exp(sQ/2).
This proves p*_n(Q)>=exp(-sQ) for every n,Q.

If E!=I, the excited block has a positive-cost eigenvector v saturating
D B^dag B D<=I at minimal s. Let h=<v|H|v> and use a two-sided energy
window P around nh, width n delta_n, delta_n->0, n delta_n^2->infinity.
The product state has ||P v^n-v^n||->0 by elementary concentration.
Contraction of (BD)^n gives ||(BD)^n P v^n||->1. Then
 chi=B^n D^n P v^n/||(BD)^n P v^n||
is supported below Q=n(h+delta_n), because B preserves the downset.
Its miss is ||D^n P v^n||^2 divided by the displayed norm squared,
at most exp[-s n(h-delta_n)]/(1-o(1)). Thus the hard exponent is s.
One must find saturation on the EXCITED block, not use the automatic
ground saturation; full rank E and H+>0 ensure it exists for E!=I.

For this qutrit,
 T+=(1/sqrt2)[[1,1/sqrt2],[0,1]],
 B+=[[sqrt2,-1],[0,sqrt2]],
 B+^dag B+=[[2,-sqrt2],[-sqrt2,3]].
Writing x=exp(s), positivity of diag(x,x^2)-B^dag B requires
 (x-2)(x^2-3)-2=(x-1)(x^2-x-4)>=0, x>=2,
so the least x is R=(1+sqrt17)/2. This matches the fully independent
generating-function calculation exactly.

6. SEPARABLE BOUND

A pure product state's maximum support energy is the sum of individual
maximum support energies. A factor capped at 0 has miss1, capped at1
has minimum miss1/2, and capped at2 has minimum miss1/4. Thus every
legal product with ceiling Q has miss>=2^-Q. If a positive mixture is
supported in the hard subspace, every positive component is too: its
outside-projector expectation cannot cancel. The same bound therefore
holds for every separable mixture. Q copies of |l> attain 2^-Q.
