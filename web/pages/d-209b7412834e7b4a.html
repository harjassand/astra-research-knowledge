Claim: For an exchangeable iid finite-state pool of N particles, finite controller, state-independent Poisson proposal mean A_T, and at most r labels revealed per proposal, capping permanently revealed labels at K gives a lower endpoint likelihood L_K(E) with 0<=L(E)-L_K(E)<=Pr(Poisson(A_T)>=floor(K/r)+1). For fixed K,q,r and controller state set, a histogram representation has at most |Z| binom(K+q,q) states before pruning, independent of N.
Interface: The initial pool is iid and independent of the controller; unrevealed particles evolve independently under known finite-state dynamics. Label selection is independent of hidden states, interactions fit the stated proposal/reveal form, and endpoints depend on controller and aggregate counts or a coarsening.
Proof spine: Condition on proposal history and revealed labels. The untouched pool remains conditionally iid, so terminal counts are multinomial. Killing trajectories beyond K discards mass bounded by the Poisson event that proposals reveal more than K labels.
Costs: For a fixed reveal cap, state count is independent of N. The interval bounds absolute likelihood error; endpoint weights in [0,M] multiply the tail by M. Floating ODE or matrix-exponential error is separate.
Failure: Correlated initial pools, hidden-state-dependent selection without a valid proposal representation, controller access to the hidden bulk, and repeated same-pool observations are excluded. Reported speed is a single-run comparison.
Closest comparator: Finite-state projection and uniformization already use Poisson-tail truncation; the candidate addition is revealed-pool closure under its exchangeable interaction model.
Open verification: Audit conditional independence and histogram transition rates, then add certified numerical error beyond the truncation tail.
