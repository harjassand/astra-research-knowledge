## Exact new interface

Fix any integer q ≥ 2. Let Dᵠ_k consist of half-open intervals
`[q^k n,q^k(n+1))`, n ≥ 0. For nonnegative integer indices, let ⊕ᵠ
mean digitwise addition in base q, with each digit reduced modulo q and
with no carries. An admissible triple at scale k satisfies
`n₀ ⊕ᵠ n₁ ⊕ᵠ n₂ = 0`. Each ordered pair determines one third index.
Its children have indices qnᵥ+aᵥ with
`a₀+a₁+a₂ = 0 mod q`; there are q² children and their three digits are
uniform and pairwise independent.

For each interval I, choose any real multiplier h_I constant on its q
children, with values d_{I,a} satisfying
`|d_{I,a}| ≤ 1` and `Σ_a d_{I,a}=0`. Choices may vary with I and scale.
For complex Fᵥ ∈ L³(R₊²), define

```
L_I(F) = q^{-k} ∫_{I₀×I₁×I₂}
         F₀(x₀,x₁) F₁(x₁,x₂) F₂(x₂,x₀)
         h_{I₀}(x₀) h_{I₁}(x₁) h_{I₂}(x₂) dx₀ dx₁ dx₂.
```

**Derived theorem.** For every finite set of scales S,

```
Σ_{k∈S} Σ_{I admissible at k} |L_I(F)| ≤ 40 ∏_{v=0}² ||F_v||₃.
```

The constant is uniform in q, S, all multipliers, interval triples,
and inputs. Thus arbitrary complex coefficients of modulus at most one
may multiply each local term independently. This is stronger in its
interface than the release's stated real-input, binary, Haar-sign theorem.
The extension is a deduction; its novelty against all prior art remains
unresolved.

## Matrix certificate, reconstructed

For positive semidefinite T, set Φ(T)=tr(T^{3/2}). A Hermitian U supported
on T has curvature

```
b_T(U) = (3/2) Σ_{i,j} |U_ij|² / (sqrt(t_i)+sqrt(t_j)),
```

on the positive support. It is the second derivative of Φ: differentiate
the square root B using `BX+XB=U`, then differentiate
`dΦ_T(U)=(3/2)tr(BU)`. In the eigenbasis of B this Sylvester operator has
positive eigenvalues `sqrt(t_i)+sqrt(t_j)`. In particular Φ is convex,
including on the closed cone by regularization.

If P ≤ aQ, a ≥ 1, and U is supported on P, then
`b_P(U) ≥ a^{-1/2} b_Q(U)`. Indeed square-root monotonicity gives
`sqrt(P) ≤ sqrt(a)sqrt(Q)`, hence the same order for the positive
Sylvester operators on Hilbert–Schmidt space; inverse order gives the
claim. Kernel inclusion follows from PSD order, and adding εI before
taking ε↓0 handles singular matrices. No scalar entrywise comparison
of P and Q is assumed.

If `-T ≤ U ≤ T`, the segment `T+tU` is bounded above by 2T and has the
same support for |t|<1. Its second derivative is therefore at least
`2^{-1/2}b_T(U)`. Integrating the midpoint Taylor remainder gives

```
[Φ(T+U)+Φ(T-U)]/2 - Φ(T) ≥ b_T(U)/(2sqrt(2)).          (A)
```

For finite Hilbert spaces Hᵥ and maps Wᵥ:H_{v+1}→Hᵥ, put
`R_v=[W_v, W_{v-1}*]`, `T_v=R_v*R_v`, `U_v=R_v*D_vR_v`, where Dᵥ
are Hermitian contractions. The dimension-independent triangle estimate is

```
|tr(D₀W₀D₁W₁D₂W₂)| ≤ (5/3) Σ_v b_{T_v}(U_v).       (B)
```

Here is a check of the complex extension, which the dyadic paper does not
state. The annular companion's Lemma 3.6 already gives the complex
mixed-trace mechanism; its full matrix section was subsequently read
and agrees with this reconstruction. Complex phase recovery is therefore
not claimed as a new discovery here. Changing Wᵥ by a scalar phase conjugates Tᵥ and Uᵥ simultaneously
by a block diagonal unitary, so each b-cost stays fixed. Choose phases
whose product rotates the triangle trace to a nonnegative real number.
On H₀⊕H₁⊕H₂ form the Hermitian adjacency matrix M with off-diagonal
blocks W₀,W₁,W₂ and their adjoints, and D=diag(D₀,D₁,D₂). Then
`tr((DM)^3)=6 Re tr(D₀W₀D₁W₁D₂W₂)`.

Writing Aᵥ=MPᵥM and Kᵥ=MPᵥDM, PSD order Aᵥ≤M² and quadratic
Cauchy–Schwarz give
`Σ_v b_{T_v}(U_v) ≥ (1/3)b_{M²}(MDM)`.
In an eigenbasis of M, with qᵢ=|mᵢ| in decreasing order, define

```
Q = Σ_{i,j} q_i²q_j² |D_ij|²/(q_i+q_j),
A = Σ_i q_i³ |D_ii|²,
E = Σ_{i<j} q_i q_j² |D_ij|².
```

Zero denominators mean zero terms. The preceding comparison is at least
Q/2, and pairing (i,j),(j,i) gives Q≥A/2+E. In the expansion of
`tr((DM)^3)`, group triples by their smallest index. The three cases are:
smallest index once, bounded by 3E; twice, bounded by 3sqrt(AE) using
row contraction and Cauchy–Schwarz; three times, bounded by A. These
arguments use `x*D_{>i}x`, real diagonal entries, and |Dᵢⱼ|², so they
work for complex Hermitian D. Consequently

```
|tr((DM)^3)| ≤ A+3E+3sqrt(AE)
               ≤ (5/2)A+(9/2)E ≤ 5Q.
```

The phase choice now gives (B). This establishes the matrix certificate
over complex Hilbert spaces, including singular row stars.

## The q-independent step

Take step-function inputs constant on a common finer q-adic atom level.
On a fixed parent triple use normalized probability measures and matrices
`W_v(i,j)=sqrt(w_i w_j)F_v(i,j)`. The normalized local integral is
`τ=tr(D₀W₀D₁W₁D₂W₂)` and its physical prefactor is q^{2k}.

Let Pᵥ,a be the projection onto child a at vertex v and Cᵥ,a=√q Pᵥ,a.
The zero-extended child edge is Cᵥ,aᵥ Wᵥ C_{v+1,a_{v+1}}. Its row-star
output Gram matrix contains the sum of two edge Gram matrices, with no
product of the two neighboring child selectors. Conditional on aᵥ,
pairwise independence makes both neighboring selectors average to I.
Convexity and equality of the nonzero spectra of RR* and R*R therefore give

```
E[e_v(child) | a_v=a] ≥ Φ(T_{v,a}),
T_{v,a}=q R_v*P_{v,a}R_v,
(1/q)Σ_a T_{v,a}=T_v.
```

Put Dᵥ=Σ_a dᵥ,a Pᵥ,a, Uᵥ=Rᵥ*DᵥRᵥ. Because |dᵥ,a|≤1 and
Σ_a dᵥ,a=0, each collection of weights `(1±dᵥ,a)/q` is nonnegative
and sums to one. Therefore

```
Φ(T_v±U_v) = Φ(Σ_a [(1±d_v,a)/q] T_{v,a})
          ≤ Σ_a [(1±d_v,a)/q] Φ(T_{v,a}).
```

Averaging the two signs removes dᵥ,a exactly. By (A),

```
E[e_v(child)]-e_v(parent)
 ≥ [Φ(T_v+U_v)+Φ(T_v-U_v)]/2-Φ(T_v)
 ≥ b_{T_v}(U_v)/(2sqrt(2)).
```

This is stronger than the crude q-way Taylor comparison, which would
lose √q. It uses randomized two-way rounding of a bounded mean-zero
multiplier, rather than a q-dependent variance bound.

For δ(parent)=E e(child)−e(parent), summing over vertices and using (B)
gives

```
δ≥0,    |τ| ≤ (10sqrt(2)/3) δ.                       (C)
```

## Global sum and full input boundary

Define J_k=Σ_{admissible I at k} q^{2k} e(I). Each admissible child has
one parent, there are q² equally likely children, and its area weight
is q^{-2} times the parent weight. Hence

```
Σ_{I at k} q^{2k}δ(I) = J_{k-1}−J_k.
```

For each row star with edge Hilbert–Schmidt norms a,b,
`Φ(R*R) ≤ (a²+b²)^{3/2} ≤ sqrt(2)(a³+b³)`.
The probability-normalized edge has
`||W||_HS³ ≤ average |F|³`. Every edge appears at two vertices and
every interval pair in exactly one admissible triple, giving

```
0 ≤ J_k ≤ 2sqrt(2) Σ_v ||F_v||₃³.
```

Use (C), insert missing scales by nonnegative differences, and telescope
to obtain `(40/3)Σ_v ||F_v||₃³`. Apply this to individually normalized
inputs to get 40 times their norm product. A zero input norm makes
every local integral vanish.

At one fixed scale, Hölder on each product probability space and then
on the admissible-triple sum gives
`Σ_I |L_I(F)| ≤ ∏_v ||F_v||₃`. Expanding differences in the three
slots gives continuity into ℓ¹. Bounded compactly supported q-adic
step functions are dense in L³. For a fixed finite set of scales,
this continuity transfers the absolute sum and the product bound to
all complex L³ inputs. Thus no infinite-dimensional Gram or unjustified
trace limit is needed. Monotone convergence subsequently permits the
sum over all integer scales, with the same bound.

## Decisive obstruction to a wider transfer

Uniform marginal child laws are insufficient. In two-dimensional
vertex spaces use

```
W₀=W₁=(1/2)[[0,1],[1,0]],
W₂=(1/2)[[1,0],[0,0]],   D_v=diag(1,-1).
```

If the only children are (left,left,left) and (right,right,right), each
coordinate is uniform but the pairs are dependent. The normalized local
trace equals −1/8. Parent energy is √2+1/4; the mean child energy is 1.
Thus `δ=3/4−sqrt(2)<0`. This is an exact 2×2 obstruction to the
conditional-averaging proof and to a monotonicity claim based on marginal
uniformity alone. It does not contradict the admissible XOR/q-ary law.

Continuous geometric partitions generally do not have this pairwise
independence with exact energy conservation. A transfer from the q-adic
model to the continuous triangular operator requires a separate analytic
representation and dissipation argument. The release's annular paper
provides additional claimed steps; this note does not validate them.

## Prior art, usefulness, and next falsifiable step

The release itself identifies cyclic traces in Kovač–Thiele–Zorin-Kranich,
2015, arXiv:1506.00861v1, equations (1.5)–(1.6), and their restricted
input theorem 1.7. Kovač's 2011 Bellman paper, arXiv:1108.0917v1,
Section 6.2, discusses the non-bipartite obstruction. Durcik–Kovač–Thiele,
arXiv:1608.00156v2, establishes scale-dependent power cancellation;
it does not assert this constant absolute-local-sum interface. Primary
arXiv pages verified the titles, authors and version dates; the KTZ
formula/theorem and Kovač obstruction receive targeted source checks in
the source ledger. A full priority search for the uniform q-ary theorem
has not been completed. It should be treated as a corollary of the new
release mechanism, with possible known analogues, rather than a claimed
independent foundational discovery.

Concrete use: the certificate applies to a Latin-square refinement law
whenever the three coordinates are uniform and pairwise independent,
each admissible edge pair has one completion, and area weights telescope.
This isolates the precise combinatorial structure needed to control a
non-bipartite triangle with L³ edge norms. It supports operator-valued
or martingale variants only after their total-energy accounting is shown;
mere triangle trace similarity is insufficient.

Best next allocation: have an independent reviewer reconstruct the

continuous paper's heat-to-hard-annulus transfer. A falsifying test is
an admissible q-ary finite array with negative J_{k-1}−J_k or a trace
exceeding (B); numerical passing cases only test implementation.
