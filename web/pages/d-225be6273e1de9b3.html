CYCLE 3 — RARE-HISTORY MINIMAX FOR A SINGLE NOISY HMM PATH
8 October 2026

STATUS

PROVED, with an exact two-point decision reduction and matching rates for a scoped two-state family. This is a new rare-occupancy/rare-marker witness construction. It does not resolve uniform-history prediction for general HMMs, and it makes no novelty or priority claim.

The learner receives one stationary, contiguous raw observation path Y_1,...,Y_T. There are no resets, conditional-law queries, supplied predictive bases, hidden-state labels, or matrix-response oracles. For the lower bound, the learner is deliberately given more model information than a rank-free learner would normally receive: it knows the two-state model family, p, the emissions, and the stationary initialization; only one transition parameter u is unknown. Thus any lower bound here also applies when rank/basis information is withheld.

The chosen losses are (i) worst-history one-step total variation over the two one-symbol contexts {0,1}, and (ii) stationary-one-symbol-history expected conditional KL for the same next-symbol forecasts. In both cases, evaluation is on a fresh population context: after the training path is observed, the evaluator draws H independently from the stationary one-symbol distribution rho (or supplies h=1 for the worst-history coordinate) and asks for the kernel f_u(H). H is not defined as the last observation Y_T, and this is not the terminal continuation target Pr(Y_{T+1}=1 | Y_1:T). The distinction between the two stated losses is decisive: the worst-history loss sees the rare context without weighting it; stationary expected KL multiplies its contribution by its stationary mass. A predicted nonempty future law at a fresh supplied context is also lower-bounded by marginalization to its first future symbol. The matching upper bounds below are for the stated one-symbol-context task, not for the supremum over all finite histories.

MODEL AND INTERFACE

Fix p in (0,1/8] and u in [1/2,5/8]. Hidden states are A,B, and the hidden chain starts in its stationary distribution pi=(1-p,p). Its transition matrix, in row/column order A,B, is

    P_u = [[1-v(u), v(u)], [1-u, u]],
    v(u) = p(1-u)/(1-p).

The stationary law is exactly pi for every u. The binary observation channel is fixed and known:

    Pr(Y=1 | A)=p^2,       Pr(Y=1 | B)=1/2.

All four emission probabilities are positive. The observation-channel matrix has determinant 1/2-p^2 >= 31/64, so its smallest singular value is bounded below by 31/(64 sqrt(2)) > 1/3. Its minimum entry is p^2. The hidden chain's Dobrushin coefficient is

    alpha(P_u)=u-v(u) <= 5/8,

so its total-variation mixing bound is uniform in p: from any initial state, distance to stationarity after k steps is at most (5/8)^k. All transition entries are positive, but the minimum is of order p: v(u) >= 3p/8. The hidden-state cap is the fixed value 2, and the predictive/Hankel rank is exactly 2 because the emission channel has rank 2 and both stationary state weights are positive.

For one-symbol history h, write f_u(h)=Pr_u(Y_{t+1}=1 | Y_t=h), and let rho(h)=Pr(Y_t=h) under stationarity. For h=1,

    rho(1) = (1-p)p^2 + p/2 = p/2 + p^2 - p^3,
    p/2 <= rho(1) <= 5p/8,
    w_1 := Pr(B | Y_t=1) = 1/[1+2p(1-p)].

The context h=1 is therefore rare, with mass Theta(p), even though the hidden chain mixes in a constant number of steps and the emission channel has constant rank/singular-value margin.

RESPONSE SEPARATION

Given posterior hidden-state mass w at time t, the next hidden B-probability is z_u(w)=w u+(1-w)v(u), and the next observation-1 probability is p^2+(1/2-p^2)z_u(w). Since the stationary distribution and emission channel are fixed, w_1 does not depend on u. For u_0=1/2 and u_1=1/2+epsilon, 0<epsilon<=1/8,

    z_{u_1}(w_1)-z_{u_0}(w_1)
      = epsilon [w_1 - (1-w_1)p/(1-p)]
      = epsilon (1-2p^2)/[1+2p(1-p)]
      >= 3 epsilon/4.

As 1/2-p^2 >=31/64,

    Delta := f_{u_1}(1)-f_{u_0}(1) >= 93 epsilon/256 > epsilon/3.

Both values f_u(1) lie in [3/16,1/3]. Thus this is a constant-margin response when epsilon is fixed, and a Delta=Theta(epsilon) local response when epsilon shrinks.

FULL RAW-PATH KL BOUND

Let mu_i be the distribution of the entire observed path Y_1:T under u_i. The initial hidden law is identical under the alternatives, and the emission channel is identical. The chain rule and data processing give

    KL(mu_0 || mu_1)
      <= KL(P_{u_0}(S_1:T) || P_{u_1}(S_1:T))
      = (T-1){ p KL(Ber(u_0)||Ber(u_1))
               +(1-p) KL(Ber(v_0)||Ber(v_1)) }.

Use KL(Ber(a)||Ber(b)) <= (a-b)^2/[b(1-b)]. Since u_1<=5/8, the B-row divergence is at most (64/15)epsilon^2. Also v_1>=3p/8, 1-v_1>=1/2, and |v_0-v_1|=p epsilon/(1-p); hence the A-row divergence is at most (1024/147)p epsilon^2 < 7p epsilon^2. After stationary row weights, the total per-step divergence is less than 12p epsilon^2. In particular,

    KL(mu_0 || mu_1) <= 12(T-1)p epsilon^2.

Choose

    epsilon_T = min{1/8, 1/[16 sqrt(Tp)]}.

Then KL(mu_0||mu_1) <= 3/64 and Pinsker gives TV(mu_0,mu_1) <= sqrt(3/128) < 1/4. This divergence is for the complete observed training path: the learner is allowed every symbol of its one trajectory, and the bound is not based on a restricted statistic or on independent episodes. It bounds how well the learner can identify the fresh-context prediction kernel from that path. Data processing is used only to upper-bound observed-path KL by the labeled hidden-path KL. No cumulative/random-time risk is used. Since terminal continuation prediction conditions on the realized training prefix and its terminal filtering distribution, this fresh-context lower bound does not by itself imply a lower bound for Pr(Y_{T+1}=1 | Y_1:T).

EXACT TWO-POINT WORST-CONTEXT MINIMAX REDUCTION

For any forecast q(S) in [0,1] after h=1, clip it to [f_{u_0}(1),f_{u_1}(1)] and put phi(S)=(q(S)-f_{u_0}(1))/Delta. Then the absolute one-step TV losses at this context are exactly

    E_0 |q-f_{u_0}(1)| = Delta E_0 phi,
    E_1 |q-f_{u_1}(1)| = Delta E_1(1-phi).

Consequently, the exact minimax risk for this forecast coordinate, with unrestricted access to the full raw path and with randomized procedures allowed, is

    Delta e_mm(mu_0,mu_1),
    e_mm := inf_{0<=phi<=1} max{ E_0 phi, E_1(1-phi) }.

This is the ordinary minimax binary-testing error on the full observed-path experiment, not on a selected summary. Every predictor whose loss takes a supremum over histories has loss at least this coordinate loss. Since e_mm >= [1-TV(mu_0,mu_1)]/2, the all-history worst-history loss has the lower bound

    R_sup >= Delta[1-TV(mu_0,mu_1)]/2 >= epsilon_T/8.

The last inequality uses TV<1/4 and Delta>epsilon_T/3. This lower bound is matched in order for the stated one-symbol-context task by the raw-data estimator below. It implies the minimax frontier

    R*_sup,1(T,p) = Theta(min{1, 1/sqrt(Tp)}).

Here R*_sup,1 is the infimum over raw-path predictors of the supremum, over u in [1/2,5/8], of expected max_{h in {0,1}} one-step TV error. Constants are universal. The matching upper bound is not claimed for arbitrary-length contexts.

STATIONARY-HISTORY EXPECTED KL: DIFFERENT LOSS, DIFFERENT SCALE

Define the one-symbol stationary-history loss for a predictor q_S(h), with H drawn independently of the training sample S from rho:

    L_i(S) = sum_{h in {0,1}} rho(h)
             KL(Ber(f_{u_i}(h)) || Ber(q_S(h))).

The two alternatives have the same stationary observed distribution, so rho(1) is identical under both. Convert q_S(1) to the test that chooses the nearer of f_{u_0}(1), f_{u_1}(1). A test error implies |q_S(1)-f_{u_i}(1)|>=Delta/2. Pinsker's inequality gives KL(Ber(f_{u_i}(1))||Ber(q_S(1))) >= Delta^2/2 on that error. The sum of the two testing error probabilities is at least 1-TV(mu_0,mu_1). Therefore

    max_i E_i L_i(S)
       >= rho(1) Delta^2 [1-TV(mu_0,mu_1)]/4
       >= p epsilon_T^2/96
       >= (1/24576) min{p,1/T}.

Thus the rare context costs Delta in worst-history TV but only rho(1)Delta^2=Theta(p epsilon^2) in stationary-history expected KL. Taking p_T=1/(100 T^2) and epsilon=1/8 leaves a constant worst-history lower bound while the stationary-history expected-KL lower bound is only Theta(T^-2). This is not a contradiction: the two losses ask different questions. More generally, the unavoidable local information term is T p epsilon^2, and the stationary KL term carries the additional test-context mass rho(1)=Theta(p).

MATCHING RAW-DATA UPPER FOR THE DECLARED ONE-SYMBOL TASK

This calculation makes the minimax rates above exact up to universal constants, rather than only a two-point lower bound. Define W_t=1{Y_t=1,Y_{t+1}=1}, t=1,...,T-1, and let Wbar be its empirical mean. Directly summing the four hidden-state pairs gives

    m_p(u) := E_u W_t
      = p u/4 + p^3(1-u) + p^4(1-2p+pu)
      = b_p + g_p u,
    b_p = p^3+p^4(1-2p),
    g_p = p(1/4-p^2+p^4) >= 15p/64.

The hidden chain has Dobrushin coefficient at most 5/8. For k>=2, conditioning on (S_t,S_{t+1},Y_t,Y_{t+1}) bounds |Cov(W_t,W_{t+k})| by m_p(u)(5/8)^(k-1); for k=1 it is at most 2m_p(u). Also m_p(u)<=p/3. Summing covariances yields Var(Wbar)<=12p/T for T>=2. The estimator

    uhat = projection_[1/2,5/8] ((Wbar-b_p)/g_p)

therefore satisfies E(uhat-u)^2 <= min{1/64, 219/(Tp)}. It reads only the observed path, runs in O(T) arithmetic operations and O(1) streaming memory, and uses no conditional prediction queries.

The O(T) statement is unit-cost arithmetic. With rational p supplied in B-bit form, the sufficient-statistic and forecast operations use rational numbers with bit lengths polynomial in B+log T; the scaling sequence p_T=1/(100T^2) itself has O(log T) bits. This is a statistical lower bound, not a computational-hardness claim.

For either one-symbol history h, f_u(h) is affine in u. Its slope at h=1 has magnitude at most 1/2; its slope at h=0 is at most 4p/7. Thus

    E max_{h in {0,1}} |f_uhat(h)-f_u(h)|
       <= (1/2) sqrt(E(uhat-u)^2)
       = O(min{1,1/sqrt(Tp)}).

For the average-KL upper bound, uniformly in u, f_u(1) is bounded between 3/16 and 1/3, while p/7 <= f_u(0) <=5p/8. The slope at h=0 is at most p and at h=1 at most 1/2. Applying KL(Ber(a)||Ber(b)) <= (a-b)^2/[b(1-b)] at the plug-in forecast f_uhat(h), and weighting by rho(1)<=5p/8 and rho(0)<=1, gives

    E L_u(S) <= 10p E(uhat-u)^2
              = O(min{p,1/T}).

Together with the two-point lower bounds, these establish

    R*_sup,1(T,p) = Theta(min{1,(Tp)^(-1/2)}),
    R*_{stationary-KL,1}(T,p) = Theta(min{p,1/T})

for the explicitly stated one-symbol-context, one-step prediction problem. Constants in the displayed estimator bounds are conservative.

FAST-MIXING SCALING COUNTEREXAMPLE AND MARGIN ACCOUNTING

For p_T=1/(100T^2), T>=2, the hidden state cap remains 2 and alpha<=5/8. The learner can even be told p_T and the entire model family. The rare context has mass rho(1) in [1/(200T^2), 1/(160T^2)] up to the exact p^2-p^3 correction; the hidden B-state is visited in expectation Tp_T=1/(100T) times. With epsilon=1/8, the forecast gap is at least 1/24, and the complete raw-path KL is at most 12(T-1)p_T/64 <= 3/(1600T). Hence the full path cannot reliably identify which forecast applies after h=1, while a forecaster evaluated on that history incurs constant worst-history error.

Every vanishing quantity is charged:
- stationary mass of B and the rare one-symbol context: Theta(p);
- minimum A-to-B transition probability: at least 3p/8, hence Theta(p) in this family;
- minimum emission probability: p^2;
- expected number of visits to the predictive state in T observations: Tp;
- complete-path information for a transition shift epsilon: at most 12Tp epsilon^2;
- context response margin: Delta >= epsilon/3;
- hidden-state mixing coefficient: at most 5/8, independent of p;
- emission rank/smallest singular value: rank 2 and bounded away from zero, despite the p^2 entry floor.

Thus fast mixing and constant hidden-state cap do not remove the rare-state information cost. The counterexample does not claim the same obstruction under uniform lower bounds on stationary state mass and every transition/emission probability. A polynomial floor in p can be charged; a uniform worst-history guarantee needs a uniform lower bound on the information in each response-relevant witness, or a loss that discounts rare contexts. The raw-path KL identifies the exact term here: T times state occupancy p times squared local transition margin epsilon^2. The stationary expected KL has the further context factor p.

COMPARISON, FAILURE MODES, AND UNKNOWN

This mechanism is not the earlier one-path DFA gap, the short-reset pair, the latent-basis nonidentifiability example, or the Cycle-2 K-state/K^2-transition hypercube. It uses a full-support, noisy observation channel and a rare stationary state whose B-to-B transition parameter affects a marker-conditioned next-symbol law. The learner sees one uninterrupted path, and the proof charges information in the complete observed experiment.

The result is consistent with spectral HMM algorithms whose guarantees assume parameter separation through singular-value/positivity conditions. Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang formulate observation-only HMM learning under such separation conditions; this example explicitly loses a state-mass/transition floor of order p and a minimum emission entry of order p^2. The cited source does not establish this lower bound. Filter-forgetting results concern stability to initialization under their own hypotheses, not estimation of a transition parameter carried by a state visited only Tp times. Douc et al. and McDonald–Yuksel provide relevant filter-stability context, but are not used in the proof.

UNKNOWN / NOT ESTABLISHED:
1. A matching minimax upper for the supremum over every finite binary history is not proved. The fresh-context lower bound does apply to that stronger worst-history loss because it contains h=1.
2. The fresh-context kernel lower bound does not automatically extend to terminal continuation prediction Pr(Y_{T+1} | Y_1:T), whose test context is the end of the observed training trajectory; that interface and loss were not analyzed.
3. No theorem here gives a general rank-free learner or an acquisition algorithm for arbitrary HMMs, even at fixed rank 2.
4. No lower is claimed when stationary-state, transition, and emission floors are uniformly bounded below independently of T; those assumptions exclude the displayed scaling family.
5. No benefit of adaptive CUR, Hankel basis search, or a queried conditional-response interface is studied. The learner has raw data only.
6. No novelty, priority, peer-review, or formal-verification claim is made.
