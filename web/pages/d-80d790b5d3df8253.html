# Electrochemical catalysis: potential, selectivity, turnover, and transport

## Verdict

The broad claim that lowering overpotential must lower Faradaic selectivity, or that raising turnover must lower selectivity, is not a law of electrochemical catalysis. It is underdetermined without a named reaction network, local reactant activities, transfer coefficients, catalyst state, cell architecture, and a defined energy boundary. A simple transport-coupled Tafel model gives an exact conditional current ceiling and shows how mass transport inflates apparent Tafel slopes. Whole-cell electricity per mole of product has a separate exact accounting identity: $w_i=n_i F V_{\rm cell}/\mathrm{FE}_i$. Thus a cathode potential or geometric current alone cannot rank energy performance.

The defensible result is a **principled rejection of a universal intrinsic tradeoff**, plus conditional bounds and a testable device-level mechanism. The algebra below is standard kinetic/mass-balance reasoning; its novelty is not established. The experimental studies are source-reported findings, not independently reproduced measurements.

## Conditional model and derivation

Let η be the magnitude of the local cathodic overpotential, $C_b$ the bulk concentration of one dissolved reactant, and $C_s$ its surface concentration. Assume a steady planar boundary layer with mass-transfer coefficient $k_m$, a single first-order surface pathway, and a cathodic Tafel kinetic coefficient $k(\eta)=k_0 e^{\beta\eta}$, where $\beta=\alpha n_eF/(RT)>0$, $n_e$ is the number of electrons in the potential-sensitive elementary step, and $n_p$ is the full electron stoichiometry per product molecule. Ignore other reactant sources, product inhibition, double-layer charging, and changes in catalyst state. The surface balance is

\[
k_m(C_b-C_s)=k(\eta)C_s=r.
\]

Solving gives

\[
r=C_b\frac{k_m k}{k_m+k},\qquad
j=n_pFr=j_{\lim}\frac{k}{k_m+k},\qquad j_{\lim}=n_pF k_m C_b.
\]

Writing $\theta=j/j_{\lim}$, the kinetic rate required to reach fraction $\theta$ of the transport limit is $k/k_m=\theta/(1-\theta)$. Therefore

\[
\eta=\frac{RT}{\alpha n_eF}\left[\ln\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}+\ln\frac{k_m}{k_0}\right],\qquad
b_{\rm app}:=\frac{d\eta}{d\log_{10}j}
=\frac{2.303RT}{\alpha n_eF}\frac{1}{1-\theta}.
\]

At 298.15 K with α=0.5 and $n_e=1$, the ideal kinetic slope is 118 mV/decade. The apparent slope is 1.18 V/decade at $j=0.9j_{\lim}$, and the kinetic current inferred by the standard series-rate correction $j_k=j j_{\lim}/(j_{\lim}-j)$ is ten times the observed current there. At $0.99j_{\lim}$, that factor is 100. These are exact consequences of this first-order model, not universal values for a real catalyst. They show why a Tafel slope or apparent turnover measured near a transport limit cannot automatically be read as an intrinsic barrier or site rate.

For two steady Faradaic pathways competing for a common reactant, let product $i\in\{t,s\}$ have partial current

\[
j_i=n_iF k_i^0 e^{\beta_i\eta}C_s^{m_i},\qquad \beta_i=\alpha_i n_{e,i}F/(RT),
\]

and assume these two products account for all Faradaic current. Their target-product selectivity $f=\mathrm{FE}_t$ obeys

\[
\ln\frac{f}{1-f}
=\ln\frac{n_t k_t^0}{n_s k_s^0}
+(\beta_t-\beta_s)\eta
+(m_t-m_s)\ln C_s.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\frac{d}{d\eta}\ln\frac{f}{1-f}
=(\beta_t-\beta_s)+(m_t-m_s)\frac{d\ln C_s}{d\eta}.
\]

There is no fixed sign. In the exact counterexample $\beta_t=\beta_s$ and $m_t=m_s$, selectivity is constant at every potential and transport coefficient. Scaling both kinetic prefactors together increases rate at a fixed η without changing FE; before the common reactant becomes transport-limited, it also lowers the overpotential needed for a specified target rate. Different transfer coefficients, orders, adsorbate coverages, local pH, or surface reconstruction can reverse or curve the trend. Adding such states enlarges the parameter set; it cannot restore a universal monotonic law.

If product $t$ consumes $\nu_t$ molecules of the limiting reactant per molecule of product, and the measured maximum reactant supply is $J_{X,\max}$, conservation gives the necessary bound

\[
j_t\le \frac{n_tF}{\nu_t}J_{X,\max},\qquad
j_{\rm total}\le \frac{n_tF}{\nu_t\,\mathrm{FE}_t}J_{X,\max}.
\]

The second inequality assumes steady state, product FE defined against total Faradaic current, and no uncounted reactant source. For aqueous CO₂ reduction, $J_{X,\max}$ must include CO₂ replenishment from carbonate/bicarbonate and any gas–liquid or porous-electrode pathway; setting it equal to $k_m C_{\rm CO2,bulk}$ is justified only in the simple dissolved-CO₂ boundary-layer model.

As a scale check, the 2024 Bi/MOF study reports 166 mA cm⁻² partial current to formate (two electrons per formate). This requires at least 8.60 mmol m⁻² s⁻¹ formate flux. If supplied only by a planar aqueous boundary layer at 30 mM dissolved CO₂, this is equivalent to $k_m\ge2.87\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. That calculation is not a reconstruction of the paper’s gas-diffusion electrode: the authors report a CO₂-solvating MOF reservoir, and the actual effective supply pathway is more complex.

## Energy and turnover accounting

For steady electrolysis at full-cell voltage $V_{\rm cell}$, target-product molar rate $\dot n_i$, total current density $j$, and product electron number $n_i$,

\[
\mathrm{FE}_i=\frac{n_iF\dot n_i}{jA},\qquad
\frac{P_{\rm cell}}{A}=jV_{\rm cell},\qquad
\frac{P_{\rm cell}}{\dot n_i}=\frac{n_iF V_{\rm cell}}{\mathrm{FE}_i}.
\]

Thus a design change improves stack electricity per mole of target if and only if

\[
\frac{\mathrm{FE}_{i,2}}{\mathrm{FE}_{i,1}}>\frac{V_{\rm cell,2}}{V_{\rm cell,1}}.
\]

With fixed product thermodynamics $E_{\rm rev}=\Delta G/(n_iF)$, the corresponding stack electrical efficiency is $\mathrm{FE}_i E_{\rm rev}/V_{\rm cell}$. This excludes gas/feed compression, pumping, heating, separation, recycle, and carbon lost to carbonate or crossover. A cathodic overpotential versus RHE is only one contribution to cell voltage; it omits the anode, membrane/electrolyte resistance, contacts, and other cell losses.

For a declared active-site areal density $\Gamma_{\rm active}$, an observed site-normalized turnover is

\[
\mathrm{TOF}_{i,\rm obs}=\frac{\mathrm{FE}_i j}{n_iF\Gamma_{\rm active}}.
\]

In the one-reactant model it cannot exceed $J_{X,\max}/(\nu_i\Gamma_{\rm active})$. A numerical intrinsic TOF therefore needs a defensible site count under operating conditions. Copper reconstruction, inaccessible sites, changing electrochemically active area, and current through non-target products make geometric partial current the more defensible primary measurement unless that site count is measured. The active-site density assumed in the Au model is a supplied model input, not an acquired universal catalyst property.

## Device-level counterevidence and qualifications

- **Electrical architecture can improve voltage and selectivity together at scale.** Rufer et al. (2024) model in-plane ohmic loss in Cu/ePTFE gas-diffusion electrodes. Their model is calibrated from a small, nearly equipotential electrode, then couples a local voltage field to measured local current–voltage/product data. They report that unmodified ePTFE scale-up reduces ethylene FE (47% at 0.5 cm² to a peak 29% at 4.2 cm²) and adds 0.81 V at 200 mA cm⁻². A 75-µm Cu wire architecture at 4-mm pitch occupies under 2% of the area; the 50-cm² cell reaches about 75% C₂+ FE at 200 mA cm⁻² and 3.52 V. The paper reports up to 0.9-V reduction versus unmodified ePTFE at scale. Large-cell comparisons were not iR-corrected and the reported 50-cm² performance points were single experiments, so this is compelling engineering evidence with limited replication, not a universal quantitative law. Source: [Rufer et al., *Nature Communications* 15, 9429 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-53523-8).

- **Hydrodynamics can move FE in either direction depending on reactor configuration.** Zeng et al. (2024) report that stirring a Cu H-cell at 350 rpm changes C₂+ FE from $51.1\pm5.3$% to $19.3\pm1.4$% and formate FE from $21.9\pm3.9$% to $43.7\pm4.3$%. In their flow cell and MEA, flow-rate-induced product-FE variation stays within 10%. Their operando Raman/FEM interpretation assigns the H-cell change mainly to local pH falling when the diffusion layer is renewed, while high current density/catholyte-free architectures retain alkalinity. The result is configuration-specific; it warns that “more flow improves CO₂ selectivity” is not a general rule. Source: [Zeng et al., *Nano Energy* 131, 110201 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nanoen.2024.110201).

- **A molecular CO₂ reservoir raises both rate and formate selectivity, but is not a pure mass-transfer intervention.** Mukhopadhyay et al. (2024) report UiO-66-CN increasing local CO₂ from about 30 mM to an estimated 0.82 M, with up to 98% formate FE and 166 mA cm⁻² formate partial current at −0.9 V vs RHE on a Bi gas-diffusion electrode. The authors’ DFT also predicts altered CO₂ activation and stabilization of *OCHO at the Bi–MOF interface; the layer changes local chemistry as well as reactant storage. They report more than 80% performance retained after 25 h, not indefinite stability. Source: [Mukhopadhyay et al., *Nature Communications* 15, 3397 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47498-9).

- **Separating anode and cathode conditions can improve the combined efficiency objective.** A 2025 Ag MEA study defines $\eta_E=(E_0/V_{\rm cell})\,\mathrm{FE}_{\rm CO}$ with $E_0=1.34$ V, measures two-electrode cell voltage, and reports higher CO partial current under non-isothermal operation than the corresponding isothermal cases. In its tested system, the maximum CO partial current at 80 °C increases from 118.6 to 195.6 mA cm⁻² in the non-isothermal case; the paper attributes the result to a hotter, faster anode and a cooler cathode with better CO₂ solubility/higher FE. Its model also exhibits a real membrane-thickness tradeoff: added thickness can raise FE through a larger thermal gradient while adding ohmic overpotential, so the energy optimum is interior. Source: [“Non-isothermal CO₂ electrolysis enables simultaneous enhanced electrochemical and anti-precipitation performance,” *Nature Communications* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59604-6). These are source-reported values; exact thermal setpoints and model boundaries should be checked in the full supplement before reproducing the numerical comparison.

The cited work supports this hierarchy of claims: (i) local reaction rates depend on potential and transport jointly; (ii) whole-cell FE–voltage accounting is exact when measured consistently; (iii) particular electrode designs have measurable Pareto curves; (iv) no universal intrinsic catalyst tradeoff follows from those facts. Catalytic selectivity is not the same metric as carbon utilization: carbonate formation, unreacted CO₂, crossover, and product separation sit outside FE.

## Spatial resistance bound for electrode design

For a thin catalyst sheet with resistivity ρ and thickness $t$, assume uniform normal current density $j$, constant sheet resistivity, and quasi-steady in-plane conduction. With one edge collected at $x=0$, charge conservation gives sheet current per unit width $K(x)=j(L-x)$. Ohm’s law $dV/dx=\rho K/t$ yields

\[
\Delta V_{\rm edge}=\frac{\rho jL^2}{2t}.
\]

If equipotential conductors are spaced by $s$, the maximum distance to a conductor is $s/2$, giving

\[
\Delta V_{\rm pitch}=\frac{\rho j s^2}{8t},\qquad
\frac{\Delta V_{\rm pitch}}{\Delta V_{\rm edge}}=\frac{s^2}{4L^2}.
\]

For a square 50-cm² electrode with side length $L=70.7$ mm and 4-mm pitch, the idealized maximum-drop ratio is $8.0\times10^{-4}$. This is a conditional sheet model, not the nonlinear operating field: local current changes with local potential. Rufer et al. solve that feedback numerically. A testable design consequence is the $s^2$ dependence before other limits dominate. The reported HCGDE wire pitch (4 mm) and its <2% footprint fit this mechanism, but do not certify the scaling formula for every catalyst layer or cell.

## Strongest attack and novelty boundary

1. **Counterexample to universality.** Set pathway activation slopes and reaction orders equal. FE is then exactly potential- and transport-independent while turnover changes with the common kinetic prefactor. This directly falsifies any universal monotone FE-versus-overpotential or FE-versus-turnover law over the stated interface.
2. **Metric mismatch.** Cathode overpotential, cell voltage, FE, carbon conversion, turnover, and energy per mole are distinct observables. Treating one as a proxy for the others silently changes the objective and boundary.
3. **Acquisition debt.** The one-reactant bound needs an effective limiting flux measured under operating geometry; the TOF needs an operating-state active-site density; energy per mole needs calibrated product flow and full-cell voltage. Feed, pump, heat, crossover, and separation loads are additional system terms.
4. **Mechanism alternatives.** Flow can change CO₂ supply, local pH, adsorbate residence, flooding, salt precipitation, and catalyst morphology. MOF layers can also change adsorption energetics. A raw FE shift cannot identify which mechanism changed.
5. **Prior art/novelty.** The series kinetic/transport expression is a standard Koutecky–Levich-type correction; Faradaic energy accounting and coupled microkinetic/continuum modeling are established. This work does not claim a new universal theorem, new catalyst, or verified priority. The conditional bounds are internally reconstructed algebra. No wet-lab experiment, independent numerical PDE replay, or systematic patent/prior-art search was performed.

## Discriminating experiment

**Question:** at fixed Cu catalyst chemistry, does scale-dependent C₂+ FE loss follow the measured spatial voltage field, a transport/pH change, or both?

Use identical 250-nm Cu on 450-nm-pore ePTFE cathodes in the same MEA design and prepare three electrical architectures: no internal collectors, 10-mm copper-wire pitch, and 4-mm pitch. Add an electrically disconnected-wire sham with the same perforation and geometric footprint. Test a small near-equipotential coupon first to acquire product-specific $j_i(\eta_{\rm local})$ curves; freeze these curves before predicting the larger-cell outcomes. At a fixed cathode composition and measured superficial CO₂ velocity, test 100 and 200 mA cm⁻², then repeat at a second flow condition while holding CO₂ stoichiometric excess fixed. Randomize architecture order and use at least three independently fabricated electrodes per condition.

Measure full-cell voltage and total current continuously; local cathode potential at several positions with reference taps; sheet resistance by four-point probe; sector-resolved H₂/CO/CH₄/C₂H₄ by GC; liquid products by NMR or HPLC; CO₂ inlet/outlet flow, carbonate crossover, pressure drop, temperature, and local pH (calibrated operando Raman or an equivalent probe). Record FE, partial current, carbon balance/single-pass conversion, and $V_{\rm cell}/\mathrm{FE}_i$-based stack energy. Report geometric partial current as primary turnover evidence; add TOF only if Cu site density is measured under operating conditions with uncertainty.

The matched controls are (a) the same Cu/ePTFE layer with the original edge collector, (b) same-pitch electrically inactive wires to detect wetting/gas-flow effects from perforation, and (c) the small-area coupon used only for kinetic calibration. A strong model comparator gets identical coupon kinetics and feed data but assumes one uniform potential; the full model adds measured sheet resistance and solves local current–voltage feedback; a transport comparator adds measured local pH/CO₂ but no electrical field. No comparator receives extra catalyst characterization unavailable to the others.

**Falsifiers:** if reducing wire pitch does not reduce measured local voltage spread, the sheet-conduction mechanism fails; if it does reduce voltage spread but the sector FE/current fails to follow coupon-calibrated local kinetics after measured pH/CO₂ are included, the voltage-only explanation fails; if electrically inactive wires reproduce the active-wire effect, conduction is not the cause; if flow changes FE after local pH, CO₂, potential, and catalyst state are matched, the proposed state variables are incomplete. A universal intrinsic FE–overpotential law would require the same local relationship to survive these architecture and flow interventions; the existing source record already makes that a poor prior.

**Cost/accounting:** the 50-cm² point at 200 mA cm⁻² requires a 10-A supply and a custom large MEA/flow field; a 5-cm² screening cell is a lower-cost first stage. Materials include matched sputtered Cu/ePTFE cathodes, 75-µm wires, AEM, IrO₂ anode, electrolyte, gas, seals, and replacement electrodes. Required instruments are a high-current power supply/potentiostat, gas flow control and GC, liquid-product assay, voltage/reference taps, and four-point resistance measurement. Operando Raman adds substantial instrument time but is the cleanest local-pH control. A compact matrix of 3 pitches × 2 flows × 2 current densities × 3 replicates is 36 steady-state holds (8.4 h at 14 min/hold, before conditioning and assays); the 14-min interval matches the cited study’s performance sweeps but should be lengthened if stationarity is not reached. No dollar estimate is supplied because no current local quotes or lab inventory were collected. A separate 100–200 h durability run is required before claiming stable scale-up.
