LOW-PAIR-NUMBER ESTIMATION FOR GENERAL HARD BCS NORMS
Worker c03_l10; initial independent attack; 7 October 2026.

CLAIM

Let F be an explicit n by n matrix over Q(i), let 0<=k<=floor(n/2), and set

 c_k(F) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty} |det F[I,J]|^2.

There is a randomized relative approximation for c_k using
O(9^k eps^(-2) log(1/delta)) samples and polynomial bit work per sample.
Thus the exact coefficient is approximable in time
 9^k poly(n,L,eps^(-1),log(1/delta)),
where L is the total rational-complex input bit length. In particular it is
polynomial time for fixed k or k=O(log n). For k near n/2 the bound is
exponential and does not solve the general hard-BCS counting gate.

The algorithm is an unbiased Rademacher estimator whose sample is computable
in polynomial bit time by a determinant and a formal square root. It does not
query c_k, a minor-sum oracle, or an acquired sampler. It applies to arbitrary
F, so it is a low-density extension beyond the known bipartite pair-support
subclass, not a solution for unrestricted canonical sectors.

DERIVATION

Write u_i=e_i and v_i=(row_i(F))^T in C^n, and let V have the 2n columns
(u_1,v_1,...,u_n,v_n). For independent uniform signs s_i in {+1,-1}, form

 A_s = sum_i s_i (u_i v_i^T - v_i u_i^T).

This is an n by n complex skew-symmetric matrix. For each row set R of size
2k, Pfaffian Cauchy-Binet gives

 Pf(A_s[R]) = sum_{|S|=k} (product_{i in S} s_i) det V[R,P(S)],

where P(S) selects both columns u_i,v_i for every i in S (the pair-column
ordering fixes the same sign throughout). Orthogonality of Rademacher
characters gives

 E_s |Pf(A_s[R])|^2 = sum_{|S|=k} |det V[R,P(S)]|^2.

Set X_k(A)=sum_{|R|=2k}|Pf(A[R])|^2. Summing over R and applying
Cauchy-Binet to each selected pair-column matrix yields

 E_s X_k(A_s)
 = sum_{|S|=k} det(V[:,P(S)]^* V[:,P(S)])
 = c_k(F).

The last equality is the baseline identity: expanding the Gram determinant
selects k identity columns and k F-row columns; a nonzero term requires
disjoint row sets I,J and contributes |det F[I,J]|^2.

For every complex skew-symmetric A, its unitary congruence normal form has
blocks [[0,sigma_j],[-sigma_j,0]] and possibly one zero coordinate. Therefore

 det(I+t A^* A) = product_j (1+t sigma_j^2)^2,
 sqrt(det(I+t A^* A)) = sum_{k>=0} X_k(A) t^k.

The second identity follows in the block form and is invariant under unitary
congruence: the vector of principal 2k-Pfaffians transforms by the unitary
exterior-power representation. Equivalently, X_k(A) is the k-th coefficient
of the unique formal square root D(t)^(1/2) with constant term 1. If
D(t)=sum d_j t^j and Q(t)=sum q_j t^j, compute exactly by

 q_0=1,
 q_m=(d_m - sum_{j=1}^{m-1} q_j q_{m-j})/2.

For rational-complex A all d_j and q_j are rational. The determinant
polynomial is a perfect square; no numerical square-root or eigenvalue
calculation is used. A standard exact characteristic-polynomial algorithm
computes the needed d_0,...,d_k in polynomial bit complexity.

VARIANCE AND APPROXIMATION COST

For fixed R, p_R(s)=Pf(A_s[R]) is a homogeneous degree-k multilinear
polynomial on the Boolean cube. Scalar degree-k hypercontractivity gives
||Re p_R||_4, ||Im p_R||_4 <= 3^(k/2) times the corresponding L2 norm.
By the triangle inequality, ||p_R||_4^2 <= 2*3^k E|p_R|^2. Minkowski in
L2 then gives

 ||X_k(A_s)||_2
 <= sum_R || |p_R(s)|^2 ||_2
 <= 2*3^k sum_R E|p_R(s)|^2
 = 2*3^k c_k(F).

Consequently E X_k^2 <= 4*9^k c_k^2. A batch of
ceil(16*9^k/eps^2) independent signs has its sample mean within relative
eps of c_k with probability at least 3/4 by Chebyshev. Taking the median of
O(log(1/delta)) independent batch means gives success probability at least
1-delta. If c_k=0, every Pfaffian coefficient is zero, so every sample is
exactly zero. If c_k>0, the same second-moment bound controls zero-valued
individual samples; the median guarantee still applies.

Each sample uses n random bits and exact construction of A_s, the degree-n
polynomial det(I+t A_s^*A_s), and its formal square root through degree k.
This is polynomial in n and the bit length of F. Determinants have polynomial
bit growth by Hadamard bounds; the square-root recurrence adds only polynomial
bit length through k steps. Averaging the stated number of rational samples
adds O(k+log n+log(1/eps)+log log(1/delta)) bits beyond the per-sample bounds.
No floating precision or finite-field false-zero budget is needed.

COMPARISON TO BASELINE / BOUNDARY

The latest baseline obtains deterministic polynomial-bit support and a witness
through the imported Hamada-Iwata represented-linear-parity algorithm, but
explicitly has no counting oracle. It also proves tractability for the
bipartite pair-support subclass and leaves arbitrary F open at a microscopic
sampling/energy gate. This estimator adds an acquired, costed approximation
for low k. It does not use the support algorithm, six-hole exchange, balancing
data, annealing, graph-matching FPRAS, or pair-support restriction.

The factor 9^k is load-bearing: for k=Theta(n), the method is exponential.
Hypercontractivity alone cannot establish a general polynomial-time FPRAS.
No claim is made that this coefficient is #P-hard to approximate, that the
estimator has low variance for large k, that physical state preparation is
implemented, or that the method is novel relative to all literature.

LITERATURE / PRIOR ART SEARCH

Primary papers found:
- Iwata and Kobayashi, A Weighted Linear Matroid Parity Algorithm,
  arXiv:1905.13371, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13371. This is an optimization
  algorithm for represented parity, not a weighted partition/counting oracle.
- Matoya and Oki, Pfaffian Pairs and Parities, arXiv:1912.00620,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00620. It identifies tractable Pfaffian parity
  classes and counts minimum-weight solutions there; the abstract does not
  give the general squared-coefficient FPRAS above.
- Hamada and Iwata, A Shortest Augmenting Path Algorithm for Linear Matroid
  Parity, arXiv:2610.03030v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03030v1. Theorem 1.1
  is deterministic support/optimization and returns witnesses; it is not a
  count or approximation algorithm.
- Celis et al., On the Complexity of Constrained Determinantal Point
  Processes, arXiv:1608.00554, https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00554, studies
  constrained DPP sampling/counting and gives hardness for binary-described
  constraints. Our all-or-none pair selector is a related constrained Gram
  ensemble, but no direct reduction to their constraint model was established.

This was a targeted outward search, not a complete priority review. The
Pfaffian Cauchy-Binet identity, Youla unitary-congruence form for complex
skew matrices, determinant coefficient computation, and Boolean-cube
hypercontractivity are imported standard tools. I did not retrieve full proofs
of the cited matroid/DPP papers and do not claim external verification of
those papers here. The FPRAS derivation is self-contained above apart from
those standard tools; priority remains unverified.
