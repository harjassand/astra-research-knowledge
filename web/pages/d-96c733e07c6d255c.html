Finite-characteristic transfers for algebraic matrix multiplication (leaf 11)
Date: 2026-10-08

STATUS
------
PROVED / PRIMARY-SOURCE THEOREM: for every field F, the algebraic matrix-
multiplication exponent over F equals the exponent over its prime field F_0.
Thus it depends at most on characteristic. This transfers bounds across fields
of the SAME characteristic; it does not transfer a characteristic-zero bound
to positive characteristic.

EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE: tensor rank of a fixed finite-dimensional algebra can
drop under extension even in characteristic p: multiplication in F_{p^2} has
rank 3 over F_p and rank 2 after scalar extension to F_{p^2}.

CHARACTERISTIC-P EXTRACTION LIMIT: ordinary derivative extraction can fail
exactly in characteristic p: (d/dt)^p(t^p)=0 in F_p[t], although [t^p]t^p=1.
This refutes that particular characteristic-zero extraction step when p divides
the derivative order. It does not refute the border-rank-to-exponent theorem;
coefficient extraction/interpolation must be used in a form valid in the field.

UNRESOLVED HERE: whether the matrix-multiplication exponents for different
characteristics coincide. No such equality follows from the same-characteristic
theorem below. No specific characteristic-zero algorithm has been reduced to
every F_p in this leaf.

1. EXACT SAME-CHARACTERISTIC TRANSFER
------------------------------------
Let F be any field, F_0 its prime field, and R_K(M_n) the bilinear rank of the
n-by-n matrix multiplication tensor over K. Use the standard expression
omega(K)=inf_{n>=2} log_n R_K(M_n) for the algebraic matrix-multiplication
exponent (equivalently, its arithmetic-circuit exponent). Schönhage's Theorem 2.8 states
that omega(F)=omega(F_0); Theorem 2.7 gives the finite-extension descent cost.
Primary source: A. Schönhage, “Partial and Total Matrix Multiplication,” SIAM
Journal on Computing 10(3) (1981), 434–455, especially Theorems 2.7–2.8,
pp. 440–441. https://doi.org/10.1137/0210032

Here is the fixed-witness accounting behind the transfer. A rank-r decomposition
of M_n over F is a solution to a finite system of polynomial equations over
F_0, with one variable for each coordinate of its linear forms. Since that
system has an F-point, its defining ideal is proper over F_0. It therefore has
a point over the algebraic closure of F_0. Only finitely many coordinates are
used, so that point is defined over some finite extension E/F_0 of degree d.
The same rank-r decomposition consequently exists over E. This step applies in
every characteristic; it does not specialize a characteristic-zero identity
modulo p.

Tensoring this one fixed decomposition s times gives a rank-r^s decomposition
of M_{n^s} over E. Multiplication in E, represented in an F_0-basis, has
bilinear rank mu_{F_0}(E) at most d^2 by the schoolbook structure-constant
algorithm. Replacing each E-scalar product by that algorithm gives

  R_{F_0}(M_{n^s}) <= mu_{F_0}(E) r^s <= d^2 r^s.

The key cost point is that E and d belong to the one fixed witness and do not
grow with s. Hence

  limsup_s log R_{F_0}(M_{n^s}) / log(n^s) <= log_n(r).

Taking the infimum over fixed n and its F-rank witnesses gives
omega(F_0) <= omega(F). The reverse inequality follows because an F_0
decomposition remains valid after extending scalars to F. This is the robust
transfer. A possibly large degree d changes a constant factor, not the
exponent, because tensor powers reuse that same E.

The same reasoning applies to a fixed border-rank witness. The secant variety
defining border rank is an algebraic variety over F_0; a point over F yields a
point over the algebraic closure of F_0 and hence over a finite E/F_0. The
standard border-rank exactification estimate over a field with enough
interpolation points gives, for a fixed tensor T with border rank at most r,

  R(T^{tensor s}) <= C s^2 r^s,

where C depends on the fixed witness, not on s. Schönhage records this
border-rank version and the O(s^2) rank bound for tensor powers in his ICM
1986 article, pp. 146–147:
https://www.mathunion.org/fileadmin/ICM/Proceedings/ICM1986.1/ICM1986.1.ocr.pdf
For a finite base field, make the interpolation overhead explicit. Freeze a
one-parameter degeneration whose coefficients lie in E, and let its powered
degree be D_s=O(s); let B_s=O(s) count poles or other bad parameter values.
Extend E by degree e_s=ceil(log_Q(D_s+B_s+1))=O(log s), where Q=|E|. This
leaves more than D_s good evaluation points. The standard exactification bound
is R_{E_s}(T^{tensor s})<=C s^2 r^s; the interpolation data have size
polynomial in s. Descending to F_0 costs at most mu_{F_0}(E_s)<=(d e_s)^2
scalar products, so

  R_{F_0}(T^{tensor s}) <= O(d^2 s^2 log^2(s) r^s).

This is algebraic coefficient extraction, not a floating-point limit. In
characteristic p, a proof that uses ordinary p-th derivatives or division by
p! is invalid. A concrete degeneration must state its degree, bad parameter
values, and coefficient field; the generic border-rank theorem does not
validate an unspecified extraction recipe. The O(s^2) estimate and the
O(log^2 s) scalar descent factor contribute only a polynomial-in-s factor,
which vanishes in the exponent.

2. EXACT FINITE-CHARACTERISTIC COUNTEREXAMPLE TO RANK PRESERVATION
------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix any prime p and let A=F_{p^2}, viewed as a two-dimensional algebra over
F_p. Its multiplication tensor T_A has rank exactly 3 over F_p.

Upper bound: choose alpha with alpha^2=c alpha+d in a basis 1,alpha. For
x=a+b alpha and y=a'+b' alpha, compute aa', bb', and (a+b)(a'+b') using
three F_p multiplications. The output coordinates are

  aa' + d bb',
  (a+b)(a'+b') - aa' + (c-1)bb'.

The formula remains valid when p=2. Lower bound: the Alder–Strassen bound
for a finite-dimensional algebra A is R(A) >= 2 dim(A) - t, where t is its
number of maximal two-sided ideals. A field has t=1, so
R_{F_p}(T_A)>=4-1=3. For this two-dimensional case, the lower bound also has
a direct explanation: in a rank-2 decomposition, slicing by the unit in
each input gives the identity map, so both output vectors and both pairs of
input functionals must be bases. The two identity-slice equations make the
input functionals proportional coordinate by coordinate. Products of two
nonzero vectors supported on different coordinates then vanish, giving zero
divisors, which a field cannot have.
Primary source: A. Alder and V. Strassen, “On the algorithmic complexity of
associative algebras,” Theoretical Computer Science 15(2) (1981), 201–211.
https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(81)90070-0

Now extend scalars to E=F_{p^2}. Finite fields are separable, so

  A tensor_{F_p} E ~= E x E.

The product algebra multiplies coordinatewise and has rank 2 over E; its
two-dimensional output flattening gives the matching lower bound. Therefore

  R_{F_p}(T_A)=3,   R_E(T_A)=2.

Over the algebraic closure its border rank is also 2. This small example is a
warning against silently identifying base-field rank, extension-field rank,
and geometric border rank. It is consistent with the exponent transfer above:
descent pays a constant extension-multiplication factor.

3. REDUCTION BOUNDARY
---------------------
A characteristic-zero decomposition with rational/integer coefficients can
be reduced modulo p only after checking every denominator and every identity
used by the construction. For one fixed decomposition, only its finitely many
denominator primes need be excluded. For an infinite family of decompositions,
the excluded primes may vary with the family index; that does not prove a
uniform result for every fixed p. Decompositions over number fields or over
characteristic-zero algebraic closures need an explicit integral model and a
good-reduction check before reduction. Positive-characteristic Frobenius can
also collapse terms, as (x+y)^p=x^p+y^p demonstrates.

Falsifier for the main transfer: an explicit pair of fields of the same
characteristic with rigorously different matrix-multiplication exponents, or
an error in the fixed-witness descent argument. The finite-tensor counterexample
would be falsified by a rank-2 F_p decomposition of T_{F_{p^2}}, contradicting
the cited Alder–Strassen lower bound. A purported p-specific degeneration
transfer is falsified by one claimed admissible p for which direct substitution
does not give the target tensor or the extraction coefficient is wrong.

4. RESTART
----------
For each proposed matrix-multiplication transfer, record: (i) the exact
characteristic of the coefficient field; (ii) the fixed tensor and whether the
input is rank or border rank; (iii) the coefficient field and its degree over
the prime field for the chosen witness; (iv) all denominators and excluded
primes; (v) the degeneration order/degree and extraction operation; (vi) the
extension degree and scalar-multiplication cost used for finite-field
interpolation; and (vii) the resulting exact rank bound before taking the
exponent. Preserve a characteristic-zero-only status if a step has not been
reduced in the target characteristic. Do not promote the same-characteristic
theorem to a cross-characteristic theorem. The next useful check is to audit
the parent programme's actual imported decompositions against this list and
verify any asserted prime-uniform reduction identity symbolically.
