# Causal transport between cell models: identifiability, invariant mechanisms, and a matched test

## Finding

A perturbation effect does not transport from one cell model to another just because untreated profiles, annotations, or source-model fit look similar. The exact boundary is causal: a target response can be standardized from a source experiment when the same well-defined intervention has the same conditional causal response given measured *pre-treatment* state and the target state distribution overlaps source support. If the response mechanism can change freely, source randomization plus even an exactly matching target observational distribution leaves the target average treatment effect unidentified.

The positive formula is classical transportability. The new work here is an explicit, cell-model-compatible no-go witness, an exact identified interval for it, and a concrete validation design that makes the assumption testable on named recipient domains. The approximate-invariance inequality is elementary bookkeeping, not a claimed novel theorem. No result here says that an in-vitro cell model transfers to intact tissue, an organism, or a clinical outcome.

## Exact mathematical result

Let `D∈{S,T}` denote source or target, `A∈{0,1}` a declared intervention assignment, `Z` measured before assignment, `Y∈[0,1]` a prespecified endpoint, and `Y^d(a)` potential outcomes. If (i) source assignment is randomized conditional on `Z`, (ii) both arms are represented wherever the target `Z` distribution has support, (iii) the intervention version/measurement is consistent, and (iv) the conditional causal means satisfy `E_T[Y^T(a)|Z=z]=E_S[Y^S(a)|Z=z]` for both arms, then

`τ_T = E_T[Y^T(1)-Y^T(0)] = ∫ [E_S(Y|A=1,Z=z) − E_S(Y|A=0,Z=z)] dP_T(z)`.

This is the direct standardization formula. It allows the target to have a different baseline mix of donor/cell states. It does not handle unmeasured effect modifiers, post-treatment adjustment, changed intervention versions, or target states outside source support. The proof and scope are in [PROOF_AND_TRANSPORT.md](PROOF_AND_TRANSPORT.md).

If conditional source-target mean response differs by at most `ε` per arm, the fitted source contrast is uniformly within `ρ` of its truth, and the estimated target state law is within total variation `δ`, then the standardized point estimate errs by at most `2ε+ρ+2δ`. The `2ε` term is unobserved target-mechanism drift; without data or an external restriction to bound it, the statement provides no useful guarantee. This inequality is a direct decomposition, not a novelty claim.

## No-go counterexample

Take source observations with `A=Y∼Bernoulli(1/2)` and randomized source interventions `do(A=a)` with `Y=a`, giving source ATE 1. Let the target observational distribution also be `A=Y∼Bernoulli(1/2)`. Two target structural models agree on every one of those observations:

- Target model I has `A=V`, `Y=A`, so target ATE is 1.
- Target model II has `A=U`, `Y=U`, so intervening on `A` leaves `Y=U` and target ATE is 0.

Here `U,V` are Bernoulli(1/2). The target effect is not identified even though source and target observational laws match exactly and the source RCT is fully known. For this target diagonal law, the sharp ATE identified set under consistency is `[0,1]`; hence any point transfer has worst-case absolute error at least `1/2`. This is a standard non-identifiability phenomenon, not a new general result. The witness states precisely what target randomization or an explicit mechanism-invariance assumption must rule out.

## Empirical evidence and prior art

Causal transportability theory already formalizes the requirement that cross-population mechanism commonalities be stated. Pearl and Bareinboim use selection diagrams and do-calculus to decide whether source experimental effects plus target observations suffice; they emphasize that invariance knowledge is a prerequisite ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-STS486), [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.01603)). Invariant causal prediction studies invariance across observed regimes and has been tested on held-out yeast gene deletions ([Peters et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12167); [Meinshausen et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1510493113)). These establish important formal/methodological precedents; none licenses every unseen tissue/species automatically.

The biological precedent is mixed rather than globally invariant. Hagai et al. treated primary fibroblasts from four mammalian species under common culture and matched control conditions, using species-aware IFN reagents and poly(I:C). They found cross-species divergence in cytokine/chemokine response programs and greater conservation in some regulatory TF/kinase groups ([Nature 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0657-2)). This motivates endpoint- and mechanism-specific tests, while remaining an in-vitro study.

Single-cell predictive methods have reported out-of-sample transfer across cell types/studies/species (scGen, 2019; CellOT, 2023), but prediction performance is not a causal identification theorem ([scGen](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-019-0494-8); [CellOT](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01969-x)). A broad 2026 benchmark across 25 datasets and 24 metrics reports that no method consistently generalized across cell types and that performance depended on effect size and metric ([Science Advances 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aed3414)). Other recent benchmark papers disagree about whether deep models beat controls under different metrics, including a 2025 Nature Methods study and a 2026 Nature Biotechnology metric-calibration study ([2025 Nature Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6); [2026 Nature Biotechnology](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-026-03307-w)). The practical consequence is to use equal-information baselines and predeclared calibrated outcome, delta, differential-expression, and distributional criteria.

## Discriminating experiment

In source and a named recipient cell context, randomize a single perturbation versus its matched negative control within donor/sample × plate blocks. Use a non-targeting guide plus matched transfection for CRISPRi, or a matched vehicle for a compound; include mock delivery and a positive assay control. Fix or deliberately randomize plate, passage, density, medium lot, oxygenation, matrix, exposure time, and harvest. Measure pretreatment state `Z` from a baseline aliquot, assay guide identity and target engagement, and lock the source-only prediction before opening randomized recipient perturbation outcomes. Compare the source response standardized to the recipient baseline-state distribution against the recipient randomized effect and report the treatment-by-domain difference. Report per-cell-type response and overall composition effects separately. Analyze independent donor/well blocks, not individual cells as independent replicates.

The detailed protocol defines the intervention-version distinction, equal-information controls (no-change, pooled source effect, simple linear/additive), falsifiers, costs, and a conservative precision calculation: for `n` independent randomized biological blocks per domain with bounded `Y∈[0,1]`, `Pr(|Δ̂−Δ|≥h)≤2 exp(−n h²/4)`, where `Δ` is the source-target difference in matched treatment effects. Familywise control across `K` prespecified contrasts is obtained with `n≥4 h⁻² log(2K/α)` per domain under the stated independent-block assumptions. See [EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md](EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md).

A failed target-engagement or positive-control check means the intended intervention/assay did not match. A successful finite panel supports only the assayed recipient contexts. Organoids, ex-vivo tissue, and intact organisms require their own target-domain tests.

## Verdict and next gate

**Verdict:** generic cross-cell-model perturbation transfer is not identified from source experiments and target baselines alone. Standardized transfer is defensible only under an explicit conditional-response invariance/overlap contract. The counterexample is a principled rejection of unconditional transfer; the proposed randomized recipient panel is the decisive next observation.

**Next gate:** select one perturbation and endpoint, then run the blocked source/recipient randomized design with source predictions locked, target engagement checked, and the target effect held out. A nonzero domain-treatment interaction outside the preregistered tolerance refutes the proposed transport contract for that endpoint and context; equivalence within tolerance yields scoped experimental support, not universal invariance.
