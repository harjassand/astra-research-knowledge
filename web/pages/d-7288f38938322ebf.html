THEOREM / INTERFACE
Supply m pairs u_l,v_l in Q(i)^d and nonnegative rational activities lambda_l.
Delete zero-activity labels, retaining m positive ones. Let V_S be the d-by-2|S|
matrix of selected interleaved pair columns and define
 Z_k=sum_{|S|=k} lambda_S det(V_S* V_S), lambda_S=product_l in S lambda_l.
The public implementation rejects noninteger/negative k and returns zero
for k>min(m,floor(d/2)). Exact span-rank R is acquired by rational elimination;
2k>R also certifies zero but its converse is not used.
Let r=min(k,m-k). There is an acquired nonnegative rational unbiased sample Y
of Z_k with
 E Y^2 <= C_r Z_k^2,
 C_r=binom(2r,r), except C_2=24/5 may be used.
The sample has polynomial bit cost in d,m,k,input length; no coefficient,
support, balancing, tiny-norm or conditional-probability oracle is supplied.
Hence a capped median-of-means relative approximation uses
 O(C_r eps^-2 log(1/delta)) samples, each with polynomial bit work.
This is polynomial for fixed public r<=A log(m+d+1). Both direct and
complement primitives and the capped estimator are implemented below.

The24/5 constant is sharp as a uniform supremum for the phase estimator;
it is attained exactly by its conceptual complex-Gaussian comparison and
approached by rational finite unit-phase inputs. It is not a claim that
binom(2r,r) is sharp at r>=3 or that every counting algorithm needs these
sample counts. On the actual hard-BCS interface m=n and k<=n/2, r=k;
low-complement symmetry alone therefore does not extend the unrestricted
dense-sector gate. It is useful for general vector-pair auxiliaries and
constrained problems with few omitted labels.

1. UNBIASEDNESS AND COMPUTABLE NORM, ATTRIBUTED RECONSTRUCTION
For scalars eta_l set B=sum_l eta_l(u_l v_l^T-v_l u_l^T). Its transpose is
ordinary transpose, not adjoint. In exterior coordinates omega=sum_l eta_l
u_l wedge v_l. Degree-two forms commute and a decomposable form squares to
zero. Thus for every row set A of size2k,
 p_A(eta)=Pf B[A]=sum_{|S|=k} det(V_S[A]) product_l in S eta_l.   (1)
If the independent eta_l have mean zero, rotational symmetry through fourth
order and E|eta_l|^2=lambda_l, orthogonality gives
 E sum_A |p_A(eta)|^2=Z_k.                                   (2)
Cauchy-Binet identifies the sum over A with each Gram determinant.

The scalar X_j(B)=sum_{|A|=2j}|Pf B[A]|^2 is acquired in polynomial bit time:
 X_j(B)=[t^j] sqrt(det(I+t B*B)), constant root coefficient1.
Unitary skew-congruence and exterior norm invariance prove this familiar
identity. It also follows algebraically from the principal-minor expansion
of Pf[[tB,I],[-I,-bar B]], with global sign(-1)^(d(d-1)/2): a matching must
use the same internal index set in both blocks; the lower block contributes
(-1)^j, and the crossing identity edges contribute the compensating sign.
Squaring this Pfaffian gives det(I+t B*B).

For literal exact computation, form M=B*B and T_j=Tr(M^j). Newton identities
give d_0=1 and j d_j=sum_{a=1}^j(-1)^(a-1)d_(j-a)T_a.
Then q_0=1 and2q_j=d_j-sum_{a=1}^{j-1}q_a q_(j-a). Return q_j=X_j(B).
This uses O(j d^3+j^2) Gaussian-rational operations with naive multiplication.
No eigenvalue, floating square root or principal-minor enumeration appears in
production. Exact determinants and prefix Gram inverses have polynomial-bit
minor bounds. The standard Youla/Pfaffian tools are prior art, not new methods.

2. RATIONAL ACTIVATION: NO ALGEBRAIC ACTIVITY ROOT OR PRECISION DEBT
For positive rational variance a/b choose a rational radius R as follows.
If a and b are perfect squares, take their rational square-root quotient.
Otherwise let t=ceil(sqrt(16ab)), computed by integer square root, and R=t/(4b).
Then lambda<=R^2<=25lambda/16<2lambda. With p=lambda/R^2 in[16/25,1], choose
 eta=0 with probability1-p, and eta=R z otherwise,
where z is uniform on{1,-1,i,-i}. These draws use exact rational Bernoulli
and two-bit phase decisions. They have E eta=0, E|eta|^2=lambda and
 E|eta|^4=lambda R^2<=2lambda^2.
The four roots suffice because(1) is multilinear: every exponent in p^2 is
at most2, so the fourth-moment exponent differences lie in[-2,2]. Thus
E eta^a bar(eta)^b=0 for unequal a,b<=2. Normalize eta/ sqrt(lambda) only
inside the proof; the algorithm never computes that square root.

3. GENERAL BINOMIAL MOMENT BOUND, NOT THE BCS SUPPORT-SIZE BOUND
Consider any homogeneous squarefree degree-r polynomial P(z)=sum_T a_T z_T.
Let independent z_l satisfy the normalized activated-phase moments above,
with beta_l=E|z_l|^4<=2. Orthogonality gives E|P|^2=sum_T|a_T|^2.
Write P^2=sum_alpha b_alpha z^alpha, alpha_l in{0,1,2}. If alpha has h repeated
coordinates, the remaining2(r-h) coordinates have exponent1. There are
 N_alpha=binom(2(r-h),r-h)
ordered decompositions into two r-sets. Cauchy-Schwarz gives
 |b_alpha|^2<=N_alpha sum_(T,U in its fiber)|a_T|^2|a_U|^2.
The fourth-moment multiplier is product_(alpha_l=2) beta_l<=2^h.
Since2^h binom(2(r-h),r-h)<=binom(2r,r), summing fibers proves
 E|P|^4<=binom(2r,r)(E|P|^2)^2.                              (3)
The binomial inequality follows inductively from
binom(2j+2,j+1)/binom(2j,j)=2(2j+1)/(j+1)>=2.
Minkowski in L2 applied to sum_A |p_A|^2 proves the direct Y moment bound.
Unlike c03_s02's BCS pointwise bound, no sparsity of coefficients S subset A
is used. This is a second-moment bound, not a pointwise cap of Y/Z.

4. ACQUIRED COMPLEMENT WITH ZERO RANDOM VARIABLES
For each row set A define the complemented polynomial
 Q_A(z)=sum_{|T|=m-k} det(V_(T^c)[A]) product_l in T z_l.
Draw z_l by rational activation with variance1/lambda_l. Orthogonality shows
 E(lambda_all sum_A|Q_A(z)|^2)=Z_k, lambda_all=product_l lambda_l.
This has degree q=m-k, so(3) gives binom(2q,q), irrespective of the much
larger original particle number k. Computing Q by listing its coefficients
would be an acquisition error; the following exact contraction avoids it.

Let H={l:z_l=0}, h=|H|. If h>k the scalar is zero. Let C contain the2h
forced vectors in H. If C is dependent the scalar is zero. Otherwise acquire
 G=C*C, D=det G>0, P=I-C G^-1 C*.
For l notin H put a_l=1/z_l and form
 B'=sum_(l notin H) a_l[(P u_l)(P v_l)^T-(P v_l)(P u_l)^T].
The required nonnegative rational sample is
 Y=lambda_all product_(l notin H)|z_l|^2 D X_(k-h)(B').        (4)
Proof: zero coordinates in Q force their labels into the original selected
set. Pull out product_nonzero z_l, leaving the exterior product of C wedged
with omega'^(k-h)/(k-h)!. Components of omega' parallel to span C vanish in
this wedge, so P may be applied. The squared exterior norm factors into
det(C*C) times the squared projected exterior norm. This gives(4), including
arbitrary complex phases, exact zeros and cancellations. Pair blocks have
even degree, so regrouping forced pairs introduces no sign.

All data in(4) are acquired rational matrices and scalars. Inverses are used
only after exact rank/nonzero tests. A small positive Gram determinant or
activity changes encoded numerator/denominator length, not sample number;
denominator-clearing determinant bounds give polynomial bit sizes. Zero
lambda labels were removed before the reciprocal construction. It is not
rejection conditioned on a rare nonzero phase pattern.
Choose the direct or complement construction according to min(k,m-k).
The k=0 and k=m endpoints are exact constants; full-volume counting can
simply compute lambda_all det(V_all*V_all).

5. SHARP TWO-PAIR BOUND24/5: COMPLETE RANK AND POLYGON ARGUMENT
For one principal four-coordinate p_A(z), write p_A=z^T C z. Its symmetric
coefficient matrix C has zero diagonal, because each line bivector is simple.
Moreover rank C<=6: the Pfaffian of a4-by-4 skew matrix is a quadratic form
in its six entries, and those entries are linear in z. Activities simply
diagonally scale C and preserve these properties.

Use the standard Takagi factorization C=U diag(tau_j)U^T, tau_j>=0, U unitary.
It has an elementary proof: the antilinear map T(x)=C bar x satisfies
T^2=C C*, Hermitian PSD. On each positive tau^2 eigenspace, T/tau is an
antiunitary involution; its real fixed subspace admits an orthonormal basis
and spans that eigenspace over C. These fixed vectors satisfy C bar u=tau u.
The zero eigenspace is killed by T, giving the asserted factorization.
This is a reconstruction of classical prior art, not a new congruence theorem.

Zero diagonal implies the polygon inequality. For the largest tau_1, each
diagonal equation gives tau_1|U_i1|^2<=sum_(j>1)tau_j|U_ij|^2. Sum i and use
orthonormal columns to obtain tau_1<=sum_(j>1)tau_j.

For at most6 nonnegative polygon lengths, the sharp inequality is
 sum tau_j^4/(sum tau_j^2)^2<=7/10.                         (5)
Here is a full finite-dimensional optimization proof. Normalize tau_1=1.
Scale all other lengths down until their sum is1; this cannot decrease the
ratio: writing s=sum x_i^2,q=sum x_i^4, its scaling derivative has the sign
of t^2 q-s<=0 since0<=x_i<=1. On the simplex of at most5 remaining lengths,
maximize F=(1+q)/(1+s)^2. On a face with t positive coordinates, interior
stationarity makes every coordinate solve x^3-a x=constant, where
a=(1+q)/(1+s). There are at most two positive roots x<y, and they satisfy
a=x^2+xy+y^2. Two coordinates of the larger value y give a positive second
derivative in their difference direction because3y^2>a, so a local maximum
with two values can have only one large coordinate. The others are l copies
of x, with lx+y=1. Along this family,
 sign F'(y)=sign[(y-x)(x+y-1)(x+y+1+xy)].
For l>=2 and x>0 this is strictly negative when y>x, hence such a point is
not stationary. For l=1, F=1/2 identically. Therefore the maximum on each
face occurs at equal positive coordinates (or its boundary). For t positives
its value is(t^2-t+1)/(t(t+1)), increasing for integer t>=2. At t=5 this is
7/10. This proves(5), including boundary/zero singular values.

For independent standard circular complex Gaussians g_j, unitary invariance
and E|g|^(2a)=a! give
 E|sum tau_j g_j^2|^2=2s,
 E|sum tau_j g_j^2|^4=8s^2+16q, s=sum tau_j^2,q=sum tau_j^4.
The normalized ratio is2+4q/s^2<=24/5 by(5).
Activated-phase fourth moments are no larger than Gaussian fourth moments:
expand P^2 into exponent fibers; all weights are positive |b_alpha|^2 and
each repeated-coordinate multiplier beta_l<=2. Thus the same24/5 bound
holds for p_A. Minkowski over A proves E Y^2<=(24/5)Z_2^2.

For complement degree2, each Q_A has the same rank-six structure. If the
2k-by-2m matrix V[A] has rank below2k, Q_A is zero. Otherwise its kernel has
dimension2(m-k)=4. Complementary Pluecker coordinates identify its full minors
with those of a rational kernel basis, up to one common nonzero scalar and
the complement sign. One concrete derivation chooses pivot columns, reduces
V[A] to[I,T] and takes kernel columns[-T;I]; Laplace expansion yields
det V[A,P(S)]=gamma(-1)^(sum P(S)) det N[P(S)^c,:].
For complete pair sets sum P(S)=k modulo2, independent of S, so the sign is
common. Q_A is therefore a four-dimensional pair-Pfaffian quadratic up to a
scalar. Rank<=6 and zero diagonal apply again. This proves the24/5 constant
also for m-k=2 without requiring any kernel basis as a supplied oracle.

6. EXACT SHARPNESS FAMILY AND FAILURE OF2^k
Use coordinates x_0,...,x_5 for a4-by-4 skew matrix with upper entries
 B01=x0+i x1, B23=x0-i x1, B02=x2+i x3,
 B13=-x2+i x3, B03=x4+i x5, B12=x4-i x5.
Its Pfaffian is sum_(j=0)^5 x_j^2. For each a=1,...,5 and sign s=+/-1,
take the simple form with x0=1,x_a=i s, others0. Its Pfaffian is zero,
so it has rank2. All entries are Gaussian integers. Acquire rational u,v
by choosing any nonzero pivot p=B_ij and taking u=B[:,i], v=B[:,j]/p;
rank2 implies and the code verifies B=u v^T-v u^T.
Take t copies of each of these ten pair labels. Between two labels the
degree-two determinant coefficient is4 for opposite signs on the same a,
2 for distinct a, and0 for identical a/sign. Direct second-moment sum gives
 Z_2=240t^2.
Under independent circular complex Gaussian phases the six coordinate
variances are(10t,2t,2t,2t,2t,2t), attaining polygon equality and ratio24/5.
For independent uniform fourth roots, exact exponent-fiber counting gives
 E X^2/Z_2^2=24/5-4/(5t)+1/(30t^2).                       (6)
For clarity, each coefficient-matrix row has squared sum48t. The Gaussian
minus phase fourth moment is2 sum_i(48t)^2-sum_(i<j)a_ij^4
=46080t^3-1920t^2, while the Gaussian fourth moment is(24/5)(240t^2)^2.
This proves(6), not a central-limit extrapolation.
At t=1 the ratio is121/30>4=2^2; at t=2 it is529/120. It tends to24/5,
proving sharpness of the uniform bound over rational finite phase inputs.
Sharpness also transfers to the low-complement mode itself: let W be the
4-by-2m full-row-rank matrix of these rational pair columns. Acquire a rational
basis for ker W, transpose it to a (2m-4)-by-2m full-row-rank matrix M, and
group M's columns into the original pairs. The kernel of M is the column
space of W^T. Complementary minors therefore identify the degree-two
complement polynomial for k=m-2 with gamma times the above Pfaffian polynomial,
for one nonzero rational-complex scalar gamma. Its mean and second moment
scale by |gamma|^2 and |gamma|^4, so(6) is unchanged. This construction is
acquired by rational elimination; no inverse counting primitive is supplied.

Independent direct sums of g such four-dimensional blocks, at total k=2g,
have moment ratio[24/5-4/(5t)+1/(30t^2)]^g. Therefore no uniform2^k or
polynomial-in-k second-moment upper bound holds for this arbitrary-pair
phase estimator. A raw sample mean of M draws has exact relative mean-square
error(ratio^g-1)/M, so bounded relative RMS demands exponential M.
This does not prove a fixed-confidence lower bound against every estimator,
alternative factoring or counting algorithm. These block counts are easy.

7. CAPPED APPROXIMATION, COST AND EXECUTION
Take batch size ceil(4C_r/eps^2); Chebyshev gives relative failure<=1/4.
An odd number>=8 ln(4/delta) of independent batches gives median failure
<=delta/4. The implementation uses only rational/integer ceil-log2 bounds.
Uniform rational draws accept each b-bit proposal with probability>=1/2.
There are at most m times total_sample_count such draw locations. Cap the
overall random-call budget at that count times(s+1), where
s>=ceil(log2(4m total_sample_count/delta))+1. Exceeding the cap implies some
draw took more than s proposals, with total probability<=delta/4. Abort
returns zero and is charged to failure. Thus every execution uses polynomial
random bits and arithmetic, and the overall relative failure is<delta.
Positive-instance zero output is not an exact-zero certificate; zero targets
always give zero because every polynomial coefficient is zero.

All radii, probabilities, reciprocal phases, Gram projections, trace powers,
Newton divisions and batch means are rational with polynomial input bits.
Uniform bit growth under averaging is also explicit: clear all input-vector
denominators with one integer D_v and all sampled-radius denominators with
one D_z. Direct samples are degree2k norm polynomials with denominator dividing
D_v^(4k) D_z^(2k). Complement samples are lambda_all times degree2q norm
polynomials, with denominator dividing D_v^(4k) D_z^(2q) times the product
of activity denominators. These integers have polynomial bit length, are
independent of the sampled zero/phase pattern, and need not be explicitly
formed by the code. Exact cancellation in(4) forces the final reduced sample
denominator to divide that common integer despite intermediate Gram inverses.
Batch means introduce only the batch-size denominator; intermediate sums
have at most polynomial input bits plus logarithmic sample-count growth.
The complement multiplier has O(m) factors; its tiny magnitude introduces no
inverse target norm. Per sample has O(k d^3+m d^2+polynomial(d,m)) field work,
including forced Gram elimination. Input denominators and determinant
bounds give polynomial bit cost. The only exponential factor is C_r.
