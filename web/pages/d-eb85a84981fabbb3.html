# A finite prime-capacity tester for exponential polynomials

7 October 2026. `rounding_prior`, verified GPT-6.1 Sol/max; no nested
workers. This is a complete conditional mathematical construction and a
costed local prototype. Historical novelty is **UNVERIFIED**. It is not a
solution of the arbitrary-finite-field sparse Descartes conjecture.

## Exact input and question

The fixed input is

    P(X) = sum_(j=1)^k f_j(X) exp(g_j(X)/h_j(X)),

where f_j,g_j,h_j are integer polynomials of total degree at most d;
h_j is nonzero; distinct exponent fractions have nonzero differences
D_jt=g_j h_t-g_t h_j. Zero f_j are permitted. An integer w>=2 bounds
every coefficient of f_j,h_j,D_jt. The descriptions need not be supplied:
the tester receives n,k,d,w and a *parameterized* exact oracle

    Eval(p,q,a,u,v) = sum_j f_j(u) a^[g_j(v)/h_j(v) mod q] mod p,

or undefined when a denominator vanishes. Here q,p are primes, q|p-1,
a has order q, u belongs to F_p^n and v to F_q^n. Thus oracle support
for changing characteristics is an explicit premise. A fixed-field-only
oracle cannot implement the prime-pool method. Program expansion,
condensation and certification of degree/height are not supplied for free.

Question: can one obtain constant one-query soundness using finitely
many acquired, verifiably distinct primes, without a prime-density
promise or choosing q numerically larger than w?

The recent primary anchor is Malavolta–Rosen, arXiv2606.08662v1,
7 June 2026. Their Theorem3.6 and Theorem4.3 (PDF pp10,14–15)
use a fixed integer coefficient witness and a resultant. Their test
samples p uniformly from all p<=q^4 with p=1 mod q, for a q meeting
Eq7 and q>2kw. Lemma3.8 (pp11–12) proves suitable q exist using
Bombieri–Vinogradov. The following refinement keeps multiplicities
of modular roots instead of charging each bad prime once.

## Lemma 1: determinant divisibility from modular nullity

Let A be an m by m integer matrix with nonzero determinant. If its
nullity modulo a prime p is r, then p^r divides det A.

Proof without needing to compute Smith form: choose m-r rows that span
the row space modulo p. A permutation puts those rows first. Every
remaining row is a linear combination modulo p of these first rows.
Lift the coefficients to integers and subtract that combination.
These are determinant-preserving integer row operations. Each of the
r remaining rows is now entrywise divisible by p. Multilinearity of
the determinant proves the claim. It also covers r=m.

## Lemma 2: a root-weighted cyclotomic norm budget

Fix a prime q. Let C in Z[z], deg C<=q-1, C nonzero, with fewer than q
nonzero coefficients and ||C||_1<=W. Put

    Phi_q(z)=1+z+...+z^(q-1),   R=Res(Phi_q,C).

The irreducibility of Phi_q and the degree/support assumptions show R
is a nonzero integer: divisibility by Phi_q would force C to be a
nonzero constant multiple of Phi_q, with all q coefficients nonzero.
For a primitive complex q-th root omega,

    |R| = product_(t=1)^(q-1) |C(omega^t)| <= W^(q-1).       (1)

For a prime p=1 mod q, let r_p be the number of primitive q-th roots
in F_p at which C is zero. Multiplication by C on the free integer
module Z[z]/(Phi_q), in the basis 1,z,...,z^(q-2), has determinant R.
Modulo p, the Chinese remainder isomorphism diagonalizes this map
with eigenvalues C(a), one for each of the q-1 distinct primitive
roots a. Its nullity is precisely r_p. Lemma1 gives

    p^(r_p) | R.

Consequently, for any finite set S of *distinct* primes p=1 mod q,

    product_(p in S) p^(r_p) <= |R| <= W^(q-1),
    sum_(p in S) r_p log2 p <= (q-1) log2 W.                (2)

No prime-density theorem, exceptional-prime list or computation of R
is needed by the tester. R is only a soundness witness in the proof.

For a fully integral sampler, set ell_p=floor(log2 p),
T_p=sum_(p in S) ell_p, and b_W=ceil(log2 W). Choose p with probability
ell_p/T_p, then a uniformly among the q-1 nonidentity q-th roots.
Since q is prime these are exactly the primitive roots. Equation2 gives

    Pr[C(a)=0 mod p] <= b_W/T_p.                            (3)

The equivalent uniform-p bound is log W/(|S| log p_min).
Sampling the identity root is unnecessary and would add its possible
failure mass. Equation3 is valid even when all coefficients disappear
modulo some p: that prime then has r_p=q-1 and is charged accordingly.

## Lemma 3: a finite capacity budget for bad inner characteristics

Let Q be a finite set of distinct primes, ell_q=floor(log2 q), and
T_q=sum ell_q. Sample q with probability ell_q/T_q. For any *fixed*
nonzero integer c with |c|<=w, the prime divisors among Q satisfy

    sum_(q|c, q in Q) ell_q <= log2|c| <= b_w=ceil(log2 w).

For each of the L=k+binom(k,2) nonzero polynomials h_j,D_jt, choose
one nonzero integer coefficient, before sampling q. If the whole
polynomial becomes zero modulo q, that chosen coefficient is divisible
by q. The union bound therefore gives

    Pr[some h_j or D_jt annihilates mod q] <= L b_w/T_q.     (4)

This witness is not acquired or evaluated by the tester. It only
establishes soundness, using the promised integer height. The argument
does not require q>w. A public prime pool still needs the stated fixed
input and valid height promise; capacity cannot be asserted for an
unbounded or p/q-adaptive input.

## Theorem: two finite pools, one algebraic query

Assume every q in Q is prime and q>k. For each such q, acquire a
finite set S_q of distinct certified primes p=1 mod q, with capacity
T_p(q)>0, and a certified order-q element gamma_p. These pools may be
constructed lazily *after q is sampled*, provided the procedure depends
only on q and the promised parameters and satisfies the stated capacity.
Put q_min=min Q, p_min=min over the used S_q, and T_p,min=min_q T_p(q).

Draw q with weight ell_q, p with weight ell_p, v uniformly in F_q^n,
u uniformly in F_p^n, and r uniformly in {1,...,q-1}; a=gamma_p^r.
Accept the zero hypothesis iff Eval is zero or undefined. For a fixed
nonzero input,

    Pr[accept] <= L b_w/T_q + d k^2/q_min
                  + d/p_min + b_(kw)/T_p,min.               (5)

Proof. Outside the bad-characteristic event of Eq4, h_j and D_jt
are nonzero modulo q. Schwartz–Zippel at random v plus the union
bound costs

    [k d + 2d binom(k,2)]/q = d k^2/q.

On the good event, the residues alpha_j=g_j(v)/h_j(v) are distinct.
Since P is nonzero, at least one f_j is a nonzero integer polynomial.
Choose one fixed monomial X^mu with a nonzero coefficient vector

    eta_j = [X^mu] f_j,   sum |eta_j| <= kw.

For this *coefficient extraction*, no f_j(u), g_j(v) or h_j(v) integer
value is used. Place eta_j at exponent alpha_j to make C_v(z).
It is nonzero, has degree<=q-1 and support<=k<q; its integer norm
is <=kw independently of the size of v. C_v depends on q,v but never
on the sampled p. If

    H_(a,v)(X)=sum_j f_j(X) a^alpha_j

is the zero polynomial over F_p, its X^mu coefficient C_v(a) is zero.
Lemma2 bounds this event by b_(kw)/T_p(q). Otherwise H has degree<=d
and random u costs at most d/p. Summing the four events gives Eq5.
This conditioning remains valid if some f_j coefficients vanish mod p.

Perfect completeness follows from the integer exponential-polynomial
zero characterization: for distinct rational exponent fractions, a
real identity forces every f_j to be zero. One direct verification is
to choose a rational point avoiding all h_j,D_jt and a chosen nonzero
f_j; such a point exists by polynomial nonvanishing. The distinct
rational exponents at that point give linearly independent exponentials
over algebraic numbers by Lindemann–Weierstrass, a contradiction.
Hence a genuine zero input gives zero at every defined algebraic query.
Undefined queries are accepted. The primary prerequisite is
Li–Wu ITCS2026, Theorem13 and Lemma23; no numerical real-zero oracle
is substituted for Eval.

A convenient explicit admission is

    q_min > max{k, 6d(k^2+1)},
    T_q >= 6 L b_w,      each T_p(q) >= 6 b_(kw).

Because p>q, the two degree terms sum to less than 1/6. The two
capacity terms are at most 1/6 each. Thus one-query error is <1/2.
Independent repetition with the same certified pools, for a fixed
input and fresh samples, reduces false acceptance to <2^(-t).

If one instead retains q>max{k,2kw,6d(k^2+1)}, any three distinct
p=1 mod q already give a useful constant bound: every ell_p is
at least b_(kw), so root error<=1/3, degree error<1/6. This one-level
corollary needs no weighted-q pool, but keeps the large-height q cost.

## Costs and boundary of the result

The prototype uses deterministic trial-division primality certification,
counts every rejected progression candidate/divisor attempt, and constructs
gamma by trying h=2,3,..., computing h^((p-1)/q) mod p and rejecting
the value1. Both primality and order are independently replayable.
This is deliberately simple. The separate `DETERMINISTIC_ACQUISITION.md`
now proves polynomial overhead in numerical d,k and log w using an
elementary ordinary-prime bound and the effective uniform Linnik theorem.
Its CRT construction supplies enough distinct progression primes without
an unverified many-prime density assumption. It does not prove polynomial
time in log q or polynomial time in the size of an arbitrary succinct
circuit. The local report charges actual observed acquisition; its timings
are not the very loose Linnik bound. The theorem's absolute effective
constant is not numerically instantiated here.

For already-certified pools of M_q primes and a used p-pool of M_p primes,
let B=max bit length of q,p,w. Storage for pool descriptions is
O((M_q+M_p)B) bits, in addition to the input and oracle. Exact weighted
sampling costs the table preparation plus O(log T_q+log T_p) random bits
per rejection trial. Uniform field sampling costs O(n(log q+log p))
random bits in expectation, r costs O(log q), and exponentiation a=gamma^r
costs O(log q) modular multiplications. A full query explicitly includes
p,q,a,u,v: with B_p=bitlen(p), B_q=bitlen(q), its integer-argument
payload is at most (n+2)B_p+(n+1)B_q bits before framing, and it returns
O(log p) bits or undefined. The frozen toy's original 72-bit counter
omitted p; `COST_AND_CORRECTIONS.md` preserves and corrects it to95bits.
The Eval cost must be added. A supplied circuit with s scalar operations
can be evaluated with polynomial-bit modular arithmetic; coefficient
reads/reductions and the s operations remain charged. The tester alone
does not make a large circuit a constant-time computation.

The two-level construction changes the required *numerical* q threshold
from height w to degree/width. It replaces this by total certified-prime
capacity linear in log w. This can reduce field widths when w is large.
It is not yet a runtime advantage: acquiring and switching many fields
has real cost, and compiled GPU support for this field family is not
implemented here. Without supplied k,d,w or a method to certify them
from a given circuit, no polynomial-in-circuit-size theorem follows.

## Strongest controls, prior and novelty

Li–Wu ITCS2026 §§3,5 supplies the exact algebraic model and a fixed-field
sparse-root bound (Theorem24), for a nonzero modular polynomial with
k>=2, with the simplified tester's error q^(-1/(k-1))+O(dk^2/q).
The printed display omits those premises; a monomial has its direct
zero-root bound and the zero polynomial cannot obey that displayed
formula. This wording omission does not invalidate the method under
its intended nonzero premise. Our norm budget directly charges full
modular coefficient disappearance. The June primary supplies stronger randomized-p
soundness under its height/good-q assumptions. Those are substantive
controls. The norm product, determinant-nullity divisibility and modular
integer-height arguments are standard mathematics; only this composed
finite-capacity interface has been independently reconstructed here.
No historical novelty follows from the absence of these exact formulas
in the inspected recent paper. The fixed-prime conjecture for arbitrary
Fp coefficients remains open in this investigation: its coefficients
need not share a bounded integer lift across primes.

Concrete downstream interface: exact probabilistic equivalence testing
of integer-coefficient, one-exponential-layer tensor-program fragments,
as in Mirage's LAX verifier. This construction can supply a finite
auditable field family once width/degree/height and parameterized evaluation
are genuinely available. It does not certify floating-point equivalence
or replace source-specific control of rational denominators/circuit expansion.
