## Interface and first variation

This audit analyzes latent landscape parameters, not certificate acquisition. W is real orthogonal and G real symmetric; define

    M=W entrywise squared, F_G(W)=tr(G M M^T),
    H=GM, E=W entrywise multiplied by H, S=W^T E.

For a skew A, write V=WA, Z=WA^2. Along W exp(tA),

    D=M'=2W circ V,
    D2=M''=2V circ V+2W circ Z,
    F'=4 tr(S^T A),
    F''=tr[G(2DD^T+M D2^T+D2 M^T)].                 (a)

Hence stationarity on the full orthogonal group is exactly S=S^T. In particular coordinate-plane tests alone need not suffice. Dividing (a) by4 gives

    F''/4=2 sum_l (W_l circ V_l)^T G(W_l circ V_l)
           +sum_kl H_kl V_kl^2+tr(S A^2).           (b)

The last trace can use S instead of S^T because A^2 is symmetric; this does NOT assume stationarity.

## Universal weighted pivot with residual exposed

Fix (i,j), set e=e_i, f=e_j, x=W^T e, u=W f, q=W_ij, p=q^2,

    m=M[:,j], c=MM^T e, z_l=W[:,l] circ u,
    A=x f^T−f x^T.

The unnormalized pivot satisfies

    V=e f^T−u x^T,
    A^2=−xx^T−ff^T+q(xf^T+fx^T).

Evaluate the three terms in (b) separately:

1. Since W_l circ V_l=delta_lj q e−x_l z_l,

       2 sum_l (...)^T G(...)
          =2p G_ii−4p H_ij+2 sum_l M_il z_l^T G z_l.

2. Squaring V entrywise yields

       sum_kl H_kl V_kl^2=(1−2p)H_ij+c^T Gm.

3. Direct matrix products give

       x^T S x=e^T Gc, f^T S f=m^T Gm,
       x^T S f=q H_ij,
       f^T S x=q H_ij+f^T(S−S^T)x.

   Therefore

       tr(S A^2)=−e^T Gc−m^T Gm+2p H_ij
                    +q f^T(S−S^T)x.

Combining all three terms proves the root identity exactly:

    F''/4=tr(G K_ij)+q f^T(S−S^T)x,

    K_ij=sym[c(m−e)^T]+2 sum_l M_il z_l z_l^T
            +2p ee^T+(1−4p)sym(e m^T)−mm^T.

Here sym(B)=(B+B^T)/2. If p<1, divide A by sqrt(1−p); second variation and residual both divide by (1−p). At a stationary W the residual is zero. It must not be discarded at a generic inverse-search fixture.

Own exact witness to the false unconditional residual-free formula:

    W=[[6/7,−2/7,−3/7], [−2/7,3/7,−6/7], [−3/7,−6/7,−2/7]],
    G=diag(1,2,3), (i,j)=(0,1).

Direct F''/4 is −3681/16807, while tr(GK) is −4941/16807. The missing residual is 180/2401; their sum is exact. This is one decisive counterexample to the omitted-term extension, not a landscape counterexample (the point is not stationary).

## Contrast gauge and K1=0

M is doubly stochastic. For any real a,

    F_(G+1a^T+a1^T)(W)=F_G(W)+2 1^T a.

Set P=I−11^T/n. The difference G−PGP is a gauge of that form, explicitly with a=G1/n−(1^TG1)1/(2n^2). Thus only PGP matters. A symmetric centered metric positive definite on 1-perp can be extended to ambient PD by adding a positive multiple of 11^T; positivity on the gauge direction has no scientific content.

Directly check K1, without relying on stationarity. The five contributions in its definition are respectively

    (m−e)/2,  2p m,  2p e,  (1−4p)(e+m)/2,  −m.

They sum to zero. This uses c^T1=m^T1=1 and z_l^T1=delta_lj. Consequently tr(GK)=tr(PGP K). The residual separately vanishes for gauge weights: their stationarity matrix is symmetric (alternatively their objective is constant, and K kills1). All curvature search can therefore be formulated on the contrast space.

## Independent uniform-reflection aggregate proof

Let n>=3, W=I−2J/n, J=11^T, and

    A_i=(2/n)(e_i1^T−1e_i^T).

This is exactly the diagonal-entry pivot above. We reconstruct the aggregate without the source's unweighted pivot numerator. Put tau=2/n, a=1−tau. Direct multiplication gives

    (WA_i)_kl=tau(delta_ki+delta_li−tau),
    (WA_i^2)_kl=tau^2[delta_ki+delta_li
                       −n delta_ki delta_li+(1−tau)].

For G=I, the objective is the sum of entrywise fourth powers, and

    F''_I= sum_kl [12W_kl^2 V_kl^2+4W_kl^3 Z_kl].

The four entry categories (count; W,V,Z) are:

- (i,i): 1; a, tau(2−tau), tau^2(3−n−tau).
- Offdiagonal incident to i: 2(n−1); −tau, tau(1−tau), tau^2(2−tau).
- Other diagonal: n−1; a, −tau^2, tau^2(1−tau).
- Other offdiagonal: (n−1)(n−2); −tau, −tau^2, tau^2(1−tau).

Substitution and elementary scalar simplification give universally

    F''_I(W;A_i)=64(n−1)(3n−8)/n^4.                 (c)

For each i let B_i=2D_iD_i^T+M D2_i^T+D2_i M^T from (a). Every B_i kills1: the gauge argument implies tr[(1a^T+a1^T)B_i]=0 for all a, equivalently B_i1=0. Simultaneous coordinate permutations fix W and permute A_i, so B=sum_i B_i is permutation-invariant. The only such symmetric matrices are alpha I+beta J. Hence B=cP. Equation(c) determines its trace and proves

    sum_i F''_G(W;A_i)=64(3n−8)/n^3 tr(PG).        (d)

No stationarity hypothesis is used in (d). It is positive for every G positive definite on the contrast space, so this W cannot be a local maximum. The coefficient does not describe arbitrary Householder v; only the uniform reflection and its signed/permuted equivalents are excluded here. At n=2 it is a signed permutation and has negative aggregate curvature, so n>=3 is essential.

A useful quantitative deduction: ||A_i||_F^2=8(n−1)/n^2. Thus at least one normalized direction satisfies

    F''_G(W;A_i)/||A_i||_F^2
       >=8(3n−8)tr(PG)/[n^2(n−1)].                (e)

If PGP>=mu P with mu>0 on contrast, this is at least 8(3n−8)mu/n^2. This is a curvature margin on this structured family, not a global convergence or exact synthesis cost bound.

## Universal generic-fixed-W search obstruction

Let V={G0 symmetric:G0 1=0}, with dimension d=n(n−1)/2, and define

    Phi_W(G0)=(S_ij−S_ji)_(i<j).

It is a linear map between two d-dimensional spaces. Choose any fixed bases. Its entries are homogeneous degree-four polynomials in W (since S=W^T[W circ(G0 W^{circ2})]).

Choose a skew A with all offdiagonal entries nonzero and an analytic orthogonal curve W(t)=I+tA+O(t^2). Then M(t)=I+O(t^2). For i!=j,

    S_ij=t A_ij(G0_ij−G0_jj)+O(t^2),
    Phi_W(t)(G0)_ij
       =−t A_ij(G0_ii+G0_jj−2G0_ij)+O(t^2).        (f)

The distance map G0 -> D, D_ii=0 and D_ij=G0_ii+G0_jj−2G0_ij, is an isomorphism between centered symmetric matrices and hollow symmetric matrices. Indeed P D P=−2G0, proving injectivity; both spaces have dimension d. Therefore the leading determinant in (f) is a nonzero constant times t^d product_(i<j) A_ij. It is not identically zero on SO(n). Signed column reflection transports rank invertibly to the other component: for a diagonal sign matrix R, M(WR)=M(W), S(WR)=R S(W) R, so every skew coordinate is multiplied by a nonzero sign. Thus nonvanishing holds on both components.

A continuously sampled W, whose law is absolutely continuous with respect to orthogonal-manifold volume, almost surely has invertible Phi_W. No nonzero centered G0 then makes W stationary; in particular no positive contrast metric does. Relevant positive-weight stationary points lie within a proper algebraic singular locus. Being in that locus is necessary, not sufficient for positive G. Structured distributions supported on it are exempt. Uniform reflections and Hadamard fixtures illustrate why arbitrary random distributions, discrete grids and all Householder vectors cannot be covered by the Haar claim.

## Quantitative rational-grid strengthening

For an exact rational inverse search draw the d independent entries of a real skew matrix A from a grid of size K and set W=(I−A)(I+A)^{-1}. I+A is nonsingular for EVERY real skew A: Av=−v would give v^TAv=−||v||^2 although v^TAv=0. Its determinant cannot vanish on a real grid.

Write W=N(A)/D(A), with N=(I−A)adj(I+A), D=det(I+A). Every numerator entry has total degree at most n. Each entry of Phi has denominator D^4 and numerator degree at most4n. After multiplying its determinant by D^(4d), it is a polynomial of degree at most4nd. The near-identity construction above shows that this polynomial is nonzero on the Cayley chart. Schwartz–Zippel therefore proves

    Pr[some positive contrast metric makes this W stationary]
       <=Pr[det Phi_W=0]<=min(1,4nd/K).            (g)

This charges the finite-grid distribution and gives a rigorous bound where “generic” alone would not. A fixed signed column reflection gives the same bound in the other component. The degree bound is conservative. It is not a useful constant when K<=4nd, and is not a statement about algorithms sampling a structured stationary family or adjusting W jointly with G.

## Scientific consequence and remaining proof gate

A generic fixed-W then stationary-G SDP wastes its inverse-search samples: continuous sampling almost surely produces only the constant-objective gauge kernel, and a large rational grid has the explicit coverage upper bound(g). An SDP infeasibility verdict at such a frame cannot support the weighted no-trap conjecture. Searching structured exact stationary loci, joint W/G systems, or a universal dual curvature certificate is meaningful; retaining constant gauge directions is not.

The universal weighted landscape remains UNPROVED. Our pivot formula supplies a legal expression, not a positivity proof: mixed G terms cannot be replaced by the unweighted scalar argument. Uniform reflections are eliminated, not all stationary families. Even a qualitative all-stationary ascent theorem would require an additional quantitative precision/rational-reconstruction proof to yield a coefficient-only polynomial-bit certificate-acquisition algorithm.
