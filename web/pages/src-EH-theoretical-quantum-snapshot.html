1. The obstruction selected
Imaginary-time reconstruction is already known to be ill-conditioned. Exact Euclidean information and finite noisy Euclidean information have very different predictive power; existing work also develops rigorous, positivity-constrained bounds on what real-time quantities are compatible with the available data. Merely rediscovering that analytic continuation can be unstable would not constitute an advance. arXiv
The sharper question I pursued was:
Can strong physical simplicity, together with access to the entire imaginary-time quantum state, remove that instability?
The result gives a negative answer under a precise fixed-input observation model. It also identifies an explicit intervention that defeats the constructed obstruction, preventing an unjustified extension to arbitrary experimental access.
2. Main theorem: an order-optimal prediction law
Let \(A\) be an unknown finite-dimensional Hermitian matrix satisfying
\[
I\preceq A\preceq 2I.
\]
Let \(v\) be a known unit vector, and let \(\Omega\) be a known reference state orthogonal to the space on which \(A\) acts.
For any chosen imaginary time \(0\le \tau\le\beta\), suppose a source returns a fresh copy of
\[
|\Psi_A(\tau)\rangle
=
\frac{|\Omega\rangle+e^{-\tau A/2}|v\rangle}
{\sqrt{1+F_A(\tau)}},
\qquad
F_A(z)=\langle v|e^{-zA}|v\rangle.
\]
This is normalized imaginary-time evolution under \(H=0\oplus A\), starting from \((\Omega+v)/\sqrt2\). It is not a Gibbs state.
The task is to estimate
\[
g_A(t)=\langle v|e^{-itA}|v\rangle
\]
to additive error \(1/4\), with success probability at least \(2/3\).
The learner may choose imaginary times adaptively, retain quantum memory, and perform arbitrary collective quantum measurements. Classical computation is unrestricted. However, the source uses the same fixed input, and does not also reveal \(A\) or provide arbitrary-input access to its propagator.
Let \(Q(t,\beta)\) denote the worst-case number of snapshots required, uniformly over the allowed matrices and dimensions.
Theorem
For sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
\boxed{
\log Q(t,\beta)
=
\Theta\!\left(t\log(2+t/\beta)\right),
}
\]
with universal implicit constants and \(t/\infty=0\).
Thus, for any fixed positive observation window \(\beta\),
\[
\boxed{Q(t,\beta)=\exp\!\bigl(\Theta_\beta(t\log t)\bigr).}
\]
Even with access to every imaginary time,
\[
\boxed{Q(t,\infty)=\exp\!\bigl(\Theta(t)\bigr).}
\]
Equivalently, the worst-case prediction horizon supported by \(N\) snapshots grows only as
\[
t_{\max}(N)=
\begin{cases}
\Theta_\beta(\log N/\log\log N), & \beta\text{ fixed},\\[2mm]
\Theta(\log N), & \text{all imaginary times available}.
\end{cases}
\]
“Matching” here means matching logarithmic order, not equality of the leading constants in the exponent.
The lower bound already holds for bounded-coupling, nearest-neighbor spin chains with a constant gap, a unique product ground state, and trajectory states of matrix-product bond dimension at most two.
3. Construction: one hidden boundary parameter
Take an odd integer \(n=2m-1\). Define two \(m\times m\) tridiagonal matrices \(A_+\) and \(A_-\) with identical off-diagonal entries
\[
(A_\pm)_{j,j+1}
=
-\frac{\sqrt{(j+1)(n-j)}}{2n},
\qquad 0\le j<m-1.
\]
Their diagonal entries are \(3/2\), except at the final site:
\[
(A_\pm)_{m-1,m-1}
=
\frac32\mp\frac{m}{2n}.
\]
The two alternatives differ in exactly one onsite coefficient. Use the first-site seed \(v=e_0\).
These matrices arise by folding a reflection-symmetric spin chain into its even and odd sectors. The underlying engineered transport mechanism belongs to the established perfect-state-transfer construction, rather than being a newly invented physical interaction. arXiv
The endpoint spectral measures are exactly
\[
\mu_+
=
2^{1-n}\sum_{k\ {\rm even}}
\binom nk\,\delta_{1+k/n},
\qquad
\mu_-
=
2^{1-n}\sum_{k\ {\rm odd}}
\binom nk\,\delta_{1+k/n}.
\]
Both spectra therefore lie in \([1,2]\).
The binomial theorem gives the imaginary-time difference
\[
\boxed{
\Delta(\tau):=F_+(\tau)-F_-(\tau)
=
2e^{-\tau}
\left(\frac{1-e^{-\tau/n}}2\right)^n.
}
\]
In contrast, at real time \(t_n=\pi n\),
\[
e^{-it_nA_\pm}=\pm e^{-it_n}I.
\]
Since \(n\) is odd,
\[
\boxed{g_+(t_n)=-1,\qquad g_-(t_n)=1.}
\]
The later real-time answers are maximally separated.
Yet, writing \(z=e^{-\tau/n}\),
\[
\Delta(\tau)=2[z(1-z)/2]^n,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{\sup_{\tau\ge0}\Delta(\tau)=2\,8^{-n}.}
\]
For a finite observation window,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le\tau\le\beta}\Delta(\tau)
\le 2\left(\frac{\beta}{2n}\right)^n.
}
\]
These scalar formulas suggest the obstruction. They do not, by themselves, prove a lower bound for access to complete quantum states. That requires another argument.
The full-state step
Set
\[
y=e^{-\tau/(2n)},\qquad
\alpha=\frac{1+y}{2},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{1-y}{2},
\]
and define
\[
f_j=e^{-\tau/2}\sqrt{\binom nj}\,
\alpha^{n-j}\gamma^j.
\]
The two filtered vectors in the folded chain have coordinates
\[
(e^{-\tau A_\pm/2}v)_j=f_j\pm f_{n-j}.
\]
Now put
\[
S=\sum_{j<m}f_j^2,\qquad
T=\sum_{j\ge m}f_j^2,\qquad
U=\sum_{j<m}f_jf_{n-j}.
\]
The construction implies
\[
T\le U,\qquad U^2\le ST,\qquad S+T\le1,\qquad \Delta=4U.
\]
For trace distance \(D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1\), direct calculation gives
\[
D(\Psi_+,\Psi_-)^2
=
\frac{4[T(1+S)-U^2]}
{(1+S+T)^2-4U^2}.
\]
The numerator is at least \(4T\), while the denominator is at most four. Conversely, the numerator is at most \(4T(1+S)\), while the denominator is at least \(1+S\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
T\le D(\Psi_+,\Psi_-)^2\le4T\le\Delta(\tau).
}
\]
This is the crucial strengthening: the entire quantum snapshots are nearly indistinguishable, not merely their selected expectation values.
4. Why adaptive collective measurements cannot evade the bound
The needed information lemma is the following.
Lemma. Suppose two classically indexed pure-state sources have trace distance at most \(d\) at every allowed query. Any protocol using at most \(N\) fresh copies, adaptive classical queries, and arbitrary quantum processing has final trace distance at most
\[
D_{\rm final}\le\sqrt N\,d.
\]
To prove it, retain the classical query history and use root fidelity. In each history block, appending a source state multiplies fidelity by at least \(\sqrt{1-d^2}\). Intervening quantum processing cannot decrease fidelity. Consequently,
\[
\mathcal F_{\rm final}\ge(1-d^2)^{N/2},
\]
and therefore
\[
D_{\rm final}^2
\le1-\mathcal F_{\rm final}^2
\le1-(1-d^2)^N
\le Nd^2.
\]
The fidelity and distinguishability ingredients are standard quantum-information mathematics; they are not claimed as new. arXiv
An estimator distinguishing the target values \(-1\) and \(1\) with success probability \(2/3\) must produce final trace distance at least \(1/3\). Combining the lemma with the full-state bound yields
\[
\boxed{
N\ge
\frac1{18}
\max\left\{
8^n,\,
\left(\frac{2n}{\beta}\right)^n
\right\}.
}
\]
This permits arbitrary collective measurements and adaptive choices of imaginary time.
The construction also covers every sufficiently large target time, rather than only \(t=\pi n\). Choose the largest odd \(n\le t/(2\pi)\) and rescale
\[
A_\pm\longmapsto I+W(A_\pm-I),
\qquad W=\frac{\pi n}{t}\in[1/4,1/2].
\]
The spectra remain inside \([1,2]\), the real-time separation occurs at exactly \(t\), and the same estimates give
\[
\log Q(t,\beta)
=
\Omega\!\left(t\log(2+t/\beta)\right).
\]
5. The matching upper bound
The upper bound is important: otherwise this would only be an unusually bad example, rather than a characterization of the worst-case prediction scale.
For every allowed \(A\), the spectral theorem gives
\[
F_A(z)=\int_1^2e^{-\omega z}\,d\mu_A(\omega),
\]
where \(\mu_A\) is a probability measure. Thus
\[
|F_A^{(k)}(z)|\le2^k
\qquad\text{when }\operatorname{Re}z\ge0.
\]
Set
\[
b=\min\{\beta,t\},\qquad
R=\sqrt{t^2+b^2},\qquad
k=\lceil4eR\rceil.
\]
Interpolate \(F_A\) at the real nodes \(\tau_j=jb/k\), using Lagrange polynomials \(\ell_j\).
The complex divided-difference remainder formula, applied in the closed right half-plane, gives
\[
\left|
F_A(it)-\sum_{j=0}^k\ell_j(it)F_A(\tau_j)
\right|
\le
\frac{(2R)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}
\le2^{-(k+1)}.
\]
The amplification of errors in the real-node data satisfies
\[
\Lambda:=\sum_{j=0}^k|\ell_j(it)|
\le
\left(\frac{2eR}{b}\right)^k.
\]
To obtain those data, measure the reference projector:
\[
p_A(\tau)
=
\Pr(\Omega\mid\Psi_A(\tau))
=
\frac1{1+F_A(\tau)}
\in[1/2,1].
\]
The inverse transformation \(F=1/p-1\) is 4-Lipschitz on this interval. Therefore ordinary Bernoulli estimation recovers each \(F_A(\tau_j)\) to accuracy \(O(1/\Lambda)\), using \(O(\Lambda^2\log k)\) copies per node.
The resulting total budget is
\[
N=O(k\Lambda^2\log k),
\]
which implies
\[
\log N
=
O\!\left(t\log(2+t/\beta)\right).
\]
This closes the upper side of the theorem.
It is a constructive but exponentially costly estimator, not an efficient new algorithm. Its value here is establishing that the lower-bound exponent has the correct order.
For the explicit binary family, the full note also proves sharper asymptotics:
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm pair}(n,\infty)=\Theta(\sqrt n\,8^n)
}
\]
and, for fixed \(\beta>0\),
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm pair}(n,\beta)
=
\Theta_\beta\!\left(
n^{3/2}\left(\frac{2n}{\beta}\right)^n
\right).
}
\]
Those sharper constants and polynomial factors are specific to the constructed pair.
6. Physical realization and finite precision
The matrices are one-excitation restrictions of the spin Hamiltonians
\[
H_\pm
=
\sum_j d_j^\pm n_j
-
\sum_j J_j
\left(
\sigma_j^+\sigma_{j+1}^-
+
\sigma_j^-\sigma_{j+1}^+
\right).
\]
All interactions are nearest-neighbor and uniformly bounded. Off-diagonal entries in the computational basis are nonpositive, so the Hamiltonians are stoquastic.
Their fermionic representation shows that many-particle energies are sums of the positive one-particle energies. Consequently, the vacuum is the unique ground state and the many-body gap is at least one.
The initial state is simply
\[
|+\rangle_0|0\rangle_1\cdots|0\rangle_{m-1}.
\]
Every subsequent state lies in vacuum plus one excitation. Across any cut, such a vector is a sum of at most two product vectors. Thus the trajectory has exact Schmidt rank at most two.
At \(t_n=\pi n\), a first-spin \(X\) measurement gives opposite deterministic outcomes under the two hypotheses. A coherent real-time experiment therefore distinguishes them in evolution time \(O(n)\), while the specified imaginary-time source requires exponentially or superexponentially many copies.
This separation is not caused by a large entanglement representation.
Exponential calibration precision is unnecessary
The construction survives matched rational rounding.
Take a slightly narrower band, using \(a=5/4\) and width \(W=1/2\), and round common bulk coefficients identically in both alternatives. If
\[
\|\widetilde A_\pm-A_\pm\|=O(1/n),
\]
then Duhamel’s formula gives
\[
\|e^{-it_n\widetilde A_\pm}-e^{-it_nA_\pm}\|
\le
t_n\|\widetilde A_\pm-A_\pm\|.
\]
Choosing the constant sufficiently small preserves a constant real-time separation.
Because the matrices are tridiagonal, this requires only \(O(\log n)\) bits per coefficient. Moreover, the rounded alternatives still satisfy
\[
\widetilde A_+^jv=\widetilde A_-^jv
\qquad (j<m).
\]
Taylor approximation gives short-window indistinguishability; polynomial approximation on a fixed complex ellipse gives an exponentially small bound uniform over all imaginary times. Together they preserve the main exponential-order separation.
This is inverse-polynomial calibration tolerance, not constant-noise robustness. It does not establish survival under fixed-strength independent disorder or nonzero decoherence over arbitrarily long evolution.
Also, \([1,2]\) is the fixed active one-excitation band. The complete many-body bandwidth is extensive.
7. A decisive limit: an extra local probe defeats the example
The fixed-input restriction is essential.
Suppose the learner may also request snapshots whose excitation starts at the last site, \(w=e_{m-1}\). Then
\[
F_\pm^{(w)}(\tau)
=
\langle w|e^{-\tau A_\pm}|w\rangle.
\]
The differing onsite coefficient is now visible at first order. Since its difference is \(m/n\ge1/2\), Taylor expansion gives
\[
F_+^{(w)}(\tau)-F_-^{(w)}(\tau)
\ge
\frac{\tau}{2}-4\tau^2e^{2\tau}
\ge\frac{\tau}{4},
\qquad 0<\tau\le\frac1{64}.
\]
The corresponding reference-measurement probabilities differ by at least \(\tau/16\).
For any fixed positive observation window, this identifies the boundary bit with a number of samples independent of chain length.
Therefore:
The theorem is an obstruction to prediction from a specified observation interface, not an obstruction to arbitrary experimentation on the system.

There are further substantive boundaries. Supplying the Hamiltonian coefficients removes the inference problem; these free chains can then be diagonalized. Gibbs-state access and arbitrary-input imaginary-time propagators are different models. Coherent state-preparation access also requires a separate argument: the note supplies a worst-case coherent-oracle corollary preserving exponential order, but the stronger copy-count formulas do not transfer unchanged.
8. What is established—and what is not
The literature already contains both the perfect-transfer mechanism and related high-order imaginary-time kernels. In particular, Silva and coauthors’ imaginary-time no-fast-forwarding theorem concerns implementing imaginary-time evolution from Hamiltonian block-encoding queries. That is a different access direction from predicting real-time behavior from supplied imaginary-time states, but the relationship is close enough that the kernel itself must not be claimed as original. Nature
Likewise, positivity-based Euclidean reconstruction already has rigorous information-theoretic formulations. The result here does not invalidate those methods; it establishes a worst-case sample law under a stronger quantum observation model and a compact active energy band. arXiv
The potentially distinct mathematical contribution is the combination of:
- a dimension-uniform minimax law for complete quantum snapshots, including adaptive collective measurements;
- a one-boundary-parameter, gapped, low-entanglement local realization;
- matching exponential-order upper bounds, sharper binary-family asymptotics, and a finite-bit construction.
