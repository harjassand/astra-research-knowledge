## Primitive divisibility lemma: no hidden higher fibre terms

Work temporarily over K=C(x,y) only to solve differential identities in
the two fibre variables. The polynomial q is primitive over C[x,y] and
irreducible in C[x,y,rho,eta]. If a polynomial P is divisible by q^k over
K[rho,eta], it is divisible by q^k over C[x,y,rho,eta]: localizing at base
polynomials cannot destroy a factor q, which divides no nonzero base
polynomial. This is the needed Gauss/UFD descent, not coordinate legality.

For a fibre-homogeneous P of degree m, delta is a nonzero constant
coefficient fibre derivation over K. Choosing a second independent fibre
form over K shows:

* If delta^2 P=0, then q^(m-1) divides P for m>=1.
* If delta^2 P is a scalar multiple of q^(m-2), then q^(m-2) divides P
  for m>=2, including the two homogeneous integration constants.

Both conclusions descend by primitiveness. A polynomial divisible by
q^k and of fibre degree m has total degree at least m+k, since q has
one base and one fibre degree. These facts will remove all omitted
higher fibre pieces, not just a selected ansatz.

## W6 and the quartic obstruction

The second equation of (1) says delta^2 W6=0. The lemma bounds its fibre
degree by three. Its nonlinear fibre terms have the full form

`W6_fib3=q^2 [a1(x,y)rho+b1(x,y)eta]`,
`W6_fib2=q [a3(x,y)rho+b3(x,y)eta]`,

with homogeneous base-linear a1,b1 and base-cubic a3,b3. Its lower
fibre terms contribute no fibre Hessian. Put

`T=partial_q(delta W6)=2(xb1-ya1)q+(xb3-ya3)`.

Direct differentiation gives `C4 n=T v`, so `det C4=-T^2`. The third
equation in (1) gives

`2E delta^2 W5=T^2`.                              (3)

Polynomiality of (3), and primitiveness of q, imply xy divides each
coefficient of T. Thus

`xb1-ya1=kappa xy`, `xb3-ya3=xy U2`,

where kappa is a scalar and U2 a homogeneous base quadratic. It follows
that

`W6_fib3=a q^3+kappa y eta q^2`,
`W6_fib2=A2 q^2+U2 y eta q`,                      (4)

with a scalar a and a homogeneous quadratic A2.

The fibre-degree-two part of (3) is

`delta^2(W5_fib4)=(2kappa^2/lambda)x q^2`.          (5)

The lemma makes W5_fib4 divisible by q^2 if the right side is nonzero.
But such a fibre-quartic polynomial has total degree at least six,
whereas W5 has total degree five. Therefore kappa=0. All higher fibre
terms of W5 are already excluded by (M), and the kernel quartic is also
excluded by the same minimum degree. This is an exact impossibility,
not a numerical fit failure.

An independent complete calculation of the ten-dimensional space of
base-linear fibre quartics gives an image of rank10 under delta^2, and
rank11 after adjoining xq^2. A stored left annihilator provides a small
additional certificate for (5). This calculation checks the entire
homogeneous component; it is not a finite sample of potential repairs.

We now have `W6_fib3=a q^3` and

`delta^2 W5=x U2^2/(2lambda)`.                     (6)

Consequently `W5_fib3=q^2 ell`, with
`ell=u rho+v eta`, u,v scalars. Define `S=delta ell=vx-uy`.

## The next two equations force a truncated cube

The fibre-degree-one part of the fourth equation in (1) is proportional
to q. The lemma then gives the full W4 fibre-cubic part as

`W4_fib3=q Q2`,

where Q2 is a quadratic in rho,eta with constant coefficients. Put
`T2=delta^2 Q2/2`, a homogeneous base quadratic. Using (6), the degree-
seven dual determinant equation becomes

`4lambda y^2 T2+(3a/lambda)U2^2-4yU2 S=0`.         (7)

The fibre-degree-two part of the last equation in (1) is

`4q^2(3a T2-S^2)=0`.                              (8)

Here `pair(C5,C1)` has no fibre-degree-two term because W3 is at most
quadratic. No other omitted pieces contribute: C4,C3,C2 have fibre
degrees at most one after the earlier deductions, while C1 is fibre
independent.

If a=0, (8) gives S=0, so ell=0, and (7) gives T2=0. Since Q2 has
constant coefficients, T2=0 makes Q2=0. All Cj are then independent
of the fibres. Thus (D5)_fibre2=0, contradicting (2).

Assume a!=0. Substitute T2=S^2/(3a) from (8) into (7) and multiply
by 3a lambda. The result is the polynomial square

`(3a U2-2lambda yS)^2=0`.

Therefore

`U2=2lambda yS/(3a)`,
`W4_fib3=q ell^2/(3a)`.                           (9)

The second equality follows from T2=S^2/(3a); no nonzero constant
quadratic Q2 lies in the kernel of delta^2. The cubic fibre pieces are
exactly a truncated shifted cube. A pure fibre-cubic completion at total
degree three is prohibited by origin membership; no such term is
silently inserted.

## Degree25 contradiction

Since C0=0, `D5=pair(C4,C1)+pair(C3,C2)`. Only the second term
can have fibre degree two. The literal Hessians of the two forced
cubic potentials give

`(D5)_fibre2=-4q ell S^2/(3a)`.                    (10)

Comparing (10) with (2), cancelling the nonzero polynomial q, gives

`S^2 ell=-a lambda^3 xq/16`.                       (11)

Apply delta. Both q and every base polynomial are delta invariant,
whereas delta ell=S. Hence (11) gives `S^3=0`, so S=0 in the
polynomial domain. But (11) then says a lambda^3 xq=0, contrary to
a!=0 and lambda!=0. This proves the theorem.

Every contradiction used only positive-degree determinant equations,
so the proof excludes a constant value of zero as well. The intended
HC4 application requires a nonzero constant and is covered.
