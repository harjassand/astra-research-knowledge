## 1. Genesis total suffix-and-transfer receiver

Reviewed `ge_controller_genesis/REUSABLE_RECEIVER.md` and `PROOF.md`. Each site stores both qutrit banks, one phase copy r_i in Z4, and one progress bit. T always accepts at exogenous rate lambda. Four unchanged phase copies determine the canonical source/target whenever all faults since the previous canonical T were on at most one distinct physical site. The plurality tie rule affects only the unrestricted malformed-state service contract, since no tie can occur in the correctable family.

Fix any interval and any observed control sector. On each of four healthy sites, the fast-path gate has either occurred once with progress one or has not occurred with progress zero. T's actual suffix makes that site's action exactly the intended U_r. Every fault on the remaining site's A/B pair, phase and progress, plus every subsequent wrong-bank fast path or suffix, is a local operator on that pair. Tracing the unowned destination converts the whole local history to a source-only qutrit Kraus family. Relative to the intended local U_r, this is an arbitrary one-site error, corrected by the fixed 26-branch decoder proved exactly in `PROOFS.md`. The proof includes faults before, during partial distributed progress, and after a local fast-path gate; it permits arbitrary repeated faults at that same site. It is reference uniform, not only a basis-state argument.

On every physical input T prepares one selected output bank in the code, the old source in vacuum, and unanimous next-phase/progress controls. This is a total canonicalizer. On a general malformed input its logical value and ghost phase are unrestricted. That qualification is essential: three wrong phase copies can choose the vacant bank and erase the real code. A block-wide faulty T can also offset every phase copy, causing future control to follow the wrong phase despite legal unanimous controls. The current proof correctly treats any such event as bad for the finite-horizon comparison; it does not automatically declare later gates correct relative to an external ghost count.

Inducting over intervals with at most one distinct faulty site gives the promised alternating logical history on the same finite A/B hardware for every finite number of ideal terminals. This is genuine finite reuse. It has no incoming live destination port and no output stream. A legitimate external packet in the bank T clears would be destroyed; adding such a port invalidates the proof.

At observation, D_hold undoes the partial next gate on each of the four healthy strands; an incorrect undo or bank choice on the affected site remains a one-site error. Recovery returns the state after the last T, and the reported phase is correct on good histories. It does not undo the cumulative logical computation or reveal an exact terminal count for free.

The homogeneous good-history probability is correctly described by the killed chain

    [a_0']   [ −5p−eta       lambda        ] [a_0]
    [a_1'] = [  5p        −lambda−4p−eta   ] [a_1].

Repeated faults at the same site are harmless self-loops for this classification; a fault at one of the other four sites kills a_1. Removing eta leaves trace −lambda−9p and determinant 20p². The supplied survival formula follows from this 2×2 matrix, including its continuous degenerate limit. The distance bound 2(1−s(t)) follows by coupling the state-independent clocks and using exact equality on good histories. One should not replace the whole good ensemble by s(t) times the *unconditioned* ideal mixture: goodness is correlated with terminal timing/count. The pathwise coupling used in the current proof is sufficient.

For unequal p_i, the charged-arrival bound is also sound. Before a site-i arrival, the chance that another site has faulted since the most recent ideal T is bounded by (nu−p_i)/(lambda+nu−p_i), starting from the canonical initial state. Summing the expected charged arrivals and adding eta t bounds the bad union. The homogeneous rate is 20p²/(lambda+4p)+eta. These are ordinary finite-code renewal/Poisson estimates once the cell receiver theorem is supplied.

The root's simpler two-primitive-fault interval estimate can be used here because T is always enabled and its interarrival time is Exp(lambda). Local fast paths are optional: setting their rates to zero leaves T doing the full transversal logical gate, QEC and bank swap. This makes standard repeated Poisson whole-code recovery a direct same-resource incumbent. The total contract and noisy partial progress are useful specifications, but do not establish a capability or efficiency advantage.

## 2. Independent cyclic move-and-commit receiver

Reviewed `ge_controller_independent/SPEC_PROOF.md`. Each track has one phase in {0,1,2}, moves A→B while applying X, then B→A while applying C. The movement channels explicitly discard the destination, transfer the selected source, and vacate the old source. Their three Kraus operators have adjoint products summing to identity on all nine A/B levels. They are total even on vacuum, double occupancy and coherently malformed A/B states. They transfer the legitimate share instead of copying it.

Four healthy tracks perform exactly X then C once between commits, ending in A with phase two. On the one affected track, arbitrary onsite A/B/phase faults and later wrong-bank resets or repeated moves are local CP maps. At commit, tracing B leaves a one-site qubit-to-qutrit map in A. Relative to the intended transversal CX, it lies in the same exact qutrit recovery span. A premature phase-two fault may skip that site's whole word, and phase rollback may repeat it; both remain correctable. No global authorization bit, program replica or noisy raw-vacancy predicate has been omitted from the table.

T is guarded by all five phases being two and then resets A to the code, B to vacuum and every phase to zero in one atomic map. One corrupted phase can make T early only on its own faulty track; the other four still finished their correct words. Every clean damaged control vector has finite rank sum_i(2−r_i)≤10, and each pending local proposal lowers it. The private terminal proposal becomes persistent once that rank is zero. Thus arbitrary malformed quantum/control states have the claimed clean retirement bound max_i Gamma_i(2,lambda)+Exp(kappa), with unrestricted logical value outside the correctable family.

This T is not always enabled. Its interval law includes local progress, so the simple Exp(lambda) renewal factor of the genesis receiver does not transfer to this table. The supplied uniform service moment m and two-race bound beta≤min(1,(rho m)²) correctly replace it. I independently obtain m=51929339/10800000 at unit local and terminal rates. The no-fault block event used to prove eventual completion under ongoing finite-rate noise is valid; resets and concurrent recovery leave phases unchanged and cannot spoil its progress.

The fixed deterministic epoch labels are chronological commits, and every primitive onsite proposal belongs to exactly one such epoch almost surely. Uniform service holds for every quantum/reference input, including after a bad previous epoch. The first-fault probability is at most rho m. Conditional on the first fault and its arbitrary physical state, the probability of a second fault before clean completion is again at most rho m. Sequential conditional application gives the all-set beta^|J| bound. Uniformity over all input/reference states bounds the corresponding CP effect by beta^|J| I, hence its diamond norm by beta^|J|. This does not require input-independent controller probabilities on already bad states. The nth-commit distance bound follows from the all-good receiver identity plus the bad union n beta.

T and the optional R_* are explicitly ideal whole-code maps. Their internal noisy implementation is excluded. Assigning them arbitrary block-wide fault events would add a separate first-order bad-event term; such events cannot be relabeled onsite or corrected by the single-track theorem without a new spread proof.

## 3. Concurrent phase-indexed recovery R_*

The extension is sound. Condition on the actual classical phase word r. A healthy track at phase r_j contains W_(r_j) applied to its previous-commit qubit in the selected bank, with the other bank vacant. On a corrupted track, selecting a wrong bank and tracing the other is an arbitrary local channel. Undoing tensor_j W_(r_j) cancels the four healthy tracks exactly and leaves a one-site qutrit error on the remaining track. The fixed R corrects it. Reapplying the same W_r and installing the selected banks prepares the legal partial-frame state L_r of the intended logical/reference input, even if the corrupted phase itself has not been changed.

Subsequent clean local moves respect the identities W_1 W_0†=X and W_2 W_1†=C. Another same-track fault remains local; another R_* again restores a legal partial frame. Therefore arbitrary interleavings of R_*, moves, wrong-bank resets and same-track faults preserve the required invariant. At all-phase-two, W_r is the transversal CX, which preserves the code, and T gives the next canonical output. No error spanning a second physical track is hidden in this argument. R_* physically reconstructs the one lost/incorrect share using the other four, which is precisely the ideal code recovery resource already declared.

This can supply an ideal phase-frame readout: select banks, undo W_r, recover and decode. It would return the last committed logical state through a partial next word. It remains a fixed whole-code observation operation and does not give a noisy readout compiler.

## 4. Logical target and compiler boundary

Neither repeating transaction is deterministic-time identity memory. On this code (CX)^3=−iI, so its logical channel has period three. The alternating X,C channel history has period six. In the genesis cell, the stored phase n mod4 does not identify the cumulative logical channel: four terminals return the same phase while applying (CX)², a nonscalar logical unitary. In the independent cell, all phases reset after every commit and do not store its cumulative count at all.

These facts do not contradict either promised nth-commit or matched-random-terminal contract. They preclude treating the same outputs as preservation of an arbitrary stored input at a deterministic time without a separate reliable history/readout operation. Retaining actual noncommuting logical gates is established; a quantum-memory threshold is not.

Both models have a fixed finite bottom-scale support and can be placed in a noninteracting array. Exterior uniformity is immediate because no exterior wire is read. This does not establish service with legitimate incoming packets, intercell CNOTs, shared ancillas, a stream of new outputs, or a self-simulating hierarchy. Replacing the fixed whole-code T or R_* by a growing encoded block would assume the unresolved operation. Neither internal agreement nor exact operator arithmetic closes that compiler.
