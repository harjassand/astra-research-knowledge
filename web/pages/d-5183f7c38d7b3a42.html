# Independent selection check: controlled exact experiment conversion

Worker: check_selection. Date: 2026-10-06. Status: independently reconstructed controlled-source extension; exact arithmetic and compiler efficiency are not supplied. Independent adversarial review by team D requested. No outside validation.

## Exact interface and programme selection

Finite state set Θ of size s≥2. A finite known menu of actions a has known finite, full-support observation laws P_i^a and deterministic positive costs c_a. Actions may be selected using previous observations and state-independent randomization. The menu collectively identifies every state pair. The target G is a known finite full-support pairwise identified iid experiment. A target policy has an integrable stopping time T and a finite terminal report Z. The source policy must report Y with exactly the same law as Z in every state; only expected measurement cost is constrained. It need not reproduce the target stopping time or physical observations online. Computational cost and real-number arithmetic are outside this interface.

Li–Liu, arXiv:2608.19897v2, Theorem 2, proves this exact conversion for one fixed iid source, with a uniform square-root remainder. I checked the finite-H residual repair, its positivity argument, and the dyadic mechanism below rather than taking the KL statement alone as evidence. Their paper is verified as v2, 28 August 2026, originally 20 August 2026; authors Zihao Li and Tianhao Liu. My proposed controlled extension below is a deduction, not their stated theorem.

The frozen-mixture LP from team D is correct but is a short composition with that theorem. The significant remaining issue is whether state-dependent measurement choices preserve *uniform exact reproduction for all finite reports*. Controlled hypothesis testing by itself addresses incorrect classification probabilities, not this interface.

## Proposition: optimal statewise first-order cost vector

Write d_ij^a=D(P_i^a || P_j^a), g_ij=D(G_i || G_j). For each i define the finite LP

    R_i = min_{w_a≥0} Σ_a c_a w_a
          subject to Σ_a d_ij^a w_a ≥ g_ij for every j≠i.

Then there is a constant C, depending on the known menu and G, such that every integrable finite-output target policy has a single controlled source reproduction satisfying, simultaneously for every i,

    E_i[source cost] ≤ R_i E_i[T] + C sqrt(E_i[T]).

No universal first-order coefficient smaller than R_i is possible in state i: exact reproduction of the target transcript G^m requires source expected cost at least m R_i for every integer m. Thus the vector (R_i) is simultaneously first-order optimal. Equality R_i=1 does not establish eventual same-budget dominance because the remainder need not vanish.

The proof below is explicit at the mathematical-policy level. It does not imply a polynomial-time converter, finite-precision exact sampling, a learned-law converter, or a computable universal threshold under arbitrary source encodings.

### 1. Reduce the LP to candidate-specific iid verifier mixtures

For a mixture q, set μ(q)=Σ_a q_a c_a and b_ij(q)=Σ_a q_a d_ij^a. Labeled pairs (a,x) sampled with law q_a P_i^a(x) form an iid experiment whose directed KL is b_ij(q). For a fixed true candidate i define

    ρ_i(q)=max_{j≠i} μ(q)g_ij / b_ij(q).

The mixture must distinguish i from every j; it need not distinguish the other states from one another. The LP is equivalent to min_q ρ_i(q). To see both directions, an allocation w with total cost r gives q=w/Σw and ρ_i(q)≤r. Conversely, q gives w_a=ρ_i(q) q_a/μ(q), meeting every LP constraint at cost ρ_i(q). An optimum exists: positive costs make bounded LP sublevel sets compact, and collective identification gives a finite feasible allocation.

Fix an optimal q_i for each i and a baseline mixture q_* with positive probability on every action. The latter identifies every pair and is used only for preliminary classification and residual correction. For every j≠i, b_ij(q_i)>0 and b_ji(q_i)>0. The second fact follows from equality of support: two distinct probability laws have positive KL in both directions. Finiteness gives bounded log increments and finitely many positive candidate-versus-alternative drifts.

### 2. Exact reproduction of an arbitrary finite common-support report H

Let h_i(y)>0 be its probabilities, λ_ij(y)=log(h_i(y)/h_j(y)), and Δ(H)=max_{i,j,y}|λ_ij(y)|. Set η>0 sufficiently small, g=log(1/η), and r=s-1. The following construction uses a constant C independent of H, its output count, and η.

Take O(g) baseline observations to produce a maximum-likelihood candidate K with P_i(K≠i)≤η. Given K=k, propose y from H_k using independent randomness. From a fresh tail, sample the fixed labeled mixture q_k and define its cumulative log likelihoods S_ij(n).

Accept verification when simultaneously S_kj(n)≥λ_kj(y)+g for every j≠k. Reject verification as soon as *any* S_jk(n)≥g, j≠k. Stop at the first of these events, with rejection winning ties. The rejection rule uses one alternative-versus-candidate comparison rather than requiring an alternative to distinguish every other state.

Under true state k, all success drifts b_kj(q_k) are positive. Standard bounded-increment simultaneous-passage bounds give

    E_k V_{k,y} ≤ t_k(y)+C sqrt(1+t_k(y)),
    t_k(y)=max_j [λ_kj(y)+g]^+ / b_kj(q_k),

after absorbing fixed additive constants. This local passage bound requires positive candidate-versus-alternative drifts only; no global pairwise identification of q_k is used. A direct proof is to union-bound Hoeffding lower tails for n≥t_k(y)+u, then sum the resulting sub-Gaussian tail. Since the hitting time is at most n whenever all boundaries hold at n, the first hitting-time tail is bounded by this same union bound.

Under true state i≠k, the direct i-versus-k rejection time has positive drift b_ik(q_k), hence E_i V_{k,y}≤C(1+g), independent of y and all H likelihood ratios. Wald's identity for iid labeled observations also gives expected verifier cost μ(q_k)E_i V_{k,y}; the action cost is independent of the previous verifier history at each active step.

Under k, exp(S_jk(n)) is a nonnegative likelihood-ratio martingale. Ville's inequality and a union bound imply that verifier rejection occurs with probability at most rη. Let p_i(k,y) denote verifier acceptance probability. On acceptance the stopped k/i likelihood is at least exp(λ_ki(y)+g). Change of measure at this integrable stopping time gives

    p_i(k,y) ≤ η h_i(y)/h_k(y) p_k(k,y),  i≠k,
    p_k(k,y) ≥ 1-rη.

The conditional source kernel q_k is the same under every true state once candidate k is fixed; its action probabilities therefore cancel in the likelihood ratio. This is the key admissibility point.

The unthinned accepted output probability is

    Q_i^raw(y)=Σ_k P_i(K=k) h_k(y) p_i(k,y).

Its own-state term is at most h_i(y); every cross-state term is at most η h_i(y). Multiply all accepted outputs by a state-independent coin of success probability

    a_η=(1-η)/(1+rη).

The resulting subprobability Q satisfies Q_i(y)≤(1-η)h_i(y). Its missing mass δ_i=1-Σ_y Q_i(y) satisfies

    η ≤ δ_i ≤ 2(r+1)η,

because the own-state accepted contribution before thinning is at least (1-η)(1-rη). Define R_i(y)=(h_i(y)-Q_i(y))/δ_i. These R_i form a common-support residual experiment, and

    Δ(R) ≤ Δ(H)+log(2(r+1)/η).

The inequality follows by bounding each numerator between ηh_i(y) and h_i(y), and every ratio δ_j/δ_i between 1/[2(r+1)] and 2(r+1).

To reproduce R exactly, take n baseline observations and let A_n be their classifier confusion matrix. Its row error decays exponentially, so ||A_n^{-1}-I||∞≤C exp(-cn) for n large enough. Set K_n=A_n^{-1}R. Its rows sum to one, and its entries are nonnegative once ||A_n^{-1}-I||∞ exp(Δ(R))≤1. Thus n≤C(1+Δ(R)) suffices and A_n K_n=R exactly. Apply this fresh-tail reproduction only when the thinned proposal fails. Its expected cost is δ_i times O(1+Δ(R)). Independence of the fresh tail conditional on the past guarantees the conditional residual law R_i.

The total output law is exactly Q_i+δ_iR_i=H_i. Integrating the own-candidate passage cost against H_i, using Jensen, and charging wrong candidates at probability at most η yields

    E_i cost ≤ B_i(H)+C[sqrt(1+B_i(H))+g+η(1+Δ(H))],
    B_i(H)=E_{Y~H_i} max_{j≠i} μ(q_i)[λ_ij(Y)]^+/b_ij(q_i).

All constants can be chosen uniformly over the finite list q_i and q_*. This is the controlled analogue of the finite-H lemma; the exactness argument is reconstructed explicitly above.

### 3. Bounded target policies

Suppose T≤m, m≥1. Finite full support implies common support of the target terminal law H and Δ(H)≤m M_G for a source-independent log-ratio bound M_G. Let S_ij^G(T) denote the target stopped log likelihood. The terminal change-of-measure identity gives

    λ_ij(Z) = -log E_i[exp(-S_ij^G(T)) | Z]
            ≤ E_i[S_ij^G(T) | Z].

The function v↦max_j μ(q_i)[v_j]^+/b_ij(q_i) is convex and coordinatewise increasing. Jensen and the last inequality bound B_i(H) by its expectation applied to the stopped target likelihood vector. Write S_ij^G(T)=g_ij T+M_ij(T). Bounded increments and optional isometry give E_i|M_ij(T)|≤C sqrt(E_iT), hence

    B_i(H) ≤ R_i E_iT + C sqrt(E_iT).

Choose η=η_0/m. The finite-H remainder is O(sqrt(m)): Δ(H)η=O(1), log(m)=O(sqrt(m)), and sqrt(1+B_i)≤C sqrt(m). Therefore the controlled converter has

    E_i cost ≤ R_i E_iT + C sqrt(m).

It also works when some target continuations stop immediately; no lower bound on E_iT is used at this bounded stage.

### 4. Integrable target policies: dyadic continuation

Partition target time into blocks L_k=2^k, with boundaries s_k=2^{k+1}-1. At a continuing target history h of length s_{k-1}, exactly reproduce the next bounded target block's report: either a terminal output or a continuing history at s_k. There are only finitely many possible block reports and histories at each finite stage. Choose all their source kernels once in advance. They are state-independent functions of the reported history.

Conditional on any source history reaching simulated target history h, fresh source randomization and observations reproduce the target continuation law in every state. Each bounded converter takes at least one baseline observation; infinitely many stages therefore require infinitely many source observations, avoiding an accumulation of stages at finite source time. Induction proves that the simulated-history distribution and all terminal-report probabilities equal the target ones, statewise. Target integrability implies almost-sure termination; nonnegative expected source costs can be summed with Tonelli. The leading term sums to R_iE_iT. The remainders satisfy

    Σ_k P_i(T>s_{k-1}) sqrt(L_k)
      = E_i[Σ_{k:T>2^k-1} 2^{k/2}]
      ≤ [sqrt(2)/(sqrt(2)-1)] E_i sqrt(T)
      ≤ [sqrt(2)/(sqrt(2)-1)] sqrt(E_iT).

Immediate time-zero stopping uses only target state-independent randomization and incurs zero source cost. This proves the stated uniform theorem and simultaneously handles arbitrary integrable target policies.

### 5. Universal first-order lower bound

For an arbitrary integrable controlled source stopping policy, actions are chosen by the same history-dependent kernel in every state. Therefore the stopped likelihood ratio is the sum of observation log likelihoods, with no action-kernel term. Bounded increments and integrable action counts yield

    D(source stopped record_i || source stopped record_j)
      = Σ_a E_i[N_a] d_ij^a.

Exact reproduction of G^m implies by data processing that this is at least m g_ij for every j≠i. Hence w_a=E_i[N_a]/m is LP-feasible and E_i cost≥mR_i. This lower bound already allows arbitrary adaptive measurement choices and terminal randomization. The upper construction uses one policy that attains the optimal vector; it does not secretly condition actions on the unknown state.

## Decisive finite examples with analytic universal consequences

### Joint allocation obstruction

Two unit-cost Bernoulli actions have state parameters (.9,.1,.899) and (.9,.899,.1); the target Bernoulli parameters are (.88,.12,.13). Every one of the six directed individually-best action KL ratios exceeds one; the minimum is 1.1608276250. Nonetheless every adaptive exact converter for a length-m target transcript has state-0 expected cost at least 1.6836117045 m.

Proof: set D=D(Ber(.9)||Ber(.1)) and ε=D(Ber(.9)||Ber(.899)). The two state-0 data-processing constraints are Dx+εy≥g_01 and εx+Dy≥g_02, where x=E_0N_A/m and y=E_0N_B/m. Adding gives x+y≥(g_01+g_02)/(D+ε). Positive simultaneous-equality occupations attain this LP bound. This is an analytic obstruction for all policies; its universal force does not come from a finite simulation.

### Adaptive versus every fixed-mixture dominance reversal

Three states and actions a=0,1,2. Action a has Bernoulli success probability .1 in state a and .9 in both other states. Let target G be the ternary symmetric experiment, probability .8 at the true-state symbol and .1 at each other symbol. Define D=.8log9 and g=.7log8.

Choose q_i concentrated on action i. Candidate-versus-every-alternative source KL equals D, so every optimal statewise coefficient is R_i=g/D=0.8280953016. The lower bound R_i≥g/D is immediate because each single-observation pairwise KL is at most D. Thus the controlled theorem implies eventual same-budget exact dominance of G.

For any fixed iid mixture q, its i/j KL is D(q_i+q_j). Some pair has q_i+q_j≤2/3, so every fixed mixture has worst-state coefficient at least 3g/(2D)=1.2421429523. Uniform q attains this value. Exact long-transcript data processing therefore prevents every fixed iid mixture from same-budget dominance, at arbitrarily large budgets. The controlled catalogue can eventually reproduce *every* finite terminal G-policy within the same statewise budget; every fixed mixture fails even for deterministic long transcripts. This is the research-changing consequence of the extension, subject to its mathematical assumptions.

## Prior art and novelty status

Nitinawarat, Atia, Veeravalli, *Controlled Sensing for Multihypothesis Testing*, IEEE TAC 58(10), 2451–2464 (October 2013), DOI 10.1109/TAC.2013.2261188, arXiv:1205.0858v6 (4 September 2013). Retrieved author-hosted PDF; text plus rendered pages 2455–2456 inspected. Equation (13) chooses a randomized design by maximizing candidate-state worst-alternative KL; Theorem 3, equations (18)–(20), gives simultaneous statewise asymptotic optimality for sequential testing. This directly anticipates state-dependent sampling and explains why the LP geometry or adaptivity idea alone is not novel.

The point not supplied by that theorem is exact reproduction of arbitrary finite terminal experiments with a remainder uniform in the target policy and output space. Li–Liu supplies the fixed-source exact converter; the candidate-dependent verifier modification above extends it to a controlled catalogue. It is a plausible new theorem-level corollary/extension, but broader novelty against the full experiment-comparison literature remains unverified. Do not claim a foundational discovery merely from this note or agreement with team D.

Nearest open direction: controlled *target* catalogues have a more demanding interface. A conservative bound replaces g_ij by max_b D(G_i^b||G_j^b) per target sample, but this is generally not jointly optimal. A sharp controlled-source/controlled-target universal comparison would need occupation-set or game analysis, not individual pairwise maxima. Continuous parameter sets, unknown observation laws, and finite computational implementations also remain unresolved.

## Next falsifiable step and downstream use

Independent reconstruction should try to break three points: (1) positive reverse drift for wrong-candidate rejection without global q_i identification; (2) exact residual law after conditional fresh-tail sampling; (3) common constant across all dyadic target histories. If one fails, retain the fixed-iid corollary and the universal obstruction; do not report adaptive sufficiency.

If the extension survives, it changes the procurement/design question for a finite catalogue of known sensors: a catalogue may universally replace a target data technology only when measurement selection can depend on observations. The symmetric example proves that no frozen mixture matches this ability. This is statistical resource theory for finite known hypotheses, not autonomous structure acquisition, a general research agent, or a guarantee for unknown scientific models.
