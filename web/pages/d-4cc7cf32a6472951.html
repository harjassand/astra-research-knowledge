## A uniform elementary duplication bound

For a finite point with reduced x-coordinate `x=a/b`, `b>0`, `gcd(a,b)=1`,
the projective x-coordinate of its double is `[F:G]`, where

`F=(a²+25b²)²`, `G=4ab(a²−25b²)`.

These formulas also cover the y=0 points: `G=0`, `F>0`, so the image is O.
For O itself use x-coordinate `[1:0]`; the same homogeneous formula maps it
to `[1:0]`. Projective height agrees with `H_x(O)=1` and with
`max(|a|,b)` for finite reduced x. Thus torsion and infinity are included.

The following two homogeneous polynomial identities are exact:

`250000 b⁷ = (400b³−12a²b)F + (125ab²+3a³)G`,

`16a⁷ = (−300ab²+16a³)F + (−1875b³−125a²b)G`.

Our script checks their coefficients independently. Let `g=gcd(F,G)`.
The identities give `g|250000 b⁷` and `g|16a⁷`. Since `16|250000`, also
`g|250000 a⁷`. Since a and b are coprime, `g|250000`. This argument includes
`G=0`; no division by G is used.

Writing `H=max(|a|,b)`, the elementary inequalities

`H⁴≤F≤676H⁴`, `|G|≤104H⁴`

give

`H⁴/250000 ≤ H_x([2]Q) ≤ 676H⁴`.

Consequently, with `h_x=log H_x`,

`|h_x([2]Q)−4h_x(Q)|≤log250000` for every rational point Q.

The canonical normalization in the source is
`2 hhat(Q)=lim_N 4^(−N) h_x([2^N]Q)`.
Successive terms differ by at most `4^(−N−1) log250000`; summing that
geometric series proves

`|h_x(Q)−2 hhat(Q)|≤c0`, where `c0=(log250000)/3`.

In particular `c0<5`: the exponential series gives `e³>13`, and
`13⁵=371293>250000`, so `log250000<15`. We may therefore use the displayed
integer error bound **c=5**. The derivation assumes the standard quadraticity
and positivity properties of the canonical height, already required by the
source, while making its uniform error bound explicit.

## An exhaustive finite generator certificate

The explicit point `P0=(-4,6)` is nontorsion (it is outside the source's exact
torsion list; alternatively its double has nonintegral x-coordinate
`1681/144`, contradicting Lutz–Nagell if it were torsion).

Let G be any generator modulo torsion. Since `P0=nG+t`, `n≠0`, canonical
quadraticity and torsion invariance give `hhat(G)≤hhat(P0)`. Apply the error
bound at both points:

`h_x(G)≤2hhat(G)+c0≤2hhat(P0)+c0≤h_x(P0)+2c0`.

As `H_x(P0)=4`, this proves

`H_x(G)≤4·250000^(2/3)<16000`.

The last comparison is exact after cubing:
`250000²=62500000000<4000³=64000000000`.

Every finite rational point in this height box can be enumerated exactly. If
`x=a/b` is reduced, then

`y²=a(a²−25b²)/b³`,

and the numerator is coprime to b. Since this reduced rational is a square,
b is a square, say `b=d²`; the integer `a(a²−25d⁴)` is a nonnegative square.
Conversely either square root gives a point. Therefore the loops

`1≤d≤126`, `−16000≤a≤16000`, `gcd(a,d)=1`,

followed by one nonnegative integer-square test enumerate **all** finite points
with `H_x≤16000`. Add O separately. No rounding or rational square-root
approximation is used.

The executed scan tests 4,032,126 integer pairs and produces 20 distinct
points including O. Four are torsion; eight belong to `±P0+T`. For each of the
remaining eight, the serialized certificate records the exact strict inequality

`H_x([2]Q)³ > 250000²·1681³`.

This means `h_x([2]Q)−h_x([2]P0)>2c0`. The canonical bounds and
`hhat([2]Q)=4hhat(Q)` imply `hhat(Q)>hhat(P0)`.

The finite box contains at least one generator G. It cannot be a torsion
point. It cannot be one of those eight strictly higher points because
`hhat(G)≤hhat(P0)`. Thus G is in `±P0+T`, which proves that
**P=(-4,6) itself is a generator modulo torsion**. Equal-height candidates are
identified by their exact finite group orbit; the certificate does not try to
separate equality by ever-finer intervals.

## Explicit amplification and growth constants

We also obtain simple rational bounds on `hhat(P)`. Its double has height
`H_x([2]P)=1681`. The comparison at this double gives

`(log1681−c0)/8 ≤ hhat(P) ≤ (log1681+c0)/8`.

The lower bound is strictly greater than `1/4` because
`1681³>250000·3⁶>250000·e⁶`, using `e<3`. The upper bound is less than 2:
`e>8/3` and `8⁸>1681·3⁸` prove `log1681<8`; combine this with `c0<5`.
All displayed integer comparisons are checked by the script.

Choose

`m_*=10`, `κ=2⁶¹`, `c=5`.

Then `m_*` is even, so it kills the exact 2-torsion subgroup, and

`50<a=2m_*² hhat(P)<400`.

Since `2<e<3<4`,

`30<logκ<61<70<2a−30`.

In particular `a>2c` and `6c<logκ<2a−6c`. These are exactly the strict
separation hypotheses needed by the source's square window and our bilinear
window. A fixed scalar chain can use the points `[2]Q,[4]Q,[8]Q,[10]Q`.
The script computes the integer `W1=H_x([10]P)` exactly; it has 274 bits.

For the source's eventual growth/Pell construction choose

`δ=1/51200`, `u0=max(W1,3⁵¹²,4)`, `ell=307201`.

Here is an explicit threshold proof, rather than an appeal to unspecified
eventual behavior. For `u≥u0`, set `x=log u` and `U=u+W1`. Then `x>512`
and `log U≤x+log2<x+1`. The bracket selects k with

`a k²−5≤log U<a(k+1)²+5≤4a k²+5`.

Since `x>10`, this gives `k²>x/(8a)`, and hence

`log g(u)≥a k⁴−5>x²/(64a)−5>x²/51200`,

where the final step uses `a<400` and `x²>512²>256000`.
On the other hand,

`log g(u)≤(log U+5)²/a+5<(x+6)²/50+5<x²<u log u`.

The penultimate inequality holds for `x>512`; the last follows from
`e^x>x`. Thus these δ,u0 satisfy the source's two growth bounds at every
required input. Finally `log2>1/2`, so

`3/(δ log2)<6/δ=307200<ell`.

This is exactly the fixed exponent condition in Proposition 5.2. The number
u0 is supplied explicitly in the JSON certificate, not just existentially.
