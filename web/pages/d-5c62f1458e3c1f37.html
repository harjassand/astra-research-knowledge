# Cycle2 lead: finite design certificates and their exact limits

2026-10-06. These are independently derived standard matrix/variational constructions; publication novelty is not claimed. They supply actual finite constants/algorithms at the stated interface, then identify why the boundary-only continuum problem remains harder. All models/bases/operators are supplied, not learned.

## 1. General controlled-radius covariance design

Let Θ⊂R^d be compact and convex inside an open convex U. Assume Θ lies in the Euclidean ball of radius R about θ₀. The finite current space is H=R^n with Euclidean current power. A fixed detector map gives m retained real outputs; F:U→R^(m×n) is C² and already includes those detectors. Assume a certified uniform Hessian bound

    ||D²F_θ[v,w]||_op ≤ H ||v|| ||w||

throughout the convex hull of Θ and θ₀. Put D_i=∂_iF_θ₀. For Q≽0 with tr Q=E>0, define

    G(Q)_ij=tr(D_i Q D_jᵀ),   γ(Q)=λ_min G(Q).

Factor Q=Σ_{l=1}^q g_l g_lᵀ in spectral directions, where q=rank Q. Apply these q physical currents and measure the same m output coordinates per current. Total energy is E and exactly qm scalars are acquired. A unit parameter direction v has squared stacked linearized response vᵀG(Q)v. Every normalized secant equals

    ∫₀¹ DF_{η+t(θ−η)}[v] dt,   v=(θ−η)/||θ−η||,

and differs from DF_θ₀[v] in operator norm by at most HR. Multiplying by Q^(1/2) gives Frobenius error at most HR√E. Consequently the complete physical output map obeys the global bound

    ||Y_Q(θ)−Y_Q(η)||₂ ≥ β(Q)||θ−η||₂,
    β(Q)=(√γ(Q)−HR√E)_+.

This is a uniform secant theorem for a general finite nonradial operator. It becomes inconclusive at large contrast; a positive local γ alone does not repair that limitation.

The finite SDP maximizing t subject to G(Q)≽tI_d, Q≽0 and tr Q=E selects the strongest displayed certificate. Its supplied data are the finite derivative matrices and the certified Hessian constant. An exact or outward-verified feasible Q with t>H²R²E certifies positivity. A solver status or sampled sensitivity minimum does not. Offline derivative acquisition, verification and optimization costs are charged. An equally informed comparator can solve the same SDP and factor Q; this is standard design, with no special random or single-current advantage. A single real current restricts Q to rank one. Scalar count qm and source switching overhead remain separate resources.

If supplied derivatives have certified operator error δ_J uniformly over unit parameter directions, subtract δ_J√E from β. A uniform forward-map error δ_F alone gives no normalized-secant transfer: endpoint error divided by ||θ−η|| can diverge near the diagonal. This is the precise discretization gap.

For z=Y_Q(θ*)+e, any admitted estimate with residual at most ||e||+η has parameter error at most (2||e||+η)/β. Computing that estimate is a separate cost. A full parameter grid of covering radius h and certified forward Lipschitz constant L provides residual slack η≤Lh, with potentially exponential dimension cost. No efficient nonlinear optimizer is asserted.

## 2. Explicit coercive inverse Hessian

On an energy Hilbert space V, let A_θ=A₀+Σ_i θ_i B_i be affine, self-adjoint and uniformly coercive: A_θ≽aI with a>0 throughout the admitted convex region. Let input B:H→V and detector C:V→R^m be fixed bounded maps, and set F_θ=C A_θ⁻¹ B. Suppose ||B(v)||≤K||v||₂, where B(v)=Σ_i v_i B_i. Direct inverse differentiation gives

    DF_θ[v]=−C A_θ⁻¹ B(v) A_θ⁻¹ B,
    D²F_θ[v,w]=C A_θ⁻¹[B(w)A_θ⁻¹B(v)+B(v)A_θ⁻¹B(w)]A_θ⁻¹ B.

Thus valid bounds are

    L≤||C||||B||K/a²,   H≤2||C||||B||K²/a³.

These are explicit once a, K, B and C are certified. For scalar conductivity with the gradient-energy norm, a is a lower conductivity bound and K can be bounded by ess sup √(Σ_i φ_i²). Input and output trace constants must be charged. Accessible boundary reachability resides in B and detector choice in C; arbitrary interior energy vectors are not free physical measurements. A resistor model supplies finite matrices. A Galerkin certificate proves the discrete model only; a continuum claim still needs certified derivative transfer δ_J.

## 3. All-contrast general graph certificate (fully accessible surrogate)

This strengthens the observation interface explicitly: every node of a known simple connected graph is an accessible current and voltage port. Let N be its node count, n=N−1 and d its edge count. Unknown edge conductances range independently over [γ_min,γ_max], with γ_min>0. This is a general nonradial network class; it is not boundary-only recovery of inaccessible interior nodes.

Write L_γ=Σ_e γ_e b_e b_eᵀ, where b_e=e_i−e_j for edge e=(i,j), and choose an orthonormal U:R^n→1⊥. Then A_γ=UᵀL_γU is positive definite and F_γ=A_γ⁻¹. If Δ is the maximum graph degree, the quadratic form or Gershgorin gives λ_max(A_γ)≤b=2γ_max Δ.

For any conductance pair,

    F_γ−F_η=−A_γ⁻¹(A_γ−A_η)A_η⁻¹,
    ||F_γ−F_η||_F ≥ ||A_γ−A_η||_F/b².

The compression preserves the Frobenius norm of L_γ−L_η, since this matrix annihilates the all-ones vector. Let J be the unoriented node-edge incidence matrix, with two ones per column. The edge outer-product Gram is exactly

    [tr(b_e b_eᵀ b_f b_fᵀ)]_{ef}=2I_d+JᵀJ.

Its diagonal is four; distinct edges sharing a node give one; disjoint edges give zero. Thus

    ||A_γ−A_η||_F²=(γ−η)ᵀ(2I_d+JᵀJ)(γ−η)
                  ≥2||γ−η||₂²,
    ||F_γ−F_η||_F≥√2 ||γ−η||₂/b².

This covers the entire contrast box. It requires no small-contrast condition, parameter-pair net, Jacobian scan or genericity assumption.

Apply n currents g_l=s U e_l, with s=√(E/n), and read n orthonormal mean-zero voltage coordinates for each current. Total physical Euclidean current energy is E and the scalar acquisition count is n². The matrix of means is Y=sF_γ and obeys

    ||γ−η||₂ ≤ b²√[n/(2E)] ||Y_γ−Y_η||_F.

This is a competent all-current baseline with explicit finite costs. Structured graph designs might use fewer scalars; no optimum is asserted. If d>n, one fixed current cannot identify an open conductance box from n voltage coordinates by the continuous dimension obstruction. Allowing more arbitrary detectors of that same n-dimensional response does not create new noiseless information.

### Exact reconstruction and noisy error

Given the observed matrix Z=sF_γ+noise, set M=(Z+Zᵀ)/(2s). If ||M−F_γ||_op≤1/(2b), then M is positive definite, ||M⁻¹||_op≤2b and

    ||M⁻¹−A_γ||_F≤2b²||M−F_γ||_F.

Compute L_hat=U M⁻¹ Uᵀ and γ_hat_e=−(L_hat)_{ij} for each known edge (i,j), then clip each coordinate to its admitted interval. Symmetric off-diagonal entries appear twice in the matrix norm, so

    ||γ_hat−γ||₂ ≤ ||L_hat−L_γ||_F/√2
                 ≤ √2 b²||M−F_γ||_F.

Dense inversion costs O(n³); edge extraction costs O(d); constructing the known mean-zero basis costs at most O(N³) by ordinary dense linear algebra. Numerical precision must be sufficient for verified matrix inversion if a machine certificate is claimed. No nonlinear optimizer or dictionary acquisition is hidden.

For independent scalar detector noise N(0,σ²), the noise matrix has n² independent entries. Its exponential moment is

    E exp(||noise||_F²/(4σ²))=2^(n²/2).

Consequently, with probability at least 1−δ,

    ||M−F_γ||_F ≤ B_δ
      =(σ/s)√[2n² log 2+4 log(1/δ)].

If B_δ≤1/(2b), inversion is admissible on this event and the parameter error is at most √2 b² B_δ. This is conservative but explicit and uniform over the full conductance box. A constrained least-squares alternative could avoid the inversion admissibility test, but its computation would need to be charged.

### Why boundary-only transfer fails

When interior nodes are hidden, the response depends on an inverse Schur complement. The map from edge contrasts to boundary Schur contrasts need not preserve the displayed edge Gram bound; insufficient accessible ports can make edge parameters unidentifiable. In a continuum PDE, harmonic extension replaces the identity input/output maps used here. Boundary observability and attenuation are precisely the unresolved lower singular-value constants. This graph theorem is a baseline and a boundary of valid transfer, not a solution to nonradial boundary EIT.
