Result: a tested error-certification mechanism, not a demonstrated foundational breakthrough
I derived an explicit error certificate for reducing kinetic transition networks, tested it on a published atomic-cluster model, and produced exact-arithmetic verification files.
The useful mathematical result is this:
The error caused by replacing discarded molecular excursions with a reduced Markov process can be expressed exactly as an occupation-weighted return-time quantity—without first computing the full passage-time distribution.

However, two challenges prevent promoting this to the breakthrough Astra is seeking. An ordinary spectral-truncation baseline was faster and more accurate on the implemented benchmark. More fundamentally, I constructed a counterexample showing that the certificate can become arbitrarily small while the reduced model loses essentially the entire probability of an important rare tail.
The appropriate status is a derived, computationally checked research result with unresolved historical priority, not a new foundational computational capability.
The REPORT.md contains the full derivation and assessment. The astra_clock_reduction_research.zip contains the code, rate-relevant source data, numerical results, counterexample, and exact certificates.
1. Scientific task and the difficulty that survives existing methods
I concentrated on first-passage-time distributions in metastable kinetic networks: given molecular configurations and transition rates, determine when a system first reaches a specified product or configuration.
The distinction between a mean and a distribution is consequential. Two mechanisms can have similar mean completion times but very different probabilities of becoming trapped or missing a deadline. Full first-passage distributions, numerical ill-conditioning, and the limitations of reduction are already explicit subjects of the relevant literature—not an overlooked task invented for this investigation. Royal Society of Chemistry Publications
The strongest relevant established machinery already does substantial work. Graph transformation removes states while preserving mean first-passage times and branching probabilities. Partial graph transformation, or pGT, retains selected states and produces a smaller Markov model. Robust elimination, kinetic path sampling, and spectral methods are therefore necessary competitors. State elimination and mean preservation cannot themselves be claimed as discoveries here. PyGT
The question I pursued was narrower:
Can we decide which states may be discarded using an explicit bound on the resulting timing error, rather than only a heuristic about which states appear fast or unimportant?

I also checked the applicability of Astra’s mathematical capital. The inspected N719 result concerns independent exponential durations on a directed acyclic graph, explicitly excludes cycles, and remains source-derived and unreviewed. It was not imported as a theorem about cyclic kinetic networks. The repository revision used was b7cada92d2eb304578be5ed974f1e44b10a89928. 
2. The mathematical result: an exact error identity for discarded excursions
Construction
Let \(Q\) be a finite continuous-time Markov-chain generator, using row-generator notation. Let \(B\) be the target set, reachable from every state, and let \(T\) be its first hitting time.
Choose retained states \(R\) containing the target and all possible initial states. Write \(I\) for the discarded states.
Define
\[
G=(-Q_{II})^{-1},
\qquad
m=G\mathbf 1.
\]
Here \(m_i\) is the expected time to return to the retained set when starting from discarded state \(i\).
The trace generator and mean-clock correction are
\[
K=Q_{RR}+Q_{RI}GQ_{IR},
\qquad
\alpha=\mathbf 1+Q_{RI}m.
\]
The reduced generator is
\[
\widehat Q=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha)^{-1}K.
\]
This construction is the existing mean-preserving reduction, expressed in matrix form. The candidate contribution is its error identity, not the reduced generator itself.
Let
\[
\eta_i=
\mathbb E_\mu\!\left[
\int_0^T\mathbf 1_{\{X_t=i\}}\,dt
\right]
\]
be the expected total time spent in state \(i\) before reaching the target, under the original process.
Exact stopped-clock identity
There is an explicit coupling of the original passage time \(T\) and the reduced passage time \(\widehat T\) for which
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(T-\widehat T)^2
=
V(R)
:=
2\sum_{i\in I}\eta_i m_i.
}
\]
The means agree:
\[
\mathbb E T=\mathbb E\widehat T.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
W_2\!\left(\mathcal L(T),\mathcal L(\widehat T)\right)
\le
\sqrt{V(R)}.
}
\]
Here \(W_2\) measures the smallest possible root-mean-square discrepancy between paired samples from the two distributions. The displayed coupling provides an upper bound; it is not asserted to be the optimal pairing.
Interpretation: a discarded state matters when the original process spends substantial time there and returning from it takes substantial time. A small individual waiting time is not sufficient grounds for elimination.
The identity does not require reversibility or a spectral-gap assumption.
Why the identity holds
Set \(h=m\) on discarded states and \(h=0\) on retained states. Then
\[
(Qh)_I=-\mathbf 1,
\qquad
(Qh)_R=\alpha-\mathbf 1.
\]
Follow an original trajectory, delete its discarded-state excursions, and stretch retained-state time by \(\alpha\). This produces the reduced process, with passage time
\[
\widehat T
=
\int_0^T
\mathbf 1_R(X_t)\alpha(X_t)\,dt.
\]
The stopped Dynkin martingale is
\[
M_t=
h(X_{t\wedge T})-h(X_0)
-\int_0^{t\wedge T}Qh(X_s)\,ds.
\]
Because \(h\) vanishes at the initial and terminal states,
\[
M_T=T-\widehat T.
\]
Its quadratic-variation integrand is
\[
\Gamma h=Q(h^2)-2hQh.
\]
Stopped Dynkin’s formula gives
\[
\mathbb E\int_0^T Q(h^2)(X_t)\,dt=0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E M_T^2
=
-2\mathbb E\int_0^T h(X_t)Qh(X_t)\,dt
=
2\sum_{i\in I}\eta_i m_i.
\]
Finite-state absorption under the stated reachability condition supplies the required integrability. This proves the identity within the stated model.
What becomes computationally obtainable
The error can be calculated through linear systems rather than reconstruction of the full timing distribution.
In particular, put \(S=R\setminus B\) and
\[
m_2=2Gm.
\]
Then
\[
V(R)=
\mu_S(-K_{SS})^{-1}Q_{SI}m_2.
\]
When the pGT factorization is already available, \(m\) has already been computed for the clock correction. Obtaining \(V\) then needs one additional eliminated-block solve and a reduced solve.
That is the surviving computational leverage: an existing reduction can be accompanied by an explicit stopped-time error bound at an identifiable additional cost.
It is not a guarantee that forming the reduction is cheap.
3. State selection and a stronger observable extension
Exact marginal value of retaining a state
The identity produces an explicit state-selection score. With
\[
z=G^\top\eta_I,
\]
adding discarded state \(j\) to the retained set improves the certificate by exactly
\[
\boxed{
V(R)-V(R\cup\{j\})
=
\frac{2m_jz_j}{G_{jj}}.
}
\]
Thus a greedy selector need not test the complete reduced distribution after every proposed addition.
There is also a diminishing-returns structure: \(V\) is decreasing and supermodular, so its reduction from a compulsory starting set is a monotone submodular benefit. The usual greedy approximation guarantee applies to benefit obtained, not to the remaining error or the actual cumulative-distribution error.
This selection structure has clear prior art. Mavroforakis, Mathioudakis and Gionis studied absorption-time minimisation and established supermodularity in Absorbing random-walk centrality. It would be incorrect to rename that structure as a new Astra primitive. The potentially distinctive connection is its identification with the specific pGT coupling error above. arXiv
Beyond elapsed time
The construction extends to stopped additive observables, not just the clock.
For a state-dependent quantity \(f\), define
\[
A_f=\int_0^T f(X_t)\,dt,
\qquad
h_I^f=Gf_I.
\]
The corresponding reduced reward rate is
\[
\widehat f_R
=
\frac{f_R+Q_{RI}h_I^f}{\alpha_R},
\]
with division componentwise.
For two quantities \(f\) and \(g\), the same coupling gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[
(A_f-\widehat A_f)
(A_g-\widehat A_g)]
=
\sum_{i\in I}
\eta_i\left(f_i h_i^g+g_i h_i^f\right).
}
\]
This supplies a joint error-covariance matrix for several accumulated quantities using the same eliminated-block factorization.
For example, a specified model could use \(f\) to count time spent in an undesirable configuration or accumulated exposure to a state-dependent degradation rate. This extension was checked on exact rational test cases, including signed rewards, but was not demonstrated on a physical multi-observable benchmark. It does not preserve arbitrary path functionals or the complete state distribution.
4. Test on published physical-model data
Input and scope
I used the Wales group’s expanded LJ38 PATHSAMPLE tutorial database, containing 64 minima and 70 saddle rows. The supplied source and target are minima 8 and 2. The rate-relevant energies, Hessian information and symmetry factors were read from the repository data.    
After identifying the source–target connected component, the tested model had 47 states and 57 undirected state pairs. The other 17 minima were disconnected from this task; removing them was not an achievement of the proposed reduction.
Rates followed the harmonic transition-state convention implemented in PyGT, including the Hessian and symmetry prefactors. Parallel saddle contributions were summed. 
This is a small, incomplete tutorial network for an established atomic-cluster potential—not experimental kinetic data, the complete LJ38 landscape, or a demonstrated prediction for industrial chemistry.

5. The challenges that stopped the breakthrough claim
A stronger ordinary representation wins this benchmark
Comparing only against poorly chosen retained states would have produced a misleading success story.
For a stable spectral representation
\[
S(t)=\Pr(T>t)
=
\sum_j c_j e^{-\lambda_jt},
\]
discarding a set of modes \(J\) gives the elementary bound
\[
\sup_{t\ge0}|S(t)-S_{\mathrm{truncated}}(t)|
\le
\sum_{j\in J}|c_j|.
\]
On the temperature-\(0.2\) benchmark, retaining six spectral modes left omitted absolute coefficient mass of approximately \(1.94\times10^{-5}\), and produced sampled CDF error \(1.80\times10^{-5}\).
That is better than the eight-state candidate’s sampled error of \(9.68\times10^{-5}\).
Matched timings for the same 5,001 output times were:
Implemented pipeline	Median time
Full eigendecomposition, coefficient truncation and CDF evaluation	0.345 ms
Certificate-guided selection, eight-state reduction and CDF evaluation	0.658 ms


These were 50-repeat, single-threaded measurements on this container, excluding shared rate construction. They are small-problem diagnostics, not portable performance predictions.
The guarantee types also differ: the spectral truncation bound assumes sufficiently accurate eigenpairs, whereas the candidate supplies a timing-distance certificate. Neither timing includes exact certificate generation and verification. No end-to-end certified speedup was established.
A better certificate can give a worse distribution approximation
The selection objective also failed to rank actual CDF accuracy reliably.
At reduced temperature \(0.3\), retaining five states gave:
- Certificate-guided selection: sampled CDF error 0.030606.
- Original-occupation ranking: sampled CDF error 0.00295008.
The certificate-guided model had the smaller timing bound but more than ten times the sampled CDF error.
This is not a contradiction. It shows that an upper bound on one discrepancy is not an ordering of all relevant discrepancies.
Small timing error does not protect rare-tail probabilities
The most important obstruction is analytical.
Consider a system that leaves its source after
\[
X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1).
\]
It then either reaches the target directly or, with probability \(p\), enters a trap with mean residence time \(M\). Thus
\[
T=X+JY,
\]
where \(J\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\), \(Y\) is exponential with mean \(M\), and the variables are independent.
Eliminating the trap gives
\[
\widehat T=(1+pM)X,
\qquad
V=2pM^2.
\]
Choose
\[
p=M^{-3}.
\]
Then
\[
V=\frac2M\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\mathbb ET\longrightarrow1.
\]
Nevertheless, at time \(t=M\),
\[
\Pr(T>M)\sim\frac{M^{-3}}e,
\]
while
\[
\Pr(\widehat T>M)
=
\exp\!\left[-\frac{M}{1+M^{-2}}\right].
\]
The reduced model therefore misses asymptotically essentially 100% of the true survival probability at that time, even while the normalised timing-distance bound tends to zero.
This rules out the proposed certificate as a general solution to rare-tail scientific questions. Improving the greedy optimiser cannot remove that structural mismatch.
6. Resource accounting, novelty and disposition
For a fixed retained set, dense eliminated-block factorisation costs \(O(|I|^3)\), with additional right-hand-side solves and possible dense fill in the reduced generator. The implemented greedy selector uses a full Green matrix: \(O(n^3)\) preprocessing, \(O(n^2)\) memory and \(O(kn^2)\) updates for \(k\) additions.
The certificate can reuse work already needed by pGT, but it does not remove the cost of constructing that reduction. Sparse structure, conditioning, precision and fill remain part of the computational problem.
The physical input cost is even more fundamental. This work inherited the configurations, saddles and rate model. It did not discover missing reaction pathways, establish the Markov approximation, or quantify physical rate uncertainty. Algebraic certainty about a supplied generator is not certainty about the material it models.
Historical priority is also unresolved. Besides graph transformation and absorption-time supermodularity, explicit Wasserstein error bounds for Markov-chain aggregation already exist. A December 2025 preprint by Fabian Michel treats transient-distribution errors through residual operators and coarse Ricci curvature. The stopped-clock observable here differs, but a different formulation does not establish novelty. arXiv
The narrow result worth preserving is:
The exact pGT stopped-clock coupling error, its occupation–return-time formula, and its extension to joint stopped additive observables.

That is a concrete mathematical result with an executable certificate, not merely a research suggestion. Its historical first occurrence and independent external proof assessment remain open.
Programme decision: retain this result as unreviewed mathematical capital, but do not make further optimisation of this representation the principal breakthrough branch. The stronger continuation target is a reduction that controls the actual rare-tail or joint scientific quantity and beats robust existing methods on a substantially larger acquired model. This investigation shows precisely why a small bulk timing error, successful compression, and an elegant theorem are insufficient.
