# Independent gate: homogeneous hidden-simplex extension

2026-10-07. **PASS for the mathematical extension described here; implementation and historical prior are separate.** This gate is conditional on a rational positive metric being supplied or acquired independently. It does not infer that metric from an arbitrary polynomial or recognize the convexity promise.

Assume the observed rational homogeneous polynomial has f(x)=H((Tx)^2), where T^TT=M>0 is the supplied rational metric, the row rays of T are rational, and H is homogeneous polynomial degree h>=2 and strictly convex on the closed probability simplex. All face restrictions inherit strict convexity. Define F=(1/2)M^-1 grad f, D=F·grad. Then for y=Tx,

    Dy_i=y_i partial_i H(y²).

The extactic determinant det[D^k x_i] has degree d=n+(h-1)n(n-1), if nonzero, because D increases homogeneous degree by 2h-2. Every hidden rational row form divides it.

## Why the nonvanishing induction extends

Choose a vertex e_j maximizing H among simplex vertices. For i!=j and any 0<t<1, strict convexity gives

    H((1-t)e_j+t e_i)<(1-t)H(e_j)+tH(e_i)<=H(e_j).

The supporting-line inequality for a differentiable convex function gives

    partial_i H(e_j)-partial_j H(e_j)
       <=[H((1-t)e_j+t e_i)-H(e_j)]/t<0.

This is strict negativity, not merely <=0. Put r_i=y_i/y_j and rescale time by ds/dt=y_j^(2h-2). Homogeneity yields

    r_i'=r_i[partial_i H(e_j+sum r_k²e_k)
                 -partial_j H(e_j+sum r_k²e_k)].

Its linearization has strictly negative diagonal entries. Continuity gives the same forward-invariant small ratio cube and exponential convergence to zero used in the quartic proof. The log y_j equation has bounded polynomial coefficients on that cube, so y_j remains nonzero for every finite rescaled time. H(e_j) need not be positive; this does not affect the argument.

Restricting y_j=0 gives the lower-dimensional field from the homogeneous polynomial H on that face. Induction, the polynomial minor and rational first-integral contradiction therefore establish E!=0. No bound on the hidden curvature or attraction rate is supplied to the learner.

## Exact acceptance certificate on each remaining face

On the unit sphere, m=y² and H_i=partial_i H. Direct differentiation gives

    grad_S f=2y∘(grad H-(sum_i m_i H_i)1),
    Hess_S f[v,v]=4(y∘v)^T Hess H(m)(y∘v)
                    +2 sum_i v_i²[H_i-sum_k m_k H_k].

At a stationary point and for a tangent v supported on active coordinates, the second term vanishes. Since sum_i y_i v_i=0, the vector y∘v is tangent to the active simplex face. Convexity makes the first term nonnegative. If at least two coordinates are active, a nonzero such sphere tangent exists. Thus a stationary point with NEGATIVE-DEFINITE tangent Hessian must be an axis, even when strict convexity is not strong convexity and some interior Hessian directions are zero.

At a maximizing vertex, the transverse sphere-Hessian entries are 2(H_i(e_j)-H_j(e_j))<0, so an acceptable nondegenerate maximum always exists on each remaining face. After extracting candidates from a line, exact restricted stationarity and exact negative definiteness can therefore serve as stage acceptance tests. Under the promise no bad line can make these tests accept a non-axis. A good line supplies all true axes, so the face-score maximum passes. The same argument repeats after exact metric-orthogonal restriction.

This gives promised-class Las Vegas axis acquisition. It does not supply an unconditional certificate that an arbitrary candidate H is strictly convex. The quartic algorithm's final positive weighted-square certificate is a stronger problem-specific verifier and does not automatically generalize.

## Bit complexity and boundaries

With degree included as a parameter, d=n+(h-1)n(n-1), a grid of polynomial size in n,d gives the same good-line probability bound. Formal ODE jets for an observed degree-(2h-1) field and univariate factorization have polynomial size/cost in n,h, coefficient bits and expanded monomial count. The repeated-derivative coefficient l1 recurrence becomes ||Dg||_1<=deg(g) C||g||_1 with degrees 1+k(2h-2), so the coefficient-height argument also remains polynomial in those parameters.

It is incorrect to call this polynomial in a binary sparse input length without qualifying the degree: an exponent h may be exponentially larger than its binary encoding. Fixed h, or a representation charging the degree, restores the intended polynomial-bit statement. The base quartic has fixed h=2 and is unaffected by this caveat.

Convexity alone is insufficient for extactic nonvanishing. H(m)=(sum_i m_i)^h is constant on the simplex; its field is radial and the n>=2 extactic determinant vanishes. Strict convexity can also be much weaker than a positive Hessian lower bound: for n=2, H(m)=(m_1-m_2)^4 is strictly convex on the simplex, but its Hessian there vanishes at m_1=m_2. Its sphere polynomial is (y_1²-y_2²)^4 and its extactic is -8 y_1 y_2(y_1²-y_2²)^3, which is nonzero and has repeated extraneous linear factors. The non-axis middle stationary points have zero tangent curvature; the negative-definite acceptance test correctly rejects them.

That explicit example checks the constants, degree formula, repeated-factor mechanism and need for exact stage acceptance without adding a hidden strong-convexity assumption. No universal implementation benchmark or novelty claim follows from it.
