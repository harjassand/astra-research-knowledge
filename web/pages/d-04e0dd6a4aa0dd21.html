# Inverse design by calibrated phase-hull margins

## Verdict

The strongest defensible result is a conditional, group-calibrated certificate for thermodynamic stability margins over a fixed finite phase panel. It combines the signed convex-hull margin with a split-conformal score taken as the *maximum* margin error over every candidate in a whole chemical system. The order-statistic argument gives finite-sample, simultaneous coverage for all candidates in a new exchangeable system, so a designer can select a winner after seeing the predictions without an additional multiplicity penalty.

This is a reconstructed connection between standard convex-hull optimization and standard conformal prediction, not a priority-cleared new theorem. The affine-error cancellation and interval LP certificate below are elementary consequences of mass balance and robust linear optimization. The potentially useful research question is whether reaction/hull-margin calibration yields tighter, honestly covered certificates than phasewise error intervals or Bayesian hull uncertainty at the same expensive-calculation budget. No materials dataset benchmark was run, so no improvement is claimed.

The guarantee is only for the declared candidate phases, thermodynamic target, and system population. It cannot certify an omitted polymorph, an unsearched composition, a finite-temperature Gibbs hull from zero-temperature energies, or experimental synthesizability without additional evidence.

## Exact interface and result

Fix a thermodynamic condition and a finite list of phases in a chemical system. Each phase $j$ has a normalized composition vector $x_j\in\mathbb{Q}^q$ and a per-atom energy $e_j\in\mathbb{R}$. The declared panel includes every allowed competitor for the claim being made. For each non-elemental target phase $i$, include elemental reference phases so its composition can be expressed as a mixture of competitors, and let

\[
\Lambda_i=\{\lambda\ge 0:\;\mathbf 1^\top\lambda=1,\;X_{-i}\lambda=x_i\}.
\]

Here the coordinates of $\lambda$ are over all panel phases other than $i$. Define the signed decomposition margin

\[
d_i(e)=e_i-\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_i}\lambda^\top e_{-i}
      =\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda_i}(e_i-\lambda^\top e_{-i}).
\]

Thus $d_i\le0$ means the phase is on or below the lower hull of its competitors; $d_i>0$ means at least one mass-balanced mixture is lower. This signed margin is the stability quantity used by Bartel et al. (2020), with the sign chosen so stable phases have negative margin and unstable phases have positive margin.

Let $\hat e_j$ be outputs from a frozen surrogate and $\hat d_i=d_i(\hat e)$. Let $T_g$ be the set of non-elemental candidate targets in system $g$; elemental phases remain competitors, not scored targets. For a calibration chemical system $g$, with reference energies $e_{gj}$, compute

\[
S_g=\max_{i\in T_g}|d_{gi}(e_g)-d_{gi}(\hat e_g)|.
\]

The maximum is over all non-elemental target candidates in the panel. From $n$ calibration systems, sort scores as $S_{(1)}\le\cdots\le S_{(n)}$, set $k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\delta)\rceil$, and use $q_\delta=S_{(k)}$ when $k\le n$; otherwise set $q_\delta=+\infty$.

**Conditional certificate theorem.** Assume model fitting, model selection, candidate generation, and $\delta$ are fixed without using calibration or test reference labels. The $n$ calibration systems and the next system must be exchangeable as whole systems under the same procedure. Then

\[
\Pr\!\left[\max_{i\in T_{n+1}}|d_{n+1,i}-\hat d_{n+1,i}|\le q_\delta\right]\ge1-\delta.
\]

On this event, simultaneously for every non-elemental candidate target in the new panel:

- certify stable relative to that panel if \(\hat d_i+q_\delta\le0\);
- certify unstable relative to that panel if \(\hat d_i-q_\delta>0\);
- otherwise return UNKNOWN.

Because coverage is simultaneous over the entire panel, any candidate may be selected using the predicted margins or an additional predicted property without invalidating the stability coverage. The theorem does not certify that the selected candidate maximizes a second property; that needs its own valid uncertainty interval and a dominance comparison.

**Proof.** The $n+1$ whole-system scores are exchangeable. The rank of the test score among them is uniform up to ties, so the stated order statistic has marginal coverage at least $k/(n+1)\ge1-\delta$ (or the $+\infty$ case is vacuous). On the coverage event, $|d_i-\hat d_i|\le q_\delta$ for every scored target. The two sign tests follow immediately. This is the standard split-conformal rank proof applied to a group-maximum score; no distributional model for the scores is assumed.

### Mass-balance cancellation

Suppose energy errors have the form $e_j-\hat e_j=x_j^\top\beta+r_j$, where $\beta$ is any shared, otherwise unbounded elemental/composition-linear offset and $r_j$ is a non-affine residual. For every decomposition $\lambda\in\Lambda_i$,

\[
(e_i-\lambda^\top e_{-i})-(\hat e_i-\lambda^\top\hat e_{-i})
=r_i-\lambda^\top r_{-i},
\]

because \(x_i=X_{-i}\lambda\). Therefore absolute, composition-linear reference shifts cancel exactly from each balanced reaction and from every hull margin. If all relevant residuals satisfy \(|r_j|\le\varepsilon\), then \(|d_i(e)-d_i(\hat e)|\le2\varepsilon\): for every mixture, the candidate residual contributes at most \(\varepsilon\), while a convex combination of competitor residuals contributes at most \(\varepsilon\); taking maxima preserves the bound.

This explains why marginal MAE in formation energies is not the right certificate statistic. The useful calibration target is the reaction-balanced hull-margin error. This cancellation does not remove structure-dependent or non-affine model error.

## Deterministic robust-interval version

For one fixed finite panel, suppose each phase has a residual interval $r_j\in[a_j,b_j]$ after removing a common affine composition error. Let $h_j$ be predicted energy and $e_j=h_j+x_j^\top\beta+r_j$. In the following LPs, $j$ ranges over competitor phases. The exact box-robust tests are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{robustly stable: } &h_i+b_i\le\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_i}\sum_j\lambda_j(h_j+a_j),\\
\text{robustly unstable: } &h_i+a_i>\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_i}\sum_j\lambda_j(h_j+b_j).
\end{aligned}
\]

Each right-hand side is one linear program. In the stable condition the target takes its highest allowed energy and every competitor its lowest; in the unstable condition a single decomposition witness remains lower even when the target is at its lowest and the competitors at their highest. If neither inequality holds, report UNKNOWN. This is an exact robust counterpart for independent interval residuals; it is not a probabilistic guarantee and it is not a new convex-hull algorithm.

For a finite candidate library, an inverse-design routine can first remove candidates robustly unstable under the desired model/fidelity, then optimize the separate target property only over robustly stable candidates. A guarantee of global optimality requires simultaneous objective-property intervals and a comparison of the selected candidate's lower bound with the upper bounds for every other feasible candidate.

## Model versus nature, and completeness boundary

The target energy must be named. For PBE/DFT at zero temperature, the theorem certifies the PBE hull on the declared structure panel. For r²SCAN it certifies the r²SCAN hull on its declared phase panel. For experiment, calibration labels must be measured Gibbs free energies or another declared experimental target at matched temperature, pressure, and synthesis conditions. DFT reference labels do not become experimental truth through conformal calibration.

Even exact energies and zero residual do not solve candidate-set completeness. In a binary system with endpoint energies 0, let the enumerated phases contain a target at composition $x_B=1/2$, energy $-0.10$ eV/atom. It is stable relative to the two endpoints. An omitted $A_2B$ phase at $x_B=1/3$, energy $-0.30$ eV/atom, changes the result: a mixture of $3/4\,A_2B+1/4\,B$ has $x_B=1/2$ and energy $-0.225$ eV/atom, so the target is unstable by $0.125$ eV/atom. The included-panel certificate is correct and the global-stability inference is false. A proof of global stability needs exhaustive structural/composition search or a valid lower bound on every omitted phase.

Zero-temperature hull membership also does not establish finite-temperature phase stability or synthesis. Configurational, vibrational and magnetic free energies, pressure, kinetic access, defects, disorder, and process conditions may change the relevant target.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

- The Materials Project defines energy above hull from formation energies and compositions, notes that the common calculation is generally a zero-temperature DFT prediction, and warns that precision and functional accuracy can affect the result: [Materials Project glossary](https://docs.materialsproject.org/frequently-asked-questions/glossary-of-terms).
- Bartel et al. define a signed decomposition enthalpy relative to the hull with the target removed, explicitly explaining its “distance below” meaning for stable phases. On 85,014 Materials Project entries, formation energy and decomposition energy are effectively uncorrelated; the reported mean absolute decomposition energy is much smaller than the formation-energy scale. They also report poor stability classification from composition-only formation-energy models, including very high false-positive rates on sparse chemical spaces: [npj Computational Materials (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00362-y). This is the strongest prior-art warning against replacing hull margins with formation-energy MAE.
- A DFT-correction uncertainty study samples phase-energy distributions and reconstructs hulls 50,000 times, while explicitly stating that some error sources (including exchange-correlation functional error) are omitted and that phase errors need not be independent: [Scientific Reports (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-94550-5). Its Sc–W–O example includes 25 WO\(_3\) phases within a 6.5 meV/atom hull uncertainty and an experimentally stable Sc\(_2\)(WO\(_4\))\(_3\) predicted just above the nominal hull.
- Convex hull-aware active learning (CAL) already places uncertainty on the whole hull and selects energy evaluations by estimated expected information gain, using Gaussian-process posterior samples over finite phase-composition grids. It directly addresses the global nature of hull uncertainty and is the strongest algorithmic comparator: [Novick et al., Materials Horizons (2024), arXiv:2402.15582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15582). CAL is Bayesian and query-efficient; the proposed conformal margin certificate instead aims for a finite-sample validity statement across held-out systems and a finite declared panel. A fair comparison must equalize high-fidelity calculation budgets and phase panels.
- GNoME combines large-scale candidate generation, learned filtering, DFT evaluation, and iterative retraining; its predictions are relative to existing competitors and some nominally stable phases can be displaced by later discoveries. It reports 11 meV/atom prediction error and hit-rate metrics, higher-fidelity r²SCAN checks, and 736 experimental matches: [Nature (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06735-9). This is evidence of scale and pipeline performance, not a completeness proof.
- Tahmassebpur et al.'s June 2026 v2 preprint, titled *A geometric basis for materials families in inorganic solids*, fits a seven-facet max-affine representation to 51,068 near-hull, one-lowest-structure-per-composition entries (one to five elements; within 25 meV/atom of the Materials Project hull). It reports 76 meV/atom held-out MAE under a random 80/20 split, states that more facets are needed for the exact hull, and describes unknown structure search as remaining combinatorial: [arXiv:2602.00254v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00254v2). The 76 meV/atom statistic is not a uniform error bound, and the random split is not a leave-one-chemical-system-out calibration. It cannot alone support candidate-level hull certificates.
- The finite-sample rank guarantee is inherited from standard split conformal inference, whose guarantee is marginal under exchangeability: [Lei et al., JASA (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2017.1307116). Taking a maximum score within a group provides simultaneous-within-group coverage by the same rank argument. This application is not priority-cleared; hierarchical/group conformal validity is established statistical machinery.

The strongest baseline is therefore two-part: CAL for efficient placement of expensive phase-energy queries, and a frozen independent-system holdout for coverage auditing. The fairer statistical comparator to the proposed score is phasewise conformal intervals plus a simultaneous correction and interval-hull LP. The proposed group maximum score may improve efficiency by calibrating the final hull margin directly and canceling common affine energy offsets, but it is not guaranteed to produce narrower bounds.

## Costs, falsifier, and next gate

**Acquired inputs:** the candidate phase panel and compositions; surrogate energies; high-fidelity or experimental reference energies for calibration systems; and a defensible rule for candidate generation. If the output concerns nature, uncertainty in the surrogate relative to nature must be represented by the calibration labels, not inferred from DFT self-consistency.

**Computation:** for $m$ phases in one system, computing hull margins or robust intervals requires convex-hull/LP work over $m$ phases and $q$ composition coordinates. Rational-input LP admits polynomial bit-complexity algorithms; a production certificate should retain primal/dual witnesses and roundoff bounds. The rank statistic costs $O(n\log n)$ after $n$ system scores. High-fidelity phase calculations dominate. Group calibration needs reference coverage of each whole panel; for 95% marginal coverage, at least 19 calibration systems are needed even to use a finite order statistic. That is only a minimum for nonvacuity, not enough to guarantee a useful threshold.

**Strongest test:** freeze one model, candidate generator, structure-search budget and thermodynamic target. Hold out complete chemical systems, stratified by family before fitting. Compare (1) nominal hull-margin screening, (2) independent phasewise conformal intervals plus an exact interval-hull LP, (3) the system-maximum hull-margin conformal certificate, and (4) CAL under the same number of expensive DFT calculations. Evaluate on a second fidelity (such as r²SCAN) and on experimentally characterized phases where available. Report simultaneous system-level coverage of all predicted stable/unstable labels, number of candidates certified, false-stable rate, high-fidelity calls, and failed/unknown cases.

**Falsifiers:** the $1-\delta$ coverage falls below its promised marginal level on exchangeability-credible held-out systems; the proposed $q_\delta$ does not yield more certified candidates than phasewise conformal/robust LP at equal cost; or reaction-space residual scores are no smaller than phasewise margins. Any omitted phase that changes the hull invalidates a global interpretation while leaving the finite-panel result intact. The highest-risk premise is system exchangeability: periodic-table chemistry and deliberate candidate selection are not naturally IID.
