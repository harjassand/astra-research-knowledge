# N33 to N35: level-set thickening, rational acquisition, and the exact safety boundary

## Verdict

For the internally reconstructed N33 proof, the deterministic-to-stochastic bridge works at the level of existence: its finite affine-minimum Lyapunov function can be eroded slightly below its maximum plateau to produce a compact positive polytope whose relative facets point strictly inward for every instantaneous rate choice. Strictness survives rational approximation, so with rational reaction data, rational rate bounds, and a rational stoichiometric class, a dovetailed rational-certificate search with an exact polynomial-inequality verifier can eventually acquire a certificate. This does not give a useful search bound or an efficient compiler.

The bare statement “there is a common compact absorbing set” is not itself an acquired certificate. The load-bearing extra input is the proof-level strict active-slope drift in N33. Once a strict collar certificate and a buffered lattice start are supplied or acquired, N35 gives exponentially small exit probability up to exponentially long but finite horizons. It gives no infinite-time survival result and no safe stochastic entrance from an arbitrary point of an unbounded class.

## Independent bridge by thickening the deterministic plateau

Fix one integer-complex endotactic network, a positive class \(P=(c+S)\cap\mathbb R^d_{>0}\), and the pointwise rate box \(k_e\in[\kappa,K]\), with \(0<\kappa\le K<\infty\). The N33 construction supplies, at a sufficiently small fixed \(h\), a finite concave piecewise-affine function

\[
 F_h(x)=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda} L_{\lambda,h}(x),\qquad
 L_{\lambda,h}(x)=r_\lambda\cdot x+b_\lambda(h),\qquad
 M_h=F_h(c),
\]

whose maximum plateau \(K_h=\{F_h=M_h\}\) is compact and lies in the positive box \(B_h=[h,h^{-1}]^d\). Its load-bearing estimate is pointwise in the rate box: whenever \(x\in B_h\cap P\setminus K_h\), every active label has \(r_\lambda\notin S^\perp\) and

\[
 r_\lambda\cdot f(x,k)\ge \delta_h:=\kappa Hh^M>0
 \quad\text{for every }k\in[\kappa,K]^R,
 \qquad f(x,k)=\sum_e k_e x^{y_e}\nu_e.
\]

Here \(H>0\) is the finite projected-sign margin of the fixed slope family, \(M=\max_e\|y_e\|_1\), and the chosen scale also obeys \(KLh^H\le\kappa H\), where \(L=\sum_e\|\nu_e\|_1\). These are the N33 weighted essential-source bounds, not a consequence of compactness of \(K_h\) alone.

Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) small and define the eroded-domain boundary

\[
 Q_\varepsilon=P\cap\{x:F_h(x)\ge M_h-\varepsilon\}
       =P\cap\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
          \{x:L_{\lambda,h}(x)-(M_h-\varepsilon)\ge0\}.
\]

This is a polytope because it is a finite intersection of halfspaces. It contains \(K_h\cap P\). For small enough \(\varepsilon\), it remains in \(B_h\subset\mathbb R^d_{>0}\): the plateau is compactly contained in the interior of \(B_h\), and the nested polyhedra \(\{F_h\ge M_h-\varepsilon\}\) decrease to the bounded plateau as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). Its recession cone is the plateau's recession cone, hence trivial. The level-set argument also gives invariance before rationalization: on \(Q_\varepsilon\setminus K_h\), every active label has positive derivative, so \(F_h(x(t))\) cannot cross downward through \(M_h-\varepsilon\); on \(K_h\), the source proof already gives forward invariance. The strict facet calculation below is the stronger local statement needed for N35.

At any relative boundary point \(x\in\partial_P Q_\varepsilon\), \(F_h(x)=M_h-\varepsilon<M_h\). If a label \(\lambda\) defines an active facet there, then \(L_{\lambda,h}(x)=F_h(x)\). It cannot have \(r_\lambda\in S^\perp\), since such a label is constant on \(P\) and has value \(L_{\lambda,h}(c)\ge M_h\). Thus the facet normal \(r_\lambda|_S\) has inward derivative at least \(\delta_h\) for every pointwise rate choice. This is a strict statement on every relative facet, including tied facets.

For each proper relative facet, the worst-case projected drift \(x\mapsto\min_{k\in[\kappa,K]^R}a_i\cdot f(x,k)\) is continuous and is at least \(\delta_h\) on the compact face. Compactness therefore gives a positive inward collar and, for example, a lower margin \(\gamma_i<\delta_h\) on that collar. This step makes no assumption about a uniform collar across different classes or networks. A quantitative collar is also available after fixing the polytope: if the worst-case drift has Lipschitz bound \(L_i\) on \(Q\), and \(\operatorname{dist}(x,F_i)\le C_i g_i(x)\) is a facial polytope error bound for facet \(F_i\), then rational \(0<\ell_i\le\delta_h/(2L_iC_i)\) gives \(\gamma_i=\delta_h/2\). The constants can be bounded from the rational facet data and polynomial derivative bounds.

### Rationalization and what “acquisition” costs

The level-set polytope initially has real coefficients because the N33 affine labels and scale need not be rational. When \(c+S\), the rate bounds, and the reaction complexes have exact rational descriptions, sufficiently small rational perturbations of the finite facet data preserve positivity, the interior point \(c\), and the strict collar inequalities; boundedness persists because the recession cone is trivial. Select rational lower bounds for \(\ell_i,\gamma_i\) and rational coordinate bounds \(U_j\). Relative class coordinates can be chosen rationally because \(S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\nu_e\}\) has an integer basis.

This yields a theoretical acquisition procedure, not a practical complexity result. Enumerate rational relative polytopes, rational collar widths and margins, and rational coordinate bounds. For each candidate, verify compactness and positive containment and decide

\[
 \forall x\in Q:\quad 0\le g_i(x)\le\ell_i
       \Longrightarrow \min_{k\in[\kappa,K]^R}a_i\cdot f(x,k)\ge\gamma_i
\]

for every relative facet. For an independent rectangular rate box, the minimum over \(k\) is obtained by choosing \(\kappa\) on terms with \(a_i\cdot\nu_e\ge0\) and \(K\) on terms with \(a_i\cdot\nu_e<0\); the quantifier over all measurable schedules is thereby discharged pointwise. With integer complexes and rational input, the remaining checks are polynomial inequalities over a compact rational polytope and can be decided by exact real-closed-field quantifier elimination. Strict existence implies that some enumerated candidate passes, so the unbounded search terminates on these promised inputs.

Every part of that search is charged: the rational description of conserved totals, the rate bounds, facet count, coefficient bit heights, reaction order, dimension \(s=\dim S\), and each exact verifier call. The N33 proof gives no usable bound on \(h,H,|\Lambda|,\varepsilon\), collar widths, or rational certificate height. Generic quantifier elimination may be prohibitive; no implementation of that general verifier or end-to-end synthesis run is claimed here. If rate endpoints or class totals are supplied only as unverified real values, exact rational acquisition does not follow; certified rational enclosures or an exact algebraic representation must be charged.

The online all-rates claim is exactly: for every measurable \(k_e(t)\in[\kappa,K]\), the deterministic inequality holds almost everywhere because it holds for every pointwise vector \(k\). A controller that sets rates to zero, uses a different rate set, or switches among modes is covered only if the same collar inequalities are separately verified for every allowed mode. The positive lower rate bound in N33 cannot be silently dropped.

## Finite-volume jump consequence and all charged corrections

Let \(y_e,y'_e\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d\), \(\nu_e=y'_e-y_e\), and \(Q=\{x\in c+S:g_i(x)=a_i\cdot x+b_i\ge0,\ 1\le i\le m\}\) be an acquired compact relative polytope with \(Q\subset (0,U_1]\times\cdots\times(0,U_d]\). Remove inequalities constant on \(c+S\); they have \(a_i\cdot\nu_e=0\) and are not stochastic facets. Require rational \(\ell_i,\gamma_i>0\) such that for all \(x\in Q\) with \(0\le g_i(x)\le\ell_i\), and every admissible rate vector,

\[
 a_i\cdot f(x,k)\ge\gamma_i.
\]

For integer volume \(V\), molecule counts \(Z^V\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d\), and concentrations \(X^V=Z^V/V\), use jumps \(\nu_e/V\) with predictable intensity

\[
 V k_e(t,\mathrm{past})\phi_{y_e}^V(X^V_{t-}),\qquad
 \phi_y^V(x)=V^{-|y|}\prod_j(Vx_j)_{y_j}.
\]

The \(k_e\) may depend on time, past, and independent randomness but must stay in the acquired rate set and be predictable. Ordinary molecule counts require integer nonnegative complexes; the real/negative-exponent extension in N33 is not itself a jump model. The propensity convention matters: here factorial divisions are absorbed into \(k_e\).

For all lattice states in the box, including insufficient-reactant states,

\[
 0\le x^y-\phi_y^V(x)\le E_y(U)/V,\quad
 E_y(U)=\sum_{j:y_j\ge2}\binom{y_j}{2}U_j^{y_j-1}
                         \prod_{l\ne j}U_l^{y_l}.
\]

Thus define

\[
 D_i=K\sum_e|a_i\cdot\nu_e|E_{y_e}(U),\quad
 J_i=\max_e|a_i\cdot\nu_e|,\quad
 B_i=K\sum_e(a_i\cdot\nu_e)^2U^{y_e},\quad
 \Lambda=K\sum_eU^{y_e}.
\]

For

\[
 V\ge V_0:=\max\left\{1,\max_i\left\lceil\frac{2D_i}{\gamma_i}\right\rceil\right\},
 \quad \mu_i=\gamma_i/2,\quad
 \theta_i=\min\{J_i^{-1},\mu_i/(3B_i)\},\quad
 c_*:=\min_i\theta_i\ell_i,
\]

the finite-volume inward drift in each collar is at least \(\mu_i\). The \(J_i^{-1}\) restriction controls one-jump overshoot, and \(B_i\) controls projected quadratic jump variation. Let \(d_i=g_i(X_0)>0\) be fixed concentration gaps for a supplied lattice-compatible start. Stopped exponential-Dynkin estimates give, for every finite \(T\),

\[
 \Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le
 \sum_{i=1}^m e^{-\theta_iVd_i}
       +2mV\Lambda T e^{-c_*V}.
\]

Consequently for any \(0<\alpha<c_*\),

\[
 \Pr(\tau_Q\le e^{\alpha V})\le
 \sum_i e^{-\theta_iVd_i}
       +2mV\Lambda e^{-(c_*-\alpha)V}.
\]

The horizon is exponential in volume while the second term decays exponentially only below exponent \(c_*\); the constants deteriorate as the certified collar widths or inward margins shrink. A rational start \(x_*\in\operatorname{relint}Q\) gives lattice starts \(Z_0^V=Vx_*\) on denominator-multiple integer volumes. For a prescribed start, verify \(Z_0^V\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d\), membership in the correct conserved affine class and reaction-lattice coset, and a fixed lower gap \(d_i\). Coordinate rounding can violate conservation or the integer reaction lattice. A one-jump-scale gap \(O(1/V)\) does not yield an exponentially small first term.

On the stopped region, total activity is at most \(V\Lambda\), which suffices for the finite-horizon estimate without asserting global nonexplosion after exit. An exact simulator may stop at exit; its expected candidate-event work up to \(T\) is bounded by \(V\Lambda T\), itself exponential when \(T=e^{\alpha V}\). This excludes rate-controller evaluation costs: a supplied predictable control may be computationally unbounded or noncomputable, and an executable application needs a finite controller evaluator. This is a safety theorem, not an efficient long-horizon simulator or a physical energy budget.

The class dimension is \(s=\dim S\). The facets and collars are relative to the \(s\)-dimensional affine class; in ambient dimension \(d\), \(Q\) may have empty interior. When \(s=0\), all \(\nu_e=0\), there is no jump motion and safety is trivial; the facet formulas are unnecessary. No constants are uniform across different stoichiometric classes without a separate uniform margin/acquisition theorem.

## Exact bounded check

Use the reversible first-order network \(A\rightleftarrows B\), rates in \([1,4]\), and class \(x_A+x_B=1\). It is endotactic and \(s=1\). For \(x=x_A\),

\[
 \dot x=k_B(1-x)-k_Ax.
\]

Take \(Q=[1/10,9/10]\), \(g_L=x-1/10\), \(g_R=9/10-x\), and collar width \(\ell=1/20\). In the left collar \(x\in[1/10,3/20]\),

\[
 \min_{k_A,k_B\in[1,4]}\dot x=(1-x)-4x=1-5x\ge1/4.
\]

In the right collar \(x\in[17/20,9/10]\),

\[
 \min_{k_A,k_B\in[1,4]}(-\dot x)=x-4(1-x)=5x-4\ge1/4.
\]

These exact rational inequalities are uniform over arbitrary measurable rate paths. The finite PL certificate \(F=\min\{0,x_A-1/10,9/10-x_A\}\) was also passed through the existing exact rational checker, which returned `CERTIFIED`, relative equation \(x_A+x_B=1\), and exterior active-label drift lower bound \(1/2\ge1/4\). For even \(V\), the example starts at counts \((V/2,V/2)\), giving fixed gaps \(d_L=d_R=2/5\). Reproduction is in [check_one_link.py](check_one_link.py); the checker and its status limits are in `work/agents/systems_metabolism/stochastic_safety/evidence/reaction_certificate.py`.

For this first-order example \(E_y(U)=0\), so \(D_i=0\) and \(V_0=1\). With \(U_A=U_B=1\), \(J_i=1\), \(B_i=8\), \(\Lambda=8\), \(\gamma_i=1/4\), \(\theta_i=1/192\), and \(c_*=1/3840\). Starting at \(x_A=x_B=1/2\) gives \(d_L=d_R=2/5\). Therefore the generic bound yields

\[
 \Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le2e^{-V/480}+32VT e^{-V/3840},
\]

and at \(T=e^{V/7680}\),

\[
 \Pr(\tau_Q\le e^{V/7680})\le2e^{-V/480}+32V e^{-V/7680}.
\]

These conservative constants are positive and explicit. They are not optimized.

## Decisive scope counterexample and unresolved gates

At constant rates \(k_A=k_B=1\), the same finite-volume process is a finite irreducible birth-death chain on \(N_A\in\{0,1,\ldots,V\}\), with transitions \(j\to j-1\) at rate \(j\) and \(j\to j+1\) at rate \(V-j\). It visits both endpoints almost surely. Starting at \(V/2\), it therefore exits \(Q=[1/10,9/10]\) eventually with probability one, despite the common deterministic absorber and strict collar. This refutes infinite-time stochastic safety as a consequence of N33; it does not refute N35's finite-horizon bound.

Remaining gates are external review of the N33 reconstruction, a runnable general rational CAD/QE or interval-verification acquisition pipeline, and application-specific verification of the count coset, buffered initial state, propensity normalization, and rate-controller envelope. The exact finite check above proves only that one network/certificate instance passes its scoped arithmetic checks.
