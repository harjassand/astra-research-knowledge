# N25 — Half-space edge law, full-fault constrained coercivity, and the zero mobility gap

Status: internally derived explicit heterogeneous model plus a weighted-spectrum obstruction. The construction proves that a normalized constrained Hessian can remain coercive when perturbations change the active support. It also proves that the continuum similarity flow has no positive weighted decay gap at a nonsingular half-space tip. It does not prove a nonlinear continuum stable-manifold theorem, a global arrest/runaway separator, or an earthquake forecast.

## Exact target and scope

This note answers the N25 support request against `work/CAMPAIGN_CONTRACT.txt` and the frozen model in `work/natural/n08_fault_nucleation/v1.txt` and `v2.txt`. The model is the quasi-static aging-law/rate-and-state reduction already stated there. The only new construction below is a one-dimensional anti-plane half-space fault with a bounded finite fault interval, positive heterogeneous direct-effect coefficient, constant positive evolution coefficient, and an exact compact similarity profile.

There are two distinct continuum questions. The static free-boundary profile can be stable under the normalized mass constraint even when its support is allowed to move. The dynamical similarity generator can nevertheless have stable rates accumulating at zero because the mobility vanishes at the support edge. A coercive static Hessian therefore does not supply the spectral gap used by the finite-dimensional nonlinear stable-manifold argument in N08.

All statements are model statements. Coefficients below are manufactured to make the profile exact. They are not inferred from a real fault, and no earthquake prediction follows.

## Half-space operator and nonsingular edge expansion

Use the Fourier convention \hat{K u}(\xi)=\kappa|\xi|\widehat u(\xi), with \(\kappa>0\). On the real line,

\[
|D|u(x)=\frac1\pi\,\operatorname{PV}\!\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{(x-y)^2}\,dy.
\]

If \(u\) is supported in \([-L,L]\), then for \(x=L+d\), \(d>0\),

\[
K u(L+d)=-\frac\kappa\pi\int_0^{2L}\frac{U(s)}{(s+d)^2}\,ds,
\qquad U(s)=u(L-s).
\]

Suppose the nonsingular-tip condition gives

\[
U(s)=A s^{3/2}+o(s^{3/2}),\qquad A>0.
\]

The integral \(I(0)=\int_0^{2L}U(s)s^{-2}ds\) is finite. Subtracting the values at \(d\) and zero and setting \(s=dt\) in the leading edge term gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(d)-I(0)
&=A\sqrt d\int_0^\infty t^{3/2}\big((1+t)^{-2}-t^{-2}\big)dt+o(\sqrt d),\\
\int_0^\infty t^{3/2}\big((1+t)^{-2}-t^{-2}\big)dt
&=2\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{z^4}{(1+z^2)^2}-1\right)dz\\
&=-2\int_0^\infty\frac{2z^2+1}{(1+z^2)^2}dz=-\frac{3\pi}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The replacement of the upper limit \(2L/d\) by infinity has an \(O(d)\) error. The \(o(s^{3/2})\) remainder contributes \(o(\sqrt d)\), by splitting the integral at a fixed small edge distance and then using the uniform smallness of the remainder there. Consequently,

\[
K u(L+d)=K u(L+)+\frac{3\kappa A}{2}\sqrt d+o(\sqrt d). \tag{1}
\]

For an all-\(W<1\) profile, the active profile equation is \(K(D_cW)=\sigma(bW-a)\). If \(a,\sigma,b\) have finite edge limits and \(W\to0\), continuity of the tip stress gives \(K(D_cW)(L+)=-\sigma(L)a(L)\). In the original similarity variable the linear exterior rate of a small positive tail is

\[
F_{\rm ext}(L+d)=-\frac{K(D_cW)(L+d)}{\sigma(L+d)a(L+d)}-1
=-\frac{3\kappa A}{2\sigma(L)a(L)}\sqrt d+o(\sqrt d). \tag{2}
\]

The state term in the \(W\) equation is quadratic in a small exterior \(W\), so it does not alter (2) when the memory field stays bounded. The sign is restoring, but the restoring rate tends to zero at the boundary. This is the precise edge-scale obstruction to a uniform exterior-invasion margin.

The exponent \(3/2\) is the nonsingular crack-tip branch: the usual \(d^{1/2}\) slip term carries the mode-III stress-intensity singularity outside the active interval. Its coefficient must vanish if the stress rate is required to have no tip singularity. Viesca's compact free-boundary formulation imposes that nonsingular-tip condition; see the source comparison below.

## Exact compact profile on a finite half-space fault

Let the finite fault be \(\Omega=(-R,R)\), and let \(K_\Omega\) be the restricted half-Laplacian: extend a field by zero outside \(\Omega\), apply \(\kappa|D|\) on \(\mathbb R\), and restrict the result to \(\Omega\). Take

\[
U(x)=(1-x^2)_+^{3/2}.
\]

The formula for \(|D|U\) can be derived directly. On \((-1,1)\), \(U'(y)=-3y\sqrt{1-y^2}\), and \(|D|=\mathcal H\partial_x\), where \(\mathcal H f(x)=\pi^{-1}\operatorname{PV}\int f(y)/(x-y)dy\). The semicircle Cauchy transform is

\[
\operatorname{PV}\int_{-1}^1\frac{\sqrt{1-y^2}}{x-y}dy=\pi x,
\quad |x|<1,
\qquad \int_{-1}^1\sqrt{1-y^2}dy=\frac\pi2.
\]

Using \(y/(x-y)=x/(x-y)-1\) gives \(|D|U=3/2-3x^2\) inside. For \(x>1\), the zero-extension kernel gives

\[
|D|U(x)=-\frac1\pi\int_{-1}^1\frac{(1-y^2)^{3/2}}{(x-y)^2}dy.
\]

With \(y=\cos\theta\), the integral is \(-\pi^{-1}J'(x)\), where

\[
J(x)=\int_0^\pi\frac{\sin^4\theta}{x-\cos\theta}d\theta
=\pi\left[-x^3+\frac32x+(x^2-1)^{3/2}\right].
\]

The last equality follows by dividing \((1-t^2)^2\) by \(x-t\) and using \(\int_0^\pi(x-\cos\theta)^{-1}d\theta=\pi/\sqrt{x^2-1}\). Differentiating gives the full-line formula

\[
|D|U(x)=
\begin{cases}
\frac32-3x^2,& |x|<1,\\[2mm]
\frac32-3x^2+3|x|\sqrt{x^2-1},& |x|>1.
\end{cases} \tag{3}
\]

At the right edge, with \(d=1-x\) from inside,

\[
U(1-d)=(2d-d^2)^{3/2}=2^{3/2}d^{3/2}+O(d^{5/2}). \tag{4}
\]

Outside, (3) gives \(|D|U(1+d)=-3/2+3\sqrt2\sqrt d+O(d)\), matching (1) with \(A=2^{3/2}\). The stress remains finite at the tip and the coefficient of \(\sqrt d\) is exactly the one in (1).

Now set \(\kappa>0\), \(R=1.001\), \(\beta=1.51\), \(\epsilon=0.01\), and define

\[
w(x)=\epsilon U(x),\qquad b=\beta\kappa,\qquad \sigma=D_c=1,
\]

and the direct-effect/loading coefficient \(a(x)=c(x)\) by

\[
c(x)=\begin{cases}
\epsilon\kappa\big[\beta(1-x^2)^{3/2}-\frac32+3x^2\big],&|x|\le1,\\[1mm]
\epsilon\kappa\big[3x^2-\frac32\big],&1<|x|<R.
\end{cases} \tag{5}
\]

The inside formula is minimized at \(x=0\): as a function of \(t=x^2\), its derivative is \(3-(3/2)\beta\sqrt{1-t}>0\), since \(\beta<2\). Thus \(c\ge0.01\epsilon\kappa>0\) inside. Outside, \(c\ge1.5\epsilon\kappa>0\). The definitions match in value and first derivative at \(|x|=1\), so \(a\) is positive and \(C^1\). Its maximum is \(\epsilon\kappa(3R^2-3/2)=0.01506003\kappa<b\). Also \(0\le w\le0.01<1\). The construction obeys positive rate-and-state coefficients and remains on the strict all-\(W<1\) branch.

Write \(H=K_\Omega-\beta\kappa I\). Equations (3) and (5) give

\[
Hw+c=0\quad (|x|<1),
\qquad g:=Hw+c=3\epsilon\kappa|x|\sqrt{x^2-1}>0\quad (1<|x|<R). \tag{6}
\]

Thus \(w\) solves the exact profile equation on its active set and satisfies the obstacle/contact inequality on the rest of the finite fault. The outside residual vanishes only at the edge and has the expansion

\[
g(1+d)=3\sqrt2\epsilon\kappa\sqrt d+O(\epsilon\kappa d^{3/2}). \tag{7}
\]

The unnormalized similarity exterior rate in this heterogeneous example uses the local coefficient \(a(1+d)=\epsilon\kappa(3(1+d)^2-3/2)\). Equations (3) and (5) yield

\[
F_{\rm ext}(1+d)=-\frac{K w(1+d)}{a(1+d)}-1=-2\sqrt2\sqrt d+O(d). \tag{8}
\]

This makes the sign and the vanishing edge rate explicit, rather than inferring either from a fixed-support Hessian.

## Full-fault constrained Hessian, including support changes

The static energy on the strict weakening branch is

\[
\Pi(v)=\frac12\langle v,K_\Omega v\rangle+\int_\Omega\left(c(x)v(x)-\frac{\beta\kappa}{2}v(x)^2\right)dx,
\quad v\ge0. \tag{9}
\]

Its Hessian is \(H=K_\Omega-\beta\kappa I\). After physical amplitude normalization, write \(C=\int_\Omega c w\) and \(q_*=w/C\), so \(\int c q_*=1\). The constraint tangent is

\[
\mathcal T=\left\{v\in\mathcal V:\int_\Omega c v=0\right\},
\qquad \mathcal V=\{v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb R):v=0\text{ a.e. on }\mathbb R\setminus\Omega\}.
\]

This tangent space contains fields supported outside \((-1,1)\), so it permits infinitesimal support expansion. The constrained Hessian form is \(Q[v]=\langle v,Hv\rangle\).

First count the negative directions of \(H\). Let \(\lambda_j\) be the eigenvalues of \(K_\Omega\), in increasing order. For any orthonormal \(\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_n\) in \(L^2(\Omega)\), extended by zero, Bessel's inequality for the unitary Fourier transform gives

\[
S_n(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^n|\widehat\varphi_j(\xi)|^2\le \frac{|\Omega|}{2\pi}=\frac R\pi,
\qquad \int_{\mathbb R}S_n(\xi)d\xi=n.
\]

Among densities bounded by \(R/\pi\) with mass \(n\), the minimum of \(\int|\xi|S_n(\xi)d\xi\) fills the centered interval \([-r,r]\), where \(r=n\pi/(2R)\). Therefore

\[
\sum_{j=1}^n\langle\varphi_j,K_\Omega\varphi_j\rangle
=\kappa\int|\xi|S_n(\xi)d\xi
\ge\frac{\kappa\pi n^2}{4R}.
\]

The min-max principle gives \(\lambda_1+\lambda_2\ge\kappa\pi/R\), hence \(\lambda_2\ge\kappa\pi/(2R)=1.56922710\kappa\) at \(R=1.001\). For the opposite bound on the first eigenvalue, use \(U\) as a Rayleigh test:

\[
\|U\|_2^2=\int_{-1}^1(1-x^2)^3dx=\frac{32}{35},\qquad
\langle U,K_\Omega U\rangle=\kappa\int_{-1}^1U(\tfrac32-3x^2)dx=\frac{3\pi\kappa}{8}.
\]

Thus \(\lambda_1\le(105\pi/256)\kappa=1.28854386\kappa\). Since

\[
1.28854386<\beta=1.51<\frac{\pi}{2R}=1.56922710,
\]

\(H\) is invertible and has exactly one negative eigenvalue.

The sign of the constraint functional against \(H^{-1}\) decides whether this one negative direction disappears after imposing \(\int cv=0\). Put \(g=Hw+c\), as in (6). Then \(c=-Hw+g\), and \(w g=0\) pointwise. Self-adjointness gives the exact identity

\[
\langle c,H^{-1}c\rangle=\langle w,Hw\rangle+\langle g,H^{-1}g\rangle. \tag{10}
\]

The first term is

\[
\langle w,Hw\rangle
=\epsilon^2\kappa\frac{32}{35}\left(\frac{105\pi}{256}-\beta\right)
=-0.20247418\,\epsilon^2\kappa. \tag{11}
\]

For the second term, (6) yields

\[
\|g\|_2^2=18\epsilon^2\kappa^2\left[\frac{R^5-1}{5}-\frac{R^3-1}{3}\right]
=1.8030018\times10^{-5}\,\epsilon^2\kappa^2.
\]

The spectral bounds above imply

\[
\|H^{-1}\|_{2\to2}\le\frac1{\kappa\min\{\beta-105\pi/256,\ \pi/(2R)-\beta\}}
=\frac{16.884163}{\kappa}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\langle c,H^{-1}c\rangle
\le(-0.20247418+0.00030443)\epsilon^2\kappa
=-0.20216975\epsilon^2\kappa<0. \tag{12}
\]

Here is the constrained-inertia step. Let \(z=H^{-1}c\), so \(Q[z]=\langle c,H^{-1}c\rangle<0\). Every \(v\in\mathcal V\) decomposes uniquely as

\[
v=\frac{\langle c,v\rangle}{\langle c,H^{-1}c\rangle}z+v_0,
\qquad v_0\in\mathcal T.
\]

The two summands are \(H\)-orthogonal because \(Q(z,v_0)=\langle c,v_0\rangle=0\). The full form has exactly one negative direction and the \(z\)-line is already negative, so \(Q\) has no negative direction on \(\mathcal T\). If the restricted form had a null vector, its weak Euler equation would be \(Hv=\alpha c\); then \(v=\alpha H^{-1}c\), and \(\langle c,v\rangle=0\) together with (12) forces \(\alpha=0\). Thus the restricted form has no kernel. The restricted half-Laplacian has compact resolvent on the bounded fault interval, so its constrained form has a strictly positive lowest eigenvalue. There is a constant \(\gamma>0\), depending on this fixed construction, such that

\[
Q[v]\ge\gamma\|v\|_2^2\quad(v\in\mathcal T). \tag{13}
\]

Since \(\langle v,K_\Omega v\rangle=Q[v]+\beta\kappa\|v\|_2^2\), (13) also controls the full \(H^{1/2}\) form norm on \(\mathcal T\). The constant is not uniform over parameter families; only existence for the displayed fixed \(R,\beta,\epsilon\) is proved.

For normalized \(q_*\), the constrained first variation is \(g/C\), with \(g=0\) inside and \(g>0\) outside. If \(q=q_*+v\ge0\), \(\int cv=0\), and the profile remains on the \(W<1\) branch, then an exterior perturbation has \(v\ge0\) wherever \(q_*=0\). The exact quadratic expansion is

\[
\Pi(q)-\Pi(q_*)=\frac1C\int_{\Omega\setminus[-1,1]}g(x)v(x)dx+\frac12Q[v]
\ge\frac\gamma2\|v\|_2^2. \tag{14}
\]

This is full-fault constrained coercivity, including support-changing perturbations. In this explicit heterogeneous case the fixed-support Hessian's one-negative-direction test *does* survive amplitude normalization and free-support perturbations once the obstacle inequality and the constraint functional are checked. The conclusion is a static local energy statement on the all-\(W<1\) branch; it is not a theorem about the full memory dynamics.

There is no uniform *linear* strict-complementarity margin at the edge. Equation (7) shows \(g(x)\to0\). For a nonnegative exterior bump \(h_\delta(x)=\alpha\psi((x-1)/\delta)\), with \(\psi\in C_c^\infty((1,2))\), its added mass is proportional to \(\alpha\delta\), while its first-order constrained energy cost is proportional to \(\alpha\delta^{3/2}\). The ratio tends to zero like \(\sqrt\delta\). Thus an estimate of the form “exterior first variation \(\ge c_0\) times added tail mass” is false for every \(c_0>0\). This does not contradict (14): the full Hessian supplies a quadratic cost even when the pointwise obstacle multiplier is small.

## The constrained Hessian is coercive while the slip flow is gapless

In the normalized physical amplitude variables, \(m(x)=q_*(x)/c(x)\) is the diagonal mobility. By (4) and \(c(1)>0\), on the active side of either edge,

\[
m(1-d)=m_0d^{3/2}(1+o(1)),\qquad m_0>0. \tag{15}
\]

On the all-\(W<1\) branch the slip linearization is self-adjoint in \(\mathcal H_m=L^2((-1,1),m^{-1}dx)\), with stable decay quotient

\[
r[v]=\frac{Q[v]}{\int_{-1}^1|v|^2/m\,dx}
\]

on the normalized-mass tangent space. The static inequality (13) is an \(L^2\)/form-norm inequality; it does not bound this weighted denominator. Choose a smooth bump \(\phi\) supported in \((1,2)\), place copies in strips of width \(\delta\) at both active edges, and subtract an \(O(\delta)\) smooth interior correction so that \(\int cv_\delta=0\). Symmetric edge copies are orthogonal to the translation mode. Scaling of the half-Laplacian form gives

\[
Q[v_\delta]=Q_0+o(1),\quad Q_0>0,
\qquad
\|v_\delta\|_{\mathcal H_m}^2
=m_0^{-1}\delta^{-1/2}\left(2\int_1^2\phi(s)^2s^{-3/2}ds\right)(1+o(1)).
\]

Therefore

\[
0\le r[v_\delta]=O(\sqrt\delta)\longrightarrow0. \tag{16}
\]

No positive weighted spectral gap can hold after removing the amplitude/time mode and the translation mode. The memory block decays at rate \(1-W^*\to1\) at the edge, and its slip coupling is proportional to \((W^*)^2=O(d^3)\); neither changes the edge sequence (16). The normalized exterior KKT residual (7) also produces a vanishing tail decay rate. Thus static support coercivity is compatible with arbitrarily slow continuum relaxation.

For the exact multiplicative physical flow, points with \(q=0\) stay zero at every finite normalized time. Support extension is therefore a singular limiting question: positive exterior tails can decay toward the compact profile, but a zero exterior field cannot become positive in finite time. This is another reason the free-boundary shape directions are not ordinary eigenfunctions of the fixed-active-set generator.

The loss of a gap refutes the direct transfer of the finite-dimensional exponential stable-manifold/Lyapunov-radius proof to this continuum profile. It does not refute nonlinear attraction or a slower algebraic basin. A full-memory nonlinear continuum basin with controlled positive \((V,\theta)\), existence/uniqueness for the moving-support flow, and physical time modulation remain open here.

## Appended supplement — explicit constrained coercivity constant and sign correction

This supplement sharpens the existence-only conclusion in (13) to a numerical lower bound for the same full-fault tangent space. It also corrects one sign in the intermediate exterior-integral wording above. No earlier claim is silently changed.

Write (e_-) for a normalized eigenvector of (H) with eigenvalue (-\alpha), and (H_+) for the positive restriction to (e_-^\perp). The spectral estimates already proved give

\[
\alpha\ge\alpha_0\kappa,\quad \alpha_0=\beta-\frac{105\pi}{256}>0,
\qquad H_+\ge\delta_0\kappa I,\quad \delta_0=\frac{\pi}{2R}-\beta>0.
\]

Put (c_-=\langle c,e_-\rangle), (c_+=(I-e_-\otimes e_-)c), and

\[
S=\langle c_+,H_+^{-1}c_+\rangle,\qquad s=\langle c,H^{-1}c\rangle=-\frac{c_-^2}{\alpha}+S.
\]

The preceding estimate (12) says (s\le-d\), where (d=\epsilon^2\kappa d_0^{\rm prof}>0\) and the exact positive dimensionless lower bound is

\[
d_0^{\rm prof}=
\frac{32}{35}\left(\beta-\frac{105\pi}{256}\right)
-\frac{18\left((R^5-1)/5-(R^3-1)/3\right)}
{\min\{\beta-105\pi/256,\;\pi/(2R)-\beta\}}.
\]

For (v=xe_-+y\in\mathcal T), (y\perp e_-), the constraint gives (x=-\langle c_+,y\rangle/c_-). Cauchy--Schwarz in the positive (H_+) form yields

\[
\alpha x^2\le p\,\langle y,H_+y\rangle,
\qquad p=\frac{\alpha S}{c_-^2}=1+\frac{\alpha s}{c_-^2}.
\]

Since (c_-^2/\alpha=S-s\ge d), we have (c_-^2\ge\alpha_0\kappa d). Also (c_-^2\le\|c\|_2^2\) and (-s\ge d), so

\[
1-p=\frac{-\alpha s}{c_-^2}\ge\frac{\alpha_0\kappa d}{\|c\|_2^2}=:\vartheta>0.
\]

The same constraint and the ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz inequality imply

\[
x^2\le\frac{\|c_+\|_2^2}{c_-^2}\|y\|_2^2
\le\frac{\|c\|_2^2}{\alpha_0\kappa d}\|y\|_2^2
=\vartheta^{-1}\|y\|_2^2.
\]

Therefore, for every full-domain tangent vector (including support-changing ones),

\[
Q[v]=\langle y,H_+y\rangle-\alpha x^2
\ge\vartheta\langle y,H_+y\rangle
\ge\frac{\vartheta\delta_0\kappa}{1+\vartheta^{-1}}\|v\|_2^2
=\eta\|v\|_2^2,
\]

with the explicit bound

\[
\eta=\kappa\,\frac{\alpha_0d_0^{\rm prof}\delta_0}
{C_2+\alpha_0d_0^{\rm prof}},\qquad
C_2=\frac{32\beta^2}{35}-\frac{3\beta\pi}{4}+\frac{21}{10}
+2\left[\frac95(R^5-1)-3(R^3-1)+\frac94(R-1)\right]. \tag{17}
\]

Here \,\(\|c\|_2^2=\epsilon^2\kappa^2C_2\), obtained by expanding the square of (5), using \(\int U^2=32/35\), \(\int U(3x^2-3/2)=-3\pi/8\), and integrating the exterior polynomial. For (R=1.001,\beta=1.51\), the exact expression (17) evaluates to

\[
C_2=0.631327206970\ldots,\quad d_0^{\rm prof}=0.202169761714\ldots,
\quad \eta=0.0039220590809\ldots\kappa.
\]

Thus the constrained Hessian has a concrete positive (L^2) margin for this fixed heterogeneous finite-fault construction. Since (K_\Omega\ge0), it also controls the form norm by
\(Q[v]+\beta\kappa\|v\|_2^2\le(1+\beta\kappa/\eta)Q[v]\).
The margin is not asserted uniform under changes in (R,\beta\), or the coefficient field.

Sign correction for the derivation of (3): with
\(J(x)=\int_{-1}^1(1-y^2)^{3/2}/(x-y)\,dy\), differentiation gives
\(J'(x)=-\int_{-1}^1(1-y^2)^{3/2}(x-y)^{-2}dy\). Hence the exterior value is
\(|D|U(x)=J'(x)/\pi\), not \,\(-J'(x)/\pi\). Its expanded expression in (3), and all subsequent edge signs and residuals, already use this correct sign.

## Appended operator note — memory does not restore a spectral gap

The edge sequence can be placed entirely inside the active interval, so no interpretation of a support-changing vector as a finite-time propagating front is needed. In the physical-clock coordinates of Sol v1, the slip block on the mass tangent is 
\(A=-mH\), (m=q_*/c\asymp d^{3/2}\). It is self-adjoint in (L^2(m^{-1}dx)), and its quadratic decay form is (Q). The two symmetric edge bumps with an interior mass correction therefore form a Weyl/Rayleigh sequence with quotient tending to zero.

For clarity about the full-memory block, use (y=qZ\), rather than (Z\), at the equilibrium. In the exact physical-clock system its linearized memory diagonal is multiplication by (q_*-\lambda_*\), bounded above by (-\delta_*=-(\lambda_*-\|q_*\|_\infty)<0\). Its slip-to-memory off-diagonal is zero. The memory-to-slip map is multiplication by (Bq_*/c\), where (B=\sigma bD_c\), hence by (O(d^{3/2})\) at the edge; it is bounded from ordinary (L^2\) into (L^2(m^{-1}dx)\), since
\[
\int\frac{|(Bq_*/c)y|^2}{m}\,dx
=\int\frac{B^2q_*}{c}|y|^2\,dx\le C\|y\|_2^2
\]
for the bounded positive coefficients in this construction. The full linearization is triangular, so its spectrum contains the gapless slip spectrum; bounded triangular coupling cannot move that spectrum away from zero. This is a linear no-gap result, not a nonlinear non-attraction result.

The amplitude clock does remove the *need to prescribe* the future blow-up time: Sol v1's exact equations give (\rho_\tau=\rho\lambda\), (t_\tau=1/\rho\), and the obstacle entropy identity. With the explicit \(\eta\) above, its small-entropy a priori estimate is available for this profile, and it allows strictly positive leakage in the equilibrium exterior and strictly positive physical memory (y_0\). This makes an open physical-data basin a viable conditional theorem without selecting a codimension-one slice at a guessed (t_i\). The condition still requires a global normalized-clock solution and convergence (or another sufficient asymptotic result); those are not proved here. Therefore neither an unconditional open-basin theorem nor a physical rupture/arrest classification is claimed.

## Appended clarification — phase projection, full-memory spectrum, and clock scope

The edge Rayleigh sequence is supported on the active side of both tips. On that sequence the obstacle residual g is zero, and tangent mass correction gives integral(c v)=0. Differentiating the exact normalized q equation then leaves the phase-fixed slip operator -mH on these test vectors. Its weighted quadratic decay form is Q. This is enough for the no-gap conclusion; no formula for the free-boundary generator on arbitrary exterior directions is needed. On exterior directions, differentiating the factor q in q_tau also produces the restoring term -(g/c)v. Since g/c tends to zero like the square root of edge distance, that term supplies no uniform decay gap either.

In the physical-clock variables (q,y), where y=qZ, the memory equation is triangular at y=0: its linearized y block is multiplication by q_* - lambda_*, bounded above by -delta_*<0, and it has no linear dependence on q. The q-to-q phase projection contributes at most a bounded rank-one map on the mass tangent. The y-to-q coupling is also bounded into the weighted slip space: its local coefficient is Bq_*/c=O(d^(3/2)), and the weighted square is controlled by integral(B^2 q_*/c times |y|^2). Thus the full triangular linearization retains the gapless slip spectrum. This clarifies the operator statement in the preceding appended note.

The explicit lower bound eta/kappa=0.003922059081 is reproducible with `python3 work/natural/n25_fault_boundary/constrained_eta_check.py`. The exact formula (17) is the proof; the script checks its constants.

The amplitude clock gives a real route from the codimension-one similarity calculation to an open set of physical initial data: it determines rho and physical time from initial V and theta without guessing the future blow-up time. For this constructed profile, the coupled entropy estimate in Sol v1 can use the explicit eta and permits q0>0 throughout the finite fault and y0>0, hence V0>0 and finite positive theta0. The resulting basin claim is conditional on proving a global normalized-clock solution and its asymptotic convergence. Those existence and convergence gates remain open, so the present result establishes a viable sufficient-condition framework, not the unconditional open-basin theorem.

For the weighted no-gap test, the analytic mass corrector can be chosen even and supported in two fixed interior subintervals, so it preserves even symmetry and removes the translation-odd component. The full-memory no-gap statement can be read directly from an invariant linearized subspace: take y=0 and q perturbations supported on the active interval with integral(c v)=0. ## Coercivity-constant correction

A final algebra audit found that the explicit eta formula in the first appended supplement overstates the lower bound: it used the bound on the negative coefficient x without carrying the same factor through the norm comparison. The coercivity proof and all profile/spectral signs are unaffected, but that displayed eta must be superseded by the following bound.

Let E_+=<y,H_+y>, p=alpha S/c_-^2, and theta=alpha0*d0_profile/C2. The previous inequalities give 0<=p<=1-theta and Q[v]>=theta E_+. The constraint, now estimated in the H_+ form, gives x^2<= (S/c_-^2)E_+=(p/alpha)E_+ <=(1-theta)E_+/(alpha0*kappa). Also ||y||_2^2<=E_+/(delta0*kappa). Therefore

  ||v||_2^2 <= E_+/kappa * (1/delta0 + (1-theta)/alpha0),

so the valid explicit margin is

  eta_correct/kappa = theta / (1/delta0 + (1-theta)/alpha0)
                    = 0.003364256578... .

This positive constant proves Q[v]>=eta_correct*||v||_2^2 on the full mass-tangent space. The exact formula and current numerical output are in `constrained_eta_check.py`; its output supersedes the earlier 0.003922... value. Simple rational enclosures of pi, together with the displayed exact formulas, give the conservative statement eta_correct>0.0033*kappa.

## Final coercivity sharpening

The preceding correction was itself too conservative in its description of the earlier 0.003922 value. Retain the actual negative-mode coefficient instead of replacing it by a lower bound: set theta_actual=-alpha*s/c_-^2=1-p. Then Q[v]>=theta_actual E_+>=theta_actual*delta0*kappa*||y||_2^2. The exact constraint gives ||v||_2^2<= (||c||_2^2/c_-^2)||y||_2^2. Multiplying these inequalities cancels c_-^2 and yields

  Q[v] >= (-alpha*s)*delta0*kappa/||c||_2^2 * ||v||_2^2
       >= alpha0*d0_profile*delta0/C2 * kappa * ||v||_2^2.

For the displayed R and beta this is eta/kappa=0.004200199143... . This is the strongest explicit bound recorded here. The 0.003364256578... value in the preceding correction is also a valid, more conservative bound; the statement that the earlier 0.003922... value overstated coercivity was incorrect. `constrained_eta_check.py` now evaluates the sharpened cancellation formula and asserts eta/kappa>0.00419.
