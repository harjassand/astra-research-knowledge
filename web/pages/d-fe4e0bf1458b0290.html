# Independent audit: query-dependent quantum decoding and improved proper loss

Date: 2026-10-07. Status: complete internal audit under the explicit measurable interface below. The query extension was proposed by `information_statistics`; the calibrated proper loss was proposed by `gaussian_sequence_audit`. Neither scientific priority nor external validation is asserted.

## Exact strengthened theorem

Observe one `X ~ N(theta,I_d)`, with a blind encoder retaining the same quantum/hybrid state `rho_X` before a coordinate query is chosen. The retained algebra is `direct_sum_j M_{d_j}`, with total `D=sum_j d_j`. All input-dependent classical registers count. Independent public randomness `S` is permitted, with dimension at most `D` at every seed. There is no uncharged entangled receiver reference or input-dependent record.

After retention, the user may choose any coordinate `i`. A decoder depending on `i` and `S`, but not on the mean or its sign, performs a POVM on the stored state and outputs one real value `Y_i`. Only a single chosen query must be answered; the proof does not require sequential measurements, nondestructive readout, or simultaneous output of all coordinates.

Assume, for each `i`, only the two scalar reconstruction guarantees

\[
\operatorname{TV}\big(\mathcal L_{+r e_i}(Y_i),N(r,1)\big)\le\varepsilon,
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}\big(\mathcal L_{-r e_i}(Y_i),N(-r,1)\big)\le\varepsilon,
\quad0<r\le1.
\tag{Q1}
\]

No target output under `theta=0` or under the other-coordinate alternatives is needed. The same stored state must support all choices `i`. Then, with natural logarithms,

\[
\boxed{\qquad
\log D\ge\frac d2\log(r/\varepsilon)
-\left(\frac54+\log2\right)d.
\qquad}\tag{Q2}
\]

The additive constant is `1.94314718056` nats, or about `2.8033688` bits, per coordinate. Thus the previous `5/4+(9/4)log 2` constant improves by exactly `(5/4)log 2` nats per coordinate. For example, taking `r=128 eps <=1` gives

\[
\log D\ge\left(\tfrac12\log128-\tfrac54-\log2\right)d
=0.4828679514\,d.
\tag{Q3}
\]

## 1. Audit of the calibrated bounded proper score

For the equal binary prior on `theta=+-r e_i`, write the positive-label indicator as `T in {0,1}`. Its posterior is

\[
p_r(x)=\Pr[T=1\mid X_i=x]
=\frac{1+\tanh(rx)}2.
\]

Define, initially for `0<p<1`,

\[
u_r(p)=\tanh\left(\frac1r\operatorname{arctanh}(2p-1)\right),
\qquad
F_r(p)=\int_{1/2}^{p}u_r(t)\,dt.
\tag{Q4}
\]

Extend `u_r` continuously to endpoints by `u_r(0)=-1`, `u_r(1)=1`. Since `u_r` is increasing, `F_r` is convex. Antisymmetry of `u_r` about `1/2` implies `F_r(0)=F_r(1)=c_r`. The loss

\[
\ell_r(s,q)=c_r-F_r(q)-(s-q)u_r(q),\qquad s\in\{0,1\},\ q\in[0,1]
\tag{Q5}
\]

is proper, with regret `D_{F_r}(p,q)`. It lies in `[0,1]` exactly: on the interior,

\[
\partial_q\ell_r(0,q)=q u_r'(q)\ge0,
\qquad
\partial_q\ell_r(1,q)=-(1-q)u_r'(q)\le0,
\]

and the four endpoint values are `ell(0,0)=ell(1,1)=0`, `ell(0,1)=ell(1,0)=1`. Continuity supplies the endpoints; no unbounded scoring rule is being used. At `r=1`, this is ordinary binary Brier loss: `ell_r(0,q)=q^2`, `ell_r(1,q)=(1-q)^2`.

The calibration is exact:

\[
u_r(p_r(x))=\tanh x.
\tag{Q6}
\]

The inverse map from the feature `v=u_r(p)` to the posterior is

\[
p(v)=\frac{1+\tanh(r\operatorname{arctanh}v)}2,
\qquad
p'(v)=\frac r2\frac{\operatorname{sech}^2(r\operatorname{arctanh}v)}{1-v^2}
\ge\frac r2.
\tag{Q7}
\]

The inequality follows from `cosh(r a)<=cosh(a)` for `0<r<=1`, and `sech^2(arctanh v)=1-v^2`. Changing variables in the Bregman integral gives, for either ordering of `p` and `q`,

\[
D_{F_r}(p,q)
=\int_{u_r(q)}^{u_r(p)}(v-u_r(q))p'(v)\,dv
\ge\frac r4\big(u_r(p)-u_r(q)\big)^2.
\tag{Q8}
\]

When the upper endpoint is smaller, both the integration orientation and the sign of the linear factor reverse. The resulting inequality is still the displayed lower bound.

Apply the report `q=p_r(Y_i)` to the selected scalar output. Blindness gives the Markov relation `T-(X,S)-Y_i`, so conditional on `X,S`, the risk difference from the full-data action `p_r(X_i)` is precisely its nonnegative Bregman regret. The two guarantees (Q1) and the loss range `[0,1]` imply

\[
\mathbb E_{Q_i}D_{F_r}(p_r(X_i),p_r(Y_i))\le\varepsilon,
\]

where `Q_i=(P_{+r e_i}+P_{-r e_i})/2`. Since

\[
\frac{dQ_i}{dP_0}(x)=e^{-r^2/2}\cosh(rx_i)\ge e^{-r^2/2},
\]

the common null coupling for this chosen query satisfies

\[
\delta_i:=\mathbb E_{P_0}(\tanh X_i-\tanh Y_i)^2
\le4e^{r^2/2}\frac\varepsilon r
\le4\sqrt e\frac\varepsilon r.
\tag{Q9}
\]

This argument averages over public seeds and therefore needs no separate accuracy guarantee at each seed. Each `Y_i` has its own coupling with the common `(X,S)`; the different `Y_i` are never required to have a joint quantum implementation.

## 2. Scalar information lower bound with the improved constant

Let `U=tanh G`, `G~N(0,1)`. It is continuous and injectively parameterizes `G`. Its differential entropy is

\[
\mathsf h(U)=\tfrac12\log(2\pi e)-2\mathbb E\log\cosh G
\ge\tfrac12\log(2\pi e)-1,
\tag{Q10}
\]

using `log cosh x<=x^2/2`. Apply the scalar entropy-distortion inequality from the main report with `U_i=tanh X_i` and `V_i=tanh Y_i`. Under the null, `X_i` and `S` are independent, and hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(X_i;Y_i,S)
&\ge I(U_i;V_i)\\
&\ge\mathsf h(U_i)-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\delta_i)\\
&\ge\tfrac12\log(r/\varepsilon)-1-\log2-\tfrac{r^2}4\\
&\ge\tfrac12\log(r/\varepsilon)-\tfrac54-\log2.
\end{aligned}\tag{Q11}
\]

Thus the stronger constant is valid before any common-output or query-dependent quantum argument is used. The original common-output theorem also inherits it.

## 3. Why incompatible measurements can still be charged together

Here the elementary single-POVM trace bound in the main report is insufficient by itself: separate POVMs do not produce a common `Y`. The correct addition uses a classical-quantum state and strong subadditivity. The following finite-partition argument handles all continuous variables without assigning a differential entropy to a quantum-classical register.

Take increasing finite partitions `L_i^(n)` of each `X_i` which generate its Borel sigma field. One explicit choice is a dyadic grid of mesh `2^-n` on `[-2^n,2^n]`, plus the two exterior rays. Under the null, these coordinate labels are independent, also at every independent seed `s`.

Fix `n,s`. Form the finite classical-quantum state

\[
\omega_{L_1\cdots L_d Q}^{(n,s)}
=\sum_{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_d}
p(\ell_1)\cdots p(\ell_d)
|\ell_1\cdots\ell_d\rangle\langle\ell_1\cdots\ell_d|
\otimes\rho_{\ell_1\cdots\ell_d,s},
\tag{Q12}
\]

where `rho_(labels,s)` is the original stored state averaged over the corresponding input cells. These classical registers are virtual registers in the proof, not extra information supplied to the decoder.

Quantum mutual information chain rule and strong subadditivity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
I(L_1\cdots L_d;Q)_{\omega}
&=\sum_i I(L_i;Q\mid L_{<i})_{\omega},\\
I(L_i;Q\mid L_{<i})_{\omega}-I(L_i;Q)_{\omega}
&=I(L_i;L_{<i}\mid Q)_{\omega}-I(L_i;L_{<i})_{\omega}\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}\tag{Q13}
\]

The ordinary last term is zero because the classical labels are independent. Also, for this finite ensemble,

\[
I(L_1\cdots L_d;Q)_{\omega}
=S(\bar\rho_s)-\sum_{\ell}p(\ell)S(\rho_{\ell,s})
\le S(\bar\rho_s)\le\log D.
\tag{Q14}
\]

For each coordinate use its own POVM. Holevo measurement data processing gives

\[
I(L_i^{(n)};Y_i\mid S=s)\le I(L_i^{(n)};Q)_{\omega^{(n,s)}}.
\tag{Q15}
\]

The same reduced memory ensemble is used for every query, which is why (Q13) applies despite measurement incompatibility. Sum (Q15), apply (Q13)-(Q14), and integrate over seeds. Independence of `L_i^(n)` and `S` yields

\[
\sum_i I(L_i^{(n)};Y_i,S)\le\log D.
\tag{Q16}
\]

For increasing finite source partitions, classical relative entropy gives

\[
I(L_i^{(n)};Y_i,S)\uparrow I(X_i;Y_i,S).
\tag{Q17}
\]

This is the usual monotone approximation of relative entropy along increasing sigma fields. The partitions generate the input sigma field while retaining the full output/seed sigma field. Since `d` is finite, pass to the limit in (Q16) to obtain

\[
\boxed{\ \sum_i I(X_i;Y_i,S)\le\log D.\ }\tag{Q18}
\]

No continuity theorem for infinite-classical quantum entropy is needed. Continuous POVM outcomes in (Q15) may themselves first be partitioned finitely and then recovered by the same classical relative-entropy limit. Equations (Q11) and (Q18) prove (Q2).

## 4. Measurability, seeds, and interface audit

The input, seed, and scalar-output spaces may be taken standard Borel. Assume the finite-dimensional density-matrix entries `(x,s) -> rho_(x,s)` are measurable and the POVM effects `M_(i,s)(B)` are weakly measurable in `s` for each Borel `B`. They define the required stochastic kernels. Averaging matrix entries over Gaussian input cells defines the finite-ensemble states in (Q12); entropy is a bounded continuous function of a finite-dimensional density matrix, so all seed integrals in the proof are measurable and finite.

Seed-dependent algebras with dimensions at most `D` may be padded into a fixed `D`-dimensional Hilbert space. Input-independent decoder ancillas and private randomness reduce to POVMs on that retained space. Input-dependent classical branch probabilities are part of the density matrix on the direct sum and contribute to its entropy; treating only the largest branch as the memory dimension would be incorrect.

The query index is supplied only after retention and identifies a coordinate, not the unknown sign or a hidden continuous value. If the encoder were told the query before retaining its state, one-coordinate storage would suffice and the linear-`d` theorem would be false. If the decoder received the mean sign as an additional oracle, it could draw the target scalar law without memory. These are different interfaces.

There is no requirement that `Y_i` be available after a different query has already destroyed the memory. The proof lower-bounds the same once-used state for every possible future choice. The nonnegative proper-score regret also prevents average-over-seed cancellations from evading the distortion bound.

Only the pair alternatives in (Q1) are used. The null distribution is an analytical common reference; it is not an extra source-reconstruction assumption.

## Established quantum premises and provenance

The finite quantum-information facts used in (Q13)-(Q15) are established standard theorems, not new deductions here. Strong subadditivity is the theorem of [Lieb and Ruskai, 1973](https://www.numdam.org/item/RCP25_1973__19__A5_0.pdf), Theorem 2, equation (1.5), PDF page 4. The finite-ensemble measurement information bound is given explicitly in [Nayak, 1999](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anayak/papers/Nayak99.pdf), Theorem 4.2, PDF page 5, crediting Holevo. The relevant primary PDFs are retained in `sources/`. The original random-access entropy mechanism is prior art; the sparse-Gaussian TV-to-distortion calibration and its stated uniform archive interface are the scope under audit.

The independently checked result is: both the query-dependent extension and the improved constant are mathematically supported under the stated model. This does not establish a new quantum information principle or certify scientific priority.
