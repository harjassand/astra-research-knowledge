# The actual harmonic-repair construction has finitely many global nodal domains

7 October 2026. **Our deduction; separate complete internal hostile gate PASS.** This is about the explicitly specified FI-based power/log/arbitrary-loss constructions, not every superexponential Schrödinger state. It does not prove finite Morse index. It instead blocks the proposed construction-specific route through infinitely many global nodal domains. Historical novelty UNKNOWN; all source construction remains the authors' contribution.

## Interface and already audited estimates

Fix one of our constructed pairs on R^n, n≥3. Put R=(K²+|x|²)^(1/2), a=a(R), ℓ=(R/a)^(1/2), κ=a^(3/2)R^(−1/2), η=κ/a²=(Ra)^(−1/2), Φ'=a and E=exp(−Φ). Either a=R^α with 0<α<1/3, or a is an admissible nondecreasing unbounded subpower frequency. In both cases η→0. Fix all parameters K,ρ,τ,σ once.

The macro partition has bounded overlap and |∇χ_i|≤C/ℓ_i. Waves are cos[a_i t_i·(x−x_i)], with a_i comparable to a, t_i·x_i=0 and active support |x−x_i|≤Cℓ. Write q=Σχ_i cos_i, f=Eq. The complete existing proof supplies

    |Df|≤CaE, |D²f|≤Ca²E,
    |DΔf|≤C_KκaE,
    |W|≤C_Kκa^(−2)E.                              (1)

W is the zero-extended harmonic correction. No derivative of W is assumed. The final function is

    F=G+H,
    G(x)=∫ψ(z)f(x−ε(x)z)dz,
    H(x)=∫K_x(y)W(y)dy,
    ε=σ/a, K_x(y)=ε^(−n)ψ((x−y)/ε).

Here ψ is one fixed nonnegative radial smooth compactly supported normalized kernel. Constants below may depend on its fixed σ and the fixed construction; they need not remain uniform as σ→0 or K→∞.

The existing proof also supplies the sparse open set Ω={|q|<τ}, with |Ω∩B(x,ρ/a(x))|<θ|B(x,ρ/a(x))| for one θ<1, and

    O={|q|<τ/2}, ΔF=0 on O,
    {F=0}⊂O, |F|≥cτE on R^n\O.                   (2)

F is globally smooth and tends uniformly to zero at infinity. These are the exact inputs, not assumed nodal topology. The original a=R^(1/3), κ=1 FI scale has the same first-order calculus; the theorem below applies whenever (1)–(2) have been verified for that literal parameterization.

## 1. Raw radial derivative

For r=|x|≥K and e_r=x/r,

    |∂rq|≤C[ℓ^(−1)+aℓ/r]≤Cκ/a.                   (3)

Indeed partition differentiation contributes C/ℓ. In a wave, t_i·e_r=t_i·(x−x_i)/r because t_i·x_i=0, and its magnitude is at most Cℓ/r. Multiply by a_i and use bounded overlap. Since R/r≤√2 in this region, both scales are ≤C√(a/R)=Cκ/a.

As ∂rΦ=ar/R, it follows exactly that

    ∂rf=−a(r/R)qE+O(κa^(−1)E).                    (4)

This uses tangency, not a crude |Dq|≤Ca estimate.

## 2. Radial kernel averaging improves the derivative error

For any C² function h on B(x,s), its spherical mean M_h(t) satisfies

    M_h'(t)=t^(1−n)/|S^(n−1)| ∫_(B(x,t))Δh,
    |M_h(t)−h(x)|≤t²/(2n) sup_(B(x,t))|Δh|.

A nonnegative normalized radial average of radius s is a convex mixture of these spherical means. Therefore

    |T_s h(x)−h(x)|≤C_ψ s² sup_(B(x,s))|Δh|.       (5)

This is an elementary integrated mean-value identity, stronger than a generic first-order freezing bound. In differentiating G, first hold ε(x) fixed in the averaging operator:

    ∂jG=T_ε(∂jf)−(∂jε)∫ψ(z)z·∇f(x−εz)dz.

Apply (5) to h=∂jf. Local comparability and (1) give

    |T_ε(∂jf)−∂jf(x)|≤C_K ε²κaE
                                 ≤C_Kκa^(−1)E.

The variable-radius contribution is bounded by

    C|Dε|aE≤CσR^(−1)E≤Cκa^(−1)E,

because |Dε|≤Cσ/(aR) and 1/R≤√(a/R). Thus, for the already fixed mollifier,

    |DG−Df|≤C_Kκa^(−1)E.                          (6)

No additional shrinking of σ is necessary. Equivalently a weaker CσaE estimate would suffice after choosing σ≪τ, but (6) avoids changing the given construction.

For H use the first kernel-jet bound |D_xK_x(y)|≤C_σ a^(n+1), supported on a ball of volume O_σ(a^(−n)). Together with the undifferentiated W estimate and local comparability this gives

    |DH|≤C_(K,σ)κa^(−1)E.                         (7)

No boundary regularity of Ω or pointwise derivative of its Dirichlet correction enters. Combining (4),(6),(7), with σ now included in the fixed constant,

    ∂rF=−a(r/R)qE+O_K(κa^(−1)E).                  (8)

Since κ/a²=η→0, choose R1≥K so the error/aE≤τ/8 for r≥R1. For |q|≥τ/2, r/R≥1/√2≥1/2, hence

    |∂rF|≥τaE/8>0,
    sign(∂rF)=−sign(q), r≥R1, x∉O.               (9)

No derivative-order-dependent parameter choice occurs: R1 is a tail threshold for this one fixed function.

## 3. No tail local extrema, including inside the harmonic zone

Take R2=R1+2ρ, using a≥1. Suppose F has a local maximum or minimum at x with |x|>R2. If x∉O, (9) rules it out. If x∈O, harmonic strong maximum principle makes F constant on the connected component C of O containing x. One may first apply the principle in a small ball and then harmonic unique continuation on C; no regularity of ∂C is needed.

Sparsity supplies y∈B(x,ρ/a(x)) outside Ω. Indeed that ball is not contained in Ω because θ<1. Since O⊂Ω, follow the line segment from x to y and let z be its first exit from O. The initial segment lies in the same component C. Thus z∈∂C, |q(z)|=τ/2 by continuity, and |z|>R1 since |z−x|≤ρ and |x|>R1+2ρ. Smoothness of F and constancy on C imply ∇F(z)=0 by approaching z along that initial segment. But (9) gives |∂rF(z)|>0. Contradiction.

Therefore F has no local maximum or minimum outside B_(R2). The argument uses an actual nearby component boundary; it does not assume a smooth level-set boundary or a Dirichlet trace formula.

## 4. Every global nodal domain reaches a fixed compact set

Let D be a connected component of {F>0} disjoint from the closed ball B_(R2). Choose x0∈D with F(x0)=b>0. Uniform F→0 supplies S>|x0| with |F|<b/2 for r>S. F attains a maximum ≥b on the compact set closure(D)∩closed(B_S). A finite boundary point of D has F=0: if it had a strictly positive value, continuity would place a connected positive ball around it intersecting D, hence inside D. The maximizing point consequently lies inside D and outside B_(R2), giving a prohibited local maximum. Negative domains are treated with −F.

Hence every global nodal domain intersects the same closed compact ball B_(R2). This statement alone would not imply finiteness for an arbitrary smooth function.

## 5. Harmonic zero neighborhoods supply local finiteness

At every zero of F, (2) gives an open harmonic neighborhood. F is real analytic there, so each sign set {F>0}, {F<0} is locally defined by an analytic inequality. At a nonzero point the sign set is locally either the entire neighborhood or empty, also semianalytic. Therefore BOTH GLOBAL sign sets are semianalytic subsets of the real analytic ambient manifold R^n, despite F not being globally analytic.

Bierstone–Milman1988, *Semianalytic and subanalytic sets*, Corollary2.7 p13: the family of connected components of a semianalytic subset is locally finite. Its proof explicitly obtains a neighborhood U with finitely many components of X∩U by the finite separating-family theorem. We read the precise definition, corollary and short proof in the primary PDF; the older source is archived with metadata. Compactness then implies only finitely many connected components of either global sign set can meet B_(R2). Since every global nodal domain meets that ball, F has FINITELY MANY GLOBAL NODAL DOMAINS.

No count, global nodal connectivity diagram, or Morse-index bound is supplied. The finite number may depend arbitrarily on the construction and the compact threshold. Local oscillation frequency tending to infinity is compatible with these globally connected domains.

## Spectral meaning and remaining decisive estimate

This internally gated deduction rules out proving infinite negative spectrum of this particular construction merely by exhibiting infinitely many nodal domains: there are not infinitely many. It does not prove finite negative spectrum. Threshold Courant gives only finite Morse index ⇒ finite nodal count, not its converse.

The remaining direct spectral route needs compactly supported tests v_j escaping to infinity with ∫(|∇v_j|²+Vv_j²)<0. Current repair estimates give |V|≤Cκ and smooth jets but do not give a negative average or a negative local spectral cell. A sufficient first new estimate is a sequence of large supported cutoffs/correctors with

    ∫V v_j² < −∫|∇v_j|²,

including every repair-region contribution. Guessing a mean −κ²/a²=−a/R from oscillations is not such an estimate. The harmonic repair changes precisely the near-zero regions where the quotient is most sensitive, so replacing V by the raw Δf/f or extrapolating a first-order signed residual is not legal.
