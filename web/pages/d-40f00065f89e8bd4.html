# Finite acquisition and polynomial source precision for the equal-charge compiler

2026-10-07. **New internal deduction; independent audit PASS:** `notes/harmonic_grid_audit/cycle8/POLYNOMIAL_BIT_COMPILER_GATE.md`. This note removes an exact-design input oracle by finite search, preserves the (N=O(D^2)) count, and bounds rational placement precision for the audited harmonic grid. It does not give polynomial-time acquisition or useful laboratory scales. No historical novelty claim is made.

## Exact interface and result

Input: integers (m\ge1,D=256m=64k,k=4m), the rational harmonic grid polynomial (P_m) in `notes/harmonic_grid_audit/audit.md`, and an integer (N>C(2D)^2), where (C) is the universal existence constant from the established spherical-design theorem. The algorithm does not need to know (C); its termination proof is conditional on this inequality. (N=O(D^2)) is allowed, and the algorithm acquires its directions rather than receiving a design.

Output: (N) distinct rational points in \(\mathbb R^3\), each carrying strength exactly (+1), with at least (8m^3) nondegenerate critical points. Each coordinate has (O(D^2+\log N)) bits when (N=O(D^2)). The finite search uses \(\exp(O(D^2\log D))\) rational bit operations in that regime (constants may depend on the chosen fixed cardinality constant). More generally the direction search has \(\exp(O(N\log(ND)))\) candidates, each processed in polynomial time in (N,D). This is an intentionally impractical existence-to-algorithm bridge. It establishes polynomial output description length and explicit finite acquisition, **not polynomial acquisition time**.

Only the retained critical points are proved nondegenerate for this finite-bit output. The separate generic perturbation theorem makes the entire real configuration Morse but is not needed here, and no bit bound for that extra global requirement is asserted.

## 1. A small rational net contains an accepted direction set

Use orthonormal real harmonics (e(\omega)=(Y_{lm}(\omega))_{1\le l\le D}), (M=D(D+2)), and normalized sphere measure. Addition gives \(\|e\|=\sqrt M\). The differentiated addition identity gives

\[
 \|D_\tau e\|^2\le\sum_{l=1}^D l(l+1)(2l+1)
 =D(D+1)^2(D+2)/2<(D+1)^4.
\]

Here the sum of squared full tangential gradients bounds every unit directional derivative. If two sphere points have chord distance at most \(\delta\le1/2\), their spherical distance is at most (2\delta); hence their evaluation vectors differ by at most (2(D+1)^2\delta).

Set

\[
 \delta=2^{-14}(D+1)^{-7},\quad
 r=[256(D+1)^5]^{-1},\quad \alpha=1/2.
\]

Approximating each node of an existing exact (2D)-design by at most \(\delta\) gives

\[
 \|b\|=\|N^{-1}E1\|\le2(D+1)^2\delta=r/32,
\quad \|S-I\|\le4(D+1)^3\delta<1/4.
\]

For the matrix estimate use (u'u'^T-uu^T=(u'-u)u'^T+u(u'-u)^T) and the fact both evaluation vectors have norm \(\sqrt M<D+1\).

Both rational stereographic charts are included:

\[
 f_\pm(u,v)=(2u,2v,\pm(1-u^2-v^2))/(1+u^2+v^2).
\]

They have derivative norm at most 2. Every sphere point has a representation in at least one chart with \(|u|,|v|\le1\). Choose the integer

\[
 Q=\lceil16(N+2)/\delta\rceil,
\]

and form the finite set of chart images of (u,v\in Q^{-1}\mathbb Z\cap[-2,2]), removing exact duplicates. Its size is at most (2(4Q+1)^2=\operatorname{poly}(N,D)), and its coordinates have (O(\log(ND))) bits.

There are **distinct** net points approximating all the exact-design points, without assuming a minimum separation. Round each chosen chart parameter to the nearest (Q^{-1})-grid point, then try the (N) parameters obtained by adding (j/Q) to its first coordinate, (0\le j<N). These remain within \([-2,2]^2\); their sphere images are distinct within that chart, and each is at chord distance less than \(2(N+2)/Q<\delta\) from the target. At most (N-1) earlier selected sphere points can be forbidden, so greedy distinct choices exist. Duplicate chart representations cause no problem because exclusions are by exact physical point.

Enumerate all (N)-element subsets of this finite net. Accept the first with

\[
 S>\tfrac12 I,\qquad \|b\|\le r/8.
\tag{A}
\]

The preceding approximation proves an accepted subset exists. Candidate count is at most \([2(4Q+1)^2]^N=\exp(O(N\log(ND)))\).

## 2. Exact rational verification; no spherical-coordinate oracle

Choose standard real solid harmonics (q_{lm}) with rational coefficients. For (m=0), use (r^lP_l(z/r)). For (m\ge1), use the real and imaginary parts of

\[
 (-1)^m(x+iy)^m r^{l-m}P_l^{(m)}(z/r).
\]

The powers of (r) combine to a polynomial by parity. Their normalized-sphere squared norms are

\[
 \nu_{l0}=1/(2l+1),\qquad
 \nu_{lm}=(l+m)!/[2(2l+1)(l-m)!]\quad(m\ge1).
\]

They are orthogonal; (Y_j=q_j/\sqrt{\nu_j}). Coefficients and norms have polynomial bit length, and \(\nu_j\ge1/(2D+1)\). For rational unit nodes, \(Q_{ji}=q_j(\omega_i)\), (H=QQ^T/N), and \(b_q=Q1/N\) are rational. Condition (A) is exactly

\[
 H-\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(\nu)>0,
 \qquad\sum_j b_{q,j}^2/\nu_j\le(r/8)^2.
\]

Rational LDL/Sylvester elimination and rational comparison decide these statements in polynomial bit complexity. Node evaluation, summation, and elimination have polynomial intermediate bit lengths by the usual determinant bounds for rational matrices. No algebraic square-root sign decision is needed. Both accepted inequalities have slack for the net set constructed above.

## 3. Rational bounded-precision inverse

The existing approximate-design gate applies with \(\alpha=1/2\). It uses

\[
 B=-E^TS^{-1}\Lambda^{-1},\qquad \|B\|_{2\to\infty}\le D+1,
\]

and a radius-(r) contraction with Lipschitz constant at most (1/4). The desired moment target obeys \(\|z\|\le r/4\), while (A) makes \(\|b\|\le r/8\). Thus the map of the radius-(r) ball actually has norm at most (r/2), leaving room for rounding at each iteration.

In rational coordinates (g=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt\nu)h), the radial displacement is

\[
 t=B_q g,\qquad B_q=-Q^TH^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}.
\]

Define \(C_j(t)=N^{-1}\sum_iq_j(\omega_i)(1+t_i)^{-l_j-1}\), and \(\zeta=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt\nu)z\). If (P=\sum c_jq_j), then \(\zeta_j=(2l_j+1)\epsilon\rho^{D-l_j}c_j\nu_j\) is rational. The exact fixed-point map is

\[
 g\longmapsto\zeta+g-C(B_qg).
\]

This is the same contraction measured in \(\|g\|_{\nu^{-1}}^2=\sum g_j^2/\nu_j\). Round each output coordinate to a common dyadic mesh of width at most \(\gamma/[(D+1)(2D+1)]\); this creates weighted norm error at most \(\gamma\). If \(\gamma\le r/8\), all iterates remain in the certified ball. After (n) steps, the error from the exact fixed point is at most

\[
 4^{-n}r+4\gamma/3.
\]

Choose \(\gamma=\eta/[100(D+1)]\) and (n) so \(4^{-n}r\le\gamma\). Then radial source error is below \(\eta/4\). Round each resulting radius to a dyadic number within \(\eta/4\); the final positions stay on the selected rational unit rays and are within \(\eta/2\) of the exact inverse. They remain distinct because rays are distinct and radii positive. Every strength remains exactly one.

Arithmetic in one iteration is polynomial: the fixed rational matrix (B_q) has polynomial bit size; the current (g) has a fixed common dyadic denominator; powers have exponent at most (D+1); the (N) rational summands can be combined exactly before the next rounding. Unrounded numerator/denominator size is bounded by a polynomial in (N,D) and the chosen mesh bit count. Exact unrounded iterations without these intermediate dyadic resets need not have this bound.

## 4. Explicit grid amplitude and scale

For the audited (24)-wave polynomial, \(\|P_l\|_{L^2(S^2)}\le3(2k)^l/l!\), because every complex wave vector has Hermitian Euclidean norm (2k). Consequently the compiler coefficient norm satisfies

\[
 S_P=\Big(\sum_{l,m}(2l+1)^2p_{lm}^2\Big)^{1/2}
 \le3(2D+1)e^{2k}.
\]

Fix dyadic values

\[
 \epsilon=2^{-16(D+1)},\quad
 \rho=2^{-32(D+1)},\quad
 \eta=\epsilon\rho^D.
\tag{B}
\]

For (D=64k\ge64), \(\epsilon S_P\le r/4\). One loose verification uses (D+1\le2^D), (2D+1\le2^{D+1}), (e^{2k}\le2^{3k}\le2^D), reducing the required bound to (2^{7D+13}\le2^{16(D+1)}\).

All exact source radii exceed (3/4). The Legendre tail of the normalized potential on \(B_3\), after division by \(\epsilon\rho^D\), is bounded by

\[
 3\,4^{D+1}\rho/\epsilon.
\]

It is harmonic there. Poisson's formula on a unit ball gives gradient norm at its center at most 3 times the boundary supremum and Hessian operator norm at most 10 times that supremum. (For a unit vector (v), the second derivative kernel is (15(\theta\cdot v)^2-5\), whose absolute value is at most 10.) Thus on \(B_2\), both derivative errors are bounded by

\[
 \tau=30\,4^{D+1}\rho/\epsilon
 =30\,2^{-14(D+1)}.
\]

Moving a source by at most \(\eta\), with all line-segment source-to-observation distances at least (1/2), changes the unscaled potential gradient by at most (16\eta\) and Hessian by at most (384\eta\). Indeed the kernel Hessian norm is (2r^{-3}\); the third derivative trilinear norm is at most (24r^{-4}\). Averaging by (N) cancels the sum. After rescaling, the gradient error is at most (16\eta/(\epsilon\rho^{D-1})=16\rho\), and Hessian error at most (384\rho^2\le384\rho\).

The combined error in each of these two norms is therefore at most

\[
 \Delta=30\,2^{-14(D+1)}+384\,2^{-32(D+1)}
 \le k e^{-10k}/320000.
\tag{C}
\]

A deliberately crude elementary verification bounds the left side by \(414\,2^{-14D}\), and the right side from below by \(k\,2^{-20k}/320000\), using (e<4). For (D=64k), it suffices that \(132480000\le k2^{876k}\), which holds at (k=1) and increases.

## 5. All grid critical points survive rational placement

Every audited witness ball lies in \(B_2\): its center has norm less than \(\sqrt3\), and its radius is less than \(10^{-4}\). Also \(\eta+2\rho<1/4\), so exact source radii at least \(3/4\) imply all segment distances used above exceed \(1/2\). The previously audited frozen Hessian (H_p=D^2U_k(p)) satisfies \(\|H_p^{-1}\|\le L=2k^{-2}e^{3k}\). Its grid ball radius is \(s=e^{-7k}/(10000k)\). For the uncompiled polynomial the Newton map has derivative norm below (1/4), and center displacement at most (s/2). Bound (C) is exactly \(\Delta\le s/(16L)\). Adding both the compiler tail and rational-placement error increases the center displacement by at most (s/16), and increases the contraction bound by at most (s/16<1/16\). Hence the new map has derivative norm below (5/16\), and maps each ball into radius at most \((1/2+1/16+5/16)s=7s/8\). Banach gives one critical point per ball. The Hessian remains invertible and has the same inertia through the symmetric straight-line path used in the original audit. All (8m^3) disjoint balls persist.

The final radius precision needs \(\log_2(1/\eta)=16(D+1)+32D(D+1)=O(D^2)\) bits, plus a logarithmic margin. Multiplying by a rational net direction adds only (O(\log(ND))\) bits. Thus output coordinates have polynomial, indeed quadratic in (D), bit length. Output size is (O(ND^2)=O(D^4)\) bits in the optimal-count regime. The retained equilibria occur at physical scale \(\rho\), with exponentially smaller witness radii; polynomial bit complexity is entirely compatible with physically inaccessible precision and tiny signals.
