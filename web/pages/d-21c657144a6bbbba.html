# Candidate scan: downstream-selective super-reduction

**Verdict:** The 2026 Nature mechanism is a concrete, distinct application-rich line in reaction selectivity. It is separate from the active H2-source TST, DAR audit, fluorescence-sign/Floquet and N33 lines. It is a promising substrate for the root's design-theorem work, but the publication already states and models the two-channel principle; do not claim a new result or novelty from that identity. Novelty of any multi-channel, resource-constrained extension remains **UNKNOWN**.

The demonstration couples 1-(2,2-dimethylcyclopropyl)propan-1-one (Ket-1) to styrene in a formal [3+2] annulation even though the ketone is about 1 V harder to reduce. The proposed sequence uses excited perylene/photogenerated solvated electrons to reduce both partners. Ket-1 radical anion β-scissions into a ketyl radical, which adds styrene and cyclizes toward annulation. The styrene radical anion is mostly returned by BET before it is protonated/hydrogenated. Selectivity therefore comes from unequal post-SET commitment and BET: transient reduction of the easier partner recycles it; rapid fragmentation/trapping of the harder partner pulls it into product. The article's SI reports a 70% A / 7% B GC outcome under perylene, 390-nm light (10:1 endpoint product ratio), plus no-light and catalyst controls.

The exact reduced two-channel law is `R=v1/v2 = g (k1/k2)(x2+k2)/(x1+k1)`, where `g=kSET1[S1]/(kSET2[S2])`, `xi=kBET,i[A]`, and `ki` is an effective productive post-SET hazard. Under equal BET hazards, `R=g u(β+1)/(β+u)`, with `u=k1/k2`, `β=x/k2`. `GATE_DERIVATION.md` independently derives this identity, gates 90% target selectivity (`R≥9`) and observed 70:7 (`R=10`), and preserves an SI capture-rate conflict: the Ket-1 prose value `4×10^9 M−1s−1` vs a Ket-1 caption value `3×10^10`, matching styrene's `3×10^10`. At the SI's separate illustrative `β=560`, the required commitment ratio for 70:7 is 10.16 under the caption-literal equal capture rates but 86.42 under the prose rates. These are illustrative algebraic thresholds, not fits to the observed product split. The threshold target commitment itself is only 1.78% or 13.36%, respectively, exposing why selectivity alone misses throughput.

The source provides unusually relevant controls: no-light constant-potential perylene gives no annulation; no-perylene/direct electrolysis and classic photoredox controls primarily hydrogenate styrene; thermal sodium/naphthalide reduction reduces both substrates but does not protect styrene; perylene-anion under fixed-potential illuminated conditions gives annulation; transient absorption and CV support BET and solvated-electron pathways. The strongest nearby prior, Bains et al. (Science, 2025), already combines super-reduction with BET mitigation via embedded PCET, while Mansell et al. (JACS, 2024) already demonstrate the formal alkyl cyclopropyl ketone [3+2] transformation by SmI2/Sm0. The potentially distinct part is substrate-pair allocation by downstream kinetics, not super-reduction, BET chemistry, or the transformation alone.

**High-ceiling design capability to pursue:** an uncertainty-aware inverse-design theorem/algorithm for N competing substrates that outputs a feasible Pareto region of target selectivity, target product flux, and resource per mole under measured channel-specific capture, BET, and post-SET hazards, with a shared finite reductant supply. Require explicit parameter intervals and total photon, electrode-charge, and cell-energy costs; compare against a redox-potential/Marcus baseline with identical substrate information and input budget. This is only a research target: the two-channel identity is already in the 2026 SI, and the source's constant-pool model does not establish the multi-channel theorem, robust feasibility, or energy optimum.

**Main obstruction/gates:** resolve the internally conflicting Ket-1 capture constant from SI S2.18/S30; measure channel-specific capture and BET/commitment hazards in the actual mixed-reductant reaction; then test whether a single calibrated model predicts held-out selectivity and target flux when donor potential, acceptor concentration, and competing sinks change. Current article does not close photon quantum yield, absorbed flux, full wall-plug energy, cell-voltage history, or matched-cost baseline. Source-reported mixed active reductants and concentration-depletion/cross-intermediate effects also violate the reduced independent-channel model if large.

**Model check:** `two_channel_control.py` and `MODEL_OUTPUT.json` show a finite illustrative mechanism ablation using SI-reported capture constants and separate SI Fig. 5c BET settings. At equal concentrations, using the prose capture ratio (`g=2/15`) gives only 11.8% target product share with no BET. With a 1000-fold illustrative target/easy commitment ratio and common BET `5.6×10^5 s−1`, target share rises to 97.96%, but only 7.7% of electron captures yield either product. This is not an experimental fit, Faradaic efficiency, or energy estimate.
