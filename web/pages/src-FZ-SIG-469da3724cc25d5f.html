DEFECT KILLING, PARTIAL-DOMAIN RISK, AND A FULL EIGHT-SITE OVERFLOW CERTIFICATE
16 October 2026. Internal derivations; external correctness and priority UNKNOWN.

This note states sufficient conditions explicitly. It does not assert that a
near-optimal envelope for the eight-site first-passage problem has been acquired.
The final exponential-tail result DOES concern the full supplied eight-site
decimal-rate mathematical generator, rather than a replacement small system.

1. DEFINITIONS AND THE EXACT OUTPUT CONTRACT

Let X be a finite-state CTMC with transient set C, absorbing success B and
optional absorbing failure A. Q=(q_xy) is the original generator, and
q_x=sum_{y != x} q_xy includes exits into A and B. Stop at absorption or deadline
T. Let p=h(0,x0)>0, where h(t,x)=P_{t,x}(tau_B<=T, tau_B<tau_A).
Boundary values are h_B=1, h_A=0; h(T,x)=0 for x in C. Probability of an exact
jump at T is zero in this finite-rate setting, so <= versus < is immaterial.

The supplied spatial benchmark has eight nonnegative integer counts, no failure
set before the deadline, initial counts all 50, target max_i n_i<=15, and T=1.
Its mathematical state graph is infinite. The finite-state statements below
transfer only with the corresponding countable-process/localization conditions;
the overflow theorem in Section 6 proves nonexplosion of its decimal generator.

2. A CONSTANT-WEIGHT REPAIR BY ARTIFICIAL DEFECT KILLING

Theorem 1. Suppose g(t,x)>0 on [0,T) x C, g_B=1, g_A=0, and g is locally
absolutely continuous in time, bounded on the finite state space, and satisfies

    Dg := partial_t g + Qg <= 0                         (1)

almost everywhere before T. A nonnegative terminal value g(T,C)>=0 is allowed;
any remaining proposal paths are declared failures at T. Define, before T,

    r_xy(t) = q_xy g(t,y)/g(t,x), y in C union B, y!=x,
    k_x(t)  = -Dg(t,x)/g(t,x).                          (2)

Here k is an added jump to an artificial failure state. Edges into A have rate
zero because g_A=0. The total proposed jump rate is

    lambda_x(t) = sum_y r_xy + k_x
                = q_x - partial_t log g(t,x).          (3)

It is nonnegative, as is clear from its representation as a sum of rates. The
corresponding killed h-transform can equivalently be defined through its
sub-Markov transition kernel; this avoids assuming bounded hazards near T.
Then

    Q^g(success) = p/g(0,x0),
    W = g(0,x0) 1{proposal reaches B by T}              (4)

is unbiased for p. In particular,

    E_Q W^2 / p^2 = g(0,x0)/p,
    Var_Q W / p^2 = g(0,x0)/p - 1.                     (5)

No likelihood-ratio product is computed, either for routing or for clocks.

Proof. Fix a successful path with finitely many jumps and let tau<=T be its
success time. Relative to its original path density, the proposed density is

    product_j [g(t_j,x_j)/g(t_j,x_{j-1})]
       * exp(integral_0^tau partial_t log g(t,X_t) dt).

The chain rule along a piecewise constant path makes this exactly
g(tau,B)/g(0,x0)=1/g(0,x0). The added killing changes the holding hazard so the
clock term cancels the between-jump time dependence of g. Thus every successful
original path has its density multiplied by the same constant. Integrating
over successful paths proves (4), and (5) follows from the Bernoulli law.

For a kernel justification, supercaloricity implies

    E_{t,x} g(s,X_{s stopped at absorption}) <= g(t,x).

Multiplying the stopped original transition kernel by g(s,y)/g(t,x) therefore
gives a sub-Markov kernel. Assign its missing mass to artificial failure and
stop remaining transient mass at T. Its local rates are (2), and its successful
path measure has the density just computed. This construction accounts for
nontermination/explosion mass as failure; a numerical event sampler still has
to implement its hazards and establish its actual cost. On finite state spaces
with g bounded away from zero on compact subintervals [0,T-epsilon], the rates
are bounded there. A singular terminal layer requires appropriate integrated
hazard or exact terminal sampling, not arbitrary rate clipping. QED.

For a static eventual-hitting problem, partial_t g=0 and (3) becomes lambda=q_x:
the original total clock is retained. Nonnegative defect killing is possible
with this constant-weight construction exactly when Qg<=0. The state-by-state
generator sign is the condition that mere relative committor accuracy lacks.

3. REPAIRING AN APPROXIMATE COMMITTOR BY ITS SNELL ENVELOPE

Theorem 2. Let v be a nonnegative bounded Markov obstacle, with v_B=1, v_A=0,
v(T,C)=0. Suppose, for some delta>=0,

    v(t,x) <= (1+delta) h(t,x) for every transient (t,x). (6)

A lower relative bound is not needed for the existence statement. Define

    g(t,x) = sup_sigma E_{t,x}[v(sigma,X_sigma)],        (7)

where stopping times sigma lie between t and absorption/deadline; boundary
payoffs are as above. Then g is the least supercaloric majorant of v and

    h <= g <= (1+delta) h.                             (8)

When the regularity in Theorem 1 holds (or using the killed-kernel version),
defect killing with g has normalized variance at most delta.

Proof. At sigma=absorption/deadline the expected payoff is h, so g>=h. The
bounded stopped process h(s,X_s) is a martingale. Therefore every admissible
stopping time has E[v(sigma,X_sigma)]<=(1+delta)h(t,x); take the supremum.
Dynamic programming shows g is supercaloric and dominates v. Conversely any
supercaloric w>=v has w(t,x)>=E v(sigma,X_sigma) for every admissible sigma by
optional sampling, hence w>=g. Equation (5) now gives the variance assertion.
QED.

This is a genuine repair of the mathematical instability, but its construction
is an obstacle/optimal-stopping problem. It is NOT an acquisition theorem. In
particular, if v<=h everywhere, its least supercaloric majorant is h itself:
g<=h because h is an eligible majorant, while g>=h by (8). Even an excellent
underestimate may therefore leave a full committor solve hidden in (7).

The smallest-excessive-majorant/Snell construction and super/subsolution
importance sampling are established machinery. This note does not claim their
invention, and no historical-priority clearance for the precise combination
is asserted.

4. AN EXPLICIT PARTIAL-DOMAIN CERTIFICATE, WITH BOUNDARY RISK CHARGED

Let u be a globally certified nonnegative supercaloric upper bound for h, with
u_B=1, u_A=0 and appropriate deadline stopping. Let D be a finite transient
domain and let sigma_D be its first exit before success/failure/deadline. Assume
v<=u. Define g_D inside D as the optimal-stopping value with obstacle v before
exit, boundary payoff u on leaving D, success payoff 1, failure/deadline payoff
0. Extend g_D=u outside D. Define the boundary-risk value

    e_D(t,x) = E_{t,x}[u(sigma_D,X_sigma_D)
                      1{sigma_D < tau_A, tau_B, T}].   (9)

Theorem 3. The extension g_D is globally supercaloric, h<=g_D<=u, and, whenever
(6) holds,

    g_D(t,x) <= (1+delta)h(t,x) + e_D(t,x).             (10)

Consequently the defect-killed estimator satisfies

    E W^2/p^2 <= 1+delta+e_D(0,x0)/p.                 (11)

If a separately justified lower bound ell<=p is available, the computable bound
g_D(0,x0)/ell also certifies the normalized second moment. In particular if a
two-sided relative promise gives p>=v(0,x0)/(1+delta), then

    e_D(0,x0) <= eta v(0,x0)/(1+delta)

is a sufficient boundary-risk test for normalized second moment <=1+delta+eta.

Proof. Since u is supercaloric and dominates both v and boundary payoffs, optional
sampling gives g_D<=u. Inside D, optimal stopping gives supercaloricity. Outside
D, replacing the values of u at neighboring inside states by smaller g_D can
only decrease the off-diagonal part of Q; thus Dg_D<=Du<=0 there. Boundary/
terminal matching and the stopped-kernel formulation account for boundary
limits. The same optional-stopping argument as in Theorem 2 gives g_D>=h.

For any admissible stopping rule, the payoff before exit is <=(1+delta)h at its
stopping state. The exit payoff u differs from (1+delta)h by at most u. Martingale
optional sampling for h therefore bounds that rule's value by
(1+delta)h(t,x)+e_D(t,x). Taking the supremum proves (10). QED.

Acquisition contract. On a finite D, e_D is a linear Dirichlet backward problem
and g_D is an obstacle backward problem; in the static case these are a sparse
resolvent solve and a finite linear complementarity/optimal-stopping problem.
All outgoing generator entries from D and all outside values u reached by
those entries are required. A global proof for u and v's promise is additional
information. Solving this local problem does not certify missing outside
physics or a relative approximation promise. A formal dense inverse is not a
cost analysis. This method can certify stopping after a small region IF its
boundary-risk ratio is small; it supplies no general guarantee of a small D.

Exact diagnostic. For the incoming three-state triangle, kappa=10^-6, p=10^-12,
v=p(1+delta,1,1-delta), delta=0.01. Full-domain majorization gives

    g0 = p(1+delta),
    g1 = g2 = p[1+delta/(1+kappa)].                    (12)

The generator defect is negative only at state 0. The exact rational script
checks every generator inequality, the repaired routing row sums, the success
law p/g_i, and the old rational witness Kz-z>0. Thus the old eventual estimator
has infinite second moment while the repaired one has normalized second moment
at most 1.01. If outside values are merely bounded by u=1, domains {0} or {0,1}
instead produce normalized moments about 10^12; their boundary risks are almost
one. This demonstrates why partial-graph construction is not automatically
efficient. An exact three-state phase-type/resolvent computation already returns
p directly and beats sampling this toy system.

5. SUCCESSFUL-PATH WORK IS NOT REMOVED

Theorem 4. Let P* be the original complete successful-path law conditioned on
success, and let C>=0 be the actual cost of explicitly producing a complete
proposal path (including failures). The defect-killed proposal satisfies

    Q^g(path | success)=P*,
    E_Q C * [E_Q W^2/p^2] >= E_{P*} C.                (13)

Proof. Successful proposal density is original successful density divided by
g0, so the conditional law is exactly P*. Hence E_Q C >=(p/g0)E_{P*} C; multiply
by g0/p from (5). Infinite expectations are interpreted in the extended sense.
QED.

This is stronger than the incoming general Cauchy-Schwarz lower bound
(E_{P*} sqrt(C))^2 for this restricted constant-weight family. It does not apply
to all estimators or to analytically contracted excursions.

More explicit primitive-jump accounting: let N_valid count original-type
internal/success jumps made before artificial killing/deadline. If h<=g<=c h
globally, occupation measures give

    (p/g0) E_{P*} N <= E_Q N_valid
                     <= c(p/g0) E_{P*} N.             (14)

Indeed the transient occupation density under Q^g at (t,i) is original killed
occupation density times g(t,i)/g0. Multiplying by r_ij=q_ij g_j/g_i and summing
gives (1/g0) integral original_occupation_i q_ij g_j. Replacing g_j between h_j
and c h_j gives (14), because the same integral with h_j equals p E_{P*} N.
There is at most one additional artificial-killing event. Thus bounded moments
retain the conditional primitive work to constant factors. Model calls used
to evaluate g, its time derivative, and its 36 successors are extra cost.

Finite-horizon diagnostic without the exact time committor. In the independent
absorption-hazard family with internal generator G, G1=0, success rate kappa*p,
failure rate kappa*(1-p), choose c=1+delta and

    g(t,C)=c p kappa (T-t), g_B=1, g_A=0.             (15)

It is a supercaloric envelope since

    -(partial_t+Q)g=p kappa[delta+c kappa(T-t)]>=0.

It is NOT the exact finite-horizon committor p(1-exp[-kappa(T-t)]). Rates are

    r_B=1/[c(T-t)],
    k=delta/[c(T-t)]+kappa,
    r_internal=q_internal.                            (16)

The combined terminal hazard is 1/(T-t)+kappa. It can be sampled exactly as
min(U, E), U uniform on [0,T], E exponential(kappa). A U event succeeds with
probability 1/c; an E event is artificial failure. The resulting constant-
weight estimator is unbiased for p(1-exp[-kappa T]), and

    normalized second moment = c kappa T/(1-exp[-kappa T])
    relative variance <= delta+c kappa T,              (17)

because 1-exp(-x)>=x/(1+x), x>=0 (equivalently exp(x)>=1+x).
At kappa=10^-6, delta=.01, T=1 the descriptive relative variance is
0.010000505000084203. This checks a finite-horizon repair without presupposing
the exact time-dependent committor. It supplies no novelty or advantage: the
independent terminal hazard makes this family exactly solvable by ordinary
phase-type/lumpability machinery, with no trajectory sampling at all.

6. A SOURCE-COMPLETE FULL EIGHT-SITE OVERFLOW CERTIFICATE

This theorem concerns the exact decimal coefficients b(n)=2.676n(n-1)+108.102,
a(n)=.040n(n-1)(n-2)+37.881n, directed diffusion rate D n_source, D=8.2207.
The open 2x4 grid has degree at most 3 and symmetric directed edges. Coefficients
are treated as rational decimals, as in the supplied mathematical contract.
This is not a floating-roundoff certificate for the supplied IEEE SSA kernel.

For any rational r>1 set V_r(n)=sum_{i=1}^8 r^{n_i}. For an undirected pair with
counts a,b its two diffusion directions contribute

    D[(r^-1-1)(a r^a+b r^b)+(r-1)(a r^b+b r^a)].

Since

    a r^a+b r^b-a r^b-b r^a = (a-b)(r^a-r^b)>=0,

the contribution is at most D(r+r^-1-2)(a r^a+b r^b). Summing edges and using
degree<=3 gives

    Q V_r(n) <= sum_i r^{n_i} F_r(n_i),
    F_r(m)=b(m)(r-1)+a(m)(r^-1-1)
                    +3D m(r+r^-1-2).                 (18)

Let K_r=max_{m>=0} r^m F_r(m). It is finite because F_r has negative leading
cubic coefficient. In particular Q V_r<=8K_r over EVERY eight-count state.

Theorem 5. If initial counts are all 50, then for tau_M=first time any count
reaches M, for every T>=0,

    P(tau_M<=T) <= [8 r^50+8 K_r T]/r^M.              (19)

The decimal-generator process is nonexplosive. This statement applies also to
overflow before target absorption, whose probability can only be smaller.

Proof. Stop at tau_M or T. Before tau_M the process has a finite state space
and bounded rates. Dynkin's formula and (18) give
E V_r(X_{tau_M wedge T}) <=8r^50+8K_r T. On {tau_M<=T}, V_r>=r^M. Markov's
inequality gives (19). For fixed r,T the right side tends to zero as M tends
to infinity. Explosion cannot occur inside any finite count box and would
require exit from every such box, so its probability before T is zero. QED.

Exact parameter and finite verification. Take r=161/100. Multiplying F_r by
r/(r-1)>0 yields the cubic

    -.040 m^3 + A m^2 + B m + C,
    A=2.676r+.120,
    B=-2.676r-37.961+3D(r-1), C=108.102r.              (20)

Here A-.040*111<0, B<0 and B*111+C<0, all checked as exact rationals. Therefore
the cubic is strictly negative for all integers m>=111. Exact enumeration of
only m=0,...,110 proves K_r is attained at m=103. The script records the exact
rational K_r and the exact rational quotient in (19). At T=1 and M=163,

    P(sup_{t<=1} max_i n_i >=163)
       <= [8(161/100)^50+8K_{161/100}]/(161/100)^163
       = 7.569068427447863... * 10^-10  < 10^-9.       (21)

The displayed decimal is descriptive. The strict comparison with 10^-9 is an
exact Fraction assertion; no outward rounding of that decimal is required.
r was chosen by a bounded exploratory grid of 100 rational values; the final
certificate needs the fixed r and 111 scalar evaluations. No global-optimality
claim is made for r or M. A sharper Lyapunov/coupling calculation could improve
this cap.

What (21) certifies. An absorbing-overflow FSP retaining counts 0,...,162 has

    computed hit mass <= true first-hit probability
                      <= computed hit mass+7.57e-10,   (22)

subject to a separately certified numerical error for its computation. A
suppressed-outward-rate calculation (as in the supplied SSA's cap argument)
instead has absolute first-hit-probability bias <=the same overflow bound,
by coupling the two laws until first overflow. It is not automatically a
one-sided lower bound. No such eight-site capped hitting calculation was run.

This supplies an acquired global truncation certificate, not a cheap finite
solver. The full retained box has 163^8=498311414318121121 states. Consequently
ordinary full-state uniformization/resolvent computation is still not an
operational solution. A tensor/other compressed solve would need its own
certified approximation and cost; splitting and related samplers remain
stronger executable incumbents under the current evidence.

7. A SMALL-REGION FAILURE CERTIFICATE ON THE ACTUAL INITIAL STATE

At (50,...,50), the local birth rate is 6664.302 per site, the local death rate
6598.05 per site, and the total diffusion rate is 8220.7. The total initial
primitive-event rate is exactly

    8(6664.302+6598.05)+8220.7 =114319.516.             (23)

For D consisting only of the initial state, target is unreachable in one jump.
With the globally safe outside upper bound u=1 before T, e_D(0,x0) is the
probability of any first event before 1:

    e_D=1-exp(-114319.516)>1-2^-114319.                 (24)

Thus a one-state repair cannot pass a boundary-risk budget near 10^-9. This
does not prove that every modest implicit representation fails: it rejects
this particular local-domain/outside-bound choice. The new exponential cap
certificate in Section 6 provides a meaningful global outside-error budget
but leaves almost 5e17 configuration states inside the corresponding box.

8. STATUS AND STRONGEST INCUMBENTS

Theorems 1-4 are explicit internally derived repair/cost statements using
existing killed h-transform, Snell-envelope, subsolution and Dirichlet-resolvent
machinery. Theorem 5 is a model-specific global certificate with an analytic
all-state argument and exact finite rational verification. No independent
external mathematical review, priority claim, physical calibration or measured
practical advantage is established.

Existing ordinary Doob/Feynman-Kac subsolutions cover the basic envelope idea;
standard Snell/obstacle algorithms cover its least-majorant acquisition;
phase-type and graph transformation solve the diagnostic fixtures outright;
the supplied fixed-level splitting is the actually executed full-lattice
incumbent. The September 2026 Hartmann et al. paper already treats value/control
formulations without long-trajectory reweighting. This note does not promote
the broad phrase "remove reweighting" as new.
