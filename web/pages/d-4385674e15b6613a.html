1. Exact application theorem and supplied premise

Let Omega be connected and open in R^m, m>=1. Let X=C(Y) be a complete
CAT(0) metric cone with vertex o and its standard cone metric/homotheties.
Let u be a supplied local KS W^{1,2}_loc energy minimizer and use its
canonical locally Lipschitz representative. Assume u is not identically o.
Then, conditional on premise R below,
 Z=u^{-1}(o) is sigma-finite for H^{m-1} and countably(m-1)-rectifiable.
No properness, finite target dimension or separate bounded-image hypothesis
is imposed. Finite local energy and L^2 distance are part of KS W^{1,2}.
For disconnected Omega, include only components on which canonical u is
not identically o. The statement includes surface cones of angle>=2pi.

Supplied premise R, admitted exactly as an internal theorem by root:
For a single locally finite measure tuple on an open Euclidean domain,
 T=[nu f^t;f beta]>=0, grad nu=2f, div f=d=tr beta,
 div beta=grad mu, 2mu<=d, |mu|<=C d,
the full locus G_N of centers with positive kernel height and
                   1/2<=P(x,s)<=N
at ALL sufficiently small radii is sigma-finite for H^{m-1} and countably
(m-1)-rectifiable, for each finite fixed N. The cutoff may depend on the
center. This uses the common normalized polynomial-kernel P=A/H convention
of the frozen abstract tuple interfaces. I do not reprove R, import its
new proof body, or infer it from the old Hausdorff-dimension statement.

Countably k-rectifiable is used in the supplied theorem's usual measure
sense: coverage by countably many Lipschitz images of R^k up to an H^k-null
remainder. The pointwise hypotheses below hold at every apex point; this
does not assert that each individual point belongs to one of the resulting
charts. For k=0, sigma-finite H^0 already implies countability.

2. The exact frozen tuple, retained without modification

Write r(x)=d_X(u(x),o), pi for the KS pullback tensor and e=tr pi. Use
energy E=(1/2)int e and the same fixed actual tuple throughout:

The previous independent baseline and exposed audit reconstructed:

                    2mu=d, |mu|=d/2.                      (2.1)
Thus R's order and domination inputs hold with equality and C=1/2.
All entries are locally finite Radon measures. No center-dependent tuple,
blowup normalization, change of reference, or deletion of apex energy is
introduced in the present application.

To identify precisely what is retained, radial distance is1-Lipschitz,
so pi-dr tensor dr is PSD. A variable radial homothety with a=1+s eta>0
for BOTH signs has exact energy

Nonidentically-apex continuity makes nu nonzero. The already supplied/read

3. Exact frequency identities hold at every center and radius

Fix an integer p>=4 and use
 phi_{x,s}=s^{-m}(1-|y-x|^2/s^2)_+^p,
 psi_{x,s}=2p s^{-m}(1-|y-x|^2/s^2)_+^{p-1}.
For Bbar_s(x) contained in Omega set
 H=int psi dnu, A=s^2 int phi dmu, D=s^2 int phi dd,

The frozen distribution/PSD calculation gives
 dot A=2A-D+R_s, dot H=2D, R_s H>=D^2,
                    D=2A.
Here dot denotes an OUTWARD log-radius derivative. Consequently
 P>=0, n=2P, dot P=q-4P^2>=0, dot log H=4P.                (3.1)

These identities are kernel tests of the actual measures, not pointwise
tests of pi at the center. The compact kernels and their required parameter
and spatial derivatives are continuous for p>=4 and admit the required
smooth-test approximations. Local finite measure on common interior rooms
therefore gives the identities at EVERY admissible center/radius. Also
A and H, and hence P by (2.2), are continuous in (x,s) on such rooms.
There is no exceptional set of centers inherited from a.e. metric
differentiability, nor from a blowup or Frostman selection.

4. Every apex point has the lower and finite upper frequency bounds

Fix ANY x in Z. Canonical local Lipschitz continuity gives
 r(y)<=L_x|y-x| and H(x,s)<=C_x s^2 for all sufficiently small s.
By (3.1), P is outward nondecreasing and has a finite inward limit P_0>=0,
bounded by P at any fixed interior radius. If P_0<1/2, choose
P_0<c<1/2 and s_0 so that P(x,s)<=c for0<s<s_0. Integrating (3.1)
backward gives H(x,s)>=H(x,s_0)(s/s_0)^{4c}, contradicting H<=C_x s^2.
Therefore
              P(x,s)>=1/2 at EVERY admissible radius.      (4.1)
This argument applies to every apex point, irrespective of its density,
vanishing order, membership in a prior measure-selected set, or whether a
unique tangent map has been constructed.

For the upper bound choose any one positive interior radius s_x. The
numerator A(x,s_x) is finite by local finiteness and compact kernel support;
H(x,s_x) is finite and strictly positive by (2.2). Thus
                 N_x=max(1,P(x,s_x))<infinity.
Outward monotonicity immediately gives
            1/2<=P(x,s)<=N_x for EVERY0<s<=s_x.             (4.2)
No universal cap over all minimizers or all apex points is needed. A
pointwise cap follows at every point from one finite actual kernel value.

5. A single finite cap on each compact exhaustion

To apply a fixed-N version of R without any ambiguity, set
 K_j={x: |x|<=j, dist(x,R^m\Omega)>=1/j}, j=1,2,...,
where distance to the empty complement is infinity. These are compact

Choose the common radius s_j=1/(4j). For every x in K_j the closed ball
Bbar_{2s_j}(x) lies in one compact interior neighborhood of K_j.
The continuity just established and (2.2) imply
 h_j=min_{x in K_j}H(x,s_j)>0,

Take the finite integer
                    N_j=ceil(max(1,a_j/h_j)).
For every x in Z intersect K_j and every0<s<s_j, (3.1) and (4.1) give
                    1/2<=P(x,s)<=N_j.                    (5.1)
Thus even a common cutoff and common finite cap are available on each
compact stratum. The constants may depend on u, Omega, j and p; no uniform
data-independent frequency assertion is made.

6. Apply R and take the countable union

Canonical continuity makes Z closed relative to Omega, so Z_j=Z intersect
K_j is compact/Borel. With the SAME tuple (2.1), all its points belong to
the full good-center locus G_{N_j} in supplied R. Hence Z_j is sigma-finite
for H^{m-1} and countably(m-1)-rectifiable: a Borel subset inherits both
properties from G_{N_j}. Explicitly, intersect Z_j with each finite-measure
piece of its sigma-finite cover; restrict the countable Lipschitz-image
cover, keeping only the inherited H^{m-1}-null remainder.

Since Z is the union of the Z_j, combine those countable finite-measure
pieces and Lipschitz-image families. Countable subadditivity preserves the

Neither the previous bound dim_H Z<=m-1 nor any inference from dimension

7. Scope controls and exact dependency status

A scalar harmonic x_1 map into a geodesic line in a surface cone has
Z={x_1=0}, pi=e_1 e_1^t and P=1/2. Its hyperplane is the sharp dimensional
and rectifiable control. In contrast, a nonapex constant Sobolev map changed
to apex on a null dimension-m set violates the claim for an arbitrary
measurable representative; the canonical representative condition remains
necessary. An identically-apex map has nu=0 and does not satisfy positive
height. The positive-curvature sub-Lipschitz tip control has P<1/2 and does
not meet R's center threshold. None refutes the present canonical theorem.

R supplies sigma-finiteness and rectifiability, not automatically local
finite H^{m-1} measure, smoothness, unique tangents at every point, or a
uniform chart count. Those stronger properties are not asserted. For m=1,
the supplied conclusion makes Z countable via sigma-finite H^0.
