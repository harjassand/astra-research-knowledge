## Result: a candidate sharp law for preserving statistical information in finite memory

I derived **matching upper and lower bounds for the memory needed to preserve a Gaussian statistical experiment when signal strength, directional sensitivity, and reconstruction accuracy change together**.

The upper bound is achieved by an explicit classical construction. The lower bound allows arbitrary quantum or hybrid memory, including encoders that jointly process all coordinates.

This advances a specific unresolved interface in the repository: its existing Gaussian result fixes the parameter set, whereas the new argument controls parameter ranges that shrink or grow with the requested accuracy. **The main theorem has a written proof, but it has not been independently reviewed or cleared for historical novelty. I would not yet describe it as a verified revolutionary breakthrough.**

## 1. How the investigation converged on this problem

I began with `00_START_HERE.txt` and `01_CORE.txt`, then screened selected mechanisms in exact-oracle learning, geometric/group constructions, and statistical/quantum reconstruction.

The learning card already identifies a substantial acquisition barrier and a correction to an earlier novelty claim. The nonsofic-hyperbolic card contains a high-consequence candidate, but also substantial unreviewed geometric dependencies and astronomical acquisition costs. Neither was a sound premise for an immediate downstream theorem without first reconstructing those dependencies.  

The Gaussian branch exposed a more self-contained opportunity. Card N07 records, for a **fixed bounded mean set** \(\Theta\) of affine dimension \(s\),

\[
e_D(\Theta)\asymp_{\Theta}D^{-2/s}.
\]

Here \(e_D\) is the best uniform reconstruction error through memory of dimension \(D\). The dependence of the constants on \(\Theta\) is crucial: this statement alone does not determine what happens when the signal range changes with the error tolerance. 

Card N08 explicitly leaves simultaneous changing-signal and precision laws unresolved. It also distinguishes the pure Gaussian problem from applications requiring a separately justified quantum-to-Gaussian comparison. 

Going into the proof, then expanding outward again, exposed three connected targets: a uniform weak/strong-signal law, an anisotropic version for unequal directions, and a consequence for known linear Gaussian inverse problems. The resulting theorem addresses all three without assuming an unverified OpenAI-release theorem or an inherited quantum Gaussian approximation.

## 2. The main theorem

### The experiment and what is being preserved

Suppose we observe one vector

\[
X\sim N(\theta,I_s),
\qquad
\theta\in\prod_{i=1}^{s}[-R_i,R_i].
\]

The dimension \(s\) is fixed. The bounds \(R_i\) are known, but **the encoder does not know \(\theta\)**. It receives only the observation \(X\).

The encoder stores information in a finite-dimensional classical, quantum, or hybrid memory. For a hybrid memory algebra

\[
\mathcal A=\bigoplus_j M_{d_j},
\]

the charged dimension is

\[
D=\sum_j d_j.
\]

Every input-dependent retained register counts. Independent shared classical randomness is permitted, but free entangled references and uncharged input-dependent side information are not.

The decoder produces a fresh observation \(Y\). The requirement is

\[
\sup_{\theta}
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(\mathcal L_\theta(Y),N(\theta,I_s)\right)
\le \epsilon.
\]

This is **preservation of the statistical experiment**, not recovery of the particular observed point \(X\).

### Candidate theorem: the anisotropic memory law

Define

\[
\psi_\epsilon(R)
=
\max\left\{
1,\sqrt{\frac{R}{\epsilon}},
\frac{R}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}
\right\}.
\]

Let \(D_{\mathrm q}\) be the minimum admissible quantum/hybrid dimension, and \(D_{\mathrm{cl}}\) the minimum classical alphabet size.

The derived result is

\[
\boxed{
c_s\prod_{i=1}^{s}\psi_\epsilon(R_i)
\;\le\;
D_{\mathrm q}(\epsilon;\mathbf R)
\;\le\;
D_{\mathrm{cl}}(\epsilon;\mathbf R)
\;\le\;
C_s\prod_{i=1}^{s}\psi_\epsilon(R_i).
}
\]

For each fixed \(s\), this holds for sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\), **simultaneously for all nonnegative radii \(R_i\)**. The constants and small-error threshold depend only on \(s\), not on the radii.

Equivalently, the memory cost in logarithmic dimension is

\[
\boxed{
B_{\mathrm{opt}}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\log_2\psi_\epsilon(R_i)+O_s(1).
}
\]

The significant point is uniformity: the radii may themselves depend on \(\epsilon\), with some tending to zero and others to infinity.

For one direction, the three regimes are:

| Signal range | Memory contribution, up to fixed-dimensional constants |
|---|---:|
| Below the accuracy scale | Constant |
| Weak but relevant: \(\epsilon\lesssim R\lesssim1\) | \(\sqrt{R/\epsilon}\) labels |
| Strong: \(R\gtrsim1\) | \(R/\sqrt{\epsilon}\) labels |

These are asymptotic orders, not an exact engineering bit budget. The proof does not establish optimal constants.

### Isotropic form

For the Euclidean mean ball \(\|\theta\|_2\le R\), the result can be written directly as an error law:

\[
\boxed{
e_D(R)\asymp_s
\begin{cases}
R\,D^{-2/s},&0<R\le1,\\[2mm]
\min\{1,R^2D^{-2/s}\},&R\ge1.
\end{cases}
}
\]

This holds for every integer \(D\ge1\). At \(R=0\), one label suffices exactly because the decoder can generate the known distribution.

**The weak-signal dependence is linear in \(R\), not quadratic.** Obtaining that dependence on both sides is one of the essential parts of the proof.

## 3. Why the proof requires more than inserting a radius into the old theorem

The full argument is in the PROOF.md[proof artifact](sandbox:/mnt/data/astra_gaussian_multiscale_20261007/PROOF.md). Its essential mechanisms are as follows.

### A. Strong signals: change the reference geometry

A fixed reference distribution centred at zero can produce approximation constants that grow very badly when the allowed means move far from zero.

I instead use a Cauchy reference whose scale follows the **known range**, not the unknown mean:

\[
g_L(x)=\frac{L}{\pi(L^2+x^2)},
\qquad
L=1+R.
\]

Its cumulative distribution transforms the real line to the unit interval:

\[
u=G_L(x)=\frac12+\frac1\pi\arctan(x/L).
\]

The transformed Gaussian density is

\[
q_\theta(G_L(x))
=
\frac{\phi(x-\theta)}{g_L(x)}.
\]

The calculation gives the uniform bound

\[
\|q_\theta''\|_1\le A_0L^2,
\qquad
A_0<3427,
\qquad |\theta|\le R.
\]

Positive triangular interpolation kernels then provide a genuine stochastic encoder and decoder with \(J\) labels and error

\[
\operatorname{TV}\le C R^2J^{-2}
\qquad (R\ge1).
\]

The construction does not truncate a Gaussian tail, so it introduces no tail logarithm. It also never needs the unknown mean.

### B. Weak signals: preserve the zero-signal distribution exactly

The preceding construction has a serious weakness when \(R\to0\): it need not reproduce \(N(0,1)\) exactly. An approximation error that remains at zero signal can overwhelm the small statistical effect we are trying to retain.

The repository’s reference-conditional decoding mechanism suggested a way around this, although its quantum summary is not used as an unproved premise here. 

Let \(a_k(G(x))\) be the encoder’s probability of retaining label \(k\), where the functions \(a_k\) form a positive triangular partition of unity. Define

\[
\alpha_k
=
\int a_k(G(x))\phi(x)\,dx.
\]

Decode label \(k\) using

\[
\Gamma_k(dy)
=
\frac{a_k(G(y))\phi(y)}{\alpha_k}\,dy.
\]

For the zero-signal distribution, the output density is exactly

\[
\sum_k\alpha_k
\frac{a_k(G(y))\phi(y)}{\alpha_k}
=
\phi(y)\sum_k a_k(G(y))
=
\phi(y).
\]

Thus the decoder introduces **no baseline error**.

The remaining task is to bound how it acts on the likelihood perturbation

\[
\frac{\phi(x-\theta)}{\phi(x)}-1
=
e^{\theta x-\theta^2/2}-1.
\]

A central-region estimate and a separate reversible-kernel tail estimate give

\[
\operatorname{TV}\le C|\theta|J^{-2},
\]

uniformly for \(|\theta|\le1\). Consequently,

\[
\sup_{|\theta|\le R}\operatorname{TV}
\le CRJ^{-2},
\qquad 0<R\le1.
\]

This is an explicit, unseeded classical construction. It does not depend on fitting or storing an estimate of \(\theta\).

### C. The weak-signal lower bound needs a different decision loss

For the converse, consider the two possible means \(-R/2\) and \(+R/2\). Their posterior probability is

\[
p_+(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-Rx}}.
\]

On a fixed central interval, the posterior changes on scale \(R\).

Ordinary squared posterior error therefore gives a lower bound proportional to \(R^2\), which is too weak. The adjustment is to use the convex function

\[
F_R(p)
=
\sqrt{R^2+\|p-u\|^2},
\qquad
u=(1/2,1/2),
\]

and its associated proper scoring loss.

That loss remains bounded independently of \(R\), while its curvature near the central posterior is of order \(1/R\). The exact posterior-risk identity then connects uniform reconstruction error to a divergence satisfying

\[
D_{F_R}(p,q)
\ge
cR
\min\left\{
\frac{\|p-q\|^2}{R^2},1
\right\}.
\]

This inequality remains valid when the decoder’s posterior target \(q\) lies far outside the central region.

The mechanism is useful beyond this example:

> A converse for a shrinking statistical model may need a bounded decision loss whose curvature follows the shrinking posterior scale.

Here, that adjustment recovers the correct linear dependence on signal strength.

### D. The quantum lower bound is a joint bound, not separate scalar bounds

A collection of one-coordinate lower bounds would establish only a maximum over coordinates. It would not prove the product in the theorem, especially against a quantum encoder that mixes all coordinates.

The proof instead uses one joint posterior event.

For an encoded state \(\sigma_x\),

\[
0\le \sigma_x\le I.
\]

If the decoder uses a positive-operator-valued measure \(M(dy)\), its trace measure has total mass \(D\). Consequently, for any relation \(A(x,y)\),

\[
\Pr\{A(X,Y)\}
\le
D\sup_y\Pr_X\{A(X,y)\}.
\]

A product prior makes the source probability of a joint small posterior neighbourhood factor across directions. The bounded-loss argument forces the actual encoder–decoder coupling to enter such neighbourhoods sufficiently often. Combining those facts yields the product lower bound.

This argument permits mixed quantum encodings, continuous decoder outputs, coupled coordinates, and independent shared seeds. It does **not** assume a coordinatewise encoder.

The conclusion is equality of **memory order**, not equality of optimal classical and quantum constants.

## 4. Consequences obtained from the theorem

### Known linear Gaussian inverse problems

Consider the supplied model

\[
Y=A\vartheta+Z,
\qquad
Z\sim N(0,\Sigma),
\qquad
\|\vartheta\|_2\le1.
\]

Whitening and taking a singular-value decomposition turns the informative part into a Gaussian mean ellipsoid. Its semiaxes are the nonzero singular values of

\[
\Sigma^{-1/2}A.
\]

Call them \(R_1,\ldots,R_s\). The candidate theorem then gives

\[
B_{\mathrm{opt}}
=
\sum_i\log_2
\max\left\{
1,\sqrt{\frac{R_i}{\epsilon}},
\frac{R_i}{\sqrt\epsilon}
\right\}
+O_s(1).
\]

This gives a sensitivity-dependent allocation rule: directions below the accuracy scale need little memory; weak informative directions and strong directions have different costs.

The restriction is substantial: the model and noise covariance are known, and the rank is fixed. This does not establish a model-free archive for arbitrary future scientific questions.

### Exact finite-memory law for iid normal samples

For \(n\) independent observations from \(N(\mu,I_s)\), an orthogonal transformation separates

\[
\sqrt n\,\bar X\sim N(\sqrt n\,\mu,I_s)
\]

from parameter-independent Gaussian residuals.

The residuals can be regenerated. This is an exact reduction, not an asymptotic Gaussian approximation.

For coordinate ranges \(|\mu_i|\le\delta_i\), substitute

\[
R_i=\sqrt n\,\delta_i
\]

into the main theorem.

In particular, with fixed nonzero ranges and \(n\to\infty\), \(\epsilon\to0\),

\[
B_{\mathrm{opt}}
=
\frac{s}{2}\log_2 n
+
\frac{s}{2}\log_2(1/\epsilon)
+
\sum_i\log_2\delta_i
+
O_s(1).
\]

The full product formula also covers ranges that shrink with \(n\) and \(\epsilon\), where this simplified expression no longer applies.

### Preservation of downstream decisions

The reconstruction criterion has an immediate operational consequence. For every decision rule \(d\) and loss \(0\le\ell\le1\),

\[
\left|
\mathbb E_\theta\ell(\theta,d(Y))
-
\mathbb E_\theta\ell(\theta,d(X))
\right|
\le\epsilon.
\]

Thus the archive supports replay of every bounded statistical decision rule **within the declared model class**, not just one chosen estimator.

It does not preserve the particular sample, its correlations with an external record, or inference under an unspecified replacement model.

### Conditional transfer to other experiments

Suppose another experiment has parameter-independent channels to and from the Gaussian experiment, each with uniform error at most \(\eta\). Composition gives

\[
|e_D(\mathcal E)-e_D(\mathcal G)|\le2\eta.
\]

Therefore the new memory law is useful at precision \(\epsilon\) only when the comparison error is sufficiently smaller than \(\epsilon\).

This keeps the repository’s quantum-comparison boundary intact: **the Gaussian theorem does not itself prove the quantum approximation or its efficient implementation.**

## 6. Novelty, significance, and what remains unresolved

The underlying subject is not new. Hayashi and Tan already establish leading compression rates for approximate sufficient statistics. Yang and collaborators already develop parametric quantum-population compression, hybrid memories, and Gaussian compression/decompression constructions. Their inspected results are important prior-art boundaries against presenting statistical-experiment compression itself as a discovery. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.02542v2)

The recent refresh also located Haapasalo’s June 2026 work on continuous statistical-experiment comparison and Girotti–Kiukas–Guţă’s March 2026 work on quantum Markov-chain statistical limits. I inspected their abstracts and metadata, not their complete proofs. They identify adjacent interfaces, but I did not acquire a theorem from either that establishes the present finite-memory law or an unconditional quantum application. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31296)

The defensible candidate contribution is narrower and precise:

**A radius-uniform, anisotropic reconstruction law, with an explicit classical upper construction and a matching converse against arbitrary admissible quantum memory, including the linear weak-signal regime.**

The most reusable combination is **exact reference preservation in the construction, scale-adjusted bounded losses in the converse, and joint posterior geometry for tensorization**.

Several consequential questions remain outside the proved scope. Thin curved mean sets, including changing-radius spheres and unitary orbits, need additional geometry. Growing dimension needs controlled constants rather than \(O_s(1)\) notation. Physical quantum applications need uniform, costed comparison and preparation channels. The ideal continuous-output constructions also require a declared observation convention before making finite-bit implementation claims.

No exact equivalent of the uniform anisotropic theorem was located in the inspected material, but the literature search does not establish historical priority. The result should remain a candidate until independently reconstructed.
