## Stronger drift interface acquired from V→0

Put d(r)=sup_(|y|≥r/2)|V(y)|. At x with |x|=r choose

    L(r)=min(r/4,d(r)^(−1/2)),

interpreting the second entry as infinity when d=0. Then L→infinity and L²||V||_(B(x,L))≤1. Scaling that ball to unit size, positive-solution Harnack and the Poisson interior gradient estimate give

    |Dphi(x)|/phi(x)≤C/L(r)→0.

The gradient bound needs bounded RHS on the scaled ball, supplied by the equation and positive Harnack; it does not require DV→0. Consequently g=log(phi) has Dg→0 uniformly in the tail. Integrating along ordinary exterior radial paths gives, for each epsilon>0,

    C_epsilon^(−1)e^(−epsilon r)≤phi(x)≤C_epsilon e^(epsilon r).

For v=u/phi, the exact transformed equation and drift constraints are

    div(phi² Dv)=0,
    Delta v+b·Dv=0, b=2Dg→0,
    curl b=0, div b=2V−|b|²/2→0.

The all-exponential property transfers between u and v. These extra curl/divergence constraints distinguish the route from arbitrary small vector drift and from arbitrary uniformly elliptic A. They do not alone acquire a theorem for signed exterior solutions.

## Signed energy identity: positivity does not cap frequency

Write w=phi². Assuming the fast-decay candidate, define

    H(r)=∫_(S_r)w v²=∫_(S_r)u²,
    Q(r)=∫_(|x|>r)w|Dv|²,
    A(r)=∫_(S_r)w|partial_r v|²,
    B(r)=∫_(S_r)w|Dv|²,
    eta(r)=H(r)^(−1)∫_(S_r)partial_r(log w) wv².

Weighted gradient integrability follows from bounded V, the u equation and the vanishing/bounded Dg. Fast decay legitimates integrations by parts through outer cutoffs or a vanishing-boundary subsequence. On radii with H>0,

    Q(r)=−∫_(S_r)wv partial_r v,
    Q'(r)=−B(r),
    H'(r)=[(n−1)/r+eta(r)]H(r)−2Q(r).               (1)

The inner boundary term Q is nonnegative but may be arbitrarily large compared with H. Thus positivity produces an upper differential inequality for H, not a lower-decay bound. In the free scalar case phi=1, the exact exterior harmonic state

    v(r,theta)=(R/r)^(ell+n−2)Y_ell(theta)

has Q/H=(ell+n−2)/r. Degrees ell are unbounded even with V=b=0. This is not a superexponential counterexample: each fixed state has a fixed polynomial tail. It does decisively reject a frequency bound determined solely by drift smallness, ellipticity and the inner radius, without boundary-complexity dependence.

A more exact scalar Rellich identity exposes the remaining control. For the vector field

    T=w[|Dv|² x−2(x·Dv)Dv],

direct differentiation and div(w Dv)=0 give

    div T=[n−2+x·D(log w)]w|Dv|².

All angular derivative terms cancel here because the leading scalar coefficient is wI. Integrate on the exterior to obtain

    r(2A−B)=(n−2)Q+J,
    J(r)=∫_(|x|>r)[x·D(log w)]w|Dv|².

For N=rQ/H,

    N'=−2r[A/H−(Q/H)²]+J/H−r eta Q/H.              (2)

Cauchy–Schwarz makes the bracket nonnegative. The last two signed coefficient terms remain. The available b→0 controls |x·b| only by o(r); it gives no bounded or sign-definite estimate on those terms. Dropping them is precisely an invalid implication in this route.

As a restricted diagnostic, if |x·D(log w)|≤epsilon eventually, equation(2) implies N'≤2epsilon N/r. Hence N≤C r^(2epsilon). Equation(1) then gives a lower bound H≥c r^(n−1−epsilon)exp(−C r^(2epsilon)) when epsilon>0, or a polynomial bound at epsilon=0. If epsilon≤1/2 this excludes an all-exponential candidate. This conditional weighted-frequency argument is not asserted as new and is not obtained from V→0 or finite index. A radial positive tail phi=exp(sqrt(r)), for example, has V=Delta phi/phi→0 but x·Dlog(w)=sqrt(r), illustrating the missing rate. Its potential can be completed smoothly; exterior form positivity is immediate from phi.

## Stochastic representation retains signed cancellation

For the diffusion with generator Delta+b·D, stopped on a smooth inner sphere and an outer sphere, Itô's formula represents v by its two boundary traces. Taking the outer radius to infinity for a bounded v→0 removes that term and gives

    v(x)=E_x[f(X_tau) 1_(tau<infinity)],

where f is the signed inner trace and tau the inner hitting time. The kernel is positive; the integrand need not be. A lower bound by a positive boundary minimum is unavailable. Cancellation can suppress many angular moments even in the free case above. No superexponential signed example or universal no-cancellation theorem is acquired from this representation.
