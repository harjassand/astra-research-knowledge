Theorem 1.1 (`thm:main`) concerns fan-in-two **division-free ordered formula trees**, where size counts all leaves and gates. It gives one rational tuple for each `n,s >= 1`, independent of the formula and all coefficient values, of dimension `2 n s^2` except for the `n=s=1` branch. It detects every nonzero such polynomial over **every characteristic-zero field**. Entry bit lengths are `O(log(ns+1)+n s^2 log(n+1))`. Construction takes polynomial bit time; evaluation over an arbitrary field is an exact oracle operation, whose bit cost is not included.

Proposition 5.1 (`prop:path-programs`) is stronger in a different parameterization. If

\[
 f_w=uB_wv,\qquad B_w=B_{i_1}\cdots B_{i_k},

with all `B_i` strictly upper triangular `m x m` matrices, then the same entry formula hits using

\[
 d=(n-1)\binom m2+m.

Every acyclic `m`-vertex ABP with affine-linear edge labels has this representation, without extra vertices. This is not a claim about shared arbitrary circuits or all cyclic weighted automata.

## 2. Independent reconstruction of the source argument

For each letter `i`, let

\[
 J_i a=\int_0^z \frac{a(t)}{t+i}\,dt,\qquad
 h_\epsilon=1,\quad h_{iw}=J_i h_w.
\]

All coefficients are rational, and `ord(J_i a) >= ord(a)+1`.

**Finite-word independence (Lemma 2.1).** Fix word-length cutoff `L`. In the truncated free algebra with basis `X_w`, `|w| <= L`, put `U=sum h_w X_w`. It satisfies `U'=A U`, `A=sum X_i/(z+i)`, and `U(0)=1`. For a based loop around pole `-i`, the continued value is `Q_i=1+tau X_i mod(nonempty words)^2`, with `tau=2 pi i`. Continuation of a solution initially equal to `b` is `U_path b`; therefore following loop `gamma` and then `delta` produces `Q_delta Q_gamma`. The set of loop values is closed under every ordered product despite this reversal.

For word `w=i_1...i_k`, the product `C_w=(Q_i1-1)...(Q_ik-1)` lies in the linear span of loop values. It has no degrees below `k` and degree-`k` term `tau^k X_w`. These `C_w` form a triangular basis of the truncated algebra. A linear functional annihilating the analytic germ `U` annihilates its loop values and hence the whole algebra. This proves complex independence without a hidden commutativity assumption. For an arbitrary characteristic-zero field, rational Taylor coefficients have a finite nonzero rational minor, obtained from the finite-dimensional descending kernel filtration. The minor stays nonzero under the canonical rational-field embedding. No embedding of the arbitrary field into the complex numbers is required.

**Formula-to-system conversion (Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2).** The source/sink graph for a leaf has two vertices. Addition takes disjoint graphs and adds two vertices; multiplication concatenates disjoint graphs with a scalar edge. With `L` leaves and `a` addition gates it has `m=2L+2a <= 2s` vertices. In topological order, let `E` be the scalar adjacency and `N_i` the letter adjacency, and put `Q=(I-E)^-1`. Scalar-path elimination gives `u=e_source^T Q`, `B_i=N_i Q`, `v=e_sink`, and `f_w=uB_wv`. Each `B_i` remains strictly upper triangular. Thus

\[
 G=\sum_{|w|<m}B_wh_w,\quad G(0)=I,\quad
 G'=\Bigl(\sum_i \frac{B_i}{z+i}\Bigr)G.
\]

The order of the letters matches the left differential equation. With `q=prod(z+i)`, the numerator `P=sum B_i q/(z+i)` has degree at most `n-1`, and `q(0)=n! != 0`. Also `uGv=h_f`, which is nonzero by finite-word independence.

**Multiplicity (Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.2).** This part does not require triangular `B_i`. For any invertible formal solution `G'=P G/q`, put `h=uG` and let the span of its components have dimension `k <= m`. Define derivative rows

\[
 R_0=u,\quad R_{j+1}=R'_j+R_jP/q,
 \qquad R_j=Z_j/q^j.
\]

Then

\[
 Z_{j+1}=qZ'_j-jq'Z_j+Z_jP,\qquad
 \deg Z_j\le j(n-1).
\]

The matrix of the first `k` derivative rows, before multiplication by `G`, has maximal minors whose denominator is `q^S`, `S=binom(k,2)`, and numerator degree at most `(n-1)S`. Some maximal minor is nonzero because `k` independent components have nonzero formal Wronskian. Their minimum valuation `delta` is at most `(n-1)S`. Cauchy–Binet, applied both to multiplication by `G` and to multiplication by its formal inverse, preserves the ideal of maximal minors and its minimum valuation. This is the step that prevents cancellation among the transformed minors from weakening the argument.

A basis of the component span has distinct orders `a_1<...<a_k`. The Wronskian valuation is exactly `sum a_j-binomial(k,2)`, by the falling-factorial Vandermonde determinant. Consequently every nonzero `y=uGv` has

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_0 y\le a_k
 \le\delta+k-1
 \le(n-1)\binom m2+m-1.
\]

The special cases `n=1`, vanishing `P`, non-full component span, and basis orders below a derivative index do not invalidate the proof.

**Explicit finite operators and rational bits.** On jets of degrees below `d`, in the column basis `1,z,...,z^(d-1)`,

\[
 (T_i)_{r,q}=
 \frac{(-1)^{r-q-1}}{r i^{r-q}},\quad 0\le q<r<d,
\]

and all other entries are zero. The first column of `f(T)` is exactly the jet of `h_f`. Taking `d` one above the multiplicity bound proves detection. For formulas, `m <= 2s` and the stated `2ns^2` bound dominates this value. Denominators have at most `B=3+ceil(log2(d+1))+d ceil(log2(n+1))` bits; the numerator is `+/-1`, so no reduction cost is hidden. Repeated powers and denominator formation use `O(nd^2)` integer operations of at most `B` bits, giving the source's schoolbook `O(nd^2 B^2)` construction and `O(nd^2 B)` binary output bounds.

## 3. Adversarial examples and exact boundaries

The commutator `x1 x2-x2 x1` has degree two but its jet starts at `-z^3/24`. Thus word degree alone cannot replace the multiplicity estimate. The executable check learns this polynomial from output-only matrix evaluations and obtains rank four.

There is a general sharp linear witness. Set

\[
 c_i=\frac{(-i)^{n-1}}{\prod_{j\ne i}(j-i)},\qquad
 L(x)=\sum_i c_i x_i.
\]

Partial fractions give `sum c_i/(z+i)=z^(n-1)/prod(z+i)`. Hence `h_L` has order exactly `n`, attaining the source's bound for `m=2`; first-column jet length `n+1` is necessary for this tuple. More generally

\[
 \operatorname{ord} h_{L^k}=nk,\qquad
 [z^{nk}]h_{L^k}=\frac1{(n!\,n)^k k!}.
\]

The proof follows by induction, since the integration operator with kernel `z^(n-1)/q(z)` raises order by exactly `n`. This illustrates cancellation and factorially small coefficients within a small layered ABP. A finite precision stability bound does not follow from exact hitting.

There is also a general lower bound on this first-column interface. Every homogeneous binary polynomial of degree `2k` can be represented on `m=3*2^k-2` acyclic states. Use left-prefix trie states of lengths `0,...,k-1`, right-suffix trie states of lengths `0,...,k`, and bridge each left prefix `a` to each suffix `b` by the affine label `sum_i c_(ai,b) x_i`. The right trie then emits exactly the remaining suffix. The total state count is `(2^k-1)+(2^(k+1)-1)`. All `4^k` coefficient choices are free. For any fixed tuple and fixed seed, the map from this coefficient space to `f(T)e_0` is linear. Faithfulness therefore requires `d >= 4^k=(m+2)^2/9`. Thus the source's quadratic state dimension is near optimal up to a constant for universal **first-column** features at `n=2`. This does not lower-bound full-matrix hitting beyond the corresponding square-root dimension constraint, and does not certify optimality of the length-marked cyclic dimension.

All `T_i` are strictly triangular. The nonzero monomial `x1^d` evaluates to zero on dimension `d`. A shared circuit can obtain exponentially high degree by repeated squaring with logarithmic gate count. This defeats extending the formula theorem to unbounded-degree shared circuits with the same dimension rule. It does not contradict the tree or ABP theorem.

Scalar black boxes cannot distinguish the commutator from zero, even with unlimited exact scalar queries. The noncommutative matrix interface is substantive information access.

Unmarked hyperlog encoding also fails for infinite cyclic series. For

\[
 R=\sum_{k\ge1}x_1^k-2\sum_{k\ge1}x_2^k
\]

there is a three-state proper recognizable representation, and `R` is nonzero. Since `h_(i^k)=log(1+z/i)^k/k!`, its unmarked image is

\[
 e^{\log(1+z)}-1-2\bigl(e^{\log(1+z/2)}-1\bigr)=0.
\]

Our exact run verifies that the original dimension-six first-column feature vanishes on this series. This refutes a naive cyclic extension of finite-word independence. The following construction repairs that exact failure.

## 4. A length-marked hitting tuple for proper recognizable series

A **proper recognizable series** here means a formal series with a supplied existence promise

\[
 R=\sum_w (uB_wv)x_w,
\]

where `u,B_i,v` are constant matrices/vectors of some dimension at most `m`; the `B_i` may be arbitrary and cyclic. Its matrix evaluation on a jointly nilpotent tuple `X` is the finite sum above, equivalently

\[
 R(X)=(u\otimes I)\Bigl(I-\sum_i B_i\otimes X_i\Bigr)^{-1}(v\otimes I).
\]

The inverse exists because of joint nilpotency. This series semantics is stronger than an arbitrary syntactic rational-expression oracle: an equivalent expression such as `x1^-1 x1` can still be undefined on nilpotent substitutions.

Fix

\[
 d=(n-1)\binom m2+m,
\]

and form the source's `T_i` of dimension `d`. Let `S_d` be the nilpotent matrix multiplying by `lambda` on `F[lambda]/(lambda^d)`. Define

\[
 W_i=S_d\otimes T_i,\qquad D=d^2.
\]

**Derived hitting theorem.** For every nonzero proper recognizable series of state dimension at most `m` over any characteristic-zero field,

\[
 R(W_1,\ldots,W_n)(e_0\otimes e_0)\ne0.
\]

The tuple is rational, depends only on `n,m`, has polynomial dimension and construction bit cost, and is jointly nilpotent.

**Proof.** Work first over `F(lambda)` and put

\[
 G_\lambda=\sum_w\lambda^{|w|}B_wh_w(z).
\]

Every coefficient of `z^r` is a polynomial in `lambda` of degree at most `r`, since `h_w` has order at least `|w|`. Thus the infinite sum is a legitimate member of `Mat_m(F[lambda][[z]])`, has constant matrix `I`, and satisfies `G_lambda'=lambda A G_lambda`. A nonzero homogeneous word-length slice of `R` has a nonzero finite hyperlog image, so `uG_lambda v` is nonzero. The source's multiplicity theorem applies over `F(lambda)` with the original state dimension `m`, denominator `prod(z+i)`, and numerator degree at most `n-1`. Some coefficient of `z^r`, `r<d`, is nonzero. Each retained coefficient has `lambda` degree below `d`; reduction modulo `lambda^d` therefore preserves it. The first seed column of `R(S_d tensor T_i)` is precisely the retained coefficient array, indexed by word length and by `z` degree. This proves the claim. Products of `d` of the `W_i` vanish, making all required proper-realization evaluations defined. No complex embedding of `F(lambda)` is needed: the source's field-transfer and multiplicity statements hold over every characteristic-zero field. QED.

For acyclic series one can use `D=d` and `W_i=T_i` directly. In the marked construction, entry rational bit length remains at most `B`; the extra marker entries are zero or one. Dense output has at most `O(n d^4 B)` bits, and exploiting the tensor sparsity improves the number of nonzero records. Both models have polynomial construction cost.

## 5. Exact output-only acquisition theorem

**Interface.** The learner receives `n`, a valid state bound `m`, and only an oracle returning the final output matrix `R(X)` for any rational jointly nilpotent tuple `X`. It does not see the formula, transition matrices, individual gates, coefficients of words, or equivalence-query answers. Arithmetic over `F` is exact. This is not an iid sample oracle, a scalar-evaluation oracle, or an ordinary fixed-length membership oracle.

Let `r` be the dimension of the left-residual span of `R` (its minimal Hankel rank), so `r <= m`. The learner constructs an equivalent `r`-state representation using at most

\[
 1+n r\ \text{oracle calls of matrix dimension at most }D+r,
\]

where `D=d` in the acyclic case and `D=d^2` in the general proper case. Query descriptions and the additional field work are polynomial in `n,m`. The output has `n r^2+2r` field entries. The zero series produces a zero-state representation.

**Residual feature and orientation.** For prefix `w`, define

\[
 R_w=\sum_v R_{wv}x_v,\quad
 \phi(R_w)=e_0^T R_w(W_1^T,\ldots,W_n^T).
\]

Here `e_0` is the first seed in the chosen `D`-dimensional tuple. Crucially the suffix matrices are **transposed**. Indeed `phi(g)=(rev(g)(W)e_0)^T`. Reversal of any residual is recognizable with the same state bound: its initial row is `v^T`, its transitions are `B_i^T`, and its final column is `(uB_w)^T`. In the acyclic case an anti-diagonal simultaneous conjugation restores strict upper triangularity. Every linear combination of left residuals has the same common transitions and some initial row, so the hitting theorem makes `phi` injective on the entire residual span.

**One output query extracts a residual feature.** For a prefix of length `k`, append a suffix block `C_i=W_i^T` to `k` scalar prefix states. The prefix chain contains a unit edge for the requested letter at each position. Its last edge enters coordinate `e_0` of the suffix block. Let this tuple of `(D+k) x (D+k)` matrices be `X_i(w)`. The source-row/suffix-column block of `R(X(w))` equals `e_0^T R_w(C)`: every path reaching the suffix must first traverse exactly `w`, then any suffix word. The whole tuple is strictly upper triangular. At `k=0`, query `R(C)` and take its first row. This proves residual extraction without a derivative or internal-gate oracle and accounts for all matrix dimensions.

**Breadth-first reconstruction.** Query the empty prefix feature. Keep any feature outside the span of those already selected; for every selected prefix `p`, query its `n` children `p i`. A dependency among features is a true dependency among residual series by injectivity. Left differentiation therefore preserves each dependency, so children of a dependent prefix need not be explored. At most `r` prefixes are selected. Once their children are processed the span is invariant under all derivatives and contains the empty residual; it contains every residual and is complete.

Express each selected-child residual as `R_(p i)=sum_q A_i[p,q] R_q` by Gaussian elimination on features. Express `R` itself by coordinates `alpha`, and let `beta_q` be the constant coefficient of `R_q`, namely the first entry of its feature. Derivative induction gives `R_w=alpha A_w` in this residual basis, hence the coefficient identity `R_w(empty)=alpha A_w beta` for every word. Thus the constructed representation is equivalent and minimal. Every selected prefix has selected ancestors, so its length is at most `r-1`; its children have length at most `r`. Only `1+nr` distinct prefixes are queried. QED.

**Complete cost scope.** The feature dimension is `D`, at most `O(n^2 m^4)` for cyclic series. A straightforward Gaussian implementation uses polynomially many exact field operations; the supplied implementation intentionally uses a simple solver rather than a claimed optimal arithmetic bound. One may charge `O(n r^3 D)` scalar operations for repeated coordinate solves, plus polynomial query construction/output work. The black box must return `(D+k)^2` field entries even though the learner retains only a row. Evaluation cost belongs in the ledger and is not one unit of scalar observation.

Over the rationals, bit complexity cannot be independent of unbounded hidden coefficient heights. If a promised realization has rational entry height at most `H`, its finitely many entries share a denominator with polynomial bit length in `n,m,H`. All query entries share a denominator of polynomial bit length in `n,m`. Joint nilpotency truncates every queried evaluation after at most `d+k` letters. Expanding finite products and sums gives response bit length polynomial in `n,m,H`; determinant bounds then give polynomial coordinate/output bit lengths. This supplies a conditional polynomial bit implementation in those parameters. With arbitrary field elements or unspecified response lengths, only construction bit cost and exact field-operation complexity are claimed.

More directly, the learner's rational processing time is polynomial in `n,m` and the total encoded response length, with oracle execution/latency charged separately. The learner need not be supplied a hidden-height parameter. A bounded hidden realization merely supplies a sufficient response-height bound. The acquired representation has arbitrary field weights; positivity, stochastic normalization, stable filtering, and a nonnegative realization are not guaranteed.

## 7. Prior art, consequences, and remaining obstacle

Finite-state residual/Hankel reconstruction is established prior art. The primary-author review by Balle and Mohri, *Learning Weighted Automata*, Section 4.1, describes exact arbitrary-field learning from word membership and counterexample equivalence queries, tracing it to Bergadano–Varricchio and Beimel et al. The original Beimel–Bergadano–Bshouty–Kushilevitz–Varricchio author PDF is recoverable in the shared source directory, but its text extraction is garbled; I did not claim full reading of that paper. The directly read review supports the interface comparison. Arvind, Mukhopadhyay and Srinivasan, *New results on Noncommutative and Commutative Polynomial Identity Testing*, arXiv:0801.0514v1 (3 January 2008), Section 3 and Theorem 3.5, explicitly obtains deterministic polynomial ABP reconstruction when the oracle permits evaluation at **any given gate**. I read that theorem and the stated polynomial matrix-evaluation cost assumption directly in the primary snapshot.

Forbes–Shpilka's black-box ABP hitting sets provide the preceding quasipolynomial generator. More decisively, its Theorem 1.5 already combines Klivans–Shpilka learning with membership and equivalence queries to obtain same-class NC ABP reconstruction; these queries can be implemented by randomized polynomial or deterministic quasipolynomial black-box PIT. Thus **output-only reconstruction as a capability is not itself new**. The polynomial deterministic bound and explicit residual feature compilation are the relevant changes supplied by family116. Corollary 4.7 gives matrices of dimension `depth+1` and a quasipolynomial hitting-list size. The arXiv record verifies v2 as 22 September 2013, but the saved PDF's title page has an internal date of 22 June 2021; both are preserved rather than silently conflated.

Nisan's 1991 coefficient-rank and path-program representation and Raz–Shpilka's 2005 white-box algorithms already make supplied-model identity testing polynomial. Hyperlogarithm independence (Deneufchatel et al., 2011/2017), Moura's 2003 multiplicity estimate, and the Bostan–Dumas 2010 formal Wronskian proof are older primitives, not discoveries attributed to the release. The source's new enabling combination is an explicit polynomial universal matrix feature.

The additional consequence reconstructed here removes internal-gate and equivalence-query access for bounded recognizable series under a strong output-only matrix oracle. It provides exact acquisition of a reusable minimal sequential representation, exact equivalence testing, and a deterministic residual basis for subsequent symbolic computation. It is a native algebraic research primitive. The cyclic length marker prevents cancellation across word lengths without unfolding the state space into exponentially many words.

Historical priority for this exact matrix-oracle corollary has **not** been established. It may be a routine consequence once polynomial hitting is available. This is not a proved resolution of general commutative PIT, general circuit reconstruction, teacher-free statistical HMM acquisition, finite precision model identification, or a local-intelligence bottleneck. In particular, a supplied transition representation has an equally informed polynomial reachable-space baseline already; novelty concerns acquiring a hidden representation through this specified oracle.

The next decisive allocation should adversarially audit the proper-series oracle assumption and the residual feature compilation, and finish the output-only reconstruction prior-art comparison. The strongest possible practical extension needs a stability theorem with charged observation precision, or a cheaper realizable matrix oracle; exact injectivity alone does not provide either.
