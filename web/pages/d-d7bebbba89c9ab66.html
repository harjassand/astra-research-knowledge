# Reconstructed comparators and obstructions

Worker: rounding_prior. Date: 2026-10-07 Australia/Brisbane. These are independently reconstructed mathematical reductions, not claims that a full implementation of the recent Komlós algorithm has been executed.

## Interface

Let P=conv(V) with V⊂{0,1}^n, rational A and y∈P. A strong optimization oracle returns an exact optimum and an optimizing Boolean vertex for every rational objective. Let k bound the support of each actual edge of P. For matroid base polytopes, k=2 and an independence oracle suffices via rank, integer submodular minimization, and exact rational optimization/separation. Define T=max_j Σ_i|a_ij|. A favorable response to cost c is a vertex b with c·(b−y)≤0. An exact marginal law has E b=y and a bound on every supported outcome. These are distinct from high-probability discrepancy and from a single unconstrained signing.

## Baseline A: deterministic exact LP rounding with radius kT

This is an elementary weighted absolute-sum extension of the floating-variable/iterative-relaxation proof. The nearest inspected source is BLRT, arXiv:2608.13983v1, Theorem 10 and Appendix A. No novelty is claimed for this comparator.

1. Start with P, all equalities a_i·x=a_i·y, and objective min c·x. Fixing a coordinate to zero or one restricts P to that coordinate face. Obtain an optimizing vertex x of the current intersection polytope, with lexicographic perturbation if needed.
2. Permanently fix all integral coordinates of x. Let U be its N fractional coordinates. Drop any remaining feature equality whose current absolute row mass Σ_{j∈U}|a_ij| is at most kT.
3. Repeat until x is Boolean. The current feasible point survives fixing and dropping, so the optimum cost never rises.

**Dimension fact.** At a point in the relative interior of an original Boolean face F, its nonconstant coordinates are exactly its fractional coordinates. Choose a basis of dim(F) actual edge directions of F. Their supports cover all nonconstant coordinates, and each has support at most k. Consequently N≤k dim(F). The edge directions span F because its edge graph is connected (or the tangent cone at a vertex spans its affine direction space).

**Progress proof.** If every retained row has mass greater than kT, their number s satisfies s kT < Σ_i Σ_{j∈U}|a_ij|≤NT, hence s<N/k≤dim(F). There is a nonzero tangent direction of F annihilating all retained rows. Since x lies in relint(F), a short segment in both directions is feasible for the intersection, contradicting that x is its vertex. Thus a row can be dropped whenever a nonintegral vertex remains. The T=0 case simply drops all zero rows. There are at most n coordinate-fixing events and m row-dropping events.

**Error proof.** Just before row i is dropped, its exact original target is still satisfied. Only the U coordinates can change afterward. Every such coordinate stays in [0,1], so the final error is at most Σ_{j∈U}|a_ij|≤kT. A never-dropped row has zero error. Therefore the final Boolean b satisfies

    c·b≤c·y,   ||A(b−y)||∞≤kT.

Signed A causes no difficulty; only the dropping test uses absolute values. With unit Euclidean columns and at most Δ nonzeros per column, T≤√Δ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Thus the matroid bound is 2T≤2√Δ, or 2ΔL when entries have magnitude at most L. For dense m-row inputs T≤√m. These comparisons use the same A and access model as the candidate.

**Bits and oracle costs.** All equalities use original A and y, coordinate bounds are integral, and dropping never introduces a coefficient. A Boolean polytope has polynomial facet encoding by determinant bounds, although its facet count may be exponential. GLS Theorem 6.4.9 implements the intersection LPs from strong optimization; Lemma 6.5.1/Remark 6.5.2 obtains vertices. The iteration count is at most n+m plus initialization, so persistent rational data and query costs are polynomial in the original encoding. For matroids, r(S)−x(S) is submodular; clearing rational denominators yields integer submodular minimization with polynomial log M. There is no claim of strong polynomial bit complexity independent of the input encoding.

## Standard favorable response to exact marginals

For fixed A,y,B, let G={b∈V:||A(b−y)||∞≤B}. If every rational c has a favorable b∈G, rational separation gives y∈conv(G). Otherwise a rational strict separator c has c·(b−y)>0 for every b∈G, contradicting the response property. Carathéodory gives an affinely independent support of at most dim(F(y))+1≤n+1 vertices. If y=a/Q, the weights have denominators dividing Q times a nonzero minor of the augmented Boolean vertex matrix. Hadamard therefore gives O(log Q+n log n) weight bits.

The polynomial algorithm retains oracle response vertices in an ellipsoid feasibility transcript, uses the uniform rational separation gap to force infeasibility of the collected separating system, then solves the exact convex-combination LP and removes affine dependencies. This needs favorable responses, not optimization over G. The inspected precursor is Cai–Daskalakis–Weinberg, arXiv:1305.4000v1, Lemma 3.2, Section 5, Corollary 5.1 and Appendix G.1. Its transcript/volume argument is substantive prior art. The mathematical implication and algorithm are not a new contribution of D.

Applying this standard extraction to Baseline A gives a deterministic polynomial-bit exact-marginal law with outcome-wise radius kT. Every support point necessarily belongs to F(y): an exposing equality tight at y has nonnegative slack on all P, and an exact mean forces every positive-weight slack to vanish.

Expected cost is preserved for every cost vector. It does not follow that every support atom separately improves every cost vector, or even the particular cost used in another response call.

## Baseline B: the cube already has a polynomial-bit exact-marginal radius below 99

Condition on Li's inspected signing theorem (arXiv:2609.30044v1, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 11.4) and its explicit bound C0=791913/8000<99 (Proposition 11.3). The source theorem is a deterministic rational polynomial-bit signing routine for every rational submatrix with unit Euclidean columns.

Given rational y∈[0,1]^n and arbitrary rational c, take q=2^K≥256n. Replace y_j by floor(qy_j)/q if c_j≥0 and by ceil(qy_j)/q if c_j<0. Call the resulting point z. Then z∈[0,1]^n, fixed Boolean coordinates stay fixed, c·z≤c·y, and ||A(z−y)||∞≤n/q≤1/256.

At levels t=K,K−1,...,1, let S be the coordinates with odd numerator on the 2^−t grid. Sign the columns A_S using Li's routine. Either σ or −σ makes c·σ_S≤0; choose that orientation. Add 2^−t σ on S. An odd numerator is strictly between the grid endpoints, so both orientations remain in the cube and make every numerator even. This coarsens the grid to 2^−(t−1) while never increasing cost. Each feature increment has infinity norm less than C0 2^−t.

The terminal b is Boolean and

    c·b≤c·y,
    ||A(b−y)||∞ < C0(1−1/q)+n/q ≤ C0+1/256 < 99.

There are K=O(log n) polynomial-bit signing calls. Apply the standard favorable-response extraction above to get an explicit exact rational mean y, support at most n+1, and hard radius below 99. This is a standard hereditary dyadic consequence, not a claim that Li itself states the marginal theorem. It dominates D's k=1 specialization in constant and makes exact marginals on cubes an unsuitable novelty claim.

Li's persistent coordinate grid lies in the cube because coordinatewise rounding preserves cube feasibility. It supplies no generic face/cost-slice reset primitive: a face such as x_1=1/3 is not preserved by coordinatewise dyadic rounding.

## Baseline C: simultaneous exchange subclasses

Ercan's Theorem 5.1/Corollary 5.2 already provide an exact-mean law with hard radius dR_m for every finite set admitting all-subset complementary exchanges and a common Gram bound. Strongly base-orderable bases have moves e_i−e_j and d≤2 for unit feature columns. Totally unimodular equality polytopes have conformal disjoint circuit moves with d≤ell, the maximum relevant circuit support. The claimed hard signing constant is below 6.84, so the strong-base-orderable existential radius is below 13.68. Table 4 explicitly does not assert a general polynomial-time kernel for the joint signing law and treats availability of exchange decompositions as input.

An equally informed deterministic comparator can instead use Li on the exchange columns when polynomial-time simultaneous exchange maps are provided. First obtain a rational decomposition y=Σ_{l=1}^r λ_l v_l with r≤n+1 (GLS Theorem 6.5.11), or receive it as input. Choose q=2^K≥256nr with q<512nr. Floor each qλ_l and assign all leftover list slots to a cheapest listed vertex under c. Call the mean of this q-point list y'. Writing the dropped weight of each vertex as ε_l∈[0,1/q), we have c·(y'−y)=Σ ε_l(c·v_min−c·v_l)≤0. Coordinate perturbation is at most r/q and ||A(y'−y)||∞≤nr/q≤1/256.

The list has polynomial size because q=O(nr), independent of the possibly huge original denominator. Thus it can be expanded fully. At a merge level with s list points, pair them. For each pair u,v, write u−v=Σ c_a using complementary all-subset moves. Any signing selects a feasible point w=(u+v)/2+(1/2)Σ σ_a c_a. Stack all move columns across the pairs in D. The new mean differs from the old by Dσ/s. Li applied to AD/d, for rational upper bound d on the move norms, gives ||ADσ||∞<d C0. Flip all signs if necessary to make c·Dσ≤0; feasibility survives because every subset is legal. Replace all pairs and halve the list.

Summing s=q,q/2,...,2 gives feature error less than d C0(1−1/q). For strongly base-orderable bases d=2, and the final error centered at the ORIGINAL y is below 2C0+1/256<198. For a Boolean TU equality system choose d=ell≥1, giving error below C0 ell+1/256<99 ell. Costs never rise. There are O(log n) Li calls on polynomial-size matrices. The exact state consists of Boolean lists, rational input-derived move matrices and Li's polynomial-bit calls. Standard favorable-response extraction then gives an explicit exact rational marginal law with at most n+1 atoms and these hard radii.

Partition matroids provide the exchange maps directly. For an arbitrary strongly base-orderable matroid given only by independence access, this deduction does not establish that the required all-subset bijection can be found efficiently: supply or verify that primitive. For an explicit TU matrix, a conformal circuit can be found as an extreme ray of its kernel intersected with the prescribed support/orthant, using a normalized rational LP. TU yields a primitive ±1 vector; its subtraction removes its whole support from u−v, so at most n such LP steps give the disjoint circuit decomposition. This is a polynomial implementation of the same decomposition used in Ercan's Corollary 5.2 proof.

An initial attempt to compress a huge original-denominator list was unnecessary: truncating weights toward a cheapest support point makes q polynomial. No compressed-list runtime assertion is required.

## Decisive obstructions

**Ordinary basis exchange is not simultaneous exchange.** In the graphic matroid of K4, take B={12,23,34}, D={13,14,24}. A complementary symmetric exchange of 12 has only partner 14; 34 also has only partner 14. Hall's condition fails for {12,34}. Thus no single all-subset exchange bijection exists, even though actual base-polytope edges change only two coordinates. This obstructs a black-box use of Ercan 5.1 for all matroids; it does not obstruct D's different tangent-space construction.

**Gaussian convex order does not imply a hard feature radius.** Take the direct sum of r rank-one matroids on two-element pairs, y=(1/2)^(2r), and row a=(1,−1,...,1,−1). Independent fair pair choices have exact means and the support includes feature errors ±r. Their Gaussian comparison can have a constant coefficient covariance factor because the pair directions are orthogonal. A different correlated two-atom law, using complementary choices with half the pairs of each sign, has error 0 for even r and ±1 for odd r. The first law disproves the claimed implication from the reference guarantee, not the existence of a better law.

**Coupling two matroids can create the real obstruction.** Perfect matchings of an even cycle of length 2r are the common bases of its two bipartition-side partition matroids. Each separate matroid has edge support 2. The joint polytope is a segment between the two alternating matchings, whose edge support is 2r. At y=(1/2)^(2r), exact marginals force equal probabilities on the two matchings. The alternating ±1 row has errors ±r. Hence no dimension-independent hard exact-mean bound transfers automatically to matroid intersection, multibase coupling or perfect matching constraints. This is an infinite exact family, not an inference from a finite test.
