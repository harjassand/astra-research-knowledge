T06 LEARNING/COMPRESSION — FROZEN INITIAL RECORD v1
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: candidate finite representation principle and finite-checked example; universal O(d) objective unresolved.

TARGET

For every binary concept class C on domain X with VC dimension d, seek a single fixed decoder rho and a compressor kappa such that every finite realizable labeled sample S is encoded as a labeled subsample T subseteq S of size at most C0 d, together with side information i from a set of size at most 2^{b(d)} (independent of |S| and |X|), and rho(T,i) agrees with every label in S. The requested linear example bound and bounded side information are not proved here.

AN EXACT FACE-CONE REFORMULATION

For finite X, let

  P(C) = {F=(A,y): A subseteq X, y in {0,1}^A, and y=c|A for some c in C}

be the set of all realizable partial labeled samples, including the empty sample. A decoder message consists of a root a=(T,z) in P(C), an auxiliary index i, and the fixed decoded hypothesis h_{a,i} in {0,1}^X, with h_{a,i}|T=z. Define its cone

  K(a,h) = { (A,y) in P(C): T subseteq A and y=h|A }.

Then a size-k, r-side-value sample compression scheme exists if and only if P(C) can be covered by cones K(a,h_{a,i}) with |T|<=k and at most r decoded hypotheses for each root a.

Proof. Given a compression scheme, associate to each legal message (a,i) its fixed decoder output h_{a,i}. Every input sample sent as (a,i) contains T and agrees with h_{a,i}, so it lies in K(a,h_{a,i}); hence the cones cover P(C). Conversely, order a finite cone cover once and for all. On input F, the compressor chooses the first cone containing F and returns its root (T,z) and index i. The root is a labeled subsample of F and the decoder output agrees with F by the cone definition. This proof makes the sample-subset condition explicit: it is not enough to identify every full concept from a fragment; every partial face must lie in a cone whose root is contained in that face.

This is an exact reformulation, not a bound on the cone-cover number in terms of VC dimension. Its value is that it separates the two requirements that are often conflated: a small code for each full concept, and coverage of every partial sample by the same fixed decoder.

FINITE TEST: WARMUTH'S CLASS C_W

Use the five coordinates and ten rows

  00011, 00110, 01100, 11000, 10001,
  01011, 10110, 01101, 11010, 10101.

Exhaustive projection checks give VCdim(C_W)=2: all ten coordinate pairs are shattered, and none of the ten triples is shattered. For every row c and every coordinate subset T of size at most 2, another row agrees with c on T; each row does have a unique 3-coordinate trace. Thus every concept has ordinary teaching size 3, while d=2. In particular, the proposed route “peel a concept with a unique d-point labeled fragment” has no first step on this class. This is a concrete finite counterexample to the unique-fragment lemma asserted in the withdrawn 2026 preprint cited below.

The cone formulation also handles the partial-sample tie problem on this same class. `finite_checks.py` supplies a fixed decoder table of 38 labeled roots, each of size at most 2 and with no side bits. A standard-library verifier exhausts all 176 realizable partial labeled samples and checks that each lies in at least one decoder cone. For an input sample, choose the first covering root; the fixed top hypothesis then agrees on the entire sample. Some decoder outputs are improper (outside C_W), which is allowed by this task's reconstruction contract. This small certificate shows that ordinary teaching dimension is too strong a proxy for compression: C_W has teaching size 3 but admits this verified size-2 partial-sample code.

The finite table is not a scalable construction. The count 38 is a count of decoder roots for this fixed five-point class; it gives no universal control on cone-cover roots for growing n or d. No claim about general linear compression follows.

WHY ONE-INCLUSION DEGENERACY DOES NOT YET GIVE A DECODER

For each finite coordinate set A, Sauer's bound implies that the one-inclusion graph on C|A has average degree at most 2d, and therefore it has a vertex of degree at most 2d. More refined degeneracy/orientation arguments support one-inclusion prediction bounds. But an orientation on C|A depends on A, while a compression decoder sees only its retained root T and bounded side index. A proof must construct fixed hypotheses h_{T,z,i} so that their cones cover every face of P(C). Neither a low-degree vertex nor an orientation by itself supplies these h values or proves coverage when an unretained sample coordinate changes the version space. The cone criterion is the remaining reconstruction obligation.

LITERATURE STATUS CHECKED 2026-10-09

1. Shay Moran and Amir Yehudayoff, “Sample compression schemes for VC classes,” arXiv:1503.06960v2 (2015), Theorem 1.3 and concluding paragraph. It proves a size 2^{O(d)} labeled compression scheme for every binary VC-d class; the paper states at lines 164–168 that whether all classes have O(d) compression remains open. Its Theorem 1.4 uses the dual VC dimension d*: k=O(dd*) base examples and a k log k total size; Assouad's d*<2^{d+1} yields the exponential universal bound. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.06960, accessed theorem/construction and conclusion ranges 69–89, 121–163, 164–168. Current arXiv page notes the earlier multi-label error does not affect the binary construction.

2. Xi Chen, Yu Cheng, and Bo Tang, “On the Recursive Teaching Dimension of VC Classes,” NeurIPS 2016, pages 1, 4–6. They report Warmuth's class C_W has VC dimension 2 and minimum teaching-set size/RTD 3 (page 4, lines 364–390); the ten rows are printed there. This record independently recomputes the projections and teaching sizes. Source: https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/6412-on-the-recursive-teaching-dimension-of-vc-classes.pdf, accessed PDF pages 4–6, lines 364–400 and 419–489.

3. Zachary Chase, “Optimally compressing VC classes,” arXiv:2201.04131 (2022), is withdrawn. The current arXiv record says the proof of Proposition 3.2 and the main theorem is invalid. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04131, accessed lines 8–20.

4. Jiahua Liu and Benchong Li, “The No-Clash Teaching Dimension is Bounded by VC Dimension,” arXiv:2603.23561v4 (2026), is withdrawn; the current record says the proof of Lemma 2 is wrong. Its v3 body defines teaching/VC notions, then presents a “compression” that takes a full concept as input rather than an arbitrary sample (sections 2–3, especially lines 98–100 and 136–167); it therefore would not establish sample compression even if its teaching claim held. Warmuth's class independently refutes its Lemma 2's stated unique-d-fragment step: d=2, but no row has a unique trace on any set of at most two coordinates. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23561 and https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23561v3, accessed abstract/current withdrawal notice and v3 lines 98–100, 136–167.

5. Current specialist results remain structured subclasses. For example, Chepoi, Knauer, and Philibert, “Labeled sample compression schemes for complexes of oriented matroids,” JCSS 144 (2024), proves proper labeled size-d compression for topes of a COM of VC dimension d; the paper describes this as a step toward the general conjecture. This does not supply an embedding for arbitrary VC classes. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000024000382, accessed abstract.

UNRESOLVED

The universal cone-cover bound required here remains missing: prove that every VC-d class has a cone cover whose roots have size O(d) and whose per-root multiplicity is 2^{O(d)} (or another explicitly bounded function of d), or produce an equally foundational finite-certificate theorem. Current work gives only the exact cone-cover characterization and one verified finite instance. The one-inclusion route still lacks a decoder construction coherent across all sample subsets. No breakthrough, novelty, or external validation is claimed.
