## P1. Source, exact promise, and nearest prior

OpenAI, *The free uniform spanning forest is a factor of IID*, manuscript date 25 September 2026, pinned 6 October release commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`. All six scientific sections were read in full. The principal assertion is one isomorphism-equivariant Borel factor rule on every infinite connected locally finite simple undirected graph. A second assertion supplies Borel IID factors for invariant countable-group strongly-Rayleigh laws, including invariant determinantal processes. Neither assertion supplies a finitary coding radius, finite input alphabet, or algorithmic running time. The conull event is graphwise, not a common event for uncountably many graphs.

For a finite strongly-Rayleigh binary law and any positive external field h, let p(h) denote its mean vector. The response identity is Dp=Cov_h(X). Off-diagonal entries are nonpositive. Symmetric homogenization adds coordinates so the total occupied count is fixed; the enlarged covariance has zero row sums. It follows that the original row absolute sums are at most 2 Var(X_i)≤1/2. Equivalently, the stochastic covering property gives nonnegative original row sums and the same conclusion. Thus

\[
\|Dp(h)\|_{\infty\to\infty}\le\tfrac12,
\qquad 0\preceq Dp(h)\preceq\tfrac12 I.
\tag{P1}
\]

The second bound follows from symmetry and the row bound; no homogeneous or full-support assumption on the original law is needed. It is sharp for X uniform on {(1,0),(0,1)}. Finite marginals and all finite positive external-field tilts preserve the strongly-Rayleigh property.

This response bound is **older prior**, not a new lemma of the release. Alishahi–Barzegar–Zamani, arXiv `2203.03935v2` (22 May 2022), published Annals of Probability 51(4), 1548–1558 (2023), Lemma 2.5 and equation (1), printed page 4, contain it. I checked that page visually and reconstructed its proof from the stochastic covering property. The lemma itself credits Ghosh–Liggett–Pemantle, Lemma 6.5 (2017). The positive-field closure/homogenization inputs trace to Borcea–Brändén–Liggett (2009); their complete proof is not rereproved here.

The Gaussian observation Y_t=tX+B_t and its posterior drift/innovation identity are also prior. El Alaoui–Montanari, arXiv `2109.00709v2` (9 September 2021), Theorem 2, provide the finite-dimensional observation equation. Nam–Sly–Zhang, arXiv `2012.09484v2` (22 January 2022), already use an infinite-dimensional Brownian construction for free Ising factors on trees; only its abstract was read here, so no stronger priority inference is made. The release's scope and countable equivariant construction must be compared to those narrower prior mechanisms, not advertised as introducing posterior diffusion.

## P2. What the release actually acquires mathematically

Finite weighted-tree determinants give p_e=c_e a_e^T L^(-1)a_e. Differentiation gives the covariance response, with nonpositive off-diagonals and zero row sums from the fixed tree size. Free finite-volume limits preserve the response bound. The finite cycle subspaces increase, so their orthogonal projections converge strongly to the closure of finitely supported cycles; principal minors determine the countable FUSF law. Finite tilts are ratios of cylinder expectations, whose denominators are positive.

The source uses the limsup of finite-observation posterior means to define a bounded Borel drift b_i(t,y) on all fields, including exceptional inputs. Along the actual observation this is E[X_i|observation history] by martingale convergence. Brownian bridge independence makes conditioning on the finite path equivalent to conditioning on its endpoint. Fubini supplies the time-almost-everywhere identity required for drift integration; it need not claim one null set for every real time.

The equation Z=w+∫b(s,Z_s)ds is solved by Picard iteration. Successive differences are bounded uniformly in all coordinates on each finite time interval by L^k t^(k+1)/(k+1)!, even when w is unbounded across coordinates. This provides a causal measurable inverse for every continuous input, and uniqueness in the class of paths with bounded displacement from w. Innovations of the reference observation are independent Brownian motions, so applying the inverse to fresh independent Brownian input reproduces the observation law. Integer-time slopes recover X simultaneously at all countably many coordinates.

Vertex IID labels are split into private coordinates and ordering keys. Distinct keys allocate disjoint random coordinates to edges; finite neighbor-key sorting supplies edgewise independent Brownian paths. The subsequent limits, time integrals, and readout are Borel and equivariant. This closes the existence theorem without an acquired algorithm for the drift's infinite-volume limits.

For a finite target law on n bits, the same primitive admits an exact finite-time statistical reduction. With bounded predictable approximate drift and true-path squared-error budget

\[
\mathcal E=E_{\rm true}\int_0^T\|b(s,Y_s)-\widehat b(s,Y_s)\|_2^2 ds,
\]

Girsanov gives KL(P_true||P_approx)=\mathcal E/2. Pinsker and the endpoint decision 1{Y_i(T)>T/2} give

\[
\operatorname{TV}(\text{output},\mu)
\le n e^{-T/8}+\tfrac12\sqrt{\mathcal E}.
\tag{P2}
\]

Bounded drifts justify the change of measure. The expectation is under the true target observation law; empirical loss on unrelated training histories is insufficient without a distribution-transfer or coverage bound. Finite-bit Brownian increments and drift-evaluation costs remain charged. The independently authored sharper Euclidean Euler analysis is in `notes/frontier_probability_bridge/cycle19/FINITE_RESPONSE_SAMPLER.md`; this note does not duplicate its algorithmic novelty claim. Exact finite-matrix DPP/UST algorithms are competent comparators when their kernels or graphs are acquired, so P2 by itself is not an advantage over them.

## P3. A uniform local-radius lower bound

Consider a graph algorithm whose marked-edge output depends only on the radius-R graph neighborhood and the IID vertex labels there. The rule is the same on every input graph and respects marked labeled isomorphisms. It may use independent internal randomness. Then, even among connected infinite simple graphs of maximum degree 4, no such rule approximates all FUSF edge marginals with error strictly below 1/8.

Let T3 be the infinite 3-regular tree. Its free spanning forest contains every edge, because every finite connected subtree has a unique spanning tree. Hence p_e(T3)=1.

Choose a finite connected 3-regular simple graph H of girth greater than L=8R+16. A constructive existence proof is supplied in P4. The ball-count lower bound gives

\[
N=|V(H)|\ge 3\,2^{4R+7}-2.
\]

Choose two distinct ports u,v in H. Make a doubly infinite chain of copies H_j, joining v_j to u_(j+1) by one bridge. The resulting infinite graph G has maximum degree 4. Its FUSF contains all bridges and has an independent UST in each H_j: every finite exhaustion by complete consecutive copies has exactly that spanning-tree decomposition, so its free limit does too.

In H, the UST inclusion probabilities satisfy

\[
\sum_{e\in E(H)}p_e=N-1,\qquad |E(H)|=3N/2.
\tag{P3}
\]

At most 6[1+3(2^(R+1)−1)]≤36·2^R edges have an endpoint within distance R+1 of a port. This is at most N/6 by the displayed lower bound. If every other edge had probability at least 3/4, P3 would imply N−1≥(3/4)(3N/2−N/6)=N, a contradiction. Therefore some edge e far from both ports has p_e<3/4.

Its radius-R marked neighborhood is exactly the corresponding marked tree neighborhood; the girth bound excludes collisions or extra edges. The IID label laws and the algorithm's output law coincide on these two inputs. If the common output probability is q, then

\[
\max\{|q-1|,|q-p_e|\}\ge(1-p_e)/2>1/8.
\tag{P4}
\]

This is a distributional obstruction with constant error, not a finite simulation or a count of source-paper inaccuracies. The posterior response is uniformly Lipschitz on both graphs, so that response bound alone cannot supply a uniform spatial truncation modulus.

## P4. Explicit finite high-girth graphs without an existence oracle

Take all reduced words of length at most L over three letters a,b,c, where adjacent letters differ. There are M_L=3·2^L−2 words including the empty word. For each letter s, the word-tree edges of color s form a partial matching. Complete it to a perfect matching on this finite vertex set by pairing the remaining vertices in any fixed order. The unmatched count is even because M_L is even and every matched edge removes two vertices.

Let τ_a,τ_b,τ_c be the resulting fixed-point-free involutive permutations, and let K be the finite subgroup of the symmetric group on these M_L vertices generated by them. Every nonempty reduced word of length at most L sends the empty word to its corresponding nonempty word: all its successive prefixes stay inside the original word-tree ball. Consequently it is not the identity permutation. The three generators are distinct nonidentity involutions, and the connected Cayley graph Cay(K,{τ_a,τ_b,τ_c}) is a simple 3-regular graph with no cycle of length at most L. This is H.

The graph is explicitly acquirable by finite group enumeration, with the severe upper bound |K|≤M_L!. No claim of polynomial acquisition or small constructed graph is made. This elementary residual-finiteness construction is used as a proof of the counterexample's existence, not as a proposed sampler. The owned transcription `check_high_girth_words.py` checks only the finite word action/involutions for small L; the universal Cayley-graph claim is proved above.

## P5. No universal almost-sure local stopping rule

The same argument rules out one exact FUSF sampler that, on every graph in this class, almost surely determines each edge using a finite graph neighborhood and its local labels. Formally its event 'halt with output 1 by radius R' must be measurable from that marked labeled neighborhood, using the same stopping/output rule on all graphs.

On T3 an exact sampler outputs 1 almost surely and halts almost surely. Thus for some R, its probability of halting with output 1 by radius R exceeds 3/4. Construct G and e using that R. The same finite-neighborhood event has the same probability there, forcing output-1 probability greater than 3/4, contrary to p_e<3/4.

This statement concerns **graph access as part of the input**. It does not exclude a finitary factor on a fixed graph when its full deterministic geometry or kernel is supplied externally. It does not contradict amenable transitive results, nor does it exclude graph-specific local factors such as the trivial factor on T3. The release explicitly permits a Borel dependence on the entire graph, and does not claim this stopping interface.

## P6. Consequence, unresolved acquisition, and next test

The Brownian factor is useful as an existence and reduction primitive: a uniformly Lipschitz family of acquired posterior means can be turned into a consistent sample, with finite-dimensional error paid in the true-path norm P2. It removes a countable causal-inversion issue. It does **not** supply the posterior means, a finite spatial boundary modulus, or training coverage. P4 proves that the missing spatial modulus cannot be graph-uniform under the claimed all-graph interface; P5 shows why Borel measurability alone cannot become a universally terminating local algorithm.

Nearest mechanisms are finite stochastic localization/innovations, the published strongly-Rayleigh covariance row bound, classical determinant/UST sampling, and existing restricted-class finitary forest constructions. Priority of P3–P5 as formulated is UNKNOWN; the proof is elementary once one distinguishes fixed geometry from graph input. A consequential next falsifiable step is to identify a graph class with an acquired quantitative boundary-response modulus and compare the charged local posterior evaluations against Wilson/determinantal sampling on the same information. No such class-specific modulus is acquired in this note.
