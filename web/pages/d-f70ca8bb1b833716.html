# Constructive 1D closure of the integrated proper score: complete alternate learner, unfavorable comparison

Status2026-10-06: mathematical algorithm and cost closure, conditional only on the independently proved 1D local-observable theorem cited below. No end-to-end learner was implemented. Dynamic programming and local Pauli estimation are established tools. This is an alternate construction, not a first all-temperature, non-SOS, near-linear-time learner. The competent existing learner can also reuse a fixed Pauli dataset; recommend checkpointing rather than substantial implementation.

## Supplied interface

An open n-qubit chain, a known list of m>=1 DISTINCT nonidentity canonical Hermitian Pauli strings P_j, each of support size<=k and diameter<=R, at most r listed terms/site. Target copies are supplied from

sigma=exp(-beta sum_j theta*_j P_j)/Z, |theta*_j|<=J.

The same known positive beta and the same coefficient cube are used for candidates. Identity coefficients are excluded. Beta,J and the desired error/confidence can be supplied rationally or with certified intervals; below exact rational inputs are assumed to make the bit interface explicit. Target coefficients themselves need not be rational. Transcendental proof constants are enclosed by rational upper/lower bounds; no exact transcendental ceiling decision is required. Target copies and trusted single-qubit Pauli product measurements are the physical interface. Copy preparation costs are not supplied as zero: the algorithm uses N copies and adds N*Cprep to physical resource accounting.

Write c=2 beta² alpha for X/Z, where

s0=min(n,1+r(k-1)), b0=beta J r s0,
alpha=exp(-2b0)/(1+b0 2^(s0/2) exp(b0)).

The independent population theorem b_math/r02 proves the FULL Uniform(0,1) source-normalized regret

Jbar(theta;theta*) >= c sum_j |supp P_j| (theta_j-theta*_j)² >= c ||theta-theta*||².

For fixed beta,J,k,r, c is positive and independent of n. This theorem is not for clipped mixtures. Physical XYZ doubles c, but X/Z suffices below.

The exact copy-loss per direction A is

O_A(theta)=beta(H-AHA)-I+Dlog_(exp(-beta H))(A exp(-beta H) A).

Its expectation differs from Jbar by a target-only constant. No target eigenbasis, target modular inverse, candidate Gibbs sample or candidate partition function is supplied.

Use only active X_i/Z_i directions which anticommute with at least one listed term. All other loss observables vanish exactly. Their count a is at most2k m; put G=2k. Normalize the loss by m, so its regret controls coefficient RMS sqrt(||theta-theta*||²/m). This avoids silently assuming m is comparable to n. Normalizing by n instead gives the site-normalized RMS directly.

## Uniform local factor oracle and constants

Let Jint=rJ. The c_priorart theorem, independently audited by r11, gives for every cube candidate and each active site direction an exact local analytic factor Oloc_A(theta_window), with

||Oloc_A-O_A||op<=t, support interval length w=O_(beta,Jint,R)(1+log(1/t)),
||Oloc_A||op<=M,
||Oloc_A(theta)-Oloc_A(theta')||op<=L ||theta_window-theta'_window||infinity.

The window contains all candidate terms wholly inside that support interval; it includes the exact terms intersecting A. Thus its coefficient dependence is genuinely local, uniformly over the entire cube. It is not a separate truncated Gibbs candidate to which the population theorem is incorrectly applied.

Explicit reproducible choices from notes/c_priorart/cycle4/INTEGRATED_GIBBS_AUDIT.md, q_A=1:

C=exp[beta Jint+2 beta Jint R exp(2 beta Jint R)],
M_mu=exp[beta Jint+2 beta Jint R exp((2 beta Jint+mu)R)],
eta=t/(64C), ell=max(0,ceil(log(2M_1/eta))), u=1+2ell,
v=4 Jint(1+R exp(R)), T=log(256 C²/t)/(2 pi),
p=max(0,ceil(beta v T+log(64 C²u/t))),
w=1+2 max(R,ell+p), M=2 beta Jint+2+4 C².

The extra constant and the4 C² bound also absorb certified operator compilation error<=t<=1, so the actual compiled observable used in the moment contraction has norm<=M.

Boundary intervals are intersected with the chain. Replace exact constants by certified upper rational bounds when choosing integer sizes. The local factor can be chosen from exact B_ell=exp(beta H_ell/2) A exp(-beta H_ell/2) and the positive logistic filter k(ad(beta H_L)) of B_ell B_ell^dagger. Araki and real-time bounds prove the stated truncation, without full-chain matrices.

A conservative Lipschitz constant is

L=2 beta m_A +2C beta exp(beta Jint u) m_u +(2 beta log(2)/pi) C² m_w,

where m_A counts listed terms intersecting A, and m_u/m_w count terms wholly in the indicated intervals. Uniformly m_A<=r, m_u<=ru, m_w<=rw. This follows by Duhamel differentiation of the two local exponentials, differentiating unitary conjugation in the logistic average, and E|t|=log(2)/pi. Use the largest L over directions or the displayed r-size bounds. For fixed admitted beta,J,r,R this L is polynomial in1/t and independent of n. The constants have very poor temperature dependence: C is double exponential in beta Jint R. Fixed beta is essential in the stated accuracy asymptotics.

## One nonadaptive target dataset serves every candidate

Each local factor is Hermitian on at most w qubits, with D=2^w. Its Pauli coefficient absolute sum is at most D M by orthogonality and Cauchy-Schwarz. Required distinct local Pauli moments are at most a4^w.

On each fresh target copy independently choose X/Y/Z measurement axes at every site, and measure all sites in parallel. For a local string P of weight h<=w, record

Y_P=3^h indicator(axes match P on its nonidentity sites) times product(outcomes on those sites).

Its expectation is tr(sigma P), and |Y_P|<=3^w. Consequently with probability at least1-delta, ALL required moments are simultaneously estimated to error nu using

N >= ceil[2*9^w*nu^(-2)*log(2 a4^w/delta)]

copies. Take nu=t/(D M). Every later candidate-factor expectation then has error<=t, uniformly over the continuous cube, without a statistical union bound over candidate assignments. The estimated moments are rational integers divided by N. One dataset can be reused throughout the optimization.

Charge N target copies, nN single-site measurements, O(nN) raw outcome/basis bits, and either their storage or streaming sufficient statistics. A conservative moment-update implementation takes O(a4^w N w) integer operations and stores O(a4^w log(N3^w)) bits. No adaptive quantum measurements, global candidate evolution or target tomography are required. This is standard local Pauli/shadow moment estimation, not a new measurement primitive.

## Coefficient grid and exact dynamic program

Compute a certified positive rational LOWER bound c0<=c from the explicit population constant. Use certified rational UPPER bounds for L and M (and for constants used to select support sizes). Let desired coefficient RMS error be0<epsilon<=1 and gamma=c0 epsilon². Set

t=min(1,gamma/(32G)), h=min(2J,gamma/(16G L)).

Take an endpoint-inclusive uniform rational grid in[-J,J] of spacing<=h, size Q=ceil(2J/h)+1. Round each true coefficient to its nearest grid value, obtaining theta0 with max coefficient error<=h/2. The LOCAL population loss increases by at most G L h/2<=gamma/32. Comparing the exact global loss adds at most2G t<=gamma/16, because truncation is uniform. Thus its exact population excess is at most3gamma/32. No global Lipschitz bound is inferred from a local oracle without this truncation term.

Compile every local factor for each assignment of its at-most rw window coefficients. Certified operator compilation error<=t, statistical expectation error<=t, rational scalar table-rounding error<=t, and original global-to-local operator error<=t give uniform normalized objective error at most4G t<=gamma/8. Tables are stored as dyadic rationals with a common denominator. The unknown target-only score constant is never evaluated and cancels in all comparisons.

Associate each coefficient variable with the leftmost site of its Pauli support; each site owns at most r variables. Every factor involves a consecutive owner-site window of length at most w. Attach its cost to that window's last site. Sweep left to right, retaining assignments to the previous w-1 owner groups, add factors when their last variable has been introduced, and minimize over assignments leaving the retained state. This is exact finite-range chain dynamic programming; it is valid for nonconvex factors. Store predecessor pointers and recover the minimizing rational coefficient vector thetahat. No gradient, local-minimum or convexity claim is used.

If f is the exact normalized population loss and fhat its finite table loss, then

f(thetahat)-f(theta*) <= 2 sup_theta |fhat-f| +f(theta0)-f(theta*)
<=gamma/4+3gamma/32 <gamma.

Population coercivity yields ||thetahat-theta*||²/m<=(c0/c) epsilon²<=epsilon² with probability at least1-delta. This is the final error guarantee. The global candidate reconstructed from the learned coefficient vector remains in the original supplied Gibbs family.

## Computation and finite bits

The number of factor entries is at most a Q^(rw). An elementary DP implementation has at most n Q^(r(w+1)) transitions, times a polynomial in w,r. Local matrix compilation uses dimension D, not2^n.

A completely finite-bit local compiler exists without a spectral-gap oracle: compute the two finite-window exponentials with certified matrix Taylor series, then approximate the logistic unitary integral by a cutoff and rectangle quadrature. For ||beta H_L||<=beta Jint w, exponential Taylor length and working precision are polynomial in beta Jint w+log(1/t). The filter tail is <=2 exp(-2 pi T'); choose T'=O(log(C²/t)). Its integrand derivative norm is <=C²(pi²+pi ||beta H_L||). Rectangle mesh O[t/(T'C²(1+||beta H_L||))] therefore suffices; all exponentials, logistic scalar functions and pi can be enclosed to additional error<=t. This costs polynomial(D,1/t,w, admitted constants, input precision). It may be much slower than spectral/divided-difference computation, but establishes constructive bit closure without assuming eigenvalue separation.

Factor table values have magnitude<=M plus the allocated errors. Dyadic table precision O(log(1/t)+log M) suffices; DP additions/comparisons need O(log a+log M+log(1/t)) bits and are EXACT. Coefficient-grid outputs need O(input coefficient-bound bits+log Q) bits each. Classical coefficient precision and matrix rounding have been included in the table error, not treated as free exact-real arithmetic.

For fixed beta,J,k,r,R, w=O(log(1/epsilon)), log Q=O(log(1/epsilon)), D and local evaluation cost are polynomial in1/epsilon. Thus total classical work is

n exp[O_(beta,J,k,r,R)(log²(1/epsilon))] times polynomial(input precision,log(n/delta)),

with quasi-polynomial accuracy dependence. Samples are conservatively

N=O[36^w M² t^(-2) log(n4^w/delta)],

polynomial in1/epsilon times log(n/delta), but with unfavorable beta-dependent exponents/constants. Physical processing adds N*Cprep+nN measurements. The method has no uncharged candidate Gibbs preparation or per-candidate fresh-copy oracle. It also does not guarantee efficiency when beta grows with n or when the locality/degree promises are acquired rather than supplied.
