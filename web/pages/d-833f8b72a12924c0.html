## Actual primary-source functional

    q²=2stu/(s²+t²+u²), s+t+u=0,
    cos(theta)=sqrt((s-3q²)/(s+q²)), 0<=q<=sqrt(s/3).

The finite-cut counterpart of the n=0 normalized partial-wave coefficient in Eq(A.29) is exactly

    C_l(s;E,Q)=integral_E^Q psi(q) K0(s,q)
                     P_l(sqrt((s-3q²)/(s+q²))) dq,
    K0(s,q)=2+3q²/s.

Eq(A.27) has the arc weight ds/(2pi s³) after taking the real-energy discontinuity. §7 admits a fixed Q<=M/sqrt(3), s>=M². Eq(5.13) requires nonnegative coefficients for every l and s, **after the scaling-limit q integral has been extended to zero**. The physical identical-scalar amplitude only needs the even-l sector. Coefficients have no eikonal sine phase: Eq(5.8) subsequently sums them against (2l+1)|a_l(s)|². This distinction is load-bearing.

Eq(5.12) prints a plus sign in its fixed-t angle in PDF and HTML despite q²=-t; I did not silently use that expression. The gravity STU angle above is unambiguous and is the object treated below.

## Exact transfer variable and geometry weight

Let r=sqrt(-t) on the forward branch, v=r²/s in[0,1/2], D=1-v+v². Exact massless kinematics gives

    q²/s=v(1-v)/D, cos(theta)=1-2v,
    q²/r²=(1-v)/D,
    dlog(q)/dlog(r)=(1-2v)/[D(1-v)].

For E<Q let r_E=r(s,E), r_Q=r(s,Q). The map is increasing; its derivative vanishes only at the admitted upper endpoint v=1/2. Set psi(q)=q f(q), where f is a supplied bounded-variation function on[0,Q]. The physical leading amplitude has transfer pole r^-2 and phase depending on r. Changing variables **exactly** gives

    integral_E^Q psi(q)K0(s,q) P_l(cos(theta))
                         sin[2g log(r(q)/Lambda)+chi] dq/r(q)²
      =integral_(r_E)^(r_Q) W_s(r) P_l(1-2r²/s)
                         sin[2g log(r/Lambda)+chi] dr/r,

where g=Gs, Lambda>0 is a separately specified phase scale, chi any real phase, and

    W_s(r)=H(v)K0(s,q(r))f(q(r)),
    H(v)=(1-2v)/D².

H(0)=1,H(1/2)=0 and H'=-6v(1-v)/D³<=0. Thus supH<=1, TV(H)<=1 uniformly. Also2<=K0<=3 and TV(K0)<=1. If F=sup|f| and V_f=TV(f),

    sup|W_s|<=3F,
    TV(W_s)<=4F+3V_f,
    sup|W_s|+TV(W_s)<=7(F+V_f).                 (BV)

No smoothness of f is needed for this part. The geometric factor is bounded at the endpoint; no false division by the vanishing dq/dr occurs. Further, q/r>=sqrt(2/3), so

    r_Q<=sqrt(3/2)Q, r_Q/sqrt(s)<=1/sqrt(2).

The constants are uniform in s,G, every l, and any lower transfer cut E(s,G). Separating the phase scale Lambda from the transfer cutoff avoids claiming that the shock-wave subtraction scale is an acquired detector energy.

## Uniform Legendre-to-Bessel comparison: no fixed-l limit

r12_geometry derived the following exact weighted bound. I independently reconstructed it from primary NIST DLMF14.12.1 and checked all constants and the curvature crossover:

    integral_0^delta |P_l(1-2u²)-J0((2l+1)u)| du/u
         <=6delta, l>=0,0<delta<=1/sqrt(2).      (L1)

Here is a complete short reconstruction. Let theta=2arcsin(u),nu=l+1/2,x=(2l+1)u,y=nu theta. The Mehler–Dirichlet representation and the Bessel angular integral have the same oscillatory cosine. Their positive endpoint-weight ratio is

    R=sqrt([(theta+t)/2]/sin[(theta+t)/2]
             *[(theta-t)/2]/sin[(theta-t)/2]).

Using sin a>=a(1-a²/6),theta<=pi/2, and arcsin u<=sqrt(2)u gives

    1<=R, R-1<=theta²/(6-theta²)<=theta²/3<=8u²/3.

The normalized Bessel weight integrates to one, so its weighted error integrates to4delta²/3. No bound depending on l has been introduced.

Next y-x<=(2l+1)u³/3, since (1-v²)^-1/2-1<=v² on v<=1/sqrt(2). The differentiated angular formula gives |J0'|<=1 and |J0'(1)|<=1/2. For H=sqrt(x)J0(x), H''+[1+1/(4x²)]H=0 and energy H²+H'² has logarithmic derivative bounded by1/(4x²); its initial value is<=2. Consequently, for x>=1, |J0(x)|<=2/sqrt(x), |J0'(x)|<=3/sqrt(x).

Write lambda=2l+1. For x<=1, the chord error is<=lambda u³/3 and its weighted integral is<=delta²/9. For x>=1 the two bounds are

    |J0(y)-J0(x)|<=min(sqrt(lambda)u^(5/2),4/sqrt(lambda u)).

They cross at a=(4/lambda)^(1/3). Integrating the first below a and the second above a gives<=24delta/5; if delta<a the first alone gives<=4delta/5. Thus the total is

    (13/9)delta²+(24/5)delta<6delta.

This proof controls arbitrary l(s,G), including exponentially growing l. A pointwise replacement theta by2r/sqrt(s) would have been invalid when lambda(r/sqrt(s))³ is large; the weighted integral handles that region by Bessel amplitude decay.

## Leading amplitude: entire UV angular export closes

Define the normalized leading-Legendre model with its actual STU geometry,

    L_l(s)=8pi Gs² integral_(r_E)^(r_Q) W_s(r)P_l(1-2r²/s)
                             sin[2glog(r/Lambda)+chi] dr/r,

and B_l by replacing P_l with J0(b_l r), b_l=(2l+1)/sqrt(s). Equation(L1) gives

    |L_l-B_l|<=48pi G s^(3/2)r_Q sup|W_s|.

With fixed Q<=M/sqrt(3), (BV) yields the all-energy error ledger

    integral_(M²)^infinity |L_l-B_l| ds/s³
        <=288pi sqrt(3/2) G F Q/M.             (EXPORT)

This is uniform in arbitrary measurable l(s,G), E(s,G), phase chi(s,G) and Lambda(s,G). It remains valid with s,G-dependent f if F is uniformly supplied. A cutoff proportional to sqrt(s) cannot be substituted: that particular error envelope ceases to be integrable. The source's fixed IR transfer scale Q is therefore preserved.

Cycle4's every-truncated-interval Mellin bound, followed by Abel integration and(BV), also gives for g>=1

    |B_l(s)|<=5600pi s(F+V_f).

Thus the exact leading-Legendre STU model has a UV-integrable high-energy envelope: the Bessel part is O(s), and its angular correction is O(GQ s^(3/2)). If the supplied f additionally has |f(q)-f(0)|<=C_f q^nu,nu>0, the low-g moving-front error is O(sg[F+C_f Q^nu/nu]), uniformly in l and E. To see it, W_s(0)=2f(0), |H K0-2|<=9r²/s and |f(q(r))-f(0)|<=C_f r^nu; integrate the difference with dr/r. Combining low g<=1, high g>=1 and(EXPORT) retains a leading-sector finite-resolution budget O(G log(1/G))+O(GQ/M), with explicit constants determined by F,V_f,C_f,nu and the phase assumption. ## Actual coefficients and finite-cut errors

For the actual coefficient C_l, the same exact r variable gives

    C_l=integral_(r_E)^(r_Q) W_s(r) r²
                                  P_l(1-2r²/s) dr/r.

The extra r² arises because there is no amplitude pole in this coefficient. Therefore

    |C_l-C_l,Bessel|<=6r_Q³ sup|W_s|/sqrt(s),
    |C_l(s;0,Q)-C_l(s;E,Q)|<=3F E²/2.           (COEFF)

These are uniform for every l,s at the actual STU angle. They may be useful in a finite admitted spectral interface. However, Eq(5.8) multiplies them by unknown spectral weights rho_l=(2l+1)|a_l|². Controlling the full arc from(COEFF) requires, for example, a supplied finite integral of [error(s)/s³]sum_l rho_l(s). Per-partial-wave contractivity does not bound this sum, and cannot silently replace the needed total spectral budget.

## Exact finite-cutoff positivity cone is empty

**Theorem.** At any fixed s and0<E<Q<=sqrt(s/3), every nonzero real integrable smearing psi has at least one negative coefficient C_l(s;E,Q). The statement also holds when only even l are admitted.

Push the finite signed measure psi(q)K0(s,q)dq through z(q)=sqrt((s-3q²)/(s+q²)). Call it mu on[0,z_E], where z_E<1. Its Legendre moments are exactly C_l. The exact spherical Poisson kernel, obtained by differentiating the Legendre generating function, is

    K_a(z)=sum_(l>=0)(2l+1)a^lP_l(z)
           =(1-a²)/(1-2az+a²)^(3/2),0<a<1.

The sum can be integrated termwise against mu, because |P_l|<=1 and the geometric weighted series is summable. Since mu is finite and its support is bounded away from z=1, K_a tends uniformly to zero on the support as a->1. Hence

    sum_l(2l+1)a^l C_l ->0.

If every C_l>=0, each fixed nonnegative term has a limit bounded by that zero sum; thus every C_l=0. Polynomial completeness implies mu=0 (all polynomial moments vanish; polynomials are dense in continuous functions on the compact support). Since the physical q map is one-to-one and K0>0, psi=0 almost everywhere, a contradiction.

For even l use [K_a(z)+K_a(-z)]/2. The support is away from both endpoints+/-1, so the same limit forces all even coefficients zero. The even moments determine the measure on z>=0, because every continuous function of z there is a continuous function of z² and can be uniformly approximated by polynomials in z². This again forces mu=0. No convergence of a Legendre series at a discontinuous hard edge is assumed.

This exact obstruction explains why a nonzero literal finite-transfer hard cutoff cannot obey the full all-l cone of Eq(5.13). Source Eq(5.9) deliberately replaces E by0 in the leading scaling limit; our theorem does not contradict that step. At finite E one must keep an approximate-negative remainder and charge its spectral weight, or change the admitted detector/smearing interface. Softer positive effects on a full channel may be meaningful but are different supplied objects; this theorem does not rule them out.
