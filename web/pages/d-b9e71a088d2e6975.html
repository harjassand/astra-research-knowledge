# Quantitative random analytic measurements: charged constants

Exact question/interface: Given iid sampling Z_i~rho and scalar m(x,z), x in compact K subset R^d, when do measurements provide uniform inverse Lipschitz conditioning in the chosen parameter norm and max data norm? The source's Theorem 2.2 guarantees exact identification with M>=2d+1 under analytic separation. Section 1.5 expressly leaves quantitative stability open. The constructions/theorem below are our deductions, not author claims.

## Decisive obstruction (complete universal construction)

Take K=[0,1], z uniform on [0,1], m_k(x,z)=x z^k, integer k>=1. Extend m analytically to R^2, so endpoints introduce no analytic issue. Every sampled system with at least one z_i>0 is injective and immersive. Full-data sup separation is exactly |x-y|; sup_z |D_x m|=1, D_x^2m=0, diameter=1, dimension=1. Yet sampled max-norm separation is a|x-y| with a=(max_i z_i)^k. The optimal inverse Lipschitz constant is L=a^-1. For every R>=1,

P(L>=R)=R^(-M/k).

Thus any confidence 1-delta bound must allow L>=delta^(-k/M). In particular P(L>=2^k)=2^-M and median L=2^(k/M). No bound depending only on dimension, measurement number, diameter, full-data sup differential and parameter Hessian upper bounds is possible. Sampling-variable analytic order/regularity or quantitative observability must be charged. The small-ball law is exactly P(|(m(x,Z)-m(y,Z))/(x-y)|<=t)=t^(1/k). Its exponent beta=1/k, which degenerates with k. Even normalized full-data injectivity is insufficient. This is an abstract analytic-family obstruction, not a claimed realization within Calderon/scattering physics.

Separately, m(x,z)=x^(2k+1) on [-1,1] has inverse Holder exponent at most 1/(2k+1), whatever M: test x=h,y=0 and let h decrease to zero. Differential injectivity fails at zero. Hence a dimension-only Holder exponent also fails under mere analytic injectivity.

## Usable quantitative theorem (complete net proof)

Let S be the family of normalized secant functions s_xy(z)=(m(x,z)-m(y,z))/||x-y|| for distinct x,y in K, with its closure in L-infinity(rho). Suppose:

1. Each s in S obeys P(|s(Z)|<=u)<=C u^beta for 0<u<=u0; C,beta,u0 are uniform charged constants.
2. The sup-norm covering number obeys N(S,r)<= (A/r)^q for 0<r<=r0, with centers in S.

For 0<t<=min(u0/2,r0), sample M independently. A t-net with N centers exists. If some s has max_i|s(Z_i)|<t, a center within t has all its sampled values <2t. Each center has probability at most [C(2t)^beta]^M by independence. The union bound gives

P(inf_s max_i |s(Z_i)|<t) <= A^q (C 2^beta)^M t^(beta M-q).

The same statement for essential sup-norm uses a common full-probability set for the finitely many center/approximation comparisons, or genuine pointwise uniform bounds. To avoid uncountable null-set subtleties assume actual bounded continuous functions and pointwise sup norm.

If beta M>q, set t to the minimum of u0/2, r0, and [delta/(A^q (C 2^beta)^M)]^(1/(beta M-q)). With probability >=1-delta, ||x-y||<=||F_M(x)-F_M(y)||_infinity/t for every pair. Any feasible reconstruction with both truth and estimate within eta of noisy observations has error <=2eta/t. Exact optimization/computation and existence of a feasible estimate are separate requirements; this theorem supplies no efficient reconstruction algorithm.

All constants C,beta,u0,A,q,r0 and their physical/model dependence are acquisition obligations. Analyticity by itself is not a verification of assumptions 1-2. This elementary bound is conservative and not asserted optimal.

## Derivative regularity supplies entropy, not small-ball

For compact convex K with diameter D, assume |D_xm|<=B and ||D_x^2m||<=H uniformly for all z. Write secants as s_(x,v,r)(z)=integral_0^1 D_xm(x+trv,z)v dt, with ||v||=1, 0<=r<=D and segment in K. For two admissible triples, both interpolation points lie in convex K, and direct subtraction bounds sup difference by H||x-x'||+(B+HD/2)||v-v'||+(H/2)|r-r'|. Thus a bounded ambient grid and choosing an admissible representative from each occupied cell produces a finite net, with radius charged by B,H,D and coordinate diameter. Crude ball covering gives exponent q=2d+1. A sphere covering improves it to 2d, but is unnecessary for this conservative result. Grid cell radii must be halved before representative selection to account for the extra factor two. Compactness of the constrained triples and continuity gives compact S. These x-derivative bounds are satisfied uniformly in k in the first counterexample (B=1,H=0), so they emphatically do not imply a uniform beta.

Nearest prior art/novelty: source Section 1.4 cites stable random manifold embeddings and Section 1.5 quantitative stability; our net/small-ball argument is a standard elementary technique and no novelty is claimed. This identifies the additional uniform quantitative observable contract needed to turn its zero-measure argument into a usable noise guarantee. Originality/priority is unverified beyond this limited comparison.
