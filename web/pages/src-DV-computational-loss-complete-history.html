Activity-sensitive simulation of complete open-system dynamics
The stronger consequence is simulation of the full physical quantum channel—not just its no-jump propagator—with cost that adapts to the activity of the unknown input state.
The construction below gives, for time-dependent Lindblad dynamics,
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_\rho Q
=
\widetilde O\!\left(
1+g\sqrt{T(1+\nu_\rho)}+h_0T
\right),
}
\tag{1}
\]
while retaining
\[
\boxed{
Q_{\mathrm{worst}}
=
\widetilde O\!\left(1+(g^2+h_0)T\right).
}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(g\) is the supplied normalization of the stacked jump-operator oracle, \(h_0\) is the Hamiltonian normalization, and \(\nu_\rho\) is the expected number of physical jumps from input \(\rho\) during \([0,T]\). The algorithm neither receives nor estimates \(\nu_\rho\). It operates on one unknown input, including an input entangled with an external reference.
The missing operation was to recover the lost amplitudes from the time-history construction. The resulting map is an isometry, so it can be implemented without a survival-probability penalty. An adaptive schedule then turns that first-event operation into the full-channel simulator.
This is a new derivation in this investigation. Independent verification and historical priority remain unestablished.
1. Exact target and comparison with current algorithms
Write
\[
\mathcal L_t(\rho)
=
-i[H(t),\rho]
+\sum_{\mu=1}^{r}L_\mu(t)\rho L_\mu(t)^\dagger
-\frac12\left\{
\sum_\mu L_\mu(t)^\dagger L_\mu(t),\rho
\right\},
\]
and define the rectangular stacked map
\[
\mathsf L(t)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{r}|\mu\rangle\otimes L_\mu(t).
\]
The input supplies coherent, controlled and inverse access to projected unitary encodings of
\[
\mathsf L(t)/g,\qquad H(t)/h_0
\]
at binary-encoded times. It also supplies valid bounds
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathsf L(t)\|&\le g,&
\|H(t)\|&\le h_0,\\
\|\mathsf L(t)-\mathsf L(s)\|&\le \ell_L|t-s|,&
\|H(t)-H(s)\|&\le \ell_H|t-s|.
\end{aligned}
\]
Zero-normalization terms can be omitted. No commutation, spectral-gap or mixing-time assumption is imposed.
Let \(\Phi_{T,0}\) be the resulting channel and \(\rho(t)=\Phi_{t,0}(\rho)\). Define
\[
\nu_\rho
=
\int_0^T
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
\mathsf L(t)^\dagger\mathsf L(t)\rho(t)
\right]dt.
\tag{3}
\]
This is the expected jump count in the supplied jump representation, and
\[
0\le \nu_\rho\le g^2T.
\tag{4}
\]
The output is an adaptive quantum implementation \(\widetilde\Phi\) satisfying
\[
\|\widetilde\Phi-\Phi_{T,0}\|_\diamond\le\varepsilon.
\tag{5}
\]
The guarantee is uniform over inputs; the expected execution cost in (1) is input-dependent.

2. The new operation: recover losses as well as survival
Consider an arbitrary factorized resolvent chain
\[
A_j=B_j^\dagger B_j+iH_j,\qquad
R_j=(I+A_j)^{-1},\qquad
F_j=R_j\cdots R_1,
\]
with \(H_j=H_j^\dagger\) and \(F_0=I\).
Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V_n x
={}&|\mathrm{survive}\rangle F_nx\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|j,\mathrm{physical}\rangle
       \sqrt2\,B_jF_jx\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^{n}|j,\mathrm{defect}\rangle
       A_jF_jx.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The rectangular output of \(B_j\), including any jump-type register, is retained.
Exact conservation identity
Since
\[
(I+A_j)^\dagger(I+A_j)-I
=
2B_j^\dagger B_j+A_j^\dagger A_j,
\]
we have
\[
I-R_j^\dagger R_j
=
R_j^\dagger
\left(2B_j^\dagger B_j+A_j^\dagger A_j\right)
R_j.
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{j-1}^\dagger F_{j-1}-F_j^\dagger F_j
={}&2F_j^\dagger B_j^\dagger B_jF_j\\
&+F_j^\dagger A_j^\dagger A_jF_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing over \(j\) proves
\[
\boxed{\mathcal V_n^\dagger\mathcal V_n=I.}
\tag{8}
\]
This includes all lost norm. The second line of (6) is the physical loss; the third is the additional defect introduced by the resolvent discretization.
The important consequence is not merely that a completion exists. All three parts can be extracted efficiently from the existing time-history construction.
Extracting the complete isometry
Pad \(n\ge2\) to a power of two. Use the previous clock construction with
\[
\theta=1-\frac1n,\qquad
b=1-\theta^n,\qquad
c=\theta^{n+1}b^2.
\]
Let the cyclic clock length be \(L_c\), and put
\[
Z=(I-\theta^{L_c}F_n)^{-1}.
\]
The main history operator is
\[
M=n(I-\theta C)+n\,\operatorname{diag}_{\mathrm{active}}(A_j).
\]
Recall the known clock matrices
\[
D_0=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}(I-C),
\qquad
K_0=-\frac{n-1}{2i}(C-C^\dagger),
\]
and the Hermitian enlargement
\[
J=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_0+n\mathsf H&-iD_0^\dagger&-i\sqrt n\,\mathsf B^\dagger\\
iD_0&0&0\\
i\sqrt n\,\mathsf B&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{9}
\]
Applying \((I+iJ)^{-1}\) to the usual source clock and input \(x\) produces three sectors
\[
\mathbf u=M^{-1}(|s\rangle x),
\qquad
\mathbf w=D_0\mathbf u,
\qquad
\mathbf v=\sqrt n\,\mathsf B\mathbf u.
\tag{10}
\]
Index the active main-history components by \(u_j\), with \(u_0\) immediately preceding the active interval. The exact recurrences give
\[
u_j=\frac{b}{\sqrt n}\theta^jF_jZx,
\qquad
v_j=b\theta^jB_jF_jZx,
\tag{11}
\]
and the survivor readout is
\[
\langle t|\mathbf u\rangle=cF_nZx.
\tag{12}
\]
The physical loss therefore needs only the known scalar readout
\[
\frac{\sqrt2\,v_j}{64b\theta^j}.
\]
The discretization defect initially appears harder because it contains \(A_j\). But the clock sector already contains it. On the active interval,
\[
w_j=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}(u_j-u_{j-1}),
\qquad
A_ju_j=\theta u_{j-1}-u_j.
\]
Hence
\[
\boxed{
\sqrt n\,A_ju_j
=
-\sqrt{\frac{2n}{n-1}}\,w_j
-\frac{u_{j-1}}{\sqrt n}.
}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus a completely known readout \(E\) produces
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{survivor:}&\displaystyle
\frac{\langle t|\mathbf u\rangle}{64c},\\[6pt]
\text{physical loss at \(j\):}&\displaystyle
\frac{\sqrt2\,v_j}{64b\theta^j},\\[6pt]
\text{defect at \(j\):}&\displaystyle
\frac{
-\sqrt{2n/(n-1)}\,w_j-n^{-1/2}u_{j-1}}
{64b\theta^j}.
\end{array}
\tag{14}
\]
Combining these equations,
\[
\boxed{
E(I+iJ)^{-1}(|s\rangle\otimes I)
=
\frac1{64}\mathcal V_n Z.
}
\tag{15}
\]
No square root of an unknown defect operator, and no additional \(A_j\)-oracle, is required.
Why the readout is inexpensive
For \(n\ge2\),
\[
\theta^n\ge\frac14,\qquad
b\ge\frac12,\qquad
c\ge\frac1{32}.
\]
The survivor row of \(E\) has norm at most \(1/2\). The physical rows have norm at most \(\sqrt2/8\). Each defect row combines two orthogonal known sectors and has norm at most
\[
\frac18\sqrt{\frac{2n}{n-1}+\frac1n}<\frac12.
\]
Different \(j\)'s use disjoint coordinate pairs, and the survivor readout uses a disjoint clock interval. Therefore
\[
\|E\|\le\frac12.
\tag{16}
\]
The readout is implemented by reversible clock arithmetic and controlled two-dimensional rotations. Its coefficients are computed to finite precision; the potentially enormous exact denominator of \(\theta^j\) is never written.
This is the cancellation that matters computationally: the main history carries a factor \(1/\sqrt n\), while the physical auxiliary contains \(\sqrt n\,B_j\). Their product has constant extraction amplitude. The clock auxiliary supplies the other loss in the same way.

The previous resolvent implementation retains the query bound

\widetilde O(1+\sqrt n\,\beta+n\gamma)

for indexed encodings of \(B_j/\beta,H_j/\gamma\). Its large clock-only Hamiltonian is explicitly fast-forwardable; interaction-picture simulation charges the unknown part rather than the clock norm. The required simulation theorem supports this distinction. arXiv
Choose \(L_c=O(n\log(1/\eta))\). Since \(\|F_n\|\le1\),
\[
\|Z-I\|
\le
\frac{\theta^{L_c}}{1-\theta^{L_c}},
\]
so (15) gives an arbitrarily accurate block encoding of \(\mathcal V_n/64\).
All singular values of \(\mathcal V_n\) equal one. Robust oblivious amplification, or rectangular singular-value transformation, therefore converts this into an implementation of the isometry with only logarithmic precision overhead. The projectors used by amplification specify clean ancillary subspaces—not the unknown input state. arXiv

Set
\[

and define

a=\frac{g^2}{2}+h_0,

\lambda=g^2T.

\]
Use a uniform grid of \(N\) cells, width \(\Delta=T/N\), with right endpoints \(t_j\). A single step has Kraus operators
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{0,j}&=R_j=(I+\Delta A(t_j))^{-1},\\
K_{\mu,j}&=\sqrt\Delta\,L_\mu(t_j)R_j,\\
K_{\mathrm{err},j}&=\Delta A(t_j)R_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Equation (7) proves exact trace preservation. The final outcome is an artificial numerical-error outcome, not a physical jump. Its probability is small:
\[
\|K_{\mathrm{err},j}\|^2\le\Delta^2a^2.
\tag{21}
\]
On this outcome the algorithm stops and returns an error-flagged state; it does not discard the execution.
An interval containing \(n\) grid cells is compiled using
\[
B_j=\sqrt{\Delta/2}\,\mathsf L(t_j),
\qquad
H_j=\Delta H(t_j).
\]
Thus its first-event query cost is
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left(
1+g\sqrt{n\Delta}+h_0n\Delta
\right).
}
\tag{22}
\]
Crucially,
\[
B_j/(g\sqrt{\Delta/2})=\mathsf L(t_j)/g.

Making the grid finer does not require a new, stronger matrix oracle.
Local channel error
For \(a\Delta\le1/2\), the recorded single-cell instrument differs from the exact physical instrument by at most
\[
\boxed{32D\Delta^2}
\tag{23}
\]
in diamond norm, when exact jump times are reported by their grid-cell index.
Here are the components of the bound.
The exact no-jump propagator \(U_j\) satisfies
\[
\|U_j-R_j\|
\le
\frac{\Delta^2}{2}
\left(a^2+g\ell_L+\ell_H\right).
\tag{24}
\]
Freezing \(A(t)\) gives the Lipschitz contribution; comparing the exponential with the resolvent gives the \(a^2\) contribution.
For a single jump at time \(s\) within the cell, compare
\[
U(t_j,s)L_\mu(s)U(s,t_{j-1})
\quad\text{with}\quad
L_\mu(t_j)R_j.
\]
Using the stacked map, their operator-norm difference is bounded by
\[
\Delta(3ag+\ell_L).
\]
After integrating and converting to the corresponding completely positive maps, this contributes at most
\[
\Delta^2(6ag^2+2g\ell_L).
\]
The total probability of two or more jumps is bounded by
\[
\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(g^2\Delta)^k}{k!}

and the numerical branch contributes at most \(\Delta^2a^2\). These bounds sum to less than (23). They remain valid after tensoring with an arbitrary reference system.
The analysis compares complete instruments before conditioning. No error bound is divided by the probability of an observed jump.

64T\Delta D(1+g^2T).

To see this, write \(\sigma_{j-1}\) for its unnormalised active state. Its jump probability in cell \(j\) is

The accumulated state error is at most \(32T\Delta D\). Also,

Comparing \(\sum_jp_j\) with the integral in (3) gives (25).

The zero-generator case is handled directly.

4. An adaptive schedule that does not know the activity
Repeatedly compiling the entire remaining interval after every jump would cost roughly
\[
g\sqrt T\times(\text{number of jumps}),
\]
which is insufficient. The schedule must change as activity is observed.
Use phases \(r=0,1,2,\ldots\). In phase \(r\), partition time into blocks of length
\[
T/2^r
\]
and permit \(2^r\) new physical jumps before advancing to phase \(r+1\).
Within a block, invoke the first-event compiler on its remaining suffix. A survivor outcome advances to the block end. A physical jump returns its cell and post-jump state; execution resumes at the next grid cell. A numerical-error outcome stops with an error flag.
No future jump count is needed.
Pathwise cost
Phase \(r\) encounters at most \(2^r\) block endings and \(2^r\) physical jumps. Its query cost is therefore
\[
\widetilde O\!\left(
2^r+g\sqrt{T\,2^r}+h_0T
\right).
\tag{27}
\]
If an execution observes \(k\) physical jumps, it reaches at most phase \(O(\log(k+1))\). Summing the geometric series gives
\[
\boxed{
Q(k)
=
\widetilde O\!\left(
1+k+g\sqrt{T(k+1)}+h_0T
\right).
}
\tag{28}
\]
The logarithmic number of phases multiplying the Hamiltonian term is included in \(\widetilde O\).
Implement each first-event instrument to diamond error at most
\[
\varepsilon/(1024N^2).
\]
Every call advances at least one grid cell or stops, so there are at most \(N\) calls. Invalid labels caused by implementation error are detected and treated as an error outcome. The total instrument error is consequently small enough to control both the final channel and the first moment of its finite jump count.
By (25),
\[
\mathbb E k\le\nu_\rho+O(\varepsilon).
\]
Jensen’s inequality applied to (28) gives
\[
\mathbb E Q
=
\widetilde O\!\left(
1+\nu_\rho+g\sqrt{T(1+\nu_\rho)}+h_0T
\right).
\]
Since \(\nu_\rho\le g^2T\), the standalone \(\nu_\rho\) term is absorbed:
\[
\nu_\rho\le g\sqrt{T\nu_\rho}.
\]
This proves (1) for the adaptive trajectory simulation.
Retaining a worst-case linear bound
Without an additional mechanism, an unusually long record could still make execution expensive.
Set
\[
q_0=\lceil1+g^2T\rceil.
\]
After observing \(q_0\) physical jumps, stop using the trajectory compiler and invoke a general time-dependent Lindblad-channel simulator once on the remaining interval and current quantum state. The established general algorithms provide this subroutine without a state-preparation oracle. arXiv
Before this fallback, (28) costs at most
\[
\widetilde O(1+(g^2+h_0)T).
\]
The fallback has the same worst-case order, proving (2).
It does not spoil the expected bound. Its trigger probability satisfies
\[
p_{\mathrm{fallback}}
\le
\frac{\nu_\rho+O(\varepsilon)}{q_0}.
\]
Its dissipative expected cost is therefore \(O(1+\nu_\rho)\), up to logarithms; its Hamiltonian expected cost is at most \(\widetilde O(h_0T)\).
Correctness survives the switch because the fallback acts on the actual current state, rather than restarting the input. All future controlled continuations are trace-preserving, so the preceding instrument errors remain bounded without conditional-probability factors.
The fallback version guarantees the final quantum channel, not the complete jump record after fallback. Omitting fallback retains the grid-resolved trajectory record and the expected-cost bound, but loses the worst-case bound (2).

Q_j=I-P_j.

The full first-failure isometry is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W x

|\mathrm{pass}\rangle P_m\cdots P_1x

\tag{29}

Given the coherent indexed reflection oracle
\[
\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes(2P_j-I),

the construction implements \(\mathcal W\), not merely its pass block, using

\boxed{\widetilde O(\sqrt m)}
\tag{30}

queries. It works on one unknown input and preserves entanglement with a reference.

\lambda=O(M\log(M/\eta)),

\[
\sqrt n\,\beta=\sqrt\lambda,

giving (30).

Full evolution with a bounded number of irreversible losses
A fixed-budget version stops and flags overflow on the \((K+1)\)-st physical jump. Using \(O(K+1)\) time blocks gives worst-case cost
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left(
K+1+g\sqrt{T(K+1)}+h_0T
\right).
}
\tag{31}
\]
If the true process has at most \(K\) jumps, this simulates the entire channel, including its binned jump record, without overflow.
A sufficient structural condition is a known grading
\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{e=0}^{K}\mathcal H_e
\]
such that \(H(t)\) preserves each grade and every \(L_\mu(t)\) lowers the grade by exactly one. Then \(L_\mu^\dagger L_\mu\) preserves grade, no-jump evolution preserves grade, and each physical jump decreases it.
Arbitrary noncommutation within these constraints is allowed. For \(H=0\) and fixed \(K\), the time dependence is square-root rather than linear.
More generally, if the probability of more than \(K\) jumps is uniformly at most \(p\), discarding the record gives full-channel error at most
\[
\varepsilon+2p.
\]
The factor is \(p\), not \(\sqrt p\), because this comparison is between the corresponding positive, trace-decreasing trajectory subchannels.
6. Resource accounting
Coherent acquisition. The algorithm requires actual time-indexed encodings of \(\mathsf L(t)\) and \(H(t)\). It does not construct them from an arbitrary matrix-entry list at no cost. If separate encodings of \(L_\mu\) have normalizations \(\alpha_\mu\), building the stacked oracle requires coherent selection and preparation of amplitudes proportional to \(\alpha_\mu\); the resulting normalization is
\[
g^2=\sum_\mu\alpha_\mu^2.
\]
Those preparation and selector costs must be charged.
Precision and gates. Let \(C_{\mathrm{or}}(b)\) bound the gate cost of a controlled or inverse oracle call at \(b\)-bit working precision. The additional computations use
\[
b=\operatorname{poly}\!\left(
\text{parameter bit length}
+\log N
+\log\frac{1+\tau}{\varepsilon}
+\log(r+2)
\right)
\]
bits. The query bounds suppress polynomial factors in these logarithmic quantities, not powers of \(N\), \(1/\varepsilon\), the system dimension or the number of jump operators.
A conservative implementation allocates oracle and arithmetic errors tightly enough that the conceptual complete trajectory instrument differs from its ideal discrete version by \(O(\varepsilon/N)\). For example, if \(Q_*\) is an upper bound for that finite construction, requiring each implemented oracle unitary to be within \(O(\varepsilon/(NQ_*))\) in operator norm suffices by a hybrid bound. The necessary precision depends on \(\log Q_*\), not \(Q_*\).
The gate bounds have the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_\rho G
&\le
\widetilde O\!\left[
\bigl(1+g\sqrt{T(1+\nu_\rho)}+h_0T\bigr)
\bigl(C_{\mathrm{or}}(b)+\operatorname{poly}(b,\log d,\log r)\bigr)
\right],\\
G_{\mathrm{worst}}
&\le
\widetilde O\!\left[
(1+\tau)
\bigl(C_{\mathrm{or}}(b)+\operatorname{poly}(b,\log d,\log r)\bigr)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
These are adaptive execution costs. Padding every branch into one fixed-length unitary circuit preserves the worst-case bound, not the expected-gate improvement.
Preprocessing. The grid size, phase thresholds and rotation constants are generated from binary parameters. There is no \(N\)-entry history table. Circuit generation is uniform and performed as needed; in particular, the full fallback circuit is not constructed unconditionally before the first-event computation. Classical generation work is bounded by the generated circuit size times polynomial working-precision overhead.
Memory and output. For final-state simulation, earlier records may be discarded after updating the current cell, phase and counter. Quantum workspace consists of the system, oracle workspaces and polylogarithmic clock/arithmetic registers—not \(N\) system registers. Retaining a trajectory requires explicitly writing its jump types and grid-cell indices; that output cost is charged.
Randomness and normalization. Quantum measurement of the completed isometry supplies the event distribution. There is no rejection loop over rare trajectories, no tomography to evaluate instantaneous hazards, and no reflection about the unknown input state. All outcomes are retained or handled by a trace-preserving error branch.
This does not provide inexpensive preparation of a specified rare trajectory or a normalized no-jump state. It implements their unconditioned physical mixture.
Verification. Norm, Lipschitz and structural bounds are input promises or supplied certificates. The algorithm does not verify arbitrary black-box operators against them for free. Likewise, this is a channel implementation, not a classical listing of its matrix or an automatic experimental diamond-norm certificate.
7. What makes the stronger bound meaningful
The activity-sensitive dependence cannot generally be improved to polylogarithmic time even when the expected number of jumps is bounded.
Take \(H=0\), initial state \(|e\rangle\), and compare
\[
L_0=0,\qquad
L_1=T^{-1/2}|g\rangle\langle e|.
\]
Both have expected jump count at most one. At time \(T\), their output states differ by a constant: the second process has decayed with probability \(1-e^{-1}\).
With common oracle normalization \(g\), block-encoding unitaries for these two inputs can differ by only
\[
O\!\left(\frac1{g\sqrt T}\right).
\]
The hybrid argument therefore requires \(\Omega(g\sqrt T)\) queries for constant-error distinction. Truncating a variable-time algorithm at a constant multiple of its maximum expected cost gives the same lower-bound order for expected queries.
Thus the square-root dependence has a genuine oracle-level obstruction even in a low-activity physical family.
Conversely, when \(\nu_\rho=\Theta(g^2T)\), equation (1) becomes linear in \(g^2T\). There is no claimed universal fast-forwarding of arbitrary dissipative computation.
Established outcome and remaining status
The strongest surviving result is:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{Complete time-dependent Lindblad-channel simulation}\\
\text{on an unknown input, with automatic activity-sensitive expected cost,}\\
\text{and a worst-case linear-cost fallback.}
\end{gathered}
}
\]
Its essential mechanism is loss-complete history readout: the physical auxiliary supplies jump amplitudes, the clock auxiliary supplies the discretization defect, and their exact conservation identity permits uniform isometry amplification. The adaptive schedule then exploits observed activity without acquiring the unknown state or its hazard rates.
The complete first-failure projector instrument and the bounded-loss physical simulator are additional consequences—not substitutes for the general adaptive theorem.
Dense noncommuting examples reproduced the isometry and complete-readout identities with residuals below \(10^{-14}\). Those checks only test the algebra; no full quantum circuit or formal proof was executed. The construction and resource analysis above establish the proposed capability within the stated oracle model, but independent correctness review and historical novelty verification remain outstanding.
