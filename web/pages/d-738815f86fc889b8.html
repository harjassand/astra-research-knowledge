**Gate: PASS for the universal explicit-output obstruction.** The output interface is an explicit rational basis of a largest weight-maximizing subrepresentation of a dense integer quiver with signed integer weights encoded in binary. The obstruction persists for dimension-three vertices, constant arrow entries, an acyclic quiver of directed depth at most two, and a unique optimizer over every field. ## G1. Lexicographic compilation directly from the subrepresentation equations

Let a chronological programme consist of fixed subspaces of K^d, followed by sums and intersections of previously named subspaces. Add a first frame vertex. Every representation vertex is K^d. If a fixed subspace is im C, add the arrow C from the frame to that vertex. For a sum J=X+Y add identity arrows X→J and Y→J. For an intersection M=X∩Y add identity arrows M→X and M→Y. No other arrows are present.

Index the N vertices from zero in programme order, with the frame first. Give the frame and intersections sign +1, and the fixed images and sums sign −1. Put

    sigma_i = sign_i (d+1)^(N−1−i).

The scalar objective is sum_i sigma_i dim W_i. This implements chronological lexicographic optimization of sign_i dim W_i: if two dimension lists first differ at i and the former has the desired improvement, its gain at i is at least B^r, where B=d+1 and r=N−1−i. The largest possible adverse tail is

    d (1+B+...+B^(r−1)) = B^r−1.

Thus the former score is strictly larger. This argument concerns dimension lists and needs no subspace enumeration.

Every feasible prefix extends. Assign K^d to every unchosen fixed-image or sum vertex and zero to every unchosen intersection vertex. A future sum receives any parent in K^d, and a future intersection sends zero into any parent. The same check covers arrows between future vertices and arrows crossing the prefix. A future fixed image receives the frame in K^d. There are no additional constraints.

Induction therefore fixes the actual subspaces uniquely. The first positive digit selects the full frame. A negative fixed-image digit selects exactly im C: every feasible image vertex contains im C, and the minimum dimension has only this subspace. A negative sum digit selects X+Y by the same containment argument. A positive intersection digit selects X∩Y: every feasible intersection vertex lies in that intersection, and maximum dimension forces equality. At each step the desired choice extends by the preceding paragraph. Consequently the unique global maximizing subrepresentation is precisely the programme. This proof holds over every field and allows rank degeneracies. It is a mathematical acquisition certificate for this admitted family, not a general support algorithm.

## G2. An eleven-operation squaring word over Z

Use homogeneous point vectors in K^3; `(x,y,1)` denotes its one-dimensional span. The nine fixed-image vertices are the six points

    O=(0,0,1), Ex=(1,0,0), Ey=(0,1,0),
    D=(1,−1,0), I=(1,0,1), P0=(2,0,1),

and three planes

    Lx=span(O,Ex), Ly=span(O,Ey), Lx1=span(I,Ey).

Their normal vectors are respectively (0,1,0), (1,0,0), (1,0,−1). Each is the image of a 3×3 matrix whose first one or two columns are the displayed generators and whose remaining columns are zero. Entries belong to {−1,0,1,2}. Each image has a unit entry or unit 2×2 minor, so its dimension is unchanged over every field.

Starting at P=(t,0,1), append these eleven vertices in the displayed order:

| Vertex | Operation | Result or plane normal |
|---|---|---|
| A | P+D | normal (1,1,−t) |
| Q | A∩Ly | point (0,t,1) |
| B | Q+Ex | normal (0,1,−t) |
| R | B∩Lx1 | point (1,t,1) |
| C | O+R | normal (−t,1,0) |
| E | P+Ey | normal (−1,0,t) |
| S | C∩E | point (t,t²,1) |
| G | S+Ex | normal (0,1,−t²) |
| T | G∩Ly | point (0,t²,1) |
| H | T+D | normal (1,1,−t²) |
| Pnew | H∩Lx | point (t²,0,1) |

Here `+` denotes sum of subspaces. Every join has rank two: the cross product of its two displayed generators has a component +1 or −1. Every meet has rank one: the cross product of its two normal vectors has last component +1 or −1. For example, C and E meet in the literal polynomial vector (t,t²,1), and H meets Lx in (t²,0,1). Thus the identities are over Z[t], with no division by t, and their dimensions hold for every specialization and every characteristic, including t=0 in characteristic two. Exact polynomial reconstruction is the appropriate implementation check; successful numerical parameters alone would not establish this assertion.

After k rounds, the final point is

    Pk = span(2^(2^k),0,1).

All identity arrows in this particular compiled representation point from a point vertex to a plane vertex. The frame has no incoming arrows. Therefore the quiver is acyclic and every directed path has length at most two, despite programme order differing from topological arrow order.

## G3. Original binary input is small; explicit rational output is large

The construction has

    N=10+11k vertices, E=9+22k arrows, A=3N total active dimension.

All arrow matrices have constant-size entries; each operation contributes two identity arrows. The absolute weight of vertex i is 4^(N−1−i), with unsigned binary bit length 2(N−1−i)+1. The sum of these lengths is exactly N². Signs require O(N) extra bits; dense arrow entries require O(E) bits; endpoint labels require O(E log N) bits. Thus the original explicit binary input has O(k²) bits. This includes the frame and all fixed-image arrows; no supplied exponentially large coordinate is hidden in the input.

Write t=2^(2^k). The final point has exactly two possible identity-pivot charts: (1,0,1/t) or (t,0,1). Their maximum numerator/denominator scalar bit length is 2^k+1. A different rational scaling cannot remove the output lower bound. For any nonzero rational vector (a,0,b) on this line, a/b=t. Writing a=A/B and b=C/D in reduced rational form gives AD=t BC. The total binary length of the four nonzero integers is at least log2(t)=2^k, up to a fixed additive constant. Hence an arbitrary explicit rational basis also has exponentially many output bits in k. Polynomial time or polynomial output height in the original O(k²)-bit input is impossible for this unrestricted explicit-basis interface.

This is a representation-size obstruction. It makes no computational-hardness claim about support values or compressed representations, and does not assert a necessary p-adic precision for every possible reconstruction method. Cycle17's particular symmetric reconstruction sufficient condition M>2H² would require log2 M=Omega(2^k) when H must cover one of these identity-chart coordinates. Structured circuit reconstruction may avoid that sufficient condition; printing the explicit rational coordinate remains expensive.

## G4. Canonical uniqueness and tangent rigidity do not supply a height bound

The optimizer constructed in G1 is unique, so it is the unique largest optimizer. For completeness, the usual rigidity implication is reconstructed here. If a nonzero quiver map W→V/W existed, its image I would be both a quotient of W and a nonzero subrepresentation of V/W. A kernel K⊂W satisfies sigma(K)≤sigma(W), so sigma(I)=sigma(W)−sigma(K)≥0. On the other hand, the preimage of I in V is a strictly larger subrepresentation containing W. It has score sigma(W)+sigma(I), forcing sigma(I)≤0 by maximality, and forcing strict negativity by largestness. This contradiction gives Hom(W,V/W)=0. The obstruction therefore survives exactly the zero-tangent hypothesis used by the canonical chart lift.

At a point component the integer generator has last coordinate one. At a plane component its displayed integer normal has a coefficient ±1. Both define primitive direct-summand lattices and reduce with the same dimensions over every prime. The reductions satisfy the literal polynomial squaring identities and are the unique modular optimizers by G1. Thus the selected optimizer and its support commute with reduction for this family at every prime, including two. This is a **selected-objective theorem for the constructed family**, not equality of the complete dimension polytopes and not a globally-good-prime certificate.

## G5. A useful small-output acquisition interface survives

    3 (1+4+...+4^(N−1)) = 4^N−1,

so it has O(N) bits. A verifier checks the compiler structure and signs, then G1 supplies a universal upper certificate matching the declared word. This certifies the support without numerical weight copies. It is a structural control for this family, not a replacement for a general weighted quiver oracle.

No empirical runtime or generic advantage is inferred from these formulas.
