COMMUTING SCALAR-RESOLVENT ACQUISITION
7 October 2026. Internal derivation; not externally reviewed. Parent scope:
exact scalar black-box commutativization of an m-state recognizable nc series.

RESULT IN ONE SENTENCE
Over characteristic zero, with mutually commuting transitions and an exact
point oracle that returns a recognized pole symbol, one can acquire a minimal
commuting realization from O(n m^4) scalar queries with success probability
1-delta; importing Bhardwaj--Saptharishi 2609.34436v1 derandomizes acquisition
at a deliberately loose polynomial query bound O(n^2 m^14+n m^4).

This does not recover arbitrary noncommuting series, remove the scalar-oracle
acquisition assumption, or establish a practical numerical/noisy learner.

1. EXACT INTERFACE
K is a characteristic-zero field with exact arithmetic/equality. Unknown
lambda in K^(1xm), gamma in K^(mx1), and A_1,...,A_n in K^(mxm) satisfy
[A_i,A_j]=0. The supplied size bound is m>=1. Put
 A(x)=sum_i x_i A_i, R(x)=(I-A(x))^{-1}, f(x)=lambda R(x) gamma.
Oracle input x in K^n returns this scalar if det(I-A(x))!=0 and a recognized
POLE otherwise. Values at removable singularities are not needed. Zero is
always legal. Cost counts all scalar calls, including POLE answers.

Alternative interface: a supplied safe domain with enough requested points.
If queries must be certified in-domain in advance for every hidden instance,
with no pole signal/safe region/height bound, even m=1 is unlearnable: after
f(0)=1, any proposed nonzero x can be made a pole by choosing a dot x=1 in
f=1/(1-a dot x). This is an interface obstruction, not a PIT lower bound.

The output is a row beta, column alpha, and commuting r x r matrices B_i,
r<=m, satisfying f(x)=beta(I-sum_i x_i B_i)^{-1}alpha as rational functions.
Its nc word coefficients agree with the hidden commuting realization.

2. MINIMAL QUOTIENT AND RESOLVENT SPAN
Let W=span{A^a gamma:a in N^n}. Because transitions commute, it is cyclic
over the commutative algebra they generate. For v=p(A)gamma and w=q(A)gamma,
define B(v,w)=lambda p(A)q(A)gamma. This is well-defined: p(A)gamma=0
implies p(A)q(A)gamma=q(A)p(A)gamma=0. It is symmetric. Its radical is
 N={v in W:lambda A^a v=0 for every a}.
N is transition-invariant. V=W/N, dimension r<=m, is reachable, observable,
and carries a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, still denoted B.

The regular resolvent vectors v(u)=R(u)gamma span V. Indeed, if a linear
functional ell on V kills every such vector, then ell R(x)gamma is zero as
a rational function over the infinite field K. Its Taylor coefficient at
x^a equals (|a|!/a!) ell A^a gamma; characteristic zero makes these factors
nonzero. Reachability then forces ell=0. This is not a monogenic-algebra
assumption: several resolvent directions can be necessary.

3. SCALAR ACCESS TO THE RESOLVENT HANKEL MATRIX
For regular endpoints u,v and x(t)=(1-t)u+t v, write R_t=R(x(t)).
All these matrices and each A_k commute. The exact matrix identity is
 d/dt [t R(u) R_t] = R_t^2.                         (1)
Proof: C=I-A(u), D=I-A(v), L(t)=C+t(D-C), R_t=L(t)^-1.
The derivative is C^-1 R_t-t C^-1 R_t(D-C)R_t
=C^-1 R_t[L(t)-t(D-C)]R_t=R_t^2.

Define scalar integrands, with integration meaning rational antidifferentiation:
 g(x)=d/ds [s f(sx)] at s=1 = lambda R(x)^2 gamma;
 h_k(x)=partial_k f(x)=lambda R(x)^2 A_k gamma.
The first identity uses d_s f(sx)=lambda R(sx)A(x)R(sx)gamma.
The second uses commutation. Equation (1) gives
 H(u,v)=lambda R(u)R(v)gamma = integral_0^1 g(x(t))dt;
 K_k(u,v)=lambda R(u)A_k R(v)gamma = integral_0^1 h_k(x(t))dt.
No analytic path, positivity, or no-interior-poles condition is imposed.

4. EXACT INTERPOLATION AND RATIONAL INTEGRATION COST
For fixed x, each s -> f(sx), and each z -> f(x+z e_k), has numerator degree
at most m-1 and denominator degree at most m. At most m sample arguments
are poles. Among any 3m distinct arguments at least 2m are legal, and 2m
legal values determine the rational function: cross multiplication gives a
polynomial of degree <=2m-1 with 2m roots. Gaussian rational interpolation
and gcd reduction cost O(m^3) field operations, conservatively.

Thus g(x) and each h_k(x) require <=3m scalar calls and O(m^3) operations,
once x is known regular. Derivatives are symbolic derivatives of the
interpolated univariate; no derivative oracle is supplied.

Along the affine segment, g(x(t)) and h_k(x(t)) have numerator and denominator
degrees <=2m (the adjugate-square expression in fact gives numerator <=2m-2).
Their poles are among <=m zeros of det(I-A(x(t))), a nonzero polynomial
because endpoints are regular. Test any 5m+1 distinct t values via f(x(t));
at least 4m+1 are legal. Evaluate g or h_k at those legal t values and
interpolate it using bounds (2m,2m).

Each pairing H or K therefore costs at most
 C_m=(5m+1)+3m(4m+1)=12m^2+8m+1 scalar calls,
including pole tests, and O(m^4) field operations using naive interpolation.

RATIONAL ANTIDERIVATIVE IS A SMALL LINEAR SYSTEM, WITHOUT FACTORIZATION.
Let the interpolated integrand be p(t)/q(t) in reduced form, both degrees
<=2m. Equation (1) supplies the hidden primitive
 F(t)=t lambda C R(u)R_t gamma,
where C=I for H and C=A_k for K. It has a representation with numerator
degree <=m and denominator degree <=m, and F(0)=0. In characteristic zero,
every pole of a rational F is a pole of F' of one higher order. Therefore
the reduced denominator q_F of F divides q. Consequently F=U/q for some
polynomial U with degree <=3m. Solve the linear coefficient equations
 U' q-U q'=p q, U(0)=0.                           (2)
There are O(m) unknowns/equations; O(m^3) field operations suffice. Regular
endpoints imply q(0)q(1)!=0. The desired pairing is U(1)/q(1). Equation (2)
has a unique rational solution after fixing F(0), because constants are the
only rational functions with zero derivative. No logarithms, numerical
quadrature, algebraic extension, or partial-fraction factorization is needed.

5. REALIZATION FROM A SPANNING SAMPLE SET
Given regular u_1,...,u_M whose resolvent vectors span V, construct H_ab=H(u_a,u_b).
It has rank exactly r. Choose row indices I and column indices J, each of
size r, with H_0=H[I,J] nonsingular. Only after choosing I,J, construct
K_{k,0}=K_k[I,J]. Define
 alpha=H_0^-1 (f(u_i):i in I)^T;
 beta=(f(u_j):j in J);
 B_k=H_0^-1 K_{k,0}.
Columns indexed by J are a basis of V, rows indexed by I the corresponding
independent B-pairings. Therefore alpha represents gamma, beta represents
lambda, and B_k represents A_k on V. They commute, and the output rational
function is exactly f. If r=0 output the zero function.
Storage for the output is n r^2+2r field elements. Prediction at a point costs
O(nr^2+r^3) arithmetic using a naive dense solve; an nc word coefficient of
length L costs O(Lr^2). Finite matrices encode all degrees/word lengths.

6. STANDALONE RANDOMIZED THEOREM WITH EXPLICIT SUCCESS BOUND
Sample m endpoints u_j independently uniformly in S^n, where S is a finite
subset of K. If any endpoint is POLE, report failure/retry. Each endpoint
determinant has degree <=m, so this event has probability <=m^2/|S|.

Fix coordinates on V. Some determinant of r resolvent vectors is nonzero
by the spanning argument. Clear their denominators using det(I-A(u_j));
its numerator is a nonzero polynomial in the r endpoint blocks of total
degree <=r(m-1), because adj(I-A(u)) has degree <=m-1. Thus the first r
random vectors fail to span with probability <=r(m-1)/|S|. This bounds the
failure probability for all m vectors as well. Hence total failure is at most
 [m^2+r(m-1)]/|S| <=2m^2/|S|.
Take |S|>=2m^2/delta to obtain correctness probability at least 1-delta.

QUERY BOUND: m+(m^2+n r^2)C_m <=m+(n+1)m^2 C_m=O(nm^4).
ARITHMETIC: O((n+1)m^6+n^2m^4), including naive interpolation, Gaussian
linear algebra, and n coordinates generated per scalar query. This is a
conservative bound, not an optimized exponent. The construction is a
Monte Carlo exact learner: insufficient samples can give an incorrect
candidate without a deterministic success certificate. The pole and span
events stated above are the only probability debt.

7. DERANDOMIZATION IMPORT FROM THE CHANGED FRONTIER
Primary source read: Shubham Bhardwaj and Ramprasad Saptharishi,
Hitting Sets for Polynomials with Small Partial Derivative Spaces,
arXiv:2609.34436v1, submitted 28 September 2026.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34436v1
Theorem 1.1, Theorem 3.1, Lemmas 2.2/2.6/2.9; full proof lines 125-309 read.
Source theorem: over characteristic zero, a polynomial with total partial-
derivative-space dimension <=rho is hit by the one-parameter generator
 G_i(t)=sum_{k=1}^{B}(i t)^k/k, B=n rho^2+rho.
For degree <=d, evaluating at dB+1 distinct t values gives a hitting set.
The source discloses AI-assisted proof provenance. Our read/audit is internal
and does not establish external certification. The transfer below assumes
that imported theorem; the standalone randomized learner above does not.

TRUNCATED RESOLVENT BRIDGE (OWN DERIVATION).
For any row ell on W or V define
 T_ell(x)=sum_{k=0}^{m-1}ell A(x)^k gamma.
Reachability spaces W_d=span{A^a gamma:|a|<=d} stabilize by d=m-1: an equality
W_d=W_{d+1} makes W_d invariant forever, and there are at most m dimensions.
Therefore nonzero ell on V implies T_ell is nonzero. For multiindex b of
degree d,
 partial^b T_ell(x)=ell A^b sum_{j=0}^{m-1-d} ((j+d)!/j!) A(x)^j gamma.
For fixed d the right-hand sides span at most m polynomials, and there are
m possible d. Hence dim partial^* T_ell<=m^2, uniformly for every ell.
The m^2 bound is important: dim partial^* of the raw ordinary generating
polynomial need not be <=m (see lower_bound/REPORT.txt for a 5-state example).

Use rho=m^2, d=m-1, B=n m^4+m^2. Let E have dB+1 distinct field elements
and Hdir={G(a):a in E}. For every nonzero ell, some v in Hdir has T_ell(v)!=0;
therefore ell R(sv)gamma is a nonzero univariate rational function of numerator
degree <=m-1, denominator degree <=m. At most m scales are poles and at most
m-1 further scales have zero numerator. Any 2m distinct scales therefore
include a legal nonzero value. Consequently the legal resolvent vectors from
 U={s G(a):a in E, s in Sscale}, |Sscale|=2m,
span V. This is uniform over all unknown rows ell and hidden coefficients.

Put M=2m[(m-1)(n m^4+m^2)+1]. Query all U and skip POLEs. Build full H on
the remaining <=M points, find rank/basis, then only n r^2 K entries.
DETERMINISTIC QUERY BOUND:
 M+(M^2+n m^2) C_m=O(n^2 m^14+n m^4).
Field arithmetic is polynomial, e.g. O((M^2+n m^2)m^4+n(M^2+n m^2)m^2
+M^2m), plus construction of G values. Rank <=m permits rank-factorization
of H in O(M^2m), rather than a full O(M^3) solve. Expanded exponents are
deliberately loose. This deterministic learner acquires the whole minimal
realization; it is stronger than merely testing scalar f=0 in this subclass.

8. IDENTIFIABILITY AND DOMAIN LIMITS
For a multiindex a, [x^a]f=(|a|!/a!)lambda A^a gamma. Commutation makes every
word with Parikh vector a have the same coefficient lambda A^a gamma.
Thus f determines all original nc word coefficients under the commuting
promise. It determines every minimal commuting realization up to similarity:
the map A^a gamma -> B^a alpha is well-defined by equality of all continuation
coefficients and observability, and intertwines transitions/output/input.
Nonminimal hidden blocks and their raw coordinates cannot be recovered;
arbitrary changes to unreachable/unobservable summands leave f unchanged.
If the hidden tuple is not promised commuting, scalar commutativization can
lose word order, and none of this restores it.

9. BIT COST / ROBUSTNESS
Above bounds are field arithmetic and exact scalar-query bounds. They do not
assume the oracle itself is cheap. Over Q, standard fraction-free linear
algebra makes bit cost polynomial in n,m and the maximum bit lengths of
queried points/answers. With rational hidden entries of bit bound H and the
displayed integer/rational sampling coordinates, answer/derived bit lengths
are bounded polynomially in n,m,H. Without a height/evaluation-bit promise,
there is no coefficient-independent polynomial bit guarantee. There is no
noise, conditioning, stability, positive-probability HMM, or local-resource
guarantee in the theorem.

10. INDEPENDENT FINITE CHECK AND NON-MONOGENIC COUNTEREXAMPLE
check_resolvent.py uses exact SymPy rational arithmetic, not numeric evidence.
It verifies (1), H, K, and realization reconstruction for K[x,y]/(x^2,y^2),
dimension four, gamma=1 and lambda extracting xy. Here f=2xy, H has rank four,
but every linear combination of the two multiplication matrices has cube
zero. A generic single-direction Krylov basis has size at most three and
would miss a state. The script also verifies the primitive identities in a
two-state diagonal example with true rational poles.
Observed output:
 PASS: exact non-monogenic 4-state commuting example; f=2*x*y; H rank 4
 PASS: exact rational 2-state diagonal example; all antiderivative identities
These checks support only those exact examples; the arguments above prove
the general claims. Script run: python3 .../commuting/check_resolvent.py.

11. PRIOR ART AND NOVELTY LIMIT
Finite-rank Hankel/Artinian-Gorenstein multiplication-matrix reconstruction
is established prior art. Relevant primary abstract inspected:
Bernard Mourrain, Fast algorithm for border bases of Artinian Gorenstein
algebras, https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01328 (2017). Also primary discovery:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05720, Polynomial-exponential decomposition from moments.
This run does not claim novelty of minimal realization/Hankel recovery.
Potential interface contribution is obtaining resolvent Hankel pairings
from scalar point evaluations using rational antiderivatives, then combining
with the new small-derivative-space hitter. A targeted prior-art search is
still needed before any novelty claim. Search also located the 2006 paper
Learning Restricted Models of Arithmetic Circuits, whose abstract says low
partial-derivative-space polynomials are learnable with membership and
equivalence queries; main primary URL failed (502) during this run.
