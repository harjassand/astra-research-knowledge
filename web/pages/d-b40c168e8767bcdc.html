## Exact local domination and cut cancellation

For even n pair columns into M=n/2 elementary blocks, m=2w qubits per block, d=2^m. Set

G=G_single^(tensor m), g_-=((1-t)/2)^m, g_+=((1+t)/2)^m,
q=g_+/g_-, G'=g_- I_d, Delta=G-G', a=tr G'=(1-t)^m, alpha=1-a, s=1-1/q.

For t>0, 0<a<1 and q>1. Spectral inequalities give G'<=G<=qG', and 0<=Delta<=sG. This version has **kappa=q**, not q², and **a=(1-t)^m**, not 1/q. At t=0 the state is maximally mixed and admits the immediate product decomposition.

Let E be the w CZ gates crossing the midpoint inside one elementary block. E²=I and every gate of U commutes with E. Hence UE is precisely U with these crossing gates removed; it factors across that entire column cut. If a central input vector is E|p> with |p> a product of single-qubit states, then its output has the cut. The scalar G' is invariant under E, so it has such a decomposition. This is an exact graph identity; it does not generalize to arbitrary circuit layers by approximation.

## Absorption and the universal positive budget

For ell elementary blocks define

Q0=G'_0 tensor G_1 tensor ... tensor G_(ell-1)+Delta_0 tensor ... tensor Delta_(ell-1),
Qb=Delta_<b tensor G'_b tensor G_>b, for 1<=b<ell.

Repeatedly using G=G'+Delta proves sum_b Qb=G^(tensor ell). Every term is positive. For b>=1 use explicit X-product spectral projectors for Delta/G and the central product decomposition of G'. Undo E on the central vectors; this leaves G' invariant and produces a physical cut after U.

For Q0, let |x_z> be the normalized X-product basis. Its G eigenvalues are e_z=product_j(1+z_j t)/2, and lambda_z=1-g_-/e_z lies in [0,s]. Thus G=sum_z e_z |x_z><x_z| and Delta=sum_z e_z lambda_z |x_z><x_z|. For a tuple of the other ell-1 labels put r=product lambda_z. The remaining central operator is G'+rDelta. Conjugating by E and dividing by g_- gives I+rK, with K=EGE/g_--I>=0 and ||K||<=q-1.

Write rK=sum_P c_P P, c_P=tr(P rK)/d. Hermiticity makes every coefficient real and |c_P|<=||rK||. The identity coefficient c_I>=0. Set R=sum_(P!=I)|c_P|. If R<1, the identity

I+rK=(1+c_I-R)I+sum_(P!=I)|c_P|(I+sign(c_P)P)

has nonnegative coefficients. Each I±P is a sum of twice the rank-one product projectors of a tensor Pauli eigenbasis with the corresponding eigenvalue. Identity factors can use the Z basis. Conjugate these central product projectors back by E. This gives an exact positive Q0 decomposition with a central physical cut.

An integer B>=ln q can be obtained as max(0,bitlength(num q)-bitlength(den q)+1). Indeed q<2^B for q>1 and ln2<1. Choose

L=1+ceil[q(4w+1+B)].

For ell>=L,

||rK|| <=(q-1)(1-1/q)^(ell-1) <=q exp[-(4w+1+B)] <=exp[-4w-1] <1/(2*4^m).

The last inequality is ln2+2w ln4<4w+1. Therefore R<(4^m-1)/(2*4^m)<1/2. This is a universal analytic bound, including residual tuples that finite random sampling almost never visits.

**Final sharpened stabilizer budget.** For the matching crossing graph, E X_A E=P_A is a tensor Pauli string. For one edge the four possibilities are II, XZ, ZX, YY, with positive sign in each case; disjoint edges multiply without additional signs. Consequently K=(1/a-1)I+sum_(A nonempty)(t^|A|/a)P_A, and its nonidentity coefficient sum is S=[(1+t)^m-1]/a. With c0=1/a-1, z=S-c0=[(1+t)^m+(1-t)^m-2]/a>=0 (binomial even terms), one has the exact decomposition

I+rK=(1-rz)I+sum_(A nonempty)(r t^|A|/a)(I+P_A).

Hence the minimum sufficient L for this decomposition satisfies z s^(L-1)<=1. The sharper closed L=1+ceil[q(1+B)] suffices because z<=q and z s^(L-1)<=q exp[-1-B]<=exp(-1). Central term trace weights simplify to a(1-rz) and r t^|A|; their sum equals a+alpha r since a z=(1+t)^m+a-2. Thus the final sampler selects a Pauli term with those exact rational weights, then a uniformly chosen product eigenvector of eigenvalue +1. This sharper proof preserves every geometry, LOCC, width-exponent and bit-cost conclusion above, and all sampled pure outputs are stabilizer states with Schmidt rank at most 2^w across a vertical cut. The adopted bound uses this smaller L throughout.

## Geometry, system boundaries, and width inversion

For M>=L take floor(M/L) groups and distribute the remaining fewer than L elementary blocks: every group has ell in [L,2L-1]. Each group supplies one deleted midpoint cut. Consecutive cuts lie in neighbouring groups, each occupying at most 4L-2 columns, so a very conservative maximum segment length is 8L columns. End segments are smaller. If M<L, use the usual known-circuit spectral mixture of the whole chain: n<2L already fits. For odd n append the remaining single column to the final segment, giving 8L+1 columns and (8L+1)w spins. There is no system-size padding/preparation requirement.

For t=tanh(beta J), q=exp(4 beta J w), B=O(1+beta J w). Thus the column bound is O(w(1+beta J) exp(4 beta J w)); the spin bound has an extra w. At fixed beta J>0, nonzero single-copy entanglement between end regions separated by R columns requires

R<=8L+1, hence w>=ln R/(4 beta J)-O_beta(log log R).

To justify the logarithmic remainder: if w>=ln R/(4 beta J) it is immediate; otherwise log w<=log log R+O_beta(1), and taking logs of the displayed upper bound gives the result. This is a necessary condition only, with fixed positive beta J. It is neither a sufficient entanglement protocol nor a zero-temperature statement. If end regions themselves span several columns, use their separation, not the total chain length.

## Exact rational sampler and charged cost

The term weights are tr Q0=a+alpha^ell and tr Qb=a alpha^b; their sum is one. Conditional on b=0, an other-block tuple has unnormalized weight

(a+alpha r) product e_z = a product e_z + alpha product(lambda_z e_z).

Thus draw between masses a and alpha^ell; in the first branch draw each label from e_z, and in the second from lambda_z e_z/alpha. Compute r and draw a central product component using its actual trace. Central identity components each have weight g_-(1+c_I-R); each selected eigenvector of I±P has weight 2g_-|c_P|. Their total is a+alpha r, so this conditional normalization is exact. There is no enumeration of d^(ell-1) tuples and no false physical-independence assumption. Qb with b>=1 uses the independent explicit eigenprojectors of its tensor factors.

For rational t all eigenweights, lambda values, Pauli coefficients, traces, and draw probabilities are rational. The product Pauli eigenvectors require only a fixed field Q(i,sqrt2), with compact tensor descriptions; no system-size-dependent eigensplitting field is needed. A local table has 2^{O(w)} entries, and a draw has O(wn*2^{O(w)}) arithmetic/output work. If N=wn and t has b bits, scalar bitlength is O(C(w)Nb); sums over fixed-width tables only change C(w). Conservative schoolbook arithmetic is O(C(w)N³(b+1)²). Exact discrete rational sampling can form a common integer denominator, draw a uniform integer below it by rejection from ceil(log2 denominator) bits (acceptance >1/2), and select a cumulative interval. This charges expected polynomial random-bit and integer work; no exact-real comparison oracle is supplied. L may be exponentially large in the bit representation, but if L>N/2 the small-system fallback is used. The procedure is an abstract implementable sampler; the independent code below is an exact fixture checker, not a complete implementation.

## Why random cuts and postselection do not evade LOCC

Let rho=sum_lambda p_lambda rho_lambda, where every pure component factors across some full-column cut separating the complete end regions A and C. The cut may depend on lambda. For a fine finite LOCC transcript omega, the accumulated Kraus operator factors over columns, K_omega=tensor_j K_(omega,j); hence it factors across every such cut. Conditional on lambda, K_omega rho_lambda K_omega† is a positive product across its particular cut. Tracing out all intermediate columns gives a positive product X_(lambda,omega)^A tensor Y_(lambda,omega)^C on the SAME end bipartition. Sum over lambda and any fine transcripts collected into a public outcome: the sum remains in the separable cone. Dividing by any nonzero outcome probability preserves separability. This proves each postselected end state separable, stronger than merely zero average entanglement. Initially separable ancillas can be included by refining their product mixture. Limits of LOCC protocols preserve the statement by closure of the finite-dimensional separable cone, for outcomes of nonzero probability.

The argument also permits operations jointly within A and within C, provided neither party crosses a separating cut. It forbids pre-shared entangled ancillas, quantum communication between columns, joint measurements of distinct intermediate columns, or multiple copies. Those are material interface restrictions. As an exact discriminator, Bell12 tensor Bell34 has pure product blocks of length two; a JOINT Bell measurement on columns 2 and 3 produces Bell14 with probability 1/4. This is outside the admitted LOCC interface. Likewise different copies can have complementary cuts; a single-copy proof cannot be reused for distillation. Random posterior weights alone do not create entanglement.
