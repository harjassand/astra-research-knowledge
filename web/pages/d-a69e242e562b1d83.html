N125 FINITE-BIT POSITIVE CORRECTION: RATIONAL FINITE-SUPPORT EXTRACTION
2026-10-08

STATUS

INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the finite scalar-kernel approximation and
its rational finite-support realization below. The N125 source card and
Round6 statements are SOURCE-REPORTED / source-derived and externally
unreviewed. This note independently derives the finite truncation bound,
coefficient conditioning, and rational quadrature error. It is not a proof
of a new separability theorem, a correction to the exact infinite series,
or a separability impossibility result. No novelty or priority claim.

1. Interface and statement

For integer N>=1 and k=0,...,N, set

  x_k=(k-N/2)^2,   A_i=(N/2+i)^2,   f_k=exp(-x_k/N).

Write B_{N,k}(p)=binom(N,k)p^k(1-p)^(N-k). Fix 0<epsilon<=1/2 and assume
epsilon is supplied as a dyadic rational (or another rational whose input
bit length L_epsilon is explicitly charged). Assume

  N >= 40 log(16/epsilon).                                  (1)

There is an explicit rational positive mixture with q=ceil((5N-1)/2)
atoms p_i in (0,1), weights mu_i>0, such that simultaneously for all k,

  |g_k-f_k| <= (epsilon/4) f_k,
  g_k = sum_(i=1)^q mu_i B_{N,k}(p_i).                       (2)

Consequently, after normalizing the positive weights to sum to one,
the resulting finite mixture of Bernoulli product laws has pointwise
relative error at most epsilon against f_k/sum_j f_j. (The same algebra is
the finite-support version of the beta coherent-product population identity
in N125; (2) alone is not a full operator-level separability theorem.)

The construction has m=2N Newton terms, q=Theta(N) rational support
points, polynomial output bit length and deterministic bit running time in
N+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon. For epsilon=2^(-s), the precision input costs
Theta(s)=Theta(log(1/epsilon)) bits. It uses only rational arithmetic in the emitted output:
there is no coefficient field extension and no continuous-support oracle.
The exact Gaussian nodes used in the proof are algebraic of degree at most
q; the implementation isolates and rounds them over Q instead of adjoining
them to a number field.

2. Analytic positive truncation

Let P_(m-1) be the degree-(at-most m-1) Newton interpolant to exp(-y/N) at
A_1,...,A_m. Its coefficient at order l is

  c_l=(-1)^l f[A_1,...,A_(l+1)]>0,

and its basis at x_k is

  B_l(k)=product_(r=1)^l(A_r-x_k)
        =product_(r=1)^l(r+k)(r+N-k)>0.

The coefficient sign follows from the divided-difference mean-value theorem
and f^(l)(y)=(-1/N)^l exp(-y/N). For independent E_r~Exp(lambda_(r,k)),

  lambda_(r,k)=A_r-x_k=(r+k)(r+N-k),  T_(m,k)=sum_(r=1)^m E_r,

the finite Lagrange formula gives the exact identity

  P_(m-1)(x_k)=f_k Pr(T_(m,k)>1/N).                          (3)

Indeed, after factoring f_k, the Lagrange sum is
sum_i exp(-lambda_(i,k)/N) product_(j!=i)lambda_(j,k)/
(lambda_(j,k)-lambda_(i,k)), the simple-pole formula for the
hypoexponential survival function. This is a finite-m identity; it does
not assert that the infinite positive series is exact.

Here is the all-k finite-tail estimate needed at m=2N. Couple E_r=U_r/lambda
with mean-one exponentials U_r. Since

  lambda_(r,k)=(r+N/2)^2-(k-N/2)^2 <= (r+N/2)^2,

T_(m,k) is stochastically no smaller than the centered comparison sum.
For s=N^2/4, Chernoff gives

  Pr(T_(m,k)<=1/N)
   <= exp{N/4 - sum_(r=1)^m log(1+N^2/[4(r+N/2)^2])}.         (4)

Put h(u)=log(1+1/[4(u+1/2)^2]). It is positive decreasing, and the
left-Riemann-sum estimate gives

  sum_(r>=1) h(r/N) >= N integral_0^infinity h(u)du-h(0),
  h(0)=log 2,  integral h=pi/4-(log 2)/2.

The omitted tail after m obeys

  sum_(r>m) h(r/N) <= (N^2/4)sum_(r>m)(r+N/2)^(-2)
                   <= N^2/[4(m+N/2)].

Therefore, with c=pi/4-(log 2)/2-1/4,

  Pr(T_(m,k)<=1/N) <= 2 exp(-cN+N^2/[4(m+N/2)]).             (5)

At m=2N the exponent is at most -N/40: the tail term is N/10, and
c>1/8 follows from pi>3 and log 2<3/4. Thus

  0 <= f_k-P_(2N-1)(x_k) <= 2 exp(-N/40) f_k.               (6)

The infinite N125 defect is not removed. Equation (6) says only that in
the regime (1), its finite partial sum already has adequate relative
accuracy.

3. Rational coefficient acquisition and conditioning

The Lagrange expression for c_l is a signed sum of l+1 exponentials:

  c_l = sum_(i=1)^(l+1) s_(l,i) exp(-A_i/N),
  s_(l,i)=(-1)^l / product_(j!=i) (A_i-A_j).

All s_(l,i) are rational. Since
|A_i-A_j|=|i-j|(N+i+j),

  S_l^0:=sum_i |s_(l,i)|
       <= 2^l/[(N+2)^l l!].

The Hermite-Genocchi formula gives

  c_l=N^(-l) integral_(Delta_l) exp(-sum_i t_i A_i/N) dt
      >= N^(-l) exp(-A_(l+1)/N)/l!,

where Delta_l has volume 1/l!. Hence, for l<2N,

  S_l^0/c_l <= (2N/(N+2))^l exp(A_(l+1)/N)
            <= exp(2N log 2+25N/4) < exp(8N).                (7)

This is the cancellation/conditioning charge: direct signed evaluation has
condition at most exp(8N), so it needs O(N) guard bits in addition to the
requested relative precision. The rational algorithm below spends 16N
binary guard bits, a safe margin because 16 log 2>8.

Set eta=epsilon/16 and the rational target rho=eta 2^(-16N). Approximate
each of the 2N values exp(-A_i/N) by a rational q_i with absolute error
<=rho. Since 2^(-16N)<=exp(-8N), the coefficient error obeys

  c_tilde_l=sum_i s_(l,i)q_i
  |c_tilde_l-c_l| <= rho S_l^0
                   <= eta exp[(8-16 log 2)N] c_l <= eta c_l,

so every c_tilde_l is strictly positive.
All exponentials can be evaluated with one rational range-reduction/Taylor
scheme. Uniformly, u_i=A_i/N<=25N/4. Choose a power of two K with
25N/4<=K<13N,
and approximate exp(-u_i/K) by the degree-J alternating Taylor polynomial
S_J(v)=sum_(j=0)^J (-v)^j/j!, v=u_i/K in [0,1]. Take

  J=ceil(log_2(12K/rho))+2.

Since the alternating remainder is at most 1/(J+1)!<=2^(-J)<=rho/(48K),
raising S_J(v) to the K-th power has absolute error <rho (use
|u^K-v^K|<=K|u-v|max(|u|,|v|)^(K-1) and (1+rho/(48K))^K<2).
Each q_i is therefore rational and can be produced in polynomial time.
Using the same K,J for
all i gives a common exponential denominator. Its bit length is

  B_exp = O(KJ(log(NK)+log J))
        = O(N(N+log(1/epsilon))log(N+log(1/epsilon))).

The rational denominators product_(j!=i)(A_i-A_j) have O(N log N) bits;
a common denominator for a coefficient is their product over i, of
O(N^2 log N) bits. Thus each c_tilde_l has

  B_c = O(B_exp+N^2 log N)

bits under unreduced rational arithmetic, and all 2N coefficients are
computed with O(N^2) rational additions/multiplications plus 2N
exponential evaluations. With grade-school arithmetic, a conservative
coefficient phase cost is O(N^2 B_c^2 + N(J+log N)B_exp^2) bit operations.
The positive Beta-kernel normalizers (N+1)(N+2)_(2l) and
Beta(l+1,l+1)=l!^2/(2l+1)! add O(N log N) bits per l.

Define the rational nonnegative polynomial

  w(p)=sum_(l=0)^(2N-1) c_tilde_l (N+1)(N+2)_(2l)[p(1-p)]^l.

The N125 beta identity yields

  P_tilde(k):=sum_l c_tilde_l B_l(k)
             = integral_0^1 B_(N,k)(p) w(p) dp.              (8)

By coefficientwise relative error and positivity, |~P(k)-P(k)|<=eta P(k)
<=eta f_k. The integral in (8) is the continuous finite-beta-component
representation; Section 4 turns it into a finite rational support ensemble.

4. Finite positive support with rational atoms

The integrand G_k(p)=B_(N,k)(p)w(p) is a polynomial of degree at most
d=N+4N-2=5N-2. Let q=ceil((d+1)/2)=ceil((5N-1)/2), and take the q-point
Gauss-Legendre rule on [0,1], with exact nodes p_i in (0,1) and weights
omega_i>0 summing to one. It integrates every polynomial of degree at most
2q-1 exactly, so

  P_tilde(k)=sum_(i=1)^q omega_i G_k(p_i).                   (9)

The output will use rational approximations p_tilde_i and omega_tilde_i.
Let d_w=4N-2 and F=2^(-ceil(N/2)); then F<=exp(-N/4)<=f_k. First,

  M:=integral_0^1 w(p)dp=sum_k P_tilde(k)<=2(N+1),

because P_tilde(k)<=(1+eta)f_k<=2. Markov's inequality for degree-d_w
polynomials on [0,1] gives ||w'||_infinity<=2d_w^2||w||_infinity.
At a point of maximum, w therefore stays at least half its maximum on a
one-sided interval of length 1/(4d_w^2), and

  W:=||w||_infinity <= 8d_w^2 M <=16d_w^2(N+1).

Also ||B_(N,k)||_infinity<=1 and Markov gives
||B'_(N,k)||_infinity<=2N^2. Thus

  ||G_k'||_infinity <= L:=2(N^2+d_w^2)W.                   (10)

The shifted Legendre nodes are roots x_i=2p_i-1 of P_q(x). The shifted
Gauss weight at a root is

  omega_i=1/[(1-x_i^2)P'_q(x_i)^2].                          (11)

The weights sum to one, so omega_i<=1 and (11) implies |P'_q(x_i)|>=1.
Markov gives ||P'_q||<=q^2 and ||P''_q||<=q^4. Since P_q(+-1)=(+-1)^q,
each root is at least q^(-2) from either endpoint. Consequently, for
|x_tilde_i-x_i|<=1/(2q^4), the rational value

  omega_tilde_i=1/[(1-x_tilde_i^2)P'_q(x_tilde_i)^2]

is positive. On this interval, 1-x_tilde_i^2>=1/(4q^4),
|P'_q(x_tilde_i)|>=1/2, and differentiating (11) gives the conservative
condition bound |d omega(x)/dx|<=256q^8. Hence

  |omega_tilde_i-omega_i|<=256q^8 |x_tilde_i-x_i|.

Choose a rational root approximation with error at most

  delta_x=min{1/(2q^4), epsilon F/(8192 q^9 W), epsilon F/(16L)}.

Then |p_tilde_i-p_i|<=delta_x/2 and
|omega_tilde_i-omega_i|<=epsilon F/(32qW). For
g_k^rat=sum_i omega_tilde_i G_k(p_tilde_i), (10) gives

  |g_k^rat-P_tilde(k)|
   <= q[epsilon F/(32qW)]W + [epsilon F/(32L)]L
   <= epsilon F/16 <= epsilon f_k/16.                       (12)

Every term is positive rational: p_tilde_i is strictly between zero and
one, omega_tilde_i>0, and w(p_tilde_i)>0 because c_tilde_0>0. Setting
mu_i=omega_tilde_i w(p_tilde_i) proves the positive-mixture claim (2).

5. Finite-bit quadrature implementation and resource ledger

Use L_q(x)=2^q P_q(x), an integer polynomial of coefficient height at most
8^q. Its roots are simple and real. Its nonzero integer discriminant and
|x_i-x_j|<=2 imply a root-separation bound

  min_(i!=j)|x_i-x_j| >= 2^(-4q^2).                         (13)

Indeed, if delta is the minimum gap and a_q is the leading coefficient,
1<=|Disc(L_q)|=|a_q|^(2q-2) product_(i<j)|x_i-x_j|^2
<= (4^q)^(2q-2) delta^2 4^(q(q-1)/2-1), which yields (13).

Sturm isolation with subresultant coefficients therefore isolates all q
roots and refines them to delta_x using

B_root=O(q^2+log(1/delta_x))
       =O(N^2+N+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon)

binary places. The subresultant Sturm coefficients have O(q^2) bits by
Hadamard bounds on their minors. A conservative schoolbook bit cost is
O(q^3 B_root (q B_root+q^2)^2) for root isolation, plus polynomial
subresultant construction. Evaluating (11) at rational isolating midpoints,
and evaluating w there, is polynomial exact rational arithmetic. The
reported atoms and weights are rational; no exact algebraic-number
arithmetic is used. Put B_mu=O(N B_c+N^2 B_root+q B_root+q^2), a conservative
bound for each unnormalized support weight. Horner evaluation of w at all
q rational atoms costs O(qN B_mu^2) bit operations with schoolbook
arithmetic. The full list of atoms and unnormalized weights has O(q B_mu)
bits; normalized rational weights have O(q^2 B_mu) total bits under a
product-denominator bound. Their exact normalization costs at most
O(q^2 B_mu^2) bit operations. If exact Gaussian
nodes were retained instead, each
node and weight would lie in a degree-at-most-q real algebraic extension,
with a common splitting field of degree at most q!; the rational rounding
above avoids this extension cost.

For fixed s>=0 and epsilon>=N^(-s), condition (1) holds for all sufficiently
large N, m=2N and q=Theta(N). Coefficient bits B_c are
O(N^2(N+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon)log(N+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon)+N^2logN),
hence polynomial; the root precision is O(N^2+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon); all q output weights and
atom coordinates have polynomial total bits. Coefficient generation,
Sturm root isolation, exact rational evaluation, output, and optional
normalization therefore all have polynomial bit time. The proof itself
does not assume an AP-time bound or claim one for a downstream observable.

Normalization is well-conditioned at the level of this nonnegative kernel.
If |g_k^rat-f_k|<=eta_0 f_k with eta_0=epsilon/4, then for
Z=sum_k f_k and Z_tilde=sum_k g_k^rat, Z_tilde/Z is in
[1-eta_0,1+eta_0]. The normalized pointwise relative error is at most
2eta_0/(1-eta_0)<=epsilon for 0<epsilon<=1/2. Z_tilde and the normalized
atom weights are positive rationals whose bit lengths are polynomial.

6. What this does and does not resolve

Resolved here: the N125 strict infinite defect does not prevent a linear-
degree positive truncation from meeting inverse-polynomial relative error
at a=1/N; furthermore, the beta integral can be extracted as an explicit
finite positive rational-support mixture with all approximation, root
conditioning, coefficient-bit, field, and runtime costs charged. This is a
finite-precision constructive result at one fixed scale.

Not resolved: exact Newton representation of exp(-a x); a relative-error
bound for all a or for a=delta/N with arbitrary delta; a larger SEP radius;
an operator-level separability theorem beyond the source's exact beta-kernel
interface; a polynomial-time downstream algorithm whose input is this
mixture; or any general separability impossibility. N117's contour/Legendre
proof is a separate source-reported route and is not contradicted or
reproved here.

Comparison with outputs/research/algebra_complexity/cycle3/lead_newton_bound/
RESULT.txt: the sibling note already proves the m=2N analytic estimate and
polynomial-time rational Newton-coefficient approximation. This result adds
the missing finite-support extraction of the beta measure using q=Theta(N)
Gauss points, a rational-root/weight implementation, explicit conditioning,
and a stable normalization bound. It does not improve the analytic exponent
or claim a stronger application result.
