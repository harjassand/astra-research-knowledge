# c01_l06 — initial quantifier and annealing audit

Checkpoint date: 2026-10-07. Internal derivation; not external review.

## Strongest exact claim

The determinant-parity project needs the following local statement, with its quantifiers read literally. For each annealing parameter t, let Ω be the common positive support of microscopic full-basis configurations, let π_t be their normalized determinant-squared law, and let K_t be a reversible two-replica update with stationary law π_t⊗π_t. Its Dirichlet form is

  E_t(Φ) = (1/2) E_{(X,Y)~π_t², (X',Y')~K_t((X,Y),·)}
                    [(Φ(X,Y)-Φ(X',Y'))²].

The proposed gate is

  Var_{π_t}(a) <= L E_t((X,Y) -> a(X)+b(Y))

for **every** real-valued a,b on Ω, with one polynomial L uniform over every positive activity point on the path. There is no centering, boundedness, low-degree, determinant-polynomial, or common-core restriction on a or b. Additive constants are immaterial. The inequality quantifies over all pairs of single-coordinate functions; it does not quantify over every H:Ω²→R.

This local statement, by itself, is not a Poincare inequality for K_t on Ω² and does not imply an annealing algorithm. A precise conditional sufficiency result does hold: if the local inequality is uniform along a shared-support path, each adjacent pair of laws is pointwise comparable, every pair update is reversible and computable, and there is an exact base refresh, then the replicated product construction gives a polynomial full-product gap. This is the correct local-to-global bridge. It is the product/ANOVA argument, not the local inequality alone, that controls arbitrary functions of all slots.

## Conditional local-to-global derivation

Assume Ω is finite, π_0,…,π_T have common support, and for every t≥1 the equal-law local gate holds with constant L≥1. Suppose the pair update has the same symmetric proposal at targets π_t⊗π_t and π_t⊗π_{t-1}, with Metropolis acceptance, and

  1/2 <= π_{t-1}(z)/π_t(z) <= 2  for every z in Ω.

The Metropolis capacity identity gives, for every pair transition s→s',

  Q(s,s') min{(π_t⊗π_{t-1})(s),(π_t⊗π_{t-1})(s')}
    >= (1/2) Q(s,s') min{(π_t⊗π_t)(s),(π_t⊗π_t)(s')}.

Thus the unequal-tier pair energy dominates half the equal-tier energy, so for every a,b,

  Var_{π_t} a <= 2L E_{t,t-1}(a(x)+b(y)).                 (1)

Now put q=32L replicas at every tier, order slots from higher to lower tier, connect each slot x at tier t to all q guides y at t−1, and refresh every base slot independently from π_0. The product target is

  ν = product_{t=0}^T π_t^{⊗q}.

For a global function f, let U_x be the slots preceding x and define

  h_x = E[f|U_x,x] - E[f|U_x],
  k_xy = E[f|U_x,y] - E[f|U_x],
  g_xy = E[f|U_x,x,y],
  r_xy = g_xy - E[f|U_x] - h_x - k_xy.

Conditioning on U_x and applying (1) to the arbitrary functions h_x(x) and k_xy(y) yields

  ||h_x||² <= 2L E_xy(h_x+k_xy).

Conditional expectation contracts the pair Dirichlet form. Since g_xy−E[f|U_x]=h_x+k_xy+r_xy, the square inequality and E_xy(r_xy)<=2||r_xy||² give

  ||h_x||² <= 4L E_xy(f) + 8L ||r_xy||².                (2)

For product ν, the orthogonal ANOVA decomposition f=Σ_S f_S gives the exact identity

  r_xy = Σ_{S⊆U_x∪{x,y}, x,y∈S} f_S.

Every such S has x,y as its last two coordinates in the fixed order. Those coordinates uniquely identify the pair, so no nonconstant ANOVA component occurs in two residuals; hence Σ_xy ||r_xy||² <= Var_ν f. Average (2) over q guides per nonbase slot, sum the martingale increments, and include the q base refreshes. This gives

  Var_ν f <= (4L/q)Σ_xy E_xy(f) + (8L/q)Var_ν f + Σ_base E_i(f).

For q=32L, absorb the residual term to obtain

  Var_ν f <= (1/6)Σ_xy E_xy(f) + (4/3)Σ_base E_i(f)
            <= (4/3)(Σ_xy E_xy(f)+Σ_base E_i(f)).        (3)

There are M=Tq²+q updates. The lazy uniform mixture has energy equal to the sum in (3) divided by 2M, so its full-product spectral gap is at least 3/(8M). This is a Poincare inequality for every f on the entire replicated product, not only additive f.

If Λ=log(1/min_z ν(z)), reversibility and laziness give from any point z

  ||P^s(z,·)-ν||_TV <= (1/2) exp(-3s/(8M)+Λ/2).

Thus polynomial M, Λ, and per-step bit cost give polynomial mixing. For rational determinant weights, Λ must still be bounded from the actual input encoding; nonzero rational-complex determinants have polynomial bit height, and a count of at most 2^m bases gives a polynomial logarithmic lower-mass bound when the activity bits are polynomial. The microscopic kernel and all its transition probabilities must also be computable in polynomial bit time.

For ratio estimation, write R_t(z)=w_t(z)/w_{t-1}(z). Then E_{π_{t-1}}R_t=Z_t/Z_{t-1} and R_t/E R_t=π_t/π_{t-1}. If the pointwise adjacent-density ratio is at most 2, the relative variance of this importance weight is at most 1. With polynomially many tiers and fresh, sufficiently mixed samples, the usual product of ratio estimates has polynomial sample cost. The adjacent-density bound, same support, local kernels, exact or controlled base sampling, finite-bit simulation, and accumulated failure budget are separate hypotheses; none follows from the local energy inequality. An approximate base refresh is not automatically a stationary update for ν; its coupling or total-variation error must be budgeted separately.

For a rank-d determinant-basis system, each basis selects d vectors. If each line activity changes by a factor at most c between tiers, an unnormalized basis weight changes by at most c^d and the normalized law by at most c^(2d). Choosing c=1+O(1/d) keeps adjacent normalized densities within a constant. This is a path design requirement, not a consequence of spectral control.

## Exact counterexamples to over-reading the local gate

### Additive control does not mean a full pair gap

Take Ω={0,1} with uniform law on each slot. Let K resample (X,Y) uniformly conditional on X xor Y, so it only mixes the two states in each parity class. Every one-coordinate real function has form a0+a1 x, and every b has form b0+b1 y. Directly conditioning on parity gives

  E_K(a(X)+b(Y)) = (a1²+b1²)/4,
  Var(a(X)) = a1²/4.

Therefore the all-a,b additive inequality holds with L=1. But φ(X,Y)=(-1)^(X xor Y) has variance 1 and zero K-energy. K has full-pair spectral gap zero. A local additive statement must not be reported as a full gap on Ω²; the replicated product proof uses base refreshes and ANOVA to handle these pair interactions.

### Local gaps do not provide annealing overlap

For ε=2^-m, set π_0=(1−ε,ε) and π_1=(ε,1−ε) on {0,1}. At either endpoint, let each pair update independently resample both coordinates from π_i. Its energy on a+b is Var_{π_i}a+Var_{π_i}b, so the all-functions local inequality holds with L=1 (and each pair chain has gap 1). Yet for a direct importance step from π_0 to π_1,

  E_{π_0}[π_1/π_0]=1,
  Var_{π_0}(π_1/π_0) = (1−2ε)^2/[ε(1−ε)] = Θ(2^m).

For m=12 this relative variance is exactly 16760836/4095 ≈ 4093.00024, and max_z π_1(z)/π_0(z)=4095. Thus even an excellent local gap at both endpoints does not give a useful direct annealing ratio estimate. A sufficiently fine overlap-controlled path is a separate requirement.

Exact rational code at `work/cycle6/c01_l06/quantifier_audit.py` checks 625 additive-function assignments for the parity kernel, exhibits the exact zero-energy pair invariant, checks 1,250 endpoint assignments for the heatbath local bound, and computes the m=12 importance variance. The finite checks corroborate the algebra above; they do not prove an assertion for determinant kernels.

## Determinant interface and unresolved gate

For the target parity problem, Ω must be the supported microscopic full-basis line sets A, with point weight

  w_t(A) = (product_{ell in A} activity_{ell,t}) |det V_A|²,
  π_t(A) = w_t(A)/Z_t.

The proposed local gate must range over all real functions on this Ω and remain uniform at every positive activity point used by annealing. It must hold for the actual computable two-replica kernel on Ω², not for the aggregate coefficient law, a Markov chain on hole sets, a low-degree observable family, a finite enumeration, or a kernel that calls Z_t. The common support must be stable through the path. Exact determinant point weights and acceptance ratios are polynomial-time computable for rational-complex input, but this does not supply a local energy inequality or an aggregate partition oracle.

The exact missing item remains: construct a reversible two-replica kernel with transitions and Metropolis ratios computable from rational input without a coefficient/partition oracle, then prove the uniform all-a,b inequality with polynomial L over the entire positive annealing path. The cycle-5 failures of positive pair-overlay and common-cut sewing exclude specified constructions only; they do not imply this arbitrary kernel inequality is false. The present checkpoint proves a conditional annealing implication and pinpoints two independent hypotheses that local energy alone cannot replace. No determinant sampler, FPRAS, or field-breaking result is claimed.
