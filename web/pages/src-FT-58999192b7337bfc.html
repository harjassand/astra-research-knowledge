# Acoustic-face exclusion of every positive isotropic transfer

Status: proved below, with exact algebra replay; internal research result, not external review. Originality: UNKNOWN.

This is a separate continuation after the frozen scalar-minimizer countermodel. It does not alter that result. All coefficient closures below are in the finite-dimensional space of homogeneous real quartic polynomials on real 2-by-2 matrices.

## 1. Statement and prior-result boundary

For a matrix \(A=(X\mid Y)=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\), write
\[
u=|X|^2,\quad v=|Y|^2,\quad w=X\cdot Y,\quad q=\det A,
\qquad uv=w^2+q^2.
\]
The target is
\[
G(A)=3(u+v-2q)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+q^2-\frac52qu.
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(\mathcal P\) be the cone of all homogeneous quartic polyconvex polynomials on this matrix space, with no symmetry imposed. Let \(\mathcal I\) be the cone of all rank-one convex homogeneous quartics invariant under both left and right \(SO(2)\):
\[
F_0(A)=\alpha |A|^4+\beta_0 q|A|^2+\gamma_0q^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Write
\[
F_*(A)=u^2+v^2+4w^2+\frac72q^2-\frac{29}{10}q(u+v).
\tag{3}
\]
Every sum defining a conic hull is finite, but its length is not uniformly bounded.

**Theorem.** Define
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal C_{\rm iso}
&=\overline{\operatorname{cone}
 \{A\mapsto F_0(AS):F_0\in\mathcal I,\ S\in GL_2(\mathbb R)\}
 +\mathcal P},\\
\mathcal C_{\rm mixed}
&=\overline{\operatorname{cone}
 \bigl(\{F_0(\,\cdot\,S):F_0\in\mathcal I,\ S\in GL_2(\mathbb R)\}
 \cup\{F_*(\,\cdot\,S):S\in GL_2(\mathbb R)\}\bigr)
 +\mathcal P}.
\end{split}
\tag{4}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{G\notin\mathcal C_{\rm mixed},\qquad
G\notin\mathcal C_{\rm iso}.}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, no positive combination of input-linear transforms of the planar Dacorogna--Marcellini family, supplemented by arbitrary homogeneous polyconvex quartics, gives \(G\), even in coefficient closure. The statement also excludes mixing those isotropic generators with the specified known \(F_*\) orbit. It allows singular-transform limits, isotropic generators on the acoustic boundary, and polyconvex addends with no rotational symmetry.

The pinned repository already proves the separate exclusion
\[
G\notin\overline{\operatorname{cone}\{F_*(\,\cdot\,S):S\in GL_2\}+\mathcal P}
\tag{6}
\]
in updates/FO/synthesis/profile_prior/cone_followup/REPORT.md. Its acoustic-zero and lifted-Jensen mechanism is used explicitly here. Equation (6) is prior repository capital, not a new result of this continuation. The new step is the classification of the acoustic-zero face of the entire right-transformed isotropic family and the compact normalization allowing it to be mixed with (3).

Bruno and Pasqualotto, Theorem 2 of [arXiv:2608.06367v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1), prove quasiconvexity for every rank-one convex quartic with the two rotational symmetries and give representation (2). This continuation does not reprove or extend that quasiconvexity theorem. It tests positive transfer from its full generator class to this anisotropic target. The exclusion proof itself uses only rank-one convexity of (2), not that external quasiconvexity theorem.

## 2. A compact acoustic normalization

Every left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant homogeneous quartic has the unique form
\[
p=A_0u^2+B_0v^2+C_0uv+E_0w^2
 +G_0uw+H_0vw+L_0uq+M_0vq+N_0wq.
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, identify the columns with complex numbers. A monomial invariant under simultaneous multiplication by \(e^{i\theta}\) contains two complex variables and two conjugates. Such monomials are quadratic products of \(u,v,w,q\). Their one quadratic relation is \(uv=w^2+q^2\); eliminating \(q^2\) gives (7), whose nine polynomials are linearly independent.

Let \(\mathcal R\) denote the rank-one convex cone in this nine-dimensional space. Put \(x=(a,b,c,d)^T\). The quadratic forms
\[
D^2p(A)[e_1\otimes e_1,e_1\otimes e_1]=x^TM_{11}x,\quad
D^2p(A)[e_1\otimes e_2,e_1\otimes e_2]=x^TM_{22}x
\]
have matrices
\[
M_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}
12A_0&3G_0&0&3L_0\\
3G_0&2C_0+2E_0&-L_0&N_0\\
0&-L_0&4A_0&G_0\\
3L_0&N_0&G_0&2C_0
\end{pmatrix},
\quad
M_{22}=\begin{pmatrix}
2C_0+2E_0&3H_0&-N_0&M_0\\
3H_0&12B_0&-3M_0&0\\
-N_0&-3M_0&2C_0&H_0\\
M_0&0&H_0&4B_0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{8}
\]
Only these necessary acoustic conditions are needed in the compactness argument; no SOS converse is assumed there.

Define the linear functional
\[
\Lambda(p)=\operatorname{tr}M_{11}+\operatorname{tr}M_{22}
=16(A_0+B_0)+8C_0+4E_0.
\tag{9}
\]
If \(p\in\mathcal R\), both matrices in (8) are positive semidefinite, so \(\Lambda(p)\ge0\). Moreover \(\Lambda(p)=0\) implies that both matrices vanish. They jointly determine all nine coefficients:
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_0=(M_{11})_{11}/12,\quad B_0=(M_{22})_{22}/12,\quad
C_0=(M_{11})_{44}/2,\\
E_0=(M_{11})_{22}/2-C_0,\quad
G_0=(M_{11})_{12}/3,\quad H_0=(M_{22})_{12}/3,\\
L_0=(M_{11})_{14}/3,\quad M_0=(M_{22})_{14},\quad
N_0=(M_{11})_{24}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\Lambda\) is strictly positive on \(\mathcal R\setminus\{0\}\).

The section
\[
\{p\in\mathcal R:\Lambda(p)=1\}
\tag{11}
\]
is compact. Indeed, the entries of either positive semidefinite matrix are bounded by its trace, which is at most one; (10) bounds every coefficient. The rank-one convex cone is closed because it is the intersection of its closed pointwise Legendre--Hadamard halfspaces. This proves closedness and boundedness of (11).

The target coefficients in basis (7) are
\[
(A_0,B_0,C_0,E_0,G_0,H_0,L_0,M_0,N_0)
=\left(\frac{107}{24},3,19,-13,0,0,-\frac{29}{2},-12,0\right),
\quad \Lambda(G)=\frac{658}{3}.
\tag{12}
\]

## 3. Compact parametrization of the isotropic orbit

Rank-one convexity of (2) implies \(\alpha\ge0\), by restriction to a line through a nonzero rank-one matrix. The two acoustic tests at \(I\) and \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), in direction \(e_1\otimes e_2\), give
\[
8\alpha+2\beta_0\ge0,\qquad 8\alpha-2\beta_0\ge0.
\]
Consequently
\[
|\beta_0|\le4\alpha.
\tag{13}
\]
If \(\alpha=0\), this forces \(\beta_0=0\), and the acoustic test at \(\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) in direction \(e_1\otimes e_1\) forces \(\gamma_0\ge0\). Such an atom is a nonnegative determinant square and is polyconvex.

If \(\alpha>0\), put
\[
U=\sqrt{\alpha}\,SS^T,\quad
\beta=\frac{\beta_0\det S}{\sqrt{\alpha}},\quad
\gamma=\gamma_0(\det S)^2.
\]
Then
\[
F_0(AS)=h_{U,\beta,\gamma}(A)
:=\sigma_U(A)^2+\beta q\,\sigma_U(A)+\gamma q^2,
\qquad \sigma_U=U_{11}u+2U_{12}w+U_{22}v,
\tag{14}
\]
with
\[
U\succeq0,\qquad |\beta|\le4\sqrt{\det U}.
\tag{15}
\]
The formula remains meaningful for singular \(U\).

Let \(\mathcal D\) be the enlarged family of all (14) satisfying (15) and rank-one convexity, with arbitrary real \(\gamma\) subject to that last condition. This family contains every atom in the first generator set of (4), including pure nonnegative \(q^2\). Enlarging the family makes the exclusion stronger.

The normalized family
\[
K_{\rm iso}=\{h\in\mathcal D:\Lambda(h)=1\}
\tag{16}
\]
is compact. Here is the additional closedness argument, needed because an arbitrary subfamily of (11) need not be closed. In basis (7),
\[
\begin{gathered}
A_0=U_{11}^2,\quad B_0=U_{22}^2,\quad
C_0=2U_{11}U_{22}+\gamma,\quad E_0=4U_{12}^2-\gamma,\\
G_0=4U_{11}U_{12},\quad H_0=4U_{12}U_{22},\quad
L_0=\beta U_{11},\quad M_0=\beta U_{22},\quad N_0=2\beta U_{12}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{17}
\]
Bounded normalized coefficients bound \(U_{11},U_{22}\), hence \(U_{12}\) by positivity of \(U\), and then \(\beta\) by (15) and \(\gamma\) by the formula for \(C_0\). Every sequence of parameters therefore has a convergent subsequence. Positivity, (15), normalization and rank-one convexity all pass to the limit. Equation (17) proves closedness of (16).

## 4. Every isotropic atom on the target acoustic face is polyconvex

Use the nonnegative acoustic functional
\[
\ell(p)=D^2p(I)[e_1\otimes e_2,e_1\otimes e_2].
\tag{18}
\]
For \(I+t(e_1\otimes e_2)\), the determinant is one and
\[
\sigma_U=\operatorname{tr}U+2tU_{12}+t^2U_{22}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\ell(h_{U,\beta,\gamma})
=8U_{12}^2+(4\operatorname{tr}U+2\beta)U_{22}.
\tag{19}
\]
The two displayed terms are nonnegative: (15) and the two-dimensional arithmetic--geometric mean inequality give
\[
4\operatorname{tr}U+2\beta
\ge4\operatorname{tr}U-8\sqrt{\det U}\ge0.
\]

Suppose \(\ell(h)=0\). There are exactly two cases.

1. If \(U_{22}=0\), positivity forces \(U_{12}=0\), and (15) forces \(\beta=0\). Thus
   \[
   h=U_{11}^2u^2+\gamma q^2.
   \]
   At \(A=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\), in direction \(e_1\otimes e_1\), its acoustic second derivative is \(2\gamma\). Rank-one convexity gives \(\gamma\ge0\). The first term is convex and the second is polyconvex.

2. If \(U_{22}>0\), equality in (19) gives \(U_{12}=0\) and \(\beta=-2\operatorname{tr}U\). The bound (15) forces equality in arithmetic--geometric mean, so \(U=tI\), \(t>0\), and \(\beta=-4t\). Consequently
   \[
   h=t^2(|A|^2-2q)^2+(\gamma-4t^2)q^2.
   \tag{20}
   \]
   At \(A=I\), in direction \(e_1\otimes e_1\), its acoustic second derivative is \(2(\gamma-4t^2)\), so \(\gamma\ge4t^2\). Since
   \[
   |A|^2-2q=(a-d)^2+(b+c)^2,
   \]
   the first term in (20) is the fourth power of the norm of a linear map and is convex. The second is a nonnegative determinant square.

The case \(U=0\) is included in the first case. We have proved the exact face inclusion
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D\cap\{\ell=0\}\ \subseteq\ \mathcal P.}
\tag{21}
\]
This argument uses global rank-one convexity at the specified additional tests. The loose necessary bound (13) alone would not suffice to certify the signs of the determinant-square remainders.

## 5. Adding the old \(F_*\) orbit

The pinned N794 exact acoustic certificate gives
\[
D^2F_*(B)[a\otimes b,a\otimes b]
\ge \frac1{16}|B|^2|a|^2|b|^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Its rational acoustic Gram and all eight leading principal minors after subtracting \(I_8/16\) are independently replayed by the accompanying checker. Thus (22) is an exact global inequality, not a numerical eigenvalue observation.

Normalize a right transform to \(|S|_F=1\), absorbing its fourth power into the positive conic coefficient. The family \(S\mapsto F_*(AS)\) is compact, allowing singular \(S\). It contains no zero polynomial: if \(S\ne0\), choose rank-one \(A\) for which \(AS\ne0\); the rank-one value of \(F_*\) is strictly positive. By (9)--(11), its \(\Lambda\) is positive and has a positive minimum on the compact unit sphere of matrices. Hence
\[
K_*=\left\{\frac{F_*(\,\cdot\,S)}{\Lambda(F_*(\,\cdot\,S))}:|S|_F=1\right\}
\tag{23}
\]
is compact.

At \(B=I\), the chain rule and (22) imply
\[
\ell(F_*(\,\cdot\,S))\ge\frac1{16}|S|_F^2|S^Te_2|^2.
\tag{24}
\]
If an atom in (23) has \(\ell=0\), then \(S^Te_2=0\), so
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}s&t\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
F_*(AS)=(s^4+4s^2t^2+t^4)|Ae_1|^4.
\tag{25}
\]
This is convex. Thus the compact union
\[
K=K_{\rm iso}\cup K_*
\tag{26}
\]
consists of normalized rank-one convex atoms and has
\[
K\cap\{\ell=0\}\subseteq\mathcal P.
\tag{27}
\]

## 6. A finite lifted witness, not a gradient law

Direct substitution gives
\[
G(I+t(e_1\otimes e_2))=-\frac1{24}+3t^4,\quad
\ell(G)=0,\quad G(I)=-\frac1{24},\quad G(J)=\frac{1271}{24}.
\tag{28}
\]
Let \(\mu\) put mass \(1/(2\cdot1297)\) at each of \(\pm36I\), and mass \(1296/(2\cdot1297)\) at each of \(\pm J\). Then
\[
\mathbb E_\mu A=0,\quad
\mathbb E_\mu\det A=0,\quad
\mathcal J_\mu(G):=\mathbb E_\mu G(A)-G(0)=-\frac{1350}{1297}.
\tag{29}
\]
For every \(P\in\mathcal P\), convex Jensen applied to a polyconvex representation \(P(A)=\Phi(A,\det A)\) gives
\[
\mathcal J_\mu(P)\ge0.
\tag{30}
\]
This law is used only as a linear functional separating polyconvex atoms. No compatible gradient with this law is constructed or asserted.

## 7. Positive mass concentration proves coefficient-closed exclusion

First suppose all polyconvex addends are left-\(SO(2)\)-invariant. If \(G\in\mathcal C_{\rm mixed}\), normalizing every nonzero generator by \(\Lambda\) gives a coefficient-convergent sequence
\[
H_j=\sum_{i=1}^{m_j}c_{ji}k_{ji}+P_j\longrightarrow G,
\quad c_{ji}\ge0,\quad k_{ji}\in K,\quad P_j\in\mathcal P.
\tag{31}
\]
There is no bound on \(m_j\). The two acoustic functionals are nonnegative on every summand. Since \(\Lambda(k)=1\),
\[
\sum_i c_{ji}\le\Lambda(H_j)=O(1).
\tag{32}
\]
Since \(\ell(G)=0\),
\[
0\le\sum_i c_{ji}\ell(k_{ji})\le\ell(H_j)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{33}
\]

The continuous functional \(\mathcal J_\mu\) is nonnegative on the compact null set \(K\cap\{\ell=0\}\), by (27) and (30). For every \(\eta>0\), compactness gives \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
k\in K,\ \ell(k)<\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal J_\mu(k)\ge-\eta.
\tag{34}
\]
Otherwise a convergent subsequence would contradict nonnegativity on that null set. Let \(M\ge0\) bound the negative part of \(\mathcal J_\mu\) on \(K\), and let \(C\) bound (32). Splitting the sum at \(\ell(k)=\delta\) gives
\[
\sum_i c_{ji}\mathcal J_\mu(k_{ji})
\ge-\eta C-\frac{M}{\delta}\ell(H_j).
\tag{35}
\]
Take a lower limit in \(j\), then let \(\eta\downarrow0\). The lower limit is nonnegative. Equation (30) also gives \(\mathcal J_\mu(P_j)\ge0\). Thus (31) would imply
\[
\mathcal J_\mu(G)=\lim_j\mathcal J_\mu(H_j)\ge0,
\]
contradicting (29).

Finally allow arbitrary, nonsymmetric \(P_j\). Average (31) under left \(SO(2)\). Every generator and the target are unchanged, coefficient convergence is preserved, and the averaged polynomial remains homogeneous quartic and polyconvex: average the convex lifted representations \(\Phi(RA,q)\). Therefore the invariant case applies to that averaged sequence. This proves (5).

This argument avoids assuming the polyconvex cone is coefficient-closed, avoids a uniform acoustic lower bound on all isotropic generators, and accounts for every degenerate isotropic atom and singular right-transform limit.

## 8. Elementary obstruction to a single input transform

Even before adding positive terms, \(G\) cannot be one input transform of a nonzero isotropic rank-one convex quartic. On rank-one matrices \(A=e_1\otimes(s,t)\), the target is
\[
G(A)=\frac{107}{24}s^4+6s^2t^2+3t^4.
\tag{36}
\]
An isotropic right-transform has rank-one restriction \(\alpha |(s,t)S|^4\), the square of a nonnegative quadratic form. Nonzero odd coefficients vanish in (36), so its putative square root has zero mixed term (the \(s^4,t^4\) coefficients are both positive). It would then require
\[
6^2=4\left(\frac{107}{24}\right)3=\frac{107}{2},
\]
which is false. This all-\(S\) square obstruction is weaker than (5), which allows arbitrarily many positive terms, arbitrary polyconvex addends and coefficient limits.

## 9. What this settles and leaves open

The specified positive prior-energy synthesis cannot be a reason that \(G\) is quasiconvex. The theorem excludes a representation route, not quasiconvexity: \(G\) could have a new heat/closed-form certificate, a signed decomposition with a separately justified inequality, or another global compatible-gradient argument. In particular, it makes no assertion about a newly acquired anisotropic heat certificate.

The frozen scalar-minimizer countermodel proves that reduced-energy convexity fails even at an actual scalar minimizer with uniformly elliptic scalar blocks, while its explicit endpoints retain a positive affine Jensen gap. The present theorem explains why a positive transfer from all known isotropic quartic generators plus this \(F_*\) does not bypass that gap. The unresolved global obligation remains a valid proof of
\[
\inf_a\mathbb E\,G(D(a,v))\ge G(B)
\]
for every affine-periodic second component \(v\) and the corresponding affine matrix \(B\), or one exact compatible field with a strictly negative gap. No such general result or negative field is obtained here.
