# Rule 166: exact model reduction and finite-drive limits

## Interface and thermodynamic bookkeeping

Use the source model’s ring of (N) binary subunits, with local motif
\(p=4x_{i-1}+2x_i+x_{i+1}\). An active enzyme for motif (p) catalyses
\(p\to p\mathbin{\mathtt{xor}}2\) while consuming one fuel equivalent; its
reverse channel returns that equivalent. The source sets every subunit-state
energy equal, uses one common fuel/waste reservoir, and takes
\[
q=c\frac{e^A}{1+e^A},\qquad r=c\frac{1}{1+e^A},\qquad A=\beta\Delta\mu,
\]
so \(q/r=e^A\) and \(q+r=c\). Symmetric spontaneous flips have rate
\(s=k_{\rm spo}\) per site and consume no fuel in this equal-energy model.

The generator must preserve reaction channels until fuel rewards are recorded.
At a site in motif \(p\), the forward channel has rate \(q\) if enzyme \(p\)
is present; independently, the reverse channel has rate \(r\) if the enzyme
for \(p\mathbin{\mathtt{xor}}2\) is present. Spontaneous flips add another
channel of rate \(s\). A net state-flip rate alone loses the distinction
between a fuel-consuming forward reaction and fuel-returning reverse reaction;
in particular, complementary active enzymes can form futile chemical cycles.

For Rule 166, the Wolfram truth table makes the active substrate motifs
\(\{001,011,101,110\}\). Thus a zero flips up when its right neighbour is one
(motif 001 or 101), while a one flips down when its two neighbours differ
(011 or 110).

## Strong-drive single-error theorem

Assume odd \(N\), no spontaneous flips during the ensuing relaxation, and the
strong-drive limit \(r=0,q=c\). Let
\(M=(N+1)/2\), the first wrong-majority count. Starting from \(0^N\) with one
spontaneously seeded 1, all reachable configurations before the threshold are
one contiguous 1-run. If its length is \(k\), then:

- at \(k=1\), the sole enabled update extends the run, at rate \(c\);
- for \(2\le k<M\), one boundary update extends it at rate \(c\), and either
  endpoint can erode it, at aggregate rate \(2c\).

Write \(T_k\) for mean time to first reach length \(M\), with \(T_M=0\). The
backward equations are
\[
T_1=c^{-1}+T_2,\qquad
T_k=(3c)^{-1}+\tfrac13T_{k+1}+\tfrac23T_{k-1}\quad(2\le k<M).
\]
The recurrence has solution \(T_k=a+b2^k+k/c\). The two boundary conditions
give
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_1T_M=\frac{2^M-M-1}{c}}.
\]
The chain has no closed class below \(M\), so it hits wrong majority with
probability one. It is a slow, one-sided nucleation route, not a correcting
response. The delay is exponentially large in \(N\), which explains a long
relaxation without implying a robust two-valued memory.

Each catalytic jump in this reduction is a forward fuel-consuming event. If
\(J_k\) is the expected number of fuel equivalents hydrolysed before majority,
\[
J_M=0,\qquad J_1=1+J_2,\qquad
J_k=1+\tfrac13J_{k+1}+\tfrac23J_{k-1},
\]
which gives
\[
\boxed{J_1=2^{M+1}-3M-1}.
\]
Both elapsed time and fuel usage therefore grow exponentially with the
threshold in the ideal source model.

## Opposite one-bit error

Starting from \(1^N\) with one zero, let \(z\) be the length of its contiguous
zero run. Before either repair (\(z=0\)) or wrong majority (\(z=M\)), the run
expands at rate \(2c\) and contracts at rate \(c\). The gambler’s-ruin formula
for up/down ratio 2 gives
\[
\boxed{\Pr_1(T_M<T_0)=\frac{1}{2(1-2^{-M})}}.
\]
This tends to \(1/2\). Hence Rule 166 can eventually funnel every nonzero
state to \(1^N\) with splitting probability one and still have an isolated
error in \(1^N\) cross the majority-readout boundary with probability close to
one half before repair. Eventual attractor splitting and finite-time decoded
memory are different observables. The mean return time from one zero to \(1^N\)
is \((2^{N-1}-1)/c\), also exponential.

## Finite affinity and spontaneous leakage

At any finite \(A\), a \(000\) motif and its product partner \(010\) are both
enzyme-absent in Rule 166. Therefore \(0^N\) has no catalytic exit even when
reverse reactions are allowed. Its first-exit rate is exactly \(Ns\), so
\[
\Pr(\tau_{\rm majority}\le t)\le 1-e^{-Nst},\qquad
\mathbb E\tau_{\rm majority}\ge (Ns)^{-1}.
\]
For fixed finite \(N,A\), after a spontaneous seed the no-spontaneous chain
from any nonzero state reaches majority with finite mean; consequently
\(\lim_{s\downarrow0}s\mathbb E_{0^N}\tau_{\rm majority}=1/N\). This is a
fixed-\(N\) limit, not uniform in \(N\): at strong drive the post-seed term is
\((2^M-M-1)/c\), so a single-seed approximation needs approximately
\(Ns(2^M-M-1)/c\ll1\).

In \(1^N\), each 111 site can reverse enzyme 101’s reaction, so the exact
first-exit rate is \(N(r+s)\), and a visit’s mean residence time is
\[
\boxed{\frac{1}{N(r+s)}}.
\]
With \(s=0\), requiring this residence time to be at least \(\tau\) requires
\[
A\ge \log(Nc\tau-1)
\]
when \(Nc\tau>1\). For \(\tau=e^{\gamma N}/c\), this demands
\(A\ge\gamma N+\log N+o(1)\): the per-fuel chemical potential must grow
extensively with ring size to give the all-one state exponential visit
residence at fixed per-enzyme capacity. If \(s>0\) is fixed, the visit
residence is at most \(1/(Ns)\). These are exact-state dwell bounds; a visit
duration is not the full occupancy probability, which includes returns.

At finite \(A\), every nonzero state has a positive-rate path to \(1^N\):
choose a 0 immediately followed by a 1 and flip it up; repeat. Each such edge
has a positive reverse catalytic channel. Thus all nonzero states communicate,
while \(0^N\) is a separate singleton when \(s=0\). The bistable all-zero / all-
one trapping structure is singular in the strong-drive limit.

## Rule 232 finite-leak local control

Rule 232 has active motifs \(010\) and \(101\). From one 1-bit error in
\(0^N\), repair rate is \(q+s\); new-error rate is
\((N-3)r+(N-1)s\). The exact probability that the very next jump repairs the
bit before any additional bit error is
\[
P_{232}=\frac{q+s}{q+(N-3)r+Ns}.
\]
For a target \(P_{232}\ge1-\delta\), this is equivalent to
\[
\delta q\ge(1-\delta)(N-3)r+[N(1-\delta)-1]s.
\]
With no spontaneous flips it requires
\(A\ge\log((1-\delta)(N-3)/\delta)\). At high drive it requires
\(s/c\le\delta/[N(1-\delta)-1]\), i.e. per-site spontaneous leakage must
shrink on the order of \(1/N\) to preserve a fixed near-one clean-repair
probability. This is a local race condition, not a full memory-lifetime bound.

For contrast, from one 1-error in \(0^N\), Rule 166 has no catalytic repair:
the clean-correction rate is only \(s\), while catalytic expansion totals
\(q+r=c\). Its clean next-jump probability is \(s/(c+Ns)\), independent of
affinity. From one zero-error in \(1^N\), the corresponding clean next-jump
probability is \((q+s)/[3q+(N-3)r+Ns]\), tending to \(1/3\) at high drive and
low leakage.

## Finite generator and resource check

`exact_rate_audit.py` constructs each one-site enzyme channel separately,
adds spontaneous channels, restricts to states with at most \(N//2\) ones,
and solves the killed-generator equations for mean time, gross hydrolyses, and
reverse fuel returns. It uses exact rational arithmetic at \(N=7\), total
\(\sum_p c_p=2\), \(e^A=100\), and \(s=1/1000\) per site. To match total
catalytic capacity, per-species \(c_p=1/2\) for Rule 166’s four enzymes and
\(c_p=1\) for Rule 232’s two enzymes. This is a normalization extension of
the source’s common-per-enzyme \(k_{\rm cat}\) comparison.

The exact rational means are 164.900057699274 for Rule 166 and 1955.253917678178
for Rule 232. Expected gross hydrolyses / reverse fuel returns / net fuel use
are respectively 17.440194169900 / 0.246988761978 / 17.193205407922 and
140.437863288055 / 129.224230890933 / 11.213632397123. Multiplying net fuel
by \(A=\log 100\) gives chemical-work estimates of 79.177636946130 and
51.640685591860 \(k_BT\), respectively. The rational generator residual is
identically zero; the logarithmic work conversion is rounded. These are
conditional model computations, not measured biomolecular performance. They
charge chemical work at clamped reservoirs but not the information free energy
of the prepared initial state, enzyme synthesis/assembly, ATP depletion,
cross-reactivity, or implementation barriers.
