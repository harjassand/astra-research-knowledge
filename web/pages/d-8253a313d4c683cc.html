# Cycle 2: a finite-precision impossibility for rounded-gradient sampling

## Verdict

The cycle-1 nearby-potential estimate applies only after approximate replies have been shown to come from one admissible potential. Under the stated $C^2$ and Hessian bounds alone, that consistency cannot be guaranteed for a precise adversarial one-grid-step gradient oracle. I give two analytic, centered, $1$-strongly convex, $2$-smooth potentials whose Gibbs laws are at least $7/8$ apart in total variation, although the same grid-valued reply map is valid for either potential at every query point. No number of adaptive queries can then ensure the release's $0.1$ target error. The shared transcript also cannot compile to an admissible nearby potential with uniform Euclidean gradient error below $7/8$.

The obstruction is scoped. It covers coordinatewise fixed-point outputs with adversarial absolute error at most one grid spacing. It does not establish impossibility for deterministic round-to-nearest applied separately to each true gradient, an unbiased independently resampled stochastic oracle, or an oracle that also returns potential values. Both potentials are $C^\infty$; the adversarial error rule is essential to indistinguishability.

The precision threshold has the expected square-root-dimension scale. The pair is indistinguishable when $2^{-b}\ge a/\sqrt d$, so this model requires $b>\frac12\log_2d-\log_2a$ to avoid the obstruction. Conversely, if a transcript is already known to be the exact gradient of an admissible $W$, the cycle-1 stability lemma gives $\operatorname{TV}(\pi_V,\pi_W)\le\sqrt d\,2^{-b}/2$. That is a conditional sufficiency statement, not a compiler. The new result is the matching-exponent obstruction to compiling arbitrary rounded replies into such a $W$.

## Interface and constants

Let
\[
 h(t)=\log\cosh t,\qquad X\sim N(0,2/3),\qquad
 m_k=\mathbb E[h(X)^k],\qquad v=m_2-m_1^2.
\]
These are explicit one-dimensional Gaussian integrals; $0<v<\infty$ and $m_2,m_3<\infty$, since $h$ is nonconstant and $0\le h(t)\le |t|$. Define
\[
 a=16\max\{v^{-1/2},\sqrt{m_2}/v\},\qquad
 d_0=\left\lceil\max\{4a^2,(16a m_3/v)^2\}\right\rceil.
\]
For $d\ge d_0$, set $\theta=a/\sqrt d$ and
\[
 V_0(x)=\tfrac34\|x\|_2^2,\qquad
 V_1(x)=\tfrac34\|x\|_2^2+\theta\sum_{i=1}^d h(x_i).
\]
Both are $C^\infty$, have $V_j(0)=0$ and $\nabla V_j(0)=0$, and share the supplied minimizer/warm start $x_*=0$. Since $h''(t)=\operatorname{sech}^2t\in(0,1]$,
\[
 I\preceq\nabla^2V_j(x)\preceq2I
\]
for all $x$: $\nabla^2V_0=1.5I$ and $1.5I\preceq\nabla^2V_1\preceq(1.5+\theta)I\preceq2I$, using $d\ge4a^2$. Their gradients differ coordinatewise by at most $\theta$, and
\[
 \sup_x\|\nabla V_1(x)-\nabla V_0(x)\|_2=a.
\]

The oracle is gradient-only. Fix an integer $b\ge0$, spacing $s=2^{-b}$, and any query $x\in\mathbb R^d$ (or a rational vector with a finite binary encoding when query bits are charged). A valid reply to $V$ is any $y\in s\mathbb Z^d$ satisfying
\[
 \|y-\nabla V(x)\|_\infty\le s.
\]
The error may be adversarially selected as a function of the query and hidden potential. There is no output overflow: signed integer parts use as many bits as needed. This is fixed-point precision, not floating-point relative error.

The only supplied warm-start information is $x_*=0$. Algorithms may be randomized and adaptive and may perform unrestricted computation. The no-go holds for every query count, including an unbounded transcript, so it is stronger than a lower bound against the pinned release's $C_\epsilon d^\epsilon$ exact-query cap.

## Target separation

The Gibbs laws factor across coordinates. Under $\pi_0$, each coordinate is $N(0,2/3)$; under $\pi_1$, each coordinate has density
\[
 q_\theta(t)=Z_\theta^{-1}\exp(-3t^2/4-\theta h(t)).
\]
Write $H=h(X)$ under the base one-coordinate Gaussian, and let $v_u=\operatorname{Var}_{q_u}(H)$, where $q_u$ is the same exponential tilt with parameter $u$. For $u\ge0$, tilting by the decreasing function $e^{-uH}$ gives $\mathbb E_{q_u}H^k\le m_k$: the covariance of increasing $H^k$ and decreasing $e^{-uH}$ is nonpositive. Differentiating the tilted variance,
\[
 v'_u=-\mathbb E_{q_u}[(H-\mathbb E_{q_u}H)^3],\qquad
 |v'_u|\le8\mathbb E_{q_u}H^3\le8m_3.
\]
Here $H\ge0$, $(H+\mathbb EH)^3\le4(H^3+(\mathbb EH)^3)$, and Jensen bounds $(\mathbb EH)^3\le\mathbb EH^3$. Since $\theta\le v/(16m_3)$, we have $v_u\ge v/2$ for $u\in[0,\theta]$.

For $T(x)=\sum_i h(x_i)$, the mean gap is
\[
 \Delta=\mathbb E_{\pi_0}T-\mathbb E_{\pi_1}T
 =d\int_0^\theta v_u\,du
 \ge d\theta v/2=a\sqrt d\,v/2.
\]
Also $\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_0}T=dv$ and $\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_1}T=dv_\theta\le d m_2$. Let $A$ be the event that $T$ exceeds the midpoint of the two means. Chebyshev gives
\[
 \pi_0(A^c)\le\frac{16}{a^2v},\qquad
 \pi_1(A)\le\frac{16m_2}{a^2v^2}.
\]
Each term is at most $1/16$ by the definition of $a$. Hence
\[
 \boxed{\|\pi_0-\pi_1\|_{\rm TV}\ge\pi_0(A)-\pi_1(A)\ge7/8.}
\]
This holds for every integer $d\ge d_0$ and uses no CLT or numerical experiment.

## Indistinguishability theorem

**Theorem.** Fix $b\ge0$. If $d\ge d_0$ and $d\ge a^2 2^{2b}$, there is one oracle map $G_b:\mathbb R^d\to s\mathbb Z^d$ valid for both $V_0,V_1$. Every randomized adaptive algorithm has the same transcript and output law on both instances under this map. Its worst-case TV sampling error is at least $7/16$, hence exceeds $0.1$.

**Proof.** Let $W=(V_0+V_1)/2$ and define $G_b(x)$ by coordinatewise nearest-grid rounding of $\nabla W(x)$, with a fixed tie rule. For each coordinate,
\[
 |(G_b(x))_i-\partial_iV_j(x)|
 \le s/2+|\partial_iW(x)-\partial_iV_j(x)|
 \le s/2+\theta/2\le s,
\]
because $\theta=a/\sqrt d\le s$. Thus one and the same reply map is valid for both potentials at every point. Couple the algorithm's internal randomness and induct on queries: equal past transcripts force equal next queries, and $G_b$ returns equal responses. The output laws coincide; if their common law is $\mu$, then
\[
 7/8\le\|\pi_0-\pi_1\|_{\rm TV}
 \le\|\pi_0-\mu\|_{\rm TV}+\|\mu-\pi_1\|_{\rm TV},
\]
so one error is at least $7/16$. $\square$

This applies even if the algorithm knows the two-candidate family and constants, provided the hidden index is not supplied. It is an information-theoretic obstruction, not a lower bound for one particular sampler.

## No compiler to a nearby admissible potential

The cycle-1 conditional stability lemma says that admissible $U,V$ with $\sup_x\|\nabla U-\nabla V\|_2\le\eta$ satisfy $\|\pi_U-\pi_V\|_{\rm TV}\le\eta/2$. If one transcript-derived admissible $\widehat W$ were within uniform gradient error $\eta<7/8$ of both $V_0,V_1$, these inequalities and the triangle inequality would give $\|\pi_0-\pi_1\|_{\rm TV}\le\eta$, a contradiction. Since the common transcript is identical, a randomized compiler has the same output law under either hidden input. The sets of outputs certifying error below $7/8$ are disjoint, so its two success probabilities sum to at most one. It cannot guarantee success probability above $1/2$ on both instances. This rules out the requested compiler in this oracle model, not sampling through another representation.

## Precision, query, and bit costs

The exact threshold is
\[
 b\le\tfrac12\log_2d-\log_2a
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{indistinguishability, for }d\ge d_0.
\]
Thus a worst-case guarantee against this error model needs more than $\frac12\log_2d-\log_2a$ fractional bits. This matches the cycle-1 *conditional* stability scale up to an additive constant: if an admissible $W$ is separately certified and each gradient coordinate differs by at most $s$, then $\eta\le\sqrt d\,s$, so the target-law error is at most $\sqrt d\,s/2$. An added TV budget $\rho$ is met by $b\ge\frac12\log_2d+\log_2(1/(2\rho))$. This does not construct $W$; it shows why a consistency premise cannot be omitted for the adversarial oracle.

For query-bit accounting, write each rational query coordinate in reduced form $x_i=p_i/q_i$, $q_i>0$, and charge
\[
 L(x)=\sum_i\big(2+\lceil\log_2(|p_i|+1)\rceil+\lceil\log_2q_i\rceil\big).
\]
For $R=\|x\|_\infty$, the common reply is $y_i=sk_i$ with
\[
 |k_i|\le2^b(3R/2+\theta/2)+1/2.
\]
Signed binary integer replies therefore use at most
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^d\left(2+\left\lceil\log_2(1+2^b(3R/2+\theta/2)+1/2)\right\rceil\right)
 =O\big(d(1+b+\log(1+R))\big)
\]
bits. A $q$-query run pays $\sum_{t=1}^qL(x_t)$ input bits plus the displayed reply lengths. The lower bound holds even if these are free and $q$ is unbounded; charging them cannot restore identifiability. The integer parts do not overflow.

## Equally informed exact-query baseline

For the same two-candidate family, a classical exact-gradient algorithm makes one query at $e_1$. Its first gradient coordinate is $3/2$ under $V_0$ and $3/2+\theta\tanh(1)$ under $V_1$, so the exact-real reply identifies the hidden bit. Given that bit, it samples $\pi_0$ directly as a Gaussian or samples $q_\theta^{\otimes d}$ with independent one-dimensional rejection samplers from $N(0,2/3)$, accepting $t$ with probability $e^{-\theta h(t)}$. The acceptance probability is at least $e^{-\theta m_1}\ge e^{-m_1/2}>0$, since $\theta\le1/2$. This uses $O(d)$ expected scalar proposals. Coordinatewise inverse-CDF sampling is another option under unrestricted exact-real computation. No finite-bit output sampler is claimed.

For the full admissible class, the pinned release claims a universal $C_\epsilon d^\epsilon$ worst-execution value/gradient query cap at TV error $0.1$ with the minimizer supplied; its high-dimensional construction uses gradient evaluations. It gives no finite-bit or runtime bound. This is the same-class exact-query comparator. The one-query candidate-family procedure has extra structural side information and isolates the oracle-channel gap; it is not a universal sampler or a practical-time result.

## Prior art and scope

Ganesh and Talwar, *Faster Differentially Private Samplers via Rényi Divergence Analysis of Discretized Langevin MCMC* (NeurIPS 2020), state that their presentation assumes real arithmetic and that standard techniques translate their discretized-Langevin results to finite precision with precision logarithmic in $d$ and iteration count $T$. This is compatible with the $\frac12\log d+O(1)$ coordinate-precision scale here, but it is an implementation remark for a particular iterative algorithm, not an adversarial-gradient-oracle theorem or a nearby-potential compiler.

Chen, Chewi, Daskalakis, and Rakhlin, *High-Accuracy Log-Concave Sampling with Stochastic Queries* (COLT 2026; arXiv:2602.14342v2), assume unbiased i.i.d. stochastic replies with specified tail control. Their sub-exponential guarantee is $\widetilde O((\kappa\sqrt d+\sigma^2/\alpha)\operatorname{polylog}(1/\delta))$ queries. It does not cover biased adversarial quantization and is not contradicted here; this theorem has neither unbiasedness nor resampling.

The pinned Astra snapshot's nearby item L08 is source-derived and externally unreviewed, concerning stability of optimal transport potentials/maps for compact log-concave sources. It supplies no noisy-gradient premise. No direct rounded-gradient compiler or lower bound was found in the task-local routes. Astra navigation and literature checks do not establish priority; novelty remains unverified.
