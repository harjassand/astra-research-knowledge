Author claim (family 325): for every bounded operator `A` on any complex Hilbert
space, every finite coefficient dimension `m`, and any matrix-valued function
`F` holomorphic near `K=closure W(A)`,

\[
\|F[A]\|\le2\max_{z\in K}\|F(z)\|. \tag{1}
\]

The sharp constant is independent of the base dimension, coefficient size,
degree, and separability. The structural paper Theorems 1.2–1.3 claim more
for finite `A` and a **supplied** bounded convex domain `Ω` with regular
real-analytic Jordan boundary and `W(A)⊂Ω`. Let `f:Ω→D` be its interior
conformal map, put `T=f(A)`, and minimize

\[
\kappa^2=\min\{\tau:I\preceq H\preceq\tau I,
T^*HT\preceq H\}. \tag{2}
\]

The minimum is attained with `κ≤2`; any minimizing metric yields
`S=H^{1/2}`, `D=STS^{-1}` with `‖D‖≤1`, `ρ(D)<1`, and
`cond(S)=κ`. The same `S` works for every analytic matrix function and
coefficient size. Its common positive density is given explicitly in
Proposition 2.1, in the disk parameter, by

\[
\Phi_D(\zeta)=(I-D/\zeta)^{-1}+[(I-D/\zeta)^{-1}]^*-I,
\quad |\zeta|=1. \tag{3}
\]

For exterior conformal coordinate `G`, with `ψ=f∘G` and positive angular
Jacobian `j=tG'(t)f'(G(t))/ψ(t)`, the source uses
`Λ(t)=j(t)Φ_D(ψ(t))` and obtains

\[
\Lambda\succeq0,\quad\int\Lambda=I,\quad
F[SAS^{-1}]=\int\Lambda(t)\otimes F(G(t))\,d\sigma(t). \tag{4}
\]

The basis/Hilbert tensor factor is first. Multiplication order of matrix
coefficients is essential. The source does **not** claim a convergent metric
or continuous density on an arbitrary limiting numerical range, nor an
optimal metric on a general infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.

## Independently reconstructed decisive comparison

The direct proof has the shorter universal route. For a univalent exterior
collar `h(λ)=cλ+c0+O(λ^{-1})`, set `q(λ)=λh'(λ)` and define Faber
polynomials by `q(λ)/(h(λ)−z)=Σ_k b_k(z)λ^{-k}`.
The mixed correction

\[
s(\lambda,\mu)=\frac{q(\lambda)}{h(\lambda)-h(\mu)}-
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-\mu}
\]

extends through the diagonal and both infinities and has only strictly
negative powers in both variables. Convexity gives
`K=1+s+conj(s)≥0` on the two circles, while integrating in either angular
variable gives one. This is a genuine Markov-kernel contraction in boundary
Hilbert `L2`, not a symmetry assertion: the kernel need not be symmetric.
For `G=Σ_k b_k C_k`, orthogonal Fourier supports therefore give

\[
\sum_k\|C_k\|_{HS}^2\le\|G\circ h\|_{L2,HS}^2
\le\|C_0\|_{HS}^2+2\sum_{k\ge1}\|C_k\|_{HS}^2. \tag{5}
\]

Let `F` be boundary-normalized and `F[A]x=γy`, `F[A]*y=γx` a top
singular pair. Write `x=vec(X), y=vec(Y)` in the base-first convention.
The original-matrix resolvent `R=q(hI−A)^{-1}` satisfies `R+R*≥0`, by
congruence with `hI−A` and the supporting half-plane of `W(A)`.
Its coefficients are `b_k(A)`. Put `M_k=Y*b_k(A)X` and
`a²=Σ_k‖M_k‖²_HS>0`.

The coefficient pairing bounds `|y*G[A]x|` by `a(Σ‖C_k‖²)^{1/2}`.
Testing **both** `FG` and `GF` gives respectively
`γx*G[A]x` and `γy*G[A]y`. Each ordered product is boundary-contractive
in Hilbert–Schmidt norm; (5) then bounds both diagonal coefficient
functionals by `a/γ` in the weighted norm with nonconstant weight 2.
Duality gives reciprocal weight `1/2` for their coefficient arrays.

Consequently the Hermitian diagonal compressions `P+P*` and `Q+Q*` of
`R+R*` have squared boundary norms at most `4a²/γ²` each. The cross
compression `M+L*` has squared boundary norm at least `a²`, by Parseval,
including its constant term `2M0`. For the positive block `Δ`, use
`J=diag(I,−I)`:

\[
0\le\operatorname{tr}(\Delta J\Delta J)
=\|P+P^*\|_{HS}^2+\|Q+Q^*\|_{HS}^2-2\|M+L^*\|_{HS}^2.
\]

Integration gives `2a²≤8a²/γ²`, hence `γ≤2`.
Shrinking analytic convex neighborhoods gives arbitrary finite numerical
ranges; a finite Krylov compression of each polynomial input transfers
the bound to every Hilbert space. Holomorphic approximation must be on a
fixed neighborhood/contour of `K`, not merely pointwise on `K`.

The structural route was also checked at its distinct load-bearing steps:
finite SDP separation gives endpoint-supported dual matrices; a unitary
colligation supplies an exact matrix-function extremal; the full matrix
cross mean vanishes; actual Cauchy adjoints preserve the two multiplication
orders; a positive block then forces `κ≤2`. Scalar zero trace is insufficient
for the Fourier orthogonality required there.

## Original deduction: a finite positive evaluation certificate

Once a stable contraction `D` is acquired, (3) has the absolutely uniformly
convergent Fourier series

\[
\Phi_D(\zeta)=I+\sum_{r\ge1}(\zeta^{-r}D^r+\zeta^rD^{*r}). \tag{6}
\]

The positive representation itself follows by the exact congruence
`(I−D/ζ)*Φ_D(ζ)(I−D/ζ)=I−D*D≥0`; its moments are
`∫ζ^kΦ_D(ζ)=D^k` for `k≥0`. Thus it is a common compression of scalar
boundary multiplication at every coefficient size.

If `D^s=0`, its density has no Fourier modes outside `±(s−1)`.
For a polynomial `H` of degree at most `d`, choose `N>d+s−1` and sample
the `N`th roots of unity `ζ_l`. Trapezoidal summation is exact because no
nonzero Fourier frequency in the integrand is a multiple of `N`:

\[
W_l=\Phi_D(\zeta_l)/N\succeq0,\quad\sum_lW_l=I,
\quad H[D]=\sum_lW_l\otimes H(\zeta_l). \tag{7}
\]

This is a finite positive evaluation certificate for **all** coefficient
sizes at the same degree, with `N=O(d+s)` boundary evaluations. This
deduction is classical Fourier quadrature applied to the source density;
no new priority is claimed.

An exact sharp witness makes every quantity explicit. Set
`A=[[0,2],[0,0]]`, use `Ω={|z|<R}` with `1<R<2`, and `f(z)=z/R`.
Writing `a=2/R`, every feasible metric in (2) obeys
`H22≥a²H11≥a²`, hence `τ≥a²`. The metric
`H=diag(1,a²)` attains the minimum. Therefore

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}(1,a),\quad D=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad\Phi_D(\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\zeta^{-1}\\\zeta&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The density has eigenvalues `0,2`, mass `I`, and (7) is exact with
`N>d+1`. This exposes why `‖D‖=1` is allowed and why a uniform strictly
positive density is not promised. As `R↓1`, `cond(S)↑2`.

For nonnilpotent `D`, suppose a **certified** power bound
`‖D^r‖≤Mρ^r`, `0<ρ<1`, has been supplied. For `0≤k≤d<N`, discrete
moment aliasing gives

\[
\Bigl\|N^{-1}\sum_l\zeta_l^k\Phi_D(\zeta_l)-D^k\Bigr\|
\le\frac{2M\rho^{N-d}}{1-\rho^N}. \tag{8}
\]

For `k=0`, the mass error is at most
`η=2Mρ^N/(1−ρ^N)`. If `η<1`, normalize all positive weights by
congruence with the inverse square root of their mass matrix. This keeps
positivity and forces exact mass `I`. For `H(z)=Σ C_k z^k`, the resulting
evaluation error is bounded by the alias term in (8) times `Σ‖C_k‖`,
plus at most `η(1+η)/(1−η) max_{|ζ|=1}‖H(ζ)‖` from normalization.
Returning through `S` multiplies the certificate error by at most `κ`.

Thus acquisition cost depends on `M`, the spectral margin `1−ρ`, degree,
and coefficient sizes, even though (1) has a universal constant. The source
does not provide dimension-free `M,ρ`, conformal-map acquisition, an exact
SDP solver, or a universal runtime bound for this certificate.

## Prior art, novelty and practical consequence

The primary Lorist–Schwenninger paper `2608.03841v2` has verified version
date August 17, 2026. Its Theorem 3 already supplies scalar constant 2 for
bounded Hilbert operators. Section 3(iv) explicitly identifies commutation
as the obstacle to passing that proof through matrix amplification. The
release's substantive advance is the complete bound and its ordered
comparison, rather than an improvement to an unknown scalar constant.

The source cites existing completely bounded bounds `1+sqrt(2)` and the
classical similarity/dilation representation machinery. Those older results
already transfer scalar approximation errors to matrix functions with a
universal loss. A change from `1+sqrt(2)` to `2` improves that loss by about
17 percent; it does not create an absent numerical approximation guarantee.
The new structural claim packages one optimizer and one boundary density
for every function and coefficient size. Its sharper constant and endpoint
comparison are worth checking; the representation should not be described
as newly invented positive functional calculus.

A concrete conditional downstream use is a certified approximation of
nonnormal coupled dynamics: if matrix-valued `F,P` satisfy
`max_K‖F−P‖≤ε`, then `‖F[A]−P[A]‖≤2ε`. Here `K` must be an acquired
numerical-range enclosure, and `F` must be holomorphic near it. Spectrum
alone is insufficient: the nilpotent example has spectrum `{0}` but
operator norm 2. This does not give an arbitrary nonholomorphic functional
calculus, unlimited transient control from eigenvalues, or a cost-free PDE
certificate.
