# Independent gate: complex symmetries of positive biquadratic quartics

7 October 2026. **PASS**, for the theorem in `notes/metric_prior/COMPLEX_MONOMIAL_AUTOMORPHISMS.md`. This is root's independent mathematical reconstruction, not an external validation or historical-priority determination.

Let f(y)=(y²)^TQ(y²), with real Q positive definite. The full complex linear stabilizer is monomial in the hidden coordinates. This statement concerns all inputs in this promise, including exceptional inputs on which the current acquisition detector might vanish.

First, the infinitesimal identity grad(f)·Ay=0 has y_i⁴ coefficient 4Q_ii A_ii, and its y_i³y_j/y_iy_j³ coefficients form the two-by-two system with matrix [[Q_ii,Q_ij],[Q_ij,Q_jj]]. Positive principal minors kill every entry of A. The stabilizer is an algebraic group over characteristic zero with zero Lie algebra; hence it has dimension zero and finitely many points. This correctly treats complex, not merely real, symmetries.

Average a Hermitian form over that finite group. The original coordinate sign flips force the average to be diagonal, with positive real entries. Positive real diagonal normalization therefore makes the entire group unitary while preserving a real PD biquadratic representation. This is an existence argument; no group enumeration or rational canonical average is asserted.

For a complex unit vector z and m_i=|z_i|²,

    |f(z)| ≤ Σ_i Q_ii m_i² + 2Σ_(i<j)|Q_ij|m_i m_j
           ≤ (Σ_i sqrt(Q_ii)m_i)² ≤ max_i Q_ii.

The middle inequality is strict whenever at least two m_i are nonzero. Thus the maximum-modulus set is precisely the coordinate circles for the largest diagonal entries. A unitary automorphism must permute this entire tier of complex lines. Their span and its orthogonal complement are invariant. Repeating on the remaining principal form identifies every tier and proves monomiality. Deflating a whole tier avoids the error of assuming one arbitrarily selected axis is invariant under every symmetry.

An involutive monomial reflection has exactly one minus-one eigenvalue. It either negates one axis or swaps one pair with reciprocal scales t,t^(-1). Preservation imposes t⁴=Q_jj/Q_ii. There are at most four complex or two real choices per pair; additional couplings can only remove choices. Consequently there are at most 2n²−n complex and n² real reflections. The Fermat quartic attains both counts. The statement uses involutive linear reflections, rather than all possible complex pseudoreflections of arbitrary order.

The same invariant-form/tier argument also independently certifies uniqueness of the unordered real hidden axes among all PD biquadratic representations. Root previously checked that conclusion using real compactness and strict simplex convexity in `HOMOGENEOUS_ACQUISITION_GATE.md`.
