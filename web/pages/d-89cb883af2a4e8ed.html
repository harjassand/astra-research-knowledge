# Teacher-free predictive-feature acquisition for finite-state processes

**Conclusion.** This investigation establishes no unconditional information-theoretic impossibility, and standard predictive-state/spectral methods solve important conditioned subclasses. There is, however, a sharp conditional computational obstruction for the teacher-free passive interface: a polynomial-time learner that receives only ordinary trajectories and returns an accurate arbitrary-prefix evaluator for every O(d)-state process would solve search-LPN. The reduction is explicit below. It is the same noisy-parity mechanism as Kearns et al. (1994), with a direct TV threshold, resource accounting, and an ordinary-HMM state conversion; it is not a new general hardness result.

This obstruction does **not** settle the Astra A06 target. A06 supplies a separate exact teacher evaluator, with complete-law KL error, during acquisition. The inspected A06 route proves useful static compression and finite-prefix column acquisition, but it does not acquire evaluable features on fresh prefixes or a stable teacher-free sequential update. The passive LPN reduction cannot be applied to the teacher-assisted service without an additional argument that removes or simulates the teacher calls.

## 1. Exact passive TV/evaluator-to-search-LPN reduction

Fix d≥1, a secret s∈GF(2)^d and noise η∈(0,1/2), with θ=1−2η. One episode emits A=(A₁,…,A_d) uniformly from GF(2)^d and then B=s·A⊕E, where E∼Bernoulli(η). It may emit any independent tail afterward. The length-(d+1) law is

\[
P_s(a,b)=2^{-(d+1)}\left(1+\theta(-1)^{s\cdot a+b}\right),\qquad
P_s(a,0)-P_s(a,1)=2^{-d}\theta(-1)^{s\cdot a}.
\]

The source is a probabilistic finite automaton with two parity tracks and a d-step clock, as in Kearns et al., Section 6.1, Theorem 16. An edge-emission-to-state-emission lift gives an ordinary finite-horizon HMM with O(d) states; the Astra N13 reconstruction gives the convention-specific bound 4d+4 (with a positive time-homogeneous symbol-transition realization of at most 2d+2 states). At η=1/4 all transition/emission probabilities can be chosen from {0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1}. The hardness is not due to large rational coefficients.

**Lemma (TV gives a parity predictor).** Suppose q is a law on length-(d+1) words and its point-probability evaluator can evaluate q(a,0), q(a,1) for any supplied a. Define f(a)=1 exactly when q(a,1)>q(a,0), with ties mapped to 0. If TV(P_s,q)≤θ/16, then

\[
\Pr_{A\sim U_d}[f(A)\ne s\cdot A]\le \frac{2\operatorname{TV}(P_s,q)}{\theta}\le\frac18.
\]

For every misclassified a, the signed q gap is on the wrong side of the true signed gap (or is tied), so
\[
\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}|P_s(a,b)-q(a,b)|\ge 2^{-d}\theta.
\]
Summing over misclassified a and using \(\sum_{a,b}|P_s-q|=2\operatorname{TV}(P_s,q)\) proves the bound. Thus \(\phi(a)=(-1)^{f(a)}\) has Fourier coefficient \(\widehat\phi(s)=\mathbb E[\phi(A)(-1)^{s\cdot A}]\ge3/4\).

The same statement holds for a polynomial-time next-bit conditional evaluator \(q(B\mid A=a)\): compare its two conditional probabilities. When q(a)>0 this is equivalent to comparing q(a,0), q(a,1); at q(a)=0 the evaluator must still return a valid pair, and the TV inequality charges such prefixes through the missing q marginal mass. The precision interface matters. For exact rational comparisons the displayed 1/8 bound is exact. If the joint point probabilities are returned with absolute error at most \(2^{-d}\theta/8\) each, an erroneous rounded comparison implies joint L1 error at least \((3/4)2^{-d}\theta\), giving classifier error at most 1/6 and Fourier coefficient at least 2/3. This needs only d+O(log(1/θ)) bits after the binary point. A conditional evaluator may instead be required to compare its conditional pair exactly or to a stated absolute precision; computing it by dividing tiny q(a) values is part of the evaluator cost, not free.

**Search reduction and costs.** Given the learned evaluator, Goldreich–Levin finds a list of masks with heavy Fourier coefficient using polynomially many arbitrary-input evaluations of f; each f query uses two q probability calls and a comparison. Since the true mask has coefficient at least 3/4 (or 2/3 under the finite joint-evaluation tolerance), it appears on the list. For each candidate v, fresh ordinary LPN samples estimate
\[
C_v=\mathbb E[(-1)^{B+v\cdot A}].
\]
Here C_s=θ and C_v=0 for v≠s. Hoeffding plus a union bound shows that O(θ⁻² log(|L|/δ)) fresh examples, with each correlation estimated to error θ/4, identify s with failure probability at most δ. The learner’s success probability and the extractor/validation failure probabilities are split in the usual way to obtain constant overall success.

If the training learner makes R reset calls and reads O symbols, an ordinary search-LPN sample service simulates it exactly: consume one fresh (a,b) sample per reset, reveal a's coordinates, then b, and generate the independent tail locally. This costs R LPN samples, O(Rd+O) local bit operations and O(d) source-side buffering, in addition to the learner’s own charged sample, time, model-size and memory costs. It does not simulate a chosen-prefix conditional oracle. The extractor costs poly(d, log(1/δ)) evaluator calls for the constant-threshold Goldreich–Levin route; evaluator bit precision and per-call time are included. Validation costs O(θ⁻² log(|L|/δ)) more LPN samples and O(d|L|θ⁻² log(|L|/δ)) bit operations by direct candidate scoring. Streaming the extractor requires only its current prefix list (at most O(1) masks at the 3/4 threshold), O(d) temporary bits, and its evaluator workspace; total memory is learner/model memory plus polynomial-in-d evaluator workspace.

There is a second version for a directly evaluable bounded conditional mean \(g(a)=q(0\mid a)-q(1\mid a)\). A direct marginal-and-joint L1 inequality gives
\[
\mathbb E_{A\sim U_d}|g(A)-\theta(-1)^{s\cdot A}|\le4\operatorname{TV}(P_s,q),
\]
so \(\widehat g(s)\ge3\theta/4\). The self-contained bucket-energy implementation recorded in the Astra N13 report uses \(O(d\theta^{-6}\log(d/(\theta\delta)))\) conditional-predictor evaluations, O(log(1/θ)) bits of conditional-value precision, and a final list of at most 16/θ² masks, followed by O(θ⁻² log(|L|/δ)) validation examples. If q’s conditional evaluator divides q(a,b) by an arbitrarily tiny q(a), its polynomial finite-bit cost must be part of the output contract. This normalized-predictor version is useful when the target is an actual next-symbol forecast; the joint-evaluator theorem above is the closest direct match to Kearns et al.’s evaluator interface.

**Status of the impossibility.** Under the standard search-LPN/noisy-parity hardness hypothesis at any fixed constant η<1/2 (for example η=1/4), no uniform randomized polynomial-time passive learner can meet that TV/evaluator specification on all these O(d)-state processes. This is conditional computational hardness, not unconditional impossibility, and it says nothing about generator-only outputs, special subclasses, more powerful query services, or superpolynomial algorithms. Kearns et al. already prove the evaluator/PFA obstruction under their Noisy Parity Assumption: Conjecture 15 and Theorem 16, PDF pp. 7–8, especially the two-track construction and evaluator classifier in the proof sketch (PDF lines 1215–1258). The thresholded TV statement and explicit search extraction above are a sharpened interface calculation, not a novelty claim.

## 2. Teacher-assisted A06 route: what is proved and what is missing

The pinned Astra source is `web/pages/d-01db0c364a53b02c.html` at commit `8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83`. Its exact target (source lines 18–31) is an unknown possibly time-inhomogeneous r-state HMM law P on [q]^H, independent complete P trajectories, and a fixed polynomially evaluable teacher Q returning exact rational probabilities at any explicitly chosen prefix. Complete-law KL(P||Q)≤ζ; Q may have arbitrary rank. Acquisition is to run in poly(r,q,H,L,1/ε,log(1/δ)) without inverse ζ, inverse singular-value, or inverse latent-mass factors. Deployment must retain only bounded stream-specific bits/state, use a finite-precision recurrence of dimension poly(r,q), and support simultaneous norm-bounded readouts of all bounded future tasks with MSE ≤ Cζ+ε², with later convex regression. Calls to Q and full-prefix storage are forbidden after acquisition.

The inspected A06 report gives a valid **static** route: its stopped-prefix Hellinger identity controls expected squared conditional TV on P- or Q-generated prefixes by KL(P||Q), with no width or horizon multiplier (lines 82–125). A bounded-task \(\ell_\infty\to\ell_2\) semidefinite compression gives rank at most 2r and source-task MSE at most (2π+2)ζ plus explicitly additive sampling/SDP error on a finite table of rows (lines 126–216). For a finite supplied or sampled prefix list, future columns can be acquired with a mixture sampler: k=O(n[n log(1+√n/α)+log(1/δ)]/α²), n×k stored entries, O(nkH) teacher token-probability evaluations, O(kH) teacher simulation steps, and bit-length depending on L and H (lines 217–265). This is a candidate/source-reported derivation in the Astra page, not an external proof certification; its local diagnostics are finite numerical checks, not theorem validation.

**The missing service is fresh-prefix recurrence.** The rank-doubling SDP returns coordinates on acquired training-prefix rows. It does not provide a polynomial-size feature map evaluable on every unseen prefix, a map from the previous compact state plus next symbol to the next state, stable change-of-basis maps, or a finite-precision error analysis over H updates. A06 states this missing implication explicitly (lines 266–295): acquire evaluable features and stable updates from the finite task-norm frames with total actual-path loss O(ζ)+ε² and no r/H multiplier on ζ. Ordinary teacher KL controls actual/stopping prefixes; it does not certify the synthetic forced prefixes used by Liu–Moitra’s spanner extension. The passive LPN reduction above cannot fill this gap because A06’s training interface includes Q’s exact chosen-prefix evaluator and simulation; those are additional services not generated by passive P trajectories.

**Astra counterexample scope.** The pinned N25 page `web/pages/d-473caf7c9b06a990.html`, lines 17–68, has a four-state paired-bit source P and a positive low-KL teacher Q whose special parity-logit rule is never visited on P’s actual support. Independently spliced source-prefix/suffix marginals reach a constant-mass rectangle with Walsh rank 2^(k−1). This blocks using complete-path KL as a uniform low-rank guarantee for all synthetic splices; it is not an impossibility theorem for every teacher-assisted learner. I did not rely on the older workspace rare-prefix result, and did not independently reopen it.

## 3. Prior art and the service boundary

**Conditional queries are stronger than passive trajectories.** Mahajan, Kakade, Krishnamurthy and Zhang, COLT 2023, define an exact conditional-probability oracle and a chosen-prefix conditional-sampling oracle. Their Theorem 1 learns any binary-alphabet rank-r law with exact conditional probabilities; Theorem 2 learns with chosen-prefix conditional samples assuming fidelity Δ*>0, in time polynomial in r,T,alphabet,1/Δ*,1/ε,log(1/δ) (PDF p. 4, lines 199–225; full theorem at p. 39, lines 2665–69). Their noisy-parity HMM satisfies rank ≤2T and fidelity θ²/2 under a basis of size at most two (Proposition 17, PDF p. 24, lines 1318–69). Thus constant-noise parity is easy with that stronger conditional-sample interface. Liu and Moitra, STOC 2025, Theorem 1.3 removes the fidelity condition for arbitrary finite-rank laws given chosen-prefix conditional queries, using poly(S,O,T,1/ε) queries/time and outputting an efficient sampler (Definition 1.2/Theorem 1.3, lines 92–102). Neither theorem is a passive trajectory result, and neither supplies A06’s misspecified-teacher-to-fresh-prefix bridge for free.

**Predictive-state representations.** PSRs represent state by predictions of future observable tests, and their linear update is normalized after appending an observation. Spectral/HSE-PSR methods estimate a fixed/test-selected basis from moments; predictive features are therefore not conceptually new. A finite test bank or a well-conditioned empirical Hankel basis is an assumption/resource that must be charged. These methods do not give a uniform teacher-free acquisition guarantee for arbitrary finite-state laws with no mass or singular-value margins. See Boots, Gordon and Gretton (2013), and Kulesza et al. (2015) on weighted spectral learning and finite test sets.

**Spectral HMM identification.** Hsu, Kakade and Zhang (2012), Condition 1, assume positive initial mass and full-rank transition and emission matrices. Their passive spectral algorithm uses empirical low-order moments and recovers observable-operator predictions; their sample bounds depend polynomially on inverse singular values. The stronger conditional-probability guarantee in Theorem 7 also assumes a positive stochasticity parameter α and a nondegeneracy parameter γ, with explicit inverse powers in the sample bound. This is valuable positive prior art on a structured subclass, not a counterexample to the LPN obstruction. Latent-state nonidentifiability alone does not imply predictive-law nonidentifiability; conversely, generic parameter identifiability results (for example Allman–Matias–Rhodes 2009) do not supply stable finite-sample feature acquisition or finite-precision recurrence.

| Interface | Data/service | What the cited result establishes | Relation to the target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passive paths | Independent ordinary finite-horizon trajectories | Kearns Thm. 16: evaluator hardness under noisy-parity assumption; the TV lemma above gives an explicit search-LPN reduction | Rules out a teacher-free universal polynomial evaluator learner under LPN |
| Conditional samples | Learner chooses any prefix and gets a source-conditional future sample | Mahajan Thm. 2 under fidelity; Liu–Moitra Thm. 1.3 without fidelity | Stronger oracle; it solves the parity example and cannot be silently substituted for passive data |
| Teacher-assisted A06 | P paths plus exact rational Q probabilities/simulation at chosen prefixes; KL(P||Q)≤ζ | Static task-norm compression and finite-prefix acquisition are source-reported; fresh-prefix recurrence remains open | The passive reduction does not remove Q or prove the missing stable update |
| Full-rank spectral HMM | Passive moments plus rank/conditioning and positivity margins | Efficient observable-operator learning with explicit inverse-margin dependence | Positive structured subclass, not arbitrary rank-deficient/no-margin HMMs |
