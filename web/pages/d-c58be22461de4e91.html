Candidate theorem (scoped field target). For every bounded C² Jordan domain D and every sequence of simply connected inside approximations Dδ by the square lattice δZ² with uniformly convergent boundary parametrizations, let hδ be normalized Lebesgue measure on
`Pδ = {h:V(Dδ)→R : h=0 on ∂Dδ; |h(u)−h(v)|≤1 for every square-lattice edge uv}`.
Does there exist a single σsq>0, independent of D and of the approximation sequence, such that hδ/σsq converges as a random distribution to the Dirichlet GFF on D with covariance (−ΔD)⁻¹? The zero field shows the fibers have nonempty relative interior; boundedness follows from the nonempty pinned boundary. The D4 symmetry predicts a scalar continuum stiffness. This is a different graph and a different theorem from family 232, whose constraints are on the six triangular-lattice directions and whose boundary has two arcs at ±λ. The more ambitious follow-up is a tuned two-arc square-lattice interface/SLE4 theorem; I do not count it as part of this first gate because contour resolution on square faces and the curve-barrier proof add separate obligations.

Why the gap is credible. Schramm’s ICM Problem 2.3 asks for GFF/SLE4 limits for the real hard-constraint model on the triangular lattice (primary source, Problem 2.3, p. 528: https://arxiv.org/html/math/0602151v2). Miller’s bounded-domain square-lattice GFF theorem assumes a symmetric uniformly convex interaction; the hammock potential `V(t)=0` for `|t|≤1`, `+∞` otherwise is outside that class (primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1002.0381v2). Miłos–Peled prove logarithmic delocalization and a maximum lower bound for hard-core surfaces on a pinned square torus, not a bounded-domain scaling law (Theorem 1.1: https://arxiv.org/html/1404.5895v3). Cohen-Alloro–Peled prove rarity of near-extremal gradients on general bounded-degree graphs for monotone Lipschitz potentials (Theorem 1.1: https://arxiv.org/html/1711.00259v2); that supports sparse-block control but does not identify a GFF limit. The current family-232 paper itself says its square-domain hard-core delocalization inputs do not provide bounded-domain field or contour limits (PDF pp. 3–4). This bounded primary-source pass makes the target plausible; it does not establish that it is open or novel.

Transfer mechanism. In the revised triangular paper, the proof chain is local filling-volume curvature and sparse defects (PDF §§3.2–3.3, pp. 6–8), unscaled average coercivity (Lemma 3.7, p. 11), stationary reflected coupling/tangent response (PDF §§4–5), and trace/conditional Dirichlet covariance (PDF §§6–7, especially p. 45). The geometric heart is Lemma 3.2, “Triangular block deficit” (PDF pp. 6–7): for a fixed large block, a convex-good base height h, shell s, and perturbation χ whose two shell fibers have positive volume, the midpoint loss in `F(s)=log vol K(s)` controls `|χ(v)−χ(w)|²` between each pair of side-midpoints. The paper’s edge-exposure construction uses triangular graph distances and a non-tie condition.

Decisive missing lemma. Prove, with constants uniform over all admissible square-grid shell data, the same midpoint-deficit inequality on `Q_L={0,…,L}²` under explicit slack assumptions: (i) h extends the shell and every edge in QL has `|Δh|≤1−δ`; (ii) every pair at graph distance at least k0 has `|h(x)−h(y)|≤(1−δ0)d1(x,y)`; (iii) L is fixed sufficiently large relative to `(k0+2)/δ0`; and (iv) both perturbed filling polytopes `K(s±χ)` have positive volume. The conclusion is `F(s)−½(F(s+χ)+F(s−χ)) ≥ c max_{v,w∈C}|χ(v)−χ(w)|²`, where C is the four side-midpoint set and c>0 depends only on the fixed slack parameters and L, not on s or χ. A plausible proof assigns a chosen radial edge length 1 and all other edges length 1−β in a weighted Manhattan metric, exposes a filling with that edge saturated and all other constraints slack, then imports the source’s dimension-finite slice-log-concavity argument. For edges within k0 of the face use its midpoint and the same-face boundary path; the other faces are at distance at least L/2, where long-pair slack absorbs the unit shift. This is a proof sketch, not a verified lemma.

If this local inequality holds, the source’s finite-dimensional quantile/slice lemma is lattice-independent. Cohen-Alloro–Peled’s bounded-degree extremal-edge estimate supplies a plausible sparse-defect input for the square graph with zero pins. Still required for a field theorem: recheck the square versions of the sparse-hole/trace estimates; establish the stationary reflected square-grid limit has finite positive tangent stiffness; and carry the conditional covariance identification through the square-grid kernels. For the two-arc SLE extension, additionally prove stopping-rule-uniform covariance/survival bounds and a square-face interface/barrier argument. None of these obligations is discharged by the index entry or by the source manuscript’s own theorem.

Early falsifier. At rational shell data on small square boxes, compute or rigorously bound `F(s)` and the midpoint deficit for perturbations of opposite side-midpoints. Search first among shell fibers satisfying (i)–(iii). A sequence of admissible fibers/perturbations for which the deficit divided by `max|χ(v)−χ(w)|²` tends to zero would kill this block-deficit transfer route. Passing a finite box test only supports the construction; it does not prove a uniform constant. The more structural failure mode is an edge-exposure configuration where every filling saturating a selected edge also forces a second unrelated facet, preventing a uniform relative facet-width bound.
