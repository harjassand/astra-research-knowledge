LAW, NORMALIZATION, LOCAL KNOWLEDGE, AND DOWNSTREAM GATES
Independent baseline scope; 2026-10-09

1. Probability law and normalization

The theorem has one externally specified probability law nu on a finite
set of local-description pairs. Each described vector has norm EXACTLY
one in the supplied common L2(M,tau). The input-dependent target table
and actual protocol table each have total mass one for every input pair.
The measured quantity is the nu-average of half total variation, and the
description discrepancy D is measured under that SAME nu.

All original local POVMs are supplied in M/its fixed right representation.
Their numbers of outcomes, the input family size, the dimension of M,
and M's type do not enter the universal error constant. Resource size and
spectral approximation cost can depend on these supplied data.

If a downstream target contains a subnormalized f_xy, replacing it by
f_xy/||f_xy|| only solves the NORMALIZED correlation problem. It does not
reproduce its mass ||f_xy||^2. A vanishing norm cannot be normalized at
all and must be separately assigned/charged. Local norms may be used in
choosing local normalized descriptions only if locally known; a norm or
filter depending on BOTH realized inputs is not authorized by this proof.

2. Size-biased laws and referee-law transfer

Suppose a downstream law is nu_xy proportional to mu_xy||f_xy||^2.
Applying this theorem under nu is legitimate if nu and the finite family
are fixed globally in advance. It does not change the referee's actual
sampling law mu. Neither an automatically correct acceptance probability
nor a comparison under mu follows merely from the pointwise normalized
sampler. Any transfer must explicitly bound missing mass/barycenter
errors and/or TV(nu,mu), together with its normalization factor.

For a bounded payoff w in [0,1], |E_nu w-E_mu w|<=TV_half(nu,mu) when
nu,mu are probability laws. This elementary observation is only a valid
interface once both distributions and their TV bound are actually
acquired; it does not supply a bound on the size-biased law itself.

biased by ||f_ir(x,y)||^2 is recorded as COORDINATOR-SUPPLIED downstream
information. No L02, new QMS, or Luna proof was read before this freeze.
The warning changes no premise of the present unit-vector theorem.

3. Local knowledge and common resource

Permitted common preprogramming: entire finite description family,
probability law nu, a bound D or its value, accuracy parameters, the
chosen finite spectral approximations, and a label range covering all of
them. The resource depends only on this preprogramming. Every realized
Alice instrument uses only her own approximation, its left modulus, and
her original POVM; Bob uses only his own approximation, its left modulus,
its polar partial isometry, and his original POVM. The globally chosen T
and number N are preagreed, not selected after learning the realized pair.

The protocol assumes full descriptions are available as mathematical
inputs. It claims no efficient representation or spectral computation for
an arbitrary finite vN algebra, no uniform bound on N/l, and no way to
learn an unknown description from samples. A single protocol/resource
works simultaneously for every listed pair/coordinate. Cross-input
commutation is literal left versus right commutation in ONE fixed pair
of algebras (M_l tensor M)^tensor N and its opposite.

4. State-preparation versus correlation interface

Successful synchronized branches carry the explicit original-M vector
x/sqrt(C). Mismatched selected indices and timeouts remain genuine
unconditional commuting outcome branches on the enlarged resource.
They need not be representable by an output state on original M. Hence
this proof gives correlation and bounded-event estimates, not a local
TP Kraus channel on fixed M. It deliberately does not preserve original
central weights, because selecting a trial changes the retained sample.
