# Cold audit: Gaussian output-sampling lemma

**Verdict.** The Gaussian-mixture identity, the bounded-coordinate estimate, and the subGaussian determinant bound are correct under the stated independence, centering, and proxy assumptions. Applied to `k` fresh whole-vector N687 values, they give a valid direct sampler for `N(m, sigma^2 I_d)`: draw independent `G_1,...,G_k ~ N(0,I_d)`, query `X_j=g(G_j)`, and output

\[
Y=\bar X+Z,\qquad \bar X=k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^kX_j,\quad Z\sim N(0,\sigma^2I_d)\text{ independent},\quad m=\mathbb E g(G).
\]

This is a useful every-execution upper bound using the actual N687 whole-vector value oracle. It is postprocessing by the sampler, not a noisy oracle interface. It therefore cannot by itself prove that a raw oracle answer hides the field or its target mean, and it does not close N687's polylogarithmic-in-d gate. The idea of sampling an unknown-mean Gaussian by adding Gaussian noise to an empirical mean is already present in the capital's bounded-iid Gaussian-mean dossier; the sharpened proxy and rare-coordinate estimates may still be useful as a refined bound. No novelty or priority claim is made.

## Exact claims and constants

For `P = Law(m+V+Z)`, `Q = N(m, sigma^2 I_d)`, and an independent copy `V'`, the density ratio is

\[
\frac{dP}{dQ}(x)=\mathbb E_V\exp\!\left(\frac{(x-m)^\top V}{\sigma^2}-\frac{\|V\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).
\]

For fixed `v,v'`, integrating the product of these two likelihood ratios against `Q` gives `exp(v^T v'/sigma^2)`. Consequently

\[
1+\chi^2(P\|Q)=\mathbb E_{V,V'}e^{V^\top V'/\sigma^2}.
\]

When the scalar coordinates `V_i` are independent, the expectation factors, so the quoted identity is

\[
\chi^2(P\|Q)=\prod_{i=1}^d\mathbb E e^{V_iV_i'/\sigma^2}-1.
\]

The prime must mean an independent copy of the whole vector. For each centered coordinate with `|V_i| <= b_i` and `v_i = Var(V_i)`, put `U = V_i V_i'/sigma^2`. Then `E[U] = 0`, `|U| <= b_i^2/sigma^2`, and `E[U^2] = v_i^2/sigma^4`. Taylor's integral remainder gives

\[
e^u\le 1+u+\tfrac12e^{|u|}u^2,
\qquad
\mathbb E e^U-1\le \frac{e^{b_i^2/\sigma^2}v_i^2}{2\sigma^4}.
\]

Thus

\[
\chi^2(P\|Q)\le
\exp\!\left(\sum_i\frac{e^{b_i^2/\sigma^2}v_i^2}{2\sigma^4}\right)-1,
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\le \tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P\|Q)}.
\]

For rare coordinates `X_i`, suppose `|X_i| <= A w` and `Pr(X_i != 0) <= C w`, with independent coordinates and `V_i = X_i - E[X_i]`. Then `|V_i| <= 2 A w`, `v_i <= C A^2 w^3`, and, for fixed `A,C,sigma` and small `w`, the exponent is at most a constant times `d w^6`. Also `|E[X_i]| <= C A w^2`, so

\[
\|\mathbb EX\|_2\le CA\sqrt d\,w^2.
\]

At `w=d^(-1/4)`, this permits a mean norm of order one while the one-query output `X+Z` is within `O(d^(-1/4))` TV of `N(E[X], sigma^2 I_d)`. Here “aggregate mean shift” means Euclidean norm; the sum of the coordinate means can instead be order `sqrt(d)`.

For the vector result, define a centered proxy by

\[
\mathbb E e^{t^\top V}\le e^{t^\top S t/2}\quad(t\in\mathbb R^d),\qquad S\succeq0.
\]

Conditioning on `V` and applying this bound to `V'` gives

\[
\mathbb E_{V'}e^{V^\top V'/\sigma^2}\le
e^{V^\top S V/(2\sigma^4)}.
\]

Use the Gaussian identity `exp(V^T S V/(2 sigma^4)) = E_H exp(H^T S^(1/2)V/sigma^2)`, where `H ~ N(0,I_d)`. Applying the proxy bound again and integrating the Gaussian quadratic form yields

\[
1+\chi^2(P\|Q)\le\det(I-S^2/\sigma^4)^{-1/2},
\qquad \|S\|_{op}<\sigma^2.
\]

No coordinate independence is used in this version. For `X=g(G)`, with `G ~ N(0,I_d)` and `g:R^d -> R^d` globally `L`-Lipschitz in Euclidean norm, each scalar projection `t^T g(G)` is `L||t||_2`-Lipschitz. Gaussian concentration therefore makes `g(G)-m` subGaussian with proxy `L^2 I_d`. Averaging `k` independent queries gives `S=L^2 I_d/k`, hence

\[
\chi^2(\mathcal L(\bar X+Z)\|N(m,\sigma^2I_d))
\le \left(1-\frac{L^4}{k^2\sigma^4}\right)^{-d/2}-1,
\quad k>L^2/\sigma^2.
\]

Solving the TV bound `0.5 sqrt(chi^2) <= epsilon` gives the exact sufficient threshold

\[
k\ge \frac{L^2/\sigma^2}{\sqrt{1-\exp[-2\log(1+4\varepsilon^2)/d]}}.
\]

For small epsilon and large d, this is

\[
k\sim \frac{L^2}{\sigma^2}\frac{\sqrt d}{\sqrt8\,\varepsilon}.
\]

The executable count is the larger of one and the ceiling of the sufficient threshold. If L=0, one query still reveals the unknown constant mean m; the asymptotic expression without this floor incorrectly tends to zero. For positive L, the bound counts whole-vector queries, or kd real coordinates read. Summation costs O(kd) arithmetic and O(d) working storage. It assumes L and sigma (or certified bounds for them) are known when choosing k.

## A fully N687-compatible rare-bump example

The rare-coordinate scaling is realizable by a conservative globally Lipschitz field, rather than only by an abstract product law. Set phi(t)=(1-|t|)_+ and w=d^(-1/4), and define:

\[
g_i(x)=Aw\,\phi(x_i/w),\qquad
F(x)=Aw^2\sum_{i=1}^d\int_0^{x_i/w}\phi(s)\,ds.
\]

Then g is the gradient of F, F is C^{1,1}, and:

\[
\|g(x)-g(y)\|_2^2\le A^2\|x-y\|_2^2.
\]

For standard Gaussian input G, the coordinates X_i=g_i(G) are independent and satisfy |X_i|<=Aw. Here phi_N denotes the standard normal density; the exact one-coordinate integrals give:

\[
\Pr(X_i\ne0)=\Pr(|G_i|<w)\le\sqrt{2/\pi}\,w,
\quad \mathbb E X_i=A\phi_{\rm N}(0)w^2+O(Aw^4),
\quad \operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\tfrac23A^2\phi_{\rm N}(0)w^3+O(A^2w^4),
\]

For fixed A and sigma, the choice w=d^(-1/4) gives:

\[
\|m\|_2\longrightarrow A/\sqrt{2\pi},\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L(g(G)+Z),N(m,\sigma^2I_d))=O(d^{-1/4})
\]


Independence in the rare-coordinate claim is essential. A finite check with all coordinates equal to one rare centered Bernoulli variable has the same bounded and rare coordinate marginals, but at d=10^4 and w=0.1 its exact one-plus-chi-square value is 1.51e33; incorrectly multiplying marginal bounds would suggest a small exponent 0.0041. General N687 fields need not have independent coordinates; use the vector-subGaussian theorem unless independence has been proved for the particular field and input distribution.

## Interface, cost, and finite precision

The N687 route is a deterministic exact-value oracle for a globally Lipschitz conservative field g=grad F, queried on chosen centers, with exact-real Gaussian seeds. It does not supply F, derivatives, VJPs, conditional means, or a mean oracle. The sampler uses k independent standard-Gaussian centers, k exact whole-vector values, and a separately generated independent Gaussian output-noise vector. The query cap is deterministic, and no derivative or hidden encoder is assumed. The conservative structure is unused; the same bound holds for every globally Lipschitz vector map.

This establishes an upper bound on the law of a sampler output. The oracle still returns each exact g(G_j) value before the caller adds noise. Data processing only says that the deliberately noised output has no more information than that exact response; it gives no upper bound on information in the raw response. The comparison target is N(m,sigma^2 I) with the same unknown mean m. For fields with means m_0 and m_1, their target Gaussians have:

\[
\operatorname{TV}(N(m_0,\sigma^2I),N(m_1,\sigma^2I))
=2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\|m_0-m_1\|_2}{2\sigma}\right)-1.
\]

Thus a constant-size mean difference remains a constant target separation; the lemma does not put both outputs near a common reference distribution. A lower bound or information-hiding argument must separately compare raw oracle transcripts or establish that competing target means are close.

The query and arithmetic statement is in the ideal-real model. If outputs are compared after a common finite-bit quantizer Q, data processing gives TV(Q(Y),Q(N(m,sigma^2 I)))<=epsilon. That does not certify finite-bit Gaussian input generation, output-noise generation, evaluation or rounding of exact oracle values, or end-to-end arithmetic error. A finite-support digital output compared to the unrounded continuous Gaussian instead has TV 1. These are separate implementation costs, not flaws in the ideal-real inequality.

## Prior-art boundary and consequence for N687

The closest local prior-art route is current capital dossier N722. It treats an iid bounded-vector oracle X in R^d with certified norm at most R, unknown mean, and sampling N(E[X],I_d). Its source explicitly records the sample-mean-plus-independent-Gaussian baseline TV<=R^2/(2n), then identifies stochastic FORS as the stronger matched bounded-oracle comparator. N722’s positive factory has an expected acquisition count polynomial in R and logarithmic in 1/epsilon, independent of d; reading its whole-vector samples and arithmetic still cost O(d m). This overlap rules out treating “average unknown vectors and add Gaussian noise” itself as a new sampler architecture.

The interface mismatch remains real: g(G) is unbounded for a general Lipschitz field on a Gaussian input, while N722 assumes a known almost-sure radius and expected iid acquisition. Applying it to N687 would need a certified truncation and mean-bias conversion plus an expected-to-every-execution cost conversion. Neither follows just from the N722 theorem.

Primary literature likewise contains nearby mechanisms: [Nielsen and Nock (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3029) give closed-form chi-square distances for members of the same exponential family and instantiate them for isotropic Gaussians. That is relevant background for the fixed Gaussian location-family calculation, not the exact random-mixture/proxy theorem derived here. [Chen–Chewi–Daskalakis–Rakhlin's 2026 FORS paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01338) constructs exponential tilts from bounded unbiased first-order estimators; [their stochastic-query paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14342) applies FORS to stochastic values and gradients. [Goldfeld–Greenewald–Polyanskiy–Weed](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13576) study TV and chi-square convergence for Gaussian-smoothed empirical measures. These are related ingredients or nearby problems, not a claim that the exact proxy determinant bound above is printed in those papers. The local N722 proof makes the closest bounded-iid comparison; the external review here was selective, not exhaustive.

For N687's globally L-Lipschitz Gaussian-input model, the generic direct bound is an explicit deterministic query cap `O(1 + (L^2/sigma^2)*sqrt(d)/epsilon)`, with `O(kd)` real arithmetic and no mean estimation. At fixed sigma/L>0, its dimension dependence is sqrt(d), not polylogarithmic. The rare-coordinate argument gives a much smaller error only when independence and small coordinate variances are justified. N687 already records a fixed-exponent `d^a * poly_a(1/epsilon)` candidate for every fixed a>0, while its polylogarithmic query goal remains open; this lemma supplies a simple, independently derived baseline and a structured special case, not closure of that gate.
