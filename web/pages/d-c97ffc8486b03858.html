# Cycle 3: the KL-to-prefix boundary for adaptive acquisition

**Result.** A teacher-free passive acquisition lower bound survives the addition of an exact chosen-prefix *teacher* when the target is uniform accuracy at every supplied prefix. The lower bound is sharp in interface: a single chosen-prefix conditional sample from the unknown source resolves the hidden bit. It does **not** refute the A06 objective, which measures prediction error on source-generated paths. The same construction is already well predicted by A06's stopped-prefix Hellinger accounting, so this is a scope boundary rather than a new canonical impossibility theorem.

## Interface tested

The pinned A06 page specifies an unknown finite-horizon HMM source (P), independent complete (P)-trajectories, and one fixed positive exact-rational teacher evaluator (Q), with (mathrm{KL}(P\Vert Q)\le\zeta). Acquisition may use (Q) at chosen prefixes. Deployment must be a finite-precision recurrence with bounded retained stream state and bounded linear readouts of future tasks; no post-acquisition (Q)-calls or full-prefix register are allowed. The scored error is actual-path MSE (O(\zeta)+\epsilon^2), not a worst-case conditional guarantee. See the pinned [A06 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83/web/pages/d-01db0c364a53b02c.html), target lines 18–31 and average-prefix lemma lines 82–125.

We test the strictly stronger contract that the returned next-symbol predictor must be within (1/3) TV at every explicitly supplied prefix, including prefixes that are exponentially rare under (P). The construction uses only alphabet size two, an (O(n))-state clock-and-flag HMM, transition/emission probabilities in \(\{0,1/2,1\}\), and an (O(n))-bit teacher description.

## Two worlds, one identical teacher

Let (X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent fair bits. For (b\in\{0,1\}), define (P_b) by making (Y=b) deterministically when (X=0^n), and otherwise making (Y) fair. Let (Q) be the uniform law on all (n+1) bits. It is the same in both worlds, is positive, and returns every next-bit conditional as exactly (1/2). Put \(\rho=2^{-n}\). The laws differ only on the rare word (X=0^n), so

\[
\mathrm{KL}(P_b\Vert Q)=\rho\ln 2=:\zeta_n,\qquad
\mathrm{TV}(P_b,Q)=\rho/2,\qquad
\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)=\rho.
\]

Both source worlds satisfy the same complete-law KL promise against the same teacher. The source is an HMM by storing a clock and one flag indicating whether all input bits so far were zero.

## Theorem: uniform-prefix accuracy costs inverse mass

Any randomized procedure given (m) independent full (P_b)-trajectories and an unlimited number of exact (Q)-queries cannot, for both (b), return a predictor with TV error at most (1/3) at (h^*=0^n) with success probability at least (2/3), if (m\le 2^{n-2}).

**Proof.** The two trajectory laws are identical unless the input is (0^n), which occurs with probability \(\rho\). Thus

\[
\mathrm{TV}(P_0^{\otimes m},P_1^{\otimes m})=1-(1-\rho)^m\le m\rho\le 1/4.
\]

Every answer from (Q) is identical in both worlds, so even an unbounded teacher transcript cannot increase this total variation. A next-bit predictor within (1/3) TV of the deterministic truth at (h^*) identifies (b) by thresholding its probability of (Y=1) at (1/2). Its purported guarantee would give a two-point test with success at least (2/3). Yet the optimal equal-prior testing success is ((1+\mathrm{TV})/2\le5/8), contradiction. Therefore more than (2^{n-2}=\Omega(1/\zeta_n)) complete trajectories, or \(\Omega(n2^n)\) observed symbols, are required for this uniform target. \(\square\)

This is an information-theoretic bound with unrestricted computation. A source-conditional chosen-prefix sample at (h^*) returns (b) in one call; specifying the prefix costs (n) bits. A (Q)-conditional sample gives a fair bit and is uninformative. These are different services.

## Boundary: the canonical actual-path objective remains attainable here

At the cut after (t\le n) input bits, only prefix (0^t) has differing continuations. It has (P_b)-mass (2^{-t}), while the conditional TV to (Q) is (2^{-(n-t+1)}). Consequently, for every such cut,

\[
\mathbb E_{h\sim P_b}\!\left[\mathrm{TV}(P_b(\cdot\mid h),Q(\cdot\mid h))^2\right]
=2^{-2n+t-2}\le 2^{-n}/4=\zeta_n/(4\ln 2).
\]

Every bounded future-task conditional-mean error is at most the conditional TV. Hence the one-state teacher recurrence itself has actual-path MSE (O(\zeta_n)) for this pair. The counterexample rules out upgrading a complete-law KL promise to mass-free uniform prefix accuracy; it says nothing against A06's average-path guarantee or its static-to-sequential construction.

## Costs, evidence, and prior-art boundary

| Service | Cost to identify (b) | What it can observe |
|---|---:|---|
| Passive (P_b) data | (>2^{n-2}) trajectories necessary for uniform-prefix guarantee | (n+1) symbols per trajectory; rare target prefix occurs with probability (2^{-n}) |
| Exact chosen-prefix (Q) evaluation/sample | No finite number suffices | Same fair conditional answer in both worlds |
| Chosen-prefix conditional sample from (P_b) | One call | One terminal bit, deterministically (b) at (0^n) |

No coefficient precision, cryptographic assumption, or computation bound drives this example. The A06 source's KL/Hellinger lemma already predicts the actual-path boundary. Conditional-prefix sampling is a stronger source oracle: Mahajan et al. (COLT 2023) and Liu–Moitra (STOC 2025) define chosen-prefix sampling from the unknown law, which is not supplied here. These primary results are linked in the child report. This cycle makes no novelty or priority claim.
