## Input, output and promised primitive

Input: a finite simple graph G on n vertices promised K_r-free; integer r>=2; positive rational vertex weights w; rational 0<epsilon<=1/2. Let

`s=sum_v w_v`, `M_G(w)=sum_{uv in E, unordered}w_u w_v`.

Output: pairwise disjoint nonempty terminal blocks A_j partitioning the vertices, rational probabilities p_j>0 with `sum_j p_j<=1`, and one optional empty atom carrying the remaining probability. At atom j set

`m_v=1/p_j` for v in A_j, and `m_v=0` elsewhere.

The source's mean-preserving decorrelation guarantee becomes

`E m_v=1`, `0<=m_v<=B_r(epsilon)=2^(r(r-2))epsilon^(-(r-2))`,

`E M_G(w*m)<=epsilon s^2`.

Our compact output has at most n+1 atoms and uses one vertex partition plus atom probabilities. No dense n by n support table is required. Coordinate means and expected edge mass can be checked exactly without sampling:

`E m_v=p_j(1/p_j)=1`,

`E M_G(w*m)=sum_j M_{G[A_j]}(w)/p_j`.

This is a correlated diagonal law. Independent mean-one multipliers would give `E m_u m_v=1` for distinct vertices and preserve the original expected edge mass; correlation is the essential service.

## Complete reconstruction of the recursive construction

For r=2 the graph has no edges: return the whole vertex set with probability1 and every multiplier1. The empty input returns the empty atom.

For r>=3 set eta=epsilon/4. Start with all vertices remaining. Whenever a remaining vertex has open-neighborhood weight greater than eta*s in the remaining induced graph, remove that neighborhood as a block V_i. Continue until no such vertex remains; call the remainder R. The V_i are disjoint, each has weight s_i>eta*s, and each excludes K_{r-1}: a clique in the neighborhood together with its chosen center would create K_r. R satisfies

`M_R(w)<=eta*s*w(R)/2<=eta*s^2/2`.

If no neighborhoods were removed, return the original whole-set atom. Its mass already meets the target, even with a factor1/8 to spare.

Otherwise let `b=sum_i s_i` and `p_i=s_i/(2b)`. Choose R with probability1/2 and use multiplier2 on R. Choose V_i with probability p_i and apply the (r-1)-construction inside it at accuracy epsilon/4, multiplying its returned multipliers by1/p_i. All multipliers outside the chosen part are zero.

Every vertex's expectation is one. Edges between different selected parts disappear. The expected edge mass is bounded by

`2M_R(w)+sum_i (epsilon/4)s_i^2/p_i`

`<=eta*s^2+(epsilon/2)b^2 <=(3/4)epsilon*s^2`.

No independence between vertices is invoked. For the magnitude, `p_i>epsilon/8`, and therefore

`max m_v <= max{2,(8/epsilon)B_{r-1}(epsilon/4)} = B_r(epsilon)`.

The equality follows by substituting the stated power of2 and epsilon exponent. This completes the induction.

Flattening the recursive selection tree proves the compact-output claim. Its nonempty terminal sets partition the input vertices: each internal split partitions its own set into its removed neighborhoods and remainder. Along a root-to-leaf chain the multiplier is exactly the reciprocal of the leaf selection probability. Merge all empty outcomes into one zero-vector atom. There are at most n nonempty leaves and one empty atom.

## Acquisition cost, without a global optimizer oracle

`work/frontier_combin3/cycle19/sparse_partition_law.py` implements this construction with Python `Fraction`. It reads no release scripts. Weighted degrees are maintained while deleting neighborhoods. At one recursion level the vertex sets are disjoint, and each original edge is scanned only a bounded number of times for degree initialization/deletion. A simple linear search for the next center costs O(n^2) per level. The recursion depth is at most r-2. Thus construction uses O(r(n^2+|E|)) rational operations, apart from the optional clique-promise check.

Let L be the total binary encoding of the rational weights and epsilon. Subset-weight sums have O(L+log n) bits; the nested accuracy parameters add O(r) bits. Each leaf probability is a product of at most r rational ratios of subset weights, with O(r(L+r+log n)) bit length. The optional empty probability is a sum/complement of at most n such probabilities, so it has polynomial bit length as well. Elementary exact rational arithmetic therefore gives polynomial bit cost in n,r,L for the promised-input constructor. The numeric B_r may be huge; its binary encoding and the output denominators are charged. There is no claim that its dependence on r or epsilon gives small practical constants for all inputs.

The K_r-free promise can be checked by enumerating r-subsets in O(n^r r^2) graph operations. This is polynomial for fixed r, and costly if r grows. The constructor itself only detects violations that reach an invalid r=2 base; it is not a general clique-promise recognizer.

The output does not itself give a large independent set or low-coloring assignment. Terminal remainders can retain edges, the target is absolute interaction mass epsilon*s^2, and a deterministic output may have larger total weight than s. Those facts are part of the exact interface.

## An exact obstruction to replacing the source's global optimizer

The main independence proof uses

`F_G(w)=sum_v w_v(1-log w_v)-M_G(w)`

at a **global maximizer**. This choice is attributed by the source to the entropy-edge potential in Davies, *Random independent sets and local sparsity*, arXiv2609.04654v1 (submitted September4; manuscript dated September3), Theorem17 and Section3.3. That source assumes sparse neighborhoods for its own conclusions. No new variational objective is claimed here.

The stationarity condition `log(1/w_v)=w(N(v))` is necessary for a maximizer, but it cannot replace the global comparison inequality in Lemma2.3. Here is a uniform counterexample to that substitution.

Take G=K_{D,D} with integer D, and give every vertex weight `w=t/D`, where t is the positive solution `t e^t=D`. Every vertex has neighborhood weight t, and `log(1/w)=t`, so all stationarity equations hold exactly. For the two parts S,D' the set weights are both t and the directed cross mass is t^2. Choose `x=log t>=1`. The alleged cross-mass conclusion at r=4, `C_4=16*4^2=256`, would require

`t^2<=256 t log t`, i.e. `t/log t<=256`.

That fails as integer D tends to infinity. A concrete exact side certificate uses D=2^4096: rational Taylor bounds certify `0.69<log2<0.70` and `exp8>2868`; hence `2800<t<2868` and `log t<8`. Therefore `t/log t>350>256`.

This stationary point is not a maximizer. Its value is `t^2+2t`, whereas assigning weight1 to one part and0 to the other gives feasible value D. In the concrete certificate, `t^2+2t<2^24<D`. The Hessian along the difference of the two part-constant vectors has second derivative `2D^2(1-1/t)>0`, making the stationary point a saddle whenever t>1.

Code/certificate: `work/frontier_combin3/cycle19/stationary_obstruction.py`, `results/frontier_combin3/cycle19/stationary_obstruction_certificate.json`. The graph is defined by its complete-bipartite formula; it was not expanded into2D vertices, and no optimization run on that graph is claimed. The proof, not a finite dense computation, supplies the family.

The decisive acquisition obstacle for a useful independence algorithm is consequently the global variational service or a substitute certificate guaranteeing its needed comparison inequality. A fixed-point solver returning a stationary vector does not suffice. The source explicitly claims existence rather than an efficient algorithm, so this is a boundary on proposed reuse, not a contradiction of its theorem.

## Full-read mechanism ledger and novelty status

The source's full proof proceeds from the exact primitive above to a cross-mass inequality at global maxima. That inequality survives vertex splitting when original directed edges are not duplicated (Lemma3.2). Weighted neighborhood walks then have average entropy O_C(log x) and a common step with small triangle-averaged row distance (Lemma4.1). Shared rejection sampling gives a joint coupling; a reversed-transition cutoff bounds each split neighborhood by exp(sqrt x), while triangle loss is O_C(log x/sqrt x) (Lemmas5.1-5.3). Iterating x->sqrt x incurs summable losses and produces triangle-to-edge control (Theorem3.1). Closed-neighborhood deletion at controlled average potential cost yields the independence bound (Proposition6.1), followed by deletion of high-degree vertices.
