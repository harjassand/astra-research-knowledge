STRONGEST CLAIM

Let d=2r and let W have d rows and 2m columns partitioned into labeled two-column blocks W_i=(u_i,v_i). For a set S of r blocks, write A_S for the ordered d-by-d matrix containing both columns of each selected block and Δ_S=det A_S. Assume Δ_S and Δ_T are nonzero for two r-block sets S,T. Fix i in S\T. Order the columns of A_S as [A_rest,A_i], with A_i the two columns of block i, and order A_T as b_1,...,b_d. For each two-subset K={p,q} of [d], with p<q, define

  A'_K=[A_rest,b_p,b_q],
  B'_K=[b_j : j not in K, in increasing order, A_i].

Then the exact two-column Laplace identity is

  Δ_S Δ_T = sum_{K in ([d] choose 2)} eps_K det(A'_K) det(B'_K),

where eps_K is the sign of the permutation (K^c,K) of [d]. Any K that uses a column from a block in S∩T gives det(A'_K)=0 because that column duplicates one already in A_rest. Thus the sum can be restricted to the 2|T\S| columns of T\S. With p=|T\S|=|S\T| and N=binom(2p,2), Cauchy–Schwarz gives

  |Δ_S|^2 |Δ_T|^2
    <= N sum_{K subset columns(T\S), |K|=2}
         |det(A'_K)|^2 |det(B'_K)|^2,

with N<=binom(d,2). The inequality is pointwise, applies to complex as well as real columns, and has no lower bound on any individual exchanged determinant.

If K is the two columns of one block j, both outputs are again paired block bases (S-i+j,T-j+i). If K takes one column from each of two different blocks, the outputs are ordinary full column bases with two split-block mismatches. Keep the register (i,K) with the two output bases. It records exactly which columns moved, makes the transformation invertible, and exposes the mismatch rather than projecting it away. Given the tag and outputs, the inputs are recovered by restoring A_i to A'_K and restoring b_p,b_q to B'_K. This gives an explicit exchange distribution

  q(K | S,T,i) = |det(A'_K)det(B'_K)|^2 / Q,
  Q = sum_K |det(A'_K)det(B'_K)|^2.

Because Δ_SΔ_T is nonzero, the identity implies Q>0 and the Cauchy bound implies |Δ_SΔ_T|^2/Q<=N. Thus source product mass can be routed to tagged output pairs with at most N load relative to the determinant-product output weight; each tagged output has one preimage for fixed i. This is a computable two-replica block-volume exchange on the enlarged mismatch space. It is not a Markov transition on paired configurations alone.

HARD-BCS INTERFACE

For an even retained coordinate set R with |R|=d=2r, take W_R=[I_R,F_R^T], with pair block i given by e_i and the transpose of row i of F, restricted to R. For S subset R, |S|=r, expanding the identity columns shows

  det(W_{R,P(S)}) = +/- det F[S,R\S],

so the full-paired partition Z_R(F)=sum_S |det F[S,R\S]|^2 is exactly a paired block-volume law. The exchange theorem therefore applies directly to the microscopic states underlying this full-basis partition, including the coordinate-pair auxiliary systems in which each state is a full paired basis. It transfers the standard determinantal basis-exchange identity to a pair-block move with one- or two-block mismatch registers. The transfer is algebraic; no partition coefficient f(U) is needed to evaluate a proposed term.

DERIVATION

Write A_T=B and C=B^{-1}A_S. Partition the columns of C as [C_rest,C_i], with C_i having two columns. Laplace-expand det C along those last two columns. Each term is indexed by a two-row set K and equals eps_K det(C_{K^c,rest})det(C_{K,i}). Expanding det[A_rest,B_K] along the two B_K columns gives det(B) times the first minor, with its Laplace sign. Expanding det[B_{K^c},A_i] along the identity columns B_{K^c} gives det(B) times the second minor, with the complementary permutation sign. Their product, multiplied by eps_K, is det(B)^2 times the K-th Laplace term. Summing and using det A_S=det(B)det(C) proves the identity. This argument also proves the identity when some exchanged determinants vanish by continuity, or directly because it is polynomial in the entries.

The squared inequality follows by applying |sum_{K=1}^N x_K|^2<=N sum_K |x_K|^2. No positivity of the signed terms is assumed. The register is necessary because K can split two pair blocks; it is not safe to retain only whole-block K.

EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE TO PROJECTING AWAY THE REGISTER

Let d=4 and let F be the directed 4-cycle permutation matrix F[i,j]=1 iff j=i+1 mod 4 (indices 0,1,2,3). Use pair blocks (e_i,row_i(F)). In the sector r=2, the paired basis determinant det F[S,S^c] is nonzero only for S={0,2} and S={1,3}; both have magnitude one. These two support points differ at all four coordinates. Every whole-block exchange term from one to the other has zero determinant product, so the target-only single-pair exchange graph is disconnected. In the block Laplace identity there are six K terms: the whole-block terms carry total squared mass zero, while a split-block mismatch term carries all the nonzero mass. The exact check records target product squared 1, whole-block mass 0, split-block mass 1, and verifies the six-term factor bound. Thus the Plucker transfer survives exactly when the mismatch register is retained; projecting it to a paired-only move destroys the inequality on this instance.

This does not contradict the coefficient-one aggregate square-root exchange theorem in work/cycle5/stable_parity_exchange.txt. That theorem controls sums over microscopic configurations; it does not identify which determinant terms realize an aggregate exchange.

COMPUTABLE KERNEL AND COSTS

For each fixed source pair and selected i, Q is a sum of at most binom(d,2) products of exact determinant-squared weights. A q_K proposal can therefore be evaluated without Z_R, c_k, f(U), or another partition oracle. For rational-complex input with total bit length L, clear the input denominators once. Hadamard bounds the bit length of every d-by-d determinant by O(d(L+log d)); products and Q retain polynomial bit length. Fraction-free elimination uses O(d^3) arithmetic operations per determinant. Evaluating all terms costs O(d^5) arithmetic operations per source pair and O(d^2) determinant storage if terms are streamed. The tag needs O(log d) bits for i and O(log d) bits per column label in K; the two output bases use O(d log m) bits. Rational proposal probabilities can be sampled exactly with expected polynomial random bits, or with a fixed b-bit uniform grid and total-variation rounding error at most binom(d,2)/2^b. All arithmetic and sampling precision is charged; no rejection by an unknown partition mass occurs.

This cost is per exchange proposal, not a complete FPRAS cost. It does not show that the q_K chain is reversible, that it returns to the paired fiber efficiently, or that its total stationary mass over mismatches is comparable to the target partition. Normalizing the source law still requires a sampling/counting method, and the paired target itself is the unresolved hard-BCS sampler.
