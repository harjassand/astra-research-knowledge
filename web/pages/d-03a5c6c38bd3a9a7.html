N11 — PLASMA RECONNECTION: INITIAL RESEARCH RECORD (FROZEN v1)
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: internally derived exact bounds and finite diagnostic; proposed synthesis is a candidate, novelty unresolved; sustained fast reconnection remains unresolved.

TARGET AND SCOPE

The target is a closure-free quantitative account of the joint constraints on (i) resistivity-independent magnetic-flux transfer, (ii) magnetic-helicity retention, and (iii) magnetic-energy release in a high-Lundquist-number plasma. The working distinction is between a rate at an instant and an O(1) amount of flux transfer sustained over an Alfvénic time. A singular initial sheet can establish the former without establishing the latter.

The clean global theorem below is for unforced incompressible resistive MHD on a periodic three-torus of side L, with constant magnetic diffusivity eta_m and zero mean magnetic flux. The explicit rate counterexample is a z-invariant two-dimensional periodic field with zero guide field, also in resistive MHD. Neither model directly describes an open, line-tied solar corona. No archived Astra mathematical claim was used.

Notation and equations. Use SI units, magnetic field B, velocity u, permeability mu_0, and magnetic diffusivity eta_m (so electrical resistivity is mu_0 eta_m):

  partial_t B = curl(u x B) + eta_m Delta B,       div B = div u = 0,
  E + u x B = eta_m curl B = mu_0 eta_m J.

Set E_B=(2 mu_0)^(-1) integral |B|^2 dV and D_eta=(eta_m/mu_0) integral |curl B|^2 dV. For a periodic zero-mean field, choose periodic Coulomb A with curl A=B and define H=integral A dot B dV. All fields and integrals below are regular enough for the integrations by parts; each member of the explicit eta-family is smooth.

1. EXACT JOINT ENERGY–HELICITY BOUND

The periodic helicity balance is

  dH/dt = -2 integral E dot B dV
         = -2 eta_m integral B dot curl B dV.

The u x B term drops pointwise. By Cauchy–Schwarz and the definitions,

  |dH/dt|
    <= 2 eta_m ||B||_2 ||curl B||_2
    = 2 mu_0 sqrt(2 eta_m E_B D_eta).                       (1)

For any T, a second Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

  |H(T)-H(0)|
    <= 2 mu_0 sqrt(2 eta_m)
       (integral_0^T E_B dt)^(1/2) (integral_0^T D_eta dt)^(1/2).  (2)

If total kinetic-plus-magnetic energy is unforced and its only losses are viscous and Ohmic, then E_B(t)<=E_tot(0) and integral D_eta dt<=E_tot(0). Thus

  |H(T)-H(0)| <= 2 mu_0 E_tot(0) sqrt(2 eta_m T).             (3)

This is an O(sqrt(eta_m)) finite-time helicity-drift bound at fixed T, conditional on a uniform energy budget. It allows an O(1) integrated Ohmic loss at the same time. It does not imply that a fast-reconnection solution exists.

The periodic curl operator has helical Fourier modes with eigenvalues +/-|k| and |k|>=k_1=2 pi/L. For each mode, the vector potential amplitude is +/-B_k/|k|, so

  |H| <= sum_k |B_k|^2/|k| <= (1/k_1) integral |B|^2 dV
      = (2 mu_0/k_1) E_B.

Therefore

  E_B(t) >= k_1 |H(t)|/(2 mu_0)
         >= k_1 (|H(0)|-delta_H(T))_+/(2 mu_0),              (4)

where delta_H(T) is the right-hand side of (2), or of (3) when its extra hypotheses hold. Equality in the first inequality is attained by a lowest-|k| curl eigenfield of the helicity sign. Equivalently, varying magnetic energy at fixed H gives curl B=lambda B; the lowest admissible curl magnitude gives the global minimum in this periodic zero-mean setting.

Consequently the magnetic energy decrease over [0,T] is at most

  E_B(0) - k_1 (|H(0)|-delta_H(T))_+/(2 mu_0).               (5)

With no forcing, total energy dissipated as heat/viscous loss is also bounded above by E_tot(0) minus this residual magnetic floor. Equation (5) is a cap, not a prediction that the system reaches the floor. Field-line mapping and other topology can impose a higher minimum; the Taylor relaxation hypothesis that global helicity is the only surviving constraint is not a theorem for arbitrary astrophysical geometry.

2. NECESSARY CONCENTRATION LAW FOR FAST RESISTIVE NONIDEALITY

Define the nonideal induction field N_eta=eta_m curl B. Then

  D_eta = (1/(mu_0 eta_m)) integral |N_eta|^2 dV.

For any threshold e_*>0, set Omega_*(t)={x: |N_eta(x,t)|>=e_*}. Directly,

  |Omega_*| <= mu_0 eta_m D_eta/e_*^2.                        (6)

Thus if instantaneous Ohmic power is uniformly O(1) and the nonideal field remains O(1) on Omega_*, its volume must be O(eta_m). For a two-dimensional field invariant in z this is an area-per-unit-z statement. A sheet of fixed in-plane length can have thickness O(eta_m); a roughly isotropic core can have radius O(sqrt(eta_m)). The bound is exact but only necessary. It does not say that a nonideal set of this size reconnects a specified amount of flux, and it does not select its geometry or rate.

If curl B is uniformly bounded along every relevant field-line segment of length at most L_gamma, then the line voltage obeys

  R_line=|integral_gamma N_eta dot dl| <= eta_m L_gamma ||curl B||_infty,

so it vanishes with eta_m. A finite line voltage at bounded segment length therefore requires loss of uniform current regularity. Conversely, the volume energy bound alone cannot control a line integral: a curve has zero volume measure, and an additional tube/coherence or trace-regularity assumption is required. For a tube of fixed cross-section A, length ell, and approximately uniform transverse current, Cauchy–Schwarz yields the conditional estimate

  P_tube >= A R_line^2/(mu_0 eta_m ell).                     (7)

At fixed finite power and nonzero line voltage, such a coherent tube must have A=O(eta_m). Without the transverse-uniformity assumption, (7) is not asserted.

In 2-D, write B=(partial_y A,-partial_x A,0). At critical points X and O of A (saddle and island extremum), the flux difference Psi=A(X)-A(O) satisfies

  dPsi/dt = eta_m [Delta A(X)-Delta A(O)]                    (8)

because u dot grad A vanishes at each critical point. This makes the singular-limit issue explicit: a uniformly bounded Delta A gives a rate O(eta_m), while a rate with a nonzero eta_m->0 limit requires a curvature difference O(1/eta_m). Equation (8) is for 2-D resistive MHD; it is not a general 3-D reconnection-rate definition.

Together, (2), (4), and (6) yield a closure-free compatibility envelope: finite energy loss and finite local nonideal electric field can coexist on a shrinking support; global helicity changes only O(sqrt(eta_m)) over fixed time under the stated energy assumptions; and the net magnetic energy release cannot exceed the helicity floor. This is a useful constraint set but not a rate-selection law.

3. EXACT SAME-INVARIANTS COUNTEREXAMPLE FOR THE RATE

This construction tests whether total magnetic energy and total magnetic helicity determine an instantaneous high-S flux-transfer rate. They do not.

Take the square torus T^2=[-pi,pi)^2, unit length in the invariant z direction, mu_0=1, eta_m=epsilon, and u(0)=0. Let

  A_0(x,y)=cos x - cos y,
  B=(A_y,-A_x,0).

There is an X saddle at X=(0,0) and an O maximum at O=(0,pi) (pi is identified with -pi). The base field has Delta A_0(X)=0 and Delta A_0(O)=-2. Hence (8) gives R_smooth=2 epsilon. Its magnetic helicity is exactly zero because the periodic vector potential can be chosen as A_vec=A_0 zhat while B has no z component.

Choose a compactly supported C^3 function

  f(q)=-q^2(1-q^2)^4/2 for |q|<1, and f(q)=0 for |q|>=1,
  g(y)=(1+cos y)/2,
  A_epsilon=A_0+epsilon f(x/epsilon)g(y).

At q=0, f=0, f'=0, f''=-1. Also g(0)=1, g(pi)=0 and g'(0)=g'(pi)=0. Thus X and O remain critical points and

  Delta A_epsilon(X)=-1/epsilon,   Delta A_epsilon(O)=-2,
  R_epsilon=epsilon |Delta A_epsilon(X)-Delta A_epsilon(O)|
           = |1-2 epsilon| -> 1.

Multiply A_epsilon by s_epsilon=sqrt(E_0/E_epsilon), where E_epsilon is its magnetic energy and E_0 is the energy of A_0. Then the fast and smooth fields have exactly the same magnetic energy for each epsilon, and s_epsilon=1+O(epsilon) because the perturbation changes energy by O(epsilon). The rate scalings above are unchanged. Every field remains planar and has H=0 exactly.

The perturbation has B_y correction -f'(x/epsilon)g(y), O(1) amplitude on an x-strip of width O(epsilon), and B_x correction of size O(epsilon). Its magnetic energy is O(epsilon). Its current is J_z=-Delta A/mu_0=O(1/epsilon) on a strip of area O(epsilon), giving initial Ohmic power eta_m integral J_z^2 dA=O(1) per unit z. The B fields remain uniformly bounded and converge strongly in magnetic-energy norm to the smooth base field, while the X-point rate stays O(1). Thus (E_B,H) alone do not determine the rate, even within one topology class of initial data and with a bounded instantaneous power budget. The selector is the local current concentration and its placement relative to the X/O flux structure.

This is an initial-rate result. Without an inflow/outflow or other sustaining mechanism, diffusion across thickness epsilon acts on time epsilon^2/eta_m=epsilon, so the construction does not demonstrate O(1) flux transfer over an O(1) Alfvén time. That missing duration result is the decisive gap for the original astrophysical question.

4. PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON AND OBSERVATIONAL STATUS

The helicity floor is Woltjer's variational principle in a particularly clean periodic geometry; it is not new. Taylor's relaxation proposal states that finite resistivity can destroy field-line-specific constraints while approximately preserving total helicity, but the proposal does not prove universal relaxation to the helicity minimizer. Berger derived geometry-independent Cauchy–Schwarz limits on helicity dissipation from energy and Ohmic power in 1984; (1)–(3) are the constant-diffusivity SI specialization and should not be claimed as a new helicity law.

Huang and Bhattacharjee's plasmoid-dominated resistive-MHD study reports nonlinear rates nearly independent of global Lundquist number over its simulated range and gives a heuristic marginal-sheet chain: L_c~S_c eta_m/V_A, delta_c~eta_m sqrt(S_c)/V_A, eta_m J~B V_A/sqrt(S_c), and N~S/S_c. Its own construction has total active 2-D area N L_c delta_c~L delta_c=O(eta_m), the concentration scaling required by (6). This is a strong consistency check of the support law, not an independent derivation of S_c or of sustained dynamics. Their reported normalized rates 0.006–0.008 are simulation results for S=5e5–3e6 under their model and noise protocol; they are not empirical measurements of astrophysical resistivity independence.

Eyink, Lazarian, and Vishniac derive a distinct stochastic-flux-freezing account and recover LV99 rates using Goldreich–Sridhar strong-turbulence and weak-turbulence assumptions. That work is relevant as a closure-based comparator, not as evidence for the exact concentration bound.

For observational context only, Takasao et al. report simultaneous inflow/outflow in one 2010 solar flare, an estimated normalized rate changing from 0.20 to 0.055, and blobs that they hypothesize to be plasmoids. These are indirect geometric/velocity estimates from a single finite event; they do not vary eta_m and cannot establish resistivity independence. Savage et al. report an EUV sheet of length >0.25 solar radii and apparent width 5,000–10,000 km with multiple reconnection signatures. These observations motivate a sheet geometry but do not resolve the kinetic current-layer thickness or validate the eta_m->0 scaling.

5. BOUNDARY, GUIDE-FIELD, AND IDEAL-LIMIT CHECKS

Periodic zero-mean B is essential for the absolute helicity and spectral floor as written. For an open or line-tied volume with nonzero normal flux, the relevant quantity is relative helicity with a reference potential field; its balance includes boundary flux. Equations (2)–(5) cannot be transplanted without controlling that boundary term, and the energy floor depends on the domain/reference-field problem. A CME or photospheric driving can export/inject helicity, so the closed-volume budget may be inapplicable over the event interval.

The local 2-D flux equation has a clean uniform-guide-field extension: for z-invariant B=(A_y,-A_x,B_g) with constant B_g, the z component of u x B is u_x B_y-u_y B_x, which vanishes at an in-plane X/O critical point. Thus (8) still gives the instantaneous flux-function rate, and constant B_g adds no J_z. The global absolute-helicity theorem above does not extend as written because a periodic domain with nonzero guide flux has no globally periodic vector potential. Relative helicity and boundary conditions must be specified before combining that guide-field case with (4) or (5). The finite diagnostic uses B_g=0.

In the smooth ideal limit, eta_m curl B ->0 uniformly implies zero nonideal line voltage over bounded line lengths, consistent with Alfvén flux freezing. The fast counterexample instead loses uniform current bounds: B stays bounded and converges in L2, but curl B develops an O(1/epsilon) sheet. No contradiction with ideal flux-freezing theorem follows because convergence is not strong enough in derivatives. The current support law shows why finite-power singular reconnection is compatible with helicity retention.

6. ALTERNATIVE ATTEMPTS, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND EXACT GAPS

A. Use total helicity as the fast-rate selector. Rejected. Equations (8) and the explicit equal-(E,H) family refute this: the same global invariants allow R~eta_m and R~1. Helicity constrains a global energy floor, not current-sheet formation or its lifetime.

B. Use Taylor relaxation to predict all releasable energy. Rejected as universal. The helicity minimizer is only a lower bound after topology has been sufficiently mixed. Ideal field-line mapping, boundary flux, guide/net flux, and higher topological data may obstruct access to it. In the explicit planar H=0 family, the helicity floor is zero, but neither the initial rate nor the eventual relaxed state follows from H.

C. Treat plasmoid-chain scaling as the new mechanism. Rejected as a novelty claim. The S-independent rate and marginal chain are in Huang–Bhattacharjee already, with nonlinear statistical behavior and heuristic closure of the chain. Our support-area law explains an exact necessary energy-budget compatibility and makes a clean comparison, but it does not derive the plasmoid threshold, nonlinear attractor, or rate coefficient.

D. Infer line voltage from global Ohmic power alone. Fails without regularity or tube geometry. A line has zero volume measure; finite L2 current energy does not control its trace. Equation (7) is valid only under the stated transverse-coherence assumption. The rigorous geometry-independent statement available here is the high-|N_eta| volume bound (6), not a universal rate–power inequality for a single 3-D field line.

E. Count a finite instantaneous rate as a fast event. Rejected. The constructed thin sheet diffuses on time O(eta_m) without replenishment. Sustained O(1) flux transfer requires the MHD dynamics to generate/replenish the sheet or a specified boundary drive. No such macroscopic-duration solution has been constructed in this record.

F. Extend the closed periodic floor to the solar corona. Rejected without relative-helicity and boundary-flux data. Coronal domains have line-tied/photospheric driving, open CME boundaries, and often a strong guide component. The exact boundary terms are part of the problem, not small by default.

7. TESTABLE CONSEQUENCES AND NEXT DECISIVE GATE

For a resolved constant-eta resistive-MHD sequence with bounded energy and Ohmic power, any set on which |eta_m curl B|>=e_* must satisfy |Omega_*|/eta_m <= mu_0 D_eta/e_*^2. In a 2-D sheet of finite length, this predicts thickness no larger than O(eta_m) if the nonideal electric field on the sheet stays O(1). A line-voltage analysis additionally needs the sheet/tube's transverse profile; volume power alone is insufficient. Under the same fixed-time energy assumptions, the absolute helicity change must be <= the measured right side of (2), scaling at most as sqrt(eta_m).

These are falsifiable necessary diagnostics, not sufficiency claims. An apparent violation in a simulation could mean unresolved current gradients, eta-dependent power, boundary helicity flux, guide/net-flux gauge issues, a nonresistive Ohm term, or numerical dissipation. An observational comparison would require independent estimates of current-layer volume, magnetic diffusivity, energy input/output, relative-helicity flux, and reconnection flux; current solar imaging alone does not establish eta-scaling.

Next decisive research gate: construct or rule out a driven, bounded-power resistive-MHD family on an open/line-tied domain with (i) identical boundary normal flux and relative-helicity budget, (ii) total energy release O(1), and (iii) flux transfer O(1) over an O(1) Alfvén time as eta_m->0, while deriving the sheet-replenishment law from the equations rather than assuming a turbulent closure or an empirical marginal-sheet threshold. The present record has not met that gate.

8. CLAIM LEDGER

Source-reported: Woltjer (1958) fixed-helicity minimum-energy variational principle for a closed volume; Taylor (1974) relaxation conjecture; Berger (1984) rigorous helicity-dissipation bounds; Huang–Bhattacharjee simulation/heuristic plasmoid chain; solar flare morphology/rate estimates described above.

Internally derived: inequalities (1)–(7), periodic Fourier energy floor, the 2-D rate law (8), and the equal-energy/equal-helicity smooth-versus-sheet initial-data family. All derivations are scoped to the hypotheses stated here.

Finite-tested: diagnostics.py evaluates the explicit family at four epsilon values and reports energy normalization, rate, and Ohmic power. It does not integrate the PDE.

Candidate: the joint helicity–energy–concentration envelope as a closure-free necessary condition for finite-power fast resistive reconnection.

Unresolved: novelty of this synthesis; nonlinear sheet formation and replenishment; sustained O(1) fast reconnection without a closure/threshold assumption; 3-D line-trace control; relative-helicity/guide-field energy floor; application to a particular astrophysical event.

No breakthrough, priority, or empirical-validation claim is made.
