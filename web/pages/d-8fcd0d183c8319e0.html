# r04_statphys — safe coarse variables require slow-mode and tail certificates

Status: two analytic obstructions and a solvable repair; no foundational discovery or general sampler theorem claimed. ## Exact question and interface

Given a supplied local Gibbs energy E(x), an invertible linear coarse/detail transform x=A^T c+B^T d, and equilibrium training configurations, can we learn effective conditional theories and generate large fields with controlled accuracy at work proportional to their number of degrees of freedom? A and B have orthonormal rows and together form an orthogonal matrix. Costs include obtaining equilibrium training data, choosing the transform, fitting conditionals, the root distribution, and sampling/correction. The desired control must specify its observables or distribution metric and its volume dependence. Ordinary low-pass averaging is a choice of retained variables, not a definition of the slow physical subspace.

Strongest actionable subquestion: can an adaptive local block transform retain every soft ordering channel while certifying a uniform gap in the eliminated conditional Hessian? A second necessary subquestion is whether all learned factors actually normalize on their stated domains.

## Our result A: the written sigmoid root model does not normalize on R

The original arXiv TeX, not merely the converted HTML, defines

\[
 V_\theta(u)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_s}\theta_\ell
 \sigma((u-u_\ell)/\Delta_\ell),\qquad
 \sigma(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1},\qquad
 p_\theta(u)=Z_\theta^{-1}e^{-V_\theta(u)}.
\]

It states a real coarse field and a finite sigmoid family; empirical support chooses centers. We found no imposed bounded domain or confining baseline in the manuscript. Original source locations: `sources/r04_statphys/2608.31114v1.main.tex:1770` through the Eq.80 block at line 1845. The full tar archive and PDF are preserved.

Proof: 0<sigma<1 gives |V_theta(u)|<=M:=sum_l|theta_l| for all real u. Thus e^-V>=e^-M, and integral_R e^-V du=infinity. Every finite coefficient vector fails to define the written probability density. Changing sigmoid centers or widths does not cure this. An actual implementation could impose an unstated cutoff, integrate the sigmoid as a force basis, or include a polynomial tail. We have not inspected code and do not infer which implementation was used.

The cited basis prerequisite [Brossollet et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02428-z), Methods Energy parametrization, also uses translated logistic potentials. Its dynamics conserves the mean (fixed to 0.6), and its written coarsest ansatz includes a quadratic interaction matrix. Those differences matter: the present root-normalization objection must not be automatically applied to the older mean-constrained model. The 2026 unconstrained, one-dimensional sigmoid-only root is the audited interface. Snapshot and excerpt metadata are preserved.

Minimal mathematical repair: add an explicit confining term, e.g. a*u^4 with a>0, or declare a compact sampling domain and its boundary rule. The choice changes the normalized root law and therefore every descendant's order-parameter law. A bounded sigmoid expansion of a force whose integral defines the energy is another possible intended model, but that is a different formula and must be stated.

### Tail completion is not identifiable from a finite interior score objective

Let all observations lie in [-R,R], let V0 be any bounded C² energy, and set

\[
 T_a(u)=a\,(|u|-R)_+^4,\quad V_a=V_0+T_a,\quad a>0.
\]

For every observed point, T_a'=T_a''=0, including at the endpoints. Hence the empirical Hyvarinen objective, the sample mean of (V_a')²/2−V_a'', is exactly independent of a. Every model now normalizes, but its normalized distribution is not identified by that objective. On the tails, bounded V0 implies partition mass grows on the order of a^-1/4 as a approaches zero; mass in the observed interval remains bounded. Therefore p_a([-R,R]) approaches zero despite an unchanged empirical score objective.

For V0=0 the calculation is exact:

\[
 Z_a=2R+\frac{\Gamma(1/4)}{2a^{1/4}},\qquad
 p_a([-R,R])=\frac{2R}{Z_a}.
\]

`work/r04_statphys/tail_completion.py` verifies the closed forms and derivative identities. For R=2, a from 1 to 10^-12 changes interval mass from .6881 to .00220 at the same zero interior score objective. This is a generic construction, not fitted paper coefficients. It proves that tail completion needs an explicit assumption/certificate rather than an empirical-loss claim.

The supplied microscopic quartic energy contains useful tail information: for the orthonormal mean u=N^-1/2 sum_i x_i, Holder gives sum_i x_i^4>=u^4/N. A physical confining root baseline can therefore be motivated from the Hamiltonian rather than from data alone. This inequality is not itself the full integrated marginal law or a calibrated tail-error bound.

## Our result B: sharp one-step restricted-curvature certificate

Assume

\[
 E(x)=\tfrac12x^TKx+V(x),\qquad K=K^T,\qquad
 \nabla^2V(x)\succeq-\beta I\quad\hbox{for every }x.
\]

At fixed c the conditional energy is E(A^Tc+B^Td) plus a d-independent constant. The chain rule gives

\[
 \nabla_d^2E(c,d)=B[K+\nabla^2V(x)]B^T
 \succeq BKB^T-\beta I.
\]

Thus lambda_min(BKB^T)>=beta+alpha is a sufficient certificate of alpha-strong convexity uniformly over c,d. If V(x)=g/4 sum_i x_i^4−beta/2 ||x||², g>0, its Hessian equals −beta I at x=0 and is everywhere larger. The displayed lower bound is attained there: the condition is also necessary for this model. No Fourier-monotonicity assumption is needed.

### Sharp retained-rank lower bound

Let t be the number of eigenvalues of K strictly smaller than beta+alpha, and retain r coordinates. If r<t, the t-dimensional soft eigenspace has nonzero intersection with the N−r dimensional detail subspace. A unit vector in that intersection has Rayleigh quotient below beta+alpha, contradicting the necessary certificate above. Hence r>=t. Conversely, retaining the t soft eigenvectors and eliminating their orthogonal complement reaches the threshold. This proves the unrestricted linear one-step minimum rank. Spectral projectors may be nonlocal; this does not solve local-filter construction, efficient retained-root sampling, or control at later RG levels.

### Bloch alias multiplicity obstruction

For translation-equivariant blocking by factor s in d spatial dimensions, each coarse Fourier fiber contains s^d fine aliases k+2pi*a/s. Suppose t aliases in one fiber have Gaussian precision at most m², while the block map retains r channels. If r<t, the kernel of the retained map intersects that soft alias subspace in dimension at least t−r. The conditional detail precision therefore has a Rayleigh quotient at most m². This conclusion is independent of how an orthogonal smoothing/filter is optimized. For a general invertible transform T with a uniform positive lower singular-value bound, choose a unit soft vector e in the retained-map kernel. Its transformed coordinate is (0,d)=Te. The detail energy Rayleigh quotient is e^TKe/||d||² <= m²/sigma_min(T)². Thus the vanishing gap persists up to fixed conditioning constants; allowing T to become singular in the critical limit violates the condition.

Example: K(kx,ky)=m²+4cos²(kx)+4cos²(ky) is a positive, finite-range lattice precision with four valleys at (±pi/2,±pi/2). Under scale-two blocking all four fold to one coarse fiber. Any one-channel retained field leaves at least three soft combinations in the details. A larger field normalization cannot repair this while remaining uniformly conditioned. This Gaussian example is not claimed to reproduce the interacting frustrated phase diagram of the 2026 paper.

## Exact local example and successful repair

On an even periodic 1D lattice,

\[
 E_m(x)=\tfrac12\sum_i[(x_i+x_{i+1})^2+m^2x_i^2],\quad m>0.
\]

The stiffness is K(k)=m²+2+2cos(k), soft at pi. Use ordinary Haar retained averages c_j=(x_2j+x_2j+1)/sqrt2 and details d_j=(x_2j−x_2j+1)/sqrt2. A constant detail field is the staggered microscopic mode erased by the retained map. Substituting x_2j=d_j/sqrt2, x_2j+1=−d_j/sqrt2 into the quadratic energy shows that the detail precision has spectrum

\[
 \lambda_d(q)=m^2+1-\cos q,
\]

and exact gap m². Conditioning adds linear terms depending on c but does not alter this Gaussian Hessian. Unpreconditioned conditional Langevin dynamics has Ornstein–Uhlenbeck rate m² in the soft detail direction. Taking m=L^-1 gives relaxation L². This is an exact statement for that dynamics, not a lower bound for every possible sampler; Fourier sampling can solve the Gaussian model directly.

Now locally demodulate y_i=(−1)^i x_i and apply Haar to y. The retained coordinate is a staggered block average, and the energy is the massive ferromagnetic gradient energy. The new detail precision is

\[
 \lambda_d^{\rm repaired}(q)=m^2+3+\cos q\geq2+m^2.
\]

The conditional OU relaxation is now uniformly below 1/2. The repair costs O(N), has condition number one, and is exact for this supplied Gaussian model. It transports the physical slow mode into the retained variables; no model expressivity or additional training was needed.

### A missed slow mode also defeats unweighted score-loss validation

For the soft detail scalar, let p=N(0,1/m²), while q=N(0,1/[(1+epsilon)m²]), epsilon>−1. Direct calculation gives

\[
 \ell(q)=\tfrac12E_p[(\partial\log q-\partial\log p)^2]
 =\tfrac12\epsilon^2m^2,
\quad D(p\|q)=\tfrac12[\epsilon-\log(1+\epsilon)].
\]

As m approaches zero, the score loss vanishes even for epsilon=1, while KL=.153426… and the staggered susceptibility is wrong by a factor two. Score loss cannot certify the relevant observable without controlling the inverse stiffness/log-Sobolev constant. This reconstructs the role of the denominator in the prior Eq.8 rather than refuting it.
