# Repeated-copy decision and binary-metrology consequences

Worker: `c07_l10`; written after immutable `INITIAL.txt`.

## Pairwise quantum discrimination is classical to the channel error

Let θ=(A,b), let (P_\theta) be the quartic law on \(\mathbb R^3\), and
let (\rho_{N,\theta}) be the critical spin state in the N20 interface. Assume
the pinned N20 channel result, in its sharper coherent/spin-sector version:

\[
\sup_\theta \operatorname{TV}(T_N\rho_{N,\theta},P_\theta)\le \delta_N,
\qquad
\sup_\theta T(S_NP_\theta,\rho_{N,\theta})\le\delta_N,
\quad \delta_N=C_{M,B}N^{-1/2}.
\]

For any two allowed parameters \(\theta_0,\theta_1\),

\[
\left|T(\rho_{N,\theta_0},\rho_{N,\theta_1})-
\operatorname{TV}(P_{\theta_0},P_{\theta_1})\right|\le 2\delta_N.
\]

For the lower inequality, apply contraction of TV to \(T_N\), then use the two
encoder errors. For the upper inequality, decode both laws with \(S_N\), use
contraction of trace distance under a channel, then use the two decoder errors.
Thus the equal-prior Helstrom error differs from the optimal classical binary
testing error by at most \(\delta_N\). Tensor powers give the same statement
for R independent copies with error at most \(2R\delta_N\) in trace distance.

This is stronger than merely invoking a decoder: it gives the exact asymptotic
binary distinguishability of the full quantum states for every fixed pair of
parameters in the compact family. It also follows from the same channel pair
that every bounded-loss decision problem has risk gap at most \(R\delta_N\).

## Explicit critical-field test

Take \(A=0\), compare \(b_+=\beta e_3\) against \(b_-=-\beta e_3\),
\(0<\beta\le B\). The corresponding coefficient of \(J_3\) in the Gibbs
exponent is \(h_\pm=\pm\beta N^{-3/4}\). The limiting densities have the same
partition function and likelihood ratio

\[
\frac{p_+(x)}{p_-(x)}=e^{2\beta x_3},
\]

so the optimal classical test is the sign of \(x_3\). Its total variation is

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_+,P_-)
 = \beta\,\mathbb E_{P_0}|X_3|+O(\beta^3)
 = \frac{\beta}{2(4/3)^{1/4}\Gamma(3/4)}+O(\beta^3)
 =0.379709553995\,\beta+O(\beta^3).
\]

The derivation uses isotropy, \(\mathbb E(|X_3|\mid |X|=r)=r/2\), and
\(\mathbb E|X|=(4/3)^{-1/4}/\Gamma(3/4)\). Consequently the quantum equal-prior
error obeys

\[
P_{\rm err}^{Q}(N,\beta)
 = \frac12-0.189854776997\,\beta+O(\beta^3)+O_{M,B}(N^{-1/2}).
\]

For fixed small \(\beta\), the signal is at a physical field separation
\(2\beta N^{-3/4}\); its leading binary-testing advantage is fully present in
the classical quartic observation. This is a finite-difference, bounded-loss
metrology statement. It does not imply convergence of the infinitesimal quantum
Fisher information: uniform trace-distance approximation alone does not control
derivatives in the parameter.

## Repetition and a scope boundary

For R independent critical blocks, tensor-product channel bounds imply
\[
\left|P_{\rm err}^{Q,(R)}-P_{\rm err}^{C,(R)}\right|
 \le R C_{M,B}N^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus any joint sequence with \(R\to\infty\) and \(R=o(\sqrt N)\) has the same
limiting bounded-loss decision performance as R i.i.d. quartic samples. In the
classical exponential family, the score for b is \(X-\mathbb E X\), and its
Fisher matrix is \(\operatorname{Cov}(X)\). Standard local asymptotic normality
then gives \(R^{-1/2}\) estimation scale for b and \(N^{-3/4}R^{-1/2}\) for the
coefficient multiplying J. The theorem does not cover R on the order of
\(\sqrt N\), growing public parameter bounds, fixed nonzero physical field, a
reference-entangled input, or dynamically generated sensor protocols.

The forward map is a mathematical collective POVM with total-spin and coherent
components, plus radial randomization. No gate-level implementation, readout
noise analysis, or preparation cost for repeated Gibbs blocks is included.

## Primary-source search and attribution

- [Aybar et al., *Critical quantum thermometry and its feasibility in spin
  systems*, arXiv:2204.02734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02734) studies finite-size
  QFI scaling/readout in a spin-1 BEC and nearest-neighbor Heisenberg XX chain.
  It is a useful critical-sensing comparator, with a different model and
  parameterization.
- [Garbe et al., *Critical Quantum Metrology with Fully-Connected Models: From
  Heisenberg to Kibble-Zurek Scaling*, arXiv:2110.04144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04144)
  studies QFI scaling across static and dynamical fully-connected protocols and
  explicitly treats protocol time; this search did not find the present
  parameter-uniform two-way channel statement there.
- [Yu, Nguyen, and Nimmrichter, *Criticality-Enhanced Precision in Phase
  Thermometry*, arXiv:2311.14578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14578) studies a
  finite two-dimensional Ising bath and probe dynamics, and warns that
  Curie-Weiss/thermodynamic mean-field descriptions may miss finite-lattice
  critical fluctuations. It rules out casual spatial transfer.
- [Kirkpatrick and Meckes, *Asymptotics of the mean-field Heisenberg model*,
  arXiv:1204.3062](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.3062) supplies the classical
  complete-graph nonnormal critical magnetization mechanism, not the quantum
  state-channel comparison.
- [Fannes, Kossakowski, and Verbeure, *Critical fluctuations for quantum
  mean-field models* (1991)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01053756)
  remains the priority-critical quantum predecessor; only its abstract has been
  inspected in the saved audit.

The primary sources support the model distinction and historical boundary; none
externally validates the internal N20 channel proof or clears priority.
