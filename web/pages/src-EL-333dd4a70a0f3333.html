## A constructive operation for a narrower high-dimensional class

Consider a finite first-order reaction system on `d` species. Each source complex is either `0` or one basis vector `e_j`; let `p_e` be the nonnegative product vector and `nu_e=p_e-y_e`. Suppose

\[
\dot x=q(x)\sum_e \nu_e k_e(t)x^{y_e},\qquad
0<\ell_e\le k_e(t)\le u_e<\infty,
\]

where `q` is a common positive, locally Lipschitz state-dependent factor and every `k_e` is an arbitrary measurable path in its interval. Require a positive constant-inflow floor for each species,

\[
b_i:=\sum_{e:y_e=0}\ell_e(p_e)_i>0.
\]

The shared factor can be removed by the internal clock

\[
s(t)=\int_0^t q(x(u))\,du.
\]

In `s` time the same trajectories satisfy a first-order affine positive system with time-warped coefficients still lying in the supplied intervals. The following finite witness then gives an explicit absorbing set.

For `c\in\mathbb Q^d_{>0}`, define the worst-case weighted drift from source species `j` by

\[
\Delta_j(c)=\sum_{e:y_e=e_j}
\max\{\ell_e\,c\cdot\nu_e,\ u_e\,c\cdot\nu_e\}.
\]

Accept a witness `c` only when every `Delta_j(c)<0`, and set

\[
\eta=\min_j\frac{-\Delta_j(c)}{c_j}>0,\qquad
B=\sum_{e:y_e=0}u_e\,c\cdot p_e.
\]

For the coordinate floors, put

\[
D_i=\sum_{e:y_e=e_i}u_e\,(1-(p_e)_i)_+,
\qquad
\delta_i=\begin{cases}b_i/(2D_i),&D_i>0,\\1,&D_i=0,\end{cases}
\qquad
\rho_i=b_i-D_i\delta_i>0.
\]

With

\[
M=\max\left\{\frac{2B}{\eta},\ 2\sum_i c_i\delta_i\right\},
\qquad
K=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:x_i\ge\delta_i\ (1\le i\le d),\ c\cdot x\le M\},
\]

`K` is a nonempty compact convex set. In internal time, `V=c·x` obeys

\[
V'\le B-\eta V,
\qquad x_i'\ge b_i-D_i x_i.
\]

At `V=M` the first bound is at most `-B`; at `x_i=delta_i` the second is `rho_i>0`. These inequalities prove forward invariance. From initial state `x0`, the internal-time entry bound is

\[
s_U=\begin{cases}
0,&V_0\le M,\\
\eta^{-1}\log\!\left(\dfrac{V_0-B/\eta}{M-B/\eta}\right),&V_0>M,
\end{cases}
\qquad
s_L=\max_i\frac{(\delta_i-x_{0,i})_+}{\rho_i},
\qquad S=\max\{s_U,s_L\}.
\]

The denominator in `s_U` is positive because `M>=2B/eta`. Before internal time `S`, the trajectory lies in the explicit positive rectangle

\[
C(x_0,S)=\prod_i\left[x_{0,i}e^{-D_iS},\ \frac{\max\{V_0,B/\eta\}}{c_i}\right].
\]

If the input includes a certified lower bound `q_0>0` for `q` on this rectangle, then physical-time entry is at most `S/q_0`. This last bound is essential: a positive common factor can become arbitrarily small near the boundary, so an internal-time bound alone is not a physical-time certificate.

The witness search is itself finite and structured. With rational stoichiometry and rational interval endpoints, the condition `Delta_j(c)<0` is a rational LP feasibility problem: introduce one variable `z_e` per first-order reaction, require

\[
z_e\ge\ell_e(c\cdot\nu_e),\quad z_e\ge u_e(c\cdot\nu_e),\quad
\sum_{e:y_e=e_j}z_e\le-1\ (\forall j),\quad c_i\ge1.
\]

A strictly feasible positive `c` can be scaled to meet these normalized inequalities, and a feasible rational LP solution is an exact witness. Checking a supplied witness, floors, and margins takes linear arithmetic in the sparse reaction list; witness acquisition is one rational LP. The state-factor lower-bound proof is a separate input obligation and must not be inferred from samples.

### Proof sketch

Because `q>0`, `s(t)` is strictly increasing wherever the solution exists. In `s` time, the reaction coefficients are `k_e(t(s))`, which remain in their stated intervals. For `V=c·x`, each source-`e_j` reaction contributes `k_e x_j(c·nu_e)` and each zero-source reaction contributes `k_e(c·p_e)`. Taking the worst endpoint coefficient edge by edge gives `V'<=B+sum_j x_j Delta_j(c)<=B-eta V`. For coordinate `i`, only reactions sourced at `e_i` can consume `X_i`; all reactions from other first-order sources can only add it. The constant-inflow floor and largest possible self-loss give `x_i'>=b_i-D_i x_i`. Scalar comparison yields the displayed upper and lower entry bounds. The boundary inequalities establish invariance of `K`.

The comparison bounds also keep every finite internal-time segment inside `C(x0,S)`. A certified `q>=q0` there implies `dt/ds<=1/q0`; hence the path reaches `K` in physical time at most `S/q0`. Compactness and positivity of `q` on the same rectangle justify continuation through that interval. All estimates hold for every admitted measurable coefficient path.

## Exact growing-dimension example

For any `d`, take one birth and one death per species,

\[
\varnothing\xrightarrow{a_i(t)q_c(x)}X_i,
\qquad
X_i\xrightarrow{b_i(t)q_c(x)}\varnothing,
\qquad
q_c(x)=\exp\!\left[-c_0\left(1+\sum_i(\log x_i)^2\right)^{1/4}\right],
\]

where `0<c_0<infinity` and `kappa<=a_i(t),b_i(t)<=KappaBar` with `0<kappa<=KappaBar`. The death flux includes its mass-action factor `x_i`. Here the certificate is `c_i=1`, `eta=kappa`, `B=d KappaBar`, `D_i=KappaBar`, `b_i=kappa`, and

\[
\delta=\frac{\kappa}{2\bar\kappa},\qquad
M=\frac{2d\bar\kappa}{\kappa},\qquad
K=\left\{x_i\ge\delta,\ \sum_i x_i\le M\right\}.
\]

Let `V0=sum_i x_{0,i}`. The internal entry time is bounded by

\[
S=\max\left\{
\frac{1}{\kappa}\log^+\!\left(\frac{\kappa V_0}{d\bar\kappa}-1\right),\quad
\frac{2}{\kappa}\max_i(\delta-x_{0,i})_+
\right\},
\]

where the logarithmic term is set to zero when `V0<=M`. For a physical-time bound, set `\bar V=max{V0,d\bar\kappa/\kappa}` and

\[
L_i=\max\left\{|\log x_{0,i}-\bar\kappa S|,\ |\log\bar V|\right\},\qquad
q_0=\exp\!\left[-c_0\left(1+\sum_i L_i^2\right)^{1/4}\right].
\]

Then every admitted path enters `K` by time `S/q_0`. This can be enormous, but it is finite, explicit, and uniform over all coefficient paths.

The same family satisfies N173's uniform logarithmic valuation condition with `tau_{i,+}(p)=0` and `tau_{i,-}(p)=p_i`. Writing `L=-log h`, one valid common error bound is

\[
\epsilon(h)=\frac{\max\{|\log\kappa|,|\log\bar\kappa|\}
 +c_0(1+dL^2)^{1/4}}{L}\longrightarrow0.
\]

For any `r`, if some `r_i<0`, the smallest relevant orders are the deaths for the most negative coordinates, and `r·(-e_i)>0`; if no coordinate is negative but some is positive, the minimum order is zero at relevant births, with `r·e_i>0`. Thus every minimizing reaction is inward, including ties and coordinate faces. The shared factor is unbounded in reciprocal size at boundary/infinity, so this example covers the subpower rate modulation that prevents a global constant-multiple-of-monomial bound.

This example is a mathematical controlled model. It does not establish that a physical biochemical mechanism really multiplies all reaction channels by this particular `q_c`.

The LP certificate also handles actual interspecies transport. Add cyclic first-order transfers `X_i -> X_{i+1}` (indices modulo `d`) at rates `r_i(t)q_c(x)x_i` with `0<sigma<=r_i(t)<=beta`. Keep the births and deaths above. Transfers conserve `V=sum_i x_i`, so the same witness `c_i=1` has `eta=kappa` and `B=d KappaBar`; the coordinate loss is now `D_i=KappaBar+beta`. Thus the explicit absorber remains

\[
x_i\ge\frac{\kappa}{2(\bar\kappa+\beta)},\qquad
\sum_i x_i\le\frac{2d\bar\kappa}{\kappa}.
\]

The entry-time formulas above still apply after replacing `delta` by `kappa/[2(KappaBar+beta)]` and using `D_i=KappaBar+beta` in the pre-entry rectangle. This is a coupled `d`-species flow with only `3d` channels and a constant-size-per-reaction certificate. The all-face tropical condition is not asserted for this transfer extension; the weighted-mass certificate proves the stated result directly.

## Hard acquisition obstruction: finite interior samples cannot certify the boundary law

Fix any finite set of positive concentration queries `Q={q_1,...,q_m}` to reaction rates. Consider the same one-species graph `0->X`, `X->0` and system A

\[
w_+^A(x)=\frac{x^2}{1+x^2},\qquad w_-^A(x)=x,
\qquad \dot x=\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}-x.
\]

The inequality `x^2/(1+x^2)<=x/2` gives `x'(t)<=-x(t)/2`, while `x'(t)>=-x(t)`. Thus every positive solution exists for all time, stays positive, and tends to zero; A is not persistent.

Choose a smooth compactly supported function `psi` on `(0,infinity)` such that `psi(q_j)=log(q_j^2/(1+q_j^2))` for every distinct query point. Such a function is obtained by placing disjoint smooth bumps around the finitely many `q_j`. Define system B on the same reaction graph by

\[
w_+^B(x)=e^{\psi(x)},\qquad w_-^B(x)=x.
\]

The two systems give exactly the same rate value for every reaction at every queried point. Outside a compact subset of `(0,infinity)`, B has vector field `1-x`. Choose `0<epsilon<1` below that support and `R>1` above it. Then the field points strictly into `[epsilon,R]` at both endpoints and is exactly `1-x` outside, so this compact interval is forward invariant and absorbing. B is permanent.

Both models have positive smooth rates and satisfy a uniform logarithmic valuation law on `x=h^p`, `p in [-1,1]`. For A the orders are `tau_+(p)=0` when `p<=0`, `tau_+(p)=2p` when `p>=0`, and `tau_-(p)=p`; the birth-order error is at most `log(2)/|log h|`. A therefore fails strict tropical inwardness for `p>0`, where the death order `p` is smaller and its projection is outward. B has orders `(0,p)` and satisfies the condition; its birth-order error is at most `||psi||_infinity/|log h|`. Thus even knowing the reaction graph, obtaining exact finite per-channel measurements throughout the positive interior cannot decide permanence for unrestricted smooth kinetics. Any sound finite-query procedure must return `UNKNOWN` on this shared dataset unless it also receives a structural rate model or a certified asymptotic modulus/order contract. This is an information obstruction for black-box acquisition; it is not a lower bound for a supplied mass-action formula or for a specified finite-dimensional parametric model.

## Comparison and research gate

The strongest direct computational comparator found is Johnston, Pantea, and Donnell's primary MILP method. It classifies endotactic and strongly endotactic reaction graphs in dimensions above two and was applied to biochemical networks. Strong endotacticity yields qualitative permanence under the relevant variable-mass-action assumptions, but that graph test does not by itself return a numerical absorber, a uniform entry-time bound, or a certificate for arbitrary smooth channel kinetics. Their paper also notes the practical burden of directly enumerating high-dimensional faces, which is the same scaling pressure visible in N173's arrangement route. See [the paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4662), especially its sections 4 and 5.

For global convergence, Gopalkrishnan, Miller, and Shiu prove the global attractor result for complex-balanced strongly endotactic systems using differential inclusions and a projection argument. That result is stronger than mere absorption for its narrower equilibrium assumptions; the certificate here covers time-varying interval coefficients and a common activity factor but does not prove convergence to an equilibrium. See [their paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.5303).

The common-factor operation is built from standard positive-system drift and time reparameterization ideas. I make no priority claim. Its candidate value is operational: a high-dimensional sparse first-order network can carry a rational LP witness, exact inward margins, a compact absorber, and a physical-time entry bound, including for a common subpower factor. The hard gate is acquiring and validating the shared-factor decomposition, coefficient intervals, and a certified lower bound for `q` on the pre-entry rectangle from the scientific model. The finite-sample construction proves that interior rate observations alone cannot discharge that gate for unrestricted kinetics. A useful next decision is whether any supplied BioModels or control-system model has this exact interface and yields a materially tighter certificate than its strongest available positive-systems/CRNT baseline.
