## Uniform additive erasure loss

Write Q=2^(k-1). The strict positive erasure margin can be relaxed to the following condition on every actual legal witness tau contained in every realizable joint syndrome sigma:

    E(sigma)-E(sigma\tau) >= kappa |tau|-C_k,
    kappa>0, C_k>=0.                                      (D1)

The opposite CSS sector is unchanged. Both energies are evaluated on realizable sectors. The constant C_k is one total loss budget for the entire erased witness, rather than a loss paid once per check or once per recursive history node.

For a fixed tau, the same injective legal eraser gives

    Pr_beta[tau subset sigma]
       <=exp(beta C_k) exp(-beta kappa|tau|).             (D2)

This follows by summing the Boltzmann ratio into the same full joint partition function. The bound is uniform over the input sigma; it can be factored outside the sum without conditioning on which history produced tau. Summing candidate witnesses only pays exp(beta C_k) once. Keeping the main note's corrected history constants and b=beta kappa gives

    p1 <=exp(beta C_k) 2nu rho(b)^Q/(1-rho(b)),
    error(t)<=exp(beta C_k) 32K gamma nu t
                                    rho(b)^Q/(1-rho(b)). (D3)

At b>=b0, rho<=1/2, and therefore

    error(t)<=64K gamma nu t exp(beta C_k) 2^(-Q),
    t_epsilon>=epsilon exp[(log2)Q-beta C_k]
                                      /(64K gamma nu). (D4)

For beta C_k=o(Q), polynomial bath intensity and fixed or separately paid prefix costs, this retains an exponential-in-Q guaranteed interval. The explicit weaker condition beta C_k<=Q(log2)/2 already gives t_epsilon>=epsilon 2^(Q/2)/(64K gamma nu). This is a sufficient energetic criterion, not a necessary characterization of self-correction.

## Local islands and a globally bounded check-diagonal disturbance

Suppose E_d has the strict margin kappa and W is scalar on the original realizable syndrome sectors with ||W||<=w_k. Then

    |W(sigma)-W(sigma\tau)|<=2w_k,

so (D1) holds with C_k=2w_k. W can create adverse or even negative local excitation penalties inside a fixed island, while the exterior retains the previous positive margin. A supplied finite set of bounded check-algebra terms has w_k<=sum ||W_X||. At fixed island size and fixed temperature the resulting factor exp(2beta w_k) is fixed in k.

For example W=delta C_a changes one defect penalty from J_a to J_a-2delta. It is covered even if the chosen local penalty becomes nonpositive, provided its total scalar norm is bounded and the whole Hamiltonian remains logical-independent. No claim is made about its unchanged ground code in that more general case. Thermal encoding uses the new full Gibbs ancillary state.

The same conclusion follows directly by comparing two commuting Gibbs states:

    exp(-beta w_k)exp(-beta H_d)
       <=exp[-beta(H_d+W)]
       <=exp(beta w_k)exp(-beta H_d),
    rho_beta(H_d+W)<=exp(2beta w_k)rho_beta(H_d).          (D5)

Their normalized partition functions are included in the second bound. Commutativity, or the explicitly scalar syndrome representation, is required. This is not an operator-monotonicity claim about noncommuting exponentials. Applying (D5) to the unchanged syndrome-diagonal good projector gives (D3) directly.

The abstract mathematical interface also admits w_k=o(Q/beta), for example a growing but sub-Q total defect-island norm. Such a sequence must separately satisfy the supplied physical range, logical independence, bath and acquisition conditions; a bound on its total norm does not itself establish those conditions.

## Do not repay an island for every Pauli prefix

For a weight-w Pauli F, its syndrome translation is realizable. If H_d has energy shift at most g_d w and ||W||<=w_k, then the total Hamiltonian has

    ||F(H_d+W)F*-H_d-W||<=g_d w+2w_k.                  (D6)

This bound charges the island once for the whole prefix rather than 2w_k per factor. There is an even simpler bound on the final eroded projector. Construct the same P_q using the same source decoder, and apply (D5) after the base-H_d prefix bound:

    Tr[(I-P_q)rho_beta(H_d+W)]
       <=exp(2beta w_k) M_q exp[beta g_d(q-1)]p1,d.      (D7)

Thus only one bounded-disturbance factor is needed for the full q-body bath guarantee. All raw and actual frequency-resolved protection remains exact because H_d+W, P_q and the dressed logical pair commute. Dynamics uses rates for H_d+W and its Gibbs state, not the old bath generator.

## An actual boundary case: all penalties removed

The strict margin, or the sub-Q additive-budget condition, cannot be dropped for arbitrary commuting energies. On the same N-qubit code choose H=0. Its Gibbs state is the maximally mixed state at every beta. Admit three independent Pauli channels per site with equal rate gamma>0; all have frequency zero and the generator is Gibbs stationary:

    L(rho)=gamma sum_(j,P=X,Y,Z)(P_j rho P_j-rho).

The one-site channel at time t is E_q=q id+(1-q)T, where q=exp(-4gamma t) and T replaces the site by I/2. The channels on distinct sites commute, so the full channel is E_q tensor N. A telescoping tensor-product bound in diamond norm gives

    ||E_q^(tensor N)-T^(tensor N)||_diamond
       <=N ||E_q-T||_diamond<=2Nq.                     (D8)

This is an exact finite-dimensional channel calculation, not a simulated memory trajectory. Every encoded input is within 2Nq of I/2^N after the evolution. Two orthogonal logical input states therefore have evolved physical outputs at distance at most 4Nq. Any fixed CPTP decoder can only decrease that distance. If both decoded outputs were within epsilon of their respective original orthogonal inputs, the triangle inequality would give 2<=2epsilon+4Nq. Hence at least one input has decoded error at least 1-2Nq.

At t=log(8N)/(4gamma), q=1/(8N), so one decoded error is at least 3/4, for every encoder/decoder pair. The infinite-temperature commuting model loses information in O(log N) time under this fully local bath. It is an actual failure of an unqualified arbitrary-energy extension; it is not a counterexample in the positive-margin class.
