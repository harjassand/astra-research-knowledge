# Restricted two-local boundary: rotated classical Gibbs states cannot hide from local X/Z SLD scores

Date2026-10-06. Status: complete all-n positive subclass theorem, an exact rational coupled two-qubit certificate, and six finite matrix checks. General noncommuting two-local Hamiltonians remain unresolved. No universal counterexample or literature-priority claim is made.

## Exact score and supplied interface

For a faithful density matrix σ and a known physical generator A, the score is the **unitary-orbit SLD** defined by

(σS_A+S_Aσ)/2=−i[A,σ].

It is not the inverse-temperature SLD, a coefficient derivative, or simply −i[A,logσ]. Fixed frame is {X_i,Z_i}; adding Y_i cannot weaken identification. The theoretical interface here supplies entire exact score matrices, or equivalently studies the zero of the exact population objective. Acquiring those matrices economically from copies is a separate task and is not accomplished by the algebra below.

The recent source is Dong and Leng, *Quantum score matching with applications to learning thermal states*, arXiv2609.28391v1, submitted23September2026. Read locations: Definition2, Eq9, Lemma6 and AppendixA.1 Theorem15; high-temperature local-frame statements are in section3/AppendixC. The source claims efficient sample-optimal bounded-locality/degree learning in a high-temperature regime. Our finite-temperature positive subclass theorem is an independent deduction; it neither removes the high-temperature assumption in their general theorem nor supplies a sample-efficient protocol.

## Fiber identity reconstructed independently

In σ's eigenbasis, populations p_j>0 give

S_A,ij=−2i(p_j−p_i)A_ij/(p_i+p_j),
K_A,ij=2√(p_i p_j)A_ij/(p_i+p_j).

A faithful competitor ρ has the same score iff it obeys

(S_A+2iA)ρ+ρ(S_A−2iA)=0.

Set Q=σ^(−1/2)ρσ^(−1/2). Direct substitution using

σ^(−1/2)(S_A+2iA)σ^(1/2)=2iK_A

and its negative conjugate gives [K_A,Q]=0. Conversely that commutation implies the score equation, whose faithful Lyapunov solution is unique. Therefore a scalar dressed commutant forces ρ=σ after trace normalization. This is the source's dressed-commutant sufficient condition, independently reconstructed rather than assumed from ordinary irreducibility.

## Theorem: arbitrary correlated product-basis-diagonal targets are identified

Let N_i=n_i·(X_i,Y_i,Z_i), with unit real n_i, act on qubit i. Suppose σ is diagonal and strictly positive in the joint product eigenbasis of the N_i. Its diagonal probabilities may be arbitrarily correlated. Then {K_Xi,K_Zi}'=CI. Consequently fixed physical local X/Z unitary-orbit SLD scores identify σ among **all faithful competitors**, not only competitors diagonal in this basis. This includes every finite-temperature Gibbs state of

H=Σ_i h_iN_i+Σ_(i,j) J_ijN_iN_j

and more general diagonal interactions. The two-local model displayed has a known graph and known local axes; it expands into ordinary weight≤2 Pauli terms. No mean-field replacement is used.

### Proof1: the dressed local filter acts only on spectators

For a configuration s of all qubits except i, write p_(+,s),p_(−,s)>0. The local generator A_i has diagonal components along N_i and off-diagonal components flipping only qubit i. The filter is1 on the diagonal and

t_i(s)=2√(p_(+,s)p_(−,s))/(p_(+,s)+p_(−,s))∈(0,1]

on either direction of that flip. Define the positive spectator operator

W_i=I_i⊗Σ_s t_i(s)|s⟩⟨s|.

It commutes with every operator on qubit i. With a the Bloch vector of A_i,

K_Ai=(n_i·a)N_i+W_i[A_i−(n_i·a)N_i].             (1)

Equivalently the local Bloch vector is M_(W_i)a, where M_t=tI+(1−t)n_i n_iᵀ. This statement uses exact correlations through W_i, not a product-state approximation.

For the two-local Gibbs model,

W_i=sech(βB_i),  B_i=h_iI+Σ_j J_ijN_j,

and its smallest eigenvalue is at least sech(β[|h_i|+Σ_j|J_ij|])>0 at every finite β. This is a filter bound, not a derived statistical sample bound.

### Proof2: square a measured dressed generator to recover the spectator filter

The parallel and perpendicular parts in (1) anticommute, so

K_Xi²=n_ix²I+(1−n_ix²)W_i²,
K_Zi²=n_iz²I+(1−n_iz²)W_i².                    (2)

At least one of the coefficients1−n_ix²,1−n_iz² is nonzero because X and Z are orthogonal. Choose that equation. If Q commutes with both dressed generators, it commutes with their squares and hence with W_i²; by the positive square root, [Q,W_i]=0. Also each dressed local generator commutes with W_i.

### Proof3: reconstruct the original physical local Pauli pair

For any t>0, M_t is invertible, with eigenvalues1,t,t. Thus

a_t=M_t e_x, b_t=M_t e_z

are linearly independent. Let c_t=a_t×b_t, and form the invertible3×3 real matrix D_t=[a_t,b_t,c_t]. The dressed generators and their Hermitian commutator

C_i=[K_Xi,K_Zi]/(2i)

have respectively those three Bloch vectors on each W_i=t eigenspace. Therefore for j=x,z there are finite scalar coefficients α_j(t),η_j(t),γ_j(t), namely D_t^−1e_j, with

σ_j=α_j(W_i)K_Xi+η_j(W_i)K_Zi+γ_j(W_i)C_i.    (3)

The spectrum of W_i is finite and strictly positive, so functional calculus in (3) is legal, including t=1. A common Q commutes with W_i, K_Xi,K_Zi and C_i, hence with physical X_i and Z_i. This holds for every i. The ordinary physical local X/Z frame generates the full n-qubit matrix algebra, so Q is scalar. The fiber identity then givesρ=σ. □

This proof handles arbitrary local axes, including axes outside the physical xz plane; merely assuming a computational-basis diagonal Hamiltonian would be narrower. It extends the earlier independently proved product-state lemma to correlated targets. It does not extend to general entangled energy eigenbases or noncommuting Hamiltonian terms.

## Exact coupled two-qubit certificate

Set

R1=[[3/5,−4/5],[4/5,3/5]],
R2=[[5/13,−12/13],[12/13,5/13]],
U=R1⊗R2,
σ=U diag(1,4,9,16)Uᵀ/30.

All entries are rational and populations are strictly positive. N_i=R_iZR_iᵀ are nontrivially rotated real local axes. This state has the exact β1 two-local Hamiltonian, up to an irrelevant identity term,

H=[log36/4]N1+[log(64/9)/4]N2+[log(9/4)/4]N1N2.

The three coordinates are approximately0.8958797,0.4904146,0.2027326, all below1. The coupling is nonzero, so this is a correlated target. The interaction graph has maximum degree1. Its Pauli expansion has at most8 nonidentity terms of weight≤2 with coefficients bounded by1; known axes and graph are retained as part of the model promise.

## Model identification versus observable access

At known finite β>0, equality of the full faithful thermal states implies H−H′ is scalar via the unique matrix logarithm. For a supplied independent nonidentity Pauli term family, scalar difference is excluded, so coefficient vectors agree. For the displayed commuting model, N_i and N_iN_j are Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal, making h_i,J_ij individually determined. At β=0 the state is always maximally mixed and Hamiltonian coefficients are unidentifiable; no coefficient-identification assertion is made there.

Our algebra reconstructs physical generators from dressed generator matrices through operator products and spectral functions. It is a proof of identifiability, not an efficient method for obtaining those matrices from sample copies. In particular, storing/tomographing full2^n-dimensional matrices may be exponentially expensive. A competent classical-model comparator can instead measure the known product axes and learn local conditional distributions; this simpler access must be included in any algorithmic advantage claim.

Positive identifiability also does not give uniform low-temperature conditioning. The bound for W_i can decrease exponentially with β and local coupling sum, and inverse coefficients in (3) can become large. Approaching zero temperature is a singular limit; strictly positive finite-temperature filters are load-bearing.

## What remains unresolved and the next discriminating step

The six-qubit blind fiber from the inverse-score team lies in an unrestricted known Pauli model of locality≤6, not a certified2-local family. It cannot be transferred by just calling its block interactions bounded degree. The two-qubit unrestricted Hamiltonian space already consists of all15 nonidentity weight≤2 Paulis, so a genuinely exact full-state local-XZ collision on two qubits would immediately qualify as2-local after finite β coefficient rescaling. Previous two-/three-qubit numerical searches found no collision, which is not a universal theorem.

For three qubits, the full nonidentity Pauli space has63 dimensions; weight≤2 has36. A candidate SLD-blind state fiber must additionally make every weight3 coefficient of −logρ vanish. A blind density tangent is not enough: applying the logarithm can produce weight3 terms, and vanishing first-order forbidden coefficients does not guarantee a finite compatible curve. The decisive next step is an exact/interval-certified intersection of the dressed-commutant fiber with this Hamiltonian subspace, or an algebraic exclusion proof for a larger genuinely noncommuting subclass.

Alternatives retained: positive product-basis targets above; stabilizer-diagonal targets independently handled by the team; two-level rank-one spectra handled by b_math; asymmetric score choices studied separately by r11. No numerical optimizer failure is promoted to identification, and no model agreement is independent external validation. Novelty of this rotated-correlated positive lemma has not been established beyond the team's earlier product-state result.
