## 1. Classical zonal identity gives direct derivative bounds

For a unit direction omega choose an orthonormal basis v_1,v_2 of its perpendicular plane. Put v_theta=cos(theta)*v_1+sin(theta)*v_2 and zeta_theta=omega+i*v_theta. Then zeta_theta.zeta_theta=0. Define

    Z_l(x,omega) = (1/(2pi)) int_0^(2pi)
                    (zeta_theta.x)^l dtheta.

Every integrand is a complex homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree l; its average is invariant under rotations fixing omega and equals 1 at x=omega. The unique real axisymmetric harmonic homogeneous polynomial with that normalization is

    Z_l(x,omega)=|x|^l P_l(omega.x/|x|).         (A4)

Uniqueness follows by writing an axisymmetric homogeneous polynomial as sum_j c_j(omega.x)^(l-2j)(|x|^2-(omega.x)^2)^j: its Laplacian determines every successive coefficient from c_0. The average is real because theta and theta+pi give conjugate integrands. Equation (A4) is the classical Laplace power-integral representation, not a novelty claim. NIST DLMF 14.12.7 gives the real exterior-domain representation; the argument here establishes the needed polynomial identity and its real interior domain directly.

For every real vector u,

    |zeta_theta.u|^2=(omega.u)^2+(v_theta.u)^2
                    <=|u|^2.

Differentiating the finite polynomial integral therefore gives, for l>=s and real directions u_1,...,u_s,

    |D^s Z_l(x,omega)[u_1,...,u_s]|
       <=(l)_s |x|^(l-s) product_j |u_j|.       (A5)

For l<s the derivative is zero. This is a full mixed derivative estimate, so it controls gradient norm and Hessian operator norm without an entry-to-operator factor or a sqrt(2)^s loss. At x=0 it holds by polynomial continuity.

    ||z||_2 <=3(2D+1) A sum_(l>=0)(2k/rho)^l/l!

## 4. Entire degree-D tail on the actual observation ball

For source R_i*omega_i with R_i>=a, the Coulomb expansion is

    |x-R_i*omega_i|^(-1)
       =sum_(l>=0) R_i^(-l-1) Z_l(x,omega_i).

Matching (A6) makes degrees 1 through D equal to A*(P_m(y)-P_0) at x=rho*y; the constant is removed by V(0). At |y|<=2 set

    q=2rho/a=128/255<2/3.                      (A11)

It is important that q>1/2. No estimate below replaces it by 1/2. The direct derivative series (A5) converges uniformly with its first two derivatives on a neighborhood of this ball. Exact geometric sums give

    ||grad E_tail|| <=rho/(A*a^2) * q^D
          *[(D+1)/(1-q)+q/(1-q)^2],

    ||D^2 E_tail|| <=rho^2/(A*a^3) * q^(D-1)
          *[D(D+1)/(1-q)+2(D+1)q/(1-q)^2
             +2q^2/(1-q)^3].                  (A12)

Both prefactors excluding 1/A are below 1. Using q<2/3 bounds the gradient bracket by 6(D+1) and the Hessian bracket by 3D(D+1)+12(D+1)+24. For every D>=1, the latter is at most 16(D+1)^2. Thus the common derivative bound is

    Delta_tail=16(D+1)^2 q^(D-1)/A.            (A13)

This replaces the old outer-ball Poisson tail, not the exact inverse. The extra cancelled degrees are unnecessary for this specific grid's coefficient growth and target amplitude.

## 6. Placement and legal all-N padding

For any N>=n_m keep normalization by the core count n_0=n_m and add N-n_0 unit sources at (R+j,0,0), j=1,...,N-n_0, where

Here eta^(-1) is an integer, so R is exactly integer. These sources are distinct and far outside the core. On the observation ball their distance is at least R/2. The extra terms divided by n_0 contribute rescaled gradient at most

    4N*rho/[n_0*A*R^2]=N/[n_0*A*R^2]<=eta/A,

    16N*rho^2/[n_0*A*R^3]=N/[n_0*A*R^3]<=eta/A.
