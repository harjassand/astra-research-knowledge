# Spatial source–growth phase response under migration

**Status.** Internally derived conditional PDE results plus a converged finite numerical check. They are not observations about cancer, not a treatment result, and not a novelty/priority claim. The biological application is an experiment hypothesis only.

## Interface

On the periodic interval of length `2π/k`, let `R(x,t)` be the density of a reversible drug-tolerant-like state over a short assay window. The independently mapped local net growth rate under the active-drug field is

`r(x) = r0 + g cos(kx)`.

The state is seeded from a maintained source population at rate

`j_s(x) = j0 + s j1 cos(kx)`, `s ∈ {+1,-1}`, with `j0 > |j1|`; the two arms therefore have identical separate spatial histograms and differ only in phase. Let `D ≥ 0` be the tolerant-state motility coefficient and `v` a measured, arm-common directional drift. The deliberately reduced model is

`∂t R_s = D ∂xx R_s + v ∂x R_s + r(x) R_s + j_s(x)`, `R_s(x,0)=0`.

The sign convention is the PDE as written (`v ∂x`, so material trajectories move in the negative x direction). No density limitation, reverse state transition, clone competition, drug-field feedback, or source depletion is represented. The mean is the spatial average over one period.

## Result A: exact ordering with unbiased migration

If `v=0`, `g j1>0`, and the model above holds, then for every `T>0`,

`mean(R_+(T)) > mean(R_-(T))`.

This is an all-horizon model statement, not a nature claim. Write `L=D∂xx+r0+g cos(kx)` and `c=cos(kx)`. The difference is

`R_+(T)-R_-(T) = 2 j1 ∫_0^T exp(τL)c dτ`.

Since `L` is self-adjoint, its mean at time `τ` is `2j1 <c, exp(τL)1>`. With `θ=kx`, `q(θ,τ)=exp(τ[Dk²∂θθ+r0+g cosθ])1` is even. For `g>0`, `w=∂θq` on `(0,π)` solves

`w_τ = Dk² w_θθ + (r0+g cosθ)w - g sinθ q`,

with zero endpoint data and zero initial data. Positivity of `q` and the parabolic maximum principle give `w<0` in the interior for `τ>0`; thus `q` is strictly increasing in `cosθ`. The integral covariance identity

`Cov(c,q) = (1/2) E[(c(X)-c(Y))(q(X)-q(Y))]`

for independent uniform `X,Y` gives `<c,q> > 0`. Reversing `g` reverses this sign. Integrating in time proves the claim. `D=0` follows directly from the pointwise exponential solution.

The `v=0` restriction is load-bearing; Result B shows why it cannot be silently generalized.

## Result B: migration can reverse the phase effect

Let `a=Dk²`, `b=vk`, `z=a-i b`. For fixed finite `T`, the derivative of the arm difference in the growth heterogeneity at `g=0` is

`∂g [mean(R_+(T))-mean(R_-(T))] at g=0 = j1 F(T)`,

where

`F(T) = Re{ [ (exp(r0 T)-1)/r0 - (exp((r0-z)T)-1)/(r0-z) ] / z }`.

Use continuous limits when a denominator is zero. Equivalently,

`F(T) = ∫_0^T exp(r0 τ) ∫_0^τ exp(-a u) cos(bu) du dτ`.

Derivation: move the semigroup to the adjoint in `<1, exp(τL)c>`. The first Dyson term around `L0=D∂xx+v∂x+r0` is

`<1, exp(τL)c> = (g/2) exp(r0τ) ∫_0^τ exp(-Dk²u) cos(vku)du + O(g²)`.

The constant-source terms cancel between arms. Integrating the difference `2j1∫_0^T <1,exp(τL)c>dτ` yields the displayed formula. Analytic dependence on bounded `g cos(kx)` implies that if `F(T)<0`, the finite-amplitude difference is negative for all sufficiently small positive `g`.

An explicit parameter point is `k=v=r0=1`, `D=0.1`, `T=3π/2`, `j1=1`. It gives `F(T)=-18.8899185474`. Thus the aligned-source arm (`s=+1`, source higher where net growth is higher) has *lower* total tolerant-state density than the opposed-source arm for sufficiently small positive `g`; this is the opposite of the diffusion-only ordering. The model-level crossover at fixed `k=v=r0=1`, `T=3π/2` occurs at `a=Dk²≈0.1703914966` in linear response (`F=0`); finite-`g` crossover may shift.

The mean result is unchanged if the two arms instead have exactly `v_s=s v`, because reflection `x↦−x` conjugates `D∂xx+v∂x+r0+g cos(kx)` to its `-v` counterpart and fixes both even source profiles. This exact special symmetry does not cover general durotactic drift `∂x(χ R ∂x M(x))`, spatially variable migration, or other arm-specific transport.

The same fixture predicts a **temporal sign reversal**, not just a negative final comparison. As `T→0`, `F(T)=T²/2+O(T³)>0`, so same-phase source/growth increases the early mean. For `D=0.1`, `v=r0=k=1`, the linear-response root is `T≈4.3008730`. At finite `g=0.01`, a grid-64 RK4 root search gives `T≈4.3008934`; evaluating that horizon on grids 64 and 128 gives mean differences about `−4.73e−9`. Direct finite checks give `+0.0804656` at `T=4.0`, `+0.00030655` at `T=4.3`, and `−0.0175125` at `T=4.35`. Thus observing only an early time point or only an endpoint could lead to opposite conclusions about the phase effect. These are model calculations in dimensionless units; actual times require measured parameter scales.

Numerical finite-amplitude check solves the PDE for the arm difference `W=R_+-R_-`, which obeys `W_t=D W_xx+v W_x+(r0+g cos(kx))W+2j1 cos(kx)`. Periodic Fourier differentiation plus classical RK4 gives `mean(W(T))=-0.188898066076` at `g=0.01`; `mean(W)/g=-18.8898066`, close to `F=-18.8899185`. An independent 200,000-panel Simpson quadrature of the time-integral form gives `-18.88991854740177`. The code and grid/time-step convergence values are in `../phase_response.py`. These validate the stated finite calculations only; the formula is the analytic result.

The physical interpretation is a finite-horizon phase lag: drift carries source-born cells through the growth field, so the spatial correlation that matters is the source correlated with *future reproductive value*, not just same-time overlap or separate field histograms. The formula charges diffusion (`Dk²`), directed migration (`vk`), growth (`r0`), and assay horizon (`T`) explicitly.

## Design calculation beyond the sign corollary

If `D,v,r0,T` are first measured and source/growth amplitudes can be held fixed as the mechanical pattern wavelength changes, one can choose a wavelength that maximizes the linear-response phase contrast instead of testing an arbitrary spacing. For the same dimensionless fixture `D=0.1`, `v=r0=1`, `T=3π/2`, numerical minimization of `F(k)` over the declared interval `0.05≤k≤10` gives `k≈1.1002978`, `F≈−23.3080664` (period `2π/k≈5.710`). At finite `g=0.01`, the PDE check gives mean difference `−0.2330838231` for `N=64` and `N=128`. This is a numerical design calculation on a declared interval, not a certified global optimization or an experimentally calibrated wavelength. The full run is reproduced by `phase_response.py`.

## Independent falsification conditions and scope

The phase result should fail to transfer if active-drug-dependent fitness `r(x)` changes when the mechanical pattern is flipped; if a matrix changes drug diffusion, binding, uptake, efflux, or target engagement; if source rates depend on cell density, oxygen, pH, nutrients, or accumulated stress; if `D` or `v` is state-/space-dependent; if there are boundaries, matrix remodeling, immune effects, reverse switching, cell-cell competition, or source depletion; or if the marker is not a fate-predictive state. These are live biological possibilities, not minor residuals. Measure the active drug and local birth/death response separately in each arm; do not substitute bulk fluorescence for active exposure.

The model does not identify a tumor cell parameter set. It also does not follow from Astra's N54 metabolite/concentration PDE result: N54 concerns a particular chemical mass-action system and heat-mixing proof interface. Here `R`, `r`, `j`, `D`, and `v` are a new, explicitly assumed cell-state model. No cell-level permanence, clinical implication, or therapy recommendation follows.
