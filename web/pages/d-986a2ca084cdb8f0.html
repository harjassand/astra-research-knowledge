## Exact general theorem and one-object quantifier

Fix n≥3 and a smooth function a:[K0,∞)→[1,∞), nondecreasing and unbounded, with

    |a^(j)(s)|≤A_j a(s)s^−j for each j≥1,
    a(s)≤s^δ eventually for every δ>0.                  (A)

Then there exist one K>K0 and one real smooth pair u≠0,V on Rⁿ such that −Δu+Vu=0 and, with R=(K²+|x|²)^1/2,

    Φ(R)=∫_K^R a(s)ds,
    |u(x)|≤C exp[−Φ(R)],
    |D^νV(x)|≤C_ν a(R)^(|ν|+3/2)R^−1/2.              (1)

K, the cover, direction choices, repair solution and mollifier radius parameter are fixed once, before any arbitrary derivative order is estimated. C_ν may depend on that fixed construction and a. No condition A_j times a small parameter must hold for all j simultaneously. This is ∃(u,V)∀ν∃C_ν, not ∀ν∃(u_ν,V_ν).

There is no lower bound on a' or on the speed of divergence. Constant intervals of any finite length are permitted, including intervals whose lengths grow arbitrarily rapidly. They do not affect any step below.

## 1. Changed scales and local constants

Use ℓ=√(R/a), κ=a^(3/2)/√R and η=(Ra)^−1/2. Exact identities:

    a/ℓ=a²ℓ/R=κ, ℓ/R=1/(aℓ)=η,
    (a/R)/κ=η, ℓ^−2/κ=η.

Since a≥1 and is nondecreasing, η decreases to zero. Assumption (A), applied by product/composition differentiation to R(x), supplies relative jets O_j(R^−j) for a,ℓ,κ,η,a²/R² and a^−1. In particular |∇ℓ|≤C(A₁)η and |∇(ρ/a)|≤C(A₁)ρ/(aR). The comparable-radius cover constants can therefore be made uniform by one sufficiently large K, using only low-order constants.

On each macro patch, logarithmic integration of |a'|/a≤A₁/R shows a(y)/a(x)=1+O(A₁ℓ/R). Thus the frozen frequencies and all overlapping patch scales are comparable uniformly for R≥K. The separated-direction coloring uses the same finite-dimensional caps on S^(n−2), requiring n≥3. First-order partition freezing has error O(ρ/(aℓ))≤Cρ η(K), so the compact nonvanishing cosine family supplies sparse Ω={|q|<τ}. This needs only order-zero and first-derivative bounds.

On micro balls B(x,ρ/a(x)), the relative logarithmic gradients of the two repair weights h₁=κ and h₂=a²/R² are at most C(A₁)/R. The envelope satisfies ∇Φ=a(R)x/R and |∇Φ|≤a. Therefore a,h₁,h₂,E=e^−Φ are locally comparable by constants fixed independently of the eventual derivative order and of K after a fixed low-order threshold. These are precisely the hypotheses of the proved weighted sparse inverse. Both h_iE∈L²: a≥1 makes E≤exp(K−R), and subpower growth controls the polynomial factors. Coercivity uses a_min≥1.

## 2. Envelope and residual jets do not need log-specific identities

Ordinary radial differentiation gives

    |D^jΦ|≤C_j aR^(1−j), |D^jE|≤C_j a^jE,
    |∇Φ|²−a²=−K²a²/R², |D^kΔΦ|≤C_k aR^(−k−1).

The E bound follows from the product/composition expansion: a term with r factors and total order j is at most a^rR^(r−j)E≤a^jE. No lower bound on a' enters.

Choose one fixed smooth partition on the cover and tangent waves with frozen frequencies a_i. All its jets exist, with |D^jχ_i|≤C_jℓ_i^−j. Consequently |D^mq|≤C_ma^m. For f=Eq retain the cancelled expression

    Δf=E[S−2∇Φ·∇q+Bq],
    S=Δq+a²q, B=−K²a²/R²−ΔΦ.

The cutoff terms of S have base sizes ℓ^−2 and a/ℓ, at most Cκ. Their derivatives cost at most a per order. On a patch, a²−a_i² has size O(a²ℓ/R)=O(κ); for k≥1 its derivative is O_k(a²/R^k), and

    (a²/R^k)/(κa^k)=(aR)^(1/2−k)≤1.                (2)

Tangency gives h_i=a_i(a/R)(x·t_i)=O(κ). Since x_i·t_i=0,

    |D^kh_i|≤C_k a²[ℓ/R^(k+1)+R^−k]≤C_kκa^k,

with the second term controlled by (2). The cutoff part of ∇Φ·∇q has base a/ℓ and the same derivative bound. Also |D^kΔΦ|≤C_kκa^k.

For the regularized-origin term, the required fixed-K constant is

    M_K=sup_(R≥K) K²√a(R)/R^(3/2)<∞.                 (3)

Subpower growth makes the ratio tend to zero; it is continuous on finite intervals. It need not be monotone, and no estimate by its value at K is assumed. Its derivatives add R^−k, which is bounded by a^k. Thus all multiindex product allocations give

    |D^mΔf|≤C_(m,K)κa^mE, |D^mf|≤C_ma^mE.           (4)

This explicitly checks the only coefficient in the log proof whose convenient monotonic formula does not transfer verbatim. The allowed finite M_K suffices.

## 3. Zeroth-order repair and all-order smoothing

On Ω, keep the origin term until comparison, using |q|<τ:

    |Δf|≤CE[κ+τK²a²/R²].

The sparse inverse with h₁,h₂ yields

    |w|≤Cρ²E[η+τK²/R²].                            (5)

Choose the sparsity fraction θ from the fixed local constants, then ρ so Cρ² is small, then τ from compact-family sparsity, then K so η(K) and first-order frequency freezing are sufficiently small. The second term in (5) is already controlled because K²/R²≤1. Thus |w|≤τE/20. For the same fixed K, (3) gives

    K²/R²≤M_Kη,
    |W|≤C_KηE=C_Kκa^−2E,                           (6)

where W is the zero extension. No derivatives of W are assumed, and M_K need not be small.

Use one radial normalized nonnegative smooth mollifier, ε=σ/a, and one σ chosen from τ and the q-gradient bound. Assumption (A) implies |D^jε|≤C_j/(aR^j) for all j. For Y=x−ε(x)z, DY is bounded and its higher j-th jets are bounded by C_j a^(j−1). In a composition term of order m with r outer derivatives, the inner jets cost at most a^(m−r). Thus f composed with Y costs a^mE; a fixed p-th derivative of f composed with Y costs a^(p+m)E; and Δf composed with Y costs κa^mE by (4).

The variable kernel K_x(y)=t(x)^nψ[t(x)(x−y)], t=a/σ, has j-th x jets at most C_j a^(n+j), supported in B(x,ε(x)). This follows from |D^jt|≤C_jtR^−j and |x−y|≤1/t. Integration against (6) and the support volume a^−n gives |D^jH|≤C_jκa^(j−2)E, hence |D^mΔH|≤C_mκa^mE.

For the smooth part G, use its exact differentiated Laplacian identity rather than the crude D^(m+2)G bound. Its three commutator coefficients are ε_j, |∇ε|² and Δε. When k derivatives land on a coefficient, their sizes respectively are a^−1R^(−k−1), a^−2R^(−k−2), and a^−1R^(−k−2). Multiply by the composed f jets a^(m−k+2), a^(m−k+2), a^(m−k+1). The results are

    (a/R)a^m(aR)^−kE,
    R^−2a^m(aR)^−kE,
    R^−2a^m(aR)^−kE,

all ≤Cκa^mE. Therefore the same single smoothed F obeys |D^mF|≤C_ma^mE and |D^mΔF|≤C_mκa^mE at every fixed order.

Radial harmonic averaging still gives ΔF=0 on O={|q|<τ/2}, while sign-preserving averaging and (5) give |F|≥cτE outside O. Define V=0 on O and V=ΔF/F where F≠0. These open sets cover space and the formulas agree on their overlap. Multiindex induction in VF=ΔF gives (1), including the boundary of O; zeros of F lie inside its harmonic region. Sparsity supplies nonzero lower witnesses in every micro ball. This proves the general construction with the one fixed K quantifier.

## 4. Limits and actual decay class

For each fixed m choose δ<1/(2m+3) in (A). Then a^(m+3/2)R^−1/2→0. This uses the same a,K; only the radius at which that limit is apparent can depend on m. No K_m is selected.

Since a→∞, for any M>0 there is S with a(s)≥2M for s≥S, so Φ(R)≥2M(R−S). Hence Φ(R)/R→∞ and u is faster than every fixed exponential, even across arbitrarily long plateaus.

Because a is nondecreasing, Φ(R)≤Ra(R). For any ε>0, subpower growth gives Φ(R)=o(R^(1+ε)). Sparsity provides y∈B(x,ρ/a(x)) with |u(y)|≥cE(y)≥c'E(x). Taking x=re₁→∞ excludes any global exp(−c_εr^(1+ε)) upper bound for this same u. The result approaches the exponential decay boundary without producing a fixed superlinear-power example.

## 5. Arbitrary positive divergent loss: explicit admissible stairs

Let L:[0,∞)→(0,∞) be any finite-valued function with L(r)→∞. No monotonicity, differentiability or measurability is required. Define its tail infimum

    ℒ(r)=inf_(s≥r)L(s).

It is nondecreasing, finite, tends to infinity, and lies below L. It may be zero on a bounded initial interval, which is harmless for an eventual claim. Choose r0 so ℒ(r)≥1 for r≥r0.

Choose a fixed C∞ nondecreasing step θ, zero on t≤0 and one on t≥1. Recursively choose finite T_k with

    T_k≥k², T_(k+1)≥T_k+2,
    e^(T_k)/2≥r0, ℒ(e^(T_k)/2)≥(k+2)^(3/2).

The limit ℒ→∞ guarantees this choice; it can be non-effective if no divergence modulus for L is supplied. Set

    a(R)=1+Σ_(k≥1)θ(logR−T_k).

The sum is locally finite and a is smooth, nondecreasing and unbounded. At most one step is transitioning at each logR, so every positive-order t derivative of a(e^t) is bounded by a fixed derivative norm of θ. Using ∂_R=R^−1∂_t inductively, a^(j)(R)=R^−j times a fixed finite linear combination of these t derivatives. Thus |a^(j)|≤C_jR^−j≤C_jaR^−j uniformly in all step locations.

Since T_k≥k², at logR=t at most √t steps have begun; a(R)≤1+√logR eventually. It is subpower. If T_j≤logR<T_(j+1), at most j steps contribute, so a(R)≤j+1 while ℒ(R/2)≥ℒ(e^(T_j)/2)≥(j+2)^(3/2). Therefore a(R)^(3/2)≤ℒ(R/2) eventually. Using T_k≥e^k, as in r15's draft, is also valid and merely gives a smaller loglog majorant; k² already suffices.

Apply (1) to this a. Once r=|x|≥K, R/2≤r and R≥r. Hence

    |V(x)|≤C a(R)^(3/2)/√R
             ≤C ℒ(R/2)/√r≤C L(r)/√r                 (7)

for all sufficiently large r. This handles the regularized R/r shift without assuming monotonicity of the supplied L. All derivatives still vanish and u is faster than every fixed exponential.

**Necessary statement correction:** if L merely is positive, its infimum on a compact interval can be zero. An eventual estimate cannot then automatically be extended globally by increasing C. For example L can take arbitrarily small values densely in a fixed bounded interval while tending to infinity afterward. State (7) eventually. A global version with (1+r)^−1/2 is valid if L is locally bounded below, or after replacing its initial values by max(1,L). This is a scope correction, not a failure of the PDE or asymptotic diagonal construction.

## 6. Critical endpoint is an existing prior, visually verified

Cruz-Sampedro (1999), Theorem 1, uses 3δ=max(0,1−2β); for β=1/2 it prohibits a nonzero H²_loc solution with exp(τr)u∈L² for every τ>0. With smooth bounded V, compact support then implies u=0 by local unique continuation. Thus the exact |V|=O(r^−1/2) endpoint already excludes faster-than-every-exponential states, even for complex potentials. The arbitrary unbounded loss result lies just above this obstruction; it does not settle a previously unknown endpoint.

To eliminate an OCR ambiguity, I rendered and inspected actual journal pages143–145. **Lemma 2.1 explicitly allows δ≥0**, and −3δ<κ≤2−δ. The text extraction lost the equality sign. Therefore δ=0,κ=1 is directly within the printed lemma, not an unprinted extension.

Its printed integration-by-parts identity at α=1+δ=1,κ=1 has radial derivative coefficient 4τ²r+2τ(3−2n), zeroth-order coefficient 2τ³−2τE, and angular term −2τr^−2uΛu≥0. The intermediate τ² zeroth-order term vanishes. For r≥ρ and large τ, all the retained coefficients are nonnegative and the zeroth-order coefficient is at least τ³. Thus

    ∫ r^(1−n)e^(2τr)|v|²dx
       ≤Cτ^−3∫r^(2−n)e^(2τr)|(Δ+E)v|²dx.

The potential term is absorbable because r^(2−n)|V|²≤C²r^(1−n). The usual inner/outer cutoff argument with all exponential weights yields compact support. I independently checked root's specialized coefficients against the rendered source; no omitted identity term was found.

## Consequence and remaining scope

The general and diagonal deductions pass: any prescribed unbounded loss above r^−1/2 allows such a real smooth superexponentially localized state in n≥3, while every fixed potential derivative vanishes. The scale's subpower and symbol-jet hypotheses are verified explicitly for the stairs, so the conclusion does not assume a sufficiently fast growth rate.

This is an existence result for jointly constructed V,u with unrestricted finite constants and no effective cover/Dirichlet solver. It does not certify historical priority, analytic/Gevrey bounds, a relative derivative gain, sign/form positivity, or finite Morse index. The bounded-loss critical obstruction is source prior.
