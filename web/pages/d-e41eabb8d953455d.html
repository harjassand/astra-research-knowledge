# Universal nonvanishing for the modified ternary face-field family

7 October 2026, cycle10. Our proof candidate; independent root gate requested. This extends the universal ternary detector theorem to the hereditary fields arising when higher-dimensional hidden coordinates are removed. It is not a proof of arbitrary-dimensional extactic nonvanishing. Novelty status UNKNOWN.

## Statement and use

Let Q be real symmetric positive definite of size3. Let f=(y^2)^TQ(y^2), H=Hess f, h=det H, and let R be any nonzero product of real diagonal quadratic forms

    R=product_l (a_l1 y_1^2+a_l2 y_2^2+a_l3 y_3^2),

including the empty product1. Coefficients can have arbitrary signs and zeros. Put

    Gamma=R adj H grad(hR),   Delta=Gamma dot grad.

Then det[y,Gamma,Delta Gamma] is nonzero. Neither a positivity premise on R nor a generic-coupling premise is required.

The field is homogeneous and equivariant under every coordinate sign flip. The projective-pencil argument in `UNIVERSAL_TERNARY_NONVANISHING.md` therefore says that a zero extactic would force

    y_i Gamma_j-y_j Gamma_i=0

for some pair i,j, or the field would be radial (which forces every pair identity). It suffices to rule out each pair. The calculation below explicitly includes R factors vanishing on that binary face.

For a full n-variable canonical Hessian field, when every omitted row form is nonzero on an active ternary set I, its exact face restriction is this family with Q=Q_II and R=product_(l outside I)4 sum_(i in I)Q_li y_i^2. The theorem gives a nonzero three-coordinate minor on that face. Zero row forms can make the full face field vanish; no automatic higher-dimensional conclusion is made from this theorem.

## Binary expansion, including the normal-gradient cross term

Fix a pair I={i,j}, write z=(u,v), and let k be the remaining coordinate. Let p=f|_(y_k=0), H_2=Hess p, h_2=det H_2 and

    q(z)=4(Q_ki u^2+Q_kj v^2).

Let m be the number of factors of R supported only on y_k. The other factors restrict to nonzero real even quadratics whose product is R_0(z). Thus

    R(z,t)=c t^(2m) R_0(z)+O(t^(2m+2)),   c!=0, t=y_k.

The binary p is squarefree and coprime to h_2 and R_0. It is also coprime to q when q!=0, by the PD squared-ratio argument from the ternary theorem.

Suppose first q!=0. To first order in t, the Hessian has tangential block H_2, normal scalar q, and cross column8t w, where w=(Q_ki u,Q_kj v) and grad q=8w. Consequently

    (adj H)_II = q adj H_2+O(t^2),
    (adj H)_Ik = -8t adj H_2 w+O(t^3),
    h=q h_2+O(t^2).

The leading normal derivative of hR is 2m c t^(2m-1) q h_2 R_0 for m>0. It contributes at the same order as the tangential derivative after multiplication by the off-diagonal adjugate. Omitting it gives a wrong leading field when m>0. With it included, the t^(4m) coefficient of Gamma_I is

    c^2 q R_0 adj H_2 [q R_0 grad h_2+h_2 q grad R_0
                       +(1-2m)h_2 R_0 grad q]

    =c^2 q^(2m+1) R_0 adj H_2 grad g,

    g=h_2 R_0 q^(1-2m).                         (1)

The second expression is rational on q!=0, and all powers cancel in the first polynomial expression. When m=0, the same calculation holds: the cross normal derivative contributes only higher order.

Euler's binary identity gives det[z,adj H_2 grad g]=3 Jac(p,g), valid as a rational identity as well. A proposed pair-ratio identity would imply Jac(p,g)=0.

Let r_0 be the number of quadratic factors in R_0. The rational homogeneous g has degree

    e=6+2r_0-4m.

If e!=0, at every root of the squarefree p all factors of g are finite and nonzero. Writing p=v^4 P(u/v), g=v^e G(u/v), the Jacobian at such a root is e P'G times a nonzero coordinate power, hence nonzero. Contradiction.

If e=0, that Jacobian identity says P G'=0, so g is constant. But this would give

    h_2 R_0=constant*q^(2m-1).                 (2)

The binary h_2 has two distinct real linear factors in u^2,v^2. For p=a u^4+2b u^2v^2+c v^4,

    h_2=48[ab u^4+(3ac-b^2)u^2v^2+bc v^4],

whose squared-variable discriminant is (ac-b^2)(9ac-b^2)>0 when b!=0. When b=0 it is144ac u^2v^2, again with distinct squared-variable factors. It cannot divide a power of the single diagonal quadratic q. Equation(2) is impossible. This also excludes the degree-zero exception; homogeneity alone would not suffice there.

If q=0, Q_ki=Q_kj=0 and the quartic splits p(z)+Q_kk t^4. The Hessian is block diagonal for every t, so there is no cross-normal term. Direct multiplication gives leading Gamma_I

    (12Q_kk)^2 c^2 t^(4m+4) R_0 adj H_2 grad(h_2 R_0).

Its rational potential is in fact polynomial, has positive degree4+2r_0 and is coprime to p. The same simple-root Jacobian argument excludes a pair identity.

Every pair is therefore nonradial. The sign-equivariant projective-pencil implication rules out a zero ternary extactic, as claimed.

## Exact scope and next step

This is a complete proof for the explicitly described ternary family, subject to its independent gate. It enlarges the lower-dimensional hereditary toolbox and preserves dense nongeneric couplings, signs and vanishing row factors. It does not make the higher-dimensional trajectory span equal to an invariant hyperplane foliation, does not establish an isolated curvature maximum, and does not justify restricting rational coefficients across a pole. Those gaps remain separate.

The source history is inherited from the classical binary Hessian and planar extactic theory recorded in cycle9. The product-R extension and its normal-gradient correction are our deductions. The next discriminating mathematical question is whether the same family in arbitrary dimension always has nonzero extactic; the ternary pencil reduction no longer applies there.
