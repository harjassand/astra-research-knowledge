WHY THE ADDITIONAL INTERFACE MATTERS

Revision03 uses the fundamental q-level representation of the full SU(q)
algebra. It does not by itself cover a physical spin-S Heisenberg model at
S>1/2: such a system has three SU(2) generators acting on 2S+1 local levels,
not the full (2S+1)^2-1 SU(2S+1) Casimir. The following extension runs the
diffusion in fundamental group coordinates while emitting states of any
supplied finite unitary representation. This closes that interface gap.
It is not a claim for arbitrary local generators without Lie relations.

THEOREM CANDIDATE AND KNOWN INPUT

Fix q>=2 and a known finite-dimensional unitary representation pi of SU(q)
on C^p, with Hermitian generators S_a=pi_*(T_a), a=1,...,D=q^2-1, where
Tr_q(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2 is the same fundamental normalization as revision03.
The matrices S_a, their exact Lie relations, and representation are supplied
or verified inputs. The S_a are not assumed to be trace-orthonormal in C^p.
Let

 G=(sum_a ||S_a||^2)^1/2,   Lrep=sqrt(2)G,
 J_a=sum_i S_a^(i),        C_N^pi=sum_a J_a^2.

For fixed beta>0,M,B,kappa>1,gamma>=0, and every known real symmetric
||A||<=M and |b|<=B, the WHOLE Gibbs density

 rho_N proportional to exp[(beta/N)C_N^pi+N^-kappa Q_A+N^-gamma J_b]

has a known-parameter positive identical-p-level-product approximation with
physical half-trace distance <=C exp(-cN), for all sufficiently large N.
The constants depend on q,beta,M,B,kappa,gamma and the supplied G. A fixed
small anisotropy at scale 1/N is also covered by the explicit neighborhood
in Section5 below. Neither theorem is dimension-free in q,p or G.

1. ACQUIRE THE CORRECT PHYSICAL HEAT SEED

The Weyl character/Gaussian proof of (G2) in revision03 applies irrep by irrep
to any finite representation, including reducible ones and its tensor powers.
Thus exactly

 [integral g_t(h) (exp(pi_*(h)))^tensor N dh]/[integral g_t(h)dh]
       =exp(t C_N^pi),
 g_t(h)=exp[-Tr_q(h^2)/(2t)] product_(i<j)sinch((h_i-h_j)/2).

The normalized heat seed is the mixture of
sigma_pi(h)^tensor N, sigma_pi(h)=exp(pi_*(h))/Tr_p exp(pi_*(h)),
under the explicit density g_t(h)[Tr_p exp(pi_*(h))]^N. The classical
dimension D and root Jacobian depend on the group SU(q), not on physical p.

If h=sum h_a T_a, then |h_a|_2=sqrt(2)||h||_F, so
||pi_*(h)||<=Lrep||h||_F. Since each S_a has trace zero,
Tr_p exp(pi_*(h))>=p by Jensen, and it is <=p exp(Lrep||h||_F).
Set

 R=4beta(Lrep+1)+4BG+4,     a=exp(-2R)/q,
 K_q=q(q-1)/(2sqrt(2)),
 c0=R^2/(8beta),
 Cseed=exp(1/beta)(8beta)^(D/2)Gamma(D/2+1).

For beta/(2N)<=t0<=beta/N, N>=K_q and ||h||_F>=R, the same Gaussian
tail proof as revision03 now uses
 -(N/(2beta))||h||^2+(NLrep+K_q)||h||
 <=-(N/(4beta))||h||^2.
It gives Pbad<=Cseed exp(-c0N). Here p^N cancels in the lower and upper
normalizers. The fundamental coordinate r_h=exp h/Tr_q exp h obeys r_h>=aI
on each retained branch, even though the PHYSICAL state is sigma_pi(h).
Replace bad branches by the corresponding coordinate I/q and its physical
state I/p; the physical trace error of the seed is <=2Pbad.

2. THE GROUP-COORDINATE KERNEL AND ITS EXACT FILTER IDENTITIES

For any positive trace-one fundamental coordinate r, put
 h_r=log r-(Tr_q log r)I/q,
 sigma_pi(r)=exp(pi_*(h_r))/Tr_p exp(pi_*(h_r)),
 k_N^pi(r)=sigma_pi(r)^tensor N.

Under fundamental normalized filtering
r(s)=exp(sT)r exp(sT)/Tr_q(exp(2sT)r), the determinant-normalized group
element r/(det r)^(1/q) is conjugated by exp(sT). Applying pi therefore
filters the physical state by exp(sS_T). Local group conjugation likewise
intertwines physical rotations. This is a group representation identity,
not a claimed CP map from arbitrary fundamental coordinate states.

Use the SAME fundamental-coordinate vector fields as before,
 v_T={T,r}-2Tr_q(T r)r,     z_T=i[T,r],
but use the PHYSICAL scalar mean
 m_T^pi(r)=Tr_p(S_T sigma_pi(r)),
 B_T^pi=2N m_T^pi+v_T.derivative,     R_T=z_T.derivative.
Then exactly

 {J_T,k_N^pi}=B_T^pi k_N^pi,
 i[J_T,k_N^pi]=R_T k_N^pi,
 {J_T^2,k_N^pi}/2=[(B_T^pi)^2-R_T^2]k_N^pi/4.

The fundamental v,z fields determine the principal diffusion, irrespective
of the physical representation. For C=A+Lambda I, alpha=N^-kappa and
u=N^-gamma, its signed covariance is the SAME D_C from revision03:

 D_C=sum_j lambda_j(v_j tensor v_j-z_j tensor z_j).

Thus D_I>=2eta^2 I and |D_A|<=10M I on r>=etaI. The drift's leading
term is now alpha N sum_j lambda_j m_j^pi v_j, instead of the fundamental
mean. Its potential is exactly Tr_pN[(alpha Q_C+uJ_b)k_N^pi], with
the same product-moment formula and the physical S_j. These coefficients
are explicit smooth matrix-log/exponential functions on the compact region;
they are not described as low-cost polynomial functions.

3. ACQUIRE A POSITIVE COMPACT DIFFUSION AND CHECK ITS LOGDET BARRIER

Let mu_a=Tr_q(T_a r), m_a=Tr_p(S_a sigma_pi(r)), cF=(q^2-1)/(2q).
Then |mu|<=1/sqrt(2) and |m|<=G. For the coordinate ell=logdet r,
the exact generator without its potential gives

 L ell=-2alpha N q m^T C mu
        -alpha q[Tr_q(rZ_C)-mu^T C mu]-u q mu_b,
 Z_C=sum_j lambda_j T_(n_j)^2,
 d<ell_martingale>/dt=2alpha q^2 mu^T C mu.             (R1)

Indeed v_T ell=-2q mu_T, z_T ell=0 and
v_T^2 ell=-4q[Tr_q(T^2r)-mu_T^2]; the coefficient 4N m_T^pi in the
drift produces the displayed cross term. This distinguishes representation
mean m from coordinate mean mu and avoids substituting a fundamental-only
logdet formula into a higher-spin model.

Choose Kbar=1+qB/sqrt(2), eta=a^q exp(-2Kbar-2),
Lambda=(20M+2)/eta^2 and Lc=Lambda+M. The known margin D_C>=1 holds
on r>=etaI/2. Require

 N^(kappa-1)>=max{2Lambda/beta,
                   qLc(sqrt(2)G+cF+1/2)},
 N>=max{K_q,1}.

Then t0=beta/N-Lambda alpha is in the required seed interval, and (R1)
obeys L ell>=-Kbar, with martingale quadratic variation <=alpha q^2 Lc.
Starting with r>=aI, stopping when lambda_min(r)=eta, the same logdet
argument gives P(tau<=1)<=exp[-N^kappa/(2q^2 Lc)]. All physical path
outputs remain positive identical-product operators with frozen positive
Feynman--Kac weights.

4. COMPARE THE WHOLE PHYSICAL DENSITY

For H=alpha Q_C+uJ_b the global physical norm is bounded by

 h_N=Lc G^2 N^(2-kappa)+u BG N.                         (R2)

This follows from ||C_N^pi||<=sum_a ||J_a||^2<=G^2N^2 and
||J_b||<=BGN. The physical Casimir still commutes with all J_a. The exact
seed-to-sandwich target relation, stopped Ito residual and Duhamel bound
from revision03 therefore apply to the physical p^N-dimensional density,
giving

 T(rho_N,sigma_N)
 <=2Cseed exp(h_N-c0N)
       +h_N exp[2h_N-N^kappa/(2q^2 Lc)].                (R3)

The target trace is >=1 by representation invariance of its seed, zero
mean J_b and positivity of Q_C. If gamma>0 then h_N=o(N); if gamma=0,
h_N/N tends to BG. Our R choice gives
c0>=4BG+4>BG, so (R3) implies C exp(-cN) in either case.

5. FIXED ANISOTROPY NEIGHBORHOOD IN ANY FINITE REPRESENTATION

For the root-proposed scope Q_(betaI+A)/N+uJ_b, 0<=u<=1, define

 Hstar=beta G^2+BG,
 Kstar=qbeta(sqrt(2)G+cF+1/2)+qB/sqrt(2),
 Lstar=2q sqrt(beta(Hstar+1)),
 eta=a^q exp(-Kstar-Lstar),
 Mstar=beta eta^2/80.

For ||A||<=Mstar use t0=beta/(2N), C=betaI/2+A, alpha=1/N. Then
||C||<=beta and D_C>=beta eta^2/8 even on r>=etaI/2. Equation(R1)
has drift >=-Kstar and variation <=q^2beta/N. Its exit probability is
<=exp[-2(Hstar+1)N]. The physical ||H||<=Hstar N, and our R choice
gives c0>=4betaG^2+4BG+4>=Hstar+2. Thus exactly as revision04,

 T(rho_N,sigma_N)<=(2Cseed+Hstar N)exp(-2N)              (R4)

for N>=max{K_q,1}. Mstar is an extremely small, explicitly charged positive
radius. This is not arbitrary fixed anisotropy or a uniform representation
theorem. It is an alternative seeded verification of the proposed broader
neighborhood statement.

HIGHER-SPIN AND COST CONSEQUENCES

For SU(2) spin S, p=2S+1, ||S_a||=S and G=sqrt(3)S. Equations(R3)/(R4)
apply to the physical Casimir J_x^2+J_y^2+J_z^2 with this charged G,
including S>1/2. This is an actual higher-spin consequence; it does not
replace the three-generator physical Hamiltonian by the full SU(p) one.

The known representation descriptor contains D matrices of p^2 entries;
acquiring/checking it and its Lie normalization is a separate input cost.
Computing sigma_pi(r) requires matrix logarithms/exponentials on q and p
dimensions with conditioning through eta. Stochastic law sampling and
weights still have no executed or efficient finite-bit compiler. The full
physical-matrix fallback now costs at least p^(2N) entries and the local
product mesh has p^2 coordinates. No dimension-free hardware, sample,
precision or energy benefit is inferred. Unknown representation/A/b
acquisition and arbitrary reference preservation are outside the interface.

Own check_higher_spin_filter.py verifies the kernel/filter/quadratic signed
identities on spin1 and spin3/2 physical representations in SU(2) coordinates,
two rational axes, N=1,2. The evidence file is higher_spin_filter_checks.json.
The finite checks are transcription support, not the quantified proof.
