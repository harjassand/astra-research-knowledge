## 1. Prime-selective bounded-degree expansion

**Source:** [release family 177](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Bounded-degree-coboundary-expanders-in-every-dimension-September-24-2026/paper.pdf), plus [Iwasa pro-stability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04998), [Morrow pro Tor-unitality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.4179), and [KMS cosystolic expansion, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05823v2). Full argument: `prime_selective_expansion.md`.

The reusable mechanism is **finite-index transfer plus a pro-zero stabilization cokernel**. In the t-power tower over F_q[t], all homology transition maps are onto after inverting q. Iwasa's *pro-surjectivity* range therefore becomes ordinary finite-level surjectivity. Two stabilizations eliminate ambient conjugation, and averaging over the finite q-group quotient forces vanishing away from q.

For each fixed d>=3 and sufficiently large fixed odd prime q, the same bounded-degree d-complex family expands in every degree i<d **simultaneously for every finite Abelian coefficient group whose order is coprime to q**, with constants independent of the tower level and alphabet size. Using pro-surjectivity rather than pro-isomorphism lowers matrix rank from N=2d+1 to N=2d. The displayed degree bound falls by a factor 2q^{2d} at the same q; the field threshold and expansion constants remain dimension-dependent.

There is an exact obstruction at q: Gamma_N(m)/Gamma_N(2m) maps onto the additive trace-zero matrices over F_q[t]/t^m, giving at least m(N^2-1) independent F_q first-cohomology classes. Thus positive degree-one coboundary expansion fails for any finite alphabet with q-torsion. Integral coefficients also fail in degree two: H_1 is finite, nonzero q-primary, so UCT contributes nonzero Ext(H_1,Z) to H^2. This is not expansion for all Abelian or even all torsion-free coefficient groups.

At the excluded characteristic, KMS cosystoles plus degree-height lifting yield q-ary CSS parameters n=Theta(q^{(m-1)(N^2-1)}), k>=m(N^2-1), cohomological distance Omega(n), homological distance at least m. These asymmetric bounds only guarantee logarithmic minimum quantum distance. For binary alphabets q is odd and H^1=0, so the corresponding binary CSS construction has no logical qubits. No decoder or competitive good quantum LDPC result follows.

**Gate:** the released affine/opposition-complex identification and all integral acyclic attachments were reconstructed at proof level, but not independently formalized or audited against every cited building theorem. The coefficient/rank extension is conditional on that geometric input. [2510.18351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18351) already proves degree-one prime avoidance, including non-Abelian groups, in other congruence quotients; this is not a new degree-one discovery. Explicit complexes retain Theta(n log n) bits; d,q are fixed but may be large.

## 2. Binary-rational weighted hafnians

**Source:** [release family 113](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-Fully-Polynomial-Randomized-Approximation-Scheme-for-Perfect-Matchings-in-General-Graphs-September-23-2026/main.pdf). Full exact reduction: `weighted_hafnian_reduction.md`.

An integer edge activity K can be represented by a simple O(log K)-vertex gadget with matching signatures inactive=1, active=K, and one-terminal=0. It encodes K directed paths via a binary recurrence, then turns paths into matching completions. Clearing binary-rational denominators costs polynomial bits. Thus the released **unweighted** general-graph FPRAS would imply a full-bit FPRAS and approximate weighted matching sampler for arbitrary nonnegative rational hafnians, with no density, weight-ratio, or numerical conditioning assumption. Signed/complex weights do not transfer: matching terms 1,-1,0 have hafnian 0 whereas absolute weights give 2.

**Gate:** family 113's long-cycle energy comparison, sampler analysis, and complete finite-bit implementation were not acquired or independently validated. Its powers/constants are enormous. This reduction is elementary and its priority is unestablished; it does not validate the release. Unweighted f-factors and fixed-size matchings are already source corollaries.

## 3. Direct nonnegative pure Gaussian photon sampler

Full proof and worst-case finite-bit construction: `nonnegative_gbs_sampling.md`. This settled derivation **does not depend on family 113**.

For supplied symmetric entrywise nonnegative rational B with ||B||<1, zero displacement, and mean total photon number E=tr(B^2(I-B^2)^(-1)), the full collision-inclusive photon law has PGF

\[
G(z)=\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)}/\sqrt{\det(I-(D_zB)^2)}.
\]

Its logarithm is a positive sum over even rooted closed walks: intensity product B_edges / walk_length. Consequently photon counts are exactly the occupation vector of independent Poisson walks. Powers of B provide a Doob bridge sampler for each walk without hafnian evaluation. Discarding walk lengths >2K, K=ceil(2E/epsilon), costs at most epsilon/4 in TV. Conservative binomial approximations to the Poisson counts and finite rational categorical draws supply a worst-case bit algorithm polynomial in encoded matrix size, **numerical E**, and epsilon^{-1}. Hence it is polynomial in input length only under a polynomial-mean-photon promise. A short encoding of exponentially large E does not satisfy that resource condition.

This represents the **measured number distribution**; it does not assert that the quantum state is a coherent-state mixture. A supplied diagonal-unitary gauge B=DAD reduces to nonnegative A. Arbitrary phases, displacement, general mixed Gaussian states, and rare conditioning remain outside this sampler's established interface. For event probability a, a rejection-based conditional error eta requires full-law error <=a eta and roughly 1/a attempts. Disjoint b=1/2 two-mode blocks have polynomial mean E=2s/3 but no-collision probability (15/16)^s; free conditioning would be a false transfer.

**Precise ancestry and novelty gate:** the exact rooted-walk/Poisson formulas are [Le Jan's discrete Markov loop measure, equations (2.4), (2.5), (4.1)](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~lejan/pdfs/stfl1.pdf). On A=[[0,B],[B,0]], h=(I-A)^{-1}1 and P=diag(h)^{-1}A diag(h) give a substochastic transition matrix with identical loop products. The photon vector is the color-forgetting discrete visit field of its standard alpha=1/2 loop soup. Bipartiteness forces evenness; the two lifts cancel alpha. Thus the loop representation is an established-mathematics consequence, not a new loop-soup theorem. An independent sibling-agent audit found this same typing and no factor/parity error in the Gaussian PGF or bridge sampling. A limited source search did not establish priority of the exact GBS specialization or finite-bit cost statement; no first-discovery claim is warranted.

[Anand et al., 2511.16558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16558) already samples the collision-free unweighted graph subset law on **all** graphs, superseding a 2025 dense-graph result. Their two-layer near-perfect ratio proof extends to bounded nonnegative weights; k copies per mode then yield a second, slower collision-inclusive sampler by a birthday-factor approximation. These earlier implications mean that a broad 'nonnegative GBS becomes easy because of family 113' claim would be false.
