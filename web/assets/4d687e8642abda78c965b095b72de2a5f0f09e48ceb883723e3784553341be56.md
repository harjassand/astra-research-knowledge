## 1. Scope, sources, and exact interface

`for every C > 16/log 2 there exists b_0(C) such that`

`for every integer b >= b_0(C) and every integer 1 <= a < b,`

`N(a,b) <= C log log b`.

The threshold matters: `C log log 2 < 0`, although `N(1,2)=1`. Neither the exact endpoint `C=16/log 2` nor a numerical threshold is claimed.

## 2. Elementary primorial size, without PNT

Write `p_j` for the j-th prime and `P_m = product_{j=1}^m p_j`. We need only

`log P_m = m log m + O(m log log(m+2))` as `m -> infinity`.

Here is a self-contained elementary derivation. For real `x >= 6`, set `n=floor(x/2) >= 3`. The largest coefficient in `(1+1)^(2n)` is central, so

`binom(2n,n) >= 2^(2n)/(2n+1) >= 2^n`.

For a prime p, Legendre's valuation formula gives

`v_p(binom(2n,n)) = sum_{j>=1} [floor(2n/p^j)-2 floor(n/p^j)]`.

Each summand is zero or one, and at most `floor(log(2n)/log p)` summands are nonzero. Thus each prime contributes at most `log(2n)` to the logarithm of the coefficient, and

`pi(x) log x >= log binom(2n,n) >= n log 2 >= (log 2/3)x`.

Consequently `pi(x) >= (log 2/3) x/log x` for `x>=6`. Taking `x=D j log(2j)` with a sufficiently large fixed D gives `pi(x)>=j` for all sufficiently large j, because `log x <= 2 log(2j)` eventually. Increasing an absolute constant to handle finitely many j yields

`j+1 <= p_j <= A j log(2j)` for every j>=1.

The lower inequality is just distinctness of the prime integers. Summing logarithms and using the elementary integral bounds for `log(m!)` gives

`log((m+1)!) <= log P_m <= log(m!)+m log log(2m)+m log A`.

Both sides imply the required estimate. This imports no prime number theorem.

Define for real `u>=2`

`ell(u) = ceil(5 log u/(6 log 2)) + 1`,

`P(u) = product of the first ell(u) primes`.

Then the number of positive divisors is

`N=2^ell(u) >= 2 u^(5/6)`,

and the elementary estimate just proved implies

`log P(u) = (5/(6 log 2)+o(1)) log u log log u`.

The extra prime changes only a lower-order term while providing the factor two needed in the collision count.

## 3. A positive nine-term supply for every sufficiently large odd integer

Fix an odd integer u and let A be the set of positive divisors of the squarefree integer `P(u)`. For every prime `p<=u`, let `H_p` be the image of A modulo p. Call p bad if `h_p=|H_p| <= p^(5/6)`.

For a bad prime, if the residue fibres have cardinalities `c_z`, the number of ordered distinct divisor pairs congruent modulo p is

`sum_z c_z^2 - N >= N^2/h_p - N >= N^2/u^(5/6)-N >= N^2/(2u^(5/6))`.

The last inequality uses `N>=2u^(5/6)`. Each nonzero difference of two divisors has absolute value less than P(u), hence at most `log P(u)/log 2` distinct prime factors. Counting pair-prime incidences in the other order therefore gives

`B(u) <= 2u^(5/6) log P(u)/log 2 = O(u^(5/6) log u log log u)`.

Quantitative Vinogradov gives at least `c_0 u^2/(log u)^3` ordered prime triples summing to every sufficiently large odd u. Its uniformity is essential. Indeed the cited asymptotic has the form

`R_3(u) = u^2 S(u)/(2(log u)^3) + O(u^2/(log u)^4)`,

where for odd u

`S(u) >= 2 product_{p>2}(1-1/(p-1)^2) > 0`.

The fixed positive lower bound follows from convergence of the sum of the subtracted quantities; it does not depend on the factorization of u. At most `3u B(u)` ordered triples contain a bad prime: choose its position, its bad value, and one further entry; the last entry is determined. Since

`3u B(u) / [u^2/(log u)^3] = O(u^(-1/6)(log u)^4 log log u) -> 0`,

every sufficiently large odd u admits a triple of primes good for this same A. Repeated primes are allowed.

For a good prime p, put `H=H_p`, `h=|H|>p^(5/6)`, and let U be the product of two independent uniform members of H. For every nonzero frequency t modulo p, Cauchy-Schwarz followed by additive orthogonality gives

`|sum_{a,b in H} exp(2 pi i tab/p)|^2`

`<= h sum_{a in F_p} |sum_{b in H} exp(2 pi i tab/p)|^2 = p h^2`.

Hence `|E exp(2 pi i tU/p)| <= sqrt(p)/h < p^(-1/3)`. For three independent such products, Fourier inversion and the triangle inequality imply

`Pr(U_1+U_2+U_3=0) >= [1-(p-1)p^(-1)]/p = 1/p^2 > 0`.

This endpoint calculation is the reason alpha need not exceed 5/6 once the collision count's factor two is supplied separately. Complex Fourier coefficients cause no sign issue: the displayed lower bound bounds their total absolute contribution to the real probability.

Lift the six residues in a witnessing event to positive divisors of P(u). The three integer products `e_1,e_2,e_3` are positive divisors of `P(u)^2`; zero residues still have positive integer lifts. Their sum is a positive multiple of p. Thus

`t=(e_1+e_2+e_3)/p` is a positive integer, `t<=3P(u)^2`,

`p=e_1/t+e_2/t+e_3/t`.

Apply this independently to the three good primes. Every sufficiently large odd u is a sum of nine positive rationals `e/t`, with `e|P(u)^2`, `t<=3P(u)^2`. The three primes can produce different rational denominators, and numerator/product witnesses may repeat. Neither `t|P(u)^2` nor `gcd(e,t)=1` is needed.

## 4. One simultaneous auxiliary integer

Fix an integer `r>=1`, to be selected from C and then held fixed. Put

`L=log log b`, `Y=b^(r+1)`, `K=P(Y)^2 floor(L)!`.

For sufficiently large b, every odd integer `1<=s<=Y` has the preceding supply with at most nine terms and `e|K`. The finitely many odd integers below the absolute threshold divide `floor(L)!` once `floor(L)` exceeds that fixed threshold, so `s=s/1` covers them. This is simultaneous over the whole interval, not a statement for each s with a separately chosen b. A positive even s is covered by the odd supply for s-1 plus `1=1/1`, hence uses at most ten terms; the finitely many initial cases may again use one term. Every constructed rational denominator satisfies `t<=3K`.

For fixed r, Section 2 and the negligible factorial contribution give

`log K = [5(r+1)/(3 log 2)+o(1)] log b L`.

Also `K>b` eventually. No factorization, primality, or coprimality condition is imposed on K.

## 5. Ordinary unreduced greedy prefix and its history

Begin with `(R,q)=(a,b)` and `T=bK`. While `R>0` and `q<T`, subtract the reciprocal with denominator

`n=ceil(q/R)`, then set `(R,q)=(nR-q,nq)` without reduction.

At every active step, `2<=n<=q` and `0<=R_new<R`. If a positive remainder reaches T, then

`0<R<b`, `bK<=q<(bK)^2`.

The positive remainder changes by at most `x^2/(1+x)`. After the first step it is below 1/2, and after j positive steps it is at most `2^(-2^(j-1))`. Before reaching T it is greater than `1/T`, because its unreduced numerator is a positive integer. Thus the prefix has

`j <= 1+ceil(log_2(log T/log 2)) = (1/log 2+o(1))L`

terms, uniformly over a. If zero is reached, this prefix already completes the expansion.

If `n=ceil(1/x)`, then `x-1/n < 1/[n(n-1)] <= 1/n`. Consequently every next prefix denominator is strictly larger; the eventual positive tail is smaller than every prefix reciprocal. No normalization of the prefix is required.

The unreduced q has a useful elementary invariant: every real `1<=w<=q` has a divisor `d|q` in `[w/b,w]`. Initially d=1 works. For a step `q_new=nq_old` with `n<=q_old+1`, use an old divisor when `w<=q_old`. When `w>=n`, use n times an old divisor at `w/n`. The only gap has `q_old<w<n<=q_old+1`; d=q_old works since `w<n<=bq_old`. These cases cover real endpoints and the small open interval between consecutive integers. The multipliers need not be coprime.

This invariant is constructive from the recorded multiplier history. Follow the preceding cases recursively and multiply the selected n's; no factorization of the final q is required. The supplied code implements this recursion. Its depth is at most j.

## 6. Odd grouping and conversion to unit fractions

At a positive stop set `X=RK`. Then `0<X<bK<=q`. Keep q and K fixed during the following grouping.

While `X_i>Y`, invoke divisor density at `w=X_i/b^r`, obtaining

`d_i|q`, `X_i/b^(r+1) <= d_i <= X_i/b^r`.

Let `s_i` be the largest positive odd integer at most `X_i/d_i`. This exists because `X_i/d_i>=b^r>=2`, and

`1<=s_i<=Y`, `0<=X_{i+1}=X_i-d_i s_i<2d_i<=2X_i/b^r`.

The residual is an integer. For sufficiently large b the contraction factor is less than one. Even the boundary b=2,r=1 terminates by strict integer decrease, although it cannot use a logarithmic bound with zero denominator.

If a positive residual `X_i<=Y` remains, use the final group `(d,s)=(1,X_i)`. It may be even and then uses ten supply terms. A zero residual needs no final group. The number G of nonfinal groups satisfies

`G <= log X/(r log b-log 2)+1`.

Each nonfinal group uses at most nine rational-divisor terms. For any group `s=sum e_j/t_j`, convert exactly by

`d s/(qK) = sum_j 1/[(q/d)(K/e_j)t_j]`.

Every displayed denominator is a positive integer, using only `d|q` and `e_j|K`. The rational denominators may differ and need not divide q or K. All summands are positive. Because their total with the prefix is `a/b<1`, none can have denominator one. The tail uses at most `9G+10` unit fractions, allowing repeats.

Combining `X<bK` with the K estimate gives

`j+9G+10 <= [(16+15/r)/log 2+o(1)] L`.

For the requested C, choose a fixed integer r with `15/r < C log 2-16`, then let b exceed the common supply threshold and the asymptotic threshold needed to absorb the remaining positive gap. This proves the stated uniform upper bound with repeated denominators. The choice of r precedes the limit in b; no uncontrolled parameter dependence is being absorbed into o(1).

## 7. Removing repetitions and separating the prefix

For any fixed `0<x<1` and fixed k, there are finitely many nondecreasing k-tuples of positive integer denominators summing reciprocally to x. The first denominator is at most k/x. After choosing it, the positive remaining sum and k-1 terms admit the same induction; a zero remainder with positive terms left is inadmissible.

For a repeated even denominator `m=2v>=4`, use

`2/(2v)=1/(v+1)+1/[v(v+1)]`.

For a repeated odd denominator `m=2v+1>=3`, use

`2/(2v+1)=1/(v+1)+1/[(v+1)(2v+1)]`.

Both preserve the sum and the term count. The sum of denominators strictly increases, by `(v-1)^2` or `2v^2`. Denominator one and a repeated denominator two are impossible when x<1. Finiteness of the set of lists, together with the strict potential increase, forces termination at a distinct k-tuple. Individual denominators may decrease; termination is not being inferred from monotonicity of each entry.

Apply this only to the positive tail R/q. Every reciprocal in its normalized representation is at most R/q, which is strictly below every prefix reciprocal. Thus every normalized tail denominator exceeds every prefix denominator. No collision occurs when the lists are joined, and the overall term count does not grow.

This completes the independent proof of the all-a, unreduced-input, distinct-denominator upper bound, using quantitative Vinogradov as prior mathematics. It does not establish the release's unrelated lower/counting proofs or formally certify the new argument.

## 8. Denominator size, acquisition, and obstructions

Before cleanup, every tail denominator is at most `3qK^2<3b^2K^4`; prefix denominators are below T. For fixed C and its fixed r, this gives maximum denominator `exp(O_C(log b log log b))` and total bit volume `O_C(log b (log log b)^2)`. This bound has not been proved to survive distinctness cleanup. The concrete replacement `[101,101] -> [51,5151]` already shows that cleanup need not preserve a given maximum denominator; it does not disprove the existence of some other cleanup with the desired asymptotic cap.

There is nevertheless a universal certificate-size bound after cleanup. For a sorted k-term expansion of a/b, the positive remainder before term i has denominator dividing `b product_{h<i} n_h`, and is at most k/n_i. Hence

`n_i <= kb product_{h<i} n_h <= (kb)^(2^(i-1))`.

The total denominator bit volume is at most `(2^k-1)log_2(kb)+k`. Choosing C arbitrarily close to `16/log 2` yields, for every fixed delta>0 and sufficiently large b, a distinct certificate with `O_delta((log b)^(17+delta))` total bits. This is a polynomial certificate-size existence statement, not a polynomial-time acquisition result.

Auxiliary-integer formation need not use an exact real-logarithm oracle. Replace the factorial cutoff by `M!=floor(log_2 floor(log_2 b))!`, where `M=O(L)` and M tends to infinity; both properties used by the proof remain unchanged. This M is obtained from integer bit lengths. The primorial index is also obtained exactly by finding the least integer h with `2^(6h)>=Y^5` and then taking ell=h+1. With this equally valid cutoff variant and the elementary prime bound, the auxiliary integer can be formed in polynomial time in log b for fixed r. Greedy preparation, ancestry-based divisor selection, grouping, and exact conversion also use only polynomial bit arithmetic once the required supplies are supplied. The unresolved acquisition bottleneck is finding those supplies: direct enumeration of `2^ell(Y)` primorial divisors is about `b^(5(r+1)/6)`, and prime searches extend to `Y=b^(r+1)`. These are exponential in input bit length. The Fourier probability lower bound `1/p^2` also gives no polynomial-in-log-p sampling guarantee. Cleanup terminates but no polynomial replacement-count bound was proved. No usable numerical b_0(C) is supplied.

Three products cannot be replaced by two from the size hypothesis alone. In F_127 let H be the nonzero quadratic residues. Then `h=63`, `h^6>127^5`, and products stay in H. Since -1 is a nonsquare, two products cannot sum to zero. The exact finite two-product count is zero, whereas the three-product count is positive. This obstructs a size-only two-product lemma; it does not rule out exploiting additional structure of actual primorial-divisor images.

Other exact interface failures are retained in the evidence: cleanup at total one fails for `[2,2]`, since two distinct reciprocals at least two cannot sum to one; arbitrary q lacks divisor density, as `b=2,q=101,w=50` has no divisor in `[25,50]`; omitting the asymptotic threshold fails at b=2. These are counterexamples to changed interfaces, not to the audited construction.

## 10. Independently checked exact-marker and counting consequence

The originating worker subsequently proposed transferring the optimized supply to the source's marked prefix. This extension also survives reconstruction, with no use of the main uniform theorem as a black box.

For an integer m>=4, reserve `1/m` and start the remaining fraction at `(R,q)=(m-1,m)`. Apply ordinary unreduced greedy steps except that a selected denominator m is replaced by m+1. An ordinary step has `R_new<R` and leaves a remainder below the selected reciprocal. In the exceptional step, the ceiling condition gives

`(m-1)R<q<=mR`, hence `R<=R_new<2R`,

`x_new < 2/[(m-1)(m+1)] < 1/(m+1)`.

Thus prefix denominators increase strictly; the exception occurs at most once; and `0<=R<2m` throughout. Every multiplier is at most q_old+1. The first step is ordinary with denominator two and leaves a remainder below 1/2. At most one later step fails to square the previous remainder bound, giving the source bound `j<3+log_2 log_2 T`. Stop at zero or at the first q>=T. For integer T, the last active denominator is at most T-1, so `q< T^2` even with a multiplier q_old+1.

Take `K=P(m^(r+1))^2 floor(log log m)!` and `T=2mK`. Then `T>=2m^2`, and at a positive stop

`X=RK<2mK<=q`, `R/q<1/K<1/m`.

The tail is also below every prefix reciprocal. The same divisor-density induction, odd grouping, and positive supplies apply with base m. The logarithm of X differs from the unmarked bound only by the constant log 2, so the marked expansion of one has

`1+j+9G+10 <= [(16+15/r)/log 2+o(1)] log log m`

terms. Normalize only the tail. Its sum is below the marker and every prefix reciprocal, so the exact term `1/m` and the distinct prefix survive. Therefore for every `C>16/log 2`, every sufficiently large integer m occurs in a distinct expansion of one of length at most `C log log m`. This strengthens the source's displayed `257/log 2` coefficient at the same interface.

For completeness, the exact-marker padding used to reach every longer length was also checked in the original `lem:exact-marker-padding`. In a distinct r>=3 term expansion of one, split the largest unmarked denominator s as `1/s=1/(s+1)+1/[s(s+1)]`. If the marker is the largest v, take s to be the second-largest denominator. That split can collide with v only when `v=s+1` or `v=s(s+1)`. If s=ab is composite, a,b>=2, replace it instead by

`1/s=1/(s+a)+1/[b(s+a)]`.

Both new denominators lie strictly between s+1 and s(s+1), and all other unchanged denominators are below s. If s=p is prime, then p is odd because there are at least three terms and a smaller denominator at least two. Write the sum of all terms below p as A/B with p not dividing B. The two collision cases would imply, after clearing denominators, respectively `0=B` or `0=2B` modulo p. Both are impossible. Thus one can always increase the length by exactly one and keep the exact marker.

Every fixed small marker is also available: m=2,3 occur in `[2,3,6]`; for fixed m>=4, the marked greedy procedure without a cutoff terminates because its integer numerator decreases at every ordinary step and the exceptional increase happens at most once. All these marked expansions have at least three terms, so the padding argument applies.

Let D_k be the exact denominators occurring in distinct k-term expansions of one, v(k) its first missing integer at least two, and F(k) the number of such expansions. Fix `c<log 2/16` and choose `C>16/log 2` with cC<1. For all sufficiently large k, every

`2<=m<=floor(exp(exp(ck)))`

has a marked expansion of length at most k, after absorbing the finitely many initial m, and can be padded to exactly k terms. Consequently

`liminf_{k->infinity} log log v(k)/k >= log 2/16`.

Each k-term expansion contributes at most k members to D_k, so `|D_k|<=kF(k)`. The covered interval therefore gives `F(k)>=(floor(exp(exp(ck)))-1)/k`, and hence

`liminf_{k->infinity} log log F(k)/k >= log 2/16`.

Finiteness of F(k) follows from the same sorted-list induction at the fixed positive sum one; unlike repeat removal, that finiteness argument does not require the sum to be below one. Neither endpoint eventual inequality with `c=log 2/16` nor sharpness of either slope is asserted. These are independently reconstructed consequences of the optimized construction and the elementary padding proof, not externally established new results.

For marked certificates there is also the elementary size bound `n_i<=k^(2^(i-1))`, since the total is one. It yields total bit volume `O_delta((log m)^(16+delta))` after choosing the length coefficient close enough to the limit. This still supplies no efficient acquisition algorithm or sharp bound on the maximum denominator.
