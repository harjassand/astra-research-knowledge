Cycle 3 leaf report: boundary-corrected Foster function for a multi-catalyst WR family
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE, conditional on the stated strict small-gain inequalities. No novelty claim.

Finding
-------
For this finite species family, the proposed function

  V(a,b)=sum_j lambda_j a_j + sum_i w_i b_i + K 1_{sum_j a_j=0}

does give a physical-time Foster proof when lambda_j,w_i>0 satisfy the two supplied strict inequalities and K is chosen sufficiently large (in particular K>max_j lambda_j). The exact boundary correction is

  L 1_{A=0} = -(sum_j alpha_j)1_{A=0} + sum_j delta_j 1_{a=e_j}.

The second term is essential: death of the sole remaining catalyst switches the indicator on and can create a positive LV contribution K delta_j. This occurs only at total catalyst count one; the substrate bilinear margins control that boundary when substrate counts are large, leaving a finite exception set. The result proves nonexplosion and positive recurrence for this whole state-space-irreducible family. It is conditional: strict small-gain feasibility need not hold for all positive rates.

1. Interface, assumptions, propensities
----------------------------------------
Assume finite integers r,s>=1; all species A_1,...,A_r,B_1,...,B_s are distinct; all k_ji are finite integers >=1. All constants alpha_j,delta_j,p_ji,q_ji,u_ji,v_ji are fixed and strictly positive. The state is x=(a,b) in N_0^(r+s). For every reaction y->y', use the standard stochastic mass-action propensity kappa x_under_y, with x_under_m=x(x-1)...(x-m+1), zero if x<m, and coordinatewise products across species. In particular the propensities are

  0 -> A_j:                         alpha_j
  A_j -> 0:                         delta_j a_j
  A_j -> A_j+B_i:                   p_ji a_j
  A_j+B_i -> A_j:                   q_ji a_j b_i
  A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i:       u_ji a_j b_i
  (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i -> A_j+B_i:       v_ji (a_j)_under_(k_ji+1) b_i.

The reaction graph is strongly connected on its complexes: each 0<->A_j pair links A_j with 0; each A_j<->A_j+B_i pair links the substrate-added complex to A_j; each autocatalytic reversible pair links (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i to A_j+B_i. Hence this network is weakly reversible. The claim below assumes supplied exact reaction data and positive rates; it acquires no model parameters and uses no volume scaling, time-varying controls, simulation, or oracle.

Let

  A=sum_j a_j,     B=sum_i b_i,
  I_0=1_{A=0},     I_j=1_{a=e_j},
  alpha_sum=sum_j alpha_j,     H=sum_j alpha_j lambda_j.

Here e_j is the catalyst-count vector with exactly one A_j and all other A counts zero. Assume the supplied weights lambda_j,w_i>0 satisfy

  gamma_j := lambda_j delta_j - sum_i w_i p_ji > 0       for each j,
  eta_ji := w_i q_ji - lambda_j k_ji u_ji > 0            for each (j,i).

Write gamma=min_j gamma_j>0, eta=min_(j,i) eta_ji>0, delta_max=max_j delta_j, lambda_max=max_j lambda_j, and k_max=max_(j,i) k_ji. Choose any rho>0 and

  K > max{lambda_max, (H+rho)/alpha_sum}.                 (K)

This is finite because alpha_sum>0. In particular V is strictly positive and norm-like. The strict small-gain inequalities are hypotheses, not consequences of weak reversibility.

2. Exact generator and boundary terms
--------------------------------------
First set W_lin=sum_j lambda_j a_j+sum_i w_i b_i. Directly summing rate times increment gives

  L W_lin = H
    -sum_j lambda_j delta_j a_j
    +sum_(j,i) w_i p_ji a_j
    -sum_(j,i) w_i q_ji a_j b_i
    +sum_(j,i) lambda_j k_ji u_ji a_j b_i
    -sum_(j,i) lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_under_(k_ji+1) b_i.

There is no approximation at the reaction boundary. For example, the reverse autocatalysis term is exactly zero when a_j<k_ji+1; the positive forward autocatalysis is zero when a_j=0 or b_i=0.

For I_0=1_{A=0}, only the immigration and single-catalyst death reactions can change I_0. Immigration from A=0 switches I_0 from 1 to 0 at rate alpha_j; a death A_j->0 switches it from 0 to 1 only at a=e_j, at rate delta_j. All reactions involving A_j+B_i require at least one catalyst and leave the all-catalysts-zero indicator unchanged when enabled. In particular, reverse autocatalysis subtracts k_ji from k_ji+1 catalyst copies and leaves one A_j; it never changes a nonzero catalyst vector into the zero vector. Thus, exactly,

  L I_0 = -alpha_sum I_0 + sum_j delta_j I_j.

Combining W_lin and K I_0 and collecting the hypothesized margins gives the full generator identity

  L V = H
    -sum_j gamma_j a_j
    -sum_(j,i) eta_ji a_j b_i
    -sum_(j,i) lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_under_(k_ji+1) b_i
    -K alpha_sum I_0 + K sum_j delta_j I_j.              (1)

The last positive term is the only boundary correction in (1). It is zero unless A=1, in which case it is at most K delta_max.

3. Explicit finite Foster set and physical-time clock
-----------------------------------------------------
At A=0 all catalytic reactions are disabled, all a_j=0, and I_j=0. For every substrate vector b,

  L V(0,b)=H-K alpha_sum < -rho                         (2)

by (K). This handles the unbounded substrate boundary where no substrate reaction is currently available.

At A=1 the catalyst vector is e_j for some j. Dropping the nonpositive reverse-autocatalytic term in (1), and using the minima gamma,eta, gives

  L V <= H-gamma+K delta_max-eta B.                      (3)

Define the integer

  B_1=max{0, ceil((H+K delta_max+rho-gamma)/eta)}.

Then (3) gives LV<=-rho whenever A=1 and B>=B_1. The potentially adverse indicator jump from catalyst death is therefore confined to the finite strip A=1, B<B_1.

When A>=2, the indicator correction in (1) vanishes. Moreover,

  sum_j gamma_j a_j >= gamma A,
  sum_(j,i) eta_ji a_j b_i >= eta A B >= 2 eta B,

so

  L V <= H-gamma A-2 eta B.                              (4)

Consequently LV<=-rho whenever A>=2 and gamma A+2 eta B>=H+rho. An explicit exceptional set is

  F_rho = {x: A=1, B<B_1}
          union {x: A>=2, gamma A+2 eta B < H+rho}.       (5)

Equation (2) shows no A=0 state belongs to F_rho. The set (5) is finite since gamma,eta>0; outside it LV<=-rho. For a crude explicit cardinality bound, put A_max=floor((H+rho)/gamma), B_max=floor((H+rho)/(2 eta)). Then

  |F_rho| <= r binom(B_1+s-1,s)
             + binom(A_max+r,r) binom(B_max+s,s),         (6)

with the first term interpreted as zero if B_1=0. This is an upper bound, not an optimized set size.

The Foster function is norm-like because lambda,w are strictly positive and K>0; all its jumps are bounded by

  J = max{K+lambda_max, max_i w_i, k_max lambda_max}.

Its generator also has the explicit global upper bound

  L V <= C_+ := H + K delta_max,                          (7)

since all terms other than H and the last boundary-death term in (1) are nonpositive under the strict margins. The state sublevel sets of V are finite and all reaction rates are bounded on each such set. Let tau_R=inf{t>=0:V(X_t)>=R}. Stopped Dynkin applied before tau_R gives, for finite t,

  E_x[V(X_(t wedge tau_R))] <= V(x)+C_+ t,
  P_x(tau_R<=t) <= (V(x)+C_+t)/R.                         (8)

Bounded jumps and bounded rates on finite sublevel sets rule out infinitely many jumps while the process remains bounded. Letting R tend to infinity in (8) rules out finite-time escape to infinity. Thus the minimal CTMC is nonexplosive/conservative.

For x outside F_rho, stop at tau_F=inf{t>=0:X_t in F_rho}. Since LV<=-rho until tau_F and V>=0, stopped Dynkin yields

  E_x[t wedge tau_F] <= V(x)/rho,
  E_x[tau_F] <= V(x)/rho.                                 (9)

This is a physical-time expected hitting bound in the CTMC's time units. Applying the standard finite-set CTMC Foster criterion to V/rho gives positive recurrence in each closed irreducible component.

4. Communicating-class check
----------------------------
Under the stated strictly positive rates and r,s>=1, the whole lattice N_0^(r+s) is in fact irreducible. From an arbitrary state, if A=0 and B>0, first immigrate one catalyst A_1. Choose any catalyst species A_j currently present. For each i, fire A_j+B_i->A_j exactly b_i times; this is enabled whenever b_i>0, requires only a_j>=1, and leaves all catalysts unchanged. Then fire A_j->0 as needed for each j. This reaches the origin. Conversely, from the origin immigrate the desired catalyst counts if the target has any; use any positive catalyst to produce each target B_i count with A_j->A_j+B_i. If the target has no catalyst but has substrates, temporarily immigrate A_1, produce the desired substrates, and remove that catalyst. All paths use enabled transitions with positive rates. Jumps remain in the nonnegative lattice by the factorial availability convention. Thus there is one closed irreducible class, and the Foster result gives positive recurrence of the entire network's CTMC, not merely a claim on a subset of classes.

5. Holding-time and fast-cycle audit
------------------------------------
The total exit rate at x is

  q(x)=alpha_sum + sum_j delta_j a_j
       +sum_(j,i)[p_ji a_j + q_ji a_j b_i + u_ji a_j b_i
                   +v_ji (a_j)_under_(k_ji+1) b_i].      (10)

It is finite at each state but unbounded. The holding time at x is Exp(q(x)), with mean 1/q(x). If X_n is the embedded reaction-event chain, then

  E[V(X_(n+1))-V(X_n) | X_n=x] = L V(x)/q(x).

Equations (8)-(9) and the Foster criterion concern elapsed physical time; they do not follow from an event-count tail or a jump-chain assertion. Here the rate-weighted generator drift includes the possibly very short holding times and directly controls the CTMC clock.

The two strict margins have a reaction-by-reaction interpretation. The A_j->A_j+B_i production terms grow B_i at rate p_ji a_j; the catalyst drain delta_j a_j dominates their weighted sum for each j through gamma_j>0. The potentially explosive autocatalytic edge grows A_j by k_ji at propensity u_ji a_j b_i; the paired A_j+B_i->A_j edge consumes B_i at propensity q_ji a_j b_i, and eta_ji>0 makes that matched negative term dominate even when reverse autocatalysis is disabled at a_j<=k_ji. When a_j>=k_ji+1, reverse autocatalysis contributes an additional nonpositive term. At b_i=0, all a_j b_i terms vanish and catalyst loss controls the p_ji a_j terms. When all catalysts vanish, all such reactions switch off, but immigration lowers the indicator barrier with drift (2). When there is exactly one catalyst, its possible death-induced barrier increase is K delta_j, handled by (3); when two or more are present, the barrier is unchanged by any one-molecule catalyst death.

6. Exact small-gain scope and limitations
------------------------------------------
For fixed positive rates, the strict inequalities are feasible exactly when there exist w_i>0 such that for every j,i

  sum_l p_jl w_l < [delta_j q_ji/(k_ji u_ji)] w_i.       (11)

Indeed, (11) is equivalent to
  (sum_l p_jl w_l)/delta_j < q_ji w_i/(k_ji u_ji)
for every i, so one can choose lambda_j strictly between the common lower bound and the smallest upper bound. Conversely, any feasible lambda,w implies (11). This is a homogeneous strict LP feasibility condition; normalize sum_i w_i=1. Equivalently maximize z subject to sum_i w_i=1, w_i>=z, lambda_j>=z, delta_j lambda_j-sum_i p_ji w_i>=z, and q_ji w_i-k_ji u_ji lambda_j>=z; the small-gain condition holds exactly if the LP admits z>0.

For r=s=1, the condition is exactly delta_1 q_11 > p_11 k_11 u_11. For multiple substrates, checking each pairwise product delta_j q_ji > p_ji k_ji u_ji is necessary but not sufficient because the first inequality aggregates all p_ji. Example: r=1,s=2, delta=1, p_11=p_12=1, q_11=q_12=2, k_11=k_12=u_11=u_12=1. Both pairwise checks give 2>1, but the full inequalities would require lambda>w_1+w_2 and lambda<2w_1, lambda<2w_2; the latter imply w_1+w_2>lambda, contradiction. This is an exact infeasibility witness to unconditional applicability, not a counterexample to the conditional Foster theorem. For arbitrary positive rates the LP may fail; stability in that case is UNKNOWN here.

What is proved is a sufficient small-gain theorem for this finite WR family, not a characterization of positive recurrence, not necessity of (11), not geometric/exponential ergodicity, not a stationary-tail estimate, and not a theorem for arbitrary weakly reversible networks. Indeed this V cannot satisfy a global linear drift LV<=C-epsilon V for any epsilon>0: along A=0 with B tending to infinity, V tends to infinity while LV=H-K alpha_sum is constant. The finite-set Foster drift is the applicable conclusion. The finite-set and physical-time bounds depend explicitly on alpha,delta,p,q,u, the chosen weights, K, and rho. No optimization or bit-complexity claim is made; exact rational-rate LP feasibility can be checked by a finite LP, but encoding, conditioning, and rate acquisition are outside the theorem. No computation or simulation was required for the symbolic proof.

7. Primary source coverage, falsifier, next gate
-------------------------------------------------
The standard positive-recurrence input is the continuous-time Foster criterion: David F. Anderson and Jinsu Kim, “Some network conditions for positive recurrence of stochastically modeled reaction networks,” arXiv:1710.11263v3 (22 August 2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11263, Theorem 3.1. Its statement requires a finite set and positive function with generator drift <=-1 outside that set; our V/rho and F_rho provide those objects, after the independent nonexplosion argument. Their network-specific results cover other classes; no priority claim is drawn from them.

For scope comparison only: Chuang Xu, “On the Regulary of Reaction Systems,” arXiv:2409.05340v2 (9 May 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05340, Theorem 3.6, gives a regularity condition requiring every reaction increasing along some positive direction to have propensity O(||x||_1). This family does not meet that criterion: for every positive direction, the reaction A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i increases the weighted count by k_ji times the positive A_j weight while its forward propensity u_ji a_j b_i is quadratic. Nonexplosion here instead follows from the matched negative q_ji term plus the boundary-corrected drift bound (7). No novelty or priority claim is made; exact-family literature coverage remains open.

Exact falsifier of the conditional theorem: exhibit finite r,s,k and strictly positive rates, weights satisfying all gamma_j>0 and eta_ji>0, and K satisfying (K), for which a statewise transition audit violates (1), or for which the explicit bounds fail to yield LV<=-rho outside (5). A stronger stochastic falsifier would be an exact proof of explosion or infinite physical-time expected return in such an admissible parameter instance. Separate falsifier of universal applicability: the r=1,s=2 LP-infeasible rates above already show not every positive rate choice satisfies the candidate small-gain hypothesis; they do not refute recurrence beyond this certificate.

Next gate: independently review the availability-switch calculation and the LP equivalence; then search for a feasible parameter example where a weaker/alternative Lyapunov function handles LP failure. The exact family’s unresolved scope is whether strict small-gain feasibility is only a convenient sufficient condition or detects a genuine recurrence/explosion phase boundary.
