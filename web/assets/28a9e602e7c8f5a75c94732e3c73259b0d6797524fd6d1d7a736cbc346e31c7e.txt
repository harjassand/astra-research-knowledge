VERDICT

I found no failed step in Sections 8–10 under their stated finite-dimensional
interface and the representation/frame hypotheses established in Sections
4–6. The branch-rank reduction, self-score normalization, coefficient solve,
linear-in-energy estimate, and ordered exponential-series count have the
stated factors. The construction controls the whole output state in trace
distance, not just a first moment. Section 13 proves a conditional extension:
once its finite classical polynomial-frame input (13.1) is supplied with the
stated uniform conditioning, the operational theorem applies. It does not
establish (13.1) for every other simple Lie type, and expressly does not claim
an unconditional all-types corollary.

1. INTERFACE AND ARBITRARY BRANCHES (SOURCE §§1, 8–10)

For a fixed carrier V_a of dimension d, the allowed encoder is blind to the
unknown orbit point g, has unrestricted classical outcomes and local
ancillas, and has quantum branch dimension at most Q in every branch. There
is no entangled reference or quantum side channel; the decoder returns to
the same carrier V_a. The risk is the full-state half trace norm, uniformly
over g and h. No covariance or block-diagonal restriction is imposed on an
arbitrary candidate channel.

If E_{y alpha}:V_a->K_y are encoder Kraus operators and D_{y beta}:K_y->V_a
are conditional decoder Kraus operators, the composite Kraus operators
A_{y beta alpha}=D_{y beta}E_{y alpha} obey rank(A_{y beta alpha})<=Q and
sum A_i^dagger A_i=I. This remains true if an A_i mixes any weight or
conjugation-sector components: the proof applies to each full matrix A_i,
not to a sector-preserving subclass. Conversely, for any such Kraus family,
the polar factorization A_i=V_i|A_i| gives an encoder into the rank(A_i)
support, records i classically, and uses the branch partial isometry V_i
(extended to a trace-preserving decoder) to recover A_i. Thus the rank-Q
Kraus characterization matches the stated instrument interface. Unlimited
classical labels are not charged in e*_Q. Continuous labels cause no issue
for the integral inequalities; the upper witness can be made finite by the
Choi-orbit quadrature in §12.

2. SELF-SCORE IDENTITY AND NORMALIZATION (SOURCE §8)

Set W_g=d tau_(h,g), so Tr W_g=d, and let alpha=Tr(W_e^2)/d. Irreducibility
gives int W_g^2 dg=alpha I. For TP Kraus operators A_i,

  ||[W,A_i]||_2^2
   =Tr(W^2 A_i A_i^dagger)+Tr(W^2 A_i^dagger A_i)
      -2 Tr(W A_i W A_i^dagger),

after cyclic rearrangement. Haar averaging the first two terms gives alpha
times sum_i Tr(A_i A_i^dagger)=alpha d=Tr(W_e^2), and likewise alpha times
sum_i Tr(A_i^dagger A_i)=Tr(W_e^2). Therefore

  Tr W_e^2 - int Tr[W_g Phi(W_g)]dg
      = (1/2) sum_i int ||[W_g,A_i]||_2^2 dg.

This uses TP and the two trace equalities, not unitality (sum_i A_i A_i^dagger
need not be I). The factor is 1/2.

The test operator W_g/M_0 is an effect: ||W_g||<=exp(2hB_0)<=M_0. Trace
distance obeys T(rho,sigma)=max_{0<=E<=I}Tr(E(rho-sigma)), with no additional
factor 1/2 in this variational formula. Since tau=W/d, averaging this test
gives the self-score gap divided by d M_0, hence

  e(Phi;h) >= (1/(2 d M_0)) sum_i int ||[W_g,A_i]||_2^2 dg.

In an eigenbasis of B_g, the divided difference for exp(h B_g) is at least
h exp(-hB_0), while d/Tr(exp(hB_g))>=exp(-hB_0). Thus
||[W_g,A]||_2 >= h exp(-2hB_0)||[B_g,A]||_2. Squaring, using h>=h_0 and
M_0=exp(2h_1B_0), gives exactly the coefficient
h_0^2 exp(-6h_1B_0)/(2d) in (8.2). For the real irreducible adjoint action,
Haar orthogonality gives

  int ||[B_g,A]||_2^2 dg = (r^2/q) E(A)/N^2 = gamma E(A)/N^2.

Finally E(A_i)>=Lambda_a(Q)||A_i||_2^2 by homogeneity and rank(A_i)<=Q,
and sum_i||A_i||_2^2=Tr I=d. This recovers c_* in (2.2). The normalization
does not presume that the channel is unital or that the code's Kraus
operators preserve sectors.

3. COEFFICIENT SOLVE, SIGNS, AND TRACE CONVENTION (SOURCE §9)

Here Y_(s,A) is Hermitian and the Gram convention is
Tr(Y_(s,A)Y_(t,B))=d G_(N,st) delta_AB; G is normalized by carrier
dimension d. The pairing used to solve for theta is the ORDINARY (unnormalized)
Hilbert–Schmidt trace, summed over the q generator labels. It therefore gives
qd G_N theta on the left, not qG theta, dG theta, or G theta:

  sum_A Tr(Y_(t,A) D_F(T_A/N))
       = sum_s theta_s sum_A d G_(N,ts)
       = qd (G_N theta)_t.

For Hermitian F, ad_F:X->[F,X] is self-adjoint in the HS inner product, so
D_F= int ad_(F_g)^2 dg is self-adjoint. Consequently

  Tr(Y_(t,A)D_F(T_A/N))
       = int Tr([F_g,Y_(t,A)]^dagger [F_g,T_A/N]) dg.

Flipping BOTH commutators changes neither product. Simultaneous real
orthogonal rotation of the two adjoint families removes the group integral
and yields exactly the sign and ordinary-trace expression

  v_t(F)=sum_A Tr([Y_(t,A),F]^dagger [T_A/N,F]).

The trace is real: each commutator is anti-Hermitian, so its adjoint product
has real trace. Cauchy–Schwarz and (4.4) give
|v_t(F)|<=sqrt(C_t) E(F)/N^2. With G_N>=g_0 I,

  ||theta||_2 <= sqrt(C_0) E(F)/(q d g_0 N^2).

The normalized Gram also gives
||sum_A x_A Y_(s,A)||_1<=sqrt(d)||sum_A x_A Y_(s,A)||_2
=d r sqrt(G_(N,ss)). Summing over s by Cauchy–Schwarz and using
Tr G_N<=g_1 gives (9.3) with
rho=r sqrt(g_1 C_0)/(q g_0). Thus ||D_F(B_e)||_1 is linear in E(F), not
only O(sqrt(E(F))). This is the load-bearing multiplicity-safe estimate;
the inverse full p-by-p Gram accounts for mixing among all m-1 adjoint
copies. It requires N>=N_0, complete adjoint multiplicity p, and G_N>=g_0I.

4. POSITIVITY AND ORDERED PRODUCT COUNT (SOURCE §10)

For A=U Sigma V^dagger, expanding the Casimir terms gives

 E(A)-[E(|A|)+E(|A^dagger|)]/2
   =sum_(generator a,i,j) sigma_i sigma_j
          |(U^dagger T_a U)_ij-(V^dagger T_a V)_ij|^2 >=0.

Both moduli keep rank and HS norm. Compactness of the rank-constrained
unit sphere therefore permits a positive minimizer F; with the normalization
in Lambda, S=Tr(F^2)=1 and E(F)/S=Lambda_a(Q). This is an existence
reduction, not an optimization algorithm.

For each fixed g, delta=[F_g,.] is a derivation. The exact ordered identity
is

 delta^2(C_B^k)
   = sum_(i=1)^k C_B^(i-1) delta^2(C_B) C_B^(k-i)
     + 2 sum_(1<=i<j<=k) C_B^(i-1) delta(C_B)
          C_B^(j-i-1) delta(C_B) C_B^(k-j).

There are k double-derivative terms and 2 binom(k,2)=k(k-1) ordered
cross terms. No factors are commuted through a derivative. Since
0<=C_B<=I, each first type is bounded in trace norm by
||delta^2(C_B)||_1. Schatten Holder bounds each cross term by
||delta(C_B)||_2^2. Averaging and inserting (10.2) proves the coefficient
k rho/(2B_0)+k(k-1)gamma/(4B_0^2) in (10.3), including its factors.

Writing ell=2hB_0, summation uses
sum k ell^k/k!=ell exp(ell) and
sum k(k-1)ell^k/k!=ell^2 exp(ell), yielding
||D_F(exp(ell C_B))||_1<=exp(ell)[h rho+gamma h^2]E(F)/N^2.
The scalar shift has D_F(I)=0; exp(hB_e) is a scalar multiple of
exp(ell C_B), and Tr exp(ell C_B)>=d. Finally
Phi_F-I=-(d/(2S))D_F and T=||.||_1/2, so (10.4)'s factor
exp(2hB_0)/(4S) is correct. The bound covers all noncommuting higher
terms and is uniform in g by covariance and in h<=h_1. The construction's
orbit Kraus operators sqrt(d/S)F_g have rank<=Q and satisfy TP by Haar
averaging; §12's Caratheodory discretization uses at most d^4+1 classical
nodes. The quantum memory per branch is Q; labels, optimizer acquisition,
bit precision, and circuit cost are not bounded by this argument.

5. GENERAL POLYNOMIAL FRAMES (SOURCE §13)

The statement for another compact connected simple group is conditional on
the finite classical input (13.1): p=rank(g) real homogeneous equivariant
polynomial maps z_s:g*->g, with their coefficient lists and degrees supplied,
and a uniform value-Gram lower bound kappa_K>0 on the compact regular
dominant set K. Constants in the frame and energy estimates depend on this
data, the chosen invariant metric, K, the polynomial coefficient sums and
degrees, and the inherited Gibbs/interface constants. Under these exact
conditions PBW symmetrization gives Hermitian tensor families; the ordered
highest-vector estimate proves G_N=H/q+O_K(N^-1/2), and the commutator
monomial expansion gives the all-F energy inequalities. Deep regularity
ensures the adjoint multiplicity equals the zero-weight multiplicity
rank(g), completing the frame. The abstract operational result then
applies, with a finite-N Casimir patch.

What is not proved in §13 is that such a well-conditioned polynomial
family has been exhibited for every other simple type. Gradients of basic
invariant polynomials are suggested, but the restriction/Jacobian and
metric assumptions must be checked or a determinant supplied. The source
explicitly preserves this as an open input; treating the sufficient
criterion as an unconditional all-types theorem would overstate it. In
SU(m), that gate is actually discharged in §§4–6 by the centered
Vandermonde bound.

6. VALIDATION AND SOURCE NOTE

Primary-source cross-check: Bisio, D'Ariano, Perinotti & Sedlak,
“Memory cost of quantum protocols,” PRA 85, 032333 (2012), §V.B, states
that a channel has memory dimension <=Q iff it has a Kraus representation
with rank(K_i^dagger K_i)<=Q (equivalently rank K_i<=Q); see the published
PDF at https://wordpress.qubit.it/wp-content/uploads/publications-dariano/1112.3853.pdf,
pp. 6–7, lines 898–914 in the extracted text. This supports the resource
characterization used in §8. The source packet's “Section V.2” locator may
refer to the preprint organization; the stable published locator is §V.B.

No counterexample or algebraic failure was found for §§8–10. Caveats are
the explicit N>=N_0 frame threshold for the large-N upper estimate, the
separate elementary patch for N<N_0, the exact regular-carrier assumptions,
and the unresolved classical input for applications beyond the types
actually verified.
