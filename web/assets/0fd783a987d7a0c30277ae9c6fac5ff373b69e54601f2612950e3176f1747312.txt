REGULAR SU(m) NEAR-FULL-RANK GREEN LOWER BOUND
8 October 2026. Independent derivation conditional only on the listed
representation-theoretic inputs. No extremizer or matching upper assumed.

Target and conclusion
--------------------
Let m>=2 be fixed, r=m-1 the rank, and p=m(m-1)/2 the number of positive
roots (do not use p=r). Let V_Lambda be an irreducible SU(m) carrier whose
Dynkin labels a_i=<Lambda,alpha_i^vee> satisfy

  cN <= a_i <= CN,       i=1,...,r,

for fixed 0<c<C and integral N>=1. Set d=dim V_Lambda. Use Hermitian
generators with Tr_fund(T_A T_B)=delta_AB/2 and

  L(A)=sum_A [T_A,[T_A,A]],  E(A)=<A,LA>=sum_A||[T_A,A]||_HS^2.

For 1<=k<d define

  Lambda_Lambda(d-k)=inf{ E(A)/||A||_HS^2 : 0!=A,
                                      rank(A)<=d-k }.

The conclusion is a uniform, all-matrix lower bound. With constants
defined explicitly below from m alone, put

  gamma_m=(m^2-1)/(2m),
  C_D=m! C_0^(p-r) [sqrt(2)(1+||rho||)]^p
                     5^(r/2) 3^((2p-r)/2),
  C_0=1+||rho||+2||rho||^2,
  A_m=C_D(1+gamma_m^(-1))^p,
  s=d/k>1, delta=s-1, T_k=k-k^2/d=k delta/s.

Here the inner product on weights has long roots of squared length 2,
rho is the Weyl vector, and ||rho||^2=m(m^2-1)/12. If p>=2, define

  R_k=(delta/A_m)^(1/p),  S_k=max{gamma_m,R_k}.

Then

  Lambda_Lambda(d-k) >=
    [ gamma_m^(-1) + (p/(p-1)) delta/S_k ]^(-1).             (1)

For p=1 (necessarily SU(2)), define log_+(x)=max{0,log x}; then

  Lambda_Lambda(d-k) >=
    [ gamma_m^(-1) + A_m(1+log_+(delta/(A_m gamma_m))) ]^(-1).
                                                                  (2)

These are finite-N inequalities for every 1<=k<d. The max/log_+ clauses
are important: the natural cutoff can fall below the spectral gap when
k is close to d. For k/d bounded away from 0 the lower bounds are
positive constants independent of N. At k=1 they give

  Lambda_Lambda(d-1) >= c_m d^(-(p-1)/p) ~=_{m,c,C} N^(-(p-1))  (p>=2),
  Lambda_Lambda(d-1) >= c_m/(1+log d) ~=_{m,c,C} 1/log(N+1)    (p=1).

All bounds apply to arbitrary complex, nonnormal matrices. The proof does
not infer an optimizer, equality, or an upper bound.

Representation-theoretic inputs and the all-T counting bound
-------------------------------------------------------------
Write the conjugation decomposition, grouping equal Casimir eigenvalues,
as

  End(V_Lambda)=C I direct-sum sum_(mu!=0) M_mu tensor V_mu,
  L|_(M_mu tensor V_mu)=C_2(mu) I,
  D_Lambda(T)=1+sum_(mu!=0) m_Lambda(mu) dim(V_mu)
                                      1{C_2(mu)<=T}.

The carrier is irreducible, so the scalar kernel is exactly C I. The
Casimir is C_2(mu)=1/2(mu,mu+2rho). Every nonzero dominant SU(m) weight
has Casimir at least gamma_m: write mu=sum_i b_i omega_i with b_i>=0;
all (omega_i,omega_j)>0, so a nonzero b_i contributes at least
1/2||omega_i||^2+(omega_i,rho)=C_2(omega_i). The fundamental values are
i(m-i)(m+1)/(2m), whose minimum is gamma_m at i=1 or m-1. Thus the stated
generator normalization fixes a valid universal gap floor. We use the
tensor-multiplicity bound

  m_Lambda(mu) <= z_mu:=dim V_mu[0].                         (3)

For clarity, (3) is the only multiplicity input needed here. One form of
the PRV tensor-multiplicity formula states, for Dynkin labels a_i,

  m_Lambda(mu)=dim{v in V_mu[0] :
                      E_i^(a_i+1)v=0 for every simple root i},

so (3) is immediate. If an independent proof of this formula/cap is not
available in the intended source chain, the all-T bound and conclusions
below are conditional on (3); a stable-range-only version of (3) would
not suffice for the Green tail.

Here is a direct polynomial bound on z_mu and hence on D_Lambda(T). The
Kostant zero-weight formula is

  z_mu=sum_(w in W)(-1)^ell(w) P(w(mu+rho)-rho),

where P(beta) counts decompositions beta=sum_(alpha>0)n_alpha alpha,
n_alpha nonnegative integers. In type A_(m-1), (rho,alpha)=ht(alpha)>=1
for each positive root. Thus any such decomposition obeys

  sum_(alpha>0)n_alpha <= (rho,beta) <= ||rho|| ||beta||.

Choose the p-r nonsimple positive-root coefficients freely. Each has at
most 1+||rho||||beta|| choices; the r simple-root coefficients are then
uniquely determined because the simple roots form a basis. For
beta=w(mu+rho)-rho, ||beta||<=||mu||+2||rho||, whence

  z_mu <= m! C_0^(p-r)(1+||mu||)^(p-r).                     (4)

This count is valid even when beta is outside the positive-root cone,
where P(beta)=0.

The Weyl dimension formula gives

  dim(V_mu)=prod_(alpha>0) (mu+rho,alpha)/(rho,alpha)
           <= [sqrt(2)(1+||rho||)]^p(1+||mu||)^p.            (5)

Indeed (rho,alpha)=ht(alpha)>=1 and ||alpha||=sqrt(2). Also
C_2(mu)>=||mu||^2/2 for dominant mu, so C_2(mu)<=T implies
||mu||<=sqrt(2T). In Dynkin coordinates b_i=<mu,alpha_i^vee>,
0<=b_i<=sqrt(2)||mu||, so the number of dominant labels in this ball is
at most (1+2sqrt(T))^r. Combining (3)--(5),

  D_Lambda(T)
   <= m! C_0^(p-r) [sqrt(2)(1+||rho||)]^p
      (1+2sqrt(T))^r (1+sqrt(2T))^(2p-r)
   <= C_D(1+T)^p,                                            (6)

using (1+2sqrt(T))<=sqrt(5)sqrt(1+T) and
(1+sqrt(2T))<=sqrt(3)sqrt(1+T). The bound is independent of Lambda,
therefore holds for all T including the top of the finite spectrum. Its
exponent is p=#positive roots: the weight-label count contributes r/2
powers of T, the zero-weight multiplicity contributes (p-r)/2, and the
Weyl dimension contributes p/2, for a total p. Replacing p by rank r,
or dropping m_Lambda(mu), changes this count and is not justified.

The regular-cone hypothesis is used for the dimension scale, not (6).
The Weyl dimension product is

  d=prod_(1<=i<j<=m)
       [a_i+...+a_(j-1)+(j-i)]/(j-i),

so (cN+1)^p <= d <= (CN+1)^p and d ~=_{m,c,C} N^p. In particular, the
codimension-one conversions in the conclusion use this regularity
assumption; they do not follow from d alone for arbitrary carrier families.

All-matrix right-kernel bridge
------------------------------
Fix any complex A with rank(A)<=d-k. Choose a k-dimensional subspace of
ker(A), and let P be its orthogonal projection. Then AP=0 and, by
cyclicity, Tr(PA)=Tr(AP)=0. Decompose

  A=aI+X, Tr(X)=0, P0=P-(k/d)I.

With the complex Hilbert-Schmidt convention <B,C>=Tr(B*C),

  ka=-Tr(PX)=-<P0,X>.

On the traceless subspace L is positive and invertible. Complex
Cauchy--Schwarz in its L^(-1/2),L^(1/2) inner product gives

  k^2|a|^2 <= G(P) E(A),
  G(P)=<P0,L^(-1)P0>
      =sum_(mu!=0) ||Pi_mu P0||_HS^2/C_2(mu).

Since all nontrivial SU(m) irreps have Casimir at least gamma_m,
||X||_HS^2<=E(A)/gamma_m. Orthogonality of aI and X, and E(A)=E(X),
therefore imply

  ||A||_HS^2 <= E(A)[d G(P)/k^2+gamma_m^(-1)].             (7)

This is orientation-free once G(P) is bounded and invokes no positivity
of A. A nonzero admissible A has E(A)>0: the kernel of L is C I, whose
nonzero elements have rank d.

For T>=0 let Pi_(0,T] be the positive-spectrum projector and set

  F_P(T)=||Pi_(0,T]P0||_HS^2.

Its dimension is D_Lambda(T)-1. Its partial trace is scalar by Schur's
lemma: Tr_2 Pi_(0,T]=(D_Lambda(T)-1)I/d. The normalized vector
vec(P)/sqrt(k) has Schmidt rank k, so its overlap with this projector is
at most k(D_Lambda(T)-1)/d. Restoring ||vec(P)||^2=k gives

  F_P(T)<= k^2(D_Lambda(T)-1)/d,
  F_P(T)<=||P0||_HS^2=T_k=k-k^2/d.                             (8)

The k^2 is essential: one k comes from the Schmidt-rank overlap of the
unit vector and the second from unnormalizing vec(P). Since positive
Casimirs are >=gamma_m, F_P(T)=0 for T<gamma_m. Finally the Green
identity is exact, term by term in the finite spectrum:

  G(P)=int_0^infinity F_P(T) T^(-2)dT.                       (9)

Cutoff calculation and finite endpoint cases
----------------------------------------------
From (6) and (8), for T>=gamma_m,

  F_P(T)<=min{T_k, A_m(k^2/d)T^p},
  A_m=C_D(1+gamma_m^(-1))^p.                                (10)

The exact high-tail cap is T_k, not merely k. Put s=d/k and
delta=s-1. The balancing cutoff R_k=(delta/A_m)^(1/p) satisfies

  A_m(k^2/d)R_k^p=T_k.

For p>=2, split (9) at S_k=max{gamma_m,R_k}. If R_k>=gamma_m,

  G(P)<= A_m(k^2/d) int_(gamma_m)^(R_k) T^(p-2)dT + T_k/R_k
       <= [1/(p-1)+1] T_k/R_k
        = p/(p-1) T_k/R_k.

If R_k<gamma_m the low integral is empty and G(P)<=T_k/gamma_m,
which is also <=p T_k/((p-1)S_k). Thus in both cases

  dG(P)/k^2 <= [p/(p-1)] delta/S_k.                          (11)

Substitution in (7) proves (1). This exact finite cutoff is preferable
to writing R_k without a max: if R_k<gamma_m, an integral from gamma_m
to R_k is empty, not a negative integral. The formula also remains valid
when R_k exceeds the top Casimir, because (6) is an all-T bound and the
high tail in (9) is still bounded by T_k/R_k.

For p=1, use S=max{gamma_m,R_k}, R_k=delta/A_m. If R_k>=gamma_m,

  G(P)<= A_m(k^2/d)log(R_k/gamma_m)+T_k/R_k
       = A_m(k^2/d)[1+log(R_k/gamma_m)].

If R_k<gamma_m, G(P)<=T_k/gamma_m. Since delta<A_m gamma_m,
this is <=A_m k^2/d. Hence uniformly

  dG(P)/k^2<=A_m[1+log_+(delta/(A_m gamma_m))],             (12)

which with (7) proves (2). The logarithm is forced by the exact
borderline integral T^(p-2)=T^(-1); a power estimate at p=1 is false.

Endpoint and interpretation checks
----------------------------------
1. Codimension k=1. Here delta=d-1. For fixed m and sufficiently large
   d, the cutoff is above the gap when p>=2, so (1) is bounded below by
   a constant times d^(-(p-1)/p). Since d ~= N^p, this is
   N^(-(p-1)). For p=1, (2) is a constant times 1/(1+log d), hence
   1/log(N+1). For small d the same finite expressions (1)--(2) apply;
   no asymptotic cutoff claim is needed.
2. Fixed nullity density k/d->theta in (0,1). Then delta->(1-theta)/theta
   is fixed. Equations (1)--(2) give a positive N-independent constant;
   if R_k<gamma_m, the max/log_+ clauses give the correct bounded-scale
   branch directly.
3. Opposite endpoint k=d-1 (rank cap one). Then delta=1/(d-1), and the
   Green lower remains constant order. The independent Schmidt-rank
   profile below gives the stronger Omega_m(N) lower at q=1. Do not read
   (1) as an all-rank sharp profile.
4. k=d is excluded: rank(A)<=0 contains no nonzero A. At q=d the identity
   matrix has zero energy.

For completeness, the same partial-trace argument for every normalized
rank<=q matrix X gives the finite profile

  Lambda_Lambda(q)>=Phi_Lambda(q)
       :=int_0^infinity [1-qD_Lambda(T)/d]_+dT.              (13)

For q<=d/2, (6) implies Phi_Lambda(q)>=c_m(d/q)^(1/p). Here is a
finite two-case choice. Put u=d/q>=2 and U_m=2^(p+1)C_D. If u>=U_m,
take T=U_m^(-1/p)u^(1/p)>=1. Then, for 0<=t<=T,
C_D(1+t)^p/u<=1/2, so Phi>=T/2. If 2<=u<U_m, the zero-mode interval
[0,gamma_m) contributes gamma_m(1-q/d)>=gamma_m/2, which is at least
gamma_m u^(1/p)/(2U_m^(1/p)). This gives a fixed positive c_m. Since
d ~= N^p, the lower is Omega_{m,c,C}(N q^(-1/p)). At q=d-1, q>=d/2
and the integrated profile is exactly

  Phi_Lambda(d-1)=gamma_Lambda/d,

where gamma_Lambda is the first positive Casimir actually present in
End(V_Lambda): D(T)=1 below gamma_Lambda, and D(T)>=2 from that point
on, making the integrand vanish for q>=d/2. The adjoint subrepresentation
is present (the differential representation embeds su(m) into End(V_Lambda)),
so gamma_m<=gamma_Lambda<=C_2(ad)=m; in particular this profile is
Theta_m(1/d). Thus (1)--(2), not a spectral-projector-only bound,
supply the near-full gain.

Audit boundary
--------------
The nullity bridge (7)--(9) is fully independent of optimizer claims and
handles every right-kernel orientation. The all-T count (6) is valid
provided (3) is available globally. If (3) has only been proved for
stable/low-Casimir constituents, the global Green tail is not established
by that proof; one must either prove (3) for all constituents or supply a
separate high-Casimir tail bound. Under global (3), the polynomial
exponent is p=#positive roots, the near-full power is (p-1)/p for p>1,
and p=1 is logarithmic. No equality or matching construction is claimed.
