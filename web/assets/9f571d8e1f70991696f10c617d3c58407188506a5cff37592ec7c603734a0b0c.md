# Exact-mean dyadic compiler for bipartite perfect matchings

## Contract

Let `G=(L,R,E)` be a finite bipartite graph with `|L|=|R|=n>=1`. Edges are labelled, so parallel edges may be treated separately. The input is a rational vector `x in Q^E` satisfying `x_e>=0` and degree sum one at every vertex, plus a rational feature matrix `A in Q^(d x E)`. These conditions are an exact feasibility certificate for the bipartite perfect-matching polytope. The algorithm outputs a finite rational law on perfect matchings `M` such that

* `E[1_M]=x` coordinate by coordinate;
* every positive-probability atom obeys `||A(1_M-x)||_infinity < 100 B`, where `B` is any supplied positive rational upper bound on `||A u_C||_2` for every simple even cycle `C` of `G`, and `u_C` is either alternating `+1,-1` incidence vector on that cycle;
* the output support has at most `|E|+1` atoms.

No claim is made that these cycle directions are edges of the perfect-matching polytope. They are feasible circulation directions for the current fractional point, used only in the numerator-halving lemma below.

The code can use the polynomial-time rational signing algorithm of Li as its `signer` callback. A fully explicit, computable choice that requires no cycle-norm optimization is

`B0 = sum_{e in E} sum_{j=1}^d |A[j,e]|`.

Indeed, `||A u_C||_2 <= ||A u_C||_1 <= B0` for every cycle. The implementation tightens this without a cycle oracle: let `c_e=ceil(||A_e||_2)`, computed by exact rational square-root comparisons, and set `B=min(B0,2n max_e c_e)`. Every simple bipartite cycle has at most `2n` edges, so the triangle inequality makes the second term a valid all-cycle upper bound as well. The code returns the certified `<100B` bound; in particular, if all actual-edge columns have Euclidean norm at most one, then `B<=2n` and its output is `<200n`. If `B=0`, every cycle feature vector vanishes, so all perfect matchings have the same feature value and any exact matching decomposition already solves the task.

## Lemma 1: exact network-flow seed

Repeatedly find a perfect matching in the positive support of the current residual matrix `r`. Let `theta=min_{e in M} r_e`, record `(theta,M)`, and subtract `theta 1_M`. The residual has equal row and column sums `rho=1-sum theta`; if `rho>0`, its support contains a perfect matching because `r/rho` is doubly stochastic. At least one positive edge becomes zero at every subtraction, so at most `|E|` matchings are recorded. The resulting rational decomposition is

`x = sum_{i=1}^s lambda_i 1_{M_i}`, `sum_i lambda_i=1`, `s<=|E|`.

All arithmetic is exact. Taking the product of input denominators as a common denominator gives bit length at most the input bit length; every subtraction coefficient is an integer multiple of its reciprocal.

## Lemma 2: unbiased dyadic initialization

Set `D=2^K`, where `K` is the least integer with `D>=2 s n`. For each decomposition weight define

`q_i=floor(D lambda_i)`, `f_i=D lambda_i-q_i`.

Then `R=sum_i f_i` is an integer. Let `F_0=0` and `F_i=sum_{h<=i} f_h`. For a uniform `U in [0,1)`, set

`b_i(U)=floor(F_i-U)-floor(F_{i-1}-U)`.

Since `0<=f_i<1`, each `b_i` is zero or one; telescoping gives `sum_i b_i=R`; and `E b_i=f_i`. Therefore

`alpha_i(U)=(q_i+b_i(U))/D`

is a probability vector, `E alpha_i=lambda_i`, and each coordinate obeys `|alpha_i-lambda_i|<=1/D`. The law is explicit with at most `s+1` atoms: its only breakpoints are `0`, `1`, and the fractional parts of `F_i`; use one rational midpoint per interval and give it the interval's exact rational length.

Each initial point `y(U)=sum_i alpha_i(U) 1_{M_i}` is a feasible matching marginal with denominator `D` and `E y=x`. For any two perfect matchings their symmetric difference is a disjoint union of alternating cycles. There are at most `n` cycles even in a labelled bipartite multigraph, because each cycle uses at least one of the `n` matching edges. Thus `||A(1_{M_i}-1_{M_j})||_infinity <= n B`. With `v_i=A(1_{M_i}-x)`, the same bound holds for `||v_i||_infinity`. Since `sum_i(alpha_i-lambda_i)=0`,

`||A(y-x)||_infinity = ||sum_i (alpha_i-lambda_i)v_i||_infinity <= s n B/D <= B/2`.

This is the only use of the initial Birkhoff decomposition's atom geometry.

## Lemma 3: odd-numerator cycle halving

Suppose a current support atom is `y=b/2^k`, with integer `0<=b_e<=2^k` and degree sum `2^k` at every vertex, where `k>=1`. The graph `H` of edges with odd numerator `b_e` has even degree at every vertex because

`deg_H(v) = sum_{e incident v} b_e (mod 2) = 2^k (mod 2) = 0`.

Decompose `H` into edge-disjoint simple cycles. They are even because `G` is bipartite. Give each cycle `C` an alternating signed incidence vector `u_C`; at every vertex, each cycle contributes one `+1` and one `-1`, so every signed sum `s=sum_C eps_C u_C` has zero degree at every vertex and equals `+/-1` on every edge of `H`.

Run the supplied Komlos signing procedure on columns `A u_C/B`. They are rational and have Euclidean norm at most one by the definition of `B`. Li's Theorem 1.1 returns signs with `||sum_C eps_C A u_C||_infinity < 99 B`. Define

`y^+ = y+s/2^k`, `y^- = y-s/2^k`.

For even `b_e`, `s_e=0`; for odd `b_e`, `b_e +/- s_e` is even and lies in `[0,2^k]`. Hence both child numerators `(b_e +/- s_e)/2` are integers in `[0,2^(k-1)]`, so the children have entries in `[0,1]`, denominator `2^(k-1)`, and vertex degree one. Their midpoint is `y`, and each feature increment is less than `99B/2^k`. If `H` is empty, the two children coincide with `y` and it is simply rewritten on the coarser grid.

This argument requires only the bipartite degree equations and box constraints. The cycles need not be actual polytope edges.

## Lemma 4: support compression

At each level, split every law atom into its two antipodal children with half its probability. The aggregate mean remains exactly `x`; every child is a feasible point on the next dyadic grid. Starting from at most `|E|+1` atoms, the temporary support has at most `2(|E|+1)` points.

Whenever more than `|E|+1` positive atoms remain, the vectors `(1,y_i)` in `Q^(|E|+1)` have a nonzero rational dependence `c` with `sum_i c_i=0` and `sum_i c_i y_i=0`. Since `c` has both signs, subtract `t c` from the current weights, with `t=min_{c_i>0} w_i/c_i`. This preserves total mass and mean, keeps all weights nonnegative, and removes at least one atom. Repeating leaves at most `|E|+1` existing points. Consequently compression never enlarges the per-atom discrepancy bound.

The repeated rational updates have a polynomial bit bound; a fresh LP at every level is unnecessary. First, the exact flow decomposition uses a common input denominator `Q_x`: every residual entry and every extracted matching weight stays in `(1/Q_x) Z`. The cumulative fractional remainders in systematic rounding, their fractional-part breakpoints, and all interval lengths therefore also have denominator dividing `Q_x`; the seed probabilities share `Q_x`. Now let `m=|E|`, let `D=2^k` be the common denominator of the current dyadic points, and let `r<=m+1` be the rank of their affine columns `(1,y_i)`. In the RREF dependence used by the code, pivot columns form a basis and one free column is expressed in that basis. A nonsingular `r x r` minor and Cramer's rule show that, after scaling to an integer dependence vector `a`, every coefficient is a determinant of an `r x r` integer matrix whose entries have magnitude at most `D`. Thus `|a_i|<=r!D^r` and each such coefficient has `O(r(k+log r))` bits. If the current probabilities share denominator `Q`, write `w_j=p_j/Q`. Eliminating at a positive coefficient `a_i` changes them to

`w'_j=(p_j a_i-p_i a_j)/(Q a_i)`.

So all new probabilities share denominator `Q|a_i|`; one elimination adds at most `O(r(k+log r))` bits to the common denominator. There are at most `m+1` eliminations in a compression. Splitting the law divides weights by two, and merging duplicate points sums weights with the same common denominator, so it adds only one denominator bit between compression phases. The seed weights share the common denominator of the input decomposition (which has `O(L_x)` bits). With `K=O(log(mn))` levels, the final common denominator therefore has

`O(L_x + K m^2 (K+log(m+1)))`

bits, up to absolute constants; all numerators are bounded by that denominator because weights stay in `[0,1]`. This is polynomial in the explicit input size. The bound follows the implemented null-relation elimination, rather than multiplying an unrestricted fresh determinant bound into the previous bit length at every round.

After `K` levels every point is integral and has degree one at each vertex, so it is an actual perfect matching. The final atomwise error is bounded by

`B/2 + 99 B sum_{k=1}^K 2^-k < 99.5 B < 100 B`.

The mean remains `x` at every level and under every compression.

## Exact implementation and costs

`matching_dyadic_compiler.py` implements the network-flow decomposition, systematic dyadic law, odd-support cycle decomposition, exact affine-dependence compression, and final matching extraction. It takes a deterministic signing callback and checks its returned signs and discrepancy exactly. `exhaustive_signer` is included only as a finite-instance fallback; it is exponential in the number of cycles. The polynomial-time theorem uses Li's rational algorithm as the callback; that source algorithm is not reimplemented in this artifact.

For explicit `n`, `E`, feature dimension `d`, and total rational input length `L`:

* the seed decomposition uses at most `E` exact maximum-matching calls; the included augmenting-path implementation scans `O(nE)` edges per call, for `O(nE^2)` combinatorial work, plus polynomial-bit rational arithmetic;
* `K=ceil(log2(2 s n))=O(log(En))`;
* at every level there are at most `E+1` parent atoms and at most `E` cycles per parent;
* each Li call is on a `d x t` rational matrix, `t<=E`, with columns of norm at most one. Li reports `O(t^2 log t)` updates, `O(dt+t^(omega+2) log^3 t)` arithmetic/comparisons, and polynomial bit complexity for rational input;
* exact dependence compression uses matrices of at most `(E+1) x 2(E+1)`, and at most `E+1` eliminations per level, hence `O(E^4)` rational field operations per level by direct Gaussian elimination. The common-denominator argument in Lemma 4 bounds the actual repeated weight updates by `O(L_x+K E^2(K+log(E+1)))` bits, rather than assuming that each round starts from fresh LP weights;
* the output has at most `E+1` matchings, each encoded by its `n` labelled edges, and rational weights of polynomial bit length.

Across all levels, there are at most `K(E+1)` Li-signing calls, each with at most `E` columns; a conservative arithmetic count is `O(K(dE^2+E^(omega+3)log^3 E))`, in addition to network flow, cycle decomposition, and `O(KE^4)` rational compression. Thus the composition with Li's stated rational algorithm has polynomial bit runtime. This report does not claim an independent implementation audit of that external signing algorithm.

## Boundaries and prior work

* The cited source for the signing subroutine is Xiaoyu Li, *Fast Spectral Signing for Vector Balancing*, arXiv:2609.30044v1, Theorem 1.1. Its premise is exactly a rational `d x t` matrix whose columns have Euclidean norm at most one; its conclusion is a deterministic signing with infinity discrepancy `<99`, with polynomial rational-bit complexity. It is a signing theorem, not an exact-mean law; the exact-mean law comes from the antipodal cycle children and subsequent support compression here. The arXiv paper is source-reported evidence, not independent proof validation.
* Astra N24 already gives an exact-mean rational compiler for the cube, with support at most `n+1` and a vector-feature bound depending on row count and marginal mass. Astra ROUND/P-round separately records a general Boolean-polytope exact-mean theorem under exact strong optimization, with bound `1161 k(P)` for unit-norm columns and support at most `n+1`; bipartite perfect matching optimization is available through exact maximum-weight matching. That generic construction is broader and has a stronger stated geometry parameter. The present cycle compiler is a direct matching-specific construction with explicit flow/cycle operations and radius `100B`, where `B` bounds graph-cycle feature norms. No theorem identifying `B` with the actual polytope-edge parameter `k(P)` is asserted.
* Astra's partition-base simultaneous-exchange route does not automatically extend to arbitrary matroids; its recorded `K4` Hall obstruction is relevant only to a broader matroid extension. The present proof uses the bipartite network-flow / alternating-cycle structure and makes no such extension.
* This is an internally derived composition candidate, not a novelty or priority claim. The K22 independent-block sampler is a special case of matching geometry but does not supply this arbitrary-graph construction. The exact mean plus finite support was already available through the more general P-round record; the distinct contribution here is the cycle-norm parameter and explicit dyadic compiler architecture, conditional on the reported Li signer.
