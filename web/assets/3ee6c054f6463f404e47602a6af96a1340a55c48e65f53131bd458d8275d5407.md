# Cycle 2: finite-data lineage hazards and establishment risk

**Status.** This is a finite-sample statistical layer for the branching-risk model in `mechanism_attack.md`, not a biological fit or a new lineage-tracking method. Coverage claims below are conditional on the stated event-observation, censoring, lineage-independence, and sampling assumptions. A failed assumption changes the output to `UNKNOWN`; it does not become a zero-rate estimate.

## Result in one paragraph

For a fully observed, typed lineage history, each per-cell birth, death, or state-switch hazard has a counting-process likelihood with event count `K` and cell-time exposure `A`. The MLE is `K/A`. If exposure is genuinely fixed/exogenous and `K ~ Poisson(θA)`, Garwood intervals give exact finite-sample limits. If the risk set is endogenous or the experiment is inspected/stopped adaptively, an exponential likelihood-ratio confidence sequence remains valid under a predictable-intensity model. In either case, **zero events imply a positive upper limit**, `−log(α)/(ρA)` when event-detection sensitivity is `ρ`, not a zero rate. A confidence set for birth/death hazards maps to an establishment-probability interval; separately, a prevalence sample maps to a hidden-type interval. Neither layer alone certifies whole-tumor rebound: their upper bounds must be combined with absolute viable founder count, future escape supply, and spatial-coverage assumptions.

## 1. Observed-history model and rate confidence sets

Let `Y_i(t)` be the number of observed, viable, at-risk cells currently assigned to phenotype/genotype `i`. For event channel `e` (birth, death, or `i → j` switch), let `N_{i,e}(t)` count its observed events and let

`A_i(t) = ∫₀ᵗ Y_i(s) ds`

be cell-time exposure. Under constant per-cell hazards `θ_{i,e}` and independent cell-level event clocks, the predictable intensity is

`λ_{i,e}(t) = θ_{i,e} Y_i(t)`.

The observed-path likelihood, omitting factors not depending on the rates, is

`L(θ) ∝ ∏_{i,e} θ_{i,e}^{K_{i,e}} exp(−θ_{i,e} A_i)`,

where `K_{i,e}=N_{i,e}(T)`. Thus `θ̂_{i,e}=K_{i,e}/A_i` when `A_i>0`. A birth leaves the mother at risk and adds a daughter to the risk set; death removes a cell; a switch transfers it to the destination state. The exposure is computed from the observed risk-set path, not from final cell count. With treatment phases or time-varying covariates, split exposures/counts by the predeclared phase or fit the corresponding intensity model.

For a truly fixed/exogenous exposure `A` with `K ~ Poisson(θA)`, the Garwood two-sided `(1−α)` interval is

`θ_L = χ²_{2K, α/2}/(2A)` (set `θ_L=0` for `K=0`),

`θ_U = χ²_{2(K+1), 1−α/2}/(2A)`.

The one-sided `(1−α)` upper limit is `χ²_{2(K+1),1−α}/(2A)`. In particular, if `K=0`,

`θ_U = −log(α)/A`,

which is `2.996/A` at 95% confidence. This is the classical exact Poisson interval of [Garwood (1936)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/28.3-4.437).

If `A=0`, there is no rate information and the upper limit is infinite. If `A>0` but `K=0`, the positive limit above is the correct finite-data statement.

**Important exposure qualification.** In a branching lineage, `A_i` is usually random and affected by the same births, deaths, and switches being counted. The full path likelihood above is still valid, but conditional on the realized random exposure, `K` need not be Poisson with mean `θA`; blindly applying the Garwood interval can therefore misstate coverage. A convenient finite-sample alternative is an anytime-valid likelihood-ratio confidence sequence. For a candidate rate `θ>0` and a fixed, predeclared `η`, define

`M_t(θ,η) = exp{ηK_t − (exp(η)−1) θ A_t}`.

Under the candidate model, this is the likelihood-ratio martingale for alternative rate `θ exp(η)` against `θ`. Ville's inequality implies that a one-sided upper confidence sequence, for fixed `η<0`, is

`U_t(α;η) = [log(1/α) − ηK_t] / [(1−exp(η))A_t]`.

For `K_t=0`, taking the alternative rate to zero gives `U_t=−log(α)/A_t`. A lower sequence uses a fixed `η>0`:

`L_t(α;η) = max{0, [ηK_t−log(1/α)] / [(exp(η)−1)A_t]}`.

The two-sided interval uses error allocations `α/2` for each side; for `m` channel/phase rates, allocate `α/(2m)` per side (or use a joint construction with proved coverage). The fixed-`η` intervals are simple and valid but may be wide; a predeclared mixture over `η` can adapt the boundary without invalid data-dependent tuning. This follows the likelihood-ratio / confidence-sequence principle of [Howard et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991). Coverage holds at all inspection times, including a stopping time, when the observed process really has intensity `θY(t)` and the integrated exposure is predictable.

If event calls are independently detected with known probability `ρ_{i,e}`, the observed intensity is `ρ_{i,e}θ_{i,e}Y_i(t)`, so rate limits for the detected hazard divide by `ρ_{i,e}`. If `ρ` is unknown and can approach zero, the true hazard has no finite upper bound from a non-detection. Spike-in events, manually curated movies, and duplicate annotation are needed to calibrate event-specific sensitivity. A known type-call confusion matrix can be included in a hidden-state likelihood; an unknown confusion matrix is confounded with type-specific hazards.

### Censored and incomplete lineage trees

For each observed cell, include its time at risk up to a recorded birth/death/switch or administrative right-censoring time. A censored cell contributes its integrated-hazard survival factor, not an event count. The simple likelihood is valid when censoring is independent of the latent event process conditional on observed type and covariates. If cells disappear because they die, die before a frame is imaged, or are less trackable in one state, that is informative truncation unless those branches and the observation process are modeled. Counting only lineages that survive to the terminal frame creates survivor bias.

This is not a hypothetical correction: [Genthon et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXLife.1.013014) develop a framework for incomplete population trees and show that ignoring dead lineages can mislead fitness estimates. If phenotype is latent, use a lineage-tree state-space likelihood or validated barcode-based inference and project its full uncertainty; do not relabel unlabeled events as known-type Poisson counts. Sister cells share ancestry, so the event-intensity likelihood can remain valid under the branching Markov model, but random culture-level drug response or shared frailty requires independent culture replicates or an explicit random-effects model.

## 2. Establishment probability under maintenance

For a stable one-type linear birth–death lineage under maintenance, with birth rate `b` and death rate `d`, eventual establishment/survival from one founder is

`q(b,d) = 0` if `b ≤ d`, and `q(b,d)=1−d/b` if `b>d`.

Given a **simultaneous** confidence rectangle `b∈[b_L,b_U]`, `d∈[d_L,d_U]`, a valid projected interval is

`q_L = 0` if `b_L≤d_U`, otherwise `1−d_U/b_L`,

`q_U = 0` if `b_U≤d_L`, otherwise `1−d_L/b_U`.

For a nonrectangular joint set `C`, use `q_L=inf_{(b,d)∈C}q(b,d)` and `q_U=sup_C q(b,d)`; preserving the joint set can materially tighten the result. If the birth/death intervals overlap the critical line `b=d`, the lower establishment bound is zero. If the data do not put `b_U≤d_L`, the upper bound may remain positive. This is the correct behavior near criticality, not a numerical failure.

With switching between types, let `q_i` be the smallest solution in `[0,1]^k` of

`0 = b_i(q_i²−q_i) + d_i(1−q_i) + Σ_{j≠i} σ_{ij}(q_j−q_i)`.

Then `1−q_i` is the establishment probability from type `i`. For a finite-dimensional joint hazard confidence set `C`, solve this system for each feasible rate vector and numerically bound `1−q_i` over `C`; report solver tolerances and verify the least-root branch. If `C` crosses a critical surface or includes unconstrained hidden switching, return a wide interval or `UNKNOWN`.

An independent limiting-dilution assay can estimate the operational colony-establishment probability directly. If `k` of `n` independent single-cell wells produce a verified, durable colony, use an exact binomial interval; with zero positive wells and perfect detection, the one-sided upper bound is `1−α^(1/n)`. With known colony-detection sensitivity `s` and no false positives it becomes `min{1,[1−α^(1/n)]/s}`. This estimates eventual branching survival only if the assay horizon and medium make non-detection equivalent to extinction; delayed colonies, finite follow-up, and assay-induced state changes need a calibrated sensitivity model. Wells, not cells from one well, are the independent units.

For `N` known independent founders of the resistant type, residual-only establishment risk is exactly `1−(1−q)^N` (upper bound `1−(1−q_U)^N`). The union bound `Nq_U` is looser but does not require independence between success events if each conditional success probability is bounded appropriately.

## 3. Hidden-type prevalence and zero detections

If `n` cells are independently sampled at random from the target compartment, true hidden-type prevalence is `π`, each sampled hidden cell is detected with known sensitivity `s`, and specificity is perfect, then the number detected is `K~Binomial(n,sπ)`. After zero detections,

`π_U = min{1, [1−α^(1/n)]/s}`.

For `k>0`, first form the one-sided exact Clopper–Pearson upper limit `p_U=Beta^{-1}(1−α;k+1,n−k)` for the *observed-positive probability* `p=sπ`, then set `π_U=min{1,p_U/s}`. A two-sided interval uses `Beta^{-1}(α/2;k,n−k+1)` and `Beta^{-1}(1−α/2;k+1,n−k)`, with the usual 0/1 endpoint conventions, followed by division by `s`. If known false-positive rate `c>0`, use `p=c+(s−c)π` and invert the same exact interval through that map; if `s` and `c` are unknown, the latent prevalence is not identified by the calls alone.

At 95%, this is approximately `2.996/(ns)`. For finite-population sampling without replacement from a known census `M`, with `R` true resistant cells and perfect detection,

`P(K=0 | R) = C(M−R,n)/C(M,n)`;

invert this hypergeometric probability to obtain the exact upper count `R_U`. For imperfect sensitivity, sum over the hypergeometric sample count `x` with zero-detection probability `(1−s)^x` before inversion. These formulas require a known sampling frame, independent/random cell selection, and calibrated sensitivity. A biopsy from one accessible region does not bound an unsampled lesion or spatial sanctuary; without a lower bound on region inclusion and on detection/shedding, the whole-tumor prevalence upper bound is the trivial 1. The companion `cycle2_observation_gate.md` gives the spatial-coverage counterexample and finite-population details.

If every one of `N` cells independently has the hidden type with probability `π` and each such cell establishes with probability `q`, the tumor-level chance of at least one establishment is

`R(π,q,N) = 1−(1−πq)^N`.

Thus a simultaneous set `π≤π_U`, `q≤q_U` gives `R≤1−(1−π_U q_U)^N`; for exactly `R` hidden cells use `1−(1−q_U)^R`. These are model-conditional projections, not claims that a tumor is spatially iid.

**Scale check.** With zero calls in `n=10,000` independently sampled cells, perfect specificity and sensitivity give `π_U=2.9953×10⁻⁴`. If the target compartment contains `N=100,000` cells and `q_U=0.01`, the upper bound on at least one establishment is still `1−(1−π_Uq_U)^N=0.259`. At `N=10⁸`, it is effectively 1. So “no resistant cell observed” can be a tight prevalence statement and still leave poor whole-tumor risk control.

## 4. Making the existing risk certificate finite-data valid

The risk-vs-pulse-duration expression in `mechanism_attack.md` is

`B(T) = q̄_R m_R(T) + ∫₀ᵀ q̄_E(t) ν(t) m_R(t) dt`,

with external founder immigration added as a separate source term. It is an algebraic union-bound certificate when the inputs are valid upper bounds. A finite-data version is obtained by building one joint `(1−α)` confidence set `C_α` for all required hazards, establishment probabilities, absolute founder counts, and source-process inputs, then reporting

`B_U(T)=sup_{θ∈C_α} B(T;θ)`.

If the branching/source assumptions hold and `θ*∈C_α`, then actual relapse risk is at most `B_U(T)`. Thus a gate `B_U(T)≤ε` has at least `1−α` statistical coverage, conditional on the biological model and sampling design. Construct `C_α` with Bonferroni allocations or a proved joint likelihood/martingale region. Do not plug in MLEs, point estimates, or separate 95% intervals and then call the result a 95% certificate. Continuous-time integrals need simultaneous rate/envelope bounds over the pulse, or a prespecified piecewise-constant model; isolated timepoint intervals do not bound interpolation or future drift.

For a source-switch hazard `ν` per resistant-cell-day and zero observed switches over exposure `A_R`, the one-sided upper bound is `ν_U=−log(α)/(ρA_R)` (or the simultaneous-adjusted version). If future source exposure is bounded by `A_future`, the union-bound contribution from those escape founders is at most `q̄_E,U ν_U A_future`. For mutation per sensitive-cell division replace cell-days by measured division opportunities `D`, and require an observed-event sensitivity floor `ρ>0`. This is the zero-event upper bound from Section 1; it never sets `ν=0`.

If “common-weight certificate” means a common positive Lyapunov weight for multiple treatment phases, it too can be made finite-data valid **as a sufficient certificate**. Let `A_r(θ)` be the mean-generator matrix in phase/regime `r` for the multitype branching process, and find `w>0`, `ε>0` satisfying the robust inequalities

`wᵀ A_r(θ) ≤ −ε wᵀ` componentwise for every phase `r` and every `θ∈C_α`.

On the confidence event, this gives `E[wᵀX(t)]≤exp(−εt)wᵀX(0)` even under arbitrary switching among the certified phases. If `w_min=min_i w_i`, then `P(X(t)≠0)≤exp(−εt)wᵀX(0)/w_min`. The confidence-set coverage makes the check finite-data valid with probability at least `1−α`; failure to find such a weight is inconclusive, not evidence of growth. This bound addresses survival in the certified regime. It does **not** by itself bound de-novo immigration, an unmeasured refuge, or establishment after a different maintenance phase; retain the source and post-pulse `q` terms in those cases.

### Exact failure if only mean trajectories are supplied

The mean generator depends on `r=b−d`, but the extinction/establishment law depends on `b` and `d` separately. For example, `(b,d)=(0.02,0.08)` and `(0.50,0.56)` per day both give `r=−0.06/day` and the same complete mean trajectory `E[X(t)]=X(0)e^(−0.06t)`. With a common later maintenance extinction probability `0.25` and a 30-day pulse, the exact one-type branching calculation already in `branch_extinction.md` gives establishment risks about `0.103` and `0.0199` from one founder, respectively. Therefore a weight computed only from net mean growth cannot identify finite-pulse rebound risk. This is a lower-bound counterexample for a **mean-only** observation interface, not for full event-resolved lineage data: observed births and deaths with known exposure can separate the two models. Likewise, if type labels or event detection are uncalibrated, the event-count likelihood estimates only detected/mixed hazards.

## 5. Measurable assumptions, acquisition cost, and falsifier

The minimum direct assay records, for each cell, its state, births, deaths, state changes, entry/exit times, and reason for loss. Use a stable live state/genotype reporter or barcode plus validated phenotype calls; calibrate event-label sensitivity and confusion by blinded controls. Preserve the full lineage tree, including dead and censored branches. Randomize multiple independent organoids/cultures to the treatment phase, track absolute counts, and assay the post-maintenance state in independent limiting-dilution wells. Separately draw a spatially representative prevalence sample or state explicitly that the risk certificate applies only to the sampled compartment.

For comparison, fit the strongest equal-information baseline to the **same** histories: the full counting-process likelihood / a correctly censored lineage-tree likelihood, with the same phase labels and detection calibration. Also report the less informative bulk count/fraction or barcode-plus-population-size baseline. Gunnarsson, Foo, and Leder already give a cancer-specific likelihood framework and structural/practical identifiability analysis for division, death, and switching; cell-number data can identify more than phenotype fractions alone ([2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111497)). Whiting et al. already infer evolving resistance phenotypes from barcode lineage distributions plus total cell counts without direct phenotype calls ([2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7)). These are informed prior-art baselines, not methods to dismiss. Compare interval coverage and held-out establishment predictions, not whether a new estimator produces a sharper point estimate.

The assay burden has a direct exposure lower bound. To certify a per-cell-day hazard below `θ*` after zero detected events at one-sided level `1−α`, perfect event sensitivity requires

`A ≥ −log(α)/θ*` cell-days.

At `θ*=10⁻³/day`, 95% confidence requires about 2,996 cell-days. With sensitivity `ρ=0.8`, this rises to 3,745 cell-days. If six one-sided event channels share a familywise 5% error budget by Bonferroni, the same sensitivity and target require `log(120)/(0.8×10⁻³) ≈ 5,984` cell-days. This is about 60 cells followed for 100 days, 100 cells for 60 days, or another design with the same validated at-risk exposure; it does not include independent-culture replication needed to detect between-culture heterogeneity. For a zero-colony direct establishment assay, `n` independent wells are required to put the 95% upper limit below `q*` only if `n ≥ log(0.05)/log(1−s q*)`; the cost grows as approximately `3/(s q*)` for small `q*`. Report microscope time, tracked cell-days, culture wells, state-call calibration, barcode/genotype sequencing, washout/rechallenge time, and sample coverage separately. No monetary cost is asserted.

Preregister a held-out set of lineage cultures and founder numbers. Freeze rates and confidence regions using training cultures, then test whether the stated hazard intervals attain their nominal coverage, whether post-maintenance colony outcomes lie within their predicted intervals, and whether the full-data risk bound dominates the equally informed baseline at the same coverage level. A direct falsifier is systematic undercoverage, a held-out rebound frequency above `B_U(T)`, or a state-specific censoring/detection rate outside calibration. The mean-only counterexample already falsifies any claimed identification from net trajectories alone. The prevalence formula is falsified by clustered/spatial sampling inconsistent with the declared inclusion model; then return `UNKNOWN` for the whole tumor.

## 6. Prior-art and scope boundary

- Garwood's exact Poisson limits are classical; the likelihood-ratio confidence-sequence construction is a standard finite-sample/optional-stopping technique, not cancer-specific novelty.
- Gunnarsson et al. (2023) already prove structural identifiability results and provide likelihood-based confidence intervals for cancer phenotype-switching rates using cell fraction or count data. Their result also cautions that fractions alone may estimate fewer parameters accurately.
- Genthon et al. (2023) already address incomplete lineage trees, dead-cell survivor bias, and lineage-based division/death estimation.
- Whiting et al. (2025) already combine genetic lineage tracing and population-size data to infer changing resistance phenotypes without direct phenotype measurement.
- The algebraic projection `C_α → [q_L,q_U] → B_U(T)` and the robust common-weight check are deductions for this particular risk-certificate interface. They are not an empirically validated clinical predictor or priority claim.

**Model versus nature.** Conditional mathematical guarantee: correct event-intensity model, correctly measured lineage states and exposures, noninformative censoring or a correct censor model, calibrated detection, independent or modeled culture effects, bounded absolute founder burden, bounded source rates over the future pulse, and a valid branching approximation. Nature may violate each through spatial refuges, density dependence, microenvironment, drug pharmacokinetics, cell-cycle/age-dependent hazards, phenotype/genotype misclassification, correlated sister fates, continuing mutation, and unobserved metastases. The certificate is valid only for the assayed compartments and named model class unless those effects are included with finite bounds. No biological hazard estimate, prospective validation, or clinical efficacy result was generated here.
