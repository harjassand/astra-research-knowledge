# Serial reset-race discrimination theorem

## Model

Under hypothesis (h\in\{0,1\}), a finite sensor starts at state 0 and must complete (m\) serial forward transitions to produce a positive output. At stage (i), it advances at rate (k_i>0), independent of (h), or resets irreversibly at rate \(\mu_h>0\). Assume (\mu_0>\mu_1), so hypothesis 1 has the lower reset hazard. The only observed terminal variable is whether the positive output occurred. Write

\[
p_h=\prod_{i=1}^m\frac{k_i}{k_i+\mu_h},\qquad r=\frac{\mu_0}{\mu_1}>1.
\]

Then every rate choice and every stage count obeys

\[
p_0\ge p_1^r.
\]

Equivalently, at true-positive rate (p_1\ge\eta), the false-positive rate cannot be smaller than \(\eta^r\). This floor is sharp as a limit over stage counts with rates retuned to keep (p_1) fixed.

For fixed (m) and fixed (p_1\in(0,1)), the smallest possible (p_0) is

\[
p_0^{\min}(m)=\left[\frac{p_1^{1/m}}{r-(r-1)p_1^{1/m}}\right]^m,
\]

attained when every stage has the same forward-completion probability under hypothesis 1, \(k_i/(k_i+\mu_1)=p_1^{1/m}\). These minima decrease to (p_1^r) as (m\to\infty).

## Proof

Set \(x_i=k_i/(k_i+\mu_1)\in(0,1)\). Since \(k_i=\mu_1x_i/(1-x_i)\),

\[
\frac{k_i}{k_i+\mu_0}=\frac{x_i}{r-(r-1)x_i}.
\]

For every (r>1), (0<x<1),

\[
\frac{x}{r-(r-1)x}\ge x^r.
\]

Indeed, this is equivalent to \(g(x)=x^{r-1}[r-(r-1)x]\le1\); (g(1)=1), and \(g'(x)=r(r-1)x^{r-2}(1-x)>0\), so (g(x)<1) for (x<1). Multiplying over stages gives (p_0\ge(\prod_i x_i)^r=p_1^r).

For the fixed-(m) optimizer, put (y_i=-\log x_i\) and (L=-\log p_1=\sum_i y_i). Define

\[
f(y)=\log\frac{e^{-y}}{r-(r-1)e^{-y}}.
\]

Then \(\log p_0=\sum_i f(y_i)\), with

\[
f'(y)=-\frac{r}{r-(r-1)e^{-y}},\qquad
f''(y)=\frac{r(r-1)e^{-y}}{[r-(r-1)e^{-y}]^2}>0.
\]

Strict convexity and Jensen give \(\sum_i f(y_i)\ge m f(L/m)\), with equality iff (y_i=L/m). The resulting formula is the claimed (p_0^{\min}(m)). Since (f(0)=0) and (f'(0)=-r), (m f(L/m)\to-rL\), giving \(p_0^{\min}(m)\downarrow p_1^r\). To see the monotone decrease, convexity and f(0)=0 imply f(y)/y is nondecreasing for y>0; as L/m decreases with m, so does mf(L/m)=L f(L/m)/(L/m).

## Time accounting

Conditional on success under hypothesis 1, the mean traversal time is

\[
\tau_1=\sum_i\frac{1}{k_i+\mu_1}.
\]

The expected time to either output or reset under hypothesis 1 is

\[
\mathbb E_1T=\sum_i\left(\prod_{j<i}x_j\right)\frac{1}{k_i+\mu_1}
=\frac{1-p_1}{\mu_1}.
\]

The last equality follows by telescoping: the (i)th term is \((\prod_{j<i}x_j)(1-x_i)/\mu_1\). Therefore, at fixed (p_1), the positive-hypothesis mean stopping time is the same for every architecture. Under hypothesis 0 it is \((1-p_0)/\mu_0\), which increases as the optimized false-positive rate falls. Conditional-success latency, expected transitions, and energetic cost are not fixed by this identity and must be charged separately. At the equalized fixed-\(p_1\) optimizer, let \(x=p_1^{1/m}\). The expected number of stage events (equivalently, total jumps including the terminal reset when it occurs) is \(\sum_{i=1}^m x^{i-1}=(1-p_1)/(1-x)\); the expected number of forward jumps alone is \(x(1-p_1)/(1-x)\). Thus stage events grow as \(m(1-p_1)/(-\log p_1)\). The common forward rate is \(k=\mu_1x/(1-x)\sim \mu_1m/(-\log p_1)\), and conditional-success latency tends to \((-\log p_1)/\mu_1\). The sharp stage-count limit therefore requires unbounded rate and event resources. If all forward rates are capped by \(K\), then \(p_1=\prod_i k_i/(k_i+\mu_1)\le[K/(K+\mu_1)]^m\), so \(m\le \log(p_1)/\log(K/(K+\mu_1))\).

## Discriminator and failure boundary

The model predicts the finite-stage curve (p_0^{\min}(m)) at fixed (p_1,\mu_0,\mu_1). A terminal-output observation below (p_1^{\mu_0/\mu_1}) falsifies at least one model assumption. It does not falsify CTMC theory generally: stage-specific reset ratios, hypothesis-dependent forward rates, reversible resets, hidden direct output channels, time-varying hazards, or non-Markovian waiting times lie outside this result.

No physical or biological procedure is prescribed. This is a synthetic mathematical discriminator only.
