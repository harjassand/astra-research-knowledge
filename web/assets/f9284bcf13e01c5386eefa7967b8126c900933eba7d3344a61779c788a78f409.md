## Exact interface being searched

For a qubit Dicke-diagonal operator

$$
S_N=\sum_{k=0}^N w_k |D_{N,k}\rangle\langle D_{N,k}|,
\qquad
w_k=\exp[-\delta(k-N/2)^2/N],
$$

phase-twirling an identical-site product with excitation probability x gives population binom(N,k) x^k(1-x)^(N-k). Consequently, exact full separability is equivalent to existence of a positive measure μ on [0,1] such that

$$
q_k:=\frac{w_k}{\binom Nk}
=\int_0^1 x^k(1-x)^{N-k}\,d\mu(x),
\quad k=0,\ldots,N.
$$

Here μ has mass sum_k w_k; for the normalized density matrix it is a probability measure. Under t=x/(1-x), this is the generalized truncated Stieltjes moment problem

$$
q_k=\int_{[0,\infty)}t^k\,d\sigma(t),\quad k<N,
\qquad
q_N=\int_{[0,\infty)}t^N\,d\sigma(t)+M,\quad M\ge0.
$$

The M term matters: it represents an endpoint atom at x=1, which contributes only to q_N. The corresponding finite Hankel test is

$$
H_0=(q_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\succeq0,
\qquad
H_1=(q_{i+j+1})_{i,j=0}^{\lfloor (N-1)/2\rfloor}\succeq0.
$$

This is a finite N+1-value problem. It is not positivity of an ordinary moment sequence in x, and it is not a claim that the continuous inverse Gaussian transform is positive.

## Verified primary prior art

Rutkowski, Banacki, and Marciniak, [*Necessary and sufficient condition of separability for D-symmetric diagonal states*](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.05522), is an exact match for the generic criterion and its scope. The paper states that D-symmetry coincides with permutation symmetry for qubits (abstract, §I), uses the unnormalized restricted Dicke basis in Eq. (8) (§III), and defines the generalized finite Stieltjes problem with top-index slack M≥0 (§VI, Eq. (18)). Theorem 5 gives the two finite Hankel PSD conditions as necessary and sufficient for that generalized moment problem; strict positive definiteness gives the strict moment problem. Theorem 8 then states, for arbitrary N and d, that a diagonal restricted-Dicke state is fully separable iff its coefficient sequence solves that generalized moment problem (§VIII). Lemma 7 explicitly builds the geometric-sequence component from a finite phase twirl of identical-site products, and Theorem 8 integrates those components against the representing measure plus the top endpoint product.

Under the normalization conversion

$$
|R_{N,2;k}\rangle=\sqrt{\binom Nk}|D_{N,k}\rangle,
$$

the coefficient in the paper's unnormalized basis is precisely q_k=w_k/binom(N,k). Thus the old theorem directly subsumes: (i) the exact finite-Hankel test for this q; (ii) equivalence between this test and full separability of the symmetric-support density; and (iii) existence of a positive product-mixture representation whenever the test passes. It does not evaluate the Gaussian sequence, prove uniform-in-N positivity at any δ interval, or give the δ≤5/4 family theorem.

Yu, [*Separability of Bosonic Systems*](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.02957), independently gives an all-N Hankel criterion for qubit Dicke-diagonal separability (Theorem 1) and a product-state decomposition bound in the abstract. I rely on Rutkowski et al. for the explicit q/generalized-moment normalization above, since it makes the unnormalized Dicke convention and endpoint correction explicit.

## Family-specific checks and proof-mechanism boundary

No inspected primary source directly states positivity of this particular Gaussian-over-binomial sequence for all N on a nontrivial uniform interval, the δ≤5/4 sufficiency claim, the 2 ln 2 universal necessary ceiling, or the asymptotic ceiling 2. Searches used formula and synonym variants around “Gaussian Dicke,” “thermal Dicke,” J_z^2/N, inverse-binomial/Stieltjes moments, the exact (k-N/2)^2/N exponent, and 2 log 2; they returned the general criteria above and unrelated Dicke entanglement/dynamics. This is a targeted no-hit, not an exhaustive priority search. Family-specific historical priority therefore remains **UNKNOWN**.

Two elementary gates can be independently checked, but are not literature findings from this search:

1. At N=2, q=(exp(-δ/2), 1/2, exp(-δ/2)). The first Hankel determinant is exp(-δ)-1/4, so exact separability holds iff δ≤ln(4)=2 ln 2. This proves the universal ceiling from the N=2 member; it does not establish the all-N threshold.
2. For even N, a separable normalized Dicke law has
   $$
   p_{N/2}=\binom{N}{N/2}\int[x(1-x)]^{N/2}d\mu(x)
   \le \binom{N}{N/2}2^{-N}.
   $$
   For the Gaussian target, the central population and the separable upper bound have asymptotics
   $$
   p_{N/2}=Z_N^{-1}\sim\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi N}},
   \qquad
   \binom{N}{N/2}2^{-N}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi N}}.
   $$
   Hence any fixed δ>2 fails for sufficiently large even N. This is weaker than the N=2 ceiling and does not determine the boundary at δ=2.

The previous continuous-deconvolution obstruction in [ROUTE_OBSTRUCTIONS.txt](../../c02_s03/axial/ROUTE_OBSTRUCTIONS.txt) survives this review with a crucial limitation. The gamma interpolation of the centered ratios is

$$
F(z)=\frac{\Gamma(c+z)\Gamma(c-z)}{\Gamma(c)^2}e^{-(\delta/N)z^2},
\qquad c=N/2+1.
$$

For δ>0, treating this whole continuous interpolation as an MGF of a positive real-logit measure would force its characteristic function to equal F(it), which is unbounded by the gamma reflection/recurrence formula (see [DLMF §5.5](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.5#E3)). This rules out that proposed continuous Gaussian deconvolution. It does not contradict finite-N separability: the physical criterion prescribes only N+1 values and finite Hankel PSD, with no analytic-continuation requirement. The internal δ≤5/4 argument respects this distinction by a pole-free truncated transform and an exact finite Bernstein correction in work/cycle6/c07_s02/phase2/BERNSTEIN_COMPLETION_PRINCIPLE.txt.

Finite-exchangeability results are also not an exact substitute. Diaconis and Freedman's [*Finite Exchangeable Sequences*](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-8/issue-4/Finite-Exchangeable-Sequences/10.1214/aop/1176994663) gives a variation-distance approximation for a k-coordinate marginal of a length-n exchangeable sequence by a mixture of iid laws (for finite alphabet size c, at most 2ck/n). Its stated object is a marginal approximation; it does not assert an exact iid-mixture representation for the entire length-N exchangeable law. The target here requires precisely that stronger whole-state product mixture, captured by the finite moment/Hankel test.
