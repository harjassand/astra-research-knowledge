Square negative-side symmetry: exact obstruction for one resolved path
Date: 2026-10-09

Status
------
The negative-corner dual-NN crosscut from the companion finite upper-half
packet does not satisfy the reflected lower-half bound on its negative side.
There is an exact 3x3-face Dobrushin example with positive probability for
which conditioning on the complete path forces an unpaired negative free
vertex and gives a conditional mean strictly below -1/2. The sign-flipped and
reversed construction does give the lower-half bound, but it uses the
positive-corner checkerboard pairing and can produce a different crosscut.
Thus the one-sided positive-side proof cannot be made two-sided for the same
resolved path by sign symmetry alone.

This is a finite counterexample to that same-path lower-bound claim. It is not
a counterexample to the triangular source theorem, and it does not rule out a
different square interface or a weighted comparison beyond exact sign-fiber
conditioning. No continuum or SLE statement is made.

Exact finite setting and counterexample
---------------------------------------
Take the 1-skeleton of a 3x3 square-face grid, with vertices (x,y),
0<=x,y<=3. Pin the bottom and right boundary arcs to +1/2 and the top and left
arcs to -1/2. The two boundary transition edges are at the lower-left and
upper-right corners. Sample normalized Lebesgue measure on the hard
nearest-neighbor 1-Lipschitz polytope.

Order the four free signs as

    (h(1,1), h(1,2), h(2,1), h(2,2)).

Use the negative-corner smoothing from
`cycle10_square_barrier_geometry_dual_nn/REPORT.txt`: orient crossed edges with
the positive endpoint on the right, and pair checkerboard crossings around
negative corners. The sign assignment

    (-, -, +, -)

has positive probability. For instance, setting the four free heights to
(-1/4,-1/4,+1/4,-1/4) satisfies every free-edge and free-to-boundary
constraint strictly; the pinned boundary transition edges may be saturated.
The sign event therefore has positive relative Lebesgue volume.

The complete crosscut for this sign assignment has the following sequence of
resolved face transitions, where each entry is (face, incoming side, outgoing
side):

    ((0,0), left, right)
    ((1,0), left, top)
    ((1,1), bottom, right)
    ((2,1), left, top)
    ((2,2), bottom, right)

At the central face (1,1), the NW, NE, SE, SW signs are (-,-,+,-). The path
enters through the bottom edge and exits through the right edge. Its cut
vertices there are the positive SE corner and negative SW and NE corners.
The NW corner is the free vertex b=h(1,2)<0, is not on any crossed edge, and
has no incident edge in the complete cut set.

The path transcript uniquely identifies this four-vertex sign pattern among
all 16 possible free-sign assignments; `witness_check.py` exhausts them and
records the exact path. Since every sign assignment has positive volume, the
event that the full resolved crosscut equals this path has positive
probability and, almost surely on that event, forces

    a=h(1,1)<0,  b=h(1,2)<0,  c=h(2,1)>0,  d=h(2,2)<0.

The boundary pins adjacent to a and d imply a,d in [-1/2,0) on this event.
Condition on a,d,c and the path. The only constraints involving b are its two
edges to a,d, its two edges to pinned -1/2 vertices (0,2) and (1,3), and its
recorded negative sign. They give the exact conditional interval

    b in [max(a,d)-1, 0].

The uniform conditional density on this interval has mean

    E[b | a,d,gamma] = (max(a,d)-1)/2 < -1/2.

Averaging over a,d,c yields

    E[h(1,2) | gamma] < -1/2.

This contradicts the proposed lower-half counterpart for this same square
negative-corner crosscut. It does not contradict the triangular source
theorem. The obstruction is exactly the extra
negative sign wall at b: it is on the negative side and lacks a positive NN
partner across the cut.

Sign-flipped companion and why it is a different path
-----------------------------------------------------
Apply the previous positive-side theorem to g=-h, whose boundary arcs are
swapped, and reverse the resulting orientation so that h is positive on the
right. This produces a sign-only companion rule for h that pairs checkerboard
crossings around positive corners. Every queried negative vertex on its
retained negative side is then a cut endpoint with an actual positive NN
partner. By the sign-flipped upper-half theorem, this companion crosscut obeys

    E[h(x) | gamma_positive-corner] >= -1/2

on its negative boundary side.

It is not the same realized crosscut as the negative-corner path. On the same
3x3 grid with free signs (-,+,+,-), the central face is the checkerboard
NW=+, NE=-, SE=+, SW=-. The negative-corner path visits that face twice, with
transitions bottom-to-left and later top-to-right. The positive-corner path
uses bottom-to-right and leaves the face. The exact sequences are in
`WITNESS_CHECK.json`. Hence global sign flip/reversal supplies a companion
one-sided law, not a two-sided estimate conditional on the original curve.

Local randomized checkerboard ties
----------------------------------
A sign-independent random tie at a checkerboard does not restore the exact
unweighted sign-fiber used by the DLR comparison. For an incoming top edge
with NW=p>0, NE=q<0, SE=r, SW=s, the non-checkerboard continuations are forced:

    r>0, s>0: right;
    r<0, s>0: bottom;
    r<0, s<0: left.

Only r>0, s<0 is a checkerboard. Let theta be the probability of taking the
right continuation there, with probability 1-theta for left. On a right path,
the off-cut sign s has likelihood 1 when s>0 and theta when s<0; equal fibers
require theta=1. On a left path, the off-cut sign r has likelihood 1 when r<0
and 1-theta when r>0; equal fibers require theta=0. No theta satisfies both.
For 0<theta<1 both events carry sign-dependent likelihood weights, so the
conditional density is no longer a uniform polytope fiber. This local check
rules out the simple randomized tie repair of the exact sign-fiber proof; it
does not rule out all possible weighted-comparison methods or nonlocal
exploration rules.

Proof status, costs, and falsifiers
-----------------------------------
The conditional-mean failure follows exactly from the positive-volume sign
pattern, exhaustive path-fiber enumeration, and the one-coordinate interval
calculation above. No limiting or regularity argument is used. The check is
finite: 16 sign assignments, two deterministic checkerboard pairings, and a
symbolic interval identity. Its earliest falsifier would be another free-sign
assignment with the same resolved path but a different sign at b, or an
additional constraint involving b that shortens the displayed interval; the
script checks the former, and the grid adjacency list checks the latter.

This does not supply a single square crosscut with both conditional bounds.
A two-sided result would need a different joint information mechanism that
controls both sign fibers, or would need to use the two distinct companion
crosscuts with an explicitly changed conditioning statement. No SLE,
continuum convergence, or square height-gap conclusion follows.
