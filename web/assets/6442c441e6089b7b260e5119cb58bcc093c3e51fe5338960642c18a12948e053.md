# Friedrichs-extension gate for the formal Kohn–Sham potential

## Verdict

**The extension passes, conditional on the paper’s stated propositions.** For the constructed real potential
\[
V=\frac{\Delta g}{2g}\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^3),
\]
the minimal quadratic form
\[
q_0[f]=\frac12\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\nabla f|^2+\int_{\mathbb R^3}V|f|^2,
\qquad D(q_0)=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C),
\]
is densely defined, nonnegative, and closable. Its Friedrichs operator has \(\widehat g=g/\sqrt2\) as a simple zero-energy ground eigenfunction and \(h\) as an eigenfunction at the exact bottom \(d>0\) on \(\widehat g^\perp\). The spin-independent three-electron operator therefore has a ground determinant with density \(n=g^2+h^2\).

This does not contradict the source’s nonrepresentability theorem: its standard potential class is \(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\), and this particular \(V\) lies outside it. It shows representability after enlarging the admissible local-potential realization to the Friedrichs operator of this specific \(L^2_{\rm loc}\) function. No counterexample to that extension was found.

## Exact assumptions inherited from the source

The source’s Proposition 4.3 gives \(g,h\in H^1\cap H^2_{\rm loc}\), continuous strictly positive \(g\), \(V\in L^2_{\rm loc}\), distributional equations
\[
(-\Delta/2+V)g=0,\qquad (-\Delta/2+V)h=dh,
\]
and \(\|g\|_2^2=2,\ \|h\|_2^2=1,\ \langle g,h\rangle=0,\ n=g^2+h^2\) ([source, Proposition 4.3](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c9_frontier_transfer/physical_design_transfer/sources/kohn_sham.txt:1424)).

The needed spectral input is Eq. (4.23), proved in Proposition 4.6 for its stated regime of sufficiently large \(D\) and sufficiently small \(\lambda\ge0\):
\[
\frac12\int g^2|\nabla\xi|^2\ge
d\left(\int g^2|\xi|^2-\frac{|\int g^2\xi|^2}{\int g^2}\right),
\qquad \xi\in H^1(g^2).
\]
Proposition 5.1 also supplies \(\int n|V|<\infty\), extends the associated ground-state inequality to
\[
E_V=\{f\in H^1:\int |V||f|^2<\infty\},
\]
and computes \(q_V[\widehat g]=0,\ q_V[h]=d\) by cutoffs ([source, Eq. Proposition 5.1 expressly avoids assuming an ordinary Hamiltonian or closed form; the construction below adds one.

## Closability and form-domain membership

For \(f\in C_c^\infty\), the source’s positive-orbital identity yields
\[
q_0[f]=\frac12\int g^2|\nabla(f/g)|^2\ge0.
\]
Let \(d\mu=g^2dx\). The map \(Uf=f/g\) is unitary from \(L^2(dx)\) onto \(L^2(d\mu)\). Positivity and continuity of \(g\) make \(d\mu\) locally equivalent to Lebesgue measure. If \(f_j\to0\) in \(L^2(dx)\) and \(q_0[f_j-f_k]\to0\), then \(Uf_j\to0\) in \(L^2(d\mu)\), while \(\nabla Uf_j\) converges in \(L^2(d\mu)\) to some \(G\). On each compact set, local equivalence implies convergence in ordinary \(L^2\) of both functions and gradients. The distributional gradient of the zero limit is zero, so \(G=0\) locally and hence \(\mu\)-a.e. Thus \(q_0[f_j]\to0\), the closability criterion. Density follows from density of \(C_c^\infty\) in \(L^2\).

Write \(\bar q\) for the closure. It is a densely defined closed nonnegative form, and its associated operator \(H_F\) is the Friedrichs extension of the minimal symmetric operator \(-\Delta/2+V\). No global Kato-smallness or \(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) bound is needed: nonnegativity is supplied directly by the ground-state identity. The closed-form representation used here is Kato, Chapter VI §2 ([Springer edition](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-66282-9)); the source itself invokes Kato VI.2.1 and VI.2.23 in Proposition 5.2.

To put \(g,h\) in the *minimal-form closure*, use the source’s \(H^1\) bounds, \(g^2+h^2=n\), and \(\int n|V|<\infty\): both lie in \(E_V\). For either \(f=g\) or \(f=h\), choose smooth cutoffs \(\chi_R\) equal to one on \(B_R\), zero outside \(B_{2R}\), with \(|\nabla\chi_R|\le C/R\). Then
\[
\|\chi_Rf-f\|_{H^1}\to0,\qquad
\int |V||\chi_Rf-f|^2\to0.
\]
For fixed \(R\), approximate \(\chi_Rf\) by smooth functions supported in a common compact set. Strong \(H^1\) convergence implies strong \(L^4\) convergence there, and \(V\in L^2_{\rm loc}\) gives convergence of the potential term. A diagonal sequence proves \(g,h\in D(\bar q)\). This core step is essential; distributional eigen-equations alone would not imply membership in the Friedrichs domain.

For compact smooth \(\varphi\), the distributional equations give
\[
\bar q(g,\varphi)=0,\qquad \bar q(h,\varphi)=d\langle h,\varphi\rangle.
\]
Density of the core in \(D(\bar q)\), form Cauchy–Schwarz, and \(L^2\) continuity extend these identities to every form-domain test vector. The first representation theorem therefore yields \(H_Fg=0\) and \(H_Fh=dh\). The source’s cutoff identity \(q_V[\chi_R f]=\varepsilon\|\chi_Rf\|_2^2+\frac12\int|\nabla\chi_R|^2|f|^2\) gives the corresponding form values \(0,d\).

## Gap and spinful ground state

The source’s weighted gap passes from \(C_c^\infty\) to \(D(\bar q)\):
\[
\bar q[f]\ge d\bigl(\|f\|_2^2-|\langle\widehat g,f\rangle|^2\bigr).
\]
Hence \(\ker H_F=\operatorname{span}\{\widehat g\}\) and \(H_F\ge d\) on \(\widehat g^\perp\). Since \(h\perp\widehat g\) and \(H_Fh=dh\), \(d\) is exactly the lowest orthogonal spectral value.

Extend \(H_F\) spin-independently as \(H_F\otimes I_{\mathbb C^2}\). Its zero-energy spin-orbital space has rank two, \(\operatorname{span}\{\widehat g\uparrow,\widehat g\downarrow\}\). On the three-particle antisymmetric space, occupation of this subspace is at most two, while every orthogonal one-particle mode costs at least \(d\). Thus the three-body sum is bounded below by \(d\). The normalized determinant
\[
\Phi=(\widehat g\uparrow)\wedge(\widehat g\downarrow)\wedge(h\uparrow)
\]
attains \(d\) and has spin-summed density \(2|\widehat g|^2+|h|^2=n\). It is a pure ground state, hence also a ground-state ensemble.

## Transfer boundary, cost, and next gate

This is a mathematical representation by a noninteracting, spin-independent, scalar local operator with the *specific* real \(L^2_{\rm loc}\) potential and its Friedrichs realization. It does not apply to arbitrary \(L^2_{\rm loc}\) potentials; establish representability in the source’s \(L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) class (which its Theorem 1.1 rules out for this density); or transfer to practical generalized-KS models with nonlocal, spin-dependent, or finite-basis operators. Nor does it establish numerical stability or usability of this potential. The “cost” of the extension is precisely the broader operator class and the canonical Friedrichs-domain choice, rather than a regular potential in the conventional class.

**Next decisive gate:** independent verification of Proposition 4.6’s parameter regime and the source’s cutoff/core argument. Given those hypotheses, the form-theoretic extension above follows without a separate spectral computation. This audit validates the extension *conditional on the source proof*; it is not independent validation of the source’s construction or a novelty claim.
