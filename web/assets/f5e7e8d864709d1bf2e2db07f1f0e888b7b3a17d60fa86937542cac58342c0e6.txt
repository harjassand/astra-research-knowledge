FAILED RELATIVE-MODULAR SHORTCUT: ORIENTATION AND GNS-SYMMETRY TRAP

This route was considered before the successful frequency factorization and
did not yield a proof. It is preserved to prevent an unjustified covariance
assumption being introduced during reconstruction.

Let D=e^(ad(log s)/2), A_op=D H D^(-1), Delta=L_rho R_sigma^(-1), v=s.
Then Delta v=rho s^(-1), sqrt(Delta)v=sqrt(rho), and the physical derivative
Z=L_(L_*rho) R_sigma^(-1) obeys Z v=A_op Delta v.

The GNS contraction represents Heisenberg evolution on Xs, whose generator is
A_op^*=D^(-1)H D, rather than A_op. Thus the relative-modular Schwarz defect
has the orientation

 A_op Delta+Delta A_op^*-Z>=0,

not the reversed expression A_op^* Delta+Delta A_op-Z. Both A_op v and
A_op^*v vanish. The correctly oriented defect merely reproduces
Zv=A_op Delta v upon application to v.

If one incorrectly reverses the orientation, its positivity and zero
expectation at v would imply A_op^* Delta v=Zv=A_op Delta v for every rho.
By complex span this forces A_op=A_op^*, a GNS-symmetry conclusion not
available for arbitrary KMS generators. This is an algebraic normalization
error, not a legal counterexample to the endpoint.

Replacing the nonsymmetric GNS operator by its HS symmetric part in the
nonlinear entropy form is likewise not justified by this shortcut. The final
proof instead obtains real symmetric scalar coefficients by exact reversal
of physical density pairs and stationary noise frequencies, retains all
Hamiltonian reference terms, and invokes the full Hardy Gram directly.
