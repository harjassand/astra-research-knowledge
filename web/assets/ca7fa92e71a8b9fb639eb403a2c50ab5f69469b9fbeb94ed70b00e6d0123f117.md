# Transfers from a supplied one-sided inverse

Status: the algebraic implications below are complete written arguments. They do not establish existence of the supplied group-ring witnesses. Applying them to OpenAI family 197 remains conditional on that construction's correctness. The underlying Toeplitz/Jacobson and Adian-Rabin mechanisms are classical; no novelty claim is made for them.

## Supplied input and characteristic boundary

Let K be a field, G a group, and a,b in K[G] satisfy ab=1 but ba!=1. These elements have finite support. If G is specified by a finite presentation, the relations proving ab=1 can be listed finitely, but verifying ba!=1 is a separate word/equality problem. A general word-problem decider has not been acquired from the presentation.

The October 4 source for family 197 claims such witnesses already over F_2 in a torsion-free finitely presented group with a finite two-dimensional classifying complex. The September 23 construction uses a finite characteristic-two extension field and retains odd-order torsion. Those are different sources and different premises. The matching-heading Lean catalogue does not by itself verify the torsion-free companion.

## 1. An embedded algebraic Toeplitz algebra

Put e=1-ba. Then e^2=e!=0, ae=0 and eb=0. For i,j>=0 let

\[
 E_{ij}=b^iea^j.
\]

The identities imply E_ij E_kl=delta_jk E_il. Each E_ij is nonzero, since a^i E_ij b^j=e. In particular K[G] contains a nonunital copy of the algebra of finite matrices indexed by the nonnegative integers, with arbitrarily many pairwise orthogonal nonzero equivalent idempotents E_ii.

More strongly the homomorphism

\[
 K\langle x,y\mid xy=1\rangle\longrightarrow K[G],
 \qquad x\mapsto a,\quad y\mapsto b
\]

is injective. Here is a direct proof avoiding an imported essential-socle theorem. Reduction by xy->1 gives a unique basis y^i x^j: the rewrite terminates and has no overlapping critical pair. If a nonzero finite linear combination F=sum c_ij b^i a^j vanished, multiply by b^k e. For k=0 this gives sum_i c_i0 b^i e=0. The vectors b^i e are linearly independent: multiply such a finite relation by a to the largest occurring exponent to isolate its top coefficient times e, then descend. Thus c_i0=0. Induct on k; the earlier columns have vanished, the terms j>k contain a^(j-k)e=0, and the k-th column is again sum_i c_ik b^i e. Every c_ij must vanish, a contradiction.

This does not imply an embedded Leavitt algebra of type (1,2): one infinite idempotent need not be properly infinite. Nor does it produce a characteristic-zero group-ring counterexample. The trace/positivity argument in complex finite von Neumann algebras excludes those one-sided inverses in characteristic zero.

## 2. Exact finite-memory cellular automata

For c=sum c_u u in K[G], define

\[
 T_c(f)(g)=\sum_u c_u f(gu),\qquad f\in K^G.
\]

Finite support makes this a uniform finite-memory rule on all configurations. Directly expanding the finite sum gives T_c T_d=T_cd, in this order. Consequently T_b is injective because T_a T_b=I.

Choose h with (ba-1)_h!=0 and let delta_h be the configuration supported at h. Every member of im(T_b) is fixed by T_b T_a=T_ba, but

\[
 (T_{ba}\delta_h)(1)=(ba)_h\ne\delta_h(1).
\]

Thus delta_h is an explicit configuration outside the image. For K=F_2 this is a binary linear cellular automaton.

Conversely, an injective linear cellular automaton T_b on K^G, with K finite, has a finite-memory linear left inverse. Its image is closed, because K^G is compact. Its inverse on the image is continuous and equivariant. The coordinate at identity of that inverse is a continuous linear functional on the image, so it factors through projection to a finite set of coordinates. Extend the resulting functional on that finite-dimensional subspace to the full finite coordinate space and use the same equivariant rule on every translate. This gives T_a T_b=I. Therefore K[G] is directly finite exactly when all injective K-linear cellular automata on K^G are surjective.

The converse argument needs a finite field and its compact full shift; it is not an algorithm for acquiring a from b.

## 3. A presentation-level non-semidecidability theorem

Theorem. Fix a finite field K. If there exists one finitely presented group D with K[D] not directly finite, then the set of finite group presentations whose K-group algebra is not directly finite is not recursively enumerable. In particular direct finiteness is undecidable from finite presentations. The same assertions hold for failure of K-linear surjunctivity. Full surjunctivity, soficity, and K-linear soficity also become undecidable and their failures non-recursively enumerable when D is a supplied counterexample.

Proof. Direct finiteness is invariant under group isomorphism and hereditary to subgroups: if H<=G, the basis inclusion K[H]->K[G] is injective, so a one-sided-inverse defect in K[H] survives in K[G]. The trivial group is a positive witness. The supplied D is a negative witness that cannot embed in any positive group. This makes the property a Markov property.

The classical Adian-Rabin construction supplies a computable transformation w->P_w from words in a fixed torsion-free finitely presented group H with undecidable word problem, with these two cases: if w=1 in H, P_w presents the trivial group; if w!=1, its group contains D. In the cited Adian construction, torsion-freeness ensures that every nonidentity w has infinite order, as required by its Main Lemma. Thus

\[
 K[G(P_w)]\text{ is not directly finite}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w\ne1\text{ in }H.
\]

The nonidentity words in H are not recursively enumerable: identity words already are, and enumerability of both would decide the word problem. A semidecider for group-ring failure would therefore give a semidecider for these nonidentity words, a contradiction.

The exact linear-automaton equivalence proves the first dynamical assertion. For full surjunctivity, an injective nonsurjective automaton on a subgroup induces the same rule on the ambient group's left cosets; it acts independently on each coset. It stays injective and nonsurjective, so full surjunctivity is also hereditary to subgroups. Soficity is hereditary to subgroups, and Elek-Szabo's theorem says a sofic group's K-group algebra is directly finite. K-linear soficity is hereditary by restricting a rank-metric embedding to a subgroup, and the obstruction in section 5 makes D a negative witness for it. The same reduction applies to these properties.

The unrestricted Adian-Rabin theorem used here does not establish this recognition result under a torsion-free promise, a hyperbolic promise, a supplied word-problem algorithm, or a finite-aspherical-complex promise. No such strengthened claim is made.

Primary source for the precise classical Markov theorem and word-dependent construction: Adian, in Nyberg-Brodda's English translation, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08560, pages 76-78 (PDF pages 76-78), Theorem 1 and Main Lemma. The theorem is explicitly stated there and is not imported from a search snippet.

For arbitrary finite presentations there is also an elementary arithmetical upper bound: direct finiteness is Pi^0_2. Enumerate finite group-ring expressions a,b and formal equality proofs s,t. Its statement is forall a,b,s [s proves ab=1 implies exists t proving ba=1]. Failure is therefore Sigma^0_2. No completeness claim at this level is proved here.

If a correct total word-problem decider is additionally supplied, failure becomes semidecidable: enumerate the finite a,b and use that decider to collect equal support words and check both products. Thus the negative theorem concerns presentation-only acquisition, rather than an inability to check an already supplied finite defect when an equality oracle is also supplied.

## 4. The isolated parity lemma actually reconstructed

The torsion-free companion's algebra step has a useful finite interface. It supplies two finite immersed loopless labeled graphs A,B, a quotient group killing each graph-component's closed paths, and chosen roots. Let S_x be the outgoing label set. Suppose |S_x intersect S_x'| is odd for all A-root-component pairs except the root/root pair, whose degree is even; and |S_x intersect S_y| is odd for every A/B root-component pair. Suppose only that the B root is protected: no path from it to another vertex in its component has identity group label.

Let g_x,h_y be root-to-vertex group labels. Define a=sum_x g_x, b=sum_x g_x^-1, c=sum_y h_y^-1 over F_2. Then ab=1, ac=0, and c!=0.

Proof. Form the simultaneous-step graph on A x A, respectively A x B. Along an edge with common label t, (g_x t)(g_x' t)^-1=g_x g_x'^-1, so the contributed group element is constant on each component. Every component with all degrees odd has even cardinality by the handshaking lemma. A component with exactly one even-degree vertex has odd cardinality. Thus all contributions cancel except one identity term in ab, and all cancel in ac. Root protection makes the identity coefficient of c exactly one. If ba=1, then c=(ba)c=b(ac)=0, contradiction.

The positive-characteristic algebra proof is complete at this finite interface. The difficult unacquired input is protection of the root after killing all graph cycles. In the October 4 manuscript this depends on its bounded-pattern probability estimate, the planar passage to arbitrary spherical arrangements, and cone surgeries. This worker read the algebra, planar interface, and topological source but has not certified that entire probabilistic/topological package. Nor does asphericity alone supply a negative-curvature metric or a linear filling inequality.

## 5. A characteristic-specific rank obstruction

For square matrices A,B over any field,

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(I-AB)=\operatorname{rank}(I-BA).
\]

Indeed A maps ker(I-BA) bijectively onto ker(I-AB), with inverse B. Equal nullities imply equal ranks. Let r_n be rank divided by matrix size, and let phi be an exactly unital K-linear model of K[G] in M_n(K). Suppose its multiplicativity errors at (a,b) and (b,a) have r_n at most epsilon. Since ab=1,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_n(\phi(e))
 &\le r_n(I-\phi(b)\phi(a))
       +r_n(\phi(b)\phi(a)-\phi(ba))\\
 &=r_n(I-\phi(a)\phi(b))
       +r_n(\phi(b)\phi(a)-\phi(ba))
 \le 2\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Consequently no rank-metric matrix ultraproduct admits an injective unital algebra homomorphism from K[G]. The defect e would have zero ultraproduct rank and hence vanish. This is a quantitative obstruction for algebra models; its separation parameter r_n(phi(e)) is part of the model interface, not an input acquired just from a group presentation.

Arzhantseva-Paunescu, Theorem 7.4, proves that an injective group homomorphism into a rank-metric ultraproduct over any fixed field K can be converted into an injective K[G] algebra homomorphism into such an ultraproduct, potentially with larger matrix sizes. Combining that classical theorem with the rank identity proves that the supplied G is not K-linearly sofic. This is stronger than its nonsoficity in the same characteristic, but does not imply failure of complex linear soficity or of hyperlinearity from a characteristic-two defect.

The change of representation is essential. Even a faithful group representation can have a nonfaithful linear extension: over F_4, embed C_3 into one-by-one matrices by mapping its generator g to a primitive cube root omega. The nonzero element g-omega*1 of F_4[C_3] maps to zero. Thus inspecting the linear extension of an arbitrary faithful group model is not the theorem's missing acquisition step.

Prior sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.6780, Proposition 2.8 and Theorem 7.4; https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305440, Corollary 4.7; and https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00109 for the classical Toeplitz/Jacobson algebra context. The source-family construction supplies no char-zero transfer.

## 6. An explicit finite permutation test from a protected root

The parity interface additionally supplies c=sum_{j=0}^{s-1}h_j^{-1}, with h_0=1 and h_j!=1 for every j>0, and identities ab=1 and ac=0. Distinctness of the h_j among themselves is not supplied and is not needed here.

Let P_0=I,P_1,...,P_{s-1} be permutation matrices of size N over F_2, with each P_j, j>0, having at most delta*N fixed points. Put C=sum_j P_j. Suppose matrices A,B satisfy

\[
 r_N(AB-I)\le\epsilon_1,\qquad
 r_N(AC)\le\epsilon_2.
\]

Then

\[
 \epsilon_1+\epsilon_2\ge
 \frac{1-(s-1)\delta}{s^2}.                               \tag{6}
\]

Proof. At least (1-(s-1)delta)*N diagonal positions of C equal one, by the union bound for fixed points. Every row and every column of C has at most s nonzero entries. Among columns with such a diagonal position, greedily choose columns with pairwise disjoint supports. Each choice removes at most s^2 candidate columns: its at most s occupied rows meet at most s columns each. Thus at least (1-(s-1)delta)*N/s^2 nonzero columns with disjoint support can be selected. They are linearly independent, giving the same lower bound on rank(C). Sylvester's inequality gives rank(C)<=rank(AC)+nullity(A), and nullity(A)<=rank(AB-I). Combining these inequalities proves (6).

For example delta<=1/(2(s-1)), when s>1, forces epsilon_1+epsilon_2>=1/(2s^2). A sufficiently accurate sofic model on the finitely many words appearing in proofs of ab=1 and ac=0 and in the protected root paths would violate this inequality. This is an independent finite robust implication from the supplied graph/witness interface; it does not acquire a graph with root protection. The sparse-column argument is the same classical mechanism behind injectivity in Elek-Szabo's proof, rather than a new replacement for that theorem.

The finite test retains all s permutation matrices, the matrices for the support words of a and b, and the finite relation proofs that bound epsilon_1 and epsilon_2. No small bound on those supplied objects, on s, or on the model size N is established from the October 4 asymptotic existence argument.
