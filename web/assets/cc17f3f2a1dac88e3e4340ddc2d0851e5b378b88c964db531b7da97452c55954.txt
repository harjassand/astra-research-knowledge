CYCLE 10 LUNA/MAX — SQUARE SIDE-HEIGHT PAIR REFERENCE GATE

Decision
========
A common *signed vector* reference law is an exact way to force deterministic
limiting side constants without reflecting a realized negative-corner path.
The argument extends the common-kernel lemma used in the pinned triangular
height-gap proof from a scalar absolute reading to the pair of signed side
readings. It gives a fixed pair (L,R), which may be asymmetric. Centering
requires one additional involutive symmetry of the *reference law* and a
common-pair conclusion across the paired experiments; field convergence,
finite mean ranges, and the pathwise sign flip alone do not provide it.

This is a conditional reduction, not an acquired square theorem. The decisive
square input is uniform lower domination of the full ordered-path local record
(or its signed-pair pushforward), including every turn-forced negative query,
by one external-record-independent reference law. The supplied CAP, SC0–SC3,
trace, and field-limit inputs do not by themselves state that domination.

Inputs and scope
================
I take the following as supplied conditional inputs, rather than re-proving
them here: the actual square negative-corner Gamma mean ranges
E[h_x|Gamma] in [c,1/2] on its positive side and [-1,-c] on its negative
side; the stated SC0–SC3 comparison interface; conditional boundary-trace
control; and the distributional GFF field limit. The ranges are from the
actual ordered-port-pair Gamma law, not a sign-reflected conditioning on the
same path. This report does not promote those conditional inputs to an
unconditional square scaling result.

Primary literature was inspected directly:

* Schramm–Sheffield, “Contour lines of the two-dimensional discrete Gaussian
  free field,” arXiv:math/0605337v3, §§3.5–3.7, Theorems 3.21 and 3.28.
  Theorem 3.21 obtains a unique local two-sided path law by rooting at an
  independent random-walk harmonic-measure landing and coupling inner and
  outer records; Theorem 3.28 upgrades the conditional mean to the fixed
  +/-lambda harmonic profile. Its setup is triangular-lattice DGFF, bounded
  boundary data, a simply connected Jordan domain, an independent simple
  random walk, and the paper’s boundary-sign compatibility condition. It is
  not a square hard-Lipschitz result. The proof mechanism is harmonic-measure
  rooting and near/far comparison, not a mass-transport or a general
  rerooting-stationarity theorem.
* Schramm–Sheffield, “A contour line of the continuum Gaussian free field,”
  arXiv:1008.2447v1, Theorem 1.3, Proposition 3.7, Lemma 4.6, and Lemma 4.7.
  Locality gives harmonic conditional means, and limits of the paper’s
  discrete local sets remain local. The theorem identifying the canonical
  zero contour additionally assumes the conformal Markov coupling with
  boundary values +/-lambda. It does not say that an arbitrary limiting
  square sign strand with harmonic conditional means is that canonical
  local set. No applicable reversal/rerooting theorem was found that removes
  this coupling requirement.

Exact common-reference vector lemma
===================================
Let K be a compact metric space with metric d, and let i range over a fixed
finite family of experiments. For each n,i let U_n^i,V_n^i be K-valued,
with U_n^i measurable with respect to A_n^i. Suppose

  E d(U_n^i,V_n^i) -> 0,

and there are events G_n^i in A_n^i with P((G_n^i)^c)->0, probability
measures nu_n on K, and one c0>0 such that, on G_n^i,

  P(V_n^i in B | A_n^i) >= c0 nu_n(B)

for every Borel B and every i. Then every subsequence has a further
subsequence and one x in K such that U_n^i and V_n^i both converge in
probability to x for all i.

Proof. By compactness, pass to a subsequence with nu_n converging weakly to
nu and the laws of all U_n^i converging weakly to mu_i. Conditional measure
domination implies, for the nonnegative function y -> d(U_n^i,y),

  E d(U_n^i,V_n^i) >= c0 E[1_{G_n^i} integral d(U_n^i,y) nu_n(dy)].

The functions x -> integral d(x,y) nu_n(dy) converge uniformly on K to
g(x)=integral d(x,y) nu(dy): they are uniformly equicontinuous and converge
pointwise on a dense set. Since P((G_n^i)^c)->0 and d is bounded, taking
limits gives

  integral_K integral_K d(x,y) mu_i(dx) nu(dy) = 0.

If independent X~mu_i and Y~nu have d(X,Y)=0 almost surely, both laws are
the same point mass. The measure nu is common, so the atom x is common to
all i. Compactness then gives convergence in probability. This proves the
claim.

Square application: precise sufficient reduction
=================================================
Take K=[-M,M]^2, with coordinates ordered by the actual positive and negative
sign sides. For each experiment and a cleared harmonic-landing branch, define
U_n=(U_n^+,U_n^-) by two protected, smoothed readings of the complete
conditional-mean profile on opposite sides of the same local strand. Define
V_n=(V_n^+,V_n^-) by the corresponding canonical local conditional means,
conditioned on the *entire* local ordered-port-pair transcript: all crossed
edge signs and all negative-corner turn queries. The test kernels and side
convention must be chosen equivariantly under the square dihedral symmetries.
The supplied locality/trace estimates would have to establish
E||U_n-V_n|| -> 0 under each branch, with the scales and Brownian access
limits stated explicitly.

The exact lower-domination premise needed is: after normalizing each
geometric branch to one canonical port label, there is a single reference
law mu_n^* for the full local record and a constant C<infinity independent
of n, domain, outer record, branch, and unused buffers such that on a cleared
outer event

  Law(local ordered Gamma record | outer record, landing branch)
       >= C^-1 mu_n^*

as measures on every measurable event of that record. Pushing mu_n^* through
the same signed two-reading function yields the common nu_n required by the
lemma. This is an event-level comparison; a bound on the marginal of one
arm, a CAP estimate for individual signs, or a lower bound for hookup alone
is not enough. In the square, the canonical local conditional mean must keep
every extra negative query T=f(Gamma); dropping it changes the sign fiber.

If this premise and the two-reading approximation hold for the finite
experiment family, the lemma forces a deterministic pair (L,R) common to
that family along a subsequence. The supplied actual ranges then pass to the
limiting trace as c<=L<=1/2 and -1<=R<=-c, assuming the trace convergence
is at the same side-sampled accesses. It does not force R=-L.

What would center the pair
==========================
There is a sharp abstract criterion. Let J(a,b)=(-b,-a). Suppose a paired
family of experiments is related by a measure-preserving transformation
that negates heights and exchanges the two side labels, so its limiting
signed vector is J(L,R). If the *same common-reference argument* forces the
same deterministic pair for both the original and paired families, then

  (L,R)=J(L,R), hence L=-R.

This comparison is between two experiments and their transformed records; it
does not require the transformed field to produce the same negative-corner
path. At finite square scale, height negation interchanges negative- and
positive-corner smoothing. Thus the natural pair is the negative-corner
experiment for h and the positive-corner experiment for -h, with port order
reversed to preserve the side convention. Their local resolution relation
is exact. But exact transformation alone sends a reference law nu_n to
J#nu_n. It does not prove a common J-invariant lower reference law. The
centering route therefore still needs either (i) a common symmetric
submeasure uniformly dominated in both paired families, or (ii) an
asymptotic two-resolution comparison strong enough to identify their signed
profile-pair limits. Neither follows from the stated finite ranges or field
limit.

A frozen 3x3 hard-Lipschitz example in
`work/agents/cycle10_square_negative_side_symmetry/REPORT.txt` shows why the
shortcut h -> -h while retaining the *same negative-corner resolution* is
invalid: the transformed path may be different, and an unpaired negative
vertex can have conditional mean below -1/2. This rules out that same-path
argument, not the paired-rule criterion just stated.

GFF covariance and the midpoint
===============================
A GFF field limit by itself gives no joint interface/local-set statement and
cannot identify L,R. If a further stopped conditional-covariance identity
holds for this actual interface, with the required second-moment uniform
integrability, it can identify the *gap* g=L-R from the covariance
normalization (in the source normalization, g^2=pi sigma^2/2). It does not
identify the midpoint (L+R)/2. A first-moment argument would need the
limiting side-label probabilities as an additional input. For example, if a
side label S has P(S=+)=p and the sampled conditional mean is L on + and R
on -, zero unconditional mean gives pL+(1-p)R=0, not L+R=0 unless p=1/2.
For g=0.6 and p=1/3, L=0.4 and R=-0.2 satisfy zero mean and the same gap
while remaining asymmetric. This is an algebraic control, not a model
counterexample.

Earliest remaining gate and falsifier
====================================
The earliest square-specific gate for a fixed pair is a uniform common
reference lower bound for the full signed pair of local conditional readings,
with all turn-forced negative constraints included and one constant surviving
all scale buffers. The earliest extra gate for a centered pair is symmetry of
that reference comparison under J (or a proved paired-resolution profile
limit). The exact finite actual-Gamma mean ranges do not settle either gate.

A decisive positive result would prove the all-event comparison above for
(Gamma_R, reversed walk record) under the finite-volume square tilted/uniform
law and then verify the two-sided locality approximation. A decisive negative
result would exhibit cleared external records for which the overlap of the
signed-vector laws with any common reference tends to zero, or a matched
paired-rule sequence whose signed trace pairs stay separated. A same-path
finite reflection check alone is not such a counterexample.

Evidence and cost
=================
The vector lemma is proved here in full. Primary-source checks were direct
reads of the arXiv HTML at the theorem/lemma locations listed above. No
simulation, scaling computation, proof assistant, external expert review, or
new square event-level comparison was performed. The square application and
centering criterion are exact conditional reductions, not claimed theorems
for the model.
