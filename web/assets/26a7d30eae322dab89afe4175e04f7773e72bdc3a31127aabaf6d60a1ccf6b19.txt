CYCLE 4 LEAF REPORT — SPARSE SUPPORT-SWITCH DRIFT AND ITS BOUNDARY
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the all-state sparse drift theorem below; finite rational checks are FINITE-EVIDENCE. The broader network is an exact counterexample to this Lyapunov template, not to stochastic recurrence.

RESULT
======
For an arbitrary finite bipartite catalyst-substrate graph E, the paired-edge family in Section 1 is nonexplosive and positive recurrent on every closed irreducible class, for every fixed strictly positive finite rate vector. More strongly, on any compact positive rate box with rational endpoints, one common support-switch geometric function gives an explicit inequality LV <= C-cV with rational R,B,A*,c,C. This controls mixed large catalyst/substrate escapes. Isolated substrates are fixed class labels; isolated catalysts remain ordinary immigration-death coordinates.

The support-switch argument does not extend unchanged to general weakly reversible networks with cross-conversion/catalyst cycles. Section 7 gives a four-species complex-balanced example with one closed irreducible class, an exact product-Poisson stationary law, and physical-time nonexplosion, but the proposed support-switch V has LV(x_n) -> +infinity. The obstruction is a multi-edge catalyst-deactivation cycle whose high-rate transition switches two substrate supports at once and has no direct reverse jump to cancel it at that state. Thus this is a failure of the chosen Foster template, not transient/null recurrence, explosion, or failure of the sparse theorem.

1. Exact sparse family and classes
==================================
Let J and I be finite catalyst and substrate sets, and let E subset J x I be any finite bipartite edge set. Counts are a_j for catalyst A_j and b_i for substrate B_i. Every propensity below is stochastic mass action, with falling factorials (n)_m=n(n-1)...(n-m+1), zero for n<m. For every j include

    0 -> A_j              alpha_j
    A_j -> 0              delta_j a_j

For every (j,i) in E include the four directed reactions

    A_j -> A_j+B_i                         p_ji a_j
    A_j+B_i -> A_j                         q_ji a_j b_i
    A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i             u_ji a_j b_i
    (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i -> A_j+B_i             v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i,

where k_ji is a fixed positive integer and every displayed rate constant is strictly positive and finite. Every edge is paired with its reverse, so the network is weakly reversible. There are no reactions on an isolated substrate i with N(i)=empty; its B_i count is a conserved class label.

Fix all isolated-substrate labels. The remaining coordinates form one irreducible closed class. From any state, for each nonisolated B_i choose a neighbor j, immigrate A_j if needed, and repeatedly fire A_j+B_i -> A_j until B_i=0; then drain all catalysts. This reaches the zero representative of the fixed-label slice. Conversely, from zero, produce each desired B_i using a temporary neighbor catalyst and A_j -> A_j+B_i, then adjust catalyst counts by immigration/death. Every finite path has positive probability. These paths use only reactions in the family, so extra edges in E do not obstruct them. Isolated catalysts have no incident edges but their independent immigration/death reactions connect all their counts. Distinct isolated-substrate label vectors are distinct closed classes.

If E is empty, every substrate count is a class label and the catalyst coordinates are independent immigration-death chains; they are nonexplosive and positive recurrent. The theorem below treats E nonempty.

2. Function and exact generator
================================
Write I_+={i:N(i) is nonempty}, h_i(a)=1_{sum_{j in N(i)}a_j=0}, I_0=1_{sum_j a_j=0}, and I_j=1_{a=e_j} (the latter means exactly one catalyst molecule in the whole system, at j). For i in I_+, let

    V(a,b) = sum_j a_j + sum_{i in I_+} R^(b_i)(1+h_i(a)) + K I_0.

No isolated-substrate term is included. On each fixed-label class V is positive and norm-like: bounded V forces all variable catalyst counts and all nonisolated substrate counts to be bounded.

Put eta=R-1 and d=1-R^(-1). Direct reaction-by-reaction evaluation gives

 LV = H - sum_j delta_j a_j
      + sum_(j,i) in E [
          p_ji eta a_j R^(b_i) - q_ji d a_j b_i R^(b_i)
          + k_ji u_ji a_j b_i - k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i ]
      - K alpha_sum I_0 + K sum_j delta_j I_j
      + sum_(i in I_+) R^(b_i) [
          -alpha(N(i)) h_i(a)
          + sum_(j in N(i)) delta_j 1_{a restricted to N(i)=e_j} ].       (1)

Here H=sum_j alpha_j, alpha_sum=sum_j alpha_j, and alpha(N(i))=sum_{j in N(i)}alpha_j. In the final indicator, counts of catalysts outside N(i) are unrestricted. Immigration of a neighbor switches h_i from one to zero and contributes the negative term. Death of the last molecule among the neighbors contributes the positive term; the indicator is exactly that a restricted neighborhood vector equals e_j. The reverse autocatalytic reaction leaves one A_j and does not switch h_i.

3. One compact positive rate box and explicit constants
======================================================
Fix a graph E and the integers k_ji. Suppose all rate constants range in one compact box with positive lower bounds and finite upper bounds

    alpha_j in [alpha_-,alpha_+], delta_j in [delta_-,delta_+],
    p_ji in [p_-,p_+], q_ji in [q_-,q_+],
    u_ji in [u_-,u_+], v_ji in [v_-,v_+],

where every lower endpoint is positive. Let r=|J|, m=|I_+|, d_A=max(1,max_j |N_j|), k_max=max_(j,i) k_ji. For rational box endpoints, the following choices and thresholds are exact rational/integer computations:

    eta = delta_-/(4 d_A p_+),  R=1+eta,  d=1-R^(-1),
    K = (r alpha_+ + 1)/(r alpha_-),
    gamma=delta_-/2,  c_A=delta_-/4.

For any j, chi_j=delta_j-sum_(i:j~i) p_ji eta >= 3 delta_-/4 > gamma. Thus the all-zero-neighbor linear margin is at least gamma. Also H-K alpha_sum <= -1 at A=0, before the additional nonpositive substrate immigration switches.

Define the edge envelope

    G(t)=p_+ eta R^t - q_- d t R^t + k_max u_+ t.

Choose B as the smallest integer B>=1 satisfying all three exact inequalities

    p_+ eta <= q_- d B/4,
    k_max u_+ <= q_- d R^B/4,
    delta_+ <= q_- d B/4.                                      (2)

Such a finite B exists since q_-d>0 and R>1. For all integer t>=B, (2) implies

    G(t) <= -(q_- d/2)t R^t.                                    (3)

Set

    M=max(0,max_{0<=t<B} G(t)),  L=d_A M.

Choose A_* as the smallest integer at least max(2,k_max+1) such that

    v_- A_*(A_*-1) >= (L+c_A) A_* .                              (4)

This exists because v_->0. Define C_A=(L+c_A)A_*, beta=min(alpha_-,q_-dB/4), and c=min(c_A,beta/2). Finally put

    C_0 = r alpha_+ + K delta_+ + r C_A + m delta_+ R^(B-1),
    C = C_0 + c(2m R^(B-1)+K).                                   (5)

Then on every fixed isolated-label class, and uniformly for all rates in the box,

    LV(a,b) <= C - c V(a,b).                                    (6)

The constants can be very conservative. They are explicit, common to the entire box, and do not require a small-gain LP. If box endpoints are not supplied as rationals, the same construction is valid as a real-parameter existence statement; rational effectivity is asserted here only for rational endpoint input.

4. Drift proof, including mixed A/B escapes
===========================================
Split edge terms into high-substrate blocks b_i>=B and low edges b_i<B. For every high b_i, omit the nonpositive reverse-autocatalytic terms. The remaining edge contribution is bounded by

    sum_(j in N(i)) a_j G(b_i) <= -(q_-d/2)b_i R^(b_i) sum_(j in N(i))a_j.

If N(i) is inactive, all these edge terms vanish and immigration switches the support correction down at rate alpha(N(i)); this gives at most -alpha_- R^(b_i). If N(i) is active, the only positive support-switch term can occur when the neighborhood contains exactly one molecule. Its size is at most delta_+ R^(b_i), and (2) bounds this by (q_-d/4)b_i R^(b_i). Hence, including both cases, the full high-substrate edge-plus-switch block is at most -beta R^(b_i). This estimate is uniform even when catalyst counts also diverge: for active neighborhoods the sink has the factor sum a_j, and a positive switch is possible only at local count one.

For a catalyst j, group its -delta_j a_j term with only its low-edge terms; all high-edge blocks have already been bounded above by nonpositive quantities. If every low neighbor has b_i=0, the catalyst coefficient is at most

    -delta_- + d_A p_+ eta <= -gamma.

If at least one low neighbor has b_i>=1, each low edge's non-factorial coefficient is at most M a_j, so their sum is at most L a_j. On one positive low neighbor, the reverse autocatalytic term is at most -v_-(a_j)_(k_ji+1), which for a_j>=k_max+1 is at most -v_-(a_j)_2. By (4), the resulting catalyst block is at most -c_A a_j for a_j>=A_*. For a_j<A_*, dropping all negative terms bounds the block by L a_j. These two cases give the single all-state estimate

    low catalyst block for j <= C_A - c_A a_j.

This argument handles mixed escapes: high substrates are controlled by their own blocks even if catalysts are large, while every catalyst is either controlled by the zero-substrate linear margin or by a low positive-substrate factorial sink. There is no sequence with both A and B diverging that falls between these cases.

At low substrates the positive last-neighbor switch is at most delta_+R^(B-1) per substrate; negative immigration switches can be discarded. The positive K-boundary term is at most K delta_+. Also H<=r alpha_+. Summing the preceding bounds yields

    LV <= C_0 - c_A sum_j a_j - beta sum_(i:b_i>=B} R^(b_i).

For low i, R^(b_i)(1+h_i)<=2R^(B-1); for high i it is at most 2R^(b_i). Thus

    V <= sum_j a_j + 2 sum_(i:b_i>=B} R^(b_i) + 2mR^(B-1)+K,

and (5) gives (6).

5. Nonexplosion, recurrence, and uniform tightness
=================================================
The rate box constants give a common finite upper bound on LV, and each fixed-label class has finite V-sublevel sets. For tau_R=inf{t:V(X_t)>=R}, localized physical-time Dynkin gives

    E_x V(X_(t wedge tau_R)) <= V(x)+Ct,
    P_x(tau_R<=t) <= (V(x)+Ct)/R.

Localization is legitimate because a finite V-sublevel set has finitely many states and every state has finitely many finite-rate outgoing transitions. The one-jump boundary is also finite. Infinite jumps cannot occur while the chain remains in a finite set, since total rates there are bounded. Since V is norm-like on the class, an explosive path would have to leave every such finite set in finite time; the displayed bound rules this out as R tends to infinity. This proves nonexplosion in physical time.

For V>(C+1)/c, (6) gives LV<=-1. The sublevel set is finite, so the continuous-time Foster criterion yields positive recurrence and a unique stationary law on each irreducible class. This conclusion is per fixed rate vector and closed class.

Because V,C,c are common over the compact box, the stationary V-moments have the common bound int V d(pi_theta)<=C/c. Therefore the stationary family is uniformly tight on any one fixed isolated-label class: {V<=M} is a common finite set and pi_theta(V>M)<=C/(cM). If isolated-substrate labels vary over unbounded values, the union of those classes is not uniformly tight in the full state space; those coordinates are invariant labels and were deliberately omitted from V. Nonexplosion, recurrence of each component, and uniform tightness over a rate box are distinct conclusions; here the last is proved only within a fixed label slice.

7. Boundary of the support-switch method: an exact WR example
============================================================
Use species A1,A2,B1,B2, all rate constants equal to one, and the following directed reactions (each arrow has the standard stochastic mass-action propensity from its source complex):

    0 <-> A1,                         0 <-> A2,
    A_j <-> A_j+B_i,                  A_j+B_i <-> 2A_j+B_i   (j,i=1,2),
    A1+B1 <-> A2+B2,
    B1+B2 -> A1+B1+B2
          -> A1+A2+B1+B2
          -> B1+B2.

The first two families contribute 4+8+8 directed channels, the cross-conversion pair contributes 2, and the three-complex cycle contributes 3, for 25 directed channels. All linkage classes are weakly reversible: the pairs are reversible and the last three complexes form a directed cycle. At c*=(1,1,1,1), every directed edge has unit complex flux; each reversible pair balances by itself and the three-cycle has one unit entering and leaving each complex. Hence c* is complex balanced.

The full lattice N_0^4 is one closed irreducible class. The subnetwork 0<->A_j and A_j<->A_j+B_i already gives paths from every state to zero and from zero to every target, using temporary catalysts as in Section 1; the added cross-conversion and cycle do not remove transitions. The complex-balanced product-form law is

    pi(a1,a2,b1,b2) = exp(-4)/(a1! a2! b1! b2!).

It is the unique stationary probability on this irreducible class. The mass-action CTMC is nonexplosive in physical time by the theorem of Anderson, Cappelletti, Koyama, and Kurtz cited below. Its stationary mean total jump intensity is exactly 25: under independent Poisson(1) coordinates every unit-rate falling-factorial monomial has expectation one, and there are 25 directed channels. This finite stationary intensity is a check, not a substitute for the all-initial-state nonexplosion theorem; reaction rates remain unbounded over the state space.

Test precisely the sparse ansatz with lambda_j=epsilon_i=1, R=6/5, K=2, h_i=1_{a1+a2=0}:

    V(a,b)=a1+a2 + R^(b1)(1+h_1)+R^(b2)(1+h_2) + 2 1_{a1+a2=0}.

At x_n=(1,1,n,n), n>=1, exact generator contributions are:

* catalyst immigration/death: 0 in total;
* the four substrate pairs: 4(R-1)R^n - 4n(1-R^(-1))R^n;
* the four autocatalytic forward channels: 4n, with their reverse channels disabled at a1=a2=1;
* the cross-conversion pair: 2n R^n(R+R^(-1)-2), since both directions from equal substrate counts move one count down and one up;
* the three-cycle: 2n^2+2n^2R^n. The first two cycle edges each add one catalyst at rate n^2. The last edge removes both catalysts and switches both h_i from zero to one; its V jump is -2+2R^n+K=2R^n, at rate n^2.

Combining terms gives the exact identity

    LV(x_n)=R^n(2n^2-3n/5+4/5)+2n^2+4n,                           (7)

which is positive and tends to infinity. Thus this V cannot give a finite-set negative drift or a geometric Foster bound for this broader network. The failure comes from a support-creating catalyst-deactivation step with rate n^2 and gain 2R^n; its reverse path consists of two different transitions, so there is no opposite jump at the same state to cancel it. The cross-conversion pair is included to test a non-star substrate/catalyst conversion cycle as well.

A direct reversible pair A1+A2+B1+B2 <-> B1+B2 would be a bad witness at equal forward and reverse constants: at x_n the opposite jumps have equal rate n^2 and opposite V increments, so their generator terms cancel exactly. The 3-complex cycle above repairs that cancellation. Even for the repaired example, (7) falsifies only this particular support-switch Lyapunov template. The CTMC is complex balanced, nonexplosive, and positive recurrent with an explicit stationary law. No transient, null-recurrent, explosive, or non-tight stationary family is claimed.
