STRONGEST CHECKPOINT CLAIM
For a smooth, nowhere-zero, divergence-free autonomous vector field X on a closed Riemannian 3-manifold (M,g), with normalized Riemannian volume μ, flow φ_t, natural-log metric entropy H=h_μ(φ_1), and S=Def X=(∇X+(∇X)^*)/2, the following continuum inequality is supported by a complete short derivation from the normal quotient cocycle and Ruelle's theorem:

    H^2 ≤ (1/2) ∫_M |S|^2 dμ.

For the explicitly chosen constitutive equation

    ∇_X X − 2ν div S + grad p = F,    ν>0,

its stationary power W=∫_M <F,X> dVol therefore obeys

    W = 2ν ∫_M |S|^2 dVol ≥ 4ν Vol(M) H^2.

This is a continuum steady-flow entropy-to-sustaining-power bound. It takes H and the physical time unit as supplied. It does not yet account for measuring/acquiring H, representing X, constructing a forcing device, numerical discretization, or any computation needed to realize the flow. No field-breaking or priority claim is made.

RECONSTRUCTED DERIVATION AND CONSTANT AUDIT
Set r=|X|>0, e=X/r, and N=e^⊥. In an orthonormal frame (e,n_1,n_2), write the symmetric trace-free strain as

    S = [[q,b_1,b_2], [b_1,-q/2+s,0], [b_2,0,-q/2-s]],

where q=<Se,e>=X(log r), b=PSe, and ±s are the eigenvalues of the traceless normal block. Hence exactly

    |S|^2 = 2s^2 + (3/2)q^2 + 2|b|^2,
    s^2 ≤ |S|^2/2.

For v=Dφ_t v_0, let w=P_{φ_t x}v. The projected derivative Q_t=P_{φ_t x}Dφ_t|_{N_x}:N_x→N_{φ_t x} is the invertible quotient cocycle induced by Dφ_t modulo the invariant X-line. Torsion-freeness and Dφ_t X(x)=X(φ_t x) give P∇_Xw=P∇_wX; thus there is no extra moving-frame term. The symmetric normal block has largest eigenvalue s−q/2, so

    d/dt log|w| ≤ s(φ_t x) − (1/2)q(φ_t x),
    log||Q_t(x)|| ≤ ∫_0^t s(φ_τx)dτ − (1/2)log(r(φ_tx)/r(x)).

Because X is nowhere zero on compact M, r is bounded above and below; the endpoint term divided by t vanishes. The X-line exponent is 0. Volume preservation gives |det Q_t|=r(x)/r(φ_tx), so the two normal exponents sum to 0 and are χ(x),−χ(x), χ≥0. Oseledets plus Birkhoff applied to the bounded observable s_1(x)=∫_0^1s(φ_τx)dτ yield ∫χdμ≤∫s dμ. Ruelle's inequality for the C^1 compact-manifold map φ_1 and invariant probability μ gives H≤∫χdμ. Then

    H^2 ≤ (∫s dμ)^2 ≤ ∫s^2dμ ≤ (1/2)∫|S|^2dμ.

For power, the advection term integrates to ∫X(|X|²/2)=0, pressure to −∫p divX=0, and closed-manifold integration by parts gives ∫<2 divS,X>=−2∫<S,∇X>=−2∫|S|². Therefore W=2ν∫|S|² with the stated signs and factor, and W≥4νVH². Changing the diffusion operator or its normalization changes this power identity and cannot be silently treated as the same physical model.

PRIMARY-SOURCE AUDIT / EXACT SCOPE
1. David Ruelle, “An inequality for the entropy of differentiable maps,” Bol. Soc. Bras. Mat. 9 (1978), 83–87, primary PDF: https://www.ihes.fr/~ruelle/PUBLICATIONS/%5B51%5D.pdf. Theorem 2(b) assumes a C^1 self-map of a compact C^∞ manifold and any invariant probability measure; it states h(μ)≤∫λ_+(x)dμ, with λ_+ the multiplicity-weighted sum of positive Lyapunov exponents. The theorem does not require ergodicity. This is precisely the imported entropy step; it is not a strain or fluid-power theorem.
2. Ya. B. Pesin, “Characteristic Lyapunov exponents and smooth ergodic theory,” Russian Math. Surveys 32:4 (1977), 55–114, primary English PDF: https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=rm&option_lang=eng&paperid=3219&what=fullteng. The introduction specifies invariant normalized measure equivalent to Riemannian volume; Theorem 5.1 states the entropy formula for a C^2 measure-preserving diffeomorphism in that framework. That equality is relevant to a proposed geodesic-flow equality example, not needed for the lower bound. It does not license applying Pesin equality to a zero-set restriction without checking measure equivalence; Ruelle is the safe inequality there.
3. Elia Brué, Maria Colombo, Carl Johan Peter Johansson, “Lyapunov exponents, entropy and mixing for DiPerna–Lions flows,” arXiv:2510.02921v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02921v1. Theorem 1.2 / 5.2 establishes Ruelle's inequality for a 1-periodic incompressible regular Lagrangian flow on T^d generated by b∈L^1_loc(R_+,W^{1,p}), p>1; Corollary 5.3 gives h_Ld(X_1)≤C(d)∫_0^1∫|∇b|. Its proof displays the dimension factor d through Σλ_i^+≤d λ_max and the time-averaged full-gradient bound. This is a close prior bound with broader temporal/regularity scope, but uses full gradient/L^1 control and a dimension-dependent constant; it does not establish the steady 3D sharp symmetric-strain L^2/power coefficient above.
4. Ankan Biswas, Amal Manoharan, Ashwin Joy, “Topological entropy of stationary three-dimensional turbulence,” arXiv:2506.20973v3 (version date shown 11 Mar 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20973v3. The authors define their S as asymptotic material-curve length growth, S=(1/T)log(l_T/l_0); derive local stretching factors from the symmetric velocity-gradient eigenvalues; and then make a stochastic/log-normal CLT step, use an eigenvalue decorrelation time plus stationary/ergodic sampling to replace Lagrangian averages with Eulerian statistics. Their reported comparison is a numerical 3D forced-Navier–Stokes simulation with relative error below 20% over two decades of Re (source-reported, not independently rerun here). This is important adjacent hydrodynamic prior art sharing the strain observable, but the named “topological entropy” is material-line complexity, not the invariant-volume KS entropy in the theorem above; the result is statistical/computational, not a rigorous universal entropy-versus-dissipation inequality. The article is an arXiv preprint; publication/priority status is not cleared here.
5. Samavaki–Tuomela, “Navier–Stokes equations on Riemannian manifolds,” arXiv:1812.09015v2, https://arxiv.org/html/1812.09015v2. They explicitly define an unhalved stress S_u=∇u+(∇u)^* and L u=div(S_u), and discuss why deformation-rate diffusion differs from Bochner/Hodge Laplacians on curved manifolds. Thus the candidate's −2ν div Def convention corresponds to −ν L in their notation; the factor of two is a convention match, not a new dissipative identity. A curvature-containing operator must be compared separately.
6. Hoeijmakers et al., “Control of mixing via entropy tracking,” Phys. Rev. E 81, 066302 (2010), APS primary abstract https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.81.066302. Their “global hydrodynamic entropy” and viscous dissipation coupling in a controlled vortex-array model are thermodynamic/stirring quantities, not KS entropy of φ_1. Only the primary abstract was accessible in this audit; no full-text derivation is claimed.
7. Manoharan–Subramanian–Joy, “Topological Entropy of Two Dimensional Turbulence,” arXiv:2412.08996v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08996v2, is 2D statistical material-line/strain prior art; it does not make the 3D volume-KS/power assertion above.

BOUNDARIES, COSTS, AND STATUS
The derivation is for a smooth autonomous divergence-free field on a closed 3-manifold, nowhere-zero X, normalized invariant Riemannian volume, natural-log entropy of the time-one map, and the specific symmetric-strain constitutive model. If the physical period is T, the rate is h(φ_T)/T. The proof is kinematic plus Ruelle and a continuum power balance; it does not establish viscosity-independent dynamics, forcing acquisition cost, actuator complexity, measurement cost, or a digitized algorithmic lower bound. It does not cover boundaries, rough/non-autonomous fields, dimension other than three, Hodge/Bochner diffusion, or near-equality stability. The lower bound is structurally credible within its interface; exact sharpness/rigidity and historical priority need separate review. The 3D turbulence source is especially relevant for terminology and prior art, but its assumptions and observable differ materially.
