Cycle 4 root audit: uniform V-geometric ergodicity on a compact rate box
=======================================================================

Claim status
------------
INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE conditional on the compact-box geometric drift
LV_theta<=C-cV established in cycle3/leaves/geometric_rate_box.txt. This
audit proves that a common finite-state minorization is available and that
the usual drift/minorization theorem can be applied with common parameters.
It does not claim a practical mixing-time bound; the constants may be very
small/large.

Theorem statement
-----------------
Fix the complete-pair network structure, finite r,s and k_ji, and a compact
box Theta whose rate intervals are bounded away from zero and infinity.
Assume the common geometric drift proved in the cycle-3 rate-box report:

  L_theta V <= C-cV,       V>=1,       theta in Theta.

Then there are M<infinity and rho<1, independent of theta, such that for
all x and n>=0,

  ||P_{nT}^theta(x,.)-pi_theta||_V <= M V(x) rho^n.              (1)

Here T can be any fixed positive skeleton interval, pi_theta is the unique
invariant probability, and ||mu||_V=sup_{|f|<=V}|mu(f)|. In continuous
physical time this yields constants M',gamma>0, independent of theta, with

  ||P_t^theta(x,.)-pi_theta||_V <= M' V(x) exp(-gamma t).        (2)

The V(x) multiplier remains: this is not a total-variation mixing bound
uniform over all starting states.

Common skeleton drift
---------------------
Take T=1 (any fixed T>0 works). From the common generator inequality,
localized Dynkin followed by Gronwall gives

  P_T^theta V <= a V+B,
  a=exp(-cT)<1, B=(C/c)(1-exp(-cT)),                           (3)

for every theta. Let lambda=(1+a)/2 and choose a finite common set

  F={x:V(x)<=max(V(0), B/(lambda-a))}.

Since V is norm-like on N_0^(r+s), F is finite. For x outside F,
B<=(lambda-a)V(x), so P_TV<=lambda V. For x in F, (3) gives
P_TV<=lambda V+B. Thus

  P_T^theta V <= lambda V+B 1_F,                              (4)

uniformly in theta.

Uniform minorization from explicit clearing paths
-------------------------------------------------
For every x=(a,b) in F choose the following reaction-channel path to 0:

1. If a=0, use 0->A_1 once.
2. For each i, apply A_1+B_i->A_1 exactly b_i times.
3. Apply A_j->0 exactly the current a_j times for every j.

In step 1, if needed, catalyst A_1 becomes active. It remains active while
all substrates are cleared. The final deaths remove every catalyst. This is
a finite path for each finite x; positivity of every rate ensures every
specified channel is enabled. The finite union of these path states has a
common upper bound Q_* on total exit rates over theta in the compact box.
Every specified channel along the finite paths has a common positive lower
rate r_l^- over the box.

For a path x=x_0->...->x_n=0, take disjoint time intervals

  I_l=[l Delta,l Delta+Delta/2], Delta=T/(n+1), l=1,...,n.

Require the lth specified jump to occur in I_l and no other jump before
time T. The joint jump-time density is bounded below by

  exp(-Q_* T) product_l r_l^-,

because the total holding time along the prescribed path is at most T and
all total exit rates on the path and at 0 are <=Q_*. Integrating over the
ordered intervals, each of length Delta/2, gives

  P_T^theta(x,{0}) >= eta_x
    := exp(-Q_*T) (Delta/2)^n product_l r_l^- >0.               (5)

For x=0, the no-jump event gives eta_0=exp(-Q_*T)>0. Since F is finite,
eta=min(1/2,min_{x in F}eta_x)>0. Therefore

  P_T^theta(x,.) >= eta delta_0(.),   x in F, theta in Theta.  (6)

This is one common one-step small-set minorization for the entire rate
family. No rate lower bound is required outside the finite path states;
compact positive rate bounds and finite F are exactly what make (5)
uniform.

Irreducibility and aperiodicity
-------------------------------
For each theta and any pair of states x,y, the reaction graph provides a
finite path with positive channel rates. Requiring those jumps in ordered
subintervals of (0,T) and no further jumps gives P_T^theta(x,y)>0. Hence
the skeleton is irreducible. Also

  P_T^theta(0,0)>=exp(-alpha_sum^+ T)>0,

by no jump from the all-zero state. The skeleton is aperiodic, with a
common positive self-loop bound at 0.

Uniformity of the ergodicity constants
--------------------------------------
Equations (4) and (6) have the same lambda, B, finite F and eta for every
theta; the minorizing law is the same delta_0, whose V moment V(0) is
finite and fixed. The common split-chain/regeneration proof of the
discrete-time drift/minorization theorem therefore gives common constants
M and rho in (1): its return-time and regeneration estimates use only
lambda, B, sup_F V, eta, and V(0), all common here. The finite set has a
common transition lower bound, not merely a separate smallness constant
for each theta.

For 0<=s<=T, the generator drift also gives
  P_s^theta V <= exp(-cs)V+(C/c)(1-exp(-cs)) <=(1+C/c)V.
The weighted-measure norm is consequently multiplied by at most
K=1+C/c under P_s. Writing t=nT+s in (1) proves (2), with
gamma=-log(rho)/T and M'=KM/rho.

The drift/minorization implication is the standard V-uniform ergodicity
criterion for a discrete-time skeleton. Source anchors for exact theorem
auditing:

* Meyn and Tweedie, *Markov Chains and Stochastic Stability*, 1993,
  Chapter 16 (V-uniform ergodicity), official chapter PDF:
  https://probability.ca/MT/Chap16.pdf
* Down, Meyn, and Tweedie, “Exponential and Uniform Ergodicity of Markov
  Processes,” Annals of Probability 23(4), 1671–1691 (1995),
  DOI 10.1214/aop/1176987798. The paper treats continuous-time geometric
  convergence and the connections between extended-generator drift and
  skeleton/resolvent chains.

The finite minorization proof above is self-contained; source review of the
exact theorem numbering/assumptions is also assigned to the Cycle-4 primary
source leaf before synthesis.

Continuous-time extension caveat
-------------------------------
The conclusion is parameter-uniform V-geometric convergence in physical
time, with a V(x) prefactor. It does not say convergence is uniform over
the unbounded state space in ordinary total variation. Constants depend
on the compact rate box, network structure, drift weights, finite F, and
chosen clearing paths; they are not optimized. If any required rate lower
endpoint approaches zero, the common path rate r_l^- can vanish and this
argument provides no uniform rate.

Finite rational illustration
-----------------------------
The checker `check_minorization.py` uses r=1,s=2,k=1 and the compact box
alpha,delta,p,u in [1/2,2], q,v in [1,3]. For the finite set
F0={(a,b1,b2): a,b1,b2 in {0,1}}, it constructs explicit paths, bounds
the largest total exit rate over all path states, and computes a rational
lower bound. It takes T=1/100, so Q_*T=9/50<1, and uses
exp(-x)>=1-x to lower-bound the no-extra-jump probability by 41/50. This
is a finite example of the construction, not a proof of the family theorem
and not a numerical mixing-time benchmark.
