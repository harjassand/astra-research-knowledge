SHARP VISCOUS WORK BOUND FOR STEADY THREE-DIMENSIONAL TRANSPORT
7 October 2026. Internally derived candidate; external review and priority open.

Theorem.
Let (M,g) be a smooth closed Riemannian three-manifold, V=Vol_g(M),
and X a C-infinity, nowhere-zero, divergence-free vector field. Let
phi_t be its flow, mu=dVol_g/V, and H=h_mu(phi_1), using natural logs.
Let S=Def X=(nabla X+(nabla X)^*)/2. Then

                         integral_M |S|^2 dVol >= 2 V H^2.       (1)

Consequently, for every nu>0 and every smooth force F and pressure p
which sustain X as a stationary solution of strain-viscosity Navier--Stokes,

             nabla_X X - 2 nu div(Def X) + grad p = F,

the integrated power per unit density satisfies

               W=integral_M g(F,X) dVol
                =2 nu integral_M |S|^2 dVol >= 4 nu V H^2.     (2)

The universal constants 2 and 4 are sharp when the manifold and metric
are allowed to vary. An explicit Sol mapping-torus example attains both.
Neither ergodicity, harmonicity, constant speed nor any computation
assumption is required. A lower bound on speed is automatic from compactness
and the stated nowhere-zero premise. No conclusion for flows with zeros is
adopted here. H is the entropy of spatial transport under normalized volume,
not the entropy of a velocity-field evolution or a thermodynamic entropy.

Proof of (1).
Put r=|X|, e=X/r, and P=I-e tensor e. Define

          q=g(S e,e)=X(log r),
          B=P S P restricted to e-perp,
          b=P S e.

The equality for q follows from g(nabla_e X,e)=e(r). Since div X=0,
tr B=-q. Write B in any orthonormal frame of e-perp as

                    [ u-q/2       v   ]
                    [   v       -u-q/2].

Let s=(u^2+v^2)^(1/2)>=0. This definition does not depend on the
chosen normal frame: its eigenvalues are -q/2 plus/minus s. Direct
orthogonal block decomposition gives

                   |S|^2=2s^2+(3/2)q^2+2|b|^2.               (3)

Consider the linear normal cocycle

 Q_t(x)=P_(phi_t x) Dphi_t(x) restricted to e(x)-perp.

It is a cocycle because Dphi_t maps the line spanned by X to the line
spanned by X. If v(t)=Dphi_t v_0 and w(t)=P v(t), write v=w+a e.
The variation equation is nabla_X v=nabla_v X. Since

          nabla_X e = nabla_e X - e X(log r),

projection of the differentiated identity v=w+a e yields

                       P nabla_X w=P nabla_w X.

Therefore, for w nonzero,

       d/dt log |w| = g(Sw,w)/|w|^2 <= s-q/2.

Integration gives

 log ||Q_t(x)|| <= integral_0^t s(phi_tau x) d tau
                         -(1/2)log[r(phi_t x)/r(x)].         (4)

The last term is uniformly bounded. The line spanned by X has Lyapunov
exponent zero, because Dphi_t X(x)=X(phi_t x) and r is bounded above
and below. Volume preservation makes the sum of all three exponents
zero. Equivalently, the normal determinant is r(x)/r(phi_t x), whose
exponential rate is zero. Thus the two normal Lyapunov exponents are
chi(x),-chi(x), with chi>=0. The Lyapunov spectra of the invariant line
and normal quotient give the full spectrum: chi,0,-chi.

Oseledets and Birkhoff apply: phi_1 is a smooth diffeomorphism of compact
M, all derivative norms and inverses are bounded, and mu is invariant.
By (4), chi(x)<=the time average of s along x for mu-a.e. x. Integrating
(or applying Birkhoff to integral_0^1 s(phi_tau x)d tau) gives

                        integral chi dmu <= integral s dmu.

Ruelle's inequality for the C1 map phi_1 and invariant probability mu
then gives H<=integral chi dmu<=integral s dmu. Cauchy--Schwarz and (3)
prove H^2<=integral s^2 dmu<=integral |S|^2 dmu/2, which is (1).

The imported result is Ruelle 1978, Theorem 2(b): the metric entropy of
every invariant probability measure of a C1 map of a compact smooth
manifold is bounded by the integral of positive Lyapunov exponents,
including multiplicities. The original five-page proof was retrieved
and read. It obtains the bound by fine chart partitions and the norm
of the full exterior derivative cocycle, followed by the Oseledets
limit. We import this established theorem; the normal-cocycle refinement
and strain decomposition above are the local derivation.

Proof of (2).
The advection and pressure terms have zero integrated pairing with X,
since M is closed and div X=0. Integration by parts gives

              integral g(2 div S,X)=-2 integral |S|^2.

The stationary momentum equation therefore yields the exact identity
in (2). Applying (1) proves its bound. Boundary work, open domains,
time-dependent velocity fields and non-Newtonian constitutive laws
have different interfaces and are not included.

A deficit certificate.
Let E_mu denote normalized-volume expectation and set

                        Delta=W/(4 nu V)-H^2 >=0.

The same proof, without dropping the last two terms of (3), gives

       Delta >= Var_mu(s)+(3/4)E_mu q^2+E_mu |b|^2.             (5)

Indeed W/(4nuV)=E_mu s^2+(3/4)E_mu q^2+E_mu |b|^2 and
H<=E_mu s. Thus near-optimal work forces nearly constant transverse
strain magnitude, small material log-speed derivative, and small cross
strain in this exact L2 sense. This is not global geometric rigidity or
a theorem that the flow is close to Sol geometry.

Sharp example and harmonic-viscosity separation.
Take A=[[2,1],[1,1]] in SL(2,Z), lambda=(3+sqrt(5))/2, and a=log lambda.
In a real eigenbasis of A use coordinates (x,y,t) on R^2 x R and the
metric

             g=e^(2at) dx^2+e^(-2at) dy^2+dt^2.

Quotient by the lattice of the torus in the eigenbasis and by the map
(v,t)->(Av,t-1). The metric is invariant under this map; the resulting
closed manifold is the mapping torus of A. Its volume density is constant.
The vector field X=partial_t descends, is unit speed and divergence-free.
The one-form X-flat=dt is closed and co-closed, hence Hodge harmonic.
Its time-one return map on every torus fiber is A, and normalized volume
disintegrates as Haar measure on fibers times Lebesgue measure in t.
Thus H=h_Haar(A)=log lambda=a. Here is a direct lower-entropy check,
so the exact value need not be accepted only as a quoted toral formula.
Choose a finite torus partition with atom diameter eta, where
(||A||+1)eta is smaller than the shortest nonzero lattice vector and
eta is smaller than the injectivity radius. For two points in the same
n-cylinder, their unique short lift differences delta_k for k=0,...,n-1
satisfy delta_(k+1)=A delta_k: the possible discrepancy is a lattice
vector of norm less than its shortest nonzero length. Consequently the
initial differences have stable width O(eta) and unstable width
O(eta lambda^(-(n-1))). Every cylinder has Haar probability at most
C_eta lambda^(-n). Its partition entropy is therefore at least
n log lambda-log C_eta. Taking n->infinity gives the lower bound a.
The matching upper bound follows from Ruelle's inequality and the
constant Lyapunov exponents a,-a. On the suspension, the measurable
fundamental domain [0,1) identifies the time-one map with (A,id).
Pull back the same torus partition to this domain; the same cylinder
probabilities give H>=a, while the three exponents a,0,-a give H<=a.
This proves the stated equality directly, using only the already
imported Ruelle theorem.

In the orthonormal frame,

     S=diag(a,-a,0), |S|^2=2a^2,
     Ric=diag(0,0,-2a^2), Ric(X)=-2a^2 X,
     nabla_X X=0,
     L X:=2 div Def X=-4a^2 X.

Therefore F=4nu a^2 X, p=0 sustain a stationary physical-strain solution
and W=4nu V a^2=4nu V H^2. The force is itself divergence-free.
The Hodge viscosity operator annihilates X, so the same field is an
unforced steady Hodge-viscosity solution. This exact example demonstrates
the constitutive difference without needing any undecidability result.
Sol geometry and this suspension are old constructions; the proposed
contribution is the sharp work inequality and deficit statement, with
their precise modern fluid-computation interface. Historical novelty
remains UNKNOWN until a specialist prior-art review.

More generally, on closed M, if X-flat is harmonic, then nabla X is
symmetric and div X=0. With the negative-spectrum rough Laplacian,
L X=(Delta_B+Ric)X=2 Ric(X), by Weitzenbock. Since nabla_X X is a
gradient, the force F=-2nu Ric(X), with pressure -|X|^2/2, sustains X.
Every pressure choice has the same positive work

         W=2nu ||nabla X||_2^2=-2nu integral Ric(X,X).

It vanishes iff X is parallel. Gradient forces cannot remove this work.
The minimum L2 force after pressure adjustment is -2nu P_Leray Ric(X).
The harmonic mechanism therefore transfers to strain viscosity by paying
an exact force, rather than by retaining unforced stationarity.

Falsification/limits.
1. General trace-free 3x3 strain alone gives coefficient 3 instead of 4;
   the autonomous neutral direction and bounded log-speed term are needed.
2. Replacing normalized volume entropy with topological entropy is not
   licensed: stretching may occur on zero-volume invariant sets.
3. Positive metric entropy does not imply universal computation; universal
   computation does not supply positive normalized-volume metric entropy.
4. The bound alone supplies no minimum cost per machine instruction,
   no finite-precision tolerance, and no unforced flat-domain compiler.
5. Scaling X to cX scales entropy by c and work by c^2; slow execution
   does not violate the inequality and defeats any speed-free power floor.
6. The closure (2) needs closed M. Walls and boundary controllers can
   supply power through additional boundary terms.
