# Collateral sensitivity under uncertain, evolving clone-by-drug fitness

**Status:** conditional model results reconstructed; empirical mechanism and therapeutic value **UNKNOWN**. The two-clone constructions below are exact algebra, not new cancer biology. The rare-clone depth formula is the standard binomial zero-detection bound combined with selection; it is not a novelty claim. The useful output is a decision rule for when unlinked drug-response marginals suffice and when lineage-linked response data can change a control decision.

## Scope and state interface

At observation time (t), let (n_{i,t}) be the count of lineage/state (i), (p_{i,t}=n_{i,t}/sum_k n_{k,t}), and let (R_t=(r_{ij,t})) be the clone-by-drug matrix of net growth rates at the tested concentrations. A control (u_t) specifies drug concentrations and duration; its integrated exposures are (x_{j,t}=int_t^{t+Delta}u_j(s),ds). A minimal evolving-state model is

\[
n_{t+\Delta}=M_t(u_t)\,\mathrm{diag}\!\left(\exp(R_t x_t)\right)n_t,
\]

where (M_t(u_t)) is a column-stochastic transition matrix for mutation or phenotypic switching. Both (R_t) and (M_t) may change with time and treatment. The fixed-matrix examples below set (M=I), no density feedback, and no drug-drug pharmacodynamic interaction. This is a submodel, so a guarantee that fails here cannot be recovered by allowing more uncertain transitions without adding assumptions.

A lineage-linked functional assay returns noisy entries (Y_{ij,t}=r_{ij,t}+\epsilon_{ij,t}), or a dose-response curve for each lineage and drug. An unlinked panel may return drug-wise marginal response distributions; it does not say which lineage had which response to each drug. If only aggregate viability is available, even those marginals are unknown and the uncertainty set must be wider. Barcode frequencies estimate (p_t), subject to cell-sampling, barcode collision, dropout, and spatial sampling errors. The controller may select another assay, but it pays culture wells/cells, sequencing molecules and reads, assay turnaround (\tau), and any exposure incurred while waiting. The therapy objective should include measured cumulative drug exposure/toxicity costs and a target burden or resistant-fraction endpoint; an in-vitro viability difference alone is not a clinical benefit.

## Exact paired-response ambiguity

Take two equally abundant lineages and two drugs. Rates are relative to a common untreated growth rate, which cancels from comparisons. The two possible matrices are

\[
R^{\mathrm{same}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\-a&-a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R^{\mathrm{split}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-a\\-a&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad p=(1/2,1/2).
\]

Each drug separately has the same two clone-level responses, \(\{0,-a\}\), in both worlds. Thus even the entire unlinked monotherapy viability curve at exposure (q\ge0) is identical:

\[
B_A(q)=B_B(q)=\tfrac12(1+e^{-aq}).
\]

Now spend one unit of total integrated exposure, (x_A+x_B=1), in a simultaneous two-drug treatment. The remaining burden is

\[
B_{\mathrm{same}}(x)=\tfrac12(1+e^{-a}),\qquad
B_{\mathrm{split}}(x)=\tfrac12\big(e^{-a x_A}+e^{-a(1-x_A)}\big).
\]

The second expression is minimized at (x_A=x_B=1/2), with burden (e^{-a/2}). The burden ratio between a monotherapy and the optimal split treatment in the complementary world is

\[
\frac{\tfrac12(1+e^{-a})}{e^{-a/2}}=\cosh(a/2).
\]

At (a=2), both monotherapy assays report survivor fraction \(0.5677\); the split treatment gives \(0.5677\) in the same-susceptibility world and \(0.3679\) in the complementary-susceptibility world. The difference is the pairing/covariance of the two drug responses across lineages, not either drug's marginal response distribution.

This is a decision ambiguity only when treatment costs can make the actions rank differently. With total-exposure-only cost and no combination-specific burden, the equal split weakly dominates in these two worlds: it ties monotherapy in the first and improves it in the second. Add a measured fixed combination overhead \(\lambda\) to the objective, \(J=\log B+\lambda\mathbf 1\{x_A,x_B>0\}\). Write \(g=\log\cosh(a/2)\). If (0<\lambda<g), monotherapy is preferred in the same-susceptibility world, while the equal split is preferred in the complementary world. For (a=2), (g\approx0.434). If \(\lambda=g/2\), any deterministic first-window policy using only these indistinguishable marginals has worst-world regret at least \(\lambda\): choosing one drug loses \(\lambda\) in the split world; using both pays at least \(\lambda\) in the same world. Randomization reduces expected worst-case regret to \(\lambda/2\) under the symmetric two-world comparison. This bound concerns the decision window before a new outcome assay returns; later adaptation can learn from realized responses, at extra burden and delay.

**Limit:** do not state this as a universal “cycling fails” theorem. It assumes additive log-growth effects during the interval and a combination cost that actually exists. Drug interactions add clone-specific terms and require combination assays; monotherapy marginals alone cannot estimate them. With no combination overhead in this example, the robust split is already safe. The ambiguity is relevant when doses, toxicity, or drug-specific costs are unequal, when candidate schedules differ, or when the robust envelope is too conservative.

## Equal-information control rule: robust over unknown response coupling

When drug-wise response marginals are measured but cross-drug lineage pairing is not, let (G_j=\exp(r_{ij}x_j)) be a lineage's survival multiplier under drug (j), and let (\mu_j) be the measured distribution of (G_j) over lineages. Define (\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_J)) as all joint distributions with those observed marginals. The worst-case burden compatible with the data is

\[
B_{\rm rob}(x)=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_J)}\mathbb E_\pi\!\left[\prod_{j=1}^J G_j\right].
\]

Then use the same-information robust objective

\[
x^*\in\arg\min_{x\in\mathcal X}\left\{\log B_{\rm rob}(x)+C_{\rm dose}(x)+C_{\rm combo}(x)\right\},
\]

with assay/turnaround costs charged separately in the value of an assay. For finite supports, (B_{\rm rob}) is a multi-marginal optimal-transport linear program: joint weights are nonnegative, preserve each measured marginal, and maximize the product objective. For two drugs the maximum is the same-rank (comonotone) pairing; in the example above it yields \(B_{\rm rob}(1/2,1/2)=\tfrac12(1+e^{-a})\). The robust controller therefore refuses to credit an unobserved complementary pairing.

This provides a precise criterion for whether clone-linked assays are needed for a choice. For proposed actions (x,y), let \(J_\pi\) include the burden and costs under coupling \(\pi\). If

\[
\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\big[J_\pi(y)-J_\pi(x)\big]>0,
\]

then (x) wins for every clone pairing compatible with the same marginal data; linkage is unnecessary for this decision. If the interval of action differences straddles zero, the marginal data do not identify the winner. A minimax controller can still act using (B_{\rm rob}); a lineage-linked assay is justified only if resolving the coupling could change the chosen action enough to pay for its extra cells, reads, culture conditions, and delay. Without a probability model over couplings, an expected value-of-information number is not identified and should not be invented.

For noisy matrix measurements, suppose each clone-drug estimate has independent sub-Gaussian error with variance proxy \(\sigma^2\). With (n\) replicates per entry, simultaneous intervals of half-width (\epsilon\) hold with probability at least (1-\delta) if

\[
n\ge \frac{2\sigma^2}{\epsilon^2}\log\frac{2KJ}{\delta}.
\]

If \(\|x\|_1\le B\) and all entry errors are at most \(\epsilon\), then

\[
\left|\log\sum_i p_i e^{R_i\cdot x}-\log\sum_i p_i e^{\widehat R_i\cdot x}\right|\le\epsilon B.
\]

Thus a nominal log-burden gap \(\Delta\) between two actions is preserved if \(\Delta>2\epsilon B\). If response entries drift by at most (\nu\) per unit time between assay and use, replace \(\epsilon\) by \(\epsilon+\nu\tau\), giving the certificate \(\Delta>2(\epsilon+\nu\tau)B\). This is a confidence-set decision bound, not a cancer-specific novelty claim. Without a defensible drift bound or transition model, a matrix measured before treatment does not certify the matrix after evolution.

## Rare-clone depth is a hard, familiar limit

Suppose an unobserved cross-resistant clone has initial frequency (q) and relative growth advantage (s) under the chosen treatment for a window of length (L). With no switching, its final fraction is

\[
f_L=\frac{qe^{sL}}{1-q+qe^{sL}}.
\]

To guarantee (f_L\le\varepsilon), the starting frequency must satisfy

\[
q\le q_{\rm crit}=\frac{\varepsilon}{e^{sL}(1-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon}.
\]

If (N\) effective independent cells are sampled with perfect sensitivity and none is detected, then the exact one-sided \((1-\alpha)\) binomial upper bound is (q_U=1-\alpha^{1/N}). To rule out all (q\ge q_{\rm crit}), one needs

\[
N\ge\frac{\log\alpha}{\log(1-q_{\rm crit})}\simeq\frac{\log(1/\alpha)}{q_{\rm crit}}.
\]

At \(\varepsilon=0.1\), (sL=10), and 95% confidence, \(q_{\rm crit}\approx5.0\times10^{-6}\) and the ideal minimum is about (5.95\times10^5) independent cells. At (sL=15), the same guarantee takes about (8.8\times10^7). If per-cell detection sensitivity is (\eta<1), replace the detection probability by (1-(1-\eta q)^N), so (N\ge\log\alpha/\log(1-\eta q_{\rm crit})). Raw sequencing reads are not independent cells; effective unique molecules and assay sensitivity matter. Spatially biased sampling violates the iid premise and can make this bound optimistic.

This is a quantitative assay-design boundary, not a new sample-complexity theorem. It says that “no cross-resistant lineage detected” cannot support a strong finite-horizon control guarantee unless the assay depth is tied to a growth-advantage bound and an acceptable final escape fraction. Unknown de-novo mutation or drug-induced state switching creates a source term after sampling and weakens any pre-treatment guarantee further. A rigorous guarantee must bound those rates, include them in (M_t(u_t)), and charge the observation delay. If (s) or transition rates are unbounded over the admissible model class, no finite pre-treatment sample guarantees escape control.

## Causal decomposition and measurement design

An observed population-average sensitivity shift can arise from selection among pre-existing lineages or a within-lineage phenotype change. For a trait (b_i) equal to lineage (i)'s growth rate under drug B, with frequencies (p_i), let exposure to drug A multiply lineage (i) by (w_i=e^{r_{iA}x_A}). Under heritable fixed traits, selection alone gives

\[
\bar b_{\rm after}-\bar b_{\rm before}=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_p(w,b)}{\mathbb E_p[w]}.
\]

If the lineage-specific response also changes by \(\Delta b_i\) during treatment, add the within-lineage term \(\sum_i p_iw_i\Delta b_i/\mathbb E_p[w]\). This is a Price-equation decomposition. It makes the causal measurement target explicit: paired lineage frequencies plus drug response by lineage over time, with vehicle and drug-exposed sister cultures. Barcode frequencies alone do not identify (b_i); endpoint bulk sensitivity alone does not separate the two terms. A genetic/isogenic perturbation is needed to assign a particular molecular driver rather than merely a lineage association.

An assay should therefore record at baseline and each prespecified evolution checkpoint: viable total cell count, barcode/clone frequencies, dose-response estimates per lineage for a fixed drug panel, uncertainty intervals, and assay turnaround. Independent A-only, B-only, combination, and vehicle arms distinguish cross-resistance, combination interaction, selection, and drift. Use separate evolution replicates, not only technical replicate wells. Any fixed-time or exposure window should be chosen from pilot growth/assay data; no clinical dose is implied. Matrix rows must be remeasured after selection because cross-resistance can be transient or replicate-specific.

## Equal-budget falsification experiment

An assay-ready system is a barcoded EGFR-mutant NSCLC evolution model, because primary work already used HCC827 lineages under gefitinib and trametinib and tracked collateral response. Start several independent barcoded populations from the same baseline pool. Evolve vehicle, A, B, and A+B arms through a prespecified number of passages; at baseline and each checkpoint, measure the lineage-linked (K\times J) response matrix, barcode abundance, total viable burden, and—on a held-out subset—single-cell state/genetic markers. Include spike-ins of a known cross-resistant lineage at frequencies around the calculated (q_{\rm crit}) to calibrate effective detection.

Compare three frozen policies on randomized held-out replicate cultures with equal total cells, sequencing molecules/reads, culture wells, turnaround, and integrated drug AUC:

1. **Marginal robust control:** use only the per-drug response distributions and the optimal-transport worst-case bound above.
2. **Lineage-linked robust control:** use the measured clone-by-drug confidence sets and a stated bound/model for state transitions.
3. **Static control:** choose the best fixed schedule using the same longitudinal linked assay data available to policy 2, but do not update its choice after later checkpoints. This isolates re-optimization value from information advantage.

Primary endpoint: held-out log viable burden and cross-resistant fraction after a fixed follow-up horizon. A result that supports the *utility* of linked sensing would require policy 2 to improve on policy 1 at the same total acquisition and treatment budgets, while beating policy 3 at the same linked information budget, with its predicted uncertainty interval covering held-out outcomes. It would not establish patient benefit.

**Falsifiers:** (i) clone-linked matrices do not reproduce across independent evolution replicates within the stated intervals; (ii) transport-robust marginal control matches lineage-linked control within a prespecified burden margin; (iii) policy 2's apparent gain vanishes after charging assay delay or higher dose/toxicity; (iv) cross-resistance/switching rates exceed the fitted uncertainty set; or (v) an abundance of cross-resistant spike-ins below the declared detection limit later establishes despite the claimed guarantee. A positive average response without held-out lineage/barcode verification does not pass this test.

**Cost ledger:** for (K) lineages, (J) drugs, (D) concentrations, (T) timepoints, and (r) biological replicates, direct clone-by-drug assays require on the order of (KJDrT) response measurements (plus controls); pooled barcoding can reduce culture-well count but shifts cost to barcode construction, competition calibration, effective read depth, and detection limits. Acar et al. used a roughly million-barcode starting population and approximately (10^8\)–(10^9)-cell-scale cultures, illustrating the scale needed to preserve large-population dynamics. Charge (N\) effective cells, unique-molecule depth, sequencing and culture cost, removed sample volume, number and duration of drug exposures (x_j), and assay delay \(\tau\) (burden changes during delay). AUC matching does not match toxicity; the combination penalty must be measured or declared a modeling parameter. The cell-line protocol omits pharmacokinetics, tumor spatial structure, immune/stromal effects, metastatic shedding, and patient-specific normal-tissue toxicity.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

- **Temporal collateral sensitivity is established.** Zhao et al. selected Ph+ ALL cells under BCR–ABL1 inhibitors, profiled intermediate resistant states across drug panels, tested a causative BCR–ABL1 mutation, and evaluated a collateral-sensitive regimen in mice ([Cell 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.01.045)). Dhawan et al. mapped evolving and holiday-dependent collateral responses in ALK-mutant NSCLC ([Scientific Reports 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-00791-8)). Scarborough et al. observed repeatable and stochastic collateral responses across Ewing sarcoma evolution replicates ([iScience 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2020.101293)).
- **Lineage-resolved steering already exists.** Acar et al. barcode HCC827 NSCLC lineages, follow clonal frequencies, and combine that with clone-specific drug screening and modeling; they report cell populations on the order of (10^8\)–(10^9) cells ([Nature Communications 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15596-z)). The proposed assay is not new. Bhang et al. use high-complexity ClonTracer to follow more than one million cells and show resistant clones were mostly small pre-existing subpopulations in two models ([Nature Medicine 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nm.3841)); rare-clone detection and combination suppression are established motivations.
- **Clone-specific combinations are prior art.** DRUG-NEM reconstructs subpopulation-specific drug effects from single-cell perturbation data and prioritizes combinations ([PNAS 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1711365115)). CaDRReS-Sc predicts cell/cluster-by-drug responses and aggregates combination benefit, with in-vitro validation in patient-derived cultures ([Genome Medicine 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-021-01000-y)). These papers already address complementary vulnerabilities across subpopulations. Our two-world calculation only identifies what is lost if clone-by-drug pairing is compressed to per-drug marginals.
- **Dynamic resistance-state inference is also established.** Whiting et al. infer resistance transitions from barcode lineage and population-size data and validate pre-existing resistance versus phenotype switching in colorectal cell lines ([Nature Communications 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7)). Their framework is not a cross-drug matrix controller, but it overlaps the temporal-state component. A 2026 ASCO abstract analyzes temporal collateral-sensitivity networks in a spatial cell automaton; it is simulation evidence, not a clinical controller ([JCO 2026 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2026.44.16_suppl.e15007)).
- **Novelty verdict:** no priority claim. The algebraic covariance example, Price decomposition, binomial detection bound, confidence-width rule, and optimal-transport robust envelope are standard mathematical consequences. The specific possible contribution is an experimentally testable *decision audit*: when linked clone-by-drug assays change a robust schedule ranking after assay, sequencing, delay, and dose costs are charged. Even this requires a broader prior-art audit and held-out validation; it is not yet a new scientific result.
