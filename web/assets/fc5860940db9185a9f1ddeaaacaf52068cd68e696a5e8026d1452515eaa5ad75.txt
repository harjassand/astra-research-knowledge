1. ALL-N MATRIX IDENTITIES, SIGN AND COEFFICIENTS

Let J_n=(1/2)sum n.sigma^(i), k_N(m)=[(I+m.sigma)/2]^tensor N,
u=n.m, v=n-u m, r=n cross m. For N=1, Pauli multiplication gives
 {J_n,k_1}=(u I+n.sigma)/2,
 [J_n,k_1]=(i/2)(n cross m).sigma.
Differentiating the tensor kernel and summing its N slots therefore gives

 {J_n,k_N}=[N u+v.grad]k_N=:B_n k_N,
 [J_n,k_N]=i(r.grad)k_N=:i R_n k_N.                       (A1)

Differential operators commute with constant matrix left/right multiplication.
Consequently
 B_n^2 k=J_n^2 k+2J_n kJ_n+kJ_n^2,
 R_n^2 k=-J_n^2 k+2J_n kJ_n-kJ_n^2,
and hence
 (B_n^2-R_n^2)k=2(J_n^2k+kJ_n^2).                        (A2)
The MINUS angular sign is correct. Replacing it by plus gives a different
quantum generator and does not prove the candidate theorem.

Set Lambda=M+1, C=A+Lambda I, Lc=2M+1, s=N^.75,
t0=2/N-Lambda/s^2 and H=Q_C/s^2+b.J/s. For N>=Lambda^2,
t0=(2/N)d0>0 with d0=1-Lambda/(2sqrt N)>=1/2.
Q_C=Q_A+Lambda J^2, so K=t0J^2+H is EXACTLY the target exponent.

Expanding (A2), with C=sum lambda_i n_i n_i^T, gives the author's Hessian
 D_C=(I-mm^T)C(I-mm^T)-[m]_cross C[m]_cross^T
and potential
 V=N[Tr C+(N-1)m^TCm]/(4s^2)+N b.m/(2s).                 (A3)
Its trace is exactly Tr(H k_N). For the drift one can simplify further:

 (v.grad)v=-m-u n+2u^2m,
 (r.grad)r=u n-m,
 (v.grad)v-(r.grad)r=-2u v.

Thus the full EXACT drift is

 a_N=(N-1)(I-mm^T)Cm/(2s^2)+(I-mm^T)b/(2s).              (A4)

The backward diffusion/Feynman--Kac operator
 L= a_N.grad+D_C:Hess/(4s^2), G=L+V
therefore satisfies G k_N={H,k_N}/2 on the entire physical N-qubit kernel.
There is no commutation assumption on the components of H or on A,b.
No forward-density/backward-generator interchange is used: k_N is the
matrix-valued test function of the diffusion.

3. EXACT HEAT SEED AND INITIAL EXPONENTIAL TAIL

I reconstruct the heat identity without importing another audit. On spin j,
angular averaging of e^(v.J) is scalar, with value
 [sinh((j+1/2)r)]/[(2j+1)sinh(r/2)].
Multiplying by sinch(r/2) changes this to sinch((j+1/2)r).
For a centered 3D Gaussian with covariance2t I,
 E[sinch(q|v|)]=E[exp(q u.v)]=exp(tq^2),
where u is any unit vector and its spherical average supplies sinch.
Multiplying by e^-t/4 gives e^(t j(j+1)). Thus on EVERY spin representation

 e^(tJ^2)=integral e^(v.J) nu_t(dv),
 nu_t=(4pi t)^-3/2 e^-t/4 exp(-|v|^2/(4t))sinch(|v|/2)d^3v.

For j0 this also shows nu_t has mass one. It proves the full N-qubit operator
identity simultaneously, including representation multiplicities.
Since e^(v.J) is a tensor product of identical local exponentials, the
normalized heat density rho0=e^(t0J^2)/Zbase has a positive Bloch mixture with
radial unnormalized density
 q(r)=r^2 sinch(r/2)exp[-r^2/(4t0)+N logcosh(r/2)],
 m=tanh(r/2)u. All r-independent Gaussian/trace constants cancel in q's
normalization; dropping them does not change any tail ratio.

Put f(x)=x^2/2-logcosh x and delta=f(1)>0. Its second derivative is tanh^2 x.
For 0<=x<=1, tanh x>=x/2, so integration twice yields f(x)>=x^4/48.
Since tanh x<=x, f(x)<=x^4/12 for every x>=0.
For x>=1, f(x)/x^2 is increasing: its derivative numerator is
 2logcosh x-x tanh x, whose derivative tanh x-x sech^2 x is nonnegative.
Hence f(x)>=delta x^2. These are precisely the needed scalar inequalities.

Let a0=2atanh(r0/2). Then |m|>r0/2 iff r>a0. As t0<=2/N,
 q(r)<=r^2sinch(r/2)e^-N f(r/2).
On [a0,2], the integral is at most
 C1 exp[-N a0^4/768], C1=(8/3)sinch(1).
For r>=2 and Ndelta>=2,
 -Ndelta r^2/4+r/2<=-Ndelta r^2/8.
Splitting that exponent in half bounds the integral by
 C2 exp[-delta N/4], C2=16sqrt(pi)delta^-3/2.
Thus the entire tail is <=(C1+C2)e^-kappa0 N, where
 kappa0=min(a0^4/768,delta/4)>0.

For the lower normalizer set r=N^-1/4 R, R in[1,2]. The exponent is
 -N f(r/2)-(N r^2/8)(1/d0-1).
Its first term is >=-1/12 and its second >=-Lambda/2, because d0>=1/2.
Also sinch>=1 and integral_1^2 R^2dR=7/3. Therefore
 Zrad>=N^-3/4 Lbase, Lbase=(7/3)e^(-Lambda/2-1/12).
This independently verifies the candidate's lower bound, including the
otherwise easy-to-miss d0 reciprocal factor.

We may take the explicit public constant

 pbad<=Ctail N^3/4 e^-kappa0N.                            (A6)
Replacing every outer branch by m=0 gives a normalized product mixture rho0'

5. STOPPED ITO, EXACT DUHAMEL, COMMUTING CASIMIR AND NORMALIZATION

Let W_t=E[w_(t wedge tau)k_N(m_(t wedge tau))]. Ito applied entrywise on this
bounded stopped domain gives, in integral form (or almost every t),
 W_t'=E[1_(t<tau)w_t {H,k_N(m_t)}/2].
Linearity of M_H(X)={H,X}/2 then gives the exact stopped defect

 W_t'-M_H W_t
   =-E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_H k_N(m_tau)].                    (A8)

No boundary local-time term is missing: the process is stopped and frozen,
not reflected. The indicator of stopped time supplies the entire defect.
Starting with W0=rho0', variation of constants yields

 e^(M_H)rho0'-W1
 =integral_0^1 e^((1-t)M_H)
       E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_H k_N(m_tau)]dt.               (A9)

For Hermitian input the semigroup is the finite matrix sandwich
 e^(uM_H)X=e^(uH/2)Xe^(uH/2), with trace-norm operator bound<=e^(u hN).
Also ||M_H k||_1<=hN since k>=0 and Tr k=1. This proves the author's
hN e^(2hN)P_exit bound. Combining w_tau<=e^(tau hN) and tau<=t actually gives
the tighter hN e^hN P_exit. Consequently

 ||e^(M_H)rho0-W1||_1
 <=2e^hN pbad+6hN exp[hN-kappa1' N^1.5].                  (A10)

while the linear b term has trace zero. Scalar Jensen on the eigenvalues

proves g>=1 across ALL multiplicities. This lower bound is independent of N.

W1 is positive with positive trace and is an expectation of positive scalar
