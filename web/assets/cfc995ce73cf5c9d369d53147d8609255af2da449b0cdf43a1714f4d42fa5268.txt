Abstract
We resolve the symmetric Mahler conjecture positively, including its equality classification.
Every origin-symmetric convex body inRn has volume product at least4n/n!, with equality
exactly for invertible linear images of Hanner polytopes.

1.1 History and context
Mahler introduced the volume-product problem in his work on convex bodies and transference
in the geometry of numbers [22]; the planar inequality also goes back to him [21]. Reisner later
characterized its equality cases as parallelograms, as a consequence of his zonoid theorem [28, p. 340].
Iriyeh and Shibata proved the complete three-dimensional symmetric theorem, including equality [14,
Theorem 1.1]. Fradelizi, Hubard, Meyer, Roldán-Pensado, and Zvavitch gave a shorter proof based
on equipartitions, together with another equality argument and stability [9]. Chen, Li, Xi, and Xu
recently gave a shadow-flow proof of that theorem in a preprint that also treats the nonsymmetric
three-dimensional problem [4, Theorem 7.1].
Hanner’s recursively constructed spaces [12] already play a central role in the known higher-
dimensional cases. Saint-Raymond proved the sharp inequality for unconditional bodies (those
invariant under coordinate sign changes) [30]; Meyer and Reisner identified the Hanner equality
cases in that class [23, 29]. Reisner also proved the sharp inequality for zonoids, which are Hausdorff
limits of Minkowski sums of centered segments, and showed that equality within that class means
a parallelotope [27, 28]; Gordon, Meyer, and Reisner later gave a short geometric proof [10]. For
local questions in Banach–Mazur distance, Nazarov, Petrov, Ryabogin, and Zvavitch established
strict local minimality, up to linear equivalence, of the cube [25]. Kim extended this to all Hanner
polytopes [16]. Kim and Zvavitch obtained stability in the unconditional class and the inequality in
a neighborhood of that class [17].
A separate line of work seeks dimension-independent exponential bounds without the sharp
constant. BourgainandMilmanestablishedsuchareverseBlaschke–Santalóinequality[ 3]; Kuperberg
obtained an explicit stronger bound by a geometric argument using Gauss linking integrals [18].
Complex-analytic approaches provide another setting for these questions. Nazarov used Hörmander’s
¯∂theorem and Bergman kernels for the reverse Blaschke–Santaló inequality [24]. Berndtsson recast

Kuperberg’s proof using complex integrals [1]. In a symplectic approach, Karasev proved the sharp

Theorems 4.2 and 4.4]. These works give analytic and geometric precedents for volume-product
estimates; their estimates are not hypotheses of our proof.
The analytic ingredient is a conformal map of the disk for which uniform angular measure on the
boundary maps to a uniform imaginary coordinate. It is a rotation of Gross’s example for the uniform
distribution in his conformal Skorokhod embedding [11, Section 3.2]. A holomorphic mass estimate
turns that boundary law into a lower bound for an integral of feasible-simplex volumes over the
primal body. We prove the mass estimate directly by Stokes’ theorem, with its precise normalization;
its general background is the theory of generalized Lelong numbers [6, Sections 3 and 5]. The
symmetric-convex extremal-function framework of Lundin and Baran was a further motivation for
the analytic search; see [20] and the formula recorded in [2, Proposition 1.2, pp. 246–247]. The proof
here instead uses the direct isolated-zero mass calculation, not those extremal-function formulas
as premises. The equality argument leads to a classical property of normed spaces: any three
pairwise-intersecting closed balls have a common point. Hanner studied the associated recursively
constructed convex bodies [12]. Lima’s norm-additive decomposition criterion [19, Theorem 1.2],
also recorded in Hansen–Lima [13, Theorem 3.6(4)], is exactly the metric-median condition used
below. Reisner’s unconditional equality theorem already connects minimal volume product to this
ball intersection structure [29, Theorem 1]. Our endpoint argument obtains the structure without
assuming unconditionality; Hansen–Lima’s finite-dimensional classification is the final input [13,
Corollary 7.4].
For a body without a prescribed center, the general Mahler problem instead minimizes|K||(K−
z)◦|overz∈intK. Its conjectured constant(n+1)n+1/(n!)2 is smaller than the symmetric constant
for n≥2; a result for that general problem alone therefore does not establish Theorem 1.1. The
argument here uses symmetry throughout and is independent of the general theorem proved in the
companion paper [26, Theorem 1.1].

8.1 Three balls and the equality classification
A normed space has thethree-ball intersection property, also called the3.2 intersection property, if
any three closed balls that intersect pairwise have a common point. Their radii may differ. The
equivalence between this intersection property and the metric-median condition is Lima’s classical
norm-additive decomposition criterion [19, Theorem 1.2]; see also Hansen–Lima [13, Theorem 3.6(4)].
We include the short direction needed here, with arbitrary radii.
Lemma 8.2(From medians to three balls).Suppose every triple of points in a real normed space
has a metric median. Then the space has the three-ball intersection property, including balls of
radius zero.
