# Hostile audit: Coulomb density without scalar local Kohn–Sham representation

## Verdict

I found no concrete gap in the load-bearing interfaces checked in §§4–7 of the supplied paper. Under the paper's stated analytic hypotheses, the three targeted steps fit together: the constrained-search certificate covers arbitrary spinful ground-state ensembles; equality in the scalar-potential energy comparison identifies any representing potential; and the translated-tail/Agmon argument excludes the entire sum space $L^{3/2}+L^\infty$. This is an internal mathematical audit of the source PDF, not independent peer review, formal verification, or a novelty judgment. The main unresolved audit dependency is the paper's long Coulomb channel construction in §§2–3, which supplies the fixed-parameter tail estimates used by §§6–7.

## Exact claim and interface

The source theorem fixes $D=10^{200}$, $e_z=(0,0,1)$, and the finite integer

\[
 Z=2+\left\lceil\sup_{0<t\le1}t^{-1}\mathbf 1_B(t)\right\rceil,
\]

where $B$ is defined by explicit spectral, density, and angle-gap tests in §7. For the three-electron Coulomb Hamiltonian with two nuclei of charge $Z$ at $(0,0,\pm D/Z)$, its spin-summed absolute-ground-state density has no representation by a ground-state ensemble of

\[
 H_s(v)=\sum_{i=1}^3(-\tfrac12\Delta_i+v(r_i)),\qquad v\in L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R),
\]

acting on antisymmetric spinors $\bigwedge^3L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb C^2)$. The claim allows arbitrary degeneracy and arbitrary complex spinful ensemble states, but fixes one real, spin-independent, multiplicative potential. The exact charge is proved finite but is not numerically evaluated; no effective numerical upper bound is supplied.

## Hostile checks of §§4–7

**Ensemble kinetic certificate (§§4–5).** The angle minimizer gives $g,h$ with $g^2+h^2=n$, $\|g\|_2^2=2$, $\|h\|_2^2=1$, and formal $V=\Delta g/(2g)$. With the weighted gap, its positive-orbital identity is

\[
q_V[f]\ge d\bigl(\|f\|_2^2-|\langle g/\sqrt2,f\rangle|^2\bigr).
\]

For a spinor this projects onto the rank-two space $P_g\otimes I_2$. For every three-fermion ensemble, the number operator for any unit spin-orbital is a projection, hence its 1-RDM obeys $0\le\gamma\le I$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\gamma=3$, including arbitrary spin coherence. Summation therefore gives

\[
T[\Gamma]+\int Vn\ge d\{\operatorname{Tr}\gamma-\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma(P_g\otimes I_2)]\}\ge d(3-2)=d.
\]

The determinant $(g/\sqrt2\uparrow)\wedge(g/\sqrt2\downarrow)\wedge(h\uparrow)$ has the same density and attains equality. The paper checks $\int |V|n<\infty$ before summing. I found no restriction to pure states, a fixed $S_z$ ensemble, or a chosen filling in this argument.

**Identification of a hypothetical potential (§5.2).** For $v\in L^{3/2}+L^\infty$, the infinitesimal form bound gives a uniform finite kinetic bound on normalized ground vectors, so any trace-one ground ensemble with density $n$ has finite kinetic energy. The determinant's kinetic certificate and same density then force equality in the $H_v$ variational principle, hence the determinant itself is a ground vector. Replacing only its down-spin $g/\sqrt2$ orbital by $f\downarrow$, the ground-state form equation gives $q_v(g/\sqrt2,f)=0$ for $f\perp g$; decomposition into the parallel and orthogonal components yields $h_v(g/\sqrt2)=e_g(g/\sqrt2)$. Subtracting the distributional equation for $g$, and using $g>0$, forces $v=V+e_g$ almost everywhere. Thus an admissible $v$ would put $V$ in the same sum space. This step handles arbitrary ground-space degeneracy; it does rely essentially on a common scalar local potential.

**Translating-plane tail contradiction (§6).** Under the contrary assumption $V=V_c+V_b$ with $V_c\in L^{3/2}$, $V_b\in L^\infty$, all escaping translates of $V_c$ vanish in local $L^{3/2}$, while a subsequence of translated $V_b$ has a bounded weak-* limit $W$. Local elliptic compactness passes the equations for $g,h$ to the limit. Along centers on the nodal plane, the density limit is proportional to $z^2e^{-2k\omega\cdot x}$; nonnegative $g_\infty$ vanishes on the plane, so Harnack forces $g_\infty=0$, and the signed $h_\infty\propto z e^{-k\omega\cdot x}$ then forces $W=d+k^2/2=:c_\infty$. For centers whose heights escape, the angle equation and its maximum-principle barrier show $\theta\to\pi/2$ locally; $h_\infty\propto e^{-k\omega\cdot x}$ forces the same $W$. Finite-height centers reduce to plane-centered ones. The paper then derives a *uniform local form* limit (not a pointwise limit of $V$) and an exterior lower bound

\[
q_V[u]\ge(\tfrac12-\varepsilon)\|\nabla u\|_2^2+(c_\infty-\varepsilon)\|u\|_2^2.
\]

Choose $k<a<\sqrt{k^2+2d}$. The weak equation for $g$, tested against a truncated exponential weight, gives an Agmon bound and $e^{a|x|}g\in H^1(\mathbb R^3)$. Since the odd orbital vanishes on $z=0$, $g=\sqrt n$ there, so the trace theorem forces $\int_{z=0}e^{2ap}n(p,0)\,dx_1dx_2<\infty$. But the source's planar lower tail $\sqrt{n(p,0)}\ge c_\lambda e^{-kp}p^{\beta-4}$ makes this integral dominate a positive multiple of

\[
\int_{p_*}^\infty e^{2(a-k)p}p^{2\beta-7}\,dp=\infty,
\]

a contradiction. The inequalities and signs in this final chain check out under the stated §3 density limits, plane lower bound, and §4 gap.

## Prior-art and transfer boundary

The nodal-plane asymptotic mechanism is prior: Gori-Giorgi, Gál, and Baerends analyze alternative density/potential asymptotics when a KS HOMO has a nodal plane, while Gori-Giorgi and Baerends study the effective equation for \(\sqrt n\). The source's distinct contribution is the asserted finite Coulomb ground-state construction, an all-ensemble certificate, and an obstruction to the full (L^{3/2}+L^\infty) class. Earlier prescribed-density cusp examples (Wagner et al.) are not finite Coulomb ground-state examples. Ullrich–Kohn explicitly distinguish pure- and ensemble-v-representability, which is why the all-ensemble certificate is load-bearing. The lattice and one-dimensional-torus existence results concern different operators/domains and do not contradict this continuum three-dimensional claim.

The result is **not** a theorem that practical or generalized KS fails. It does not exclude distributional or spin-dependent local potentials, nonlocal generalized-KS operators, finite-basis matrix representations, or approximation of the density by a sequence of scalar potentials. For spin-dependent potentials, the energy couples to spin-resolved densities and the same-density scalar comparison no longer follows; for nonlocal operators it couples to the full 1-RDM rather than only $n$. The theorem also gives no evidence that a usual chemical/material system falls outside the class: $Z$ is defined nonnumerically, $D$ is enormous, and the model is nonrelativistic. No practical failure rate or materials prediction follows.

## Costs, status, and next decisive gate

The charge prescription is mathematically finite but non-effective: it quantifies over auxiliary couplings and every folded minimizing angle, uses exact spectral/density data, and supplies no evaluated $Z$, executable certificate, or physical validation. I found no concrete proof counterexample in §§4–7; the nonrepresentable density itself is a source-reported mathematical counterexample, and the checked interfaces are internally reconstructed. The next decisive gate is a blinded external proof audit of the source-dependent Coulomb channel/tail estimates in §§2–3 and the uniform-all-minimizers step in §§4/7, followed by an independently checkable fixed-$Z$ witness. Without that, treat the result as a strong source theorem, not externally validated or priority-cleared.

The earlier elasticity fallback is retained only as a short finite-data gate: for $m$ continuous scalar readouts on a smooth-admissible $(m+1)$-dimensional Lamé perturbation sphere, Borsuk–Ulam gives antipodal coefficient fields with identical readouts. This proves finite-readout nonidentifiability under those exact hypotheses; the next gate there is an acquisition model with calibrated noise, vector-valued/complex observations, and physical admissibility margins.
