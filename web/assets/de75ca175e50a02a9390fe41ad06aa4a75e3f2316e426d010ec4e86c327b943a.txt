Leaf 08 — Uniform-in-time Vlasov chaos: an exact unstable counterexample
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: COUNTEREXAMPLE DERIVED (closed-form, not proof-assistant checked)

Result
------
Finite-time propagation of chaos does not become uniform over t in the full
class of smooth, globally Lipschitz mean-field forces and finite-first-moment
data. The obstruction already occurs for the one-dimensional deterministic
Vlasov equation with the quadratic interaction kernel K(r)=r^2/2 and iid
standard Gaussian initial data. For every fixed T, the tagged one-particle
position law is O_T(N^-1) close in W1 to its Vlasov limit. At the logarithmic
horizon t=log N+c, however, its W1 error tends to exp(c)/(2 sqrt(pi)). The
fixed phase-space W1 stability modulus also grows at least proportionally to
exp(t). This is an explicit repulsive/unstable mode, not a numerical result.

Model and exact quantifiers
---------------------------
For t >= 0, let Z_i^N=(X_i^N,V_i^N), i=1,...,N, solve

  d X_i^N / dt = V_i^N,
  d V_i^N / dt = (1/N) sum_{j=1}^N K'(X_i^N-X_j^N)
                = X_i^N - bar X_N^N,
  K(r)=r^2/2,  bar X_N^N=N^-1 sum_j X_j^N.

The corresponding Vlasov equation is

  partial_t f + v partial_x f + (x-m_x[f_t]) partial_v f = 0,
  m_x[f_t]=int x f_t(dx,dv).

For every N >= 1, take the initial states iid with law
  f_0 = Normal(0,I_2),
so X_i^N(0),V_i^N(0) are independent standard normals. The limiting Vlasov
solution f_t is the pushforward of f_0 by

  (x,v) -> (cosh(t)x+sinh(t)v, sinh(t)x+cosh(t)v).

The mean remains zero, and the position marginal of f_t is
  Normal(0, q(t)),  q(t)=cosh(2t).

Let f_{1,t}^N be the law of (X_1^N(t),V_1^N(t)); write rho_{1,t}^N and rho_t
for its and f_t's position marginals. Equip phase space with the standard
kinetic W1 cost |x-y|+|v-w|. Projection to position is 1-Lipschitz, hence
W1(f_{1,t}^N,f_t) >= W1(rho_{1,t}^N,rho_t).

Exact solution and error
------------------------
The center of mass obeys bar X_N(t)=bar X_N(0)+t bar V_N(0). The centered
coordinates satisfy d^2(X_i-bar X_N)/dt^2 = X_i-bar X_N. Put
c=cosh(t), s=sinh(t). Then

  X_1^N(t)=bar X_N(0)+t bar V_N(0)
              +c(X_1^N(0)-bar X_N(0))+s(V_1^N(0)-bar V_N(0)).

The Gaussian sample mean is independent of the centered residual vector.
Consequently rho_{1,t}^N is centered Gaussian with variance

  q_N(t) = (1-1/N) cosh(2t) + (1+t^2)/N
         = q(t) - Delta_N(t),
  Delta_N(t) = [cosh(2t)-1-t^2]/N >= 0.

For centered one-dimensional Gaussians, monotone coupling gives the exact
W1 distance, so

  W1(rho_{1,t}^N,rho_t)
    = sqrt(2/pi) [sqrt(q(t))-sqrt(q_N(t))]
    = sqrt(2/pi) Delta_N(t)/(sqrt(q(t))+sqrt(q_N(t))).             (1)

The numerator is nonnegative because cosh(2t)>=1+2t^2 for t>=0. Also
q_N(t)>0 for every N,t, as is immediate from its first expression.

Finite-time statement. For every fixed T<infinity and every N>=1,

  sup_{0<=t<=T} W1(f_{1,t}^N,f_t)
    >= sup_{0<=t<=T} W1(rho_{1,t}^N,rho_t),

and the position term is bounded above by
  sqrt(2/pi) cosh(2T)/N,
using (1), q(t)>=1 and Delta_N(t)<=cosh(2T)/N. Thus the tagged
position observable has the stronger O_T(N^-1) rate. The usual
empirical-measure Dobrushin theorem gives finite-time convergence of the full
tagged phase-space law in W1 as well (see comparison below).

Logarithmic-time failure. Fix any real c0. For all sufficiently large N let
t_N=log(N)+c0. Then

  q(t_N)/N^2 -> exp(2c0)/2,
  Delta_N(t_N)/N -> exp(2c0)/2,
  Delta_N(t_N)/q(t_N) -> 0.

Substitution into (1), by rationalizing the difference of square roots,
yields

  W1(rho_{1,t_N}^N,rho_{t_N}) -> exp(c0)/(2 sqrt(pi)) > 0.       (2)

Therefore, for every epsilon>0 and every c0 with exp(c0)/(2sqrt(pi))>epsilon,
there is N_0 such that for all N>=N_0,
  W1(f_{1,t_N}^N,f_{t_N}) > epsilon.
In particular liminf_{N->infinity} sup_{t>=0} W1(f_{1,t}^N,f_t)
  >= 1/(2sqrt(pi)), by taking c0=0. More strongly, for each fixed finite N,
the position W1 error diverges as t->infinity:

  W1(rho_{1,t}^N,rho_t)
    ~ exp(t)/sqrt(pi) * (1-sqrt(1-1/N)) -> infinity.

The small-error lifetime has logarithmic scale. For each fixed c0, (2) says
that at t=log N+c0 the asymptotic error is exp(c0)/(2sqrt(pi)); solving
this limiting expression for a fixed target epsilon gives
  t_epsilon = log N + log(2sqrt(pi) epsilon) + o(1),
as N->infinity. This is a threshold asymptotic at fixed epsilon, not a bound
uniform over all epsilon or all N.

Stability corollary. Let f_0^a=Normal(0,a^2 I_2) and f_0^1=Normal(0,I_2),
with a>0 and a !=1. Both Vlasov solutions stay centered and are transported
by the same hyperbolic matrix. Their position W1 distance at time t is
sqrt(2/pi)|a-1|sqrt(cosh(2t)). Their initial phase-space W1 distance under
the cost |x-y|+|v-w| is at most 2sqrt(2/pi)|a-1|, by synchronous coupling.
Thus the ratio W1(f_t^a,f_t^1)/W1(f_0^a,f_0^1) is at least
  0.5 sqrt(cosh(2t)) ~ exp(t)/(2sqrt(2)).
No time-independent Lipschitz stability constant in this fixed W1 norm can
hold even on this family of centered Gaussian data.

Primary-source comparison and scope
-----------------------------------
The strongest directly relevant primary-source estimate located is
Iacobelli's (2022) Theorem 2.1, DOI 10.1007/s00205-021-01705-9. For the
kinetic phase-space W1 cost |x-y|+|v-w| and a smooth convolution potential
with B=||D^2 K||_infinity finite, for probability solutions with finite
first moment it states

  W1(f_1(t),f_2(t)) <= min{ (1+t) exp[(2/3)B((1+t)^3-1)],
                              exp[(1+2B)t] }
                         W1(f_1(0),f_2(0)).

The paper's Section 1.2 permits spatial domain X=R^d or T^d, while the
displayed proof of Theorem 2.1 writes integrals over T^d. Direct use of the
refined first factor for this R^d quadratic model is therefore a source-scope
item to confirm. The same article unambiguously records the classical
Dobrushin estimate in the general-domain setup:

  W1(f_1(t),f_2(t)) <= exp[(1+2B)t] W1(f_1(0),f_2(0)).

Here K(r)=r^2/2 has B=1. For each realization, the empirical measure of the
N-particle system is itself a measure-valued Vlasov solution, so this
estimate applies to it and f_t. On each fixed [0,T], its factor is at most
exp(3T); iid empirical measures with finite first moment converge in W1,
which gives the usual pathwise finite-time mean-field limit. In the present
Gaussian case, the empirical W1 error also tends to zero in expectation
(finite second moment gives uniform integrability). By exchangeability,
E[mu_N(t)] is the tagged first-particle law; convexity of W1 therefore gives
finite-time convergence for that law. Both candidate factors grow with t
and give no all-time conclusion. Formula (2) shows that the failure is real
in this same W1 topology, despite the O_T(N^-1) tagged-position error. The
exact Gaussian computation is sharper than either generic stability bound
for the observable and data considered here.

Uniform-in-time results do exist under extra mechanisms, and so do not
contradict this counterexample. For example Monmarché (2017), DOI
10.1016/j.spa.2016.10.003, proves a uniform-in-time first-marginal chaos
bound in W2 for a kinetic Vlasov-Fokker-Planck/Langevin model with friction,
nonzero noise, a strictly convex confining external potential, and a
semiconvex interaction under his Assumption 1. Those hypotheses remove the
undamped repulsive hyperbolic mode used here. This is not a same-model or
same-norm theorem; it is recorded to prevent overgeneralizing the negative
result.

Acquisition, precision, computation
-----------------------------------
Acquisition was a bounded primary-source pass: Dobrushin (1979) for the
original mean-field framework, Iacobelli (2022) for the directly matching
W1 stability estimate and its assumptions, and Monmarché (2017) for a
positive uniform-time result under dissipative stochastic assumptions. The
read ledger gives URLs, dates, and the portions actually used. No source
corpus or dataset was downloaded. The model and Gaussian data were chosen
analytically; no fitting, simulation, or numerical ODE solving was used.
All stated variances and finite-N formulas are exact. The only asymptotic
step is the elementary limit N->infinity at t=log N+c0, with each c0 fixed.
No computer arithmetic or precision tolerance enters the result.

Falsifier / next audit
----------------------
The central claim would be falsified by an algebraic error in either (i) the
center/relative-coordinate decomposition, (ii) the Gaussian variance q_N,
or (iii) the standard one-dimensional Gaussian W1 identity. A decisive
independent check is to solve the two scalar mode equations
  bar X''=0,  (X_i-bar X)''=(X_i-bar X),
then recompute Var(X_1^N(t)) from the orthogonal Gaussian modes. Any alleged
uniform-in-time W1 theorem covering this exact deterministic system, this
iid Normal(0,I_2) datum, and the tagged one-particle law would directly
contradict (2); audit its topology, centering, metric weights, time window,
or added dissipation assumptions before treating it as a falsifier.

This refutes a universal claim over smooth Lipschitz deterministic Vlasov
models. It does not rule out uniform chaos for stable, confining, damped,
noisy, analytic, or otherwise restricted subclasses, nor for time-dependent
metrics that shrink the unstable mode.
