SUPPLIED REGULAR SU(3) GIBBS CARRIER: COMPLETE OPERATIONAL--ENERGY TRANSFER
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. No expert/formal or novelty validation.

Main result. On a supplied irreducible carrier V_(a,b), with cN<=a,b<=CN
for fixed 0<c<=C<infinity, blind recovery of the full weak Gibbs orbit
exp[(h/N)pi(H_g)]/Z through maximum quantum branch dimension Q has optimal
half-trace error comparable to Lambda_(a,b)(Q)/N^2, uniformly over every
Q, every N>=1, and h in a fixed positive compact interval. The constants
are explicit below. This resolves the adjoint-multiplicity operational
gate on this supplied regular carrier; no general-Q rank-energy profile
or iid qutrit-sector transfer is assumed. The exact first posterior
harmonic has two components. For a generic H the second is nonzero at
order h^2, so the scalar moment inference is false even for Gibbs states.

1. PRECISE INTERFACE AND QUANTIFIERS

Fix real constants 0<c<=C<infinity, 0<h0<=h1<infinity, and a nonzero
traceless Hermitian 3-by-3 matrix H. The carrier and H are supplied.
For N>=1 and integers a,b satisfying cN<=a,b<=CN, let

  V=V_(a,b),     d=(a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2,
  U_g=pi_(a,b)(g),       g in SU(3).

Use Hermitian fundamental generators t_A, A=1,...,8, with
Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2, and write

  T_A=d pi(t_A),         H=sum_A x_A t_A,
  r^2=sum_A x_A^2=2 Tr_fund H^2>0,
  T_H=sum_A x_A T_A,     B_g=U_g T_H U_g^dagger/N,
  tau_(h,g)=exp(h B_g)/Z_h,       h in [h0,h1].             (1.1)

Here d pi(t_A) denotes the Hermitian generator convention, rather than
the anti-Hermitian derivative without its factor i. Z_h=Tr exp(h B_e)
is independent of g. H may have any nonzero spectrum. If its three
fundamental eigenvalues are distinct, its orbit is a full flag SU(3)/T^2.
No absence of accidental equal represented-weight energies is needed:
the positive matrix logarithm implies that the stabilizer of tau inside
SU(3) equals that of H, and d pi is injective on the simple Lie algebra.
An ambient U(d) stabilizer must not be mistaken for the SU(3) stabilizer.

One unknown state tau_(h,g) is supplied, not iid samples. The encoder is
blind to g. A legal code is a quantum instrument with arbitrary classical
labels y, quantum memory space K_y of dimension <=Q in every branch, and
a trace-preserving decoder from each K_y back to V. Arbitrary local
ancillas and randomness are allowed; no pre-shared entanglement, quantum
side channel, or parameter-dependent state preparation oracle is allowed.
Classical labels are not charged against Q, and may be unlimited in the
converse. The full output density operator on the same carrier is scored:

  e(Phi;h)=sup_g (1/2)||Phi(tau_(h,g))-tau_(h,g)||_1,
  e*_Q(h)=inf_legal Phi e(Phi;h),
  e*_Q([h0,h1])=inf_legal Phi sup_(h,g)
                  (1/2)||Phi(tau_(h,g))-tau_(h,g)||_1.    (1.2)

This is not preservation of a reference purification, an observable-only
task, a total classical-plus-quantum memory optimum, or inter-carrier
recovery. The same upper code below works for every h in the interval.
The lower also applies to Haar-average error for each fixed h.

Define the Casimir energy and its unrestricted rank minimum by

  E(A)=sum_A ||[T_A,A]||_HS^2,
  Lambda_(a,b)(Q)=min {E(A): rank A<=Q, ||A||_HS=1}.       (1.3)

All matrices are permitted in the minimum; no normality or diagonal
restriction is imposed. The minimum exists by compactness.

2. THEOREM WITH UNIFORM CONSTANTS

Set the following constants before choosing N,a,b,Q:

  K=C^2+2C,       L=c^6/(3K),       C_E=14K+1/2,
  B0=r sqrt(K),      gamma=r^2/8,
  rho=r [1/c+sqrt(C_E)/sqrt(L)],
  c_*=(h0^2 gamma/2)exp(-6h1 B0),
  C_*=(1/4)exp(2h1 B0)[h1 rho+gamma h1^2].               (2.1)

For every instance of Section 1 and every integer 1<=Q<=d,

  c_* Lambda_(a,b)(Q)/N^2 <= e*_Q(h)
                          <= C_* Lambda_(a,b)(Q)/N^2,   (2.2)

uniformly for h in [h0,h1]. The same two bounds hold for e*_Q([h0,h1]).
At Q=d both sides are zero and the identity code attains zero error.
The upper is existential unless an appropriate positive filter is
explicitly supplied. It does not acquire the minimizer of (1.3).

Proof dependencies are given in full in Sections 3--9. Section 10 gives
the corrected posterior, Section 11 the Gibbs-specific failure of a
scalar posterior, and Section 12 an affine control. Section 13 gives the
exact rank-one control Lambda(1)=2(a+b), hence e*_1=Theta(1/N).

3. LEGAL MEMORY IMPLIES RANK-Q COMPOSITE KRAUS OPERATORS

Write a Kraus operator of branch y's encoder as E_(y,alpha):V->K_y
and one of its decoder as D_(y,beta):K_y->V. The overall channel has
composite Kraus operators

  A_(y,alpha,beta)=D_(y,beta)E_(y,alpha),
  rank A_(y,alpha,beta)<=dim K_y<=Q,
  sum_i A_i^dagger A_i=I.                               (3.1)

Consequently sum_i||A_i||_HS^2=d. Branch-dependent decoders, randomness,
and unrestricted classical labels do not change the rank bound. With
continuous labels the sums are integrals of the same positive quantities;
the finite-dimensional score identities extend by integration, or by
finite approximation and closure. There is no unitality assumption on
this arbitrary legal overall channel.

Conversely, any finite trace-preserving Kraus family with rank A_i<=Q
is legal in this model: write A_i=V_i|A_i| by polar decomposition,
encode |A_i| into its support of dimension <=Q and store label i,
then decode by the support isometry V_i. Encoder completeness is
sum_i|A_i|^2=sum_i A_i^dagger A_i=I. Thus the rank constraint describes
the permitted unlimited-label hybrid channel class exactly, not merely
a relaxation for the converse.

This rank-Kraus memory characterization is established prior work:
Bisio--D'Ariano--Perinotti--Sedlak (2012), Section V.2, arXiv:1112.3853v1.
It is recalled for the legal interface, not claimed as new.

4. SELF-SCORE IDENTITY AND GIBBS DIVIDED DIFFERENCES

For fixed h let W_g=d tau_(h,g). By irreducibility,

  int W_g^2 dg=[Tr W_e^2/d]I.                            (4.1)

For any trace-preserving channel with Kraus A_i, expansion of the square
of a commutator gives

  Tr W_e^2-int Tr[W_g Phi(W_g)]dg
        =(1/2)sum_i int ||[W_g,A_i]||_HS^2 dg.           (4.2)

Indeed, the two positive terms of ||W_g A-A W_g||^2 are
Tr(W_g^2 A A^dagger) and Tr(W_g^2 A^dagger A). After Haar averaging,
(4.1) makes both terms [Tr W_e^2/d]||A||_HS^2. Summing uses only
sum_i||A_i||^2=d, which follows from trace preservation. The cross
terms are twice Tr(W_g A W_g A^dagger); this trace is real for Hermitian
W_g. Thus (4.2) does not require sum_i A_i A_i^dagger=I.

The Casimir bounds of Section 6 imply ||B_g||_op<=B0. Write
zeta=Z_h/d. Then exp(-h B0)<=zeta<=exp(h B0), and

  0<=W_g<=M I,              M=exp(2h1 B0).              (4.3)

W_g/M is therefore a legal effect for testing the output state. The
unknown effect need not be physically acquired by the code; it is a
family of witnesses in the converse. Half-trace distance bounds each
such effect discrepancy from above, so averaging (4.2) yields

  e(Phi;h)>=1/(2dM) sum_i int ||[W_g,A_i]||_HS^2 dg.   (4.4)

In an eigenbasis of B_g, each matrix entry of the commutator with W_g is
multiplied by (exp(h b_i)-exp(h b_j))/zeta. The real mean-value theorem
on [-B0,B0], including the equal-eigenvalue limit, gives for every A

  h^2 exp(-4h B0)||[B_g,A]||_HS^2
      <=||[W_g,A]||_HS^2
      <=h^2 exp(4h B0)||[B_g,A]||_HS^2.                 (4.5)

Let R(g) be the real orthogonal adjoint matrix in the t_A basis. Schur
orthogonality of this irreducible adjoint representation gives

  int (R(g)x)_A(R(g)x)_B dg=(r^2/8)delta_AB,
  int ||[B_g,A]||_HS^2 dg=gamma E(A)/N^2.              (4.6)

Thus the positive Gibbs orbit Dirichlet form
E_W(A)=int||[W_g,A]||^2dg is uniformly comparable to E(A)/N^2.
No decomposition of End(V) or posterior moment is used in this step.

Combining (3.1), (4.4)--(4.6), and the weights w_i=||A_i||^2/d gives

  e(Phi;h)>=(h0^2 gamma/2)exp(-6h1 B0)
               sum_i w_i E(A_i)/||A_i||^2 /N^2
            >=c_* Lambda_(a,b)(Q)/N^2.                (4.7)

This proves the lower in (2.2), including arbitrary nonunital codes.
The standalone compact-group version is in general_self_score.txt.

5. A POSITIVE RANK-ENERGY MINIMIZER AND LEGAL FILTER CHANNEL

The scalar carrier Casimir is sum_A T_A^2=C2 I. If
A=U diag(sigma_i)V^dagger is a singular-value decomposition, define
F_L=U diag(sigma_i)U^dagger and F_R=V diag(sigma_i)V^dagger. Directly
expanding the energies in these two bases gives

  E(A)-[E(F_L)+E(F_R)]/2
    =sum_(A,i,j) sigma_i sigma_j
       |(U^dagger T_A U)_ij-(V^dagger T_A V)_ij|^2>=0.  (5.1)

The scalar Casimir cancels the individual square terms. Both moduli are
positive and preserve the rank and HS norm. At least one therefore has
energy <=E(A), so the minimum (1.3) is attained by some positive F.
This does not claim that each modulus individually lowers the energy,
or that every invariant positive Dirichlet form has the same property.

For any nonzero positive F of rank q<=Q, put S_F=Tr F^2 and

  F_g=U_g F U_g^dagger,
  Phi_F(X)=d/S_F int F_g X F_g dg,
  D_F(X)=int [F_g,[F_g,X]]dg.                            (5.2)

Schur's lemma gives int F_g^2 dg=(S_F/d)I, so Phi_F is completely
positive, trace preserving, unital, and covariant. Moreover

  Phi_F-I=-(d/(2S_F))D_F.                               (5.3)

Its Kraus operators factor through supp(F), and thus through q<=Q.
The classical group outcome and finite-label existence are accounted
for in Section 14. Covariance makes its error constant over g for fixed h.

6. TWO ADJOINT COPIES, EXACT GRAM, AND REGULAR-CONE CONDITIONING

In the normalization of Section 1 let

  {t_A,t_B}=delta_AB I/3+d_ABC t_C,
  d_ABC=2Tr_fund({t_A,t_B}t_C),
  D_A=d_ABC T_B T_C,
  C2=(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b)/3,
  C3=(a-b)(2a+b+3)(a+2b+3)/18.                          (6.1)

D_A is Hermitian and transforms as an adjoint tensor. The standard
quadratic and cubic central elements act as C2 I and C3 I, respectively.
Their normalization can also be fixed by the shifted diagonal highest
weight u=a+1,v=b+1:

  l_1=(2u+v)/3, l_2=(-u+v)/3, l_3=(-u-2v)/3,
  C2=(1/2)sum_i l_i^2-1,        C3=(1/2)sum_i l_i^3.    (6.2)

These are the Harish-Chandra central polynomials. The cubic coefficient
is fixed by C3(1,0)=10/9; outer conjugation precludes lower even terms.

The exact representation-independent quartic identity is

  sum_A D_A^2=C2(C2/3+1/4)I.                            (6.3)

Here is a central-element proof, independently checked by the spectral
programme. The left side is central of filtration degree at most four.
For a traceless Hermitian fundamental X=x_A t_A, its leading symbol is

  sum_A[d_ABC x_B x_C]^2=(1/3)(sum_A x_A^2)^2.           (6.4)

To verify (6.4), the represented vector d(x,x) corresponds to
2X^2-(2/3)Tr X^2 I. The traceless 3-by-3 Cayley-Hamilton identity gives
Tr X^4=(1/2)(Tr X^2)^2; taking its squared norm gives (6.4).
After subtracting C2^2/3, the remaining central element has degree <=3.
Invariant polynomials on traceless 3-by-3 matrices are generated by
Tr X^2 and Tr X^3. Outer conjugation X->-X^T preserves the left side
and C2 but negates the cubic invariant, so the cubic coefficient vanishes.
There is no invariant linear term. The trivial representation removes
the constant. The remainder is therefore v C2. In the defining carrier,
the contraction d_ABC d_EBC=(5/3)delta_AE and the anticommutator give
D_A=(5/6)t_A, so sum D_A^2=(25/36)(4/3)I=25I/27. Subtracting
(4/3)^2/3 gives v=1/4. This proves (6.3) as a central identity.

Define

  alpha=C3/C2,
  Delta=C2(C2/3+1/4)-C3^2/C2,
  S_A=(D_A-alpha T_A)/N,
  C_S=Delta/N^2.                                       (6.5)

Invariance and contraction now give the exact ORDINARY-trace identities

  Tr(T_A T_B)=d C2 delta_AB/8,
  Tr(T_A S_B)=0,
  Tr(S_A S_B)=d C_S delta_AB/8,
  sum_A S_A^2=C_S I.                                   (6.6)

The factor d is essential; normalized trace would omit it.
Direct substitution in (6.5) factorizes the Gram determinant:

  C2 Delta=ab(a+2)(b+2)(a+b+1)(a+b+3)/12.               (6.7)

One exact algebraic check uses u=a+1,v=b+1 and S0=u^2+uv+v^2.
For J=(u-v)(2u+v)(u+2v), 4S0^3-J^2=27u^2v^2(u+v)^2.
Then [S0-3]^2[4(S0-3)+9]-J^2 equals
27(u-1)(v-1)(u+1)(v+1)(u+v-1)(u+v+1), proving (6.7).

Consequently Delta>0 for a,b>0, and the two embeddings T_A,S_A are
nonzero and orthogonal. They are ALL the adjoint copies. To prove exact
multiplicity two, tensor (a,b) with 3: the three present terms are
(a+1,b),(a-1,b+1),(a,b-1). Tensoring each with 3bar gives exactly one
copy of (a,b). Since 3 tensor 3bar=1+8, the singlet removes one of
these three copies and the adjoint multiplicity is two. This also
explains the one-copy boundary a=0 or b=0.

The quantitative regular bounds required below follow from (6.1),(6.7):

  c^2 N^2<=C2<=K N^2,
  Delta>=L N^4,       C_S>=L N^2.                       (6.8)

For the second bound the numerator in (6.7) is >=4c^6 N^6,
while C2<=K N^2. Also alpha^2<=C2/3+1/4, because Delta>=0.
By Cauchy-Schwarz and sum T_A^2=C2 I,
||T_H||_op<=r sqrt(C2)<=B0 N. This justifies Section 4's bounded B_g.

7. COMMUTATOR CONTROL OF THE SECOND COPY

For any matrix A, let E_S(A)=sum_A||[S_A,A]||_HS^2. The letter A
used as generator index is distinguished from the matrix by its context.
The estimate is over arbitrary matrices, not merely positive filters.
First use

  [D_A,F]=sum_(B,C) d_ABC Y_BC,
  Y_BC=[T_B,F]T_C+T_B[T_C,F].                           (7.1)

The coefficient map d from the 64 pair coordinates to the 8 adjoint
coordinates has squared operator norm 5/3, since its rows obey
sum_(B,C)d_ABC d_EBC=(5/3)delta_AE. On HS-valued coordinates,

  sum_A||[D_A,F]||^2 <=(5/3)sum_(B,C)||Y_BC||^2
                  <=(20/3)C2 E(F).                     (7.2)

For the last inequality, the sum of ||[T_B,F]T_C||^2 over B,C equals
C2 E(F) by cyclicity and sum_C T_C^2=C2 I. Likewise the sum of
||T_B[T_C,F]||^2 equals C2 E(F). The elementary norm square bound
for their sum contributes a factor two to each, giving 4C2 E(F).

Subtract alpha T_A and divide by N. Equations (6.8) and the bound on
alpha therefore give

  E_S(F)<=[(40/3)C2+2alpha^2]E(F)/N^2
         <=[14K+1/2]E(F)=C_E E(F).                      (7.3)

This makes no claim of a uniform estimate for an arbitrary sequence
approaching a weight wall; Section 8 will also divide by sqrt(C_S).

8. THE COVARIANT DOUBLE-COMMUTATOR BLOCK

D_F is covariant, self-adjoint on HS matrices, and Hermiticity preserving.
By exact adjoint multiplicity two it must have the form

  D_F(T_A)=u T_A+v S_A.                                (8.1)

Its coefficients are real. Invariance of the trace pairings, (6.6), and
integration by parts for the commutator give exactly

  u=E(F)/(d C2),
  v=E_TS(F)/(d C_S),
  E_TS(F)=sum_A Tr([S_A,F]^dagger[T_A,F]).               (8.2)

To see the normalization explicitly, sum Tr(T_A D_F(T_A)) over A;
the result is E(F), and sum Tr(T_A^2)=d C2. Pairing instead with S_A
gives E_TS and denominator d C_S. Simultaneous adjoint rotation of both
families preserves these sums, so the Haar integral changes no coefficient.
For a Hermitian F, E_TS is real. Cauchy-Schwarz and (7.3) imply

  |E_TS(F)|<=sqrt(E_S(F)E(F))<=sqrt(C_E)E(F).            (8.3)

Write S_H=sum_A x_A S_A. Equations (6.6) and Cauchy-Schwarz give
||S_H||_op<=r sqrt(C_S), just as ||T_H||_op<=r sqrt(C2).
Consequently, for B=T_H/N,

  ||D_F(B)||_1
    <=(d/N)[|u|r sqrt(C2)+|v|r sqrt(C_S)]
    <=r E(F)/N [1/sqrt(C2)+sqrt(C_E)/sqrt(C_S)]
    <=rho E(F)/N^2.                                    (8.4)

This linear-in-energy estimate is the full two-copy repair. Dropping v
without proof would be invalid. A square-root-in-energy estimate would
not suffice for the claimed precision law.

9. ORDERED GIBBS SERIES: WHOLE NONCOMMUTING STATE UPPER

Let C_B=(B+B0 I)/(2B0), so 0<=C_B<=I, and put ell=2h B0. The scalar
shift cancels upon normalization:

  tau_(h,e)=exp(ell C_B)/Z_C,         Z_C>=d.             (9.1)

Equations (8.4), (4.6) give

  ||D_F(C_B)||_1<=c1 E(F)/N^2,      c1=rho/(2B0),
  int||[F_g,C_B]||_HS^2dg=c2 E(F)/N^2,
                                      c2=gamma/(4B0^2). (9.2)

For the second identity, conjugate F_g back to F and integrate the
orbit of B; (4.6) supplies the exact second moment.

For every integer k>=1, apply the derivation delta_g(X)=[F_g,X] twice
to C_B^k. Terms differentiating a single factor give
sum_(i=0)^(k-1) C_B^i delta_g^2(C_B) C_B^(k-1-i).
Terms differentiating two distinct factors give
2 sum_(0<=i<j<=k-1) C_B^i delta_g(C_B) C_B^(j-i-1)
                                      delta_g(C_B) C_B^(k-1-j).
After integration the first class has trace norm <=k||D_F(C_B)||_1.
Each ordered cross term has trace norm <=||delta_g(C_B)||_HS^2 by
Schatten Holder and ||C_B||_op<=1. Thus

  ||D_F(C_B^k)||_1
       <=[c1 k+c2 k(k-1)]E(F)/N^2.                     (9.3)

There is no commutativity requirement on D_F(C_B) and C_B. This handles
the entire density matrix, including its higher harmonics and all their
noncommuting operator products; no state-level estimate is inferred
from a first moment alone.

Summing the norm-convergent exponential series yields

  ||D_F(exp(ell C_B))||_1
     <=exp(ell)[c1 ell+c2 ell^2]E(F)/N^2.               (9.4)

Using (5.3), (9.1) and the half-trace convention,

  (1/2)||Phi_F(tau)-tau||_1
    <=exp(2h B0)[h rho+gamma h^2]E(F)/(4S_F N^2)
    <=C_* [E(F)/S_F]/N^2.                              (9.5)

Choose the positive minimizer supplied by (5.1). This proves the upper
in (2.2). The derivation did not assume the regular-carrier rank profile,
a coherent optimizer, a Gaussian approximation, or an iid sector limit.

10. EXACT POSTERIOR: BOTH ADJOINT COMPONENTS

Let x_A(g)=2Tr_fund(t_A g H g^dagger)=(R(g)x)_A, and let

  M_A=int x_A(g)tau_(h,g)dg,
  mu_T=Tr(tau_(h,e)T_H),       mu_S=Tr(tau_(h,e)S_H).    (10.1)

Schur orthogonality and the complete orthogonal embeddings (6.6) give

  M_A=(1/d)[(mu_T/C2)T_A+(mu_S/C_S)S_A].                (10.2)

For example, the T component of tau_e is
sum_A [8Tr(tau_e T_A)/(dC2)]T_A. Multiplying its rotation by x_A(g)
and integrating R_AB R_CD=delta_AC delta_BD/8 gives the first term
in (10.2); the S term is identical. Every other isotypic component
integrates to zero against this adjoint matrix coefficient.

The Haar state average is I/d. Therefore for any nonzero POVM effect E,
the EXACT posterior first moment under the uniform orbit prior is

  E[x_A(g)|E]
     =[(mu_T/C2)Tr(E T_A)+(mu_S/C_S)Tr(E S_A)]/Tr E.     (10.3)

The coefficients are finite traces of supplied carrier matrices. They
do not require an acquired posterior oracle or an asymptotic Gaussian
model. If h has an additional specified prior, average the two
coefficients over that prior; do not silently use a supplied h.
Neither (10.2) nor (10.3) is used in the converse of Section 4.

11. GIBBS-SPECIFIC SCALAR-INFERENCE FAILURE

The invariant tensor Tr(S_A T_B T_C) has zero antisymmetric B,C part,
since Tr(S_A[T_B,T_C])=i f_BCD Tr(S_A T_D)=0. The invariant triple
tensor space has dimension two: apply Section 6's adjoint-multiplicity
proof to V_(1,1)=8. The independent tensors d_ABC and f_ABC span it.
Symmetry in B,C removes f, so the remaining part is a scalar multiple
of d_ABC. Contracting with d and using
D_A=N S_A+alpha T_A fixes the scalar:

  Tr(S_A T_B T_C)=[3d Delta/(40N)]d_ABC.                (11.1)

Indeed sum d_ABC^2=40/3, while sum_A Tr(S_A D_A)=d Delta/N.
Since d_ABC x_A x_B x_C=4Tr_fund H^3, this gives

  Tr(S_H T_H^2)=3d Delta Tr_fund H^3/(10N).             (11.2)

The zeroth and first Taylor coefficients of mu_S(h) vanish by (6.6),
but the second is

  mu_S(h)=3h^2 Delta Tr_fund H^3/(20N^3)+O(h^3).        (11.3)

More precisely mu_S(h)/N has this quadratic coefficient divided by N,
and a remainder O_(c,C,H)(h^3) uniformly over all regular instances
for h in any fixed small compact interval. This follows directly from
||B||<=B0, ||S_H||/N<=r sqrt[K(K/3+1/4)], and the exponential Taylor
remainder. The normalization Z/d has constant term one and zero linear
term because Tr B=0.

For Tr_fund H^3!=0 and a,b>0, (11.3) is nonzero at sufficiently small
positive h; it is not an identity-zero coefficient for the Gibbs family.
Analyticity then gives nonzero mu_S for generic positive h. As a concrete
small carrier take (a,b)=(1,1), N=1, H=diag(2,-1/2,-3/2). Here
C2=3, C3=0, Delta=15/4, Tr H^3=9/2, and

  mu_S(h)=(81/32)h^2+O(h^3).                            (11.4)

Thus an inference M_A=kappa T_A from covariance alone is false for an
actual weak Gibbs orbit, not merely for an arbitrary manufactured state.
This refutes that inference; it does not refute the repaired theorem.
The nonzero quadratic term does not assert nonvanishing at every h.

12. EQUALLY INFORMED AFFINE ORBIT CONTROL

For 0<h B0<1, let tau_aff,g=(I+h B_g)/d. This is positive. Its exact
posterior moment is the special case of (10.2) with

  mu_T=(h/N)C2 r^2/8,        mu_S=0.                     (12.1)

For any legal channel use the effect (B_g+B0 I)/(2B0). The Haar mean
of B_g is zero and its squared Haar mean is scalar. The same commutator
expansion as Section 4 gives the exact averaged affine score deficit

  int Tr[(B_g+B0 I)/(2B0)(tau_aff,g-Phi(tau_aff,g))]dg
       =h/(4B0 d)sum_i int||[B_g,A_i]||^2dg.             (12.2)

This again needs only trace preservation, not unitality. Consequently
the affine optimum obeys

  [h gamma/(4B0)]Lambda(Q)/N^2 <= e*_aff,Q(h)
                          <=[h rho/4]Lambda(Q)/N^2.    (12.3)

The upper uses the same unital filter channel and (8.4). This control
confirms the linear orbit regime and pinpoints the new Gibbs component.
It is not substituted for the Gibbs theorem. Constants in (2.2) are
not claimed optimal as h approaches zero; h0>0 is a fixed assumption.

13. EXACT RANK-ONE CONTROL AND CLASSICAL-ONLY CONSEQUENCE

For a unit vector psi and P=|psi><psi|,

  E(P)=2[C2-sum_A <psi,T_A psi>^2].                     (13.1)

Let the real mean vector be mu_A=<T_A>. Rotate it to a Cartan using
the adjoint action, since a traceless Hermitian fundamental matrix can
be diagonalized. On the Cartan, the mean is a convex combination of
carrier weights. The weight set is inside conv(Weyl orbit of lambda),
and hence its norm is at most ||lambda||. Equivalently, the support
function of this convex hull bounds mu dotted with its own direction
by ||mu||||lambda||. Thus ||mu||<=||lambda||. The highest-weight vector
attains equality. In the present normalization

  ||lambda||^2=(a^2+ab+b^2)/3,
  C2-||lambda||^2=a+b.                                  (13.2)

The positive rank-one minimum is therefore 2(a+b). The two-modulus
inequality (5.1) reduces the unrestricted rank-one problem to this
positive problem with unchanged norm, so

  Lambda_(a,b)(1)=2(a+b).                              (13.3)

Under cN<=a,b<=CN, (2.2) proves e*_1=Theta_(c,C,H,h0,h1)(1/N).
The upper is the known covariant highest-weight measure-and-prepare
filter; it needs no rank-energy optimizer. This is a fully classical
memory comparator, with finite classical labels as below. It already
shows a nontrivial recovery improvement over constant-error guesses.
No corresponding fixed-Q>1 profile is asserted without its own proof.

14. CLASSICAL LABELS, OPTIMIZER ACQUISITION, AND PRECISION COSTS

For a given F, the Hermitian Choi orbit
J_g=|vec(F_g)><vec(F_g)| lives in a real vector space of dimension d^4.
Its Haar average lies in the convex hull of the compact orbit.
Caratheodory gives positive weights p_y, sum p_y=1, and at most d^4+1
group nodes g_y reproducing this average EXACTLY. Scaling by d/S_F
therefore reproduces the entire Phi_F channel, including trace
preservation and covariance, not just its action on the Gibbs family.

Node y has composite Kraus sqrt(d p_y/S_F)F_(g_y). Factor it as
encoder sqrt(d p_y/S_F)F U_(g_y)^dagger into supp(F), followed by the
decoder isometry U_(g_y) from that support back into V. Their encoder
effects sum to I. Each branch has dimension q<=Q. The upper needs

  at most d^4+1 classical labels,
  ceil(log2(d^4+1)) classical bits,
  ceil(log2 Q) quantum bits,
  total direct-sum memory dimension <=Q(d^4+1).          (14.1)

Here d=Theta_(c,C)(N^3), so the label bound is 12log2 N+O_(c,C)(1)
bits. The converse permits unlimited labels. This is not a statement
of optimum total hybrid memory or optimum labels.

Caratheodory is an existence theorem, not an explicit cubature-selection
algorithm. The group nodes and weights, the positive minimizing F, and
a precision-certified gate implementation remain acquisition tasks.
Optimizing (1.3) is nonconvex. A dense ideal carrier realization has
polynomial dimension in N for fixed SU(3), but that does not establish
polynomial-time minimizer acquisition or well-conditioned control.
Unlike the previously banked symmetric CP orbit, this note supplies
no explicit phase/Jacobi cubature for the regular flag filter. These
two finite-label assertions must be kept distinct.

If an implemented overall channel is certified within eta_impl in
half diamond norm of Phi_F, its recovery error increases by at most
eta_impl. To preserve a matching asymptotic order requires
eta_impl=o(Lambda(Q)/N^2). No gate-count/conditioning bound at that
accuracy is established. The theorem is about ideal finite-dimensional
channels. Its existential all-Q comparator is a mathematical optimum,
not an efficient all-Q compiler.

15. BOUNDARIES, FALSIFIERS, AND UNRESOLVED GATES

- Both Dynkin coordinates are bounded below by cN. Although Delta>0
  for every regular a,b, sqrt(C_S) can degenerate relative to N near
  a wall. The constants here do not control such sequences. The exact
  one-copy wall can be handled separately, but no uniform crossover
  theorem is claimed by this regular-cone proof.
- The Gibbs exponent is h pi(H)/N with h in a fixed compact positive
  interval and supplied H. Growing inverse temperature or a direction
  generator whose norm grows with N changes the divided-difference
  constants. A strongly polarized transverse boson limit is separate.
- The output remains V_(a,b). A code that transports across distinct
  Schur sectors or irreps needs an independent score/comparison theorem.
  The theorem is not automatically an iid qutrit collision result.
- Lambda's regular-SU3 small-rank and near-full profiles are representation
  and variational questions. A dimension d=Theta(N^3), or a flag of
  dimension six, does not by itself prove a Q exponent 1/3.
- Exact minimizing filters, leading error constants, optimal label count,
  explicit full-flag cubature, efficient optimizer acquisition, and
  precision-certified circuits remain unresolved.
- Testable load-bearing falsifiers are failure of the nonunital identity
  (4.2), Gibbs divided differences (4.5), exact quartic Gram (6.3),
  multiplicity two in Section 6, normalized u,v in (8.2), or the ordered
  power trace bound (9.3). Each is proved above; finite checks below
  are only normalization diagnostics.
