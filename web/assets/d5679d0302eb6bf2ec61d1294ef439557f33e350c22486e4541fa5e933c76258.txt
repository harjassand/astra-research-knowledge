1. EXACT THEOREM AND CONVENTIONS

There is a universal alpha in (0,1/2) with the following property. For every
d,ell>0 there is a finite C_{d,ell} such that, simultaneously for every h>0,
every K subset h Z^2, every pole z in h Z^2 with dist(z,K)>=d, every x in R^2,
and every r>=0,

  P_z(T_K<infinity, X_{T_K} in K intersect closed B(x,r))
       <= min{1, C_{d,ell}(r+h)^alpha}.                         (1.1)

Here X is the ordinary discrete-time simple nearest-neighbor random walk on
h Z^2, and T_K=inf{t>=0:X_t in K}. The sole geometric assumption is:
for every v in K there is a finite sequence of actual K sites containing v,
with successive Euclidean distances <=2h and diameter >=ell. A contained
anchor path whose successive ambient graph distances are <=2 satisfies this.
There is no killing at intermediate sites between successive anchor sites.

The same bound holds with T_K<tau_D in place of T_K<infinity, for every
arbitrary subset D of h Z^2 containing the pole, where
tau_D=inf{t>=0:X_t notin D}. In particular D may be any finite rough domain.
The steps retain probabilities 1/4 in the four coordinate directions; a step
outside D kills the walk. This is not an induced-domain walk with its moves
renormalized, nor a probability conditioned on surviving or hitting K.

The assumptions and conclusion are deterministic in K,D. Thus they also hold
quenched for random sets whenever the hypotheses hold for the realized sets.
No independence, stationarity, domain regularity, or ambient-volume bound is
used. All sites of the target K are anchored; arbitrary isolated additional
target sites are not admitted by the stated global hypothesis.

In fact the proof of the estimate for one target ball needs only one v in
K intersect B(x,r) with such an anchor and |z-v|>=d. Arbitrary other sites of K
then help absorption, including other sites in that same target ball.

2. ONLY PRIMARY ANALYTIC INPUT AND UNIVERSAL CONSTANTS

Let S be simple NN walk on Z^2. Write c=2/pi and a for its potential kernel.
The permitted primary source is Gregory F. Lawler and Vlada Limic,
Random Walk: A Modern Introduction, author-hosted PDF:
  https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~lawler/srwbook.pdf
Pinned local bytes: PRIMARY_LAWLER_LIMIC_srwbook.pdf,
SHA256 cb10ab9e665913f5041fd12511c2492c41d3e4bbebcf2d6ba7162c78acfbd9ea.
Exact used statements: Proposition 4.4.2 (printed p87), Theorem 4.4.4 (p88),
and Proposition 4.6.2(b) (p97). Theorem 4.4.4 includes the bipartite NN walk.

These statements give a(0)=0, (P-I)a=1_{0}, and a(z)=c log|z|+c_0+O(|z|^-2)
as |z| tends to infinity. Consequently the following exact universal number
is finite:

  A=max{1, sup_{z in Z^2}|a(z)-c log(|z|+1)|}.                 (2.1)

For any finite set F and y,w in F, with Green function counting visits at
times 0<=t<tau_F, Proposition 4.6.2(b) gives

  G_F(y,w)=E_y a(S_{tau_F}-w)-a(y-w).                         (2.2)

Optional stopping of a(S_t) is therefore legal on a finite annulus avoiding
0: it is bounded on the annulus and its one-step enlargement. No Green
formula on an unbounded recurrent domain is used.

Define fixed universal real constants, using natural logarithms:

  L=8 exp((2A+2)/c),
  U=c log(L+4)+2A,     W=c log(L+2)+2A,
  H=max{4, (2/L) exp(2WU/c)},
  N_0=ceil(max{24,H}),
  C_1=c[log(12(L+4))+2]+2A,
  p_0=1/(C_1+2),      q_0=1-p_0,
  alpha=-log(q_0)/log H.                                    (2.3)

They are deliberately inefficient, but finite and independent of every
instance. In particular 0<p_0<1/2, H>=4, and 0<alpha<1/2. No numerical bound
on the remainder in the primary asymptotic theorem is needed for existence.

3. RADIAL SELECTION USES ONLY ACTUAL K SITES

Fix an anchored v and a physical radius 0<R<=ell/8, with N=R/h>=N_0.
Translate v to 0 and divide coordinates by h. Since the finite anchor has
diameter >=ell, some anchor site is at distance >=ell/2 from v: apply the
triangle inequality to a pair realizing its diameter. Follow the portion of
the anchor from v to that site. It reaches radial distance 2R, and each hop
changes radial distance by at most 2h.

Put m=floor(N/12), n=m+1, and thresholds t_j=3N/2+6j, 0<=j<=m.
Choose w_j as the first anchor site on this portion whose radius is >=t_j.
The preceding site has radius <t_j, whence

  t_j<=|w_j|<=t_j+2,   3N/2<=|w_j|<=2N+2,
  |w_j-w_k|>=||w_j|-|w_k||>=6|j-k|-2>=4|j-k|  (j!=k),
  N/12<=n.                                                  (3.1)

Thus these are distinct actual killed sites. Let S_N={w_0,...,w_m}.
We never insert a NN connector, thicken K, or replace the anchor by a curve.

4. GREEN LOWER BOUND IN A FINITE ANNULUS

Let F_N={z in Z^2:|z|<LN}, I_N={z:|z|<=N/H}, E_N=F_N\I_N.
Write tau for exit from F_N and T for first entry to I_N. The incoming
starting states considered below satisfy N-1<=|y|<=N. Since N>=N_0 and
H>=4, they belong to E_N. Every w in S_N also belongs to E_N.

At exit from F_N, LN<=|S_tau|<=LN+1. Thus, for w in S_N,
  |S_tau-w|+1 >=(L-3)N,
whereas |y-w|+1<=4N, because N>=3. Equations (2.1),(2.2) give

  G_{F_N}(y,w)>=c log((L-3)/4)-2A>=2.                       (4.1)

The final inequality follows from L>=6 and (L-3)/4>=L/8.
For every z in I_N and w in S_N, |z-w|>=N because H>=4, and
|S_tau-w|+1<=(L+4)N because N>=2. Therefore

  G_{F_N}(z,w)<=c log(L+4)+2A=U.                           (4.2)

To estimate q=P_y(T<tau), stop a(S_t) at T wedge tau. On inner entry,
a(S_T)<=M_in=c log(N/H+1)+A. On outer exit,
a(S_tau)<=M_out=c log(LN+2)+A. At the incoming state,
a(y)>=c log N-A. Optional stopping yields

  a(y)<=q M_in+(1-q)M_out,
  q<=[M_out-a(y)]/[M_out-M_in]
    <=W/[c log(LH/2)]<=1/(2U).                             (4.3)

Indeed N>=H implies N/H+1<=2N/H; hence the denominator is at least
c log(LH/2). Also LN+2<=(L+2)N bounds the numerator by W. The choice of H
in (2.3) implies c log(LH/2)>=2WU. Inner and outer exit events are disjoint,
their probabilities sum to one, and stopping is a.s. finite since E_N is
finite. This computation does not assume monotone radial motion.

Splitting the ball Green function at first inner entry by strong Markov gives

  G_{E_N}(y,w)=G_{F_N}(y,w)
            -E_y[1_{T<tau}G_{F_N}(S_T,w)]
           >=2-q U>=3/2>=1.                               (4.4)

All stopping and Green functions in this step are in finite lattice sets.

5. UNIFORM LOCAL-TIME HIT CHANCE, WITH FREE CONNECTORS

First bound the expected total visits to S_N from any w_j in S_N before
exit from E_N. By Green monotonicity and (2.1),(2.2),(3.1),

  G_{E_N}(w_j,w_k)<=G_{F_N}(w_j,w_k)
      <=c log((L+4)N/(|w_j-w_k|+1))+2A
      <=c log(12(L+4)n/(|j-k|+1))+2A.                      (5.1)

For the last inequality use N<=12n and |w_j-w_k|+1>=|j-k|+1, including
j=k. The diagonal logarithm is retained, not discarded. At most two k's
give each value of |j-k|+1, so

  sum_{k=0}^{m} log(n/(|j-k|+1))
     <=2 sum_{s=1}^{n}log(n/s)<=2n.                         (5.2)

The latter inequality follows from log(n!)>=n log n-n, obtained by
integrating log t on [1,n]; it is also valid for n=1. Thus

  max_{w in S_N} E_w[number of visits to S_N before exit from E_N]
       <=C_1 n.                                           (5.3)

On the other hand (4.4) gives the expected count from incoming y at least n.
Let T_{S_N} be first hit of the actual selected sites. All visits occur at
or after that stopping time. Strong Markov there, retaining its initial
visit, and (5.3) imply

  n<=E_y[number of visits to S_N before exit from E_N]
    <=P_y(T_{S_N}<T wedge tau) C_1 n.

Consequently P_y(T_{S_N}<T wedge tau)>=1/C_1>=p_0.
Since S_N is a subset of the original K, this event implies T_K<T.
Therefore, in the full plane, even allowing every later outward excursion,

  P_y(T_{closed B(0,N/H)}<T_K)<=1-p_0=q_0.                 (5.4)

The local trial stops at its first outer exit, but (5.4) does not. A trial
hit of S_N already prevents any future successful inward entry before K.
On the complement, including trial outer exit followed by any number of
returns, the future inward success probability is bounded by 1. This is
why the fixed hit chance, rather than a bound only on inward crossing
before outer exit, handles repeated outward excursions.

If the incoming state itself lies in K, the left side of (5.4) is zero.
Thus the inequality is uniform at every possible first-entry state.

6. ITERATE INWARD FIRST ENTRIES AND KEEP THE MESH CUTOFF

Suppose K intersect B(x,r) is nonempty; choose an anchored v in this set.
Then all its target sites lie in closed B(v,2r), and |z-v|>=d. Set

  R_* = min{d/2,ell/8},   s=max{4r,N_0 h}.                  (6.1)

First work in the full plane. If s<R_*, let
J=floor(log(R_*/s)/log H), R_j=R_* H^{-j}, 0<=j<=J,
and t_j=inf{t>=0:|X_t-v|<=R_j}. The pole is outside R_*.
At each first entry, the NN step gives R_j-h<|X_{t_j}-v|<=R_j.
Moreover R_J>=s and

  R_J-h>=s(1-1/N_0)>s/2>=2r.                              (6.2)

Hence any path whose first K hit is in the target must have t_J<T_K, not
merely t_J<=T_K. All preceding first entries also occur strictly before K.
For 0<=j<J, we have R_j>=H s>=N_0 h and R_j<=ell/8, so (5.4) applies at
t_j to the next inner entry t_{j+1}. Strong Markov and the possibility of
infinite entry times give

  P_z(t_{j+1}<T_K)<=q_0 P_z(t_j<T_K),
  P_z(first K hit in target)<=P_z(t_J<T_K)<=q_0^J.           (6.3)

If J=0 this is simply the probability bound by 1. If s>=R_* the same trivial
bound is sufficient. Using J>=log(R_*/s)/log H-1 in the nontrivial case,
and enlarging the bound in the trivial case, gives in every case

  P_z(first K hit in target)
     <=min{1,q_0^{-1}(s/R_*)^alpha}
     <=min{1,q_0^{-1}(N_0/R_*)^alpha (r+h)^alpha}.           (6.4)

Here max{4r,N_0h}<=N_0(r+h). This proves (1.1) with the explicit choice
C_{d,ell}=q_0^{-1}(N_0/min{d/2,ell/8})^alpha. Empty target sets have
probability zero. The case r=0 is included; its cutoff is N_0h. Coarse
meshes and large radii are covered by the trivial branch of (6.4).

For every fixed D, couple the killed walk to this same full-plane walk up
to tau_D. The event {T_K<tau_D, X_{T_K} in target} is a subset of the
full-plane target event. Thus extra exterior killing only reduces the
unconditional probability, with exactly the same constants. No regularity
or connectedness of D enters this comparison.

7. SCOPE, COST, AND THE DOWNSTREAM EXPONENT INTERFACE

This proves a positive, unoptimized universal power exponent from exactly
the actual <=2h anchors, using only the three primary kernel statements.
Its constants are huge; no claim to the sharp Beurling exponent 1/2 is made.
The proof is symbolic, not a scan or a numerical fit. It needs finitely many
actual witness sites per annulus, n=floor((R/h)/12)+1, and O(log(R_*/h))
inward stopping levels. No representation, area, or ambient-size factor is
hidden in the probability constant. It also requires no moment assumption
on a random ambient field and no control of conditional survival laws.

For the supplied downstream interface that asks only for some p in (1,2)
with p>2-alpha, choose p=2-alpha/2. This is arithmetic; availability and use
of that p-Sobolev theorem remain a separate supplied premise. This document
does not prove a random-field limit, SLE statement, or source final theorem.

Historical priority of this anchor-only formulation is unverified. The
mechanism is a standard potential-theoretic local-time argument applied
to radially separated actual anchor sites; the present contribution is its
explicit fit to the required killing/rough-domain/return-excursion interface.
