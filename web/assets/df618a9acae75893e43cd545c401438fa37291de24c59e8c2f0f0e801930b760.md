# Candidate polynomial strong hitting lists for affine pencils in every characteristic

Status: direct theorem candidate with full construction; q-Newton expansion, every-root symbol-division lifting and degree bounds, finite-field/semisimple specialization, and the operational rational-formula consequence have received independent adversarial passes. No external correctness, historical novelty, or whole-source certification is claimed. Audit notes are linked below. A subsequent integer-list corollary removes the characteristic input entirely; see UNIVERSAL_INTEGRAL_STRONG_LIST.md.

## Exact theorem

There is a deterministic machine which, given a promised prime p in binary and n,B>=1 in unary, prints a polynomial-size list of n-tuples of square matrices over F_p in bit time polynomial in n+B+log p. The same list has the following uniform property over **every** coefficient field F of characteristic p. The cases B=0 (empty family) or n=0 (constant pencils) have a trivial dimension-one output.

For every family of at most B affine pencils

    L_j(x)=A_(j,0)+sum_(i=1)^n A_(j,i)x_i,

of orders at most B, if each pencil admits an invertible matrix evaluation over an extension of F, one tuple in the list makes all pencils simultaneously invertible. All dimensions, entry bits, and the complete dense list size are polynomially bounded. No pencil or coefficient is input to the generator.

Feasibility is a supplied promise, not an acquired fact. The construction does not ask for the individual witnesses. This theorem would extend the characteristic-zero strong affine-pencil conclusion of OpenAI's *Polynomial Hitting Lists for Noncommutative Rational Formulas* to every characteristic, including p=2 and arbitrary extension-field coefficients.

## 1. Uniform near-full-rank test

Use the q-Newton reversal construction proved in `FOLDED_NEWTON_EXPANDER.md`, with w=n+1, Delta=4w^3B, and M the smallest power of 2 larger than B Delta for p!=2 or smallest power of 3 for p=2. Set N=M-Delta. Thus M is prime to p and M is polynomial in n,B.

The explicit square matrices R_i(q,eta) over F_p[q,eta], i=0,...,n, have a padded common order M. Every feasible order-r<=B pencil satisfies

    rank(sum_i A_i tensor R_i) >=rN>(r-1)M

over F(q,eta). Coefficients of R_i can be constructed by triangular monic Newton-basis conversion, eta weighting, reversal, and padding; this is polynomial exact arithmetic.

For a conservative degree bound, each Newton polynomial has q-degree at most M(w+M), and the unit triangular inverse coordinate matrix has q-degree at most M^2(w+M). Hence every R_i entry has q-degree at most 2M^2(w+M), eta-degree<M. Any larger explicit polynomial bound can be used below.

## 2. Symbol-division lift over extension parameters

For any primitive M-th root omega in an algebraic extension and any field K containing it, the source's Lemma 3.1 Fourier lift works when M is prime to char K:

    R_i^*(q,eta,u,v)=sum_(a,b=0)^(M-1) c_(i,a,b)(q,eta) u^a v^b D^a S^b,
    c_(i,a,b)=M^(-1) sum_(j=0)^(M-1) omega^(-aj) (R_i)_(j,j-b).

Here D e_j=omega^j e_j and S e_j=e_(j+1), cyclically. It specializes to R_i at u=v=1. The same ordered-monomial proof shows that the central localization generated by uD,vS is a division algebra of degree M. Block-matrix ranks inside it are multiples of M. Therefore the preceding strict near-full-rank inequality implies that every feasible pencil has

    P_(L,omega)=det(sum_i A_i tensor R_i^*) !=0

over F(omega)(q,eta,u,v). The constant pencil L=1 implies det R_0^*!=0.

Choose, for example,

    D0=10 B M^3(w+M+1).

This safely bounds total degree in q,eta,u,v for every determinant of an order-rM<=BM lifted pencil. The field coefficients may be arbitrary in F; neither their representation nor heights enter this polynomial degree statement.

## 3. A polynomial grid and a separable coefficient algebra

Let rho=deg Phi_M<=M, with Phi_M the integral cyclotomic polynomial reduced modulo p. Because M is prime to p, Phi_M is separable and every root is primitive of exact order M. It can be constructed deterministically by the usual exact polynomial divisions of z^M-1 (or directly for a prime power), with polynomial cost.

Choose an integer H>=max(16,rho(B+1)D0). The extra constraint is the constant-pencil determinant R_0^*. The product of all determinant constraints over all rho choices of omega is nonzero, with total parameter degree at most H.

If p>H^2, let E=F_p, e=1, and use the grid G={0,...,H^2}. No large output enumeration of F_p is required.

If p<=H^2, choose the least e with Q=p^e>H^2. Then Q<=pH^2<=H^4 and e<=2log_2H+1. Construct E=F_Q by enumerating all Q monic degree-e polynomials over F_p and testing irreducibility exactly. Irreducibility testing uses repeated modular exponentiation and polynomial gcds; its cost is polynomial in e,log p. Existence of degree-e irreducibles over F_p guarantees that enumeration terminates. Here Q itself is polynomial in H, so this avoids assuming a general deterministic irreducible-polynomial algorithm polynomial in e,log p. Use G=E.

In both cases the grid cardinality |G| is polynomial in H and

    |G|>eH.

For the small-p case this follows from Q>H^2 and 2log_2H+1<H for H>=16. The large-p case has e=1 and |G|=H^2+1.

Now work in the explicit finite-dimensional algebra

    A=E[omega]/Phi_M(omega).

It need not be factored. Its dimension over F_p is e rho, and it is separable: after base change to an algebraic closure of any F it is a product of e rho copies of that closure. The branches correspond to the e embeddings sigma_j of E, which are Frobenius maps, and the rho primitive M-th roots of unity. All arithmetic in A uses coefficient arrays and exact modular polynomial reduction.

For each grid point (q,eta,u,v) in G^4, construct the R_i^* over A, replacing every scalar in A by its multiplication matrix on an F_p basis. This gives ordinary square F_p matrices of order d=M e rho. If the resulting R_0^* is singular, discard the grid point. Otherwise output

    X_i=(R_0^*)^(-1) R_i^*,  1<=i<=n,

using ordinary exact Gaussian elimination over F_p. This construction prints a polynomial number of dense matrix tuples and charges every entry and elimination operation.

## 4. Why one grid point works in every branch simultaneously

Fix a family of at most B feasible pencils over an arbitrary F. For every root omega, all the determinant polynomials are nonzero by the symbol-division argument. A nonzero polynomial of total degree at most D0 vanishes at no more than D0 |G|^3 points of G^4 when G is viewed in an algebraic closure containing E and F (the elementary Schwartz–Zippel count; no randomness is used).

In branch j, a grid point is sent to (sigma_j(q),sigma_j(eta),sigma_j(u),sigma_j(v)). Each sigma_j is a bijection of G in the finite-field case. In the large-p case there is only the identity embedding. Therefore each branch has the same upper bound D0 |G|^3 for its bad grid points, even though F-coefficients need not be fixed by sigma_j.

There are e rho branches and at most B+1 constraints. The union of all bad points has size at most

    e rho (B+1)D0 |G|^3 <=eH |G|^3 < |G|^4.

Hence some grid point makes every constraint nonzero on every branch. At that point R_0^* is invertible and retained. The original affine evaluation factors as

    L(X)=(I_r tensor (R_0^*)^(-1)) (sum_i A_i tensor R_i^*).

The two right factors are invertible on every semisimple branch. Therefore their ordinary base-field matrix representations are invertible after scalar extension to F. The retained output tuple simultaneously hits all pencils.

The argument never treats a nonzero element of a coefficient ring as automatically invertible. It explicitly enforces all separable branches and pays for their number.

## 5. Arbitrary rational formulas: the standard domain bridge

The source's own existential division lift starts from a witness dimension d and chooses a primitive d-th root; that argument cannot simply be copied when p divides d. A standard, separately checked bridge bypasses it.

Hrubeš and Wigderson, *Non-Commutative Arithmetic Circuits with Division*, Theory of Computing 11(14),2015, Theorem2.1, defines the free skew field operationally over an arbitrary field F: correctness is equivalent to having a nonempty matrix-evaluation domain, and equality of correct expressions is equivalent to agreement on all common matrix domains. Thus an admissible expression Phi with some defined nonzero matrix value represents a nonzero free-skew-field element; the expression Phi^(-1) is correct.

Their Proposition7.3 says an affine pencil invertible over the free skew field admits an invertible matrix evaluation (over an extension; passing to F(t) if needed makes the coefficient field infinite). The proof takes a simultaneous matrix evaluation of every entry of its formal inverse. It has no characteristic-zero or witness-index-root-of-unity assumption.

The released source's Section4 affine realization and domain certificate are algebraic block identities valid over every field. A size-s formula yields at most s+1 pencils, each of order at most 2s+1: the pencils at inverse-node occurrences enforce the ORIGINAL expression's domain, and the root augmented pencil J_Phi has Schur complement -Phi. For a nonzero admissible expression, every such pencil is invertible over the free skew field and hence feasible by Proposition7.3. Apply the universal strong list with B=2s+1. One retained tuple makes every domain pencil and J_Phi invertible. The same block identities then imply that every inverse occurrence of the original expression is defined, and its final value is invertible.

Consequently the construction gives the candidate corollary: from p,n,s alone, generate a polynomial-size list of ordinary F_p matrix tuples which gives a defined invertible value for **every nonzero admissible size-s noncommutative rational formula over every field of characteristic p**. Arbitrary inverse nesting and arbitrary coefficient-field constants are allowed. The generator receives no expression or constants. Its polynomial-bit cost excludes execution of the unknown formula over an unspecified coefficient-field representation.

Exact primary source: https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v011a014/v011a014.pdf , Theorem2.1 (page5), Proposition7.1/Corollary7.2 (pages21–22), Proposition7.3 (pages22–23). Local text `sources/hw2015.txt` provides the complete statements and proof. An independent operational-domain audit passed at `cyclic_shift_audit/OPERATIONAL_RATIONAL_GATE_AUDIT.md`; the pencil theorem itself does not require the rational-expression bridge.

The simpler safe corollary is identity/singularity testing for a supplied feasible-pencil family under exact full matrix or rank-output service. Neither an unknown formula's coefficients nor a useful physical oracle is acquired by this construction. Arithmetic evaluation over an unspecified coefficient field still has its own representation/bit obligations.
