Cycle 04 prior-literature comparison: finite-family broadcasting and one common EB reconstruction
Date of source check: 2026-10-09

Scope and exact candidate interface
-----------------------------------
Let H be finite-dimensional and E={rho_i}_{i=1}^n a finite family of states on H. Define C(E)=max_p chi(p,E), where chi(p,E)=S(sum_i p_i rho_i)-sum_i p_i S(rho_i), with logarithms in nats. Let b(E) be the infimum over one CPTP map B:L(H)->L(H tensor H) of max over i and a in {1,2} of (1/2)||Tr_{other a} B(rho_i)-rho_i||_1. Let e(E) be the infimum over one entanglement-breaking CPTP map Lambda:L(H)->L(H) of max_i (1/2)||Lambda(rho_i)-rho_i||_1. The current candidate endpoint is

  e(E) <= min{1, 400 sqrt((C(E)+1)/ln(1/b(E)))} for 0<b(E)<1,
  and b(E)=0 implies e(E)=0.

The target is a dimension- and label-count-independent modulus in the max-prior Holevo capacity. It asks for one EB channel working in worst-state trace distance for the entire family, from approximate two-output marginal broadcasting only. The theorem is a candidate from the programme, not established by this literature survey.

Verdict
-------
The strongest direct quantitative antecedent found is Chiribella's finite de Finetti theorem for symmetric M-output broadcast channels. It yields, by an elementary iteration of a two-output broadcaster, a dimension-dependent qualitative implication: for each fixed d=dim(H), b(E)->0 forces e(E)->0. Its bound depends on d^2 and log(d^2/b), and contains no Holevo-capacity parameter. It therefore does not supply the candidate's uniform-in-dimension, capacity-controlled rate.

Exact no-broadcasting results already settle the b=0 endpoint in finite dimension: exact common broadcasting of a finite experiment is equivalent to that experiment being classically equivalent, hence it admits an exact EB reconstruction. The quantitative small-b bridge remains a different question.

The de Finetti and statistical-experiment results below are close in subject but have distinct interfaces. They do not directly imply or refute the candidate inequality. This is a bounded source comparison, not an exhaustive priority search; no novelty claim follows from the absence of a matching theorem here.

1. Exact no-broadcasting and classical sufficient experiments
--------------------------------------------------------------
Kuramochi, arXiv:1710.01599v1 (submitted 2017-10-04), Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, is the most directly relevant exact structural antecedent in the inspected set. A statistical experiment E=(M,Theta,(phi_theta)) is broadcastable when one channel from the composite system back to M returns E in each marginal. Theorem 1 says that an experiment F can be extracted from E without disturbing E iff F is a randomization of the classical part E_cl of the minimal sufficient experiment. Taking F=E gives Corollary 1: E is broadcastable iff it is normally CP-equivalent to a classical experiment. In finite dimension, the two channels implementing this equivalence compose to a measure-and-prepare channel fixing all rho_i, so e(E)=0.

This gives the exact b=0 case once the infimum defining b is attained; it is attained because the finite-dimensional CPTP set is compact and the objective is continuous. It gives no stability modulus when b>0, no Holevo-capacity dependence, and no uniformity as dimension and the number of states vary. The result is exact and structural rather than quantitative.

Koashi–Imoto, arXiv:quant-ph/0101144v2 (last revised 2002-01-17), Theorem 3 and its maximal-decomposition discussion, is an earlier finite-dimensional exact non-disturbance theorem. It decomposes the support of a finite family as direct sums H_J^(ell) tensor H_K^(ell), with rho_s=direct-sum_ell p(s,ell) rho_J,ell^(s) tensor rho_K^(ell); operations preserving every state have a constrained block form. This identifies classical, protected quantum, and redundant components. It supplies important exact geometry but no approximate theorem controlled by b or C.

2. Closest quantitative broadcaster-to-EB theorem: finite de Finetti
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Chiribella, “On quantum estimation, quantum cloning and finite quantum de Finetti theorems,” arXiv:1010.1875v4 (2010-11-01), Section 2.1, Theorems 4 and 5, treats a CPTP channel E_M:L(H_in)->L(H^tensor M) whose full output is invariant under permutations. Theorem 5 states that there is a measure-and-prepare channel Etilde_M with product-state outputs such that, for its k-output restriction,

  ||Etilde_M^(k)-E_M^(k)||_diamond <= 4(1-sqrt(d^+_(M-k)/d^+_M)) <= 2 k d^2/M,

where d=dim(H), and d^+_m is the symmetric-subspace dimension. The first bound is the theorem's stated estimate; the second follows from the theorem by replacing the local dimension by d^2 for arbitrary permutation-invariant outputs. Theorem 4 is the sharper symmetric-support case with d in place of d^2. PDF locations: pp. 10-12; output symmetry is defined in Eq. (15), p. 10; Theorems 4-5 are on pp. 11-12.

An elementary inference from this theorem applies to the present b interface. First average B with its swapped version; both output marginals then coincide and remain within b of rho_i. Iteratively apply this channel at every leaf of a balanced binary tree. After t levels, each leaf marginal is within at most t b (half trace distance) of rho_i, by contractivity and the triangle inequality. Average the resulting 2^t-output channel over output permutations. Apply Theorem 5 with k=1 to obtain a single EB channel whose extra half-trace error is at most d^2/2^t. Therefore

  e(E) <= t b(E) + d^2/2^t.

For 0<b<1, choosing 2^t >= d^2/b gives e(E) <= b(ceil(log_2(d^2/b))+1). For b=0, let t tend to infinity to obtain e(E)=0 (or use the exact theorem above). This iteration is our inference, not a result stated by Chiribella. Its constant and dimension dependence are explicit consequences of the theorem and the tree construction.

This is a meaningful fixed-d stability theorem, but it has an unavoidable gap relative to the target interface: the estimate depends on d, while the candidate asks for a modulus through C(E) alone. The source assumes no M-output broadcaster as supplied data; the tree constructs one from B and pays the factor t b. It controls full channel diamond distance after de Finetti, which is stronger at that final step, but the construction's dependence on d is not removed by that strength.

3. Entropic and recent channel de Finetti results
-------------------------------------------------
Brandao–Harrow, “Quantum de Finetti Theorems under Local Measurements with Applications,” arXiv:1210.6367v5 (2014-07-23), Theorem 1, p. 6, gives an entropic/one-way-measurement result. If rho_AB is k-extendible, mu is a distribution over operations E_m:A->Atilde, and M is the set of measurements on B, then

  min_{sigma in Sep(A:B)} max_{Lambda_B in M} E_{m~mu} ||(E_m tensor Lambda_B)(rho_AB-sigma_AB)||_1
       <= sqrt(2 ln(dim Atilde)/k).

The bound is independent of the original subsystem dimensions except through the tested output dimension. Its norm is an average over the chosen operations and a maximum over local measurements; it is not a maximum over labels in E, full trace distance, or a common state-reconstruction channel. The separable comparison state is also not by itself a trace-preserving EB map on H. Thus this does not close the candidate gate, despite its useful dimension-light entropic mechanism.

Ye–Bergh–Datta, “Quantum de Finetti theorems for states and channels in any distance measure,” arXiv:2609.40343v1 (submitted 2026-09-30), Theorem 2, pp. 5-6, is the closest recent channel-level theorem inspected. For a permutation-covariant N^(n):A^tensor n -> B^tensor n and its reduced k-channel defined by inserting maximally mixed states in discarded inputs and tracing discarded outputs, there is a probability measure nu over CPTP maps Phi:A->B such that

  (1-epsilon_nk) Tr_{n-k} N^(n) <=_CP integral Phi^tensor k d nu(Phi),

with epsilon_nk <= d_A^2 d_B^2 k/n + 2 d_A k sqrt((d_A^2 d_B^2-1)/(n+d_A^2 d_B^2-1)) + d_A^2 k^2/(n+d_A^2 d_B^2-1).

Theorem 2 does not require no-signalling, although the operational reduced-channel interpretation uses it. Theorem 3 adds no-signalling and approximates by mixtures of almost-iid channels with an exponentially small error in a suitable parameter regime. Theorems 2-3 concern n inputs and n outputs with simultaneous permutation covariance; their component channels Phi are arbitrary channels, not EB maps. There is no finite family, max-prior Holevo capacity, or worst-label reconstruction objective. This 2026 preprint is an important close antecedent, but its quantifiers do not match the one-input-to-many-output problem.

4. Le Cam deficiency, approximate sufficiency, and fixed-prior Holevo information
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Jenčová, “Randomization theorems for quantum channels,” arXiv:1404.3900v1 (2014-04-15), Definition 2 and Theorem 2, pp. 12-13, gives the quantum finite-experiment comparison criterion. For experiments E={rho_i} and F={sigma_i}, F is epsilon-deficient with respect to E exactly when every decision channel from E can be matched from F with state errors at most 2 epsilon uniformly over i; equivalently, for full deficiency there is a randomization F' of F with max_i ||sigma'_i-rho_i||_1 <= 2 epsilon. This is the right language for e(E): every finite-dimensional EB map factors as a measurement followed by preparation, so e(E) is the optimal worst-state round trip through a finite classical experiment. However, the randomization theorem compares a specified F to E; it does not construct a useful classical F from b(E) and C(E).

Koashi–Imoto's exact maximal decomposition and Kuramochi's classical-part theorem describe exact sufficient classical information. Neither source gives the required approximate stability estimate. Janzing–Steudel, “A Quantum Broadcasting Problem in Classical Low Power Signal Processing,” arXiv:quant-ph/0609162v1 (2006-09-21), Section 3, Definition 2, instead studies loss of a fixed-prior ensemble Holevo quantity under a two-output map. Its fixed prior and average information objective should not be conflated with C(E)=max_p chi(p,E), nor with worst-state trace reconstruction by one EB map.

Historical significance and the exact remaining literature gate
---------------------------------------------------------------
If the candidate endpoint survives independent proof review and a wider priority audit, the potentially new content is the uniform modulus controlled by max-prior Holevo capacity: for every finite family in every finite dimension, small worst-label two-output marginal error forces one common EB channel to reconstruct all labels with the displayed capacity-dependent rate. The established exact theory already says zero-error broadcasting is classical; finite de Finetti already supplies a dimension-dependent approximate route; entropic de Finetti supplies low-dimensional measurement-norm bounds; Le Cam theory names the desired classical round trip. The literature gap left by these theorems is to combine those ingredients with a bound depending only on C(E), while retaining worst-case labels and producing one EB map from an approximate two-shareability witness.

The precise gate to check in a broader historical audit is whether any prior theorem proves: for all finite H and all finite E, existence of one CPTP B with both marginals within b of each rho_i and max-prior Holevo capacity at most C implies existence of one EB Lambda with max_i half-trace error o_C(1) as b->0, with a dimension- and label-count-independent rate. Fixed-prior Holevo loss, average-prior distortion, pairwise recovery, local-measurement norms, and exact broadcasting criteria do not state that interface. No priority conclusion is made here.
