Question and normalization
--------------------------
Independently derive the SU(2) tensor-product energy spectrum, low-energy projector dimensions and partial traces for unequal spins, then check Section 4 of `outputs/research/sol_collision_precision/quantum_branch.txt`. The convention throughout is

  [J_a,J_b]=i epsilon_(abc) J_c,
  sum_a (J_a^(j))^2 = j(j+1) I,
  H = -sum_(a=1)^3 J_a^(j) tensor J_a^(k).

With this convention, the source's factors are correct. If the interaction were defined as -2 J·J, all eigenvalues and deficits below would double.

1. Spectrum and exact cutoff dimensions
---------------------------------------
Put M=max(j,k), m=min(j,k), delta=M-m, and d_j=2j+1, d_k=2k+1, d_M=2M+1, d_m=2m+1. The Clebsch–Gordan decomposition, with multiplicity one, is

  V_j tensor V_k = direct-sum_(l=0)^(2m) V_(delta+l).

Here l is integral even when delta is half-integral; 2m and 2delta are integers. Let Pi_l denote the projector onto total spin delta+l and Pi_t=sum_(l=0)^t Pi_l. The Casimir identity gives

  H = (j(j+1)+k(k+1)-J_tot^2)/2.

Thus the largest eigenvalue and its eigenspace are

  lambda_max(H) = (j(j+1)+k(k+1)-delta(delta+1))/2 = jk+m,
  rank Pi_0 = 2delta+1,

and the deficit on the l-th summand is

  E_l = lambda_max(H)-lambda_l(H)
      = ((delta+l)(delta+l+1)-delta(delta+1))/2
      = l(2delta+l+1)/2.                                (A)

In particular E_0=0 and E_(t+1)-E_t=delta+t+1.

Each summand V_(delta+l) has dimension 2delta+2l+1. Therefore for every integer 0<=t<=2m,

  D_t := rank Pi_t = sum_(l=0)^t (2delta+2l+1)
       = (t+1)(2delta+t+1).                             (B)

At the endpoint,

  D_(2m)=(2m+1)(2delta+2m+1)=d_m d_M=d_j d_k,

as required for the full tensor-product dimension. This also handles unequal spins without a parity or integer-spin assumption. If m=0, there is only l=0, the whole tensor product is the top eigenspace, and no nonzero rank penalty can follow from energy; the source's later separate k=0 treatment is necessary.

2. Partial traces and the rank-Q projector inequality
-----------------------------------------------------
Pi_t commutes with U_j(g) tensor U_k(g) for every g in SU(2). Consequently Tr_k Pi_t commutes with the irreducible spin-j representation, and Tr_j Pi_t commutes with spin k. Schur's lemma makes each a scalar identity; taking traces fixes the scalars:

  Tr_k Pi_t = (D_t/d_j) I_j,
  Tr_j Pi_t = (D_t/d_k) I_k.                             (C)

These are exact, including t=0 and t=2m. In particular the two denominators are not interchangeable for unequal spins.

For a normalized pure vector psi of Schmidt rank q<=Q, write

  psi = sum_(u=1)^q sqrt(lambda_u) |u>_j |u'>_k,
  rho_j=Tr_k |psi><psi|,  rho_k=Tr_j |psi><psi|.

For arbitrary z, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

  |<psi,z>|^2 <= q sum_u lambda_u |<u,u'|z>|^2
              <= Q <z|(rho_j tensor I_k)|z|.

The symmetric argument on the k-side yields the operator inequalities

  |psi><psi| <= Q rho_j tensor I_k,
  |psi><psi| <= Q I_j tensor rho_k.                      (D)

Tracing (D) against Pi_t and using (C),

  <psi|Pi_t|psi> <= min{1, Q D_t/d_j, Q D_t/d_k}
                  = min{1, Q D_t/d_M}.                  (E)

The source's denominator 2M+1 is therefore correct: the smaller of the two marginal bounds uses the larger spin dimension. Using 2m+1 here would be an error. By convexity, (D)–(E) also hold for mixed states with Schmidt number at most Q.

3. Integrated spectral-CDF profile
----------------------------------
For any normalized pure Schmidt-rank-Q state (or mixed state of Schmidt number at most Q), set p_l=Tr(rho Pi_l), F_t=sum_(l<=t)p_l. Then (E) says

  F_t <= min{1, Q D_t/d_M}.

Writing the energy deficit g=lambda_max(H)-Tr(rho H), Abel summation gives the exact identity

  g = sum_(t=0)^(2m-1) (E_(t+1)-E_t) (1-F_t)
    = sum_(t=0)^(2m-1) (delta+t+1) (1-F_t).

It follows that the integrated projector profile is

  g >= G_Q(j,k)
    := sum_(t=0)^(2m-1) (delta+t+1)
          [1 - Q (t+1)(2delta+t+1)/(2M+1)]_+.           (F)

An empty sum is zero. Equivalently, let T be the largest t in {0,...,2m-1} with QD_t<d_M, or T=-1 if there is none. Then

  G_Q(j,k)=sum_(t=0)^T (delta+t+1)(1-QD_t/d_M).

This is the exact optimum of the relaxed scalar problem that retains only the CDF constraints F_t<=min(1,QD_t/d_M): the pointwise maximal feasible CDF F_t=min(1,QD_t/d_M) is nondecreasing and itself defines a probability law on l. It is not proved to be attainable by Schmidt-rank-Q vectors, so (F) is a certified lower bound, not an exact formula for the rank-Q minimum energy deficit.

The source's one-cutoff bound (4.5),

  max_(0<=t<2m) E_(t+1)[1-QD_t/d_M]_+,

is implied by (F). Indeed D_u<=D_t for u<=t, so summing the first t+1 terms in (F) gives at least E_(t+1)[1-QD_t/d_M]_+. Thus there is no gap in (4.5); the integrated CDF form just keeps all simultaneously available threshold deficits instead of the best single one. The source notes a weighted version, but (F) records its explicit increments and the exact unequal-spin D_t.

4. Same-regime constant improvement over source (4.6)
-----------------------------------------------------
The source assumes

  delta <= M/(16Q),   M >= 16Q,                           (G)

and reports g>=M/(32Q). The weighted profile yields a simple stronger constant under exactly (G). Put x=M/Q>=16. For any t with E_t<=x/4, (A) gives t<=sqrt(x/2), and

  D_t = 2E_t+2delta+t+1
      <= x/2 + x/8 + sqrt(x/2)+1
      <= x.

The last inequality holds for x>=16 (for example sqrt(x/2)<=x/4 and 1<=x/16). Therefore

  QD_t/d_M <= Qx/(2M+1)=M/(2M+1)<1/2,

so every such cutoff contributes at least half its energy increment in (F). Let T be the largest t with E_t<=x/4. It exists since E_0=0 and is <2m: under (G), m=M-delta>=15M/16, while E_(2m)=m(2M+1)>x/4. Then E_(T+1)>x/4, and

  g >= sum_(t=0)^T (delta+t+1)(1-QD_t/d_M)
    >= (1/2) sum_(t=0)^T (delta+t+1)
    = (1/2) E_(T+1) > x/8 = M/(8Q).                      (H)

So (H) improves the stated M/(32Q) constant by a factor of four, with no change to the hypotheses. It is still a lower bound, and not a sharp asymptotic claim. A further improvement may come from optimizing the full sum (F) rather than using the uniform 1/2 tail bound.

5. Check against quantum_branch.txt §4
--------------------------------------
- (4.1) is correct under the displayed H normalization; the top eigenvalue is jk+m, with a (2delta+1)-dimensional top irrep when j!=k, and the deficit is (A).
- (4.2) is exactly (B)–(C). For unequal spins, the partial-trace scalar on V_j is D_t/(2j+1), and that on V_k is D_t/(2k+1).
- (4.3) is correct for pure Schmidt-rank<=Q vectors; its mixed extension requires Schmidt number<=Q, not merely an arbitrary mixed state whose matrix rank is at most Q.
- (4.4) correctly uses the minimum of the two bounds, hence denominator 2M+1. It includes the trivial cap 1.
- (4.5) follows from a single CDF cutoff; the integrated profile (F) is stronger and valid by the same method.
- The proof of (4.6) is valid but loose. Its intermediate estimate gives g>=3M/(64Q), which indeed implies the displayed M/(32Q). Using several cutoffs proves (H).
- Edge case: when m=0 the max over t<2m is empty. Energy is identically zero, so this case must be read as a separate zero bound; Section 5 does handle k=0 separately.
- No factor-of-two error found. A different angular-momentum convention would rescale all energy quantities and must not be mixed into the source convention.

6. Remaining gap
-----------------
The marginal/CDF argument is rigorous but forgets representation-specific constraints on the possible sequence (p_l), including Schmidt structure inside each total-spin irrep and compatibility across nested projectors. The relaxed profile (F) is therefore not shown tight for the true rank-Q optimization. A potentially sharper next step is to characterize the minimum Schmidt rank or maximal Schmidt-rank-Q overlap inside the low-total-spin projectors, especially for delta>0, then compare its exact CDF with (E). No such strengthening is claimed here.
