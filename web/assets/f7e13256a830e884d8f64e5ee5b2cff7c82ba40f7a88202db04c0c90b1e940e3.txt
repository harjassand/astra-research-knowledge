THEOREM. Let sigma be a faithful density on M2. Let L be a finite-dimensional
sigma-stationary KMS-symmetric positive Markov generator, so exp(-t L) is
unital CP and H=Gamma_sigma^(1/2) L Gamma_sigma^(-1/2) is HS self-adjoint,
Gamma_sigma(A)=sigma^(1/2) A sigma^(1/2). Let R be any finite untouched quantum
reference. For every faithful joint density rho on R tensor M2,

  J=Tr[(id_R tensor L_*)rho (log rho-I_R tensor log sigma)]
    >= pi E,
  E=Tr[sqrt(rho) (id_R tensor H) sqrt(rho)].             (T)

Any faithful ancillary reference cancels pointwise from J and E. All joint
states, including entangled states with noncommuting local blocks, are allowed.
The result does not impose a fixed reference marginal. It implies the weak
constant two since E>=0. No assertion that pi is optimal is made here.

The authorized frozen Cycle07 full-generator representation states that H is

  H_N(Q)={V_N,Q}/2-N Q N,

Each elementary map is a legal stationary KMS positive generator after the

sigma=(I+tanh(alpha)Z)/2, alpha>=0. A diagonal unitary and positive scaling

2. Exact physical reduction for one normalized noise

Set A=cosh(alpha/2), B=sinh(alpha/2), c=cosh(alpha), h=sinh(alpha).
Tensor local Pauli operators with the reference identity. For N=X+aZ,
  V=(a^2+c)I-h Z+2a(B/A)X,
  K=sigma^(1/4)N sigma^(-1/4)=A X+iB Y+aZ,
  D=sigma^(1/4)V sigma^(-1/4),
  L_*(rho)=(D rho+rho D*)/2-K rho K*.
Here (D+D*)/2=K*K, and the remaining skew part is the physical Hamiltonian.
Its trace against log rho is zero; its stationary-log contribution is retained.
Thus
  J=Tr[L_*(rho)(log rho-alpha Z)],
  E=Tr[Vrho]-Tr[N sqrt(rho)N sqrt(rho)].

Choose a full joint eigenbasis with rho=sum_i lambda_i |i><i|, lambda_i>0.
For every unordered pair order p=lambda_i>=q=lambda_j and define
  w=p+q, beta=(log p-log q)/2>=0,
  m_beta=2beta tanh(beta), n_beta=1-sech(beta),
  d_kappa(u)=2u tanh(u)-kappa(1-sech(u)).
Put x=<i|X|j>, y=<i|Y|j>, z=<i|Z|j>, v_ij=(x,i y,a z)^T.
Let

  M_kappa(alpha,beta)=
  [[ A^2 m_beta-kappa n_beta+B^2 d_kappa(alpha), -P, A m_beta-kappa n_beta ],
   [ -P, B^2 m_beta+A^2 d_kappa(alpha),          -Q ],
   [ A m_beta-kappa n_beta,                     -Q, d_kappa(beta) ]],
  P=h beta+(alpha c-kappa h/2)tanh(beta),
  Q=2B beta+[(alpha-kappa B)/A]tanh(beta).

The independent frozen identity gives exactly

  J-kappa E
   =sum_(i<j) w v_ij* M_kappa(alpha,beta_ij) v_ij
      +d_kappa(alpha) sum_i lambda_i
          [B^2 |<i|X|i>|^2+A^2 |<i|Y|i>|^2].           (1)

For clarity, its only full-state cancellations are Pauli products:
  sum_(i<j)(p-q) Im[x* y]=-Tr[rho Z],
  sum_(i<j)(p-q) Im[z* y]= Tr[rho X].
The full entropy pair is
  (p-q)(log p-log q)(|A x+a z|^2+B^2|y|^2)
   +2B(p+q)(log p-log q) Im[(A x+a z)*y].
The root pair is (sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2 |x+a z|^2.
The reference potential is
  2alpha(h-c Tr[rho Z])+(2a alpha/A)Tr[rho X].
The root potential is (c-1)-h Tr[rho Z]+2a(B/A)Tr[rho X].
The residual constant is c d_kappa(alpha), allocated exactly as
  d_kappa(alpha) Tr[rho(B^2 X^2+A^2 Y^2)].
These formulas imply (1) in every reference dimension; no block functional
calculus or partial-trace gap comparison is used.

3. Exact three-point congruence

For any real u,v and lambda=kappa/2, define
  k_lambda(u)=u sinh(u)-lambda(cosh(u)-1),
  T_lambda(u,v)=u sinh(v)+v sinh(u)
                 -4lambda sinh(u/2)sinh(v/2),
  K_lambda(u,v)=T_lambda(u,v)/[2cosh((u-v)/2)].
Thus K_lambda(u,u)=k_lambda(u). This convention is important: the Hardy
coefficient is lambda=kappa/2, not kappa.

Let
  S=[[1,1,0],[1,-1,0],[0,0,1]],
  (u_1,u_2,u_3)=(beta-alpha,beta+alpha,beta), C=cosh(beta).
Then the exact identity is

  (C/2) S^T M_kappa(alpha,beta) S
       =[K_lambda(u_r,u_s)]_(r,s=1,2,3).               (2)

This is an invertible congruence, since det S=-2 and C>0. To verify every
independent entry without a hidden limiting argument:
  C(M_xx+M_yy-2P)=2k_lambda(beta-alpha),
  C(M_xx+M_yy+2P)=2k_lambda(beta+alpha),
  C(M_xx-M_yy)=T_lambda(beta-alpha,beta+alpha)/c,
  C(M_xz-Q)=T_lambda(beta-alpha,beta)/A,
  C(M_xz+Q)=T_lambda(beta+alpha,beta)/A,
  C M_zz=2k_lambda(beta).
The first/second identities use sinh(beta+-alpha)=c sinh(beta)+-h cosh(beta).
For the third, the root product identity is
  2sinh((beta-alpha)/2)sinh((beta+alpha)/2)=cosh(beta)-cosh(alpha).
For the fourth/fifth, use
  2sinh(beta/2)sinh((beta+-alpha)/2)=cosh(beta+-alpha/2)-A.
These identities are valid at zero, coincident, and opposite nodes as written.

4. Hardy positivity on ALL real arguments, including negative nodes

For real |t|<1 let
  a(t)=(1-t^2)atanh(t)/t, a(0)=1,
  b(t)=sqrt(1-t^2)>0,
  N_lambda(t,u)=[a(t)+a(u)-lambda b(t)b(u)]/(1-tu).
The authorized prior-cycle Hardy lemma gives N_(pi/2) a PSD kernel on the
entire real interval (-1,1), meaning EVERY finite Gram matrix is PSD.
This is stronger than a two-point determinant inequality and is precisely
what (2) needs. It cannot be replaced here by a two-point-only statement.

For completeness, its elementary Hardy operator proof is self-contained.
Take analytic a(z)=(1-z^2)atanh(z)/z and b(z)=sqrt(1-z^2), b(0)=1, on the
unit disk. Both are bounded analytic. On its boundary, almost everywhere,
  2 Re a(e^(i theta))=(pi/2)|b(e^(i theta))|^2.
Indeed (1-z^2)/z=-2i sin theta, and Im atanh(e^(i theta)) is pi/4 times
the sign of sin theta. Therefore on H^2,
  M_a+M_a*=(pi/2)M_b* M_b.
The operator corresponding to the numerator of N_(pi/2) is
  M_a+M_a*-(pi/2)M_b M_b*
    =(pi/2)(M_b* M_b-M_b M_b*)>=0.
The last inequality follows from
  ||M_b* f||=||P_H2(bar b f)||<=||bar b f||=||M_b f||.
Evaluating this positive operator on reproducing kernels proves the finite
Gram positivity of N_(pi/2), including the real parameter domain above.

The exact hyperbolic interface is
  t=tanh(u/2), r=tanh(v/2),
  K_lambda(u,v)=2sinh(u/2)sinh(v/2) N_lambda(t,r).     (3)
For nonzero u this uses a(t)=u/sinh(u); the zero case follows by continuity
and both sides vanish because sinh(u/2)=0. The signed factors for negative
u are harmless: (3) is a real diagonal congruence of a PSD Gram matrix.
Consequently [K_(pi/2)(u_r,u_s)] is PSD for ANY finite set of real nodes.
In particular the three nodes in (2) are covered even when beta-alpha<0.

5. Finish, degeneracies, and generator sums

Equations (2)-(3) imply M_pi(alpha,beta)>=0 for every alpha,beta>=0. Also
  d_pi(alpha)=2k_(pi/2)(alpha)/cosh(alpha)>=0,
either by Hardy diagonal positivity or the scalar inequality for constants
up to four. Thus every summand and the diagonal remainder in (1) is
nonnegative, proving (T) for N=X+aZ and all real a.

No division by d_pi(beta), beta, alpha, or beta-alpha occurs. In particular:
  alpha=0: the three nodes coincide; M_pi=d_pi(beta)(1,0,1)(1,0,1)^T.
  beta=0: nodes are (-alpha,alpha,0), giving the displayed diagonal kernel.
  beta=alpha: one node is zero; the physical stationary null vector is
             (1,1,0) and is included in the Gram proof.
Degenerate full-state eigenvalues require no special state perturbation.
Faithfulness avoids logs at zero; no lower bound on eigenvalues is charged.

Pure Z elementary noise obeys J>=4E>=pi E by diagonalizing the full joint
state and using (p-q)(log p-log q)>=4(sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2. Phase rotations,
positive scaling, and sums of individually legal elementary noises preserve
the endpoint. The supplied finite KMS qubit decomposition therefore proves
the stated full-generator theorem, with no modular/GNS assumption.
