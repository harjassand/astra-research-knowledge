SU(m) MULTIPLICITY-TO-ZERO-WEIGHT INJECTION
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact finite-dimensional proof. The bound is valid for every dominant
carrier weight, hence in particular for regular weights. Regularity is not
used. No asymptotic or novelty claim is made.

1. STATEMENT AND QUANTIFIERS

Fix an integer m>=2 and G=SU(m). Let lambda be any dominant integral
highest weight, V_lambda the corresponding finite-dimensional irreducible
unitary representation, and let G act on End(V_lambda) by conjugation. For
every dominant integral weight nu, define

  M_{lambda,nu}=Hom_G(V_nu, End(V_lambda)),
  mult_{lambda}(nu)=dim M_{lambda,nu}.

Then

  mult_{lambda}(nu) <= dim V_nu[0],                             (1)

where V_nu[0] is the zero-weight space for a fixed maximal torus T. This
holds for all lambda, not just regular lambda (regular means every Dynkin
coefficient of lambda is positive). If nu is outside the SU(m) root
lattice, both sides of (1) are zero. If nu is in the root lattice, V_nu[0]
is nonzero, and the inequality is substantive.

The compact-group and Lie-algebra versions agree here: SU(m) is connected,
finite-dimensional unitary representations are completely reducible, and
highest-weight vectors for the complexified sl_m action determine the
intertwiners.

2. ROOT LATTICE AND CENTRAL CHARACTER

Write nu=sum_{i=1}^{m-1} n_i omega_i, with n_i>=0. The center generator
z=exp(2 pi i/m) I_m acts on V_nu by

  z |_{V_nu}=exp((2 pi i/m) c(nu)) I,
  c(nu)=sum_{i=1}^{m-1} i n_i (mod m).                          (2)

For type A_{m-1}, P/Q is Z/mZ and the class of nu is c(nu), so nu is in
the root lattice Q exactly when c(nu)=0 mod m. The center acts trivially on
End(V_lambda)=V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*, hence an irreducible constituent
V_nu can occur only if c(nu)=0.

For SU(m), this condition is also exactly the condition that V_nu[0] be
nonzero. To check sufficiency, lift nu to the polynomial GL(m) highest
weight partition

  Lambda_j=sum_{i=j}^{m-1} n_i  (1<=j<m),  Lambda_m=0.

Its size is |Lambda|=sum_i i n_i. A GL(m) weight restricts to zero on the
SU(m) torus precisely when its m coordinates are all equal. Thus a zero
SU(m) weight is possible only if |Lambda|=m q for an integer q. Conversely,
the content (q,...,q) occurs in the Schur module S_Lambda(C^m): the standard
type-A weight/content criterion says it occurs exactly when Lambda
majorizes (q^m); this holds because the first j parts of a decreasing
partition of average q have sum at least jq. The corresponding GL(m)
weight has zero SU(m) restriction. This proves

  V_nu[0] != {0}  iff  nu in Q  iff  c(nu)=0 mod m.              (3)

No converse about occurrence in End(V_lambda) is asserted.

3. INJECTIVE HIGHEST-VECTOR COEFFICIENT MAP

Put mu=lambda^*, the highest weight of V_lambda^*. Since G is compact,
End(V_lambda) is semisimple and the multiplicity can be computed using
quotient-oriented Hom:

  mult_lambda(nu)
    =dim Hom_G(V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*, V_nu).

Tensor-Hom currying gives a canonical linear isomorphism

  Hom_G(V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*, V_nu)
       ~= Hom_G(V_lambda^*, V_lambda^* tensor V_nu).             (4)

For clarity, the general map is

  F:A tensor B -> C  |->  F_tilde:B -> A^* tensor C,
  F_tilde(b)=sum_j a_j^* tensor F(a_j tensor b),

for any basis (a_j) of A and dual basis (a_j^*) of A^*. It is basis
independent and G-equivariant. In (4), A=V_lambda, B=V_lambda^*,
C=V_nu; hence A^*=V_lambda^*. This fixes the dual convention and uses
V_nu, not V_nu^*. Equivalently, both sides are the same invariant-tensor
space after interchanging the V_lambda and V_lambda^* factors.

Let v_mu be a nonzero highest-weight vector in V_mu. Given

  phi in Hom_G(V_mu, V_mu tensor V_nu),

its image w=phi(v_mu) is a highest vector of total weight mu. Decompose it
by the first-factor weight as

  w=sum_{beta in Q_+} w_beta,
  w_beta in V_mu[mu-beta] tensor V_nu[beta],                     (5)

where Q_+ is the nonnegative integer span of the simple roots. The beta=0
component is uniquely

  w_0=v_mu tensor p_phi,  with p_phi in V_nu[0].                (6)

Define Phi(phi)=p_phi. This is linear. We now prove Phi is injective.

Suppose Phi(phi)=0 and w!=0. Choose beta in Q_+ of minimum root height
among the nonzero w_gamma. Then ht(beta)>0. Write e_i for the simple
raising operator associated with simple root alpha_i. Since w is highest,

  (e_i tensor 1 + 1 tensor e_i)w=0  for every i.                (7)

Consider the component of (7) in

  V_mu[mu-beta+alpha_i] tensor V_nu[beta].

Its first term is (e_i tensor 1)w_beta. Its second term can only come from
w_{beta-alpha_i}; if beta-alpha_i is not in Q_+, that weight component is
absent, and if it is in Q_+, it has strictly smaller height and is zero by
the minimal choice of beta. Therefore

  (e_i tensor 1)w_beta=0  for every simple i.                   (8)

In an irreducible highest-weight module V_mu, the common kernel of all
simple raising operators is exactly the highest-weight line C v_mu.
Consequently

  intersect_i ker(e_i tensor 1) = C v_mu tensor V_nu.

But w_beta has first-factor weight mu-beta, which is not mu because
beta!=0. Equations (8) therefore force w_beta=0, contradicting its
choice. Thus Phi(phi)=0 implies w=0. Since v_mu generates V_mu, w=0
implies phi=0. This proves injectivity of

  Phi:Hom_G(V_mu,V_mu tensor V_nu) -> V_nu[0].                 (9)

Combining (4) and (9) proves (1). The proof uses no PRV component theorem,
no regularity of lambda, and no estimate on the number of tensor-product
copies beyond the explicit injection.

4. STRONGER EXACT FORMULA IN THE LITERATURE; PRV CAVEAT

The preceding argument proves an injection; it does not identify its image.
A stronger standard multiplicity formula is recorded by Onishchik and
Vinberg as formula (F11) in their reference chapter on linear
representations of complex semisimple Lie algebras. In cleaned notation,
for dominant Lambda,M,N,

  mult(V_N, V_Lambda tensor V_M)
   =dim {v in V_M[N-Lambda] : e_i^(Lambda_i+1)v=0 for every simple i}
   =dim {v in V_N[Lambda-M] : e_i^(M_i+1)v=0 for every simple i}, (10)

where Lambda_i=<Lambda,alpha_i^vee> and e_i acts in the indicated module.
Taking Lambda=N=mu=lambda^* and M=nu gives the exact common-kernel
subspace of V_nu[0]; (1) follows immediately. The source cites [47] and
Exercise 14 of section 9, Chapter VIII, for (10). Formula (10) is a
multiplicity characterization, stronger than the coefficient injection
proved here; the direct proof above does not assume its surjectivity claim.

Do not infer (1) from the PRV component theorem. PRV's component statement
(or its all-Weyl extension) certifies occurrence of distinguished
constituents; by itself it gives no upper bound on the total number of
copies generated by other highest vectors. Either the injective map (9),
the exact multiplicity formula (10), or the induced-representation
inclusion below supplies the needed upper bound.

5. PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON

(a) Fronsdal-Hirai (1981), strongest direct global statement. C. Fronsdal
and T. Hirai, “A remark on the tensor product of irreducible representations
of a compact Lie group,” Japan. J. Math. 7(1) (1981), 201-211,
DOI 10.4099/math1924.7.201, publisher scan:
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/math1924/7/1/7_1_201/_pdf .

Their Theorem 1 states that for connected compact G and irreducibles U_1,
U_2 with highest weights lambda_1,lambda_2, the tensor product embeds
isomorphically as a G-module in the induced representation

  Ind_T^G chi_{lambda_1+w_0 lambda_2},

where w_0 is the longest Weyl element and chi_eta is the torus character
of weight eta. Setting U_2=U_1^* gives lambda_2=-w_0 lambda_1, so the
character is trivial and

  End(U_1)=U_1 tensor U_1^* embeds in Ind_T^G 1=L^2(G/T).

Frobenius reciprocity gives

  mult(V_nu,End(U_1)) <= dim V_nu^T = dim V_nu[0].

This is a stronger whole-representation inclusion and requires neither
regularity nor a PRV inference. Their Theorem 3' (added in proof, pp.
210-211) states more concretely that, for highest v_1 in U_1 and lowest
v_2 in U_2, v_1 tensor v_2 is a cyclic vector for U_1 tensor U_2. Applied
to U tensor U^*, the highest-line projector v_lambda tensor bar(v_lambda)
is T-fixed and cyclic. In an isotypic block V_nu tensor C^q, cyclicity
forces the q coefficient vectors of this cyclic vector to be linearly
independent; since they lie in V_nu[0], q<=dim V_nu[0]. Theorem 3' is
therefore another direct route to the bound.

(b) Dawson-Hernandez-Eliseo (2025), flag-symbol formulation. M. Dawson and
Y. Hernandez-Eliseo, “A Representation-Theoretic Approach to Toeplitz
Quantization on Flag Manifolds,” arXiv:2406.10804v2, dated 2025-02-12:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10804 ; primary HTML:
https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10804v2 . Their Theorem 3.10 states that a
highest-weight vector in one compact-group irreducible tensor a
lowest-weight vector in another is cyclic in the tensor product. In section
5 they project v_lambda tensor bar(v_lambda) to each copy of an irreducible
pi in End(V_lambda), note that these projected vectors are linearly
independent and lie in V_pi^L, and conclude

  mult_G(pi,End(V_lambda))=dim K_pi^lambda <= dim V_pi^L.

For a regular SU(m) weight, L=T and the fixed space is exactly the
zero-weight space. For singular lambda their natural L is larger than T,
so their stabilizer-fixed bound can be sharper. Their theorem is a later
reformulation/application of highest-lowest cyclicity; it is not needed for
the direct proof in section 3.

(c) Onishchik-Vinberg formula (F11), exact copy-space refinement. An
accessible scan of the reference chapter records the two equalities in
(10):
https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/drutu/tcc6/onishchik-vinberg.pdf . This
characterizes the image by raising-operator nilpotence inside a weight
space; our proof establishes the upper bound by an explicit injection but
does not prove that its image is exactly that common kernel.

6. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE EXPECTED CUMULATIVE CASIMIR COUNT

This bound supports the proposed exponent N in a uniform cumulative count,
provided all quantities below are interpreted for fixed m and standard
Casimir normalization. Put

  r=rank(SU(m))=m-1,
  N=|Phi^+|=m(m-1)/2,
  c_2(nu)=(nu,nu+2rho)/2,

where long roots have squared length 2; this agrees with Hermitian
fundamental generators satisfying Tr_fund(T_a T_b)=delta_ab/2 and
L(X)=sum_a[T_a,[T_a,X]]. For t>=0, define D_lambda(t) as the full dimension
of the Casimir spectral subspace in End(V_lambda) with eigenvalue <=t,
including all multiplicities and all representation dimensions. Then

  D_lambda(t)=sum_{nu:c_2(nu)<=t} mult_lambda(nu) dim V_nu
             <=sum_{nu:c_2(nu)<=t} dim V_nu[0] dim V_nu.          (11)

The last sum may include all dominant weights; omitting the root-lattice
restriction only enlarges it. Let R=1+sqrt(t). Since c_2(nu)>=||nu||^2/2
for dominant nu, there are O_m(R^r) dominant lattice weights in the ball.
Weyl's dimension formula gives dim V_nu=O_m(R^N). The type-A zero-weight
multiplicity bound gives dim V_nu[0]=O_m(R^(N-r)); alternatively,
PBW bounds it by the Kostant partition count, at most
(1+ht(nu))^(N-r), by choosing coefficients of the N-r nonsimple positive
roots and solving uniquely for the simple-root coefficients. Hence

  D_lambda(t) <= C_m R^(r+N+N-r)
               = C_m (1+t)^N,                                  (12)

uniformly in lambda. There is no missing multiplicity factor: the two
factors in each summand are exactly the number of copies and the carrier
dimension of a copy. The Casimir ball contributes r dimensions at radius
sqrt(t). The powers combine to R^(2N)=t^N.

Thus the expected exponent N is consistent and the composition has no
exponent error. Conditions to retain in any statement: (i) D counts full
isotypic dimensions, not just the number of highest weights; (ii) m is fixed
so C_m is allowed to depend on m; (iii) t>=0; (iv) the quadratic Casimir
has the specified normalization. If the Casimir is multiplied by c>0, the
spectral parameter rescales by c and the profile constant changes. The
root-lattice condition can improve constants but is not needed for this
upper bound.

The cumulative count bound (12) alone, inserted into the partial-trace /
Schmidt-rank profile, yields the coarse rate

  inf_{||X||_HS=1, rank(X)<=Q} <X,LX> >= c_m (dim V_lambda/Q)^(1/N)

when dim(V_lambda)/Q exceeds a fixed m-dependent threshold. It is a lower
bound from a relaxed spectral CDF constraint; no matching construction or
claim of sharpness follows from this multiplicity injection.

SCOPE

The exact multiplicity inequality and the finite-dimensional profile bound
are established statements. The asymptotic sharpness of the resulting
low-rank energy law, any matching extremizer, and any novelty/priority claim
are not established here.
