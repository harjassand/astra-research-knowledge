CYCLE 4 LEAF — SPARSE CLASSWISE STATIONARITY AND MIXING
========================================================
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the displayed sparse paired-edge
family. No general weakly reversible theorem or novelty claim is made.

MAIN CONCLUSION
---------------
For a fixed finite sparse support graph and a compact box of strictly
positive, finite rate constants, the support-switch Lyapunov function can
be strengthened from the earlier pointwise Foster estimate to a common
geometric generator drift on the variable coordinates of every class. The
result is uniform over rate vectors and over all isolated-substrate labels.
It gives:

  * physical-time non-explosion and positive recurrence in every class;
  * a common bound on the stationary first moment of catalyst counts and
    exponential moments of every non-isolated substrate count;
  * uniform tightness of the stationary laws after projecting out frozen
    isolated coordinates; and
  * V-geometric convergence in continuous time, with common constants over
    the rate box and class labels.

The drift's V has a linear catalyst term and exponential active-substrate
terms. Thus the generic conclusion is not an exponential moment for
catalyst counts. Nor can full-state stationary tightness or moments be
uniform over infinitely many isolated-substrate labels: each such label is
conserved. A single immigration-death catalyst with a frozen substrate is
an exact counterexample to those stronger full-state claims.

MODEL, RATE BOX, AND EXACT CLASSES
---------------------------------
Let J and I be finite catalyst and substrate index sets, and let E be a
fixed bipartite edge set. Write N(i)={j:(j,i) in E} and
N(j)={i:(j,i) in E}. For each j include

  0 -> A_j       at rate alpha_j,
  A_j -> 0       at rate delta_j a_j.

For each (j,i) in E include

  A_j -> A_j+B_i                         at rate p_ji a_j,
  A_j+B_i -> A_j                        at rate q_ji a_j b_i,
  A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i             at rate u_ji a_j b_i,
  (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i -> A_j+B_i             at rate v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1),

where k_ji is a fixed positive integer and (a)_m=a(a-1)...(a-m+1),
zero when a<m. Every displayed rate constant varies in a fixed interval
[rate^-,rate^+] with 0<rate^-<=rate^+<infinity. The dimensions, support,
and k_ji are fixed while the rate vector theta varies. This is standard
stochastic mass action.

Set I_0={i:N(i)=empty} and I_+={i:N(i) nonempty}. Every reaction has zero
stoichiometric change in B_i for i in I_0, so z=(b_i)_(i in I_0) is a
pathwise constant. The exact closed communicating class for label z is

  Gamma_z = { (a,b): a in N_0^J, b_i in N_0 for i in I_+,
                       b_i=z_i for i in I_0 }.

There are no additional class labels in this family. To reach the zero
state on the variable coordinates, clear any active B_i by first
immigrating one catalyst in N(i), then using A_j+B_i -> A_j repeatedly;
finally remove all catalysts by death. To build a target state, immigrate
a neighbor catalyst for each positive target b_i, use A_j -> A_j+B_i the
needed number of times, and then adjust the catalyst counts with immigration
and death. Each step has positive rate and the unwanted channels can be
excluded along this finite path with positive probability. Thus Gamma_z is
irreducible. Different z give disjoint closed classes. The active-coordinate
generator is independent of z, so these class chains are isomorphic.

COMMON GEOMETRIC DRIFT
---------------------
For i in I_+, define

  h_i(a)=1_{sum_(j in N(i)) a_j=0},  psi_i=R^(b_i),  A=sum_j a_j.

Choose one common R>1 sufficiently close to 1 that

  chi_j := delta_j^- - sum_(i in N(j)) p_ji^+(R-1) > 0
  for every j.

This is possible because J and E are finite and delta_j^->0. Put
H_+=sum_j alpha_j^+, alpha_sum^-=sum_j alpha_j^-, and choose K>=1 with
K>(H_++1)/alpha_sum^-. Define the class-relative function

  V(a,b_+) = A + sum_(i in I_+) R^(b_i)(1+h_i(a)) + K 1_{A=0}.

It is at least 1 and norm-like on the variable coordinates. It does not
include the fixed label z.

For a fixed theta, let

  g_ji^theta(m) = p_ji(R-1)R^m
                  - q_ji(1-R^(-1))mR^m + k_ji u_ji m.

Direct generator summation gives, for A=0,

  L_theta V = sum_j alpha_j - K sum_j alpha_j
              - sum_(i in I_+) alpha(N(i)) R^(b_i),

and for A>=1,

  L_theta V = sum_j alpha_j - sum_j delta_j a_j
     + sum_(j,i)in E a_j g_ji^theta(b_i)
     - sum_(j,i)in E k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i
     + K sum_j delta_j 1_{a=e_j}
     + sum_(i in I_+) R^(b_i)[ -alpha(N(i))1_{h_i=1}
         + sum_(j in N(i)) delta_j 1_{a restricted to N(i)=e_j} ].

Here alpha(N(i))=sum_(j in N(i))alpha_j. The last bracket is the exact
support-switch term: immigration of a neighbor removes the extra h_i
weight, while death of the last neighbor adds it. The finite exact check
listed below verifies this identity on its stated fixture, including
boundaries; the all-state estimate is the analytic argument that follows.

Uniformity of the substrate tail. Let P=max_(j,i) p_ji^+,
Q=min_(j,i) q_ji^->0, U=max_(j,i) k_ji u_ji^+, and d=max_j |N(j)|.
For each edge and all rates in the box,

  g_ji^theta(m)/R^m <= P(R-1)-Q(1-R^(-1))m+U m/R^m -> -infinity

uniformly as m->infinity. Thus for any chosen L<infinity there is a common
integer M_1 such that g_ji^theta(m)<=-L R^m for every edge, rate, and
m>=M_1. For M>=1, the positive low-coordinate bound

  G_M := max_(j,i,theta, 0<=m<M) (g_ji^theta(m))_+
  <= P(R-1)R^(M-1)+U M

satisfies limsup_(M->infinity) d G_M/R^M <= d P(R-1)/R. Choose
L_c>d P(R-1)/R and L_s>max_(i in I_+,j in N(i))delta_j^+.
Take M large enough that both

  d G_M <= L_c R^M,
  g_ji^theta(m) <= -(L_c+L_s+2)R^m for m>=M

hold uniformly. If E is empty, the high-substrate argument is omitted.

Split the active substrates into H={i:b_i>=M} and its complement. For each
catalyst j with a high neighbor, choose one high edge e_j^c=(j,i_j^c). Its
negative term pays for every positive low-substrate g term in that catalyst
group, because their sum is at most d G_M a_j <= L_c R^M a_j <=
L_c R^(b_i_j^c)a_j. For each high substrate i with h_i=0, choose one edge
e_i^s=(j_i,i) with a_(j_i)>=1. Its negative term is reserved to pay the
support-switch term. If h_i=1, no such edge is chosen; the switch itself is
negative.

The edgewise tail bound has coefficient L_c+L_s+2. An edge can be chosen
at most once as e_j^c (its catalyst endpoint is fixed) and at most once as
e_i^s (its substrate endpoint is fixed). On e_j^c, reserve L_c a_jR^(b_i)
to pay all the catalyst's low-edge positives, and one more unit of
a_jR^(b_i) for negative catalyst drift. On e_i^s, reserve L_s a_jR^(b_i)
to pay the only possible positive support switch, at most delta_j^+R^(b_i),
and one more unit of a_jR^(b_i)>=R^(b_i) for negative substrate drift.
If the same edge has both selections, these four allocations use at most
L_c+L_s+2, so both residual units remain. Unselected high-edge terms and
the reverse factorial terms are nonpositive and can be discarded. Thus
each catalyst with a high neighbor retains a uniform negative linear term,
and each high substrate with h_i=0 retains a uniform negative exponential
term. For h_i=1, the switch contributes
-alpha(N(i))R^(b_i), with alpha(N(i))>=sum_(j in N(i))alpha_j^->0.
Low-substrate switch terms are bounded uniformly because b_i<M.

For a catalyst j with no high neighbor, all adjacent b_i are below M. If
all those b_i are zero, its linear coefficient is at most -chi_j. If at
least one adjacent b_i is positive, the corresponding reverse reaction
contributes at most -v_min(a_j)_(k_ji+1), since k_ji>=1 and b_i>=1. All
positive terms on this finite low-substrate box are bounded by C_M a_j;
the falling factorial has degree k_ji+1>=2 and therefore dominates this
linear bound for large a_j. The remaining bounded a_j values are absorbed
in a uniform additive constant. Thus each such catalyst also has a common
bound C_j-c_j a_j with c_j>0.

The boundary term K sum_j delta_j 1_{a=e_j}, immigration constants, and
low-substrate switch terms are uniformly bounded. Combining the groups
gives constants C_1,c_1,c_2>0, independent of theta and z, such that on
A>=1,

  L_theta V <= C_1 - c_1 A - c_2 sum_(i in H) R^(b_i).

All low substrate terms R^(b_i) are bounded by R^M, while V is bounded
above by A plus a fixed multiple of sum_(i in H)R^(b_i) plus a constant.
After adjusting constants, L_theta V<=C-cV on A>=1. On A=0 the exact
formula above and alpha(N(i))>0 give the same inequality: V=K+2 sum_i R^(b_i)
and each substrate exponential coefficient has a strictly negative drift.
Therefore there are common C<infinity and c>0 such that

  L_theta V <= C-cV       on every Gamma_z, for every theta in the box.

The proof requires a fixed finite graph, fixed finite k_ji, and rates
bounded above and bounded away from zero. It extends the previous sparse
outside-finite-set Foster calculation; that previous conclusion by itself
would not imply this geometric drift.

PHYSICAL-TIME AND STATIONARY CONSEQUENCES
-----------------------------------------
V is norm-like on each Gamma_z, and every sublevel set is finite in active
coordinates. Localized stopped Dynkin at tau_n=inf{t:V(X_t)>=n}, using
L_theta V<=C, gives

  P_x(tau_n<=t) <= (V(x)+Ct)/n.

Exit rates are bounded on each finite sublevel set, uniformly over the
rate box. Infinite jumps in finite time while staying in one such set are
therefore impossible; finite-time explosion would have to cross every
sublevel. Letting n increase proves non-explosion in physical time. The
positive-drift boundary is handled before applying recurrence or mixing
criteria; no jump-chain-to-CTMC inference is used.

The class chain is irreducible. The common geometric drift gives positive
recurrence and a unique class invariant probability pi_(theta,z). It also
gives a uniform stationary moment bound

  sup_(theta,z) pi_(theta,z)(V) <= M_0 < infinity.

For example, apply the geometric drift to a fixed-time skeleton as in the
next section; its usual invariant-moment bound is common over the box.
Consequently, for every active i and every N,

  sup_(theta,z) pi_(theta,z)(B_i>=N) <= M_0 R^(-N),
  sup_(theta,z) E_pi[A] <= M_0,
  sup_(theta,z) E_pi[R^(B_i)] <= M_0.

Thus active substrates have uniform exponential moments at exponent
eta=log R, and catalysts have a uniform first moment. No exponential
catalyst moment follows from this V. These are uniform stationary bounds
over rate vectors and class labels after projecting to variable
coordinates.

V-GEOMETRIC MIXING: HYPOTHESES AND UNIFORMITY
---------------------------------------------
Fix any skeleton interval Delta>0. Localized Dynkin/Gronwall gives the
common semigroup estimate

  P_t^theta V <= exp(-ct)V + (C/c)(1-exp(-ct)).

Set r=exp(-c Delta)<1 and B=C/c. Choose lambda in (r,1), then a common
finite sublevel C_*={V<=R_*} large enough that B<=(lambda-r)V outside
C_*. This yields

  P_Delta^theta V <= lambda V + b 1_{C_*}

with common b and finite C_*. The skeleton is irreducible: for any active
states x,y, a finite path of positive-rate reactions from x to y can be
followed in time Delta and then held at y for the remainder with positive
probability. It is aperiodic since P_Delta(0,0)>0 (the no-jump event at
zero has probability exp(-Delta sum_j alpha_j)>0).

There is also a uniform one-step minorization on C_*. From each x in this
finite set, fix one finite positive-rate path to the active zero state
(a=0,b_+=0; on Gamma_z this is (a=0,b_+=0,b_0=z)).
Every selected reaction rate on those finitely many path states has a
positive lower bound over the rate box, every total exit rate there has a
finite upper bound, and the probability of no jump from zero to Delta has
a positive common lower bound. Taking the minimum over the finite starting
set gives epsilon>0 with

  P_Delta^theta(x,{0}) >= epsilon,  x in C_*, all theta,

where 0 denotes the reduced active-coordinate zero state.

The discrete-time geometric drift/minorization theorem (Meyn and Tweedie,
1994, Theorem 2.3) states that a psi-irreducible, aperiodic chain with
V>=1, a small set C, and P V<=lambda V+b 1_C for lambda<1 has a unique
invariant probability and constants M<infinity, rho<1 such that
||P^n(x,.)-pi||_V <= M V(x) rho^n. Here the drift, minorization,
sup_(C_*) V, and irreducibility/aperiodicity bounds are all common across
theta and z. The standard split-chain proof (or its quantitative form in
that theorem) therefore supplies common M,rho. The skeleton's invariant
law is the CTMC invariant law. The common semigroup bound controls the
fractional final interval s in t=n Delta+s, so for some common M',gamma>0,

  ||P_t^(theta,z)(x,.)-pi_(theta,z)||_V
       <= M' V(x) exp(-gamma t),  t>=0.

This is classwise V-geometric mixing. The prefactor depends on V(x); no
uniform mixing time over all unbounded starting states is asserted. A
full-state V that also charges the frozen z coordinates cannot satisfy a
uniform negative drift because those coordinates have zero drift. The
class-relative V above is the right statement.

ISOLATED-CLASS OBSTRUCTION TO FULL-STATE UNIFORMITY
---------------------------------------------------
If I_0 is nonempty, the class laws on the full lattice have the form

  pi_(theta,z)(a,b_+,b_0) = pi_theta^*(a,b_+) 1_{b_0=z}.

For any isolated i and eta>0,
E_pi[exp(eta B_i)]=exp(eta z_i), whose supremum over z_i is infinite.
For any finite set F in the full countable state space, choose z_i larger
than every isolated-coordinate value in F; then pi_(theta,z)(F)=0. Thus
{pi_(theta,z):z in N_0^(I_0)} is not uniformly tight (its supremum mass
outside any finite set is 1), even with a single fixed rate vector.
Each individual class law is tight. A selected mixture over labels may
also be tight, but the family of extremal class laws is not, and the
reducible full CTMC has no unique invariant law.

The smallest exact witness is E=empty, J={1}, I={1}: 0<->A at rates
alpha,delta and an inert B. The classes are {(a,z):a>=0}; their unique
stationary laws are Pois(alpha/delta) tensor delta_z. This is also a
complex-balanced example. It blocks a uniform full-state exponential
moment/tightness claim while leaving every class uniformly well behaved.

COMPLEX-BALANCED SUBFAMILY: EXACT LAWS
-------------------------------------
The geometric-drift result does not require complex balance. For reference,
the exact complex-balance equations for this sparse family are as follows.
There must be c_j,c_i>0 such that, for each edge (j,i),

  c_j = alpha_j/delta_j,
  c_i = p_ji/q_ji,
  c_j^(k_ji) = u_ji/v_ji.

For each active i, p_ji/q_ji must be the same for all j in N(i); for each
j, (u_ji/v_ji)^(1/k_ji) must equal alpha_j/delta_j for every i in N(j).
These conditions are necessary and sufficient: balance at the high-order
complex gives u c_j c_i=v c_j^(k+1)c_i; balance at A_j+B_i then reduces
to p c_j=q c_j c_i; balance at A_j reduces to alpha_j=delta_j c_j;
the equation at 0 follows by summing the catalyst equations. An isolated
substrate does not occur in any complex, so its Poisson mean is arbitrary
in an unrestricted full-lattice product law.

For these complex-balanced rates the active class law is exactly

  pi_theta^*(a,b_+) = product_j Pois(c_j)(a_j)
                         product_(i in I_+) Pois(c_i)(b_i),

and the full class law tensors this with delta_z. This is the standard
complex-balanced product-Poisson theorem of Anderson, Craciun, and Kurtz
(2010, Theorem 4.1). It has moments
E exp(eta A_j)=exp(c_j(e^eta-1)) and
E exp(eta B_i)=exp(c_i(e^eta-1)). Within the compact positive rate box,
c_j<=alpha_j^+/delta_j^- and c_i<=p_ji^+/q_ji^- (for any neighbor), so
the exact laws have uniform exponential moments for active catalyst and
substrate counts on the complex-balanced subfamily. This stronger
catalyst conclusion uses product form and should not be attributed to the
generic Foster V. The class restriction fixes every isolated coordinate,
so no full-label uniform moment results.

The product-Poisson theorem gives the formal invariant. Non-explosivity
for complex-balanced stochastic mass action is separately established by
Anderson, Cappelletti, Koyama, and Kurtz (2018, Corollary 2). In the
displayed family, the geometric Foster proof above already gives
non-explosion for every rate vector in the specified box, whether or not
it is complex balanced.

SCOPE, FAILURE BOUNDARY, AND STATUS
----------------------------------
This theorem is for a fixed finite paired-edge family, with positive
immigration and linear death for every catalyst, every included edge
reversible with positive rates, and fixed k_ji>=1. Missing reverse
channels, zero lower rate endpoints, unbounded rate endpoints, or new
reaction channels are outside the common-box conclusion. The previous
pointwise sparse support-switch escaping-sequence proof gives a finite-set
Foster bound at fixed rates; by itself it does not imply geometric drift,
uniform stationary moments, or mixing. The stronger box drift was proved
here by reserving separate parts of each large-substrate edge term for
low-edge catalyst contributions, support-switch surcharges, and residual
linear/exponential coercivity.

The strict positive lower and finite upper endpoints are sufficient
uniformity assumptions, not asserted to be individually necessary for each
network. Uniformity can genuinely fail at a box boundary: with E=empty,
alpha=1 and delta in (0,1], the class stationary law for A is
Pois(1/delta), so its mean and first-moment tail bound diverge as delta
decreases to zero. Allowing alpha to grow without bound likewise destroys
uniform tightness in this immigration-death example.

This exact class classification does not generalize to arbitrary weakly
reversible networks. N77 supplies a distinct complex-balanced family with
infinitely many nonlinear stochastic classes and class stationary laws
escaping within one deterministic stoichiometric plane. That obstruction
does not occur in the present sparse paired-edge family because its
explicit build/clear paths prove there are no additional active-coordinate
classes.

No claim of literature novelty, external proof validation of the sparse
uniform drift, or a general recurrence theorem is made. A mathematical
review should check the allocation of high-edge drift among overlapping
catalyst and support-switch obligations, which is the load-bearing step.
