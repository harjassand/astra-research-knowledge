C01_S02 — TWO EXACT FAILURES OF NAIVE HIGH-SECTOR TRANSFER
These are route-specific variance obstructions, not counting hardness.

1. ORIGINAL SIGN ESTIMATOR REALLY HAS EXPONENTIAL HIGH-k VARIANCE
Let n=2k and F be k independent unit bidirected swap blocks. For independent
signs, the full skew Pfaffian is product_b(s_(2b-1)-s_(2b)), up to sign.
Therefore Y=product_b(s_(2b-1)-s_(2b))^2, with
  E Y=2^k, E Y^2=8^k, E Y^2/(E Y)^2=2^k.
Y is nonzero with probability2^-k. A sample mean or median of batch means
that sees no nonzero sample outputs0; success probability <=T2^-k for T
total independent samples. Constant-confidence relative accuracy below1
therefore requires Omega(2^k) samples, regardless of the loose9^k upper bound.
This family is exactly counted by the competent monomial/frontier baselines.
It proves no hardness and does not refute another estimator.

For uniform fourth roots, one block has |z1-z2|^2 values0,2,4 with probabilities
1/4,1/2,1/4. Thus its mean2, second moment6, and the product's relative
second moment is(3/2)^k. Nonzero probability is(3/4)^k. c01_s01 independently
derived this phase case and stronger dense counterexamples; credited as peer
origin for the four-phase extension. Neither sign nor independent phase
sample-mean schemes furnish a general high-k polynomial guarantee.

2. PHYSICAL SECTOR-GROWTH SAMPLE RATIOS CAN FAIL EVEN FROM k=1 TO k=2
For generic paired volumes and positive k-pair S define
  R(S)=sum_{i notin S} det(U_i^* P_S U_i),
where U_i is its two-column pair and P_S projects off the selected span.
By the Schur complement, g(S) times the ith term equals g(S union{i}).
Summing over S counts each positive (k+1)-pair set k+1 times, so
  E_(pi_k) R=(k+1) Z_(k+1)/Z_k.
This unbiased ratio identity does NOT bound relative variance.

Canonical n4 example: only F_12=a=2^-L and F_34=1 are nonzero.
The k1 paired weights are a^2 and1; c1=1+a^2. The unique k2 paired set has
weight a^2; c2=a^2. On the rare a^2-weight state R=1, and on the common state
R=a^2. Consequently
  E R=2a^2/(1+a^2), E R^2=a^2,
  E R^2/(E R)^2=(1+a^2)^2/(4a^2)=Omega(4^L).
All entries have O(L) encoding bits. Arbitrarily accurate low-sector draws do
not repair the rare sample needed by this ratio estimator. If no rare state
is seen, the sample mean is a^2, approximately half the true mean; for L>=2
relative tolerance1/3 requires at least one rare observation. The probability
of that event in T independent draws is <=T a^2/(1+a^2), giving an
Omega(4^L) constant-confidence sample lower bound for that direct ratio route.
Again the example itself is trivially countable and supplies no general
hardness, no obstruction to rational-activity annealing with a separately
acquired sampler, and no bound against adaptive structural methods.
