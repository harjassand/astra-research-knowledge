# Cycle 2 — from kinetic assays to a finite-horizon graph-safety certificate

## Result and status

I composed an acquisition-to-control interface for the finite-volume stochastic safety theorem in [`PROOF.md`](PROOF.md). It turns time-uniform confidence intervals for stationary kinetic coefficients into a finite list of exact worst-case box-face checks; if every mode passes, the existing N35-style exit estimate applies to operation chosen after the assay. The confidence-sequence argument, interval endpoint arithmetic, and union-bound composition are standard tools combined here. I claim no novelty or priority. There are no physical measurements, and no real material has been certified.

The new content is a conditional theorem and executable arithmetic checker, not an empirical controller. It is stronger than a supplied-parameter theorem only in this explicit sense: the reaction and reciprocal-hop coefficients may be unknown before a pilot assay, and the controller may select a stopping time from the pilot and then switch predictably among a finite menu of operating modes. The acquired interval event carries a separately charged error probability.

## Acquired interface

Fix a finite graph, positive site-volume factors, the concentration box and collar widths, a finite menu of modes, the integer stoichiometry, a stated reciprocal finite-volume propensity model, and a justified finite cap (K_j) for each unknown coefficient. In mode (m), each unknown but stationary coefficient θ_j has channel intensity

\[
\lambda_j(t)=\theta_j h_j(X_t,m_t),
\]

where the nonnegative predictable exposure rate (h_j) is known from observed molecule counts, the mode, geometry, and the supplied model. For a local reaction at site (i), (h=\mathbf1\{m_t=m\}\alpha_iV\phi^{\alpha_iV}_{y}(x_i)). For one reciprocal edge/species coefficient, combine both hop directions and use (h=\mathbf1\{m_t=m\}V(x_{i,s}+x_{j,s})). Counts and exposures are (N_j(t)) and (A_j(t)=\int_0^t h_j(X_u,m_u)\,du).

For a fixed Gamma(shape (a), rate (b)) mixing law on the alternative rate, define

\[
M_{j,t}(\theta)=\frac{b^a\Gamma(a+N_j(t))}{\Gamma(a)(b+A_j(t))^{a+N_j(t)}}\,\theta^{-N_j(t)}e^{\theta A_j(t)}.
\]

Under the candidate coefficient θ, each likelihood ratio inside the Gamma mixture is a nonnegative martingale, since the common predictable exposure cancels from the counting-process likelihood. Their mixture (M_{j,t}(\theta)) is therefore a nonnegative martingale of initial value one. Ville's inequality gives

\[
\Pr_\theta\!\left(\exists t: M_{j,t}(\theta)\ge P/\delta\right)\le\delta/P,
\]

for (P) acquired coefficients. Thus the simultaneous confidence sets

\[
\mathcal C_j(t)=\{\theta\in[0,K_j]: M_{j,t}(\theta)<P/\delta\}
\]

cover every true coefficient for all assay times with probability at least (1-\delta), by a union bound. Their closures are intervals because (\log M_{j,t}(\theta)) is convex in θ. Empty intervals, zero exposure, or an interval touching zero where positive inward drift is required cause certification to fail. In particular, optional stopping after seeing the intervals does not invalidate coverage.

For each operating mode, let ([L_{ir,m},U_{ir,m}]) be the acquired reaction-coefficient intervals. For lower face ((i,s,-)), collar width ℓ, and box corners (a,b), the worst reaction drift is bounded below by

\[
\underline\gamma^-_{i,s,m}=
\sum_{\nu_{ir,s}>0}\nu_{ir,s}L_{ir,m}\,a_s^{y_{ir,s}}\prod_{t\ne s}a_t^{y_{ir,t}}
-\sum_{\nu_{ir,s}<0}|\nu_{ir,s}|U_{ir,m}\,(a_s+\ell)^{y_{ir,s}}\prod_{t\ne s}b_t^{y_{ir,t}}.
\]

For the upper face it is

\[
\underline\gamma^+_{i,s,m}=
\sum_{\nu_{ir,s}<0}|\nu_{ir,s}|L_{ir,m}\,(b_s-\ell)^{y_{ir,s}}\prod_{t\ne s}a_t^{y_{ir,t}}
-\sum_{\nu_{ir,s}>0}\nu_{ir,s}U_{ir,m}\,b_s^{y_{ir,s}}\prod_{t\ne s}b_t^{y_{ir,t}}.
\]

These are minima over the full rectangular rate intervals and collar: each monomial is coordinatewise increasing, so no nonlinear optimizer is required. Correlations between coefficient intervals are discarded, which may reject a safe design but cannot create a false positive. Apply the formulas for every mode and take the minimum margin. For each reciprocal edge coefficient, take the largest acquired upper endpoint over modes and incident edges, (C_{i,s}=\sum_j C_{ij,s}). The effective margin for a face is

\[
\bar\gamma_q=\min_m\underline\gamma_{q,m}-C_{i,s}\ell_q/\alpha_i.
\]

If any γ-bar is nonpositive, stop without a certificate. Otherwise substitute these margins and conservative coefficient caps into the constants of [`PROOF.md`](PROOF.md); the theorem there handles arbitrary predictable switching among the certified finite modes.

## Joint guarantee

Let δ be the total calibration error budget, and let

\[
\varepsilon_{\rm op}(T;D)=\sum_q e^{-\theta_qVg_q(X_0)}+2MV\Lambda T e^{-c_*V}
\]

be the operation bound from `PROOF.md`, with all constants computed from the acquired intervals and supplied geometry. With probability at least (1-\delta) over the pilot, every true coefficient is in its reported interval at every look. On any pilot record in that coverage event, if rates are stationary per mode and operation starts with the stated gaps, its conditional exit probability is at most (\varepsilon_{\rm op}(T;D)). If deployment is gated on positive margins and (\varepsilon_{\rm op}(T;D)\le\varepsilon_{\rm target}), then

\[
\Pr\{\text{pilot coverage fails or operation exits }Q\text{ by }T\}
\le \delta+\varepsilon_{\rm target}.
\]

Proof: on the simultaneous coverage event, the true parameters lie in every interval. The endpoint calculations therefore bound each mode's deterministic collar drift below. The symmetric graph maximum-principle estimate subtracts at most (C_{i,s}\ell_q/\alpha_i), leaving the strictly positive effective margin. The existing finite-volume exponential-supermartingale proof applies uniformly to every predictable mode schedule and yields conditional operational exit probability at most ε-op for that pilot record. The deployment gate enforces ε-op at most ε-target; the complement of coverage has probability at most δ. Adding the two failure probabilities proves the display. This is finite-horizon safety, not infinite-time permanence.

## Assay cost and operational cost

The minimal sufficient statistics per channel are the event count and integrated exposure, but obtaining them still requires calibrated event detection and the supplied covariate (h_j). A coefficient with zero observed exposure cannot acquire a positive lower bound. Under a separate clamped assay with constant known (h_j\ge h_{j,\min}>0), Poisson Chernoff gives a simple planning bound: relative error η in every one of (P) coefficients with (\theta_j\ge\theta_{\min}) and failure at most δ is ensured if

\[
\theta_{\min} A_{\min}\ge 3\eta^{-2}\log(2P/\delta),\qquad
t_{\rm assay}\le A_{\min}/h_{\min}
\]

when channels are measured in parallel to that deterministic exposure; set (h_{\min}=\min_jh_{j,\min}). If assays must be run separately, elapsed time is bounded by (\sum_j A_{\min}/h_{j,\min}). The expected number of observed events per weakest-rate channel is then at least the left side. This is an explicit lower-rate/excitation requirement, not an optimal sampling strategy. Real costs not priced by the model include preparation and reset, concentration mapping, event-classification and calibration error, chemical feedstock, temperature/voltage drive, waste handling, and the energy needed to clamp the assay state. In operation, the total CTMC activity bound is (V\Lambda); direct event simulation/thinning to horizon (T) can require (O(V\Lambda T)) candidate events. The exponential-risk horizon in the two-site example is computationally impractical to verify by brute-force trajectory sampling.

`certificate_checker.py` computes the mixture confidence interval, interval endpoint margins, and reciprocal transport collar loss. `acquired_graph_demo.py` runs the entire pipeline on a **synthetic summary**, not measurements: four reaction channels each supplied with (N=A=10{,}000), one hop channel with (N=10{,}000,A=200{,}000), total calibration error δ=0.05 split across five channels, and caps 2 for reactions and 0.2 for hopping. The resulting intervals are [0.9575, 1.0438] and [0.04766, 0.05242]. On the two-site box example with α=(1,3), these produce effective lower/upper face margins (0.1616, 0.1401) and (0.1703, 0.1488). At (V=25{,}000), the certified model bound is (7.51\times10^{-6}) through a horizon of (2.82\times10^{11}) rate-time units. The (V\Lambda T) event-activity envelope is (7.48\times10^{16}), so brute-force simulation is not a credible validation plan. The fixture uses 50,000 supplied pilot events and 240,000 integrated-exposure units; physical elapsed time, instrument energy, preparation, and reset remain unmeasured. `test_certificate_checker.py` passes three checks, including that zero exposure does not invent a positive lower rate. These diagnostics check arithmetic only; they do not prove coverage or validate nature.

## Model versus nature and hostile boundary

Stationarity of a per-mode coefficient, correct reaction labels, the factorial propensity law, known state-dependent exposure, finite-volume scaling, known site volumes, and reciprocal edge transport are supplied assumptions. The confidence sequence estimates coefficients inside that model. It does not test whether the model family is true, infer reciprocity from finite counts, or protect against unresolved channels, drift, activity-coefficient changes, correlated hops, changing geometry, missed reactions, or concentration measurement bias. A separate model-checking and calibration protocol would be required, and finite data cannot certify that no hidden mechanism exists. If transport is directed or unbalanced, this branch's symmetric maximum principle does not apply; the engineering branch is handling that distinct graph interface.

The theorem's acquisition step is compatible with a many-run experiment under stationarity. A physical material may fail earlier: it may have no positive restoring reaction margin near one of the safety limits, or the interval radius may be too large to preserve a positive margin after worst-case migration. Those are explicit certificate failures. Initial margin, throughput, future drift, controller latency, mesh growth, and full energy and waste costs remain application-specific. In the worked model in `PROOF.md`, irreducibility plus immigration/removal implies eventual exit almost surely; the certificate only bounds a prescribed finite horizon.

## Prior art and separation from N33

The source foundation is the supplied N35 stochastic acquired-polytope certificate and its internal full proof at `work/agents/systems_metabolism/chemostat_control/astra/jump_safety.txt`. That result already supplies the well-mixed finite-volume exponential exit argument. The present branch uses the box-specialized reciprocal heterogeneous-graph extension proved in `PROOF.md`; it does not repeat the engineering branch's directed/unbalanced edge extension.

Time-uniform confidence sequences and nonnegative-mixture-martingale/Ville arguments are standard; see Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe, and Sekhon, “Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences,” *Annals of Statistics* 49(2), 1055–1080 (2021), [DOI 10.1214/20-AOS1991](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991). The particular Gamma-mixture likelihood above follows directly from the counting-process likelihood and is derived here. RDME finite-volume hopping prior art is Stundzia–Lumsden (1996), [DOI 10.1006/jcph.1996.0168](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1996.0168). Large-volume chemical-jump exit asymptotics are covered by Agazzi–Dembo–Eckmann (2018), [DOI 10.1214/17-AAP1344](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-AAP1344); they do not equal this finite-volume acquired, predictable-mode guarantee.

This result uses N35 only: an acquired finite-time stochastic safety certificate for integer-jump reaction systems. It neither proves nor assumes N33, the separate deterministic all-endotactic permanence result. It does not claim permanence, an absorbing set, an infinite-time stochastic guarantee, or a rate bound uniform over growing graph families.
