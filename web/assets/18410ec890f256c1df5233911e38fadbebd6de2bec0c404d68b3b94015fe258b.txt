# Exact margin-to-volume scaling in the m=2 return certificate

For (M=2^b), set (kappa=1), (K=M^2),
(p=1/(M+1)), and (q=M/(M+1)^2=p-(M+1)^{-2}). These are rational
inputs with (O(b)) total bit length. The certificate function used in
`INITIAL.txt` is

\[
f(q)=(1-q)^2-M^2q^2
     ={2M^3+3M^2+2M+1\over(M+1)^4}.
\]

For (m=2), its buffer is

\[
\eta={f(q)\over4(1+M^2)}\sim {1\over2M^3}.
\]

The sufficient even-volume floor is the least even integer at least
\(\max(4,2/q,1/\eta)\), so it satisfies (V_0\sim2M^3=2^{3b+1}).
Thus certificate output can remain polynomial in the *binary* input length
while its sufficient numeric volume threshold is exponential in that length.
For the sharp Bernoulli exit exponent,
\(\Delta=2D(q\Vert p)\sim M^{-3}\), by the second-order expansion at
(q=p). This confirms margin dependence: a fixed positive exponent per unit
volume cannot be uniform over these short inputs.

The scaling is a cost obstruction for this sufficient certificate and a
uniform-margin claim. It is not a lower bound on necessary volume, an
algorithmic hardness result, or a refutation of fixed-input return bounds.
`margin_volume_scaling.py` checks the exact rational identity and threshold
calculation for (b=2,\ldots,12); the asymptotic statements follow from the
displayed formulas, not those finite checks.
