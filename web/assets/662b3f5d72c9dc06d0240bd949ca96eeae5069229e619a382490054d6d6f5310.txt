PRIMARY-SOURCE AUDIT: SCHMIDT-RANK PROJECTOR OVERLAP AND THE GREEN CUTOFF
8 October 2026. Bounded targeted audit; not a novelty or priority assessment.

Question and normalization
--------------------------
The local claim in ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt is the following. Let
H=C^d carry an irreducible unitary representation U, identify End(H) with
H tensor conjugate(H) by a Hilbert-Schmidt isometry vec, and let Pi be an
orthogonal projector onto a conjugation-invariant subspace of dimension b.
For a rank-k orthogonal projection P on H,

  ||Pi vec(P)||^2 = ||Pi P||_HS^2 <= k^2 b/d.                 (A)

Here traces are ordinary (unnormalized) traces, ||P||_HS^2=k, and the unit
vector vec(P)/sqrt(k) has Schmidt rank k. The representation hypothesis and
Schur's lemma give Tr_2(Pi)=(b/d) I (and likewise for the other marginal).
If a different vectorization convention is used, conjugate the second factor;
the dimension, Schmidt rank, and marginal normalization are unchanged.

Exact derivation and source-level overlap
-----------------------------------------
The bound is a direct consequence of the Schmidt-number reduction inequality.
For a unit vector x of Schmidt rank r<=k, rho=|x><x| and
rho_1=Tr_2 rho satisfy

  rho <= r (rho_1 tensor I).

For completeness, write x=sum_{i=1}^r sqrt(p_i) u_i tensor v_i. For every y,
|<x,y>|^2 <= r sum_i p_i |<u_i tensor v_i,y>|^2
             <= r <y,(rho_1 tensor I)y>,
which proves the operator inequality. Pair it with Pi>=0 and use the partial
trace identity:

  <x,Pi x> <= r Tr(Pi(rho_1 tensor I))
             = r Tr(rho_1 Tr_2 Pi) = rb/d <= kb/d.

Putting x=vec(P)/sqrt(k) proves (A). In particular, this is not limited to
irreducible spectral projectors: it applies to every positive operator Q with
Tr_2 Q=(b/d)I, where b=Tr Q. Invariance is one sufficient way to get that
marginal condition.

The closest exact primary antecedent is Terhal and Horodecki's reduction-map
argument. Their arXiv v4 Lemma 2 states that Lambda_p(X)=Tr(X)I-pX is k-positive
for 1/(k+1)<p<=1/k. Their definition of k-positivity therefore gives, for any
state rho of Schmidt number <=k,

  (id tensor Lambda_{1/k})(rho)=rho_1 tensor I-rho/k >=0,
  hence rho <= k(rho_1 tensor I).

Their Lemma 1 separately states that a Schmidt-number-k density matrix on
C^d tensor C^d has overlap at most k/d with every maximally entangled
rank-one state. This is exactly (A) when b=1 and Pi is a maximally entangled
projector. The reduction-map inequality plus Tr_2 Pi=(b/d)I extends that exact
overlap to arbitrary b. Thus the generic overlap step has a clear, older
entanglement-theoretic route; the group representation is only used to verify
the maximally mixed marginal.

Primary sources checked
-----------------------
1. B. M. Terhal and P. Horodecki, “A Schmidt number for density matrices,”
   arXiv:quant-ph/9911117v4, submitted 29 Nov 1999, v4 dated 6 Mar 2000;
   Phys. Rev. A 61, 040301(R) (2000), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.61.040301.
   Primary versions: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9911117v4 and
   https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9911117v4 . Lemma 1 uses unit-trace density
   matrices and maximizes over normalized maximally entangled vectors; it gives
   f(rho)<=k/d. Lemma 2 gives the k-positive reduction map above, but attributes
   that map threshold to earlier references. It does not state the b-dimensional
   projector version or a spectral inverse-eigenvalue sum.

2. N. Johnston and D. W. Kribs, “A Family of Norms With Applications In Quantum
   Information Theory,” arXiv:0909.3907v4, submitted 22 Sep 2009, v4 dated
   20 Aug 2010; J. Math. Phys. 51, 082202 (2010), DOI 10.1063/1.3459068.
   Primary versions: https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3907v4 and
   https://arxiv.org/html/0909.3907v4 . Theorem 3.3 defines the s(k)-norm of a
   bipartite vector as the supremum of |<w,v>| over unit Schmidt-rank-at-most-k
   vectors w, and evaluates it as the square root of the sum of the k largest
   squared Schmidt coefficients of v. For the normalized maximally entangled
   vector this squared norm is k/d; Example 4.4 equivalently gives S(k)-norm
   k/d for its rank-one projection. This exactly matches b=1. It does not
   evaluate a general rank-b invariant projector's S(k)-norm or any Green sum.

3. G. Alagic and A. Russell, “Uncertainty Principles for Compact Groups,”
   arXiv:math/0608702v2, submitted 28 Aug 2006, v2 dated 29 Aug 2008;
   Illinois J. Math. 52(4), 1315–1324 (2008). Primary versions:
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608702v2 and
   https://arxiv.org/html/math/0608702v2 . Theorem 1 uses normalized Haar
   measure and operators on L^2(G): if P commutes with every measurable-set
   projection and R commutes with left translations, then
   ||PR||_HS=||P chi_G||_2 ||R||_HS, hence ||PR||_op is at most that product.
   For finite G, Corollary 1 gives ||PR||_op^2<=rank(P)rank(R)/|G| under the
   corresponding coordinate/Fourier commutation assumptions. This is a close
   representation-uncertainty analogue, but its P is a spatial multiplier and
   R is translation invariant. It is not the Schmidt-rank vector overlap (A),
   has a different norm/normalization, and contains no lambda_j^{-1} weighting.

4. T. S. Cubitt, A. Montanaro, and A. Winter, “On the dimension of subspaces
   with bounded Schmidt rank,” arXiv:0706.0705v1, 5 Jun 2007; J. Math. Phys.
   49, 022107 (2008), DOI 10.1063/1.2862998. Primary version:
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.0705 . Their exact theorem gives maximum dimension
   (d_A-r+1)(d_B-r+1) for a subspace all of whose nonzero vectors have Schmidt
   rank at least r. This is a subspace-dimension/intersection theorem, not an
   upper bound on the overlap of a low-Schmidt-rank vector with a specified
   invariant projector, and it has no energy or Green weighting.

5. L. Pankowski, M. Piani, M. Horodecki, and P. Horodecki, “A few steps more
   towards NPT bound entanglement,” arXiv:0711.2613v2, submitted 16 Nov 2007,
   v2 dated 15 Feb 2010; IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 56, 4085–4100 (2010),
   DOI 10.1109/TIT.2010.2050810. Primary version:
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.2613 . They study the maximum overlap of a
   particular projector Q with Schmidt-rank-two vectors (the “half-property”);
   the paper proves the target 1/2 bound for broad classes and an all-vector
   upper bound c<3/4. This confirms the projector-overlap problem family is
   established, but the Q is specific, the desired constant is not obtained
   universally, and there is no Casimir/eigenvalue-weighted Green statement.

6. P. Bérard and D. Meyer, “Inégalités isopérimétriques et applications,”
   Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. (4) 15(3), 513–541 (1982), DOI 10.24033/asens.1435.
   Primary publisher copy: https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.1435.pdf .
   Theorem 1.5 assumes a compact smooth n-manifold without boundary with
   Ricci curvature bounded below by n-1; for a smooth domain V, its first
   Dirichlet eigenvalue is at least that of the equal-volume geodesic ball V*
   in the unit sphere. This is a spatial-domain first-eigenvalue comparison.
   It does not constrain a matrix kernel, Schmidt rank, spectral-projector
   overlaps, or the inverse-eigenvalue quadratic form in the target.

7. J. Bertrand and B. Colbois, “Capacité et inégalité de Faber-Krahn dans
   l'espace euclidien,” arXiv:math/0504170v1, submitted 8 Apr 2005. Primary
   version: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0504170 and
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0504170 . Their Theorem 2.2 defines a Dirichlet
   capacity for a subset A removed from a compact manifold with smooth boundary.
   It controls the shifts of the Dirichlet eigenvalues of Omega\A; for small
   capacity it gives 0<=lambda_k(Omega\A)-lambda_k(Omega)<=C_k Cap(A)^(1/2),
   with constants depending on Omega and k. This is a genuine capacity/spectral
   perturbation result, but its variable is a spatial excision set. It supplies
   no inequality for arbitrary matrix kernels or the target Green sum.

The Green cutoff: what follows and what does not
------------------------------------------------
For the stated conjugation-invariant self-adjoint L, let Pi_j be its positive
spectral projectors, with eigenvalues 0<lambda_1<...<lambda_J and dimensions
b_j. Since each Pi_j kills scalars, Pi_j P_0=Pi_j P, where
P_0=P-(k/d)I. The spectral theorem and (A) give, for every 0<=K<=J,

  G(P)=sum_{j=1}^J ||Pi_j P||_HS^2/lambda_j
      <= (k^2/d) sum_{j=1}^K b_j/lambda_j
         + (1/lambda_{K+1}) sum_{j>K}||Pi_j P_0||_HS^2
      <= (k^2/d) sum_{j=1}^K b_j/lambda_j + k/lambda_{K+1},

where lambda_{J+1}=infinity. In the final step,
sum_j||Pi_jP_0||_HS^2=||P_0||_HS^2=k-k^2/d<=k. Thus the stated B_K
cutoff is a valid consequence of (i) the generic partial-trace/Schmidt-rank
overlap and (ii) spectral ordering plus the total mass identity. The references
above supply or parallel the overlap ingredient; none of the checked primary
sources states this inverse-eigenvalue weighted sum, the cutoff B_K, or the
resulting rank-(d-k) lower bound for arbitrary matrices. Classical Faber-Krahn
and capacity theorems concern spatial domains and do not bridge that mismatch.

Audit boundary
--------------
This was a targeted primary-source search, not an exhaustive literature review.
The exact local overlap follows from a standard Schmidt-number reduction
inequality; any claim of novelty for its application to the invariant Green
quadratic form would need a broader priority search. No priority conclusion is
made here.
