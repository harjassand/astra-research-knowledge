PRIMARY SOURCE LEDGER — INVERSE-PROBLEM ARCHIVES
Retrieved/checked 2026-10-08. These establish prior-art boundaries, not the new scoped archive candidate.

1. Masahito Hayashi and Vincent Y. F. Tan, “Minimum Rates of Approximate Sufficient Statistics,” arXiv:1612.02542, submitted 2016-12-08; later IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 64(2), 1094–1115 (2018).
   Primary version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02542
   Relevant scope: approximate sufficient-statistic coding for parametric families; its abstract describes d/2 log n + O(1) rates under stated asymptotic criteria. This is antecedent compression theory, not the late unknown singular-direction query theorem here.

2. Jan Johannes and Maik Schwarz, “Adaptive Gaussian inverse regression with partially unknown operator,” arXiv:1204.1226, submitted 2012-04-05; published Communications in Statistics—Theory and Methods (2013), DOI 10.1080/03610926.2012.731548.
   Primary version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.1226
   Relevant scope: inverse regression with known eigenfunctions and unknown singular values observed with Gaussian noise; adaptive minimax estimation and data-driven regularization. This explicitly models spectrum acquisition, but not whole-law archive TV or unknown singular vectors.

3. Clément Marteau and Theofanis Sapatinas, “Minimax Goodness-of-Fit Testing in Ill-Posed Inverse Problems with Partially Unknown Operators,” arXiv:1503.08562, submitted 2015-03-30.
   Primary version: https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08562
   Relevant scope: singular functions known, singular values partially unknown and observed with Gaussian noise; non-asymptotic testing radii for ordinary/supersmooth classes. Prior art for charged noisy operator acquisition.

4. A. S. Holevo, “Bounds for the Quantity of Information Transmitted by a Quantum Communication Channel,” Problemy Peredachi Informatsii 9(3), 3–11 (1973); English translation Problems of Information Transmission 9(3), 177–183.
   Primary journal archive: https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi/v9/i3/p3
   Relevant scope: accessible information bound for generalized measurements. The use in N36 also needs the Gaussian proper-score transfer and sequential/conditional entropy argument.

5. Ashwin Nayak, “Optimal Lower Bounds for Quantum Automata and Random Access Codes,” arXiv:quant-ph/9904093, submitted 1999-04-27; FOCS 1999, DOI 10.1109/SFFCS.1999.814608.
   Author-hosted primary paper: https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anayak/papers/Nayak99.pdf
   Relevant scope: entropy/Holevo lower bounds for random-access decoding. It is a mechanism antecedent; it does not establish the continuous-output Gaussian lower constant inherited from N36.

6. Johannes and Schwarz, publisher record for the inverse-regression paper.
   https://doi.org/10.1080/03610926.2012.731548
   Used only to confirm bibliographic publication metadata; the arXiv manuscript is the mathematical source read here.

7. A. S. Holevo, “Statistical decision theory for quantum systems,” Journal of Multivariate Analysis 3(4), 337–394 (1973), published December 1973.
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0047259X73900286
   Relevant scope: operator-valued measures and quantum statistical decision framework; cited as foundational setting, not as the new archive theorem.

No full search of all inverse-problem, rate-distortion, or quantum-memory literature was performed. Exact priority of the N30/N31/N36/N66 scoped candidates remains UNKNOWN.
