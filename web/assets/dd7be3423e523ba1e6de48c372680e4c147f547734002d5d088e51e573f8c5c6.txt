CYCLE 4 LEAF — ADVERSARIAL SWITCH-BOUNDARY AUDIT
Date: 2026-10-08

Question
--------
Stress-test the sparse support-switch exponential Foster function under
near-sparse supports and one-way/missing-reverse reaction variants. Identify
which positive rates are needed by each switch and by the high-coordinate
tail arguments. Give exact generator counterexamples when a necessary
certificate term is absent, and keep certificate failure separate from
recurrence/transience.

Conclusion
----------
The support correction is sound for a fixed finite support graph when every
dynamic substrate has at least one local immigration route, every active
edge has positive substrate-removal rate q, every catalyst has positive
linear death rate delta, and every positive-substrate catalyst tail has a
positive reverse-amplification rate v (or an explicit replacement
coefficient condition). For the pointwise theorem, these rates need only be
fixed and positive; compact positive lower bounds are needed for uniform
constants over a rate box.

Weak reversibility of every pair is stronger than the drift proof needs:
p_ji and u_ji enter as nonnegative positive drift terms, so setting either
to zero does not hurt this Foster argument. They may be needed for the
claimed communicating-class structure. In contrast, setting q_ji=0 while
p_ji,u_ji,epsilon_i,delta_j are positive destroys the exponential substrate
tail at the local last-neighbor boundary. Setting v_ji=0 can destroy the
large-catalyst tail on a positive-substrate stratum. Setting a local
immigration rate to zero can leave a non-global inactive face with no
negative switching drift. These are exact failures of this Lyapunov
certificate; recurrence is decided separately below.

1. Baseline and exact local switch identity
-------------------------------------------

Use finite catalyst set J, substrate set I, and declared support E. For
(j,i) in E the full paired family has propensities

  0 -> A_j: alpha_j,
  A_j -> 0: delta_j a_j,
  A_j -> A_j+B_i: p_ji a_j,
  A_j+B_i -> A_j: q_ji a_j b_i,
  A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i: u_ji a_j b_i,
  (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i -> A_j+B_i:
                            v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i,

where (a)_m is the falling factorial and k_ji>=1. Let N(i) be the actual
local neighbor set, h_i(a)=1{sum_(j in N(i)) a_j=0}, I_0(a)=1{sum_j a_j=0},
and

  V(a,b)=sum_j lambda_j a_j
         +sum_{i:N(i)!=empty} R_i^(b_i)(1+epsilon_i h_i(a))
         +K I_0(a),

with lambda_j>0, R_i>1, epsilon_i>0. Isolated B_i coordinates are frozen
labels and must be omitted from h_i; the Foster statement is classwise after
fixing them.

Write c_i=R_i-1, d_i=1-R_i^(-1), J_ij(a)=1{a_j=1 and all other local
neighbors of i have count zero}, alpha(N(i))=sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j,
alpha_sum=sum_j alpha_j, and H=sum_j alpha_j lambda_j. The exact generator is

  LV = H - sum_j lambda_j delta_j a_j
       + sum_(j,i) [p_ji c_i a_j R_i^b_i
                   -q_ji d_i a_j b_i R_i^b_i
                   +lambda_j k_ji u_ji a_j b_i
                   -lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i]
       -K alpha_sum I_0 + K sum_j delta_j 1{a=e_j}
       +sum_i epsilon_i R_i^b_i[-alpha(N(i))h_i
                                  +sum_(j in N(i))delta_j J_ij].          (1)

Every last-neighbor death switch in (1) is local: J_ij=1 forces a_j=1,
regardless of how many non-neighbor catalysts are present. On that state the
same edge contributes -q_ji d_i b_i R_i^b_i; the positive switch is
epsilon_i delta_j R_i^b_i. Thus q_ji>0 and R_i>1 make the negative term
dominate the surcharge for b_i>epsilon_i delta_j/(q_ji d_i), before also
absorbing the other edge-positive terms. This is the exact mechanism; a
global one-catalyst case split is not needed.

At an inactive local face h_i=1, the switch is
-epsilon_i alpha(N(i))R_i^b_i. It is negative only if some positive-rate
reaction leaves that face (or if the face is exactly I_0 and the separate K
switch supplies the required drift). At an active face, a positive q-removal
rate is needed on every edge that can be the sole active neighbor unless an
alternative negative term is proved for that active subset.

2. Minimal assumptions for the stated proof
-------------------------------------------

These are minimal for the corresponding displayed mechanism, not necessary
conditions for recurrence by arbitrary Lyapunov functions.

  * Proper substrate weight and removal tail. R_i>1 is necessary for this
    particular exponential norm-like term and gives d_i>0. For a dynamic
    B_i with a potentially active catalyst, q_ji>0 on every edge used to
    control it gives -q_ji d_i b_i R_i^b_i. Fixed q_ji>0 suffices
    pointwise; a rate-uniform tail threshold needs q_ji bounded below.

  * Inactive local-face switch. epsilon_i>0 and alpha(N(i))>0 give the
    negative term -epsilon_i alpha(N(i))R_i^b_i. Only the sum over local
    neighbors is needed for this term; positivity of every alpha_j is a
    stronger, convenient condition for full-lattice irreducibility. If
    N(i)=J, then h_i=I_0 and the global K switch may replace this local
    switch. If a non-neighbor catalyst can be active while all of N(i) is
    zero, K I_0 cannot replace it.

  * Last-local-catalyst death switch. A death rate delta_j>0 creates the
    positive surcharge epsilon_i delta_j R_i^b_i when j is the last local
    neighbor. It is safe under the stated proof because the same edge has
    q_ji>0 and the q term has an extra factor b_i. If delta_j=0, this
    surcharge vanishes; no positivity requirement is needed for that switch
    itself.

  * Global all-catalysts-zero switch. At A=0,
    LV=H-K alpha_sum-sum_i epsilon_i alpha(N(i))R_i^b_i. A sufficient
    condition for LV<=-1 on this entire face is
    K alpha_sum+sum_i epsilon_i alpha(N(i)) >= H+1,
    because the supremum over b is at b=0. The simpler condition
    K>(H+1)/alpha_sum assumes alpha_sum>0 and ignores the favorable local
    switches. If alpha_sum=0 and all local alpha(N(i))=0, A=0 is closed and
    the generator is zero there; no strict drift can be supplied by K.

  * Catalyst escape with all adjacent substrates zero. For catalyst j, the
    exact linear coefficient at b_i=0 is
      -lambda_j delta_j + sum_(i in N(j)) p_ji(R_i-1).
    It must be strictly negative in the stated escaping-sequence proof. With
    finite p_ji and delta_j>0, choose R_i>1 close enough to one to achieve
    this. If delta_j=0 while some p_ji>0, the coefficient is positive for
    every R_i>1.

  * Catalyst escape with some adjacent substrate positive and bounded.
    v_ji>0 supplies a negative falling factorial of degree k_ji+1>=2 and
    dominates all edge-positive terms of degree at most one. If v_ji=0,
    this argument is unavailable. An alternative is to prove directly that
    for every catalyst j the coefficient
      -lambda_j delta_j + sum_(i in N(j))
        [p_ji c_i R_i^b_i-q_ji d_i b_i R_i^b_i
         +lambda_j k_ji u_ji b_i]
    is strictly negative on every nonzero bounded local substrate vector
    that can occur along an escape. Since q>0 makes each edge term tend to
    -infinity as its b_i grows, this is a finite residual check after a
    threshold is selected. Without v or this replacement check, the
    high-catalyst drift can be positive.

  * Forward channels. p_ji and u_ji are nonnegative positive-drift terms.
    Positivity of p_ji or u_ji is not needed for the negative-drift proof;
    setting either to zero removes a positive term and can only help the
    displayed inequalities. Positive p_ji is used for the simple
    full-lattice reachability proof (substrate creation); p_ji=0 can make
    B_i nonincreasing and the chain reducible. Positive u_ji is part of the
    reversible family but not needed for this Foster argument.

For the original all-positive paired-edge theorem, the compact assumptions
alpha_j,delta_j,p_ji,q_ji,u_ji,v_ji>0 for every declared coordinate are a
simple sufficient package. For the drift mechanism alone, a useful weaker
package is alpha_sum>0, alpha(N(i))>0 for every dynamic substrate whose
inactive face is not globally empty, delta_j>0, q_ji>0 on each relevant
active edge, finite p_ji,u_ji>=0, and v_ji>0 or the explicit residual
coefficient check above. This weaker package may define a reducible chain;
only closed communicating classes inherit a positive-recurrence conclusion
from an appropriate Foster theorem.

3. Exact local counterexamples: missing rates
---------------------------------------------

All values below use ordinary falling-factorial propensities. The formulas
are exact, not finite-box estimates.

3a. Missing q: the active-edge removal and death-switch terms fail together

Take one catalyst A, one substrate B, k=1, and state (a,b)=(1,n). For the
potential above, h=0 and I_0=0. Directly from (1),

  LV(1,n)=alpha lambda-lambda delta+K delta
           +(p(R-1)+epsilon delta-q d n)R^n+lambda u n.                (2)

If q=0 and p,epsilon,delta>0, (2) tends to +infinity as n tends to
infinity, for every fixed R>1 and every finite K,lambda,u. Even if epsilon
is set to zero, p>0 still forces positive exponential drift; if p=0 but
u>0, the positive lambda u n term remains. The high-order reverse-v term
is exactly zero at a=1. Thus the missing q edge cannot be hidden by v, and
the local last-neighbor switch is unpaired.

This is an exact failure of the proposed Foster inequality. It does not by
itself assert that every network with q=0 is transient. A simple separate
one-way network does have a genuine recurrence failure: take 0<->A at rates
alpha,delta>0 and A->A+B at rate p a, with no reverse B-removal. A is an
autonomous immigration-death chain with stationary mean alpha/delta. By its
ergodic theorem, integral_0^t A_s ds tends to infinity linearly almost
surely. The B-birth count is a time-changed Poisson process with integrated
intensity p integral_0^t A_s ds, so B_t tends to infinity almost surely.
Every finite state set is eventually left; this one-way variant is
transient. This conclusion uses monotonicity of B and is separate from the
certificate calculation (2).

3b. Missing local immigration alpha(N(i)): non-global inactive face

Use catalysts A_1,A_2, one substrate B, E={(1,1)}, and rates

  alpha_1=0, alpha_2=delta_1=delta_2=1,
  p_11=q_11=u_11=v_11=1, k_11=1.

The local inactive face h_1=1 contains states with A_2 present, so it is not
the global A=0 face. At (a_1,a_2,b)=(0,1,n), all edge reactions are disabled,
alpha(N(1))=alpha_1=0, and the exact generator is

  LV(0,1,n)=H-lambda_2 delta_2+K delta_2=K.                              (3)

At (0,0,n), LV=lambda_2-K, so making the global zero-catalyst face
negative requires K>lambda_2. Equation (3) is then positive for every n,
independent of lambda_1, R, and epsilon. No single K correction repairs this
non-global inactive face. In the closed subspace A_1=0,B=n, A_2 is simply an
immigration-death chain with Poisson(1) stationary law. Thus this certificate
failure occurs alongside recurrent closed classes; it is not transience.

This gives the sharp local condition: either alpha(N(i))>0 on the inactive
face or that face must be covered by another correction whose generator is
negative there. Global alpha_sum>0 alone is insufficient on a sparse graph.

3c. Missing epsilon_i support switch: all original rates positive

Take two catalysts A_1,A_2, one substrate B with E={(1,1)}, and all
alpha_j=delta_j=p_11=q_11=u_11=v_11=1, k=1. Try the same function with
epsilon_1=0. At (0,1,n), no B-edge is active and the exact drift is

  LV(0,1,n)=H-lambda_2+K=lambda_1+K.                                    (4)

At (0,0,n), LV=H-2K. Any choice making the all-zero face strictly negative
has K>H/2>0, so (4) is positive along an escaping sequence. The full CTMC
with these strictly positive rates is in the scoped sparse paired-edge
family, whose epsilon_1>0 support-corrected candidate proves classwise
positive recurrence. Hence (4) refutes the uncorrected Lyapunov template,
not recurrence of the process.

The topology matters: if every catalyst is a neighbor of i, h_i=I_0 and the
global K switch can sometimes replace epsilon_i. For a genuinely sparse
support with a remote active catalyst, a local correction or another
face-specific negative switch is needed.

3d. Missing v: positive drift on a fixed positive-substrate tail

Take one edge, k=1, lambda=1, delta=1, p=1, q=1/10, u=2, and set v=0.
For any R satisfying the stated catalyst margin

  chi=1-(R-1)>0, i.e. 1<R<2,

states (a,b)=(n,1) with n>=2 have h=0 and I_0=0. Equation (1) reduces to

  LV(n,1)=H+n[-1+(R-1)R-(1/10)(R-1)+2]
         =H+n[1+(R-1)(R-1/10)] > 0.                                     (5)

Thus no admissible R can make this candidate drift negative along the
positive-substrate catalyst tail. With v>0, the missing term is
-v n(n-1), which eventually dominates the positive linear coefficient.
Equation (5) is a drift-certificate failure; it does not assert recurrence
or transience for that exact q-positive network.

There is also an exact one-way transience witness for the missing reverse
amplifier in a frozen-catalyst slice. Let B be conserved at b>=1 and retain
0<->A plus A+B->2A+B at rate u a b, but omit 2A+B->A+B. The A process on
that class has birth rate alpha+ub a and death rate delta a. If ub>delta,
it is a supercritical linear birth-death process with immigration; immigrant
families have a positive survival probability, and infinitely many
immigration attempts imply escape to infinity almost surely. This is a
one-way, non-weakly-reversible recurrence counterexample; it is separate
from the drift-only example (5).

3e. Missing delta: loss of the zero-substrate catalyst margin

For one edge set delta=0 and p>0. At (a,b)=(n,0), n>=2, all support and
global-zero switches vanish, the q and u/v terms contain b=0, and

  LV(n,0)=alpha lambda + p(R-1)n -> +infinity.                            (6)

The condition chi=lambda delta-p(R-1)>0 is impossible. Again, this proves
failure of the displayed function. A minimal actual transient variant is
the pure immigration network 0->A at rate alpha>0 with no reverse A->0:
A is a Poisson counting process tending to infinity, so its states are
transient. It is not weakly reversible. If other reactions are retained,
their effect must be analyzed separately; (6) is not itself a general
transience theorem.

3f. Missing p or u: no drift obstruction, but a class/reachability change

Set p_ji=0 while retaining q_ji>0, alpha,delta,v>0. Then

  g_ji(m)= -q_ji d_i m R_i^m + lambda_j k_ji u_ji m -> -infinity,

and chi_j is easier to satisfy. The all-state escaping-sequence drift proof
is unchanged, though B_i is nonincreasing and the original full-lattice
irreducibility claim is false. Likewise u_ji=0 removes the positive
lambda_j k_ji u_ji a_j b_i term; with q_ji,v_ji>0 the drift proof still
works. A one-way reverse-amplifier death edge may improve drift even though
it is not weakly reversible.

These are concrete counterexamples to the claim that every direction of
every reversible pair is necessary for this Foster drift. Reversibility is
needed by the original named class/reachability theorem, not by each sign in
the generator inequality.

4. Near-sparse supports and near-zero rates
-------------------------------------------

One-edge degree is enough. The estimates depend on each substrate's local
neighbor set, not on graph completeness. A degree-one substrate works if its
one neighbor has positive immigration and the relevant q-removal rate is
positive. No lower bound on the degree beyond N(i) nonempty is needed for a
fixed finite network.

If an edge is removed so N(i)=empty, b_i is conserved and must be treated as
a class label, not as a dynamic coordinate controlled by h_i. Exact example:
J={1}, I={1}, E=empty, with 0<->A at rates alpha,delta>0. For every n,
the class B=n has stationary law

  pi_n(a,b)=Poisson(alpha/delta)(a) * 1{b=n}.

Each class is positive recurrent, but {pi_n} is not tight on the full
lattice. This is a cross-class obstruction, not failure of recurrence inside
any class. Keeping an empty-neighborhood h_i in the proof would be a category
error: it cannot switch and cannot create a negative generator term.

Strictly positive but small edge rates preserve the fixed-rate proof while
destroying any uniform threshold over a family approaching zero. For the
one-edge formula (2), set q_n=n^(-2) and b=n, with fixed p,delta,epsilon,
R>1. Then

  (p(R-1)+epsilon delta-q_n d n)R^n
      = (p(R-1)+epsilon delta-d/n)R^n,

which is positive and diverges. Each individual q_n>0 still gives eventual
negative drift when b is sufficiently larger than 1/q_n; there is no common
finite threshold or common Foster set as q_n downarrows zero.

Similarly, if alpha(N(i))=alpha_n=R^(-n) and b_i=n, the inactive switch
has magnitude only epsilon_i, not a quantity tending to infinity. A fixed
positive alpha(N(i)) does give the required tail as b_i->infinity, but the
threshold depends on log_R(1/alpha(N(i))). The same applies to epsilon_i
downarrow zero. If v_n=n^(-2), k=1, and the positive linear coefficient in
(5) is fixed, choosing a=n leaves the reverse term v_n a(a-1)=O(1) while
the positive term is O(n); the negative-catalyst threshold is not uniform
over v downarrows zero.

No uniformity is claimed for rates that approach zero, for support graphs
whose edge set changes, or for R approaching one. The sibling Cycle-4 audit
`outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle4/leaves/sparse_switch_adversarial/AUDIT.txt`
gives a separate exact R_n downarrow one obstruction even with all rates
equal to one and a strict catalyst margin.

5. Reversibility/recurrence decision table
------------------------------------------

Variant                         Drift certificate                 Recurrence
-----------------------------   -------------------------------   --------------------------
All p,q,u,v>0; alpha,delta>0    Proven scoped candidate            Positive recurrent per class
p=0, q,v>0                     Same escape proof; classes change  No whole-lattice irreducibility claim
u=0, q,v>0                     Same escape proof; forward term gone Analyze classes separately
q=0, p,u,delta>0                Exact LV->+infinity at A_j=1       Not implied; one-way example is transient
v=0, u>delta (example (5))      Exact LV grows linearly at B=1      Not implied; supercritical slice can be transient
alpha(N(i))=0 on remote face     Exact LV=K at (0,1,n)              Recurrent closed slices exist
epsilon_i=0, remote catalyst     Exact LV=lambda_1+K at (0,1,n)     Full positive family remains recurrent candidate
delta_j=0, p_ji>0                Exact LV grows at B_i=0              Not implied; pure immigration is transient
N(i)=empty                      Omit h_i; fix B_i label             Classwise recurrence, no cross-class tightness

The table deliberately separates “the displayed LV bound fails” from “the
CTMC is transient.” Missing q in a one-way substrate-birth network and
missing v in a supercritical frozen-gate slice do yield exact transience
examples. The epsilon/local-alpha examples show certificate failure with
recurrent closed classes. The p=0 and u=0 variants show that one-way rates can
also leave the drift proof intact.

6. Falsifiers and quantitative boundary
----------------------------------------

For a proposed sparse support correction, the decisive local checks are:

  1. For every dynamic substrate i, is N(i) defined from actual positive
     substrate-changing edges, and does alpha(N(i))>0 on each non-global
     inactive face?
  2. For every j that can be the last active local neighbor, is q_ji>0 or
     is an alternative drift term explicitly paired with
     epsilon_i delta_j R_i^b_i?
  3. For every unbounded catalyst direction with some adjacent b_i>0, is
     there a positive reverse-factorial coefficient v_ji, or a checked
     strict linear coefficient on every residual bounded-b vector?
  4. At b_N(j)=0, is
     lambda_j delta_j>sum_i p_ji(R_i-1)?
  5. Is the all-zero catalyst generator negative after including every
     support switch, rather than relying on a K chosen for a different
     support pattern?

If any answer fails, the exact states in Sections 3a-3e are falsifiers for
the named template. They do not automatically falsify a different Lyapunov
function or imply transience. For rate-uniform claims, also expose a common
lower bound on q, v, and local alpha(N(i)), a fixed R>1, and the resulting
finite tail thresholds. Positivity without quantitative lower bounds is
pointwise, not uniform.

7. Verification and scope
-------------------------

The edgewise identities in (1)-(6) are algebraic consequences of the
falling-factorial propensities. The local missing-rate values are checked by

  python3 -B outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle4/leaves/switch_boundary/verify_variants.py

using exact Fraction arithmetic at selected rational states. This finite
check is not used as an all-state proof. The existing sparse identity checker is
`outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle3/leaves/exponential_foster_outside_gain/validate_sparse.py`;
it covers the fixture E={(A1,B1),(A2,B1),(A2,B2)} on 4096 states only.
It was rerun and passed, which checks the existing generator identity on that
fixture only.

Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the scoped all-positive sparse
paired-edge Foster theorem; exact counterexamples to its naive missing-rate
extensions are proved here; recurrence/transience is separately established
only for the explicit one-way examples stated above. No novelty, priority, or
general weakly reversible recurrence claim is made.
