CLAIM / EXACT INTERFACE

Fix d>=2 and rational 0<=theta<1/d. Theta may be a fixed public constant.
Let D=binom(N+d^2-1,d^2-1) and let R be a supplied rational Hermitian
permutation-invariant trace-one operator with ||R||_op<=2. Define

 C_theta=conv{sigma^{tensor N}: Tr sigma=1, sigma>=theta I},
 v_theta(R)=min_{S in C_theta}(1/2)||R-S||_1.                         (O1)

For any rational 0<eps<1, a finite-bit classical algorithm computes at most
D rational one-qudit states sigma_j>=theta I, and dyadic nonnegative
weights summing to one, whose mixture S_hat satisfies

                 T(R,S_hat)<=v_theta(R)+eps.                         (O2)

The total Turing time and output bits are polynomial in

                 N, L, and log(1/eps), for each fixed d,              (O3)

where L includes rational input/constant encodings. In particular theta=0
improves the earlier net compiler's inverse-accuracy dependence. R need
not be positive, which permits its rational Gibbs approximation. For a
known fixed-coefficient collective quadratic Gibbs input with ||K||=O(N),
the earlier exact rational Taylor construction and (O2) imply

 T(rho_K,S_hat)<=distance(rho_K,C_theta)+eps
 in poly_d(N,L,log(1/eps)) time.                                     (O4)

This result minimizes distance to the IID cone, not to the cone of arbitrary
nonidentical product states. It does not alone eliminate an iid-distance
error floor. The very large fixed-d exponent and constants are charged;
no practical polynomial or implemented certified pipeline is asserted.

2. A D-DIMENSIONAL DUAL WITH A PUBLIC INTERIOR BALL

Trace-norm duality and compact convex minimax give

 v_theta(R)=max_{W Hermitian, ||W||_op<=1/2}
            [Tr(W R)-max_{sigma>=theta I,Tr sigma=1}
                                  Tr(W sigma^{tensor N})].           (O5)

W can be restricted to A_N: permutation averaging preserves all displayed
pairings and cannot increase op norm. Let f_theta(W,sigma)=Tr(W sigma^N)
and introduce a scalar a. The dual feasible body is

 K_theta={(W,a): ||W||_op<=1/2, -1<=a<=1,
                 a>=f_theta(W,sigma) for every admitted sigma}.      (O6)

Since |f_theta|<=1/2, the a box does not change the optimum. Optimize
c(W,a)=Tr(W R)-a. Coordinates are the D real coefficients of W and a.

A supplied center is x0=(0,3/4). A known inner radius is

 r0=[64 D d^{2N}]^{-1};                                               (O7)

indeed a coordinate perturbation of that radius changes ||W|| by at most
2D d^{2N}r0=1/32, and a by at most r0. All inequalities in (O6) remain
strict. Coordinates of an op-norm-bounded W are bounded by sqrt(d^N)/2,
using the orthogonal Hermitian orbit basis; a is bounded by one. Thus
R0=4(D+1)d^N is a known outer radius around x0. log(R0/r0)=O_d(N+log D).
These radii are uniform over ANY nonempty collection of admitted sigma,
including every finite collection of atom cuts retained below. No inner
radius of C_theta, or separability-volume oracle, is required.

An explicit upper bound C>=1 for the Euclidean norm of the objective's
coordinate vector is C=4D d^{3N}+1: |Tr(H_l R)|<=4d^{3N}. Its logarithm
has O_d(N+log D) bits. The raw objective vector has norm at least one,
because its a coefficient is -1, and at most C. Optimize this raw objective
with weak-output tolerance min(eps/(100C),r0/2). This matches GLS1981's
objective normalization convention. Exponentially large coordinate
coefficients cost bits, not inverse-error running time.

3. FIXED-VARIABLE POLYNOMIAL OBJECTIVE, WITH CHARGED DECISION ORACLE

Parametrize an arbitrary one-qudit state tau by p=d^2-1 real variables:
first d-1 diagonals and real/imaginary upper off-diagonals; trace fixes its
last diagonal. All these variables lie in [-1,1]. Positivity is expressed
by the 2^d-1 nonempty principal minors, of degree at most d. Admitted
sigma has the exact affine form

                    sigma=theta I+(1-d theta) tau.                   (O8)

For a rational supplied W, the polynomial

 F_W(tau)=Tr(W [theta I+(1-d theta)tau]^{tensor N})                    (O9)

has real rational coefficients, degree <=N, and only p fixed variables.
For example compute it as sum_m s_m conjugate(w_m)
prod_ab sigma_ab^{m_ab}; conjugate-pair terms give a real polynomial.
Expanding the fixed-number-of-variables affine factors needs polynomial
time and only binom(N+p,p)=poly_d(N) monomials. Polynomial coefficient
bits are polynomial in N, bits(W), and bits(theta); the multinomial
expansion adds O_d(N) bits. W is supplied numerical data in each call,
not another block of quantified variables.

Consequently the existential predicate

 exists tau: principal minors >=0, coordinates in the given rational
 intervals, and F_W(tau)>=u                                           (O10)

is decidable in polynomial BIT time in N, the coefficient/bound bits and
the bits of u, for fixed d. The imported result is the fixed-variable
specialization of Basu–Pollack–Roy quantifier elimination: number of
polynomials and variable count are fixed here; the degree is O(N); its
arithmetic-operation bound is a fixed power of the degree, and its
intermediate integer bits are bounded by coefficient_bits*degree^{O(p)}.
The exact bit bound is displayed in Theorem2.18 of Basu's author-hosted
survey (reproducing BPR's book theorem); the original BPR1996 paper and
Renegar1992 are the primary classical origins. This is an imported
decision ALGORITHM, not a conjectured efficient nonlinear optimizer.

Only the decision algorithm is needed. An algebraic witness, a lower
bound on optimizer gaps, and exact irrational density preparation are
NOT left as extra oracles. The next paragraph acquires rational witnesses
by repeated calls to (O10).

4. ACQUIRE A RATIONAL NEAR-MAXIMIZER WITHOUT ENUMERATING A NET

Assume ||W||<=1/2, as checked before this step. For any 0<t<=1, binary
search in [-1/2,1/2] using (O10) obtains a rational feasible level
u>=max F_W-t/8 in O(log(1/t)) calls. The feasible level set is nonempty.
Maintain a coordinate box [-1,1]^p intersecting that level set. For each
coordinate, bisect its interval and keep a closed half interval for which
(O10) answers YES. At least one half is feasible. Continue until all
interval widths are <=gamma=t/(80d^2N). This takes
O_d(log(N/t)) further decision calls, always in the SAME p variables.
Replace old bounds when bisecting; the constraint count does not grow.

Let Q be the rational trace-one Hermitian matrix at the midpoint of the
final coordinate box. Some density tau_* in that box still has F_W>=u.
Writing delta=t/(20dN), the coordinate row-sum bound gives
||Q-tau_*||_op<=delta. Therefore

 tau_hat=(Q+2delta I)/(1+2d delta),
 sigma_hat=theta I+(1-d theta)tau_hat                                (O11)

are rational normalized PSD matrices, with sigma_hat>=theta I. As in
the earlier net proof, ||tau_hat-tau_*||_1<=5d delta. Tensor telescoping
and ||W||<=1/2 give

 |F_W(tau_hat)-F_W(tau_*)|<=5dNdelta/2=t/8.

Thus Tr(W sigma_hat^N)>=max_sigma Tr(W sigma^N)-t/4. Output entry bits
are polynomial in the query bits and log(N/t), and all decisions have
polynomial bit cost. This acquires ONE state per call, not an inverse-t
size grid. Boundary states, degeneracies, and zero eigenvalues are allowed
by the decision predicate; the rational repair (O11) makes positivity exact.

5. AN ACTUAL RATIONAL WEAK SEPARATION ORACLE

Given rational y=(W,a) and tolerance t>0:

(i) Check -1<=a<=1 and the two rational PSD LMIs
G L_{I/2+W}>=0, G L_{I/2-W}>=0. A violation yields a rational separating
hyperplane valid for the shared box/op-norm constraints. Rational PSD
testing AND a rational negative vector are polynomial-bit operations,
by symmetric fraction-free elimination. In particular a negative pivot
gives such a vector; a zero pivot with a nonzero off-diagonal gives one
by a rational two-coordinate combination; positive pivots recurse on
the Schur complement. Determinant bit bounds keep all vectors polynomial.
This does not enumerate 2^D principal minors.

(ii) If those checks pass, acquire sigma_hat via Section4 with tolerance
t'=min(t,1). Evaluate q=Tr(W sigma_hat^N) exactly rationally. If q>a,
return the VALID violated atom cut

                    Tr(W' sigma_hat^N)-a'<=0                         (O12)

and retain sigma_hat. Normalize each nonzero rational cut normal by its
maximum absolute coordinate. Its infinity norm is exactly one and its
Euclidean norm lies in [1,sqrt(D+1)]. This pins the lower-norm convention
used by GLS1981; any upper-normalization convention is obtained with a
polynomial dimension factor. Exact valid cuts and central ellipsoid
updates are unchanged by this scaling, and their bit length stays polynomial.

(iii) Otherwise q<=a and max_sigma f(W,sigma)<=a+t'/4. Increasing only
a to max(a,max f) puts y inside K_theta, at distance <=t'/4<=t. This
adjustment remains within [-1,1], since max f<=1/2. Return weak acceptance.

Every rejection is an EXACT valid rational cut; every acceptance is within
the requested Euclidean tolerance. Cut/point bits and running time are
polynomial in N, query bits and log(1/t). There is no assumed positivity,
unexplained separation certificate, or inverse target probability.

6. ELLIPSOID TRANSCRIPT RECOVERY: ACQUIRE FINITELY MANY GOOD ATOMS

Run the standard bounded-body rational weak-optimization ellipsoid algorithm
on (O6), with center/radii (O7) and raw objective c. Use precision so that
its final rational x satisfies

 distance(x,K_theta)<=eps/(100C),
 max_{K_theta} c(x') <= c(x)+eps/100.                                 (O13)

Such a run uses polynomially many weak-separation calls and query bits in
D, log(R0/r0), the objective/input bits, and log(C/eps). Initialize the
retained atom list with I/d and append EVERY state emitted by an atom cut.
Denote the final finite list by P, and define K_P by replacing 'every
admitted sigma' in (O6) with 'every sigma in P'. Its SAME center/radii
remain valid, and K_theta is a subset of K_P.

CRITICAL TRANSCRIPT ARGUMENT, rather than an unprovided decomposition oracle:
Each box/PSD cut in the run is valid for K_P, since its constraints are
shared. Each atom cut is valid for K_P, because its state is in final P.
Each accepted query is within its declared tolerance of K_theta, hence
also of K_P. Therefore the entire realized optimization transcript is a
valid transcript of the SAME weak-optimization algorithm for K_P. One
can define a valid oracle for K_P which gives these saved responses on
the queried inputs and otherwise uses its explicit finite constraints.
The standard algorithm's proof depends on validity of the responses,
the uniform radii, and precision, not on how the oracle chose its cuts.
Thus its final optimization guarantee applies to K_P as well:

                    max_{K_P} c <= c(x)+eps/100.                     (O14)

This is an a posteriori use of the final list P, not a rerun whose cuts
could change P. By the first condition of (O13) and ||c||<=C,
c(x)<=max_{K_theta}c+eps/100. Together,

                  max_{K_P}c <= v_theta(R)+eps/50.                   (O15)

The final point need not exactly satisfy all infinite inequalities. Its
weak-distance error was explicitly charged in this comparison.

6A. EXPLICIT FAILED-THRESHOLD VOLUME VERSION OF THE RECOVERY

This supplies a concrete implementation of the preceding transcript
principle, following c07_s01's adversarial audit request. It does not infer
the finite optimum merely from having a good true-dual point.

Let m=D+1, V=d^N+1 and g=eps/128. Every finite or infinite dual optimum
is at most V: ||R||_1<=2d^N, ||W||<=1/2, and |a|<=1/2 at an optimum.
This uses the generic indefinite-R interface; a constant bracket would
not suffice. The center has objective c(x0)=-3/4. Set

 t_sep=g/(16C), rho_ball=g r0/[16C(V+1)].                             (O18)

For a rational threshold z, run a bounded rational central-cut ellipsoid
feasibility procedure in the outer radius-R0 ball. If c(y)<z, cut by the
exact level constraint c>=z. Otherwise call Section5 with t_sep. An
acceptance stops with weak success; a rejection gives an exact valid
central cut. Use rounded rational ellipsoid updates preserving every
cut intersection and shrinking volume by at least exp[-1/(4(m+1))]
per step, as in the classical rational ellipsoid analysis. Run at most

 T=ceil[4m(m+1) log(R0/rho_ball)]+1                                  (O19)

steps (replace the logarithm by any computed rational upper bound).
If there was no acceptance, declare FAILURE TO CONTAIN A rho_ball BALL,
not exact infeasibility. The number of steps and rounding bits are
polynomial in m, log radii, and input bits.

If a run accepts, its point is within t_sep of K_theta and c(y)>=z;
therefore v_theta(R)>=z-C t_sep=z-g/16.                               (O20)

If a run fails, retain every emitted atom, initializing the list with
I/d. Let P be that run's finite list, or a superset from all runs. Suppose
its finite optimum v_P were >=z+g and take an optimal x_* in K_P.
Mix it with x0 using s=g/[4(V+1)]. The center
y_*=(1-s)x_*+s x0 has objective at least z+3g/4: mixing loses at most
s(V+1)=g/4. Convexity and the known center ball give

 B(y_*,s r0) subset K_P.

The radius rho_ball in (O18) is <=s r0 and C rho_ball<=g/16. Hence
B(y_*,rho_ball) lies in K_P intersect {c>=z}. All common and retained
atom cuts in the failed transcript, and all threshold cuts, are EXACTLY
valid for this finite intersection. The ellipsoids therefore contain
that ball. But (O19)'s volume is strictly smaller than a radius-rho_ball
ball, contradiction. Thus every failed run gives

                             v_P<z+g.                              (O21)

The dependence on eps is logarithmic: log(1/rho_ball) is
O(log(1/eps)+log C+log V+log(1/r0)), not a power of 1/eps.

Bisect thresholds between the initially accepted -3/4 (center) and the
failed V+g. The latter cannot accept, by (O20) and v_theta<=V. Keep the
last failed upper threshold h and accepted lower threshold l until
h-l<=g, and retain the atoms from the last failed run (or all runs).
Equation(O20) gives l<=v_theta+g/16, while (O21) for h gives

 v_P<h+g<=l+2g<=v_theta+(2+1/16)g<v_theta+eps/50.                     (O22)

This is precisely (O15), with all objective-mixing and volume margins
specified. If all cuts across multiple runs are retained, K_P only
shrinks, and the same upper bound holds. There are polynomially many
runs, calls and rational atoms. The final finite primal SDP is Section7.
The rational ellipsoid update/bit theorem is still a classical import;
