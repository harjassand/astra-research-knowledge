# Source notes — stochastic hybrid safety

All local PDFs/text extractions are under `sources/`. Direct URLs are fixed below. The 2007, 2008, 2022, and 2024 arXiv manuscripts were downloaded and read in the relevant modeling/theorem sections. The 2026 publisher article record was checked via a web search result and matched by title/authors to the arXiv preprint; I did not compare the final journal text against that version.

## Primary literature

1. Alessandro Abate, Maria Prandini, John Lygeros, Shankar Sastry, “Probabilistic reachability and safety for controlled discrete time stochastic hybrid systems,” *Automatica* 44(11), 2008, 2724–2734, DOI [10.1016/j.automatica.2008.03.027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2008.03.027). Full paper PDF: [Oxford-hosted PDF](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/alessandro.abate/publications/APLS08.pdf). Local: `sources/abate2008.pdf`, `sources/abate2008.txt`; PDF SHA-256 `955ac83c850cce583a91041c37ac77f94d9810dfae221f40ccdbac8643b29ee3`. It defines separate transition/reset kernels and inputs over a hybrid Borel state and derives a finite-horizon maximally safe Markov policy via dynamic programming; forced discrete-time transitions are encoded through invariant exit.

2. Stephen Prajna, Ali Jadbabaie, George J. Pappas, “A Framework for Worst-Case and Stochastic Safety Verification Using Barrier Certificates,” *IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 52(8), 2007, DOI [10.1109/TAC.2007.902736](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2007.902736). Primary author copy: [MIT-hosted PDF](https://web.mit.edu/~jadbabai/www/papers/TAC05-Barrier.pdf). Local: `sources/prajna2007.pdf`, `sources/prajna2007.txt`; PDF SHA-256 `b6c354c20c81509c5a0a37d5775402f987cdfca12f7959b76f3f8a4ee255056a`. The deterministic hybrid section defines location guards and reset maps and requires the destination barrier to remain safe after every allowed reset. Its stochastic theorem treats switching diffusions with a generator term over Markov mode transitions, where the continuous state is unchanged at a mode switch.

3. Abolfazl Lavaei, Sadegh Soudjani, Emilio Frazzoli, “Safety Barrier Certificates for Stochastic Hybrid Systems,” [arXiv:2208.03478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03478), 2022. Local: `sources/lavaei2022.pdf`, `sources/lavaei2022.txt`; PDF SHA-256 `3451cd830c67693cccdd28273e0a57eeebd09d635c837a5ded478947b4ec5054`. It models Brownian/Poisson flows with sampled-data inputs and stochastic reset equations at inter-reset times in a bounded window; an elapsed-time counter augments the state, and the certificate conditions account for both flow and reset transitions.

4. Mahdieh Zaker, Omid Akbarzadeh, Behrad Samari, Abolfazl Lavaei, “Compositional design of safety controllers for large-scale stochastic hybrid systems,” *Automatica* 190 (August 2026), article 113082, DOI [10.1016/j.automatica.2026.113082](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2026.113082); preprint [arXiv:2409.10018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10018). Local: `sources/zaker2026.pdf`, `sources/zaker2026.txt`; PDF SHA-256 `7cff3619e3ccfe33a737955db14dd02fba7e933f4def933153508a3c152abf98`. Read Definitions 2.1/2.7, Proposition 2.10, and Theorem 5.4. The subsystem jump times satisfy (t_{k+1}-t_k in {ε1τ,...,ε2τ}), represented with an elapsed-time counter. The paper composes subsystem A-CSBCs under small-gain conditions and accounts for SDE/Poisson flow, disturbance coupling, and stochastic reset maps. It is not a state-triggered guard model in the read sections.
