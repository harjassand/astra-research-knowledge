## N33 interface reconstructed

Fix a finite actual exponent-arrow diagram `y_e -> y'_e`, `nu_e=y'_e-y_e`, its real stoichiometric span `S`, a positive class `P=(c+S) intersect R^d_{>0}`, and one supplied rate box `0<kappa<=k_e(t)<=K<infinity`. The rates are arbitrary measurable functions of time and the ODE is interpreted almost everywhere in the Caratheodory sense. The theorem chooses one compact convex positive absorber for this class before an initial point or rate path is chosen. Entry time may depend on the initial point, but not on the rate path. It does not claim one entry-time bound over an unbounded class or uniform bounds over different classes.

The endotactic convention is the essential-source one: for a direction `w`, first restrict to edges with `w dot nu_e != 0`; among their source complexes, every essential edge at the maximum `w`-source level points strictly inward (`w dot nu_e < 0`). This is crucial. Neutral edges at that source may be present and are irrelevant to the condition and to the projected drift.

## Weighted drift reconstruction

The candidate's source set for comparisons is

`D = {e_i} union {z-y : y,z in C, z != y}`.

For each slope `r` in the finite affine family, it uses the positive, possibly discontinuous tolerance `E(r)=min(1/2, |r dot v|/(2||v||_1) for v in D with r dot v != 0)`. The affine family is fixed before the small scale `h` is chosen. After the family is fixed, the finite nonzero comparison products define `H>0`. Thus an active slope `r` at `x=h^p` in `B_h=[h,h^-1]^d` satisfies, for every nonzero `r dot v`, both the same sign for `p dot v` and `|p dot v|>=H`.

Take an active `r` outside `S^perp`. Then at least one edge has `r dot nu != 0`. Choose `e_*: w -> w'` with minimum `r dot y_e` among those edges. Applying endotacticity at `-r` says every essential edge at that minimum source level has `r dot nu>0`; in particular `r dot nu_*>=2H`. Every negative projected edge is essential and has source strictly above this minimum. Its source difference is in `D`, so its monomial is smaller by a factor at most `h^H`: `x^{y_e}<=h^H x^w`. Finally `||r||_infinity<=1`, hence `|r dot nu_e|<=||nu_e||_1`. For every pointwise rate vector in the box,

`r dot f(t,x) >= x^w(2 kappa H - K L h^H)`, where `L=sum_e ||nu_e||_1`.

When `K L h^H<=kappa H`, this is at least `kappa H x^w`. Since `x=h^p`, `||p||_infinity<=1`, and `M=max_e ||y_e||_1`, including negative source coordinates gives `x^w=h^{p dot w}>=h^M`; thus the uniform lower bound is `delta_h=kappa H h^M>0`. The rate quantifiers are pointwise: the selected positive edge is charged at the lower rate bound, while the whole negative sum is charged at the upper bound. The projection factors are included; unweighted flux comparison would be insufficient.

I checked the possible tier/tie gap directly. The minimum is taken only over the nonzero-projection edge set, so its selected edge is essential under `-r`. Every edge tied at the minimum is positive by the definition, not just the selected one. A negative edge cannot lie in that tied tier. Its strictly positive source gap is one of the finitely many products used to define `H`. Reaction vectors and source differences are both in `D`. No weak reversibility, connectedness, source=target assumption, or fixed rate schedule enters this argument.

## Exact rational stress fixture

The replayable script `exact_checks.py` checks the two-species diagram

`(1,3)->(2,3)`, `(3,3)->(2,3)`, and `(0,0)<->(0,1)`.

It is not weakly reversible: `(2,3)` is not a source, so the two horizontal arrows have no return path. It is not strongly endotactic: at `w=e_2`, all sources with maximal second coordinate have horizontal, tangent reactions; the essential vertical edge is lower. It is all-endotactic for every real `w`: when a horizontal projection is nonzero, the outer of the two horizontal sources in the `w_1` direction points inward; when a vertical projection is nonzero, the outer source in the `w_2` direction points inward. The maximum among all essential sources is one of these within-component extrema, and any tie between components preserves inward signs. The script also checks the exact linear sign arrangement (all source-order and reaction-tangency hyperplanes) on 28 rational chamber/boundary representatives; every representative passes the exact definition.

For the exact drift fixture choose `r=(1/2,1/3)` and `p=(1/2,1/5)`. Direct computation over all nonzero directions in `D` gives `E(r)=H=1/6`; `||p-r||_infinity=2/15<1/6`. Here `L=4`, `M=6`, and with `kappa=1`, `K=2`, `h=48^(-30)`, the small-scale condition holds exactly: `K L h^H = 1/31,850,496 <= 1/6`. The exact minimum of `r dot f` over all 16 rate-box corners is attained at rates `(1,2,1,2)` in edge order and is strictly greater than the claimed lower bound `delta=H h^6`. This is a conditional active-slope fixture (the nonconstructive affine lemma is not numerically instantiated); it stresses the proof's signs, projection weights, source-tier ratios, and worst-case independent rate choice.

The same diagram's switched ODE is

`x' = y^3(a_1(t)x-a_2(t)x^3)`, `y'=a_3(t)-a_4(t)y`, with every `a_i(t) in [1,2]`.

For arbitrary measurable switching, `[1/2,2]` is forward invariant for `y`; `[1/2,2]` is forward invariant for `z=x^2`, because `z'=2y^3 z(a_1-a_2z)` points inward or tangent at both endpoints. This verifies that the candidate's strict drift argument does not need rates to settle or have dwell time. These simple barriers check invariance directions only; they are not substituted for the N33 finite-entry proof.

## Nonnested scale absorption

At a fixed small scale, one active constant label at the class representative makes `M_h=F_h(c)` a global upper bound. The plateau `K_h={F_h=M_h}` is a closed convex intersection of halfspaces. Activity of the zero slope forces `K_h intersect B_h` inside `int B_{sqrt(h)}`; convexity and the segment from `c` rule out plateau points outside `B_h`, making the whole plateau compact and positive.

Within a fixed class, a slope in `S^perp` is constant on the class. Below the plateau, `F_h<M_h`, so such a label cannot be active. The finite-minimum chain rule holds almost everywhere along absolutely continuous solutions, including ties; every active slope has nonnegative projected drift in `B_h`, and all active slopes below the plateau have drift at least `delta_h`. Thus `F_h` is nondecreasing inside the box and grows at least linearly until plateau entry. A trajectory that stays in `B_h` enters by `W_h=(M_h-min_{B_h}F_h)/delta_h`, independent of the rate path. Starting in the plateau gives invariance and compact-positive continuation.

For any initial `x0>0`, choose a possibly much smaller `h0<=hP` such that `||log(x0)/log(h0)||_infinity<H`. The same coordinate sign margin forces all active slopes at `x0` to be zero, so `x0 in K_{h0}`. Then set `h_{j+1}=min(sqrt(h_j),hP)`. If the trajectory is in `K_{h_j}`, it remains there, hence in `B_{sqrt(h_j)} subset B_{h_{j+1}}`; box-conditional entry then places it in `K_{h_{j+1}}`, which is invariant. This implication uses no inclusion between `K_{h_j}` and `K_{h_{j+1}}`. Repeated square roots reach `hP` in finitely many steps, and `sum_j W_{h_j}` is a finite path-independent entry bound for this `x0`.

The key potential quantifier is present: `hP`, the slopes, and each `W_h` are fixed from the class/diagram/rate box before the rate path; only `h0` and the number of scale transitions depend on `x0`. No uniformity over all unbounded initial states is smuggled in. Exact arithmetic check of the scale bookkeeping: `h0=2^-32`, `hP=2^-4` yields scales `2^-32,2^-16,2^-8,2^-4`, with `B_{sqrt(h_j)} subset B_{h_{j+1}}` at every step. This checks the box direction and finite-step formula; it does not claim a nonnested fixture arose from a specific affine family.

## Failure boundary and missing items

Different stoichiometric spans really do break the classwide switching extension. Let mode A be `0<->A` and mode B be `0<->B`; each is endotactic and has its own one-dimensional span. Their union span is `R^2`. If mode A remains selected, then `B(t)=B(0)` forever. Arbitrarily large positive `B(0)` rules out one compact absorber for the union positive class. This confirms the common-span hypothesis is load-bearing and matches N33's scope boundary.

No counterexample, endpoint failure, or missing quantifier was found in the requested drift and scale steps. Residual gates are external mathematical review/formal proof and priority; the finite fixture cannot validate the universal affine lemma. The affine family, real offsets, tolerance cover, scale `hP`, and waiting times have no useful size/precision/runtime bounds, so the proof does not yield a practical absorber compiler. Global positive rate lower/upper bounds are substantive; dropping them is outside this result. The separate stochastic certificate results do not establish this deterministic classwide theorem.
