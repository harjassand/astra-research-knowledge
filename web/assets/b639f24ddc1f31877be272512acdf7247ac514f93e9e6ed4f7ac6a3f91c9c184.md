# Anisotropic Clifford-vector star bound

## Statement and scope

Let \(m=2n+1\), \(d=2^n\), and let \(\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_m\) be an irreducible Hermitian Clifford frame on \(\mathbb C^d\):
\[
\Gamma_i\Gamma_j+\Gamma_j\Gamma_i=2\delta_{ij}I.
\]
Use the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt convention \(\tau(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)/d\), so the \(\Gamma_i\) are orthonormal. For every positive operator \(Q\) supported on their real span, the two-output star obeys
\[
\lambda_{\max}(H_Q)\leq \operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q+\lambda_{\max}(Q),
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{canEB}}(Q)=\lambda_{\max}(Q).
\tag{1}
\]
Here
\[
H_Q=\sum_{i,j}Q_{ij}\,\Gamma_i^T\otimes(\Gamma_j\otimes I+I\otimes\Gamma_j),
\]
and \(S_{\mathrm{canEB}}\) is the support function of canonical measure-and-prepare maps that measure a POVM and prepare each effect normalized to trace one. For a compatible marginal \(\Phi\) that is also Hilbert--Schmidt self-adjoint, every swap-symmetric two-output Choi state with equal marginals satisfies
\[
2\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Phi)\leq \operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q+S_{\mathrm{canEB}}(Q).
\tag{2}
\]
For a non-self-adjoint marginal, the star expectation instead tests the symmetric part \((\Phi+\Phi^*)/2\).

This closes all **anisotropic duals confined to the Clifford vector sector**. It does not control bivector or higher Clifford-form components, arbitrary positive \(Q\) on the full traceless operator space, or produce a common all-sector EB comparator for a general map. The qubit corollary below is known prior work and is recorded only as a consistency control. The higher-dimensional vector-frame extension has an internal algebraic proof and independent hostile audit; the bounded source pass recorded in SOURCE_NOTES.md is not a novelty or priority clearance.

## Exact canonical-EB support

By a \(\operatorname{Spin}(m)\) rotation, diagonalize \(Q\) with eigenvalues \(a_1\geq\cdots\geq a_m\geq0\). Any real symmetric \(Q\) admits an eigenbasis of determinant \(+1\), so this rotation lifts to the irreducible spinor representation. For any state \(\rho\), put \(r_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\Gamma_i)\). For a unit vector \(u\), \(\sum_i u_i\Gamma_i\) is a Hermitian involution, hence
\[
\sum_i r_i^2=\sup_{\|u\|=1}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\rho\sum_i u_i\Gamma_i\right)^2\leq1.
\]
A canonical POVM \(\{M_z\}\), with \(\rho_z=M_z/\operatorname{Tr}M_z\) and \(p_z=\operatorname{Tr}(M_z)/d\), therefore has score
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Psi)=\sum_zp_z\sum_i a_i\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_z\Gamma_i)^2\leq a_1.
\]
The projective POVM \(P_\pm=(I\pm\Gamma_1)/2\), preparing \(P_\pm/\operatorname{Tr}P_\pm\), has score \(a_1\): its normalized effects have \(\Gamma_1\) expectation \(\pm1\) and all other Clifford-vector expectations zero. Thus \(S_{\mathrm{canEB}}(Q)=a_1=\lambda_{\max}(Q)\).

## An unweighted \(k\)-generator sum-of-squares

For any \(k\leq m\), define the unweighted star using the first \(k\) generators. On the three-system Choi space let
\[
A_i=\Gamma_i^T\otimes\Gamma_i\otimes I,
\qquad B_i=\Gamma_i^T\otimes I\otimes\Gamma_i.
\]
These are Hermitian involutions with \([A_i,A_j]=[B_i,B_j]=0\), \([A_i,B_i]=0\), and \(\{A_i,B_j\}=0\) for \(i\ne j\). Joint eigenspaces of the commuting \(A_i\)'s are labelled by \(x\in\{\pm1\}^k\); \(B_j\) preserves \(x_j\) and flips every \(x_i\) for \(i\ne j\). The shifts generated by the \(B_j\)'s have one orbit for even \(k\), and the two parity orbits for odd \(k\). The only shift relation for odd \(k\) is the all-\(B\) product, whose value can be either central sign \(p=\pm1\) on an irreducible block. Thus every irreducible block is the corresponding invariant block of the \(k\)-qubit model
\[
A_i=Z_i,\qquad B_i=\eta_i\prod_{j\ne i}X_j,\qquad \eta_i\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
For odd \(k\), local conjugations by products of \(Z_j\)'s change the \(\eta\)-pattern by every even-parity sign vector, so it reduces to \(\eta_i=p\) for all \(i\). Both chirality signs are included. For even \(k\), every sign pattern is gauge-equivalent to all plus. This orbit argument allows multiplicities and does not assume that the extension is supported on a Bose subspace.

Write \(P=\prod_iX_i\) and \(J_\alpha=\frac12\sum_i\sigma_i^{(\alpha)}\), with \(Z_i=\sigma_i^{(z)}\) and \(X_i=\sigma_i^{(x)}\). For either uniform sign \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), the canonical star is
\[
H_{k,\varepsilon}=2(J_z+\varepsilon P J_x).
\]
The operator \(P\) commutes with \(J_x\), anticommutes with \(J_y,J_z\), and commutes with \(J^2\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
\frac{H_{k,\varepsilon}^2}{4}
=J^2-J_y^2-\varepsilon iP J_y
=J^2+\frac14-(J_y+\varepsilon iP/2)(J_y-\varepsilon iP/2)
\preceq J^2+\frac14.
\tag{3}
\]
On total-spin \(s\leq k/2\), this is at most \((s+1/2)^2I\), and hence
\[
\lambda_{\max}(H_{[k]})\leq\|H_{[k]}\|\leq k+1.
\tag{4}
\]
The inequality holds on the entire bit-model space and therefore on every parity/chirality block.

## Layer-cake reduction for every anisotropy

In the diagonal frame let \(K_i=A_i+B_i\), \(H_{[k]}=\sum_{i=1}^kK_i\), and \(a_{m+1}=0\). Then
\[
H_Q=\sum_{k=1}^m(a_k-a_{k+1})H_{[k]}.
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative, so \(\lambda_{\max}\) is subadditive under this sum. Applying (4),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\max}(H_Q)
&\leq\sum_{k=1}^m(a_k-a_{k+1})(k+1)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^m a_i+a_1
=\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q+\lambda_{\max}(Q),
\end{aligned}
\]
which proves (1) and (2).

## Qubit control, with explicit novelty boundary

For \(m=3,d=2\), the vector sector is the complete traceless Hermitian space. Direct exact Pauli-matrix arithmetic gives, for \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(a,b,c)\),
\[
\det(xI-H_Q)=x^2\left(x^3-4(a^2+b^2+c^2)x-16abc\right)^2.
\]
The general bound above gives \(\lambda_{\max}(H_Q)\leq a+b+c+\max(a,b,c)\). For a unital, trace-preserving, Hilbert--Schmidt-self-adjoint compatible qubit channel, diagonalize its transfer matrix by a unitary and write its Pauli eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\). Set \(\mu_i=(2\lambda_i-1)_+\). Taking \(Q\) to be the projector onto the active Pauli axes in (2) yields \(\sum_i\mu_i\leq1\). The POVM
\[
M_{i,\pm}=\mu_i(I\pm\sigma_i)/2,
\qquad M_0=(1-\sum_i\mu_i)I
\]
with each effect normalized and prepared has transfer eigenvalues \(\mu_i\). It uses at most seven outcomes and satisfies \(\mu_i\geq2\lambda_i-1\) in every direction, so it is one canonical EB comparator for that qubit channel.

This qubit \(c=2\) theorem was already established in the supplied Astra record and is also identified as prior in the C4 report. The reconstruction is a control for the all-anisotropic-star technique and an explicit seven-outcome realization, **not a new scientific result**. The vector-sector bound for \(m\geq5\) is separate from the old qubit result but has not received exhaustive external-priority review.

## Falsifiers and next gate

The internal proof and independent hostile audit cover the \(A_i,B_i\) algebra, its orbit/block representation (including both odd-\(k\) central signs), the SOS identity (3), the normalized-Choi star, and canonical-EB support normalization. `scripts/clifford_vector_sos_check.py` also performs exact Pauli-matrix identity checks for both twist signs at small \(k\); this remains a consistency check rather than an external formal-verifier result.

The unresolved high-value test is an anisotropic \(Q\) with bivector or mixed Clifford-form support beyond the Spin(5)-covariant ququart theorem in `SPIN5_QUQUART_COVARIANT.md`. A successful vector-only bound does not imply the all-\(Q\) inequality needed for the general dimension-free EB lift. In physical-channel terms, a new application also needs a channel family whose vector-sector invariant is measurable and whose remaining transfer sectors are either controlled or explicitly discarded.
