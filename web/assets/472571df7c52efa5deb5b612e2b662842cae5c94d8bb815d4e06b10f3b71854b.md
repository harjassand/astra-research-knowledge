# Spatial ecology / resistance refugia: attacked candidate

**Verdict:** the spatial invasion calculation is correct within its stated PDE, and it gives a strong counterexample to using only mean fitness. It is not a new mechanism: the critical-patch-size/eigenvalue machinery is established in ecology, a 2024 resistance paper varies periodic drug-pattern spacing at fixed total drug amount using principal-eigenvalue fitness, and cancer papers already show spatial suppression and dependence on resistant-clone layout. The defensible output is a prospective cancer-specific validation design, not a novelty claim.

## Candidate mechanism and exact calculation

Linearize a resistant clone (R) while it is rare in a fixed sensitive-cell and treatment background. The one-dimensional periodic-domain model is

\[
\partial_tR=D\partial_{xx}R+V(x)R,\qquad x\in[0,L),
\]

where (D>0) is the clone’s effective dispersal coefficient and (V(x)) is its **net per-capita growth rate in that background** (drug response, local crowding and other fixed effects included). Periodic boundaries are a convenient analytic idealization, not a tumor boundary claim. The leading growth exponent is the principal eigenvalue \(\lambda_1(D\partial_{xx}+V)\); positive means deterministic initial invasion and negative means decay in this linearized model.

For a refuge of width (w) with (V=a>0), surrounded by a hostile interval of width (h=L-w) with (V=-b<0), the symmetric zero-eigenvalue solution has cosine profile in the refuge and hyperbolic-cosine profile outside. Matching the value and derivative at the interfaces gives

\[
\sqrt{a/D}\tan\!\left(\sqrt{a/D}\,w/2\right)
=\sqrt{b/D}\tanh\!\left(\sqrt{b/D}\,h/2\right).
\]

More generally, the principal eigenvalue is the root of the same matching equation after replacing the two square roots by \(q=\sqrt{(a-\lambda)/D}\) and \(k=\sqrt{(b+\lambda)/D}\). This is an exact threshold for this piecewise-constant 1D model.

An equal-information counterexample to a mean-field closure uses \(D=1\) length²/day, \(a=0.1\)/day, \(b=0.2\)/day, and \(L=100\) length units. Both layouts have exactly the same growth-rate histogram and mean \(\bar V=0.1(0.1)+(-0.2)(0.9)=-0.17\)/day:

| Layout | Favorable area | Principal exponent | Mean-field prediction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One contiguous refuge, width 10 | 10% | +0.0479211/day | −0.17/day |
| Ten periodic refuges, width 1 each | 10% | −0.1630057/day | −0.17/day |

The contiguous-refuge critical width for these parameters is \(w_c=6.0419528\). Thus the same spatially averaged growth rate predicts decline in both cases, while the PDE predicts initial growth in one and decay in the other. If the contiguous-case exponent persisted for 24 days, the linear model predicts a 3.16-fold increase; for the fragmented case it predicts a 0.020-fold remainder. These are model outputs, before saturation and stochastic loss.

Numerical check: I independently diagonalized periodic finite-difference matrices for \(D\partial_{xx}+V\), using NumPy’s symmetric dense eigensolver. At grid spacing \(\Delta x=0.2,0.1,0.05\), the contiguous-case estimates were +0.0479645, +0.0479319, +0.0479238/day; the ten-patch estimates were −0.154153, −0.162986, −0.163001/day. They converge to the matching-equation roots above. The threshold equation gives \(\lambda_1=-0.0008025\)/day at \(w=6.0\) and +0.0010957/day at \(w=6.1\), bracketing the computed critical width. This verifies arithmetic for the specified operator only.

## Novelty attack and boundary

The exact refuge-width result is a standard principal-eigenvalue/critical-patch-size calculation. Critical habitat size and persistence under diffusion were studied long before cancer applications; an experimental reaction–diffusion critical-domain-size test also exists in *E. coli*.

More directly, Freire et al. (2024) study multi-drug resistance in bacteria, define global mutant fitness using the principal eigenvalue under spatially varying drug response, and vary periodic spatial drug-pattern period while conserving total drug amount (their Fig. 8). That already overlaps the proposed spatial-pattern/spacing mechanism: [PLOS Computational Biology, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012098](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012098).

Cancer-specific prior art also narrows any novelty claim. Bacevic et al. (2017) compared CDK-inhibitor-sensitive and resistant HCT-116 cells in monolayers and spheroids; the low-dose advantage appeared in spheroids, supporting a role for spatial competition: [Nature Communications, DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-01516-1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5722825/). Strobl et al. (2022) compared an on-lattice cancer model with its well-mixed ODE and found treatment outcomes changed when the same resistant cells were clustered versus split into nests: [Communications Medicine, DOI 10.1038/s43856-022-00110-x](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9053239/). A 2015 cancer model already analyzes poorly penetrated sanctuary compartments, migration and the origin of resistance: [PLOS Computational Biology, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4366398/).

The remaining plausible contribution is a **cancer-specific, prospective test** of a quantitative threshold using a measured resistant-cell growth field and dispersal coefficient, under layouts with the same drug histogram and integrated exposure. This is a transfer/validation target. No cancer measurements were acquired here, so improvement over existing cancer models and practical relevance remain UNKNOWN. The bacterial multi-drug result also means even the fixed-total-drug layout effect is not a new general principle.

## Same-data comparator and discriminating prediction

Use a matched mean-field comparator \(\dot R=\bar V R\), supplied the same dose-response calibration, initial clone count, measured local growth-rate histogram, total exposure, and spatial images as the spatial model. It predicts the same −0.17/day slope for both layouts. The PDE additionally uses the spatial arrangement and measured \(D\), predicting +0.0479/day for the single patch and −0.163/day for the fragmented layout. Both models should be fitted using the same uniform-dose calibration data, then compared prospectively on held-out patterned layouts. Giving the mean-field fit the same raw images avoids claiming an advantage from additional data; it remains structurally unable to use coordinates.

**Falsifier:** first verify that the two patterned conditions have the same resistant-cell (V(x)) histogram and integrated drug exposure within a predeclared tolerance, and that measured (D) and the mapped geometry imply opposite eigenvalue signs. Then measure the early resistant-clone log-abundance slope before saturation. If the two signs are not opposite, or the spatial PDE fails to predict the held-out slope better than the mean-field comparator, this mechanism does not improve that assay. A single positive exponent is not a prediction of inevitable cancer relapse.

## Proposed acquisition and control ledger (not performed)

Use a microfluidic 1D channel or patterned culture so refuge widths can be set after a pilot measures \(D\) and the local net-growth contrast. Use a sensitive/resistant fluorescent pair such as the HCT-116 pair in Bacevic et al. as a precedent, not as a claim that their line has the illustrative parameters above. Compare one broad low-drug band with ten narrow low-drug bands; both have the same total low- and high-drug areas and exposure histogram. Add a uniform-exposure control calibrated to the same mean resistant-cell net growth for the equal-mean model comparison.

One concrete resource-counted design is 12 independent chambers per condition, 3 conditions (36 chambers), each seeded with 1,500 cells and 1% resistant cells: 54,000 starting cells in total, including 540 resistant cells. Image one full field every 2 hours for 24 days (289 fields/chamber, 10,404 time-point fields; two fluorescent channels give 20,808 channel images). Endpoint flow cytometry at 10,000 events/chamber adds 360,000 acquired events. These counts are a proposed design inventory, not a power calculation or completed experiment.

Calibrate local net rates at six uniform drug levels for both genotypes, four wells per genotype-dose pair (48 wells), and estimate \(D\) from single-cell trajectories in four sparse-culture wells per genotype (8 wells; target at least 100 usable tracks per well). The patterned assay uses the same calibrated rates and full images for both models. Verify target engagement across the bands in separate validation chambers (suggested: two patterned layouts × three times × three chambers = 18 fixed samples); an inert tracer alone is not evidence of active-drug penetration. The assay’s equipment, reagents, and labor dollar cost were not priced, and the 12-chamber replication has not been justified by pilot variance; monetary cost and statistical power are UNKNOWN. This explicit ledger is the acquisition burden, not a claim that such an assay is currently routine.

## Assumptions and failure cases

- (V(x)) is stationary during the measured interval and already incorporates drug effect and competition from the sensitive background. A drug concentration map alone does not determine (V(x)).
- Dispersal is unbiased, scalar and constant; phenotype switching, new mutation, active chemotaxis, tissue barriers and clone-dependent dispersal are absent.
- The geometry is 1D and periodic. Radial spheroids, vascularized 3D tumors and metastases require different operators and boundary conditions.
- The calculation linearizes around a fixed background and ignores resistant-cell saturation and ecological feedback. It predicts initial deterministic invasion/decay, not eradication, clinical response, or relapse time.
- If \(D\) is large enough to mix the domain rapidly, the spatial advantage shrinks and the mean-field model approaches the PDE result. If (V(x)<0) everywhere, a refuge-geometry argument cannot produce a positive exponent. At very small cell counts a positive deterministic exponent can coexist with high extinction probability; demographic stochasticity must be measured separately.
