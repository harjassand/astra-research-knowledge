1. MODEL AND FIXED EXPOSURE

Let (M,tr) be a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial
state. Let sigma in M be positive, bounded, tr(sigma^2)=1. The state
vector is sigma in L2(M,tr). Alice has left POVMs M_xvec^avec in M and
Bob right POVMs R(N_yvec^bvec), N_yvec^bvec in M. Define

  chi(A,B)=tr(sigma A sigma B).

For positive A,B this is positive, and chi(I,H)<=1 for H<=I. Products
of left/right representations commute even when A,B do not commute in M.

Fix the proper tested set C, event E, probability p>0, m live positions,
d_C=d^|C| and L=log(d_C/p). Relabel live positions in one FIXED order.
The flags are Omega_j=(X_C,Y_C,X_<=j,Y_>j), 0<=j<=m. Given any flag,
full Alice and Bob question strings are independent under the ORIGINAL
question law mu^k: at every live position one question is fixed and the
opposite one has its conditional mu-law, independently between positions.

For tested answer strings alpha,gamma, coarse-grain their POVM effects.
Set F_j^alpha=E[M_Xvec^alpha|Omega_j], H_j^gamma=E[N_Yvec^gamma|Omega_j].
The testing mask theta(X_C,Y_C,alpha,gamma) in {0,1} is the product of
the original acceptance predicates on C. For every j,

  E_Omega_j sum_{alpha,gamma} theta chi(F_j^alpha,H_j^gamma)=p. (A1)

All these operator expectations are finite convex combinations indexed
by CLASSICAL questions, not unproved operator conditional expectations.

At live i delete its question: r0=(X_C,Y_C,X_<i,Y_>i) with law nu_i.
Before conditioning on E, r0 is independent of (X_i,Y_i)~mu. Set

  F_x^alpha=E[M_Xvec^alpha|r0,X_i=x],
  H_y^gamma=E[N_Yvec^gamma|r0,Y_i=y],
  F_y^alpha=sum_x mu(x|y)F_x^alpha,
  H_x^gamma=sum_y mu(y|x)H_y^gamma.

Zero conditional weights are omitted. r=(r0,alpha,gamma) has weight
w_i(r)=nu_i(r0)theta(r), with sum_r w_i(r)<=d_C. It need not sum to one.

2. THE EXACT RESOLVENT FEATURE IN AN INFINITE FINITE ALGEBRA

For every effect 0<=T<=I let

  (F_T h)(s)=T(T+sI)^(-1)h,
  F_T: H -> L2((0,infinity),ds;H),
  f(t)=t log t, f(0)=0.

This applies to any faithful representation, left or right. Functional
calculus gives F_T^*F_T=T since integral t^2/(t+s)^2 ds=t. If positive
q_j sum to one and B=sum_j q_j A_j, then

  sum_j q_j (F_Aj-F_B)^*(F_Aj-F_B)
  <=sum_j q_j f(A_j)-f(B).                                 (A2)

Here is the full kernel/spectrum justification. First suppose B and all
A_j are bounded below by delta I. Put D_j=A_j-B. The convergent scalar
identity f(t)=integral [t/(1+s)-t/(t+s)]ds and the exact second-resolvent
identity yield

  sum q_j f(A_j)-f(B)
  =integral s sum q_j (B+s)^(-1)D_j(A_j+s)^(-1)
                           D_j(B+s)^(-1) ds.

The linear term cancels because sum q_j D_j=0. The left side of (A2)
has the same integral with (A_j+s)^(-1) replaced by s(A_j+s)^(-2),
since A_j(A_j+s)^(-1)-B(B+s)^(-1)=s(A_j+s)^(-1)D_j(B+s)^(-1).
The scalar spectral inequality s(A_j+s)^(-2)<=(A_j+s)^(-1) proves (A2)
by congruence and integration. At infinity the integrand is O(s^-2);
at zero the lower bounds give norm convergence.

For GENERAL effects in a von Neumann algebra, do not say that removing
ker B makes B boundedly invertible. Replace A_j by (1-delta)A_j+delta I
and B accordingly, then let delta down to zero. f is norm-continuous
under this functional calculus. The scalar map

  t -> f_t, where f_t(s)=t/(t+s), f_0=0,

is continuous [0,1]->L2(ds), by pointwise continuity and the integrable
dominating square (1+s)^(-2). It is uniformly continuous. Thus the
regularized F_Aj converge in OPERATOR norm to F_Aj, as do F_B. The
positive operator cone is norm closed, so (A2) holds with continuous
zero spectrum. This repairs the finite-dimensional inverse shortcut
when transferring the source proof to arbitrary finite M.

3. UNNORMALIZED VECTOR ERRORS: EXACT ENTROPY TELESCOPE

Initially use two independent resolvent variables. In
L2(ds_A ds_B; L2(M,tr)) put

  v_inf(s,t)=F_x^alpha(F_x^alpha+s)^(-1) sigma
                                  H_y^gamma(H_y^gamma+t)^(-1),
  u_inf(s,t)=F_x^alpha(F_x^alpha+s)^(-1) sigma
                                  H_x^gamma(H_x^gamma+t)^(-1),
  z_inf(s,t)=F_y^alpha(F_y^alpha+s)^(-1) sigma
                                  H_y^gamma(H_y^gamma+t)^(-1).

The two feature maps have norm at most one. Integration and cyclicity
of tr show ||v_inf||^2=chi(F_x,H_y), ||u_inf||^2=chi(F_x,H_x),
||z_inf||^2=chi(F_y,H_y). Hence the exact (M1),(M2) of the companion
hold, and all these mode masses equal the original p.

Let e_A replace F_x by F_y (v_inf versus z_inf), and e_B replace H_y
by H_x (v_inf versus u_inf). Define potentials

  U_j=E_Omega_j sum theta chi(f(F_j^alpha),H_j^gamma),
  Z_j=E_Omega_j sum theta chi(F_j^alpha,f(H_j^gamma)).

They satisfy -pL<=U_j,Z_j<=0. For example, at each flag let
g=chi(I,H)<=1, h=chi(F,H). Scalar Jensen on the positive functional
A ->chi(A,H), normalized by g when g>0, gives

  -chi(f(F),H)<=h log(g/h)<=-h log h.

The formula is zero if g=0. Average over flags and d_C answer pairs,
including zero theta terms: concavity gives -U_j<=p log(d_C/p).
The proof for Z is identical. Upper bounds use f(T)<=0 for effects.

Linearity gives the EXACT differences

  U_i-U_(i-1)=E_{nu_i,mu_Y} sum theta
     chi(sum_x mu(x|y)f(F_x)-f(F_y),H_y),
  Z_(i-1)-Z_i=E_{nu_i,mu_X} sum theta
     chi(F_x,sum_y mu(y|x)f(H_y)-f(H_x)).

Apply (A2) against the opposite-side positive functional. Each change
dominates its e_A or e_B and is nonnegative. Summing along the fixed
order gives E_i e_A(i)<=pL/m=p tau and E_i e_B(i)<=p tau. There is
no question-support-gap factor. These are unnormalized errors, before
posterior mass is sampled. No assertion of posterior independence is
made. The common feature variables are needed only for this analysis.

4. FINITE AUXILIARY COMPRESSION WITHOUT FINITE-MATRIX DENSITY

Fix epsilon>0. The family {f_t:t in [0,1]} from Section 2 is a compact
subset of real L2(ds). There is a finite-dimensional real subspace K
with orthogonal projection P such that sup_t||(I-P)f_t||<=epsilon.
Include e_1=1_[1,2] in K, and choose a real orthonormal basis e_l with
that as its first vector. This is possible by adding a finite epsilon
net and Gram--Schmidt. For every t>0, <e_1,f_t>>0. Put

  c_l(t)=<e_l,f_t>, q(t)=sum_l c_l(t)^2,
  g_l(t)=sqrt(t)c_l(t)/sqrt(q(t)) for t>0, g_l(0)=0.

Then q(t)>0 for t>0, and g_l are bounded continuous scalar functions
on [0,1]. Continuity at zero follows from |g_l(t)|<=sqrt(t).
The exact normalization is

  sum_l g_l(t)^2=t.                                         (A3)

Under the basis embedding K ->L2(ds), the finite feature g(t) differs
from f_t by at most sqrt(2)epsilon<=2epsilon: its orthogonal tail is
at most epsilon, while ||g-Pf_t||=sqrt(t)-sqrt(q(t))<=epsilon.
Spectral calculus gives ONE common finite-column feature for every T:

  F_T^epsilon=(g_l(T))_l,
  (F_T^epsilon)^*F_T^epsilon=T EXACT,
  ||F_T^epsilon-F_T||<=2epsilon.                            (A4)

The last norm is operator norm after the common time-basis embedding.
No unbounded inverse is placed in M: the ratios are combined into the
bounded scalar functions g_l. This is not the source's finite-dimensional
span compression, which would not work for an infinite algebra.

The exact norm identity is indispensable: approximate individual norms
without normalizing would not give exact posterior marginal identities.
Only the auxiliary time factors are made finite; M remains unchanged.

5. LEFT-COLUMN / RIGHT-ROW REALIZATION IN ONE FINITE ALGEBRA

Let n=dim K and N=M_n tensor M_n tensor M with normalized matrix traces.
Embed the original vector as

  xi=n(e_11 tensor e_11 tensor sigma) in L2(N,tr_N).

It is positive, bounded, and ||xi||_2=1. For effects T,S in M define

  A(T)=sum_l e_l1 tensor I tensor g_l(T),
  B(S)=sum_j I tensor e_1j tensor g_j(S).

Alice applies A(T) by LEFT multiplication; Bob applies B(S) by RIGHT
multiplication. The column/row orientations are deliberately different:

  A(T)^*A(T)=e_11 tensor I tensor T,
  B(S)B(S)^*=I tensor e_11 tensor S.                        (A5)

Define v=A(F_x)xi B(H_y), u=A(F_x)xi B(H_x), z=A(F_y)xi B(H_y).
Expanding matrix units and using (A3) gives

  ||A(T)xi B(S)||_2^2=tr(sigma T sigma S)=chi(T,S).           (A6)

Thus the finite vectors still satisfy exact (M1),(M2), with the same p
and the SAME posterior Q. The rectangular support subspace
n sum_{l,j} e_l1 tensor e_1j tensor h_lj is isometrically identified with
sum_{l,j} e_l(s)e_j(t)h_lj; the normalized matrix trace cancels n^2.
Under this common auxiliary basis embedding,
these vectors differ from v_inf,u_inf,z_inf respectively by at most
4epsilon (each of the two feature maps costs at most 2epsilon).
All six vectors have norm at most one. Therefore each squared pair
distance differs by at most 32epsilon: a difference of vector pairs
is at most 8epsilon, and the sum of the two pair norms at most 4.
Since sum_r w_i(r)<=d_C, their averaged finite errors obey

  e_u,e_z <= p tau +32 d_C epsilon.                        (A7)

For any eta>tau choose epsilon<=p(eta-tau)/(32d_C), making e_u,e_z<=p eta.
This may require very large finite auxiliary dimension, depending on
p,k,d; no uniform resource or efficient algorithm claim is made.
The p>0 is fixed before sending epsilon down to zero. There is no
repeated-game-table approximation in (A3)--(A7).

6. EXACT FINE-ANSWER TRANSPORT WITH NONCLOSED RANGES

Define the fine averaged effects

  G_x^(alpha,a)=E[M_Xvec^(alpha,a_i=a)|r0,X_i=x],
  K_y^(gamma,b)=E[N_Yvec^(gamma,b_i=b)|r0,Y_i=y].

They are positive and sum over a to F_x, over b to H_y. For a positive
T and G_a<=T in a von Neumann algebra, the densely defined form
T^(-1/2)G_a T^(-1/2) on ran(T^(1/2)) is bounded between zero and I,
and has a unique bounded extension C_a on supp T. Equivalently by
Douglas factorization,

  T^(1/2) C_a T^(1/2)=G_a, 0<=C_a<=supp T, sum_a C_a=supp T.

These C_a belong to the algebra: the factorization is canonical on
support and commutes with every operator in its commutant. Positivity
and uniqueness on the dense range prove the asserted sum. No bounded
inverse of T is assumed. For T=0 all G_a vanish.

For Alice write A(T)=V sqrt(T_corner), its von Neumann polar decomposition,
with V^*V=supp(T_corner). Let C_a occupy that corner and choose one
default answer a_*. Then the left POVM

  M_a=V C_a V^*+1_{a=a_*}(I-VV^*)

is positive, complete and satisfies A(T)^*M_a A(T)=G_a, in the corner.
All operators are in N and depend only on (i,r,x).

For Bob, because RIGHT multiplication has opposite product orientation,
write B(S)=sqrt(S_corner)W, W W^*=supp(S_corner). With corner effects
D_b satisfying sqrt(S)D_b sqrt(S)=K_b, the right POVM uses

  N_b=W^*D_b W+1_{b=b_*}(I-W^*W),
  B(S)N_b B(S)^*=K_b.                                      (A8)

These are right-multiplication POVMs in the commutant; they depend
only on (i,r,y). The two local measurement algebras commute exactly.

For v=A(F_x)xi B(H_y), trace cyclicity and (A8) yield

  <v, L(M_a)R(N_b)v>
  =tr_N(xi^* G_x,corner xi K_y,corner)
  =chi(G_x^(alpha,a),K_y^(gamma,b)).                        (A9)

The n factor in xi cancels the normalized n^2 matrix trace. The output
alphabet is the ORIGINAL A,B, with defaults only on unused supports.

Given r0,x,y BEFORE E, unrevealed X_>i and Y_<i are independent, with
law product_{j>i}mu(X_j|Y_j) product_{j<i}mu(Y_j|X_j). Neither law
depends on the live pair. Averaging the original fine product effects
therefore gives chi(G_x,K_y). If theta(r)=1, the tested alpha,gamma
already imply E. Dividing (A9) by ||v||^2 on Q-positive tuples gives
the EXACT conditioned answer law at live i. Thus the ideal win mean
q is unchanged by compression, even for arbitrarily tiny conditional
masses. Zero norm tuples carry zero Q-mass and their POVMs are complete.

7. COMPOSITION AND INFERENCE SCOPE

Sections 1--6 supply precisely the companion's weighted mass hypotheses
in a single finite tracial N. The normalized u and z are arbitrary
unit vectors in L2(N), with one-question descriptions; positivity of
these vectors is not assumed by S. The original state xi is positive
and input-independent. Both original answer measurements and the new
extended-input measurement families are legal left/right POVMs.

Only S and L are assumed in the endpoint composition. In particular:
- no arbitrary-Hilbert-space vector sampler is inferred from S;
- no finite-dimensional approximation of M is used;
- no strong posterior independence or posterior-product TV is used;
- no input atom or resampling spectral gap is introduced;
- no quantum conditional mutual information or recovery theorem is used;
- no statement of Song's final repetition theorem is an axiom.

The fixed-order scalar-operator entropy telescope, exact weighted mass
normalization and finite-feature renormalization are sufficient for the
conditional-coordinate inequality, at arbitrary fixed k and p>0.
