# Failed routes, model attacks, and decision gates

## Attacks on the mechanism-to-design transfer

1. **Forward SET is not literally equal in the retrieved experiment.** The Ket-1 caption/prose conflict leaves $k_{ET,hard}/k_{ET,easy}$ either 1 or $2/15$ at equal substrate levels. Using the latter requires a much larger post-SET commitment advantage. The measured TA rates are from substrate-quenching experiments, not a fully calibrated per-channel set in the operating DMF/electrochemical mixture.
2. **The exact selectivity formula is source-reported.** The 2026 SI already gives the two-channel QSS expression. Repeating it or its high-level kinetic-pulling interpretation is not a fresh theorem or a novelty result.
3. **BET and fragmentation are not acquired in situ as complete hazards.** The source offers transient evidence and DFT calculations, but the aliphatic Ket-1 ketyl is not directly observed in the cited TA experiment. A DFT ΔG‡ cannot be substituted for the effective product-first-passage hazard without validating the reaction network, solvent, concentrations, and entropic/standard-state corrections.
4. **The published potential map uses illustrative parameter choices.** Its diffusion cap, acceptor level, chemical sink rate and redox potentials are assumptions for a model; the $\beta=560$ setpoint calculation is a conditional sensitivity gate, not a fit to the 70:7 endpoint.
5. **The catalyst state is not a free control.** The extension assumes a two-state pool $D+A=C_T$, but the optimized experiment is a mixed photochemical/electrochemical protocol and the source allows multiple active reductant states. A practical design needs a measured transfer function from light/current/potential to $A(t),D(t)$, and its efficiency.
6. **A product fraction is not yield or efficiency.** The source's 70%/7% GC values are endpoint product amounts; $70/(70+7)=90.9\%$ is the fraction among those two products. It says nothing alone about photons absorbed, electrons per product, material balance, or isolated yield.
7. **The steady-state pool model omits depletion and cross-reactions.** It assumes constant substrates and independent radical channels. Ketyl fragmentation, styrene addition, crossover reactions, proton sources, catalyst degradation, and current/potential drift may couple channels or make rates time-dependent.
8. **Static optimum does not optimize arbitrary schedules.** The proof certifies a static acceptor setting. Pulsing may help or hurt if intermediates retain memory or if $A,D$ dynamics are slow; time-dependent control is outside the result and must not be inferred from the static theorem.
9. **The “robust” intervals are user-supplied.** Exact rational arithmetic certifies a theorem from intervals, but it does not acquire trustworthy intervals. Shared systematic errors or parameter correlations violate the rectangular uncertainty premise unless represented in a more detailed uncertainty set.

## Failed shortcuts

- No new reaction mechanism was inferred from generic Astra reaction-network cards. The selective Astra route contains no directly applicable photoredox/catalysis card; nearby permanence and event-chain cards do not certify product ratios.
- No theorem is claimed from the Article's abstract, source-reported DFT barriers, or a finite simulation alone.
- No wall-energy claim is made from the 1-mA current without measured cell-voltage integral, and no photonic energy claim is made from wavelength alone without light power and absorption/quantum-yield measurements.
- No priority claim is made. Nearby super-reduction/BET and cyclopropyl-ketone annulation work predates the candidate source; the source itself states the two-channel mechanism.

## Gates to a real design capability

1. Resolve the S2.18/S30 Ket-1 capture rate in a blinded, independently replicated measurement, with styrene measured in the same solvent and optical/electrochemical mixture.
2. Acquire channel-specific capture, BET, and full-path productive commitment hazards under the exact reaction conditions, including proton activity/water and cross-channel sinks. Directly test the predicted branch probabilities rather than extracting every rate from a single endpoint yield.
3. Measure the operando acceptor/donor fractions and their response to light intensity, wavelength, current, and potential. Quantify Zn consumption and close electron/material balance.
4. Provide cell $V(t)$, current $I(t)$, LED wall power, incident and absorbed spectral photon flux, reaction volume/time, workup recovery, and analytical calibration. Compare matched substrate amount and product rate, not just selectivity, against the Marcus/redox-potential baseline.
5. Fit bounded parameters on one condition, freeze the parameter intervals, then predict held-out acceptor concentrations and a second competitor. A held-out product split outside the robust interval, failure of the measured ordering $q_{target}^{lo}\ge\max q_{side}^{hi}$, or a better matched-resource baseline stops the capability claim.
6. Only after these pass, test whether time-varying control improves the static frontier; that requires the full time-dependent intermediate/catalyst-pool model and charged light/current schedule.
