## 1. Exact target

Find a quantum memory with a growing number of logical qubits, noisy single-round syndrome extraction, a finite-work real-time decoder, and a rigorous logical-error bound under coherent noise with a shared environment and arbitrary temporal memory. The desired bridge is from (i) single-shot QLDPC correction for coherent reduced-weight errors under per-location product circuit noise to (ii) a local non-Markovian fault-path model in which fault amplitudes can remain coherent across locations and rounds. Count the quantum and classical circuits, include their faults, and do not assume Euclidean-global gates are free.

The proposed code is a quantum Tanner code (QTC) family with parameters `[[n,k,d]]`, `k ≥ Rn`, `d ≥ δ_Q n` for constants `R,δ_Q>0`, constant-weight checks, and constant qubit-check degree. This family is combinatorially local on a left-right Cayley complex, not geometrically local on a fixed-dimensional Euclidean lattice.

## 2. Source-established ingredients

### 2.1 QTC parameters and a one-round Pauli decoder

Gu–Tang–Caha–Choe–He–Kubica, arXiv:2306.12470v2 (11 Apr 2024), published *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 405, 85 (2024), https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12470. Their Theorem 2.3 gives, under the explicit local-code distance/product-expansion hypotheses and constant-degree Ramanujan left-right Cayley graphs,

\[
k\ge (1-2\rho)^2n,\qquad
d\ge \frac{d_r^2\kappa^2}{256\Delta}\,n.
\]

Checks have constant weight and each qubit belongs to a bounded number of checks; the code’s locality is on the Cayley complex. The paper explicitly distinguishes this from geometrically local topological codes. QTC check extraction uses one round of constant-weight measurements; the bounded check degrees permit a constant-depth gate schedule and `O(n)` checks/ancillas/locations. This is graph-local hardware connectivity. It does not give a constant-depth embedding into a fixed-dimensional Euclidean layout.

For the parallel mismatch decoder, their Theorem 4.20 states that if

\[
A|e|_R+B|D|_V\le \min(C_\delta n,d/\Delta),
\]

then after `k` parallel iterations the Pauli correction `fhat^(k)` obeys

\[
|e+\hat f^{(k)}|_R\le \alpha_k |e|_R+\beta |D|_V,
\quad
\alpha_k=\frac{24}{5\kappa}(1-\gamma)^k,
\quad
\beta=\frac{6\Delta^2}{\kappa\delta},
\quad
\gamma=\frac{1-18\delta}{16},\quad 0<\delta<1/18.
\]

For any fixed target `alpha_k<1`, `k` is a constant independent of `n`; each parallel iteration is local on a bounded-degree code graph. The paper’s Theorem 3.5 gives a repeated-round invariant for deterministic Pauli data and syndrome errors: if `|e_i|≤Rn` and `|D_i|≤Sn` on every round, then

\[
|e'_i|_R\le \frac{\alpha R+\beta S}{1-\alpha}\,n.
\]

Corollary 3.8 further treats `p`-bounded stochastic errors with arbitrary temporal correlations. These are source theorems for Pauli/support noise, not shared-bath coherent fault paths.

### 2.2 Coherent reduced-weight recovery under product local circuit noise

Christandl–Fawzi–Goswami, arXiv:2512.02760v1 (2 Dec 2025), published *PRX Quantum* 6, 040334 (13 Nov 2025), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.02760 and https://doi.org/10.1103/k4cm-pp9p. Their Definition 9 allows a general superoperator decomposition

\[
V=\sum_i E_i(\cdot)E_i'{}^*,
\qquad
\max_i\{|E_i|_{\rm red},|E_i'|_{\rm red}\}\le t,
\]

where reduced operator weight is defined by Pauli expansion modulo stabilizers. Their Corollary 1 extends QTC single-shot recovery to arbitrary linear data error operators. Their Theorem 5 then handles a single noisy syndrome-extraction round for an input reduced-weight superoperator. In the paper’s notation, for constants `R,S,beta` with `beta S n+R n < d_min/2`, circuit depth `d=O(1)`, `kappa=e^d`, `h` code blocks, and

\[
5\delta,2^{d+1}\le S,
\qquad
h\kappa e\exp(-n\delta/3)\le 1,
\qquad
|V|_{\rm red}\le \beta Sn+Rn,
\]

the one-round output is within trace norm

\[
h\kappa e\exp(-n\delta/3)
\]

of a superoperator `V'` whose reduced weight is at most

\[
|V'|_{\rm red}\le \beta Sn+5n\delta,2^{d+1}.
\]

The theorem permits input error correlations across code blocks. Its crucial circuit-noise hypothesis is different: in each layer, noisy operations tensorize across disjoint locations, and each local channel is diamond distance at most `delta` from the ideal channel (their Eqs. 9–10 and 45). It does not model a common bath that coherently correlates faults at distinct locations. It proves coherent data-operator recovery through noisy extraction for the product-local circuit model, not the shared-bath extension sought here.

### 2.3 Coherent code-capacity qLDPC threshold

Han–Zhao–Xu–Wang–Liu, arXiv:2609.20537v1 (17 Sep 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20537. Their Theorem 2 considers any stabilizer qLDPC family with `d_n=Omega(log n)` and a coherent error unitary of fixed depth `D`, each layer a product of disjoint gates `exp(-i eta h)` with `||h||≤1` and support at most fixed `r`. After the error, all stabilizers are measured perfectly and a CPTP recovery follows. For their minimum-weight recovery,

\[
\sup_U \varepsilon_n^{\rm MW}(U,\eta)
\le C_{\rm MW} n e^{-c_{\rm MW}d_n}
\quad (|\eta|<\eta_{\rm th}^{\rm MW}).
\]

Their optimal-recovery version has `C sqrt(n) exp(-c d_n)`. Their cluster resummation avoids a naive exponential-in-`n` bound. The paper states that support locality need not be geometric and notes that noisy circuit-level syndrome extraction remains a next step (Discussion, lines 181–185). The theorem does not provide noisy measurements, a real-time decoder, or a shared environment.

### 2.4 Standard coherent local-noise fault-path bounds

Aharonov–Kitaev–Preskill (AKP), *Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation with Long-Range Correlated Noise*, *Physical Review Letters* 96, 050504 (published 7 Feb 2006), https://preskill.caltech.edu/pubs/preskill-2006-long-range.pdf, arXiv:quant-ph/0510231. They formulate a local non-Markovian condition by grouping fine-grained paths into coarse fault paths. For a specified set `I_r` of `r` faulty macrolocations, with arbitrary behavior elsewhere, their Eq. (9) requires

\[
\|E(I_r)\|\le \epsilon^r.
\]

Their long-range pairwise Hamiltonian model has

\[
H_{SB}(t)=\sum_{i<j} H_{ij}(t),\qquad
\max_{i,t}\sum_{j\ne i}\|H_{ij}(t)\|t_0<\mu_0,
\]

and yields the local fault-path condition for sufficiently small `mu_0`; their stated conversion is `epsilon = e^{1-1/(2e)} sqrt(2 mu_0)` for two-qubit interactions (see Eqs. 21–24). The bath Hamiltonian may be arbitrary and the bath is not refreshed. AKP then invokes a standard recursive fault-tolerance theorem for fixed-size error-correcting gadgets. It does not prove that the `n`-dependent QTC single-shot extraction/decoder gadget has a uniformly controlled bad-part norm.

Terhal–Burkard, arXiv:quant-ph/0402104, published *Physical Review A* 71, 012336 (2005), https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0402104. Their threshold theorem assumes “noninteracting qubits have noninteracting baths,” and a concatenated code correcting two errors with spread one. They explicitly say in Sec. 3 that a general common bath shared across disjoint rectangles prevents the factorization needed for their bad-rectangle recurrence. AKP’s stronger long-range fault-path treatment is the more suitable source model for this target.

### 2.5 Geometric locality obstruction

Bravyi–Poulin–Terhal, arXiv:0909.5200, *Physical Review Letters* 104, 050503 (2010), https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5200, prove `k d^2=O(n)` for geometrically local commuting constraints in 2D. The higher-dimensional BPT form is

\[
k d^{2/(D-1)}=O(n)
\]

for fixed-dimensional `D`-dimensional geometrically local stabilizer constraints under their hypotheses. Thus a finite-rate, growing-distance QTC family cannot at the same time have bounded-diameter Euclidean stabilizer checks in fixed `D`. The candidate hardware model is bounded-degree graph-locality on the Cayley incidence graph. A Euclidean implementation would need to account for long-range wiring, routing, or a different code family.

## 3. Frozen candidate mechanism and internal reduction

Let one memory cycle contain: (i) one constant-depth extraction of all QTC checks using `O(n)` data/measurement ancillas and `O(n)` two-qubit gates, (ii) the fixed-iteration parallel mismatch decoder, and (iii) a Pauli-frame update or parallel one-qubit correction. Include idle locations, resets, readout, decoder message edges, local decoder gates, and corrections in the cycle. Compile the decoder as a bounded-fan-in/fan-out circuit on the bounded-degree Tanner/Cayley graph. For a fixed iteration count, it has depth `O(1)` and `O(n)` local operations. All graph edges used by check extraction and decoder communication are counted; none are identified with fixed-dimensional Euclidean nearest-neighbor edges.

**Candidate light-cone statement.** If the complete extraction-plus-decoder circuit has depth `D_0=O(1)`, bounded fan-in/fan-out, and gate arity at most two, then one faulty location has a causal cone of at most `s_0=O(1)` data, syndrome, and correction bits. Therefore a fault set `F` of size `f` induces, for each Pauli term in its operator expansion, data-error and syndrome/control-error supports bounded by

\[
|e(F)|_R\le a f,
\qquad |D(F)|_V\le b f,
\]

for constants `a,b` depending on `D_0` and graph degree. This is an internal circuit-geometry bound conditional on local decoder compilation; it is not a source theorem for the full noisy decoder implementation.

For the ideal classical decoder, choose a parallel iteration count with `alpha<1`. Suppose before a round the reduced data error is at most `r n` and the round has at most `tau n` faulty locations. The deterministic single-shot recurrence would give

\[
r_{j+1}\le \alpha(r_j+a\tau)+\beta b\tau + c_{\rm dec}\tau,
\]

where `c_dec tau n` accounts for faults in local decoder/control/correction operations after including their bounded causal cones. Its fixed point is

\[
r_* = \frac{(\alpha a+\beta b+c_{\rm dec})\tau}{1-\alpha}.
\]

Taking `tau>0` sufficiently small makes the one-round premise

\[
A(r_*+a\tau)+B b\tau\le C_*:=\min(C_\delta,d/(\Delta n))
\]

hold and makes `r_*<d/(2n)`. This is a valid algebraic implication **if** the stated Pauli support reduction applies uniformly to each fault path and if the decoder logic faults have the claimed bounded causal effect. The one-shot QTC theorem then places each good Pauli path in a correctable residual class. CFG’s operator extension indicates how to preserve coherent superpositions when all path terms are inside that class.

For a full coherent bath, the target model would supply a fault-path operator `W_F` for each fault set. If one had an absolute exact-path bound

\[
\|W_F\|\le \eta^{|F|},
\]

and `N_0 n` fault locations per cycle, then truncating at `|F|≤tau n` would give

\[
\|B_{\rm cycle}\|
\le \sum_{j>\tau n}{N_0n\choose j}\eta^j
\le \frac{q^{\lceil\tau n\rceil}}{1-q},
\qquad q:=\frac{eN_0\eta}{\tau}<1.
\]

This is an elementary bound for that **stronger exact-path hypothesis**. If the good-path recovery were proved to act as logical identity tensored with an environment operator, telescoping over `T` rounds would yield logical diamond error at most `O(T q^{tau n})`, hence exponential memory lifetime for fixed `eta` below threshold and `O(log(T/epsilon))` block size for target error `epsilon` over `T` rounds. This end-to-end step is not established here.

The physically broader AKP hypothesis is instead the aggregate-cylinder bound `||E(I_r)||≤epsilon^r` for specified faulty locations, allowing arbitrary behavior elsewhere. It is **not** the exact-path bound `||W_F||≤eta^{|F|}` above. A sub-sum of bad fault paths is not automatically norm-bounded by the cylinder sums because coherent terms can cancel in the aggregate. AKP’s fixed-gadget malignant-set analysis resolves this for fixed `t`-fault-tolerant gadgets; applying it with a code and gadget size growing as `O(n)` requires a uniform high-distance gadget lemma and a bound on the resulting constants. Neither that lemma nor the full noisy local decoder circuit proof is in hand. No lifetime/error theorem is claimed.

## 4. Exact obstruction and counterexample

Single-site marginals do not imply a useful logical threshold. For any distance-`d` code, let `L` be a minimum-weight logical Pauli and define

\[
\mathcal N_p(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+pL\rho L^\dagger.
\]

Each qubit has error-event marginal at most `p`, yet the encoded logical channel fails with probability `p`, independent of `n`. For a coherent version, let a shared environment qubit control a unitary rotation generated by `L\otimes Y_E`; tracing out that environment produces coherent/logical dephasing with nonvanishing logical error while every qubit in `supp(L)` has the same local event marginal. These examples violate local fault-path domination: for every nonempty `S\subseteq supp(L)`, the probability of all sites in `S` being hit is `p`, not `p^{|S|}`. They show why a marginal calibration number alone is insufficient; they do not refute the AKP Hamiltonian-locality model.

The unresolved shared-bath point is stronger than this example. A common bath may correlate fault amplitudes across locations; an assumption only on individual gate-channel diamond distances does not control this. Conversely, an AKP-style `E(I_r)` bound controls coherent sums over paths but is not by itself a bound on arbitrary selected sub-sums. We need either (i) prove the QTC recovery circuit is a high-distance fault-tolerant gadget to which the AKP aggregate fault-path theorem applies with uniform `n`-dependence, or (ii) find a counterexample satisfying the exact AKP local norm condition that defeats this gadget.

## 5. Alternatives tested and limitations

1. **Use p-bounded support noise and arbitrary temporal correlation.** QTC Theorems 3.5 and Corollary 3.8 already provide the repeated-round support invariant and a union-bound memory guarantee. This is established stochastic/adversarial Pauli prior art, not a coherent shared-bath result.
2. **Use per-location coherent circuit noise.** CFG Theorem 5 gives single-shot coherent reduced-weight contraction through noisy extraction for a product of local channels. This is established prior art, but its channel tensorization excludes common-bath correlation between disjoint locations.
3. **Use coherent cluster resummation.** Han et al. prove code-capacity thresholds for bounded-depth coherent local circuit errors with perfect syndrome and MW decoding. Their component-resummation step depends on the local circuit expansion, and noisy extraction is explicitly left open. A shared bath can make disconnected location operators noncommute through bath degrees of freedom; replacing the interaction graph by “all share the bath” makes it dense and destroys the constant-degree cluster count. No valid adaptation was obtained.
4. **Use AKP as a black-box threshold theorem.** AKP proves the norm domination `||E(I_r)||≤epsilon^r` for suitable common-bath Hamiltonians, then thresholds fixed-size recursive gadgets. A QTC single-shot cycle appears to correct a linear number of faults at constant cycle depth, but treating it as a variable-size gadget needs proof that (a) all faulty quantum and classical locations have the claimed support spread, (b) its coherent good-path map is logical identity tensored with bath evolution, and (c) the bad-part norm has a uniform exponentially small bound. This is the exact open interface, not a completed reduction.
5. **Demand fixed-dimensional Euclidean locality plus finite rate and linear distance.** Under BPT hypotheses this is impossible for stabilizer checks. QTC graph locality does not evade this theorem by embedding in the plane; it changes the connectivity model.

## 6. Resource, calibration, and baseline ledger

For one QTC memory block: `n` data qubits; `O(n)` constant-weight checks; `O(n)` measurement ancillas if one ancilla is assigned per check; `O(n)` CNOT/readout/reset/idle locations; `O(1)` quantum extraction depth; `O(1)` decoder iterations with `O(n)` local classical operations and messages; `O(n)` parallel correction/frame outputs. Exact constants depend on the local component codes and Cayley degree. The assumed hardware graph must contain all check-ancilla edges and decoder communication links. No Euclidean routing cost has been included; a Euclidean layout would require a separate embedding/routing analysis.

The QTC decoder uses code constants and a fixed iteration count; it need not be given the actual per-round error realization or a learned probability distribution. A coherent shared-bath theorem would assume a certified upper bound on the relevant Hamiltonian/fault-path norm (`lambda t0` or the AKP aggregate `epsilon`). The decoder does not estimate this parameter. Estimating/certifying a bound on all correlated fault paths from finite calibration data is not provided; one-qubit average infidelity does not certify it.

Matched prior baselines:

- QTC with p-bounded stochastic data and syndrome support noise, arbitrary correlation in time: known repeated-round support theorem, but stochastic support model.
- QTC with per-location general circuit channels within diamond `delta`: known coherent reduced-weight single-shot theorem, but tensorized channel model.
- General qLDPC coherent code-capacity noise: known 2026 cluster-resummation theorem, but perfect readout and postmeasurement recovery.
- Concatenated distance-five fault-tolerance with local non-Markovian baths/AKP long-range Hamiltonian noise: known coherent fault-path threshold, but different gadget/code architecture and no cited application to the constant-rate QTC single-shot round.

- **Source-reported:** coherent reduced-weight recovery through noisy extraction for per-location product circuit noise (CFG, Theorem 5).
- **Source-reported:** coherent code-capacity qLDPC threshold for fixed-depth bounded-support circuit errors with perfect syndrome (Han et al., Theorems 1–2).

- **Internally derived, conditional:** constant graph-depth QTC extraction/decoder implies a constant causal-cone factor per faulty location; the Pauli recurrence admits a positive linear fault fraction `tau` while keeping residual below `d/2`.
- **Unresolved:** derive a correctable good-path superoperator and an exponential bad-round norm for the QTC extraction+local decoder gadget from the AKP aggregate fault-path condition, including shared bath across rounds and all classical/measurement/control locations. Need an exact theorem with `n`-uniform constants and diamond/trace norm, or a counterexample under the same model.
