1. EXACT CONTRACT AND CANDIDATE LAW

The source contract is full n-copy density reconstruction for

  rho_(r,v)=(I+r v.sigma)/2, v in S^2,
  0<c0<=r sqrt(n)<=c1<infinity,

with sufficiently large n, known n and r, unknown v, worst-case half trace
error, no retained reference or entanglement assistance, and maximum quantum
branch dimension Q. Arbitrarily many classical labels are allowed in the
converse and are separately charged in the construction. Every channel and
normalization assumption is as in `quantum_branch.txt` Sections 1-5.

The audited claim, with constants depending only on c0,c1, is

  Q_min=Theta(r/epsilon),                         r^2<=epsilon<=r;
  Q_min=Theta(min{n+1,sqrt(n[1+log(r^2/epsilon)])}), 0<epsilon<r^2.

For epsilon=n^(-alpha), fixed alpha>1/2, this gives Q_min=Theta(n^(alpha-1/2))
for 1/2<alpha<1, Theta(sqrt(n)) at alpha=1, and Theta(sqrt(n log n)) for
alpha>1, until the exact branch cap n+1 takes over at exponentially small
accuracy. This is a maximum-branch theorem, not a total-hybrid-dimension lower.

2. LOAD-BEARING LOWER-BOUND AUDITS

Let t=atanh(r), d=2j+1, R_j=Tr(tau_(j,z)J_z), and a_j=R_j/(j+1). Direct
differentiation of the exact partition function gives

  a'_j=2[(coth(dt)+coth(t))/(d+1)^2
                   -dt csch^2(dt)/(d+1)].

Since coth(t)=1/r and coth(dt)<=1/r,

  a'_j<=1/[r(j+1)^2].

For j>=2/r, u=dt>=4. The negative derivative term is at most
2t csch^2(u); after multiplying by r(d+1)^2 this is bounded by
8(r/t)u^2/sinh^2(u)<=1. The positive term is at least
2/[r(d+1)^2]. Thus

  a'_j>=1/[4r(j+1)^2].

The exact truncated-geometric occupation mean yields
`a_j>=1-(1+r)/(2r(j+1))>=1-3/(4rj)` for r<=1/2. Combining both derivative
bounds gives `a_j-j a'_j>=1-7/(4rj)>=1/2` for rj>=4, and therefore
`(a_j/j)'<=-1/(2j^2)`. An independent audit also derives the sharper
sufficient threshold rj>=3 from the same coarse bounds; the source's rj>=4
threshold is safe. Full derivation: `derivative.txt`.

For a rank-at-most-Q Kraus component from spin j to spin k, the exact score
identity and Schmidt-rank energy lemma in `quantum_branch.txt` give

  Delta_(jk)>= (a_k-a_j)^2/2
       + a_j^2/j - a_j a_k/max(j,k)
       + a_j a_k g_Q(j,k)/(jk).

The middle term is nonnegative because a_j increases and a_j/j decreases.
With Q>=1/r and input j in [16Q,32Q], the near-output projector bound,
the lower derivative estimate for far-lower outputs, and the decreasing
a_j/j estimate for far-higher outputs give, for every output k,

  Delta_(jk)>=1/(786432 r Q^3).

For near outputs use `g_Q>=max(j,k)/(32Q)` and `a_j,a_k>=1/3`; for far-low
outputs integrate `a'_u>=1/[4r(u+1)^2]` from
`k0=j(1-1/(16Q))` to j; for far-high outputs integrate
`-(a_u/u)' >=1/(2u^2)` from j to `k0=j/(1-1/(16Q))`. These steps are valid
through the comparison points because rj>=16 and rk0>=15. The k=0 case has
target score at least 1/18 and also satisfies the stated tiny lower bound.

The bound is per normalized Choi/Kraus vector. For each input j, the actual
conditional weights are `w_(jkz)=||A_(kjz)||_HS^2/(2j+1)` and sum to one by
trace preservation. Composing a branch-dimension-Q encoder with any decoder,
pinching output sectors, and regenerating parameter-independent Schur
multiplicities preserve Kraus rank at most Q. Since every input/output pair
has nonnegative deficit, sector-changing decoders cannot cancel the selected
input band's gain. Full score and transport audit: `score.txt`.

The exact Schur law gives, for allowed parity L=2j,

  P_L>=((L+1)^2/(n+1)) pi_0(L)(1-r^2)^(n/2),
  pi_0(L)=2^(-n) binom(n,(n-L)/2).

For 0<=L<=n/2, Stirling with its explicit remainder gives
`pi_0(L)>=(2 sqrt(n))^(-1) exp(-2L^2/(3n))`; also
`(1-r^2)^(n/2)>=exp(-2c1^2/3)`. For integer Q<=n/128, the band
L in [32Q,64Q] lies in [0,n/2] and contains at least 16Q parity points.
Summing the pointwise bounds yields

  P(16Q<=j<=32Q)>=4096 exp(-2c1^2/3) Q^3 n^(-3/2)
                                  exp(-(8192/3)Q^2/n).

This independently reproduces both numerical constants and uses no local
limit assumption. Full factorial, parity, and domain audit: `spin_band.txt`.

The bounded score has global spectral width 2. Multiplying the last two
displayed bounds and dividing by that width gives

  e>=c_T/n exp(-C_T Q^2/n),
  c_T=exp(-2c1^2/3)/(384c1), C_T=8192/3,

for integer 1/r<=Q<=n/128. Here `r sqrt(n)<=c1` converts
`1/(r n^(3/2))` to at least `1/(c1 n)`. At Q0=ceil(1/r), for sufficiently
large n, `Q0<=n/128` and `Q0^2/n<=9/(4c0^2)`. Thus

  e_Q^*>=c_*/n for Q<=Q0,
  c_*=c_T exp(-9 C_T/(4c0^2))>0.

For epsilon<c_*/n, any successful code has Q>Q0. Either Q>n/128, or the
tail converse inverts to
`Q>=sqrt((n/C_T)log(c_T/(n epsilon)))`. With
kappa=r sqrt(n) in [c0,c1], this logarithm is uniformly comparable to
`1+log(r^2/epsilon)` on that range. For c_*/n<=epsilon<r^2, the fixed-Q
converse already gives Q=Omega(sqrt(n)); the logarithm in the target law is
bounded there. These two cases close the lower endpoint without a gap and
give Omega(n) once the logarithm is order n. Detailed endpoint algebra:
`endpoints.txt`.

3. MATCHING FINITE TAPERED-CODE UPPER

Use the positive polar filter and finite positive SU(2) quadrature already
specified in `quantum_branch.txt` Sections 9-11. In sector L, the branch is
identity if L+1<=Q; otherwise the full-state thermal estimate gives

  e_j<=(4r/Q)(1+rL) exp(tL).

Combining with the exact Schur upper weight and X, a sum of n unbiased signs,
gives

  e<=8r/(Qn) E[(X+1)^2(1+rX) exp(2tX) 1_(X>=Q)].

Set Y=(|X|+1)^2(1+r|X|)exp(2tX). Then Hoeffding followed by Cauchy-Schwarz
changes `Pr(X>=Q)<=exp(-Q^2/(2n))` to the correct tail factor
`exp(-Q^2/(4n))`. For the second moment,

  (|X|+1)^4(1+r|X|)^2
       <=16(X^4+1+r^2X^6+r^2X^2).

Using `E exp(uX)<=exp(nu^2/2)`, the even-moment bounds
`E X^8<=105n^4`, `E X^12<=10395n^6`, and `E X^4<=3n^2`, and
`nt^2<=16c1^2/9`, one obtains exactly

  sqrt(E Y^2)<=M(c1)n,
  M(c1)=4 sqrt(sqrt(105)+1+c1^2(sqrt(10395)+sqrt(3)))
             exp(128c1^2/9).

Therefore `e<=8M(c1)(r/Q)exp(-Q^2/(4n))`; for Q>=1/r this is
`e<=C_U(c1)n^(-1)exp(-Q^2/(4n))`, `C_U=8M(c1)c1^2`. The tail upper,
including the nested square roots and all factors, is independently
rederived in `tail_upper.txt`; `tail_upper_spectral.txt` is a second
independent audit with log positivity and exact cutoff details.

For epsilon<c_*/n choose an integer ceiling of the maximum of 1/r and
`2 sqrt(n log(C_U/(n epsilon)))`; the log is positive on this range.
If this exceeds n, use Q=n+1, which makes every Schur branch exact. Q=n
is not the exact cutoff because the top L=n sector still has dimension n+1.
For c_*/n<=epsilon<r^2, the coarser O(r/Q) bound uses Q=O(sqrt(n)); for
r^2<=epsilon<=r it uses Q=O(r/epsilon). The classical alphabet stays
O(n^3), each branch dimension is charged, and total direct-sum dimension is
O(n^3 Q). Schur/gate synthesis, numerical precision, runtime, and physical
output preparation are separate unpriced implementation costs. The endpoint
and integer-rounding audit is in `tail_upper_spectral.txt`.

5. COSTS, PRIOR ART, FALSIFIERS, AND OPEN GATES

Supplied inputs are n,r,c0,c1 and target epsilon; the direction v is not
acquired. The lower permits unlimited classical labels and arbitrary
sector-changing decoding, with no free side register or entangled reference.
The upper uses an explicit finite O(n^3)-label design and max branch Q, so
its total D is O(n^3 Q). It is not a minimum-total-D claim. An exact Schur
tail archive gives a smaller all-accuracy total-D upper O(n[1+log(1/epsilon)]);
these optimize different resources.

Adjacent primary sources already checked in the base proof include Terhal
and Horodecki, arXiv:quant-ph/9911117 v1 (1999-11-29; Schmidt number), Bagan
et al., arXiv:quant-ph/0510158 v1 (2005-10-20; directional posterior
moment), Breuer, arXiv:quant-ph/0506224 v1 (2005-06-27; rotationally
invariant spin systems), and Aschieri, Ruba, Solovej, arXiv:2404.04133 v1
(2024-04-05; SU(2)-equivariant channels). Yang, Chiribella, Hayashi,
arXiv:1606.02893 v3 (2016), is a close Schur-compression baseline. These
sources provide tools, not this triangular critical branch-precision law.
This bounded local-source comparison is not a priority search; novelty and
external correctness remain UNKNOWN.

Falsifiers include an error in the exact spin derivative, a rank-Q output
sector with score gap below 1/(786432 r Q^3), failure of the parity/Stirling
mass bound, a normalization/width error in converting score to trace error,
or a legal finite code that beats the converse. The independent audits found
none. Remaining gates are expert external proof review, priority/literature
search, optimized finite-gate and precision costs, and the optimal joint
classical/quantum total-D tradeoff. The unknown-radius statement should retain
the exact c1-scaled transition or state its restricted epsilon range.
