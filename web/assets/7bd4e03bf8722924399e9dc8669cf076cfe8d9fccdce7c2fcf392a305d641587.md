# c7 scientific measurement lead: endpoint force-order assay

## Verdict

The strongest result is a conditional endpoint measurement theorem for two-state force-driven molecular kinetics. It survives arbitrary **fixed, measured ramp waveforms**, common endpoint transitions/background, and unknown initial occupancy. With three equally spaced plateau durations, the ratio of adjacent AB−BA order contrasts identifies the relaxation rate λA=k01(A)+k10(A) without calibrating the FRET-emission amplitude. A current HJ optical-tweezer/smFRET setup could implement the force loops in the dark and take a common low-dose endpoint read.

This is not a validated capability or priority-cleared discovery. Pulse-order kinetics, HMM rate fitting, and HJ force-smFRET all have prior art. The result's practical value is a narrowly specified, initial-state-free randomized endpoint estimand that does not require a no-jump ramp gate. The rate inversion is sample-expensive, and a full photon-arrival likelihood on the same active record is the mandatory comparator and can dominate. The durable status is **conditional model theorem / proposed physical protocol / physical transfer UNKNOWN / novelty UNKNOWN**.

## Mechanism and quantitative consequence

Each fixed two-state waveform is an affine occupancy map F(x)=ax+b. For A and B loop maps, the difference between the AB and BA endpoint occupancies is

\[
F_B(F_A(x))-F_A(F_B(x))=(a_B-1)b_A+(1-a_A)b_B,
\]

so it is independent of the pre-pulse occupancy. A loop with fixed C→A and A→C ramps around an A plateau of duration s has aA(s)=A0 exp(−λA s) and bA(s)=B0+B1 exp(−λA s). Thus, for a fixed B loop, the order contrast after a common terminal read is D(s)=C0+C1 exp(−λA s). Three equally spaced durations give

\[
\frac{D(s_0+2\tau)-D(s_0+\tau)}{D(s_0+\tau)-D(s_0)}=e^{-\lambda_A\tau}.
\]

The measured terminal channel can include transitions during exposure, common Poisson background, and photon noise; it multiplies all three contrasts by the same unknown response. No-pulse reset is needed. Fixed ramps need not be transition-free. The load-bearing gates are a two-state Markov interface, repeated ramps, nonzero curvature, and one history-independent common read channel.

This identifies the sum of directional rates, not both rates separately. With heterogeneous molecules and same-sign curvature, the pooled ratio is a response-weighted mean of exp(−λi τ), which brackets contributing rates but is not their population mean. Opposite response signs break that bracket, and a class with zero order curvature is invisible. Current HJ literature reports substantial kinetic heterogeneity, so those are real scope restrictions.

The active-control connection is exact but narrow. Under the c4 full-likelihood lower bound, all actions preserve the same uniform stationary law and mix at fixed contraction. In a two-state CTMC, a shared stationary occupancy makes force generators commute and the AB−BA effect vanishes. Force inputs with πA≠πB leave that action class and can produce an endpoint-law gap O(κ), hence O(κ²) KL under a weak reporter, versus the passive theorem's O(κ⁴) class. This is not a refutation of c4: it identifies the exact mechanism—changing the stationary occupancy under control—that its hypotheses exclude. A constructed two-state witness has passive C-only laws exactly equal and active order gap 0.2398κ, but needs about 514κ⁻² endpoint records per arm at 95% paired-difference Hoeffding control—about 51,400 per arm at κ=0.1.

## Physical assay and cost

Hohng et al. demonstrated HJ force-smFRET at 100-Hz bandwidth with force applied by a piezo sample scanner and the confocal beam tracking the molecule. They acquired five 10-second force traces at 10-ms integration and one to five cycles before bleaching (50–250 seconds), observed force-dependent population shifts and asymmetric rate changes, and fit the trajectories with an HMM. This establishes the basic force/FRET hardware and a force-shifted equilibrium, but not the proposed AB/BA endpoint protocol or loop ramp settling. The source does not give photon-arrival totals, per-endpoint backgrounds, or dynamic ramp impulse responses.

Proposed treatment: return to the same baseline C, apply an A force loop and a B force loop in either AB or BA order, return to C, then illuminate for the same short endpoint FRET read. Keep fluorescence dark during the force loops to reduce bleaching, record force and piezo trajectories, and interleave randomized order arms. A no-tether/no-acceptor source-off control and identical common-read exposure gate arm-specific background. This is physically grounded by the existing piezo/tweezer/beam-following design, but dark-pulse endurance and loop repeatability remain untested.

At an explicitly illustrative planning point, the Hohng XR value Δxeq=4.4±0.8 nm and a 1.7-pN force increment, inserted into a two-state equilibrium logistic model near a half-populated baseline, imply a shift Δπ≈0.360. Combining this with a separate 2025 HJ FRET state separation of about 0.35 and plateau durations of 3/λ gives scalar endpoint gap g≈0.114, and a distribution-free two-arm test needs n≈2,276 independent molecules per order arm at 95% two-sided error control. The 0.35 is only a rough reported FRET-level separation from a separate experiment; it is not a calibrated common endpoint channel or a measured background/leakage-corrected gap. With the same Δπ and dwell, assumed scalar read contrast ζ=0.1 yields g≈0.0325 and n≈27,878 per arm; ζ=1 yields g≈0.325 and n=279. The nominal ζ=0.35 case requires 4,552 endpoint molecules across the two arms—about 6–15 times the 151–360 molecules per static force condition reported by 2025 MAESTRO, which does not provide programmable force loops. These values combine separate studies and are conditional scale estimates: neither exact force/rates nor the read-channel gap were measured in one setup.

Rate estimation costs much more. In the same scale illustration, choosing λAτ=log(1.5) gives adjacent contrast differences about 0.0399 and 0.0266. A simultaneous paired-difference Hoeffding precision of 4×10⁻⁴ on each of the three order contrasts needs about 59.8 million molecules per arm, six treatment arms total, for an approximately 12% rate interval. This conditional bound presumes the curvature differences are separated from zero and share a sign; if the sign is not fixed by an independent calibration, a sign/dynamic-range pilot or a sign-agnostic simultaneous interval must be charged too. It likewise assumes independent founders, so does not count repeated photons as independent molecules. This conservative result rules out treating the algebraic ratio as automatically practical. Exact endpoint photons, repeated measures, clustering, and a pre-calibrated common read may change constants; none is sourced. The full photon-arrival likelihood of the common endpoint read is the strongest equal-dose comparator for the proposed dark-pulse assay, and data processing means the scalar endpoint summary cannot beat that record at matched cost.

I also computed a matched force-on-time control for the simpler no-ramp model. Given total plateau time T=sA+sB, the order gap is |πB−πA|(1−e⁻λA sA)(1−e⁻λB sB). Its unique maximizing split equates marginal log gain, λA/(e^(λA sA)−1)=λB/(e^(λB sB)−1). In a conditional T=2 s, |πB−πA|=0.6, (λA,λB)=(0.5,2) s⁻¹ example, optimized durations (1.251,0.749) s raise the gap from 0.2041 to 0.2166 and reduce the conservative per-arm count bound from 709 to 629. This 11% count reduction assumes rates are known; a real design must charge the pilot and optimize using measured ramp maps. It is not globally waveform-optimal or photon-Fisher-optimal. No actual gain over the best active photon-likelihood protocol is established.

## Failure attacks and next gate

The finite check composes nontrivial piecewise-constant ramps and verifies the ratio to 1.1×10⁻¹⁶ and initial-state spread zero across four starting occupancies. A same-sign heterogeneous mixture stays within its contributing exponential-rate range; an opposite-sign mixture gives a ratio outside every class range. These are synthetic formula checks only.

Return `UNKNOWN` if the observed force loops differ by order; a shared-read background/dye channel is not stable; force mismatch cannot be translated through a calibrated rate-force modulus; the read-channel gap ζ or order curvature is too small to resolve; the adjacent duration differences do not have a resolved common sign; a three-state/open-intermediate model fits better; or heterogeneous curvature signs may cancel. Compare against a full two-state CTMC/HMM photon-arrival likelihood under identical force waveform, photon budget, wall time, calibration, and blank exposure. A passive C-only baseline is weaker for force-dependent kinetics and is not the active information comparator.
