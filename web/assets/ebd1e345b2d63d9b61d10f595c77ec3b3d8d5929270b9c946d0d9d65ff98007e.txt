Cycle06 weak-endpoint prior-art memo
Checked 2026-10-09. This is a bounded primary-source audit; source PDFs and hashes are in SOURCES.txt and sources/.

Decision

The target qubit estimate

  J(rho) >= 2 E(rho)

for every faithful rho and faithful stationary sigma of a KMS-symmetric qubit QMS is exactly the p=1 case of weak L_p-regularity, under the Kastoryano–Temme (KT) normalization. The matching general theorem is not established by the checked sources. KT's Conjecture 19 predicts the stronger all-p weak-regularity statement for every primitive finite-dimensional Liouvillian; their separate reversible/KMS clause predicts strong regularity, which is stronger than J>=2E and is now known to fail for KMS qubits. No inspected theorem proves weak p=1 for every KMS qubit, or for all KMS-symmetric one-step channels. This conclusion is a source-status report, not a priority claim from search absence.

The apparent quantum Stroock–Varopoulos theorems do not close the gap: their decisive hypothesis is GNS symmetry. GNS, tracial, modular-phase-covariant-with-KMS, and the cited thermal/Davies classes have established stronger regularity. A general faithful-state KMS qubit need not belong to any of those subclasses.

Exact target and picture conventions

Use a Heisenberg QMS P_t=e^{-tL}, where P_t is unital CP, sigma is faithful and invariant, L is the positive generator, and KMS symmetry is self-adjointness in the sandwich inner product Tr[X* sigma^(1/2) Y sigma^(1/2)]. The Schrödinger dual Phi_t=P_{t*} is CPTP and preserves sigma; its adjoint Phi_t*=P_t is unital. Do not impose unitality or bistochasticity on the Schrödinger channel unless sigma=I/d.

With Gamma_sigma(X)=sigma^(1/2) X sigma^(1/2), put f=Gamma_sigma^(-1)(rho). KT's p=1 and p=2 forms give

  E_1(f) = J(rho)/2,
  E_2(I_{2,1} f) = E(rho),
  I_{2,1} f = sigma^(-1/4) sqrt(rho) sigma^(-1/4),

when the positive-generator/KMS-adjoint conventions above are used. Their Definition 9 therefore gives weak p=1 iff J>=2E, and strong p=1 iff J>=4E. The target weak estimate is not the strong endpoint and must not inherit its KMS counterexample.

For a single KMS-symmetric Heisenberg channel P with faithful invariant sigma, Poissonization e^{t(P-I)} is a KMS QMS with positive generator L=I-P. Its generator p=1 forms are exactly the channel losses J=Tr[(rho-P_*(rho))(log rho-log sigma)] and E=Tr[sqrt(rho) (I-T_P)(sqrt(rho))], where T_P=Gamma_sigma^(-1/2) P_* Gamma_sigma^(1/2). Thus a primitive-QMS theorem would imply the primitive-channel claim. For a nonprimitive channel, P_epsilon=(1-epsilon)P+epsilon E_sigma, E_sigma(X)=Tr(sigma X)I, is primitive and KMS-symmetric; continuity as epsilon decreases to zero transfers any established pointwise bound. This is an interface deduction, not a cited literature theorem.

What the primary sources establish

KT (2013), Definitions 6–9 and Conjecture 19, are the exact historical match. Definition 6 calls Gamma_sigma L=L* Gamma_sigma “detailed balance” or “reversible”; this is KMS symmetry. Definition 9 defines weak regularity by E_p(f)>=E_2(I_{2,p}f) for 1<=p<=2, so the requested inequality is the p=1 endpoint. Conjecture 19 states weak L_p-regularity for every primitive Liouvillian and strong L_p-regularity when it is reversible. In the 2013 source the authors say their numerical searches found no counterexample and leave this as a conjecture. Their J. Math. Phys. journal version retains the definitions and conjecture; its outlook still lists the conjecture as unsettled. This is the direct prior statement: weak p=1 is conjectured in a broader class, not proved for arbitrary KMS qubits.

The established stronger theorem is under GNS symmetry. Bardet, Definition 4.2 and Theorem 4.1, assumes self-adjointness in Tr[sigma X*Y] and proves strong L_p-regularity for every p>=1. Beigi–Datta–Rouze, Theorem 14, assumes “strongly sigma-reversible,” which their Section 3.1 defines as GNS self-adjointness; its quantum Stroock–Varopoulos comparison is not KMS-only. Rouze's 2018 thesis, Theorem 5.4.2, is a useful hidden-theorem check: it assumes the conditions of Theorem 5.2.6(b), namely omega-DBC/GNS. For its nontracial modular group, KMS symmetry alone becomes omega-DBC only with modular covariance (Lemma 5.2.5 and the discussion around equation (5.16)). So this later broad-form presentation does not establish the target for general KMS symmetry.

The subclasses separate as follows.

* Tracial/unital: when sigma=I/d, the KMS and GNS inner products coincide. KT's primitive unital reversible result gives strong regularity, hence J>=4E and weak J>=2E.
* Modular-phase-covariant: if a KMS-symmetric map also commutes with the modular automorphism group of the same sigma, it is GNS-symmetric. Vernooij–Wirth state this equivalence for finite matrices. In a nondegenerate qubit reference basis this is covariance under the corresponding phase rotations. The existing GNS theorem then applies.
* Davies/thermal: KT Theorem 20 proves strong L_p-regularity for bounded thermal (Davies) semigroups in their thermal-generator class. This is a special class result, not a theorem for every KMS generator.
* General KMS qubits: Liu–Wan–Wu Proposition 5.1 gives an explicit primitive KMS-symmetric, non-GNS M_2 semigroup. This confirms that general KMS cannot simply be identified with the GNS/modular-covariant subclass. Their paper proves MLSI and gives a CMLSI obstruction, not weak L_p-regularity. The exact eta witness audited in the separate counterexample memo violates J>=4E but has J/E approximately 3.8078>2, so it does not refute weak p=1.

Gao–Wang v2 (5 Oct 2026) states that strong L_p-regularity is known under GNS but not known to follow from KMS symmetry, then derives MLSI bounds by another route without L_p-regularity. That is not a weak-p=1 theorem. Liu–Wan–Wu's universal primitive-KMS MLSI result likewise compares entropy to entropy production with generator/state-dependent constants; it does not compare J to the square-root Dirichlet energy E. Neither result closes the present endpoint by implication.

Prior-art gate and next decisive step

The exact status after this bounded check is: (i) theorem for GNS/tracial/modular-covariant and cited thermal/Davies subclasses; (ii) KT conjecture for weak regularity in all primitive dimensions; (iii) no checked theorem for the full KMS qubit class; (iv) no checked weak-p=1 counterexample in that class. Therefore a proved universal qubit J>=2E result would resolve the qubit restriction of a named longstanding conjecture, but no priority or novelty claim follows from this audit.

For the parent candidate, the deciding proof obligation is to establish J>=2E for every faithful rho and every faithful sigma for an arbitrary primitive KMS-symmetric qubit generator, including non-GNS generators such as Liu–Wan–Wu's. A source-level falsifier would be an exact faithful rho on such a generator with J<2E. The existing strong-endpoint witness has the wrong threshold and is not such a falsifier. If the qubit semigroup theorem is proved, the Poissonization/epsilon-regularization argument above transfers it to all faithful-state KMS qubit channels with the stated one-step J/E interface.

Scope note: the literature check was intentionally narrow. “No theorem found” means UNKNOWN in the checked sources, not novelty by absence. This memo did not inspect proof-worker reports or claim an external review.
