FINAL STATUS

UNRESOLVED at the full static hard-projected BCS interface. The direct
bosonic N81/KLM transfer fails at exact fermionic gadgets: Pauli exclusion
removes the required double-occupancy branch, the Walsh repeated-row readout
has zero determinant, and the supplied NS contraction does not have a
scalar 0/1 fermionic branch. These are route-specific failures, not a general
no-go. c03_l02 independently obtained the overlapping KLM/Walsh obstruction;
I do not claim priority or novelty for that center.

The deeper alternative-route audit found that Beenakker et al.'s
interleaved-charge-measurement model has a fixed postselected CNOT branch:
p1=p2=1 and ancilla outcome z=0 give CNOT with exact amplitude 1/(2 sqrt(2))
and probability 1/8. However, an exact Fock calculation shows why this does
not immediately compile to the target's one terminal hard projector. For
the named polarizing-beam-splitter detector, the interleaved charge-one
branch is the even-spin-parity projector, while a terminal charge-one
projector after undoing the splitter is identity on the full one-per-arm
input subspace. Opposite-spin inputs are killed in the actual detector
branch but survive after simply moving the projector to the end. A second
exact two-site check shows that a site-local rational spin rotation of a
singlet creates up-up and down-down terms, outside the opposite-spin-only F
source family. This rules out those simple substitutions, not teleportation,
ancilla, symmetry-preserving, or other encodings.

The full missing gate remains an explicit polynomial-size map from a
postselected charge circuit to one rational opposite-spin matrix F' and a
terminal hard projection/prefix, preserving the needed branch probability
or conditional law and polynomial bit costs. No PP-collapse, #P-hardness,
FPRAS, or approximate-sampling-hardness claim is established for the target.
