# Cycle 2: passivity barrier for hidden-state adaptive materials

## Result

The no-pumping statement can be made broader than the equilibrium-Hessian argument from cycle 1. For a complete passive material system coupled to one heat bath at temperature $T$, every cyclic protocol that returns the full material state obeys

$$W_{\mathrm{on}}=T\Sigma\ge 0,$$

where $W_{\mathrm{on}}$ is net work supplied to the material through all work ports and $\Sigma$ is total entropy production. Equivalently, the material's net work output is nonpositive. This result does not assume linear elasticity, a unique equilibrium branch, fixed contact topology, or absence of hysteresis. Contact switching, friction, viscoelasticity, and phase hysteresis can dissipate work; they cannot be a repeatable source in a closed full-state cycle. A finite metastable or chemical store can produce a one-time output, bounded by its decrease in available free energy.

The 2023 Shaat–Park paper does not evade this theorem by its contact mechanism. Its equations explicitly introduce a non-potential constitutive work term while calling every deformation leg reversible and source-free. More concretely, the simulated work-producing path changes the rigid-circle profile twice using a slider actuator (printed pp. 16–17 and Appendix A.1, p. 23). The paper says the slider needs an actuator but excludes its work from the ledger on the ground that the material stress is unchanged during switching. That zero-work assertion is a testable premise, not a consequence of the reported circle-center stress–strain accumulator. The precise escape is to drop at least one of the paper's conditions: count nonzero work at the profile-control port; identify a nonequilibrium resource; show that the complete state is not restored; or treat switching as irreversible and account for its dissipation. The paper acknowledges “static thermodynamic non-equilibrium” but gives no energy/exergy port for it.

This remains a model-level audit of a published finite-element claim. I have not received or rerun its raw Abaqus files, and no specimen was tested.

## No-pumping theorem

Take the system boundary to contain the complete material and every internal degree of freedom: elastic deformation, spins, contact state, phase fraction, stored chemical fuel, and any hidden controller or contact-profile slider. Let it interact with one heat reservoir at fixed temperature $T$ and let $W_{\mathrm{on}}$ denote work supplied to it, positive inward. Include mechanical forces and torques, electrical and magnetic work, chemical work, and every boundary or geometry-control actuator in that port sum. There is no other free-energy or exergy supply. The system is taken through an arbitrary protocol and ends in the same full state (or the same full-state distribution) as it began.

For a stochastic full state, take $S$ and $F=U-TS$ to be the entropy and nonequilibrium Helmholtz free energy of its complete distribution, not just its mean configuration. The first law and Clausius inequality are

$$\Delta U=Q+W_{\mathrm{on}},\qquad \Delta S=Q/T+\Sigma,\quad \Sigma\ge0,$$

with $Q$ heat into the system. Subtracting $T\Delta S$ from $\Delta U$ gives

$$\Delta F=\Delta U-T\Delta S=W_{\mathrm{on}}-T\Sigma.$$

For a closed full-state cycle, $\Delta F=0$, so $W_{\mathrm{on}}=T\Sigma\ge0$. Thus $W_{\mathrm{out}}=-W_{\mathrm{on}}\le0$. The reversible limit has $\Sigma=0$ and exactly zero net work. This proof is independent of the constitutive law and any hidden-coordinate elimination.

For a noncyclic run starting from a finite metastable or chemically charged state, the same derivation gives

$$W_{\mathrm{out}}\le F_{\mathrm{initial}}-F_{\mathrm{final}}.$$

This is the finite-store bound: one-shot mechanical output must be paid for by the decrease in complete-system free energy. If a device delivers $w>0$ per cycle for $N$ cycles without external exergy, its initial availability must satisfy $B\ge Nw$; if the full state is restored each cycle, the right-hand side is zero and repeatable positive output is impossible.

For several thermal reservoirs at distinct temperatures, the single-bath premise fails. A heat engine can produce work by consuming thermal exergy. With ambient reference temperature $T_0$, the available heat-work contribution is the exergy $\sum_i Q_i(1-T_0/T_i)$, with signs for heat entering the device. Chemical-potential gradients, a magnetic-field supply, a powered motor, periodic boundary actuation, or a driven controller are likewise resources. They must be counted; this theorem does not exclude devices powered by them.

## Hidden equilibrium coordinates and unilateral contact

If a quasistatic material state minimizes a scalar potential $U(x,y)$ over internal coordinates $y$, then $F(x)=\min_y U(x,y)$ is a scalar value function. Wherever differentiable, the effective load is $\nabla F$ and its tangent is symmetric. This is the equilibrium subcase of the no-pumping theorem.

Ideal frictionless unilateral contact does not add an energy source. With gap $g(q)\ge0$ and compressive multiplier $\lambda\ge0$, complementarity gives $\lambda g=0$. While contact is active, $g=0$ and quasistatic admissible motion has $\dot g=0$, so contact power $\lambda\dot g=0$; while open, $\lambda=0$. A conservative elastic penalty/contact potential gives the same conclusion, including contact-set changes. If the contact transition snaps or has hysteresis, the process is not reversible; the associated energy is dissipated or supplied by a change in hidden state. Neither case yields a repeatable source-free output.

The theorem also covers passive shape-memory and phase-change structures. A cycle that restores the full phase and internal state has nonnegative work input at one bath temperature. If a one-way transformation changes phase fraction, residual stress, or chemical state and delivers work, that is a finite-store discharge; the reset step must be included in the complete cycle. A PCM operated between two temperature reservoirs is a heat engine/thermal device and must be charged for heat exergy.

## Full energy-equation audit of Shaat–Park (2023)

Primary source: Shaat and Park, “Chiral nonreciprocal elasticity and mechanical activity,” *Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids* 171 (2023) 105163, [full author PDF](https://people.bu.edu/parkhs/Papers/shaatJMPS2023.pdf).

1. **Their reversible balance.** Section 4.1.1 starts from $dU=\delta Q+\delta W$ and the reversible/irreversible entropy inequality, then declares for isothermal elastic deformation that $\delta W=d\psi$ with $\psi=U-TS$ (PDF p. 13, lines 1491–1510). They say the model contains no energy source and always satisfies this balance during reversible interactions.
2. **Their continuum power.** Eq. (32) identifies the internal-energy variation as $\delta w=\tau_{ji}\,\delta u_{i,j}+\widehat M\,\delta\theta_z$, including spin work. Eq. (33) splits it into a potential part $\delta w_p$ and a chiral-nonreciprocal term $\delta w_{cn}$ (p. 13, lines 1548–1639).
3. **The decisive declaration.** Immediately after Eq. (33), they state $\delta w_p$ can be obtained from a quadratic free-energy function, whereas $\delta w_{cn}$ “cannot be expressed as the variation of potential energy” (p. 14, lines 1642–1646). Eq. (34) integrates that non-exact term around deformation space and Eq. (35) treats the resulting nonzero contour integral as work extracted (pp. 14–15, lines 1647–1709).
4. **Why the derivation fails as a passive reversible cycle.** If $\delta W=d\psi$ holds on every leg, its integral on a closed full-state cycle is zero. If the $\delta w_{cn}$ term is real and does net work, then that full work differential is not $d\psi$; the steps are not the reversible equilibrium processes assumed in Eq. (31), or an internal free-energy/exergy port is omitted. Calling the elements passive does not supply the missing term. Their own introduction describes the proposed constitutive response as “static thermodynamic non-equilibrium” (p. 12, lines 1487–1490), which is precisely outside equilibrium passivity.
5. **Finite-element ledger.** Appendix A says the only nonzero Abaqus energy terms are internal energy and external work, with their per-process residual approximately zero (pp. 23–24). It then says endpoint stresses and all displacements are zero and $\oint d\psi=0$, yet asserts $\oint\delta W\ne0$ (p. 24). Their cumulative work is separately computed from stress/strain increments over 40,000 increments (pp. 24–25). This is not a complete energy reconciliation: each deformation process is simulated separately, and the circle-profile/slider switches are omitted from that per-process check. If the integrated external-work port is the same $\delta W$ in Eq. (31), the claims are inconsistent; if not, the omitted conjugate port or differing work definition must be identified.
6. **The omitted geometry-control port.** Their positive-work ABAQUS paths change the circle profile at States 3 and 4 to set partial and then full/no contact (Appendix A.1 and Fig. A.6, printed p. 23). The text explicitly says this is practically done by a slider that “would require a mechanism actuating it,” but asserts it contributes no work because it moves rigidly and leaves the material stress state unchanged. The reported cycles yield $+16.6$ and $-16$ kJ/m$^3$ in two orientations (p. 17). The ABAQUS energy check is reported for each of the four deformation processes separately; the stress–strain accumulator in Eq. (A.4) sums circle-center forces/moments against material displacements/spins, and includes no slider force–displacement term. The slider's work may in principle be zero, but that must be established from its conjugate generalized force and displacement (and any reset/control energy), not inferred from unchanged stress alone. A nonzero value is an external work port; an irreversible switch is a dissipative leg. The exact physical falsifier therefore meters both the specimen ports and the profile-slider port through the full profile-and-deformation cycle.
7. **The contact caveat.** Their proposed mechanism specifically changes ligament/circle contact during a cycle (PDF pp. 15–17), while stating that normal contact has no tangential/dissipative friction (pp. 6–7). If contact-branch switching is reversible and the complete state including slider returns, the no-pumping result applies. If switching is metastable or dissipative, Eq. (31)'s reversible equality is invalid on that transition, and the contact-state free-energy change and dissipation must be reported.

The precise invalidating assumption is therefore not “hidden coordinates might exist”; hidden coordinates are covered by the theorem. The source describes a concrete additional port, the actuated slider, but omits its generalized work from the measured and simulated ledger. Its asserted zero contribution remains unverified. The other logically possible exits are: (i) the non-exact $\delta w_{cn}$ law represents unmodeled nonequilibrium forcing; (ii) the claimed loop is not closed in the full internal state; or (iii) a leg is irreversible/hysteretic, so the paper cannot apply its reversible Eq. (31). These possibilities are distinct: the existence of a slider establishes a port that must be measured, but does not by itself prove that its net work is nonzero.

## Measurement, falsifier, and resource ledger

### Raw-data falsifier

The decisive reanalysis needs the original Abaqus input deck and output database, not only plotted curves. Extract at each increment:

- every prescribed and free boundary translation and rotation, reaction force and reaction couple;
- the profile slider's position, actuator force/reaction, switching trajectory, hold/reset work, and the work of any mechanism that changes contact geometry;
- contact active-set, normal gap/traction, any friction/sliding variables, and contact work;
- all circle spins and ligament displacement/strain fields, plus any persistent state variables;
- Abaqus external work, elastic/internal energy, kinetic energy, artificial/contact energy, and numerical energy residuals;
- the authors' computed stress–strain accumulator and the exact state used for its 40,000 increments.

Recompute the signed work using all conjugate ports,

$$W_{\mathrm{on}}=\oint_{\partial\Omega}(\mathbf t\cdot d\mathbf u+\mathbf m\cdot d\boldsymbol\theta)+W_{\mathrm{other\ ports}},$$

and add $W_{\mathrm{slider}}=\int F_s\,ds$ (with the sign convention that positive work enters the complete device), then compare total input with $\Delta U$, contact/viscous dissipation, and any stored-state change. Run clockwise and counterclockwise loops, then repeat each loop for many cycles with the slider returned to its initial profile and a full internal-state reset. The source-free passive prediction is $W_{\mathrm{on}}\ge0$ per cycle; reversible fully equilibrated contact predicts zero. A first-cycle output that decays as contact geometry, elastic energy, or spin state changes is a finite-state release, not a repeatable pump. If the slider port is experimentally zero within a certified error bound and the full state is cyclic, a positive material-port output would directly falsify at least one remaining premise of the model or of the measurements.

The original Abaqus .odb, raw force/moment histories, model files, and exact energy tables were not present in the available workspace or source pages. Therefore no raw replay was performed and the paper's plotted work remains source-reported evidence.

### Physical test and equally informed controls

Use one two-mode displacement/rotation test frame and a specimen with the paper's chiral ligament/circle geometry. Run (a) its claimed changing-contact path, (b) a same-specimen path that keeps contact state fixed, (c) a mirror-chiral specimen, and (d) an achiral straight-ligament control matched for mass and small-signal stiffness. Include the work input to reset any metastable state. A separate driven-piezo or periodically driven-gear specimen is a positive control for a real powered nonconservative response; meter its power supply or drive torque.

Measure load and torque at every material boundary, full-field internal displacement and rotation, contact state, specimen temperature, and all thermal/electrical/chemical inputs. For a single-bath passivity test, keep the specimen and all thermal boundaries at the same controlled temperature. If the device uses different temperature reservoirs, report their heat flows and temperatures and compare output with thermal exergy instead of calling the device source-free.

For sampled generalized coordinates $q_j$ and conjugate forces/couples $p_j$, compute $\widehat W_{on}=\sum_j p_j\cdot(q_{j+1}-q_j)$. With force error at most $\epsilon_p$, path length $L$, maximum coordinate step $h$, and a measured Lipschitz bound $M_p$ for force along the path, the left-rule quadrature error is at most $\epsilon_pL+M_phL/2$ for exact coordinates; add coordinate, torque, synchronization, temperature and supply-meter errors. The confidence interval must exclude zero and be smaller than the asserted work to establish its sign.

### All relevant costs

- **Mechanical:** two independent generalized actuators; force/torque sensors on every boundary port; a load cell and position/phase sensor on the profile slider; calibration of actuator compliance; work to move the specimen through each path; slider hold/reset work; reset work for metastable contact/phase states.
- **Internal state:** synchronized optical/DIC or equivalent imaging of every circle, ligament, spin, contact gap and contact switch; specimen inspection for wear and permanent set across cycles.
- **Thermal/chemical/electrical:** heat-flow sensors and reservoir thermometry; voltage/current and coil-field energy meters; chemical-fuel concentration/chemical-potential accounting; temperature-control energy. Convert non-work supplies to exergy when comparing with mechanical output.
- **Inference:** sampling rate adequate to resolve contact transitions; sensor phase-delay calibration; quadrature and calibration uncertainty; repeated cycles to test drift and determine whether output decays.
- **Device accounting:** specimen mass/volume, cycle time, net work delivered to a load, losses, reset energy, external drive energy and energy per useful cycle. The controller and reset process belong in a device-level efficiency claim.

## Scope and next gate

This is a theorem about passivity and a source-level contradiction, not a proof that every passive-looking device is at equilibrium. It allows powered odd-elastic beams, externally driven gears, chemically fueled active matter, multibath heat engines, and transient work from metastable free-energy release, provided those resources are included. For examples, the experimental active odd-micropolar beam uses piezoelectric elements and electronic feed-forward control ([Chen et al., 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26034-z)); the odd-mass metamaterial uses powered sensing/actuation circuits ([Wu et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2209829120)). Passive odd-viscoelastic fluids can exhibit odd response, while the cited analysis distinguishes linear viscoelastic chiral solids as requiring activity ([Lier et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.105.054607)); that fluid response is not a counterexample to the full-state cyclic work inequality.

The next decisive gate is source-data access to reconcile Eq. (31), Eqs. (32)–(35), and the ABAQUS boundary-work ledger including the slider actuator. If work output survives all-port integration over repeated full-state cycles with the slider work measured as zero, then either the model violates the single-bath passivity theorem or an unmeasured resource, state change, or irreversibility remains. If the slider supplies positive work, the material's apparent output is powered by an external geometry-control port. In either event, the existing paper does not yet establish a source-free passive work source.
