## Theorem for the scale measure actually used

Let `A(Y) >= 0` be a measurable `d x d` matrix, with the moments below finite.
Let `f:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity)` be measurable with `f(0)=0`, and define

    Z_T | Y ~ N(0, I + f(T A(Y))).

For any `r>1`, put

    J_r(f) = integral_0^infinity f(u)^2 du/u^r.

If `J_r(f)<infinity` and the fixed-scale second moments are finite for almost
every `T` (automatic for bounded `f`), then

    integral_0^infinity I(Y;Z_T) dT/T^r
       <= J_r(f)/4 * E Tr[A(Y)^(r-1)].                 (1)

The fixed-scale entropy calculation is the one in INITIAL: Gaussian maximum
entropy gives `I(Y;Z_T) <= 1/4 E Tr f(TA)^2`. Diagonalizing each realization
of `A`, for every eigenvalue `a>0` substitute `u=Ta`:

    integral_0^infinity f(Ta)^2 dT/T^r
       = a^(r-1) J_r(f).

Zero eigenvalues contribute zero because `f(0)=0`; for almost every `T`, the
fixed-scale entropy comparison applies. Tonelli gives (1). Thus the power in
the posterior spectral moment is dictated by the scale measure. The
linear trace transfer in INITIAL is exactly the `r=2` case, not a generic
property of every scale integral.

For the saturating profile `f_L(u)=L u/(L+u)`, direct beta integration gives

    J_r(f_L)=L^(3-r) B(3-r,r-1),       1<r<3.

At the endpoints `r=1` and `r=3` this integral diverges (at infinity and
zero, respectively). In particular, for the `dT/T^2` interface,
`J_2=L`, so

    integral I(Y;Z_T) dT/T^2 <= (L/4) E Tr A(Y).       (2)

This is finite but carries the cap linearly. For a posterior covariance
`C_Y=Cov(X|Y)`, total covariance gives `E C_Y <= Sigma=Cov(X)`, and hence

    (1/d) integral I dT/T^2 <= (L/4) Tr(Sigma)/d
                                      <= (L/4)||Sigma||op.

For a fixed observation map `B`, `A=B C_Y B*` changes the trace bound to
`(L/4)||Sigma||op Tr(B*B)`. These are explicit costs; none is uniform in L
unless L is fixed or an additional profile/field argument removes the cap.

For general posterior spectral powers `p=r-1`, if `0<p<=1`, operator
concavity and monotonicity of `x^p` give
`E Tr(C_Y^p) <= Tr((E C_Y)^p) <= Tr(Sigma^p)`. If `p>1` and the posterior
field has the additional deterministic bound `C_Y<=M I`, then
`E Tr(C_Y^p) <= M^(p-1) Tr(Sigma)`. Without this extra bound, covariance
alone controls only the first moment, not the higher spectral moment.

## Gaussian heat and posterior-entropy identities

Now let `X` be square-integrable in `R^d`, `Y_s=X+sqrt(s)G`, with standard
Gaussian `G`, and `C_s(Y_s)=Cov(X|Y_s)`. Define

    M(s) := integral_0^infinity I(Y_s;Z_s,T) dT/T^2,
    Z_s,T | Y_s ~ N(0, I + f(T C_s(Y_s))).

For fixed `s>0`, scale the observed
variable by `1/sqrt(s)` and set `gamma=1/s`; then `Y_s` is equivalent to the
unit-noise channel `sqrt(gamma)X+G`. Write

    I_X(gamma)=I(X; sqrt(gamma)X+G).

The vector I-MMSE identity is

    I_X'(gamma)=1/2 E Tr C_gamma.

Consequently, if the field in the conditional `Z_T` channel is exactly
`C_gamma` (or `B C_gamma B*` for the corresponding observed channel),

    M(gamma) := integral I(Y_gamma;Z_gamma,T) dT/T^2
           <= (J_2(f)/2) I_X'(gamma).                 (3)

Integrating from `0` to `Gamma` and using Gaussian maximum entropy for the
additive observation yields

    integral_0^Gamma M(gamma) dgamma
      <= J_2(f)/2 I(X;sqrt(Gamma) B X+G)
      <= J_2(f)/4 log det(I+Gamma B Sigma B*).         (4)

The full singular spectrum of `B` is charged by the log-determinant. For
`B=I`, division by `d` gives at most `J_2(f)/4 log(1+Gamma||Sigma||op)`.
This is a cumulative SNR-flow bound, not a pointwise archive or KLS result.

There is also an exact heat-flow form. Let `mathcal J(Y_s)` denote the
ordinary Fisher information of the density of `Y_s`. De Bruijn's identity
and I-MMSE in noise-variance coordinates give

    E Tr C_s = d s - s^2 mathcal J(Y_s).               (5)

Indeed, `d/ds I(X;Y_s)=mathcal J(Y_s)/2-d/(2s)` by de Bruijn, while
`d/ds I(X;Y_s)=-E Tr C_s/(2s^2)` by I-MMSE; equating proves (5). Therefore,
when (2)'s field really is this posterior covariance,

    M(s) <= (J_2(f)/4) min{Tr(Sigma), d s-s^2 mathcal J(Y_s)}.  (6)

Equation (5) is exact for `s>0` under the standard finite-moment/smoothed-
density hypotheses. It makes explicit which entropy/Fisher flow a posterior
covariance identification would import.
