A. RATIONAL-COMPLEX TO FINITE-FIELD TRANSFER FOR THE SUPPORT OPTIMUM

The reduction in INITIAL converts maximum support weight into maximum weight of a common feasible set of two projected linear delta-matroids on N=2n elements. Here is the missing finite-field/bit-cost argument, conditional only on the cited Koana–Wahlström weighted-intersection theorem and its high-probability search routine.

Input and determinant bound. Let L be the binary encoding length of rational real and imaginary parts of F, with positive denominators. Let D be the product of all denominators and G=DF. Then D and every component of G have O(L) bits; in particular |G_ij|<2^(2L+1) is a safe bound. The same nonzero scalar D multiplies every k-by-k minor by D^k and so preserves its zero/nonzero status. For any nonzero k-by-k Gaussian-integer minor δ of G, k≤n,
  |δ| ≤ k! 2^((2L+1)k) ≤ n! 2^((2L+1)n),
so its Gaussian norm N(δ)=δ conjugate(δ) is a positive integer satisfying
  1 ≤ N(δ) < 2^B,
where B=1+2n ceil(log2(max{2,n}))+(4L+2)n. This is a numeric polynomial in n,L, not an exponentially large value in the input length. Fix one optimum common-feasible witness X* over Q(i); the determinant δ of its corresponding F[I,J] minor is nonzero, hence this bound applies. Although X* is unknown, the bound is uniform over every minor.

Polynomially many deterministic candidate primes. There are B+1 distinct odd primes among the integers ≤x=2(B+2)^4. One elementary bound suffices: for even x, binom(x,x/2)≥2^x/(x+1); the p-adic valuation of that binomial is at most floor(log_p x), so each prime contributes at most x and therefore π(x) log2 x ≥ x log2−log2(x+1). Writing M=B+2, use log2 x≤M², log2(x+1)≤2M², and log2≥1/2 to get π(x)≥M²−2≥B+2. Removing prime 2 leaves at least B+1 odd primes. A sieve to x takes polynomial bit time because x is polynomial in B. Their product is at least 3^(B+1)>2^B, so not all B+1 can divide N(δ). At least one candidate p avoids N(δ).

Finite fields of the required size. The cited weighted-intersection theorem needs common field cardinality Ω(N²). Choose for each candidate odd p an extension F_(p^d) with q=p^d≥N^3 and, when p≡3 mod4, choose d even. When p≡1 mod4, i maps to a square root of −1 already in F_p; when p≡3 mod4, even d ensures F_(p^d) contains F_(p²) and a root of X²+1. Since the least admissible d overshoots by at most two, q≤p²N^3 (or pN^3 in the split case), hence q is polynomial in n,L because p≤x=2(B+2)^4. Deterministic construction can enumerate monic degree-d polynomials over F_p, test irreducibility with Frobenius/gcd tests, and stop at the first irreducible; fewer than p^d=q candidates are inspected. This exhaustive construction is polynomial in the numeric input size because q itself is polynomial. Scan the resulting field for a root of X²+1 where needed. For sufficiently large N, q≥N³ meets the source's Ω(N²) threshold; the finitely many smaller N may be handled by direct enumeration. All primes are odd, so skew-symmetry has its usual nonsingular-principal-submatrix interpretation.

Correctness over the prime family. Embed Gaussian integers into the selected field via i↦rho with rho²=−1. If p does not divide N(δ), the image of the fixed optimum minor is nonzero: for p≡1 mod4 the norm is the product of the two split images, so neither can vanish; for p≡3 mod4 the norm is the field norm from F_(p²), so its residue is nonzero. Therefore X* stays feasible at some candidate prime and that prime's modular optimum is at least its Q(i) weight. Conversely, any modular common feasible X uses a principal determinant that is the image of a Gaussian-integer determinant of A[X]. If it is nonzero modulo p, that original determinant was nonzero; the D_0 projection gadget is constant and field-independent. Thus every modular witness lifts to a genuine Q(i) common feasible set and its weight cannot exceed the characteristic-zero optimum. Taking the largest verified witness across all primes gives exactly the Q(i) optimum whenever the weighted-intersection search succeeds at at least one good prime. This one-sided lift also prevents modular false-positive support.

Probability and running-time accounting. For a target failure probability η, amplify the source search routine by independent repetition on each prime; if one boosted run succeeds with probability at least 3/4, K=ceil(log_4((B+1)/η)) repetitions suffice to make the probability that all runs at the (unknown) good prime fail at most η. Verify every returned candidate over Q(i) and retain the highest-weight verified witness. The source's Corollary 6 search cost is O(W N^(ω+1)) field operations per run for maximum element weight W; total is O((B+1)K W N^(ω+1)) field operations. Field element bit lengths, deterministic extension construction, denominator clearing, and exact Q(i) witness verification are polynomial in n,L, so the combined bit cost is polynomial in n,L,W,K. Hence this is polynomial bit time for unweighted maximum support (W=1) and pseudo-polynomial in numeric W for integer-weight maximum support. A useful forced-prefix corollary follows: delete excluded sites, and give each forced site weight M+w_i, each other allowed site weight w_i, where M>sum_{i not forced}w_i. If a support containing every forced site exists, its score beats every support missing one forced site; the max-weight witness therefore decides forced-prefix feasibility and optimizes the remaining support weight. This still decides only zero/nonzero support and never computes a positive f(U).

Caveats: the argument uses a fixed unknown optimal minor only for the uniform bit bound, not as input. The imported optimization algorithm is randomized; repeated runs address its probability, not the logical theorem's truth. The source cost is in field operations, converted here using polynomially bounded finite fields and standard exact representation. No source implementation was built in this cycle. Existing cycle-5 deterministic linear-matroid parity work is a separate route and is not invoked as a substitute for weighted delta-matroid intersection.

B. WHY THE NAIVE TWO-REPLICA MATCHGATE CONSTRUCTION FAILS LOCALLY

There is a natural state-sum reading of f(U): a BCS/fermionic Gaussian amplitude has terms det F[I,J]; multiply by its conjugate and sum over configurations with the same occupied sites, with no site doubly occupied, to obtain a sum of squared minors. In a two-replica contraction, the local tensor that identifies bra/ket configurations and enforces at-most-one orientation has four Boolean legs ordered (row in replica 1, column in replica 1, row in replica 2, column in replica 2). Its signature E is 1 on exactly
  0000, 1010, 0101
and 0 on the other 13 strings.

This is not a matchgate signature in the natural basis. Use the Cai–Gorenstein MGI (Theorem 2.1) at α=1000 and β=0111. The four summands, indexed p=1..4 with signs (−1)^p, are:
 p=1: E(0000)E(1111)=0;
 p=2: E(1100)E(0011)=0;
 p=3: −E(1010)E(0101)=−1;
 p=4: E(1001)E(0110)=0.
The required sum is −1, not 0. Thus the direct local-copy plus exclusion tensor is not a matchgate; a construction that simply doubles the BCS Gaussian state and contracts this local tensor cannot stay inside the usual planar matchgate signature class. This explains a local obstruction behind the whole-f example in INITIAL. It does not rule out a nonlocal encoding, arbitrary holographic basis change, larger non-Gaussian tensor family, or a different integral identity. It is a specific failed route, not a hardness theorem.

C. OUTWARD-SEARCH BOUNDARY

The Koana–Wahlström result is a tractable optimization theorem for (projected) linear delta-matroids, and it converts the support layer of this determinant aggregate into an optimization problem. It does not classify f as a Holant signature: Holant dichotomies classify problems generated from a fixed finite signature set (the cited recent complex result explicitly assumes a conjugate-closed signature set), whereas here the arity and entries vary with the input matrix. The two-replica obstruction shows why merely saying “squared Pfaffian amplitudes are fermionic” is insufficient: the equality/exclusion gadget itself is outside the matchgate class. Any stronger route needs a new encoding of this local tensor or a sum/trace construction beyond Gaussian signatures.

D. SMALLER REPHASING CLASS ALSO CANNOT REPAIR THE COPY TENSOR

The MGI failure of E survives wire permutations and invertible diagonal changes on individual legs. A diagonal leg change multiplies each signature coordinate Γ_S by a nonzero product of per-leg scale factors. The 4-bit even MGI is the signed sum over the three perfect pairings of the four coordinates, plus the vacuum/full product. E has zero full-occupancy coordinate and, among the six two-occupancy coordinates, exactly one complementary pair product is nonzero (the two occupied strings 1010 and 0101); the other two pairing products vanish. After any wire permutation these two complementary two-sets still form exactly one pairing. After diagonal rescaling that one product remains nonzero, while all the zero products remain zero. Therefore no wire reordering, bitwise phase, or diagonal gauge can make E a natural matchgate signature. This still leaves genuinely non-diagonal holographic changes and larger encodings open.
