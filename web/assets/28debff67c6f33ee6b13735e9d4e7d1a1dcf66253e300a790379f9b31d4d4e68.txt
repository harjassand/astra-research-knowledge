LOCAL-MATRIX PRECONDITIONING FOR CONDITIONAL GAUSSIAN HERALD SAMPLING
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: internally derived exact-real and finite-bit candidate; sibling audit found no fatal gap in the sampler derivation; fixtures pass; not externally validated

1. The proposed extension
=========================

The PC-18 certificate allows one scalar variance per optical mode. Here the
preconditioner keeps the complete rational 2 by 2 quadrature covariance of
each positive-count mode. Let

    B = direct_sum_(j in H_+) B_j,  B_j=B_j^T>0 rational,

and suppose a supplied rational certificate proves

    0<s_- <= s_+,       s_- B <= S <= s_+ B.                (LC)

This permits arbitrary local squeezing and q-p covariance in each herald
mode. The certificate only controls cross-mode correlations after whitening
each local block. The intended canonical choice is B_j=S_(j,j), the local
2 by 2 diagonal block of the tilted positive-count covariance S; this is
rational and acquired directly from S. The sampler does not cover arbitrary
strong standardized cross-mode correlation when (LC) has a large ratio.

Use the PC-16 interface: C is rational and strictly positive as a coherent-
quadrature P covariance, H is the herald set, H_+ its m positive-count
modes, kappa=sum_j k_j is charged numerically, U is the unheralded output,
and

    C°=(C^(-1)+P_H)^(-1),
    S=C°_(H_+,H_+), K=C°_(U,H_+)S^(-1),
    V=C°_(U,U)-KSK^T.

All matrices, B, s_-, s_+, and sandwich tests are rational. Zero-target
herald modes have already been integrated into C°. For m=0, use the PC-17
all-zero Gaussian branch and do no core rejection. Below assume m>0.

2. Exact target and a modewise anisotropic proposal
==================================================

For x_j in R^2 define

    W(x)=prod_j (||x_j||^2/2)^(k_j)/k_j!,
    pi(x)=phi_S(x)W(x)/Z_S.

Set Q=s_+B and

    Delta=S^(-1)-Q^(-1) >=0,
    q(x)=phi_Q(x)W(x)/Z_q.

The normalizer factors across modes. For a rational SPD block B_j, write
mu_(j,k)=E_(u uniform on S^1) (u^T B_j u)^k. Then

    Z_q=prod_j s_+^(k_j) mu_(j,k_j).

If tr_j=tr(B_j) and dsc_j=tr(B_j)^2-4 det(B_j)>=0, the angular moment is
the exact rational

    mu_(j,k) = sum_(r=0)^floor(k/2)
      binom(k,2r) (tr_j/2)^(k-2r) (dsc_j/4)^r binom(2r,r)/4^r.

Indeed, in an eigenbasis u^T B_j u=tr_j/2+sqrt(dsc_j) cos(2theta)/2;
odd cosine powers integrate to zero and E cos(2theta)^(2r)=binom(2r,r)/4^r.
Thus Z_q is computable by O(kappa+m) rational operations with bit length
polynomial in the binary input length and numeric kappa.

The ratio phi_S/phi_Q is a constant times exp(-x^T Delta x/2). Accepting
q with probability

    a(x)=exp(-x^T Delta x/2)

therefore gives pi exactly; no Z_S, determinant envelope, p_H, or herald
probability oracle is used by the accept coin.

3. Exact local angular sampler (no condition-number rejection)
=============================================================

To derive and sample q, write the underlying Gaussian coordinates as
X_j=sqrt(s_+)B_j^(1/2)Z_j with base Z_j~N(0,I_2), then use polar form
Z_j=sqrt(2G_j)u_j. The radial and angular distributions under the
polynomially weighted proposal factor. Under q, G_j has Gamma(k_j+1,
scale 1), independently of u_j. In an
orthonormal eigenbasis of B_j, let lambda_+>=lambda_->0 and
Delta_j=lambda_+-lambda_-. For a uniform base direction, T=cos^2(theta)
has Beta(1/2,1/2) law. The q angular density is proportional to
(lambda_-+Delta_j T)^(k_j). Expand its nonnegative polynomial:

    w_(j,l)=binom(k_j,l) lambda_-^(k_j-l) Delta_j^l c_l,
    c_l=binom(2l,l)/4^l,       l=0,...,k_j.

Its sum is mu_(j,k_j). Sample l with probabilities w_(j,l)/mu_(j,k_j),
then sample

    T ~ Beta(l+1/2,1/2),
    u_eig=(sigma_1 sqrt(T), sigma_2 sqrt(1-T)),

where sigma_1,sigma_2 are independent fair signs. Rotate u_eig by an
orthogonal eigenbasis Q_j of B_j. Explicitly, for
B_j=[[a,c],[c,d]], set g=sqrt((a-d)^2+4c^2) and
lambda_+=(a+d+g)/2, lambda_-=(a+d-g)/2. If g=0 use the identity basis. If
c=0, use the coordinate basis ordered by the larger diagonal entry. If
c!=0, normalize (2c,d-a+g) as the lambda_+ eigenvector and use its
90-degree rotation as the lambda_- eigenvector. The beta density is the arcsine density
multiplied by T^l and renormalized, so this mixture is exactly the desired
angular density. The radial and angular parts factor, proving the exact q
sampler.

Every Beta draw has a direct Gamma construction:

    T=A/(A+D),
    A=sum_(r=1)^l E_r + Z_1^2/2 ~ Gamma(l+1/2,1),
    D=Z_2^2/2 ~ Gamma(1/2,1),

with independent unit exponentials E_r and standard normals Z_1,Z_2. The
denominator A+D is Gamma(l+1,1), so for rational 0<zeta<1,

    P(A+D<zeta) <= zeta^(l+1)/(l+1)! <= zeta.

This is the only ratio-conditioning event in the finite-precision Beta
coupling; it will be charged explicitly below.

All mixture probabilities are algebraic numbers of degree at most two. For
finite-bit sampling, evaluate their intervals using the quadratic eigenvalue
formula. A positive rational denominator bound is

    mu_(j,k) >= lambda_min(B_j)^k
                 >= (det B_j / tr B_j)^k.

The last expression has O(k L) bits when B_j has input length L. Hence
normalized mixture probabilities can be approximated to total variation
epsilon_mix with O(kL+log(k/epsilon_mix)) interval bits; the dyadic
categorical quantile adds at most epsilon_mix. No rejection probability of
order (lambda_min/lambda_max)^k appears.

4. Global acceptance and its correlation dependence
===================================================

Let rho=s_+/s_-. Whiten the positive core by

    Y=B^(-1/2)X,  S'=B^(-1/2) S B^(-1/2).

The exact certificate implies eigenvalues(S') in [s_-,s_+] and

    Delta'=B^(1/2) Delta B^(1/2)=S'^(-1)-s_+^(-1)I >=0,
    ||Delta'|| <= s_-^(-1)-s_+^(-1).

Under q, each block Y_j=sqrt(s_+) Z_j has
E||Y_j||^2=2s_+(k_j+1), regardless of B_j. Thus, with K0=m+kappa,

    alpha=E_q a(X)
      >= exp[-s_+ K0(s_-^(-1)-s_+^(-1))]
       = exp[-K0(rho-1)].

The exact-real expected number of global proposals is at most
exp[K0(rho-1)]. For

    Lambda=2^ceil(2K0(rho-1)),

we have alpha>=1/Lambda since ln(2)>1/2. The global rejection exponent
depends on standardized cross-mode covariance, not the condition number of
any individual 2 by 2 block. Local anisotropy only changes polynomial-time
quadratic-algebraic arithmetic and the numerical core/output caps. In the
special case B_j=b_j I_2, the angular law is uniform and the theorem reduces
to PC-18 exactly.

5. Finite-bit coupling in whitened coordinates
================================================

The original-coordinate cap in the first draft used ||K|| times a cap for
||X||. It is valid, but can be exponentially loose when local squeezing
makes K large while K B^(1/2) stays bounded. The following implementation
replaces that cap and is the finite-bit statement used in the revised
Section 8.

Let

    Y=B^(-1/2)X,  G=B^(1/2) Delta B^(1/2),
    A=K B^(1/2),  L L^T=V.

Under the proposal q, the exact local construction is

    Y_j=sqrt(2s_+ G_j) u_j,
    G_j~Gamma(k_j+1,1),

where u_j has the positive Beta-mixture law of Section 3. The target
acceptance is exp(-Y^T G Y/2). The retained amplitude is A Y+LZ for
Z~N(0,I_r), r=2|U|. The matrices G and A may be algebraic, but their
entries have certified rational intervals computed from B^(1/2), Delta,
and K. No inverse-covariance oracle is used.

Define K0=m+kappa, rho=s_+/s_-, and

    Lambda=2^ceil(2K0(rho-1)),
    J=ceil(log_2(2048 Lambda/delta)),
    T_Z=8(K0+J),
    Ymax=ceil(sqrt(s_+ T_Z)),
    Lbar_a=1+1/s_-,
    A_*>=||A||_2,
    tau=delta/[512 Lambda(1+Lbar_a+A_*)].

Here A_* is a certified rational upper bound computed from algebraic
intervals for A. Enclose every entry so the interval-centre matrix A_c has
||A-A_c||_F<=1/2. Use integer-square-root intervals to compute a rational
U_c with ||A_c||_F<=U_c<||A_c||_F+1/2, and set A_*=U_c+1. Then
||A||_2<=A_*<=||A||_F+2. This does not use the generally much larger
bound ||K|| sqrt(||B||); for the family in Section 8, A=I/3 and A_*=2
is a valid fixed bound. Compute the output LDL factor L to a certified
Frobenius error gamma_L as below. The Gamma tail bound gives

    q(||Y||^2>s_+T_Z)
      <=2^K0 exp(-T_Z/4) <=delta/(2048 Lambda).

At the end of finite core generation, radially project the rational vector
onto the Euclidean ball of radius Ymax. On the good event the exact vector
lies in that ball, so exact projection is nonexpansive. Approximate the
projection to Euclidean error tau/512. If this rational approximation lies
outside the ball, use square-root intervals to choose a rational inward
scale whose relative error is at most tau/[512(Ymax+1)]; this certifies
that the final vector has norm at most Ymax. The projection approximation,
inward scaling, and its last rational rounding together add less than
tau/64 to the good-event coupling error. The projection is always applied,
so the deterministic output cap holds on bad events too.

Here is a bounded-bit proposal coupling. Put r_c=2m and choose

    delta_u=tau/[64(Ymax+1)],  delta_T=delta_u^2/32,
    zeta_B=delta/[4096 Lambda m],
    N_E=m+2kappa, zeta_E=delta/[4096 Lambda N_E],
    eta_E=min(tau^2/[128s_+K0], zeta_B delta_T/[64(kappa+1)]).

For exponentials use a dyadic-uniform mesh h_E<=min(zeta_E/2,
 zeta_E eta_E/4); approximate -log of each midpoint within eta_E/2 and
clamp the result to [0,infinity). Except when an exact uniform is below
zeta_E, the midpoint and exact uniform are both at least zeta_E/2, so the
exponential error is at most eta_E. The total such bad-uniform probability
is at most delta/(4096Lambda). The radial errors satisfy

    sum_j |sqrt(2s_+G_j)-sqrt(2s_+Ghat_j)|^2
       <=2s_+K0 eta_E <=tau^2/64.

Approximate each radius sqrt(2s_+G_j) to absolute error
 tau/[64 ceil(sqrt(m))], adding at most tau/64 in Euclidean norm.

For the Beta mixture use the exact nonnegative weights of Section 3,
interval-normalize them using
mu_(j,k)>=(det B_j/tr B_j)^k, and approximate each categorical law in TV
to epsilon_mix=delta/(4096Lambda m). This takes
ceil(log_2(4(k_j+1)/epsilon_mix)) random bits per mode and
O(k_j L+log(k_j/epsilon_mix)) interval bits. For a Beta draw use
T=A_0/(A_0+D_0), where A_0 is the sum of at most k_j exponentials and one
half-square normal and D_0 is an independent half-square normal. Set

    eta_Z=zeta_B delta_T/[256(R_N+1)],
    J_N=ceil(log_2(8192mLambda/delta)), R_N=2J_N,
    eta_CDF<=min(delta/[32768mLambda],phi_lo eta_Z/8),
    phi_lo=2^(-(R_N+1)^2-2),
    h_Z<=min(phi_lo eta_Z/8,eta_CDF/8),

and use the following polynomial-bit inverse-normal routine with
coordinate error eta_Z on |Z|<=R_N. Put R=R_N+1 and X_N=R^2/2. The
Taylor polynomial for exp(-x^2/2) has degree
N_CDF=ceil(6X_N+2log_2(4/eta_exp)), where
eta_exp=eta_CDF/[8(R+1)]; its integrated remainder is bounded by eta_CDF/4
on [-R,R]. Integrate it termwise as a rational polynomial. Enclose pi by
Machin's identity pi=16 atan(1/5)-4 atan(1/239) with alternating-series
remainders, and enclose (2pi)^(-1/2) by rational square-root intervals
with width at most eta_CDF/[8(R+1)], so the resulting CDF interval has
width at most eta_CDF. On [-R,R] the normal
density is at least phi_lo, so inverse-CDF bisection takes
O(log(R/eta_Z)) steps. If a target falls in an endpoint CDF interval,
return that endpoint and charge the ambiguity; over 2m coordinates its
probability is at most 4m eta_CDF<delta/(2048Lambda). Otherwise bisection
brackets the true inverse and returns a point within eta_Z. The degree,
precision and bisection count are polynomial in R_N^2+log(1/eta_Z). This
couples a continuous normal with the finite algorithm's output; it is not a
TV comparison of a continuous Gaussian with a grid distribution.

The Beta denominator A_0+D_0 has Gamma(l+1,1) law, so
P(A_0+D_0<zeta_B)<=zeta_B. Across m modes the probability is at most
m zeta_B. On the complement, the numerator and denominator errors are at
most kappa eta_E+(R_N+1)eta_Z and
kappa eta_E+2(R_N+1)eta_Z, respectively. The denominator error is less
than 3zeta_B delta_T/128, so the computed denominator is at least
zeta_B/2. Since both exact and computed numerator lie between zero
and their corresponding denominators,

    |T-T_hat| <= zeta_B^(-1)[2kappa eta_E+3(R_N+1)eta_Z] < delta_T.

A denominator below zeta_B/2 triggers T_hat=1/2; this fallback is not used
on the good event. The map T -> (sqrt(T),sqrt(1-T)) changes by at most
sqrt(2|T-T_hat|). Approximate those square roots to delta_u/16 each and
the algebraic orthogonal eigenbasis to operator error delta_u/4. The
direction error per mode is below delta_u; its aggregate effect in Y is at
most Ymax delta_u<=tau/64. Rationally round the final core coordinates so
the aggregate rounding error is at most tau/64, then perform the inward
rounded radial projection above, whose total contribution is below tau/64.
The total radial, directional and rounding terms give

    P(||Y-Yhat||>tau/4) <= beta,  beta<=delta/(128Lambda).

All uniform grids, algebraic intervals and square-root intervals have bit
length polynomial in the rational input length L, numeric kappa, log Lambda,
log(delta^(-1)), log(Ymax+1), and the exact certificate/factorization bit
lengths. Near-isotropic eigenbasis branches use the identity; any nonzero
rational gap has magnitude at least 2^(-O(L)), so the eigenbasis precision
is polynomial in L even for nearly degenerate local blocks.

The transformed precision G is PSD with ||G||<=s_-^(-1)-s_+^(-1)
<=s_-^(-1). Therefore the acceptance function has global Lipschitz
constant at most sqrt(||G||/e)<=Lbar_a. Approximate every entry of G by a
rational interval of width at most

delta/[4096 Lambda r_c (Ymax+1)^2],

and evaluate qhat=max(0,Yhat^T Ghat Yhat/2). The resulting quadratic
error is below delta/(8192Lambda). Approximate exp(-qhat) in [0,1] to
additive delta/(4096Lambda) and use a dyadic acceptance uniform with mesh
at most delta/(4096Lambda). Combining the proposal coupling, Lipschitz
error, coefficient/exponential error and coin error, the one-trial
acceptance indicators disagree with probability

epsilon_try <= beta+Lbar_a tau+delta/(1024Lambda)
             < delta/(64Lambda).

Since the exact success probability is at least 1/Lambda, the finite one is
at least 1/(2Lambda). The normalized-acceptance coupling lemma of Section 3 above then couples the exact and finite accepted cores within
tau except with probability less than delta/16. The finite uncapped mean
proposal count is at most 2Lambda (the exact-real mean is at most Lambda).
Cap the loop at

    N=ceil(4Lambda ceil(log_2(2/xi)))

trials. Its timeout probability is at most xi/2.

For the output Gaussian, use an exact rational LDL factorization
V=R diag(v_i) R^T with positive rational pivots and set L=R diag(sqrt(v_i)).
Approximate by a rational Lhat with ||L-Lhat||_F<=gamma_L, where

    Jz=ceil(log_2(512r/delta)), Rz=2Jz, cr=ceil(sqrt(r)),
    L_*=ceil(sqrt(tr V)),
    gamma_L=delta/[512(1+cr Rz)],
    gamma_Z=delta/[512(1+L_*+gamma_L)],
    gamma_A=delta/[512(Ymax+1)].

Rational LDL and absolute-precision square roots take polynomial bit time;
a tiny pivot is not inverted or resolved to relative precision. Approximate
A by a rational Ahat with Frobenius error at most gamma_A. For output normals
use the same inverse-CDF routine with coordinate target gamma_Z/(8cr),
truncation Rz, R=Rz+1, phi_lo=2^(-R^2-2),
eta_CDF<=min(delta/(4096r),phi_lo gamma_Z/(8cr)),
eta_exp=eta_CDF/[8(R+1)], and uniform mesh
h<=min(phi_lo gamma_Z/(8cr),eta_CDF/8). The Taylor/Machin interval
calculation and bisection give a vector Zhat coupled to Z with
||Z-Zhat||<=gamma_Z, except on Gaussian-tail and endpoint-ambiguity events
of total probability below delta/128. The Gaussian tail satisfies
P(max_i|Z_i|>Rz)<=2r exp(-Rz^2/2)<=delta/256. Clip Zhat coordinatewise to
[-Rz-1,Rz+1].

On the good core/normal events,

    ||(A Y+LZ)-(Ahat Yhat+Lhat Zhat)||
       <= A_* tau+gamma_A Ymax+gamma_L cr Rz
                              +(L_*+gamma_L)gamma_Z
       < delta/128.

For a Poisson intensity vector with mode amplitudes u_j, the product
Poisson affinity for u and v is

    exp(-1/2 sum_j (||u_j||/sqrt(2)-||v_j||/sqrt(2))^2).

The reverse triangle inequality and Fuchs--van de Graaf imply
TV(Pois_u,Pois_v)<=||u-v||/sqrt(2). Thus the amplitude coupling transfers
to the final count record without asserting TV closeness of continuous and
discrete Gaussian measures.

6. Output-rate cap and finite Poisson sampling
==============================================

The finite core and normal satisfy ||Yhat||<=Ymax and
||Zhat||<=cr(Rz+1), while ||Ahat||<=A_*+gamma_A and
||Lhat||_2<=L_*+gamma_L. Hence every finite total Poisson rate is bounded by

    M_out=1/2[(A_*+gamma_A)Ymax
                    +(L_*+gamma_L)cr(Rz+1)]^2.

Set Jp=ceil(log_2(64/delta)) and Q=ceil(16(M_out+Jp)). For total rate
lambda<=M_out, Q>=8lambda and the Poisson Chernoff bound gives
P(Poisson(lambda)>Q)<delta/32. Approximate the probabilities
exp(-lambda)lambda^n/n!, n=0,...,Q, by rational lower endpoints of width
at most delta/[128(Q+1)] and assign the residual to overflow. A dyadic
uniform quantile adds at most delta/64. If overflow is chosen, return a
fixed record. Conditional on total n<=Q, use a dyadic multinomial
sampler for mode probabilities lambda_j/lambda with total error at most
delta/32 over at most Q draws; if lambda=0 return the zero record. The
finite Poisson stage differs from the ideal product-Poisson law at the
finite amplitude by less than delta/8. Its bit time is polynomial in
Q,M_out, rate bit length, and log(delta^(-1)).

7. Total error and bit cost
===========================

The accepted-core coupling contributes less than delta/16. The output
normal tail/endpoint event and the Poisson-Hellinger transfer contribute
less than delta/64 altogether; the finite Poisson stage contributes less
than delta/8; the rejection timeout contributes at most xi/2. The remaining
uniform, mixture and arithmetic events are already included in the proposal,
acceptance or output budgets. The sum is below delta+xi.

The worst-case bit and random-bit cost is polynomial in d, rational input
length L, numeric kappa, Lambda, M_out, delta^(-1), log(xi^(-1)), and the
exact rational certificate, covariance, LDL and real-algebraic interval
costs. Crucially, the output map is charged through the whitened matrix
A=K B^(1/2) and its certified numeric bound A_*, not through ||K|| alone.
A polynomial-in-L corollary still requires numeric kappa, Lambda, M_out and
delta^(-1) to be polynomially bounded. There is no p_H or log(1/p_H) cost
for abstract conditional-record sampling; physically producing a herald
still costs 1/p_H.

For m=0, use the exact all-zero Gaussian branch of PC-17. For U empty,
return the empty count record after core acceptance.

8. A constant-output, rare-herald high-anisotropy family
========================================================

The earlier Section 8 fixture had conditional output mean O(2^-n), so its
output was TV-close to vacuum. The following rational family removes that
weak-output caveat while retaining the large local condition number.

Let n>=2, a=2^(-2n), gamma=1/3, and

    B=diag(a^2,a),  R=B^(1/2)=diag(a,sqrt(a)),
    T=C°=[[B,gamma R],[gamma R,I_2]],
    P_H=diag(I_2,0).

Both entries of R are rational because sqrt(a)=2^-n. T is rational SPD:
its Schur complement with respect to B is (1-gamma^2)I_2=(8/9)I_2.
The raw rational covariance

    C=(T^(-1)-P_H)^(-1)

has O(n) bits in its fixed 4 by 4 representation: its entries reduce to
rational functions with denominators 1-a and 1-a^2. It is positive
definite exactly when B<I_2. Indeed, T^(-1)-P_H is positive
definite iff I-T^(1/2)P_HT^(1/2)>0; the nonzero eigenvalues of
T^(1/2)P_HT^(1/2) are those of P_HTP_H|_H=B. This condition holds for
0<a<1. Importantly, T itself has an eigenvalue greater than one: on the
coordinate associated with the eigenvalue a of B, the determinant of the
principal block of I-T is -gamma^2 a<0. Thus positivity is not being
silently inferred from ||T||<1.

A block Woodbury identity gives

    C=T+T_:H (I_2-B)^(-1) T_H:,
    C_HH=B(I_2-B)^(-1),
    C_HU=gamma (I_2-B)^(-1)R,
    C_UU=I_2+gamma^2 B(I_2-B)^(-1).

It follows that tr(C)/2=1+O(a), so the raw input mean energy is bounded
and tends to one. The herald is one photon in the first mode and the second
mode is retained. The tilted positive-count covariance is S=B, so the
local-matrix certificate has s_-=s_+=1, rho=1, and Lambda=1. The Gaussian
regression and residual covariance are

    K=gamma B^(-1/2),  V=(1-gamma^2)I_2,
    A=K B^(1/2)=gamma I_2.

Although ||K||=gamma/a grows as 2^(2n), the actual whitened output map A
has constant norm. This is precisely why the original-coordinate M_out
bound was too large.

The herald probability is exactly

    p_H=Z_S det(I+C P_H)^(-1/2)
       =(tr B/2) sqrt(det(I-B))
       =a(1-a)(1+a)^(3/2)/2
       ~a/2=2^(-2n-1).

Here det(I+C P_H)=det(I-B)^(-1). Thus physical repeat-until-herald
preparation costs Theta(1/a)=Theta(2^(2n)), even though the finite abstract
conditional-record sampler below is polynomial in n and its requested
accuracy.

Because S=B, the proposal equals the target core and the rejection
acceptance is exactly one. Under the weighted core, Y=B^(-1/2)X has
||Y||^2=2G with G~Gamma(2,1), hence E||Y||^2=4. With
Z~N(0,(8/9)I_2), the retained amplitude is U=gamma Y+Z, giving

    E[N_U|H]=(tr V+gamma^2 E||Y||^2)/2=1+gamma^2=10/9.

The output is bounded away from vacuum. Averaging the Gaussian factor and
the Gamma radius gives

    P(N_U=0|H)
       =(1+v)^(-1) (1+gamma^2/(1+v))^(-2)
       =(2-gamma^2)/4=17/36,    v=1-gamma^2,

so the nonvacuum mass, and the total-variation distance to the vacuum
count law, is 19/36.

For any isotropic scalar proposal Q=beta I_2 dominating B, beta>=a.
At herald count k=1 its exact acceptance is

    alpha_scalar=sqrt(det B/det Q) (Z_B/Z_Q)
       =a^2 sqrt(a)(1+a)/(2 beta^2)
       <=sqrt(a)(1+a)/2.

The best scalar choice beta=a therefore uses
Theta(a^(-1/2))=Theta(2^n) expected proposals, exponential in the O(n)-bit
rational input description. By contrast, PC-19 has acceptance one and, in
whitened coordinates, A_*=O(1), V=(8/9)I, kappa=1, Lambda=1, and
M_out=O(log^2(1/delta)) for this fixed two-mode example. Its finite-bit
conditional count sampler is polynomial in n, delta^(-1), and
log(xi^(-1)), with no 1/p_H term. A direct-output baseline is also easy:
the same k=1 positive angular mixture and Gamma(2,1) radius sample the core,
then one samples a two-dimensional Gaussian and one Poisson count. In
particular the comparison establishes an exponential weakness of the
scalar rejection proposal, not a lower bound against all exact samplers or
a separation from the generic low-herald-count hafnian method.

The exact n=40 rational/algebraic fixture checks raw covariance positivity
and reconstruction, the determinant herald formula, S,K,V,A, energy,
output vacuum probability, and scalar/local acceptance. It is finite
evidence, not proof validation. Prior-art boundary: Quesada and Arrazola's
exact Gaussian boson sampler is exponential in detected photons and includes
displacements ([Phys. Rev. Research 2, 023005, 3 Apr 2020](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.023005)); for this k=1 toy fixture that baseline is itself trivial. Quesada et al. give heralded Gaussian-state Fock matrix elements and herald probabilities using loop hafnians ([arXiv:1905.07011, first posted 16 May 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07011)). These sources do not establish novelty or priority for PC-19.
