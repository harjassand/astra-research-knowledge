1. Dilute-sector coercivity from the contact-density inequality
--------------------------------------------------------------
The already proved pure-critical estimate, for normalized m-sector psi, is

 <W><=4m(m-1)/(L-1)
       +4(m-1)sqrt(m/(2J))sqrt(<A_L>)/L.                (T5)

The sine transform gives A_L>=c_L N with c_L->lambda_c. Since
lambda<lambda_*<=lambda_c, choose c0 in(max(lambda,0),lambda_c); for all
large L, c_L>=c0. Put d0=c0-lambda>0 and

 b=min(1/4,d0/(4c0)), K_J=4/sqrt(2J).

For m<=epsilon L and L>=2, (T5) yields

 <W><=8epsilon m+K_J epsilon sqrt(m<A_L>).              (T6)

Young's inequality gives, with u=kappa K_J epsilon,

 u sqrt(m<A_L>)<=b<A_L>+u^2 m/(4b).

Therefore every vector in that sector obeys

 <B_L>>=(1-b)<A_L>
           -[lambda+8kappa epsilon+u^2/(4b)]m.

Use another b<A_L> plus the sine gap. Since b<=d0/(4c0),

 <B_L>>=b<A_L>
       +[d0/2-8kappa epsilon-u^2/(4b)]m.               (T7)

Choose fixed epsilon>0 sufficiently small so
8kappa epsilon+u^2/(4b)<=d0/4. If kappa>0, one sufficient choice is

 epsilon<=min(1/4,d0/(64kappa),sqrt(b d0/2)/(kappa K_J)); (T8)

for kappa=0 choose any epsilon<=1/4. Thus in ALL m<=epsilon L sectors,

 B_L>=b A_L+(d0/4)N.                                   (T9)

This bound explicitly controls mesoscopic m: the contact bound scales
like epsilon m, not merely like O(1/L) at a fixed m. It is not inferred
from the macroscopic ground theorem or from a fixed-particle limit.

2. Dense-sector coercivity from the random-profile liminf
---------------------------------------------------------
Put Delta=lambda_*(kappa)-lambda>0. We claim that, for all sufficiently
large L and uniformly over m>=epsilon L,

 inf spec B_{L,m}>=(Delta/2)m.                          (T10)

If not, take a violating subsequence with m/L->p>=epsilon and normalized
sector ground vectors. Their energies satisfy <B><=(Delta/2)m, hence
<A><=<B>+[kappa+max(lambda,0)]m=O(L). Their physical critical energy
D=<A>/L^2 is O(1/L), so the random-law kinetic/contact liminf applies.
Every empirical measure has mass exactly m/L; its limiting law Pi is
supported on profiles with integral rho=p. Consequently

 liminf L^{-1} inf spec B_{L,m}
       >=E_Pi E_{kappa,lambda}(theta)>=Delta p,

whereas the violating upper bound gives limsup<=Delta p/2. This is a
contradiction. The law, the actual contact rho^2 and the mass constraint
are all retained; no mean-profile substitution appears here.

On every m-sector we also have B_L>=A_L-CN with
C=kappa+max(lambda,0). Convexly combine that form bound with(T10).
For q=Delta/2 and

 a_h=min(1/4,q/[4(C+q)]),

 B_L>=a_h A_L+[(1-a_h)q-a_h C]N
                                      >=a_h A_L+(q/2)N (T11)

throughout m>=epsilon L. Thus(T9),(T11) give constants a,d>0 and L0 such
that the SINGLE all-sector operator inequality holds:

 B_L>=a A_L+d N, every L>=L0.                           (T12)

Vacuum has B=0 and A=N=0; every positive-m vector has energy>=dm, so the

For compact parameter sets separated from lambda_* by a uniform Delta,
