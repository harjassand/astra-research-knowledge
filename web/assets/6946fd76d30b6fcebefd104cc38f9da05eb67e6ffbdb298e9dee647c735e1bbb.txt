# N02 addendum v2 — Lax-root form of the one-pulse conversion envelope

This addendum preserves and strengthens the frozen record in `v1.txt`; it does not replace v1. The main improvement is a direct map from a nonreal Lax zero to the full angular first-pulse envelope and its strict charge-budget gap. The exact soliton used is still the source-reported solution of Fiorillo–Raffelt (2023, Appendix C); the rewrites and corollaries below are internal deductions.

## Lax zero to pulse parameters

For the diagonal state \(\mathbf D_v=G(v)\hat z\), use the collisionless homogeneous Lax scalar
\[
 \ell(u)=\int_{-1}^1\frac{vG(v)}{u-v}\,dv.
\]
Assume \(G\in L^1([-1,1])\), \(G_1=\int vG(v)dv\ne0\), and that there is a nonreal zero \(u=a+ib\) with \(b\ne0\). The fast linear dispersion relation in Fiorillo–Raffelt, Eq. (44), is
\[
 \int_{-1}^1\frac{v^2G(v)}{vG_1-G_0+\Omega}\,dv=1.
\]
Set \(u=(G_0-\Omega)/G_1\); then
\[
 \int\frac{v^2G(v)}{v-u}\,dv
 =G_1+u\int\frac{vG(v)}{v-u}\,dv
 =G_1-u\ell(u).
\]
Therefore the dispersion equation is \(1=1-u\ell(u)/G_1\). A nonzero unstable root is exactly a nonreal Lax zero. With the conjugate chosen to give positive growth in the source's frequency convention, its pendulum parameters are
\[
 \omega=-G_1a,\qquad \Gamma=|G_1b|,\qquad
 \lambda^2=\omega^2+\Gamma^2=G_1^2|u|^2.
\]
The real-frequency sign convention is fixed here by the denominator in Eq. (44) and by \(S=2\omega\) in the soliton reconstruction. For the three-beam example, \(u=7/11\pm2i\sqrt7/11\), hence \(\omega=-7/3\), \(\Gamma=2\sqrt7/3\), matching the independent moment calculation in v1.

## Spectral form of the pulse envelope and its exact signed balance

Insert \(w=vG_1\), \(\lambda^2=G_1^2|u|^2\), \(\omega=-G_1a\), and \(\Gamma=|G_1b|\) into the source-reported temporal soliton. The pulse profile becomes
\[
 \frac{D_v^z(t)}{G(v)}
 =1-2\chi_u(v)\operatorname{sech}^2\!\bigl(|G_1b|(t-t_p)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \chi_u(v)=\frac{b^2v^2}{|u|^2[(v-a)^2+b^2]}.
\]
This rewrites the nonlinear conversion envelope using just the selected Lax zero \(u\) and the initial ELN weight \(G(v)\). Its nonnegativity and the exact complete-flip criterion follow from
\[
 1-\chi_u(v)=
 \frac{(av-|u|^2)^2}{|u|^2[(v-a)^2+b^2]}\ge0.
\]
Thus the one-pulse conversion fraction is \(0\le\chi_u\le1\), and complete reversal occurs only at
\[
 v_* = \frac{|u|^2}{a}\quad(a\ne0).
\]
If \(a=0\), no finite angle has complete reversal. The resonance is physical only if \(v_*\in[-1,1]\). For the example, \(|u|^2=a=7/11\), so \(v_*=1\).

There is also a useful exact cancellation derived from the Lax zero itself. With \(d(v)=(v-a)^2+b^2\), write \(\ell(a+ib)=0\) in real and imaginary parts:
\[
 \int\frac{vG(v)}{d(v)}dv=0,\qquad
 \int\frac{vG(v)(a-v)}{d(v)}dv=0.
\]
The first identity inserted in the second gives
\[
 \int\frac{v^2G(v)}{d(v)}dv=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \int G(v)\chi_u(v)dv
 =\frac{b^2}{|u|^2}\int\frac{v^2G(v)}{d(v)}dv=0.
\]
So equality of positive- and negative-ELN charge conversion on the pulse is not an extra closure assumption: it is an algebraic identity of the complex Lax root. It is consistent with the exact conservation of \(D_0^z\), and displays the same balance directly in spectral variables.

For \(A_m=\min(A_+,A_-)\), the exact one-pulse budget gap therefore has the root-only form
\[
 2A_m-C(t_p)=
 2\int_{\text{minority sector}} |G(v)|
 \frac{(av-|u|^2)^2}{|u|^2[(v-a)^2+b^2]}dv.
\]
It is positive for every nonatomic minority measure with nonzero total weight, because the numerator vanishes at one point at most. This is an exact obstruction to saturating the *charge-budget upper bound on this one pulse*. It says nothing by itself about a later trajectory with additional Lax modes or spatial structure.

## Exact first-swing crossing-removal criterion

The outgoing ELN sign at the pulse center is the sign of
\[
 G_{\rm pulse}(v)=G(v)R_u(v),\qquad
 R_u(v)=1-2\chi_u(v).
\]
Thus the crossing is absent at the pulse center if and only if \(G(v)R_u(v)\) is one-signed almost everywhere. This is a complete criterion within the one-pulse solution, not a criterion for the asymptotic state. The flip set is determined by
\[
 R_u(v)<0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (a^2-b^2)v^2-2a|u|^2v+|u|^4<0.
\]
When \(|a|>|b|\), it is the interval between roots \(|u|^2/(a+b)\) and \(|u|^2/(a-b)\), after ordering and intersecting with \([-1,1]\). For \(|a|<|b|\), the quadratic opens downward and the flip set is outside the roots. The equality case \(|a|=|b|\) is linear and should be handled directly from the displayed inequality. In the worked example the roots are \((7\mp2\sqrt7)/3\); only \(v=1\) lies in the flip set among its three beams. The positive minority beam flips, while both negative beams keep their sign, so the first swing removes the crossing while still exactly saturating the discrete charge budget.

For a smooth single-crossed spectrum whose entire minority support lies in the flip set and whose majority support lies outside it, this same formula predicts crossing removal in a coherent first swing, with a strictly positive residual charge-budget gap. This is a conditional angular diagnostic: it applies only if one dominant homogeneous soliton controls the first nonlinear excursion. Transport, multiple fastest roots, collisions, or later pulses invalidate that inference.

## Scope gate strengthened

The spectral map adds leverage only in the integrable homogeneous collisionless system. It supplies an exact angle-resolved *first-pulse envelope* for any selected nonreal Lax root. It does not solve mode competition. If the initial data have several roots with the same maximal growth, their seed projections remain comparable at \(t\sim\gamma^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon)\), so a single \(u\)-selected pulse need not describe the total first excursion. If one root is uniquely fastest, the finite-dimensional three-beam seed-matching argument in v1 is rigorous after reduction to the hyperbolic pendulum saddle. Its continuous-spectrum counterpart still needs an infinite-dimensional nonlinear error bound on logarithmic times.

For periodic inhomogeneous evolution, the integrated total \(D_0\) and characteristic beam lengths still constrain a charge budget, but \(\ell(u)\) no longer generates a time-independent spectral curve. The root-only pulse envelope and its root-derived charge balance do not extend without an additional invariant or an exact traveling-wave reduction. In the collisional equation, the source term changes trajectory lengths and generally changes \(D_0\); no version of the above gap is asserted.
