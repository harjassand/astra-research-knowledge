# Even closed walks sample gauge-nonnegative pure GBS

**Status:** internally derived complete construction for the stated interface, with exact small-instance implementation checks. This is not an external correctness or priority certificate. Unlike the first route investigated here, the main construction does **not** assume correctness of release family 113. It uses a Gaussian normalization identity and an elementary positive generating-function expansion. A limited primary-source search did not locate this exact sampling construction; standard loop-soup and infinitely divisible point-process literature remains a priority-validation target.

## Exact interface

Input is an explicit symmetric matrix B in Q^{m x m}, all entries nonnegative, with ||B||_2 < 1; a binary-rational tolerance 0 < epsilon < 1; and ideal random bits. Define the pure zero-displacement photon distribution

\[
p_B(n)=\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)}\,
\frac{\operatorname{haf}(B_n)^2}{\prod_i n_i!},\quad n\in\mathbb N^m.
\tag{1}
\]

B_n repeats each row/column n_i times; haf of an odd-sized matrix is zero and haf of the empty matrix is one. Diagonal B_ii entries appear as edges between distinct repeated copies and are allowed. All photon collisions are retained. The exact mean total photon number is

\[
E=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(B^2(I-B^2)^{-1}\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]

**Claim.** There is a randomized classical algorithm whose output is epsilon-close to (1) in total variation, with worst-case bit cost polynomial in the matrix encoding length, m, E, and epsilon^{-1}. Hence it is polynomial in the full encoded input under the additional promise that the **numerical** E is polynomially bounded in that length. The promise cannot be replaced by a short binary encoding of an exponentially large E.

No physical Gaussian device, perfect-matching counter, approximate hafnian oracle, MCMC mixing theorem, or rare-event postselection is supplied to this algorithm. A representation B = D A D with diagonal unitary D and nonnegative rational A gives the same number distribution as A, because local phase shifts preserve photon counts; that gauge representation must be supplied or separately acquired.

## 1. Gaussian normalization gives the probability generating function

The [Hamilton et al. pure-state formula](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01199), also explained in [the general Gaussian point-process treatment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11972), supplies (1). The required normalization identity can also be obtained directly: for any real symmetric contraction C,

\[
\sum_n\frac{\operatorname{haf}(C_n)^2}{n!}
=\det(I-C^2)^{-1/2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed, the left side is the squared norm of exp(a^\dagger C a^\dagger/2)|0>. Orthogonally diagonalize C. A number-preserving orthogonal change of modes preserves that norm, and each one-mode factor is

\[
\sum_{r\ge0}\frac{(2r)!}{2^{2r}(r!)^2}c^{2r}
=(1-c^2)^{-1/2}.
\]

For z in [0,1]^m, put D_z = diag(z). Multiplying an n-pattern amplitude by product z_i^{n_i/2} replaces B by C_z = D_z^{1/2} B D_z^{1/2}. Its norm is below one. Thus (3) and det(I-UV)=det(I-VU) give

\[
G_B(z)=\mathbb E\prod_i z_i^{N_i}
=\frac{\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)}}
{\sqrt{\det(I-(D_zB)^2)}}.
\tag{4}
\]

There is no sign assumption needed for (3)-(4). Nonnegativity enters the next step.

## 2. Positive log coefficients give independent Poisson walks

Because rho(D_z B) <= rho(B) < 1 for nonnegative B and 0 <= z <= 1, the convergent log-determinant expansion yields

\[
\log G_B(z)
=\sum_{\substack{L\ge2\\L\text{ even}}}
\frac{\operatorname{tr}((D_zB)^L)-\operatorname{tr}(B^L)}{L}.
\tag{5}
\]

For a rooted closed walk w = (v_0,...,v_{L-1},v_L=v_0), let a(w)_i count occurrences of i in v_0,...,v_{L-1} and define

\[
\lambda(w)=\frac1L\prod_{s=0}^{L-1}B_{v_s v_{s+1}}\ge0.
\]

Expansion of the trace rewrites (5) as

\[
\log G_B(z)=\sum_{L\text{ even}}\sum_{w:\,|w|=L}
\lambda(w)\bigl(z^{a(w)}-1\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]

This is precisely the PGF of the random vector sum_w C_w a(w), where the C_w are independent Poisson(lambda(w)). The rooted walks are labeled sequences, so repeated roots or orientations are not identified; the factor 1/L already gives the correct multiplicities, even for periodic walks. Total intensity is finite:

\[
\Lambda=\sum_{L\text{ even}}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(B^L)}L
=-\frac12\log\det(I-B^2).
\]

Equality of PGFs on an open neighborhood of zero gives equality of every photon-pattern probability. Thus this is an exact probabilistic representation of the full distribution (1), including collisions. It does not assert a positive Glauber--Sudarshan P representation or a mixture of coherent quantum states; it represents the measured number law only.

**Precise classical ancestry.** The rooted weight product(P_edges)/L and its independent Poisson realization are exactly the discrete Markov loop measure in [Le Jan, *Markov Paths, Loops and Fields*, equations (2.4), (2.5), (4.1)](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~lejan/pdfs/stfl1.pdf). To put our matrix into that interface, take the bipartite double cover A=[[0,B],[B,0]], h=(I-A)^{-1}1>0, and P=diag(h)^{-1}A diag(h). Its row sum at v is 1-1/h_v<1, so P is a killed Markov transition matrix; similarity preserves every closed-walk product. All loops have even length, and each B walk has two color lifts. The standard soup of intensity alpha=1/2 therefore projects, on forgetting color, to exactly (6). This identifies the construction as a specific Gaussian-number-law consequence of established loop-soup mathematics; it is not a new loop-soup representation theorem. An independent sibling-agent audit also found this same double-cover identification and no factor/parity defect in (4)-(7).

## 3. Draw one closed walk using matrix powers

Let lambda_L = tr(B^L)/L. Instead of enumerating m^L walks, first draw C_L ~ Poisson(lambda_L), then draw C_L independent walks of that length with probability proportional to their edge-weight product.

If tr(B^L)>0, choose the initial vertex i with probabilities

\[
\Pr(v_0=i)=\frac{(B^L)_{ii}}{\operatorname{tr}(B^L)}.
\]

With r edges remaining and current vertex v, choose the next vertex j according to

\[
\Pr(v_{s+1}=j\mid v_s=v,v_0=i)
=\frac{B_{vj}(B^{r-1})_{ji}}{(B^r)_{vi}}.
\tag{7}
\]

The denominator is positive for any prefix sampled with positive probability, and summing the numerators gives it. The probabilities telescope to product B_edges / tr(B^L). The last step returns to i. Increment the occupation counts of the L departure vertices, excluding the repeated endpoint.

All powers up to the cutoff below have binary-rational entries of polynomial bit length. Rational arithmetic and sampling from finite rational tables suffice; no hafnian is evaluated.

## 4. A finite worst-case algorithm and explicit TV budget

If E=0, symmetry implies B=0 and vacuum is exact. Otherwise set

\[
K=\left\lceil\frac{2E}{\epsilon}\right\rceil,
\qquad L_{\max}=2K.
\]

Discard all walks longer than 2K. The intensity discarded is at most

\[
\sum_{r>K}\lambda_{2r}
\le\frac1{2K}\sum_{r>K}\operatorname{tr}(B^{2r})
\le\frac E{2K}\le\frac\epsilon4.
\tag{8}
\]

Couple the full and truncated Poisson processes. Their occupation vectors coincide unless at least one discarded walk occurs, whose probability is <= the discarded intensity. Equation (8) is therefore a TV bound; it does not condition on a high-probability photon event or estimate a determinant normalizer.

For every even L<=2K with lambda_L>0, replace Poisson(lambda_L) by Binomial(R_L,lambda_L/R_L), where

\[
R_L=\left\lceil\max\left\{1,\lambda_L,
\frac{8K\lambda_L^2}{\epsilon}\right\}\right\rceil.
\tag{9}
\]

Le Cam's bound, in the conservative form TV <= 2 lambda_L^2/R_L, is at most epsilon/(4K). Summing over K lengths costs <=epsilon/4. Every trial and every walk uses finite rational categorical probabilities. The number of Bernoulli trials is bounded by sum_L R_L, and the number of walk-vertex draws by sum_L R_L(L+1), both polynomial in E and epsilon^{-1}, since lambda_L<=E/2. Approximate each finite categorical draw with r-bit uniform randomness and exact rational interval boundaries. A table of at most m entries changes by at most m 2^{-r} in TV. Choosing r from a bound on **all** trials and draws makes their accumulated error <=epsilon/4. There is no unbounded waiting or numerical real-arithmetic oracle in this version.

The final TV error is <=3epsilon/4. The spare epsilon/4 can cover an explicit chosen implementation tolerance. Precomputation uses O(Km^3) rational arithmetic operations and retains O(Km^2) rational entries. A common denominator argument bounds the bits per B^r entry by O(r(L_B+log m)); exact matrix inversion computes E in polynomial bit time. Counts in the output vector are at most sum_L L R_L, with corresponding binary storage. The reference `loop_sampler.py` uses these conservative finite bounds, plus optional local workload guards. It caches at most O(Km^2) categorical tables of m entries, a further O(Km^3) retained integers/rationals; these caches can be omitted without changing the proof. Its metadata charges actual cache sizes. Removing a workload guard gives the abstract algorithm; a guard rejection is not a sampler for the requested instance.

## 5. Two cross-checks and why family 113 is unnecessary here

First, there is a matching-based independent route. [Anand et al., 2511.16558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16558), Theorem 1.1, already samples the collision-free **unweighted graph subset law** on all graphs. Lemma 4.2 compares near-perfect and perfect matchings in two identical copies of a graph joined by unit vertical edges. Its AM--GM proof works verbatim with arbitrary nonnegative edge weights. The ratio is <2n^2 independently of the weights; for edge activities bounded by one the Jerrum--Sinclair running-time bound stays polynomial.

Replace each mode by k copies and put weight B_ij/k between copies of modes i,j (including distinct copies of the same mode when B_ii>0). For each count pattern n with n_i<=k, the projected matching-subset law is proportional to

\[
\frac{\operatorname{haf}(B_n)^2}{n!}\,r_k(n),
\qquad r_k(n)=\prod_i\frac{(k)_{n_i}}{k^{n_i}},
\]

and r_k=0 otherwise. The birthday bound 1-r_k(n)<=N(N-1)/(2k) and the pure-Gaussian moment identity

\[
\mathbb E[N(N-1)]=E^2+E+2\sum_i\nu_i^2
\le3E^2+E,\quad\nu_i=\lambda_i(B)^2/(1-\lambda_i(B)^2),
\]

show that this finite graph law is within (3E^2+E)/(2k) of the full photon law. This gives a second, slower polynomial sampler under the same E promise. It is an elementary extension of prior art, not a family-113-dependent conclusion.

Second, family 113 would give a third route by sequential marginal hafnian estimation. In doubled indices write C=[[0,B],[B,0]], Q=(I-C)^{-1}. For paired retained indices J and complement T, the Schur complement gives

\[
I-Q_{JJ}^{-1}
=C_{JJ}+C_{JT}(I-C_{TT})^{-1}C_{TJ}\ge0.
\]

The marginal hafnian matrix is X_J(I-Q_{JJ}^{-1}); it remains symmetric and entrywise nonnegative. Its probabilities follow [the standard Gaussian marginal formula](https://hafnian.readthedocs.io/en/stable/gbs_sampling.html). Thus every conditional table can be approximated with the weighted nonnegative hafnian reduction. This corrects my initial overly restrictive assessment that only individual pure probabilities could be evaluated. The route is conditional on family 113; the direct walk construction above is not.

## 6. Boundaries and prior-art status

For signed or complex B, walk products in (6) can be negative or complex. There is then no positive Poisson intensity. A four-mode pattern with matching products 1,-1,0 has hafnian zero, while replacing entries by their moduli gives hafnian two. Gauging away phases is therefore a substantive supplied/acquired interface condition.

The algorithm samples the **full** unconditioned photon distribution. Conditioning on a fixed photon total, no-collision event, or specified induced-subgraph size can require rejection probability exponentially small in the input. Both its acceptance probability and amplified error must be charged. If a=P_B(H)>0 and the full-law error is delta<a, then conditional TV is at most delta/a. To obtain conditional error eta by rejection therefore requires delta<=a eta as well as roughly 1/a attempts. This is not a polynomial guarantee when a is exponentially small.

For a concrete polynomial-energy falsification, let B consist of s disjoint 2x2 off-diagonal blocks with b=1/2. The total mean is E=2s/3, but the pattern with all 2s counts equal to one has probability (3/16)^s; the entire no-collision event has probability (15/16)^s. These examples refute a cost-free generic conditioning step, not hardness of their simple conditioned laws. Separate prior graph-subset algorithms can handle their own finite target laws directly. The walk representation is not an FPRAS for arbitrary general-graph perfect-matching counts. Individual encoding patterns may be exponentially rare.

The [2025 dense-graph GBS sampler](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64442-7) and [the subsequent all-graph subset sampler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16558) already refute broad claims of novelty for nonnegative graphical GBS. Their proved target law is a finite collision-free subset law, whereas this construction targets the infinite collision-inclusive physical law. General Gaussian determinant PGFs are also established prior mathematics; e.g. [Fitzke et al., 2209.05330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05330). The contribution claimed here is only an independently derived **typed construction and complete cost proof**, not first discovery, publication priority, or a resolution of general quantum sampling hardness. The exact double-cover identification above resolves the immediate ancestry question in favor of standard Markov loop-soup mathematics. A deeper search is still needed to establish whether this explicit GBS specialization and finite-bit resource statement have already appeared.
