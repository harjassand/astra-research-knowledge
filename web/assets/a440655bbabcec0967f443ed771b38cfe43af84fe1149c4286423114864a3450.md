# Source and prior-art notes

Scope is the abstract mathematical identifiability task. No biological evidence is used to set the CTMC or infer that it describes nature.

1. David Blackwell and Lambert Koopmans, “On the Identifiability Problem for Functions of Finite Markov Chains,” *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 28(4), 1011–1015 (1957), DOI [10.1214/aoms/1177706802](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177706802). The source abstract treats identifiability of a finite Markov chain from a coarsened function of its states, gives explicit finite-word conditions for one 4-state example, and discusses larger state counts. This is primary prior art on hidden/coarsened finite-chain observations. It is not the theorem proved here.

2. Tobias Rydén, “On identifiability and order of continuous-time aggregated Markov chains, Markov-modulated Poisson processes, and phase-type distributions,” *Journal of Applied Probability* 33(3), 640–653 (1996), DOI [10.2307/3215346](https://doi.org/10.2307/3215346). The primary abstract defines the continuous-time identifiability question as whether two parameters induce the same observable-process law, studies minimum representation order, and reports corresponding results for MMPPs and phase-type distributions. This is closest in problem class. The theorem here concerns a restricted terminal-probability interface, not the full observable process law treated in that tradition.

3. Elizabeth S. Allman, Catherine Matias and John A. Rhodes, “Identifiability of Parameters in Latent Structure Models with Many Observed Variables,” *The Annals of Statistics* 37(6A), 3099–3132 (2009), DOI [10.1214/09-AOS689](https://doi.org/10.1214/09-AOS689), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.5032). Their primary theorem gives generic-identifiability results for latent-structure models, including HMMs, under richer conditional-independence observations. The result here is not a challenge to those results: it records the exact confounding left by this specific scalar terminal-summary interface.

4. Baron Gaspard de Prony, “Essai expérimental et analytique…” *Journal de l'École Polytechnique* 1(2), 24–76 (1795). The historical source for exponential-sum reconstruction is identified in the mathematical literature; a modern mathematical paper's citation is listed [here](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1005035). This report's Hankel-pencil argument is classical Prony reconstruction, so no novelty claim is attached to it.

## Retrieval boundary

The citations above were opened through publisher or archival pages for bibliographic and abstract-level claims. The proof in PROOF.md is independently written and does not rely on an imported theorem. The full texts of Blackwell–Koopmans and Rydén were not reconstructed; therefore this is not an exhaustive priority search over CTMC observability, realization theory, or exponential fitting.
