# Primary-source and cost ledger

## Candidate source

Edgecomb, J. M.; Sau, A.; Manoj, N.; Resmini, M. D.; Meyer, A. F.; Paton, R. S.; Damrauer, N. H.; Wickens, Z. K. “Selectivity emerges from indiscriminate photoreduction.” *Nature* 656, 630–637 (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7). Official article: [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10897-7). Official SI PDF: [Springer Nature](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10897-7/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10897_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

The abstract says super-potent photoreductants can render substrate redox potentials irrelevant when forward outer-sphere SET becomes diffusion-limited, with selectivity then arising from downstream reaction versus BET. The authors demonstrate cyclopropyl-ketone/styrene formal [3+2] annulation despite around a 1-V redox mismatch. The full main text is subscription-previewed here; the official SI is open and carries the quantitative locators below.

## Mechanism and measurements

- SI S7–S8, Table S1.1: with perylene and 390-nm light, GC reports 70% desired annulation A, 7% styrene hydrogenation B, and 1%/8% remaining ketone/styrene. The A:B endpoint ratio is 10:1 among these two products only.
- SI S10–S13: dark constant-potential perylene controls show no meaningful A/B formation; perylene under fixed potential plus light gives 49% A and 2% B. Direct/mediated electrolysis controls primarily hydrogenate styrene and do not deliver the same annulation selectivity.
- SI S14–S19: compares conventional photoredox and thermal reductions. The authors report failures or nonselective outcomes for several alternative reductants/conditions and another thiolate photoreductant that supports the design premise.
- SI S25–S30: transient absorption/cyclic-voltammetry and pathway evidence. The Ket-1 aliphatic ketyl radical is not directly resolved by transient absorption; the authors rely on solvated-electron quenching and electrochemical evidence for SET, plus computed mechanisms.
- SI S73, Figure S2.18: Ket-1-quencher transient traces. The plot's fit label gives $k_{ET}=4\times10^9\ \mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}$. The final figure-caption sentence instead says $3\times10^{10}$, and appears copied because it calls capture “by pyrene” although the page is headed Ket-1 as quencher. SI S30 prose also gives $4\times10^9$ for Ket-1 and $3\times10^{10}$ for styrene. Preserve both readings: equal substrate concentrations imply $g=1$ from the caption versus $g=2/15$ from the prose/fit label.
- SI S81–S86: source kinetic model. It assumes excess/constant substrate and redox-partner concentrations and a quasi-steady-state reduction network; the paper prints the two-channel identity as Eq. S9. The same source uses Marcus rates with a diffusion cap in its simulated redox-potential maps.
- SI S81: the toy map assigns $k_{\rm proto}=6\ \mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}$, $k_{\rm frag}=10^8\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $k_{\rm coup}=2.4\times10^2\ \mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}$, and $k_{\rm annul}=1.7\times10^2\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ from DFT. These are model inputs, not in situ measured hazards.
- SI S97, Figure 5c model: $k_{\rm diff}=10^{10}\ \mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}$, acceptor $[A]=5.6\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{M}$, competitor sink $k_2=10^3\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, and thus illustrative $\beta=k_{\rm diff}[A]/k_2=560$. These are not fitted Ket-1 reaction parameters. For this β, the exact rate-ratio gates are in the executable certificate.
- SI S99–S100: the authors test chain propagation/inhibition and overestimate propagation rate; the modeled broad selectivity map remains similar. This is source analysis, not an independent reproduction.
- SI S114 onward: DFT pathway context reports ketyl β-scission as favorable (computed $\Delta G=-10.0\ \mathrm{kcal/mol}$; $\Delta G^\ddagger=5.5\ \mathrm{kcal/mol}$) and computes a 20.4-kcal/mol barrier for BET from the open ketyl intermediate, versus later addition/cyclization steps. Other computed barriers and alternative routes are listed in the SI. These are DFT-derived and concentration-corrected, not measured rate constants.
- SI S80 reports estimated capture rates and concentrations that do not remove the S2.18/S30 Ket-1 rate discrepancy. The SI also states that under the optimal constant-current protocol multiple reductant states may contribute; fixed-potential/light establishes perylene-radical-anion competence, not exclusivity.

## Physical inputs charged

For the screen, SI S6–S8 reports a divided H-cell under nitrogen, 0.3 mmol of each substrate, substrate concentration about 0.075 M, perylene at 7.5 mol%, DMF, TEAPF6/TEABr electrolyte, Zn-rod sacrificial anode, reticulated-vitreous-carbon cathode, Kessil light at 390 nm, 1-mA current, and roughly 7.75 h / 1 F mol−1 protocol. Anodic and cathodic electrolyte are reported as 0.4 M in the general procedure. Workup involves extraction and silica chromatography.

One faraday per mole on 0.3 mmol substrate corresponds to $0.0003F\approx28.9$ C; at 1 mA this is 8.0 h. The separately tabulated 7.75-h screen corresponds to 27.9 C, so the protocol is approximate or the charge/time varies across experiments. I report both as source-scoped values instead of forcing exact equality. The article does not give a run-long cell-voltage integral, LED wall-plug power, optical irradiance/incident photon count, absorbed photon fraction, or quantum yield. Thus current/charge and wavelength are charged, but electrical joules/mol, photon joules/mol, and full process energy are `UNKNOWN`.
