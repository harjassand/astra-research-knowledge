1. THE PRECISE SUFFICIENT QUADRATIC LEMMA

Let (A,tau) be a finite-dimensional C*-algebra with faithful normalized trace,
and let Phi be UCP, tau-preserving, positive and selfadjoint on L2(A,tau).
If there is a UCP tau-preserving positive selfadjoint EB Psi satisfying

    I-Psi <= c(I-Phi),                                            (3)

then for every Hermitian H, ||H||<=theta,

    ||Psi H-H||_(1,tau)^2
      <= ||Psi H-H||_(2,tau)^2
      <= <H,(I-Psi)H>_tau
      <= c <H,(I-Phi)H>_tau
      <= c theta ||Phi H-H||_(1,tau).                            (4)

The first inequality is normalized Holder. The second uses 0<=Psi<=I, so
(I-Psi)^2<=I-Psi. The last uses trace duality and the operator-norm bound.
Thus (3), if proved in full generality, would settle the tracial remainder
with a single EB map and a dimension-free square-root modulus. It also avoids
the known slow-classical counterexample to THE final fixed-point projection.

General existence of such Psi with any universal c is CONJECTURAL here.
Ordinary constant-factor Grothendieck comparison does not establish the
near-one deficit comparison (3); no such substitution is made.

2. EXACT QUBIT SPECTRAL CONSTRAINTS FROM SELFCOMPATIBILITY

Let Phi be a positive HS-selfadjoint bistochastic qubit channel. Its real
Bloch matrix is symmetric PSD. Simultaneous unitary conjugation diagonalizes
it in three orthogonal Pauli axes A_1,A_2,A_3:

    Phi(I)=I, Phi(A_i)=lambda_i A_i, 0<=lambda_i<=1.

Every proper orthogonal Bloch rotation comes from a qubit unitary; signs of
eigenvectors can ensure the diagonalizing rotation is proper.

Selfcompatibility supplies a two-output CPTP B whose two marginals are Phi.
Its normalized Choi state omega=J_B/2 has an input marginal I/2. Therefore

    Tr[omega A_i^T(A_i^B+A_i^C)] = 2 lambda_i.                    (5)

Pair constraint. The exact SOS from cycle3 applies to every two Pauli axes:
for K_ij=A_i^T(A_i^B+A_i^C)+A_j^T(A_j^B+A_j^C),

    8I-K_ij^2=M_ij^* M_ij>=0,
    ||K_ij||<=2 sqrt(2),
    lambda_i+lambda_j<=sqrt(2).                                 (6)

The M expression is entirely explicit in
work/cycle3/large_clique_broadcast.txt. This is familiar two-setting
monogamy, proved there directly; no assumption about the compatibilizer's
output correlations is used.

Triple constraint. The three-axis witness K_123 has norm 4. To see it,
conjugate its input by Pauli Y, which maps A_i^T to -A_i for all three axes.
The witness becomes

    -sigma_A dot (sigma_B+sigma_C) = -4 s_A dot J_(BC),

where s_A has spin1/2 and J_(BC) is spin0 or spin1. In the spin0 sector
the eigenvalue is 0. In spin1 coupled with spin1/2, total spin3/2 gives
-2 and total spin1/2 gives 4. Thus its spectrum is contained in {-2,0,4}.
Using (5),

    lambda_1+lambda_2+lambda_3<=2.                              (7)

This is an exact eight-dimensional operator calculation, not an extrapolated
numerical spectrum or an assumed optimal cloning theorem.

3. THE EXPLICIT EB ROUNDING

Set mu_i=max(0,2 lambda_i-1). The constraints above imply sum_i mu_i<=1:

- With zero or one active axis, this follows from lambda_i<=1.
- With two active axes, sum mu=2(lambda_i+lambda_j)-2
  <=2 sqrt(2)-2<1, by (6).
- With three active axes, sum mu=2 sum lambda-3<=1, by (7).

Let D_i be complete dephasing in the eigenbasis of A_i, and let R be the
replacement channel X->Tr(X)I/2. Define

    Psi = sum_i mu_i D_i + (1-sum_i mu_i) R.                    (8)

Each D_i and R is explicitly measure-and-prepare, hence (8) is EB. It is
bistochastic, positive and selfadjoint on HS space. Its eigenvalues are 1
on I and mu_i on A_i. Since

    0<=mu_i<=lambda_i,
    1-mu_i<=2(1-lambda_i),                                     (9)

equation (1) follows in the same eigenbasis. Equation (4) then proves (2),
uniformly over the ENTIRE bounded-likelihood family, not just selected axes.

The constructed map depends only on Phi's Bloch eigendecomposition, not on
the labels in the experiment. Its existence and finite qubit compilation are
explicit; this is not a proposed optimization that was left unrun.

4. GROWING TENSOR PRODUCTS AND OTHER CLOSURES

Let Phi=Phi_1 tensor ... tensor Phi_k, with factors as in section3, and take
Psi=Psi_1 tensor ... tensor Psi_k. Tensor products of EB channels are EB.
These pairs are simultaneously diagonal on the tensor operator eigenbasis.
For any eigenvalue choice, write 0<=mu_j<=lambda_j<=1. By telescoping,

    1-product_j mu_j
      =sum_j (1-mu_j) product_(l<j)mu_l
      <=2 sum_j (1-lambda_j) product_(l<j)lambda_l
      =2(1-product_j lambda_j).                               (10)

Thus the tensor product still satisfies (1) with constant 2, independent of
k and D=2^k. It includes factors with lambda_z=1-delta_j for arbitrarily
small delta_j; slow classical information is retained by (8), rather than
discarded by the final fixed projection.

If pairs (Phi_j,Psi_j) satisfy (1), their corresponding convex mixtures do
also. EB, UCP, tracial preservation, positive selfadjointness, and the
quadratic Loewner inequalities are all preserved. Simultaneous conjugation
Phi->Ad_U Phi Ad_(U*) and Psi->Ad_U Psi Ad_(U*) preserves these properties.
The unitary U may entangle all tensor factors: no locality assumption is
added to the promised experiment. A convex mixture may use different such

5. DEPOLARIZING QUDITS AND SHARPNESS OF c=2

For a positive depolarizing Phi_lambda(X)=lambda X+(1-lambda)Tr(X)I/d,
selfcompatibility implies

    lambda <= (d+2)/(2(d+1)).                                  (11)

A self-contained proof uses the normalized maximally entangled projector P
on input-output registers. The two-output Choi state has both singlet
fractions F=(1+(d^2-1)lambda)/d^2. The sum P_(RB)+P_(RC) has norm 1+1/d
(the overlap norm of the two projector ranges is 1/d), so 2F<=1+1/d,
which rearranges to (11).

The Haar estimation EB channel is

    E(X)=d int <psi|X|psi> |psi><psi| dpsi
        =(X+Tr(X)I)/(d+1),                                    (12)

using the elementary second moment int psi^(tensor2)=2P_sym/[d(d+1)].
It has nontrivial eigenvalue 1/(d+1). Depolarizing maps with
0<=mu<=1/(d+1) are convex mixtures of (12) and replacement, hence EB.
Choose mu=max(0,2lambda-1), which lies in that interval by (11). This proves
(1) for such factors, and they can be combined with qubit factors in (10).

At lambda=(d+2)/(2(d+1)), any EB map satisfying a tracial quadratic
comparison can be Haar-twirled without weakening that comparison. The
twirled EB map is depolarizing with eigenvalue <=1/(d+1); its Choi singlet
fraction is <=1/d, since separable states have that overlap bound. Therefore
the best comparison constant is at least

    [1-1/(d+1)]/[1-(d+2)/(2(d+1))]=2.                           (13)

The actual compatible channel at this lambda is
B(X)=2P_sym(X tensor I)P_sym/(d+1); its two marginals are Phi_lambda.
Thus c=2 is attainable and sharp for this raw positive compatible class.
For the actual Petz square of that particular marginal, eigenvalue lambda^2,
the optimal constant is smaller: 2/[(1+lambda)] (1.2,1.23077,1.25 for d2,3,4).
No claim of that smaller constant for all Petz-corrected channels is made.

Let (A_j,tau_j) be finite-dimensional algebras with faithful normalized
traces. Suppose S_j,Psi_j are UCP and tau_j-preserving, and are positive
selfadjoint contractions on L2(A_j,tau_j). Suppose Psi_j is EB and, with
ONE c>=1,

    I-Psi_j <= c(I-S_j).

    [aI+b(Psi_1 tensor Psi_2)]
      -[(aI+bPsi_1) tensor(aI+bPsi_2)]
      = ab(I-Psi_1) tensor(I-Psi_2) >=0

give S_1 tensor S_2<=aI+b(Psi_1 tensor Psi_2), equivalently the original
comparison with the SAME c. Induct on the number of factors. The argument
also includes c=1, where a=0. It uses positivity of S_j for tensor-order
monotonicity and 0<=Psi_j<=I for the final PSD tensor; a selfadjoint norm
bound permitting negative eigenvalues is not the assumed interface.

10. INDEPENDENT ADDENDUM: ARBITRARY BISTOCHASTIC INSTRUMENT CLASS

The companion report's stronger instrument/Petz extension is correct.
Supply K_y in A with sum K_y* K_y=sum K_y K_y*=I. Let sigma be the faithful
central density representing tau(A)=Tr[sigma A], and set

    L(A)=sum_y K_y A K_y*,  M_y=K_y* K_y,  p_y=tau(M_y),
    Psi(A)=sum_(p_y>0) tau(M_y A) M_y/p_y,
    C=(L+Psi)/2,  Phi=C_dagger C.

Here dagger denotes the L2(tau) adjoint. L is a UCP tau-preserving
contraction by Kadison and trace preservation, so L_dagger L<=I; it need
not be selfadjoint or a positive Hilbert-space operator. Psi is canonical
EB, UCP, trace preserving and a positive selfadjoint contraction. Terms
with p_y=0 vanish because tau is faithful.

The actual instrument-append joint channel is

    B_K(rho)=sum_y K_y rho K_y* tensor [sigma M_y/p_y].

The prepared states are positive and have trace1, and sum M_y=I proves
the full-domain TP condition. Averaging B_K with its receiver swap gives
common PHYSICAL marginal C_phys(rho)=sigma C(sigma^(-1)rho) on A. The
central sigma commutes with every K_y. Thus C_phys fixes sigma and its
Petz recovery is R_phys(rho)=sigma C_dagger(sigma^(-1)rho), an actual CPTP
map. Applying this recovery to BOTH receivers gives common marginal
sigma Phi(sigma^(-1)rho). Consequently Phi is UCP, tau-preserving,
positive selfadjoint and selfcompatible. For sigma=I/d the physical and
likelihood formulas coincide, so the companion's C and C* are literal
matrix-space channels. On weighted blocks the displayed coordinate
conversion is essential to interpret those same formulas physically.

The exact SOS is

    2(I-Phi)-(I-Psi)
      = (I-L_dagger L)+(Psi-Psi^2)
                         +(L-Psi)_dagger(L-Psi)/2 >=0.

Expansion gives I+Psi-(L+Psi)_dagger(L+Psi)/2 exactly, including both
cross terms. Each summand is PSD. This proves c=2 for this supplied
instrument construction, with no claim that Psi<=Phi. The tensor result
in section9 now applies to arbitrary finite products of these corrected
