# Cycle 10: Grothendieck rounding attempt for the common barycenter gate

Date: 2026-10-08. This note attacks the arbitrary-frame gate directly. It is a proof attempt and a route failure record, not a theorem or counterexample.

## Exact legal interface

Fix a legal channel/broadcaster pair and an active positive dual

\[
Q=\sum_{i=1}^r w_i|A_i\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle A_i|,
\qquad w_i>0,\quad \tau(A_i)=0,\quad \tau(A_iA_j)=\delta_{ij}.
\]

Write \(\mathcal B^*:M_d\otimes M_d\to M_d\) for the unital CP adjoint of a supplied symmetric broadcaster. Its local restrictions are

\[
\mathcal B^*(X\otimes I)=\Phi^*(X)=\Phi(X),\qquad
\mathcal B^*(I\otimes X)=\Phi^*(X)=\Phi(X),
\]

where the last equality uses Hilbert--Schmidt self-adjointness. The tracial input induces

\[
\sigma_{AB}=\mathcal B(I/d),\qquad \sigma_A=\sigma_B=I/d.
\]

For a canonical input POVM \(M_y\), with \(p_y=\tau(M_y)\), define \(t_{iy}=\tau(M_yA_i)\). Its support is \(\sum_iw_i\sum_y t_{iy}^2/p_y\). The exact desired inequality is

\[
S_d(Q)\ge 2\sum_iw_i\tau(A_i\Phi(A_i))-\sum_iw_i.
\tag{G0}
\]

Every measurement used below is an actual POVM pulled back by \(\mathcal B^*\), so the broadcaster's reference and both output marginals stay fixed. The obstacle is to make its scalar score penalty independent of \(r\).

An exact sufficient moment interface for a Grothendieck-style rounding is now explicit. For this fixed positive \(Q\), it suffices to produce a POVM \((E_y)\) on \(AB\) and real estimators \(f_i(y)\) such that

\[
\mathcal B^*\!\left(\sum_yf_i(y)E_y\right)=\Phi(A_i)\quad(1\le i\le r),
\qquad
\sum_iw_i\sum_y\tau(\mathcal B^*(E_y))f_i(y)^2\le\sum_iw_i.
\tag{G*}
\]

The effects \(M_y=\mathcal B^*(E_y)\) automatically give the canonical barycenter; applying \(t^2/p\ge2tf-pf^2\) proves (G0). Thus an abstract factorization needs to output not just scalar states or a Gram factor, but a **joint legal measurement-estimator pair** satisfying (G*). This is sufficient, not necessary: the optimum support may pass (G0) even if no unbiased estimator with this variance budget exists.

## One-scalar Gaussian/spherical spectral rounding

Take \(u\) Haar-uniform on the real sphere \(S^{r-1}\) and, for each \(u\), measure the spectral PVM \((P_{u,k})_k\) of the single output observable

\[
G_u=\sum_{j=1}^r\sqrt{w_j}u_j(A_j\otimes I+I\otimes A_j),
\qquad G_u=\sum_k\lambda_{u,k}P_{u,k}.
\]

The randomized effects \(M_{u,k}=\mathcal B^*(P_{u,k})\,d\nu(u)\) form a continuous input POVM. They can be understood through the compact canonical-map set (or finitely represented after convex reduction); the construction does not use an unconstrained pure seed. Choose the regression functions

\[
f_i(u,k)=\frac{r\,u_i\lambda_{u,k}}{2\sqrt{w_i}}.
\]

Isotropy, \(\mathbb E[u_iu_j]=\delta_{ij}/r\), gives the exact unbiased first moment

\[
\int d\nu(u)\sum_k f_i(u,k)P_{u,k}
=\frac12(A_i\otimes I+I\otimes A_i),
\]

and hence

\[
\int d\nu(u)\sum_k f_i(u,k)M_{u,k}=\Phi(A_i).
\tag{G1}
\]

For every positive \(p\), \(t^2/p\ge2tf-pf^2\). Apply this separately to \(t_{i,u,k}=\tau(M_{u,k}A_i)\) and sum. The first term becomes the target correlation in (G0), by (G1). The exact penalty is

\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=\sum_iw_i\int d\nu(u)\sum_k\tau(M_{u,k}) f_i(u,k)^2\\
 &=\frac{r^2}{4}\int d\nu(u)\,\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{AB}G_u^2)\\
 &=\frac r4\sum_iw_i\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\sigma_{AB}(A_i\otimes I+I\otimes A_i)^2\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{G2}
\]

Here the last equality uses \(\mathbb E u_iu_j=\delta_{ij}/r\). Each local second moment is one. If \(c_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{AB}A_i\otimes A_i)\), state Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(|c_i|\le1\), so

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\sigma_{AB}(A_i\otimes I+I\otimes A_i)^2\right]
=2+2c_i\le4,
\qquad P\le r\sum_iw_i.
\]

Therefore this *legal common-POVM construction* proves only

\[
S_d(Q)\ge 2\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Phi)-r\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q.
\tag{G3}
\]

This is exact and basis-independent but loses a factor equal to active frame rank in the negative term. It matches the desired penalty only at \(r=1\); the earlier two-output product-PVM construction is sharper at \(r=2\). For large \(r\), (G3) is not the exceptional factor-two gate and can be much weaker than the already-known tree bound.

The loss is not an artifact of dropping the barycenter: the randomized effects are legal and sum to \(I\). It comes from asking one scalar spectral sketch \(G_u\) to carry first-moment information for all \(r\) coordinates. The unbiased coefficient is \(r u_i\); its squared cost contributes \(r^2\), while \(\mathbb E G_u^2\) contributes only \(1/r\), leaving \(r\).

## Why the standard Grothendieck interface does not close the gap

The direct application would be to the symmetric bilinear form

\[
u_\Phi(A,B)=\tau(A\Phi(B))
\]

or to a map from \(M_d\) into a Hilbert/operator-Hilbert space built from a Kraus or extension representation. Standard noncommutative Grothendieck and little-Grothendieck results furnish state/positive-functional domination for bilinear forms or maps; a bounded primary interface check is in [Haagerup--Musat, *Factorization and dilation problems for completely positive maps on von Neumann algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.0778) and [de Laat, *On the Grothendieck Theorem for jointly completely bounded bilinear forms*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3256). Those results are not, by their stated conclusion alone, an input POVM \((M_y)\) satisfying all three requirements:

1. **One common moment:** its scalar factors must control \(\sum_iw_i\tau(A_i\Phi(A_i))\) for every positive frame dual, not one observable or one bilinear norm at a time.
2. **POVM barycenter:** the rounded pure-state moments must arise from effects summing to \(I\), equivalently a pure ensemble with \(\int P\,d\mu=I/d\). Arbitrary factorization states need not have this barycenter.
3. **Legal marginal source:** the effects must be obtainable from a measurement on the supplied \(AB\) system through \(\mathcal B^*\), with \(\sigma_A=\sigma_B=I/d\). An unconstrained state-functional decomposition can discard the fixed Choi marginals.
4. **Correct order constant:** a Grothendieck factor 2 in a bilinear/operator norm theorem is not the assertion \(S_d(Q)\ge2\operatorname{Tr}(Q\Phi)-\operatorname{Tr}Q\). The latter is a quadratic PSD-cone inequality with a specific negative \(\operatorname{Tr}Q\) term. A valid transfer must derive that term and its coefficient, rather than relabel a norm factorization.

The standard theorem therefore supplies a plausible language for the missing conversion but does not close it. The random-spectral attempt above is an explicit test: it preserves all legal constraints and has exactly the desired unbiased correlation, yet its estimator penalty scales as \(r\). A successful Grothendieck/2-summing bridge must improve this *quadratic POVM penalty* to \(\sum_iw_i\), not merely improve an abstract bilinear norm constant.

## Status and next test

No proof of (G0), no globally valid counterexample certificate, and no claim of novelty or priority. Status remains **UNKNOWN**. The next useful Grothendieck test is to formulate the map for which the source states furnished by the theorem are forced to be tracial/barycentric, then prove that its Pietsch/Gram factorization is realizable as a legal measurement on \(AB\) with the penalty in (G0). If that implication fails, an exact state/frame exhibiting the failure should be retained. The rank-loss calculation (G2) rules out the simplest one-random-linear-combination spectral rounding.
