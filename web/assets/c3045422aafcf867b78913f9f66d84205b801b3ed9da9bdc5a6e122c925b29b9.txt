BIPARTITE PAIR-MATRIX GUTZWILLER PREPARATION: PRIOR-ART AND SCOPE AUDIT
7 October 2026. Primary-source screen; priority remains open.

MAIN ASSESSMENT

The proposed result is a useful, sharply specified conditional application of the recent Chen–Liu determinant-counting theorem and the N63 coherent compiler. It extends the direct counting route to fully hard-projected, fixed-number paired states with cross-sublattice support in their explicitly supplied pair matrix. The particle-hole conversion of BCS states into Slaters, projected BCS/RVB ansatz, determinant amplitude evaluation, canonical projection, and several quantum RVB preparation methods are old. The plausible residual contribution is the physical reduction that preserves log-concave onsite tables and turns physical number into an admissible quota, with a finite-bit coherent guarantee independent of original projection rarity. It is not a new counting mechanism or the first RVB sampler/preparation algorithm.

I found no checked primary source giving the exact uniform guarantee for every explicit rational complex pair matrix in this subclass, without a gap, conditioning, or inverse projection-weight factor. This is a bounded literature search result, not novelty certification. The imported Chen–Liu theorem is recent and has not been independently proof-certified or implemented here. N63 is an internally audited conditional compiler, not an externally established state-preparation theorem.

EXACT CLAIMED INPUT AND REDUCTION

Input: a site partition A disjoint-union B=[n], an explicit F in Q(i)^(n x n) with F_AA=F_BB=0, rational squared double-occupancy filter weights t_i in [0,1], and a physical pair count k. Target, if nonzero:

  |psi_k> proportional to P_(N=2k) D_t exp(sum_ij F_ij c_up,i^dagger c_down,j^dagger)|0>.

For spin singlets require F=F^T; the reduction also admits nonsymmetric opposite-spin F. It does not acquire F, optimize a variational ansatz, or identify a Hamiltonian ground state.

Each cross-sublattice pair creates one particle on A and one on B. Consequently N_A=N_B in every term, and physical N=2N_A. Particle-hole transformation of BOTH spin modes on B maps the Gaussian to a graph Slater of transformed rank r=2|B|, with orbital matrix

  G=[M; I_(2|B|)],  M=[[0,F_AB],[-F_BA^T,0]],

in grouped row order (up_A,down_A,hole-up_B,hole-down_B). The physical vacuum maps to the filled B-hole sea. Mode reordering and particle-hole Fock phases are explicit quadratic parity functions, so a Born-weight identity alone is not being mistaken for a coherent state identity. The transfer worker verified these signs in work/cycle4/hard_bcs_transfer_checks.py/.json: 12 complex rational matrix cases, 688 configuration checks, 331 nonzero sign checks and 54 canonical norm checks. These are finite algebra checks, not a counting or quantum compiler implementation.

On A, transformed local load equals physical load. On B it is its complement 2-load. The squared tables are therefore

  b_A=(1,1,t_i),  b_B=(t_i,1,1).

Both have interval support and satisfy raw load log-concavity for all 0<=t_i<=1, including t_i=0. Canonical physical pair count is the hard transformed quota N'_A=k, rather than rare postselection after grand-canonical preparation. Its site constraints and color-union constraint form a laminar family; equivalently the complement polynomial can be a product of two fixed-degree block-load polynomials on the disjoint color sets. This gives the direct Chen–Liu counting instance at the desired sector. Conditional N63 complexity is polynomial in input encoding, n, inverse accuracy and log inverse compiler failure probability, with no inverse original filter or original number-sector probability.

For t_i=0 at every site, any allowed coefficient has disjoint up/down site sets U,D. The determinant det F[U,D] never accesses a diagonal entry. Arbitrary F_ii can therefore first be removed, and the hard-only sufficient condition weakens to bipartite OFF-DIAGONAL support. This does not extend the same deletion to arbitrary soft filters.

At all-hard t=0, the legal transformed supports at fixed k themselves have a direct-sum matroid description: the A component is a rank-k truncation of the partition matroid allowing at most one mode per site; the B component is the dual of the analogous rank-k partition matroid on B. Its rank is 2|B|-k. Thus Chen–Liu Corollary C.13(a) already explicitly counts the resulting determinant weights over matroid bases. Classical approximate counting and Born self-reduction here are a physical corollary of an existing stated general theorem, not a separate new algorithm. Soft tables use their general complementary log-concave framework, including C.18–C.19 and the exact-support argument.

PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISONS

1. Tao Li and Hong-Yu Yang, Topological Order in Gutzwiller Projected Wave Functions for Quantum Antiferromagnets, cond-mat/0604516 (2006), and the earlier cond-mat/0405034 (2004).
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0604516
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0405034
The general pairing section starts from a mean-field Hamiltonian with hopping and pairing terms connecting opposite sublattices. Down-spin particle-hole transformation on ALL sites makes it number conserving; a determinant/Gram argument gives the Marshall sign after physical single-occupancy projection. This is strong old evidence for the BCS/Slater and bipartite-sign toolkit, but the premise is a Hamiltonian support condition, not the candidate's explicit pair-matrix support condition. The physical up/down single-occupancy choices become transformed double/empty choices, which do not give the candidate's interval-support onsite tables. No FPRAS, uniform Born-mixing bound, or coherent preparation guarantee is claimed in the checked sections. Local captures: work/cycle4/bipartite_bcs_sources/cond-mat_0604516.txt and cond-mat_0405034.txt.

2. Seiji Yunoki and Sandro Sorella, Two spin liquid phases in the spatially anisotropic triangular Heisenberg model, Phys. Rev. B 74, 014408 (2006), cond-mat/0602180.
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0602180
Appendix A Eqs. (59)–(67) explicitly describes the established down-spin particle-hole conversion to a number-conserving Slater and determinant evaluation. It credits Yokoyama–Shiba. Appendix B Eqs. (74)–(81) proves Marshall signs under real symmetric bipartite hopping/pairing and a nondegenerate ground state. Again the transformed physical basis is empty/double on each site. Appendix D gives old Pfaffian/projected-BCS descriptions of short-range RVB via planar orientation choices. These results defeat claims of a first projected-BCS determinant description or first bipartite particle-hole trick. The numerical VMC procedure and O(L^2) derivative evaluation per configuration are not a uniform complexity bound for sampling every input. Local capture: cond-mat_0602180.txt.

3. Luca Capriotti, Federico Becca, Alberto Parola and Sandro Sorella, Projected BCS Wave Functions for Low Dimensional Frustrated Spin Systems (2002), cond-mat/0208371.
https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0208371
Their Eqs. (4)–(5) explicitly use exp(sum f_ij c_up,i^dagger c_down,j^dagger), fixed electron number and no-double-occupancy projection. The physical ansatz and canonical RVB interpretation are old. Their Fourier relation f_q=Delta_q/(xi_q+sqrt(xi_q^2+Delta_q^2)) also exposes a scope distinction. For ordinary nearest-neighbor square-lattice hopping and d-wave pairing, q->q+Q flips xi and Delta, so f_(q+Q)=-Delta_q/(E_q-xi_q), generally not -f_q as cross-sublattice pair-matrix support would require. A bipartite lattice or bipartite BdG Hamiltonian alone does not satisfy the candidate's premise. Local capture: cond-mat_0208371.txt.

4. Anders W. Sandvik and Hans G. Evertz, Loop updates for variational and projector quantum Monte Carlo simulations in the valence-bond basis, Phys. Rev. B 82, 024407 (2010), 0807.0682.
https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0682
Section III uses positive amplitude-product valence-bond coefficients and samples a bra/ket/spin extended ensemble with weights w_l w_r <V_l|V_r>. Combined spin-bond sweeps reduce O(N^2) work to O(N); bond-loop variants are also given. This is substantive old efficient practical RVB sampling. The displayed scaling is work per update cycle, not a uniform total-variation mixing bound in input bit length. Positivity of those bosonic valence-bond coefficients is an additional structure, absent for arbitrary complex F in the candidate. The checked text explicitly leaves autocorrelation performance dependent on the state. Local capture: 0807.0682.txt, Section III.

5. Julia Wildeboer and Alexander Seidel, Correlation functions in SU(2)-invariant RVB spin liquids on nonbipartite lattices, Phys. Rev. Lett. 109, 147208 (2012), 1203.6621.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6621
They rewrite planar nearest-neighbor RVB spin amplitudes as Pfaffians, reducible to determinants, using Kasteleyn orientations, then use positive spin weights |a_I|^2 and local amplitude-ratio observables. The supplement proves an ergodicity property of nearest-neighbor spin exchanges within a topological sector. The main numerical work uses 8000 equilibration sweeps and reports low measured autocorrelation on selected lattices; it does not prove a uniform polynomial mixing guarantee for arbitrary supplied pair matrices. Efficient amplitude evaluation and sign-free physical weights must not be equated with efficient independent sampling. Local capture: 1203.6621.txt, amplitude representation/results and supplement ergodicity section.

6. Martin Schwarz, Toby Cubitt, Kristan Temme, Frank Verstraete and David Perez-Garcia, Preparing topological PEPS on a quantum computer, Phys. Rev. A 88, 032321 (2013), 1211.4050.
https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.4050
Theorem 4 already prepares G-injective PEPS, including relevant RVB examples, with cost depending on the maximal projector condition number and inverse minimal gap of the sequence of partial parent Hamiltonians. This is old polynomial quantum RVB preparation under explicit extra promises. Those promises are not supplied by arbitrary rational F_AB,F_BA; the theorem is not the all-input rarity-independent guarantee under audit. Local capture: 1211.4050.txt, Theorem 4.

EXPLICIT COUNTEREXAMPLE TO A MARSHALL-POSITIVE SCOPE DESCRIPTION

Let A={0,1}, B={2,3}, F_AB=X=[[1,1],[1,1/2]], F_BA=X^T, and F_AA=F_BB=0. This is a real symmetric singlet pair matrix satisfying the candidate's exact premise. Hard-filter to k=2, so every site is singly occupied. In the site-ordered physical spin tensor basis, the coefficients of up-up-down-down, up-down-up-down, up-down-down-up are respectively 1/2,-1,1/2. Multiplying by (-1)^(number of down spins on A) gives 1/2,1,-1/2. No global phase makes all coefficients nonnegative. Thus arbitrary cross-support F is not a Marshall-positive class, even with real symmetric inputs.

Exact expansion using rational arithmetic passed in work/cycle4/bipartite_scope_checks.py; output work/cycle4/bipartite_scope_checks.json. The example is a falsification of the proposed scope identification, not a negative result for the count compiler. Classical Born sampling still uses nonnegative squared determinant weights, and the coherent construction restores explicitly computable signs/phases.

PRIORITY AND CLAIM WORDING

Defensible wording: "Conditional on the Chen–Liu counting theorem and the finite-bit prefix-count compiler, we derive polynomial-time approximate Born sampling and coherent preparation of canonical repulsively Gutzwiller-filtered opposite-spin paired states whose explicit pair matrix has cross-sublattice support. This includes hard projection and holes; the cost has no inverse original filter or number-sector weight. The construction uses a particle-hole rewrite preserving admissible load tables and quotas."

Do not claim first PH conversion, first canonical or projected BCS/RVB ansatz, first determinant evaluation, first efficient RVB algorithm, all usual BCS states on bipartite lattices, a generic sign-problem solution, ground-state optimization, or unconditional externally validated preparation. Relative counting, classical sampling and randomized-integration coherentization must be attributed to their existing sources. Exact priority of this physical rewrite and its quantitative coherent corollary remains open. A field-breaking label is unsupported by this audit alone.
