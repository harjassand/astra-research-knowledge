STRONGEST CLAIM

For the previously stated easy-plane XXZ class, replace each Euler gate I+hA by the rational quadratic gate

    P_2(hA) = I + hA + (hA)^2/2.

The gates remain positive and homogeneous log-concave in exactly the local representation needed by Chen–Liu’s homogeneous counting theorem. Their palindromic product has global generator error at most

    || m log(B) - beta(H + C I) || <= (43/60) tau^3/m^2,
    tau = beta D, D = 2C,

whenever m >= 2 tau. Thus a partition-function target epsilon needs

    m = ceil max{1, 2 tau, sqrt(43 tau^3/(15 epsilon))},

instead of the audited Euler schedule m = Theta(tau^2/epsilon) at large tau. This lowers the dominant imported-count arithmetic exponent from p^12 tau^24 epsilon^-14 to p^12 tau^18 epsilon^-8, up to logarithms, for one relative-epsilon partition estimate. Here p is the number of local terms. Charging direct evaluation of the product coefficient oracle adds one factor mp in unit-cost rational arithmetic; bit-length and scalar-output costs are stated below.

The result is conditional on Chen–Liu, arXiv:2610.06724v1, Theorem 5.1 and its rational-bit Remark A.4. It does not implement that FPRAS, validate its theorem externally, establish priority, or give a practical runtime guarantee.

INTERFACE AND SHIFTS

Let G be any loopless graph on n qubits and

    H = sum_(e=uv) [alpha_e (X_u X_v + Y_u Y_v) + gamma_e Z_u Z_v]
        + sum_v [b_v X_v + c_v Z_v],

where alpha_e >= |gamma_e|, b_v >= 0, c_v are arbitrary, and all inputs including beta >= 0 are rational. Set

    C = 3 sum_e alpha_e + sum_v (b_v + |c_v|),
    D = 2 C,
    A_e = 3 alpha_e I + alpha_e (XX+YY) + gamma_e ZZ,
    A_v = (b_v+|c_v|) I + b_v X + c_v Z.

Each A_i is positive semidefinite, sum_i A_i = H + C I, and sum_i ||A_i|| <= D. For edges the eigenvalues are 3 alpha+gamma (twice), 5 alpha-gamma, alpha-gamma; for fields positivity follows from b+|c| >= sqrt(b^2+c^2). C=0 is trivial.

LOCAL GATE LEMMA

Write p_2(x)=1+x+x^2/2. For an edge, the six-vertex matrix P_2(h A_e) has weights

    low = p_2(h(alpha-gamma)),
    mid = p_2(h(3 alpha+gamma)),
    high = p_2(h(5 alpha-gamma)),
    a = mid, b = (high+low)/2, c = (high-low)/2.

The hypotheses imply 0 <= alpha-gamma <= 3 alpha+gamma <= 5 alpha-gamma. Since p_2 is increasing on the nonnegative axis, |a-b| <= c. Also b >= c and a > 0, so c <= a+b. The four Hessian eigenvalues of its complemented two-qubit polynomial are a+b+c, a-b-c, -a+b-c, -a-b+c; exactly one is positive and the other three are nonpositive. This is the degree-two log-concavity criterion. All coefficients are nonnegative rationals. It covers the boundary |gamma|=alpha and the zero gate.

For a field, set r=b+|c| and write P_2(h A_v)=[[v,d],[d,u]], with

    v = 1+h(r+c)+h^2((r+c)^2+b^2)/2,
    u = 1+h(r-c)+h^2((r-c)^2+b^2)/2,
    d = hb+h^2 rb.

Here u,v>0, d>=0, and uv-d^2>0 because the eigenvalues of P_2(hA_v) are p_2 applied to nonnegative eigenvalues of A_v. The unchanged-row/complemented-column polynomial d(1+xy)+ux+vy is log-concave when d^2<=uv. Its rational homogeneous lift

    d z1 z2 + (u/2) x(z1+z2) + (v/2) y(z1+z2) + d x y

is also log-concave under this condition: rescale x,y to balance u and v, after which the four-variable Hessian has one positive eigenvalue iff d<=sqrt(uv). This is the same field lift used in the earlier audit, now applied to the positive rational quadratic gate.

Thus for every local gate the polynomial factor on four fresh variables has degree two and is homogeneous log-concave. Multiply all gate factors into P. Pair the two halfedges of every physical wire with (x_i+x_j), and impose the uniform-matroid polynomial e_f on the 2f field dummy variables, where f is the number of field-gate occurrences. Their product Q is homogeneous log-concave. If B=W W* is one palindromic layer and W is the ordered product of p local gates, then B^m has 2mp gates. The standard exact-once coefficient [x^U](P Q) is Tr(B^m); each wire chooses one endpoint, and the dummy rank f sums the field lift back to its original 2x2 matrix. No full 2^n matrix is an algorithmic input. Positive initial monomials are local: choose a positive diagonal term in each gate, one incidence per wire, and any f dummy variables.

SECOND-ORDER ERROR DERIVATION

Let h=beta/(2m), F_i=p_2(hA_i)=exp(G_i), G_i=log p_2(hA_i) >= 0, S=sum_i G_i, and B=F_1...F_p F_p...F_1. Put g=sum_i ||G_i|| <= hD. If hD<=1/4, then g<=1/4.

First, for 0<=x<=1, Taylor’s positive tail gives 0 <= e^x-p_2(x) <= x^3 e^x/6. Hence p_2(x)e^-x >= 1-x^3/6 and

    0 <= x-log p_2(x) <= -log(1-x^3/6) <= x^3/5.

Functional calculus and sum_i ||A_i||^3 <= D^3 give

    ||2S - 2h sum_i A_i|| <= (2/5) h^3 D^3.

Second, the degree-zero, one, and two terms of B’s noncommuting exponential product agree with those of exp(2S): they are I, 2S, and 2S^2. The norm of the degree-k tail of either series is at most (2g)^k/k!, so each tail from degree three is at most (2g)^3 e^(2g)/6. Therefore

    ||B - exp(2S)|| <= (8/3) g^3 e^(2g) <= (16/3) h^3 D^3,

using e^(1/2)<2. Each F_i >= I, so B >= I, and exp(2S)>=I. The integral formula for log and the resolvent identity imply ||log X-log Y|| <= ||X-Y|| whenever X,Y>=I. Combining these bounds gives

    ||log B - 2h sum_i A_i|| <= (86/15) h^3 D^3.

Since 2mh=beta and tau=beta D, multiplying by m yields

    ||m log B - beta(H+C I)|| <= (43/60) tau^3/m^2.

Choosing m as in the strongest claim ensures this is <=epsilon/4. The ordered eigenvalue perturbation principle then gives a multiplicative trace ratio between exp(-epsilon/4) and exp(epsilon/4). The same log-generator estimate controls the normalized Gibbs state by the established dimension-free bound ||rho-sigma||_1/2 <= tanh(epsilon/8).

IMPORTED COUNT AND TOTAL COST

The primary source was inspected live: Chen and Liu, “An FPRAS for Counting Common Bases of Two Matroids,” arXiv:2610.06724v1, submitted 5 Oct 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06724v1. Theorem 5.1 states an every-execution bound

    O(N^10 B0^2 rho^-2 log^2(2 B0/rho)),
    B0 = N + ln(R_P R_Q),

arithmetic operations and value-oracle calls for relative error rho. Remark A.4 supplies polynomial bit time on every execution for rational inputs and polynomial-length rational oracles. Here N=8mp, Q has coefficient range one, and log R_P=O(mp ell), ell=1+L+log m for rational input length L. A P-oracle answer is the product of O(mp) local rational coefficients; its bit length is O(mp ell). It takes O(mp) rational multiplications to construct directly. Thus the imported arithmetic core is O((mp)^12 ell^2 rho^-2 log^2(2mp ell/rho)); instantiating every value-oracle call directly adds a factor mp, for O((mp)^13 ell^2 rho^-2 log^2(2mp ell/rho)) unit-cost rational operations. Big-integer multiplication and the FPRAS’s other arithmetic remain charged by their polynomial operand lengths; the paper does not state a sharper bit-operation exponent for this composition.

For a relative-epsilon partition estimate, set rho=epsilon/8 and use m above. The gate trace error is exp(+-epsilon/4); estimate Tr(B^m) to relative epsilon/8; compute exp(-beta C) with a deterministic rational relative-error epsilon/8 interval. On the good FPRAS event, the output ratio is at most exp(epsilon/4)(1+epsilon/8)^2 <= exp(epsilon/2) <= 1+epsilon, and at least exp(-epsilon/4)(1-epsilon/8)^2 >= 1-epsilon. A range-reduced Taylor interval for exp(-beta C) needs precision polynomial in beta C, input length, and log(1/epsilon); a rational explicit output can have O(beta C + log(1/epsilon)) fractional/integer scale bits. This is parameter-polynomial in numerical beta C, not uniformly polynomial in binary log(beta C). The imported FPRAS has success 3/4; a 1-delta success target uses O(log(1/delta)) independent median repetitions.

At large tau>=epsilon, the partition count has mp=Theta(p tau^(3/2) epsilon^-1/2), so the abstract-oracle arithmetic core is, up to logarithms,

    p^12 tau^18 epsilon^-8 ell^2,

and the direct local-oracle arithmetic is p^13 tau^(39/2) epsilon^(-17/2) ell^2. The Euler version’s corresponding bounds are p^12 tau^24 epsilon^-14 and p^13 tau^26 epsilon^-15. These are polynomial-exponent improvements, not claims about constants or wall-clock speed. If beta C is fixed, the new single partition count scales as epsilon^-8 up to logs; the Euler schedule gives epsilon^-14.

PHYSICAL CONSEQUENCES AND PRECISION SCHEDULES

1. Largest eigenvalue. Let A=sum_e alpha_e, B1=sum_v b_v, T=sum_v |c_v|. As in the earlier exact shift audit, lambda_max(H)>=max(A+B1,T-A)>=(3A+4B1+T)/5>=C/5, while ||H||<=C. Set beta=20(n+1)/(epsilon C), so tau=40(n+1)/epsilon. A constant relative count (rho=1/8), a constant generator budget (epsilon_gen=1/4), and an additive-constant approximation to log Tr(B^m) suffice because n log 2/beta plus the log-estimation error is <epsilon C/5. The new m is O((n/epsilon)^(3/2)); the old Euler m is O((n/epsilon)^2). Dense p=O(n^2) gives abstract count arithmetic O(n^42 epsilon^-18) versus O(n^48 epsilon^-24), up to input and logarithmic factors. This is a conventional relative bit-FPRAS for lambda_max, conditional on the imported theorem. Since the scalar shift is subtracted in log space, no tiny exp(-beta C) rational output is needed for this interface.

2. Classical Gibbs measurement sampler. For computational-basis prefixes, use the diagonal open-boundary count with the same dummy rank f. Estimate both child masses at each of n steps with relative precision rho=epsilon/(8n), failure budget O(epsilon/n), and O(log(n/epsilon)) median repetitions. Normalizing two good child estimates costs at most rho in the next Bernoulli parameter regardless of prefix probability; rounding each probability to O(log(n/epsilon)) random bits adds the same order of error. The existing coupling argument gives total variation epsilon with no inverse prefix-probability factor. This uses 2n coefficient estimates, so the imported core has an additional O(n^3 epsilon^-2 log(n/epsilon)) factor; direct product-oracle evaluation adds mp as above. It samples the classical measurement law, not a quantum Gibbs preparation.

3. k-qubit reduced density matrix. For d=2^k, open k physical wires and pin the remaining ones. If boundary strings a,b differ in Hamming weight by Delta=|a|-|b|, the field uniform-matroid rank is f+Delta; keeping f would incorrectly delete nonzero X-field entries. Estimate d^2 entries and the normalizer at rho=epsilon/(100d^2), with median amplification as requested. The entrywise estimates and clipping proof from the earlier audit give trace-norm error epsilon. This adds O(d^6 epsilon^-2 log(d/delta)) to the imported count core, plus polynomial d x d matrix processing. For k=c log_2 n with fixed c, d=n^c and this is polynomial n^(6c). No global state-preparation circuit follows.

NO PRACTICAL REGIME CERTIFIED

The asymptotic schedule is materially better, but the imported exponent remains severe. Example only: for n=20, p=60, beta C=1, epsilon=0.1, tau=2, the displayed schedule chooses m=16, hence mp=960 gates and N=7,680 variables. Even before the coefficient-range term, rational bit arithmetic, confidence amplification, and scalar precision, Theorem 5.1’s displayed upper-bound polynomial contains N^10 rho^-2. Its hidden constants and full bit-operation profile are not reported here, and the FPRAS was not run. This establishes no useful machine-size threshold or practical thermal computation.
