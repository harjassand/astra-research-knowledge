CYCLE10 SQUARE MACRO-ANCHOR THICKENING AND SPREAD-MEASURE GATE
Independent Luna/max derivation; 2026-10-09.
Status: conditional finite-volume lemma plus a deterministic geometry extension.
No source theorem, frozen proof, or candidate archive was edited.

RESULT
The connected-anchor exponential-moment theorem in
work/agents/spin1_anisotropic_sol/cycle10_square_macromoments can be applied
when a possibly disconnected macro pin set K has a fixed graph-distance-k
thickening that is connected, provided the thickening consists of actual
vertices of the finite constraint graph. Condition on the free coordinates
in that thickening and integrate them back out. Their conditioned heights are
bounded by B+k, so every positive-volume fiber falls under the existing
bounded-data connected-anchor theorem. The original law is recovered exactly;
no new pin value is imposed on it.

Separately, the deterministic macro-coercivity step does not intrinsically
need connected pins. It works for any zero-pin coarse set carrying a fixed
Frostman probability measure of some exponent alpha>0. This follows from the
already-derived square hole-filling and trace estimates for arbitrary
1<p<2, by choosing 2-alpha<p<2. A covering radius merely O(R) does not imply
this condition and does not suffice for the present deterministic coercivity
route.

Neither result proves the square GFF limit, a zero-boundary trace theorem,
or the reflected comparison. The thickening result is a reduction to the
frozen macro-moment theorem, and the Frostman result generalizes its
coercivity geometry conditional on the frozen sparse-block input.

1. EXACT THICKENING-CONDITIONING LEMMA

Let G=(V_G,E_G) be the finite nearest-neighbor square-lattice constraint
graph, with free vertices V and prescribed fixed vertices Pi. Every edge
incident to a free vertex imposes |h(x)-h(y)|<=1. Assume the uniform law on
the free-coordinate polytope P_b has positive Lebesgue volume and each free
component touches a fixed vertex. All prescribed heights satisfy |b_x|<=B.
Let K be a finite subset of Pi, contained together with the tested support Q
in a coordinate box of diameter at most C0 R, and suppose diam(K)>=c0 R.
Let K_k={x in V_G: dist_G(x,K)<=k}, for a fixed integer k>=0, and assume
K_k is nearest-neighbor connected in G. Here dist_G uses actual vertices and
edges of the finite constraint graph; no absent lattice sites are added.

For deterministic q satisfying
  sum_x |q_x|<=C0,  max_x |q_x|<=C0 R^(-2),
  supp(q) subset Q,  diam(Q)<=C0 R,  R>=1,
the existing connected-anchor theorem implies constants a>0 and M_k<infinity
such that
  E_b exp(a |sum_x q_x h(x)|) <= M_k,
where a,M_k depend only on c0,C0,B,k and the frozen square-lattice
parameters. They do not depend on dimension, shape, component count, or the
number of free connector sites in K_k.

Proof. Let F=K_k intersect V be the free connector coordinates and Y=h_F.
The finite-dimensional Fubini disintegration of normalized Lebesgue
measure on P_b gives
  mu_b(dh)=int mu_{b,y}(dh_{V\F}) eta(dy).
For eta-almost every y, the fiber has positive Lebesgue volume and mu_{b,y}
is exactly its normalized Lebesgue law. This is ordinary disintegration of a
uniform polytope; it is not a comparison approximation.

For each x in F choose a path of at most k edges from x to K. Let z be the first prescribed fixed vertex encountered along the path from
x toward K. The segment before z contains only free vertices, so every edge
on it is constrained; since |b_z|<=B, this gives |h(x)|<=B+k. Thus every
feasible conditioned coordinate obeys |y_x|<=B+k. Existing fixed coordinates already obey |b|<=B. This argument
uses only constraints on edges with at least one free endpoint; it does not
silently use a fixed-fixed Lipschitz inequality.

In the fiber, Pi_y=Pi union F is the fixed set. K_k is contained in Pi_y:
each of its vertices is either already prescribed or was conditioned. It is
connected and has diameter at least diam(K)>=c0R. Its union with Q lies in a
box enlarged by at most k in each coordinate, so the box diameter is at most
C0R+2k <= (C0+2k)R. The q bounds are also valid with C0'=C0+2k.
Removing F cannot create an unanchored free component: each remaining
component either reaches an original pin or a vertex of F along an original
path. The all-zero fixed-data reference fiber has positive volume, since the
all-zero free vector has strict slack on every constrained edge and a small
open cube around it remains feasible. Fibers drawn from eta are feasible, so
any fixed-fixed feasibility conditions already hold there.

Apply the frozen zero-data macro-moment theorem with parameters c0,C0' to
this conditional model, then its bounded-data coupling with pin bound B+k.
This gives one bound M_k for eta-almost every y. Integrating the conditional
bound proves the displayed estimate for the original law. The constants are
uniform in y, hence also uniform over the whole conditional family. This
proves the wrapper, conditional only on the cited frozen theorem and its
bounded-data coupling.

Interpretation: a graph-distance-2 connected thickening is enough. The
intermediate coordinates are not prescribed in the original ensemble; they
are disintegrated and integrated. In each fiber they act as bounded pins.
This closes the macro-anchor condition for that class, but says nothing by
itself about a same-bath boundary comparison or a vanishing trace error.

2. FROSTMAN ANCHORS: DISCONNECTED ZERO PINS

Here is the exact geometric condition used by the frozen hole/trace proof.
Let N=ceil(R/L), where L is its fixed block side. Let S be the coarse square
of side comparable to N containing the coarse q-weights, with the fixed
larger buffer S+ used for hole filling. Let D be a deterministic set of
coarse cells on which the truncated representative field u is zero; for
zero physical pins, D can be the coarse cells containing pins, since each
representative and adjacent face center is within a fixed O(L) distance of
that pin. Suppose there are constants A<infinity and 0<alpha<=2, independent
of N, and a probability measure nu supported on D such that
  nu(B_infinity(x,r)) <= A ((r+1)/N)^alpha
for every x in S+ and 0<=r<=C N, where C covers S+.
Then the anchor part of the frozen simultaneous-coercivity lemma holds with
constants depending on A,alpha and the fixed square-lattice/block constants,
without any connectivity assumption on D. In combination with the unchanged
retained-density/overlap argument, it yields the same zero-data exponential
moment conclusion for weights in the anchored branch, conditional on the
frozen all-set sparse-block theorem.

Proof of the geometry replacement. The existing hole proof gives, for every
fixed 1<p<2, on one event of probability bounded below independently of N,
  ||grad v||_(p,S) <= C sqrt(E) N^((2-p)/p),
for the deterministic hole-filled field v and the good-edge energy E. The
existing trace proof gives
  |nu(v)-nu(1) mean_S(v)| <= C N^(1-2/p) ||grad v||_(p,S)
whenever alpha>2-p. Choose p with max(1,2-alpha)<p<2. The powers cancel:
  N^(1-2/p) N^((2-p)/p)=1.
Thus |nu(v)-nu(1)mean_S(v)|<=C sqrt(E).

The filling can overwrite a point of D, so it is not valid to set nu(v)=0.
Instead the deterministic filling estimate gives
  |nu(v)| <= C sum_z nu(z)(1+d_z),
where d_z is the local hull diameter at z. The frozen sparse animal bound
implies sup_z E(1+d_z)<infinity. Since nu is deterministic and has mass one,
the right side has bounded expectation; Markov's inequality bounds it by a
fixed constant outside an event of arbitrarily small fixed probability.
Intersect with the hole-energy and no-buffer-crossing events. The result is
a single event of probability at least 1/2 on which
  |mean_S(v)| <= C + C sqrt(E).
The coarse q-weight measure has mass at most C0, point masses at most
C0/N^2, and hence a Frostman upper bound of exponent 2 by square counting.
The same trace proof applies to the signed coarse weight measure w because its
total variation has the exponent-2 growth bound. It controls
  |sum_z w_z v_z - (sum_z w_z) mean_S(v)| <= C sqrt(E).
Since |sum w_z|<=C0, the preceding mean bound controls sum w_z v_z.
Truncation and the fixed-block representative error add only constants.
Every estimate is simultaneous over all endpoint displacements, since the
good event depends only on the midpoint bad-block geometry. The frozen
retained-density, overlap-loss, and Prekopa steps therefore apply unchanged.

This is a geometry substitution inside the frozen proof, not a new sparse
estimate: the all-set CAP/sparse-block premise, finite-dimensional convex
marginal, and overlap argument remain charged inputs. The proof requires a
fixed positive alpha. It does not say Frostman growth is necessary.

A useful sufficient condition from pin density. Suppose D is rho-dense in a
coarse square of side N, in the sense every site is within graph distance rho
of D, and rho<=C N^(1-eta) for a fixed eta>0. A maximal rho-separated subset
D0 of D is a 2rho-net and has cardinality at least c(N/rho)^2. A ball of
radius r contains at most C(1+r/rho)^2 points of D0. Uniform measure on D0
therefore obeys
  nu(B(x,r)) <= C ((r+rho)/N)^2.
For r>=rho this is at most C(r/N)^alpha for alpha<=2; for r<rho it is at
most C(rho/N)^2<=C N^(-2eta), which is at most C((r+1)/N)^alpha for any
fixed alpha<2eta. Thus any 0<alpha<min(2,2eta) works. Constants can depend on
the density constants and eta. This deduction is for coarse pin cells and
requires the dense square to be the same O(N) scale as S.

3. WHY O(R)-COVERING ALONE DOES NOT CLOSE THIS INTERFACE

A one-point set D={0} in S_N=[-N,N]^2 has covering radius O(N), so it meets
the weak statement “uniformly R-dense” when R is the box scale. But its only
probability measure is delta_0 and it cannot satisfy a uniform Frostman
bound with alpha>0: at r=0 the bound would require 1<=A N^(-alpha).

The corresponding deterministic coercivity statement really fails. Put
q_x=|S_N|^(-1) for x in S_N and f_N(x)=log(1+||x||_1). Then q has mass one,
max q<=C N^-2, and support diameter O(N). Also f_N(0)=0 and f_N is
nearest-neighbor 1-Lipschitz. On edges crossing the l1 shell r, its increment
is at most 1/(r+1), and there are O(r+1) such edges, so
  sum_{e in S_N} |grad f_N(e)|^2 <= C sum_{r<=2N} 1/(r+1) <= C log N.
A fixed positive fraction of S_N has ||x||_1>=cN, whence
  sum_x q_x f_N(x) >= c log N.
Therefore no N-uniform constants C0,C1 can make
  |sum_x q_x f_N(x)| <= C0+C1 sqrt(sum_e |grad f_N(e)|^2)
for every globally Lipschitz field vanishing on D. This falsifies the
current deterministic anchor-coercivity interface under only O(N) covering;
it is not a counterexample to the random-polytope exponential-moment target.

4. STATUS, PRIOR, AND CRITICAL REMAINDER

The exact source target required a nearest-neighbor connected fixed anchor
of diameter c0R (TARGET_AND_PREMISES.txt). The thickening lemma removes that
physical connectedness condition when a fixed-radius thickening is connected,
by an exact finite-dimensional conditioning identity. The Frostman lemma
removes it under a different, distributed-anchor condition. Both are
reusable reductions of the already-existing finite square macro theorem;
neither is claimed as historically new. The square programme status and
priority remain those in CYCLE10_UPDATE_12.txt (internal candidate; no
external/formal validation; priority unknown). The ASTRA task router was
checked; no topic card supplied this geometry lemma, so the proof is sourced
to the frozen in-workspace finite theorem rather than an ASTRA card.

Critical missing interface for a downstream square-GFF argument: a
boundary-incident reflected-comparison/trace result that is uniform over the
conditioned connector heights (or a direct comparison theorem that tolerates
the disconnected original anchor). Macro exponential moments do not imply
that comparison, a zero-boundary trace, stiffness, or convergence. The
separate boundary-anchored tilt proof is scoped to its stated finite zero-bath
and does not automatically cover these nonzero conditioned fibers.

Falsifiers / audit checks:
1. A counterexample to the thickening theorem must satisfy the exact finite
   model hypotheses while having K_k connected in the constraint graph and
   yet violate the inherited uniform moment bound. More locally, disprove
   some premise in the Fubini/bounded-data transfer, e.g. exhibit positive
   eta-mass of zero-volume fibers or connector values exceeding B+k.
2. For the Frostman extension, a failure of the generalized trace bound for
   some alpha>2-p, or a failure of the uniform local hull-diameter moment
   under the frozen all-set sparse input, invalidates the deterministic
   geometry substitution.
3. O(N)-covering alone is already falsified as a sufficient deterministic
   premise by the logarithmic witness above; this does not falsify the full
   moment theorem.

Charged costs and quantifiers: k is fixed before the domain varies; constants
may depend on k. The thickening route is uniform in the cardinality of K_k,
free dimension, reference system, domain shape, and conditional fiber y. The
Frostman route additionally charges A and a fixed alpha>0. Neither argument
pays an algorithmic runtime bound or asserts a vanishing normalized error.
