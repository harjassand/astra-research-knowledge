## Result: a candidate theorem package for collective thermal bound entanglement

The strongest outcome is a family of quantum states that can be generated from a completely unentangled, maximally mixed input by ideal collective thermalization, yet exhibit an unusual combination:

**Every one-qubit-versus-rest partition is separable, while the whole state becomes almost maximally distinguishable from every fully separable state.**

I developed this into all-size arguments, a local-measurement certification principle, quantitative entanglement bounds, finite-time preparation guarantees, and an **executed exact-rational four-qubit certificate**.

This is an internally derived research result, **not an independently verified field-breaking discovery**. Correctness and priority still require specialist review. The literature and repository checks identified substantial prior work that must not be presented as new.

## 1. The main mathematical result

Consider \(N\) qubits with collective spin operators
\[
J_a=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_a^{(i)}.
\]
Start with
\[
\rho(0)=\frac{I}{2^N}
\]
and evolve under
\[
\frac{d\rho}{dt}
=
\Gamma\left[(\nu+1)\mathcal D[J_-]+\nu\mathcal D[J_+]\right](\rho),
\]
where
\[
\mathcal D[L](\rho)
=
L\rho L^\dagger-\frac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}.
\]

Here \(\nu>0\) is the bath occupation at the transition frequency. The model assumes **uniform collective coupling**, with no additional local noise or drive during preparation. This collective thermal master equation, including entanglement generation from maximally mixed two-qubit inputs, already appears in Li–Xu’s work. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0505216)

Let \(\rho_N\) be the stationary state selected by this particular initial condition. The candidate theorem package establishes the following.

### Exact separation across every singleton cut

For every \(N\ge2\),
\[
\boxed{
\rho_N\text{ is separable across every }1|(N-1)\text{ cut}
\iff
\nu\ge \nu_*:=\frac{\sqrt{15}-3}{6}.
}
\]
Numerically,
\[
\nu_*=0.1454972243679028\ldots.
\]

The same condition is equivalent to positivity of every singleton partial transpose.

**The numerical constant itself is not new:** it is already present in Li–Xu’s two-qubit analysis. The extension here is the all-size statement, including the upgrade from positive partial transpose to actual separability across each singleton cut. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0505216)

### Nevertheless, strong global nonseparability

Write \(\mathrm{SEP}_N\) for the set of states that are fully separable across all \(N\) individual qubits, and use trace distance
\[
D(\rho,\sigma)=\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1.
\]

For every fixed finite \(\nu\),
\[
\boxed{
D(\rho_N,\mathrm{SEP}_N)\longrightarrow1
\qquad(N\to\infty).
}
\]

The derivation gives the explicit asymptotic lower bound
\[
D(\rho_N,\mathrm{SEP}_N)
\ge
1-O_\nu\!\left(N^{-1/6}(\log N)^{2/3}\right).
\]

Thus, for fixed \(\nu\ge\nu_*\) and sufficiently large \(N\), the state is separable across every singleton cut but is very far from having a fully separable decomposition.

There is no contradiction: **different bipartitions may admit different separable decompositions, without there being one decomposition into products of all individual qubits.**

### A logarithmically growing entanglement quantity

The argument also yields, in natural-logarithm units,
\[
\boxed{
\frac14\log N-O_\nu(\log\log N)
\le
E_R^\infty(\rho_N)
\le
E_R(\rho_N)
\le
\frac12\log(3N+1).
}
\]

Here \(E_R\) is relative entropy minimized over fully separable states. In the regularization, each of the \(N\) laboratories may hold arbitrarily correlated registers from multiple copies; I did **not** restrict the competing separable states to independent copies.

These properties coexist with an explicit stationary-state decomposition of **entanglement depth at most two**. Whenever the state is entangled, its depth is exactly two. Moreover, every subset of \(o(\sqrt N)\) qubits approaches the maximally mixed state, and entropy per qubit approaches one bit.

The distinction is therefore precise: **strong global separation from full separability does not require genuine \(N\)-party entanglement, a low-entropy state, or visibly entangled small marginals.**

For \(\nu\ge\nu_*\), singleton separability prevents the \(N\) separated parties from distilling pure entanglement by local operations and classical communication. Combined with global nonseparability, this gives multipartite bound entanglement in that separated-party sense. It does not establish positivity across every larger bipartition.

## 2. Why the arguments work

### Conserved spin sectors retain information that ordinary thermalization would erase

The collective jumps preserve total spin. Decompose the Hilbert space into spin-\(j\) sectors,
\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_j V_j\otimes\mathbb C^{m_{N,j}}.
\]

The initial maximally mixed state fixes their weights:
\[
w_{N,j}=\frac{(2j+1)m_{N,j}}{2^N}.
\]

Each sector thermalizes internally, but its weight is retained. With
\[
\beta=\log(1+1/\nu),
\qquad
Z_j=\sum_{m=-j}^{j}e^{-\beta m},
\]
the selected stationary state is
\[
\rho_N
=
\frac1{2^N}
\bigoplus_j
\frac{2j+1}{Z_j}
e^{-\beta J_z^{(j)}}\otimes I_{m_{N,j}}.
\]

This is not a unique-fixed-point claim for arbitrary inputs. The preserved sector weights are essential.

An invertible product filter converts the problem into the rotationally invariant operator
\[
F_\beta=\sum_j\frac{2j+1}{Z_j}P_j.
\]

Separating one qubit from the rest then reduces the analysis to qubit–spin-\(k\) blocks of the form
\[
aI+b\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf K.
\]
Their partial-transpose eigenvalues give the exact threshold. An explicit twirl of anti-aligned product states supplies the corresponding separable decompositions.

### Collective fluctuations expose the entanglement

The conserved sector distribution gives
\[
\langle J^2\rangle=\frac{3N}{4}.
\]
Meanwhile, writing \(M_N=-\langle J_z\rangle\),
\[
\frac{M_N}{\sqrt N}\longrightarrow\sqrt{\frac2\pi}.
\]

Every fully separable state obeys
\[
\operatorname{Var}(J_x)+\operatorname{Var}(J_y)+\operatorname{Var}(J_z)
\ge\frac N2.
\]
This is an established collective-spin entanglement witness, not a new inequality introduced here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1048)

For the stationary state,
\[
\sum_a\operatorname{Var}(J_a)
=
\frac{3N}{4}-M_N^2,
\]
so eventual entanglement follows from \(M_N>\sqrt N/2\).

That detects entanglement, but it does not by itself establish distance approaching one. The stronger conclusion required another argument.

### The reusable mechanism: a common-axis anti-concentration test

Choose a random angle \(\theta\), independently of the state source, and measure
\[
J_\theta=J_x\cos\theta+J_y\sin\theta.
\]
Accept when the outcome falls within a window of width \(w\) around a prescribed center.

This measurement needs only local spin measurements along a common axis and classical addition of outcomes—not full-state tomography.

For a product state, the outcome is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables. Fourier inversion bounds its largest probability mass. Averaging over the measurement angle prevents a product state from making the relevant outcome nearly deterministic in every direction.

The resulting bound holds for **every fully separable state**, with no permutation-symmetry assumption:
\[
\Pr_{\mathrm{SEP}}(\text{accept})\le B(a),
\qquad
a=\frac{2\sqrt\pi(w+1)}{\sqrt N},
\]
where, for \(0<a<1\),
\[
B(a)=\frac2\pi
\left[
\arcsin a+
a\log\frac{1+\sqrt{1-a^2}}a
\right].
\]

For the thermal target,
\[
v:=\operatorname{Var}(J_x)+\operatorname{Var}(J_y)
\le(2\nu+1)\mathbb E j
=O_\nu(\sqrt N).
\]
Chebyshev’s inequality gives
\[
\Pr_{\rho_N}(\text{accept})\ge1-\frac{v}{2w^2}.
\]

Consequently,
\[
D(\rho_N,\mathrm{SEP}_N)
\ge
1-\frac{v}{2w^2}-B(a).
\]

A suitable window makes the target acceptance probability approach one while the acceptance probability of every fully separable alternative approaches zero.

**This anti-concentration principle is the most reusable mathematical component of the investigation.** It applies beyond the thermal family whenever two collective variances are sufficiently small.

The regularized relative-entropy bound follows by applying this local test sequentially to multiple copies. Conditional on any previous outcomes, a fully separable alternative remains separable across laboratories. Its next acceptance probability is therefore still bounded. The classical relative-entropy chain rule then handles even alternatives with quantum correlations across copies inside each laboratory.

## 3. From stationary existence to actual finite-time preparation

I pursued the preparation question rather than leaving the result at an infinite-time limit.

Define
\[
c_\nu=
\left(\sqrt{\nu+1}-\sqrt\nu\right)^2.
\]
For the specified maximally mixed input, the argument gives
\[
\boxed{
D(\rho(t),\rho_N)
\le
\frac12e^{-c_\nu\Gamma t+\beta/2}
}
\]
whenever \(\Gamma t\ge\beta/(2c_\nu)\).

Thus fixed trace-distance accuracy \(\epsilon<1/2\) is obtained by
\[
\Gamma t\ge
\frac{\beta/2+\log(1/(2\epsilon))}{c_\nu}.
\]

The proof compares each spin-sector population chain with a reflecting constant-rate birth–death chain, while explicitly charging the initial chi-square divergence.

**That comparison strategy and finite-time thermal entanglement are already part of Astra’s N120 capital.** The repository refresh exposed that record before delivery, and the final note credits it. 

The further step here is to protect **exact positivity of the partial transpose**, which does not follow merely from being close to a bound-entangled stationary state.

For strict \(\nu>\nu_*\), put
\[
\Delta=4-\cosh\beta>0.
\]
I obtained the explicit stationary margin
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\rho_N^{T_i})
\ge
m_N:=
\frac{2\Delta}{9}e^{-N(\beta+\log2)}.
\]

Combining this margin with the mixing estimate proves that
\[
\boxed{
\Gamma t_N\ge
\frac{
\beta/2+N(\beta+\log2)+\log(9/\Delta)
}{c_\nu}
}
\]
is sufficient for every singleton partial transpose of the actual finite-time state to remain strictly positive. Its distance from full separability still tends to one.

Therefore, **for every fixed \(\nu>\nu_*\), actual finite-time multipartite bound-entangled states exist for all sufficiently large \(N\)**, with a conservative preparation-time guarantee \(O_\nu(N)/\Gamma\).

The normalization matters. These bounds use unnormalized collective jumps \(J_\pm\). With \(J_\pm/\sqrt N\), physical times acquire another factor of \(N\). Neither estimate establishes constant hardware or energy cost. The finite-time PPT theorem also excludes the endpoint \(\nu=\nu_*\).

## 4. Downstream capability—and its costs

### Distillation becomes possible when parties form two larger laboratories

Although separated individual parties cannot distill pure entanglement, two groups can perform joint operations within their respective laboratories.

The protocol measures each group’s total spin, retains a suitable equal-spin branch, and applies explicit filters. The resulting pair of spin systems has maximally entangled singlet fraction greater than \(1/d\). The implication from that threshold to distillability is an established Horodecki–Horodecki result. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9708015)

For fixed bath occupation and fixed selected spin, the complete heralding probability scales as
\[
p_{\mathrm{herald}}=\Theta(N^{-3})
\]
along compatible parity sequences.

In the four-qubit example, this is about **203 preparations per heralded mixed qutrit pair**, before the additional cost of distillation.

The restrictions are substantive. Both receiving groups need joint quantum operations; the heralded state is mixed rather than a Bell pair; and high-temperature filtering constants can be very poor. There is no uniform polynomial-in-temperature efficiency claim.

This is also not Smolin’s stronger unlocking pattern, in which joining one pair enables two still-separated recipients to obtain entanglement. That phenomenon is established prior work, with a different operational arrangement. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0001001)

### A post-preparation noise guarantee

Applying independent local depolarization with Bloch-vector retention factor
\[
\lambda>\sqrt{\frac\pi8}\approx0.626657
\]
still leaves detected multipartite bound entanglement for sufficiently large \(N\), at every fixed \(\nu\ge\nu_*\).

This is a sufficient threshold, not an exact noise boundary. It concerns noise **after preparation**. It does not establish robustness to unequal couplings or symmetry-breaking noise during evolution, nor preservation of the near-unit distance bound under fixed depolarization.

The prior-art audit materially narrowed the novelty claim. Li–Xu already supplied the two-qubit threshold and maximally mixed activation; N120 already supplied thermal activation and finite-time analysis; collective-spin witnesses and thermal bound-entanglement detection are established; and invariant-state separability has an extensive existing theory. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0505216)

The result also does not contradict separability preservation for fully excited Dicke superradiance: that result concerns a different initial-state and symmetry-sector setting. Here the full-space maximally mixed input populates all total-spin sectors. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13646)

The strongest candidate contribution is therefore the **combined quantitative structure**: all-size singleton separability, asymptotically maximal distance from full separability, logarithmic regularized relative entropy, an accessible local-measurement test, and finite-time singleton-PPT preparation with explicit margins.

I also attempted to close the \(1/4\)-versus-\(1/2\) gap in the logarithmic relative-entropy coefficient. Sharp individual spin-sector overlap bounds were insufficient after summation, and a proposed conditional strengthening failed on an explicit two-qubit product-state counterexample. That failed route is preserved rather than silently discarded.

Nothing here establishes genuine high-depth entanglement, resolves the bipartite NPT-bound-entanglement problem, or demonstrates a quantum-computational, metrological, or thermodynamic advantage.
