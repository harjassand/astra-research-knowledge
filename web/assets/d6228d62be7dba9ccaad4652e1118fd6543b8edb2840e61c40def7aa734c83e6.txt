## Final status

The sparse-input, high-contrast solver requested in A27 remains **not achieved**. What is established internally is (i) a residual-to-energy certificate for any supplied candidate inverse, and (ii) an exact recursive block-inverse formula with a conditional error recurrence. The unresolved premise is rank control for the recursively perturbed Schur blocks, with finite-precision rank-truncation costs charged. The polylogarithmic-contrast ordinary-ℓ2 rank result of Bebendorf arXiv:1410.3717v1 is downgraded: its displayed Theorem 4 proof uses a false auxiliary lemma, so it cannot currently supply that premise. The journal-version correction status is **UNKNOWN**. An earlier exact-rank path observation is withdrawn as an approximate-rank obstruction.

No implementation, finite-precision run, matched numerical comparison, or end-to-end termination theorem was produced. The candidate wrapper may safely reject; no success bound is proved. A02's factor-width obstruction is unchanged.

## Input and objective boundary

The intended finite input is the sparse assembled P1 Dirichlet FEM matrix (A\succ0), the shape-regular quasi-uniform mesh in fixed dimension (d), and a certified ellipticity lower bound (a_->0) after normalizing the maximum tensor eigenvalue to one. An arbitrary measurable coefficient function alone is not a finite algorithmic input unless its element integrals are supplied or an integration oracle and its cost are specified.

The target is a deterministic or Las Vegas construction of a structured SPD approximate inverse (M), with setup, storage, application, local eigensolves, and verification costing (O(n\,\mathrm{polylog}(n\kappa/\varepsilon))), and with a certified relative energy error. The record below closes the certification subproblem and an algebraic recursion subproblem only.

## Completed residual certificate

For symmetric \(M\), let

\[
R=AMA-A,\qquad E=A^{-1/2}RA^{-1/2}=A^{1/2}MA^{1/2}-I.
\]

If a rigorous computation gives \(\|R\|_F\le U\) and a certified \(0<\underline\lambda\le\lambda_{\min}(A)\), then

\[
\|E\|_2\le \|E\|_F\le U/\underline\lambda.
\]

Consequently, when \(U\le\delta\underline\lambda\), \(0\le\delta<1\), the matrix \(M\) is SPD and for every right-hand side \(b\),

\[
\frac{\|Mb-A^{-1}b\|_A}{\|A^{-1}b\|_A}\le\delta,
\qquad
\operatorname{cond}(A^{1/2}MA^{1/2})\le\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}.
\]

This certificate does not solve the original system or use a spectral oracle. For the stated FEM contract, Poincaré and mass-matrix norm equivalence give the mesh-dependent bound \(\underline\lambda\ge c_{\Omega,\mathrm{mesh}}a_-h^d\asymp c/(\kappa n)\), with the constants supplied for the mesh family. If \(M=ZZ^T\) with square nonsingular \(Z\), the alternative certificate \(\|Z^TAZ-I\|_F\le\delta\) avoids the \(\lambda_{\min}\)-scaled tolerance.

For a disjoint H-block partition and a low-rank block \(UV^T\),

\[
\|UV^T\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}((U^TU)(V^TV)).
\]

Thus the residual can in principle be enclosed from hierarchical products and block Gram calculations. A valid implementation must include truncation and rounding bounds, e.g. accept only if

\[
U=\|\widetilde R\|_F+\eta_{\rm trunc}+\eta_{\rm round}\le\delta\underline\lambda.
\]

No such interval H-arithmetic implementation was built. Uncertified floating-point residuals do not meet the theorem.

## Exact completed recursive inverse formula

This section makes explicit the algebraic recurrence. It is an exact identity for any SPD block input and SPD child approximate inverses; it does not itself establish low ranks.

At one recursive node write

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}C&D\\D^T&F\end{pmatrix}\succ0,
\qquad S=F-D^TC^{-1}D.
\]

Suppose recursive calls return SPD \(M_C,M_S\), and a computed SPD compressed Schur input is \(\bar S\). Define the returned inverse by

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
M_C+M_CDM_SD^TM_C&-M_CDM_S\\
-M_SD^TM_C&M_S
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Direct block inversion gives the exact identity

\[
M=\widehat B^{-1},\qquad
\widehat B=\begin{pmatrix}
M_C^{-1}&D\\D^T&D^TM_CD+M_S^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\]

and, crucially,

\[
\widehat B-B=\operatorname{diag}\big(M_C^{-1}-C,\ M_S^{-1}-T\big),
\qquad T:=F-D^TM_CD.
\]

The first child solves the principal block \(C\); the second child solves the compressed Schur input \(\bar S\), formed from \(T\). Hence the output error can be controlled without expanding the displayed inverse into a dense matrix.

Here is the local error recurrence, with its quantitative assumptions stated. Set

\[
e_C=\|C^{1/2}M_CC^{1/2}-I\|_2,
\qquad e_S=\|\bar S^{1/2}M_S\bar S^{1/2}-I\|_2,
\]

and assume \(e_C,e_S\le1/4\), \(\bar S\succ0\), and

\[
\|\bar S-T\|_2\le a\,\lambda_{\min}(B).
\]

Writing \(Q=D^TC^{-1}D\), one has \(0\preceq Q\preceq F\preceq\lambda_{\max}(B)I\), \(S\succeq\lambda_{\min}(B)I\), and

\[
-e_CQ\preceq D^T(M_C-C^{-1})D\preceq e_CQ.
\]

Therefore, with \(K_B=\lambda_{\max}(B)/\lambda_{\min}(B)\),

\[
(1-K_Be_C)S\preceq T\preceq(1+K_Be_C)S.
\]

The two diagonal perturbations in \(\widehat B-B\), the inverse perturbation bound \(e/(1-e)\), and the spectral bounds on \(B,C,S,\bar S\) imply, for a universal constant \(c\) whenever the right side is at most a sufficiently small absolute constant,

\[
\|B^{-1/2}(\widehat B-B)B^{-1/2}\|_2
\le cK_B(e_C+e_S+a)=:\theta.
\]

If \(\theta<1\), \(\widehat B\succ0\), and

\[
e_B:=\|B^{1/2}MB^{1/2}-I\|_2
\le\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}.
\]

In particular, for small errors there is \(q=c'K_B\) such that

\[
e_B\le q\max(e_C,e_S,a).
\]

The absolute compression condition above can be implemented as a certified operator- or Frobenius-norm bound; a Frobenius bound implies the operator bound. This recurrence is local to a node whose input is the actual SPD matrix \(B\). It does **not** prove that its compressed Schur inputs have bounded H-rank or that the necessary compression can be performed within the target cost.

For a balanced binary matrix split with depth \(D=O(\log n)\), exact dense leaves, and a uniform bound \(K_B\le K\) on the **actual** node inputs, unrolling the max recurrence along any root-to-leaf path yields

\[
e_{\rm root}\le\sum_{j=0}^{D-1}q^{j+1}a_j,
\qquad q=c'K.
\]

Thus the *inverse-error recurrence alone* can meet a root budget \(\delta\) by choosing, for example, \(a_j=\delta/(2Dq^{j+1})\). This gives \(e_{\rm root}\le\delta/2\). It is not an end-to-end construction: it assumes every node's compression at that error is available, SPD, and computationally rank controlled, and assumes actual node condition numbers stay uniformly bounded. For the original FEM matrix, a spectral estimate gives \(\lambda_{\max}(A)\le C\) and \(\lambda_{\min}(A)\ge c/(\kappa n)\), so exact principal and Schur references inherit \(K\le C\kappa n\). This reference bound does not by itself establish the same bound for recursively compressed Schurs. If it did hold uniformly, then \(\log(1/a_{\min})=O(\log(1/\delta)+\log n\log(\kappa n))\) under the balanced-depth schedule. This says only that requested bit precision would be polylogarithmic, not that the rank premise holds.

There is an additional reference issue. A principal block of an exact Schur complement is itself an exact Schur complement of a principal restriction: for \(S=A/A_E\) and \(I\) among the remaining indices,

\[
S_{II}=A_{II}-A_{IE}A_{EE}^{-1}A_{EI}
=\operatorname{Schur}_{E}(A_{E\cup I,E\cup I}),
\qquad (S_{II})^{-1}=(A_{E\cup I,E\cup I}^{-1})_{II}.
\]

This closure identity is exact. It does not allow one to apply a continuum inverse-block rank theorem automatically: the coordinate constraints may define perforated principal-restriction spaces, and no valid uniform rank theorem for those spaces, nor for recursively perturbed Schurs, was established here. One must prove that theorem and close the error-to-reference-rank transfer before the recurrence becomes an algorithm with the requested complexity. No circular rank-reset argument is accepted in this record.

## Rank costs and what remains conditional

If a computed H block has skinny rank-\(p\) factors, an exact truncated SVD of the skinny core after QR costs \(O((|I|+|J|)p^2+p^3)\). If temporary products have \(p=\operatorname{poly}(R)\) and every completed output can be reset to rank \(R=\operatorname{polylog}(n\kappa/\delta)\) at its allocated error, a fixed-depth-per-level H-tree would have near-linear \(O(n\operatorname{polylog}(n\kappa/\delta))\) setup/storage/application. But that reset premise requires a valid rank envelope for the actual perturbed Schur outputs. Rank existence for exact continuum Green blocks does not supply it. Frobenius aggregation over \(O(n\log n)\) blocks, all local SVD/eigensolve work, and interval rounding must be charged; none was implemented or proved end-to-end.

The strong baselines in v1 remain the relevant comparison: Bebendorf 2005/2007 H-LU transfers exact Schur rank approximants, while its Remark 4.1 explicitly leaves the ranks of practical truncated recursive H-LU intermediate blocks uncontrolled. Willems 2012/2013 gives robust adaptive multilevel convergence under its hypotheses, but the dense local eigenproblem costs and a contrast-uniform bound on local dimensions/coarsening are not established for the arbitrary tensor class in A27. A27 produced no matched solver benchmark.

## Primary-source proof downgrade and version status

The source actually retrieved and pinned is M. Bebendorf, “Low-rank approximation of elliptic boundary value problems with high-contrast coefficients,” arXiv:1410.3717v1 (2014), https://arxiv.org/html/1410.3717v1; the independently checked PDF is `work/applied/research/bebendorf1410.3717v1.pdf`, SHA-256 `88dcdcf0f49f459df80151ae5a84ef11123929f715f595f4897a6227b5b46688`.

The Appendix Lemma 8 states, for every harmonic \(u\in H^1(D)\) and \(K\Subset D\), a bound \(\|u\|_{(1,D)}\le C(D,K)\|u\|_{(1,K)}\), where \(\|u\|_{(1,U)}^2=\|u\|_{L^2(U)}^2+\operatorname{diam}(U)^2\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(U)}^2\). This is false. For \(D=B_1(0)\subset\mathbb R^3\), \(K=B_r(0)\), \(0<r<1\), and

\[
u_m(x)=\operatorname{Re}(x_1+ix_2)^m,
\]

each \(u_m\) is a nonzero harmonic polynomial and homogeneity gives exactly

\[
\|u_m\|_{(1,B_r)}^2=r^{2m+3}\|u_m\|_{(1,B_1)}^2,
\qquad
\frac{\|u_m\|_{(1,D)}}{\|u_m\|_{(1,K)}}=r^{-m-3/2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

The source's Theorem 4 proof, at lines 497--514 of the pinned version, invokes this lemma/Eq. (9) to transfer a local flux-domain norm estimate to ordinary \(L^2\). Therefore the stated proof of Theorem 4 does not establish its displayed contrast-polylogarithmic ordinary-\(L^2\) rank estimate as written. The exact counterexample and scope are documented independently in `work/applied/director_elliptic_source_counterexample_v1.txt`. This invalidates that proof chain; it does **not** prove the theorem's conclusion false, rule out another proof, or show low-rank approximation impossible. Accordingly, v1's “source-reported” polylog-contrast ordinary-norm rank input is downgraded to **unverified/unusable for the construction** pending a corrected argument.

Latest-source check for this bounded closure: the arXiv record exposed v1 only; `/abs/1410.3717v2` returned 404. The SIAM DOI page confirmed the 2016 journal publication and abstract, but the full journal text was not retrieved through the available route; the author-hosted metadata route did not expose an accessible full text. No erratum/corrigendum was located in the checked routes. Hence the statement “the journal version repairs this lemma/proof” is **UNKNOWN**, not false and not confirmed. No further literature search is part of this closure.

## Withdrawn exact-rank objection

An earlier selected-path construction for a regular two-dimensional P1 stencil yields an endpoint inverse block of exact rank \(N\), with \(N=\Theta(h^{-1})\). That fact is not an approximate-rank lower bound at the tolerances relevant here. The endpoint Green entries decay like \(c\rho^m\), \(0<\rho<1\), for path length \(m=\Theta(h^{-1})\); the corresponding singular values can be exponentially small while the full inverse norm is order one. They may therefore fall below tolerances of the form \(\exp(-\operatorname{polylog}n)\). **Withdrawn:** no numerical low-rank theorem is refuted by this exact-rank example. No claim that all singular values exceed the theorem's absolute or relative target has been established.
