ROOT AND WEIGHT CONVENTIONS

In the stated metric (X,Y)=Tr_fund(XY)/2, the Cartan coordinates e_i are
orthonormal. The Dynkin-to-coordinate conventions used by the audit are:

* B_l: lambda_i=sum_{k=i}^{l-1}a_k+a_l/2 for i<l and lambda_l=a_l/2;
  simple-coroot pairings are lambda_i-lambda_{i+1} and 2 lambda_l.
  rho=(l-1/2,l-3/2,...,1/2). Roots are +/-e_i and +/-e_i+/-e_j.
* C_l: lambda_i=sum_{k=i}^l a_k; simple-coroot pairings are
  lambda_i-lambda_{i+1} and lambda_l. rho=(l,l-1,...,1). Roots are
  +/-2e_i and +/-e_i+/-e_j.
* D_l: lambda_i=sum_{k=i}^{l-2}a_k+(a_{l-1}+a_l)/2 for i<=l-2,
  lambda_{l-1}=(a_{l-1}+a_l)/2, lambda_l=(a_l-a_{l-1})/2;
  pairings are the successive differences and lambda_{l-1}+lambda_l.
  rho=(l-1,l-2,...,1,0). Roots are +/-e_i+/-e_j.

In all cases rho has simple-coroot coordinates (1,...,1), and the root
counts are respectively 2l^2, 2l^2, and 2l(l-1). The exact checks also
verify that the supplied positive regular Dynkin labels map to the stated
open chambers. B_1 and C_1 pass; D_2 is correctly excluded as reducible.

FRAME AND D SPIN/PFAFFIAN AUDIT

For B_l and C_l, the odd-power evaluation matrix has determinant
  (prod_i z_i) prod_{i<j}(z_j^2-z_i^2).
The B/C regular chamber has z_i nonzero and distinct squares, so this is
nonzero. For D_l, the l-1 odd powers plus the Pfaffian-gradient column
w_i=prod_{k!=i}z_k have determinant
  (-1)^(l-1) prod_{i<j}(z_j^2-z_i^2).
The D chamber requires distinct squares but permits one zero coordinate;
the polynomial identity remains nonzero there. An odd-power-only matrix
does become singular when a coordinate is zero, confirming why the
Pfaffian repair is needed. When l is even, the Pfaffian gradient can have
the same degree as an odd-power gradient; the determinant still proves
independence. The SO/Spin equivariance is correct: a determinant-minus-one
orthogonal transformation flips the Pfaffian, but it is outside SO(2l),
and the Spin adjoint action factors through SO(2l). Both D spinor chiral
weight conventions are included below.

WEYL MULTIPLICITY AND COMPLETENESS

The adjoint weights are the roots above plus zero with multiplicity l. For
the stated simple-coroot coordinates, every B_l, C_l, and D_l adjoint
weight has each simple-coroot pairing bounded in absolute value by
M_ad=2 (including B_1 and C_1). Since delta_K=min_{lambda in K,i}
lambda(alpha_i^vee)>0, the source cutoff
  N_dom=ceil(max(1,M_ad/delta_K))
ensures (N lambda+mu)(alpha_i^vee)>=0 for every adjoint weight mu,
simple coroot, and N>=N_dom. Equality on a wall is sufficient: adding rho
makes the alternant strictly dominant.

The Weyl-character identity used is
  chi_Lambda chi_ad=sum_mu mult_ad(mu) A_{Lambda+rho+mu}/A_rho.
Above N_dom every term is the character chi_{Lambda+mu}. Only mu=0 has
highest weight Lambda, and its weight multiplicity in the adjoint is the
rank l. Thus the adjoint multiplicity in End(V_Lambda) is exactly l.
This is an alternant argument, not an assumed multiplicity-one/Pieri
claim. Frobenius reciprocity identifies this coefficient with the
adjoint-isotypic multiplicity in End(V_Lambda). The l polynomial families
then span the full isotypic block when the upstream quantum Gram is
positive definite; the multiplicity count alone is not being used as a
substitute for that Gram condition. Adding roots preserves either D_l
spinor coset, so the Weyl count applies to both chiralities.

FINITE-N CASIMIR AND RESET PATCH

With the report's trace metric the quadratic Casimir is
  C2(Lambda)=||Lambda||^2+2(Lambda,rho).
For every nonzero dominant integral Lambda, (Lambda,rho)>0. In these
coordinates the full simply-connected weight lattices and shortest
nonzero squared lengths are
  B_l: Z^l union (Z+1/2)^l, sigma=min(1,l/4);
  C_l: Z^l,                         sigma=1;
  D_l: Z^l union (Z+1/2)^l, sigma=min(1,l/4).
Therefore C2(Lambda)>=||Lambda||^2>=sigma. A central quotient only
restricts the allowed weight lattice, so the same lower bound remains
valid (or improves). The commutator Laplacian on End(V) has one scalar
kernel and Casimir at least sigma on every nontrivial summand. Hence

  E(A)>=sigma (||A||_HS^2-|Tr A|^2/d).

For ||A||_HS=1 and rank(A)<=Q<d, |Tr A|^2<=Q, giving
Lambda(Q)>=sigma(1-Q/d)>=sigma/d. This handles complex, nonnormal A as
well as positive A.

For any positive root alpha, Weyl's dimension formula is
  dim V_(N lambda)=prod_{alpha>0}
       (1+N <lambda,alpha^vee>/<rho,alpha^vee>).
An explicit fixed-cone bound for every N<N_cut is therefore
  d_max_bound=prod_{alpha>0}
       (1+N_cut L_alpha/<rho,alpha^vee>),
  L_alpha=max_{lambda in K}<lambda,alpha^vee> < infinity.
Every denominator is positive. This is the coordinate-maxima bound
described in the source, including half-integral D spin weights. With
C_small=N_cut^2 d_max/sigma, using this bound for d_max gives

  C_small Lambda(Q)/N^2
    >=(N_cut/N)^2 d_max/d >=1  (N<N_cut, Q<d).

Thus a reset code of error <=1 proves the small-N upper patch. At Q=d,
the identity channel has zero error. N_cut>=1 by construction, and the
N_dom equality case is valid because the shifted highest weights remain
dominant. No small-N polynomial-frame independence is required.
