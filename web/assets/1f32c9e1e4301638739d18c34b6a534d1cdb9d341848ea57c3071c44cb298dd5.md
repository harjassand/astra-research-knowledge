# Family149 transfer: prior art, exact scope, and control consequences

Date: 2026-10-07. Source-level review; candidate deduction, not external certification. Parent requested an independent literature/implications review of the bounded measurable-rate extension. During this review I found the stronger all-endotactic weighted-flux deduction below and sent it to root and the independent auditors.

**Main assessment.** The consequential claim is full permanence for finite endotactic networks under arbitrary measurable rates in a fixed positive interval. Merely treating feedback, time dependence, or common invariant sets in known strongly endotactic/single-linkage/planar subclasses is not new. The family149 manuscripts explicitly state fixed positive rates and weak reversibility. Their affine lemma and classwise plateau argument appear to support the stronger claim after replacing directed cuts by lowest-essential-source domination. This remains dependent on the correctness of that affine lemma; several agents are independently auditing it. A literature search found no prior all-dimensional general-endotactic permanence theorem, but does not certify priority.

**Exact proposed interface.** Let a finite embedded directed graph have complexes (C\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d), reactions (e:y_e\to y'_e), vectors (\nu_e=y'_e-y_e), and (S=\operatorname{span}\{\nu_e\}). Suppose the graph is endotactic. Fix (0<\kappa\le K<\infty) and a positive class (P=(c+S)\cap\mathbb R_{>0}^d). For every measurable (k:[0,\infty)\to[\kappa,K]^R), consider the Caratheodory equation

\[
\dot x(t)=\sum_e k_e(t)x(t)^{y_e}\nu_e.
\]

The candidate theorem gives one nonempty compact convex set (Q_P\subset P), depending on the graph, interval and class, which is invariant for every allowed rate schedule and absorbs every positive solution. In fact the entry bound can be made (T(x^0)) independent of the particular schedule and starting time. Thus one pair of positive lower/finite upper concentration bounds eventually serves all schedules and all starting points in (P), while the transient depends on the initial point. Polynomial vector fields are locally Lipschitz in (x) uniformly for bounded rates, so exogenous measurable schedules have unique local absolutely continuous solutions; trapping supplies positivity and global continuation.

The geometric proof also seems valid for finite real embedded graphs/power-law edge systems: monomials are smooth on the positive orthant and the needed lower bound is (x^y\ge h^{\|y\|_1}) on (B_h=[h,h^{-1}]^d), even if some entries of (y) are negative. This does not automatically cover generalized kinetics with an unrelated kinetic-order diagram; an endotactic realization of the actual power-law vector field must be supplied.

**Primary definition, including orientation.** [Gopalkrishnan--Miller--Shiu, arXiv:1305.5303v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5303v2), Definitions 3.9 and 3.14: (R_w=\{e:w\cdot\nu_e\ne0\}); the (w)-support consists of the (w)-maximal sources of (R_w). Endotacticity requires every essential reaction from that support to satisfy (w\cdot\nu_e<0), for every (w). Strong endotacticity additionally requires a strictly inward reaction from a globally maximal source whenever (w\notin S^\perp). The distinction between globally maximal sources and maximal *essential* sources is crucial. In particular, using (w=-r), the reactions from the lowest (r)-level essential sources have positive (r\cdot\nu_e).

An equivalent direct domination definition appears in [Craciun--Deshpande, arXiv:1906.08384v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1906.08384v1), Definition 2.1: each edge with negative (w\cdot\nu) has a positive (w\cdot\nu) edge whose source has strictly smaller (w\)-projection. Theorem 5.2 embeds bounded-rate endotactic dynamics into toric differential inclusions. This already organizes the same source-dominance mechanism; the proposed transfer's new input is the finite affine trapping/absorption construction, not discovery of inward source domination.

**Reconstructed usable lemma and deduction.** Take the finite slopes (r_\lambda\in[-1,1]^d) and affine functions (F_h(x)=\min_\lambda(r_\lambda\cdot x+b_\lambda(h))) from supplied family149 `sections/affine.tex`. Use the tolerance from `sections/trapping.tex`, with comparison directions consisting of coordinate vectors and all differences of distinct complexes. There is a fixed (H>0) such that, whenever (lambda) is active at (x=h^p\in B_h),

\[
r_\lambda\cdot(z-y)>0\Longrightarrow x^z\le h^H x^y,
\qquad r_\lambda\cdot\nu_e\ne0\Longrightarrow
|r_\lambda\cdot\nu_e|\ge2H.
\]

Set (L=\sum_e\|\nu_e\|_1) and (M=\max_{y\in C}\|y\|_1). For an active (r\notin S^\perp), choose a reaction (e_*:y_*\to y'_*) whose source minimizes (r\cdot y) among (r)-essential reactions. Endotacticity in direction (-r) gives (r\cdot\nu_{e_*}>0), and every negative edge has source strictly above (y_*). Dropping all other positive terms gives the actual weighted estimate

\[
\begin{aligned}
r\cdot f_k(x)
&\ge \kappa(r\cdot\nu_{e_*})x^{y_*}
 -K\sum_{e:r\cdot\nu_e<0}|r\cdot\nu_e|x^{y_e}\\
&\ge x^{y_*}(2\kappa H-KLh^H).
\end{aligned}
\]

Choose the common scale threshold to satisfy (KLh^H\le\kappa H). Then

\[
r\cdot f_k(x)\ge\kappa Hx^{y_*}\ge\kappa Hh^M=:\delta_h>0
\]

uniformly in (k\in[\kappa,K]^R), all eligible active labels, and all (x\in B_h). If (r\in S^\perp), the projection is exactly zero. All projection coefficients have been charged; simply comparing unweighted positive and negative fluxes would be insufficient.

Choose the representative (c) and final threshold (h_P) as in family149. The geometry of the plateau

\[
Q_h=\{x:F_h(x)=F_h(c)\}\subset\operatorname{int}B_{\sqrt h}
\]

is independent of the rates and the directed-cut argument. A slope in (S^\perp) is constant on (c+S) and cannot be active below the plateau. Hence every active slope off (Q_h) has the strict estimate above.

For measurable kinetics, replace the all-times right-derivative lemma by an almost-everywhere finite-minimum chain rule. Along an absolutely continuous solution, the finite affine minimum is absolutely continuous; at almost every time its derivative is the minimum of the projections of active slopes. Consequently (F_h\) is nondecreasing while the trajectory stays in (B_h), and increases at rate at least (\delta_h) while it remains in (P\setminus Q_h). Plateau invariance, compact continuation, and finite entry follow by integration. The capped square-root iteration (h_{j+1}=\min\{\sqrt{h_j},h_P\}) yields (Q_P=Q_{h_P}\cap(c+S)) without requiring nested plateaux. A uniform stage bound is

\[
\tau_h\le\frac{F_h(c)-\min_{B_h}F_h}{\delta_h}.
\]

Choosing one initial scale for a compact initial set gives a common transient for that set. If (S=\{0\}), the class is a singleton and the strict-increase condition is vacuous; the conclusion is immediate.

**A coupled witness outside the old scopes.** Consider six reactions

\[
2A\to2B\to2C\to2A,\qquad4A\rightleftharpoons6B,
\qquad A+B\to2B.
\]

The first five form a weakly reversible graph, so they are endotactic. For the added edge, a negative projection means (w_B<w_A). If (w_C>w_B), (2B\to2C) is positive with source (2w_B<w_A+w_B). Otherwise (2C\to2A) is positive with source (2w_C\le2w_B<w_A+w_B). Thus the union is endotactic. It is not weakly reversible because (A+B) has an outgoing edge and no incoming edge. It is not strongly endotactic: for (w=-(1,1,1)), the globally maximal sources have total degree 2 and all their outgoing reactions are (w)-tangent. The stoichiometric vectors ((-2,2,0),(0,-2,2),(-4,6,0)) have determinant 8, so (S=\mathbb R^3). Its class is unbounded, and it is not first order. The general transfer applies directly, with (L=34), (M=6), to all six rates in the chosen interval. This example is a scope witness, not evidence that the general theorem is true.

**Control, catalysts, switching, and periodic consequences.**

1. Bounded feedback (k_e(t,x,\xi)\in[\kappa,K]) along any well-defined closed-loop absolutely continuous trajectory is an allowed rate schedule. The same trap and eventual bounds follow, even if the upstream controller has complicated dynamics. Existence/uniqueness of the closed loop requires the usual assumptions on that controller; the inclusion argument itself does not supply them. Feedback dependence was already explicit in the planar work.

2. A chemostatted/external catalyst multiplies a base mass-action monomial by its concentration factors. If those concentrations are independently known to stay in a compact positive range, the effective rates have the required bounds. This gives a downstream persistence guarantee under arbitrary upstream signals. The catalyst bounds are supplied premises; they cannot be assumed for unknown internal species to prove their own persistence. General Michaelis--Menten kinetics, transport, delays and additive disturbances are not automatically covered.

3. For a finite family of endotactic graph modes on a common complex universe, each spanning the same (S), the labels can be built once using all complex differences. If each active edge rate lies in ([\kappa,K]), the projected estimate holds mode by mode using union (L,M). Every measurable switching schedule has the same convex invariant absorber, with no dwell-time requirement; edges may be off if the active mode remains admissible. The same is true for convex combinations of admissible mode vector fields. This is stronger than all edges of one graph being bounded below, but the common-(S) condition is essential to the advertised classwise uniformity.

4. For (T)-periodic exogenous measurable rates, the time-(T) solution map is continuous in the initial state and maps the common compact convex (Q_P) into itself. Brouwer gives a fixed point and hence at least one strictly positive (T)-periodic solution in each class. This is a standard consequence of the convex trap. It is an existence assertion; no uniqueness or attraction is obtained.

5. Under autonomous continuous locally Lipschitz bounded feedback, the same convex set yields a positive equilibrium. One proof applies Brouwer to time-(1/n) maps, extracts a convergent sequence of fixed points, and uses the uniform short-time expansion to get (f(x_*)=0). The fixed-point mechanism is classical; extending the invariant set to all endotactic networks carries the new scope.

For comparison, [Al-Radhawi--Angeli--Sontag, arXiv:2307.13678v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13678v4), Theorems 38--39, obtains globally attracting periodic responses under graphical Lyapunov, trivial-siphon and weak-contractivity conditions, with a compact invariant domain. The proposed permanence theorem can supply such a domain when its graph is endotactic, but does not establish those contraction conditions. The earlier [robust Lyapunov framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02086) seeks common functions for general monotone kinetics in restricted graph classes; this differs from a universal mass-action bounded-rate theorem. Their stronger convergence conclusions should not be attributed to permanence alone.

**Sharp falsifications of overbroad interfaces.**

- Drop the positive lower bound: for (0\rightleftharpoons X), take birth (e^{-t}) and death rate 1. Then (x(t)=e^{-t}(x^0+t)\to0), despite strict positivity of both rate functions.
- Drop the finite upper bound: the same graph with birth (1+t) and death rate 1 has (x(t)=t+x^0e^{-t}\to\infty).
- Permit only a weakly reversible *union* of switching graphs: turning off the birth of the preceding graph leaves (X\to0) and permits extinction. The union graph gives no return path at a particular time.
- Drop common stoichiometry in an arbitrary-mode uniform theorem: in two dimensions choose mode 1 (0\rightleftharpoons X) and mode 2 (0\rightleftharpoons Y). Both are weakly reversible. Constant mode 1 freezes arbitrary positive (Y^0), so there cannot be a compact absorber common to all initial points of the union class (\mathbb R_{>0}^2).
- Extend to all monotone kinetic laws without relative monomial bounds: (0\rightleftharpoons X) with forward rate 1 and reverse flux (x/(1+x)) gives (\dot x=1/(1+x)>0) and unbounded growth. The effective reverse coefficient tends to zero; this falls outside bounded mass-action rate uncertainty.

No trajectory simulation is required for these counterexamples; they follow from the exact equations.

**Resources and unresolved acquisition.** The theorem takes as supplied the finite reaction graph, its endotacticity, a rate interval, and a class representative. The finite label family, scale thresholds, offsets and resulting polytope must be acquired or constructed. Family149 proves existence by induction and compactness and does not provide a polynomial-time synthesis or useful bit/precision bound. The threshold (H), number of labels, smallest geometric neighborhood and final (h_P) may be extremely unfavorable. The explicit flux estimate above quantifies the dynamics *after* these objects are supplied, but does not remove their acquisition cost.

[Johnston--Pantea--Donnell, arXiv:1412.4662](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4662) already gives an MILP approach for deciding endotacticity and strong endotacticity, implemented in CoNtRol. This is graph-property acquisition, not universal absorbing-polytope synthesis. [Ding--Deshpande--Craciun, 2022](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/dcdsb.2022143) constructs minimal invariant/global attracting regions for two reversible reactions. A rational exact polytope certificate/search procedure in arbitrary dimension would require its own termination and complexity proof, and may substantially overlap the general existence theorem rather than create a fast controller design method. Root's certificate worker is treating that question separately.
