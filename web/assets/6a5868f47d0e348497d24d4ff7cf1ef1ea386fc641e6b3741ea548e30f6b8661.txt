FERMIONIC ALGORITHMS — CYCLE 2 LEAD RESULT
8 October 2026

Target and conclusion
--------------------
For an explicitly supplied A in Q(i)^(n x n), the fixed hard-filter target is

  Z_k(A) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J = empty} |det A[I,J]|^2,

with 0 <= k <= floor(n/2). This is the canonical opposite-spin BCS norm after
fixed total pair number and the local no-double-occupancy projector. Do not
replace it by an arbitrary CNF filter, an unfiltered Gaussian law, a parity
predicate, or the unrestricted charge coefficient.

Cycle 2 establishes an internally proved candidate reduction from general-graph
perfect-matching counting to the exact fixed-filter target, including a
one-query metric reduction and an approximation-preserving transfer. Thus exact
arbitrary-rank evaluation is #P-hard; an FPRAS for this Z_k would solve the
still-open general-graph perfect-matching FPRAS problem. This is a hardness
boundary, not an inapproximability theorem. A general arbitrary-rank FPRAS,
classical exact sampler, or efficient coherent preparation remains UNKNOWN.
The cycle also gives an exact paired-Gram/complementary-coefficient identity,
failures of both direct Chen–Liu log-concavity factorizations, polynomial exact
algorithms for bounded rank and bounded blocks, and severe counterexamples for
specified rejection and local-walk proposals.

Lead derivation: two exact coefficient forms
--------------------------------------------
Put X=diag(x_1,...,x_n), Y=diag(y_1,...,y_n), and

  f(x,y;u)=det(I+u X A Y A*),
  g(x,y,z)=product_i (x_i y_i + z x_i + z y_i).

By principal-minor expansion and Cauchy–Binet,

  f = sum_{S,T, |S|=|T|} u^|S| |det A[S,T]|^2 x^S y^T.

At site i the coefficient of x_i y_i has a unique completion from g if
(1_{i in S},1_{i in T}) is (0,0), (1,0), or (0,1), and none if it is (1,1).
The respective completing terms are x_i y_i, z y_i, z x_i. Therefore

  [x_1...x_n y_1...y_n u^k z^(2k)] f g = Z_k(A).

No multiplicity or phase cancellation is hidden in this identity. The
intermediate factors [u^k]f and [z^(2k)]g are homogeneous multiaffine
complementary-degree polynomials on 2n variables. For A in Q(i) with total
input bit length L, all determinant values and coefficient ranges have
polynomial bit height: clear entry denominators with a common D of log D<=L;
nonzero Gaussian-integer minors have squared modulus at least one, and
Hadamard bounds their maxima by 2^(O(k(L+log n))).

There is also a Gram lift. Let V=[I_n, A^T], let G=V*V, and let P(S) select
both columns associated with every i in S. For |S|=k, Cauchy–Binet and
expansion along the identity columns give

  det G[P(S),P(S)] = sum_{J subset [n]\\S, |J|=k} |det A[S,J]|^2,
  Z_k(A) = sum_{|S|=k} det G[P(S),P(S)].

Thus Z_k is a paired-principal-minor partition function, equivalently a
weighted linear matroid parity count. This is an exact polynomial-size
representation, but it is not by itself a polynomial-time evaluation
algorithm: there are binomial(n,k) pairs to sum in the direct expansion.

Exact log-concavity boundary for the Chen–Liu route
--------------------------------------------------
The direct coefficient identity gives a log-concave local no-double factor
candidate, but the determinant-squared k-slice fails log-concavity already for
the rational unitary swap matrix A=[[0,1],[1,0]], n=2,k=1:

  [u]f = x_1 y_2 + x_2 y_1,
  Z_1(A)=2.

At the all-ones point the log-Hessian has positive eigenvalue 1/2. Hence the
direct factorization does not meet Chen–Liu Theorem 5.1. In the Gram
factorization the volume measure is log-concave, but its whole-pair selector
is e_(n-k)(x_(e_1)x_(f_1),...,x_(e_n)x_(f_n)); that selector fails
log-concavity for every nontrivial 1<=k<=floor(n/2). The smallest witness is
x_1x_2+x_3x_4: at ones, vector (1,1,-1,-1) has positive log-Hessian
quadratic form 2. Differentiation/deletion reduces every nontrivial sector to
this witness. These are failures of two explicit routes, not a universal
no-lift theorem.

Chen and Liu, arXiv:2610.06724v1, Theorem 5.1 requires both complementary
homogeneous multiaffine measures to be log-concave, plus rational value
oracles, coefficient-range bounds, and positive support starts. The theorem
has polynomial cost under those hypotheses. The two exact forms above supply
the required degree, rational arithmetic, and range interfaces, but each has
one factor outside the log-concavity cone. This is the highest-ceiling
representation bottleneck surfaced in this cycle: find a polynomial-size,
positive, legal representation of the fixed pair-selector contraction that
admits either Chen–Liu's count service or another full bit-costed sampler;
or prove a lower bound ruling out the plausible representation classes.

Positive restricted algorithms
-------------------------------
1. Bounded rank. If rank(A)=d, factor A=UV^T and retain the two determinant
   replicas in four exterior spaces. Per site, transition by empty/up/down;
   never apply both occupied updates at one site. The final degree-(k,k,k,k)
   diagonal contraction is exactly Z_k. The state count is at most 16^d;
   normalizer cost O(n d^2 16^d) arithmetic and exact prefix self-reduction
   sampler O(n^2 d^2 16^d), with polynomial rational bit heights. This is
   polynomial for fixed d, not for arbitrary d. Proof and costs:
   cycle2/leaf-12/RESULT.txt.

2. Bounded blocks. If a shared site partition makes A block diagonal with
   width at most w, compute each block's unconstrained and no-double local
   charge polynomials by enumerating at most 4^w patterns, then convolve by
   exact coefficient DP. This computes Z_k, the exact Born sampler, and
   physical success probability W_k/Z_k in
   O(s 4^w poly(w,L)+s k w poly(n,L)) bit work. Polynomial for fixed or
   logarithmic w; not a generic sparse-graph result. A direct-sum family of
   unitary swap blocks has condition number 1 and physical filter acceptance
   asymptotic to sqrt(pi s) 2^(-s), so classical exact sampling and physical
   postselection expenditure must be kept separate. Proof/costs:
   cycle2/leaf-06/RESULT.txt.

3. Monomial/permutation support. The N89 cycle-transfer subclass remains an
   exact bounded-state route even when A has unbounded rank. This is narrower
   than general sparse A; see cycle1 leaf 18 and N89.

4. Path/cycle nearest-neighbour pair matrices. Suppose A has zero diagonal
   and its only possible nonzero entries join adjacent vertices of one path or
   one simple cycle. Form the bipartite double cover with row i joined to
   column j whenever A_ij is nonzero. For a path this cover is a forest; for
   an odd cycle it is one 2n-cycle, which cannot be fully selected under
   I∩J=empty; for an even cycle it is two n-cycles. A square minor has more
   than one matching term only if its selected bipartite graph contains a full
   cycle component. Disjointness excludes this except for an even cycle at
   k=n/2 and (I,J)=(Even,Odd) or (Odd,Even). Thus all other minor-squared
   weights are unique-matching products.

   Let w_e=|A_ij|^2+|A_ji|^2 for edge e={i,j}. The base coefficient is the
   size-k weighted matching partition function on the path/cycle, with edge
   activity w_e. For an even cycle at half filling, correct its two exceptional
   terms: for each parity orientation replace the sum of the two alternating
   matching monomial squares by the exact squared 2-term determinant
   |det A[Even,Odd]|^2 or |det A[Odd,Even]|^2. A two-state path/cycle matching
   DP computes the base coefficient in O(nk) rational arithmetic operations;
   the two exceptional determinants have only two product terms each and use
   O(n) arithmetic. Exact prefix/suffix
   masses give an exact classical sampler. At the exceptional cycle sector,
   remove the four exceptional oriented matching atoms by a constant-state
   DP that tracks whether each partial orientation still agrees with either
   forbidden parity string, then mix the residual mass with the two corrected
   atoms. With total rational input length L and a common denominator cleared,
   all masses have O(kL+n) bits; total bit cost is O(nk poly(n,L)) and exact
   sampling uses expected polynomial fair bits (or a capped sampler with an
   explicit abort-TV budget). A rational-Gaussian exact checker passes a
   six-site path, an odd five-cycle, and even four- and six-cycles across all
   14 tested sectors; this is finite evidence only. Its script and receipt are
   outputs/research/fermionic_algorithms/cycle2/evidence/nearest_neighbor_check.py
   and nearest_neighbor_check.json. No physical energy or gate synthesis is
   included. This is a fixed-filter subclass, not a general sparse-matrix
   algorithm.

What the negative results do and do not show
--------------------------------------------
- A rational bounded-entry perturbation of a directed 2q-cycle gives a
  two-heavy-state cut separated by an exponentially light bridge for the
  named one-label-exchange Metropolis chain. It proves torpid mixing only
  for that chain; it is not a hardness result for every sampler.
- The swap-pair block family gives exponentially small rejection acceptance
  for the unfiltered canonical Slater/BCS proposal despite condition number
  one, while the filtered output itself has a simple direct sampler.
- The exact no-double projector has local rank 3, unlike one occupation/parity
  branch of rank 1,2,or 4. A two-site filtered Slater violates Wick's rule;
  covariance updates after single Gaussian measurement branches therefore
  cannot handle this filter. Unions, richer states, and other samplers remain
  possible.
- Quartic unobserved-loss completion can be conditioned onto the desired
  filtered fixed sector, but for k>=2 this event is synthetic tail mass. Its
  small full-law TV error does not give efficient conditional samples or a
  count of Z_k.
- Code-weight enumerator hardness under an externally supplied parity filter
  does not transfer to the intrinsic no-double rule. Block-product Slaters
  give full-cube/frozen-bit supports, not a parity gadget.
- Exact Z_k is in #P after clearing denominators: nondeterministically choose
  disjoint I,J of size k and an integer below |det B[I,J]|^2 for a common
  denominator-cleared integer matrix B. This does not establish #P-hardness.

Exact #P-hardness of the fixed-filter normalizer
------------------------------------------------
An internally derived one-query metric reduction encodes perfect
matchings of a general graph directly into the fixed no-double target. Let H
have an even number m of vertices and E>=1 edges. If E<m, add new vertices
u,v, the edge uv, and edges u-w for every old vertex w. Since v is a leaf,
uv is forced and the perfect-matching count is unchanged; the padded graph
has m'=m+2 and E'=E+m+1>=m'. For simplicity rename the padded graph H and its
parameters m,E. Put D=E-m and N=E+D=2E-m, which is even. Build an N-row
paired-column matrix [B,C]: for every graph edge {u,v}, its pair is
(e_u,e_v); for each of D dummy pairs, use two private coordinate vectors.
Every selected N/2 pair set is an N-column square matrix. At epsilon=0 it
has nonzero determinant exactly when it contains all dummy pairs and its
remaining m/2 graph edges form a perfect matching; each such determinant is
plus or minus one. Therefore

  R(0)=sum_{|S|=N/2} det([B,C]_{P(S)})^2=#PM(H).

Every column of B is a coordinate vector, so B is a functional-map matrix:
B e_j=e_{f(j)}. Its nonzero eigenvalues are roots of unity and the others
are zero (equivalently, B^(N+r)=B^N for r=lcm(1,...,N)). Thus B+epsilon I
is nonsingular for every rational 0<epsilon<1. Define

  A_epsilon^T=(B+epsilon I)^(-1) C,
  R(epsilon)=|det(B+epsilon I)|^2 Z_(N/2)(A_epsilon).

For every selected pair set, the determinant in R(epsilon) has degree at most
N/2. Hence R is a nonnegative polynomial of degree at most N, and exact
interpolation from N+1 rational epsilon values recovers R(0). More strongly,
because each base column is a coordinate vector, every coefficient in an
individual determinant expansion is a sum of at most binomial(N/2,j)
numbers in {0,+1,-1}. For N epsilon<=1/2 this gives the conservative bound

  |R(epsilon)-R(0)| <= 8 (N/2) 2^N epsilon.

Taking epsilon=2^(-O(N+log N)) makes the right side less than 1/4. One exact
Z query, multiplication by the known rational determinant factor, and
nearest-integer rounding then recover #PM, including zero instances. The
entries of A_epsilon have polynomial bit length: for epsilon=2^-q, write
T=2^q B+I; then B_epsilon^(-1)=2^q adj(T)/det(T), whose numerator and
denominator heights are O(N(q+log N)) by determinant/cofactor bounds. The
dense target encoding is polynomial in graph size. The chart inversion is
also explicitly condition-charged: ||B+epsilon I||_2<=2 sqrt(N), while the
functional-map spectrum gives |det(B+epsilon I)|>=epsilon^N for epsilon<=1/2.
Therefore sigma_min>=epsilon^N/(2 sqrt(N))^(N-1), and
kappa_2(B+epsilon I)<=(2 sqrt(N))^N epsilon^(-N). For q=O(N+log N), the
the coarse upper bound is 2^(O(N^2)) for the exact-rounding choice q=O(N+log N); its logarithm is polynomial.
Exact rational construction does not use floating-point inversion, and no
physical gate-precision or condition-independent preparation is claimed.
Therefore exact Z is #P-hard under one-query metric reductions. After clearing
all input denominators by Q, Q^(2k)Z_k(A) is in #P: guess disjoint I,J,
compute the Gaussian-integer minor determinant d, and guess an integer below
|d|^2. This gives #P-completeness for the denominator-cleared integer-valued
function under metric reductions. Valiant's permanent theorem supplies the
#P-hard perfect-matching source.

The same perturbation gives an approximation-preserving transfer. A graph
perfect-matching decision algorithm handles zero instances. If #PM(H)>0, then
#PM(H)>=1, so choose epsilon from the explicit bound using this lower bound,
not the unknown count, to make |R(epsilon)-#PM(H)|<=delta/16. If an FPRAS for
Z is supplied with relative accuracy delta/4, multiply its estimate by the
exact rational |det(B+epsilon I)|^2. Its relative error plus the perturbation
is below delta; this is one oracle call with polynomial input and precision
bits. Hence an FPRAS for this fixed-filter Z would give an FPRAS for perfect
matchings in general graphs. The latter remains open in the cited primary
literature; this transfer does not imply NP=RP.

There is also a precise sampling transfer. Put w_t(S)=d_S(epsilon)^2,
P=#PM(H), and p_t(S)=w_t(S)/R(epsilon); at epsilon=0 the distribution p_0
is uniform over perfect matchings together with forced dummy pairs. If
Delta=sum_S|w_t(S)-w_0(S)|, then TV(p_t,p_0)<=Delta/(P-Delta), hence at most
Delta/(1-Delta) when P>=1. A TV-zeta sampler for Z therefore yields an
almost-uniform perfect-matching sampler after decoding and checking validity;
rejection of invalid outputs has constant success when zeta and Delta are
small constants, and a polynomial cap plus a found matching controls the tail.
The standard edge-exposure self-reduction then turns this approximate uniform
sampler into an FPRAS. Full sample and confidence accounting is in
outputs/research/sol_fermionic_filter/RESULT.txt. This is a consequence to an
open approximation problem, not a lower bound against all samplers.

Other representation boundaries
--------------------------------
The native four-leg no-double signature fails the arity-four
Grassmann–Plücker/Pfaffian identity exactly (residual 1); the doubled
bra/ket-equality tensor also fails it. Therefore the direct FKT/matchgate
contraction route does not handle all sites, even on planar/bounded-genus
underlying graphs. A fixed-genus network with m exceptional filters admits
an explicit 3^m 4^g Pfaffian expansion, useful only when m and genus g are
bounded. The source-specific tensor and ordering are in cycle2/leaf-14.

At half filling there is also a positive Fourier representation

  Z_(N/2)(A) = E_{sigma in {+1,-1}^N}
                 |Pf(D_sigma A - A^T D_sigma)|^2,

from minor summation and character orthogonality. This removes the sign of
each Pfaffian term but not rare-event variance: for d directed 4-cycle blocks,
Z_(2d)=2^d while the random Pfaffian-square estimator has relative variance
2^d-1. That family is also blockwise easy, so this only rejects naive uniform
Fourier averaging; it is not a target hardness result.
