# Approximate image penalties: gap tolerance and the remaining row-access gate

Date: 2026-10-08  
Status: **INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE** for the perturbation inequalities and the stated route-specific cost barriers. **UNKNOWN** whether the BMVZ complement-LCU image has any polynomial-row operator-norm approximation to a constant-gap enforcer. No general sparse-approximation obstruction is proved.

## Result

Exact annihilation of the LCU image is stronger than the sparse-Hamiltonian promise-gap argument needs. If an exact positive enforcer $C$ is replaced by a Hermitian row-sparse $\widetilde C$ with a certified operator-norm error below half the exact output gap, the completeness and soundness intervals remain disjoint. For the BMVZ LCU comparison, the admissible error is constant when the BMVZ NO energy is constant and the YES energy is negligible; it is inverse-polynomial when the exact output gap is inverse-polynomial.

This relaxation does not itself produce a sparse matrix. The explicit Neumann approximation to the incidence enforcer has exponentially many candidate row entries already at constant error, because each BMVZ clause acts on $R$ repeated five-qubit blocks. The existing polynomial-size rowwise-ℓ2 approximation to $C_0$'s rows also does not imply an operator-norm approximation: the generic conversion through Frobenius norm requires exponentially smaller row error. These are concrete obstructions to the two natural generator-based arguments, not lower bounds against every row-sparse approximation.

## Fixed interface

Let $H=\sum_j\pi_j h_j$ be the normalized positive BMVZ output on the audited family, and let $V$ be the complement-LCU isometry. Write $P=VV^*$ and $Q=I-P$. The one-sparse test $B=(I-|0\rangle\langle0|)_{\rm label}\otimes I$ obeys

\[
  H\le V^*BV=2H-H^2\le2H,
\]

so the comparison constants are $\alpha=1$ and $\beta=2$. The BMVZ hard family has YES energy at most $e(n)$ and NO energy at least $c>0$ on the canonical constant-gap output; retain $e,c$ symbolically below. The zero-padding used for the incidence route gives $H\le3I/4$.

The exact candidates already available are:

* $C_0=I-VV^*$, a positive projection with $C_0\ge Q$, so $\gamma=1$.
* $C_{\rm inc}=T^*T/16$, with $\ker C_{\rm inc}=\operatorname{ran}(V)$, $0\le C_{\rm inc}\le I$, and $C_{\rm inc}\ge Q/16$, so $\gamma=1/16$. Here $Y=I-H\ge I/4$, $Tz=(z_a-K_aY^{-1}z_0)_{a>0}$, and $K_a=\langle a|U|0\rangle$.

Both candidates have the exact all-state inequality $C\ge\gamma Q$ and $CP=0$. For $C_0$, specified-entry evaluation is polynomial but the exact combined row lower bound is $\lceil5^R/D\rceil$, with $D=M+1=\operatorname{poly}(n)$. For $C_{\rm inc}$, the inverse-dependent row access is open. See the Cycle 3 complement-LCU interface and connection-enforcer derivation in the read ledger.

## Exact promise gap before approximation

The useful sparse-Hamiltonian combination is

\[
  K=\frac{C+\tau B}{1+\tau},\qquad \tau=\gamma/8.
\]

It is a positive contraction when $C,B\le I$. YES completeness follows by putting a logical low-energy state into the exact image:

\[
  \lambda_{\min}(K)_{\rm YES}\le
       \frac{\tau\beta e}{1+\tau}.
\]

For soundness, take any unit vector $y=Vx+z$ with $z\perp\operatorname{ran}(V)$, and set $t=\|z\|$. Let $r=\min\{\alpha c,1\}$. In a NO instance,

\[
 \|B^{1/2}Vx\|^2\ge r\|x\|^2,
 \langle y|C|y\rangle\ge\gamma t^2,
 \langle y|B|y\rangle\ge
   \bigl(\max\{0,\sqrt r\|x\|-t\}\bigr)^2.
\]

If $t^2\ge r/16$, the $C$ contribution is at least $\gamma r/16$. Otherwise $t<\sqrt r/4$ and $\|x\|^2=1-t^2>15/16$; hence $\|B^{1/2}Vx\|-t\ge(\sqrt{15}-1)\sqrt r/4>\sqrt{r/2}$. The $\tau B$ contribution is then at least $\tau r/2=\gamma r/16$. Thus

\[
  \lambda_{\min}(K)_{\rm NO}\ge
       \frac{\gamma r}{16(1+\tau)}.
\]

The exact separation is at least

\[
  \Delta_0:=\frac{\gamma r/16-\tau\beta e}{1+\tau}.
  \tag{1}
\]

It is positive when $\beta e<r/2$. For the complement LCU, $r=\min\{c,1\}$, $\beta=2$, and $\tau=\gamma/8$. If $e$ is negligible and $c\le1$, (1) is $c/18+o(1)$ for $C_0$, and $c/258+o(1)$ for $C_{\rm inc}$. The weaker constant for $C_{\rm inc}$ is the explicit $1/16$ incidence-gap loss.

## Operator-norm approximation budget

Suppose a Hermitian sparse matrix $\widetilde C$ satisfies

\[
  \|\widetilde C-C\|\le\epsilon_C.
\]

If $\widetilde C$ is also a positive contraction, use $\widetilde K=(\widetilde C+\tau B)/(1+\tau)$. Weyl's inequality gives the concrete YES and NO bounds

\[
  \lambda_{\rm YES}(\widetilde K)\le
     \frac{\tau\beta e+\epsilon_C}{1+\tau},\qquad
  \lambda_{\rm NO}(\widetilde K)\ge
     \frac{\gamma r/16-\epsilon_C}{1+\tau},
\]

and therefore

\[
  \Delta_{\rm new}\ge
    \frac{\gamma r/16-\tau\beta e-2\epsilon_C}{1+\tau}.
  \tag{2}
\]

Thus the sufficient error condition is

\[
  \epsilon_C<\tfrac12(\gamma r/16-\tau\beta e).
  \tag{3}
\]

Taking $\epsilon_C\le(\gamma r/16-\tau\beta e)/4$ retains at least half of the certified exact gap. For negligible $e$ and $c\le1$, these half-gap tolerances are $c/64$ for $C_0$ and $c/1024$ for $C_{\rm inc}$. The weaker bounds $c/32$ and $c/512$, respectively, suffice only to keep a positive gap. These are operator-norm errors, not entrywise errors.

If $\widetilde C$ is PSD but only known to have norm at most $1+\epsilon_C$, use denominator $1+\tau+\epsilon_C$ instead; the numerator separation and positivity condition are unchanged. There is also a useful positivity repair if the row-sparse approximant is Hermitian but not PSD. Since $C\ge0$,

\[
  \widehat C=\widetilde C+\epsilon_C I
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  C\le\widehat C\le C+2\epsilon_C I.
\]

The repair adds only one diagonal entry per row. The raw positive Hamiltonian $\widehat C+\tau B$ has NO energy at least the exact lower bound and YES energy at most the exact upper bound plus $2\epsilon_C$; its gap is at least $\gamma r/16-\tau\beta e-2\epsilon_C$. Normalize by $1+\tau+2\epsilon_C$ to make a positive contraction. The same error condition (3) suffices. This also shows why approximate annihilation of $V$ is harmless at the promise-gap level: its YES cost is charged explicitly in $2\epsilon_C$.

More generally, if an exact sparse output $F$ has YES threshold $u$, NO threshold $v$, and gap $\Delta=v-u>0$, and the produced Hermitian family $\widetilde F$ has $\|\widetilde F-F\|\le\delta$ on every instance, then

\[
  \lambda_{\rm YES}(\widetilde F)\le u+\delta,
  \qquad
  \lambda_{\rm NO}(\widetilde F)\ge v-\delta.
\]

The intervals are guaranteed disjoint iff $2\delta<\Delta$. This condition is sufficient and worst-case necessary when the only information about the perturbation is its norm bound: independent errors may raise a YES ground vector by δ and lower a NO ground vector by δ. If the matrix is (C+\tau B) and both pieces are approximated, replace $\delta$ by $\epsilon_C+\tau\epsilon_B+\epsilon_{\rm arithmetic}$ before common normalization.

For an inverse-polynomial exact gap $\Delta=1/\operatorname{poly}(n)$, it suffices to certify operator error $<\Delta/2$, which costs $O(\log n)$ precision bits in the error parameter. If a row-sparse Hermitian approximation has at most $s$ listed entries per row and each listed entry is rounded with absolute error at most $\zeta$ using symmetric rounding, then $\|E_{\rm entries}\|\le s\zeta$ by the maximum absolute row-sum bound. Charge $s\zeta$ inside the total operator-error budget; $O(\log(s/\text{slack}))$ bits per entry suffice once an operator-norm truncation certificate is supplied. Entrywise precision alone does not certify the omitted-entry error.

For the circuit-defined canonical projector, if a compiled unitary $\widetilde U$ satisfies $\|\widetilde U-U\|\le\eta_U$, then $\|\widetilde U B\widetilde U^*-UBU^*\|\le2\eta_U$. An $L$-gate product compiled with per-gate operator error at most $\zeta_g$ has $\eta_U\le L\zeta_g$ by telescoping, so the circuit contribution fits an $\epsilon_C$ budget if $\zeta_g\le\epsilon_C/(2L)$. This charges precision only; $\widetilde U B\widetilde U^*$ still has no polynomial row-list guarantee.

## Obstruction 1: the direct Neumann row expansion is exponential

For $C_{\rm inc}$, $Y=I-H\ge I/4$ and $\|H\|\le3/4$. The Neumann polynomial

\[
  M_d=\sum_{j=0}^d H^j
\]

approximates $Y^{-1}$ in operator norm with

\[
  \|Y^{-1}-M_d\|\le4(3/4)^{d+1}.
  \tag{4}
\]

Let $T(M)z=(z_a-K_aMz_0)_{a>0}$. Since $\|[K_a]_{a>0}\|\le1$, $\|T(Y^{-1})\|\le4$, and

\[
  \left\|\frac{T(M_d)^*T(M_d)-T(Y^{-1})^*T(Y^{-1})}{16}\right\|
     \le\frac{(8+\delta_Y)\delta_Y}{16},
  \qquad \delta_Y=\|M_d-Y^{-1}\|.
  \tag{5}
\]

For a target $C_{\rm inc}$-error $\epsilon$, it is enough to set $\delta_Y\le\epsilon$ (for $\epsilon\le8$), so (4) needs only $d=O(\log(1/\epsilon))$. The approximation degree is not the bottleneck.

The row cost is. For a five-qubit local projection $P_{j,r}$, the row degree of $Q_{j,r}=I-P_{j,r}$ is at most $32$; hence the exact repeated clause $Q_j=\otimes_{r=1}^R Q_{j,r}$ has row degree at most $32^R$, and $h_j=I-Q_j$ at most $32^R+1$. With $M=\operatorname{poly}(n)$ clauses one may bound

\[
 s_H\le1+M(32^R+1),\qquad
 s(M_d)\le\sum_{j=0}^d s_H^j\le(d+1)s_H^d.
\]

Each $K_a$ is a sum of $M+1$ clause reflections, so a literal row enumeration has $s_K\le(M+1)(32^R+1)$. The rows of $T(M_d)$ then have at most $s_T=1+s_Ks(M_d)$ candidate entries. A row of $T^*T$ can be supported on at most $d_{\rm out}s_T$ columns, where $d_{\rm out}=M2^N$ bounds the number of output rows of $T$: each nonzero row of $T$ contributes at most $s_T$ columns, and a fixed input column can occur in at most $d_{\rm out}$ such rows. Thus the direct product has the safe bound

$$
s(C_{\rm inc,d})\le M2^N\left[1+(M+1)(32^R+1)(d+1)\bigl(1+M(32^R+1)\bigr)^d\right].
$$

At the error budget in (3), $d\ge1$ for the stated inverse route: the augmented H contains the positive-weight repeated seed projection $h_\psi$ with weight $3/16$, so $\|H\|\ge3/16$, and consequently $\|Y^{-1}-I\|\ge3/16>\epsilon$ for the listed constant-error targets. The literal expansion therefore includes the $H$ term. The appended repeated $\psi$ seed has an exact row of $h_\psi$ with $4^R$ nonzeros; the full normalized $H$ row has at least $2^R+4^R-1$ nonzeros on the audited augmentation. Thus term-by-term row expansion already visits exponentially many candidates before it combines the polynomial in $H$. The safe candidate upper bound above is $2^{N+O(R(d+1))}\operatorname{poly}(M,d)$. This is a cost obstruction to the literal row-enumeration method, not a lower bound on the final nonzero support after cancellations. The polynomial-time specified-entry formula does not reduce this row-output cost.

This is a route-specific obstruction: the direct term-expansion row oracle for the truncated-Neumann construction has superpolynomial candidate output. It does not rule out an algebraic simplification or a different sparse approximant; cancellations in $T^*T$ have not been ruled out by this count.

## Obstruction 2: rowwise ℓ2 truncation does not certify operator error

For the fixed BJSW local alphabet, the Cycle 3 lemma gives, for every computational-basis row of $P=VV^*$, a set of size $\operatorname{poly}(M,1/\rho)$ outside which the row $\ell_2$ norm is at most $\rho$. The proof truncates tensor-product rows with many nontrivial copy factors. This is valuable row-mass information, but it is not an operator-norm certificate.

For a (d_{\rm phys}\)-dimensional matrix error $E$, if every row has ℓ2 norm at most ρ then

\[
  \|E\|\le\|E\|_F\le\sqrt{d_{\rm phys}}\,\rho.
  \tag{6}
\]

Thus the direct Frobenius lift needs $\rho\le\epsilon/\sqrt{d_{\rm phys}}$. Here $d_{\rm phys}=(M+1)2^N$, with $N=\operatorname{poly}(n)$ BMVZ data qubits, so substituting this precision into the rowwise lemma gives a support bound polynomial in $M,\sqrt{d_{\rm phys}}/\epsilon$, which is exponential in the encoded input size. It does not produce a polynomial row list. If the per-row truncations are not Hermitian, symmetrizing them also needs a separate row-degree and error proof; no such bound follows from rowwise existence alone.

The gap is not a technicality of the inequality. Let $u=d^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)$ and $P_u=|u\rangle\langle u|$. Every row of $P_u$ has $\ell_2$ norm $1/\sqrt d$, so the zero matrix approximates every row with vanishing $\ell_2$ error as $d\to\infty$, while $\|P_u-0\|=1$. This is a counterexample to inferring operator-norm approximation from dimension-dependent rowwise $\ell_2$ tails. It is not a counterexample about the particular LCU matrix.

Also, the Cycle 3 lower bound $s(C_0)\ge\lceil5^R/D\rceil$ concerns **exact** nonzeros. It does not imply a constant-error lower bound: small exact entries may be deleted, and the fixed-alphabet row lemma in fact concentrates each individual row's $\ell_2$ mass on polynomial support. No flat-row or other operator witness currently upgrades that exact support lower bound to a constant operator-norm approximation lower bound.

## Conclusion and scope

Approximate penalties preserve the sparse-Hamiltonian promise-gap consequence under the explicit norm budget (2)–(3). They remove the exact-kernel requirement from that consequence but leave the central representation question intact: obtain a polynomial-output row oracle for a Hermitian approximant with a certified operator-norm error in the stated budget. The literal Neumann term expansion does not furnish a polynomial row oracle, and rowwise $\ell_2$ truncation is not an operator-norm proof. No construction or universal impossibility theorem for a polynomial-row $\widetilde C$ at the required constant error is established here.

N26 does not decide this gate. Its sharp bound assumes a positive budget-one decomposition into terms each reading at most $r$ fixed independent syndrome bits. Arbitrary row-sparse $\widetilde C$ does not imply such a decomposition. N11's commuting-repetition tail theorem supplies no finite-dimensional row oracle or common positive sparse encoding. These remain separate interfaces.
