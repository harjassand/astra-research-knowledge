3. Literal source-square retention is impossible after another B transition

THEOREM (specific source pair). For every completely positive linear map
R:M10(C)->M6(C) and every real lambda>0,
  B R B S != lambda B S
as linear maps on M10(C).
This rules out keeping the exact source square unchanged up to a positive
scalar by routing its output through R and applying B again. In particular
R may be any rational or complex isometric compression, any unitary-subspace
alignment, or a sum of CP branches. The theorem permits arbitrary CP R;
there is no trace-preserving premise.

Proof. Suppose equality holds, and set C=R B:M6->M6. Since B is PPT and R is
CP, C is PPT. Since B T6=B, C T6=C. Let
  F=(C+T6 C)/2.
Both summands are CP, so F is CP; it has input and output transpose
invariance. As B also satisfies B T6=B, we have B F=B C. Surjectivity
range(S)=W therefore gives
  B F(Y)=lambda B(Y)  for every Y in W.
The symmetric-kernel statement implies F(Y)-lambda Y is in C K for Y in W.

Consequently there are real symmetric matrices H,Z such that, on all complex
matrices,
  F(X)=lambda (X+X^T)/2 + Tr(HX)K + Tr(KX)Z.
To justify the form, first restrict F to Sym_6. On the codimension-one
subspace W its difference from lambda identity has range in C K, so it is
a scalar linear functional times K. The action on the complementary line
C K is represented by the second functional Tr(KX) times a symmetric matrix.
F annihilates antisymmetric inputs, extending this form to all M6. Because F
preserves Hermiticity and both transpose symmetries, the coefficient matrices
can be chosen real symmetric. Numerical values of H,Z are not assumed.

In reference-first Choi convention its matrix is
  J(F)=lambda J(Psym)+H tensor K+K tensor Z,
  J(Psym)=(Omega Omega^T+Flip)/2, Psym=(Id+T6)/2.
Use a real orthonormal basis diagonalizing K as diag(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1).
This simultaneous real change of basis preserves J(Psym), positivity, and
the real symmetric form of H,Z. Put h_i=H_ii, z_i=Z_ii, a_i=h_i+z_i.
For w_ij=e_i tensor e_j-e_j tensor e_i, with i!=j,
  w_ij* J(F) w_ij = -lambda + k_j a_i + k_i a_j.
The Omega term vanishes, Flip acts by -1, and the off-diagonal H/Z terms
vanish because K is diagonal. CP would require all these values nonnegative.

For i,j among the three positive indices, this gives a_i+a_j>=lambda.
For i,j among the three negative indices, it gives a_i+a_j<=-lambda.
For positive i and negative j, it gives a_j-a_i>=lambda.
Let Aplus and Aminus be the sums of the three a_i in the two groups.
Summing the three within-positive inequalities gives 2 Aplus>=3 lambda.
Summing the three within-negative inequalities gives 2 Aminus<=-3 lambda.
Hence Aminus-Aplus<=-3 lambda.
Summing all nine cross inequalities gives
  3 Aminus-3 Aplus>=9 lambda,
or Aminus-Aplus>=3 lambda. This is impossible for lambda>0. Thus F cannot
be CP, contradicting its construction, and proving the theorem.

Scope. This is a no-go for the displayed literal-retention equation. It does
not show every B R B S is EB, rule out a different nonseparability witness,
or classify arbitrary local-filter transfers. Common invertible input and
output filters applied to BOTH sides of the displayed equation can be
cancelled and do not evade it. Different filters or a different triple
factorization require a new argument.
