SU(3) WALL: EXACT U(2) ZERO-WEIGHT FIBER LABELS AND THE MISSING
WEIGHTED-BLOCK COMPARISON
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact branching and a conditional counting reduction; the required
comparison for L_S is unresolved. No counterexample to the weighted count
has been found here.

1. SETUP AND NORMALIZATION

Let a>=b>=1, N=a+b, lambda=(a,b), and use the normalization

  Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2,
  c2(p,q)=(p^2+pq+q^2+3p+3q)/3,
  L_T=sum_A ad(T_A)^2,  L_S=sum_A ad(S_A)^2,
  L_w=L_T/N^2+L_S/(N sqrt(C_S)).

The supplied carrier dimension is
  d=(a+1)(b+1)(N+2)/2.
For a>=b, the exact parameter identity is

  C_S/[b(b+2)] = a(a+2)(N+1)(N+3)/(12 C2 N^2),
  C2=(a^2+ab+b^2+3N)/3.                              (1.1)

Thus sqrt(C_S) is comparable to B=b+1 with absolute constants for
a>=b>=1. Along a/N->1, b/N->0, the factor in (1.1) tends to 1/4;
in particular sqrt(C_S)~(1/2)sqrt(b(b+2)) if b grows, and is of order
one when b is fixed. On End(V_lambda)=direct_sum_nu V_nu tensor M_nu,

  L_w|_nu = c2(nu)/N^2 I + K_nu/(N sqrt(C_S)),           (1.2)

where K_nu is the positive matrix induced by L_S on M_nu. The weighted
count at issue is, with the scalar mode excluded,

  D_w(t)=sum_nu dim(V_nu) # {eigenvalues kappa of K_nu:
                    c2(nu)/N^2+kappa/(N sqrt(C_S))<=t}
       <= C d[(N^2/B)t^2+N^3 t^3],  0<t<=c0/N.           (1.3)

The sum is over root-lattice highest weights because End(V_lambda) has
trivial central character.

2. EXACT SU(2)-FIBER LABELS INSIDE V_(p,q)[0]

Use the standard U(3) lift of V_(p,q), with highest row
(p+q,q,0), and restrict to the U(2) acting on the first two
coordinates. Gelfand-Tsetlin interlacing gives middle rows

  p+q >= mu_1 >= q >= mu_2 >= 0.                         (2.1)

Put s=(p+2q)/3. A zero SU(3)-weight is possible exactly when s is an
integer, equivalently p congruent q (mod 3). In a GT pattern the bottom
entry is then s and the middle-row sum is 2s. Write

  (mu_1,mu_2)=(s+j,s-j).

The U(2) branch has SU(2) spin j. Its unique weight with equal first
two coordinates is the zero SU(3)-weight vector. Applying every bound
in (2.1) gives exactly

  |p-q|/3 <= j <= (p+q+min(p,q))/3,  j in Z,            (2.2)

and every integer in this interval occurs once. Consequently

  dim V_(p,q)[0] = min(p,q)+1       if p congruent q (mod 3),
                   0               otherwise.            (2.3)

The two labels that vanish at the CP^2/base edge are visible in (2.2):
the smallest allowed spin is |p-q|/3, while j increases in unit steps.
Thus a small-spin restriction forces q close to p and also keeps the
branch index j close to its lower endpoint. This is the precise
two-fiber-coordinate geometry behind the proposed T^2(1+NBt) count.

3. WHERE THE CARRIER MULTIPLICITY IS EXACTLY THIS BRANCHING SPACE

Let m_(p,q)=dim M_(p,q). The Weyl character coefficient formula for
End(V_(a,b)) can be written, with the dual orientation made explicit,

  m_(p,q)=sum_(w in W) (-1)^ell(w)
      mult_(V_(p,q))(lambda+rho-w(lambda+rho)).           (3.1)

To fix the dual convention, use
Hom(V_(p,q),V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*) ~=
Hom(V_lambda,V_lambda tensor V_(q,p)); the tensor-product coefficient
formula and mult_(V_(q,p))(gamma)=mult_(V_(p,q))(-gamma) give exactly
(3.1). The identity Weyl term in (3.1) is dim V_(p,q)[0]. For w!=1, the shift
delta_w=lambda+rho-w(lambda+rho) lies in Q_+ and has simple-root height
at least b+1: in its inversion-set expansion each positive-root
coefficient is a positive integer at least min(a+1,b+1)=b+1, and
every positive root has height at least one. Every weight gamma of
V_(p,q) satisfies

  <gamma,rho^vee> <= <(p,q),rho^vee>=p+q,

since rho^vee is dominant and the weight polytope is conv(W(p,q)).
As <delta_w,rho^vee>=ht(delta_w), no nonidentity shift is a weight
when p+q<=b. Therefore the exact stable range is

  m_(p,q)=dim V_(p,q)[0] for p+q<=b.                      (3.2)

This is an equality of dimensions. It is not an identity that
diagonalizes K_(p,q) in the U(2)-spin basis.

For a Casimir cutoff T=N^2 t, c2(p,q)<=T implies p,q<=sqrt(3T).
Hence all labels under that cutoff lie in (3.2) if
T<=b^2/12. In particular, over the full requested window T<=c0 N,
the stable reduction covers the whole window only when b is at least a
fixed constant times sqrt(N). If b=o(sqrt(N)), an increasing portion
of the requested Casimir window has p+q>b and requires a nonstable
argument as well.

4. THE EXACT BLOCK MODEL AND THE COMPARISON THAT WOULD CLOSE THE COUNT

For any orthonormal intertwiners F_r:V_nu->End(V_lambda), the block
entries are exactly

  (K_nu)_(r,s) = (1/dim V_nu) sum_A
       Tr_(V_nu)[ F_r^* ad(S_A)^* ad(S_A) F_s ].          (4.1)

Formula (4.1) is an exact reduced matrix-element definition; it does
not assert that the matrix is diagonal in a branching basis. In the
stable range, (3.2) gives an abstract isomorphism between M_nu and
V_nu[0], whose U(2)-spin basis is indexed by (2). The established
dimension formula alone does not identify (4.1) with the U(2) Casimir.

Here is a precise sufficient block comparison. Let xi=v_lambda tensor
v_lambda^* be the T-fixed highest/lowest cyclic vector in
V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*. Its nu-isotypic projection is a tensor in
V_nu[0] tensor M_nu. Viewing that tensor as a coefficient map
C_nu:M_nu^*->V_nu[0], cyclicity makes C_nu injective. In the stable
range it is an isomorphism by (3.2). Let U_nu be its polar isometry,
with the dual on M_nu identified in the natural conjugate convention.
On V_nu[0], let J_nu be the positive diagonal operator whose
eigenvalue on the zero vector of the U(2) branch (2) is j(j+1).
The following uniform inequality would suffice on all blocks:

  K_nu >= c U_nu^* J_nu U_nu,                             (4.2)

with c>0 independent of a,b,nu, and with the analogous injection/polar
map outside the stable range. Equation (4.2) is a load-bearing claim,
not proved here. The polar isometry is specified to prevent a hidden
condition-number assumption: a mere vector-space bijection in (3.2)
does not preserve the Hilbert norm or quadratic form.

To see the exact counting implication, put T=N^2 t and
J_* = C sqrt(N B t), using sqrt(C_S) comparable to B. A counted
block eigenvalue has c2(p,q)<=T. Loewner domination (4.2) and the
min-max principle bound the number of K_nu eigenvalues below
N sqrt(C_S)t by the number of branch labels with j<=J_*. By (2.2),
such labels require |p-q|<=3J_*. For J_*<=sqrt(T), each
p<=C sqrt(T) allows at most
C(1+J_*) choices of q, and for each (p,q) at most C(1+J_*) branch
spins; the Weyl dimension obeys dim V_(p,q)<=C(p+1+J_*)^3. Summing
over p gives

  D_w(t) <= C T^2(1+J_*)^2.

For J_*>sqrt(T), the global bound m_nu<=dim V_nu[0] gives the ordinary
Casimir count O(T^3), which is at most O(T^2(1+J_*)^2). (For T<3 the
nontrivial count is zero by the root-lattice Casimir gap.) Thus in both cases

  D_w(t) <= C T^2(1+N B t)
          = C[T^2+(B/N)T^3],                             (4.3)

which is (1.3) because d is comparable to N^2 B for a>=b. The
constant and cutoff in (4.2) must be uniform; a fixed-carrier estimate
does not establish (4.3).

5. WHY THE CURRENT IDENTITIES DO NOT PROVE (4.2)

The known multiplicity argument is a cyclic-vector coefficient
injection into V_nu[0], giving m_nu<=dim V_nu[0] globally and equality
in (3.2). It is only a statement about rank/dimension.
It does not show that the polar image U_nu intertwines any U(2) action
on M_nu (M_nu has no such diagonal-SU(3) action), nor that the
quadratic form K_nu pulls back the U(2) Casimir. The Gram matrix
C_nu^* C_nu may depend on a,b,p,q; a nonunitary coefficient map
cannot silently be treated as an orthonormal branching basis.

The exact mixed-operator aid M=sum_A ad(T_A)ad(S_A) gives on a
c2(nu)=c block L_S>=M^2/c. In the adjoint block its two eigenvalues
split into a soft and a hard mode near the wall, but this does not
control the M_nu matrix for growing p,q or multiplicity. Moreover the
square/c factor may lose one power of the desired fiber Casimir.
No exact identity currently relates M_nu, K_nu and J_nu with the
uniform form domination (4.2).

The ordinary comparison L_w>=L_T/N^2 also cannot fill this gap:
the exact stable zero-weight count gives D_T(T)>=cT^3 for T up to a
constant multiple of b^2, whereas the proposed right side in (4.3)
has only T^2+(B/N)T^3. The sequence b=floor(N^(1/4)) and T~b^2
already makes their ratio diverge. This refutes the ordinary-Casimir
shortcut only; it is not a counterexample to D_w.

6. ADJOINT CHECK AND CURRENT VERDICT

For nu=(1,1), (2) gives j=0,1. The exact adjoint block from
adjoint_block_and_count_obstruction.txt is

  K_adj = [[3 C_S/C2, -6(alpha/N)sqrt(C_S/C2)],
           [-6(alpha/N)sqrt(C_S/C2), kappa]].

As b/N->0, one mode of the full L_w has eigenvalue
(3+o(1))/N^2 and the other is Theta(1/(N sqrt(C_S)))=Theta(1/(NB)).
This is consistent with one base branch plus one penalized fiber mode,
but the T,S basis used in that exact matrix has not been identified
with the polar U(2)-branch basis. It neither proves (4.2) nor gives a
counterexample to it.

Conclusion: the U(2) branch labels explain exactly why a vertical
quadratic lower would give the target T^2+(B/N)T^3 powers. The
unresolved fact is a carrier-uniform, multiplicity-space comparison
between L_S and this branch-spin quadratic, including the nonstable
part when b<<sqrt(N). No weighted-count counterexample sequence is
known from this analysis; the previous near-wall sequence attacks only
the ordinary Casimir count.
