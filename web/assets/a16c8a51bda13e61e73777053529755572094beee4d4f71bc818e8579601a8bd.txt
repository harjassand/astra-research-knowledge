Primary-source mechanism audit: gap amplification, qLTCs, and the N11 encoding gate
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: source-derived mechanism audit plus one scoped branch-expander obstruction. No theorem below constructs the requested full common encoding. This is not a general impossibility theorem for active workspace encodings.

1. Exact target being tested

For each member H of the explicit augmented BMVZ hard family, the N11 interface asks for a polynomially constructed physical space and a single efficient isometry V from the full logical space into it, P=VV*, and positive contractions B,C such that

    ker C = ran V,
    C >= gamma (I-P),
    alpha H <= V* B V <= beta H,

where alpha,beta,gamma>0 are independent of input size and repetition depth. B must be diagonal in the fixed physical computational basis; C and B must have polynomial maximum row support and efficient row/entry access (B's diagonal entries in particular), with polynomial coefficient precision/height and charged workspace and circuit costs. The inequalities are operator order, so they preserve the whole logical low-energy/kernel structure, not merely the NO-instance ground energy. Exact compression V*BV=cH is stronger than this order-equivalence requirement.

The augmented family here includes the base bounded-degree QMA-hard input and two separate five-qubit seed singleton layers of constant total weight (the BJSW phi and psi tests), then the BMVZ repetition/amplification of all layers. The seed append is a hardness-preserving legal weighted layered input, not a claim that an unnamed bare BMVZ term table necessarily contains both repeated seeds. The prior source audit gives this exact membership construction and the full output interface in `outputs/research/games_hamiltonians/cycle2/positive_encoding_cycle2.md` and `bmvz_bjsw_source_audit.md`.

2. Closest direct theorem: BMVZ gap amplification

Bergamaschi--Metger--Vidick--Zhang (BMVZ), “Derandomised tensor product gap amplification for quantum Hamiltonians,” arXiv:2510.01333v1, Definitions 1.6–1.9 and Theorems 1.2, 1.4, and 7.3, is the closest positive mechanism. Its input is a normalized layered Hamiltonian

    H = sum_chi w_chi H_chi,
    H_chi = average_i Pi_i^chi,

where each layer is a family of commuting projections, the number of layers and inverse minimum layer weight are controlled, and a bounded-degree 5-local QMA-hard family can be put into this form. It places an expander on each layer's clause indices, lists length-t expander walks, and outputs clauses

    h_(chi,f) = I - tensor_(a=1)^t (I - Pi_(f(a))^chi).

For one derandomized 2t-fold step, Theorem 1.2 gives locality 2tk, d^(2t)m terms (d is fixed expander degree), completeness at most 2t lambda_min(H), and soundness at least the stated minimum of Theta(log(t)/t) and Theta(sqrt(t/log(t))*lambda_min(H)); constants depend on the input and expander. Iterating the constant-step map gives a deterministic QMA-hard constant-promise-gap family, but locality rises to order k/epsilon. The streaming variant (Theorem 1.4) has polynomially many terms, each an O(n)-fold tensor product of O(1)-local projections, and a constant ground-energy promise gap.

This is a theorem about transforming the Hamiltonian and its clause list. It does not provide an isometry V, a common diagonal physical effect B with compressed energy comparable to the entire H, or a positive invalid-image operator C with polynomial rows and constant normalized gap. In the construction, the expander is over constraint indices. It derandomizes the sampling of a tensor-product AND test; it is not an expander on an encoding/history space enforcing consistency between different decoded copies. The new tests are explicitly locality-increasing. Thus the fact that the output has a constant *promise gap* does not settle the N11 constant *image gap*.

There is also a concrete row-sparsity mismatch in the canonical matrix representation. For the augmented seed singleton layer, repetition gives

    Q_(v,T) = I - (I-|v><v|)^(tensor T).

The BJSW seeds have computational supports of sizes 2 and 4. The source-checked full augmented output has a computational row with at least 2^T+4^T-1 nonzeros. Thus the direct output matrix is not polynomial-row-sparse for this witness. This falsifies only the direct-matrix shortcut. It does not rule out a different V and a diagonal B realizing order-equivalence.

BMVZ directly distinguishes sparse quantum PCP from ordinary locality: it defines sparse qPCP using efficiently computable sparse matrix entries, says its structured tensor terms do not yield it through known black-box sparsification, and leaves exploiting the tensor-product structure as an open question (arXiv:2510.01333v1, Section 1.2 “Sparse quantum PCP,” especially the paragraph ending at line 209 of the HTML). This is an unusually exact source-level warning against attributing an N11 sparse encoding to the gap theorem.

3. Mechanism-level obstruction: an expander on the direct clause branches

The direct clause-branch lift has an exact logical test already. Given H=sum_j w_j h_j and h_j=U_j*D_jU_j with D_j diagonal positive, take

    V|psi> = sum_j sqrt(w_j)|j> U_j|psi>,
    B = sum_j |j><j| tensor D_j.

Then V*BV=H exactly and B is a diagonal positive contraction. The missing object is C. A natural attempt is to replace a clock path by a reversible expander on these clause labels and gauge its graph Laplacian by the frames U_j:

    S = diag(sqrt(pi)) K diag(1/sqrt(pi)),
    C_K = (1/2) G ((I-S) tensor I) G*,   G = direct-sum_j U_j,

where K is reversible with stationary law pi_j=w_j. This gives the correct image kernel when the branch graph is connected, and its gap equals half the Markov gap. But on the actual augmented family this direct-frame expander cannot have polynomial rows as T grows.

Proof under the stated architecture. Here K is reversible with stationary distribution pi_j=w_j, and S is its symmetric conjugate on ordinary Euclidean branch coordinates. Let A be all branches belonging to the repeated phi singleton layer. Their total stationary mass is exactly its layer weight 1/4; the complement has mass 3/4. If K has spectral gap at least delta>0, Poincare applied to 1_A gives edge flow

    sum_(x in A,y notin A) pi_x K_xy >= delta pi(A)pi(A^c) = 3 delta/16.

In particular, some transition x->y crosses the seed-layer cut. Every x in A uses U_phi^(tensor T) on the fresh seed blocks. Every branch outside A uses identity on those phi blocks (other base clauses act on the disjoint base register; the psi singleton layer acts on a separate seed register). The relative frame U_x U_y* therefore contains U_phi^(tensor T) or its adjoint on all T phi blocks. Since U_phi is a Clifford decoder for a state with two computational support points, its rows have support at least 2; tensoring T disjoint blocks gives at least 2^T nonzeros per row. In C_K the (x,y) off-diagonal clock block is a nonzero scalar multiple of this relative unitary. Distinct y labels occupy disjoint physical columns, so other graph edges cannot cancel those row entries. Hence C_K has a row with at least 2^T nonzeros. This rules out polynomial-row constant-gap connectors in this exact direct-clause-frame/reversible-graph architecture.

Scope matters. This proof assumes (i) branch labels are the clause labels with stationary weights equal to the Hamiltonian weights; (ii) the frames are the direct clause decoders above, identity on the fresh other seed register; and (iii) C is the gauged reversible graph Laplacian. It is not a lower bound for arbitrary positive C, for intermediate decoder labels, or for a data-dependent active isometry. It is therefore a sharper diagnosis of the tempting expander-clock route, not a no-go for N11.

This differs from the existing reversible cube bound for the pair-only phi/psi interpolation: here the full-list branch law has a constant-mass seed-layer cut and the block obstruction is an expander-flow argument; it does not rely on the fixed-image Chebyshev estimate or assert that all possible C share the graph form.

4. Detectability-lemma routes: useful gap filters, wrong output object

Aharonov--Arad--Landau--Vazirani (AALV), “The Detectability Lemma and Quantum Gap Amplification,” arXiv:0811.3412, Lemma 7.2, starts from a 2-qudit-per-edge QSAT projection system on a fixed-degree expander. For a t-walk it makes the projector onto violation of at least one constraint along the walk, a t*log(q)-local test, and proves a lower bound of the form c(lambda) K(q,d,theta) min{t QUNSAT(H),1}. This is the quantum analogue of expander-walk gap amplification. Again, the expander samples/combines constraints; the output test touches all t positions. It is a Hamiltonian gap theorem, not a common diagonal compression/image-enforcer theorem.

The Aharonov--Arad--Vidick survey (arXiv:1309.7495, standard formulations) and BMVZ both state the quantum PCP problem as the open strengthening from inverse-polynomial to constant normalized promise gap at bounded locality. AALV's introduction explicitly says a walk test needs t queries and is too nonlocal for PCP without further locality/degree reduction. BMVZ closes a gap-amplification step but not that reduction.

A different AAV-family result, Anshu--Arad--Vidick, “A simple proof of the detectability lemma and spectral gap amplification,” arXiv:1602.01210v3, Theorem 5, coarse-grains a frustration-free nearest-neighbor 1D projector Hamiltonian with gap gamma into overlapping blocks of length r, with the same ground space and a coarse-grained projector sum whose gap is at least 1/4 - exp[-(r-4)sqrt(gamma/2)/2]. Taking r=O(gamma^(-1/2)) makes this a constant spectral gap. This is an authentic positive gap statement, but its input is frustration-free and geometrically local, and the output's interaction range grows as inverse square-root gap. It supplies neither a bounded-norm polynomial-row C guarantee (a generic r-qubit projector can have row support 2^r), nor any B or logical compression comparing the full hard H. If gamma is inverse polynomial, r is polynomial, so the theorem does not imply polynomial matrix-row access. This cannot be read as a solution to the requested output representation.

One-shot detectability filters such as I-(D_DL^q)*D_DL^q can have the common frustration-free ground space and a constant absolute gap after q=Theta(1/Gamma) sweeps (AAV16 plus Gao's converse). The filter is global. Its sequential-projector expansion uses conditioned prefixes and has no theorem-provided polynomial row list; on the augmented BMVZ seed factors the canonical tensor products have exponential row support. Moreover, for general QMA promise instances the NO side is not frustration-free, while an exact kernel enforcer requires a specified image/ground space. The filter provides no shared diagonal B satisfying the N11 logical comparison.

5. qLTCs: an available sparse code Hamiltonian, but not the N11 package

As of the 2026 sources checked here, Gay--Granha Jeronimo, “Asymptotically Good Quantum Locally Testable Codes,” arXiv:2609.20780v1, Theorem 2.4.3, constructs binary CSS codes with constant rate, linear distance, constant qLTC soundness, and uniformly bounded check weight and qubit incidence. Their normalized check Hamiltonian is

    H_code = (1/m) sum_i (I-S_i)/2,
    H_code >= (sigma/N) D_Q,

where D_Q is the quantum Hamming-distance observable to the code (Definition 1.2.1). This does supply an explicit sparse positive local operator with kernel the code space. It does not give a constant spectral gap as a contraction: a weight-one Pauli error produces a state orthogonal to a distance-Theta(N) code while violating only O(1) bounded-incidence checks, so its normalized energy is O(1/N). The qLTC inequality gives the matching Omega(1/N) lower scale on the first nonzero level. The unnormalized check sum can have constant gap but norm Theta(N), outside the target 0<=C<=I normalization. More basically, qLTC soundness is a statement about Hamming distance from one code space, not a reduction mapping the entire augmented QMA-hard Hamiltonian to one diagonal physical effect. No common V and B for that family is constructed by the code theorem.

This distinction survives the stronger 2026 qLTC parameters: positive rate/distance and bounded checks make the code a structured kernel, but do not identify its logical subspace with the particular N11 image or realize H through a diagonal B. The source itself states that constructing qLTCs does not supply the reduction required by quantum PCP (arXiv:2609.20780v1, Introduction around the paragraph “Quantum locally testable codes are motivated…”). The earlier Aharonov--Eldar work, arXiv:1310.5664, defines qLTC soundness and derives limitations; it is likewise a code-testing theorem, not a QMA-hardness/encoding theorem.

6. Status and route boundary

No primary result reviewed supplies all three N11 objects at once, or any theorem that upgrades a BMVZ constant-gap output to a sparse common positive encoding. The strongest near-matches are orthogonal:

* BMVZ gives a QMA-hard constant-promise-gap Hamiltonian after iterating, but the locality grows and the canonical matrix/term rows can be exponential.
* Detectability/coarse-graining gives constant absolute spectral gap for special frustration-free ground spaces, at a growing interaction range/global-filter cost; it supplies no logical-energy lift.
* qLTC constructions give sparse positive checks with a code kernel and soundness against Hamming distance; the normalized all-state spectral gap is inverse system size, and no BMVZ energy comparison follows.

A direct clause-frame encoding does satisfy the strongest energy condition exactly, V*BV=H. The branch-expander calculation above shows why the most obvious way to add a constant-gap C to that same V fails, under precise reversible/gauged-graph assumptions. It leaves open precisely the avenues outside those assumptions: an active data-dependent workspace, a new code whose complete codespace is the desired image, or a different positive sparse enforcer not represented as a frame-gauged graph walk. The full-list clock-history lift already constructed in Cycle 2 has exact scaled compression and polynomial rows but only Theta(K^-2) image gap; this report does not repeat the separate fixed-image upper bound for that clock image.
