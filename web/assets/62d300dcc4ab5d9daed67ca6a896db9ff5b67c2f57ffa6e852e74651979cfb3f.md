# Cycle 2: positive stochastic embedding of an N51 hit, with an assay-margin audit

**Verdict.** Every finite rational tuple of matrices, including every integer tuple printed by the pinned N51/family-116 candidate, has an exact embedding into strictly positive doubly stochastic kernels on one extra state. The chosen kernels are also time-one transitions of strictly positive homogeneous continuous-time Markov chains. An affine decoder recovers the original tuple exactly, and for the target formula

\[
\Phi(X_1,\ldots,X_5)=s_4(X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4)(I+X_5)^{-1}
\]

the raw formula evaluated on the kernels remains defined and nonzero whenever the source tuple is an N51 hit. This is a real positive capability result beyond the two-state obstruction. It does **not** make N51 a useful physical test by itself: the explicit embedding scale is inverse-polynomial in the *entry magnitude*, which can be exponentially large in the source bit length, and the raw output is suppressed by its fourth power. The source supplies neither a selected manageable hit nor biochemical controls, calibrated state readout, or a robustness margin.

This note continues [the cycle-one two-state obstruction and source reconstruction](FINAL_REPORT.md). Its principal new result is the exact positive embedding and its precision cost. The embedded example below is an independently found small integer witness; it is **not claimed to occur in the N51 output list**.

For the particular formula here, the only inverse node is \((I+X_5)^{-1}\). At an N51 hit \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_5)\), both \(I+A_5\) and \(\Phi(A)\) are invertible; hence \(s_4(A_1,\ldots,A_4)=\Phi(A)(I+A_5)\) is invertible too. The embedding proof below preserves the inverse gate and this nonzero output, but not by pretending that an unconstrained integer matrix is itself a probability kernel.

## Exact embedding

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_5\in\mathbb Q^{d\times d}\), put \(m=d+1\), and use column vectors. Define

\[
E=\begin{bmatrix}e_1-e_m&\cdots&e_d-e_m\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb Q^{m\times d},\qquad
F=\begin{bmatrix}I_d&0\end{bmatrix}-\frac1m\mathbf1_d\mathbf1_m^T\in\mathbb Q^{d\times m},
\]

\[
J=\frac1m\mathbf1_m\mathbf1_m^T,\qquad
S=[\mathbf1_m\ E].
\]

Direct multiplication gives

\[
F\mathbf1_m=0,\quad \mathbf1_m^TE=0,\quad FE=I_d,\quad EF=I_m-J,
\]

and therefore \(S^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf1_m^T/m\\F\end{bmatrix}\). Thus the lower \(d\)-coordinate block in this basis is exactly the zero-sum subspace.

Use entrywise absolute sum \(\|M\|_{e,1}=\sum_{ij}|M_{ij}|\), and set

\[
K=1+\max_{1\le i\le5}\{\|A_i\|_{e,1},\|EA_iF\|_{e,1}\},\qquad
t=\frac1{64mK},\qquad c=\frac12.
\]

Define

\[
P_i=J+E(cI_d+tA_i)F=cI_m+(1-c)J+tEA_iF=P_0+\Delta_i.
\]

Here \(P_0=cI_m+(1-c)J\), and \(\Delta_i=tEA_iF\). The basis identities yield the exact formula

\[
S^{-1}P_iS=\operatorname{diag}(1,cI_d+tA_i),\qquad FP_iE=cI_d+tA_i.
\]

Since \(F\mathbf1=0\) and \(\mathbf1^TE=0\), \(P_i\mathbf1=\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf1^TP_i=\mathbf1^T\). The kernels are doubly stochastic. Further, each entry of \(\Delta_i\) has magnitude at most \(t\|EA_iF\|_{e,1}<1/(64m)\). The off-diagonal baseline entries of \(P_0\) equal \(1/(2m)=32/(64m)\); diagonal baseline entries are larger. Consequently every entry of every \(P_i\) is greater than \(31/(64m)\). This is a strict positivity margin, not just nonnegativity.

The original rational matrices are recovered with no approximation:

\[
A_i=t^{-1}(FP_iE-cI_d).
\]

So a matrix-output assay that can measure each pulse kernel and knows \(t\) can reconstruct the exact input tuple in noiseless arithmetic. In particular, the exact decoded evaluation \(\Phi(A)\) is preserved.

For direct evaluation on the stochastic kernels, \(s_4\) is multilinear and vanishes when any argument is a scalar multiple of the identity: fixing the other three factors, inserting the identity in the four possible positions gives alternating signs whose sum is zero. Therefore

\[
S^{-1}s_4(P_1,P_2,P_3,P_4)S=\operatorname{diag}(0,t^4s_4(A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4)).
\]

Also

\[
S^{-1}(I_m+P_5)S=\operatorname{diag}(2,\tfrac32I_d+tA_5).
\]

The inverse gate is always legal in this construction: \(t\|A_5\|_2<1/(64m)\), so \(\tfrac32I+tA_5\) is invertible by the Neumann bound. The full raw output is

\[
S^{-1}\Phi(P_1,\ldots,P_5)S
=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(0,t^4s_4(A_1,\ldots,A_4)(\tfrac32I+tA_5)^{-1}\right).
\]

Thus this raw matrix is nonzero iff \(s_4(A)\ne0\), and in particular is invertible on its lower block for an N51 hit. If that lower block is called \(Y\), the source evaluation is recovered by

\[
\Phi(A)=t^{-4}Y(\tfrac32I+tA_5)(I+A_5)^{-1}.
\]

The raw output is a known rescaling/linear-fractional transform of the target, not the identical \(d\times d\) target value. The affine decoder above is the simpler exact way to preserve the original evaluation.

## A continuous-time pulse implementation, conditional on controllability

The construction can be strengthened from positive stochastic matrices to time-one kernels of positive-rate CTMCs. For each \(i\), \(\|\Delta_i\|_2<1/(64m)\). The symmetric baseline \(P_0\) has spectrum \(\{1,1/2\}\), and the whole path \(P_0+u\Delta_i\) for \(0\le u\le1\) remains in the right half-plane by the normal-matrix perturbation bound. Its principal matrix logarithm is real. With \(Q_i=\log P_i\), the Fréchet derivative identity for the principal logarithm gives

\[
\|Q_i-Q_0\|_2
\le \|\Delta_i\|_2\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{(s+1/2-\|\Delta_i\|_2)^2}
<4\|\Delta_i\|_2<\frac1{16m},
\]

where \(Q_0=\log(P_0)=\log(1/2)(I-J)\). Every off-diagonal entry of \(Q_0\) is \(\log 2/m>1/(2m)\); an entry is bounded by the spectral norm of the perturbation. Hence every off-diagonal entry of \(Q_i\) exceeds \(7/(16m)>0\). Since \(P_i\mathbf1=\mathbf1\) and \(\mathbf1^TP_i=\mathbf1^T\), functional calculus gives \(Q_i\mathbf1=0\) and \(\mathbf1^TQ_i=0\). Thus \(Q_i\) is a valid dense rate generator and \(e^{Q_i}=P_i\).

This is a local sufficient construction within the classical Markov embedding problem: general stochastic matrices need not be exponentials of rate generators. Casanellas, Fernández-Sánchez, and Roca-Lacostena review that distinction and the determinant/logarithm obstructions in [The embedding problem for Markov matrices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00818). The near-\(P_0\) argument here is an elementary sufficient region tailored to the encoding.

The biological interpretation remains conditional. Receptor and allosteric state models have primary antecedents in [del Castillo and Katz (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1957.0018), [Monod, Wyman, and Changeux (1965)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-2836(65)80285-6), an experimentally used two-state A1 receptor kinetic model in [Guo et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1111/bph.14184), and GPCR Markov-state modeling in [Lu et al. (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-25020-9). Those works motivate finite-state kinetics; they do not provide arbitrary all-to-all control of \(m=d+1\) states or show that a molecule can realize these five individually programmed \(Q_i\). This bridge would require a known resolved state space, preparation of each state, independently programmable transition rates, pulse stationarity, and endpoint classification. The rate matrices have \(m(m-1)\) positive channels each, so control and calibration scale as \(5m(m-1)\), while reading all five kernels requires \(5m^2\) start/end transition probabilities. Directly estimating all 24 words of \(s_4\) takes up to \(24m^2\) start/end conditions, before replicate counts. A fair comparator is a direct search over those same 24 ordered pulse sequences with identical preparation, calibration, and readout budgets.

## Precision and practical cost

Let \(H=\max_{i,ab}|(A_i)_{ab}|\). The entrywise norm bounds give

\[
\|A_i\|_{e,1}\le d^2H,\qquad
\|EA_iF\|_{e,1}\le \|E\|_{e,1}\|A_i\|_{e,1}\|F\|_{e,1}<4d^4H,
\]

so \(K\le1+4d^4H\) and

\[
t\ge [64(d+1)(1+4d^4H)]^{-1}.
\]

All embedding arithmetic is exact rational arithmetic with polynomial bit cost in \(d\) and the tuple's total bit length \(L\); the dense output has \(5(d+1)^2\) rational entries. For N51's integer matrices, if the hit makes \(s_4(A)\ne0\), then \(\|s_4(A)\|_2\ge1/\sqrt d\). Since \(\|\tfrac32I+tA_5\|_2<2\), the zero-sum block \(Y\) of the raw output obeys

\[
\|Y\|_2>\frac{t^4}{2\sqrt d}.
\]

This is a quantitative nonzero margin in terms of the tuple's *numerical height*. It is not generally inverse-polynomial in the source input length. If \(H\le2^L\), the guaranteed signal can be only \(2^{-O(L)}\), and direct raw-formula readout asks for entrywise transition estimates on the order of \(t^4/\operatorname{poly}(d)\); bounded-variance endpoint sampling then costs \(t^{-8}\operatorname{poly}(d)\), which can be exponential in \(L\). There are \(O(L+\log d)\) bits in the required precision, but that distinction does not make the sample count polynomial. N51's polynomial entry-*bit* bound is insufficient to claim polynomially many measurements or a robust condition-number margin.

There is a less attenuated route for integer hits: estimate \(FP_iE=cI+tA_i\) to error below \(t/2\) and round the recovered entries to their known integer lattice. Since \(F\) and \(E\) are explicit, per-entry transition precision \(O(t)\) suffices up to dimension factors, with sample cost \(t^{-2}\operatorname{poly}(d)\). This still can be exponential in \(L\). More fundamentally, once the integer tuple and formula are already known, their exact matrix evaluation is an offline computation; a wet assay adds value only if the tuple is a realizable control encoding of an unknown pathway response. N51 provides no such interpretation or acquisition mechanism.

For a concrete scale check, the exact independent \(d=3\) witness below has \(K=10\), \(t=1/2560\), and \(\max_{ab}|\Phi(P)_{ab}|=1/12884901888000\approx7.76\times10^{-14}\). A simple signed estimator of a 24-term output entry uses bounded-variance Bernoulli endpoints; taking equal replicates for the 24 words gives variance at most \(6/N\), so even bringing standard error to the signal scale needs about \(10^{27}\) replicates per word. This is a scale diagnostic, not an optimized experiment design or an N51-list member.

The rates \(Q_i=\log P_i\) are generally irrational even though \(P_i\) is rational. Their numerical values are computable from the rational inputs, but a physical system must still realize/calibrate those dense rates. Exact algebraic encodability therefore does not mean molecular controllability.

## Exact small capability fixture

The saved [`cycle2_embedding_check.py`](cycle2_embedding_check.py) uses only Python's exact `Fraction` arithmetic. It verifies

\[
A_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\-1&-1&0\\-1&1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\-1&0&-1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

\[
A_3=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\-1&-1&0\\0&-1&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad
A_4=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad A_5=0.
\]

For this tuple, \(s_4(A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2&1\\4&-2&2\\-3&6&1\end{pmatrix}\), with determinant \(24\); \(I+A_5=I\). The construction gives \(m=4,K=10,t=1/2560\). All five \(P_i\) are strictly positive doubly stochastic matrices; the smallest entry is \(637/5120\). The raw output has the exact nonzero physical-basis entry \(\Phi(P)_{1,1}=1/64424509440000\). The script verifies all row/column sums, positivity, block decoding, formula block identity, and the selected readout entry. This example proves capability for a small noncommutative witness independently of the N51 generator; it is not evidence of an N51-generated manageable witness or of a molecule realizing the kernels.

## Preregistered decisive gate

For any proposed N51 tuple, compute and publish \(d,H,K,t\), all five rational \(P_i\), and the predicted raw output before collecting data. Freeze the physical state labels, five pulse conditions, a direct-sequence-search comparator, and the readout protocol. For direct raw-output confirmation, estimate all required transition probabilities so that the propagated confidence radius of the preselected nonzero output entry is less than half its exact rational value; do not relax that threshold after observing data. For the displayed fixture, that value is exactly \(1/64424509440000\). A held-out confidence interval containing zero, or failure to implement/calibrate the dense \(Q_i\) at that frozen precision, falsifies the proposed assay bridge for that tuple. For an N51-specific application, the additional admission gate is that the tuple be an actually materialized list member and the controls fit the measured state count and resource budget; a synthetic tuple or an offline-only list theorem does not pass it.

The mathematical embedding itself has a direct exact falsifier: a rational input for which a checked \(P_i\) fails strict positivity, row/column stochasticity, \(FP_iE=cI+tA_i\), or the displayed \(s_4\) block identity. The general proof establishes these identities symbolically; the script only checks the one finite fixture. The biological gate is conditional and is not represented as an experiment already performed.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

- The target identity is the classical Amitsur–Levitzki standard polynomial result, [“Minimal identities for algebras” (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1950-0036751-9). Its two-state vanishing was the cycle-one obstruction; the present construction uses dimension \(d+1\), where an \(s_4\) witness can survive.
- The expression-domain reduction is the supplied N51 route together with Hrubeš and Wigderson, [“Non-Commutative Arithmetic Circuits with Division” (2015)](https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v011a014/v011a014.pdf), Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 7.3. That interface preserves every inverse occurrence of the original syntax tree at a selected unrestricted matrix substitution. The stochastic embedding is separate new work here and does not validate the N51 source proof.
- Markov embeddability is a classical constrained problem; see Casanellas et al., [“The embedding problem for Markov matrices”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00818). The result here is the explicit near-uniform stochasticization and a local positive-generator certificate, not a general solution of the embedding problem.
- The receptor and allostery papers cited above are biochemical model/experiment prior art. They do not establish the particular all-to-all programmable kinetics or the very high precision demanded here.

No claim is made that this encoding is historically novel, that N51's complete generator is externally verified, or that any biomolecule implements it. The exact construction is internally derived; the fixture is finite exact computation; the physical assay is conditional and unperformed.
