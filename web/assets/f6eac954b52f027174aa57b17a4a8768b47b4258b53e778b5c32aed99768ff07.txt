# T15 — Scale-free progress for arbitrary rational LP

Date: 2026-10-09  
Status: **No strongly polynomial algorithm or new theorem obtained.** This record freezes an internally derived projective/circuit obstruction, an exact LP family, and a specific missing progress lemma. The calculations are finite-tested exactly; they are not a lower bound against arbitrary algorithms.

## Exact target

Input is a rational LP in standard form

    minimize c^T x, subject to A x = b and x >= 0,

with total binary encoding length L, dimensions m,n, and no restriction on A beyond rationality. The requested principle must give a correct algorithm whose number of exact arithmetic operations and comparisons is polynomial in m,n and independent of L, while every intermediate and output rational has bit length polynomial in L,m,n. Thus its Turing running time is polynomial in L,m,n, but the number of arithmetic steps may not hide a dependence on coefficient size or conditioning.

The algorithm must also terminate with one of the following exact outputs:

* An optimum certificate (x,y,s): Ax=b, x>=0, A^T y+s=c, s>=0, and c^T x=b^T y.
* An infeasibility certificate y: A^T y>=0 and b^T y<0.
* A feasible-unboundedness certificate (x0,d): Ax0=b, x0>=0, d>=0, Ad=0, and c^T d<0.

The work below concerns progress for the matrix/circuit geometry. It does not supply the required initialization, status distinction, or certificates, so the target is not achieved.

## Attempt 1: remove scale with a maximum-volume chart

Assume A has rank r and has at least one nonsingular r-column basis. Normalize every nonzero column to have l1 norm one; this is a positive variable rescaling in the LP, with the objective and variable coordinates changed consistently. Choose a maximum-volume basis B of the normalized matrix. For any nonbasic column a_j write a_j=B lambda^(j). Replacing column i of B by a_j changes its determinant by the factor lambda_i^(j). Maximum volume therefore gives

    |lambda_i^(j)| <= 1   for every i,j.

This is a genuine scale-free chart bound: all nonbasic coordinates in the B chart are bounded by one, with no assumption of total unimodularity. It is not an algorithm for finding B, and it gives neither a progress rule nor an LP certificate. There may be binomial(n,r) candidate bases.

The candidate potential was Phi(B)=log |det B|. It has two immediate defects. First, the exact determinant gain of an adjacent exchange need not be bounded below in terms of m,n. Second, even a strictly increasing basis potential only bounds the number of pivots by the number of bases, which can be exponential. The first defect is visible in the following exact family; it is not, by itself, a lower bound against an algorithm that chooses a different move or jumps between bases.

## Exact projective family and LP embedding

Let epsilon=2^-L for integer L>=1 and let the four columns of A_epsilon in R^2 be

    a1 = (1, 0)
    a2 = (0, 1)
    a3 = (1/2, 1/2)
    a4 = (1/(2+epsilon), (1+epsilon)/(2+epsilon)).

Every column has l1 norm one. The first two columns form a maximum-volume basis, with determinant one; every 2-by-2 minor has absolute value at most one. For B=(a1,a2), the two nonbasic coordinates are (1/2,1/2) and (1/(2+epsilon),(1+epsilon)/(2+epsilon)), all bounded by one.

The three columns a1,a3,a4 have the support-minimal dependence

    h0 = (epsilon/[2(2+epsilon)], 0, -(1+epsilon)/(2+epsilon), 1/2),
    A_epsilon h0 = 0.

The ratio of largest to smallest nonzero absolute coefficient is exactly

    kappa(h0) = 2(1+epsilon)/epsilon = 2^(L+1)+2.

To see a scale-free invariant, let u1=(1,0), u2=(0,1), u3=(1,1), u4=(1,1+epsilon) be unnormalized representatives of the same four projective directions and set D_ij=det(u_i,u_j). Then

    R = (D_13 D_24)/(D_14 D_23) = 1/(1+epsilon),
    1-R = epsilon/(1+epsilon).

For any invertible 2-by-2 row map T and positive column scales t_i, each determinant transforms as D_ij -> det(T)t_i t_j D_ij. All factors cancel in R. Hence this small Plucker cross-ratio gap cannot be removed by changing row coordinates or by independently rescaling columns. In the l1-normalized representative above, the same projective configuration has an elementary circuit with imbalance exponential in L.

The adjacent bases (a1,a3) and (a1,a4) have volumes 1/2 and (1+epsilon)/(2+epsilon). Their relative gain is

    det(a1,a4)/det(a1,a3) - 1 = epsilon/(2+epsilon) < epsilon/2.

Thus the corresponding log-volume difference is positive but at most epsilon/2. This disproves any dimension-only lower bound on every adjacent determinant gain. It does not say that a maximum-volume search must take many steps on this instance: the basis (a1,a2) is already available and has larger volume. The exact point is narrower: determinant normalization does not create a uniform local progress quantum.

The same circuit appears in a rational LP with a simple, known optimum. Set b=a3 and c=(1, 2^(2L), 0, 1). Then x*=e3 is feasible and optimal because c is componentwise nonnegative and c^T e3=0. Rescale h0 so its third coordinate is -1:

    h = (epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)], 0, -1, (2+epsilon)/[2(1+epsilon)]).

Direct substitution gives A_epsilon h=0 and c^T h=1. Starting from x*, the feasible circuit augmentation x*+t h is allowed for 0<=t<=1. For t in [0,1], its objective increase is t and its first coordinate is

    x_1(t) = t epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)].

The other feasible tangent circuit with positive first coordinate is q=(1/2,1/2,-1,0), for which A_epsilon q=0 and c^T q=(2^(2L)+1)/2. The feasible tangent cone at e3 consists exactly of the nonnegative combinations of h and q: after fixing d3=-1, the equation A d=0 asks for a nonnegative representation of a3 using a1,a2,a4, and the representation polytope is a segment whose endpoints use {a1,a2} and {a1,a4}. Its initial first-coordinate slope per unit objective increase is 1/(2^(2L)+1). For L>=2,

    epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)] > 1/(2^(2L)+1).

Indeed, after substituting epsilon=2^-L, this is equivalent to 1-2epsilon-epsilon^2>0, true for epsilon<=1/4. So h is the best first-coordinate circuit direction for small positive objective gaps in this exact LP, despite its coefficient imbalance growing as 2^(L+1)+2. This makes the family a useful test for a circuit-progress rule rather than only a matrix condition example. It still does not force many iterations: a procedure could select h immediately by exact comparisons.

## Attempt 2: replace metric progress by circuit coverage

The more promising representation is not a determinant potential but a cover of the feasible circuit-augmentation curves. In the bounded-optimization regime of the source construction, for a feasible optimum x*, and a kernel circuit h with c^T h>=0, define

    x^h(g) = x* + min{ g/(c^T h), min_{j:h_j<0} x*_j/(-h_j) } h,   g>=0,

omitting the first term when c^T h=0 and treating a minimum over no blocking coordinates as infinity. Each curve remains feasible, has objective increase at most g, and each coordinate is piecewise-linear with at most two pieces. Unblocked zero-cost rays that make a coordinate maximum infinite require a separate unbounded-face/status treatment; this note does not supply it. For coordinate i, call a circuit h an alpha-dominator of h' if x_i^h(g)>=alpha x_i^{h'}(g) for every g>=0. An alpha-circuit cover S_i is a set of circuits that alpha-dominates every cost-nondecreasing circuit on coordinate i.

The source-supported mechanism is that a conformal circuit decomposition gives a factor-n approximation to the maximum possible coordinate at each gap g; an alpha-cover of that circuit envelope with |S_i| curves gives SLC_{alpha/n}(x_i^max)<=|S_i|+1. The SLLS interior-point method then converts this path-complexity bound into an iteration bound, provided it is initialized and the iterates' bit lengths are controlled. Dadush, Koh, Natura, Olver, and Vegh construct such covers for generalized flows using graph-specific path/bicycle domination. General matrix arguments can obtain covers whose accuracy depends on the circuit-imbalance quantity kappa_A, which may be as large as 2^L in the family above; then logarithmic dependence on kappa_A still depends on coefficient bit size.

This suggests the exact missing lemma for the attempted route:

    For arbitrary rational A and c, construct in poly(m,n) arithmetic steps,
    with polynomial-bit intermediate data, a per-coordinate circuit cover
    of size poly(m,n) and accuracy sufficient for a polynomial SLLS bound,
    without dependence on coefficient magnitudes, and without assuming x* is
    supplied; also provide initialization and exact certificates for all LP
    statuses.

No such lemma is proved here. Nor is it established that a circuit cover is necessary for every strongly polynomial algorithm. Existing exponential straight-line-complexity and self-concordant-barrier lower bounds block a universal polynomial path-following argument of the covered type, but do not rule out a non-path algorithm or a different combinatorial progress measure. The precise tolerance in any proposed cover theorem must be matched to the iteration theorem; one cannot infer impossibility just from an exponential path example without checking that tolerance.

## Other progress ideas tested and why they stop

1. **Use the largest determinant as a condition-free progress measure.** The exchange lemma bounds chart coefficients, but exact exchanges can have vanishing gain and the state space is exponentially large. A determinant maximum is also not known until one has solved a combinatorial search over bases. Result: a useful normalization lemma, not an algorithm.
2. **Use a barrier or central-path energy after projective normalization.** Projective cross-ratios survive row and column rescaling, so normalization cannot bound all geometric ratios. More decisively, the cited lower bound for self-concordant-barrier path-following methods gives a family with Omega(2^n) iterations. Result: no generic self-concordant path can be the desired progress certificate.
3. **Use a circuit cover with the known imbalance-dependent guarantee.** It is polynomial for special matrix classes or when the relevant imbalance is suitably bounded, but in the displayed rational family the normalized circuit imbalance is exponential in L. Result: condition substitution does not meet the requested boundary.
4. **Use exact rational signs to skip tiny gaps.** This is compatible with strong polynomiality in principle: one exact comparison can distinguish a rational epsilon with L bits, while the Turing cost remains polynomial in L. No rule was derived that uses this to select a globally useful set of circuits in poly(m,n) operations. Result: not an impossibility argument and no algorithm.

## Literature boundary checked on 2026-10-09

Tardos's 1986 algorithm has arithmetic-step dependence on the size of the constraint-matrix entries and is independent of the right-hand side and objective sizes; it supplies the combinatorial-LP boundary, not the arbitrary-matrix target. Vavasis–Ye's layered least-squares method gives an exact algorithm with iteration count O(n^3.5 log(chi-bar_A+n)); the condition measure can grow with coefficient geometry. Dadush et al. (STOC 2024) give a strongly polynomial algorithm for minimum-cost generalized flow and therefore LPs with at most two nonzeros per row or at most two per column, by proving polynomial straight-line complexity through graph-specific circuit/path covers. These are relevant boundaries, not a solution for arbitrary matrices.

The SLLS method of Allamigeon et al. relates iterations to straight-line complexity (SLC), gives an exponential upper bound, and cites exponential-SLC examples; each iteration is strongly polynomial, but the iteration count need not be. Allamigeon–Gaubert–Vandame give an Omega(2^n) lower bound for self-concordant-barrier path-following on an explicit n-dimensional family with 2n constraints. Standard ellipsoid/interior-point algorithms remain weakly polynomial in the rational encoding length. These results reject a generic path-following solution, not every possible strongly polynomial algorithm.

As of this check, the arXiv entry for Samuel Awoniyi's v10 preprint (last revised 2026-07-04) claims a general O(m+n)-iteration algorithm. I did not accept that claim as validated frontier: in the downloaded text, Lemma 6.1's “arbitrarily close to zero” conclusion is used in Corollary 1 to set coordinates exactly to zero, and a finite approximation parameter alone does not imply exact zero. This is a narrow unresolved proof concern, not a complete refutation; the general problem status is therefore stated cautiously here.
