# A directly checkable sufficient condition for the all-a,b gate

For a reversible pair transition (X,Y)→(X',Y') under π⊗π, write Δa=a(X)−a(X') and Δb=b(Y)−b(Y'). Then

  E(a(X)+b(Y)) = A(a)+C(b)+2J(a,b),
  A(a)=(1/2)E[Δa²],  C(b)=(1/2)E[Δb²],
  J(a,b)=(1/2)E[Δa Δb].

Suppose, on centered one-coordinate functions, A(a)≥λ Varπ(a) and there is a uniform increment-correlation bound

  |J(a,b)| <= ρ sqrt(A(a) C(b))   for every a,b,   with ρ<1.

Then

  E(a+b) >= A(a)+C(b)-2ρ sqrt(A(a)C(b))
          >= (1−ρ)(A(a)+C(b))
          >= (1−ρ)λ Varπ(a).

Thus L=[(1−ρ)λ]^-1 suffices for the all-a,b inequality. In operator language ρ is the norm of the cross block after whitening the two marginal energy forms; the exact necessary-and-sufficient condition remains the Schur complement A−BC†B*≥L^-1I.

The two binary examples separate the quantities: the involution θ(x,y)=(1−y,1−x) has maximal increment correlation ρ=1 and fails the companion-function quantifier, while resampling conditional on parity has zero cross block (ρ=0) and satisfies the additive gate, yet retains a non-additive pair invariant. Hence a proposed microscopic kernel can be audited in two stages: control the cross block on additive directions, then separately use the replica/ANOVA lift to eliminate interactions.

This is a sufficient analytic test only. Computing the worst-case cross-operator norm for a large determinant kernel could itself be hard, and no determinant application or new sampler follows from the criterion.
