1. FULL PATHS AND THE SS SCREEN

Work in the finite physical slit U with its prime-end closed disk. Let A
be one cyclic arc, Ac its complement, and t1,t2 their transition accesses.
Every continuous internal path from A to Ac contains a simple crosscut
with those endpoints. That crosscut separates t1 from t2. The original
connected path image consequently separates at least one of those accesses
from the protected pole. Do NOT discard the rest of the image when it
contains the particular point visited by the Brownian path.

For a finite family of such connected images, apply the SS visible-frontier
argument to the full images. Classify by which transition they separate
from the pole (if needed a path can be put in both classes). Two visible
members of one class cannot be disjoint: two disjoint separating crosscuts
for the same transition are nested in the prime-end disk, and the outer
one shields the entire connected inner image from the observer component.
Equivalently, use SS's route from the transition to their union and the
observer component; disjointness would make a route cross the other path.
No Euclidean coincidence of boundary accesses changes this disk argument.

Fix one visible member of each nonempty class. All other visible members
intersect it. If their diameters are at most R, their whole images are
therefore within distance 2R of any selected point of that member. That
point lies within R of the physical boundary. This yields the two balls,
including visible spurs of the full path rather than just its pruned arc.

For the infinite path union, use strict diameter R first. It is open in U:
an extra short spur from a path point to a nearby point preserves its two
endpoints and its strict diameter bound. One may cover this union by a
countable family of open path tubes, with connected closures of diameter
less than 2R and the same endpoint accesses. Finite unions have the two-ball
observer-frontier cover just proved, with fixed enlarged constants. A
Brownian path entering any such tube must first cross that finite union's
visible frontier. The annulus hit bound is uniform over the finite family.
Increasing finite subfamilies and monotone convergence give the same hit
probability for the union. Endpoint/closure choices can instead be handled
by R->2R and compact subsequences of the two centers. Neither construction
changes U, a killing site or the marginal stochastic process.

With ball centers y at O(R) from boundary, dist(z,y)>=rho/2. In the enclosing
ball of radius B about y, optional stopping of the bounded annulus harmonic
function gives P_z(hit B(y,CR) before exit U)<=log(2B/rho)/log(B/(CR)).
Use two balls and then each of the at most m0 individual cyclic arcs against
its complement. This is exactly the screen needed by the coupling proof.
Partitioning into A and Ac is a topological operation on labels only; it
does not substitute a new slit geometry or absorbing boundary problem.

2. THE PRECISE VALID LOCAL COMPARISON

Let U1,U2 be finite slit domains and w in a common ball B(p,R/2), with
dist(w,C)<=r for a common boundary barrier C. Assume the observer components
of Uj intersected with B(p,R), containing w, are the SAME open set G, and
their absorbing access data agree on partial G inside B(p,R). The common
barrier has a connected part from a point within distance r of w out to
the appropriate R-scale annulus. Let bounded harmonic data have modulus
at most A in each domain. Then
                    |u1(w)-u2(w)| <= C A sqrt(r/R).

Run the same free Brownian path until it leaves G. On local physical-boundary
exit the two stopped boundary values agree. Only exit through the artificial
R-sphere can distinguish the continuations, by at most 2A. Outward Beurling
against the common barrier bounds that probability by C sqrt(r/R). This
proves the claim with its full local-component/data premise. Equality of
one strand alone is insufficient. For a finite-mesh NN version also keep
the same ACTUAL absorbing-site/data problem locally and use the LL bound
with sqrt((r+h)/R); drawn connector equality is not absorbing-site equality.

3. EXACT GEOMETRIC COUNTERCONTROL TO THE WEAKER SHARED-PIECE READING

Fix D=(-4,4)x(0,4), h=1/k^3, r=1/k, delta=2r+h/2, and k sufficiently large.
The original square signs are positive below the row y=delta and negative
above. The two original transition marks are the edge midpoints (-4,delta)
and (4,delta). All crossed-edge face ports are opposite, so the compatible
resolved contour is exactly horizontal. Take its short prefix from x=-4
to x=-3 and its long prefix from x=-4 to x=2. Both prefixes satisfy the
exact simple square geometry. They allow the same protected live pole
(0,3), e.g. rho=1/2. Optional extra queried corners can be omitted.

Give BOTH original-arc labels scalar value +1 and BOTH strand-bank labels
value -1. Keeping equal scalar values on distinct labels is allowed. Both
domains share the bottom boundary segment and its unique accesses in
B((0,0),1). Let w=(0,r), a live lattice point and a continuum point of both
observer domains. The short-prefix harmonic value u_short(w) tends to 1:
until leaving the unit half-disk it sees only the common bottom data, and
outward Beurling bounds escape by C sqrt(r).

In the long-prefix domain, the local observer component below the slit is
the rectangle (-1,1)x(0,delta). Its horizontal data are +1 at y=0 and -1
at y=delta. The affine harmonic comparison v(y)=1-2y/delta has
                    v(r)=1-2r/delta -> 0.
The error from the two vertical sides is at most twice their exit probability
from this thin rectangle, which is O(exp(-c/delta)) by its elementary
separation-of-variables barrier. Thus u_long(w)->0.

Consequently |u_short(w)-u_long(w)|->1, although the common bottom piece
and its incident accesses agree, max|data|=1, and sqrt(r/R)=sqrt(r)->0.
Equivalently, the recoded function (u+1)/2 is the probability of original-
boundary rather than strand exit; it tends to 1 versus 1/2. The failure
already occurs for data in [0,1], not just for a signed boundary value.
The other boundary inside the ball is the long prefix itself. This refutes
the weaker literal reading of origin lines 321--329, while showing exactly
why their phrase 'identical absorbing boundary data until escape' needs
the additional full local-observer-component premise. The countercontrol
does not challenge the protected-pole exit theorem or its marked limit.
