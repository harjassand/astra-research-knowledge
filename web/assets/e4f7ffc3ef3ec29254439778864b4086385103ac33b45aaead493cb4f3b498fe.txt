CYCLE 3 RESULT — SEQUENTIAL-KL DECOMPOSITION AND A ONE-STREAM OBSTRUCTION

Status: exact population identity proved for all finite-alphabet stationary processes; finite-sample lower bound proved for an explicit stationary ergodic two-state HMM family. The lower bound rules out a distribution-free rank-free predictive approximation guarantee from one raw path when no mixing lower bound is supplied. It does not rule out guarantees that depend on a mixing/coverage parameter.

1. Scope and result

The population decomposition is
  E KL(P(F | H) || q(F | T))
    = I(F;H | T) + E KL(P(F | T) || q(F | T)),
where H is a stationary observable history, F is the next H-block, and T is any statistic of H. The first term is exactly the task-relevant predictive information discarded by T; the second is decoder fitting error. Parameter differences that leave P(F|H) unchanged on stationary support are task-irrelevant for this loss.

This identity does not create a finite-sample oracle: both terms involve unknown conditional laws. A full finite-sample counterexample follows. For every training length n, a stationary ergodic HMM with two hidden states and four noisy output symbols has a constant-size common component in the law of the one observed training path, while its stationary one-step prediction task has positive Bayes predictive information. Any algorithm, including an oracle-nuisance full-path likelihood/model-selection baseline, has expected population predictive-KL risk greater than 0.0141 nats on one of the two models. The transition gap is positive but can be arbitrarily small. Thus D, alphabet size, finite horizon, and a representation identity alone do not imply a uniform raw-path guarantee.

2. Evaluation target and the exact representation/fitting identity

Let (Y_t)_{t in Z} be a stationary process on a finite alphabet A. Fix a history length L>=1 and a prediction horizon H>=1, and define
  H_t = Y_{t-L+1:t},       F_t = Y_{t+1:t+H}.
For a stationary law P and a conditional predictor q(.|T_t), where T_t is a statistic of H_t, define the population loss
  R_{P,L,H}(q,T)
    = E_P[ KL(P(F_t in . | H_t) || q(.|T_t)) ].
The expectation is over a fresh stationary evaluation block. If the predictor was trained on a path S, the risk is conditional on that fitted predictor and is still evaluated on this stationary population block; the evaluation block is not extra training access.

Assume T is generated from H by a deterministic map or a stochastic kernel independent of F conditional on H. Define P(F|T) by averaging P(F|H) conditional on T. For each value of H,T, insert P(F|T) inside the logarithm:
  log(P(F|H)/q(F|T))
    = log(P(F|H)/P(F|T)) + log(P(F|T)/q(F|T)).
Taking expectation gives the exact identity
  R_{P,L,H}(q,T)
    = I_P(F;H | T)
      + E_P[ KL(P(F|T) || q(.|T)) ].                         (1)
If q=P(F|T), the second term is zero and (1) is the irreducible representation loss. If T=H, the first term is zero and the loss is purely fitting. No hidden-state realization, rank, or dictionary is required for the identity. It says which prediction directions matter; it does not estimate either term from a path.

The two-model version is also exact. Let Θ be a model index with prior weights w_θ, and let q(.|h) be one common predictor not told Θ. Then
  sum_θ w_θ E_{P_θ}[ KL(P_θ(F|H) || q(.|H)) ]
    = I(Θ;F | H) + E_{H~P_mix}
          KL(P_mix(F|H) || q(.|H)),                           (2)
where P_mix is the prior mixture. Consequently, the minimum average KL risk of any common decoder is I(Θ;F|H). Formula (2) is used below on a common subexperiment of the training path.

For L,H fixed and finite alphabet, all conditional laws in (1)-(2) are ordinary finite probability vectors; KL is allowed to be +infinity if a predictor gives zero mass to an event of positive true probability. The chain-rule identity remains valid with extended values. If T is learned from an independent training path S, condition on S and apply (1) to T=T_S. Nothing in this conditioning makes the stationary conditional probabilities observable from S.

3. Counterexample family

Fix D=2 known and output alphabet A={0,1} x {0,1}, with a raw output Y_t=(Z_t,W_t). Let the hidden chain X_t in {0,1} have transition matrix
  T_lambda = [[1-lambda, lambda],
              [lambda, 1-lambda]]
and stationary law (1/2,1/2). For every lambda in (0,1/2), the chain is irreducible and aperiodic, so the stationary output process is ergodic.

There are two candidate HMMs indexed by theta in {-1,+1}. The emissions are conditionally independent in their two coordinates:
  P(Z_t=z | X_t=x) = 1-delta if z=x, delta otherwise;
  P(W_t=1 | X_t=0,theta) = 1/2;
  P(W_t=1 | X_t=1,theta) = 1/2 + theta b,
with delta=1/100 and b=2/5. The probability for W_t=0 is the complement. Every one of the four output symbols has positive probability in each hidden state under both models. Z is a noisy state-correlated measurement, not a hidden-state label. W is noisy even in state 1: its success probabilities are 0.9 and 0.1. The two models differ only in the sign of this state-1 emission bias.

The learner receives exactly one raw path S=Y_1,...,Y_n. It receives no state labels, resets, Hankel entries, feature dictionary, or conditional-sampling queries. To make the lower bound stronger, the proof may additionally tell the learner lambda, delta, b, and the two-model family; only theta still needs to be inferred. Any guarantee for unknown transitions and emissions would also have to hold in this easier, nuisance-revealed subfamily. D=2 is known exactly.

The raw likelihood is the hidden-state sum
  P_{lambda,theta}(y_1,...,y_n)
    = sum_{x_1,...,x_n in {0,1}} (1/2)
        [product_{t=1}^{n-1} T_lambda(x_t,x_{t+1})]
        [product_{t=1}^{n} e_theta(y_t | x_t)],
where e_theta is the emission law just specified. This is the full-data likelihood available to exact forward likelihood, maximum likelihood, and model-selection baselines.

4. Exact finite-sample lower bound against every raw-path learner

For a given n, set
  lambda_n = 10^(-10)/n.
This is positive, so each model is stationary and ergodic; its hidden-chain spectral gap is 2 lambda_n, which has no uniform positive lower bound.

Let E_n be the hidden-path event X_1=...=X_n=0. Its probability under either model is
  w_n = (1/2)(1-lambda_n)^(n-1).
Conditional on E_n, all emissions follow state 0, whose law is independent of theta. Thus the two raw path laws P_{lambda_n,+}^{(n)} and P_{lambda_n,-}^{(n)} each contain the same subprobability measure w_n R_n, where R_n is the state-0 iid observation law. This is a statement about overlap of the raw observation laws; the learner is not told E_n and does not observe X.

For the population task take the stationary one-step loss with L=H=1:
  R_theta(q) = E_{P_{lambda_n,theta}}[
      KL(P_{lambda_n,theta}(Y_1 in . | Y_0)
         || q(. | Y_0)) ].
This uses the full observed symbol Y_0=(Z_0,W_0) as the evaluation history and predicts the full next symbol Y_1=(Z_1,W_1). Let Θ be uniform on {-1,+1}, independent of the training/evaluation paths. Define
  J_lambda = I(Theta;Y_1 | Y_0)
under this prior and the stationary two-model mixture. By (2), for every single common predictor q,
  (1/2) sum_theta R_theta(q) >= J_lambda.                    (3)

Now let any learning algorithm map S to any predictor q_S(.|h), with arbitrary computation, randomization, or learned representation. The training path law under each theta has the common component w_n R_n. Since KL risk is nonnegative, averaging over the prior and retaining only that component gives
  (1/2) sum_theta E_{S~P_{lambda_n,theta}^{(n)}} R_theta(q_S)
    >= w_n E_{S~R_n}[(1/2)sum_theta R_theta(q_S)]
    >= w_n J_{lambda_n}.                                      (4)
On the common component, the law of S is exactly the same under both theta, so q_S is a common predictor and (3) applies. The worst-case expected risk is at least the prior average in (4).

The predictive information J_lambda is explicit. For y=(z,w), y'=(z',w'), let
  p_{theta,lambda}(y,y')
    = (1/2) sum_{x,x' in {0,1}} T_lambda(x,x')
                         e_theta(y|x)e_theta(y'|x').
Then
  J_lambda = (1/2) sum_{theta in {-1,+1}} sum_{y,y' in A}
                p_{theta,lambda}(y,y')
                log( p_{theta,lambda}(y'|y) /
                     p_mix,lambda(y'|y) ),                    (5)
where p_mix is the conditional law under the uniform mixture over theta. This finite expression is continuous in lambda because all emissions are strictly positive.

At lambda=0, the hidden state is constant with uniform initial state and the pair law simplifies to
  p_{theta,0}(y,y') = (1/2) sum_x e_theta(y|x)e_theta(y'|x).
The full finite sum (5), checked by the scratch script, evaluates to J_0=0.07490792119477882 nats. For a conservative analytic lower bound, use the single current history h=(Z_0,W_0)=(1,1). Under the uniform theta prior,
  P(H=h)=1/4,       P(theta=+1 | H=h)=112/125.
The hidden-state posteriors at this h are 891/896 under theta=+1 and 99/104 under theta=-1. Therefore the next W marginal has probabilities
  P_+(W_1=1 | h)=2011/2240,
  P_-(W_1=1 | h)=31/260,
with total-variation separation d=22671/29120. The full next-symbol laws are at least this far apart in TV. Pinsker's inequality applied to the conditional mutual information gives
  J_0 >= (1/4) 2 (112/125)(13/125) d^2
      = 513974241/18200000000
      > 0.0282403 nats.
This lower bound, rather than the displayed floating-point evaluation, is enough for the theorem.

For lambda>0, couple the lambda chain and the lambda=0 chain with the same X_0. Their two-state paths through time 1 differ with probability lambda; using the same emission randomness when states agree shows
  TV( law(Theta,Y_0,Y_1)_lambda,
      law(Theta,Y_0,Y_1)_0 ) <= lambda.
Apply the finite-alphabet entropy continuity inequality
  |H(P)-H(Q)| <= t log(m-1) + h_2(t)
when TV(P,Q)=t. Since
  I(Theta;Y_1|Y_0)
    = H(Theta,Y_0)+H(Y_1,Y_0)-H(Y_0)-H(Theta,Y_1,Y_0),
the four relevant alphabet sizes are 8,16,4,32, and therefore
  |J_lambda-J_0|
    <= lambda[log 7+log 15+log 3+log 31] + 4 h_2(lambda).    (6)
For lambda<=10^(-10), the right side is less than 1.1 x 10^(-8) nats. With lambda_n=10^(-10)/n, the conservative bound above and (6) give J_{lambda_n}>0.02824. Also Bernoulli's inequality gives
  w_n >= (1/2)(1-(n-1)lambda_n)
       >= (1/2)(1-10^(-10)).
Combining with (4), for every n>=1,
  inf_{all raw-path learners} sup_{theta in {-1,+1}}
      E_{S~P_{lambda_n,theta}^{(n)}} R_theta(q_S)
       > 0.0141 nats.                                        (7)
Thus no uniform finite-sample guarantee with risk tending to zero as n grows can depend only on known D=2, alphabet size 4, and the fixed one-step task. A mixing lower bound (or another coverage/observability condition) must enter.

The same overlap also lower-bounds model identification. Since the two length-n path laws share subprobability mass w_n,
  TV(P_{lambda_n,+}^{(n)},P_{lambda_n,-}^{(n)}) <= 1-w_n.
Any equal-prior test of theta therefore has error at least w_n/2, which is greater than 0.2499999999. This applies to the full-path likelihood-ratio test and any model-selection baseline using the same n outputs. The predictive-risk statement (7) is stronger for the stated task: it shows a constant loss lower bound even for predictors that do not attempt to identify theta.

5. Matched raw-path baseline and cost

The baseline is the strongest same-interface comparator: exact full-path likelihood/model selection over the two HMMs, with the same n observed symbols and no extra oracle. The lower bound was proved for every measurable learner, so the full-path baseline cannot beat it. In the common state-0 component its observations have a theta-independent law; the common-component argument remains valid even though the learner computes the marginal hidden-state likelihood by summing all possible hidden paths.

Acquisition cost is n consecutive alphabet symbols, n emissions, and n-1 hidden transitions generated by the one stationary stream. There are zero resets and zero action/query interventions. The lower bound is statistical and allows unlimited computation; charging forward-algorithm or optimization cost cannot improve it. The verifier script in the scratch directory checks J_0, the finite-sum J_lambda, the rational event-wise Pinsker bound, the entropy-continuity allowance, and the resulting lower bound.

6. What the identity does and does not buy

Equation (1) is a rank-free population approximation statement. If the true decoder P(F|T) were supplied, the optimal prediction error after compression would be exactly I(F;H|T). This removes parameter directions irrelevant to the specified prediction task and avoids demanding recovery of a hidden-state labeling.

From one path, however, the discarded-information term and the conditional decoder are not observed quantities. The lower bound shows that even the full-history representation T=H can have a fitting/acquisition obstruction: with constant probability the training data are drawn from a component where the unknown predictive direction is absent. No algebraic re-expression of the population loss can infer that direction from those same observations. The exact identity is therefore not a rank-free approximation oracle without additional acquisition/coverage assumptions.

A sufficient theorem would need additional quantitative conditions, for example a lower bound on mixing/absolute-regularity rate for one-path concentration, plus a lower bound on relevant-history mass or an alternative smoothing guarantee, and an explicit method to estimate/validate the statistic and decoder from the same data. No such conditions are assumed in this task. The counterexample does not rule out a rank-free theorem under stated mixing and observability margins.

7. Failure conditions, scope, and UNKNOWN

Established:
- Exact decomposition of stationary expected conditional finite-H KL into predictive information discarded by a statistic and fitting error.
- A finite-sample lower bound for known hidden cap D=2, four output symbols, positive noisy emissions, stationary ergodic transitions, one unreset raw path, and the one-step stationary-history KL loss.
- The lower bound applies even when transition/emission nuisance values are revealed to the learner and hence also obstructs the larger unknown-transition/emission problem.
- The same-data full-path likelihood/model-selection baseline is covered because (7) applies to every learner.

Not established:
- A uniform one-path learning theorem under a positive mixing bound, or a finite-sample estimator of I(F;H|T).
- An impossibility result if the evaluator supplies independent reset trajectories, a lower mixing bound, or a conditional-sampling/Hankel oracle. The lower bound uses the explicitly chosen one-symbol history and one-step future; it does not assert the same risk for conditioning on the entire infinite past or for every (L,H).
- A claim that all candidate representations require full model identification; (1) shows the opposite at the population oracle level.
- Novelty or priority beyond the cited literature; no exhaustive review or expert review was performed.

The ergodicity qualification matters. At lambda=0 the model is stationary but non-ergodic; that exact boundary gives J_0. The lower bound itself uses lambda_n>0, so every selected counterexample is ergodic, but its mixing time diverges with n. This is the failure mode when no transition-gap or mixing lower bound is supplied. If such a lower bound is assumed, (7) no longer applies as stated and a separate positive theorem may be possible.
