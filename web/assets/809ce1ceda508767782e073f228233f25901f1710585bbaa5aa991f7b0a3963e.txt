DEEPER ATTACK: NEAR-TOP PARTICLE-HOLE / JACOBI DUALITY

For invertible F, Jacobi's complementary-minor identity gives, for |I|=|J|=k,

  |det F[I,J]|^2 = |det F|^2 |det G[J^c,I^c]|^2,  G=F^{-1}.

If I and J are disjoint, set K=J^c and L=I^c. Then
|K|=|L|=n-k, K union L=[n], and |K intersect L|=h=n-2k. Therefore

 c_k(F) = |det F|^2
   sum_{|K|=|L|=n-k, K union L=[n], |K intersect L|=h}
      |det G[K,L]|^2.                                      (J)

This is the exact high-density dual. When k is close to n/2, h is small, but
the inverse minors remain size n-k, and the condition is *exactly h common
indices*, not disjoint h-minors. Consequently it does not convert the dense
sector to the existing low-k estimator on F^{-1}. The failure is explicit:

  n=4, k=1, h=2, F=I+2 E_12, G=F^{-1}=I-2 E_12.

Directly c_1(F)=4. The right-hand side of (J) is 4, contributed by a 3x3
inverse minor with 2 common indices. But c_2(G)=0 because G has only diagonal
entries and one off-diagonal entry, so no disjoint pair of 2-sets supports a
nonzero 2x2 minor. Thus the tempting substitution
c_k(F) ?= |det F|^2 c_{n-2k}(F^{-1}) is false even on a rational unit-
determinant shear.

The finite exact audit `near_top_jacobi_audit.py` checked (J) on 50 invertible
rational matrices for n=2,3,4,5 and all k<=floor(n/2), and the counterexample
above. Output is `near_top_jacobi_audit.json`. This confirms the index/transpose
bookkeeping on finite cases; Jacobi's identity proves the formula in general.
It does not establish a lower bound or rule out a different high-density
transform.

OUTWARD SEARCH / PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY

The search found Okada, *Pfaffian Formulas and Schur Q-Function Identities*,
arXiv:1706.01029, whose Theorem 3.2 supplies a general Pfaffian Cauchy-Binet
identity. That validates the identity family as established machinery, not
the Rademacher estimator's novelty. The exact FPRAS still depends on the
Boolean-cube degree-k hypercontractive inequality and the estimator-specific
Gram/Pfaffian assembly.

The full Celis et al. paper, *On the Complexity of Constrained Determinantal
Point Processes*, arXiv:1608.00554v3, was checked. It proves exact algorithms
for a single budget constraint with running time polynomial in the unary
cost magnitude and gives an approximation-preserving hardness connection to
mixed discriminants for binary-encoded budget instances. Our sum is indeed a
constrained DPP partition function on the 2n paired columns of
V=[e_1,v_1,...,e_n,v_n], with the rule “select both columns from a site or
neither, and select k site-pairs.” But no reduction from their binary-budget
hard instances to this particular pair-block constraint was found. Their
binary-budget theorem therefore cannot be cited as AP-hardness, nor as a
negative result against this estimator. The precise relation is a useful
future reduction target, still open here.

UPDATED STATUS / NEXT GATE

The low-sector estimator's core proof survives this independent attack:
Pfaffian Cauchy-Binet gives unbiasedness, and scalar degree-k hypercontractivity
plus Minkowski gives E[X_k^2] <= 4*9^k c_k^2. The exact formal-square-root
coefficient computes each nonnegative sample without eigenvalues. Existing
finite checks remain the only executable validation of that estimator; this
revision adds an exact audit of the Jacobi dual and its failed shortcut.

No dense-sector FPRAS, approximation-hardness theorem, new structural class,
novelty clearance, physical-state compiler, or peer-reviewed result has been
obtained. For k=O(log n), the estimator is polynomial; for k=Theta(n), the
proved 9^k bound remains exponential. The next decisive gate is either (i) a
valid reduction from the binary-budget constrained-DPP hard family to this
all-or-none paired-column subclass, or (ii) a variance theorem exploiting
additional structure of the Pfaffian coefficient polynomial at high degree.
Neither is currently in hand.
