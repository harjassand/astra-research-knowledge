VERDICT
No fatal mathematical gap found. The claimed singular-support transport,
strong-convexity stability and O_(M,lambda)(b^(1/8)) bound are valid. The
bounded-Holevo broadcasting=>EB statement is indeed equivalent to its fixed
two-sided-sandwich restriction, after spelling out the routine common-support
channel compression described below. The broad statement remains OPEN.

There is also a sharper valid transport argument: compatible-smoothing
preservation improves from O(b^(1/8)) to O(b^(1/4)) at fixed M,lambda. This
does not remove the exponential-in-accuracy smoothing constants or prove
EB reconstruction. Exact formulas and derivation are below.

1. MINIMIZER / STRONG CONVEXITY / CHANNEL IMPORT

Fix density sigma, M>=1, lambda>0, and K_sigma={tau>=0, tau<=M sigma,
Tr tau<=1}. This is nonempty compact convex in finite dimension. Every
feasible tau is supported on supp(sigma). On that support Q_sigma(tau)
=||sigma^(-1/4)tau sigma^(-1/4)||_2^2 is a positive definite quadratic
form. The root fidelity f(rho,tau) is concave in tau and continuous,
including zero eigenvalues. Thus J=-f+lambda Q has a unique minimizer G.

For Hermitian X supported on sigma, choose its sign unitary U (extended
arbitrarily on the zero eigenspaces). Schatten Holder and Tr sigma=1 give

 ||sigma^(1/4) U sigma^(1/4)||_2 <= ||sigma^(1/4)||_4^2=1,
 ||X||_1=Tr U X <= sqrt(Q_sigma(X)).

For Y=sigma^(-1/4)tau sigma^(-1/4), tau<=M sigma implies
0<=Y<=M sqrt(sigma). Although squaring is not operator-monotone, the
TRACE inequality is valid: Tr[Y(M sqrt(sigma)-Y)]>=0. Therefore
Q_sigma(tau)<=M Tr[Y sqrt(sigma)]=M Tr tau<=M.

The exact quadratic identity and concavity give
J((1-t)tau+t v) <= (1-t)J(tau)+tJ(v)
                  -lambda t(1-t)Q_sigma(v-tau).
Minimality at tau yields J(v)-J(tau)>=lambda(1-t)Q_sigma(v-tau);
letting t down to0 proves the stated strong-convexity gap. No gradient,
boundary differentiability or invertibility of rho is assumed.

For a CPTP T, fidelity data processing applies to subnormalized tau by
writing tau=s omega and using homogeneity sqrt(s). Sandwiched-Renyi-2
DPI similarly gives Q_(T sigma)(T tau)<=Q_sigma(tau): its normalized
form is exp(D_2(omega||sigma))=Q_sigma(omega), and multiplying by s^2
extends it. Since T tau<=M T sigma, all output inverses are on the
appropriate support. Zero tau is trivial. Generalized inverses do not
introduce any condition-number constants.

2. ORIGINAL TRANSPORT CONSTANTS CHECK

Let d_s=||sigma-nu||_1 and tau=sqrt(sigma) A sqrt(sigma), 0<=A<=M I,
extending A by zero outside supp(sigma). Put tau'=sqrt(nu) A sqrt(nu).
It satisfies tau'<=M nu even when the reference supports differ.

Expanding tau'-tau into two square-root-difference terms, Holder gives
||tau'-tau||_1<=2M||sqrt(nu)-sqrt(sigma)||_2<=2M sqrt(d_s)=delta.
If t'=Tr tau'>1, clipping kappa=tau'/t' removes trace t'-1<=delta;
otherwise kappa=tau'. Hence kappa in K_nu and ||kappa-tau||_1<=2delta.
The original 4M sqrt(d_s) distance bound is correct.

The exact bounded-likelihood identity is
Q_sigma(tau)=Tr[A sqrt(sigma) A sqrt(sigma)],
Q_nu(tau')=Tr[A sqrt(nu) A sqrt(nu)].
It remains valid when A has components outside supp(nu), because the
outer sqrt(nu) factors annihilate them. Expanding their difference gives
two terms, each bounded by M^2||sqrt(nu)-sqrt(sigma)||_2. Thus
|Q_nu(tau')-Q_sigma(tau)|<=2M^2 sqrt(d_s). Downward clipping only lowers Q.

For densities rho,rho' and positive trace<=1 tau,kappa,
|f(rho,tau)-f(rho',kappa)|
 <=||sqrt(rho)-sqrt(rho')||_2||sqrt(tau)||_2
   +||sqrt(rho')||_2||sqrt(tau)-sqrt(kappa)||_2
 <=sqrt(d_r)+sqrt(||tau-kappa||_1).
Consequently the original one-way objective bound holds with
xi_old=sqrt(d_r)+2sqrt(M)d_s^(1/4)+2lambda M^2 sqrt(d_s).
Applying the same transport in reverse bounds the two minimum values by
|h(rho,sigma)-h(rho',nu)|<=xi_old. It does not assert symmetric Q closeness
after clipping; only the one-way bound is required in each direction.

3. COMPATIBILITY ARGUMENT CHECK

Set tau=G(rho,sigma), nu=T sigma, tau_1=G(T rho,nu). T tau is feasible
for the second problem and DPI gives J_(T rho,nu)(T tau)<=h(rho,sigma).
The two-sided minimum-value estimate gives
J_(T rho,nu)(T tau)-h(T rho,nu)<=xi_old.
Strong convexity and Q>=trace-norm-squared imply
||T tau-tau_1||_1<=sqrt(xi_old/lambda).

Transporting tau to kappa gives J_(T rho,nu)(kappa)<=h(rho,sigma)+xi_old
<=h(T rho,nu)+2xi_old, so
||kappa-tau_1||_1<=sqrt(2xi_old/lambda).
The triangle inequality now yields exactly

 ||T tau-tau||_1 <= (1+sqrt(2))sqrt(xi_old/lambda)+4M sqrt(d_s).

If half trace errors on rho and sigma are <=b, d_r,d_s<=2b; at fixed
M,lambda the leading exponent is b^(1/8). This is UNIFORM over rho,
dimension, number of labels, supports and the channel. The sigma need
only be approximately preserved. The bound never inserts a fixed
smoothing error into the preservation error of the smoothed experiment.

If T sigma=sigma exactly, the reported better dr^(1/4)/sqrt(lambda)
constant follows directly in the OLD feasible set: by fidelity variation,
J_(rho,sigma)(T tau)<=J_(T rho,sigma)(T tau)+sqrt(d_r)
<=h(rho,sigma)+sqrt(d_r). Strong convexity around tau finishes it.

4. SHARPER TRANSPORT: QUARTER-POWER COMPATIBILITY

The previous fidelity step unnecessarily replaces a structured transport
by generic trace-distance continuity. Let W_sigma=sqrt(sigma) A^(1/2),
so tau=W_sigma W_sigma*. Since

 f(rho,W W*)=Tr sqrt(sqrt(rho) W W* sqrt(rho))
             =||sqrt(rho) W||_1,

the same A gives the stronger direct estimate

 |f(rho,tau')-f(rho,tau)|
 <=||sqrt(rho)[sqrt(nu)-sqrt(sigma)] A^(1/2)||_1
 <=sqrt(M)||sqrt(nu)-sqrt(sigma)||_2<=sqrt(M d_s).

Also t'-Tr tau=Tr[A(nu-sigma)], so its absolute value is <=M d_s.
If t'>1, downward clipping costs t'-1<=M d_s in trace norm, and

 f(rho,tau')-f(rho,kappa)
  =(sqrt(t')-1)f(rho,kappa)<=sqrt(t')-1<=(t'-1)/2<=M d_s/2.

Hence the objective/minimum-value argument works instead with

 xi_new=sqrt(d_r)+(sqrt(M)+2lambda M^2)sqrt(d_s)+(M/2)d_s,
 ||kappa-tau||_1<=2M sqrt(d_s)+M d_s.

Repeating Section3 verbatim proves

 ||T G-G||_1 <= (1+sqrt(2))sqrt(xi_new/lambda)
               +2M sqrt(d_s)+M d_s.

This is O_(M,lambda)(b^(1/4)) when both half trace errors are <=b.
All identities allow singular references and rank-deficient A. This
improvement was sent to the proof worker for separate independent checking;
it is an analytic derivation, not inferred from a numerical optimizer.

5. SUBSTATE, NORMALIZATION AND REFERENCE CHECK

The source Jain–Nayak1103.6067 Theorem1 uses SQUARED fidelity, requires
supp(rho)<=supp(sigma), and gives tau_0<=[(1-eta)^(-1)
2^(D_obs_bits/eta)]sigma with squared fidelity>=1-eta. Its stated
D_obs_bits<=D_rel_bits+1 exactly yields
M=(1-eta)^(-1)exp((C+ln2)/eta). Thus the report's bits/nats conversion
and fidelity convention match the primary theorem.

With eta=epsilon^2/8 and lambda=eta/M, minimality and Q(tau_0)<=M imply
f(rho,G)>=1-2eta=1-epsilon^2/4. Thus t=Tr G>=f^2>=1-epsilon^2/2>1/2.
The normalized smoother Ghat=G/t satisfies Ghat<=2M sigma. Fidelity
homogeneity and the ordinary fidelity/trace-distance bound give the
even stronger T(rho,Ghat)<=epsilon/sqrt(2), so the report's epsilon
bound is safe. Linearity gives exactly
||T Ghat-Ghat||_1=||T G-G||_1/t, with no normalization-dependent
channel choice.

For lambda<=1/4, J_(sigma,sigma)(v)>=-sqrt(t)+lambda t^2,
t=Tr v in[0,1]. This scalar function decreases there and takes its minimum
at t=1; v=sigma attains it. Uniqueness proves G(sigma,sigma)=sigma and
therefore Ghat(sigma,sigma)=sigma. The chosen lambda<1/8 is admissible.
Thus adding sigma to the original experiment puts EXACT sigma into the
normalized smoothed experiment, rather than a nearby substitute.

6. CENTER / SUPPORT / SUPREMUM / SEQUENCE QUANTIFIERS

Closing E is harmless in finite dimension: entropy and channel errors are
continuous, and finite-prior capacity is unchanged. The compact convex hull
of {(rho,S(rho)):rho in E} has real affine dimension<=d^2. Maximizing
S(mean)-mean entropy gives an attained finite prior with<=d^2+1 atoms.
Its mean sigma satisfies D(rho||sigma)<=C(E) for every rho: mixing in
mass t on rho has derivative D(rho||sigma)-C(E). A component outside
supp(sigma) instead gives an infinite positive directional derivative,
contradicting maximality. The center argument therefore meets the support
assumption of the substate import.

Routine clarification required in the source: restricting an arbitrary
broadcaster's OUTPUT to supp(sigma) is not automatically CPTP. Write
P=supp(sigma), and use the CPTP map
R(X)=P X P+Tr[(I-P)X]sigma, with codomain B(PH).
It fixes every original promised state and sigma. Define
B'=(R tensor R) B restricted to inputs B(PH); output trace errors only
decrease by contractivity. Channels on PH extend to H by input pinching
and fixed preparation on Pperp; EB maps remain EB. Hence this is a fully
valid support restriction with no additional approximation. The source's
short sentence can be expanded with this formula; it does not invalidate
the theorem.

Choose M,lambda from ONE uniform capacity bound C, not a growing per-n
bound. Then all smoothing/dilution constants stay independent of n.
Because sigma is a finite mixture of promised states, EVERY marginal of
the same B approximately preserves it with the same error. The compatible
lemma can consequently be applied simultaneously to all promised rho and
both marginal endomorphisms, using the SAME G and SAME broadcaster.

The estimate e(E)<=e(Ehat)+2epsilon holds channel by channel via
contractivity and the two approximation legs. It entails no minimax
exchange. For fixed theta in(0,1), U=2M>2 and a=theta/(U-1) satisfy
0<a<=theta<1. The diluted tau=(1-a)sigma+a rho lies between
(1-theta)sigma and(1+theta)sigma, and sigma remains an included state.
Linearity gives b2(F)<=b2(Ehat). Operator monotonicity of log gives
D(tau||sigma)<=ln(1+theta), hence C(F)<=ln(1+theta).

For EVERY single EB Lambda, the affine inverse identity gives
sup_Ehat T(Lambda rho,rho)<=(2-a) sup_F T(Lambda tau,tau)/a.
Taking infima preserves it. Since sigma is included, its reconstruction
error is controlled; this inclusion is load-bearing. There is no family
of label-dependent reconstruction channels in the reduction.

Assuming the fixed-sandwich implication, fix epsilon BEFORE taking n to
infinity. Uniform compatible preservation gives b2(F_n)->0; thus
e(F_n)->0 and e(Ehat_n)->0 at the fixed positive affine factor a.
Then limsup_n e(E_n)<=2epsilon; taking epsilon down to0 proves the broad
implication. The reverse implication uses the automatic capacity bound.
If constructing a contrapositive counterexample from failure of convergence,
first pass to a subsequence with e(E_n)>=c>0 and choose epsilon<c/4.
The report's contrapositive wording should make that subsequence explicit.
