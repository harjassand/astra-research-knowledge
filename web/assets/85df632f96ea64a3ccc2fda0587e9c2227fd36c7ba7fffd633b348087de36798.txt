QUANTUM COMPLEXITY — QUANTITATIVE SPARSE-LIFT GATE AND FRESH QPCP INTERFACE
7 October 2026. Internal derivation; external correctness and priority unreviewed.

STRONGEST CONCRETE RESULT

A universal approximately sparse lift cannot repair the old exact universal-
monomial obstruction cheaply. The statement below allows arbitrary frame
weights, arbitrary sparse complex matrices, and no representation, continuity,
positivity, homomorphism, or norm assumption on those matrices.

Let 2 <= d <= N, 1 <= s < d, and V:C^d -> C^N be an isometry. Put

  b(d,N,s) = (d-s)/(d-s+1)
             [binom(N,s) binom(d-1,s-1)]^(-1/(d-s)).

If EVERY U in SU(d) has an N-by-N matrix M_U with <=s nonzero entries
per row and ||M_U V - VU||_op <= epsilon, then

  epsilon^2 >= b(d,N,s).                                      (1)

More strongly the Haar-average best possible squared operator error is >=b.
For Haar random U, the probability that ANY such M achieves error <=epsilon
is at most (1-b)/(1-epsilon^2), for epsilon<1. This last bound can be trivial
at small d; it tends to zero in the polynomial-resource regime below.

For monomial lifts (s=1), even if the lift is not required to be unitary,

  N >= [(d-1)/(d epsilon^2)]^(d-1).                            (2)

For d=2^n and log N,s each polynomial in n, b=1-O(poly(n)/2^n).
The universal error must approach 1, the error of the zero matrix. For a
fixed epsilon<1, s=Omega(2^n/(log N+n)) when s<=d/2. Thus polynomial physical
label bits require exponentially many row entries. A common finite frame
cannot cheaply support approximate universal logical operations merely by
abandoning exact intertwining or the group-homomorphism requirement.

STATUS AND SCOPE

(1) is an internally proved candidate lemma, reconstructed below from elementary
complex-sphere volume. Its geometry is standard Grassmann quantization. I do
not claim a new mathematical technique or a field-breaking theorem. The new
scoped contribution in this run is its explicit approximate sparse-lift
interface and resource inequality, extending the supplied exact-all-word gate.

This does NOT obstruct the desired selected-positive-test sparse QMA reduction.
That target asks only for a common V and positive sparse C,B with protected
invalid states and alpha H <= V*BV <= beta H; B need not preserve ran V.
It does not ask for sparse lifts of every logical unitary. Short selected words,
term-specific frames, sector coupling, noninvariant B, and general Hamiltonian
encodings remain outside (1). Approximate lifts of individual universal gates
also remain outside unless one charges their accumulated word error and the
word-net length. There is no locality-preserving quantum PCP resolution here.

FULL DERIVATION

Write the rows of V as r_j in C^(1xd); let a_j=||r_j|| and ignore zero rows.
For every s-element coordinate subset S of {1,...,N}, let L_S be the complex
row span of the rows r_i for i in S. It has dimension <=s. There are at most
K=binom(N,s) such subspaces. Define, for a unit row vector z,

  D(z) = min_S ||z - P_(L_S)z||^2,

where projection notation means row-space orthogonal projection. All row
supports of size <=s are covered, since they can be padded to size s.

For any s-row-sparse M, the jth row of MV belongs to one L_S. Consequently

  ||MV-VU||_F^2 >= sum_j a_j^2 D((r_j/a_j)U).

Since rank(MV-VU)<=d and sum_j a_j^2=Tr(V*V)=d,

  ||MV-VU||_op^2 >= q_V(U)
     := d^(-1) sum_j a_j^2 D((r_j/a_j)U).                    (3)

For Haar U, each normalized nonzero row (r_j/a_j)U is a Haar unit vector.
No independence of these rows is needed. Thus

  E_U q_V(U) = E_z D(z).                                    (4)

The equality removes any equal-row-norm/tight-uniform-frame assumption.

For a fixed s-plane L, squared distance T=1-||P_L z||^2 of a Haar complex
unit vector has distribution Beta(d-s,s). This follows directly by writing
z=g/||g||, where the |g_i|^2 are independent exponential random variables:
the sums over L and L-perp are independent Gamma(s,1),Gamma(d-s,1).
For 0<=t<=1 the beta density therefore gives

  Pr[T<=t]
   = ((d-1)!/((d-s-1)!(s-1)!))
      integral_0^t u^(d-s-1)(1-u)^(s-1) du
   <= binom(d-1,s-1) t^(d-s).                               (5)

For a span of dimension <s, enlarge it to an s-plane, which only increases
this small-distance probability. A union bound over all supports gives

  Pr[D(z)<=t] <= A t^q,
  A=binom(N,s)binom(d-1,s-1), q=d-s.

Integrating the tail up to t0=A^(-1/q)<=1 yields

  E D >= integral_0^t0 (1-A t^q)dt
        = q/(q+1) A^(-1/q) = b(d,N,s).

Combine with (3)-(4) for (1), including the best-M Haar-average version.
No measurable choice of M_U is needed: q_V(U) is continuous and is a lower
bound for every M. Finally 0<=q_V(U)<=1. If q_V(U)<=epsilon^2 on a set of
Haar mass p, then E q_V <= p epsilon^2+(1-p). Together with E q_V>=b this
gives p<=(1-b)/(1-epsilon^2). Every successfully lifted U lies in that set.

The resource inequality follows from

  s log(eN/s) + log binom(d-1,s-1)
    >= (d-s) log[(d-s)/((d-s+1)epsilon^2)],                   (6)

using binom(N,s)<=(eN/s)^s. Logs in (6) are natural. For s<=d/2 and fixed
epsilon<1, its right side is Omega(d), while its left is at most
O(s(log N+log d)). Formula (2) is (1) specialized to s=1.

FINITE-WORD / FINITE-DESIGN INTERFACES

If a selected family of logical unitaries is an eta-net of SU(d) in operator
norm, and every member has an s-row lift with error <=epsilon_0, then apply
(1) with epsilon=epsilon_0+eta. Acquiring such a net and all lifts is charged.
For monomial unitary lifts of generators with error tau, a length-T product
has error <=T tau and is still monomial. It only gives a universal obstruction
when length-T logical words form an eta-net. An uncharged net or word length
would be an invalid transfer. For s>1, products may have s^T row entries.

There is also a finite design version. Suppose the logical unitaries form an
exact unitary k-design, and their mean best lift squared error is <=epsilon^2.
For any a with epsilon^2<a<1, set K=binom(N,s). Then necessarily

  K >= (1-epsilon^2/a)(1-a)^k (d)_k/(s)_k,                  (7)

where (x)_k=x(x+1)...(x+k-1). For a Haar unit vector and an s-plane,
E||P_L z||^(2k)=(s)_k/(d)_k. An exact unitary k-design has the same moments.
Markov plus the support union bound gives
Pr[D<=a] <= K(s)_k/((d)_k(1-a)^k), while Markov on D gives
Pr[D<=a]>=1-epsilon^2/a. Average over the row weights to get (7).
This avoids a continuum quantifier, but high design order and acquisition
cannot be omitted; a low-order Clifford design does not simulate Haar caps.

DECISIVE GENERIC COUNTEREXAMPLE AT THE COMBINATORIAL-TO-ENERGY GATE

For B>=1 put r=2^-B and define rational 1-qubit projectors

  P0 = [[0,0],[0,1]],
  Pr = (1+r^2)^(-1) [[r^2,r],[r,1]],
  h  = (P0+Pr)/2.

The distinct kernels imply every nonzero vector violates at least one term;
|0> exactly satisfies P0 and violates Pr. Hence combinatorial energy=1/2.
Yet

  lambda_min(h) = (1 - 1/sqrt(1+r^2))/2,
  r^2/[4(1+r^2)] <= lambda_min(h) <= r^2/4.                 (8)

For n qubits take H_n=n^(-1)sum_j h^(j), uniformly distributing its 2n
projectors. At each qubit no reduced density matrix can have zero expectation
against both projectors, so every global, including entangled, state violates
at least n terms. The product |0>^tensor n achieves exactly n. Thus the
combinatorial gap stays 1/2, locality is 1, each qubit degree is 2, row sparsity
is <=n+1, and normalized ground energy remains (8). Taking B=n gives
polynomial-bit rational coefficients but exponentially small spectral energy.

This is an elementary near-parallel-projector counterexample, not a claim of
novelty or a counterexample to the special BNZ output Hamiltonians. Its n-varying
projectors also lie outside their fixed finite input constraint-set assumption.
It proves that generic finite-bit, sparse, bounded-degree/locality assumptions
plus combinatorial soundness cannot supply an energy upgrade. A quantitative
angle/conditioning/robust-extraction premise is indispensable. Exact support
rank or exact violated-term counts do not certify spectral margins.

PRACTICAL VERIFICATION / COSTS

1. A universal frame proposal can be rejected using (1) before building its
   astronomical label space. Charge log N physical basis bits, s row entries,
   and epsilon in OPERATOR norm. Per-entry agreement is insufficient.
2. For an explicit small V, enumerate its s-row spans and compute q_V(U) for
   test unitaries. It lower-bounds every possible sparse lift, so one need not
   solve for all M. Cost O(binom(N,s)) subspace factorizations plus row tests;
   this enumeration is not efficient for large N/s. Haar unitary generation
   also costs at least matrix-sized work. Finite fixtures do not certify all U.
3. A tensor-network proposal that sparsifies EVERY virtual-bond basis change
   through one common lift obeys the same lemma with d the bond dimension.
   Fixed tensor families / selected gauges are outside. No PEPS contraction or
   acquisition speedup follows from this gate alone.
4. For an amplification proposal, measure smallest eigenvalues or prove a
   quantitative robust-extraction/angle bound. Replaying exact constraints or
   compiling a tensor network only addresses the combinatorial contract.
