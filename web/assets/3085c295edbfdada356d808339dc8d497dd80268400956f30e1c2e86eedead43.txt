1. CONTRACT AND UNIFORM OBSTRUCTION

  C_(m,k): Sym^M((C^3)^*) -> V_(k,m).

The cold source is NOW MIXED, with isotropic density

  rho_(M,q),v=Z_M(q)^(-1) q^(M-n_v),
  Z_M(q)=sum_(N=0..M)(N+1)q^N,
  q in [q0,q1], 0<q0<=q1<1.                              (1.1)

The unknown direction v ranges over all CP2. The comparison target in
V_(k,m) is any normalized isotropic physical Gibbs state

  sigma_(k,m),v(z)=Z'_(k,m)(z)^(-1) z^N_v, 0<=z<=1,

where at v=e3 the physical number is N=E33-lambda3. In the harmonic
fundamental/dual realization, N=n3_fund+(m-n3_dual). The target has equal
eigenvalues within the whole number-N layer, including its distinct U(2)
components. This is the degenerate p1=p2 plane-only control, not the full
positive-critical-c iid model.

Uniformly along k>=1, M->infinity, k/M->0 and q in the stated compact
temperature interval,

  inf_(z in [0,1]) sup_v
    T(C_(m,k)(rho_(M,q),v),sigma_(k,m),v(z))
      >=[q(1-q)^3/2+o(1)] k/M.                           (1.2)

The o(1) is uniform in q and independent of target temperature z. In
particular k~sqrt(m) forces a whole-trace defect of order m^(-1/2).
Any retuning of the source temperature within [q0,q1], including
q_source=q_target+O(k/m), retains this defect. No claim is made here for
source temperatures escaping to zero or one.

An explicit finite sufficient domain is M>=4 and

  k/M<=(1-q1)/1204,

on which

  T(...)>=q0(1-q1)^3 k/(4M).                             (1.3)

The loose threshold is a proof constant, not a computational boundary.

2. A BOUNDED TWO-COMPONENT TEST THAT ANY ISOTROPIC TARGET FAILS

At physical N=1 the branching rule gives two components:

  Pi_+: (s,t)=(0,1), spin (k+1)/2, dimension k+2;
  Pi_-: (s,t)=(1,0), spin (k-1)/2, dimension k.

For every target temperature z, its number-one eigenvalue is a common
scalar on both components. Therefore the fixed Hermitian operator

  H=Pi_--[k/(k+2)]Pi_+                                  (2.1)

has target expectation zero. Its spectral interval is
[-k/(k+2),1], with width less than 2. If the converted source has number-one
eigenvalues c_+,c_- on these components, then

  Tr H C_(m,k)(rho_(M,q))=k(c_--c_+),
  T(converted,target)>=k(c_--c_+)/[1+k/(k+2)]
                      >=k(c_--c_+)/2                    (2.2)

whenever c_->=c_+. For general v rotate this witness covariantly. Covariance
makes the error and the expectation independent of v; the witness is used
only for the converse test and is not an acquired decoder frame.

3. EXACT POSITIVE SOURCE MIXTURE AND CANONICAL TILT

Write B=diag(q,q,1) on the dual fundamental. The coherent resolution and
the projective change of variables v=B^(-1/2)w/||B^(-1/2)w|| give the exact
positive source mixture

  rho_(M,q)=int mu_q(w) P_bar(w)^M dw,
  mu_q(w)=d_M q^(-2)/[Z_M(q)(w^dag B^(-1)w)^(M+3)],
  d_M=(M+1)(M+2)/2.                                     (3.1)

The exponent M comes from the coherent tensor amplitude; the additional
3 and determinant factor q^(-2) are the CP2 Jacobian. Trace normalization
makes this a probability measure. With u=1-|w3|^2 its radial law is

  2u du * d_M q^(-2)/[Z_M(q)(1+(q^(-1)-1)u)^(M+3)].     (3.2)

It follows from the PURE converter identity that
C_(m,k)(rho_(M,q)) is exactly the mixture of normalized top-fiber projectors
P_(k,m),w/(k+1) under (3.1). This positive mixture is used to compute the
actual converter, not to infer a lower in an invalid direction.

Let i=0,...,k index the N=0 spin basis, with E22 weight lambda2+i. Under
the canonical tilt mixing e1,e3, that vector is highest for SU(2)_(13)
with root length M-i. Its N=1 density amplitude is

  u(1-u)^(M-i-1) E31 |i><i| E13.

The exact polar creation channels on number zero have coefficients
sqrt(m) in Pi_+ and sqrt(m+k+1) in Pi_-. Their SU(2) Clebsch weights
are respectively (i+1)/(k+1) and (k-i)/(k+1). This gives squared root
norm m(i+1)/(k+1)+(m+k+1)(k-i)/(k+1)=M-i, consistent with the exact
SU(2)_(13) binomial amplitude. Both coefficients are retained.

Averaging over the internal U(2) makes the converted density scalar on
Pi_+,Pi_-. Put r=k+1 and

  I_i=int mu_q(w) u(1-u)^(M-i-1)dw.

The exact component eigenvalues are

  c_+=m/[r^2(k+2)] sum_(i=0..k)(i+1)I_i,
  c_-=(m+k+1)/[r^2 k] sum_(i=0..k)(k-i)I_i.             (3.3)

The denominators include the input fiber normalization r, the Clebsch
weight r, and the output component dimensions. Omitting any of those
factors would change the branch/converse normalization.

4. ELEMENTARY BETA FORMULA AND UNIFORM EXPANSION

Set A=q^(-1)-1 and make the exact substitution

  t=u/[q+(1-q)u].

Then u=q t/[1-(1-q)t], 1-u=(1-t)/[1-(1-q)t], and the integral in (3.2)
with its extra u(1-u)^(M-i-1) becomes a beta expectation. Precisely,

  I_i=4d_M q/[Z_M(q)(M-i)(M-i+1)(M-i+2)] F_i,
  F_i=E_(T~Beta(3,M-i))[(1-(1-q)T)^i],
  I_0=2q/[Z_M(q)M].                                    (4.1)

All formulas are exact for i<=k and m>=1; M-i>=m prevents a singular
beta endpoint. No asymptotic integral extension or small-u truncation has
been taken.

For M>=4 and 0<=i<=M/4,

  I_i/I_0=1+3qi/M+epsilon_i,
  |epsilon_i|<=200(i^2+i)/M^2.                          (4.2)

Here is a full remainder proof. The pointwise Taylor inequality
0<=(1-x)^i-1+ix<=i(i-1)x^2/2 on [0,1] and the beta moments give

  F_i=1-3i(1-q)/(M-i+3)+R_i,
  0<=R_i<=6i(i-1)(1-q)^2/[(M-i+3)(M-i+4)].

Thus |F_i-1+3i(1-q)/M|<=[15i^2+12i]/M^2. On the other hand,

  P_i=M(M+1)(M+2)/[(M-i)(M-i+1)(M-i+2)]
      =1+3i/M+delta_i,
  |delta_i|<=[64i^2+3i]/M^2.

For the latter bound write P_i=prod_(j=0..2)(1-i/(M+j))^(-1).
Each argument is <=1/4. The product's second derivatives are <=9 there,
so multivariable Taylor remainder is at most 64i^2/M^2; its linear sum
differs from 3i/M by at most 3i/M^2. Finally F_i<=1 and 1+3i/M<=7/4.
Multiplying P_i and F_i gives an error <=[100i^2+24i]/M^2, which is
bounded by (4.2). The constants are uniform for every q in [0,1].

5. NONRADIAL DEFECT SURVIVES EVERY SCALAR TEMPERATURE REPAIR

Normalize the two weights in (3.3) into averages. Their index means are

  mean_+(i)=2k/3,
  mean_-(i)=(k-1)/3.

Using (4.2), the component difference is

  c_--c_+=q/[Z_M(q)M] [1-q+R_(m,k,q)],
  |R_(m,k,q)|<=602 k/M, M>=4, k<=M/4.                   (5.1)

To check the algebra, the unperturbed bracket before division by r is

  (m+k+1)(1+q(k-1)/M)-m(1+2qk/M)
    =r[1-q+q(2k-1)/M].

Each normalized weighted epsilon average is <=200(k^2+k)/M^2. The
remaining error after division by r is at most
200(2m+r)k/M^2<=600k/M; the displayed exact extra term costs at most
2k/M. This proves (5.1) without assuming i or k fixed.

If k/M<=(1-q1)/1204, the bracket is >=(1-q)/2. Also
Z_M(q)<=1/(1-q)^2. Consequently the positive witness expectation obeys

  k(c_--c_+)>=q0(1-q1)^3 k/(2M).

Combining with (2.2) proves (1.3). Along k/M->0, the finite geometric
normalization satisfies 1/Z_M(q)=(1-q)^2+o(1) uniformly in the stated

This is a concrete retained root-coupling term. On the critical carriers

6. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT CLOSE

The exact converter is a legal deterministic finite CPTP map with discarded
environment dimension (k+1)(k+2)/2 and explicit epsilon/symmetrizer formulas.
Neither unknown v nor an external sample is an input. Its efficient gate
realization, synthesis precision and runtime remain unproved. The witness
is bounded independently of m,k and tests the whole output density.

The result falsifies the specific proposal that C_(m,k) acting on an
isotropic cold Gibbs carrier becomes an isotropic physical Gibbs carrier
at o(k/m) after a scalar temperature adjustment. A higher-order corrected
converter, a mixture of distinct covariant Stinespring channels, or a
direct weighted score on the actual iid experiment may still succeed.
No optimization over all covariant channels has been performed.

The actual critical c>0 common-U2-twirled carrier additionally has unequal
number-one eigenvalues of its own. Its full Gibbs state retains the hot
spin and both anisotropic modes. This theorem does not replace that state
by the isotropic control and does not assert an obstruction for a converter
specifically designed to reproduce its exact contrast. A valid iid lower
must still handle those states and rectangular output-sector Kraus blocks.
