1. CANONICAL-TRACE RE-EMBEDDING
===============================

Let G be hyperlinear, with a faithful homomorphism into the metric ultraproduct of unitary groups U(n_i) equipped with normalized Hilbert–Schmidt distance. Work in the corresponding tracial matrix ultraproduct M and write tau for its normalized trace. For each g in G put t_g=tau(phi(g)); then |t_g|<=1 and

  ||phi(g)-1||_2^2 = 2 - 2 Re(t_g).

If g!=1, faithfulness in the metric ultraproduct gives ||phi(g)-1||_2>0, hence t_g!=1.

First replace each finite-dimensional unitary U by U⊕I. This is a homomorphism U(n)->U(2n) and changes normalized trace t to

  a=(1+t)/2.

For |t|<=1 and t!=1, |a|<1: equality in |1+t|<=1+|t|<=2 would require t=1. Thus |a_g|<1 for every nonidentity group element.

Next use the conjugation representation Ad(U):X |-> UXU* on M_n(C), equivalently U⊗bar(U) on C^n⊗bar(C^n). It is a homomorphism U(n)->U(n^2), and its normalized trace is

  tr_{n^2}(Ad(U)) = |tr_n(U)|^2.

Iterating k times changes trace a_g to |a_g|^(2^k), which tends to zero for every g!=1. Each map is well-defined on the Hilbert–Schmidt ultraproduct: direct sum is Lipschitz, and the tensor/conjugation map satisfies

  ||U⊗bar(U)-V⊗bar(V)||_2 <= 2 ||U-V||_2.

For each k, the composite remains injective because |a_g|^(2^k)<1 for g!=1, so the image of g remains at positive Hilbert–Schmidt distance from I. Take an outer nonprincipal ultraproduct over k, with the original ultraproduct inside. The resulting dimensions are N_{i,k}=(2n_i)^(2^k), and the induced trace satisfies tau_new(g)=0 for every g!=1 while tau_new(1)=1. The metric embedding remains faithful; in fact distinct group elements become orthogonal and have pairwise distance sqrt(2).

Extend the group map linearly to pi:C[G]->M_new. For x=sum_g c_g g, only finitely many coefficients are nonzero, and

  tau_new(pi(x)* pi(x)) = sum_g |c_g|^2.

Therefore pi is injective and preserves the canonical group trace. Since M_new is a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, it is stably finite; the unital *-subalgebra C[G] is stably finite as well. This direct-finiteness conclusion over C is already known for every group, so the contribution here is the explicit re-embedding interface, not a new class consequence.

Primary checked source: Pestov–Kwiatkowska, “An Introduction to Hyperlinear and Sofic Groups,” Theorem 4.2 and proof, especially the conjugation trace computation and iterative separation argument (PDF pp. 12–14): https://www.math.cmu.edu/~eschimme/Appalachian/PestovNotes.pdf . The source proves the stronger orthonormal re-embedding statement. The reconstruction above makes the trace formula explicit to connect it to the Cycle 3 stability bound.

2. REFINED COMMON-KERNEL STABILITY CONSEQUENCE
===============================================

Suppose the Cycle 3 conditional premise holds: a finite presentation P=<S|R> has a nonidentity word w of expanded length L which is killed by every homomorphism to every sofic group. Mal'cev residual finiteness then forces every exact finite-dimensional linear representation over every field to kill w. If G is additionally hyperlinear, first choose the canonical-trace re-embedding above. Its finite-dimensional microstates satisfy tr_n(w(A_n))->0 and are unitary, hence

  ||w(A_n)-I||_2^2 = 2 - 2 Re tr_n(w(A_n)) -> 2.

For any two generator tuples A,B in the same dimension, telescoping gives ||w(A)-w(B)||_2<=L max_s||A_s-B_s||_2. Every exact representation B has w(B)=I, so each canonical-trace microstate has distance at least ||w(A_n)-I||_2/L from every exact representation. The asymptotic lower bound is sqrt(2)/L, with no trace-microstate assumption beyond hyperlinearity because the model may be re-embedded. This still proves instability only; it does not exclude hyperlinearity. The re-embedding dimensions have no useful cost bound for the candidate's 158-digit expanded-length witness.

3. EXACT FAILURE OF THE INCIDENCE-INDEPENDENT SCALAR TRIANGLE COMPLETION
==========================================================================

Use one legal local Cartesian block with r=2, coordinate moduli s=t=2, index set i=(alpha,beta) in (Z/2)^2, and field F_7. For u,v in {1,2}, set

  X_{i,u,v}=c_{u,v},     (c_{u,v}) = [[4,5],[5,6]] over F_7,

independently of i. Every X is invertible. Its only nondegenerate 2-by-2 rectangle has cross ratio

  c_11 c_22 (c_21 c_12)^(-1) = 24/25 = 6 = -1 in F_7,

so rank(I-W)=1, the maximal possible scalar-block rank separation.

The source wiring equations on this block are

  L_{u, alpha(i)+v} X_{i,u,v} R_{v, beta(i)+u}^{-1}=I,

with cyclic coordinates modulo 2. Since i=(alpha,beta) ranges bijectively over all four pairs and shifts preserve bijectivity, for each fixed u,v these equations require

  R_{v,k}=c_{u,v} L_{u,j}  for every j,k in Z/2.

As c_uv is invertible, L_{u,j} must be independent of j and R_{v,k} independent of k. Write their constant values L_u,R_v. Then c_uv=R_v L_u^(-1); because the c_uv are scalars, their 2-by-2 determinant must vanish. But

  det([[4,5],[5,6]])=24-25=-1=6 != 0 in F_7.

Therefore no invertible choices of L and R complete this incidence-independent scalar array to the full triangle wiring. The contradiction is exact for every matrix size D as well, using c_uv I_D: after constancy, the scalar determinant obstruction remains.

The same proof works for every complete Cartesian block with coordinate moduli s,t>=r and a bijective incidence map, using c_uv=r+u+v in any field where 1,...,3r are distinct. The 2-by-2 minor on u,v=1,2 is always (r+2)(r+4)-(r+3)^2=-1. Thus the exact no-completion obstruction applies locally to the whole incidence-independent scalar family at either N65 q/r parameter pair, provided such a block is instantiated as specified.

An exact checker enumerates all 16 ordered rectangle slots in the tiny fixture and all 279,936 scalar L/R assignments after fixing the common-scalar gauge; it finds zero completions and checks the listed N65 q/r minors. A separate short exact checker in this package independently verifies the F7 cross-ratio, coordinate coverage, and zero constant scalar solutions. The exhaustive checker is `work/programmes/groups_operator/leaves/d5_simplex_candidate/cycle4_triangle_wiring/check_triangle_noncompletion.py`; the independent one is `outputs/research/groups_operator/cycle4/verify_f7_triangle.py`; the proof ledger is `work/programmes/groups_operator/leaves/d5_simplex_candidate/cycle4_triangle_wiring/RESULT.txt`.

This is a strengthened adversarial boundary relative to the Cycle 3 rectangle countermodel: the scalar family defeats the rectangle-only inference, but it cannot itself be turned into a full-wiring linear-sofic model. It does not rule out incidence-dependent matrices X_{i,u,v}, non-scalar blocks, or a different rank-metric mechanism. The candidate group's linear-soficity and hyperlinearity remain UNKNOWN.

4. PRECISE FRONTIER / NEXT DECISIVE GATES
==========================================

The coefficient-field reduction sharpens the remaining positive-characteristic gate. For each prime p it is enough to prove stable finiteness over F_p. The exact source route is Bartholdi–de Cornulier–Kochloukova, “Hopfian wreath products and the stable finiteness conjecture,” Corollary 1.8: stable finiteness over one field of characteristic p implies stable finiteness over every field of characteristic p; the proof uses local embeddings of finite coefficient sets into matrix algebras over F_p. Its Corollary 3.16 reformulates Kaplansky’s conjecture as direct finiteness of F_p[G] for every group G and every prime p. Thus a representation-level attack need only target one fixed prime field at a time. The 2026 Steinberg source independently states the characteristic-p stable-finiteness question remains open.

The hyperlinear-to-complex-trace route does not itself reach that gate: no unital field homomorphism F_p->C exists, and an HS microstate has no general rank-null defect conversion. The next decisive positive-characteristic gate is an explicit method that takes only the complex unitary metric model and produces a faithful F_p rank-ultraproduct group-algebra representation, or an independent characteristic-p detector of one-sided inverses. A verified F_p[G] one-sided inverse would be a counterexample to direct finiteness for that group, but no such witness is acquired here.

For the N65 geometry/rank mechanism, the exact next gate is to use incidence-dependent variables in the full triangle system. Either prove a uniform rank inequality from all triangles and rectangles for arbitrary GL_D(F_q) matrices, or furnish one finite set of matrices satisfying the full system while violating the intended obstruction. The scalar no-completion theorem narrows the simplest adversary but does not decide the full system.

No claim of novelty, priority, full presentation materialization, expert review, or solution of the positive-characteristic open problem is made.
