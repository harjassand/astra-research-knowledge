ZERO-HESSIAN-RANK AUDIT FOR AFFINE DETERMINANT REPRESENTATIONS
Date: 2026-10-08
Leaf: cycle3/permanent_invariant
Status: exact proof of the classical quadratic Hessian bound, plus a proved ceiling showing this invariant cannot yield a super-cubic determinant-size lower bound. No new lower bound or resolution of permanent versus determinant is claimed.

DECISION

Candidate tested: the maximum rank of the polynomial Hessian on the zero hypersurface,
  H_0(f) = max { rank Hess(f)(x) : f(x)=0 }.
For an invertible affine change x=B y+c, the Hessian transforms by congruence
  Hess_y(f(By+c)) = B^T Hess_x(f)(By+c) B,
so H_0 is invariant under invertible affine changes of variables and nonzero scalar multiplication. For m >= 2 over characteristic zero,
  H_0(perm_m) = m^2,
while for the size-r determinant,
  H_0(det_r) = 2r  (r >= 2).
More generally, for every affine-linear matrix map M(x) of size r,
  rank Hess_x det(M(x)) at any zero x is at most 2r.
Consequently any exact affine determinant representation of perm_m has
  r >= ceil(m^2/2).

This is the Mignon–Ressayre quadratic lower bound, reconstructed below. It is a permanent-specific comparison, but the invariant reaches its absolute rank ceiling m^2 on perm_m. Since its general affine-determinant comparison is linear in r, it cannot prove a super-cubic lower bound in m: its strongest possible conclusion is r >= m^2/2. Thus it kills the proposed use of zero-point Hessian rank as a route beyond the cubic ceiling mentioned in N79. This does not kill other invariants or validate N79's underlying Family 108 claim.

The unqualified variant “Hessian rank at an arbitrary point” fails even more directly: Hess(det_r) at I_r has rank r^2, not O(r). The zero-value restriction is essential.

SCOPE AND QUANTIFIERS

Let K be a characteristic-zero field (in particular Q, R, or C). Define
  perm_m(X) = sum_{sigma in S_m} product_{i=1}^m x_{i,sigma(i)}
in m^2 variables. An affine determinant representation of size r means an exact identity
  perm_m(x) = det(A_0 + sum_{i,j} x_{ij} A_{ij})
with A_0,A_{ij} in K^{r x r}; no homogeneity, regularity, symmetry, or rank promise is imposed on the coefficient matrices. The lower bound below applies to every such representation. The checker uses integer/rational inputs and exact arithmetic only.

PROOF 1: EVERY AFFINE DETERMINANT HAS SMALL HESSIAN RANK AT A ZERO

Let F(x)=det(M(x)), where M(x)=A_0+L(x) is an r x r affine-linear matrix, and let x_0 satisfy F(x_0)=0. Put M_0=M(x_0). By invertible constant row and column operations, and multiplication of F by a nonzero scalar, we may assume
  M_0 = diag(I_s,0_a),  s+a=r,  a>=1.
These operations preserve Hessian rank, and only change the linear map L.

Write Z=L(x-x_0). The Hessian at x_0 is the Hessian of the quadratic homogeneous part of det(M_0+Z), pulled back along L. Pullback by a linear map cannot increase rank.

If a>=3, every determinant monomial needs at least a entries from Z to fill the a zero rows/columns. The Taylor expansion has no term of degree at most two, so the Hessian is zero.

If a=2, the quadratic term is det(Z_{bottom 2 x 2}). Its Hessian in the four bottom-block coordinates has rank 4, so after pullback the rank is at most 4 <= 2r.

If a=1, write the last row and column as the deficient ones. The quadratic term is
  Q(Z) = Z_rr * sum_{i<r} Z_ii - sum_{i<r} Z_ir Z_ri.
The diagonal variables contribute a rank-2 Hessian block; each of the r-1 pairs (Z_ir,Z_ri) contributes rank 2. Thus rank Hess(Q)=2+2(r-1)=2r. Pullback cannot increase it.

Therefore, for every r and every zero x_0,
  rank Hess(F)(x_0) <= 2r.                                      (1)
For r=1 the Hessian is zero, also satisfying (1). The bound is attained at a corank-one diagonal matrix when r>=2, so H_0(det_r)=2r.

The chain-rule form makes the scope explicit. If L is represented by an r^2 x n coefficient matrix, then
  Hess_x F(x_0) = L^T Hess_M(det_r)(M_0) L,
so the restriction to the m^2 permanent variables, including arbitrary redundant variables or directions, cannot evade (1).

PROOF 2: THE PERMANENT HAS A ZERO WITH FULL-RANK HESSIAN

For m>=3, take X_m with first row
  a=(1,1,...,1,-(m-1))
and every other row equal to (1,...,1). Since the permanent is linear in the first row,
  perm_m(X_m) = (m-1)! sum_j a_j = 0.                           (2)

Index Hessian coordinates by pairs (i,j). Multilinearity gives
  H_{ij,kℓ}=0                         if i=k or j=ℓ,
  H_{ij,kℓ}=perm of the submatrix deleting rows i,k and columns j,ℓ
                                      otherwise.

Set alpha=(m-2)! and beta=(m-3)!. For i=1<k and j!=ℓ the cofactor permanent is alpha. For i,k>1, i!=k and j!=ℓ it is
  beta * sum_{q not in {j,ℓ}} a_q = -beta(a_j+a_ℓ).              (3)

We show the kernel of H is zero. Let z be in the kernel; write x_j=z_{1j}, y_{ij}=z_{ij} for i>1, and define
  S=sum_{i>1,j} y_{ij},   c_j=sum_{i>1} y_{ij},
  s_i=sum_j y_{ij},       t_i=sum_j a_j y_{ij},
  T=sum_{i>1}t_i,          X=sum_j x_j.
The row-1 Hessian equations and (3) give
  0 = alpha(S-c_j).
Thus c_j=S for every j. Summing over j gives S=mS; since m>=2, S=0 and all c_j=0.

For i>1, the (i,j) Hessian equation, divided by beta, is
  0 = (m-2)(X-x_j) + a_j s_i - T + t_i - 2 a_j y_{ij}.          (4)
Summing (4) over i>1 and using S=c_j=0 gives
  (m-1)(m-2)(X-x_j) - (m-2)T = 0.
Since m>=3, this yields X-x_j=T/(m-1), independent of j. Hence all x_j are equal and
  x_j=T/(m-1)^2.                                                 (5)

For j<m, a_j=1. Substituting (5) in (4) gives
  2 y_{ij}=s_i+t_i-T/(m-1),
so y_{ij}=u_i is independent of j<m. Put v_i=y_{i,m}. The j=m equation in (4), using a_m=-(m-1), simplifies to
  0=-T/(m-1)-(m-1)(m-2)u_i.
Thus every u_i is the same constant. But c_j=0 for j<m implies sum_{i>1}u_i=0, hence u_i=0 and then T=0. The j<m equation now says s_i+t_i=0. With y_{ij}=0 for j<m, we have s_i=v_i and t_i=-(m-1)v_i, so (m-2)v_i=0 and v_i=0. Equation (5) gives x_j=0. Therefore z=0 and
  rank Hess(perm_m)(X_m)=m^2.                                  (6)

For m=2, perm_2=x_11 x_22+x_12 x_21; at X_2=[[1,-1],[1,1]] it vanishes and its 4 x 4 Hessian is a permutation matrix of rank 4. Thus (6) holds for all m>=2. Since the Hessian has only m^2 rows, H_0(perm_m)=m^2 exactly.

COMPARISON AND INVARIANT CEILING

If perm_m=det(M(x)) with an arbitrary affine r x r matrix M, evaluate at X_m. Equation (2) makes M(X_m) singular. Equations (1) and (6) imply
  m^2 <= 2r,  hence r >= ceil(m^2/2).                           (7)
This derivation covers all affine determinant representations, not only homogeneous linear pencils or representations whose constant matrix has corank one.

The invariant is fully saturated on the permanent: rank Hess is always at most its ambient dimension m^2, and (6) attains that maximum at a zero. Bound (1) converts this invariant to at most r >= m^2/2. Therefore this invariant, even with the best possible choice of permanent zero, cannot yield r=omega(m^3), or any super-quadratic determinant-size bound. This is an invariant-specific ceiling, not a general impossibility theorem for other geometric or algebraic methods.

EXPLICIT COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE UNRESTRICTED HESSIAN VARIANT

For r>=2, at the nonzero point I_r,
  D^2 det_r(I_r)[U,V] = tr(U)tr(V)-tr(UV).
This bilinear form is nondegenerate: if it vanishes for all V, nondegeneracy of the trace pairing gives U=tr(U)I_r; tracing then gives (r-1)tr(U)=0, so U=0. Hence Hess(det_r)(I_r) has rank r^2. The size-r determinant family itself therefore disproves any proposed O(r) Hessian-rank bound that is not restricted to zeros. The zero-point condition in (1) cannot be dropped.

STATUS AGAINST PRIOR WORK AND N79

Mignon and Ressayre's 2004 primary paper defines affine determinantal complexity and proves dc(perm_m)>=m^2/2 over characteristic zero using Hessians at zeroes. The proof above reconstructs that mechanism with an explicit rational witness and a direct kernel calculation. This leaf does not claim that result as novel. [Primary publisher record / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792804142566) · [author-hosted paper](https://math.univ-lyon1.fr/~ressayre/PDFs/permdet.pdf).

The local N79 card says it is based on a supplied summary only and that the full Family 108 note/code/package was absent. I did not verify the card's degree-times-variable/smoothness argument or its Fermat determinant-representation assertion, and make no claim about their correctness or priority. This leaf tests a separate permanent-specific Hessian invariant. The N79 source gap remains open.

COST AND EVIDENCE SCOPE

The witness has m^2 entries, each with O(log m) bits, so its dense encoding costs O(m^2 log m) bits (or O(m log m) bits when described by the explicit row template plus m). The Hessian has dimension m^2 x m^2; its m^4 entries at the witness are 0 or factorial-sized integers with O(m log m) bits each, for O(m^5 log m) bits if stored densely. The rank calculation in the proof is a uniform exact argument and does not require materializing this dense Hessian. The checker explicitly builds these Hessians and uses rational Gaussian elimination for m=2,...,8; it also checks determinant Hessian ranks at identity and at diagonal zeroes for r=2,...,8. It is a finite diagnostic, not a proof of the all-m identities or an external validation.

FALSIFIER AND RESTART

A single m>=3 exact calculation contradicting (3), the kernel equations (4), or full rank (6) falsifies the permanent part. A single affine determinant zero whose Hessian rank exceeds 2r falsifies (1). The finite checker can catch transcription errors in small orders but cannot falsify the uniform proof by itself.

Restart by: (1) independently checking the Taylor expansion at each determinant corank; (2) independently replaying the permanent Hessian kernel proof and checker; (3) keeping the Mignon–Ressayre attribution and the N79 missing-source status separate; (4) if continuing toward super-cubic lower bounds, replace Hessian rank with an invariant whose permanent value exceeds O(m^2) or whose determinant-representation comparison is nonlinear in r. Do not infer anything about unrelated invariants from this Hessian ceiling.
