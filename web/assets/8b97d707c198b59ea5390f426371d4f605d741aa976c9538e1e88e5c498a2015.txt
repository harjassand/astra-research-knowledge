  d=3: h=(3,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,3/2), sum_a 1/h_a = 9/2 <= d^2=9.

2. ORBIT BASIS AND MULTIPLICITIES

For each count vector m, I constructed S_m by summing all distinct ordered
words in the local basis. The exact number of words is s_m=N!/prod_a m_a!,
and direct Q(i) matrix traces give

  Tr(S_m^2)=s_m prod_a h_a^(m_a).

Every nonidentity S_m has trace zero. The number of count vectors is
D=binom(N+d^2-1,d^2-1), which matched the exact enumeration in all cases:

  (d,N)=(2,2): D=10;   (2,3): D=20;
  (d,N)=(3,2): D=45;   (3,3): D=165.

Because local words form an orthogonal tensor basis and site permutations
permute precisely the words with a fixed count vector, these orbit sums are
an orthogonal basis of the permutation-invariant operator space. The exact
coefficient recovery c_m=Tr(S_m E)/Tr(S_m^2) recovers every planted
coefficient and returns zero on every other orbit in each example.

4. CORRECTION STATE AND FULL MATRIX IDENTITY

For an orbit m with r active (nonidentity) sites and epsilon=sign(c_m), I
enumerated each distinct word v in the orbit uniformly and each sign vector
on its r active sites uniformly subject to product of signs epsilon. The
number of allowed sign vectors is 2^(r-1), including exactly one sign vector
when r=1. Each local factor (I+s_i T_(v_i))/d is PSD by the exact local check;
inactive sites use I/d. Averaging the product states gives, exactly,

  Omega_m = I/d^N + epsilon S_m/(s_m d^N).

I checked this equality as a full d^N by d^N Q(i) matrix for every
nonidentity orbit in all four cases. Since a_m=d^N c_m and A_m=s_m |a_m|,

  A_m Omega_m = (A_m/d^N) I + c_m S_m.

Let A=sum_m A_m. The exact matrix identity checked in every case is

  S - w I/d^N + (w-A) I/d^N + sum_m A_m Omega_m = S+E=R.

The top-level weights are nonnegative and sum exactly to one:

  N=2: 1-w=3/4, w-A=3/16, correction weights sum to 1/16.
  N=3: 1-w=7/8, w-A=3/32, correction weights sum to 1/32.

Within a correction set the weights vary with orbit index u according to the
rational formulas in Section 3; no uniform-weight assumption is used.

All four targets are permutation invariant, trace one, and have Gaussian-
rational entries. The first branch is PSD because each sigma_j-zeta I is
exactly positive definite and
sigma_j^tensorN-zeta^N I is the sum over nonempty subsets of tensor products
of sigma_j-zeta I and I with nonnegative powers of zeta. The middle branch is
PSD because w-A>=0. Each correction branch is an explicitly enumerated convex
mixture of PSD product states. Thus R is PSD by an exact sum-of-positive-terms
certificate; no floating-point PSD decision is involved. The source identity
is sound for these cases.

5. CONDITIONAL WHITE-BRANCH LAW

For both iid atoms take

  sigma_+- = I/d +/- T_X01/(4d),  with atom probabilities 1/2,
  zeta=1/(2d), p=d zeta=1/2,
  tau_+- = (sigma_+- - zeta I)/(1-p)
          = I/d +/- T_X01/(2d).

Exact principal-minor checks show sigma_+- and sigma_+-zeta I are positive
definite, so S= (sigma_+^tensorN+sigma_-^tensorN)/2 is rational and full rank
with the required floor. The tau states are also exact PSD trace-one states.
For each atom, sigma=p I/d+(1-p)tau holds exactly.

For a white-site mask W, excluding the all-white mask, the conditional
probability is

  P(W | W != all-white) = p^|W| (1-p)^(N-|W|)/(1-p^N).

Here p=1/2, so each of the 2^N-1 allowed masks is equiprobable: 1/3 for
N=2 and 1/7 for N=3. Exact enumeration of every mask and exact tensor
arithmetic verify, for each atom j,

  E[ product_i (I/d if i is white, tau_j otherwise) | not all white ]
    = (sigma_j^tensorN - w I/d^N)/(1-w).

Averaging the two atoms gives (S-wI/d^N)/(1-w), the normalized first
branch. The unconditional branch probability is 1-w, and its joint law first
chooses the atom with probability 1/2 then uses this conditional mask law.

The sequential probabilities are also exact. After an all-white prefix with
r sites left, the probability of white at the next site is

  p(1-p^(r-1))/(1-p^r),

and the probability of nonwhite is (1-p)/(1-p^r). At r=1 these are 0 and 1:
the forced nonwhite outcome preserves the valid support. Once a nonwhite site
has appeared, the remaining transitions are the original (p,1-p). For p=1/2,
the all-white-prefix transitions have white probabilities 1/3 at r=2, 3/7 at
r=3, and 0 at r=1; after a nonwhite they are (1/2,1/2). Exact path
probabilities from these transitions match the direct conditional mask law
for every allowed mask in all four cases. A finite-bit implementation must
keep zero-probability/impossible outcomes out of the rounded support, e.g.
by assigning rounding residual only to a valid category; the source's
abstract rational sampler has no such support problem.
