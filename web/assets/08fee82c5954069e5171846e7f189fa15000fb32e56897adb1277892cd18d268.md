## Result in one paragraph

The literature contains three adjacent, but distinct, interfaces. (1) Linear matroid parity algorithms optimize support and return a maximum feasible parity set; they do not sum its squared-Gram weights. Pfaffian parity counting adds a constant-determinant condition. (2) The new Chen–Liu common-base FPRAS is a powerful coefficient/convolution theorem, but its log-concavity hypothesis fails for the direct local “both or neither” pair factor (1+xy); this rejects that direct encoding only. (3) Fermionic linear optics with mode-resolved measurements is classically simulable, but charge-resolved/coarse-grained two-mode measurements are a non-Gaussian resource and are known to support nondeterministic universality when interleaved. At half filling, the target coefficient (c_{n/2}(F)) is exactly the norm after single-occupancy projection at every site, making that literature physically close. The missing conversion is still from an interleaved charge-measurement circuit to one static rational opposite-spin BCS matrix with only final onsite projection. No cited source supplies that conversion, a general FPRAS, or an AP-hardness theorem for the target.

## Exact target and source interfaces

For an explicit (F\in\mathbb{Q}(i)^{n\times n}), the hard-projection charge coefficient is

\[
c_k(F)=\sum_{\substack{|I|=|J|=k\\I\cap J=\varnothing}}|\det F[I,J]|^2.
\]

The input supplies the rational-complex pair matrix and the requested sector. There is no supplied sampler, coefficient oracle, rare-event lower bound, or favorable conditioning assumption. A general-purpose result must preserve this weighted sum, including exact zeros and multiplicative approximation; support optimization or one sample from a different physical measurement model is not enough.

At (k=n/2), every disjoint pair of (n/2)-sets covers all (n) sites. Therefore

\[
c_{n/2}(F)=\sum_{|I|=n/2}|\det F[I,\bar I]|^2,
\]

which is the unnormalized probability weight of the balanced BCS sector after requiring one fermion at each site. Given total particle number (n), “no double occupancy at every site” and “single occupancy at every site” coincide. The local charge-one projector is

\[
P_1=n_{i\uparrow}(1-n_{i\downarrow})+(1-n_{i\uparrow})n_{i\downarrow},
\]

a sum of two spin-resolved occupation projectors. This is the exact physical overlap with the charge-measurement literature; it does not imply that this norm is hard to approximate.

## Literature audit

### Matroid parity and counting

Hamada–Iwata, *A Shortest Augmenting Path Algorithm for Linear Matroid Parity*, arXiv:2610.03030v1, Theorem 1.1, gives a deterministic algorithm for the **unweighted maximum-cardinality** linear matroid parity problem in (O(Nr^2\log r)) time in the uniform field-cost model (or (O(Nr^2)) with fast multiplication), returning a feasible witness. The theorem is optimization over support, not a partition-function algorithm. For rational inputs, bit-cost/finite-field conversion is an additional step; the field-operation statement alone is not a bit-complexity bound. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03030v1

Matoya–Oki, *Pfaffian Pairs and Parities*, arXiv:1912.00620v2, Definition 2.8 and Proposition 2.9, defines a Pfaffian parity by requiring \(\det A[B]=c\ne0\) for **every** parity base (B). Then the number of parity bases is a Pfaffian divided by (c); the paper also treats minimum-weight parity bases inside this class. This is not a formula for arbitrary \(\sum_B\det(A[B]^*A[B])\), whose terms vary with (B). Source: https://arxiv.org/html/1912.00620v2

Chen–Liu, *An FPRAS for Counting Common Bases of Two Matroids*, arXiv:2610.06724v1, Theorem 1.6, takes two nonnegative set-weight oracles \(\mu,\nu\), log-concave multi-affine generating polynomials \(g_\mu,g_\nu\), positive starting sets, coefficient-range bounds, and an error parameter. It approximates a specified coefficient of \(g_\mu g_\nu\) in \(\widetilde O(n^{11}(n+\ln(R_\mu R_\nu))^2/\epsilon^2)\) arithmetic/oracle calls. The paper separately states a polynomial-bit arithmetic route for rational oracle values in Appendix A.5. The direct pair-choice polynomial \(1+xy\) is not log-concave: the determinant of the Hessian of \(\log(1+xy)\) is \(((xy)^2-1)/(1+xy)^4<0\) whenever \(xy<1\). Hence the natural local support \((1,0,1)\) (“neither or both”) fails the direct hypothesis. This calculation does **not** rule out a different encoding/lift or an independently proved curvature condition. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06724v1

### Gutzwiller/RVB norm, exact MPS, and approximation boundaries

Liu–Wu–Tu–Xiang, *Efficient conversion from fermionic Gaussian states to matrix product states*, arXiv:2408.01155v2, states that local Fock tensors for a Gaussian MPS can in principle be converted exactly, but that in their Gutzwiller-projected workflow the projection destroys Gaussianity and they truncate the resulting tensors. This is important prior art for Gaussian-to-MPS conversion and for local projection; it is not the exact rational-bit theorem below and is not a worst-case complexity result for the norm. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01155v2

The same paper's Section II uses the Gaussian correlation matrix/Schmidt decomposition for its MPS construction. Fishman–White, arXiv:1504.07701, gives a numerically stable correlation-matrix compression method for pure fermionic Gaussian states and notes extensions to BCS states. These sources support the general tensor-network method, not an exact polynomial-bit cost under an input-bit model. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01155v2 and https://arxiv.org/html/1504.07701

Kang–Kwon–Scarola–Park, *Scalable Quantum Algorithms for Gutzwiller Projection*, arXiv:2606.06919v1, gives amplitude-amplified quantum state preparation with query cost depending on the projected weight (W) (measurement postselection scales as (O(1/W)), amplitude amplification as (O(1/\sqrt W)), up to accuracy factors). It is a state-preparation protocol with the rare-projection weight still charged, not a classical FPRAS or a hardness result for (c_k(F)). Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06919v1

### Fermionic linear optics and charge measurements

Knill, *Fermionic Linear Optics and Matchgates*, arXiv:quant-ph/0108033v2, establishes efficient classical simulation for fermionic linear optics with particle measurements in the mode-resolved model. DiVincenzo–Terhal, *Fermionic Linear Optics Revisited*, arXiv:quant-ph/0403031v1, explains the interface: a one-mode occupancy projector maps a Slater determinant to a Slater determinant, but the two-mode charge-one projector \(P_1=P_{10}+P_{01}\) is a sum of two terms and generically raises Slater number to two. They also state that fermionic linear optics plus nondestructive charge detection permits nondeterministic quantum computation; their Corollary 1 includes the grouped charge measurement \(P_{0,1}/P_2\), which is exactly the local “no double / double” test. The no-go theorem there concerns **exact zero-failure** implementations, not postselected/nondeterministic ones. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0108033v2 and https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0403031v1

Beenakker–DiVincenzo–Emary–Kindermann, *Charge Detection Enables Free-Electron Quantum Computation*, arXiv:quant-ph/0401066v1, Eq. (1) writes charge-one as a sum of the two spin-resolved terms. It observes that requiring (M) sites to be singly occupied produces a sum of (2^M) determinant terms, then constructs CNOT using beam splitters and charge detectors. This is an explicit physical and algebraic interface to projected BCS norms, but the paper does not prove that this sum is #P-hard or lacks an FPRAS. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0401066

At half filling, (c_{n/2}(F)) is precisely the squared norm of a BCS state after the product of local charge-one projectors. At general (k), the hard Gutzwiller event is the product of grouped \(P_{0,1}\) outcomes, with the number sector controlling the vacancy count. These measurement operations are non-Gaussian as aggregate outcomes. Thus Knill’s mode-resolved simulation does not evaluate the aggregate Gutzwiller success norm. Conversely, the circuit universality of charge measurements is interleaved/adaptive and does not itself compile to one static BCS pair matrix.

Chiu–Chen–Chuang, *Fermionic Measurement-based Quantum Computation*, arXiv:1207.5846v1, constructs universal fermionic MBQC from fPEPS resources with multiple modes per site and specified single-site measurement bases. It supports the general point that measurement and resource interfaces matter; it does not furnish a two-mode-per-site BCS-plus-final-Gutzwiller compiler. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/1207.5846

## Independent exact check details

The new checker uses a standalone `Fraction` Gaussian-elimination determinant/rank implementation and does not import c03_s03 code. For each of four explicit rational matrices, it forms the complete site-occupation amplitude matrix across every contiguous cut from the complementary-minor formula, including the fermionic reordering sign, and compares its exact rank with \(2^{\mathrm{rank}F[L,R]+\mathrm{rank}F[R,L]}\). It separately enumerates the dense staircase law for every (2\le n\le8) and (1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor). All checks pass. The staircase formula also has a short independent argument: if two same-color sites are consecutive in the sorted union of selected row and column sites, the corresponding rows/columns are identical; hence only the two alternating colorings survive. Their determinants are \(\alpha(\alpha-\beta)^{k-1}\) and \(\beta(\beta-\alpha)^{k-1}\). Summing their squared magnitudes over \(\binom n{2k}\) supports yields \(\binom n{2k}(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)|\alpha-\beta|^{2k-2}\).

The checker supports the peer's proposed costed subclass and demonstrates why it is not covered by graph-treewidth width bounds. It does not verify the universal exact tensor-construction bit-height theorem, does not give a new hardness theorem, and does not certify novelty.

## Exact next gate and status

The strongest plausible literature-led route is now sharply delimited: use the known interleaved FLO-plus-charge-measurement universality to motivate a hardness reduction, but construct an explicit polynomial-bit map from that circuit (or a postselected MBQC branch) to a single (F\in\mathbb Q(i)^{n\times n}) and a fixed (c_k(F)) or occupation prefix. It must preserve the desired branch probability with multiplicative precision and account for herald probability, number sector, and measurement basis. Known fermionic MBQC sources use multi-mode fPEPS resources and prescribed measurement bases, not the two-mode opposite-spin static interface. No reordering identity is valid in general because the local hard projector does not commute with Gaussian mode mixing (the exact swap witness is already frozen in INITIAL). A teleportation/resource construction might still succeed, but no such gadget was found or derived here.

Status: **UNRESOLVED** for general FPRAS/#P-hardness; **VERIFIED_INTERNAL** for the finite cut-rank checks and exact literature-interface distinctions above; **CONDITIONAL/OUTSIDE-INTERFACE** for interleaved FLO universality; novelty remains **UNKNOWN**.
