CYCLE10 FREQUENCY-EPOCH TEST
Exact stress-tuple obstruction audit

Status
No exact counterexample with locally infinite H^{m-1} was found. The exact scalar and vector harmonic realizations force the good-center set into a harmonic zero set, which has locally finite H^{m-1}. This does not establish that the full abstract measure-valued stress system has that representation or local finiteness. The established abstract conclusion remains sigma-finite rectifiability.

Exact abstract input
On a connected open Omega in R^m, the allowed tuple is
  nu >= 0, f vector-valued, beta symmetric matrix-valued, mu signed,
  d = tr(beta),
  [nu f^T; f beta] >= 0,
  grad(nu)=2f, div(f)=d, div(beta)=grad(mu),
  2mu <= d, and |mu| <= C d locally.
The measures are locally finite Radon measures; the order inequalities are measure inequalities. The exact good-center input on B4 has Z⊂B1 Borel, H(x,r)>0 and 1/2<=P(x,r)<=N for every x∈Z and 0<r<2, with
  H(x,r)=integral psi_{x,r} d nu,
  A(x,r)=r^2 integral phi_{x,r} d mu,
  P(x,r)=A(x,r)/H(x,r).
No proof body from the candidate source is used here.

Exact scalar harmonic realization and frequency calculation
For any nonzero harmonic polynomial u on R^2, set
  nu=u^2 dx, f=u grad(u) dx,
  beta=grad(u) tensor grad(u) dx,
  mu=|grad(u)|^2/2 dx, d=|grad(u)|^2 dx.
Then the block measure is the rank-one PSD measure [u;grad(u)][u;grad(u)]^T dx,
  grad(nu)=2f,
  div(f)=|grad(u)|^2+u Delta u=d,
  div(beta)=grad(|grad(u)|^2/2)=grad(mu),
and 2mu=d, |mu|=d/2. Thus every listed order and distribution identity holds exactly.

For a homogeneous harmonic polynomial h_j(y-x) of degree j, radial integration by parts gives
  integral |grad h_j|^2 (1-|y-x|^2/r^2)^p dy
    = (2 p j/r^2) integral h_j^2 (1-|y-x|^2/r^2)^(p-1) dy.
With the prescribed kernels this means A_j(x,r)=(j/2)H_j(x,r). A harmonic polynomial expanded at x is a sum of homogeneous harmonic terms of degrees 0 through L. Distinct degrees are orthogonal under radial integration, so P(x,r) is a positive weighted average of j/2 over its nonzero Taylor terms. Consequently:
- if u(x)=0, its degree-zero term vanishes and 1/2<=P(x,r)<=L/2 for every r with room;
- if u(x)!=0, the degree-zero term dominates as r tends to zero, so P(x,r)->0.
Thus, for the exact kernel quotient, the good-center set of this model is precisely the zero set restricted to the stated interior/radius range.

The explicit family u_L(z)=Re(z^L) has L nodal diameters in B1, hence H^1(Z∩B1)=2L, while P<=L/2 on all of Z for all permitted radii. Taking N=L/2 gives H^1=4N. This is an exact stress tuple, not an arbitrary monotone profile. It falsifies any proposed local H bound uniform in N and shows that the frequency cap must enter quantitatively. It does not falsify local finiteness for each fixed N.

Vector-valued harmonic maps and positive mixtures
For a finite vector U=(u_1,...,u_q) of harmonic functions, summing the scalar tuples gives
  nu=|U|^2 dx, f=sum_a u_a grad(u_a) dx,
  beta=sum_a grad(u_a) tensor grad(u_a) dx,
  mu=(1/2)sum_a |grad(u_a)|^2 dx.
All exact identities and PSD hold termwise and after summation. Under local smoothness, P tends to zero at any point where U is nonzero. Hence the good centers lie in U^{-1}(0), which is contained in the zero set of any one nontrivial scalar component. Harmonic nodal sets have locally finite H^{m-1}; this model cannot produce infinite local measure.

The same obstruction applies to locally normal positive mixtures of scalar harmonic tuples. On each compact set, assume the mixture and its first derivatives converge enough that nu=a dx and d have continuous locally bounded densities. At a good center, a(x)>0 would force P(x,r)->0, so a(x)=0. If the already established Z is sigma-finite for H^{m-1}, Tonelli applied on a countable finite-measure decomposition of Z∩K shows that for almost every component parameter, its harmonic function vanishes H^{m-1}-a.e. on all of Z∩K. Choosing one nontrivial such component bounds H^{m-1}(Z∩K) by its local nodal measure. This rules out a positive-mixture counterexample under those normality assumptions. It is not a representation theorem for arbitrary abstract stress tuples.

Accumulating interfaces
The usual attempt to put infinitely many nodal hypersurfaces into a compact interior region fails for a single harmonic component: a nonzero harmonic function is real analytic, and its nodal set has locally finite H^{m-1}. A vector map has a common zero set, not the union of the components' nodal sets. Taking components of increasing degree and positive weights therefore does not turn their separate zero sets into an accumulating interface family. In the exact family above, making the number of interfaces diverge also makes the allowed N diverge.

Exact missing quantitative lemma
The remaining decision gate is the following abstract local-finiteness statement, for the exact measure PDE rather than a harmonic representation:

For each compact K⊂⊂B1 there is a finite C=C(m,p,N,C_order,dist(K,∂B4)) such that every nonzero tuple satisfying the displayed PSD, distribution, and order conditions, and every Borel good-center set Z with H>0 and 1/2<=P<=N on 0<r<2, satisfy
  H^{m-1}(Z∩K) <= C.

This lemma would rule out locally infinite H^{m-1} and upgrade sigma-finite rectifiability to local finiteness. It is stronger than the currently stated tail-set bounds, which control scale-R frequency-tail pieces with constants at scale R but do not sum to a uniform fixed-region bound as R↓0. It is also weaker than a full uniform Minkowski estimate; finite H^{m-1} alone does not supply a tubular-volume bound at every scale.

Falsifier for an overstrong version: u_L=Re(z^L) above rules out a C independent of N; any viable local-finiteness lemma must at least allow C(N) to grow linearly in the planar homogeneous examples. The example does not decide whether C(N)<∞ for each fixed N in the full abstract stress class.

Primary prior and boundary
Logunov, “Nodal sets of Laplace eigenfunctions: polynomial upper estimates of the Hausdorff measure,” Annals of Mathematics 187 (2018), Theorem 6.1, proves a local estimate for harmonic functions:
  H^{m-1}({u=0}∩Q) <= C diam(Q)^{m-1} N_u(Q)^alpha
with a doubling-index control (in the paper's local setting). This establishes the required local finiteness in the scalar harmonic model, not for the abstract measure tuple. Source: https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v187-n1-p04-p.pdf

The abstract tuple permits 2mu<d and potentially singular measures. No proof that it decomposes into harmonic components, nor an exact bad tuple, is supplied here. The target local-finiteness lemma is therefore unresolved. No global H^{m-1} or Minkowski estimate is claimed.

Provenance
This is an independent exact-model check and missing-lemma statement. It is conditional on the exact interface above and does not certify the candidate proof or claim an external breakthrough.
