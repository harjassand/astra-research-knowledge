The exact N77 reconstruction supports this statement: a single-linkage,
deficiency-zero mass-action network has a positive deterministic class
\(B+C=2\) whose trajectories converge to \((1,1,1,1)\), while the integer
lattice in its nonnegative closure decomposes into infinitely many closed
positive-recurrent classes \(\Gamma_n\) with unique laws \(\pi_n\) satisfying
\(\pi_n(A\ge n)=1\) and \(\pi_n(B=0)\to1\). Therefore the family
\(\{\pi_n:n\ge0\}\) is not tight. The precise false implication is

> one globally attracting deterministic equilibrium on a positive
> stoichiometric class implies uniform tightness of stationary laws over all
> stochastic communicating components in that class.

The counterexample does **not** show failure of componentwise recurrence,
explosion, or existence of stationary distributions. Every fixed \(\pi_n\)
is tight and positive recurrent. The full lattice slice also has a tight
stationary mixture; its mass on \(\Gamma_n\) is
\(e^{-1}(1/2+1/(n+1)+1/(2P_n))/(2n!)\), which has a factorial tail. The
uniformity over components is exactly what fails.

The deeper attack classifies the whole slice: all nonnegative states with
\(B+C=2\) are either in some \(\Gamma_n\), or are one of the absorbing
singletons \((0,1,1,d)\) or \((a,2,0,d)\) for \(a=0,1,2\). It also proves
\(\pi_n(BC=0)\to1\) and
\(\pi_n(A,B,C,D>0)=(1-e^{-1})2(n+2)(n+3)/Z_n\to0\), even though every
\(\Gamma_n\) contains strictly positive states.
