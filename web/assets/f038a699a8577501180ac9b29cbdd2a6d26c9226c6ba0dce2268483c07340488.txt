Leaf 14 — linear response of a kinetic Fokker–Planck mode
Date: 2026-10-08 (Australia/Brisbane)
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the explicit model below; the underlying Kramers/Kubo frameworks are established prior art. No novelty claim.

Question and scope
------------------
Does the zero-frequency response stay uniformly bounded when a kinetic relaxation gap closes, even though each fixed-gap system relaxes and its response is finite at every finite time? The answer is no, already for the homogeneous velocity mode of the Kramers/Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation. The one-mode formula is exact and saturates the natural inverse-gap bound.

Model and assumptions
--------------------
Fix particle mass m>0, bath temperature T_b>0, and friction γ>0. Let β=(k_B T_b)^(-1) and σ²=(βm)^(-1). For a normalized velocity density f(v,t), take the homogeneous kinetic Fokker–Planck equation with a spatially uniform applied force F(t):

  ∂_t f(v,t) + (F(t)/m) ∂_v f(v,t)
       = γ ∂_v [v f(v,t) + σ² ∂_v f(v,t)],   v∈R.

Assume decay at |v|→∞ sufficient for integration by parts, initial equilibrium f(v,0)=M(v), and a field small enough to define the derivative at F=0. Here M(v)=(2πσ²)^(-1/2) exp(-v²/(2σ²)). The equation is the velocity-homogeneous Kramers equation; position is absent because only the spatially uniform current response is under study. Force, rather than acceleration, is the input.

Linear response and resolvent
-----------------------------
Write f=M+εh+o(ε), F=εF_1, and let L be the zero-field forward Fokker–Planck operator. Since M'(v)=-(v/σ²)M(v), the first-order equation is

  ∂_t h = Lh + β F_1(t) v M.

The first Hermite mode is an exact eigenmode:

  L(vM)=γ ∂_v [v²M+σ²∂_v(vM)] = -γvM.

Thus h(v,t)=a(t)vM(v), a(0)=0, and

  a'(t)=-γa(t)+βF_1(t),
  a(t)=β∫_0^t e^{-γ(t-s)}F_1(s) ds.

For current j(t)=∫v f(v,t)dv, the derivative at ε=0 is

  δj(t)=σ²a(t)=(1/m)∫_0^t e^{-γ(t-s)}F_1(s) ds.

Consequently the causal response kernel, Laplace transform, and zero-frequency value are

  χ_γ(t)=1_{t≥0} e^{-γt}/m,
  χ̃_γ(z)=1/[m(z+γ)]  (Re z>0),
  χ̃_γ(0)=∫_0^∞χ_γ(t)dt=1/(mγ).

For a constant field switched on at t=0, δj(t)/F_1=(1-e^{-γt})/(mγ), and the stationary current derivative is 1/(mγ). In fact the OU equation's mean obeys j'=-γj+F/m exactly, so the same mean formula holds for any constant F, while χ̃_γ(0) remains its derivative at F=0.

Fluctuation–dissipation identity and proof
------------------------------------------
The stationary velocity process is

  dV_t=-γV_t dt + sqrt(2γσ²) dW_t,

with E[V_0²]=σ². Solving this scalar SDE gives V_t=e^{-γt}V_0 plus an independent centered Gaussian term. Therefore, for t≥0,

  C_γ(t):=E_eq[V_t V_0]=σ²e^{-γt}.

Comparing with the response kernel above proves the classical force-to-velocity FDT, with all normalization factors explicit:

  χ_γ(t)=β C_γ(t),   t≥0.

There is also a direct kinetic proof. Set g=h/M and use the backward equilibrium generator

  𝓛φ=γ(σ²φ''-vφ'),

which is self-adjoint and nonpositive in L²(Mdv). The linearized equation becomes ∂_t g=𝓛g+βF_1(t)v. Hence

  δj(t)=β∫_0^t F_1(s) ⟨v,e^{(t-s)𝓛}v⟩_M ds.

Stationarity identifies ⟨v,e^{τ𝓛}v⟩_M=C_γ(τ), proving the FDT for this force/current pair without importing a general theorem. The resolvent form is

  χ̃_γ(z)=β⟨v,(z-𝓛)^(-1)v⟩_M.

Since 𝓛v=-γv, this gives βσ²/(z+γ)=1/[m(z+γ)], exactly as the direct calculation does. Equivalently, in the one-dimensional spectral subspace spanned by v, the static response is β⟨v,(-𝓛)^(-1)v⟩_M=βσ²/γ.

Gap-closing conclusion and sharpness
------------------------------------
The backward OU generator has Hermite eigenvalues 0,-γ,-2γ,… in L²(Mdv), so its mean-zero spectral gap is exactly γ. The field and the measured current both couple to the first Hermite mode v. Thus the static response is the variance in that mode divided by its decay rate, times β; no other modes or cancellations are involved. This is a sharp single-mode example.

For every fixed γ>0, χ_γ(t) decays exponentially and the stationary susceptibility is finite. However, as γ↓0,

  χ̃_γ(0)=1/(mγ)→∞.

There is no gap-independent zero-frequency bound from a finite-time bound or from pointwise decay at each positive gap. For any fixed horizon T_f and constant field F_0,

  |δj(T_f)| ≤ |F_0|T_f/m,

uniformly in γ, while the infinite-time derivative grows like 1/γ. The decay is not uniform in γ: for each fixed t, χ_γ(t)→1/m as γ↓0. At γ=0 the Maxwellian is no longer a unique attracting equilibrium and a nonzero constant force accelerates the mean indefinitely, so the singular static limit has a physical as well as a resolvent interpretation.

More generally, for a reversible equilibrium generator with spectral expansion 𝓛ψ_k=-λ_kψ_k and a matched source/observable A, the same correlation-resolvent structure gives χ̃(0)=β∑_k |⟨A,ψ_k⟩|²/λ_k (on the mean-zero subspace, when the sum is finite). A uniform bound as the gap shrinks requires the slow-mode overlaps to vanish fast enough; a lower gap bound is one sufficient condition, not a consequence of FDT. In this OU example the overlap stays fixed and the one slow mode forces divergence.

Equal-input baseline
--------------------
Both calculations use the same m, T_b, γ, initial Maxwellian, force convention, and measured current.

  Method                    Impulse response              Step response at T_f       Zero-frequency value
  Direct linearized PDE      e^(-γt)/m                    (1-e^(-γT_f))/(mγ)         1/(mγ)
  Equilibrium FDT              βC_γ(t)                      β∫_0^T_f C_γ(s)ds          β∫_0^∞ C_γ(s)ds

The entries agree algebraically for every m,T_b,γ>0 and every finite T_f. For the dimensionless illustration m=k_BT_b=F_0=T_f=1, both methods give step currents 0.6321206, 0.9516258, and 0.9950166 at γ=1, 0.1, and 0.01, respectively; their corresponding infinite-time susceptibilities are 1, 10, and 100. The values at finite time stay bounded as the dc value diverges.

The matched comparison is deliberately exact: no method receives a measured correlation, fitted relaxation time, or extra information unavailable to the other. It checks the identity within the stated model; it is not independent experimental validation or a general theorem about non-reversible/non-equilibrium systems.

Acquisition and precision costs
-------------------------------
For the analytic claim, m,T_b,γ and the kinetic equation are supplied parameters; no data acquisition is needed. The mode reduction and formula evaluation take O(1) arithmetic operations. For χ̃(0)=1/(mγ), relative input errors satisfy, to first order, |Δχ|/|χ|≤|Δm|/m+|Δγ|/γ. To guarantee absolute output error at most ε from a γ error alone, it is sufficient locally to require |Δγ|≤ε mγ²; the absolute-precision demand therefore tightens as the gap closes. If the response is instead inferred from a finite trajectory, observing a relative decay down to ε requires a time horizon at least γ^(-1)log(1/ε); sample-count and stochastic-estimation costs are not claimed here.

Falsifier and limitations
-------------------------
An algebraic falsifier is any disagreement, for some m,T_b,γ>0, between (i) the first-order PDE solution and (ii) β times the equilibrium velocity autocorrelation under the same conventions. The derivation predicts exact equality at all t≥0. A second falsifier of the gap-closing conclusion would be a uniform finite bound on 1/(mγ) over all γ>0 with m fixed; the formula itself rules this out.

This establishes a scoped counterexample to uniform zero-frequency control from pointwise relaxation alone. It does not show that every kinetic model's susceptibility diverges when a gap closes: observables may be orthogonal to slow modes, the overlap may shrink with the gap, or the relevant response may be non-reversible or non-equilibrium. The result is standard linear response/OU behavior and is not a field-breaking advance.
