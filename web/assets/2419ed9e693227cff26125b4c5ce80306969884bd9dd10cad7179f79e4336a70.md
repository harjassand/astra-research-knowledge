# Primary-source and prior-art notes

## Reversible enzyme modules

Wolfram Liebermeister, Jannis Uhlendorf, and Edda Klipp, “Modular rate laws
for enzymatic reactions: thermodynamics, elasticities and implementation,”
*Bioinformatics* 26(12), 1528–1534 (2010),
[doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btq141](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btq141).

The source presents reversible enzyme rate laws for arbitrary stoichiometry
and names Michaelis–Menten and uni–uni reversible Hill laws as special cases
(abstract). Its general rate form has a numerator resembling a reversible
mass-action law and a positive denominator polynomial whose terms represent
enzyme-binding states (§1, equations 1–2). The common modular law (§2.1,
equation 3) includes `A+B <-> 2 C`; the text specifies its denominator as
`D=1+a'+b'+a'b'+c'+c'+c'^2` and says only bound states `AB00` and `00CC`
contribute to the forward/reverse numerator, while all seven binding states
contribute to the denominator. The compiler fixture aggregates duplicate
`c'` terms to coefficient 2. Unit rational constants in that fixture are
structural regression values, not a reported data fit. The source warns that
the thermodynamically safe parameterization matters for kinetic fitting; no
parameter acquisition is claimed here.

The present exact theorem is restricted to modules whose directional
numerators are a common positive factor times the two endpoint monomials and
whose pair shares one positive denominator. The compiler accepts positive
shared numerator factors, so a common rational activator can be split into
such a factor and a positive denominator factor. The primary source allows broader regulation and molecularity choices;
those are not automatically in this interface. In particular, its structure
numbers can differ from stoichiometric coefficients in some parameterizations.

## Shared-enzyme alternative substrates (negative boundary)

Santiago Schnell and Claudio Mendoza, “Enzyme kinetics of multiple
alternative substrates,” *Journal of Mathematical Chemistry* 27, 155–170
(2000), [doi:10.1023/A:1019139423811](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1019139423811).

The paper's scheme (1) is `S_i+E <-> ES_i -> E+P_i`. Its QSSA gives
`[ES_i]=[E_0][S'_i]/(1+sum_j[S'_j])` in equation (18), with
`S'_i=[S_i]/K_i`; equation (20) gives
`d[S'_i]/dt=-kappa_i[S'_i]/(1+sum_j[S'_j])`, where
`kappa_i=k_{2i}[E_0]/K_i` (equation 21). Equation (38) states the paper's
QSSA condition, including the enzyme/substrate-scale smallness requirement.
The resulting common-occupancy law is an example with one common positive
denominator and irreversible substrate-to-product channels. The exact
counterdirection and extinction inequality in this package apply to that
reduced rational model; they do not establish an approximation error bound
for the unreduced enzyme-complex ODE. The fixture's positive rational kinetic
parameters are regression placeholders, not fitted or paper-reported values.
At its supplied initial state, the fixture's Eq. 38 enzyme-scale ratios are
1/3, 1/6, and 2/9, with maximum 1/3. This does not demonstrate the required
much-less-than-one regime or establish QSSA validity.

## Reaction-network theorem and comparator

Manoj Gopalkrishnan, Ezra Miller, and Anne Shiu, “A geometric approach to the
Global Attractor Conjecture,” arXiv:1305.5303v2,
[primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5303v2). Definition 3.14 gives
standard strong endotacticity using a source globally maximal among **all**
reactants; neutral reactions are not deleted for this strong-source maximum.
Theorem 1.1 states strongly endotactic reaction networks are permanent.
Lemma 4.5 is cited in the paper for weakly reversible networks being
endotactic. Corollary 3.19 states a sufficient strong condition: each
linkage class has the same stoichiometric subspace as the full network. These
are published theorems used only at the stated transfer points. The
rank-one strong gate is also directly proved from paired edges in
`COMMON_FACTOR_PROOF.md`.

Johnston, Pantea, and Donnell, “A computational approach to persistence,
permanence, and endotacticity of biochemical reaction systems,”
[arXiv:1412.4662](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4662). The paper gives MILP
recognition for explicit finite reaction diagrams. This is a comparator after
the lifted graph is materialized; this package's algorithm reads only
factorized rational modules and never constructs that graph.
