## 1. Exact target and supplied inputs

The target is the infinite, collision-inclusive photon law of a zero-displacement pure Gaussian state with an **explicit supplied** real symmetric, entrywise nonnegative rational matrix \(B\), \(\|B\|_2<1\):

\[
p_B(n)=c_B\frac{\operatorname{haf}(B_n)^2}{n!},
\qquad c_B=\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)},\qquad n\in\mathbb N^m.
\]

Odd-sized hafnians vanish. When a mode is repeated, a diagonal entry \(B_{ii}\) gives edges between distinct copies; the ordinary hafnian does not pair a copy with itself. These entries are legal and do not create odd photons.

If \(B=DAD\) for diagonal unitary \(D\) and an entrywise nonnegative rational \(A\), then \(\operatorname{haf}(B_n)=(\prod_i D_{ii}^{n_i})\operatorname{haf}(A_n)\). Therefore their photon laws agree. This does not acquire the gauge transformation or a rational nonnegative representation for the user. The sampler's input is the supplied nonnegative matrix, or a separately acquired gauge representation of it.

The physical photon/hafnian connection is established in [Hamilton et al., *Gaussian Boson Sampling*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01199). The following reconstruction does not assume correctness of the OpenAI release's weighted-hafnian approximation theorem.

## 2. Independent reconstruction of (4)–(6)

For every real symmetric contraction \(C\), the squared norm of \(\exp(a^\dagger C a^\dagger/2)|0\rangle\) equals

\[
\sum_n\frac{\operatorname{haf}(C_n)^2}{n!}
=\det(I-C^2)^{-1/2}.
\]

An orthogonal mode change diagonalizes \(C\) and preserves the vacuum and norm. Each one-mode factor is \(\sum_{r\ge0}\binom{2r}{r}c^{2r}/4^r=(1-c^2)^{-1/2}\). This argument also covers negative real eigenvalues: it requires no entrywise sign condition.

For \(0\le z_i\le1\), take \(S=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{z_i})\). Since \(\operatorname{haf}((SBS)_n)=\prod_i z_i^{n_i/2}\operatorname{haf}(B_n)\),

\[
G(z)=\sum_n p_B(n)z^n
=\frac{\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)}}{\sqrt{\det(I-(D_zB)^2)}}.
\tag{A1}
\]

The replacement of \((SBS)^2=SBD_zBS\) by \((D_zB)^2\) follows from \(\det(I-UV)=\det(I-VU)\), including when some \(z_i=0\). The positive square-root branch follows from continuity and the contraction condition.

For entrywise nonnegative \(B\), \(\rho(D_zB)\le\rho(B)<1\). Expanding the log determinant gives

\[
\log G(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{tr}((D_zB)^{2r})-\operatorname{tr}(B^{2r})}{2r}.
\tag{A2}
\]

Thus the denominator is \(L=2r\), with no additional factor of two. Expanding each trace over labeled rooted sequences \(w=(v_0,\ldots,v_{L-1},v_0)\), and counting the \(L\) departure vertices, yields

\[
\log G(z)=\sum_{\substack{L\ge2\\L\text{ even}}}\sum_{w:|w|=L}
\frac{\prod_{s=0}^{L-1}B_{v_sv_{s+1}}}{L}
\bigl(z^{a(w)}-1\bigr).
\tag{A3}
\]

Every coefficient is nonnegative. The independent Poisson counts with these intensities have finite total intensity

\[
\Lambda=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(B^{2r})}{2r}
\le\frac12\sum_{r\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(B^{2r})=E/2<\infty.
\]

Their occupation-vector PGF is (A1). Equality of the analytic PGFs around zero identifies all pattern probabilities. Since every jump has even total occupancy, the representation preserves photon parity exactly. It includes collisions of all orders.

Rooted sequences are not rotation classes. A periodic loop has fewer distinct rooted sequences than a primitive loop; summing \(1/L\) over its distinct roots automatically supplies the conventional reciprocal multiplicity. Dividing once more by a rotation count or identifying orientations without adjusting the measure would break the result. The worker's convention is correct.

Two independent closed-form checks expose the dangerous constants. For \(B=[b]\), the result is \(G(z)=\sqrt{1-b^2}/\sqrt{1-b^2z^2}\), the even one-mode squeezed law. For \(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&b\\b&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), it is \(G(z)=(1-b^2)/(1-b^2z_1z_2)\), so \(N_1=N_2\) is geometric. Both agree with (A3).

## 3. Actual classical ancestry: a discrete loop soup

Put

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
h=(I-A)^{-1}\mathbf1,\qquad H=\operatorname{diag}(h),\qquad P=H^{-1}AH.
\]

The Neumann series shows \(h\ge\mathbf1\). From \(Ah=h-\mathbf1\), every row sum of \(P\) is \(1-1/h_v<1\). Thus \(P\) is a finite transient sub-Markov transition matrix. It has zero diagonal, even if \(B\) has positive diagonal. It is reversible with weights \(h_v^2\), and diagonal similarity leaves every closed-walk edge product unchanged.

In [Le Jan, *Markov Paths, Loops and Fields*](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~yves.le-jan/pdfs/stfl1.pdf), equations (2.4)–(2.5), PDF page 21, give discrete rooted weight \(\prod P_{v_sv_{s+1}}/L\) and total length-\(L\) mass \(\operatorname{tr}(P^L)/L\). Section 4.1, PDF page 43, defines the Poisson soup of intensity \(\alpha\mu\) and its exponential formula (4.1). The corresponding occupation variables used here are **numbers of discrete visits**, not the continuous holding-time occupation field.

Take \(\alpha=1/2\). Bipartiteness forces every closed walk in \(A\) to be even. Each rooted \(B\)-walk has exactly two color lifts, each with intensity \(\prod B_{v_sv_{s+1}}/(2L)\). Forgetting color adds these intensities and gives (A3). The photon count at mode \(i\) is the sum of visits to its two lifts. Equivalently, the discrete visit PGF of the standard soup is

\[
\left[\frac{\det(I-P)}{\det(I-D_tP)}\right]^{1/2};
\quad t=(z,z),\quad
\det(I-D_tA)=\det(I-(D_zB)^2).
\]

This proves an exact interface identification, rather than an analogy to loop soups or squared Gaussian fields. [Chang–Le Jan, *Markov loops in discrete spaces*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.1064), Proposition 3.4 and the pointed-loop definitions, independently clarify the periodic-loop convention. [Le Jan, *Markov loops, coverings and fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.02708) supplies another discrete-loop treatment; the useful point here is the same quotient by loop multiplicity.

The representation is therefore established classical loop-soup mathematics specialized to a Gaussian photon PGF. I did **not** establish whether a prior author already stated this particular full-GBS sampler or the worker's finite-bit bound. The broad representation is not a new loop-soup theorem. This identification also says nothing about a positive Glauber–Sudarshan quantum-state representation: only the measured photon law is represented.

## 4. Equation (7): the bridge is normalized and support-safe

For \(L\) with \(\operatorname{tr}(B^L)>0\), first select \(i\) with probability \((B^L)_{ii}/\operatorname{tr}(B^L)\). With current vertex \(v\) and \(r\) edges remaining, the next-vertex weights are

\[
q(j)=\frac{B_{vj}(B^{r-1})_{ji}}{(B^r)_{vi}}.
\]

Matrix multiplication proves \(\sum_jq(j)=1\). Any selected positive weight leaves \((B^{r-1})_{ji}>0\); hence the next denominator is positive. Multiplying the initial and conditional probabilities cancels every intermediate power and yields \(\prod B_{v_sv_{s+1}}/\operatorname{tr}(B^L)\). When \(r=1\), \(B^0\) forces the endpoint to be \(i\).

The code's finite-bit categorical boundaries are \(\lceil2^q F_j\rceil\), where \(F_j\) is a rational cumulative probability. If an entry has zero weight, consecutive boundaries coincide, so it receives no interval of random integers. Rounding may remove a tiny positive entry but cannot introduce an impossible edge. The bridge still closes exactly and every output remains in the true even-photon support.

## 5. Finite time and complete resource qualification

The mean is exactly computable over rationals:

\[
E=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(B^2(I-B^2)^{-1}\bigr)
=\operatorname{tr}((I-B^2)^{-1})-m.
\]

For \(E>0\), \(K=\lceil2E/\epsilon\rceil\), and the total intensity of lengths \(>2K\) is at most \(E/(2K)\le\epsilon/4\). Coupling by deleting those loops gives that TV bound. This truncates individual loop lengths, **not** the total photon number of the sample.

Replacing each Poisson count by \(\operatorname{Bin}(R_L,\lambda_L/R_L)\), with
\(R_L=\lceil\max(1,\lambda_L,8K\lambda_L^2/\epsilon)\rceil\), gives at most \(\epsilon/(4K)\) per length by the conservative Le Cam bound. There are at most \(K\) lengths. Since \(\lambda_L\le E/2\),

\[
T:=\sum_LR_L=O(K+KE+K^2E^2/\epsilon),\qquad
V:=\sum_LR_L(L+1)\le(2K+1)T.
\]

These bounds are deliberately loose, but finite. At most \(T\) Bernoulli and \(V\) categorical draws occur. A Bernoulli threshold changes by at most \(2^{-q}\); a table with at most \(m\) entries changes by at most \(m2^{-q}\). The code chooses \(q\) from the bound \(T+mV\), so the accumulated finite-bit error is at most \(\epsilon/4\), even with adaptive bridge tables. Combined TV is at most \(3\epsilon/4\).

With a common denominator for the input entries, entries of \(B^r\) have \(O(r(L_B+\log m))\) bits, where \(L_B\) bounds the full input bit length. The matrix-power arithmetic, rational inversion, loop generation, and output storage therefore have polynomial **bit** cost in \(L_B,m,E,\epsilon^{-1}\). The retained powers require \(O(Km^2)\) rational entries. The implementation also caches at most \(O(Km^2)\) conditional/start tables, each of length \(m\), hence \(O(Km^3)\) additional rational/integer entries plus their keys. Both retained resources and entry bit lengths must be charged. The worker now explicitly charges them.

A compact encoding of exponentially large \(E\) does not establish polynomial input-length time: this is a numerical-\(E\) bound. Similarly the tolerance is inverse-polynomial as a numerical quantity, not merely short in binary. The reference implementation has workload guards; it explicitly rejects an over-budget instance and is then not a sampler for that instance. Removing guards yields the abstract construction. The unseeded API uses `SystemRandom`; the command-line default is a seeded diagnostic whose status is labeled accordingly. Reproducible seeded fixtures are not a proof of the independent-random-bit interface.

## 6. Conditioning: the missing cost is accuracy as well as attempts

Let the target law be \(p\), the full-law sampler be \(q\), \(\operatorname{TV}(p,q)\le\delta\), and \(a=p(H)>0\). If \(\delta<a\), then \(b=q(H)>0\) and

\[
\operatorname{TV}(p(\cdot\mid H),q(\cdot\mid H))\le\delta/a.
\tag{A4}
\]

Indeed let \(S=\sum_H|p-q|\), \(d=|a-b|\). The complement contributes at least \(d\) to full \(\ell_1\) error, so \(S+d\le2\delta\); normalized conditional \(\ell_1\) error is at most \((S+d)/a\). Consequently, conditional tolerance \(\eta\) requires a full-law tolerance \(\delta\le a\eta\), in addition to rejection attempts of expected order \(1/a\). The sampler's dependence on \(\delta^{-1}\) can itself become exponential. Obtaining or certifying a usable lower bound on \(a\) is another interface requirement.

An explicit counterexample to cost-free generic conditioning uses \(s\) independent blocks \(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1/2\\1/2&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Each block has \(\Pr(N_1=N_2=k)=(3/4)(1/4)^k\), and the total mean is only \(E=2s/3\). All \(2s\) counts being one has probability \((3/16)^s\); the entire no-collision event has probability \((15/16)^s\). These are exponential rejection costs with polynomial mean. Their conditional laws can be elementary, so this example proves a limitation of generic rejection, **not** intrinsic computational hardness of conditioned sampling. Odd-total conditioning is a zero-probability event and is undefined.

An additive-TV full sampler also does not approximate exponentially rare pattern probabilities to relative error. No FPRAS for arbitrary perfect-matching counts follows from this construction.
