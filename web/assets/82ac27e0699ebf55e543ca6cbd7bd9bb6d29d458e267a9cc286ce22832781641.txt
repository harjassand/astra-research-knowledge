Cycle06 KMS counterexample and priority memo
Checked 2026-10-09. This memo is separate from cycle06_kms_regularities/; that earlier memo is preserved unchanged. The new LWW example and evaluation supersede the earlier memo's “no counterexample” status for the strong p=1 KMS endpoint.

Decision

The strong p=1 endpoint of Kastoryano–Temme's published Conjecture 19 is false for primitive KMS-symmetric quantum Markov semigroups. A primary-source qubit example of Liu–Wan–Wu (arXiv:2609.13726v1, Proposition 5.1) is primitive KMS-symmetric and not GNS-symmetric. Direct evaluation of the p=1 endpoint on one faithful state gives J < 4E. Liu–Wan–Wu introduce that semigroup to prove failure of complete modified log-Sobolev inequalities; they do not state or claim this regularity counterexample. The p=1 violation below is an independently reconstructed consequence of their explicit generator combined with the exact definitions in Kastoryano–Temme.

This disproves the strong part of Conjecture 19, including its KMS/reversibility case, but does not refute the weak p=1 inequality: for this state J/E is about 3.8078, which is greater than 2. It also does not settle the proposed classification of complete p=1 regularity by GNS symmetry. The literature checked gives GNS sufficiency and the equivalence “KMS symmetry plus modular covariance iff GNS symmetry”; it does not state that the complete p=1 inequality itself forces modular covariance.

Exact example and interface

Liu–Wan–Wu, Sec. 5, set

  V = [[0,1],[2,0]],   sigma = diag(1,4)/5,
  L(X) = (V*V X + X V*V)/2 - V* X V.

They write the Heisenberg QMS as P_t = exp(-t L) and prove in Proposition 5.1 that it is primitive, KMS-symmetric for sigma, and not GNS-symmetric. In the Schrödinger convention used in the current target, Phi_t is the trace adjoint of P_t: Phi_t is CPTP, Phi_t(sigma)=sigma, and Phi_t* = P_t is unital. The Heisenberg generator is G=-L. Thus the example meets the faithful stationary-state and KMS hypotheses without imposing bistochasticity or unitality on the Schrödinger channel.

Take the faithful test state

  eta = [[1/4, -2/5],[-2/5, 3/4]],
  eigenvalues eta = (10 +/- sqrt(89))/20.

For the positive Heisenberg generator L and its Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint L_*, define

  J(eta) = Tr[L_*(eta)(log eta - log sigma)],
  E(eta) = Tr[sqrt(eta) sigma^(-1/4)
                 L_*(sigma^(1/4) sqrt(eta) sigma^(1/4)) sigma^(-1/4)].

Equivalently, E(eta) is the KMS L2 Dirichlet form of
g=sigma^(-1/4)sqrt(eta)sigma^(-1/4):
E(eta)=Tr[sigma^(1/2) g sigma^(1/2) L(g)]. For a Schrödinger channel Phi, the same quantity is Tr[sqrt(eta) T_L(sqrt(eta))], where T_L(X)=sigma^(-1/4)L_*(sigma^(1/4)X sigma^(1/4))sigma^(-1/4). These expressions use the trace adjoint in the Schrödinger term and the unital Heisenberg generator in the KMS Dirichlet form.

The exact evaluations are

  E(eta) = (10 - sqrt(11))/(4(10 + sqrt(11)))
         = 0.12547051739576406...,
  J(eta) = (1/2) ln 2
           + (7/(20 sqrt(89))) ln((10+sqrt(89))/(10-sqrt(89)))
         = 0.4777645301574934...,
  J(eta)-4E(eta) = -0.0241175394255628... < 0.

The state is strictly positive, the reference is faithful, and L_*(sigma)=0. The negative gap is well separated from numerical roundoff; the coordinator separately reports an exact rational interval certificate giving J-4E < -3/125. That certificate is outside this literature memo and was not read here.

To match the historical definition, let Gamma_sigma(X)=sigma^(1/2)X sigma^(1/2), f=Gamma_sigma^(-1)(eta), and let I_{2,1}(f)=sigma^(-1/4)sqrt(eta)sigma^(-1/4). Kastoryano–Temme use the Heisenberg generator G=-L. KMS balance gives Gamma_sigma G=G_* Gamma_sigma. Their equations (25)–(26) therefore yield E_1(f)=J(eta)/2 and E_2(I_{2,1}f)=E(eta). Definition 9 says strong p=1 regularity is E_1(f)>=2E_2(I_{2,1}f), exactly J>=4E. Weak p=1 regularity is E_1(f)>=E_2(I_{2,1}f), exactly J>=2E. This witness violates only the strong endpoint among those two p=1 claims.

Historical and priority audit

Kastoryano–Temme arXiv:1207.3261v2 (12 Jun 2013), Definition 6, call Gamma_sigma detailed balance “reversible”; this is KMS symmetry. Definition 9 gives the weak and strong Lp comparisons, and Conjecture 19 predicts weak regularity for every primitive Liouvillian and strong regularity whenever it is reversible. The published J. Math. Phys. 54, 052202 (2013), DOI 10.1063/1.4804995, retains the same definitions and Conjecture 19: the checked journal PDF has Definition 6 on printed page 052202-7, Definition 9 on 052202-8, Conjecture 19 on 052202-15, and the open-question discussion on 052202-29. Its outlook explicitly calls settling Conjecture 19 unresolved. A bounded title/DOI search surfaced no separate correction or erratum; that is not proof that none exists.

The distinction between the 2013 KMS conjecture and later GNS theorems is decisive. Bardet, arXiv:1710.01039v1, Definition 4.2 and Theorem 4.1, assume self-adjointness in the GNS inner product Tr[sigma X*Y]. The paper explicitly says this sigma-DBC implies, and is stronger than, its KMS/reversibility condition. Under that stronger assumption it proves strong Lp regularity for every p>=1. Tensoring a GNS-symmetric generator with the identity remains GNS-symmetric for the product reference sigma tensor tau, so the theorem also gives the sufficient direction for every product-reference ancilla.

Vernooij–Wirth, arXiv:2303.15949v1, lines 161–171, state the finite-matrix symmetry relation precisely: GNS symmetry is equivalent to KMS symmetry together with commutation with the modular group. This identifies the structural condition in the proposed classification. It does not identify that condition with the complete p=1 inequality.

Gao–Wang, arXiv:2609.20753v2 (5 Oct 2026), Section 3.1, say strong Lp regularity is satisfied by GNS-symmetric semigroups and “not known to follow from KMS symmetry.” Their statement is an epistemic status report, not a counterexample. The LWW generator and the direct evaluation above provide a concrete failure of the KMS-only strong endpoint that the checked Gao–Wang passage does not discuss.

The open classification question, stated without overclaiming priority, is whether for every finite d, faithful sigma, and primitive KMS-symmetric QMS generator, strong p=1 regularity on the system tensored with one qubit for every faithful product reference sigma tensor tau is equivalent to commutation of the Heisenberg generator with the modular operator Delta_sigma (equivalently, GNS symmetry under KMS symmetry). GNS implies this complete endpoint by the cited strong-regularity theorem and tensoring. The converse would require a new argument. The coordinator's proposed Hessian and modular-frequency tuning route is a candidate proof mechanism, not a source theorem or verified result in this memo. The LWW example shows that the converse obstruction occurs for at least one non-GNS generator already without an ancilla, but it does not prove the iff classification.

The main missing lemma for that classification is: from the p=1 inequality for every faithful joint state on system tensor a qubit, with every faithful product reference sigma tensor tau, derive [G,Delta_sigma]=0. The proof must control the second-order expansion at the product reference and justify the ancilla parameter limit that isolates each modular frequency. A direct falsification test is a KMS-symmetric, non-GNS generator that nevertheless satisfies the amplified p=1 endpoint for every such qubit reference; the LWW example is not that test because its system-level endpoint already fails.

Scope: this is a narrow source audit plus a two-dimensional evaluation, not a novelty or priority claim for the counterexample mechanism or the complete-endpoint classification. The source papers do not themselves state the derived J<4E inequality. The independent formulas above make the consequence reproducible; the universal necessity direction remains UNKNOWN in the sources checked.
