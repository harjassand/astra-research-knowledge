THEOREM

For m>=2,N>=1, H=Sym^N(C^m), d=binom(N+m-1,m-1), and the Hermitian
irreducible sector W_k of End(H), let B_k,a be tau-orthonormal and
D_k(X)=sum_a B_k,a X B_k,a/r_k. Then for every0<=k,l<=N,

  D_k|W_l=eta_k(l)I,
  eta_k(l)=4F3[-k,k+m-1,-l,l+m-1;m-1,-N,N+m;1].         (1)

1. THE CANONICAL INVARIANT COUPLING BASES AND DIRECT MATRIX ACTION

The multiplicity-free decomposition H tensor bar H=direct-sum W_k has
self-dual real sectors W_k, realized concretely by their Hermitian matrix
subspaces. Conjugation acts by a real orthogonal matrix on every
Hermitian tau-orthonormal basis B_k,a. The invariant space of the four
alternating factors H tensor bar H tensor H tensor bar H has dimension
N+1. Its canonical normalized basis for the(12)(34) pairing is

  w_k(i,j,h,t)=1/[d sqrt(r_k)] sum_a (B_k,a)_ij(B_k,a)_ht.

This is invariant because the Hermitian sector basis transforms
orthogonally. Its norm is1 and distinct k are orthogonal: use
Tr(B_k,a B_l,b)=d delta_kl delta_ab. Each intermediate sector occurs
once, so this is the usual normalized invariant coupling vector for W_k
up to one unit phase; no choice of a basis inside W_k changes w_k.

For the(14)(32) pairing the canonical basis is

  w'_l(i,j,h,t)=1/[d sqrt(r_l)] sum_b (B_l,b)_it(B_l,b)_hj.

The overlap matrix U_kl=<w_k,w'_l> is therefore unitary. Hermiticity and
cyclic trace give the completely explicit formula

  U_kl=1/[d^2 sqrt(r_k r_l)]
           sum_ab Tr(B_k,a B_l,b B_k,a B_l,b).            (2)

Covariance and multiplicity one make D_k scalar on W_l. Pairing its
action against the r_l tau-orthonormal B_l,b yields

  sum_ab Tr(B_k,a B_l,b B_k,a B_l,b)=d r_k r_l eta_k(l).

Thus EXACTLY

  eta_k(l)=d U_kl/sqrt(r_k r_l).                         (3)

There is no dimension or transpose factor hidden in this equation. The
same identity follows from normalized Choi state Q_k/r_k, where
Q_k=sum_a|B_k,a>><<B_k,a|/d is the orthogonal sector projector, and its
reshuffled superoperator d reshuffle(Q_k)/r_k.

Because B_0=I, the two canonical zero rows/columns are positive:

  U_0k=U_k0=sqrt(r_k)/d.                                (4)

For example (2) with B_0=I gives sum_a Tr(B_k,a^2)=d r_k.

2. THE PRIMARY RACAH COEFFICIENT AND PHASE CALIBRATION

Mironov--Morozov--Sleptsov, arXiv1709.02290, Section2 kind I, gives the
exact symmetric/conjugate-symmetric recoupling coefficient. At q=1 its
literal factorial sum is

  R_raw(k,l)= k!^2 l!^2 (N-k)!(N-l)!(m-1)!(m-2)!
                 /[(N+k+m-1)!(N+l+m-1)!]
       *sum_(z=max(k,l)..min(N,k+l))
        (-1)^z (N+m-1+z)!
        /[(z-k)!^2(z-l)!^2(N-z)!(k+l-z)!(k+l+m-2-z)!].    (5)

The source's normalized kind-I matrix is

  S_kl=(-1)^(N+k+l)sqrt(r_k r_l)R_raw(k,l).              (6)

It is the overlap of the same two irreducible coupling bases in the
alternating invariant space, with its own Clebsch--Gordan vertex phases.
Multiplicity one makes each canonical basis differ from its source
basis by one unit phase. Therefore U=D S E for diagonal unit phase
matrices D,E. This is the standard exact recoupling identity being
imported; no interpretation of a numerical 6j array as a channel is made.

At k=0, only z=l contributes in(5), and cancellation gives

  R_raw(0,l)=(-1)^l/d,
  S_0l=S_l0=(-1)^N sqrt(r_l)/d.

Together with(4), the equations U_0l=D_0 S_0l E_l and
U_k0=D_k S_k0 E_0 force D_0 E_l=D_k E_0=(-1)^N for EVERY k,l.
All D_k are one common phase; all E_l are one common phase. Consequently

  U=(-1)^N S,
  eta_k(l)=(-1)^(k+l)d R_raw(k,l).                       (7)

This fixes the entire matrix analytically from the zero row and column,
not only isolated diagonal signs or small-spin examples. In SU2 the
source raw is(-1)^N times the conventional Wigner raw. The diagonal(7)
therefore reduces to the already banked(-1)^N d Wigner6j convention.

3. ONE EXPLICIT FINITE SEARS TRANSFORMATION TO THE CHANNEL SERIES

Use the established terminating balanced4F3 transformation, NIST DLMF
16.4.14, with

  n=k, a=-l, b=l+m-1, c=k+m-1,
  d0=m-1, e=-N, f=N+m.

The balance is a+b+c-k+1=d0+e+f=2m-1. It states that the right side of(1)
equals

  P *4F3[-k,-l,-l,-k;
             m-1,N-k-l+1,-N-m-k-l+1;1],                (8)
  P=(-N+l)_k(N+m+l)_k/[(-N)_k(N+m)_k].

For k+l<=N, write P in factorials:

  P=(N+m-1)!(N-k)!(N-l)!(N+k+l+m-1)!
       /[N!(N+k+m-1)!(N+l+m-1)!(N-k-l)!].

Expanding(8) and substituting z=k+l-r gives EXACTLY(-1)^(k+l)d times(5).
For clarity, the r-th expanded term after multiplying P is

  (N+m-1)!(N-k)!(N-l)! k!^2 l!^2 (-1)^r
          (N+k+l+m-1-r)!
   /[N!(N+k+m-1)!(N+l+m-1)!
       (k-r)!^2(l-r)!^2(N-k-l+r)!(m-2+r)!r!]
       *(m-2)!.

This matches the source after z=k+l-r, including its sign because
(-1)^(k+l)(-1)^z=(-1)^r. For k+l>N, explicitly deform BOTH lower
parameters: e=-nu and f=nu+m, with nu noninteger near N. Holding f fixed
would violate the balance and is not permitted. This simultaneous
deformation keeps d0+e+f=2m-1 for every nu, so the exact terminating
Sears identity applies before taking the limit. The original finite
series is regular at nu=N since N>=max(k,l).

Set P(nu)=(-nu+l)_k(nu+m+l)_k/[(-nu)_k(nu+m)_k]. The r-th transformed
term after the prefactor is

  T_r(nu)=P(nu)(-k)_r^2(-l)_r^2
    /[(m-1)_r(nu-k-l+1)_r(1-nu-m-k-l)_r r!].

The singular factor cancels analytically:

 lim_(nu->N) P(nu) Gamma(nu-k-l+1)
  =(N-l)!(N-k)!(N+m-1)!(N+m+k+l-1)!
      /[N!(N+m+l-1)!(N+m+k-1)!].

For example, the gamma reflection identity gives exactly

 Gamma(l-nu+k)Gamma(nu-k-l+1)/Gamma(l-nu)
    =(-1)^k Gamma(nu-l+1),
 lim_(nu->N) Gamma(-nu)/Gamma(-nu+k)
    =(-1)^k (N-k)!/N!,

    =(-1)^r Gamma(nu+m+k+l)/Gamma(nu+m+k+l-r).

Consequently the finite limit of T_r(nu) is the displayed factorial
coefficient, with reciprocal Gamma(N-k-l+r+1) in place of the potentially
negative factorial. It vanishes exactly for r<k+l-N and is finite for
all survivors. The surviving range max(0,k+l-N)<=r<=min(k,l) maps by
z=k+l-r to max(k,l)<=z<=N. Thus(8),(7) imply(1) for every allowed
