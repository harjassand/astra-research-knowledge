# Finite-observation impossibility: hidden autocatalytic order

## Statement and model

Work with one species $X$, unit-volume stochastic mass action, and integer count $n\in\mathbb N_0$. Compare

\[
\mathsf B:\quad \varnothing\xrightarrow{1}X,\qquad X\xrightarrow{4}\varnothing
\]
with

\[
\mathsf H_m:\quad \varnothing\xrightarrow{1}X,\qquad X\xrightarrow{4}\varnothing,
\qquad mX\xrightarrow{1}(m+1)X,
\quad m\ge3.
\]

All rate constants lie in the same supplied box $[1,4]$. The added source order $m$ is not bounded in advance. This is an unrestricted mass-action construction, not an endotactic one: in direction $w=1$, its maximal essential source is $m$, and the outgoing stoichiometric change is $+1$.

The baseline deterministic equation is $\dot x=1-4x$, with globally attracting positive equilibrium $a=1/4$. Its CTMC has birth rate $1$, death rate $4n$, is nonexplosive and positive recurrent, and has invariant distribution $\mathrm{Pois}(1/4)$.

The added deterministic equation is
\[
\dot x=f_m(x)=1-4x+x^m.
\]
Set $s_m=2^{-m-1}$. Then $f_m(a)=a^m>0$; also $a+s_m\le1/2$, so
\[
f_m(a+s_m)=-4s_m+(a+s_m)^m\le -2^{1-m}+2^{-m}=-2^{-m}<0.
\]
On \([a,1/2]\),
\[
f'_m(x)=-4+mx^{m-1}\le -4+\frac{m}{2^{m-1}}\le -\frac{13}{4}<0
\]
for $m\ge3$. Thus there is a unique root in $(a,a+s_m)$, and it is locally asymptotically stable. Since $f_m(x)\to+\infty$, there is a larger root and beyond the largest positive root the vector field is positive. For all sufficiently large $x$, $f_m(x)\ge x^m/2$, whence
\[
\int_R^\infty\frac{dx}{f_m(x)}\le \frac2{m-1}R^{1-m}<\infty.
\]
Solutions from sufficiently large initial concentrations therefore blow up in finite time. The network is locally stable near (a), but is not globally permanent/global ODE-complete.

The added CTMC has birth rate $b_n=1+(n)_m$ and death rate $d_n=4n$, where $(n)_m=n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)$. It has positive probability of explosion. For sufficiently large $N$,
\[
1-p_n=\frac{d_n}{b_n+d_n}\le \frac{4n}{(n)_m},\qquad p_n=\frac{b_n}{b_n+d_n},
\]
and $\sum_{n\ge N}4n/(n)_m<\infty$ for $m\ge3$. Hence $\prod_{n\ge N}p_n>0$: from $N$, there is positive probability every subsequent embedded jump is a birth. The holding times along that path have total expected value $\sum_{n\ge N}1/(b_n+d_n)<\infty$, so their sum is finite almost surely. From any lower count there is positive probability to reach $N$ by consecutive births. Thus $\mathsf H_m$ is explosive with positive probability from every finite starting count. This rules out stochastic safety in the nonexplosion sense, despite the nearby stable deterministic equilibrium.

## Exact indistinguishability and passive sample bound

At every count $n<m$, $(n)_m=0$; the generators of $\mathsf B$ and $\mathsf H_m$ agree exactly. Couple their event clocks until the first visit to $m$. From common start $n_0<m$, the baseline can reach $m$ by time $T$ only if it has at least $r=m-n_0$ immigration events. Its immigration count is $Y_T\sim\mathrm{Pois}(T)$. Therefore the total-variation distance between the two **complete event-labelled trajectory laws** on $[0,T]$ is at most
\[
\pi_{m,n_0}(T)=\Pr\{\mathrm{Pois}(T)\ge m-n_0\}.
\]
For $q$ independent trajectories, TV is at most $q\pi_{m,n_0}(T)$ by coupling/union bound. Any partial, noisy, or coarsened observation has no greater TV. With equal prior probabilities, every test has average error at least
\[
\frac{1-q\pi_{m,n_0}(T)}2.
\]
If $r>T$, the Poisson Chernoff bound gives
\[
\pi_{m,n_0}(T)\le e^{-T}\left(\frac{eT}{r}\right)^r.
\]
Thus, to achieve average error at most $\epsilon<1/2$, passive observation from $n_0$ requires at least
\[
q\ge \frac{1-2\epsilon}{\pi_{m,n_0}(T)}
\]
independent paths (a necessary condition, not a sufficient estimator guarantee). For every finite $q,T$ and fixed $\epsilon<1/2$, an unbounded unknown order $m$ can make this lower bound arbitrarily large. If a finite experiment adaptively queries or prepares counts only up to $B$, choosing $m>B$ makes the data exactly equal, regardless of measurement precision.

This is a minimax impossibility over unrestricted reaction order. It does not apply when a maximum order is supplied, when the experiment can controllably prepare the hidden threshold, or under a proved structural promise such as endotacticity. Indeed the modified network is non-endotactic, so it does not contradict Astra N33.

## High-count falsifier and its measurement cost

Prepare/reset the system at $n=m$, and acquire total at-risk exposure $\tau$ at that count while recording the $+1$ transition. The predicted cause-specific birth intensities are
\[
r_0=1\quad(\mathsf B),\qquad r_1=1+m!\quad(\mathsf H_m).
\]
With an ideal clamp giving fixed exposure, the event counts are Poisson with means $r_j\tau$. Their Hellinger affinity is
\[
A=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\tau}{2}(\sqrt{r_1}-\sqrt{r_0})^2\right].
\]
Since equal-prior optimal testing error is at most $A/2$, the exposure
\[
\tau\ge\frac{2\log(1/(2\epsilon))}{(\sqrt{1+m!}-1)^2}
\]
suffices for error at most $\epsilon$ in this idealized test. Without a clamp, use the exact event-history likelihood with accumulated occupancy time at $m$, and charge the cost of repeated resets/preparations. An observed $+1$ rate significantly above $1$ at a measured count $m$ falsifies the baseline; absence of excess only rules out this particular order and parameter choice, not all higher hidden orders.

The measured quantities are count $n$, labelled event direction, and time-at-risk; at finite volume use the corresponding volume-rescaled mass-action propensity. If reaction channels share a jump vector, the test observes the aggregate rate in that direction, which is exactly where the extra channel enters.

## Known-order positive route

Enciso–Erban–Kim, Theorem 3.1, gives a complementary exact-data result. If the maximum source order $N$ is known and all transition vectors and their exact rate values are supplied at every lattice state in
\[
\mathbb S_N=\{x\in\mathbb N_0^d:\|x\|_1\le N\},\qquad |\mathbb S_N|=\binom{N+d}{d},
\]
then the Newton/falling-factorial triangular algorithm reconstructs a unique order-at-most-$N$ mass-action reaction system, when its coefficients are nonnegative. This is identifiability from a complete finite rate map, not a finite trajectory sample-complexity theorem. Experimental realization must acquire/estimate each needed transition intensity, charge state visits or active state preparation, and supply rate-separation/conditioning margins. The paper also notes conservation-restricted classes can be structurally non-identifiable from one hyperplane.

## Boundary with Astra evidence

- N33 is an internally reconstructed deterministic theorem candidate: with the actual finite endotactic exponent-arrow diagram and a supplied global rate interval, it asserts a classwise common compact absorber under arbitrary measurable rate paths. It acquires no model from data and excludes stochastic jumps.
- N34's exact checker returns `CERTIFIED`, `REFUTED`, or `UNKNOWN` for a supplied candidate. A finite local/box certificate is not a global absorber on an unbounded class.
- N50 is a conditional computability candidate: finite real-closed-field obligations and rational all-scale affine reconstruction would give terminating search on promised rational endotactic inputs. General CAD/QE synthesis is not implemented and no complexity bound is supplied.
- N35 proves only finite-horizon jump safety from a supplied strict inward-polytope certificate. In its closed $2A\to A+B,\ 2B\to A+B$ example, each even $V\ge6$ count chain is finite irreducible and eventually exits the certified interval almost surely. So exponential finite-horizon safety is not infinite-time persistence.
- N76 only source-reports an upward event-chain potential inequality for weakly reversible systems; it explicitly lacks negative drift and a physical-time nonexplosion/recurrence result. Its original proof/code packet was unavailable.
- N77 only source-reports an example of a deterministic classwise global attractor coexisting with positive recurrent stochastic components whose stationary laws escape every common compact set. Its original proof/code packet was unavailable; treat this as a source-reported counterexample, not an externally validated theorem.

## Scope and next gate

The decision-changing experiment is a controlled copy-number ramp with direct event-direction observation and a preregistered upper reaction-order hypothesis. The decisive falsifier is a conditional (+1) intensity excess at the first count at/above that bound. If no finite upper order or high-count intervention is defensible, passive finite records cannot certify global permanence against the hidden-tail family. Deterministic global permanence additionally needs exact network/rate-box acquisition plus a verified global certificate; stochastic all-time safety needs separate nonexplosion/recurrence/tightness evidence. The current Astra results do not provide that latter general decision theorem.
