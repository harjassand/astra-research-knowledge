COMPLETE EXACT DERIVATION
Write XX=sigma_x tensor sigma_x, etc. Direct Pauli multiplication gives
 J^2=3I/2+(XX+YY+ZZ)/2,
 J_x^2-J_y^2=(XX-YY)/2,
 J_z=(ZI+IZ)/2.
The scalar 3I/2 cancels on Gibbs normalization. Set
 u=a/(2sqrt(2)), v=b/2^(3/4), d=sqrt(u^2+v^2).
The remaining exponent H has even-parity block, in (00,11),
 H_even=I/2+u X+v Z,
and odd-parity block, in (01,10),
 H_odd=-I/2+X.
Thus, with E=exp(1/2), O=exp(-1/2), S(d)=sinh(d)/d, S(0)=1,
 exp(H_even)=E[cosh(d) I+S(d)(u X+v Z)],
 exp(H_odd)=O[cosh(1) I+sinh(1)X].
Let Z=2[E cosh(d)+O cosh(1)] and define
 m=2E v S(d)/Z,
 c_x=2[E u S(d)+O sinh(1)]/Z,
 c_y=2[O sinh(1)-E u S(d)]/Z,
 c_z=2[E cosh(d)-O cosh(1)]/Z.
Their only nonzero Pauli moments are mean_z=m at each qubit and the diagonal
correlations c_x,c_y,c_z. In particular
 rho=1/4[I+m(ZI+IZ)+c_x XX+c_y YY+c_z ZZ].

For r in {+/-e_x,+/-e_y,+/-e_z,0}, put tau_r=(I+r.sigma)/2.
The seven weights in this order are
 (c_x/2,c_x/2,c_y/2,c_y/2,(c_z+m)/2,(c_z-m)/2,1-c_x-c_y-c_z).
Expansion of tau_r tensor tau_r gives exactly the preceding Pauli moments.
The last weight is equivalently 4O[cosh(1)-sinh(1)]/Z>0. Therefore they sum
exactly to one; it remains to prove all six other weights nonnegative.

For the admitted rational rectangle,
 |u|<=1/(2sqrt(2))<3/8,
 |v|<=1/(2*2^(3/4))<3/10,
 d^2<=1/8+1/(4*2^(3/2))<1/4.
The second strict bound follows on squaring from 8>625/81.
For 0<=d<=1/2, S(d)<=S(1/2)<21/20. To verify the last inequality, its first
nonconstant positive series term is 1/24, and each subsequent term/previous
term is <=1/80. Their sum is <=(1/24)/(1-1/80)=10/237<1/20.
Also exp(2)>=1+2+2+4/3+2/3=7, hence
 exp(-1)sinh(1)=(1-exp(-2))/2>=3/7,
 exp(-1)cosh(1)=(1+exp(-2))/2<=4/7.
Consequently
 |u| S(d)<63/160<3/7,
 cosh(d)-|v| S(d)>=1-(3/10)(21/20)=137/200>4/7.
These are precisely the required inequalities c_x,c_y>0 and c_z>|m|.
All seven weights are strictly positive, uniformly over this rectangle.

Noncommutation is explicit: the even-block anisotropy uX and field vZ have
commutator -2iuvY, nonzero whenever ab!=0. This is a small admitted quantum
instance, not a commuting shortcut or a claim about all anisotropies/fields.
