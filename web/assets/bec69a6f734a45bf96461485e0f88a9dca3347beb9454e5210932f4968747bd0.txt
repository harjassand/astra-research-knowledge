ERRATUM TO v1

In v1, Section 3, withdraw the clause “even within one topology class of initial data.” The perturbation A_epsilon=A_0+epsilon f(x/epsilon)g(y) is proven to preserve the named X/O critical points, total energy after normalization, and H=0; it has not been proved to preserve the full field-line topology or to introduce no other critical points. The valid counterexample is only that total magnetic energy and total magnetic helicity do not determine the instantaneous 2-D rate. v2's anastrophy calculation is exact for both fields after mean-zero gauge fixing.

ALTERNATIVE ATTEMPT: TOPOLOGY-PRESERVING AREA-PRESERVING SHEAR

To isolate rate variation while preserving all ideal 2-D flux-function Casimirs and the level-set topology, consider the area-preserving torus diffeomorphism

  phi(x,y)=(x+s q(y), y),
  A_s(x,y)=A_0(phi(x,y))=cos(x+s q(y))-cos y,

where q is smooth and periodic, q(0)=0, and q vanishes near y=pi so the O point there is unchanged. Every phi is a smooth diffeomorphism with Jacobian determinant 1. Thus A_s and A_0 are equimeasurable and have exactly the same level-set connectivity. The exact derivatives are

  A_x=-sin X,
  A_y=-s q' sin X + sin y,
  Delta A=-cos X(1+s^2 q'^2)-s q'' sin X+cos y,
  X=x+s q(y).

If q'(0) is large, the X saddle at (0,0) has Delta A_X=-s^2 q'(0)^2; with q=q'=0 near (0,pi), Delta A_O=-2. The flux rate is therefore

  R=eta_m |2-s^2 q'(0)^2|.                                 (17)

The magnetic energy and Ohmic power can also be integrated exactly over x:

  E_B = 2 pi^2 + (pi/2)s^2 integral q'(y)^2 dy,

  P_eta = eta_m { pi integral [1+2s^2 q'^2+s^4 q'^4]dy
                  +pi s^2 integral q''^2dy
                  +2 pi integral cos^2(y)dy }.               (18)

The last power term in (18) is nonnegative, and bounded P_eta implies eta_m s^2||q''||_2^2=O(1). Bounded E_B implies s||q'||_2=O(1). The one-dimensional Sobolev estimate

  |q'(0)|^2 <= C( ||q'||_2^2 + ||q'||_2 ||q''||_2 )

then gives

  eta_m s^2 |q'(0)|^2 = O(eta_m)+O(sqrt(eta_m)) -> 0.       (19)

So this smooth shear family cannot produce a nonzero eta_m-independent rate while keeping both magnetic energy and Ohmic power uniformly bounded. This is an exact no-go for this ansatz, not for all area-preserving rearrangements. The q'' term is the obstruction: localizing a large shear slope creates extra current whose Ohmic cost defeats the desired rate.

This failed route is useful because it shows how a topology-preserving rearrangement can carry large pointwise curvature yet fail the global power budget. A successful same-topology construction would need to concentrate the rate-producing curvature without paying the q''-type cost in a larger surrounding region, or use dynamics that replenishes a sheet. Neither has been achieved here.

UPDATED STATUS

The rate-energy synthesis has two scoped exact pieces: in 3-D closed periodic zero-mean resistive MHD, helicity gives an energy floor and a finite-time O(sqrt(eta_m)) helicity-drift bound; in 2-D periodic zero-guide resistive MHD, the flux-function Casimirs give an O(eta_m) finite-time distribution-drift bound and an anastrophy energy floor. A high local rate requires current concentration, but the bounded-power topology-preserving shear attempt above shows that not every geometrical way to create high curvature is admissible.

The decisive unresolved target remains a dynamically sustained O(1) reconnection rate and O(1) energy release over fixed Alfvén time, derived from the MHD equations with measured boundary/guide-field budgets and without a phenomenological turbulent closure or assumed marginal-plasmoid threshold. The 2-D Casimir floor is not a 3-D astrophysical result; the 3-D helicity floor is not a rate selector; and no empirical observation cited in v1 establishes a resistivity-independent scaling.

No breakthrough or novelty claim is made.
