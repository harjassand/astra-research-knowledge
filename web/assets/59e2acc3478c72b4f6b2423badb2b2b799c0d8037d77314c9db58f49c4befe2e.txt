T25 — multiblocker exchange snapshots and candidate cores
Version: v1 (finite Phase-IV assignment)
Status: partial derivations and exact obstructions; target theorem unresolved.

0. Scope and claim discipline
-----------------------------
Target: for k fixed matroids on a common known n-element identity ground set, fixed
nonnegative adversarial weights, uniform random arrival, irrevocable accept/reject,
and independence-oracle queries only on subsets of arrived elements, obtain expected
value Ω(OPT/k), where OPT is the maximum common-independent weight on this same
realized instance. The imported target is an O(k)-competitive algorithm with a
per-optimum-element acceptance probability Ω(1/k).

No such algorithm or matching global lower bound is proved here. The useful results
below are (i) an exact sample-to-candidate identity, (ii) a pointwise exchange
charging lemma for exact sample optimizers, and (iii) a sharp k-blocker construction
for the imported snapshot invariant. The third item is an obstruction to that
invariant, not to all algorithms. All finite computations are sanity checks only.

1. Pinned comparison: reversible greedy snapshots
-------------------------------------------------
Primary source inspected: Sahil Singla, “The Matroid Secretary Conjecture is True,”
arXiv:2609.14555v2, revised 29 September 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14555
and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14555. Relevant text is Theorem 5.1 and its proof,
PDF pp. 11–12 (arXiv PDF lines 735–840). This is a source-reported result, not
independent external validation.

For each j∈[k], let Greedy_j(a) be the decreasing-weight greedy independent set in
M_j and let B(a)=∩_j Greedy_j(a). The preprocessing draws a Bernoulli(p) sample P
with p=1−1/(2k), runs decreasing-value greedy H(T) subject to all k matroids, and
sets a_e=w_e exactly when e∉P and e∈H(P∪{e}); otherwise a_e=0. The source invokes
the overlapping-optima theorem of Feldman–Svensson–Zenklusen (FSZ22, Theorem 3.3)
to assert E_P[a(B(a))]≥OPT/(4k²). The inner reversible virtual-sample algorithm
then has the following exact analysis, conditional on P:

* For U=V\P, it generates a second sample S and virtual order Π such that
  Pr[S=T, Π=π | P]=2^{-|U|}/|U|! for every T⊆U and permutation π of U.
* For each fixed π, a permitted update C↔C△{e} preserves the consistency test on
  all finalized memberships in every matroid in both directions. Thus the update is
  an involution on the 2^U reference states. The snapshot C_e before processing e
  is uniform on 2^U and independent of Π.
* For e∈B(a), conditional on e∉C_e, inserting e changes each matroid greedy set
  by at most one exchange. Let D be the union of the distinct displaced elements;
  |D|≤k. The consistency test passes exactly when e precedes every member of D,
  which has probability 1/(|D|+1)≥1/(k+1). Since Pr[e∉C_e]=1/2,
  Pr[e accepted | P]≥1/(2(k+1)).

Consequently E[w(ALG)]≥OPT/[8k²(k+1)] in the source. The local loss is not merely
a loose union bound: the construction in §4 attains k distinct blockers for this
exact invariant.

2. Exact flip identity for candidate mass
-----------------------------------------
Let P include each element independently with probability p∈(0,1), q=1−p. Let
H(T) be any deterministic rule returning a feasible subset of T. Define

  C_H(P) = {e∉P : e∈H(P∪{e})}.

Then the following identity is exact:

  E_P[ Σ_{e∈C_H(P)} w_e ] = (q/p) E_P[w(H(P))].                 (2.1)

Proof. Every pair (P,h) with h∈H(P) maps bijectively to the candidate occurrence
(Q,h), where Q=P\{h}. Indeed h∉Q and h∈H(Q∪{h})=H(P), so h∈C_H(Q); conversely
every h∈C_H(Q) has the unique preimage P=Q∪{h}. Bernoulli probabilities obey
Pr[Q]= (q/p)Pr[P] when Q=P\{h}. Summing w_h over this bijection proves (2.1).

If H is an exact maximum-weight common-independent set on its input, then
E[w(H(P))]≥E[w(O∩P)]=p·OPT, since O∩P is feasible. Thus

  E[w(C_H(P))] ≥ q·OPT.                                      (2.2)

The exact optimizer is a diagnostic strengthening, not the source's efficient
preprocessor; finding it for general k by enumeration may take exponential time.
Its set P is already revealed, and P∪{e} is available at e's arrival, so the
arrived-elements-only oracle boundary is respected, but no polynomial-time claim
is made.

If H is the source's decreasing-weight greedy for k matroids, the standard k-factor
approximation gives only E[w(H(P))]≥p·OPT/k and hence

  E[w(C_H(P))] ≥ q·OPT/k.                                    (2.3)

Equation (2.2) concerns raw candidate weight, not the weight of a common-independent
subset of C_H(P), and it is not an online selection algorithm. In particular, even
for one rank-one matroid, C_H(P) can contain many parallel elements when they all
beat the sampled maximum; its total weight can be much larger than its feasible
value. That example alone does not refute a bound relative to OPT, since OPT is
also rank one. The needed statement is a lower bound on feasible candidate value
relative to the original OPT, or an online coupling that avoids constructing that
subset in advance.

There is also no per-optimum-element candidate guarantee for exact H. For d a
multiple of four, take goods g_1,…,g_d of weight 1 and decoys f_1,…,f_d of weight
c=3d/4. Let M_1 have the single rank-one block D={f_i}; for each j, let M_{j+1}
have the rank-one block D∪{g_j}; all remaining elements are singleton blocks. Then
O={g_1,…,g_d} is common independent and has value d. Every decoy conflicts with
every good in at least one matroid, while the goods are mutually feasible. With a
p=1/2 sample, condition on g_j∉P, let s=|P∩O|, and suppose P contains a decoy and
s+1<c. The exact optimizer on P∪{g_j} chooses a decoy (value c) rather than the
goods (value s+1), so g_j∉C_H(P). The event has probability tending to one as
d→∞, by concentration of s around d/2 and the probability 1−2^{-d} of sampling
some decoy. This refutes a constant lower bound on each fixed good's candidate
probability. It does not refute a constant feasible candidate-core bound: a
high-weight decoy may itself survive as a candidate, and the decoys collectively
conflict. This construction was supplied by T20 and the probability estimate above
is internally derived here; no claim about the optimum value of C_H(P) is made.

3. Exchange charging lemma for exact sample optimizers
-----------------------------------------------------
This is an internally derived pointwise lemma. Let H(P)=G be an exact maximum-weight
common-independent set on P, let O be a fixed maximum-weight common-independent
set on E, and define C(P)=C_H(P). Ignore zero-weight elements in the argument.
Then

  Σ_{e∈(O\P)\C(P)} w_e ≤ k·w(G\O) ≤ k·w(G).                (3.1)

Proof. Fix a positive-weight e∈(O\P)\C(P). Since H(P∪{e}) does not contain e,
its chosen set is a subset of P and has weight at most w(G). Since G itself is
feasible on P∪{e}, the optimum value on P∪{e} equals w(G). If G+e were common
independent, it would have strictly larger weight, a contradiction. Hence e is
dependent with G in at least one matroid.

Set S=G∩O and contract S in each M_j. In M_j/S, the sets G'=G\O and O'=O\G
are disjoint independent sets. For each j, let B_j be the set of e under
consideration that lie in cl_{M_j}(G). For e∈B_j, let N_j(e) be the elements of
G' in the fundamental circuit of e with respect to G' in M_j/S. For every
A⊆B_j, A⊆cl(∪_{e∈A}N_j(e)); A is independent in M_j/S, so

  |A| = r(A) ≤ r(∪_{e∈A}N_j(e)) ≤ |∪_{e∈A}N_j(e)|.

Hall's theorem therefore gives an injection e↦h_j(e)∈N_j(e) for all e∈B_j.
For each e, let X_e be the union of its assigned h_j(e) over the matroids in
which e is dependent with G. Then X_e⊆G\O, |X_e|≤k, and G−X_e+e is common
independent: in each dependent matroid, deleting its assigned circuit element
breaks the fundamental circuit, and deleting further elements preserves
independence. In each matroid j, the matching uses any h at most once; hence each
h∈G\O occurs in at most k of the sets X_e.

Optimality of G on P gives w(G)≥w(G−X_e+e), so w_e≤w(X_e). Summing over e and
using the multiplicity bound proves (3.1). Zero-weight e may be dropped; without
this convention, strict improvement from G+e is not guaranteed. A fixed tie-break
can be used for H, but the weight argument only needs positive w_e.

This does not close the core-value gap. Since O is common independent,

  w(O∩C(P)) ≥ w(O\P) − k·w(G\O),

but its expectation can be vacuous: (3.1) has a subtractive term and supplies no
upper bound on E[w(G\O)] small enough to retain Ω(OPT/k), let alone Ω(OPT).
The missing step is a complementary charge from high sample blockers to a feasible
candidate witness or to an online-recoverable shadow. No such step is proved here.

4. Sharp k-blocker instance for the source invariant
----------------------------------------------------
For each j∈[k], define M_j as the direct sum of U_{1,2} on {e,d_j} and the free
matroid on all other elements. Set auxiliary weights a_e=1 and a_{d_j}=1/2. Then
Greedy_j(a) contains e and excludes d_j, while it contains every other d_ℓ.
Therefore B(a)=∩_j Greedy_j(a)={e}. Let T={d_1,…,d_k}; in each M_j, Greedy_j(T)
contains d_j. Inserting e exchanges e for d_j in M_j, so the distinct blocker set
is exactly D={d_1,…,d_k}. On the reference snapshot T with e∉T, the full-finalized
membership consistency test passes exactly when e precedes every d_j. Under a
uniform order Π this probability is

  Pr[e is first among {e,d_1,…,d_k}] = 1/(k+1).

Thus a random choice of pivot matroid cannot improve the source-style test while
preserving its requirement that no finalized greedy membership change: all k
distinct blockers must be unfinalized. This is not a lower bound for arbitrary
secretary algorithms. On this instance the common-independent optimum is the
blocker set {d_j} with value k/2 for k≥3 (and e for k≤2); an algorithm may exploit
that aggregate value, and direct greedy is not ruled out.

The same gadget gives the exact average gate probability for the inner snapshot
when e∉C_e. Conditional on that event, T\{e} is uniform on {d_1,…,d_k}, and the
number X of actual blockers is Bin(k,1/2). Hence

  Pr[consistency passes | e∉C_e]
    = E[1/(X+1)] = (2−2^{-k})/(k+1),

using Σ_{x=0}^k binom(k,x)/(x+1)=(2^{k+1}−1)/(k+1). Multiplying by
Pr[e∉C_e]=1/2 gives the exact per-element acceptance probability
(1−2^{-(k+1)})/(k+1) in this conditional auxiliary instance. Thus the source-style
gate is Θ(1/k) here, matching its asymptotic per-element loss up to constants.

5. Accepted-only shadow update is not reversible
------------------------------------------------
For the tempting relaxation that checks preservation only on already accepted
elements, the rank-one uniform matroid U_{1,3}, equal weights, and fixed virtual
order (1,0,2) already destroy snapshot uniformity. Enumerating all eight initial
reference bit-vectors, the snapshot immediately before virtual step t=2 has
multiplicities:

  all-finalized test: each of the 8 subsets exactly once;
  accepted-only test: {1}:2, {1,2}:2, {0}:1, {0,2}:1, ∅:1, {2}:1.

Thus states {0,1} and {0,1,2} are absent under the accepted-only test. The executable
enumeration is `work/theory/t25_exchange_snapshot/finite_checks.py`; it checks the
rank-one greedy rule and the stated order by exhaustive enumeration. The intended
conclusion is only that this relaxation is false, not that all feasible online
shadow schemes must be biased.

6. Common-shadow potential idea: unresolved bridge
-------------------------------------------------
The proposed one-shadow state uses a common-independent sample set G and a common-
independent H⊇G, with gap X=|H\G|. When a sample event adds e to G, if H+e is
infeasible, the matroid-intersection exchange axiom permits removing at most one
blocker per matroid from H\G before adding e; this decreases X by at least one.
When a real event finds H+e feasible, adding e to H increases X by one. This
suggests a drift balance and avoids maintaining k independent shadows.

No valid online lemma follows yet. The process must specify how H is updated under
all events, handle changes to G's sample optimizer, and prove that the real items
retained by the potential are candidates whose order law remains uniform and whose
identities are usable at their physical arrivals. An offline common optimum H(P)
or a sorted-value process is not by itself an online decision rule. The exact
flip identity in §2 supplies candidate mass but does not supply this bridge.

7. Claim ledger and exact remaining target
------------------------------------------
* Source-reported: arXiv:2609.14555v2, Theorem 5.1, gives 8k²(k+1)-competitive
  arrived-elements-only secretary selection for k matroids; PDF pp. 11–12.
* Internally derived: exact flip identity (2.1); exact-optimizer lower bound (2.2);
  exchange charging inequality (3.1); and the k-blocker probability in §4.
* Finite-checked: the accepted-only rank-one snapshot bias in §5.
* Unresolved: a feasible candidate-core lower bound strong enough for O(k), an
  online common-shadow coupling with matching order law, or a model-matched global
  Ω(k) lower bound. The local blocker construction only proves tightness of the
  imported invariant's per-element gate.

The exact next inference, if continuing this line, is either to show that the
sample-blocker term k·w(H(P)\O) in (3.1) can be paired with a common-independent
candidate witness of comparable value without future-oracle access, or to prove a
joint contention/exchange orientation whose conditional law charges each optimum
element only O(k) total loss across all matroids. Neither is established here.
