1. TARGET AND INPUT CONTRACT

The physical model during each science block is one fixed finite irreducible CTMC
Q, with stationary law pi, reciprocal positive edge support, and all hidden variables
even under time reversal. Its entropy-production rate, in nats per physical-time
unit, is

  sigma(Q) = sum_{i<j} (pi_i q_ij - pi_j q_ji)
                       log[(pi_i q_ij)/(pi_j q_ji)].

The science sensor observes only Y_t=h(X_t) in a known finite alphabet A, through
the same deterministic map at every readout. It is stationary only in the target
population statement; the observed validation record may start from an arbitrary
hidden state. If the desired quantity is heat rather than stochastic EP, local
detailed balance and a heat convention are additional premises.

Fix 0<s_1<...<s_m=s and T=2s. In each science block of duration T, let

  U=(Y_{s-s_1},...,Y_{s-s_m}),   V=(Y_{s+s_1},...,Y_{s+s_m}),

so the two words reflect about the midpoint of the block. Write P_uv=Pr_pi(U=u,
V=v) for the stationary table. Before observing validation data, choose a symmetric
copositive D-by-D matrix W, D=|A|^m. Copositivity is proved by a finite certificate,
not granted by a numerical conic solver. Let M=max_{u,v}|W_uv|>0 and define

  Z=W_{U,V}/M in [-1,1],       mu=E_pi Z,
  delta=[-mu]_+.

The candidate W may be selected from past coarse data, but it must be frozen before
its validation epoch. A rank-one choice W=ff^T is always copositive because
x^T W x=(f^T x)^2. Other usable choices include an explicit certified matrix such
as a Horn witness. The procedure never calls a complete-positivity or copositivity
oracle.

2. POPULATION MAP FROM A COPOSITIVE VIOLATION TO PHYSICAL EP

This section records the exact population statement needed by the finite-data
procedure. It is a consequence of root v2/v4, not a new physical theorem.

Condition on X_0=i at the reflected-window midpoint. By the hidden Markov property,
the two half-paths are conditionally independent. Let a_i and b_i be the respective
probability vectors of V and U. Stationary time reversal identifies the outward
past half-path with Q* run forward from i. The stationary averaged forward and
reverse half-path divergences are each s sigma, so data processing gives

  sum_i pi_i [D(a_i||b_i)+D(b_i||a_i)] <= 2s sigma = T sigma.

The observed table is P=sum_i pi_i b_i a_i^T. For symmetric copositive W,

  b_i^T W a_i
    = ((a_i+b_i)^T W(a_i+b_i) - (a_i-b_i)^T W(a_i-b_i))/4
    >= -M ||a_i-b_i||_1^2/4
    = -M TV(a_i,b_i)^2.

Here the first quadratic form is nonnegative by copositivity, and the second is at
most M||a_i-b_i||_1^2 by the entrywise bound on W. Thus

  delta <= sum_i pi_i TV(a_i,b_i)^2.

For probability vectors at total variation v, the Jeffreys divergence obeys
D(p||q)+D(q||p) >= 4v atanh(v). This follows by data processing to the set
{p>q}; among binary laws whose probabilities differ by v, the Jeffreys divergence
is minimized at the symmetric midpoint. The function

  g(x)=4 sqrt(x) atanh(sqrt(x)) = 4 sum_{k>=1} x^k/(2k-1)

is increasing and convex on [0,1). Jensen's inequality therefore yields

  sigma T >= sum_i pi_i g(TV(a_i,b_i)^2)
          >= g(sum_i pi_i TV(a_i,b_i)^2)
          >= g(delta).                                      (A26.1)

So a validated upper confidence bound U<0 for mu gives

  sigma >= g(-U)/T.                                         (A26.2)

The conclusion is a lower confidence bound on the physical stationary EP rate for
every compatible finite all-even hidden CTMC. It does not estimate the exact rate,
and it does not make separate single-lag factorizations into one common generator.

3. WHY A FINITE COARSE RESET AUDIT CANNOT CERTIFY HIDDEN STATIONARITY

There is no distribution-free way to infer from finitely many coarse reset records
that their hidden start law is close in total variation to the target stationary
law, with no bound on hidden dimension or rates. The following exact dormant-copy
construction makes the missing information invisible.

Start with any finite irreducible base model (Q,pi,h) with EP sigma_A. For w in
(0,1), form states (i,0),(i,1). The active layer 0 runs Q. The dormant layer 1 has
no internal jumps. Add only bridges

  (i,0)->(i,1) at rate epsilon(1-w)/w,
  (i,1)->(i,0) at rate epsilon,

and set h'(i,0)=h'(i,1)=h(i). The stationary law is

  pi'(i,0)=w pi_i,       pi'(i,1)=(1-w) pi_i.

Every bridge has equal stationary forward and backward flow, so the bridge
contributes zero EP; active internal currents are multiplied by w. Consequently
sigma(Q')=w sigma_A. The visible stationary marginal is exactly the same as for
the base process.

Now suppose a proposed reset control always prepares the active layer with law
nu'(i,0)=pi_i, nu'(i,1)=0. Its hidden reset error is exactly

  ||nu'-pi'||_TV = 1-w,

which can approach one. Its visible reset marginal nevertheless equals pi h. In a
science block of duration H, the probability of no bridge is
exp[-epsilon(1-w)H/w]. Conditional on no bridge, the complete coarse record has
exactly the base-model law. Across any finite collection of reset blocks and gaps
with total physical duration L, the joint observed-law distance from the base
experiment is at most

  1-exp[-epsilon(1-w)L/w] <= epsilon(1-w)L/w.

For fixed finite L, choose epsilon small. The coarse calibration transcript is then
arbitrarily close to the base transcript even though the reset's hidden TV error is
1-w and the true stationary EP is only w sigma_A. Therefore no finite coarse-only
test can uniformly certify a nontrivial upper bound on hidden reset TV error.
The same construction warns that a reset-started negative moment can overstate the
stationary moment: the reset samples only the active driven layer, while stationary
mass and EP are reduced by w. This is an exact interface obstruction, not a
simulation result.

The pinned Astra route also contains a stronger source-reported one-path
metastability bound: N185 states that, over unrestricted hidden extensions, an
honest lower confidence bound has P_A(L_T>=c sigma_A)<=alpha/c for every finite
horizon, with an anytime version and an independent-stationary-start refinement.
N185 is marked source-derived/unreviewed, and this A26 record does not claim to have
externally validated it. Its scope supports the same conclusion: apparent sample
count from overlapping or dependent windows does not supply an effective sample
size.

4. ADDITIONAL CONTROL CHANNEL: ACQUIRE A VALID MIXING ENVELOPE

The protocol adds a temporary, non-invasive full-state calibration channel before
the coarse science record. This is an explicit change to the information interface.
The calibration instrument must provide:

  (i) a certified complete finite registry S of every microstate, so d=|S| is
      acquired and no unseen state is possible;
  (ii) exact state labels and jump times during calibration, sufficient to report
       every transition count N_ij and state exposure A_i;
  (iii) a guarantee that turning this channel on does not change Q, and that Q is
        unchanged when the sensor returns to coarse mode.

No stationary probabilities, hidden count, transition rates, or mixing time are
provided as numerical inputs. The controller supplies the complete state inventory
as measurable control information. If no instrument can certify (i)-(iii), Section
3 applies and this finite-data repair is unavailable.

Rate calibration from one trajectory. Let

  A_i(t)=integral_0^t 1{X_r=i} dr,
  N_ij(t)=number of observed i->j jumps by time t.

For a CTMC, in local time A_i the edge count N_ij is a rate-q_ij Poisson process.
This follows from the independent-clock construction of a finite CTMC and requires
no stationary initialization or mixing assumption. Set predeclared local-exposure
levels a_l=2^l a_0, l=0,1,... . At phase l wait until every state has accumulated
exposure a_l and read each edge count at its own local-exposure time. For
alpha_cal in (0,1), allocate

  alpha_lij = alpha_cal 2^(-(l+1)) / [d(d-1)]

to each directed edge interval. The two-sided exact Poisson (Garwood) interval is

  q^-_ij,l = chi2_quantile(2 N_ij(a_l), alpha_lij/2)/(2a_l),
  q^+_ij,l = chi2_quantile(2(N_ij(a_l)+1), 1-alpha_lij/2)/(2a_l),

with q^-=0 when N=0. Exact Poisson tail inversion is equivalent. Bonferroni over
all l and all directed edges gives one event C_cal with
P(C_cal)>=1-alpha_cal on which every interval at every phase contains its true
rate. A production certificate must round lower endpoints down and upper endpoints
up; ordinary floating point quantiles do not certify C_cal.

At any phase l, form the directed graph E_l={(i,j):q^-_ij,l>0}. If there is a
root r such that every state has a directed path in E_l to r, choose one such path
gamma_i=(x_0=i,...,x_ki=r) for each i. Define

  ell_i = min_{edge e in gamma_i} q^-_e,l,
  Lambda = max_i sum_{j!=i} q^+_ij,l,
  t0 = 1/Lambda,
  delta0 = min_i [ exp(-1) (ell_i/Lambda)^ki / ki! ].       (A26.3)

For the root path k_r=0, its term is exp(-1). On C_cal, Lambda bounds every
exit rate and each selected path rate is at least ell_i. The probability of making
exactly the k_i prescribed jumps along gamma_i during [0,t0] and no other jump is
at least exp(-Lambda t0) ell_i^ki t0^ki/ki!, by integrating its path density over
the ordered-time simplex. Thus P_{t0}(i,{r})>=delta0 for every i. The Doeblin
minorization implies

  sup_x ||P_{n t0}(x,.)-pi||_TV <= (1-delta0)^n.            (A26.4)

This derives, rather than assumes, a valid mixing envelope from a finite full-state
control record. No value of pi is needed. If Q is finite and irreducible, its
positive-edge graph has an in-arborescence. As a_l grows, the lower intervals on
every edge of that tree become positive and the upper exit bounds remain finite;
so the phase condition eventually occurs almost surely. The observed calibration
time is finite almost surely for each such Q. There is no uniform deterministic or
uniform expected cost bound over the unrestricted class: an unreported tiny rate
or stationary mass can make calibration arbitrarily long. The realized time is
charged and logged. If a deterministic budget is exhausted before a tree is
certified, return no EP lower bound.

Choose a desired TV error beta_* in (0,1) before validation, then set

  n_gap = ceil[ log(beta_*)/log(1-delta0) ],   g=n_gap t0.

After each coarse block, leave the same Q running for gap g before the next block;
also charge this gap before the first science block if the starting state is
arbitrary. Equations (A26.3)-(A26.4) guarantee that, conditional on the complete
past record and the hidden state at the previous block end, the next block-start
law is within beta_* of pi. Mixtures over that unknown endpoint state preserve the
same bound. Training may use any past coarse data; after freezing W, start a fresh
validation epoch after a full gap.

The robust score calibration is

  theta_k=E[Z_k | F_(k-1)],       |theta_k-mu| <= 2 beta_*.

Indeed the block-path law from a start distribution within beta_* of pi is within
beta_* in TV of its stationary law by data processing through the Markov path
kernel; expectations of a score in [-1,1] then differ by at most 2 beta_*. This
one-sided bias correction does not assume the validation scores are independent.

5. SEQUENTIAL CERTIFICATE WITH ADAPTIVE WITNESSES

Fix one witness for a validation epoch. Let D_k=Z_k-theta_k. Since Z_k lies in an
interval of width 2, conditional Hoeffding gives, for each lambda>0,

  E[ exp(-lambda D_k) | F_(k-1) ] <= exp(lambda^2/2).

Hence M_n(lambda)=exp[-lambda sum_{k<=n}D_k - n lambda^2/2] is a nonnegative
supermartingale starting at one. For any a>0, Ville's inequality bounds the
probability that M_n(lambda) ever reaches exp(a) by exp(-a). This yields an
anytime upper confidence sequence for the average predictable mean
bar_theta_n=n^-1 sum theta_k:

  bar_theta_n <= bar_Z_n + lambda/2 + a/(n lambda), for all n,

except on an event of probability exp(-a).

For an explicit countable schedule use lambda_j=2^-j and weights
w_j=1/[(j+1)(j+2)], j>=0; these weights sum to one. For adaptive witness epochs
e=0,1,..., use v_e=1/[(e+1)(e+2)], also summing to one. Given total sequential
error budget alpha_seq, set

  a_ej = log(1/[alpha_seq v_e w_j]),
  B_e,n,j = bar_Z_e,n + lambda_j/2 + a_ej/(n lambda_j).

With probability at least 1-alpha_seq, simultaneously for every epoch e, every
sample size n in that epoch, and every j, bar_theta_e,n<=B_e,n,j. The validation
upper endpoint for the stationary reflected score is

  U_e,n = min(1, min_{0<=j<=J(n)} B_e,n,j + 2 beta_e),
  J(n)=ceil(log2(n+1)).                                    (A26.5)

If the raw right side before clipping is below -1, return zero for that look as a
fail-safe; on the simultaneous coverage event this cannot happen since mu>=-1.
For finite n the displayed minimum is computed over a finite set. As n grows, the
dyadic set contains a near-optimal lambda and its radius is
O(sqrt((log(1/alpha_seq)+log log n)/n)). The actual endpoint in (A26.5), rather
than an asymptotic approximation, is used for reporting.

Proof of coverage with the mixing correction. On the martingale event,
bar_theta_e,n<=min_j B_e,n,j-2 beta_e. Each block obeys theta_k>=mu_e-2 beta_e,
so mu_e<=bar_theta_e,n+2 beta_e<=min_j B_e,n,j+2 beta_e=U_e,n (with the stated
clipping/fail-safe convention). Thus, simultaneously over all epochs and looks,

  sigma >= L_e,n := 1/T_e * g((-U_e,n)_+)                         (A26.6)

whenever U_e,n<0; otherwise set L_e,n=0. The overall confidence is at least
1-alpha_cal-alpha_seq, plus any separately allocated failure budget for the
complete-state inventory or calibration sensor. Optional stopping is valid: the
Ville event covers every n. Witnesses, stencils, or calibrations may be adapted
between epochs using all earlier data, provided the new W and gap are frozen before
that epoch's validation data and its epoch budget v_e is charged. Selecting among
K witnesses on the same validation scores instead requires a K-way error split or
separate future epochs.

The score computation uses no CP optimization. A finite bank of exactly certified
W matrices can be searched on a training portion; or a training eigendirection can
be rationalized and converted to W=ff^T, whose certificate is the square identity.
Training selection affects power only. The validation martingale starts after the
selection and gap, so no iid training/validation claim is used.

6. COST, SAMPLE SIZE, AND COMPUTATION

Calibration acquisition. At phase l the controller must collect local exposure
a_l in every one of d states and log d(d-1) edge counts. The realized physical
calibration duration is tau_cal; it is included in the record. On each stage,
interval construction costs O(d^2) quantile inversions, the graph search costs
O(d^2), storage is O(d^2), and each calibration jump updates one count. There is no
uniform cap on tau_cal without lower rate or stationary-mass information. The
complete-state inventory, perfect jump timing, and non-disturbance guarantee are
material control costs, not free assumptions.

For n coarse validation blocks, each of span T and gap g, elapsed science time is
at least g+nT+(n-1)g, plus tau_cal and any training blocks/gaps. Each block uses
2m observation reads. The gap is charged even though its observations are not used.
At target stationary score gap Delta=-mu>0, the correction must satisfy 2 beta_*<
Delta. With d_eff=Delta-2 beta_*, a simple planned choice lambda about d_eff gives
a Hoeffding sample scale n about 2a_ej/d_eff^2 to cross zero with margin; exact
stopping uses the endpoint in (A26.5). If beta_*>=Delta/2, no amount of validation
data can overcome the supplied bias correction; increase the gap (and charge it)
or report zero. This explicitly exposes the near-equilibrium and metastable cost.

Science computation. With D=|A|^m, full empirical symmetric-table training costs
O(n_train+D^3) time and O(D^2) memory if it uses the A13 minimum eigenvector;
a fixed bank of K witnesses costs O(n_train K) score updates plus its stored
certificates. A validation word takes O(m) symbol encoding and one W lookup. The
confidence endpoint costs O(log n) arithmetic if recomputed at every block; at
predeclared checkpoints the total can be kept near O(n) score work. Certified
implementation rounds q^- and delta0 down, q^+ and beta up, and U up. The current
record contains mathematical pseudocode, not a compiled executable certificate.

The full state calibration adds a substantial sensor/control channel. It reveals
the finite state inventory and transitions during calibration, so this is not a
solution from coarse data alone. It is nevertheless a finite-cost way to acquire a
usable mixing envelope without being handed the hidden count, pi, rates, or a mixing
time. If the sensor changes the generator, or its state census can omit dormant
states, the envelope is invalid and no positive A26 claim is made.

7. EQUALLY INFORMED BASELINES AND SCOPE

Matched fixed-time baseline. Use the same coarse blocks, same calibrated gap beta,
same frozen W, same alpha, and same calibration channel, but choose n in advance.
Conditional Hoeffding gives the fixed-n score endpoint

  bar_Z_n + sqrt(2 log(1/alpha_seq)/n) + 2 beta.

It avoids the log-log-time price and is generally sharper at that chosen n, but it
does not permit unrestricted data-dependent stopping. This is the closest
equal-information baseline for A26. The sequential endpoint is for users who may
stop when a useful bound appears.

A13 fixed-split baseline. A13 uses a training/validation split of independent
stationary windows and a fixed-time empirical-Bernstein interval. At a predeclared
sample count it may be narrower through empirical variance adaptation, with no full
state calibration, but it requires independent stationary starts. It does not
cover one dependent record merely because the record contains many overlapping
windows. No numerical matched-power comparison was run; no efficiency advantage is
claimed.

N186 in the pinned Astra source gives a standard NWJ betting confidence sequence
for observed path KL, conditional on independent stationary starts or a supplied
mixing envelope. A26's extension is different in its statistic and physical map:
it validates a time-even reflected-word copositive witness, including cases where
the observed path law is exactly time-reversal invariant, and converts its negative
population moment through the nonlinear midpoint function g. The acquired mixing
envelope in Section 4 is an added control channel, not a theorem from the coarse
record alone.

Live primary-source comparison: M. Aznagulov, “The Spectral Cost of Detecting
Nonequilibrium at Finite Temporal Resolution,” arXiv:2609.37205v1,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37205 (accessed 2026-10-09), assumes a stationary start,
fully observed finite irreducible CTMC alternatives, passive state snapshots with
adaptively chosen gaps bounded below, and tests against all finite hidden reversible
models. Its two-clock marked-current test uses one dependent trajectory (main text
Secs. Observation model and Guarantee against hidden equilibrium; Eqs. (8)-(10)).
That is a primary counterexample to any blanket claim that dependent trajectories
can never support valid inference. It does not supply A26's coarse time-even
reflected-word moment: the paper's test is antisymmetric in observed transition
direction, and its alternative is fully observed. Its spectral snapshot cost result
does not certify reset fidelity for this hidden coarse observation interface.

Sequential-statistics prior art: S. Howard, A. Ramdas, J. McAuliffe, and J. Sekhon,
“Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences,” Ann. Statist.
49(2), 1055-1080 (2021), DOI 10.1214/20-AOS1991,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08240 (v9, accessed 2026-10-09). Their Sec. 2 formulates
confidence sequences for averages of conditional means without requiring iid data;
Theorem 1 and the supermartingale framework provide time-uniform coverage. A26's
bounded Hoeffding mixture is a direct elementary instance of that established
machinery, not a new concentration inequality.
