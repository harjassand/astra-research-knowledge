1. INTERFACE AND THEOREM

Let H be a finite-dimensional or separable Hilbert space and X a nonempty
trace-norm compact family of pure projectors P_x=|x><x|, ||x||=1. The unknown
input is ONE P_x itself and the decoder reconstructs the same complete density
on H. Neither a purifying reference, unknown quantum side, entanglement nor
shared retained frame is supplied. Public description of X is allowed.

Let e_Q(X) be the infimum worst-case half-trace error over encoder/decoder
pairs factoring through an arbitrary classical outcome and a quantum branch
of dimension <=Q, Q>=1 an integer. All retained labels count separately;
the converse permits any alphabet. On each FINITE input/output restriction,
composite Kraus operators have rank<=Q, since each decoder-encoder Kraus
composition factors through that branch. Section 4 completes the infinite-
dimensional/continuous-instrument case via finite restrictions and compactness;
it does not presume a globally bounded generalized Kraus representation.
Nonunital channels, arbitrary output coherences,
ancilla regeneration and factor mixing are admitted.

Fix an anchor P_x0 in X. For any x, allow a finite path in X from x0 to x
with nonzero adjacent overlaps. Its weighted length is

  ell(path)=sum_edges 1/|<x_(i-1),x_i>|.

Assume a finite uniform bound

  kappa=max{1,sup_x inf_paths ell(path)}<infinity.          (1.1)

Actual unit-vector phases do not matter. A common anchor overlap >=b>0
is the special case kappa<=1/b. A path is a proof premise about the public
family, not an acquired parameter supplied to the encoder or decoder.

Define the convex Q-subspace width

  omega_Q(X)=inf_(0<=T<=I, Tr T<=Q) sup_x [1-Tr(T P_x)].   (1.2)

Positive T with finite trace is trace class. In finite dimension this is
the usual convex hull of orthogonal projections of rank <=Q; it is not
the requirement that one deterministic Q-subspace serves every state.

The quantitative theorem is

  omega_Q(X)/(148 kappa^2)
      <=e_Q(X)<=min{1,2sqrt(omega_Q(X))}.                 (1.3)

The upper is an infimum, with arbitrary accuracy slack and finite labels
as specified in Section 5. No exact optimizer or equality of the two bounds
is asserted. Their epsilon exponents differ; for factorial/exponential
spectral tails they still give the same order of required quantum dimension.

The exact variational dual is

  omega_Q(X)=max_(probability pi on X)
       [1-sum_(i=1..Q)lambda_i(sigma_pi)],
  sigma_pi=int_X P_x dpi, lambda_i nonincreasing.          (1.4)

Thus any declared prior supplies a valid branch converse, while the width
itself chooses the hardest prior. Its acquisition/optimization is not free.
Section 7 derives explicit coherent chart consequences without an oracle.

2. TRACE ERROR CONTROLS BOTH INFIDELITY AND OFF-DIAGONAL OUTPUT

First consider any FINITE family/path span with a TP channel having rank-Q
Kraus A_a and worst-case error epsilon. Sums may be Kraus integrals for a
measurable alphabet. Finite input/output dimension makes their common
matrix-valued refinement legitimate; Section 4 supplies the general completion.
For each x write

  A_a x=lambda_(a,x)x+r_(a,x), <x,r_(a,x)>=0,
  f_x=sum_a||r_(a,x)||^2,
  u_x=sum_a conjugate(lambda_(a,x))r_(a,x).

TP gives ||lambda_x||^2=1-f_x. The target projector is a bounded effect,
so 0<=f_x<=epsilon. Crucially also

  ||u_x||<=epsilon.                                     (2.1)

Indeed u_x is exactly (I-P_x)Phi(P_x)x. If u_x!=0 use the Hermitian
off-diagonal test H_x=|u_x/||u_x||><x|+|x><u_x/||u_x|||, whose operator norm
is one. Its expectation on Phi(P_x)-P_x is 2||u_x||. Trace duality gives
2||u_x||<=||Phi(P_x)-P_x||_1. The zero case is immediate. This estimate
uses the aggregate Kraus coherence, whose cancellations matter. Bounding
it only by sqrt(f_x) would lose the stronger conclusion below.

3. OFF-DIAGONAL TP IMPROVES THE SCALAR KRAUS COEFFICIENT BOUND

Let x,y be any adjacent pair with b=|<x,y>|>0, and put
d_xy=||lambda_x-lambda_y||. Applying TP to the INPUT off-diagonal matrix
element gives

  <x,y>=sum_a<A_a x,A_a y>.

After expansion the leading term is <x,y><lambda_x,lambda_y>. In one
cross term replace the coefficients lambda_x by lambda_y, and conversely
in the other. The resulting aligned terms are controlled by u_x,u_y in
(2.1); the remaining coefficient differences by Cauchy-Schwarz and
sqrt(f_x),sqrt(f_y). The residual-residual term is <=sqrt(f_x f_y).
Therefore

  b |1-<lambda_x,lambda_y>|
       <=3epsilon+2sqrt(epsilon)d_xy.                    (3.1)

Also
Re(1-<lambda_x,lambda_y>)=(f_x+f_y+d_xy^2)/2>=d_xy^2/2.
For epsilon>0 the resulting quadratic is

  b(d_xy/sqrt(epsilon))^2
       <=6+4(d_xy/sqrt(epsilon)).

Its positive root is <=6/b for 0<b<=1. Consequently

  d_xy<=6sqrt(epsilon)/b.                               (3.2)

At epsilon=0 the same inequality follows directly with d_xy=0. Summing
(3.2) along a path and then taking the infimum of its weighted length gives

  ||lambda_x-lambda_x0||<=6kappa sqrt(epsilon)            (3.3)

uniformly in every state. Compactness is not used in this channel inequality;
it will be used for infinite-family completion, exact minimax and finite
approximation of the width.
No state is replaced by an estimate, and the path does not enter the code.

4. KRAUS RANGES PRODUCE A FEASIBLE CONVEX Q-WIDTH OPERATOR

Let R_a be the range of A_a, with orthogonal projection P_a of rank<=Q;
zero Kraus have weight zero and can be discarded. Set

  W=sum_a|lambda_(a,x0)|^2=1-f_x0.

For epsilon<=1/2, W>=1/2. With B_a=A_a-lambda_(a,x0)I,

  sum_a||B_a x||^2
    <=2f_x+2||lambda_x-lambda_x0||^2
    <=74kappa^2 epsilon.                                (4.1)

Since (I-P_a)A_a=0,

  |lambda_(a,x0)|^2||(I-P_a)x||^2<=||B_a x||^2.

The positive operator

  T=sum_a [|lambda_(a,x0)|^2/W] P_a                      (4.2)

is a contraction and has trace<=Q. Summing the previous inequality proves

  1-Tr(TP_x)<=148kappa^2 epsilon                        (4.3)

for every x, hence omega_Q<=148kappa^2 epsilon. This bounds arbitrary
input-dependent hybrid outcome distributions using a fixed convex operator
constructed from the anchor Kraus coefficients. It does not presume that
the encoder chooses a random subspace independently of its input.

If epsilon>1/2, the lower in (1.3) is already automatic because omega_Q<=1
and kappa>=1. Taking the infimum over legal codes proves the universal lower.
Whenever a bounded rank-Q Kraus sum/integral exists globally, (4.2) is also
trace class in infinite dimension by its positive trace bound. But the proof
does NOT require that representation for every continuous instrument.

Here is the general completion. Given any finite test set F in X, include
the anchor and finite overlap paths of length <=kappa+delta to its members.
Let V be the FINITE span of all these vectors. Restrict the encoder input to
V and compose the decoder output with

  C_V(Y)=P_V Y P_V+Tr[(I-P_V)Y]P_x0.

This CPTP map fixes all selected targets, contracts their trace error and
preserves the quantum branch budget. The resulting code has finite input
and output dimension. For a continuous classical instrument, dominate its
outcome measures by the scalar measure obtained from I_V/dim(V). Its finite
matrix Choi densities are positive; finite Kraus refinements of each encoder
and decoder yield measurable composite matrices of rank<=Q. Null measure
sets can be discarded. Thus the preceding proof applies on V, even when the
original instrument resembles a generalized position measurement on H.

It produces a feasible finite-rank T_F with every selected state's tail
<=148(kappa+delta)^2 epsilon. Send delta to zero and use weak-* compactness
of the feasible trace-Q contractions (proved in Section 6) to obtain the
same inequality with kappa. These closed finite-set constraints have the
finite-intersection property, so compactness supplies a feasible T satisfying
(4.3) on ALL X. Equivalently apply the finite-set bound to finite priors,
approximate any prior's compact-family mean in trace norm, and use the dual
in Section 6. No global generalized Kraus boundedness, countable outcome
assumption, uncharged reference or output-energy constraint is needed.

5. POSITIVE FINITE PROJECTION CODE: LABELS AND TOTAL D CHARGED

First take H of finite dimension D. For any feasible T, diagonalize it with
eigenvalues t_i in [0,1], sum t_i<=Q. The polytope

  {t in [0,1]^D:sum t_i<=Q}

has only 0/1 extreme points: if two coordinates are fractional they can be
perturbed oppositely; if one is fractional and the sum constraint is slack
it can be perturbed alone; one fractional coordinate cannot saturate the
integer-Q constraint. Thus T is a convex combination of commuting rank<=Q
orthogonal projections onto subsets of its OWN eigenbasis. Caratheodory in
R^D gives at most D+1 projections, better than a D^2-dimensional bound.

Choose the projection index j at the encoder according to that public
mixture, RETAIN it as a classical label, and perform projection/fallback
compression into R_j. Decode by its public isometry. For each target x,
with delta_j=1-Tr(P_j P_x), the conditional channel has error <=2sqrt(delta_j)
by the gentle bound. Averaging and Jensen give

  e<=2sqrt(sum_j p_j delta_j)=2sqrt(1-Tr(TP_x)).            (5.1)

For a nonempty R_j the fallback is a fixed vector in R_j; its branch is
dim R_j<=Q. A zero projection is realized by a fixed-state branch of
dimension one. Thus C_labels<=D+1 and

  D_total=sum_j dim(branch_j)<=(D+1)Q.                    (5.2)

There is no shared random seed supplied to the decoder: the selected index
is retained and charged. The input state and both maps are otherwise
parameter independent. This constructs the upper with every quantum side.
Projection probabilities, eigenbasis and optimizer are public family data,
not unknown parameter values; computing them is a separate cost.

For infinite H and a feasible trace-class T, its first D eigenmodes form
T_D with ||T-T_D||_op<=Q/(D+1). Given slack delta>0 take D>=Q/delta.
Its width is <=omega_Q+delta plus any chosen optimizer slack. The preceding
finite eigenbasis code has C<=D+1=O(1+Q/delta), D_total<=C Q. Consequently
finite-label archives approach 2sqrt(omega_Q) arbitrarily closely. One can
set delta to a specified fraction of epsilon^2 for a target error allocation;
then this generic existence bound uses O(Q/epsilon^2) labels and
O(Q^2/epsilon^2) total dimension. It is not claimed optimal. Explicit chart
cutoffs in LOCAL_CHART_PROFILE use one label and do better in total cost.

This is a variational/existence theorem unless the family supplies T and
its spectrum. Computing an optimal T over an infinite parameter set, worst-
case separation oracles, finite-precision eigensystems/projections, quantum
gates, conditioning, runtime and synthesis accuracy are not obtained merely
by (1.3)-(1.4). In a finite supplied D-dimensional family specification,
(1.2) is a semidefinite convex problem with all those oracle issues exposed.

6. EXACT COVARIANCE-SPECTRUM DUAL AND ZERO-ERROR CONSEQUENCE

The feasible contractions form a compact set in the weak-* topology of
B(H), paired with trace-class operators. Positivity and I-T positivity are
closed. The trace<=Q condition is closed because it is equivalent to all
finite orthogonal compressions having trace<=Q; a positive operator obeying
these conditions is trace class. The operator-norm unit ball is weak-*
compact. Probability measures on trace-norm compact X are weakly compact.

For f(T,pi)=1-Tr(T sigma_pi), fixed pi gives weak-* continuity in T, and
fixed T gives continuity in pi because Tr(TP_x) is continuous on X. The
function is affine in both variables. Compact convex minimax therefore gives

  inf_T sup_pi f(T,pi)=sup_pi inf_T f(T,pi).

For every density sigma, maximizing Tr(Tsigma) over the feasible set gives
exactly its sum of Q largest eigenvalues: diagonalize sigma, use its diagonal
coefficients 0<=T_ii<=1 and sum T_ii<=Q, and fill the largest Q entries.
An orthogonal top-Q spectral projection attains the maximum. This proves
(1.4). The max over pi is attained: the mean-state map from measures on
compact X is trace-norm continuous (approximate X uniformly by a finite
partition of trace-norm diameter), and the Ky Fan sum is trace-norm Lipschitz.
Alternatively finite priors already supply every lower without attainment.

If the span of X has dimension >Q, choose Q+1 independent members and the
uniform finite prior. Its covariance has rank Q+1 and a positive smallest
nonzero eigenvalue, so omega_Q>0. Under (1.1), every fixed Q has a positive
error floor. If span dimension<=Q, its deterministic projection stores the
family exactly. Thus zero-error memory equals the span dimension on an
overlap-connected pure family. This is a quantitative extension consistent
with established exact nondisturbance/sufficiency results, not a new claim
to that exact principle.

7. COHERENT CHART CONSEQUENCES: FIXED AND GROWING RADII

Two-mode coherent vectors obey
|<alpha,beta>|=exp(-||alpha-beta||^2/2). In the radius-R ball, connect each
alpha to vacuum by ceil(||alpha||) equal displacement steps of length<=1.
All intermediate states stay in the ball; the zero displacement needs no
edge. Hence

  kappa<=exp(1/2)(R+1),
  e_Q>=omega_Q/[148 e (R+1)^2].                          (7.1)

Unlike a direct vacuum-overlap bound exp(-R^2/2), this chain estimate does
not lose exponentially with radius. Radius itself is still charged in the
converse. For the prior uniform in the complex two-dimensional radius-R
ball, its volume is pi^2 R^4/2. The coherent resolution
int_(C^2) P_alpha d^4alpha/pi^2=I follows by Gaussian/Fock monomial
integration, so its mean obeys sigma_pi<=2/R^4 I. Consequently

  omega_Q>=max{0,1-2Q/R^4},
  e_Q>=[1-2Q/R^4]_+/[148 e (R+1)^2].                    (7.2)

For example if R>=1 and epsilon R^2<=1/(1184 e), e<=epsilon forces
Q>=R^4/4. Every archive on a larger domain must obey the same lower for a
subball. Choosing R=c epsilon^(-1/2) with fixed sufficiently small c>0
therefore supplies Q=Omega(epsilon^(-2)) whenever that growing-radius ball
is actually in the experiment. This is a valid growing-chart pure lower,
not a claim that a bounded chart has the same capacity. Any proposed local
comparison must support this radius and its propagation/error costs.

For fixed R and epsilon->0, the sharper factorial covariance tail or the
finite-grid lemma of LOCAL_CHART_PROFILE gives the matched polylogarithmic
order. The width theorem also strengthens that lemma: a D-vector frame with
Gram minimum g and common anchor overlap b has covariance eigenvalues of
G/D. For Q<=D/2 its tail is >=g/2. Thus

  epsilon>=b^2 g/296,                                   (7.3)

instead of the earlier weaker (b g/56)^2. Both still imply
Q_min=Theta_R[(log(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon))^2] for the bounded coherent
phase chart; no old local proof needs repair. All these consequences refer
to the PURE source and SAME pure target, with no environmental purification.

The uniform-ball covariance can also be evaluated exactly without an oracle.
Its number-N eigenvalue, of multiplicity N+1, is

  nu_N=(2/R^4) int_0^(R^2) e^(-t)t^(N+1)/(N+1)! dt
      =(2/R^4) Pr{Poisson(R^2)>=N+2}.                    (7.4)

Independent phase integration makes it diagonal; a one-dimensional beta
integral over the two occupation radii yields the displayed scalar. These
eigenvalues decrease with N and sum with multiplicities to one. Filling the
largest Q entries gives an explicit covariance-tail certificate at every
integer Q. In particular at fixed R, nu_N is bounded below by its first
Poisson term and above by the corresponding factorial tail. With Q of order
N^2, the logarithm of its remaining tail has order -sqrt(Q)log(Q+2).
Equations (1.3),(7.4) and the deterministic Poisson cutoff therefore give a
second, covariance-based proof of the bounded-chart rank order, separate
from finite Gram conditioning. This is not an assertion that uniform-ball
prior attains the worst-prior maximum in (1.4).

8. PRECISE FAILURES AND THE MIXED IID GATE

The overlap-path premise is essential. For the D orthogonal basis states,
measurement and preparation gives Q=1, zero error, D retained labels. But
omega_1=1-1/D, from their uniform covariance. There is no finite overlap path
between distinct members. Therefore no universal e_Q>=c omega_Q can hold
without a connectivity/overlap cost. This is an exact finite counterexample,
with labels charged, not a hypothetical limiting concern.

Purifying a thermal state changes the input experiment and reconstruction
interface. The pure Kraus coefficient and residual bounds above cannot be
assigned to a mixed input's eigenvectors: the archive is required to preserve
the mixed density only. The actual qutrit iid problem supplies no such
purification. In particular (7.2) is NOT an iid cold lower, even if an ideal
oscillator pure chart exists as a control. The mixed local upper in
LOCAL_CHART_PROFILE and the thermalized epsilon-lift obstruction remain
separate gates. A thermal mixed covariance has no automatic pure width
interpretation.

The supplied independent-product theorem has its own bounded thermal
self-score and positive kernel, robust to arbitrary joint Kraus rank. It
does not follow by multiplying the width bounds for two subfamilies. The
actual full U3 ensemble and rectangular Schur-sector transitions still need
a direct mixed score or a legally acquired global mixed comparison. Neither
an uncharged frame, bounded-chart extrapolation nor wrong-direction positive
mixture can bypass that requirement.
