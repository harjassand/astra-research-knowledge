Selective source/read ledger — leaf 08
Read date: 2026-10-08 (UTC date)
Purpose: check the primary finite-time W1 comparison and locate uniform-time
theorem assumptions that would otherwise make a broad negative claim unsafe.

1. R. L. Dobrushin, “Vlasov equations,” Functional Analysis and Its
Applications 13(2), 115–123 (English translation, 1979). DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01077243
Record and full-text entry:
https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=faa&option_lang=eng&paperid=1898&wshow=paper
Read: MathNet bibliographic entry and abstract; primary article PDF via the
MathNet full-text endpoint. The opening setup defines the general interaction
ODE and Vlasov measure solutions; Theorem I states existence/uniqueness and
finite-time weak convergence from weak convergence of initial empirical
measures. This source establishes the historical finite-time framework. It
was not used for a quantitative W1 constant in this leaf.

2. Mikaela Iacobelli, “A New Perspective on Wasserstein Distances for Kinetic
Problems,” Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis 244, 27–50 (2022),
published online 2022-02-07. DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-021-01705-9
Primary publisher page:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00205-021-01705-9
Read: Section 1.2 definitions and general domain, including X=R^d and the
phase-space W1 cost; the introductory statement of the classical Dobrushin
estimate; Section 2.2, Theorem 2.1 and its proof. The directly applicable
R^d estimate is W1(f1(t),f2(t))<=exp((1+2B)t)W1(f1(0),f2(0)) for smooth
convolution potential with bounded Hessian B; for this leaf B=1. The refined
Theorem 2.1 also states the minimum of an adapted early-time factor and the
classical exponential factor. Its Section 1.2 introduces both T^d and R^d,
but the displayed proof writes integrals over T^d; direct use of the refined
factor for the quadratic R model is therefore a source-scope item to confirm.
The leaf relies on the general-domain classical factor. The exact empirical
Gaussian calculation is not taken from the paper. The atom-valued empirical
solution is within the measure-valued characteristic framework; this
application should be checked against the full theorem wording in any later
formal audit.

3. Pierre Monmarche, “Long-time behaviour and propagation of chaos for mean
field kinetic particles,” Stochastic Processes and their Applications 127(6),
1721–1737 (June 2017). DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2016.10.003
Primary publisher page/search record:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304414916301788
Read: abstract, Assumption 1, Theorem 3, and the stated model scope from the
publisher-indexed full-text/search extract. The result is uniform in time,
but for a Vlasov–Fokker–Planck/Langevin system with friction and noise,
strictly convex external confinement, and semiconvex interaction; it is
recorded only as a scope contrast. Full publisher page was access-restricted
in the browser, so the theorem statement was cross-checked against the
publisher-indexed extract rather than a downloaded PDF.

Scope and stopping rule: three targeted primary-source checks were sufficient
to establish (i) a directly matching W1 finite-time stability estimate and
(ii) the assumptions of a positive uniform-time theorem in another model
class. No broad literature search or novelty/priority claim was attempted.
