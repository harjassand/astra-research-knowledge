POLYNOMIAL-TIME PREDICTION FOR ARBITRARY STATIONARY RENEWALS
Primary-source novelty/priority check
Search cutoff: 2026-10-08 (queries run 2026-10-08)

RESULT

Status: UNKNOWN for an absolute nonexistence or priority claim. In the primary
sources located and inspected for this audit, I found no theorem giving a
polynomial-time predictor with the exact interface and O(n^{-1/2}) expected
terminal conditional-KL guarantee below. This bounded search does not establish
that no such algorithm exists. It does establish two useful controls: (i) the
known exact-rate predictor is an averaged Shtarkov/NML predictor for which the
benchmark paper explicitly leaves efficient computation open; and (ii) CTW
gives a polynomial-time, same-path predictor with a derived O(log n/sqrt(n))
guarantee, while older polynomial estimators use a moment promise and a
different loss/evaluation interface. I found no renewal-specific computational
hardness theorem that would justify a hardness claim.

TARGET INTERFACE

Input: one stationary binary renewal path X_1,...,X_n. A 1 denotes a renewal;
inter-renewal times after the initial residual waiting time are iid from an
arbitrary law mu on positive integers with finite mean. There is no common
upper bound on E_mu[T], no tail or moment bound uniform over the class, no
reset samples, and no supplied context/features. Output a distribution q(.|x_1:n)
for X_{n+1}. Loss is the source-expectation

  E_P[ KL(P(X_{n+1}|X_1:n) || q(.|X_1:n)) ].

The target minimax rate is Theta(n^{-1/2}) over the full finite-mean stationary
renewal class. “Polynomial time” below means a worst-case polynomial in path
length n (including representation/arithmetic costs); it cannot hide a
dependence on a tail or mixing parameter that the interface does not supply.

BENCHMARK AND MAIN FINDING

Han, Jiang & Wu, “Prediction from Compression for Models with Infinite Memory,”
arXiv:2404.15454v1, submitted 2024-04-23; COLT 2024 version, PMLR 247 (2024),
pp. 2270–2307. Primary sources:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15454v1
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/han24a.html
  https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/han24a/han24a.pdf

Theorem 4 and §1.1 (arXiv HTML, lines 168–182) define the stationary renewal
class with finite mean and give minimax terminal next-symbol KL risk
Theta(1/sqrt(n)). Risk (1) is expected KL from the true conditional given the
observed length-n prefix. Section 3.4 (HTML lines 283–306) upper-bounds the
class’s minimax redundancy by Theta(sqrt(n)); it then obtains the terminal
predictor by averaging the one-step conditionals of a finite-horizon Shtarkov /
NML distribution, plus the renewal-memory term O(log(n)/n). The paper states
that finding a computationally efficient optimal predictor is open/unclear.
This is an information-theoretic existence construction; no polynomial-time
evaluation algorithm for that NML mixture is supplied there.

The polynomial dynamic program in the same paper is not a renewal solution:
Theorem 1 and §3.3 address HMMs with a supplied, bounded finite state count and
alphabet, with time n^{O(k^2+k|A|)}. Its runtime result cannot be transferred to
the countably infinite countdown-state realization used to represent general
renewals.

3. Garivier, “Redundancy of the Context-Tree Weighting Method on Renewal and
   Markov Renewal Processes,” IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 52(12),
   5579–5586 (December 2006), DOI 10.1109/TIT.2006.885484. Primary publication
   listing: https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/aurelien.garivier/www.math.univ-toulouse.fr/_agarivie/index11d8.html?q=listepublications
   Author’s 2006 thesis, Chapter 3 (contains the proof and definitions):
   https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~biblio/theses/BJHTUP11_2006__0708__P0_0.pdf
   Inspected thesis Theorem 22 and surrounding Chapter 3.2–3.3. The theorem
   gives CTW pointwise individual redundancy on the stationary renewal class
   between c sqrt(n) log(n) and C sqrt(n) log(n), up to constants, i.e. an
   efficient universal code within a logarithmic factor of the minimax
   individual redundancy. It is not a theorem that CTW’s own terminal
   next-symbol KL risk is Theta(n^{-1/2}), and its lower bound on CTW code
   regret does not imply a lower bound for every predictor or for a separately
   analyzed suffix aggregate.
   Decision: strongest same-class efficient control found; gives a log-factor
   near-match under the target loss by the derived reduction below, not exact
   O(n^{-1/2}) as currently certified by the cited result.

4. Derived CTW terminal-risk control (not a theorem stated in Garivier).
   Han et al. Proposition 1 converts a sequential code Q with cumulative
   redundancy R_m into the terminal predictor

      qbar_{n+1|n}(.|x_1:n) = (1/n) sum_{t=1}^n
          Q_{t+1|t}(.|x_{n-t+1:n}).

   It bounds target expected terminal KL by R_n/n plus the renewal memory term
   O(log(n)/n). The suffixes are all computed from the one supplied path; this
   construction does not use independent resets. Substituting Garivier’s
   CTW upper bound R_n=O(sqrt(n) log n) gives O(log(n)/sqrt(n)). This is a
   derived guarantee, not an exact-rate theorem for CTW. It leaves open whether
   direct analysis of CTW or a different polynomial predictor removes the log.

   Runtime accounting for a straightforward binary CTW suffix implementation
   (derived, not source-reported): for each t, run/update a depth-t context tree
   on the length-t suffix; at most O(t) suffix nodes are updated per symbol,
   hence O(t^2) rational arithmetic operations for that suffix. All n suffixes
   take O(sum_{t<=n} t^2)=O(n^3) rational operations and O(n^2) active tree
   nodes if runs are sequential. With exact KT factors and CTW recursion, a
   conservative intermediate rational bit-length bound is polynomial (one
   coarse bound is O(t^2 log(t+2)) bits per length-t run, since the total
   context-symbol count is O(t^2)); the final n-term average also has
   polynomial bit length. Thus a naive schoolbook implementation has a
   polynomial bit-operation bound (conservatively O(n^7 log^2(n+2)) using
   O(n^3) operations on O(n^2 log n)-bit values). This is an upper bound for
   this explicit construction, not Garivier’s stated runtime. It processes
   retained suffixes of the same path; it is not a memory-saving advantage
   over a full-data method. No claim is made that this derived runtime is tight.

5. Oliveira, “Stochastic Processes with Random Contexts: A Characterization
   and Adaptive Estimators for the Transition Probabilities,” IEEE Trans. IT
   61(12), 6910–6925 (2015), DOI 10.1109/TIT.2015.2496200; preprint arXiv:1309.2819
   (2013/2016 versions). Primary source:
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2819
   Inspected Theorem 4 and Remark 8. The renewal estimator assumes a uniform
   (2+gamma)-moment bound on the interarrival law. Its guarantee is high
   probability expected total variation between the true transition and the
   estimate evaluated on an independent fresh infinite past, at order
   O(sqrt(log(n)/n)) under that moment promise. Remark 8 gives enhanced suffix
   tree computation O(|A|^2 n^2) time and O(|A| n) space. This is a useful
   polynomial-time result but differs in all load-bearing respects: uniform
   tail/moment promise, TV loss rather than terminal KL, and fresh-past rather
   than the observed terminal prefix. It does not imply the target claim.

CLAIM STATUS

SOURCE-REPORTED: HJW Theorem 4 minimax rate; HJW efficient-computation caveat;
Garivier CTW individual redundancy; Oliveira moment-bounded TV guarantee and
suffix-tree runtime; listed candidate scopes.

DERIVED HERE: HJW online-to-batch applied to Garivier’s CTW upper redundancy,
yielding O(log(n)/sqrt(n)) terminal KL for a same-path suffix aggregate; the
explicit polynomial rational-operation/bit-cost accounting for that aggregate.

NOT ESTABLISHED: an exact O(n^{-1/2}) polynomial-time algorithm under the target
interface; a lower bound against all polynomial-time predictors; impossibility
or priority/novelty; or whether direct CTW terminal risk itself has a sharper
analysis.
