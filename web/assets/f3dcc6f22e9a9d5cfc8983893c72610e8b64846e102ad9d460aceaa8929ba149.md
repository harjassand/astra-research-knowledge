# Complement-LCU, square-root, and Naimark variants for the full sum

Date: 2026-10-08  
Status: **Exact compressions derived; one scoped no-go proved; polynomial-row access for the complement-LCU image remains UNKNOWN.** The direct term-frame and active-flag Naimark images admit an exact row-mass obstruction for every positive constant-gap enforcer on a BMVZ-admissible hard family. Complement LCU retains a constant-factor compression and a unit-gap canonical image penalty, but the penalty's row support is not bounded polynomially by the present analysis. No full sparse encoding or unrestricted impossibility is claimed.

## 1. Target and generated instance

The target interface is an isometry $V:\mathcal L\to\mathcal K$, a common positive contraction $0\le B\le I$ diagonal in the physical computational basis, and a positive contraction $0\le C\le I$, with

\[
 \ker C=\operatorname{ran}V,\qquad
 C\ge\gamma(I-VV^*),\qquad
 \alpha H\le V^*BV\le\beta H,
\]

where all three constants are independent of input length and BMVZ repetition. The inequalities hold on all logical and physical vectors; there is no promise on an auxiliary input state. Row support and row-list access for $C$ are separate requirements from circuit and entry access.

Use an explicit generated Hamiltonian
\[
 H=\sum_{j=1}^{M} w_j h_j,\qquad w_j>0,\qquad \sum_jw_j=1,
\]
where each $h_j$ is a projection. A BMVZ amplified clause has the form
\[
 h_j=I-Q_j,\qquad Q_j=\bigotimes_{r=1}^{R}(I-P_{j,r}),
\]
where each $P_{j,r}$ is a fixed-locality input projection on its own amplified data copy. Thus the clause is the OR of its $R$ local violations. BMVZ Definitions 1.6–1.9 specify the weighted commuting-layer input, expander-path clause list, and OR projector; the small-layer rule repeats a singleton clause. One can append two separate singleton layers carrying the BJSW five-qubit rank-one seeds
\[
 |\phi\rangle=(|00010\rangle-|10010\rangle)/\sqrt2,\qquad
 |\psi\rangle=(|00010\rangle-|01110\rangle-|10110\rangle-|11010\rangle)/2
\]
on a fresh register, each with weight $1/4$, and scale the original hard input by $1/2$. The seed projectors have a common kernel of dimension at least 30, so this augmentation preserves the QMA-hard energy promise up to a constant factor. After $R$ repetitions the generated seed clauses are
\[
 h_{v,R}=I-(I-|v\rangle\langle v|)^{\otimes R},\qquad v\in\{\phi,\psi\},
\]
each still of weight $1/4$. This is an allowed augmented input, not a claim that both seeds occur in every unmodified BJSW table. The seed kets are from BJSW's 5-LCH propagation decomposition, equations (4)–(7).

## 2. Direct term-frame Naimark dilation: exact $H$, but no sparse constant-gap enforcer

For each clause choose a unitary $U_j$ and a diagonal projection $D_j$ with $h_j=U_j^*D_jU_j$. Define
\[
 V_{\rm term}|z\rangle=\sum_j\sqrt{w_j}|j\rangle U_j|z\rangle,\qquad
 B_{\rm term}=\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes D_j.
\]
The branch labels are orthogonal, so $V_{\rm term}^*V_{\rm term}=I$, and
\[
 V_{\rm term}^*B_{\rm term}V_{\rm term}=\sum_jw_jU_j^*D_jU_j=H.
\]
In general, if $v_x=V^*|x\rangle$ for a physical basis row $x$, then $F_x=|v_x\rangle\langle v_x|$ obeys $\sum_xF_x=I$, and every diagonal $B=\sum_xb_x|x\rangle\langle x|$ compresses as $V^*BV=\sum_xb_xF_x$. A common diagonal physical test can therefore compress noncommuting logical terms; compression does not preserve commutativity in general.

The exact image projector and its canonical penalty have blocks
\[
 (P_{\rm term})_{j,k}=\sqrt{w_jw_k}\,U_jU_k^*,
\]
\[
 (C_{\rm term})_{(j,y),(k,z)}
 =\delta_{jk}\delta_{yz}-\sqrt{w_jw_k}(U_jU_k^*)_{yz},
 \qquad C_{\rm term}=I-P_{\rm term}.
\]
The kernel is exactly the encoded image and the gap is one. A row $(j,y)$ contains the union of relative-unitary row supports $(U_jU_k^*)_{y,*}$ over $k$, with possible cancellations but no general polynomial bound. For factorwise local decoders, one specified entry can be computed by multiplying $R$ constant-size algebraic entries; that is polynomial-time entry access. Listing a row can still be exponential.

For the forced BJSW seed pair, exact Clifford multiplication gives a one-copy relative matrix $U_\phi U_\psi^*$ with exactly 8 nonzeros per row, each of magnitude $1/\sqrt8$. Its $R$-copy relative matrix has $8^R$ nonzeros per row, each of magnitude $8^{-R/2}$. In the full term-frame projector, fix a row in the $\phi$ branch and a basis word on the untouched hard registers; restrict columns to the $\psi$ branch with the same hard-register word. Then
\[
 \sum_{z\in J}|(P_{\rm term})_{iz}|^2=m=1/16,\qquad
 |(P_{\rm term})_{iz}|^2\le a=(1/16)8^{-R}.
\]
Other clause labels cannot cancel these entries because they occupy different blocks.

The row-mass lemma used here is as follows. Let $\gamma(C)$ be the least eigenvalue of $C$ on $\operatorname{ran}(P)^\perp$. If $P$ is a projection, $0\le C\le I$, $\ker C=\operatorname{ran}P$, and every row of $C$ has at most $s$ nonzeros, suppose some row of $P$ has squared mass at least $m$ on columns $J$, with each selected entry squared at most $a$. Put
\[
 D=\frac{\ln(m/(2a))}{\ln(s+1)}.
\]
When $D>1$, the Chebyshev-filter argument gives
\[
 \gamma(C)\le\left[\frac{\ln(8/m)}{4(D-1)}\right]^2.
\]
For completeness, use
\[
 q_\ell(x)=
 \frac{T_\ell((1+\gamma-2x)/(1-\gamma))}
 {T_\ell((1+\gamma)/(1-\gamma))}.
\]
It has $q_\ell(0)=1$ and $\|q_\ell(C)-P\|\le2e^{-2\ell\sqrt{\gamma}}$, since the affine argument maps $[\gamma,1]$ to $[1,-1]$. A degree-$\ell$ polynomial in an $s$-row matrix has row support at most $(s+1)^\ell$. Take $\ell=\lfloor D\rfloor$, so $\ell\ge D-1$ and $(s+1)^\ell a\le m/2$. At least $m/2$ of the selected $P$-row mass lies outside the support of the corresponding row of $q_\ell(C)$. Hence $\sqrt{m/2}\le2e^{-2\ell\sqrt{\gamma}}$, which rearranges to the displayed bound.

Substitution for the exact Clifford seed witness gives
\[
 \gamma(C)\le
 \left[\frac{\ln128\,\ln(s+1)}
 {4(R\ln8-\ln2-\ln(s+1))}\right]^2
 \quad\text{when }R\ln8-\ln2>\ln(s+1).
\]
Thus any polynomial-row positive enforcer for this exact image has vanishing gap when $R$ grows polynomially and $s$ is polynomial. This excludes every $C$ for the direct term-frame image, not just $I-P$, a graph Laplacian, or a chosen positive-term decomposition. For arbitrary factorwise diagonalizers of the two rank-one seeds, the basis-independent per-copy bound $\max_{y,z}|(U_\phi U_\psi^*)_{yz}|^2\le7/8$ gives the same conclusion with $R\ln(8/7)$ replacing $R\ln8$. The exact 8-entry figure is a finite algebraic check of the specified Clifford frames; the rank-one bound does not depend on that tableau choice.

Resource ledger: $V_{\rm term}$ uses the branch-amplitude state and controlled clause decoders, with polynomial gate count when the term table and local decoders are explicit. Rational weights yield named algebraic square-root amplitudes and exact rotations in that algebraic gate model; synthesis over a fixed finite gate set is approximate and needs an explicit error budget. $B_{\rm term}$ has one nonzero per physical row, but computing $D_j(y)$ scans the $R$ local predicates. Its Boolean OR may depend on all $R$ blocks, so one-row-sparsity is not constant qubit locality. $C_{\rm term}$ has a unit gap and polynomial-time entry formulas, but the theorem forces superpolynomial row support on the augmented seed family.

## 3. Active-flag Naimark dilation: the same gap obstruction survives

A different exact dilation makes the outcome flag explicit. Let $F_j(y)$ be the diagonal OR predicate for $D_j$, and let $J_j|y\rangle=|y\rangle|F_j(y)\rangle$. Set
\[
 W_j=J_jU_j,\qquad
 V_{\rm flag}|z\rangle=\sum_j\sqrt{w_j}|j\rangle W_j|z\rangle,\qquad
 B_{\rm flag}=I_{\rm branch,data}\otimes|1\rangle\langle1|.
\]
Then $W_j^*B_{\rm flag}W_j=h_j$, so $V_{\rm flag}^*B_{\rm flag}V_{\rm flag}=H$. For valid output rows and columns, the projector block is
\[
 (P_{\rm flag})_{j,k}=\sqrt{w_jw_k}\,J_jU_jU_k^*J_k^*.
\]
The $J_k^*$ removes the valid input flag, and $J_j$ writes the target branch's flag; flags on the two branches need not agree. Hence the same $\phi$-row/$\psi$-valid-column witness has $m=1/16$ and $a=(1/16)8^{-R}$. The Chebyshev bound rules out a polynomial-row constant-gap enforcer for this exact active-flag image as well. This is a proved no-go for these specified images, not for arbitrary active workspace or a redesigned isometry.

The circuit costs are polynomial in $M,R,n$: prepare the branch amplitudes, apply controlled local diagonalizers, reversibly compute the $R$-bit OR, and write the flag. $B_{\rm flag}$ is one-bit local and row-one-sparse. The $C$ row obstruction remains even though the flag itself is cheap.

## 4. Square-root Naimark dilation: a unit-gap image with an uncharged root

For any positive contraction $H$, the canonical square-root isometry is
\[
 V_{\sqrt H}|z\rangle=
 \begin{pmatrix}H^{1/2}z\\(I-H)^{1/2}z\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad B_{\sqrt H}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It satisfies $V^*V=I$ and $V^*BV=H$. The image projector and its exact unit-gap complement are
\[
 P_{\sqrt H}=VV^*=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 H&\sqrt{H(I-H)}\\
 \sqrt{H(I-H)}&I-H
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 C_{\sqrt H}=I-P_{\sqrt H}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 I-H&-\sqrt{H(I-H)}\\
 -\sqrt{H(I-H)}&H
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The square roots commute because both are functions of $H$; $P$ is a projection, so $\ker C=\operatorname{ran}V$ and the gap is one.

This formula does not preserve sparse rows automatically. Even if $H$ had $s_H$-sparse rows, the off-diagonal block is the matrix function $f(H)=\sqrt{H(I-H)}$; a row of $C$ needs the supports of both a row of $H$ and a row of $f(H)$. For BMVZ, individual OR clauses act on $R$ disjoint local factors, so their physical rows may already have $2^{\Theta(R)}$ entries. More generally, efficient computation of $H^{1/2}$, $\sqrt{I-H}$, and their row lists does not follow from a polynomial term list or a polynomial circuit for block-encoding $H$. No lower bound for this canonical square-root image on the full BMVZ family is claimed here.

There is a useful conditional positive boundary. If an exact efficient spectral representation is supplied,
\[
 H=W^*\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_z)W,\qquad 0\le\lambda_z\le1,
\]
then encode each eigenlabel with a one-qubit state
\[
 |\eta_z\rangle=\sqrt{1-\lambda_z}|0\rangle+\sqrt{\lambda_z}|1\rangle,\qquad
 V_{\rm spec}|\psi\rangle=\sum_z|z\rangle\langle z|W|\psi\rangle|\eta_z\rangle.
\]
With $B=I\otimes|1\rangle\langle1|$, $V_{\rm spec}^*BV_{\rm spec}=H$. Its projector and complement are block diagonal in $z$:
\[
 P_{\rm spec}=\sum_z|z\rangle\langle z|\otimes|\eta_z\rangle\langle\eta_z|,
\]
\[
 C_{\rm spec}=\sum_z|z\rangle\langle z|\otimes|\eta_z^\perp\rangle\langle\eta_z^\perp|,
 \qquad |\eta_z^\perp\rangle=\sqrt{\lambda_z}|0\rangle-\sqrt{1-\lambda_z}|1\rangle.
\]
This $C$ has at most two entries per row and gap one. Polynomial gate and row access require polynomial exact descriptions of $W$, the eigenvalue lookup, and the controlled algebraic rotations. BMVZ supplies local projectors and their path table, not such a diagonalizer or eigenvalue oracle. This conditional construction prevents the scoped no-go above from being misread as a theorem about every possible image of the same abstract positive operator. It also illustrates that row sparsity alone does not imply constant qubit locality: the diagonal eigenvalue control can depend on the whole label.

## 5. Complement-LCU failure channel: exact constant-factor compression, unresolved rows

Let $H=\sum_jw_jh_j$ be the full normalized projection sum. Define reflections $R_j=I-2h_j$ and the weighted LCU target
\[
 Y=I-H=\tfrac12 I+\tfrac12\sum_jw_jR_j.
\]
Prepare a label register in
\[
 |\chi\rangle=2^{-1/2}|id\rangle+\sum_j\sqrt{w_j/2}|j\rangle,
\]
let
\[
 SELECT=|id\rangle\langle id|\otimes I+\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes R_j,
\]
and choose a unitary $A$ with $A|0\rangle=|\chi\rangle$. One exact explicit choice is the Householder reflection
\[
 A=I-2|u\rangle\langle u|,\qquad
 |u\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle-|\chi\rangle}{\sqrt{2(1-\langle0|\chi\rangle)}}.
\]
Its entries are directly computable from the branch weights, and a polynomial-size exact state-preparation circuit for $u$ implements the reflection. The unitary
\[
 U=(A^*\otimes I)SELECT(A\otimes I)
\]
has exact block $\langle0|U|0\rangle=Y$. Define
\[
 V_{\rm LCU}|\psi\rangle=U(|0\rangle|\psi\rangle),\qquad
 B_{\rm LCU}=(I-|0\rangle\langle0|)_{\rm label}\otimes I.
\]
Since $Y=Y^*$,
\[
 V_{\rm LCU}^*B_{\rm LCU}V_{\rm LCU}
 =I-Y^2=2H-H^2,\qquad H\le2H-H^2\le2H.
\]
This is an exact all-state comparison with $\alpha=1,\beta=2$. The ordinary success-flag block-encoding instead gives selected probability $H^2$ and no size-independent lower ratio near small eigenvalues. The canonical enforcer
\[
 C_{\rm LCU}=U B_{\rm LCU}U^*=I-V_{\rm LCU}V_{\rm LCU}^*
\]
is a projection with exact kernel and gap one.

The image projector can be written row by row without hiding the LCU mixing. Let $A_{\ell a}=\langle\ell|A|a\rangle$, $\chi_{id}=1/\sqrt2$, $\chi_j=\sqrt{w_j/2}$, and $R_{id}=I$. The data operator in output label row $a$ is
\[
 K_a=\langle a|U|0\rangle=\sum_{\ell}\overline{A_{\ell a}}\,\chi_\ell R_\ell.
\]
Consequently
\[
 (P_{\rm LCU})_{a,b}=K_aK_b^*,\qquad
 (C_{\rm LCU})_{(a,y),(b,z)}=\delta_{ab}\delta_{yz}-\langle y|K_aK_b^*|z\rangle,
\]
where
\[
 \langle y|K_aK_b^*|z\rangle
 =\sum_{\ell,m}\overline{A_{\ell a}}\chi_\ell A_{mb}\overline{\chi_m}
   \langle y|R_\ell R_m|z\rangle.
\]
For BMVZ $h_j=I-Q_j$, so $R_j=2Q_j-I$ and
\[
 R_jR_k=4Q_jQ_k-2Q_j-2Q_k+I.
\]
If each input projection is $k$-local and clauses are flattened over $R$ copies, each $Q_jQ_k$ factor acts on the union of at most two $k$-local supports per copy. A conservative row-support upper bound is therefore
\[
 \max_i\operatorname{rowsupp}_i(C_{\rm LCU})
 \le \min\{(L+1)2^N,\ O(L^3 2^{2kR})\},
\]
where $L$ is the padded number of nonzero LCU terms and $N$ is the data-qubit count. For the 5-local family this is $O(L^3 2^{10R})$. This is only an upper bound: cancellations may reduce rows, and this analysis gives no superpolynomial lower bound for this different image. It gives no polynomial row-list algorithm either. An individual entry is computable from $O(L^2R)$ constant-size local algebraic products and the preparation amplitudes, so polynomial-time entry access does not certify polynomial-time row access.

Resource ledger: $B_{\rm LCU}$ touches $\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil$ label qubits, has one nonzero per row, and has trivial entry access. $V_{\rm LCU}$ has polynomial circuit size given the explicit term table: branch preparation uses polynomially many exact algebraic rotations, and SELECT applies the controlled reflection for one generated clause. The Householder choice above also makes every queried $A_{\ell a}$ entry explicit, so the entry formula does not rely on generic circuit-amplitude estimation. For an OR clause, compute the local diagonal predicates, reversibly AND the $R$ no-violation bits, phase the result, uncompute, and undo the local diagonalizers. This uses $O(R)$ fixed-local gates per selected term plus polynomial reversible label lookup. These are ideal exact algebraic circuits; compilation to a fixed finite gate set is approximate and must be charged against exact compression and any small spectral floor. $C_{\rm LCU}$ is globally supported in general; its circuit and entry descriptions do not yet meet a sparse-row interface.

## 6. A separate barrier for scalar-polynomial spectral channels

The preceding LCU image is not a scalar polynomial in $H$. On the fine-spectrum augmentation from the prior exact audit, let
\[
 H_{\rm fine}=\tfrac12H_B\otimes I+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\delta_kP_k,\qquad
 \delta_k=\frac{2^{-k}}{4(1-2^{-r})},
\]
where the $P_k$ are rank-one projectors on fresh five-qubit registers. A BJSW unary-completeness choice $p(n)=n^3$ gives yes minimum $\epsilon_n\le2^{-n^3}\operatorname{poly}(n)$. The two exact eigenvalues used below are $\lambda_0=\epsilon_n/2$ and $\lambda_1=\lambda_0+\Delta$, with $\Delta=1/[4(2^r-1)]$; they need not be consecutive. They come from fixing a ground eigenvector of $H_B$ and changing only the dummy-register string from all-zero to its least-weight excitation. Thus $\epsilon_n/\Delta\to0$, while the augmented instance remains QMA-hard. The displayed nonzero-term weights sum to $3/4$; add a zero projector of weight $1/4$ if a normalized convex-combination presentation is needed.

Consider this explicit dilation subclass:
\[
 V|\psi\rangle=\sum_{a=1}^{A}|a\rangle q_a(H_{\rm fine})|\psi\rangle,\qquad
 \sum_a|q_a(x)|^2=1\quad(0\le x\le1),
\]
where each scalar channel $q_a$ has polynomial degree at most $D$, and let $B=\sum_ab_a|a\rangle\langle a|\otimes I$, $0\le b_a\le1$. Then $V^*BV=F(H_{\rm fine})$ with $F(x)=\sum_ab_a|q_a(x)|^2$. If $\alpha H_{\rm fine}\le F(H_{\rm fine})\le\beta H_{\rm fine}$, put $\tilde q_a=\sqrt{b_a}q_a$. For sufficiently large $n$,
\[
 \|\tilde q(\lambda_1)-\tilde q(\lambda_0)\|
 \ge\sqrt{\alpha\lambda_1}-\sqrt{\beta\lambda_0}
 \ge\tfrac12\sqrt{\alpha\Delta}.
\]
A degree-$D$ vector polynomial of norm at most one on $[0,1]$ is $2D^2$-Lipschitz there by Markov's inequality. Hence
\[
 D\ge\frac{\alpha^{1/4}}{2\Delta^{1/4}}=\Omega(2^{r/4}).
\]
No polynomial-degree scalar-channel dilation in this class gives the constant-factor comparison on this family, before an enforcer is considered. Complement LCU evades this barrier because its output channels are sums of distinct, generally noncommuting clause reflections $R_j$, not scalar polynomials $q_a(H)$. This is not an impossibility theorem for general Naimark instruments, active workspaces, or arbitrary isometries.

## 7. Outcome and exact falsifiers

The direct term-frame and active-flag isometries each have an exact common diagonal test for the complete noncommuting sum, but on the stated hardness-preserving seed augmentation every exact positive enforcer with polynomial row support has vanishing gap. The canonical square-root dilation has a unit gap but no row bound; its matrix root can be dense and is not supplied by a term-list representation. Complement LCU gives a polynomial-size exact ideal isometry, a one-row-sparse diagonal $B$, the full-space sandwich $H\le V^*BV\le2H$, and an exact unit-gap $C=I-VV^*$. Its only missing target property in this ledger is a polynomial-row support bound with a row-list algorithm for $C$. The scalar-polynomial functional-calculus class is independently blocked on the fine-spectrum family.

A positive completion of the complement-LCU route must supply a polynomial bound on each row of the displayed $K_aK_b^*$ blocks and an algorithm that lists exactly those entries in polynomial time, including cancellations and coefficient representation. An exact row-support lower bound for this complement-LCU image would instead settle this implementation negatively; the direct-frame row-mass theorem cannot be transferred to it without a new selected-row witness. The conditional spectral encoding is a positive counterexample to an unrestricted matrix-level no-go once an exact diagonalizer and eigenvalue oracle are supplied for an actual family member.
