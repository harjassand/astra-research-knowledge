# Endpoint-only state observation: exact identifiability and intervention-choice audit

**Progress label:** reconstructed conditional theorem and finite numerical design check. This is a theorem about a two-state model, not a biological claim or a novelty/priority claim.

## Question and interface

Can a terminal cell-state fraction at one fixed time identify the cell-state transition timescale needed to optimize a time-integrated target-state objective? The observation interface is the state fraction at (T) under each fixed treatment arm. The objective is expected time spent in target state (B) over ([0,T]). There is no lineage readout, intermediate observation, birth/death, cell heterogeneity, dose uncertainty, or measurement error in the exact theorem.

## Conditional theorem

Let one cell follow a continuous-time Markov chain (A\rightleftarrows B), with constant rates (a>0) from (A) to (B) and (b\geq 0) from (B) to (A), starting in (A). Write λ = a+b. Then

\[
p_B(t)=\frac{a}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t}),\qquad
J(T):=\int_0^T p_B(t)\,dt
=\frac{a}{\lambda}\left(T-\frac{1-e^{-\lambda T}}{\lambda}\right).
\]

For any endpoint value (q=p_B(T)\in(0,1)), every rate pair

\[
\lambda\geq -T^{-1}\log(1-q),\qquad
a_\lambda=\frac{q\lambda}{1-e^{-\lambda T}},\qquad
b_\lambda=\lambda-a_\lambda
\]
has the same terminal state fraction (q). Thus a single exact endpoint fraction does not identify the rates or (J(T)). Along this endpoint-equivalence curve,

\[
J_\lambda(T)=q\left(\frac{T}{1-e^{-\lambda T}}-\frac1\lambda\right),
\]
which varies with λ (strictly increases for λ>0, since its derivative is (q[1/\lambda^2-T^2e^{-\lambda T}/(1-e^{-\lambda T})^2]>0); positivity follows from (2\sinh(\lambda T/2)>\lambda T)).

**Proof.** The forward equation is (p'_B=a(1-p_B)-bp_B=a-\lambda p_B), with (p_B(0)=0). Solving gives the display. Solving (p_B(T)=q) for (a) gives the endpoint-equivalence curve; (b\geq0) is equivalent to (q\leq1-e^{-\lambda T}). Integrating (p_B(t)) gives (J), and substitution of (a_\lambda) gives the final expression. The positive derivative proves that the target-occupancy objective is not constant on the endpoint-equivalence curve.

An intermediate read at (T/2) resolves the remaining scalar ambiguity in this model because

\[
\frac{p_B(T/2)}{p_B(T)}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda T/2}},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{2}{T}\log\left(\frac{p_B(T/2)}{p_B(T)-p_B(T/2)}\right).
\]
Then (a=q\lambda/(1-e^{-\lambda T})) and (b=\lambda-a). This inversion becomes ill-conditioned as the midpoint ratio approaches 0 or 1; it assumes constant rates and error-free state classification.

## Decision consequence

Suppose two treatment arms (U,V) are compared by integrated occupancy (J). If two model worlds induce exactly the same endpoint observation distribution under both arms, but (U) is better than (V) by (g_1>0) in world 1 and (V) is better than (U) by (g_2>0) in world 2, any endpoint-only policy has the same probability (x) of choosing (U) in both worlds. Its worst-case expected regret is at least

\[
\min_{x\in[0,1]}\max\{(1-x)g_1,xg_2\}=\frac{g_1g_2}{g_1+g_2}>0.
\]

The equality follows by balancing the two linear regret terms at (x=g_1/(g_1+g_2)). This is a two-world decision-theoretic consequence, not a lower bound for richer readouts or models.

## Exact numerical witness

Set (T=1). Treatment (U) has two endpoint-indistinguishable rate pairs:

| World / arm | (a) | (b) | (p_B(1)) | (J(1)) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| World 1, (U) | 1.000000 | 1.000000 | 0.432332 | 0.283834 |
| World 2, (U) | 0.683940 | 0.316060 | 0.432332 | 0.251607 |

In both worlds the fixed competitor (V) has ((a,b)=(0.971475,1.028525)), (p_B(1)=0.42), and (J(1)=0.275737). Thus the terminal fraction ranks (U) above (V) in both worlds, but the integrated-occupancy objective ranks (U>V) in world 1 and (V>U) in world 2. The randomized endpoint-only minimax regret for this witness is (0.0060623).

The most separating single intermediate time for these two (U) worlds is (t^*=0.3798855T). There, the predicted fractions are (0.2661132) and (0.2161662), respectively, a difference of (0.0499471). Under the additional assumption that one observes one independent founder lineage per Bernoulli trial and classifies its state perfectly, thresholding the midpoint proportion at the two predictions' midpoint has one-sided error at most α if

\[
n\geq \left\lceil\frac{2\log(1/\alpha)}{\Delta^2}\right\rceil.
\]

For α=0.05 and Δ=0.0499471, this is 2,402 independent founder units. Raw cells in one well or clone do not count as independent founder units; common exposure, shared ancestry, state-assignment error, and between-well variation must be charged or modeled.

The midpoint formula above is model-based. The script `endpoint_aliasing.py` computes the witness and chooses the maximum-separation time over (0<t<T); its output is saved in `endpoint_aliasing_results.json`.

## Discriminating experiment and comparator

For a nominated target cell type and two fixed interventions, sort or otherwise define the initial (A) population, randomize independent founder units to arms, and sample destructive matched aliquots at (t^*\approx0.38T) and (T). Estimate target-state fractions with a prespecified classifier and independently record viable absolute counts and division/death. Compare (i) the equal-information terminal-only policy with (ii) terminal plus intermediate sampling. The exact-model prediction is that the midpoint read distinguishes the two rate worlds while terminal fractions alone do not; fitting the rates then predicts the integrated occupancy. A held-out time point or an independently measured occupancy reporter can falsify the constant-rate model.

**Falsifier:** pre-register the two-rate model and the assay; if midpoint fractions, held-out fractions, or integrated occupancy fall outside uncertainty bands generated from the fitted rates, reject this model for that condition. Heterogeneous rates, intervention-dependent state classification, differential division/death, cell-cell interactions, and non-Markov memory are plausible failure causes and require explicit richer models. The result does not say that the procedure will work in tissues or in vivo.

## Prior-art boundary from primary sources

- Hormoz et al. (Cell Systems 2016), *Inferring Cell-State Transition Dynamics from Lineage Trees and Endpoint Single-Cell Measurements*, combines live-cell pedigrees with endpoint smFISH. It infers rates from kin-state correlations, notes that transitions faster than the cell cycle leave no pedigree correlation signature, and extends to time-varying rates using correlations at all lineage distances. This is direct prior art on pairing endpoint state assays with lineage information; it is not an endpoint-only theorem. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015>.
- Gupta et al. (Bioinformatics 2020), *Transcompp*, fits Markov switching and optional unknown phenotype-specific proliferation to fraction time series across flexible initial compositions; its interface is repeated time points and replicates. It is direct prior art for rate fitting from cell fractions. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btz991>.
- Gunnarsson, Foo & Leder (2023), *Statistical inference of the rates of cell proliferation and phenotypic switching in cancer*, provides likelihood-based inference for stochastic division, death, and switching using state fractions or absolute cell numbers at one or more time points and discusses parameter identifiability. This is especially close prior art; any novelty claim would require a detailed comparison beyond the present scope. DOI/arXiv: <https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.06.13.544777>; <https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08096>.
- Zañudo & Albert (2015), *Cell Fate Reprogramming by Control of Intracellular Network Dynamics*, uses asynchronous logical networks/stable motifs and predicts transient node perturbations to reach a target attractor. This is a different control interface from a dose-calibrated stochastic reach–avoid policy. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004193>.
- Primary reprogramming studies provide motivation for timing and tissue-specific validation but do not validate the two-state model: Ocampo et al. (Cell 2016), DOI <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.11.052>; Abad et al. (Nature 2013), DOI <https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12586>; adult-cardiomyocyte OSKM follow-up, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111333>; Xu et al. (Nature Aging 2024), DOI <https://doi.org/10.1038/s43587-024-00594-3>; Sahu et al. (Sci Transl Med 2024), DOI <https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adg1777>. The reported induction schedules, outcomes, cell populations, and delivery systems differ; nominal pulse lengths are not transferable evidence for a common kinetic model.

## Evidence status and next gate

The forward equations, endpoint non-identifiability family, and minimax regret witness are proved for the stated model. Numeric values are a finite calculation only. The next decisive gate is a primary-data reanalysis or controlled pilot that supplies independently randomized founder units, intermediate and terminal state fractions, and absolute viable counts, then tests out-of-sample schedule predictions against a matched terminal-only design. No priority claim is made; standard system identification and the cited trajectory-fitting methods make broad novelty unlikely.
