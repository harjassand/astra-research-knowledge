# Primary-source record

Retrieval date: 2026-10-08. This is a bounded primary-source check, not a complete priority search.

1. Agazzi, Dembo, and Eckmann, “Large deviations theory for Markov jump models of chemical reaction networks,” Annals of Applied Probability 28 (2018), [arXiv:1701.02126](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126). Source comparator for mass-action chemical jump processes and large-volume exit-time asymptotics. It does not by itself supply the finite-V directed-graph predictable-control formula proved in PROOF.md.

2. Anand, Jagtap, and Zamani, “Verification of Switched Stochastic Systems via Barrier Certificates,” [arXiv:2109.12420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12420), published at CDC 2019 (arXiv v1 submitted 2021). The primary full text was checked. Theorem III.4 explicitly allows bounded Lipschitz state-dependent CTMC mode-transition intensities \(\lambda_{mm'}(x)\), coupled to mode-specific multiple barriers through the jump-generator term \(\sum_{m'}\lambda_{mm'}(x)B_{m'}(x)\). Its continuous state follows a diffusion in each mode. This is closer switching prior art than a fixed-rate-only comparison; the interface difference retained here is finite-volume chemical jump state plus guard-triggered switching and explicit volume-dependent constants. Exact subsumption was not established.

3. Zaker, Akbarzadeh, Samari, and Lavaei, “Compositional Design of Safety Controllers for Large-Scale Stochastic Hybrid Systems,” Automatica 190 (2026), article 113082, [DOI:10.1016/j.automatica.2026.113082](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2026.113082), primary preprint [arXiv:2409.10018v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10018). The 2026 paper develops compositional control sub-barrier certificates for interconnected stochastic hybrid systems with Brownian/Poisson flows and discrete reset dynamics, under small-gain conditions. It is the current broad controller-synthesis comparator. Exact embedding of the state-dependent molecular propensity interface into its Poisson/reset representation was not verified; it may subsume some or all of the use case.


4. Wisniewski, Bujorianu, and Sloth, “p-Safe Analysis of Stochastic Hybrid Processes,” IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 65(12) (2020), 5220–5235, [DOI:10.1109/TAC.2020.2972789](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2020.2972789), [author manuscript](https://findresearcher.sdu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/178985434/Wisniewski_etal_IEEE_TAC_2020_p_Safe_analysis_of_stochastic_hybrid_processes.pdf). Its abstract and Section VII explicitly treat PDMPs with spontaneous Poisson-style and guard-forced jumps; the paper obtains p-safety using generator/evolution equations and occupation-measure optimization. A fixed finite-volume CTMC is within the broad PDMP process class (zero deterministic flow and spontaneous state-dependent jumps). However, the main theorem is for a fixed strong-Markov process; controller-selected predictable rate envelopes and arbitrary stopping-time switch policies are not directly its stated theorem interface. This predates our guarded-mode example and is a strong hybrid-safety comparator; it does not supply the explicit finite-volume exponential constants proved here.

5. Hutzenthaler et al., “Stochastic reaction-diffusion equations on networks,” [arXiv:2011.12780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12780). Its network reaction-diffusion model is a stochastic PDE driven by Gaussian noise. It establishes a distinct reaction-diffusion representation, not the finite-volume molecular transfer process here.

6. Santoyo, Dutreix, and Coogan, “A barrier function approach to finite-time stochastic system verification and control,” Automatica 125 (2021), article 109439, [DOI:10.1016/j.automatica.2020.109439](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2020.109439), preprint [arXiv:1905.12077](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12077). It gives finite-horizon stochastic barrier synthesis and control for affine-in-control stochastic systems. This supports the generic generator/barrier method as established prior art.

7. Parent repository evidence: N35 source-derived proof work/agents/systems_metabolism/chemostat_control/astra/jump_safety.txt proves the finite-volume finite-horizon exponential bound for a supplied common strict-inward polytope and bounded predictable reaction rates. Its source card work/agents/systems_metabolism/chemostat_control/astra/N35-controlled-chemical-safety.txt reports a reciprocal graph extension and lists transport asymmetries as an open boundary. Those are local source-corpus claims, not external verification.

## Novelty status

The new candidate's exact step is the box-face drift bound for directed first-order transport with heterogeneous site volumes, inserted into the N35 finite-volume factorial-correction and exponential-potential argument, then restarted at guarded switches. Existing state-dependent stochastic mode-switching/multiple-barrier, compositional stochastic-hybrid, and PDMP p-safety literature is close; PDMP p-safety explicitly includes forced guard jumps. A fixed CTMC lies inside the process class, and controller state may sometimes be included in an augmented Markov model. The targeted search did not establish whether the explicit chemical-jump/finite-volume directed-face constants are already covered or improve the strongest application-matched reachability solver. Priority and novelty therefore remain UNKNOWN.
