# Hostile audit: anisotropic Clifford-vector dual

## Verdict

I found no counterexample to the vector-sector inequality. The proof survives
the potentially dangerous case: a restricted odd subset of an odd-dimensional
Clifford frame can have both signs of the central product of its $B_i$'s.
The bound holds in both signs. In particular, one must not infer the restricted
subset sign from the chirality of the full $m$-generator product; the
representation argument below handles the two signs separately.

There is one support-attainment correction worth making explicit. A full
\(\operatorname{Spin}(m)\) orbit of one Clifford eigenstate has anisotropic score
\(\operatorname{Tr}(Q)/m\), not generally
\(\lambda_{\max}(Q)\). The canonical-EB support value is nevertheless
\(\lambda_{\max}(Q)\): it is attained by measuring in an eigenbasis of the
largest-weight Clifford generator. The anisotropic candidate report uses this
projective measurement, so this is a clarification rather than a failure of
its stated bound.

The theorem is for a real positive-semidefinite matrix on the real Clifford
vector space. This makes $Q$ real symmetric and the star Hermitian. A general
complex-Hermitian matrix on the complexified span is outside this argument and
cannot simply be diagonalized by a Spin rotation.

## Ordinary-tensor algebra, including the odd restricted case

For any selected subset $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ with $k=|I|$, set

\[
A_i=\Gamma_i^T\otimes\Gamma_i\otimes I,
\qquad
B_i=\Gamma_i^T\otimes I\otimes\Gamma_i.
\]

The transpose of a Hermitian Clifford generator is again a Hermitian
involution and the transposed generators still anticommute. Directly in the
ordinary three-fold tensor product,

\[
A_i^2=B_i^2=I,\quad [A_i,A_j]=[B_i,B_j]=0,
\quad A_iB_j=(-1)^{[i\ne j]}B_jA_i.
\tag{A}
\]

There is no graded-tensor sign hidden here. For $i\ne j$, $A_i,A_j$
anticommute once in the reference factor and once in the first output factor,
so commute overall; the same two-sign cancellation holds for $B_i,B_j$.
For $A_i,B_j$, only the reference factor anticommutes when $i\ne j$.

The common eigenspaces of the $A_i$'s are labelled by
\(x\in\{\pm1\}^k\). Conjugation by $B_j$ preserves $x_j$ and flips all
other coordinates. In binary notation these flips are the columns of
\(I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\).

* If $k$ is even this matrix is invertible over \(\mathbb F_2\). The shifts
  act freely and transitively on all $2^k$ labels. Choose one joint
  eigenspace as a multiplicity space and transport it with products of the
  commuting $B_j$'s. This gives the canonical bit model, with arbitrary
  constant signs removable by products of $Z$'s.
* If $k$ is odd, the shift group has rank $k-1$, its only label relation
  is the all-coordinate product, and there are two orbits, distinguished by
  \(D=\prod_i A_i=\pm I\) on an orbit. The operator
  \(C=\prod_i B_i\) commutes with every $A_j,B_j$, so split into its
  central eigenspaces $C=\eta I$, \(\eta\in\{+1,-1\}\). In that sector
  \(B'_i=\eta B_i\) obey \(\prod_iB'_i=I\). Choose a base $A$-eigenspace
  in either nonempty orbit and transport it by the $B'_j$'s. The commuting
  involutions make the transport path-independent after using the sole
  product relation. This identifies the representation with a direct sum
  of copies of one of the two parity blocks of
  \(A_i=Z_i,\ B'_i=P X_i\), where \(P=\prod_jX_j\). The other parity block
  need not occur in the physical representation.

  In the physical three-spinor representation, when this is a proper subset
  ($k<m$), both central signs really do occur. Choose \(\ell\notin I\).
  The ambient \(A_\ell\) anticommutes with \(C=\prod_{i\in I}B_i\) and
  commutes with \(D=\prod_{i\in I}A_i\); the ambient \(B_\ell\) commutes
  with \(C\) and anticommutes with \(D\). They are involutory unitaries, so
  they flip the two signs independently and all four \((C,D)\) sectors have
  equal rank. For the full odd set $k=m$, the Clifford volume element can
  force a single chirality sector; that special fact is not used for proper
  subsets.

Equivalently, allowing signs before gauge fixing, the bit model is
\(B_i=\eta_i\prod_{j\ne i}X_j\). Conjugating by \(\prod_jZ_j^{s_j}\)
changes the sign pattern by
\(t_i=(-1)^{S-s_i}\), with \(S=\sum_j s_j\pmod2\). For even $k$, every
pattern is reachable. For odd $k$, exactly the even-parity sign changes
are reachable, preserving $\prod_i\eta_i=\eta$; it can be made the uniform
pattern \(\eta_i=\eta\). Thus both central signs are included. This argument
does not assert that a subset product inherits the full-frame chirality.

The exceptional $k=1$ case need not use this representation: directly
\(\|A_1+B_1\|\le2=k+1\), even though the physical $B_1$ need not equal
the canonical one-qubit $PX_1=I$.

## Sum-of-squares and weighted reduction

In the canonical model, for either twist \(\varepsilon=\pm1\),

\[
H_{k,\varepsilon}=\sum_i Z_i+\varepsilon\sum_i P X_i
=2(J_z+\varepsilon P J_x),
\qquad J_\alpha=\tfrac12\sum_i\sigma_i^{(\alpha)}.
\]

Here $P$ commutes with $J_x$, anticommutes with $J_y,J_z$, and is a
Hermitian involution. Keeping these anticommutation signs gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{k,\varepsilon}^2/4
&=J_z^2+J_x^2-\varepsilon iP J_y\\
&=J^2+\tfrac14-
(J_y+\varepsilon iP/2)(J_y-\varepsilon iP/2)\\
&\preceq J^2+\tfrac14.
\end{aligned}
\]

The product subtracted is $TT^\dagger$ for
\(T=J_y+\varepsilon iP/2\), so it is positive. Since total spin satisfies
\(s\le k/2\), \(J^2\preceq k(k+2)I/4\), hence
\(\|H_{k,\varepsilon}\|\le k+1\). The representation reduction makes this
valid on every physical block, including each odd-$k$ sector $C=\pm I$.

For real $Q\succeq0$ on the Clifford vector space, use an oriented
$SO(m)$ eigenbasis and write $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_m\ge0$. Spin covariance
implements this basis change on the ordinary tensor factors. With
\(a_{m+1}=0\),

\[
H_Q=\sum_{k=1}^m(a_k-a_{k+1})H_{[k]}.
\]

Every coefficient is nonnegative. Thus either the triangle inequality for
operator norm or subadditivity of \(\lambda_{\max}\) yields

\[
\lambda_{\max}(H_Q)\le\|H_Q\|
\le\sum_k(a_k-a_{k+1})(k+1)
=\sum_i a_i+a_1
=\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q+\lambda_{\max}(Q).
\]

The telescoping identity is exact: \(\sum_k k(a_k-a_{k+1})=\sum_i a_i\)
and \(\sum_k(a_k-a_{k+1})=a_1\).

## Canonical-EB support and Spin-orbit nuance

For a state \(\rho\), let \(r_i(\rho)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\Gamma_i)\).
For every unit $u$, \(\sum_i u_i\Gamma_i\) is a Hermitian involution;
therefore \(\|r(\rho)\|_2\le1\). In the diagonal frame the canonical
measurement-preparation score is

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Psi)
=\sum_z p_z\sum_i a_i r_i(\rho_z)^2\le a_1,
\qquad p_z=\operatorname{Tr}(M_z)/d.
\]

For attainment, choose $j=1$ at a largest eigenvalue and measure the
projective POVM \(P_\pm=(I\pm\Gamma_1)/2\), preparing
\(P_\pm/\operatorname{Tr}P_\pm\). Each normalized effect has vector moment
\(\pm e_1\), and \(p_+=p_-=1/2\), so the score is $a_1$. The map is
trace-preserving and unital. Refining each degenerate projector into a
rank-one eigenbasis gives the same score if rank-one outcomes are required.

A single full \(\operatorname{Spin}(m)\) orbit of a \(+1\) eigenstate of
\(\Gamma_1\) does average to $I/d$, but its vector second moment is
isotropic: \(\mathbb E[(g e_1)(g e_1)^T]=I/m\). Its anisotropic score is
\(\operatorname{Tr}(Q)/m\), so it does not prove support attainment at
\(a_1\) unless $Q$ is isotropic. The projective eigenbasis above is the
correct anisotropic witness.

Finally, with normalized Choi convention,
\(\operatorname{Tr}[J(\Phi)(A^T\otimes B)]=\tau(B\Phi(A))\). Each output
branch of a swap-symmetric two-output Choi state therefore contributes the
same \(\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Phi)\), giving
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega H_Q)=2\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}(Q\Phi)\). Since
\(\Omega\) is a density matrix, this is at most \(\lambda_{\max}(H_Q)\).
The matrix inequality is a vector-sector dual bound; by itself it does not
settle duals with bivector/higher-form support or a common full-channel EB
comparison.

## Exact finite check

`proof/exactchecks/physical_odd_subset_m5_k3.py` constructs the physical
ordinary-tensor $m=5$, $k=3<m$ subset using exact SymPy matrices. It
verifies (A), verifies that all four pairs
\((C,D)=(\pm1,\pm1)\) occur with rank 16, and recovers sector
characteristic polynomials by exact Newton identities. Each equals the
fourth power of the corresponding canonical rank-4 block polynomial. Both $C$ signs occur, and the full restricted star has largest root 4.
This check supports the representation bookkeeping but is not the proof for
general $m,k$.

## Final assessment

The claimed inequality and support formula are correct with the stated
real-vector-sector scope. The potentially fatal odd-subset assumption is
avoided by retaining both central signs and treating each as a canonical
parity-block representation. The only repair needed in presentation is to
avoid using a full Spin orbit as the anisotropic support maximizer; use the
\(\Gamma_j\) eigenbasis instead. No web sources were needed for this
algebraic audit.
