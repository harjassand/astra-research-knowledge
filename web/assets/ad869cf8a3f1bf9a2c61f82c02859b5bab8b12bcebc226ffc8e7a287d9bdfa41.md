# Cycle 2: stationary feedback with average and peak resource limits

## Decision

For a capped birth–death output, the sharp stationary reliability/precision frontier under a peak birth cap and an average translation budget is a finite linear program over stationary probabilities. A vertex controller uses the peak birth rate on every supported edge except at most one, then cuts off the support. This structure does **not** imply a monotone single-cutoff policy. An exact three-state counterexample shows that the usual threshold can be strictly suboptimal for minimizing a low-copy tail at fixed mean.

For shared outputs, the correct state is the joint count vector. I derived its exact occupation-flow LP and a sharp two-output shared-cap formula. A pair of outputs can each satisfy its scalar peak check and jointly satisfy the average production budget while remaining infeasible under the shared instantaneous cap. The construction gives an explicit state-feedback resource allocator and an observation-cost gate.

The general LP method is prior art in controlled queues and constrained continuous-time Markov decision processes (CTMDPs). Resource-aware molecular control is also established. I make no novelty or biological-performance claim for these model results. The consequential open capability is a molecular or instrumented controller whose sensing, memory, dynamic resource pool, latency, and growth effects are included in the same measured stochastic model; the finite-state formulas below give an analytic benchmark for that work.

## Single-output theorem and exact frontier

Let (N_t\in\{0,\ldots,M\}). A controller chooses a predictable unit-birth intensity (u_t\in[0,B]), with births blocked at (M). Each molecule dies independently at rate \(\gamma>0\), so the total death intensity at state (n) is \(\gamma n\). Write \(\pi_n\) for a stationary law and (m=\sum_n n\pi_n\) for its mean.

Stationary flow across the edge (n\leftrightarrow n+1) gives

\[
  \gamma(n+1)\pi_{n+1}=\pi_n\bar u_n\le B\pi_n,
  \qquad
  \bar u_n=\mathbb E[u_t\mid N_t=n].
\]

Conversely, every normalized nonnegative vector satisfying these adjacent inequalities is implemented by stationary feedback

\[
  u_n=\gamma(n+1)\pi_{n+1}/\pi_n
\]

on its positive support, with zero rates above the support. Thus the feasible stationary laws at fixed (m) are exactly

\[
  \mathcal P_M(q,m)=\left\{\pi\ge0:\ \sum_n\pi_n=1,\ \sum_n n\pi_n=m,\ (n+1)\pi_{n+1}\le q\pi_n\ (0\le n<M)\right\},
  \qquad q=B/\gamma.
\]

Stationarity also gives the exact average translation identity

\[
  \mathbb E[u_t]=\gamma m.
\]

An average translation cap (C_{\rm tr}) is the additional linear constraint \(\gamma m\le C_{\rm tr}\). At fixed (m), the dropout objective \(D_h=\Pr(N<h)=\sum_{n<h}\pi_n\) and variance \(V=\sum_n n^2\pi_n-m^2\) are both linear in \(\pi\). Hence each fixed-mean dropout/variance frontier is the exact image of this polytope. Under an inequality average budget, scan (0\le m\le\min(C_{\rm tr}/\gamma,\bar m_M(q))), where

\[
  \bar m_M(q)=q\,\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}q^n/n!}{\sum_{n=0}^{M}q^n/n!}
\]

is the maximum feasible mean. This parameterization accounts for both the average and peak production constraints; it does not silently equate a peak cap with an average budget.

At an extreme point with positive support \(\{0,\ldots,L\}\), the normalization and mean are two independent equalities. If two or more supported edge inequalities were strict, they would split the positive support into at least three blocks. Rescaling the three block masses along a nonzero direction preserving total mass and first moment would give two distinct feasible laws whose midpoint is the proposed point, contradicting extremality. Thus a vertex has at most one strict edge inside the positive support. An optimizer of any linear scalarization of dropout and variance can therefore be chosen with (u_n=B) on all supported edges but at most one, followed by a support cutoff. The partial edge may occur before later peak-rate edges, so this is a bang–bang-plus-one-partial structure, not a usual threshold rule.

For \(M=2\), the entire frontier is elementary. Put (t=\pi_2), (q=B/\gamma), and fix mean (m\). Then

\[
  \pi=(1-m+t,\ m-2t,\ t),
\]

and feasibility is equivalent to

\[
  0\le m\le \bar m_2(q)=\frac{q(1+q)}{1+q+q^2/2},\qquad
  \max\!\left(0,\frac{m-q(1-m)}{q+2}\right)\le t\le\frac{qm}{2(1+q)}.
\]

For (h=2), the two requested scores are

\[
  D_2=1-t,\qquad V=m-m^2+2t.
\]

These formulas are the full sharp dropout/precision frontier, not merely bounds. At (q=1,m=1/2), (0\le t\le1/8), so (D_2=1-t) and (V=1/4+2t). Lower dropout costs increased variance along the whole Pareto segment.

### Exact threshold counterexample

Take \(M=2,\gamma=B=1, h=2, m=C_{\rm tr}=1/2\). The law

\[
  \pi=(5/8,1/4,1/8)
\]

is implemented by rates \((u_0,u_1)=(2/5,1)\), has mean (1/2), dropout (7/8), and variance (1/2). Every standard single-cutoff policy with this mean has (u_0=1,u_1=0), law \((1/2,1/2,0)\), dropout (1), and variance (1/4). A cutoff reaching state 2 must already use full rate at state 0, which raises the mean above (1/2). Thus the standard cutoff is not globally optimal for the fixed-mean lower-tail objective. This is an exact counterexample, not a statement about threshold policies under other queueing costs.

The independent rational checker also verifies a smaller exact instance at \(q=1/2,M=2,m=1/64,h=2\): the optimum is \(\pi=(379,4,1)/384\), with failure (383/384), while every standard cutoff has failure (1). ## Shared multi-output resource competition

For (K) outputs with states (x\in S=\prod_i\{0,\ldots,M_i\}), birth flows (z_{x,i}=\pi_x u_i(x)), and linear deaths \(\gamma_i x_i\), the exact stationary occupation-flow constraints are

\[
\begin{aligned}
  &\pi_x\ge0,\quad \sum_x\pi_x=1,\quad z_{x,i}\ge0,\quad z_{x,i}=0\text{ if }x_i=M_i,\\
  &\sum_i z_{x,i}\le B\pi_x,\\
  &\sum_i\bigl(z_{x-e_i,i}+\gamma_i(x_i+1)\pi_{x+e_i}\bigr)
   =\sum_i\bigl(z_{x,i}+\gamma_i x_i\pi_x\bigr)\quad(x\in S).
\end{aligned}
\]

Any stationary causal controller induces these flows. Conversely (u_i(x)=z_{x,i}/\pi_x) realizes each feasible flow pair on positive-mass states. Joint dropout probabilities, all-output success, mean constraints, average translation limits, and linear state/action costs are then LP objectives or constraints. If a resource pool, controller molecule, or previous action has its own dynamics, it must be added to the state. With censored observation the full-state LP is an information relaxation, not necessarily an implementable policy. The number of states is \(\prod_i(M_i+1)\), so the exact LP scales exponentially in the number of outputs.

For two binary outputs with equal death rate \(\gamma\), equal stationary means (m\in(0,1)), and joint-success probability (d=\Pr(N_1=N_2=1)\), the masses are forced to be

\[
  \pi_{11}=d,\quad \pi_{10}=\pi_{01}=m-d,\quad \pi_{00}=1-2m+d.
\]

For positive masses, the sharp shared peak birth cap required to realize this (d) is

\[
  B_{\rm exact}(m,d)=\gamma\max\!\left\{\frac{d}{m-d},\frac{2(m-d)}{1-2m+d}\right\}.
\]

The first lower bound follows from balance at state 11: births into that state must supply death flux (2\gamma d\), and its two singleton predecessor states have total mass (2(m-d)\). The second follows from state 00, which must supply total birth flux (2\gamma(m-d)\). Both are attained by the reversible state-feedback controller: in state 00, give each output rate \(r=\gamma(m-d)/(1-2m+d)\); in either singleton state, give the absent output rate \(b=\gamma d/(m-d)\); in state 11, use zero rates. The shared peak is exactly \(\max(2r,b)\).

At (m=0.1,\gamma=1), minimizing this cap over the admissible joint mass gives

\[
  y=m-d=\frac{\sqrt{1+4m(1-m)}-1}{2},\quad
  d=0.0169048105\ldots,\quad
  B_*=\frac d y=0.2034390994\ldots.
\]

Each scalar output alone needs only rate \(m/(1-m)=1/9<0.2\), and the total mean production flux is exactly (0.2). Nevertheless, a shared peak (B=0.2) is jointly infeasible. The exact shared feedback table attains (B_*). A constant equal split meets the same marginal means at (B_{\rm static}=2m/(1-m)=0.2222222\ldots), so the feedback table reduces the required translation-only peak by (0.01878312\ldots). At (m=1/2), the minimum cap for any joint success is \(\sqrt{2}\gamma\); for cap (B\ge\sqrt2\gamma), maximum joint success is (B/[2(B+\gamma)]), attained by the same state-dependent fill-the-missing-output rule.

At the static baseline cap (B=2/9), the same fixed means (m=0.1) can attain joint success (d=1/55\approx0.01818) under feedback, versus (m^2=0.01) under the constant equal split. The attaining table uses rate (0.1) for each output at state 00, rate (2/9) for the absent output at each singleton, and zero at 11; its stationary masses in order (00,10,01,11) are ((90,9,9,2)/110). This is an exact joint-success gain at the same translation cap and average production. Its monitor cost is a separate resource coordinate below, so this is not a free-feedback comparison.

This shows why separate one-output frontiers and an average production check do not determine reliability under a shared peak cap. It also supplies a small exact benchmark for resource-allocation algorithms.

## State-observation and controller costs

The rate tables above assume full count-state observation and zero-delay action updates. A concrete event-triggered digital implementation that reads the exact state after every birth or death processes an average of (2\gamma m) state changes per unit time for one output. For (K) outputs it processes (2\sum_i\gamma_i m_i) changes per unit time. The rate identity follows because stationary births and deaths each have intensity \(\sum_i\gamma_i m_i\). A two-binary-output state needs two bits of discrete memory; a general count table needs at least \(\lceil\log_2|S|\rceil\) state labels. These are interface costs, not estimates of the resources used by any natural circuit.

In the (m=0.1\) two-output construction, the average read/update rate is (0.4) per time unit. The statewise jump intensities are (B_*=0.203439\ldots) in state 00, (1+B_*=1.203439\ldots) in either singleton state, and (2) in state 11. If each read costs (c_{\rm obs}) resource units and this processing competes instantaneously with translation, an explicit combined cap is

\[
R_{\rm fb}(c_{\rm obs})=\max\{B_*(1+c_{\rm obs}),\ B_*+c_{\rm obs}(1+B_*),\ 2c_{\rm obs}\}+r_{\rm ctrl},
\]

where (r_{\rm ctrl}) is any uniform controller burden in the same units. The constant split baseline requires (R_{\rm static}=0.222222\ldots) with no observation cost. If (r_{\rm ctrl}=0), the state-feedback policy keeps this combined worst-case demand below the static baseline only for \(c_{\rm obs}<0.0156078\ldots\) under this statewise accounting model. Separately, under an average-only controller budget, its required average resource is (0.2+0.4c_{\rm obs}+r_{\rm ctrl}). These are explicit model thresholds, not measured biochemical conversions.

Noisy, periodic, delayed, or censored sensing creates a partially observed control problem; the full-state LP gives an upper bound unless the sensor state is included and its information constraints are modeled. Sensing power, molecule synthesis/degradation, switching, latency, growth dilution, and dynamic ribosome/ATP pools are not supplied by the abstract queue model. The earlier peak-only coupling result in [epistasis_identifiability/CYCLE2_REPORT.md](epistasis_identifiability/CYCLE2_REPORT.md) applies only when production has a hard peak cap and the objective is a monotone low-count tail: maximum constant birth intensity attains that special frontier. It does not settle average-budget or shared-output design.

### Finite-bandwidth sample-and-hold diagnostic

To charge an explicit observation schedule, I also lifted the (m=0.1) shared-output controller to a 16-state CTMC. Let (Y) store the most recent perfect joint readout of (N\in\{00,01,10,11\}). Independent reads arrive at rate \(\nu\), reset (Y\leftarrow N), and the action table is held between reads. With \(\delta=\nu/\gamma\), the table uses

\[
  p_*=(6-\sqrt{34})/10,\qquad
  r=(\sqrt{34}-4)/18,\quad b=2r=B_*/\gamma,
\]

and rows (Y=00:(r,r),\ Y=10:(0,b),\ Y=01:(b,0),\ Y=11:(0,0)), with coordinates ordered by output. For joint states (x=(n,y)), the generator contains the held birth transitions, physical deaths at rate \(\gamma n_i\), and read transitions ((n,y)\to(n,n)) at rate \(\nu\) whenever (y\ne n). Reads with (y=n) are still charged. The [50-digit solver](crispr_drive/sample_hold_16state.py) computes the stationary law without trajectory simulation.

The fast-read limit recovers (P(N=11)=p_*=0.0169048105\ldots) and both means (0.1). The singular slow-read limit is exact: (P(N=11)\to(6-\sqrt{34})/24=0.0070436710\ldots) and each mean tends to ((8-\sqrt{34})/24=0.0903770044\ldots). At finite \(\nu\), stale actions lower the means below (0.1). For example, at \(\delta=1.3220897723\), the joint success is (0.01238479537\) and each mean is (0.09559063552\); at \(\delta=0.46775104\), joint success crosses the no-read matched-mean constant-split baseline (0.01) while each mean is (0.0932120919\). This is an exact finite-state model calculation with numeric rate values, not an equal-mean finite-observation theorem.

For a declared utility (J(\delta)=P(N=11)-\kappa\delta), where \(\kappa=c\gamma\) charges (c) per read, the ratio ([P_\delta(N=11)-0.01]/\delta) has a numerically located maximum (0.001803807443\ldots) near \(\delta=1.3220897723\). The stationary solver plus high-precision derivative root reproduces this point. This cost threshold applies only to the fixed held table and the stated comparison baseline; it is not a globally optimized POMDP policy or a measured molecular read cost. The matching derivation, endpoint limits, and controlled-observation prior-art boundary are in [SAMPLE_HOLD_16STATE.md](crispr_drive/SAMPLE_HOLD_16STATE.md). Huang, Kavitha, and Zhu already study continuous-time jump MDPs with joint observation-time and control choices, including queue examples ([2019 paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ALLERTON.2019.8919744)).

## Prior art and the stronger molecular-feedback gate

The stationary LP is established queue-control/CTMDP machinery. Serfozo’s 1981 paper studies controlled birth–death processes and queues under average reward, gives conditions for monotone policies under its reward assumptions, and discusses a linear program ([primary article](https://doi.org/10.2307/1426467)). Guo, Huang, and Song’s 2012 SIAM paper formulates constrained average-cost CTMDP optimization for randomized history-dependent policies as an equivalent LP and illustrates it with a controlled birth–death system ([primary article](https://doi.org/10.1137/100805169)). This subsumes the general occupation-measure method. The single-cutoff counterexample concerns a different fixed-mean tail objective and does not refute objective-specific threshold theorems.

Biological resource-aware control is also prior art. Synchronous orthogonal-ribosome allocation with a LacI-based controller was implemented and reduced gene coupling in a two-gene circuit ([Nature Communications 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-02898-6)). A 2024 study compares antithetic controllers in a resource-coupled two-module model using stochastic simulations and fluctuation–dissipation analysis, with protein noise as its objective ([Chakravarty, Zhang, and Tian](https://doi.org/10.1109/cdc56724.2024.10886586); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11987709/)). Sokolowski et al. optimize a spatial gap-gene network under maximum production/degradation rates and average resource utilization to study positional information ([PNAS 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2402925121)). These sources establish that feedback and resource-constrained gene-expression optimization are active, but they do not supply the finite-state hard-cap joint-threshold formula above.

The higher-value molecular question is therefore narrower than “does feedback reduce noise?”: can a specified molecular or instrumented allocator meet a joint single-cell reliability target under a measured shared ribosome budget after its readout, controller, delay, and growth costs are charged? A decisive next study would (i) measure two output count trajectories and the shared resource state, (ii) estimate birth, degradation, growth, and observation-transition rates, (iii) fit the finite-state model without using the reliability holdout, and (iv) compare the implementable controller with the constant split and the full-state LP bound under matched peak and average resources. The (m=0.1) example supplies an explicit gate: the full-information cap advantage is only (0.01878) translation-rate units, and the ideal monitor makes (0.4) state updates per time unit. If measured controller plus sensing costs exceed that advantage after conversion into the same resource units, this allocator is not competitive. The specific molecular outcome remains unresolved; no new circuit is claimed.

The earlier primary-source check for bounded immigration–death domination is recorded in [FINAL_REPORT.md](FINAL_REPORT.md). Hemberg and Barahona’s sampling work concerns bounded current-state reaction hazards and known-stationary dominators; it does not itself state the average-cost frontier or an arbitrary history-dependent feedback theorem ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.106.099390)).
