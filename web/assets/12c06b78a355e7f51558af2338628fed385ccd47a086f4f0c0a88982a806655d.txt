CYCLE 4 — POLYNOMIAL RAW-PATH PREDICTION FOR STATIONARY RENEWALS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: strong internally audited candidate; external correctness and novelty UNKNOWN

VERDICT

The broad goal—discovering and certifying an efficient predictive structure
from arbitrary noisy finite-state observations—remains open. This cycle found
a narrower, consequential target with a concrete construction: exact
polynomial-time terminal prediction from one raw path for the full
stationary finite-mean binary renewal class, with no mean/tail/mixing
promise, reset, or renewal oracle. The candidate matches the source-reported
minimax expected conditional-KL rate n^{-1/2}. A separate row-urn argument
extends it to fixed finite *observed* event marks with arbitrary gap/mark
dependence. This is prediction under a supplied renewal-class contract; it
does not discover that the source is a renewal process or reconstruct its
kernel, and it is not a general HMM/state-acquisition theorem.

The binary construction is fully described with proofs and executable exact
checks in `renewal_integer_code/RESULT.txt` and
`outputs/research/root_renewal_prediction/PROOF.txt`. The observed-mark
extension, including the independent lead and Luna audits, is in
`marked_renewal_extension/RESULT.txt`; Sol's full proof/compiler packet is at
`outputs/research/sol_marked_renewal/`. The focused source comparison is in
`renewal_novelty_check/RESULT.txt`. No novelty, firstness, or external proof
validation is claimed.

1. EXACT INTERFACE

Binary case: the input is one stationary path X_1,...,X_n where 1 marks a
renewal. Inter-renewal gaps have an arbitrary unknown law on positive
integers with finite but unbounded mean. The learner predicts X_{n+1} under
source-expected terminal conditional KL. It receives no resets, gap labels
beyond visible events, support cutoff, mean, context dictionary, hidden state,
or conditional-probability query.

Observed-mark case: a fixed supplied q is known. Symbols are 0 (no event) or
one of q *observed* event marks. After mark i, the next positive gap g and
destination mark d have unknown joint law p_i(g,d). A stationary version has
finite weighted mean under a stationary distribution pi of the embedded
mark chain. Reducibility, periods, zero transitions, and arbitrarily large
finite means are allowed. Hidden marks/times, mixtures of distinct kernels,
or arbitrary HMM emissions are outside the theorem.

In both cases the target is the actual next symbol following the same raw
training path. This is not fresh independent-history risk, worst-context TV,
high-confidence certification, cumulative loss, or a multi-step forecast.

2. BINARY TYPE-FIRST CODE AND PREDICTOR

For horizon N=n+1, every nonzero word has a multiset C=(c_j) of completed
positive gaps, span S=sum j c_j<=N-1, and R=sum c_j. There are exactly
(N-S)R!/product_j c_j! strings of that type. Add a separate allzero type;
choose uniformly among this type and the integer partitions C of spans
S<N, then choose uniformly inside the selected type. Writing
K_N=1+sum_{S<N}p(S), the resulting full-support block distribution is

  Q_N(0^N)=1/K_N,
  Q_N(x)=product_j c_j!/[K_N(N-S)R!] for x with at least one 1.

For a stationary renewal word, the exact likelihood is an equilibrium
first-residual factor, the product of completed-gap probabilities, and a
right-censoring factor. The boundary factors are at most one and the
multinomial probability of the completed gap type is at most one, giving
P_N(x)/Q_N(x)<=K_N N. The partition generating function implies
log K_N=O(sqrt(N)), uniformly without a source tail or mean bound. A direct
prefix DP compiles exact Q_N marginals. Sharing its marked coefficient tables
over all suffixes gives O(n^3) integer coefficient/summand updates; exact
integer/rational bit costs are polynomial. The detailed formula, common
denominators, prefix code, and checker evidence are in the binary result.

Apply the stationary reverse-suffix compression-to-prediction reduction to
the *same fixed* Q_N: average its n conditional predictions on the n
suffixes of the observed path. The renewal countdown state can be clipped at
n+2 values without losing information about an (n+1)-symbol block. Thus

  sup_mu E_mu KL(P_mu(X_{n+1}|X_1^n)||qhat_n(.|X_1^n))
       <= O(n^{-1/2}).

Han–Jiang–Wu (2024) report the matching minimax lower order Theta(n^{-1/2})
for this stationary finite-mean renewal interface. Accordingly the candidate
has the optimal rate order, but not a sharp constant or a claim of external
proof. Their paper states that an efficient exact-rate renewal predictor is
unclear; whether this construction resolves that stated computational gap is
a priority question, not an internally settled claim.

The horizon-indexed Q_N need not be projectively consistent. Every suffix
conditional in the predictor is a marginal of this one Q_N. Replacing it by
a separately normalized Q_t changes the argument and is not done.

3. FIXED FINITE OBSERVED-MARK EXTENSION

For q fixed, Sol's row-urn code chooses a uniform q^2-coloured transition
count pool of total gap span S<N, an anchor uniform on {1,...,N-S}, and a
starting mark uniform on [q]. Each visit draws an outgoing (destination,gap)
item without replacement from that mark's row. It stops as soon as the
*current* row empties, even if other rows retain unused counts. A distinct
allzero option is necessary and included. This defines a proper block
distribution without Euler-trail/connectivity normalization. The exact
target pool gives

  P_N(x)/Q_N(x) <= q K_N N,
  log K_N <= log 2 + 2q sqrt(2N).

For a raw prefix, ordered observed urn draws give a product of local falling
factorials divided by falling factorials of row totals. The remaining
current-row probability of no event in b observed zeros is 1 if that row is
empty, and otherwise the fraction of remaining row items with gap strictly
greater than b. A DP over total span and q row totals, plus one additive
marked tail moment, sums all pools exactly. Grouped denominators divide N!,
so the exact prefix marginals are integer-scaled. The conservative compiler
cost is O(q^2 N^{q+3}) integer updates per marginal and
O_q(n^{q+6}log^2(n+2)) bit operations for the full fixed-q predictor. The
same capped countdown argument gives the explicit bound

  [log 2 + 2q sqrt(2(n+1)) + log q + log(n+1)
       + log(q(n+1)+1)]/n,

which is O_q(n^{-1/2}). The binary subclass and KL coarsening transfer the
source-reported lower bound, hence fixed-q minimax order Theta_q(n^{-1/2}).
No uniform q-dependence is proved. This is a class-specific universal
predictor, not hidden mark discovery or source-parameter estimation.

4. HORIZON-FREE ONLINE PREDICTION

An existing Luna follow-up wraps the binary construction as a causal
anytime predictor. After observing a prefix of current length t, it runs
the t-specific fixed-horizon suffix-average predictor based on Q_{t+1}; it
does not need a final horizon T. For every t, the same-path terminal risk is

  [pi sqrt(2t/3) + 2 log(t+1) + log 2 + log(t+2)]/t <= 8/sqrt(t).

Multiplying these online conditional outputs (with a fair initial X_1 code)
defines a new projective sequential law and gives expected cumulative
sequence KL at most log 2+16 sqrt(T). This is a valid anytime law even though
the fixed-block family Q_N is not projective: the follow-up explicitly checks
Q_2(X_1=1)=1/2 but Q_3(X_1=1)=17/30. At every time it recomputes from the
current prefix with that horizon's Q_{t+1}; no marginal identity between
different Q_N is used.

This closes horizon-free online output at polynomial cost, not compact-state
acquisition. The algorithm retains the full raw prefix, recomputes at each
time, and has O(t^3) coefficient updates per output; the conservative
schoolbook bit-time bound is O(t^9 log^2(t+2)). Recomputing through T costs
O(T^4) coefficient updates. The projective sequential law has an expected
cumulative-KL bound, not pointwise likelihood-ratio domination. Full proof,
costs and finite checks (516 output equalities and 114 projectivity checks
through length 6) are in `renewal_anytime/RESULT.txt`.

5. EXACT ARITHMETIC IMPLEMENTATION CONSEQUENCE

An N!-scaled implementation preserves the same fixed-horizon Q_N and every
prediction and risk statement above. For nonzero-suffix marginals, the
per-suffix common denominator products are replaced by integer sums over
(N!)^2, using F_q | N! and each slack V_beta | N!. The unchanged all-zero
branch still uses Fraction arithmetic, and the final suffix average still
has large exact outputs.

On the alternating length-120 history, median time over three runs fell from
1.362 s to 0.399 s (3.41x); the largest transient marginal integer fell from
10,234 to 1,367 bits. Traced peak memory changed only 2.8%, and final output
components still reached 20,519 bits. The all-zero history showed no useful
gain. Both implementations have the same O(n^3) coefficient/summand updates;
these measurements are for a narrow implementation comparison, not an
asymptotic runtime improvement. Exact equivalence passed for 1,022 histories
of lengths 1–9 and 40 fixtures through length 20 (8,774 suffix comparisons).
Full method, costs, benchmark rows and the independent literal-formula
comparison are in `renewal_integer_code/nfactorial/RESULT.txt` and
`renewal_integer_code/nfactorial/benchmark.json`.

6. FIXED-HORIZON HIGH-PROBABILITY TERMINAL RISK

Let R_n(mu,X^n) be the conditional KL divergence between the true law of
X_{n+1} given the same observed path and an arbitrary full-path predictor's
output. For every n >= 40,000 and every possibly randomized predictor, there
is a stationary finite-support renewal law (allowed to depend on n) such that

  P_mu{R_n(mu,X^n) >= (log(2)-1/2)/(18 sqrt(n))} >= 1/160.

The construction adapts the balanced-support posterior-ambiguity prior in
HJW Appendix E. It keeps the equilibrium initial wait and terminal survival
factors in the path likelihood, and forces the target gap to be absent from
all observed complete gaps on the ambiguity event. An exact Fraction checker
passes four small exhaustive instances; the all-n argument is analytic and
has not received external review. The type-first predictor has the matching
fixed-confidence upper order B_n/delta by Markov's inequality, so for each
fixed delta < 1/160 the minimax same-path terminal conditional-risk quantile
is Theta_delta(n^(-1/2)). This is a per-horizon minimax result: its lower-bound
source changes with n. It is not an independent-history statement, a
time-uniform confidence sequence, or a constant-probability lower bound for
the realized one-step log score. Full definitions, proof, costs, exact
diagnostics and limitations are in `renewal_terminal_tail/RESULT.txt`.

7. VALIDATION, BASELINES, AND PRIOR ART

Binary finite checks: the root exact checker reports, through N=9, 2,035
prefix equalities, 1,013 conditional normalizations and 5,110 stationary
likelihood-ratio fixtures. The predictor checker covers 510 histories and
3,586 suffix conditionals through n=8; the shared suffix-DP check covers
1,538 conditionals through n=7; fixed-horizon code round trips were checked
through length 10. These are finite diagnostics, not all-N proof or external
validation.

Observed-mark tests: Sol's independent enumerator and DP pass 3,099 exact
prefix equalities, 1,157 categorical normalizations, and 6,578 pointwise
stationary regret checks over q=1,N<=8; q=2,N<=6; q=3,N<=4. The lead reran
that checker. A sibling Luna independently rederived the prefix and
denominator formulas and matched the global-allzero implementation on 424
prefix equalities and 178 normalizations over q=1,N<=6 and q=2,N<=4. Root
also independently read the proof and reported no flaw. These internal
reviews preserve the candidate status; no proof assistant or external expert
has validated it.

The strongest statistical baseline is Han–Jiang–Wu's finite-block NML
construction: it reaches the minimax order but its exact renewal marginal is
not supplied as polynomial-time computable. A polynomial context-tree
control exists. Garivier's primary 2006 thesis, Theorem 22 at printed p.85,
was visually checked: CTW renewal redundancy is Theta(sqrt(n) log n), giving
a derived O(log(n)/sqrt(n)) terminal risk under the HJW suffix conversion.
Theorem 23's n^{2/3}log n gap-chain result concerns a Markov chain on
unbounded interarrival-time values, not the fixed finite observed-mark
kernel. This is a logarithmic-factor control, not a lower bound against all
predictors or priority evidence.

A full-data learner retaining the same path can compute or emulate the
predictor; no information-theoretic advantage over that equally informed
baseline is claimed. The candidate's contribution, if the proof and priority
survive, is an explicit efficient code/marginal compiler for a narrowly
specified process class.

8. WHAT IS AND IS NOT CLOSED

Internally closed: the binary type-first normalization and redundancy proof;
the exact prefix formulas and shared O(n^3) suffix DP; the fixed-q observed-
mark row-urn normalization, pointwise domination, prefix DP, N! denominator
and capped-countdown conversion. Root and an independent Luna found no flaw
in the row-urn proof, with finite exact checks as recorded above.

Not closed: external proof validation; a comprehensive current prior-art or
priority search; asymptotically improved implementation; compact-state or
sublinear-memory anytime prediction; hidden event-mark exact compilation;
arbitrary HMM structural acquisition; source reconstruction; or a
field-breaking claim. The fixed-n high-probability section closes only the
stated same-path quantile bracket; sharp delta dependence, independent-history
risk, and one-source time-uniform tails remain unknown. The current work does
not remove the general rank/conditioning/mixing and representation acquisition
gates documented in Cycles 2-3.

For hidden finite event marks, Sol has an internally proved candidate that
the exact projected-code prefix/full-block function is #P-hard for q=2. The
reduction uses O(m) code queries with explicit raw horizons N=O(|G|^2), and
the #P membership statement is scoped to unary N or N bounded by the explicit
trajectory size. A focused independent Luna audit reconstructed the
full-span extraction, padding/Vandermonde interpolation, Hadamard transform,
Eulerian medial construction, pinned-root recovery, and use of the cited
bounded-degree counting theorem; it found no fatal gap. The audit also
isolates the encoding caveat: arbitrary binary-encoded N with a short prefix
does not support the claimed polynomial output length, but the hardness
queries use explicit trajectories with N=|x|. The result does not establish
hardness of the suffix-averaged terminal predictor, every optimal predictor,
or relative approximation. Internal audit is not external proof validation.
See `outputs/research/sol_hidden_renewal/RESULT.txt` and
`outputs/research/acquisition_learning/cycle4/hidden_renewal_audit/RESULT.txt`.

The N!-denominator implementation and its measured scaling, the fixed-n
high-probability terminal-risk lower bound, and the horizon-free theorem are
banked above. The online theorem leaves compact-state update open. Sol's
hidden-mark compiler has a focused independent audit with no fatal gap found;
keep exact compiler hardness separate from the observed-mark theorem and from
statistical prediction hardness.

9. RESTRICTED BOND FPRAS AUDITS AND FIXED-q CONSEQUENCE

Three reused existing Luna leaves completed independent audits of the
q=2, full-span, multiplicity-at-most-two hidden row-urn likelihood FPRAS:
the degree-two equality graph/correction tail, the conditional tensor and
Ising reduction, and the complete cost/interface ledger. They found no fatal

`bond_fpras_audit/`; the printed JS93 theorem is exact-real, so the finite-bit
rational simulation remains an internal repair, not an executable FPRAS.

The same pair-bond mechanism has an internally reconstructed fixed-q
extension: for every fixed q>=2, the full-span binary likelihood is relatively
approximable in polynomial bit time when every gap label occurs at most twice.
The pair tensor is [[q^2+1,1],[1,1]]; collapsing the q-1 nonzero Fourier
states gives positive ghost fields, and the q-color multinomial correction
has an inverse-polynomial mean for fixed q. The coarse arithmetic exponent
grows with q. Finite exact checks cover q=2,3 on short words. This does not
cover growing q, multiplicity >=3, censored prefixes, suffix prediction or
source reconstruction, and no novelty/priority claim is made. Full proof,
independent derivation, checker and claim ledger are in
`fixed_q_pair_bond_fpras/`.
