# Released-data audit: JUN bZIP specificity and a double-mutant nomination

**Decision:** use the 2024 JUN–bZIP BindingPCA map as a public, small reanalysis dataset, and nominate JUN E32A+A49R (`1eA+4aR`) for an *experimental test of improved average FOS-subfamily specificity*. This is a model-derived nomination, not a demonstrated design. Its predicted worst FOS-family partner is slightly below the strongest predicted off-target, so it does **not** meet a strict every-target-above-every-off-target criterion.

## Dataset and acquisition

Bendel et al. measured JUN single substitutions against the human bZIP partner panel in yeast BindingPCA. The processed dataset is GEO [GSE245326](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE245326), specifically `GSE245326_Supplementary_Data_3.txt.gz`, stored here at 12,982,675 bytes compressed. I downloaded this one file; I did not download SRA FASTQs. GEO access and download require no account or payment. NCBI places no general restrictions on GEO data use but cautions that submitters may assert rights; the separate deposited table has no explicit license I could confirm. Attribute the source and accession, and verify submitter rights before commercial reuse or redistribution. The paper is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, while the authors’ analysis repository is MIT-licensed.

The table has 30,180 JUN-variant × partner rows and 52 measured partner names. It exposes `count_e1_s0`–`count_e6_s0` (input counts) and `count_e1_s1`–`count_e6_s1` (selected-output counts) for six biological replicates. The source pipeline says each output library was sequenced twice and those two technical runs were summed before table generation; they cannot be separated for technical-variance estimation from this file. It also supplies replicate-level `fitness1_uncorr`–`fitness6_uncorr` and `sigma1_uncorr`–`sigma6_uncorr`, pooled `fitness`/`sigma`, model output `mean`/`std`/`ci95`, energy parameters `ddG_mut`, `ddG_bZ`, `dG_mult`, and source decompositions `rel`, `glob`, `spe` with p-value/FDR fields. `fitness` is a DiMSum log-enrichment binding score normalized to JUN-WT×FOS, not a direct dissociation constant.

Source QC marks a row `filt=TRUE` when any replicate has input count ≤10 or output count 0. The deposited table has 26,648 unflagged rows and 3,532 flagged rows. There are 580 JUN variants with at least one usable row, and all accepted JUN sequences differ from WT at at most one residue; there are **no JUN double mutants**. Only 294 non-WT variants have a complete 52-partner profile after the source filter. The two nominated single mutants each have all 52 partners passing QC. This is a left-censored/selection issue for low-count pairs, not random missingness; an independent count-level sensitivity analysis in the parent task found no change to either nominated single profile or their ranking. It cannot settle the unmeasured double’s epistasis.

Sampling is unusually useful for specificity: the paper reports all substitutions at 32 JUN bZIP positions, recovering 616 of 641 possible amino-acid variants (96%) with a median 21 barcodes per substitution; measurements target all 54 human bZIPs plus 19 negative controls, with 52 bZIP partners retained for analysis. Source-reported quality checks include replicate Pearson correlation 0.93, an additive MoCHI fit with R²=0.94, and FRET correlation of inferred partner energies R=0.78/0.85 (n=34). A splitFAST assay in HCT116 cells confirmed 21 measurements; relative-score correlation was R=0.89 for n=13. These support measurement/model utility but do not validate the nominated JUN double or make BindingPCA a direct affinity measurement.

The source model represents each JUN substitution and partner as one-hot/dummy encoded additive energy terms passed through a two-state binding function, with a partner-specific multiplicative slope to reduce residual bias. Its reported 30% holdout is a random variant–partner-combination split, not a held-out JUN genotype or an external double-mutant test. The public 2024 table is therefore a strong single-mutant map but not a pre-existing validation set for our pair prediction.

## Reproducible candidate calculation

Run [`candidate_analysis.py`](candidate_analysis.py) with Python 3; it uses only the standard library and completed locally in about 8 seconds. It reads the processed table, preserves the source `filt` decision, fits the shared two-state link parameters to the exported MoCHI `mean` values (RMSE 0.00431 on 26,648 rows), and computes three pair baselines. It does **not** refit the replicate count likelihood, impute filtered rows, or estimate double-mutant uncertainty.

Let `g_b(Δ)` denote the reconstructed source energy-to-score link for partner *b*, using the partner’s `dG_mult`; `Δ_i` and `Δ_j` are the exported JUN mutation energy terms. Define residuals `r_i,b = fitness_i,b − g_b(Δ_i)` and `r_WT,b = fitness_WT,b − g_b(0)`. The nominated H1 prediction, expressed relative to that partner’s measured JUN-WT score, is

`Δfitness_ij,b = g_b(Δ_i+Δ_j) − g_b(0) + (r_i,b − r_WT,b) + (r_j,b − r_WT,b)`.

Subtracting the WT residual once per mutation is necessary: it makes the formula recover the observed relative single effect when the other mutation is WT. This assumes additive energy changes and additive WT-centered single-mutant residuals; the residual additivity is untested for JUN cis doubles. The exported `ddG_mut` values are model energy units; they are not reported here as kcal/mol. The pair has `Δ_i=-0.175663`, `Δ_j=-0.211245` in those units.

The prespecified target set for this exploratory screen is **FOS, FOSB, FOSL1, FOSL2**. The other 48 partners remain off-targets, including ATF4; the source excludes ATF4 from its MoCHI specificity decomposition because of model-fit bias, but it remains in our measured-profile specificity objective. The exact sorted 52-partner panel used is:

`ATF1, ATF2, ATF3, ATF4, ATF5, ATF6, ATF6B, ATF7, BACH1, BACH2, BATF, BATF2, BATF3, CEBPA, CEBPB, CEBPD, CEBPE, CREB3, CREB3L1, CREB3L2, CREB3L3, CREB3L4, CREB5, CREBL2, CREBRF, CREBZF, CREM, DBP, DDIT3, FOS, FOSB, FOSL1, FOSL2, HLF, JDP2, JUN, JUNB, JUND, MAF, MAFA, MAFB, MAFF, MAFG, MAFK, NFE2, NFE2L1, NFE2L2, NFE2L3, NFIL3, NRL, TEF, XBP1`.

For `1eA+4aR`, H1 predicts relative scores FOS +0.451, FOSB +0.400, FOSL1 +0.662, FOSL2 +0.308; the FOS-family mean is +0.455, and maximum off-target is ATF7 +0.317. The mean-target-minus-maximum-off-target margin is +0.138, but the family minimum minus that off-target is −0.009. The naïve sum of observed single scores gives mean +0.460, minimum +0.311, maximum off-target +0.314 (ATF7), margin +0.145, and strict gap −0.003. The model-only nonlinear energy baseline without mutation residuals gives mean +0.080, minimum +0.046, maximum off-target +0.298 (XBP1), margin −0.218. Thus the nomination depends on retaining partner-specific single-mutant residuals; simple global energy alone does not nominate it.

Among 294 complete single-mutant profiles, the script scans distinct-position pairs and keeps pairs with predicted family mean ≥0.30 and family minimum ≥0.15. Of 105 retained pairs, `1eA+4aR` ranks first by mean-target-minus-maximum-off-target margin (+0.138); `1eA+3eR` is second (+0.123) but also misses strict separation (minimum +0.281 vs CEBPB +0.282). These screening cutoffs are exploratory choices, not source standards. Component single-score errors (mutant and matching WT `sigma` combined in quadrature) are ~0.026–0.036: for 1eA, FOS +0.306±0.027, FOSB +0.222±0.036, FOSL1 +0.442±0.028, FOSL2 +0.206±0.032, ATF4 +0.355±0.031; for 4aR, FOS +0.150±0.026, FOSB +0.181±0.032, FOSL1 +0.225±0.027, FOSL2 +0.105±0.031, XBP1 +0.437±0.028. These are single-measurement errors, not a confidence interval for the predicted double; no residual covariance or pair-model uncertainty is available.

## Falsifiable experiment and prior art

Construct JUN WT, E32A (`1eA`), A49R (`4aR`), and E32A+A49R, then run the same six-biological-replicate BindingPCA panel against all 52 partners. This is 208 genotype×partner cells per replicate (1,248 pair-by-replicate measurements). Keep all 48 non-FOS partners, especially ATF7, ATF4, XBP1, and BATF-family members; measure mutant abundance in parallel if feasible. The original pipeline’s matched input/output design sums duplicate output sequencing runs. A small confirmatory splitFAST panel could follow for all four FOS partners and the leading off-targets, but it is not a substitute for the 52-partner primary readout.

Primary outcome: `Γ = mean(relative score for four FOS partners) − max(relative score over the other 48)`. Pre-register success as Γ>0.10, all four FOS-family relative scores ≥0.20, and a replicate-block bootstrap 95% lower bound for Γ above zero, with WT and both single-mutant profiles as controls. Also report the stricter worst-case statistic `min(FOS-family) − max(other 48)` rather than hiding it in the mean. H1 predicts the primary point threshold but fails the strict worst-case criterion by 0.009. A falsifying result is Γ≤0, any target below the floor, or a non-positive lower confidence bound. Per-partner epistasis is `ε_b = observed double relative score − H1 prediction`; test whether those residuals differ systematically between the four FOS partners and the 48 off-targets. No exact wet-lab price was present in the source, so none is invented; dataset acquisition/reanalysis itself has no data fee.

The generic pair/epistasis premise is established prior art: Diss & Lehner 2018 measured >120,000 FOS/JUN trans mutation pairs and 17,688 FOS cis double amino-acid changes, showing thermodynamic and structural epistasis. Their peer-review response explicitly says JUN cis doubles were not assayed. Thus this proposal is **not** a claim to discover protein-pair epistasis or to improve general double-mutant prediction; its narrow contribution is a prospective JUN cis-double test of whether first-order residual addition can nominate a 52-partner FOS-family specificity profile. The 2024 JUN-wide map itself contains singles only.
