  Q>=min{ C r/epsilon, 1/(32r) }.                       (1.2)

The bound holds for every integer Q>=1, with identity branches when their

2. RANK-Q CHANNEL STRUCTURE AND EXACT SCHUR REDUCTION

If E_y has Kraus A_(y,alpha): H_in -> H_y and D_y has Kraus
B_(y,beta): H_y -> H_out, D E has Kraus B_(y,beta) A_(y,alpha), each of
matrix rank <=Q. Mixtures and independent seeds preserve such a Kraus
representation. Equivalently the Choi matrix has Schmidt number <=Q.
For continuous labels, the finite-dimensional closed convex cone of Choi
matrices with Schmidt number <=Q contains the corresponding integral;
it therefore also has a finite rank-Q pure-vector decomposition. No outcome
count is used in this converse. The converse only needs this necessary
structure; no characterization of the minimum classical alphabet is assumed.

The exact Schur model is

  R_v = direct-sum_j P_j tau_(j,v),
  tau_(j,v)=exp(beta J_v)/Tr exp(beta J_z),
  beta=log[(1+r)/(1-r)]=2 atanh(r),
  j=L/2, L=n,n-2,..., dimension d_j=2j+1.

The multiplicity factor is maximally mixed and parameter independent. It can
be discarded and regenerated exactly. Applying these zero-error channels
before/after an arbitrary proposed reconstruction cannot increase error or
Kraus rank. Pinching the output in its Schur sectors and tracing output
multiplicities likewise preserves rank-Q structure (each new Kraus operator
is obtained by multiplication by a row projection). Thus it suffices to
consider a rank-Q Kraus channel from the direct sum to itself.

For each input j, output k, and Kraus label z, let A_(kjz): V_j -> V_k.
Completeness gives sum_(k,z) A_(kjz)^dagger A_(kjz)=I_j. Define

  w_(jkz)=||A_(kjz)||_HS^2/d_j,   sum_(k,z) w_(jkz)=1.    (2.1)

These are true prior-averaged transition weights. No condition on the output
sector marginal is imposed. Twirling is optional, not necessary for the
proof: rotations multiply each A by unitaries and hence preserve rank Q,
and Haar averaging cannot increase the worst-case error. If used only as an
analytic reduction, its continuous seed is not a free implementation claim.

3. TWO EXACT THERMAL-RADIUS MONOTONICITIES

Set R_j=Tr(tau_(j,z) J_z), a_j=R_j/(j+1), and a_0=0. Then 0<a_j<1
for j>0. For all positive half-integer spins at the SAME beta:

  a_j increases strictly with j;
  a_j/j decreases strictly with j.                     (3.1)

First, the coherent-state likelihood at pole z is proportional to
(1+r x)^(2j), x=u.z in [-1,1]. Its conditional direction mean equals a_j:

  a_j = integral_-1^1 x(1+r x)^(2j) dx /
        integral_-1^1   (1+r x)^(2j) dx.                (3.2)

The identity follows either by elementary integration or by the vector
moment identity below. Increasing j gives a strict monotone likelihood ratio
in x, hence increases this mean. This also defines a smooth extension to
real j>=0, which is useful for the uniform derivative argument.

For the second assertion put t=beta/2=atanh(r), d=2j+1. The exact partition
function yields R_j=[d coth(dt)-coth(t)]/2. The standard partial-fraction
identity coth(x)=1/x+2x sum_(m>=1)(x^2+pi^2 m^2)^(-1) gives

  a_j/j = R_j/[j(j+1)]
    =4t sum_(m>=1) pi^2 m^2 /
       [(d^2 t^2+pi^2 m^2)(t^2+pi^2 m^2)].              (3.3)

Every positive summand decreases strictly in d. The series converges
absolutely and uniformly on compact positive t,d ranges. This proves the
second assertion without a large-spin approximation.

The exact first-harmonic identity, with normalized uniform prior dv on S^2,
is

  integral v_i tau_(j,v) dv = R_j J_i/[d_j j(j+1)].     (3.4)

Indeed the integral is a vector irreducible tensor in End(V_j), hence a
scalar multiple of J_i. Taking its trace against J_i and using
Tr J_i^2=d_j j(j+1)/3 fixes the coefficient. This ingredient is published
direction-estimation prior art (Bagan et al., quant-ph/0510158, III.1);
the subsequent rank-Q and sector-transport use is not claimed novel here.

4. SCHMIDT-RANK PROJECTOR / ENERGY LEMMA

On V_j tensor V_k let H=-sum_i J_i^(j) tensor J_i^(k), M=max(j,k),
m=min(j,k), delta=M-m. Its largest eigenvalue is jk+m. The total-spin
irreps have s=delta+l, l=0,...,2m, and their deficit below this maximum is

  E_l=l(2delta+l+1)/2.                                 (4.1)

The projection Pi_t onto l=0,...,t has dimension

  D_t=(t+1)(2delta+t+1),
  Tr_k Pi_t=(D_t/d_j)I_j, Tr_j Pi_t=(D_t/d_k)I_k.        (4.2)

The second identities follow from rotation invariance and irreducibility,
with the scalar fixed by the trace.

Every normalized Schmidt-rank<=Q vector psi obeys

  |psi><psi| <= Q rho_j tensor I_k,
  |psi><psi| <= Q I_j tensor rho_k.                     (4.3)

To check (4.3), write psi=sum_(u=1)^q sqrt(lambda_u)|u>|u'>, q<=Q.
Cauchy-Schwarz bounds its quadratic form by q sum_u lambda_u |z_(u,u')|^2,
which is at most Q times the quadratic form of rho_j tensor I_k. Therefore

  <psi|Pi_t|psi> <= min{1,Q D_t/(2M+1)}.                (4.4)

This is valid for every Schmidt-rank-Q vector, not merely a highest-weight
or tapered diagonal ansatz. Let g_Q(j,k) denote any proved lower bound on
the energy deficit (jk+m)-<H>. A general exact usable bound is

  g_Q(j,k)>=max_t E_(t+1) [1-Q D_t/(2M+1)]_+,          (4.5)

where t<2m. The weighted version summing spectral CDF deficits is also valid.

In particular, if delta<=M/(16Q) and M>=16Q, then

  g_Q(j,k)>=M/(32Q).                                  (4.6)

Proof: use energy cutoff E=M/(16Q), and let Pi contain all E_l<=E. For its
largest l=t, t<=sqrt(2E) and
D_t=2E_t+2delta+t+1<=2E+2delta+sqrt(2E)+1.
Write x=M/Q>=16. This is <=x/8+x/8+sqrt(x/8)+1<=x/2,
since sqrt(x/8)+1<=x/4 for x>=16. Thus (4.4) is <=1/4.
At least 3/4 of the state has energy deficit >E, giving deficit >=3M/(64Q),
which is stronger than (4.6). The result also holds for mixed states with
Schmidt number <=Q by convexity.

For equal spins the commutator form is the following noncommutative
Faber-Krahn inequality: if rank X<=Q<=j/16,

  sum_i ||[J_i,X]||_HS^2 >= j/(16Q) ||X||_HS^2.         (4.7)

Indeed vectorization and input time reversal identify half this energy with
the deficit of H below j(j+1). Constants are loose; rank 1 has exact minimum
2j, and a tapered rank-Q polar filter attains order j/Q.

5. CHANNEL-TO-SCORE NORMALIZATION AND SECTOR TRANSPORT

For output spin k>0 define the bounded direction-dependent score

  F_k(v)=a_k v.J^(k)/k - a_k^2 I_k/2; F_0=0.            (5.1)

Globally, -3/2 I<=F(v)<=1/2 I, since 0<=a_k<=1. The width is 2.
Consequently for any density outputs S_v and targets R_v,

  integral Tr F(v)[R_v-S_v] dv <= 2 e.                 (5.2)

This follows by adding 3I/2 and dividing by 2 to form an effect; the common
shift cancels because both states have trace one. A parameter-specific
interval [-1.5 a_k,.5 a_k] would be false; only the correct global interval
is used here.

The target score contribution per input j>0 is

  T_j=a_j R_j/j-a_j^2/2=a_j^2/2+a_j^2/j.               (5.3)

For a nonzero Kraus A: V_j -> V_k, let its normalized vectorized Choi vector
be |A>>/||A||_HS. Its Schmidt rank is rank A<=Q. Let
h_A=sum_i Tr(A J_i^(j) A^dagger J_i^(k))/||A||_HS^2.
In the Choi convention this is the expectation of sum J_i^T tensor J_i.
The standard spin time-reversal unitary sends every J_i^T to -J_i; therefore
h_A is the expectation of H of Section 4, with the SAME sign. Equations
(2.1) and (3.4) give exactly

  integral Tr[A tau_(j,v) A^dagger F_k(v)] dv
     = w_(jkz) [a_j a_k h_A/(jk)-a_k^2/2].             (5.4)

No Choi transpose is omitted. For j=0 or k=0 the vector term is zero.
By (4.1)-(4.6), h_A<=jk+min(j,k)-g_Q(j,k). Subtracting its score upper
bound from (5.3) yields the nonnegative pair deficit

  Delta_(jk) >= (a_k-a_j)^2/2
      + a_j^2/j - a_j a_k/max(j,k)
      + a_j a_k g_Q(j,k)/(jk).                         (5.5)

The middle term is nonnegative: when k<=j use a_k<=a_j; when k>=j use
a_k/k<=a_j/j. For k=0 simply Delta_(j0)=T_j>=a_j^2/2.
For j=0, the actual output score is -w a_k^2/2<=0, while target score is 0.
Thus EVERY input/output sector pair has nonnegative target deficit, even
without any output-marginal constraint. This is the key transport repair:
one sector cannot pay for a positive error in another sector.

  Delta_(jk)>=a_j a_k/[32Q min(j,k)]>=r/(240 B Q).      (6.2)

FAR OUTPUT: delta>M/(16Q). Put x=2r j and extend a smoothly as in (3.2).
In variables x,r its exact expression is

  A(x,r)=[(x+r)coth((x+r)atanh(r)/r)-r coth(atanh(r))]
           /(x+2r).                                  (6.3)

On every compact positive x interval, A and its x derivative converge

  L(x)=coth x-1/x,  L'(x)=1/x^2-csch(x)^2>0.           (6.4)

Uniform convergence follows directly by differentiating (6.3):
atanh(r)/r->1 and r coth(atanh(r))=1 exactly. There is no n-dependent
approximation premise. Let

  m_B=min_(x in [1,2B]) L'(x)>0.

Choose r0(B)>0 so that A_x(x,r)>=m_B/2 on [1,2B] for r<=r0(B).
This choice is independent of Q. Write s=1/(16Q)<=1/16. If k>=j, far

  |a_k-a_j|>=m_B/(16Q),
  Delta_(jk)>=m_B^2/(512Q^2)>=m_B^2 r/(16Q).            (6.5)

The last inequality uses Q<=1/(32r). Set

  c_B=min{1/(240B), m_B^2/16}.

Equations (6.2)-(6.5) prove, for EVERY output spin k,

  Delta_(jk)>=c_B r/Q   whenever 1/r<=j<=B/(2r).        (6.6)

7. POSITIVE BAND MASS AND CONCLUSION

The exact Schur law has L=2j. With measurement in the true direction, its
joint spin/magnetization law satisfies L>=|S_n|, where S_n is a sum of n
independent +/-1 variables of mean r. Let c=r sqrt(n) in [c0,c1]. The
uniform bounded-summand central limit theorem gives a fixed alpha>0 and
n0, depending only on c0,c1, such that

  P(L>=2/r)>=alpha.

Explicitly the standardized threshold for S_n>=2/r is
(2/c-c)/sqrt(1-r^2), which is bounded above uniformly on this compact
c interval; choose a positive Gaussian tail below its infimum and absorb
the Berry-Esseen O(n^(-1/2)) error and the lattice rounding. The equality
case at a nonlattice 2/r is interpreted through the next attainable L.

The exact Casimir identity is E[L(L+2)]=3n+n(n-1)r^2. Hence

  P(L>B/r)<=[3nr^2+n(n-1)r^4]/B^2
            <=(3c1^2+c1^4)/B^2.

Choose B>=2 with the last bound <=alpha/2. Then
P(1/r<=j<=B/(2r))>=alpha/2. All pairs outside this band have nonnegative
score deficit. Using (2.1), (5.2), and (6.6),

  2e >= sum_j P_j sum_(k,z) w_(jkz) Delta_(jk)
      >=(alpha/2)c_B r/Q.

Thus (1.1) holds with C=alpha c_B/4, for sufficiently small r and the
corresponding sufficiently large n. For Q>1/(32r), the trivial dimension
alternative gives (1.2). This closes the FIXED-Q gate directly: no bounded
quantum register plus unlimited classical labels can retain all order-r
quantum residual to error o(r) in the critical full-density experiment.

9. TAPERED RANK-Q COVARIANT INSTRUMENT: EXACT CODE AND ENERGY

Fix a spin j>0 and an integer 1<=Q<d=2j+1. In the polar occupation basis
|j,j-k>, k=0,...,2j, define the positive operator F with

  f_k=(1-k/Q)_+,   s=Tr F^2=(Q+1)(2Q+1)/(6Q)>=Q/3.     (9.1)

Its rank is Q. For F_U=U F U^dagger, normalized Haar measure dU, define

  Phi_j(X)=(d/s) integral F_U X F_U dU.                 (9.2)

Since integral F_U^2 dU=(s/d)I, this map is completely positive, trace
preserving, unital, and SU(2)-covariant. It has a legal Q-dimensional
instrument: outcome U has encoder Kraus sqrt(d/s) F U^dagger, with the
range of F identified with C^Q, and decoder embeds that C^Q and applies U.
No direction or radius is measured as a supplied parameter. In particular
F, the code, and the quadrature below depend only on j,Q, not on beta or r.
When Q>=d use the identity channel with one label and quantum dimension d.

Let E(F)=sum_i ||[J_i,F]||_HS^2. The ladder coefficients give exactly

  E(F)=Q^(-2) sum_(k=1)^Q k(2j-k+1),
  E(F)/s=3(2j+1)/(2Q+1)-1 <=6j/Q.                    (9.3)

The first formula includes the edge k=Q, where f_Q=0. Omitting that edge
would give a false improvement. Q<d ensures it is an actual ladder edge.
For Q=1 the formula gives E(F)=2j, as required by the coherent EB channel.
Thus the energy order j/Q matching the lower spectral cutoff is attained
by an explicit filter; no assertion of globally optimal filter is needed.

10. FULL TRACE-DISTANCE UPPER, WITHOUT A MULTIPOLE CONJECTURE

For S=J_z/j, ||S||_op=1, the double commutator identity is

  Phi_j(X)-X=-(d/(2s)) integral [F_U,[F_U,X]] dU.        (10.1)

Rotation covariance and the vector irrep imply

  integral [F_U,[F_U,S]] dU=E(F)S/[d j(j+1)],
  integral ||[F_U,S]||_HS^2 dU=E(F)/(3j^2).             (10.2)

For the first coefficient, pair against S. Its left trace is the second
quantity, while Tr S^2=d(j+1)/(3j). This also checks the exact dipole
eigenvalue 1-lambda_1=E(F)/[2s j(j+1)].

For every integer m>=1, apply twice the derivation D_U=[F_U, .] to the
ordered product S^m. The terms with both derivations on the same factor
sum to integral m E(F) S^m/[d j(j+1)]. The m(m-1) ordered cross terms
contain two factors D_U(S), separated and flanked by contractions S^a.
Schatten Holder gives trace norm at most ||D_U(S)||_HS^2 for EACH cross
term, independently of how far apart the two differentiated factors lie.
Using (10.1)-(10.2) and ||S^m||_1<=d therefore proves

  ||Phi_j(S^m)-S^m||_1
     <=d E(F)/(2s) [ m/[j(j+1)] + m(m-1)/(3j^2) ].     (10.3)

This is a full trace-norm estimate, not only a first-moment estimate. Put
h=beta j>=0 and tau=exp(hS)/Z. Since Tr S=0, Jensen gives Z>=d. The
thermal series converges absolutely in trace norm in this finite dimension;
sum m h^m/m!=h e^h, sum m(m-1)h^m/m!=h^2 e^h. Thus

  (1/2)||Phi_j(tau)-tau||_1
    <= E(F)/(4s j^2) (h+h^2/3)e^h
    <= [3/(2Q)] (beta+beta^2 j/3)e^(beta j).            (10.4)

The first inequality uses j(j+1)>=j^2. By covariance it holds for every
direction v. The identity branches have zero error. This establishes the
upper at whole-state reconstruction precision, rather than inference from
a dipole contraction alone.

There is a separate direct check using the diagonal stochastic kernel of
Phi_j. It is doubly stochastic and sum_n M_(mn)n=lambda_1 m; consequently
sum_(mn) M_(mn)(n-m)^2=2(1-lambda_1)d j(j+1)/3. Taylor expansion of
exp(beta n) at m gives another valid upper
e_j<=[beta j/2+beta^2 j(j+1)e^(3beta j)/6](1-lambda_1).
This looser exponential is also integrable under the Schur law. It is not
needed in (10.4), but independently checks the mechanism and normalization.

11. EXACT FINITE ALPHABET AND UNIFORM SCHUR AVERAGE

The continuous Haar instrument can be replaced EXACTLY by a finite one.
As a function of a rotation U, the superoperator
X -> F_U X F_U has only integer SU(2) matrix coefficients of spin ell<=4j:
Ad_U on End(V_j) contains ell=0,...,2j, and its superoperator conjugation
has tensor-product spins at most 4j. In Euler angles alpha,theta,gamma,
these coefficients have form exp(-im alpha) d^ell_(mn)(theta) exp(-in gamma).
Since F commutes with J_z, the superoperator is unchanged under a right
z rotation and has only n=0 coefficients. Set gamma=0, take alpha uniformly
on the N=4j+1 equally spaced angles in [0,2pi), and cos(theta) at q=d=2j+1
Gauss-Legendre nodes with normalized weights (ordinary weights divided by 2).
The discrete angle sum kills every nonzero integer m with |m|<=4j<N.
For the surviving m=n=0,
d^ell_(00)(theta)=P_ell(cos theta), of degree ell<=4j; the q-node Gaussian
quadrature is exact through degree 2q-1=4j+1. Thus this finite positive
quadrature gives exactly (9.2), including its trace-preserving identity.

There are at most

  C_j=(4j+1)(2j+1)

outcomes in sector j; each encoder Kraus has rank Q and is multiplied by
the square root of its positive quadrature weight. Half-integer U lifts
cause no ambiguity because the filter conjugation is insensitive to the
central sign. For Q>=d use a single identity outcome. The complete Schur
code first reads j, applies this instrument, and retains (j,quadrature index)
and its quantum state. Decoder applies the indicated isometry, regenerates
the parameter-independent maximally mixed multiplicity, and inverts Schur.
Total classical labels C_cl<=sum_j[(4j+1)(2j+1)+1]=O(n^3), max quantum
dimension Q, total D<=Q C_cl. Thus classical label storage is at most
3 log_2(n+1)+O(1) bits, quantum storage ceil(log_2 Q) qubits. These label
costs are part of the construction, not a free continuous-direction resource.

It remains to average (10.4) at all spin sizes; a fixed bounded-h cutoff is
unnecessary. For L=2j, exact multiplicity m_L=2(L+1)/(n+L+2) binom(n,k),
k=(n-L)/2, and t=atanh r, the exact Schur law gives

  P_L=m_L (ab)^(n/2) sinh((L+1)t)/sinh t
     <= [2(L+1)^2/n] pi_0(L) e^(tL),                  (11.1)

where a=(1+r)/2, b=(1-r)/2, pi_0(L)=2^(-n) binom(n,k).
Indeed (ab)^(n/2)<=2^(-n), m_L<=2(L+1)binom(n,k)/n, and
sinh((L+1)t)/sinh t=sum_(u=0)^L exp[(L-2u)t]<=(L+1)e^(tL).
The formula has its continuous interpretation at r=0.

Let X be a sum of n unbiased +/-1 variables. Its mgf obeys
E exp(uX)<=exp(nu^2/2), E X^4<=3n^2, E X^6<=15n^3. Cauchy-Schwarz
and (11.1) give the explicit bounds

  E_P e^(tL) <=4(sqrt(3)+1)e^(4nt^2)=:C_0,
  E_P[rj e^(tL)] <=2r sqrt(n)(sqrt(15)+1)e^(4nt^2)=:C_1.
                                                               (11.2)

For example use (|X|+1)^2<=2(X^2+1) in the first line; in the second
line bound (X+1)^2 X on X>=0 by 2(|X|^3+|X|), remembering j=L/2.
For r<=1/2, t<=4r/3 and r sqrt(n)<=c1. Consequently one may take

  C_0=4(sqrt(3)+1) exp(64c1^2/9),
  C_1=2c1(sqrt(15)+1) exp(64c1^2/9).

Since beta=2t<=8r/3, averaging (10.4) proves

  e <= [4C_0+(32/9)C_1] r/Q =: C_up(c1) r/Q.           (11.3)

This is uniform in all directions and requires no positive lower radius.
At r=0 the code is exact because each Phi_j is unital. Hence precisely the
same radius-independent encoder/decoder on the entire critical Bloch ball
||b||<=c1/sqrt(n) has error <=C_up(c1)c1/[sqrt(n)Q]. Restriction to one
fixed critical sphere gives the matching lower order for Q<=c sqrt(n).

The ideal mathematical CPTP code has an explicit finite positive design and
no uncharged acquisition oracle. Preparing its algebraic quadrature nodes,
rotations and Kraus dilations, and implementing the Schur transform, are
computation resources. This file does not prove an optimized circuit gate
count or condition-number bound. A circuit approximation of total channel
diamond error delta contributes at most delta/2 to the half-trace error;
hardware/gate precision must be chosen for delta=O(epsilon). Exact finite
memory dimension counts above do not imply exact finite gate synthesis.

12. DISTINCT SINGLE-SPIN CONSEQUENCE AND REMAINING GATES

Consider the single-spin experiment tau_(j,v) proportional to
exp[(h/j)J_v], with supplied h in a fixed positive compact interval and
unknown v. It has no Schur-sector distribution. For Q<=j/16, Section 4
and the same score, normalized using its exact width 2a_j, give

  e_Q^*>=a_j/(64jQ).

Here a_j=R_j/(j+1)->coth h-1/h uniformly, bounded away from zero. The
filter in (9.2) and (10.4) gives e_Q^*<=C_h/(jQ). Thus

  e_Q^*(j,h)=Theta_(h0,h1)(1/(jQ)), 1<=Q<=j/16,

with O(j^2) finite classical labels and an exactly specified Q branch.
This is a genuine whole-state quantum-memory consequence of the rank-energy
argument, not a general-qudit collision assertion. Its prior-art status is
UNKNOWN pending a dedicated source audit.

sqrt(n), a finite radius-independent tapered code, and full-ball extension.
