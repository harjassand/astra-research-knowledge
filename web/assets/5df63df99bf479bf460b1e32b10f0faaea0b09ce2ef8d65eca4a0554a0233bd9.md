# Chemical reaction-network identifiability: quantifier repair and partial-sensor theorem

**Status:** primary-source statement/proof audit complete; correction independently reconstructed; partial-observation result is an internally proved, model-conditional extension; novelty and external mathematical review remain open.

## Executive finding

The published Theorem 3.3(2) in Faul–Hoessly–Xia, arXiv:2505.07638v3 and DOI 10.1017/S0956792526100382, has a quantifier error. Its Theorem 5.15 and proof in §7.3 establish that the networks are confoundable exactly when the source sets agree and the two source cones intersect at **every** source. Negating this statement gives unconfoundability when source supports differ, or, when they agree, when the cones are disjoint at **at least one** source. Theorem 3.3(2) instead says they must be disjoint at every source.

The supplied example — `0→A, A→0` versus `0→2A, A→0` — is a direct counterexample to the printed criterion. The source-0 cone intersection is empty, while the source-A cones coincide. The diffusion generators nevertheless cannot agree for any positive rates, so the networks are unconfoundable. The paper’s own confoundability theorem implies this corrected conclusion.

The sourcewise decomposition also yields a concrete extension to partial species readout under controlled, known initial concentrations. For a sensor map $H$, replace each jump signature $(v,vv^T)$ by its observed signature $(Hv,(Hv)(Hv)^T)$. Equality of the local observed drift/covariance response fields then decomposes source by source exactly as in the full-observation proof. A single disjoint projected source cone guarantees a distinguishing initial-concentration intervention, even when only $HX$ is measured. I prove a finite-design/noisy-oracle version and give an A/B catalyst example: observing A alone and testing two known B setpoints separates two networks whose deterministic ODEs are confoundable.

This extension is about **local conditional characteristics under full-state intervention**, not a general Markov or output-law theorem for hidden-state projections. The projected process $HX_t$ need not be Markov. A disjoint projected cone gives a sufficient, finite intervention-based distinction; intersections at every source only say local characteristics can be matched and do not imply equal partial-output path laws.

## 1. Primary-source audit

The arXiv v3 PDF (31 March 2026) and Cambridge first-view article use the same definitions and problematic quantifier. Definition 3.2 defines unconfoundability by universal separation over bounded open regions, initial states, and positive rates; its negation gives a confoundability witness on some region/initial state/rate pair. Theorem 3.3(2) states that, for networks with the same source complexes, unconfoundability holds iff the cones are disjoint **for every** source. The cone definition uses strictly positive reaction-rate coefficients. Source locations: arXiv v3 PDF lines 303–365 (Definition 3.2 and Theorem 3.3), 1012–1017 (Theorem 5.15), and 1381–1569 (§7.3 proof); published Cambridge HTML lines 619–632 and 908–920.

The load-bearing proof is §7.3, not the theorem sentence. Starting from equal generators, the authors equate polynomial drift and covariance fields. Their Lemma 7.2 (distinct monomials are linearly independent on an open set) implies equality of the drift/covariance coefficient pair separately at each source. That pair belongs to both source cones, so every source intersection is nonempty. Conversely, if every source intersection is nonempty, §7.3 chooses positive rate subvectors independently for each source, assembles them into global rate vectors, and sums the matching source coefficients to obtain equal generators. Thus:

\[
\text{generator-confoundable}
\iff
\text{same source support and }\forall y,\quad
K_{\mathcal N}(y)\cap K_{\mathcal N'}(y)\ne\varnothing.
\]

Negation gives:

\[
\text{generator-unconfoundable}
\iff
\text{different source support, or (same support and) }\exists y,\quad
K_{\mathcal N}(y)\cap K_{\mathcal N'}(y)=\varnothing.
\]

Proposition 7.1 transfers the generator conclusion to the paper’s SDE-law notion under its local uniqueness/martingale-problem hypotheses. The paper says in its conclusion that all species are observed and identifies partial observation as a future direction; this extension therefore treats a real stated boundary of the source result.

### Independent exact counterexample

For a one-species state $a>0$, let

\[
\mathcal N: 0\xrightarrow{\alpha} A,\quad A\xrightarrow{\beta}0,
\qquad
\mathcal N': 0\xrightarrow{\alpha'}2A,\quad A\xrightarrow{\beta'}0,
\]

with all rates positive. Both source supports are \(\{0,A\}\).

At source $0$, the reaction signatures are
\[
(1,1) \quad\text{and}\quad (2,4),
\]
where the entries are drift jump and diffusion-variance jump. Hence
\[
K_{\mathcal N}(0)=\{(s,s):s>0\},\qquad
K_{\mathcal N'}(0)=\{(2t,4t):t>0\}.
\]
An intersection would require $s=2t=4t$, impossible for $t>0$. At source $A$, both networks have the signature $(-1,1)$, so
\[
K_{\mathcal N}(A)=K_{\mathcal N'}(A)=\{(-u,u):u>0\}.
\]
The source-A intersection is nonempty. Thus the printed “for every source disjoint” condition fails, while the correct existential condition holds.

The same conclusion follows without invoking the cone theorem. The Langevin drift and variance fields are
\[
A_{\mathcal N}(a)=\alpha-\beta a,\quad B_{\mathcal N}(a)=\alpha+\beta a,
\]
\[
A_{\mathcal N'}(a)=2\alpha'-\beta'a,\quad B_{\mathcal N'}(a)=4\alpha'+\beta'a.
\]
Equality on an open interval forces equality of the constant coefficients, hence both \(\alpha=2\alpha'\) from drift and \(\alpha=4\alpha'\) from variance. This contradicts positive \(\alpha'\). The networks are therefore unconfoundable. The loss coefficients can match with \(\beta=\beta'>0\), so the lone obstruction is exactly the source-0 mismatch.

## 2. Partial-observation sourcewise theorem

Let $\mathcal N,\mathcal N'$ be finite mass-action networks on the same $n$ species, with positive rate constants. Let $H\in\mathbb R^{p\times n}$ be a known linear readout. The full initial state $x\in\mathbb R^n_{>0}$ is known and can be set at the start of an experiment; only $HX_t$ is read during the short observation window. Define the local observed response
\[
F_H^{\mathcal N,\kappa}(x)
=\left(HA_{\mathcal N,\kappa}(x),\;\operatorname{vech}(HB_{\mathcal N,\kappa}(x)H^T)\right),
\]
where $A$ and $B$ are the Langevin drift and covariance fields. This is the conditional infinitesimal mean and covariance response of the observed coordinates given the controlled full initial state. It is well-defined even if the projected process is not Markov.

For a reaction jump $v=y'-y$, define its readout signature
\[
\psi_H(v)=\left(Hv,\;\operatorname{vech}((Hv)(Hv)^T)\right).
\]
For each source $y$, define
\[
K_{\mathcal N}^H(y)=\left\{\sum_{r:y\to y'\in\mathcal R}\alpha_r\psi_H(y'-y):\alpha_r>0\right\},
\]
and set $K_{\mathcal N}^H(y)=\{0\}$ if $y$ is not a source. Zero projected jumps are allowed and produce zero signatures.

**Theorem (projected local-response criterion).** Let $Y$ be the union of the two source supports. For fixed positive rate vectors, the fields $F_H^{\mathcal N,\kappa}$ and $F_H^{\mathcal N',\kappa'}$ agree on any nonempty open set of controlled full concentrations iff their source coefficient vectors agree for every $y\in Y$. There exist positive rate vectors for which these fields agree iff
\[
K_{\mathcal N}^H(y)\cap K_{\mathcal N'}^H(y)\ne\varnothing
\quad\text{for every }y\in Y.
\]
Consequently, no positive rate vectors match the full local response field on an open intervention region iff at least one source $y\in Y$ has disjoint projected cones.

**Proof.** Group the mass-action expressions by source:
\[
F_H^{\mathcal N,\kappa}(x)=\sum_{y\in Y}x^y c_{\mathcal N,H,\kappa}(y),\qquad
c_{\mathcal N,H,\kappa}(y)=\sum_{r:y\to y'}\kappa_r\psi_H(y'-y).
\]
The difference of the two fields is a vector-valued polynomial in $x$. Distinct source complexes are distinct monomial exponents; if this polynomial vanishes on a nonempty open set, each coefficient vanishes. This proves the fixed-rate statement. The rate variables for distinct source complexes are disjoint, so a common coefficient can be selected at every source exactly when every projected cone intersection is nonempty. Combining those independently selected subvectors gives positive global rate vectors. Negating proves the final statement. $\square$

Unlike the full-state theorem, this criterion treats different source supports through the union $Y$ and the convention $K(y)=\{0\}$ for a missing source. If all reactions at a source are invisible under $H$, that source contributes zero and a support difference there may not distinguish the measured response. This is a genuine sensor limitation.

### Finite noisy intervention algorithm

The continuous open-set condition can be replaced by finitely many controlled concentrations. Let $m=|Y|$, and let $W_{jy}=x_j^y$ be the monomial evaluation matrix.

1. Choose $m$ admissible positive rational states $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ so $W$ is nonsingular. Such states exist in every nonempty rational open control box: otherwise the evaluation rows would span a proper subspace, and a nonzero linear combination of the monomials would vanish throughout that box. A terminating exact algorithm enumerates rational states in the box and appends a row whenever its exact rational rank increases.
2. At each $x_j$, estimate $F_H(x_j)$. Stack these $m$ response vectors as rows of a matrix $Z$. Since $Z=WC$, recover the source coefficients by $\widehat C=W^{-1}\widehat Z$. If each pointwise estimate has Euclidean error at most $\eta$, then
\[
\|\widehat C-C\|_F\le \sqrt m\,\|W^{-1}\|_2\eta.
\]
3. For a source witness $y_0$, suppose both models' per-reaction rates lie in a supplied closed interval $[\kappa_{\min},\kappa_{\max}]$ with $\kappa_{\min}>0$. The possible source coefficient sets $P,P'$ are compact linear images of rate boxes. If the projected positive cones are disjoint then $P\cap P'=\varnothing$, so their distance $\delta=\operatorname{dist}(P,P')$ is positive. Classify $\widehat c(y_0)$ by nearest set. It is correct whenever
\[
\sqrt m\,\|W^{-1}\|_2\eta<\delta/2.
\]

The margin is computable from the supplied stoichiometries, $H$, and rate box: it is the minimum Euclidean distance between two linear images of boxes, a convex quadratic/SOCP calculation. A certified lower bound on $\delta$ suffices. Without a positive lower rate bound, uniform robust discrimination is impossible in this local-response interface: scaling both networks' rates toward zero sends their response fields toward zero even when their positive cones are disjoint. This records the precision/rate cost instead of silently equating structural identifiability with practical identifiability.

For exact rational stoichiometry and rational $H$, each cone-intersection check is a strict linear feasibility problem. Normalize the sum of positive coefficients to one and maximize a common lower bound $t$ on every coefficient. A positive optimum is equivalent to intersection. Empty-source and zero-projected-jump cases are handled directly. Testing a supplied sensor panel takes one such LP per source. Searching for a smallest coordinate sensor panel can enumerate all $2^n$ panels and stop at the first separating panel; this is finite but exponential in species count and is not presented as a scalable optimizer.

## 3. Concrete partial-sensor experiment

Take species A and B, observe A only ($H=(1,0)$), and compare
\[
\mathcal N: B\xrightarrow{k} A+B,\quad A\xrightarrow{\mu}0,
\qquad
\mathcal N': B\xrightarrow{\lambda}2A+B,\quad A\xrightarrow{\mu'}0.
\]
The B-catalysed production reaction leaves B unchanged, so $b$ is a known controlled substrate/catalyst level even though B is not a measured output. At input $x=(a,b)$, the A-only local drift/variance pairs are
\[
F_H^{\mathcal N}(a,b)=(kb-\mu a,\;kb+\mu a),
\qquad
F_H^{\mathcal N'}(a,b)=(2\lambda b-\mu'a,\;4\lambda b+\mu'a).
\]
At source B the projected signatures generate the disjoint rays $\{(s,s):s>0\}$ and $\{(2t,4t):t>0\}$. At source A both models have the common ray $\{(-u,u):u>0\}$. Thus one source witness suffices.

Use two interventions with the same $a=1$ and $b=1,2$. Subtract the measured local-response vectors. The A-loss term cancels, leaving $(k,k)$ under $\mathcal N$ and $(2\lambda,4\lambda)$ under $\mathcal N'$. These cannot be equal for positive $k,\lambda$. In the equally informed deterministic-ODE baseline, choose $k=2\lambda$ and $\mu=\mu'$; the measured A drift then agrees for every $(a,b)$. The Langevin variance slope in $b$ is $k$ versus $4\lambda$, so second-order fluctuations resolve this confounding using only the A sensor and a controlled B input.

For a quantitative noisy version, restrict $k,\lambda,\mu,\mu'\in[1,2]$ in dimensionless time/concentration units. The source-B coefficient sets are
\[
P=\{(k,k):1\le k\le2\},\qquad P'=\{(2\lambda,4\lambda):1\le\lambda\le2\}.
\]
Their Euclidean distance is exactly $2$: the variance-coordinate difference is at least $4\lambda-k\ge2$, with equality at $k=2,\lambda=1$. If each of the two estimated local-response pairs has worst-case Euclidean error at most $\eta$, their difference has error at most $2\eta$; nearest-set classification is guaranteed for $2\eta<1$, i.e. $\eta<1/2$. The experiment costs two controlled concentration settings, $R$ independent short trajectories per setting, one measured species, and four scalar local-characteristic estimates (drift and variance at each setting). The theorem does not supply $R$: it must be set from a validated finite-window/readout-noise error model. The protocol assumes known controlled $a,b$, positive rates within the stated interval, the Langevin approximation over the measurement window, and access to enough replicate trajectories to estimate the short-time drift and quadratic variation.

## 4. Prior-art and novelty attack

- **Direct source, exact interface.** Faul, Hoessly & Xia, *Identifiability of SDEs for reaction networks*, arXiv:2505.07638v3 (31 March 2026), published first view 23 April 2026, DOI 10.1017/S0956792526100382. Their §§2–3 define the mass-action Langevin approximation and all-species unconfoundability; Thm 5.15 and §7.3 prove sourcewise intersection for confoundability. Their conclusion explicitly assumes all species are observed and names partial observation as an open direction. The published Cambridge HTML repeats the bad universal quantifier in Thm 3.3(2), while §7.3 still proves the all-intersections confoundability statement. A targeted search did not surface an author correction/erratum; it was not exhaustive.
- **Few-species deterministic identifiability.** Jeronimo, Pérez Millán & Solernó, *Identifiability from a few species for a class of biochemical reaction networks*, arXiv:1811.08336v2 / DOI 10.1007/s11538-019-00594-0, proves that selected species and successive derivatives identify parameters for restricted phosphorylation and cascade classes. It prevents any broad novelty claim for partial-output reaction-network identifiability; the present result is instead an arbitrary finite network-pair criterion for projected Langevin local characteristics under controlled full initial concentrations.
- **Stationary diffusion data.** Grunberg & Del Vecchio, *Identifiability of Chemical Reaction Networks with Intrinsic and Extrinsic Noise from Stationary Distributions*, SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 22 (2023), 2206–2241, supplies a computational LNA/stationary-distribution identifiability framework. Its data interface differs from intervention-conditioned local drift/covariance fields.
- **Experiment design.** Existing stochastic-kinetics design work optimizes Fisher-information criteria for parameter precision. The proposed test is a model-discrimination certificate for two candidate structures and an explicitly charged observation panel/setpoint design. I did not establish priority over all optimal-design or partial-observation literature.

The partial theorem follows from the paper’s source decomposition by applying $H$ to the drift and covariance channels, so its proof is short. It is a credible conditional extension, not yet a demonstrated field discovery or strong priority claim. Cycle 2 derives a conservative finite-window, bounded-readout theorem for repeated starts and applies it to the A/B variance-slope witness; see [the cycle-2 proof and prior-art attack](cycle2/FINITE_SAMPLE_REPORT.md). Unknown dynamic hidden initial states and field-level feasibility remain open.

## 5. Costs, model boundary, and decisive next gate

**Supplied information:** candidate reaction graphs, species identities, mass-action monomial sources, sensor matrix $H$, admissible open intervention region, and rate interval for a robust guarantee. The intervention sets the full initial concentration; hidden species are not inferred from the output.

**Acquired data:** estimated A/drift and covariance (quadratic variation) responses at each chosen initial concentration. In the worked example this is two B setpoints and repeated A-only short-time trajectories. Any readout noise, finite-window bias, calibration, failed preparations, and replicate count must be added before claiming empirical capability.

**Computation:** $O(|Y|)$ strict LP checks for a fixed sensor panel; exact rational monomial-rank search and matrix inversion (dense arithmetic $O(|Y|^3)$ field operations); a convex distance problem for the robust margin. Sensor-panel minimization by exhaustive enumeration costs up to $2^n$ panel checks. Numerical conditioning of $W$, feasible concentration ranges, and bit precision can dominate; the theorem guarantees existence in an open box, not a well-conditioned design.

**Scope:** a mathematical theorem about Langevin-model local conditional characteristics. It does not prove the actual chemical system follows that diffusion, does not prove equality of observed path laws from cone intersections, and does not infer hidden initial conditions. The practical falsifier is to compare the two prespecified structures over the two interventions with independent calibration and a confidence interval for the local drift/variance contrast: if the contrast fails to exclude zero at the predicted bounded-rate margin despite validated measurement error below the stated threshold, the experimental bridge or model assumptions fail.

**Next gate:** obtain independent CRNT/SDE review, validate instrument/reset/stop bounds, and run a matched simulation comparing the variance-slope rule, stopped-CIR robust likelihood, and ODE-only baseline under identical data costs. The finite-$h$ theorem now exists, but its stated instance costs 12 million fresh starts and assumes a calibrated $10^{-6}$ endpoint-noise envelope; it is not a demonstrated practical experiment. External review, empirical execution, and novelty/priority remain unknown.

The total is 12 million starts, at least 24 million endpoint reads, plus continuous A-channel safety monitoring. These are assumed instrument guarantees, not measured values.

The new finite-data result is therefore a valid conditional guarantee but no inference-advantage claim.
