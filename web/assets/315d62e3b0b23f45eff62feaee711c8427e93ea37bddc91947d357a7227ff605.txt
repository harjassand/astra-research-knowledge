CYCLE 07 — π-CONSTANT PRIOR CHECK (bounded primary-source comparison)

Scope
Compare only these supplied candidate statements with checked primary sources:
(i) the stationary Hessian inequality J2 >= pi E2 for finite KMS-symmetric QMS, with pi claimed sharp even for primitive examples; and
(ii) the global qubit inequality J(rho) >= pi E(rho) for arbitrary faithful stationary state and KMS-symmetric QMS, without a GNS assumption.
This is a source comparison, not a proof or priority determination. No candidate proofs were consulted.

Closest exact interface: Kastoryano–Temme

Kastoryano and Temme, “Quantum logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and rapid mixing,” arXiv:1207.3261v2 (12 June 2013), especially Definition 6, Proposition 8, Definition 9, Conjecture 19, and Theorem 20:
https://arxiv.org/html/1207.3261v2

Their Heisenberg generator L is detailed-balanced when Gamma_sigma L = L* Gamma_sigma (Def. 6), where Gamma_sigma(X)=sigma^(1/2) X sigma^(1/2); this is the KMS-weighted symmetry needed for the following change of variables. For positive f, their p=1 and p=2 Dirichlet forms are
  E_1(f) = -(1/2) Tr[Gamma_sigma(L f)(log(Gamma_sigma f)-log sigma)]  (Prop. 8, Eq. 26),
  E_2(g) = -<g,Lg>_sigma,  <g,h>_sigma=Tr[Gamma_sigma(g)h]             (Prop. 8, Eq. 25).
Their p=1 weak-regularity clause is E_1(f) >= E_2(I_{2,1}(f)) (Def. 9, Eq. 33); the strong clause has right side (2/p)E_2, hence 2E_2 at p=1.

For a faithful state rho, set f=Gamma_sigma^(-1)(rho). Detailed balance gives Gamma_sigma L(f)=L_*(rho), so E_1(f)=J(rho)/2 for J(rho)=-Tr[L_*(rho)(log rho-log sigma)]. Also I_{2,1}(f)=Gamma_sigma^(-1/2)(sqrt(rho)). Under the root-coordinate transform Q=Gamma_sigma^(-1/2) L_* Gamma_sigma^(1/2)=Gamma_sigma^(1/2) L Gamma_sigma^(-1/2), E_2(I_{2,1}f)=-Tr[sqrt(rho) Q(sqrt(rho))], exactly the corresponding root Dirichlet form. Thus the published weak p=1 endpoint would imply J >= 2E, and the strong p=1 endpoint would imply J >= 4E, in these conventions. A candidate J >= pi E is strictly stronger than the weak endpoint and weaker than the strong endpoint.

Crucially, the general claim is not a theorem in this source: Conjecture 19 proposes weak Lp-regularity for every primitive Liouvillian and strong Lp-regularity when it is reversible. Its weak statement covers every p, so it contains the p=1 comparison as one endpoint; it does not establish that endpoint. The source proves regularity for particular classes: primitive unital Liouvillians are weakly regular, primitive reversible unital ones are strongly regular (lines/equations 80–81); and bounded thermal/Davies generators are strongly regular (Theorem 20, Eqs. 84–93). Those classes give, respectively, J>=2E or J>=4E through the exact mapping above, but do not prove the pi bound for arbitrary non-GNS KMS qubits. The source’s p=1 formulas also concern positive definite f; extension to boundary states is by continuity if the target formulation includes singular rho.

Stationary Hessian is not matched by the source’s spectral-gap expansion

Kastoryano–Temme expand their LS1 entropy and production near stationarity in Eqs. 60–65, then derive alpha_1 <= the spectral gap in Eq. 68. The quadratic metric in that expansion is Xi_sigma, with eigenvalues xi_ab=sqrt(sigma_a sigma_b) log(sigma_b/sigma_a)/(sigma_b-sigma_a) (Eq. 63). Their resulting comparison is Xi_sigma-weighted and concerns an LS1 constant versus a spectral gap. It is not the candidate Hilbert–Schmidt quadratic form J2/E2 in root coordinates, and those equations supply no conversion to the constant pi. A nontrivial metric/tangent conversion would be needed before treating it as evidence for or against the stated stationary theorem.

Nearby entropic theorem with a different symmetry hypothesis

Gao–Rouzé, “Complete Entropic Inequalities for Quantum Markov Chains,” arXiv:2102.04146v3 / published article, Theorem 3.3 and Eqs. 27–30:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00205-022-01785-1
For finite-dimensional GNS-symmetric QMS they prove CMLSI with lambda/C_cb(E) <= alpha_CMLSI <= 2 lambda. They also give a resolvent entropy-production formula and a jump-form Dirichlet energy. This is a GNS-symmetric entropy-decay theorem, not the pointwise J>=pi E comparison for arbitrary KMS-symmetric generators. It therefore supplies no direct transfer to either candidate claim.

Record

The strongest exact prior interface found here is the KT p=1 regularity comparison, with a clean normalization map and constants 2 (weak) / 4 (strong). In the cited paper, universal weak regularity is explicitly conjectural; the established examples are restricted subclasses. Its second-order LS1/spectral-gap calculation uses a BKM-type metric, not the candidate root Dirichlet Hessian. The checked GNS CMLSI theorem has different symmetry and conclusion. No exact primary theorem matching the universal constant pi and the stated interfaces was found in this bounded check.
