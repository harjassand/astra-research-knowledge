## Verdict

I found no fatal algebraic, uniformity, stopped-residual, or trace-normalization
error in S1-S12. The proof, if its displayed differential identities and tail
estimates are accepted, does give a fixed-M,B, large-N, known-parameter
mathematical construction of a positive mixture of identical products with
whole-density error at most C exp(-cN), uniformly over all real symmetric A
with ||A||<=M and all |b|<=B. The constants can be very poor. This is a
separability-distance theorem and positive path representation, not a finite-bit
sampler or a hardware preparation result.

The field-wide upper is plausibly a genuine strengthening of the O(N^(-3/2))
baseline; there is no matching positive lower, and no such claim survives the
scalar-A subfamily, where the heat-kernel representation is exactly separable
for sufficiently large N. The phrase “sharp N^(-1/2)” for a separate decoder
must be read as sharp for that particular uncorrected radial comparison unless
a separate minimax lower theorem is supplied. It is not a consequence of S1.

## Independent equation and quantifier checks

Write Lambda=M+1, C=A+Lambda I, Lc=2M+1, s=N^(3/4), and
H=Q_C(J)/s^2+b.J/s. Since A is real symmetric with ||A||<=M, uniformly
I<=C<=Lc I and Tr C<=3Lc. The identity

  K_N = (2/N-Lambda/N^(3/2)) J^2 + H

is exact. Thus the heat seed is positive when 2 sqrt(N)>Lambda; the stronger
large-N seed threshold N>=Lambda^2 used for the lower radial normalizer is
sufficient. All subsequent bounds depend only on fixed M,B through Lambda,Lc,
Tr C<=3Lc, r0, a0, and the tail constants; none depends on a particular
unknown direction or eigenvalue gap of A. The construction itself is
parameter-dependent: its diffusion matrix and drift contain C and b. It does
not learn them from rho_N or from data.

For a product kernel k_N(m), the one-site identities

  {n.sigma/2,(I+m.sigma)/2} - (n.m)(I+m.sigma)/2
      = ((n-(n.m)m).sigma)/2,
  [n.sigma/2,(I+m.sigma)/2]
      = i ((n cross m).sigma)/2

sum over tensor factors to give {n.J,k}=B_n k and [n.J,k]=i R_n k.
Using R_n k=-i[n.J,k], one obtains

  B_n^2 k - R_n^2 k = 2{(n.J)^2,k},

so the factor 1/(4s^2) in S3 is correct. Expanding the variable-coefficient
vector fields produces the stated Hessian matrix and first-order drift in S4.
The zeroth-order term is also independently checked from

  Tr(Q_C(J) k_N(m))
    = [N^2 m^T C m + N(Tr C-m^T C m)]/4,
  Tr(b.J k_N(m)) = N b.m/2,

which agrees with V_N. The skew angular diffusion contribution is essential;
dropping it would change the generator.

For D_C, set P=I-mm^T and let [m]_x be the cross-product matrix. The identities
v_i=P n_i and r_i=-[m]_x n_i give

  D_C=P C P - [m]_x C [m]_x^T.

Since C>=I and C<=Lc I, the first term is at least P^2 and the second at most
Lc |m|^2 I. Its smallest eigenvalue is therefore bounded below by

  (1-|m|^2)^2 - Lc |m|^2.

At |m|^2<=r0^2=1/[16(Lc+1)], this is greater than 3/4. Also
D_C<=Tr(C) I<=3Lc I. Thus the stopped SDE has a smooth principal square root
on a fixed ball with a uniform spectral margin; no unknown PSD or gap test is
hidden in existence. Its diffusion covariance D_C/(2s^2) gives generator
D_C:Hess/(4s^2), as required.

The claimed uniform drift estimate follows from |u_i|,|v_i|,|r_i|<=r0 on the
ball, |(v_i.grad)v_i|<=2r0, |(r_i.grad)r_i|<=r0, and
|b-(b.m)m|<=B. Summing lambda_i<=Tr C yields

  |a_N|<=Tr(C)r0/(2 sqrt(N)) + 3Tr(C)r0/(4N^(3/2)) + B/(2N^(3/4)).

It tends to zero uniformly at fixed M,B, so an N0 can be chosen so the drift
moves at most r0/8 by time one. From any retained initial |m0|<=r0/2, a hit of
|m|=r0 then requires martingale displacement at least 3r0/8. Each coordinate
has quadratic variation at most Tr(C)/(2s^2). The coordinate/sign union bound
supports the weaker displayed S9 exponent (the direct coordinate threshold
also gives a stronger constant). No uniformity is lost over A,b.

For the seed tail, t0<=2/N gives the pointwise radial upper density
r^2 sinhc(r/2) exp[-N f(r/2)], with f(u)=u^2/2-logcosh(u). On the compact
interval [a0,2], f(r/2)>=a0^4/768. On r>=2, f(r/2)>=delta r^2/4; when
N delta>=2, the sinhc factor is absorbed into a Gaussian tail. Dividing this
numerator by the seed lower normalizer N^(-3/4)Lbase yields the N^(3/4)
exp(-kappa0 N) bound. This is uniform because a0, delta, Lbase depend only on
fixed Lambda. The scalar estimates are conservative but consistent.

## Trace and normalization audit

Let G=e^(M_H)rho0 and W=W_1. Since J^2 is central, G=e^(K_N)/Z0, so
G/Tr G=rho_N exactly. The sector trace satisfies
Tr H_j=Tr(C)j(j+1)(2j+1)/(3s^2)>=0; scalar trace Jensen gives
Tr e^(H_j)>=(2j+1), and summing sectors gives Tr G>=1. The stopped Ito
calculation gives an exact Duhamel residual supported on {tau<=t}; it does not
assert that the un-stopped globally indefinite diffusion is positive. With
|V_N|<=h_N, ||M_H||_(Hermitian trace->trace)<=h_N, and
||e^((1-t)M_H)||<=e^hN, the residual bound h_N e^(2hN)P(tau<=1) is
valid (loose). Replacing the bad seed mass costs at most 2p_bad in trace norm
before the semigroup and at most 2e^hN p_bad after it. These are the two terms
in S11.

For positive unnormalized G,W with Tr G>0,

  T(G/TrG,W/TrW) <= ||G-W||_1 / Tr G.

This follows by adding and subtracting W/Tr G and using
|Tr G-Tr W|<=||G-W||_1. Therefore the lower bound Tr G>=1 correctly removes
an inverse partition-function factor in S12. W has positive trace because it
is an expectation of strictly positive weights times trace-one product
kernels. Finally h_N=O_(M,B)(sqrt(N)+N^(1/4))=o(N), so polynomial tail factors
and e^(h_N), e^(2h_N) are absorbed into a smaller positive exponential rate.

## Boundaries and remaining gate

- Uniformity is only for fixed public finite M,B. Growing M or B requires a new
  analysis; the constants/r0/N0 can deteriorate sharply.
- The SDE law is specified from known A,b, but Brownian sampling, stopping,
  evaluation of Feynman-Kac weights, and branch selection are not implemented.
  The factor e^(h_N)=exp(O(sqrt N)) is explicitly charged in the proof and
  blocks an inference of polynomial finite-bit cost from this argument.
- The output is a whole-density trace approximation and hence controls every
  POVM on that output. It does not control purifications or external references.
- The strong claim is about distance to the fully separable cone, not uniform
  reconstruction from unknown parameters. The one-observation decoder and
  the known-parameter SDE have different inputs.
- The scalar-A exact heat-mixture case prevents a positive uniform lower rate.
  Exponential upper does not establish a tight exponent or a quantum advantage.
- The primary 1991 full text and historical priority remain unresolved. No
  outside validation or hardware evidence is added by this audit.
