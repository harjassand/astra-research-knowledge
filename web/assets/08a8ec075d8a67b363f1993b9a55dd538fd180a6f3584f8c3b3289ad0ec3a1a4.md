## Opportunity 1: safe excitation without losing regression information

Release 097 states that for every prescribed sequence of unit-ball vectors in
R^d there are signs making *all* signed prefixes have norm at most C sqrt(d),
where C is independent of sequence length. Its proof is existential and uses
the entire sequence. The source expressly gives neither an online rule nor an
efficient signer. I inspected the exact PDF introduction, transference proof,
computational limitation and construction overview. I did not validate the
analytic proofs underlying this frontier statement.

A concrete scientific consequence follows. Supply a complete sequence of
regressors u_t, controllable polarity, and a norm certificate ||B|| <= L for an
unknown linear integrator. Then signs can keep every physical displacement
below LC sqrt(d). Under independent noisy increment observations, the Fisher
information matrix remains (sum_t u_tu_t^T) tensor Sigma^{-1}: polarity changes
the state excursion but leaves the design's information exactly intact. The
response matrix B is acquired from observations; the planned regressors,
actuation symmetry, incremental sensor interface and norm certificate are
supplied. This is a useful safety-versus-information mechanism, not a new
regression theorem.

The missing lemma is **an efficient offline signer with a computable safe
bound for the complete rational input sequence**. Once signs are found,
verification is cheap: accumulate the input prefixes and check their squared
norms against the actual safety budget, charging rational bit lengths. The
source does not supply this acquisition step. Its universal C is also not a
numerical laboratory budget.

Matched controls are decisive. Known orthogonal basis probes already admit a
simple alternating sign rule with prefix bound sqrt(d). More generally, for
a known r-vector palette with Gram spectral norm <= Lambda, alternation per
vector gives sqrt(Lambda r), with r counters. Improving these easy settings
does not need the released theorem.

The unrestricted online transfer is false. In dimension two an adaptive
adversary chooses each new unit regressor perpendicular to the current signed
prefix. Either polarity then adds exactly one to its squared norm. All online
rules, including randomized rules against an adversary observing past signs,
therefore have prefix norm sqrt(t). The theorem's complete-sequence promise
cannot be silently replaced by adaptive active-experiment access.

**Consequence if the constructive gap is solved:** arbitrarily long preplanned
linear identification experiments could preserve their Fisher design while
retaining a dimension-scale excursion budget, without physical resets. Exact
actuation, model validity, all stored regressors/signs and sensor samples remain
charged. This remains a candidate avenue, not a demonstrated new algorithm.

## Opportunity 2: certified experiment-plan search without exact mass

Release 103 Theorem 1.2 claims deterministic approximation of arbitrary
acceptance probabilities for every fixed polynomial-time logspace randomized
machine. For accuracy delta, the claimed scratch is O(log N+log(1/delta)) and
time is N^{O(1)} delta^{-O(1)}. Its totality and arbitrary-probability guarantee
are stronger than a gapped language decider. Source Corollary 12.4 already
derives accepting computations from an inverse-polynomial success lower bound.

I independently reconstructed that deduction. At each configuration estimate
all possible continuation success values within delta <= gamma/(8T), then
choose the largest estimated value. The true continuation value loses at most
2delta per step. From starting success >= gamma, its terminal value remains
positive and hence is exactly one. A dry run followed by deterministic replay
ensures that invalid output is never emitted, even outside the success promise.
Current/candidate/best configurations and scores fit logarithmic scratch;
the entire plan can be streamed. No unique witness or exact success numerator
is required.

This differs from Astra N16's quantum exactification interface: exact dyadic
mass is unnecessary here because the positive depends instead on the classical
low-space probability-estimation theorem. It does not repair general search:
polynomial source time, logarithmic complete live state, polynomial success
density and exact verification are load-bearing.

The active-identification application is exact and narrow. Supply a fixed
number h of deterministic Moore-machine hypotheses with known starts, legal
actions and noiseless outputs. Simulate their states and track which pairs have
different transcripts. If a polynomial-time logspace randomized generator
produces a distinguishing action word with inverse-polynomial probability,
the conditional compiler deterministically emits such a word with the same
scratch order. One physical execution identifies the unknown hypothesis.

The full supplied model tables count as retained input. The simulations use
O(h log S+h^2) bits. Stored actions and observations add
T(log |A|+log |O|) bits. The physical trial uses T actions. The equally informed
Monte Carlo control already searches offline until a valid word is found,
requiring expected 1/gamma proposal trials and the same one physical experiment.
The conditional gain is deterministic worst-case search reliability with
small scratch, not cheaper acquisition of the model or fewer observations.
Growing h can make product-state time exponential and cannot be omitted.

There is also a legal noisy-environment version. Greedily selecting the action
with largest approximate baseline action value gives

    P(success) >= baseline success - 2Tdelta.

With an independently certified uniform row-TV model error epsilon, it gives

    P_true(success) >= modeled baseline success - 2Tdelta - T epsilon.

The environment's randomness remains. Choosing a favorable environmental
outcome as though it were an action is invalid: a one-action fair-coin
laboratory succeeds with probability 1/2 even though its favorable accepted
path has terminal value one. Unknown physical transitions cannot be supplied
to 103 by derandomizing their actual random outcomes. Acquired model tables,
reset/sampling access and their confidence certificates remain charged.

The rollout mechanism predates this release; see the primary control papers:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07998 and
https://web.mit.edu/dimitrib/www/ADP-MPC.pdf

**Read depth:** exact introduction, Lemmas 6.1-6.3, Sections 12.3-12.5 and
relevant Section 13 compiler/resource formulas were read, with decisive pages
visually inspected. The difficult estimator/compression/fingerprint/controller
induction in Sections 7-11 was not independently validated. Thus the positive
compiler consequence remains conditional. The full compiler was not executed;

## Reconstructed cross-field lemma: conditional affine expanders

Release 103 Lemma 6.1 obtains a uniform finite-action gap for
SL_H(Z) semidirect Z^H from old relative and full Kazhdan constants. I rebuilt
the argument and sharpened its elementary distance-to-invariants bound.
Cover translation coordinates with ceil(H/2) coordinate pairs; their invariant
projections commute. Use the projection union bound and orthogonality rather
than the source's triangle sum over all binom(H,2) pairs. The resulting bound is

    ||xi-P_inv xi||^2
      <= [100 ceil(H/2)+kappa_H^{-2}]
         max_{g in S} ||rho(g)xi-xi||^2.

For the source lazy walk, m=2H^2, this gives

    gap >= 1/{8H^2[100 ceil(H/2)+kappa_H^{-2}]}.

The source's corresponding denominator uses
(10 binom(H,2)+kappa_H^{-1})^2. With the older Kassabov rank-scale constant,
the revised certificate is Omega(H^{-3}) and independent of finite field,
composite torus modulus and conditioning-box width. This is a reusable
elementary improvement to the displayed proof; comparable semidirect-product
rank bounds are prior art, and fixed-H asymptotics of 103 do not change.

The resulting conditional-coordinate walk averages a uniform full-rank frame
and one retained response in a box even when the unconditional retention event
is extremely rare. Its limited-memory, delayed-identifier setting matters.
If the conditioning identifier is available, a direct full-rank frame sampler
plus a uniform box point is an equally informed and computationally superior
control, with frame acceptance probability >0.57. Multiple observations of
one common unknown map create correlated conditional coordinates; assigning
independent channels changes that scientific model. The p=2, r=1, L=1
two-distinct-point fixture even has an empty legal conditional space.

Primary prior art, read directly:
https://numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1999__90__145_0/
and https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0311487

## Screened route 3: deterministic finite-field moment-law recovery

Release 142's deterministic factorizer can be composed with the classical
Prony/Berlekamp-Massey reduction. For a supplied prime p and sparsity bound s,
2s exact moments of a law m_j=sum_i c_i lambda_i^j, with distinct field roots
and nonzero weights, determine its annihilator and weights. The nonsingular
Vandermonde-Hankel factorization proves identifiability; factor its annihilator
then solve the Vandermonde system. The new source removes randomization in
the factoring step, conditional on its arithmetic proof. It does not acquire
exact moments, solve discrete logarithms or certify noisy real-system recovery.
The interpolation mechanism is old, for example:
https://www.cecm.sfu.ca/personal/monaganm/teaching/TopicsinCA15/BenOrTiwari.pdf

The `number_theory_algorithms` worker owns the full 142/029 audit. I inspected
its exact source driver and complexity files and retained only the transfer
and a concrete finite-resource control. The explicit branch p <= B^200000,
B=20+(n+1)(ceil(log2 p)+1), searches gcd(h,b-a), a=0,...,p-1. With the
canonical legal nonscalar b=x and h=(x+1)(x+2), p>3, a first proper split occurs
at a=p-2. Thus this allowed driver implementation takes Theta(p) trials on a
trivial polynomial across ordinary field sizes. A smarter choice of b can
avoid that fixture; it is not a factoring lower bound. The source's huge
polynomial upper bound alone would not prove practical slowness.

The root's independent retuning audit may improve the arithmetic upper
exponents, but no new practical sparse-law recovery guarantee is established
here. Exact field data and smaller randomized factoring controls must be
counted fairly.

## Verification and next decisive work

The standard-library exact controls completed in about 0.23 seconds and
3.36 MB traced Python peak, including source hashing. They contain:

* an eight-state affine torus walk with exact characteristic polynomial and
  a rigorous spectral-gap lower certificate 17/729 via three-step contraction;
* a three-machine, two-action distinguishing experiment, random success 1/4,
  with recovered word 11 and exact continuation values 1/4, 1/2, 1;
* 200 exact rational rollout/model-error fixtures checking the displayed bounds;
* 32,760 joint projection configurations checking the scalar projection inequality.

An initial unsupported assumption that the walk's numerator spectrum was
entirely integral failed and was rejected. The failed assumption and remaining
polynomial are retained in `checks.json`; the final certificate is exact and
does not rely on rounding eigenvalues. These fixtures do not validate the
general group theorem, 097's analytic proof, 103's estimator, 142's factorizer,
historical novelty or end-to-end discovery.

The next load-bearing tasks are explicit: construct an efficient offline signer
for the 097 safe-excitation interface, or independently validate and numerically
instantiate the full 103 probability estimator. Additional applications of the
already known greedy rollout or Prony reduction will not by themselves cross
the feasible-discovery bottleneck.
