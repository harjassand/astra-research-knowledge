# Counterexamples to overreading the sign audit

These calculations use the exact Table 1 rate conversion, 50/20/330 μM Nature S8 start, `I520=3.8 W cm^-2`, and the 0–5 s RF-on interval. The 520-nm rate uses the documented source-adjacent scaling `Iσ=1652(I520/1.7) s^-1`; Nature does not publish its exact conversion.

## A one-sign correction recovers the observed direction and scale

Under the literal published separation-fraction law, `βoff=.68` and `βon=.7151`, the source model gives `Goff(5s)=3.68630 μM`, `GRF(5s)=3.52368 μM`, and `ΔG/Goff=−4.4114%`.

Keeping the same baseline and changing only the separation-fraction increment to `−.117 mT^-1` gives `βon=.6449`, `GRF(5s)=3.86496 μM`, and `ΔG/Goff=+4.8467%`. After a 2-s green prehistory, the response is positive and monotone at the sampled times: `+0.87%` at 0.1 s, `+3.14%` at 0.5 s, `+4.30%` at 1 s, `+4.81%` at 2.5 s, and `+4.82%` at 5 s. These values are close to the plotted high-intensity `B1=.3 mT` response. No parameters were refit; this only tests whether the sign discrepancy implies a false experiment. It does not.

The machine-readable trajectory is in `../proof/protocol_check.json`; the full source-rate integration and step-convergence values are in `../proof/sign_audit_results.json` and `../proof/convergence_check.json`.

## Complement convention

If a private Nature variable named `β` were actually the recombination fraction `γ=1−β_Xiang`, then raising γ on RF-on lowers the Xiang separation fraction and increases G. For a baseline-preserving comparison, the equivalent Xiang fraction must move from `.68` to `.6449` (the sign-corrected case above). If one instead interprets the fitted Nature values themselves as recombination fractions, the Xiang separation fraction moves from `.32` to `.2849`; the example 5-s increase is `+11.17%`, so that relabeling changes baseline semantics and would require a quantitative refit. Both conventions can give the correct sign; only one matches the printed S8 description.

## A bright radical cannot rescue the literal high-intensity trace

Take a fixed linear detector map `Fdet=G+ρR+B`, where `ρ` is the radical's brightness relative to G and `B≥0` is constant. At 5 s under the literal β increase, `ΔG=−0.16262 μM`, `ΔR=+0.16264 μM`. For any `0≤ρ≤1` the response is nonpositive or essentially zero. Positive sign requires `ρ≈1`; yet even the radical-only limit `ρ→∞` is only `ΔR/Roff≈+0.351%` at the 5-s endpoint. The plotted positive response is about +4.8%. A positive background cannot increase that limit and reduces finite-ρ contrast. Thus a small residual radical fluorescence, or a normalization/offset change, cannot explain the published amplitude within this six-state mapping.

JACS explicitly models the radical as nonfluorescent and adds a constant 3% background. An observation map with β-dependent quantum yield, nonlinear detector response, or a different emissive product would be a different, presently undocumented model and remains a possible implementation gap rather than a rescue derived from these sources.
