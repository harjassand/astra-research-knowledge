Linear Variance of the Random 3-SAT Hitting Time
OpenAI

Abstract
For random 3-SAT onn Boolean variables, with independent uniformly signed clauses on
three distinct variables sampled with replacement, we prove that the first unsatisfiable prefix
has varianceΘ(n). The upper bound removes the logarithmic loss in the earlier variance
estimate; the matching lower bound follows from Wilson’s transition-width theorem.

Context and contribution
Friedgut proved sharpness of the randomk-SAT threshold for every fixedk≥ 2[ 5, Theorem 1.3].
Quantifying the width of that transition is a finer problem. Wilson showed that, fork≥ 3, a fixed
decrease in the satisfiability probability requires at least a constant times√n additional clauses [7].
More recently, Carenini obtained anO(n/logn )central clause-window bound for fixedk≥ 3,
including independently sampled proper constraints [3, Corollaries 7.10–7.11]. Her subsequent
paper [2, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3, Corollary 1.2], made public on October 5, 2026, proves for every
fixed k≥ 3a central window boundOk,η(n1/2+1/k)at each fixed0 <η < 1/2and a hitting-time
variance boundOk(n1+2/k). Together with the Abbe–Montanari criterion, her result establishes
1
the limiting satisfiability threshold for every fixedk≥ 3. We credit Carenini with priority for
the resolution of the satisfiability conjecture. The present paper concerns the sharper fluctuation
question for three-literal clauses.
A bound on a fixed central window alone does not control the variance, which also depends
on the tails. In the other direction, Theorem 1.1 and Wilson’s lower bound show that the clause
window over which satisfiability drops from1−η to η has order√n for every fixed0<η < 1/2;
see Corollary 5.2.
The companion preprintVariance of the Randomk-SAT Hitting Time[6] develops a clause-
omission and root-test framework. It proves varianceΘk(n)for every fixedk≥ 4and an upper
bound O(nlogn )for k = 3. Our argument uses that framework, which we reprove here, and
replaces its critical occupation estimate in the three-literal case. The companion has broader
general-k and capped formulations; the present paper concerns the uncapped proper 3-clause
process defined above.
