CYCLE 2 / NEAR-FULL-RANK VARIATIONAL AUDIT

Status and scope

This note formulates rank defect as a Grassmannian family of compressed Casimirs, gives exact Green/resolvent identities for each fixed hole, derives the strongest lower bound obtainable from the singular-value trace cap plus the invariant Schur spectral-CDF constraints, and tests a coherent-hole ansatz. An exact spin-1 witness shows that the flat coherent-hole complement X proportional to I-P_coh is not optimal, even when its kernel is the coherent hole itself. This does not refute the narrower claim that a minimizing kernel subspace can be chosen coherent; the witness uses the same coherent kernel.

1. Rank defect as compression

Let H be d-dimensional and let C be a positive conjugation-invariant Casimir on HS(H) for an irreducible carrier. Assume ker C=span{e0}, e0=I/sqrt(d), and let gamma>0 be its first positive eigenvalue. For a k-dimensional hole K subset H, let P=P_K be its orthogonal projection and define

  S_P = {X in HS(H): X P=0},
  Q_P(X) = X(I-P).

Q_P is the orthogonal projection from HS(H) onto S_P. With vec(X)=sum_ij X_ij e_i tensor conjugate(e_j), its vectorized form is

  Qhat_P = I_H tensor conjugate(I-P),
  S_P corresponds to H tensor conjugate(K^perp).

A matrix has rank at most d-k exactly when its right kernel contains some k-plane K. Therefore, for the rank-at-most problem

  Lambda_k := inf_{||X||_HS=1, rank X<=d-k} <X,CX>,

there is the exact compression identity

  Lambda_k = min_{rank P=k} lambda_min(C_P),
  C_P := (Q_P C Q_P)|_{S_P}.                                (1)

For fixed P, the Rayleigh quotient minimum is lambda_min(C_P). Since the only null vector of C is I and I P=P!=0 for k>=1, C_P is positive definite. If G_P=C_P^(-1) is the constrained Green operator, then also

  lambda_min(C_P) = 1/||G_P||_op,
  Lambda_k = 1 / max_{rank P=k} ||G_P||_op.                    (2)

The Grassmannian of k-planes is compact, so these extrema are attained. The formulation is exact for rank at most d-k. If one insists on rank exactly d-k, an at-most optimizer of lower rank can be perturbed by arbitrarily small entries on additional allowed columns, preserving the infimum by continuity.

The constrained Green is not simply Q_P C^+ Q_P: C^+ ignores the identity component, while S_P is not orthogonal to e0. Here is an exact correction. Write P0=|e0><e0|, R_P=I-Q_P, and for any eta>0 set

  C_eta=C+eta P0,     G_eta=C_eta^(-1)=C^+ + eta^(-1)P0,
  B_P=(Q_P C_eta Q_P)|_{S_P},     u_P=Q_P e0.

Then C_P=B_P-eta|u_P><u_P|. The inverse of the positive compression B_P is the Schur complement of the full regularized Green:

  B_P^(-1) = (Q_P G_eta Q_P)|_{S_P}
       - Q_P G_eta R_P (R_P G_eta R_P|_{S_P^perp})^(-1)
                            R_P G_eta Q_P,                   (3)

where the right side is restricted to S_P. Sherman–Morrison then gives

  G_P = B_P^(-1)
       + eta B_P^(-1)|u_P><u_P|B_P^(-1)
           / (1-eta<u_P,B_P^(-1)u_P>).                       (4)

The denominator is positive because C_P is positive definite. Equations (3)-(4) exhibit the required compression of the Casimir Green operator and the correction for its zero mode. They also identify the error in treating the inverse of a compression as the compression of an inverse.

2. A constrained Green/resolvent identity

There is a second exact characterization below the first Casimir gap. Put H0=e0^perp, C0=C|_{H0}, and define the surjective map

  A_P:H0 -> V_P,     A_P(Y)=Y P,
  V_P={Z in HS(H): Z=ZP},     b_P=P/sqrt(d).

Surjectivity holds for k<d: for Z=ZP, the traceless matrix
  Y=Z-(Tr Z)/(d-k)(I-P)
obeys YP=Z. Write a normalized candidate as X=a e0+Y. The hole condition becomes the exact affine constraint

  XP=0  iff  A_P(Y)=-a b_P,
  ||X||_HS^2=|a|^2+||Y||_HS^2,   <X,CX>=<Y,C0Y>.             (5)

For 0<=lambda<gamma define the shifted Green and the positive operator on V_P

  G0(lambda)=(C0-lambda I)^(-1),
  M_P(lambda)=A_P G0(lambda) A_P^*,
  g_P(lambda)=<b_P,M_P(lambda)^(-1)b_P>.                    (6)

The minimum of <Y,(C0-lambda)Y> subject to A_PY=-a b_P is exactly |a|^2 g_P(lambda), by the weighted least-norm identity for a surjective linear constraint. If a=0, then Y lies in ker A_P and C0-lambda is positive there. It follows in both directions that

  lambda_min(C_P)>=lambda  iff  g_P(lambda)>=lambda,
                       for 0<=lambda<gamma.                 (7)

If lambda_min(C_P)<gamma, it is the unique root g_P(lambda)=lambda in (0,gamma): M_P(lambda) increases with lambda, hence g_P(lambda) decreases, while the right side increases. This root form is exact, not just a bound. At lambda=0, g_P(0) is the minimum Casimir energy needed to cancel the identity on the hole when the identity coefficient a is fixed to one; normalization of X is handled by the root equation, not by g_P(0) alone.

For a Casimir decomposition C=sum_l c_l Pi_l with c_0=0 and c_l>=gamma for l>=1, the shifted Green in (6) is explicitly Schur-resolved:

  G0(lambda)=sum_{l>=1} (c_l-lambda)^(-1) Pi_l,
  M_P(lambda)=sum_{l>=1} (c_l-lambda)^(-1) A_P Pi_l A_P^*.

Thus the fixed-hole variational problem is determined by the full compressed Schur projector data A_P Pi_l A_P^*, not by Casimir eigenvalues or multiplicities alone. A simple singular-value bound from (6) is

  g_P(lambda) >= ||b_P||^2 / ||M_P(lambda)||_op
                = (k/d)/||A_P G0(lambda) A_P^*||_op.          (8)

It yields a valid universal certificate whenever its right side is at least lambda. Retaining the full matrix M_P(lambda), as in (7), is stronger than replacing it by its top singular value.

3. Singular-value and Schur-profile lower bounds

Let X be HS-unit with singular values s_1,...,s_q, q<=Q. The identity Schur component has mass

  p_0=|<e0,X>|^2=|Tr X|^2/d <= (sum_i s_i)^2/d,              (9)

by von Neumann's trace inequality. Since C>=gamma(I-P0),

  <X,CX> >= gamma(1-(sum_i s_i)^2/d).                       (10)

For rank at most Q, Cauchy–Schwarz gives (sum_i s_i)^2<=Q, hence

  <X,CX> >= gamma(1-Q/d)=gamma k/d,
  when Q=d-k.                                                  (11)

This is sharp as a universal statement using only the gap gamma and singular values: if all positive Casimir levels were collapsed to gamma, a rank-Q partial isometry with equal nonzero singular values saturates the trace/nuclear-norm relaxation. For an actual Casimir, higher Schur energies may make it strict.

Use the full Schur data to improve (10). Let 0=c_0<c_1<...<c_M be the distinct Casimir eigenvalues, Pi_l their projectors, D_l=sum_{i<=l}Tr Pi_i, and p_l=||Pi_l X||_HS^2. The invariant partial-trace theorem gives the cumulative rank bounds

  F_l:=sum_{i<=l}p_i <= min(1,Q D_l/d),  l>=0,

while (9) improves the zero-level constraint to F_0<=nu^2/d, nu=sum_i s_i. Layer cake for this discrete spectral law gives the combined bound

  <X,CX> >= c_1(1-nu^2/d)
       + sum_{l=1}^{M-1}(c_{l+1}-c_l)[1-QD_l/d]_+.           (12)

For rank-only input, use nu^2<=Q and (12) reduces to the integrated profile

  Phi(Q)=sum_{l=0}^{M-1}(c_{l+1}-c_l)[1-QD_l/d]_+.

Moreover, (12) is the exact minimum of the linear energy over the relaxed probability vectors (p_l) satisfying only these cumulative CDF caps: choose each CDF F_l at its allowed cap, which is monotone, and take p_l=F_l-F_{l-1}. Thus it is the strongest universal lower bound obtainable from these singular-value/Schur CDF data alone. It need not be attained by an actual matrix X; orientation and the simultaneous geometry of the Schur components are discarded by this relaxation.

For SU(2) spin j=N/2, d=N+1, c_l=l(l+1), multiplicity 2l+1, and D_K=(K+1)^2. Equation (12) specializes to

  <X,CX> >= 2(1-nu^2/d)
       + sum_{K=1}^{N-1}2(K+1)[1-Q(K+1)^2/d]_+.              (13)

If Q=d-k and k<3d/4, every K>=1 term vanishes; the rank-only bound is just 2k/d. The full Schur multiplicity profile therefore certifies a nonzero near-full-rank penalty but does not determine the compression minimum. The exact Green identity (7), which retains the hole orientation, is the stronger route when that information is available.

4. Coherent-hole test: a finite exact counterexample to the flat complement

For SU(2) spin j, let d=2j+1 and take a rank-one coherent hole P=|j,j><j,j|. The flat coherent-hole complement is

  X_coh=(I-P)/sqrt(d-1).

In the magnetic basis, for diagonal X=diag(x_m),

  <X,CX>=sum_{m=-j}^{j-1}(j-m)(j+m+1)|x_{m+1}-x_m|^2.      (14)

This follows because the J_1 and J_2 commutators together give the squared J_+ ladder coefficient on each adjacent edge, while J_3 commutes with diagonal X. For X_coh, only the last edge contributes; its weight is 2j and ||I-P||_HS^2=2j. Hence its normalized energy is exactly 1.

Now take the smallest nontrivial carrier j=1, d=3, with basis ordered as m=-1,0,1. The same coherent kernel is P=|1><1|. Consider the rank-two diagonal family

  X=diag(a,b,0),   a,b real,   a^2+b^2=1.

Equation (14) gives

  <X,CX>=2(a-b)^2+2b^2
         = [a b] [[2,-2],[-2,4]] [a b]^T.

The smallest eigenvalue of this 2-by-2 compression is 3-sqrt(5), attained at

  b/a=(sqrt(5)-1)/2,
  a=(1+((sqrt(5)-1)/2)^2)^(-1/2).

Therefore

  inf_{||X||_HS=1, X|1>=0} <X,CX> <= 3-sqrt(5) < 1
       = <X_coh,CX_coh>.

This is an exact counterexample to optimality of the projector-complement ansatz X proportional to I-P_coh, even for its own fixed coherent hole. It uses a non-flat taper across the remaining levels. In fact, for any spin j>=1, the diagonal compression for the coherent rank-one hole is a path Laplacian with one Dirichlet endpoint. Its constant vector on the remaining d-1>=2 sites is not an eigenvector (the compressed Laplacian sends it to a nonzero vector supported only at the site adjacent to the hole), so its Rayleigh quotient 1 is strictly above the minimum of that diagonal compression. The spin-1 case supplies the exact numerical separation.

Boundary of this counterexample: the better X still has the coherent state in its right kernel. It refutes the flat complement, not the distinct hypothesis that the optimizing hole subspace itself can be chosen coherent. I do not have a noncoherent K whose full compressed minimum is below the coherent-K minimum. That orientation question remains open in this audit.

For a coherent highest-weight cap K of dimension k, the flat complement X=(I-P_K)/sqrt(d-k) has only one boundary edge, of weight k(2j-k+1)=k(d-k), and therefore normalized energy k. Equation (14) shows why this is only a convenient witness: allowing the diagonal entries outside K to taper changes the Rayleigh quotient and can strictly lower it whenever at least two complement sites remain.

5. Conclusions

The exact near-full-rank problem is a minimum over k-hole compressions of the Casimir, equivalently the reciprocal of the largest constrained Green norm. Equations (3)-(7) give exact full-Green and fixed-hole resolvent identities. Singular values alone yield the universal gap floor gamma k/d; combining the identity-component nuclear-norm bound with all Schur CDF caps gives the optimal lower bound for that relaxed data-only problem, equation (12). For SU(2), the near-full rank profile can be very weak (2k/d), and an explicit spin-1 taper beats the flat coherent-hole projector complement. The kernel-orientation optimality question is separate and not refuted by this example.
