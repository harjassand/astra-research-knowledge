# N35 reverse transfer: controlled jump-process safety

## Verdict

N35 yields a finite-horizon theorem for a time-inhomogeneous, history-adaptively controlled pure-jump process. Its stochastic mechanism is the standard exponential-Lyapunov/compensator argument: impose inward drift in a boundary collar, bound jump size and quadratic jump activity, stop at first exit, and control the generator of a sum of exponential facet barriers. Markov feedback and stationarity are unnecessary because the hypotheses hold predictably at each pre-jump time.

The chemistry-specific bridge is useful and exact: molecular propensities use falling factorials, and the source gives a uniform O(1/V) discrepancy from deterministic monomial rates, including count states with insufficient reactants. This converts an acquired deterministic inward-facet certificate into explicit finite-volume drift and activity constants. Once those bounds are established, the probability estimate is a general jump-process lemma and does not use chemical reaction structure.

The two-species example has a sharper chemical calculation than the generic drift/variance constants: an exact birth/death ratio permits a much larger exponential parameter and removes the general V0 drift-correction threshold. This is a model-specific exact-generator refinement, not a new general stochastic principle. It does not yield all-time safety or permanence: for each fixed even V >= 6, the same finite closed birth-death chain exits the certified interval almost surely.

## Reconstructed chemical interface and charged resources

The supplied reaction diagram has integer complexes y_r -> y'_r in N^d, jumps nu_r=y'_r-y_r, and stoichiometric span S. The supplied region is a compact positive polytope relative to one compatibility class,

Q = {x in c+S : g_i(x)=a_i dot x+b_i >= 0, i=1,...,m},

with relative facets and coordinate bounds 0 < x_j <= U_j. For each facet i, the certificate supplies collar width ell_i>0 and verifies

a_i dot f(x,k) >= gamma_i > 0 whenever x in Q and 0 <= g_i(x) <= ell_i,

where f(x,k)=sum_r k_r x^(y_r).nu_r. The rate envelope is 0 <= k_r <= K. Switching may turn reactions off only if every allowed mode retains every collar margin. The source specifically assumes integer molecular complexes. Fractional powers with integer jumps define a different density-dependent model, and rational jumps need a separate lattice definition.

For integer volume V, counts Z^V and concentrations X^V=Z^V/V jump by nu_r/V at predictable intensity

V k_r(t,past) phi^V_{y_r}(X^V_{t-}), with phi^V_y(x)=V^(-|y|) product_j (V x_j)_{y_j}.

The controller can depend on time, the full past, and independent randomness. Predictability is the only control-information condition in the proof; it is not a computability guarantee. A supplied start must be a count vector in Q and the correct affine/lattice class, with all concentration gaps at least d_i>0. Coordinatewise rounding can break conserved totals or the reaction-lattice coset. A rational interior point gives compatible starts at denominator-multiple volumes, but prescribed totals/cosets need their own feasibility check.

For coordinate bounds U, the factorial correction is

E_y(U)=sum_{j:y_j>=2} binom(y_j,2) U_j^(y_j-1) product_{l != j} U_l^(y_l),

and on every count-lattice point in the box,

0 <= x^y-phi^V_y(x) <= E_y(U)/V.

This includes insufficient-reactant states: writing each count-lattice factor as max(x_j-q/V,0) makes the factorial propensity zero as soon as a reactant is unavailable; telescoping the nonnegative products yields the bound. The actual finite-volume inward drift differs from the deterministic projected drift by at most D_i/V, where

D_i=K sum_r |a_i dot nu_r| E_{y_r}(U).

The remaining constants are

J_i=max_r |a_i dot nu_r|,
B_i=K sum_r (a_i dot nu_r)^2 U^(y_r),
Lambda=K sum_r U^(y_r),
V0=max(1, max_i ceil(2D_i/gamma_i)),
mu_i=gamma_i/2,
theta_i=min(1/J_i, mu_i/(3B_i)),
c_*=min_i theta_i ell_i.

For V>=V0, the finite-volume inward drift in collar i is at least mu_i; total pre-exit hazard is at most V Lambda; projected quadratic jump activity is at most B_i; and a projected jump has magnitude at most J_i/V.

The stochastic theorem takes the acquired Q, collars, rate envelope, and lattice-feasible buffered start as premises. It does not pay to find them. ## General predictable-control lemma

Let X^V be a pure-jump process with finitely many jump marks nu_r/V, r=1,...,R, and predictable intensities V lambda_r(t). The lambda_r may depend on time, the pre-jump state, observed history, and independent randomization. Let Q={x:g_i(x)=a_i dot x+b_i>=0, i=1,...,m} be closed and tau=inf{t>=0:X^V_t notin Q}. Assume X^V_0 in Q. Before tau, almost surely and for Lebesgue-a.e. t:

1. |a_i dot nu_r|<=J_i for all i,r, with J_i>0.
2. sum_r lambda_r(t)<=Lambda.
3. sum_r lambda_r(t)(a_i dot nu_r)^2<=B_i, with B_i>0.
4. In collar i, if 0<=g_i(X^V_{t-})<=ell_i, then sum_r lambda_r(t)(a_i dot nu_r)>=mu_i>0.

Choose theta_i=min(1/J_i, mu_i/(3B_i)) and c=min_i theta_i ell_i. Then every finite T satisfies

P(tau<=T) <= sum_i exp(-theta_i V g_i(X^V_0)) + 2mV Lambda T exp(-cV).

If g_i(X^V_0)>=d_i>0, set c_0=min_i theta_i d_i. For every fixed alpha<c and fixed time unit t_0>0,

P(tau<=t_0 exp(alpha V)) <= m exp(-c_0 V) + 2mV Lambda t_0 exp(-(c-alpha)V).

N35 uses alpha=c/2. Any alpha<c gives an exponentially small upper bound, with a factor V in the second term.

**Proof.** Define Phi_i(x)=exp(-theta_i V g_i(x)) and Phi=sum_i Phi_i. The predictable jump compensators give the instantaneous drift before exit:

A_t Phi_i(x)/Phi_i(x) = V sum_r lambda_r(t)(exp(-theta_i z_ir)-1), where z_ir=a_i dot nu_r.

In collar i, exp(u)-1 <= u+u^2 exp(|u|)/2, theta_i J_i<=1, and e<3 give

A_t Phi_i/Phi_i <= V(-theta_i mu_i + (3/2)theta_i^2 B_i) <= -V theta_i mu_i/2 <= 0.

For g_i(x)>ell_i, Phi_i(x)<=exp(-theta_i V ell_i). Since theta_i J_i<=1, the largest positive jump multiplier is at most e-1<2. Thus

A_t Phi_i(x) <= 2V Lambda exp(-theta_i V ell_i).

Summing over facets gives A_t Phi<=2mV Lambda exp(-cV) in Q. At exit at least one gap is negative, so Phi(X_tau)>=1. Each projected exit overshoot is at most J_i/V, hence Phi(X_{t wedge tau})<=sum_i exp(theta_i J_i)<=3m. The total stopped hazard is bounded by V Lambda, so the stopped process has finitely many jumps on bounded intervals and the compensated Dynkin formula applies. It yields

E Phi(X_{T wedge tau}) <= Phi(X_0)+2mV Lambda T exp(-cV).

Nonnegativity and Phi(X_tau)>=1 imply the stated exit bound. Conditioning proves the random-start version with an almost-sure deterministic buffer.

An exact-cumulant refinement replaces the quadratic and drift assumptions by the directly checked local condition

sum_r lambda_r(t)(exp(-theta_i a_i dot nu_r)-1) <= 0

throughout collar i for some theta_i<=1/J_i. The same outside-collar and stopped-potential argument applies. This is useful when reaction structure yields exact up/down rate ratios and the generic quadratic Taylor bound is loose.

## Chemistry-specific sharpening and explicit obstruction

For 2A -> A+B and 2B -> A+B, take fixed total A+B=V, even V, rates 1<=k_A,k_B<=4, initial A=B=V/2, and Q=[1/4,3/4] in coordinate x=A/V. With g_L=x-1/4, g_R=3/4-x, and ell=1/32, the deterministic facet margin is 5/16; the left-collar worst deterministic drift is at least 205/1024.

The exact scaled birth and death rates are b=k_B y(y-1/V) and d=k_A x(x-1/V), y=1-x. In the left collar x<=9/32, if d>0 then

b/d >= y^2/(4x^2) >= 529/324.

If d=0, the inward inequality is immediate. With theta=12/25, the source's rational Taylor bound gives exp(theta)<=283/175<529/324, so the exact facet generator is nonpositive:

b(exp(-theta)-1)+d(exp(theta)-1)=(exp(theta)-1)(d-b/exp(theta))<=0.

The right collar is symmetric. On Q, total scaled activity is at most 5/2; exp(theta)-1<=108/175. This gives

P(tau_Q<=T) <= 2 exp(-3V/25) + (108/35) V T exp(-3V/200).

At T=exp(3V/400) and V=3000, the horizon is approximately 5.91e9 model time units and the bound is below 1.57e-6. This is a bound, not a simulation. Direct thinning at the stated horizon can require approximately 4.43e13 expected candidate events; the controller still needs a supplied evaluator. Fixed-Q count storage is O(d log V) bits. In open systems, maintaining rates over such a horizon can require cumulative feed of order VT; even the closed example retains the physical requirement that the assumed rates and controls are realizable.

The sharper example is not an all-time result. For fixed positive constant rates and each even V>=6, the communicating birth-death class A=1,...,V-1 is finite and irreducible, and contains A=1 and A=V-1 outside Q. Therefore it exits Q almost surely eventually. This directly blocks promoting the exponential finite-horizon statement to all-time survival, stochastic permanence, or an invariant-set claim. Separately, the source gives A+B -> 2A at rate 2 and A -> B at rate 1: its deterministic ODE has the same Q with strict inward margins 1/8 and 3/8, while the finite count chain reaches absorbing A=0 almost surely. That second network is not endotactic; it shows that deterministic inward geometry alone does not imply non-extinction.

## Prior-art comparison and novelty boundary

The strongest directly relevant chemical-process antecedent identified is Agazzi, Dembo, and Eckmann, “Large deviations theory for Markov jump models of chemical reaction networks,” Annals of Applied Probability 28(1) (2018), [DOI 10.1214/17-AAP1344](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-AAP1344), [arXiv:1701.02126](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126). It establishes sample-path large deviations for a class of Markov jump CRNs and develops Wentzell–Freidlin asymptotic exit-time theory. Their geometric companion, “On the geometry of chemical reaction networks: Lyapunov function and large deviations,” Journal of Statistical Physics (2018), [DOI 10.1007/s10955-018-2035-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-018-2035-8), [arXiv:1710.07240](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07240), develops reaction-polytope/Lyapunov geometry. These are richer asymptotic action/quasipotential results for their Markov CRN interfaces; they do not make N35's explicit certificate-derived constants or arbitrary predictable-control quantifiers novel by themselves.

The control/adaptivity step is also standard counting-process territory. Van de Geer, “Exponential inequalities for martingales, with application to maximum likelihood estimation for counting processes,” Annals of Statistics 23(5) (1995), [DOI 10.1214/aos/1176324323](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176324323), proves exponential inequalities for martingales and uniform bounds for integrals against counting processes with predictable integrands. N35's predictable controller works because the compensated jump formula accepts predictable intensities, not because chemical kinetics supplies a novel filtration argument.

The supported novelty boundary is narrow: an acquired strict deterministic mass-action facet certificate is converted, with explicit falling-factorial finite-volume loss and exact lattice/start assumptions, into an explicit uniform finite-V exit bound valid under arbitrary predictable bounded controls. The generic stochastic theorem is a standard exponential-barrier consequence. The source itself acknowledges exponential Lyapunov and chemical large-deviation antecedents and makes no general priority claim. The local exact birth/death ratio materially sharpens constants in one network; it does not establish a sharper general exit law, optimal exponent, quasipotential, stationary distribution, or infinite-time behavior.
