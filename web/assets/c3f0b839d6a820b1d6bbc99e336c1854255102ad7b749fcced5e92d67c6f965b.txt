Gamma is the completed ordered port-pair open strand with NEGATIVE-corner

If its embedding is observed, a given port pair has the same arc whether the
face has two or four crossings. Gamma does not reveal unused loops, checkerboard

There is a universal c>0, independent of domain and admissible gamma, such that
  c <= E[h_x | Gamma=gamma] <= 1/2,   x in V_+(gamma),             (1.1)
 -1 <= E[h_x | Gamma=gamma] <= -c,    x in V_-(gamma).             (1.2)
This includes unqueried free bulk vertices and the original pins. There is
no claim of a -1/2 lower bound on the negative side of this SAME Gamma.

  a = 1/8,
  p_L = 2^(-M-1),
  C_0 = 16 max{1, ln(1/p_L)} = 16 max{1,(M+1) ln 2},
  d_* = min{a/2, 1/(2 C_0)} > 0,
  c = min{d_*/2,1/2} = d_*/2.                                  (1.3)

For actual Gamma there are initially no quantitative caps on any free W site.

The conditional uniform law is on a convex polytope cut out by NN inequalities,
pins, and vertex sign intervals. Its coordinate marginals are log-concave.
For completeness, the small-ball constant in (1.3) can be recovered without
equating it with a. Let X have density f on [0,2] and P(X>a)>=p_L. A log-concave
density has increasing hazard f(s)/S(s), S(s)=P(X>s): the ratios
f(s+u)/f(s) decrease with s, so S(s)/f(s) decreases wherever f(s)>0. Thus for
0<=s<=a/2 with f(s)>0,
  p_L <= S(a) <= S(s) exp(-(a-s) f(s)/S(s))
                  <= exp(-(a-s) f(s)),
and f(s)<=16 ln(1/p_L)<=C_0. The claim is trivial where f(s)=0. Consequently
  P(X<=d_*) <= C_0 d_* <= 1/2.                                  (3.1)
The same argument holds under all allowed caps on other sites, because the
insertion tail is uniform and the capped polytope remains convex.

The sign/cap uniform law also has the FKG property: coordinatewise minimum
and maximum preserve all NN constraints, pins and vertex intervals, so its
indicator density has the lattice condition. Equivalently apply finite-lattice
FKG and bounded-polytope limits. Given a history of success/failure tests at
other positive sites, first impose caps X_j<=d_* on its failure set. Under
that capped law, the event X>d_* and the intersection of all recorded success
events are increasing. FKG and (3.1) therefore give
  P(X>d_* | full prior success/failure history, Gamma) >= 1/2.    (3.2)
Histories of probability zero need not be conditioned on. At its own test,
X remains uncapped, as required by the scope correction.

5. ORDERED PIN/WALL COMPARISON IN THE SAME FREE COORDINATES

We use the following elementary finite comparison. Two bounded, positive-volume
uniform unit-Lipschitz polytopes have the same free graph and fixed set. Suppose
their pin vectors and both endpoints of each free vertex interval are ordered
coordinatewise. If they have a common feasible free lattice point for all
sufficiently fine meshes 1/N in the chosen subsequence, their coordinate means
are ordered in the same direction.

Add a common inactive large box to bound the free coordinates. A one-site
conditional interval has endpoints
  max(lower wall, neighbor heights minus 1),
  min(upper wall, neighbor heights plus 1),
including incident pin values among the neighbors. Rounding to a lattice
preserves both endpoint orders, even for real non-lattice pin values. Common
random coordinate updates and the same uniform quantile preserve ordered
states, starting from the common feasible lattice point.

Each finite lattice heat-bath chain is irreducible and aperiodic. Its feasible
set is closed under coordinatewise minimum, so has a feasible minimum m.
If h_i>m_i, lowering h_i by 1/N cannot violate a lower wall or a fixed pin
because h_i-1/N>=m_i. A blocking free neighbor must be exactly one unit higher,
and that neighbor is also above its coordinate in m. Following blockers cannot
cycle, so some coordinate can be lowered. Repetition reaches m; reversed moves
are also allowed. Each heat-bath move and holding event has positive probability.
Uniform lattice laws are stationary. Pass the coupled marginals to equilibrium,
then pass normalized lattice sums to Lebesgue integrals on the bounded full-
dimensional polytopes. This proves the stated comparison. Real pins cause no
obstruction; only free/free edge capacity 1 needs the integer-mesh equality.

The required common starts are explicit in each application below. We do not
invoke a general theorem that arbitrary interval enlargement is monotone.

10. COUNTERCONTROLS AND SCOPE

One cannot replace the actual law by an arbitrary fixed-wall law. Free W
coordinates may be arbitrarily close to zero under (2.1): realize the same
sign cell by free heights +/-epsilon for any sufficiently small epsilon>0,
with boundary pins +/-1/2. All free-edge constraints remain strict. Fixing
a positive queried A_x< c makes its mean equal A_x<c. Conditioning that tested
site by 0<h_x<=d_*/2 also destroys the fair-mark claim at threshold d_*.
This is why marks are only on uncapped FREE sites and why A is auxiliary.

Nor is the reference mean c true for each fixed mark vector. Even the simple
one-free-site NN kernel with one pin B is uniform on [B-1,B+1], of mean B.
When B is fair on {0,d_*}, only the averaged reference mean equals c. This
elementary kernel is an algebraic illustration, not a claimed Gamma sample.

The theorem is global within finite components. It does not require a queried
opposite partner near each unqueried bulk site, a harmonic representation,
screened dynamics, macroscopic trace geometry, or a survival/landing estimate.
Those are separate gates for a continuum application. It does not assert
a symmetric negative [-1/2,-c] range for this asymmetric Gamma, or equality
in law to an affine diagonal contour. No scan, numerical integration, SLE,
external expert validation, formal proof replay, or novelty claim occurs here.
