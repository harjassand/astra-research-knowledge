Worker c01_l02; 2026-10-07 UTC

This sharpens the INITIAL two-column Laplace transfer. The useful transfer is
ordinary one-column symmetric basis exchange on the two *full column bases*.
For paired block bases it has only O(d) candidate mismatch tags (rather than
O(d^2)), admits an exact reversible exchange kernel on the enlarged pair-of-
bases space, and gives a pointwise local-capacity lower bound. A cycle family
shows both why direct paired exchange/ambient conditioning fails and that a
polynomial-energy interpolation can nevertheless exist for that family. The
general two-replica target energy gate remains open.

1. UNIVERSAL TWO-BASIS IDENTITY

Let V be a d-by-N matrix over C, and let A,B be ordered nonsingular d-by-d
column submatrices using labeled columns from V. Fix e=A_i in A\B. For each
f=B_j in B\A, let A^{ij} be A with its i-th column replaced by f, and B^{ji}
be B with its j-th column replaced by e; retain the original slots/order.
Set C=A^{-1}B. Then C^{-1}=B^{-1}A, and determinant multilinearity gives

  det(A^{ij}) = det(A) C_{ij},
  det(B^{ji}) = det(B) (C^{-1})_{ji}.

For all j=1,...,d, the diagonal matrix-product identity gives

  sum_j C_{ij}(C^{-1})_{ji} = (C C^{-1})_{ii}=1.

If B_j is a labeled column common to A and B, then B_j=A_k for some k != i
(because e is not common), so C_{ij}=delta_{ik}=0. Thus common columns add
zero terms and the sum restricts to B\A. Consequently the signed, exact
identity is

  det(A) det(B) = sum_{f=B_j in B\A} det(A^{ij}) det(B^{ji}).       (1)

No positivity or real-valuedness is used. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to (1), with
k=|B\A|, gives

  |det(A)det(B)|^2 <= k sum_{f in B\A}
      |det(A^{ij})|^2 |det(B^{ji})|^2.                              (2)

This is stronger for paired bases than the two-column expansion in INITIAL:
if A=A_S and B=A_T are nonzero paired block bases, S,T each have r blocks,
and i0 is a site in S\T, choose either column e in block i0. Then
B\A consists of exactly the 2|T\S| labeled columns from the differing blocks.
For every candidate f in a site j in T\S, j != i0. The outputs are full-size
column sets and, whenever the exchange term is nonzero, are ordinary full
column bases; each has exactly one column at each of sites i0 and j. All other
selected blocks remain paired. Tag the output by (e,f). Restoring
the two exchanged labels recovers the source pair, so the tag is reversible.
Thus the mismatch is explicitly two-site and cannot be erased by projection.
Here k=2|T\S|<=d, instead of binom(2|T\S|,2) terms in the block Laplace
route.

2. EXACT PROPOSAL AND REVERSIBLE KERNEL

Write w(A)=a(A)|det(A)|^2, where a(A) is the product of positive per-column
activities. For a pair X=(A,B), let mu(X)=w(A)w(B). A symmetric exchange
preserves the multiset union of columns, so a(A)a(B) is unchanged. Define

  R_{ij} = |C_{ij}|^2 |(C^{-1})_{ji}|^2.

For Y_{ij}=(A^{ij},B^{ji}), the replacement formulas show
mu(Y_{ij})=mu(X) R_{ij}. Therefore for fixed e=A_i the exact proposal

  q(f=B_j | X,e) = R_{ij}/sum_{g=B_l in B\A} R_{il}

is the same as normalizing the output determinant-product-squared weights.
The denominator is positive by (1), and (2) gives
sum_l R_{il} >= 1/k. This proposal requires neither the paired partition nor
any coefficient f(U). The register is the labeled column pair (e,f), not a
block-level projection.

There is also a simple globally reversible kernel on the enlarged pair-of-
bases space. Its states are all ordered pairs X=(A,B) of nonsingular
d-column subsets of the column ground set, with unnormalized target mu(X).
Join X to Y by any valid symmetric exchange e in A\B, f in B\A. There are at
most d^2 distinct neighbors. With D=d^2 set

  P(X,Y) = mu(Y) / (D (mu(X)+mu(Y)))       for each such Y != X,
  P(X,X) = 1 - sum_{Y != X} P(X,Y).

The row is stochastic because each summand is at most 1/D and the degree is
at most D. The reverse move exchanges f and e, and

  mu(X)P(X,Y) = mu(X)mu(Y)/(D(mu(X)+mu(Y))) = mu(Y)P(Y,X),

so detailed balance is exact. In implementation use
P(X,Y)=R_{ij}/(D(1+R_{ij})); determinants, activities, and the unknown
normalizer cancel. The global unnormalized measure is the product of two
ordinary volume-sampling measures; its normalizer is
det(V diag(lambda) V*)^2 by Cauchy-Binet when column activities are lambda.
The exchange kernel itself preserves the multiset union, so it is reducible
across different union-components; reversibility does not claim ergodicity.
Inside a fixed union its support graph is the two-basis symmetric-exchange
reconfiguration graph (after contracting A intersect B and restricting to the
union). Do not infer that this graph is connected from the single-exchange
axiom: in July 2026 Larson gave a rank-9 binary matroid with a disconnected
basis-pair graph, using an invariant that separates complement-basis pairs
with split counts 3 from those with counts 7 or 9. See the author's primary
preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02208, Example 1.4 and Theorem 1.5. The
preprint discloses LLM-assisted discovery and gives a written proof. This is a
counterexample for binary matroids, not automatically for a matrix over Q(i):
its binary representation cannot simply be reinterpreted in characteristic
zero. Thus it blocks a generic matroid-axiom connectivity argument but is not
a counterexample to the specific complex-linear kernel here. The 2026 proof
for regular matroids gives polynomial exchange paths there, but arbitrary
rational-complex hard-BCS inputs are not assumed regular; see
https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07130, Theorems 1.1-1.2 (published in JCTB 177,
2026). Even connectivity on the actual represented subclass would still
leave a weighted congestion/Poincare estimate to prove.

The pointwise inequality gives a local conductance guarantee. Let C(X,Y) be
the unnormalized reversible edge conductance mu(X)P(X,Y), and consider only
the neighbors obtained from this fixed e. Let k=|B\A|. Equation (2) says
sum_f mu(Y_f)>=mu(X)/k. If some mu(Y_f)>=mu(X), its edge conductance is at
least mu(X)/(2D). Otherwise every edge has conductance at least mu(Y_f)/(2D),
so their sum is at least mu(X)/(2Dk). In either case,

  sum_{f in B\A} C(X,Y_f) >= mu(X)/(2Dk) >= mu(X)/(2d^3).            (3)

Thus no source pair loses all one-step reversible capacity to an exponentially
small determinant ratio. This is a *local* bound only; it does not bound
conductance across arbitrary cuts or a Poincare constant.

3. COSTS

For rational-complex input V with total binary input length L, C=A^{-1}B and
C^{-1} can be computed by exact Gaussian-rational linear algebra in O(d^3)
field operations. The d^2 values R_ij and the at-most-d^2 transition
probabilities then require O(d^2) further rational operations. The bound on
the bit lengths of minors from Hadamard, together with fraction-free
elimination, keeps all relevant numerator/denominator lengths polynomial in
d and L. Equivalently, clear all input denominators to Gaussian integers;
all basis determinants become Gaussian integers with O(d(L+log d))-bit real
and imaginary parts, and the common activity/determinant scale cancels from
the transition probabilities. Exact rational categorical sampling over at
most d^2 outcomes uses polynomially many random bits in expectation. A direct
recomputation of every output determinant would cost O(d^5) arithmetic;
using C and C^{-1} reduces the exchange-kernel computation to O(d^3).
Storage is O(d^2) rational entries, plus O(d log N) bits for a pair of bases
and the mismatch labels. These are per-step bounds, not a mixing or counting
bound.

4. EVEN-CYCLE TEST FAMILY: CONDITIONING FAILS, A MISMATCH LIFT PASSES

For every even n=2r, let F be the cyclic-shift permutation, so pair block i
in W=[I,F^T] is (e_i,e_{i+1}) with indices modulo n. For S of size n/2,
coordinate row j occurs x_j+x_{j-1} times, where x_j=1_{j in S}. The paired
determinant is nonzero exactly when x_j+x_{j-1}=1 for every j. There are
exactly two alternating S, each with determinant of magnitude one. Hence the
paired support has no single-block exchange edge.

Every full basis of [I,F^T] chooses exactly one of the two labeled copies of
each standard basis vector; all 2^n such bases have determinant magnitude
one. The paired fiber has mass 2 of total mass 2^n, so rejection from ordinary
volume sampling accepts with probability 2^{1-n}. For two independent
replicas, the probability that both are in the paired fiber is 2^{2-2n}.
This rules out generic conditioning of the ordinary volume sampler as a
polynomial-time paired sampler.

There is a positive, but special, repair in the same family. For an ordered
pair whose union consists of all 2n labeled columns, each valid basis pair
assigns exactly one copy of each row to A and the other to B. The exchange
graph on these states is the n-dimensional hypercube: its n valid moves swap
the two copies of one row. Every pair weight is one. Under the reversible
kernel above (D=n^2), each hypercube edge has probability 1/(2n^2). The two
paired endpoints are antipodes x0 and x1. For endpoint values a,b, define on
the augmented state (A_x,B_{1-x}) the extension

  g(x)=a+(b-a) dist_H(x,x0)/n.

For a unit endpoint difference, the two-point paired variance is 1/4, while
the uniform hypercube extension has variance 1/(4n) and Dirichlet form
1/(4n^3). Thus the endpoint variance is exactly n^3 times this augmented
Dirichlet form. The standard-library Bareiss checker
cycle_family_lift_check.py verifies the paired supports for n=4,6,8 and
exactly enumerates the extension variance and Dirichlet form in those cases;
the formulas above prove the family statement for all even n. This shows a
polynomial mismatch-aware energy lift can cross the disconnected paired
support in this test family, but supplies no extension for general F.

5. REMAINING GATE / SCOPE

The first reusable gate is now stronger than INITIAL: (1)-(3) provide a
computable one-column exchange distribution, an exact reversible local kernel
on enlarged basis pairs, and a polynomial local capacity lower bound. The
four-cycle's lost split mass is therefore accessible to a reversible kernel.
The cycle family also rejects the tempting claim that exponential paired
fiber rarity by itself rules out an energy lift.

Still missing is the central general result: a polynomial-bit construction of
an extension of every paired observable to mismatch states, together with a
global congestion/Poincare estimate uniform along the hard-BCS annealing path.
The local capacity bound (3) does not control a bottleneck between mismatch
components; the pair-exchange kernel preserves column union and can be
reducible; and neither ordinary-volume conditioning nor the cycle-specific
Hamming interpolation handles general determinant weights. No microscopic
two-replica energy theorem, general paired sampler, or FPRAS is claimed.

The standard determinantal comparator remains Anari et al., “Log-Concave
Polynomials IV,” arXiv:2004.07220v2, Theorem 1 (down-up mixing for homogeneous
log-concave generating polynomials), https://arxiv.org/html/2004.07220v2.
It applies to standard DPP/volume-sampling laws with neighboring-weight access;
the paired-block law in the cycle family is not connected under whole-block
single exchanges. The precise source boundary and other OA113 comparison
details are in immutable INITIAL.txt. The current result is an internal
algebraic derivation and exact small-instance audit, not external review or a
priority claim.
