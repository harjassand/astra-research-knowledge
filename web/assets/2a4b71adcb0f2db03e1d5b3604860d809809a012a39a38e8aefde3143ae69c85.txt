Frozen record: 2026-10-09

Claim status
------------

INTERNALLY DERIVED: the symmetric, finite-volume recycling model of Sugimori,
Hyuga, and Saito has two absorbing homochiral states only because its reaction
network omits all reverse channels. Its stochastic selection is a finite-size
absorption effect, not a nonzero stationary mean and not a robust thermodynamic
limit at fixed observation time. The martingale and finite-time bound below are
exact.

CONSTRUCTED / INTERNALLY CHECKED: a finite-range, parity-symmetric lattice of
interconverting chiral molecules with homochiral contact interactions and a
finite-affinity fuel-driven local cycle has the exact Ising Gibbs distribution
as its unique finite-volume stationary law. The cycle has nonzero current and
positive extensive entropy production, while the thermodynamic chiral-ordering
transition survives unchanged. This is a constructive candidate network, not
an identified prebiotic chemical pathway.

SOURCE-REPORTED: a four-site chiral-molecule model has been simulated with a
racemic-to-L-rich/D-rich liquid transition and a reported 3D-Ising finite-size
scaling signature. This supports the plausibility of homochiral contact
interactions in a molecular model only; it is not experimental validation in a
real prebiotic mixture.

UNRESOLVED: whether a specific plausible prebiotic molecule has the required
interconversion rate, positive effective contact coupling, contact-graph
connectivity, and accessible finite-affinity cycle simultaneously. The exact
driven construction uses engineered state-dependent transition barriers; no
empirical rate set is claimed.

1. Target and conclusion
------------------------

The target is a symmetry-unbiased mechanism by which finite microscopic
fluctuations yield a robust macroscopic handed phase without assuming that a
product catalyzes its own enantiomeric production. The exact condition is not
“noise alone.” A finite symmetric irreducible network has a symmetric unique
stationary law. Macroscopic handedness requires either (i) absorbing classes
created by a singular kinetic boundary, (ii) a thermodynamic limit with
parity-related phases and a diverging switching barrier, or (iii) an explicitly
applied parity-odd bias. The construction here realizes (ii), with finite fuel
affinity and detailed accounting of the rates and fuel cost.

There is an important separation of roles. Achiral pair interactions create
the ordering transition; fluctuations select one of its two signs in each
finite-volume run. A local fuel cycle drives the chemistry out of equilibrium
without favoring either sign and without changing the exact stationary
distribution. Thus the construction proves compatibility of robust handed
phases with finite achiral driving; it does not claim that the drive alone
causes ordering when homochiral contacts are absent.

2. General finite-system symmetry obstruction
----------------------------------------------

Let a finite continuous-time Markov chain have state space Ω, generator Q, and
an involution P that exchanges every L state with its reflected D state. Exact
achirality means Q(Px,Py)=Q(x,y) for every pair of states. If the chain is
irreducible, it has a unique stationary distribution π. The reflected law
Pπ is also stationary, since Q commutes with P. Uniqueness therefore gives
Pπ=π. For every odd order parameter M(Px)=−M(x),

                         E_π[M] = 0.                         (1)

This does not rule out a bimodal law with peaks at ±M*. It rules out a finite
irreducible achiral system having a stationary ensemble preference for one
hand. A trajectory may spend a very long time in one well, but a finite
ergodic chain eventually visits its mirror well. A persistent sign requires a
limit in which the switching time diverges, a non-ergodic absorbing boundary,
or an external odd field. This distinction separates finite-size switching,
deterministic pitchforks, and genuine thermodynamic symmetry breaking.

3. Exact audit of the stochastic “recycling” mechanism
------------------------------------------------------

The primary model in Sugimori, Hyuga, and Saito has the reactions

                 A → R,       A → S,       R + S → 2A,

with equal first-order production rate k₀ and second-order cross-removal rate
μ₀/V. The total molecule count N=A+R+S is fixed. At state (A,R,S), the jump
rates are k₀A for each production channel and μ₀RS/V for cross-removal. Set
D=R−S. Production jumps change D by +1 or −1 at equal rates; cross-removal
changes D by zero. The exact generator identities are

                         ℒD = 0,
                         ℒD² = 2k₀A.                       (2)

Thus D_t is a bounded martingale. The states (A,R,S)=(0,N,0) and (0,0,N) are
absorbing because both A→R/S production channels stop when A=0 and there is no
opposite enantiomer for R+S→2A. Every other state can reach an absorbing state
with positive probability, so finite-state absorption occurs almost surely.
Optional stopping then gives, for initial D₀,

 P(R-only absorption) = (N+D₀)/(2N),
 P(S-only absorption) = (N−D₀)/(2N).                      (3)

The racemic initial condition therefore gives 1/2 for each sign. The mean
signed chirality remains zero across runs; the mean absolute terminal ee is
one only because each run ends in one of two absorbing states.

The finite-time result is sharper than the asymptotic phrase “noise creates
homochirality.” Since A_t≤N, (2) yields

 E[D_t²] = D₀² + 2k₀ E[∫₀ᵗ A_s ds] ≤ D₀² + 2k₀Nt,

so for D₀=0 and any ε>0,

 P(|D_t|≥εN) ≤ 2k₀t/(ε²N).                              (4)

At every fixed physical time this probability tends to zero as N→∞. The limits
do not commute: taking t→∞ first gives a random absorbing sign, while taking
N→∞ first leaves a racemic deterministic state at every finite t. In the
small-cross-removal regime, a quasi-steady diffusion approximation on the
neutral line gives a characteristic absorption time of order
V/μ₀=N/(cμ₀), where c=N/V. This agrees with the primary paper’s reported
linear-in-N slow selection time. Dilution lowers the encounter rate and
lengthens that time further.

The network also fails a closed-system detailed-balance check as written. Its
three forward stoichiometric vectors sum to zero:

       (A→R) + (A→S) + (R+S→2A) = 0.                       (5)

This is a closed stoichiometric cycle with every reverse rate set to zero, so
its cycle affinity is infinite. For reversible elementary steps with finite
standard chemical potentials, the Wegscheider constraint is

                  K₁ K₂ K₃ = 1,                            (6)

for K₁=a_R/a_A, K₂=a_S/a_A, and K₃=a_A²/(a_Ra_S). Adding positive reverse
rates removes the absorbing states and restores irreducibility. In a closed
system, rate constants satisfying (6) give detailed balance; in a chemostatted
system, positive reverse rates alone do not imply detailed balance, because
finite cycle affinities can remain. Either way, the original absorption proof
no longer applies. For an ideal, noninteracting mixture with equal R/S standard
free energies, the reversible equilibrium is symmetric and has only
O(N^{-1/2}) composition fluctuations, not macroscopic ee.

The 2009 open-system paper reports inflow of enantiomers and outflow of
heterogeneous dimers, and reports an asymptotic stochastic homochiral state.
The abstract alone is not enough to assess its complete finite-state
generator. Any version with strictly positive symmetric L and D inflow on a
finite state space is irreducible after suitable bounded outflows are included;
then (1) forces a symmetric stationary law. A double peak or long-lived
conditional state remains possible, but “asymptotically homochiral” must be
checked against irreducibility and the order of limits. The 2008 closed model
equations above were accessible in full and are the one audited here.

4. Finite-affinity, parity-even driven contact network
------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Chemical state space and achiral interaction

Take a periodic square lattice with one interconverting chiral molecule at
each site i. Let s_i=+1 denote L and s_i=−1 denote D. A local enantiomeric
flip is chemically possible (racemization/epimerization) and contact energies
favor like-handed neighbors. The achiral energy is

                 H(s) = −J Σ_{⟨ij⟩} s_i s_j,       J>0.   (7)

If E_same is the energy of an L-L or D-D contact and E_opp is the energy of an
L-D contact, then J=(E_opp−E_same)/2 after dropping a chirality-independent
constant. Equation (7) is parity-even: H(−s)=H(s). This interaction is a
thermodynamic contact preference, not a molecule catalyzing its own synthesis.
It is an explicit physical assumption that must be measured or calculated for
any proposed compound.

Partition sites into fixed disjoint horizontal dimers b. A dimer’s four
configurations are c₀=LL, c₁=DL, c₂=DD, c₃=LD. For a fixed configuration η of
all sites outside b, let E_k(η)=H(c_k,η) and

       ρ_k(η)= exp[−βE_k(η)] / Σ_{r=0}^3 exp[−βE_r(η)].     (8)

The local reaction cycle is

       LL → DL → DD → LD → LL,                              (9)

where each arrow epimerizes one molecule in the dimer. Couple each forward
arrow to consumption of one achiral fuel molecule F and production of waste W;
the reverse arrow consumes W and regenerates F. Under whole-system reflection,
LL↔DD and DL↔LD, so each forward arrow maps to another forward arrow. The fuel
cycle is therefore parity-even and has no L/D preference.

For any fixed external contact configuration η, choose constants C>I>0 with
units of probability flux per time. For the oriented edge c_k→c_{k+1} (indices
modulo 4), assign

 q_k^+(η) = (C+I)/ρ_k(η),
 q_k^−(η) = (C−I)/ρ_{k+1}(η).                              (10)

All rates are strictly positive and finite. Since a dimer has a finite number
of contact neighbors, ρ_k is bounded away from zero for fixed finite βJ and
coordination, so the maximum rate is finite. C can be reduced to fit any
specified upper bound on absolute reaction rate without changing the
stationary law or affinity. The finite set of local neighbor patterns can be
represented by a finite set of contact-complex reaction channels; no
system-size-dependent rate constant is used.

4.2 Local detailed balance, finite drive, and exact stationary law

From (8) and (10),

 q_k^+/q_k^− = [(C+I)/(C−I)] [ρ_{k+1}/ρ_k]
              = exp[A − β(E_{k+1}−E_k)],
 A = ln[(C+I)/(C−I)] ∈ (0,∞).                              (11)

Equation (11) is local detailed balance for a forward transition consuming a
fuel with chemical-potential drop Δμ=k_BT A. It does not impose a hidden
infinite-affinity step or an arbitrary violation of microscopic reversibility.
For example, I/C=1/2 gives A=ln 3 per step and cycle affinity 4 ln 3. The
reverse channels have finite positive rates.

The conditional probability flux along every forward edge is
ρ_k q_k^+=C+I, and along the reverse edge it is
ρ_{k+1}q_k^−=C−I. Thus the net current is 2I on each edge. At every cycle
state, one forward and one reverse edge enter and leave, so (ρ₀,…,ρ₃) is
stationary despite the current. Each dimer’s entropy production rate, in
units of k_B per time, is

                σ_b = 4(2I)A = 8IA > 0.                    (12)

The net fuel consumption is 8I molecules per dimer per unit time. With N sites
and N/2 dimers, total fuel use is 4NI per time and chemical power is
4NI k_BT A. Costs are extensive in the number of molecules, not hidden in a
zero-cost drive.

For each fixed η, the dimer generator preserves its conditional Gibbs law
(8). The full Gibbs law

                 π(s) ∝ exp[−βH(s)]                        (13)

has precisely those conditional laws for every dimer, so every local block
generator preserves π and their sum preserves π. Positive rates on all four
cycle edges make every dimer configuration reachable; updating all dimers
makes the finite full chain irreducible. Therefore (13) is its unique
stationary distribution. Because I>0, edge probability fluxes differ and
detailed balance fails; the cycle affinity is 4A, finite and nonzero. Because
the cycle orientation is preserved under parity, the full driven generator
still obeys (1). This is an exact finite-affinity driven network with a
symmetric finite-volume law and nonzero fuel dissipation.

This construction is deliberately explicit about what supplies the
nonlinearity: pair-contact free energy and local configuration-dependent
epimerization barriers. No chiral molecule catalyzes production of itself.
The drive changes kinetic currents but, by construction, not the invariant
thermodynamic measure. It demonstrates finite-affinity compatibility and a
separable current signature; it is not a claim that generic fuel-driven
networks have this invariant law.

4.3 Ordering threshold, finite size, and switch signature

For the square-lattice Ising measure (13), the exact infinite-volume critical
condition is

            sinh(2βJ_c)=1,     βJ_c=0.44068679… .           (14)

Below T_c (βJ>βJ_c), the two extremal phases have spontaneous magnetizations

      m*=±[1−sinh^{-4}(2βJ)]^{1/8}.                          (15)

At finite N the unique law is exactly a 50:50 mirror-symmetric mixture in the
large-volume regime, with peaks near ±m*. Thus a single sample has a robust
random handedness while the ensemble mean remains zero. In the thermodynamic
limit, imposing plus or minus boundary conditions selects the corresponding
pure phase. Equivalently,

 lim_{h→0+} lim_{N→∞} E[m] = m*,
 lim_{N→∞} E[m]_{h=0} = 0,                                  (16)

where h couples as −hΣ_i s_i. If a tiny parity-odd energy h is present, the
two-phase approximation gives

 P(+) ≈ [1+exp(−2βhNm*)]^{-1}.                              (17)

To favor one sign with probability at least 1−δ requires
h ≥ [k_BT/(2Nm*)] ln[(1−δ)/δ]. The selected sign is therefore random under
exact symmetry; any systematic sign needs a measured odd bias or boundary
condition.

For local spin-flip dynamics below T_c on a periodic square, a path from the
plus phase to the minus phase must cross a configuration with macroscopic
interfaces. The equilibrium probability of the bottleneck set is
exp[−2βσ(T)L+o(L)] for a square torus, where σ(T)>0 is the interfacial free
energy per unit length (the factor 2 counts the two interfaces). Since the
constructed rates are local and uniformly bounded above at fixed βJ and
finite A, the stationary flux through that set is at most a polynomial in N
times this probability. Consequently the phase dwell time has an exponential
in L lower bound. This gives a finite-size switching signature distinct from
the recycling-absorption model’s O(N/(cμ₀)) selection time. The exact
thermodynamic stationary distribution and the finite-current entropy
production are both directly measurable in principle: chirality histograms
should be two-peaked; single-run sign changes should be exponentially
suppressed with linear system width in 2D; mirror-related runs should have
equal sign frequencies; and fuel turnover should show the cycle current in
(12).

4.4 Dilution and chemical accessibility

The lattice construction assumes a connected contact network at unit
occupancy. If only a random fraction p of sites carries an interconverting
chiral molecule and contacts act only along occupied nearest-neighbor bonds,
then for p at or below the square-lattice site-percolation threshold
p_c≈0.592746 (numerical estimate), all interaction clusters are finite
almost surely. Each finite cluster has a
parity-symmetric stationary distribution, so it cannot produce a nonzero bulk
order parameter. Above p_c, connectivity is necessary but not sufficient:
the effective contact coupling must still exceed the diluted Ising critical
line. A mean-field estimate βz pJ>1 is only an estimate and is not used as a
theorem. In a molecular solution this means a dense associating phase,
polymer/aggregate, or other percolating contact network is required; an ideal
dilute solution with independent molecules has only O(N^{-1/2}) ee noise.

The conversion rate also matters. The equilibrium phase transition assumes
the L↔D transitions actually occur on the observation timescale. If
racemization is frozen, total L−D is conserved and a racemic initial condition
can only segregate into opposite-handed domains; it cannot choose a globally
homochiral phase. If racemization is extremely slow, the mathematical
equilibrium phase is kinetically inaccessible. A chemical candidate must
therefore satisfy all three independent gates: positive homochiral contact
coupling, a percolating interaction network at adequate concentration, and
accessible chiral interconversion. The finite-affinity fuel loop adds a fourth
gate only if the system is required to operate as a driven reactor.

6. Executable evidence and reproduction
---------------------------------------

Run from the campaign workspace:

    python3 work/natural/n05_homochirality/verify_driven_contact_network.py

The script exactly enumerates all 2^16 states of a periodic 4×4 lattice,
computes local conditional rates for eight dimer blocks, and checks parity,
local stationarity, finite affinity, stationary mean chirality, net cycle

mirror-energy/probability/rate errors, stationary mean magnetization
3.0×10^{-17}, local stationarity residual 1.3×10^{-17}, affinity error

per time unit, and total entropy production 0.703111864745 k_B per time unit.
The finite 4×4 most-probable
magnetization is −16; its reflected +16 state has equal probability. This is
exact finite-state verification of the specified generator, not an external
chemical validation or a thermodynamic-limit proof. The latter uses the exact

large-volume argument.

7. Failed alternatives and counterexamples
-------------------------------------------

* Independent symmetric production with no coupling: ee is O(N^{-1/2}); no
  macroscopic order.
* The one-way recycling network: gives exact random absorption only through
  its absorbing boundaries; it fails the fixed-time thermodynamic-limit test
  (4), and the isolated closed network has an infinite-affinity cycle.
* Reversibilizing the recycling reactions: finite irreducible symmetric
  systems have zero stationary mean by (1); positive reverse rates do not
  imply detailed balance in a driven chemostat, so affinities must be checked
  separately.
* Homochiral contact ordering below percolation: no infinite connected
  interaction cluster, hence no bulk spontaneous magnetization for finite
  range coupling.
* Frozen chirality: order may spatially segregate, but global ee cannot change
  from zero if chirality is exactly conserved.
* A tiny odd field can pick a sign but is not spontaneous symmetry breaking;
  its finite-size selection threshold is equation (17).

9. Network-level robustness criterion (conditional large-deviation form)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a density-dependent reaction network with count vector n∈Z^k, volume scale
N, concentration x=n/N, stoichiometric jumps ν_r, and propensities
Nw_r(x)+o(N), the path Hamiltonian is

       H(x,p)=Σ_r w_r(x)(exp[p·ν_r]−1),
       L(x,v)=sup_p{p·v−H(x,p)}.                           (18)

If the stationary laws satisfy a large-deviation principle with speed a_N→∞,
write π_N(x≈y)≈exp[−a_N V(y)]. Exact mirror symmetry implies
V(Py)=V(y). Robust random handedness requires two parity-related global
minima y_± with M(y_±)=±m*≠0, plus a barrier separating their neighborhoods
whose height times a_N diverges. A unique racemic minimizer gives only
vanishing macroscopic ee. A pair of minima with an O(1) transition barrier
gives finite switching and no persistent sign. In well-mixed density-scaled
networks a positive quasipotential barrier typically yields
τ_switch≈exp[NΔV]; for a short-range spatial system the slow mode is an
interface and the exponent scales like the interface area instead. This is
the proper driven-network replacement for importing a free-energy argument
without detailed balance.

This statement is conditional on the stationary large-deviation principle and
the stated scaling of the barrier; it is not asserted for every CRN. It gives
an exact audit checklist: establish irreducibility at finite N; establish
parity of the full generator; identify the stationary rate function or
quasipotential; locate its global minimizers; and prove the barrier grows
under the actual volume/dilution scaling. A deterministic pitchfork alone
answers only the infinite-population drift question. The 2008 recycling model
instead has absorbing boundaries and a diffusive neutral direction, with the
different linear-in-N selection time quantified in (4).

The square-lattice threshold and spontaneous magnetization used in (14)–(15)
are the classic exact results of Onsager, “Crystal Statistics. I. A
Two-Dimensional Model with an Order-Disorder Transition,” Phys. Rev. 65,
117–149 (1944), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.65.117, and Yang, “The
Spontaneous Magnetization of a Two-Dimensional Ising Model,” Phys. Rev. 85,
808–816 (1952), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.85.808. They validate the
statistical-mechanical ingredient, not its application to any particular
chemical species.
