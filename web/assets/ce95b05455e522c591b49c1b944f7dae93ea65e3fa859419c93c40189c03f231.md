# Cycle 2: robustness and cost of the weighted-design inverse

## Finding

The exact weighted-projector formula is a canonical least-squares inverse on the lifted Hermitian matrix, and becomes ordinary raw-row least squares when the physical row amplitudes encode the design weights. Its lifted sensing condition number is exactly \(\sqrt{d+1}\), independent of how the positive weights are distributed. A direct perturbation theorem gives separate terms for row-gain calibration, projective-direction calibration, and additive readout error. On Jasper–Mixon's near-minimal Sidon construction, the two design weights are asymptotically near-uniform; their construction does not have an asymptotic weight-concentration problem.

There is a meaningful implementation distinction: if the design weights are only used in the decoder while every unit row has the same additive output-noise level, the weighted inverse is weighted least squares and can lose to ordinary least squares on the same raw rows. If instead the physical row norm is set so \(\|a_j\|^4\propto p_j\), the closed-form inverse is exactly the raw-data ordinary least-squares estimator and is Gauss–Markov optimal among linear unbiased estimators for homoscedastic raw output noise. Known heteroskedastic noise calls for generalized least squares.

## Interface and perturbed-data theorem

Let \(d\ge2\). The supplied nominal directions \(u_j\in\mathbb C^d\) are unit vectors and \(p_j>0\), \(\sum_jp_j=1\), form a weighted complex projective 2-design:
\[
 \sum_jp_j\Pi_j^{\otimes2}=\frac{I+F}{d(d+1)},\qquad \Pi_j=u_ju_j^*.
\]
The unknown is \(x\in\mathbb C^d\), \(X=xx^*\), with \(\|x\|_2\le R\). Each physical row has known nominal amplitude \(t_j>0\), and \(S=\sum_jt_j^4\). For the main amplitude-coded model assume \(p_j=t_j^4/S\). The true normalized row direction \(v_j\) obeys projective chordal calibration error
\[
 \min_{|\omega|=1}\|v_j-\omega u_j\|_2\le\delta,
 \qquad \|v_j\|_2=1,
\]
and the row has an unknown relative *intensity* gain \(\gamma_j\) with \(|\gamma_j|\le\gamma<1\). The raw scalar output is
\[
 y_j=t_j^2(1+\gamma_j)\,|v_j^*x|^2+\eta_j.
\]
The decoder is supplied the nominal \((t_j,u_j,p_j)\), the error bounds \((\delta,\gamma)\), and the raw outputs \(y_j\), but not the realized calibration errors. It returns the nominal-design inverse
\[
 \widehat X=\sum_j\frac{t_j^2y_j}{S}D_j,
 \qquad D_j=d(d+1)\Pi_j-dI.
\]
The channel count is \(m\), one scalar intensity channel per row. \(\eta_j\) is raw-output noise in the same units as \(y_j\); deterministic bounds below allow arbitrary correlated noise. The radius \(R\) is an upper bound, not a known exact norm.

### Theorem (deterministic finite-precision/calibration bound)

Under the interface above,
\[
 \boxed{\|\widehat X-X\|_F\le
 R^2\!\left[\sqrt2\,\gamma+(1+\gamma)\!\left(2\sqrt{d+1}\,\delta+\sqrt{d(d+1)}\,\delta^2\right)\right]
 +\sqrt{\frac{d(d+1)}{S}}\,\|\eta\|_2.}
\]
The bound is uniform over every signal with norm at most \(R\), every admissible rowwise gain/direction error, and every additive error vector. If the desired lifted reconstruction error is \(\varepsilon\), a sufficient calibration budget is
\[
 \sqrt2\gamma+(1+\gamma)\bigl(2\sqrt{d+1}\delta+\sqrt{d(d+1)}\delta^2\bigr)
 \le \varepsilon_{\rm cal}/R^2,
\]
and a sufficient raw-output noise/rounding budget is
\[
 \|\eta\|_2\le\varepsilon_{\rm out}\sqrt{S/[d(d+1)]},
 \qquad \varepsilon_{\rm cal}+\varepsilon_{\rm out}\le\varepsilon.
\]
For small errors, uniform relative intensity-gain error contributes \(O(\gamma R^2)\), and uniform projective-direction error contributes \(O(\sqrt d\,\delta R^2)\). Thus this inverse requires direction calibration on the order of \(\varepsilon/(R^2\sqrt d)\), not just an unspecified “small perturbation.”

### Proof

Contracting the design moment identity gives, for every Hermitian \(H\),
\[
 \sum_jp_j\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jH)\Pi_j
 =\frac{H+\operatorname{Tr}(H)I}{d(d+1)},
 \qquad \sum_jp_j\Pi_j=I/d.
\]
Define the intensity analysis map \(A_pH=(\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jH))_j\), with output inner product \(\langle q,r\rangle_p=\sum_jp_jq_jr_j\). Its frame operator is
\[
 F_p=A_p^*A_p,\qquad F_pH=(H+\operatorname{Tr}(H)I)/[d(d+1)].
\]
It has eigenvalue \(1/d\) on the scalar matrices and \(1/[d(d+1)]\) on traceless Hermitian matrices. Therefore \(F_p^{-1}H=d(d+1)H-d\operatorname{Tr}(H)I\), and the canonical inverse \(F_p^{-1}A_p^*\) has exact operator norm \(\sqrt{d(d+1)}\) from weighted \(\ell_2(p)\) to Frobenius norm. Its norm follows from \((F_p^{-1}A_p^*)(F_p^{-1}A_p^*)^*=F_p^{-1}\).

Write \(z_j=|u_j^*x|^2\), \(\widetilde z_j=|v_j^*x|^2\). The first and second design moments yield
\[
 \|z\|_{\ell_2(p)}^2=\sum_jp_jz_j^2=\frac{2\|x\|_2^4}{d(d+1)}\le\frac{2R^4}{d(d+1)}.
\]
After choosing the phase of each \(v_j\) to minimize its distance to \(u_j\), put \(e_j=v_j-u_j\), so \(\|e_j\|\le\delta\). Then
\[
 |\widetilde z_j-z_j|\le2|u_j^*x|\,|e_j^*x|+|e_j^*x|^2.
\]
Since \(\sum_jp_jz_j=\|x\|_2^2/d\le R^2/d\), \(|e_j^*x|\le\delta R\), and \(\sum p_j=1\), Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
 \|\widetilde z-z\|_{\ell_2(p)}
 \le 2\delta R^2/\sqrt d+\delta^2R^2.
\]
The normalized observation is \(y_j/t_j^2=(1+\gamma_j)\widetilde z_j+\eta_j/t_j^2\). Its difference from \(z_j\) is \(\gamma_jz_j+(1+\gamma_j)(\widetilde z_j-z_j)+\eta_j/t_j^2\). Apply the exact inverse norm and the two bounds above. Because \(p_j=t_j^4/S\) in the amplitude-coded implementation below, \(\sum p_j(\eta_j/t_j^2)^2=\|\eta\|_2^2/S\). This gives the display. \(\square\)

## The raw-row least-squares control is the same estimator

Let the actual *nominal* lifted raw-row operator be
\[
 (A_tH)_j=t_j^2\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jH).
\]
If the physical amplitude encodes the design weights, \(p_j=t_j^4/S\), then
\[
 A_t^*A_tH=\sum_jt_j^4\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jH)\Pi_j
 =\frac{S}{d(d+1)}(H+\operatorname{Tr}(H)I).
\]
Consequently the analytic formula above is exactly \(A_t^\dagger y=(A_t^*A_t)^{-1}A_t^*y\), the ordinary raw-row least-squares solution on the lifted Hermitian variable. The singular values are \(\sqrt{S/d}\) on the trace direction and \(\sqrt{S/[d(d+1)]}\) on the traceless directions, so the lifted condition number is exactly \(\sqrt{d+1}\), independent of the individual \(p_j\).

For independent, zero-mean raw-output errors of variances \(\sigma_j^2\), the exact noise contribution is
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat X-\mathbb E\widehat X\|_F^2
 =K_d\frac{\sum_jt_j^4\sigma_j^2}{S^2},
 \qquad K_d=\|D_j\|_F^2=d^2(d^2+d-1).
\]
For common variance \(\sigma^2\), this is \(K_d\sigma^2/S\), equal to \(\sigma^2\operatorname{Tr}[(A_t^*A_t)^{-1}]\). Hence the estimator is Gauss–Markov optimal among linear unbiased estimators for homoscedastic raw-output noise. If the noise variances are known and unequal, the strongest linear unbiased raw-data control is generalized least squares,
\[
\widehat X_{\rm GLS}=(A_t^*\Sigma^{-1}A_t)^{-1}A_t^*\Sigma^{-1}y,
 \qquad \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2,\ldots,\sigma_m^2),
\]
which is covariance-optimal in that class and can improve on the closed-form inverse. Rank-one PSD constrained raw-row least squares uses the same data and can use the signal prior; there is no information-theoretic advantage of the weighted formula over that equally informed control. The theorem above is a deterministic guarantee for a cheap explicit inverse, not a claim that nonlinear constrained least squares is dominated.

For comparison, if a global minimizer \(\widetilde X\in\{zz^*:z\in\mathbb C^d\}\) of the raw residual is available and \(y=A_tX+e\), then feasibility of \(X\) gives \(\|A_t\widetilde X-y\|_2\le\|e\|_2\). Hence \(\|\widetilde X-X\|_F\le2\sqrt{d(d+1)/S}\,\|e\|_2\). The explicit inverse has the same scale with constant one for arbitrary residuals; rank-one optimization may improve actual error by exploiting the signal class, at additional iterative/global-optimization cost. It sees the same \(m\) data values and nominal rows.

If instead all physical row amplitudes are one and \(p_j\) is used only as a software weight, the same reconstruction is weighted least squares under the implied heteroskedastic model. With independent equal-variance normalized-output noise \(\sigma_z^2\), it has exact risk
\[
 K_d\sigma_z^2\sum_jp_j^2.
\]
Ordinary least squares on the unweighted raw rows is then at least as good as a linear unbiased estimator under homoscedastic noise. The quantity \(m_{\rm eff}=1/\sum_jp_j^2\) is the effective number of rows for the weighted inverse; it can be much smaller than \(m\) for a concentrated design. The row-scaled implementation \(t_j^4\propto p_j\) avoids this mismatch for equal absolute detector noise.

## Exact calibration obstruction

An upper bound cannot eliminate the need to know the absolute gain. Suppose \(\|x\|\) is not supplied and a single common intensity gain \(g\in[1-\gamma,1+\gamma]\) is unknown. For any unit \(v\), the two instances
\[
 (g_-,X_-)=(1-\gamma,R^2vv^*),\qquad
 (g_+,X_+)=\left(1+\gamma,\frac{1-\gamma}{1+\gamma}R^2vv^*\right)
\]
produce identical noiseless intensities for every possible sensing row, but
\[
 \|X_--X_+\|_F=\frac{2\gamma}{1+\gamma}R^2.
\]
Any decoder therefore has worst-case Frobenius error at least \(\gamma R^2/(1+\gamma)\). More measurements cannot break this ambiguity; an independent gain calibration or known signal norm is required. This lower bound is absent when the target is only a unit-norm direction and the norm is known exactly.

## Jasper–Mixon weights and exposure

Jasper and Mixon’s Theorem 7 applies the Bodmann–Haas construction to a Sidon set \(S\) of size \(d\) in a finite abelian group \(G\), with \(N=|G|\). Their unnormalized weights are \(w_\chi=d^2/(2N)\) for the \(N\) character vectors and \(w_r=1/2\) for the \(d\) coordinate vectors. Since their weights sum to \(d(d+1)/2\), the normalized probabilities are
\[
 p_\chi=\frac{d}{N(d+1)},\qquad p_r=\frac1{d(d+1)},\qquad \frac{p_r}{p_\chi}=\frac{N}{d^2}.
\]
The Sidon bound gives \(N\ge d(d-1)+1\); their near-minimal construction has \(N=d^2+O(d^{1.525})\). Therefore \(\tau=\max_j|mp_j-1|=|N-d^2|/[d(d+1)]=O(d^{-0.475})\) in the stated near-minimal regime, and the row-amplitude ratio \(t_r/t_\chi=(N/d^2)^{1/4}=1+O(d^{-0.475})\). The weights are not asymptotically concentrated in the construction used for the \(d^2+O(d^{1.525})\) bound.

For equal-scale unit rows, the same near-uniformity also controls comparison to unweighted raw least squares. Put \(F=\sum p_j|\Pi_j\rangle\langle\Pi_j|\) (the exact design frame operator) and \(F_0=m^{-1}\sum|\Pi_j\rangle\langle\Pi_j|\). If \(\tau<1\), then \((1-\tau)F_0\preceq F\preceq(1+\tau)F_0\). Under identical homoscedastic normalized-output noise, the weighted inverse and unweighted raw OLS risks differ by at most a factor \((1+\tau)/(1-\tau)=1+O(d^{-0.475})\). For the amplitude-coded rows, the inverse and raw OLS coincide exactly, with no weight-ratio penalty.

For arbitrary weighted 2-designs, the weights always satisfy \(p_j\le2/[d(d+1)]\): evaluate the second-moment identity on \(u_j^{\otimes2}\) and retain the \(j\)-term. Thus a design with many more than \(d^2\) points can still have weighted-inverse effective sample size only on the order of \(d^2\), whereas Jasper–Mixon's near-minimal construction has \(m\asymp d^2\) and near-uniform weights. This penalty concerns noisy *software weighting* of unit-row data; amplitude coding converts the formula to raw OLS and eliminates it under the stated additive-noise model.

The base theorem counts all \(m\) intensity rows measured once. It does not assume that a random sample of row indices has been drawn according to \(p\). If directions are instead selected randomly per exposure, each occurrence and its shot-noise law must be counted; exact finite-design identities alone do not grant free coverage of low-probability rows. For \(N_{\rm exp}\) repeated independent raw measurements with allocation \(n_j\) per row, \(\sum_jn_j=N_{\rm exp}\), and variance \(\sigma^2/n_j\), the exact risk becomes
\[
 K_d\frac{\sigma^2}{S^2}\sum_j\frac{t_j^4}{n_j}.
\]
Without a one-read-per-row floor, the minimizing allocation is \(n_j\propto t_j^2\), giving minimum \(K_d\sigma^2(\sum_jt_j^2)^2/(S^2N_{\rm exp})\). With a coverage constraint \(n_j\ge1\), the optimum is the corresponding clipped allocation and requires \(N_{\rm exp}\ge m\). Poisson/shot-noise risk is not inferred from this additive-noise calculation and needs a specified photon-count model.

## Precision, storage, and arithmetic ledger

- **Measurements:** Jasper–Mixon’s construction gives \(m=N+d=d^2+O(d^{1.525})\) scalar intensity channels, with one observation per row in the basic design. It measures all rows; using weighted 2-design weights does not reduce the number of rows or the raw-data acquisition calls.
- **Readout precision:** if each raw scalar lies in a declared range of width at most \(B_{\rm dyn}\) and is rounded to \(b\) bits, a uniform quantizer has max error at most \(B_{\rm dyn}2^{-b}\). Sufficient for readout contribution \(\varepsilon_{\rm out}\) is
  \[
  b\ge\left\lceil\log_2\frac{B_{\rm dyn}\sqrt{m d(d+1)/S}}{\varepsilon_{\rm out}}\right\rceil.
  \]
  The data stream costs \(mb\) bits, plus row/index descriptions if the measurement schedule is not pre-agreed. Signal clipping and quantizer saturation must be excluded by the declared dynamic range.
- **Calibration precision:** a sufficient separate budget is the displayed inequality in \((\gamma,\delta)\). For small errors and a target \(\varepsilon_{\rm cal}\), this requires roughly \(\gamma\lesssim\varepsilon_{\rm cal}/R^2\) and \(\delta\lesssim\varepsilon_{\rm cal}/(R^2\sqrt d)\). An explicit unstructured description of \(m\) complex projective directions at chordal precision \(\delta\) takes \(O(md\log(1/\delta))\) bits, and the \(m\) gains take \(O(m\log(1/\gamma))\) bits. A shared algebraic generator may shorten the nominal design description; it does not remove the need to validate physical row calibration. Calibration shots cannot be converted to a count without their reference, detector, and noise model.
- **Linear reconstruction:** the generic formula needs \(O(md^2)\) arithmetic to accumulate a dense \(d\times d\) matrix and \(O(d^2)\) working storage. For Jasper–Mixon character rows, \(\Pi_\chi(s,t)=\chi(s-t)/d\); the character contribution can be computed by a finite-group Fourier transform of the \(N\) character measurements, followed by filling the \(d^2\) entries and adding the \(d\) coordinate rows. Assuming a standard FFT for the supplied group representation, this is \(O(N\log N+d^2)\) arithmetic and \(O(N+d^2)\) memory. The raw OLS estimator uses the same design identity and same order of work. A generic dense LS solve would cost more and is not the fair baseline.
- **Low-dimensional output:** \(\widehat X\) has \(d^2\) real degrees of freedom. If it is PSD rank one, a vector factor up to phase uses \(2d-1\) real parameters. With noise/model error, PSD rank-one projection or factorization incurs a further perturbation step; the bound proved here is for \(X\), before that step. Retaining every raw intensity is \(m\) scalars, also \(\Theta(d^2)\) at this row count. Thus the algebraic inverse establishes stable reconstruction but does not establish a measurement or raw-data bit advantage; only a justified rank-one factor store can reduce retained state, with its own numerical factorization cost.
- **Evaluation:** a future bilinear query \(a^*Xb\) costs \(O(d^2)\) using dense \(X\), or \(O(d)\) from an available rank-one factor \(x\). The source’s noiseless reconstruction formula is not itself an acquisition or exposure model.

## Primary literature and novelty boundary

1. Balan, Bodmann, Casazza, and Edidin, [*Painless Reconstruction from Magnitudes of Frame Coefficients*](https://www.math.uh.edu/~bgb/pp/BBCE07.pdf), Theorem 3.4 and Remark 3.5, give the equal-weight projective-2-design reconstruction formula and identify its reconstruction operators as the canonical dual frame. The paper's theorem is exact and noiseless; its text has no detector-noise model. The weighted formula here is the same canonical dual-frame calculation after a weighted design or amplitude scaling, not a new inversion algorithm.
2. Jasper and Mixon, [*Nearly Tight Weighted 2-Designs in Complex Projective Spaces of Every Dimension*](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14938v1), Theorem 7 and its proof give the exact two weight levels above; Theorem 4 gives \(n(d)\le d^2+O(d^{1.525})\). Their goal is design cardinality/Zauner's conjecture, not robust phase-retrieval acquisition, detector exposures, or calibration.
3. Alharbi et al., [*Stable Phase Retrieval and Perturbations of Frames*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13681), Theorems 1.2–1.3 quantify degradation of global phase-retrieval stability under sensing-vector perturbation, with dimension-independent bounds in their frame norm. Their target is the nonlinear vector phase-retrieval map, not the weighted lifted inverse or the rowwise relative-gain/additive-noise interface here.
4. Gupta and Dokmanić, [*Total Least Squares Phase Retrieval*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00927), explicitly models both sensing-vector and measurement errors, derives LS/TLS solution sensitivities, and reports optical hardware experiments. This is direct prior art for the general need to account for calibration and output noise, so no broad novelty claim is appropriate. The displayed theorem is a closed-form bound for the special exact weighted-design lifted inverse, with explicit per-row error and precision costs.
5. The local pinned Astra acquisition route was checked selectively; its acquisition topic table/cards contain no phase-retrieval or projective-design stability mechanism. No Astra result is used as a mathematical premise.

## Status and decisive next gate

**Status:** internally derived perturbation theorem and identifiability counterexample; not externally reviewed and not novelty-cleared. The exact inverse is established prior art. The primary literature already contains broad phase-retrieval stability/perturbation and TLS treatments; this report does not claim a new general stability theory.

The decisive next validation is to implement a noisy Jasper–Mixon instance with both (i) the amplitude-coded raw OLS inverse and (ii) unit rows plus weighted inverse and unweighted OLS, under a preregistered homoscedastic and heteroscedastic readout model. Compare against direct lifted GLS and rank-one nonlinear least squares using identical measurements, calibration files, and arithmetic precision. Any physical dose conclusion must then replace additive output noise with a stated Poisson/read-noise model and count calibration exposures. A specialist should review the perturbation bound and confirm the assumed finite-group FFT representation before turning it into an implementation claim.

The small finite check in [`cycle2_check.py`](cycle2_check.py) constructs the \(d=2\), \(G=\mathbb Z_3\), \(S=\{0,1\}\) Bodmann–Haas/Jasper–Mixon design (five rows, weights \(2/9\) and \(1/6\)). It verifies the weighted moment identity to \(1.7\times10^{-16}\), equality of the explicit inverse and raw lifted OLS to \(3.6\times10^{-15}\), and one randomized gain/direction/noise fixture inside the deterministic error bound. This only checks that fixture and arithmetic path; it does not validate the general proof, exposure model, or FFT scaling.
