## Result

I found a structural obstruction to a broad natural representation class:
finite gadgets built from homogeneous Lorentzian local signatures and
Lorentzian bond/matching signatures, with internal wires implemented by
nonnegative fixed-coefficient extraction, always produce a Lorentzian
effective boundary signature. This remains true at arbitrary finite gadget
size, degree, support size, and coefficient bit length. Since an easy-axis
XXZ edge signature has two positive Hessian eigenvalues, no such gadget can
realize that edge gate in the standard row/complemented-column convention.

The result is scoped to the all-Lorentzian gadget class and standard positive
coefficient contractions. It does not exclude high-degree tensors that are
merely log-concave but not Lorentzian/strongly log-concave, signed or complex
cancellation, non-Lorentzian bonds, or a global algorithm using a different
counting primitive. It is not a hardness result for easy-axis XXZ.

## Proof sketch and exact edge boundary

If `p_v` are homogeneous Lorentzian vertex signatures and `x_i+x_j` are the
two-state wire signatures, then their product `F` is Lorentzian by product
closure. Internal coefficient extraction is

    [y^a]F(x,y) = (1/a!) (partial_y^a F)(x,0).

Lorentzian polynomials are closed under partial derivatives and nonnegative
linear substitutions, including setting auxiliary variables to zero. Thus
the nonzero effective boundary signature remains Lorentzian. These are
imported Brändén--Huh facts, not new theorems here. See the detailed derivation
and exact limits in [lorentzian_gadget_closure.md](revisions/lorentzian_gadget_closure.md).

For `alpha>=0`, an Euler edge gate `I+s(kappa I+h)` with
`h=alpha(XX+YY)+gamma ZZ` has weights

    a=1+s(kappa+gamma), b=1+s(kappa-gamma), c=2s alpha,

and boundary polynomial

    q=a(x1*x2+x3*x4)+b(x1*x4+x2*x3)+c(x1*x3+x2*x4).

Its four Hessian eigenvalues are

    a+b+c,
    a-b-c = 2s(gamma-alpha),
    -a-b+c,
    -a+b-c = -2s(gamma+alpha).

For strict `gamma>alpha`, the first and second are positive; for strict
`gamma<−alpha`, the first and fourth are positive. The anisotropy eigenvalues
do not depend on `kappa`. When coefficients are positive, the same eigenmodes
give positive log-Hessian curvature, so the local log-concavity boundary is
exactly `|gamma|<=alpha`.

I also derived the same sharp boundary for exact local exponential gates at
every step `t>0`. The weights of `exp(t(kappa I+h))` are

    a=e^{t(kappa+gamma)},
    b=e^{t(kappa-gamma)} cosh(2 alpha t),
    c=e^{t(kappa-gamma)} sinh(2 alpha t).

The anisotropy eigenvalues simplify to

    a-b-c = e^{t(kappa-gamma)}(e^{2 gamma t}-e^{2 alpha t}),
    -a+b-c = e^{t(kappa-gamma)}(e^{-2 alpha t}-e^{2 gamma t}).

The radial eigenvalue is positive and the remaining eigenvalue is
`-e^{t(kappa-gamma)}(e^{2 gamma t}+e^{-2 alpha t})<0`. Therefore the exact
local gate is Lorentzian iff `|gamma|<=alpha`, independent of the scalar
shift. In a finite product-form Trotter word with distinct variables per gate
occurrence, a positive local curvature remains a positive block of the
global log-Hessian. This extends the finite-word obstruction to exact
exponential gates in that same product-signature representation.

## Costs, status, and decisive next gate

The gadget obstruction is size-independent within its exact class; it incurs
no algorithmic resource bound because it rules out the representation itself.
The accepted easy-plane FPRAS remains conditional on the 5 Oct 2026 Chen--Liu
preprint and its Theorem 5.1/Remark A.4, with the large imported polynomial
arithmetic bound and no end-to-end implementation. Exact-exponential gate
coefficients are generally transcendental, so this derivation does not give
a rational value oracle. The c06_l03 Taylor construction is a complementary
rational route, still within the same cone.

Status: **VERIFIED_INTERNAL, conditional structural no-go** for
Lorentzian-factor gadgets using nonnegative fixed-coefficient contraction;
**UNRESOLVED** for larger classes and the physical easy-axis problem. The
decisive next gate is to test a concrete proposed wider representation's full
boundary polynomial, bond contraction and global count polynomial. Any escape
must exhibit which closure hypothesis it violates and still prove that the
global polynomial meets the Chen--Liu (or alternate) counting interface with
degree, support, coefficient range, bit precision and temperature costs
charged.
