1. COMPLETE ACCURACY LAW, WITH SEPARATELY CHARGED CLASSICAL LABELS

Fix 0<c0<=r sqrt(n)<=c1<infinity, sufficiently large n, and 0<epsilon<=r.
Let Q_min(n,r,epsilon) be the least possible MAXIMUM quantum branch
dimension in a full n-copy qubit density reconstruction with worst-case
half-trace error <=epsilon. Arbitrarily many classical labels are allowed
but charged separately; no purifying reference or entanglement assistance.
The channels and input contract are precisely those of quantum_branch.txt.

Up to positive constants depending only on c0,c1, the candidate law is

  Q_min = Theta(r/epsilon),                   r^2<=epsilon<=r;

  Q_min = Theta( min{n+1,
                    sqrt[n(1+log(r^2/epsilon))]} ), 0<epsilon<r^2.   (1.1)

The upper is a finite, explicit radius-independent tapered-filter archive
with O(n^3) charged labels, at most log_2 Q+O(1) quantum bits, and total
D=O(n^3 Q); exact recovery at Q=n+1. This total D is larger than the exact
Schur tail archive's D=O(n log(1/epsilon)); these constructions optimize
different coordinates. For an unknown-radius ball ||b||<=c1/sqrt(n),
replace r by n^(-1/2) in the order statement; the same code is radius blind.

Consequences for epsilon=n^(-alpha), fixed alpha>1/2:
- 1/2<alpha<1: Q_min=Theta(n^(alpha-1/2));
- alpha=1: Q_min=Theta(sqrt(n));
- alpha>1: Q_min=Theta(sqrt(n log n)).
At errors exponentially small in n, the required branch has order n.
No claim about optimal total information bits follows from these branch laws.

2. EXACT LARGE-SPIN DERIVATIVE ESTIMATES

Use a_j=R_j/(j+1), t=atanh(r), d=2j+1, as defined and proved in
quantum_branch.txt Section 3. Differentiating its smooth real-spin extension
gives exactly

  a'_j=2[(coth(dt)+coth t)/(d+1)^2
               -dt/(d+1) csch^2(dt)].                         (2.1)

Since coth t=1/r and coth(dt)<=1/r, the positive part proves

  a'_j<=1/[r(j+1)^2].                                        (2.2)

If j>=2/r, u=dt>=4. In (2.1) the positive part is at least
2/[r(d+1)^2]. The negative part has magnitude <=2t csch^2(u),
which is <=1/[r(d+1)^2], because

  2rt(d+1)^2 csch^2(u)
       <=8(r/t)u^2/sinh^2(u)<=8u^2/sinh^2(u)<=1,  u>=4.

The last function decreases for u>0 and is below 1 at 4. Hence

  a'_j>=1/[4r(j+1)^2],             j>=2/r.                    (2.3)

The exact Gibbs moment also gives

  a_j>=1-(1+r)/[2r(j+1)]>=1-3/(4rj), r<=1/2.                 (2.4)

For rj>=4, (2.2)-(2.4) yield a_j-j a'_j>=1-7/(4rj)>=1/2.
Consequently

  (a_j/j)'<=-1/(2j^2),            rj>=4.                     (2.5)

These are uniform exact inequalities at arbitrarily large spins; no
compact-scaled-spin expansion or unproved triangular LAN premise is used.

3. A RARE-INPUT BAND GIVES A DEFICIT FOR EVERY OUTPUT SECTOR

Assume Q>=1/r and take an input j in [16Q,32Q]. Use the bounded score and
nonnegative pair deficit of quantum_branch.txt (5.5). There is a numerical
constant c_Delta=1/786432 such that for EVERY output k and every rank-Q
Kraus operator,

  Delta_(jk)>=c_Delta/(r Q^3).                                (3.1)

NEAR OUTPUT: if delta=|j-k|<=M/(16Q), M=max(j,k), then M>=16Q and
the exact Schmidt-Q projector lemma gives energy loss >=M/(32Q).
Also rj>=16, k>=15j/16>=15/r, so a_j,a_k>=1/3. Thus
Delta>=1/[288Q min(j,k)]>=1/(9216Q^2)>=1/(9216r Q^3).
The last inequality uses rQ>=1.

FAR LOWER OUTPUT: k<j(1-1/(16Q)). Let k0=j(1-1/(16Q))>=15/r.
By exact monotonicity and (2.3), with (j+1)^2<=4j^2,

  a_j-a_k>=a_j-a_(k0)
       >=[j/(16Q)]/[4r(j+1)^2]>=1/(256rjQ).

The nonnegative middle term of the score deficit is now
a_j(a_j-a_k)/j>=1/(768rj^2 Q)>=1/(786432r Q^3).
If k=0 the target score >=a_j^2/2 also satisfies (3.1).

FAR HIGHER OUTPUT: k>j/(1-1/(16Q)). Let k0=j/(1-1/(16Q)). By (2.5),

  a_j/j-a_k/k>=integral_j^(k0) du/(2u^2)=1/(32jQ).

Its middle score contribution is at least 1/(96jQ)>=1/(3072Q^2),
which is at least 1/(3072rQ^3). This treats arbitrarily distant outputs
using exact monotonicity, not a claimed asymptotic expansion there.

Every input/output pair outside the selected input band still has
nonnegative deficit by the exact global monotonicities in quantum_branch.txt.
Therefore no sector transport can cancel (3.1).

4. ELEMENTARY SCHUR BAND LOWER MASS: NO LOCAL-LIMIT ASSUMPTION

Assume also Q<=n/128 and n>=4. The selected band corresponds to
L=2j in [32Q,64Q], all of which satisfy L<=n/2. The exact Schur weights
are those of quantum_branch.txt (11.1). Since
sinh((L+1)t)/sinh t>=L+1 and n+L+2<=2(n+1),

  P_L >= [(L+1)^2/(n+1)] pi_0(L)(1-r^2)^(n/2),
  pi_0(L)=2^(-n) binom(n,(n-L)/2).                            (4.1)

The last common factor is >=exp(-2c1^2/3) because r<=1/2 and
log(1-r^2)>=-r^2/(1-r^2). For all parity-allowed 0<=L<=n/2,

  pi_0(L)>=(1/(2sqrt(n))) exp[-(2/3)L^2/n].                   (4.2)

Proof of (4.2): write x=L/n, k=n(1-x)/2. The elementary Stirling bounds
sqrt(2pi k)(k/e)^k <=k!<=sqrt(2pi k)(k/e)^k exp(1/(12k))
give the binomial lower prefactor at least
2exp(-1/6)/sqrt(2pi n)>1/(2sqrt(n)). Its entropy exponent is nI(x),
where I(x)=[(1+x)log(1+x)+(1-x)log(1-x)]/2. Since I(0)=I'(0)=0
and I''(x)=1/(1-x^2)<=4/3 on [0,1/2], I(x)<=2x^2/3.

Combining (4.1)-(4.2), n+1<=2n, L+1>=32Q and at least 16Q
parity-allowed points in the band proves

  P(16Q<=j<=32Q)
     >=4096 exp(-2c1^2/3) Q^3/n^(3/2)
                 *exp[-(8192/3)Q^2/n].                        (4.3)

The right side is only a deliberately loose lower bound; it is never used
as an approximation of the rare mass.

5. FINE-ACCURACY QUANTUM-BRANCH CONVERSE

The score has global width 2, and the actual normalized rank-Q Kraus
weights in each input sector sum to one. From (3.1), (4.3), and the same
exact Choi score identity as quantum_branch.txt (5.4),

  e >= exp(-2c1^2/3)/(384 r n^(3/2))
                         *exp[-(8192/3)Q^2/n]
    >= c_T/n *exp[-C_T Q^2/n],
  c_T=exp(-2c1^2/3)/(384c1), C_T=8192/3,                     (5.1)

whenever 1/r<=Q<=n/128. Classical outcome count is unrestricted; a decoder
need not preserve sectors. This is a maximum-quantum-branch lower bound.

For sufficiently large n, Q0=ceil(1/r)<=n/128. Since r<=1/2,
Q0^2/n<=9/(4c0^2). For EVERY Q<=Q0, monotonicity of the allowed resource
set gives e_Q^*>=e_(Q0)^*>=c_*/n, with

  c_*=c_T exp[-9C_T/(4c0^2)]>0.                              (5.2)

Thus for epsilon<c_*/n, any successful code has Q>Q0, and either Q>n/128
or (5.1) implies

  Q>=sqrt[(n/C_T)log(c_T/(n epsilon))].                      (5.3)

Equivalently it has the minimum of these two lower bounds, with constants.
Because epsilon<c_*/n, the logarithm is uniformly positive and comparable
to 1+log(r^2/epsilon), with constants depending only on c0,c1. For the
remaining interval c_*/n<=epsilon<r^2, quantum_branch.txt (1.2) gives
Q=Omega(sqrt(n)); the candidate expression in (1.1) is also of that order
there, since its logarithm is bounded by a fixed interval-dependent constant.
This proves the finer-accuracy lower side throughout (1.1), including the
linear-n saturation when its logarithm exceeds order n.

6. MATCHING FINITE TAPERED UPPER: EXPONENTIAL BRANCH TAIL

Use exactly the code already specified in quantum_branch.txt Sections 9-11.
Its identity branches have zero error whenever d=L+1<=Q. Hence only
L>=Q contributes. For r<=1/2, the full-thermal error (10.4) gives

  e_j <=(4r/Q)(1+rL)e^(tL), t=atanh r.

With the exact Schur upper weight (11.1) and X a sum of n independent
unbiased +/-1 variables, this proves

  e <=8r/(Qn) E[(X+1)^2(1+rX)e^(2tX) 1_(X>=Q)].             (6.1)

All factors inside the expectation are nonnegative on that tail. Set
Y=(|X|+1)^2(1+r|X|)e^(2tX). Hoeffding and Cauchy-Schwarz give

  E[Y 1_(X>=Q)] <=sqrt(E Y^2) exp[-Q^2/(4n)].                (6.2)

An explicit uniform second-moment bound is sqrt(E Y^2)<=M(c1)n, where

  M(c1)=4 sqrt[ sqrt(105)+1+c1^2(sqrt(10395)+sqrt(3)) ]
                 * exp(128c1^2/9).                           (6.3)

Indeed (|X|+1)^4(1+r|X|)^2
<=16(X^4+1+r^2 X^6+r^2 X^2). Bound each expectation after multiplication
by exp(4tX) by Cauchy-Schwarz, the mgf E exp(uX)<=exp(nu^2/2), and
E X^(2k)<=(2k-1)!! n^k. The latter follows coefficientwise from
(cosh u)^n<=exp(nu^2/2): all series coefficients are nonnegative and each
cosh coefficient is bounded by the corresponding exp(u^2/2) coefficient.
Use moments of order 8,12,4 respectively and nr^2<=c1^2, nt^2<=16c1^2/9.

Consequently for EVERY Q>=1, the explicit finite code obeys

  e <=8M(c1)(r/Q) exp[-Q^2/(4n)].                             (6.4)

For Q>=1/r, r/Q<=r^2<=c1^2/n, giving

  e <=C_U(c1)/n exp[-Q^2/(4n)], C_U=8M(c1)c1^2.               (6.5)

For any epsilon<c_*/n, define the integer trial budget explicitly by

  Q_try=ceil(max{1/r, 2sqrt[n log(C_U/(n epsilon))]}).

If Q_try<=n, use the finite taper code with Q=Q_try. This includes Q_try=n:
the top spin still has dimension n+1 and is tapered, not kept exactly.
If Q_try>=n+1, take Q=n+1 and keep every Schur carrier exactly. This realizes the upper
order in (1.1). For c_*/n<=epsilon<r^2, the simpler e<=C r/Q bound gives
Q=O(sqrt(n)); for epsilon>=r^2 it gives Q=O(r/epsilon).
All codes retain the same explicit O(n^3) classical alphabet; increasing
precision does not add an uncharged continuous label.

7. UNIFORM UNKNOWN-RADIUS BALL AND LIMITATIONS

Put R=c1/sqrt(n), the exact public outer radius, and allow all ||b||<=R.
The code in (6.4) never uses the actual r; its worst-case upper is obtained
by replacing r by R in the bound. A fixed critical sphere inside the ball,
including the endpoint sphere r=R, supplies the converse. Thus, with
constants depending only on the fixed envelope c1, the exact-domain order is

  Q_min=Theta(1),                                      epsilon>=R;
  Q_min=Theta(R/epsilon),                      R^2<=epsilon<=R;
  Q_min=Theta(min{n+1,sqrt[n(1+log(R^2/epsilon))]}),
                                                     0<epsilon<R^2.

At shared endpoints the orders agree. No interval is omitted when c1>1.
The trial-budget prescription above uses the public R in place of r,
so its first term is 1/R;
the actual radius is never estimated or acquired. The exact branch cutoff
remains n+1, including on this ball. This is a
whole-density reconstruction theorem, not one-coordinate archiving.
