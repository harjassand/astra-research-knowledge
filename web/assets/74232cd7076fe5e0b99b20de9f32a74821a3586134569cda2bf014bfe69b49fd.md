# Exact classwise explosion proof

## CTMC interface

Take the two-species reaction diagram

\[
r_1:0\to2A,\quad r_2:2A\to4A+B,\quad
r_3:4A+B\to6A+4B,\quad r_4:6A+4B\to3A.
\]

For fixed constants \(\kappa_i>0\), the count process is the minimal CTMC on
\(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2\) with exact stochastic mass-action propensities
\[
\lambda_i(a,b)=\kappa_i (a)_{\underline{y_{i,A}}}
 (b)_{\underline{y_{i,B}}},\qquad
(q)_{\underline m}=q(q-1)\cdots(q-m+1),
\]
and zero propensity when a source count is insufficient. There is no volume
rescaling or time-varying rate control.

## One closed irreducible class

Let \(C=\{(a,b):a\ge3,\ b\ge0\}\). Reactions \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) increase
the A count, and \(r_4\), whenever enabled, maps \(a\ge6\) to \(a-3\ge3\)
and \(b\ge4\) to \(b-4\ge0\). Thus C is closed.

The following finite reaction words give the indicated changes and are
feasible from the stated starting counts:

| Change | Reaction word | Starting condition |
|---|---|---|
| \((a,b)\mapsto(a,b+1)\) | \(r_2\,r_3^8\,r_4^6\) | \(a\ge3,b\ge0\) |
| \((a,b)\mapsto(a,b-1)\) | \(r_2\,r_3^2\,r_4^2\) | \(a\ge3,b\ge1\) |
| \((a,b)\mapsto(a+1,b)\) | \(r_2\,r_3\,r_4\) | \(a\ge3,b\ge0\) |
| \((a,b)\mapsto(a-1,b)\) | \(r_2\,r_3^9\,r_4^7\) | \(a\ge4,b\ge0\) |

For example, the first word has net change
\((2,1)+8(2,3)+6(-3,-4)=(0,1)\). At its last r4 firing the state
has at least \(a+3\ge6\) A and \(b+5\ge5\) B, so all six firings are
enabled. The other rows follow by the same cumulative-count check; for the
last row, just before the seventh r4, the count is at least \((a+2,b+4)\).

From any \((a,b)\in C\), repeatedly apply the second word until B is zero,
then the last word until A is three. Conversely, from \((3,0)\), use the
first word b times and then the third word \(a-3\) times to reach any
\((a,b)\in C\). Every step of each finite word has positive propensity.
Hence C is one closed irreducible communicating class.

## Exact factorial staircase and explosion

For every integer \(n\ge3\), the following three reactions form one cycle:
\[
(n,0)\xrightarrow{r_2}(n+2,1)
\xrightarrow{r_3}(n+4,4)
\xrightarrow{r_4}(n+1,0).
\]
Each full cycle raises A by one and returns B to zero. At its three states,
the desired reaction rates and total rates are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{state}&\text{desired rate}&\text{total rate}\hline
(n,0)&\kappa_2(n)_{\underline2}&
 \kappa_1+\kappa_2(n)_{\underline2}\\
(n+2,1)&\kappa_3(n+2)_{\underline4}&
 \kappa_1+\kappa_2(n+2)_{\underline2}
 +\kappa_3(n+2)_{\underline4}\\
(n+4,4)&24\kappa_4(n+4)_{\underline6}&
 \kappa_1+\kappa_2(n+4)_{\underline2}
 +4\kappa_3(n+4)_{\underline4}
 +24\kappa_4(n+4)_{\underline6}.
\end{array}
\]
The exact probability \(p_{j,n}\) of selecting the desired channel at the
corresponding row is its desired rate divided by the displayed total rate.
With all four rates fixed and positive,
\[
1-p_{2,n}=O_\kappa(n^{-2}),\quad
1-p_{3,n}=O_\kappa(n^{-2}),\quad
1-p_{4,n}=O_\kappa(n^{-2}).
\]
The first ratio compares an order-zero channel to an order-two channel; the
second compares orders zero and two with order four; the third compares
orders zero, two, and four with order six after B is fixed at four. Every
\(p_{j,n}\) is strictly positive, and the sum of all failure probabilities
is finite. Therefore the infinite product
\[
\prod_{n=3}^{\infty}p_{2,n}p_{3,n}p_{4,n}>0.
\]
It is the probability of following the complete staircase from \((3,0)\).

Conditional on selecting a channel at a state, the holding time (the minimum
of the competing exponential clocks) is independent of which channel wins.
At the three successive states its mean is respectively bounded by
\[
\frac1{\kappa_2(n)_{\underline2}},\qquad
\frac1{\kappa_3(n+2)_{\underline4}},\qquad
\frac1{24\kappa_4(n+4)_{\underline6}}.
\]
Their sum over \(n\ge3\) is finite. By the strong Markov property and the
independence of each holding time from its winning channel, the total elapsed
time conditional on following the infinite prescribed sequence has finite
expectation, hence is finite almost surely. Thus the process makes infinitely
many jumps by a finite time with positive probability while remaining in C.
This is explosion of the minimal CTMC on a closed irreducible class.

The criterion used here is elementary and reusable: if a prescribed infinite
path has conditional channel-selection probabilities \(p_k>0\) with
\(\sum_k(1-p_k)<\infty\), and the conditional mean holding times along it
sum to a finite value, then that path has positive probability and completes
in finite time. No deterministic ODE absorber or event-index potential is
used.
