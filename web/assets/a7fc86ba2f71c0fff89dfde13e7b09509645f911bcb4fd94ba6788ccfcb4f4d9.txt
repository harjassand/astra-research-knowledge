A19 transport computation — Phase II bounded record v1
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: candidate construction internally derived under a strengthened compact-support/coverage assumption; no discovery, novelty, or scalability claim established. The computational open problem in the cited paper is not solved in its original scope. A finite interpolation diagnostic is recorded separately.

1. Target, exact interface, and source boundary

The motivating objective is the explicitly open question in Nietert and Goldfeld, “Estimation of Stochastic Optimal Transport Maps,” NeurIPS 2025 / arXiv:2512.09499v1, Discussion: can a computationally efficient estimator attain the optimal rate when an optimal Lipschitz kernel exists?

For p=1 their loss is

  E_1(kappa; mu,nu)
   = [ int ||x-y|| d kappa(y|x)d mu(x) - W_1(mu,nu) ]_+
       + W_1(kappa#mu,nu).

The first summand is excess transport cost and the second is target-feasibility error. A kernel is scored under the true pair (mu,nu), not only at the observed source atoms. The source paper’s general domain is X,Y subset R^d; its Section 4 Assumption 1 requires an optimal kernel kappa* with W_p(kappa*_x,kappa*_{x'}) <= L||x-x'||^alpha on X. Theorem 3 accepts arbitrary proxies muhat,nuhat satisfying W_p(muhat,mu)<=rho_mu and W_p(nuhat,nu)<=rho_nu, and gives E_p <= O(L rho_mu^alpha+rho_mu+rho_nu). It assumes no density lower bound, global support covering, or compact cube. Corollary 1 specializes to mu,nu in P_{2p}(R^d), uses empirical proxies, and states the minimax rate up to logs with probability .9. The estimator there is the all-kernels Wasserstein-DRO argmin and is explicitly called computationally inefficient.

This record explores a restricted p=1 candidate. Input: n iid source samples X_1,...,X_n and n iid target samples Y_1,...,Y_n; known d, n, rational lower density bound m>0, rational Lipschitz upper bound L>=1, and a rational precision parameter. Assumptions: X=[0,1]^d, Y=[0,1]^d; mu and nu have Lebesgue densities at least m everywhere on the cube; and there is an E_1-optimal kernel kappa* with W_1(kappa*_x,kappa*_{x'})<=L||x-x'|| for all x,x' in the cube. The desired output is a finite-precision evaluable Markov kernel kappa_hat(x) supported on observed target points. This is strictly stronger than the source paper’s support assumptions and materially excludes distributions with holes, lower-dimensional supports, and point masses. The lower density bound is used to acquire a global geometric cover from data; it is not free geometric information.

2. Candidate finite algorithm

Assume n is large enough that M=1 satisfies the cover inequality below, and let M be the largest positive integer satisfying it:

  (M+1)^d exp[- n m d^{-d} M^{-d}] <= delta/2,

Here m is supplied as a rational lower bound and delta in (0,1) is requested failure probability. The cube of side s/d directed into the domain from any grid vertex lies inside its radius-s ball, so d^{-d}s^d is a rational lower bound on ball volume. This computable one-dimensional search defines a regular grid G of the cube with mesh s=1/M and K=(M+1)^d vertices; choosing the largest admissible M makes the grid as fine as the union bound permits. For each grid vertex v, compute a(v) in [n] nearest to v among the source sample and b(v) in [n] nearest to v among the target sample. Exact ties are broken by smallest index. Define the Freudenthal triangulation of every grid cube. For x in a simplex with vertices v_0,...,v_d and barycentric weights lambda_0(x),...,lambda_d(x), the returned kernel will be

  kappa_hat_x = sum_{l=0}^d lambda_l(x) q_{v_l},

where each q_v is a probability vector on the n observed target points. Thus evaluation never requires an ambient-space mesh finer than G; one query needs simplex location and a mixture of at most d+1 stored distributions.

Choose a target-side interpolation kernel T_y as follows. Locate y in its grid simplex and set

  T_y = sum_{l=0}^d lambda_l(y) delta_{Y_{b(v_l)}}.

On the joint cover event A that every grid vertex is within s of some source sample and some target sample, every grid-edge pair of target anchors is at distance at most 3s. For a single simplex, barycentric weights satisfy sum_l |lambda_l(y)-lambda_l(y')| <= 2 sqrt(d)||y-y'||/s when y,y' stay in that simplex. Kantorovich duality, after subtracting the integral against one reference vertex measure, gives

  W_1(T_y,T_{y'}) <= 6 d^(3/2)||y-y'||.

Subdivision of a line segment across simplex boundaries gives the same global bound. Also every target anchor used at y is within (1+sqrt(d))s of y, hence W_1(T_y,delta_y)<=(1+sqrt(d))s. For rational LP coefficients, set A_d=ceil(6d^(3/2)) and B_d=d+1, so Lip(T)<=A_d and the displacement is at most B_d s. Use these integer constants below.

The optimization variables are q_v in P({Y_1,...,Y_n}), one per grid vertex. For each grid edge (v,w), impose

  W_1(q_v,q_w) <= L A_d ||X_{a(v)}-X_{a(w)}||.

For each observed source point X_i, compute its Freudenthal simplex and weights lambda_{iv}. Its induced kernel value is q_{X_i}=sum_v lambda_{iv}q_v. Minimize the empirical E_1 objective

  [ n^{-1} sum_i sum_v lambda_{iv} sum_j q_{v,j}||X_i-Y_j||
       - W_1(muhat,nuhat) ]_+
    + W_1( n^{-1} sum_i q_{X_i}, nuhat ).                         (A)

This is a finite convex program. It is an LP: q variables are simplex vectors; each edge constraint is represented by a nonnegative n-by-n coupling flow z^{vw} with row/column marginals q_v,q_w and cost at most its right-hand side; the final W_1 term is represented by another n-by-n flow; a nonnegative scalar represents the positive-part term. The empirical W_1(muhat,nuhat) is computed once by a standard discrete OT solve. The output is the above interpolated kernel. If A fails, return the constant kernel delta_0; its E_1 loss on the cube is at most 2sqrt(d).

3. Conditional theorem and complete derivation

All statements in this section are internal derivations for the restricted assumptions in Section 1. They do not validate a novelty claim. Treat the LP as solved to additive objective tolerance epsilon_LP and exactly feasible for its rational input. Initially use exact Euclidean costs; finite precision is accounted below.

Coverage. For each grid vertex v, a cube of side s/d directed into [0,1]^d lies inside B(v,s) intersect [0,1]^d, so this intersection has volume at least d^{-d}s^d, including boundary vertices. Since each density is at least m, the probability a given ball contains no sample from one of the two independent size-n samples is at most exp(-n m d^{-d}s^d). A union bound over K vertices and both samples gives P(A^c)<=2K exp(-n m d^{-d}s^d)<=delta by the displayed M choice. The anchors at adjacent grid vertices are at distance at most 3s on A.

Target interpolation. If y lies in a Freudenthal simplex with vertices v_0,...,v_d, then T_y is a mixture of the target anchors at these vertices. For any 1-Lipschitz test function f, subtract int f d delta_{Y_{b(v_0)}} from the signed mixture difference. Each remaining integral has absolute value at most W_1(delta_{Y_{b(v_l)}},delta_{Y_{b(v_0)}}), which is at most 3ds by a path of at most d grid edges. Therefore

  W_1(T_y,T_{y'}) <= 3ds sum_l |lambda_l(y)-lambda_l(y')|
                  <= 6d^(3/2)||y-y'||

inside one simplex. A segment crossing multiple simplices is partitioned into finitely many such pieces, proving the global bound. The displacement claim follows from |Y_{b(v_l)}-y|<=|Y_{b(v_l)}-v_l|+|v_l-y|<=(1+sqrt(d))s and convexity of expectation.

Feasible comparator. Define q*_v=T#kappa*_{X_{a(v)}}. Markov-kernel contraction under a W_1-Lipschitz kernel gives

  W_1(q*_v,q*_w) <= A_d W_1(kappa*_{X_{a(v)}},kappa*_{X_{a(w)}})
                  <= L A_d ||X_{a(v)}-X_{a(w)}||.

Thus q* is feasible for every edge constraint. Its interpolant kappa_tilde satisfies, uniformly in x on A,

  W_1(kappa_tilde_x,kappa*_x)
     <= W_1(T#kappa*_x,kappa*_x)
        + sum_{v in simplex(x)} lambda_v(x) W_1(T#kappa*_{X_{a(v)}},T#kappa*_x)
     <= B_d(1+A_d L)s =: e_h.

Here each source anchor X_{a(v)} is within (1+sqrt(d))s<=B_d s of x, and T is A_d-Lipschitz in W_1. This establishes comparator feasibility and its uniform approximation without assuming the unknown kernel itself is computable.

Empirical objective of comparator. Put rho_mu=W_1(muhat,mu) and rho_nu=W_1(nuhat,nu). For a W_1-Lipschitz kernel of constant L, coupling the two source laws and then optimally coupling the conditional outputs gives

  |C_1(kappa*;muhat)-C_1(kappa*;mu)| <= (1+L)rho_mu,
  W_1(kappa*#muhat,nu) <= L rho_mu,
  |W_1(muhat,nuhat)-W_1(mu,nu)| <= rho_mu+rho_nu.

Since C_1(kappa*;mu)=W_1(mu,nu), it follows that

  E_1(kappa*;muhat,nuhat) <= (2+2L)rho_mu+2rho_nu.

Uniform W_1-kernel error e_h changes the cost term by at most e_h and the target marginal by at most e_h. Hence the feasible comparator kappa_tilde has empirical objective at most (2+2L)rho_mu+2rho_nu+2e_h. The LP minimizer, with additive tolerance epsilon_LP, has no larger objective plus epsilon_LP.

Output regularity and transfer to the population. On A, each source-grid edge satisfies ||X_{a(v)}-X_{a(w)}||<=3s. Thus its constraint gives W_1(q_v,q_w)<=3L A_d s. Any two vertices in one Freudenthal simplex are joined by at most d grid edges, so their q values are within 3d L A_d s in W_1. The same dual subtraction and barycentric-weight estimate as above imply

  Lip_{W_1}(x -> kappa_hat_x) <= 6d^(3/2) L A_d <= 42 d^3 L =: L_hat.

For any W_1-Lipschitz kernel with constant L_hat, direct coupling and the triangle inequality give

  |E_1(kappa;mu,nu')-E_1(kappa;muhat,nu')| <= 2(1+L_hat)rho_mu,
  |E_1(kappa;muhat,nu)-E_1(kappa;muhat,nuhat)| <= 2rho_nu.

Consequently on A,

  E_1(kappa_hat;mu,nu)
   <= (4+2L+84d^3L)rho_mu + 4rho_nu
        + 2B_d(1+A_d L)s + epsilon_LP.                         (B)

The stability inequalities are also stated in Nietert–Goldfeld, Lemmas 3 and 4 for general p; the p=1 version follows from the W_1 triangle inequality. The proof above records the terms to make the supplied/acquired boundary explicit.

For compactly supported laws on [0,1]^d, empirical W_1 convergence has expected rate tilde-O_d(n^{-1/(d vee 2)}). Thus, after the fallback on A^c and with delta=n^{-2}, (B) yields the candidate expected bound

  E E_1(kappa_hat;mu,nu) = tilde-O_{d,L,m}(n^{-1/(d vee 2)}) + epsilon_LP.

The covering term s is order (d^d log(n/delta)/(m n))^{1/d} up to constants depending on d and m. For d>=3 it is at the same n^{-1/d} exponent as the empirical W_1 term; for d=2 it is order sqrt(log n/n); for d=1 it is smaller than n^{-1/2}. The factor 2sqrt(d)P(A^c) from the fallback is charged. This is an upper bound only. The source paper’s unrestricted minimax lower bound cannot be inherited as a lower bound for this density-restricted subclass; no class-matched lower bound is proved here.

4. Finite precision, information acquisition, and resource ledger

Supplied: d,n,m,L,delta, coordinate precision b, and two iid sample arrays. Acquired: source and target grid anchors by exact nearest scans; simplex weights; all n-by-n source-target and target-target distances; the finite LP plan. No manifold, subspace, score, or transport oracle is supplied. The density lower bound m is supplied and is a strong structural assumption. The algorithm does not estimate m or L.

Let K=(M+1)^d and let E be the number of grid edges, E<=dK. Exact brute-force anchor discovery costs O(dKn) arithmetic operations and O(Kd+n d) memory; with the chosen covering scale K is at most a dimension- and m-dependent multiple of n/log(n/delta), so this is O(d n^2/log n) nearest-distance work up to constants. Pairwise cost acquisition costs O(dn^2) operations and stores O(n^2) distances. Computing every observed-source simplex and barycentric weights costs O(nd log d) (plus indexing); evaluating the output kernel at one x costs O(d log d) to locate its Freudenthal simplex plus O(dn) to emit its full probability vector. Sampling one y from the represented mixture costs O(d) after alias tables are built in O(Kn) time and memory.

The LP stores Kn kernel masses, E n^2 edge-flow variables, n^2 final-margin-flow variables, and O(En) marginal/cost constraints; its sparse constraint matrix has O(E n^2) nonzeros. Since K=Theta(h^{-d}), this is O(d h^{-d} n^2) memory and variables, which is O(d n^3/log n) at the selected bandwidth up to constants. The LP is polynomial-time in the bit model by general rational LP algorithms, but that statement hides a severe exponent and constants; it is not an empirical runtime claim and does not establish practical scalability. A general-purpose interior-point solve may have additional conditioning costs. An exact ellipsoid/bit-complexity guarantee avoids silently assuming a condition number, at a substantially worse practical runtime.

For b-bit rational sample coordinates, nearest-anchor identities and grid barycentric weights can be computed from rational squared distances and rational grid coordinates. Use rational upper approximations A_d for the interpolation constant and rational lower bound m. Replace each Euclidean cost by a certified rational interval of width eta. Use upper endpoints in objective costs, a lower endpoint for the subtracted constant W_1(muhat,nuhat), and upper endpoints for anchor distances and edge-flow costs. Increase edge right-hand sides by eta. With L_0=L A_d, the candidate comparator remains feasible; any LP-feasible output then has true edge W_1 at most L_0||X_a-X_a'||+(L_0+1)eta. Therefore its output Lipschitz constant is at most 6d^(3/2)L_0+2d^(3/2)(L_0+1)eta/s. The objective interval changes E_1 by at most 3eta because all couplings have mass one. Thus the finite-precision version of (B) adds 3eta+epsilon_LP and uses the displayed inflated Lipschitz constant in the stability term. Choosing eta<=s/(L_0+1) keeps the extra Lipschitz term at most 2d^(3/2); smaller eta can make it any prescribed epsilon_geom. Exact rational LP optimization has no numerical feasibility residual; the additive objective solve error epsilon_LP is separately charged. This is a bit-model design, not an implemented robust solver. Floating-point solvers require primal feasibility and objective-gap certificates; no unverified floating result counts as an output guarantee.

5. Prior art and novelty gate

Primary source checked live on 2026-10-09: Nietert and Goldfeld, arXiv:2512.09499v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09499v1. Relevant exact locations: §1.1 defines stochastic maps and E_p; §1.2 gives empirical W_p rates; §2 equation (4) is the loss; §3 Theorem 2 gives the rounded estimator rate tilde-O(n^{-1/(d+2p)}) and computation O((C_infty+d)n^{2+o_d(1)}); §4 Assumption 1, Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 give the Lipschitz-kernel minimax-rate information reduction via computationally inefficient WDRO; Remark 4 explicitly says for p=1 the WDRO guarantee is matched by minimizing empirical E_1 plus a W_1-Lipschitz regularizer; §7 Discussion asks whether an efficient estimator can attain the optimal rate. The paper’s point-mass lower-bound reduction is not valid on the full-support subclass imposed here.

Primary deterministic-map precedent checked live: Manole, Balakrishnan, Niles-Weed, and Wasserman, “Plugin Estimation of Smooth Optimal Transport Maps,” Annals of Statistics 52(3), 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12364 and https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOS2379. Their empirical optimal coupling plus linear-smoother extension is minimax under a Lipschitz Brenier map, but their loss estimates a unique deterministic map in L^2, unlike E_1 for stochastic kernels. This makes the interpolation principle itself established prior art.

Other primary prior checked live: Weed and Bach, “Sharp asymptotic and finite-sample rates of convergence of empirical measures in Wasserstein distance,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00087; Divol, “Measure estimation on manifolds: an optimal transport approach,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07595; Huguet et al., “Geodesic Sinkhorn for Fast and Accurate Optimal Transport on Manifolds,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00805. These confirm that intrinsic metric/sample complexity and graph-based manifold OT are already active lines. Multi-marginal treewidth algorithms are also established (Fan et al., PMLR 2022; Altschuler and Boix-Adsera, 2022), so treewidth is not a new target.

The present candidate is essentially a finite convex, Lipschitz-constrained plugin/smoother instantiation of the source paper’s own p=1 regularization remark. Its exact finite LP and proof are internally reconstructed here, but this is too close to known methodology to claim discovery. More seriously, full-support lower density is added solely to obtain global grid covers; it narrows the paper’s open problem, and cubic-in-n LP memory may not be scalable. The exact class-matched minimax lower bound, a lower-memory solver, removal of the density lower bound, and independent novelty review remain open. Therefore the algorithm and rate below are candidate/internal derivations, not externally established and not a solved open problem.

6. Counterexamples, boundaries, and alternatives

• If source or target has a hole or lower-dimensional support, the global cube-grid nearest-sample cover event can fail at all scales described by the density lower bound; the construction must use an adaptive support geometry and pay its acquisition error. No such replacement is proved.
• A nearest-neighbor extension of empirical conditionals alone is discontinuous and need not have bounded W_1-Lipschitz constant; this is why a grid interpolation and explicit edge constraints appear. The added interpolation smoothness can blur the optimizer, and its O_d(Lh) cost is charged in (B).
• Grid and target sample counts have dimension-dependent constants at least of order d^d in this conservative cover bound; the n^{-1/d} exponent is not dimension-free. This construction does not remove the high-dimensional sample curse.
• Exact LP is polynomial in input size but has O(d h^{-d}n^2) flow variables. It cannot be advertised as practical-scale without a solver and memory reduction.
• The Astra transport cards N393 (planar radial Coulomb non-Monge) and N395 (all-N repulsive Monge obstruction) are marked source_derived_unreviewed; they supply no computational theorem used here. N60 records overlap warnings. The earlier transport-theorem bound mismatch is preserved only as an audit caveat; it is not a premise or discovery target in this construction.
