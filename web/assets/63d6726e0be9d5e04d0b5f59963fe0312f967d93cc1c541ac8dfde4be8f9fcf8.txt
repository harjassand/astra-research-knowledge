Independent internal cost audit: restricted bond/Ising FPRAS
Date: 2026-10-08
Target audited: outputs/research/sol_bond_renewal/RESULT.txt

Verdict
-------
For the exact class stated in the target result—q=2, a full-span binary
block x(w) with positive integer gaps w=(g_1,...,g_m), N=1+sum g_t, and
each observed gap label occurring at most twice—the reported composition has
no hidden exponentially rare observation, rejection step, query oracle, or
succinct horizon. Conditional on the exact bond/Ising identities and the
correction lower-bound proof in the target, the FPRAS cost is polynomial in
the explicit raw block length N, epsilon^{-1}, and log(delta^{-1}); the
relative-error guarantee does not divide by the target likelihood.

This is an internal audit of the end-to-end cost and encoding ledger, not an
independent reproof of every tensor identity, priority claim, or external
review of the reduction. In particular, the imported Ising result is stated
in an exact-real arithmetic model; the target's finite-bit conversion is a
necessary internal argument. It is plausible and can be implemented by
fixed-precision rational simulation as detailed below, but this leaf has no
executable Ising FPRAS or runtime benchmark. The target's finite exact checks
do not validate the randomized approximation theorem.

1. Actual input and output interface
------------------------------------
The input is the explicit N-bit block whose ones are at
1, 1+g_1, ..., 1+sum_t g_t=N. Thus it begins and ends with an event, has m+1
ones, and m<=N-1. The gap labels are durations read from the raw block. The
restriction is that each duration value appears at most twice. The target
computes

  Z(w)=sum_{u in {0,1}^{m+1}} [product_{i,d,g} c_{i,d,g}!]/[r_0! r_1!],
  Q_N^bin(x(w))=Z(w)/(2 K_N),

where K_N is the exactly counted bounded-span pool normalizer. Full span
forces the pool counts to be exactly the observed counts; this is not the
censored-prefix likelihood. The algorithm's output is a rational estimate
of this one full-block probability, or equivalently of Z. K_N is computed
by the target's polynomial colored-partition DP. Its bit length is
polynomial in N (a crude bound from counting all count vectors is
O(N log N)); exact division by 2K_N preserves relative error.

The horizon is not supplied succinctly. If someone supplied only a short
binary-encoded gap list and asked for time polynomial in that list's bit
length while N=sum(g_t)+1 could be exponentially larger, this theorem would
not give that guarantee. Polynomial in N is polynomial in the stated raw
block encoding. There is no oracle call to Q_N or to a trajectory source:
the Ising instances are derived internally from the observed block and
partial bond assignments. This is a likelihood computation, not a general
prefix-marginal or predictor interface.

2. Rare-event and relative-error ledger
---------------------------------------
Write L for the number of doubled labels, so L<=floor(m/2). The exact
identity in the target is

  Z = W C_m mu,   mu=E_{sigma~mu_U}[g(sigma)],
  C_m=binom(m,floor(m/2))/m!,  0<g<=1.

The target proves, for every allowed word and m>=1,

  mu >= p0 = 1/[1024(m+1)^3].

This is the essential relative-error safeguard. Estimating a bounded mean
with relative accuracy takes O(epsilon^{-2}/mu) samples, hence at most
O(m^3 epsilon^{-2}) up to the confidence logarithm. The rare-likelihood
scale of Q_N itself is irrelevant: Q_N=Z/(2K_N) is divided by a known
positive exact integer only after estimating Z, so no inverse-Q_N factor
appears.

The algorithm does not use accept/reject correction. It samples the
unweighted bond law mu_U by sequential self-reduction and averages the
bounded importance correction g. On the stated class the same proposal's
rejection acceptance is also at least p0, so even direct rejection would
have polynomial expected proposals O(m^3); however direct rejection is not
the cost mechanism used in the proof. The target's one-label/repeated-gap
example has exponentially small acceptance only when a label occurs m
times, which violates the audited multiplicity-at-most-two restriction.
That example is a proposal obstruction outside this theorem's class, not a
counterexample to this restricted FPRAS.

For self-reduction, at a partial bond assignment h let W_{h0},W_{h1} be the
two child partition sums. Adding a bond imposes at most two equalities, so
term by term U_{h0}/4<=U_{h1}<=U_{h0}, and after summation

  Pr(next bit=1 | h)=W_{h1}/(W_{h0}+W_{h1}) in [1/5,1/2].

Thus neither branch is exponentially rare. The other branch has probability
in [1/2,4/5]. No rare conditional event needs to be witnessed by sampling.

Error accounting from the target, with 0<epsilon<1 and 0<delta<1/2:

  xi = epsilon p0/32,
  rho = gamma = xi/(16L)   (when L>0),
  T = ceil(1536 epsilon^{-2} p0^{-1} log(8/delta)).

Here rho is the relative accuracy of each child W estimate and gamma its
failure probability. If both child estimates are rho-good, their normalized
branch probability differs by at most 2rho. The per-stage failure
probability from either estimate is at most 2gamma=xi/(8L); finite-bit
Bernoulli rounding is allocated at most xi/(4L). Sequential coupling over
L stages gives total variation at most xi (with slack in the constants).
For nu, the law actually sampled, |E_nu g-mu|<=xi<=epsilon mu/32 and
E_nu g>=p0/2. With T independent samples, the stated multiplicative
Chernoff bound estimates E_nu g within relative epsilon/8 except with
probability delta/2. The independent W FPRAS uses relative epsilon/8 and
failure delta/4. Their product has relative error below epsilon, and the
total failure probability is at most delta.

A wording issue in the target's sentence “Bad calls use any valid fallback
branch probability” should be made precise. An FPRAS failure is not
observable, so the algorithm cannot detect that a call is bad. Detection is
unnecessary: convert every pair of nonnegative returned estimates into a
valid probability (clamp to [0,1], and choose a fixed probability if both
are zero). On the event of accurate estimates it is close to the target
conditional; off that event charge total variation at most one. The union
bound and sequential coupling then give the same result. This is a wording/
presentation repair, not an exponential-cost flaw.

For m=0 or L=0, the target handles the relevant computation exactly; the
formulas involving 1/L are not used.

3. Number and cost of approximation calls
----------------------------------------
Since p0^{-1}=1024(m+1)^3,

  T = O(m^3 epsilon^{-2} log(delta^{-1})),
  rho^{-1}=gamma^{-1}=512 L/(epsilon p0)=O(m^4/epsilon),
  log(gamma^{-1})=O(log(m/epsilon)).

Each sampled bond assignment uses at most 2L child partition estimates.
Thus the correction-mean phase makes

  2LT = O(m^4 epsilon^{-2} log(delta^{-1}))

calls to the W_h FPRAS, each on an Ising graph with v=O(m) sites and
e=O(m) interactions. The separate estimate of W uses one more such FPRAS
at accuracy epsilon/8 and confidence 1-delta/4.

The primary Jerrum--Sinclair theorem's displayed annealing/sampling bound,
in its exact-real arithmetic model, is of order

  C_W(v,e,rho,gamma) =
    O(rho^{-2} e^2 v^{11} log(v+1)
      [e+log(v/rho)] log(gamma^{-1})).

The log(gamma^{-1}) factor can be obtained by independent median
amplification of the constant-success estimate. Substituting the bond
parameters gives a coarse per-call arithmetic-operation bound

  O(m^{21} epsilon^{-2} log(m+1)
      [m+log((m+1)/epsilon)] log((m+1)/epsilon)).

Consequently a conservative bound for the correction phase is

  O(m^{25} epsilon^{-4} log(delta^{-1}) log(m+1)
      [m+log((m+1)/epsilon)] log((m+1)/epsilon))

exact-real arithmetic operations, before rational bit-operation overhead.
The standalone W estimate is lower order in m and epsilon under this
coarse bound. Exact component correction costs O(m^2) integer operations
per sample, or O(m^5 epsilon^{-2} log(delta^{-1})) total, also dominated
asymptotically by the repeated W calls. Computing K_N adds polynomial
time in N. Since m<=N-1, all are polynomial in the explicit input length.
These are deliberately pessimistic theorem-level bounds, not practical
runtime estimates; the high degree is material.

4. Ising sampler precision and bit costs
---------------------------------------
The cited source explicitly works with exact unit-cost real arithmetic.
It cannot by itself support the target's phrase “polynomial-bit FPRAS.”
The target supplies a finite-bit repair; the key checks are:

* The irrational interaction is replaced by rational dyadic r approximating
  5^(1/4). Bisection gives |r^4-5|=O(rho/e) with O(log(e/rho)) bits. The
  configuration-wise log weight perturbation is at most rho/8, so the
  partition perturbation is multiplicative and can be absorbed in the
  Ising FPRAS tolerance.
* For every merged interaction multiplicity t<=O(m), the high-temperature
  activity (r^(2t)-1)/(r^(2t)+1) and cosh(t log r)=(r^t+r^(-t))/2 are exact
  rationals. Their numerator/denominator bit lengths are O(m log(m/rho));
  products/prefactors over O(m) edges remain polynomial-bit (at most a
  coarse O(m^2 log(m/rho)) bound). The annealing penalties (v-k)/v,
  observables, sample sums, and products are rational too.
* In the source's Metropolis chain, a one-edge update changes only that
  edge activity and at most two odd-degree penalties. Its acceptance
  probability is therefore rational and exactly computable from these
  polynomial-bit quantities. With v=O(m), e=O(m), and external penalty
  phi>=1/v, the source's fixed number of steps is
  O(e^2 v^8 [e+log(eta^{-1})])=poly(m,log(eta^{-1})).
  No inverse edge activity or inverse partition function is charged in
  this mixing bound.
* To avoid an unbounded exact-rational Bernoulli/rejection loop, approximate
  each transition probability by a dyadic categorical probability. For a
  chain of T_chain steps and total variation budget tau, use a fixed
  s=O(log(T_chain e/tau)) random bits per update and dyadic edge-choice
  draw. Integer threshold arithmetic computes the quantized rational
  probabilities exactly. Each step's kernel error is at most O(tau/T_chain);
  telescoping/coupling bounds the whole chain error by tau. Allocate the
  other half of eta to the ideal chain's mixing error. This has fixed,
  worst-case polynomial time and no rare-event rejection.
* Annealing sample counts are polynomial in v and rho^{-1}; the observable
  ratios have bounded max-to-mean ratio in the cited proof, including its
  final zero-penalty indicator, so no exponentially small estimator event
  is hidden there. Amplify each partition estimate by O(log(gamma^{-1}))
  independent fixed-cost runs.

These facts give polynomial bit time, not merely unit-cost arithmetic,
provided the one-step rational transition calculation and dyadic simulation
are written as specified. The finite-bit repair does not state a sharp
bit-operation exponent; it gives a polynomial overhead in m, log(1/rho),
and the chain precision. Combining this with the coarse call bound above
still yields a polynomial-bit FPRAS. Because epsilon^{-1} enters the
sample count polynomially, this is an FPRAS in the standard sense, not a
runtime polynomial only in the bit length log(epsilon^{-1}).

The returned sample correction has common denominator
2^(m+1) binom(m,floor(m/2)); its numerator has O(m) bits. Averaging T
samples adds O(log T) bits. C_m has O(m log m) bits, and K_N has polynomial
bit length. Thus the rational output itself is polynomial length even if
the probability Q_N^bin is exponentially small in N. No numerical
underflow model or floating-point relative tolerance is assumed.

5. Encoding boundary, baseline, and audit status
------------------------------------------------
The direct same-input baseline is exact hidden-path enumeration over the
2^(m+1) assignments u; no polynomial exact full-block baseline is established
in the target (its colored-partition DP computes K_N, not Z). The FPRAS
therefore gives a polynomial-time computational route relative to this
straightforward exponential exact baseline, conditional on the theorem; it
does not claim fewer raw observations or less data than a method given the
full path, nor does this audit establish that it beats every alternative
approximation. For the Ising approximation, the FPRAS consumes only the same
explicit block x(w), then internally constructs O(m)-size graphs. Section 6's
exponential rejection and variance example concerns the unweighted
importance proposal outside the multiplicity restriction and correctly is
not promoted to a universal lower bound.

Primary source: Mark Jerrum and Alistair Sinclair, “Polynomial-time
approximation algorithms for the Ising model,” author preprint (Theorem 2,
Lemma 4, Theorem 5), [author PDF](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sinclair/ising.pdf),
published version [DOI 10.1137/0222066](https://doi.org/10.1137/0222066).
The author PDF's Theorem 2 supplies the fixed-step sampler; Lemma 4 controls
the annealing observable; Theorem 5 gives the Ising FPRAS. The source's
exact-real arithmetic convention is noted in the target result and is why
the rational-input/finite-bit simulation argument must be charged
separately.

Audit conclusion: no asymptotic cost or encoding flaw found for the
restricted full-span multiplicity-<=2 theorem, assuming the target's exact
identities and p0 proof. Important status boundary: theorem-level internal
candidate; no FPRAS implementation, empirical runtime, external proof
review, generic censored-prefix oracle, or result for arbitrary gap
multiplicity is established here.
