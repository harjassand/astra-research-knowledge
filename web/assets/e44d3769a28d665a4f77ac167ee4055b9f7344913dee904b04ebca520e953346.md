# Positive-budget audit of the inverse-resolvent image enforcer

Date: 2026-10-08  
Status: **INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE** for the exact kernel/gap and norm-budget identities below, and for the finite-difference obstruction to a fixed-auxiliary, bounded-syndrome-local decomposition of this particular enforcer family. **UNKNOWN** for a polynomial-row/row-list implementation of the inverse-resolvent enforcer and for every other exact image enforcer. This does not instantiate N26's all-nonzero-syndromes soundness hypothesis.

## Result

For the full hard-plus-seed BMVZ family with the disjoint fine-spectrum dummy projectors, the incidence enforcer

\[
 C_{\rm inc}(x)=\tfrac1{16}T_x^*T_x,\qquad
 (T_xz)_a=z_a-K_{a,x}Y_x^{-1}z_0,\qquad Y_x=I-H_x,
\]

is an exact positive contraction with

\[
 \ker C_{\rm inc}(x)=\operatorname{ran}V_x,\qquad
 C_{\rm inc}(x)\ge \tfrac1{16}(I-V_xV_x^*)
\]

for every dummy-bit string \(x\). Its explicit row-factor decomposition has \(m-1\) positive terms, each a contraction, so its positive operator-norm budget is between \((m-1)/16\) and \(m-1\), where \(m\) is the fixed LCU output-label dimension. After normalizing this particular decomposition to budget one, its guaranteed gap is only \(\Omega(1/m)\). More seriously for N26, at least one term (the row factor indexed by \(b\)) depends on **all** dummy syndrome bits through \(Y_x^{-1}\) and acts globally on the data register; the finite-difference theorem shows every exact decomposition must include a term with this global syndrome dependence.

There is an exact stronger locality statement. Let \(x\in\{0,1\}^q\) indicate which of the \(q\) disjoint dummy projectors are included, transferring omitted weights to a silent zero-projector label. For this family, a fixed matrix element of the cross-block \((C_{\rm inc}(x))_{b0}\) has nonzero full \(q\)-way mixed finite difference. Therefore no exact representation

\[
 C_{\rm inc}(x)=\sum_\ell w_\ell E_{\ell,x|S_\ell},\qquad
 w_\ell\ge0,\quad 0\le E_{\ell,z}\le I,\quad |S_\ell|<q,
\]

on one fixed auxiliary Hilbert space can exist, regardless of its positive budget or number of terms. In fact, positivity is not needed for this finite-difference contradiction. This is a premise-failure/lower-bound result for the inverse-resolvent family, not a no-go for other enforcers.

## Exact family and full-space gap

Use the audited augmented Hamiltonian, splitting it into disjoint registers as

\[
 H_x=H_{\rm old}+H_s+\sum_{k=1}^q \delta_k x_kP_k,
 \qquad H_s=\tfrac18h_{\phi,R}+\tfrac18h_{\psi,R},
 \qquad \sum_k\delta_k=\tfrac14.
\]

Here \(H_{\rm old}\) is the rest of the scaled BMVZ hard Hamiltonian, \(h_{\phi,R},h_{\psi,R}\) are the two forced amplified five-qubit seed clauses on their common fresh seed registers, and \(P_k=|10000\rangle\langle10000|\) acts on dummy register \(k\), disjoint from both. Take the exact dyadic weights

\[
 \delta_k=\frac{2^{-k}}{4(1-2^{-q})},\quad k=1,\ldots,q.
\]

The base term weights sum to \(1/2\). In the normalized projection-list presentation, give the silent zero projector weight \(1/4+\sum_{k:x_k=0}\delta_k\); thus the weights sum to one for every \(x\), with a fixed padded label set. The seed weights stay \(1/8\) each. The full all-one instance is the fine-spectrum hard augmentation; every other \(x\) retains the hard base at weight one half. Since the all-one Hamiltonian is at most \(3I/4\), positivity gives \(0\le H_x\le3I/4\) for every \(x\), hence \(Y_x\ge I/4\).

For each \(x\), use the complement-LCU isometry with fixed label dimension \(m\). Its distinguished block is \(K_{0,x}=Y_x\), and its blocks obey \(\sum_aK_{a,x}^*K_{a,x}=I\). Because the two seed weights are equal, \(\chi_{\phi,x}=\chi_{\psi,x}=1/4\). Choose a legal PREPARE completion whose output column \(b\ne0\) has entries \(A_{\phi,b}=1/\sqrt2\), \(A_{\psi,b}=-1/\sqrt2\), and zero elsewhere. This column is orthogonal to the first column for every \(x\). It gives the same exact block for the whole family:

\[
 K_{b,x}=\tau(R_{\phi,R}-R_{\psi,R}),\qquad \tau=\frac1{4\sqrt2}.
\]

In particular \(K_{b,x}\ne0\), since the two seed projectors, and therefore their amplified reflections, are distinct.

Set \(G_{a,x}=K_{a,x}Y_x^{-1}\), and define \((T_xz)_a=z_a-G_{a,x}z_0\) for \(a>0\). Then

\[
 \sum_{a>0}G_{a,x}^*G_{a,x}=Y_x^{-2}-I\le15I.
\]

It follows that \(T_xT_x^*=I+G_xG_x^*\) lies between \(I\) and \(16I\). Also \(T_xz=0\) iff \(z_a=K_{a,x}Y_x^{-1}z_0\), which is exactly \(z=V_x(Y_x^{-1}z_0)\). Thus \(\ker T_x=\operatorname{ran}V_x\); the nonzero singular values of \(T_x\) are at least one. This proves the displayed kernel, contraction, and gap bounds for every state in the full physical space, including every label sector. No auxiliary sector is omitted.

## Positive term budget and physical support

Write \(T_x\) as its output-row maps \(T_{a,x}\), so

\[
 C_{\rm inc}(x)=\sum_{a>0}E_{a,x},\qquad
 E_{a,x}=\tfrac1{16}T_{a,x}^*T_{a,x}\ge0.
\]

Each \(T_{a,x}T_{a,x}^*=I+G_{a,x}G_{a,x}^*\). Since \(\|G_{a,x}\|^2\le\|\sum_{a>0}G_{a,x}^*G_{a,x}\|\le15\),

\[
 \tfrac1{16}\le\|E_{a,x}\|=\tfrac1{16}(1+\|G_{a,x}\|^2)\le1,
 \qquad \frac{m-1}{16}\le L_x:=\sum_{a>0}\|E_{a,x}\|\le m-1.
\]

Thus the displayed factorization is an exact positive decomposition, but not a budget-one one. Replacing \(E_{a,x}\) by \(E_{a,x}/\|E_{a,x}\|\) and using weights \(\|E_{a,x}\|/L_x\) represents \(C_{\rm inc}(x)/L_x\); its normalized full-space gap is at least \(1/(16L_x)=\Omega(1/m)\). This is a property of this factorization, not a lower bound on the minimum possible positive norm budget over all decompositions.

Each \(E_{a,x}\) couples only label sectors \(0\) and \(a\), but its data blocks contain \(G_{a,x}=K_{a,x}Y_x^{-1}\). In particular, the tested \(b0\) block contains the full-data resolvent. The exact row support and an efficient exact row-list/entry algorithm for \(Y_x^{-1}\) are **not established**; the finite-difference theorem is a lower bound on dependence on the dummy inclusion bits, not a physical-qubit row-support lower bound. Approximating \(Y_x^{-1}\) by a polynomial in \(H_x\) is possible on the spectral interval \([1/4,1]\), but the resulting approximate relation does not annihilate \(V_x\) exactly; it is not the stated exact-kernel certificate. No claim of a proven exponential row lower bound for \(C_{\rm inc}\) is made here.

## Finite-difference locality theorem

**Theorem.** For the exact family above, every exact decomposition of \(C_{\rm inc}(x)\) on a common Hilbert space into fixed-weight terms \(w_\ell E_{\ell,x|S_\ell}\), with each term depending only on a strict subset \(S_\ell\subsetneq[q]\), is impossible. The claim holds even if the terms are arbitrary operators rather than positive effects, provided the sum is well-defined with summable term norms.

**Proof.** Choose any eigenvector \(|\omega\rangle\) of \(H_{\rm old}\), with eigenvalue \(\lambda\). Fix the dummy-register state once and for all as \(|d_*\rangle=|10000\rangle^{\otimes q}\); it is independent of \(x\), and every \(P_k\) has eigenvalue one on it. On the subspace \(|\omega\rangle\otimes\mathcal H_s\otimes|d_*\rangle\),

\[
 Y_x=(c-\sum_k\delta_kx_k)I_s-H_s,\qquad c=1-\lambda.
\]

The cross-block of the incidence operator is exactly

\[
 (C_{\rm inc}(x))_{b0}=-\tfrac1{16}K_{b,x}Y_x^{-1}.
\]

The \(K_{b,x}=K_b\) block is independent of \(x\). Since \(H_x\le3I/4\), every denominator \(c-\mu-\sum_k\delta_kx_k\), for \(\mu\in\operatorname{spec}(H_s)\), is at least \(1/4\). For a positive real variable \(s\), the scalar full mixed difference is

\[
 F(\mu):=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^q}(-1)^{q-|x|}
 \frac1{c-\mu-\sum_k\delta_kx_k}
 =\int_0^\infty e^{-s(c-\mu)}
   \prod_{k=1}^q(e^{s\delta_k}-1)\,ds>0.
\]

The integral converges because \(c-\mu-\sum_k\delta_k\ge1/4\). By spectral calculus, \(F(H_s)\) is positive definite. Hence \(K_bF(H_s)\ne0\), since \(K_b\ne0\) and right multiplication by an invertible operator cannot make a nonzero operator zero. There are therefore fixed seed basis vectors \(|u\rangle,|v\rangle\) for which the fixed physical matrix coefficient

\[
 f(x)=\langle b,u,\omega,d_*|C_{\rm inc}(x)|0,v,\omega,d_*\rangle
\]

has nonzero full mixed difference \(\sum_x(-1)^{q-|x|}f(x)\).

In contrast, for any term \(E_{\ell,x|S_\ell}\) with \(|S_\ell|<q\), this same fixed bra/ket matrix coefficient depends only on \(x|S_\ell\); summing its alternating difference over a missing coordinate cancels it exactly. Every such term has zero full mixed difference, as does any fixed-weight summable sum of them. This contradicts the nonzero value above. \(\square\)

The coefficient vectors are fixed across \(x\); the proof does not smuggle the syndrome into an \(x\)-dependent auxiliary state. Appending an unused fixed workspace tensor factor does not change the result. More generally, the same contradiction applies to any workspace extension for which this cross-block matrix coefficient is preserved on a fixed workspace vector.

## Exact N26 boundary and costs

N26 assumes a fixed auxiliary Hilbert space and a positive budget-one mixture of effects, each selected by at most \(r\) syndrome bits, with a small zero-syndrome spectral value and an all-state lower bound on every nonzero syndrome. Its bound is \(c\le\eta+(r/t)(1-\eta)\), or \(c\le\eta+(r/t)(L-\eta)\) for total positive norm budget \(L\). The incidence family has the fixed-space / fixed-weight setup, but its exact local-term premise fails on these \(q\) dummy inclusion bits: the mixed-difference theorem forces some term to read all \(q\). If one packages the entire \(C_{\rm inc}(x)\) as one positive effect, it has budget one and reads all \(q\), making the N26 ratio one and the bound vacuous. If one uses the row-factor decomposition, its budget is \(\Theta(m)\) and at least the \(b\)-row factor reads all \(q\); after normalization its gap also shrinks as above.

The family \(C_{\rm inc}(x)\) has a kernel for every \(x\), so it does **not** satisfy N26's hypothesis that every nonzero syndrome block is bounded below on the entire auxiliary space. Therefore the N26 gap theorem is not applied to it. The precise conclusion is the separate finite-difference lower bound on local representation, not an N26 soundness theorem.

This is an operator-parameter locality result, distinct from physical-qubit locality. The fixed test vectors make the result robust to adding workspace on which the tested block is preserved. An encoding that uses an \(x\)-dependent identification of workspace or only penalizes outside branch-dependent valid subspaces escapes both this theorem's fixed-matrix-element premise and N26's fixed-auxiliary/all-state interface. No statement here rules out such a redesign.

For the actual augmented family, \(q=n\), \(m=\operatorname{poly}(n)\), the data include the original hard registers, \(5R\) seed qubits and \(5q\) dummy qubits, and the LCU label uses \(\lceil\log_2m\rceil\) qubits (with any padding included in \(m\)). The coefficients \(\delta_k\) have \(O(q)\) bits and the family is polynomial-time describable. The exact algebraic LCU circuit and the exact abstract incidence formula are polynomial-size descriptions; neither gives polynomial row enumeration for \(C_{\rm inc}\). Its row/entry runtime and exact coefficient precision requirement remain open. The nonzero mixed difference is exact, but it may be very small: the integral lower bound on \([0,1]\) gives only \(F(\mu)\ge e^{-1}(\prod_k\delta_k)/(q+1)\), and \(\prod_k\delta_k\) is exponentially small in \(q^2\). Thus this is not an approximate-locality lower bound at inverse-polynomial precision.

## Scope, next test, and source ledger

The theorem excludes only exact bounded-syndrome-local positive decompositions of this inverse-resolvent enforcer family under the displayed dummy-toggle parametrization. It does not rule out (i) a different exact enforcer for the same \(V_x\), (ii) a sparse construction with terms that read all dummy bits, (iii) an approximate relation whose exact kernel is repaired by another mechanism, or (iv) an active, branch-dependent workspace construction. The parent synthesis should retain **UNKNOWN** for general positive sparse enforcement.

A decisive next lemma would show that every exact full-gap positive enforcer \(C_x\) with \(\ker C_x=\operatorname{ran}V_x\) has a nonzero \(q\)-way mixed difference in some fixed matrix coefficient, or else construct one with all such coefficients of bounded syndrome degree and provide its exact kernel/gap. Cinc proves that the inverse resolvent itself has full degree; it does not transfer that degree obstruction to arbitrary enforcers.
