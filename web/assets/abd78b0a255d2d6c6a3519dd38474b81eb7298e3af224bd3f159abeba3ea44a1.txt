1. LEGAL INTERFACE AND THE EXACT CONDITIONAL ENSEMBLE

Fix d>=2. Supply N>=1, rational q_lambda>=0 summing exactly to one, and a
Gaussian-rational d by d matrix g. Input length L includes its numerator
and denominator bits. Positive common filters are a special case; the
construction permits arbitrary g. Put E=g* g, and let r=rank(E), acquired
by exact rational elimination. With rho from(C1) in the companion core,
the target is
 rho_g=g^tensorN rho (g*)^tensorN / Z, Z=Tr[g^tensorN rho(g*)^tensorN]. (F1)
If Z=0 this state is undefined; the compiler detects this exactly and
reports it. For Z>0 and rational 0<eps<1 it emits products of pure rational
cluster densities, cluster size at most d, whose mean has trace distance
less than eps from(F1). Time, output bits and fair-bit count have
deterministic bounds polynomial in N,L,log(1/eps), for FIXED d.

No matrix-exponential acquisition for g, physical application success
probability, flat ensemble list, variable-d claim, or hardware compiler
is imported. The native output is the same classical cluster interface
as the core. A very small acceptance norm is never compared relatively
to a rounded normalized ray.

Let r_lambda=dim U_lambda. For a legal block ell(lambda)<=r write
 Z_lambda=chi_lambda(E)/r_lambda,
 q'_lambda=q_lambda Z_lambda / sum_mu q_mu Z_mu.             (F2)
The blocks ell(lambda)>r have Z_lambda=0 and are removed. For legal lambda,
let w_lambda be the normalized column-wedge product from the core and
 t_lambda(U)=||g^tensorN U^tensorN w_lambda||^2.
The continuous conditional ensemble chooses lambda with law(F2), draws
Haar U with density t_lambda(U)/Z_lambda, independently draws a uniform
site permutation, and emits the normalized filtered column wedges.
It is exactly(F1). The filter commutes with site permutations; Haar and
permutation averaging before filtering gives the normalized full block.
The trace of that filtered block is chi_lambda(E)/r_lambda because the
permutation multiplicity cancels. Original q and uniform Haar without
these two reweightings would produce the wrong target.

2. POSTERIOR LABELS ARE EXACT RATIONALS WITH POLYNOMIAL BITS

For k=1,...,d, compute e_k(E) as the sum of k by k principal minors; set
e_0=1, h_0=1, h_n=0 for n<0, and recursively acquire
 h_n=sum_(k=1)^d (-1)^(k-1) e_k h_(n-k), n=1,...,N+d.
Jacobi–Trudi gives
 chi_lambda(E)=det[h_(lambda_i-i+j)]_(i,j=1)^d.              (F3)
This is the polynomial GL(d) character; diagonalizing E for its proof
does not make diagonalization an input oracle. Gaussian-rational E has
real rational characteristic coefficients, and(F3) is a real rational.
The recurrence has fixed order d, rational coefficient bits O_d(L), and
integer n<=N+d. A common denominator raised to n gives numerator/denominator
bit bounds O_d(N(L+log(N+1))). Determinants and summing polynomially many
labels preserve polynomial bits. Positive characters give the exact
legal-block test. Formula(F2) is acquired with rational division, including
arbitrarily small positive Z; its inverse still has polynomial bit length.

Weyl dimensions are the positive integers
 r_lambda=product_(i<j)(lambda_i-lambda_j+j-i)/(j-i).
Use the publicly computable integer upper bound
 R=(N+d)^[d(d-1)/2]>=r_lambda for every label.               (F4)
The number of labels is at most (N+1)^(d-1). Thus these steps are polynomial
in the input and N for fixed d; no exponentially long list of basis states
or irreducible matrix realization is required.

3. ACQUIRE A RATIONAL ENVELOPE WITHIN FACTOR TWO

Let the positive eigenvalues of E be a_1>=...>=a_r>0. C04_S02's independently
proved sharp envelope and character mean are
 M_lambda=product_(i<=r) a_i^lambda_i,
 0<=t_lambda(U)<=M_lambda,
 Z_lambda=HaarMean t_lambda(U)>=M_lambda/r_lambda.           (F5)
For singular g these formulas restrict to ell(lambda)<=r. Each wedge
determinant is bounded by the norm of wedge^h E; on the lambda sector the
restricted filter has norm sqrt(M_lambda). This also follows by taking a
positive invertible approximation to E and passing to the limit.

Acquire a rational envelope without algebraic arithmetic as follows. For
r>=1 set
 m=e_r(E)/e_(r-1)(E)>0, H=Tr(E)>0.
Because 1/m=sum_(i=1)^r 1/a_i, m<=a_r, and all positive eigenvalues lie in
[m,H]. Exact rational eigenvalue counting (constant-degree characteristic
polynomial, squarefree factors and Sturm signs, with repeated-root
multiplicities retained) allows bisection for each ordered quantile.
The endpoints or a rational eigenvalue encountered exactly are handled
by exact root counts. No eigenvalue gap promise is needed. After
O_d(log(H/m)+log(N+1)) bisections obtain rational upper endpoints b_i with
 a_i<=b_i<=a_i+m/(16N).
All integer/rational data have polynomial input bits, including m^{-1}.
Take
 B_lambda=product_(i<=r) b_i^lambda_i.
Then
 M_lambda<=B_lambda<2 M_lambda,                            (F6)
since b_i/a_i<=1+1/(16N) and sum_i lambda_i=N. Its bit length is polynomial
in N,L. This acquisition charges filter conditioning through the supplied
input bits, while proposal precision below has no conditioning factor.

4. FINITE HAAR PROPOSALS PRESERVE THE ENVELOPE EXACTLY

The core constructs an EXACT Gaussian-rational unitary U_hat from a fixed
number of finite uniform bits. Under its Haar coupling choose total error
 ||U_hat-U||op<=a, a=eps/(256 N R),                         (F7)
by taking Haar midpoint precision P so C_d 2^[-P/(2(d-1))]<=a/2 and the
rational-arithmetic error xi<=a/2. The wording correction in the companion
CORE_WORDING_ADDENDUM.txt specifies squared pre-square-root tolerance.
Thus P and arithmetic precision are O_d(log(N+1)+log(1/eps)), with the
core's polynomial elementary arithmetic cost. Exact rational unitarity
implies t_lambda(U_hat)<=M_lambda<=B_lambda for EVERY proposal. There is
no need for clipping an invalid acceptance probability.

Define the unnormalized accepted kernel
 F(U)=|g^tensorN U^tensorN w_lambda><g^tensorN U^tensorN w_lambda|/B_lambda.
Both vectors before filtering are in the lambda sector. Restricted filter
norm sqrt(M_lambda), tensor telescoping, and(F6) give
 ||F(U_hat)-F(U)||_1<=2N a.                                (F8)
Independent permutation averaging can only decrease this norm. This is
an UNNORMALIZED-kernel bound; no near-zero normalized-ray Lipschitz bound
is used. Let K be the Haar/permutation mean of F(U), K_hat the finite-frame
mean. Their traces obey
 mu=Tr K=Z_lambda/B_lambda>=1/(2r_lambda),
 Delta=||K_hat-K||_1<=2Na,
 mu_hat=Tr K_hat>=1/(4r_lambda).                            (F9)
For positive kernels A,B with Tr B>0,
 T(A/Tr A,B/Tr B)<=||A-B||_1/Tr B.
Consequently finite-frame conditional trace error is at most
 Delta/mu<=4r_lambda N a<=eps/64.                          (F10)

5. EXACT RATIONAL CLUSTERS AND DOWNWARD DYADIC ACCEPTANCE

For each distinct column height h, compute its ordered-string ray using
the determinants of the first h columns of the rational matrix g U_hat:
 z_h(j_1,...,j_h)=det[(g U_hat)_(j_a,b)]_(a,b=1)^h.
Its squared norm s_h is an exact nonnegative rational, and
 D_h=s_h/h!=det[(U_hat* E U_hat)_(1:h,1:h)].
If s_h=0 and that height is used, t_lambda(U_hat)=0, and reject. Otherwise
emit the exact Gaussian-rational density
 z_h z_h*/s_h,                                            (F11)
copied onto the corresponding columns. It is positive rank one, trace
one, and has at most d^d entries for fixed d. Compute acceptance exactly as
 p(U_hat)=t_lambda(U_hat)/B_lambda
          =product_h D_h^[lambda_h-lambda_(h+1)]/B_lambda in[0,1].
The powers have at most N multiplications and polynomial rational bit
growth. Each local nonzero norm s_h is a positive rational with polynomial
bits; exact division in(F11) has polynomial output bits even if s_h is tiny.
No lower bound on s_h is supplied for, or needed by, the mean-state proof.

With b=ceil log2(256 R/eps), draw b fair bits V in{0,...,2^b-1} and accept
iff V<floor(2^b p(U_hat)). This has exact acceptance probability p_b<=p
and 0<=p-p_b<e=2^-b<=eps/(256R). The unnormalized accepted kernel loses
trace norm at most e, since each emitted state has trace one. Therefore
 T(rho_b,rho_hat)<=e/mu_hat<=4R e<=eps/64,
 mu_b>=1/(8r_lambda).                                     (F12)
These are rational finite-bit acceptance decisions, with no real-number
or unbounded Bernoulli oracle.

6. DETERMINISTIC TRIAL CAP AND COMPLETE ERROR BUDGET

Independently propose and decide acceptance at most
 K_trials=8R ceil log2(64/eps)                             (F13)
times. The failure probability is at most
 (1-mu_b)^K_trials<=exp[-K_trials/(8R)]<=eps/64.
Given at least one acceptance, the first accepted frame has precisely
the p_b-reweighted finite proposal law; the bounded cap does not bias that
conditional distribution. On total failure emit the unfiltered rational
column state w_lambda (then use the same permutation routine). It remains
a legal <=d cluster output. The resulting mean-state error contribution
is at most the failure probability, without requiring this rare fallback
state to be produced by a physical filter.

Round the acquired posterior(F2) onto dyadics with TV<=eps/64 by the core's
nonnegative rounding/residual rule, and implement Fisher–Yates with stage
precision giving total permutation TV<=eps/64. Zero-weight labels are
never assigned residual. Choose the permutation only after acceptance
or fallback; common g commutes with it. Each of the five errors (posterior,
finite Haar plus unitary arithmetic, finite acceptance, bounded failure,
permutation) is at most eps/64, hence total T<eps.              (F14)

Each proposal costs polynomial rational arithmetic and at most d^2 P+b
fair bits; K_trials is polynomial N times log(1/eps) for fixed d. Label
acquisition, spectral envelope, every proposal, the cap, permutation and
cluster output are all charged. Maximum bit growth and time are polynomial
in N,L,log(1/eps), independent of the value of a tiny normalizing Z except
through its polynomial rational input/output representation. The output
has no greater than N clusters, each of size <=d; common repeated heights
can share the same density description.
