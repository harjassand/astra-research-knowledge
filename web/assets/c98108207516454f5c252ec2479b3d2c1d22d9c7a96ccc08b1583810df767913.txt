SU(m) PBW HEIGHT-SHELL CONDUCTANCE: INDEPENDENT DERIVATION
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: conductance upper and low-height ramp energy. No rank-profile
lower bound, operational theorem, or novelty claim is asserted.

RESULT

Let m>=2 be fixed, p=|Phi^+|=m(m-1)/2, and let V_lambda be an
irreducible SU(m) representation whose Dynkin labels satisfy
0<=lambda_i<=C N (the proposed regular regime additionally has
lambda_i>=cN). Define depth of a weight mu by

  depth(mu)=<rho^vee,lambda-mu>.

Use the standard root vectors E_ij (i<j) with E_ij^dagger=E_ji and
[E_ij,E_ji]=H_ij. Then for every existing integer depth ell>=0,

  sum_{v in an orthonormal weight basis of depth ell}
       sum_{i<j} ||E_ij v||^2
       <= C_{m,C} N (ell+1)^p.                            (1)

This proves the requested estimate, in fact without restricting
ell<=c'N. The exponent p must mean the number of positive roots. If p
instead denotes rank m-1, the claim is false already for regular SU(3);
see the final section. With p=|Phi^+|, the linear depth-ramp filter

  F_R v=(1-depth(v)/R)_+ v,      R>=1,

has unnormalized Casimir energy E(F_R)<=C'_{m,C} N R^(p-1). Roots of
height greater than one are included explicitly; they contribute a
height-squared factor and extend the active source shells by that height.

1. NORMALIZATION AND CASIMIR ENERGY

For each i<j, use the standard fundamental matrix units and Hermitian
generators

  t^x_ij=(E_ij+E_ji)/2,     t^y_ij=(E_ij-E_ji)/(2i).

Both have Tr_fund(t^2)=1/2, and E_ij=t^x_ij+i t^y_ij has
Tr_fund(E_ij E_ji)=1. In a unitary representation their adjoints and
commutator obey the same relations. Together with an orthonormal
Tr_fund(t_a t_b)=delta_ab/2 basis of the diagonal Cartan, these are the
normalization for

  E(X)=sum_a ||[T_a,X]||_HS^2.

If X is scalar f(ell) on each weight of depth ell, its Cartan
commutators vanish. For a positive root alpha_ij of height
h=j-i, E_ij maps depth ell to ell-h. Since X is Hermitian,
||[E_ij,X]||_2=||[E_ji,X]||_2, and the root-pair identity is

  ||[t^x_ij,X]||_2^2+||[t^y_ij,X]||_2^2
       =||[E_ij,X]||_2^2
       =sum_ell c_ij(ell)|f(ell)-f(ell-h)|^2,             (2)

  c_ij(ell)=sum_{v:depth(v)=ell}||E_ij v||^2.

Thus (1) is in exactly the normalization needed for the Casimir ramp
energy: no extra factor two is omitted. A convention replacing E_ij by
E_ij/sqrt(2) would change (1) and (2) by a factor 1/2 and must not be
mixed with this one.

2. EXACT ROOT-TRACE RECURRENCE

Write h=j-i. Each E_ij changes depth by -h. For q>=0 define

  g_q=dim span{weight spaces of depth q},
  t_ij(q)=sum_{v:depth(v)=q}<v,H_ij v>
        =sum_{v:depth(v)=q}(mu_i-mu_j).

Let c_ij(q)=sum_{depth(v)=q}||E_ij v||^2, with c_ij(q)=0 for
0<=q<h: raising by height h cannot reach a negative depth. For q>=0,
the block E_ij: V[q+h]->V[q] and its adjoint E_ji give equal squared
Hilbert-Schmidt norms. Taking the trace of
[E_ij,E_ji]=H_ij on V[q] therefore gives

  c_ij(q+h)-c_ij(q)=t_ij(q).                            (3)

In particular,

  c_ij(ell)=sum_{r>=1: ell-rh>=0} t_ij(ell-rh).          (4)

The sum is over one residue class modulo h, not every shell. The sign
in (3) follows from [E_ij,E_ji]=H_ij; reversing the root-vector
convention reverses both the root and the recurrence. Since c_ij is a
sum of squared norms, it is nonnegative, while for an upper bound (4)
may safely be bounded by the sum of absolute trace contributions.

3. WEIGHT AND PBW BOUNDS

For type A, represent lambda by a weakly decreasing partition
(Lambda_1,...,Lambda_m), up to its common shift, with
lambda_i=Lambda_i-Lambda_(i+1). The weight polytope is the convex hull
of the Weyl orbit of lambda. Every weight mu consequently satisfies

  |mu_i-mu_j|<=Lambda_1-Lambda_m
               =sum_{r=1}^{m-1}lambda_r <=(m-1) C N.     (5)

This is global over all weights, not only shallow ones. Hence
|t_ij(q)|<=(m-1) C N g_q.

PBW gives the needed cumulative shell-volume bound. The module is spanned
by ordered monomials in the p positive-root lowering operators applied
to a highest-weight vector. A monomial with exponents n_alpha has depth
sum_alpha ht(alpha)n_alpha. Thus

  V(L):=sum_{q=0}^L g_q
       <= #{(n_alpha)>=0: sum_alpha ht(alpha)n_alpha<=L}
       <= #{(n_1,...,n_p)>=0: sum n_s<=L}
       = binom(L+p,p)
       <= A_p (L+1)^p,       A_p=p^p/p!.                (6)

The middle inequality uses ht(alpha)>=1. This direct cumulative count
avoids an incorrect extra power that would result from first using the
coarse bound g_q=O(q^p) and then summing it. The PBW monomials need only
span the irreducible module; no independence assertion is needed.

Combining (4)--(6), and bounding a single residue class by all shells,

  c_ij(ell)
    <=(m-1) C N sum_{q=0}^{ell-h}g_q
    <=(m-1) C A_p N (ell+1)^p.

There are p positive roots, so (1) follows with, for example,
C_{m,C}=p(m-1) C A_p. The lower regularity constant c and the cutoff
c'N are not needed for this upper bound; they may be needed elsewhere
to ensure stable shell formulas or lower bounds on the ramp norm.

4. RAMP ENERGY AND ROOTS OF HEIGHT GREATER THAN ONE

For alpha_ij of height h=j-i, the ramp is 1/R-Lipschitz, so
|f(ell)-f(ell-h)|<=h/R. A nonzero difference can occur only for
ell<=R+h. By (2) and (1),

  E(F_R)
    <= C_{m,C} N/R^2
       sum_{h=1}^{m-1}(m-h)h^2
          sum_{ell=0}^{R+h}(ell+1)^p
    <= C'_{m,C} N R^(p-1),       R>=1.                  (7)

Here there are exactly m-h positive roots of height h. We used
sum_{ell=0}^{R+h}(ell+1)^p<= (R+h+1)^(p+1), and for fixed m,
R+h+1<=2(m+1)R for R>=1. Thus all higher-height roots are covered:
they shift the edge by h, produce the h^2 slope penalty, and contribute
only a fixed m-dependent factor. Dropping them or pretending they have
height one would not be a valid exact energy identity, although fixed m
makes their total compatible with the same asymptotic bound.

5. EXPONENT WARNING / SU(3) FALSIFIER IF p MEANS RANK

The notation p is decisive. The PBW cumulative exponent in (6) is
|Phi^+|=m(m-1)/2, not rank m-1. If the proposed p was intended to be
rank, its shell bound fails. For SU(3), let a=b=N and use the principal
height shells for 0<=q<=N. They have

  g_q=floor((q+2)^2/4),
  c_12(q)=sum_{s=0}^{q-1}(N-s/2)g_s.

For q<=N, N-s/2>=N/2 and g_s>=(s+1)^2/4, so

  c_12(q)>=(N/8)sum_{j=1}^q j^2>=N q^3/24.

The total positive-root conductance is at least c_12(q). No constant
can bound it by C N(q+1)^2 uniformly for q growing with N. Thus rank
exponent p=2 is false; the correct positive-root count exponent is
p=3. The same shell count is the PBW growth dimension for SU(3).

CONCLUSION

With p=binom(m,2) and the standard matrix-unit root normalization, the
claimed conductance and ramp-energy upper bounds hold, indeed for every
existing shell, with constants depending only on m and the upper
Dynkin-label comparability constant C. Root heights h>1 are handled by
the exact recurrence on residue classes and the h^2 ramp factor. If p
was meant to denote rank, the claim is explicitly falsified by the
regular SU(3) example above.
