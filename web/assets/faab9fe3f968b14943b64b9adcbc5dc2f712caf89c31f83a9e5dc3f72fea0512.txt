BOUNDED-CAPACITY BROADCASTING: COMPATIBLE SMOOTHING AND A FIXED-SANDWICH REDUCTION
7 October 2026. Status: internally derived candidate lemmas, with full proofs
below; broad bounded-Holevo implication remains OPEN. No EB reconstruction
theorem or counterexample to that implication is claimed.

STRONGEST CONCRETE RESULT

The broad target is equivalent to its restriction to experiments containing
a common faithful reference sigma and satisfying, for any one prescribed
constant 0<theta<1,

  (1-theta)sigma <= rho <= (1+theta)sigma   for every promised rho.

In particular theta=1/2 suffices. These restricted experiments automatically
have Holevo capacity <=ln(1+theta), regardless of dimension/cardinality.
The reduction uses a new candidate compatible-smoothing lemma: a fixed-
accuracy substate approximation can be selected so that ANY channel nearly
fixing rho and sigma also nearly fixes the selected approximation, with a
dimension-independent O_{M,lambda}(b^(1/8)) bound. It does not assume exact
sigma preservation and does not convert measured residuals to trace residuals.

This eliminates the entropy-radius-to-domination step as a separate logical
gate, subject to independent audit of the lemma. The sole unresolved theorem
can be concentrated on the fixed two-sided sandwich class above.

DEFINITIONS

T(rho,tau)=||rho-tau||_1/2. e(E)=inf_EB Lambda sup_{rho in E}T(Lambda rho,rho).
b2(E)=inf_CPTP B:H->H tensor H sup_{rho in E,a=1,2}T(B_a rho,rho).
Outputs may be correlated; one full-domain channel works for every promised
state. Capacity C(E)=sup_finite_priors [S(sum p rho)-sum p S(rho)], in nats.
Every Hilbert space is finite-dimensional, but dimension and label counts
are unrestricted and may grow. Acquisition/runtime/output-alphabet costs
are not bounded by the lemmas.

1. A CANONICAL SUBNORMALIZED SMOOTHER

For a density sigma, with inverses on its support, put

  K_sigma(M)={tau>=0: tau<=M sigma, Tr tau<=1},
  f(rho,tau)=||sqrt(rho)sqrt(tau)||_1,
  Q_sigma(tau)=||sigma^(-1/4)tau sigma^(-1/4)||_2^2,
  J_{rho,sigma}(tau)=-f(rho,tau)+lambda Q_sigma(tau).

For M>=1, lambda>0 let G_{M,lambda}(rho,sigma) be the unique minimizer of
J over K_sigma(M). Feasibility is compact. Root fidelity is concave in tau;
Q is a strictly positive quadratic form on supp(sigma), giving uniqueness.
Allowing Tr tau<=1, rather than forcing Tr tau=1, is crucial for the support-
independent transport estimate below.

Three elementary facts used repeatedly:

  (i)  Q_sigma(X) >= ||X||_1^2 for Hermitian X supported on sigma.
       By duality, Tr U X is the Hilbert-Schmidt pairing of
       sigma^(1/4)U sigma^(1/4) and sigma^(-1/4)X sigma^(-1/4).
       Holder gives ||sigma^(1/4)U sigma^(1/4)||_2<=1 for any unitary U.

  (ii) tau<=M sigma gives Q_sigma(tau)<=M Tr tau<=M.
       For Y=sigma^(-1/4)tau sigma^(-1/4), one has
       0<=Y<=M sigma^(1/2), hence Tr Y^2<=M Tr(Y sigma^(1/2)).

  (iii) If tau minimizes J and v is feasible, then
       J(v)-J(tau)>=lambda Q_sigma(v-tau).
       Apply concavity of f and the exact quadratic identity to
       (1-t)tau+t v; use minimality and let t down to zero.

The imported channel inequalities are

  f(T rho,T tau)>=f(rho,tau),
  Q_{T sigma}(T tau)<=Q_sigma(tau),

for CPTP T. The second is sandwiched Renyi-2 data processing. Both extend
to subnormalized tau by homogeneity. These imports have no dimensional
constant; support-singular outputs use generalized inverses.

2. UNIFORM TRANSPORT OF THE CONSTRAINT AND THE OBJECTIVE

Let sigma,nu be density matrices and tau in K_sigma(M). Write
tau=sigma^(1/2)A sigma^(1/2), 0<=A<=M I, extending A by zero off the support.
Set tau'=nu^(1/2)A nu^(1/2). By Powers-Stormer and Holder,

  ||tau'-tau||_1 <= 2M ||sqrt(nu)-sqrt(sigma)||_2
                  <= delta := 2M sqrt(||nu-sigma||_1).

Let kappa=tau'/max{1,Tr tau'}. Then kappa in K_nu(M) and

  ||kappa-tau||_1<=2 delta.

The quadratic form has the useful bounded-likelihood representation

  Q_sigma(tau)=Tr(A sigma^(1/2) A sigma^(1/2)),
  Q_nu(tau')=Tr(A nu^(1/2) A nu^(1/2)).

Expanding the difference and applying Hilbert-Schmidt Holder gives

  |Q_nu(tau')-Q_sigma(tau)|<=2M^2 sqrt(||nu-sigma||_1).

Trace clipping only decreases Q. For density rho,rho' and positive tau,kappa
of trace<=1, root-fidelity variation and Powers-Stormer give

  |f(rho,tau)-f(rho',kappa)|
     <=sqrt(||rho-rho'||_1)+sqrt(||tau-kappa||_1).

Thus, with d_r=||rho-rho'||_1 and d_s=||sigma-nu||_1,

  J_{rho',nu}(kappa)<=J_{rho,sigma}(tau)+xi,
  xi := sqrt(d_r)+2 sqrt(M) d_s^(1/4)+2 lambda M^2 sqrt(d_s).

Transport in the opposite direction obeys the same bound. Therefore the
two minimum objective values h(rho,sigma) and h(rho',nu) differ by at most xi.
No bound on the number of states, spectral condition number, or dimension
appears.

3. COMPATIBLE-SMOOTHING LEMMA

Let T be any CPTP endomorphism and suppose

  ||T rho-rho||_1<=d_r,  ||T sigma-sigma||_1<=d_s.

Put tau=G(rho,sigma), nu=T sigma and tau_1=G(T rho,nu). T tau is feasible
for the second optimization. Data processing gives

  J_{T rho,nu}(T tau)<=h(rho,sigma).

Since h(T rho,nu)>=h(rho,sigma)-xi, strong convexity implies

  ||T tau-tau_1||_1<=sqrt(xi/lambda).

Transport tau to kappa in K_nu(M). Its objective is at most h(rho,sigma)+xi,
hence at most h(T rho,nu)+2xi. The same argument gives

  ||kappa-tau_1||_1<=sqrt(2xi/lambda).

Combining these with ||kappa-tau||_1<=2delta proves

  ||T G(rho,sigma)-G(rho,sigma)||_1
    <= (1+sqrt(2)) sqrt(xi/lambda)+4M sqrt(d_s),

  xi=sqrt(d_r)+2 sqrt(M) d_s^(1/4)+2 lambda M^2 sqrt(d_s).

In particular, if both trace distances T(T rho,rho), T(T sigma,sigma) are
<=b, the right side is O_{M,lambda}(b^(1/8)) and tends to zero uniformly
over all promised rho. Exact sigma preservation is not required.

For comparison, if T sigma=sigma exactly the same proof without transport
gives the sharper ||T G-G||_1<=(2b)^(1/4)/sqrt(lambda).

4. BOUNDED RELATIVE ENTROPY GIVES A CLOSE NORMALIZED SMOOTHER

Assume D(rho||sigma)<=C. Fix 0<epsilon<1, eta=epsilon^2/8, and choose

  M=(1-eta)^(-1) exp((C+ln 2)/eta),  lambda=eta/M.

The Jain-Nayak quantum substate theorem supplies a DENSITY tau_0<=M sigma
with squared fidelity >=1-eta. Its use of observational divergence in bits
is bounded by D(rho||sigma)/ln 2+1, giving the stated M.
Thus f(rho,tau_0)>=1-eta and Q_sigma(tau_0)<=M. Minimality gives

  f(rho,tau)>=1-2eta=1-epsilon^2/4,
  t:=Tr tau>=f(rho,tau)^2>=1-epsilon^2/2.

Let hat G=tau/t. It is a density matrix and obeys

  hat G<=2M sigma,
  T(rho,hat G)<=epsilon,
  ||T hat G-hat G||_1=||T tau-tau||_1/t.

Hence, at FIXED epsilon (and therefore fixed M,lambda), approximate
preservation of rho and sigma implies vanishing preservation error of
hat G, without adding a constant epsilon to that broadcasting error.

For lambda<=1/4 one also has G(sigma,sigma)=sigma. Indeed Q_sigma(tau)>=t^2
and f(sigma,tau)<=sqrt(t); -sqrt(t)+lambda t^2 is decreasing on [0,1].
Our chosen lambda<1/8 satisfies this condition.

5. HOLEVO CENTER AND THE EXACT FIXED-SANDWICH REDUCTION

For each finite-dimensional experiment E, close it in trace norm. A prior
maximizing chi exists: the graph (rho,S(rho)) has compact convex hull, and
the objective S(mean)-mean entropy is continuous. Caratheodory gives a
finite-support maximizing prior (at most dim(H)^2+1 points). Its average
sigma lies in conv(E) and satisfies

  D(rho||sigma)<=C(E) for all rho in E.

Proof: mix the maximizing prior with mass t on rho. The derivative of chi
at t=0 is D(rho||sigma)-C(E); any larger value contradicts maximality.
Support outside sigma would give an infinite positive derivative. If sigma
is singular on the original ambient H, let P project onto its support and
use R(X)=PXP+Tr((I-P)X)sigma. For an arbitrary original broadcaster B use
(R tensor R)B restricted to the input support. R is CPTP, fixes every
promised state, and contracts the marginal errors. Thus the same argument
works on a Hilbert space on which sigma is faithful; maps extend to the
original input algebra by measuring P and replacing the complementary block.

If B broadcasts E with marginal error b, linearity and convexity give the
same error bound on sigma. Add sigma to E. For fixed epsilon apply hat G
statewise with the same sigma, M,lambda. The resulting experiment E_hat
contains sigma, is dominated by U sigma with U=2M, and the SAME B satisfies
b2(E_hat)->0 by Section 3 whenever b2(E)->0. Also

  e(E)<=e(E_hat)+2epsilon.

Now fix ANY 0<theta<1 and put a=theta/(U-1)>0. For rho in E_hat define

  tau_rho=(1-a)sigma+a rho,
  F={tau_rho:rho in E_hat}.

Then sigma in F and

  (1-theta)sigma<=tau_rho<=(1+theta)sigma,
  C(F)<=ln(1+theta),
  b2(F)<=b2(E_hat).

The capacity bound follows from D(tau_rho||sigma)<=ln(1+theta), using
operator monotonicity of logarithm and the relative-entropy radius identity
(or the direct identity sum p D(tau||sigma)=chi+D(mean||sigma)).

For any EB Lambda with F-error delta, the fact that sigma itself belongs to
F gives, for each rho,

  T(Lambda rho,rho)
     <=[T(Lambda tau_rho,tau_rho)+(1-a)T(Lambda sigma,sigma)]/a
     <=(2-a)delta/a.

Thus e(E_hat)<=(2-a)e(F)/a. This is an exact affine identity, not an
information-extraction assertion.

Consequently, for sequences of arbitrary finite-dimensional experiments:

  A: bounded C(E_n), b2(E_n)->0 => e(E_n)->0

is EQUIVALENT to

  B_theta: sigma_n in F_n and every rho in F_n lies between
           (1-theta)sigma_n and (1+theta)sigma_n,
           b2(F_n)->0 => e(F_n)->0.

A implies B_theta immediately. For the reverse implication fix epsilon:
compatible smoothing and dilution produce F_n in B_theta, so e(F_n)->0,
hence e(E_hat_n)->0 and limsup e(E_n)<=2epsilon. Let epsilon down to zero.

Contrapositive: take a subsequence on which original e stays bounded below.
Any counterexample to A can then be converted to a counterexample
to B_theta at any prescribed positive theta, with all states faithful and
uniformly comparable to a reference, and capacity <=ln(1+theta). Fix
epsilon below one quarter of the surviving original EB-error lower bound;
all dilution constants then remain independent of n and the lower bound
after dilution remains positive. It is not claimed to remain numerically
large when theta is small. M and lambda are chosen from ONE uniform capacity
bound C for the entire sequence, not separately from changing C(E_n).

Another consequence: validity of the broad implication for experiments of
capacity at most any ONE fixed C0>0 would already imply its validity for all
bounded capacities. Choose theta with ln(1+theta)<=C0 and use the reduction.
There can be no qualitative positive capacity threshold separating truth
from falsehood of this implication.

G is a nonlinear comparison-family selector, not a proposed blind CPTP
preprocessor. The resulting EB map acts directly on the original input.
The affine inverse in the error estimate is an analytic identity; no
unphysical inverse-dilution operation is implemented.

6. THE REMAINING LOAD-BEARING QUESTION

Prove or refute B_(1/2): all states between sigma/2 and 3sigma/2, sigma
included, b2->0, yet can uniform EB error stay positive? This is still open
here. Pair fidelity-affinity gaps alone cannot settle it: the root's
matching construction has exactly such bounded comparability and vanishing
pair gap, but its b2 is bounded below, so it is not a counterexample.

The Pauli accessible-information obstruction remains valid. Neither the
smoother nor the reduction removes the need to use broadcasting itself.
The finite-label extractor still carries its label-count factor; the proof
above never asserts a measured-to-trace norm conversion.

7. REJECTED ROUTES / FALSIFICATION ATTEMPTS

Naive substate approximation: T(rho,rho')<=epsilon only gives
b2(E')<=b2(E)+2epsilon. A qualitative dominated-family theorem is therefore
insufficient without the compatible-selection argument in Sections 1-4.

Canonical positive likelihood clipping: sigma^(-1/2)rho sigma^(-1/2) may
have a huge eigenvalue carried by the entire pure vector even when relative
entropy is bounded and the noncommuting component is small. Clipping that
operator can remove essentially all rho mass. This route was rejected.

Correlated-extension ladder: conditioning a k-extendible Omega_RB^k by
bounded effects F_x, then taking dyadic marginals and uniform flags, gives
capacity-bounded exact-shift broadcasters. However a bottom-family EB lower
bound is insufficient: an EB reconstruction sees the whole high-sector
extension and may estimate the bottom states from it. No valid obstruction
with nonclassicality persisting over enough dyadic levels was obtained.

Exact-reference correction of B was investigated, but is unnecessary for
the final smoother proof. No unproved channel-correction map is imported.
