Separate candidate theorem: sparse catalyst-substrate support
=============================================================

Status
------
This is a separately scoped extension, not an assumption or step in the
complete-pair proof in REPORT.txt. The case analysis below closes on paper
for the specified paired-edge family; it has not received independent
expert review. A finite exact generator consistency check is in
validate_sparse.py, but it is not an all-state drift certificate. Status:
INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE.

Family and communicating classes
---------------------------------
Let J and I be finite. Let E subset J x I be any bipartite edge set. For
every (j,i) in E include the two reversible reaction pairs (four directed
reactions) from the main report with their positive finite constants
p_ji,q_ji,u_ji,v_ji and integer
k_ji>=1. For every j include 0 <-> A_j with positive alpha_j,delta_j.
There are no substrate reactions for nonedges. Let N(i)={j:(j,i) in E}.
An isolated substrate i with N(i)=empty has its B_i count b_i unchanged by
every reaction, hence it is a conserved class label. Fix all
isolated-substrate counts. The claim is positive recurrence of every
communicating class within each such fixed-label slice.

If N(i) is nonempty for every i, the full lattice is one irreducible class.
More generally, in a fixed isolated-count slice the remaining coordinates
form one irreducible class: from any state, immigrate a neighbor catalyst
when necessary to clear each nonisolated B_i through A_j+B_i -> A_j, then
drain catalysts; conversely immigrate catalysts, produce each required B_i
through A_j -> A_j+B_i, and use temporary neighbor catalysts for substrate
targets whose final catalyst vector has no active neighbor. Isolated B
counts cannot change, so distinct labels give distinct closed classes.
Every reaction has its reverse, hence the graph is weakly reversible.

Candidate Lyapunov function and exact generator
------------------------------------------------
Fix lambda_j>0 and epsilon_i>0 for nonisolated i (one may take
epsilon_i=1). Let

    h_i(a)=1_{sum_{j in N(i)} a_j=0},
    c_i=R_i-1, d_i=1-R_i^(-1),
    V(a,b)=sum_j lambda_j a_j + sum_i R_i^(b_i)(1+epsilon_i h_i(a)) + K 1_{A=0}.

For isolated i the h term is omitted and R_i^(b_i) is constant on each
class. Choose rational R_i>1 close enough to one that

    chi_j=lambda_j delta_j-sum_{i:(j,i) in E} p_ji (R_i-1)>0.

Set H=sum_j alpha_j lambda_j and K>(H+1)/sum_j alpha_j. On every fixed
isolated-count class V is positive and norm-like; its nonconstant substrate
weights are at least R_i^(b_i).

The exact generator identity is

    LV = H - sum_j lambda_j delta_j a_j
       + sum_(j,i) in E [
           p_ji a_j c_i R_i^(b_i)
         - q_ji a_j b_i d_i R_i^(b_i)
         + lambda_j k_ji u_ji a_j b_i
         - lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i ]
       - K alpha_sum 1_{A=0} + K sum_j delta_j 1_{a=e_j}
       + sum_(i nonisolated) epsilon_i R_i^(b_i) [
           -alpha(N(i)) 1_{h_i=1}
           + sum_(j in N(i)) delta_j 1_{a restricted to N(i)=e_j} ].

Here alpha(N(i))=sum_(j in N(i)) alpha_j. The last bracket is the only
new term. Immigration of a neighbor switches h_i from 1 to 0 and decreases
V. Catalyst death switches h_i from 0 to 1 only when the dying molecule is
the last active neighbor in N(i); that creates the positive term displayed.
The autocatalytic reverse reaction leaves one A_j and therefore does not
create this switch.

Finite-set drift proof by escaping sequences
--------------------------------------------
Work in one fixed isolated-count class. Suppose a state sequence escapes
every finite set. By passing to a subsequence, each catalyst count and each
nonisolated substrate count is either eventually constant or tends to
infinity, and every h_i is eventually constant.

For each catalyst j group its death, all edge terms involving a_j, and the
reverse factorial terms as

    D_j = a_j[-lambda_j delta_j + sum_(i~j) d_ji(b_i)]
          - sum_(i~j) lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1) b_i,
    d_ji(m)=p_ji c_i R_i^m-q_ji d_i m R_i^m+lambda_j k_ji u_ji m.

If a_j tends to infinity and every neighboring b_i is zero, then
    D_j <= -chi_j a_j -> -infinity.
If some neighboring b_i is eventually positive and bounded, its negative
factorial term has degree k_ji+1>=2 in a_j and dominates every linear
term. Other neighboring b_l tending to infinity have d_jl(b_l)->-infinity,
so their linear coefficients are eventually negative; zero-b edges
contribute only the already strictly negative chi_j margin. Thus D_j tends
to -infinity whenever a_j tends to infinity.

If a_j is eventually a positive constant and some neighboring b_i tends
to infinity, then d_ji(b_i)->-infinity and that edge makes D_j tend to
-infinity. If a_j is zero, its edge terms vanish. Therefore every D_j is
bounded or tends to -infinity along the chosen subsequence; no catalyst
group can diverge positively.

Now consider an unbounded nonisolated substrate b_i. If h_i=1, its switch
term is -epsilon_i alpha(N(i)) R_i^(b_i), tending to -infinity because
N(i) is nonempty. If h_i=0, at least one neighbor catalyst j has a_j>0.
For b_i large, on every active edge (j,i),

    p_ji c_i R_i^(b_i) - q_ji d_i b_i R_i^(b_i)
      + lambda_j k_ji u_ji b_i

is negative, with its negative q term of order b_i R_i^(b_i). If the
positive last-neighbor-death switch occurs, the defining indicator forces
the relevant neighbor to have a_j=1 and all other neighbors zero; its
positive switch is only epsilon_i delta_j R_i^(b_i), which is dominated
by the corresponding -q_ji d_i b_i R_i^(b_i) edge term. If there is no
positive switch, an active neighbor edge itself tends to -infinity. Hence
every unbounded active substrate contributes a net negative divergent
term after pairing any positive switch with its matching q edge.

Switch terms on bounded substrate coordinates are bounded. Catalyst
boundary terms are bounded constants. Since the graph is finite, the cases
above show LV tends to -infinity along every escaping sequence in the
fixed class. Thus F_C={x in C: LV(x)>-1} is finite. Also LV is bounded
above on C (it is <=-1 off F_C and has a finite maximum on F_C).

Physical-time nonexplosion and recurrence
-----------------------------------------
On the fixed class, V has finite sublevel sets and finite reaction rates
there. The localized stopped-Dynkin argument from Section 3 of REPORT.txt
applies verbatim: LV<=C_C yields

    P_x(tau_R<=t) <= (V(x)+C_C t)/R,

for tau_R=inf{t:V(X_t)>=R}. Rates are bounded on finite sublevel sets,
so infinitely many jumps cannot occur in a bounded set. Letting R increase
rules out finite-time escape and proves nonexplosion in physical time.
The continuous-time Foster criterion then proves positive recurrence of
each closed irreducible class. The proof is pointwise in the fixed rates;
it supplies no uniform-in-rate stationary tightness or mixing bound.

Scope boundary
--------------
The complete-pair theorem in REPORT.txt is the primary result and does not
depend on this extension. The sparse proof above relies on every included
edge having all its paired reactions with positive rates, and on positive
immigration alpha_j and linear death delta_j for all catalysts. Missing
reverse edges, zero rates, non-mass-action propensities, or a different
support-switch mechanism are outside this claim. The indicator-corrected
function must not be replaced by a constant-coefficient exponential
substrate weight when N(i) is sparse: the substrate can be frozen while all
its neighbors are absent.

Finite exact fixture
--------------------
validate_sparse.py checks the generator identity for
E={(A1,B1),(A2,B1),(A2,B2)}, alpha_j=delta_j=p_ji=1,
q_ji=v_ji=2, u_ji=1, k_ji=1, lambda_j=1, R_i=6/5, epsilon_i=1, K=2.
These parameters satisfy chi_1=4/5, chi_2=3/5 and K>(H+1)/alpha_sum.
The checker uses rational arithmetic on all 8^4=4096 states with
a_j,b_i in {0,...,7}, including all reaction boundaries. It checks only
the switch and generator identity; it does not certify an all-state Foster
set.
