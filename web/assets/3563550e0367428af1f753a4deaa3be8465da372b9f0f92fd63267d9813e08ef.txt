Cycle 2 near-full-rank memo — capacity beyond the projector profile
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally proved for the stated finite-dimensional operators. No novelty or priority claim; external validation UNKNOWN.

Question and notation

For a positive invariant operator L on HS(H), with d=dim(H), define

  Λ_L(Q)=inf{ <X,LX> : ||X||_HS=1, rank(X)≤Q }.

The near-full case is Q=d−k, especially k=1. The exact projector-profile
theorem available in the parent cycle-2 result gives, for an irreducible
carrier and invariant spectral projectors Π_t,

  Λ_L(Q) ≥ Φ_L(Q):=∫_0^∞ [1−Q D(t)/d]_+ dt,
  D(t)=Tr Π_t.

This memo checks what this lower bound says near full rank, solves the SU(2)
Casimir case at k=1, and gives a sharp counterexample to recovering capacity
from dimension and spectral gap alone. All operator and representation
parameters are specified below; no unnamed KLS-equivalent assertion is used.

1. What the profile necessarily loses near full rank

Suppose ker L is one-dimensional and the first positive eigenvalue is λ_1.
Then D(t)=1 for 0≤t<λ_1, while D(t)≥2 for t≥λ_1. If k≤d/2 and Q=d−k,
then for t≥λ_1,

  QD(t)/d ≥ 2(d−k)/d ≥ 1.

Consequently the entire integrated profile is just

  Φ_L(d−k)=λ_1 k/d.                                      (1)

This is a valid lower bound, but uses only the scalar kernel and first gap.
If instead dim ker L=g≥2, then for Q≥d/2, Qg/d≥1 and this profile gives
zero. Thus neither a nontrivial kernel nor a high-rank capacity is captured
by a dimension-only scalar formula in general.

2. SU(2) spin-j Casimir: Λ_N(d−1)=Θ(1/log N)

Let H=V_j, j=N/2, d=N+1, and let J_1,J_2,J_3 be the Hermitian spin
generators, [J_a,J_b]=i ε_{abc}J_c. Use

  L(X)=Σ_{a=1}^3 [J_a,[J_a,X]],
  E(X)=<X,LX>=Σ_a ||[J_a,X]||_HS².

Under conjugation,

  End(V_j)=⊕_{ell=0}^N V_ell,
  L|_{V_ell}=ell(ell+1) I,   dim V_ell=2ell+1.

The scalar sector is ell=0, and the gap is 2. Schur's lemma and the trace
of each spectral projector give Tr_2 Π_ell=(2ell+1)I/d. At Q=d−1, (1)
therefore says only Φ_L(d−1)=2/d.

Here is a stronger lower bound using the rank defect directly. Let X have
rank at most d−1 and HS norm one. Write

  X=α I/√d + Y,   Tr Y=0,   u=|α|²,   ||Y||_HS²=1−u,
  Y=Σ_{ell=1}^N Y_ell.

There is a unit vector v in the right kernel of X, so Yv=−αv/√d and
|<P_v,Y>_HS|²=u/d for P_v=|v><v|. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz in the
orthogonal Casimir sectors gives

  u/d ≤ E(X) Σ_{ell=1}^N ||Π_ell P_v||_HS²/[ell(ell+1)].

For each ell, the rank-one projector-mass bound and the partial trace above
give ||Π_ell P_v||_HS²≤(2ell+1)/d. Thus, with

  S_N=Σ_{ell=1}^N (2ell+1)/[ell(ell+1)]
     =H_N+H_{N+1}−1,

we have E(X)≥u/S_N. The gap also gives E(X)≥2(1−u). Minimizing the
maximum of these two lower bounds over 0≤u≤1 yields

  Λ_L(d−1) ≥ 2/(2S_N+1) ~ 1/(2 log N).                (2)

For a matching-order upper bound, use the J_z eigenbasis indexed by
r=0,...,N, with magnetic number j−r. For diagonal X=diag(f_0,...,f_N),
the ladder-operator formula gives the exact energy

  E(X)=Σ_{r=0}^{N−1} w_r(f_{r+1}−f_r)²,
  w_r=(r+1)(N−r).

Set R_N=Σ_{r=0}^{N−1}1/w_r=2H_N/(N+1), f_0=0, and
f_{r+1}−f_r=1/(w_r R_N). Then f_N=1, every f_r for r≥1 is positive,
and X has rank d−1. Its energy is 1/R_N=d/(2H_N). The weights are
symmetric, so f_r+f_{N−r}=1; pairing these terms (and including the
middle term when N is even) gives ||X||_HS²=Σ_r f_r²≥d/4. Therefore

  2/(2S_N+1) ≤ Λ_L(d−1) ≤ 2/H_N,
  hence Λ_L(d−1)=Θ(1/log N).                          (3)

No asymptotic optimization or numerical extrapolation is involved. The
coherent-hole trial (I−P)/√(d−1), for a coherent rank-one P, has energy
E(P)/(d−1)=N/N=1 here, so it is much worse than the tapered diagonal trial.
The projector profile is weaker than the true order by Θ(d/log d).

3. Product-group check: the exact profile can remain loose

For G=SU(2)^n on H=(C²)^{⊗n}, d=2^n, take the sum of the n local
conjugation Casimirs. The exact eigenvalue 2r has multiplicity
binom(n,r)3^r, and the cumulative dimension through 2r is

  D_r=Σ_{s=0}^r binom(n,s)3^s.

For Q=d−k with k/d≤3n/(3n+1), QD_1/d≥1, so all profile intervals
after [0,2) vanish and

  Φ_L(d−k)=2k/d.                                      (4)

At k=1 this is 2/d. The rank-(d−1) hole from a pure product projection
P has E(P)=n and gives Λ_L(d−1)≤n/(d−1). Thus the exact profile and this
explicit high-rank construction leave a factor of order n between them.
This calculation does not determine the product-group optimum; it shows
that the first-gap profile alone is not a sharp near-full estimate.

4. Same dimension and gap, capacities differing by Θ(d)

There is a sharper obstruction to a scalar capacity claim, even on the same
irreducible SU(2) carrier. Keep H=V_j and its multiplicity-free decomposition
End(V_j)=⊕_{ell=0}^N V_ell, with d=N+1≥3. Define two invariant positive
self-adjoint operators by their eigenvalues on these sectors.

For L_flat, use eigenvalue 0 at ell=0 and eigenvalue 1 at every ell≥1.
For L_M, use eigenvalues 0 at ell=0, 1 at ell=1, and M=d² at ell≥2.
Both have the same one-dimensional kernel and the same first positive gap
1. At Q=d−1, both integrated profiles equal 1/d: before the gap D(t)=1,
and after it the positive part [1−QD(t)/d]_+ is zero. Yet their capacities
are different in order.

For L_flat, E(X)=||X−(Tr X/d)I||_HS². Since
|Tr X|≤√Q ||X||_HS for rank(X)≤Q,

  Λ_flat(Q)≥1−Q/d.

The normalized rank-Q projection attains equality, so
Λ_flat(d−1)=1/d exactly.

For L_M, write X=aI+Y_1+Y_h, where Y_1 is the ell=1 component and
Y_h the sum of ell≥2 components. Set s=d|a|². A unit right-kernel vector
v exists for rank(X)≤d−1. Since Xv=0,

  |a|=|v*(Y_1+Y_h)v|≤||Y_1||op+||Y_h||op.

Write Y_1=Σ_{a=1}^3 b_a J_a. The identities
Tr(J_aJ_b)=δ_ab d j(j+1)/3 and Σ_a J_a²=j(j+1)I imply

  ||Y_1||op≤√(3/d)||Y_1||_HS,   ||Y_h||op≤||Y_h||_HS.

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz with weights 1 and M gives
|a|²≤(3/d+1/M)E_M(X), hence E_M(X)≥s/(3+d/M). Normalization and
M≥1 also give E_M(X)≥||Y_1||²+||Y_h||²=1−s. Minimizing the maximum
of these two bounds yields

  E_M(X)≥1/(4+d/M).

For the upper bound, X=I+J_z/j has exactly one zero eigenvalue and all
other eigenvalues nonzero, so its rank is d−1. It has only scalar and
ell=1 components, and direct use of Tr(J_z²)=d j(j+1)/3 gives

  E_M(X)/||X||_HS²=(j+1)/(4j+1)=(d+1)/(4d−2).

With M=d² this proves

  d/(4d+1) ≤ Λ_M(d−1) ≤ (d+1)/(4d−2),
  so Λ_M(d−1)→1/4 while Λ_flat(d−1)=1/d.             (5)

This is a factor Θ(d) difference with the same carrier dimension, kernel
dimension, and gap; moreover the profile value at Q=d−1 is 1/d for both.
It directly falsifies any claim that those scalar data or this near-full
integrated profile determine Λ. These are invariant positive operators,
not both generator-sum Casimirs; the example is a limitation on arbitrary
invariant-L inference, not a counterexample to a separately stated theorem
restricted to a particular Lie-algebra Dirichlet form.

5. SU(m) symmetric powers: coherent-hole scale remains unresolved

For H_N=Sym^N(C^m), d_N=binom(N+m−1,m−1), the first Casimir eigenvalue
is m and the scalar kernel is one-dimensional. For k≤d_N/2, the exact
profile therefore gives only

  Λ_N(d_N−k)≥m k/d_N.                                 (6)

Under Tr_fund(T_aT_b)=δ_ab/2, a highest-weight coherent rank-one projector
P has E(P)=N(m−1). Thus the normalized hole is an admissible rank-(d_N−1)
operator and

  Λ_N(d_N−1)≤N(m−1)/(d_N−1).                        (7)

For fixed m≥3 this upper scale is Θ(N/d_N), a factor Θ(N) above (6).
These two bounds neither establish nor refute the proposed coherent-hole
order for this SU(m) Casimir. A stronger lower bound would need additional
rank-sensitive structure beyond the scalar projector profile. For m=2,
(3) supplies that structure and the coherent-hole order is not sharp.

Conclusions and limits

The exact profile implies λ_1 k/d for a simple-kernel, near-full-rank
operator, and zero for a kernel of dimension at least two in the stated
range. It is not an exact capacity principle. For the SU(2) spin Casimir,
an explicit kernel-vector argument and a resistance-profile diagonal trial
prove Λ_N(d−1)=Θ(1/log N), improving the profile 2/(N+1) by a factor of
order N/log N. The product SU(2)^n calculation shows a further potentially
large gap without resolving its optimum. The arbitrary-invariant-L pair
shows sharply that dimension, kernel dimension, gap, and the near-full
profile do not determine capacity. For SU(m), m≥3, the coherent-hole
heuristic remains between a proved lower bound of order 1/d_N and an upper
bound of order N/d_N; it remains UNKNOWN whether the latter is sharp.

All claims above are finite-dimensional derivations from the displayed
decompositions, partial traces, and explicit matrices. No external
validation, novelty search, operational memory theorem, log-Sobolev/KLS
result, or transfer beyond the stated operators is claimed.
