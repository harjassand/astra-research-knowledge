N04 CELLULAR SENSING — FROZEN RESEARCH RECORD v1
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: internally derived, exactly checked for a finite model; priority and empirical status unresolved.

Question
Can a finite-time predictive accuracy and causal memory determine the energetic cost of an autonomous cellular sensor when the environmental law and response speed are fixed? The construction below answers “no” for chemical work inferred from the sensor state trajectory: parallel reaction channels can have identical aggregate rates, hence identical complete input-output path laws, while carrying different net fuel fluxes. It also gives the exact work interval for one small channel family. This is a model-specific identifiability obstruction, not a claim that the general hidden-dissipation issue is new.

Claim ledger
- Source-reported: autonomous sensory networks can have information-acquisition rates not bounded by their thermodynamic dissipation rate (Barato, Hartich & Seifert, 2014, Sections II–IV); a general memory-minus-prediction term is related to dissipated work in a specified driven-system setting (Still et al., 2012, Eq. 14); resource-constrained predictive information has been studied for Gaussian cellular signals and specific network motifs (Tjalma et al., 2023); near-equilibrium lag error bounds for reversible Markov generators were reported by Chen & Limmer (2026).
- Internally derived: two channel-resolved, locally detailed-balanced sensor CRNs below have the same exogenous input law, the same full observed (input, memory) trajectory law, the same 1-bit memory and response time, and the same finite-horizon prediction error, but their ATP-work rates differ exactly by (3/20) kBT ln 4 per chosen time unit. A positive-dimensional family preserves those observables while varying the work rate over a sharp interval.
- Finite-tested: exact rational stationary-state and rate-sum checks, and floating-point entropy-production/work identity checks, are in `channel_nonidentifiability.py`. These certify this finite construction only.
- Unresolved: priority, external mathematical review, empirical realization in a named pathway, and any general lower bound for a restricted class with channel-resolved stoichiometry fixed.

Model and physical boundary
Take a bath at temperature T, beta=1/(kBT). The exogenous input E has two states + and − and switches in either direction at rate lambda=1. This is an unbiased telegraph source: its law is autonomous and is not affected by the sensor. The sensor memory M has two states 0 and 1. Its state is the measured output. Time is measured in units for which lambda=1.

At fixed E, there are three reversible chemical channels for each M transition. For channel nu, let d_{nu,e} be the rate for M=1 to M=0 at input e, and let u_{nu,e}=r_nu d_{nu,e} be the rate for M=0 to M=1. The local detailed-balance ratios are

    r = (4, 1, 1/4),       A_nu = ln r_nu = (ln 4, 0, -ln 4).

The two sensor states have equal standard free energy in this minimal example. Channel 1 couples a forward transition to one unit of fuel hydrolysis, channel 2 is fuel-free, and channel 3 couples a forward transition to the reverse fuel reaction. Thus the chemical work delivered to the sensor on a forward channel-nu event is kBT A_nu; reverse events carry the opposite work. The environmental state acts as a catalyst that changes both directional rate prefactors of a channel by the same factor, leaving its local detailed-balance ratio unchanged. The E transitions have equal forward and reverse rates and no chemical affinity. All rates listed below are positive, so every used channel has its reverse.

This is an autonomous finite-state CRN with a fixed stochastic input process; there is no clock, programmed switching protocol, or reset controller during sensing. The explicit energy ledger includes every sensor-channel fuel event. The symmetric E switch costs zero in this model. A laboratory signal generator that forces E away from this equilibrium telegraph law would be a separate work source and would have to be added to the boundary.

The observable aggregate sensor rates are fixed as follows:

    E=+:  u_+ = 2, d_+ = 1;       E=−:  u_- = 1, d_- = 2.

Here u_e is the total 0-to-1 rate and d_e is the total 1-to-0 rate, after summing the three pathways. Thus the sensor's conditional relaxation rate is u_e+d_e=3 in either input state. Following a fixed input switch, its conditional mean relaxes exponentially with time constant 1/3. This is the response-speed constraint used in the comparison.

Two hidden channel realizations
For each e, the down rates d_nu,e and the ratios r_nu determine all six channel-resolved rates. The first realization uses

    Network A, E=+: d=(23/60, 5/12, 1/5),
               u=(23/15, 5/12, 1/20).
    Network A, E=−: d=(1/15, 1/3, 8/5),
               u=(4/15, 1/3, 2/5).

The second uses

    Network B, E=+: d=(13/30, 1/6, 2/5),
               u=(26/15, 1/6, 1/10).
    Network B, E=−: d=(7/60, 1/12, 9/5),
               u=(7/15, 1/12, 9/20).

In both networks, summing u and d over nu gives exactly (2,1) at E=+ and (1,2) at E=−. The E transitions are also the same. Therefore the continuous-time generator on the observed states (E,M) is exactly the same in A and B. This equality is stronger than matching a steady mean or an impulse response: every finite-dimensional distribution of the observed input/output trajectories is identical.

Stationary memory and prediction
Write q_e=P(M=1|E=e) in stationarity. The stationary master equations are

    (lambda+R_+) q_+ - lambda q_- = u_+,
    -lambda q_+ + (lambda+R_-) q_- = u_-,

where R_e=u_e+d_e. At lambda=1 and R_+=R_-=3, these give q_+=3/5 and q_-=2/5. The stationary joint law, in state order (+,0),(+,1),(−,0),(−,1), is

    p = (1/5, 3/10, 3/10, 1/5).

Both marginals are uniform. M therefore stores H(M)=ln 2 nats (one bit). The best decoder is M=1 -> predict E=+ and M=0 -> predict E=−. Current accuracy is 3/5.

The symmetric telegraph semigroup gives E[E(t+tau)|E(t)]=exp(-2 lambda tau) E(t). At stationarity the correlation of the signed variables is c=1/5. Since M and E have uniform marginals, their joint binary law is determined by the correlation. For every finite forecast horizon tau>=0,

    accuracy(tau) = 1/2 [1 + (1/5) exp(-2 tau)],
    error(tau)    = 1/2 [1 - (1/5) exp(-2 tau)],
    I(M_t; E_{t+tau}) = ln 2 - h(error(tau)),

where h(x)=-x ln x-(1-x)ln(1-x). In particular, the prediction accuracy and predictive information are identical in networks A and B for every tau, not only at one selected horizon.

Channel-resolved work
Let p_{e0},p_{e1} be the stationary probabilities and define the net forward current in channel nu at fixed input e by

    J_{nu,e} = p_{e0} u_{nu,e} - p_{e1} d_{nu,e}.

The net fuel-hydrolysis current is J_fuel=sum_e(J_{1,e}-J_{3,e}); channel 2 is fuel-free and channel 3 runs with the opposite stoichiometry. Local detailed balance gives the exact steady chemical power

    beta P_chem = ln(4) J_fuel.

For A, the channel currents (nu=1,2,3) are

    E=+: (23/120, -1/24, -1/20),
    E=−: (1/15,    1/30, -1/5),

so J_fuel=61/120 and P_A=(61/120) kBT ln 4 per time unit. For B they are

    E=+: (13/60, -1/60, -1/10),
    E=−: (7/60,   1/120, -9/40),

so J_fuel=79/120 and P_B=(79/120) kBT ln 4 per time unit. Hence

    P_B-P_A = (3/20) kBT ln 4 per time unit > 0.

The full channel-resolved steady entropy production equals beta P_chem: the environment edges have zero affinity, and the stationary system-state entropy terms cancel when summed over all channels. The executable record checks this equality numerically as well as the exact rational currents. The difference is not hidden in an uncounted preparation/reset protocol; it occurs continuously in the autonomous sensing network.

Sharp rate-allocation interval for this example
For either input state, write d_e(theta)=d_e(A)+theta(1,-5,4). Since

    (1,-5,4) dot (1,1,1) = 0,
    (1,-5,4) dot (4,1,1/4) = 0,

the aggregate down and up rates remain fixed. The common strictly positive feasible interval is -1/20 < theta < 1/15. Along it,

    J_fuel(theta)=61/120+3 theta,
    beta P_chem(theta)=ln(4)(61/120+3 theta).

Thus the sharp closure of the work-rate range compatible with this entire observed process is

    (43/120) kBT ln 4 <= P_chem <= (17/24) kBT ln 4

per time unit. The endpoint allocations turn off one pathway under one catalyst state; they are attainable if a zero rate means that the corresponding channel is structurally gated off in that state. If the model class requires every listed channel to remain strictly positive, the interval is open and the same numbers are the infimum and supremum. The A and B points lie strictly inside it.

General identifiability statement supported by the construction
For an observed two-state transition with channel rates u_nu=r_nu d_nu, a state-only record determines only

    D=sum_nu d_nu,       U=sum_nu r_nu d_nu.

The channel-resolved fuel power also depends on

    beta P = sum_nu (p_0 r_nu d_nu - p_1 d_nu) ln r_nu,

which is not determined by those two sums when there are at least three distinct channel ratios and the feasible allocation set has nonzero dimension. A perturbation v with v dot 1=0 and v dot r=0 leaves the complete observed generator unchanged. Its work derivative is

    d(beta P)/dtheta = sum_nu v_nu (p_0 r_nu - p_1) ln r_nu,

which is generically nonzero. The example supplies an explicit positive v and two realizations, so this is an attained non-identifiability result, not merely a dimension count.

What this does and does not show
The result rules out a universal exact function that maps only the environmental law, observed response speed, causal memory, and predictive accuracy to total chemical work across channel-resolved CRNs. It does not rule out lower bounds under a declared microscopic boundary, known reaction stoichiometries, known chemical affinities, and measured channel fluxes. For a single specified pathway per edge, those additional details can make the work identifiable. Conversely, adding a disconnected fuel-consuming cycle can raise whole-cell work without changing the sensor output at all; that familiar construction is weaker than the matched parallel-path example above and is not used as the main evidence.

Preparation, observation, and reset accounting
This is a steady-state autonomous experiment rather than a one-shot protocol. No sensor is prepared by a timed intervention: the joint distribution is generated by the fixed rates above. The input generator starts in its stationary uniform distribution. The measured record is the M state (for example, a reporter of active/inactive signaling protein); its observation energy is outside this minimal chemical state model, so no physical fluorescence detector cost is claimed to be zero. The predictor uses M_t to label E_{t+tau}; it does not mutate M or feed back on E. No memory reset is performed between forecasts in the stationary regime. If an experimental protocol extracts a persistent record or resets the reporter, that readout and reset are additional reactions and work terms; they must be matched across A and B. Since the underlying observed paths are identical, identical readout/reset modules can be appended to both networks without removing their ongoing power difference.

Biological consequence and discriminating experiment
The model predicts that matching only a cell's ligand-to-reporter response kernel, single-cell state occupancy, or forecast score does not identify its ATP budget. An enzyme/catalyst perturbation could redistribute flux among parallel phosphorylation-like routes while leaving their summed state-transition rates unchanged. A reporter-only assay would then see the same adaptation and predictive performance, while metabolite turnover differs. A discriminating experiment would drive matched cells with the same binary ligand telegraph, tune their reporter transition rates and conditional response time to match, and measure ATP/ADP turnover (ideally with pathway-specific isotope or flux readouts) alongside single-cell reporter trajectories. This is a model-derived prediction for an engineered or identified pathway, not evidence that natural chemotaxis uses the specific three-channel construction.

The biological setting is motivated by primary modeling of bacterial chemotaxis: Barato, Hartich & Seifert model a stochastic receptor signal driving a downstream phosphorylation/dephosphorylation cycle with ATP-dependent reverse reactions required by thermodynamic consistency (their Eqs. 22–23). Lan et al. report a feedback-network energy-speed-accuracy trade-off and measurements in E. coli. Neither source validates the parallel-channel pair here.

Alternative attempts and failure boundary
- Quasistatic allosteric coupling of E and M gives a clean reversible memory benchmark, but the coupling back-acts on E or requires explicit signal work when the input is held exogenous. It also invokes externally timed coupling/removal. It was dropped as the main construction after the fixed-input/autonomous requirement was sharpened.
- A standalone ATP-consuming futile cycle would prove only that whole-cell ATP turnover can be unrelated to sensor output. The parallel-channel example keeps the hidden work inside the sensor transition itself, so it preserves the entire observed (E,M) generator and still changes fuel flux.
- The result cannot support a claim that “more prediction requires more ATP.” In this example, prediction and memory are held constant while ATP work changes; other feasible reaction networks may have different accuracy/work curves. Any useful law must state the channel-level resource boundary, fuel affinities, response-time constraint, environmental law, and readout/reset task.
