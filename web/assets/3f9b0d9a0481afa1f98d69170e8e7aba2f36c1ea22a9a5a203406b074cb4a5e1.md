# Quantitative obstructions for Paneth-niche repair

## 1. Homeostatic event-budget bound

Let one crypt have (P) mature Paneth cells in a stationary homeostatic regime. Let the mean residence time of a Paneth cell be \(\tau_P\); under a steady replacement model the total production of mature Paneth cells is (B=P/\tau_P) per day. Let (D) be the number of ISC divisions per crypt per day at positions where the measured age-selective mitochondrial partition applies. If a fraction (a=0.15) of those divisions produce an old-mitochondria-enriched daughter, then the candidate event flux is (A=aD).

Suppose each candidate daughter produces, in expectation, (pc) mature Paneth cells, where (p\in[0,1]) is its fate probability and (c\geq0) is expected PC output conditional on that fate. Since those cells are part of all mature Paneth births,

\[
  aDpc \leq B = P/\tau_P.
\]

For \(D,c,\tau_P>0\), this gives

\[
  p \leq P/(aDc\tau_P).
\]

If the right side is below one, deterministic commitment (p=1) is impossible in this model. If all new PCs came through this route, equality is required; if other routes also contribute, the inequality can be strict. If daughter progeny amplify before PC fate, their expected amplification belongs in (c). The relationship is a mass-balance theorem conditional on the stated renewal model, not a new theorem about real crypts.

### What is supplied and what is missing

Andersson et al. report (a\approx0.15) for observed age-selective divisions, only at positions +3/+4; they report an ISC-mitochondrial-age-enriched fraction of approximately 9% and typically 1–2 such cells per crypt. The paper explicitly says deterministic PC differentiation would overpredict PC turnover and concludes that the mitochondria-age state biases, rather than fixes, fate.

The paper does not provide a joint estimate of (D), (P), the residence-time distribution, and (c) for the same crypt/animal/age/treatment condition. In particular, a quoted 57-day reporter-inheritance delay from Ireland et al. is not automatically an exponential mean residence time. Historical turnover estimates differ by method; substituting one into the inequality without a residence model would manufacture a numeric bound. The experimentally decisive quantities are the position-specific division flux (D), the per-crypt Paneth count (P), the residence-time distribution, and the lineage-resolved output (c).

## 2. Crypt-coverage bound from a pooled renewal fraction

For a collection of equal-sized crypts, write (R_i\in[0,1]) for the fraction of mature PCs in crypt (i) that are renewed over the label window, and let (q=\mathbb E R_i). Define (\pi=\Pr(R_i\geq\tau)), where \(\tau\in(0,1)\) is an independently calibrated renewal threshold needed to cross a local functional criterion.

On the event (R_i\geq\tau), (R_i\geq\tau), so (q\geq\pi\tau), giving

\[
 \pi \leq \min(1,q/\tau).
\]

Also (R_i\leq1) on the passing event and (R_i<\tau) on the failing event. Thus (q\leq\pi+(1-\pi)\tau) up to an arbitrarily small strict-boundary term, giving the sharp lower bound in the closure,

\[
 \pi \geq \max(0,(q-\tau)/(1-\tau)).
\]

The bounds are sharp in the closure: distributions supported near \((0,\tau)\) and \((1)\) can approach the lower bound; mass at \((0)\) and \((\tau)\) can approach the upper bound. When the threshold counts equality as passing, some endpoints are infima/suprema rather than attained values. If crypts have unequal Paneth counts, a cell-weighted bulk fraction does not equal \(q\) under equal crypt weighting; crypt-level counts or explicit weights are then required.

At (q=\tau=1/2), these data alone do not guarantee a positive fraction of crypts strictly above threshold; the attainable coverage can range from arbitrarily close to zero to one depending on how equality is handled. The synthetic exact witness in [counterexamples/aggregate_alias.md](../counterexamples/aggregate_alias.md) avoids using this bound as a biological estimate.

## 3. EdU fraction does not identify a heterogeneous replacement rate

Assume a Paneth cell is labeled as renewed if at least one replacement event occurs in a window of length (T), and each crypt has a constant Poisson replacement hazard \(\lambda_i\). Conditional on \(\lambda_i\), the EdU-positive fraction is

\[
  f_i(T)=1-\exp(-\lambda_iT).
\]

Across heterogeneous crypts, a pooled label fraction is

\[
  \bar f(T)=\mathbb E[1-\exp(-\lambda_iT)].
\]

This is one value of the Laplace transform of the rate distribution, not its mean or distribution. Since (1-e^{-x}) is concave, Jensen gives the conditional lower bound

\[
  \mathbb E[\lambda_i] \geq -T^{-1}\log(1-\bar f(T)),
\]

but not a point estimate. The run script gives two exact synthetic distributions with the same 21-day pooled fraction, \(\bar f=0.5\), and mean hazards 0.033007/day versus 0.051193/day. EdU uptake/detection, label dilution, cell loss, and non-stationarity would add further parameters outside this minimal model.

## Scope

The functional threshold (\tau), normalized old/new Notum levels, no-cross-crypt coupling, and per-crypt reporter model are explicitly hypothetical until measured. Paneth-derived Notum is secreted and crypt biology is spatially coupled; the local-threshold model is a testable abstraction, not an assertion that Notum acts only within one crypt. No clinical or organism-wide rejuvenation conclusion follows.
