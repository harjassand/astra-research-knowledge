# Exact aggregate alias under crypt heterogeneity

This witness checks an information boundary, not a fit to the published mouse data.

## Construction

Take 100 equal-sized crypts with 8 Paneth cells each. Normalize old-cell Notum output to 1 and newly made-cell output to 0.1. A crypt passes the illustrative local threshold if its mean normalized Notum is at most 0.55. This is equivalent to requiring at least half of its Paneth cells to be renewed, because

\[
  N_i=(1-R_i)\cdot1+R_i\cdot0.1=1-0.9R_i,
  \qquad N_i\leq0.55\iff R_i\geq0.5.
\]

### Assignment A: diffuse replacement

Every crypt has (R_i=1/2). Half the cells are EdU-positive/new in each crypt. Every crypt is exactly at the threshold, so all 100 pass.

### Assignment B: spatially clustered replacement

Fifty crypts have (R_i=1), and fifty have (R_i=0). The first 50 pass and the second 50 fail.

## Identical pooled readouts

Both assignments have exactly the same:

- pooled EdU-positive Paneth fraction: 0.5;
- total Paneth count;
- global mean normalized Notum: 0.55;
- mean Notum among EdU-positive PCs: 0.1;
- mean Notum among EdU-negative PCs: 1.

Yet functional crypt coverage is 100% versus 50%. If damage is concentrated in the unreplaced crypts, tissue-level recovery can differ even further. A bulk label, bulk qPCR, or sorted EdU-positive/negative qPCR cannot distinguish the two assignments. Per-crypt co-registration is needed to identify distribution and coverage.

The exact arithmetic is generated by [crypt_repair_identifiability.py](../models/crypt_repair_identifiability.py), with output in [crypt_repair_identifiability_run.json](../models/crypt_repair_identifiability_run.json). The fitted threshold and normalized signals are synthetic; no source figure is being digitized or treated as having these values.
