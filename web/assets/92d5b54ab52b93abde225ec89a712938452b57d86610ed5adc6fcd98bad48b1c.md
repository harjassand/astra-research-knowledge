# Cycle 3: can lineage assays resolve a higher-order ecDNA resistance signature?

## Decision

**Conditional GO for a preclinical, event-resolved assay; NO-GO for inferring the whole-tumor signature from bulk ctDNA, imaging totals, or one spatially unqualified biopsy.** A paired mother-to-daughter readout can identify a conditional ecDNA partition kernel and a predeclared tail probability. A bulk readout can miss that distinction exactly. Even a well-powered paired assay does not by itself establish resistance: the tail event must be linked to viable resistant outgrowth, and its per-division frequency must be combined with a measured division rate and an explicit spatial-coverage model before it can enter the risk-vs-duration (`risk-vs-T`) certificate.

The practicable version below is a cell-line/organoid mechanism assay. It is not a clinical ctDNA assay: ecTag signal is lost during metaphase, daughter identity and both daughter copy states are required, and ordinary patient tissue/plasma samples do not preserve mitotic mother–daughter links.

## An observable kernel that isolates the higher-order difference

Condition on a mother cell with four ecDNA copies before replication and eight copies available for segregation at mitosis. This convention matches the ecDNA segregation model in which a mother with `N` copies partitions `2N` copies between daughters. Let `X` be the number inherited by one daughter; the other receives `8-X`. Use the daughter-order-invariant tail statistic

`Y = 1{max(X, 8-X) >= 6}`.

Here are two symmetric, integer-valued candidate kernels. They are deliberately constructed alternatives for assay design, **not** claims that either is an observed tumor law.

| Kernel | Partition law for `X` | `E[X]` | `Var(X)` | `E[(X-4)^4]` | `κ4` | `P(Y=1)` |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A, moderate asymmetry | `P(2)=1/4, P(4)=1/2, P(6)=1/4` | 4 | 2 | 8 | -4 | 1/2 |
| B, rare extreme partition | `P(0)=1/16, P(4)=14/16, P(8)=1/16` | 4 | 2 | 32 | 20 | 1/8 |

Both have exactly the same first two moments, and both match the mean and variance of the independent-copy random-partition reference `Binomial(8, 1/2)`. The reference has `P(Y=1)=74/256≈0.2891` and `κ4=-1`. Kernels A and B differ by 24 in fourth cumulant and by a factor of four in the predeclared tail probability. One independent Bernoulli trial is **one mother mitosis**, not two daughter cells; daughters in a pair are dependent because their copy counts sum to eight.

For variable mother copy number, estimate `K_m(x)=P(X=x | mother count m, cell state, treatment, spatial stratum)` or predeclare an analogous fraction threshold. Do not pool different mother copy counts or cell-cycle states and then call the mixture's kurtosis a partition signature. A change in the distribution of mothers alone can change pooled higher moments.

## Observation and error model

For each attempted mother track `i`, record:

- `O_i=1` only if the mother state, one mitosis, both daughters, and both daughter copy measurements are linked with an unambiguous lineage ID;
- the true partition event `Y_i` and observed call `Y_i*`;
- whether the event is censored, leaves the imaging field, dies before post-mitotic scoring, or spans an unresolved frame gap;
- well/culture, mother copy number, cell-cycle state, drug exposure, and spatial region.

On complete pairs, use a calibrated event-call sensitivity `s=P(Y*=1|Y=1)` and false-tail rate `f=P(Y*=1|Y=0)`. The observed tail-call probability under true tail frequency `p` is

`q = f + (s-f)p`.

For the explicit operating scenario `s=0.90, f=0.01`—acceptance targets for this design, **not literature-reported performance**—the calls become `q_B=0.12125` and `q_A=0.455`. Estimate `s,f` from blinded positive/negative partition standards and replicate DNA-FISH/copy assays. If `s=f`, the observed calls contain no information about `p`; if their uncertainty is ignored, the nominal confidence bound is invalid. Copy-level dropout, merged FISH foci, ecDNA clustering, and an incorrect mother count must be represented in the calibrated confusion matrix rather than treated as ordinary binomial sampling.

Let `N` be all initiated mother tracks, `n` complete and scorable mitoses, `c=N-n` missing/censored outcomes, and `y` observed tail calls. Under missing completely at random conditional on recorded covariates, analyze the `n` complete events and inflate enrollment for the completion rate. Without that assumption, even perfect calls only give the deterministic partial-identification interval

`y/N <= p <= (y+c)/N`.

For the noisy assay, construct a binomial confidence interval `[q_L,q_U]` on complete calls and invert it as

`p in [max(0,(q_L-f)/(s-f)), min(1,(q_U-f)/(s-f))]`,

then widen for unknown outcomes and calibration uncertainty. A high or copy-number-dependent censoring rate cannot be repaired by simply imaging more cells. If treatment, high-copy daughters, or cell size predicts attrition, report the worst-case interval or use a justified censoring model with a sensitivity analysis.

### Cadence and missed divisions

Under an exponential single-lineage division hazard `lambda`, a frame gap `Delta` contains two or more divisions with probability

`P(N_Delta >= 2) = 1 - exp(-lambda Delta)(1+lambda Delta)`.

Keeping that probability below 1% requires `lambda Delta <= 0.149`, or `Delta <= 0.215 tau_d` for `tau_d=ln(2)/lambda`. At a 24-hour doubling time, this is `Delta<=5.2 h`; 15-minute sampling gives `P(N_Delta>=2)≈2.6e-5` and 30-minute sampling gives `≈1.03e-4` under this model. These are cadence calculations, not protection against out-of-plane movement, segmentation errors, or marker dropout. Histone/chromatin and cell-boundary tracking should establish the division; ecDNA copy should be read after daughters re-enter a countable state.

The ecTag primary study reports that its ecDNA fluorescence is diluted/not visible during metaphase and reappears after mitosis, so ecTag alone does not directly read the partition at the instant of segregation. It also notes a roughly 48-hour live-imaging window in that setup because of laser-associated cell stress. Use a stable mitotic/lineage marker and post-mitotic daughter scoring, with breakpoint DNA-FISH or an independently calibrated single-cell copy assay. A PGCC ovarian-cancer study provides a temporal feasibility precedent: H2B/tubulin/FUCCI-labelled Hey cells and PGCCs were imaged every 15 minutes with a 10x objective for up to one week, at 1,000 plated cells per well. That is 672 frames per continuously imaged position for a full week, before image processing and daughter validation. The two studies use different models and do not establish that ecTag can itself support a week-long exposure without phototoxicity.

### Spatial inclusion and the whole-tumor limit

Suppose a fraction `c_sp` of viable tumor cell mass is covered by a known, mass-weighted sampling frame and the covered part has one-sided tail bound `p_U`. Without assumptions on the unobserved part, the sharp whole-tumor bound is only

`p_global <= c_sp p_U + (1-c_sp)`.

For example, even `p_U=0.02` is compatible with `p_global<=0.804` if only 20% of tumor mass has known coverage. If a lesion/region has zero or unknown inclusion probability, no nontrivial uniform global bound follows: place all high-tail or resistant founders in an unsampled refuge with negligible shedding. For `J` equal regions, one refuge, `b` regions sampled uniformly, `m` cells measured in a hit region, and per-cell event detection `eta`, the miss probability is

`P_miss = 1 - (b/J)[1-(1-eta p_refuge)^m]`.

It approaches one when the refuge's inclusion probability is allowed to approach zero. This is consistent with primary spatial-barcode work showing patchy clone distributions, location-sensitive biopsy recovery, and model-dependent cfDNA shedding; it is not evidence for a universal coverage floor.

## Exact detection limits and assay burden

The following numbers are exact enumerations for independent mother mitoses, a single predeclared tail endpoint, and the indicated error model. They are optimistic if there is well-level clustering, multiple testing, copy-number stratification, or uncertain assay calibration.

1. **Frozen reference, one-sample test.** If kernel B is trained independently and frozen as the null, an exact one-sided binomial test under perfect classification first reaches size `<=0.05` and power `>=0.80` at `n=10`, rejecting at `Y>=4` (`alpha=0.02746`, power `0.82813`). At `s=0.90,f=0.01`, use `q_B=0.12125,q_A=0.455`; `n=11`, again rejecting at `Y*>=4`, has `alpha=0.03529` and power `0.81789`.
2. **Two kernels estimated in a matched experiment.** If the control and treatment probabilities are both unknown, a one-sided Fisher exact comparison needs `23` complete pairs per arm under perfect calls (`alpha=0.01747`, power `0.81654`). With `s=0.90,f=0.01`, exact enumeration gives `26` complete pairs per arm (`alpha=0.02045`, power `0.80172`). These sample sizes test the large `0.375` true tail-probability contrast, not subtle biological shifts.
3. **Zero-call upper bound.** With `n` complete pairs and zero observed tail calls, a one-sided 95% exact upper limit is `q_U=1-0.05^(1/n)`, then `p_U=(q_U-f)/(s-f)` clipped to `[0,1]`. For `s=.90,f=.01`, this gives `p_U≈0.111` at `n=26` and `p_U≈0.0219` at `n=100`. It is conditional on event-independent missingness, calibrated `s,f`, and the sampled stratum.

At a 70% complete-event yield, the exact binomial completion calculation requires 44 initiated tracks per arm to have at least 26 complete pairs with probability at least 95%; for a practical target of 100 complete pairs it requires 156 initiated tracks per arm with that assurance. A 70% yield is a planning scenario: three independent 10%-dropout measurements for mother and two daughters alone give `0.9^3=0.729` completion before field loss or segmentation failure. Under informative missingness, these enrollment numbers give no population guarantee.

**Recommended measurement budget:** target 100 complete mother-plus-two-daughter events per arm, spread across at least six independent wells/cultures and stratified by mother copy number/state; under a 70% completion planning yield, start about 156 tracks per arm. Across two arms this is 200 scored mother divisions, 400 daughter observations, and about 312 initiated tracks. It is intentionally above the 26-per-arm minimum to estimate strata, diagnose overdispersion, and reserve a held-out comparison. With 100 events per arm spread across six wells, a rough cluster design effect is `1+(m-1)rho`; at about 17 events/well and pilot ICC `rho=0.05`, `n_eff≈100/[1+16(0.05)]≈55` per arm. If the ICC is larger, power must be recalculated from independent-well variation. Do not count hundreds of daughter cells from one well as hundreds of independent biological replicates.

This scale is plausible for a cell assay, not proven for every model. The ecDNA inheritance primary study used Aurora-B immunofluorescence plus sequence-specific daughter-cell DNA-FISH and reported 206 treated and 177 control cell pairs in one comparison. That demonstrates pair scoring at the hundreds scale; it does not establish our tail-call sensitivity or validate the constructed kernels. Monetary cost depends on the imaging/FISH platform and is not inferred here; the auditable burden is tracks, frames, wells, fixed-pair assays, and calibration controls.

## What enters the risk-vs-T program—and what does not

The paired assay estimates `p_tail = P(Y=1 | mother state, dose, region)`, not a resistant-founder source rate by itself. For a tracked controlled state `i`, a candidate **per-cell** source coefficient is

`kappa_i(t) = lambda_i(t) * p_tail,i(t) * f_res,i(t)`,

where `lambda_i` is the division hazard and `f_res,i=P(a tail event yields an independently viable, treatment-resistant escaping lineage | tail event)`. Simultaneous one-sided upper bounds give the conservative plug-in `kappa_i^U=lambda_i^U p_tail,i^U f_res,i^U` for the source/escape term of the existing risk-vs-T certificate. The model's bookkeeping must match the biology: if the high-copy daughter is counted as an escaped founder, do not also count it as an ordinary tracked birth in the drift matrix. A per-mitosis partition measurement estimates none of birth/death separately, `lambda`, post-pulse establishment, or spatial coverage.

For a constant division hazard and a fixed, externally specified cell-time exposure `E`, if `D` divisions are observed with a valid Poisson count model, the exact one-sided upper confidence limit is

`lambda_U = chi2_quantile(1-alpha, 2(D+1))/(2E)`;

for `D=0`, this is `-log(alpha)/E`, so showing `lambda < lambda_star` after zero events requires `E >= -log(alpha)/lambda_star`. This is exact for fixed exposure under the stated model; random at-risk exposure altered by the same divisions requires an event-time/counting-process likelihood, not naive Garwood substitution. Similarly, `p_tail` and `f_res` need simultaneous one-sided binomial intervals; split the error budget across the upper bounds used in `kappa_U`. With zero resistant outgrowth among `n_r` fully followed tail daughters and perfect detection, `f_res,U=1-alpha^(1/n_r)`. With censored or untracked descendants, count every unresolved tail daughter as resistant for the worst-case upper bound or explicitly model censoring.

The exact `n=26` comparison is conditional on the large prespecified alternative `p_A-p_B=0.375` (observed-call gap `0.333`). If the two kernel rates, assay sensitivity, or source-to-resistance probability are completely unknown and no minimum effect size/lower sensitivity is supplied, there is no finite sample size with uniform power: the alternatives can be arbitrarily close, or sensitivity can approach zero. This is a structural no-go, not a reason to silently extrapolate the illustrative sample size.

If `f_res` is unmeasured, the safe value is `f_res^U=1`, which can leave the certificate useless. To estimate it, separately culture/track high-tail and non-tail daughter lineages through the same drug challenge and test durable outgrowth. If none of `n_r` tail daughters establishes and calls are perfect, its one-sided 95% upper probability is `1-0.05^(1/n_r)`; at `n_r=20` that is still about 0.139. If high-tail daughters cannot be isolated or followed, claim only an ecDNA inheritance kernel, not a resistant-founder signature.

With no event-resolved data, the observation law has an exact counterexample. In either kernel, every division yields two daughters and conserves all eight ecDNA copies. Across each matched mother, the bulk cell-count increment is `+1` and total ecDNA content remains eight; the daughter mean and variance are both 4 and 2. Therefore aggregate cell burden, total ecDNA molecules, and assays reporting only these first two moments are identical under A and B at that time. A clone-specific high-tail event could later change selection, but then endpoint plasma odds also depend on clone-specific release and survival. A sufficiently rich **cross-sectional single-cell** assay can see the different marginal fourth moment, but without mother–daughter linkage it cannot identify that the difference arose through the partition kernel rather than pre-existing cell-state mixtures, differential death/selection, or changing mother-state composition. The transition is the target, so event resolution is required.

## Preregistered discriminator and go/no-go

Use a baseline/sham and a treatment-exposed split of the same ecDNA-positive, barcoded clone. Match mother cells on pre-replication ecDNA count (start with the `N=4` stratum), cell-cycle state, imaging window, and culture conditions. Record the mother’s interphase ecTag signal before mitosis, track with H2B/chromatin plus cell boundaries at cadence chosen from measured cell-cycle times, then score both daughters after post-mitotic signal recovery. Fix the same tracked coordinates for breakpoint DNA-FISH and calibrate the pre-mitotic live signal and both daughter copy calls. If hubs prevent reliable mother copy stratification, do not infer the conditional kernel by conditioning on daughter sums; stop or replace the assay with a validated copy-number readout. Include fixed paired-mitosis controls. Fit the kernel only in training wells. Freeze `s,f`, strata, the tail threshold, and the predicted treatment effect before opening held-out wells.

**Held-out sample-size discriminator:** at minimum, collect 26 complete pairs per arm and compare observed tail calls with the one-sided Fisher exact test at 5%; under the stated `s=.90,f=.01` operating point, its power is 80.2% for B-to-A (`.12125` to `.455` observed rate). With 70% independent completion, initiate 44 tracks per arm for a 95% chance of meeting that minimum. The recommended 100 complete pairs per arm are for robustness/stratification, not needed to obtain the quoted idealized power. If B is an independently frozen null, the 11-pair one-sample threshold test is a separate, cheaper screening option, not a substitute for a matched two-arm holdout.

**Prediction:** after matching mother count and state, treatment shifts held-out tail frequency from the B-like region toward the A-like region while mother-conditioned daughter mean/variance remain compatible within prespecified, calibration-based equivalence margins. **Falsifier:** the held-out exact test fails and its error-corrected confidence bound excludes the prespecified minimum tail shift; or, after separately following daughter lineages under equal challenge, high-tail daughters do not have greater durable resistant outgrowth. The latter falsifies the resistance interpretation even if the partition-kernel difference is real.

Proceed to model integration only if (i) mother and both daughters are linked in at least 90% of initiated tracks or missingness is shown conditionally noninformative and worst-case bounds retain separation; (ii) the calibrated tail classifier meets `s>=0.90,f<=0.01` with calibration uncertainty included; (iii) at least 100 complete events per arm are obtained across independent wells, with the held-out analysis still achieving its predeclared power after the estimated well ICC; (iv) mother copy number and division cadence are observed; and (v) the daughter-to-resistance link and its uncertainty are measured. A failure at (i)–(iv) is **NO-GO for identifying the conditional kernel**. A failure only at (v) is **NO-GO for calling it a resistance-source signal**, although an inheritance result may remain valid.

For whole-tumor or clinical risk bounds, add spatially explicit strata with nonzero, quantified inclusion probabilities and a viable-burden bound. If a low-shedding refuge can have zero sampling probability, set the global founder/source uncertainty to `UNKNOWN` and do not optimize `T` using this measurement.
