UNRESOLVED. No general hardness reduction or field-breaking result is claimed.

1. EXACT LEGAL INTERFACE

Input F is an explicit n-by-n matrix over Q(i), of total binary length L.
There are two physical modes u_i,d_i per site. The k-pair sector has amplitudes
(-1)^{k(k-1)/2} det F[I,J], for |I|=|J|=k in fixed up-then-down Fock order.
Every site is hard projected: a surviving occupation is 0,u,or d, never ud.
Thus the unnormalized canonical squared norm is

 c_k(F)=sum_{I cap J=empty, |I|=|J|=k} |det F[I,J]|^2.             (1)

Write Z_F(z)=sum_k c_k(F) z^k; the grandcanonical target here is Z_F(1).
An occupation-prefix counter restricts each site to a supplied subset of
{0,u,d}; all these are genuine physical local constraints. It reports every
c_k of that restriction. An exact-zero canonical sector returns ZERO and
cannot produce a normalized sample. No ground-state acquisition, pair-matrix
learning, Hamiltonian promise, balancing oracle or large filter overlap is
included in the input. Precision is charged in rational bit operations.

Diagonal F_ii has no effect on (1). However, deleting it before a rank
factorization can increase rank: J has rank one, while J-I has rank n for
n>1. The rank theorem is about the supplied F (or an explicitly constructed
diagonal completion), and the implementation factors the supplied F.

2. COMMON DETERMINANT AND EXTERIOR-ALGEBRA LIFT

Let X=diag(x_i), Y=diag(y_i), and star mean Hermitian transpose. Then

 D_F(x,y)=det [[X,F],[-F*,Y]]
         =sum_{|I|=|J|} |det F[I,J]|^2
            product_{i notin I} x_i product_{j notin J} y_j.      (2)

Proof: select the principal diagonal variables used by a determinant term.
The remaining principal submatrix has the form [[0,A],[-A*,0]], where
A=F[I,J]. It is singular unless |I|=|J|; at common size k the block-column
exchange sign (-1)^k cancels the k lower-left minus signs and its determinant
is det(A)det(A*)=|det A|^2. Principal diagonal cofactor signs are positive.
This argument proves the polynomial identity, including singular inputs.

On each site use the linear functional

 T_i(1)=0, T_i(x_i)=1, T_i(y_i)=z, T_i(x_i y_i)=1.

The four cases mean forbidden double occupation, down, up, and empty.
Consequently product_i T_i applied to (2) gives Z_F(z). Disallowed local
prefix cases are simply assigned value zero.

For an explicit contraction, use four Grassmann generators per site, ordered
(bar u_i,u_i,bar d_i,d_i), and define A_i=bar u_i u_i, B_i=bar d_i d_i.
Generators anticommute and square to zero. Integration means extraction of
the coefficient of the complete ordered product product_i A_i B_i, normalized
so integral exp(sum bar psi_a M_ab psi_b)=det M. Expanding that exponential
selects every bar/psi exactly once; the ordering sign is the determinant sign.

The local hard factor is

 P_i=A_i+z B_i+A_i B_i.                                         (3)

Multiply (3) by the off-diagonal chiral exponential. A directed F_ij supplies
two even factors (1+F_ij bar u_i d_j)(1-conj(F_ij) bar d_j u_i).
The full coefficient integral is Z_F. All factors are even, so their order
can be changed without sign; internal generator-order signs are retained.
Restricting P_i to its empty, up or down terms implements any physical prefix.

3. EXACT LOW-RANK ALGORITHM

Acquire F=UV, U n-by-r and V r-by-n, by exact Gaussian elimination. Choosing
the pivot columns of F as U and the first r rows of its RREF as V gives this
identity. Their entries are ratios of input minors, hence have polynomial
bit length. The code verifies UV=F exactly. Factor acquisition is polynomial
bit time and finds r=rank(F); a supplied nonminimal factorization also works.

Set M0=diag(X,Y), C=[[U,0],[0,-V*]], D=[[0,V],[U*,0]]. The augmented identity

 det [[M0,C],[-D,I_(2r)]]=det(M0+CD)=D_F(x,y)                    (4)

uses only the invertible constant lower-right identity. It is a polynomial
identity and never divides by x_i, y_i or a projected norm.

Introduce 2r auxiliary modes, ordered first all (bar xi1_a,xi1_a), then all
(bar xi2_b,xi2_b). There are exactly 4r auxiliary generators. In the exponent
of (4), the physical-auxiliary terms at site i are

 bar u_i U_ia xi1_a,       -bar d_i conj(V_ai) xi2_a,
 -bar xi1_a V_ai d_i,      -bar xi2_a conj(U_ia) u_i.

There are no interactions between different physical sites. Integrating the
four physical generators at a site against (3) therefore gives the even
auxiliary polynomial

 q_i=1 + z sum_{a,b} U_ia conj(U_ib) bar xi2_b xi1_a
         - sum_{a,b} V_ai conj(V_bi) bar xi1_a xi2_b.            (5)

For empty occupation the A_i B_i term directly saturates the site. For up,
zB_i is completed by the two u couplings; sorting yields the positive
bar-xi2/xi1 expression in (5). For down, A_i is completed by the two d
couplings and gives its minus sign. These are all possible local cases.
In increasing auxiliary-mask order the coefficient of BOTH nonconstant
terms is negative: the up term bar xi2_b xi1_a must first be reversed.
This detail is explicit in the implementation and exact complex tests.

It follows from (4), site integration and the determinant identity that

 Z_F(z)=integral_aux exp(sum_a bar xi1_a xi1_a
                             +sum_b bar xi2_b xi2_b) product_i q_i. (6)

Every forbidden prefix case deletes its corresponding constant/up/down
summand of q_i. This is an exact physical-prefix formula, not a restriction
only on the paired Gram indices.

Represent a polynomial by (generator bit mask, z degree) -> rational-complex
coefficient. To multiply masks a,b, discard a&b!=0; otherwise multiply their
coefficients and use (-1)^(number of generator inversions) on mask a|b.
Starting from 1, multiply q_1,...,q_n. Each q_i has at most 1+2r^2 terms.
Every up term contains a distinct xi1_a, so its z degree is at most r by
nilpotence. The message has at most (r+1)2^(4r) keys.

For the final auxiliary integral, an auxiliary mode's two-generator mask
must be either 00 or 11: a missing pair is supplied by its diagonal
exponential; a singly present generator cannot be completed. Complete mode
pairs have even degree, hence completion adds no sign. Sum the coefficients
of all admissible masks separately by degree. Individual coefficients can
be complex; only their total is real and nonnegative. Do not test each
individual mask coefficient for reality.

This proves correctness of counts_factors in revisions/lowrank_bcs.py. Its
arithmetic bound is O(n(r+1)(1+2r^2)16^r), plus polynomial acquisition. Its
storage is O((r+1)16^r) rational-complex words. For r=0 the same formula gives
only the empty state; degrees above min(r,floor(n/2)) are exact zero.

Bit bound: acquired U,V entries have polynomial bit size. Each input factor
entry and its conjugate is used at most once in the augmented expansion, or
at most twice in (5). A common denominator dividing the squared product of
all factor-entry denominators works for every monomial and every intermediate
message. Each full monomial has polynomially many factors, and the number
of precollected monomials is at most (1+2r^2)^n times the auxiliary completion
choices. The logarithm of this number is polynomial in n,r. Numerator and
denominator lengths therefore remain polynomial in n,L,r. This establishes
the stated bit bound, including ill-conditioned rational input.

In particular rank(F)<=C log n for any fixed C is an F-only exact polynomial
counting/prefix/sampling class. The parameter is acquired, not promised by
an unavailable decomposition. Dense graph support causes no extra cost.

4. SUPPORT WIDTH AND SPARSE-PLUS-LOW-RANK COMBINATION

The frozen INITIAL derives exact support-width contraction. Its graph has
edge {i,j} when F_ij or F_ji is nonzero, i!=j. An order has induced width w
if cliquing neighbors at elimination never leaves more than w neighbors.
At a site, multiply every incident factor, retain monomials containing all
four site generators, and remove that complete block. Extracting a degree
four block introduces no extra sign. No outside factor can supply a missing
eliminated generator. Induction proves the resulting message represents
the same partial coefficient integral; each scope is in a filled clique,
so a bucket contains at most w+1 physical sites.

For F=S+UV, instead use M0=[[X,S],[-S*,Y]] in (4) and retain all auxiliaries
until the end. The same local P_i and S-edge factors are combined with the
four physical-auxiliary factors per (i,a) displayed before (5). The physical
scope graph is still the support of S. A bucket has at most 4(w+1)+4r
generators, and eliminating complete physical blocks is valid for the same
reason. At the end use the auxiliary 00/11 test in (6). This proves the
counter in revisions/band_lowrank_bcs.py, for every local prefix as well.

With g=4(w+1+r), d_S directed nonzero entries, and coefficient degrees <=n,
a loose count is O((n+d_S+nr)(n+1)^2 3^g) coefficient multiplications/additions.
The implemented generic nested-mask product also performs up to 4^g overlap
checks per multiplication; these checks must be charged in actual bit time.
Both bounds give poly(n,input length)2^{O(w+r)} time, with O((n+1)2^g)
message words. This clarifies the arithmetic-only 3^g bound in INITIAL;
the asserted poly*2^{O(w)} bit class there is unchanged. Denominators and
numerators are polynomial-bit for the same single-use factor reason as in
Section 3. Arbitrary interference is retained, with no positive-factor
assumption or relative-error cancellation estimate.

An acquired width order suffices. The executable acquires a min-degree
heuristic order and measures its true filled width exactly; it makes no
optimal-width guarantee. For a class stated in terms of graph treewidth,
Korhonen Theorem 1 provides, given G and bound t, a width <=2t+1 decomposition
or a correct certificate tw(G)>t in 2^{O(t)}n graph time. Trying t through
a fixed C log n is polynomial. Leaf-bag elimination turns a decomposition
into an order without increasing width. The graph theorem is imported;
this external acquisition routine was not implemented here. Reading and
constructing the explicit support graph is charged polynomial input time.

There is no acquired algorithm for an arbitrary optimal sparse-plus-low-rank
split. The following concrete F-only rule avoids supplying one:

 H=first r row indices; K=last r column indices; B=F[H,K].
 If B is nonsingular, U=F[:,K], V=B^(-1)F[H,:], S=F-UV.           (7)

Everything in (7) is acquired exactly in polynomial bit time. Measure the
rank and support width, or use the imported width acquisition on S. Trying
r=1,...,floor(C log n) is polynomial for fixed C. Accept only the actual
computed splits with r+width(S)=O(log n). This does not assert that a failed
fixed corner disproves existence of another favorable split.

Infinite family: let S_n have zero diagonal, +1 directly above and -1
directly below the diagonal; let F_n=S_n+J_n. For n>=4 its first-row/last-
column corner B is 1. Rule (7) with r=1 yields

 S'_ij = (S_n)_ij - delta_(i,n-2) - delta_(j,1)
                         - delta_(i,n-2)delta_(j,1).           (8)

Hence the residual support is contained in a path plus the two hubs 1,n-2.
Bags {i,i+1,1,n-2} give width <=3. The support of F_n is the complete graph:
nonadjacent entries are 1 and adjacent opposite orientations are 2 and 0,
so every unordered pair has a nonzero orientation.

For n=2m+1, det(S_n)=0 while its leading 2m principal minor is 1 (the path
determinant recurrence is D_j=D_(j-2), D_0=1,D_1=0). Its one-dimensional
kernel is v=(1,0,1,0,...,1). Since S_n is skew symmetric, adj(S_n)=vv^T;
the scalar is 1 from its first principal cofactor. The rank-one determinant
identity then gives det(F_n)=1^T adj(S_n)1=(m+1)^2>0. Thus this acquired
exact-polynomial family has full rank and maximal support treewidth for
arbitrarily large odd n. Its low-rank-plus-width parameter stays bounded.

5. FINITE-RANDOM-BIT SAMPLING AND COSTS

Use any exact prefix counter above. At each site compute its three branch
masses a_0,a_u,a_d, whose sum equals the current mass. For canonical sampling
use its c_k coefficient; for grandcanonical use the coefficient sum.
An initial exact-zero test precedes sampling. Nonempty branches always
retain the same rank factorization/support width; no acquired structure
depends on a favorable sampled prefix.

Let B=ceil(log2(2 max(n,1)/epsilon)) and D=2^B. Replace the two internal
cumulative probabilities by floor(D times that probability)/D, leaving
endpoints 0 and 1 exact. Draw B fair bits and choose the corresponding
interval. Zero-weight branches have zero interval. Each conditional law
differs by at most 2/D in TV. Sequential coupling bounds total TV by
2n/D<=epsilon. This is a fixed nB-bit process, without rejection or
geometric waiting; every execution uses at most 3n+1 prefix counts.
There is no inverse c_k or filter success probability in its runtime.
Exact ratios use polynomial-bit rational division even when c_k is tiny.

The rank implementation reuses U,V across all calls. The support-width
implementation reuses its acquired order. The combined counter implements
all required prefix calls; its sampler follows this identical self-reduction
but was not added as a separate executable wrapper. These are physical
occupation Born samples; a universal-circuit output distribution or a
coherent magic-state contraction is not silently substituted.

6. CERTIFIED APPROXIMATE LOW RANK

The following certificate gives a rigorous approximation class without an
unknown norm lower bound. Let G be a low-rank matrix constructed from F,
e.g. the UV part of (7). Let delta=||F-G||_F, and acquire a rational M>=1
that bounds both ||F||_F and ||G||_F; the sum of absolute real and imaginary
entries is an explicit such bound. Let b=c_k(G), computed by Section 3.

Claim: the unnormalized projected state vectors satisfy

 ||psi_k(F)-psi_k(G)||_2 <= k delta M^(k-1)=D.                   (9)

Proof: the matrix of all k-by-k minors is exterior power wedge^k F. If A_k
is the isometric antisymmetric-tensor embedding, this matrix is
A_k* F^(tensor k) A_k. Left/right isometries contract Frobenius norm.
The telescoping identity for F^(tensor k)-G^(tensor k), Frobenius-norm
multiplicativity for tensor products and the triangle inequality bound its
norm by k delta M^(k-1). Selecting disjoint-row/column entries is a coordinate
projection, also contractive. The sector's common Fock-order phase has no
effect. This proves (9), including singular and complex matrices.

For grandcanonical state psi(F)=direct_sum_k psi_k(F), replace D^2 by

 delta^2 sum_{j=1}^{floor(n/2)} j^2 M^(2(j-1)),                 (10)

and b by Z_G(1). Different sectors are orthogonal, so squared bounds add.
The empty-sector difference is zero. Both (9)^2 and (10) are rational and
have polynomial encoding length; no square-root algorithm is needed.

For target error 0<epsilon<=1, accept precisely when

 b>0 and D^2 <= epsilon^2 b/64.                               (11)

If (11) fails, report NOT CERTIFIED. When it holds, put eta=D/sqrt(b)
<=epsilon/8. If a is the target norm squared for F, triangle inequalities
give sqrt(a)>0 and (1-eta)sqrt(b)<=sqrt(a)<=(1+eta)sqrt(b).
Consequently

 |a-b|/a <= (2eta+eta^2)/(1-eta)^2 <=4eta<=epsilon/2.            (12)

The last inequality uses eta<=1/8. Normalization gives

 ||psi(F)/sqrt(a)-psi(G)/sqrt(b)||_2 <=2eta<=epsilon/4.          (13)

For (13), split the difference into the norm rescaling of psi(F) and the
unnormalized vector difference, both divided by sqrt(b). Pure-state trace
distance, and hence Born TV, is at most this vector distance. Sampling G
with Section 5 at TV error epsilon/2 gives TV to F <=3epsilon/4<=epsilon.
Returning b gives the relative guarantee (12). No actual norm a is queried.

All acquisition, residual calculation and certificate comparisons are
polynomial-bit operations. Low-rank counting/sampling has the Section 3
cost. Thus trying the fixed small corners in (7) with acquired ranks
O(log n), and accepting only (11), is an F-only polynomial approximation
admission rule. The inequalities depend on a computed projected norm,
not solely on spectral decay, numerical rank, a condition number or an
uncontrolled absolute error. k>rank(G) yields b=0 and cannot be accepted
for that canonical sector. Grandcanonical acceptance can still cover small
high-sector tails. This certificate is conservative and may reject a good
approximation; completeness for every approximately low-rank F is not claimed.

7. TWO EXACT APPROXIMATION DISCONTINUITIES

First, n=4 and J the all-ones matrix. For rational e>0 define
 F_A=J+e E_01, F_B=J+e E_10.
They have rank two and distance e to the same rank-one J. In sector k=2,
F_A has exactly two nonzero allowed minors:
 I={0,2},J={1,3}; I={0,3},J={1,2}.
Their determinants have magnitude e, so c_2(F_A)=2e^2. F_B has the two
reversed supports I={1,2},J={0,3}; I={1,3},J={0,2}, with the same norm.
The normalized Born laws have disjoint supports and TV=1 for every e,
and the coherent projected states are orthogonal. c_2(J)=0. Pair-matrix
distance is sqrt(2)e although the normalized laws remain maximally separated.

Second, even k<=approximation rank is insufficient: let G=diag(1,0),
 F_A=G+e E_01, F_B=G+e E_10, n=2, k=1.
All three matrices have rank one. Each perturbed matrix is distance e from
G; both c_1 equal e^2. One Born law is the deterministic occupation u_0 d_1,
the other u_1 d_0, so TV=1. The common approximation has zero projected norm,
and (11) correctly rejects it. These examples establish the lack of a
uniform postprojection stability guarantee from absolute matrix closeness.
They do not refute the exact rank algorithm or certified rule (11).

8. POSTSELECTED FLO: WHERE THE REDUCTION FAILS

Primary source screen: DiVincenzo-Terhal quant-ph/0403031 distinguishes
single-mode Fock measurements (Gaussian/Slater closure) from coarse two-mode
measurements. Its charge/parity universality interfaces involve interleaved
nondestructive projections and Gaussian operations. Beenakker et al.
quant-ph/0401066 constructs CNOT using charge parity, spin rotations,
beam splitters, measurements and feed-forward; its offline construction
also uses non-Gaussian ancillas/teleportation. These are not a theorem that
one explicit opposite-spin F followed only by final onsite hard projectors
has a universal circuit's selected branch probability as its norm.

The exact reordering failure in INITIAL is elementary. On sites i,j start
from u_i^dagger d_j^dagger|vac>, which hard P allows. Swap the two u modes
by a passive Gaussian operation G. The result is a double at j and is
killed by P. Hence P G|state>=0 while G P|state>!=0. Intermediate filters
cannot be commuted through arbitrary Gaussian operations to the end.

A coherent bra is also not a diagonal norm projector. For n=2,
 F_sign=[[0,1],[sign,0]], sign=+1 or -1,
both canonical hard norms are c_1=2. The unnormalized bra that sums the two
oriented occupation states gives squared amplitude |1+sign|^2, equal to
4 or 0. Hard projection followed by a norm loses this interference. This
refutes that direct substitution, not every possible gadget reduction.

The four-mode magic state |1100>+|0011> is not a two-particle Slater state:
its Pluecker residual a_12 a_34-a_13 a_24+a_14 a_23 equals 1. Passive FLO,
Fock ancillas and single-mode Fock postselection preserve Slater states,
so that Gaussian-only closure cannot prepare its coherent effect. Allowing
additional hard/coarse projectors can break this closure, and is exactly
the missing gadget issue. Fermionic MBQC arXiv:1207.5846 Section IV uses
six/eight-mode resource sites and multi-mode coherent measurements; its
interface is not admitted by merely identifying each of our two-mode sites.
Fermion Sampling arXiv:2012.15825 was screened at abstract level only; its
magic-input hardness theorem was not imported as a static-norm reduction.

9. ZERO-PRESERVING COMPILATION IMPLICATION

The baseline supplies a deterministic polynomial-bit test of c_k(F)>0,
via linear matroid parity. To see the exact link, let the ith pair of
columns be (e_i,f_i), with f_i row i of F transposed. For selected pair
indices I, Cauchy-Binet gives

 det(V[P(I)]* V[P(I)])
       =sum_{J subset [n]\I, |J|=|I|} |det F[I,J]|^2.

It is positive iff those 2|I| columns are independent. Thus c_k(F)>0 iff
there exist k independent pairs; a maximum parity support algorithm decides
this. The rational-complex input has a polynomial-bit reduction to finite
fields: clear denominators; bound numerators of all relevant minors by
polynomial-length determinant bounds; try enough suitable finite-field
primes so their product exceeds these bounds. A nonzero minor cannot vanish
at every prime. Imported deterministic linear-parity algorithms give the
finite-field decisions; neither this compiler nor the parity solver was
implemented here. Hamada-Iwata arXiv:2610.03030 Theorem 1.1 was checked as
a current primary parity source; the no-go needs only the established
polynomial decision fact, not that preprint's improved arithmetic complexity.

Suppose a deterministic polynomial-bit compiler took arbitrary selected
branches from an exact universal/postselected circuit class and returned
only an explicit rational-complex F and k, with

 c_k(F)>0 iff that selected branch probability p>0.             (14)

Examples include c_k=alpha p for an explicitly nonzero positive alpha, or
a two-sided relative comparison that preserves exact zero. For a CNF phi
on m bits, prepare the uniform superposition, compute phi reversibly into
an accept bit, and select accept=1. Its probability is #SAT(phi)/2^m, zero
iff phi is unsatisfiable. Apply the hypothetical compiler and the polynomial
support test. This decides SAT in P, and therefore implies P=NP.
The source class must contain these exact logical circuits; no approximate
gate-set/success-probability assumption is silently substituted. The argument
is conditional on the compiler and source interface and independent of a
particular physical CNOT implementation.

For a grandcanonical unconditioned output norm, Z_F(1)>=c_0=1 outright,
so equality to a positive multiple of arbitrary zero branch probabilities
is algebraically impossible. Neither observation excludes reductions using
always-positive probabilities, signed differences, a positive baseline,
extra coherent bras or enlarged projector interfaces. It gives no generic
FPRAS, no approximate-counting hardness theorem and no proved complexity
separation. A #P-hard nonnegative counter can have easy support. The scope
here is output (F,k) alone; no polynomial support claim is extended to
arbitrary extra physical prefix measurements without a separate proof.
