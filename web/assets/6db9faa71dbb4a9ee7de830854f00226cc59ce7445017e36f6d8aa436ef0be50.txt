SECTION 2 DERIVATIVE AUDIT
Scope: outputs/research/sol_collision_precision/quantum_tail_precision.txt, §2 only.
Status: analytic audit passed under 0<r<=1/2; no finite test was used as proof.

Definitions and exact derivative
Let t=atanh(r), d=2j+1, and use the real-spin extension

  Z_j=sinh(dt)/sinh(t),
  R_j=(1/2) d/dt log Z_j=(d coth(dt)-coth(t))/2,
  a_j=R_j/(j+1)=(d coth(dt)-coth(t))/(d+1).

Here beta=2t, so the derivative of log Z with respect to t is 2R_j.
Differentiating with respect to d and using dd/dj=2 gives exactly

  a'_j=2[(coth(dt)+coth(t))/(d+1)^2
              - d t csch^2(dt)/(d+1)].

Since d>=1 and coth is decreasing, coth(dt)<=coth(t)=1/r. The second
term is nonnegative before subtraction. Thus

  a'_j <= 4/[r(d+1)^2] = 1/[r(j+1)^2].

Lower derivative and numerical constant
If j>=2/r, then u=dt >= (2j+1)r >=4+r>4, since t>=r. The positive
term in a'_j is at least 2/[r(d+1)^2]. The magnitude of its negative
term is at most 2t csch^2(u). Moreover,

  2 r t(d+1)^2/sinh^2(u)
    = 2(r/t) u^2 ((d+1)/d)^2/sinh^2(u)
    <= 8 u^2/sinh^2(u),

using r<=t and (d+1)/d<=2. The function u^2/sinh^2(u) decreases on
u>0: its logarithmic derivative is 2/u-2 coth(u)<0, since tanh(u)<u.
At u=4, e^4>24 and e^-4<1 imply sinh(4)>23/2>sqrt(128), so

  8(4)^2/sinh^2(4) < 512/529 < 1.

Therefore 8u^2/sinh^2(u)<=1 for every u>=4, and the negative term is
at most 1/[r(d+1)^2]. Subtracting it from the positive lower bound proves

  a'_j >= 1/[r(d+1)^2] = 1/[4r(j+1)^2],  j>=2/r.

The u>=4 condition and the numerical inequality in §2 are both valid.

Thermal moment and threshold
Put q=e^(-2t)=(1-r)/(1+r). With occupation k=j-m in {0,...,2j},
the truncated geometric distribution gives

  E[k] = q/(1-q) - d q^d/(1-q^d) <= (1-r)/(2r),
  R_j=j-E[k].

Consequently a_j>=1-(1+r)/[2r(j+1)]>=1-3/[4rj] for r<=1/2.
Combining this with the derivative upper bound gives, with x=rj,

  a_j-j a'_j >= B(x,r)
    := 1-(1+r)/[2(x+r)]-x/(x+r)^2.

The printed simplification B>=1-7/(4x) is correct and proves the claimed
1/2 lower bound for x>=7/2; hence the source's x>=4 condition is valid.
Keeping the denominators gives the sharper exact threshold for this lower
bound: B(x,r)>=1/2 iff

  x >= T(r) := [3-r+sqrt(9-2r+r^2)]/2.

For 0<r<=1/2, T is decreasing and sup T(r)=3 as r down to zero. Also
B increases in x for x>=3. Thus x>=3 is the least uniform sufficient
threshold furnished by the displayed moment and derivative bounds. It is
not asserted to be the least threshold for the exact quantity a_j-j a'_j.
Since x>=3 implies j>=2/r, it also meets the lower-derivative hypothesis.

Finally, (a_j/j)'=(j a'_j-a_j)/j^2, so a_j-j a'_j>=1/2 implies
(a_j/j)'<=-1/(2j^2). This establishes §2's (2.5), and in fact establishes
it under the refined uniform condition rj>=3; the stated rj>=4 is safe.
