# Hostile audit: closure of bounded-potential noninteracting ground densities

## Verdict

Under the standard fixed-(N), fermionic **ensemble** definition, the set (R_C) is closed in (X=L^1(\mathbb R^3)\cap L^3(\mathbb R^3)) for every finite (C), when potentials are real scalar multipliers in (Y=L^{3/2}+L^\infty) and are bounded by the quotient sum norm modulo additive constants. The proof needs no uniform infinitesimal form bound and no convergence of Schrödinger operators. It uses weak compactness of bounded potential decompositions, strong convergence in both dual-paired density norms, and lower semicontinuity of the ensemble noninteracting kinetic functional.

Consequently, if a fixed-(N) density (n_\star\in X) is not ensemble ground-state representable by **any** such local potential, then for each finite (C)

\[
  \operatorname{dist}_X(n_\star,R_C)>0
\]

(with distance (+\infty) if (R_C=\varnothing)). This is only a qualitative, (C)-by-(C) barrier. The argument gives no computable lower bound, and it does not imply positive distance from the union over unbounded potential norms: the decreasing gaps may tend to zero as (C\to\infty).

## Exact interface and hypotheses

Write

\[
 \|v\|_Y=\inf_{v=f+g}\big(\|f\|_{3/2}+\|g\|_\infty\big),\qquad
 \|n\|_X=\|n\|_1+\|n\|_3,
\]

and take the quotient potential norm

\[
 \|[v]\|_{Y/\mathbb R}=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\|v+c\|_Y.
\]

For fixed integer (N\ge1), let (T_s[n]) be the ensemble constrained-search kinetic energy over trace-one density matrices on the antisymmetric (N)-electron space with spin, with spin-summed spatial density (n). Extend (T_s=+\infty) outside the (N)-representable finite-kinetic domain. Define (R_C) as the densities of actual ground-state ensembles of

\[
 H_v=\sum_{i=1}^N\left(-\tfrac12\Delta_i+v(x_i)\right),\qquad
 v\in Y\text{ real},\quad \|[v]\|_{Y/\mathbb R}\le C.
\]

The ground ensemble is allowed to be mixed and to occupy any degenerate ground space. A ground ensemble at density (n) minimizes (T_s[m]+\int vm) over all admissible fixed-(N) densities (m). In fact its kinetic energy must equal (T_s[n]), since replacing it by a lower-kinetic ensemble at the same density would lower the Hamiltonian energy.

The claim concerns this ensemble model. A determinant-only version, a spin-dependent potential class, a density topology weaker than global (L^1\cap L^3), or a potential bound that is not a uniform quotient-sum bound needs a separate statement.

## Proof reconstruction

Let (n_j\in R_C) and suppose (n_j\to n) strongly in (X). For each (j), choose a representative \(\tilde v_j=v_j+c_j\) of its potential class and a decomposition \(\tilde v_j=f_j+g_j\) such that

\[
 \|f_j\|_{3/2}+\|g_j\|_\infty\le C+o(1).
\]

This is possible even if neither infimum is attained: choose both approximations within (1/j). Adding (c_j) does not change any fixed-(N) ground space. Constants also cancel from every comparison of two (N)-electron densities because both integrate to (N).

**1. Bounded kinetic energy follows without a uniform form estimate.** Choose one smooth, compactly supported, ensemble (N)-representable trial density (m_0\in X) with (T_s[m_0]<\infty). The ground-state variational principle gives

\[
 T_s[n_j]+\langle \tilde v_j,n_j\rangle
 \le T_s[m_0]+\langle \tilde v_j,m_0\rangle.
\]

The potential-density pairing obeys

\[
 |\langle w,u\rangle|\le \|w\|_Y\|u\|_X,
\]

by Hölder for the (L^{3/2}\)-(L^3) and (L^\infty\)-(L^1) parts. Since (n_j\to n) in (X), the sequence (n_j) is bounded there, and hence

\[
 T_s[n_j]\le T_s[m_0]+(C+o(1))\|m_0-n_j\|_X=O(1).
\]

This bound is the key response to critical concentration: the energy of a ground ensemble cannot hide unbounded kinetic energy behind a potential expectation when both the quotient potential norm and the density (X)-norm are bounded. No estimate of the form \(q_{v_j}\ge (1-\epsilon)T-B_\epsilon\) uniform in (j) is used.

**2. Extract a limiting local potential.** The (L^{3/2}) component is reflexive and the bounded (L^\infty) ball is weak-star sequentially compact because (L^1(\mathbb R^3)) is separable. After a subsequence,

\[
 f_j\rightharpoonup f\quad\text{in }L^{3/2},\qquad
 g_j\stackrel{*}{\rightharpoonup}g\quad\text{in }L^\infty.
\]

Weak lower semicontinuity of these norms, together with the uniform sum bound, gives \(\|f\|_{3/2}+\|g\|_\infty\le C\). Thus $v=f+g$ is real, belongs to $Y$, and has quotient norm at most $C$.

Each such limit potential separately defines a closed semibounded Schrödinger form: an (L^{3/2}) function is infinitesimally form bounded after truncating its large-value tail, and the (L^\infty) part is bounded. This is an individual-potential fact; no uniform bound along (v_j) is inferred.

For the moving density, strong convergence in exactly the two dual-paired norms yields

\[
 \langle f_j,n_j\rangle\to\langle f,n\rangle,
 \qquad
 \langle g_j,n_j\rangle\to\langle g,n\rangle.
\]

For example, the first difference is bounded by
\(\|f_j\|_{3/2}\|n_j-n\|_3+|\langle f_j-f,n\rangle|\); the second is bounded by
\(\|g_j\|_\infty\|n_j-n\|_1+|\langle g_j-g,n\rangle|\).
The same pairing convergence holds for each fixed (m\in X).

**3. Pass the ground-state variational inequality to the limit.** For every admissible $m$ with $T_s[m]<\infty$,

\[
 T_s[n_j]+\langle \tilde v_j,n_j\rangle
 \le T_s[m]+\langle \tilde v_j,m\rangle.
\]

The ensemble constrained-search functional (T_s) is lower semicontinuous in (X). Therefore

\[
 T_s[n]+\langle v,n\rangle
 \le \liminf_j\big(T_s[n_j]+\langle \tilde v_j,n_j\rangle\big)
 \le \limsup_j\big(T_s[n_j]+\langle \tilde v_j,n_j\rangle\big)
 \le T_s[m]+\langle v,m\rangle.
\]

The first inequality also shows $T_s[n]<\infty$, using the uniform upper bound from step 1 and bounded pairings. The fixed-density constrained search at $n$ is attained: a minimizing sequence has bounded kinetic energy and fixed spatial density, hence is tight in configuration space; local Rellich compactness gives a trace-norm convergent subsequence and kinetic lower semicontinuity. Let \(\Gamma\) attain $T_s[n]$. For any (N)-electron ensemble \(\Lambda\) with density $m$,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(H_v\Lambda)\ge T_s[m]+\langle v,m\rangle
 \ge T_s[n]+\langle v,n\rangle
 =\operatorname{Tr}(H_v\Gamma).
\]

Thus \(\Gamma\) is an actual ground-state ensemble for $v$, so $n\in R_C$. This proves closedness.

The lower semicontinuity and attainment facts can also be obtained by the standard direct-method proof for the ensemble constrained-search functional. For lower semicontinuity with varying densities, take near-minimizing ensembles \(\Gamma_j\) with bounded kinetic energy. Strong (L^1) convergence gives uniform tightness, since the probability that any coordinate lies outside (B_R) is at most \(\int_{B_R^c}n_j\). Uniform kinetic energy plus this tightness gives trace-norm precompactness by energy truncation and Rellich compactness on bounded configuration-space regions. The limit has trace one and density $n$; the density map is trace-norm continuous into (L^1), and kinetic energy is lower semicontinuous.

## Hostile cases

- **Critical (L^{3/2}) concentration:** It defeats any blanket claim of uniform infinitesimal form bounds. For a compactly supported (W\ge0) and coupling \(\lambda\) for which \(-\tfrac12\Delta-\lambda W\) has a negative ground state \(\phi\), the scaled potentials
  \[
  v_\epsilon(x)=-\lambda\epsilon^{-2}W(x/\epsilon)
  \]
  have constant (L^{3/2}) norm, while their one-electron ground orbitals are \(\phi_\epsilon(x)=\epsilon^{-3/2}\phi(x/\epsilon)\). The densities satisfy \(\|n_\epsilon\|_3=\epsilon^{-2}\|n\|_3\), so they cannot converge strongly in (X). This is a counterexample to a uniform form-lower-bound premise, **not** a counterexample to (R_C)-closedness.
- **Weak-star (L^\infty) drift:** It is harmless here: the weak-star limit exists along a subsequence, and the (L^1)-strong density convergence passes its pairing to the limit.
- **Additive gauges:** Choose bounded-norm representatives after adding constants; fixed particle number cancels those constants from the variational inequalities. Diverging choices of (c_j) therefore do not obstruct extraction.
- **Collapse and kinetic blow-up:** The ground-state comparison with one fixed trial density proves \(\sup_jT_s[n_j]<\infty\), even though the potential forms have no uniform relative bound. Strong (L^3) also prevents a fixed-mass scale-collapse profile from converging in (X).
- **Escape to infinity:** Strong (L^1) convergence makes the densities uniformly tight. If the topology were weakened to local convergence, translated wells and their translated ground densities would escape while converging locally to zero, which is not a fixed-(N) density.
- **Degeneracy:** No eigenvector selection is needed. The inequalities use ensembles, and the limit ensemble may occupy an enlarged degenerate ground space.

No counterexample to the stated (X)-closedness survived these checks.

## Limitation and transfer consequence

For the pinned three-electron target whose source theorem excludes every real spin-independent (v\in L^{3/2}+L^\infty), this closure result implies a positive approximation distance from (R_C) for each finite potential budget (C), in the global (L^1\cap L^3) norm. It gives no rate in (C), no numeric separation constant, no computable method to find a separating ball, and no conclusion for sequences whose potential quotient norm diverges. This is therefore an exact qualitative barrier, not yet an effective theorem for assay/design use.

The strongest topology limitation is global tightness: translated fixed-norm wells give ground densities converging to zero locally, so a local topology would lose fixed particle number. The strongest analytic limitation inside the stated model is that critical concentration really can destroy uniform form estimates; the closure proof succeeds because it bypasses form convergence and uses strong density convergence plus the variational principle.
