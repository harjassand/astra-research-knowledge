# Spatial computation candidate: a Poisson sensing ceiling for pattern displacement

**Status:** proved for the stated statistical interface; likely a standard corollary of established positional-information limits, not a novelty claim. No inference about a particular organism follows without validating the count model and parameters.

## Question and interface

How many morphogen-receptor events must a multicellular program observe to tell whether an exponential gradient, and hence a fixed-threshold boundary, has shifted by a distance `δ`?

Take cells at fixed positions `x_i`. During an observation window, cell `i` records an unsaturated event count

`Y_i | H_0 ~ Pois(μ_i)`, independently across cells. Under `H_1`, the exponential field is shifted by `δ`:

`c_1(x) = exp(δ/λ)c_0(x)`, so `Y_i | H_1 ~ Pois(q μ_i)`, where `q = exp(δ/λ)` and `λ` is the known decay length. For a fixed concentration threshold, this shift moves every interior threshold boundary by `δ`. Let `M = Σ_i μ_i` be the total expected receptor-event count under `H_0` during the full measurement window.

After observing the vector `Y`, the tissue may run any stochastic gene circuit, any cell-to-cell relay, and any decoder. These operations must be a stochastic postprocessing of `Y`; all computation and communication are treated as free. The objective is to return the correct one of the two boundary patterns with worst-case error at most `ε < 1/2`.

## Theorem (two-pattern sensing lower bound)

Every such program must satisfy

`M >= -log(4 ε (1-ε)) / (exp(δ/(2λ)) - 1)^2`.

For `|δ|/λ -> 0` and fixed `ε in (0,1/2)`, this gives

`M >= [4 log(1/(4 ε (1-ε))) + o(1)] (λ/δ)^2`.

Thus, for fixed target error bounded away from `1/2`, the best-case molecular-event requirement scales at least as `Θ((λ/δ)^2)`. Increasing the number of cells while holding the total expected count `M` fixed cannot evade this bound. If cell-cell messages or downstream reactions add noise, their output is another postprocessing and cannot improve the bound. They may require additional resources.

## Proof

Let `P_0` and `P_1` be the full receptor-count-vector distributions. The Hellinger affinity of independent Poisson coordinates is

`A(P_0,P_1) = exp[-(1/2) Σ_i (sqrt(μ_i)-sqrt(q μ_i))^2]`
`= exp[-(M/2)(sqrt(q)-1)^2]`.

For any binary test, the minimum equal-prior average error is `(1-TV(P_0,P_1))/2`; a decoder with worst-case error at most `ε` therefore requires `TV(P_0,P_1) >= 1-2ε`. The standard inequality `TV(P_0,P_1) <= sqrt(1-A(P_0,P_1)^2)` then gives

`exp[-M(sqrt(q)-1)^2] <= 4 ε (1-ε)`.

Rearranging proves the claim. Since any circuit and relay is a stochastic kernel applied to the count vector, data processing can only reduce total variation. Also, `T=Σ_i Y_i` is sufficient for this binary test: `T~Pois(M)` under `H_0` and `T~Pois(qM)` under `H_1`. Spatial redistribution of a fixed total expected event budget supplies no extra information for this particular global multiplicative perturbation.

An explicit upper-bound comparator is the likelihood-ratio test on `T`. Its Chernoff bound gives equal-prior error at most `(1/2) exp[-M C(q)]`, where

`C(q) = max_{0<=s<=1} [s + (1-s)q - q^(1-s)] = (1/8 + o(1))(δ/λ)^2`

for small `|δ|/λ`. Consequently, at every fixed `ε` strictly between zero and one-half, the quadratic scaling in `λ/δ` is tight up to constants in this ideal count model. This does not make the assay, circuit, or communication cost-free in an organism; it only isolates the sensing-event cost.

## What this does and does not say

The result is a model theorem, not a biological measurement. Its event counts must be interpreted as all independent ligand/receptor observations retained by the cells, including temporal samples if present. The profile is known except for the specified global displacement; `λ` and the cell positions are known; there is no saturation, unknown additive background, receptor heterogeneity, or extra independent spatial cue. It treats event counts as Poisson and independent. Diffusion-induced correlations or non-Poisson kinetics require a different likelihood and may change the bound.

The lower bound is intentionally optimistic for the program: it grants lossless access to every count and free global pooling. It says nothing about the additional cost needed to implement a relay, the speed of boundary formation, stability after the morphogen is removed, or whether cells realize this decoder. Nor does it imply that contact signaling cannot sharpen a boundary when it receives independent information or a distinct local cue.

## Prior-art attack and novelty boundary

The mathematical route is close to established work, so this note should not be promoted as a new biological principle without a more specific gap:

1. Tostevin, ten Wolde, and Howard, “Fundamental limits to position determination by concentration gradients,” *PLoS Computational Biology* 3(4), e78 (2007), DOI [10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030078](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030078). Their model has first-order reaction-diffusion gradient formation, intrinsic biochemical noise, a threshold position, and an ideal detector/readout; they derive position precision versus protein counts and temporal averaging. The introduction explicitly treats the ideal detector as a lower-bound assumption (paper pp. 1–2, lines 101–145 in the primary PDF). This substantially overlaps the physical question, although the present two-point calculation is a generic Poisson testing statement for a whole multi-cell count vector rather than their stochastic spatial field calculation.
2. Gregor et al., “Probing the limits to positional information,” *Cell* 130 (2007), DOI [10.1016/j.cell.2007.05.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2007.05.025), quantify Bicoid positional precision including random morphogen arrival and downstream readout noise. This is direct prior art for receptor-count-limited positional decoding.
3. Mugler, Levchenko, and Nemenman, “Limits to the precision of gradient sensing with spatial communication and temporal integration,” *PNAS* 113 (2016), E689–E695, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1509597112](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1509597112), derive a communicating multicellular sensing limit and a finite communication length scale. Ellison et al., “Cell–cell communication enhances the capacity of cell ensembles to sense shallow gradients during morphogenesis,” *PNAS* 113 (2016), E679–E688, DOI [10.1073/pnas.1516503113](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1516503113), combine mammary-organoid experiments with a noisy LEGI-style relay model; their supplemental Eqs. S43–S46 give a communication-limited gradient-sensing precision result. These papers already rule out treating downstream intercellular pooling as noiseless or automatically beneficial.
4. Bojer, Kremser, and Gerland, “Robust boundary formation in a morphogen gradient via cell-cell signaling,” *Physical Review E* 105, 064405 (2022), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevE.105.064405](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.105.064405), compare AND/OR/SUM combinations of morphogen and nearest-neighbor signals, including boundary fuzziness, tunability, and size scaling. This is close prior art for the pattern-output rather than sensing-only interface.
5. Vetter and Iber, “Precision of morphogen gradients in neural tube development,” *Nature Communications* 13, 1145 (2022), DOI [10.1038/s41467-022-28834-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28834-3), study amplitude and decay-length variability in exponential gradients and report that amplitude changes shift interior thresholds together while leaving their spacing unchanged. Thus the displacement mode used here is already recognized biologically.
6. Song and Hyeon, “Cost-precision trade-off relation determines the optimal morphogen gradient for accurate biological pattern formation,” *eLife* 10:e70034 (2021), DOI [10.7554/eLife.70034](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.70034), link morphogen production/thermodynamic cost to threshold-position precision and optimize the decay length. This raises the standard for any claimed resource result: production, receptor events, cell-cell messages, energy, and precision must not be conflated.

The narrow surviving contribution is an exact finite-error testing inequality whose charged resource is total expected raw receptor events across the complete cell array, with arbitrary downstream stochastic processing allowed. It is best treated as a clean baseline/corollary until a genuinely new ingredient is added (e.g. a sharper architecture-independent bound with a realistic shared-diffusion likelihood, an identified nuisance-parameter obstruction, or a costed relay theorem not implied by the cited LEGI results).

## Decisive next gate

Compare the exact count-budget bound with the source-specific Tostevin et al. position-variance formula after matching observation time, receptor-event count, tissue geometry, and noise assumptions. If the same scaling and operational conclusion follow, retain this as a compact theorem/baseline and redirect research effort. A stronger biological claim would require a measured receptor-event budget, a measured `λ`, a target boundary tolerance `δ`, and an equally informed control; none is supplied here.
