Source020 audit — Proposition 8.2 one-time quartic deletion
Date: 2026-10-08

STATUS AND SCOPE
I independently reconstruct Proposition 8.2 (the `prop:quintic-discard` global exceptional-value lemma) for the pinned source020 manuscript. The proof gives a finite exceptional family depending only on the fixed quartic f, and an O_f(X^{1/5}) count of its integer values in [-2X,2X]. This is a load-bearing uniformity/deletion step used in the quartic branch of the large-prime tail, not a verification of Proposition 1.4 or of the full density theorem.

The source’s claim: a degree-five polynomial N over the fixed splitting field L, with four distinct critical points in L taking the four fixed conjugate roots θ_i as critical values, has all its integer values at L-inputs in one fixed finite-family value set E_f. That set has O_f(X^{1/5}) values of size at most 2X. Section 11 deletes this set once, before prime intervals, boxes, and curve components are counted. I find the quantifiers and this one-time use consistent. A separate Section 3 adaptive-deletion audit is appended below.

INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION
Fix a primitive irreducible quartic f, its splitting field L, and its four distinct roots θ_1,…,θ_4. Write r=[L:Q]. Define F_f to be the set of Φ∈L[t] of degree five for which there are distinct z_1,…,z_4∈L with z_1=0, z_2=1, Φ'(z_i)=0, and Φ(z_i)=θ_i. Let

  E_f={n∈Z : n=Φ(z) for some Φ∈F_f and z∈L}.

The checked statement is: F_f is finite; # (E_f∩[-2X,2X]) ≪_f X^{1/5} for all X≥1; and if N∈L[s] has degree five and distinct τ_i∈L with N'(τ_i)=0 and N(τ_i)=θ_i, then every integer N(s) with s∈L belongs to E_f.

1. Finiteness after normalization. Consider the finite-type locus in six coefficient variables for Φ and two variables z_3,z_4, with z_1=0,z_2=1, equations Φ(z_i)=θ_i and Φ'(z_i)=0, and open conditions that the leading coefficient and all z_i−z_j are nonzero. If a reduced irreducible component had positive dimension, its function field F over C would have a nonzero C-derivation ∂: differentiate a transcendence-basis element and extend through the finite separable extension (characteristic zero).

Let Q(t)=∂Φ(t), differentiating only coefficients. Differentiating the four value equations gives Q(z_i)=0 because Φ'(z_i)=0. The four distinct z_i exhaust the roots of the degree-four polynomial Φ'; hence Φ' divides Q, and Q=AΦ' for a polynomial A of degree at most one. Differentiating Φ'(z_i)=0 gives

  0=Q'(z_i)+Φ''(z_i)∂z_i
   =Φ''(z_i)(A(z_i)+∂z_i).

The roots of Φ' are simple, so Φ''(z_i)≠0. Since z_1 and z_2 are fixed, A(0)=A(1)=0; therefore A=0, Q=0, and ∂z_3=∂z_4=0. Thus ∂ kills every coefficient and variable generating F, contradicting ∂≠0. Every component is zero-dimensional, so the finite-type locus has finitely many complex points; its subset over L is finite. The finiteness depends only on the four fixed θ_i and hence on f, not on N or its critical-point heights.

2. Integrality and height. Fix one Φ in this finite family. Choose an integer c>0 such that cΦ(t)=A_5t^5+…+A_0 with A_i∈O_L and A_5≠0. If Φ(z)=n∈Z and w=A_5z, multiplying cΦ(z)=cn by A_5^4 gives

  w^5+A_4w^4+A_3A_5w^3+A_2A_5^2w^2
      +A_1A_5^3w+(A_0-cn)A_5^4=0.

This is monic over O_L, so w is integral over O_L. Since O_L is integrally closed, w∈O_L and z∈Λ:=A_5^{-1}O_L. The lattice Λ is fixed for Φ.

For every embedding σ:L→C, σ(Φ)(σ(z))=n. The elementary root bound for a fixed degree-five polynomial gives |σ(z)|≪_{Φ,L} X^{1/5} whenever |n|≤2X and X≥1. In a fixed Z-basis of Λ, the embedding matrix is invertible; applying its inverse shows that all r integer lattice coordinates of z are O_{Φ,L}(X^{1/5}).

3. Count on one fixed curve. In embedding coordinates w_σ=σ(z), the condition Φ(z)∈Q is exactly that the values σ(Φ)(w_σ) are all equal. The embedding change of coordinates is invertible over C. Equivalently, the rational-value locus in C^r is cut out by the r−1 equations

  σ(Φ)(w_σ) − σ₀(Φ)(w_{σ₀}) = 0  (σ≠σ₀).

Introducing their common value v makes its coordinate ring

  C[v,(w_σ)] / (σ(Φ)(w_σ)−v : σ:L→C).

After dividing by the leading coefficients, these are monic degree-five equations in separate variables. The ring is finite free of rank 5^r over C[v], with basis ∏_σ w_σ^{e_σ}, 0≤e_σ<5. Hence the rational-value locus has dimension one. The displayed r−1 equations have degree five and codimension r−1, so its curve components have bounded total degree (at most 5^{r−1} by Bézout); zero-dimensional components are bounded as well.

For completeness, a fixed bounded-degree algebraic set of dimension at most one has O(R) integer lattice points in a coordinate box of radius R: on each curve component choose a coordinate nonconstant on that component. There are O(R) integer slices of that coordinate, and each slice meets the projective closure in at most the component degree many points; zero-dimensional components contribute O(1). Applying this with R≪_{Φ,L}X^{1/5} gives O_{Φ,L}(X^{1/5}) possible z, hence no more integer outputs n. Summing over the finite family F_f yields # (E_f∩[-2X,2X])≪_f X^{1/5}.

4. Uniformity for moving N. Given any N as in the statement, set

  Φ(t)=N(τ_1+(τ_2−τ_1)t).

Because τ_2≠τ_1, this is degree five; its normalized critical points are 0, 1, and the two other distinct elements (τ_i−τ_1)/(τ_2−τ_1) of L, and its ordered critical values remain θ_i. Thus Φ∈F_f. For any s∈L, z=(s−τ_1)/(τ_2−τ_1)∈L and N(s)=Φ(z). If N(s) is an integer, it lies in E_f. This step removes all dependence on coefficient height, critical-point locations, and the curve or box that produced N.

COUNTING ROLE AND DEPENDENCIES
The saving comes from an O(X^{1/5}) lattice-point count on a fixed one-dimensional locus, rather than the O(X) range for n alone. The constants may depend on f (through L, the finite family, denominators, bases, and degrees), but are independent of X, N, τ_i, arithmetic box, and prime interval. Section 11’s “remove once” order is essential: the same set E_f is deleted globally, so its O_f(X^{1/5}) cost is not multiplied by the number of moving curves, boxes, or dyadic prime intervals. Section 9 uses this to rule out the equality-degree-five curve alternative; after deletion it raises the quartic curve degree threshold by one. Section 11 then compares this one global cost against the tail saving and decreases the final saving if necessary.

IMPORTED-RESULT AUDIT
No power-free-value, sieve, determinant, or other analytic-number-theory theorem is used in the reconstructed lemma. The elementary inputs are: derivations extend through finite separable field extensions in characteristic zero; O_L is integrally closed; a degree-bounded curve has bounded hyperplane-slice cardinality (the Bézout form stated above); and the fixed-polynomial root bound. These are the only standard algebraic inputs. The source’s broader Proposition 1.4 also depends on affine-counting, lattice, Hilbert, quartic-surface, and curve estimates not checked here.

FALSIFIERS / RESTART
A counterexample to finiteness would be a positive-dimensional family of normalized degree-five polynomials with the four ordered critical values θ_i. A counterexample to the deletion count would be a fixed Φ with more than O(X^{1/5}) lattice points in its rational-value locus inside embedding boxes of radius O(X^{1/5}); the fixed-curve slicing bound rules this out under the stated degree control. The most direct audit target is the complete-intersection/degree bound for the rational-value locus, then the claim that the lattice basis is fixed after Φ is fixed.

ADAPTIVE DELETION AUDIT (SECTION 3)
The additional pinned page read for this check is Section 3, page d-2280908f8c11379e (`build/sections/03-affine-counting.tex`), including Lemmas 3.2–3.4 and Theorem 3.1. I found no counterexample to Lemma 3.4; its adaptive deletion bound passes the following independent coupling check. This does not certify all inputs or the entire Theorem 3.1 proof.

Exact checked form of Lemma 3.4. Fix z∈S, fix the other prime blocks, and let the i-th pool P_i be disjoint from them, with |P_i|→∞ and primes in [X^ζ,C_rX^ζ], where ζ>0 is fixed. Fix integers ℓ≥1 and T_0≥1 independently of X. Choose t uniformly from {ℓ,…,ℓ+T_0−1}, then B uniformly among t-subsets of P_i. For the full deterministic path associated with the blocks, let Δ_0(z),…,Δ_r(z) be its nonzero integer patch minors. The claim is that the expected fraction of q∈B dividing at least one Δ_j(z) is at most B_0/T_0+o(1), uniformly in z and the fixed other blocks. Here B_0=∏_j(M_j+1), with M_j the dimension of the fixed degree-≤D_j polynomial space used at stage j; all these bounds and T_0 are fixed before X grows.

Independent coupling proof. Conditional on t, choose q uniformly from B and put B^-=B\{q}. Then B^- is uniform among (t−1)-subsets of P_i, and, conditional on B^-, q is uniform in P_i\B^-. The expected fraction in the lemma equals the probability that this q divides a full-path minor. Encode the path’s codimensions ρ_j∈{0,…,M_j} by

  R(B)=Σ_j ρ_j(B) ∏_{k>j}(M_k+1),    0≤R(B)<B_0.

Adding q to B^- only shrinks each cumulative residue class from stage i onward. Up to the first changed stage, the parent patch is unchanged; the whole kernel of bounded-degree rational polynomials vanishing on the residue class therefore enlarges, so its codimension cannot increase. If the codimension at that stage is unchanged, nested kernels are equal. The deterministic patch convention then gives the same next patch; induction shows that an unchanged rank vector gives identical full paths and minors. The mixed-radix encoding makes R(B)≤R(B^-), with a strict decrease whenever the rank vector changes. Thus for F(t)=E[R(B_t)] under a uniform t-subset,

  Pr(R(B_t)≠R(B^-_{t−1})) ≤ F(t−1)−F(t).

Average over t=ℓ,…,ℓ+T_0−1 and telescope: this probability is at most [F(ℓ−1)−F(ℓ+T_0−1)]/T_0≤B_0/T_0. If the record is unchanged, the paths and minors agree, so a q dividing a full-path minor also divides the corresponding deleted-path minor. Conditional on B^-, the deleted path is fixed. Each of its r+1 minors is a nonzero integer of size ≤C X^C, uniformly over z and all block choices by the source’s bounded-height patch construction. Their product has at most (r+1)C/ζ large-prime divisors q≥X^ζ, since each such divisor contributes at least ζ log X to its logarithm. The removed q is uniform in a complement of size |P_i|−t+1→∞, so this second event has uniform probability o(1). The union bound yields B_0/T_0+o(1), as claimed.

Transfer check and limits. The source’s deterministic choice of the *whole* vanishing-polynomial kernel and first patch is essential for “same record implies same minors”; selecting an arbitrary single cut polynomial would not justify the event inclusion. The bounded-height and nonzero-minor properties are also essential for the uniform large-prime divisor estimate. Theorem 3.1 uses Lemma 3.4 by a union bound over its fixed r+1 stages and Markov’s inequality to select blocks for which each fixed point has at most a ξ-fraction of bad primes. In its stated parameter regime, the cut product exponent checks: if the first i blocks have ζt_j≥a_j+γ and Σ_{j≤i}a_j=E_{h_i}, then after deleting at most a ξ-fraction, the exponent is at least (1−ξ)(E_{h_i}+iγ)≥δ_{h_i}+γ/2 when ξ(E_1+rγ)<γ/2. This is a consistency check of the deletion-to-regular-points transfer, not a reconstruction of every affine-patch, determinant, lattice, or terminal counting input in Theorem 3.1. In particular, no external Heath-Brown/Salberger result is invoked by this coupling proof; cited background results elsewhere in Section 3 were not separately checked against their primary publications.
