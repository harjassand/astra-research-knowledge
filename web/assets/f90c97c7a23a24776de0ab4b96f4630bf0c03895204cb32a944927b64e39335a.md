# Rare-event safety under feedback: theorem boundary and acquisition cost

## Verdict

The strongest relevant Astra result already gives a finite-volume, finite-horizon containment certificate uniform over predictable feedback for molecular jump systems, conditional on an acquired strict inward-polytope certificate. The correct next scientific point is the boundary around that result: it is an operation-time guarantee from supplied structure, while black-box estimation of a frozen feedback policy's rare failure probability has an unavoidable inverse-risk sample cost. I proved the latter in the elementary Bernoulli interface below and matched it to current chance-constrained, robust, and importance-sampling baselines.

No new importance-sampling algorithm or novel control theorem is claimed. The direct binomial lower bound is standard in substance; the contribution of this workstream is an explicit matched-information/cost ledger and the reconciliation with the acquired-certificate chemical theorem. Astra claims remain source-derived, unreviewed candidates.

## Fixed interface and state of evidence

The safety target is a **joint path event**: for a fixed finite operation horizon \(T\) (continuous time) or \(H\) transitions, require \(\Pr^\pi(\tau_Q\le T)\le\varepsilon\), where \(\tau_Q\) is first exit from the safe set under the implemented feedback policy \(\pi\). A distinct statistical confidence level \(1-\alpha\) applies if risk is acquired from data; it must not be confused with the operation-risk budget \(\varepsilon\). For control synthesis, use \(\min_\pi \mathbb E^\pi[\int_0^T c(u_t)dt]\) subject to that path-risk constraint, or the corresponding worst-case expected cost if a distribution ambiguity set is supplied. Report four ledgers separately: operation horizon and risk, expected intervention cost, acquisition/exploration exposure, and compute/model-certificate cost.

The operation model is a controlled continuous-time molecular jump process. Integer complexes \(y_r,y'_r\), jumps \(\nu_r=y'_r-y_r\), and a bounded predictable rate controller generate intensities \(V k_r(t,\mathcal F_{t-})\phi^V_{y_r}(X_{t-})\). The initial count is a supplied lattice-compatible point in a compact positive polytope \(Q\), with fixed positive facet gaps. A supplied facet certificate requires every admitted mode to have deterministic inward drift at least \(\gamma_i>0\) in a collar of width \(\ell_i\). Finite-volume factorial correction, total activity, projected jump sizes, controller-rate bounds, molecule count, time horizon, reaction turnover, and certificate-acquisition work all remain in scope.

Under those conditions, N35 derives an explicit bound

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le\sum_i e^{-\theta_i V g_i(X_0)}+2mV\Lambda T e^{-cV},
\]

using a sum of exponential facet functions, stopped Dynkin's formula, and bounded overshoots. The proof handles arbitrary predictable history dependence because its generator inequality is uniform over the allowed instantaneous rate vectors. This is a finite-time safety result. It establishes neither all-time containment nor model learning, controller synthesis, minimum intervention cost, external generator validation, or stochastic permanence.

For the supplied closed two-species example, the exact bound is

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le 2e^{-3V/25}+\frac{108}{35}VT e^{-3V/200}.
\]

At \(V=3000\), it certifies risk at most \(10^{-6}\) through horizon approximately \(3.77\times10^9\) rate-time units; the corresponding rate-\(V\Lambda\) thinning upper bound is approximately \(2.83\times10^{13}\) candidate events. The source's horizon \(e^{3V/400}\approx5.91\times10^9\) has a reported bound \(1.5662\times10^{-6}\) and candidate count \(4.43\times10^{13}\). These operational calculations make the theorem's probability-versus-compute distinction concrete. In this same finite closed chain, every fixed positive admissible rate pair eventually exits almost surely; finite exponentially long safety does not imply permanence.

The acquisition boundary is exact in the black-box setting. For a fixed resettable feedback policy and horizon \(H\), let \(p\) be the unknown probability of any failure in a complete run. If the only information is independent runs and the model family contains a hidden rare shock with identical no-failure trajectories, then every uniformly valid one-sided \((1-\alpha)\) upper confidence procedure must have

\[
N\ge\left\lceil\frac{\log\alpha}{\log(1-\varepsilon)}\right\rceil
\]

independent no-failure episodes before it can certify \(p\le\varepsilon\). At 95% confidence, \(\varepsilon=10^{-6}\) requires 2,995,731 trajectories. If each trajectory is \(10^6\) transitions, this costs about \(2.996\times10^{12}\) transition evaluations. The executable [safety_costs.py](safety_costs.py) computes the bound and these costs. The proof is at [bernoulli_feedback_certification.md](proofs/bernoulli_feedback_certification.md).

This lower bound concerns black-box acquisition, not a certificate supplied from a validated model. It survives within-trajectory feedback because each observation is the full closed-loop trajectory; it can only be improved by adding structure that reveals or reweights the rare mechanism. Data gathered while changing the policy do not automatically certify a single frozen policy. Physical experiments also consume operational exposure risk: at the risk boundary and the minimum sample size, there is at least a \(1-\alpha\) chance of a calibration failure. Simulation consumes trajectory-generation compute. These are separate ledgers.

## Baselines with matched information

| Information available to every comparator | Strong baseline | Exact guarantee / practical limit |
|---|---|---|
| Full finite-state transition kernel and intervention costs, fixed initial law, finite horizon | Exact joint chance-constrained dynamic programming over the safe set; augment a binary safe/failed state and solve the cost-safety Pareto problem | Computes the best policy for the stated horizon and objective. Schmid et al. give an optimal finite-horizon method for Markov systems, including mixtures when needed, with a bisection/DP algorithm. For the finite count chain, continuous-time reachability can be solved from its killed generator or by uniformization. It has no black-box model-acquisition guarantee. |
| Same known jump generator, but only need a probability estimate | Doob/zero-variance importance-sampling transform using the exact finite-horizon committor; otherwise a support-complete proposal with exact path likelihood ratios | The ideal transform is as strong as the reachability value function and so requires comparable model/solve information. A proposal must dominate every target failure path; weights and event rate-time are charged. It evaluates risk; it does not establish physical model fidelity. |
| Specified disturbance ambiguity set or a data-calibrated uncertainty set, plus same state/action information | Distributionally robust Bellman safety recursion; moment/DRO chance constraints or nested CVaR when tail severity also matters | Protects against laws inside the stated set. The set's coverage and geometry are supplied assumptions/acquisition costs. Its guarantee is only as good as that set and its dynamic rectangularity/recursion conditions. |
| Only independent reset trajectories under one frozen black-box feedback policy, no parametric law or likelihood ratios | Exact one-sided Clopper–Pearson/binomial confidence bound; any valid DRO set must also cover the same Bernoulli submodel | The zero-failure lower bound is exact on the hardest path. With zero failures, no procedure can certify risk below \(\varepsilon\) before the stated number of trials. Importance sampling is unavailable without a valid change-of-measure model. |
| Known Gaussian-mixture uncertainty and fixed chance-constrained optimization structure | Large-deviation-based sampling-free rare chance-constraint approximation of Tong–Subramanyam–Rao | Can make computational size insensitive to rarity under its structural hypotheses. This is genuinely stronger than black-box Monte Carlo when those distributional/model inputs are known, so it is not a matched-information improvement over the unstructured Bernoulli bound. |

This comparison keeps estimation and operation distinct. A tilted test distribution is not the operating distribution. If adaptation changes the policy between episodes, its data cannot be pooled as i.i.d. observations of one policy without a sequential/weighted argument. If a simulator is used, its transition model and its likelihood ratios are part of the interface; a nominal-vs-simulated mismatch is not repaired by more tilted samples.

## Current primary literature and precise overlap

- Hartmann and Jöster, *Risk-neutral limit of adaptive importance sampling of random stopping times* (2024), analyze exit problems with unbounded stopping times and show pathologies of naive variance minimization, including non-absolute-continuity and formally zero-variance proposals with infinite trajectory length. This directly supports charging support and path runtime for stopping-time IS. [arXiv:2402.08476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08476).
- Yang et al., *Adaptive safety performance testing for autonomous vehicles with adaptive importance sampling* (2025), adapt the testing policy during large-scale AV simulation using learned dynamics, RL, and adaptive IS to estimate performance. It is an evaluation method with learned/simulated dynamics; it does not on its abstract alone supply a physical deployment-safety theorem. [Transportation Research Part C, DOI 10.1016/j.trc.2025.105256](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trc.2025.105256).
- Arief et al., *Deep Probabilistic Accelerated Evaluation* (AISTATS 2021), explicitly note that black-box IS can lose efficiency guarantees and propose a relaxed efficiency certificate plus event-set learning to bound event probabilities. That certificate is extra structure beyond bare black-box trajectories, matching the lower bound's information boundary. [PMLR 130:595–603](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/arief21a.html).
- Tong, Subramanyam, and Rao, *Optimization under Rare Chance Constraints* (SIAM Journal on Optimization, 2022), use large-deviation structure for Gaussian-mixture uncertainty to obtain sampling-free approximations independent of event rarity under stated conditions. Their model family is materially stronger than the black-box interface here. [SIAM DOI 10.1137/20M1382490](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1382490).
- Schmid et al., *Computing Optimal Joint Chance Constrained Control Policies* (IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, 2025), recover a Markov dynamic program for finite-horizon joint chance constraints by augmenting the state with a binary safe/failed flag, characterize optimal (possibly mixed) policies, and solve a cost-safety tradeoff by dynamic programming and bisection. This is the correct known-kernel chance-constrained benchmark when the discretized finite-state interface and cost objective match. [IEEE DOI 10.1109/TAC.2025.3546078](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2025.3546078), [arXiv:2312.10495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10495).
- Chapman et al., *Risk-sensitive safety analysis using Conditional Value-at-Risk* (2021), develop tractable under-approximations of CVaR-based safe sets for maximum trajectory constraint violation. It targets path severity and risk-sensitive safe sets; it is not a model-free rare-event probability oracle. [arXiv:2101.12086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12086).
- Yang, *A dynamic game approach to distributionally robust safety specifications for stochastic systems* (Automatica, 2018), maximizes worst-case all-time safety over an ambiguity set and derives a dynamic-game/Bellman formulation; for moment uncertainty the reformulation preserves optimality under stated conditions. The ambiguity set is an acquired input. [Automatica DOI 10.1016/j.automatica.2018.04.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2018.04.022).
- Van Parys, Kuhn, Goulart, and Morari, *Distributionally robust control of constrained stochastic systems* (IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, 2016), optimize linear feedback under distributionally robust chance or CVaR constraints when first and second disturbance moments are known. These moment data are additional information absent from the black-box Bernoulli interface. [IEEE DOI 10.1109/TAC.2015.2444134](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2015.2444134).
- Schießl et al., *Closed-loop analysis of linear stochastic MPC with risk-averse constraints* (2026 preprint), establish recursive feasibility and closed-loop constraint satisfaction for their indirect-feedback risk-averse MPC, with averaged near-optimality under terminal/dissipativity assumptions. This is recent closed-loop risk-control work, but the interface is a supplied stochastic linear system with risk functionals, not black-box tail calibration. [arXiv:2604.11183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11183).
- Engelaar, Lazar, and Haesaert, *Planning with elastic chance constraints in stochastic MPC* (2026), optimize adaptive risk allocations with closed-loop guarantees for linear systems under central convex unimodal disturbances. This is close prior art for feedback risk allocation and should be treated as a comparator before pursuing a new risk-budget controller. [Systems & Control Letters 214 (2026), 106474](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2026.106474).
- Agazzi, Dembo, and Eckmann's chemical-reaction large-deviation results are an important prior-art boundary. N35 itself says its finite certificate is an explicit exponential-martingale application, not a new large-deviation principle. [arXiv:1701.02126](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.02126) and [arXiv:1710.07240](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07240).

The 2025 and 2026 control/testing sources are current primary literature discovered in this audit. Their abstracts establish relevant scopes, not independent validation of every theorem or implementation.

## Research status and next decisive gate

**Result status:** the source-derived finite-volume theorem is faithfully reconstructed from the supplied full proof text; external correctness and novelty are unresolved. The independent Bernoulli statement is proved here and has standard exact-confidence content, so it is not a claimed frontier advance. No benchmark or new simulation was run.

The strongest continuation would be to replace N35's supplied inward certificate with a costed, computable controller-synthesis service, then compare it on a finite closed reaction chain with exact finite-horizon chance-constrained dynamic programming and a robust adversarial-rate recursion. The pass/fail test is whether the synthesized predictable controller gives a strictly larger certified safe horizon or lower expected intervention cost at the same generator information and compute budget, while retaining a valid finite-volume generator inequality. Failure to improve the exact DP/robust benchmark means retaining N35 as a conservative analytic certificate. The general certificate-acquisition step is an open cost: Astra N34 explicitly retains `UNKNOWN` for general all-scale recognition and does not claim efficient synthesis.
