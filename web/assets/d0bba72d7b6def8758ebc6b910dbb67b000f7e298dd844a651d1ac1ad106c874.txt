# N68 query efficiency: a sparse-formula theorem and an exact interface barrier

Date: 8 October 2026. This leaf gives an internally proved, scoped one-query identity test and exact obstructions to treating it as a compression of N51. It does not claim a general formula or circuit PIT result, novelty, or independent validation of either source card.

## Exact positive composition

Fix n,t >= 1. The promised class consists of division-free noncommutative formula trees f in Q<x_1,...,x_n> with at most m tree vertices, total binary encoding length C for all rational leaves, and at most t nonzero words in the fully expanded polynomial. The bounds n,m,C,t are supplied, t is written in unary (equivalently, require t <= poly(n+m+C)), and the oracle returns the full exact rational matrix response to one matrix-tuple query. No inverse gate is allowed. Thus every query is defined; there is no hidden domain promise.

Put D=2t and B=n+1. For i in [n], define A_i in Z^(D by D) by

$$
A_i[a,b] = binom(b,a) B^a i^(b-a)  if a <= b, and 0 otherwise,
$$

for 0 <= a,b < D. This is the N68 affine-action encoding. If v_D(z)=(1,z,...,z^(D-1)), then v_D(z) A_i = v_D(Bz+i). For a word w=x_(i_1)...x_(i_l), set

$$
alpha(w) = B^l + sum_(q=1)^l i_q B^(l-q),    alpha(empty)=1.
$$

It follows that v_D(1) A_w = v_D(alpha(w)). The base-B digits are 1,i_1,...,i_l, with 1 <= i_q < B; hence distinct words, including the empty word, have distinct positive integer codes.

Write f=sum_(j=1)^r c_j w_j, where r <= t, the w_j are distinct, and c_j in Q minus {0}. The full response Y=f(A_1,...,A_n) contains the 2t moments

$$
(1,...,1)Y = (mu_0,...,mu_(2t-1)),
mu_q = sum_(j=1)^r c_j alpha(w_j)^q.
$$

If Y=0, then (mu_0,...,mu_(r-1))=0. The r by r Vandermonde matrix on the distinct nodes alpha(w_j) is nonsingular, so every c_j=0, a contradiction. Therefore

$$
f != 0  iff  f(A_1,...,A_n) != 0.
$$

This proves a one-query hitting tuple for the stated sparse, division-free class. The query is generated from n,t, independent of coefficients. Full reconstruction by the N68 Prony/Hankel decoder is optional for PIT; if used, its output-sensitive polynomial time is charged in the actual response length.

## Costs, including exact response length

Let L_A be the maximum bit length of an integer query entry. Since binom(b,a) <= 2^b and B^a i^(b-a) <= (n+1)^b,

$$
L_A = O(t log(n+1)).
$$

The dense query has nD^2 = 4nt^2 entries and total size O(nt^3 log(n+1)) bits; Pascal recurrence and repeated squaring generate it deterministically in time polynomial in n,t. The full response has D^2 = 4t^2 rational entries.

For a response-height bound, multiply all rational-leaf denominators to a common Q, with log Q <= C. Every expanded parse term has denominator dividing Q and numerator at that denominator of O(C) bits. A formula with m vertices has at most 2^m parse terms, so every coefficient numerator after clearing Q has O(C+m) bits. A word of length at most m evaluated at D by D matrices with entry bound 2^(L_A) has entries of at most

$$
O(m (L_A + log D)) = O(mt log(n+1))
$$

bits, by the standard product-entry bound D^(l-1) times norm(A)_max^l. Summing at most 2^m parse terms gives the conservative per-entry response height

$$
H = O(C + m + mt log(n+1)),
$$

including numerator and denominator. Thus the entire exact response is O(t^2 H) bits. Under t <= poly(n+m+C), both the query and response are polynomial-bit. A naive exact formula evaluator uses O(mD^3)=O(mt^3) rational arithmetic operations; with common-denominator representations and schoolbook arithmetic, O(mt^3 H^2) bit operations is a conservative bound. The black-box oracle's computation is separate from tuple-generation cost. The PIT decision itself scans the 4t^2 entries and returns nonidentity iff any is nonzero.

The 2t-dimensional reconstruction route is output-sensitive: a polynomial-time decoder in n,t,R, where R is the actual full-response bit length, is not automatically polynomial in a succinct circuit description when R is exponential. For the formula-tree promise above, degree is at most m, and the displayed bound makes R polynomial whenever t is polynomial. For a circuit with repeated squaring, x^(2^q) has support one and circuit size O(q), but at n=1,t=1,D=2 the encoded response contains 2^(2^q)-1, with 2^q bits. That is outside the promised formula-tree interface.

## Exact failures of direct N68-to-N51 substitution

First, formula size does not bound N68 sparsity polynomially. Define

$$
F_k = product_(j=1)^k (x_(2j-1)+x_(2j)).
$$

Its tree has 4k-1 vertices, degree k, and exactly 2^k distinct words with coefficient one. The N68 parameter must therefore be t >= 2^k, giving dimension 2^(k+1), 4^(k+1) response entries, and dense query length O(k 8^k log(k+1)) bits. This is an exact obstruction to substituting t=poly(m) into N68 for arbitrary formulas. It is not a lower bound on other PIT methods. The obstruction holds at low degree; the separate repeated-squaring example above exposes the output-size issue for succinct circuits.

Second, the response predicate differs. N68's identity test asks whether the response matrix is zero; N51's rational-formula corollary asks for a defined, invertible value at some tuple, preserving every inverse occurrence. On the N68 tuple for n=1,t=2, A_1 is upper triangular with diagonal 1,2,4,8. The nonzero division-free formula x_1-1 has a nonzero but singular response. More seriously, Phi(x_1)=(x_1-2)^(-1) is admissible and nonzero (at scalar x_1=0, its value is -1/2), but its original inverse node is undefined at the N68 tuple because A_1-2I is singular. Treating the rational expression as a simplified polynomial or silently accepting an undefined query changes the promised problem.

There is also a list-cardinality obstruction to compressing N51's strong interface to one tuple. Let any integer list have h >= 1 tuples X^(j), and work over an algebraic closure containing an eigenvalue lambda_j of X^(j)_1. The nonzero rational-expression tree

$$
Psi(x_1) = (product_(j=1)^h (x_1-lambda_j))^(-1)
$$

has 4h vertices. At list member j, its original inverse operand is singular, so the expression is undefined. At a scalar avoiding the finite set of lambda_j, it is defined and invertible. Thus a list promised to give a defined invertible hit for all nonzero size-s rational formulas over all coefficient fields must have h >= floor(s/4)+1.

For the stronger N51 pencil interface, the h >= B+1 lower bound follows directly: if h <= B, choose one eigenvalue lambda_j of each tuple's first matrix and the family of h feasible order-one pencils L_j(x)=x_1-lambda_j. At tuple j, L_j is singular, so no tuple simultaneously hits the family. These lower bounds concern the stated strong interface, not ordinary zero/nonzero PIT, and do not contradict N51's polynomial-size list. A one-query zero/nonzero PIT theorem and a multi-query, defined-invertible strong list are different contracts.

## N51 source status and constants

N51 is treated as a source-derived, internally audited candidate. The local R06 source claims a universal integer list from unary n,B, preserving original rational-expression domains, and uses B=2s+1 for size-s formulas. Its all-characteristic/all-extension strong-pencil claim is not independently validated here. The source explicitly says the full promised-size generator was not executed and its constants are impractical. The public 24 September 2026 rational-formula manuscript is a separate characteristic-zero/Q source; it must not be used to silently upgrade R06's all-field integer-list status.

The archived R06 parameters already show why polynomial output-bit complexity is not practical execution. For n=B=1, its large branch has w=2, Delta=32, M=64, d=M^2/2=2048; with E0=3M^2(w+M+1), T0=Bd(dE0+1), and K=max(M,BT0+2), the five-grid has (K+1)^5 approximately 4.91 x 10^62 tuples. This is a source-derived cost calculation, not a generator run. The claim “polynomial output size/time” and the claim “usable generator executed” are different; only the former is asserted by R06.

## Status, falsifiers, and restart

* **Internally proved candidate:** the sparse division-free one-query hitting theorem above, its bit bounds, the exponential-support formula family, the direct inverse-domain counterexample, and the list-size adversary.
* **Finite evidence:** exact Python replay for small sparse polynomials, product-family support counts, the N68 singular/undefined examples, and the N51 eigenvalue-list fixture. This is consistency evidence, not validation of universal source theorems.
* **Source-reported:** Lakhani–Mukhopadhyay's official ECCC revision states a deterministic s-dimensional sparse-NC PIT tuple over C with O(s log n)-bit entries. The local N68 2s-dimensional full-response acquisition/decoder is a distinct construction and remains source-derived/unreviewed. R06's all-field integer-list theorem also remains source-derived/unreviewed and unexecuted. The public rational-list preprint is Q-scoped.
* **Unknown:** independent proof validation/priority for N68 and R06, and any polynomial-time method extending the one-query sparse route to arbitrary division-free formulas or to N51's rational inverse-domain contract.

The main falsifier of the positive claim is a nonzero t-sparse rational polynomial whose encoded response is zero; the Vandermonde proof reduces this to a collision of distinct word codes or a singular Vandermonde matrix. The formula-size obstruction would fail if F_k had fewer than 2^k distinct words or a tree larger than 4k-1. The domain obstruction would fail if A_1-2I were invertible or if (x_1-2)^(-1) were not defined and nonzero at scalar zero. A generator with a single tuple that satisfies N51's all-field defined-invertible guarantee would refute the list obstruction.

Restart by first choosing whether the target is zero/nonzero PIT, full polynomial recovery, or defined-invertible rational-formula hitting. For sparse formulas, retain t, total coefficient bits, response bits, and the formula-tree degree cap explicitly. For general formulas, do not substitute formula size for support. For rational formulas, require per-query original-tree domain status; a polynomial evaluation or a block-summed tuple does not provide it. Treat a source theorem, an internal reconstruction, a small exact replay, and an actually executed astronomical generator as four separate statuses.
