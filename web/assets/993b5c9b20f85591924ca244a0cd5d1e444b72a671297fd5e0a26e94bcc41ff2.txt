STRONGEST CLAIM

A constructive sufficient condition, independent of Fisher eigenvalues, gives a blind D-label reconstruction codec for a high-dimensional observation whenever its known likelihood family factors (or is uniformly close in TV) through one supplied nonlinear scalar statistic with a common uniform reference law and uniform second-derivative envelopes. A positive, reference-preserving Durrmeyer kernel on a curvature-adapted periodic partition has error O(D^-2), so log2(D) = (1/2)log2(1/epsilon)+O(1) bits for fixed envelopes, irrespective of the ambient observation dimension or the number of Fisher-orthogonal score coordinates.

This is a constructive subclass theorem, not a solution of the covariance-only N75 information gap, not a Fisher-to-memory theorem, and not a priority claim. The latent statistic, conditional reference sampler, and derivative envelopes are supplied. Discovering them and finite-bit/runtime implementation are not included.

INTERFACE AND THEOREM

Let (X, B, mu) be a standard Borel probability space and T:X -> R/Z measurable with T_#mu equal to uniform Lebesgue measure dt. Let mu_t be a regular conditional law of X given T=t. The target family P_theta has densities p_theta with respect to mu. Assume either:

(Exact factorization) p_theta(x)=g_theta(T(x)), where g_theta>=0 and integral g_theta dt=1; or

(Approximate factorization) there are such g_theta with
  eta = sup_theta TV(P_theta, Ptilde_theta) < infinity,
  Ptilde_theta(dx)=g_theta(T(x)) mu(dx).

Suppose g_theta is C^2 periodic for every theta, and known continuous envelopes A1,A2 satisfy
  |g'_theta(t)| <= A1(t),   |g''_theta(t)| <= A2(t)
for all theta,t. Fix a supplied positive C^1 monitor q on the circle. Write
  Q=int q(t)dt, q_- = min q, L=||q'||_infinity.
For integer D>=3, set delta=Q/D and choose cyclic knots a_j with
  int_[a_j,a_(j+1)] q(t)dt = delta.
Let h_j=a_(j+1)-a_j. For D >= max(3, 4 Q L/q_-^2), let
  A2,D(y)=sup{A2(s): dist(s,y)<=4 delta/q(y)}.
Then the following encoder and decoder have uniform reconstruction error
  sup_theta TV(Law_theta(Xout), P_theta)
   <= 2 eta + delta^2 [
        (8/3) L int A1(t)/q(t)^3 dt
        + 4 int A2,D(t)/q(t)^2 dt ].

For fixed q,A1,A2, continuity makes A2,D bounded uniformly as D grows, so the exact-factorization memory rate is at most (1/2)log2(1/epsilon)+O(1). A fully explicit conservative coefficient replaces A2,D by ||A2||_infinity. For approximate factorization the irreducible term is 2 eta; the target precision must exceed it.

ENCODER / DECODER

For each periodic piecewise-linear hat phi_j at knot a_j, phi_j>=0 and sum_j phi_j=1. Its mass under dt is
  m_j=int phi_j dt=(h_(j-1)+h_j)/2.
On input x, send label j with probability phi_j(T(x)); this is blind to theta. On label j, output x' from
  rho_j(dx') = phi_j(T(x')) mu(dx')/m_j.
This is normalized because T_#mu=dt. All input-dependent memory is the single label J in {0,...,D-1}; total memory dimension is D. The conditional output law can be sampled from the supplied reference disintegration and the label.

On the latent circle the induced density operator is
  K_D f(y)=sum_j phi_j(y) [int phi_j(t) f(t)dt]/m_j.
It is positive, fixes 1, and preserves dt: int K_D f = int f. Applying the same channel to P_theta and Ptilde_theta contracts their TV distance by eta. Comparing the reconstructed Ptilde_theta to P_theta costs another eta, which explains 2 eta.

PROOF OF THE CURVATURE-ADAPTED ERROR

On the support of hat j, direct integration gives its circular centroid
  c_j = a_j + (h_j-h_(j-1))/3.
For y in [a_i,a_(i+1)], put lambda=(y-a_i)/h_i. Only hats i,i+1 are nonzero at y. The first moment of the reconstruction kernel around y is
  B(y)=(1-lambda)(h_i-h_(i-1))/3
        +lambda(h_(i+1)-h_i)/3.

Since q-mass of each interval is delta, h_j<=delta/q_-. The support of the two relevant hats lies within 2 delta/q_- of y. The stated lower bound on D implies q(s)>=q(y)/2 throughout those supports. Hence each relevant gap is at most 2 delta/q(y). By the mean-value theorem, for either adjacent gap difference,
  |h_j-h_(j-1)| <= 16 L delta^2/q(y)^3,
so |B(y)|<=16 L delta^2/(3q(y)^3).

Every decoded latent point contributing at y lies within 4 delta/q(y) of y. Taylor expansion at y, with the supplied derivative envelopes, yields
  |K_D g_theta(y)-g_theta(y)|
   <= A1(y)|B(y)| + (1/2) A2,D(y)(4 delta/q(y))^2
   <= (16/3)L delta^2 A1(y)/q(y)^3
      + 8 delta^2 A2,D(y)/q(y)^2.
Integrating and dividing by 2 proves the displayed TV bound. No parameter, Fisher, or posterior estimate is used in the codec proof.

The monitor changes local cell width approximately to delta/q(t): more memory cells are allocated where q is large. If the curvature term dominates and A2 is positive, q proportional to (A2+tau)^(1/3), tau>0, minimizes the leading term int A2/q^2 among fixed-mass monitors, by Holder's inequality. The first-moment drift term involving L and A1 must still be evaluated; this choice is not asserted to minimize the full bound.

RELATION TO THE CAMPAIGN / LIMITS

The cycle-1 analytic shared-scalar sensor is a sanity instance: its many orthogonal Fourier score functions are all functions of one scalar circle coordinate, with finite uniform second-derivative envelope when the coefficients decay exponentially. A uniform monitor gives the familiar O(D^-2) positive codec. This is a reconstruction of the supplied construction, not a new Fisher counterexample.

The new piece here is a checked formula for nonuniform monitor-quantile knots: centering drift is governed by adjacent gap differences, which are O(D^-2) under a C^1 monitor. This restores second-order error despite nonuniform cell widths. The theorem gives a constructive path only after a common one-dimensional statistic and its conditional reference sampler are known. It does not derive either from covariance, KLS, the posterior, or N75 heat flow. In particular, N75's supplied summary reports a Poincare plateau and explicitly leaves a likelihood-entropy / scale-integrated mutual-information estimate missing; Poincare or this codec condition does not supply that estimate.
