ROBUST RATIONAL HITTING: QUANTITATIVE ACQUISITION FRONTIER
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: quantitative consequences over Q; all-characteristic extension remains a separate local candidate.

SCOPE AND MODEL

Let H={X^(1),…,X^(m)} be a fixed nonadaptive matrix list for n variables, with each tuple dimension e_j≤d and rational entry height at most q bits. Let F_{s,h} be the finite class of admissible noncommutative rational formula trees of size at most s over Q, with every rational constant represented in lowest terms using at most h bits in numerator and denominator. Size counts leaves and gates, including inverse gates. A matrix evaluation is defined only when every inverse occurrence in the original tree receives an invertible matrix. The oracle returns an exact UNDEFINED flag or a matrix value plus independent additive Gaussian noise N(0,σ²I) on its real entries. The status flag is assumed exact; if status is noisy, its channel needs a separate margin model.

The Gaussian model has meaning only over an ordered/normed coefficient field such as Q embedded in R (or C with the corresponding real Gaussian model). It does not apply to arbitrary coefficient fields or positive characteristic. Treat each noisy scalar as one fixed-precision observation word; the continuous Gaussian law is the idealized statistical channel used for the KL calculation. A p-bit response encoding adds its own p-bit cost per scalar. The source-supported version of the hitting theorem used below is the public Q preprint. The all-field integer-list extension in N51 is local and unreviewed; none of the quantitative real bounds below validates that extension.

A RESPONSE-SEPARATION BOUND FROM BIT HEIGHTS

For two distinct rational functions f,g in F_{s,h}, suppose their difference is admissible, as in the standard common-domain free-field model. The formula f−g has size S≤2s+O(1), with constants no larger than the original coefficient-height bound plus fixed small constants. Apply the Q hitting list for size S. At some list point X^(j), both original trees are defined and

    Δ_j = f(X^(j))−g(X^(j))

is invertible. This is stronger than finding one nonzero entry and is why the quantitative response can be stated using the least singular value.

First bound the rational height of any response. A size-S formula has an affine matrix-inverse realization of order r≤S+1. At a tuple of dimension e≤d, the realized pencil has order N=re≤(S+1)d. Its entries are rational sums of O(S+n) products of formula constants and query entries. A conservative entry-height bound is

    b0 = C(S+n+1)(h+q+log(S+n+2)),

for an absolute construction-dependent C. Clearing the denominators of the N² pencil entries and applying determinant/cofactor bounds gives an output-entry height at most

    B = C' N^3 (b0+log(N+2)).

The same bound applies to every subtree value at a defined query by using its own affine realization of order at most S+1. Thus it bounds the rational entries of every inverse operand as well as the root output. This is a crude but polynomial bound in n,S,d,h,q; exact rational evaluation costs can depend on B.

Now let Y be an invertible e×e rational matrix whose entries have numerator and denominator bit lengths at most B. The product D of all e² denominators has log₂D≤e²B. The integer matrix C=DY has entries of bit length at most (e²+1)B, and det(C) is a nonzero integer. Hence |det Y|≥2^(−e³B). Also ||Y||₂≤e·2^B. Since |det Y| is the product of singular values,

    σ_min(Y) ≥ |det Y| / ||Y||₂^(e−1)
             ≥ 2^[−e³B−(e−1)(B+log₂e)].

Consequently, with

    Γ(d,B)=d³B+d(B+log₂(d+1)),    δ=2^(−Γ(d,B)),

there is a hit-list index j for every distinct pair f,g such that

    σ_min(f(X^(j))−g(X^(j))) ≥ δ,
    ||f(X^(j))−g(X^(j))||_F ≥ δ.

For identity testing, take g=0. The universal list therefore gives a response margin δ, not merely a nonzero response. But δ⁻¹ can be exponential in the bit parameters: N and B are polynomial in n,s,d,h,q, so log(1/δ)=poly(n,s,d,h,q), while 1/δ=2^poly(n,s,d,h,q). For the N51 Q list, m,d,q are themselves polynomially bounded in n,s; the bound is still exponentially small in general s and h.

The all-field candidate supplies no analogue of this rational-height argument for arbitrary F: there is no coefficient bit height or Gaussian metric there. The Q calculation should not be presented as a quantitative corollary over every characteristic.

INVERSE-DOMAIN CONDITIONING AND QUERY STABILITY

At the separating point X^(j), the difference tree being defined certifies that both original trees are defined. Every inverse operand is a rational matrix with entries of height at most B. Applying the determinant estimate to each such operand yields an explicit guard

    κ := min over inverse gates u of σ_min(u(X^(j)))
       ≥ 2^(−Γ(d,B)),

with κ=1 by convention when there are no inverse gates. A similar bound holds at any defined rational point in the bounded query region. The bound is weak but effective. It shows why exact algebraic definedness does not mean numerical stability: the guaranteed κ can be exponentially small.

For an invertible operand A with σ_min(A)≥κ and a perturbation E with ||E||₂≤κ/2,

    A+E is invertible, and
    ||(A+E)^(−1)−A^(−1)||₂ ≤ 2||E||₂/κ².

Propagating this estimate through additions, products, and the at most S inverse gates gives a local Lipschitz bound Λ for every gate output, including each inverse operand and the root. If M bounds the norms of all gate values on the tested region, a conservative recurrence gives Λ≤[C(1+M)^2 max(1,κ^(−2))]^(C S), absorbing n and d into C and M. Thus query-entry perturbations of size at most κ/(2Λ) keep all original inverse gates defined; perturbations below δ/(4Λ) preserve a separating response to constant factors. If query matrices are written exactly, κ is not needed in the output-noise KL calculation below, but it is needed for finite-precision query generation, physical evaluation, or any noisy-input interpretation.

ADAPTIVE GAUSSIAN KL AND THE LIST-SIZE COST

For two hypotheses f,g queried at a common-defined tuple X, the one-response KL divergence is

    KL(N(vec f(X),σ²I) || N(vec g(X),σ²I))
      = ||f(X)−g(X)||_F²/(2σ²).

For any adaptive strategy choosing X_t from past observations, the chain rule gives

    KL(P_f^T || P_g^T)
      = (1/(2σ²)) E_f[ Σ_(t=1)^T ||f(X_t)−g(X_t)||_F² ],

for queries where both are defined. A deterministic mismatch of exact definedness flags is easier and can be treated as infinite divergence. Because one of the m hit-list points separates each distinct pair by at least δ, one full sweep over the list has transcript KL at least δ²/(2σ²); repeating every list point r times gives at least rδ²/(2σ²). Uniformly sampling one list point per call gives average one-call KL at least δ²/(2mσ²). The list contributes a factor m to a sweep or uniform-query cost, while the signal floor contributes δ⁻².

For the finite coefficient-bounded syntax class, the number N_f of formulas obeys log N_f=O(s(h+log(n+s+2))): count tree shapes/gates, variable labels, and at most s rational constants of h-bit height. Repeating each list point r times and using maximum likelihood/nearest mean over the finite response code gives the sufficient statistical bound

    r = O(σ² δ^(−2) log(N_f/α)),
    total evaluations = m r.

This is a query-complexity bound, not a computationally efficient enumerator. It is polynomial in the input length only when δ⁻¹, m, and the required arithmetic/response encoding are polynomially bounded. The exact N51 residual-feature learner can be made robust by standard least-squares steps only with another promise: all basis-selection and transition-regression feature matrices must have smallest nonzero singular value at least an inverse polynomial. Pairwise output separation alone does not condition those realization-recovery steps.

No universal Ω(m) lower bound follows from list size alone. N51 promises an existential hit index, but its theorem does not say that a size-s formula can isolate exactly one arbitrary list point. If the class contains m alternatives whose responses differ from a common null only at distinct single indices, then a uniform hidden-index prior gives the usual adaptive bound: under the null, each query hits the unknown active index with average probability at most 1/m, so average KL after T calls is at most Tδ²/(2mσ²), and Le Cam forces T=Ω(mσ²/δ²). Without such an isolated-witness family, m is a sufficient scan cost, not a theorem-derived minimax lower bound.

A SCALING OBSTRUCTION EVEN WITH COEFFICIENT NORMALIZATION

Let ε_h=2^(−h), and use the constant-size formula

    Φ_h(x_1)= ε_h + x_1 + (−1)x_1.

At every square-matrix query X, Φ_h(X)=ε_h I_e. It has no inverse gates, so inverse-domain conditioning is perfect/vacuous. Its syntactic coefficients have maximum magnitude 1; as a reduced rational function, the numerator/denominator pair (ε_h,1) also has joint coefficient maximum 1. Thus primitive syntax or joint numerator/denominator normalization does not control response amplitude.

Against the zero formula, one Gaussian response at dimension e has KL

    e ε_h²/(2σ²)=e·2^(−2h)/(2σ²),

independent of the query entries. For any adaptive T-query strategy with dimensions e_t≤d, the chain rule yields

    KL(P_0^T || P_Φh^T) ≤ T d·2^(−2h)/(2σ²).

If the total cost includes writing each query and reading its e_t² fixed-precision response slots, it is at least a constant multiple of Σ_t e_t². Since Σe_t≤Σe_t², any polynomial-bit-time strategy has KL at most poly(n,s,h)·2^(−2h)/(2σ²), which tends to zero when h grows. Pinsker/Le Cam then gives constant testing error unless total cost is Ω(σ²2^(2h)). Adaptivity and a larger list do not help: the mean is the same tiny scalar matrix at every point. This is an explicit scaling counterexample for absolute fixed-variance Gaussian noise, even with bounded coefficients, constant formula size, arbitrary query entries, and no ill-conditioned inverse.

Therefore the raw exact hitting theorem does not yield polynomial bit-time robust acquisition for arbitrary h-bit coefficients. A natural sufficient normalization is a quantitative response-margin promise: either a known response normalization making every nonzero target have max_j σ_min(Φ(X^(j)))≥1/poly, or for full function learning, a pairwise list-response margin max_j||f(X^(j))−g(X^(j))||_F≥1/poly for distinct targets. Add an inverse-domain guard κ≥1/poly and, for descriptor recovery, a feature-regression gap γ≥1/poly. Under these promises, m repetitions/scans, Gaussian averaging, and robust linear algebra are polynomial in n,s,h,σ,log(1/α),κ^(−1),γ^(−1). This is a new conditioning promise, not a consequence of coefficient-height bounds or qualitative hitting. A relative-noise oracle or an oracle that reports a known scale-normalized response would also remove the scalar-amplitude obstruction, but changes the observation interface.

COMMUTATIVE SCALAR ACCESS

The matrix hit list cannot be queried through an ordinary scalar black-box interface. Evaluating the same syntax over matrices changes the algebra: x_1x_2−x_2x_1 is the zero commutative polynomial, but its matrix lift can be nonzero. Thus noncommutative matrix hits do not give a correct test for scalar commutative identities. Conversely, restricting to commuting/scalar substitutions makes the noncommutative commutator invisible. The constant scaling example Φ_h=2^(−h) is also invisible to robust acquisition under fixed absolute Gaussian noise in the scalar model: every scalar query has mean 2^(−h), so T calls have KL T·2^(−2h)/(2σ²). The interface distinction and the amplitude obstruction are separate issues.

SOURCE STATUS AND COMPARISON

The public Sept. 24 “Polynomial Hitting Lists for Noncommutative Rational Formulas” preprint states a polynomial-bit rational-matrix list for rational formulas over Q and preserves the original tree domains. The quantitative bit-height estimates above concern that rational setting, subject to the preprint’s mathematical correctness. N51’s all-field/characteristic-free integer list is a further local q-Newton/Casoratian and semisimple-descent candidate; it has no real Gaussian interpretation and is not externally validated here. No priority claim is made.

For context, Arvind–Chatterjee–Mukhopadhyay’s ECCC TR23-147 revision 6 gives deterministic quasipolynomial-size hitting sets for general noncommutative rational formulas and uses domain-preserving depth reduction. Hrubeš–Wigderson (Theory of Computing 11(14), 2015) define the noncommutative rational-formula model and explain why matrix substitutions are needed: a nonzero commutator vanishes on scalar substitutions. These sources support the interface comparison, not the all-field extension or the noisy lower bounds.

VERDICT

At bounded rational coefficient and query-entry bit heights, exact hit points give an explicit response and inverse-domain margin of at least 2^(−poly(n,s,d,h,q)). This is enough for a finite robust statistical acquisition theorem, but it is generally exponentially small in the input bit parameters. Polynomial bit-time acquisition requires an additional inverse-polynomial response margin; robust descriptor reconstruction also needs inverse-polynomial feature conditioning. Without such a promise, Φ_h gives an Ω(2^(2h)) absolute-noise lower bound even for constant-size formulas. The list-size factor m controls scan cost and average random-hit rate; a matching minimax m factor needs an isolated-witness subclass and does not follow from the hitting theorem alone.
