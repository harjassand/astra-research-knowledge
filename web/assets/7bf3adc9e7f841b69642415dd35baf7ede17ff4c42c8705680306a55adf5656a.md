**Version:** 2026-10-08 phase 2. **Status:** `read` (primary-source theorem interiors were opened; no proof reconstruction or proof audit). This is a bounded source comparison, not a novelty or priority conclusion. Phase 1 remains in `report.md` and is not overwritten.

## Exact target supplied by the coordinator

For an arbitrary permutation-invariant (N)-qubit state in Schur–Weyl form
\[
 \rho_N=\bigoplus_j p_j\,\rho_j\otimes I_{m_j}/m_j,
\]
assume the sector random variable (M=2j) has a uniform subgaussian-scale exponential-moment bound
\[
 \mathbb E e^{tM/\sqrt N}\le A e^{Bt^2+Ct}\quad(t\ge0),
\]
with fixed (A,B,C). Writing \(\tau=\rho_1\), the target is centered Petz \(\chi^2(\rho_r\|\tau^{\otimes r})=O((r/N)^2)\) and \(D(\rho_r\|\tau^{\otimes r})=O((r/N)^2)\), uniformly for \(r=o(N)\), including the exact maximally-mixed (white) sector weights as an example. The coordinator further states that a spin-variance violation gives an all-separable-sound entanglement certificate with fresh-preparation cost \(\Theta(N^2/r^2)\) when at most \(r\) sites are accessed per preparation, for \(2\le r=o(N)\), and that \(r=1\) is impossible. These are supplied claims, not verified here.

## Result of this pass

No source opened in this pass states the conjunction of the sector-tail premise, arbitrary within-sector states, centering at the actual one-qubit marginal, and the claimed Petz \(\chi^2\)/KL rates. I also found no contradiction. This bounded search does not establish novelty.

The nearest genuinely useful general comparison is Brandão–Cramer’s local-equivalence proposition: arbitrary global states are locally trace-close to a finite-correlation-length reference when their *global* relative entropy to that reference is sufficiently small. Permutation invariance makes its averaged block bound apply to the common \(r\)-marginal. The proposition does not itself prove the needed global entropy premise from the sector-tail condition, and its conclusion is trace norm at its stated region scales, not the target centered Petz \(\chi^2\) or KL expansion for every sublinear \(r\). It is therefore a possible partial route, not a subsuming theorem.
