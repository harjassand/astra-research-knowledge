# Cycle 2: equilibrium does not identify the escape exponent

## Scope

This note proves an exact low-temperature capacity exponent for finite reversible Arrhenius chains, gives a sharp static-information minimax obstruction, and records a finite-sample estimator interface. The theorem is a reconstruction of standard potential-theoretic/Freidlin–Wentzell machinery, not a priority claim.

## Finite graph theorem

Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite connected undirected graph and let disjoint nonempty $A,B\subset V$. Fix state energies $E_x\in\mathbb R$. For each undirected edge $e=\{x,y\}$, fix a symmetric prefactor $a_e>0$ and a transition level $S_e\in\mathbb R$. For a physical transition-state interpretation one often additionally imposes $S_e\ge\max(E_x,E_y)$; this inequality is not needed for the proof. For inverse temperature $\beta$, set

\[
\mu_\beta(x)=e^{-\beta E_x},\qquad
q_\beta(x,y)=a_e e^{-\beta(S_e-E_x)}.
\]

Then $\mu_\beta(x)q_\beta(x,y)=a_e e^{-\beta S_e}=\mu_\beta(y)q_\beta(y,x)$, so the CTMC is reversible with Gibbs law proportional to $e^{-\beta E_x}$. Its (unnormalized-measure) edge conductance is $c_\beta(e)=a_e e^{-\beta S_e}$.

Define the bottleneck transition height

\[
\gamma(S)=\min_{\omega:A\to B}\max_{e\in\omega}S_e.
\]

The Dirichlet capacity is

\[
\operatorname{cap}_\beta(A,B)=
\min_{f|_A=1,\ f|_B=0}\sum_{e=\{x,y\}\in E}c_\beta(e)(f(x)-f(y))^2.
\]

For fixed finite $G$ and prefactors $a_e$,

\[
\boxed{\lim_{\beta\to\infty}-\beta^{-1}\log\operatorname{cap}_\beta(A,B)=\gamma(S).}
\]

Writing $E_A=\min_{x\in A}E_x$, the capacity-normalized escape-rate proxy
$k_\beta(A,B)=\operatorname{cap}_\beta(A,B)/\mu_\beta(A)$ therefore satisfies

\[
\boxed{\lim_{\beta\to\infty}-\beta^{-1}\log k_\beta(A,B)=\gamma(S)-E_A.}
\]

Normalizing $\mu_\beta$ to a probability measure changes neither ratio nor exponent. Under a metastable local-equilibration/rare-exit hypothesis, the same exponent is that of the mean escape time. In general, $\mu(A)/\operatorname{cap}(A,B)$ is a capacity time scale; it is not asserted to equal a first-passage mean at finite temperature for every initial law.

### Proof

There is a simple path $\omega_*$ attaining $\gamma$, because a minimizer can be chosen simple on a finite graph. For every admissible $f$, telescoping along this path and Cauchy–Schwarz give

\[
1=\left|\sum_{e\in\omega_*}\nabla_e f\right|^2
\le\left(\sum_{e\in\omega_*}c_\beta(e)(\nabla_e f)^2\right)
\left(\sum_{e\in\omega_*}c_\beta(e)^{-1}\right).
\]

Since $S_e\le\gamma$ on $\omega_*$, the resistance sum is at most
$C_*e^{\beta\gamma}$, where $C_* =\sum_{e\in\omega_*}a_e^{-1}$ is independent of $\beta$. Thus every Dirichlet energy is at least $C_*^{-1}e^{-\beta\gamma}$, so
$\operatorname{cap}_\beta\ge C_*^{-1}e^{-\beta\gamma}$.

For the other direction, let $C$ be the set of vertices reachable from $A$ using only edges with $S_e<\gamma$. Then $B\cap C=\varnothing$, or else an all-sublevel path would contradict the definition of $\gamma$. Every edge in the cut $\partial C$ has $S_e\ge\gamma$. The trial potential $f=1_C$ yields

\[
\operatorname{cap}_\beta(A,B)\le\sum_{e\in\partial C}a_e e^{-\beta S_e}
\le\Big(\sum_{e\in\partial C}a_e\Big)e^{-\beta\gamma}.
\]

The two constant-factor bounds imply the capacity exponent. Finally,
$-\beta^{-1}\log\mu_\beta(A)\to E_A$ by the finite log-sum-exp identity, which gives the rate-proxy exponent.

## Equilibrium fiber lemma

At any fixed $\beta$, a positive measure $\mu$ does not determine a reversible generator. For every choice of positive symmetric edge conductances $c_{xy}=c_{yx}$, setting $q(x,y)=c_{xy}/\mu(x)$ gives detailed balance $\mu(x)q(x,y)=c_{xy}=\mu(y)q(y,x)$. Conversely, every reversible generator defines exactly this conductance field. Thus the equilibrium law fixes only the vertex measure; the missing kinetic degrees of freedom are one positive conductance per allowed undirected edge. In the Arrhenius subfamily, their low-temperature logarithmic slopes are the edge transition levels $S_e$.

## Static-only minimax lower bound

Consider the three-state path $0-1-2$, with $A=\{0\}$, $B=\{2\}$, state energies $(E_0,E_1,E_2)=(0,1,-1)$, and all prefactors one. The entire Gibbs family is $\pi_\beta\propto(1,e^{-\beta},e^{\beta})$, and the conventional state-energy saddle is $\max(E_0,E_1,E_2)=1$.

Model $M_0$ has $(S_{01},S_{12})=(1,1)$. Model $M_\delta$ has $(S_{01},S_{12})=(1,1+\delta)$, for arbitrary $\delta>0$. Both are reversible with exactly the same Gibbs family, state-energy profile, state saddle, and allowed transition graph. Their barrier exponents are $1$ and $1+\delta$.

The exact birth–death mean hitting time also verifies the comparison. Write $m_i=\mathbb E_i\tau_2$. First-step conditioning gives $m_0=q_{01}^{-1}+m_1$ and $m_1=(q_{10}+q_{12})^{-1}+q_{10}m_0/(q_{10}+q_{12})$. Solving yields the formula below. With unnormalized masses $\mu_j=e^{-\beta E_j}$ and edge conductances $c_{01}=e^{-\beta S_{01}}$, $c_{12}=e^{-\beta S_{12}}$,

\[
\mathbb E_0\tau_2
=\frac{\mu_0}{c_{01}}+\frac{\mu_0+\mu_1}{c_{12}}
=e^{\beta S_{01}}+e^{\beta S_{12}}(1+e^{-\beta}),
\]

so $\lim_{\beta\to\infty}\beta^{-1}\log\mathbb E_0\tau_2=\max(S_{01},S_{12})$.

Let $T$ be any exponent predictor whose input consists only of the Gibbs family and state-energy landscape/saddle (it may also know this same graph). The two models give it identical input. By the triangle inequality,

\[
\max\{|T-1|,|T-(1+\delta)|\}\ge\delta/2.
\]

This bound is sharp: the midpoint is minimax for the two-point uncertainty set. Since $\delta$ is unbounded absent a kinetic prior, there is no finite uniform static-only error guarantee. If prior knowledge bounds the missing barrier to an interval of width $W$, no estimator based on the remaining static inputs can guarantee error below $W/2$ in this two-model construction.

This identifies the necessary additional input in this model class: the transition graph and edge conductance/transition-level information along competing paths. In a non-Markovian projection, the state must also retain enough history or hidden coordinates to make those transition rates well-defined; the theorem itself assumes a Markov state representation.

## Sharp interval estimator for the asymptotic exponent

Suppose the graph and energies are known and each edge level is only known to lie in $S_e\in[\underline S_e,\overline S_e]$. Compute

\[
\gamma^- = \min_{\omega:A\to B}\max_{e\in\omega}\underline S_e,
\qquad
\gamma^+ = \min_{\omega:A\to B}\max_{e\in\omega}\overline S_e.
\]

Then $\gamma\in[\gamma^-,\gamma^+]$. This interval is sharp if the edge-level uncertainty set is the full box: the map $S\mapsto\gamma(S)$ is continuous and coordinatewise monotone; it attains both endpoints, and the line segment between the endpoint vectors plus the intermediate value theorem gives every value between them. Therefore the midpoint $\widehat\gamma=(\gamma^-+\gamma^+)/2$ has worst-case absolute error exactly $(\gamma^+-\gamma^-)/2$, which is minimax for the information in those intervals. Subtract $E_A$ to estimate the rate exponent. To certify error at most $\varepsilon$, edge measurements must make $\gamma^+-\gamma^-\le2\varepsilon$.

Each bottleneck-path value can be computed by sorting edge levels and finding the first threshold at which an $A$-to-$B$ path appears; a union–find sweep is $O(|E|\log|E|)$ after sorting. This is a sharp uncertainty calculation, not an additional theorem of nature.

## Finite-sample kinetic assay and capacity-rate interval

At a fixed $\beta$, suppose the finite graph is a valid reversible CTMC, its Gibbs masses $\mu(x)$ are known, and for each undirected edge $e=\{x,y\}$ we collect its directed transition count from source $x$ during a fixed cumulative occupation exposure $t_e$ in $x$. Under the CTMC model, $N_e\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(t_e q(x,y))$. The exposure is time actually spent in the source state, not wall-clock duration; full edge labels and state occupancy are recorded.

For a two-sided marginal error $\alpha_e$, the exact Garwood interval for $q_e=q(x,y)$ is

\[
q_e^- = \frac{\chi^2_{2n,\alpha_e/2}}{2t_e},\quad
q_e^+ = \frac{\chi^2_{2(n+1),1-\alpha_e/2}}{2t_e},
\]

with $q_e^-=0$ for $n=0$. Set $c_e^-=\mu(x)q_e^-$ and $c_e^+=\mu(x)q_e^+$. For $m=|E|$, choosing $\alpha_e=\alpha/m$ gives simultaneous coverage at least $1-\alpha$ by Bonferroni; edge-count independence is unnecessary for this coverage statement. Reversibility requires the same true conductance from the reverse direction. Measuring both directions supplies a model check and can tighten the interval by intersecting their conductance intervals when compatible.

Capacity is coordinatewise increasing in edge conductances: for each admissible potential, its Dirichlet sum is increasing, and taking the infimum preserves monotonicity. Hence, with probability at least $1-\alpha$,

\[
\operatorname{cap}(c^-)\le\operatorname{cap}(c)\le\operatorname{cap}(c^+),
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{cap}(c^-)}{\mu(A)}\le k_\beta(A,B)\le
\frac{\operatorname{cap}(c^+)}{\mu(A)}.
\]

The interval endpoints are found by solving two finite Dirichlet problems (or equivalent weighted Laplacian systems). This estimates the finite-temperature capacity rate, without a prefactor assumption. Under a verified metastable local-equilibration condition, its reciprocal has the mean escape-time scale to leading order.

If the Arrhenius prefactors $a_e$ are independently known and the barrier model is appropriate, the same counts give barrier intervals

\[
S_e\in\left[
E_x+\beta^{-1}\log\frac{a_e}{q_e^+},\;
E_x+\beta^{-1}\log\frac{a_e}{q_e^-}
\right],
\]

where the upper endpoint is $+\infty$ if $n=0$. Propagating these through $[\gamma^-,\gamma^+]$ above yields a simultaneous finite-sample confidence interval for the low-temperature exponent $\gamma-E_A$. An unobserved bottleneck edge therefore prevents any finite upper confidence bound on its transition level from a single-temperature Poisson assay. If $a_e$ is unknown, it is confounded with $S_e$ at one temperature; acquire prefactor calibration or rates at multiple temperatures, otherwise mark the asymptotic barrier exponent `UNKNOWN`.

## Two-coordinate reaction-coordinate instantiation and falsifier

For heterogeneous nucleation, take a preregistered coarse state $z=(n,j)$: largest stable-phase cluster size $n$ and candidate nucleation zone $j$. Include a parent-phase basin as $A$ and a supercritical product basin as $B$. Estimate transitions between adjacent measured $(n,j)$ states from cumulative occupation times, calculate conductances and the capacity interval, and compare its held-out prediction against direct first-passage / forward-flux estimates. The static coordinate supplies $\mu(n,j)$; the kinetic coordinate supplies measured transition hazards. Their combination is dynamics-aware in a way a one-dimensional free-energy maximum is not.

This is exact only if the projection to $(n,j)$ is Markov (for a finite CTMC, strong lumpability suffices) and reversible at the aggregated level. For a general projection, hidden cluster shape, local strain, defect state, or recent history can change the conditional hazard. Before using the estimator, test the coordinate by committor distributions and lag/Chapman–Kolmogorov checks. A broad committor distribution conditional on a given $(n,j)$, non-exponential residence times, or systematic lag dependence falsifies the two-coordinate Markov model and requires adding a coordinate or retaining microscopic states.

Primary prior art already supports this diagnostic. Yao and Jack's 3D random-field Ising study reports that cluster size alone loses a sharp committor at moderate/strong disorder, while a location-localized size coordinate improves it; their Becker–Döring rate predictions still deviate with increasing disorder and they attribute this to shape fluctuations. This is simulation evidence in that model, not a theorem about materials. The proposed network-confidence interface is a direct construction from observed transition hazards and potential theory; novelty/priority has not been established.

## Scope limits

The exponent theorem proved above assumes a fixed finite graph, reversible rates with fixed Arrhenius edge levels, and fixed positive prefactors. Temperature-dependent exponential prefactors, nonreversibility, changing topology, or coarse non-Markovian coordinates require the more general transition-cost / virtual-energy theory and can change the formula. The finite-sample intervals are exact only conditional on the stated CTMC Poisson exposure model, correct edge enumeration, known Gibbs masses (for finite-temperature capacity), and valid count labels. Nothing here establishes a specific real material's graph, barriers, Arrhenius law, Markov coordinates, or local-equilibration time scale.
