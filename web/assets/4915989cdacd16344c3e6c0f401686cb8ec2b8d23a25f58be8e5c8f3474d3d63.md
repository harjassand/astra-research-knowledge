# Rare-event reaction pathways and kinetic identifiability

**Status:** exact method reconstructed from primary sources; one conditional path-space no-go proved and checked on a finite CTMC; no claim of a new general rare-event sampler or scientific priority. The no-go is a useful discriminator for whether a reported result actually identifies a physical rate.

## Scoped question and interface

The target is a stationary continuous-time Markov reaction model (or a stochastic atomistic model) with two specified basins (A,B). Given its generator/propensities, a full state sufficient for the dynamics, an initial distribution in (A), and interfaces separating (A) from (B), estimate the directed rate (k_{AB}) and the correctly weighted distribution of reactive trajectories. For a molecular application, the corresponding supplied objects are the force field or potential, solvent and temperature model, basin definitions, integration rule, and a state descriptor that includes velocities or history when those affect the dynamics.

The missing acquired inputs are the calibrated kinetics/force field, the initial (A)-basin ensemble, the positive flux into the first interface, a route-covering interface family, and enough independent trajectories to resolve crossing probabilities and multiple channels. A learned progress variable is an efficiency aid, not a substitute for the clock, flux, or true dynamics. Model error and statistical sampling error must be reported separately.

## Reconstructed method: forward-flux sampling

Let (lambda(x)) be a progress observable with (lambda_A<lambda_0<\cdots<\lambda_n=lambda_B). For a valid family, every reactive (A\to B) path reaches these interfaces in order; a jump process must define crossing consistently when a reaction jump overshoots one or more levels. Let (Phi_{A,0}) be the first-crossing flux from (A) to (lambda_0), normalized by physical time spent in (A). Define

\[
p_i = \Pr\{\text{reach }\lambda_{i+1}\text{ before returning to }A\mid
  \text{a first crossing of }\lambda_i\text{ from }A\},
\qquad P_{AB}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}p_i.
\]

Then the steady directed event frequency per unit (A)-occupation is

\[
k_{AB}=\Phi_{A,0}P_{AB}.
\]

In the source notation this is (k_{AB}=\bar\Phi_{A,0}P(\lambda_n\mid\lambda_0)/\bar h_A), with (P(\lambda_n\mid\lambda_0)=\prod_iP(\lambda_{i+1}\mid\lambda_i)). The path-history conditioning matters: the distribution at interface (i) is the one induced by paths arriving from (A), not an assumed equilibrium or memoryless distribution on the interface. The source derives that the product factorization makes no Markov assumption on the projected coordinate. An unsuitable coordinate can cost much more, and missing the sequential-crossing coverage condition invalidates this implementation.

Operationally, first run the unbiased dynamics in (A), count first crossings of (lambda_0) per unit (A)-time, and store (N_0) full crossing states. From those states, run (M_0) independent continuations until they return to (A) or hit (lambda_1); estimate (p_0=N_s^{(0)}/M_0) and retain successful endpoints with their parent links. Repeat at each interface. Concatenate successful partial paths backward through those links to recover the transition-path ensemble (TPE). Forward and reverse calculations are separate in a nonequilibrium network; the 2005 switch study explicitly reports that their path ensembles need not coincide.

The exact conditional path-space route distribution can also be studied with the committor (q_B(x)=\Pr_x(\tau_B<\tau_A)). A transition-state ensemble is the set of configurations with (q_B\approx 1/2), not necessarily a single maximum on a one-dimensional free-energy profile. Estimate (q_B) by unbiased short shootings from held-out configurations; a histogram concentrated around 1/2 on a proposed transition-state surface is a calibration check, not a proof that an ML reaction coordinate has captured every channel.

For original propensities (a_r(x)) and a biased jump proposal \(\tilde a_r(x,t)\) with common support, the exact path likelihood ratio through time (T) is

\[
\log\frac{d\mathbb P}{d\widetilde{\mathbb P}}
=\sum_{j:t_j\le T}\log\frac{a_{r_j}(X_{t_j-})}{\tilde a_{r_j}(X_{t_j-},t_j)}
-\int_0^T\bigl[a_0(X_t)-\tilde a_0(X_t,t)\bigr]dt,
\quad a_0=\sum_r a_r.
\]

This offers a principled alternative for controlled/steered trajectories, but it must retain the integrated waiting-time term. Omitting it reduces the data to an embedded jump path and destroys absolute rate information, as proved below.

## Costs, uncertainty, and numerical scale

The primary efficiency analysis separates the costly acquisition of a first-interface point from the trial segments. In its notation, the cost per initial λ0 point is

\[
\mathcal C_{\rm FFS}=R+\frac1{N_0}\sum_i M_iC_i
=R+\sum_i m_iC_i,
\qquad m_i=M_i/N_0,
\]

where (R\simeq1/(\Phi_{A,0}\Delta t)) is the expected unbiased steps to acquire a first-interface point and (C_i) is the average trajectory steps until λ(i+1) or a return to (A). The source’s Φ is expressed per simulation step; convert to seconds or physical time explicitly. Its ideal independent-trial relative variance is

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\hat P_{AB})}{P_{AB}^2}
\approx \frac1{N_0}\sum_i\frac{1-p_i}{p_i m_i}.
\]

This is not an unconditional guarantee for a path bank with genealogical correlations. The source separately analyzes landscape variance and warns that too few first-interface roots cause genetic drift. Estimate uncertainty by independent (A\to\lambda_0) root blocks, not by pretending every descendant branch is independent. Memory and storage are not included in the cited cost formula: charge saved coordinates, parent pointers, force/propensity evaluations, model training, failed paths, and all flux-generation simulation time.

For direct brute force in a metastable regime, if transitions are approximately Poisson with rate (k), then (N_T\sim\operatorname{Pois}(kT)), relative standard error is about (1/\sqrt{kT}), and a 10% one-standard-error target requires roughly 100 observed transitions. With no events, the exact one-sided 95% Poisson upper limit is (k\le -\log(0.05)/T\approx2.996/T). For atomistic MD, full cost is (T/\Delta t) force steps times measured force-call cost; for exact stochastic simulation, count propensity/event evaluations. A short shooting trajectory costs its full integration time even when it returns to (A).

An idealized scale example makes the asymptotics concrete, but is not an empirical system: choose ten interfaces with (p_i=0.2), total (P_{AB}=0.2^{10}=1.024\times10^{-7}), and physical first-interface flux (\Phi=0.1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}). The rate is (k=1.024\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}), the mean wait is 3.09 years, and direct simulation needs about 309 years for 100 transitions. With 5,000 independent trials at every interface, the delta-method variance of the product is (10\cdot0.8/(5000\cdot0.2)=0.008), or 8.94% conditional one-sigma error. A separate 1,000-crossing Poisson flux estimate adds relative variance 0.001, giving a combined idealized error of 9.49%. This is 50,000 conditional interface trials plus flux acquisition versus (100/P_{AB}=9.77\times10^8) direct interface excursions. The ∼19,500-fold trial-count ratio assumes equal segment costs, independent starts, and no genealogical collapse; it is not a wall-clock speedup claim.

A real published comparison is more informative about practical costs. For the authors’ symmetric genetic-switch KMC model, FFS gave (\Phi/k=1.221\times10^{-2}), (P_{AB}=7.8\times10^{-5}), and (k_{AB}=(9.4\pm0.2)\times10^{-7}k). The direct simulation gave the same reported rate after (9\times10^9 k^{-1}) total time and 8,808 flips. Their approximate step counts were (1.1\times10^{11}) for FFS versus (1.48\times10^{12}) for brute force, a 13.5-fold step reduction in this *fast-flipping, unoptimized* example. The seven crossing probabilities were (0.25,0.20,0.30,0.26,0.24,0.24,0.34). FFS used 1,000 first-interface points and 4,000–6,000 continuations per interface. The displayed flux and probability are rounded: their product is about 9.52e−7k, while the paper reports 9.4e−7k from unrounded estimates. Since this is a nonequilibrium model, (\Delta G^\ddagger) as an equilibrium free-energy barrier is not defined by these numbers. The measurable dimensionless conditional path cost is instead −ln(7.8e−5)=9.46; it is not a thermodynamic barrier.

For unit conversion only: if an equilibrium unimolecular measured rate were (1.024\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}) at 298.15 K, the Eyring relation (k=\kappa(k_BT/h)e^{-\Delta G^\ddagger/RT}) gives an *apparent* (\Delta G^\ddagger=118.63\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}) if (\kappa=1). This number is not an observation. The transmission coefficient/prefactor must be specified independently; multiplying the kinetic clock by ten while holding (\kappa) fixed would shift this inferred value by −RT ln 10 = −5.71 kJ/mol.

## Principled no-go: untimed reactive paths do not identify rates

**Claim.** Let (X_t) be a pure-jump CTMC with generator (Q), and observe only the sequence of visited states along successful (A\to B) paths, conditional on success. For every (c>0), the model with generator (Q_c=cQ) has exactly the same law for those embedded state sequences and the same committor, but its physical transition frequencies are multiplied by (c). Therefore no estimator using only these untimed state-sequence paths can identify an absolute rate or an Eyring barrier across the family {(cQ:c>0)}.

**Proof.** For (i\ne j), the embedded jump probability is (P_{ij}=q_{ij}/\sum_{\ell\ne i}q_{i\ell}), so (P_{ij}^{(c)}=cq_{ij}/\sum_\ell cq_{i\ell}=P_{ij}). The event of reaching (B) before returning to (A), every committor, and every conditional sequence probability depend only on this embedded chain; they are unchanged. Couple the scaled process by (X_t^{(c)}=X_{ct}). This has generator (cQ), exactly the same ordered state path, and every holding time divided by (c). Thus all rates per physical time scale by (c). Since the observation distributions are identical but the targets (k_{AB}) differ, no sequence-only estimator can be correct on both. The missing information is a clock: holding times, flux per physical time, or an externally supplied rate scale. The witness script gives an exact three-state rational example.

This is a no-go for an *untimed path archive*. It does not apply to a method that retains the model generator, real timestamps, the positive flux, or a properly time-weighted likelihood ratio. In equilibrium, the stationary distribution and equilibrium free-energy profile are unchanged by global time scaling; what becomes unidentifiable from path geometry alone is the dynamical prefactor/rate. In nonequilibrium systems, do not call −ln(path probability) a Gibbs barrier.

## Adversarial comparison and decisive discriminator

The strongest small-model control is exact: solve the backward committor equation (Q_{TT}q=-Q_{TB}\mathbf1) on the transient state space and solve the corresponding absorbing/steady master equation for the flux. Compare FFS rates and channel weights against these exact values where the state space is finite or truncation tails are rigorously bounded. Then compare against direct Gillespie simulation on the same propensity model and against a committor-guided Doob transform on toy instances. Charge the exact solver/truncation and committor acquisition; an exact committor is not an equally informed baseline if only the FFS method receives it.

For an atomistic equilibrium control, use TIS/RETIS or TPS under the same force field, basin definitions, and time integrator; assess transition-path diversity and committor calibration. For nonequilibrium biochemical KMC, FFS/BG/Rosenbluth have an interface that does not require a known equilibrium density, while equilibrium-only free-energy/TPS expressions cannot be transferred without their assumptions. A current algorithmic comparator is GenAIMMD (2026 preprint): it jointly learns a committor and samples independent TPS shooting points. Its authors report a 2D system reaching RESS ≈0.8 after about 10 cycles / 107 seconds on an RTX 5070, and test an 11-degree-of-freedom seven-monomer polymer; the polymer reference used 900,000 decorrelated paths from 13.5 million TPS shooting trials. That is strong path-ensemble prior art, but its benchmark is distributional/autocorrelation performance, not an independently validated absolute rate for a nonequilibrium reaction network. Its own discussion flags mode coverage and flow-scaling limits. The 2024 reactivity-biased shooting work similarly learns shooting CVs from TPS and tests acetyl-chloride hydrolysis; it is a strong path-generation comparator, not a resolution of the clock-identifiability issue.

The route/clock discriminator is exact: multiply every input propensity by (c\), preserving all jump probabilities and committors. An untimed successful-path archive must be statistically indistinguishable while (k_{AB}) changes by factor (c). Any reported absolute rate must therefore change only through its clock/flux input. For FFS, also reparameterize λ monotonically and move interfaces without changing (A,B); within finite-sampling error, (\Phi\prod_i p_i) must agree. A missed route, wrong interface history, missing waiting-time term, or unaccounted flux will fail one of these checks.

## Bounded check, costs, and falsifier

I pre-stated the finite-state witness above as the decision-changing check: use states (A,I,B) with (A\to I) rate (a=1), (I\to A) rate (r=999), (I\to B) rate (s=1). Scale all rates by (c=10). Exact rational arithmetic gives (P_I(B\text{ before }A)=1/1000) in both models, and the only successful reactive state sequence is (A,I,B). The rate per unit (A)-occupation changes from (a s/(r+s)=1/1000) to (1/100); the mean holding time at (I) changes from (1/1000) to (1/10000). This witness is a first-passage CTMC rather than a stationary model; the general time-rescaling proof also applies to stationary CTMCs. The reproducible command is `python3 work/agents/chemistry_reaction_rare_events/check_time_change.py`; it prints those values and asserts the identities. This is an exact algebraic finite-state check, not a simulation, general rate-validation, or novelty proof.

The direct falsifier is an error in the time-change identities or a distinct untimed path law under (Q\mapsto cQ); the exact generator algebra rules that out under the stated CTMC model. The practical falsifier for the method package is more useful: on an exactly solvable small CTMC, if FFS’s rate and channel weights differ from the exact solution beyond root-block uncertainty despite correctly measured flux and a covering interface family, the reconstruction/implementation is wrong. A second falsifier is scale invariance of a claimed absolute rate when the clock/flux is multiplied by (c).

Costs actually paid in this bounded check were local CPU for a single rational calculation and one Python invocation; no trajectory model, reaction-rate acquisition, laboratory time, or force-field cost was used. The next decisive scientific gate is a two-channel jump-network FFS reproduction with exact master-equation rates, one deliberately poor progress coordinate, direct SSA, and independent root-block uncertainty; record reaction-event evaluations, physical simulated time, path storage, parent-lineage ESS, and all propensities. Only after that should a molecular case charge model calibration and compare FFS against current committor-guided path sampling.

## Verdict

FFS is the best-supported exact reconstructable method here for rare directed rates in stationary stochastic reaction networks, including nonequilibrium models. Its rate identity requires both a separately measured physical flux and conditional crossing probabilities; path histories are correctly weighted only if all successful partial paths and genealogy are retained. The most useful new internal result from this track is the time-change identifiability theorem, which gives a sharp audit test for claims that infer absolute kinetics from geometric/path-sequence data alone. The proof is elementary and its *novelty is unverified*; do not claim an external breakthrough. The 2026 learned-committor/TPS preprint makes transition-path distribution and channel coverage a stronger benchmark than brute-force speed alone. The Astra N35/N76/N77 boundaries reinforce that finite-horizon safety, event-chain growth, and deterministic attraction do not on their own yield elapsed-time rare-event kinetics.
