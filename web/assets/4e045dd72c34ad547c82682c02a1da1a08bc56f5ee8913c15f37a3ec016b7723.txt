Cycle 2 literature memo: affinity / support / Casimir profile
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: primary-source audit of compact-group uncertainty, Faber–Krahn, and rank-constrained spectral profiles. This is a literature memo, not a novelty certification.

Finding

Alagic–Russell (2008) is a close antecedent for rank-sensitive uncertainty on compact groups, and its operator theorem immediately yields a support-constrained low-energy profile for any finite spectral band commuting with translations. Bérard–Meyer (1982) give a geometric Faber–Krahn profile under a positive-Ricci hypothesis. Urakawa (1984 preprint; published 1986) explicitly computes zonal Dirichlet spectra for particular domains in simply connected compact simple Lie groups. These results substantially narrow the claim: neither rank/Fourier uncertainty in general, nor compact-group Casimir spectra, is new. In the primary sources checked, however, I found no theorem whose constraint is matrix rank (equivalently Schmidt rank after vectorization) and whose conclusion is a low-Casimir spectral-dimension profile of the form Q D(t)/d. Priority for that narrower interface remains UNKNOWN after this bounded search.

1. Alagic–Russell: exact prior theorem and a derived energy profile

Primary source: G. Alagic and A. Russell, “Uncertainty Principles for Compact Groups,” arXiv:math/0608702v2 (29 Aug 2008), published in Illinois J. Math. 52(4) (2008), 1315–1324: https://arxiv.org/html/math/0608702v2 and https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608702.

Their Theorem 1: for compact G with Haar measure μ and bounded operators P,R on L²(G), if P commutes with projection onto every measurable subset of G, and R commutes with every left translation, then

    ||P R||_HS = ||P 1_G||_2 ||R||_HS,

and consequently ||P R||_op ≤ ||P 1_G||_2 ||R||_HS. They note the left/right variant. Their Theorem 2 (with Haar measure normalized by μ(G)=1) says that every nonzero f∈L²(G) satisfies

    μ(supp f) Σ_{ρ∈Ĝ} d_ρ rank(f̂(ρ)) ≥ 1.

For finite G their Corollary 1 gives ||P R||_op² ≤ rank(P)rank(R)/|G|, provided P commutes with point-mass coordinate projections and R commutes with left multiplication. The coordinate-diagonal condition on P is part of the theorem; this is not an arbitrary-rank-subspace bound.

Immediate consequence (deduced here from Theorem 1, not presented by the paper as a named Dirichlet theorem). Let G be a compact Lie group with a bi-invariant metric, let A=−Δ≥0, and let R_t=1_[0,t](A). Ellipticity makes R_t finite rank; set D(t)=Tr R_t=Σ_{ρ:c_ρ≤t}d_ρ², where c_ρ is the Casimir eigenvalue. For measurable S, take P=1_S. The theorem gives ||P R_t||_HS²=μ(S)D(t), hence ||R_t u||²≤μ(S)D(t)||u||² whenever u is supported in S. For u∈H¹(G), ||u||₂=1, spectral layer-cake therefore yields

    <u,A u> = ∫₀^∞ (1−||R_t u||²) dt
             ≥ ∫₀^∞ [1−μ(S)D(t)]_+ dt.

This is already an additive low-energy profile, but its constraint is spatial support measure μ(S), not matrix rank. It holds for the stated Casimir spectral projections (more generally any finite-rank translation-commuting spectral projections). Theorem 2 also implies μ(supp f)D(t)≥1 for a nonzero function entirely band-limited to R_t. The full energy profile above uses Theorem 1 and spectral layer-cake, not merely Theorem 2.

2. Faber–Krahn: precise scope and mismatch

Primary source: P. Bérard and D. Meyer, “Inégalités isopérimétriques et applications,” Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Sup. 4e série 15(3) (1982), 513–541, Theorem 1.5: https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.1435.pdf.

After normalizing the metric, their theorem assumes a smooth compact boundaryless n-manifold (M,g) with Ric(M)≥(n−1)g and a domain V with smooth boundary. Let V* be a geodesic ball in the canonical unit sphere Sⁿ whose relative volume equals that of V, i.e. Vol(V)/Vol(M)=Vol(V*)/Vol(Sⁿ). Then

    λ₁ᴰ(V) ≥ λ₁ᴰ(V*),

with equality iff (M,g,V) is isometric to (Sⁿ,can,V*). This is a volume-constrained spatial Dirichlet profile. A compact semisimple group with a bi-invariant metric can be rescaled into the positive-Ricci setting; a group with a torus factor does not satisfy the positive-Ricci hypothesis. The theorem has no matrix-rank or representation-carrier parameter. Also, Alagic–Russell’s lower bound on support measure cannot by itself be inserted into the Faber–Krahn profile: λ₁ᴰ(V*) decreases as the comparison volume increases, so a lower bound on volume is in the wrong direction. An independent upper bound on the support volume would allow Faber–Krahn to apply.

3. Direct compact-simple-group Dirichlet spectra

Primary source: H. Urakawa, “Spectra of some Domains in Compact Lie Groups and their Applications,” MPIM Preprint Series 1984 (56), scan: https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/883/1/preprint_1984_56.pdf; published as Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 97(4) (1986), 717–723, DOI https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1986-0845995-X.

Theorem 1 assumes M is simply connected, compact, and simple, with bi-invariant Riemannian metric induced by the negative Killing form. If T is a maximal torus, C is the cut locus of the identity, r=d(e,C), and Ω(ε) is Urakawa’s union of inner conjugates of the ε-neighborhood of the torus cut locus, then for 0<ε<r he studies the Dirichlet problem on M\overline{Ω(ε)}. The eigenvalues admitting zonal-spherical eigenfunctions are exactly

    λ_{Λ,ε} = (r/(r−ε))² |Λ+δ|² − |δ|²,     Λ∈D,

where D is the set of dominant integral forms, δ is half the sum of positive roots, and the norm is induced by the negative Killing form. He also gives the corresponding zonal eigenfunctions explicitly. Corollary 1 identifies the first Dirichlet eigenvalue as

    λ₁(ε) = (r/(r−ε))² |δ|² − |δ|²
           = (r/(r−ε))² dim(M)/24 − dim(M)/24

in his normalization. This is important prior art for explicit Casimir/Dirichlet spectral calculation on compact simple groups (and the paper notes the SU(2) case was known earlier). It is tied to a particular conjugation-invariant family of domains and zonal eigenfunctions; there is no rank-Q matrix constraint or D(t)-versus-rank profile.

4. Overlap and priority assessment

The candidate carrier inequality under comparison has different input data: an irreducible π:G→U(H), d=dim(H), a nonnegative self-adjoint L on HS(H) commuting with conjugation by π, its low-band projector Π_t of rank D(t), and a unit X∈HS(H) of matrix rank at most Q. Vectorization makes X a Schmidt-rank-Q vector in H⊗H̄. Invariance and Schur’s lemma give Tr₂Π_t=(D(t)/d)I. If U is the left Schmidt support (dim U≤Q), then |vec X><vec X|≤P_U⊗I, so positivity gives <X,Π_tX>≤Tr[(P_U⊗I)Π_t]=Tr[P_U Tr₂Π_t]≤QD(t)/d, capped by 1. Layer-cake gives E_L(X)≥∫₀^∞[1−QD(t)/d]_+dt. Irreducibility is essential for this scalar profile; in the reducible case the corresponding direct estimate uses the sum of the Q largest eigenvalues of Γ_t=Tr₂Π_t, not QD(t)/d in general. The factor QD/d has the same projection-overlap flavor as Alagic–Russell, but its constraint is Schmidt/matrix rank on one irreducible carrier, rather than spatial support or coordinate rank in L²(G). Their theorem does not directly imply this arbitrary-carrier statement.

Accordingly:
- “A rank-sensitive uncertainty principle on compact groups” is too broad; Alagic–Russell already prove one, including matrix ranks of Fourier coefficients.
- “A support-constrained Casimir energy profile on compact groups” is also not a safe novelty claim; it follows directly from Alagic–Russell Theorem 1 as above, and Faber–Krahn provides a separate geometric comparator.
- The potentially distinct contribution is the irreducible-carrier, matrix-rank/Schmidt-rank profile for general conjugation-equivariant L, plus a sharp explicit family (such as the SU(m) symmetric-power Casimir profile and matching taper). This is a narrow interface claim, not a new uncertainty mechanism. It remains UNKNOWN as a priority claim: the search was targeted and primary-source based, not exhaustive, and it did not establish absence of a theorem under different terminology.

Sources inspected are the primary Alagic–Russell arXiv manuscript, the Bérard–Meyer journal article scan, and the MPIM scan of Urakawa’s preprint / its published AMS record. No secondary-source claim is used as proof of a theorem.
