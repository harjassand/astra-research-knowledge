THEOREM

Let mu on R^n satisfy scalar Poincare with constant P for locally Lipschitz
functions of finite energy. Let A(x) be a C1 real symmetric m-by-m field with

 alpha I <= A(x) <= beta I almost everywhere,
 0<alpha<=beta<infinity, K=beta/alpha.

Assume finite covariance-metric gradient energy. Define the centered Gaussian
observation q(z|x)=N(0,A(x)) and its trace channel Fisher information

 J_q(x)=(1/2)sum_(a=1)^n
        tr[A(x)^(-1)(partial_a A) A(x)^(-1)(partial_a A)].

Then, with natural logarithms,

 I(X;Z) <= P c(K) E J_q(X),
 c(K)=[K-1-log K]/(log K)^2 for K>1, c(1)=1/2.             (T)

The coefficient is dimension-free in BOTH n and m, permits noncommuting
values/derivatives of A, and does not assume log-concavity or log-Sobolev.
Its contrast order K/log^2 K is optimal up to numerical constants, even for
analytic scalar covariance fields and smooth heat-logconcave references,
by revisions/01_contrast_and_scale.txt with K=64R.

This supersedes revision1's valid but looser K^2 upper. It is an admitted
information inequality, not an acquired codec, sampler, or KLS solution.
The balancing matrix below is used analytically; no free computational
oracle for it is being supplied. Exact prior matching remains UNKNOWN.

1. A BALANCED MATRIX REFERENCE EXISTS

Let B range over positive definite matrices. Put

 F(B)=E tr[log(B^(-1/2) A(X) B^(-1/2))^2].                 (1)

F is continuous. Its sublevel sets lie in a compact subset of the positive
definite cone. To see this without invoking a Riemannian existence theorem,
if lambda_max(B)>beta and u is a corresponding unit eigenvector, then

 u^T B^(-1/2) A B^(-1/2)u <= beta/lambda_max(B).

At least one eigenvalue of that relative covariance is no larger than this
ratio. Thus F(B)>=log(lambda_max(B)/beta)^2. Similarly, if
lambda_min(B)<alpha, at least one eigenvalue is >=alpha/lambda_min(B), so
F(B)>=log(alpha/lambda_min(B))^2. These bounds are uniform in X and give
coercivity toward the singular boundary and infinity. A global minimum exists
in the open cone. No uniqueness is required.

The trace in (1) also equals tr[log(B^(-1)A)^2], by similarity. For a
symmetric perturbation H, analytic trace functional differentiation yields

 DF(B)[H]=-2 E tr[log(B^(-1)A) B^(-1)H].

For H=B^(1/2) C B^(1/2), this becomes

 DF(B)[H]=-2 tr[C E log(B^(-1/2) A B^(-1/2))].

The derivative may pass through expectation because A is confined to a
compact positive order interval and B is confined locally to the open cone.
At any minimizer B,

 E log(B^(-1/2) A(X) B^(-1/2))=0.                          (2)

This is the familiar Karcher balancing equation. The argument above derives
the exact existence and stationarity needed here without importing its
uniqueness, monotonicity, algorithm, or convergence rate.

Moreover alpha I<=B<=beta I. If u is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
lambda>beta, scalar spectral Jensen for the concave log gives

 u^T log(B^(-1/2) A B^(-1/2))u
 <=log(u^T B^(-1/2) A B^(-1/2)u)<=log(beta/lambda)<0,

contradicting (2). If lambda<alpha, apply the same argument to the inverse
relative covariance S^(-1)=B^(1/2) A^(-1) B^(1/2):

 u^T log S u=-u^T log S^(-1)u
 >=-log(u^T S^(-1)u)>=-log(lambda/alpha)>0,

again contradicting (2). Thus all relative-covariance eigenvalues lie in
[1/K,K].

2. MATRIX-LOG COORDINATES DO NOT INCREASE COVARIANCE-METRIC ENERGY

Write S(x)=B^(-1/2) A(x) B^(-1/2), L(x)=log S(x). Equation(2) says E L=0.
In an eigenbasis of S with eigenvalues lambda_i, the Frechet derivative of
the matrix log has entries

 (D log_S[H])_ij=d_ij H_ij,
 d_ij=(log lambda_i-log lambda_j)/(lambda_i-lambda_j),
 d_ii=1/lambda_i.

For positive a,b,

 |log a-log b|/|a-b| <=1/sqrt(ab).                         (3)

Indeed with a/b=exp(2t) this is |t|<=sinh|t|. Consequently

 ||D log_S[H]||_F^2 <=tr(S^(-1) H S^(-1) H).               (4)

For H=partial_a S, congruence invariance of the trace makes the right side
exactly tr[A^(-1)(partial_a A)A^(-1)(partial_a A)]. No eigenvectors are
differentiated, and no commutativity is assumed. Repeated eigenvalues are
covered by the divided-difference limit. The theorem is stated for C1 fields.
An extension to a weighted Sobolev domain requires that the Poincare form and
log composition are admitted in that closed domain; it is not assumed for
arbitrary singular measures from bare Lebesgue weak differentiability.

Apply scalar Poincare entry by entry to L, using E L=0:

 E||L||_F^2 <=P E sum_a ||partial_a L||_F^2
            <=2P E J_q(X).                               (5)

This is the load-bearing dimension-free centering step. An arbitrary fixed
log reference would retain a nonzero E L term and would not establish (5).

3. GAUSSIAN KL AND THE EXACT CONTRAST COEFFICIENT

Let Q_B=N(0,B), qbar be the true output mixture. The mixture KL identity is

 E KL(q(.|X)||Q_B)=I(X;Z)+KL(qbar||Q_B)>=I(X;Z).             (6)

For centered real Gaussian covariances,

 KL(q(.|x)||Q_B)
   =(1/2)tr[S-I-log S]=(1/2)tr[exp L-I-L].                 (7)

For every real t<=ell, ell=log K>0,

 exp(t)-1-t <= c(K) t^2.                                  (8)

This follows from
(exp(t)-1-t)/t^2=int_0^1 (1-s)exp(st)ds,
which is increasing in t, with value c(K) at ell and limiting value1/2 at0.
Every eigenvalue of L lies in [-ell,ell], so spectral calculus in (7),(8)
gives KL<=c(K)||L||_F^2/2. Combine (5),(6):

 I(X;Z)<=c(K) E||L||_F^2/2<=P c(K) E J_q(X).

This proves (T). At K=1 the field is constant alpha I and both information
and derivative energy are zero. The defined c(1) is the continuous limit.

4. SHARPNESS AND SCALE-INTEGRATED CONSEQUENCE

Revision1 provides analytic fields with alpha=1, beta=K=64R and references
mu_R having P<=5, Cov<=2 for all R>=32. It derives

 I/(P E J_q)>= [1-exp(-2R)]/[5760 e^(5/2)] * K/log^2 K.

Together with c(K)~K/log^2 K, this proves the asymptotically sharp order of
the BEST uniform contrast-only factor. All constructions and bounds are
explicit. Numerical fixture agreement is not needed for this comparison.

For a family of covariance fields A_T with contrasts bounded by K_T,
one may integrate the admitted inequality (T), provided measurability and
finite integrals are checked:

 int I(X;Z_T) w(T)dT <=P int c(K_T) E J_T(X) w(T)dT.         (9)

For the N75-style capped field I+f_L(T Rfield^2), an actual cap 0<=f_L<=L
would give K_T<=1+L and thus c(1+L), independent of matrix dimension. The
contrast loss grows as L/log^2 L and cannot generically be discarded.
Revision1 refutes discarding it even on the bounded window T in [1,2].
