# Direct finiteness theorem check

Checked 2026-10-07. No source-release retrieval performed.

## Result

I did not acquire an established unconditional theorem proving direct or stable finiteness of F2[G] for every torsion-free hyperbolic G without an additional soficity/surjunctivity/embedding hypothesis. The ordinary FJ calculation K0(F2[G]) = Z, with augmentation as inverse to scalar extension, does not by itself give direct finiteness. This is a proved failure of the generic algebraic inference, not a counterexample in the class of group rings. A stronger universal regular-coefficient FJ bridge remains unacquired; the material below does not prove such a bridge impossible.

## Primary statements

1. Bartels–Lück–Reich, *On the Farrell-Jones Conjecture and its applications*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703548 . Theorem 0.2(ii), printed p.3, gives K0(R) -> K0(RG) bijective for regular R and torsion-free G satisfying K-theoretic FJ. Theorem 0.12, p.8, obtains trivial group-ring idempotents from rational K0 induction only with one extra condition: RG stably finite, R a characteristic-zero field, or R a skew-field and G sofic. Remark 5.2 and Lemma 5.4, pp.28–29, explicitly use stable finiteness to pass from projective class zero to projective module zero. The prime-characteristic discussion p.8 separately assumes soficity. Therefore this proof chain does not close the F2 case from K0 alone.

2. Wolfgang Lück, author-hosted book Chapter 2, https://him-lueck.uni-bonn.de/data/ic_projective%20class_group.pdf . Printed pp.53–54, Theorem 2.81, repeats the same three extra conditions. Printed p.54, Remark 2.83, retains soficity after obtaining an integral K0 isomorphism in prime characteristic: short exact quote, “If we furthermore assume that G is sofic”. The same p.54 now describes Formanek's torsion-free hyperbolic result with a characteristic-zero field restriction. This is stronger and more recent evidence for retaining the missing modular step than the unsupported implication sentence in the July 2025 survey https://him-lueck.uni-bonn.de/data/2507.11337v1.pdf , printed p.4. It does not logically rule out an additional argument using all coefficient rings simultaneously.

3. Elek–Szabó, *Sofic groups and direct finiteness*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305440 , Corollary 4.7, printed p.8: for a sofic group G and division ring D, D(G) is stably finite. This applies to F2 when soficity is established independently.

4. Ceccherini-Silberstein–Coornaert–Phung, *First-order model theory and Kaplansky's stable finiteness conjecture for surjunctive groups*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09451 , Theorem 1.1, printed p.2: K[G] is stably finite for any field K and surjunctive group G. This is a broader sufficient hypothesis, but no universal hyperbolic surjunctivity premise was acquired in this check.

## Why K0 positivity is the missing step

Put A = F2[G]. Suppose xy = 1 and define e = 1 - yx. Then e is idempotent. For right A-modules, yxA is isomorphic to A via multiplication by y with inverse multiplication by x. Consequently

    A = yxA ⊕ eA ≅ A ⊕ eA,

so [eA] = 0 in K0(A), regardless of the K0 calculation. Augmentation sends e to zero. Knowing K0(A) = Z does not prove eA = 0.

More generally, for a unital ring A the assertion that every finitely generated projective P with [P] = 0 is zero is equivalent to stable finiteness. Indeed [P] = 0 gives P ⊕ Q ≅ Q for some finitely generated projective Q; adding a finite projective complement to Q gives P ⊕ A^n ≅ A^n. Stable finiteness excludes precisely such a nonzero summand. Thus assuming the proposed class-zero positivity supplies the desired conclusion rather than proving it from K0.

## Explicit counterexample to bare K0 + augmentation implication

Let T = F2<x,y | xy = 1>. There is a split scalar augmentation epsilon:T -> F2 with epsilon(x) = epsilon(y) = 1. Let e = 1 - yx and S = Te. On the countable vector space with basis v0,v1,..., act by y(vi)=v(i+1), x(v0)=0, and x(v(i+1))=vi. Then xy=1 while e(v0)=v0, so e is nonzero and T is not directly finite.

Primary source: Iovanov–Sistko, *On the Toeplitz-Jacobson algebra and direct finiteness*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00109 , printed pp.2–3 (Theorem 1, Corollary 2 and the preceding paragraph). It identifies nonzero S and classifies projectives using T and S; it also gives T ≅ T ⊕ S. The finite-projective classification and [S]=0 show K0(T) is generated by [T]. The augmentation sends [T] to 1, so the scalar map Z=K0(F2) -> K0(T) and augmentation map back are inverse isomorphisms. Hence T has exactly the proposed K0=Z and split augmentation data, together with a nonzero projective class-zero defect.

This example has no asserted group-ring realization. Its use is limited to disproving the generic algebraic deduction; it does not contradict FJ or a hyperbolic-group theorem.
