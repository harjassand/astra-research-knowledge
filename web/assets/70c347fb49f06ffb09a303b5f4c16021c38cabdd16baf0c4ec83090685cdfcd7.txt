PHOTONIC COMPLEXITY: LOSSY GAUSSIAN OPTICS AND RARE HERALDS
Programme result, 8 October 2026

STATUS

This is a scoped research result, not a validated theorem package or novelty claim. The main N81 candidates are a mode-selective noise extension of the inherited postselection obstruction and a conditioned-positive-P comparison showing that fixed inverse-polynomial all-mode additive noise erases the retained conditional statistic on that supplied family. A separate exact-real rejection theorem gives a rare-herald-free conditional sampler, with a finite-bit TV implementation and explicit conditioning, precision, covariance-certificate, numeric-herald, and output-count costs whenever a centered positive-P covariance has a supplied block-relative certificate. The PC-19 local-matrix extension handles arbitrary local 2x2 squeezing/correlation without placing its condition number in the rejection exponent or in the whitened output cap. Its revised rational rare-herald family has constant conditional output energy and nonvacuum mass 19/36, but establishes only an exponential weakness of the scalar rejection proposal: direct output sampling is also easy on the two-mode, one-photon fixture. Neither result settles arbitrary mixed/displaced Gaussian optics. Separately, the N42 positive-kernel rare-herald sampler has an explicit finite-bit reduction to simple-unweighted perfect matching, but its algorithmic consequence depends on OA113, a newly released general-graph perfect-matching FPRAS that has not been independently audited here.

No result establishes PP != BPP, a runtime lower bound proportional to inverse herald probability, or a boundary for all mixed/displaced Gaussian inputs. No novelty or priority conclusion is made.

1. MODE-SELECTIVE NOISE ROBUSTNESS OF THE RARE-HERALD OBSTRUCTION

Inherited N81 interface. A rational centered pure Gaussian source is compiled from a postselected linear-optical circuit, followed by uniform pure-vacuum loss of transmissivity eta=1/R, a specified binary photon-number pattern on h=O(R) herald modes, and the complete joint count record on two retained modes. R is a power of four and polynomial in the source computation size. The exact conditional herald probability is not supplied, but N82 gives the rational lower-bound certificate

    p >= w = R^(-h) g^(2h) 2^(-q-1) >= 2^(-s),
    s = h ceil(log2 R) + 2 ell h + q + 1 = O(R^2 log R),
    g=2^(-ell), q=O(R log R).

This lower bound makes log(1/w) polynomial in R; p itself can be exponentially small. The source-rounding and optical compiler claims are inherited N81/N82 candidates and have not been independently replayed in this programme.

Candidate extension. Work in the output-mode basis after the passive interferometer and before uniform loss. Add independent classical Gaussian displacement noise of variance nu_H to the herald modes H and variance nu_S to every unheralded mode S, including retained outputs. Equivalently, pull this rational covariance perturbation back through the known passive unitary; uniform loss commutes with passive optics. Choose

    nu_H = 2^(-[2s + ceil(log2(20000m))]),
    nu_S = R^(-a), for any fixed a>0.

Every mode then has strictly positive noise, so the Gaussian state is full rank. The bit length of the nu_H denominator is O(R^2 log R); nu_S has O(log R) bits. The added numerical energy is O(m nu_S)=O(R^(1-a)) (at most O(R)), while h=O(R), 1/eta=R, and the input matrix/circuit description remain polynomial size.

The herald-mode perturbation has global trace distance at most delta_H <= sqrt(2m nu_H) <= w/100 from the ideal lossless-source state before the common optical channels. Data processing preserves this joint herald/output bound. If p and p_H' are the ideal and perturbed herald masses, then p_H' >= p-delta_H and

    TV(P(.|H), P_H'(.|H))
       <= delta_H / min(p,p_H')
       <= delta_H/(p-delta_H)
       <= 1/99.

The denominator is the post-loss N82 bound w, not the larger pre-loss source probability. This is the corrected conditional-normalization bound.

The unheralded-mode noise acts only on the unmeasured side of the herald, so its trace-preserving local channel leaves the herald probability exactly unchanged. It commutes through uniform loss as A_nu followed by loss = loss followed by A_(eta nu). For any state rho of m_S modes and mean photon number E, a purification/Weyl-displacement argument gives

    T(rho, A_tau(rho)) <= sqrt(tau (2E + m_S)).

For the N81 conditional state after loss, E <= eta(h+0.01), eta=1/R, and m_S=O(R). Thus the extra conditional count-law error from S-noise is bounded by

    epsilon_S <= sqrt(nu_S [m_S/R + 2(h+0.01)/R^2]) = O(sqrt(nu_S)).

The bracket is uniformly O(1). For each fixed a>0, nu_S=R^(-a) therefore gives o(1) conditional-TV error. A sufficiently small fixed rational nu_S also gives, for example, epsilon_S<0.01. Combining a 0.01 hypothetical sampler error, <=1/99 for H-noise, <=0.01 for S-noise, and the inherited <0.02 compiler/filter error leaves the N81 constant decision gap positive. Hence a uniform classical sampler for this full-rank mixed family, polynomial in the rational input length, numerical m,h,E,1/eta, epsilon^-1, and log(1/w), would imply PP=BPP. The quantum analogue gives the inherited PP=BQP implication. These remain conditional on N81/N82.

The mode-selective proof is about a specified detector partition: the large noise is on unheralded output modes only. Noise on the herald modes remains exponentially small in this N81 reduction. The all-mode case is addressed below by a conditioned positive-P comparison. Generic global continuity only certifies a conditioned-law perturbation <=gamma when

    nu <= gamma^2 w^2 / (2m(1+gamma)^2),

which is 2^(-O(R^2 log R)). At inverse-polynomial uniform nu this estimate is vacuous. The conditioned positive-P proof below avoids that normalization loss for the N81 family, but it does not establish a universal efficient sampler for noisy Gaussian inputs.

N81-specific false-herald floor. The mixed worker also derives an exact obstruction to applying the same continuity proof directly to uniform detector-plane noise on the N81 instances. Write d for the source mode count, H for the herald modes, h for the number of one-photon targets, and h0=|H|-h for zero targets. N81 has source mean E<=2dg^2, so the input probability of vacuum on every herald mode is at least 1-E>=1/2 for large R. Add the same per-quadrature noise variance nu=R^(-a) in all detector modes, then loss eta=1/R and put t=eta nu. The phase-covariant noise/loss/counting effect is diagonal in input Fock number on H. The herald-vacuum block alone contributes

    p_floor >= (1/2) [t/(1+t)^2]^h [1/(1+t)]^h0
            = (1/2) t^h/(1+t)^(2h+h0).

The pure N81 source has total-number generating function

    G(x)=E[x^(N_total)]
        = sqrt(det(I-BB*)/det(I-x^2 BB*)).

For Takagi values beta_j<=g, choose x=1/(2g)>=2. Then G(x)<=(4/3)^(d/2), and the ideal exact herald of h detected photons satisfies

    p_id <= Pr(N_total>=h) <= x^(-h)G(x)
         <= (2g)^h (4/3)^(d/2).

Since N82 has ell>=ceil((q+10)/2), q>=R ceil(log2 R), and g=2^(-ell), while d,h,h0=O(R), the ratio

    p_floor/p_id >= (1/2)[t/(2g)]^h (1+t)^(-(2h+h0))(3/4)^(d/2)

diverges for each fixed a>0: log2(t/g)>=R log2(R)/2-O(log R), whereas the monitor penalty is exp(-O(Rt))=1-o(1) and the determinant penalty is only exp(-O(R)). Thus one explicit noise-generated herald branch is larger than the entire ideal herald probability, not just the supplied lower bound w. This floor by itself does not control the other source blocks or the retained conditional law; the conditioned positive-P lemma below resolves that stronger question. It is an exact construction-specific obstruction to continuity around the ideal event, not a hardness conclusion.

N81 all-mode noise erases the compiled conditional statistic. There is a sharper result than the false-herald floor. Let B be the exact rational centered pure Gaussian matrix at the detector plane, ||B||<=g=2^(-ell), and let V_B be its exact quadrature covariance. The Takagi values beta_j of B give ordinary covariance eigenvalues (1/2)(1+beta_j)/(1-beta_j) and (1/2)(1-beta_j)/(1+beta_j), so ||V_B-I/2||<=g/(1-g). Add isotropic displacement noise nu=R^(-a), for a fixed integer a>=1, to every detector mode before uniform loss eta=1/R. Since nu>g/(1-g), the noisy state has a positive Gaussian P representation with covariance nu I+(V_B-I/2). After loss its coherent-amplitude covariance is t(I+E), where t=eta nu and ||E||<=delta=g/((1-g)nu).

For the B=0 reference, the herald probability is p_ref=t^h/(1+t)^(2h+h0), with h one-targets and h0 zero-targets. Rescale latent coherent quadratures by sqrt(t). Under the reference conditioned on the binary herald, their squared norm S has exact moment generating function

    E exp(theta S)=(1-2theta)^(-(d-|H|))
                 (1-2theta/(1+t))^-(|H|+h).

The actual conditional latent law has density r/Z relative to this reference posterior, where

    r(y)=det(I+E)^(-1/2) exp( y^T[I-(I+E)^(-1)]y/2 ),
    Z=E_ref[r].

For delta<=1/4, |log r(y)|<=C delta(2d+S). The gamma moment-generating function gives exp(-C d delta)<=Z<=exp(C d delta) and E_ref[r^2]<=exp(C d delta). Hence the conditional latent chi-square divergence is at most exp(C d delta)-1, and TV is O(sqrt(d delta)). The unheralded photon counts are a common Poisson-mixture channel of these latent laws, so data processing gives the same TV bound for the complete retained count record. In N81, ell>=R, d,h,h0=O(R), and therefore d delta=O(R^(a+1)2^(-R))->0 for every fixed a>0. The actual herald mass is p_ref(1+O(d delta)); log(1/p_ref)=O(R log R). The two retained modes are consequently TV-o(1) from vacuum, erasing the N81 encoded constant-TV amplification; subleading probabilities may still encode information. Full proof: outputs/research/photonic_complexity/all_mode_noise_n81_proof.txt.

The global trace-to-vacuum shortcut alone would not prove this. Its exact source distance is sqrt(1-det(I-BB*)^(1/2))<=g sqrt(d/[2(1-g^2)]), while

    log2(delta_vac/p_ref) <= -ell +(1/2)log2 d +O(1)
                          +(a+1)h log2 R +(2h+h0)log2(1+t).

For linear h the supplied bounds do not force this ratio to vanish. Comparing conditioned positive-P posteriors, rather than dividing a global source error by p_ref, is essential.

All-mode noise does not uniformly preserve rare-herald laws. A separate exact counterfamily shows why the H-noise qualification above matters; it is not an N81 hardness construction.

Fix R=4^k, an integer a>=1, lambda=2^(-R^2), nu=R^(-a), eta=1/R, and TMSV source

    |psi_lambda> = sqrt(1-lambda^2) sum_(n>=0) lambda^n |n,n>.

Use one idler input plus R-1 vacuum ports and a normalized Walsh-Hadamard interferometer of size R on the idler arm. Because sqrt(R)=2^k, every interferometer entry is rational dyadic. Add identical isotropic classical displacement noise of quadrature covariance nu I to all R+1 modes before uniform loss. Herald exactly one photon in each of the R idler outputs and retain the signal count. The covariance is rational; lambda needs O(R^2) bits, nu and eta need O(log R) bits, and the dense rational interferometer description has O(R^2 log R) bits. The output has m=R+1 modes, numerical herald count h=R, and 1/eta=R. The additive channel makes the state full rank. Its input mean energy is 2lambda^2/(1-lambda^2)+(R+1)nu=O(R) for every fixed a>0.

Ideal conditional law. If the source has n<R pairs, the herald cannot contain R detected photons. At n=R, the idler splitter puts one photon in each output with probability R!/R^R; all R herald photons survive loss with probability eta^R. Put q_R=eta^R R!/R^R=R!/R^(2R). The n=R branch has mass (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2R)q_R. Since the source tail above R has mass lambda^(2R+2), its fraction after conditioning is at most

    lambda^2 / ((1-lambda^2) q_R) -> 0.

Indeed q_R >= (eR)^(-R) by R!>=(R/e)^R, whereas lambda^2=4^(-R^2). The conditional first moment of the tail has only an additional O(R) factor and also vanishes. Given n=R and the herald, the signal arm independently loses photons binomially, so the retained signal law is Bin(R,1/R). Le Cam's bound gives TV(Bin(R,1/R),Poisson(1))<=O(1/R). Thus the ideal conditional law tends in TV to Poisson(1), with mean tending to one.

Noisy conditional law. The pure source has trace distance exactly lambda from the global vacuum, because |<0,0|psi_lambda>|^2=1-lambda^2. Apply the same noise, fanout, and loss channels to both inputs. Contractivity makes their full output states, and then their joint photon-count laws, at most lambda apart in trace distance/TV. The vacuum reference remains a product thermal state: isotropic additive noise is invariant under passive fanout. After loss its per-mode mean is t=eta nu=R^(-(a+1)); the exact all-one herald probability is

    p_0 = [t/(1+t)^2]^R.

For every fixed a>0, lambda/p_0 -> 0, since log(lambda/p_0)=-(log 2)R^2+(a+1)R log R+o(R^2). Hence the noisy source herald mass p_nu is at least p_0-lambda>0. The conditional-TV inequality gives

    TV(P_nu(.|H), P_0(.|H)) <= lambda/(p_0-lambda) -> 0.

In the reference product thermal state the herald is independent of the signal, whose conditional law is thermal of mean t; that law is TV t/(1+t) from vacuum. The noisy source's conditional retained signal therefore tends to vacuum. The TV distance between the noisy and ideal conditional laws tends to TV(Poisson(1), vacuum)=1-exp(-1).

This counterexample is internally proved from trace-distance contraction and exact geometric/thermal laws; it does not require termwise expansion of noisy hafnians. It refutes any universal theorem that uniform inverse-polynomial additive noise preserves every rare-herald conditional law, even with polynomial-bit rational inputs, h=R, 1/eta=R, polynomial log inverse success, and bounded retained conditional energy. The noisy herald log inverse is Theta(R log R); the ideal one is Theta(R^3), still polynomial as a numerical parameter. The family is easy in both versions and does not prove hardness or easiness for the N81-specific noisy compiler family.

Energy check. For the ideal law, the conditional retained mean is eta times the conditional source pair mean; the n=R branch contributes one and the tail first moment vanishes. For the noisy law, move the signal's local additive channel through loss; it adds t to the retained mean. The remaining herald-conditioned signal contribution is at most

    eta lambda^2 / ((1-lambda^2) p_nu),

because the herald-side instrument has an effect 0<=E_H<=I, so Tr[(N_signal tensor E_H) rho] <= Tr[N_signal rho]. Since p_nu>=p_0-lambda and lambda^2/p_0 ->0, this term vanishes. Thus both retained conditional means stay O(1), with limits one and zero.

Near-isotropic positive-P covariance gives a conditional sampler with no rare-herald factor. This is an algorithmic result distinct from the preceding N81 statistic-erasure comparison. Suppose a centered d-mode Gaussian has, after loss, a strictly positive rational P covariance C=tA in quadrature coordinates, with t>0 and a supplied exact rational certificate

    (1-eps_bar)I <= A <= (1+eps_bar)I, 0<=eps_bar<=1/4.

For any specified PNR pattern k_j on s herald modes, write kappa=sum_j k_j (its numerical value is charged). Set c=1+eps_bar and draw a proposal coherent vector x from N(0,ct I), conditioned on that exact herald. This posterior is explicit: each herald radius squared is Gamma(k_j+1, scale 2ct/(1+ct)); every other mode has Gamma(1, scale 2ct); phases are independent uniform. In y=x/sqrt(t), compare the target Gaussian prior f_A with the proposal prior f_c:

    f_A(y)/f_c(y)=M exp(-y^T D y/2),
    M=c^d/sqrt(det A),  D=A^(-1)-c^(-1)I >=0.

Accept a proposal with probability exp(-y^T D y/2), then sample remaining counts as independent Poissons with means t||y_j||^2/2. The determinant envelope M cancels in conditional normalization and is not needed by the algorithm. The accepted latent density is exactly the target P posterior, so the exact-real count sampler is exact and does not query p_H.

The proposal posterior has E||Y||^2=2c(d-s)+2c(s+kappa)/(1+ct)<=2c(d+kappa). The certificate gives ||D||<=2eps_bar/(1-eps_bar^2), so Jensen yields

    alpha = p_H/(M q_H) >= exp(-C(d+kappa)eps_bar),

where q_H=(ct)^kappa/(1+ct)^(kappa+s) is the proposal herald mass. Thus expected proposal trials are at most exp(C(d+kappa)eps_bar), independent of 1/p_H. The expected proposal count is polynomial when (d+kappa)eps_bar=O(log L); a polynomial-bit runtime in L additionally requires numeric kappa, M_out, eps_out^-1, and log(xi^-1) polynomially bounded in L. The proof allows arbitrary phase correlations in A; it uses only isotropic domination, not an entrywise-positive Bargmann kernel.

This is an exact-real rejection identity, not a finite-bit claim by itself. The leaf supplies a separate coupled dyadic implementation: truncate proposal radius at T=4c[(d+kappa)log 2+log(8/rho)], couple the gamma/phase draws with Euclidean error O(rho/[1+sqrt(eps_bar)+sqrt(t)]), approximate the acceptance exponential and rational-rate Poisson kernels to O(rho), and choose rho=eps_out/(C Lambda), Lambda>=exp(C(d+kappa)eps_bar). Normalization costs at most Lambda, while a cap O(Lambda log(1/xi)) makes timeout at most xi. The resulting output law is within eps_out+xi TV, with time polynomial in exact input bit length L, d, numeric kappa, the explicit output-mean cutoff M_out=tT/2, eps_out^-1, log(xi^-1), and Lambda. It is not polynomial in the bit length of an arbitrarily large binary herald count: the stated gamma implementation takes O(d+kappa) exponential variates. N81's binary herald has kappa=h<=d=Theta(R), and its t=R^(-(a+1)) gives polynomial M_out; its requested accuracy budgets can also be chosen polynomially bounded in L, so these conditions hold for that application.

The covariance certificate is part of the interface. Exact rational LDL checks both PSD differences, and rational elimination computes A^-1; these operations are polynomial in d and matrix bit length L. For N81, form A from rational B, noise nu=R^-a, and loss eta=1/R; the analytic bound eps_bar=g/((1-g)nu) provides the near-isotropic enclosure, which is checked by exact rational arithmetic. Since g<=2^-ell with ell>=R, d,kappa=O(R), and fixed a>=1, (d+kappa)eps_bar=O(R^(a+1)2^-R)=o(1), hence the exact-real expected proposal count is 1+o(1). The noise-only herald mass is p_ref=t^h/(1+t)^(2h+h0), t=R^(-(a+1)); the N81 posterior comparison gives p_H=p_ref exp(O(d eps_bar)), so log(1/p_H)=O(R log R) though p_H is exponentially small. Added input energy is O(d nu)=O(R^(1-a)), post-loss total mean energy is O(R^(-a)), and the unheralded conditional output count mean is O(td)=O(R^(-a)); the leaf derives the latter using its likelihood-ratio second moment and Cauchy-Schwarz. The finite-bit truncation charges M_out=tT/2 explicitly. This sampler targets the all-mode-noisy conditional law, which is vacuum-like and has lost the N81 encoded constant-TV amplification; subleading probabilities may still encode information. It does not restore the ideal amplification or imply hardness/easiness for arbitrary noisy inputs. If instead one physically produces and discards unsuccessful optical events, the expected number of experimental runs remains 1/p_H.

Primary-method comparison checked 8 October 2026. Rahimi-Keshari, Ralph, and Caves, [arXiv:1511.06526](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06526), give positive-quasiprobability sufficient conditions for complete output sampling and analyze loss/noise thresholds for ordinary boson-sampling outputs; they do not state a rare-event conditioning bound. Quesada et al., [arXiv:1905.07011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07011), calculate conditional heralded-state Fock matrix elements and the herald probability through loop hafnians, with costs exponential in modes/occupations. Quesada and Arrazola, [arXiv:1908.08068](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08068), explicitly give sequential sampling from Gaussian marginal probabilities with runtime O(m N^3 2^N) after N detected photons. By inference from their sequential chain rule, fixing the herald outcomes as the prefix and sampling remaining modes with ratios p(H,o_<j,n)/p(H,o_<j) gives a conditional adaptation; it avoids an inherent 1/p_H repetition cost but remains exponential in total detected photon number. Oh et al., [arXiv:2306.03709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03709), analyze a tensor-network approximation for lossy experimental GBS and mention postselected state preparation as a possible application, without analyzing that interface. This comparison makes the candidate advance narrower: the exact-real proposal overhead is polynomial when (d+kappa)eps_bar=O(log L); a polynomial-bit runtime in L additionally requires numeric kappa, M_out, and accuracy budgets to be polynomially bounded. Direct adaptation, bit precision, and priority remain independent audit gates.

2. POSITIVE-KERNEL HERALD SAMPLING AND ITS COUNTING DEPENDENCY

The N42 candidate interface is a supplied finite-bit physical Gaussian density kernel

    R(z,w) = c exp(1/2 xi^T K xi + ell^T xi),  xi=(z,w),

whose coefficients K and ell are real and entrywise nonnegative in a supplied number-preserving phase gauge, with the required Hermiticity/physicality conditions. It allows mixedness, displacement, collisions, and an arbitrary exact photon-count herald, provided the final supplied kernel remains in this cone. The target is the full remaining count vector conditioned on the herald. The source-derived time bound is polynomial in input-bit length, mode count, numerical herald count h, numerical mean energy E, and inverse TV error; it has no 1/p_H factor. This is not a physical repeat-until-herald sampler.

The mechanism is relative branch counting, not additive approximation of a rare probability. Positive loop-hafnian coefficients are decomposed into pair choices and singleton activities. Fixed-pair-number terms are padded to perfect matchings by labeled dummy vertices; a common rational scaling clears denominators. Every positive integer weighted edge W is implemented by an explicit simple graph with O(log W) vertices and O(log^2 W) edges: a bit-indexed DAG has exactly W source-to-target paths, and the bipartite L/R split with diagonal loop edges and arc edges gives port signature (1,W,0,0). Exact enumeration for W=1,...,20 matches the signatures in the independently runnable outputs/research/photonic_complexity/verify_weight_gadget.py and weight_gadget_check.json; this is a finite sanity test, not proof validation. Zero singleton support is tested separately; zero activities are replaced by a sufficiently small dyadic value with an explicit precision budget so their spurious mass is below the target relative error. Summing nonnegative coefficient estimates and sampling their normalized branches avoids an inverse-p_H rejection factor.

The source-derived positive-kernel energy bound is E[N_remaining | H] <= F_+(E,h)=3h+6(2h+1)E, where E is the input mean energy and h the numeric total herald count. For a cap T, the leaf proves

    sum_i Pr[X_i>T | H] <= E[sum_i X_i | H]/(T+1) <= F_+(E,h)/(T+1).

This pointwise count bound yields a whole-record truncation error without an extra mode-count factor. Choosing T=O(F_+/epsilon_tail) leaves at most m(T+1) candidate branch queries per path, with repeated-index graphs polynomial in numerical m,h,E and T. Together with relative branch counts and finite-bit confidence/rounding budgets, this is the claimed way to keep alphabet truncation polynomial without an inverse-herald factor.

The separate dummy-padding and integer-weight gadget do not come from OA113; they are reconstructed in the leaf note. The earlier Štefankovič–Vigoda–Wilmes paper (2017) states that the general-graph case remained open then. OA113 is a primary-source claim, not independently audited or formally replayed here. 3. EXACT POSITIVITY BOUNDARY IN A CENTERED THERMAL SUBCLASS

For a centered gauge-invariant Gaussian state with one-particle covariance N>=0, define Q=N(I+N)^(-1). In the fixed detector phase frame its Bargmann density kernel is det(I-Q) exp(z^T Q bar(w)). Every coefficient is real nonnegative iff every entry of Q is real nonnegative: sufficiency follows by expanding the exponential, while the z_i bar(w_j) coefficient divided by the constant coefficient equals Q_ij. If local phase rotations are allowed, a nonnegative gauge exists exactly when every cycle phase product of the nonzero-entry graph is one.

This is an exact boundary for the N42 coefficient-positive representation in this subclass, not a complexity boundary. For example, N=[[2,1,0],[1,2,1],[0,1,2]] is positive definite and has a positive classical P representation. After any uniform loss eta>0, Q_out=eta N(I+eta N)^(-1) has Q_12,Q_23>0 but Q_13<0; the triangle phase product is negative and cannot be removed by local rephasing. Yet unconditional counts are easy by sampling a complex Gaussian amplitude and then independent Poisson counts. Thus covariance/P positivity does not imply the N42 kernel promise, and failure of that promise is not hardness.

Conversely, positive P is not necessary for easy conditional sampling: the leaf's lossy two-mode squeezed example has a direct exact heralded count sampler despite lying outside the positive-P regime. Whether P positivity alone suffices for all specified rare heralds remains UNKNOWN.

4. BOUNDARIES THAT SURVIVED THE AUDIT

Fixed vertical-edge sectors. The exact doubled-graph identity maps the complete variable-cardinality graph-GBS law to weighted perfect matchings on G square K2. Fixing occupied-set cardinality is equivalent to fixing one edge color inside a perfect matching. Anand et al.'s sampler makes perfect matchings common among all matchings; it gives no lower bound on a chosen vertical-edge count. For m disjoint edges and c=1/2, the sector |S|=2 has mass (m/16)(16/17)^m, so rejection is exponential. The sector is trivial to sample directly; this only refutes a generic polynomial-cost conditioning inference. Fixed-sector sampling or certified coefficient extraction with polynomial condition number is still open.

Unconditional versus conditional approximation. For a herald of mass p, generic conditioning amplifies full-law TV error by order 1/p, and rejection requires expected 1/p runs. A two-black-box sample-only argument gives Omega(p^-1 log(1/epsilon)) samples. This does not lower-bound algorithms given a structured optical description: N42's relative-count route exploits that structure. N81 only rules out a fixed-accuracy sampler polynomial in log(1/p) under its collapse candidate; it does not rule out polynomial dependence on 1/p.

Rare-event resource counterexample. A two-mode squeezed source and a product thermal source can be arranged to have equal input energy and exactly equal exponentially small herald probability after the same loss, while their conditional outputs converge respectively to Poisson(1) and vacuum, with TV tending to 1-e^-1. This refutes covariance/energy/success-probability-only reset rules; both examples are easy to sample from their descriptions and imply no hardness.

Phases. The four-mode C4 witness has exact cancellation in one phase choice and a nonzero all-one hafnian after magnitude replacement. A negative cycle flux is gauge-invariant. Multiple small-phase islands can interfere destructively unless their centers are aligned or the cancellation mass is bounded. An exact phase-twirled bipartite sampler is derived under weighted phase orthogonality and phase-independent normalization, including collisions and without inverse-herald cost; finite-bit implementation remains unresolved, and the fixed-phase general case is outside it.

Practical spectral simulation. A candidate compression inequality is

    TV(P_T,P_Tk) <= sqrt(1-(1-W_k)_+^2),
    W_k = sum_{i>k} sigma_i^2 <n_{v_i}>.

Composed with N71 it gives a rank-compressed reset guarantee controlled jointly by discarded output energy and retained sixth-power singular-value mass. Nine one-mode fixtures passed, plus vacuum/thermal generating-function checks. These are arithmetic diagnostics only; the finite-bit N73 engine and combined sampler remain unexecuted. This practical unconditional result does not sample a rare fixed herald.
