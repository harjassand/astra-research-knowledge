# Proof record: finite-stage CTMC discrimination

## Scope and status

This note is about an abstract two-hypothesis Markov discrimination model. The results below are exact conditional theorems for the stated model; the underlying tools are standard likelihood-ratio, data-processing, and CTMC path-likelihood arguments. They do not establish a new general information-theory theorem.

## Model A: all-pass N-stage chain

Let the hypotheses be `H=h`, `h in {0,1}`, with `0 < p_h < 1`, `p_0 != p_1`, and a common event rate `lambda > 0`. There are N ordered event slots. At each slot, after an independent `Exp(lambda)` holding time, a mark is pass with probability `p_h` and fail with probability `1-p_h`, independently across slots conditional on H.

The finite CTMC stores only the slot index and an all-passes flag. Its states are the initial state `(0,good)` and `(i,good),(i,bad)` for `1 <= i <= N`; the two states at slot N are absorbing. From `(i,good)`, `i < N`, the generator has rates `lambda*p_h` to `(i+1,good)` and `lambda*(1-p_h)` to `(i+1,bad)`. From `(i,bad)`, it has rate `lambda` to `(i+1,bad)`. Thus every design uses exactly N jumps, and its terminal output is the single bit `Y=1` iff all N marks passed.

The probability of a positive output is

`P_h(Y=1) = p_h^N`.

So the likelihood ratio on the rare positive output is `(p_0/p_1)^N` (or its reciprocal, depending on the ratio convention). This is an exponential endpoint-likelihood amplification, but it does not imply an increase in total information.

For the physical all-pass CTMC, marks after the first failure are ignored and are not represented in its state path. If the entire CTMC state path is observed, including the failure slot, its KL is `J_N(p_0)*d`, where `J_N(p)=sum_{i=0}^{N-1}p^i`: slot i contributes d only when the chain is still in the good state, which has H=0 probability `p_0^i`. The terminal bit has no more information than this state path and loses information strictly for N>=2, p0 != p1.

For strictness on the physical state path, its possible outputs are failure at slot `j+1` for `j=0,...,N-1`, or success at N. The likelihood ratios of failure at slots 1 and 2 differ by the factor `p_0/p_1`, and both events have positive probability. The terminal bit merges those two failure outputs, so the log-sum inequality is strict.

The number of reachable CTMC states is `2N+1`. The total elapsed time is Erlang with shape N and rate `lambda`, independent of H, so

`E_h[T] = N/lambda`, `Var_h(T) = N/lambda^2`.

Only one terminal output is read. If each transition must be externally observed, the path-observation budget is N; the internal endpoint design itself requires one terminal readout.

## Theorem A: exact information in full marks and endpoint loss

Write

`d = D(Ber(p_0) || Ber(p_1)) = p_0 log(p_0/p_1) + (1-p_0) log((1-p_0)/(1-p_1))`.

Let `Z` be the complete event-mark path through the N slots and let `Y` be the all-pass endpoint bit. Then

1. `D(P_0^Z || P_1^Z) = N*d` for the full N-mark input record.
2. `D(P_0^Y || P_1^Y) = D(Ber(p_0^N) || Ber(p_1^N)) <= N*d`.
3. For `N >= 2` and `p_0 != p_1`, the endpoint inequality is strict.
4. Every terminal decision based on Y is a decision based on Z, so the full-mark experiment Blackwell-dominates the endpoint experiment for every prior and every decision loss.

### Proof

For a mark vector `z=(z_1,...,z_N)`,

`P_h(z) = p_h^{K(z)} (1-p_h)^{N-K(z)}`, where `K(z)=sum_i z_i`.

The path log-likelihood ratio is the sum of the N one-mark log-likelihood ratios. Taking expectation under H=0 gives `N*d`. The endpoint is the deterministic map `Y=1{K=N}`. The KL data-processing inequality gives the second claim. Equivalently, the KL chain rule writes

`D(P_0^Z || P_1^Z) = D(P_0^Y || P_1^Y) + sum_y P_0(Y=y) D(P_0(Z|Y=y) || P_1(Z|Y=y))`.

For N>=2, conditioning on `Y=0` leaves at least two mark patterns with different likelihood ratios: the patterns with zero and with one pass have likelihood-ratio ratio `p_0/p_1 != 1`, and both have positive probability. Hence the conditional KL term is positive and the inequality is strict. Blackwell dominance follows because Y is a deterministic function of the complete path: any rule using Y can be run by first observing Z and computing Y. QED.

The holding times add zero KL here: the total rate is `lambda` at every transient stage under both hypotheses, so all N inter-event times have the same independent `Exp(lambda)` law under H=0 and H=1. The physical all-pass CTMC state path is a coarsening of this full mark record because it stops distinguishing marks once its flag is bad; its exact KL is `J_N(p_0)*d` as stated above.

## Stopped variant and exact expected-jump accounting

If failure instead terminates immediately, the number of jumps L is `min(G,N)`, where G is the first failed slot plus one. Under H=h,

`E_h[L] = J_N(p_h) = sum_{i=0}^{N-1} p_h^i = (1-p_h^N)/(1-p_h)`.

The full stopped path has

`D(P_0^path || P_1^path) = J_N(p_0)*d`.

Proof: the event at slot i is observed only if the first i marks passed, an H=0 event of probability `p_0^i`; conditional on reaching it, its mark contributes KL d. Summing gives the formula. Expected time is `J_N(p_h)/lambda` under H=h. The terminal success/failure bit still has KL `D(Ber(p_0^N)||Ber(p_1^N))`, strictly smaller when N>=2. This stopped form gives the exact statement “one-step KL per expected active jump is d” for the path, while the endpoint loses information.

## Model B: fixed-N likelihood-ratio counter CTMC

This is the matched one-readout baseline. At slot i, the state stores the number k of passes so far. For `0 <= i < N` and `0 <= k <= i`, event transitions occur at total rate `lambda`:

`(i,k) -> (i+1,k+1)` at rate `lambda*p_h`,

`(i,k) -> (i+1,k)` at rate `lambda*(1-p_h)`.

At the final slot, the transition goes to terminal output A iff `K >= c`, and to terminal output R otherwise. This is a time-homogeneous finite CTMC on a layered state space. It makes exactly N jumps; elapsed time is Erlang(N,lambda), with mean N/lambda and variance N/lambda^2; the terminal output costs one readout. For fixed threshold c, the number of states is

`S_N = sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(i+1) + 2 = N(N+1)/2 + 2`.

The terminal output has

`P_h(A) = sum_{k=c}^N binom(N,k) p_h^k (1-p_h)^(N-k)`.

## Theorem B: optimal terminal binary test for fixed event budget

Assume `p_1 > p_0`. Among all possibly randomized binary decisions based on N marks with false-positive probability at most alpha under H=0, the rule that accepts H=1 for large K (with randomization at at most one boundary value) maximizes power under H=1. The counter CTMC above realizes each deterministic threshold; adding a hypothesis-independent final coin realizes the boundary-randomized points.

### Proof

For a mark vector z, the likelihood ratio is

`L(z)=P_1(z)/P_0(z) = (p_1/p_0)^K * ((1-p_1)/(1-p_0))^(N-K)`.

When `p_1>p_0`, `L` is strictly increasing in K. The Neyman-Pearson lemma therefore places the rejection region at the largest K values, with randomization only at a tie at the boundary. The state variable K is sufficient because the likelihood depends on the full mark vector only through K. The CTMC counter tracks K and applies exactly this decision at slot N. QED.

The all-pass chain is the special threshold `c=N`; it is Neyman-Pearson optimal at its own level `alpha=p_0^N`, but it is not the optimal operating point for every other alpha. It should not be described as universally suboptimal: its exact performance depends on the prescribed false-positive level and loss.

## Matched comparison and cost ledger

The common input channel for each slot is `Exp(lambda)` time plus a Bernoulli mark `Ber(p_h)`. The matched raw-path baseline receives the same N marks and the same N exponential intervals. It has path KL `N*d`; the Erlang time record is ancillary. It may store all N marks (at most `2^N` records) or, for this binary simple-hypothesis problem, only K (N+1 counter values), plus the slot index supplied by the schedule. The internal count CTMC stores `(i,k)` in `N(N+1)/2+2` states and emits one terminal bit. It exactly matches the Neyman-Pearson decision while replacing N externally read marks by a larger internal state graph. The all-pass CTMC uses `2N+1` states and one terminal bit but only implements the extreme-tail threshold.

| Design | CTMC/internal states | Event jumps | Expected elapsed time | Intermediate event observations | Terminal readout | Information/decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| All-pass endpoint | `2N+1` | exactly N | `N/lambda` | 0 externally; the state internally retains stage and pass/fail | 1 bit | `Ber(p_h^N)`; KL below its complete state path for N>=2 |
| All-pass full-state path | same device, `2N+1` | exactly N | `N/lambda` | N transition observations | state path/failure slot | KL `J_N(p_0)*d`; exact matched readout ceiling for this device |
| LR-count CTMC | `N(N+1)/2+2` | exactly N | `N/lambda` | 0 externally; count is retained internally | 1 bit | Neyman-Pearson optimal for each false-positive level |
| Raw-mark baseline | event source plus observer; counter has N+1 values and slot index i | exactly N | `N/lambda` | N marked-jump observations | 1 bit after computing the same threshold | Same Neyman-Pearson ROC as LR-count CTMC; full mark KL `N*d` |

Observation and state are separate budgets. Under an event-observation budget of N, the raw-mark baseline reaches the information ceiling. Under a one-terminal-readout budget, the counter CTMC can match its binary decision by paying `Theta(N^2)` layered CTMC states; the all-pass design pays `Theta(N)` states and deliberately retains less of the path. A state label for the counter uses only `O(log N)` bits, but the autonomous time-homogeneous implementation has `Theta(N^2)` reachable stage-count pairs. This is a state-cardinality accounting, not an energy estimate. The comparison makes no claim about construction complexity or physical realizability.

## Identifiability obstruction 1: endpoint confounds stage count and per-stage pass rate

From an all-pass endpoint alone, the observable parameter is `s_h=p_h^N`. If both N and p_h are unknown, the map is not injective. For example, `(N,p)=(2,1/2)` and `(N,p)=(4,1/sqrt(2))` both give success probability `1/4`. For a pair of endpoint success rates `(s_0,s_1)`, every feasible N yields a corresponding pair `p_h=s_h^(1/N)`. Arbitrarily many endpoint repetitions estimate s_h, but cannot identify N and p_h jointly without another observation or constraint. If N is known, p_h is structurally identifiable from s_h; the obstruction is the joint unknown-parameter interface.

## Identifiability obstruction 2: a fixed-time CTMC transition matrix can alias generators

This is a separate cyclic-CTMC counterexample. On states Z_3, let Q(a,b) have clockwise rate a, counterclockwise rate b, and diagonal entries `-(a+b)`. Its eigenvalues are

`0, -3(a+b)/2 +/- i*sqrt(3)*(a-b)/2`.

At horizon T=1, set `Q0=Q(5,5)` and `Q1=Q(5+2*pi/sqrt(3), 5-2*pi/sqrt(3))`. Both rates in Q1 are positive. The nonzero eigenvalues of Q0 are `-15,-15`; those of Q1 are `-15 +/- 2*pi*i`. The matrices are diagonalized by the same Fourier basis, so `exp(Q0)=exp(Q1)` exactly, even though `Q0 != Q1`. Thus even the full state-to-state transition matrix at one fixed time does not identify a general CTMC generator. The path's jump directions or transition counts together with state exposure times distinguish these two generators. This alias is not claimed for the acyclic stage chain; it is a counterexample to a general snapshot-identifiability claim.

## Falsification and scope boundaries

Theorem A fails as written if holding-time rates depend on H: dwell times then carry information, and the path KL has additional waiting-time terms. The theorem also does not give an optimum over arbitrary state-dependent adaptive channels; in that setting the correct upper bound is the full conditional path experiment, and an adaptive policy may alter which channels are sampled. Theorem B assumes two simple hypotheses, iid Bernoulli marks with known p0,p1, a fixed N, and an event clock independent of H. Composite hypotheses, unknown nuisance rates, correlated marks, or a different per-stage channel require a new optimality analysis.

The decisive model checks are analytic: substitute the two endpoint distributions, verify the path KL chain rule, check the `N=1` equality case, and verify the explicit generator alias by its shared Fourier eigenbasis. No numerical test or biological experiment was performed.
