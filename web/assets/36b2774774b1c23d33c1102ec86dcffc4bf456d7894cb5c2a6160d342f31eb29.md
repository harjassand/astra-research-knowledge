# Source map and prior-art boundary

## Primary statistical literature

- J. Neyman and E. S. Pearson, “On the Problem of the Most Efficient Tests of Statistical Hypotheses,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 231 (1933), 289–337. [Royal Society DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1933.0009). Relevant result: most-powerful likelihood-ratio test for two simple hypotheses at a fixed type-I size; section III treats simple hypotheses.
- A. Wald and J. Wolfowitz, “Optimum Character of the Sequential Probability Ratio Test,” *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 19(3) (1948), 326–339. [Project Euclid DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177730197). Relevant result: the SPRT simultaneously minimizes the two expected observation counts among tests meeting the same two error constraints for a simple i.i.d. pair. The present stopped-KL inequality is the elementary converse; it does not extend the exact optimality claim to arbitrary adaptive composite models.
- H. Chernoff, “Sequential Design of Experiments,” *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 30(3) (1959), 755–770. [Project Euclid DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177706205). Relevant precedent: experiments/actions can be selected sequentially based on acquired evidence; this is why an adaptive information-per-charged-cost bound is the useful accounting object here.
- J. Przyborowski and H. Wilenski, “Homogeneity of Results in Testing Samples from Poisson Series,” *Biometrika* 31(3/4) (1940), 313–323. [Oxford DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/31.3-4.313). Relevant result: conditioning independent Poisson samples on their total yields the binomial comparison used to eliminate a common unknown rate.
- T.-P. Li and Y.-Q. Ma, “Analysis Methods for Results in Gamma-Ray Astronomy,” *Astrophysical Journal* 272 (1983), 317–324. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/161295). Relevant application: likelihood-based on/off counts and significance for source detection. This report uses the exact conditional binomial policy for calibration rather than relying on a Gaussianized significance approximation.
- G. J. Feldman and R. D. Cousins, “A Unified Approach to the Classical Statistical Analysis of Small Signals,” *Physical Review D* 57 (1998), 3873. [APS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.57.3873). Relevant boundary: finite-count Poisson inference with background must preserve stated coverage; this is interval construction, not a replacement for the fixed simple-hypothesis NP detector.

## Selective Astra inputs

- [N18 expected-input-cost detection card](../astra/cards/N18-cost-detection.txt): known finite-dimensional memoryless channel, pure null output, exact zero-false-alarm rejection; source-derived/unreviewed status and expected input cost. It does not include background acquisition, nonzero dark-click baseline, hard spectral support, or total apparatus work.
- [N19 constructive detection card](../astra/cards/N19-constructive-detection.txt): known (E,H) and coherent-window preparation costs under a different channel/probe interface. Not used as proof of the classical bound.
- [N32 hard-energy detection card](../astra/cards/N32-hard-energy-detection.txt): reported two-use witness under a whole-support spectral cap; full linked proof missing from supplied files. It does not solve expected-cost robust detection.
- [N42 rare-Gaussian herald card](../astra/cards/N42-rare-gaussian-heralding.txt): conditional count sampler from an entrywise nonnegative rational Bargmann kernel, conditional on an exact-zero/relative perfect-matching approximation oracle; abstract computation rather than laboratory success.
- [N43 herald-energy card](../astra/cards/N43-herald-energy.txt) and [N64 all-phase Gaussian herald-tail card](../astra/cards/N64-all-gaussian-herald-tails.txt): conditional energy/tail promises for feasible specified heralds. They do not provide generic-phase conditional sampling or remove inverse-success physical preparation cost.
- [Astra expected-versus-hard energy blocker](../astra/indexes/blockers.json): prevents transferring a mean-cost typical-window argument to a hard total-energy-support statement.

All Astra cards are treated as source-indexed claims with their recorded limitations, not as independently verified theorems or priority evidence.
