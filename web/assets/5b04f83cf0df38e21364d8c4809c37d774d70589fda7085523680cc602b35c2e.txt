COLD ANALYTIC AUDIT — MIXED-COFACTOR EXTENSION
Date: 2026-10-08
Audited source: ../RESULT.txt
Scope: corrector g(a,Q)=(1+Q)/(1+Q+a), mixed falling-factorial toggles,
       stated kappa condition and rational N/R selection.

VERDICT
-------
I found no fatal algebraic gap or state/ray counterexample in the displayed
all-state Foster argument. The revised sharper H bound is valid for
arbitrary overlapping mixed cofactors. The active/inactive g estimates are valid, and
the high-b absorption does include the potentially troublesome
2 epsilon Lambda a term because C1 contains 2 Lambda. The rational search
for epsilon, R, N is compatible. This audit reconstructs the generator and
proof directly; it does not rely on the finite checker. The conclusion is
conditional on the exact network grammar and strict hypothesis kappa>0.

1. RECONSTRUCTED GENERATOR
--------------------------
For f=f(a,c,b), with invalid boundary channels omitted, the physical-time
generator is

  Lf = alpha [f(a+1,c,b)-f]
     + delta a [f(a-1,c,b)-f]
     + sum_i gamma_i [f(a,c+e_i,b)-f]
     + sum_i mu_i c_i [f(a,c-e_i,b)-f]
     + p a [f(a,c,b+1)-f]
     + q a b [f(a,c,b-1)-f]
     + sum_j eta_j a phi_j(c) [f(a-1,c,b)-f]
     + sum_j zeta_j phi_j(c) [f(a+1,c,b)-f].

All estimates below use this CTMC generator, not its embedded jump chain.

2. THE SHARPER MIXED-ORDER H BOUND
----------------------------------
The revised source sets h(0)=0, h(1)=1, and h(M)=M+M^2/4 for M>=2.
Put Q_i=Q(c-e_i), Delta_i=Q-Q_i, M=M_i, and write the i-th unweighted
summand of H as T_i=c_i Delta_i/[(1+Q_i)(1+Q)]. The proof's split is valid
termwise and survives overlaps: Q and Delta_i are sums of nonnegative
monomial contributions, and each active monomial belongs to exactly one
of the categories below, according to its exponent nu_ji in coordinate i.

If c_i>M, every active monomial survives the death and
  phi_j(c-e_i)/phi_j(c)=(c_i-nu_ji)/c_i.
Thus Q_i >= ((c_i-M)/c_i)Q and Delta_i<=M Q_i/(c_i-M). Since Q>=Q_i,

  T_i <= [c_i M/(c_i-M)] Q_i/(1+Q_i)^2
      <= [c_i M/(c_i-M)]/4
      <= M(M+1)/4.

If 1<=c_i<=M, active terms with nu_ji<c_i survive, and their decrement
relative to their surviving value is nu_ji/(c_i-nu_ji)<=M. Their total
contribution to Delta_i is therefore at most M Q_i. The terms with
nu_ji=c_i are exactly the active terms annihilated by the death; their
total contribution is at most Q. Terms with nu_ji>c_i are inactive and
contribute zero. Using Q>=Q_i and Q_i/(1+Q_i)^2<=1/4, the surviving part
of T_i is at most c_i M/4; the annihilated part is at most c_i, since
Q/[(1+Q_i)(1+Q)]<=1. If M>=2, their sum is bounded by
c_i(M/4+1)<=M^2/4+M=h(M). If M=1, then c_i=1 and there is no nonzero
surviving decrement: nu_ji<c_i forces nu_ji=0, whose monomial is unchanged
in coordinate i. The threshold part is at most 1=h(1). For M=0,
Delta_i=0. The high-case bound M(M+1)/4 is also <=h(M) for every M>=1.
Multiplying by mu_i and summing proves
  H(c)<=sum_i mu_i h(M_i)=H0.

The overlap control is exact: it uses additivity of Q and Delta_i and a
partition by the single coordinate exponent; no disjoint-support or
non-overlap assumption is needed. The bound still retains threshold deaths
that annihilate a mixed cofactor and does not claim H tends to zero along
escaping rays.

3. EXACT DRIFT OF a/(1+Q)
-------------------------
Let u=1/(1+Q). A/deactivation channels give alpha u-delta a u-aEu+Qu;
cofactor-preserving substrate immigration gives the nonpositive term
 a sum_i gamma_i[u(Q(c+e_i))-u(Q)]. Substrate death gives aH. Thus exactly

  L(au) = u[alpha+Q-(delta+E)a] + aH
           + a sum_i gamma_i[u(Q(c+e_i))-u(Q)].

Since E>=rho Q,
  (delta+E)/(1+Q) >= min(delta,rho),
and (alpha+Q)/(1+Q)<=max(alpha,1). Therefore

  L(au) <= max(alpha,1)-kappa a.

The signs and coefficient kappa=min(delta,rho)-H0 agree with the revised
candidate, where H0=sum_i mu_i h(M_i). In particular, the mixed
threshold-death cost is covered by the sharper bound above.

4. EXACT DRIFT OF THE g CORRECTOR
--------------------------------
Write x=1+Q and D_a=x/[(x+a-1)(x+a)] for a>=1. A death of A or a forward
toggle changes g by +D_a. Reverse toggles and A immigration increase a and
decrease g. C immigration can increase g by at most one per jump, while C
death can only decrease Q and hence g. Thus, for a>=1,

  (L(Wg))/W <= delta a D_a + a E D_a + Gamma,
  Gamma=sum_i gamma_i.

As x+a-1 >= (x+a)/2 and 4ax<=(x+a)^2,
  delta a D_a <= 2 delta ax/(x+a)^2 <= delta/2.
Also E<=Lambda Q and Q<=x, so
  a E D_a <= 2 Lambda a Qx/(x+a)^2 <= 2 Lambda a.
These are valid uniformly in all c, including mixed-cofactor threshold
faces. At a=0, g(0,Q)=1 for every Q, so C jumps have zero g increment;
A immigration and reverse toggles have total rate alpha+Q and take g to
(1+Q)/(2+Q). Hence exactly

  L(epsilon Wg)|_{a=0}
    =-epsilon W (alpha+Q)/(2+Q) <= -epsilon tau W,
  tau=min(alpha/2,1)>0.

No hidden mixed C-death increment enters this corrector.

5. CHECK OF THE GLOBAL b SPLIT
------------------------------
The B-edge drift of W[1+epsilon g] is exactly
  a[1+epsilon g] G_R(b),
  G_R(n)=[p(R-1)-q(1-R^{-1})n]R^n.
For b>=N, the first inequality in (6) implies
  G_R(b)<=-q(1-R^{-1})b W/2.
For a>=1, the positive active-g correction is at most
  epsilon W(delta/2+2 Lambda a+Gamma)
    <= epsilon C1 a W,
  C1=delta/2+2 Lambda+Gamma.
The second inequality in (6) then gives, with cB=epsilon tau/2,

  a[1+epsilon g]G_R(b)+epsilon W(delta/2+2 Lambda a+Gamma)
    <= aW[-q(1-R^{-1})b/2+epsilon C1]
    <= -a cB W <= -cB W.

This verifies the key absorption: q(1-R^{-1})N/2 >= epsilon C1+cB
contains 2 epsilon Lambda, and multiplication by a>=1 supplies the
required factor for the 2 epsilon Lambda a term. The concern that a bare
constant threshold would fail to absorb that term is resolved by the
actual C1 definition.

For b<N, W<=R^(N-1), G_R(b)<=g_+(R), and the total coefficient of a is
at most
  -kappa+(1+epsilon)g_+(R)+2epsilon Lambda R^(N-1) <= -kappa/2.
The remaining positive term is bounded by
  epsilon R^(N-1)(delta/2+Gamma).
At a=0, (5) gives the negative -epsilon tau W, including on both low- and
high-b regions. The weighted C drift is exactly
  sum_i w_i gamma_i - sum_i w_i mu_i c_i.
The boundary indicator is active on the whole set a+sum_i c_i=0, including
states with b>0. Its positive drift comes only from a sole A or sole C_i
death, bounded by K max(delta,max_i mu_i). From any state on that set, an A
immigration jump has increment 1-K-epsilon W/2 (since Q=0 and W=R^b);
this is nonpositive for K>=1. A C_i immigration jump has increment w_i-K.
At the literal origin b=0, W=1, recovering the source's stated increment.
These boundary terms are therefore bounded as claimed.
Together these yield the claimed form

  LV <= C0 - (kappa/2)a - (min_i mu_i)sum_i w_i c_i - cB R^b,

after adding the bounded low-b correction to C0. This split covers arbitrary
simultaneous growth of a,c,b; no face restriction is used.

6. RATIONAL SEARCH AND RAY CHECK
--------------------------------
For epsilon=1/k, R=1+1/k^2, set d=1-R^{-1}=1/(k^2+1). The least N satisfying
the first two inequalities can be chosen as the ceiling of the maximum of

  2p(R-1)/(q d),
  2(epsilon C1+cB)/(q d).

The first quantity is O(1); the second is O(k), so N=O(k) and
R^(N-1)->1. Positive G_R(n) requires n<pR/q, a uniformly bounded finite
range as k grows, and each such G_R(n)->0. Thus g_+(R)->0 and the third
inequality's left side tends to zero, while kappa/2 is fixed positive.
The search terminates and uses rational tests for rational rates.

I tested the likely adversarial regimes analytically: (i) a=0 and Q large,
where g remains exactly 1 and the correction supplies a uniform negative
W drift; (ii) a>=1, low b, and Q large, where active toggle gain is at most
2epsilon Lambda aW and is absorbed by the kappa margin; (iii) a>=1, high
b, where the b-sink absorbs epsilon C1 aW; and (iv) threshold C-deaths,
where H0 covers the nonvanishing decrement. None falsifies the bounds.

7. CONSEQUENCE AND LIMIT
------------------------
V is norm-like: the weighted C term controls unbounded c; on bounded c,
u(Q) is bounded below and au controls unbounded a; R^b controls unbounded
b. The stated drift is a direct physical-time CTMC Foster inequality.
Together with finite local rates, localization on finite V-sublevels gives
nonexplosion; irreducibility follows from the independent A/C immigration
and death channels and the A-controlled B pair. The positive-recurrence
conclusion is therefore supported under the stated hypotheses. This audit
does not establish coverage beyond the exact displayed grammar, validate
novelty, or replace external proof review.
