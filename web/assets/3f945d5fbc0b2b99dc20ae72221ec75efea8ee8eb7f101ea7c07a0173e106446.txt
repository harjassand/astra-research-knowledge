1. EXACT LOWER BOUND FOR SIMULTANEOUS STRONG HITTING

Fix n>=1, B>=1, and any finite list H of h tuples of ordinary square matrices over a base field k; dimensions may vary. Assume h<=B. Let F be an algebraic closure of k. For each j=1,...,h choose an eigenvalue a_j in F of the first matrix X_1^(j) in tuple j. Form the h order-one affine pencils

    L_j(x)=x_1-a_j.

Each pencil is feasible over F: evaluate x_1=(a_j+1)I_1, yielding 1, and choose arbitrary values for unused variables. At tuple j, L_j(X^(j)) is singular by the eigenvalue choice. Consequently no tuple makes all h pencils invertible. Therefore a list valid for EVERY family of at most B order-at-most-B feasible pencils over EVERY coefficient extension has h>=B+1.

This applies separately in every characteristic, including characteristic two, and to any integer list after reduction into the prime subfield. It needs no finite computational evidence. Its force comes specifically from arbitrary coefficient extensions: the scalar a_j need not belong to the prime subfield or Q. It does not contradict the original source's rational-coefficient theorem or the candidate list theorem.

2. ORIGINAL EXPRESSION DOMAINS FORCE A LINEAR LIST LENGTH

With the same eigenvalues define the one-variable polynomial expression

    P(x_1)=product_(j=1)^h (x_1+(-a_j)).

Use h factors, each with three vertices, and h-1 ordered product vertices, so the formula tree has size 4h-1. At candidate j the j-th factor is singular, and det P(X_1^(j)) is the product of factor determinants, hence zero. P is a nonzero polynomial over F, and is invertible at a scalar b avoiding the finite set {a_j}. Since an algebraic closure is infinite, such b exists.

Thus a list of h tuples cannot give an INVERTIBLE value for every nonzero division-free formula of size >=4h-1. A universal size-s strong formula list therefore needs at least floor((s+1)/4)+1 tuples.

For a lower bound that defeats even defined-nonzero rather than merely invertible-value hitting, retain an original inverse occurrence:

    Phi(x_1)=1+0 * (P(x_1))^(-1).

The tree has (4h-1)+1+2+2=4h+4 vertices. It is admissible and nonzero: at scalar b as above, every gate is defined and the value is 1. At every listed tuple P is singular, so the inverse occurrence is undefined, although the free-field function represented by Phi is 1. Therefore a domain-respecting hitting list for size-s formulas must contain at least floor(s/4) tuples (for the nontrivial range s>=8).

This is a direct exact-domain counterexample to any attempted cancellation of zero-multiplied branches. It also explains why forming one block-diagonal tuple from a hitting list fails: inversion of a block-diagonal operand requires every block to be invertible, whereas the list promise gives only one successful candidate. The elementary example Phi=x_1^(-1) with candidates x_1=1 and x_1=0 already shows the failure of direct-sum compression.

3. ARBITRARY-FIELD q-NEWTON EXPANSION: INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION

Let k be ANY field, q transcendental over k, K=k(q), and a_i=(1+q)^i for i=0,...,w-1. Their multiplicative q-orbits are disjoint. Indeed, (1+q)^(i-i')=q^r forces r=0 by valuation at q=0, and then i=i' by valuation at q=-1. These valuation arguments work in every characteristic, even when a positive integer exponent is divisible by the characteristic.

For L_(i,d)(z)=product_(j=0)^(d-1)(z-a_i q^j), monicity gives a unit triangular Newton basis. Its first d coefficients vanish exactly when the polynomial vanishes at the first d points of the i-th q-orbit. For an h-dimensional V subset P_M(K), choose Newton echelon indices d_(i,1)<...<d_(i,h). The folded Casoratian

    W(z)=det(f_j(q^r z))_(0<=r<h,1<=j<=h)

is nonzero: a basis with distinct top degrees e_j has top coefficient det(q^(r e_j)), a nonzero Vandermonde because q has infinite multiplicative order. Its degree is <=h(M-1). At z=a_i q^ell, each column with d_(i,j)>ell+h-1 vanishes in every row; every determinant term therefore has at least that many factors z-a_i q^ell. Summing these multiplicities over the disjoint orbits yields

    sum_(i,j) d_(i,j) <=h(M-1)+w h(h-1).

Over K((eta)), define reversal T_i by sending the Newton coefficient c_a to c_a eta^a L_(i,M-1-a). The source's integral-lattice proof uses only a discrete valuation ring and coefficient changes constant in eta, so it applies over K in every characteristic. A normalized vector with a unit coefficient at an index <=A has minimum output eta-valuation <=A. Its leading output only contains indices a<=A, hence is divisible by L_(i,M-1-A). Applying this to j independent low-index projections gives the fixed-output-filtration inequality

    b_(i,ell-j+1)>=M-1-a_(i,j).

Summing input and output folded bounds shows that ell<= (w-1)h contradicts Delta=4w^3B for h<=B. This proves the asserted small-subspace expansion. The kernel/shrunk-subspace argument then yields rank(sum A_i tensor R_i)>=rN>(r-1)M for every feasible order-r<=B pencil. A witness over any extension compares in a fresh Laurent-series field; pre-existing coefficient variables must not be identified with the fresh q or eta.

4. SYMBOL LIFT AND ALL-EXTENSION SEMISIMPLE DESCENT

The recent manuscript's Section 3 uses primitive roots and Fourier inversion. Its ordered-monomial proof requires only M!=0 and a primitive root omega of exact order M; no step otherwise uses characteristic zero. With X=uD, Y=vS, XY=omega YX, ordered monomials are linearly independent over K by algebraic independence of u,v. A lexicographic leading-term argument makes their algebra a domain. Localization at nonzero central polynomials in u^M,v^M has dimension M^2 and remains a domain, hence is a division algebra. Block Gaussian elimination makes ordinary ranks multiples of M. The near-full-rank inequality therefore forces each lifted determinant to be nonzero for EVERY primitive root.

The prime-specific coefficient algebra A=E[omega]/Phi_M is finite separable over F_p because p does not divide M. After scalar extension to an algebraic closure of ANY coefficient field F, it has e rho branches. A branch fixes arbitrary F coefficients; it maps the finite grid coordinates through one Frobenius embedding of E. Those embeddings permute E, so a degree-D0 polynomial has at most D0|G|^3 bad points per branch. Counting e rho(B+1) constraints leaves one common point. Every branch is enforced; nonzero elements of a ring are never mistaken for units. The good R_0 representation is invertible already over F_p and is retained by the filter.

In the integer branch, C=Z[omega]/(omega^(M/2)+1) splits into primitive-root branches in every odd characteristic and characteristic zero. Clearing Fourier's sole scalar denominator M gives integer polynomial U_i. Put Z_i=adj(U_0)U_i. The polynomial det L(lambda Z) is nonzero because, in the generic parameter field, lambda=1/det U_0 gives U_0^(-1)U_i. Crucially, det U_0 is the ordinary determinant of the entire regular representation: one scalar normalizes every branch. The five-variable grid enforces actual pencil determinants; U_0 need not be nonsingular at the final grid point. All smaller primes are appended as individually charged prime-specific lists.

5. ACTUAL INVERSE DOMAINS AND THE PRIMARY SOURCES

I inspected the recent manuscript's Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.2. The leaf, sum, product, and inverse block recursions use only 0,1,-1 and ordered block identities, so they extend to every field including characteristic two. Every inverse NODE pencil is retained; an inverse-node pencil is the augmentation of its child's realization. The root augmentation's Schur complement is minus the formula value. Bottom-up induction therefore enforces the domain of the actual tree, even in canceled or zero-multiplied branches.

I inspected HW2015 Section 2, Theorem 2.1 (printed pp. 361-362), and Proposition 7.3 (printed pp. 378-379). Section 2 starts with arbitrary commutative F. It relates correct expressions to nonempty matrix domains and free-field equality to equality on common domains. Proposition 7.3's proof evaluates the rational entries of the formal inverse simultaneously, with an algebraic-closure witness; this is sufficient for the candidate's extension-field feasibility interface. If initial operational admissibility/nonzero is witnessed only over E/F, perform this free-field argument over E. The certificate coefficients stay in F and their witnesses still lie over an extension of F. A universal prime-field tuple working over E has determinant conditions with coefficients in F, so invertibility descends to F. This avoids any root of unity tied to the initial witness dimension, including dimensions divisible by p.

Consequently the precise audited operational implication is a deterministic universal integer LIST for original size-s admissible noncommutative rational expressions over any field, with a defined invertible hit for every nonzero expression. Under an exact oracle returning definedness and zero/nonzero (or the full matrix), querying the list gives deterministic black-box identity testing. Costs of unknown-formula evaluation and response encoding remain separately charged. This does not imply scalar commutative PIT, general circuit PIT, or an efficient representation of arbitrary field constants.

6. DETERMINISTIC COMPLETE-OUTPUT BIT COST

The input bounds n,B are unary. M, d, E0, T0, and K are explicit fixed polynomials in their numerical values. Unit triangular Newton conversion introduces no characteristic or parameter division. Finite polynomial products and triangular inversion have polynomial coefficient heights; the number of central parameters is fixed. Integer grid evaluation adds polynomial-degree times log K to entry bit lengths. Every numeric adjugate may be computed by d^2 exact determinant computations, including singular matrices. Hadamard's determinant bound makes cofactor bit lengths polynomial, and exact Gaussian/Bareiss-style arithmetic gives deterministic polynomial bit time. Every tuple and dense entry is charged.

For small p, choosing the least e with p^e>H^2 gives Q<=H^4. Enumerating all Q monic degree-e candidates and deterministic irreducibility tests therefore costs polynomial time in H; no uncharged general finite-field factoring or irreducible-construction oracle is needed. The grid has at most H^16 points. Enumerating every prime p<=K by trial division is polynomial in K, which suffices because K is polynomial in unary n+B. Converting all F_p residues to integer representatives preserves their reduction in every characteristic-p coefficient extension.

These are honest asymptotic polynomial bounds, not practical runtime.
