## Primary explicit kernel source

V. Strassen, “Gaussian Elimination is not Optimal,” *Numerische Mathematik* 13 (1969), 354–356, DOI [10.1007/BF02165411](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165411).

* Primary paper scan downloaded from the University of Illinois course archive: [PDF](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs357/su2013/Honors/Strassen.pdf); local copy `sources/Strassen1969_Gaussian_Elimination_is_not_Optimal.pdf`, SHA-256 `8f70cdc4fa462d60d239f1bcfe7c86db94ee4be8134121dc8dde76e72b402751`.
* Decisive span: pp. 354–355 (PDF pages 0–1), formulas for seven block products and four outputs; p. 355 gives the recursive seven-multiplication count and addition bound. The equations were reconstructed from the primary scan and independently checked in `strassen_verify.py`.
* The scan’s printed bound on general `n` and its historical cost model are not imported. The precise `2^k` operation counts used here are re-derived from the displayed recurrence.

## Exact local verification

* `strassen_verify.py` exhaustively checks all 64 structure-constant entries of the seven-term bilinear tensor identity over `Z`; it also runs 48 seeded exact integer product fixtures (sizes 1,2,4,8) and one overlapping-rectangle/residual output fixture.
* `checks.json` records the result. These checks verify this fixed decomposition and tested compiler behavior only. They do not certify the 9/4 manuscript, general masks, or external novelty.
* The exact inequality `7^100 < 2^281` is checked with Python big integers in the same script; it gives `log_2(7)<2.81` and justifies the mask-volume exponent in the theorem.
