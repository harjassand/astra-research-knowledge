THEOREM CANDIDATE

Let sigma=s^2>0 be a finite-dimensional density, L a positive KMS symmetric
sigma-stationary Heisenberg generator, and H=Gamma^(1/2)L Gamma^(-1/2),
Gamma(X)=sXs. For every unit vector psi, let P=|psi><psi| and
rho_e=(1-e)P+e I/d, 0<e<1. Then
 lim_(e->0) J(rho_e) >= pi E(P),
where the limit can be positive infinity,
 J(rho)=Tr[L_*(rho)(logrho-logsigma)], E(P)=Tr[P H(P)].
This is a boundary theorem along the stated faithful regularization. It does
not prove the inequality for arbitrary full-rank mixed rho.

1. Finite versus infinite boundary entropy production

Use the established full KMS Hermitian-noise representation
 H=sum_j H_Bj, H_B(X)={V_B,X}/2-BXB,
 V_B=2 Lyap_s^-1(BsB), Lyap_s(X)=sX+Xs,
 K_B=s^(1/2)B s^(-1/2), B=B*.
The physical drift C_B=s^(1/2)V_B s^(-1/2) satisfies
 C_B+C_B*=2K_B*K_B, so the Hamiltonian correction is retained.

With Q=I-P, let a_e=1-e+e/d and b_e=e/d. Trace preservation gives
 Tr[L_*(rho_e)logrho_e]=log(a_e/b_e)Tr[P L_*(rho_e)].
The limiting coefficient is
 g=Tr[P L_*(P)]=sum_j(||K_j psi||^2-|<psi,K_j psi>|^2)
               =sum_j||Q K_j psi||^2 >=0.
If g>0, J(rho_e)->+infinity, since the stationary-log contribution is bounded.
If g=0, psi is an eigenvector of EVERY K_j, and the self-entropy contribution
is O(e|log e|)->0. Hence
 J_0:=lim J(rho_e)=-Tr[L_*(P)logsigma].
This classification uses the actual physical generator, not weighted H alone.

2. Shift each noise to make the common eigenvector dark

Each K_j is similar to Hermitian B_j, so its eigenvalue on psi is REAL.
Adding a real scalar to B leaves H_B and the physical generator unchanged.
We may therefore shift every B independently so K_B psi=0. Set
 d0=s^(1/2), v=d0^-1 psi; then Bv=0 and <v,s v>=1.
Decompose the positive matrix BsB as sum_l |w_l><w_l|, for example
w_l=s_l^(1/2)B|l> in an eigenbasis of s. Every w_l is orthogonal to v.

Let s_i>0, u_i=log s_i in that eigenbasis and
 a_li=conj(w_li)v_i. Then sum_i a_li=<w_l,v>=0 and
 V_ij=2sum_l w_li conj(w_lj)/(s_i+s_j).

The energy contribution is exactly
 E_B(P)=Tr[V_B P]-<psi,Bpsi>^2,
 Tr[V_B P]=sum_l sum_ij conj(a_li)a_lj sech((u_i-u_j)/2).            (E)
The subtracted expectation square is nonnegative and is not discarded with
the wrong sign. V_B>=0 since the Lyapunov inverse is a CP integral, although
the displayed Gram identity also proves positivity of its expectation.

Since K_B psi=0,
 L_B,*(P)=(C_B P+P C_B*)/2,
 J_0,B=-Re<v,s logsigma V_B v>.
Entrywise expansion and symmetrization give
 J_0,B=-2sum_l sum_ij conj(a_li)a_lj
                  (s_i u_i+s_j u_j)/(s_i+s_j).
Use
 (s_i u_i+s_j u_j)/(s_i+s_j)
   =(u_i+u_j)/2+(u_i-u_j)tanh((u_i-u_j)/2)/2.
The additive first term cancels EXACTLY because sum a_li=0. Therefore
 J_0,B=-sum_l sum_ij conj(a_li)a_lj
                         (u_i-u_j)tanh((u_i-u_j)/2).              (J)
All factors of two use logsigma=2log s. Both (E) and (J) are real.

3. A scalar conditional Fourier comparison

For any finite real u_i and complex coefficients a_i with sum a_i=0,
define F(xi)=sum_i a_i exp(i u_i xi). The identities
 sech(t/2)=int_R sech(pi xi) exp(i t xi)d xi,
 -t tanh(t/2)=int_R mu(xi)[cos(t xi)-1]d xi,
 mu(xi)=pi cosh(pi xi)/sinh(pi xi)^2,
imply
 -sum_ij conj(a_i)a_j (u_i-u_j)tanh((u_i-u_j)/2)
     =int_R mu(xi)|F(xi)|^2 d xi,
 sum_ij conj(a_i)a_j sech((u_i-u_j)/2)
     =int_R sech(pi xi)|F(xi)|^2 d xi.
The conditional identity is an ordinary convergent integral: near0,
F(xi)=O(xi), cancelling mu(xi)=O(xi^-2); at infinity mu decays exponentially.
There is no uncharged distributional boundary term because sum a_i=0.

Pointwise for xi!=0,
 mu(xi)-pi sech(pi xi)
     =pi/[sinh(pi xi)^2 cosh(pi xi)]>0.
Thus the first quadratic form is at least pi times the second.

For completeness, the first Fourier identity follows by shifting the sech
contour by i, as reconstructed in root_cycle07/MODULAR_ORBIT_ENERGY_RECONSTRUCTION.
For the second, mu=-(d/dxi)csch(pi xi). Integrating by parts against
cos(t xi)-1 (whose boundary products vanish at0 and infinity) gives
 -t int_R sin(t xi)csch(pi xi)d xi=-t tanh(t/2).
The sine integral follows by expanding csch(pi xi)=2sum_(n>=0)
exp(-(2n+1)pi xi) for xi>0, integrating, and using
 tanh(t/2)=4t sum_(n>=0)[(2n+1)^2 pi^2+t^2]^-1.
This last elementary partial fraction identity is also obtained by a logarithmic
derivative of the classical cosh product in NIST DLMF4.36.2, checked directly
at https://dlmf.nist.gov/4.36 on2026-10-09 (version1.2.8).
Absolute convergence/interchange follows from |sin(t xi)|<=|t|xi near0 and
sum(2n+1)^-2<infinity. No recent model-generated mathematical premise is used.

4. Finish and scope

Apply the scalar comparison separately to every a_l in (E),(J):
 J_0,B>=pi Tr[V_B P]>=pi E_B(P).
Sum over the represented noise terms; scalar shifts left each original H_B
unchanged. If any jump has nonzero leakage, the boundary entropy is infinite
instead. E(rho_e)->E(P) by finite-dimensional root continuity. This proves the
stated candidate inequality for every pure boundary state along the given ray.

The proof includes arbitrary finite composite systems when the generator is
legal KMS there; a finite product reference is a special case. It neither
treats general entangled MIXED states as pure nor provides a convex extension:
J-kappa E is not known convex, and the earlier zero-buffer counterexample
already warns against such a shortcut. Equality/optimality within this pure
boundary class has not been analyzed. Universal stationary pi sharpness is a
separate theorem, not a boundary-class sharpness premise.
