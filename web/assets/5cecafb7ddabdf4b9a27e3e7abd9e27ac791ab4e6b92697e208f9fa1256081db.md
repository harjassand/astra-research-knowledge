## Primary literature consulted

1. Martin Hemberg and Mauricio Barahona, “Perfect Sampling of the Master Equation for Gene Regulatory Networks,” *Biophysical Journal* 93(2), 401–410 (2007), DOI `10.1529/biophysj.106.099390`, https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.106.099390. Read the primary full-text HTML. Section 2 states the order-preserving Markov-CME and reversible, known-stationary dominating-process requirements. Section 3 (“Detailed application to the first-order reaction”), immediately after Eqs. (10) and (12), states that Hill and Monod birth rates are bounded above by k and uses the first-order process from Eq. (4) as the dominating process. Section 7 applies the sampler to toggle-switch and repressilator models. This is the decisive priority boundary for the core domination mechanism. It covers Markov propensities and its specified ordering/reversibility setting, not arbitrary predictable-history controls.
2. JaeJun Lee and Julian Lee, “Quantitative analysis of a transient dynamics of a gene regulatory network,” *Physical Review E* 98, 062404 (2018), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevE.98.062404`, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.98.062404. Abstract supports the warning that positive-feedback protein can become extinct stochastically despite deterministic convergence to a nonzero level; basal production shifts that risk.
3. Chentao Yong and Andras Gyorgy, “Stability and Robustness of Unbalanced Genetic Toggle Switches in the Presence of Scarce Resources,” *Life* 11(4), 271 (2021), DOI `10.3390/life11040271`, https://doi.org/10.3390/life11040271. The paper analyzes shared transcription/translation resource competition and toggle robustness using a mechanistic reduced model and potential barriers; it is relevant prior art but not the exact finite-chain statement here.
4. Andrew Mugler et al., “Noise Expands the Response Range of the Bacillus subtilis Competence Circuit,” *PLOS Computational Biology* 12(3), e1004793 (2016), DOI `10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004793`, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004793. Primary stochastic modeling and fluorescence evidence show that noise can help maintain responsive population behavior; reliability is objective-dependent.
5. Lucy Ham, Megan A. Coomer, Kaan Öcal, Ramon Grima, and Michael P. H. Stumpf, “A stochastic vs deterministic perspective on the timing of cellular events,” *Nature Communications* 15 (2024), DOI `10.1038/s41467-024-49624-z`, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49624-z. The paper compares deterministic and stochastic first-passage times for birth-death, telegraph, and autoregulatory models, so it bounds interpretation of a stationary dropout theorem.
