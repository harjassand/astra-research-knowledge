DECISION

The strongest exact contact is a ground-cost reformulation of the sharp-2
gate. Parekh–Thompson give a sharp product-state approximation for Quantum
Max Cut (QMC), driven by a nonlinear three-qubit monogamy inequality. Its
objective and hypotheses do not imply the target: it is a maximum-energy
rounding theorem, whereas the target is a lower bound on the cost of every
legal tracial two-output extension against a centered, tracial product
baseline. The complement operation adds a scale-dependent floor.

Johnson–Viola give an exact, dimension-independent-in-the-right-units
calibration: the isotropic two-output extension boundary, applied to the
depolarizing channel and a complete traceless frame, gives equality in
(V-k)/(V-s)=2 for every d. This is an exact legal sharpness witness, not a
counterexample. It leaves anisotropic/general frames open because isotropic
twirling erases the frame weights.

Generic finite symmetric-extension conversion and quantum Poincare routes
also miss the required interface: the former retains a d-dependent additive
error (and a stronger Bose-support hypothesis in the cited theorem); the
latter assumes semigroup structure and a spectral gap and outputs an
observable inequality, not a common EB map. No arbitrary-frame centered
rounding theorem or exact integrality-gap family matching all target
constraints was established here. This is a scoped negative finding, not a
novelty claim.

1. EXACT PRODUCT-HAMILTONIAN REFORMULATION (DERIVED HERE)

Let tau=Tr/d, let A_i be traceless Hermitians, V=sum_i tau(A_i^2), and let
Q=sum_i |A_i><A_i|. Let R be the normalized, B-C-symmetrized Choi state of a
legal supplied broadcaster. Thus its three one-register marginals are I/d,
and its AB and AC Choi marginals represent the same unital, trace-preserving,
Hilbert–Schmidt-self-adjoint channel Phi. Define

  H_Q = (1/2) sum_i ((A_i^{T,A}-A_i^B)^2 +
                       (A_i^{T,A}-A_i^C)^2) >= 0,

where T is transpose in the Choi basis and the superscript names a register.

Writing W_Q=sum_i A_i^T tensor (A_i^B+A_i^C), the target's Choi identity is
Tr(R W_Q)=2s_Phi(Q). The fixed tracial marginals give

  Tr(R H_Q)=2V-Tr(R W_Q)=2(V-s_Phi(Q)).

For a pure-state ensemble mu={(p_y,pi_y)} with sum_y p_y pi_y=I/d, form the
tracial product extension sigma_mu=sum_y p_y pi_y^{T,A} tensor pi_y^B tensor
pi_y^C. The conjugated input ensemble is also pure and tracial. Direct
expansion, using Tr(pi_y^T A_i^T)=Tr(pi_y A_i), gives

  Tr(sigma_mu H_Q)=2(V-sum_y p_y sum_i [Tr(pi_y A_i)]^2).

Minimizing over these ensembles gives 2(V-k(Q)). Therefore the conjectural
G2 inequality 2s_Phi(Q)<=V(Q)+k(Q) is exactly

  Tr(R H_Q) >= (1/2) min_{tracial product extensions sigma_mu}
                         Tr(sigma_mu H_Q) = V(Q)-k(Q).

This is a centered lower-energy/integrality inequality for every legal
three-register extension. It includes the tracial local constraints and
uses one product ensemble for the whole frame. For d>2, expanding H_Q
contains non-scalar one-register terms sum_i (A_i^2)^T on A and sum_i A_i^2
on B,C; their expectations are
fixed on legal R and tracial product extensions, but not on unrestricted
product states. This distinction is part of the theorem interface.

2. PRODUCT-STATE QMC ROUNDING: STRONG BUT WRONG POLARITY/BASELINE

Parekh and Thompson, arXiv:2206.08342v2 (25 March 2026), Theorem 1: QMC has
a 1/2-approximation with respect to the level-2 quantum Lasserre relaxation,
outputting a product state. Their Lemma 1 gives, for every n-qubit density
matrix and the three SWAP expectations on a triangle, both
0<=s_ij+s_ik+s_jk<=3 and a nonlinear quadratic inequality among the three
values; the second inequality is the load-bearing monogamy input for the
sharp QMC analysis. QMC's edge terms are rank-one singlet projectors. Their
Definition 3 requires positive strictly quadratic two-local terms with no
one-local Pauli terms. The paper says the QMC method extends to more general
positive strictly quadratic terms, but the general-term guarantee is
numerically verified, not established as the same exact theorem (Introduction,
lines 120-122; Appendix C).

Transfer failure: even where H_Q fits a positive two-local form, Theorem 1
rounds a maximum-energy objective. The exact gate above asks every legal R
to have cost at least half the minimum cost of tracial product extensions.
Applying max-energy rounding to K I-H_Q, when admissible, gives a bound with
the additive offset K; it does not cancel K to produce a deficit bound.
Additionally, unrestricted product states need not have I/d one-register
marginals or use the prescribed conjugate-on-A, common-pure-state-on-B,C
ensemble. The nonlinear SWAP-triangle
constraint is scalar and qubit-specific; it does not state a matrix-weighted
inequality for arbitrary Q or a tracial common-product rounding rule.

Concrete missing conversion: a centered rounding/certification theorem that
preserves the tracial one-register marginals and proves
Tr(R H_Q)>=1/2 min_mu Tr(sigma_mu H_Q), uniformly in dimension and Q. A
standard multiplicative approximation does not suffice.

3. EXACT ISOTROPIC EXTENSION BOUNDARY: LEGAL SHARPNESS, NO GENERAL GAP

Johnson and Viola, arXiv:1305.1342v3 (11 September 2013), define isotropic
states rho(Phi+) invariant under U* tensor U and prove in Theorem III.8 that
rho(Phi+) is 1-n sharable iff Phi+<=1+(d-1)/n. Their parameter is Phi+=dF,
where F is the singlet fraction; rho(Phi+) has maximally mixed marginals.
For n=2 the exact symmetric-extension threshold is Phi+=(d+1)/2.

Take the depolarizing channel Phi_lambda(X)=lambda X+(1-lambda)tau(X)I.
Its normalized Choi state is lambda |Omega><Omega|+(1-lambda)I/d^2, so its
isotropic parameter is Phi+=d[lambda+(1-lambda)/d^2]. At the threshold
Phi+=(d+1)/2, lambda=(d+2)/(2(d+1)). Theorem III.8 supplies a symmetric
two-output extension R; each marginal is I/d, and Phi_lambda is unital,
trace-preserving and HS-self-adjoint.

For any complete traceless Hermitian basis with tau(A_i A_j)=delta_ij,
there are d^2-1 elements, V=d^2-1, and for every pure pi,
sum_i [Tr(pi A_i)]^2=d-1. Hence k=d-1, s=lambda(d^2-1), and

  (V-k)/(V-s) = d(d-1) / ((1-lambda)(d^2-1)) = 2.

Thus factor 2, if true, is exactly tight in a family matching the Choi,
tracial-marginal, channel self-adjointness, and common-broadcaster interface.
It is not an integrality gap beyond 2. The theorem controls the isotropic
one-parameter sector; twirling a general Q-dependent objective to this
sector destroys its anisotropic weights, so it does not prove G2 for general
frames.

4. WHAT GENERAL SYMMETRIC-EXTENSION AND DIRICHLET RESULTS BUY

Navascues, Owari, and Plenio, arXiv:0906.2731v1, Theorem 2 and Corollary 5:
for an N-Bose-symmetrically-extendible bipartite operator with output
dimension d, mixing the state with local white noise at rate at most d/(N+d)
produces a separable state; a separable approximation obeys
||rho-rho_sep||_1<=2(d-1)/(N+d-1). This is a genuine dimension-dependent
conversion for a stronger Bose-support extension (their Definition 1).
At N=2 its error bound is O(1) as d grows, and it is an additive state-norm
statement rather than a relative centered score bound. The target permits a
swap-invariant extension, not necessarily support on the symmetric output
subspace. This route does not yield a dimension-free deficit estimate or an
exact target obstruction.

Junge and Wang, arXiv:2601.06005v2 (31 August 2026), Theorem 1.2: for a
trace-symmetric quantum Markov semigroup T_t=e^{-tL} with spectral gap
alpha>0 and fixed-point conditional expectation E, self-adjoint x obeys
||x-E(x)||_p <= p/sqrt(2 alpha) ||Gamma(x,x)||_{p/2}^{1/2}, p=2 or p>=3.
This is a real dimension-free Poincare mechanism under its stated gap and
semigroup assumptions. It does not imply that an arbitrary supplied
self-adjoint CP broadcaster marginal is a semigroup element; the gap enters
the constant; and its conclusion controls observables by a generator
Dirichlet form, not a common EB comparison against k(Q). No embedding or
uniform-gap conversion is supplied by the theorem. It is therefore a
different, assumption-strengthened problem.

THEOREM GATE AND NEXT DECISIVE CHECK

The live sharp-2 statement is: for every finite d, positive frame Q, and
legal R as above, Tr(R H_Q)>=V(Q)-k(Q). For some finite universal C, the
corresponding general gate is V-k<=C(V-s); equivalently its star score bound
must hold for every legal R. Neither source route above proves this arbitrary
frame statement, and none yields a valid counterexample.

The next focused test is the qubit anisotropic case: diagonalize the 3x3
positive frame covariance Q by a common Bloch rotation and maximize the star
score over exact swap-symmetric three-qubit Choi states with all one-body
marginals I/2. Compare the resulting exact support to V+lambda_max(Q).
This is a finite exact-geometry check for the first anisotropic gap; passing
it would not prove higher-dimensional G2. A falsifier for C=2 must give exact
A_i and a legal Phi,B with V-k>2(V-s), including a rigorous upper bound on
unrestricted k. A falsifier for every finite C needs a legal family whose
ratio diverges. No novelty claim is made from this bounded source check.
