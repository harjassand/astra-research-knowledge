0. GEOMETRY AND ACTUAL SCOPE

The slip field is periodic along a one-dimensional interface between elastic
halfspaces, period P=2R. This is a continuum periodic array in actual halfspace
elasticity; it is not a finite spring network or a thin-slab Laplacian. The exact
Fourier multiplier is

  K_per e^(2pi i n x/P)=kappa |2pi n/P|e^(2pi i n x/P),
  kappa=mu/2 (anti-plane), or mu/[2(1-nu)] (in-plane).

The dynamic material domain is the whole circle. No active support is fixed in
the evolution. Positive physical initial slip rates stay positive at every finite
time; the compact free support is the limiting rescaled profile. Attraction below
is in the halfspace energy norm H^(1/2) and hence in every finite Lp norm. It
includes positive exterior leakage and excludes initial exact zeros in the
positive part of the target profile. No Hausdorff theorem for finite-time
level-set boundaries or rupture/arrest classification is asserted.

The physical model is the full logarithmic aging-law model, zero external
stressing rate and fixed sigma=D_c=1 in the explicit construction:

  V_t/V= -K_per V/a +(b/a)(V-1/theta),
  theta_t=1-V theta,   a(x)>0, b(x)>0.                      (0.1)

The physical approximation has no inertia, radiation damping, evolving normal
stress or thermal/hydrological weakening. A conclusion about (0.1) is not an
empirically validated earthquake forecast.

1. CONSTRUCTION WITHOUT AN ASSUMED ACTIVE SET IN THE DYNAMICS

Fix R=1001/1000, P=2R, beta=151/100, kappa>0. Let

  U(x)=(1-x^2)_+^(3/2), -R<=x<=R, extended periodically;
  W_*(x)=epsilon U(x), 0<epsilon<=1;
  B=b=beta kappa,   H=K_per-B.

Write delta=R-1. On the active interval (-1,1), the whole-line operator is

  K_line U=kappa(3/2-3x^2).

The exact periodic image correction is

  T(x)=(kappa/pi) sum_{n!=0} int_{-1}^1
                     U(z)/(x-z-nP)^2 dz,
  K_per U=K_line U-T(x), |x|<=1.                          (1.1)

The series is absolutely convergent and analytic on a neighborhood of the
closed active interval because the nearest image support is distance 2delta
from its edge. T is positive, even and strictly convex there; hence T'(1)>0.

Define c=a on the active interval by

  c(x)=epsilon[beta kappa U(x)-K_per U(x)].                (1.2)

Since beta<2, the elementary function
beta(1-s)^(3/2)+3s-3/2 increases for 0<=s<=1. Thus
c>=epsilon kappa(beta-3/2)>0 inside. Put

  c_e=c(1)=-epsilon K_per U(1)>0,
  d_e=c'(1-)=epsilon[6kappa+T'(1)]>0.

On the gap from x=1 to the next active edge x=P-1, write z=x-1 in [0,2delta]
and extend

  c(x)=c_e+d_e z(1-z/(2delta)).                           (1.3)

This is positive, equals c_e at both edges, has derivatives d_e and -d_e there,
and has derivative zero at the midpoint. Together (1.2),(1.3) define a positive
periodic C^(1,1/2) material coefficient. A constant exterior extension would fail
the first-derivative matching; the arch is needed.

For x in the open gap the exact kernel gives

  K_per U(x)=-(kappa/pi)int_R U_per(z)/(x-z)^2 dz,
  (K_per U)''(x)=-(6kappa/pi)int_R U_per(z)/(x-z)^4 dz<0.

The endpoint values are equal by periodic reflection symmetry. Therefore
K_per U(x)>=K_per U(1) throughout the gap. Define

  G=H W_*+c.

Then G=0 on (-1,1), G>0 in the open gap, and G=0 at its endpoints. Hence
W_* obeys the all-W<=1 profile equation plus the contact/invasion inequality on
its exterior, with nonsingular tips W=W'=0. Its support is a solved compact
subset of the whole circle, not a dynamic boundary imposed as a condition.

2. EXPLICIT FULL-DOMAIN CONSTRAINED COERCIVITY

All operator/forms in this section act on the full circle. The spectrum of H is
exactly one negative eigenvalue -beta kappa (the constant Fourier mode) and
positive remaining eigenvalues at least

  kappa d_+=kappa(pi/R-beta)>1.628 kappa.

across a gap use |U'|<=3sqrt(2d) at each tip, yielding cross-gap constant <=6.
For any C^(1,alpha) function f, split the symmetric second-difference formula

  |D|f(x)=(1/(2pi))int_R [2f(x)-f(x+r)-f(x-r)]/r^2 dr

at |r|=h when comparing x and x+h. If [f']_alpha=L, the inner second-difference
is bounded by 2^alpha L|r|^(1+alpha)/(1+alpha), and its x derivative by

  [K_per U]_(1/2)
  <=8kappa[2^(3/2)/((3/4)pi)+4/pi]<20kappa.               (2.1)

Consequently c<=4.6epsilon kappa throughout the circle. In the gap, (2.1) and

  0<=G<=24epsilon kappa sqrt(delta),

Periodic images only reduce the U elastic energy relative to the isolated
profile: <U,K_per U><=<U,K_line U>=3pi kappa/8. Also ||U||_2^2=32/35. Therefore

  h[W_*]=<W_*,HW_*><=-epsilon^2 kappa d_0,
  d_0=beta(32/35)-3pi/8=0.2024741834...>0.

Since c=-HW_*+G and <W_*,G>=0,

  s=<c,H^-1c>=h[W_*]+<G,H^-1G>

  h[v]>=eta ||v||_2^2,

      >=kappa beta d d_+/(P(4.6)^2)>0.011708 kappa.        (2.5)

We use the conservative eta=kappa/100 below. This holds for every full-circle

3. PHYSICAL CLOCK, EQUILIBRIUM AND DATA

For physical initial V0>0, theta0>0, define

  rho0=int cV0,  q0=V0/rho0,  y0=1/(rho0 theta0),
  rho=int cV,  q=V/rho, y=1/(rho theta), d tau/dt=rho.

Then the exact full-memory equations are

  q_tau=-(q/c)(Hq+c lambda+B y),
  y_tau=y(q-y-lambda),
  lambda=-h[q]-int B qy,
  rho_tau=rho lambda, t_tau=1/rho, int cq=1.               (3.1)

Let C=int cW_*=-h[W_*]>0, q_*=W_*/C, lambda_*=1/C,
g=G/C=Hq_*+c lambda_*, Q=||q_*||_infinity=epsilon/C.
Thus g>=0, gq_*=0 and h[q_*]=-lambda_*.

Take q0,y0 periodic C^(1+alpha), 0<alpha<1/2, both strictly positive. Define

  F0=int c{q_* log(q_*/q0)+q0-q_*}+int B y0.             (3.2)

The initial set satisfying F0<ell is nonempty and open in the physical
C^(1+alpha) topology: q0 can be the mass-normalized q_*+small positive constant,
and theta0 can be chosen arbitrarily large but finite so y0>0 is small.
No blow-up time occurs in (3.2). The condition includes strictly positive slip
outside the equilibrium active interval. The entropy is infinite if an exact
zero removes positive profile mass, as required by support invariance.

4. STRICT ALL-W<1 BASIN AND QUANTITATIVE TIME BOUND

For 0<epsilon<1 put delta_*=lambda_*-Q>0. Let B_min=B_max=B here; retaining
general notation below makes constants transparent. Choose ell>0 with

  ell<=1/4,
  Qell<=delta_*/2,
  B_max^2 ell^2/(eta B_min)<=1/2.                         (4.1)

For F0<ell, write v=q-q_*, A=int gq, L=int By, Y2=int By^2. The exact identity

  F'= -h[v]-A-int B[(lambda-q_*)y+y^2]                    (4.2)

follows by differentiating the reverse q entropy and the linear y storage: the
two int Bvy couplings cancel. Expanding lambda gives the absorbing estimate

  F'<=-h[v]/2-A/2-delta_*L/2-Y2/2.                      (4.3)

Thus int(h[v]+A+Y2)dtau<=O(F0), int Ldtau<=2F0/delta_*, and

  0<=int_0^S(lambda_*-lambda)dtau<=D
  :=2F0[3+Q/delta_*+B_max/sqrt(B_min eta)]                (4.4)

on every interval of existence. This does not assume lambda>=Q along the

Once global tau existence is proved below, rho grows exponentially and

  1/(rho0 lambda_*) <= T-t0 <=exp(D)/(rho0 lambda_*),

These are model-predicted finite-time bounds from present physical data and

5. CRITICAL TOUCH Wmax=1 WITHOUT A UNIFORM MEMORY GAP

Set epsilon=1. Then q_*=U/C, Q=lambda_*, and

  delta(x)=lambda_*-q_*(x)=lambda_*[1-U(x)]
          >=k x^2, k=lambda_*/R^2, -R<=x<=R.             (5.1)

Here the pointwise memory gap is exactly zero at x=0. Let P_y=int Bdelta(x)y
and Y2=int By^2. For every r>0, splitting inside/outside |x|<=r yields

  L<=sqrt(2Br Y2)+P_y/(k r^2).

With r=[(P_y+Y2)/(2Bk^2)]^(1/5), this proves

  D_y:=P_y+Y2>=k0 L^(5/3),
  k0=k^(1/3)/(2^(7/3)B^(2/3)).                           (5.2)

The inequality also holds if r exceeds R, since the interior measure is still
at most 2r and the exterior term then vanishes. Choose ell>0 satisfying

  ell<=1/4, Qell<=lambda_*/2,
  B_max^2 ell^2/(eta B_min)<=1/4,
  ell<=(k0/(4Q))^3.                                    (5.3)

The same cancellation, without assuming a positive minimum gap, gives

  F'<=-h[v]/2-A/2-D_y/2.                                (5.4)

Indeed QL^2<=D_y/4 follows (5.2),(5.3), and Lint Bvy is absorbed into h[v]/4
and Y2/4 by Young's inequality. Consequently int(h[v]+A+Y2)dtau<infinity
and int L^(5/3)dtau<infinity. Although int Ldtau is not yet controlled,

  I=int Bqy<=Qell+h[v]/2+(B_max^2/(2B_min eta))Y2,

so lambda_- has the uniform lifetime bound

  int lambda_-dtau<=D_c:=F0[7+B_max^2/(B_min eta)].        (5.5)

This supplies the maximum bound used below. L' is bounded once q,y,lambda are
bounded, so the integrability in (5.4) implies L->0. Energy convergence below
then gives lambda->lambda_*. Thus rho grows as exp((lambda_*+o(1))tau), T is
finite and (T-t)rho->1/lambda_* by sandwiching the clock tail using the eventual
bounds lambda_*-zeta<=lambda<=lambda_*+zeta. No L1 rate for lambda-lambda_*
is assumed at the critical point. The nonlinear memory damping and spatial
quadratic detuning replace the lost uniform linear gap.

6. PERIODIC NONLINEAR MAXIMUM AND A PRIORI ENERGY

The constant Fourier mode makes the whole-line cubic maximum inequality false
on a circle without a floor. Let M=max q, m=int q<=1/c_min. The periodic kernel
contains the whole-line kernel for displacements |r|<=P/2. At a maximum and
r0=m/M, if M>=8m/P, then

  |D|_per q(x_M)
  >=(1/pi)int_{r0<=|r|<=P/2}(M-q(x_M+r))/r^2 dr
  >=(1/pi)[M^2/m-4M/P]>=M^2/(2pi m).                   (6.1)

Consequently, for M above M_floor=8/(c_min P),

  D^+M<=-alpha_M M^3+p_max M^2+lambda_-M,
  alpha_M=kappa c_min/(2pi c_max), p_max=B_max/c_min.

Let E=int lambda_- and N=e^-E M. At every prospective upward crossing with
N>=max(M_floor,p_max/alpha_M), the cubic term defeats the quadratic term,
since its extra factor e^(2E) grows faster. Thus, with D_life=D or D_c,

  M<=Mbar=e^(D_life)max(M0,M_floor,p_max/alpha_M).          (6.2)

The memory maximum equation gives similarly

  y<=Ybar=e^(D_life)max(y0_max,Mbar).                     (6.3)

These bounds are uniform on every lifetime, including before global existence
has been proved; they therefore do not assume the desired conclusion.

Let Lambda=-h[q], zeta=Hq+cLambda. Then int q zeta=0. The normalized energy
E_q=h[q]/2 satisfies exactly

  E_q'=-int(q/c)zeta^2-int(q/c)zeta B y
       <=-D_q/2+(Mbar B_max/(2c_min))Y2,
  D_q=int(q/c)zeta^2.                                  (6.4)

The obstacle energy gap is

  E_q-E_q*=A+h[v]/2>=0.                                (6.5)

Equations (4.3) or (5.4) make both the forcing Y2 in (6.4) and this energy gap
time-integrable. Thus the energy has a finite limit: add the tail integral of
its forcing to make a monotone nonnegative quantity. Its time-integrable gap
forces the limit to equal E_q*. Therefore

  h[q-q_*]->0,  A->0,  q->q_* in H^(1/2) and every finite Lp. (6.6)

The form norm follows <v,K_per v>=h[v]+B||v||^2 and (2.5). In particular the
normalization and dynamic limiting support are controlled on the whole circle.
Uniform q,y bounds imply I<=Mbar L. For the strict branch, L->0 follows its
integrability and bounded derivative; for the critical branch use L^(5/3).
Together (6.6) gives lambda->lambda_* in both branches.

Uniform energy and (6.3) also give a uniform lambda bound without circular
regularity assumptions:

  lambda_*-2[E_q(0)-E_q*+(Mbar B_max/c_min)F0]-Mbar F0
  <=lambda<=lambda_*.

7. CONTINUUM EXISTENCE, UNIQUENESS AND CONTINUATION IMPORTS

This section is the key audit gate: it is not an assumption of global solutions.
For positive C^(1+alpha) data and c in C^(1,1/2), there is a unique maximal
classical periodic solution. One local construction freezes the positive
operator A0=(q0/c)K_per, solves u_tau+A0u=f, and iterates the remainder
(q0-q)K_per q/c plus the polynomial reaction and memory ODE. The linear
operator is uniformly elliptic of order one. Linear solvability is obtained by
freezing its spatial coefficient locally, the Fourier Poisson semigroup for
the frozen constant operator, and method of continuity with the Schauder
estimate listed below. No spatial boundary is present. The local contraction
uses the parabolic C^(1+alpha) space, with time and space scaled equally for
order one; differences with zero initial data have a C^alpha norm smaller by
T^vartheta, vartheta>0, than their C^(1+alpha) norm. Thus the two coefficient
remainder factors and the reaction/memory difference yield a contraction for
small T. The scalar nonlocal lambda is locally Lipschitz in this construction:

  h[q1]-h[q2]=<q1-q2,H(q1+q2)>,

and H(q1+q2) is bounded for bounded C^(1+alpha) iterates. Its time Hölder
regularity follows the same formula and interpolation. This is the standard
linear-freezing proof, and its hypotheses are explicitly checked here rather
than treating positivity or sectoriality as an unproved profile consequence.

To rule out finite maximal tau, let tau_max<infinity. At a minimum of q on the
circle, K_per q<=0. Since lambda<=lambda_* and y<=Ybar,

  min q(tau)>=min q0 exp[-(lambda_*+p_maxYbar)tau].        (7.1)

Hence on every finite tau interval the coefficient a_dyn=q/c is bounded above
and below by positive constants. The equation is

  q_tau+a_dyn K_per q=f,
  f=q[(B/c)q-(B/c)y-lambda],                              (7.2)

with bounded q,f, independently of derivative estimates. Lift the periodic
fields to R; the kernel is exactly a_dyn(x,t)kappa/(pi|z|^2), symmetric in z.

PRIMARY IMPORT A: Schwab--Silvestre, arXiv:1412.3790v1, Theorem 1.1 (and its
precise Theorem 7.1), proves space-time Holder regularity for bounded solutions
with bounded forcing and measurable kernels satisfying A1--A4. Our order is
one, drift zero, kernel symmetric and pointwise comparable to |z|^-2 with the
finite-interval bounds from (7.1),(6.2). Thus all A1--A4 hold. The lifted solution
is bounded globally in x. Covering the circle on an interval away from tau=0
gives a uniform C^gamma bound up to any putative finite tau_max. This step does
not assume q or lambda is Holder beforehand.

The memory ODE with bounded coefficients makes y C^gamma in space, uniformly
on a finite interval, by subtracting the equations at two spatial points and
applying Gronwall; lambda is scalar in space and cancels in that difference.
Therefore a_dyn and f in (7.2) have uniform C^gamma spatial moduli, while their
time dependence, including lambda, may still be merely bounded/measurable.

PRIMARY IMPORT B: Dong--Jin--Zhang, arXiv:1710.01253v3, Theorem 1.6, allows
coefficients and forcing measurable in time and uniformly C^gamma in space,
0<gamma<1, order sigma=1, symmetric cancellation condition (1.2). It gives
uniform C_x^(1+gamma) control and C_x^gamma control of q_tau on interior time
cylinders. All its kernel annular Holder bounds follow a_dyn=q/c; drift is
zero; periodic lift supplies its global spatial C^gamma norm. This avoids a
circular assumption of time regularity for h[q] or lambda. The theorem is
applied to the already classical maximal solution as an a priori estimate;
one may lower gamma below min(alpha,1/2) to match its initial smoothness.

Consequently q has uniform C^(1+gamma) spatial bounds near tau_max. The y ODE
propagates the same spatial regularity; differentiating in x and applying
Gronwall uses these q bounds and c regularity. The bounded q_tau norm and
compact interpolation give positive limits q(tau_max), y(tau_max) in
C^(1+gamma') for any gamma'<gamma. Restarting the local construction contradicts
maximality. Therefore tau_max=infinity.

Uniqueness on a finite interval can also be checked directly. For two classical
solutions, put v=q1-q2 and w=y1-y2. Multiply the v equation by cv/q1; its leading
term is -<v,K_per v>. The other local coefficients, time derivative of c/q1,
and Hq2 are bounded on the interval by (7.1) and the Schauder bounds. The scalar
lambda difference is bounded by C(||v||_2+||w||_2) using the displayed symmetric
quadratic identity. Combining with the memory difference gives
d/dtau[int(c/q1)v^2+||w||_2^2]<=C[int(c/q1)v^2+||w||_2^2].
Gronwall proves uniqueness and continuous dependence; it does not require a
uniform positive lower bound for q at tau=infinity.

8. RESULTING PHYSICAL THEOREM AND MEANING OF FREE SUPPORT

For every displayed material construction and each initial positive
C^(1+alpha) physical pair whose normalized entropy satisfies (4.1) or (5.3),
the physical solution of (0.1) exists up to a finite T determined by its initial
data, and

  (T-t)V(x,t)->W_*(x)=epsilon(1-x^2)_+^(3/2)
       in H^(1/2) and every finite Lp on the circle.        (8.1)

The state memory remains finite and positive at every t<T and is fully included
in the basin. The equilibrium active set (-1,1) is the same limiting profile
for every admitted initial datum, even when its exterior slip is positive. Thus
the support may differ from the initial effective slipping region; the nonlinear
flow selects the compact free support under material pinning. For any fixed
positive threshold whose target level set has measure zero, convergence in
Lp gives convergence of the thresholded active sets in measure. No assertion
of an exact finite-time moving zero front is made, since the original positive
rate-and-state model has no such front.

For almost every spatial point, the aging-law logarithmic state exponent obeys

  log theta(x,t)/log(T-t) -> W_*(x).                      (8.2)

Indeed int(v^2+y^2)dtau<infinity pointwise almost everywhere by Fubini, so
int_0^tau v ds=o(tau), int_0^tau y ds=o(tau), and
log y=log y0+int(q-y-lambda)ds=(q_*-lambda_*)tau+o(tau).
Together log rho=lambda_*tau+o(tau) and log(T-t)=-lambda_*tau+o(tau), this yields
(8.2). At the epsilon=1 contact point x=0, no pointwise logarithmic correction
is inferred from an almost-everywhere result; the theorem handles attraction
and physical blow-up there through the nonlinear memory inequality (5.2).

The finite entropy basin and explicit time interval in the strict case give
quantitatively distinguishable predictions within this constitutive model:
the cubic-root tip shape, exterior restoring exponent proportional to sqrt
distance, a fixed normalized nucleation patch, and aging state logarithmic
exponents. These must be compared with observations or experiments separately.
For epsilon=.01 the whole material is rate weakening because c<=.046kappa
while b=1.51kappa. At epsilon=1 some exterior material is rate strengthening;
that is part of the manufactured heterogeneous profile, not an unnoticed
uniform b>a assumption.
