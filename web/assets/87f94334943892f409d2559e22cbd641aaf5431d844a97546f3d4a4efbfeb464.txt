CYCLE 3 — LEAF 03: DETERMINISTIC PERMANENCE FOR THE CATALYST-COUPLED WR FAMILY
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED for classwise deterministic permanence under fixed positive rates and under arbitrary measurable selections from compact positive rate intervals. No novelty claim. ODE, positivity formulas, stoichiometric span, and the catalyst exposure estimate are reconstructed here.

QUESTION / SCOPE

Fix finite nonempty index sets J={1,...,p} and I={1,...,q}; let A_j (j∈J) and B_i (i∈I) be distinct species. Fix integers k_{ji}≥1. For each j and (j,i), include the reversible reaction pairs

  0 <-> A_j,
  A_j <-> A_j+B_i,
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_{ji}+1)A_j+B_i.

The only deterministic interface used below is ordinary mass action on the positive orthant. Each directed edge has its own coefficient. Either coefficients are fixed positive constants, or each is a measurable function of time lying in a nonempty compact interval [l_r,u_r]⊂(0,∞). A common box 0<κ≤k_r(t)≤K<∞ is a convenient stronger notation; for per-edge intervals take κ=min_r l_r and K=max_r u_r. There are 2p+4pq directed reactions, so the network is finite. All k_ji are finite; no bound on their maximum other than finiteness is needed for the theorem.

ODE DERIVATION

Write the six coefficient families as α_j, β_j, γ_ji, δ_ji, η_ji, θ_ji, respectively:

  0 -> A_j: α_j;       A_j -> 0: β_j;
  A_j -> A_j+B_i: γ_ji; A_j+B_i -> A_j: δ_ji;
  A_j+B_i -> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i: η_ji;
  (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i -> A_j+B_i: θ_ji.

For concentrations a_j=[A_j], b_i=[B_i], the reaction vectors are e_{A_j}, e_{B_i}, k_ji e_{A_j} and their negatives. Thus the mass-action equations are

  a'_j = α_j(t) - β_j(t)a_j
          + Σ_i k_ji[η_ji(t)a_j b_i - θ_ji(t)a_j^(k_ji+1)b_i],
  b'_i = Σ_j[γ_ji(t)a_j - δ_ji(t)a_j b_i].                         (1)

The A_j <-> A_j+B_i pair changes only B_i; the high-order pair preserves B_i and changes A_j by ±k_ji. In particular, the catalyst equation aggregates all substrates. Define

  C_i(t,a)=Σ_j γ_ji(t)a_j,       D_i(t,a)=Σ_j δ_ji(t)a_j.

Then b'_i=C_i-D_i b_i. It is incorrect to treat each A_j as supplying an independent catalyst equation; the aggregate is the exact equation.

FULL STOICHIOMETRIC CLASS

The 0 <-> A_j pairs contribute each basis vector e_{A_j}; the A_j <-> A_j+B_i pairs contribute each e_{B_i}. Therefore the stoichiometric subspace S=R^(p+q), and the unique positive stoichiometric compatibility class is P=R_{>0}^{p+q}. The nonnegative orthant is the corresponding invariant polyhedron. The reaction graph has one linkage class: every A_j is joined to 0, and each A_j+B_i and (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i is joined to A_j by a reversible edge. Since every edge is included with its reverse, this graph is strongly connected and weakly reversible.

POSITIVITY AND LOCAL WELL-POSEDNESS

With measurable bounded rates, (1) is a Caratheodory ODE: its right side is measurable in t and locally Lipschitz in the state uniformly on bounded sets. Hence each initial state has a unique maximal absolutely continuous solution.

Along any such solution, rewrite the first equation as a scalar linear equation in a_j with a trajectory-dependent locally integrable coefficient:

  a'_j = α_j(t)+h_j(t)a_j,
  h_j(t)=-β_j(t)+Σ_i k_ji[η_ji(t)b_i(t)-θ_ji(t)a_j(t)^k_ji b_i(t)].

For H_j(t)=∫_0^t h_j(s)ds, the integrating-factor identity is

  a_j(t)=e^{H_j(t)}[a_j(0)+∫_0^t e^{-H_j(s)}α_j(s)ds].             (2)

Thus a_j(t)>0 for positive initial a_j and every finite t before the maximal time. Even from a_j(0)=0, (2) gives a_j(t)>0 for t>0 because α_j≥κ>0.

For catalysts, the exact variation-of-constants formula is

  b_i(t)=e^{-G_i(t)}[b_i(0)+∫_0^t e^{G_i(s)}C_i(s,a(s))ds],
  G_i(t)=∫_0^t D_i(s,a(s))ds.                                   (3)

For b_i(0)>0 this stays strictly positive. If b_i(0)=0, then every a_j(s)>0 for s>0 by (2), so C_i(s,a(s))>0 and (3) gives b_i(t)>0 for all t>0. This proves positive invariance and strict interior preservation up to any possible explosion time; global continuation is addressed below.

EXPLICIT CATALYST EXPOSURE ESTIMATE

If κ≤γ_ji,δ_ji≤K and a_j≥0, then C_i/D_i∈[r_-,r_+] whenever D_i>0, with r_-=κ/K and r_+=K/κ. (For per-edge intervals use min_j l^γ_ji/u^δ_ji and max_j u^γ_ji/l^δ_ji.) Since all a_j(t)>0 for t>0, D_i(t)>0 there. Equations (3) and C_i∈[r_-D_i,r_+D_i] give the sharp exposure-time sandwich

  b_i(0)e^{-G_i(t)}+r_-[1-e^{-G_i(t)}]
       ≤ b_i(t) ≤
  b_i(0)e^{-G_i(t)}+r_+[1-e^{-G_i(t)}].                          (4)

This is explicit and rate-path robust, but it is measured in cumulative substrate exposure G_i, not clock time. The equations alone do not provide a uniform lower bound on D_i in physical time when catalyst levels are large; consequently (4) is not by itself a clock-time absorber proof. The permanence theorem below supplies the coupled high/low-coordinate argument.

PERMANENCE THEOREM GATE AND APPLICATION

Primary theorem: Gopalkrishnan–Miller–Shiu, “A geometric approach to the Global Attractor Conjecture,” SIAM J. Appl. Dyn. Syst. 13(2) (2014), 758–797, DOI 10.1137/130928170; arXiv:1305.5303v2 (18 Dec 2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.5303v2 . The theorem is for reaction systems/differential inclusions, not just one fixed coefficient vector:

• Definition 3.8 assigns each reaction a nonempty compact interval of positive coefficients (“tempering”); Definition 3.23's mass-action differential inclusion permits all reaction-rate choices in those intervals at each state/time. Any measurable coefficient schedule in the supplied intervals gives an absolutely continuous solution whose derivative lies in the inclusion fiber a.e.; the source writes trajectories as differentiable curves, but its drift bounds are pointwise in every interval-fiber velocity, and the chain-rule/invariance steps extend to these Caratheodory selections by the a.e. chain rule.
• Corollary 3.20 states that every weakly reversible network with exactly one linkage class is strongly endotactic.
• Theorem 1.1 states every strongly endotactic reaction network is permanent. Equivalently, for a fixed network, a supplied compact positive rate box and a positive invariant polyhedron/class, the associated mass-action differential inclusion has one compact subset of the class interior such that every trajectory defined on a ray eventually remains there. Theorem 1.3 gives the immediate corollary that every one-linkage weakly reversible network is permanent.
• The proof's relevant intermediate statements are Theorem 7.5 (the entropy g(x)=Σ_s[x_s log x_s−x_s] decreases outside a compact set uniformly over the tempering), Theorem 8.1 (bounded trajectories), Theorem 8.5 (persistence), and Lemmas 8.7–8.8 plus Theorem 1.1 (eventual compact containment/permanence).

Our graph has one linkage class and is weakly reversible, so Corollary 3.20 and Theorem 1.1 apply in every finite dimension p+q. All complexes are nonnegative integer vectors; every rate lies in a compact positive interval. S=R^(p+q) means there is only P=R_{>0}^{p+q}. Hence the theorem proves: there is a common compact K⊂R_{>0}^{p+q}, depending on the finite network and the rate intervals but not on x(0) or the selected rate schedule, such that every positive solution eventually remains in K. Fixed rates are the singleton-interval special case. This also yields global existence. Theorem 7.5 gives a compact Γ such that dU/dt<0 outside Γ for every rate selection. Along any local solution, U is therefore bounded above by max{U(x(0)), sup_Γ U}: a first upward crossing of this level must occur outside Γ, where the derivative is negative. Each scalar term x log x-x is bounded below by -1 and tends to +infinity as x tends to infinity, so this upper level bound gives a finite coordinate upper bound, ruling out finite-time blow-up. On that bounded finite-time interval the integrals in (2)–(3) are finite and their formulas prevent finite-time boundary loss. Standard Caratheodory continuation then excludes a finite maximal time.

This is a deterministic result only. It implies nothing here about recurrence/nonexplosion of the stochastic CTMC; molecule-count factorial propensities and physical-time holding costs are a distinct interface.

RATE CONDITIONS, COMPLEX BALANCE, AND WEAK REVERSIBILITY

Complex balance is rate-dependent and unnecessary. To see that the theorem covers non-complex-balanced coefficients, take p=q=1 and any k≥1, with rates α=2, β=1, γ=δ=η=θ=1. The graph is the path 0 <-> A <-> A+B <-> (k+1)A+B. Complex balance at the two end complexes forces c_A=2 and c_A^k=1, impossible for k≥1. Yet the one-linkage permanence theorem applies. Weak reversibility alone is generally weaker than the one-linkage hypothesis in multi-linkage networks; this family has exactly one linkage class, which is the load-bearing structural simplification for the literature transfer. Strong endotacticity is the geometric condition actually used in the all-dimension permanence theorem; here it follows from one-linkage weak reversibility. The 2010 two-species endotactic κ-variable permanence theorem (Craciun–Nazarov–Pantea, arXiv:1010.3050, Theorem 19) is not the right general-dimensional gate; the 2014 theorem covers this family for arbitrary p,q.

ENTRY-TIME QUANTIFIER AND LOWER BOUND

The common absorber K is independent of initial state, but the cited permanence result gives eventual entry trajectory-by-trajectory, not a uniform entry time over all positive initial states. Uniformity over all initial concentrations is impossible even in a one-pair member of the family, including for fixed non-complex-balanced rates. For p=q=1, rates (2,1,1,1,1,1) as above, equations are

  a'=2-a+k ab(1-a^k),       b'=a(1-b).

Start at a(0)=3, b(0)=b_0>1. Whenever a≥3, a'≤2-a≤−1 because 1-a^k≤0. Thus a(t)≤3. The second equation solves exactly:

  b(t)=1+(b_0-1)exp(-∫_0^t a(s)ds)
       ≥ 1+(b_0-1)e^{-3t}.                                      (5)

Let M=max_{(a,b)∈K}b. Since b(t)>1 at every finite time for these starts, an absorbing K must have M>1. First entry into K requires b(t)≤M, so (5) implies

  T_entry ≥ (1/3) log((b_0-1)/(M-1)).                             (6)

As b_0→∞ this diverges. Therefore no class-wide finite entry bound independent of x(0) can be claimed, even at a single fixed rate vector. No explicit K thresholds or computable upper bound on T_entry(x0) are extracted from the cited proof; (6) is a quantitative lower bound falsifying the stronger uniform-time interpretation.
