# Primary mathematical source notes

Search/read date: 2026-10-08. Scope is mathematical information theory and Markov processes only.

1. **Blackwell, David (1951), “Comparison of Experiments,” Proceedings of the Second Berkeley Symposium on Mathematical Statistics and Probability, pp. 93–102.** [Project Euclid record](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.bsmsp/1200500222); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520411586-009). Primary prior art for ordering statistical experiments by whether one can simulate a decision rule for one experiment from another. The present report uses only the elementary special case that a deterministic endpoint statistic is a garbling of the full path, so cannot improve any decision risk.

2. **Neyman, Jerzy and Egon S. Pearson (1933), “The testing of statistical hypotheses in relation to probabilities a priori,” Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society 29(4).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S030500410001152X). Primary prior art for the likelihood-ratio optimal test between two simple hypotheses. The report applies it to iid Bernoulli event marks, where the log-likelihood ratio is monotone in the number of passes, giving an exact threshold-counter design.

3. **Chazottes, Jean-René, Cristian Giardina, and Frank Redig (2006), “Relative entropy and waiting times for continuous-time Markov processes,” Electronic Journal of Probability 11, 1049–1068.** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v11-374). Primary CTMC path-space prior art on relative entropy and waiting-time likelihoods for finite-state continuous-time Markov processes. The present derivation is a finite, nonstationary absorbing-chain calculation and does not import their asymptotic limit theorems.

4. **Dupuis, Paul, Markos A. Katsoulakis, Yannis Pantazis, and Petr Plecháč (2016), “Path-Space Information Bounds for Uncertainty Quantification and Sensitivity Analysis of Stochastic Dynamics,” SIAM/ASA Journal on Uncertainty Quantification 4(1), 80–111.** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1025645); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.05136). Primary mathematical prior art deriving relative-entropy and Fisher-information formulas for path-space laws, including CTMCs. Relevant boundary: the report's eventwise KL accumulation is a special case; no new general path-space information inequality is claimed.

5. **Casanellas, Marta, Jesús Fernández-Sánchez, and Jordi Roca-Lacostena (2020), “The embedding problem for Markov matrices.”** [arXiv:2005.00818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00818). Primary prior art on existence and nonuniqueness of CTMC generators Q whose fixed-time transition matrix is `exp(Q)`, including rate identifiability. The report gives a separate explicit three-state circulant alias pair as a concrete instance; it does not claim to solve the embedding problem.

6. **Israel, Robert B., Jeffrey S. Rosenthal, and Jason Z. Wei (2001), “Finding Generators for Markov Chains via Empirical Transition Matrices, with Applications to Credit Ratings,” Mathematical Finance 11(2), 245–265.** [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9965.00114). Primary prior work on when an empirical fixed-time transition matrix has a continuous-time generator, and on selecting among generators. The report's fixed-time alias is deliberately outside a blanket uniqueness claim; we do not reproduce or rely on their conditions.

## Evidence boundary

The exact serial-chain KL identity, binomial likelihood-ratio statistic, and the numerical generator alias are reconstructed in `proof.md`. Source articles establish the prior-art boundary, not external validation of this report. No external reviewer or independent formal checker was used.
