For characteristic-p pairs K^p⊆H⊆K and L^p⊆C⊆L with relative p-ranks at least two, define V=K*/H* as an Fp-vector space and quotient V⊗V by the Steinberg tensors [x]⊗[1−x]. An Fp-linear bijection of V preserving the vanishing of pure tensors in both directions is uniquely a nonzero Fp scalar times the map induced by an isomorphism of field pairs. Infinite relative p-rank is allowed.

The following independently checked points carry the extension:

1. Every strict intermediate adjunction has degree p because x^p lies in the relative base. Zorn gives a relative p-basis, and every element has a **finite** monomial expansion. Euler derivations are well defined on these finite expansions. Neither perfectness nor algebraic closedness of the relative base is used.
2. If x,y are relatively p-independent, the alternating logarithmic-derivative pairing descends through the relative Steinberg quotient and takes {x,y} to 1. Thus pure-symbol vanishing forces y∈H(x). Only this implication and explicit affine Steinberg pairs are used; no converse or deep differential-symbol injectivity is silently assumed.
3. In H(X,Y), the intersection XH(Y/X)∩UH(V/U) leads to q_X+q_Y=1 and A_Xq_X+A_Yq_Y=0. The ratio A_X/A_Y is killed by D_X+D_Y; because H(X)∩H(Y)=H, the A's have the same nonzero Euler eigenvalue. This yields the unique common power correction. For U^n=a+bX^n and V^n=c+dY^n, the explicit witness (cU^n−aV^n)^r, nr≡1 mod p, lies in both spaces. Any two witnesses have identical X,Y logarithmic derivatives, so their quotient lies in H. The claimed one-dimensional intersection follows.
4. The complete multipartite graph of distinct degree-p image subfields is connected. The four affine Steinberg pairs propagate one exponent, independent of neighbor and affine coefficient. Applying the argument to the inverse, and the nonzero binomial coefficient at x^(r−1), proves line equality rather than just line inclusion.
5. Infinite-dimensional projective lifting is legitimate: finite spans and their intersections are preserved, images of a projective basis form a basis, every coordinate vector has finite support, and two-coordinate planes determine a single additive and multiplicative base-field map. Normalizing the lift at 1 and comparing S(f·) with S(f)S(·) forces field multiplicativity and sends H onto C.

The rank-one counterexample uses a separate classical differential-symbol theorem; it is useful sharpness evidence but is not needed for the positive theorem. The main theorem is a structural reconstruction statement with an entire invariant and a globally compatible bijection supplied. It does not furnish finite-query acquisition, compute that bijection, recover a chosen integral model, or establish a polynomial bit bound.

This extends the release's function-field/algebraically-closed-base/full-relation-space interface to arbitrary exponent-one pairs and a pure-zero predicate. It is new research capital for classification of height-one inseparable field data, not a claimed foundational breakthrough. The next meaningful gate is exact prior art for this relative invariant and an effective framed input model; finite symbolic examples cannot certify global predicate preservation.
