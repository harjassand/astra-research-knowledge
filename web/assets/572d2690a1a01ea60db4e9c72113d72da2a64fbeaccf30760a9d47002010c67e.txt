1. DECISIVE REPRESENTATION

Use the Gaussian phase-retrieval model from v1. Clean observations are iid

    a ~ N(0,I_d),   Y=(a^T x*)^2+zeta,
    q=||x*||>0,
    E[zeta|a]=0, E[zeta^2|a]=sigma^2, E[zeta^4|a]<=K4^4,

where the two conditional moments are constants, and the conditional law of zeta may otherwise depend arbitrarily on its own sensing vector a. Write r=K4/q^2 and assume the learner knows R>=1+r^2 (it is enough that R=1+r_up^2 for a supplied upper bound r_up>=r). The adversary sees the complete clean batch and replaces up to epsilon m pairs (a_i,Y_i) arbitrarily, including both entries of each pair.

For a scale L>0 define

    c_L(y)=clip(y,[-L,L]),
    g_L(y)=L+c_L(y) in [0,2L],
    Z_L=xi sqrt(g_L(Y)) a,

where xi is an independent Rademacher sign generated by the learner after receiving the corrupted sample. The sign is not needed for the outer product; it makes E Z_L=0 even when the conditional noise law depends on a and the unsiged feature has nonzero mean. Every corrupted pair is transformed by the same rule and is still an arbitrary outlier to the robust-PCA algorithm.

The representation changes only the spectral feature, not the measurement or final objective. It turns a potentially signed response into a nonnegative, bounded scalar weight on a Gaussian design row. Its population second moment is close to the known rank-one spike

    S_L = E[(L+Y)aa^T]
        = (L+q^2)I_d + 2x*x*^T.                                      (2.1)

The top eigendirection is x*/q, the eigengap is exactly 2q^2, and the eigenvalues are L+3q^2 and L+q^2. The actual feature second moment is Q_L=E[Z_L Z_L^T]=E[g_L(Y)aa^T]. Because |c_L(y)-y|<=y^2/L,

    ||Q_L-S_L||op
       <= L^{-1} || E[Y^2 aa^T] ||op
       = (15q^4+sigma^2)/L.                                          (2.2)

For the last equality, Gaussian Wick moments give

    E[Y^2 aa^T]=(3q^4+sigma^2)I_d+12q^2 x*x*^T.                      (2.3)

In particular the noise term is sigma^2 I, not 3 sigma^2 I. Formula (2.3) also confirms that the matrix printed in Das–Batra Question 4.8, q^2 I+2xx^T, is E[(a^T x*)^2 aa^T], not E[Y^2 aa^T] for their earlier vector feature Ya. Here (2.1) is intentionally the target for a different, PSD-shifted feature.

2. FINITE-SAMPLE ADAPTIVE-DELETION STABILITY THEOREM

Fix a deterministic interval I=[L_-,L_+] contained in (0,infinity), mesh spacing Delta>0, and the deterministic grid L_0,...,L_J covering I with J+1<=2+(L_+-L_-)/Delta. The interval is fixed by the unknown data-generating parameter q for the proof; the algorithm need not know it. Let

    Qhat_L = m^{-1} sum_i g_L(Y_i) a_i a_i^T,
    Ahat   = m^{-1} sum_i a_i a_i^T,
    t      = d log 9 + log(4(J+1)/delta),
    H      = ||Ahat||op.

With probability at least 1-delta over the clean batch, both

    sup_{L in I} ||Qhat_L-Q_L||op
       <= C L_+ (sqrt(t/m)+t/m) + 2 Delta (H+1),                   (2.4)

and, for k=ceil(alpha m)<=m/2,

    sup_{I subset [m], |I|<=k} ||m^{-1} sum_{i in I} a_i a_i^T||op
       <= G_{m,k,delta}
       := [sqrt(k)+sqrt(d)+sqrt(2(k log(e m/k)+log(4/delta)))]^2/m.  (2.5)

The event (2.5) implies the same bound for every fractional vector v_i in [0,1] with sum_i v_i<=alpha m, because for each fixed unit u the maximum of sum_i v_i(a_i^T u)^2 is attained by placing unit weights on the largest k directional squares and a possible fractional endpoint. The event is uniform after seeing all (a_i,Y_i), so the deletion may depend jointly on the full covariate-response batch.

For any alpha in [0,1/2), any fractional retained weights w_i in [0,1] with m^{-1}sum_i(1-w_i)<=alpha, and any L in I, define

    M_w(L) = [m^{-1} sum_i w_i g_L(Y_i) a_i a_i^T]
             / [m^{-1} sum_i w_i].

Let S_L be (2.1), let b=(15q^4+sigma^2)/L_-, and set

    B = C L_+ (sqrt(t/m)+t/m) + 2 Delta(H+1)
        + b + 2L_+ G_{m,ceil(alpha m),delta} + alpha(L_+ + 3q^2).

If

    B <= (1-alpha) gamma (L_-+q^2),                               (2.6)

then, simultaneously for every L in I and every such adaptive fractional deletion,

    (1-gamma)S_L <= M_w(L) <= (1+gamma)S_L.                       (2.7)

This is stability relative to the deterministic reference S_L, not a claim that the empirical covariance equals S_L. The clipping bias is charged explicitly in b; this choice makes the target's eigengap exact and avoids a second Davis–Kahan perturbation in the PCA step.

Proof. For fixed L and unit u, W_i=g_L(Y_i)(a_i^T u)^2 is nonnegative and has subexponential norm at most C L_+, since 0<=g_L<=2L_+ and (a_i^T u)^2 has dimension-free subexponential norm. The W_i are independent over i even though g_L(Y_i) and a_i may be dependent. Scalar Bernstein and a 1/4-net of S^{d-1} of size at most 9^d give the fixed-grid bound C L_+(sqrt(t/m)+t/m) simultaneously over the grid. Pointwise, |g_L(y)-g_{L'}(y)|<=2|L-L'|. Thus

    ||Qhat_L-Qhat_{L_j}||op <= 2|L-L_j| H,
    ||Q_L-Q_{L_j}||op     <= 2|L-L_j| ||E[aa^T]||op = 2|L-L_j|,

which proves (2.4) for the entire continuum. For (2.5), for each fixed subset I of k rows the Gaussian matrix A_I with rows a_i^T satisfies

    Pr{||A_I||op > sqrt(k)+sqrt(d)+sqrt(2s)} <= 2e^{-s}.

Union-bound over at most (em/k)^k subsets, take s=k log(em/k)+log(4/delta), and divide ||A_I||op^2 by m. Smaller subsets are covered by extension to size k.

Now let v_i=1-w_i and alpha_w=m^{-1}sum_i v_i. Write D_L=m^{-1}sum_i v_i g_L(Y_i)a_i a_i^T. Since 0<=g_L<=2L_+,

    0<=D_L<=2L_+ m^{-1}sum_i v_i a_i a_i^T,
    ||D_L||op<=2L_+G_{m,ceil(alpha m),delta}.

The exact identity is

    M_w(L)-S_L = [(Qhat_L-S_L)-D_L+alpha_w S_L]/(1-alpha_w).

By (2.2), (2.4), and (2.5), the numerator norm is at most B. Also lambda_min(S_L)=L+q^2>=L_-+q^2, and alpha_w<=alpha. Inequality (2.6) gives ||M_w-S_L||op<=gamma lambda_min(S_L), which is equivalent to (2.7). QED.

The Gaussian submatrix tail (2.5) is an explicit finite-sample bound. A convenient coarser form for alpha m>=1 and alpha<=1/4 is

    G_{m,ceil(alpha m),delta}
       <= C[d/m + alpha log(e/alpha) + log(1/delta)/m].           (2.8)

The alpha log(e/alpha) term is unavoidable for Gaussian designs; see Section 6.

3. PARAMETERIZED PHASE-RETRIEVAL CONSEQUENCE

Set R=1+r_up^2. Use the source robust scalar mean estimator on the observed responses to obtain qhat with

    |qhat-q|<=q/27                                                       (3.1)

with probability at least 1-delta_s, provided m>=C R log(2/delta_s) and epsilon<=c/R. This is the source-reported Theorem E.1 of Das–Batra, using E[Y]=q^2, Var(Y)=2q^4+sigma^2<= (2+r_up^2)q^4. The algorithm selects

    Lhat = C0 R qhat^2.

For analysis define the deterministic interval

    I_q = [C0 R (26/27)^2 q^2, C0 R (28/27)^2 q^2].              (3.2)

On event (3.1), Lhat lies in I_q. The learner computes only qhat and Lhat; it does not need q or the interval endpoints. Apply the stability theorem uniformly on I_q. Let Delta=theta q^2 for a sufficiently small fixed theta; this is an analysis grid, not a computation in the algorithm. The grid size satisfies J+1<=C(1+C0R/theta), so t=O(d+log(R/delta)). On the event H<=C_H (which follows from standard Gaussian sample-covariance concentration for m>=C(d+log(1/delta))), and with alpha=21epsilon, the exact condition (2.6) becomes

    C L_+ (sqrt(t/m)+t/m) + 2 Delta(C_H+1)
      + (15q^4+sigma^2)/L_-
      + 2L_+ G_{m,ceil(21epsilon m),delta}
      + 21epsilon(L_+ + 3q^2)
       <= (1-21epsilon) gamma_0 (L_-+q^2).                       (3.3)

Here L_-=C0R(26/27)^2q^2 and L_+=C0R(28/27)^2q^2. All terms have units q^2. The ratio sigma^2<=K4^2<=r_up^2 q^4 is used only in the bias term. Choose universal constants in the following order: a sufficiently small target gamma_0=c_gamma/R; a sufficiently large C0 so the bias term is smaller than a fixed fraction of gamma_0 L_-; and a sufficiently small theta so the grid term is smaller than another fixed fraction. Then (2.8) shows it suffices, up to universal constants, that

    21epsilon < gamma_0/20,
    epsilon log(e/epsilon) <= c/R,
    m >= C R^2 [d+log(R/delta)].                                  (3.4)

The first line is the contamination prerequisite for the near-linear robust-PCA theorem; the second and third control adaptive deletion and empirical covariance error. Constants in (3.4) depend only on the universal constants of the stability and robust-PCA results. For fixed r_up and fixed confidence, m=O(d R^2), and the admissible contamination is epsilon=O(1/(R log(eR))) as a sufficient condition. This is a sufficient tradeoff, not an optimal noise/corruption frontier.

No labels or outlier identities are used. The scale estimate, feature threshold, and PCA all use one corrupted batch. Dependence of qhat on that batch is harmless for stability because (2.4) holds simultaneously over the entire deterministic interval (3.2), and (3.1) is intersected with this uniform event rather than conditioned upon.

4. TRANSFER FROM THE CLEAN BATCH TO THE ROBUST-PCA INLIER DISTRIBUTION

The nearly-linear robust-PCA theorem of Diakonikolas–Kane–Pensia–Pittas (ICML 2023, Theorem C.1) assumes a decomposition of the corrupted empirical distribution P=(1-epsilon)G+epsilon B in which G is (20epsilon,gamma)-stable relative to its target matrix. The clean-batch property (2.7), with deletion parameter alpha=21epsilon and stability gamma_0, transfers to an arbitrary set J of surviving inlier indices chosen after observing all clean data:

* The unweighted survivor covariance M_J is covered by (2.7), since the total full-batch deletion is at most epsilon.
* For any weight w on J with E_G w>=1-20epsilon, the full-batch weights 1_J w retain mass at least (1-epsilon)(1-20epsilon)>=1-21epsilon. Their normalized weighted covariance is therefore also covered by (2.7).
* Comparing both Loewner sandwich bounds relative to S_L gives

      [(1-gamma_0)/(1+gamma_0)] M_J <= M_{J,w}
        <= [(1+gamma_0)/(1-gamma_0)] M_J.

Thus G is (20epsilon, gamma_in)-stable with gamma_in=2gamma_0/(1-gamma_0). Choose gamma_0 so gamma_in is the robust-PCA parameter gamma=c/R required by its theorem, while 20epsilon<gamma. The adversary may select J using all clean covariates and responses; the full-batch event was uniform over all deletion weights. The vectors passed from the inlier pairs to PCA have an independent learner sign, preserving every outer product and making their distribution mean zero.

The robust PCA theorem then returns u with probability at least 0.99 such that

    u^T S_{Lhat} u >= (1-C_P gamma) lambda_1(S_{Lhat}).           (4.1)

The population target has top eigengap 2q^2, so (4.1) implies

    1-|<u,x*/q>|^2
        <= C_P gamma (Lhat+3q^2)/(2q^2)
        <= C'_P gamma R.

Take c in gamma=c/R small enough that sin(angle(u,x*))<=1/50. Together with (3.1), the initializer x0=qhat u satisfies

    min_{s in {−1,+1}} ||x0-sx*|| <= q/27 + sqrt(2)q/50 < q/9.  (4.2)

The last strict inequality is numerical: 1/27+sqrt(2)/50<1/9. Thus it meets the basin interface required by the downstream robust gradient-descent result of Buna–Rebeschini (AISTATS 2025).

5. OPERATIONAL COSTS AND THE GLOBAL-ADVERSARY GATE

Initialization uses m=C R^2[d+log(R/delta)] raw pairs in one pass over the feature data, plus the source robust scalar mean call. On the stated exact-real model, feature construction costs O(md); robust scale estimation with a supplied variance upper bound can be implemented in Õ(m) time in dimension one. The DKP robust-PCA routine has runtime

    Õ(m d / gamma^2) = Õ(m d R^2)

for a stable inlier set (the paper states O(md gamma^{-2} log^4(d/epsilon))). Its memory is near-linear in md for the batch version. The scale estimate can be acquired from the same observations using the uniform-L proof; no separate scale batch is charged. If confidence delta smaller than the base constant is required, repeat the randomized PCA routine O(log(1/delta)) times and take a projective medoid: on the event that a strict majority of runs satisfy (4.1), all good outputs lie in one small projective ball and every maximum-neighborhood output is within three radii of x*/q. This amplifies only the algorithmic randomness; sample concentration and scalar-scale failure probabilities are separately set to delta/3.

For an end-to-end phase-retrieval optimizer, append the cited robust gradient descent with P fresh gradient batches of size

    m_g = C max{d log d, log(1/delta)} r_up^2

and P=O((1-sqrt(epsilon))^{-1} log(1/(r_up sqrt(epsilon)))) as in Das–Batra Theorem 4.1 / Buna–Rebeschini Theorem 3.3 (the exact theorem constants and a possible harmless additive +1 in the zero-noise sample expression are not transcribed as proved here). Its source-reported error is O(sigma sqrt(epsilon)/q), with total sample m+P m_g. The cited full runtime is

    T_scale(m) + Õ(m d R^2) + P T_rob-mean(m_g,d),

where T_rob-mean is the explicit robust-mean cost in the source theorem. No claim of overall near-linear runtime is made unless that final robust-mean cost is instantiated and the batch interface below is justified.

The following is a material unresolved issue. The source defines a globally strong adversary that sees every clean pair before replacing any points. It then says independent fresh gradient batches can be obtained by randomly partitioning one corrupted data set. Random partitioning does ensure a count bound on the number of corrupted rows in each batch, but it does not by itself prove that the iterate computed from earlier corrupted batches is independent of the clean rows in later batches: an earlier replacement may be an arbitrary encoding of future clean samples, and the adversary can select replacement indices after seeing all clean values. A robust-mean theorem for a fixed query cannot be applied conditionally on an iterate that was made a function of future clean samples by this channel without an additional uniform-in-iterate guarantee. The source's batch-size/count argument alone does not discharge this dependency.

Accordingly, the end-to-end composition is valid under a causal batch oracle (each clean batch is drawn after the current iterate and then independently contaminated) or under a proved uniform robust-gradient oracle over the whole basin, but the latter uniform theorem and its sample/runtime costs remain open in this record. The spectral initializer above does handle the full strong-adversary batch model. A separate independent worker A24 is auditing this global gradient gate; no result from that worker is imported here.

6. SHARPNESS, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND PRIOR ART

The deletion price epsilon log(e/epsilon) in (2.8) is inherent even with no response noise: for a~N(0,I), Y=1, and a fixed unit v, delete {|a^T v|>t} where epsilon=P(|G|>t). Then

    1 - E[(a^T v)^2 1{|a^T v|<=t}]/(1-epsilon)
       = [2t phi(t)]/(1-epsilon)
       =(2+o(1)) epsilon log(1/epsilon).

This identifies the logarithm as a true strong-adversary deletion effect, not an artifact of a union bound. The phase-retrieval noiseless case has a corresponding deleted-coordinate construction recorded in A12's independent candidate analysis, though that construction concerns the Ya representation rather than this PSD-shifted one.

The principal algorithmic baseline is Das & Batra, “Tractable Gaussian Phase Retrieval with Heavy Tails and Adversarial Corruption with Near-Linear Sample Complexity,” AISTATS 2026, PMLR 300, official page https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/das26a.html and PDF https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v300/main/assets/das26a/das26a.pdf. Its formal baseline uses norm-truncated Ya vectors, the Kong et al. robust rank-one PCA routine with O(m^2 d) filtering time, then robust gradient descent. Proposition 4.7/F.2 says a stability-based near-linear PCA replacement would work if the truncated clean empirical vectors were stable; Question 4.8 asks for that result. The printed reference matrix in Question 4.8 is inconsistent with E[(Ya)(Ya)^T], as v1 records. The representation in this version is different: it PSD-shifts and clips the response before taking its square root, with target (2.1). The live primary-literature search found no exact use of this shifted/clipped feature. That bounded search is not a novelty or priority proof.

The robust PCA primitive is Diakonikolas, Kane, Pensia, and Pittas, “Nearly-Linear Time and Streaming Algorithms for Outlier-Robust PCA,” ICML 2023, PMLR 202:7886–7921, official page https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/diakonikolas23a.html. The relevant inputs are Definition 2.4 (fractional deletion stability), Setting 2.5 (the (20epsilon,gamma)-stable inlier interface), and Theorem C.1 (O(md gamma^{-2} log^4(d/epsilon)) runtime and top-Rayleigh output). Its theorem is a conditional algorithmic primitive; it does not prove stability for the phase-retrieval distribution.

For the downstream phase-retrieval basin, compare Buna and Rebeschini, “Robust Gradient Descent for Phase Retrieval,” AISTATS 2025, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/buna25a.html. The AISTATS 2026 paper reproduces its basin/error interface as Theorem 4.2. Recent 2025 work on outlier-robust phase retrieval in nearly-linear time is a competing baseline for the noiseless subcase; its existence was verified in the primary OpenReview PDF at https://openreview.net/pdf/105d4e7d58fd187ba288242338af32926f0f7a2b.pdf. No novelty claim is made for noiseless robustness.

Equally informed comparison: the source's original spectral estimator uses the high-variance vector Ya and a general robust-PCA filter; the candidate transform makes each clean feature a bounded scalar-weighted Gaussian row, enabling a transparent stability proof with O(dR^2) samples for fixed confidence and R, at the cost of a small-constant contamination condition epsilon log(e/epsilon)<=c/R and a PCA runtime factor R^2. This comparison is conditional on (2.6), theorem constants in the cited stable-PCA algorithm, and the source's gradient stage. No numerical speedup was measured. A trivial sample covariance is not an equally informed baseline under strong contamination; standard robust PCA and the source's Theorem 3.2 routine are the relevant controls.
