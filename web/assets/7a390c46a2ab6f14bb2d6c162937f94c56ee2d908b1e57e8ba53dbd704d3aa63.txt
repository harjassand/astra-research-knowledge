N06 — Evolutionary acquisition of predictive heredity
Append-only continuation v3; 2026-10-09. v1 and v2 remain frozen and unchanged.

Status: internally proved finite-founder construction, with a sufficient rather than necessary establishment bound. It combines mutation-accessible predictor tables with finite branching drift. The model is a finite-founder, unbounded branching population in a shared exogenous Markov environment; it is not a finite carrying-capacity Moran/Wright–Fisher result. No novelty, priority, empirical validation, or general biological-law claim is made. The construction supplies a generic table architecture, environmental state interface, and local mutation graph, but does not supply P or the target table. Whether an organism can evolve that architecture from a less structured genome remains unresolved.

1. New target

v2 established a mutation-accessible, local-fitness-ascent result only in the infinite-population rare-substitution idealization, and separately a finite-founder survival condition for a fixed table. Here I combine the ingredients into one finite-founder process. The target table need not be present among founders, and intermediate tables need not be supercritical or beneficial. A lower bound is given for the probability that mutation constructs the target along a lineage and that the resulting target lineage persists despite offspring sampling.

This only removes the initial-presence and positive-intermediate-growth assumptions under explicit mutation-connectivity and branching assumptions. It does not prove finite carrying-capacity fixation, that the population takes a likely route, or that natural selection reliably guides every mutation step toward the predictor.

2. Finite-type evolutionary branching model

Let S be a finite environmental and phenotype alphabet, and let E_t be an exogenous irreducible Markov chain on S with P(e'|e)>0. Let Θ be a finite set of inherited response tables θ, each with a row-stochastic transition table K_θ(b|e) satisfying the common floor

    K_θ(b|e) ≥ ε > 0       for all θ,e,b.

The environmental state is supplied as a present-state cue: after hard viability selection, every living parent in generation t has phenotype E_t. A potential offspring of a parent with table θ is independently processed as follows:

(a) it passes a genotype-independent reproduction/maintenance gate with probability a∈(0,1];
(b) its inherited table θ' is drawn from a fixed row-stochastic mutation kernel Q(θ'|θ);
(c) its state is drawn from K_{θ'}(·|E_t);
(d) it survives iff its state equals E_{t+1}.

Each adult has B potential offspring. Mutations in Q are table changes, not losses from the tracked table family; a is the separate common reproduction/maintenance gate. This separation avoids counting the same table-changing event both as a mutation and as an intact-copy loss. If mutations can leave the table family, add an explicit loss state or lower a accordingly.

Conditional on a transition e→e', a parent of table θ has B independent categorical offspring trials. The probability of producing a surviving child of table θ' in each trial is

    a Q(θ'|θ) K_{θ'}(e'|e).

Thus the random vector of genotype counts is a finite-type branching process in a Markov environment, with conditional mean matrix

    M_{e,e'}(θ,θ') = B a Q(θ'|θ) K_{θ'}(e'|e).                (10)

The environment is common to all lineages in a generation. Offspring sampling is independent conditional on the environmental path. Population genotype frequencies are endogenous counts; they are not an external estimate of P. The mutation kernel does not depend on e or e', so it is not an externally supplied learning rule.

3. Finite-founder acquisition-and-persistence theorem

Assume the mutation graph on Θ is connected in the directed sense needed below. Fix a founder table θ_0 and a target table θ_*; let

    θ_0, θ_1, ..., θ_ℓ=θ_*

be a directed mutation path such that Q(θ_j|θ_{j−1})≥η>0 for every j=1,...,ℓ. (If θ_0=θ_*, take ℓ=0.) Assume each target-table self-copy probability satisfies Q(θ_*|θ_*)≥q_0>0. Define

    α = 1 − (1 − a η ε)^B,
    p_* = a q_0 ε.

If

    B p_* > 1,                                                   (11)

let q_* be the smallest solution in [0,1] of

    q = (1 − p_* + p_* q)^B.                                   (12)

Then, from one founder of table θ_0 in any current environmental state, the probability that a θ_* individual is produced after ℓ generations and that a θ_* descendant lineage never becomes extinct thereafter is at least

    α^ℓ (1 − q_*).                                              (13)

The bound is uniform over every future environmental path, hence also over the Markov environment. The target lineage in the event of (13) uses only descendants whose table remains θ_*; all mutant descendants are simply ignored in this embedded subpopulation.

Proof. For a parent of table θ_{j−1} at any time and any environmental transition e→e', each of its B offspring has probability

    a Q(θ_j|θ_{j−1})K_{θ_j}(e'|e) ≥ aηε

of being a surviving child of table θ_j. The offspring trials are independent, so the chance of at least one such child is at least α. If one exists, select the first (by a fixed ordering of the B trials) and continue from that child. Conditional on all previous events and on any environmental history, the same bound applies at the next step. Iterated conditioning gives probability at least α^ℓ of producing a θ_* adult along this path.

Now start from one θ_* adult. Each of its B offspring trials yields a surviving θ_* child with probability

    a Q(θ_*|θ_*)K_{θ_*}(e'|e) ≥ a q_0 ε = p_*

for every environmental transition. Use independent uniform random variables for the child trials. A trial that is a θ_* child in the actual process whenever its uniform is at most the actual probability is also a θ_* child whenever the same uniform is at most p_*. This couples the actual θ_* descendant count from below by a Galton–Watson process with offspring distribution Binomial(B,p_*), independently of the environmental path. Its mean is Bp_*>1. Its extinction probability is the smallest fixed point q_* in (12), so it survives with probability 1−q_*. Apply this conditional lower bound after the path event to obtain (13). ∎

The proof explicitly handles finite demographic sampling of descendants and arbitrarily subcritical intermediate tables: the path event asks only for one matching child per step. It does not use a positive average invasion exponent for intermediates. The strong floor condition (11) is sufficient, not necessary; it forces the target's self-copy sublineage to be supercritical on every environmental transition. A target can have positive long-run log growth under the weaker v2 condition while failing (11), so (13) does not characterize all establishment.

4. Exact growth premium after mutation retention

For a fixed target table θ_* whose self-copy probability is q=Q(θ_*|θ_*), the embedded no-table-change lineage has one-step mean

    m_*(e,e') = B a q K_{θ_*}(e'|e).

Under the same hard-selection/exogenous-environment assumptions as v2, its environmental log-mean is

    λ_*(q) = log(Ba q) + Σ_{e,e'} μ(e)P(e'|e)log K_{θ_*}(e'|e)
            = log(Ba q) − H(E_{t+1}|E_t)
              − E_{E_t} D_KL(P(·|E_t)||K_{θ_*}(·|E_t)).       (14)

For a memoryless baseline K_0(e'|e)=μ(e') with the same gate a and no table-specific retention loss, subtracting yields

    λ_*(q)−λ_0
       = I(E_t;E_{t+1})
         − E_{E_t}D_KL(P(·|E_t)||K_{θ_*}(·|E_t)) + log q.       (15)

Any architecture-specific maintenance or recombination cost must be added separately as in v2; (15) assumes it is common or already included in a and q. If q differs from the baseline retention, it is a real log-growth cost. Equation (15) is the already-known conditional log-score/information identity from v2 plus mutation retention; it is not a new information law. The new statement in this continuation is the explicit finite-founder probability (13) under mutation reachability.

5. Fully specified binary example and executable check

Take S={0,1}, P(e|e)=3/4, P(1−e|e)=1/4, so μ is fair and

    I(E_t;E_{t+1}) = log 2 − h(1/4) = 0.1308120 nats/generation.

Use L=4 table rows with K(1|e)=r_e/4, r_e∈{1,2,3}. The nine tables are Θ={1,2,3}². Every row entry is at least ε=1/4. The exact target table is θ_*=(1,3), i.e. K_*=P. A local mutation changes one row coordinate r_e by ±1 when still in {1,2,3}. Give each legal neighbor probability 0.01 and place the remaining probability on self-copy. The maximum graph degree is four, so every self-copy probability is at least q_0=0.96; every table is at graph distance at most ℓ=4 from θ_*.

Let B=100, a=s exp(−c)=0.99 exp(−0.02)=0.9703966866. Then

    α = 1−(1−a·0.01·0.25)^100 = 0.2156452865,
    p_* = a·0.96·0.25 = 0.2328952048,
    Bp_* = 23.28952048 > 1.

The Binomial(100,p_*) extinction root is q_*=3.05823×10^(−12). Thus the theorem gives the conservative finite-founder acquisition-and-persistence bound

    α^4(1−q_*) = 0.0021625187393.

This is a positive bound from any one of the nine starting tables and any environmental state, despite finite offspring sampling. It is modest, not a high-probability guarantee. The exact target's q is 0.98 (it has two legal neighbors), but q_0=0.96 is used for a uniform table-wide bound.

For the same architecture, (15) gives a conservative net predictor premium over a no-mutation memoryless table of

    I + log(0.96) = 0.0899900414 nats/generation > 0.

The environmental source here is exogenous; it supplies the same present state cue and matching survival interface to all tables. No algorithm is supplied with q=3/4 or θ_*. The mutation process is blind and can reach all nine tables. The proof establishes a nonzero chance of producing and maintaining the predictor, not that selection directs the population toward θ_* or that it will be the dominant type at carrying capacity.

Reproduce these calculations with:

    python3 work/natural/n06_evolutionary_memory/check_acquisition_bound.py

The script checks the nine-node mutation graph and diameter, computes the exact lower-bound inputs, numerically solves (12) by iteration from zero, and evaluates the information/cost values. It is a finite arithmetic check, not a biological simulation or empirical validation.

6. Scope, counterexamples, and decisive distinction

• The positive probability in (13) does not imply certainty. With finite founders, extinction remains possible; if B a q_0 ε≤1 the embedded-GW proof gives no survival bound. At equality the embedded process is critical and dies out almost surely under Binomial offspring, even though the actual multitype process may still persist by using other genotypes.
• The lower bound becomes very small for long mutation paths or small per-edge mutation probabilities. It is explicit precisely so path length, mutation supply, floor, fecundity, and maintenance cost can be tested rather than hidden inside “eventually evolves.”
• The model has unbounded branching. A hard carrying capacity changes descendants' competition and may make an established predictor displace, coexist with, or be lost against resident types. Neither fixation probability nor stationary frequency under Moran/Wright–Fisher sampling follows from (13) or (15).
• Intermediate variants need not be beneficial. This avoids the rare-substitution path assumption in v2, but also means the lower bound relies on blind mutation along a particular path rather than adaptive uphill steps.
• The generic architecture remains supplied: the environment is represented by the same finite cue alphabet S, every row of K is mutable, local table mutations connect the grid, and hard selection rewards a state matching the next environment. Thus the result does not solve open-ended invention of the environmental representation or a genotype-to-phenotype circuit.
• No sexual reproduction is included. A two-parent offspring kernel and the mate distribution would change the branching types and possibly introduce frequency dependence.
• If the environment is IID, then P(·|e)=μ and I=0; a table-specific mutation loss q<1 makes (15) negative unless offset by other fitness effects. Predictive acquisition cannot be claimed without environmental regularity and a positive net score.
• A finite table family that excludes all good predictors defeats acquisition: connectivity within a family only guarantees access to its own tables. The theorem's representational assumption is explicit and falsifiable.

7. Prior-art boundary

The finite-branching component is standard evolutionary-rescue logic, not a new branching-process theorem. King and Masel (2007), “The evolution of bet-hedging adaptations to rare scenarios,” Theoretical Population Biology 72, DOI 10.1016/j.tpb.2007.08.006, analyze finite-N Moran fixation probabilities for switching modifiers and show when drift blocks the infinite-population optimum; this directly rules out translating asymptotic score superiority into finite-pop fixation. Peischl and Kirkpatrick (2012), “Establishment of new mutations in changing environments,” Genetics 191, 895–906, DOI 10.1534/genetics.112.140756, use time-dependent branching processes for establishment in changing environments. Uecker and Hermisson (2011), “On the fixation process of a beneficial mutation in a variable environment,” Genetics 188, 915–930, DOI 10.1534/genetics.110.124297, study fixation under variable selection. These are close methodological precedents; the elementary coupling in §3 is not asserted as novel priority.

The closest functional precedent for acquisition of epigenetic bet-hedging parameters remains Xue and Leibler (2016), PNAS 113, E7256–E7262, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1608756113, whose transgenerational feedback learns marginal phenotype frequencies. The conditional transition-table score and information identity in v2 are within established fitness-value-of-information and stochastic bet-hedging theory (Kussell–Leibler 2005; Bergstrom–Lachmann 2005); the present continuation does not claim those identities as discoveries. No primary source located in this bounded search states exactly bound (13) for a table-valued hereditary predictor, but absence from this search is not evidence of novelty. Source records and limitations remain in v2; v3 adds the primary-source establishment comparisons above.
