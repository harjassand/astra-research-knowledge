COMMON-METRIC EXTENSION: FROZEN CANDIDATE PROOF, VERSION 2
Status: internally reconstructed full extension; independent audit pending.
All finite spectral-crossing inputs are conditional on the pinned 3D manuscript
being correct. No external validation or novelty/priority claim is made.

Candidate theorem. For every 4/9<v<1 and 1<c<9v/4 there is ONE complete smooth
metric g on R^3, Euclidean near the origin, Ric>=0, AVR(g)=v, such that
 limsup_{k integer -> infinity} h_k(R^3,g)/(k+1)^2 > c.
The strict >c can be weakened to >=c without changing the construction.

0. Fix parameters and bands.
Set a=sqrt(v). Choose q and a small angular neighborhood U with
 2sqrt(C_*)/(3a) < q < 1/sqrt(c),
 K_h>kappa>a^2, and lambda_j(h)<=C_* l(l+1) for B_{l-1}<j<=B_l.
These are the source's curvature and min-max conditions, with C_* arbitrarily
close to 1. Fix complete round-degree bands I_n=(B_{M_{n-1}},B_{M_n}], n>=1,
where M_n increase sufficiently fast that M_{n-1}/M_n->0. Each M_{n-1} is
also chosen beyond all finite crossing thresholds needed for band n in a
C^{n+2}-small neighborhood of round. Finite look-ahead is possible: choose
M_{n-1} after fixing the required neighborhood for n, and then choose M_n
arbitrarily larger. Omit the first B_{M_0} labels entirely from the claimed count.
Let p_n=B_{M_n} and k_n=ceil(q M_n).

1. A compact angular skeleton with every band boundary protected.
In C-infinity(S^2), the source Lemma 2.2 gives an open dense condition of
simplicity through every finite cutoff. Baire gives a base parameter w_* simple
at all positive eigenvalues. There is a smooth stem w(x), x in [0,1], from 0 to
w_*, simple at every x>0. To justify this directly, in the Baire space of smooth
paths with these fixed endpoints, impose simplicity through cutoff r on every
compact [1/s,1], r,s positive integers. Each condition is open. It is dense by
the source's finite-cutoff path-avoidance construction, using perturbations
supported away from x=0; the intermediate left endpoint is allowed to vary.
The countable intersection gives the stem. At x=0 the selected block boundaries
p_n still have the strict round-sphere gap, so every p_n has a positive minimum
gap on the whole stem, including its round endpoint.

For each adjacent pair r,r+1 internal to I_n, obtain its exact isolated double
by the source's within-block or cross-block construction. On the local exact-double
constraint set, simplicity of all other eigenvalues through cutoff Q is open and
dense, by Lemma 2.3. This local constraint set is Baire: it is a relatively open
part of the closed zero set of the two smooth cluster constraints in C-infinity;
the isolation gaps are retained. Hence one may retain the chosen double while
making every other eigenvalue simple, by countable intersection.

Choose a split crossing segment with its nonzero trace-free first jet at the
double. In the smooth path space retaining its value and first jet there, remove
every other coincidence by Baire. For each finite cutoff, there is an interval
around the double on which its other gaps are already strict; finite-cutoff
avoidance is applied outside that interval. Perturbations can be supported away
from the double and can preserve its first jet. Keeping the target split jet is
an open condition and assures that its own equality occurs only at the prescribed
point in the local segment. The rest of the loop is joined by paths simple at
every positive position, using the same countable version of Lemma 2.2.

For band n, all small crossing detours may be taken C^{n+2}-close to round by an
amount tending to zero. Cross-block parameters tend to round in every fixed
smooth norm as their degree tends to infinity (source pp.12-15); the within-block
and avoidance perturbations can be arbitrarily small. Each detour joins to a
point w(x_n) of the fixed stem, x_n->0, and the main part of its based loop follows
the fixed stem from w_* down to w(x_n) and back. There are only finitely many
loops in each band. Their union has a C-infinity compact closure: any sequence of
points either lies in finitely many compact loop images, lies on the stem, or
lies in detours tending smoothly to round. Every selected p_n gap is positive
on this closure. Indeed it is positive on each finite earlier loop and on the
stem, and the later small detours are close to the positive round p_n gap.

The based loop for each adjacent transposition crosses exactly that pair and
no other equal eigenvalues. Its other ranks return to themselves. Concatenating
in increasing r produces the source's single cycle C_n of I_n. The cycles of
different bands commute as permutations and preserve ALL band boundaries.

2. Exact averaging with growing finite programs.
Let P_m concatenate the cycles of I_1,...,I_m, with angular phase period S_m.
Each loop is constant near its based joins. Let Q_m be the least common multiple
of the finite band sizes. A label i in I_n, n<=m, visits every position in I_n
equally at each corresponding phase of Q_m repetitions. Hence its phase mean is
at most
 beta_n = (sqrt(C_*)/a) [F(M_n)-F(M_{n-1})]/[B_{M_n}-B_{M_{n-1}}],
 F(M)=(M+1)(M+2)(4M+3)/6.
The identity follows by summing the ordered frequencies over its band at every
phase before integrating. In particular beta_n/M_n -> 2sqrt(C_*)/(3a)<q.
Enlarge the M_n if needed so beta_n<k_n for every n.

3. Uniform derivative envelopes without degree-uniform spectral estimates.
Fix every loop and every crossing bump/direction BEFORE radial stage starts.
There are finitely many crossing types in each band n. Normalize their directions
by positive factors so their C^{n+2} norms are <=1. A positive normalization
retains the nonzero off-diagonal sign; its lower bound may be very small and
depends on n. Choose D_m nondecreasing, sufficiently large to bound time derivatives
of the angular metrics and the first p_m reference eigenframe through the required
spatial orders <=m. In stage m use phase speed eta_m(t)=t^(-3/4)/D_m.

Each fixed-cutoff frame has uniform spatial norms on the skeleton by elliptic
estimates and a bounded eigenvalue range. Its phase derivatives on each finite
P_m have finite bounds. Normalizing by D_m makes the time derivative envelope
O_n(t^(-3/4)) for each FIXED cutoff p_n, including all later stages. The finitely
many earlier stages only enlarge the finite constant O_n. The corresponding
metric time derivatives, including one angular derivative for Ricci, are
O(t^(-3/4)); second time derivatives are O(t^(-3/2)).

Crossing nu in band n during stage m has perturbation amplitude
 z_nu eta_m(t_nu)^(3/4) psi_nu chi_nu(s), |z_nu|<=1,
with the positive normalization already included in psi_nu. Choose stage starts
so these amplitudes are <=2^(-n) for band-n controls, and all perturbations stay
inside the curvature neighborhood U. The closed family of possible controlled
links is C-infinity compact: late-band perturbations have C^{n+2} norm <=2^(-n),
and earlier-band controls range in finitely many compact segments. Moreover,
for each fixed spatial order, controlled minus reference link is O(t^(-9/16))
uniformly in ALL controls and stages. Metric and reference-frame time derivative
envelopes are likewise fixed independently of the subsequent start times.

4. One radial factor accommodates the entire countable control product.
Put epsilon(t)=t^(-3/4). Choose the compact concave slope transition and exact
late tail f''(r)=-C epsilon(log r)^2/r exactly as in source Proposition 4.1.
The total tail slope loss is C integral epsilon(t)^2 dt<infinity; move the initial
tail start sufficiently far out and supply the remaining 1-a in the compact
transition. Then a<=f'<=1, f/r->a, and b_tt+b_t=-C epsilon(t)^2.
The source's exact Ricci block formulas apply to any smooth fixed-area angular
path, not just a finite-dimensional one. A=H^{-1}H_t and div(A/2) are O(epsilon),
(A/2)_t=O(epsilon^2), and K_H>kappa. The tangential Ricci block has a fixed
positive lower bound for sufficiently late motion. A fixed large C makes its
Schur complement positive. Thus all controlled metrics are complete smooth,
Euclidean near zero, Ric>=0 and strictly positive on the moving tail. Fixed
pointwise area gives AVR=a^2 and exact radial distance r.

5. The fixed-cutoff transfer estimates extend uniformly to this compact skeleton.
For each fixed n, its reference gap has a global positive minimum gamma_n and
its largest cone frequency is bounded, so tau_{*,n}>0. The operator-norm freezing
proof in source Lemma 5.1 uses only compact links, vanishing time derivatives,
whole-ball L^2 contraction and the limiting half-cylinder equation. It therefore
gives a uniform modulus Omega_n(t)->0 over this controlled family and all stage
start choices satisfying the derivative envelopes. No n-uniform modulus is needed.

The finite-frame estimate uses a cylinder of length O(log t). The global derivative
envelopes give residual O_n(t^(-3/4)log t), and the control perturbation is
O_n(t^(-9/16)). Source Proposition 5.2 consequently gives a unique graph
V^{(n)}_j above the first p_n modes, for j>=a finite J_n, with
 ||V^{(n)}_j|| <= C_n j^(-9/16),
 A^{(n)}_j = diag(alpha_i(j)) + E^{(n)}_j,
 ||E^{(n)}_j|| <= C_n j^(-9/16),
and ||T_LQ||<=Omega_n(j). All graphs/matrices are continuous in the countable
control product, by the same fixed-cutoff contraction and finite-rank continuity
proof as the source. The constants come from the fixed skeleton, envelopes and
cutoff, not from a completed future schedule.

Near a crossing belonging to band n in stage m, its local reference loop and
direction are among finitely many fixed band-n types. On O(log t) comparison
cylinders contained in its plateau, the sharper finite-frame residual is
 O_n(eta_m(t) log(1/eta_m(t)) + t^(-3/2)log(1/eta_m(t))).
For fixed m, this divided by eta_m^(3/4) tends to zero. The graph correction is
bounded by C_n Omega_n(t) t^(-9/16). Dividing it by the desired eta_m^(3/4)
multiplies Omega_n by D_m^(3/4). Hence after a finite T_m this correction and the
local residual are smaller than any prescribed fraction of every band-n signed
effect, simultaneously for n<=m. Source Proposition 5.3 gives the nonzero signed
off-diagonal entry uniformly over all other controls.

6. Choose stage starts by finitely many known inequalities.
For each n fix a small coordinate-box radius rho_n>0 so nonlinear terms involving
columns of radius rho_n cannot reverse the band-n crossing effect. There are
finitely many crossing types for this n, so one positive rho_n suffices. Let
epsilon_m->0 be smaller than 2^(-m) min_{n<=m}rho_n times the needed finite
denominator constants. Outside a chosen pair's own crossing plateaux, its ratio
|r_hi-1| has a positive minimum on the fixed finite program P_m, with its full
continued-label period. Pick T_m large enough that, for every n<=m,
 |F_hi(j)| <= epsilon_m |r_hi(j)-1| outside that pair's plateaux,
the local signed effect dominates all residuals on its own plateau,
all O_n(eta_nu^(1/4)) crossing trial bounds are <rho_n/10,
and C_n D_m Q_m S_m T_m^(-1/4)<2^(-m) for frequency averaging.
Also require T_m>=2 T_{m-1}. These requirements are finite, and follow respectively
from the global E bound, the local signed estimate, slow phase speed, and the
bounded periodic frequency primitive. Continue P_{m-1} until the chosen threshold
and terminate that stage at a complete Q_{m-1} label period. At such late times
one more period costs o(T_m), so the threshold requirements remain valid. Changing
program at the common constant base is smooth. This is noncircular: the compact
skeleton, D_m, gamma_n, Omega_n and all finite-program minima precede T_m.

7. Matching all NEW bands simultaneously.
For each n and i in I_n, seek v_i^{(n)}(j)=e_i+sum_{h!=i,h<=p_n}u_hi(j)e_h,
j>=J_n within the initial constant part of stage n, with |u_hi(j)|<=rho_n.
Only NEW-band columns are unknown in cutoff n. Each lower h has a uniformly
smaller reference eigenvalue than i, since their intervening band boundary is
protected, and is always forward-stable. Each pair in I_n crosses infinitely
often, with simple alternating zeros. Use the exact recurrence and zero-cut /
two-incoming-cut rules of source Section 6. Every crossing creates one mismatch
for its birth-band system and has exactly one geometric control.

Outside this pair's plateaux, telescoping gives stable forcing bound epsilon_m
independently of elapsed time or tiny internal gaps. On its plateaux, the source
local Gaussian estimate gives O_n(eta_nu^(1/4)). Between successive zero cuts
there is only one matching crossing, so these bounds place every trial propagation
strictly inside rho_n. At a matching crossing, the plateau endpoints carry bounded
incoming values. Their influence at its center is O(rho_n exp(-c_n/eta_nu)); the
central forcing sum has the sign of z_nu and size >=c'_n eta_nu^(1/4). Thus the
two endpoint controls give opposite mismatch signs, uniformly in all other
variables. Changing stage speed outside the plateau does not affect this argument.

All trial outputs for a fixed coordinate are finite sums: within-band future
zero cuts exist, and lower-band columns only use forward sums from J_n. Exact
graph dependence on all future controls is continuous by Step 5. The product of
all coordinate boxes and controls is countable compact. Brouwer on the first N
coordinates, with the rest frozen, followed by coordinatewise diagonal extraction
produces one fixed point. Endpoint signs force every control to be interior and
its mismatch to vanish. Every exact line recurrence then holds. Trial coordinates
tend to zero: their adjacent zero cuts tend to infinity, epsilon_m->0, eta_nu->0,
and lower-band stable ratios have a uniform strict gap. This is the acquired
countable analogue of source Section 6; it asks for no growing-p graph at a fixed
initial radius and imposes no duplicate equations across cutoffs.

8. Growth and independence.
Lift each selected line through its own W^{p_n}, multiply its exact positive line
multipliers and use whole-ball Dirichlet extensions, as in source Section 7.
This gives entire smooth harmonic functions. For each fixed n,
 log sigma_i(j)=d(b(j)^(-2)lambda_i(t_j))+o(1),
and b(j)->a, so both the matrix error and radial-factor difference have Cesaro
mean zero. Unit-mesh frequency/integral error is O_n(t^(1/4)). In stage m>=n,
a bounded mean-zero primitive over Q_m S_m has size O_n(Q_m S_m). Integration
by parts with dt/ds=D_m t^(3/4) bounds the stage averaging remainder by
O_n(D_m Q_m S_m t^(3/4)). The start requirement makes its relative size <=2^(-m).
Geometrically increasing T_m imply the sum of completed-stage remainders is o(t),
and the partial current-stage remainder is also o(t). Hence each band-n function
has upper spherical growth exponent <=beta_n<k_n.

Whole-ball restriction is an all-radius L^2 contraction. Uniform ellipticity of
the normalized divergence equation on fixed-width logarithmic cylinders gives
the L^2-to-sup bound with a uniform constant. Thus each such function belongs to
H_{k_n}. For the finitely many bands 1,...,n, all selected vectors on a late sphere
are their distinct reference eigenvectors plus o(1), because u_hi->0 and V^{(n)}->0.
They are independent. Their number is p_n-B_{M_0}. Therefore
 h_{k_n}(g) >= (M_n+1)^2-(M_0+1)^2,
and the desired limsup is at least 1/q^2>c.

This construction has nonunique tangent cones. Every fixed low-band loop is
traversed infinitely often, with time speed tending to zero and control amplitudes
tending to zero. Rescaling at corresponding phases gives the cone links a^2 H(s).
A base simple spectrum and an isolated-double phase give nonisometric links.
This is consistent with Huang's unique-cone asymptotic coefficient v.

AUDIT BOUNDARY
Steps 1,5,6 are the nontrivial extensions beyond the source. In particular,
compactness must include the normalized perturbation directions, the uniform
Omega_n must be taken over derivative-bounded families before radial stage starts,
and the plateau sign must dominate the future-graph term after D_m scaling.
The proof is frozen for independent scrutiny. If any of these assertions fails,
the full common-metric theorem must revert to CONDITIONAL/UNRESOLVED while the
exact averaging, telescoping, and one-control-per-birth-band lemmas remain valid.
