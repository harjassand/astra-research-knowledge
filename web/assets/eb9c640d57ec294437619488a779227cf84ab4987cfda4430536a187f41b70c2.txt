- This includes diagonal-sign variants and the ordinary doubled-graph representation of signed-SDD forms.

## Exact target

For a conforming P1 finite-element stiffness matrix of a uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator, test whether a contrast-independent support-graph/SDD preconditioner can work on general tensor coefficients, including anisotropy that is not aligned with the mesh. The diagnostic must charge the construction and include diagonal sign changes and signed double-cover reductions.

The result below is a lower bound for the whole same-space signed-SDD preconditioner class, not just an obstruction to a particular sign choice. It isolates a remaining solver requirement: a robust general-tensor method must use preconditioner energy generators whose support or structure can represent a contrast-dominant mode involving at least three nodes, or use a different mechanism.

## Explicit seven-element FEM family

Let the inner triangle have vertices

\[
I_0=(0,0),\qquad I_1=(1,0),\qquad I_2=(0,1),
\]

with centroid \(c=(1/3,1/3)\). Let the outer triangle have vertices \(O_i=c+4(I_i-c)\), so \(O_0=(-1,-1), O_1=(3,-1), O_2=(-1,3)\). The domain is the outer triangle. Its ring between inner and outer triangles is triangulated, cyclically, by

\[
(O_i,O_{i+1},I_{i+1}),\qquad (O_i,I_{i+1},I_i),\quad i=0,1,2,
\]

with indices modulo 3. Add the central face \((I_0,I_1,I_2)\). Impose homogeneous Dirichlet data at the three outer vertices. The three inner vertex values \(u=(u_0,u_1,u_2)^T\) are the free unknowns.

Put the constant tensor

\[
K_\kappa=R_{\pi/4}\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}R_{\pi/4}^{T}
=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}\kappa+1&\kappa-1\\\kappa-1&\kappa+1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \kappa\ge 1,
\]

on the central face and coefficient \(I\) on all six ring faces. For each finite \(\kappa\), the piecewise coefficient is uniformly elliptic, with global eigenvalue range \([1,\kappa]\); hence the contrast is exactly \(\kappa\). The mesh and number of elements are fixed as \(\kappa\) grows.

On the reference central face, the P1 basis gradients are \(g_0=(-1,-1),g_1=(1,0),g_2=(0,1)\), and the area is \(1/2\). Directly evaluating \(A^T K_\kappa A\) gives the local stiffness

\[
A_T(\kappa)=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}
4\kappa&-2\kappa&-2\kappa\\
-2\kappa&\kappa+1&\kappa-1\\
-2\kappa&\kappa-1&\kappa+1
\end{pmatrix}
=\kappa ww^T+vv^T,
\]

where

\[
w=(1,-1/2,-1/2)^T,\qquad v=(0,1/2,-1/2)^T.
\]

The equality follows either by multiplication or from
\(\int_T (\nabla u)^T K_\kappa\nabla u = \frac{\kappa}{4}(u_1+u_2-2u_0)^2+\frac14(u_1-u_2)^2\).

Let \(S\) be the assembled stiffness of the six ring faces with coefficient \(I\), with outer values fixed to zero and the three inner values free. Then \(S\succ0\): if \(y^TSy=0\), every affine ring-face function has zero gradient; connectivity makes the function constant throughout the ring; its zero outer trace forces the constant and then all inner nodal values to be zero. Therefore the assembled three-by-three matrix is

\[
A_\kappa=\kappa ww^T+C,\qquad C:=vv^T+S\succ0,
\]

where \(C\) is a fixed matrix independent of \(\kappa\).

Exact rational assembly for the listed ring triangulation gives
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}7&-3/2&-7/2\\-3/2&19/2&-4\\-7/2&-4&23/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C=\begin{pmatrix}7&-3/2&-7/2\\-3/2&39/4&-17/4\\-7/2&-17/4&47/4\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Sylvester's criterion verifies \(S,C\succ0\). Also \(\|C\|_2\le\|C\|_F=\sqrt{1389}/2\), so the bound below implies the concrete weaker estimate \(r\ge\kappa/(5\sqrt{1389})\).

For large enough \(\kappa\), its off-diagonal signs are \(A_{01}<0,A_{02}<0,A_{12}>0\), since the ring contribution is fixed while the central terms are \(-\kappa/2,-\kappa/2,+\kappa/4\). Their product is positive. Under any diagonal sign matrix \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\), the product of the three transformed edge signs is unchanged because each vertex sign appears twice. If all three transformed edges were nonpositive, their product would be negative. Thus no diagonal sign change turns this family into an M-matrix. The absolute diagonal-dominance deficit at rows 1 and 2 is \(\kappa/2+O(1)\), so it is not signed-SDD either for large \(\kappa\).

## Lower bound for all signed-SDD preconditioners

A symmetric signed-SDD matrix \(B\succ0\) is one whose off-diagonal signs are arbitrary but whose diagonal satisfies
\[
B_{ii}\ge\sum_{j\ne i}|B_{ij}|.
\]
It has the exact rank-one decomposition
\[
B=\sum_{i=0}^2 d_i e_ie_i^T+\sum_{0\le i<j\le2} b_{ij}(e_i+s_{ij}e_j)(e_i+s_{ij}e_j)^T,
\]
where \(b_{ij}=|B_{ij}|\ge0\), \(s_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(B_{ij})\) when the entry is nonzero, and \(d_i=B_{ii}-\sum_{j\ne i}|B_{ij}|\ge0\). Thus all generators lie in
\[
\mathcal G=\{e_i:0\le i\le2\}\cup\{e_i+e_j,e_i-e_j:0\le i<j\le2\}.
\]
This class includes diagonal-sign transforms of SDDM matrices. It also includes signed graph/double-cover reductions: a pair term \((x_i-x_j)^2\) is represented by same-layer edges in two copies, and \((x_i+x_j)^2\) by cross-layer edges; restricting to the antisymmetric subspace gives the signed form. Diagonal slack is represented by grounded edges.

Let \(B\) be any signed-SDD preconditioner and define
\[
r=\operatorname{cond}(B^{-1/2}A_\kappa B^{-1/2}).
\]
Write \(\lambda_{\min}=\min_{x\ne0}x^TA_\kappa x/(x^TBx)\), and rescale \(B_0=\lambda_{\min}B\). Scaling preserves signed-SDD, and the generalized-eigenvalue definition gives
\[
B_0\preceq A_\kappa\preceq rB_0.
\]
Let \(P\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(w^\perp\). For every \(g\in\mathcal G\), \(Pg\ne0\), since \(w\) has three nonzero coordinates and each generator has support at most two. Put \(x_g=Pg/\|Pg\|_2\). Then \(x_g\perp w\), \(g^Tx_g=\|Pg\|_2\), and
\[
\beta_g\|Pg\|_2^2\le x_g^TB_0x_g\le x_g^TA_\kappa x_g=x_g^TCx_g\le\|C\|_2,
\]
where \(\beta_g\) is the coefficient of \(gg^T\) in the decomposition of \(B_0\). Directly, the minimum squared projection norm over these nine generators is
\[
\min_{g\in\mathcal G}\|Pg\|_2^2=\frac13
\]
(the minimum is attained at \(g=e_0\), since \(\|w\|_2^2=3/2\) and \(e_0^Tw=1\)). Consequently \(\beta_g\le3\|C\|_2\) for every generator. A direct sum gives
\[
\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}(g^Tw)^2=\frac{15}{2},
\]
so
\[
w^TB_0w=\sum_g\beta_g(g^Tw)^2\le\frac{45}{2}\|C\|_2.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
w^TA_\kappa w=\kappa\|w\|_2^4+w^TCw\ge\frac94\kappa.
\]
Using \(A_\kappa\preceq rB_0\) on \(w\) proves the explicit bound
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cond}(B^{-1/2}A_\kappa B^{-1/2})\ge \frac{\kappa}{10\|C\|_2}.}
\]
The constant \(C\) is the fixed stiffness contribution of one fixed mesh, independent of contrast. Thus every same-space signed-SDD, sign-switched SDDM, or standard signed-double-cover preconditioner has condition number growing linearly in coefficient contrast for this family. The proof attacks the 2-node sign-switch repair: it does not rely on a fixed sign assignment, a single positive off-diagonal, or failure of ordinary diagonal dominance alone.

The geometric reason for the bound is precise. The stiff mode \(w\) has nonzero entries on all three inner nodes. Every signed-SDD generator has support on at most two. If its coefficient were made order \(\kappa\), it would also create order-\(\kappa\) energy on some vector in \(w^\perp\), where the target energy remains bounded by \(C\). Since all generator weights are nonnegative, cancellation cannot repair that leakage.

## Executable finite check

`check_v1.py` assembles all seven P1 face matrices using exact rational arithmetic, verifies \(A_\kappa=\kappa ww^T+C\), checks positive leading principal minors for \(S\) and \(C\), and confirms the frustrated sign cycle and absolute diagonal-dominance deficits at \(\kappa=100,1000\). Run it with `python3 work/applied/a02_elliptic_solver/check_v1.py`. This is a finite assembly check, not an independent proof of the symbolic signed-SDD lower bound.

## What the result does and does not imply

The patch has only seven elements and three free degrees of freedom, and its matrix is assembled in O(1) operations and storage. It therefore has no hidden hierarchy, coarse-space, or local generalized-eigensolve construction cost: the lower bound is already present after exact assembly. Any method whose final preconditioner belongs to the signed-SDD class pays at least \(\Omega(\kappa)\) in its spectral condition certificate, even if its signed graph is solved exactly. This only rules out that preconditioner class. It does not imply an unconditional lower bound for arbitrary algorithms, arbitrary block preconditioners, or general lifts whose reduced preconditioner is not signed-SDD; nor does it rule out tailored solvers exploiting this tiny family.

In particular, the statement that a PCG iteration certificate based on this preconditioner would inherit a \(\sqrt{\kappa}\) factor is a consequence of the usual condition-number-based PCG estimate, not a lower bound on the actual iteration count for every right-hand side. The task's full contrast-robust near-linear solver remains open in this record.

## Primary-source comparison (live, exact sources)

1. Erik G. Boman, Bruce Hendrickson, Stephen Vavasis, “Solving Elliptic Finite Element Systems in Near-Linear Time with Support Preconditioners,” arXiv:cs/0407022v4, https://arxiv.org/html/cs/0407022v4, accessed 2026-10-09, especially Theorem 5 and discussion at HTML lines 267–320 and 509–520. They treat scalar conductivity \(\nabla\cdot(\theta\nabla u)=-f\), factor the stiffness as an incidence/diagonal/bounded-conditioning product, and obtain a weighted graph Laplacian. Their contrast-independent statement assumes the mesh respects large conductivity jumps; their bound also contains mesh-quality, quadrature and intra-element scalar-variation factors. The method requires mesh/PDE information, not just the assembled matrix (lines 512–514). It does not establish robustness for a general anisotropic tensor: the rotated tensor above has a positive-product signed triangle and a contrast-dominant three-node generator.

2. Yin Tat Lee, Richard Peng, Daniel A. Spielman, “Sparsified Cholesky Solvers for SDD linear systems,” arXiv:1506.08204v2, https://arxiv.org/html/1506.08204v2, accessed 2026-10-09, Theorem 5.10 at lines 420–440. It gives a linear-work vertex-sparsifier chain for an SDDM matrix and, once supplied that chain, an \(\epsilon\)-approximate solve in O(m log(1/epsilon)) work. The signed-SDD/double-cover algebra above is explicit; our lower bound says this graph route does not handle this tensor FEM family with contrast-independent same-space spectral quality.

3. Mario Bebendorf, “Low-rank approximation of elliptic boundary value problems with high-contrast coefficients,” arXiv:1410.3717v1, https://arxiv.org/html/1410.3717v1, accessed 2026-10-09. Theorem 1/3 (HTML lines 379–385 and 450–474) proves existence of rank O(|log epsilon|^{d+1}) separated Green-function approximation in a coefficient-dependent flux norm for arbitrary measurable SPD tensor coefficients bounded above/below. The norm is defined through \(-\Delta\phi_v^D=\mathcal L v\), \(\phi_v^D=v\) on \(\partial D\) (lines 216–239). Theorem 4 and proof (lines 477–521) show conversion to ordinary L2 error incurs a contrast-dependent logarithmic rank term, \(k\sim|\log(\epsilon\kappa_A^{-3/2}/\lambda_{\max})|^{d+1}\). The paper gives hierarchical matrix storage/matvec estimates O(kn log n), but the existence/rank theorem is not an energy-norm spectral preconditioner theorem, and no contrast-independent construction cost for its local subspaces is charged there. This is a potentially useful object for future work, not a solver result here.

## Remaining mathematical gap

To meet the original target, one must give an explicit algorithm for a broader preconditioner/factorization class and prove all of the following on a stated mesh and coefficient class: (i) \((1-\varepsilon)A^{-1}\preceq M\preceq(1+\varepsilon)A^{-1}\) or an equivalent energy norm guarantee; (ii) contrast-independent bound on total setup, storage, application, and solve work, charging any local spectral/eigenproblem solves; (iii) stability under adversarial tensor orientations, channels, and the three-node patch above. Neither the SDD route nor the coefficient-dependent low-rank existence theorem supplies those missing steps. No candidate meeting this bar was derived in this finite investigation.
