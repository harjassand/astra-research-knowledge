1. Connected complement, REPORT 125-138 and 170-177

Replace the instruction to join q to a distant point inside the connected
complement by the closed connected obstacle K=D^c union [y,q]. No path
connectivity is assumed. Under inversion centered at y, K adjoined to
infinity meets every radial circle and its part in the closed unit disk
has radial projection [0,1]. The primary Beurling projection theorem and
the slit calculation give the inward bound. Use B(y,4r) as the target
before an exit into B(y,2r) to avoid simultaneous obstacle/target hitting.
The companion full audit proves mu_epsilon(B(x,r))<=8 sqrt(r/d), where
d=dist(z,D^c), for epsilon<d/4. This repairs the justification, not the
positive theorem or its exponent.

2. Capacity inference, REPORT 16-17, 81-84 and 225-227

The verified Piranian example proves that harmonic-measure-null boundary
sets can have positive ambient Sobolev p-capacity, despite full topological
support of harmonic measure. Consequently, for arbitrary Borel boundary
data, zero omega-a.e. does not imply zero p-q.e. This prohibits an automatic
null-set upgrade. The set argument alone does not construct an ambient
W1p function with a nonzero Sobolev trace supported on that exceptional
set; narrow the wording about arbitrary Sobolev functions to this precise
null-set distinction unless a separate trace-realization proof is supplied.
The positive harmonic theorem makes no q.e. inference and needs no such
realization example. Positive p-capacity is reconstructed using the prior
alpha=1 trace bound; the exact Adams-Hedberg theorem number is not a
freshly verified primary citation in this audit.

3. Cusp trace convention, REPORT 21-24 and 183-205

The Hajlasz example has W0^{1,p} trace and zero classical boundary limits
away from a harmonic-null cusp, with 1<=p<4/3. Its zero extension is
indeed ambient W1p in that range. It is unbounded and has no p>3/2
ambient energy, so it falls outside the positive theorem. 'Harmonic-measure
trace' in this counterexample means that boundary-limit datum; it does
not assert a uniformly bounded W1p-to-Lp(omega) operator there, or that
the zero datum is the harmonic function's Poisson terminal variable.

4. Whole boundary, local arcs, and physical screened use

The exposed offset theorem requires a fixed ambient neighborhood of the
ENTIRE domain closure and the FULL harmonic measure. A marked restriction
on a controlled arc alone leaves the other exit contribution. The own
independent local Green theorem handles it under boundedness and explicit
other-exit mass accounting. The actual tail extension is not harmonic.
Using its zero trace to bound the conditional mean requires the mean H
to be genuinely bounded harmonic and to have a compatible ambient/local
W1p extension, or a separately proved boundary-comparison substitute.
Those actual application acquisitions remain CONDITIONAL here.
