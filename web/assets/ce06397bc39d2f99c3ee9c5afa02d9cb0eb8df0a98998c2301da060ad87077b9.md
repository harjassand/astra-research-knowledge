# Cycle 2: stationary reliability at a fixed translation budget

**Verdict.** In the single-count birth–death model below, the exact stationary lower-tail frontier is a finite linear program. Its extreme points are bang-bang with at most one interior birth rate on the positive support, followed by a support cutoff. This does not imply a usual monotone threshold. An exact capacity-two example has strictly better target-threshold reliability than every single-cutoff policy at the same mean translation rate. The mathematical result is fully derived here, but novelty is **unverified**: general constrained continuous-time Markov-decision-process (CTMDP) LP theory is close prior art, and no direct priority search has established that the specialized vertex theorem is new.

## Interface and charged quantities

The state (N_t) is a single nonnegative molecular count. At state (n), a time-homogeneous Markov feedback policy chooses a birth/translation intensity (lambda_nin[0,B]). Each molecule dies independently at rate (gamma>0), so the total death intensity is (gamma n). For finite capacity (M), states are (0,ldots,M) and (lambda_M=0). For infinite capacity, the state set is (mathbb N_0). There are no burst births, hidden promoter states, observation errors, switching delays, cell-to-cell parameter variation, or other resource species in this model.

The target is an integer (hge1). Failure means (N<h); minimize its stationary probability at fixed mean translation rate (C=mathbb E_pi[lambda_N]). Stationarity gives (C=gammamathbb E_pi N). Write (mu=C/gamma) for the fixed mean count and (q=B/gamma) for the dimensionless birth-rate cap. One birth is charged as one translation event; if each event has known energetic cost (e_{m tr}), the modeled energy rate is (e_{m tr}C). Sensing, promoter implementation, ATP cost calibration, and all other molecular costs are outside the model and remain uncharged.

The policy is fully state-observing and may select each (lambda_n) independently. Thus all results below are model theorems, not claims that a natural or synthetic circuit can realize every listed rate table.

## Exact stationary LP

For finite (M), let (Z_K(q)=sum_{n=0}^K q^n/n!). The stationary reliability frontier is

\[
\Phi_{M,h}(q,\mu)=\min_{\pi\in\mathcal P_M(q,\mu)}\sum_{n=0}^{h-1}\pi_n,
\]

where

\[
\mathcal P_M(q,\mu)=\left\{\pi\in\mathbb R_+^{M+1}:\sum_{n=0}^M\pi_n=1,\quad\sum_{n=0}^M n\pi_n=\mu,\quad(n+1)\pi_{n+1}\le q\pi_n\ (0\le n<M)\right\}.
\]

This is an exact equivalence, not a relaxation. A birth–death stationary law has zero net flux across every edge, so
\(\pi_n\lambda_n=\gamma(n+1)\pi_{n+1}\), giving the adjacent inequalities. Conversely, any feasible vector has prefix support (0,\ldots,K): if \(\pi_k=0\), the next adjacent inequality forces every later probability to zero. On its positive support define
\[
\lambda_n=\gamma(n+1)\pi_{n+1}/\pi_n,
\]
and set rates above the support to zero. These rates obey the cap and detailed balance makes \(\pi\) stationary. Since the law has finite mean, stationary drift of (N) yields \(\mathbb E\lambda_N=\gamma\mathbb E N\).

The feasible mean interval is exactly
\[
0\le\mu\le\mu_M(q):=q\,Z_{M-1}(q)/Z_M(q),
\]
so the feasible translation-rate interval is \(0\le C\le B Z_{M-1}/Z_M\). To see the upper bound, set \(w_n=q^n/n!\). The edge constraints make \(\pi_n/w_n\) nonincreasing, so \(\pi\) is likelihood-ratio (hence first-order stochastic) dominated by the normalized truncated-Poisson law \(w_n/Z_M\). The lower and upper means are attained by \(\delta_0\) and that truncated-Poisson law. Convex mixtures are feasible because all LP constraints are linear, so every intermediate mean occurs. For \(h>M\), failure is identically one; for \(1\le h\le M\), the LP gives the sharp value.

### Vertex structure and explicit frontier menu

At a vertex with support \(\{0,\ldots,K\}\), the positive-coordinate face has \(K+1\) variables and two independent equalities (normalization and mean). The \(K\) supported-edge cap rows are linearly independent path-incidence rows. A vertex therefore needs at least \(K-1\) active supported-edge constraints. Hence at most one supported edge \(j<K\) can be strict. In rate form, an optimal policy can be chosen with \(\lambda_n=B\) on every supported edge except at most one interior edge \(j\) with \(0<\lambda_j<B\), and then a cutoff at \(K\) (or the physical boundary \(M\)). The zero-tail coordinates impose the cutoff; they are not extra “partial birth rates.” A zero partial-rate endpoint is represented by moving the support cutoff down to that edge.

For each proposed support end \(K\), all-cap rates give weights \(w_n=q^n/n!\), (0\le n\le K\). If the one partial edge is (j<K), the unnormalized weights are
\[
u_n=\begin{cases}q^n/n!,&0\le n\le j,\\
\alpha q^n/n!,&j<n\le K,\\
0,&n>K,
\end{cases}\qquad 0\le\alpha\le1.
\]
Define (A_r=\sum_{n=0}^j n^r q^n/n!\) and (T_r=\sum_{n=j+1}^K n^r q^n/n!\), with (n^0=1). The mean constraint gives
\[
\alpha=\frac{\mu A_0-A_1}{T_1-\mu T_0}
\]
when the denominator is nonzero; retain the candidate exactly when \(\alpha\in[0,1]\). If numerator and denominator both vanish, the mean is independent of \(\alpha\) and the fractional-linear tail objective has an endpoint minimizer. Evaluate
\[
\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\min(j,h-1)}q^n/n!+\alpha\sum_{n=j+1}^{\min(K,h-1)}q^n/n!}{A_0+\alpha T_0}
\]
over all feasible (K,j) and the all-cap cases. This is the exact finite frontier: a linear objective on a compact polytope attains its minimum at a vertex, and every vertex is in this menu. A direct candidate scan takes (O(M^2)) candidate evaluations; prefix sums reduce each candidate evaluation to constant-time arithmetic after (O(M)) precomputation. Exact rational inputs permit exact rational outputs, with arithmetic bit growth charged separately.

The statement is bang-bang-plus-one-partial, not standard threshold structure. A single upper cutoff has \(\lambda_n=B\) below its cutoff, possibly one partial cutoff rate, and zero above it. The extremal form proved here allows the exceptional partial rate to occur first and then rates to return to (B) at larger counts.

## Exact capacity-two counterexample to a single cutoff

Take \(\gamma=1\), \(B=1/2\), \(M=2\), \(h=2\), and \(\mu=C=1/64\). Set (t=\pi_2). Normalization and the mean constraint force
\[
\pi_1=\mu-2t,\qquad \pi_0=1-\mu+t.
\]
The edge cap at (0\) is slack throughout this feasible line segment; the edge cap at (1\) says (2t\le\tfrac12(\mu-2t)), or (t\le\mu/6=1/384). Nonnegativity says (t\ge0) and (t\le\mu/2), so (0\le t\le1/384). Since \(P(N<2)=\pi_0+\pi_1=1-t\), the sharp optimum is
\[
\pi=(379,4,1)/384,\qquad \Phi_{2,2}(1/2,1/64)=383/384.
\]
The corresponding rates are \((\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2)=(4/379,1/2,0)\), which exactly use \(\mathbb E\lambda_N=1/64\). Success probability is (P(N\ge2)=1/384).

For the matched standard-cutoff baseline, a cutoff at or above state 1 requires \(\lambda_0=B\). Even with every later rate set to zero, the stationary mean is \(B/(B+\gamma)=1/3>1/64\); adding births at higher states cannot lower it. Thus the only feasible standard cutoff is at state 0, with \(\lambda_0=1/63\), support \(\{0,1\}\), and \(P(N<2)=1\). This is an exact counterexample to threshold optimality for the fixed-mean lower-tail objective.

The reliability criterion is not a variance criterion. The selected law has \(\mathbb E N^2=1/48\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(N)=253/12288\). The matched cutoff law \((63/64,1/64,0)\) has \(\mathbb E N^2=1/64\) and variance \(63/4096\), which is lower. The LP chooses the rarer high-count mass because the objective is (P(N\ge2)), despite increased variance. [The exact checker](CYCLE2_frontier.py) independently enumerates all capacity-two LP vertices by rational active-set solving and agrees with the structural candidate family.

## Infinite-capacity boundary

For \(M=\infty\), use the same LP constraints for all \(n\ge0\) in \(\ell^1\). The recursion gives
\[
0\le\pi_n\le\pi_0q^n/n!\le q^n/n!.
\]
The factorial envelope controls both probability and first-moment tails uniformly. Thus the feasible set is compact in \(\ell^1\), the mean constraint is closed, and the tail objective is continuous; the minimum is attained. The maximum feasible mean is (q), uniquely attained by Poisson\((q)\), and every \(\mu\in[0,q]\) is feasible by convex mixing with \(\delta_0\). The translation budget interval is \([0,B]\).

For an extreme feasible law with infinite positive support, two strict supported-edge inequalities would divide it into three positive blocks. Scale the three blocks by (1\pm\varepsilon c_r), where nonzero (c\in\mathbb R^3) solves the two homogeneous equations preserving total mass and first moment. For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), positivity and the two strict boundary inequalities survive, while all within-block edge ratios are unchanged. This gives two distinct feasible laws averaging to the original, contradicting extremality. Therefore an infinite-support extreme law has at most one strict edge. A finite-support extreme law has at most one strict edge before its cutoff by the finite argument above. Krein–Milman applied to the compact minimizing face yields an extreme optimizer. Consequently the infinite sharp frontier is the minimum over the countable family of finite-support one-partial candidates and the infinite-tail one-partial candidates. For infinite support at edge (j), replace (T_0,T_1) by
\[
T_0=e^q-A_0,\qquad T_1=qe^q-A_1,
\]
and use the same mean and objective formulas with upper endpoint (\infty). At \(\mu=q\), the unique frontier law is Poisson\((q)\); at \(\mu=0\), it is \(\delta_0\).

## Prior art and novelty boundary

The strongest matched mathematical baseline found is Guo, Huang, and Song, “Linear Programming and Constrained Average Optimality for General Continuous-Time Markov Decision Processes in History-Dependent Policies,” *SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization* 50(1), 23–47 (2012), [DOI 10.1137/100805169](https://doi.org/10.1137/100805169). Its publisher abstract explicitly covers constrained expected average costs under randomized history-dependent policies, formulates an equivalent LP, and illustrates the framework with a controlled birth–death system. This makes the LP viewpoint and constrained-average optimization established prior art. I read the publisher abstract and metadata, not the full theorem proofs; exact assumption-by-assumption subsumption is therefore **UNKNOWN**. The specialized adjacent-ratio formulation, one-partial-edge vertex proof, and M=2 threshold counterexample are reconstructed here; novelty/priority is **not established**.

Serfozo, “Optimal Control of Random Walks, Birth and Death Processes, and Queues,” *Advances in Applied Probability* 13(1), 61–83 (1981), [DOI 10.2307/1426467](https://doi.org/10.2307/1426467), studies sequentially controlled birth–death and queue processes, monotone optimal policies under stated conditions, and a linear program. This is strong classical control/LP prior art, but its abstract concerns reward control rather than the fixed-mean lower-tail constraint here. Rezaei et al., “Optimal Admission Control in Queues with Abandonments,” *Operations Research Letters* 50(6), 712–718 (2022), [DOI 10.1016/j.orl.2022.10.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orl.2022.10.012), proves threshold policies for average-profit objectives with admission rewards, holding costs, and abandonment costs. Its monotonic threshold result is tied to that different objective. Abate, D’Onofrio, and Matucci, “Optimal Control of a Birth-and-Death Process Population Model,” *Mathematical Biosciences* 23(1–2), 87–111 (1975), [DOI 10.1016/0025-5564(75)90122-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0025-5564(75)90122-4), treats steering a population mean to a target and cost optimization, including variance-related cost; it does not supply this capped state-feedback tail frontier.

The Hemberg–Barahona “Perfect Sampling” lead is a sampling/domination result rather than the optimization result above. Hemberg and Barahona, “Perfect Sampling of the Master Equation for Gene Regulatory Networks,” *Biophysical Journal* 93(2), 401–410 (2007), [DOI 10.1529/biophysj.106.099390](https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.106.099390), has no separately numbered theorem establishing arbitrary controlled policies. In its section “The Dominated Coupling From The Past–Stochastic Simulation Algorithm,” it states the need for a reversible known-stationary dominator and that the relevant Hill/Monod/linear unimolecular networks can be bounded by first-order networks with multivariate-Poisson stationaries; see the [arXiv full-text PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-bio/0610050), pp. 3 and 8 (the bound on Hill/Monod birth rates appears on PDF p. 8). The scalar constant-birth, linear-death process is immigration–death with Poisson\((B/\gamma)\) stationary law. The paper’s setup is a time-homogeneous Markov chemical master equation with current-state propensities and coupling/order conditions. It does not state a theorem for arbitrary history-dependent policies. A separate elementary coupling extends the scalar upper bound to predictable birth hazards \(\lambda_t\le B\): thin a rate-(B\) Poisson clock for accepted births and share per-particle death clocks; this is not attributed to Hemberg–Barahona.

## Cost, biological interface, and decisive next gate

The model charges exactly the average birth/translation rate (C), equivalently mean protein count (\mu=C/\gamma), along with the hard instantaneous cap (B) and optional hard abundance capacity (M). The dimensionless structure depends on (q=B/\gamma), (\mu), and (h). Rate-table optimization needs no data once (B,\gamma,M,h,C) are supplied. Calibrating those values, acquiring reliable single-cell counts, observation error, promoter and sensing implementation, dynamic delay, cell growth, energy per translation, and parameter uncertainty are external costs not paid here. For rational finite-capacity inputs, the frontier menu has quadratically many ((K,j)) candidates; exact integer bit complexity depends on input encoding and is not bounded by the operation count. The capacity-two checker is exact finite evidence for that instance, not independent proof of the general result or experimental evidence.

The decisive mathematical gate is a full-text mapping against Guo–Huang–Song’s constrained-average LP and controlled birth–death example, followed by a search specifically for fixed-mean lower-tail optimization or equivalent occupation-measure extreme-point statements. Unless the explicit one-partial-edge theorem survives that comparison, treat the contribution as a useful derivation of a standard CTMDP specialization rather than a novelty claim. The decisive biological gate is to restrict \(\lambda_n\) to a measured, implementable promoter/translation family and compare its best achievable frontier with the unrestricted LP and with the matched threshold control. Acquire \(B,\gamma,M,h\) and single-cell stationary counts, and count translation events per unit time. The abstract policy \((4/379,1/2,0)\) need not be realizable by a standard Hill/Monod promoter; a measured family that cannot attain the LP advantage would refute the proposed design relevance while leaving the model theorem intact.
