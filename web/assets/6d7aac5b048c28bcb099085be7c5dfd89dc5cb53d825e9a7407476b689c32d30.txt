A. Hyperbolic-groups paper: statement and where proof is delegated
Primary source: Arthur Bartels, Wolfgang Lück, Holger Reich, “The K-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjecture for hyperbolic groups,” Invent. Math. 172(1) (2008), 29–70, DOI 10.1007/s00222-007-0093-7.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-007-0093-7
- Full primary author-host PDF: https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/blr.pdf
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0701434
- The paper’s Main Theorem is at browser-extracted lines 6–15 (PDF page P0): every hyperbolic G satisfies the K-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjecture with coefficients.
- Its applications preamble is at lines 119–125 (PDF P2). It says the following are applications, does not state the most general versions, and directs readers to reference [6] “for explanations, proofs and further applications.” It assumes there that G satisfies K-theoretic FJ with any ring; the Main Theorem supplies this for hyperbolic G.
- The positive-characteristic Kaplansky assertion itself is explicitly stated at lines 157–163 (PDF P3): if R is a prime-characteristic-p field (or a prime-characteristic-p skew-field with G sofic) and every finite subgroup is a p-group, then RG has only the trivial idempotents. The sofic condition attaches only to the skew-field alternative. This is an application statement in this article, not a self-contained proof of the idempotent claim in its body.
- The paper’s proof section for the Main Theorem is §§1–7; the hyperbolic reduction is Lemma 2.1, lines 328–353 (PDF P6–7). That proves the FJ input, not the listed idempotent corollary itself.

B. Companion J. Topology paper: general route versus exact torsion-free hyperbolic route
Primary source: Bartels, Lück, Reich, “On the Farrell–Jones Conjecture and its applications,” J. Topol. 1(1) (January 2008), 57–86, DOI 10.1112/jtopol/jtm008. Publisher gives first online publication as 2007-10-28.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1112/jtopol/jtm008
- Publisher page: https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1112/jtopol/jtm008
- Full primary author-host PDF: https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/blr2.pdf
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0703548
- General Theorem 1.12, lines 237–245 (PDF P5; printed p. 62): assumes K0(R)⊗Q -> K0(RG)⊗Q is an isomorphism plus one of RG stably finite, R a characteristic-zero field, or R a skew-field and G sofic. The proof is in §6: Theorem 6.3 gives stable finiteness only in the characteristic-zero-field or sofic-skew-field cases, and Lemma 6.4 uses stable finiteness plus the K0 map; lines 1085–1119 (PDF P22–23, printed pp. 79–80). This generic K0/stable-finiteness theorem does not on its own cover a possibly nonsofic group ring over F2.
- The exact case needed here is then covered separately in §1.6, lines 279–289 (PDF P6; printed pp. 63–64). The authors state Formanek’s theorem for a prime-characteristic field F: no p-torsion in G and no nonidentity g conjugate to g^(p^k), k>=1, imply trivial idempotents in FG. They then state torsion-free word-hyperbolic groups satisfy those conditions, hence conclude trivial idempotents for FG for any field F. This is not a new proof by BLR of Formanek’s result; BLR cite Formanek [30] (and [17]) and apply it to torsion-free word-hyperbolic groups.

C. Exact conflict and route
N129 claims torsion-free word-hyperbolic G and R=F2 with ab=1, ac=0, c!=0. The companion’s Formanek paragraph alone already rules out nontrivial idempotents in F2[G]; independently, the hyperbolic-groups paper lists the broader field/F_p-group corollary. From the displayed identities, ba!=1 and ba!=0, and e=1-ba is a nontrivial idempotent. Therefore the N129 conclusion conflicts with the published primary result. No soficity claim is required for this torsion-free hyperbolic field case. The companion’s generic Theorem 1.12 is not the applicable proof step.

Primary-source audit answer: the hyperbolic-group paper proves the Farrell–Jones main theorem and states the positive-characteristic idempotent consequence as an application, pointing to the companion for proof details; it does not give a self-contained proof of that consequence in the applications section. For the precise torsion-free hyperbolic / field case in N129, the companion’s §1.6 explicitly relies on Formanek’s theorem and verifies its group hypotheses. The specific mathematical result used to exclude N129 is therefore the Formanek theorem as cited and applied by BLR, not the companion’s generic Theorem 1.12. The broader all-finite-subgroups-p-groups statement appears in the hyperbolic paper as an FJ application; do not claim the narrower Formanek route proves that broader torsion case.
