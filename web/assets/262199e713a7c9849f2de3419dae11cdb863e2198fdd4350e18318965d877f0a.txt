## Baseline claim and scope

c10_s03 constructs an exact-rational certificate compiler for an admitted
open class of weakly reversible bimolecular networks. It requires: unary
species-to-zero drain paths; an acyclic support-production grammar from zero;
and an acquired partition/positive weight satisfying sourcewise linear and
quadratic Foster inequalities over rational rate boxes. It derives a global
`L_t W <= B-N` inequality, nonexplosion under predictable controls, expected
return to a compact count set, and an explicit but potentially very large
expected hitting bound for the all-species `q`-core using a finite drain and
production path with bounded hazards. It says general bimolecular boundary
recovery and novelty remain open/unknown.

This is a replay of finite diagnostics, not an independent proof of all
unbounded-state claims. The proof-bearing part is the symbolic sourcewise
Foster argument plus the acquired path and retry construction.

My manual audit found no counterexample to these steps under the stated
hypotheses:

1. Grouping reactions by reactant complex makes the generator drift of
   `W=w.x` a sum of nonnegative falling-factorial propensities times
   sourcewise weight coefficients. Independent rate-box vertices therefore
   certify the coefficient inequalities for the whole rectangle, including
   every predictable time/history value.
2. For `i` in the quadratic partition, completing the square gives
   `-x_i^2+(c_i+1)x_i <= -x_i + max(0,c_i+2)^2/4`; for the other species the
   normalized unary coefficient contributes at most `-x_i`. Mixed and
   remaining diagonal quadratic terms are nonpositive. This yields the stated
   boundary-inclusive `L_t W <= B-N <= B-W/w_max` inequality.
3. Stopping at increasing `W` levels gives nonexplosion because each bounded
   count region is finite and has bounded total rate. The negative drift
   outside the acquired compact set gives expected return at most `W(x)`.
4. The unary drain and recursively constructed production word are enabled
   from every point in the compact set. With a path-state count cap `D`, the
   total propensity is at most `Q=U(D+1)^2`; every required firing has
   intensity at least `ell_min`. Conditional survival and event-rate bounds
   give the per-step lower probability `ell_min/(2Q)`, valid after arbitrary
   controller history. The retry calculation uses an unconditional
   `E[1_failure W(X_t0)] <= E W(X_t0)` bound and the geometric upper bound on
   the number of attempts; this supports the displayed `HIT` constant.

No substantive error was found in the exact basis search or checker: endpoint
enumeration is complete for separately boxed rates, feasible vertices are
checked against every row, and the separate finite diagnostic recomputes
generator drifts from the reactions. The reported finite checks are scoped
diagnostics, not a proof of the symbolic claims. I did not independently
rederive every conservative bit-complexity constant (`2^O(L^3)`); the
qualitative termination and finite-output claim follows from the finite exact
basis enumeration, while that specific exponent remains an audit item rather
than a newly verified bound.

The embedded balanced two-species rejection is a useful incompleteness
witness, not a counterexample to the theorem: the network has a familiar
positive recurrent fixed-rate dynamics but violates this particular
sourcewise ansatz. Likewise, `NO_CERTIFICATE_IN_ADMITTED_FOSTER_CLASS` must
not be interpreted as no stochastic recovery. My INITIAL parity-trap
counterexample is separate: `2A <-> 2B`, total two, cannot reach `(1,1)` from
the even-A coset even though the deterministic ODE points inward at the
positive-orthant boundary. It illustrates why a boundary drift surrogate
alone is inadequate, but is not a counterexample within c10_s03's admitted
class.

## Prior art and attribution limits

The exact package is not a basis for a novelty claim. Published work already
contains linear Foster/LP ergodicity criteria for important bimolecular
classes and polynomial-drift tests (Gupta, Briat & Khammash 2014, especially
Propositions 10–11), support-exhaustion tools for count-state irreducibility
(Gupta & Khammash, Lemma 3.1), and a positive-weight, fixed-parameter
bimolecular drift criterion (Briat & Khammash 2017, Theorem 3). The latter
paper also treats uncertainty in selected settings; neither that theorem nor
the cited sources establish arbitrary predictable boundary control. Anderson
& Kim 2018 prove
positive recurrence for weakly reversible binary single-linkage networks
after adding all species inflows/outflows; their 2023 follow-up proves
exponential ergodicity/mixing bounds for the same classes. Those results do
not establish the arbitrary predictable-control statement of c10_s03, but
make static recurrence and the Foster/LP principle established prior art.
Xu's 2026 revision reports second-order regularity and says the general
bimolecular weakly reversible positive-recurrence conjecture remains open.
That general open question is not resolved by c10_s03's restricted acquired
class.
