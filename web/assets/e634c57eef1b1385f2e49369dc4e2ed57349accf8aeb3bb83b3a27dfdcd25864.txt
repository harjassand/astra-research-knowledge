1. Exact target and claim ledger

Target selected: characterize scalar versus completely contractive functional calculus for the finite-dimensional Parrott first-jet tuple with unitary coefficients, then seek a quantitative matrix-level witness and a similarity lower bound. This is deliberately the smallest hard class in which scalar boundedness and complete boundedness can separate.

Claim ledger:
- SOURCE-REPORTED / CLASSICAL: Parrott-type jets on the tridisc can be contractive and fail complete contractivity. Bagchi–Bhattacharyya–Misra (2002), §§3–4, prove for a normalized unitary triple (I,U,V) that the jet homomorphism is completely contractive iff U and V commute; for noncommuting 2x2 U,V it already fails 2-contractivity.
- INTERNALLY DERIVED: an explicit residual-block witness gives a quantitative lower bound for the cb norm and a matrix level at most the residual dimension. This proof is elementary and may be implicit in the classical result; novelty is not established.
- FINITE-TESTED BY EXACT CALCULATION: the Pauli pair X,Z gives the scalar polynomial norm √6 and an amplified norm 3, hence cb norm at least √(3/2). The 3x3 direct-sum example below reduces to the same exact witness at level 2.
- UNRESOLVED: exact cb norm for a general finite unitary family, sharp dependence on the phase-alignment margin, and whether the quantitative result has any literature novelty. No new foundational result or frontier closure is claimed.

2. Parrott first-jet setup and scalar contractivity

Let K be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space and let V_1,...,V_m be unitaries on K, with m≥3. On H=K⊕K define

    N_j = [ 0  V_j ]
          [ 0   0  ].

All products N_iN_j vanish, so the tuple commutes. For a scalar holomorphic f on the polydisc D^m, its functional calculus is exactly

    ρ_V(f) = [ f(0) I       Σ_j (∂_j f)(0) V_j ]
              [   0                    f(0) I          ].

If ||f||∞≤1 and a=f(0), the polydisc Schwarz lemma applied after the disk automorphism sending a to 0 gives

    Σ_j |(∂_j f)(0)| ≤ 1-|a|².

Indeed, for g=(f-a)/(1-conj(a)f), g(0)=0 and sup_{z∈D^m}|g(z)|≤1; applying g(rz)/r as r↓0 gives sup_{|z_j|≤1}|Σ z_j ∂_j g(0)|≤1, whose left side is Σ|∂_j g(0)|. The chain rule gives the displayed bound for f.

Since every V_j is unitary, ||Σ (∂_j f)(0)V_j||≤Σ|∂_j f(0)|≤1-|a|². The block matrix [aI B;0 aI] is a contraction exactly when ||B||≤1-|a|², by the Schur complement of I-T*T. Thus ρ_V is contractive for every unitary coefficient tuple. This is the scalar-versus-matrix gap mechanism: scalar derivatives only see an ℓ1 bound, while matrix derivatives are tested against tensor amplifications of the V_j.

Normalize by a common left unitary: set W_1=I and W_j=V_1*V_j. Conjugating the tuple N_j by diag(V_1,I) removes V_1 from every upper-right block, so the cb norm and complete contractivity are unchanged.

3. Exact structural criterion and constructive dilation

For normalized coefficients W_1=I,W_2,...,W_m, the jet is completely contractive if and only if W_2,...,W_m commute pairwise.

Sufficiency is constructive. Pairwise commuting unitaries have a common orthonormal eigenbasis. On each common eigenline, the tuple is (I,λ_2,...,λ_m) times the same 2x2 nilpotent shift J_2=[0 1;0 0]. Let S be any unitary dilation of J_2. Then (S,λ_2S,...,λ_mS) are commuting unitaries and dilate that scalar block. Taking the direct sum over the common eigenbasis gives a commuting-unitary dilation for the whole tuple, hence complete contractivity.

For necessity, it suffices to find a noncommuting pair among the relative unitaries and restrict to the three coordinates (I,U,V). The following gives a quantitative witness, not only a yes/no obstruction.

Let E be the span of all common eigenvectors of U and V. Because U,V are unitaries, every common eigenline reduces both, so E and K_0=E^⊥ reduce both. If U,V fail to commute, K_0 is nonzero; by construction U|K_0 and V|K_0 have no common eigenvector. Put r=dim K_0 and

    M(U,V) = max_{|z_0|=|z_1|=|z_2|=1}
             || z_0 I_{K_0} + z_1 conj(U|K_0) + z_2 conj(V|K_0) ||.

Then M(U,V)<3. The maximum is attained by compactness; equality in the triangle inequality for three unit vectors would force a common eigenvector of conj(U|K_0),conj(V|K_0), contrary to the definition of K_0.

Use the r×r matrix polynomial

    F(z)=z_0 I_r + z_1 conj(U|K_0) + z_2 conj(V|K_0).

Its polydisc norm is M(U,V), by the maximum principle in each variable. At amplification level r, its jet image has upper-right block

    G = I⊗I + conj(U|K_0)⊗U|K_0 + conj(V|K_0)⊗V|K_0.

For Ω=Σ_{a=1}^r e_a⊗e_a, (conj(U)⊗U)Ω=Ω and (conj(V)⊗V)Ω=Ω. Hence GΩ=3Ω and ||G||≥3. Therefore

    ||ρ_(I,U,V)||_cb ≥ ||ρ_(I,U,V)^(r)|| ≥ 3/M(U,V) > 1.

This proves non-complete-contractivity and gives a witness at level r≤dim K. Applying this to any noncommuting pair W_i,W_j proves necessity for m variables. The criterion is exact for the unitary-coefficient Parrott class.

4. Candidate quantitative geometric invariant

Define the residual phase-alignment defect

    Δ(U,V)=3-M(U,V)

on K_0, and set Δ=0 when K_0=0. It is invariant under simultaneous unitary conjugacy and under independent scalar phases on U,V (the phase variables absorb those scalars). It vanishes exactly when U,V commute, and in the noncommuting case

    ||ρ_(I,U,V)||_cb ≥ 1/(1-Δ(U,V)/3).

Consequently any invertible S for which S^{-1}ρ_V(·)S is completely contractive must satisfy

    ||S|| ||S^{-1}|| ≥ 1/(1-Δ(U,V)/3),

because amplification of S^{-1}ρ_V(·)S changes each output norm by at most the factor ||S||||S^{-1}||. This is a lower bound on every supplied similarity, not an algorithm for finding one.

A second geometric quantity is the joint approximate-eigenvector defect on K_0:

    η(U,V)= min_{||x||=1, x∈K_0} [ inf_{|λ|=1}||Ux-λx||²
                                  + inf_{|μ|=1}||Vx-μx||² ]
          = 4 - 2 max_{||x||=1, x∈K_0}
                         ( |<Ux,x>| + |<Vx,x>| ).

It is positive on K_0. If x and phases attain M, each pairwise phase-aligned vector difference is bounded in squared norm by 9-M², giving η≤2(9-M²), hence Δ≥η/12. Conversely, choose x attaining η and align the phases of conj(U)x,conj(V)x with x; the triangle inequality gives Δ≤√(2η). Thus

    η/12 ≤ Δ ≤ √(2η),
    ||ρ_(I,U,V)||_cb ≥ 3/(3-η/12).

These constants are coarse; the exact margin Δ is the stronger witness. This invariant is a candidate useful for robust certification, not a novelty claim.

If the two coefficient matrices used in F are approximated within ε in operator norm while the target pair U,V remains exact, then ||F_hat||∞≤M+2ε and the amplified output is within 2ε of G, so its norm is at least 3-2ε. Therefore the strict witness survives whenever 4ε<Δ. This is a finite precision tolerance for this explicit certificate; it does not establish a dimension-free tolerance because Δ may shrink with dimension.

5. Explicit finite stress tests

(a) 2x2 Pauli pair. Let X=[[0,1],[1,0]] and Z=[[1,0],[0,-1]]. They are real self-adjoint unitaries and do not commute. For |u|=|v|=1, A=I+uX+vZ satisfies

    A*A = 3I + 2 cos(α)X + 2 cos(β)Z + 2 sin(β-α)Y,

where u=e^{iα}, v=e^{iβ}, and Y is the third Pauli matrix. The squared length of the Pauli coefficient vector is 4S, where

    S=cos²α+cos²β+sin²(β-α)
     =2+cos(α+β)cos(α-β)-cos²(α-β) ≤ 9/4.

Equality is attained when |cos(α-β)|=1/2 and cos(α+β) has the matching sign. Thus sup||A||²=3+3=6 and ||F||∞=√6. The amplified G=I⊗I+X⊗X+Z⊗Z has eigenvalue 3 on the Bell vector (e_1⊗e_1+e_2⊗e_2)/√2 and norm 3. Hence cb≥3/√6=√(3/2). This is the √(3/2) Pauli witness already associated with Parrott's example, not a discovery.

(b) 3x3 reducible triangular extremal. Set U=1⊕X and V=1⊕Z on C⊕C². They share e_1, so the global phase-alignment pencil has norm 3 and the global defect alone sees no gap. The joint-eigenvector residual is K_0=C², where the preceding exact Pauli calculation applies. The resulting three commuting square-zero matrices N_j act on C^6; complete contractivity fails at level 2 with cb≥√(3/2). This is a stress test that requires removing common reducing eigenlines before using the margin.

(c) 3x3 irreducible clock-shift test. Let ω=e^{2πi/3}, U=diag(1,ω,ω²), and V e_j=e_{j+1 mod 3}. U,V have no common eigenvector, so K_0=C³ and M(U,V)<3 by compactness. The explicit witness has matrix level 3 and gives cb≥3/M>1. No exact value for M is claimed. In the N×N clock-shift family the commutator norm tends to zero; the present proof gives no dimension-free lower bound on Δ, cb−1, or similarity distortion.

(d) Single-variable nonnormal controls. J_2 and J_3, the 2×2 and 3×3 nilpotent Jordan shifts, are contractions. For every scalar or matrix-valued disk-algebra function, von Neumann's inequality gives a completely contractive functional calculus. Thus nonnormality and nilpotence alone do not create the separation. The three-variable square-zero tuple above does.

6. Literature comparison and exact boundary

Primary sources checked live on 2026-10-09:

1. B. Bagchi, T. Bhattacharyya, G. Misra, “Some thoughts on Ando’s theorem and Parrott’s example,” Linear Algebra and its Applications 341 (2002), 357–367, author-hosted primary PDF https://www.math.iisc.ac.in/~gm/gmhomefiles/papers/cvscc.pdf. §§3–4, PDF pp. 6–8 (Theorems 3.7 and 4.3): for normalized (I,U,V), complete contractivity iff U,V commute; arbitrary noncommuting finite-dimensional U,V yield non-CC; the 2x2 noncommuting case already fails at level 2. This directly covers the main structural theorem here. The all-m extension follows by restricting to each triple (I,W_i,W_j) and simultaneous diagonalization.
2. G. Pisier, “A polynomially bounded operator on Hilbert space which is not similar to a contraction,” arXiv:math/9602207 (1996), https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9602207, pp. 8–9. CAR/Clifford coefficient families give the classical √(n/2) cb lower bound and the non-similarity construction. Thus the suggested Pauli-to-Clifford growth route is explicitly prior work.
3. V. I. Paulsen, “The maximal operator space of a normed space,” Proc. Edinburgh Math. Soc. 39 (1996), 309–323, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-edinburgh-mathematical-society/article/maximal-operator-space-of-a-normed-space/2FD3D621A0E647EAD9AC0809819BB91D. The paper identifies CB(MIN(X),MAX(Y)) with a Hilbert-space factorization ideal and gives estimates for free-unitary spans. This confirms that a generic MIN/MAX or Clifford extension is a classical operator-space route.

The residual phase-alignment formula and η comparison above were derived internally. A targeted search did not establish whether those quantitative formulations are new; do not assert priority or novelty. The exact structural result itself is known, and this phase therefore does not meet the campaign's foundational-discovery objective.

7. Remaining hard obstruction / next gate

The strongest unresolved quantitative target is to determine the cb norm (or a sharp dimension-free modulus for cb−1) from relative unitary geometry beyond the one-polynomial lower bound Δ. In particular, for almost-commuting clock-shift pairs, this record proves only non-CC at every finite dimension and a positive but unquantified Δ_N; it neither proves Δ_N stays bounded below nor that the full cb norm tends to 1. Resolving that would require a genuinely new upper/lower mechanism, and cannot be replaced by the classical Clifford estimate or by solving a Lyapunov inequality and reporting its condition number.

No software or external numerical solver was used. The norm computations in the Pauli case are exact algebra; the strict 3x3 clock-shift gap follows from compactness and absence of common eigenvectors, without a numerical value.
