PART A. AUDITED EXACT GENERATOR, INCLUDING THE SIGN

Put tau(m)=(I+m.sigma)/2, k_N(m)=tau(m)^tensor N. For unit n, define
 u=n.m, v=n-u m, r=n cross m,
 B_n=N u+v.grad, R_n=r.grad, F=n.J.
For one qubit,
 {n.sigma/2,tau(m)}=(u I+n.sigma)/2,
 [n.sigma/2,tau(m)]=i(n cross m).sigma/2.
The product rule over N factors therefore proves, at every N and |m|<1,
 {F,k_N}=B_n k_N, [F,k_N]=i R_n k_N.
Constant left/right matrix multiplication commutes with m differentiation.
Adding the nested anticommutator and commutator gives
 B_n^2 k_N-R_n^2 k_N=2(F^2 k_N+k_N F^2).
Thus {F^2,k_N}/2=(B_n^2-R_n^2)k_N/4. The negative angular
second-order term is essential; omitting it changes the quantum Gibbs target.

For positive C with eigenaxes (lambda_i,n_i), H=Q_C/s^2+b.J/s,
M_H(X)={H,X}/2, its exact action on product kernels is
 M_H k_N=G k_N,
 G f=V(m) f+a(m).grad f+D(m):Hess f/(4s^2),
 D=(I-mm^T)C(I-mm^T)-[m]_cross C[m]_cross^T,
 a=(N-1)/(2s^2)[Cm-(m^TCm)m]+[b-(b.m)m]/(2s),
 V=N(N-1)m^TCm/(4s^2)+N Tr C/(4s^2)+N b.m/(2s).
To check the drift without eigenaxis decisions, directly differentiate:
 (v.grad)v=-m-u n+2u^2 m, (r.grad)r=u n-m.
Their difference is -2u(n-u m); adding 2N u v gives 2(N-1)u v.
The zeroth-order part of B_n^2 is N^2u^2+N(1-u^2), which gives V.
The rotation term has no zeroth-order part. Taking trace confirms
 V(m)=Tr[H k_N(m)]. All three coefficients are basis-independent polynomials.

PART B. A SHIFT WITH BOUNDED CONDITION NUMBER, AND A FIXED STOPPING BALL

Use the public bound M, which may depend on N, and choose
 Lambda=2M+1, C=A+Lambda I,
 cmin=M+1, L=3M+1.
Then cmin I <= C <= L I and L/cmin <=3. This is the improvement over
c07_s02's shift Lambda=M+1, whose condition number grew with M and forced
its stopping radius to shrink. Set r0=1/4 for every M,N.

For |m|<=r0 and any vector z,
 z^TDz >= [cmin(1-|m|^2)^2-L|m|^2]|z|^2
        >= (177/256)cmin |z|^2.
Also D <= (I-mm^T)C(I-mm^T) <= L I because ||I-mm^T||<=1.
These are operator inequalities; no simultaneous diagonalization of C and
mm^T is presumed. The square root is smooth on a neighborhood of the ball,
with a public positive margin. D need not be PSD near the pure-state boundary.

The simplified drift gives the sharper bound
 |a(m)| <= beta_N:=L r0/(2 sqrt N)+B/(2s).
Indeed Cm-(m^TCm)m=(I-mm^T)Cm and the projection-like factor has norm <=1.
Assume the explicit drift condition
 L/sqrt N+4B/N^(3/4) <=1/4.                            (A1)
Then beta_N <=r0/8.

Let m_t, until tau=inf{t:|m_t|=r0}, solve the three-dimensional SDE
 dm_t=a(m_t)dt+sqrt(D(m_t)/(2s^2))dW_t.
Freeze both position and weight at tau, and put
 w_t=exp[integral_0^(t wedge tau) V(m_u)du].
Smooth, locally Lipschitz coefficients on the certified closed ball give the
usual stopped solution. This is a mathematical probability law, not an
executed exact-real sampler. Every w_t k_N(m_(t wedge tau)) is positive
and lies in the cone of identical products.

Set t0=2/N-Lambda/s^2 and H=Q_C/s^2+b.J/s. Then
 K=t0 J^2+H, with J^2 central in the permutation-invariant spin algebra.
Assume N>=Lambda^2. This implies t0=2d0/N>0, d0=1-Lambda/(2sqrt N)>=1/2.
A global bound, used for weights and the quantum semigroup, is
 h_N=L sqrt N/4+L/(2sqrt N)+B N^(1/4)/2 >=||H||.
Here Q_C <=L J^2 and max spin is N/2. Consequently |V|<=h_N,
||exp(u M_H)||_(Hermitian trace norm)<=exp(u h_N), and

PART C. THE EXACT HEAT SEED, AND FULLY EXPLICIT OUTER-MASS CONSTANTS

The normalized density rho0=exp(t0 J^2)/Tr exp(t0 J^2) has a positive
identical-product mixture with radial field law
 q_t(r) proportional to r^2 sinch(r/2)
                    exp[-r^2/(4t0)+N logcosh(r/2)], r>=0,
 m=tanh(r/2) u, u uniform on the unit sphere.
This identity can be checked on every spin-j irrep, not assumed from a
classical saddle-point approximation. Since
 k_N(tanh(r/2)u)=exp(r u.J)/(2cosh(r/2))^N,
sphere integration is scalar on spin j and equals
 sinh((2j+1)r/2)/[(2j+1)sinh(r/2)] times the identity.
Multiplying by r^2 sinch(r/2) and integrating leaves
 (2/(2j+1)) integral_0^infinity r exp[-r^2/(4t0)]
                                    sinh((2j+1)r/2)dr
 =2 sqrt(pi) t0^(3/2) exp(t0/4) exp[t0 j(j+1)].
The prefactor is independent of j, proving the stated normalized heat mixture.

Define fixed constants
 a0=2atanh(r0/2)=ln(9/7), delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0,
 kappa0=min(a0^4/768,delta/4),
 U0=(8/3)sinh(1)+16sqrt(pi) delta^(-3/2),
 C0=(3/7) exp(1/12) U0.
The bad seed event |m|>r0/2 is r>a0.
For f(u)=u^2/2-logcosh u, on 0<=u<=1,
 u^4/48 <=f(u)<=u^4/12.
This follows by integrating f''=tanh^2 u twice, using u/2<=tanh u<=u.
For u>=1, f(u)>=delta u^2: logcosh(u)/u^2 is decreasing because
u tanh u-2logcosh u has nonpositive derivative u sech^2u-tanh u.
Since t0<=2/N, the unnormalized density is bounded above by
 r^2 sinch(r/2) exp[-N f(r/2)].
For a0<=r<=2 its integral is at most (8/3)sinh(1) exp[-Na0^4/768].
For r>=2 and N delta>=2, sinch(r/2)<=exp(r/2) and
 -N delta r^2/4+r/2 <=-N delta r^2/8.
Split the last exponential into two equal factors, bounding one by
exp(-N delta/4), and integrate the other Gaussian over all r>=0.
The exterior integral is <=16sqrt(pi)delta^(-3/2) exp(-N delta/4).

For the lower normalizer, use r=N^(-1/4)R, 1<=R<=2. Its exponent is
 -N f(r/2)-N r^2(1/d0-1)/8 >=-1/12-Lambda/2,
because d0>=1/2. Also sinch>=1 and integral_1^2 R^2 dR=7/3.
Thus the lower normalizer is >=(7/3)N^(-3/4)exp[-Lambda/2-1/12], and
 pbad <= C0 exp(Lambda/2) N^(3/4) exp(-kappa0 N).        (A2)
All dependence on the growing parameter M is explicit in exp(Lambda/2).

Replace each bad branch by m=0, retaining the good branches, to obtain rho0'.
Then ||rho0'-rho0||_1<=2pbad and every new starting point has |m_0|<=r0/2.

PART D. EXIT, EXACT STOPPED RESIDUAL, AND NORMALIZATION

Under (A1), a path exiting by time one has martingale displacement at least
3r0/8. Each coordinate's quadratic variation is <=L/(2s^2). A coordinate
has supremum absolute value >=3r0/(8sqrt3); the exponential-martingale
maximal bound and the six signed coordinates yield
 P(tau<=1) <=6exp(-E_N), E_N=3r0^2 N^(3/2)/(64L).       (A3)
This uses only a public upper variance bound and is uniform in all starting
branches and all admitted A,b.

Let W_t=E[w_t k_N(m_(t wedge tau))]. Ito's formula, in integrated form,
gives W_t'=E[1_(t<tau) w_t M_H k_N(m_t)] for almost every t. Hence
 W_t'-M_H W_t=-E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_H k_N(m_tau)].
Duhamel gives the exact finite-dimensional identity
 exp(M_H)rho0'-W_1
 =integral_0^1 exp[(1-t)M_H]
        E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_H k_N(m_tau)]dt.
There is no claim that the globally indefinite differential generator gives
a globally positive diffusion. On the stopped branches, w_tau<=exp(tau h_N)
and tau<=t, so the semigroup factor and weight together cost <=exp(h_N).
This slightly improves the exposed exp(2h_N) residual bound:
 ||exp(M_H)rho0'-W_1||_1<=h_N exp(h_N)P(tau<=1).
Including the seed replacement gives
 ||G_N-W_1||_1<=2 exp(h_N)pbad+6h_N exp(h_N-E_N),
 G_N=exp(M_H)rho0=exp(K)/Zbase.

The trace g_N=Tr G_N is >=1. In each spin-j block,
 Tr H_j/(2j+1)=Tr(C)j(j+1)/(3s^2)>=0; the field has trace zero.
Scalar Jensen on the eigenvalues implies Tr exp(H_j)>=(2j+1).
The central seed weighting and positive multiplicities preserve this bound.
For any positive G,W, T(G/TrG,W/TrW)<=||G-W||_1/TrG, by the triangle
inequality and |TrG-TrW|<=||G-W||_1. Normalize W_1 to get sigma_N in C_iid,N.

The public certificate is therefore
 T(rho_N,sigma_N)
 <=2 C0 N^(3/4) exp[h_N+Lambda/2-kappa0 N]
     +6h_N exp[h_N-E_N],                               (A4)
provided N>=max{1,Lambda^2,2/delta} and (A1). This is a whole-density bound.

PART F. EXACT FINITE-N DISTANCE AT THE SQRT-N ANISOTROPY SCALE

Fix kappa>0 and A_N=-2sqrt N I-kappa sqrt N e_z e_z^T, b_N=0.
Then ||A_N||=(2+kappa)sqrt N and K=-kappa J_z^2/N exactly.
Its faithful diagonal density is a classical distribution over all spin strings.
Let K_z be the number of up spins. Under I/2^N,
 p_N(k)=2^(-N)binom(N,k), 0<=k<=N.
Let
 g_N(k)=exp[-kappa(k-N/2)^2/N], z_N=sum_k p_N(k)g_N(k),
 q_N(k)=p_N(k)g_N(k)/z_N.
The target and the maximally mixed state are uniform within each Hamming
class, so their half trace distance is
 D_N(kappa)=(1/2)sum_k p_N(k)|g_N(k)/z_N-1|.             (B1)

Let C_N={k:g_N(k)>=z_N}. This is a symmetric central integer interval
{l,l+1,...,N-l}; equality endpoints have no effect. The difference q_N-p_N
is nonnegative precisely there. Thus
 q_N(C_N)-p_N(C_N)=D_N(kappa).

For every p in [0,1], Bin(N,p)(C_N)<=Bin(N,1/2)(C_N).
Here is a finite exact proof for any symmetric central interval, without an
asymptotic normal argument. For 1<=l<=N/2, u=N-l, differentiate its mass:
 d/dp sum_(k=l)^u binom(N,k)p^k(1-p)^(N-k)
 =N[binom(N-1,l-1)p^(l-1)(1-p)^(N-l)
       -binom(N-1,u)p^u(1-p)^(N-1-u)].
The two binomial coefficients are equal. Their probability ratio is
[(1-p)/p]^(u-l+1), so the derivative is nonnegative below 1/2 and
nonpositive above 1/2. The full interval is trivial; endpoint limits follow
by continuity. This also covers odd N.

For any qubit density tau, measurement of the N z spins on tau^tensor N
has law Bin(N,p), p=(1+Tr tau sigma_z)/2. Coherences of tau cannot change
this measurement distribution. The event projector E_(C_N) consequently
has expectation <=p_N(C_N) under EVERY sigma in C_iid,N. The two-outcome
measurement gives
 T(rho_N,sigma)>=Tr E_(C_N)(rho_N-sigma)>=D_N(kappa).
The state I/2^N is itself an identical product and attains (B1). Therefore
 inf_(sigma in C_iid,N) T(rho_N,sigma)=D_N(kappa)        (B2)
at every finite N, including the trivial D_1=0. This is an exact closest-state
result, not merely a lower-bound test on a few product states.

PART G. EXPLICIT POSITIVE LIMIT OF THE EXACT DISTANCE

Set Z=2(K_z-N/2)/sqrt N under p_N. Its characteristic function is
[cos(t/sqrt N)]^N, tending to exp(-t^2/2); hence Z tends to a standard
normal. The bounded continuous weight is exp(-kappa Z^2/4). Thus
 z_N ->(1+kappa/2)^(-1/2),
and bounded-continuous convergence applied to the absolute-value expression
in (B1) proves convergence of D_N to the TV distance between centered
Gaussians with variances 1 and (1+kappa/2)^(-1).
Let beta=1+kappa/2 and a_kappa=sqrt[2ln(beta)/kappa]. The densities cross
at +/-a_kappa in standard-normal coordinates. Consequently
 D_N(kappa) -> D_infinity(kappa)
   =2[Phi(a_kappa sqrt(beta))-Phi(a_kappa)]>0.          (B3)
This holds for every fixed kappa>0. For kappa=2 the limit is about 0.166.
No full density of size 2^N is needed to evaluate the scalar certificate.

This proves a scale boundary for the claimed IDENTICAL-product conclusion:
small constant multiples of the sqrt-N anisotropy envelope admit the
exponential upper bound, whereas envelopes exceeding (2+kappa)sqrt N for
fixed kappa admit a constant error lower bound. The exact constant separating
the regimes is not identified. It also makes clear why fixed-parameter S1
cannot be promoted to all growing parameters by hiding M in the constants.
