CYCLE 10 — REPETITION LOWER-BOUND AUDIT
Independent literature review; 2026-10-09

STATUS
This report audits lower-bound obstructions to the proposed conditional consequences. It does not establish either proposed upper bound. It does not inspect the active sampler, bridge, or wrapper proof bodies.

MAIN RESULT
The strongest model-matched obstruction found is the Kempe–Regev binary line game. It has answer-product size d=4 and exact commuting one-round gap epsilon_m=1/(2m-1). For every repetition count ell >= m^2, its repeated commuting value is at least (1-O(m^-2))^ell. Since m^-2=Theta(epsilon_m^2), no universal all-wins exponent f(epsilon,d) can satisfy f(epsilon,4)/epsilon^2 -> infinity as epsilon -> 0. In particular, a general fixed-alphabet rate proportional to epsilon, or epsilon^p for any p<2, is impossible. The proposed epsilon^3/(epsilon+log d) rate is consistent with this obstruction and has room for one epsilon power of improvement at fixed d; the audit does not prove that improvement.

1. EXACT COMMUTING-MODEL OBSTRUCTION: THE LINE GAME

For integer m>=2, the line game L_m has questions [m] for each player and answer alphabets {1,2}. The verifier chooses uniformly one of the 2m-1 edges of the path with vertices 1,...,m, including one loop at each vertex. All constraints require equal answers except the loop at vertex 1, which forces both answers to 1, and the loop at vertex m, which forces both answers to 2. Thus d=|A||B|=4.

Kempe–Regev, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.2, prove
  omega(L_m) = omega_tensor(L_m) = omega_ns(L_m) = 1 - 1/(2m-1).
Here omega is the classical value, omega_tensor is the shared-entanglement/tensor value, and omega_ns is the no-signaling value. Every tensor strategy is a commuting strategy, and every commuting strategy is no-signaling: for commuting measurement effects A_a^x and B_b^y, summing over b gives the Alice marginal <psi|A_a^x|psi>, independent of y. Hence
  omega_tensor(L_m) <= omega_co(L_m) <= omega_ns(L_m),
so the commuting value is also exactly 1-1/(2m-1), with epsilon_m=1/(2m-1).

For the conventional ell-fold parallel product, each coordinate's question edge is sampled independently from this same law; Alice and Bob receive their full question vectors simultaneously, return answer vectors, and the verifier accepts iff every coordinate constraint is satisfied. Kempe–Regev, Theorem 4.4, prove for ell>=m^2
  omega_ns(L_m^ell) >= omega_tensor(L_m^ell) >= omega(L_m^ell) >= (1-O(m^-2))^ell.
The final inequality is witnessed by a classical repeated strategy, so it is also a valid lower bound on omega_co(L_m^ell). As epsilon_m=1/(2m-1), m^-2=Theta(epsilon_m^2); the repetition threshold ell>=m^2 is Theta(epsilon_m^-2). Therefore, for some constants C,c>0 and all sufficiently large m, this family has
  omega_co(L_m^ell) >= exp(-C epsilon_m^2 ell)    (ell >= c epsilon_m^-2).
This rules out any uniform upper bound exp(-c_0 f(epsilon,d) ell) with a fixed c_0>0 and f(epsilon,4)/epsilon^2 unbounded along epsilon_m. It is an actual commuting-model obstruction, not an inference from a classical gap alone.

Historical importance: this paper answered the then-open strong parallel-repetition question for entangled and no-signaling provers in the negative. Its binary line game shows that answer size two suffices for a quadratic-gap obstruction; the paper separately gives a three-answer unique-game variant. The line game is especially useful here because its base values coincide exactly across classical, tensor-entangled, and no-signaling models, making the commuting gap exact by inclusion.

2. CLASSICAL ODD CYCLE: WHY THE MODEL CHECK MATTERS

Raz's odd-cycle game has binary answers, classical gap 1/(2m), and a classical repeated-game lower bound of the form (1-1/(4m^2))^{O(ell)} for sufficiently large ell. On the classical gap parameter, this is the familiar epsilon^2-rate obstruction at fixed answer size. It must not be transferred using that same epsilon to the commuting theorem: Cleve–Hoyer–Toner–Watrous show the game's optimal quantum value is cos^2(pi/(4m)), so its entangled gap is sin^2(pi/(4m))=Theta(m^-2), not Theta(m^-1). They also record perfect parallel repetition for entangled XOR games. The classical odd-cycle construction therefore does not supply an independent epsilon^2-in-the-commuting-gap obstruction; the line game above does.

3. GENERIC PRODUCT STRATEGY AND THRESHOLD TAIL

For the classical, tensor, and commuting models, coordinatewise independent use of an approximately optimal one-round strategy (using product copies of its shared resource) gives
  omega_M(G^ell) >= omega_M(G)^ell.
For a nonattained supremum, use a strategy within eta of the value and send eta to zero after fixing ell. Thus any universal all-wins exponent rate is at most -log(1-epsilon)=Theta(epsilon). This generic fact is weaker than the line-game epsilon^2 obstruction but applies without any special game structure.

For the threshold event of winning at least a fraction 1-epsilon+delta, where 0<delta<=epsilon, the same coordinatewise strategy gives X distributed as Binomial(ell,1-epsilon) in the limit of approximate optimizers. For 0<delta<epsilon, its upper-tail probability has exponential rate
  D(1-epsilon+delta || 1-epsilon),
where D is binary relative entropy. Consequently no uniform threshold upper exponent can exceed this rate (up to asymptotic constants). When delta=o(epsilon),
  D(1-epsilon+delta || 1-epsilon) = (1+o(1)) delta^2/[2 epsilon(1-epsilon)],
which is approximately delta^2/(2 epsilon) when epsilon is small.
At delta=epsilon, the endpoint all-wins rate from this product strategy is -log(1-epsilon)=Theta(epsilon).

For L_m, every threshold with delta<=epsilon_m contains the all-wins event. The line-game lower bound therefore caps any uniform threshold exponent at O(epsilon_m^2) for bounded d, even at thresholds below the all-wins endpoint. Combining these controls gives the following necessary ceiling on a putative threshold rate, for fixed d and small epsilon, in the ranges covered by the examples:
  rate <= O(min{D(1-epsilon+delta || 1-epsilon), epsilon^2}).
This is a lower-bound ceiling only; it is not a proven matching threshold theorem. In particular, the proposed delta^5/[epsilon^2(delta+log d)] rate is below both controls, and the audit establishes no matching upper bound.

4. ANSWER-ALPHABET / LOG d DEPENDENCE

For calibration, Holenstein's classical general-game upper bound is val(G)<=1-epsilon implies val(G^ell)<=(1-Omega(epsilon^3))^{ell/log(|A||B|)}. It is a classical theorem; it does not itself prove the proposed commuting consequence. Feige–Verbitsky show that the answer-size cost cannot simply be removed classically. Holenstein's primary 2009 paper records the quantitative statement: for infinitely many total answer counts s, Theta(log s/log log s) repetitions leave the value larger than 1/8 from a base value at most 3/4. For balanced alphabets |A|=|B|=q, s=Theta(q) and log(|A||B|)=2 log q, so this makes the classical logarithmic answer-size denominator nearly necessary up to a log-log factor. Holenstein's 2007 primary paper likewise explicitly says the logarithmic answer-size dependence in Raz's theorem is essentially necessary.

This does not establish the same lower bound with epsilon defined by omega_co. The classical construction is useful for the classical model, but a commuting-value upper certificate for that family is needed before its classical base gap can be substituted into a commuting statement. No such certificate was derived here. The line game has d=4 and therefore settles the epsilon-power obstruction without testing the log d dependence.

5. COMPARISON WITH THE PROPOSED RATES

At d=4, epsilon+log d is bounded above and below by positive constants. The proposed all-wins exponent is therefore Theta(epsilon^3); the line game permits at most O(epsilon^2) as a universal rate, so one power is potentially recoverable, while any rate asymptotically larger than epsilon^2 is ruled out.

At d=4, the proposed threshold rate is Theta(delta^5/epsilon^2). In the small-deviation regime delta=o(epsilon), the product strategy only permits a rate of order delta^2/epsilon; near delta=epsilon, the line-game all-wins event permits at most order epsilon^2. These are distinct controls and must both be respected by any improvement. The literature audit alone does not prove an upper bound matching their minimum.

6. TWO ACTIONABLE DIRECTIONS (TARGETS, NOT RESULTS)

1. For the all-wins consequence, attempt to improve the bounded-answer dependence from epsilon^3 to epsilon^2, while separately accounting for log d. Use L_m as a hard falsifier: any proposed universal fixed-d rate f(epsilon,4) with f/epsilon^2 unbounded is impossible. To claim a sharper log d dependence, first obtain a commuting-value gap certificate for the Feige–Verbitsky answer-size family; classical evidence alone does not pass that interface.

2. For the threshold consequence, target a rate that respects both the product-strategy scale D(1-epsilon+delta||1-epsilon) (approximately delta^2/epsilon for delta=o(epsilon)) and the line-game cap O(epsilon^2) throughout delta<=epsilon. A proof matching min{D,epsilon^2} up to the answer-size factor would be a concrete improvement target, but it remains open in this audit and is not implied by the lower bounds.

7. CLAIM STATUS / LIMITS

Established from primary sources: the line-game definitions, exact single-game classical/tensor/no-signaling values, its repeated classical lower bound, the odd-cycle quantum value, and the cited classical answer-size phenomenon.

Derived here: the exact commuting line-game value by the tensor/no-signaling sandwich; the epsilon^2 fixed-answer all-wins obstruction; the generic product strategy bound; the binomial threshold lower bound and its small-delta expansion; and the resulting constraints on proposed exponent improvements.

Not established: either proposed universal upper theorem; a general epsilon^2 all-wins upper rate; a threshold upper rate matching the derived minimum; a commuting-model Feige–Verbitsky answer-size obstruction; or any optimal dependence on log d for the commuting value.

FALSIFIERS
The strongest direct falsifier for a claimed all-wins rate is L_m: a verified universal rate larger than C epsilon^2 at d=4 contradicts Theorem 4.4. For a threshold rate, independent coordinate play falsifies any rate larger than the binary relative-entropy tail scale in the small-delta regime, and L_m falsifies rates larger than O(epsilon^2) for delta<=epsilon. Any assertion that the classical Feige–Verbitsky lower bound transfers unchanged to omega_co is falsified unless the base commuting gap for that family is proved.
