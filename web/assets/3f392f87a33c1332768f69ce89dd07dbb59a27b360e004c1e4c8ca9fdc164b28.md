# Source ledger: nonlinear observability and feedback

## Closest prior art and proof pointers

1. **Hermann & Krener (1977), “Nonlinear Controllability and Observability,” IEEE TAC 22(5), 728–740.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1977.1101601); author-hosted [PDF](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~krener/1-25/10.IEEETAC77.pdf). Theorem 3.1 says the observability rank condition implies local weak observability. Theorem 3.12 gives the equivalence between weak observability, local weak observability, and the observability rank condition for weakly controllable analytic systems. Example 3.3 warns that local weak observability does not imply global observability. The local PDF is scanned; relevant pages 733–738 were inspected by OCR. The theorem is qualitative and noise-free, with controls supplied.

2. **Hausman et al. (2016), “Observability-Aware Trajectory Optimization for Self-Calibration with Application to UAVs.”** [arXiv:1604.07905](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.07905). Sections III–V define global/weak local observability, construct local/empirical Gramians, use the least singular value as an observability metric, and optimize trajectories under motion constraints; the simulated UAV comparison includes EKF covariance and heuristic paths. The system is nonlinear and the input trajectory is deliberately chosen for better estimation. This is directly relevant active-observability prior art, although its metric is local/linearized and its demonstration is simulation-based.

3. **Alaeddini, Morgansen & Mesbahi (2019), “Augmented State Feedback for Improving Observability of Linear Systems with Nonlinear Measurements,” Systems & Control Letters 133, 104520.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2019.104520); [arXiv:1908.11329](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11329). Proposition 1 proves asymptotic stability for the proposed augmented feedback on controllable linear dynamics with nonlinear observation. Theorem 1 establishes parameter choices preserving positive running cost under a Lipschitz output and a stable closed loop. Section 5.1 gives a two-dimensional integrator with bearing-only output where LQR drives the system along an unobservable trajectory; an observability term adds a decaying oscillatory probe. Full state is assumed in synthesis; the paper separately illustrates the state-estimation/control coupling.

4. **Knüfer & Müller (2023), “Nonlinear Full Information and Moving Horizon Estimation: Robust Global Asymptotic Stability.”** [arXiv:2105.02764v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02764). The model is a general nonlinear discrete-time system with known inputs and unknown process and measurement disturbances. Time-discounted incremental input/output-to-state stability (i-IOSS) is the detectability condition. Theorem 11 proves RGAS for full-information estimation; Theorem 14 gives sufficient finite-horizon conditions for RGAS MHE and explicit convergence/disturbance gains. This covers substantially more disturbance structure than the candidate, but takes the input trajectory as given rather than optimizing its excitation energy.

5. **Deole & Mesbahi (2025 preprint), “Estimation-Aware Trajectory Optimization with Set-Valued Measurement Uncertainties.”** [arXiv:2501.09192v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09192). Section 3 defines finite-horizon weak observability for set-valued output tubes. Theorem IV.1 proves concavity of a lower bound on degree of observability in the state trajectory for output-regular maps, which is then used for estimation-aware trajectory optimization. This is the closest conceptual prior: the present square-law sensor with bounded additive noise is an elementary set-valued output map. It leaves open whether the exact minimax energy law below is already an immediate specialization of their framework or existing set-membership control results.

6. **Guo & Streif (2025), “MHE in Output Feedback Control of Uncertain Nonlinear Systems via IQCs.”** [arXiv:2511.03221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03221), IEEE Control Systems Letters 9, 2513–2518. Theorem 5.2 gives ISS of the closed loop with a moving-horizon estimator under IQC-based robust detectability and a state-feedback controller that is already ISS when given the true state; the horizon must satisfy the paper’s matrix-dependent lower bound. This is a strong current output-feedback estimation/control baseline with nonparametric uncertainty. It assumes a detectability certificate and does not claim the candidate's excitation-energy optimum.

7. **Wang et al. (2026 preprint), “Nonlinear Moving-Horizon Estimation Using State- and Control-Dependent Models.”** [arXiv:2604.00309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00309). Theorem 1 bounds the estimate under uniform observability, bounded disturbances, bounded arrival-cost error, and stated compactness/Lipschitz hypotheses; Algorithm 1 gives an iterative MHE solver and the paper compares it with EKF/UKF/full nonlinear MHE. It is a current computational state-estimation baseline, not an excitation-design theorem.
