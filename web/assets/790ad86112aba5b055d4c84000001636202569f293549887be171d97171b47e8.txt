CYCLE 2 RESULT: UNKNOWN-TRANSITION, UNKNOWN-EMISSION HMM FULL-HISTORY PREDICTION
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the exact two-state class and losses below. Novelty and external validation: UNKNOWN.

1. CONCLUSION

For a stationary symmetric two-state HMM with both its transition and emission parameters unknown, a learner can use one legal raw observation path to construct a predictor that works uniformly after every finite observed history. On the stated compact parameter class, its worst-history one-step expected total-variation risk is O(T^(-1/2)); its worst-history one-step predictive KL risk is O(T^(-1)); and its H-step conditional future KL risk is O(H/T). The one-step TV and KL orders are minimax-matched against every estimator of the complete raw path.

The construction uses two empirical lag moments, not an entry oracle, hidden-state labels, or a supplied parameter value. The observations are dependent, but these particular lag-product statistics are finite-dependent because the symmetric two-state chain has an independent-flip representation. This finite-dependence fact is proved below and is special to this family; it is not inferred from geometric mixing alone.

The result is a margin-cost control for a structured HMM family, not a general-HMM acquisition theorem. All structural margins are fixed and charged. Predictive risk remains controlled even on arbitrarily long, exponentially rare histories because the filtering recursion contracts uniformly. Removing the parameter box or extending the uniform filter-stability argument to arbitrary HMMs remains UNKNOWN.

2. MODEL, DATA, AND TARGET

Hidden states and observations are in {−1,+1}. The stationary hidden state S_1 is uniform. For unknown parameters

    lambda in [1/2,3/4],       m in [1/2,3/4],

the HMM is

    Pr(S_(t+1)=s' | S_t=s) = (1 + lambda s s')/2,
    Pr(Y_t=y | S_t=s) = (1 + m y s)/2.

The learner knows this family and the interval but not lambda or m. It observes only one stationary raw path Y_1,...,Y_T. Hidden states and emission/transition coins are never exposed. No resets, membership queries, selected word-event calls, or interventions are supplied. The output is a predictive model that can be evaluated after any finite history h in {−1,+1}*.

For a returned model q, define

    TV risk: R_T^TV(q) = sup_(lambda,m) E[ sup_h TV(P_(lambda,m)(Y_next | h), q(.|h)) ],
    KL risk: R_T^KL(q) = sup_(lambda,m) E[ sup_h D_KL(P_(lambda,m)(Y_next | h) || q(.|h)) ].

For a future horizon H>=1, define

    R_(T,H)^future(q) = sup_(lambda,m) E[ sup_h D_KL(P_(lambda,m)(Y_(1:H) | h) || q_(1:H)(. | h)) ].

Here q_(1:H) is the joint law generated by the returned sequential predictor. KL uses natural logarithms. Expectations are over the full length-T training path. The supremum over histories is literal; it includes contexts whose probability is exponentially small in their length.

3. EXACT PROMISES AND MARGINS

Every hidden transition and emission probability is at least 1/8. The hidden chain is stationary and reversible, and its Dobrushin contraction coefficient is lambda<=3/4. From a point initial state, its total-variation distance to stationarity after t steps is lambda^t/2, so the 1/4 mixing time is at most 3. The emission matrix has singular values 1 and m, hence sigma_min>=1/2. The transition matrix has singular values 1 and lambda, hence sigma_min>=1/2.

The observed process has full Hankel rank 2. Its length-two pair block, with rows/columns indexed by observed symbols, is

    J(a,b) = Pr(Y_t=a,Y_(t+1)=b) = (1 + m^2 lambda ab)/4.

Its singular values are 1/2 and m^2 lambda/2, so the complete (one-symbol prefix, one-symbol suffix) witness has second singular value at least 1/16. A two-state HMM bounds the full Hankel rank above by 2; this nonzero minor proves equality. The observed one-symbol contexts have mass exactly 1/2. Every next-symbol conditional probability is at least the emission floor 1/8. A length-j history has mass at least (1/2)(1/8)^(j-1), so there is no uniform positive lower bound on long-history mass; the predictor below does not enumerate or estimate those histories.

These margins are part of the theorem, not hidden constants. The transition/emission floors, their smallest singular values, mixing contraction, pair-Hankel gap, and observation alphabet are all bounded by fixed constants. The state cap is 2 and the predictive rank is exactly 2 throughout this family.

4. RAW-STREAM ESTIMATOR

Use the two observable moments

    r_1 = E[Y_t Y_(t+1)] = m^2 lambda,
    r_2 = E[Y_t Y_(t+2)] = m^2 lambda^2.

From the one raw path, form

    rhat_1 = (T-1)^(-1) sum_(t=1)^(T-1) Y_t Y_(t+1),
    rhat_2 = (T-2)^(-1) sum_(t=1)^(T-2) Y_t Y_(t+2).

Clip rhat_1 to [1/8,27/64] and rhat_2 to [1/16,81/256], the ranges containing all valid population moments. Call the clipped values s_1,s_2. Set

    lambdahat = clip_[1/2,3/4](s_2/s_1),
    mhat      = clip_[1/2,3/4](s_1/sqrt(s_2)).

These are defined for every path because the clipped denominators are positive. At population moments they equal lambda and m exactly. The learner returns the HMM with parameters (lambdahat,mhat) and predicts after h by its ordinary Bayesian filter.

5. WHY THE RAW MOMENTS HAVE VARIANCE O(1/T)

Let D_t=S_t S_(t+1) be the hidden transition sign, and E_t=Y_t S_t the emission sign. The symmetric transition law implies that D_t are iid, independent of uniform S_1, with E[D_t]=lambda. The emission channel implies that E_t are iid, independent of the whole hidden chain, with E[E_t]=m. Thus

    Y_t = S_t E_t,
    W_t^(k) := Y_t Y_(t+k) = E_t E_(t+k) product_(j=t)^(t+k-1) D_j,
    E[W_t^(k)] = m^2 lambda^k.

This identity cancels the initial hidden sign S_1. The statistic W_t^(k) depends only on the emission coins with indices {t,t+k} and the transition increments with indices {t,...,t+k-1}. If d>k, the underlying E- and D-index sets for W_t^(k) and W_(t+d)^(k) are disjoint. Those variables are therefore independent. For 1<=d<=k their covariance has absolute value at most 1 because both variables lie in {−1,+1}. Consequently, for n_k=T-k,

    Var(rhat_k) <= (1+2k)/(T-k).

In particular, for T>=4,

    E[(rhat_1-r_1)^2 + (rhat_2-r_2)^2]
      <= 3/(T-1) + 5/(T-2)
      <= 14/T.                                                   (1)

This is the only dependence argument used. It is an exact finite-range dependence proof for the lag products in this symmetric two-state model. A generic mixing HMM does not make its raw observation blocks finite-dependent.

6. PARAMETER ERROR FROM THE TWO MOMENTS

On the rectangle

    r_1 in [1/8,27/64],   r_2 in [1/16,81/256],

the inverse formulas lambda=r_2/r_1 and m=r_1/sqrt(r_2) have coordinate derivative bounds

    |d lambda/d r_1| <= 81/4,    |d lambda/d r_2| <= 8,
    |d m/d r_1|      <= 4,       |d m/d r_2|      <= 27/2.

Clipping a scalar estimate to an interval containing its truth cannot increase its absolute error. Clipping the inverse estimates to [1/2,3/4] likewise cannot increase their errors. The mean-value theorem therefore gives, with S=|lambdahat-lambda|+|mhat-m|,

    S <= (97/4)(|s_1-r_1|+|s_2-r_2|)
      <= (97 sqrt(2)/4) ||(rhat_1-r_1,rhat_2-r_2)||_2.

Combining with (1),

    E[S^2] <= 65863/(4T).                                       (2)

All parameters are estimated from the raw stream. The hidden transition and emission matrices themselves are not input to the learner.

7. UNIFORM FILTER STABILITY AND ALL-HISTORY PREDICTION

For a finite observed history h, let ell(h) be the posterior hidden-state log-odds after processing h, starting from the stationary prior ell(empty)=0. For symbol y in {−1,+1}, the exact filtering recursion is

    ell(hy) = g_lambda(ell(h)) + 2 y atanh(m),
    g_lambda(z) = 2 atanh(lambda tanh(z/2)).

The next-symbol law is

    P(Y_next=+1 | h) = [1 + m lambda tanh(ell(h)/2)]/2.          (3)

These formulas also define the learner's predictor with (lambdahat,mhat). They explicitly use the same uniform initial filter state for truth and estimate; no unknown filter initialization is omitted.

For all real z and lambda<=3/4,

    |partial_z g_lambda(z)|
      = lambda(1-tanh^2(z/2))/(1-lambda^2 tanh^2(z/2)) <= lambda <= 3/4,
    |partial_lambda g_lambda(z)|
      = 2|tanh(z/2)|/(1-lambda^2 tanh^2(z/2)) <= 2/(1-lambda^2) <= 32/7.

For m<=3/4, |d(2 atanh(m))/dm|<=32/7. Applying these bounds to the two filters on the same symbol string and summing the geometric contraction gives the uniform whole-history bound

    sup_h |ell(h)-ellhat(h)|
       <= (128/7)(|lambda-lambdahat| + |m-mhat|).              (4)

The bound is independent of |h| and holds even for histories of probability as small as (1/2)(1/8)^(|h|-1).

Using (3), |tanh(u/2)-tanh(v/2)|<=|u-v|/2, and m lambda<=9/16, equations (4) and the parameter ranges imply

    sup_h TV(P_(lambda,m)(Y_next|h), P_(lambdahat,mhat)(Y_next|h))
      <= (165/56)(|lambda-lambdahat| + |m-mhat|)
      <= 3 S.                                                  (5)

For any two Bernoulli laws p,q in [1/8,7/8],

    D_KL(Ber(p)||Ber(q)) <= chi^2(Ber(p)||Ber(q))
                           = (p-q)^2/[q(1-q)]
                           <= (64/7) TV(Ber(p),Ber(q))^2.

The true and estimated filters predict probabilities in [7/32,25/32], a subset of [1/8,7/8]. Thus (5) implies the uniform one-step conditional KL bound (576/7)S^2. For an H-symbol future, apply the KL chain rule. At every future prefix the history is simply a longer string h, so the same uniform filter bound applies at every step. Hence

    sup_h D_KL(P_(lambda,m)(Y_(1:H)|h) || P_(lambdahat,mhat)(Y_(1:H)|h))
       <= (576/7) H S^2.                                      (6)

No lower bound on the probability of h is used in (4)-(6).

8. UPPER BOUNDS AND COST

For every T>=4, (2), (5), and Cauchy-Schwarz give

    R_T^TV <= 3 sqrt(65863/(4T)) < 385/sqrt(T),
    R_T^KL <= 1,354,896/T,
    R_(T,H)^future <= 1,354,896 H/T.                            (7)

These are worst-case over the stated parameter box and worst-case over every finite history. The large constants are explicit conservative derivative bounds, not optimized constants.

Acquisition uses one stationary raw stream, T observed symbols, and no reset beyond the stream's initial stationary start. It reads the stream once, updates two integer sums in O(T) time, and stores O(log T) bits of counters plus two parameter values. The returned model stores two numbers. Computing a prediction after a history of length j takes O(j) scalar filtering operations and O(1) filter memory. The class/model description is fixed and supplied; there is no hidden-state or observation-word dictionary to acquire.

The moment sums and clips are rational operations on integers of O(log T) bits. lambdahat is rational before output rounding; mhat uses one square root of a positive rational. A b-bit fixed-point output can be computed by integer bisection with time polynomial in log T+b. Rounding each parameter to absolute error at most eta adds at most 2 eta to S in (5); for TV target epsilon choose eta<=epsilon/12. For the H-step KL target kappa, (6) permits eta<=sqrt(7 kappa/(4608 H)) after reserving the statistical portion of the error budget. Filter arithmetic can be rounded with per-update error eta_f; contraction 3/4 makes its accumulated log-odds error at most 4 eta_f, which can be charged through (3) and reserved in the same output budget. Thus b=O(log(1/epsilon)) for TV or O(log(H/kappa)) for KL suffices, with the stated constants allocated by reducing eta and eta_f by fixed factors. These arithmetic statements concern this explicit scalar estimator, not general spectral matrix routines.

Matched baseline: an estimator retaining the complete raw path can compute these same two moments and reproduce this predictor exactly. More generally the lower bound below applies to every function of the entire raw path, so no full-data likelihood or adaptive postprocessing can improve the minimax order on this family. The two-moment learner is a sufficient-statistic compression for this specified risk; no statistical advantage over the strongest equally informed raw-data baseline is claimed.

9. RAW-PATH MINIMAX LOWER BOUNDS

Fix m=5/8 and lambda_0=5/8. For T>=15 set

    Delta = sqrt(7/[32(T-1)]),   lambda_1=lambda_0+Delta.

Then lambda_1<=3/4. Under either model the initial hidden distribution is the same uniform distribution and the emissions are the same. For one hidden transition, the stay/flip probabilities under lambda_0 and lambda_1 differ by Delta/2, and the alternative probabilities lie in [1/8,7/8]. Therefore

    D_KL(P_lambda0(S_(t+1)|S_t) || P_lambda1(S_(t+1)|S_t))
       <= chi^2 <= (Delta^2/4)(1/(1/8)+1/(7/8))
       = (16/7)Delta^2.

The Markov chain rule gives KL at most (T-1)(16/7)Delta^2=1/2 for the complete hidden path. The conditional emission law is identical, so the same is the joint hidden-and-observed path KL; by data processing it upper-bounds the KL of the observed raw training stream. Pinsker yields total variation between the two raw-data laws at most 1/2. This is a lower bound against the full training path, not a lower bound for a summary statistic.

At the one-symbol history h=(+1),

    P_lambda(Y_2=+1 | Y_1=+1) = [1 + m^2 lambda]/2,

so the two target forecasts have TV separation

    d = m^2 Delta/2 = 25 Delta/128.

Given any estimator's forecast at h, choose the nearer of these two candidate Bernoulli laws. If that test is wrong, its TV prediction error is at least d/2. Le Cam's inequality and raw-data TV<=1/2 make the average testing error at least 1/4. Therefore

    inf_q sup_(lambda,m) E[sup_h TV(P(Y_next|h),q(.|h))]
       >= d/8
       = (25/1024) sqrt(7/[32(T-1)]).                         (8)

By Pinsker, the one-step KL loss is at least twice squared TV. Jensen's inequality applied to the same two-model Bayes risk gives

    inf_q sup_(lambda,m) E[sup_h D_KL(P(Y_next|h)||q(.|h))]
       >= d^2/32
       = 4375/[16777216(T-1)].                                 (9)

For every H>=1, the KL divergence between the H-symbol future distributions is at least that of their first symbol by data processing, so (9) is also a lower bound for the H-step future KL risk. For fixed H, (7)-(9) establish minimax orders Theta(T^(-1/2)) in worst-history one-step TV and Theta(T^(-1)) in worst-history one-step/fixed-horizon future KL. The H-dependent upper is linear in H; no matching dependence on growing H is claimed.

The lower varies the transition parameter while holding the emission parameter fixed. Since the upper estimates both parameters and the risk supremum ranges over both, this is a lower bound for the full unknown-transition/unknown-emission class. The learner receives the same class and margin information in the upper and lower comparisons.

10. SOURCE CONTEXT, COUNTEREXAMPLES, AND UNKNOWN

Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang establish efficient raw-observation spectral HMM learning under quantitative singular-value separation; Gassiat, Cleynen, and Robin establish population identifiability under full-rank transitions and linearly independent emissions. Those papers provide relevant positive context, not premises for the direct two-moment proof above. This result is not presented as novel or externally validated.

Failure gates are explicit:

* If lambda approaches 0, r_2/r_1 becomes poorly conditioned and the chosen lag-moment inversion no longer has the stated constants.
* If m approaches 0, the two-state observable rank collapses and the Hankel gap vanishes; the theorem does not give uniform rank acquisition there.
* If lambda approaches 1, the log-odds contraction constant approaches 1 and both filter-stability and raw-moment effective sample costs worsen.
* For an arbitrary finite-state HMM, observation blocks generally have infinite-range dependence and this finite-dependence variance proof does not apply. A generic mixing argument must charge its own covariance/tail constants.
* General unknown-HMM prediction after all histories without known filter contraction, and rank-free risk-certified truncation when Hankel gaps vanish, remain UNKNOWN.
