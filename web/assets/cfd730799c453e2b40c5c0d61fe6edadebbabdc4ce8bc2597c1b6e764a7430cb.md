# Dependent concentration: explicit inhomogeneous Markov bound and novelty rejection

## Verdict

The plausible theorem is correct and useful as a finite-horizon lemma, but it is not a defensible new concentration result. Inhomogeneous Markov-chain concentration from martingale/mixing coefficients is already explicit in Kontorovich–Ramanan (2008), while Paulin (2015) gives Markov-chain bounded-difference, variance, and Bernstein bounds, including nonstationary extensions of the homogeneous-chain results. The exact conditional-variance refinement below is a direct Doob-martingale plus Freedman specialization. Keep it as a model-specific tool; reject it as a standalone novelty claim.

The load-bearing distinction is that a scalar contraction profile controls the *range* of a Doob increment and gives a simple sub-Gaussian bound, but a predictable-variance Freedman bound can avoid the extra factor (1/(1-\rho)) that range-only Azuma pays on slowly mixing chains. The variance refinement requires supplied or acquired transition kernels (or certified conditional-variance bounds). Pairwise decorrelation alone cannot replace this interface.

## Model interface and theorem

Let `X_1,...,X_n` be a finite-state, possibly time-inhomogeneous Markov chain. Its initial law is `mu` on `S_1`; for `t>=2`, `K_t(x,.)` is the transition from `S_{t-1}` to `S_t`. No stationarity, reversibility, or common state space is assumed. Let `f_t:S_t->R`, and set
\[
 S=\sum_{t=1}^n f_t(X_t),\qquad b_t=\operatorname{osc}(f_t).
\]
For `i<=j`, write `K_{i+1:j}=K_{i+1}...K_j`, with `K_{i+1:i}` the identity. Define the future-value functions
\[
 q_i=\sum_{j=i}^n K_{i+1:j}f_j,\qquad c_i=\operatorname{osc}(q_i).
\]
Then the Doob martingale `M_i=E[S|X_1,...,X_i]` has differences
\[
 D_1=q_1(X_1)-\mu q_1,\qquad
 D_i=q_i(X_i)-K_iq_i(X_{i-1})\quad(i\ge2),
\]
so `|D_i|<=c_i`. Put
\[
 V_n=\operatorname{Var}_{\mu}(q_1)+\sum_{i=2}^n
 \operatorname{Var}_{K_i(X_{i-1},\cdot)}(q_i),
 \quad c=\max_i c_i.
\]
Freedman's inequality gives, for (s,v>0),
\[
 \Pr\{S-\mathbb ES\ge s,\ V_n\le v\}
 \le \exp\!\left[-\frac{s^2}{2(v+cs/3)}\right].
\]
The same bound applies to the lower tail, hence a factor of two for a two-sided event. If all kernels are known, the deterministic proxy
\[
 v_* = \operatorname{Var}_{\mu}(q_1)+\sum_{i=2}^n
 \max_{x\in S_{i-1}}\operatorname{Var}_{K_i(x,\cdot)}(q_i)
\]
dominates `V_n` pathwise and yields an ordinary Bernstein-shaped tail bound. Since a variable supported on an interval of width `c_i` has variance at most `c_i^2/4`, this also recovers the bounded-difference bound by setting `v=sum_i c_i^2/4`.

The backward recursion `q_n=f_n`, `q_i=f_i+K_{i+1}q_{i+1}` computes all `q_i` and `v_*`. With sparse transition tables and all `f_i` supplied, this takes time linear in the total number of listed transition entries, plus statewise variance calculations. The theorem centers at the model expectation; it is not distribution-free inference from one observed path.

### Dobrushin profile corollary

For `t>=2`, let
\[
 \delta_t=\sup_{x,x'}\|K_t(x,\cdot)-K_t(x',\cdot)\|_{\rm TV}.
\]
The convention is `||P-Q||_TV=sup_A |P(A)-Q(A)|`, so `osc(K_t g)<=delta_t osc(g)`.
Therefore
\[
 c_i\le \bar c_i:=\sum_{j=i}^n b_j\prod_{k=i+1}^j\delta_k,
\]
where the empty product is one. This yields
\[
 \Pr\{|S-\mathbb ES|\ge s\}
 \le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2s^2}{\sum_i\bar c_i^2}\right).
\]
This is valid for arbitrary initial law and time-varying kernels. It becomes a certified bound only if the `b_i` and `delta` profile are known upper bounds; calculating each `delta` exactly from dense finite kernels can cost quadratically in the number of rows. In practice, use supplied row-contraction certificates or directly compute `c_i=osc(q_i)` when the full kernels are available.

### Proof sketch

The Markov property gives `M_i=sum_{j<i} f_j(X_j)+q_i(X_i)`. Subtracting `M_{i-1}` yields the displayed differences. Each `D_i` is a value of `q_i` minus a conditional mean of values in the same range, proving `|D_i|<=osc(q_i)`; its conditional variance is exactly the corresponding term in `V_n`. Freedman applies to this martingale. For the Dobrushin corollary, iterate the oscillation contraction through `K_{i+1},...,K_j`, use subadditivity of oscillation, and then apply the bounded-increment Azuma-Hoeffding inequality. No independence of the observations is invoked.

## Sharpness diagnostic: two-state persistent chain

Take `X_t in {-1,+1}` with stationary uniform initial law and
\[
 \Pr(X_{t+1}=X_t\mid X_t)=\frac{1+\rho}{2},\quad 0\le\rho<1,
 \qquad f_t(x)=x.
\]
Here `delta_t=rho`, `g_i=sum_{j=i}^n rho^{j-i}=(1-rho^{n-i+1})/(1-rho)`, and `q_i(x)=g_i x`. The range-only profile has width `2g_i`; for `n >> (1-rho)^-1`, its squared-width sum is approximately `4n/(1-rho)^2`, so Azuma loses one factor `1/(1-rho)` compared with the true variance scale. In contrast,
`D_1=g_1 X_1` and `D_i=g_i(X_i-rho X_{i-1})` for `i>=2`,
and the exact predictable-variance proxy is
\[
 v_*=g_1^2+(1-\rho^2)\sum_{i=2}^n g_i^2
 \asymp \frac{n}{1-\rho}
 \quad(n\gg(1-\rho)^{-1}),
\]
which equals `Var(S)` in this stationary example. The scale is necessary because
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(S)=n+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(n-k)\rho^k
 \sim n\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}.
\]
This diagnoses slack in range-only martingale bounds, not a new Markov-chain Bernstein theorem: spectral-gap Bernstein bounds for homogeneous reversible chains are established prior art.

## Obstruction: pairwise independence is insufficient

Let `U_1,...,U_m` be independent uniform signs and index `N=2^m-1` variables by nonzero `a in F_2^m`:
\[
 X_a=\prod_{j:a_j=1}U_j.
\]
Distinct characters are orthogonal, so these `N` signs are pairwise independent. If `T=sum_a X_a`, then `Var(T)=N`; yet with probability `2^-m=1/(N+1)`, all `U_j=+1` and `T=N`. Thus the endpoint tail is at least `1/(N+1)`, ruling out any universal bound of the usual variance-proxy form `P(|T-E T|>=t)<=2 exp(-t^2/(C Var(T)))` with fixed `C` based only on bounded marginals and pairwise covariance. Higher-order dependence remains despite exact pairwise independence. A usable concentration premise must control conditional laws / mixing, martingale differences, a spectral gap, or another genuinely high-order interface.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

1. Kontorovich & Ramanan, *Concentration inequalities for dependent random variables via the martingale method*, Ann. Probab. 36(6), 2126–2158 (2008), [DOI 10.1214/07-AOP384](https://doi.org/10.1214/07-AOP384); primary preprint [arXiv:math/0609835](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609835). Its abstract explicitly includes applications to **inhomogeneous Markov chains** and hidden Markov chains, with constants in terms of mixing coefficients. For a Markov chain, multi-step conditional total-variation sensitivity is bounded by the product of one-step Dobrushin coefficients; the influence sum above is the additive-observable specialization of that martingale method. This closes the proposed novelty route.
2. Paulin, *Concentration inequalities for Markov chains by Marton couplings and spectral methods*, [arXiv:1212.2015v5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.2015). The source states bounded-difference inequalities, variance bounds, and Bernstein inequalities for empirical averages; for nonreversible chains it uses the pseudo-spectral gap and includes an extension section for nonstationary initial distributions/chains. This is the stronger comparator whenever homogeneous-chain spectral information is available.
3. Freedman, *On tail probabilities for martingales*, Ann. Probab. 3(1), 100–118 (1975), [DOI 10.1214/aop/1176996452](https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176996452), supplies the conditional-variance tail inequality; combining it with this Doob decomposition is standard martingale technology.
4. Kontorovich, *Obtaining Measure Concentration from Markov Contraction*, [arXiv:0711.0987v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.0987), is additional primary background on extracting concentration from Markov contraction.

The precise time-varying `q_i,v_*` formulas are recorded as a self-contained derivation and computational interface. I did not find grounds to claim the dynamic-program specialization itself is new; direct reconstruction of the cited full theorem text was not completed in this bounded pass. Novelty is therefore **rejected / not supported**, not claimed absent from all literature.

## Practical interface, costs, and next gate

For a chemical/event-chain application, the theorem applies to discrete reaction-event index when the embedded transition kernels and observables are specified. It does not automatically bound physical-time integrals: state-dependent holding times require a separate compensator or continuous-time argument. A useful deployment would need an explicit finite or certified truncated state space, transition-kernel acquisition, observable bounds, and a declared loss under truncation. In a black-box single trajectory with unknown transitions, the displayed expectation and variance proxy are not free inputs; one must add estimation error and account for burn-in/model-fitting costs.

The only worthwhile continuation is application-driven: identify a concrete kinetic model whose supplied structure gives computable statewise conditional variances far below the global oscillation proxy, then compare this recursion with Paulin’s spectral Bernstein bound and the best equal-information baseline. If the full kernel is too large to acquire, or the resulting `v_*` does not improve the best baseline, stop. No implementation or simulation was needed to settle the novelty question, so none was run.
