EXACT THEOREM AND EXPLICIT CONSTANTS

Let rho_N be any permutation-invariant state of N qubits. Its Schur-Weyl
decomposition is

 rho_N=direct_sum_j p_j rho_j tensor I_(m_j)/m_j,
 sum_j p_j=1, Tr rho_j=1,

with arbitrary density matrices and coherences within each spin-j irrep.
Put M=2j, X=M/sqrt(N). Assume, uniformly in N and for every real t>=0,

 E_p exp(t X) <= A exp(B t^2+C t),
 A>=1, B>=0, C>=0 fixed and finite.                      (H)

Let tau=rho_1=(I+b.sigma)/2, rho_r be any r-site marginal, q=r/N, and use
the centered Petz weighted chi-square functional

 chi_P^2=Tr[rho_r^2(tau^tensorr)^(-1)]-1
         =Tr[(rho_r-tau^tensorr)^2(tau^tensorr)^(-1)].

Define the following constants depending only on A,B,C:

 L=log A+B+C,
 D=B+9/4,
 E=C+L,
 K_0=A exp(E^2/(4D)),
 H_0=16D exp(2),
 c_0=min{1/16, 1/(4H_0)},
 N_0=max{1,ceil(4L^2)},
 K_chi=9K_0^2 H_0^2.

For every N>=N_0 and 1<=r<=c_0 N,

 chi_P^2(rho_r||rho_1^tensorr) <= K_chi (r/N)^2.          (1)

At r=1 the functional is exactly zero. Thus (1) holds eventually and
uniformly for every sublinear sequence r_N=o(N). The one-body state is
strictly positive in this range. Natural-log relative entropy also obeys

 D_rel(rho_r||rho_1^tensorr) <= K_chi(r/N)^2.              (2)

These constants are conservative rather than optimized. No assumption on
the exact mean Casimir, initial sector weights, one-body isotropy, or
dynamical reachability is used.

PROOF

1. Control the one-body state.

Since ||<J>_rhoj||<=j and b=2<J>/N,

 |b|<=E M/N=E X/sqrt(N).

Jensen's inequality and (H) at t=1 give E X<=log E exp(X)<=L. Therefore

 |b|<=L/sqrt(N)<=1/2 for N>=N_0.                        (3)

In particular tau has eigenvalues at least 1/4. The bound is valid even
when the within-sector states are pure and maximally polarized.

2. Exact centered Gram expansion.

Write A_a=sigma_a-b_a I, a in {x,y,z}. Under the operator inner product
<U,V>_tau=Tr(tau U^dagger V), I is orthogonal to every A_a. The centered
Gram matrix is

 G_ab=delta_ab-b_a b_b+i sum_c epsilon_abc b_c.

Its eigenvalues are 1-|b|, 1+|b|, 1-|b|^2, so
lambda_min(G)>=1-|b|>=1/2. Products with different site supports are
orthogonal under tau^tensorr, and the Gram matrix on a size-k support is
G^tensork.

Let d_(a_1...a_k)=Tr[rho_N A_(a_1)^(1)...A_(a_k)^(k)] and
T_k=sum_(a_1,...,a_k) d_(a_1...a_k)^2. Permutation invariance makes d_k a
real symmetric tensor. Put delta=rho_r-tau^tensorr and
R=delta(tau^tensorr)^(-1). Then

 <R,R>_(tau^tensorr)=chi_P^2,
 <A_S,R>_(tau^tensorr)=Tr(A_S^dagger delta).

The constant coefficient and all degree-one coefficients vanish exactly.
Gram-matrix Parseval therefore gives

 chi_P^2
 =sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k) d_k^dagger (G^(-1))^tensork d_k
 <=sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k) 2^k T_k.                   (4)

This identity handles noncommuting rho_r and tau^tensorr. The Gram inverse
is charged separately at every Pauli degree; no full-r condition-number
factor is inserted.

3. Exact generating polynomial on a spin sector.

For a real unit vector u, put Q_i=u.sigma^(i), beta=u.b, and

 B_k^cent(u)=binomial(N,k)^(-1)
             sum_(|S|=k) product_(i in S)(Q_i-beta I).

The Q_i commute and have eigenvalues +/-1. With S=sum_i Q_i=2J_u, the
eigenvalue corresponding to magnetization h is exactly

 P_k(h,beta)=binomial(N,k)^(-1) [z^k] F_(N,h,beta)(z),

 F_(N,h,beta)(z)
 =[1+(1-beta)z]^((N+h)/2)
  [1-(1+beta)z]^((N-h)/2).                              (5)

On spin j, h ranges over {-M,-M+2,...,M}. The two exponents in (5) are
nonnegative integers, since this spin grid has the N-qubit parity. Thus
F is an actual degree-N polynomial, not an approximate generating function.
Within-sector coherences do not affect its operator-norm bound.

4. Bound its coefficient from the full tail hypothesis.

For complex y with |y|<=1/2,

 log|1+y|<=Re y+|y|^2.

This follows from the power series for log(1+y): the absolute value of its
terms of order >=2 is at most |y|^2/[2(1-|y|)]<=|y|^2.
Take |z|=R<=1/4. Since |beta|<=1/2, every logarithm in (5) lies in this
range. Its linear sum is (h-N beta)z, while the quadratic sum is bounded
by N(1+|beta|)^2 R^2<=9NR^2/4. Consequently

 max_(|z|=R) |F_(N,h,beta)(z)|
 <=exp[(M+N|beta|)R+9NR^2/4]                            (6)

for every |h|<=M.

The coefficient bound is elementary Fourier orthogonality on the circle:

 [z^k]F=R^(-k)(1/(2pi)) integral_0^(2pi)
                       F(R exp(i theta)) exp(-ik theta) dtheta.

Hence its absolute value is at most R^(-k) times (6). Apply this uniformly
on each spin irrep and average with p_j. Using (3) and (H) with t=R sqrt(N),
the diagonal centered tensor polynomial
f_k(u)=sum d_(a_1...a_k) u_(a_1)...u_(a_k)
       =Tr(rho_N B_k^cent(u)) obeys

 |f_k(u)|
 <=[A/(binomial(N,k)R^k)]
    exp[DNR^2+E sqrt(N)R].                             (7)

The inequality uses |Tr(rho_j B_j)|<=||B_j|| and is valid for arbitrary
rho_j. It does not replace those states with classical or coherent states.

For 1<=k<=r<=N/16 choose R=sqrt[k/(2DN)]. Since D>=9/4,
R^2<=1/72<1/16, so the earlier radius condition is satisfied. Also

 DNR^2=k/2,
 E sqrt(N)R=E sqrt[k/(2D)]<=k/2+E^2/(4D).

The last step is the inequality (sqrt(k)-E/sqrt(2D))^2>=0. Thus

 |f_k(u)| <=K_0 exp(k) (2DN/k)^(k/2) k!/(N)_k,          (8)

where (N)_k=N(N-1)...(N-k+1). This is the decisive replacement for the
white-sector Hilbert-Schmidt identity.

5. Recover the tensor and sum the centered degrees.

For uniform u on S^2, spherical moments give

 E_u f_k(u)^2 >= [k!/(2k+1)!!] T_k.                     (9)

For clarity, the 2k-th sphere moment is the sum of Kronecker-delta pairings
divided by (2k+1)!!. The k! fully cross pairings of the two symmetric tensors
give their squared Frobenius norm. Every other pairing gives a nonnegative
squared Frobenius norm of an iterated tensor trace. The moment formula
itself follows by expanding exp(|v|^2/2), the Gaussian moment generating
function, and dividing by E|g|^(2k)=(2k+1)!! for g in R^3.

Combine (8)-(9) with (4). Use

 (2k+1)!!/k! <=(2k+1)2^k,
 binomial(r,k)<=r^k/k!,
 (N)_k>=(15N/16)^k for k<=r<=N/16,
 k!/k^k<=1.

Each degree-k summand is at most

 K_0^2 (2k+1)
 [8D exp(2)(16/15)^2 (r/N)]^k [k!/k^k]
 <=K_0^2(2k+1)(H_0 q)^k.                              (10)

All factors in (10) are explicit: one factor 2^k is the centered Gram
inverse and another is the sphere-to-tensor factor. There is no missing
factorial or dependence on the marginal Hilbert-space dimension.

If q<=c_0, x=H_0q<=1/4. Extending a positive series to infinity gives

 chi_P^2<=K_0^2 sum_(k=2)^infinity (2k+1)x^k
         =K_0^2 x^2(5-3x)/(1-x)^2
         <=9K_0^2 H_0^2 q^2.

This proves (1). Relative entropy satisfies
D_rel(rho||sigma)<=log Tr(rho^2 sigma^(-1))=log(1+chi_P^2)
for positive sigma: diagonalize rho and sigma and apply Jensen's logarithm
inequality to probabilities lambda_i |<i|j>|^2. This proves (2), including
the noncommuting case.

EXPONENTIAL-SQUARE ASSUMPTIONS SUFFICE

If for some fixed eta>0 and K>=1,

 E exp(eta M^2/N)<=K                                   (11)

uniformly in N, then tX<=eta X^2+t^2/(4eta) gives
E exp(tX)<=K exp[t^2/(4eta)]. Thus the theorem applies with
A=K, B=1/(4eta), C=0, giving explicit constants through the formulas above.

Conversely (H) implies a uniform positive exponential-square moment.
If B>0, Chernoff optimization gives
P(X>=x)<=A exp[-(x-C)^2/(4B)] for x>=C. Since
(x-C)^2>=x^2/2-C^2 and A>=1, the bound

 P(X>=x)<=A exp[C^2/(4B)] exp[-x^2/(8B)]

is valid for all x>=0 (also where its right side exceeds one). At
eta=1/(16B), integration of this tail gives

 E exp(eta X^2)<=1+A exp[C^2/(4B)].                     (12)

If B=0, letting t tend to infinity in the Chernoff bound gives X<=C almost
surely, so every exponential-square moment is uniformly bounded by
exp(eta C^2). Therefore the stated mgf assumption and some uniform positive
exponential-square moment are qualitatively equivalent, up to constants.

FINITE MOMENTS, EVEN ALL FIXED ORDERS, DO NOT SUFFICE

Here is a counterexample that can additionally retain the exact mean
Casimir <J^2>=3N/4, so failure is not caused by abandoning that physical
constraint. Choose rare weight epsilon_N<=1/(4N) on the fully polarized
state |up>^tensorN, whose spin is j_max=N/2. For the remaining weight use
an SU(2)-invariant mixture of the two adjacent allowed spin sectors whose
j(j+1) values bracket

 K_N=[3N/4-epsilon_N N(N+2)/4]/(1-epsilon_N).

Select the convex weights to give this value exactly, and use the maximally
mixed irrep and multiplicity state inside each baseline sector. Denote this
baseline state by omega_N. Then

 rho_N=(1-epsilon_N)omega_N+epsilon_N |up><up|^tensorN.   (13)

It is permutation-invariant, has the exact mean Casimir, and has one-body
state tau_N=(I+epsilon_N Z)/2. The construction is legal: epsilon_N<=1/(4N)
keeps K_N between the smallest and largest allowed Casimir values; for
odd N the lower condition is equivalent to epsilon_N<=3/(N+3), which is
weaker. Moreover K_N<=N, so a bracketing baseline sector satisfies
2j/sqrt(N)<=4. This bounded baseline spin contributes only a constant to
every fixed normalized-spin moment.

The rare sector has X=sqrt(N). Every marginal satisfies
rho_r>=epsilon_N |up><up|^tensorr. On |up>^tensorr, the product reference
has eigenvalue [(1+epsilon_N)/2]^r. Consequently

 chi_P^2(rho_r||tau_N^tensorr)
 >=epsilon_N^2 [2/(1+epsilon_N)]^r-1.                  (14)

One may check (14) directly in the reference eigenbasis:
Tr(rho_r^2 sigma_r^(-1))>=<up|rho_r^2|up>/sigma_up
>=<up|rho_r|up>^2/sigma_up. Thus no commutativity assumption is needed
for the lower bound.

For any prescribed finite order s_0, choose p>=max{1,s_0/2} and
epsilon_N=(1/4)N^(-p). Then for every 0<s<=s_0,

 E X^s<=4^s+(1/4)N^(s/2-p)<=4^s+1/4.

Nevertheless r_N=ceil[(2p+2)log_2 N]=o(N) makes (14) at least
(1+o(1))N^2/16-1. A finite set of polynomial moment assumptions is
therefore insufficient, even with the exact mean Casimir.

More strongly, choose
epsilon_N=min{exp(-sqrt(N)),1/(4N)}.
For every fixed integer s>=1,

 E X^s<=4^s+sup_(x>=1) exp(-x)x^s
       <=4^s+(s/exp(1))^s,

uniformly in N. Even E exp(X)<=exp(4)+1 is uniformly bounded. But
r_N=ceil[(3/log 2)sqrt(N)]=o(N) makes (14) grow at least as
(1+o(1))exp(sqrt(N))-1. Thus bounded moments of every fixed order, without
a growth restriction across orders, and one ordinary exponential moment
still fail to guarantee the centered local O((r/N)^2) bound.

SEPARATING STRETCHED-EXPONENTIAL TAILS

For every fixed 0<alpha<2 and eta>0, use the same construction (13) with

 epsilon_N=min{exp[-2eta N^(alpha/2)],1/(4N)}.

It obeys the uniform weaker stretched-exponential assumption

 E exp(eta X^alpha)<=exp(eta 4^alpha)+1.

Yet r_N=ceil[(5eta/log 2)N^(alpha/2)]=o(N), and (14) grows as
at least (1+o(1))exp[eta N^(alpha/2)]-1. Thus positive exponential-square
control is substantively stronger than every fixed stretched-exponential
control of order below two for this uniform, all-sublinear guarantee.
This is a sufficient-condition and separation statement, not a claim that
the particular mgf constants or finite-range constants are necessary.
