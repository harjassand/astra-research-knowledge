FACTORIAL FOURIER TENSORS FOR HIDDEN TWO-STATE RENEWALS
2026-10-08 | /root/sol_idempotent_conflict | no descendants

Outcome: INTERNALLY-DERIVED REPRESENTATION OBSTRUCTIONS, not a generic
approximation algorithm or an approximation-hardness theorem.

Every transition-color factorial tensor has an exact nonnegative Walsh
representation by permutation-cycle XOR constraints. Its value at any local
configuration is computable in polynomial bit time, even at unbounded arity.
Nevertheless, at multiplicity three it is not windable, it violates planar
matchgate identities in every terminal order, and it has no nonnegative
ferromagnetic binary-spin gadget on its individual Fourier terminals. Even
after separately encoding each four-pattern parity space into two Boolean
bits, every one of the 576 encoding pairs fails log-supermodularity. Positive
zero pinnings transfer the individual-terminal obstructions to all higher
multiplicities.

These statements close specific local gadget routes. They do not exclude a
global aggregation, another basis, an FPRAS for the legal word networks, or
an optimal hidden-renewal predictor. In fact the multiplicity-two tensor is
also nonwindable, while the separate sol_bond_renewal packet constructs a
restricted FPRAS by collapsing equality wires globally. The row-factorial
denominator additionally has a negative Walsh entry already at two observed
gaps. Thus local Fourier nonnegativity alone does not supply a sign-free
representation of the full evidence.

The mathematical derivations below are internal. Primary sources are used
only for windability/matching-circuit closure and necessary matchgate
identities. No external validation or novelty/priority claim is made. Root
has received the main formulas and witnesses, but this packet has not yet
received an independent full proof audit. Exact finite checks are diagnostic
evidence; the analytic proofs below carry the all-arity claims.

1. Exact likelihood interface and transform conventions
------------------------------------------------------
Use the full-span hidden q=2 row-urn evidence already defined in
outputs/research/sol_hidden_renewal/RESULT.txt and
outputs/research/sol_bond_renewal/RESULT.txt. An explicit duration word
w=(g_1,...,g_m) of positive integers gives a binary block of length
N=1+sum_t g_t with events at positions 1,1+g_1,...,N. The binary block is
explicit, so m<=N-1; no succinct exponentially encoded horizon is assumed.

For hidden event states u_0,...,u_m in {0,1}, put

 c_{i,d,g}=#{t:(u_{t-1},u_t,g_t)=(i,d,g)},
 r_i=#{t:u_{t-1}=i},
 a(u)=product_{i,d,g} c_{i,d,g}!,
 W(w)=sum_u a(u),
 Z(w)=sum_u a(u)/(r_0! r_1!).

The proper projected code has Q_N^bin(x(w))=Z(w)/(2K_N), with the separate
allzero mode included in K_N. Nothing below removes the row denominator.

For a gap with n occurrences, its ordered transition-pair colors are
y_t=(u_{a_t-1},u_{a_t}) in A=(Z_2)^2. Its local factor is

 f_n(y_1,...,y_n)=product_{c in A} n_c!,
 n_c=#{t:y_t=c}.

Let chi_z(y)=(-1)^(z dot y), with dot product modulo two. The orthonormal
Walsh matrix on one Boolean port is T_ab=2^(-1/2)(-1)^(ab). On one ordered
pair the kernel is (1/2)chi_z(y). Therefore the transformed n-pair tensor is

 Fhat_n(z_1,...,z_n)=2^(-n) sum_{y in A^n}
                  f_n(y) product_t chi_{z_t}(y_t).                 (1)

Although the individual Boolean transform uses sqrt(2), the local tensor in
(1) is rational. The canonical 2n Boolean terminals are the n incoming
Fourier bits followed by the n outgoing Fourier bits. A different ordering
of those terminals will be considered explicitly in the matchgate test.

2. Nonnegative all-arity permutation-cycle formula
-------------------------------------------------
For each fixed color word y, product_c n_c! counts precisely the permutations
pi of {1,...,n} that preserve its colors. A permutation preserves all colors
if and only if each of its cycles is monochromatic. Thus

 f_n(y)=sum_{pi in S_n} product_{cycles C of pi}
                         1[y_t are all equal for t in C].          (2)

For a cycle C of length k, transforming its equality factor yields

 2^(-k) sum_{y in A} product_{t in C} chi_{z_t}(y)
       =2^(2-k) 1[XOR_{t in C} z_t=0].                            (3)

The constraint in (3) means that BOTH the incoming bits and the outgoing
bits have even XOR on that cycle. Multiplying (3) over cycles and summing (2)
gives the exact identity

 Fhat_n(z)=2^(-n) sum_{pi in S_n} 4^(number of cycles of pi)
                      product_C 1[XOR_{t in C} z_t=0].             (4)

In particular every entry is nonnegative. The numerator is an integer at
most n!4^n, so entries have O(n log(n+1)) bits, with denominator 2^n.
This bound is not an efficient n!-term algorithm; Section 8 supplies a
polynomial-bit local evaluator.

The support is exactly

 Even_n(incoming bits) x Even_n(outgoing bits).                    (5)

Necessity follows by XORing all cycle constraints. Sufficiency follows
because one n-cycle then meets the XOR constraint, for n>=1. The empty
tensor Fhat_0 is 1.

Zero-pair recurrence. If the last Fourier pair is z_n=0, any permutation on
n-1 points extends in exactly two kinds of ways: make n a new singleton
cycle, contributing an extra factor 4; or insert n after one of the n-1
old points, without changing the number of cycles. Insertion of the zero
color changes none of the XOR constraints. Including the transform scale,

 Fhat_n(z_1,...,z_{n-1},0)=(n+3)/2 * Fhat_{n-1}(z_1,...,z_{n-1}).   (6)

Iterating gives, for n>=3,

 Fhat_n(z_1,z_2,z_3,0,...,0)
     =(n+3)!/[6! 2^(n-3)] * Fhat_3(z_1,z_2,z_3),                  (7)

and Fhat_n(0,...,0)=(n+3)!/[3!2^n]. All constants are positive rational.
Consequently any gadget class closed under zero pinning and positive scalar
multiplication would have to realize Fhat_3 in order to realize Fhat_n for
any n>=3 on the same individual Boolean terminal interface.

3. The exact multiplicity-three tensor
-------------------------------------
For n=3, the identity permutation has three cycles, the three transpositions
have two cycles, and the two 3-cycles have one cycle. Formula (4) becomes

 Fhat_3(z_1,z_2,z_3)
  =8*1[z_1=z_2=z_3=0]
   +2*sum_i 1[z_i=0 and the other two z's are equal]
   +1[z_1 XOR z_2 XOR z_3=0].                                   (8)

Hence the allzero entry is 15. A configuration with one zero pair and two
equal nonzero pairs has value 3. The three distinct nonzero pair colors give
value 1. Other entries are zero.

On each triplet of incoming or outgoing bits, order its four even patterns
as 000,011,101,110. The six-terminal tensor, restricted to its support, is
the following four-state pair matrix:

             outgoing
             000 011 101 110
 incoming 000 15   3   3   3
          011  3   3   1   1
          101  3   1   3   1
          110  3   1   1   3.                                  (9)

Write this matrix as M_3. The distinguished color 0 has row and column
(15,3,3,3); each other color i has entries 3 at colors 0 and i and entries
1 at the other two nonzero colors. These values are used below without
rounding or numerical tests.

4. Ferromagnetic binary-spin gadget obstructions
-----------------------------------------------
A nonnegative Boolean function H is log-supermodular (LSM) if

 H(x AND y) H(x OR y) >= H(x) H(y)                               (10)

for all x,y. Every nonnegative binary ferromagnetic interaction is LSM;
unary weights and fixed-bit pins are LSM. Products and finite marginalization
preserve LSM. Thus summing any number of auxiliary spins from a product of
such interactions and unaries can only produce an LSM terminal function.
This claim allows arbitrary graph topology and nonuniform fields.

For completeness, marginalization closure can be proved one summed bit at
a time. For a,b on the remaining bits, put c=a AND b,d=a OR b. Same-last-bit
products are bounded directly by LSM. For the cross terms put

 A=H(c,0)H(d,1), B=H(c,1)H(d,0),
 p=H(a,0)H(b,1), q=H(a,1)H(b,0).

LSM gives B<=A, p<=A, q<=A, and pq<=AB. These imply p+q<=A+B: if p<=B it
is immediate; for B<=p<=A, use q<=AB/p and the convex function p+AB/p,
whose endpoint values are A+B. The case of zero A or p follows directly.
Adding the two same-bit bounds proves (10) for the marginal.

4a. Individual Fourier terminals.
The support Even_3 x Even_3 is not closed under meet and join. For example,
incoming patterns 110 and 011, with outgoing pattern 000, both have value 3;
their meet 010 and join 111 have value 0. Therefore Fhat_3 is not LSM.

Any independent complement of Boolean terminals changes each triplet's
support to an even- or odd-parity coset. Neither parity coset on three bits
is a Boolean sublattice: the even case has the displayed witness; for the
odd case the patterns 100 and 010 have meet 000 outside the coset. Positive
terminal unary scaling preserves support. Thus bit gauges and such scalings
cannot give a positive ferromagnetic-spin gadget on these six terminals.

4b. Every two-bit encoding of the four parity patterns.
One might first eliminate the parity support, representing each of its four
allowed incoming patterns by two Boolean bits, and likewise for outgoing
patterns. This avoids the support objection but still fails. For EVERY pair
of bijections from the four incoming/outgoing colors to {00,01,10,11}, the
resulting four-bit function from (9) has a face with diagonal-product ratio
at most 1/3. Here is an all-encoding proof.

Case 1: zero is encoded at a middle corner 01 or 10 on either side. Fix the
other side to the nonzero color encoded at the opposite middle corner on
the varying side. On this varying two-bit face, both middle entries are 3
and both extreme entries are 1. Thus diagonal products are 1<9.

Case 2: both zero colors are at extreme corners, but at opposite extremes.
The two nonzero colors adjacent to zero on each side are two-element
subsets of the same three-color set, so they have a common color h. Vary
along the row edge zero--h and the column edge zero--h. Because the zeros
are opposite extremes, these edges have opposite Boolean orientations.
The diagonal products are 9<45.

Case 3: both zero colors are at the same extreme. Simultaneously complement
all four bits if needed, so zero is 00 on both sides. Let k be the incoming
color encoded at 11. Of the two outgoing middle-corner colors, at least one
h differs from k. Its incoming encoding is therefore a middle corner.
Vary the incoming edge h--k and the outgoing edge zero--h. In increasing
Boolean orientation the face is

                column zero    column h
 row h                  3             3
 row k                  3             1,

so its diagonal products are 3<9.

The cases exhaust all 24*24 encodings. Therefore there is no exact positive
binary ferromagnetic auxiliary-spin gadget for M_3 in ANY such encoding.
The checker also stores a negative face for each encoding separately in
ENCODING_CERTIFICATES.json.

The strict ratio gives a robust obstruction to uniformly relative gadgets:
if a function agrees with every positive M_3 entry to relative error at most
epsilon, its negative face still violates LSM whenever

 epsilon < (sqrt(3)-1)/(sqrt(3)+1).                              (11)

Indeed its smaller product is at most (1+epsilon)^2 times the original,
whereas the larger product is at least (1-epsilon)^2 times the original.
This is a local approximation statement, not an AP-hardness reduction.

5. Nonwindability and nonnegative matching-circuit obstruction
------------------------------------------------------------
Use McQuillan, Approximating Holant problems by winding, arXiv:1301.2880v1,
Definition 2 (PDF p4). A nonnegative Boolean function F is windable if for
each x,y its product F(x)F(y) splits into nonnegative B(x,y,M), over
partitions M of the disagreement coordinates into pairs and singletons,
and each B is unchanged when x and y are simultaneously flipped on one
block of M. Even-windability restricts the partitions to pairs.

Let the six terminals be (s_0,s_1,s_2,e_0,e_1,e_2), and take

 x=000000, y=110110,
 disagreement set D={s_0,s_1,e_0,e_1}.

By (9), Fhat_3(x)Fhat_3(y)=15*3=45. In any proposed windability decomposition,
a partition containing a singleton must have B=0: flipping that singleton
makes at least one parity triplet odd, so both transformed entries are
zero; nonnegative decomposition and flip invariance force B=0 at x,y.
Likewise any pair crossing from s to e changes both triplet parities, so
has a zero flipped product and forces that partition's B to be zero.

The ONLY partition left is

 M_*={{s_0,s_1},{e_0,e_1}}.                                      (12)

It must therefore carry all the product 45. Simultaneously flipping the
start pair produces x'=110000,y'=000110. Each has value 3, so their product
is 9. The same zero-parity argument makes (12) the only possible partition
at x',y', and its B must be 9. Flip invariance requires this very B to equal
45, a contradiction. Thus Fhat_3 is neither windable nor even-windable.

This witness survives all bit gauges and positive independent terminal
unary weights. Gauges merely relabel x,y. Unary scaling multiplies each of
the four products in the argument by the same positive factor, because
simultaneous disagreement flips preserve the multiset of bits at each
terminal. A uniformly relative approximation preserving the zero entries
also cannot be windable for

 epsilon < (sqrt(5)-1)/(sqrt(5)+1),                              (13)

because 45(1-epsilon)^2>9(1+epsilon)^2.

Imported implication. McQuillan's Lemma 16 (PDF p17) closes windability under
circuits of windable functions. Section 7 (PDF p25) defines matching circuits
with nonnegative rational edge weights and nonnegative vertex fugacities;
no planarity assumption is imposed. Lemma 28 (PDF p28, proof p29) says every
matching-circuit weight function is windable. Hence Fhat_3 has no such
matching circuit, in particular no nonnegative weighted perfect-matching
gadget, on its canonical individual terminals. More generally it is not
expressible by any circuit entirely of windable functions. Formula (7)
extends these obstructions to all Fhat_n with n>=3.

Caution about Ising Fourier networks. Nonnegative parity-weight functions
F(x)=a for even parity and b for odd parity are windable for a,b>=0. One
can fix any pairing of the disagreement coordinates, with one singleton
if their number is odd, and put all F(x)F(y) mass on that partition. Pair
flips preserve both parities; when there is a singleton the two parities
are opposite and a simultaneous flip preserves the product ab. Together
with nonnegative unaries and diagonal edge weights these cover the standard
nonnegative high-temperature Ising primitives. Lemma 16 therefore also
excludes a local realization of Fhat_3 wholly within that interface. This
does not exclude an Ising representation after a global change of variables
or grouping of terminals.

6. Signed or complex planar matchgate obstruction
------------------------------------------------
For a planar matchgate signature F with F(empty)!=0, the matchgate identities
give, for ordered terminals i<j<k<l,

 F(empty)F(ijkl)
     =F(ij)F(kl)-F(ik)F(jl)+F(il)F(jk).                         (14)

This necessary identity follows from Cai--Gorenstein, Matchgates Revisited,
Theory of Computing 10(7),2014, Theorem 2.1, equation (2.6), journal p172 /
PDF p6. Their definition permits weights over any field, so the identity is
not restricted to nonnegative matching weights.

Take two start terminals and the corresponding two end terminals in Fhat_3.
The allzero entry is 15, the four-terminal entry is 3, each within-start or
within-end two-terminal entry is 3, and every cross start/end two-terminal
entry is zero. Thus the left side of (14) has magnitude 45, while the right
has a single possibly signed nonzero product of magnitude 9. No circular
terminal ordering repairs this discrepancy. The checker explicitly examines
all 6!=720 orders, with residuals 36 or 54.

Consequently Fhat_3 is not a planar matchgate signature even with signed or
complex weights. Positive terminal scalings preserve the identity, since
each term gains the same terminal factor. Bit gauges preserve the full set
of identities by replacing their alpha,beta with alpha XOR gauge,beta XOR
gauge. Zero pinning preserves planar matchgate realizability, so (7) again
extends the obstruction to all n>=3 on individual terminals. Arbitrary
invertible non-diagonal basis transformations have NOT been excluded here.

7. A decisive multiplicity-two control and the limits of these obstructions
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
For n=2, (4) is

 Fhat_2(z_1,z_2)=4*1[z_1=z_2=0]+1[z_1=z_2].                     (15)

It enforces equality of the two incoming Fourier bits and equality of the
two outgoing Fourier bits. On those two equality-wire variables its matrix
is [[5,1],[1,1]], which is ferromagnetic. In a complete word network these
deterministic wires can be collapsed first, and the resulting physical-spin
network is the basis of the restricted sol_bond_renewal FPRAS.

But as a four-individual-terminal signature, Fhat_2 itself is nonwindable:
with x=0000,y=1111, only the partition pairing the two starts and the two
ends can survive; its product changes from 5*1 to 1*1 after the start-pair
flip. It also fails the corresponding four-point matchgate identity.
Therefore nonwindability or direct matchgate failure by itself is NOT an
approximation obstruction. The additional multiplicity-three conclusion
is that its parity-three wires cannot be collapsed by the same deterministic
pair-equality procedure, and that every independent two-bit encoding of the
remaining incoming/outgoing pattern spaces fails the ferromagnetic gadget
criterion. Larger groupings, different bases, or word-specific global
identities remain possible.

Local signatures are not arbitrary legal-word instances. A word imposes
chronological endpoint identifications. None of the proofs above embeds a
general Holant instance into a legal duration word, nor supplies an AP
reduction. In particular they give no NP=RP or #BIS consequence for hidden
renewal prediction. The exact projected-code #P-hardness already banked in
sol_hidden_renewal remains at its previously stated scope and does not imply
approximation hardness.

8. Exact polynomial-bit local tensor evaluation at all arities
------------------------------------------------------------
The permutation sum in (4) has n! terms, but a four-variable rational
generating function computes any specified entry efficiently.

Let E_c,c in A, be independent unit-rate exponential random variables, and
put L_z=(1/2)sum_c chi_z(c)E_c. The exponential moment identity
E[E_c^k]=k! gives directly from (1)

 Fhat_n(z_1,...,z_n)=E[product_t L_{z_t}].                        (16)

This expectation is used only for deriving an exact formal generating
function, not as a randomized estimator. Let d_z=#{t:z_t=z}, sum_z d_z=n,
and introduce four formal variables X_z. Then

 H(X)=product_{c in A}(1-sum_{z in A} chi_z(c)X_z)^(-1),
 Fhat_n=2^(-n)(product_z d_z!) [X^d] H(X).                       (17)

Let D=1/H. D is the product of four degree-one integer polynomials, has
constant term 1 and degree at most 4, and has at most binom(8,4)=70 possible
monomials. Writing h_alpha=[X^alpha]H gives

 h_0=1,
 h_alpha=-sum_{0<beta<=alpha} D_beta h_{alpha-beta}.              (18)

Compute (18) throughout the box 0<=alpha_z<=d_z in increasing degree or
lexicographic order. It has at most (n+1)^4 states and a constant number of
predecessors per state. Taking absolute coefficients in (17) bounds them by
the coefficients of (1-sum_z X_z)^(-4), hence, for |alpha|=j<=n,

 |h_alpha|<=binom(j+3,3) * j!/(product_z alpha_z!)
           <=binom(n+3,3)4^n.                                  (19)

Intermediate sums in (18) add only a constant factor to this bit bound.
Thus h values have O(n) bits, and the final factorial multiplication and
2^n denominator have O(n log(n+1)) bits. This yields polynomial worst-case
bit time and space for every local entry of every arity. It does not provide
a polynomial contraction algorithm for the complete word network.

9. The full row-weighted evidence still has a negative Fourier factor
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The unweighted W network uses the tensors above. For Z, the row denominator
can be represented by the global start-state factor

 h_m(v_1,...,v_m)=1/[r_0!r_1!]=binom(m,r_0)/m!,
 r_0=#{t:v_t=0}.                                               (20)

For m=2 its truth table at 00,01,10,11 is (1/2,1,1,1/2). Applying the
orthonormal two-bit Walsh transform gives

 hhat_2=(3/2,0,0,-1/2).                                        (21)

Thus transforming the row-weighted network at the same basis does not leave
all local entries nonnegative. If degree-three copy tensors are introduced
to attach the row factor, the transform of a copy tensor is a positive
multiple of Even_3, so the explicit negative row entry cannot be blamed on
an avoidable negative copy convention. Other ways to handle the denominator
remain UNKNOWN; (21) does not prove an intrinsic sign problem for all bases.

This issue is separate from the canonical row-weighted bond FKG failure
already proved in sol_bond_renewal. Its exponential-variance counterexample
also remains banked: on a single gap repeated m=2k times the SAME unweighted
permutation proposal has exponentially small mean correction and an
exponential lower bound on its relative second moment. Hence extending that
proposal without a new control is not justified. That single-label instance
also has a polynomial exact DP, so neither the variance failure nor (21)
is a counting impossibility.
