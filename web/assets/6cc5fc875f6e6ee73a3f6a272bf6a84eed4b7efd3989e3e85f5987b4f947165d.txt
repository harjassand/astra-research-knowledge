1. FINITE-INFORMATION MAPPING WITHOUT INFINITY MINUS INFINITY

For a normal extension tau_ABC of kappa, assume
 I=I(A:B)_tau<infinity, delta_B=I_e(A:C|B)_tau<infinity.
By S3 on S1,
 J=I(A:BC)_tau=I+delta_B<infinity.                       (1)
Data processing under Tr_B gives I(A:C)_tau<=J<infinity.
Applying the same S1 formula with B,C interchanged gives
 delta_C=I_e(A:B|C)_tau=J-I(A:C)_tau.                  (2)
Thus delta_C is automatically finite in this particular regime.

Take sigma=tau_A tensor kappa_BC and N=id_A tensor Tr_C. Then
 D(tau||sigma)=J,
 D(Ntau||Nsigma)=I.
Both are finite, and their loss is precisely delta_B. For the other
partial trace the loss is delta_C. No marginal entropy must be finite;
no formal subtraction of two infinite relative entropies is used.

Apply S2 with reference kappa_BC and partial trace once to select R_B,
and once with B,C interchanged to select R_C. In the application with
sigma=tau_A tensor kappa, stabilization removes tau_A from the LOCAL
recovery map. If tau_A is singular, restrict A to supp(tau_A), where it
is faithful; tau is supported there. Hence, for every such tau,
 F_root(tau,(id_A tensor R_B)tau_AB)>=exp(-delta_B/2),
 F_root(tau,(id_A tensor R_C)tau_AC)>=exp(-delta_C/2).  (3)
The selections R_B,R_C depend only on kappa, not A, tau_A, or either
deficit. The same common averaging density is fixed before tau is chosen.

Fuchs--van de Graaf gives the HALF trace errors
