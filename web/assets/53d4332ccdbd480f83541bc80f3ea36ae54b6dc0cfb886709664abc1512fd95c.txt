FINITE-ALPHABET LOWER BOUND FOR THE COLLECTIVE LIE-GIBBS ARCHIVE
2026-10-08 | /root/memory_information

STATUS AND SCOPE

This is an internal parameter-identification lower bound for a wholly
classical archive. It does not prove optimality of the polynomial
Schur/coherent cubature alphabet, nor a lower bound depending on the carrier
scale. It applies to the full unknown-vector family, and separately to a
fixed regular orbit. The proof uses the family trace-error guarantee and
charges the finite message alphabet; it makes no apparatus or acquisition
claim.

Let G be fixed compact connected simple, g=dim(G), and use an orthonormal
real basis X_1,...,X_g of its Lie algebra for the invariant metric in the
archive theorem. Let H_Lambda be a nontrivial irreducible carrier with
Lambda=sum_i L_i omega_i and carrier scale L=sum_i L_i. Write

  A_a = T_(X_a)^(Lambda)/L,
  rho_theta = exp(sum_a theta_a A_a)/Z_L(theta),
  theta in B(0,h), h>0,

and suppose the input consists of n identical copies of rho_theta. A legal
finite classical archive has at most M messages and produces a fixed
decoded state sigma_y for each message y. Assume

  sup_(||theta||<=h) (1/2)||rho_theta^tensor n - C(rho_theta^tensor n)||_1
       <= epsilon_(n,L),   epsilon_(n,L)->0 as L->infinity.

The candidate archive has epsilon_(n,L)<=B_G h/L. If the message uses B
fixed-length bits, M<=2^B. The same lower applies to independent public
randomness, by conditioning on that seed.

MAIN LOWER

For any sequence n->infinity, L->infinity and epsilon_(n,L)->0, every such
archive for the full vector ball obeys

  log_2 M >= (g/2-o(1)) log_2 n.                         (1)

The o(1) is in the coefficient as n->infinity, uniformly over nonzero
highest weights of carrier scale L. Thus the unknown length and direction
jointly force at least g/2 leading classical bits per log_2 n. No lower
bound in log L follows from this argument.

For an experiment restricted to one nonzero regular coadjoint orbit, replace
g in (1) by dim(G/T)=g-rank(G)=2|R^+|. More generally the coefficient is
one half the dimension of the chosen smooth orbit. A singular orbit uses its
own smaller dimension. At theta=0 the orbit is a point, so that orbit-only
statement correctly gives no lower bound; the full-ball statement still
holds because it uses an interior neighborhood and the Fisher metric is
nondegenerate at zero.

PROOF

1. Uniform local identifiability, including theta=0.

There is a group constant C_A with ||A_u||_infinity<=C_A||u|| for every
carrier and u in the Lie algebra. Indeed sum_a T_(X_a)^2=C_2(Lambda)I and
C_2(Lambda)<=C_G L^2. Define F_L(theta)=log Z_L(theta) and

  mu(theta)=grad F_L(theta)
        =(Tr rho_theta A_1,...,Tr rho_theta A_g).

For every u, the Hessian is the Kubo-Mori variance

  u^T Hess F_L(theta) u
    = int_0^1 Tr[rho_theta^s (A_u-mu_u I)
                         rho_theta^(1-s)(A_u-mu_u I)] ds,
  A_u=sum_a u_a A_a.

On ||theta||<=h, ||sum theta_a A_a||<=C_A h. If d=dim H_Lambda, all
eigenvalues of rho_theta lie between exp(-2C_A h)/d and
exp(2C_A h)/d. In a rho_theta eigenbasis each coefficient
rho_i^s rho_j^(1-s) is therefore at least exp(-2C_A h)/d. Since every
Lie generator is traceless,

  u^T Hess F_L(theta)u
   >= exp(-2C_A h) Tr(A_u^2)/d
   = exp(-2C_A h) C_2(Lambda)/(g L^2) ||u||^2
   >= kappa_G ||u||^2,                                  (2)

where kappa_G>0 is independent of L and Lambda. For the last inequality,
C_2(Lambda)=(Lambda,Lambda+2rho)>=||Lambda||^2 and

  min_{x_i>=0, sum x_i=1} ||sum_i x_i omega_i|| > 0,

because the fundamental weights are linearly independent and the simplex is
compact. Consequently mu is uniformly strongly monotone on the ball:

  ||mu(theta)-mu(theta')|| >= kappa_G ||theta-theta'||. (3)

This argument does not divide by ||theta||. It includes theta=0, where
mu(0)=0 but its derivative is the positive invariant-trace covariance.

2. A uniformly successful classifier from n physical copies.

Choose a fixed cube [-a,a]^g inside B(0,h). For n sufficiently large set

  A_n = sqrt(C log log n),   delta_n=A_n/sqrt(n),

with C a large group-dependent constant. Take a delta_n-separated lattice
packing {theta_j}_{j=1}^K in that cube. Volumetric counting gives

  log K >= (g/2)log n - g log A_n - O_(g,h)(1)
        = (g/2-o(1))log n.                              (4)

On each input copy, measure A_a projectively on a disjoint subset of at
least floor(n/g) copies for coordinate a. Outcomes are in [-C_A,C_A],
independent within each subset, with mean mu_a(theta). Let mu_hat be the
vector of empirical means, and classify by the nearest mu(theta_j). From
(3), any two packed mean vectors are separated by at least
kappa_G delta_n. Hoeffding and a union bound show uniformly in j

  P_theta_j{wrong class} <= 2g exp(-c_G n delta_n^2)
                      <= 2g (log n)^(-c_G C),           (5)

where c_G>0 depends only on the fixed group, h, and the uniform operator
bound. Choose C so the right side tends to zero. The coordinate-wise
projective measurements together are one legal POVM on the n-copy state.

Apply exactly this same POVM and classifier to the decoded output. A
measurement changes distributions in total variation by at most the input
half trace distance, so its class error is at most

  p_e <= 2g(log n)^(-c_G C) + epsilon_(n,L) = o(1).     (6)

This step only uses the family reconstruction guarantee. The decoder may
output an entangled state across all n sites; the trace-distance bound still
controls the chosen collective POVM.

3. Finite-alphabet cost.

Let J be uniform on the K packed parameters, Y the archive message, and
J_hat the above decoder-output classifier. The channel gives the Markov
chain J -> Y -> J_hat (condition on any independent public seed). Fano's
inequality and data processing imply

  log M >= I(J;Y) >= I(J;J_hat)
       >= (1-p_e)log K - h_2(p_e).

Together with (4)--(6), this proves (1). If the archive has a variable-length
prefix-free message, the same argument lower-bounds its expected length by
H(Y), hence by the displayed Fano quantity.

For a fixed smooth orbit of dimension s, take a compact coordinate patch
inside the orbit and pack that patch at spacing delta_n. The ambient mean
map lower bound (3) and a bi-Lipschitz chart on the patch give (4) with s
in place of g. A regular orbit has s=g-rank(G); at a singular orbit, replace
s by its actual dimension.

INFORMATION-CAPACITY UPPER AND WHY IT DOES NOT FORCE log L

The same model has an N-independent upper on how much information the
unknown vector itself can carry. For any prior on a compact g-dimensional
parameter set, a Euclidean 1/sqrt(n)-net has K<=C_(G,h)n^(g/2) points. The
one-copy relative entropy is the Bregman divergence of F_L. The Hessian of
F_L is uniformly upper-bounded by C_A^2 I, so

  D(rho_theta^tensor n || rho_theta_j^tensor n)
       <= (C_A^2/2)n||theta-theta_j||^2 <= C'_G,h

for a nearest net point. Put sigma=K^(-1) sum_j rho_theta_j^tensor n.
Since sigma>=rho_theta_j^tensor n/K, operator monotonicity of log gives

  D(rho_theta^tensor n || sigma) <= C'_G,h + log K.

The Holevo identity
int D(rho_theta^tensor n||sigma)d pi
 = chi(pi)+D(rho_bar||sigma)
therefore yields

  chi({pi(theta),rho_theta^tensor n})
       <= (g/2)log n + O_(G,h)(1),                       (7)

uniformly in the carrier scale L. For a smooth fixed orbit, use a net on
that orbit and replace g by its dimension s. Thus the identifiable
parameter information is Theta(log n) on a full-dimensional vector ball,
not Theta(log(nL)).

Equation (7) is not an upper bound on the number of archive messages needed
to reconstruct the full quantum state: labels can carry information about
the input beyond the chosen parameter statistic, and the archive is judged
in whole-state trace norm. It does show that no argument based only on
estimating theta, Fisher information, Holevo information, or packing
distinguishable parameter values can force an additional log L term.

There is also a direct error-resolution obstruction. For nearby parameters,
the uniform one-copy Fisher upper bound gives

  T(rho_theta^tensor n,rho_(theta+v)^tensor n)
       <= C_G sqrt(n)||v||.

At ||v||=c epsilon/sqrt(n), this distance is O(epsilon), which is no larger
than the reconstruction tolerance scale. The two target trace balls of
radius epsilon can overlap (their midpoint is a common output whenever the
pairwise distance is <=2epsilon). Hence a packing at the nominal
epsilon/sqrt(n) state-metric resolution does not force distinct archive
messages. Raising the target precision from a constant to 1/L does not
upgrade the robust parameter-packing lower beyond log n.

WHAT THIS SETTLES AND WHAT REMAINS OPEN

Established here, conditional only on the stated fixed-group representation
normalization: for a full unknown vector ball and any wholly classical
finite archive whose full-family trace error tends to zero, the retained
alphabet has at least n^(g/2-o(1)) messages. The lower is sharp as a
parameter-identification bound: both the direct testing construction and
the Holevo upper have leading information coefficient g/2. It remains
UNKNOWN whether the whole-state reconstruction problem requires any
additional factor growing with L, whether the O_G(log(nL)) archive bits are
leading-optimal, or whether an O_G(log n) finite archive exists at error
O(1/L). Resolving that gap needs a convex-geometric/channel-complexity
argument not reducible to identifiable-parameter packing.

This is for one shared unknown theta on n iid copies of one known carrier.
It is not a result for n independent unknown parameters, unrestricted
bounded-Holevo families, hybrid quantum archives, or a continuous label
alphabet. No numerical test is needed for the analytic argument; no source
or external referee review was performed.
