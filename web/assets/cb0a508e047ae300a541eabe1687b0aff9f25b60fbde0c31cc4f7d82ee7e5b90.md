# Lineage-aware regeneration and safety: model audit

Date: 2026-10-08 (Australia/Brisbane)

## Question and verdict

Can a controlled cell-state graph or per-cell Markov transition matrix certify clonal regeneration/reachability or bound a lineage-level unsafe-fate probability? In general, no. It omits the joint offspring law at a division. Two binary-fate branching models can have the same per-daughter state probabilities and the same expected progenitor count, yet materially different regenerative-clone survival and unsafe-fate risk. This is an exact model counterexample, not a biological claim and not a novelty claim for branching-process theory.

## Model and proof

There are two cell states: a dividing progenitor `S` and terminal state `F`. Each `S` cell divides once into exactly two daughters. Each daughter is `S` with marginal probability `p` and `F` otherwise; `F` has no daughters. Start from one `S` cell. Compare two offspring laws:

1. **Common-fate sisters:** with probability `p`, both daughters are `S`; with probability `1-p`, both are `F`.
2. **Independent sisters:** each daughter independently is `S` with probability `p` and `F` with probability `1-p`.

For either daughter, both models have the same one-step fate kernel `S -> p S + (1-p) F`; they also have the same mean number `2p` of `S` daughters. The generating functions for the number `N` of `S` offspring are

`G_c(z)=1-p+p z^2` and `G_i(z)=(1-p+p z)^2`.

For a Galton–Watson process the extinction probability is the smallest fixed point `q=G(q)` in `[0,1]`. When `p>1/2`, the nontrivial roots give

`q_c=(1-p)/p`, while `q_i=((1-p)/p)^2`.

At `p=3/5`, both models have mean `E[N]=6/5`, but `q_c=2/3` and `q_i=4/9`; survival probabilities are `1/3` and `5/9`, respectively. Even at the first division, `Pr(N>0)` is `3/5` under common-fate sisters and `1-(2/5)^2=21/25` under independent sisters. The independent model also has `Pr(N=1)=2p(1-p)=12/25`, while the common-fate model assigns probability zero to exactly one `S` daughter.

If `F` is instead designated an unsafe fate, its marginal probability per daughter remains `1-p=2/5` in both models, but the probability that at least one daughter is unsafe is `2/5` under common-fate sisters and `1-p^2=16/25` under independent sisters. Thus the same graph and edge marginals do not identify a two-daughter safety probability either.

This is a counterexample to sufficiency of the per-daughter transition matrix and first-moment offspring matrix for clonal extinction/reachability and family-level safety. The relevant object is the offspring probability-generating function (or the full joint daughter-state distribution), with the usual branching-process assumptions stated explicitly. It does not refute lineage-resolved models that already estimate joint offspring laws, nor does it establish actual tissue survival under finite niche capacity, spatial interaction, cell-cell signaling, or density-dependent division.

A robust but conservative finite-horizon corollary needs no sibling independence: if each of at most `H` at-risk mother divisions produces at most `b` daughters and every daughter has conditional unsafe-fate probability at most `epsilon`, then the probability of any unsafe daughter is at most `min(1,H b epsilon)` by the union bound. For one two-daughter division with common marginal unsafe probability `epsilon`, the sharp range over all sister couplings is `[epsilon, min(1,2 epsilon)]`. The bound is only as good as the measured conditional marginal, the deterministic cap `H`, the daughter cap `b`, and state classification; it does not solve hidden clone heterogeneity or assay censoring.

## Biological interface and quantitative falsifier

A useful benchmark is the existing human cerebral-organoid regeneration assay in Lindenhofer et al. (2024): barcode tracing found a broad lineage-size distribution, the model used symmetric and asymmetric progenitor behavior, and a puromycin perturbation depleted wild-type cells while a resistant subpopulation could replenish tissue. About 10% resistant input produced near-complete rescue in their experiment. The paper reports that lineage output changed with neighboring tissue depletion. Its measurements and model establish a plausible assay, not validation of the two-law counterexample or a controller guarantee.

**Testable comparison.** In the same organoid system, compare vehicle against day-11 puromycin with 10% and 20% barcoded puromycin-resistant cells, matching the published design. Record each progenitor division with a live state reporter or post-division paired-daughter profiling, and retain the barcode for each daughter. Classify both daughters jointly as proliferative progenitor, neurogenic/differentiated, death, or other prespecified fate. Existing markers in the cited study include SOX2 for radial glia/progenitors, EOMES for intermediate progenitors, and NEUROD2 for neurons. Follow parallel lineages to the published recovery window and measure barcode-resolved clone sizes, total organoid cell count, and progenitor/neuron composition. Include untreated mixed organoids as the matched environment control and biological replicates across independent differentiations. A destructive single-cell snapshot alone does not observe sister pairs; live imaging or a validated paired-daughter assay is required.

Estimate (i) per-daughter fate marginals, (ii) the full sister-pair/offspring-count distribution conditional on parent state and treatment, and (iii) clone-level random effects. Compare held-out clonal rescue and unsafe-tail predictions from a transition-only independent-daughter model with a model that uses the measured joint offspring law. The main falsifier for the transition-only model is a held-out discrepancy in the frequency of progenitor persistence, tissue rescue, or prespecified aberrant clone expansion after its per-daughter marginals and mean growth have been calibrated. A direct test of the stark toy contrast is the mixed-sister fraction: at `p=0.6`, the independent model predicts `0.48` mixed S/F pairs per observed S division, while the common-fate model predicts zero. With five independent divisions, the independent model has probability `1-0.52^5 = 0.962` of producing at least one mixed pair. This is a design-floor calculation for distinguishing these exact models only, not a powered sample-size calculation for organoids; biological divisions within one clone/organoid are clustered, so inference must resample at clone and organoid/differentiation level.

Predeclare a safety endpoint that fits the system (for example, a maximum lineage-size or aberrant progenitor fraction at a specified time) and an acceptable risk threshold before looking at treatment outcomes. The model-level robust gate is `H b epsilon_U <= eta`, where `H` is a measured upper bound on at-risk progenitor divisions during the horizon, `b=2` in this assay, `epsilon_U` is a one-sided upper confidence bound for unsafe daughters per division, and `eta` is the tolerated finite-horizon risk. If the required `H` or state-specific `epsilon_U` cannot be established, report UNKNOWN. Organoid-size rescue is not by itself evidence of normal function, long-term safety, or in-vivo regeneration.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

- Branching-process lineage theory is established. The PNAS 2020 CLiNC paper explicitly models the **joint** distribution of daughter-cell types for each parent (`D_i`), and notes that it can represent symmetric, asymmetric, or stochastic fate outcomes. It also lists independence of each parent-cell process and mean-field interactions among its assumptions. This is direct prior art for the model interface; do not frame the need for joint offspring laws as a new discovery.
- Single-cell pedigree experiments have measured family and sister dependence. Hawkins et al. (2009) tracked stimulated primary B-cell lineages and reported that 45.1% of observed founder families had progeny die in the same division and 90.2% within adjacent rounds; sibling division times were strongly correlated. These are not measurements of the proposed neural-organoid fate variables, but they make independence an empirical question rather than a safe default.
- Whiting et al. (2025) infer resistance dynamics from barcoding and population sizes using a multitype process and show different pre-existing versus phenotype-switching routes in two colorectal cancer cell lines. It is already a strong lineage-data modelling precedent.
- The exact numerical two-offspring witness here is an elementary reconstruction from standard Galton–Watson fixed-point equations. No source, corpus, or search here establishes priority or novelty. A novelty claim would require a targeted literature search on controlled multitype branching, offspring-law identification under lineage observation, and lineage-resolved stochastic viability; this lane did not establish that.

## Costs, limits, and next gate

Costs needed to execute the protocol include barcoding/library integration and validation, perturbation batches, longitudinal imaging and lineage reconstruction, paired-daughter state classification, endpoint single-cell assays, organoid/differentiation replicates, and exact clone-level analysis. No data collection or wet-lab experiment was performed in this lane. No clinical recommendation follows.

Next gate: acquire a dataset with sister-resolved states and clone ancestry under the depletion perturbation; fit both the transition-only model and the joint-offspring model under equal-information conditions; evaluate on held-out organoids and prespecified rescue/safety endpoints. If the observed offspring joint law is empirically near independent and the transition-only model predicts held-out outcomes within a prespecified margin, the counterexample remains a warning about model contracts, not evidence that it matters in this assay. If it is strongly dependent and materially improves held-out risk predictions, pursue a controlled multitype branching viability theorem with finite horizon, density dependence, clone acquisition costs, and tissue-level safety thresholds.
