# Physical-interface gate: NV nuclear-spin fluorescence

## Verdict

The strongest named interface found is the intrinsic \(^{14}\mathrm N\) nuclear spin of a diamond NV center, repeatedly read through electron-spin-dependent fluorescence. This is a real native hidden state with repeatable photon output, no nuclear-state reset, a measurable transition process, and reversible controls that change the observed persistence. It is a better-defined testbed than unspecified biological cryo-arrest.

It does **not** pass the conditional slow-read theorem's physical gate. Primary studies report setting-specific lifetimes and readout fidelity, but do not supply a protocol-wide, one-sided transition-probability envelope over the complete control path; do not show one shared, action-invariant emission matrix across slowdown settings; and do not compare the best active likelihood with the best passive likelihood at matched walltime and detected-photon dose. The slow-read theorem must remain model-conditional; an NV application is `UNKNOWN`, not validated.

## Actual device and action

The hidden state is the host nitrogen nuclear spin \(m_I\in\{-1,0,+1\}\); the electron spin is a probe/ancilla. In the 2010 Science experiment, a nuclear-state-selective microwave operation maps the nuclear projection onto the electron, and a short optical pulse reads the electron's fluorescence. The electron is re-polarized for each read, but the nuclear state is not reset. A trace point consists of 5 ms and 2,000 repeated read cycles. At 0.65 T, the authors report 92\(\pm\)2% readout fidelity. They reconstruct a three-state transition matrix from about 10,000 jumps. This is repeated photon acquisition of one nuclear spin, not an independent-founder sample in the model theorem.

The best source-backed slowdown knob is the axial field. Near the excited-state level anticrossing (~50 mT), hyperfine flip-flops drive nuclear jumps; detuning the field suppresses them. Neumann et al. observe an approximately quadratic increase of lifetime with field detuning. Pfender et al. later measure the memory lifetime during continuous single-shot readout, report approximate quadratic field scaling and ~1 s at 3 T, and show that lifetime depends on the microwave, wait, and laser pulse timing. This is a paid hardware action: a high-field magnet, field alignment, retuned microwave control, and nonzero ramp/settle time. The field changes the measurement Hamiltonian and readout resonance conditions, so the emissions must be calibrated per setting; shared \(E\) is not source-established.

A newer room-temperature action uses the repeated-interaction measurement schedule itself. Zhang et al. (2025) report one optical read pulse of 220 ns per RIM, about 0.05 detected photon per RIM, and a 97\(\pm\)2% bright/dark readout fidelity after 80,000 RIMs in 1.695 s at a specified small-field setting. They report that shortening the excitation pulse and adding waits increases the measured nuclear-spin \(T_1\), with a maximum of 15\(\pm\)3.5 s. The 80,000-read point corresponds to about 4,000 detected photons in expectation (0.05 per RIM). The high-fidelity operating point and maximum-lifetime point are different points in the article's schedule sweep and cannot be combined. The paper explicitly finds that field angle and RIM interval alter the metastable channel and the number of visible levels. Its exact physical description is a discrete-time quantum instrument acting on the nucleus, not a common CTMC multiplier \(Q\mapsto\rho Q\) with invariant emissions.

## Why no proved perturbation bound is available from these sources

The 2010 source gives a transition matrix estimated from a long trajectory and lifetime error bars; the 2017/2025 sources give fit-derived lifetime-versus-control curves. These are source-supported empirical observations, not a finite-protocol, one-sided upper confidence envelope for every hidden state and every read block. None charges a field ramp or supplies jump counts during that ramp. The room-temperature schedule study's longest \(T_1\) is not a one-sided bound on the state-change probability during a particular 80k-read protocol. The NV electron is initialized/repolarized inside each read, which costs optical pulses and time, while the target nucleus is not reset; a per-read cost ledger must include those operations.

A defensible next experiment would predeclare one complete control waveform and a maximum read block. It would acquire state-conditioned photon distributions under each setting, use a likelihood confidence set for the latent transition instrument (including no-jump and transition alternatives), and report an upper confidence bound on block disturbance that includes both ramp/settling and hold. If calibration requires state preparation, its RF/readout resources and selection error must be charged; a hidden-state reset or free label cannot be substituted. A finite-sample theorem could then be conditional on the stated quantum/classical instrument class and the reported confidence set. The current papers do not provide the underlying raw event records and calibration protocol needed to compute that bound retrospectively.

## Matched discrimination test and strongest passive control

The source papers do not report a hidden-mechanism discrimination comparison. For a same-device follow-up, predeclare a candidate pair of nuclear-spin transition instruments and randomize across a high-persistence action and a passive reference schedule. Match both elapsed walltime and analyzed detected-photon dose (thin the higher-count stream if needed, while recording the unthinned photons and laser pulse energy/fluence separately); charge field ramps/settling, repeated electron preparation, microwave/RF control, detector dead time, and failed tracks. Estimate setting-specific photon laws instead of assuming a shared \(E\). Score held-out sequences using the exact sequential instrument likelihood when its Hamiltonian/Kraus family is supplied, and give the passive arm the same nuisance-parameter fitting budget. A classical HMM is an additional comparator only after its approximation to the quantum instrument is checked.

The prediction that is supported now is only a persistence/readout tradeoff: more axial-field detuning or lower optical duty can reduce measured nuclear-spin relaxation in the cited settings, while increasing magnet/setup time or elapsed time per detected photon. There is no source-grounded numerical likelihood-ratio or information-per-photon advantage over the optimal passive sequence likelihood. In particular, the 97%/1.695 s point and 15 s lifetime point are not a fair pair of matched outcomes.

The theorem's existing passive lower bound remains confined to independent two-read pair laws of the constructed CTMC witness. It does not apply to this quantum instrument or to an optimal photon-by-photon passive likelihood. Likewise, the active clustering test cannot beat the optimal likelihood on the same active read data; at best it supplies a constructive estimator once the action's full disturbance and resource ledger is measured.

## Full gates that remain open

- **Transition disturbance:** no ramp-inclusive, statewise, one-sided block bound at the exact selected field or pulse schedule.
- **Emission calibration:** repeated photon laws exist, but action-invariant \(E\) is not demonstrated. Re-estimate emissions separately at each setting.
- **Sample dependence:** published traces are repeated reads of one nucleus. The theorem's independent-founder transition-row bound needs either a new independent native-founder design or a dependence/mixing proof for the full trace.
- **Resources:** the sources give examples of read durations and photon counts, but not equal-resource active/passive likelihood scores, magnet ramp/settle time, or per-setting calibration/failed-track costs.
- **Model interface:** the 2025 action induces a discrete-time open quantum channel and metastability. It is not the scalar positive-rate CTMC slowdown assumed by the current theorem.

**Disposition:** retain NV \(^{14}\mathrm N\) as a serious experimental testbed and quantify its operating points, but do not claim it passes the slow-read acquisition theorem. Current physical application gate: `UNKNOWN`.
