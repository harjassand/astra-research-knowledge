## 1. Exact interface and theorem checked

Let `G` be a finite two-player one-round game with finite classical questions,
nonempty finite answers `A,B`, law `μ`, and Boolean acceptance predicate.
A strategy is a unit vector `ψ` on any Hilbert space `H` and local POVMs
whose Alice and Bob effects commute for **every** pair of local inputs and
answers. Their generated von Neumann algebras `𝒜,ℬ` commute. Set
`v=ωᶜᵒ(G)<1`, taking the supremum over all such representations and vectors.
The repeated strategy may use arbitrary joint string POVMs.

On each positive support component of the pair graph (pairs joined by a
shared local question), use the conditional-expectation projections `P_X,P_Y`
on `L²(μ_j)`, and define `P_j=(P_X+P_Y)/2`. Let `σ` be the least gap of
`I−P_j` off constants on nonsingleton components, or 1 if all components are
singletons. The proposed conclusion is, for every `k≥1`, `0<δ<1−v`,

\[
\Pr\{W_k\ge\lceil(v+\delta)k\rceil\}
\le\exp\!\left[-\frac{\sigma\delta^3k}{2048(1+\log d)}\right],
\qquad d=|A||B|. \tag{T}
\]

It is uniform over every repeated commuting strategy and passes to its
supremum without attainment. Connected support permits denominator 1024.
The exponent is conditional on the given graph gap; efficient sampling alone
does not acquire an inverse-polynomial gap. `ωᶜᵒ` cannot be replaced by a
smaller finite-tensor value without a separate theorem.

## 2. Common resolvent maps and a norm-safe Jensen limit

Write `K=L²((0,∞),ds)` and, for bounded `T≥0`, define

\[
(F_Th)(s)=T(T+sI)^{-1}h.
\]

The spectral identity `∫₀∞t²/(t+s)² ds=t`, applied by positive integration,
gives `F_T*F_T=T` on arbitrary `H`. For effects, `F_T` is a contraction.
Its matrix coefficient against any `e∈K` belongs to the original algebra
containing `T`: `‖T(T+s)⁻¹‖≤min(1,‖T‖/s)` is square integrable in `s`,
so multiplication by the scalar `e(s)` makes the coefficient integral
absolutely convergent in operator norm. No finite-dimensional spectral sum
is used.

For finite positive ensembles `B=Σ_lq_lA_l`, put `D_l=A_l−B`.
At a positive lower spectral bound the exact resolvent identity gives

\[
\sum_lq_lf(A_l)-f(B)
=\int_0^\infty s\sum_lq_l(B+s)^{-1}D_l
(A_l+s)^{-1}D_l(B+s)^{-1}\,ds,
\quad f(t)=t\log t, f(0)=0. \tag{1}
\]

The difference Gram has the same positive integrand with the middle factor
`s(A_l+s)⁻¹` replaced by `s²(A_l+s)⁻²`. Scalar functional calculus gives
the domination. It does not require different `A_l` to commute.

In infinite dimension, restricting to `supp B` does **not** make its inverse
bounded. Instead use `A_l+εI,B+εI`: their integral identities converge in
operator norm at fixed `ε>0`, near zero by the lower bound and at infinity
by `O(s⁻²)`. Positivity above 1 does not invalidate (1).
For each scalar spectral value `t≥0`,

\[
\|F_{t+\varepsilon}-F_t\|_K^2
=\varepsilon^2\int_0^\infty
\frac{s^2}{(t+\varepsilon+s)^2(t+s)^2}\,ds
\le\varepsilon.
\]

Hence `‖F_{T+εI}−F_T‖≤√ε`. Continuous functional calculus for `f` on a
fixed compact interval makes both sides converge in norm as `ε↓0`, proving

\[
\sum_lq_l(F_{A_l}-F_B)^*(F_{A_l}-F_B)
\preceq\sum_lq_lf(A_l)-f(B). \tag{2}
\]

Zero ensemble weights are omitted. Kernels and continuous spectra at zero
are covered by this single argument.

## 3. Bounded Douglas transport and von Neumann membership

Let `G_a∈𝒜` be positive and `Σ_aG_a=T∈𝒜`. Since `G_a≤T`, its form on
the range of `√T`,

`q_a(√T h,√T k)=⟨h,G_a k⟩`,

is well defined and bounded by `‖√T h‖‖√T k‖`. Extension to its closure
gives a unique positive contraction `C_a` supported on `P_T`, with

\[
G_a=\sqrt T C_a\sqrt T,\quad 0\preceq C_a\preceq P_T,
\quad\sum_aC_a=P_T. \tag{3}
\]

This avoids interpreting an unbounded inverse as an operator. Algebra
membership follows from bounded regularizations:

\[
(T+\varepsilon I)^{-1/2}G_a(T+\varepsilon I)^{-1/2}
=r_\varepsilon(T)C_ar_\varepsilon(T)\longrightarrow C_a
\quad\hbox{strongly},
\quad r_\varepsilon(T)=\sqrt{T/(T+\varepsilon I)}.
\]

Each regularization belongs to `𝒜`, is uniformly bounded, and converges
strongly because `r_ε(T)→P_T` strongly. Thus `C_a∈𝒜` by strong closure.
The limit generally fails in norm when positive spectrum accumulates at
zero; only strong convergence is claimed or needed.

The bounded polar column is

\[
(V_Th)(s)=\sqrt T(T+sI)^{-1}h,
\quad V_T^*V_T=P_T,
\quad F_T=V_T\sqrt T.
\]

Its boundedness follows from `∫₀∞t/(t+s)² ds=1` for `t>0` (and zero
on the kernel), not an operator-norm-integrable bound near `s=0`.
Equivalently `V_T=SOT-lim F_T(T+εI)⁻¹/²`, so every matrix coefficient
is in `𝒜`. For all `D∈𝒜'`,

`V_TD=(I_K⊗D)V_T`.

Therefore

\[
Q_a=V_TC_aV_T^*+\mathbf1_{a=a_*}(I-V_TV_T^*)
\in B(K)\,\bar\otimes\,\mathcal A \tag{4}
\]

by the amplified commutant criterion. The operators are positive and sum
to identity, and `F_T*Q_aF_T=G_a` exactly. `T=0` gives the default-answer
POVM. No finite-rank polar decomposition or bounded inverse is assumed.

## 4. All-input commutation and exact joint pullback

Give Alice and Bob separate copies `K_s,K_t`. Their transported algebras
on `K_s⊗K_t⊗H` are

`B(K_s)⊗I_t ⊗̄𝒜` and `I_s⊗B(K_t) ⊗̄ℬ`.

They commute: scalar ancilla actions use distinct factors and all original
matrix coefficients commute. Infinite operator matrices can be checked by
bounded finite coordinate compressions and their strong limits; no norm
summation of matrix entries is presumed. This holds across **different**
inputs and transcripts, not only for the pair whose state is being tested.

For `T,G_a∈𝒜`, `S,H_b∈ℬ`, let

\[
\Psi_{T,S}(s,t)=T(T+s)^{-1}S(S+t)^{-1}\psi.
\]

Apply `F_T`, then the lifted `F_S`. The latter intertwines Alice's
transported `Q_a`, while `F_T` intertwines every Bob operator.
Applying first Bob's pullback and then Alice's gives the operator identity

\[
\langle\Psi_{T,S},Q_aR_b\Psi_{T,S}\rangle
=\langle\psi,G_aH_b\psi\rangle,
\quad\|\Psi_{T,S}\|^2=\langle\psi,TS\psi\rangle. \tag{5}
\]

Products in (5) are positive because the factors commute. Separate marginal
identities alone would be insufficient; the intertwining proves the joint
identity. No approximation of commuting correlations by tensor correlations
occurs. The only new tensor factors here are scalar ancillas.

### Optional local projectivization does not change the comparison model

Lin's general commuting-correlation definition explicitly permits POVMs;
some internal arguments use projective strategies. The possible interface
difference can also be removed directly, without a representation theorem.
For an Alice POVM `(M_x^a)_a`, let `J=ℓ²(A)⊗H` and
`W_xh=Σ_a|a⟩⊗√M_x^a h`. Then `W_x*W_x=I`. The block map

`U_x:H⊕J→J⊕H`,
`U_x(h,k)=(W_xh+(I−W_xW_x*)k, W_x*k)`

is unitary: its first two terms are orthogonal, its total squared norm is
`‖h‖²+‖k‖²`, and its inverse is
`(y,z)↦(W_x*y,(I−W_xW_x*)y+W_xz)`.
Conjugate the coordinate-answer projections on `J` by `U_x`, assigning
the extra output `H` summand to one default answer. This yields a PVM on
the fixed input space `H⊕J`; its compression to `h⊕0` is exactly `M_x^a`.
All rectangular blocks are in the Alice amplification, since the entries
of `W_x` are `√M_x^a∈𝒜`.

Use a separate finite ancilla for Bob. Every cross-input pair of dilated
PVMs commutes by the same amplified-algebra argument, and compression of
both fixed ancilla inputs recovers `M_x^aN_y^b` exactly. Thus the commuting
POVM and PVM value suprema coincide on this finite question/answer
interface. The construction may enlarge a representation, which is allowed
by the audited strategy class. This additional gate was proposed by the
repetition checker and independently reconstructed here.

## 5. Mixed exposure, entropy and its exact weighted law

Fix tested `C`, positive acceptance probability `p`, and `m=k−|C|` live
coordinates. Put `L=log(1/p)+|C|log d` and `τ=L/m`. The mixed flag
`Ω_j=(X_C,Y_C,X_≤j,Y_>j)` fixes one question at each live coordinate.
Under the **original** product-of-pairs law, remaining Alice and Bob
strings become independent. Conditioning on answers first would be invalid.

At mode `i`, `r₀=(X_C,Y_C,X_<i,Y_>i)` is independent of fresh `(x,y)`.
For tested answer strings `α,γ`, coarse effects `F_x^α,H_y^γ` and finer
effects remain in `𝒜,ℬ`. The mask `θ` depends on `r₀,α,γ` only.
The weights `w_i(r)=Pr(r₀)θ(r)` need not sum to one. The exact law is

`Pr(r,x,y | E_C)=w_i(r)μ(x,y)‖Ψ_xy^r‖²/p`.

The squared norm supplies the tested-answer mass; normalizing only `w_i`
would alter the law. Equation (5), plus independence of unfixed strings,
proves the conditional answer identity for the transported measurements.

For positive Bob effect `H`, the functional on Alice's algebra is

`χ(A,H)=⟨√Hψ,A√Hψ⟩`.

It is normal and positive, with mass `g≤1`. Its spectral measure gives
`−χ(f(F),H)≤h log(g/h)≤−h log h`, where `h=χ(F,H)`.
Zero mass contributes zero. Finite-answer Jensen then bounds both exposure
potentials in `[-pL,0]`. This is scalar spectral entropy under a vector
functional; neither a density matrix nor a tracial state is involved.

The change from `Y_i` to `X_i` is the operator Jensen increment (2)
evaluated against `H_y`; it bounds the Alice-side squared vector error.
The reversed Bob potential controls the other error. Each telescope costs
at most `pL`. With `w_ir=w_i(r)/m`, common vectors `f_ir(z)`, local
anchors `a_ir(x),b_ir(y)`, and the transported winning effect `W_ir(z)`,

\[
\sum_{ir,z}w_{ir}\mu(z)\|f_{ir}(z)\|^2=p,
\quad\sum_{ir,z}w_{ir}\mu(z)
(\|f-a\|^2+\|f-b\|^2)\le2p\tau,
\]
\[
\sum_{ir,z}w_{ir}\mu(z)\langle f_{ir}(z),W_{ir}(z)f_{ir}(z)\rangle
=p\bar s. \tag{6}
\]

`W` is an effect: its terms are a subset of commuting positive POVM
products summing to identity. All questions and flags at fixed `k` are finite;
the common codomain may be infinite, which the commuting model permits.

## 6. Graph barycenters, normalization and disconnected drift

On a component, conditional expectation is orthogonal projection in
`L²(μ_j)`. The fixed space of `(P_X+P_Y)/2` is the intersection of the
local-function spaces, hence constants by connectedness. The scalar
Poincaré inequality applies to arbitrary Hilbert-valued `f`: there are only
finitely many values, so their vector span is finite. This scalar proof
does not compress the measurements or turn the strategy finite-dimensional.
Least-squares optimality gives

`σ_j Var_j(f)≤(E_j‖f−a(x)‖²+E_j‖f−b(y)‖²)/2`.

Summing (6) gives `E=Σ_jλ_jE_j≤pτ/σ`. Define barycenters
`β_ir,j=E_{μ_j}f_ir(z)`, original component norm mass `P_j`, and
barycenter norm mass `P̂_j`. Orthogonal decomposition gives exactly
`P̂_j=P_j−E_j≤P_j`.

For `P̂_j>0`, the normalized vector

`P̂_j⁻¹/² Σ_ir√w_ir |ir⟩⊗β_ir,j`

on a finite shared flag register times the common Hilbert space is a legal
commuting resource. Both players' POVMs are diagonal in the same register.
Distinct blocks multiply to zero; within a block all cross-party inputs
commute by Section 4. The state is independent of fresh questions. Its
success is `T̂_j/P̂_j≤v_j=ωᶜᵒ(G_j)`. If `P̂_j=0`, its accepting mass
also vanishes, so no division is made.

The effect comparison and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz give

`T_j≤v_jP_j+2√(P_jE_j)`.

Each local positive-mass input determines the component. Taking finite
tensor products of near-optimal **shared commuting representations** of
the components proves `Σ_jλ_jv_j≤v`. This tensor product combines different
representations; it never factors Alice and Bob inside any original one.

The conditional component weights are exactly
`λ̃_j=λ_jP_j/p=m⁻¹Σ_iPr(J_i=j | E_C)` by (6).
Their joint conditioned likelihood ratio is at most `1/p` relative to
the unconditioned product law `λ^⊗m`. Relative entropy to a product
dominates the sum of marginal entropies. Pinsker and Cauchy–Schwarz yield

`TV(λ̃,λ)≤√(log(1/p)/(2m))`.

This term cannot be omitted for disconnected supports. Since `0≤v_j≤1`,
the final estimate is

\[
\bar s\le v+\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/p)}{2m}}
+2\sqrt{\tau/\sigma}. \tag{7}
\]

## 7. Scalar transfer, short lengths and endpoint

## 8. Adversarial stress cases and failed stronger interpretations

- **Accumulating zero spectrum:** `T/(T+ε)` need not converge to `P_T`
  in norm. A proof demanding bounded `T⁻¹/²` or norm convergence of the
  transport would fail. Sections 2–3 use the correct norm/SOT boundaries.
- **Genuinely non-type-I algebra:** on the standard space of `L(F₂)`, let
  free group unitaries be `u,v`, take `T=(2+u+u*)/4` and
  `C=(2+v+v*)/4`, and define `G₁=√T C√T`, `G₂=T−G₁`.
  `T` has continuous spectrum reaching zero, and `C` does not commute
  with `T`. Let Bob's effects act in the commuting right algebra.
  These facts have direct checks: `u` acts as a sum of bilateral shifts,
  so the zero set of `(2+u+u*)/4` has spectral measure zero although zero
  lies in its spectrum. Distinct reduced group words make `[T,C]≠0`.
  Central square-summable coefficients are constant on conjugacy classes;
  every nonidentity conjugacy class in `F₂` is infinite, forcing the center
  to be scalar. The finite canonical trace and the infinitely many
  orthogonal powers `uⁿ` make this an infinite-dimensional finite factor,
  hence not type I.
  The bounded-form construction recovers exactly `C` (support `P_T=I`)
  and its complement; every transported Alice coefficient stays in the
  amplified left algebra. This is an analytical stress case, not a finite
  approximation or a computational certificate for the factor.
- **Postselected easy components:** treating conditioned component weights
  as `λ` would fail; (7) pays their actual drift.
- **Fixed representation or preparation:** the barycenter and scalar
  ancillas may leave a prescribed factor/representation class. The proof
  permits all Hilbert representations as `ωᶜᵒ` does. It proves no finite
  tensor approximation, bounded-memory synthesis, or economical state
  preparation.
- **Efficient sampler versus graph gap:** let `X` be uniform on `q` symbols.
  Independently reveal it to Bob with probability `p=1−1/q`, retaining an
  independent reveal tag; otherwise Bob sees one common erasure symbol.
  This law has a sampler using `O(log q)` random bits and an `O(log q)`
  description, but maximal local correlation is `√p`. Conditional projection
  on the centered span of `f(X)` and `1_reveal f(X)/√p` has eigenvalue
  `(1+√p)/2`. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the matching upper correlation bound,
  so `σ=(1−√(1−1/q))/2≈1/(4q)`. Thus efficient specification does not
  promise an inverse-polynomial unanchored gap in description length.
