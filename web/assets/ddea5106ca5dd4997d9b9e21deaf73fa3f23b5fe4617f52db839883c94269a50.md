# Reversible detailed-balance escape witness

## Exact interface

Use four internal species `A,B,C,D`, a fixed chemostat activity `a_Q=1`, and the four reversible pairs

1. `2C + Q ⇄ A + B + C`;
2. `A + B + C + 2Q ⇄ 3A + 2B`;
3. `3A + 2B ⇄ 2C + 3Q`;
4. `2C + Q ⇄ 2C + D`.

Treat every species as carrying one unit of a conserved elemental moiety. Each lifted reaction is then element-balanced. The chemostatted `Q` amounts are incorporated into effective internal rate constants; at `a_Q=1`, all effective, dimensionless directional constants are one. The model is a well-mixed stochastic mass-action system at the chosen reference volume, with propensity `λ_{y→y'}(x)=∏ᵢ(xᵢ)_{yᵢ}` and falling factorials, and the corresponding deterministic mass-action ODE. No volume-asymptotic claim is made.

After clamping `Q`, the internal reaction vectors are

`ν₁=(1,1,−1,0)`, `ν₂=(2,1,−1,0)`, `ν₃=(−3,−2,2,0)`, `ν₄=(0,0,0,1)`.

They have rank three, with left nullspace spanned by `(0,1,1,0)`. Hence `B+C` is the sole linear conservation law and the positive class `P={(a,b,c,d)>0:b+c=2}` contains `x*=(1,1,1,1)`. The internal reaction-complex graph has four complexes, one linkage class, and rank three, so its deficiency is zero. Each reversible edge pair balances at `x*`; the system is detailed-balanced (therefore complex-balanced).

Clamping `Q` at activity one gives the open internal subsystem analyzed below. If `Q` is instead made dynamic, the enlarged closed network conserves `A+B+C+D+Q`, but its propensities depend on the changing `Q` count; that different CTMC is outside this claim.

## Deterministic dynamics and thermodynamics

For `j_r=x^{y_r}−x^{y'_r}` (with the fixed `Q` activity absorbed), the deterministic equation is `ẋ=Σᵣνᵣjᵣ`. The point `x*` is detailed-balanced. By the global attractor theorem for complex-balanced mass-action systems, it attracts every positive trajectory in `P`; uniqueness also follows directly from the detailed-balance equations plus `b+c=2`.

Take the standard chemical potentials equal and zero in the normalized activity units. For each reversible pair,

`ΔG_r(x)=RT log(x^{y'_r}/x^{y_r})`,

and therefore `j_r ΔG_r = RT (u−w) log(w/u) ≤ 0`, where `u=x^{y_r}` and `w=x^{y'_r}`. Equality holds precisely when the forward and reverse activities agree. Thus the kinetic law obeys local detailed balance. For the first three reactions, `ν₁+ν₂+ν₃=0`, so their cycle affinities sum to zero. An internal steady net flux satisfies `v₁=v₂=v₃` and `v₄=0`; the three downhill inequalities then force every cycle term to be zero, and detailed balance forces the net flux to vanish. This is the usual exclusion of thermodynamically infeasible internal cycle flux.

## Count classes

For each integer `n≥0`, define

`Γ_n={(n,0,2,d),(n+1,1,1,d),(n+3,2,0,d):d∈ℕ}`.

All states satisfy `B+C=2`. The pathwise invariant on these states is

`H(A,B)=A−B(B+1)/2=n`.

The internal reaction transitions enabled in the three phases are:

- at `(n,0,2,d)`: `R₁` forward to `(n+1,1,1,d)`, `R₃` reverse to `(n+3,2,0,d)`, and both `R₄` directions changing `d`;
- at `(n+1,1,1,d)`: `R₁` reverse to the first phase and `R₂` forward to the third;
- at `(n+3,2,0,d)`: `R₂` reverse to the middle phase and `R₃` forward to the first.

Every enabled transition preserves `H`. The listed phase edges are bidirectional and have positive rates, and `R₄` gives `d→d+1` and `d→d−1` whenever enabled. Hence `Γ_n` is closed and irreducible. The main phase rates are finite at fixed `n`; the only unbounded rate is the linear death rate `2d` and the immigration rate is bounded by two. Domination by a bounded-rate immigration process plus a finite-state phase chain gives nonexplosion.

## Exact stationary law

Let

`Z_n=1/2 + 1/(n+1) + 1/[2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)]`.

For a state in phase `ℓ` with D count `d`, the law is

`π_n(a,b,c,d)=p_{ℓ,n} e^(−1)/d!`,

where

`p_{0,n}=1/(2Z_n)`,
`p_{1,n}=1/((n+1)Z_n)`,
`p_{2,n}=1/(2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)Z_n)`.

Proof by detailed balance: on a state `x` with an enabled forward reaction `y→y'` and `x'=x−y+y'`, the product-Poisson weights obey

`(1/x!) (x)_{y} = 1/(x−y)! = 1/(x'−y')! = (1/x'!)(x')_{y'}`.

This checks every paired reaction edge. Equivalently, consecutive phase weights have ratios `p₁/p₀=2/(n+1)`, `p₂/p₁=1/[2(n+2)(n+3)]`, and `p₂/p₀=1/[(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)]`; on the phase-0 edges that change `D`, the Poisson factor satisfies `e^{-1}/d! · 2 = e^{-1}/(d+1)! · 2(d+1)`. Together these give detailed balance on every edge. Thus `π_n` is a probability distribution. Nonexplosion and irreducibility imply positive recurrence and uniqueness on `Γ_n`.

All oriented channels have the same stationary gross event rate `1/Z_n`: for example, the first-pair forward rate is `2p₀=1/Z_n`, and its reverse is `(n+1)p₁=1/Z_n`; the other two pairs give the same value. The `D` birth and death channels each have rate `2p₀=1/Z_n` in stationarity. Net flux is zero for every pair; these are gross forward/reverse event counts, not a cycle current.

Since `A≥n` on `Γ_n`, `E_{π_n}A≥n`. For any compact subset of concentration/count space with `A≤M`, choose `n>M`; then `π_n` gives that compact set probability zero. Hence `{π_n}` is not uniformly tight, although all measures lie in the same deterministic stoichiometric class `B+C=2` and the deterministic system has the same global attractor `x*` throughout that class.

## Scope

The theorem is a counterexample to “common deterministic attraction implies uniform tightness of stochastic stationary laws across all communicating classes inside the same real stoichiometric class.” It does not refute positive recurrence on an individual component, the product-Poisson theorem, deterministic convergence, or thermodynamic looplessness. The source-system example N77 already has the escape phenomenon for a one-way cycle; this note adds reverse reactions and verifies detailed balance. Priority is unresolved.
