RANK-FREE STOPPING FROM A SINGLE RAW TRAJECTORY
Cycle 2 independent result package

Status: conditional theorem proved in this note; finite examples checked by the
included exact-arithmetic script. This is not an externally reviewed theorem,
an optimized learner, or a claim of novelty. The certificate is statistical
(failure probability at most delta under stated promises), not a deterministic
claim about an unknown population. It does not recover a future-prediction
realization or its coefficients.

1. Interface and promises
-------------------------
Let Sigma be a known alphabet of size q >= 2. The only data interface is one
stationary output stream Y_1,Y_2,... . The learner may read the next symbol and
decide whether to continue; it cannot reset the process, request a word
probability, choose an initial state, or query a Hankel entry. It receives no
feature dictionary or basis. It enumerates all words itself. At each look it
uses disjoint length-B windows from the stream, separated by skipped symbols.
No independent validation sample is assumed.

Write f(w)=Pr(Y_1...Y_|w|=w), f(empty)=1, and let the infinite predictive
Hankel matrix be H[u,v]=f(uv), u,v in Sigma*. Assume H has finite rank r, the
minimal predictive rank. Known promises are:

  (i) r <= R, where R is a state/rank cap;
  (ii) a known witness length L >= R-1;
  (iii) a known rational gap gamma > 0 such that every nonzero singular
       value of the finite witness H_L is at least gamma;
  (iv) for the optional all-history acquisition gate, every u in
       W_L=Sigma^{<=L} has f(u) >= p_min > 0, with known rational p_min;
  (v) known rational C_beta >= 1 and rational tau > 0 with
      beta(k) <= C_beta exp(-k/tau).

The beta convention is the standard absolute-regularity convention, indexed
by the number g of skipped symbols, so n blocks separated by at least g
skipped symbols can be coupled to n independent blocks with the same
marginals except on an event of probability at most (n-1) beta(g). In the
common convention indexed by the gap between time indices, this is the
coefficient at lag g+1. A known hidden-state cap D implies R=D, since predictive
rank is at most D. The gap and mixing envelope are supplied promises, not
estimated for free from this one path. Full support at level L is deliberately
strong; the rank-only theorem below can omit p_min and the history-coverage
gate.

Put W_L=Sigma^{<=L}, N=|W_L|=(q^(L+1)-1)/(q-1),
M=|Sigma^{<=2L+1}|=(q^(2L+2)-1)/(q-1), and B=2L+1. The finite matrix
H_L=(f(uv))_{u,v in W_L} is N by N. The one-symbol shifted blocks
H_a=(f(uav)) are also recoverable from the same windows, but the rank
certificate itself only uses H_L.

2. Finite witness identity
--------------------------
Choose a minimal linear representation f(w)=alpha A_w omega of dimension
r=rank(H). Minimality makes it reachable and observable, so the row vectors
alpha A_u and the column vectors A_v omega each span an r-dimensional space.
Each span begins at dimension one. If one growth step fails to increase its
dimension, it is invariant under every letter matrix and has already
stabilized. Thus both spans have dimension r by length r-1. For L >= r-1, the
finite row matrix and finite column matrix have full rank r, and their product
H_L has rank r. Therefore rank(H_L)=rank(H)=r.
This is the standard finite-rank realization framework; the horizon argument
here is proved directly rather than imported from a spectral-learning result.

3. Acquisition and rank certificate
------------------------------------
Allocate rational delta in (0,1/4). At look j=0,1,... acquire a fresh batch of
n_j=2^j length-B windows from the continuing stationary stream. Place g_j
skipped symbols between successive windows in that batch, with g_0=0 and,
for j>=1,

  delta_j = delta / (3 * 2^(j+1)),
  ell_j = max(0, ceil(log_2(C_beta*(n_j-1)/delta_j))),
  g_j = max(1, ceil(tau * ell_j)).

For each word w of length at most 2L+1, form the exact prefix count C_j(w)
among the n_j windows and fhat_j(w)=C_j(w)/n_j. In particular, for all
u,v in W_L set Hhat_j[u,v]=fhat_j(uv). No word probabilities are queried.
Also record which u in W_L has appeared at least once.

The entrywise confidence radius and its matrix consequence are

  eta_j = sqrt(log(2 M / delta_j) / (2 n_j)),
  epsilon_j = N eta_j,
  ||Hhat_j-H_L||_2 <= ||Hhat_j-H_L||_F <= epsilon_j.

For an exact implementation, put k_j=ceil(log_2(2M/delta_j)) and use the
slightly larger radius E_j=N sqrt(k_j/(2 n_j)). Since ln(x)<=log_2(x),
E_j >= epsilon_j. The test E_j < gamma/4 is checked exactly by squaring
rational quantities, equivalently 8 N^2 k_j < n_j gamma^2. Likewise
h_j=ceil(log_2(N/delta_j)) is an integer upper bound on ln(N/delta_j).
Every ceiling of a binary logarithm of a rational is computed by integer
comparisons, so no floating logarithm or square root is needed. Let
Ghat_j=Hhat_j^T Hhat_j. For a positive rational t, define

  I_j(t) = positive-inertia(Ghat_j - t I)
         = #{i : sigma_i(Hhat_j)^2 > t}.

This is an exact rational-arithmetic calculation, not a floating-point SVD
threshold. At look j stop and return d_j if all four checks pass:

  (a) E_j < gamma/4;
  (b) each u in W_L has positive observed count;
  (c) I_j(gamma^2/16) = I_j(9 gamma^2/16) = d_j;
  (d) d_j <= R.

The two inertia counts certify that the empirical singular spectrum has no
singular value in (gamma/4, 3 gamma/4]. The gap promise and
E_j < gamma/4 imply, on the confidence event, that exactly r singular values
lie above 3 gamma/4 and the others lie below gamma/4. Hence both exact counts
equal r and d_j=r. Conversely, when the promises and the confidence event
hold, these checks pass as soon as the deterministic sample inequalities in
Section 4 hold. The rank is unknown throughout; only R, L, gamma, and the
mixing/coverage promises are supplied.

The stopping time is data-dependent: the exact empirical inertia counts and
the observed-history coverage determine whether a look is accepted. It is
rank-free because the algorithm never receives r. It is not a deterministic
population certificate: with noisy trajectory data, the confidence event is
what connects the exact finite-sample count to the unknown population rank.

4. Probability and sample bound
-------------------------------
At look j, Berbee-type block coupling fails with probability at most
(n_j-1) beta(g_j) <= delta_j. This follows by maximal-coupling each block
after the first to a fresh copy with its stationary marginal, independent of
the preceding copies; absolute regularity bounds each mismatch probability by
beta(g_j), and a union bound gives the display. On the coupled independent
blocks, Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over M prefix indicators give

  Pr(max_{|w|<=2L+1} |fhat_j(w)-f(w)| > eta_j) <= delta_j.

If all histories have mass at least p_min, the chance that any u in W_L is
unseen in the independent coupled batch is at most N exp(-n_j p_min). This is
at most delta_j whenever n_j >= p_min^(-1) log(N/delta_j). A union bound over
all looks is valid even though the batches are dependent on each other: the
per-look failure estimates use only the marginal law of that batch and the
within-batch coupling. Since sum_j delta_j=delta/3, all concentration failures
across all looks cost at most delta/3, all mixing-coupling failures at most
delta/3, and all coverage failures at most delta/3. Total failure is at most
delta.

Let k_j=ceil(log_2(6 M 2^(j+1)/delta)) and
h_j=ceil(log_2(3 N 2^(j+1)/delta)). Let J be the first j satisfying both

  2^j > 8 N^2 gamma^(-2) k_j,
  2^j >= p_min^(-1) h_j.

The first inequality is exactly E_j < gamma/4. At look J, the probability of
coupling failure and the independent-batch coverage failure are each at most
delta_J and are charged separately. On the simultaneous concentration event,
Weyl's singular-value inequality yields

  sigma_r(Hhat_J) >= gamma - epsilon_J > 3 gamma/4,
  sigma_(r+1)(Hhat_J) <= epsilon_J < gamma/4.

Singular-value lists are zero-padded beyond dimension N if r=N. So the rank
certificate passes and returns the unknown r. This is a finite
stopping guarantee with probability at least 1-delta. If p_min is not promised,
omit (b), the second sample inequality, and the coverage-failure allocation;
the rank-only guarantee remains, with total failure at most 2delta/3 (or
rescale the two remaining budgets to delta/2 each).

The theorem's main count is n_J windows in the final fresh batch. Because
earlier looks use fresh batches rather than reusing a dependent prefix, the
total number of length-B windows read is

  sum_(j=0)^J 2^j = 2^(J+1)-1 < 2 n_J.

With nondecreasing g_j, the total raw trajectory length, including skipped
symbols, is at most

  B sum_j n_j + sum_j (n_j-1) g_j < 2 n_J (B+g_J).

Thus the charged symbol input is O(n_J (L+tau log(C_beta n_J 2^J/delta))).
The gap dependence is gamma^(-2); the all-history exposure term is
p_min^(-1) log(N 2^J/delta). These terms are not claimed to be jointly tight.
The p_min term is optional for rank alone: it is paid because this version also
requires that every enumerated candidate history has actually appeared.

5. Exact computation, storage, and bit costs
--------------------------------------------
Let n=n_J. A block contributes all its prefixes through length B, so streaming
the raw data takes O(B) counter updates per window, not M updates. The
algorithm enumerates the M word labels using base-q integer indices; it need
not retain the strings. Enumerating/initializing the table costs O(M) integer
operations. The count table has M integer counters of O(log(n+1)) bits, plus
the shared denominator n. The exact probability table therefore uses
O(M log(n+1)) bits. The input stream itself costs at most
ceil(log2 q) bits per symbol; storing rather than streaming the retained
windows would cost O(B n log q) bits at the final look and less than twice
that over all looks. Gaps are read and charged even though not retained.

Building Hhat costs O(N^2) table lookups. Storing Hhat uses
O(N^2 log(n+1)) bits. The common-denominator Gram matrix
Ghat has entries C_ab/n^2 with C_ab=sum_i c_ia c_ib and
0<=C_ab<=N n^2, so its integer numerators use O(log N+2 log n) bits and
storing it uses O(N^2(log N+log n)) bits. Each inertia check is an exact
symmetric rational/integer elimination with O(N^3) arithmetic operations.
After clearing denominators, fraction-free elimination and pivoting keep
intermediate subdeterminants to O(N(b+log N)) bits by the determinant bound,
where b=O(log N+2 log n+s_gamma) and s_gamma is the total bit length of
gamma's numerator and denominator. The thresholded integer matrices use
O(N^2 b) bits. With schoolbook
integer arithmetic this gives the conservative per-look bit-operation bound
O(N^5 (b+log N)^2); construction of Ghat has O(N^3) integer operations on
O(log N+log n)-bit inputs. This cost is for two thresholds; constants are
absorbed. Certified positive inertia can be checked by an exact fraction-free
LDL^T/equivalent rational symmetric-elimination certificate. No numerical SVD
rounding error is hidden in the rank test.

Across J+1 looks the arithmetic bound is the sum of these per-look costs, plus
the streamed counter updates. The dimension N is exponential in L, and the
table size M is exponential in 2L. The method is therefore a transparent
finite-block baseline, not an efficient adaptive feature-discovery result.

The output includes d, the rational count table (or its hash plus retained
table if independent replay is desired), the two exact inertia counts, and the
confidence parameters. The table lets a verifier recompute each count exactly;
this is reproducible certification, not a short proof-carrying certificate.
If full LDL^T factors are serialized to avoid recomputation, allow up to
O(N^3(b+log N)) additional rational bits for the two factorizations. It does
not output predictive transition/operator
coefficients. Consequently its coefficient-bit output cost is zero; the
probability table costs O(M log n) bits as above. If a realization is added,
its coefficient precision and stability need extra conditioning assumptions
on the chosen row/column basis and gauge. A Hankel gap alone does not give a
coordinate-wise coefficient-bit bound. No future-horizon prediction guarantee
is claimed.

6. Comparison with same-data controls
-------------------------------------

Full-block empirical Hankel/SVD, same raw data. This learner sees exactly the
same n windows, words, and skipped symbols. It builds the same N by N matrix
and estimates every one of the M word probabilities. An SVD gives O(N^3)
arithmetic operations and the usual data-dependent singular-value rank
estimate; with a known gap it has the same Hoeffding/Weyl sample bound above.
The present method adds (i) sequential looks with a confidence allocation and
(ii) exact rational inertia checks at two gap thresholds. It is a rank-free
stopping/certification wrapper around the full empirical block, not a sample
or dictionary reduction versus this control. The inertia route avoids
floating-point threshold ambiguity but does not remove the exponential-in-L
matrix/table cost.

Retained-sample control. Keeping all sampled blocks costs O(B n log q) bits
and permits later reprocessing, but it does not improve the statistical bound.
The theorem's streaming implementation can discard raw windows after updating
the M counters and history-seen flags; this trades raw retention for the
O(M log n) count table. An independent validation set is not used. If one is
required for a downstream claim, acquire and charge another spaced batch and
its own concentration budget.

Basis-supplied control. If an oracle instead supplies a valid row/column
basis, this is additional structural information absent from the present
interface. Such an oracle may reduce matrix construction from N^2 entries to
r^2, but its supplied basis, search cost, and certification cost must be
charged separately. We do not compare our result as though that basis were
free, and do not claim to discover a small basis adaptively from trajectory
data.

7. Necessity examples and failure boundaries
---------------------------------------------

Gap lower bound with full history mass and fast mixing. Consider the stationary
binary Markov chain

  P_theta(1|0)=1/2-theta,  P_theta(1|1)=1/2+theta,  0<theta<=1/4.

Its stationary marginal is uniform. The theta=0 process is iid fair and has
rank one; theta>0 has predictive rank two. On W_1={empty,0,1},

  H_1(theta) = [[1, 1/2, 1/2],
                [1/2, 1/4+theta/2, 1/4-theta/2],
                [1/2, 1/4-theta/2, 1/4+theta/2]],

with singular values 3/2, theta, 0. Both one-symbol histories have mass 1/2,
and the chain's nontrivial transition eigenvalue is 2 theta <=1/2, so mixing
is uniformly geometric. The one-transition divergence from the perturbed
chain to iid fair is

  d(theta)=(1/2+theta)log(1+2theta)+(1/2-theta)log(1-2theta)
          =2 theta^2+O(theta^4).

For a fixed T-observation transcript, D(P_theta||P_0)=(T-1)d(theta). In the
reverse direction, the one-transition divergence is

  d_0(theta)=D(Bern(1/2)||Bern(1/2+theta))
            =-(1/2)log(1-4 theta^2)=2 theta^2+O(theta^4).

If a sequential test stops at a stopping time with finite expected transition
count under theta=0, the stopped-path KL D(P_0^stop||P_theta^stop) is
E_0[tau] d_0(theta): the one-step conditional KL is d_0(theta) for either
previous symbol, and the KL chain rule applies up to the stop. Any test with
both error probabilities at most 1/3 therefore has E_0[tau]=Omega(theta^(-2))
by data processing to its binary decision. Thus no uniform finite stopping bound exists as the gap tends to zero, even
with fixed state cap, horizon, positive history mass, and constant mixing.

Rare-history exposure. For 0<epsilon<1/4, take a two-state output Markov
chain with P(1|0)=epsilon and P(1|1)=1/2. Its stationary mass of history "1"
is 2 epsilon/(1+2 epsilon)=Theta(epsilon), while its second transition
eigenvalue is 1/2-epsilon, so the mixing rate is uniformly geometric. The
epsilon=0 limit is deterministic all-zero (rank one); every epsilon>0 model
has rank two. From a stationary length-T path,

  Pr_epsilon(no 1 observed) = (1-pi_1)(1-epsilon)^(T-1),
  TV(P_epsilon^T, P_0^T) = 1-Pr_epsilon(no 1 observed)
                         <= pi_1+(T-1)epsilon.

For a test capped at T symbols, this gives T=Omega(1/p_min) to distinguish the
rank-one and rank-two models, and in particular to witness a prefix whose mass
is p_min in this acquisition task. Here the Hankel gap also vanishes with
epsilon; this example proves the exposure cost for seeing the rare prefix,
not an independent lower bound after fixing gamma. It explains why the
p_min term is present only when coverage of every history is part of the
output requirement.

Witness horizon/state cap. For any m, define a stationary order-m binary
Markov chain whose state is the last m symbols s and

  P(Y_next=1|s)=1/2+rho*(-1)^(sum_i s_i),  0<rho<1/2.

The context chain is irreducible and aperiodic. Uniform context mass is
stationary: for each target context, its two predecessor parities are
opposite, so the two incoming probabilities sum to one. Every length-m output
block is therefore uniform, hence every shorter block is iid-fair, but

  P(w1)=2^(-m) (1/2+rho*(-1)^(sum_i w_i)),  |w|=m,

so the infinite predictive rank exceeds one. For any finite word horizon K,
choose m>=K; all probabilities through length K agree exactly with iid fair
although one process has rank one and the other has larger rank. The number of
states is 2^m, so this is precisely what the supplied cap/witness horizon
excludes. No uniform finite rank-discovery horizon exists without such a cap.
This is a finite-horizon/state-cap obstruction; it is not used as a separate
lower bound against a uniformly specified mixing envelope.

Mixing necessity for one non-resettable path. The stationary process that is
all zeros forever with probability 0.9 and all ones forever with probability
0.1 has rank two, all length-one histories have mass at least 0.1, and its
W_1 Hankel matrix has nonzero singular values 1+sqrt(0.73) and
1-sqrt(0.73). But it is not beta-mixing. Its length-T path law has total
variation 0.1 from the deterministic all-zero rank-one process for every T.
Equal-prior testing error is at least 0.45 for all T. Thus finite witness,
state cap, and fixed gap/history mass do not rescue single-path acquisition
without an ergodicity/mixing promise (or independent restarts).

8. Claim boundary
-----------------
Proved here: rank(H_L)=rank(H) for L>=R-1; the sequential raw-window rule
returns the unknown rank with probability at least 1-delta under the explicit
state/rank cap, witness horizon, rational gap, mixing, and (if coverage is
required) minimum-history promises; all raw-symbol, counter, matrix, and exact
inertia costs above are charged; the gap and no-horizon/no-mixing counterexamples
are explicit.

Not proved: a subexponential-in-L or adaptive small-basis acquisition method;
sharpness of the upper bound's M, N, gamma, or p_min dependence beyond the
specific lower bounds given; a coordinate-stable observable realization or
future-time prediction bound; estimation of gamma, p_min, or the beta envelope
from the same path; or novelty/priority. A downstream learner that claims a
model must add its own conditioning, coefficient precision, and independent
validation analysis.
