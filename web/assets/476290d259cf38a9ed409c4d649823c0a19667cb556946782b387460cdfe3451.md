# Quantum/classical sensing boundary: lossy local phase estimation

Status: internally derived standard-model comparison; external theorem/priority not claimed. The numerical QFI comparison below is an exact calculation for a specified optical channel and probe class. It is not an experimentally validated advantage and does not price a particular NOON-state source.

## Exact sensor contract

Estimate a relative phase φ in a two-arm interferometer, locally around a known operating point. The encoding is

\[
U_\phi=\exp(-i\phi J_z),\qquad J_z=(n_a-n_b)/2.
\]

Each arm undergoes independent, equal, vacuum pure loss of transmissivity \(\eta_s\). Each output is read by one photon-number-resolving detector of efficiency \(\eta_d\). With equal efficiencies, pure-loss covariance under passive optics lets detector loss be moved before the final passive analyzer, so the complete effective transmission is \(\eta=\eta_s\eta_d\). There are no dark counts, thermal photons, mode mismatch, or phase diffusion. One copy means one use of each of the two arms. The phase prior is restricted to a single local fringe (width less than \(\pi\)); a globally unknown phase needs a coarse-estimation stage whose probes and energy must be added separately.

The quantum probe per copy is

\[
|\mathrm{NOON}_2\rangle=(|2,0\rangle+|0,2\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\]

with exactly two incident photons. In the ideal optical mode model it can be prepared deterministically by sending one indistinguishable photon into each input of a balanced beam splitter (Hong--Ou--Mandel interference); this uses exactly those two photons and no extra optical ancilla photons. The strongest classical comparator here is any two-mode optical state with a positive Glauber--Sudarshan \(P\) measure, including arbitrary classical randomization/correlation of coherent amplitudes across the \(m\) copies, and any joint output POVM. A coherent state of mean two in one input and vacuum in the other, passed through the same balanced interferometer, attains the classical bound. No quantum ancilla or nonclassical reference probe is included. Both strategies receive the same \(m\) two-arm channel uses and the same two output PNR detector channels per copy.

## QFI calculation and resource comparison

The NOON state has generator variance \(\operatorname{Var}(J_z)=1\), hence lossless QFI \(4\). Any loss of one or more photons reveals which arm carried the photons to the environment and destroys the NOON coherence; those output sectors are phase independent. The two-photon no-loss sector occurs with probability \(\eta^2\), so

\[
F_Q^{\rm NOON_2}=4\eta^2\quad\text{per copy},\qquad F_Q^{(m)}=4m\eta^2.
\]

For a coherent probe \(|\alpha_a,\alpha_b\rangle\), set \(n=|\alpha_a|^2+|\alpha_b|^2\). After phase and loss its amplitudes are \(\beta_{a,b}=\sqrt\eta\,\alpha_{a,b}e^{\mp i\phi/2}\), so the pure-state QFI is

\[
4\sum_j|\partial_\phi\beta_j|^2=\eta n.
\]

QFI convexity gives \(F_Q(\int P(d\alpha)\,|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|)\le\int P(d\alpha)F_Q(|\alpha\rangle)\). The same calculation on the joint multimode coherent state gives \(F_Q\le\eta N_{\rm in}\) for any classically correlated positive-\(P\) probe across all copies. Consequently, with the same total incident mean photon number \(N_{\rm in}=2m\), every comparator in this classical class satisfies

\[
F_{\rm cl}^{(m)}\le 2m\eta.
\]

The NOON advantage is therefore certified in this model when \(4m\eta^2>2m\eta\), equivalently \(\eta>1/2\). At \(\eta=0.8\): \(F_Q^{(m)}=2.56m\), while \(F_{\rm cl}^{(m)}\le1.6m\). The local asymptotic Cramér--Rao variances are bounded by \(0.390625/m\) and \(0.625/m\), respectively; the quantum variance bound is 37.5% lower. This is an asymptotic local-precision comparison, not a finite-shot global mean-square-error theorem.

The QFI is attained locally by applying a balanced passive analyzer and number resolving both outputs. On the no-loss branch, the three count probabilities can be chosen as \((\cos^2\phi/2,\sin^2\phi,\cos^2\phi/2)\), whose Fisher information is 4; photon-number sectors below two carry no phase information and are identified by the same PNR detectors. The classical coherent probe reaches \(\eta n\) by standard interferometric photon counting at the same two outputs. Detector accounting per \(m\) copies is thus two detector channels and \(2m\) detector readouts for either strategy, expected detected photons \(2m\eta\) for either, and expected lost probe photons \(2m(1-\eta)\) for either. This explicitly includes detector inefficiency in \(\eta\), rather than silently treating detector efficiency as ideal.

## Energy and implementation boundary

The comparison fully matches incident sensor photons, copies/channel uses, equal arm loss, detector count, expected detected photons, and detector efficiency. The ideal two-photon preparation above requires no extra optical photons; it does not establish a wall-plug or total-laboratory-energy advantage because single-photon source drive energy, indistinguishability engineering, and switching/control work are technology-dependent and absent from the channel contract. A useful break-even accounting is to let \(a_Q\) and \(a_C\) be extra photon-equivalent preparation/control energies per copy. At \(\eta=0.8\), quantum precision per total photon still beats the coherent comparator only if

\[
\frac{2.56}{2+a_Q}>\frac{1.6}{2+a_C},\qquad\text{i.e. }a_Q<1.2+1.6a_C.
\]

In particular, if the classical source/control is treated as free beyond its two incident photons, the quantum source overhead must be below 1.2 photon-equivalents per copy for this particular NOON witness to retain the advantage. A platform-specific energy audit can change the practical conclusion. Dark counts also require a separate extension; they are not hidden inside \(\eta\).

The pinned source is Astra commit `8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83`. Exact cards and SHA-256 values are recorded in `SOURCE_NOTES.md`.

* **N92, instrument-channel classicalization.** For a supplied bistochastic Kraus family \(\{K_y\}\), its canonical measure-prepare channel \(\Psi_M\), and a supplied reference/Petz construction, the card's load-bearing SOS is
  \[
  2(I-\Phi)-(I-\Psi_M)=(I-L^\dagger L)+(\Psi_M-\Psi_M^2)+(L-\Psi_M)^\dagger(L-\Psi_M)/2\succeq0.
  \]
  In the explicit compatible classes this yields the dimension-independent half-trace error \(e\le\tfrac12\sqrt{2\theta r}\). Its hypotheses include a finite-dimensional tracial setting, a supplied instrument/Kraus family, and the card's positivity/self-compatibility conditions. It does not search for an instrument or classical decoder and does not apply to the phase-encoding unitary plus bosonic loss model above. In particular, it cannot be used to erase the QFI gap.
* **N93, bounded-Holevo gates.** The general implication “bounded Holevo information plus vanishing pairwise broadcast error implies uniform EB reconstruction” remains open in the card. The card's explicit flagged ladders delimit proposed shortcuts; they establish neither an actual phase-sensing advantage nor its absence. No N93 premise is used in the QFI proof.
* **N71/N74, passive-loss/count boundaries.** These cards concern Gaussian squeezed-vacuum output count-law approximation by a centered-reset sampler, with N71's cubic full-spectrum functional and N74's algorithm-specific sharpness/obstructions. They do not cover NOON probes or phase Fisher information, so they are not used as a loss theorem here. N45's photon-event statements concern multiplicative rare-event/graph-count reductions, not this ordinary local interferometric estimator.

The result is established-model metrology rather than an Astra-derived discovery. Lossy interferometry and the fragility of NOON states are longstanding primary-literature results; the two-photon threshold above is a direct special-case calculation and is not a novelty claim. See Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al. (2009), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.80.013825`, and the physical-resource analysis of Sahota, Quesada, and James (2016), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.94.033817`.

## Falsifiers and next gate

The exact mathematical claim is falsified if, under the specified local generator, equal vacuum-loss channel, and fixed input mean energy, a positive-\(P\) probe plus any output POVM has QFI above \(\eta N_{\rm in}\), or if direct derivation of the lossy NOON state gives QFI other than \(4\eta^2\). An experimental claim requires measuring arm transmissions and both detector efficiencies, calibrating dark counts and mode mismatch, verifying the local-phase prior, and including the NOON preparation energy/work against the explicit break-even inequality. Without that platform ledger, status stays a channel-level theoretical advantage only.
