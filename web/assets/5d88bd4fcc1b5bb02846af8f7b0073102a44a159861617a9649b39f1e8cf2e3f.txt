H_B baseline: legal amplification for every n
---------------------------------------------
Section 4 proves the bound for n>=8L/a, where L and a depend only on the base
game, delta, answer alphabets, and B. This uniformity matters: it allows the
same block count to work for every strategy at a given n. For any original
length n>=1, choose the finite integer

  m = max(1, ceil(8L/(a n))).

Then mn>=8L/a. The construction in Section 6 tensors m copies of the entire
original n-fold commuting representation on H^(tensor m). It does not assume
that the original n coordinate measurements factor, commute within a player,
or have independent win indicators. Each new block receives its own n-tuple
of questions and uses the original joint n-question POVM on one tensor factor.
Across Alice and Bob, the block product effects commute factor by factor;
positivity and normalization follow by tensoring POVMs. Hence this is a legal
strategy for G^(mn), provided H_B quantifies over the constructed strategy
class. The stated premise does: it covers every length and every legal
strategy. For finite-dimensional strategies, tensor powers remain finite
dimensional; for the commuting-operator model, tensoring the whole common
Hilbert-space representation also preserves cross-party commutation.

The product state and independent product-question law make the m constructed
blocks independent and identically distributed, each with the original tail
probability P. Thus the probability that every block reaches
t=ceil((v+delta)n) is exactly P^m. On that event,

  m ceil((v+delta)n) >= ceil(m(v+delta)n)
                         = ceil((v+delta)mn).

This ceiling inequality is exact for integer m and real (v+delta)n. Therefore
the mn-copy tail probability is at least P^m. Apply the large-length bound to
the constructed strategy and take an m-th root. If P=0 the target inequality
is immediate; if P>0 the root is valid. The original n may be any positive
integer, and no prefactor, additive gap loss, or independence assumption on
the original coordinates is introduced. The per-coordinate ell stays
ln(|A||B|), since the large theorem is applied to G at length mn, not to an
n-block supergame treated as a new base game.

The same argument applies to the R_K addendum's rare threshold tail. It
explicitly requires R_K with the same tau_C, for all strategies, lengths,
conditioning sets, and constructed amplifiers. Its large-length cutoff is
strategy-independent, and the product-block probability and ceiling step are
unchanged. At delta=epsilon, t=n exactly, so each block event is the original
all-wins event and the product event is the mn-coordinate all-wins event.
The cubic all-wins exponent is therefore conditional on R_K; the baseline
H_B alone supplies only its separate fifth-power all-wins specialization.

State representation note
-------------------------
Section 6 writes the original strategy using a unit vector psi. If the chosen
definition of a legal commuting strategy already uses a vector state, the
construction is literal. If it permits a mixed density operator rho, the
same proof uses rho^(tensor m) and the same tensor-product POVMs; alternatively
one may purify and extend both parties' effects by the identity. This is a
routine representation choice, not an obstruction to the amplification.
The premise must still cover the resulting strategy class. The report states
that scope requirement explicitly.

Suprema and endpoint checks
---------------------------
The large-length estimates and cutoff depend on parameters of G and on B (or
K), not on the particular repeated strategy. The short-length reduction
therefore gives the same right-hand side for every n-strategy. Taking the
supremum over strategies preserves it, yielding the corresponding bound on
omega_co(G^n). No maximizing strategy is needed, and tensoring is applied to
each fixed strategy before taking a supremum.

For 0<delta<=epsilon=1-v, the integer threshold lies in {1,...,n}. If
delta>epsilon, it exceeds n and the event is empty; delta=0 has only the
trivial probability bound 1. If epsilon=0, there are no positive deltas and
the all-wins probability is at most 1. At v=0, the empty conditioning set has
tau=0, so either premise gives E[W]/n<=v=0; hence W=0 almost surely and every
positive threshold event is empty. At delta=epsilon>0, the threshold is
ceil(n)=n exactly, including v=0.

For d=|A||B|=1, both answer sets are singletons. The sole-outcome POVMs are
identities, so each win indicator is a fixed function of its coordinate's
question sample. Independent coordinate sampling makes W a sum of iid
Bernoulli(v) variables; this independence is forced by the game structure
only in this endpoint and is not asserted for general strategies. The H_B
baseline's Chernoff/Hoeffding calculation gives

  P[W>=ceil((v+delta)n)] <= exp(-2 delta^2 n)
                              <= exp(-c_B delta^4 n),

since 0<delta<=epsilon<=1 and c_B=1/(1024B^4)<=2. The R_K addendum's
singleton estimate also follows because
delta^4/epsilon^2<=delta^2 and its constant
1/(16384 max(1,K)^4)<=2. Thus ell=0 is handled directly, with no division by
ell and no generic scalar inference from an abstract premise.

Comparison with the frozen independent derivations
--------------------------------------------------
The independent H_B report at
work/agents/cycle10_nonlocal_games_luna/conditional_bound_consequences/REPORT.txt
gets the same all-n threshold exponent by a direct factorial-moment argument:
when its iteration length X is below 2, the empty-set conditional bound and
Markov handle the case. The Sol baseline instead extends its large-n proof by
tensoring whole strategies. Both routes are valid under uniform H_B; the
Sol block amplification is a distinct, exact closure argument.

For R_K, the independent loss-sensitive report at
work/agents/cycle10_nonlocal_games_luna/loss_sensitive_consequences/REPORT.txt
uses the same stronger premise and the same threshold exponent, but its
direct small-X route yields a more conservative explicit constant
1/(18,874,368 max(1,K)^4). The frozen Sol addendum gives
1/(16,384 max(1,K)^4), a factor 1,152 larger, through its barrier argument
and block amplification. This compares only scalar wrappers under the exact
same named R_K interface; it says nothing about whether R_K holds for
commuting strategies.

At the premise level, R_K implies an H_B-type conditional estimate with
B=2K_0: for 0<=tau<=1, use alpha<=1 and sqrt(tau)<=tau^(1/4) to get

  epsilon <= alpha+2K_0 tau^(1/4),
  q=1-alpha <= v+2K_0 tau^(1/4).

For tau>1 the latter H_B inequality is automatic because B>=1 and q<=1.
Thus the R_K wrapper uses a genuinely stronger scalar input and yields the
epsilon^-2 gain in the threshold exponent. The H_B baseline does not
establish R_K; neither wrapper acquires its quantum premise here.
