1. Normalization, Casimir, and total variance

With Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2, the coordinate pairing of a traceless
Cartan matrix H=diag(h_i) with the highest weight is lambda(H)=sum_i a_i h_i.
Since Tr(xi H)=sum_A mu_A x_A/2, xi=diag(l_i/2) is the correct identification
when l_i=a_i/N in traceless weight coordinates. Thus |mu|^2=2Tr(xi^2)
=1/2 sum_i l_i^2. The factor 1/2 in (3.1) is necessary and consistent.

The generator metric agrees with C2(lambda)=(lambda,lambda+2rho):
|mu_rho|^2=1/2 sum_i ((m+1-2i)/2)^2=m(m^2-1)/24=K_rho, and
beta=2<mu,mu_rho>=sum_i l_i(m+1-2i)/2. Directly, for each i<j,
the two Hermitian root generators contribute N(l_i-l_j)/2 on the highest
vector. Summing gives N beta, so C_N=N^2|mu|^2+N beta with no convention
factor missing. Strictly positive adjacent gaps imply beta>0.

For delta_A=T_A/N-mu_A I, expansion of the squared norms gives exactly
sum_A||delta_A psi||^2=C_N/N^2-|mu|^2=beta/N. The bounds
|l_i|<=(m-1)C, |mu|^2<=K_0, beta<=2 sqrt(K_0 K_rho)=beta_0,
C_N<=KN^2, ||T_A||<=sqrt(K)N, and ||sum x_A T_A/N||<=r sqrt(K)
follow from width, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the positive Casimir sum. Also
||xi||_op^2<=Tr(xi^2)<=K_0/2<=C_F K=b^2, since C_F>=3/4. Hence the same
b bounds both X and xi in the later telescoping estimate.

2. Partial trace, power commutators, and constants

The basis {t_A, I/sqrt(2m)} is a complete orthogonal fundamental matrix
basis, each element having squared HS norm 1/2. Expanding an arbitrary
operator-valued block matrix Z in this basis gives Parseval equality
(sum over A plus the identity coefficient)=2||Z||_HS^2. Dropping the
nonnegative identity-block contribution proves (4.3), with precisely the
factor 2. The ordering in the coefficient 2Tr_f[(t_A tensor I)Z] is
consistent with this expansion.

For X=sum_A t_A tensor T_A/N, direct contraction gives
||[X,I tensor F]||_HS^2=E(F)/(2N^2). The noncommutative identity
[X^s,B]=sum_{j=0}^{s-1}X^j[X,B]X^{s-1-j} and the two-sided HS ideal
inequality give the stated s b^(s-1) bound. Applying (4.3) to this
commutator yields (4.4) with no missing factor: 2 times E/(2N^2).
The sum over s of the resulting coefficients is C_0 as defined.

The partial trace defining Y_(s,A) is Hermitian because it is cyclic against
operators on the traced fundamental factor. It is unnormalized and Y_(1,A)
=T_A/N. Expanding 2Tr(t_A t_B t_C)=(d_ABC+i f_ABC)/2 and using
f_ABC f_BCD=m delta_AD verifies (4.5), including the negative linear term
-m T_A/4. That correction is genuinely finite-N and cannot be omitted.

3. Central Gram reduction and quantitative error

Adjoint covariance makes (A,B) -> Tr(Y_(s,A)Y_(t,B)) an invariant bilinear
form on the irreducible complex adjoint, hence a scalar multiple of delta_AB.
The scalar is real because the trace of a product of two Hermitian matrices
is real; trace cyclicity makes it symmetric in s,t. The symmetrized
contraction Z_st is invariant and therefore scalar on irreducible V_a.
Taking its normalized trace proves q G_(N,st)=<psi,Z_st psi> exactly. This
step does not replace an unknown trace by a semiclassical trace limit.

For any unit fundamental u, Cauchy–Schwarz in the A-index and (3.3) give
||(X-xi tensor I)(u tensor psi)||<=sqrt(C_F beta/N). The ordered
(noncommuting) telescoping formula in §5 is correct. Bounding the left
X^j factor by b^j and the right xi^(s-1-j) factor by b^(s-1-j) gives
s b^(s-1)sqrt(C_F beta/N). Diagonalizing t_A before taking the partial
trace bounds its A-component by 2||t_A||_1 times this quantity. Since
||t_A||_1^2<=m/2 and 2qm C_F=q^2, the vector error constant is exactly
v_s=q sqrt(beta_0) s b^(s-1); the symbol bound is u_s=sqrt(2m)b^s.
Expanding the inner products, taking real parts, and applying Cauchy–Schwarz
then yields (5.6) with J_st=u_s v_t+u_t v_s+v_s v_t. Replacing 1/N by
1/sqrt(N) in the last term and Frobenius-bounding the p-by-p error gives
J_0/(q sqrt(N)); no factor p or q is omitted.

4. Vandermonde conditioning and uniform cutoff

For V_(i,s)=eta_i^s and P_0=I-11^T/m, H=2V^T P_0 V. The augmented
Vandermonde determinant and Schur complement give exactly
det H=(2^p/m) product_(i<j)(eta_i-eta_j)^2. Adjacent label gaps imply
eta_i-eta_j>= (j-i)c/2. Therefore the displayed D_0 is valid. Also
Tr H<=2m sum_s b^(2s)=A_0. For positive H, each eigenvalue other than
lambda_min is at most Tr H, so lambda_min(H)>=det(H)/(Tr H)^(p-1)>=kappa;
the p=1 convention is correct. The perturbation bound gives
||G-H/q||<=J_0/(q sqrt(N)). For N>=ceil(max(1,(2J_0/kappa)^2)), it is
at most kappa/(2q), whence G>=kappa/(2q)I=g0 I and
Tr G<=A_0/q+p kappa/(2q)=g1. This establishes a genuine fixed-interface
uniform N_0; it is not uniform as c->0 or as m grows.

Easy valid sharpening: retain the separation (j-i)c/2 rather than replace
every gap by c/2. The determinant lower bound can be increased to
D_gap=(2^p/m)(c/2)^(m(m-1)) product_(d=1)^(m-1) d^(2(m-d)), and kappa can
be replaced by D_gap/A_0^(p-1). For m=4,c=1 this improves D_0 by factor
144. This changes only conservatism, not validity of the stated N_0.

The explicit cutoff is extraordinarily loose. At m=3,c=C=1, direct
floating-point evaluation of the displayed formulas gives N_0 about
1.37e19 (rough estimate, not an exact ceil computation). This is a cost
limitation of the proof constants, not a counterexample: for each fixed
m,c,C the constants are finite. The gap factors also show why no uniform
claim should be extended to a weight wall (c=0) or to growing m without new
bounds.
