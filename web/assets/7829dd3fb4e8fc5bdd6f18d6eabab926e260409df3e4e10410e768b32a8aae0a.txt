CYCLE10 CONDITIONAL MOVING-BOUNDARY TRACE FROM DISCRETE HITTING MEASURES
2026-10-09

Status: internal proof of a conditional discrete-to-limit trace theorem. The
assumption that the hit measures have uniform (r+h)^alpha growth is treated
as supplied and is not proved here. The theorem proves zero trace for every
weak limit of these measures when the finite-element functions vanish at
every atom. It does not by itself identify the limit measure as the harmonic
measure of a limiting domain, prove a full boundary condition, or identify a
Poisson extension without those extra hypotheses.

1. PRECISE INTERFACE

Let U be a fixed open subset of R^2 and X a fixed compact subset of U. For
each n, let T_n be a shape-regular, quasiuniform triangulation in a fixed
neighborhood of X, with mesh size h_n -> 0. Let u_n be continuous P1 on T_n,
with a uniformly bounded W^{1,p}(U)-norm for each fixed 1<p<2. It is enough
that the u_n have extensions to U with a uniform extension bound. No
zero-extension across a moving boundary is assumed.

Let nu_n be positive finite Borel measures with support on mesh vertices
Z_n where u_n(z)=0. Assume supp(nu_n) lies in a fixed compact set X' subset U,
and, for fixed A,r_0,alpha>0 independent of n,

    nu_n(B(x,r)) <= A (r+h_n)^alpha,
       x in R^2, 0 <= r <= r_0.                         (G)

Assume also sup_n nu_n(X') <= M < infinity. This mass bound follows from
(G) if the supports remain in a fixed compact set and r_0 is fixed, by a
finite cover; it is stated separately to make the non-normalized case
explicit. Suppose along a subsequence nu_n converges weakly to nu, while
u_n converges weakly in W^{1,p}(U) and strongly in L^p(U) to u. The latter
compactness is available after taking a subsequence on a bounded Lipschitz
localization, or from the assumed L1 compactness plus the W1p bound and
interpolation.

The support-on-zero-vertices condition is load-bearing. The result does not
apply if nu_n charges points on boundary edges where P1 interpolation is
nonzero, unless a separate transport or trace estimate handles those values.
The stated path-diameter condition for the killed walk is not used after (G)
and exact zero support are granted; it can be relevant upstream to deriving
(G) or proving convergence of the walks/domains.

2. DISCRETE TRACE ESTIMATE, INCLUDING THE ATOM SCALE

Fix 1<p<2 with p+alpha>2, and put

    theta = (p+alpha-2)/p > 0.

For a fixed nonnegative smooth mollifier rho of integral one, write
M_s v = rho_s*v. For h_n <= s <= r_0/c, the following estimate holds with a
constant depending on p, alpha, A, the mesh regularity, rho, and the fixed
localization, but not on n, the number of zero vertices, or nu_n(X') except
through the displayed growth constant:

    ||u_n - M_s u_n||_{L^p(nu_n)}
       <= C A^(1/p) s^theta ||grad u_n||_{L^p(U)}.       (1)

In particular, since u_n(z)=0 at every atom,

    ||M_s u_n||_{L^p(nu_n)}
       <= C A^(1/p) s^theta ||grad u_n||_p.              (2)

The atom-scale step is essential. At each mesh vertex z, the P1 inverse
estimate and local Poincare imply

    |u_n(z)-M_{h_n}u_n(z)|^p
       <= C h_n^(p-2) integral_{B(z,C h_n)} |grad u_n(y)|^p dy.

Integrating over nu_n and reversing the order of integration, the mass of
centers z with y in B(z,C h_n) is at most nu_n(B(y,C' h_n)) <= C A h_n^alpha.
Thus

    ||u_n-M_{h_n}u_n||_{L^p(nu_n)}^p
       <= C A h_n^(p+alpha-2) ||grad u_n||_p^p.          (3)

For h_n<=t<=r_0/c, local Poincare gives

    |M_t v(z)-M_{2t}v(z)|^p
       <= C t^(p-2) integral_{B(z,Ct)} |grad v|^p.

After integration against nu_n, the center mass over each such ball is
bounded by A(Ct+h_n)^alpha <= C A t^alpha. Therefore

    ||M_t v-M_{2t}v||_{L^p(nu_n)}^p
       <= C A t^(p+alpha-2) ||grad v||_p^p.              (4)

Minkowski and a dyadic telescope from h_n to s sum the pth-root errors.
Since theta>0, this is a convergent geometric sum bounded by C s^theta;
(3) is the first term and is no larger than the same bound. This proves (1).
The argument uses no count of atoms. At r=0, (G) also gives each atom mass
at most A h_n^alpha, exactly the scale used in (3).

3. LIMIT MEASURE AND ZERO TRACE

For fixed r<r_0, the weak limit obeys nu(B(x,r))<=A r^alpha (up to harmless
constant changes at the localization boundary). To see this without a
Portmanteau direction error, test nu_n against a continuous cutoff equal to
one on B(x,r) and supported in B(x,r+epsilon), pass to the weak limit, use
(G), then send epsilon down to zero. The same argument shows nu has no
atoms when alpha>0. If supp(nu_n) approaches a compact K in Hausdorff
distance, then supp(nu) is contained in K. An upper growth bound alone does
not force supp(nu)=K; any conclusion on all of K requires a lower-mass or
full-support condition.

For fixed s>0, strong Lp convergence of u_n gives uniform convergence of
M_s u_n to M_s u on X'. Weak convergence of nu_n and (2) then give

    ||M_s u||_{L^p(nu)} <= C A^(1/p) s^theta sup_n||grad u_n||_p. (5)

The continuous-measure version of (4), using nu(B(x,r))<=A r^alpha, shows
that M_{2^-j s_0}u is Cauchy in Lp(nu). Define

    T_nu u = lim_{s downarrow 0} M_s u in Lp(nu).

The same dyadic estimate controls all scales, not only the chosen sequence.
Letting s down to zero in (5) yields

    T_nu u = 0  nu-almost everywhere.                    (6)

All bounds are homogeneous in the measure. No probability normalization is
used: constants may depend on A and the finite mass bound M. If nu_n are
subprobabilities or their masses vary, (6) still holds for every weak limit;
zero-mass limits give a vacuous trace statement. When the measure is used in
an integral, the conclusion is integral |T_nu u| dnu=0, not a normalization
claim.

The trace here is the mollifier-defined Sobolev restriction to nu. For
alpha-Frostman nu and p+alpha>2 it is bounded W1p -> Lp(nu). Its capacity
bound implies that it agrees nu-a.e. with the p-quasicontinuous Sobolev
representative. This is a measure-relative trace statement; an upper
Frostman bound does not prove zero p-quasieverywhere on every point of K.

4. CHOICE OF EXPONENT

The alpha in (G) must be one fixed positive exponent, uniform in n. For any
such alpha, choose

    p in (max(1,2-alpha), 2).

The hypothesis supplies the W1p bound for this fixed p, and theta>0. For
example if 0<alpha<=1, p=2-alpha/2 gives theta=alpha/(2p)>0 and p>1. If
alpha>1 the same conclusion follows by using any fixed alpha_0 in (0,1]
with alpha_0<=alpha in the small-scale bound and choosing
p>max(1,2-alpha_0). If the only available data were bounds for p in a
restricted subset below 2, the exact requirement would instead be that
some available p satisfy p+alpha>2.

For a nonzero weak limit on a fixed planar compact set, a uniform bound with
alpha>2 cannot hold: a cover by O(r^-2) balls gives total mass O(r^(alpha-2))
as r down to zero. In that case the weak limit is zero and its trace claim
is vacuous. Natural nonzero limiting hit laws therefore have alpha<=2.

5. HARMONIC-EXTENSION INTERFACE

Equation (6) is enough to pass zero data to an actual harmonic extension
only after a separate identification:

  (i) the weak limit nu of the discrete hit laws is the relevant continuum
      harmonic measure (or a specified nonzero scalar multiple of it);
  (ii) the harmonic function H has the Sobolev/mollifier trace T_nu H and
       its Poisson representation uses that same trace; and
  (iii) the exit law has no uncontrolled cemetery, lost-boundary, or
        additional-component mass.

Under these conditions, if T_nu H=0, then its Poisson integral is zero.
For a full probability harmonic measure this gives H(z)=0 at the pole. If
nu=m omega^z with m>0, the integral is m H(z), so it still gives H(z)=0.
If nu is merely a subprobability and omitted mass contributes an unknown
boundary/cemetery term, the trace result controls only the nu-part and does
not force H(z)=0. No assumption in (G) alone identifies nu with continuum
harmonic measure, proves moving-domain convergence, or gives Poisson
representation. The path-diameter condition may be useful for such a
separate convergence/Beurling argument, but its presence does not substitute
for that proof.

6. SQRT(h log(1/h)) CUTOFF AND TRANSPORT

For measures already supported on exact-zero mesh vertices, no transport
cutoff is needed: (3) regularizes at the actual mesh scale h_n, and the
fixed-s limit argument above then sends s to zero. This is preferable to
introducing an artificial sqrt(h log(1/h)) scale.

If an independent scatter/transport construction gives two finite measures
mu_n,nu_n with uniformly bounded mass and the same growth, coupled by pi_n
with |x-y|<=L eta_n, their traces satisfy the following estimate whenever
eta_n>=h_n:

    integral |u_n(x)-u_n(y)| d pi_n(x,y)
       <= C eta_n^theta ||grad u_n||_p.                  (7)

Here “same growth” can mean either continuous Frostman growth
mu_n(B),nu_n(B)<=A r^alpha at every radius, or the discrete mesh growth
mu_n(B),nu_n(B)<=A(r+h_n)^alpha. In the latter case the measures may be
atomic or supported on mesh-scale boundary sets. The same atom-scale P1
estimate applies to arbitrary centers in a simplex, since local P1 inverse
estimates hold uniformly there. Since u_n is continuous P1, its values are
the measure traces. Replace each value by its eta_n mollification, costing
C eta_n^theta by (1) (or its continuous-measure version). For coupled
points within L eta_n, local Poincare gives
|M_eta u(x)-M_eta u(y)|^p <= C eta^(p-2) integral_{B(x,C eta)}|grad u|^p.
Integrating uses the growth bound on the x-marginal at radius C eta_n,
yielding the same eta^(p+alpha-2) pth-power cost. Hölder converts the Lp
bound to the displayed L1 bound, with a factor depending on the uniform
coupling mass M. With eta_n=sqrt(h_n log(1/h_n)), the error
is

    O((h_n log(1/h_n))^(theta/2)) -> 0.

Thus the square-root cutoff halves the convergence exponent but does not
destroy convergence for any theta>0. This statement requires the two
marginals' Frostman bounds (or an equivalent local-mass bound at scale
eta_n). A displacement bound by itself, with an uncontrolled source
marginal, does not give (7). The exact-zero-atom route avoids this issue.

7. FALSIFIERS AND UNRESOLVED GATES

The proved trace theorem fails to apply if (a) alpha is not uniform and
shrinks to zero with n, (b) nu_n places mass on nonzero edge interpolants,
(c) measures escape the common compact localization or have unbounded mass,
or (d) no fixed p with p+alpha>2 has a uniform W1p bound. These are
hypothesis failures, not counterexamples under the stated assumptions.

The main downstream falsifier is a sequence satisfying all assumptions for
which (6) fails; the displayed atom-scale and dyadic estimates rule this out
within the stated P1/shape-regular setup. A separate falsifier to the
harmonic upgrade is a family of discrete exit laws satisfying (G) whose weak
limit is not the continuum harmonic measure, or whose missing exit mass
contributes nonzero Poisson data. No moving-domain convergence theorem was
proved here.

Costs: the trace decay exponent is theta=(p+alpha-2)/p and deteriorates as
p+alpha approaches 2. Constants depend on fixed p, alpha, the growth/mass
constants, mesh regularity, mollifier, and localization. No dimension,
number-of-atoms, or family-cardinality factor occurs in (1)-(6). No external
literature, simulation, or formal proof assistant was used; this is a direct
Poincare/Fubini reconstruction conditional on (G).
