1. EXACT IDENTITY AND CONSTANT

Let the mean sigma>0 commute with every rho_x and L_x=sigma^(-1/2)rho_x
sigma^(-1/2)<=M I. Then int L_x=I and rho_x=sigma L_x. For a TP channel
Phi with Kraus A_i, set B_i=A_i sqrt(sigma), C=int L_x^2 and
D=sum_i int ||L_x B_i-B_i L_x||_HS^2. TP gives sum B_i^dag B_i=sigma
and sum B_i B_i^dag=Phi(sigma), with sum ||B_i||_HS^2=1.

The first squared norm sums to Tr C Phi(sigma), the second to Tr C sigma.
Because sqrt(sigma) commutes with L_x, the cross term is exactly
2 int Tr L_x Phi(rho_x). Also int Tr L_x rho_x=Tr C sigma. Therefore

  D/2=int Tr L_x(rho_x-Phi(rho_x))
       +(1/2)Tr C(Phi(sigma)-sigma).                         (1.1)

No unitality, covariance or preservation of sigma has been assumed. In
particular the matrices B_i can contain arbitrary rectangular sector blocks.
For e=int T(Phi(rho_x),rho_x), the effect L_x/M bounds the first term by M e.
The identity int(L_x-I)^2=C-I>=0 and L_x^2<=M L_x imply I<=C<=MI.
The trace-zero reference discrepancy thus has
|Tr C(Phi(sigma)-sigma)|<=(M-1)T(Phi(sigma),sigma)<=(M-1)e.
Consequently D<=(3M-1)e, and e>=Lambda_L(Q)/(3M-1) for rank-Q Kraus codes,
where Lambda_L(Q) minimizes int||[L_x,B]||_HS^2 over the ENTIRE matrix space
subject to rank B<=Q and ||B||_HS=1. Source-sector minimization alone is
not justified. If C is scalar or Phi(sigma)=sigma, e>=D/(2M).

2. KNOWN-SPECTRUM FULL UNITARY ORBIT: NO EXPONENTIAL ENVELOPE

For fixed p with every p_i>0, the n-copy Schur decomposition is

  rho_U^tensor n=direct_sum_lambda P_lambda rho_lambda,U tensor I_mult/f_lambda.

Haar averaging over U(d) makes
sigma=direct_sum_lambda P_lambda I_carrier/d_lambda tensor I_mult/f_lambda.
This reference commutes with every input, and whitening is exactly

  L_U=direct_sum_lambda d_lambda rho_lambda,U tensor I_mult.

Hence M=max_lambda d_lambda ||rho_lambda||_op<=max_lambda d_lambda.
For fixed d the Weyl dimension product bounds this by
C_d(n+1)^(d(d-1)/2), independent of how small P_lambda is. The exponentially
large input multiplicities cancel in whitening. Thus fixed-spectrum Haar
orbits cannot have exponentially large M from rare extreme Schur sectors.
This statement does not apply automatically to varying-spectrum priors, whose
sector probabilities can also change inside the whitened likelihood ratio.

3. CRITICAL QUBIT AND QUTRIT ENVELOPES

For a qubit carrier L=2j, q=(1-r)/(1+r),

  d_L ||rho_L||=(L+1)(1-q)/(1-q^(L+1))
               <=1+(L+1)(1-q)<=1+2(n+1)r.                  (3.1)

The elementary inequality uses (1-x)^s<=1/(1+sx), 0<=x<1. Therefore at
r=Theta(n^(-1/2)), the full orbit has M=O(sqrt n); on L=O(sqrt(n log n))
its carrier likelihood is O(sqrt(log n)). Neither is an acquired converse.

For GLOBAL_CAPTURE's qutrit p=(a+c/(2sqrt n),a-c/(2sqrt n),1-2a), put
k=lambda1-lambda2, m=lambda2-lambda3 and q=p2/p1. The carrier partition
function contains the entire N=0 spin fiber, so

  s_lambda(p)>=p1^lambda1 p2^lambda2 p3^lambda3
                (1-q^(k+1))/(1-q).

The highest weight has maximal Gibbs eigenvalue. The Weyl dimension is
d_lambda=(k+1)(m+1)(m+k+2)/2; thus

  d_lambda ||rho_lambda||
    <=[(m+1)(m+k+2)/2][1+(k+1)(1-q)]
    =O_a,c1(n^(5/2)).                                      (3.2)

In the typical window k=O(sqrt(n log n)), m=Theta(n), this bound is
O_a,c1(n^2 sqrt(log n)). The full-space rank variational problem remains
unresolved. No deletion of constant sector mass, free alternative reference
or fixed-source-sector converse is licensed by these envelope estimates.
