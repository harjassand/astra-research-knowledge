## Primary experimental sources

1. Hohng, Zhou, Nahas, Yu, Schulten, Lilley & Ha, “Fluorescence-force spectroscopy maps two-dimensional reaction landscape of the Holliday junction,” *Science* 318 (2007), DOI [10.1126/science.1146113](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1146113). Full article PDF: [Illinois course-hosted copy](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys498bio/sp2010/Ha_Science_2007.pdf). Relevant source spans: PDF pp. 1–2, printed pp. 279–281; hybrid force/FRET at 100-Hz bandwidth (PDF p. 1); piezo sample motion, long-linker force damping, and beam tracking to maintain excitation/detection (p. 1); force-conditioned populations and rates, 10-s traces at 10-ms integration, one to five cycles before bleaching (p. 2); state-rate slopes and transition-state distances averaged across five molecules (p. 2); Table 1 Dxeq=4.4±0.8 nm for XR, with errors explicitly SD across five molecules (p. 4). The article uses HMM-idealized trajectories and force-conditioned transition-rate fits. It demonstrates a real mechanical input and force-dependent rate/population changes. It does not report endpoint AB/BA order randomization, force-ramp latency, repeated-loop calibration, photon totals, or a force-history-independent terminal photon kernel.

2. Wisna et al., “Multi-axial DNA origami force spectroscopy reveals hidden dynamics of Holliday junctions,” *Nature Communications* (2025), [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12259136/), PMID 40661366. Lines 133–139 of the source report MAESTRO applying up to 9 pN from four directions, HJ tests, and T7 endonuclease cleavage effects. Lines 162–179 describe the 45-nm origami, entropic-spring force control, HJ FRET levels around 0.6 and 0.25, BNP-FRET analysis, and force validation. Lines 181–190 report five kinetic classes and a 50-s observation window; lines 194–196 report 151–360 molecules per single-axis force condition and 141 under a multi-axial condition. Methods lines 298–300 report alternating excitation, 20-ms exposures per colour, 40-ms effective bins, and 5,000 frames per field. Lines 204–215 report multi-axial kinetic slowing, intermediate states, and longer intermediate lifetimes. This source is a strong heterogeneity/multistate warning and a current parallel assay precedent. Its DNA-origami springs set static force geometries; it does not establish time-programmable A/B force loops in that high-throughput platform.

3. Hartmann et al., “An automated single-molecule FRET platform for high-content, multiwell plate screening of biomolecular conformations and dynamics,” *Nature Communications* 14, 6511 (2023), [article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42232-3). The authors demonstrate automated 96-well smFRET and a range of molecule/ligand assays (article lines 44–46, 79–82, 87–96). This supports high-throughput sampling of static experimental conditions; it is not a force spectroscopy implementation and does not establish dynamic order-pulse capability.

4. Kramm et al., “DNA Origami-Based Single-Molecule Force Spectroscopy and Applications,” *Methods in Molecular Biology* 2694 (2024), DOI [10.1007/978-1-0716-3377-9_23](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-0716-3377-9_23). The [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37824019/) states that force-dependent protein–DNA interactions can be studied on thousands of molecules in parallel using confocal smFRET. This is a methods chapter and the cited record is an abstract, not a dynamic-loop demonstration. It supports sample throughput for force conditions, not the temporal pulse protocol proposed here.

## Mechanism prior art and comparator boundary

5. Hohng et al. already fit force-dependent HJ transition rates with hidden Markov idealization. The proposed endpoint observable must therefore be judged against a full photon-arrival HMM on the same force-pulse records, with unknown emissions included and the same photon/time/force budget. The endpoint statistic is a coarsening and cannot have more information than the full record.

6. Prior force pulse protocols exist. For example, Wiita et al., “Force-dependent chemical kinetics of disulfide bond reduction observed with single-molecule techniques,” *PNAS* (2006), [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1464324/), used sequential force pulses to separate unfolding and disulfide reduction and fit force-dependent kinetics. Thus generic force-pulse kinetics is not novel. This source uses extension fingerprints and force-clamp trajectories, not the initial-state-independent, same-endpoint AB/BA contrast with an unknown read channel derived here.

7. The active-vs-passive comparison in PROOF.md is relative to the internal c4 conditional theorem at `work/agents/c4_hidden_acquisition/no_freeze_full_likelihood/PROOF.md`. That theorem assumes a known weak categorical reporter, uniform initial law, and every allowed transition kernel doubly stochastic with Dobrushin coefficient bounded by fixed r<1. Its `kappa^-4` result does not apply once force changes the two-state stationary occupancy. The external Hohng source qualitatively supports force-dependent population shifts, but no physical assay is shown to satisfy the weak-reporter model or the c4 kernel class.

## Unresolved source-to-lab quantities

The cited force-FRET papers do not provide a common data package containing applied-force waveforms during transitions, ramp/settling impulse responses for AB/BA loops, actual terminal donor/acceptor photon-arrival records, per-endpoint background laws, per-molecule order-loop curvature signs, or equal-photon/order-randomized controls. Hohng's transition rates at exact proposed plateau forces must be recalibrated in the target setup. The logistic equilibrium estimate and sample sizes in the protocol script are planning calculations only.
