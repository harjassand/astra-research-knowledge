RESULT
======

For the open spin-1/2 XY chain at beta_L=tau L^2, L=N+1, anisotropy
gamma_L=kappa L^(-a), and saturation detuning lambda L^(-b), the scaled
pairing between every fixed opposite-parity sine-mode pair is of order
L^(1-a). It vanishes for a>1, is finite and nonzero for a=1, and diverges
for a<1. The fixed-mode kinetic/detuning diagonal is order one exactly at
b=2 (and tends to the zero-detuning diagonal for b>2). Thus the compact
z=2 critical BdG experiment is preserved when a>=1 and b>=2; its paired
limit is nontrivial precisely when a=1. For b<2 and nonzero lambda the
field leaves the critical window. Fixed-low-mode pairing balances that
detuning at a=b-1, while for 0<b<2 and lambda>0 the growing XX Fermi shell
has a distinct anisotropy threshold a=b/2. These are exact matrix-element
crossovers, not a proof of a state limit in the divergent regimes.

1. Model, interface, and exact Jordan–Wigner/BdG form
---------------------------------------------------

Assume J,tau>0, an open path, n_i=(1-Z_i)/2, and

 H_L = -J sum_{i=1}^{L-2}[(1+gamma_L)X_iX_{i+1}
                              +(1-gamma_L)Y_iY_{i+1}]
        +(4J-lambda L^(-b)) sum_{i=1}^{L-1} n_i,
 beta_L=tau L^2,
 gamma_L=kappa L^(-a).

The scaling exponents a,b and the bounds on the amplitudes are public; the
amplitudes lambda,kappa are fixed and may be unknown to the encoder. Unless
otherwise stated, kappa !=0. The XY convention is the one in the prior
cycle-1 addendum, so the pair matrix element includes its factor of 2.

Open-chain Jordan–Wigner and the parameter-independent sine modes
q_k=pi k/L, phi_k(i)=sqrt(2/L)sin(q_k i), give the exact scaled operator

 G_L=beta_L H_L=sum_k A_Lk d_k^*d_k
                  +sum_{k<l} K_Lkl(d_k^*d_l^*+d_l d_k),

 A_Lk=tau[4J L^2(1-cos(pi k/L))-lambda L^(2-b)],
 K_Lkl=-2tau J kappa L^(2-a) B_Lkl,

 B_Lkl=0 if k+l is even, and otherwise
 B_Lkl=-(4/L) sin(pi k/L)sin(pi l/L)/(cos(pi k/L)-cos(pi l/L)).

This is an exact finite-L identity. No periodic-chain boundary condition or
independent k,-k pairing has been substituted. The Nambu matrix is
h_L=[[A_L,K_L],[-K_L,-A_L]].

2. Fixed low modes: the z=2 crossover
-------------------------------------

For fixed k, Taylor expansion gives

 A_Lk = tau[2J pi^2 k^2 - lambda L^(2-b) + O(k^4/L^2)].

For fixed opposite-parity k,l, directly expanding the exact sine quotient
gives

 L B_Lkl -> -8kl/(l^2-k^2),
 K_Lkl = (16tau J kappa kl/(l^2-k^2)) L^(1-a)(1+o(1)).

In particular, for (k,l)=(1,2), L B_L12 -> -16/3 and

                 K_L12 = (32/3)tau J kappa L^(1-a)(1+o(1)).       (2.1)

The Nambu compression to the particle-1 / hole-2 coordinates therefore
has an off-diagonal entry with exactly this magnitude. Equivalently, the
matrix elements of G_L between the vacuum and the two-particle vector
d_1^*d_2^*|vac> have this value. For L>3 that two-dimensional Fock
compression is not an invariant subspace: other pair terms also leave the
vacuum. We use (2.1) only as an exact low-mode matrix-element diagnostic,
not as an isolated two-mode Gibbs calculation.

The exponents are now immediate:

  a>1: fixed-mode scaled pairing -> 0;
  a=1: fixed-mode scaled pairing -> finite nonzero continuum kernel;
  a<1: fixed-mode scaled pairing diverges in the z=2 units.

For detuning, b=2 gives the finite unknown field lambda; b>2 sends the
effective field to zero; b<2 sends |lambda_eff|=|lambda|L^(2-b) to
infinity for fixed nonzero lambda. The simultaneous finite, nontrivial
critical experiment is therefore a=1,b=2. At a>1,b=2 it reduces to the
XX product limit. At a=1,b>2 it is the zero-detuning paired limit. At
a>1,b>2 it is the zero-detuning XX limit.

3. Exact occupation estimate and what it does certify
-----------------------------------------------------

The prior open-XY proof's effective compact parameters become

 lambda_eff(L)=lambda L^(2-b),   kappa_eff(L)=kappa L^(1-a).

If a>=1 and b>=2, then |lambda_eff|<=|lambda| and
|kappa_eff|<=|kappa|. The exact residual spectral-measure argument from
the prior proof applies uniformly. With B0>=|lambda| and K0>=|kappa|,
k0=max(1,ceil(sqrt(B0/(4J)))), and k>=k0, it gives

 Tr(rho_L d_k^*d_k) <= 4pi^2 kappa^2 L^(2(1-a))/k^2
                              + exp(-2tau J k^2).               (3.1)

Thus for M>=max(1,k0-1),

 Tr(rho_L sum_{k>M}d_k^*d_k)
   <= 4pi^2 kappa^2 L^(2(1-a))/M + sum_{k>M}exp(-2tau J k^2),

uniformly in L and the unknown amplitudes in the supplied compact sets.
This makes the common-Fock cutoff uniform. Together with the previous
cycle's common-core/covariance argument, it transfers the internally
proved candidate full trace-norm limit to the effective parameters above.
No new finite-L rate follows from that theorem.

If a<1 or b<2, respectively kappa_eff or lambda_eff escapes every fixed
compact set. In particular (3.1) no longer gives a tail uniform in L at a
fixed M. This does NOT prove that the finite-L states have no limit: a
separate rescaling, energy subtraction, or a new occupation estimate might
still yield a different limiting experiment. It does prove that the prior
compact critical theorem cannot simply be invoked in those regimes.

4. Detuning crossovers outside the critical window
-------------------------------------------------

Let lambda be fixed and nonzero and b<2. For fixed k,l, the detuning in
A_L is order L^(2-b), while K_Lkl is order L^(1-a). Their ratio has order

                       L^(b-a-1).                              (4.1)

Consequently fixed-low-mode pair mixing is subleading if a>b-1,
comparable if a=b-1, and dominant in the compressed BdG matrix if a<b-1.
This fixed-mode comparison does not decide all occupations when the number
of modes in the detuned band grows.

For the particularly informative range 0<b<2 and lambda>0, the XX
dispersion predicts a growing but subextensive low-momentum shell with

 k_F ~ sqrt(lambda/(2J pi^2)) L^(1-b/2),
 q_F=k_F pi/L ->0.

For adjacent opposite-parity modes k,k+1 with 1<<k<<L, expansion of the
exact quotient yields

 L B_L,k,k+1 ~ -4k,
 K_L,k,k+1 ~ 8tau J kappa L^(1-a)k,
 A_Lk (kinetic scale at k_F) ~ 2tau J pi^2 k_F^2.

The pairing-to-kinetic ratio on that shell is therefore proportional to

                       L^(b/2-a).                              (4.2)

So the shell-scale pairing vanishes for a>b/2, is order one at a=b/2,
and dominates the XX shell scale for a<b/2. The fixed-low-mode threshold
(4.1) and the moving-shell threshold (4.2) answer different questions;
they should not be collapsed into one line. Equation (4.2) is a comparison
to the XX shell scale, not an occupation theorem for the paired state.

For lambda<0, the detuning suppresses low particles rather than creating
an XX Fermi shell; (4.1) remains the exact fixed-mode comparison, but
(4.2) is not applicable. At b<=0 a finite or increasing physical field
shift is outside the z=2 window as well, and the 0<b<2 shell asymptotic
must not be extrapolated to that endpoint.

5. Unknown amplitudes, acquisition, costs, and comparator
---------------------------------------------------------

The regime a>=1,b>=2 retains bounded effective parameters even when
lambda,kappa are unknown. The prior theorem uses a fixed sine-mode
embedding and its tail bounds without estimating either amplitude. It
therefore gives a parameter-blind quantum archive upper bound in this
compact regime; it does not provide efficient preparation of the thermal
input, efficient output synthesis, or a lower bound on optimal memory.
For a>1, kappa_eff tends to zero, so the exact XX number-sector code is
available asymptotically (and for gamma=0 exactly). For a=1 and nonzero
kappa the XX number-count decoder loses vacuum/two-particle coherence;
the exact N=2 witness is in the prior anisotropy addendum. The paired
parameter-blind Fock archive is the strongest currently proved same-input
baseline in this workspace.

A parameter-aware quasiparticle basis/decoder is a useful stronger
algorithmic comparator only if it is explicitly charged for acquiring
lambda and kappa from the one supplied unknown state. No such acquisition
protocol, sample budget, or finite-precision cost is included here. When
a<1 or b<2, the old compact-parameter archive proof does not apply and no
new memory or thermal-preparation cost is certified. Retaining the full N
spins is an exact trivial comparator; no optimal compression claim is
made.

Costs charged by this audit: exact symbolic derivation and a small SymPy
replay only. There is no hidden numerical diagonalization, acquisition,
state preparation, or gate synthesis. The derivation uses O(1) exact
symbolic operations; the replay runs in under a second in the recorded
local environment. The analytical bounds depend on public J,tau and
compact amplitude bounds; they have no per-input oracle cost.

6. Falsifiers, proof gaps, and status
------------------------------------

Falsifier for the fixed-mode exponents: an error in the exact finite-L
sine pairing kernel, or an incorrect nonzero limit of L B_L12. The replay
checks the general fixed-(k,l) limit symbolically and the exact (1,2)
constant. Falsifier for (3.1): a failure of the prior residual estimate
for h_L or loss of compactness in effective parameters; the latter is
explicitly excluded by a>=1,b>=2.

Open proof gap: for a<1 or b<2, these matrix-element comparisons alone do
not prove a limit or nonconvergence of the normalized many-body Gibbs
states, nor do they prove a diverging particle count. The occupation bound
(3.1) loses uniformity exactly when the effective coefficients diverge.
Closing this requires a new state-level occupation/free-energy estimate
or an exact BdG spectrum/covariance analysis in the moving scales. In
particular, (4.2) identifies where a changed shell experiment should be
expected, but does not establish its limiting covariance.
