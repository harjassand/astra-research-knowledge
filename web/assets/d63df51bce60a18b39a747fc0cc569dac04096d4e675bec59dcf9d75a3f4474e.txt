POSITIVE CONTROL — COMPONENTWISE COMPLEMENT REMOVES THE TWO-BLOCK CUT

For F12=diag(F6,F6), the two 6x6 blocks are explicit connected components of the bipartite support graph of F12. In the fixed k=6 sector every positive basis has three selected row-lines in each block. Define R1 to complement I inside block 1 and R2 to complement I inside block 2. Each is an involution on the positive support and its exact Metropolis ratio is computable from the two corresponding block determinants. For the four heavy states, each individual block flip has equal source/target weight and acceptance 1.

Let R_block choose R1 or R2 with probability 1/2, and set Q_block=(M+R_block)/2. From either mode in S={OO,EE}, R_block flips exactly one of the two mode bits and therefore leaves S with probability 1. Hence Q_block(S,S^c)>=1/2, independent of epsilon. The positive-support one-coordinate M component keeps the full chain irreducible. The pair chain (Swap+Q_block,A+Q_block,B)/3 has a constant energy contribution on f(A)+f(B); the epsilon^2 bottleneck is removed.

The pair chain is P_block=(Swap+Q_block^(A)+Q_block^(B))/3, where Q_block^(A) updates only replica A and Q_block^(B) only replica B. This is an explicit repair for the displayed block-diagonal family, not a general algorithm. The support graph makes the two blocks detectable in this example. The unresolved general gate is to acquire polynomial-cost local cycle or mismatch moves for arbitrary rational F, prove their exact reversible proposal law, and bound their energy/congestion without needing unknown hole coefficients. The counterexample therefore isolates the failure of a single global complement, while also identifying the more specific update that bypasses it here.
