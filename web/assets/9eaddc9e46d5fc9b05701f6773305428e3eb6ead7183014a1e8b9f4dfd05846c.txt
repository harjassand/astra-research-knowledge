STRONGEST CLAIM

A finite-net family of uniformly sandwich-bounded states admits an explicit finite-outcome classical compression channel from either (i) a corrected broadcaster's finitely many promised likelihood states, using a recent normalized-Hilbert-Schmidt simultaneous-commuting theorem, or (ii) directly, a supplied bistochastic instrument and its canonical measurement reconstruction, with a better residual rate. This avoids rounding the whole CP range of the broadcaster marginal. The outcome count is bounded by the family net size and likelihood-ratio precision, independent of Hilbert-space dimension and of the original instrument alphabet. The result does NOT settle the global bounded-Holevo conjecture or the missing full CP-range lifting theorem.

1. FINITE-ALPHABET COARSENING LEMMA (INDEPENDENT DERIVATION)

Let E be a finite-dimensional state family with faithful reference sigma in its convex domain and
  (1-theta)sigma <= rho <= (1+theta)sigma,  0<theta<1,
for every rho in E. Let {rho_i}_{i=1}^N be an internal trace-distance net: for every rho in E there is i with T(rho,rho_i)=||rho-rho_i||_1/2<=a. Suppose an entanglement-breaking channel has a finite measure-and-prepare representation
  Lambda(rho)=sum_y Tr(M_y rho) omega_y,
with POVM M_y and density operators omega_y. Write p_0(y)=Tr(M_y sigma), p_i(y)=Tr(M_y rho_i). If p_0(y)=0 then p_i(y)=0 by the sandwich; omit such outcomes. Otherwise q_i(y)=p_i(y)/p_0(y) lies in [1-theta,1+theta].

Partition the outcome labels y by the N-vector (q_1(y),...,q_N(y)), quantizing each coordinate with a fixed grid of width delta>0. At most
  K^N outcomes remain, K=ceil(2 theta/delta)+1.
For a cell J define M_J=sum_{y in J}M_y and
  omega_J=sum_{y in J}p_0(y)omega_y / p_0(J).
The coarse channel Lambda_c(rho)=sum_J Tr(M_J rho)omega_J is EB. For a net state rho_i, put qbar_i(J)=p_i(J)/p_0(J). Direct subtraction gives
  Lambda(rho_i)-Lambda_c(rho_i)
    =sum_J sum_{y in J} p_0(y)(q_i(y)-qbar_i(J))omega_y.
Within each cell q_i(y) and its p_0-weighted mean qbar_i(J) lie in one interval of width delta. The trace norm is therefore at most sum_J delta p_0(J)=delta, so T(Lambda rho_i,Lambda_c rho_i)<=delta/2. For arbitrary rho within a of rho_i, channel contractivity and the triangle inequality give
  sup_{rho in E} T(Lambda_c rho,rho) <= sup_E T(Lambda rho,rho) + 2a + delta/2.
Thus fixed-length outcome messages use at most ceil(N log2 K) bits. This bound charges only the transmitted outcome. It does not bound the cost of discovering the net, storing/implementing M_J, or preparing omega_J.

2. GENERAL FINITE-NET ROUNDING ROUTE

Use the tracial full-matrix interface sigma=I/d and
  rho_i=(I+theta A_i)/d, A_i=A_i*, Tr(A_i)=0, ||A_i||<=1.
Let Phi be a positive, trace-preserving, selfadjoint UCP marginal of a symmetric two-output broadcaster, and set b=max_i ||Phi(rho_i)-rho_i||_1 (full trace norm; T has the factor 1/2). The internally derived Kadison/Stinespring estimate in work/cycle3/sandwich_broadcast_algebra.txt, equations (13)-(14), gives
  max_{i,j} ||[A_i,A_j]||_(2,tau) <= c0 sqrt(b/theta),
  c0=4+2 sqrt(2), tau=Tr/d.

Primary-source theorem used: Bansil--Kachkovskiy, arXiv:2608.31000v1, Corollary 1.4. For any N selfadjoint contractions, there are mutually commuting selfadjoint contractions A_i' with
  sum_i ||A_i-A_i'||_(2,tau)^2
    <= C(N) max_{i,j}||[A_i,A_j]||_(2,tau)^(2/3^(N-1)),
where C(N) depends only on N. The source's two-matrix exponent 1/3 is sharp under its stated constraint; the N-tuple exponent and C(N) are not dimension dependent but deteriorate with N.

Choose a common eigenbasis for the A_i' and let D be rank-one dephasing in that basis. It is a full-domain EB channel and fixes every A_i'. Since D is an L2(tau) contraction,
  ||A_i-D(A_i)||_(2,tau) <= 2||A_i-A_i'||_(2,tau).
Consequently each net state has
  T(D rho_i,rho_i) <= theta ||A_i-A_i'||_(2,tau)
  <= theta sqrt(C(N)) (c0 sqrt(b/theta))^(1/3^(N-1)).
Apply the coarsening lemma above to D and the same N states. For any delta>0 this yields an EB channel with at most K^N outcomes and
  sup_E T(Lambda_c rho,rho)
    <= 2a + delta/2
       + theta sqrt(C(N)) (c0 sqrt(b/theta))^(1/3^(N-1)).       (A)
This is an explicit finite-task implication of approximate broadcasting to a classical code with outcome cost independent of d. At fixed N, theta, and a, its net error vanishes as b->0. Taking a,delta->0 gives vanishing error for a sequence whose covering numbers stay bounded at each fixed accuracy. When N grows, the exponent collapses and this bound gives no uniform conclusion.

3. STRONGER EXACT SUBCLASS FROM SUPPLIED INSTRUMENT STRUCTURE

Let K_y be a finite Kraus family on M_d with sum_y K_y*K_y=sum_y K_y K_y*=I. Set L(A)=sum_y K_y A K_y*, M_y=K_y*K_y, p_y=tau(M_y), and
  Psi(A)=sum_y tau(M_y A) M_y/p_y,
  C=(L+Psi)/2,  Phi=C*C.
All formulas are on the full matrix algebra. The physical instrument-append map
  B_K(rho)=sum_y K_y rho K_y* tensor [sigma M_y/p_y]
is trace preserving; averaging it with its receiver swap gives common marginal C. Applying the tracial Petz adjoint C* to both outputs yields the actual broadcaster with common marginal Phi. The exact identity
  2(I-Phi)-(I-Psi)
    =(I-L*L)+(Psi-Psi^2)+(L-Psi)*(L-Psi)/2 >=0
uses L*L<=I and 0<=Psi<=I; hence I-Psi<=2(I-Phi). This is an exact SOS for this supplied-instrument class, not a representation theorem for all compatible channels.

For rho=sigma(I+A), ||A||<=theta and tau(A)=0, let b=sup_E ||Phi(rho)-rho||_1. Dirichlet energy obeys
  <A,(I-Phi)A>_tau = tau(A(A-Phi A)) <= theta ||A-Phi A||_(1,tau) <= theta b.
Since 0<=Psi<=I, (I-Psi)^2<=I-Psi, so
  ||A-Psi A||_(2,tau)^2 <= <A,(I-Psi)A>_tau <= 2 theta b.
The physical EB reconstruction error is at most (1/2)||A-Psi A||_(1,tau), hence
  sup_E T(Psi rho,rho) <= (1/2)sqrt(2 theta b).
Coarsen Psi using the same net to obtain at most K^N classical outcomes and final error
  sup_E T(Psi_c rho,rho) <= 2a + delta/2 + (1/2)sqrt(2 theta b).   (B)
This route has no bad N-dependent residual exponent. It still requires the actual bistochastic Kraus interface and the state sandwich. The channel and decoder state-description costs remain unbounded by these inequalities.

4. OUTWARD SEARCH AND WHAT IT DOES NOT CLOSE

The decisive external bridge is Bansil--Kachkovskiy (2026), arXiv:2608.31000v1, Corollary 1.4: normalized-Hilbert-Schmidt almost-commuting finite tuples admit commuting approximants, but the exponent is 2/3^(N-1) at the squared-error level. The proof is for a selected finite tuple, not a linear/CP map on its whole operator-norm ball. Applying it to N=d^2 map outputs would not give a dimension-free rate. This supports replacing whole-CP-range rounding by a finite scientific task/net, but does not validate full map lifting.
