# Source notes: molecular cadence theorem

Sources were checked for the specific theorem interface and prior-art boundary, not merely for title-level relevance.

## Reaction-network diffusion identifiability

Faul, Louis; Linard Hoessly; Panqiu Xia. “Identifiability of SDEs for reaction networks.” *European Journal of Applied Mathematics* (first view, 2026), DOI [10.1017/S0956792526100382](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382), [Cambridge article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-applied-mathematics/article/identifiability-of-sdes-for-reaction-networks/7D2B84958974B0400D0DAC737FF823F2), [arXiv:2505.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07638).

- Read scope: Cambridge article search extract, including its abstract, Remark 3.5, Theorem 3.3 summary, and conclusion.
- Source claim: for a fixed source complex, the reaction-rate contribution to the CLE appears through pairs consisting of reaction displacement and its outer product; Theorem 3.3 characterizes rate identifiability by linear independence of the corresponding family. The article also reports distinct networks with identical diffusion laws.
- Exact connection used here: the four zero-order scalar displacements \(j=1,2,3,4\) map to \((j,j^2)\in\mathbb R^2\). The contrast \((-1,3,-3,1)\) annihilates both coordinates and is a null direction. It additionally annihilates the total-rate coordinate and has nonzero third mark moment.
- Boundary: the paper does not provide a finite-cadence endpoint-count testing limit. It assumes the reaction-network/CLE interface described by its theorem; our endpoint model is separately specified.

## Compound-Poisson sampling-scale prior art

Duval, Céline; Marc Hoffmann. “Statistical inference across time scales.” *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 5 (2011), 2004–2030. DOI [10.1214/11-EJS660](https://doi.org/10.1214/11-EJS660); [arXiv:1106.1031](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.1031).

- Read scope: full arXiv PDF. Theorems 2.2, 2.4, 2.5 establish LAN/information transitions for the paper’s one-parameter symmetric-Bernoulli compound-Poisson model. Section 3, “Extension to more general compound laws,” discusses a general known lattice jump law, then says analogous results could presumably be obtained if the required small/large-argument asymptotic expansions were available.
- Boundary: the paper establishes the broad cadence-information mechanism. The inspected text does not state a fixed-pair testing law whose exponent is indexed by the first differing cumulant. Its arbitrary-law extension is presented as a route needing expansions, not as the general-\(k\) theorem here.

Duval, Céline. “When is it no longer possible to estimate a compound Poisson process?” *Electronic Journal of Statistics* 8 (2014), 274–301. DOI [10.1214/14-EJS885](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-EJS885).

- Read scope: source abstract and an accessible full-text copy of the preprint abstract/introduction. It studies centered finite-variance compound-Poisson observations over a growing horizon with macroscopic sampling; it reports a parametric Fisher-information example, a no-consistency lower bound beyond the square-root sampling scale, and asymptotic equivalence to Gaussian observations in specified regimes.
- Rescaling: \(s=Vt\) gives horizon \(S=VT\) and cadence \(d=V\Delta\); the square-root relation \(d\asymp\sqrt S\) becomes \(\Delta\asymp\sqrt{T/V}\).
- Boundary: this settles prior art for the broad \(k=2\) exponent and Gaussian information loss. The article’s detailed parametric example was not reconstructed theorem-by-theorem in this work, so exact overlap with the fixed-pair coefficient remains unresolved. No novelty or priority claim is made.

Petrov, V. V. “Local Limit Theorems for Non-Identical Lattice Distributions.” *Theory of Probability and its Applications* 7(3) (1962), 333–335. DOI [10.1137/1107033](https://doi.org/10.1137/1107033).

- Use: classical local lattice Edgeworth and local-limit methodology for the central expansion. These citations do not establish the pair-specific tilted \(p_1^2/p_0\) tail bound; that is separately derived in PROOF.md section 3.
