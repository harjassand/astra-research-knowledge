Independent dichotomy and norm-band audit

Conclusion: the binary geometric dichotomy is sound conditional on the supplied
outer-balance proposition. Its geometry is colour-independent. A Q-colour
extension with t occurrences of every colour needs Q-balanced outer tests and
full-support sparse shifts on the existing literal keys. No deterministic
norm-band residue coverage follows from the source; a concrete exceptional AP
defeats that proposed coverage. This is an audit/extension derivation, not a
claim that family160 already proves the Q-colour statement.

Source paths below are relative to sources/build/sections/.

1. Geometry audit

* 05-dichotomy.tex:11-31: real representatives in a box of coordinate width
  <=2H satisfy A+jB on their occurrence indices: the integer triple-affinity
  defect has coordinate absolute value <=2H(j3-j1)<1. No colour assumption.
* 05:37-80: a heavy full label (multiplicity m*>k/M) supplies closest return
  h<=2M and |u_i|<=2MH_x/k, |v_i|<=4MH/k, with v=lambda*u as real vectors.
* 05:83-136: rational path x_j=x_0+j(t+u)/h and
  y_j=y_0+j(lambda*t+v)/h; gcd(t_1,...,t_D,h)=1. Incongruent indices modulo h
  have different full labels. The convex-box gcd argument and first-system
  separation are valid, including the half-open convention.
* 05:139-171: every heavy interval, not only the initial heavy label, has
  |v_i| <= (2M/k)|V(y_i(n_j*))|. The proof uses endpoint congruence and
  |Delta_i-a*v_i|<1; it does not incorrectly assume no intervening cut crossing.
* 05:196-234: h<=h0 implies h|lambda; w=v/h is a q^{-i} lattice step with
  nonzero |w_i|>=q^{-D}>=alpha. If a cut is crossed, all coordinate samples
  have V widths <=2H(k+1)|w_i|<|w_i| and pairwise circle separation >=|w_i|,
  contradicting a heavy label. Thus the whole centered y-path is affine.
  Initial/final exact cut hits are covered by the closed segment argument.
* 05:236-307: for h>h0, each rotating scalar period exceeds h0, and the
  nonregular fraction is <=D(4eta+1/h0)=.004+1/D<1/2. A block containing a
  heavy index is eligible by the relative-width estimate and mesh neighbors;
  that anchor need not be regular. Used blocks cover >=4k/5 positions.

Replace the binary outer-test requirement (04-outer.tex:68-78) by at least
1/(2Q) of each test's positions in every colour. The unchanged argument gives
at least delta*k/(2Q)=k/(20Q) of each outer colour in the light case
(05:188-194), and >=k/(5Q) of each in the long-period case (05:295-306).
Thus every AP is outer-rich with margin beta*k, beta=1/(20Q), or globally affine.

2. Norm-band facts and the exact deterministic-shell obstruction

The key is (the literal V^D box, floor(||q*centered_y||_2^2)), not merely the
integer band (06-perturbation.tex:15-23). Every key occurs <=4 times on every AP
(06:29-96): a fixed box makes the sampled representatives affine, scaled
points have pairwise distance >=1, and a unit-width squared-norm band meets
each half-line in length <=1. The proof works for prime-power q.

This is a multiplicity statement, not a statement that an affine path visits
each residue of its integer norm bands. The latter is false for the natural
colour floor(f) mod Q. For integer 2<=Q<=h0, take

    n_j = j*q^(D-1) in Z/q^D Z, 0<=j<k.

The dilation lemma gives Q|lambda (02-setup.tex:33-41). For q>8*lambda*k^3,
which holds at the original scales, all full labels are the same:
first coordinates are zero except x_D=j/q, contained in [0,H/lambda), while
second coordinates are zero except centered_y_D=lambda*j/q, contained in
the V interval to the right of zero. That interval has width >=H/8, either
from 02:175-197 at zero or directly from the exponential mesh. Hence c0 is
constant on this entire AP. It has a global affine lift, and

    f(n_j)=lambda^2*j^2,
    floor(f(n_j)) mod Q = 0 for every j.

Thus outer labels combined with this deterministic shell-residue rule still
miss Q-1 colours on an actual exceptional affine AP. The AP is nonzero-step
and distinct because q>=P>k. A different deterministic shell assignment would
require a new uniform coverage lemma; source multiplicity supplies none.

3. A sufficient sparse perturbation, with quantitative bounds

For each literal key independently choose E in Z_Q with
P(E=0)=1-p and P(E=e)=p/(Q-1) for every e!=0; set C(n)=c0(n)+E_key mod Q.
The original Bernoulli bit modulo Q lacks full support when Q>2.

Rich case: for a target a choose s=ceil(beta*k/4) distinct keys, each carrying
one chosen base-a position. This is possible by key multiplicity <=4. If a
occurs fewer than t times finally, fewer than t of those chosen keys have
E=0. For 1<=t<=s/2, therefore

    P(fewer than t occurrences of a) <= 2^s * p^(s-t+1)
                                      <= 2^s * p^(s/2),
    R <= Q*q^(2D)*2^s*p^(s/2).

The selected keys are fixed before sampling; no 2^k subset factor is needed.

Affine case: choose one fixed representative from every distinct key of a
literal signature. There are >=k/4, and each independently hits target a
with probability >=r=p/(Q-1), assuming p<=1/2. Thus their hit count dominates
Bin(ceil(k/4),r). For t<=pk/[8(Q-1)], a standard elementary Chernoff bound gives

    A <= Q*T_aff*exp(-pk/[32(Q-1)]).

The literal-signature count remains exactly the same: fixed Q-valued outer
colours are determined by the full-label word. Source 06:120-184 gives

    log T_aff <= O(D^3 log k) + 6 log q + O(log k).

Therefore the precise perturbation entropy requirement is
log(Q*T_aff)=o(pk/Q), together with the rich union-bound inequality above.

For p=k^{-eps} and q chosen at k^{c*k/(Q*D)},
log R <= (2c-eps/160)*(k/Q)*log k + lower-order terms, using beta=1/(20Q).
Any c<eps/320 suffices. Keeping the original N exponent c*k independent of Q
does not support unbounded Q: its AP-count cost eventually exceeds the rich
failure exponent. Scaling the exponent by 1/Q fixes that particular obstacle.

With D=k^a, M=k^b, Q<=k^rho, sufficient exponent conditions are

    3a+rho < b < 1,  rho < a,  eps < a,
    3a+eps+rho < 1.

The last condition is needed for the global-label contribution to affine
entropy; it is not implied by the first three. Q-balancing the source's outer
LLL additionally uses tails <=Q*exp(-l/(8Q)): its first-family entropy requires
M/Q >> D^3 log k, and its local incident entropy requires D/Q >> log k.
These are the first two displayed exponent conditions. The original choices
a=.1, b=.5, eps=.05 work for every fixed rho<.1 after scaling q's exponent by
1/Q. They give the sufficient robust target t<=floor(k^.95/[8(Q-1)]) for large k.

The current sparse mechanism naturally supplies t at scale pk/Q. A claim of
t proportional to k/Q at the same k^(c*k/Q) group size would need a different
argument: constant perturbation density loses the log k gained by rare flips
in the rich-case union bound. This is a limitation of this proof mechanism,
not an impossibility theorem for stronger colourings.
