# Cycle 2: acquiring a recurrent predictive state from persistent readouts

**Result.** For a concrete O(d)-state process with a hidden parity bit that persists and is read out repeatedly with independent noise, passive trajectories acquire a two-coordinate predictive feature map. The learner first recovers the unknown parity mask from repeated readouts and a GF(2) system; it then deploys a one-bit parity recurrence that updates online without storing the observed history. Every bounded future task has a norm-bounded two-dimensional linear readout. The construction is teacher-free and does not assume the feature map or mask is supplied.

This is a useful positive subclass and a boundary on the negative result in [the original report](FINAL_REPORT.md): persistence supplies independent measurements of the same latent bit. It does not make a claim about arbitrary finite-state processes. The direct majority/GF(2) decoder is the equal-data baseline and produces the same model at the same cost, so this is a feature-acquisition certificate, not an advantage claim. Historical novelty has not been cleared.

## Process and acquired representation

Let d≥1, let s∈GF(2)^d be unknown, and let η∈[0,1/2) be known rational with θ=1−2η≥β>0. A reset episode of finite horizon H=d+R emits

\[
A_1,\ldots,A_d\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2),\qquad
Z=s\cdot A\pmod 2,\qquad
B_j=Z\oplus E_j,\quad E_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\eta),\quad 1\le j\le R.
\]

Write \(Y=(A_1,\ldots,A_d,B_1,\ldots,B_R)\) for the observed length-H trajectory.

The input bits are fresh on each reset. The readout errors are independent **conditional on the same persistent Z**. A finite-state realization keeps a clock and running parity through the input phase, then retains the parity while emitting noisy readouts; an edge-to-state HMM lift has O(d) hidden states. The known process family contains no teacher, chosen-prefix access, hidden-state observation, or feature map.

After acquisition, define the two-coordinate feature at any prefix h of length \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,H\}\) by

\[
\phi_\ell(h)=e_{p_\ell},\qquad p_\ell=\sum_{i\le\min(\ell,d)}s_iA_i\pmod 2,\qquad e_0=(1,0)^\top,\ e_1=(0,1)^\top.
\]

After all inputs, p_t=Z and every readout leaves this feature unchanged. This is computed by an online update: on input A_i, swap the two coordinates iff s_iA_i=1; on a readout token, do nothing. The stream retains one parity bit plus an index t (O(log H) bits); the acquired model stores d mask bits. It never needs the complete prefix. The update schedule is time dependent through the learned mask bits, all of which are charged in the model.

At each fixed cut \(\ell\) and for every future task g with 0≤g≤1, define

\[
w_{\ell,g}(p)=\mathbb E[g(Y_{\ell+1:H})\mid p_\ell=p],\qquad p\in\{0,1\},
\]

Since s and η are now known, these two expectations are taken under an explicit finite generative model. For every source history h at cut t,

\[
\mathbb E[g\mid h]=\phi_\ell(h)^\top w_{\ell,g},\qquad
\|\phi_t(h)\|_2=1,\qquad \|w_{t,g}\|_2\le\sqrt2.
\]

The factorization is exact and simultaneous over every bounded future task: conditional futures depend on the observed history only through public phase ℓ and current parity p_ℓ. The feature state itself is acquired from samples; it is not a renamed teacher-supplied feature bank.

For an efficiently evaluable task g, the readout can be approximated from the acquired model rather than queried from the source. At a fixed cut ℓ, generate M independent futures from each of the two parity states, evaluate g, and average. Hoeffding gives both coefficients within ρ with probability at least 1−δ when

\[
M\ge \left\lceil\frac{1}{2\rho^2}\log\frac{8}{\delta}\right\rceil.
\]

The cost for one cut/task pair is at most 2M generated futures, O(MH) sampler operations plus the cost of evaluating g on those futures, and O(H+workspace(g)) temporary bits if one future is streamed. The resulting prediction error is at most ρ on every history at that cut, hence MSE≤ρ². For J requested cut/task pairs, replace δ by δ/J in M and multiply the readout cost by J. This does not claim a finite uniform estimate for an uncountable task class; it gives an exact two-dimensional representation and a charged on-demand readout for each supplied evaluable task.

## Acquisition guarantee and proof

Choose

\[
N=d+\left\lceil\log_2(2/\delta)\right\rceil,
\]

and an odd integer

\[
R\ge\frac{2}{\beta^2}\log\frac{2N}{\delta}.
\]

Collect N independent reset episodes, each with d inputs and R readouts. For episode i, majority-decode the R readouts into \(\tilde Z_i\). Then solve the linear equations \(A_i\cdot s=\tilde Z_i\) over GF(2). If all majority labels are correct and the N×d input matrix has rank d, this returns the exact unknown s.

For one episode, after multiplying each readout sign by the true sign of Z for analysis, the R independent signs have mean θ≥β. Hoeffding bounds the probability of an incorrect majority by \(e^{-R\beta^2/2}\le\delta/(2N)\). A union bound makes every decoded label correct with probability at least 1−δ/2. The A_i rows are independent and uniform; for each nonzero v, \(\Pr[A_iv=0\ \forall i]=2^{-N}\). Hence rank failure is at most \((2^d-1)2^{-N}<\delta/2\). The mask, recurrence, and readout family are therefore acquired with probability at least 1−δ.

All costs are explicit:

- Source observations: \(N(d+R)=O((d+\log(1/\delta))[d+\beta^{-2}\log((d+\log(1/\delta))/\delta)])\) symbols.
- Computation: O(NR) bit work to decode plus O(Nd²) conservative Gaussian-elimination work over GF(2). Incremental elimination stores O(d²) basis bits; stream buffers and counters add O(d+R) bits.
- Deployed model: d mask bits and the rational η encoding (Lη bits). The online state is one parity bit and a position counter, with O(1) bit updates per token. Each readout probability takes O(1) rational operations on O(Lη)-bit parameters; a complete word probability uses O(H) rational operations and outputs O(HLη+d) bits. Its bit-time is O(H·Mul(HLη)), where Mul(b) denotes b-bit integer multiplication cost. Model storage is O(d+Lη+log H) bits.
- Readout: for a supplied bounded evaluator g and one cut, 2M model continuations cost O(MH) Bernoulli draws plus 2M evaluations of g. For η=a/b, a Bernoulli draw uses k=ceil(log₂b) bits per rejection attempt, with acceptance probability at least 1/2 and expected O(Lη) bit cost. Capping at K=ceil(log₂(4MH/δ)) attempts makes the total sampler abort probability at most δ/2 over 2M futures of length at most H, with worst-case O(MH Lη log(MH/δ)) random-bit work. The displayed M makes the Monte Carlo coefficient error fail with probability at most δ/2; adding sampler failure gives total at most δ. Thus the charged readout cost is this sampler work plus 2M·time(g); no additional source observations or teacher calls are used.

No held-out test is needed for the theorem because it assumes membership in this exact process class and the recovery proof already gives a finite-sample success probability. If a deployment wants a data certificate, freeze s and independently estimate each desired two-state readout; those extra samples should be charged separately.

## Equally informed controls

**Same passive data, direct decoder.** Give a baseline the same N raw trajectories, known d and β, and runtime. It can majority-decode each episode and solve the same GF(2) system. This is exactly the mask-recovery step above and outputs the same explicit generator/evaluator and recurrence with identical sample, runtime, and memory bounds. Therefore the feature acquisition has no demonstrated advantage over direct decoding; it demonstrates that after the latent coordinate is learned, an exact bounded recurrent feature realization follows.

**Chosen-prefix conditional-sample control.** A conditional-sampling oracle can be queried at each length-d input prefix a=e_i. In response it returns the R future readouts, all noisy copies of s_i. Majority over these R bits recovers each s_i; d chosen-prefix queries and R≥2β⁻² log(2d/δ) make all coordinates correct except with probability δ. This costs d queries, dR returned readout symbols, and d² bits to specify the prefixes, then O(dR) majority work. The conditional oracle avoids random design and Gaussian elimination because it can force low-probability prefixes; that is a stronger interface and not a passive-data baseline. Under the source's natural distribution, any one of these prefixes has probability 2^-d.

**Fixed-feature control.** A two-state parity feature is not available to a fixed-map learner unless s is supplied. Crediting that map without charging the N(d+R) observations and recovery computation would erase the acquisition problem. Here the recovered mask is the concrete certificate that licenses the feature map.

## Scope and relation to the general gap

The persistent-readout assumption is load-bearing. Without independent repeated observations of the same Z, the majority step is unavailable; repeating one already-noisy label does not create more information. The theorem handles one finite-state subclass and is compatible with computational hardness for the one-shot noisy-parity process. It does not solve fresh-prefix feature acquisition for arbitrary HMMs, arbitrary low-rank laws, or A06's KL-misspecified teacher service.

This recurrence is a two-dimensional predictive-state representation with acquired update operators. PSR/spectral prior art already uses predictions of future tests as state coordinates. The concrete result here is the full interface-matched construction and the same-data control; no historical priority claim is made. The one-shot versus persistent-readout process distinction and a related direct decoder were also recorded in the pinned Astra N13 page (lines 97–140, 186–193); this report independently derives the recovery and recurrence bounds above and does not count that earlier page as external validation.

## Source and evidence status

- The recovery theorem follows directly from Hoeffding, a union bound, and the uniform random-matrix rank bound; no simulation evidence is required for its proof.
- The finite-state realization and recurrence are explicit constructions above. Conversion constants depend on whether the chosen convention is edge-emitting or state-emitting; all are O(d).
- The conditional-query comparator is consistent with the oracle definition in [Liu–Moitra, Definition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07536) and the earlier conditional-sample interface in [Mahajan et al., Definitions 3–4 and Theorem 2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/mahajan23a/mahajan23a.pdf). These are stronger interfaces, not data controls for the passive algorithm.
- No claim of novelty, arbitrary-HMM coverage, or advantage over the direct decoder is supported.
- The original report's inherited rare-prefix result remains unverified and is not used here.
