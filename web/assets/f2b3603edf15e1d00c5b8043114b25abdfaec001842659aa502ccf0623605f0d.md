# Cycle 2 proof details

## 1. Rank and finite-state realization

At cut t, a history h=((x₁,z₁),…,(x_t,z_t)) determines

a_t(s,h)=1{x₁…x_t=s₁…s_t}.

The z coordinates do not affect this bit. Therefore the conditional-future row P(·|h) is one of at most two rows, corresponding to alive and dead; every cut Hankel matrix has rank at most 2.

Before step i, retain clock i−1 and a_{i−1}. Choose x uniformly, set a_i=a_{i−1}1{x=s_i}, then choose z with probability 3/4 if a_i=1 and 1/4 otherwise. This is an edge-output automaton with two state bits per clock. Lift each edge label (x,z) to a deterministic-emission state containing (i,a_i,x,z), at most eight states per i plus an initial state. Emitting transition weights are products of 1/2 with 1/4 or 3/4, hence 1/8 or 3/8; termination may use a deterministic weight-1 edge. The clock advances deterministically, so the construction is representable by a single time-homogeneous transition matrix on the augmented state space. This gives at most 8T+1 states.

For general marker noise η<1/2, use Ber(1−η) when alive and Ber(η) when dead.

## 2. Conditional-sample feature learner

Assume ŝ₁…ŝ_{k−1}=s₁…s_{k−1}. Query

h_{k−1}=((s₁,1),…,(s_{k−1},1)).

Its target mass is

P_s(h_{k−1})=∏_{i<k} Pr[X_i=s_i] Pr[Z_i=1|A_i=1]=((1−η)/2)^{k−1},

which is positive. Given this history, A_{k−1}=1 regardless of the z observations. Define R=(-1)^{X_k}(Z_k−1/2). When s_k=0, the X_k=0 branch contributes mean (1/2)(1/2−η), and the X_k=1 branch contributes mean (1/2)(1/2−η), so E[R]=1/2−η=(1−2η)/2. The sign reverses for s_k=1. Also R∈[−1/2,1/2], an interval of length 1.

For m independent conditional samples, Hoeffding yields

Pr[sign(R̄) is wrong] ≤ exp(−2m((1−2η)/2)²)=exp(−m(1−2η)²/2).

Set m=ceil(2(1−2η)⁻² log(2T/δ)). For the first wrong coordinate k, all prior estimated bits are correct, so the bound applies conditionally; a union bound over k proves Pr[ŝ≠s]≤δ. If ŝ=s, then the returned map φ̂_t(h)=1{x₁…x_t=ŝ₁…ŝ_t} agrees with the exact predictive feature simultaneously on every history.

## 3. Exact one-path TV for neighboring words

Let s,s′ differ only in their last bit. The X word is uniform under each. Conditional on X, the laws of Z₁…Z_{T−1} are identical because all earlier live bits use the same target prefix. If X₁…X_{T−1} differs from the shared prefix, both final states are dead. If it matches, then for either X_T value, one target ends alive and the other dead. The last-marker laws are Ber(1−η) and Ber(η), at TV 1−2η. Averaging over X gives

TV(P_s,P_{s′})=2^{−(T−1)}(1−2η).

By the product hybrid bound, TV(P_s^N,P_{s′}^N)≤N·2^{−(T−1)}(1−2η). Le Cam gives average last-bit error at least

(1−N·2^{−(T−1)}(1−2η))/2.

Success at least 2/3 requires N≥2^{T−1}/(3(1−2η)), even if the first T−1 secret bits are given.

## 4. Independent validation

Freeze ŝ after training. For each k, make fresh calls at ĥ_{k−1}=((ŝ₁,1),…,(ŝ_{k−1},1)). Put a=(1−2η)/2. If the prefix is correct, E[R]=(-1)^{ŝ_k}a when the candidate bit is also correct. If the first earlier mismatch has occurred, the live bit is zero and E[R]=0. At the first wrong coordinate, the prefix still matches and the mean has the sign opposite the candidate.

Use m_val=ceil(8(1−2η)⁻² log(4T/δ)) samples per level and accept a candidate bit only if R̄ has the candidate sign and |R̄|≥a/2. Hoeffding controls false rejection for a correct word; at a first wrong coordinate it controls acceptance of the opposite sign. The union bound gives the validation split stated in the report.
