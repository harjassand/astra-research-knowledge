EXACT THEOREM

Let C_N be the class of permutation-invariant N-qubit density operators
with exactly white Schur-Weyl sector weights:

 rho_N = direct_sum_j p_j rho_j tensor I_(m_j)/m_j,
 p_j = (2j+1)m_j/2^N,
 m_j = binomial(N,N/2-j)-binomial(N,N/2-j-1),
 Tr rho_j = 1.

Here j=0,1,...,N/2 for even N, and j=1/2,3/2,...,N/2 for odd N.
Within each spin irrep rho_j is an arbitrary density operator, including
coherences. Permutation invariance excludes coherence between inequivalent
spin sectors and forces the stated identity on multiplicity spaces.

For rho_N in C_N, put tau=rho_1=(I+b.sigma)/2 and

 chi(rho_N,r) = Tr[rho_r^2 (tau^tensorr)^(-1)]-1,
 M(N,r) = sup_(rho_N in C_N) chi(rho_N,r),
 q = r/N, h_N = sqrt(3/N), a_N = sum_j p_j j.

All matrix inverses exist for N>=256. For N>=256 and integers
2<=r<=N/128, the following finite bounds hold:

 3r(r-1)(1-2a_N/N)^2/(N-1)^2
       <= M(N,r)
       <= 3r(r-1)/[(N-1)^2(1-h_N)^2] +885735 q^3.       (T1)

In particular the exact uniform remainder is bounded by

 |M(N,r)-3r(r-1)/N^2|
       <= 42 q^2/sqrt(N) +900000 q^3.                   (T2)

Consequently:

 * For each fixed integer r>=2,
     M(N,r) = [3r(r-1)+o(1)]/N^2,
   along all N, including both parities. The error in (T2) is O_r(N^-5/2).
 * For every integer sequence r=r_N tending to infinity with r=o(N),
     M(N,r) = [3+o(1)](r/N)^2.
 * For r=1 the centered chi-square is identically zero whenever tau is
   invertible; the equality rho_1=tau also gives the natural zero extension
   if tau is singular.

The coefficient 3 concerns this centered Petz chi-square minimax problem.
No sharp relative-entropy, testing, or entanglement-certification constant
is claimed. Those quantities require separate analysis.

PROOF

1. Exact symmetrized spin covariance and the centered pair tensor.

Write J_a=(1/2)sum_i sigma_a^(i), mu_a=Tr rho_N J_a, and define the real
symmetrized covariance matrix

 V_ab = (1/2)Tr rho_N {J_a-mu_a I,J_b-mu_b I}.

For every real vector u,
u^T V u=Tr rho_N (u.(J-mu I))^2>=0. Thus V is real symmetric and positive
semidefinite. Unsymmetrized complex quantum covariance is not used here.

Permutation invariance gives b=2mu/N. The white weights imply
Tr rho_N J^2=3N/4, hence

 |b|^2 <= 4 Tr rho_N J^2/N^2 =3/N.                     (P1)

Let c_ab=Tr rho_N sigma_a^(1)sigma_b^(2). The entries are real because
the two factors act on different sites, and c_ab=c_ba by permutation
invariance. Expanding the symmetrized collective product gives exactly

 4 Tr rho_N (J_a J_b+J_b J_a)/2
       =N delta_ab+N(N-1)c_ab.

Define C_ab=c_ab-b_a b_b, X=4V/N, and Z=X+b b^T. Then

 C = (Z-I)/(N-1).                                     (P2)

Both X and Z are positive semidefinite. Their traces are

 Tr X=3-N|b|^2,    Tr Z=3-(N-1)|b|^2.

In particular s=Tr Z lies in [0,3]. Therefore

 ||Z-I||_F^2 =Tr Z^2-2s+3
             <=s^2-2s+3 <=6,

where the last inequality follows by maximizing this convex quadratic on
[0,3]. Equation (P2) proves the exact uniform bound

 T_2:=sum_(a,b) C_ab^2 <=6/(N-1)^2.                    (P3)

Grouping b b^T into Z avoids an unnecessary O(N^-1) perturbation in (P3).
This two-body estimate only requires permutation invariance and the mean
Casimir value 3N/4. The white weights are used below to control the tail.

2. Exact centered Gram decomposition, including noncommuting states.

Put A_a=sigma_a-b_a I, t=|b|, and use the operator inner product
<U,V>_tau=Tr tau U^dagger V. The centered Gram matrix is

 G_ab=Tr tau A_a A_b
     =delta_ab-b_a b_b+i sum_c epsilon_abc b_c.

It is complex Hermitian, with eigenvalues 1-t,1+t,1-t^2. In particular
lambda_min(G)>=1-t. In the finite range of the theorem,
t<=h_N<1/9 and tau is strictly positive.

For a k-site support, let

 d_(a_1,...,a_k)=Tr rho_N A_(a_1)^(1)...A_(a_k)^(k),
 T_k=sum_(a_1,...,a_k) d_(a_1,...,a_k)^2.

This is a real symmetric tensor by permutation invariance; d_1=0 and d_2=C.
Different supports are orthogonal under tau^tensorr. The Gram matrix on a
fixed support of size k is G^tensork. With sigma=tau^tensorr,
delta=rho_r-sigma and R=delta sigma^(-1), cyclicity of trace gives

 <R,R>_sigma=Tr delta^2 sigma^(-1)=chi(rho_N,r),
 <A_S,R>_sigma=Tr A_S^dagger delta=d_S.

The constant coefficient is zero. Exact finite-dimensional Gram Parseval
therefore gives

 chi(rho_N,r)
   =sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k) d_k^dagger (G^(-1))^tensork d_k
   <=sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k)(1-t)^(-k)T_k.             (P4)

This identity is an operator-space identity. It does not assume that
rho_r commutes with tau^tensorr. Applying (P3) to the k=2 term gives

 chi_2 <=3r(r-1)/[(N-1)^2(1-t)^2].                    (P5)

3. White-weight centered coefficient bound, stated with its full proof.

The following bound was already proved in this agent's frozen
CENTERED_REPORT.txt, equations (7)-(12):

 T_k <=36k^2 4^k (2k+1)!!/[k! binomial(N,k)].           (P6)

For completeness, all ingredients needed here are reproduced next. For
a real unit vector u, put Q_i=u.sigma^(i), beta=u.b, and

 B_l=binomial(N,l)^(-1)sum_(|S|=l) product_(i in S)Q_i,
 B_k^cent=binomial(N,k)^(-1)
          sum_(|S|=k) product_(i in S)(Q_i-beta I).

The exact expansion is

 B_k^cent=sum_(l=0)^k binomial(k,l)(-beta)^(k-l)B_l.

The unshifted B_l are mutually orthogonal under omega_N=I/2^N, with
Tr omega_N B_l^2=1/binomial(N,l). Thus

 Tr omega_N (B_k^cent)^2
  =sum_(l=0)^k binomial(k,l)^2 beta^(2(k-l))/binomial(N,l)
  <=4^k/binomial(N,k).                                (P7)

To verify the last inequality, use beta^2<=3/N and
binomial(N,k)/binomial(N,l)<=N^(k-l)l!/k!. Writing m=k-l,
each term times binomial(N,k) is at most binomial(k,m)3^m/m!, whose sum
is at most sum_m binomial(k,m)3^m=4^k.

The elementary-symmetric operator recurrence, with E_l=binomial(N,l)B_l,
S=sum_i Q_i=2J_u, E_0=I and E_1=S, is

 S E_l=(l+1)E_(l+1)+(N-l+1)E_(l-1).

Hence B_k^cent is a real polynomial of degree at most k in J_u. On the
spin-j grid {-j,-j+1,...,j}, every real polynomial P of degree <=k obeys

 max_(grid) P(m)^2
    <=36k^2 [sum_(grid)P(m)^2/(2j+1)].                 (P8)

If j<4k^2, (P8) follows immediately from 2j+1<=9k^2. Otherwise the classical
Markov derivative inequality on [-j,j] is
||P'||_infty<=k^2||P||_infty/j. There is an interval of length at least
j/(2k^2) on which |P| is at least half its continuous maximum. This
interval contains at least j/(4k^2) grid points, since j/(2k^2)>=2.
Its contribution to the discrete average proves (P8), using
(2j+1)/j<=9/4. The grid maximum is at most the continuous maximum.

The Markov inequality is the only external theorem used. Its exact
hypotheses are a real polynomial of degree at most k on a real interval;
rescaling [-1,1] gives the stated [-j,j] inequality. The primary theorem
was checked in Lawrence A. Harris, "A proof of Markov's theorem for
polynomials on Banach spaces," Theorem 1, author PDF page 3, restricted to
X=Y=R and the first derivative. The PDF was checked during this agent's
earlier frozen proof and is retained locally with a content hash.
Source: https://www.ms.uky.edu/~larry/paper.dir/proof.pdf
DOI: 10.1016/j.jmaa.2010.02.050.

Because p_j=(2j+1)m_j/2^N are exactly the white sector weights, weighted
Cauchy-Schwarz and (P8)-(P7) imply, for arbitrary rho_j,

 |Tr rho_N B_k^cent(u)|^2
   <=sum_j p_j ||(B_k^cent)_j||^2
   <=36k^2 Tr omega_N (B_k^cent)^2
   <=36k^2 4^k/binomial(N,k).                          (P9)

The polynomial f_k(u)=Tr rho_N B_k^cent(u) is the contraction of d_k with
u^tensork. The sphere moment identity in three dimensions gives

 E_(u uniform on S^2) f_k(u)^2
    >=[k!/(2k+1)!!]T_k.

Of all moment pairings, the k! fully cross pairings give T_k; all other
pairings give squared Frobenius norms of iterated traces of the real
symmetric tensor and are nonnegative. No isotropy or tracelessness of
d_k is assumed. Combining this identity with (P9) proves (P6).

4. Uniform cubic bound for degrees at least three.

Let x=4q/(1-t). Since t<1/9 and q<=1/128,

 x <=(9/2)q <=9/256.

Use (P6), binomial(r,k)/binomial(N,k)<=q^k, and (P4) only for k>=3:

 chi_(>=3)
   <=36sum_(k=3)^infinity k^2 (2k+1)!! x^k/k!
    =108x[g(x)-1-10x],
 g(x)=(1+3x)/(1-2x)^(7/2).                            (P10)

Here sum_k (2k+1)!! x^k/k!=(1-2x)^(-3/2). Applying (x d/dx)^2 and
subtracting the k=1 and k=2 terms proves (P10). Direct differentiation gives

 g(0)=1, g'(0)=10,
 g''(x)=105(1+x)/(1-2x)^(11/2).

For 0<=x<=9/256 the following rational upper bound is strict:

 g''(x) <=(105*265/256)(128/119)^6 <180.

Taylor's integral remainder therefore gives g(x)-1-10x<=90x^2. Consequently

 chi_(>=3)<=9720x^3<=885735q^3.                        (P11)

All constants here are explicit, with no asymptotic interchange of sums.
Equations (P5), (P1), and (P11) establish the upper bound of (T1).

5. Legal extremizing family, valid for both N parities.

In every spin-j sector choose

 rho_j^pm=(|j,j><j,j|+|j,-j><j,-j|)/2.

For j=0 this is just the unique spin-zero state. Tensor the identity on
each multiplicity space and use the exact white p_j. This gives a state in
C_N with b=0, so tau=I/2 and G=I exactly. Under this state,

 <J_x^2>=<J_y^2>=a_N/2,
 <J_z^2>=sum_j p_j j^2=3N/4-a_N,

and all symmetrized cross moments vanish. Thus

 C=[(1-2a_N/N)/(N-1)] diag(-1,-1,2).

The k=2 contribution is exactly

 chi_2=3r(r-1)(1-2a_N/N)^2/(N-1)^2.                   (P12)

Every other Gram contribution is nonnegative, giving the lower bound in
(T1). The bound a_N<=sqrt(sum_j p_j j^2)<=sqrt(3N/4) suffices; no Maxwell
limit for the sector distribution is needed. In particular
1-2a_N/N>=1-h_N>0 in the stated finite range.

6. Derive the uniform remainder and both sharp limits.

Put A_0=3r(r-1)/N^2. From (T1),

 M(N,r)/A_0 >=(1-h_N)^2 >=1-2h_N.

For the upper two-body term, since 1/N<=h_N and h_N<=1/9,

 (1-1/N)^(-2)(1-h_N)^(-2)
     <=(1-h_N)^(-4) <=1+8h_N.

The latter bound follows from
sup_(0<=h<=1/9) d/dh (1-h)^(-4)=4(9/8)^5<8.
Since A_0<=3q^2 and 24sqrt(3)<42, (T1) now gives (T2), rounding the cubic
constant upwards from 885735 to 900000. Dividing (T2) by A_0 also shows
uniform relative error O(N^-1/2+r/N) for every r>=2 in the stated range.

For fixed r, both remainder terms are o(N^-2). For r tending to infinity
with r=o(N), divide (T2) by q^2 and use r(r-1)/r^2 tending to one. Every
such sequence eventually belongs to the finite range, completing the
claimed sharp minimax asymptotics.
