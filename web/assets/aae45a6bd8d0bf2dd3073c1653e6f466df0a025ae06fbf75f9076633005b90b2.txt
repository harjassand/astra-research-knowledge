ASSIGNMENT AND STATUS

This addendum records the post-freeze, root-assigned informed-breadth search for an
external theorem or reusable construction proving finite Hankel positivity for the
Gaussian-Dicke sequence
  q_k = k!(N-k)! exp[-delta (k-N/2)^2/N],  0 <= k <= N,
at a fixed delta that does not shrink with N. The requested scope is eventual
delta < 2 as N grows, with the finite-N N=2 threshold 2 log 2 and the
large-N variance/log-convexity obstruction at 2 in view.

No primary source located in this bounded targeted search proves PSD of the
required finite moment matrices for this q sequence at any uniform positive
delta, or gives a positive measure for its finite moment segment. One close
statistical-mechanics paper has exact finite-exchangeability and moment methods,
including sharp asymptotics for a different antiferromagnetic Curie-Weiss family.
Its model mismatch is load-bearing and is made explicit below. The literature
search therefore contributes a reusable framework and a warning against a
false import, not the missing positivity lemma and not a novelty claim.

EXACT SEQUENCE REWRITING AND TWO NEGATIVE MECHANISM CHECKS

Put a=N/2+1 and m=k-N/2 (integer or half-integer according to N). Then
  q_k = Gamma(a+m) Gamma(a-m) exp[-delta m^2/N].
The beta-prime integral, with t=exp(s), gives the exact identity
  Gamma(a+m) Gamma(a-m)/(N+1)!
    = integral_R exp(ms) [2 cosh(s/2)]^(-(N+2)) ds,
valid for |m|<a. Thus the target values are integer/half-integer samples of
the bilateral Laplace transform of the log-beta-prime density proportional to
sech^(N+2)(s/2), multiplied by exp[-delta m^2/N].

This identifies the Gaussian factor as a backward-heat multiplier in the
Laplace variable: if M(m)=integral exp(ms)f(s)ds, forward heat
exp(t d^2/ds^2) multiplies M by exp(+t m^2); the target uses exp(-t m^2),
t=delta/N. Therefore standard positive Gaussian convolution is the wrong
direction. Also exp(-t m^2) alone is not a moment sequence for t>0:
its adjacent log-convexity ratio is exp(-2t)<1. Consequently the standard
Hadamard/Schur closure route (multiply two moment sequences) cannot certify
this tilt. These facts do not refute positivity of the finite q segment.

There is a stronger exact obstruction only to an infinite analytic extension.
If the function
  M_delta(z)=Gamma(a+z)Gamma(a-z) exp[-delta z^2/N]
were the bilateral mgf of a positive measure for all real z in an open
neighborhood of zero, analyticity would force its characteristic function to be
  M_delta(it)/M_delta(0)
    = |Gamma(a+it)|^2 exp[delta t^2/N]/Gamma(a)^2.
For every delta>0 this is unbounded as |t| tends to infinity, since the gamma
factor decays only exponentially in |t| while the displayed Gaussian grows
quadratically in the exponent. This violates the characteristic-function bound
|phi(t)|<=1. Hence the continuous analytic continuation of these prescribed
gamma-Gaussian values is not an mgf. The finite problem asks only for N+1
values, so it may admit a different measure; this obstruction cannot be used
against finite Hankel PSD.

A direct necessary bound follows from 2-by-2 Hankel log-convexity. For
1<=k<=N-1,
  q_(k-1) q_(k+1) / q_k^2
    = ((k+1)(N-k+1)/(k(N-k))) exp[-2 delta/N].
For even N and k=N/2, PSD requires
  delta <= N log(1+2/N).
This tends to 2 from below. In particular any fixed delta>2 is excluded for
all sufficiently large even N; delta=2 is already excluded for every finite
even N by this minor. At N=2 the same necessary condition is exactly
delta<=2 log 2, matching the stated universal necessary endpoint. This is
only a low-order necessary test; it proves no sufficiency below 2.

CLOSEST PRIMARY LITERATURE: FINITE EXCHANGEABILITY / CURIE-WEISS

Thomas M. Liggett, Jeffrey E. Steif, and Bálint Tóth, “Statistical mechanical
systems on complete graphs, infinite exchangeability, finite extensions and a
discrete finite moment problem,” Annals of Probability 35 (2007), 867–914,
arXiv:math/0512191, DOI 10.1214/009117906000001033.

The paper is genuinely relevant in method and subject:
• It defines infinite extendibility of a finite exchangeable Bernoulli law as
  representation by a mixture of iid Bernoulli product laws, which is exactly
  the classical probability analogue of the binomial-mixture condition.
• Theorem 1.16 gives a necessary-and-sufficient finite-support moment criterion
  for a random variable supported on {0,...,l}; the result is based on testing
  nonnegative polynomials with simple roots in that support.
• Proposition 1.17 gives a formula for an l-extension of the antiferromagnetic
  Curie-Weiss law when its proposed extension weights Q(j) are all
  nonnegative. The authors explicitly warn that Q may be signed even in cases
  where some extension exists.
• Theorem 1.15 has a sharp asymptotic threshold for a different question:
  fix the sample size n and field parameter a, let the extension size l grow,
  and set the Curie-Weiss parameter b=1+c/l. For rho=a/(1+a), c below
  1/[2 rho(1-rho)] gives l-extendibility for all sufficiently large l, while
  c above that threshold gives non-extendibility for all sufficiently large l.
  The result is about l-extensions of this classical model, not finite Hankel
  PSD of the current q family.

The mismatch is exact. In their {0,1} classical Curie-Weiss model, each
configuration with k ones has weight proportional to a^k b^(k(N-k)).
Its count law has the extra factor binom(N,k). In the current symmetric
Dicke state the Dicke population is proportional to exp[-delta m^2/N],
so the per-configuration weight required by a product-mixture representation
is q_k/N! = k!(N-k)! exp[-delta m^2/N]/N!, up to normalization. The
factorial term cannot be absorbed into a^k b^(k(N-k)) by choosing a,b for
general N. Further, Theorem 1.15 changes the coupling with extension length
as b=1+c/l, whereas the target varies with N and has no l parameter.
The paper's statement that a fixed antiferromagnetic Curie-Weiss law is not
infinitely extendible therefore does not give a counterexample here, and its
asymptotic threshold does not prove the requested eventual delta<2 result.
The finite-moment criterion and extension formula are possible tools for
future derivations only after the exact target sequence is inserted and all
positivity conditions are proved.

OTHER TARGETED LITERATURE ROUTES AND LIMITS

• Nengkun Yu, “Separability of Bosonic Systems,” Phys. Rev. A 94, 060101(R)
  (2016), arXiv:1501.02957, gives the exact finite Hankel/localizing criterion
  for symmetric Dicke-diagonal qubit states. It supplies the gate, not a
  Gaussian-Dicke PSD theorem. This is recorded only as a reduction, not as the
  requested literature result.
• Belton, Guillot, Khare, and Putinar, “Totally positive kernels, Pólya
  frequency functions, and their transforms,” J. Analyse Math. 150 (2023),
  83–158, arXiv:2006.16213, develops infinite continuous Hankel and
  Pólya-frequency-kernel structure. Its Gaussian-convolution closure is forward
  heat, while this target is an inverse/backward-heat tilt, and its infinite
  kernel conclusions do not establish the finite N Hankel gate.
• Di Dio and Langer, “The Hadamard Product of Moment Sequences, Diagonal
  Positivity Preservers, and their Generators,” Integral Equations and Operator
  Theory 97 (2025), article 32, arXiv:2407.19933, concerns preservation of
  moment sequences under entrywise/Hadamard maps. The particular scalar
  multiplier exp(-delta m^2/N) fails the elementary 2-by-2 moment condition,
  so this framework cannot be applied as a positive moment multiplier.
• Sidles, “Positive P-Representations of the Thermal Operator from Quantum
  Control Theory,” arXiv:quant-ph/0401165, constructs a positive diagonal
  spin-coherent P representation for a single-spin linear-axis thermal
  operator exp(-beta t dot s). It has neither the quadratic anisotropy nor the
  finite-N multi-qubit Gaussian-Dicke gate.
• Targeted searches for “discrete Gaussian binomial mixture,” “Gaussian
  exchangeable Bernoulli,” factorial/Gamma moment sequences with a negative
  quadratic exponential tilt, inverse Bernstein positivity, and coherent-spin
  thermal anisotropy returned no primary result matching the exact finite
  sequence and quantifiers above.
