RESULT — no parameter-uniform entropy rate for kinetic Fokker–Planck

Status: REFUTED for a positive exponential rate uniform in collision frequency or spatial gap. The counterexample is exact, positive, and finite-entropy. A fixed-parameter quadratic L2 hypocoercive estimate is available from primary literature and is reconstructed below. The modified energy used in that estimate is quadratic L2, not the full nonlinear relative entropy. No novelty or priority claim is made.

Model and hypotheses

Let d≥1, x∈T^d_ℓ=(R/ℓZ)^d with periodic boundary conditions, v∈R^d, and

    M(v)=(2π)^(-d/2) exp(-|v|²/2).

The forward kinetic Fokker–Planck equation is

    ∂t F + v·∇x F = ν ∇v·(∇v F + vF),       ν>0.

The equilibrium is the spatially uniform Maxwellian M. Write h=F/M. Then

    ∂t h = Gν h := -v·∇x h + ν(Δv h - v·∇v h).

For quadratic entropy use the Hilbert space L²(dx M dv) for h, equivalently L²(dx M⁻¹dv) for F. For the nonlinear statement use probability densities F≥0 with finite relative entropy H(F|M)=∫F log(F/M) dx dv, with normalized spatial measure. Frequencies are ξ=2πn/ℓ and the spatial gap is k*=2π/ℓ.

Exact entropy identity and failure of direct Fisher coercivity

For smooth positive solutions,

    -d/dt H(Ft|M) = ν I_v(Ft|M),
    I_v(F|M)=∫F |∇v log(F/M)|² dx dv.

The transport term contributes zero because it is divergence-free and periodic. If F0=M(1+ε cos(k*x1)) with 0<ε<1, then H(F0|M)>0 but I_v(F0|M)=0. Thus the bare entropy-dissipation inequality I_v≥cH fails for every c>0. This is the exact macro/micro obstruction: ΠF=Mρ(x), QF=F−ΠF; the collision operator dissipates Q but leaves Π untouched.

An exact positive mode gives the rate obstruction. In one spatial direction set k=k* and

    φk(x,v)=cos(kx-kv/ν),      r=k²/ν.

Direct differentiation gives Gνφk=−r φk. Hence for 0<ε<1,

    Ft(x,v)=M(v)[1+ε exp(−rt) φk(x,v)]

is a positive probability solution for every t≥0. Define

    J(a)=(2π)⁻¹∫₀^{2π}(1+a cos θ) log(1+a cos θ) dθ.

Uniform averaging in x gives H(Ft|M)=J(εe⁻ʳᵗ), and Taylor expansion at zero gives J(a)=a²/4+O(a⁴). Therefore

    H(Ft|M) ~ (ε²/4) exp(−2k²t/ν)       as t→∞.

Any estimate H(Ft|M)≤C exp(−λt)H(F0|M) with finite C must have λ≤2k*²/ν. The same mode gives the exact quadratic-entropy ratio exp(−2k*²t/ν).

The collision-only mode h(v)=v1 satisfies Gνh=−νh, so the quadratic L2 decay exponent cannot exceed 2ν. If a KL inequality is asserted for every probability density, its ε→0 expansion at M gives the corresponding L2 semigroup estimate first for bounded mean-zero perturbations. Bounded truncations of v1 are dense in L²(Mdv), so the estimate extends to v1 and forces λ≤2ν. Consequently every all-state KL estimate with finite prefactor must obey

    λ ≤ 2 min{ν, k*²/ν}.

This implication is parameterwise; it still permits C to depend on ℓ and ν. It rules out a positive rate uniform over either ℓ→∞, ν→0, or ν→∞. The spatial mode alone already refutes uniformity as ℓ→∞ or ν→∞.

There is also an explicit macro-start trajectory showing how dissipation is transferred. For h0=cos(kx),

    ht=e^(−Cν,k(t)) cos(kx−kAν(t)v),
    Aν(t)=(1−e^(−νt))/ν,
    Cν,k(t)=νk²∫₀ᵗAν(s)²ds
            =(k²/ν)t−(2k²/ν²)(1−e^(−νt))
             +(k²/(2ν²))(1−e^(−2νt)).

Here the initial Fisher dissipation is zero, while Cν,k(t)/t→k²/ν and the quadratic entropy ratio is exactly exp(−2Cν,k(t)). The commutator [∂v,j,v·∇x]=∂x,j explains the transfer; at high ν the effective spatial diffusion coefficient is 1/ν.

Modified quadratic entropy and fixed-parameter estimate

The standard quadratic entropy E2(F)=½||F||²_{L²(dx M⁻¹dv)} obeys

    −dE2/dt = ν∫M|∇v(F/M)|² dx dv ≥ ν||QF||².

For each Fourier mode after the time change s=νt, let η=ξ/ν, L0F=∇v·(∇vF+vF), TηF=i(v·η)F, and ΠF=M∫Fdv. The DMS/ADSW twisted quadratic entropy is

    Hδ[F]=½||F||² + δ Re⟨AηF,F⟩,
    Aη=(I+(TηΠ)*TηΠ)⁻¹(TηΠ)*,
    AηF=−i M (η·∫vF(v)dv)/(1+|η|²).

Its exact derivative is −Dδ, where

    Dδ=−⟨L0F,F⟩ + δ⟨AηTηΠF,F⟩
        + δ Re⟨AηTηQF,F⟩ − δ Re⟨TηAηF,F⟩
        − δ Re⟨AηL0F,F⟩.

This is a modified L2 entropy for the linear equation. It is not a proved nonlinear KL Lyapunov functional. In the Gaussian model the exact macro/micro constants are λm=1, λM=|η|², ΠTηΠ=0, and the improved Fourier-mode bound in [ADSW, §II.1.3.2] is

    ||AηF|| ≤ |η|/(1+|η|²)||QF||,
    ||TηAηF|| ≤ |η|²/(1+|η|²)||QF||,
    ||AηTηQF|| ≤ √3 |η|²/(1+|η|²)||QF||,
    ||AηL0F|| ≤ |η|/(1+|η|²)||QF||.

Thus the source gives CM(|η|)=|η|(1+√3|η|)/(1+|η|²), and the commutator/macro transport closes the missing density control. The primary-source DMS estimate [BDMMS, Cor. 5 and Cor. 9] gives Λ=1/12 for the unit Gaussian and unit collision frequency. Time rescaling and the torus Poincaré gap yield the closed bound

    ||Ft−F∞||²_{L²(dx M⁻¹dv)}
       ≤ 3 exp(−λDMS t)||F0−F∞||²,
    λDMS=(1/12) νk*²/(ν²+k*²).

The zero spatial mode decays in squared norm at rate 2ν, which is faster than λDMS. The displayed λDMS is a sufficient exponent for quadratic entropy, not a sharp rate and not a full-KL lower bound. It has the same parameter scale as the exact upper obstruction: νk*²/(ν²+k*²) lies between ½ min{ν,k*²/ν} and min{ν,k*²/ν}.

Strongest equal-input baseline audited

For the normalized scalar case d=1, ℓ=2π, ν=1, the strongest rigorous L² method among the audited comparisons is the Fourier-mode optimization of Arnold–Dolbeault–Schmeiser–Wöhrer (ADSW). Brigati (2023, §6.2) reports its squared-L² exponent as λ≈0.176048 with a multiplicative prefactor. This decimal is a source-reported numerical maximization of the explicit ADSW admissibility formula; it is not an exact symbolic constant and was not independently recomputed or certified here. The underlying ADSW proposition is a rigorous modewise estimate once admissible coefficients are fixed; the decimal is only the reported optimized value. ADSW §II.1.3.2 supplies the modewise A operator and Gaussian bounds, while its §II.1.2 gives the sharper two-by-two quadratic-form criterion used in the optimization. Brigati’s table gives DMS 1/24≈0.041667 and ADSW≈0.176048 on this same normalized model. The ≈0.4 entry in that table is explicitly attributed to numerical computation and is not used as a theorem baseline. These are the strongest rates among the methods audited here, not a global-best claim; weak-norm estimates and general-domain results are different interfaces and are not compared numerically here.

Inputs, costs, falsifier, and limits

Supplied inputs are only d, ℓ, ν, the standard Gaussian equilibrium, and the generator. No oracle, data acquisition, or numerical computation is used. The obstruction requires one Fourier mode, one Gaussian velocity coordinate, and O(1) exact symbolic operations. The cited modewise L² baseline assumes the same equilibrium and known collision coefficient; its evaluation requires no acquired information. Any computational discretization/precision cost is outside this continuum theorem.

Falsifier: differentiate φk and check whether Gνφk=−(k²/ν)φk; or insert F=M(1+εφk) into the claimed KL estimate and compare its t→∞ exponent. A claimed parameter-uniform λ>0 is contradicted by choosing ℓ or ν so that 2k*²/ν<λ. A claimed uniform low-ν rate is contradicted by the collision-only tangent mode, which forces λ≤2ν.

Known caveats: the fixed-parameter modified-energy estimate is quadratic L2 only. The KL obstruction is exact but is not a proof of a matching nonlinear KL lower bound. Exact optimal global semigroup growth rates beyond these eigenmode upper bounds are not established here. No novelty, field priority, or external mathematical validation is claimed; the key mechanism and fixed-torus methods are established prior art.
