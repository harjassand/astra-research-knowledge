# Rejected initial theorem route

Let `S` be the species-by-reaction stoichiometric matrix, `v` a signed net flux vector, and `μ` the species chemical potentials. Thermodynamic consistency makes the reaction-affinity vector `g=Sᵀμ`. Thus

`vᵀg = μᵀSv`.

At exact steady state `Sv=0`, so total chemical power across a closed boundary is zero. If every active reaction has `v_j g_j≤−η|v_j|` for some `η>0`, then `η||v||₁≤|μᵀSv|`; near steady state, a norm bound follows from the mass-balance residual. A gauge-safe version uses the projection of `μ` onto `im(S)`.

This is a useful auditable consistency check when uncertainty intervals, boundary exchange, and measurement costs are explicit, but the theorem is a one-line consequence of chemical-potential duality. Beard et al. already characterize steady flux thermodynamic feasibility via stoichiometric cycle structure; Fleming et al. combine mass balance, the second law, reversible kinetics, and bounded experimental quantities. I reject it as a novelty candidate.

## Failure modes in application

- FBA net fluxes are often bounded only on internal species; omitting chemostatted exchange species breaks the identity unless their free-energy flow is charged.
- Flux measurement error and metabolite activity/Gibbs-energy uncertainty must be propagated jointly. Point estimates can fabricate an affinity margin.
- At a strict steady-state margin, a nonzero closed-system internal flux is impossible; a contradiction diagnoses an omitted boundary flow, a wrong direction, or inconsistent measurements rather than a new physical effect.
- The identity does not reconstruct rate constants, determine kinetic reachability, prove stationarity, or exclude stochastic component effects.
