# Exact sparse enforcers: information-theoretic existence versus access

Status: universal sparse compilation is impossible, even information-
theoretically, for a polynomial-circuit isometry. A Walsh/Reed--Muller family
gives an unconditional exponential row-degree lower bound, including after
any fixed product workspace is appended. Dense exact enforcers remain
constructible; in the counterexample their individual entries even have a
polynomial-time exact classical formula. This isolates row enumeration as the
obstruction. It does not rule out changing the encoded subspace itself.

## Interface and basic distinction

Let V:C^K -> C^N be an isometry, S=ran(V), and P=VV*. The demanded enforcer
has C=C* >= 0, ker(C)=S, and C >= gamma(I-P), with gamma>0. A usual sparse
matrix interface also charges Delta(C)=max_x |{y:C_xy != 0}|, a polynomial-
time algorithm to enumerate those y and their entries, exact or sufficiently
precise coefficient representations, and normalization such as ||C||<=1.

For every V, the dense projector

    C_dense = I-P

is an exact positive contraction, has kernel S, and satisfies the gap bound
with gamma=1. Thus information-theoretic existence of an exact enforcer is
automatic if density is allowed. If V has a polynomial-size unitary dilation
U with V|psi> = U(|psi>|0^a>), then

    C_dense = U [I - (I_input tensor |0^a><0^a|)] U*.

The reflection 2C_dense-I is implemented by U, a phase flip on the invalid
workspace subspace, and U*; combining that reflection with identity gives a
constant-overhead, normalization-one block encoding of C_dense. Equivalently,
the projector can be measured by applying U*, testing whether the workspace
is clean, and uncomputing. This is constructible from V's circuit, but it is
not a sparse-row implementation. Its entry formula is

    (C_dense)_{x,y} = delta_{x,y} - sum_{z in [K]} V_{x,z} conjugate(V_{y,z}),

which has K terms; a short circuit for V by itself does not make this sum a
polynomial-time classical entry oracle in general.

The exact linear-algebraic necessary condition is sharper than a parameter
count. For a subspace S define its coordinate minimum support

    d_min(S^perp) = min_{0 != u in S^perp} |supp(u)|.

**Row-support lemma.** Every square matrix C with ker(C)=S has a nonzero row
whose support is at least d_min(S^perp). In particular Delta(C) >=
d_min(S^perp). This applies to every positive constant-gap enforcer.

Indeed, each row of C annihilates S, so its conjugate coefficient vector lies
in S^perp. Since S is a proper subspace, C has positive rank and at least one
row is nonzero. No spectral-gap or coefficient-size assumption is needed.
Consequently, if d_min(S^perp) is superpolynomial in the input length, no
exact polynomial-row enforcer exists at any precision or construction cost.

## An efficiently specified image with exponential necessary row degree

Take an even number n of logical qubits, set m=n+1 (odd), N=2^m and
K=2^n=N/2, and put d=(m-1)/2=n/2. Let H_m=H^{tensor m} be the normalized
Walsh--Hadamard transform. Define the low- and high-weight frequency spaces

    L_d = span{|a>: |a| <= d},
    L_>d = span{|a>: |a| >= d+1}.

Binomial symmetry for odd m gives dim(L_d)=dim(L_>d)=2^(m-1)=K. The target
image is

    S = H_m L_>d.

It has a polynomial-size exact isometry V from n to m qubits. To specify it,
write an m-bit string as (b,x), where x has n=m-1 bits, and let r(x)=0||x.
Exactly one of r(x), complement(r(x)) has weight at least d+1, because m is
odd. Call this high-weight representative beta(x). The map beta is a
bijection from n-bit strings to the weight->d strings: each x represents one
pair {0||x, 1||complement(x)}, and the pair has exactly one high member.
Then

    V|x> = H_m |beta(x)>.

For a unitary-circuit implementation rather than only an isometry circuit,
extend beta to a permutation on m bits: for input (b,x), output the high
member of the pair when b=0 and its low member when b=1. This permutation
and its inverse require only Hamming-weight counting, comparison with d,
and conditional complementing, hence have polynomial-time Boolean
algorithms and polynomial-size reversible circuits. A standard
compute/swap/uncompute implementation uses polynomially many Toffoli/CNOT
gates and returns its scratch bits to zero. Prepare b=0, apply that
permutation, then apply H_m. Thus the image is specified exactly by O(poly(n))
gates over H and reversible classical gates; no arbitrary real amplitudes
are built into the circuit.

The orthogonal complement is

    S^perp = H_m L_d.

For u in this space, its coordinates, up to the common factor 2^(-m/2), are

    f(x) = sum_{|a|<=d} c_a (-1)^(a dot x).

Writing x_i in {0,1}, each character is
(-1)^(a dot x)=product_{i:a_i=1}(1-2x_i). These characters form a triangular
basis for all multilinear polynomials of degree at most d over C. Every
nonzero such polynomial has at least 2^(m-d) nonzero evaluations. A short
induction proves the field-independent bound: write

    f(x',x_m)=g(x') + x_m h(x'),

with deg(g)<=d and deg(h)<=d-1. If h=0, the two slices both equal the
nonzero g, so induction doubles its support. If h!=0, at every x' with
h(x')!=0, the two values g(x') and g(x')+h(x') cannot both vanish. The
support is therefore at least the support of h, which by induction is
2^((m-1)-(d-1))=2^(m-d). The base cases d=0 and d=m are immediate.

It follows that

    d_min(S^perp) >= 2^(m-d) = 2^(n/2+1).

Therefore every exact enforcer for this O(poly(n))-gate image, regardless of
whether it is positive, has some row with at least 2^(n/2+1) nonzeros. In
particular, no polynomial-row oracle exists for this image. This is an
information-theoretic obstruction, not merely a failure of a proposed
compiler. Representative dimensions are:

    logical n | output m | N=2^m | K=2^n | d | minimum row degree
    ----------+----------+-------+-------+---+-------------------
         2    |     3    |   8   |   4   | 1 |         4
         4    |     5    |  32   |  16   | 2 |         8
         6    |     7    | 128   |  64   | 3 |        16
         8    |     9    | 512   | 256   | 4 |        32

## Dense enforcer entry access, precision, and charged costs

The dense enforcer for the example is especially explicit:

    C = I - VV* = H_m P_{L_d} H_m.

It is an orthogonal projector, so ||C||=1, ker(C)=S, and the gap is exactly
gamma=1. For x,y in {0,1}^m,

    C_{x,y} = 2^(-m) sum_{|a|<=d} (-1)^(a dot (x xor y)).

If r=|x xor y|, this is

    C_{x,y} = 2^(-m) sum_{j=0}^d sum_{ell=0}^j
                   (-1)^ell binom(r,ell) binom(m-r,j-ell).

The formula uses O(m^2) integer arithmetic operations on O(m)-bit values,
so an exact classical entry oracle runs in polynomial bit time and returns a
rational with denominator 2^m and O(m) bits. Every nonzero entry has
magnitude at least 2^(-m), so O(m) fractional precision also separates zero
from nonzero entries. This enforcer is not sparse:
the row-support lemma forces at least 2^(n/2+1) entries in some row. A sparse
row oracle that lists all nonzeros, or returns the kth nonzero column in
polynomial time with a polynomial row degree, cannot exist. Thus polynomial
entry access, a polynomial-size circuit/block encoding, and polynomial-row
sparsity are genuinely different resources.

The row-degree lower bound is independent of entry precision and of the gap.
Even if every coefficient is supplied as an exact algebraic/rational number,
and even though this example has only O(m)-bit rational entries, explicitly
writing the forced row takes Omega(2^(n/2)) output time. Rounding entries
does not provide an exact enforcer unless the exact kernel is retained; this
argument is specifically for exact-kernel representations. The dense
projector itself needs no rescaling or hidden normalization.

## Workspace boundary

The lower bound survives any fixed product workspace state, not just a clean
zero register. Let the enlarged target be S' = S tensor |a>, where |a> is
any fixed normalized state on an arbitrary finite workspace, and let C be
any square exact enforcer with ker(C)=S'. Its row space is (S')^perp. Contract
each row vector against <a| on the workspace. The contracted vector lies in
S^perp, and its support is at most the original row support. These
contractions span all of S^perp, because u tensor |a> is in (S')^perp for
every u in S^perp. At least one row contracts to a nonzero vector, so

    Delta(C) >= d_min(S^perp) >= 2^(n/2+1).

This covers arbitrary workspace dimension and arbitrary fixed workspace
state when the physical target is the product embedding V|psi> tensor |a>.
It does not cover a data-dependent workspace isometry, a history-state
encoding, or a different code subspace. Those change ran(V); an efficient
sparse enforcer for such a re-encoded image would not be an enforcer for the
original S tensor |a>. Dense existence remains explicit in this case too:

    C_workspace = I - (VV* tensor |a><a|)

is a gap-one projector with kernel S tensor |a>. If |a> has a polynomial
preparation circuit, this projector again has a polynomial circuit/block
encoding, while the contraction argument still forbids a polynomial-row
matrix in the fixed computational basis.

## Classification for the N11-style route

1. **Information-theoretic dense existence:** always, via I-VV*; norm and
   gap can both be exactly one.
2. **Sparse existence for every circuit image:** false. The explicit
   Walsh/Reed--Muller isometry above has no polynomial-row exact enforcer,
   even allowing arbitrary coefficients and any fixed product workspace.
3. **Constructibility without sparsity:** yes as a circuit/projector
   block-encoding from V and V*. A generic classical entry oracle is not
   supplied by that circuit alone.
4. **Efficient dense entry access:** yes for the explicit counterexample by
   the Krawtchouk/binomial formula, with exact O(n)-bit coefficients.
5. **Polynomial sparse-row access:** impossible for the counterexample,
   since row output size itself is exponential.
6. **Images with extra structure:** some do have sparse enforcers. For a
   coordinate embedding, I-P is diagonal and 1-sparse. The circuit-size
   hypothesis alone does not distinguish these cases; the minimum support
   of S^perp is a decisive necessary invariant.

This settles the universal-enforcer question but not the project-specific
N11 target. N11 may choose a specially structured common encoding of its
particular hard family; the counterexample only says that an arbitrary
polynomial-circuit isometry cannot be assumed to admit the required sparse
enforcer. Nor does it rule out solving that target by constructing a
different data-dependent workspace encoding whose image is not the original
subspace.
