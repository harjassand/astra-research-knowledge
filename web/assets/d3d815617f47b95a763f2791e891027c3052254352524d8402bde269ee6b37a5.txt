SOL ROBUST RECOVERY V1 — FROZEN ORIGINAL CONSTRUCTION
2026-10-09. Internal derivation, not externally validated. Append-only record.

1. Capability objective and exact boundary

The objective is robust recovery of x* from iid real Gaussian quadratic measurements, with heavy-tailed noise and adaptive replacement of both coordinates of a pair. The original A07 vector ya uses fourth noise moments to support robust PCA. The construction below instead uses a bounded scalar response before converting the sample into a PCA vector. It proves a near-linear-in-input spectral initializer with only a conditional noise second moment. Full optimal-error recovery against one noncausal adversary is not proved in this V1: refinement requires a uniform-in-iterate argument or a causal fresh-batch interface. This is a real remaining inference, not a result inferred from initialization.

Let d>2, x* in R^d, q=||x*||>0 and u=q^2. Independent clean pairs obey a~N(0,I_d), y=(a^T x*)^2+zeta, E[zeta|a]=0, E[zeta^2|a]<=sigma^2. Equality in the second moment is unnecessary for this construction. No fourth moment is assumed. The learner knows eps, confidence delta and R>=sigma/u. The original task's known r_up>=K4/u supplies this R automatically. A strong adversary inspects the entire clean initializer sample and replaces <=eps m pairs arbitrarily. The same corrupted sample is used to estimate u and compute PCA; neither independence of the scale estimate nor a causal adversary is assumed for initialization.

Arithmetic/runtime statements use the standard real-arithmetic model of the imported PCA primitive. They are not a bit-complexity theorem for arbitrary exact real or enormous adversarial coordinates. Finite-precision rounding remains a separate interface below.

2. New representation and population identity

For L>0 define f_L(y)=max(-L,min(y,L)), g_L(y)=L+f_L(y) in [0,2L], and
  X_L = xi sqrt(g_L(y)) a,
where xi is an independent learner Rademacher sign. Every raw outer product is g_L(y) aa^T, independent of xi. The sign makes the clean vector mean zero if desired; the actual PCA proof uses only raw second moments. No norm cutoff on a is required. A bounded multiplier of a Gaussian is subGaussian in every direction, even when the multiplier depends on a and on the noise.

Define
  S_L=(L+u)I_d+2 x*x*^T,
  Q_L=E[g_L(y) aa^T].
The eigenvalues of S_L are L+3u in the x* direction and L+u elsewhere, with gap 2u. Conditional centering and the Gaussian fourth-moment identity give E[y aa^T]=u I+2x*x*^T. Since
  |f_L(y)-y|=(|y|-L)_+ <= y^2/L,
for every unit v,
  |v^T(Q_L-S_L)v| <= E[y^2(v^Ta)^2]/L
                         <=(15u^2+sigma^2)/L.
Indeed E[y^2 aa^T]=E[(a^Tx*)^4aa^T]+E[zeta^2|a]aa^T is bounded in PSD order by (3u^2+sigma^2)I+12u x*x*^T. Consequently
  ||Q_L-S_L||op <= b(L):=(15u^2+sigma^2)/L.                 (2.1)
This proof permits arbitrary conditional dependence of zeta on a.

The stability target below is S_L, not Q_L. Definition 2.4 of the PCA source permits any PSD target. The clipping bias (2.1) is therefore charged on the left side of the stability inequality. There is no hidden claim that Q_L equals S_L.

3. Scalar scale acquisition under adaptive replacement

Let nu in (0,1/2) be prescribed. Var(y)<=v:=(2+R^2)u^2. Fix
  K=ceil(32 eps m+32 log(2/delta_s)).
If K<=m/2, partition K disjoint blocks of b=floor(m/K) points, ignoring leftovers, and set h=max(0,median(block means of the corrupted y's)). This is linear time and requires no variance input. On the original clean data, each block average differs from u by more than sqrt(8v/b) with probability <=1/8. Hoeffding implies that fewer than K/4 blocks are bad, except on probability exp(-K/32)<=delta_s/2. An arbitrary strong adversary can affect at most eps m<=K/32 blocks. Hence a strict majority remain within sqrt(8v/b) of u. Since b>=m/(2K),
  |h-u| <=4 sqrt(v K/m)
          <=4u sqrt((2+R^2)[32eps+(32log(2/delta_s)+1)/m]). (3.1)
This is a deterministic implication for every replacement of <=eps m pairs once the clean block event holds, even if the corruption encodes future data.

Thus the explicit sufficient conditions
  K<=m/2,
  16(2+R^2)[32eps+(32log(2/delta_s)+1)/m]<=nu^2             (3.2)
give h in [(1-nu)u,(1+nu)u]. In particular, eps<=nu^2/[1024(2+R^2)] and m>=32(2+R^2)(32log(2/delta_s)+1)/nu^2 imply the second inequality. The finite scalar cost is included in the common sample m.

4. Uniform Gaussian deletion, including fractional adaptive weights

For beta in (0,1/2), let k=ceil(beta m)<=m. Let t_D=log(1/delta_D). With probability >=1-delta_D over the clean Gaussian design,
  sup_{w_i in [0,1], sum_i w_i<=beta m}
   ||m^{-1}sum_i w_i a_i a_i^T||op <= G_k,
  G_k=[sqrt(k)+sqrt(d)+sqrt(2[k log(em/k)+t_D])]^2/m.      (4.1)

Proof. For a fixed k-subset I, its k by d Gaussian row matrix obeys
  Pr(||A_I||op>sqrt(k)+sqrt(d)+sqrt(2t))<=exp(-t).
Union bound over <=(em/k)^k subsets gives (4.1) for integral subsets. For any fixed direction v, maximizing sum_i w_i(v^Ta_i)^2 over the deletion polytope uses at most k coordinates, with at most one fractional coordinate; its value is bounded by the top k sum. Taking the supremum over v proves the fractional version. This includes noninteger beta m and weights chosen after observing all y_i,a_i. Smaller integral subsets can be extended to k-subsets by PSD ordering.

Because 0<=g_L<=2L, the corresponding deleted sample second moment, simultaneously over every L<=Lmax and every such weight vector, is <=2Lmax G_k in operator norm. No statistical relation between deletion, L and a is required.

5. Full clean covariance concentration uniform over data-selected scale

For fixed L and unit v let Z=g_L(y)(v^Ta)^2. Then 0<=Z<=2L G^2 pointwise, where G=v^Ta~N(0,1). For integers j>=2,
  E Z^j <=(2L)^j (2j-1)!! <=(4L)^j j!,
  E|Z-EZ|^j <=2^j E Z^j <=(8L)^j j!.
The last inequality uses convexity (EZ)^j<=EZ^j and |r-s|^j<=2^{j-1}(r^j+s^j). The Bernstein moment condition therefore holds with variance parameter 128L^2 and scale 8L. Scalar Bernstein gives, for t>0, a two-sided probability <=2exp(-t) at deviation
  16L[sqrt(t/m)+t/m].
A 1/4-net of the unit sphere has <=9^d elements and a symmetric matrix H satisfies ||H||op<=2 max_net |v^THv|. Thus for a deterministic set of J scalar thresholds in [Lmin,Lmax], with probability >=1-delta_C,
  max_grid ||Qhat_L-Q_L||op <=
  E_m:=32Lmax[sqrt(t_C/m)+t_C/m],
  t_C=d log9+log(2J/delta_C).                             (5.1)

The map L->g_L(y) is 2-Lipschitz for every real y. Let Ahat=m^{-1}sum aa^T. A further Gaussian norm event gives
  ||Ahat||op<=H_m:=(sqrt(m)+sqrt(d)+sqrt(2log(1/delta_A)))^2/m
with probability >=1-delta_A. If the mesh is at most Delta and endpoints are included, every L in the interval is within Delta of a grid point. Then
  ||Qhat_L-Q_L||op<=E_m+2Delta(H_m+1)                    (5.2)
uniformly over that entire interval. This is a uniform clean-data event, so L may be selected arbitrarily from all corrupted observations. The mesh is an analysis device and is not computed by the algorithm.

6. Exact initializer theorem and complete stability proof

Set beta=21eps<1/2. Choose A>0 and use L=A h, with h from Section3. On the scale event let Lmin=A(1-nu)u and Lmax=A(1+nu)u. Take any grid mesh Delta>0 covering this interval, with J<=ceil((Lmax-Lmin)/Delta)+2. Let gamma in (0,gamma0) satisfy 20eps<gamma, where gamma0 is the absolute constant in the robust PCA source. If (3.2) and

 E_m+2Delta(H_m+1)+(15u^2+sigma^2)/Lmin
       +2Lmax G_k+beta(Lmax+3u)
    <=(1-beta)gamma(Lmin+u),                            (6.1)

then with probability >=1-delta_s-delta_C-delta_A-delta_D, every clean empirical distribution at every L in the allowed interval is (beta,gamma)-stable with respect to S_L, including arbitrary fractional adaptive deletion.

Proof. For weights w_i in [0,1] with alpha=m^{-1}sum(1-w_i)<=beta, let D=m^{-1}sum(1-w_i)g_L(y_i)a_i a_i^T. The weighted covariance is
  Q_w=(Qhat_L-D)/(1-alpha).
By Sections2,4,5,
  ||Q_w-S_L||op
  <=[E_m+2Delta(H_m+1)+b(Lmin)+2LmaxG_k+alpha||S_L||op]/(1-alpha)
  <=gamma(Lmin+u)<=gamma lambda_min(S_L).
The last two inequalities follow from (6.1), ||S_L||<=Lmax+3u and alpha<=beta. Thus (1-gamma)S_L<=Q_w<=(1+gamma)S_L in Loewner order. This argument is completely uniform in w and L.

Remove the <=eps m original clean points that the adversary replaced. Any subsequent fractional deletion of at most a 20eps fraction of the survivor set has original clean deletion mass at most eps+(1-eps)20eps<=21eps. Hence the empirical survivor distribution G is (20eps,gamma)-stable relative to the same S_L. This explicitly repairs the insufficient (eps,gamma) hypothesis in the printed AISTATS PropositionF.2.

Invoke Diakonikolas--Kane--Pensia--Pittas ICML2023, TheoremC.1 and optimized Algorithm5, not merely its conditional certificate Algorithm2. For input uniform P=(1-eps)G+eps B, it runs in
  O(md gamma^{-2} log^4(d/eps))                           (6.2)
and with probability >=0.99 returns a unit v satisfying
  v^T S_L v >=(1-C_P gamma)(L+3u),                       (6.3)
for an absolute C_P. The PSD target S_L need not equal Q_L; empirical stability is the primitive's exact hypothesis. Fraction smaller than eps can be padded in the mixture by assigning arbitrary additional inliers to B; the same clean-deletion calculation still applies.

Since S_L has gap2u, (6.3) gives
  1-|<v,x*/q>|^2 <= C_P gamma(L+3u)/(2u),
  dist(v,x*/q)^2<=C_P gamma(L+3u)/u.                     (6.4)

For confidence delta_P rather than 0.99, repeat the PCA primitive independently J_P=ceil(8log(2/delta_P)) times on the same corrupted data. Let rho0 be an individual success radius from (6.4). Select a candidate minimizing the radius that includes a strict majority of candidates in the sign-invariant Euclidean metric dist(v,w)=min(||v-w||,||v+w||). The clean-data stability event is fixed under the algorithm seeds, so at least a strict majority of calls succeed except with probability <=exp(-0.48J_P)<=delta_P. All successful candidates are mutually within2rho0. The selected majority ball of radius<=2rho0 intersects the successful majority, hence the selected candidate is within3rho0 of x*/q. Pairwise distances and selection cost O(J_P^2d); no independent validation sample or noise-scale knowledge is used.

Return x0=sqrt(h)v_selected. Then
  dist(x0,x*)/q <= |sqrt(h/u)-1|+3rho0 <=nu+3rho0.        (6.5)
For nu=1/27 and rho0=1/81 this is <=2/27<1/9, entering the source refinement basin.

7. Consequential explicit asymptotic regime

Choose nu=1/27. Take alpha>0 a sufficiently small absolute constant such that C_P alpha<=1/81^2 and alpha<gamma0/4. Set
  A=C_L(15+R^2), with C_L sufficiently large absolute,
  gamma=alpha/[A(1+nu)+3],
  Delta=alpha u/1000.
The clipping bias is u/[C_L(1-nu)] and J=O(A/alpha). The sample conditions (3.2),(6.1) and 20eps<gamma hold whenever
  eps log(e/eps)<=c/(1+R^2),
  m>=C(1+R^2)^2[d+log((1+R^2)/delta)],                  (7.1)
where c,C are absolute constants depending only on the fixed basin radius and the source's absolute PCA constants. To verify this, use (sqrt p+sqrt q+sqrt r)^2<=3(p+q+r) in G_k, k/m<=beta+1/m, and klog(em/k)/m<=C[beta log(e/beta)+log(em)/m]. For beta m>=1, k<=2beta m and the last expression is O(beta log(e/beta)); for beta m<1, k=1 and log(em)/m<=O(log m/m). The latter term is absorbed by increasing C and adding a harmless log m if necessary. More explicitly, one may take
  m>=C(1+R^2)^2[d+log((1+R^2)/(delta eps))]
to avoid any asymptotic absorption ambiguity. The exact sufficient theorem remains (6.1), including the ceiling k.

Initializer runtime after confidence boosting is
  O(md(1+R^2)^2 log^4(d/eps) log(1/delta)
      +d log^2(1/delta)+m).
For fixed R,eps,confidence this is near-linear in the input md and uses O(d) samples up to confidence logarithms. Compared with the original ya route, it needs only finite conditional variance, improves the sufficient contamination scaling from roughly r_up^{-4} to R^{-2}/log(1/eps), and uses a different sample/noise tradeoff m~dR^4. This is not a claim that this contamination level is maximal or that all noise/sample dependencies are optimal.

The unavoidable Gaussian deletion loss is eps log(e/eps) even at y constant. Thus a generic bounded-amplitude PCA stability proof cannot remove that logarithm. Whether the noise-dependent portion can be improved using a signed matrix algorithm or another representation is open in this record.

8. Remaining end-to-end obligations

(a) For one noncausal global adversary, previous corrupted batches/initializer can encode future clean batches. Random splitting balances corruption counts but does not itself establish that future clean observations are independent of the iterate. A uniform-in-iterate robust gradient guarantee, an alternative refinement procedure, or a carefully specified causal fresh-batch oracle is necessary.
(b) The conditional noise variance sigma may be unknown. A near-linear mean primitive supplied only a loose covariance upper bound gives a floor involving that upper bound, not automatically sigma. Candidate repair: fully variance-oblivious Depersin--Lecue robust mean estimation, with a strong-replacement extension checked from the original deterministic block event. This source has been retrieved but is not yet imported here.
(c) Exact recovery when sigma=0 follows only in a limit/precision statement after finite optimization, not from setting the displayed statistical error to zero at finite iterations. Any final theorem must keep the decaying optimization term or an accuracy input.
(d) A bit-complexity guarantee needs input encoding, rounding and the PCA solver precision model. Current (6.2) is real arithmetic. Near-linear initialization alone is not end-to-end near-linear robust nonlinear recovery.
