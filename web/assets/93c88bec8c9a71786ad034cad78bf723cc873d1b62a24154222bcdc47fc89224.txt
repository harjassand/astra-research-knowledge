1. What changed after v1

The v1 theorem starts with independent Fourier blocks. That makes its Poisson proof exact but does not explain when a physical-space dependence mechanism produces the required decorrelation. This record replaces those supplied blocks by a non-Gaussian, finite-range dependent physical coefficient field. The finite-range field creates random, dependent Fourier means. A fixed Gaussian component in physical space is retained as an explicit regularizer: after the DFT, it makes the blocks conditionally independent and gives a uniform small-singular-value estimate.

The result is therefore a physical-space universality theorem with an explicit regularization cost. It is not a theorem for arbitrary dependent matrices, and it does not remove the Gaussian floor. The limit depends on the finite-range field only through its covariance spectrum. Letting the Gaussian floor tend to zero with n, or deleting it, is unresolved below.

2. Physical model and exact Fourier representation

Fix a block size p>=2, a deterministic complex p by p matrix D, an integer m>=0, and a regularization parameter sigma>0. Let (V_j)_{j in Z} be a stationary, centered, proper complex process in C^(p^2), bounded almost surely by a fixed constant, and m-dependent: sigma-fields generated by index sets separated by more than m are independent. Proper means E[V_j V_l^T]=0 for all j,l. Write

    R_h = E[V_{j+h} V_j^*],
    S(theta) = sum_{h=-m}^m exp(-i h theta) R_h,  0<=theta<2 pi.

S(theta) is the continuous nonnegative Hermitian spectral covariance matrix of the finite-range process; it is allowed to be singular. Independently, let G_j be iid standard proper complex Gaussian vectors in C^(p^2), with covariance I_(p^2). Identify C^(p^2) with p by p matrices by a fixed vectorization map. For each n, use the first n coordinates of both sequences, set indices modulo n for the convolution, and define

    A_{r,n} = 1_{r=0} D + n^(-1/2) mat(V_r + sigma G_r),  0<=r<n,
    (C_n x)_r = sum_{s=0}^{n-1} A_{r-s mod n,n} x_s,
        x in C^n tensor C^p.

With the unitary DFT convention that diagonalizes this convolution, put theta_k=2 pi k/n and

    Vhat_{k,n} = n^(-1/2) sum_{j=0}^{n-1} exp(-i theta_k j) V_j,
    Ghat_{k,n} = n^(-1/2) sum_{j=0}^{n-1} exp(-i theta_k j) G_j,
    B_{k,n} = D + mat(Vhat_{k,n}) + sigma mat(Ghat_{k,n}).

Then exactly

    (F_n tensor I_p) C_n (F_n tensor I_p)^* = direct_sum_{k=0}^{n-1} B_{k,n}.

The Ghat_{k,n} are independent standard proper Gaussian vectors: this follows from the unitary invariance of the joint Gaussian law of (G_0,...,G_(n-1)). Conditional on the entire physical sequence V_0,...,V_(n-1), the blocks B_{k,n} are therefore independent Gaussian matrices with random means

    M_{k,n}=D+mat(Vhat_{k,n})

and common entry covariance sigma^2 I_(p^2). The Fourier means are not independent at finite n. The conditional block independence is derived from iid noise in physical space, rather than assumed as a law for the Fourier blocks.

3. Physical dependence estimates

For any fixed order r>=3, any coordinate choices, any conjugation pattern, and any frequencies k_1,...,k_r, multilinearity of cumulants gives a sum over physical indices j_1,...,j_r with prefactor n^(-r/2). A cumulant of V-coordinates vanishes unless the index dependency graph is connected. For an m-dependent sequence and fixed r, the number of connected r-tuples is at most C_r n(2rm+1)^(r-1); boundedness gives a uniform bound on each remaining cumulant. Hence

    |cum(Vhat_{k_1}^{(a_1)},...,Vhat_{k_r}^{(a_r)})|
        <= C_(r,m,V) n^(1-r/2),  r>=3,

uniformly in the frequency tuple. This is the explicit higher-cumulant mechanism: every fixed higher cumulant of normalized Fourier modes vanishes, even though the full vector of all n modes is not independent.

The second cumulants have a sharper frequency calculation. For k=l,

    E[Vhat_{k,n} Vhat_{k,n}^*] = S(theta_k)+O(n^(-1)),

uniformly in k. For k!=l, the full geometric sum cancels and only O(|h|) endpoint terms remain for each lag h, giving

    ||E[Vhat_{k,n} Vhat_{l,n}^*]|| <= C_(m,V)/n.

All pseudo-covariances vanish by properness. Consequently, for any fixed number of distinct modes, their joint law converges to independent proper Gaussian vectors with respective covariances S(theta_k), in the triangular-array sense: along every subsequence on which the frequencies converge, the covariance limits are the indicated S-values, all cumulants above order two vanish, and uniform moment bounds imply moment convergence to the Gaussian law. The conclusion is uniform over distinct mode tuples by compactness of the frequency torus. No lower bound on S is needed because the sigma-noise later smooths each block.

Define the random empirical measure of Fourier means

    nu_n^V = (1/n) sum_{k=0}^{n-1} delta_{M_{k,n}}.

Then nu_n^V converges weakly in probability to the deterministic probability measure

    nu = (1/(2 pi)) integral_0^(2 pi) Law(D+mat(Z_theta)) d theta,
    Z_theta ~ CN(0,S(theta)).

Here CN may be degenerate when S(theta) is singular. To verify the empirical convergence, take bounded continuous phi. The one-mode CLT and a Riemann sum give convergence of E integral phi d nu_n^V to integral phi d nu. For distinct k,l, the two-mode CLT gives asymptotic independence uniformly over distinct pairs; the diagonal pairs contribute only O(1/n) to the variance of the empirical average. Thus the variance tends to zero. The same cumulant estimates give uniform moments of every fixed order for Vhat_{k,n}; truncation then extends the empirical convergence to continuous test functions with any fixed polynomial growth, provided the growth is bounded by such a moment.

4. Fixed-block Gaussian smoothing lemma

For a deterministic p by p mean M, let Q_{M,sigma}=M+sigma G, with G a standard complex Ginibre p by p matrix. Let q_M(z) be its one-point eigenvalue intensity (counting all p eigenvalues), and q_M^(2)(z,w) its second factorial intensity. For every compact K subset C there are constants C_K and a finite exponent a_p, depending only on p, sigma and K, such that

    sup_{z in K} (|q_M(z)|+|partial_z q_M(z)|+|partial_zbar q_M(z)|)
        <= C_K (1+||M||_F)^(a_p),
    sup_{z,w in K} q_M^(2)(z,w) <= C_K (1+||M||_F)^(a_p).

These bounds follow directly from complex Schur decomposition. In the Gaussian matrix density, write Q=UTU* with diagonal lambda_i and strict upper entries t_ij. After integrating the t_ij, the eigenvalue density has Vandermonde factor |Delta(lambda)|^2 and a unitary integral whose integrand is bounded by

    exp(-sigma^(-2) [sum_i |lambda_i-(U*M U)_(ii)|^2
                      + sum_(i>j) |(U*M U)_(ij)|^2]) <= 1.

For a one- or two-point intensity, integrate the remaining eigenvalue coordinates against the Gaussian factors. The Vandermonde is a fixed polynomial, and each diagonal center has magnitude at most ||M||_F. Gaussian moments of that polynomial are bounded by a polynomial in 1+||M||_F. Differentiating in z adds only another fixed polynomial factor. This proves the stated finite-power bounds. In particular, for measurable H subset K,

    E[Z_M(H)] <= C_K (1+||M||_F)^(a_p) |H|,
    E[Z_M(H)(Z_M(H)-1)] <= C_K (1+||M||_F)^(a_p) |H|^2,

where Z_M(H) counts eigenvalues in H and |H| denotes planar area. The constants can change between displays.

5. Candidate local theorem

For z in C define

    rho(z) = (1/p) integral q_M(z) nu(dM).

This is finite and continuous by the polynomial bound and the moment bounds for the Gaussian mixtures Z_theta. It is strictly positive for every z: for each fixed M, the matrix density of M+sigma G is positive on all of C^(p by p), and its Schur eigenvalue intensity q_M(z) is positive at every z.

Let N=np. Fix z_0 in C and define the point process

    Xi_n = sum_{lambda in spec(C_n)} delta_{sqrt(N rho(z_0)) (lambda-z_0)}.

Claim. Under the assumptions in Section 2, Xi_n converges in distribution in the vague topology to homogeneous planar Poisson point process of intensity one.

Proof. Condition on V_0,...,V_(n-1). The blocks B_{k,n} are conditionally independent. For a fixed compact K in the rescaled plane, let

    H_n = z_0 + (N rho(z_0))^(-1/2) K.

Then |H_n|=O(1/n). From Section 4, conditionally on V,

    E[Z_{k,n}(H_n)|V] <= C_K n^(-1)(1+||M_{k,n}||)^(a_p),
    E[Z_{k,n}(H_n)(Z_{k,n}(H_n)-1)|V]
        <= C_K n^(-2)(1+||M_{k,n}||)^(a_p).

The empirical mean measure convergence and its polynomial-growth extension imply

    (1/(np)) sum_k q_{M_{k,n}}(z) -> rho(z)

locally uniformly in z in probability. Local uniformity follows from convergence on finite nets and the derivative bounds in Section 4. Also

    (1/n) sum_k (1+||M_{k,n}||)^(2a_p) = O_p(1).

Thus the total conditional probability that some block contributes two points to H_n is O_p(1/n), and the sum of squared conditional hit probabilities is O_p(1/n).

For f>=0 continuous with compact support, let xi_{k,n} be the rescaled eigenvalue measure from block k, and put

    a_{k,n}=E[1-exp(-<f,xi_{k,n}>) | V].

Replacing 1-exp(-<f,xi_{k,n}>) by the sum of 1-exp(-f) over individual eigenvalues costs at most a constant times Z_{k,n}(H_n)(Z_{k,n}(H_n)-1). Summing the error over modes gives o_p(1). Conditional independence and the preceding squared-hit estimate give

    E[exp(-<f,Xi_n>)|V] = product_k (1-a_{k,n})
        = exp(-sum_k a_{k,n}+o_p(1)).

With w=sqrt(N rho(z_0))(z-z_0), the local uniform density convergence gives

    sum_k a_{k,n}
      = integral_C (1-exp(-f(w)))
          [ (1/(np rho(z_0))) sum_k q_{M_{k,n}}(z_0+w/sqrt(N rho(z_0))) ] dA(w)
          + o_p(1)
      -> integral_C (1-exp(-f(w))) dA(w)

in probability. The conditional Laplace functional is bounded by one, so its expectation converges to the same deterministic limit. This is the unit-intensity planar Poisson Laplace functional. The proof uses no local law and no entry independence for C_n. The sigma-regularizer is fixed as n grows.

Universality statement actually proved: if two bounded, centered, proper, m-dependent physical processes have the same covariance matrices R_h, then they have the same S(theta), nu, rho, and limiting unit-density local process, for the same D and sigma. Their higher moments and other finite-range dependence details do not enter the limit. This is a conditional universality class with a fixed Gaussian component, not a claim for every dependent non-Hermitian ensemble.

6. Small singular values and an explicit robustness cost

For every deterministic M, z in C, and t>=0, independent shifted Gaussian columns give

    P(s_min(z I_p - M - sigma G) <= t) <= p^2 t^2 / sigma^2.

Indeed, if s_min(A)<=t, then for some column j its distance to the span of the other columns is at most sqrt(p)t: choose a unit vector x with ||Ax||<=t and a coordinate |x_j|>=1/sqrt(p). Conditional on the other columns, the projection of column j onto their one-dimensional orthogonal complement is a shifted complex Gaussian of variance sigma^2. Its probability of lying in the disk of radius sqrt(p)t is at most p t^2/sigma^2. A union bound over j proves the claim. The estimate is uniform in M and z, so it remains true after conditioning on the dependent physical signal.

Since s_min(zI-C_n)=min_k s_min(zI_p-B_{k,n}),

    P(s_min(zI-C_n)<=t) <= min(1, n p^2 t^2/sigma^2).

For any realized matrix C and fixed z, the least operator-norm perturbation E for which z belongs to spec(C+E) is exactly s_min(zI-C): the lower bound is the singular-value perturbation inequality, and equality is attained by a rank-one perturbation built from left and right singular vectors. Therefore, for every fixed z and delta>=0,

    P(exists E with ||E||<=delta and z in spec(C_n+E))
        <= min(1, n p^2 delta^2/sigma^2).

This bound is a robust point-prediction capability with an honest cost. At perturbation radius delta=c/sqrt(n), the upper bound is min(1,p^2 c^2/sigma^2). The cost diverges as sigma decreases; no uniform sigma->0 conclusion is asserted. For the full epsilon-pseudospectrum the exact identity remains

    sigma_epsilon(C_n) = union_k { z : s_min(z I_p-B_{k,n}) <= epsilon }.

This handles nonnormality through singular values. The rank-one cyclic/open shift counterexample in v1 still shows why a norm-small boundary change alone cannot give eigenvalue stability.

7. Nonnormal examples inside the class

Take p=2 and D=LJ with J=e_1 e_2^*. For fixed L, each Fourier block has a Gaussian component of full support, so the commutator is nonzero almost surely. Conditional on the Fourier means, the block commutators are independent with polynomially bounded moments. The conditional variance of their normalized sum tends to zero, and Section 3 controls the empirical law and moments of the means. Consequently,

    (1/(np)) ||[C_n,C_n^*]||_F^2
       -> (1/p) integral E ||[M+sigma G,(M+sigma G)^*]||_F^2 nu(dM)

in probability. This limit is positive. As L grows through fixed values, [J,J*]=diag(1,-1) gives

    L^(-4) E ||[LJ+M_0+sigma G,(LJ+M_0+sigma G)^*]||_F^2 -> 2,

for any random M_0 with bounded fourth moment independent of L. Therefore the normalized commutator density grows like L^4/p (equal to L^4 for p=2). Every fixed L is covered by the theorem; L=L_n growing with dimension is not.

8. Frontier comparison and status

The primary comparison is limited; none of these comparisons establish novelty or priority.

* Ajanki, Erdős and Krüger, “Local Spectral Statistics of Gaussian Matrices with Correlated Entries,” J. Stat. Phys. 163 (2016), 280–302, DOI 10.1007/s10955-016-1479-y. Their model is self-adjoint Gaussian with translation-invariant entry correlations; they prove an optimal local law and bulk universality using Fourier structure. This is a substantial nearby precedent but has a different self-adjoint large-matrix mechanism, rather than fixed-size nonnormal symbols and a planar Poisson limit.
* Bose, Hazra and Saha, “Poisson Convergence of Eigenvalues of Circulant Type Matrices,” Extremes 14 (2011), 365–392, author manuscript at https://repository.ias.ac.in/5488/1/327.pdf. Their Poisson point process concerns frequency-marked extreme eigenvalues for scalar circulant-type models, with partial two-sided moving-average extensions. It is relevant dependence precedent, but not the fixed-z bulk microscopic process considered here.
* Tao and Vu, “Random matrices: Universality of local spectral statistics of non-Hermitian matrices,” arXiv:1206.1893v7. Their bulk point-process universality is for jointly independent entries and a Ginibre-type limit; it emphasizes singular-value/log-determinant control in the non-Hermitian setting. The present candidate has a repeated-coefficient block-circulant structure and a Poisson limit.

Targeted live search also found no direct fixed-block nonnormal bulk point-process result. This is a limited search, so novelty and priority remain unresolved. 9. Exact finite gate if the Gaussian floor is removed

With sigma=0, the conditional independence and the small-singular-value lemma disappear. For compact planar sets A_1,...,A_r in the rescaled plane, set

    H_n(A_i)=z_0+(np rho(z_0))^(-1/2) A_i,
    X_{k,n}(A_i)=#(spec(D+mat(Vhat_{k,n})) intersect H_n(A_i)).

A sufficient missing rare-event decorrelation estimate, for every fixed r>=2 and such A_i, is

    sum_{k_1,...,k_r all distinct}
      | E product_{i=1}^r X_{k_i,n}(A_i)
          - product_{i=1}^r E X_{k_i,n}(A_i) |  -> 0.                 (GATE)

Together with one-mode intensity convergence and a same-mode multiple-hit bound, (GATE) would make the factorial moment measures of the total process converge to those of Poisson. The cumulant estimate in Section 3 is not enough by itself: it controls fixed moments of the Fourier values, whereas (GATE) concerns matrix eigenvalue events at area O(1/n) in as many as n^r mode tuples. No proof of (GATE) is supplied here. A second needed estimate for sigma=0 is a uniform small-singular-value bound for zI_p-D-mat(Vhat_{k,n}) at t of order n^(-1/2). Thus the present theorem reaches a precise, physically dependent regularized class and leaves this exact non-Gaussian unregularized bridge open.
