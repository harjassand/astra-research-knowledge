ADVERSARIAL AUDIT: ROOT CODIMENSION GREEN BOUND, §§1–2
8 October 2026. Independent derivation/check; no external validation claimed.

Scope and verdict
-----------------
The arbitrary-matrix bridge, complex Hilbert-space Cauchy–Schwarz step,
invariant-projector mass factor, and stated finite spectral converse are
valid under the hypotheses in ROOT_CODIMENSION_GREEN_BOUND.txt. Positivity of
A is not used. The stated cutoff B_K(k) is valid, including K=0 and K=J, but
uses the coarser total-mass estimate k. The exact traceless mass
T_k = k - k^2/d yields a uniformly stronger version of every cutoff bound.
“Exact finite spectral statement” should mean an explicit finite-dimensional
inequality; the displayed lower bound is not asserted to equal the true
rank-constrained infimum.

1. Arbitrary complex A and the Green/Cauchy–Schwarz bridge
---------------------------------------------------------
Use <B,C> = Tr(B* C), conjugate-linear in the first slot. Let

  Lambda_k(L) = inf { <A,LA>/||A||_HS^2 : 0 != A in End(H), rank(A)<=d-k },

where 1<=k<d. Since nullity(A)>=k, choose a rank-k orthogonal projection P
onto a k-dimensional subspace of ker(A). Thus AP=0, and cyclicity gives
Tr(PA)=Tr(AP)=0. Decompose A=aI+X with a=Tr(A)/d and Tr(X)=0. Then

  0 = Tr(PA) = ka + Tr(PX),   hence   ka = -<P0,X>,
  P0 = P-(k/d)I.

The last equality uses P=P*, Tr(X)=0, so <P0,X>=Tr(PX). For complex X and
complex a, take absolute values; there is no real-valuedness assumption.
Because L is self-adjoint, positive, and ker(L)=span{I}, it restricts to a
positive invertible operator on the traceless subspace. With its positive
square roots there,

  <P0,X> = <L^(-1/2)P0, L^(1/2)X>,
  k^2 |a|^2 <= <P0,L^(-1)P0> <X,LX> = G(P) E(A).

Here E(A)=<A,LA>=<X,LX>: self-adjointness and LI=0 imply L maps the
traceless space to itself, and the scalar component is orthogonal to it.
If lambda_1 is the least positive eigenvalue, spectral calculus also gives
||X||^2<=E(A)/lambda_1. Orthogonality of aI and X gives

  ||A||^2 = d|a|^2+||X||^2
          <= E(A) [ d G(P)/k^2 + 1/lambda_1 ].                 (A)

All factors in (A) check: the d is from ||aI||^2=d|a|^2, and k^2 comes
from |ka|^2. No positivity, normality, or diagonalizability of A is used.
For a nonzero admissible A, E(A)>0: otherwise A is a scalar multiple of I,
and a nonzero such matrix has rank d, contrary to k>=1. Thus division in
the ensuing Rayleigh quotient is legitimate.

2. Invariant projector mass and rank normalization
---------------------------------------------------
Fix vectorization vec: End(H)->H tensor conjugate(H), so
vec(UAU*)=(U tensor conjugate(U))vec(A) and ||vec(A)||=||A||_HS. For an
orthogonal projector Pi onto an invariant subspace of dimension b, its
invariance and irreducibility of U on H imply

  Tr_2(Pi) commutes with every U, hence Tr_2(Pi)=(b/d) I.

Indeed its trace is Tr(Pi)=b, which fixes the scalar. (The choice of traced
factor is tied to this vectorization; the conjugate representation on the
other factor is irreducible as well.)

For rank-k P, v=vec(P)/sqrt(k) is a unit vector of Schmidt rank k. Let Q be
the projection onto its first-factor support; Q=P in this convention and
rank(Q)=k. With R=Q tensor I, v=Rv and 0<=R Pi R<=R. Therefore

  ||Pi v||^2 = <v,Pi v> = <v,R Pi R v>
             <= ||R Pi R|| <= Tr(R Pi R)
              = Tr(Q Tr_2 Pi) = kb/d.

The norm-square, not amplitude, is bounded here. Restoring
||vec(P)||^2=k multiplies the bound by k, giving

  ||Pi P||_HS^2 <= k^2 b/d.                                  (B)

This verifies the rank-k normalization in the source. A common erroneous
factor would be kb/d for the unnormalized P; that misses the extra factor k.

For the distinct positive spectral projectors Pi_j, b=b_j. They annihilate
the scalar identity, so Pi_j P=Pi_j P0. The exact total positive-spectrum
mass is

  sum_(j=1)^J alpha_j = ||P0||^2
    = Tr((P-(k/d)I)^2) = k-k^2/d =: T_k,
  alpha_j := ||Pi_j P||^2 >= 0.                              (C)

The equality in (C) follows by expanding P^2=P and Tr(P)=k. In particular
T_k<=k, with strict inequality for every admissible k,d.

3. Cutoffs, endpoints, and the finite converse
-----------------------------------------------
Since lambda_1<...<lambda_J, for any integer 0<=K<=J,

  G(P) = sum_j alpha_j/lambda_j
       <= (k^2/d) sum_(j=1)^K b_j/lambda_j
          + (sum_(j>K) alpha_j)/lambda_(K+1)
       <= (k^2/d) sum_(j=1)^K b_j/lambda_j + k/lambda_(K+1)
        = B_K(k),

where lambda_(J+1)=infinity and 1/infinity is defined as zero. In the first
inequality, (B) bounds each low-frequency alpha_j, and every high-frequency
denominator is at least lambda_(K+1). This verifies the written B_K(k).

Endpoint checks:
* K=0: the low sum is empty and the source gives B_0=k/lambda_1. The
  high-frequency sum is the entire positive spectrum.
* K=J: the high-frequency set is empty; the tail is exactly zero by the
  infinity convention, and B_J=(k^2/d) sum_(j=1)^J b_j/lambda_j.
* If J=1, these are the two valid choices K=0,1; no intermediate cutoff is
  needed. Under 1<=k<d and ker(L)=span{I}, the traceless space is nonzero, so
  J>=1.

There is a simple uniform sharpening. By (C), the high-frequency mass is at
most T_k, not merely k. Thus all the same arguments give

  B_K^sharp(k) := (k^2/d) sum_(j=1)^K b_j/lambda_j
                    + T_k/lambda_(K+1),
  G(P) <= B_K^sharp(k) <= B_K(k).                            (D)

For K=0 this reads G(P)<=T_k/lambda_1, the direct bound from
||P0||^2=T_k. For K=J the tail is zero, so B_J^sharp=B_J. The strict
improvement at K<J follows from T_k<k and lambda_(K+1)>0. Taking minima
over K preserves validity and can strengthen the final converse. (D) is a
valid improvement, not a claim that the cutoff estimate is optimal: the
low- and high-mass bounds need not be simultaneously attained.

Set B(k)=min_(0<=K<=J) B_K(k). Combining G(P)<=B(k) with (A), for every
nonzero A of rank at most d-k,

  E(A)/||A||_HS^2 >= [ d B(k)/k^2 + 1/lambda_1 ]^(-1),

and hence the same bound holds for Lambda_k(L). The identical statement
holds with B^sharp(k)=min_K B_K^sharp(k), which is no larger than B(k) and
therefore produces a no-weaker lower bound. This is a finite spectral
converse requiring only (d,k), eigenvalues, and multiplicities. It does not
prove equality for Lambda_k(L), a matching optimizer, or any geometric
specialization. The argument assumes throughout that L is positive
self-adjoint with kernel exactly span{I}, and that its spectral projectors
are invariant under an irreducible unitary carrier.

Audit conclusion: §§1–2 are mathematically sound as written on their stated
hypotheses. Recommended edit is to state the stronger T_k tail variant (D)
or explicitly call the existing k-tail a convenient coarse bound, and to
describe (2) as an explicit finite spectral lower bound rather than wording
that could be read as an exact evaluation of the infimum.
