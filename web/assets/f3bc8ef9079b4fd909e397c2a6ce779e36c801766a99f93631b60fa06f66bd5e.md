# Primary-source notes and boundaries

Search checked 8 October 2026. This is a bounded source check, not an exhaustive novelty or priority search. Read primary publisher/PubMed Central full text or article records where available; no raw sequencing dataset was downloaded or analyzed. All below are source-reported findings, not results reconstructed from their raw data.

## Closest mechanistic precedents

1. **Wray et al. (2025), “Time-varying stimuli that prolong IKK activation promote nuclear remodeling and mechanistic switching of NF-κB dynamics,” Nature Communications 16, 7329.** [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-62837-0), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-62837-0).
   - U2OS dual-reporter and microfluidic experiments measure NEMO/complex-I and nuclear RelA dynamics in the same cells. Equal cumulative IL-1 exposures delivered in short pulse trains can produce disproportionately greater nuclear RelA AUC than a single pulse; the 4×1.5-minute versus 1×6-minute comparison is a representative result. CI adaptation around/above 80 minutes associates with pseudo-zero-order nuclear export.
   - The paper reports that its D2FC baseline model does not reproduce the pulse-train switch, including after many parameter fits. D2FC2 adds explicit NF-κB DNA binding and NF-κB-dependent chromatin remodeling and recapitulates the behavior within that model/data setting.
   - It also measures RelA immobilization and H3K4me3, and reports a perturbation that removes the H3K4me3 increase with little change in immobilized RelA. This cautions against using H3K4me3 alone as a causal proxy for RelA retention.
   - Boundary: this is already a strong dynamic signaling-plus-chromatin explanation for the pulse-train phenomenon. The present LTI gain law cannot explain that result; the exponential target-enhancer kernel below is a distinct, simpler hypothesis for gene-specific decoding *conditional on measured TF trajectories*.

2. **Wang et al. (2025), “Macrophage memory emerges from coordinated transcription factor and chromatin dynamics,” Cell Systems 16(2), 101171.** [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12147866/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2025.101171).
   - The study combines single-cell NF-κB dynamics, chromatin-accessibility profiles, transcriptional profiles, sequential inflammatory signals, and an in-vivo sepsis model. It documents that memory can be encoded upstream in signaling and at chromatin, and that co-regulating TF families (including IRF, AP-1, and C/EBP) complicate an NF-κB-only account.
   - Its learned expression predictor uses both TF dynamics and chromatin/motif information. Thus joint data-driven prediction of macrophage memory is prior art, and the new candidate cannot be framed as the first multiscale predictor or first evidence for two-layer memory.
   - Boundary: it does not establish the exact causal perturbation decomposition claimed here; that is a proposed falsification target, not evidence of novelty.

3. **Luo et al. (2023), “Dynamic network-guided CRISPRi screen identifies CTCF-loop-constrained nonlinear enhancer-gene regulatory activity during cell state transitions,” Nature Genetics 55.** [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11012226/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-023-01450-7).
   - A time-resolved CRISPRi screen in the hESC-to-definitive-endoderm transition finds temporally varying enhancer effects and reports that enhancer perturbations have delayed effects and much weaker effects after transition completion, consistent with nonlinear saturation of core TF circuitry.
   - Boundary: generic “time-resolved enhancer perturbation identifies state-transition drivers” is preempted. The candidate is restricted to a small, explicitly observed TF/feedback/enhancer interface and makes no cell-fate claim.

## Cell-fate/control boundary

4. **Lv et al. (2025), “Landscape control for cell fate transitions,” Communications Physics 8, 240.** [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02160-8), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-025-02160-8).
   - A landscape-control approach is benchmarked on modeled MISA, EMT, and hESC networks against earlier optimal least-action control, with sparse intervention recommendations.
   - Boundary: generic landscape-based in-silico cell-fate control is already published; its model predictions are not experimental reprogramming evidence.

5. **Rashid & Hegade (2025), “Operating principles of interconnected feedback loops driving cell fate transitions,” npj Systems Biology and Applications 11, 2.** [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41540-024-00483-w), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-024-00483-w).
   - Analyzes feedback-loop topologies and multistability in theoretical GRNs; supports the point that durable transitions require feedback/attractor mechanisms absent from a finite-decay enhancer-memory model.

## Search boundary and unresolved novelty

The primary-source pass was directed at (i) NF-κB signal/chromatin memory, (ii) dynamic enhancer perturbation during fate transition, and (iii) recent GRN control methods. It was not a systematic review of all gene-circuit system-identification/control literature, patent search, or priority search for the derived kernel or sign law. Both local models below are simple applications of standard linear/bilinear systems reasoning. No assertion of firstness, priority, novelty, clinical relevance, or natural cellular capability is supported.
