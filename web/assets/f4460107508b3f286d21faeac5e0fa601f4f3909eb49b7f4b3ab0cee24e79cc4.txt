VERDICT

The scalar-to-operator boundary passage (S10)-(S12) checks out. The apparent
large weight e^(O(sqrt(N))) is paid twice where needed: once for the stopped
Feynman-Kac branch and once for the remaining quantum sandwich semigroup. It is
dominated by the e^(-kappa N) initial-tail and e^(-kappa N^(3/2)) hitting costs.
The target trace is at least the heat-seed trace, so normalization introduces no
small-denominator loss. I also independently reconstructed the Bloch generator,
inner-ball ellipticity, drift and martingale estimates used to obtain those
scalar probabilities. No defect was found in these estimates. This remains an
internal proof audit; it does not certify external priority or implementation.

1. DIFFERENTIAL INTERTWINING, INDEPENDENTLY DERIVED

For |m|<1 write tau_m=(I+m.sigma)/2 and k_N(m)=tau_m^(tensor N). For unit n,
let u=n.m, v=n-u m, r=n cross m and define

  B_n=N u+v.grad_m,       R_n=r.grad_m.

At one site, with S_n=n.sigma/2,

  S_n tau_m+tau_m S_n = u tau_m + (v.sigma)/2,
  [S_n,tau_m] = i (r.sigma)/2.

The product rule gives {n.J,k_N}=B_n k_N and [n.J,k_N]=iR_n k_N.
Applying these exact representations again,
B_n^2 k_N={F,{F,k_N}} and R_n^2 k_N=-[F,[F,k_N]], F=n.J. Hence

  {F^2,k_N}/2=(B_n^2-R_n^2)k_N/4.                         (A)

For C=A+Lambda I with Lambda=M+1, C>=I; diagonalize C=sum lambda_i n_i n_i^T.
With s=N^(3/4), t0=2/N-Lambda/s^2,
H=Q_C/s^2+b.J/s, the sandwich generator M_H(X)={H,X}/2 acts on k_N as
G k_N, where

  G=sum_i lambda_i(B_ni^2-R_ni^2)/(4s^2)+B_b/(2s).

Expanding B_n^2 and R_n^2 gives

  Gf=V_N f+a_N.grad f + D_C:Hess f/(4s^2),
  D_C=sum_i lambda_i(v_i v_i^T-r_i r_i^T),
  V_N=[N^2 m^T C m+N(Tr C-m^T C m)]/(4s^2)+N b.m/(2s).

Explicitly, the drift is

  a_N=sum_i lambda_i/(4s^2)
       [2N u_i v_i+(v_i.grad)v_i-(r_i.grad)r_i]
       +[b-(b.m)m]/(2s).

The drift is exactly (S4). Also V_N=Tr(H k_N(m)); this follows from
E(J_aJ_b)=([N^2m_am_b+N(delta_ab-m_am_b)]/4) in the product state.

For P=I-mm^T and the cross-product matrix [m],
  D_C=P C P-[m] C [m]^T.
For every z, C>=I and C<=L_c I imply

  z^T D_C z >= [(1-|m|^2)^2-L_c|m|^2]|z|^2.

At r0^2=1/[16(L_c+1)], the bracket is
1-(L_c+2)/[16(L_c+1)]+1/[256(L_c+1)^2] >=29/32, hence >3/4.
Also D_C<=P C P<=L_c I<=Tr(C) I. Thus the square root and SDE coefficients
are smooth with a uniform positive margin on the stopped ball.

The drift estimate follows from |u_i v_i|<=r0,
|(v_i.grad)v_i|<=2r0, |(r_i.grad)r_i|<=r0 and
||b-(b.m)m||<=B there:

  sup |a_N| <= Tr(C)r0/(2sqrt(N))
                  +3Tr(C)r0/(4s^2)+B/(2s)=beta_N.

For fixed M,B this is <=r0/8 for all sufficiently large N. If tau is the first
hit of radius r0 and |m_0|<=r0/2, then on {tau<=1} the martingale displacement
has norm at least 3r0/8. One coordinate therefore has absolute maximum at
least r0/8. Each coordinate quadratic variation is at most Tr(C)/(2s^2).
The scalar exponential-martingale maximal inequality, over both signs and
three coordinates, gives

  P(tau<=1) <= 6 exp[-r0^2 s^2/(64 Tr(C))]
             <= 6 exp[-3r0^2 s^2/(256 Tr(C))]
             <= 6 exp[-r0^2 s^2/(256 L_c)].               (B)

This reconstructs (S9), with a slightly stronger first exponent. The constants
are uniform over every retained initial branch.

2. INITIAL SCALAR TAIL

The exact positive heat identity gives the seed as an identical-product
mixture: exp(t0 J^2)=int exp(x.J) nu_t0(dx), with
nu_t(dx)=(4 pi t)^(-3/2)exp(-t/4-r^2/(4t))sinhc(r/2)d^3x.
Writing m=tanh(r/2)u gives the seed radius density proportional to
r^2 sinhc(r/2) exp[-r^2/(4t0)+N logcosh(r/2)]. Since t0<=2/N, its exponential
part is <=exp[-N f(r/2)], f(u)=u^2/2-logcosh(u). For u<=1,
tanh(u)>=u/2 gives f(u)>=u^4/48; for u>=1,
logcosh(u)/u^2 decreases and f(u)>=delta u^2,
delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0. With a0=2atanh(r0/2), the bad event |m|>r0/2
means r>a0. On [a0,2], its unnormalized tail is at most
C exp[-N a0^4/768]; on [2,infty), for N delta>=2, it is at most
C_delta exp[-N delta/4].

The change R=N^(1/4)r and integration on 1<=R<=2 give the normalizer lower
bound N^(-3/4)(7/3)exp[-Lambda/2-1/12] when N>=Lambda^2. On this interval
the residual quadratic exponent is -Lambda R^2/(16d0),
d0=1-Lambda/(2sqrt(N))>=1/2, while N f(R/(2N^(1/4)))<=R^4/192<=1/12.
Dividing proves

  p_bad <= C_(Lambda,delta) N^(3/4) exp(-kappa_0 N),
  kappa_0=min(a0^4/768,delta/4).                           (C)

Replacing bad branches by tau_0 changes the seed by trace norm at most 2p_bad:
couple original and modified branch labels; changed branches are two states at
trace distance at most one.

3. SCALAR STOPPING LOSS TO RAW OPERATOR TRACE NORM

Let M_H(X)={H,X}/2. Its exponential is the exact congruence

  exp(u M_H)(X)=exp(uH/2) X exp(uH/2).

If h=||H||_op, the Schatten ideal inequality yields for every X
  ||exp(uM_H)(X)||_1 <= exp(u h)||X||_1.                    (D)
This applies to the signed Hermitian residuals below; positivity of M_H is not
needed. For each normalized product k_N(m),

  ||M_H k_N(m)||_1
    <= (||H k_N||_1+||k_N H||_1)/2 <= h.                  (E)

Uniformly over spin sectors,

  h_N=L_c (N/2)(N/2+1)/s^2+B N/(2s)
      =L_c sqrt(N)/4+L_c/(2sqrt(N))+(B/2)N^(1/4).

For the stopped SDE let w_t=exp(int_0^(t wedge tau)V_N(m_u)du) and
W_t=E[w_t k_N(m_(t wedge tau))]. Since |V_N|=|Tr(Hk_N)|<=h_N,
0<w_t<=exp(h_N). Ito's formula and (A) give

  W'_t=E[1_(t<tau) w_t M_H k_N(m_t)].

Linearity gives M_H W_t=E[w_t M_H k_N(m_(t wedge tau))]. Subtracting,

  W'_t-M_H W_t=-R_t,
  R_t=E[1_(tau<=t) w_tau M_H k_N(m_tau)].

This is the exact boundary residual. Solving this finite-dimensional
inhomogeneous operator ODE gives

  exp(M_H)rho0' - W_1 = int_0^1 exp((1-t)M_H)R_t dt.

Equations (D)-(E), the weight cap, and P(tau<=t)<=p_hit imply

  ||exp(M_H)rho0'-W_1||_1
    <= int_0^1 exp((1-t)h_N) h_N exp(h_N) p_hit dt
    <= h_N exp(2h_N) p_hit.                              (F)

The last scalar factor can be sharpened to (exp(2h_N)-exp(h_N))p_hit by
integrating the exponential in t; (F) is sufficient. The initial replacement
costs ||exp(M_H)(rho0-rho0')||_1<=2exp(h_N)p_bad. Therefore

  ||exp(M_H)rho0-W_1||_1
    <=2exp(h_N)p_bad+h_N exp(2h_N)p_hit.                  (G)

This is the scalar-to-operator passage that controls the complete density,
including multiplicity spaces; it does not merely compare collective moments.

4. NORMALIZATION AND EXPONENTIAL RATE

Since J^2 is central, exp(M_H)rho0=exp(K)/Z0, Z0=Tr exp(t0J^2). On spin j,
Tr H_j/d_j=(Tr C)j(j+1)/(3s^2)>=0; the field has zero trace. Scalar Jensen
therefore gives Tr exp(H_j)>=d_j, whence Tr exp(K)/Z0>=1. For positive G,W,

  T(G/TrG,W/TrW)<=||G-W||_1/TrG.

This follows by adding and subtracting W/TrG, using
|Tr(G-W)|<=||G-W||_1, and dividing by two. The stopped path law is positive,
so W_1/TrW_1 is an identical-product mixture. Applying this inequality with
G=exp(M_H)rho0 and (G) yields

  T(rho_N, sigma_N)
    <=2 C N^(3/4) exp[h_N-kappa_0 N]
      +6 h_N exp[2h_N-kappa_1 N^(3/2)],
  kappa_1=r0^2/(256L_c).

Because h_N=O_(M,B)(sqrt(N)), the first term is <=C' exp(-cN) for any fixed
c<kappa_0 after increasing N0; the second is smaller still. No inverse target
normalizer or filter-success probability appears. The cutoff N0, c and C may
be poor and are uniform only for fixed M,B.

5. CHECKS AND BOUNDARIES

The sign in (S2) matters: [F,k]=iRk implies R^2 k=-[F,[F,k]], so B^2-R^2,
not B^2+R^2, represents 2{F^2,k}. On the inner ball D_C is elliptic; outside
it can be indefinite, so the proof requires both clipping the initial outer
mass and stopping the diffusion. The stopped representation is valid because
the exact residual is supported on hit branches and bounded by their weight
times h_N. A global diffusion claim would be false for anisotropic C with
unequal transverse eigenvalues.
