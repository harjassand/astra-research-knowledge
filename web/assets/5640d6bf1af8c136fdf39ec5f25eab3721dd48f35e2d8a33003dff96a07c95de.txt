STRONGEST EXACT CLAIM

Bounded covariance, a bounded optimal Poincare constant, smooth Gaussian heat
regularization, small field mean, normalized field second moment, bounded field
Dirichlet energy, and EXACT scalar Sylvester/commutator identities do NOT imply
a universal cap-independent scale-integrated covariance-observation information
bound. This remains false with dimension one and the source's exact cap
f_L(q)=q/(1+q/L), covariance 1+f_L(T R(Y)^2), and dT/T^2 weighting.

Precisely, for every fixed heat time tau>=0 there are centered log-concave laws
eta_n and smooth positive scalar fields R_n such that
  Cov(eta_n)<=1+tau, P(eta_n)<=4+tau,
  E R_n^2=1, E |R_n'|^2<=1, E R_n->0,
  R_n'=H_n R_n, 0<=H_n<=1, [H_n,R_n]=0,
and for every fixed L>=256,
  liminf_(n->infinity) integral_0^infinity I(Y;Z_T) dT/T^2
       >= c_tau log(L/256),
where Z_T|Y ~ N(0,1+f_L(T R_n(Y)^2)) and c_tau>0 independent of n,L.
For tau=0 one may take c_0=Pr{|G|>=1}/128. For tau>0 one may take
  c_tau=(1-e^-1)e^(-tau/2) Pr{|G|>=1}/128.
Consequently no finite universal upper can follow from the displayed premises.

1. EXACT CONSTRUCTION

Let X_n have density e^-x/Z_n on [0,n+1], Z_n=1-e^(-(n+1)), n>=256.
Put V=sqrt(tau)G independently, W=X_n+V, Y=W-EW. Define
  h(u)=1/(1+e^-u), m_n=E h(W-n)^2,
  a_n=m_n^-1/2, R_n(y)=a_n h(y+EW-n).
The law of Y is centered and log-concave: the truncated exponential is
log-concave, as is its convolution with a Gaussian. For tau>0 its density is
smooth and strictly positive. R is smooth and positive even at tau=0.
The construction uses exact definitions, not an acquired density oracle or
an uncharged latent variable at the decoder. Z_T is a declared observation.

Since h'=h(1-h),
  E R^2=1, R'=(1-h)R, E(R')^2<=1.
This supplies scalar Sylvester R'=(H R+R H)/2, H=1-h, with bounded H and
zero commutators. Centering does not alter derivatives or information.

The base law has Var(X_n)=1-(n+1)^2 e^(-(n+1))/Z_n^2<=1 and
  P(X_n)=1/(1/4+pi^2/(n+1)^2)<=4.
For completeness, the selfadjoint generator is -f''+f' with Neumann boundary
conditions. Substitution f=e^(x/2)g gives -g''+(1/4)g=lambda g and Robin
conditions g'+g/2=0 at both ends. Positive eigenvalues are exactly
lambda_k=1/4+(k pi/(n+1))^2, k>=1; the remaining eigenvalue is zero.
The spectral expansion proves the formula. Conditional variance plus the base
and Gaussian Poincare inequalities gives P(W)<=P(X_n)+tau<=4+tau:
  Var f(X+V)=E_V Var_X f(X+V)+Var_V E_X f(X+V)
      <=(4+tau)E |f'(X+V)|^2.
Cov(W)<=1+tau follows by independence.

2. FIELD NORMALIZATION AND RARE MASS

For tau=0 an exact substitution u=x-n yields
  m_n=e^-n [h(1)-h(-n)]/Z_n,
  p_n=Pr(X_n>=n)=e^-n(1-e^-1)/Z_n,
so p_n a_n^2=(1-e^-1)/[h(1)-h(-n)]>=1/2.
Also E(R')^2 equals
  [(1-h(-n))^3-(1-h(1))^3]/[3(h(1)-h(-n))],
which is bounded by one. The small mean follows from
  E h(X_n-n)<=e^-n(n+1)/Z_n,
and m_n>=p_n/4, hence E R_n=O((n+1)e^-n/2)->0.

For fixed tau>0, extend the normalization integral from its actual interval
to the real line and substitute after conditioning on V:
  m_n<=e^-n E e^V/Z_n * integral_R e^-u h(u)^2 du
       =e^-n e^(tau/2)/Z_n.
The last integral is one because e^-u h(u)^2=h'(u).
The event X_n>=n,V>=0 is included in W>=n, giving
  p_n=Pr(W>=n)>=e^-n(1-e^-1)/(2Z_n),
  p_n a_n^2>= (1-e^-1)e^(-tau/2)/2.
Moreover m_n>=p_n/4, while
  E h(W-n)<=e^-n e^(tau/2)(n+1)/Z_n.
Thus E R_n->0 also after fixed Gaussian heating.

3. INFORMATION LOWER: A SINGLE EXPLICIT OUTPUT TEST

Write alpha=Pr(|G|>=1)>0. Fix the source cap L and use scales
  s=T a_n^2 in [256,min(n,L)].
Set A_s={|Z_T|>=sqrt(s)/(2sqrt(2))}, and let q_s=Pr(A_s).
For every y with W>=n, h(W-n)>=1/2. Since s<=L and h<=1,
  f_L(s h^2)>=s h^2/2>=s/8.
The conditional Gaussian variance is at least s/8. Therefore
  Pr(A_s|Y=y)>=alpha on W>=n.

On W<=n/2, h(W-n)<=e^-n/2, so the conditional variance is at most
1+s e^-n<=2. The elementary Gaussian Chernoff bound gives
  Pr(A_s|W<=n/2)<=2e^-s/32.
For tau=0,
  Pr(W>n/2)<=e^-n/2/Z_n<=2e^-n/2<=2e^-s/32.
For tau>0, conditioning on V gives the same bound with e^(tau/2):
  Pr(W>n/2)<=e^(tau/2)e^-n/2/Z_n.
For fixed tau and all sufficiently large n (e.g. n>=16tau/15), this is
at most 2e^-s/32 because s<=n. Consequently in both cases
  q_s<=4e^-s/32.

Mutual information is E_Y KL(P_(Z|Y)||P_Z). Applying relative-entropy data
processing to the binary output event A_s yields, for a_y=Pr(A_s|Y=y),
  KL(P_(Z|Y=y)||P_Z)>=d_bin(a_y||q_s)
       >=a_y log(1/q_s)-log 2.
On W>=n this is at least alpha(s/32-log 4)-log 2.
The expression is >=alpha s/64 for s>=256 (checked directly; alpha=erfc(1/sqrt2)).
The other conditional KL terms are nonnegative. Hence
  I(Y;Z_T)>=p_n alpha s/64.
No binary entropy of the rare input event is silently substituted for the
continuous input information, and no likelihood-ratio approximation is used.

Change variables s=T a_n^2; dT/T^2=a_n^2 ds/s^2. The selected scale interval
therefore gives
  integral I(Y;Z_T)dT/T^2
      >=p_n a_n^2 alpha/64 * log(min(n,L)/256).
The normalization bounds in section2 prove the exact claimed constants.
Take n->infinity with L fixed FIRST. The resulting lower grows as log L.
This matches the source's order of limits and rules out a cap-independent
universal constant under these premises.
