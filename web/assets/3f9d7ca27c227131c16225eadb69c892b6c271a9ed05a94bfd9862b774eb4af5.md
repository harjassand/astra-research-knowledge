# Source ledger and reconstruction boundary

## Primary model paper

Qing Xu, Hongda Jiang, and Shenshen Wang, “Adaptive Mechanical Proofreading Toward Robust yet Tunable Humoral Immunity,” *PRX Life* 4, 023007 (published 17 April 2026), [DOI 10.1103/vfqt-t3b9](https://doi.org/10.1103/vfqt-t3b9), [APS article](https://journals.aps.org/prxlife/abstract/10.1103/vfqt-t3b9), [APS supplemental material](https://journals.aps.org/prxlife/supplemental/10.1103/vfqt-t3b9/SM.pdf).

Local preservation: `aps_article.html`, `aps_supplement.html`, `vfqt-t3b9_SM.pdf`. The independent model reconstruction used the supplement's single-cluster reactions S10–S15 and the public implementation's stopping rules. The article's Fig. 5c design is independently drawn antigen counts for the two affinity cases, `L ~ max(5, round Normal(100, σ_L))`, `σ_L=0...30`; it compares `F(t)` and `F(m_max)` at `F0=800 pN`, `β=5`, `tc=mc=60`, `Eb=13.3 kBT`, `ΔEb=0.5 kBT`, `kon=0.05 s^-1`, and cutoff `tmax=1800 s`. The figure-code implementation uses five batches × 6000 pairs and random half-credit for exact score ties.

The authors' public model repository was cloned to `Adaptive-MPR` at commit `eb537e8393ecc6db158c073a2aa7e26b78fef07a`; relevant files are `MPR_cleaned_code/OneAg_Gillespie.py` and `MPR_cleaned_code/Fig.5.py`. The README describes the scripts as cleaned/annotated, with annotation assistance; the unannotated notebooks are also present. We used the published implementation as a reference, not as proof or biological validation.

## Primary experimental constraint

Hannah C. W. McArthur, Anna T. Bajur, Maro Iliopoulou, and Katelyn M. Spillane, “Antigen mobility regulates the dynamics and precision of antigen capture in the B cell immune synapse,” *PNAS* 122(20), e2422528122 (2025), [DOI 10.1073/pnas.2422528122](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422528122), [full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12107191/), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40354540/).

This primary experiment uses DNA-nanostructure antigen on DOPC (more mobile) and DPPC (less mobile) supported bilayers, DNA tension sensors, single-particle tracking, and live imaging. It reports mobility-dependent force magnitude, location, and timing; low mobility increased speed and probability of extraction and improved affinity discrimination. The paper reports the first extraction at about 8 s on DPPC versus about 200 s on DOPC; mean time to first internalized cluster was 74 s versus 421 s; final captured amounts can become similar by 45 min because DOPC permits recruitment from a larger pool. These observations establish mobility as a controlled physical variable that the one-cluster uniform-dose model does not represent.

## Evidence boundary

- `established external`: only the publication metadata and reported experimental/model descriptions above.
- `internally reconstructed`: reaction interface and exact birth–death derivation in `../proof/finite_dose_threshold.md`.
- `finite computation`: all new sensitivity, ranking, and control results in `../code/`; they use the source model, not primary-cell data.
- `not established`: B cells implement `F(m_max)`, clinical or vaccine impact, true mechanical work or ATP expenditure, robustness for every finite antigen dose, or novelty of a new sensing principle.

The current work did not reanalyze the PNAS raw images or the APS source data. The listed numerical experiment results are simulator outputs. The work proxy `Σ(F/m) x` is a Bell-barrier contribution and is not calibrated force-displacement work.
