ASTRA ULTRA — Cycle 10 Luna/max
Square hard-Lipschitz fields: sparse-block and animal estimate

Status

I derive the square zero-boundary sparse-block bound from Cohen–Alloro–Peled (CAP), Theorem 1.1, with an explicit degree-four constant, then convert it to a rooted connected-animal tail. The derivation is uniform over finite volumes under the hypotheses below. For a tilted law, the same conclusion follows from a uniform L2 bound on its density relative to the zero-boundary law; the source-style small macroscopic tilt is sufficient only once the square analogue of the cited average-moment proposition is established. I do not claim that CAP applies directly to a tilted law. This is an internal proof reconstruction from a published primary theorem, not a formal replay or independent external audit.

The square scaling/GFF conclusion remains open in this work. The estimate supplies the square f:sparse gate after the frozen square block lemma; it does not prove the later comparison, tangent-response, covariance, or distributional tightness theorems.

Exact finite-volume statement

Let G=(V,E) be a finite connected subgraph of the square lattice with maximum degree at most four, let V0 be a nonempty set of pinned vertices with height zero, and let mu0 be uniform Lebesgue measure on the remaining heights satisfying |h(x)-h(y)|<=1 on every edge. Assume the partition function is finite and positive. For an application to a square-grid polygon, take its boundary vertices as pins. More generally, a finite union of graph components is allowed when each component contains a pin; the components are independent and the CAP bound below multiplies across them.

Fix an enlargement radius q>=0, rounded up to an integer, and set

  B_z = Lz + {0,...,L}^2,
  B*_z = Lz + ([-qL,L+qL]^2 intersect Z^2).

An index z is eligible only if B*_z is a full square patch of the graph and contains no pinned vertex. Blocks in a fixed collar of the pin set or finite-volume boundary are deterministic pin/collar blocks and are excluded from the random bad set. On eligible z define

  N_z = {some edge in B*_z has |grad h| > 1-delta},
  F_z = {some x,y in B*_z with d1(x,y)>=k0 and
                       |h(x)-h(y)| > (1-delta0)d1(x,y)},
  Bad_z = N_z union F_z.

For every target 0<rho<1, q, and finite volume there are fixed parameters 0<delta<=delta0<=1/16, integer k0>=4/delta0, and even L>=8(k0+2)/delta0, depending only on rho and q, such that for every finite set A of eligible indices

  mu0(A subset Bad) <= rho^|A|.                         (1)

The constants do not depend on the volume, shape, number of components, or location of A. Any block with Bad_z false has both the local-edge slack and the long-pair d1 slack required by the frozen square block-deficit lemma on B_z. No triangular non-tie tests are part of Bad_z: those tests address a triangular facet tie in the pinned source's block geometry and are not hypotheses of the square weighted-Manhattan block lemma.

Primary extremal-edge input

CAP, Theorem 1.1, applies to a finite connected graph with nonempty zero pin set, maximum degree Delta, an even monotone potential with finite support, and its normalized random-surface Gibbs law. The hard “hammock” potential U(t)=0 for |t|<=1 and +infinity otherwise is allowed; CAP explicitly gives its small parameter as eps. Its proof, Section 5, gives C(Delta)=2^(10Delta+2). Thus on the square graph C4=2^42 and for every set S of j distinct edges and 0<u<=1/8,

  mu0(|grad h(e)| >= 1-u for every e in S)
       <= (C4 u)^(j/C4).                                (2)

We use (2) only under the zero-pinned law, with C4u<1. It is not a statement about arbitrary boundary data or exponential tilts. For a disconnected graph whose every component is pinned, apply (2) componentwise and use the product law; the exponents add to j/C4.

Proof of (1)

1. Fix constants and choose parameters in the order required by the square block lemma.

Put C=C4 and choose the exact positive number

  eps = 2^(-4C)/C,   delta0 = eps/2.

Then eps<=1/8, delta0<=1/16, C eps=2^(-4C)<1, and

  lambda := 2(C eps)^(1/(2C)) = 1/2.                     (3)

Choose k0>=4/delta0 and then take L to be the least even integer at least 8(k0+2)/delta0. Write sL=(1+2q)L+1 for the side length of B*_z. For L>=1 its number of internal edges and ordered vertex pairs are bounded respectively by

  |E(B*_z)| <= A_q L^2,   A_q=2(2q+2)^2,
  |B*_z|^2 <= P_q L^4,   P_q=(2q+2)^4.                  (4)

Given a desired separated-patch bound eta^n, first choose k0 sufficiently large that

  b := P_q L^4 lambda^k0 <= (eta/2)^2.                  (5)

This is possible: for fixed eps, delta0, q, the chosen L is O(k0), while lambda=1/2, so the exponential factor dominates L^4. Finally choose delta in (0,delta0] so small that Cdelta<1 and

  a := A_q L^2 (Cdelta)^(1/C) <= (eta/2)^2.             (6)

All these parameters are finite and are fixed before the finite volume is chosen. The small value of eps makes the constants enormous; no optimization is claimed.

2. Bound simultaneous long-pair failures in separated patches.

For every pair x,y in a coordinate square B*_z, fix a deterministic Manhattan shortest path gamma_xy. Such a path stays inside B*_z because coordinate squares are geodesically convex for d1. Let m=d1(x,y)>=k0. If F_z is witnessed by (x,y), the sum of absolute edge increments on gamma_xy is at least |h(x)-h(y)|>(1-delta0)m. If fewer than m/2 edges had |grad h|>=1-eps, the sum would be at most m-eps(m/2)=(1-delta0)m, since eps=2delta0. Hence at least m/2 distinct edges on that path have |grad h|>=1-eps.

For a fixed path of length m, take a union bound over its at most 2^m edge subsets. Every subset that can witness the preceding conclusion has at least m/2 distinct edges, so (2) and Ceps<1 give

  mu0(gamma_xy has the required high-edge witness)
       <= 2^m (Ceps)^(m/(2C)) = lambda^m.               (7)

Now take n eligible blocks whose enlarged patches are vertex-disjoint, and fix any subset K of l of these blocks. If all F_z occur for z in K, choose one witness pair and its fixed path in every patch. The paths use distinct edges across different patches. Union over at most (P_q L^4)^l witness-pair choices and over the high-edge subsets on those paths. Applying (2) to the union of all selected edges gives, without any independence assumption,

  mu0(intersection_{z in K} F_z)
       <= (P_q L^4 lambda^k0)^l <= ((eta/2)^2)^l.       (8)

The distinct-edge condition in CAP is satisfied exactly because the enlarged patches are disjoint. A bound for one arbitrary edge set alone would not imply (8); the path witness, its subset count, and the disjointness argument are needed.

3. Bound simultaneous near-extremal-edge failures.

For n separated eligible patches, if N_z occurs in every z of a subset J of size j, choose one witnessing edge in each patch. These are j distinct edges. There are at most (A_q L^2)^j choices, so another union bound using (2) yields

  mu0(intersection_{z in J} N_z)
       <= (A_q L^2 (Cdelta)^(1/C))^j
       <= ((eta/2)^2)^j.                                (9)

4. Combine the two failure types without assuming their independence.

Let S be a separated collection of n eligible blocks. Assign to each z in S one type, N or F. For an assignment with J of type N and K of type F, (8), (9), and P(E cap F)<=min(P(E),P(F))<=sqrt(P(E)P(F)) give

  mu0(intersection_{z in J}N_z intersection intersection_{z in K}F_z)
       <= (eta/2)^n.

This also holds when J or K is empty, using probability at most one for the empty intersection. There are 2^n type assignments, and their union covers the event that every z in S is bad. Therefore

  mu0(S subset Bad) <= eta^n.                            (10)

5. Remove the separation condition by finite coloring.

Take an integer M>1+2q. Color the coarse block grid by residues modulo M in each coordinate; this gives J=M^2 colors, and patches in one color are vertex-disjoint. For any finite A, one color class S inside A has |S|>=|A|/J. Apply (10) with eta=rho^J. Since A subset Bad implies S subset Bad,

  mu0(A subset Bad) <= eta^|S| <= rho^|A|,

which proves (1). No probabilistic independence between colors or blocks is used.

6. Rooted connected-animal tail.

Let the bad-block cluster C(z) use star adjacency (eight neighbors), as in the source's hole-filling step. The number of star-connected sets of size n containing a fixed root z is at most 8^(2(n-1)): choose a deterministic depth-first traversal of a spanning tree, of length 2(n-1); its visited vertices recover the set. Each particular set S has probability at most rho^n by (1). Thus

  mu0(|C(z)|>=n) <= 8^(2(n-1))rho^n <= (64rho)^n.      (11)

The first inequality also holds for a nearest-neighbor cluster with 16 replacing 64. Consequently any requested theta in (0,1) is obtained by first choosing rho<=theta/64. For the maximum over all roots in a finite volume with N eligible blocks, the valid bound is N theta^n; the theorem does not assert a volume-free bound for the existence of an animal somewhere.

For a fixed block-radius thickening, let M0 be the maximum number of sites in one radius-r0 neighborhood. If every site of A lies within that radius of a random bad site, select one witness per site. Each distinct witness can serve at most M0 sites, so the witness set has size at least |A|/M0. Counting at most M0^|A| assignments and applying (1) with base parameter p gives

  mu0(A subset Bad^(r0)) <= (M0 p^(1/M0))^|A|.

Choosing p<= (rho/M0)^M0 gives any desired thickened per-set parameter rho, hence another rooted animal bound. Deterministic pin collars remain excluded as anchors, not random bad sites.

Tilt transfer and its exact scope

Let mu_Y be any finite-volume exponential tilt of mu0 with Radon–Nikodym density R=dmu_Y/dmu0 and suppose ||R||_(L2(mu0))<=M2 uniformly over the volume family. Necessarily M2>=1. For the same bad-block event E_A={A subset Bad}, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

  mu_Y(E_A) <= M2 mu0(E_A)^(1/2)
             <= M2 rho0^(|A|/2).                        (12)

To obtain the no-prefactor target mu_Y(E_A)<=rho^|A| for every nonempty A, run the zero-boundary construction with rho0=(rho/M2)^2. Then the right side of (12) is rho^|A| M2^(1-|A|)<=rho^|A|. The thresholds and L are chosen for this smaller rho0. Equation (11) follows under mu_Y with the same target rho and animal counting. Thus the tilt statement is exact for any uniformly L2-bounded density, but CAP itself is only used under mu0.

Here is the source-style sufficient small-tilt condition, with its remaining square input stated rather than hidden. Assume a square version of the source's macroscopic exponential-moment proposition: uniformly in finite volumes, for weights q with sum |q_x|<=C*, max |q_x|<=C*R^-2, support diameter O(R), and either sum q_x=0 or a connected zero-pin anchor of diameter at least c*R within an O(R) box of the support, one has E0 exp(a* |sum q_x h(x)|)<=M*. If Y=sum b_x h(x), its support lies in an O(R) box, |b_x|<=c R^-2, and it obeys the same zero-mass or anchor alternative, then q_x=b_x/c has the required bounds (the O(R) square contains O(R^2) sites). When 2c<=a*,

  E0 exp(2Y) <= M*,
  E0 exp(Y) >= exp(E0Y) >= M*^(-c/a*),
  ||dmu_Y/dmu0||_2 <= M*^(1/2+c/a*).                 (13)

This is the small macroscopic tilt regime: coefficients are O(R^-2); for a nonzero-mass statistic the pin anchor must be macroscopic and co-located in an O(R) box, while a zero-mass statistic needs no such anchor. Multiple fixed tests can be included only if their combined weights preserve these conditions and the amplitude is reduced accordingly. The square f:averages proposition has not been established in this task, so (13) is a conditional sufficient criterion, not an additional unconditional square theorem. For arbitrary linear tilts, bounded boundary data, or a density whose L2 norm grows with volume, no uniform tilted animal estimate is claimed.

Source audit and square-specific points

The actual pinned triangular source is `work/sources/openai-math/preprints/Uniform-real-Lipschitz-surfaces-on-the-triangular-lattice-October-6-2026/build/sections/foundations.tex`, labels f:sparse, f:near, f:far, and f:tilts. Its f:sparse proof first applies CAP to edge witnesses, turns far failures into long-path edge witnesses, separates enlarged boxes, combines N/F failure bounds by a geometric mean, and then adds four conditional-shell non-tie tests specific to its triangular block geometry. Its long metric is the triangular graph metric. This square argument replaces that metric with Manhattan d1 geodesics inside coordinate-square enlargements and omits the non-tie tests because the accepted square block lemma has no triangular tied-facet case. The source's tilt lemma explicitly assumes the preceding macroscopic average-moment proposition and gives only an L2-density transfer; it does not apply CAP directly to a tilted measure.

The primary CAP article is Cohen–Alloro–Peled, “Rarity of extremal edges in random surfaces and other theoretical applications of cluster algorithms,” arXiv:1711.00259v2, Theorem 1.1 and Section 5 (especially the final constant calculation). The web source was opened directly and checked at the theorem hypotheses, formula for delta(U,eps), the hammock specialization, and the line stating C(Delta)=2^(10Delta+2). URL: https://arxiv.org/html/1711.00259v2 . The theorem is for a finite connected graph, nonempty zero pins, even monotone potential, and finite support; its displayed complete-bipartite example explains why the degree dependence cannot be dropped. Square degree four is within the theorem. This source gives only the edge rarity input; (1), (11), and the tilt transfer are derived here.

Scope, missing inputs, falsifier, and costs

This closes the zero-pinned square f:sparse set estimate and rooted bad-cluster tail as an internal derivation from CAP and the stated square geometry. It is the immediate post-block finite-volume input identified in the dependency map. It does not close the square macroscopic moment proposition, the local reflected comparison, stationary tangent response, positivity or identification of a stiffness, test-function CLT, boundary approximation, tightness in a negative Sobolev space, or any GFF/SLE scaling claim. For small macroscopic tilts the exact remaining gate here is a square f:averages estimate giving the uniform L2 norm in (13); alternatively, any independently verified uniform L2 Radon–Nikodym bound suffices.

The argument would fail if eligible patches were allowed to overlap (the CAP edge witnesses would not be distinct), if a selected Manhattan path left its patch, if the finite graph failed CAP's connected/nonempty-pin/normalizability hypotheses, or if the long event were measured in a metric for which the chosen paths are not geodesics. A concrete finite-volume counterexample to (1) under these stated hypotheses would contradict the CAP-derived union bounds; none was found. A tilt with unbounded L2 density is outside the transfer claim.

No simulation, field sampling, finite-volume enumeration, Lean formalization, kernel replay, external expert review, or novelty search was performed. The constants are deliberately explicit but extremely small/large. Source correctness and historical priority for the broader square scaling target remain unverified/UNKNOWN.
