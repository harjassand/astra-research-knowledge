CYCLE 3 — DIRECT PASSIVE HMM MINIMAX: RARE-REGIME OBSTRUCTION

RESULT

Without a supplied mixing/rare-transition lower bound, there is no uniformly vanishing finite-sample rate for stationary-context expected next-symbol KL, even for a three-state HMM family with positive well-conditioned emissions, stationary masses bounded below, full-rank transition matrices, and a constant rank-complete one-symbol-pair witness. For every raw-path length T, a two-model subfamily has minimax H=1 risk greater than 0.01328. The lower bound uses the complete raw observation trajectory; it does not rely on episode truncation, an exponentially large model class, hidden labels, or computational limits.

INTERFACE BOUNDARY: FRESH STATIONARY CONTEXT, NOT TERMINAL CONTINUATION

The loss below draws a new context X~nu independently after the training path S=Y_1:T and scores q_S(.|X) against P_i(Y_1|Y_0=X). The constant lower bound uses the fact that this fresh stationary context can be associated with the unobserved {1,2} block, with nu(a)=101/400, even when the training path stayed in state 0. It is not a lower bound for forecasting P_i(Y_{T+1}|Y_1:T) at the endpoint of the training path.

On the common latent path Z_1:T=0^T, the two models have the same transition row out of state 0. Conditional on the current hidden state being 0, their next-symbol continuation laws are therefore identical; the total probability of exiting to the differing {1,2} block on the next step is rho, so any one-step effect relative to emission O_0 is at most O(rho). Thus the common-submeasure proof does not transfer its constant lower bound to terminal continuation prediction. This report does not establish an O(rho) bound for the predictor conditioned only on the observed history Y_1:T; its filtering posterior needs a separate analysis. Nor does it address random-time prediction risk, time-averaged loss, or cumulative future log loss.

MODEL CLASS

Hidden states are 0,1,2; alphabet is {c,a,b}; stationary hidden law is pi=(1/2,1/4,1/4). For 0<rho<=1/100, define Q0=[[3/4,1/4],[1/4,3/4]] and Q1=[[1/4,3/4],[3/4,1/4]], and

  P_i(rho) = [[1-rho, rho/2, rho/2],
               [rho, (1-rho)Q_i(1,1), (1-rho)Q_i(1,2)],
               [rho, (1-rho)Q_i(2,1), (1-rho)Q_i(2,2)]].

Both have stationary law pi. Emissions are fixed, known, and identical under the two candidates:

  O(rows c,a,b; columns 0,1,2) = [[.98,.01,.01], [.01,.98,.01], [.01,.01,.98]].

All emission entries are >=.01, and singular values are 1,.97,.97. Stationary masses are >=1/4. For rho<=.01, each P_i has sigma_min>.47 (perturbation from rho=0); the pair observation matrix J_i=O diag(pi) P_i O^T has sigma_min>.11. Since the full HMM Hankel rank is <=3 and this 3x3 pair witness is full rank, predictive rank is exactly three and witness horizon (1,1) is complete.

The one deteriorating margin is mixing: the hidden cluster indicator 1{Z in {1,2}} has eigenvalue 1-2rho; min transition entry is rho/2. The class permits rho->0. For each T choose rho_T=1/(100T), with only one unknown bit i in the hard pair.

TARGET AND LOWER BOUND

Training is one stationary raw path S=Y1:T, with no labels, resets, queries, dictionaries, or gap. Define stationary one-symbol context X=Y0, kernel K_i(.|x)=P_i(Y1 in .|Y0=x), and

  R_i(q)=E_(S~P_i^T) E_(X~nu_i) KL(K_i(.|X)||q_S(.|X)).

At X=a, P(Z0=0,1,2|X=a)=(2,98,1)/101 and nu(a)=101/400. The two one-step kernels satisfy

  TV(K0(.|a),K1(.|a))=9409(1-rho)/20200 > .4611.

For the full training path, latent path Z1:T=0^T contributes the same subprobability measure O_0^(tensor T) under both models, of mass p_T=(1/2)(1-rho)^(T-1). Thus the raw path laws have overlap at least p_T. At rho_T=1/(100T), p_T>=.495.

For any proposed q at context a, Pinsker plus triangle gives KL(K0||q)+KL(K1||q)>=TV(K0,K1)^2. Integrating over the common training submeasure and context a yields

  max_i R_i(q) >= (p_T/2)(101/400) TV(K0(.|a),K1(.|a))^2 > .01328.

This holds for every T, so minimax risk over the class does not converge to zero. The same common mass implies any full-path model selector has equal-prior error at least p_T/2=(1/4)(1-rho)^(T-1). For a fixed rho, error target delta<1/4 requires T >= 1+log(1/(4delta))/[-log(1-rho)] = Omega(rho^(-1) log(1/delta)). Full-path likelihood cannot escape this lower bound on the same observations.

FINITE-H SCOPE

The positive-emission numerical lower above is for H=1. For every fixed H>=1 there is also an explicit deterministic-emission extension (state 0/1/2 emits c/a/b): at context a^H the prediction kernels differ by (1-rho)/2, and the smaller context-word mass is (1/4)[(1-rho)/4]^(H-1). It gives

  max_i R_i^(H) >= (1/64)(1-rho)^(T+H) 4^(-(H-1)).

With rho=1/(100T) and T>=H, this is >=(0.98/64)4^(-(H-1)), constant in T for each fixed H. This extension permits zero emissions; the positive-emission result is claimed only for H=1. The bound decays with H and does not establish an infinite-past-context obstruction.

METRIC ENTROPY AND COSTS

The restricted model class has only a one-dimensional rho parameter and two separated branches: N(eta) <=2(1+ceil(1/(100eta))) in transition-entry sup norm, so log covering entropy is O(log(1/eta)). The hard pair itself has O(log T)-bit rational descriptions and one unknown index bit. No large dictionary is responsible.

Training costs T three-symbol observations (at most 2T bits in a fixed-length encoding) and one stationary path start. The H=1 context is an evaluation variable; if supplied physically, add one symbol. A three-outcome forecast takes O(b) output bits at b-bit precision. Known-pair full-path likelihood uses O(TD^2) arithmetic and O(D) memory, but any likelihood/model-selection rule has the same common-submeasure error floor. Splitting N symbols into R stationary segments gives common all-state-0 mass (1/2)(1-rho)^(N-R); resets do not remove the obstruction.

The full derivation and constants are in `work/programmes/acquisition_learning/cycle3/passive_minimax/proof.md`. This is an exact no-uniform-rate obstruction for the fresh-context loss above, not a claim about terminal continuation prediction, random-time/cumulative loss, or the optimal rate when rho or a mixing modulus is fixed and supplied. Population identifiability results such as Gassiat–Cleynen–Robin (https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657) are compatible: every positive-rho member is full-rank/linearly independent, while the finite-path bound is nonuniform as rho tends to zero.
