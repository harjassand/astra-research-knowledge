For kappa>2lambda define alpha=1/2-lambda/kappa in(0,1/2).
For each queried moment s, choose a sufficiently large integer K=K(s) and start at(n,0),n>K. Let T_K be the first physical time hitting the finite axis set{(a,0):a<=K}. Then

At kappa=2lambda, for every s>0 and all sufficiently large K(s), E T_K^s is infinite for every sufficiently large n, although T_K<infinity almost surely.

PHYSICAL CLOCK: A COMPLETE MOMENT TRANSFER
Continue the cycle process forever even after its first hit on the axis set. Let R_j be cycle durations and H_j their initial B=0 holds.
Section2 gives fixed theta>0,M<infinity such that E[exp(theta R_j)|past]<=M, uniformly in the current A. For deterministic m,
  E exp(theta sum_{j<m}R_j)<=M^m.
Thus, with m=floor(delta*t),delta>0 sufficiently small,
  P(T_K>t)<=P(tau_K>m)+exp(-c*t)
for all large t, because on tau_K<=m the passage time is at most the first m continued cycle durations.
Conversely,H_j are iid Exp(lambda) and independent of the entire event skeleton determining tau_K. T_K>=sum_{j<tau_K}H_j. For m=ceil(2lambda*t), Chernoff's inequality for the Gamma(m,lambda) sum gives
  P(T_K>t)>=P(tau_K>m)*(1-exp(-c'*t)).
The two tail comparisons and the identity E Y^s=s integral_0^infinity t^(s-1)P(Y>t)dt show that T_K and tau_K have exactly the same finite/infinite positive moment classification. This is stronger than merely comparing their means and excludes clock-induced changes in the reported passage exponents.

This theorem uses a specified finite axis set. It is not a claim about every arbitrary finite target or exact regular variation of the tail. A separate invariant-measure asymptotic is not needed.
