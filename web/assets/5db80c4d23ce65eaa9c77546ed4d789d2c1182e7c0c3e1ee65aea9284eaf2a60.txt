1. SU(2) counterexample: VERIFIED.

Let |a_u|=|b_v|=1, with each of the two families pairwise distinct. Define
  X_uv = 1/sqrt(2) [[a_u,b_v],[-conj(b_v),conj(a_u)]].
Its columns are orthonormal and det X_uv=(|a_u|^2+|b_v|^2)/2=1.
Write X_uv=A_u+B_v, with
  A_u=diag(a_u,conj(a_u))/sqrt(2),
  B_v=[[0,b_v],[-conj(b_v),0]]/sqrt(2).
Therefore Y=(X_uv) factors as the sum of two matrices of rank at most 2:
  Y = col_u(A_u) row_v(I_2) + col_u(I_2) row_v(B_v).
Hence rank_C Y<=4, for every r. For r>=2 it is in fact 4: differences
of two block columns give the full two-dimensional constant-block subspace,
since B_v-B_v' is invertible; col_u(A_u) has a two-dimensional image
disjoint from that subspace, since A_u-A_u' is invertible.

Put P_v=X_uv X_u'v*. Its entries are
  P_v = [[p,q_v],[-conj(q_v),conj(p)]],
  p=(1+a_u conj(a_u'))/2, q_v=b_v(a_u'-a_u)/2.
Thus the specified upper-right formula has the correct sign and factor.
The rectangle W=P_v P_v'* has normalized trace (Tr_2/2)
  tau(W)=|p|^2+Re(q_v conj(q_v'))
        =1-|a_u-a_u'|^2 |b_v-b_v'|^2/8.
For nondegenerate rectangles this is <1, so W!=I. A nonidentity SU(2)
unitary cannot have eigenvalue 1: its two eigenvalues are conjugate and
their product is 1. Consequently rank_C(I-W)=2, exactly.

This refutes any dimension-uniform lower bound on rank(Y)/D growing with r
that assumes only full normalized rank separation of these rectangles.
For example rank(Y)>=r^2 D/(2r-1) already fails at D=2,r>=4.
It does not refute a lower bound assuming canonical/approximately zero
rectangle trace; rank separation and trace separation are different here.

2. Conditional complex-certificate obstruction: VERIFIED.

Assume a discrete group G with exact N06 triangle equations and all the
specified nondegenerate rectangles nonidentity. Let M=L(G), with canonical
trace tau_G(lambda(g))=1 if g=1 and 0 otherwise. On M_k(M) use
  T_k=Tr_k tensor tau_G,
where Tr_k is UNNORMALIZED. Define rank_M(A)=T_k(s(AA*)) for square A,
and the analogous left support trace for rectangular matrices. Left and
right support traces agree. Multiplication cannot increase support rank;
rank is subadditive, and for scalar H,
  rank_M(H tensor Z)=rank_C(H) rank_M(Z).
The last equality follows from a scalar singular-value decomposition.

Y_i=(lambda(x_iuv)) is r by r. Direct expansion gives
  T_r(Y_i Y_i*)=r^2,
  T_r((Y_i Y_i*)^2)
    =sum_{u,u',v,v'} tau_G(x_iuv x_iu'v^-1 x_iu'v' x_iuv'^-1)
    =2r^3-r^2=r^2(2r-1).
The degenerate union u=u' or v=v' has exactly 2r^3-r^2 terms; all
remaining traces vanish by the nonidentity hypothesis. If Q=s(Y_iY_i*),
Cauchy–Schwarz gives
  r^4 <= T_r(Q) T_r((Y_iY_i*)^2),
and therefore rank_M(Y_i)>=r^2/(2r-1).

Let Z(z)=sum_{i in I(z)}Y_i. For a regular Cartesian block b, exact
triangles yield x_iuv=L_b,u,alpha(i)+v^-1 R_b,v,beta(i)+u. The bijection
i<->(alpha(i),beta(i)) and independent cyclic coordinate shifts imply
  sum_{i in I_b}Y_i=C_b A_b,
  (C_b)_u=sum_j lambda(L_buj)^*, (A_b)_v=sum_k lambda(R_bvk),
with inner module dimension 1. Thus each Cartesian block has support rank
at most 1, and Z(z) has rank at most 2 at regular z, at most r at exceptions.

For arbitrary complex N by N matrices H(z) with rank_C H(z)<=1 put
  B=sum_z H(z) tensor Z(z), D=diag_i(Y_i), R=2m+r|E|.
Then
  rank_M(B)<=2(m-|E|)+r|E|<=R,
  rank_M(D)=sum_i rank_M(Y_i)>=N r^2/(2r-1).
If sum_{z:i in I(z)}H(z)=-E_ii for all i, distributivity gives B=-D.
This is impossible whenever R<N r^2/(2r-1). The slightly sharper bound
2(m-|E|)+r|E| may replace R throughout if desired.

3. Approximate residual and all constants: VERIFIED.

Put K_i=E_ii+sum_{z:i in I(z)}H(z), so
  F=sum_i K_i tensor Y_i=D+B.
Let P=s(BB*) in M_Nr(M). Then (1-P)B=0 and T_Nr(P)<=R. Hence
  ||F||_2^2 >= ||(1-P)D||_2^2
             =T_Nr(DD*)-T_Nr(PDD*)
             >=N r^2-sqrt[R N r^2(2r-1)].                 (A)
Here ||A||_2^2=T_Nr(AA*) and the last step is the trace Cauchy–Schwarz
inequality. The noncommuting projection P is allowed; no diagonal
support or scalar rank argument is silently assumed.

Under the same unnormalized trace,
  ||K_i tensor Y_i||_2=||K_i||_F ||Y_i||_2=r||K_i||_F.
Minkowski therefore gives
  ||F||_2^2<=r^2 (sum_i ||K_i||_F)^2.                     (B)
Combining (A)-(B), whenever the radicand below is positive,
  sum_i ||K_i||_F >=
    sqrt[N-sqrt(R N (2r-1)/r^2)].                         (C)

If the N06 numerical gap Delta=N r/4-R is positive, R<N r/4 and (C)
implies the strict uniform bound
  sum_i ||K_i||_F > sqrt[N(1-1/sqrt(2))].
Indeed sqrt(R N (2r-1)/r^2)<N sqrt((2r-1)/(4r))<N/sqrt(2).
This rules out every vanishing-residual sequence of complex rank<=1 lifts
for the fixed finite incidence data. Frobenius norm is unnormalized.

4. Basepoints / groupoid gauges: no hidden obstruction.

For vertices v choose a path p_v from a basepoint to v. Associate to an
oriented edge e:v->w the based element p_v e p_w^-1. Then a triangle
L X R^-1 stays an exact group relation, and an edge rectangle based at V
becomes p_V w p_V^-1. Nonidentity is preserved. For the split row/column
vertex variant, changing paths gives X_uv -> A_u X_uv B_v^-1, hence
Y ->diag_u(A_u) Y diag_v(B_v)^*, while every rectangle is conjugated
by A_u. Moments, support ranks and the factorization are preserved.

5. Scope / next attack.

No hyperlinear conclusion follows: the argument uses L(G) itself, which
exists for every discrete group. Its conclusion is that the proposed
complex scalar certificates/lifts cannot exist. Finite-field power sums
use q=0 and do not lift to characteristic zero. The result blocks this
specific transfer route. A route to nonhyperlinearity would require a new
global obstruction that controls analytic unitary errors without these
