# Rational certificates for robust endotactic trapping

The fixed-scale strict margins in source 149 can be converted into finite rational certificates. I reconstructed that conversion, implemented an exact checker and an exhaustive rational search specification, and obtained unconditional global certificates for a small non-weakly-reversible endotactic example and a fractional-complex variant. A finite inward-facet certificate by itself does not certify global absorption. The general all-endotactic permanence theorem remains a mathematical claim to be audited independently; the computations here neither establish it nor replace it.

The proposed extension replaces weak reversibility in the active-slope flux argument by endotacticity. That extension is being reconstructed by `unexplored_interfaces` and `endotactic_from_scratch`. The certificate derivation below assumes precisely the fixed-scale conclusions, not their external correctness or novelty.

Supplied input consists of:

- Finitely many nonnegative rational complexes `y_e,y'_e` in dimension `d`, with `nu_e=y'_e-y_e`.
- Rational `0<kappa<=K`, and arbitrary rates in this box. The pointwise inequalities apply to time variation and state feedback whenever an absolutely continuous solution exists. They also apply directly to the differential inclusion generated by the rate box.
- A positive rational `c`, the rational subspace `S=span{nu_e}`, and `P=(c+S) intersect R^d_{>0}`.
- Optionally, a supplied rational compact initial polytope `A subset P`, represented by its vertices.

No samples or trajectories are supplied. The affine labels, boxes, positivity margins, geometric witnesses and entry-time bounds are acquired or proposed and then checked. The implementation does not assume endotacticity to accept a certificate: acceptance proves the specified safety claim for any network with these inputs. Endotacticity is relevant to the conditional existence/completeness argument.

## Finite certificate and theorem it proves

Use rational affine functions

`L_i(x)=a_i.x+b_i`, `F(x)=min_i L_i(x)`,

including the constant zero label. Put `Q={x in c+S: L_i(x)>=0 for every i}`. A local certificate supplies a rational outer box `B`, a strictly positive inner box `B_in subset int B`, and rational `delta>0`, with the following checked conditions:

1. Every label is nonnegative at `c`, so `c in Q` and `F<=0` everywhere.
2. Rational Farkas witnesses establish `Q subset int B_in`. Thus `Q` is nonempty, compact, convex and positive.
3. For every label with `a_i notin S^perp`, every `x in B intersect(c+S)` where `L_i` is active, and every admissible rate vector,

   `a_i . sum_e k_e x^(y_e) nu_e >= delta`.

Labels in `S^perp` have exactly zero derivative. Their values on the class equal their nonnegative values at `c`, so they cannot be active when `F<0`.

Consequently every absolutely continuous trajectory segment in `B intersect P` has nondecreasing `F`; outside `Q`, its almost-everywhere derivative is at least `delta`. This follows from the chain rule for a finite minimum of absolutely continuous affine evaluations. The source's everywhere right-derivative argument for a smooth autonomous field must be changed to this almost-everywhere statement for measurable rates.

The compact positive plateau `Q` is forward invariant. A trajectory starting there is global under the usual continuation hypothesis, or as a solution of the rate-box differential inclusion. Any global trajectory that remains in `B` from time `t0` enters `Q` no later than

`t0 - F(x(t0))/delta`.

The last assertion is conditional on remaining in `B`. It is not a theorem that all trajectories in `P` enter this particular `Q`.

For a bare invariance certificate, it is sufficient to check nonnegative inward derivatives on the facets of `Q`. The stronger active-cell inequalities on all of `B` establish the quantitative box-conditional entry statement. These are different interfaces.

## Why rational fixed-scale certificates exist

Here is a complete rationalization lemma, conditional only on the fixed-scale hypotheses just stated for the original real labels.

Fix one positive compact box `B` and a real finite affine family with constant zero plateau. Suppose its plateau contains `c`, is strictly inside a positive inner box, and every essential active label has a uniform positive derivative on its compact active set, uniformly over the compact rate box. Suppose in addition that the nonconstant labels are strictly positive at `c`; for an initial compact `A`, require them strictly positive throughout `A`. Source 149 supplies these strict center/interior properties by taking its scale sufficiently small.

For an essential label `i`, define the worst-rate derivative

`g_i(x)=min_k a_i.f(x,k)`.

It is continuous on `B`. Choose a small positive threshold below its positive minimum on the active cell. The compact set where `g_i` is below that threshold is disjoint from the active cell; on this bad set, the continuous inactivity gap `L_i-F` has a positive minimum. A sufficiently small coefficient perturbation therefore cannot make that label active on the bad set. Uniform continuity of the derivative in both slope and state preserves a smaller positive derivative margin. A label with an empty active cell has a positive inactivity gap on the entire box and remains inactive after a small perturbation.

Keep the zero constant label exactly zero. Approximate every essential slope and offset rationally. For every originally orthogonal slope, approximate its slope *inside the rational linear subspace* `S^perp`; density of its rational points follows from rational Gaussian elimination. This preserves the zero derivative exactly. Positive constant labels remain positive constants. There are only finitely many labels, so all derivative and inactivity conditions survive one common perturbation size.

Nonconstant affine values have a strict positive gap at `c` (and throughout `A` when relevant), so the perturbed plateau contains `c` (and `A`). On the relative boundary of an inner box containing the original plateau, `F` has a strict negative maximum. That gap also survives. If the new convex plateau had a point outside the inner box, the segment joining it to `c` would cross this boundary at a plateau point, a contradiction. Thus compactness and positive distance from the coordinate boundary survive rationalization. A smaller rational `delta>0` is available.

This argument only rationalizes one fixed positive scale. It does not rationalize all scales simultaneously. The affine approximation lemma's original real slopes, power exponents and compact-subcover choices do not automatically become a single finite rational parametrization valid as `h -> 0`.

If the *classwide permanence theorem* has independently been established, a rational globally absorbing plateau also exists: rationalize its final-scale family on `B_*`. Every trajectory already eventually remains in the original `K_* subset int B_*`; box-conditional finite entry then makes the rational plateau globally absorbing. This is a valid existence argument. Local certificate acceptance alone does not identify which rational plateaux have that global basin.

## Exact decision and search procedures

For a fixed rational candidate, full first-order quantifier elimination is available in principle. Let `D` be a common denominator of the source exponents and substitute `x_i=z_i^D`, `z_i>0`. Then every mass-action monomial is polynomial in `z`; the class equations, box inequalities, label-activity conditions and derivative inequalities become a first-order formula over real closed fields with rational coefficients. The variable-rate quantifier can be eliminated without enumerating its vertices: because monomials are positive, the minimizing rate is `kappa` for terms with `a_i.nu_e>=0` and `K` for terms with negative sign. Quantifier elimination decides the formula; no efficient or small-degree claim follows. `D` itself can be huge in a binary encoding.

The runnable alternative in `certificate.py` uses only the Python standard library:

1. Derive exact rational class equations from a basis of `S^perp`.
2. Find and check Farkas containment witnesses by exhaustive conic-basis enumeration.
3. For each essential label, construct its bounded rational polyhedral active cell.
4. Enumerate all rational vertices of the active cell in a box, including degenerate cells. No vertices means empty, because the cell is bounded.
5. Tighten to its coordinate hull. Minimize the rate box termwise. Bound each rational power outward using integer comparisons and dyadic rational endpoints; combine the resulting monomial bounds with the proper coefficient signs.
6. Accept a leaf only if its exact lower derivative bound is at least `delta`. Otherwise subdivide or increase root precision. An active rational point with a rigorous upper derivative bound below `delta` refutes that *strict-margin candidate*. A finite resource limit returns `UNKNOWN`.

On a fixed compact active cell whose true minimum is strictly above the proposed `delta`, refinement eventually succeeds: continuous generalized monomials admit arbitrarily tight interval bounds, and the active cell's coordinate hulls shrink with the boxes. This is a positive semi-decision procedure. Dovetailing candidates and finite budgets avoids getting stuck on an invalid candidate.

`enumerate_local_candidates` and `search_local` implement a brute-force rational enumeration: at height `H`, all rational coefficients with numerator/denominator at most `H`, at most `H` nonconstant labels, boxes `[1/n,n]^d` with `4<=n<=H`, margins `1/H`, and verification depth/precision `H` are considered. The constant label is supplied by the search protocol, not by a teacher. Any one strict rational certificate of this form occurs at a finite height and is eventually checked with enough resources.

Conditional on the endotactic fixed-scale construction, this is a total *safety compiler for a supplied compact initial set*: a sufficiently large `n` makes the source plateau contain `A` in its interior and places it strictly inside `[2/n,n/2]^d`; the rationalization lemma supplies an enumerated candidate. It outputs an invariant positive rational polytope containing `A`, with a box-conditional entry certificate. It does not certify one classwide absorber merely because the first local candidate passes.

## Global absorption: finite chains and the remaining algorithmic gap

A finite sequence of local certificates `(F_j,B_j,Q_j,delta_j)`, with

`A subset Q_0`, `Q_j subset int B_(j+1)`,

is a genuine global-absorption certificate for *the supplied initial set `A`*. First `Q_0` keeps the trajectory global and positive. Invariance of `Q_j` keeps it inside `B_(j+1)` until the next finite entry. Induction establishes eventual residence in `Q_N`.

The rational bound for transition `j -> j+1` is

`-min_(x in Q_j) F_(j+1)(x)/delta_(j+1)`.

The minimum is exactly computable from rational polytope vertices. Sum these bounds. `check_chain` implements the containment checks and this entry bound. The demo checks a two-member chain ending at the example's final interval and gives total entry time at most `3/5` from the supplied initial segment `[1/16,15/16]`.

The source proof can supply such a finite chain for each prescribed compact initial set, ending at a classwide target. Every cross-box containment is strict, so a finite chain can be rationalized jointly. This is sufficient for source-derived finite entry, and no nesting of plateau sets is needed.

What is **not** proved here is a finite rational *all-scale* representation and a complete algorithm that recognizes a classwide globally absorbing target on an arbitrary unbounded class using only finite local inward-sign checks. One can require chains for every member of a countable compact exhaustion, but that is an infinite completeness obligation, not a finite accepted certificate. Generic real-algebraic tests of local invariance do not decide this obligation. A finite rational parametric family with a verified all-scale property would resolve this specific algorithmic gap; its existence has not been established in this report.

There is an important unconditional finite special case. If rational Farkas certificates show the entire nonnegative class is inside the outer box, and `y_(e,i)>=1` for every reaction that consumes species `i`, the active-cell certificate proves global absorption on that class. On a bounded class, this source-exponent condition gives `x_i'>=-C x_i`, excluding finite-time arrival at zero. Boundedness and continuation give global solutions. The demo uses this extra finite proof, including for its fractional-complex variant. The source theorem's more general real-exponent positivity argument should not be silently replaced by this narrower condition.

## Why inward facets do not imply global attraction

Even a weakly reversible scalar mass-action system can invalidate that implication. Consider

`0 <-> A` with forward/backward rates `6,11`, and `2A <-> 3A` with rates `6,1`.

Its vector field is

`f(x)=6-11x+6x^2-x^3=-(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)`.

The compact positive interval `[1/2,3/2]` has strictly inward derivatives: `f(1/2)=15/8>0`, `f(3/2)=-3/8<0`. It is forward invariant. Nevertheless `x(t)=3` is a positive equilibrium forever outside that interval. Thus a strict rational inward-facet certificate, even on an endotactic/weakly reversible network, cannot alone be relabeled as a globally absorbing certificate. A local plateau certificate on a slightly larger box has the same limitation.

## Worked non-weakly-reversible endotactic network

Take

`2A -> A+B <- 2B`,

with both rates independently allowed to vary in `[1,4]`. Its graph has no directed return path from the sink `A+B`; it is not weakly reversible. It is endotactic: for a direction with `r_A<r_B`, the lower essential source is `2A` and its reaction has positive projection `r_B-r_A`; the negative reaction's source `2B` is strictly higher. The opposite inequality is symmetric. At equality all reaction projections vanish. This example is also strongly endotactic relative to its one-dimensional stoichiometric subspace, so it is not evidence that the general higher-dimensional extension is new or correct.

On `P={x+y=1,x,y>0}`,

`x'=k_2 y^2-k_1 x^2`, `y'=-x'`.

Use

`F(x,y)=min(0,x-1/4,3/4-x)`.

When `x<=1/4`, the worst derivative of its active lower label is at least

`(3/4)^2-4(1/4)^2=5/16`.

When `x>=3/4`, the upper label's derivative has the same lower bound. Thus

`Q={x+y=1,1/4<=x<=3/4}`

is invariant and globally absorbing for every admissible measurable rate trajectory. Since `F>=-1/4` throughout the positive class, every initial point enters within `4/5` time units. Positivity follows from the ordinary mass-action exponents and bounded class; the proof does not require sampled trajectories or a common equilibrium across rates.

The exact checker finds two active cells and exact margin `5/16`, with no subdivision. The positive inner-box containment has rational Farkas witnesses. A restricted acquisition search over intervals `x in [1/n,1-1/n]` rejects the strict `n=3` certificate and finds `n=4`; its acquired margin `1/4` gives the looser uniform entry bound `1`.

The `n=3` boundary coincides with an extreme-rate equilibrium. For `k_1=4,k_2=1`, trajectories below `1/3` approach it asymptotically, so the minimal robust interval is not a finite-time absorber. Expanding it supplies a strict margin. This illustrates why invariance/global attraction and finite-time absorption must be distinguished.

Decisive falsification: widening the upper rate bound to `16` while retaining the same quarter interval yields the exact outward derivative `-7/16` at `(x,y)=(1/4,3/4)` with rates `(k_1,k_2)=(16,1)`. `failed_wider_rates.json` retains this witness. It refutes that proposed uncertainty certificate; it does not refute permanence of the network with the larger rate box.

For the stochastic-safety transfer, the normals are `(1,0)` and `(-1,0)`, each with `l1` norm `1`, and jumps are `(-1,+1)` and `(+1,-1)`. The rational layer width `1/32` preserves margin

`(23/32)^2-4(9/32)^2=205/1024 >= (5/16)/2`.

These are deterministic inward-layer data, not themselves a stochastic lifetime theorem.

## Fractional-complex example

Replace the reactions by

`(3/2)A -> (1/2)A+B`, `(3/2)B -> A+(1/2)B`,

and the rate box by `[1,2]`. Stoichiometry, class, non-weak-reversibility and the endotactic proof remain as above. The same quarter interval has worst inward margin

`(3/4)^(3/2)-2(1/4)^(3/2)=(3 sqrt(3)-2)/8 > 1/4`.

The checker computes the rational outward lower bound `857960633/2147483648`, proving the requested rational margin `1/4` with integer comparisons. It gives uniform global entry time at most `1`. The consuming source exponent is `3/2>=1`, so the finite positivity check applies. This is a deterministic power-law example; its noninteger complexes are not automatically a physically defined molecular jump process.

## Prior art and novelty limits

The general machinery here uses standard real-algebraic decision, polytope geometry, interval positivity and inward-vector-field arguments. I do not claim novelty for rational brute-force certificate search or the elementary example.

[Johnston, Pantea and Donnell (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4662) give MILP tests of endotacticity and strong endotacticity, implemented in CoNtRol. This addresses structural recognition, whereas the present output also contains numerical concentration and entry certificates.

[Ding, Deshpande and Craciun](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/dcdsb.2022143) construct invariant and globally attracting regions for systems of two reversible reactions with bounded variable rates. Their results are direct prior art for explicit robust trapping regions; no novelty conclusion can be drawn from this small example.

[Al-Radhawi, Angeli and Sontag (2020)](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007681) develop robust Lyapunov functions and the LEARN computational package for structural attractivity. Their advertised target excludes multistability and oscillation in a restricted graph class, while an absorbing plateau does not assert convergence to one equilibrium. The distinction is about the proven interface, not superiority.

Live search also found the maintained [CRNToolkit changelog](https://github.com/cbriat/CRNToolkit/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md), whose June 2026 release includes an implementation of LEARN's LP method, and [crnt-lean](https://github.com/marpaia/crnt-lean), which advertises formal CRNT results and a separating-confinement predicate. Neither codebase was audited or executed here. Their existence is a warning against claiming that computational or formal CRNT certification itself is new.

The consequential conditional deduction is narrower: *if* the general endotactic fixed-scale lemma has been established, rational-input instances admit finite exact safety certificates without supplying a real-valued affine family as a teacher. The acquisition may be astronomical. The classwide global theorem supplies a further rational-absorber existence consequence, but the finite globally recognized acquisition interface remains incomplete on arbitrary unbounded classes.

No simulation is needed for any accepted claim. The Python implementation is a small ordinary program, not a formally verified arithmetic kernel.

For `N` labels, `d` species and `s=dim S`, one active-cell vertex pass examines at most `binomial(N+2d,s)` candidate bases with exact rational Gaussian elimination. Farkas search examines conic bases of size at most `d+1` from `N+2(d-s)+1` columns. A depth-`b` subdivision can retain exponentially many nodes. The rate minimum takes `O(number of reactions)` terms rather than `2^m` rate vertices. Integer bit heights, rational-power numerator/denominator sizes, root precision and retained proof trees are all real costs.

At search height `H`, the label pool alone can have `O(H^(2(d+1)))` entries; enumerating its subsets is much larger. There is no polynomial-time, polynomial-bit, practical all-network or finite user-budget guarantee. The affine existence argument supplies no useful a priori label/height bound. Failed or budget-limited candidates are retained as `REFUTED` or `UNKNOWN`, respectively.

The named demo certificates each use two essential active cells, no subdivision and millisecond-scale diagnostic execution on the local Python 3.14 runtime. These timings are scoped to the tiny examples and are not a general complexity result. The 36 checks establish the exercised arithmetic directions, empty/degenerate polytope handling and named positive/negative cases only; they do not establish the universal mathematical theorem.
