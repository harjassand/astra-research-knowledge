PRECISE PRINCIPAL RESULT

There exists an explicitly specified irreducible stationary continuous-time Markov chain with ten states, rational transition rates, uniform stationary distribution, and a deterministic observation map with six output letters, such that:

(1) Its entire stationary observed law is time-reversal invariant. Its observable reflection Hilbert space has dimension eight, with a selfadjoint contraction semigroup.

(2) For every finite collection of finite future cylinder events, at arbitrary nonnegative times, the matrix pairing their time-reflected past events with their future events is classically completely positive. All such matrices have one common ten-atom nonnegative factorization. Here complete positivity means a matrix factorization BB^T with B entrywise nonnegative, not complete positivity of a quantum channel.

(3) Nevertheless its law lies outside the finite-dimensional-distribution closure of the stationary observations of reversible hidden Markov processes with deterministic state observations and the usual regular conditional Markov interface (in particular, standard-Borel hidden state spaces). In particular no sequence of finite equilibrium hidden CTMCs can approximate all its rational-time cylinder probabilities, even with unbounded hidden dimension, rates, activity and arbitrarily small hidden stationary masses.

(4) The separation is witnessed by one explicitly specified rational, bounded function w of seven observed letters at times
    -t-h, -t, -h, 0, h, t, t+h,
    t=1/1000, h=1/10^12.
It satisfies |w|<=1 and E_equilibrium w>=0 for every stationary reversible hidden Markov realization under that observation interface, while for the constructed process
    E_target w < -1/(6*10^45).
The rational coefficient file and a word evaluator are included. Hidden states are used to construct and prove the certificate; evaluating the fixed statistic uses only the seven observed letters.

(5) Consequently EVERY stationary finite all-even hidden CTMC producing this observed process has entropy production rate
    sigma > 6*10^(-44) nats per model time unit,
with the usual stationary CTMC path-relative-entropy convention. This includes completions with arbitrarily many hidden states and arbitrarily fast rates. It is not a claim about the entropy production of just the supplied ten-state model.

The observed path-reversal relative entropy is zero and every CP reflection test passes. The indispensable obstruction is consistency with a reversible evolution of the common hidden factors.

WHY THE PROOF WORKS

The ten states consist of five internal points on a strictly convex cyclic pentagon and five individually observed marker states. Internal affine functions and marker indicators form an eight-dimensional invariant observable space. The restriction of the nonreversible generator to this space is selfadjoint. Projecting reversed-past features onto this space yields the corresponding nonnegative future features, which proves all-word complete positivity without asserting that actual hidden past and future conditional laws coincide.

Marker-event positivity forces every admissible internal static feature point into the pentagon. A saturated unit-circle second moment forces those points onto its vertices; a nonsingular five-moment matrix fixes their weights. A rational monotone vector field on these points has a strictly negative pairing with the required drift. Reversible evolution would produce symmetric two-time couplings, for which that pairing must be nonnegative.

The closure proof uses uniformly bounded conditional expectations of one fixed finite future basis. It passes to symmetric couplings at fixed positive lag and never differentiates a sequence of hidden generators. An exact Taylor remainder certifies the contradiction already at t=1/1000.

The constructive finite-observation proof replaces exact feature identities by residuals whose reflected squared norms are observable. Polygon projection, circle-defect rounding and Young inequalities absorb these errors into a single linear inequality in a seven-time probability table. Degree-four and degree-sixteen matrix polynomials make every coefficient rational. The resulting raw statistic has target expectation at most -1.173761546566416e-6, with rigorously certified pointwise magnitude below 6e39. These displayed decimals summarize exact rational comparisons; the proof does not depend on floating-point signs.

The final entropy-production consequence uses path-affinity domination. Let p and q be stationary hidden path densities for any compatible finite generator Q and its stationary adjoint Q*, and let s be the path density of S=(Q+Q*)/2. Stationarity makes their initial and holding-time factors agree. Edgewise arithmetic-geometric mean gives sqrt(pq)<=s pointwise. Also sqrt(pq)<=(p+q)/2=:m. Consequently
    TV(m,s)<=1-integral sqrt(pq)
             <=1-exp[-D(p||q)/2]=1-exp(-T sigma/2).
The second inequality is Jensen's inequality, and the last identity is the usual stationary all-even CTMC entropy-production formula. The witness is pointwise invariant under reversing its seven letters, so its expectation under Q equals its expectation under m. Its range is at most two. Its equilibrium expectation is nonnegative and its target expectation is below -delta. Thus
    delta<=2[1-exp(-T sigma/2)],
    sigma>=-(2/T)log(1-delta/2)>=delta/T>6e-44,
    T=2(t+h), delta=1/(6*10^45).
One-way positive stationary flux has infinite entropy production and satisfies the bound automatically. The earlier Pinsker-based bound >2e-89 remains valid but is quantitatively superseded. The complete affinity argument is appended in observable_separation/v3.txt. Its historical priority is unestablished; additive reversibilization and the path-KL identity are known ingredients.

The mathematical distinction is between a static common nonnegative representation of every past/future reflection pairing and a dynamically compatible equilibrium realization. The construction separates them even at finite observation resolution. The companion finite-support theorem characterizes equilibrium closure by a finite symmetric-flow feasibility problem under its precise finite-support hypothesis; it is not a universal finite algorithm for all hidden processes.

The explicit numerical certificate is extremely inefficient. No practical experiment, improved hardware capability, or useful numerical dissipation estimate follows from those costs. All-even hidden variables, stationarity and the specified observation interface are essential to the entropy statement. Odd time-reversal variables such as momenta, memory-bearing observation devices and nonstationary experiments are not silently included. The process is an explicit mathematical model; no natural system was empirically shown to obey it.

TARGET
Close the compatibility gap between reflected completely positive probability kernels and a single finite all-even reversible CTMC realization. The reduction below eliminates the unknown semigroup and converts detailed balance into a finite nonnegative edge-flow feasibility problem once a static frame is selected. Its unresolved part is selecting a frame from observable data, or proving/refuting that full reflection CP guarantees an admissible frame.

1. OBSERVABLE FINITE-RANK SPACE
Let Y be a stationary, reflection-invariant, stochastically continuous process on a finite alphabet A. Include time 0 in the future sigma-algebra. For bounded future cylinder functions F,G put
  B(F,G)=E[F(Y_{-t}:t>=0) G(Y_t:t>=0)].
Assume B is positive semidefinite. Let H be its Hilbert quotient and completion, and assume dim H=r<infinity. Write Omega=[1]. Its norm is 1.

The future shift U_s gives T_s[F]=[U_s F]. This is a well-defined self-adjoint contraction semigroup. Here is a proof of the potentially load-bearing contraction step: stationarity and reflection give B(U_sF,G)=B(F,U_sG). Put h(s)=B(U_sF,U_sF)>=0. Cauchy-Schwarz for B gives h(s)^2<=h(0)h(2s), while |h(s)|<=E[F^2] by ordinary probability Cauchy-Schwarz. Iteration yields h(s)<=h(0)^(1-2^-n)(E[F^2])^(2^-n), hence h(s)<=h(0). Null vectors therefore shift to null vectors. Symmetry and the semigroup law follow from the stated identity. Stochastic continuity of the finite alphabet makes shifts continuous in ordinary L2 for cylinders, hence in B norm; density gives strong continuity. Thus T_s=exp(-s A), where A is a symmetric positive semidefinite r-by-r operator and A Omega=0.

Boundary multiplication M_a F=1_{Y0=a}F gives orthogonal projections E_a on H: B(M_aF,G)=B(F,M_aG), M_a^2=M_a, and cross terms for different a are zero. Also sum_a E_a=I and ||E_a[F]||^2<=||[F]||^2. The vectors obtained by products of T_s and E_a acting on Omega span H. Observed word probabilities are
  Pr(Y0=a0,Yt1=a1,...,Ytm=am)
  =<Omega,E_a0 exp(-t1 A) E_a1 exp(-(t2-t1)A)...E_am Omega>.
This is the observable Osterwalder-Schrader realization. No Markov positivity of exp(-s A) relative to a lattice has been inferred.

2. EXACT REVERSIBLE FRAME THEOREM
Under the preceding assumptions, Y has an exact finite stationary reversible CTMC realization with deterministic state emissions if and only if there exist a finite n, vectors x_i in H, positive weights pi_i, labels a_i in A, and symmetric nonnegative numbers w_ij=w_ji for i!=j such that
  (F1) <Omega,x_i>=1;
  (F2) sum_i pi_i x_i x_i^T=I_H;
  (F3) E_a x_i=1_{a_i=a} x_i for every a,i;
  (F4) sum_{j!=i} w_ij (x_j-x_i)=-pi_i A x_i for every i.
The weights automatically sum to 1: test (F2) on Omega and use (F1). They also have barycenter Omega: apply (F2) to Omega.

Necessity. For a reversible hidden chain Q with invariant pi, map [F] to the conditional future expectation h_F(i)=E[F|X0=i]. Conditional past/future independence and reversibility imply B(F,G)=sum_i pi_i h_F(i)h_G(i), so this is an isometry j:H->L2(pi). Its future shift intertwines j exp(-tA)=exp(tQ)j. Boundary multiplication intertwines j E_a=D_a j, with D_a the hidden diagonal output projection. Define x_i by (jv)(i)=<x_i,v>. Then (F1)-(F3) follow from jOmega=1, isometry and deterministic emission. Differentiating the semigroup relation and multiplying by pi_i gives (F4) with w_ij=pi_i Q_ij, symmetric by detailed balance. Hidden stationary zero-mass states can be removed.

Sufficiency. Set Q_ij=w_ij/pi_i, Q_ii=-sum_j Q_ij. This is a finite CTMC generator, stationary and reversible for pi. Define (jv)(i)=<x_i,v>. (F2) makes j an isometry and (F1) makes jOmega=1. (F3) gives jE_a=D_a j. (F4) gives Qj=-jA, so exp(tQ)j=jexp(-tA) for all t>=0. Inserting these intertwinings into every word probability proves that the output of Q with labels a_i has exactly Y's finite-dimensional distributions. This constructs an actual common generator. Reducible realizations are allowed; irreducibility is an additional condition on the w graph and is not implied.

3. THE MISSING CONDITION IS A MONOTONE-FIELD INEQUALITY
For a fixed finite static frame satisfying (F1)-(F3), (F4) is a linear feasibility problem in the w_ij>=0. Farkas's lemma gives the following exact dual criterion:
  sum_i pi_i <z_i,A x_i> >=0
for every assignment z_i in H satisfying
  <z_i-z_j,x_i-x_j> >=0 for every i<j.               (M)

Proof. On the vector space H^n, let the column for edge ij be the vector c_ij having x_i-x_j in component i, x_j-x_i in component j, and zero elsewhere. (F4) is equivalent to b=sum_{i<j}w_ij c_ij with b_i=pi_i A x_i. The finitely generated cone of the columns is closed. Its dual is precisely the assignments in (M); cone separation gives the equivalence. The signs can also be checked directly: summing <z_i,pi_i A x_i> over (F4) gives sum_{i<j}w_ij<z_i-z_j,x_i-x_j>.

This is a genuine graph-flow compatibility test, not just PSD of A. Constant z and linear symmetric positive semidefinite fields give necessary consequences already implied by the frame covariance, but arbitrary finite monotone fields impose extra constraints. To use it as an observable criterion one must quantify over compatible frames or characterize their existence; that selection problem remains open here.

4. STATIC CP FRAME AND ITS LIMIT
Let C be the cone generated by all nonnegative future cylinder functions in H. Because 0<=[1_F]<=Omega and a finite collection spans H, Omega is an interior order unit of C. Thus D={x:<Omega,x>=1, <x,c>>=0 for c in C} is compact. A completely positive factorization of every finite Gram subkernel can be glued to a probability measure mu on D with integral xx^T dmu=I (the independent T13 argument supplies global factor consistency). Finite-dimensional conic Caratheodory then selects at most r(r+1)/2 atoms for a static normalized frame. Boundary emissions force its atoms into the union of the E_a eigenspaces, because E_a Omega are disjoint events and cross second moments vanish.

However, this static finite frame does NOT establish (M). The intrinsic semigroup preserves C, equivalently exp(-tA)D subset D, but that only certifies a forward positive linear drift on the observable state domain. Symmetric nonnegative edge flows with the same stationary frame require (M), which is a stronger detailed-balance lifting problem. No implication from static CP to (M) is claimed.

MAIN THEOREM AND CONSEQUENCE

There is a stationary irreducible finite all-even CTMC, with positive finite entropy production and six deterministic observed labels, whose observed path law P has all of the following properties:
  (a) P is exactly invariant under ordinary time reversal;
  (b) EVERY finite reflected future/past probability kernel, at arbitrary times and including the reflection boundary, is classically completely positive, with a single common ten-atom nonnegative factorization;
  (c) its observable reflection Hilbert space has finite rank eight and a self-adjoint positive contraction semigroup;
  (d) nevertheless P is outside the rational-cylinder closure of observations of ALL finite stationary reversible CTMCs, with no bounds on hidden dimension, rates, activity, stationary masses, or reducibility.

Thus even globally compatible complete positivity of every reflected word table does not characterize hidden equilibrium. A genuine positive common semigroup/detailed-balance obstruction survives ALL reflection CP tests, not merely individual pair/covariance tests. The construction uses a nonreducing observable subspace: the forward generator is invariant and self-adjoint there, while the adjoint leaks out of that subspace.

A general rigidity theorem below also gives a necessary-and-sufficient observable characterization of equilibrium closure for a finite-rank class whose static positive factors are forced onto finitely many state vectors. In that class equilibrium closure equals exact finite reversible realizability, and a finite nonnegative edge-flow linear program decides the remaining compatibility once the forced vectors/weights are known. This is a subclass characterization, not a universal solution for arbitrary path laws.

1. OBSERVABLE SETUP AND THE REVERSIBLE FRAME CONDITION

Use the reflection Hilbert space H, unit Omega, semigroup T_t=exp(-t A), A symmetric positive semidefinite, and boundary output projections E_a from v1. Its vectors are equivalence classes [F] of bounded future-cylinder functions; <[F],[G]>=E[F(reversed past)G(future)]. Rational times suffice when the target is stochastically continuous, because finite-alphabet cylinders at other times are limits in ordinary L2 and hence in reflection norm.

Let C be the cone generated by classes of nonnegative rational future-cylinder functions. Let
 D_a={x in H: <Omega,x>=1, E_a x=x, <x,c>>=0 for every c in C},
 D=union_a D_a,
 Gamma={mu in Prob(D): integral_D xx^T dmu(x)=I_H}.

D is compact. Choose finitely many nonnegative future-cylinder indicators whose classes span H. Each is between zero and Omega in the cone order. Every x in D therefore evaluates each chosen indicator in [0,1], bounding all coordinates of x; the defining conditions are closed. This also supplies an order-unit proof of compactness, without relying on a preferred CP decomposition.

Any exact finite reversible realization gives a frame satisfying F1–F4 of v1. Equivalently it supplies a finitely supported mu in Gamma with labels and symmetric nonnegative flows
  sum_j w_ij(x_j-x_i)=-pi_i A x_i.                       (1)
Conversely (1) constructs the common reversible generator Q_ij=w_ij/pi_i. For a fixed frame, feasibility is equivalent to
  sum_i pi_i <Z_i,A x_i>>=0
for every pairwise monotone field Z_i satisfying
  <Z_i-Z_j,x_i-x_j>>=0.                                 (2)
This is finite Farkas separation, proved in v1.

Global reflection CP is exactly the static condition Gamma nonempty at finite rank. The forward implication can be derived from T13's common-factor gluing: choose a finite basis [F_j]. All zero reflection-norm linear relations hold almost surely in its common nonnegative feature factor; these relations identify a vector x in H evaluating all future cylinders. The constant is one. Boundary0 events have disjoint second moments and probabilities summing to one, so their conditional probabilities are deterministic zero/one; all boundary multiplication relations give E_a x=x. The feature second moments are I. Conversely any mu in Gamma gives every reflected kernel as integral <x,[F]><x,[G]> dmu, entrywise nonnegative for nonnegative F,G. Finite-dimensional Caratheodory can make the STATIC factorization finite. It does not infer (1).

2. GENERAL FINITE-SUPPORT RIGIDITY THEOREM

Theorem. Assume the target P is stationary, stochastically continuous, reflection-invariant, reflection-positive, and has finite-dimensional H as above. Suppose there is one finite set S subset D such that every mu in Gamma is supported on S. Then the following are equivalent:
  (i) P lies in the rational-cylinder closure of finite stationary reversible CTMC output laws;
  (ii) P has an exact reversible CTMC realization with at most |S| states;
  (iii) some probability weights on S with second moment I admit the symmetric nonnegative edge flows (1).
Zero-weight points may be discarded. This does not require irreducibility of the comparison models. Equivalence (ii)<->(iii) is the frame theorem. The important implication is (i)->(iii).

Proof of (i)->(iii), including the unbounded-dimension/rate issue.

Let P_n be equilibrium outputs converging to P on all rational cylinders. In each hidden model let pi_n, Q_n be its stationary distribution and reversible generator. Choose a basis of H consisting of linear combinations of finitely many bounded future-cylinder indicators. For v in H, define j_n v to be the same linear combination of the corresponding CONDITIONAL future probabilities under model n, given its hidden state at time0. All these functions are uniformly bounded, independently of hidden dimension/rates. Choose an orthonormal basis e_1,...,e_r of H and define the feature vector x_n(i) by (j_n v)(i)=<x_n(i),v>. The x_n lie in one fixed compact box.

For any fixed bounded future cylinder F, write [F]=v_F in H. The reflection norm in model n of F minus the basis combination for v_F tends to zero, because its square is a finite linear combination of observed reflected-cylinder probabilities. Under equilibrium this reflection norm is exactly the hidden L2(pi_n) norm of the conditional future expectation. Hence
  ||h_F^n-j_n v_F||_(L2(pi_n)) ->0.                     (3)
The same applies to the constant function, boundary multiplication M_a, and shifted future functions U_t F at any fixed rational t. Consequently
  ||j_n Omega-1||_2 ->0,
  ||j_n E_a v-D_a^n j_n v||_2 ->0,
  ||j_n T_t v-exp(t Q_n)j_n v||_2 ->0.                  (4)
Here D_a^n is the actual hidden output indicator. The last identity uses the Markov property before taking the limit. No derivative, rate, activity, or state-count convergence is invoked.

Let mu_n be the stationary distribution of (x_n(i),g_n(i)), with the finite label included. Compactness gives a subsequential limit. Gram convergence gives integral xx^T dmu=I. Equations (3)-(4) imply <Omega,x>=1, the label projection relation E_a x=x, and <x,[F]>>=0 for every nonnegative rational cylinder F. For the last step use the continuous negative part: its squared expectation is bounded by the squared error in (3). A countable intersection over rational cylinders is sufficient. Thus mu belongs to Gamma and, by rigidity, is supported on S. The label is determined by x because x is nonzero and belongs to exactly one orthogonal projection block.

For each rational t>0 take the stationary two-time joint distribution kappa_n,t of (x_n(X0),x_n(Xt)). It is symmetric by reversibility and has both marginals mu_n. A diagonal subsequence yields a limit kappa_t with both marginals mu, supported on S x S. For bounded continuous f on the compact feature box and v in H, (4), the Markov property, and Cauchy-Schwarz show
  integral f(x)<y,v> dkappa_t(x,y)
    =integral f(x)<x,T_t v> dmu(x).
Indeed the difference before the limit is bounded by ||f||_infty times the L2 error in the last relation in (4). Since S is finite, continuous f can select each x_i, giving the row regression identity
  sum_j kappa_t(i,j) x_j=pi_i T_t x_i.                 (5)

There are two equivalent ways to finish. A fixed monotone field satisfies
  sum_i pi_i<Z_i,x_i-T_t x_i>
   =1/2 sum_ij kappa_t(i,j)<Z_i-Z_j,x_i-x_j>>=0.        (6)
Dividing by t and taking t down to0 gives every inequality (2). Finite Farkas separation gives (1).
Alternatively the feature energy is
  sum_ij kappa_t(i,j)||x_i-x_j||^2
    =2 tr(I-T_t)<=2t tr(A).
Distinct points of S have a positive minimum separation, so all off-diagonal kappa_t(i,j)/t are bounded. A subsequence at small rational t converges to symmetric nonnegative w_ij, and dividing (5) minus pi_i x_i by t yields (1) directly. Both arguments construct a genuine common generator at the target; neither assumes that the approximating generators have bounded rates or converge.

T13 — COMMON FACTORS, SHIFT COMPATIBILITY, AND THE FINITE-STATE GAP
Frozen record v1 | 9 October 2026
Status: internally derived structural lemma and exact finite-state criterion; no novelty claim. A countable-state counterexample proves that projective CP alone does not imply a finite hidden-state realization. The criterion does not derive the shift axiom from CP. The closure hierarchy below is convergent in the cylinder topology but is not a finite-time decision procedure.

TARGET
Given a stationary observed process on a finite alphabet, understand whether projectively consistent classical completely positive (CP) reflected-cylinder kernels force one common hidden state space and a reversible continuous-time Markov realization. Keep separate (i) common factor gluing, (ii) temporal Markov compatibility, (iii) finite hidden dimension, and (iv) closure of finite-state realizations.

CONVENTIONS AND INPUT
Let A be a finite output alphabet and D=Q_{>=0}. A one-sided path law is a probability measure on A^D with its product sigma-field; when the observed process is cadlag, rational-time cylinders determine its law. Let W_n=A^{D_n}, where D_1 subset D_2 subset ... are finite sets exhausting D. For a word/cylinder u, [u] denotes its cylinder event. A reflected kernel H(u,v) is the observed probability that the reversed-past cylinder u and future cylinder v both occur across a fixed reflection boundary. We assume:

 (i) H is nonnegative, symmetric, normalized, and projectively consistent under cylinder refinement;
 (ii) for each n, the complete-word matrix H_n=(H(x,y))_{x,y in W_n} is classically completely positive, i.e. H_n=B_n B_n^T for some entrywise nonnegative B_n;
 (iii) the row/column marginals agree with the supplied one-sided observed cylinder law.

Here CP always means the cone generated by xx^T for x>=0. It is not complete positivity of a quantum channel. The result uses CP itself; replacing it with positive semidefinite plus entrywise nonnegative is invalid in general at matrix order at least five.

1. COMMON PROJECTIVE FACTOR GLUING

Lemma 1. Under (i)–(iii), there exists one probability measure mu on K=Prob(A^D) such that for every pair of finite cylinders u,v,

                 H(u,v) = integral_K nu([u]) nu([v]) d mu(nu).       (1)

The same mu gives the observed one-sided marginals:

                 P^+([u]) = integral_K nu([u]) d mu(nu).             (2)

Proof. Fix n and write H_n(x,y)=sum_r b_r(x)b_r(y), b_r>=0. For every nonzero column b_r put s_r=sum_x b_r(x), lambda_r=s_r^2, and h_r(x)=b_r(x)/s_r. Since sum_{x,y}H_n(x,y)=1, sum_r lambda_r=1; each h_r is a probability vector on W_n. Extend h_r to a probability law nu_r on all of A^D, for example by fixing all coordinates outside D_n to one arbitrary symbol. Then mu_n=sum_r lambda_r delta_{nu_r} is a probability measure on K. Its second moments reproduce H(u,v) for cylinders supported on D_n: obtain shorter cylinders by summing over exact assignments in W_n and use projective consistency.

Let F_n be the set of probability measures mu on K satisfying (1) for all cylinders supported on D_n. The construction gives F_n nonempty. The constraints are nested because H is projectively consistent. The cylinder maps nu -> nu([u]) are continuous on K, so each F_n is closed. Since A^D is compact metrizable, K=Prob(A^D) and Prob(K) are compact. Thus the nested closed sets F_n have a common element. The first-moment identity (2) follows by summing (1) over a complete second-side cylinder partition and using the stipulated marginal consistency. This proves the lemma.

Interpretation and exact limitation. Equation (1) constructs a common classical positive feature space: the functions h_u(nu)=nu([u]) are nonnegative in L2(mu), and H(u,v)=<h_u,h_v>. It removes the factor-by-factor gluing obstruction for the single reflected cut. It does NOT construct transitions between feature points, a Markov kernel on K, or operators P_t. In particular, the feature shift nu -> theta_t nu is a deterministic shift of an entire output law; in a hidden Markov model, the conditional future law after time t is instead a mixture over hidden states. There is no reason for that mixture kernel to be encoded by H's second moments. Projective CP therefore supplies a common factor measure, not a semigroup.

2. AN OBSERVABLE SHIFT-SIMPLEX CRITERION FOR FINITE REALIZATION

The remaining compatibility has an exact formulation in terms of a finite simplex of conditional future laws. This is stronger and more concrete than asking for unspecified factors at each time.

Definition (finite reversible shift simplex, FRS). A stationary observed law P on A^D has an FRS of size N if there exist weights pi_i>0 (sum_i pi_i=1), output labels g_i in A, one-sided path laws nu_i in Prob(A^D), and a strongly continuous matrix semigroup S_t (t>=0) such that:

 (a) nu_i(Y_0=g_i)=1 for every i, and P^+=sum_i pi_i nu_i;
 (b) each S_t is row-stochastic, S_0=I, S_{s+t}=S_s S_t, and pi_i S_t(i,j)=pi_j S_t(j,i);
 (c) for every rational t>=0 and every future cylinder C,

             (theta_t nu_i)(C) = sum_j S_t(i,j) nu_j(C),             (3)

where theta_t is the push-forward by the future shift omega(s) -> omega(s+t).

The finite family nu_i is a simplex of observed conditional futures. Equation (3) says its convex hull is invariant under every time shift, with the same reversible barycentric dynamics S_t. It is stated entirely using conditional output laws and cylinder equalities; it is an observed positive-realization criterion. If the nu_i are linearly independent, the barycentric coordinates are unique, and theta_{s+t}=theta_s theta_t forces the semigroup composition law whenever the shift equations hold. For general redundant families, composition is retained explicitly in (b).

Theorem 2 (exact equivalence). P has an N-state reversible hidden CTMC realization with deterministic observation if and only if it has an FRS of size at most N.

Forward direction. Given a finite reversible generator Q, stationary pi, and output labels g_i, let S_t=exp(tQ) and let nu_i be the conditional future observed path law from hidden state i. The hidden Markov property gives (3); detailed balance gives pi_i S_t(i,j)=pi_j S_t(j,i). The first output is g_i deterministically and P^+=sum pi_i nu_i.

Reverse direction. A strongly continuous stochastic semigroup on a finite state space has a generator Q with off-diagonal entries nonnegative and row sums zero; detailed balance of S_t implies pi_i Q_ij=pi_j Q_ji. Build the stationary CTMC with generator Q and observe g_i. Let rho_i be its conditional future law from i. For any cylinder with first time 0, rho_i and nu_i agree at time 0 by (a). If the next observation time is t>0, the Markov recursion for rho_i and (3) give the same mixture over j of the suffix cylinder. Induction on the number of observation times shows rho_i=nu_i on every rational cylinder. Hence the constructed stationary observed future law is sum_i pi_i rho_i=P^+. A stationary two-sided law is determined by its nonnegative-time finite-dimensional marginals after translating the earliest queried time to zero. Thus the full observed law equals P. The hidden chain is reversible by detailed balance.

Under Theorem 2, every reflected kernel is automatically a *single compatible* CP factorization:

                 H(u,v)=sum_i pi_i nu_i([u])nu_i([v]).              (4)

This follows from conditional independence of past and future given X_0 and equality of their conditional laws under detailed balance. The converse from (1) to (3) is the unresolved compatibility step: (1) alone has no barycentric shift maps S_t.

3. PROCESS-LEVEL COUNTEREXAMPLE: CP DOES NOT FORCE FINITE HIDDEN DIMENSION

For n>=1 let lambda_n=1-2^{-n}, w_n=2^{-n}, and define a countable-state hidden chain with states (n,b), b in {0,1}. The label n is fixed forever; conditional on n, b flips in either direction at rate lambda_n. The stationary law is pi(n,b)=w_n/2. The chain is reversible, with bounded exit rate below one. Observe Y=b.

This gives a stationary reversible observed path law with

                 Cov(Y_0,Y_t) = (1/4) sum_{n>=1} w_n exp(-2 lambda_n t).

Every finite reflected word table is CP, with one common countable-state factorization using h_{n,b}(u)=Pr(Y_future in [u] | X_0=(n,b)); the countable sum converges entrywise. The factor laws are projectively compatible and the hidden state has a strongly continuous reversible Markov semigroup.

Nevertheless, no finite-state reversible CTMC can realize this observed law. For any N-state reversible CTMC and centered f=Y-EY, the spectral theorem for its self-adjoint generator gives

                 4 Cov(Y_0,Y_t) = sum_{j=1}^{r} a_j exp(-gamma_j t),

with a_j>0, distinct gamma_j>=0, and r<=N. The zero rate is allowed because a finite reversible chain may be reducible. The target has infinitely many distinct rates beta_n=2 lambda_n in [1,2) and weights w_n>0. To see this without appealing to asymptotic curve fitting, define m_k=(-1)^k (d^k/dt^k)[4 Cov(Y_0,Y_t)] at zero. Then

                 m_k = sum_{n>=1} 2^{-n} beta_n^k.

For every d and every nonzero polynomial p(x)=sum_{i=0}^d c_i x^i,

                 sum_{i,j=0}^d c_i c_j m_{i+j}
                   = sum_{n>=1} 2^{-n} p(beta_n)^2 > 0,

because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish at infinitely many distinct beta_n. Hence every finite Hankel moment matrix (m_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^d has rank d+1. A sum of r exponentials has Hankel rank at most r. Taking d=N contradicts r<=N. This is an exact observable obstruction to finite hidden dimension, beyond any one finite CP check.

Closure caveat. This countable-state law IS in the total-variation closure of finite-state reversible CTMC outputs. Truncating labels to n<=M and renormalizing gives a finite 2M-state model. The omitted mixture mass is sum_{n>M}w_n=2^{-M}; coupling the full and truncated mixtures shows path-law total variation at most 2^{-M}, uniformly on every time horizon. Thus the example proves that CP/common factors do not force an exact finite realization; it does not refute sufficiency for the closure of finite realizations.

4. A CONVERGENT CLOSURE HIERARCHY

This hierarchy is separate from exact FRS. It gives an operational characterization of the closure of finite-state reversible CTMC observations in the rational-cylinder topology; it is not claimed to decide exact finite realization in finite time.

Enumerate the rational-time cylinder events as E_1,E_2,..., with every finite cylinder eventually included. For m, let Gamma_m={E_1,...,E_m}. A capped model class M_{N,R,delta} has at most N hidden states, all active stationary masses pi_i>=delta, maximum exit rate at most R, reversible generator, and a deterministic map g into A. Parameterize it by pi and symmetric conductances c_ij=pi_i Q_ij=pi_j Q_ji>=0, with sum_{j!=i} c_ij/pi_i<=R. For fixed N,R,delta and a fixed output map, this is a compact parameter set; there are only finitely many output maps. Cylinder probabilities are continuous functions of the parameters, computed from exp(tQ).

Define

 d_{m;N,R,delta}(P) = min_{M in M_{N,R,delta}}
                  sum_{k=1}^m 2^{-k}|P(E_k)-M(E_k)|,

and d_m(P)=inf_{N,R,delta} d_{m;N,R,delta}(P). For fixed caps this is a compact finite-dimensional global optimization. An increasing exhaustion N_l,R_l -> infinity and delta_l -> 0 gives d_{m;l} decreasing to d_m. With computable input cylinder probabilities, validated interval methods can approximate each capped minimum to any requested error; no polynomial-time claim is made. The parameter count is O(N^2), plus finitely many output labels; each objective evaluation uses finite matrix exponentials.

Closure equivalence:

 P is in the cylinder-topology closure of finite-state reversible CTMC outputs
       iff d_m(P)=0 for every m.

If P is a limit of such outputs, every finite cylinder list has infimum distance zero. Conversely, if d_m=0 for all m, select for each m one finite model M_m whose error on Gamma_m is at most 1/m. For each fixed k, its E_k probability converges to P(E_k); hence M_m converges to P in the projective cylinder topology. This is a real convergence guarantee, but it has no finite stopping rule: strict positivity of d_m can be hard to certify when N, R, and 1/delta are unbounded.

For exact finite realization, Theorem 2 gives the stronger common-FRS requirement. A separate compactness corollary is available: if one fixed cap (N,R,delta) works at every finite cylinder level with exact zero residuals, compactness of that parameter set yields a single model matching all cylinders. Dropping the uniform cap invalidates this compactness inference, as the countable mixture demonstrates.

5. RELATION TO PRIMARY REFLECTION-POSITIVITY WORK

Jorgensen and Tian, “Reflection positivity and spectral theory,” arXiv:1705.05262v2 (6 June 2017), Section 7, Theorem 7.1 and Remark 7.2 (HTML lines 1228–1270; https://arxiv.org/html/1705.05262). The theorem identifies Markov property for their stationary covariance process with the covariance semigroup law, while reflection positivity gives a weaker Hankel positivity condition. Their scalar Gaussian examples include reflection-positive covariances beyond the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck exponential, but only the exponential is Markov in that scalar-process setting. This is primary evidence for the general positivity-versus-Markov distinction, not a theorem about classical CP word kernels or hidden finite CTMC realizations. The present factor-gluing lemma and FRS criterion are derived here; priority is unknown.
