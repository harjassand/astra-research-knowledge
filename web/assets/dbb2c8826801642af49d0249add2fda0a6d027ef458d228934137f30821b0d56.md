# Hidden CTMC memory and discrimination: scoped result

## Finding

For a finite hidden sensor with a transient memory mode, later discrimination can be modeled as a sum of decaying exponentials. Delay-only discrimination identifies each mode's decay rate and its occupancy-times-effect amplitude, but cannot separate how often that mode was initialized from how strongly it changes the readout. This is an exact observational equivalence. The result is purely mathematical and makes no claim about biological mechanisms.

The proof is in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md); machine-readable claim status is in [CLAIMS.json](CLAIMS.json).

## Interface and theorem

The latent state is a pure-death CTMC with one absorbing baseline state and \(k\) transient modes. At a delay \(\tau\), one of two abstract test inputs produces a single Bernoulli score. The discrimination curve is the difference between the two success probabilities, with the uninitialized/reset difference measured as a control:

\[
D(\tau)-D_0=\sum_{r=1}^k p_r C_r e^{-\delta_r\tau}.
\]

For known \(k\), pairwise distinct \(\delta_r>0\), and nonzero \(p_rC_r\), \(2k\) equally spaced exact discrimination means recover the unordered \((\delta_r,A_r)\), where \(A_r=p_rC_r\), by a generalized eigenvalue problem on two Hankel matrices followed by a Vandermonde solve. Fewer than \(2k\) scalar discrimination means cannot generically identify those \(2k\) real parameters. Even a continuum of terminal challenge-probability curves leaves initialization occupancies confounded with state-specific emission effects; a labeled occupancy readout or externally known initialization probabilities separates them. The minimum \(2k\) count applies to the scalar discrimination curve, not to every richer output design.

## Observable and cost ledger

- Latent state count: \(k+1\); unknown aggregate curve parameters: \(2k\).
- Exact structural readout: \(2k\) delay points, spaced by \(h\), over a span \((2k-1)h\); plus the reset control \(D_0\). Each difference point uses both test inputs.
- At \(n\) Bernoulli replicates per channel, the discrimination-only acquisition uses \(4kn+2n\) output bits: two inputs at \(2k\) primed delays and two inputs in the reset control.
- Computation: dense \(k\times k\) generalized eigensolve and Vandermonde solve, \(O(k^3)\) arithmetic and \(O(k^2)\) storage. This is an arithmetic count; finite-precision cost depends on Hankel/Vandermonde conditioning.
- Separation of occupancy from effect: a labeled mode channel over independent initialized trials estimates the \(p_r\)'s; each label uses at most \(\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil\) bits. Treat known \(p_r\)'s as supplied calibration information and charge its acquisition separately.
- For \(k=1\), if excess signals at two delays have relative errors at most \(\eta<1\), then
  \[
  |\widehat{\delta}-\delta|\leq
  \frac{\log((1+\eta)/(1-\eta))}{|\tau_2-\tau_1|}.
  \]
  Bernoulli sampling with a nonzero smallest excess signal \(z_{\min}\) needs \(O(\eta^{-2}z_{\min}^{-2}\log(K/\alpha))\) replicates per measured channel to reach that relative error with failure probability at most \(\alpha\); \(K\) is the number of scalar channel means estimated. Since \(z_{\min}\) decays with the latest delay, long waits cost quadratically more samples. For \(k>1\), conditioning also depends on mode separation; no uniform noisy recovery bound is claimed.

The matched controls are the same two test inputs after a reset/no initialization (to estimate \(D_0\)) and the \(k=0\) no-memory model. Comparisons must use the same test inputs and delay schedule.

## Prior-art and novelty boundary

The proof is a model-specific combination of a pure-death CTMC occupancy calculation and classical exponential-sum reconstruction. Prony's method predates this application; no novelty claim is made for Hankel reconstruction. Blackwell and Koopmans studied identifiability from finite-Markov-chain functions; Rydén studied parameter identifiability and minimal order for aggregated continuous-time chains and Markov-modulated Poisson processes; Allman, Matias and Rhodes proved generic identifiability results for richer hidden Markov observation models. This report does not claim priority over any of those results, nor identify the full path law of a hidden CTMC.

The attached theorem's useful boundary is the exact product confounding under a deliberately compressed terminal-probability interface. A full path observation, extra emission channels, unknown model order, recurrent hidden transitions, or noise-robust estimation requires a separate analysis.
