1. EXACT CLONE / CHIRAL PROOF

Let alpha_j=sqrt(t_j), beta_j=sqrt(1-t_j). Use 3n vertices U,V,W and define

  B = [ 2 F diag(beta), sqrt(2) F diag(alpha) ],
  H = [[0_(n x n), B], [B*, 0_(2n x 2n)]].

This is the proposed chiral Hermitian matrix with BOTH U and V vertex scales
sqrt(2), and W scale 1. The U-V entries are 2 beta_j F_ij; U-W entries are
sqrt(2) alpha_j F_ij. Confusing edge scale with vertex scale changes factors.

For a uniformly random A subset [n], retain the fixed-size set

  S_A = U_A union V_(A^c) union W_[n],    |S_A|=2n.

Claim, as a polynomial identity:

  Q(x) := 2^(-n) sum_A det(x I_(2n)-H[S_A])
        = sum_k (-1)^k c_k x^(2n-2k)
        = x^(2n) Z(-1/x^2).                                  (1)

Proof of EVERY factor: a chiral principal-minor characteristic polynomial
has coefficient (-1)^k x^(2n-2k) equal to the sum of squared k x k minors
between the retained U side and retained (V,W) side. This follows either
from the rectangular singular values or the determinant expansion followed
by Cauchy–Binet. There are no odd-degree terms.

Choose U rows I, |I|=k. Choose k columns among V and W. If both V_j and W_j
are chosen, their columns are proportional, so that minor vanishes. This
remains true if one clone is zero. Each surviving selection therefore has
an underlying J subset [n], |J|=k, and a choice R subset J of V columns;
the other columns are W. The minor squared is

  2^(k+|R|) |det F_(I,J)|^2
        product_(j in R) (1-t_j) product_(j in J minus R) t_j.

It occurs only if I subset A and R subset A^c. If I intersect R is nonempty
its occurrence probability is zero; otherwise its probability is exactly
2^(-k-|R|). Thus ALL powers of 2 cancel. Summing R subset J minus I gives

  product_(j in I intersect J) t_j
    product_(j in J minus I) [(1-t_j)+t_j]
  = product_(j in I intersect J) t_j.

This proves (1), including k=0, t=0/1, zero clones, singular F, arbitrary
complex phases, and every determinant ordering sign (which disappears in
the squared modulus). In particular Q is monic of degree 2n.

Stability: P(y)=det(diag(y_U,y_V,y_W)-H) is real stable. Direct proof: if all
Im y_l>0, a nonzero vector in the kernel would have positive imaginary
quadratic form, contradicting Hermiticity of H. Principal minors of H are
real, so P has real coefficients.

For each i, D_i=partial_(U_i)+partial_(V_i) preserves stability, or produces
zero. This does NOT need P to have nonnegative coefficients. Substitute
y_Ui+s and y_Vi+s into a stable polynomial, differentiate in s, then
specialize s=0. Positive imaginary parts are preserved by the substitution;
ordinary differentiation and real specialization preserve stability. These
elementary closure properties are Borcea–Brändén Proposition 2.2. Therefore

  Q(x)=2^(-n) [ product_i D_i P(y) ]_(all y_l=x)

is univariate real stable. The derivative expansion deletes exactly ONE
vertex from each pair (U_i,V_i), retaining the other and every W. Each
intermediate/final polynomial is nonzero: its highest-degree terms sum
positively, with the final coefficient 2^n before the averaging factor.
Hence Q is real rooted. If Z has degree d, Q has 2n-2d roots at zero; its
other roots occur as real pairs +/-s. A zero w of Z is nonzero because
c_0=1, and satisfies w=-1/s^2<0. This proves the theorem.

No limiting argument is required for endpoints. Real rootedness also proves
c_k>0 for every 0<=k<=d even when some t_i=0. With all t_i>0, d=rank(F):
every nonzero k-minor has positive weight. With hard t_i=0, d can be smaller
than rank(F); rank(F) alone must NOT be used as that sector's feasibility test.

2. STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCES AND SHARP SCOPE

If the roots are -lambda_l, lambda_l>0, then

  Z(z)=product_(l=1)^d (1+z/lambda_l).

At any z>0, the pair-number law p_z(k)=c_k z^k/Z(z) is therefore a Poisson
binomial law, with Bernoulli parameters z/(z+lambda_l). These parameters
are a STRUCTURAL representation, not efficiently supplied/acquired roots.
In particular Newton's inequalities give ultra logconcavity of
c_k/binom(d,k), ordinary logconcavity/unimodality of p_z, and
Var_z(K)<=d/4. This does not make the many-body state Gaussian.
It also does not imply multivariate physical-occupation stability: at n=1
the occupation polynomial is 1+w x_up x_down for w=|F_11|^2 t_1>0, which
vanishes at x_up=x_down=i/sqrt(w). The scalar charge theorem has a different
interface from a strongly Rayleigh physical-occupation law.

Repulsive endpoint is sharp uniformly over F: with n=2, F=[[0,1],[1,0]],
and t_1=t_2=T>1, Z=1+2z+T^2 z^2. Its roots are nonreal. For T=2 the
discriminant is -12. The maximal tilted p_z(1)=1/(T+1), which is exponentially
small in the binary input length when T=2^b. Thus the current attractive
grandcanonical counting interface cannot automatically be converted to a
canonical one by this proof.

For t<1/2 the STRUCTURAL theorem remains valid, but the present Nambu
grandcanonical FPRAS interface does not. Real rootedness of one univariate
polynomial does not supply its partition count or the configuration sampler.
Hard projected opposite-spin BCS preparation is not established by this audit.
Same-spin/Pfaffian BCS and arbitrary coherent sums also remain outside.

4. ACQUIRED FUGACITY WITH POLYNOMIAL BIT LENGTH

Use the total input bits L. Let D be the product of all F-entry real/imaginary
denominators, so log_2 D<=L. A nonzero complex minor has |det|^2>=D^(-2k).
The product of all t denominators also has log_2 <=L. Consequently

  every positive c_j >= 2^(-(2n+1)L),
  every c_j <= 2^(2nL + 2n log_2(n+1) + 3n).

The upper bound is deliberately loose (Leibniz, at most binom(n,j)^2
minors, t_i<=1). Thus every positive coefficient ratio is <=2^B, for a
computable B=O(nL+n log n); for example

  B=(4n+2)L+4n ceil(log_2(n+1))+8n

is sufficient. This is an ACQUIRED encoding bound, not a minimum-c_k promise.

Set h=1/(16n^2), q=1+h, and examine the rational grid z_j=q^j with

  |j|<=J=64n^2 [ B+2ceil(log_2(n+1))+6 ].

Its endpoints exceed the needed coefficient-ratio bracket: the lower
endpoint has E K<1/8 and the upper has E K>r-1/8. Indeed, at sufficiently
small z, sum_(j>=1) c_j z^j/c_0<=n 2^B z; at sufficiently large z,
sum_(j<r)c_j z^j/(c_r z^r)<=n 2^B/z. Multiplying these masses by n gives
the mean bounds. q^J>=2^(B+2log_2(n+1)+6) follows from log(1+h)>=h/2.

Each z_j has numerator/denominator bit length O(J log(n+1)), polynomial
in n,L. Do NOT treat an unbounded real chemical potential or exact root
oracle as free. The larger bit length is included in each subsequent
coefficient oracle and FPRAS range bound.

For 0<k<r, mu(z)=E_z K is continuous strictly increasing and

  d mu / d log(z) = Var_z(K) <= n^2/4.

Thus neighboring grid means differ by at most 1/64. Binary search uses
estimated means with absolute error <=1/16. If the estimate is within 1/8
of k, stop; the true mean is within 3/16. Otherwise its sign relative to k
is correct and updates the bracketing grid index. On adjacent bracketing
indices choose either: its true mean is within 1/64. Thus the search returns
a z with |mu(z)-k|<=1/4 without ever evaluating Z or its roots exactly.

Each mean estimate uses independent approximate grandcanonical Born samples
of K in [0,n], with TV tau_s<=1/(128n). The TV-induced mean bias is <=1/128;
Hoeffding sampling with O(n^2 log((1+log J)/delta_tilt)) samples per query
controls the remaining error below 1/16. There are O(log J) queries. Split
counting/sampling failure budgets across the polynomial number of calls.
Endpoints are simpler: k=0 is exactly the vacuum; k=r can use the large
grid endpoint directly, whose mass at r is bounded below by a constant.

The Nambu oracle/FPRAS uses m=2n and range parameter
  B_gc=m+ln(R_f R_g)=poly(n,L,bitlength(z)),
with a uniform bound for all prefixes. All table, determinant and rational
arithmetic costs grow with the acquired z encoding and are charged.

5. CONDITIONING PROBABILITY / BORN AND COHERENT COSTS

Self-contained unimodal near-mean lemma: for a unimodal law p on 0..n,
if |E K-k|<=1/4 then

  p(k) >= a := 3/[4(n+1)^2].                                (2)

If a mode lies left of k, every tail p(j), j>=k, is <=p(k). Therefore
E K<=k-1+(n-k+1) Pr[K>=k]<=k-1+(n-k+1)^2 p(k), and the lower mean bound
proves (2). If a mode lies right of k, every prefix p(j), j<=k, is <=p(k),
and E K>=k+1-(k+1) Pr[K<=k]>=k+1-(k+1)^2 p(k); use the upper mean bound.
If k itself is a mode, p(k)>=1/(n+1). Plateaus satisfy one of these cases.
Hence logconcavity here more than suffices; no Darroch theorem is imported.

Classical conversion: request grandcanonical TV error tau=O(epsilon a),
reject samples unless K=k. Actual acceptance is >=a/2, and conditional
TV error is <=2tau/a. Cap trials at O(a^(-1) log(1/delta_post)); charge
the cap/failure. Independence and conditioning mean the first accepted
sample has the same conditional law. The tilt factor z^k cancels exactly.

Canonical relative counting follows too: c_k=Z(z) p_z(k)/z^k. Approximate
Z(z) with the grandcanonical FPRAS and estimate the event K=k to relative
error O(epsilon), requesting sampler TV O(epsilon a). Since its true mass
is >=a, a Chernoff estimate needs O(a^(-1) epsilon^(-2) log(1/delta)) samples.
Multiplication/division by rational z^k has polynomial encoding length and
charged arithmetic cost. Zero sectors were already decided by exact rank.

Quantum conversion: compile the tilted normalized pure state psi_z to trace
error eta<=epsilon a/8 with compiler failure delta_compile. Compute K
reversibly (polynomial gates/workspace), measure only K=k, and uncompute
the counter/equality ancillas. On a good compiler tape the actual acceptance
is >=a-eta>=a/2, and the accepted state is at trace distance <=2eta/a from
psi_k. Measuring the total number does not reveal I or J. Cap independent
preparation/measurement attempts at O(a^(-1) log(1/delta_post)). Reuse the
same compiled circuit; compile cost is paid once, gate cost every attempt.

Let C_comp(n,L_z,eta,delta_compile) be inherited compiler cost and
G_gc its padded reversible circuit gate cost, including coefficient-oracle
programs, random tapes, history, uncomputation, determinant phase and rotation
synthesis. The added measured-protocol cost is

  C_tilt + C_comp(n,L_z,Theta(epsilon a),delta_compile)
    + O(a^(-1) log(1/delta_post)) [G_gc(n,L_z,Theta(epsilon a))+poly(n)],

where L_z includes O(J log(n+1)) fugacity bits. This is polynomial but
substantial. It pays inverse a=O(n^2), not inverse original c_k/Z(1), not
inverse filter norm. Optional coherent amplification would require U AND U*,
the reversible K projector, and a separately justified robust amplification
implementation; it is unnecessary for the claimed heralded preparation.

The inherited counting portion of one grandcanonical coherent compiler is
O(m^13 B_gc^2 eta^(-2) log^2(2 B_gc m/eta) log(m/(delta_compile eta))),
plus explicit oracle/arithmetic/bit/gate costs. Substitution eta=Theta(epsilon
/(n+1)^2) adds O(n^4) to its inverse-precision factor; measured retries add
O(n^2 log(1/delta_post)) to execution gates. The failure budgets for tilt,
compiler and postselection are all separate and sum to delta. Compiler seed
success need not be efficiently recognizable, just as in the inherited N63
interface. A classical count estimator does not become a coherent oracle
for free: the every-execution cap and reversible explicit programs matter.
