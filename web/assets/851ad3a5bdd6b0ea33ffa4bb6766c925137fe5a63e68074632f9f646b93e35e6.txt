## Result: a sharp entanglement law and a stronger preparation theorem

**I derived a candidate theorem package that closes Astra’s recorded \(1/4\)-versus-\(1/2\) entanglement-entropy gap, strengthens finite-time preparation from positive partial transpose to actual singleton separability, and gives exact three-qubit certificates.**

The strongest conclusion is
\[
\boxed{
E_R^\infty(\rho_N(t))
=
\left(\frac14+o(1)\right)\log N,
\qquad
E_R(\rho_N(t))
=
\left(\frac14+o(1)\right)\log N,
}
\]
at an explicitly bounded preparation time independent of \(N\), under the **unnormalized, uniformly coupled collective generator** specified below.

These are internally derived results with complete written arguments and executed checks—not externally reviewed theorems or a priority-cleared, field-breaking discovery. The previous repository audit explicitly left the leading entropy coefficient unresolved and distinguished its finite-time PPT certificate from a proof of singleton separability.

## 1. The main theorem

Consider \(N\) qubits, initially maximally mixed,
\[
\rho_N(0)=\frac{I}{2^N},
\]
evolving according to
\[
\frac{d\rho_N}{dt}
=
\Gamma\left[
(\nu+1)\mathcal D[J_-](\rho_N)
+
\nu\mathcal D[J_+](\rho_N)
\right],
\]
where
\[
J_a=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_a^{(i)},
\qquad
\mathcal D[L](\rho)
=
L\rho L^\dagger-\frac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}.
\]

Here \(\nu\) is the bath occupation. The model assumes ideal uniform collective coupling, with no additional local noise or drive during preparation. Collective thermal generation of entanglement is not itself new; Li and Xu studied this mechanism in small systems well before the present investigation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505216)

Define
\[
\beta=\log(1+1/\nu),\qquad
c_\nu=(\sqrt{\nu+1}-\sqrt{\nu})^2,
\qquad
\delta_\beta=\frac{4-\cosh\beta}{3}.
\]

For
\[
\nu>\nu_*:=\frac{\sqrt{15}-3}{6},
\]
we have \(\delta_\beta>0\). Set
\[
\boxed{
T_{\rm sep}
=
\frac{
2\beta+\frac12\log(\nu+1)+\log(4/\delta_\beta)
}{
\Gamma c_\nu
}.
}
\]

### Candidate theorem

For **every \(N\ge2\)** and **every \(t\ge T_{\rm sep}\)**, the actual evolved state is separable across every single-qubit-versus-rest cut.

Nevertheless, for every fixed admissible \(\nu\), at any fixed such time,
\[
\inf_{\sigma\in\mathrm{SEP}_N}
\frac12\|\rho_N(t)-\sigma\|_1
\longrightarrow1,
\]
and, using natural logarithms,
\[
\boxed{
\frac14\log N-O_\nu(\log\log N)
\le E_R^\infty(\rho_N(t))
\le E_R(\rho_N(t))
\le\frac14\log N+O_\nu(1).
}
\]

Here \(\mathrm{SEP}_N\) is the set of fully separable states. The regularization keeps the \(N\) laboratories separate while allowing each laboratory to process its own copies jointly.

There is no contradiction between singleton separability and distance from full separability: **different cuts can require different separable decompositions, without there being one decomposition into products of all \(N\) individual states.** Consequently, these are globally entangled states from which the separate laboratories cannot distill pure entanglement.

The advance relative to the recorded bounds is:

| Quantity | Previous recorded guarantee | Result derived here |
|---|---|---|
| Leading relative-entropy coefficient | Between \(1/4\) and \(1/2\) | Exactly \(1/4\) asymptotically |
| Finite-time singleton property | Positive partial transpose | Actual separability |
| Sufficient time, jumps \(J_\pm\) | \(O_\nu(N)/\Gamma\) | \(O_\nu(1)/\Gamma\) |
| Sufficient time, jumps \(J_\pm/\sqrt N\) | \(O_\nu(N^2)/\Gamma\) | \(O_\nu(N)/\Gamma\) |

The previous finite-time bounds and their normalization caveat are recorded in N133. These comparisons concern **sufficient bounds**, not optimal preparation times. 

For example, at \(\nu=1/5\),
\[
\Gamma T_{\rm sep}\approx14.8656276812.
\]
The \(N\)-independence is therefore a precise mathematical statement under one generator normalization—not a claim of constant hardware, energy, or laboratory cost.

## 2. The step that closes the entropy gap

The decisive change was to stop comparing the entangled state only with \(I/2^N\).

Instead, consider
\[
\tau_s=\frac{e^{-sJ_x^2}}{Z_N(s)},
\qquad
Z_N(s)=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-sJ_x^2}.
\]

**This state is fully separable.** It is diagonal in the tensor-product \(\sigma_x\) basis. Its spin configurations have correlated classical probabilities, but every basis state in the mixture is a product state.

Therefore,
\[
E_R(\rho)\le D(\rho\|\tau_s)
=
-S(\rho)+s\langle J_x^2\rangle_\rho+\log Z_N(s).
\]

The partition function is an explicit binomial sum:
\[
\frac{Z_N(s)}{2^N}
=
2^{-N}\sum_{k=0}^N
\binom Nk e^{-s(k-N/2)^2}.
\]
A bound on the largest binomial coefficient, followed by a Gaussian-sum estimate, gives
\[
\frac{Z_N(s)}{2^N}
\le
\frac{1+\sqrt{\pi/s}}{\sqrt{N+1}}.
\]

For the prepared thermal states, the proof establishes
\[
N\log2-S(\rho_N(t))
\le\frac12\log(3N+1),
\qquad
\langle J_x^2\rangle=O_\nu(\sqrt N).
\]

Choosing \(s=N^{-1/2}\) now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_R(\rho_N(t))
&\le
\underbrace{\frac12\log N+O(1)}_{\text{entropy deficit}}
+
\underbrace{O_\nu(1)}_{\text{quadratic-spin term}}
+
\underbrace{\left(-\frac14\log N+O(1)\right)}_{\text{classical reference correction}}\\
&=
\frac14\log N+O_\nu(1).
\end{aligned}
\]

That matches the leading coefficient of the lower bound.

**The old identity comparison counted a classical correlation contribution as though it all had to be quantum entanglement.** The product-basis Gibbs reference removes that contribution.

The general idea of comparing against diagonal states belongs to established coherence and entanglement theory; it is not claimed as a new resource-theory principle. The scoped result here is its application to obtain the sharp law for this family. [APS Journals](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.140401)

### Why the lower bound survives regularization

The lower bound uses a common-axis local test: choose a transverse direction, measure every qubit along it, and test whether their summed outcome lies in a narrow interval.

The target’s transverse fluctuations are \(O(\sqrt N)\), whereas a fully separable alternative cannot concentrate its outcomes sufficiently often over the randomly chosen directions. The resulting test gives the \(1/4\log N\) lower coefficient.

The important regularization point is that the test can be applied sequentially to copies. Conditional on previous outcomes, an alternative separable across the laboratories remains separable across those laboratories—even when each laboratory initially has quantum correlations between its own copies. A classical relative-entropy chain rule then gives the same lower bound per copy.

Restricted-measurement approaches to regularized relative entropy have substantial prior art; the packet reconstructs the specific argument rather than treating regularization as automatic. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2705)

## 3. Why exact separability can be reached without an \(N\)-dependent time

The previous sufficient preparation bound compared a global approximation error with a stationary partial-transpose eigenvalue that becomes exponentially small in \(N\). That forces unnecessarily strong global accuracy.

The replacement is **relative convergence inside symmetry sectors**.

In a spin-\(j\) sector, put \(L=2j\). The population dynamics is a birth–death chain on \(r=0,\ldots,L\), with geometric stationary distribution
\[
\pi_L(r)\propto e^{-\beta r}.
\]
The reconstructed spectral comparison gives
\[
\boxed{
\max_r
\left|
\frac{p_{L,t}(r)}{\pi_L(r)}-1
\right|
\le
\sqrt{\nu+1}\,
e^{-L(c_\nu\Gamma t-\beta)}.
}
\]

The next ingredient is a uniform separable margin after a mathematical product-filter transformation. For a qubit coupled to a rest-spin block, the stationary operator can be written
\[
F_{\beta,k}
=
\delta_\beta f_+ I+S_k,
\]
where \(S_k\) is separable and \(\delta_\beta>0\) does not shrink with the block size.

Finally, the proof supplies an explicit decomposition establishing
\[
\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\frac14
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
I+E
\text{ is separable on }\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^d
\]
for every finite \(d\).

Relative relaxation makes the perturbation small **compared with the separable component in that same block**. It does not need to resolve the smallest eigenvalue of the entire many-body state. Combining these estimates gives \(T_{\rm sep}\).

Separable neighborhoods of the identity are established territory; the useful connection here is their combination with sector-relative relaxation and the particular stationary block geometry. [APS Journals](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevA.66.062311)

The product filter is only a proof coordinate change. **No inverse filter or postselection is required in the proposed dissipative preparation.**

## 4. The result extends to growing bath occupation

The explicit occupation dependence allows a further escalation.

Let
\[
r_N=\frac{1+\nu_N}{\sqrt N},
\qquad
\nu_N>\nu_*,
\qquad
t_N\ge T_{\rm sep}(\nu_N,\Gamma).
\]

Whenever \(r_N\to0\),
\[
D(\rho_N(t_N),\mathrm{SEP}_N)
\ge
1-O\!\left(r_N^{1/3}\log(1/r_N)\right),
\]
and both entanglement quantities satisfy
\[
\boxed{
E_R^\infty,\ E_R
=
\frac12\log\frac{\sqrt N}{1+\nu_N}
+
O\!\left(
\log\log\frac{\sqrt N}{1+\nu_N}
\right).
}
\]
The upper remainder can be bounded by \(O(1)\).

For example, if \(\nu_N=N^\alpha\), with \(0<\alpha<1/2\), the leading coefficient becomes
\[
\left(\frac14-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\log N.
\]

This is not free temperature robustness. At high occupation,
\[
\Gamma T_{\rm sep}=2\nu\log\nu+O(\nu).
\]
Thus the displayed power-law occupation scaling uses sufficient time
\[
\Theta(N^\alpha\log N)/\Gamma
\]
with unnormalized jumps, and another factor of \(N\) with \(J_\pm/\sqrt N\).

This establishes a sufficient growing-occupation regime, not an exact temperature phase boundary.

## 5. Exact three-qubit certificates—and a false shortcut eliminated

I checked the stronger conjecture that singleton PPT might hold at *all* times whenever the stationary state is singleton-separable. **It is false.**

For
\[
N=3,\qquad \nu=\frac3{20},
\]
the same trajectory has the following rigorously distinguished states:

| Time | Certified property |
|---|---|
| \(t=0\) | Fully separable \(I/8\) |
| \(\Gamma t=8/3\) | A singleton partial-transpose Rayleigh quotient is \(<-7/100000\): the state is NPT |
| \(\Gamma t=4\) | Every singleton partial transpose has minimum eigenvalue \(>1/250000\), while a full-separability witness is violated |

At the later time,
\[
\sum_{a=x,y,z}\operatorname{Var}(J_a)-\frac32
<
-\frac1{1000}.
\]
Every fully separable three-qubit state satisfies the opposite inequality. Thus this is an actual finite-time state that is PPT across every cut but is not fully separable.

For the ideal state, the conclusion strengthens to singleton separability: the rest laboratory has locally distinguishable spin-1 and spin-0 blocks, of dimensions \(2\times3\) and \(2\times1\) across the cut. The established \(2\times3\) PPT criterion makes the first block separable; the second is trivial. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9605038)

These are not floating-point sign tests. The scripts use rational uniformization, reconstruct the full \(8\times8\) states, and charge the omitted Poisson tails explicitly. The late-time certificate checks eight positive exact LDL pivots.

The PPT and non-full-separability conclusions at \(\Gamma t=4\) survive an additional **state trace-norm error of \(10^{-6}\)**. This is a small state-error margin—not a demonstrated tolerance to local noise during preparation. Arbitrary perturbations can also destroy the block structure used for the stronger singleton-separability inference.

## 6. A reusable entropy–variance bound

The classical-reference argument also gives a statement that does not require thermal dynamics or permutation symmetry.

For any \(N\)-qubit state, define
\[
\Delta S=N\log2-S(\rho).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
E_R(\rho)
\le
\Delta S-\frac12\log(N+1)
+
\frac12\log\!\left[
2\pi e\left(\operatorname{Var}_\rho(J_x)+\frac1{12}\right)
\right].
}
\]

A second derivation makes its meaning clear. Dephase in the local \(x\) basis, obtaining a fully separable classical spin distribution. Its entropy is bounded by the entropy of its total magnetization plus the logarithm of the largest magnetization-sector degeneracy. A discrete entropy–variance bound controls the first term.

Combining this upper bound with the angular-test lower bound gives an entropy budget for suppressing fluctuations in two collective directions. If
\[
v=\operatorname{Var}(J_x)+\operatorname{Var}(J_y),
\qquad
R=\frac{N}{v+1}\to\infty,
\]
then
\[
\frac12\log R-O(\log\log R)
\le E_R^\infty(\rho)
\le E_R(\rho)
\le\Delta S-\frac12\log R+O(1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\Delta S\ge\log R-O(\log\log R).
}
\]

This provides a transferable way to relate entropy deficit, collective fluctuation suppression, and entanglement. It is **not** a work or heat bound without an additional thermodynamic model, and historical novelty of this general formulation has not been established.

## 7. What was checked, and what the results do not establish

The executed evidence includes 896 exact-rational stationary-margin checks, 896 numerical spectral-gap comparisons, 32 population evolutions at 100-digit precision, and 72 checks of the constructive separable decomposition. The entropy work adds 80 reference-state tests through \(N=128\) and 75 general-state entropy–variance checks. Both three-qubit certificates were replayed successfully, with an additional full-Hilbert-space numerical reconstruction of the transient example.

These checks support the derivations and detect implementation errors; they do not replace all-size proofs or independent review.

The strongest practical limitation is that **large distance from full separability is not the same as a useful distillation rate or sensing advantage**. The two-group extraction probes did not establish an improved growing yield. For homogeneous rotations, the prepared states satisfy
\[
F_Q=O_\nu(\sqrt N),
\]
so they do not outperform the \(F_Q=N\) benchmark available to ideal pure product probes. That comparison does not assign equal preparation costs to those probes and the initially maximally mixed thermal protocol.

Nor does this contradict results showing that fully excited symmetric Dicke input can remain separable during collective decay: that is a different initial-state and sector restriction. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13646)

The broader search examined the KLS information obstruction, stochastic reaction-network drift, and rare-conditioned Gaussian simulation before concentrating on this route. Those other problems were not resolved. No independent subagents or proof assistant were available, and this theorem package does not depend on an unverified theorem from the new OpenAI release.
