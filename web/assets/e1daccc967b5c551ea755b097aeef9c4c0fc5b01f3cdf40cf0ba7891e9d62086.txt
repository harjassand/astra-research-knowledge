FROZEN ROOT DERIVATION V1 — 9 OCTOBER 2026
Status: internally derived candidate; historical novelty and strongest consequences unresolved.
This is a mathematical inference result for stationary all-even CTMC models, not an empirical discovery or universal statement about physical equilibrium with momentum variables.

TARGET
Can a time-reversible observed process force positive dissipation in EVERY compatible hidden continuous-time Markov realization, even when hidden dimension/rates are unbounded? Can the inference extend to a complete observable characterization rather than a single covariance witness?

1. ADDITIVE REVERSIBILIZATION WITH AN ENTROPY BUDGET
Let Q be a finite-state CTMC generator, with stationary probability pi_i>0, and assume reverse edges exist whenever forward edges do. Reducibility is allowed on the support of pi. Set Q*_{ij}=pi_j Q_{ji}/pi_i (i!=j), S=(Q+Q*)/2, with generator diagonals. Stationarity implies Q and Q* have the same escape rate at every state. S is reversible with respect to pi.

Use entropy production rate in natural units
 sigma = sum_{i<j}(a_ij-b_ij) log(a_ij/b_ij),
 a_ij=pi_i Q_ij, b_ij=pi_j Q_ji.
Zero pairs are omitted. If exactly one rate is zero, sigma is infinite and the finite lower bounds below are trivial.

For stationary path laws on [0,T], the CTMC likelihood formula gives
 D(P_Q^T || P_S^T)=T J,
 J=sum_{i<j}[a log(2a/(a+b))+b log(2b/(a+b))].
The initial distributions coincide. Integrated holding-time terms vanish (indeed escape rates coincide pointwise). This identity does not rely on an observed Markov property.

For each positive pair, J_pair <= sigma_pair/4. Proof: put x=(a-b)/(a+b), and use symmetry to take 0<=x<1. Let
 h(x)=(1+x)log(1+x)+(1-x)log(1-x).
Then J_pair=(a+b)h(x)/2 and sigma_pair/4=(a+b)x atanh(x)/2. The difference vanishes at x=0 and has derivative proportional to x/(1-x^2)-atanh(x)>=0, since the derivative of the latter is 2x^2/(1-x^2)^2>=0. Thus
                  D(P_Q^T || P_S^T) <= T sigma/4.        (1)
The coefficient 1/4 is sharp for this comparison in the weak-drive limit, since h(x)/(4x atanh(x)) tends to 1/4. This is NOT an optimality assertion for every downstream observable bound.

Any fixed observation channel on paths preserves the KL bound by data processing. Therefore for the observed law P_obs^T, and any set E_T containing all observed stationary reversible CTMC laws under the same specified observation interface,
        sigma >= (4/T) inf_{R in E_T} D(P_obs^T || R).   (2)
The same statement applies to finite statistics whose equilibrium comparison set is an outer relaxation. No hidden-state-number, minimum stationary-mass, activity, or rate cap occurs in (1)-(2). The infimum is not automatically computable, and an arbitrary time-reversal-invariant comparison set is too large to give the desired witness.

2. NEGATIVE AUTOCORRELATION AS AN OBSERVABLE CERTIFICATE
For a real state observable f with pi(f)=0 and |f|<=1, reversibility gives
 C_S(t)=<f,exp(t S)f>_pi = ||exp(t S/2)f||_pi^2 >=0.
For the corresponding Q autocorrelation C_Q(t), the endpoint product lies in [-1,1]. Pinsker and (1) give
 |C_Q(t)-C_S(t)| <= 2 TV(P_Q^t,P_S^t) <= sqrt(t sigma/2).
Consequently if C_Q(t)=-c<0 then
                        sigma >= 2c^2/t.                (3)
For a bounded noncentered observable, center it using the common one-time marginal and include its actual supremum norm; rescaling f by M changes the denominator to M^4. This bound applies to EVERY finite-state all-even hidden CTMC producing the same observed covariance and observation range.

For binary observation Y in {0,1} with mean p in (0,1), stationarity makes its two-time table
 P=(q, p-q; p-q, 1-2p+q), q=Pr(Y0=1,Yt=1).
Every reversible CTMC completion has q>=p^2, even under deterministic coarse graining, because covariance is nonnegative. The symmetrized comparison shares p. If q<p^2, convexity and differentiation of D(P||R(q')) show its minimum over feasible q'>=p^2 occurs at q'=p^2: the unconstrained unique minimum is q'=q and the derivative is increasing through zero in the convex objective. Thus
                  sigma >= 4 I(Y0;Yt)/t                 (4)
in this negative-correlation regime. Mutual information alone is NOT generally a dissipation lower bound; positive equilibrium correlations invalidate any omission of the regime condition.

3. REFLECTED-WORD COMPLETE POSITIVITY
Choose 0<s1<...<sm and deterministic observation Y=g(X). Take U=(Y_-s1,...,Y_-sm), V=(Y_s1,...,Y_sm), both in the same finite word alphabet. The total observation span is T=2sm. Under the stationary reversible chain S, past and future are conditionally independent given X0, and time reversal makes their conditional word laws identical. Write h_i(u)=Pr_S(V=u|X0=i). Hence
                 H_uv = sum_i pi_i h_i(u) h_i(v).        (5)
This table is a completely positive matrix in the classical matrix-cone sense: H=BB^T with B entrywise nonnegative. This is distinct from complete positivity of a quantum channel. It has total sum one. Its one-time marginals match the observed stationary one-time distribution. Its FULL one-sided word marginal generally does NOT match that of Q; imposing this would invalidate the relaxation.

Let C_m be the compact set of completely positive word tables with total sum one, with optional valid one-time marginal constraints. Since the finite-dimensional CP cone is closed and the tables bounded, compactness holds. If P_uv is the measured reflected-word table for Q, (1) and data processing imply
               sigma >= (4/T) inf_{H in C_m} D(P||H).    (6)
This is a convex information projection, but exact CP optimization can be expensive; no polynomial-time claim is made. Replacing CP by the larger cone of doubly nonnegative matrices gives a computable convex relaxation. A copositive witness W with <W,H>>=0 on the CP cone gives a direct finite certificate if <W,P><0. For W symmetric and max_uv |W_uv|<=1, Pinsker yields
                 sigma >= 2[-<W,P>]_+^2/T.              (7)
The equality <W,P>=E W_{UV} is observed. Formula (7) follows because the expectation gap is bounded by 2TV; its coefficient uses the entire window T. Neither a negative eigenvalue nor a copositive certificate assumes the observed process is Markov.

4. A TIME-REVERSIBLE OBSERVATION WITH STRICTLY POSITIVE REQUIRED EP
The natural-science director supplied an exact completion: a 3-state ring, clockwise rate a>0 and counterclockwise b>0, a!=b, stationary uniform, Y=1_{X=0}. The reversal Q* interchanges a,b. The state permutation k -> -k conjugates Q to Q* and preserves Y, so the full stationary Y path law is time-reversal invariant. (An alternating-renewal argument gives the same conclusion.) Nonetheless
 C_Y(t)=(2/9) exp[-3(a+b)t/2] cos[sqrt(3)(a-b)t/2]
is negative at some positive times. Therefore (3) or (4) supplies a strictly positive dissipation lower bound for EVERY compatible all-even finite CTMC, while observed path-reversal KL is exactly zero. This proves a realizability gap, subject to checking prior art; it does not establish novelty.

5. LIVE OBSTRUCTIONS AND STRONGER OBJECTIVE
The elementary covariance certificate is not itself a historic discovery. The potentially stronger target is a complete characterization or convergent operational hierarchy for the closure of reversible hidden Markov path laws, with quantitative dissipation certificates. Individual CP reflected-word tables need compatible factorizations, transition semigroups and a Markov positive cone; no sufficiency theorem is proved here. Ordinary reflection positivity is weaker than a Markov realization, as prior primary literature explicitly distinguishes. Complete positivity may be a stronger necessary condition but must not be asserted sufficient merely by compactness.

Odd time-reversal variables, inertial dynamics, nonstationarity, or observation channels carrying temporal memory require separate analysis. Equilibrium underdamped oscillations are not counterexamples to the all-even CTMC theorem; they do refute an unqualified universal natural-law interpretation.
