THE INDEPENDENT OBSTRUCTION PROOF
Suppose R is CP and lambda>0 satisfies the asserted identity. Since the
image of S is W, for every Y in W,
B R B(Y)=lambda B(Y).
Let F=R B:M6->M6. It is CP and PPT because B is PPT and R is CP; PPT is
preserved by compatible CP pre/post compositions. Choose any nonzero real
vector y with y^T K y=0. Then P_y=yy^T lies in W, and Z=F(P_y) is PSD.
The identity gives B(Z-lambda P_y)=0. Taking the transpose-symmetric part
of the kernel decomposition yields
Re Z = lambda P_y + alpha K
for a real scalar alpha, because Z is Hermitian and K is real symmetric.

The real part of a Hermitian PSD matrix is a real symmetric PSD matrix:
it is (Z+conjugate Z)/2, the average of two PSD matrices. In the positive
and negative eigenspaces of K there are nonzero real vectors u,v orthogonal
to y; each eigenspace has dimension 3. Testing the displayed equation gives
u^T Re Z u = alpha ||u||^2 >=0,
v^T Re Z v = -alpha ||v||^2 >=0.
Hence alpha=0. For every real w perpendicular to y,
w^* Z w=w^T Re Z w=0. Positivity implies Zw=0. Those real w span the
complex orthogonal complement of y, so range Z is contained in C y.
The equation for Re Z then determines Z=lambda P_y, including its
potential imaginary entries. No assumption that R has real outputs is used.
No claim that output symmetrization of R is CP is used either.

Write a finite Kraus representation F(X)=sum A_r X A_r^*. The rank-one
output F(P_y)=lambda P_y forces every A_r y to lie in C y: each positive
rank-one summand must have range in the range of their sum. Choose real
orthonormal eigenbases p_1,p_2,p_3 for K=+1 and q_1,q_2,q_3 for K=-1.
Every p_i+q_j and p_i-q_j is isotropic, so every Kraus matrix A preserves
each of those 18 lines. Adding/subtracting their images shows
A p_i and A q_j lie in span_C(p_i,q_j) for each i,j.
For a fixed i, intersecting these spaces over two distinct j gives
A p_i in C p_i. Likewise, for a fixed j, intersection over two distinct i
gives A q_j in C q_j. Thus A is diagonal in the combined eigenbasis.
Preservation of each p_i+q_j then equates the diagonal eigenvalues at p_i
and q_j, for all i,j. Each Kraus matrix A_r is therefore a_r I6.
Consequently F=(sum |a_r|^2) id6=lambda id6.

This is impossible for a PPT map with lambda>0. The partial transpose of
the Choi matrix of lambda id6 is lambda times the swap operator, which has
negative eigenvalues on antisymmetric tensors. This contradiction proves
the claim. The argument depends only on the exact image/kernel geometry,
CP/PPT closure, and the signature of K; the source square non-EB theorem
is not a premise.
