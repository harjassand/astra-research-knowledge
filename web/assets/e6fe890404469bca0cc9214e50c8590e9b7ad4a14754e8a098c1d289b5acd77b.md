# Continuous-time Markov sensor discrimination: result and limits

Date: 2026-10-08

For a finite-state CTMC observed over time $T$, the full state path has an exact KL information budget. Any hidden readout, sampled path, classifier feature, or posterior-state decoder can only reduce that budget. Consequently, every equal-prior detector based on the same record has error at least one quarter of the exponential of minus the full-path KL. This gives a necessary observation-time bound, not a promise that a hidden sensor can attain the full-state rate.

For generators $Q_0,Q_1$ with common edge support and initial laws $\mu_0,\mu_1$, the full-path divergence is
\[
D_T=D(\mu_0\Vert\mu_1)+\int_0^T\sum_i\Pr_0(X_t=i)
\sum_{j\ne i}\left[q^0_{ij}\log(q^0_{ij}/q^1_{ij})-q^0_{ij}+q^1_{ij}\right]dt.
\]
For any common readout channel $Y$,
\[
D(P_0^Y\Vert P_1^Y)\le D_T,\qquad P_e(Y)\ge \tfrac14 e^{-D_T}.
\]
From stationarity, the KL grows exactly as the initial divergence plus $T$ times the relative-entropy rate. If all exit rates are at most $\Lambda$ and shared edge-rate log ratios are at most $L$, that rate is at most $\Lambda(1+L)$ independent of state count. Under a per-edge cap $r$, the corresponding worst-case ceiling is $(N-1)r(1+L)$. State count alone gives no finite information bound without rate and support restrictions.

The analysis also gives a local Fisher-information contraction for parameter estimation and an exact Chernoff path-affinity formula. These results compare methods on identical observations: the likelihood-ratio test is the optimal simple-hypothesis decision rule; feature compression and hidden-state smoothing cannot improve its statistical risk. More computation can improve implementation of that test, but extra training, calibration, or model-selection records add information and must be counted separately.

Two exact counterexamples set the identifiability boundary. First, a four-state CTMC can be strongly lumped into two visible states with rates $\alpha,\beta$, while arbitrary within-block rates $u,v$ leave the entire visible path law unchanged. Those hidden rates are unrecoverable even with infinite observation time. Second, two distinct positive three-state ring generators can have the same one-step matrix $e^Q$. Fixed-interval samples therefore fail to identify the continuous-time generator, even when all sampled states are visible. Recording jump times or a second incommensurate sampling lag resolves this specific alias.

Primary prior art supports the mechanisms: Chazottes, Giardinà, and Redig develop path-space relative entropy for finite-state continuous-time Markov processes; Koopmans treats likelihood-ratio discrimination rates for Markov processes; Gassiat, Cleynen, and Robin give finite-HMM identifiability under full-rank transition and linearly independent emissions; Metzner et al. discuss the non-injectivity of the matrix exponential in generator estimation. Their positive identifiability theorem does not apply to duplicated deterministic emissions, and sampled transition-matrix identifiability does not by itself identify a CTMC generator.

The focused discriminator is a controlled four-state lumping fixture with fixed $\alpha,\beta$ and varied $u,v$, observed only through the two-block readout. It predicts identical output laws and chance-level expected discrimination for every horizon; full-state logs should distinguish the models. A second three-state ring fixture compares unit-lag sampling, an added incommensurate lag, and continuous jump-time logging. Any successful classifier on the same-law output fixture or violation of the stated finite-time KL error floor would falsify the result or reveal a broken observation/accounting assumption.
