# Sparse history enforcer for a circuit-described common image

**Result.** A polynomial-size isometry circuit \(V\) and one-sparse diagonal
test \(B\) admit a Feynman--Kitaev history Hamiltonian with an exact common
image kernel, exact terminal-test compression, constant row degree, and a
constant *unnormalized* promise gap. The coefficients and norm are polynomial,
so normalizing to a contraction leaves only an inverse-polynomial gap. This
is the standard sparse-QMA history simulation with a useful exact-kernel
interface; it is not constant-gap sparse PCP amplification.

## Theorem and assumptions

Let
\[
 V|\psi\rangle=U_L\cdots U_1(|0^a\rangle|\psi\rangle)
\]
where \(a\) work qubits are initialized to zero and each circuit gate acts on
at most \(g=O(1)\) qubits. Let \(B\) be a positive diagonal contraction,
one-sparse in the computational basis, on the output work register, with a
polynomial-time entry/value oracle. Suppose
a positive logical Hamiltonian \(H\) obeys
\[
 \alpha H\preceq V^*BV\preceq\beta H,
 \qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(H)\le e\ \text{(YES)},\quad
 \lambda_{\min}(H)\ge c\ \text{(NO)}.
\]
Assume \(\alpha,\beta,c>0\) are constants and \(\beta e<\min(\alpha c,1)/2\).
The complement-LCU application has \(H\preceq V^*BV\preceq2H\).

There is an explicit positive Hermitian matrix \(K_{\rm hist}\) on
\(N_{\rm work}+\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil\) qubits such that:

1. \(\ker C_{\rm hist}=\operatorname{ran}(V_{\rm hist})\) for a specified
   history isometry \(V_{\rm hist}\), and
   \(C_{\rm hist}\succeq\frac12(I-V_{\rm hist}V_{\rm hist}^*)\).
2. \(V_{\rm hist}^*B_{\rm end}V_{\rm hist}=V^*BV\) exactly.
3. \(K_{\rm hist}\) has a uniform polynomial-time complete row-list oracle,
   row degree \(O(2^g)\), polynomially bounded norm, and YES/NO minimum
   energies at most \(\beta e\) and at least \(r/2\), respectively, where
   \(r=\min(\alpha c,1)\).

Thus the raw separation is at least \(r/2-\beta e>0\). After division by
\(\|K_{\rm hist}\|\), the promise gap is inverse-polynomial in \(L,a\).

## Construction and proof

Use a binary clock register with legal states \(|t\rangle\), \(0\le t\le L\),
and \(q=\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil\) clock qubits. Define
\[
 h_t=\frac12\bigl(|t\rangle I-|t+1\rangle U_{t+1}\bigr)
                 \bigl(\langle t|I-\langle t+1|U_{t+1}^*\bigr),
 \qquad H_{\rm prop}=\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}h_t.
\]
Let \(P_{\rm bad}\) be the diagonal projector onto invalid binary clock
strings \(t>L\), and set \(H_0=H_{\rm prop}+P_{\rm bad}\). On the legal-clock
subspace, conjugating by the clock-controlled circuit prefixes maps
\(H_{\rm prop}\) to the path Laplacian with edge weight \(1/2\). Its kernel is
the arbitrary-input history space
\[
 \mathcal K=\left\{\frac1{\sqrt{L+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{L}
       |t\rangle U_t\cdots U_1|\eta\rangle:\ |\eta\rangle\text{ arbitrary}\right\},
\]
where the empty prefix for \(t=0\) is the identity. The first nonzero
eigenvalue is
\[
 \delta_L=1-\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{L+1}\right)
       \ge \frac{2}{(L+1)^2}.
\]
Invalid clock strings have energy one, so
\(H_0\succeq\delta_L(I-P_{\mathcal K})\).

Add the input penalty and choose its scale as follows:
\[
 W_{\rm in}=(L+1)|0\rangle\langle0|_{\rm clk}\otimes
                 \sum_{i=1}^{a}|1\rangle\langle1|_i,\quad
 b=(L+1)a,\quad
 \Lambda=\frac{(1+2b^2)(L+1)^2}{2},\quad
 C_{\rm hist}=\Lambda H_0+W_{\rm in}.
\]
The compression of \(W_{\rm in}\) to \(\mathcal K\), under the history
identification with the initial work space, is exactly the ancilla Hamming
weight. Hence
\[
 \ker C_{\rm hist}=\mathcal S=\operatorname{ran}(V_{\rm hist}),\qquad
 V_{\rm hist}|\psi\rangle=
 \frac1{\sqrt{L+1}}\sum_{t=0}^{L}
 |t\rangle U_t\cdots U_1(|0^a\rangle|\psi\rangle).
\]

For the full-space gap, let \(y\perp\mathcal S\) be unit and decompose
\(y=p+q'\), \(p\in\mathcal K\), \(q'\perp\mathcal K\). Since
\(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal K\), \(p\perp\mathcal S\). The compressed input
penalty gives \(\langle p|W_{\rm in}|p\rangle\ge\|p\|^2\), while
\(\|W_{\rm in}\|\le b\). Therefore
\[
 \langle y|C_{\rm hist}|y\rangle
 \ge \|p\|^2+\Lambda\delta_L\|q'\|^2-2b\|p\|\|q'\|.
\]
Because \(\delta_L\ge2/(L+1)^2\), we have
\(\Lambda\delta_L\ge1+2b^2\).
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-b\\-b&1+2b^2\end{pmatrix}\succeq\tfrac12 I,
\]
so \(C_{\rm hist}\succeq\frac12(I-V_{\rm hist}V_{\rm hist}^*)\).
This holds on every physical state, including illegal-clock components.

Define the terminal test
\[
 B_{\rm end}=(L+1)|L\rangle\langle L|_{\rm clk}\otimes B.
\]
Its compression is exactly \(V_{\rm hist}^*B_{\rm end}V_{\rm hist}=V^*BV\):
the terminal history amplitude has squared weight \(1/(L+1)\), canceled by
the prefactor.

Set \(G=16(L+1)\) and \(K_{\rm hist}=G C_{\rm hist}+B_{\rm end}\). A YES
witness in the logical low-energy space has history energy at most \(\beta e\).
For NO, write any unit vector as \(y=s+z\) with \(s\in\mathcal S\),
\(z\perp\mathcal S\), and \(t=\|z\|\). The enforcer contributes at least
\(8(L+1)t^2\). Also
\[
 \|B_{\rm end}^{1/2}s\|\ge\sqrt r\,\|s\|,\qquad
 \|B_{\rm end}^{1/2}z\|\le\sqrt{L+1}\,t.
\]
If \(t^2\ge r/[16(L+1)]\), the enforcer alone contributes at least \(r/2\).
Otherwise, the reverse triangle inequality and
\(\|s\|=\sqrt{1-t^2}\ge\sqrt{15}/4\) give
\[
 \|B_{\rm end}^{1/2}y\|
 \ge \frac{\sqrt r(\sqrt{15}-1)}4,
\]
whose square exceeds \(r/2\). This proves the stated NO bound.

## Access and cost ledger

* **Qubits:** \(N_{\rm work}+\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil\), polynomial for a
  polynomial-size verifier. If \(L=0\), pad with an identity gate.
* **Locality:** propagation terms act on \(q+g=O(\log L)\) qubits; input
  penalties on \(q+1\); invalid-clock penalties on \(q\); terminal tests on
  \(q+\operatorname{supp}(B)\). This is logarithmic locality.
* **Row degree:** each legal clock row touches at most two adjacent
  propagation edges, each gate has at most \(2^g\) outputs, and other terms
  are diagonal. A conservative bound is \(4\cdot2^g+3\), independent of
  \(L\).
* **Uniform row oracle:** from a clock/work basis string, enumerate the at
  most two adjacent edges, query the corresponding gate-local matrix rows,
  and add the diagonal input, terminal, and invalid-clock terms using the
  entry/value oracle for \(B\). Runtime is polynomial in the circuit
  description and requested precision.
* **Norm:** \(\delta_L^{-1}=O((L+1)^2)\),
  \(\Lambda=O((1+(L+1)^2a^2)(L+1)^2)\), and
  \(\|K_{\rm hist}\|=O((a+1)^2(L+1)^5)\). Coefficients have polynomial
  bit complexity for a fixed finite gate set with efficiently computable
  entries. Normalization to a contraction therefore reduces the raw
  constant gap by at most an inverse polynomial.
* **Precision:** compiling to gate operator error \(\varepsilon_U\) changes
  the compressed test by at most \(2\varepsilon_U\); choose this below a
  fixed fraction of the source promise separation. The relation graph is
  exact for the compiled circuit.

## Consequence boundary

This closes a legal **representation** interface: the circuit-described
common image can be enforced by an explicit sparse-row Hamiltonian while
preserving the test compression. Its normalized gap is inverse-polynomial,
its locality is logarithmic, and its size/norm are polynomial. This is
within the standard Feynman--Kitaev sparse-QMA simulation. It supplies no
constant normalized gap, no constant-locality theorem, and no PCP
amplification.

The direct circuit-measurement route is even simpler. For the complement-LCU
form \(C_0=I-VV^*=UBU^*\), measure \(B\) after applying \(U^*\); combining this
test with \(B\) gives
\[
 K_0=\frac{C_0+B/8}{1+1/8}.
\]
If \(H\preceq V^*BV\preceq2H\), the YES minimum is at most \(2e/9\) and the
NO minimum is at least \(\min(c,1)/18\). This is a polynomial-circuit QMA
verifier, not a classical row-list representation or an additional
complexity-class consequence.

The remaining gate is specific: does the BMVZ/LCU construction admit a
uniform positive full-space image enforcer with polynomial complete
row lists, controlled polynomial norm, and a **constant normalized** gap while
preserving the same diagonal-test compression? The history construction
does not answer it. No such construction or all-enforcer impossibility is
established in this package.
