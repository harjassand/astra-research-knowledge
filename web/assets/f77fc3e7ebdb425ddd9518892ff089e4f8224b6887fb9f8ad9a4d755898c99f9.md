# Sparse Gaussian experiment archives: prior art and adversarial audit

Date: 2026-10-07. Worker: `/root/sparse_gaussian_prior`.

**Status:** primary-source comparison, an explicit counterexample to an unrestricted signal-uniform claim, and a proved deduction for a compact countable needle family. The companion converse, finite-hat upper, and infinite-sequence conditional-bin reports were read here; their mathematical statements and resource interfaces were checked. These remain internal proofs, not external correctness reviews. Historical priority remains unresolved. Absence of an exact match in the bounded search is not evidence of novelty.

## 1. Verdict and claim boundary

The unrestricted statement “small `eps/r` implies `log D = Theta(d log(r/eps))` for all signals `r`” is false. When the signals are sufficiently separated, classification followed by fresh Gaussian sampling uses only `2d+1` states. A concrete joint sequence has `r -> infinity`, `eps -> 0`, and `eps/r -> 0`, yet only `O(log d)` bits suffice; see Section 3.

For the explicitly weak range `0 < r <= 1`, the companion proof gives the dimension-uniform converse

\[
\log D\ge\frac d2\log(r/\varepsilon)-Cd,
\qquad C=\frac54+\frac94\log2.
\tag{1}
\]

The fixed-geometry exponent is already present in supplied internal card N07. The potential improvement is the constants and joint quantifiers: arbitrary growing `d`, vanishing `r`, unrestricted joint quantum or hybrid encoders, and bounded nonnegative decision regret transferred to one common product null law. The entropy, rate-distortion, posterior-orthogonality, proper-score, and accessible-information mechanisms themselves have substantial prior art.

For polynomial ellipsoid axes, the exponent `(A/eps)^(1/alpha)` already occurs in deterministic metric entropy. A theorem giving that exponent for **blind whole-experiment archival simulation with finite total quantum/hybrid dimension** is a different operational statement, but its priority is not cleared. An even sharper consequence uses a compact countable set of coordinate needles: its parameter covering entropy is only logarithmic, while its archive cost is polynomial. This avoids framing the result as merely rediscovering ellipsoid covering entropy.

## 2. Exact interface used in the comparison

The encoder receives one observation `X ~ N(theta,I_d)`. It knows the family, `d`, `r`, and requested `eps`, but is not supplied the unknown mean label. Its retained state belongs to

\[
\mathcal A=\bigoplus_k M_{d_k},\qquad D=\sum_k d_k,
\quad B=\log_2D.
\]

Input-dependent retained classical labels count through the direct-sum blocks. Input-independent public classical randomness is allowed with a dimension cap `D` at each seed; the correctness guarantee may average over that seed. There is no uncharged input-dependent side information or entanglement assistance. The decoder can use an arbitrary POVM and fresh randomness, and may output a continuous random vector. The **same** encoder and decoder must satisfy

\[
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\operatorname{TV}(P_\theta,\mathcal L_\theta(Y))\le\varepsilon,
\qquad \operatorname{TV}=\tfrac12\|P-Q\|_1.
\tag{2}
\]

This is reconstruction of the law of a statistical experiment. It is not squared error estimation of `theta`, identification of its label, pointwise reconstruction of the realized sample, or a visible code supplied the label. For a classical intermediate statistic, its forward deficiency is zero because it is obtained from the source; the decoder requirement bounds its reverse deficiency. Quantum storage extends the admissible intermediate representations.

Only retained storage is charged by `B`. Encoding time, temporary precision/workspace, source acquisition, and an exact continuous sampler implementation are separate resources. In the infinite sequence version, observations and reconstructions live on the product measurable space `R^N`; a standard Gaussian sequence is not an `ell_2` vector. The result should not be presented as a finite-time local implementation that reads infinitely many coordinates.

## 3. Strongest elementary counterexample: separated means

Let `Theta={0,+-r e_i:1<=i<=d}`. Choose the nearest mean

\[
\widehat\theta(X)\in\arg\min_{\eta\in\Theta}\|X-\eta\|_2.
\]

Store its label in `D=2d+1` classical states, and output a fresh `Y ~ N(hat theta,I_d)`. For true mean `theta`, the event that a competitor `eta` is at least as near has probability

\[
\Phi(-\|\eta-\theta\|_2/2)
\le e^{-\|\eta-\theta\|_2^2/8}
\le e^{-r^2/8}.
\]

There are `2d` competitors. Thus, uniformly in the true mean,

\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L_\theta(Y),P_\theta)
\le\Pr_\theta(\widehat\theta\ne\theta)
\le2d e^{-r^2/8}.
\tag{3}
\]

For `d>=2`, take

\[
r_d=\sqrt{24\log d},\qquad \varepsilon_d=d^{-1}.
\]

Equation (3) is at most `2d^-2 <= eps_d`. Consequently `B <= log_2(2d+1)`, whereas `d log(r_d/eps_d)=Theta(d log d)`. The ratio `eps_d/r_d` also tends to zero. Any purported lower bound with a positive absolute coefficient multiplying `d log(r/eps)` in this unrestricted regime is refuted.

The theorem therefore needs a bounded weak-signal range, or a separate large-signal regime with saturation. This counterexample does not refute the fixed-`r`, fixed-`d`, `eps -> 0` result: eventually its required error is below the nonzero classification error.

Two further boundary controls are useful. If the unknown label were supplied, `2d+1` states would reconstruct the family exactly. At the other extreme, a dimension-one decoder that always draws `N(0,I_d)` works whenever

\[
\varepsilon\ge2\Phi(r/2)-1.
\]

Thus both blindness and a genuinely small relative error are essential.

## 4. What the new converse does, and what is inherited

The full argument is in `work/agents/sparse_gaussian_converse/report.md`. The report credits the root agent with the smooth-score proposal and records its independent audit. The load-bearing step is a family of binary priors, one for each coordinate, rather than a prior diluted across all `2d` sparse labels.

For prior `T=+-1` on `theta=T r e_i`, the posterior is `m(x)=tanh(r x_i)` and the mixture density dominates the null by `exp(-r^2/2)`. The convex potential `F_r(u)=sqrt(r^2+u^2)` induces the bounded proper loss

\[
\ell_t(u)=-\frac{r^2+t u}{\sqrt{r^2+u^2}}.
\]

Its nonnegative regret is a squared chord distance after normalizing the two-vector `(r,u)`. With

\[
h_r(x)=\frac{\tanh(rx)}{\sqrt{r^2+\tanh^2(rx)}},
\]

uniform TV error gives, under the same null law for every coordinate,

\[
\mathbb E_0[h_r(X_i)-h_r(Y_i)]^2
\le\frac{4e^{r^2/2}}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}\frac\varepsilon r.
\tag{4}
\]

The transformed scalar Gaussian has an entropy lower bound uniform over `0<r<=1`. Squared-error information lower bounds then add across independent null coordinates. Arbitrary quantum/hybrid memory cannot carry more than `log D` accessible information, even with a continuum of encoded states and averaged public seeds. This yields (1).

The proper-score representation and Bregman regret are standard; the spherical score is not newly invented here. [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) give the convex-potential representation and proper-score framework. The posterior/RD bridge is particularly close to [Zhu and Lafferty's Sobolev-ellipsoid quantized estimation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.07368v3): their equation (2.1) splits Bayes risk into unquantized posterior risk plus posterior reconstruction error, followed by an information bound and product tensorization. Those mechanisms must be credited.

The distinguishing step is that preservation of a family of **bounded decision problems**, hence TV reconstruction, forces accurate encoding of independent continuous null coordinates even though the hypothesis label itself contains only `O(log d)` bits. A direct application of a discrete random access code theorem to that label would not prove this: the arbitrary `d`-bit inputs required by an ordinary RAC are absent.

## 5. Strong consequence: compact countable needles

Let

\[
K_{A,\alpha}=\{0\}\cup\{\pm A j^{-\alpha}e_j:j\ge1\},
\quad A>0,\ \alpha>0.
\]

This is a compact countable subset of `ell_2`. It lies in the ellipsoid

\[
E_{A,\alpha}=\left\{\theta:\sum_{j\ge1}
\theta_j^2/(A^2j^{-2\alpha})\le1\right\}.
\]

Its Euclidean covering number is small. Cover the needles with amplitude greater than `eps` individually, and cover the remaining tail by the ball at zero. Conversely, needles with amplitude greater than `2eps` are pairwise more than `2eps` apart. As `A/eps -> infinity`,

\[
\log N(K_{A,\alpha},\varepsilon)
=\Theta_\alpha(\log(A/\varepsilon)).
\tag{5}
\]

For Gaussian shifts, `TV(P_theta,P_eta)=2Phi(||theta-eta||/2)-1`, so the family of output laws has the same logarithmic covering-entropy order at small TV radius.

However, the anisotropic companion converse states

\[
\log D\ge\frac12\sum_j
\log_+\frac{r_j}{e^{2C}\varepsilon}
\quad\text{for included needles }\pm r_j e_j,
\quad 0<r_j\le1.
\tag{6}
\]

This directly applies to a finite block of the **exact** needle sizes `r_j=A j^-alpha`. To make the bound explicit, choose `Q=exp(2C+2)` and `L=(A/(Q eps))^(1/alpha)`. On indices `ceil(L/2)<=j<=floor(L)`,

\[
Q\varepsilon\le A j^{-\alpha}\le2^\alpha Q\varepsilon.
\]

If `eps<=1/(2^alpha Q)` and `L>=6`, every selected needle is weak and contributes at least one nat to (6). There are at least `L/3` such indices, so

\[
B(K_{A,\alpha},\varepsilon)
\ge c_\alpha(A/\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}.
\tag{7}
\]

No smaller-amplitude needles have been silently added to `K`. The proof uses different amplitudes and the common product-null law. Coordinates outside the selected block are independent null Gaussians under the restricted subexperiment and can be integrated into private encoder randomness.

The complete internal conditional-bin upper in `work/agents/information_statistics/report.md` applies to the containing ellipsoid for arbitrary supplied `A>0`. Containment therefore supplies the matching upper for `K`, giving, in the small-accuracy regime stated above,

\[
B(K_{A,\alpha},\varepsilon)
=\Theta_\alpha((A/\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}).
\tag{8}
\]

Equations (5) and (7) already prove that the logarithm of the number of nearby parameter laws is not an adequate archive-memory measure. This is sharper than using the whole ellipsoid, whose own covering entropy already has the polynomial order.

For white noise `Z_j=theta_j+n^-1/2 G_j` with radius `L`, whitening gives `A=sqrt(n)L`. The internal archive law therefore has order `(sqrt(n)L/eps)^(1/alpha)`. In quantized minimax **estimation**, the familiar information threshold for retaining the usual minimax risk is of order `n^(1/(2alpha+1))` at fixed radius. These are different tasks and scales; `eps` here is an archive TV tolerance, not the noise parameter in the estimation papers.

The general-amplitude upper needs no strong-signal hat theorem. It bins coordinate `j` at width `h_j=eps/a_j` inside `[-T_j,T_j]`, with `T_j=a_j+sqrt(2log(8/h_j^2))`, and has two exterior-ray cells. Decode the null Gaussian conditional on the actual cell. The null is preserved exactly. Conditional exponential-family KL is bounded by squared tilt times conditional variance: central variances are at most `h_j^2/4`, ray variances at most one, and shifted tail probability at most `h_j^2/4`. Thus `KL(P_theta_j||Q_theta_j)<=theta_j^2 h_j^2/4`. Coordinates with `a_j<=eps` are omitted and regenerated as null Gaussians. The ellipsoid constraint bounds the total KL uniformly, with no per-coordinate TV allocation. Finally each active coordinate has at most `C(a_j/eps)^2` labels; the positive-log axis sum is `O_alpha((A/eps)^(1/alpha))`. This validates the order and uniform amplitude dependence. The hat construction supplies the sharper one-half leading coefficient in the weak finite-dimensional law; the bin construction alone does not.
