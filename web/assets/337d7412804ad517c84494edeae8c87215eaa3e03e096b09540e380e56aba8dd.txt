1. ATTACHED EXACT INTERPOLATION PREMISE

In C4, U_n is the CONTINUOUS PIECEWISE-AFFINE interpolation of the
specified FILLED VALUES at EVERY original square-lattice vertex x/n.
Each square of side 1/n has one fixed diagonal, or a member of a fixed
finite collection of shape-regular finite-cell templates. The vertices
of the template and its incidence data are fixed before sampling; its
shape constants do not depend on n, hole diameters, filled values, the
environment, or the comparison realization. Filling chooses nodal
VALUES and does not change the microscopic interpolation-cell geometry.

The local marked limit in C5 is the limit of these same nodal values
and edge differences, with the SAME fixed-cell interpolation rule.
The norm estimate, rooting, and extracted discrete gradient cannot refer
to three different interpolations. One may alternatively replace this
attachment by an explicit uniform L^p bound, p>1, on each volume-rooted
scaled FILLED nearest-edge increment, together with the stated local
compatibility and weak derivative identification. For the actual affine
filling, the bound is derived below, so no new probability input is added.

A generic W^{1,p} function with arbitrary values assigned at individual
vertices does not meet this premise. In dimension two, p<=2 does not
embed W^{1,p} into continuous point evaluations. Changes on finitely
many points do not even alter the Sobolev norm. Such an interpretation
would invalidate the lattice-gradient UI inference; it is excluded.

2. EXPLICIT SQUARE-CELL NORM EQUIVALENCE

Use the diagonal from (0,0) to (1,1) in each reference square. Let a_00,
a_10,a_01,a_11 be its filled values at the four scaled vertices of one
cell K of side 1/n. Put
  g_b=n(a_10-a_00),  g_r=n(a_11-a_10),
  g_t=n(a_11-a_01),  g_l=n(a_01-a_00).
On the lower-right triangle the affine gradient is (g_b,g_r). On the
upper-left triangle it is (g_t,g_l). Each triangle has area 1/(2n^2).
Consequently the following is an EXACT identity:
  integral_K |grad U_n|^p
   =(1/(2n^2))[(|g_b|^2+|g_r|^2)^(p/2)
               +(|g_t|^2+|g_l|^2)^(p/2)].                    (2.1)
Define c_p=2^max(1-p/2,0), c'_p=2^max(p/2-1,0), and
S_p=|g_b|^p+|g_r|^p+|g_t|^p+|g_l|^p. The two-dimensional norm
inequalities give, for every finite p>=1,
  (1/(2c_p)) n^-2 S_p
       <= integral_K |grad U_n|^p
       <= (c'_p/2) n^-2 S_p.                                 (2.2)
No hypothesis on the a_ij and no dependence on their magnitudes occurs.
For the source range 1<p<2, c'_p=1 and 2c_p=2^(2-p/2).

The other fixed diagonal gives the same inequalities with the relevant
edge pairs regrouped. More generally, on each fixed finite-cell template
the nodal edge-increment expression and the gradient L^p norm are
continuous seminorms on a finite-dimensional coefficient space. Their
common nullspace consists of constants because the template is connected
and the interpolant is affine on its fixed connected triangulation.
They are norms on the quotient by constants and hence equivalent.
Take the largest constant over the FIXED FINITE template family. This
is the finite-dimensional norm equivalence required in the review;
shape degeneracy or cells growing with hole size would require new
constants and are not being silently allowed.

For any fixed bounded Lipschitz macroscopic Q inside the tested free
region, choose Q' with closure Q inside Q'. Every lattice edge whose
root x/n is in Q is in one of its unit cells lying in Q' for large n.
Alternatively the deterministic union of those cells lies inside the
O(1/n) thickening Q_{sqrt(2)/n}. Summing (2.2) and accounting for at
most a fixed cell/edge overlap yields
  n^-2 sum_{nearest edges with roots in nQ}
       |n[U_n(j/n)-U_n(i/n)]|^p
    <= C_p integral_{Q_{c/n}} |grad U_n|^p
    <= C_p integral_{Q'} |grad U_n|^p.                         (2.3)
The convention counts either positive coordinate orientation once or
both orientations; C_p absorbs this fixed choice. The constant depends
only on p and the fixed templates, not on holes or data.

Let X_n be uniform on ALL lattice vertices in nQ, independent of the
field/marks, with N_n=|Z^2 cap nQ| and N_n/n^2 bounded below by a
positive constant. For i=1,2, (2.3) and C4 imply
 E |n[U_n((X_n+e_i)/n)-U_n(X_n/n)]|^p
  =N_n^-1 E sum_{x in nQ}|n[U_n((x+e_i)/n)-U_n(x/n)]|^p
  <= (n^2/N_n) C_p E integral_{Q'} |grad U_n|^p
  <= C_{p,Q,Q'} < infinity,                                  (2.4)
uniformly in n. This is a FULL-volume bound, including both square
parities and all filling outcomes permitted by C4. If C4 sets its
filled field to zero on an exceptional event, its nodal values and
affine interpolation must both be set to zero on that same event.
Uniform integrability follows since p>1. Fixed-offset increments are
also controlled by telescoping a fixed finite nearest-edge path and
using |sum_{k=1}^m t_k|^p<=m^(p-1)sum|t_k|^p.

Thus local convergence of the marked filled gradients passes to their
expectations and to the stationary L^p limit. The mean derivative has
the correct normalization: the average gradient of a square cell in
coordinate 1 is (g_b+g_t)/2 and in coordinate 2 is (g_l+g_r)/2. The
two terms in each average are spatial shifts of the corresponding
nearest-edge gradient. Conditioning on the spatial invariant sigma-
field makes their means equal. Hence the conditional cell-gradient mean
is q, with NO extra triangle-area factor. Section 8's tower identity
E[q|Y]=grad m uses exactly this deterministic compatibility plus C4/C5.
It does not assert UI for the ORIGINAL unfilled gradients from their
tightness alone.

3. PHYSICAL STEP DISPLACEMENT IS THE STATIONARY SCALAR FIELD

At step k take any representative v of the relative potential, and put
  u_x=(J_k v)_x-v_x.
Since every free cluster J fixes constants, u is independent of the
spatial additive constant used for v. If the field is root-anchored at
that step, v=w and w_0=0; the same u results. Translation of the rooted
cocycle gives v^{shift}_y=v_{x+y}-v_x and translates J's clusters/word.
Therefore
  u_y(tau_x(E,w))=u_{x+y}(E,w).                                (3.1)
This is the spatially stationary, uncentered PHYSICAL step displacement.
Its moments, Huber coordinate estimates, and cluster conservation in
the frozen proof refer to u. It can be nonzero at the root.

The next ROOT-ANCHORED field is instead
  w_x(k+1)=(J_k w(k))_x-(J_k w(k))_0.
Using w_0(k)=0 gives the exact identity
  w_x(k+1)-w_x(k)=u_x-u_0.                                   (3.2)
This anchored difference is a cocycle and vanishes at the root. It is
not asserted to be a stationary SCALAR field. In particular, one cannot
replace u by u-u_0 in a cluster mass-conservation assertion:
  sum_{x in C}u_x=0,
whereas sum_{x in C}(u_x-u_0)=-|C|u_0 generally need not be zero.
The finite-n stationarity test in Section 9 uses the physical z_n and
its uncentered displacement/source residual, before root anchoring.

For time invariance of the slope only zero affine growth of the anchored
difference is needed. Choose its spatial growth center c_R=-u_0. Then
  [(u_x-u_0)-c_R]=u_x,
and by (3.1), for any delta>0,
 E[|B_R cap Z^2|^-1 #{x:|u_x|>delta R}]
       =P(|u_0|>delta R)->0.                                 (3.3)
Finiteness of u_0 suffices; a moment is not needed for this particular
argument. Markov's inequality gives the bad-fraction convergence in
probability. Thus the anchored update has slope zero and the unique
affine-volume slope of w is unchanged by a time step. Countable
intersection over all integer k makes this time invariance simultaneous.
No false stationarity of the root-normalized increment is used.

The Huber functional F_R is additive-constant invariant, so
  F_R(Jw)=F_R(Jw-(Jw)_0)=F_R(w(k+1)).
Stationarity of the root-anchored law therefore makes F_R(Jw) and F_R(w)
identically distributed as required. The quadratic loss uses the SAME
gauge for input and Jw:
  D_C=||v_C||^2-||J_C v_C||^2.
It is not ||w_C(k)||^2-||w_C(k+1)||^2 with separately anchored outputs.
The latter expression could add gauge terms and is not the loss used
in the theorem. The cluster flux is also gauge invariant; subtracting
a constant from u does not change its centered first moment, but
conservation statements still use the physical uncentered u.

The identical bookkeeping applies to the filled correction. Let
hat v=Fill(v) use an additive-constant-equivariant filling in the COMMON
gauge. Then r_x=v_x-hat v_x is a finite stationary scalar field. If
one separately anchors both potentials to zero at the root, their
correction becomes r_x-r_0 and is a cocycle rather than a stationary
scalar field. The same choice of spatial center and stationary-tail
argument as (3.3) transfers affine growth in either gauge. The frozen
C5 wording 'in a COMMON gauge' means the first convention.
