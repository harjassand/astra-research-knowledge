LEAF 09 — KINETIC FLUCTUATIONS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the stated linear model; SOURCE-REPORTED for cited prior results. No novelty or field-priority claim.

Question and answer
-------------------
For a one-dimensional underdamped mean-field Langevin system, the empirical fluctuation field has the usual N^(1/2) scale on every fixed time interval. The linearized covariance equation is an exact matrix Lyapunov equation for the empirical mean mode. A uniform-in-time O(N^(-1/2)) error bound for every fixed 1-Lipschitz observable follows when the centered kinetic motion and the mean mode are both exponentially stable. At a zero mean-mode eigenvalue the standard finite-time CLT still holds, but the unscaled mean-field error grows to order one on times of order N. With a positive eigenvalue, it reaches order one on a logarithmic time scale. Thus a uniform-in-time hydrodynamic approximation requires a uniform stability margin; entropy or logarithmic Sobolev assumptions are not needed for this exactly solvable model. A matched primary result already proves a uniform-in-time *scalar-test Gaussian approximation* for a nearby smooth, weakly interacting kinetic Langevin class; its assumptions do not automatically cover this model's unbounded mean-force interaction, and it does not assert convergence of the full empirical fluctuation process in a measure-valued path topology.

Model and full quantifiers
--------------------------
Fix N>=1, gamma>0, omega>0, sigma>0, and kappa in R. Let (B^i) be independent standard real Brownian motions, independent of iid initial vectors Z_0^i=(X_0^i,V_0^i) with mean m_0 and covariance Sigma_0. For the fluctuation-field CLT assume E|Z_0^1|^4<infinity; the exact mean covariance and the finite-size bound below need only tr(Sigma_0)<infinity. Set m_N^x=N^(-1) sum_i X_i^N and define

  dX_i^N = V_i^N dt,
  dV_i^N = (-omega^2 X_i^N - gamma V_i^N + kappa m_N^x)dt + sigma dB_i.

The empirical measure is mu_t^N=N^(-1)sum_i delta_(X_i^N,V_i^N). The nonlinear limit mu_t is the law of

  dX=V dt,
  dV=(-omega^2 X-gamma V+kappa m_t^x)dt+sigma dB,
  m_t=E(X_t,V_t).

Write e_2=(0,1)^T, A_0=[[0,1],[-omega^2,-gamma]], a=omega^2-kappa, and A=[[0,1],[-a,-gamma]]. Then m_t=e^(At)m_0. The centered nonlinear particle Z_t-m_t solves the linear kinetic OU equation with matrix A_0 and noise sigma e_2 dB. Since gamma,omega>0, A_0 is Hurwitz and the centered covariance is uniformly bounded in t. The mean matrix A is Hurwitz exactly when a>0.

The hydrodynamic equation, in weak form for phi in C_c^infinity(R^2), is

  d mu_t(phi) = mu_t(L_t phi)dt,
  L_t phi = v partial_x phi + (-omega^2 x-gamma v+kappa m_t^x)partial_v phi
                +(sigma^2/2)partial_vv phi.

In particular, m'_x=m_v and m'_v=-a m_x-gamma m_v. All parameters and the iid initial law are supplied; none is learned from an oracle.

Exact mean mode and fluctuation scale
-------------------------------------
Let M_N=N^(-1)sum_i Z_i^N. Summing the particle equations cancels no terms other than the already displayed empirical mean, and gives exactly

  dM_N = A M_N dt + sigma N^(-1/2)e_2 dW_N,
  W_N=N^(-1/2)sum_i B_i,

where W_N is standard Brownian motion and is independent of M_N(0). Therefore G_N=sqrt(N)(M_N-m) satisfies, for every N,

  dG_N=A G_N dt+sigma e_2 dW_N,
  G_N(0)=N^(-1/2)sum_i(Z_0^i-m_0).

For iid initial data with finite covariance, the ordinary vector CLT and independence from W_N imply G_N converges in C([0,T];R^2), for each fixed T<infinity, to the Gaussian kinetic OU process G with G(0)~N(0,Sigma_0). If the initial vectors are Gaussian, this process law is already exact at every N. Its covariance matrix is

  C(t)=e^(At) Sigma_0 e^(A^T t)
        +sigma^2 integral_0^t e^(As)e_2e_2^T e^(A^T s) ds,

and hence, by differentiating the integral (or by matrix Itô),

  C'(t)=A C(t)+C(t)A^T+sigma^2 e_2e_2^T,  C(0)=Sigma_0.       (1)

Equation (1) is exact for Cov(G_N(t)) as well as for the limiting covariance. This proves the linearized covariance equation for the mean mode without an asymptotic remainder. When a>0, its stationary solution is

  C_infinity = diag(sigma^2/(2 gamma a), sigma^2/(2 gamma)).

The position variance diverges like a^(-1) as the restoring mean-mode stiffness a decreases to zero.

Empirical-field SPDE and covariance identity
--------------------------------------------
For eta_N=sqrt(N)(mu_N-mu), Ito's formula and expansion of the scalar mean interaction give, for every compactly supported smooth phi,

  d eta_N(phi) = eta_N(A_t phi)dt
       + (kappa/sqrt(N)) eta_N(x) eta_N(partial_v phi)dt + dM_N^phi,

  A_t phi = L_t phi + kappa x mu_t(partial_v phi),
  [M_N^phi,M_N^psi]_t
       = sigma^2 integral_0^t mu_s^N(partial_v phi partial_v psi)ds.       (2)

The quadratic term in (2) is the only nonlinear fluctuation remainder. For finite T and a finite family of compact smooth tests, it vanishes in probability after time integration. One direct justification uses the exact coupling below: the independent empirical fluctuation processes and the scaled common shift are tight on [0,T], so the two factors eta_N(x) and eta_N(partial_v phi) are O_P(1), leaving N^(-1/2) times a tight product. The brackets in (2) converge to sigma^2 integral mu_s(partial_v phi partial_v psi)ds.

For completeness, the finite-test path CLT can be obtained without importing a full-field theorem. Couple each particle to an iid nonlinear copy bar Z_i driven by the same B_i and same initial vector. The difference Delta_N=Z_i^N-bar Z_i is the same for every i and solves

  dDelta_N=A Delta_N dt+kappa e_2(bar M_N^x-m_x)dt, Delta_N(0)=0,

where bar M_N is the empirical mean of the iid copies. For a finite family of tests, the processes
U_N^phi(t)=sqrt(N)(N^(-1)sum_i phi(bar Z_i(t))-mu_t(phi))
obey the iid functional CLT in C([0,T]) under E|Z_0|^4<infinity: smooth bounded tests and the coordinate x have fourth increments bounded by C_T|t-s|^2. The same coupling shows sqrt(N)Delta_N is a continuous linear transform of U_N^x. Taylor expansion of phi(z+Delta_N), with bounded second derivatives, gives

  eta_N(t,phi)=U_N^phi(t)+sqrt(N)Delta_N(t) dot mu_t(grad phi)+o_P(1)

uniformly on [0,T]. This identifies the Gaussian cylinder limit eta by (2) and proves convergence for each finite family of tests. This report does not claim a new convergence theorem in a full distribution-valued path topology.

Let Gamma_t(phi,psi)=E[eta_t(phi)eta_t(psi)]. Applying the product rule to the limiting linear equation (2), and taking expectations (the martingale terms have mean zero), proves

  d/dt Gamma_t(phi,psi)
    = Gamma_t(A_t phi,psi)+Gamma_t(phi,A_t psi)
       +sigma^2 mu_t(partial_v phi partial_v psi),                   (3)

with Gamma_0(phi,psi)=mu_0(phi psi)-mu_0(phi)mu_0(psi). For the two coordinate tests x,v, eta(1)=0 and (3) reduces to (1). Thus (1) is also the coordinate restriction of the full linearized covariance equation.

One explicit finite-size error bound
------------------------------------
Assume a>0. Choose constants M_0,M_A>=1 and rho_0,rho_A>0 with

  ||e^(A_0t)|| <= M_0 e^(-rho_0 t),  ||e^(At)|| <= M_A e^(-rho_A t), t>=0.

Such constants exist because both matrices are Hurwitz. Define

  K = M_0^2 (tr(Sigma_0)+sigma^2/(2rho_0)).

Then for every N>=1, every t>=0, and every deterministic f:R^2->R with Lipschitz constant at most one,

  E |mu_t^N(f)-mu_t(f)|
       <= [sqrt(K) (1+|kappa| M_A/rho_A)] / sqrt(N).             (4)

Consequently the supremum over t>=0 and over all such fixed test functions of the expected absolute test error obeys the same bound. This is not an assertion about E[W_1(mu_t^N,mu_t)]: the supremum over tests remains outside the expectation.

Proof. For one nonlinear copy, the centered vector bar Z_t-m_t evolves with A_0, so

  E|bar Z_t-m_t|^2 <= M_0^2 tr(Sigma_0)e^(-2rho_0t)
       +sigma^2 M_0^2 integral_0^t e^(-2rho_0s)ds <= K.

For Y_N=bar M_N-m, independence gives E|Y_N(t)|^2<=K/N. The common shift satisfies

  Delta_N(t)=kappa integral_0^t e^(A(t-s))e_2 Y_N^x(s)ds.

Minkowski's inequality yields sup_t (E|Delta_N(t)|^2)^(1/2)
<= |kappa| M_A sqrt(K)/(rho_A sqrt(N)). For a 1-Lipschitz f,

  |mu_N^N(f)-mu_t(f)|
    <= |Delta_N| + |N^(-1)sum_i f(bar Z_i)-E f(bar Z_1)|.

The expected second term is at most sqrt(Var(f(bar Z_t))/N), and
Var(f(bar Z_t))<=E|bar Z_t-m_t|^2<=K. Combining the two estimates proves (4). Only second moments of the iid initial condition were used.

When is the approximation uniform?
-----------------------------------
1. Fixed horizon: for every fixed T<infinity, the sqrt(N) finite-test Gaussian SPDE limit above holds for any kappa in R. No spectral gap or entropy assumption is needed for this finite-time result in the linear model. Constants in path estimates may depend on T.

2. Uniform time and N^(-1/2) mean-field error: if gamma>0, omega>0 and a=omega^2-kappa>0, both A_0 and A have exponential decay. The fluctuation covariance is uniformly bounded, and (4) is uniform over all t>=0 for each such fixed parameter choice. For a parameter family, uniform constants require uniform semigroup bounds, in particular a stability margin bounded away from zero. As a decreases to zero with gamma fixed, the exact stationary mean-position variance is sigma^2/(2gamma a N), while the coupling estimate (4) can be coarser because it uses 1/rho_A; no sharp near-critical rate for the full test bound is claimed.

3. Uniform-in-time finite-test CLTs are already known in a matched kinetic model. Bernou and Duerinckx (arXiv v4, 2025-02-16; journal publication listed in 2026) prove Theorem 1.4 for the Langevin system (1.1) in their paper, for every N>=1, t>=0, and phi in C_c^infinity(R^(2d)): under iid initial law mu_0 with integral exp(|z|^theta) mu_0(dz)<infinity for some theta>0, smooth bounded even W, quadratic uniformly convex A(x)=a|x|^2/2 (a>0), beta>0, and interaction 0<=kappa<=kappa_0, their scalar second-order Zolotarev distance obeys

  d_2(sqrt(N) mu_t^N(phi-mu_t(phi)), nu_t(phi))
       <= C_0 (N^(-1/2)+exp(-lambda_0 t) N^(-1/3)) ||phi||_(W^(4,infinity))^2.

Here lambda_0,kappa_0>0 depend only on d,beta,W,A,theta, and C_0 depends only on d,beta,W,A,mu_0, as stated in their theorem; d_2 tests scalar laws against twice differentiable g with g'(0)=0 and ||g''||_infinity=1. Thus their finite-test Gaussian approximation is uniform over the unbounded time axis (the displayed rate is at most O(N^(-1/3)) uniformly, and is O(N^(-1/2)) at late times). Their Remark 1.5 gives a sharper bound, hence O(N^(-1/2)) uniformly in time when the normalized variance c_t(phi) of the noise-free linearized fluctuation stays bounded below uniformly in t. This is scalar-at-each-time convergence, not a joint path-space convergence theorem for the measure-valued process and not a supremum over a test class. The paper obtains the required decay from a hypocoercive ergodic estimate for the linearized Vlasov-Fokker-Planck equation (Theorem 2.13); it assumes no entropy-chaotic initial condition or log-Sobolev inequality for Theorem 1.4. The small-coupling, smoothness, confinement, and stretched-exponential-moment assumptions are substantive. This result makes the existence of an all-time finite-test CLT known in a nearby kinetic class; direct coverage of the present model is not claimed because its mean-force kernel is unbounded and does not meet the paper's stated bounded-potential hypothesis. The explicit estimate here is derived independently for that linear model; no priority claim is made.

4. Critical counterexample: set kappa=omega^2, take Z_0^i=(0,0) deterministically, and keep gamma,sigma>0. Then m_t=0, the exact scaled empirical mean satisfies dG_v=-gamma G_vdt+sigma dW_N and dG_x=G_vdt. Direct integration gives

  Var(m_N^x(t)-m_x(t))
   = sigma^2/(gamma^2 N) [ t - 2(1-e^(-gamma t))/gamma
                                    +(1-e^(-2gamma t))/(2gamma) ].       (5)

At t=cN for any c>0, (5) tends to sigma^2 c/gamma^2. Hence no N-uniform-in-time O(N^(-1/2)) hydrodynamic error is possible. The scaled mean fluctuation remains Gaussian and its finite-time CLT is exact; what fails is boundedness/tightness of that fluctuation over unbounded time and the unscaled mean-field approximation at t of order N.

5. Unstable counterexample: set q=kappa-omega^2>0, and r=(-gamma+sqrt(gamma^2+4q))/2>0. With the same deterministic zero initial data, the left eigenvector l=(r+gamma,1) satisfies l^T A=r l^T and l dot e_2=1. Therefore

  Var(l dot (m_N(t)-m_t))=sigma^2(e^(2rt)-1)/(2rN).

At t_N=(log N)/(2r)+s this tends to sigma^2 e^(2rs)/(2r)>0. The mean-field approximation already fails on logarithmic time scales.

Assumption audit
----------------
Entropy: no relative-entropy, log-Sobolev, or entropy-chaos assumption enters (1)-(5). The proof uses exact linear equations, iid sampling, and synchronous coupling. Entropy may be a useful proof method for rough singular kernels, but it is not logically required for this model.

Spectral stability: the centered matrix A_0 must decay to keep single-particle centered second moments uniformly bounded. Here gamma>0 and omega>0 guarantee this. Uniform-in-time control of the interaction-sensitive mean mode requires A Hurwitz, equivalently gamma>0 and omega^2-kappa>0. A zero eigenvalue gives diffusive accumulation; a positive eigenvalue gives exponential amplification. The constants in (4) expose the decay-rate and nonnormal-conditioning costs. This is a drift-semigroup stability condition, not a separately assumed Poincare or logarithmic Sobolev inequality.

Moments: finite covariance of Z_0 is necessary for the covariance formula, the explicit second-moment bound, and the iid finite-test CLT at fixed times. Finite fourth moment is a sufficient condition used here for tightness of finite smooth-test paths in C([0,T]) and the Taylor-remainder argument. Gaussian initial data make the mean-mode CLT exact. No initial density or exponential moment is needed for (1), (4), or (5).

Matched primary results
-----------------------
Fernández Fernández and Méléard, “A Hilbertian approach for fluctuations on the McKean-Vlasov model,” Stochastic Processes and their Applications 71(1), 33–53 (published 30 October 1997), DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4149(97)00067-7. Their source-reported result is a sqrt(N) empirical fluctuation CLT, with convergence to a Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck/Langevin field in weighted negative Sobolev spaces on fixed [0,T]. It matches the scaling and linearized Gaussian-field mechanism here. We do not transfer their full-space theorem to our unbounded linear coefficients; the cylinder CLT above is proved directly.

Hao, Zhang, and Zhao, “Quantitative Propagation of Chaos and Fluctuations for Kinetic McKean–Vlasov SDEs with Singular Interaction Kernels,” arXiv:2607.13379v1, submitted 15 July 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13379. Theorem 4.5 proves a finite-horizon weighted negative anisotropic Besov-space CLT for iid initial data in the fluctuation regime b=0 and time-independent K, assuming K in K_beta intersect B^(beta-2,infinity)_(2;a), |K|^2 in K_0, and mu_0 in B^(2d+1,infinity)_(1;a) intersect B^(2d+1,infinity)_(2;a), with beta in (1,2). Theorem 4.10 gives an N^(-1/2) smooth-test expectation bound uniform over t in [0,T]. Their separate path-space entropy theorem (Theorem 1.5) assumes exchangeable initial data and bounds k-particle path relative entropy by C_t k/N times (initial entropy+1). The entropy-chaotic premise is needed for that entropy conclusion, not a spectral-gap premise for their finite-horizon CLT. Their Assumption 4.1 is not verified for our unbounded linear interaction kernel, so this is a matched method/interface comparison, not an imported proof.

Dawson, “Critical dynamics and fluctuations for a mean-field model of cooperative behavior,” Journal of Statistical Physics 31(1), 29–85 (April 1983), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01010922. The source-reported critical oscillator result has non-Gaussian fluctuations on a slower time scale. It shows why the harmonic zero-mode example should be read narrowly: it refutes uniform hydrodynamic closeness by diffusive growth, but does not claim that nonlinear critical mean-field models retain Gaussian scaling.

Bernou and Duerinckx, “Uniform-in-time estimates on corrections to mean field for interacting Brownian particles,” arXiv:2405.19306v4 (submitted 29 May 2024; v4 dated 16 February 2025), later Probability Theory and Related Fields 194 (journal record, 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19306; DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/S00440-025-01381-W. Theorem 1.4 is an all-t>=0 quantitative scalar-test CLT for kinetic Langevin under iid stretched-exponential initial moments, smooth bounded even interaction W, uniformly convex quadratic confinement A, beta>0, and sufficiently small nonnegative interaction strength. It bounds the second-order Zolotarev distance by C_0(N^(-1/2)+e^(-lambda_0t)N^(-1/3)) times the squared W^(4,infinity) test norm; Remark 1.5 improves this to O(N^(-1/2)) when the non-noisy linearized test variance is uniformly nondegenerate. This is an all-time scalar-test theorem, not a measure-valued path-topology CLT. Its uniform-time mechanism is a hypocoercive ergodic estimate for the linearized Vlasov-Fokker-Planck semigroup combined with Lions expansions and Stein's method. We did not import this theorem into the exact harmonic calculation. Direct applicability to the present model is not claimed: its mean-force kernel is unbounded, unlike the paper's stated W in C_b^infinity.

Cost and comparator
-------------------
The theorem takes N, gamma, omega, kappa, sigma, and the initial law as supplied. The exact computation uses 2x2 matrix exponentials/Lyapunov equations. Evaluating the microscopic drift at one numerical time step needs one global mean reduction and O(N) local arithmetic; no query/oracle or parameter-acquisition cost is charged. All bounds are in exact real arithmetic. In a numerical implementation the conditioning cost is governed by M_A/rho_A and M_0/rho_0; these blow up near a zero mode. No time-discretization or finite-precision error is claimed.

The matched finite-size baseline is the empirical measure of iid nonlinear copies bar Z_i driven by the same initial samples and Brownian motions and by the supplied deterministic mean path m_t. Its fixed-test sampling error is at most sqrt(K/N). The all-to-all interaction contributes the additional shift term at most |kappa|M_A sqrt(K)/(rho_A sqrt(N)). This comparator has the same initial information, diffusion, observables, and N; it is stronger than comparison to a generic independent OU law.

Falsifier and unresolved gates
-----------------------------
Equations (1), (4), and (5) are falsified by any exact solution violating their stated covariance or bound. In particular, the critical and unstable formulas provide closed-form checks against any alleged N-uniform estimate without a stability margin. A useful next falsifier for the stable claim is an exact stable parameter choice and finite-second-moment initial law for which the left side of (4), computed analytically, exceeds its displayed right side.

Open: (i) a sharp uniform-time Berry–Esseen rate for the entire empirical fluctuation field in a specified kinetic distribution topology; (ii) extension of the stable test bound to nonlinear/singular interactions with explicit constants and a uniform spectral gap; (iii) model-dependent critical scaling when a nonlinear term regularizes a neutral mode; (iv) numerical discretization and finite-precision costs. The internal proof establishes no new novelty or priority claim; external mathematical review remains a gate.
