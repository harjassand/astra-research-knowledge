REVISION — PARTITION OUTPUT BIT HEIGHT IS POLYNOMIAL
7 October 2026

This separates exact output size from the unresolved cost of computing a
microscopic partition or hole coefficient.

Let V be an explicitly supplied rational-complex column matrix of dimension
r and let each line activity theta_l=p_l/q_l be positive rational. For any
full basis A of r/2 lines,

  w(A)=|det V_A|^2 product_(l in A) theta_l.

Let D be the product of all input component denominators in V, and
B=product_l q_l. If the full input has ell bits, log D, log B are O(ell).
Every full-basis determinant has denominator dividing D^r and bit height
O(r(ell+log r)) by Hadamard. Every selected activity product has denominator
dividing B. Therefore all basis weights have a common denominator dividing
D^(2r)B, whose bit length is O(r*ell+ell). There are at most
binom(M,r/2)<=2^M bases, so summing their nonnegative numerators adds at most
M bits plus the individual numerator bound. When the explicit line list has
polynomial size M, the exact partition Z has polynomial output bit length.
Any positive quotient of two such partition values also has polynomial bit
length.

The same conclusion holds for f(U)=sum_(I,J disjoint, |I|=|J|=k)
|det F[I,J]|^2. All k-minors share denominator D^k and squared norms share
D^(2k); the number of terms is at most 4^n, adding O(n) bits. Positive
rational activities can again be cleared by the product of their input
denominators. Thus no exponential-length exact answer is hidden in f(U).

This is only a representation-size bound. Evaluating Z or f(U) by summing
all states may still require exponentially many terms; the deterministic
linear-parity support/witness algorithm returns no positive count. Likewise,
the pointwise MH cancellation removes Z from an acceptance ratio, but it
cannot replace a proposal that asks to sample a completion-conditioned law.
The actual unresolved issue is computational acquisition/counting or a
sampler/energy theorem, not an inherently exponentially long rational output.
