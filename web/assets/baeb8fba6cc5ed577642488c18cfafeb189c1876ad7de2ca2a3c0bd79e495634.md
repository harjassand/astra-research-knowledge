# Finite-sample design: response invariance versus a target-context reversal

## Decision target and limits

This protocol tests one predeclared perturbation, one bounded functional endpoint, and a finite panel of mechanistic readouts across contexts `S` (source), `T` (recipient of interest), and `H` (a context held out of model fitting, tuning, endpoint choice, and margin choice). It does **not** test universal mechanism invariance. Here “measured response invariance” means that randomized assignment effects on every named outcome are equivalent within fixed margins across shared pre-treatment-state strata. A result cannot establish equality of unmeasured molecular pathways.

The statistical output is one of: `MEASURED_RESPONSE_EQUIVALENCE`, `TARGET_SPECIFIC_REVERSAL`, `TRANSFER_FAILURE_NOT_TARGET_SPECIFIC`, `OTHER_HETEROGENEITY`, or `INCONCLUSIVE`. Finite-sample randomization cannot classify every finite sample without error; this design gives familywise nonasymptotic error control under its stated block assumptions. It is not an exact permutation test of a weak average-effect null. Exact randomization p-values are available for sharp unit-level nulls, but those nulls are stronger than equality of average causal effects.

## Causal estimand and experimental interface

Let `c ∈ {S,T,H}` index context, `k=1,…,K` index pre-treatment state strata, `j=1,…,M` index predeclared outcomes, and `g=1,…,G` index independent guide versions for a genetic perturbation. Treatment is randomized within each independent biological block to target-guide or matched non-targeting-guide wells. The unit is an independent donor/clone block, not a cell. Multiple wells and cells within a donor are aggregated before inference; if several clones share a donor, they are not counted as independent donors unless their dependence is modeled.

For each context, state stratum, outcome, and randomized arm `a`, define the block-level arm mean `X_{cikja}` on matched wells, where `a=0` is the matched NTC/vehicle arm and `a=g` is one of the `G` independently randomized target-guide arms. Define the block-level randomized contrast

`D_{cikjg} = X_{cikjg} − X_{cikj0}`.

Normalize each endpoint before data collection so `X∈[0,1]`; hence `D∈[-1,1]`. Under valid within-block randomization, consistency, no cross-block interference, and independent blocks, `E[X_{cikja}]=m_{ckja}` is the assignment-arm outcome mean and `E[D_{cikjg}]=μ_{ckjg}=m_{kjg}-m_{kj0}` is the assignment-effect estimand. For a compound, replace guide assignment by compound versus matched vehicle. Do not condition the primary causal effect on realized knockdown or another post-treatment variable.

Measure baseline state `Z` in a pre-treatment aliquot or with a non-destructive baseline assay. Choose common-support weights `w_k≥0`, `Σ_k w_k=1`, before opening outcomes, and define

`τ_{cjg}=Σ_k w_k μ_{ckjg}` and the per-arm response surface `M_{cja}=Σ_k w_k m_{ckja}`.

These are common-state standardized assignment effects and arm means. If the target has state mass outside source support, report that mass and do not extrapolate it with this design. Never define `k` using a post-treatment cell state; that can select on a mediator or collider. Testing arm-specific means, rather than only treatment-control contrasts, matches the classical transport condition on both potential-outcome arms.

The measured response vector is `(M_{cja}, τ_{cjg}, μ_{ckjg})` over the finite `j,k,g,a` panel. This vector is a practical operationalization of response-mechanism signatures, not a complete structural causal mechanism. A simple observational equivalence demonstrates the limit: with an unmeasured mediator `M`, source equations `M:=A, Y:=M` and target equations `M:=0, Y:=A` both produce observed `Y=A` under randomized `A` if `M` is omitted, yet their internal pathways differ. For any finite panel, an additional unmeasured node can remain. Universal structural-mechanism equality therefore requires extra graph/measurement/intervention assumptions.

## Matched exposure, state, and controls

1. Lock the perturbation identity, vector/guide lot, delivery reagent, nominal dose or MOI, induction schedule, exposure duration, washout, harvest time, medium, matrix, density, passage range, temperature, oxygenation, and assay protocol across contexts. Match the negative control to the same delivery/vehicle and guide backbone. Add a mock-delivery control and an assay-positive control in every context and batch. Across species, validate ortholog targeting and the guide/construct's functional equivalence; sharing a gene symbol does not establish a common intervention version.
2. Before the confirmatory experiment, use a separate pilot to choose a common exposure regimen and verify that target engagement is within a predeclared tolerance across contexts. Measure engagement directly (for example, target protein or allele/editing efficiency) in parallel aliquots. The confirmatory comparison remains intention-to-treat by randomized assignment; do not discard low-engagement cells or adjust for post-treatment engagement. If context-specific doses are needed to obtain matched engagement, call the tested intervention an **engagement-matched regimen**, report each dose, and limit inference to that regimen. It does not estimate a same-dose effect.
3. Randomize wells within donor/clone × context × differentiation-batch × baseline-state blocks; randomize plate position and blind outcome processing. Keep target-guide identities separate for at least two non-overlapping guides, with NTC controls, to expose guide-specific results. Require the two guides to show compatible target engagement and response direction before labelling the effect a gene-level response.
4. Freeze the source model, prediction, outcome panel, standardization weights, margins, exclusion rules, and analysis before seeing `T` or `H` outcomes. `H` is a context-level holdout: it is never used for fit, hyperparameter choice, feature selection, endpoint selection, margin tuning, or exposure-pilot selection. The same randomized intervention and assay are nevertheless run in `H` so it can serve as a genuine held-out response control.
5. Include enough independent donors/clones to meet the formula below. Thousands of cells from one donor do not increase `n`. For cross-species or native-tissue comparisons, donor pairing may be impossible; use independent context-specific blocks and state that the intervals do not exploit pairing.

## Simultaneous finite-sample intervals

Suppose there are `n` independent blocks per context/state stratum, with equal `n` for the simple formula below. For each treatment-effect mean `μ_{ckjg}`, Hoeffding gives

`P(|mean(D)−μ_{ckjg}| ≥ r) ≤ 2 exp(−n r²/2)`

because `D∈[-1,1]`, whose range length is 2. Let `C=3` and `L=C K M G` mean-level quantities. A union bound gives simultaneous coverage at least `1−α_s` using

`r_n = sqrt((2/n) log(2 L / α_s))`.

The standardized `τ_{cjg}` interval has the same radius `r_n` because the `w_k` are nonnegative and sum to one. Its centre is `Σ_k w_k mean(D_{ckjg})`.

To test the transport-relevant arm-specific response surfaces, form `V_{cikja}=X_{cikja}−X_{Sikja}` for `c∈{T,H}` and each arm `a∈{0,1,…,G}`. If all contexts use the same independent donor blocks, this is a paired contrast; if they do not, pair independent sampled blocks by a fixed index only for calculation. In either case, independent `V` values lie in `[-1,1]`, so

`P(|mean(V)−(m_{ckja}−m_{Skja})| ≥ t) ≤ 2 exp(−n t²/2)`.

With `Q_a=(C−1) K M (G+1)` arm/state/outcome/context comparisons, all conditional arm-mean differences are simultaneously covered at level `1−α_e` by

`t_n = sqrt((2/n) log(2 Q_a / α_e))`.

Standardized arm-mean differences `M_{cja}−M_{Sja}` use the same `t_n` radius. If independent blocks are not equal-sized, use the per-stratum/per-context radii `r_{ckjg}=sqrt((2/n_{ckjg}) log(2L/α_s))` and `t_{ckja}=sqrt((2/n_{ckja}) log(2Q_a/α_e))`, then sum their weighted radii. The arm-mean intervals directly test equality of both assigned-arm outcome means; a separate effect-difference interval can be formed by subtracting treatment and control intervals, at the cost of the corresponding summed radii. These bounds require independent block-level randomization and a bounded, predeclared endpoint. They do not treat cells as independent observations.

For an overall familywise level `α`, allocate `α_s+α_e≤α` (for example, `α_s=α_e=α/2`) so sign and equivalence declarations made together retain coverage at least `1−α`.

## Predeclared decision rule

Choose an arm-mean equivalence margin `δ_j>0` for each endpoint and a reversal margin `γ_j>δ_j` before randomization. For an endpoint/guide, let `CI_{cjg}=[τ̂_{cjg}−r_n, τ̂_{cjg}+r_n]`. For arm `a`, let `CIm_{cja}=[M̂_{cja}−t_n, M̂_{cja}+t_n]` and `CIΔm_{cja}=[Δ̂m_{cja}−t_n, Δ̂m_{cja}+t_n]` for `Δm_{cja}=M_{cja}−M_{Sja}`.

- **Measured response equivalence:** declare `MEASURED_RESPONSE_EQUIVALENCE` only if, for every predeclared outcome, randomized arm, state stratum, and context `c∈{T,H}`, each arm-specific response interval is wholly inside its margin: `|m̂_{ckja}−m̂_{Skja}|+t_n < δ_j`. This compares both target-guide and matched-control outcomes conditional on pre-treatment state, not only their contrast. Also require target engagement, delivery, assay-positive-control, and common-support checks to pass. The conclusion is still restricted to the measured panel and margins.
- **Target-specific reversal:** declare `TARGET_SPECIFIC_REVERSAL` only if for the primary endpoint both independent guides show a source interval wholly above `+γ` and a `T` interval wholly below `−γ` (or the opposite signs), while all `H−S` arm-mean intervals lie wholly within `[-δ,+δ]` for the same endpoint, guide arms, NTC arm, and state strata. This supports a reversal for the randomized regimen, standardized state distribution, endpoint, and named target context; it does not identify its molecular cause.
- **Transfer failure, not target-specific:** if `T` reverses relative to `S` but the held-out `H` also leaves the source-equivalence band or reverses, the source prediction failed in more than the target context. Do not label this target-specific.
- **Other heterogeneity / inconclusive:** any other pattern, failed exposure or assay checks, lack of state support, or interval crossing a decision margin returns `OTHER_HETEROGENEITY` or `INCONCLUSIVE`. Do not force a binary result.

The source model's predicted effects for `T` and `H` are scored only after this contract is locked. Report the equal-information controls: no-change, pooled source effect, and a simple additive/context-mean model. They do not replace the randomized target comparison.

## Sample sizes and randomization cost

The formulas are conservative distribution-free precision bounds. They bound interval coverage, not biological variability inferred from a pilot. For a desired sign declaration under true effects `τ_S=+a`, `τ_T=−a` and reversal margin `γ`, it is sufficient on the simultaneous coverage event that `r_n < (a−γ)/2`. For true zero source-context differences in both randomized arms and arm-mean equivalence margin `δ`, it is sufficient that `t_n<δ/2` for each interval to fall inside its margin on that event. These conditions turn the coverage guarantee into a conservative probability of making the intended declaration.

Illustration: `C=3`, one state stratum `K=1`, one functional endpoint `M=1`, two guides `G=2`, familywise `α=0.05`, and an equal split `α_s=α_e=0.025`. Then

`r_n = sqrt(log(480)·2/n) ≈ sqrt(12.348/n)`,

`t_n = sqrt(log(480)·2/n) ≈ sqrt(12.348/n)`.

If the design is intended to detect true effects `+0.60` in source and `−0.60` in target beyond a reversal margin `γ=0.30`, then `a−γ=0.30`; `r_n<0.15` is met at `n=549` independent donor/clone blocks per context. If all source-target and source-heldout **arm-specific means** are equal and the equivalence margin is `δ=0.30`, `t_n<0.15` also requires `n=549` blocks per context in this illustration. These are conservative sufficient counts; a variance-based design could be smaller only by supplying and validating additional distributional assumptions, which should not be silently substituted for these bounds.

With two guide arms and one NTC arm, either illustrative design requires at least `3 × C × n` randomized donor-context-arm units: 4,941 arm-units at `n=549`. These counts do not include mock-delivery wells, positive controls, baseline-state aliquots, target-engagement assays, multiome/sequencing reads, assay calibration, pilot cultures, failed blocks, or technical replicate wells. If one guide is used, the guide-specificity check and multiplicity factor change; it is not a free equivalent design. Target randomization in `T` is essential: without it, reversal versus invariance remains unidentified under unrestricted target-mechanism shift.

## Falsifiers and interpretation

The invariance contract is refuted for this response panel if any randomized `T−S` or `H−S` interval excludes its prespecified equivalence band while the delivery, target engagement, assay, randomization, and common-support checks pass. A target-specific reversal is refuted if the recipient intervals fail the opposite-sign margin or if the held-out context also reverses. Failed engagement means the nominal perturbation was not matched; it is not evidence for biological mechanism shift. A good held-out-context result supports prediction only for this finite set of contexts, not transfer to all tissues, species, or intact organisms.
