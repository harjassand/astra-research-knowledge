EXACT THEOREM AND LOG/ERROR CONVENTIONS
Fix any density kappa_BC on B tensor C. For each such kappa there exists
ONE normal EB CPTP F:B->B tensor C, chosen only from kappa, such that for
EVERY separable quantum A and EVERY density tau_ABC with tau_BC=kappa and
finite I=I(A:B)_tau, the state

 omega_ABC=(id_A tensor F)(tau_AB)

has omega_A=tau_A and omega_BC=kappa EXACTLY. Put
 delta_B=I_e(A:C|B)_tau, delta_C=I_e(A:B|C)_tau,
 r_B=sqrt(1-exp(-delta_B)), r_C=sqrt(1-exp(-delta_C)).
CMI is its usual lower semicontinuous extension in infinite dimensions.
All information quantities are in NATS; T=||rho-sigma||_1/2 is HALF trace.
For finite deficits, let s=r_B+r_C and define
 gbar(I,0)=0,
 gbar(I,x)=min{1,6 sqrt[(I+1)/ln(1/x)]}, 0<x<1,
 gbar(I,x)=1, x>=1.
Then the SAME map satisfies

 T(omega,tau)<=min{1,r_B+gbar(I,s)}.                    (T)

When s>=1 the right side is simply one. When both deficits vanish it is
zero: the same map reconstructs tau exactly. Allowing an infinite deficit
with r_j=1 extends (T) only by the trivial cap one. No equality of B,C
dimensions or interchange symmetry of kappa is assumed. EB here means
input-B versus any reference: omega is separable across A:(BC), not
necessarily across B:C. Kappa_BC may itself be entangled.

SUPPLIED LOCAL NORMAL-CHANNEL PREMISE
For every actual normal CPTP broadcaster D:B->B1 tensor B2 and fixed
sigma_B, ONE normal sigma-preserving EB Lambda_B, depending only on
D,sigma_B, works for every separable A and every rho_AB with rho_B=sigma_B:
 T((id_A tensor Lambda_B)rho,rho)<=g(I(A:B),b),
 b=max_j T((id_A tensor D_j)rho,rho),

1. PRECISE PRIMARY UNIVERSAL RECOVERY IMPORT
Read directly: Junge--Renner--Sutter--Wilde--Winter,
Universal recovery maps and approximate sufficiency of quantum relative
entropy, arXiv:1509.07127v3 (7 August 2018), Theorem 2.1, Remark 2.2,
and stabilization/perfect-reference properties in Remark 2.4:
 https://arxiv.org/html/1509.07127v3
The averaged recovery depends only on a trace-class reference sigma and
channel N, recovers sigma from N sigma, and lower-bounds root fidelity by
exp[-(D(rho||sigma)-D(Nrho||N sigma))/2] when that difference is finite.
It is defined on the output-reference support and stabilizes with an
untouched faithful reference. The theorem covers separable Hilbert spaces.
Use its averaged rotated-Petz map, not a rho-selected rotation.

Also directly checked: Sutter--Fawzi--Renner, arXiv:1504.07251v3,
Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.3, primary HTML lines 79-92:
 https://arxiv.org/html/1504.07251v3
They give universal Markov recovery for a fixed BC marginal and all
separable A; exact reference recovery follows from a product extension.
Their log_2 and root-fidelity conventions convert to the same nats bound.
The separable application below is routed through the general relative-
entropy theorem plus the explicit extended-CMI identity, so it does not
rely on interpreting undefined entropy differences in an introduction.

 https://arxiv.org/html/1506.06377v2
The unique lsc extended CMI equals I(A:BC)-I(A:B) on S1={I(A:B)<infinity}.
Its finite-rank projection formula uses homogeneous mutual information,

Thus finite I(A:B) and finite delta_B imply
 I(A:BC)=I(A:B)+delta_B<infinity.                       (CMI1)

 delta_C=I(A:BC)-I(A:C).                               (CMI2)
Every relative entropy in the ensuing recovery applications is finite.

3. SAME LOCAL RECOVERY MAPS FOR EVERY REFERENCE/EXTENSION
Let N_B=Tr_C and N_C=Tr_B, with common input BC. Apply the primary
universal recovery construction to kappa and these two fixed channels.
Call its maps R_B:B->BC and R_C:C->BC. They depend only on kappa and obey
 R_B(kappa_B)=kappa, R_C(kappa_C)=kappa.                 (Ref)
Stabilization with tau_A, faithful on its support, identifies the recovery
for sigma=tau_A tensor kappa and id_A tensor N_j with id_A tensor R_j.
Hence varying A or tau_A NEVER varies R_B or R_C. In finite dimensions
one can verify this by cancellation of tau_A powers in each rotated-Petz
sandwich; in separable dimensions use the actual adjoint/stabilization
statement, not an unbounded inverse treated as a bounded operator.

The target tau is supported on supp(tau_A) tensor supp(kappa), by positivity
and its exact marginals. D(tau||tau_A tensor kappa)=I(A:BC). The output
relative entropies are I(A:B) and I(A:C). Equations (CMI1)--(CMI2) and
universal recovery therefore give root fidelities at least exp(-delta_j/2).
The upper half-trace/fidelity bound yields SIMULTANEOUSLY

 T(tau,(id_A tensor R_B)tau_AB)<=r_B,
 T(tau,(id_A tensor R_C)tau_AC)<=r_C.                   (Rec)

The same R_j are used for all extensions with this fixed kappa. Root
fidelity is ||sqrt(rho)sqrt(sigma)||_1, so the half-distance upper bound
is sqrt(1-F^2); this gives 1-exp(-delta_j), not 1-exp(-2delta_j).

FULL-DOMAIN/NORMAL COMPLETION. The source recovery is CP trace non-increasing
and exact on the support P_j=supp(kappa_j). Its adjoint has R_j*(I)<=P_j;
exact reference recovery with kappa_j faithful on P_j forces equality on
P_j. It is therefore TP on that full support algebra. Define on all trace
class inputs Y
 R_j^full(Y)=R_j(P_j Y P_j)+Tr[(I-P_j)Y]kappa.
This is normal CP TP, recovers kappa_j, and depends only on kappa. Every
tested marginal tau_Aj is supported on A tensor P_j, so completion does
not affect (Rec). No zero-eigenvalue inverse, lower spectral premise or
state-dependent completion is used. We henceforth write R_j for these
full normal channels.

4. AN ACTUAL BROADCASTER ON TWO COPIES OF B, WITH UNEQUAL C ALLOWED
Put L_C=Tr_C R_C:C->B. Define the actual full-domain normal CPTP map

 D_B=(id_B tensor L_C) R_B:B->B1 tensor B2.             (D)

Its first marginal is D_1=Tr_C R_B; its second is
D_2=L_C Tr_B R_B. It is a real joint channel on ALL inputs, not a pair
of abstract approximately compatible maps or a broadcaster on ABC.
B2 is produced by the C-to-BC recovery followed by discarding its C output,
so unequal dimensions are harmless. No cloning assumption is inserted.
At kappa_B, (Ref) gives BOTH marginals exactly kappa_B. If common marginals
are required by the supplied local interface, average D_B with its output
swap once; this stays kappa dependent and keeps the maximum error bound.

For a tested extension set rho=tau_AB. Trace contraction of the first
inequality in (Rec) gives
 b_1=T((id_A tensor D_1)rho,rho)<=r_B.
The intermediate AC marginal of (id_A tensor R_B)rho is within r_B of
tau_AC. Apply id_A tensor L_C to it, and then use the second inequality
in (Rec), traced over its C output. The triangle inequality gives
 b_2=T((id_A tensor D_2)rho,rho)<=r_B+r_C.
Thus the ACTUAL broadcaster error obeys
 b_D=max(b_1,b_2)<=min{1,s}, s=r_B+r_C.                 (Err)
This is the load-bearing conversion from two approximate Markov conditions
to legal local broadcastability; no mere agreement of recovered BC
marginals is substituted for the needed AB error.

5. ONE FIXED EB RECONSTRUCTOR, EXACT MARGINALS, AND ERROR TRANSPORT
Apply the supplied local normal theorem ONCE to (D_B,kappa_B), obtaining
one normal EB Lambda_B with Lambda_B(kappa_B)=kappa_B. It depends only
on kappa through the fixed construction (D), not on the tested tau or
its deficits/information. Set

 F=R_B Lambda_B:B->BC.                                 (F)

Composition with a normal CPTP channel retains normality, CP, TP and EB.
In separable dimensions, separable means trace-norm closed convex hull of
product states; trace-continuous local postprocessing preserves that set.
Hence the result is separable across A:(BC) even when kappa is entangled
across B:C. The input is tau_AB, not tau_ABC itself; no A operation occurs.

Exact marginals follow directly:
 omega_A=tau_A by TP,
 omega_BC=F(kappa_B)=R_B(kappa_B)=kappa.
Neither the AB nor AC marginal is asserted exactly unchanged.

For s<1, monotonicity of g(I,x) in x, (Err), channel contraction and (Rec)
give
 T((id_A tensor F)rho,tau)
 <=T((id_A tensor R_B Lambda_B)rho,(id_A tensor R_B)rho)+r_B
 <=T((id_A tensor Lambda_B)rho,rho)+r_B
 <=gbar(I,b_D)+r_B<=gbar(I,s)+r_B.
The universal trace bound supplies the cap one. If s>=1, the cap already
proves (T) and no logarithm of a nonpositive denominator is evaluated.
If b_D=0, use the local theorem's exact endpoint for the same Lambda;
no limiting choice of map is needed. If s=0, both recovery errors and
b_D are zero, proving exact whole-state reconstruction by the same F.

All information/broadcasting parameters select only an inequality for
this fixed map. They never enter its construction. Finite I is precisely
the budget used by the supplied local theorem; no I(A:C) or I(A:BC) is
silently substituted in g. This theorem proves a mathematical consequence,
not an algorithm to acquire the universal recovery or EB maps.
