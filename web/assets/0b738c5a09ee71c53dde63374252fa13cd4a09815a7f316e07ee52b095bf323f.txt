POSTEXPOSURE DETAILS FOR THE UNCHANGED INDEPENDENT ORTHOGONALITY BASELINE

The construction and robust bound were already in the frozen baseline.
This addendum supplies explicit algebra behind two standard steps, and
records its mixing consequence. No claim of blindness is made for this
new addendum; the baseline hash remains unchanged.

1. Explicit component-entropy algebra.

Use bits, eta=1/20, h=h_2(eta), a=1-h. For one product component
X~p, Y~q on nonzero binary m-vectors, let
 G={y:P(X dot y=1)<=eta}, r=dim span(G), tau=q(V minus G).
A basis selected from G gives r independent linear coordinate functions
on X, each with one-probability <=eta. Completing that basis to an
invertible coordinate map and using entropy subadditivity gives
 H(X)<=m-a*r.
Conditioning Y on membership of G gives
 H(Y)<=h_2(tau)+(1-tau)r+tau*m<=r+tau*m+h_2(tau).
Also H(Y)<=m. Since both inequalities hold, their convex combination
with respective weights a,h gives
 H(Y)<=a*[r+tau*m+h_2(tau)]+h*m.
Adding the X bound cancels the r term exactly:
 H(X)+H(Y)<=[1+h+a*tau]m+a*h_2(tau).
This avoids any piecewise optimization or assumption about r or tau.

The component nonorthogonality error e satisfies tau<=e/eta by Markov's
inequality. For a mixture with total nonorthogonal mass <=delta, its
weighted mean tau_bar<=delta/eta. Concavity gives
 average h_2(tau)<=h_2(tau_bar).
For delta<=1/100, tau_bar<=1/5, so monotonicity on [0,1/2] gives the
frozen conditional-entropy bound. Neither each individual tau<=1/5
nor each individual e<=delta is required.

2. Entropy continuity with the full charged alphabet.

On a finite alphabet of size L, laws p,q at TV delta have a coupling
(U,V) with P(U!=V)=delta. Let B=1_{U!=V}. Then
 H(U|V)<=H(B)+P(B=1)H(U|V,B=1)
          <=h_2(delta)+delta*log_2(L-1),
because U differs from the known V value when B=1. Since
H(U)-H(V)=H(U|V)-H(V|U)<=H(U|V), exchanging U,V gives
 |H(p)-H(q)|<=h_2(delta)+delta*log_2(L-1).
Here L=N^2, not N. Since N<2^m, the continuity loss is bounded by
h_2(delta)+2*delta*m bits. The maximal coupling exists explicitly:
put min(p_i,q_i) mass on matching values, and couple the two remaining
residual laws; their supports are disjoint.

3. Clean rigorous constants.

At delta=1/100, delta/eta=1/5. The linear coefficient is
 alpha=1-2delta-h-(1-h)delta/eta=.78-.8h.
For 0<p<1,
 h_2(p)<=p[ln(1/p)+1]/ln2,
using -(1-p)ln(1-p)<=p. The bounds ln2>2/3, ln20<3 and ln100<5
give h_2(1/20)<.3 and h_2(1/100)<.1. Thus alpha>.54>1/2,
and the additive loss 2+h_2(delta)+(1-h)h_2(delta/eta)<3.1<4.
These imply the stated bound H(C)>=(m/2-4)ln2 for every m>=3.
For smaller delta the coefficient only improves and the continuity
loss decreases. The logarithm estimates are elementary: ln2 is the
positive atanh series at 1/3, e^3's Taylor sum through degree 8 exceeds
20, and e^5's Taylor sum through degree 6 exceeds 100.

4. Uniform mixing of the same stationary construction.

With N=2^m-1, d=2^(m-1)-1, c=2^(m-2)-1 and u=2^(m-2), the frozen
identity A^2=u I+c J implies d^2=u+cN. Set Pi=J/N and beta=u/d^2.
Then
 T^2=(u/d^2)I+(c/d^2)J=beta I+(1-beta)Pi.
For m>=3, 0<beta<=2/9. T^2 has every entry positive. For any laws
mu,nu on V,
 TV(mu*T^2,nu*T^2)=beta*TV(mu,nu).
Thus the chain is irreducible, aperiodic, and geometrically mixing;
the memory obstruction is present even with a uniform two-step
contraction factor at most 2/9. This mixing strengthening is an
exposed consequence of the frozen independent construction, not an
additional construction or an alteration of its original baseline.
