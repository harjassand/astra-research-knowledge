CYCLE 5 EXTENSION AUDIT — MULTI-CATALYST SOURCE ORDER
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: REFUTED for the unchanged local support-correction potential on the
explicit reversible fixture below; this is not a recurrence counterexample.

Question
--------
Can the Cycle 5 local potential proof omit the source-catalyst-order-one
restriction when catalyst toggles are allowed to have additional catalyst
co-reactants? No. An independently growing catalyst can multiply the rate of
a last-neighbor removal, while the support correction jump is exponential in
the substrate count. The q-removal sink from the base graph only has the
usual factor b_i. The same local potential can therefore have LV tending to
+infinity even when D>=m (here m=0).

Exact fixture
-------------
Species A,C,B, with A and C catalysts and B the only substrate. The positive
base paired channels are

    0 <-> A              rates 1, a
    0 <-> C              rates 1, c
    A <-> A+B            rates a, a b
    A+B <-> 2A+B         rates a b, (a)_2 b.

Add the reversible catalyst-co-reactant pair

    A+C <-> C            rates a c, c.

All propensities are stochastic mass action and all directed rates are
strictly positive where the source complex is present. Every pair is
reversible, so the network is weakly reversible. The additional pair
preserves B and has substrate degree m=0, but its forward catalyst-source
order is two, outside the Cycle 5 theorem's order-one grammar.

For any fixed R>1, epsilon>0, integer D>=0, and fixed K>=1, use the same
local potential as Cycle 5:

    V(a,c,b)=1+a+c+R^b[1+epsilon 1_{a=0}/(1+b)^D]
                 +K 1_{a+c=0}.

Along x_n=(a,c,b)=(1,M_n,n) with M_n=n^(D+2), the K indicator is zero
before and after every enabled transition for n>=1. Exact reaction-by-
reaction calculation gives

    LV(x_n)=1-M_n +(R-1)R^n -(1-R^-1)nR^n + n
               +epsilon(1+M_n)R^n/(1+n)^D.                    (1)

Explanation of the added-pair term: at a=1,c=M, the forward event has rate
M and increment -1+epsilon R^n/(1+n)^D (it removes the last A and turns on
the correction); the reverse event has rate M and increment +1 (it adds an
A while the correction remains off). Their linear-A increments cancel,
leaving epsilon M R^n/(1+n)^D. The additional epsilon R^n/(1+n)^D in (1)
is the base A-death last-neighbor switch. The substrate-pair terms are the
exact base production/consumption increments, and the k=1 autocatalytic
forward term contributes n; its reverse is disabled at a=1.

Dividing (1) by n^2 R^n and using M_n=n^(D+2) gives

    LV(x_n)/(n^2R^n) -> epsilon > 0.

Indeed the catalytic support-switch gain contributes epsilon n^2 R^n
asymptotically, the base q sink is only order nR^n, and the C immigration-
death drift is polynomial. Thus LV(x_n)->+infinity. This defeats the
unchanged local potential for every fixed D, epsilon, R, K in the displayed
range, despite D>=m=0.

This is only an obstruction to the candidate Foster function and its
source-order-one proof. The fixture is itself detailed balanced at unit
activity: every reversible pair has equal forward and reverse complex flux
at (a,c,b)=(1,1,1). Its product-Poisson(1) law is stationary by the
complex-balanced product-form theorem; the complex-balanced non-explosion
theorem applies as well. Hence this ray must not be reported as transience,
null recurrence, explosion, or failure of stochastic permanence.

Next gate
---------
A higher-source-order extension needs a different certificate or extra
structure. One plausible direction is a catalyst-weighted correction that
accounts for unbounded co-reactant counts; its jump increments must be
checked against every reverse/boundary transition and against the physical
clock. A clean restricted theorem could instead require all catalyst
co-reactants in a support-deactivating source to be locally bounded by the
same substrate face (or a certified linear/exponential sink), but that
condition and its necessity are unproved. The present example only proves
that the Cycle 5 local potential cannot be extended verbatim.

Primary source boundary: Anderson, Craciun, and Kurtz, “Product-form
stationary distributions for deficiency zero chemical reaction networks,”
arXiv:0803.3042v4, https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3042v4; Anderson, Cappelletti,
Koyama, and Kurtz, “Non-explosivity of stochastically modeled reaction
networks that are complex balanced,” arXiv:1708.09356,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09356. These sources support the status of this
fixture, not the general Cycle 5 drift inequality.
