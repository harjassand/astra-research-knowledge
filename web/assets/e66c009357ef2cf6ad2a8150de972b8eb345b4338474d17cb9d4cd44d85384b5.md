# Cycle 3: exact matched-diffusion witness under a tumor-resistance assay

**Verdict.** A polyploid giant cancer cell (PGCC) burst is the strongest exact biological mapping of the four-outcome, matched-CLE pair. A shared death channel can be added without breaking the equality of drift and covariance. The offspring distributions then give different probabilities of at least one maintenance-resistant descendant, but that contrast is conditional on an unmeasured daughter-level probability. The primary literature gives neither the needed per-daughter probability nor a transportable PGCC burst-size law. The exact five-event distinction is a jump-process-versus-diffusion distinction, not a new resistance mechanism and not an advantage over a fully informed multitype branching likelihood. The corresponding ecDNA partition map is more constrained: exact conservation and exchangeable sisters force the centered third cumulant to be zero, so the nonzero third-cumulant pair cannot be transferred to ordinary sister partitioning.

## 1. The exact PGCC transition pair

The source witness is the catalytic pair in [`FINAL_REPORT.md`](../chemistry_kinetic_observability/FINAL_REPORT.md): channels indexed by `k=1,2,3,4`, with rate vectors

\[
a^A=(1,1,4,0),\qquad a^B=(0,4,1,1).
\]

For the cancer-state map, let `G` be one therapy-induced giant cell and `R` a post-burst candidate descendant. Channel `k` is `G → kR`, with propensity `a_k G`. Its jump is `ν_k=(-1,k)` in coordinates `(G,R)`. Add the same competing giant-cell death channel `G → ∅` at rate `δG` to both models. This shared channel represents the observed competing fate and changes no between-model difference. It also makes explicit that `k` counts descendants conditional on a burst, not all fates of a PGCC.

For both burst laws,

\[
M_0=\sum_k a_k=6,\quad M_1=\sum_k k a_k=15,\quad M_2=\sum_k k^2a_k=41,
\]

while `M_3=117` for A and `123` for B. Including the shared death channel, the drift and covariance per unit `G` are

\[
A_G=(-(6+\delta),15),\qquad
B_G=\begin{pmatrix}6+\delta&-15\\-15&41\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They agree exactly for all states and every common `δ≥0`; the RRR entry of the third jump-moment tensor differs by 6 per unit `G`. Conditional on a burst, the offspring laws are

\[
P_A(K=1,2,3,4)=(1,1,4,0)/6,\qquad
P_B(K=1,2,3,4)=(0,4,1,1)/6.
\]

Both have `E[K]=5/2`, `Var(K)=7/12`, but third cumulants `−1/2` and `+1/2`. The burst hazard is 6 in both; including death, the event time is `Exp(6+δ)` in both and the first-event burst probability is `6/(6+δ)`. Thus neither mean trajectories, the full two-coordinate CLE law, nor the first-event time distinguishes the pair. Direct offspring counts do.

The pair is algebraically and biologically *possible* as a many-descendant burst: a PGCC can produce multiple daughter cells. The specific integer rates and support `{1,2,3,4}` are a constructed witness, not fitted tumor parameters. If an empirical system regularly gives no daughters at a completed neosis event, or more than four, these exact candidate laws are falsified; the shared death channel accounts for death before a burst, not zero-size bursts. Also, with one or a few `G` cells and discrete jumps of size at most four, a CLE is not automatically a controlled biological approximation. The exact match is a counterexample to second-order diffusion identifiability, not evidence that a tumor follows that diffusion limit.

## 2. Resistance-tail contrast and its calibration limit

Define the operational endpoint as: an individually tracked post-burst daughter produces a durable lineage that survives the specified maintenance/rechallenge assay. Let `s` be the probability of that endpoint for one candidate daughter, and assume daughter outcomes are conditionally independent given `K`. Writing `u=1-s`, the probability of at least one successful lineage conditional on a burst is

\[
R_A(s)=1-\frac{u+u^2+4u^3}{6},\qquad
R_B(s)=1-\frac{4u^2+u^3+u^4}{6}.
\]

Consequently

\[
R_B(s)-R_A(s)=\frac{s^3(1-s)}6.
\]

This is positive for `0<s<1`, is maximized at `s=3/4`, and its maximum is `9/512=0.017578125` (1.76 percentage points) **conditional on a burst**. At `s=3/4`, `R_A=15/16=0.9375` and `R_B=489/512≈0.95508`. At `s=1/2`, the respective risks are `19/24≈0.79167` and `77/96≈0.80208`, a `1/96≈1.04pp` gap. For a PGCC starting state that can die before bursting, each risk and their difference are multiplied by `6/(6+δ)`.

These values are sensitivity calculations, not calibrated tumor risks. Zhang et al. use a colony definition of more than 50 cells and follow single irradiated 4T1 PGCCs over a 14-day cloning assay, with PGCC imaging every 2 hours for five days; this supplies an operational clonogenic threshold and feasible observation cadence, but not `s` for a separately isolated daughter under a specified maintenance/rechallenge regimen ([primary study, *Molecular Oncology* 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8334289/)). The endpoint needed here is stricter: a named daughter must cross a stated colony threshold and retain growth on a specified drug rechallenge. The paper does not report a per-daughter probability for that endpoint.

Semple et al. single-cell seeded 960 docetaxel-treated, post-release PC3 polyploid cells; only two yielded progeny, after two and 2.5 months. One progeny failed at first passage; the other was sensitive to both docetaxel and cisplatin ([primary study, *Oncogene* 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11370377/)). Thus this particular assay produced no confirmed durable drug-resistant lineage among 960 seeded PGCCs. Under independent wells and perfect endpoint detection, zero successes gives a one-sided 95% binomial upper limit `1−0.05^(1/960)=0.003116` (0.312%) for the **whole-PGCC** durable-resistance endpoint in that exact PC3 setting. It does not identify `s`: burst occurrence, burst size, daughter success, and endpoint sensitivity are not separately resolved. Nor is this PC3/docetaxel estimate transportable to irradiated 4T1 or a patient tumor. The strong negative result does rule out treating `s=0.75` as an empirically plausible value for that PC3 setting without explaining the assay discrepancy.

Accordingly, an empirical risk-tail gap at the requested resistant-colony threshold is **not currently computable from the cited data**. The defensible report is the exact formula above, an empirically grounded parent-level upper limit for one distinct setting, and a new matched assay to estimate `s`, the burst hazard, and the burst law. Replacing the missing `s` with the 14-day colony fraction or with a literature-wide PGCC percentage would conflate non-equivalent endpoints.

## 3. Sample size, readout sensitivity, and cadence

### Fixed-model identification from observed burst sizes

With perfect burst-size calls and exactly the two fixed candidate laws above, the exact multinomial likelihood-ratio test needs five independent completed bursts for the sum of type-I and type-II error to fall below 5%: `181/3888≈0.04655`. Four bursts cannot reach the target; the minimum combined error is `65/648≈0.10031`. Waiting times provide zero discrimination because the burst hazard agrees. This is a model-based lower bound for these two simple hypotheses, not a biological sample-size claim and not a comparison against a fully informed branching fit. The optimal decision is: choose A if any `K=1` occurs; choose B if any `K=4` occurs; otherwise choose A if `n_3>n_2` and B if `n_2>n_3`. With five observations a tie is impossible. If both a 1 and a 4 occur, both candidate models are falsified and the likelihood ratio is undefined (`0/0`); that event has probability zero under either candidate.

If each daughter is independently detected with calibrated sensitivity `ρ`, with no false-positive daughters, the observed count is a binomial thinning of `K`: `p̃_j=Σ_{k≥j}p_k C(k,j)ρ^j(1−ρ)^(k−j)`. For `n` observed bursts, the minimum possible sum of the two testing errors is `Σ_{n_0+⋯+n_4=n} min{Mult(n_0,…,n_4; p̃_A), Mult(n_0,…,n_4; p̃_B)}`. Exact enumeration gives the following minimum burst counts for combined error ≤5%:

| Daughter detection `ρ` | Minimum completed bursts |
|---:|---:|
| 1.00 | 5 |
| 0.99 | 6 |
| 0.975 | 7 |
| 0.95 | 8 |
| 0.90 | 12 |
| 0.80 | 21 |
| 0.70 | 37 |

This calculation assumes independent per-daughter misses, perfectly classified mother death versus burst, and known sensitivity; it does not include false positives or uncertainty in `ρ`. At `ρ=0.5`, the same enumeration has not reached 5% combined error by 50 bursts. An assay with uncalibrated daughter visibility can confound offspring tails with detection thinning, so the ideal five-burst result is not robust to arbitrary measurement error.

### Power for the binary resistance endpoint

Even with `s` known and the simple binary endpoint perfectly scored, direct estimation of the risk difference is much more expensive than identifying the toy offspring law. Using a two-sided 5% normal-approximation two-proportion test with 80% power gives about 2,581 independent **completed bursts per arm** at the maximally separated sensitivity point `s=0.75`; at `s=0.5`, it gives about 23,416 bursts per arm. The calculation is

\[
n\approx\frac{\left[z_{.975}\sqrt{2\bar p(1-\bar p)}+z_{.80}\sqrt{p_A(1-p_A)+p_B(1-p_B)}\right]^2}{(p_B-p_A)^2},\quad \bar p=(p_A+p_B)/2.
\]

These numbers are not optimized designs and exclude multiplicity, culture-level clustering, dropout, and parameter estimation. Near `s=0` or `s=1`, the risk gap tends to zero; no finite fixed sample size works uniformly over unknown `s`.

As a scale illustration only, the PC3 study's `2/960` progeny-generating fraction is approximately `0.00208` in its particular delayed single-cell assay. If that were the burst probability in a new matched system, collecting 2,581 completed bursts would require roughly 1.24 million initially screened PGCCs per arm before censoring/failed tracking. Testing only the parent-level binary endpoint without conditioning on observed burst status is harder still: at `s=0.75`, scaling the two model risks by `2/960` yields a normal-approximation requirement around 23 million PGCCs per arm. This is a design sensitivity illustration, not a transferable rate estimate; the two PC3 progeny were not both durable or drug resistant.

For a first mechanistic assay, image every 2–12 hours from treatment through the initial 5-day window, when the 4T1 PGCC fraction peaked and PGCC fates diverged; follow single PGCCs at least every 12 hours through day 14 and score the colony threshold then. If the question is durable resistance, extend single-cell follow-up through rechallenge and for months when indicated: the PC3 progeny appeared only after 2–2.5 months. These are source-informed cadences, not a validated optimum. Record the microscope-hours, cell-wells, tracking failures, calibrated daughter-detection sensitivity, and independent culture replicates separately. The 2-hour imaging schedule and 12-hour single-cell follow-up come from the 4T1 study; the month-scale follow-up comes from the PC3 study cited above.

## 4. Why ecDNA partitioning does not carry this third-cumulant pair

Suppose the parent has `M` replicated ecDNA copies available at mitosis, and sister 1 receives `K` while sister 2 receives `M−K`. If sister labels are exchangeable, then `K` and `M−K` have the same distribution. For `X=K−M/2`, this implies `X` and `−X` have the same law, hence `E[X^3]=0` and the centered third cumulant vanishes. Ordinary independent random segregation (`K~Binomial(M,1/2)`) is a special case. Therefore an exact nonzero `−1/2` versus `+1/2` centered offspring-cumulant pair cannot be imposed on an exchangeable, copy-conserving two-daughter partition without adding an orientation, asymmetric localization, or a hidden state that breaks sister exchangeability. Those additions change the mechanism and require their own empirical evidence.

There is still a tail non-identifiability example under conservation, but it is **not** the matched-third-cumulant witness. For `M=4`, compare sister-pair laws A: `K∈{0,2,4}` with probabilities `(.05,.90,.05)`, and B: `K∈{1,2,3}` with probabilities `(.20,.60,.20)`. Both conserve exactly `K+(4−K)=4`; both have `E[K]=2`, `Var(K)=0.4`, and zero centered third cumulant. Yet at a purely illustrative copy threshold `K≥3`, one specified daughter crosses with probabilities .05 versus .20; at least one sister crosses with probabilities .10 versus .40. No resistance threshold is inferred here: an ecDNA copy cutoff must be calibrated against the particular oncogene, expression, and drug response. ecDNA segregation, heterogeneous copy number, and treatment adaptation are already directly studied, including live tracking and random-inheritance models by Lange et al. ([*Nature Genetics* 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/9534767/)); coordinated inheritance of multiple ecDNAs is also prior art ([Hung et al., *Nature* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07861-8)).

## 5. Fully informed baseline, prior art, and decision

On the same ideal lineage records, the matched baseline is the full multitype branching/CTMC likelihood: record `G` event times and cause, every burst offspring count `K`, daughter identities, subsequent birth/death/switch histories, and the assay endpoint. The two fixed `K` laws have ordinary multinomial likelihoods. Their optimal likelihood-ratio test is exactly the five-burst rule above. With a general burst law `h(z)=E[z^K]` and one-daughter success `s`, the branching model predicts

\[
P(\text{at least one resistant lineage from one }G)
=\frac{6}{6+\delta}\,[1-h(1-s)].
\]

It therefore predicts the tail risk exactly once the same data identify `h`, `δ`, and `s`. A moment-only CLE/ODE cannot tell the pair apart; a fully informed branching likelihood can. The claimed separation is strictly diffusion-vs-jump, not an advantage against the equally informed baseline.

Biological prior art also preempts a new PGCC-neosis or ecDNA-segregation mechanism claim. Time-lapse imaging and single-cell cloning already establish irradiated PGCC death and neosis-associated repopulation in selected models ([Zhang et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8334289/)); docetaxel-induced polyploid cells and budding daughters with altered/resistant phenotypes have been reported ([Makarovskiy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5418452/)); older primary work describes neosis and daughter-cell generation ([Sundaram et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.4161/cbt.3.2.663)). For ecDNA, the 2026 primary study by Shibata et al. combines barcoded clonal tracking with single-cell copy measurements and reports drug-associated active reconfiguration of segregation behavior in a clone, directly preempting a generic stress-adaptive-segregation claim ([*Nature Communications* 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13547322/)). Earlier work already links random ecDNA inheritance to copy-number heterogeneity and rapid adaptation ([Lange et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9534767/)). The present witness introduces no new cancer mechanism. Its only narrow value is a transparent example that matching drift and covariance does not identify an offspring tail or its branching-risk functional.

**Decision:** retain the PGCC pair as a conditional identifiability counterexample; reject it as a new biological mechanism or a calibrated patient-risk predictor. The decisive next dataset is a preregistered, independently replicated single-PGCC lineage assay that jointly measures death-versus-burst latency, complete offspring counts, daughter detection sensitivity, and the same durable resistance endpoint for each daughter. Fit the full censored branching likelihood to those exact histories as the matched informed baseline. A falsifier for the exact witness is an offspring count outside its support or a likelihood/goodness-of-fit rejection of either fixed law. A falsifier for a causal PGCC-risk interpretation is failure of tracked daughters to show persistent resistant growth on rechallenge, or a held-out whole-PGCC rebound frequency inconsistent with the fitted branching prediction. Outside the assumptions of independent cell lineages, time-homogeneous hazards, reliable type calls, noninformative censoring, and a correct maintenance assay, mathematical identifiability does not establish what occurs in a tumor.
