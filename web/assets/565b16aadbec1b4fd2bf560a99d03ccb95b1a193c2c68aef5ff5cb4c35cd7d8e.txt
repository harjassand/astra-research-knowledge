1. Charles Bordenave and Benoît Collins, “Norm of matrix-valued polynomials in random unitaries and permutations,” arXiv:2304.05714v2, 10 Jan 2024.
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05714
Full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05714v2

Relevant source locations:
- Lemma 7.4, §7.2: concentration of the linear polynomial Schatten norm about its expectation; proof gives the Lipschitz estimate and cites Meckes Theorem 5.17.
- Lemma 8.1, §8.1: exact degree-l to degree-l/2 norm linearization with coefficient amplification 2|B_(l/2)| and theta bound.
- Theorem 9.2 / Lemma 9.3, §9.3: strong convergence of random tensor-leg representations of F_d^k and the expectation estimate for additive coefficient polynomials.

The pertinent finite expectation inequality in Lemma 9.3 is

  E||A_N|| <= ||A_(F^k)|| (2n)^((k+1)/p)
              N^(k(k+1)/(2p)) p^(3k(k+1)/(2p))
              (1+c/sqrt(N))^k,

for N>=d^70 and even 2<=p<=N^(1/(16d+80)). The theorem statement does not print c, but the specialized unitary path proof is reconstructed in evidence/CONSTANT_AUDIT.txt and yields c<=780 for the selected d=65 model. The lemma remains an expectation bound; cycle4 uses separate concentration and does not infer a p-tail from it.

The finite d=65 threshold also re-runs the product-leg interpolation from
Theorem 5.1's ell_0 moment condition, rather than reading a larger p range
into published Lemma 9.3. This is an internal parameter-range extension;
its proof and explicit leading factor are in evidence/STRONGER_EXPONENT_GATE.txt
and its d/slack optimization is in evidence/JOINT_PARAMETER_SEARCH.txt.

2. Elizabeth S. Meckes, “The Random Matrix Theory of the Classical Compact Groups,” Cambridge University Press, 2019, Theorem 5.17.
Author-hosted primary PDF: https://case.edu/artsci/math/mwmeckes/elizabeth/Haar_book.pdf

Theorem 5.17 gives, for independent Haar factors from SO(n_j), U(n_j), and other listed compact groups under the L2-sum Hilbert–Schmidt metric,

  P(F >= EF+t) <= exp(-(n-2)t^2/(24 L^2)),

where n=min n_j and F is L-Lipschitz. With a linear additive polynomial over dk generator variables and L<=2 sqrt(dk)||A_F||, this yields the denominator 96dk used in RESULT.txt.
