# Routine polynomial cyclic baseline from release116

7 October 2026. Independently checked at root's request, with no new experiment or literature search. This is a prior-comparison correction, not a new research claim.

**Conclusion.** The release's acyclic hitting tuple already yields a polynomial proper-cyclic detector by standard bounded-length unrolling and a nilpotent length marker. Our Volterra/scalar-grid and row-batched work improves dimensions and charged query construction; it does not establish the first polynomial proper-cyclic capability.

Let F have characteristic zero and let the proper recognizable series have a valid m-state bound, m≥1:

\[
 f_w=uB_wv,\qquad f=\sum_w f_wx_w.
\]

Its semantics at every jointly nilpotent rational tuple are assumed available from an exact output-only matrix oracle. This does not enlarge the inverse-gate domains of an arbitrary rational expression.

1. **Short coefficient suffices.** The row spaces \(V_j=\operatorname{span}\{uB_w:|w|\le j\}\) stabilize by j=m−1. A single equality V_j=V_(j+1) makes the span invariant under every B_i; absent an earlier equality, its dimension reaches m by m−1. Thus vanishing of all coefficients of length<m implies vanishing on the entire reachable space. Consequently f≠0 iff g=f_<m≠0.
2. **Standard unrolling.** Use m layers, each containing the m hidden states. Put B_i from each layer into the next layer, initial u only on the first layer, and final v on every layer. This realizes exactly g with M=m² states. All transition matrices are strictly upper triangular in layer ordering, regardless of cycles in B_i. The generator need not acquire or compute these hidden transitions: their existence supplies the class promise for the uniform tuple.
3. **Use the released acyclic detector.** The source's Proposition5.1 generates T_i over Q at

\[
 d=(n-1)\binom{m^2}{2}+m^2=O(nm^4)
\]

and detects every nonzero g in that acyclic class, uniformly in its coefficient values. Write g=\(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}f_k\), with f_k homogeneous of word length k. Since g(T)≠0, at least one f_k(T) is nonzero.

4. **Query the actual cyclic oracle.** Let S_m shift the marker basis e_0,...,e_(m−1), and query W_i=S_m tensor T_i. Then every product of m W_i vanishes, and

\[
 f(W)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}S_m^k\otimes f_k(T).
\]

The matrices I,S_m,...,S_m^(m−1) are linearly independent; applying to marker seed e_0 puts each f_k(T) into its distinct marker row. Therefore f(W)≠0 whenever some f_k(T)≠0. Conversely the zero series evaluates to zero. The tuple has dimension

\[
 D=m\left((n-1)\binom{m^2}{2}+m^2\right)=O(nm^5).
\]

It is universally generated from n,m in polynomial bit time and lies in the proper-series oracle domain. At m=1, S_1=0 and the guarantee reduces to the constant coefficient, which determines whether a one-state series is zero.

The source proof additionally gives g(T)e_0≠0. Hence some f_k(T)e_0≠0, and the same baseline is faithful on a fixed first seed e_0 tensor e_0. The already checked transposed-prefix residual construction consequently gives a routine polynomial exact-acquisition baseline under the same strong matrix oracle: ≤1+nr queries of dimension≤D+r for minimal rank r≤m. Returned matrices, response encoding and oracle execution remain charged. This learning consequence uses the existing residual-span proof; no new execution evidence is asserted here.

This leaves the syntactic-domain and statistical-access boundaries intact.
