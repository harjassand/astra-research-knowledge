# Exact attack on the finite Hankel target

**Result:** I did not prove the full Hankel PSD claim through $\delta=2\ln 2$. I obtained an exact normalization, the sharp universal obstruction from $N=2$, and a closed-form threshold showing that every $2\times2$ principal Hankel minor is nonnegative throughout $0\le\delta\le2\ln2$. This also proves eventual strict positivity of all such minors for any fixed $\delta<2$. The gap is all higher-order principal minors. No computation was run; the work below is exact algebra and was kept within the requested bounded-call budget.

## Normalized finite-grid matrices

For $0\le k\le N$, put

\[
q_k=k!(N-k)!\exp\!\left[-\frac{\delta(k-N/2)^2}{N}\right]
    =\frac{N!e^{-\delta N/4}e^{\delta k-\delta k^2/N}}{\binom Nk}.
\]

Use $H_0=(q_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$ and $H_1=(q_{i+j+1})_{0\le i,j\le\lfloor(N-1)/2\rfloor}$.

For the even Hankel matrix, with $i,j$ in its appropriate range,

\[
q_{i+j}=N!e^{-\delta N/4}
  d_i d_j\,M^{(0)}_{ij},\qquad
d_i=e^{\delta i-\delta i^2/N},\qquad
M^{(0)}_{ij}=\frac{e^{-2\delta ij/N}}{\binom N{i+j}}.
\]

For the shifted matrix,

\[
q_{i+j+1}=N!e^{-\delta N/4+\delta-\delta/N}
  \tilde d_i\tilde d_j\,M^{(1)}_{ij},\qquad
\tilde d_i=e^{\delta i-\delta(i^2+2i)/N},\qquad
M^{(1)}_{ij}=\frac{e^{-2\delta ij/N}}{\binom N{i+j+1}}.
\]

The factors outside $M^{(0)},M^{(1)}$ are positive diagonal congruences and hence do not affect PSD. This reduces the question to two *finite-grid* kernels with reciprocal-binomial weights. It does not create a positive-measure representation by itself.

## Sharp $N=2$ obstruction

For $N=2$, $q_0=q_2=2e^{-\delta/2}$ and $q_1=1$. The degree-two even Hankel matrix is

\[
H_0=\begin{pmatrix}2e^{-\delta/2}&1\\1&2e^{-\delta/2}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H_0=4e^{-\delta}-1.
\]

Thus PSD for every $N$ requires $\delta\le\ln4=2\ln2$. At equality $H_0$ is rank one, so this is a PSD boundary, not a positive-definite claim.

## Exact adjacent-minor lemma

For $N\ge2$ and every $1\le k<N$, direct cancellation of factorials and the quadratic exponent gives

\[
\frac{q_{k-1}q_{k+1}}{q_k^2}
 =e^{-2\delta/N}\frac{(k+1)(N-k+1)}{k(N-k)}
 =e^{-2\delta/N}\left(1+\frac{N+1}{k(N-k)}\right).
\]

Hence all adjacent log-convexity inequalities $q_k^2\le q_{k-1}q_{k+1}$ hold exactly when

\[
\delta\le\delta_{\rm adj}(N):=
\frac N2\ln\!\left(1+\frac{N+1}{\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}\right),
\]

because $k(N-k)$ is maximized at the middle integer(s). This threshold is minimized at $N=2$:

- If $N=2m$, then $\delta_{\rm adj}=2m\ln(1+1/m)$. The function $m\ln(1+1/m)$ is increasing, since its derivative is $\ln(1+1/m)-1/(m+1)>0$. Its minimum is $\ln2$ at $m=1$.
- If $N=2m+1$, then $\delta_{\rm adj}=\frac{2m+1}{2}\ln(1+2/m)$. Using $\ln(1+x)\ge2x/(2+x)$ gives $\delta_{\rm adj}\ge(2m+1)/(m+1)\ge3/2>2\ln2$.

Therefore every adjacent minor is strictly positive for $N>2$ whenever $\delta\le2\ln2$; at $N=2,\delta=2\ln2$ the unique adjacent determinant is zero. Since positive adjacent log-convexity implies log-convexity across every arithmetic progression, this also makes every $2\times2$ *principal* minor of both parity Hankel matrices nonnegative.

For $N=1$, both Hankel matrices are positive scalars for every $\delta\ge0$.

For a fixed $\delta<2$, $\delta_{\rm adj}(N)\to2$. More explicitly, the even subsequence obeys $\delta_{\rm adj}\ge2-1/m$ and the odd subsequence obeys $\delta_{\rm adj}\ge2-1/m-1/m^2$, by $\ln(1+x)\ge x-x^2/2$. Thus all adjacent minors are strictly positive for all sufficiently large $N$. This is only the order-two part of the requested eventual PSD statement.

## Two routes that do not close the proof

**Moment-multiplier / Gaussian deconvolution.** At $\delta=0$, $k!(N-k)!/(N+1)!$ is the $k$th moment of the beta-prime density $(1+x)^{-N-2}$ on $x>0$. But the added factor $g_k=e^{-\delta k^2/N}$ is itself log-concave for $\delta>0$: $g_k^2>g_{k-1}g_{k+1}$. Every nonnegative-measure moment sequence is log-convex, so $g_k$ cannot be the moments of an independent positive multiplicative factor. This rules out the naive positive Mellin-convolution proof; it does not rule out a different measure matching only the finite list $q_0,\ldots,q_N$.

**Schur-product / total-positivity shortcut.** The congruence above leaves $M_{ij}=\binom N{i+j+s}^{-1}r^{ij}$, where $s=0$ or $1$ and $r=e^{-2\delta/N}\in(0,1)$. The factor kernel $K_{ij}=r^{ij}$ is not PSD: its leading $2\times2$ determinant is $r-1<0$. So the Schur product theorem cannot establish PSD by treating $K$ as a positive kernel. The reciprocal-binomial factor may compensate, as the $N=2$ equality case shows; indefiniteness of $K$ is not a counterexample to the target matrix.

**Log-convexity as a sufficient condition.** The exact adjacent-minor bound is sharp and covers all order-two principal minors, but does not control larger minors. For example, the unrelated positive rational sequence $(1,1,11/10,10,100)$ is log-convex at every adjacent index while its $3\times3$ Hankel determinant is $-69331/1000$. This only disproves the inference from log-convexity alone; it is not a counterexample to the target sequence.

The requested lift therefore still needs a bound or representation for higher-order Hankel determinants (or a finite-grid positive-measure construction). I did not extend the c07_s02 $5/4$ argument and make no claim about the truth of the stronger all-$N$ target. The supplied c07_s02 join itself says its exact finite certificate is pending fresh independent verification; this note does not verify that certificate.
