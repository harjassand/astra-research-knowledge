# Vanishing viscosity with no-slip walls: Kato layer budget

Status: the classical Kato theorem and the proof mechanism are reconstructed at the stated smooth-Euler/Leray–Hopf interface. Vanishing dissipation in a strip of thickness proportional to viscosity is necessary and sufficient for strong energy convergence. The ordinary Leray energy estimate does not imply this little-o condition. An exact shear example shows strong convergence despite loss of uniform enstrophy. I found no rigorous equal-data, unforced Navier–Stokes example violating Kato; a February 2026 paper says none was known to its authors.

## Interface and quantifiers

Let $d=2$ or $3$, let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^d$ be bounded with $C^2$ boundary, and fix $T>0$. Let $u^\nu$ be any Leray–Hopf solution of the unforced incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

\[
 \partial_t u^\nu+(u^\nu\cdot\nabla)u^\nu-\nu\Delta u^\nu+\nabla p^\nu=0,
 \qquad \nabla\cdot u^\nu=0,
 \qquad u^\nu|_{\partial\Omega}=0,
\]

with a common initial datum $u^\nu(0)=u_0\in L^2(\Omega)$, divergence free and tangent to the boundary in the weak trace sense. Assume Euler has a classical solution $U\in C^1([0,T];C^2(\bar\Omega))$ with $U(0)=u_0$, $\nabla\cdot U=0$, and $U\cdot n=0$ on $\partial\Omega$. The tangential trace $U_\tau|_{\partial\Omega}$ may be nonzero. The result applies on any interval where this classical Euler solution exists; it does not assert global smooth Euler existence in 3D.

Write $\Gamma_\delta=\{x\in\Omega:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\delta\}$. For a fixed $c>0$, define

\[
 D_K^\nu(T;c)=\nu\int_0^T\int_{\Gamma_{c\nu}}|\nabla u^\nu|^2\,dx\,dt.
\]

Kato's criterion is

\[
 \|u^\nu-U\|_{L^\infty(0,T;L^2(\Omega))}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D_K^\nu(T;c)\longrightarrow0.
\]

One fixed positive $c$ suffices for the sufficient direction; convergence forces the condition for every fixed $c>0$, since it forces total viscous dissipation to vanish. The strongest baseline here uses the same initial datum for every viscosity, the same domain and time interval, and no forcing. A version with $u_0^\nu\to U(0)$ strongly in $L^2$ has the same proof and adds $\frac12\|u_0^\nu-U(0)\|_2^2$ to the convergence bound. This is energy convergence, not pointwise convergence of gradients, pressure, wall shear, or a Prandtl expansion.

## Exact local energy budget in the boundary strip

For a smooth Navier–Stokes solution, set $e=|u^\nu|^2/2$ and

\[
 \mathcal F^\nu=(e+p^\nu)u^\nu-\nu(\nabla u^\nu)^T u^\nu.
\]

Dotting momentum with $u^\nu$ and using incompressibility gives the pointwise identity

\[
 \partial_t e+\nabla\cdot\mathcal F^\nu+\nu|\nabla u^\nu|^2=0.
\]

For $0<\delta<\delta_0(\Omega)$, let $\Sigma_\delta=\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)=\delta\}$ be the inner interface of $\Gamma_\delta$, and let $n_\delta$ point outward from the strip. Integration gives the exact balance

\[
 \frac12\int_{\Gamma_\delta}|u^\nu(t)|^2\,dx
 +\nu\int_0^t\int_{\Gamma_\delta}|\nabla u^\nu|^2\,dx\,ds
 +\int_0^t\int_{\Sigma_\delta}\mathcal F^\nu\cdot n_\delta\,dS\,ds
 =\frac12\int_{\Gamma_\delta}|u^\nu(0)|^2\,dx.
\]

The physical-wall flux vanishes because $u^\nu=0$ there. The remaining signed flux through $\Sigma_\delta$ exchanges kinetic energy with the fluid outside the strip. With a body force $f$, add $\int_0^t\int_{\Gamma_\delta}f\cdot u^\nu$ to the right side. This is an exact identity for smooth solutions. A weak solution has only a local energy inequality when suitable/local-energy hypotheses are known; Kato's theorem itself needs only the global Leray–Hopf energy inequality.

The same local balance can be written with flux $(e+p)u-2\nu D(u)u$ and dissipation $2\nu|D(u)|^2$, where $D(u)=(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)/2$. The two local forms differ by a divergence identity and give the same global no-slip dissipation. The gradient form is used here to match Kato's criterion.

## Quantitative sufficient condition and proof

For a fixed $c>0$, choose a divergence-free corrector $v^\nu$ supported in $\Gamma_{c\nu}$ whose boundary trace equals $U|_{\partial\Omega}$. For small enough $\nu$, the standard Kato construction gives

\[
 \|v^\nu\|_{L^\infty_tL^2_x}
 +\|\partial_t v^\nu\|_{L^\infty_tL^2_x}\le C_K\nu^{1/2},\qquad
 \|\nabla v^\nu\|_{L^\infty_tL^2_x}\le C_K\nu^{-1/2},\qquad
 \|\nabla v^\nu\|_{L^\infty_{t,x}}\le C_K\nu^{-1},
\]

where $C_K$ depends on $\Omega,c$ and fixed norms of $U$, not on $\nu$. Then $U-v^\nu$ is divergence free and vanishes at the wall, so it is an admissible weak Navier–Stokes test field.

Let $w^\nu=u^\nu-U$, $X^\nu(t)=\frac12\|w^\nu(t)\|_2^2$, $M=\|\nabla U\|_{L^\infty([0,T]\times\Omega)}$, and $E_0=\|u_0\|_2$. Testing with $U-v^\nu$, subtracting the resulting cross term from the Navier–Stokes energy inequality, and using the Euler equation yields

\[
 X^\nu(t)+\nu\int_0^t\|\nabla u^\nu\|_2^2\,ds
 \le X^\nu(0)+2M\int_0^tX^\nu(s)\,ds+R_\nu(t),
\]

with

\[
 |R_\nu(t)|\le C_1\left(\nu^{1/2}+D_K^\nu(T;c)+\sqrt{D_K^\nu(T;c)}\right),
 \qquad
 C_1=C_1(\Omega,c,T,\|U\|_{C^1_tC^2_x},E_0).
\]

The residual estimates are as follows.

* Hardy/Poincaré for a zero-trace field in the strip gives
  $\|u^\nu\|_{L^2(\Gamma_{c\nu})}^2\le C_{\Omega,c}\nu^2\|\nabla u^\nu\|_{L^2(\Gamma_{c\nu})}^2$.
  Thus $|\int (u^\nu\otimes u^\nu):\nabla v^\nu|\le C D_K^\nu$, using $\|\nabla v^\nu\|_\infty\le C_K/\nu$.
* Cauchy–Schwarz and $\|\nabla v^\nu\|_{L^2_{t,x}}\le C_K(T/\nu)^{1/2}$ give
  $|\nu\int\nabla u^\nu:\nabla v^\nu|\le C_K\sqrt{T}\sqrt{D_K^\nu}$.
* Energy control and the $L^2$ corrector bounds make the endpoint terms $\int u^\nu(t)\cdot v^\nu(t)$ and $\int u_0\cdot v^\nu(0)$, and the term $\int u^\nu\cdot\partial_t v^\nu$, all $O(\nu^{1/2})$.
* The Euler viscous cross term satisfies
  $|\nu\int\nabla u^\nu:\nabla U|
  \le(\nu\int\|\nabla u^\nu\|_2^2)^{1/2}
     (\nu\int\|\nabla U\|_2^2)^{1/2}
  \le C\nu^{1/2}$
  by the global energy inequality.
* The uncorrected convection is $-\int(w^\nu\otimes w^\nu):\nabla U$, bounded by $2MX^\nu$. The $U\otimes w^\nu$ term integrates to zero because $\nabla\cdot w^\nu=0$ and $w^\nu\cdot n=0$.

Gronwall gives the explicit modulus

\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}X^\nu(t)
 \le e^{2MT}\left[
 X^\nu(0)+C_1\left(\nu^{1/2}+D_K^\nu(T;c)+\sqrt{D_K^\nu(T;c)}\right)
 \right].
\]

Hence $D_K^\nu(T;c)\to0$ is sufficient for strong $L^\infty_tL^2_x$ convergence. The estimate displays every vanishing-viscosity term; its constant depends only on fixed geometry, time, Euler regularity and the initial energy.

For necessity, the global Leray–Hopf inequality and Euler energy conservation give, at almost every comparison time $t$,

\[
 \nu\int_0^t\int_\Omega|\nabla u^\nu|^2\,dx\,ds
 \le\frac12\|u_0\|_2^2-\frac12\|u^\nu(t)\|_2^2.
\]

Strong convergence to energy-conserving $U$ makes the right side tend to zero. The strip dissipation is bounded by the whole-domain dissipation, so $D_K^\nu(T;c)\to0$. For essential-time convergence choose a valid comparison time just beyond $T$ and restrict the nonnegative dissipation integral back to $[0,T]$.

## Energy/enstrophy test

The basic uniform estimate is

\[
 \sup_{t\le T}\|u^\nu(t)\|_2^2
 +2\nu\int_0^T\|\nabla u^\nu(t)\|_2^2\,dt
 \le\|u_0\|_2^2.
\]

It yields only $0\le D_K^\nu(T;c)\le\frac12\|u_0\|_2^2$, an $O(1)$ bound rather than $o(1)$. It does not locate dissipation relative to the wall. If a genuinely viscosity-uniform enstrophy estimate is available, such as $\sup_{\nu,t\le T}\|\nabla u^\nu(t)\|_2^2\le C_E$, then

\[
 D_K^\nu(T;c)\le\nu T C_E\longrightarrow0.
\]

More generally, $\int_0^T\|\nabla u^\nu\|_2^2dt\le C\nu^{-\alpha}$ with fixed $\alpha<1$ implies $D_K^\nu\le C\nu^{1-\alpha}\to0$. A natural $O(\nu^{-1})$ integrated-enstrophy bound gives only $O(1)$. The uniform $H^1$ estimate is much stronger than Leray energy control and is not available generically for no-slip vanishing-viscosity families.

## Exact shear check and failure-example status

On the periodic channel $\mathbb T_L\times(0,1)$, take

\[
 u^\nu(y,t)=(v^\nu(y,t),0),\quad
 \partial_t v^\nu=\nu\partial_{yy}v^\nu,\quad
 v^\nu(0,t)=v^\nu(1,t)=0,\quad v^\nu(y,0)=1.
\]

The nonlinear term vanishes. The same-data Euler shear $U=(1,0)$ with constant pressure is stationary and impermeable. The initial tangential trace mismatch is allowed for the $L^2$ Leray datum. The exact sine series is

\[
 v^\nu(y,t)=\frac4\pi\sum_{k=0}^\infty
 \frac{\sin((2k+1)\pi y)}{2k+1}e^{-\nu(2k+1)^2\pi^2t}.
\]

Equivalently, $v^\nu(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta_D}1$. Strong continuity of the Dirichlet heat semigroup gives

\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u^\nu(t)-U\|_2
 \le L^{1/2}\sup_{0\le s\le\nu T}\|e^{s\Delta_D}1-1\|_{L^2(0,1)}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

Near one wall the half-line heat profile is $v(y,t)=\operatorname{erf}(y/(2\sqrt{\nu t}))$, so $|\partial_yv|^2=(\pi\nu t)^{-1}e^{-y^2/(2\nu t)}$. Its Kato-strip contribution is bounded by

\[
 \frac{L}{\pi}\int_0^T\frac1t\int_0^{c\nu}e^{-y^2/(2\nu t)}\,dy\,dt
 \le C_{L,c}\nu\bigl(1+\log_+(T/\nu)\bigr)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Indeed, for $t\le\nu$ the inner integral is $O(\sqrt{\nu t})$, and for $t\ge\nu$ it is at most $c\nu$. The opposite wall has the same bound. The method-of-images heat kernel adds far-wall Gaussian terms that are exponentially small in $1/\nu$ on fixed $T$, so the same asymptotic estimate holds on the finite channel. This exact example has strong velocity convergence but no viscosity-uniform integrated enstrophy: its wall gradient is of size $(\nu t)^{-1/2}$ and $\int_0^T\|\nabla u^\nu\|_2^2dt$ grows on the order of $\nu^{-1/2}$. It is a benign boundary mismatch, not a failure of Kato.

A flat-channel scale profile makes the energy estimate's limitation visible. For the fixed strip constant $c$, choose smooth $g$ with $g(0)=-1$ and $g(s)=0$ for $s\ge a$, where $0<a<c$, and set
$u^\nu(y)=(1+g(y/\nu)+g((1-y)/\nu),0)$ for $\nu<1/2$.
It is no-slip at both walls and differs from $U=(1,0)$ by squared $L^2$ norm $O(\nu)$. At an instant,
$|\nabla u^\nu|\sim\nu^{-1}$ in a strip of volume $O(\nu)$, so
$\nu\int_{\Gamma_{c\nu}}|\nabla u^\nu|^2\sim1$.
This is only a spatial profile, not an unforced Navier–Stokes trajectory or a violation of Kato. Its added $\nu$-thick perturbation diffuses on time scale $\nu$ and has $O(\nu)$ energy; the constant outer-flow mismatch contributes the benign heat-layer term estimated above. Maintaining the full profile for $O(1)$ time as a steady shear would require a body force $f^\nu=-\nu\partial_{yy}u^\nu$ of size $O(\nu^{-1})$ in the layer, outside the common bounded-force baseline.

Thus the requested actual failure example remains open in this leaf. Schröder and Wiedemann report in their February 2026 paper that, to their knowledge, no rigorous example violating Kato is known for homogeneous or inhomogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes. The scale profile diagnoses why the global energy bound alone cannot give a small Kato term; it must not be promoted to an unforced counterexample.

## Falsifiers and limits

* **Falsifier of the sufficiency proof:** an admissible family with $D_K^\nu(T;c)\to0$ but nonvanishing $\sup_{t\le T}\|u^\nu-U\|_2$. That would contradict Kato and indicate an incorrect hypothesis or derivation.
* **Candidate that would resolve the failure-example request:** an equal-data, unforced no-slip Navier–Stokes family and smooth Euler limit with $\limsup_{\nu\to0}D_K^\nu(T;c)>0$. By the criterion it must fail strong energy convergence. None is constructed here.
* A true uniform $L^\infty_tL^2_x$ gradient bound forces $D_K=O(\nu)$, so cannot coexist with a positive Kato dissipation limit. Mere global energy control can remain $O(1)$.
* This work establishes no Prandtl expansion, layer stability, turbulence, anomalous dissipation, or failure of Euler uniqueness. No formal proof-assistant check or independent expert review was performed.

## Viscosity and mesh costs

The analytic strip has thickness $c\nu$. The corrector has $L^2$ size $O(\nu^{1/2})$, $L^2$ gradient size $O(\nu^{-1/2})$, and pointwise gradient $O(\nu^{-1})$. No numerical mesh or simulation was used. To diagnose the strip on a uniform $d$-dimensional mesh needs $h\lesssim\nu$; asymptotic resolution calls for $h/\nu\to0$. At $h\asymp\nu$ this is $O(\nu^{-d})$ cells. An explicit method with $O(1)$ characteristic speed has the usual step restriction
$\Delta t\lesssim\min(h/U_{\max},h^2/\nu)=O(\nu)$,
so it uses $O(\nu^{-(d+1)})$ cell updates over fixed $T$. These are scale estimates, not a complexity theorem for a specified solver.
