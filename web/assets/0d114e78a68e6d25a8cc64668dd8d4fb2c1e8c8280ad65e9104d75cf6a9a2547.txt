CHECKS THAT CARRY THE RESULT

1. Inverse slope and estimator (proof §§4, acquisition §§4). For the finite
two-particle trace, the Duhamel second derivative is a sum of nonnegative
terms in an eigenbasis, so z_2(g) is convex and decreasing. Pointwise
finite-chain convergence preserves these properties in the continuum. The
continuum ground-state argument is sound: a nonnegative minimizing vector with
zero collision trace would satisfy the free weak equation; its positive
inner product with the unique positive free ground state then forces it to be
that ground state, whose collision trace is nonzero. Thus E(g) strictly
increases, and the ground term makes z_2(g_+)>z_2(2g_+). Convexity transfers
this endpoint secant to every interval inside [g_-,g_+]. Division by the
public upper bound z_1^* squared gives (4.2) with the stated direction.

The staged d_* search is a genuine finite stopping procedure, conditional on
its imported explicit fixed-two-body rate H_2: the strict continuum gap is
fixed, Euler trace errors and H_2 tend to zero, and the stated test eventually
passes. The later scalar choice of L_0 bounds all L>=L_0, not merely a
subsequence. It is a constructive existence/acquisition proof, not a reported
numerical acquisition. The ratio p_0 p_2/p_1^2 cancels lambda exactly. On the
Hoeffding event, p-hat_1>=p_*/2 and the displayed derivative bounds give the
claimed C_rat. Monotone inversion, finite trace enclosures, clipping, and grid
rounding fit within the enlarged C_loc bound. The Hoeffding union bound uses
exactly 3 events and eta=p/4.

2. Common-form leakage (proof §5). For an A_g eigenvector psi, testing the
weak eigen-equations against an A_h eigenvector gives the stated identity.
The potentially unbounded expression involving V*V psi is legitimate when
read as the bounded functional chi -> <V chi,V psi> on the A_h+1 form norm;
Riesz representation and ||V(A_h+1)^(-1/2)||^2<=1/h give the weighted
coefficient sum bound. This avoids requiring V*V psi to be a Hilbert-space
vector. The scalar inequality (E_j+1)/(E_j-E)^2<=8/R holds for E_j>R,
E<=R/2, R>=1. Also ||V psi||^2<=E/g, yielding (5.1). Splitting the Gibbs
state at R/2 gives (5.2); averaging over Fock sectors is justified by the
finite grand-canonical first-energy moment C_E. I found no hidden kinetic
second-moment assumption.
