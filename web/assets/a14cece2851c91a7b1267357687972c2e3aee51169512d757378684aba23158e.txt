N51 — PROOF-LEVEL AUDIT AND ACQUISITION CONSEQUENCE
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: scoped audit of a local all-field candidate; not external proof certification or priority clearance.

RESULT

N51 is best read as a coefficient-blind, nonadaptive matrix hitting-list claim for noncommutative rational formula trees. Under its strongest local version, the list is generated from the variable count and unary formula-size bound alone, has polynomially many explicitly printed integer entries/bits, and reduces into the prime subfield of every coefficient field. Every nonzero admissible formula has at least one list point where the original tree is defined and its output matrix is invertible.

The most important qualification is provenance. The publicly browsed Sept. 24 OpenAI/math preprint states the polynomial-list theorem over Q. The card’s all-characteristic/all-coefficient-field extension is a further local q-Newton and semisimple-descent proof candidate. I found no counterexample in the supplied audits and independently checked the main interfaces and the acquisition reduction below, but the extension has not had external specialist or formal review, and its full generator has not been executed. Thus the broad theorem and all consequences explicitly marked conditional below remain candidate claims.

FORMULA AND ORACLE INTERFACE

Let F be any commutative coefficient field, n≥1, and let Φ be a rooted formula tree over F in variables x_1,…,x_n. Leaves are variables or constants from F; binary nodes are addition or ordered multiplication; unary nodes are inversion. Tree size counts all leaves and gates, including every inverse occurrence. At a tuple X∈Mat_e(F)^n, constants act as scalar multiples of I_e. Evaluation is defined recursively only when every inverse gate receives an invertible matrix. This is a promise about the original tree: cancellation or multiplication by zero does not erase an inverse-domain condition. For example, 1+0·(x_1x_2−x_2x_1)^{-1} is undefined wherever the commutator matrix is singular, despite representing 1 in the free skew field.

For this hitting statement, use “admissible” for a formula having at least one defined matrix evaluation (allowing an extension field in the witness), and “nonzero” operationally for one having a defined nonzero matrix evaluation. The strong conclusion is that a nonzero admissible Φ has a defined evaluation with invertible output. A zero formula need not be defined at every test point; an exact oracle must distinguish UNDEFINED from a defined zero matrix. Any identification with a free-skew-field element relies on the relevant field/domain theorem, rather than being needed as an extra generator input.

For size s, the local candidate reduces the formula problem to at most s+1 simultaneous feasible affine pencils of order at most B=2s+1. Its claimed stronger primitive is a list H_{n,B} of n-tuples of square integer matrices that hits every feasible family of at most B such pencils: at some list tuple, all pencil evaluations are invertible. The entries of the list are independent of F, its characteristic, all formula constants, and the formula itself. Map integers to F by the prime-subfield homomorphism (ordinary embedding in characteristic zero, reduction modulo p in characteristic p).

Complexity claim, with the bounds supplied in the candidate: on unary (n,B), a fixed deterministic Turing machine prints the whole list in poly(n+B) bit operations; list length, each tuple dimension, entry bit lengths, and total dense-output bits are all poly(n+B). The constants in these polynomials are not a practical-runtime guarantee. Formula constants and their encodings are not inputs to this generator. Evaluation-oracle time, field arithmetic, and response encoding are separate costs and can depend on hidden coefficient heights.

WHAT THE HIT LIST LETS AN ORACLE LEARN

With an oracle that accepts each listed matrix tuple and returns either UNDEFINED or the exact output matrix (a definedness bit plus a zero/nonzero bit also suffices for identity testing), scan the list. A zero free-field formula has only zero defined outputs. For a nonzero admissible formula, the strong hit guarantees a defined invertible, hence nonzero, output. This is deterministic nonadaptive rational identity testing in the matrix-evaluation oracle model, with polynomially many queries and polynomial query-list encoding. It is a decision acquisition: the list alone does not reveal the hidden coefficient values or gate tree. Full-output query and response costs remain charged.

A stronger, concrete acquisition consequence is available conditionally on N51. Suppose the oracle returns full exact matrix values and supports exact field operations/equality. For one unknown admissible size-s formula, a learner can reconstruct a polynomial-order affine matrix-inverse descriptor

    Φ(x) = α (C − Σ_i D_i x_i)^{-1} β,

such that the displayed pencil is invertible at every tuple where the original formula tree is defined and the descriptor returns Φ there. The descriptor is a linear representation; this does not claim a polynomial-size scalar formula using only +, ordered multiplication, and inverse gates. The inverse-domain guarantee is one-way containment of the original domain in the descriptor’s domain.

Proof sketch and audit gates for this consequence:

1. Generate H_{n,2s+1} and query until finding a defined centre A. For this search use only the formula’s inverse-node domain pencils, not a root augmentation requiring Φ(A) invertible; this handles formulas whose value is zero. There are at most s such feasible pencils, and their simultaneous hit makes every original inverse gate defined at A. Let d be the centre dimension; d is polynomial in n,s under N51.

2. Replace each x_i by A_i plus a generic matrix perturbation with d² independent letters. The (1,1) output entry is a proper recognizable noncommutative series g in k=nd² letters. A domain-preserving affine realization of the original formula has order at most s+1, so this series has a linear representation of state dimension at most R=(s+1)d.

3. Every residual combination of this series has dimension at most R and, if nonzero, has a nonzero coefficient on a word of length <R. Its truncation P_f to lengths <R has a polynomial-size affine inverse realization (order O(R²)). N51 applied to that realization supplies a finite feature map: evaluate residuals on Z_j⊗J_R for every tuple Z in a second hitting list, and flatten the resulting matrices. If P_f is nonzero, some list point makes P_f(Z) invertible; therefore not all homogeneous components vanish, and one upper block diagonal of f(Z⊗J_R) is nonzero. Thus the feature map is linear and injective on the unknown residual space.

4. Each feature is obtainable from the original oracle. For a prefix word p, query upper block-triangular perturbation matrices whose first superdiagonals force p and whose final diagonal block is the nilpotent-tail tuple. The cross block of the response is the corresponding left residual evaluated on the tail. Every diagonal block at every inverse gate equals its value at A; since those are invertible, all these post-centre queries are in the original formula’s domain. This is the key domain check: it makes the learner’s observations legal without knowing the hidden gates.

5. Breadth-first linear-algebra discovery over the injective features finds a basis of at most R residuals and their transitions under each of k letters. It therefore recovers a proper realization of g. Substituting the generic-entry perturbation back to x gives the displayed descriptor. For domain preservation, the learned realization is the reachable/unobservable quotient of the hidden proper realization. At an original-domain tuple the hidden pencil is invertible (it is obtained from the original domain-preserving affine realization and the regular centre). In a basis adapted to the invariant reachable and unobservable spaces, the learned pencil is a diagonal block of this invertible triangular matrix, hence is itself invertible. This avoids relying on equality of rational functions alone, which would not settle domains.

If L0 is the centre-list length, L1 the second-list length, and e_max its largest dimension, the proposed learner uses at most L0+(1+kR)L1+R original-oracle queries, each of dimension at most dR(R+1)e_max. These are polynomial in n,s assuming N51’s output bounds. Over an abstract field the cost is polynomial field operations plus the oracle costs. Over Q, total bit bounds additionally depend on hidden coefficient height and complete response lengths; no coefficient-height-free runtime is claimed. This acquisition consequence is itself conditional on N51 and on the stated exact full-matrix oracle. The local reduced toy replay (70 rational cases for one small example) is a diagnostic, not validation of the universal learner or generator.

A simpler consequence of the affine-pencil primitive is exact noncommutative-rank acquisition for rectangular affine pencils: introduce block-completion variables U,V so that a square completion C_t is invertible exactly when rank A(X)≥te, project the universal list to the original variables, and maximize rank(A(X))/e over its tuples. This yields an exact rank-only-oracle consequence when the usual blow-up characterization/attainment is available. The published Q construction states this rank corollary; extending it to arbitrary fields again depends on the local all-field pencil theorem and the matching field-extension rank characterization.

SHARP BOUNDARIES

This does not test ordinary commutative PIT. The list points are generally noncommuting matrices. The commutative polynomial x_1x_2−x_2x_1 is identically zero after imposing commutativity, yet its syntactic noncommutative lift is a nonzero commutator and has nonzero matrix evaluations. Thus applying the list to the matrix semantics of the same syntax can return a false positive for a commutative identity. Restricting all points to commuting/scalar tuples is not a repair: the noncommutative commutator then vanishes everywhere. A separate commuting-point hitting result or a different interface is needed. This also gives the sharp reason the result cannot be relabeled as a solution to general commutative PIT. General arithmetic circuits add a further gap: their shared DAGs can expand exponentially when converted to formula trees.

The all-field coefficient quantifier also creates an unavoidable list-length lower bound. If a list has h≤B tuples over a base field, pass to its algebraic closure and choose an eigenvalue a_j of the first matrix in tuple j. The feasible order-one pencil x_1−a_j is singular at tuple j, so no tuple hits all h pencils. Hence any list that hits every family of up to B feasible pencils over arbitrary coefficient extensions needs at least B+1 tuples. This is a real obstruction to a single common point or direct-sum compression under the strong quantifier, not a contradiction to the polynomial-list claim.

Polynomial output size is not practical execution. The supplied exact parameter diagnostic reports that already at n=B=1 the large branch has 490,997,805,735,020,350,923,752,387,395,914,902,495,012,994,134,412,855,390,021,875 grid points (about 4.91×10^62) and about 2.06×10^69 dense integer entries; K is about 3.45×10^12. These are parameter-arithmetic diagnostics from the local construction, not a run of the generator. No full promised-size generator execution occurred. Thus one may claim polynomial asymptotic bit generation only if the all-field proof and explicit bounds are correct; one may not claim a usable finite experiment, efficient total PIT, or practical learner execution from those asymptotics.

PROOF STATUS AND PRIMARY COMPARISON

The published Sept. 24 primary preprint, “Polynomial Hitting Lists for Noncommutative Rational Formulas,” states a polynomial-bit rational-matrix list for rational formulas over Q and preserves original-tree domains. The candidate’s all-characteristic statement is not that theorem: it replaces characteristic-zero Wronskian/Taylor-reversal steps with a folded q-Newton/Casoratian expansion, then adds prime-specific separable descent, adjugate normalization, and exceptional-prime lists. The local proof notes give a coherent route and a prior audit reports no counterexample, but the folded Newton filtration inequality and the all-extension semisimple branch count are load-bearing and still need outside review. No full external priority survey was done.

The comparison point for general rational black-box identity testing is Arvind, Chatterjee, and Mukhopadhyay, ECCC TR23-147 revision 6 (2025): it gives deterministic quasipolynomial-size hitting sets for unrestricted inverse nesting and explicitly preserves the formula’s original evaluation domain under depth reduction. Hrubeš and Wigderson, Theory of Computing 11(14) (2015), supplies the rational-formula/free-skew-field and matrix-domain framework. These sources establish the problem context and inherited interface; they do not validate N51’s all-field extension or the conditional learner above.

Audit verdict: the exact oracle boundary and original inverse-domain promise are coherent; the conditional descriptor-learning upgrade has a plausible finite-feature and quotient-domain proof; the commutative-PIT obstruction is decisive. The central all-field hitting theorem remains an unreviewed local proof candidate, not an externally established result. The construction is asymptotically polynomial but computationally unusable at its reported tiny parameter example.
