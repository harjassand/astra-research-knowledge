1. GRAPH THEOREM AND POSITIVE ROOT

Let G be a finite simple r-regular graph, r>=1, with clique number omega>=2.
Let H_k contain two copies of G identified only at their vertex k. Put
 mu=1-1/omega,
 t=[sqrt((r/mu)^2+4r)-r/mu]/2,
 lambda=r+t.
Then t>0 and
 t^2+(r/mu)t-r=0.                                 (G1)
Since t^2>0, (G1) gives t<mu<1. Thus
 rho(A_Hk)<=r+t<r+mu                             (G2)
for every k. Connectedness or vertex transitivity is not required for
this graph inequality. Equality in the first bound holds when G is
balanced complete omega-partite, including omega=2 or parts of size one.
No uniqueness characterization of equality is claimed.

2. ELEMENTARY MOTZKIN-STRAUS INEQUALITY

For any finite simple graph Q of clique number at most q>=1 and any
nonnegative vector z, one has
 z^T A_Q z <= (1-1/q)(sum_i z_i)^2.               (MS)
At q=1 the graph is edgeless and both edge scores vanish.

Proof: normalize sum z=1. If two positive-weight vertices u,v are not
adjacent, transfer all their combined weight to whichever of u,v has
the larger weighted neighbor sum. There is no u-v edge or diagonal
term, so this transfer does not decrease the quadratic objective and
reduces its support. Repeating ends on a clique of size ell<=q.
There the objective is 1-sum_i z_i^2<=1-1/ell<=1-1/q by Cauchy-Schwarz.
Uniform weights on a maximum clique attain equality. This also proves
the exact maximum over the probability simplex, rather than importing
a numerical or relaxed clique bound.

3. NEIGHBORHOOD RESOLVENT AND POSITIVE SUPERSOLUTION

Fix k. Its neighborhood N has r vertices and induced adjacency matrix Q.
Every clique in N extends by k, hence its clique number is at most
omega-1. Put nu=1-1/(omega-1), which is zero for omega=2.
Since Q has maximum degree <=r-1 and lambda>r,
 z=(lambda I-Q)^(-1)1
exists and is entrywise strictly positive, for example by its Neumann
series. Let S=1^T z. Multiplying its defining equation by z gives
 lambda||z||^2-z^T Qz=S.
By (MS) and ||z||^2>=S^2/r,
 S>= (lambda/r-nu)S^2.
The coefficient is positive, so
 S<=r/(lambda-r nu)=r/[t+r/(omega-1)].            (G3)

Let h=(1-t)z>=0. Equation (G1) is equivalently
 t[t+r/(omega-1)]=r(1-t),
so (G3) gives
 sum_{u in N} h_u<=t.                            (G4)

On each copy of G define a positive test vector y by
 y_k=1,
 y_u=(1+h_u)/2 for u in N,
 y_u=1/2 for u outside N union {k}.
The copies agree at their common root. Check every row of A_Hk:

Root: its two neighbor sets give
 (A_Hk y)_k=2 sum_{u in N}(1+h_u)/2
           =r+sum h<=r+t=lambda y_k.

Neighbor u: the one root neighbor has value one, while its other r-1
neighbors have baseline 1/2 and the induced-neighborhood edges add h/2.
Thus
 (A_Hk y)_u=(r+1+(Qh)_u)/2
           =(lambda+lambda h_u)/2=lambda y_u,
using lambda h-Qh=(1-t)1 and lambda+1-t=r+1.

Nonneighbor u: it has no edge to the root. Its r neighbors contribute
baseline r/2 and at most half the total h on N, so
 (A_Hk y)_u<=r/2+(sum h)/2<=lambda/2=lambda y_u.

Hence A_Hk y<=lambda y with y strictly positive. For clarity this
implies rho(A_Hk)<=lambda even for disconnected graphs: conjugate by
diag(y); the resulting nonnegative matrix has row sums at most lambda,
so its spectral radius is at most lambda. This proves (G2) without
assuming a regular neighborhood or silently ignoring distant vertices.

4. BALANCED COMPLETE MULTIPARTITE EQUALITY

Let G have omega parts of common size s. Then r=(omega-1)s, and the
root neighborhood is complete (omega-1)-partite with common size s.
Its adjacency Q is regular of degree (omega-2)s=r nu. Thus z is the
constant vector 1/(lambda-r nu), all inequalities in (G3) are equalities,
and h is constant t/r. Every nonneighbor of k is in its own part and
has all r neighbors in N, making that row inequality equality as well.
The positive vector has values
 root 1, neighbors lambda/(2r), nonneighbors 1/2.
Every row of A_Hk y=lambda y is exact. Hence lambda is its Perron
eigenvalue and rho(A_Hk)=r+t. This agrees with direct equitable-partition
elimination, whose equation for t is (G1).

5. EXACT QUANTUM STAR SECTORS: NO LARGER OMITTED BLOCK

Let d=|V(G)|. For each edge {u,v} use the traceless Hermitian pair
 X_uv=E_uv+E_vu, Y_uv=i(E_uv-E_vu).
Define
 W=sum_A A^T tensor(A tensor I+I tensor A)
on reference A and receivers B,C.

For one pair, transpose reverses the sign of Y and expansion gives
 X^T tensor X+Y^T tensor Y
   =2(E_uv tensor E_uv+E_vu tensor E_vu).
Thus AB terms simultaneously change equal A,B indices along an edge;
AC terms simultaneously change equal A,C indices. No BC term occurs.

For each k the invariant basis block consists of
 {|v,v,k>:v in V(G)} union {|v,k,v>:v in V(G)},
with their common vector |k,k,k> counted once. These are two copies of
G glued at k, and the W matrix in that block is exactly 2 A_Hk.
Blocks belonging to distinct k are disjoint. Every remaining basis
vector has its reference index different from both receiver indices,
so every W term annihilates it. The remaining zero block has dimension
d^3-d(2d-1)=d(d-1)^2.

Consequently the COMPLETE Hermitian spectral decomposition is
 W unitarily permutation-equivalent to
   [direct_sum_k 2 A_Hk] direct_sum 0_{d(d-1)^2}.
Since each H_k is a nonnegative adjacency matrix,
 lambda_max(W)=2 max_k rho(A_Hk)<=2(r+t).          (Q1)
No additional sector can violate this maximum, regardless of graph
connectedness, signs of other adjacency eigenvalues, or receiver symmetry.

6. EXACT PURE SCORE AND TRACIAL ENSEMBLE ATTAINMENT

For a unit vector psi put p_v=|psi_v|^2. Edge phases cancel between the
two frame components:
 <X_uv>_psi^2+<Y_uv>_psi^2=4p_u p_v.
Hence the full pure score is
 F_G(psi)=4 sum_edges p_u p_v=2p^T A_G p.
By (MS) its UNCONSTRAINED maximum is exactly
 max_pure F_G=2mu.                                (Q2)
Uniform modulus on a maximum clique of size omega attains it, with
arbitrary phases. This formula holds for all finite simple graphs,
whether or not they are regular or vertex transitive.

Now suppose G is vertex transitive. Fix a maximum clique C={c_0,...,c_(omega-1)}.
The omega pure Fourier states
 psi_j=omega^(-1/2)sum_l exp(2pi i j l/omega)|c_l>, j=0,...,omega-1,
each attain 2mu; their uniform average state is P_C/omega.
Average this ensemble over the finite automorphism group of G.
Each permutation preserves the frame score. Vertex transitivity gives
the averaged diagonal state I/d, because the total clique support
weight is one and every vertex receives the same weight.
Thus the resulting finite pure ensemble has barycenter I/d and still
scores 2mu. Combining this with (Q2) proves
 k_frame=max_{sum p_y pi_y=I/d}sum_y p_y F_G(pi_y)=2mu. (Q3)
It uses at most omega|Aut(G)| listed states, with duplicates allowed.

The transitivity premise matters for tracial attainment. For a regular
graph without transitivity, only the unconstrained maximum (Q2) and
the upper bound k_frame<=2mu have been established here. No automatic
tracial ensemble attainment is inferred for such a graph.

7. V, CENTERED C2 AND ITS PRECISE SCOPE

Each edge pair has total squared HS norm Tr(X^2)+Tr(Y^2)=4.
With tau=Tr/d and |E|=dr/2,
 V=sum_A tau(A^2)=4|E|/d=2r.                      (Q4)
For a vertex-transitive graph, (Q1)-(Q4) yield the strict operator bound
 W< (V+k_frame)I,
with uniform positive gap at least 2(mu-t).

For ANY normalized positive three-party state R, define
 s_R=Tr(R W)/2.
Then
 2(V-s_R)-(V-k_frame)=V+k_frame-Tr(R W)
                    >=2(mu-t)>0.                (Q5)
In particular this gives the claimed centered C2 inequality for every
tracial three-party extension. If R is the normalized Choi state of a
supplied equal-marginal broadcaster, s_R is exactly
sum_A tau[A Phi(A)]. If the two marginal channels differ, s_R is their
average frame score, not a separate upper bound on each receiver.
No CP/HS-self-adjointness premise beyond the stated Choi interpretation
is needed for the direct positive-state operator estimate.

This is a FIXED uniform graph-frame support/score inequality. It does not
by itself prove an operator comparison I-Gamma<=2(I-Phi) on the whole
matrix algebra or on every positive weighting of the graph span.
One canonical pure ensemble achieving k_frame supplies the aggregate
frame comparator, but arbitrary weighted-frame simultaneous control
would require additional inequalities. No such step is asserted here.

9. OPTIONAL EXACT LEGAL SPECTRAL CONTROL, STILL BEFORE EXPOSURE

For vertex-transitive G, the exact star maximum is also attainable by
a supplied-form tracial equal-marginal broadcaster, with HS-self-adjoint
marginal. Here is an independent construction, requested for the audit.
Use sigma=rho(A_Hk), which is independent of k by transitivity and >r.
The latter follows from the average degree of the root connected
component after gluing. The top eigenvector uses identical copies, with
root coefficient one. Its single-copy coefficient vector solves
 (sigma I-A_G)f=(sigma/2)e_k.
Thus if T=(sigma I-A_G)^(-1), the symmetric nonnegative matrix
 F=(sigma/2)T has F_kk=1 for every k. Transitivity also makes
 q=(F^2)_kk=sum_i F_ki^2 constant. Put N=2q-1>0.

Define one input-to-two-output Kraus operator L_k for each k by
 L_k|k>=|k,k>/sqrt(N),
 L_k|i>=F_ki(|i,k>+|k,i>)/sqrt(N), i!=k.
For each fixed k the columns are orthogonal, and
 sum_k L_k^dagger L_k=I
because its ith diagonal entry is [1+2sum_(k!=i)F_ki^2]/N=(2q-1)/N.
Every L_k is BC symmetric, so B(X)=sum_k L_k X L_k^dagger has two
identical CP TP marginals Phi.

The marginal acts by real Schur multipliers on offdiagonal units,
 Phi(E_ij)=(F^2)_ij E_ij/N, i!=j,
and has population matrix
 P_ii=q/N, P_ji=F_ji^2/N for j!=i.
The population matrix is symmetric, with every row/column sum one.
Thus Phi is unital and TP, and both its population and coherence
blocks are HS-self-adjoint. Symmetry of F and constancy of q are
essential; they have not been replaced by a trace-preservation claim.

The corresponding normalized Choi vectors are
 psi_k=|k,k,k>+sum_(i!=k)F_ki(|i,i,k>+|i,k,i>),
R=(dN)^(-1)sum_k |psi_k><psi_k|.
Each psi_k has norm squared N and is a top eigenvector of its W block,
so Tr(R W)=2sigma and s_Phi=sigma. All three single marginals are I/d
by the proved TP/unital properties. This is a legal tracial witness,
not an unconstrained Perron eigenvector substituted for a broadcaster.

Balanced complete omega-partite graphs have sigma=r+t and hence
attain the graph/star upper bound exactly. Their centered frame ratio is
 (V-k)/(V-s)=2(r-mu)/(r-t)<2.
For balanced complete bipartite graphs with r tending to infinity,
mu=1/2 and t=sqrt(r(r+1))-r tends to1/2. The ratio tends to two.
Therefore no uniform constant smaller than two can cover this family
of graph-frame centered comparisons, although each finite member has
the strict quantitative gap proved in Section 7. This is fixed-frame
sharpness, not a weighted-frame or full-domain comparator theorem.
