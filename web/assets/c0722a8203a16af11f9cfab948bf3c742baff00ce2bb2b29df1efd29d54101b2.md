# Hard-feasibility inverse design: a shared phase-hull oracle

**Workstream:** engineering control / materials and energy  
**Status:** mathematical counterexample and interval rule reconstructed; application and priority unverified  
**Scope:** maximize a target material property subject to an energy-above-hull limit and other hard constraints. This report does not claim a nature-level synthesizability guarantee.

## Finding

A hard stability constraint is not naturally a pointwise label on the proposed crystal. For a candidate at composition (c), its energy above hull depends on the best mixture of *all competing phases* at (c), including unproposed polymorphs. A new low-energy competitor can change the feasibility labels of many candidates at once. The expensive oracle is therefore a shared phase-energy service, not one independent feasibility query per proposed candidate.

This gives an exact obstruction: with M unqueried possible competing polymorphs at one composition, an exact point-energy oracle cannot certify a candidate's hull feasibility in fewer than M worst-case queries. With uniform decision error at most δ<1/2, a randomized algorithm needs expected at least M(1−2δ) competitor queries in the all-zero world. Supplied complete phase data or sound lower bounds for every unseen competitor avoid the obstruction, but acquiring and validating them has a cost.

A useful positive interface follows. If every phase energy has a valid interval, two linear programs give lower and upper bounds on a candidate's competing-phase hull. These bounds yield robust `FEASIBLE`, `INFEASIBLE`, or `UNKNOWN` decisions, and a single queried phase updates the hull bounds for all candidate compositions. This is a route to cost-aware, hull-aware acquisition; it is not implemented or empirically validated here.

## Exact task interface

Let a proposed design (x) specify a composition, crystal structure, and any process variables needed for the downstream engineering objective. Let (\mathcal P) be the universe of candidate and competing phases; (c_y\in\mathbb Q^d) is the normalized elemental-composition vector of phase (y), and (E_y) is its relaxed formation energy per atom under a single declared DFT functional, pseudopotential, reference-energy convention, and numerical setup. A given phase energy is the result of structural relaxation, not an abstract free label.

For (x\in\mathcal P), define the best competing-phase mixture while excluding (x):

\[
H^{-x}(c_x)=\min_{\lambda\ge0}\left\{\sum_{y\ne x}\lambda_yE_y:\sum_{y\ne x}\lambda_y=1,\quad\sum_{y\ne x}\lambda_yc_y=c_x\right\}.
\]

If no competing mixture exists, take (H^{-x}=+\infty). The energy-above-hull gap is
\[
\Delta_x=\max\{0,E_x-H^{-x}(c_x)\}.
\]
A representative hard-design problem is
\[
\max_{x\in\mathcal X} f(x)\quad\text{subject to}\quad \Delta_x\le\tau,\quad g_j(x)\le0\;(j=1,\ldots,J),
\]
where (f) could be capacity, conductivity, strength, or another application property; (g_j) includes hard composition, force-convergence, thermal, toxicity, cost, geometric, or process constraints; and τ is a declared metastability allowance. A (50\,\mathrm{meV/atom}) cutoff is used in recent materials-design work, but that numerical threshold is a modeling convention, not a universal synthesizability theorem.

The query/action interface must distinguish:

- **Supplied:** a versioned seed phase database, its structures, energies and calculation settings; candidate-generator weights or a fixed candidate pool; and any prior model or calibration data. A database hull is only the hull of its supplied phases.
- **Acquired:** generated structures; failed or successful relaxations; low-fidelity interatomic-potential energies; high-fidelity DFT energies and forces; phase-competition energies; and objective-specific calculations such as hybrid-functional gaps, phonons, or diffusion barriers. Experimental synthesis and characterization are separate later acquisitions.
- **Output:** a candidate with the objective evidence, a competing-phase mixture witness, hard-constraint margins, and a sound feasibility state. Return `UNKNOWN` when acquired information cannot support a decision. A (0\,\mathrm K) DFT hull result does not by itself establish kinetic accessibility, finite-temperature stability, or a reproducible synthesis route.
- **Budget:** charge each generated structure and each evaluation. At minimum record (C_{\rm gen}), (C_{\rm MLIP}), (C_{\rm DFT}(n_{\rm atom},E_{\rm cut},k\text{-mesh},\text{SCF/relax tolerance})), (C_f), hull/LP runtime, surrogate fitting and acquisition-optimizer runtime, storage, and total CPU/GPU node-hours and energy. If an experiment is part of the target, charge synthesis/characterization separately. Equal iteration counts are not equal resource budgets.

For an offline DFT search, querying an infeasible structure wastes compute but is not a physical safety violation. If the “hard” constraint means that an experiment or controller may never enter an unsafe state, safe Bayesian optimization needs a supplied safe seed and a sound reachable-safe-set rule; the ordinary unknown-constraint BO baseline below does not provide that guarantee.

## Recent primary literature and informed controls

The supplied full 12-page arXiv version 2 of Tahmassebpur et al., [“A geometric basis for materials families in inorganic solids”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00254v2) (13 June 2026), is directly relevant. It defines formation energy at composition (c) as the minimum over locally stable atomic arrangements and notes that identifying it requires search over structures. From a Materials Project set, the authors retain one lowest-energy structure per composition, keep 51,068 compounds within (0.025\,\mathrm{eV/atom}) of the database hull, and fit a seven-facet max-affine model in 92 composition coordinates. Its random 80/20 split has reported test MAE (0.076\,\mathrm{eV/atom}). The method uses Adam for 1,600 epochs with batch size 512; the manuscript does not report a comparable end-to-end GPU time or the cost of acquiring the underlying Materials Project data. The authors also report that the seven-facet model captures trends in defects and elemental mixing, while stating that absolute defect-energy errors exceed DFT accuracy, that sparse composition regions blur facet assignments, and that finite-temperature entropy is absent.

The seven-facet result is a promising representation prior, not a hard stability oracle. The (76\,\mathrm{meV/atom}) *mean* energy error is already larger than the (50\,\mathrm{meV/atom}) cutoff used in recent screening, and a random-record MAE is not a uniform or one-sided bound on any candidate or competing phase. It does not certify composition-family transfer, phase completeness, or the error of a candidate-minus-hull difference. The paper is an arXiv preprint in this source snapshot; its empirical claims remain source-reported here.

For the strongest defensible point-label BO control, Hickman et al., [“Anubis: Bayesian optimization with unknown feasibility constraints for scientific experimentation”](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5DD00018A), use a variational GP classifier for binary feasibility and a GP regression surrogate for objective measurements. The paper compares feasibility-weighted, feasibility-interpolated, and feasibility-constrained acquisition rules; for highly constrained hybrid organic-inorganic perovskite design, it recommends the risk-averse constrained-acquisition setting FCA-0.8. It also reports that UCB performed at least as well as EI in its feasibility-aware benchmarks, and uses a genetic acquisition optimizer after SLSQP showed convergence issues. The paper explicitly does not count acquisition-optimizer overhead. This is a strong baseline for the **pointwise unknown-constraint** contract, but it assumes that a candidate's feasibility label can be acquired as a label on that candidate. It does not price or model a shared, incomplete phase-hull oracle.

Alvi et al., [“Deep Gaussian process-based cost-aware batch Bayesian optimization for complex materials design campaigns”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-026-01981-7) (npj Computational Materials, 2026), are a current cost-allocation control: a deep-GP surrogate, heterotopic batch queries, and evaluation cost in an UCB extension are applied to refractory high-entropy alloys. Sinha et al.'s [September 2026 portfolio-based constrained multi-objective BO preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19550) adaptively selects among conventional and feasibility-focused acquisitions and reports in-silico comparisons on five synthetic benchmarks and two materials case studies. These methods improve property/cost allocation or acquisition-policy selection; the cited summaries do not provide a complete-phase lower-bound guarantee. For the next empirical comparison, use the best reproducible combination of these cost-aware controls and Anubis-style risk control, with identical data, candidate generator, high-fidelity query budget, and measured node-hours.

A complementary 2026 battery study, Park et al., [element-mapping BO for NASICON cathodes](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-026-01958-6), reports 16 preferred substitutions in 50 iterations using a Matérn-5/2 GP and UCB to target a voltage window of 2.5–4.3 V. Its score penalizes values outside the window and is zero inside it; this is target-window optimization, not a hard hull-feasibility guarantee. [InvDesFlow-AL](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-025-01830-z) combines DPA-2 relaxation, FormEGNN energy prediction, force-convergence rejection, and hull-based selection. It illustrates a multi-stage workflow but does not provide a cost ledger that makes candidate generation, relaxations, hull calls, and high-fidelity calculations comparable on equal total resources.

## Quantitative result 1: stability conditioning under uniform energy error

For a fixed finite competitor set and composition (c), write
\[
H_E(c)=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda(c)}\lambda^TE,
\]
where every λ is a convex mixture. If (\lVert E-E'\rVert_\infty\le\epsilon), then for every feasible λ, |λᵀ(E-E')|≤ε because its entries are nonnegative and sum to one. Taking minima in both directions gives
\[
|H_E(c)-H_{E'}(c)|\le\epsilon.
\]
The competing-hull gap (\Delta_x=\max(0,E_x-H^{-x}_E(c_x))) is therefore (2\epsilon)-stable:
\[
|\Delta_x(E)-\Delta_x(E')|\le2\epsilon.
\]
Thus a uniform, simultaneous energy-error bound ε permits robust threshold classification only at margin greater than (2\epsilon). If an approximate gap is Δ-hat, `FEASIBLE` is certified when Δ-hat ≤ τ − 2ε; `INFEASIBLE` is certified when Δ-hat > τ + 2ε. Near a facet switch, the identity of the supporting facet can change under arbitrarily small perturbations, but the hull value remains Lipschitz; acquisition should target gap uncertainty, not facet-identity stability.

This bound needs a uniform error envelope over the candidate and all relevant competitors. It cannot be inferred from a test-set MAE of (76\,\mathrm{meV/atom}). A distribution-free Markov bound using that MAE is vacuous for a (50\,\mathrm{meV/atom}) per-candidate margin. Even under the stronger, unsupported simplification that (76\,\mathrm{meV}) were a uniform per-energy error, the induced gap error could be (152\,\mathrm{meV/atom}), three times the example threshold.

## Quantitative result 2: hidden-polymorph query lower bound

**Proposition (phase-oracle lower bound).** Fix a candidate (x) at composition (c_0=(1/2,1/2)), with known energy (E_x=0), and known endpoint phases at compositions ((1,0)) and ((0,1)), both with energy 0. Fix a threshold τ≥0 and η>0. There are (M) possible competing polymorphs (z_1,\ldots,z_M), all at (c_0). The oracle returns the exact relaxed energy of one queried phase at cost (c_E); no sound lower bounds for unqueried (z_j) are supplied.

Compare world (W_0), where every (E_{z_j}=0), with world (W_j), where (E_{z_j}=-(\tau+\eta)) and all other (z_k) have energy 0. In (W_0), (\Delta_x=0) and (x) is feasible. In (W_j), the competing hull at (c_0) is at most −(τ+η), so (\Delta_x\ge\tau+\eta) and (x) is infeasible.

Any deterministic exact-query algorithm that must correctly decide feasibility in every world needs all (M) competitor queries in the worst case. If a randomized algorithm has error probability at most δ on (W_0) and each (W_j), for δ<1/2, then its expected number of competitor queries on (W_0) is at least (M(1-2\delta)). Consequently, with equal per-phase cost, its expected phase-energy spend is at least (c_E M(1-2\delta)). This is a lower bound for deciding one candidate; if one phase participates in hulls for many target candidates, its acquisition cost is shared across all of them.

**Proof.** In (W_0), let (F) be the event that the randomized algorithm outputs `FEASIBLE`; (\Pr(F)\ge1-\delta). For each (j), until the query to (z_j), the transcript in (W_j) is identical to the transcript in (W_0). Correctness in (W_j) therefore implies (\Pr(F\cap\{z_j\text{ is not queried in }W_0\})\le\delta); otherwise the algorithm would output the same false-feasible decision in (W_j). Hence (\Pr(z_j\text{ queried in }W_0)\ge\Pr(F)-\delta\ge1-2\delta). Summing over j gives the expected-query lower bound. For a deterministic algorithm with zero error, leaving any (z_j) unqueried makes (W_0) and (W_j) indistinguishable, so it cannot decide correctly in both.

The proposition is an internal theorem about the declared exact-energy-oracle model. It does not apply if the full competing-phase hull is supplied, a certified lower bound rules out all unseen low-energy phases, a validated structure-search oracle supplies those bounds, or the output may remain `UNKNOWN`. Those are alternate information interfaces whose acquisition cost must be stated. It is not a claim that practical searches always require exhaustive structure enumeration.

## Interval-hull algorithm sketch

Suppose every phase y in the declared finite universe has simultaneous valid bounds (L_y\le E_y\le U_y). For a candidate x, solve two LPs over its competitors:
\[
H^-_x=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{-x}(c_x)}\sum_{y\ne x}\lambda_yL_y,
\qquad
H^+_x=\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{-x}(c_x)}\sum_{y\ne x}\lambda_yU_y.
\]
Then (H^-_x\le H^{-x}_E(c_x)\le H^+_x). A sound robust decision is:

- `FEASIBLE` if (U_x-H^-_x\le\tau);
- `INFEASIBLE` if (L_x-H^+_x>\tau);
- `UNKNOWN` otherwise.

These are sufficient interval conditions, not generally necessary. They are exact with respect to the supplied intervals and finite phase universe. For valid (1-\delta) simultaneous confidence coverage, the decision is valid with at least that coverage; pointwise marginal intervals need a joint-coverage correction. Every newly acquired phase-energy interval changes the LP for every composition it can support. This is the central structural difference from independent per-candidate constraint classifiers.

A cost-aware follow-on policy would select among objective evaluations, candidate relaxations, and competing-phase evaluations by expected reduction in the best feasible objective bound per total query cost, while accounting for each phase query's shared impact on the hull. If no candidate is robustly feasible yet, it can prioritize queries that shrink hull-gap intervals for promising candidates. This acquisition rule is only a specification here; no convergence or utility theorem is claimed.

## Falsification and bounded next experiment

The immediate falsifier is information-contract based: if an exact hard-feasibility label can be returned for the single proposed structure while its (M) possible competing polymorphs remain unqueried and no sound lower bounds/full hull are supplied, the proposition's oracle model is wrong or the label is relying on hidden supplied information. Document that service and charge it.

A discriminating materials experiment should use a phase family with multiple known polymorphs and deliberately withhold competing phases by prototype or chemical-system family. Use the same versioned seed database, candidate generator, target objective, and total DFT budget for:

1. a strong pointwise control: Anubis-style variational-GP feasibility model with FCA-0.8 for the hard-risk setting, paired with current cost-aware batch DGP/UCB allocation (and optionally the recent portfolio policy); and
2. a hull-aware control: calibrated energy intervals, shared hull LP bounds, and acquisition that charges each competitor query once and shares its value across designs.

Report false-feasible rate at the declared τ, false-infeasible rate, number of `UNKNOWN` outputs, best target property among robustly feasible candidates, candidate relaxations and competitor relaxations separately, and wall-clock/CPU/GPU node-hours and energy. Calibrate intervals on a disjoint chemical-system/prototype holdout; random row splits are not an adequate transfer test. The positive control uses a supplied complete hull, with all phase-acquisition costs explicitly listed. A genuine hard synthesis or controller safety test is a separate later experiment.

The hull-aware acquisition is useful only if it reduces false-feasible outputs or reaches equal confidence with fewer total DFT and resource costs than the strongest equal-information pointwise control. If it merely changes candidate rankings while all methods lack calibrated lower bounds, it does not solve hard feasibility.
