EXACT STATEMENT

For every integer q>=2, choose any finite-dimensional traceless Hermitian
Clifford generators Gamma_1,...,Gamma_q satisfying
  Gamma_i Gamma_j+Gamma_j Gamma_i=2 delta_ij I_d.
Let A_i=Gamma_i, L=qI_d, Y=(q-1)I_d, and take the legal product-tracial
Choi state R=tau_d tensor tau_d tensor tau_d, tau_d=I_d/d. Thus Phi is
the depolarizing channel and the supplied broadcaster outputs tau_d
tensor tau_d on every input. Its marginal Phi is unital, TP,
HS-self-adjoint and entanglement breaking. R has full support and all
the required symmetries and marginals.

On BC put
  M_i=(Gamma_i tensor I+I tensor Gamma_i)/2,
  rho_BC=I_(d^2)/d^2.
For EVERY POVM {F_z} and real labels x_z with finite second moment,
  D_i=sum_z x_(zi) F_z=M_i,
  N=sum_z ||x_z||^2 F_z,
one has
  Tr(rho_BC N)>=q^2/4,                       (A1)
while the available frame energy is V=Tr(L)/d=q. The same conclusion
holds for general outcome spaces by replacing sums with integrals.

Since q is arbitrary, for each proposed finite constant K choose q>4K.
Then no such unbiased POVM or commuting dilation can satisfy
  Tr(rho_BC N)<=K V.
Consequently no dimension-free constant-cost lemma retaining these exact
unshrunk first moments can prove even a nonsharp gate via this route.

FULL PROOF OF THE SECOND-MOMENT LOWER BOUND

For every real u, anticommutation gives
  (sum_i u_i Gamma_i)^2=||u||^2 I_d.
Thus for every density matrix sigma, its vector
r_i=Tr(sigma Gamma_i) has norm at most one: for any unit u,
  |u dot r|<=||sum_i u_i Gamma_i||=1.
For any density matrix on BC, the M-expectation vector is the mean of
the two local Clifford-expectation vectors, so it too has norm at most
one. This statement includes entangled BC states.

For each nonzero POVM effect set
  p_z=Tr(F_z)/d^2,
  r_(zi)=Tr(F_z M_i)/Tr(F_z).
Then p_z>=0, sum_z p_z=1, and ||r_z||<=1. Because Gamma_i is traceless,
  M_i^2=(I tensor I+Gamma_i tensor Gamma_i)/2,
  Tr(M_i^2)/d^2=1/2.
The exact first moment equations therefore imply
  sum_z p_z x_z dot r_z
    =sum_i Tr[M_i sum_z x_(zi)F_z]/d^2
    =sum_i Tr(M_i^2)/d^2=q/2.
Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz now gives
  q^2/4
    <=(sum_z p_z ||x_z||^2)(sum_z p_z ||r_z||^2)
    <=sum_z p_z ||x_z||^2
    =Tr(rho_BC N).
This proves (A1). Zero effects contribute nothing. If the second moment
is infinite, the lower bound is automatic. No assumption about finite
outcome count, separability of the effects, or dimension of the dilation
was used. Since rho_BC has full support, merely restricting the equations
to that support does not weaken them.

For a commuting self-adjoint dilation with an isometry V0 and
V0^* N_i V0=M_i, its joint spectral measure compresses to the POVM F.
Its compressed total second moment V0^*(sum_i N_i^2)V0 is the N above.
The same lower bound therefore holds for every such dilation, whether
finite or infinite dimensional, provided the indicated moments exist.

The ratio forced by this route is at least q/4. For the explicit even
family d=2^m, q=2m, it is at least (log2 d)/2. This is a lower bound on
UNBIASED DILATION COST ONLY. It is not a lower bound on the optimal EB
comparison constant or on every state-sensitive centered rounding method.

WHY THE ORIGINAL GATE IS NOT FALSIFIED

For the depolarizing marginal, s_Phi=0, so V-s_Phi=q. The scalar dual
pencil for Y=(q-1)I has least eigenvalue (||x||-1)^2, hence is admissible.
Its objective q-1 is far below Tr(RH)=2q. In fact the depolarizing Phi
itself is already an EB comparator with factor one. The growing lower
bound in (A1) is entirely an artifact of requiring exact averaged quantum
first moments on the two output registers.

The precise biased/correlation-sensitive route (CR) in PROOF.txt is not
refuted: here Tr(RW)=0, and the single outcome label x=0 satisfies its
required correlation inequality immediately. A successful all-frame
rounding theorem can therefore avoid this obstruction by respecting the
actual R correlations instead of reproducing every M_i exactly.
