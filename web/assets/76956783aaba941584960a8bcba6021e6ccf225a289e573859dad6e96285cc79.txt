CYCLE 3 SYNTHESIS — RANK-FREE TASK-RISK ACQUISITION FROM RAW PREDICTIVE STREAMS
Date: 8 October 2026
Status: one conditional finite-H HMM oracle theorem, two risk-based compression/certificate results in restricted classes, and scoped impossibility/counterexample results. All new proofs are internal candidates; novelty, priority, and external correctness remain UNKNOWN.

VERDICT

The broad goal remains open: no result here learns and certifies an efficient predictive representation for arbitrary noisy finite-state processes from one raw path when mixing, emission, context-mass, or task-horizon controls are unavailable. A crucial prior-art correction changes how to rank this cycle. Han, Jiang and Wu already prove minimax-optimal expected terminal next-symbol prediction for the entire stationary HMM class with fixed known hidden-state and observation-space sizes, without mixing, rank, identifiability, or probability-floor assumptions, using dynamic programming in n^{O(k^2+kℓ)} time. Therefore the finite-cover theorem below is not a general advance in rank-free HMM prediction and does not beat that baseline. It is a fully explicit, independently derived finite-H block oracle inequality for a different loss and learner protocol: a predictor selected once, then scored on a fresh context of fixed finite depth and a joint H-symbol future, under supplied Doeblin/emission floors.

The main finite-cover result is useful as a transparent conditional guarantee, not a ceiling claim. For HMM state count d, alphabet q, context h and future horizon H, a cover of size N_d is searched, B spaced length-(h+H) blocks are scored, and the raw path costs B(h+H)+(B-1)g symbols, with g of order rho^-1 log(B/delta). Its known minorization rho and emission floor e, fresh finite-block target, and exponential search distinguish it from HJW terminal prediction. A concrete advantage of this result is its explicit high-probability, finite-H oracle guarantee for the frozen fitted model; HJW's theorem gives expected terminal one-step risk, not a high-confidence post-selection certificate. Fully observable finite Markov chains admit a separate rank-free K-group stationary-risk compression result under known mixing and a predictive probability floor.

The defensible addition is an explicit bound and cost ledger for frozen-model finite-H risk, plus restricted observable-Markov risk aggregation and several counterexamples. It does not improve the HJW result at terminal one-symbol expected prediction. It does not solve gap-free rank acquisition, unknown-mixing high-confidence certification, finite-H frozen-model adaptation, all-history reusable HMM realization, or efficient compression. The evidence does not support a field-breaking or priority claim.

1. INTERFACE AND TARGETS — KEEP THESE DISTINCT

The target varies across results. The lead theorem below learns expected conditional KL for a stationary fresh context of a fixed length h and an H-symbol continuation. The separate certificate theorem audits a selected predictor on another fresh stationary finite-block draw. These are not predictions after the terminal observed training path. Several lower bounds concern a fresh context drawn independently after training; they do not transfer to realized-history filtering. Other results explicitly study continuation from the observed terminal transcript, but either increase the future horizon with sample length or use a specified fixed truncation. A stationary random-time or cumulative-risk theorem is not an endpoint lower bound unless the conversion is proved. The reports state each interface at its proof.

Likewise, worst-supported-context loss and stationary-context average KL are different. A context of mass p can force constant worst-context TV/KL but contribute only p times its local loss to stationary average risk. Conversely, a risk theorem for a fresh stationary context need not control the endpoint context of the same training trajectory. No synthesis below moves between these criteria without an explicit argument.

Legal acquisition throughout the main results means one or more actual raw observed paths and the declared task input. A latent label, conditional-probability service, Hankel entry oracle, externally supplied feature dictionary, or free independent reset is not smuggled in. Where independent episodes or supplied side information are used, that boundary is recorded in the corresponding leaf.

2. LEAD THEOREM: FINITE-H PREDICTIVE-KL ORACLE INEQUALITY WITHOUT RANK

Let the raw alphabet have q>=2 symbols. A stationary HMM has d hidden states, transition A_ij>=rho/d for every i,j, and emission E_i(a)>=e for every state and symbol, where 0<rho<1 and 0<e<=1/q are known rational class bounds. The learner sees only one contiguous stationary path X_1:T. Fix a context length h>=0, future horizon H>=1, L=h+H, a state cap D, and confidence 0<delta<1. The loss is

  R_H^h(P,Q) = E_{X_1:L~P}[ KL(P(X_(h+1):L | X_1:h) || Q(X_(h+1):L | X_1:h)) ],

with expectation over a fresh stationary length-L block. No exact rank, SVD gap, emission-row independence, hidden label, probability query, or feature dictionary is supplied. The Doeblin condition gives hidden-state contraction at most 1-rho; the emission floor bounds every candidate H-future log loss by M_H=H log(1/e).

Theorem (known cap). For each d<=D, write each transition row as rho/d times the all-ones row plus (1-rho) times a residual simplex point, and each emission row as e times the all-ones row plus (1-qe) times a residual simplex point. Round residual rows to denominator-K compositions. A sufficient resolution is

  K_d >= max{12 d^2/(rho^2 tau), 12 d(L-1)/(rho tau), 12 L/(e tau)},

for 0<tau<=1 (omit the middle term when L=1). This gives a rational cover of size

  N_d = binom(K_d+d-1,d-1)^d binom(K_d+q-1,q-1)^d.

Let N_D=sum_(d<=D) N_d. Split the single raw path into B length-L blocks with g discarded symbols between selected blocks, where

  g = ceil(rho^-1 log(2(B-1)/delta)),  T_req=B L+(B-1)g   (B>=2).

Choose a cover model with minimum empirical conditional negative log likelihood on those B blocks, with per-candidate certified numerical score error at most zeta. Then with probability at least 1-delta,

  R_H^h(P,Qhat) <= inf_(d<=D,Q in Theta_d(rho,e)) R_H^h(P,Q)
                      + tau + 2 M_H sqrt(log(4N_D/delta)/(2B)) + 2 zeta.

Thus a true in-class model has an absolute finite-H risk bound; there is no exact-rank decision. A same-block saturated conditional table gives a separate oracle bound of order its finite cover complexity and has q^h rows by q^H outcomes. The all-path raw-retaining learner can replay either estimator and additionally use omitted symbols, so no data or information advantage is claimed.

Proof in brief. Doeblin decomposition gives contraction <=1-rho and stationary masses >=rho/d. Entrywise grid perturbation a=1/K_d moves a stationary word log probability of length s by at most

  Lambda_s=phi(d^2/(K_d rho^2))+(s-1)phi(d/(K_d rho))+s phi(1/(K_d e)),
  phi(u)=-log(1-u).

The stated K makes Lambda_L<=tau/2, hence each conditional block log score changes by at most tau under the cover. The selected blocks are separated by g unused symbols; their joint law couples to B iid stationary length-L blocks with mismatch probability at most (B-1)exp(-rho g)<=delta/2. Each candidate score is in [0,M_H]; Hoeffding plus a union bound over N_D candidates gives uniform empirical-to-population error M_H sqrt(log(4N_D/delta)/(2B)) with failure <=delta/2. Empirical minimization, cover approximation, and the two deviations give the inequality. The full coupling, entropy calculation, bit costs, and nonmixing counterexample are in `rank_free_hmm_mle/RESULT.txt`.

For target statistical slack epsilon_stat, it suffices to take B>=2 M_H^2 log(4N_D/delta)/epsilon_stat^2; all gapped symbols count. Forward scoring costs O(B L sum_d N_d d^2) arithmetic operations for exhaustive search, exponential in d^2+dq. Rational transition/emission grid heights, stationary linear solves, forward precision and output storage are charged in the leaf. A cap-free structural-risk variant over d>=1 is proved there only when common known rho,e are supplied; its finite search cutoff follows from a growing penalty, not an unknown-margin adaptation theorem.

The no-mixing counterexample in the same leaf uses a stationary reducible two-state HMM and fresh empty-history one-step risk: two models have a shared raw-path component of mass 1/2 for every path length, but stationary next-symbol laws at TV 1/4. Pinsker and the two-point argument give max expected KL >=1/64 nats. It is not a lower bound for prediction conditioned on an infinite observed past or for endpoint continuation.

3. SECONDARY POSITIVE CERTIFICATES AND TASK-BASED COMPRESSION

The lead's `lead_beta_certificate/RESULT.txt` proves a generic finite-alphabet post-selection certificate when a beta-mixing envelope is known. For alphabet size a, context length L and future length H, let K=a^(L+H), fit any floor-bounded q_S(v|c) from a training prefix, then use M later blocks of L+H symbols with gap g. With r=min{1,sqrt(((K+1)ln2+ln(1/delta))/(2M))}, it gives

  R(q_S) <= min{ln(1/xi), Rhat(q_S)+r ln(1/xi)+2 sup_(u<=r)[h_2(u)+u ln K]}

with probability >=1-delta-M beta(g+1), for expected conditional KL on a fresh stationary block. This is any-predictor post-selection validity, but the envelope is supplied, not estimated. It is exponential in L+H, certifies average stationary context rather than every history, and does not cover terminal continuation. A raw-retaining baseline can replay the selected model and certificate.

`risk_state_aggregation/RESULT.txt` gives a second positive theorem on fully observable stationary finite Markov chains (deterministic-emission HMM subclass). From one raw path with known geometric beta envelope, it chooses at most K groups of observed states by floored stationary next-state KL risk and certifies the selected model using held-out transition pairs. It needs no anchor, rank gap, row separation, transition positivity, or lower bound on stationary state masses; rare states are weighted by their actual mass. It pays O(d^2 epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)) selected pairs times the explicit mixing spacing, exponential partition search <=K^d, a prediction floor lambda/d, and a held-out buffer. This is genuine risk-based compression, not identification of exact predictive equivalence classes. The full-count/raw-retaining baseline can replay it and use more of the path.

Two related finite-task statements give boundaries, not general raw one-path learner wins. `predictive_quotient/RESULT.txt` proves for a finite history variable Hist, future F, and label C=f(Hist) the exact KL projection identity

  E KL(P(F|Hist)||q(F|C))
    = sum_c P(C=c) I(Hist;F | C=c) + E KL(P(F|C)||q(F|C)).

Minimizing over <=K labels yields the exact finite-task quotient distortion. The same report constructs a four-state HMM whose full Hankel rank is 3 for every p>0 while a finite joint-block SVD tail is <=3p/4; its row-normalized rank-one prediction has worst-context TV >=23/32. Thus an unnormalized Frobenius singular tail tending to zero does not by itself certify a rare context's conditional forecast. It is a population projection/counterexample, not a raw-sample acquisition theorem.

`hankel_tail_task_risk/RESULT.txt` gives an add-one-smoothed raw Hankel-row predictor under n independent stationary trajectories: for M=q^m contexts and S=q^H futures, expected stationary-context KL is at most (S-1)/(n+1) sum_u[1-(1-p_u)^(n+1)] <= M(S-1)/(n+1), without rank or gap. The exponential context/future table is charged; it does not establish the corresponding one-stream concentration without dependence analysis. It explicitly makes no implication from an unweighted SVD tail.

`full_future_loss/RESULT.txt` proves the stationary full-past chain-rule identity K_H=H r and discounted identity R_gamma=r/(1-gamma), charging startup/filter mismatch separately. A fixed short-H bound therefore does not give a uniform discounted or infinite-future guarantee; a rare history can also be poor under a small stationary-average risk.

4. SHARP COUNTEREXAMPLES AND WHAT THEIR COSTS DO

Rare contexts versus stationary average. `minimax_rare_history/RESULT.txt` analyzes a two-state full-rank HMM family with hidden-chain Dobrushin coefficient <=5/8 and a constant emission-channel rank margin. A one-symbol context has stationary mass Theta(p); its next-symbol forecast gap is Delta>=epsilon/3. For the complete raw path, KL<=12(T-1)p epsilon^2. The resulting and matching one-symbol-context rates are worst-context TV Theta(min{1,(Tp)^-1/2}) and fresh stationary-history expected KL Theta(min{p,1/T}). This sharply separates the objectives and charges the rare-event information term. The family does not keep all probability floors uniform: its minimum transition probability is Theta(p), and its minimum emission entry is p^2. The report's matching upper bounds apply to the stated one-symbol-context tasks only, not arbitrary histories or realized endpoint continuation.

`adaptive_raw_features/RESULT.txt` strengthens the worst-context barrier in a two-state positive, identifiable HMM with hidden spectral gap bounded below by 1/4. For each path length n it chooses rare-state mass alpha=1/(16n), emission noise eta=alpha/16, and r0=1/4,r1=3/4. The observable context Y_t=1 has mass Theta(1/n), while conditional next-symbol forecasts differ by >2/5. A coupling gives TV of the complete length-n observation laws <1/16. Every learner, even given the model pair, D=2 and the target context, has max-model probability of KL>1/50 at least 15/32 and expected KL at least 3/80. The full-path likelihood test is the equally informed benchmark; adaptive feature selection is only post-processing. This is a worst-supported-context result: alpha and the emission floor shrink with n, and it does not refute stationary-average guarantees that discount the rare context.

No uniform mixing promise. `passive_minimax/RESULT.txt` gives a three-state positive-emission HMM pair with fixed stationary masses, emission entries >=.01, a constant rank-complete one-symbol-pair witness, and transition switching rate rho=1/(100T). For every raw path length T the two-model lower bound is >0.01328 nats for the fresh independent stationary-context, H=1 expected KL task. The common path component and constant fresh-context difference are explicit. The result deliberately does not transfer to terminal continuation: on the shared state-0 path, next-step candidates agree except for O(rho) exit probability. Random-time, cumulative and observed-history filtering risks are not proved.

`sequential_kl/RESULT.txt` proves the predictive-information decomposition E KL(P(F|Hist)||q(F|T))=I(F;Hist|T)+E KL(P(F|T)||q(F|T)), then gives a stationary ergodic D=2 noisy HMM lower bound >0.0141 nats for fresh stationary one-step risk at every sample length when the positive transition gap may shrink with length. The two-model training-path laws share a constant submeasure. This lower bound rules out a uniform guarantee based only on state cap and fixed task; it does not rule out a supplied mixing/coverage lower bound or terminal filtering.

`one_stream_certificate/RESULT.txt` instead addresses continuation after the exact observed training path. For n observations, two stationary HMMs share transcript 0^n with probability >=.495 in one model; conditional future-block length L=100n laws after this transcript have TV >=1-exp(-1). Any common predictor has max conditional future KL >=(1-exp(-1))^2/2 >0.19 nats on that transcript. This is horizon L proportional to n, not a fixed-H obstruction as n grows. The report also charges an IID validation-only sample lower bound Omega(epsilon^-1 log(1/alpha)) and the dependence gap required to turn a same-stream split into a valid holdout.

`sequential_certificate/RESULT.txt` uses finite-state parity sources with 2^N states: complete N-symbol prefix laws are identical and full-support, while next predictions after N separate. Any adaptive certification procedure capped at N with uniform level delta has probability of issuing the claimed certificate <=2delta on the hard pair. This is a fixed-budget, growing-state statement; it does not exclude larger samples, pointwise eventual certification, or fixed bounded state cap with supplied mixing.

`unknown_mixing_certificate/RESULT.txt` is strictly post-hoc: it proves no distribution-free finite-time confidence certificate for the stationary-history KL risk of an arbitrary supplied predictor q under an unrestricted stationary HMM class. Reducible models remain indistinguishable on an infinite path; an irreducible two-state construction defeats every almost-surely finite stopping rule by selecting a transition rate below its stopping horizon. This is not an impossibility theorem for every data-dependent learner. It explicitly respects mixing-free observable-Markov terminal prediction results such as Han–Jana–Wu rather than contradicting them.

5. TRUNCATION, OPERATOR, AND CAUSAL BOUNDARIES

`horizon_gap_modulus/RESULT.txt` gives a fixed K=2, positive, rapidly mixing HMM family with fixed transition-entry and emission floors and one-symbol context mass 1/2. Its weak Hankel direction has gap gamma=rho/8; the population rank-one SVD truncation is iid fair. On a fresh independent stationary test history with the full observed past revealed, its deterministic H-step KL is between 2H gamma^2 and (512/15)H gamma^2; for each gamma>0 the full-future KL diverges. This is fixed-truncation bias, not an acquisition lower: a nontruncating same-stream estimator recovers the weak direction at the matching gamma^-2 sample scale. It does not cover random stopping or a learner that chooses truncation from data.

`positivity_operator/RESULT.txt` gives a persistent two-state mixture where a rank-one positive stochastic observable-operator prediction has small Hankel residual but incurs about 0.319 nat cumulative KL by horizon Theta(1/q). The exact population residual and loss are checked; it refutes a naive residual-to-risk transfer for that calibrated rank-one construction, not all positive operators or all adaptive predictors. Its full-path add-one comparator has exponential path-space dependence. `raw_path_concentration/RESULT.txt` separately proves delayed-martingale concentration for a finite raw prefix/suffix feature tensor and an online path-average Brier-risk confidence sequence, but explicitly does not turn it into stationary future risk without dependence control.

`causal_boundary/RESULT.txt` is outside passive behavior-law learning: two controlled HMMs induce the same rank-one iid-fair behavior trajectories under action a, yet opposite b-action transitions. One charged intervention changes the example's minimax control regret from 1/16 to 3/64. It separates passive prediction from intervention/control identification and does not establish a universal advantage for active representation discovery.

`predictive_precision/RESULT.txt` proves on a known two-state symmetric HMM family that a vanishing Hankel gap gamma_M=1/(4M^2) at task tolerance epsilon_M=1/(64M^4) entails raw-path sample lower bound T>=1+kl(1-eta||eta)/(144 epsilon_M), matched in order by a simple pair-count/full-path likelihood estimator. Statistical testing and finite-bit aliasing are distinct costs. It is not a general finite-precision spectral lower bound.

6. EQUAL-INFORMATION BASELINE AND ACQUISITION PRINCIPLE

`equal_information_baseline/RESULT.txt` proves the exact interface identity: for any model, any representation and randomized prediction kernel measurable from the same raw observations and side information can be simulated path-for-path by an unrestricted learner retaining that data. Thus it cannot improve minimax statistical risk against the strongest equal-information retainer; it may save priced storage or later arithmetic. A scoped two-state finite-horizon construction quantifies an unavoidable sample-versus-horizon tradeoff. This is a formal control on any purported “acquisition advantage”: demonstrate a downstream resource or a new information source, then charge it. It does not say compression has no engineering value.

The three routes are kept separate: task-risk aggregation on observable Markov states; conditional-likelihood search over a positive HMM class; and conditional finite-horizon prediction of a selected arbitrary predictor under known beta mixing. None supplies an exact teacher, Hankel dictionary or queried entry. None proves that a compressed object contains more statistical information than the raw path.

7. PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON AND PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY

* Han, Jiang, Wu, “Prediction from compression for models with infinite memory, with applications to hidden Markov and renewal processes,” arXiv:2404.15454v1 (2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15454 . Their Theorem 1 concerns exactly the expected terminal next-symbol risk after one stationary raw path, maximized over all stationary k-state, ℓ-symbol HMMs. It has no transition/emission margin, rank, identifiability, or mixing premise. For n>=Ck(k+ℓ), they give a matching minimax upper/lower rate of order (kℓ/n)log(n/(kℓ))+(k²/n)log(n/k²), with an n^{O(k²+kℓ)} dynamic-programming estimator. For fixed k,ℓ this is Θ(log n/n). Proposition 1 derives a terminal predictor from joint-code redundancy plus a memory term using a Cesàro/reverse-suffix construction. This is the strongest same-interface prior-art control for expected terminal next-symbol prediction; it is not a high-confidence certificate or a reusable finite-state realization, and the dimension-dependent exponent is charged. Exact source spans read: abstract, §1.1 / Theorem 1, Proposition 1 in §2, and §3.3. The cycle's fresh-context, frozen finite-H theorem is weaker at this terminal prediction task and is recorded only for its different contract.
* Hsu, Kakade, Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models,” COLT 2009/JCSS 2012, https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413. Raw-observation spectral learning under a known hidden-state count and quantitative full-rank/singular-value conditions. It gives a stronger all-history prediction guarantee in that restricted regime. The finite-cover result avoids rank/singular separation for a different finite-H expected KL loss by paying known mixing/emission floors and exhaustive search.
* Gassiat, Cleynen, Robin, “Finite state space non parametric Hidden Markov Models are in general identifiable,” arXiv:1306.4657 (2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657. * Han, Jana, Wu, “Optimal prediction of Markov chains with and without spectral gap conditions,” NeurIPS 2021, https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/5d69dc892ba6e79fda0c6a1e286f24c5-Paper.pdf. Mixing-free expected terminal prediction for observable Markov chains is an important boundary. It prevents overclaiming that lack of known mixing universally forbids prediction; the HMM certificate and fresh-population lower bounds here concern different interfaces.

9. FAILURES, UNKNOWNS, AND RESTART

The main positive theorem depends on known rho and e, a finite context/horizon, stationarity, and exponential finite-net computation. It neither learns those constants nor gives an efficient global HMM optimizer. Its target is fresh finite-H risk for a predictor selected once, not a high-confidence or infinite-history certificate. At terminal one-step expected KL it is weaker than Han–Jiang–Wu's source-derived all-HMM dynamic-programming result; the report does not substitute it for or claim novelty over that result. The observable-Markov aggregation result depends on a known beta envelope and floored predictor; the generic certificate depends on a known beta envelope and has exponential alphabet/horizon cost. The independent-episode Hankel-row theorem is not a single-stream theorem.

No theorem here provides risk-certified adaptive observable-basis discovery or a compact reusable predictive realization that improves on the HJW terminal predictor for general unknown HMMs with unknown mixing, unknown rare-context masses, or no emission floor. The main open gates are high-confidence post-selection certification; unknown-state-count/dimension computational cost; a frozen predictor's finite-H joint future risk; and memory/computation gains over a retained raw path. No all-history upper bound matches the rare-history lower under the same target. No general relationship converts arbitrary unnormalized SVD/Hankel residuals to task loss. No strict statistical sample advantage over a raw-path retainer is established. These remain UNKNOWN.

The most decisive next attack is not exact rank recovery: seek a risk-truncation theorem for a substantive HMM class under uniform mixing/bounded state count, with a same-data raw empirical/full-likelihood baseline and a pathwise or fresh-risk certificate. Either prove that weak directions can be discarded below a future-risk modulus without selecting exact rank, or give a fixed-H, terminal-continuation lower bound that charges all mixing, state-mass, emission, and witness-horizon margins. Use observable-Markov terminal prediction literature as a control; do not mistake a fresh independent context for an endpoint history.

Verification performed: rank-free HMM cover diagnostics and lead beta-certificate entropy-continuity diagnostics ran (the latter checks 11,025 rational fixtures); several leaf exact/rational checkers report success in their own packages. These are scoped finite diagnostics, not proof review. The two top-level claims are internally derived candidates. No external validation, publication, or messages to third parties were performed.
