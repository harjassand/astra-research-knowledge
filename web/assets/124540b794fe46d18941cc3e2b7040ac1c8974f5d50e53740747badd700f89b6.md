# Cycle 9 primary-source audit: Fog/GPCR mechanochemical recruitment

**Scope.** Independent read of the 2026 Drosophila posterior-endoderm Fog/GPCR study, its supplementary figures and transparent peer review, and the strongest directly relevant primary precedents. This is a source/mechanism audit, not an independent replication or priority claim.

## Primary 2026 result and exact measurements

Mundhe et al., *Nature Communications* 17, 1721 (2026), DOI [10.1038/s41467-026-68418-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68418-z), received 13 May 2025 and was accepted 6 January 2026. The key causal design rescues Concertina/Gα12/13 only in the Huckebein-positive posterior endoderm primordium in a Gα-null embryo. Initial primordium invagination is close to WT, but MyoII recruitment advances only 2.5 ± 0.06 cell rows versus 7.1 ± 0.3 in WT (mean ± s.e.m.; 4 WT and 7 rescue embryos). Thus the PZ needs Gα signaling independently of Gα's role in starting the invagination. Fog RNAi plus an RNAi-resistant primordium-only Fog transgene restores propagation to 5.3 ± 0.2 cells; the source-restricted membrane-tethered Fog-VSV-G version gives only 1.7 ± 0.1 cells despite similar initial primordium invagination. This is direct evidence that a mobile Fog fraction from the primordium supports non-autonomous PZ recruitment. It does **not** show that a moving Fog diffusion front sets wave velocity.

The 2026 paper establishes that the MyoII profile is exponential in the front reference frame, but this alone is descriptive. WT e-fold length is 3.18 ± 0.29 μm. Adding two Fog copies in the primordium raises front amplitude from 11.55 ± 0.10 to 32.59 ± 0.32 AU, raises the thresholded range from 27.08 ± 5.72 to >38.01 ± 1.92 μm, and raises length from 3.18 ± 0.29 to 5.26 ± 0.58 μm. In a separate comparison, gprk2 RNAi raises amplitude 9.72 ± 0.08 to 14.25 ± 0.14 AU, range 20.86 ± 5.72 to >33.59 ± 2.5 μm, and length 2.96 ± 0.45 to 5.48 ± 0.58 μm. The `>` ranges are censored lower bounds: the main-text profiles do not extend far enough to observe the threshold crossing. They should not be treated as full measured ranges. α-amanitin-treated embryos retain a MyoII exponential of statistically similar length to controls (Supplementary Fig. 4; n=9 water, 8 drug; p=0.0944), supporting transcription-independent profile maintenance.

The key spatial observation is not an extracellular Fog concentration gradient. Endogenous Fog::YFP is approximately uniform in the perivitelline extracellular space, including when Fog::YFP is made only zygotically from the primordium. In the PZ, FCS resolves a fast Fog fraction (84% ± reported measurement error not stated in this text; D=51 ± 2 μm²/s) and slow fraction (16 ± 1%; D=1.6 ± 0.2 μm²/s); the extracellular/invagination Fog estimate is D=55 μm²/s (sec::GFP is 87). Smog::GFP has D=0.54 ± 0.04 μm²/s. The authors infer, rather than directly establish, that Fog-slow is transiently membrane/receptor associated. gprk2 RNAi raises the slow fraction to 30 ± 15% and flattens its embryo-wise spatial slope (WT −1.9×10⁻³ ± 1.7×10⁻³ μm⁻¹, n=10 embryos, one-sided p=.0034; gprk2 RNAi 6.5×10⁻⁴ ± 2.6×10⁻³ μm⁻¹, n=11, p=.419 against zero; between-condition p=.0145). The plotted spatial bin comparison is 0–20 versus 20–70 μm. These are FCS point measurements at distinct sites, not images or longitudinal tracks of the slow pool.

Smog::GFP brightness is reduced by Fog RNAi and increased by gprk2 RNAi, with VSV-G::GFP as an oligomerization control. The inferred Smog oligomer fraction drops by about one half over 30–35 μm (example: trimer fraction 26% to 13%), while MyoII falls by >10-fold in that same interval. This mismatch is a clue for a nonlinear downstream gain, not evidence that the published data identify its transfer function. Fluorescence-brightness-to-monomer/oligomer conversion is model-dependent; authors use an assumed monomer-plus-oligomer mixture. The study uses Smog as a receptor prototype and explicitly does not claim it is the sole Fog receptor; Mist may contribute.

Integrin αPS3/Scab knockout depresses MyoII amplitude while leaving an exponential profile and a roughly WT-like thresholded range; the decay length increases. Removing αPS3 in gprk2 RNAi embryos returns MyoII amplitude and range toward the αPS3-null phenotype, so the Gprk2 effect depends functionally on integrin. But αPS3 RNAi does not reduce Smog brightness (new Supplementary Fig. 8F; n=106 measurements/16 embryos vs 90/17; p=.0016, reportedly in the non-decreasing direction). The authors therefore infer that integrins are not directly required for Smog clustering and may be signaling cofactors downstream or alongside the receptor. Gprk2 RNAi also expands the βPS contact/puncta profile and the tissue zone touching the vitelline membrane (dextran-exclusion assay, n=11 control/10 RNAi, p=.0315). Whether this is cause, consequence, or a feedback branch is not isolated: the response notes that higher MyoII may stiffen the tissue, widen outward bending and thereby expand contact.

## What the supplement and peer review add

The [Supplementary Information PDF](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-026-68418-z/MediaObjects/41467_2026_68418_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) verifies: (i) Fog-VSV-G stays within ectopic wingless stripes and can signal cell-autonomously (Fig. S3); (ii) α-amanitin profile check (S4); (iii) functional endogenous Fog::SYFP2 knock-in and zygotic-only extracellular distribution (S5); (iv) FCS fit-routine/photophysics diagnostics (S6); (v) junctional as well as apical Smog-brightness trends with VSV-G controls (S7); (vi) integrin knockout, Smog brightness after αPS3 RNAi, contact-zone expansion and E-cad-to-VM geometry (S8). The main text says raw images are too large and available on request; source-data files are supplied. I did not re-fit the source data.

The [transparent peer-review file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-026-68418-z/MediaObjects/41467_2026_68418_MOESM2_ESM.pdf) is especially informative about the claim boundary:

- Authors agree the hierarchy that converts uniform extracellular Fog into activity gradient is not fully understood. They explicitly leave Fog PTM, extracellular binding/inhibitor/protease, and receptor cofactor possibilities open; maternal Fog is more abundant but less potent than zygotic Fog, potentially because of PTM.
- The Fog-slow component and receptor oligomerization are local FCS measurements; reviewer-requested images cannot be produced from these readouts. Authors explain that pooled linear-fit R² is weakly interpretable because of embryo-to-embryo variability and show per-embryo slope tests instead.
- Authors explain the apparent 2019/2026 conflict carefully: 2019 rejects a *non-steady diffusive expansion front* as the process determining wave kinematics; 2026 says primordium Fog is still needed to set a self-renewed activity pattern while cell mechanics determine propagation. This is the authors’ synthesis, not a new measured velocity law.
- Reviewer concern about the shallower Smog gradient is answered by a proposed nonlinear cascade (integrin/G-protein/Rho1/Rok); the >10x MyoII change versus 2x oligomer-fraction change is shown, but no dose-response curve or gain parameter is measured.
- Reviewer concern about integrins directly promoting receptor clustering is answered by new S8F: αPS3 RNAi does not decrease Smog brightness. Authors favor integrins as GPCR signaling cofactors and the increased-contact geometry as a feedback component; exact molecular cross-talk remains open.
- They could not separately image the engineered Fog-VSV-G transgene in the primordium because endogenous Fog cannot be distinguished there after RNAi injection. The ectopic stripe assay validates tethering, and the matched source expression functional assay supports non-diffusion, but it is not direct primordium localization imaging.
- Reported range uses a control-baseline +3 SD threshold for MyoII/βPS and smoothing; contact range uses 10% normalized sec::GFP threshold. The different operational definitions limit naive numerical comparisons.

The authors themselves toned down the framing from “Fog is a mechanogen” to “Fog acts with a mechanism similar to a mechanogen.” That is the prudent phrasing. The data are strongest for mobile primordium Fog being needed for non-autonomous PZ MyoII recruitment, a front-frame surface signaling state gradient, and functional Fog/GPCR/integrin coupling. They do not directly prove receptor-bound identity of Fog-slow, the molecular mechanism setting the gradient, or a quantitative chemical control of wave speed.

## Adversarial primary prior art / novelty boundary

1. **Bailles et al. 2019, Nature**, [DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1492-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1492-9), [open-access full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8985608/). The MyoII front moves at 2.2 ± 0.2 μm/min (about one cell/3 min); posterior endoderm propagates across 8 ± 1 rows. Blocking transcription or removing the source Fog after primordium contraction does not stop propagation. Increasing Fog dosage raises MyoII levels but does not raise front speed; homogeneous Fog overexpression does not abolish the wave. The authors reject an expanding transient Fog diffusion front as the speed-setting mechanism and model mechanochemical, contact/adhesion-coupled propagation. This is the critical falsifier of any claim that the ordinary moving-frame `D/v` reach estimate explains the wave’s speed or that more Fog should make it run faster.
2. **Jha et al. 2018, Current Biology**, [DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2018.03.068](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.03.068), [open-access full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934411/). Fog-induced Smog homoclusters, Gprk2/β-arrestin-dependent removal of active Smog, and quantitative effects on Rho1/MyoII in gastrulation were already established. The 2026 novelty is the source-restricted, front-co-moving activity pattern in the posterior-endoderm PZ and its coupling to contact geometry/integrins—not GPCR clustering or endocytic tuning in general.
3. **Collinet et al. 2024, Developmental Cell**, [DOI 10.1016/j.devcel.2023.11.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2023.11.022), [open-access full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10783558/). MyoII recruitment and integrin-mediated VM adhesion form positive feedback; after detachment, actin-cortex contraction advects/concentrates MyoII. Prolonged VM contact lengthens integrin–MyoII feedback, slows detachment, and slows the tissue wave. It quantitatively localizes activation to contact and advection to post-detachment. Therefore a cell-local contact-age/adhesion response is a strong rival to interpreting any front-coordinate exponential as a diffusion-decay profile.

## Opportunity that could exceed the passive reach bound

The strongest consequential next result is an **identifiability test of a front-renewed ligand/receptor gradient versus a contact-age mechanochemical response**, not another `ell ≤ D/v` calculation. In a constant-speed wave, distance ahead of the front is proportional to time until contact/front passage; any exponential in front coordinates can therefore be generated by an exponential per-cell temporal response. The 2026 Fog-slow and Smog-brightness measurements add real molecular structure but are sampled at different locations/embryos, while the MyoII profile is front-registered and time-averaged. Existing evidence does not align Fog-slow, receptor state, contact onset, MyoII recruitment, and detachment in the same cells.

A decisive protocol is to track identical PZ cells from first VM contact through detachment while measuring contact geometry, MyoII, and a validated receptor activity/occupancy readout; independently perturb wave/contact timing mechanically and Fog availability/endocytosis genetically. Compare (a) collapse of signals against each cell’s measured time since VM contact, (b) front-frame decay lengths and receptor-state ordering, and (c) the velocity dependence of fitted lengths. With fixed D and removal rate, the linear moving reaction–diffusion model predicts `ell(v)=2D/(v+sqrt(v^2+4Dk))`, which decreases with front speed `v`. A cell-age exponential with time constant τ has `ell=vτ` only when the relevant contact-onset boundary moves at that speed; otherwise use the independently measured contact-boundary trajectory, not furrow speed as a proxy. At one speed the spatial profiles can be non-identifiable; a controlled speed/contact sweep can make the hypotheses diverge. The contact-age rival must also be challenged against the measured Fog-slow/Smog front gradients, not just MyoII.

**Hard falsifier:** If changing propagation/contact timing at fixed Fog/Gprk2 and matched initial geometry makes each-cell MyoII traces collapse as a function of measured time since VM contact, while receptor-state changes do not precede MyoII conditional on contact age and the front-frame lengths fail the independently parameterized transport law, the diffusion-decay interpretation is not the operative profile generator. Conversely, if slowing/speeding the mechanical wave leaves the front-frame length controlled by measured D and an independently measured receptor/Fog removal rate, while receptor state consistently precedes MyoII in matched cells, that supports an active chemical field beyond a pure age response. A clean result needs orthogonal control of tissue geometry because Gprk2 RNAi itself changes contact length and amplitude.

This is a proposed test, not achieved evidence. The current 2026 results materially improve on a passive bound, but the primary literature already contains decisive mechanics and prior GPCR-endocytosis controls; the novelty claim must be limited to a validated causal/quantitative link that survives these alternatives.
