c09_s03 phase-2 acquired conditional decoder for a specified posterior model

ORIGIN AND STATUS

The positive-hat archive and conditional log-gradient argument come from the
reviewed c09_l03 INITIAL. Its decoder mu_j was a supplied conditional sampler.
The following Sol phase-2 repair acquires that decoder on a narrower, known,
finite-digital weak-interaction Gibbs experiment. Dobrushin coupling and heat-
bath sampling are established methods; this is a scoped acquisition closure,
not a new general sampling theorem. Priority for this particular combination
is UNKNOWN. Primary antecedent: Thomas P. Hayes, "A simple condition implying
rapid mixing of single-site dynamics on spin systems", FOCS 2006, author PDF
https://www.cs.unm.edu/~hayes/papers/EigenvalueDobrushin/EigenvalueDobrushin-FOCS06.pdf.
The elementary coupling needed here is derived in full below. No KLS premise
or proof is used.

INTERFACE: SUPPLIED, ACQUIRED AND CHARGED

Supply a finite simple graph on d vertices, nonnegative rational symmetric edge
weights lambda_ij, and W=max_i sum_j lambda_ij<4. A sparse adjacency list is part
of the model, not discovered. Supply positive rational s_i with sum_i s_i^2<=rho^2,
known rational rho<1, and rational 0<eps<1. All input/output observations are
digital: x_k=-1+(2k+1)/N, k=0,...,N-1, N=2^p. The reference distribution is

  mu_p(x) proportional to exp(sum_edges lambda_ij x_i^2 x_j^2).

For unknown v with ||v||_2<=1, the actual one-observation experiment is

  P_v(x)=mu_p(x) [1+sum_i s_i v_i x_i].

Independent coordinate sign flips imply E_mu_p x_i=0, so these are probability
distributions; the ratio is at least 1-rho. The encoder and decoder know the
supplied model, eps, and p, but neither knows v. The encoder receives an actual
d p-bit input sample from P_v; it stores only a finite tuple of active hat labels.
The decoder returns d p-bit output coordinates. Full graph description, p,
calibration, and spectrum are public and charged; they are not an unrecorded
input-dependent side channel. No partition function, conditional mass or global
reference sampler is supplied to the algorithm.

This is explicitly the finite experiment above, not the law of a rounded
continuous Gibbs sample: integrated cell weights differ from midpoint weights.
No atomic-output versus continuous-TV claim is made. Acquisition of the known
model from observations is a separate, unresolved task.

ARCHIVE PLAN

Let L=2W, C=12+324L, m the smallest integer with sum_{i>m}s_i^2<=eps^2/64,
M=ceil(sqrt(max(m,1))), and J0=max(3,ceil(6L)). Set

  J_i=max(J0,ceil(sqrt(4 C M s_i/eps))),  i<=m.

Use graded nodes u_j=-1+6(j/J_i)^2-4(j/J_i)^3 and their standard linear hats.
Take p with N>=16 max_i J_i^2 and N>=264/eps. Empty active set uses Jmax=3.
Grid spacing delta=2/N then satisfies delta<=1/(8J_i^2) and delta<=eps/132.
The encoder independently samples each hat label with probability phi_j(x_i),
using exact rational Bernoulli coins, and stores a mixed-radix tuple of size

  D=product_{i<=m}(J_i+1),   archive bits=ceil(log2 D).

Nodes are computed from rational integer formulas on demand; binary search
uses O(log J_i) comparisons and does not allocate a J_i-entry probability table.

IDEAL DIGITAL HAT ERROR

For a fixed i and all other labels, the reference marginal weight q(t) is a
finite sum of positive exp(beta t^2) terms, beta in [0,W], times nonnegative
other-label factors. Thus |q'(t)|<=L q(t) on [-1,1], uniformly in the label.
The continuous hat centroid estimate from c09_l03 is <=C_L/J_i^2 with
C_L=4e+36e^2 L<=C. We independently checked that estimate and its mesh geometry.

Here is the additional finite-grid estimate. A hat phi around node a has
support width w<=6/J, area m0=w/2>=2/J^2, and continuous weighted denominator
m=integral phi q>=qmin m0. Since J>=6L, Lw<=1, so qmax/qmin<=e on its support.
For a piecewise C1 function F, midpoint quadrature has error at most
(delta/2) integral |F'|. Applied to phi q and (t-a)phi q this gives

  E0<=delta qmax(2+Lm0)/2,
  E1<=delta qmax(m0+2w+Lwm0)/2.

With delta/m0<=1/16 and L delta<=1/32, E0/m<=5e/64<1/2. The digital denominator
is at least m/2. The continuous centroid displacement is trivially at most w;
therefore the digital minus continuous centroid displacement is bounded by

  2(E1+w E0)/m <=delta e(1+4w/m0+2Lw)<=11e delta<33delta.

Thus the digital conditional centroid obeys

  |E_mu_p[x_i|labels]-u_j|<=C/J_i^2+33delta.

The ideal reference-preserving hat channel reproduces every node combination
and hence has pointwise density-ratio error at most

  C sqrt(sum_{i<=m} s_i^2/J_i^4) +33rho delta+2||s_tail||_2
  <=eps/4+eps/4+eps/4=3eps/4.

Consequently its uniform TV error is <=3eps/8. The same bound applies when
m=0, with just the omitted-tail term. This digital centroid derivation is an
extra lemma; it is not inferred from a mesh fixture.

ACQUIRING EACH CONDITIONAL mu_(p,label)

Given the label tuple, define its target conditional reference distribution

  pi_j(x) proportional to exp(sum_edges lambda x_i^2 x_j^2)
                            product_{i<=m} phi_(i,j_i)(x_i).

Every allowed one-coordinate support has a grid point by the p condition. A
single-site heat-bath update at vertex i has unnormalized conditional weight

  w_i(t)=phi_i(t) exp(beta_i t^2),
  beta_i=sum_{neighbors k} lambda_ik x_k^2 in [0,W].

For unretained coordinates phi_i=1. Propose uniformly among grid points in the
hat support (uniform full grid if unretained), and accept with probability

  phi_i(t) exp[-beta_i(1-t^2)].

This rejection sampler draws the exact conditional in ideal real arithmetic
and never evaluates a global normalizer. For a retained hat, its average
height over the proposed grid is at least 1/4: midpoint quadrature for phi has
error <=delta, while the number of proposal grid points times delta is at most
w+delta. Since delta/m0<=1/16, the average is at least 15/33>1/4. For an
unretained coordinate the average is one. Ideal acceptance is at least e^-W/4.

INFLUENCE AND MIXING PROOF

For any fixed nonnegative base measure on t in [-1,1], the normalized
exp(beta t^2)-tilt has beta derivative in L1/2 equal to
(1/2) E|t^2-E t^2|<=1/4. The last inequality holds for every random variable
in [0,1], whose mean absolute deviation is at most 1/2. Changing one neighbor
k changes beta_i by at most lambda_ik. Hence its conditional-TV influence is
at most c_ik=lambda_ik/4. Both row and column sums of this symmetric influence
matrix are at most alpha=W/4<1, uniformly in labels, supports and grid p.

Couple two random-scan chains using the same uniformly chosen coordinate and
a maximal conditional coupling. If H is their number of differing coordinates,
then one update satisfies
E[H_next|states]<=H-(H/d)+(alpha H/d)
                  =(1-(1-alpha)/d)H.
Since initially H<=d, coupling to a stationary chain gives TV distance at most

  d (1-(1-alpha)/d)^T <=d exp[-(1-alpha)T/d].

Choose T by an exact rational comparison so that the first bound is <=eps/8.
This is uniform over every archive label. It applies even when some labels
have tiny marginal probability: the local influence bound uses the hat as a
base measure, not the global conditioning probability.

FINITE EXPONENTIAL PRECISION

The implementation does not use floating exp. Let c=3^-ceil(W)<=e^-W and
delta_step=eps/(8T). Approximate exp(-u), 0<=u<=W, by its rational Taylor
polynomial, clipped to [0,1], with absolute error eta<=c delta_step/2. The
Lagrange remainder is at most W^(r+1)/(r+1)!; increase r until this is <=eta.
Every local acceptance probability is rational and its coin is sampled exactly
by uniform integers, using unbiased bits and rejection. Clipping cannot
increase the exponential error. If w and wtilde are the accepted proposal
weights, then their normalized output distributions have TV at most

  E_proposal |w-wtilde| / E_proposal w <=eta e^W<=delta_step.

The finite-rational acceptance remains >=c/8, so expected proposals per local
update are <=8/c. The total perturbation of T updates is at most T delta_step
=eps/8, by telescoping Markov kernels. Combining it with mixing yields

  sup_label TV(implemented decoder law, pi_j)<=eps/4.

Mixing each label's decoder with the actual encoder label law adds at most
eps/4 TV for every unknown v. The version with unrestricted rejection obeys

  sup_{||v||<=1} TV(Law(output),P_v)<=5eps/8<eps.

HARD RESOURCE CUTOFFS USED BY THE EXECUTABLE

The code additionally caps rejection loops, with their errors charged. Choose
P by exact rational comparison so that T(1-c/8)^P<=eps/8, and allow at most P
proposals at each update. If none is accepted, leave that coordinate unchanged.
Coupling to the unrestricted rejection sampler gives at most eps/8 additional
TV by a union bound over T local updates. Every uniform-integer subroutine uses
b=(n-1).bit_length() unbiased bits and accepts the result if it is below n;
each attempt fails with probability less than 1/2. At most
Q=m+T(2P+1) such calls occur in the encoder and decoder together. Choose S with
Q 2^-S<=eps/8 and cap each call at S attempts. On an encoder cutoff return the
legal archive zero; on a decoder cutoff return its current legal grid state.
Coupling to the uncapped bits adds at most eps/8 total TV. The actual bounded
implementation therefore satisfies

  sup_v TV(Law(output),P_v)<=7eps/8<eps.

Every loop now terminates after an explicit finite number of bit operations.
Let Qlambda be the largest lcm of edge denominators at a vertex. Each exponent
u denominator divides Q_i N^4, Q_i<=Qlambda, and a degree-r Taylor polynomial
denominator is at most (Qlambda N^4)^r r!. A hat probability denominator is at
most 2 N Jmax^3. Thus all acceptance-coin denominators are at most
2 N Jmax^3 (Qlambda N^4)^r r!. Taking B as the maximum of this integer's bit
length, p and ceil(log2 d), the encoder plus decoder requests at most Q S B
bits. Their arithmetic involves polynomially bounded rational operands; all
cutoff fallback events are included in the guarantee, not silently discarded.

COSTS

Model: sparse graph O(d Delta) rational coefficients, d rational spectrum
entries, rho, eps and precision. A source-data draw is d p input bits and the
output has d p bits. Archive is ceil(log2 product(J_i+1)). Encoder has m binary
searches, O(sum log J_i) exact comparisons and m rational coins. Decoder uses
d p bits of transient state plus the derived label tuple, not an uncounted
input-dependent record. It outputs all d coordinates; its dimension cost is
charged even when the archive is spectrum-truncated.

T=O(d/(1-W/4) log(d/eps)) local updates. Expected proposal count is at most
8*3^ceil(W)*T and the hard cap is TP, with P=O(3^ceil(W) log(T/eps)).
Each update reads at most Delta neighbor values; every proposal computes
a degree-r rational polynomial. If b_lambda bounds coupling bits, beta(1-t^2)
has O(Delta b_lambda+4p+log Delta) bits before rational reductions, charging
the lcm of distinct coupling denominators. Polynomial arithmetic has at most
O(r(Delta b_lambda+4p+log Delta)+log(r!)+log Jmax) bit operands;
exact successful coin generation uses the recorded denominator length, not a
costless real random number. Cutoff errors are charged as above. r is acquired
by the explicit remainder loop and recorded.
The plan's exact mixing comparison also costs rational arithmetic on O(T log d)
bits. This is polynomial for fixed W<4 and bounded supplied coefficient bits.

For geometric s_i and fixed W, the J_i archive upper allocation keeps c09_l03's
leading log-squared coefficient. The continuous entropy lower is not transferred
to this discrete model. Runtime and full-output precision still grow
with d. No general joint-log-gradient reference sampler, singular score law,
unknown interaction graph, or general posterior discovery gate is closed.

The script uses Python's seeded Random only for reproducible examples, not as
a certified physical entropy source. Its distributional proof uses iid unbiased
random bits, and the code counts every requested bit including rejections.
Finite tests check rational probabilities, remainder bounds and a real planned
decoder run; they do not validate the asymptotic theorem or priority.
