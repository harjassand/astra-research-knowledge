# Equality in the complex phase-retrieval condition-number bound

## Executive finding

For complex dimension (d\ge 2), equality in the 2026 universal ℓ2 condition-number bound is characterized exactly by weighted complex projective 2-designs of the row directions, with weights proportional to fourth powers of row norms. This gives explicit exact optimizers whenever such a design is available. It also implies that at least (d^2) nonzero scalar measurements are needed to attain the universal optimum; at exactly (d^2), the design must be a SIC/equiangular tight frame with equal row norms.

This is a short, internally reconstructed equality-case corollary of Peng–Han–Huang's new lower-bound proof combined with standard projective-design identities. It is a partial answer to their broad optimal-design discussion, not a solution for every prescribed ((m,d)), and it is not priority-cleared as a novel result. Projective 2-design inversion and the minimal SIC characterization were known well before the 2026 phase-retrieval paper.

## Exact interface and normalization

Peng, Han, and Huang study
\[
  \Psi_A(x)=(|a_j^*x|^2)_{j=1}^m,\qquad
  \operatorname{dist}(x,y)=\|xx^*-yy^*\|_*.
\]
The output norm is the ordinary, unnormalized ℓ2 norm on the (m) raw intensity coordinates. The lower and upper constants are the infimum and supremum of the intensity difference divided by this nuclear-norm distance, over pairs with nonzero distance; their condition number is (\beta=U/L). The paper also proves
\[
 L=\inf_{\substack{\|u\|=\|v\|=1\\\langle u,v\rangle\in\mathbb R}}
       \left(\sum_j |\operatorname{Re}(u^*a_ja_j^*v)|^2\right)^{1/2},
 \qquad U=\|A\|_{2\to4}^2.
\]
There is no division by (m), and no photon-count, exposure, or calibration model in this definition. Multiplying all rows by a common scalar multiplies (L) and (U) by the same squared scalar, so (\beta) is invariant. Nonuniform row scaling changes the weighted design condition below.

For each nonzero row set (t_j=\|a_j\|_2), (u_j=a_j/t_j), (\Pi_j=u_ju_j^*), and (S=\sum_jt_j^4). Zero rows can be discarded. The design probabilities are
\[
 p_j=t_j^4/S.
\]
Thus the normalization is explicitly by (t_j^4), not by (t_j^2), and the unit vectors are the normalized row directions. A weighted complex projective 2-design means
\[
 \sum_jp_j\Pi_j^{\otimes2}=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym}
 =\frac{I+F}{d(d+1)},
\]
where (F) swaps the tensor factors. Equivalently,
\[
 \sum_j t_j^4\Pi_j^{\otimes2}=\frac{2S}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym}.
\]

## Equality characterization and proof

**Claim.** For complex (d\ge2), nonzero (A\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}) with (L>0),
\[
 \beta_{\Psi_A}^{\ell_2}=2
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{(u_j,p_j):p_j=t_j^4/S\}\text{ is a weighted complex projective 2-design.}
\]

**Sufficiency.** The design identity yields, for every Hermitian (X),
\[
 \sum_j t_j^4(\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jX))^2
 =\frac{S}{d(d+1)}\bigl(\|X\|_F^2+(\operatorname{Tr}X)^2\bigr).
\]
For unit (u,v) with (c=\langle u,v\rangle\in\mathbb R), put (H=(uv^*+vu^*)/2). Then \(\operatorname{Tr}H=c\) and \(\|H\|_F^2=(1+c^2)/2\); the lower-constant characterization gives
\[
 L^2=\min_{-1\le c\le1}\frac{S(1+3c^2)}{2d(d+1)}
 =\frac{S}{2d(d+1)}.
\]
For a unit (x), the same tensor identity gives \(\sum_j|a_j^*x|^4=2S/[d(d+1)]\), so
\[
 U^2=\frac{2S}{d(d+1)},\qquad \beta=2.
\]

**Necessity.** Let (S=\sum_j\|a_j\|^4>0). The proof of Peng–Han–Huang Theorem 3.1, after reduction to complex dimension 2, establishes
\[
 U_A^2\ge S/3,\qquad L_A\le M_A,\qquad M_A^2\le S/12,
\]
where (M_A) is the minimum in the lower-constant formula restricted to orthogonal unit pairs. Hence \(\beta=2\) forces equality throughout, in particular (U_A^2=S/3).

Take any complex two-dimensional subspace (V\subseteq\mathbb C^d), and compress every row to (V). The paper's restriction inequality gives \(\beta_{A|V}\le\beta_A\), while its dimension-2 universal bound gives \(\beta_{A|V}\ge2\). Thus the compressed system also has equality. In the paper's complex (d=2) proof, (U_{A|V}^2) is the maximum over the Bloch sphere of a nonnegative quartic (h_V), and the sphere average is exactly \(\sum_j\|P_Va_j\|^4/3\). Equality of maximum and average makes (h_V) constant. Therefore (q(x)=\sum_j|a_j^*x|^4) is constant on the unit sphere of every such (V). Any two unit vectors lie in a subspace of dimension at most two, so (q) is constant on the full sphere.

Homogeneity gives (q(x)=c\|x\|^4\) for all (x). Polarization of this Hermitian biquadratic form implies
\[
 \sum_j (a_ja_j^*)^{\otimes2}=cP_{\rm sym}.
\]
Taking traces on the symmetric subspace, of dimension (d(d+1)/2), gives (c=2S/[d(d+1)]), which is precisely the weighted design identity above. This uses the full equality chain, not merely the numerical fact that 2 is a lower bound.

## Minimum number of rows; equality case

For a weighted design write (Q_j=\Pi_j-I/d\), as a vector in the real Hilbert space of traceless Hermitian matrices, dimension (n=d^2-1). The first and second design moments imply
\[
 \sum_j p_jQ_j=0,
 \qquad \sum_jp_j|Q_j\rangle\langle Q_j|=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}I_n.
\]
The covariance has rank (n), so there must be at least (n+1=d^2) positive-weight rows. At exactly (m=d^2), let (R) have columns \(\sqrt{p_j}Q_j\). Then (RR^T=cI_n), (c=1/[d(d+1)]), and its null vector is \((\sqrt{p_j})_j\). Since (R) has (n+1) columns,
\[
 R^TR=c(I-ss^T),\qquad s=(\sqrt{p_j})_j.
\]
The diagonal entries give (p_j(d-1)/d=c(1-p_j)), hence (p_j=1/d^2). The off-diagonal entries give \(\operatorname{Tr}(Q_iQ_j)=-c\), hence \(\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_i\Pi_j)=1/(d+1)\) for (i\ne j). Also \(\sum_j\Pi_j=dI\). Thus the normalized directions are a SIC (equivalently, a complex equiangular tight frame of (d^2) unit vectors). Because (p_j\propto\|a_j\|^4), all row norms are equal as well. Conversely, a SIC is a projective 2-design and attains \(\beta=2\).

Consequences:

- No complex system with fewer than (d^2) nonzero scalar intensity channels can attain the exact \(\ell_2\) optimum (\beta=2\), though phase retrieval itself is possible with (O(d)) channels in many dimensions.
- A SIC, whenever available in dimension (d), gives a minimum-cardinality exact optimizer with (m=d^2\).
- A complete set of (d+1) mutually unbiased bases gives a 2-design with (m=d(d+1)\) in prime-power dimensions, hence an exact optimizer there. It is not measurement-minimal when a SIC exists.

## Explicit inverse and test

For an equal-weight unit-vector design with (m) rows, let (y_j=|u_j^*x|^2) and (T=\sum_jy_j\Pi_j). The moment identity gives
\[
 T=\frac{m}{d(d+1)}(xx^*+\|x\|^2I),\qquad
 \|x\|^2=\frac d m\operatorname{Tr}T,
\]
so
\[
 xx^*=\frac{d(d+1)}mT-\frac d m\operatorname{Tr}(T)I.
\]
For general weighted designs realized by rows (a_j=t_ju_j), set (y_j=|a_j^*x|^2), (p_j=t_j^4/S), and
\[
 T=\sum_jp_j\frac{y_j}{t_j^2}\Pi_j.
\]
Then (T=(xx^*+\|x\|^2I)/[d(d+1)]) and (xx^*=d(d+1)T-d\operatorname{Tr}(T)I). The formula is the weighted moment contraction; the fourth-root scaling (t_j=p_j^{1/4}) realizes any design weights at (S=1).

A dense (d\times d) accumulation of (T) takes (O(md^2)), but reconstructing the rank-one matrix/vector need not form all of (T): compute its (d) diagonal entries from the rank-one rows in (O(md)), choose an index (k) with nonzero (X_{kk}), accumulate only row (k) of (T) in another (O(md)), and recover (x) up to phase from that row and (X_{kk}). Thus the exact arithmetic count is (O(md)) with explicit stored rows, consistent with Balan–Bodmann–Casazza–Edidin’s Theorem 3.4 discussion (pp. 8–9); for (m=\Theta(d^2)) this is (O(d^3)). A special fast Fourier/graph implementation may be faster for structured MUBs, but none is assumed for the general weighted design. The full matrix-output version would cost (O(md^2)).

The script [design_test.py](design_test.py) constructs the six Pauli eigenvectors in (d=2), verifies the tensor moment and reconstruction identities for one complex vector, and reports max absolute errors (4.44\times10^{-16}) and (2.24\times10^{-16}), respectively. This is a finite arithmetic check only. The exact formula is prior art (Levenshtein/Scott/Balan et al.). No noisy finite-precision implementation benchmark was run; the global deterministic inverse stability follows from the condition number theorem, but the cost above counts exact arithmetic and storage explicitly.

## What question does this answer?

The arXiv HTML of Peng–Han–Huang, section 5 (“The Optimal Sensing Matrix”), says exactly: “While the harmonic frame is known to be an optimal sensing matrix minimizing the condition number in \(\mathbb R^{m\times2}\) for \(p=2\), designing sensing matrices \(A\in\mathbb H^{m\times d}\) with optimal condition number in general spaces remains an open and challenging problem.” Their Theorem 3.1 gives \(\beta_{\ell_2}\ge2\) for complex sensing matrices; equation (4) defines (L,U); the equality analysis uses their equations (25)–(29), in particular \(U^2\ge S/3\), \(M^2\le S/12\), and restriction monotonicity. The published Oxford record confirms the article's publication on 10 June 2026, but the full discussion text available for this audit was the arXiv manuscript.

Our result constructs/characterizes optimizers when row directions and weights form an exact projective 2-design. It closes the optimum for those ((m,d)) admitting the stated designs and proves the exact minimum count (d^2). It does **not** solve the article's unqualified problem for every prescribed (m,d), every structured sensing family, or an experimental acquisition model. Their phrase is a broad research direction, not a formal theorem question with fixed constraints on (m,d), admissible matrices, or hardware.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

The ingredients that make this a short corollary are old:

1. Balan, Bodmann, Casazza, and Edidin, *Painless Reconstruction from Magnitudes of Frame Coefficients*, Theorem 3.4 (attributed there to Levenshtein, with a Scott attribution), state the exact 2-design inverse formula. Their pages 7–8 also state that maximal unions of MUBs give complex projective 2-designs and that maximal equiangular tight frames do so. Their page 8 gives (O(Nd)), or (O(d^3)) at (N=\Theta(d^2)), reconstruction cost.
2. Klappenecker and Rötteler (2005) identify complete MUBs as complex projective 2-designs; complete MUB sets are known in prime-power dimensions.
3. Scott (2006) identifies weighted 2-designs with tight rank-one informationally complete measurements, gives painless tomography reconstruction, and treats them as optimal for linear quantum state tomography. This is related but uses a different statistical/linear-tomography optimality criterion than the Peng–Han–Huang global nonlinear ℓ2 Lipschitz ratio.
4. The minimal (d^2)-point weighted 2-design calculation above identifies a SIC, a standard quantum-information object. The assertion is not a new SIC existence result.

Searches for prior papers explicitly stating the equality case of this particular Peng ℓ2 condition number as “if and only if weighted projective 2-design” found no direct match. That absence is not a proof of novelty. The precise relation is a plausible new statement relative to the 2026 lower-bound paper, but it follows in a short chain from its proof plus a classical moment identity. Characterize it as an internally reconstructed corollary pending specialist prior-art review, not as a major new theorem.

## Costs, comparison, and physical boundary

- **Rows/readouts:** exact optimality needs (m\ge d^2) active intensity channels. A SIC reaches this count when one exists; MUBs use (d(d+1)) channels in prime-power dimensions. Generic complex phase retrieval can be injective with (4d-4) measurements, so exact worst-case optimal conditioning may cost quadratically more rows than injectivity alone.
- **Computation:** the weighted moment formula has an (O(md)) exact-arithmetic recovery route that computes all diagonals and one nonzero matrix row, hence (O(d^3)) for SIC/MUB-scale designs when rows are explicitly stored. Forming the entire moment matrix directly costs (O(md^2)) or (O(d^4)) at (m=\Theta(d^2)); the output matrix itself has (d^2) entries. Count and representation details depend on the implementation.
- **Precision:** the inverse is a linear frame inversion, with explicit scale (d(d+1)/m); the frame is tight on traceless Hermitian directions. This exact ℓ2 deterministic conditioning does not charge finite-precision representation, row calibration, or normalization errors. In an approximate design, errors in the second moment perturb the inverse and the Lipschitz constants; no quantitative approximate-design perturbation theorem was established here.
- **Exposure/noise:** the phase-retrieval paper studies exact intensities plus deterministic additive ℓ2 output perturbations. It has no Poisson, shot-noise, number-of-copies, exposure-time, or saturation accounting. If implemented as projective quantum measurements, a complete MUB set comprises (d+1) basis settings, with (d) outcomes per setting; repetitions over copies are required to estimate probabilities. A SIC POVM is one (d^2)-outcome measurement with appropriately scaled POVM effects. The actual exposure and calibration costs require a specified physical apparatus/noise model and are not supplied by this theorem.
- **Equal-information baselines:** compare only designs with the same raw intensity ℓ2 metric and known sensing vectors. Gaussian rows are the paper's strongest generic randomized baseline: its real theorem gives (\beta\le\sqrt3+\delta) when (m\ge C\delta^{-4}d^2), with stated probability; the complex case is only remarked to converge by a law-of-large-numbers argument there. This is not a deterministic sample-complexity guarantee for complex rows. The paper's separate ℓ1 result has (O(d\delta^{-2}\log(1/\delta)))-type sampling and cannot be compared as the same output norm/objective. The MUB/SIC advantage is exact attainment and a closed-form inverse, at a quadratic readout count; it does not establish lower photon risk.

## Source theorem caveat

The 2026 article's Theorem 3.1 is worded “For any matrix (A\in\mathbb H^{m\times d})” and omits (d\ge2). Literally this has a (d=1) counterexample: for nonzero scalar sensing rows, \(\Psi_A(x)=(|a_j|^2|x|^2)_j\) and \(\operatorname{dist}(x,y)=||x|^2-|y|^2|\), so (L=U=\|(|a_j|^2)_j\|_2\), giving \(\beta=1\), not 2. The proof itself reduces to the first two columns and thus also presumes (d\ge2). The equality result and all claims here explicitly assume (d\ge2).

## Next decisive gate

Ask a phase-retrieval/frame-theory specialist to check (i) the equality propagation through the source's (U,L,M) bounds, (ii) polarization from constant (q(x)=\sum|a_j^*x|^4\) to the full tensor identity, and (iii) whether a previous paper states this exact ℓ2 equality characterization. A stronger advance would require a quantitative stability theorem: if \(\beta\le2+\varepsilon\), how close must the weighted fourth moment be to a projective 2-design, and how does this force a nontrivial measurement-count gap when (m\ll d^2)? That step remains open in this work.
