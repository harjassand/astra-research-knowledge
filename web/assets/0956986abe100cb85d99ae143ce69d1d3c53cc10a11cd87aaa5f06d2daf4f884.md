# Conditional contact–depletion bound

## Statement

Consider independent semi-infinite diffusion columns indexed by position x. Let each column have the same time-independent diffusivity D>0, initial concentration c0>0, and Faraday constant F. The imposed nominal-area current density is a constant j_nom>0. Suppose every local stripping flux is nonnegative and satisfies

\[
0\leq j(x,s)\leq \kappa\,\bar j\qquad(0\leq s\leq t)
\]

for a known finite amplification bound \(\kappa\geq1\). Then no column reaches zero surface concentration before

\[
t_{\min}=\frac{\pi D c_0^2 F^2}{4\kappa^2\bar j^2}
=\frac{\mathrm{VSC}}{\kappa^2\bar j^2},
\qquad \mathrm{VSC}:=\frac{\pi D c_0^2F^2}{4}.
\]

Consequently, a sufficient diffusion-only operating condition for delivered charge per nominal area (Q=\bar j t) is

\[
\boxed{\kappa^2\bar j Q<\mathrm{VSC}.}
\]

Equivalently, for a target (Q_*>0), the constant nominal current should satisfy

\[
\bar j<\frac{\mathrm{VSC}}{\kappa^2 Q_*},
\qquad \kappa<\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{VSC}}{\bar jQ_*}}.
\]

If the remaining interface is a uniform active fraction \(\alpha\) and all current passes through it, then \(\kappa=1/\alpha\); the sufficient bound becomes \(\bar j<\alpha^2\mathrm{VSC}/Q_*\). This quadratic area penalty follows from both the local flux and the shortened local Sand time. It is a sufficient comparison bound, not a necessary criterion for a heterogeneous electro-chemo-mechanical cell.

## Why a scalar impedance does not identify kappa

There is an exact counterexample in a parallel-patch interface model. Let nominal area be A, terminal voltage be V, and measured total conductance be G=I/V. For any active fraction alpha between 0 and 1, put all conductance on a patch of area alpha*A with local specific conductance g=G/(alpha*A), and make the rest insulating. Every member has the same measured Z=1/G and total I. But local patch current density is g*V=I/(alpha*A), so the amplification kappa=(local current)/(I/A)=1/alpha can be arbitrarily large while scalar impedance stays fixed. Therefore, absent a bound on local conductance or a validated uniform-patch model, global EIS and applied current do not provide the required kappa. If specific conductance is known uniform on active patches and lateral spreading is negligible, impedance may identify alpha; those premises do not follow from EIS alone and must be separately validated.

## Proof

For a one-dimensional semi-infinite diffusion column with a prescribed inward stripping flux, the surface concentration is

\[
c_s(x,t)=c_0-\frac{1}{F\sqrt{\pi D}}\int_0^t\frac{j(x,s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}\,ds.
\]

Using nonnegative flux and the assumed pointwise bound,

\[
c_s(x,t)\geq c_0-\frac{\kappa\bar j}{F\sqrt{\pi D}}
\int_0^t(t-s)^{-1/2}\,ds
=c_0-\frac{2\kappa\bar j\sqrt t}{F\sqrt{\pi D}}.
\]

The right side is positive for (t<\pi D c_0^2F^2/(4\kappa^2\bar j^2)). This proves the time bound. Multiplying by nominal current density \(\bar j\) gives the delivered nominal-area charge bound. Setting \(\kappa=1\) recovers the Sand relation and the VSC identity reported by Ji et al. The argument does not model lateral transport, evolving diffusivity, void mechanics, electrolyte fields, nonlinear kinetics, temperature/pressure dependence, or cycling fatigue.

## Composed screening rule (cross-paper candidate, not validated on LiMgLa)

For a real candidate, a diffusion limit alone is not enough. A conservative screening envelope would also impose a separately acquired mechanical/contact limit:

\[
Q_*<\min\!\left\{
\frac{\mathrm{VSC}}{\kappa^2\bar j},
C_{\rm cr}^{\rm particle}(p,\bar j,a,a_0,f,Z,Z_0;\text{Li creep data}),
Q_{\rm fatigue}
\right\}.
\]

Here \(C_{\rm cr}^{\rm particle}\) is the critical capacity in Vema et al.'s idealized particle-deposition/creep model (their Supplementary Eqs. S18–S25); \(Q_{\rm fatigue}\) denotes a measured cycling limit, not a parameter supplied by either model. The minimum is only valid when all three limits are independently appropriate for the same material and protocol. It is not a theorem for real LiMgLa cells.

Vema et al.'s no-initial-particle monolayer relation is

\[
H_{\rm cr}=\frac{4a^3}{3f\ell_{\rm cr}^2},\qquad
C_{\rm cr}=H_{\rm cr}\frac{F}{\Omega_{\rm Li}},
\]

with \(\ell_{\rm cr}\) obtained from their creep/stripping balance, Eq. S18. For nonzero starting interface resistance, their correction is Eq. S25 and requires \(Z,Z_0,a_0\) as well. Their own paper reports that particles deposit nonuniformly and the uniform-layer model misses the gradual voltage rise before the critical capacity. Particle-layer transfer to LiMgLa is therefore an experimental hypothesis; elemental maps do not give \(a,f,Z\), particle conductivity, or the Li creep law for the inoculant-derived phase.

## Conditional numeric design screen

Ji et al.'s Supplementary Table 3 reports LiMgLa \(\mathrm{VSC}=1.46\times10^6\ \mathrm{A^2s\,m^{-4}}\), equal to 4.0556 in the product units \((\mathrm{mA\,cm^{-2}})(\mathrm{mAh\,cm^{-2}})\). At the paper's reported LiMgLa critical pair \(\bar j=2.2\ \mathrm{mA\,cm^{-2}}, Q_*=1.1\ \mathrm{mAh\,cm^{-2}}\), the independent-column sufficient bound requires \(\kappa<1.295\); under the uniform-active-fraction idealization this is \(\alpha>0.772\). This is a proposed measurable acceptance threshold, not evidence that the source cell has that active fraction or that the paper's critical point is governed by the Sand model.

The source-data fit ambiguity matters at this threshold. Regressing the eight Fig. 2f LiMgLa depletion points with an unconstrained intercept yields \(\beta=0.00845084\) per \(\mathrm{mA\,cm^{-2}}\), intercept \(0.00117725\ \mathrm{s^{-1/2}}\), and \(R^2=0.9763\). Transforming only \(\beta\) to \(1/(\beta/10)^2\) gives (1.4002\times10^6\ \mathrm{A^2s\,m^{-4}}\), but the nonzero intercept means this is not the canonical through-origin Sand law. Re-fitting through the origin gives \(1.1634\times10^6\) and a different threshold \(\kappa<1.155\), or \(\alpha>0.866\). These are alternative internal fits, not a confidence interval. Table 3's reported \(D,c_0\) algebraically gives \(1.4203\times10^6\), near its rounded VSC value. No source equation defect is indicated; the exact provenance of the reported effective coefficient and treatment of the free intercept require clarification.

## Decisive physical test

Use same-batch LLZTO and same nominal area, temperature, stack pressure, and matched initial interfacial resistance for three anodes: pure Li, Li97.5Mg2.5, and Li97.5(Mg–1 wt% La)2.5. In separate cells at the reported 2.2 mA cm−2 / 1.1 mAh cm−2 target and at lower matched rates, obtain:

1. operando or rapidly interrupted contact-area maps that estimate a spatial upper bound \(\kappa(t)=\sup_x j(x,t)/\bar j\) (area fraction alone suffices only under the uniform-active-area assumption);
2. three-electrode voltage plus impedance during stripping, rest, and restart;
3. blinded cryo-PFIB/SEM/EDS maps at preregistered capacities to quantify Mg/La-rich inclusion radii, coverage/volume fraction, and whether residues remain at the interface after full stripping;
4. same-condition transport calibration for \(D,c_0\) (including uncertainty) and independent Li creep/pressure calibration for any proposed particle gate.

The primary outcome is the first capacity at which contact loss or the predeclared overpotential/voltage criterion occurs, jointly predicted by \(\kappa^2\bar jQ\), measured starting resistance, and inclusion descriptors. A residue-history mechanism is supported only if residue coverage predicts restart resistance/critical capacity after matching starting resistance and if the predicted pressure dependence is reproduced. It is falsified for this composition/protocol if maps show no retained interface residues or residue measures add no predictive value beyond initial resistance, pressure, and the Sand/current-constriction covariates. Pure Li and Mg-only controls distinguish the specific La-assisted inoculant from generic alloying; replicate cells and blinded mapping are needed because Vema et al. report strong cell-to-cell starting-interface variation.

## Cost and observability

The design bound is not a free control law. It requires a real-time or protocol-level upper bound on the maximum local flux, not only applied current or global impedance. Acquiring it needs validated spatial operando imaging/current inference; cryo-SEM/EDS is destructive and cannot supply the on-line bound. The diffusion parameter \(D\) and initial active Li concentration \(c_0\) must be measured for the same composition, temperature, pressure, and state. Vema's particle model also charges stack pressure, creep constants, particle size/fraction, and starting resistance. No lab-runtime, cell-count, energy, or instrumentation costs were available in these papers; no external experiment was performed here.

## Status

The comparison theorem is internally derived under its stated diffusion interface. The VSC arithmetic and source-data fits are finite computations. The particle-capacity gate is source-reported for high-purity Li/LLZO cells. Applying either gate to LiMgLa as a physically predictive model is UNKNOWN. Novelty and priority of the composed envelope are unreviewed; standard Sand depletion, contact-area constriction, and impurity-layer accumulation each have prior art.
