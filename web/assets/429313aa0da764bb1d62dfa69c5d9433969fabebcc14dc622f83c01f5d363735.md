# Finite graph reaction–diffusion safety from the same facet certificate

7 October 2026. This is an elementary corollary of the proved stopped-generator
theorem in `chemical_jump_certificate.md`. Its inputs are a supplied full
dimensional reaction polytope and uniform local reaction margins. It provides
an explicit finite-volume probability bound, including the number of sites and
transport turnover. It supplies no continuum-limit theorem or efficient
trajectory simulation. Historical priority of this composition is UNKNOWN.

## Exact spatial interface

Let G be a supplied finite simple undirected graph with N vertices, E edges and maximum
degree Delta. Each vertex v has d species counts n_v in Z_{>=0}^d and the same
local volume parameter V, with concentration X_v=n_v/V. At every vertex the
reaction increments are the same fixed integer vectors nu_r. The local
reaction intensities are V a_{vr}^V(t), predictable/nonanticipating, possibly
dependent on the full past of the graph. Uniformly over sites and allowed
controls, while the relevant local concentration is in Q,

    a_{vr}^V <= abar_r,  Lambda_R=sum_r abar_r,
    s_ir=n_i dot nu_r,  Q_Ri=sum_r abar_r s_ir^2.

Here

    Q={x in R^d: g_i(x)=n_i dot x+b_i >= 0, i=1,...,m}

is compact, full dimensional and contained in the strictly positive orthant;
only proper facets are retained. A compact bound 0<x_j<=R is supplied. Unlike
the single-vessel theorem, this corollary does not use a proper affine local
conservation class. Individual molecular hops leave such a class even when
the deterministic diffusion happens to preserve the corresponding totals.

For every oriented version v->w of an edge and species j, the transport event
moves one molecule of species j from v to w, with intensity

    V D_{vw}(t) X_{v,j}(t-).

The coefficient is predictable, 0<=D_{vw}<=D_max, symmetric D_{vw}=D_{wv},
and common across all species. It may vary by edge, time and past history.
Symmetry and the common coefficient are part of the interface: arbitrary
directed transport or different species coefficients do not have the algebra
used below. There are no additional transport channels or reservoirs.
Each jump is one declared local reaction or one declared molecular hop;
simultaneous batches would require separate combined-increment bounds.

Assume the actual finite-V reaction drift satisfies

    sum_r a_{vr}^V s_ir >= delta_Ri > 0
    whenever X_v in Q and 0<=g_i(X_v)<=eta_Ri,

uniformly over the stated controls and sites. This can be supplied directly or
obtained from the exact mass-action correction in the companion theorem. If
the limiting reaction margin is gamma_i and its local factorial drift error is
at most D_i/V, V>=2D_i/gamma_i gives delta_Ri=gamma_i/2. This V threshold is
local; it does not acquire an extra factor N.

Choose any new collar widths eta_i<=eta_Ri with

    delta_i := delta_Ri-D_max Delta eta_i > 0.                 (G1)

For example eta_i=min{eta_Ri,delta_Ri/(2 D_max Delta)} gives
delta_i>=delta_Ri/2, with the second term interpreted as infinity when
D_max Delta=0. Set

    J_i=max{max_r |s_ir|, max_j |n_{i,j}|},
    Q_i=Q_Ri+2 D_max Delta R sum_j n_{i,j}^2,
    Lambda_loc=Lambda_R+2 D_max Delta d R,
    theta_i=min{1/J_i,2 delta_i/(e Q_i)},
    B_i=Lambda_loc[exp(theta_i J_i)-1],  rho_i=theta_i eta_i.

Strict reaction drift implies the relevant J_i and Q_i are positive. Initial
counts must be lattice compatible and have every X_v(0) in the interior of Q.
Let tau be the first actual jump outside Q^N. Then, for every finite T,

    P(tau<=T) <= sum_{v=1}^N sum_{i=1}^m
                       exp[-V theta_i g_i(X_v(0))]
                   + V T N sum_{i=1}^m B_i exp[-V rho_i].      (G2)

For starts with uniform gaps g_i(X_v(0))>=zeta_i>0, put
alpha_*=min_i theta_i zeta_i and rho_*=min_i rho_i. With constants uniform in
V and graph size, N(V)<=exp(beta V) and T(V)=exp(c V) give vanishing error if

    beta<alpha_*,  beta+c<rho_*.                              (G3)

In particular fixed graphs allow every 0<c<rho_*. If the initial gaps exceed
the new collar widths, beta+c<rho_* also covers the initial-error term.
The node count multiplies the bound; it cannot be suppressed from an
exponentially growing family of graphs.

## Proof, including transport and stopping

The predictable concentration drift of facet i at vertex v is its reaction
drift plus

    n_i dot sum_{w~v} D_{vw}(X_w-X_v)
      =sum_{w~v} D_{vw}[g_i(X_w)-g_i(X_v)].

Before exit all neighboring gaps are nonnegative. In the new collar the
transport contribution is therefore at least -D_max Delta eta_i. By (G1) the
total drift is at least delta_i. No deterministic contraction to an inner set
is needed.

For phi_{vi}=exp[-theta_i V g_i(X_v)], only reactions at v and hops incident
to v alter phi_{vi}. Its generator is

    L phi_{vi}/(V phi_{vi})
      =sum_r a_{vr}^V[exp(-theta_i s_ir)-1]
       +sum_{w~v,j} D_{vw}
          {X_{v,j}[exp(theta_i n_{i,j})-1]
           +X_{w,j}[exp(-theta_i n_{i,j})-1]}.

Each projected jump has absolute size at most J_i. The sum of squared
projected jumps times their scaled intensities is bounded by Q_i, since the
two directed intensities on one incident edge contribute at most
2 D_max R n_{i,j}^2. The total scaled intensity of all events affecting v is
at most Lambda_loc. Taylor's integral remainder, exactly as in the companion
proof, now yields

    L phi_{vi}<=0  when 0<=g_i(X_v)<=eta_i,
    L phi_{vi}<=V B_i exp(-V rho_i) throughout Q^N.

Sum over vertices and facets. A hop changes two vertices, but linearity of
the generator makes the sum valid without an independence assumption; the
per-vertex intensity bound intentionally counts an incident hop at both ends.

The actual global pre-exit intensity obeys the tighter bound

    V{N Lambda_R+2 D_max d R |E|}
      <= V N Gamma,  Gamma=Lambda_R+D_max Delta d R.           (G4)

Thus there is no explosion before exit for each fixed finite graph and V.
An exit event changes any affected site-facet gap by at most J_i/V, so the
stopped sum Phi=sum_{v,i} phi_{vi} is bounded by N sum_i exp(theta_i J_i).
The compensated stopped jump process is a true martingale on a finite time
interval. At exit at least one gap is negative, hence Phi>=1. Stopped Dynkin
and the generator bound prove (G2). No independence of site states or control
histories is used; the declared conditional jump intensities, predictability
and uniform bounds are sufficient.

## A concrete failure when species hop at different rates

Consider two sites joined by one edge. At both sites use the weakly reversible
linear reactions 0<->A and 0<->B, with births of intensity 2V and deaths equal
to the relevant molecule counts. Their exact finite-V drift is

    F(x,y)=(2-x,2-y).

The full dimensional positive polytope

    Q={1<=x<=3, 1<=y<=3, x+y>=3}

has reaction drift at least 1 into every proper facet on that facet. In collars
of width 1/4 all five reaction margins are at least 3/4. For common edge
coefficient D<=1, (G1) gives delta_i>=1/2, and (G2) applies with N=2, Delta=1,
R=3, Lambda_R=10, J_i=1, Lambda_loc=22. The four coordinate facets have
Q_i=11, the sum facet has Q_i=22, and a valid choice is theta_i=1/(e Q_i).
Starting both sites at (2,2), all five gaps are 1, and rho_*=1/(88e).

Now instead let A hop at rate 1 and B at rate 3. At site states (1,2) and
(2,1), the first site's total drift is

    (1,0)+(1,-3)=(2,-3),

whose sum-facet component is -1. Thus the product polytope fails to be
invariant even though the reaction vector field points strictly into each
local facet.

This failure persists for explicitly buffered initial states. Set
epsilon=1/1000 and use (1+epsilon,2) and (2,1+epsilon). Every proper facet gap
is positive initially. The deterministic reaction–diffusion system is linear.
At the first site, writing u=exp(-t), its sum-facet gap is exactly

    g(t)=1-(1-epsilon)[u+u^3/2-u^7/2].

At t=-log(99/100) this rational expression is negative. The accompanying
script checks the exact rational inequality. This is a counterexample to
automatic product-polytope invariance with unequal species transport, not
a claim that every unequal-diffusion network is unsafe or that no different
spatial certificate exists.

`chemical_graph_checks.py` verifies all 200 reaction/neighbor/coefficient
corner cases for the five collars with common D, the exact projected noise
and intensity bounds, and the fully buffered unequal-D exit gap
-1621541844091699/200000000000000000. Its output is
`chemical_graph_checks.json`. Affinity in the two site states and the common
coefficient makes these endpoint checks a certificate for this finite
example's local inequalities; the general probability theorem rests on the
analytic proof above.

## Charged resources and the mesh-refinement limit

The supplied or acquired inputs include the reaction source list, actual local
drift certificates, compact bound R, graph, coefficient symmetry/common-species
rule, D_max, local volume V, and every initial site count. Acquiring the
polytope, its collars and reaction inequalities retains the potentially large
geometry/polynomial cost of the companion theorem. Graph transport adds
O(md) projected data once the reaction certificate is acquired. An explicitly
listed graph and initial state contain N+|E|+Nd entries to read; computing unstructured
initial facet gaps costs O(Nmd). Uniform bounds let a single local certificate
serve all sites, rather than requiring N independent reaction proofs.

The product process retains Nd integer counts, a graph with N vertices and
|E| edges, the model/certificate and whatever history its supplied controller
requires. Count storage is O(Nd log(1+VR)) bits for fixed integer encodings;
graph labels and adjacency have their own O((N+|E|)log(1+N)) bit cost. The
physical molecule inventory while inside Q is of order VN: a strictly positive
compact Q bounds it between d V N x_min and d V N R. Compressed counts do not remove
that physical resource.

A full stochastic trajectory executes, in expectation before time T or exit,
at most V{N Lambda_R+2 D_max d R |E|}T events. Hop turnover contributes
2V D_max d R |E|T to this envelope. Selecting and updating events, evaluating
history-dependent rates, random-bit precision and output storage are further
costs. This theorem evaluates a probability certificate with finitely many
constants; it does not execute those events or supply a shortcut for their
full trajectory distribution.

There is no uniform continuum conclusion from fixed-graph exponential safety.
For a conventional mesh with spacing h and hopping coefficient kappa/h^2,
bounded degree and a fixed local reaction certificate, this generic choice
has eta_i=O(h^2), theta_i=O(h^2) and rho_i=O(h^4). Thus the displayed safety
bound needs V(h)rho_*(h) to dominate log N(h)+log[V(h)T(h)] to remain useful,
as well as the initial-gap term. For N(h) of order h^{-p} and local volume
V(h)=Omega h^p, its reliability exponent scales as Omega h^{p+4}, while the
hop-event envelope scales as Omega h^{-2}T. These are limits of this generic
certificate and direct simulation ledger, not necessary scaling laws for the
actual process or an optimal spatial rare-event estimate.

The common-coefficient drift calculation is a finite graph maximum-principle
calculation. Invariant-region methods for reaction–diffusion equations long
predate this release; the publisher's original first page of Chueh, Conley
and Smoller (1977) was inspected:
[Chueh, Conley and Smoller, original first page](https://iumj.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/abstracts/26029_abs.pdf).
Neither that deterministic mechanism nor exponential stopped-Lyapunov
arguments are claimed new. This report's contribution is a precise,
costed finite stochastic composition with the supplied recent affine
certificate and a concrete compatibility falsification. Priority is not
cleared and no external mathematical validation is claimed.
