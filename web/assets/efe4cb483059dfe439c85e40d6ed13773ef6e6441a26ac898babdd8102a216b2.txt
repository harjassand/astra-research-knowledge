RESULT

Let n=2q, F an explicit rational-complex opposite-spin pair matrix, and
1<=k<=q. Acquire G from the fixed pairs (0,1),(2,3),...:
 retain G_ii=F_ii;
 retain real a_j=Re F_(2j,2j+1), b_j=Re F_(2j+1,2j);
 set all other off-diagonal entries of G to zero.
Let beta_j=a_j^2+b_j^2, beta_min=min_j beta_j, and
Delta^2=||F-G||_F^2, all exactly computable rationals.

If
 beta_min>0 and Delta^2<=beta_min/(256 k^2),                     (C)
then c_k(F) has a polynomial-bit FPRAS with
O(epsilon^-2 log(1/failure)) samples, each polynomial-bit time in n,L.
There is no exponential k factor, and k can equal n/2. Every draw is between
3c_k(G)/4 and 4c_k(G)/3, so the relative second moment is at most 256/81<4.
No unknown partition or success probability is supplied. The baseline
c_k(G)=e_k(beta_1,...,beta_q) is acquired by an elementary-symmetric recurrence.
The admissibility test (C) is F-only and rational; failure is NOT_CERTIFIED.

If (C) holds with k=q, the same sample vector admits simultaneous relative
approximation of all canonical coefficients and the grandcanonical norm;
use confidence failure/(q+1) for each coefficient and a union bound. This
needs O(epsilon^-2 log((q+1)/failure)) shared sign draws. The implementation
below has a complete single-sector FPRAS wrapper; the all-vector confidence
wrapper follows this proof and is not separately implemented.

This is a norm counter for a certified neighborhood of real matching
matrices, not a general matrix theorem or an arbitrary physical-prefix

1. THE PEER ESTIMATOR AND AN INVARIANCE IT LEAVES AVAILABLE

Write u_i=e_i, v_i=row_i(F)^T; paired columns V=(u_0,v_0,...,u_(n-1),v_(n-1)).
For arbitrary fixed complex phases alpha_i of modulus one, and independent
uniform real signs s_i, put t_i=alpha_i s_i and

 A_t(F)=diag(t)F-F^T diag(t),
 X_k(t;F)=sum_{|R|=2k}|Pf(A_t(F)[R])|^2.                       (1)

Pfaffian minor summation gives, for each R,

 Pf(A_t[R])=sum_{|S|=k} (product_(i in S) alpha_i s_i)
                                det V[R,P(S)].               (2)

For S!=T, E chi_S(s)chi_T(s)=0. For S=T, the phase product has squared
modulus one. Consequently

 E X_k(t;F)=sum_S sum_R |det V[R,P(S)]|^2=c_k(F).               (3)

The last step is Cauchy-Binet with the forced identity columns. Thus fixed
site phases preserve unbiasedness for every F, not only the admitted class.
Independence or identical distribution of the complex t_i is unnecessary;
only the real signs are independently sampled. The n phases below are
exactly 1 or i, so no irrational field or numerical phase approximation is
needed.

The algebraic sample is computable in polynomial time:

 X_k(t;F)=[z^k]sqrt(det(I+z A_t(F)* A_t(F))).                   (4)

Under unitary skew congruence A has q blocks [[0,sigma_j],[-sigma_j,0]].
The squared norm of its k-fold exterior two-form is e_k(sigma_j^2), and
det(I+z A*A)=product_j(1+z sigma_j^2)^2. This proves (4). Unitary congruence
acts unitarily on exterior coordinates, so the norm identity holds before
the change of basis. The square root is the unique formal series with
constant term one, not a numerical square root.

2. A BASELINE WITH EVERY SAMPLE EXACTLY EQUAL TO ITS MEAN

Choose alpha_(2j)=1 and alpha_(2j+1)=i. On the real matching G, its skew
matrix has independent blocks with off-diagonal entry

 a_j s_(2j)-i b_j s_(2j+1).

The squared modulus is exactly beta_j=a_j^2+b_j^2, for every sign word.
Thus its nonzero singular values are sqrt(beta_j), each repeated twice,
and every draw has

 X_k(t;G)=e_k(beta_1,...,beta_q)=c_k(G).                        (5)

The elementary symmetric recurrence dp[l]+=beta_j dp[l-1], descending l,
computes (5) in O(nk) rational operations. G's arbitrary diagonal cancels
from A_t and cannot enter the hard minors. Negative real matching entries
are allowed; only beta_min>0 is required.

For comparison, with unphased real signs and a_j=b_j=1 at k=q,

 X_q(s;G)=product_j(s_(2j)-s_(2j+1))^2
         =4^q with probability 2^-q, zero otherwise.

Its relative second moment is 2^q. This is c03_s02's raw-estimator failure,
not hardness of the exactly countable matching norm. The phased estimator
has relative second moment exactly one on the same input.

3. ROBUSTNESS: A UNIFORM BOUND FOR EVERY DRAW

Let E=F-G. For every t with |t_i|=1,

 ||A_t(F)-A_t(G)||_2
 <=||diag(t)E||_2+||E^T diag(t)||_2<=2||E||_2<=2Delta.          (6)

Transpose preserves singular values even for complex matrices. Ordered
singular values obey |sigma_l(B)-sigma_l(C)|<=||B-C||_2. This follows from
the min-max characterization: for every unit vector, its image norm changes
by at most ||B-C||_2; apply the same dimension-minimax to each index and
then interchange B,C.

Put rho=2Delta/sqrt(beta_min). Condition (C) gives rho<=1/(8k).
Every singular value of A_t(G) is at least sqrt(beta_min), so the sorted
singular values of A_t(F) satisfy

 (1-rho)sigma_l(A_t(G))<=sigma_l(A_t(F))
                            <=(1+rho)sigma_l(A_t(G)).         (7)

Both skew matrices' singular values occur in identical pairs. Taking one
from each pair, every k-fold product of their squared singular values is
bounded by factors (1-rho)^(2k) and (1+rho)^(2k). Sum all such products to
obtain, using (4) and (5),

 (1-rho)^(2k)c_k(G)<=X_k(t;F)<=(1+rho)^(2k)c_k(G).             (8)

There is no cancellation estimate in (8); it is a positive elementary-
symmetric polynomial of squared singular values. It holds for all sign
words and arbitrary complex residual E. By Bernoulli's inequality the
lower factor is at least 1-2k rho>=3/4. For m=2k and mx<=1/4,
(1+x)^m=sum_j binomial(m,j)x^j<=sum_(j>=0)(mx)^j<=4/3.
This proves the claimed every-execution interval

 3c_k(G)/4<=X_k(t;F)<=4c_k(G)/3.                              (9)

Taking expectations also places c_k(F) in the same interval. Therefore

 E X_k^2/c_k(F)^2<=( (4/3)/(3/4) )^2=256/81<4.                (10)

This is the missing variance reduction: it holds at k=q=Theta(n) on the
admitted class. A numerical/approximate singular-value computation is not
part of the admission algorithm; (C) is squared, exact, and rational.

4. COMPLETE FPRAS, ACQUISITION AND BIT COST

Draw n independent fair bits to choose s. Form t with the fixed 1/i phases.
Let B=A_t*A_t. Compute traces T_j=tr(B^j) through j=k and the determinant
coefficients by Newton's recurrence

 d_0=1, d_m=(1/m)sum_(j=1)^m (-1)^(j-1)d_(m-j)T_j.

Then the exact formal-square-root coefficients satisfy

 x_0=1, x_m=(d_m-sum_(j=1)^(m-1)x_j x_(m-j))/2.

Return x_k. This is O(k n^3+k^2) rational-complex arithmetic by naive
matrix multiplication. Trace imaginary parts and final coefficient imaginary
parts vanish exactly. No principal-minor or partition oracle is called.

A batch of m=ceil(16/epsilon^2) independent samples has relative-error
failure <=1/4 by (10) and Chebyshev. Use an odd number R=8b+1 of independent
batches, where b=ceil(log2(1/failure)), and take their median. Hoeffding gives
median failure <=exp(-R/8)<=2^-b<=failure. This uses a fixed, capped
mR samples and nmR fair bits on every execution. k=0 is exactly one.

The acquisition step constructs G from F and computes Delta^2,beta_min
and (C), all in polynomial rational bit time. No optimal matching finder,
F acquisition or best-approximation oracle is assumed. A different supplied
matching can be used after its coordinate permutation is charged; the
F-only public rule uses the fixed adjacent pairs.

To verify bit height, clear input real/imaginary denominators by a common
integer D of O(L) bits. The fixed phases are Gaussian integers of unit
modulus. Each principal Pfaffian of order 2k has denominator D^k, so each
sample has denominator dividing D^(2k) and polynomial numerator bits by
factorial/Hadamard bounds. Matrix powers and Newton recurrences likewise
have polynomial bit heights through k<=n/2. All samples share this common
denominator, so averaging mR draws adds only O(log(mR)) numerator bits.
There is no inverse c_k(F), rare-event simulation, input conditioning or
variable precision hidden in the runtime. Costs are polynomial in n,L,
epsilon^-1 and log(1/failure), including dense sectors.

5. AN INFINITE DENSE FULL-RANK HIGH-CUTRANK FAMILY

Take even n, k=n/2, P the adjacent swap permutation, and H_ij=1/(i+j+1),
i,j=0,...,n-1. Let

 F=P+tH, t=1/(32kn).                                         (11)

The fixed acquired matching G retains the real perturbed matching edges,
so beta_min>=2. The off-matching residual has Frobenius norm <=tn=1/(32k),
which satisfies (C) since 1/(1024k^2)<=2/(256k^2). Diagonal H contributions
are copied to G and do not count as residual. The perturbation is only
polynomially small, not exponentially small in n. Every off-diagonal entry
is positive and the support graph is complete.

The unperturbed P is orthogonal and ||tH||_2<=tn<1. Therefore every singular
value of F is at least 1-tn>0, so F has rank n and is well conditioned.
If n is a multiple of four, the middle cut lies between matching blocks:
both cross blocks of P are zero, and F's cross blocks are t times square
Cauchy matrices H[L,R],H[R,L], each full rank n/2. To prove this directly,
a linear dependence among p columns of 1/(x_i+y_j) would give a rational
function sum_j c_j/(x+y_j) with p distinct zeros x_i. Its numerator has
degree at most p-1 and must vanish identically. Distinct poles then imply
every residue c_j=0. Thus the ordered cut-rank sum is n.

This family has acquired rank n, support treewidth n-1, and ordered cutrank
n, for arbitrarily large n. Those exact structural solvers' displayed
parameters are large. Nonetheless (9) gives a dense-sector norm FPRAS.
No theorem excludes some other special exact representation or another
tractable route for this family. It is a certified subclass, not a
complexity separation. The general pair matrix is still unresolved.

6. WHY IID COMPLEX PHASES ALONE DO NOT GIVE THIS RESULT

For one unit swap block with independent identically distributed unit phases
z_1,z_2 and E z=0, put Y=|z_1-z_2|^2. Then E Y=2 and

 E Y^2=6+2|E z^2|^2>=6.

More generally for iid mean-zero complex variables of E|z|^2=1, expansion
gives E Y^2=2 E|z|^4+4+2|E z^2|^2>=6; the mixed terms vanish by E z=0.
Independent blocks therefore have relative second moment at least (3/2)^q.
Uniform circle phases and uniform fourth roots have E z^2=0 and attain this
bound; complex Gaussian choices are no improvement here. The exact four-
root one-block check gives mean 2, second moment 6. The successful phases
in (5) are heterogeneous fixed phases times real signs, preserving the
required Fourier orthogonality while forcing each matching block's two
orientations into quadrature. The iid lower bound does not cover this
heterogeneous construction or adaptively chosen joint distributions.
