Leaf 18 — Relative-entropy tail obstruction in the Boltzmann Euler scaling
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: ANALYTIC_COUNTEREXAMPLE_TO_ENTROPY_ONLY_FLUX_CONTROL; NOT A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO A HYDRODYNAMIC-LIMIT THEOREM

Question and verdict

Does bounded (or vanishing, correctly scaled) relative entropy alone give uniform integrability of the third-velocity-moment energy flux in the Boltzmann hydrodynamic limit? No. An explicit smooth, spatially homogeneous Gaussian-mixture family has exactly the reference mass, momentum, and energy, has H(f_eps|M)=O(eps^3)=o(eps^2), and converges to M in L1, while its energy-flux vector tends to a nonzero constant. Thus the scaled fluctuation's third-moment flux diverges. This isolates a missing proof input: entropy convergence by itself does not justify passing the unbounded third-moment flux. It does not show failure of the Boltzmann limit for actual solutions; in particular this is an initial-data family, not an evolution counterexample.

1. Interface, scaling, and entropy inequality

Take x on a unit-volume periodic box and v in R^3. Let M(v)=(2 pi)^(-3/2) exp(-|v|^2/2), the unit-mass, zero-mean, unit-variance Maxwellian. Consider the standard incompressible Euler scaling

  eps partial_t f_eps + v dot grad_x f_eps = eps^(-gamma) Q(f_eps,f_eps),  gamma>1.

For a nonnegative density f, define

  H(f|M) = integral [f log(f/M)-f+M] dv,

and, for a Grad-cutoff collision kernel, define the entropy production

  D(f) = (1/4) integral B (f'f_*' - f f_*) log((f'f_*')/(f f_*)) d omega dv_* dv >= 0.

The collision invariants imply integral Q(f,f) log M dv=0, while the H-theorem gives integral Q(f,f) log f dv=-D(f). Integrating the kinetic equation over the periodic x domain, multiplying by log(f_eps/M), and integrating in (x,v), the transport term vanishes and yields, for smooth solutions,

  H(f_eps(t)|M) + eps^(-(gamma+1)) integral_0^t integral_x D(f_eps) dx ds = H(f_eps(0)|M).

For renormalized solutions this is the corresponding entropy inequality. This estimate supplies nonincrease of relative entropy and integrated dissipation; it does not by itself bound a third velocity moment uniformly integrably.

For f_eps=M(1+eps g_eps), the entropy density is

  M[(1+eps g_eps)log(1+eps g_eps)-eps g_eps]
       = (eps^2/2) M g_eps^2 + higher-order terms

where an expansion is justified by suitable integrability. The hydrodynamic regime uses H(f_eps|M)=O(eps^2), and entropic convergence strengthens this to H/eps^2 -> (1/2) integral M g^2. The counterexample below satisfies the stronger zero-limit case H/eps^2 -> 0.

The basic entropy Young inequality for probability densities is

  integral f phi dv <= H(f|M) + log integral M exp(phi) dv.

For phi=a|v|^2 with 0<a<1/2, integral M exp(a|v|^2)=(1-2a)^(-3/2), so entropy controls a second-moment bound. For phi=a|v|^3 with any a>0 that Gaussian exponential integral is infinite. This is the precise exponent gap: the cubic energy-flux weight is beyond the Gaussian entropy dual's quadratic range.

2. Explicit family and full moment/entropy calculation

Let eps -> 0, R_eps=eps^(-3), c_eps=eps^3, and delta_eps=c_eps/R_eps^2=eps^9. Set

  b_eps = -delta_eps R_eps/(1-delta_eps),
  s_eps^2 = 1 - c_eps/[3(1-delta_eps)]
            - c_eps^2/[3 R_eps^2 (1-delta_eps)^2].

For sufficiently small eps, s_eps^2>0. Let G_{mu,s^2} be the probability density of N(mu e_1,s^2 I_3), and set

  f_eps(v) = (1-delta_eps) G_{b_eps,s_eps^2}(v)
             + delta_eps G_{R_eps,1}(v).

The family is smooth, positive, and independent of x. Directly from the parameter definitions:

  integral f_eps dv = 1,
  integral v f_eps dv = [(1-delta)b+delta R]e_1 = 0,
  integral |v|^2 f_eps dv
     = (1-delta)(3s^2+b^2)+delta(3+R^2)=3.

Thus mass, momentum, and kinetic energy exactly agree with M for every sufficiently small eps, rather than merely converging to the same values.

For d=3, the Gaussian relative entropy is

  H(G_{mu,s^2}|M) = (1/2)[|mu|^2+3(s^2-1-log s^2)].

Convexity of f -> H(f|M) gives

  0 <= H(f_eps|M)
     <= (1-delta) H(G_{b,s^2}|M) + delta H(G_{R,1}|M)
     = O(eps^6) + delta R^2/2
     = eps^3/2 + O(eps^6).

Consequently H(f_eps|M)=O(eps^3), H/eps^2 -> 0, and the family satisfies the entropy scaling with room to spare. Gaussian continuity in mean and variance gives ||f_eps-M||_L1=O(eps^3); hence g_eps=(f_eps/M-1)/eps tends to zero in L1(M dv), consistently with the zero entropic limit.

I omit the conventional factor 1/2 in the energy-flux definition; this constant does not affect the obstruction. For V distributed as N(mu e_1,s^2 I_3), expansion V=mu e_1+Z and E|Z|^2=3s^2 give

  E[|V|^2 V] = (mu^3+5s^2 mu)e_1.

Therefore the energy-flux vector q(f)=integral |v|^2 v f(v) dv satisfies

  q(f_eps)
    = delta(R^3+5R)e_1 + (1-delta)(b^3+5s^2 b)e_1
    = [1+5eps^6-5s_eps^2 eps^6+O(eps^18)]e_1
    -> e_1,

whereas q(M)=0. The tail's mass is only eps^9, its energy is eps^3, and its third moment is order one. Since q(f_eps)-q(M)=eps integral |v|^2 v M g_eps dv, the third-moment flux of the scaled fluctuation is eps^(-1)q(f_eps), which diverges like eps^(-1).

For every fixed velocity cutoff L, the tail component eventually lies at speeds R_eps >> L and contributes order one to integral_{|v|>L}|v|^3 f_eps dv. Thus the third moments are not uniformly integrable, despite exact matching of the first three collision-invariant moments and H/eps^2 -> 0. This also shows why moment matching through energy does not repair the missing tail step.

3. A sufficient exponential-moment condition

Here is a precise sufficient condition for the scaled cubic-flux passage. Suppose f_eps=M(1+eps g_eps), and for some a in (0,1/2),

  sup_eps integral exp(a|v|^2) |g_eps(v)| M(v) dv <= C.

Then for every L>0,

  integral_{|v|>L} |v|^3 |g_eps| M dv
    <= C sup_{r>L} r^3 exp(-a r^2) -> 0 as L -> infinity,

uniformly in eps. Therefore if g_eps converges weakly on every bounded velocity set against bounded tests, truncation plus this bound passes the full cubic energy-flux moment to the limit. The same condition controls any fixed polynomial moment, replacing 3 by its degree.

An equivalent direct formulation for the flux difference is

  sup_eps eps^(-1) integral exp(a|v|^2)|f_eps-M| dv < infinity.

For a varying local Maxwellian reference, impose the analogous exponential bound on the scaled absolute fluctuation relative to that reference, with its density, mean, and temperature uniformly in a compact nondegenerate set. This is sufficient, not necessary. The exact minimal requirement for the cubic flux is uniform integrability of |v|^3|g_eps|M itself (or the corresponding local-reference quantity); an L^(3+eta) bound is another sufficient option.

The Saint-Raymond (2009) general-data theorem's published tail assumption is different: it is a velocity-weighted L2 bound on (f_eps-M)/M, stated pointwise in (t,x), and the proof uses it together with the dissipation decomposition. In the notation here this requires a bound on integral (f_eps-M)^2/M. The tail term alone has squared weighted norm delta^2 integral G_{R,1}^2/M = delta^2 exp(R^2)=eps^18 exp(eps^-6); the centered bulk difference has bounded weighted L2 norm, so the triangle inequality shows the family violates (2.3) strongly. The exponential condition here is a transparent stand-alone sufficient replacement for moment passage, not a claim that it is the theorem's original hypothesis.

4. Comparison with primary-source baselines

• Saint-Raymond (2003), “Convergence of Solutions to the Boltzmann Equation in the Incompressible Euler Limit,” Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis 166 (2003), 47–80, DOI 10.1007/s00205-002-0228-3. This is the well-prepared-data baseline; its abstract explicitly states the result for well-prepared data without an additional assumption. The 2009 retrospective distinguishes this result from an earlier Golse route that used nonlinear weak compactness: Saint-Raymond says the extra momentum-flux condition was removed for the well-prepared Boltzmann result using refined dissipation estimates. The present construction targets the more general cubic energy-flux step discussed in 2009 and does not contradict the 2003 theorem.

• Saint-Raymond (2009), “Hydrodynamic limits: some improvements of the relative entropy method,” Annales de l’Institut Henri Poincaré C, Analyse non linéaire 26 (2009), 705–744, DOI 10.1016/j.anihpc.2008.01.001. This is the closest baseline. Section 2.1 explicitly says that for general initial data the energy flux is a third moment and “such a control ... cannot come from the relative entropy” because the entropy Young inequality is quadratic in velocity (pp. 712–713). Theorem 2.1 adds condition (2.3), a nonuniform large-velocity bound, and Remark 2.2(i) points to stronger classical solutions near Maxwellians as a source of that control. Thus the primary source already identifies exactly the proof limitation audited here; the Gaussian family gives an explicit, moment-matched sharp witness to it.

• Guo (2003), “Classical solutions to the Boltzmann equation for molecules with an angular cutoff,” Archive for Rational Mechanics and Analysis 169 (2003), 305–353, DOI 10.1007/s00205-003-0262-9. This is a stronger-solution baseline, not an entropy-only baseline. Saint-Raymond cites the Guo solution framework as one way to supply velocity-tail control. The stronger classical/weighted estimates carry assumptions beyond the relative entropy bound and exclude the constructed extreme tail.

5. Scope, falsifier, costs, and status

Validated claim: no deduction from bounded relative entropy, even H(f_eps|M)=o(eps^2), plus exact mass/momentum/energy matching can imply uniform integrability or convergence of the cubic energy flux. The construction is analytic and has no computational cost. Its exact scale costs are: tail mass eps^9; tail energy eps^3; relative entropy at most eps^3/2+O(eps^6); unscaled energy flux -> e_1; scaled fluctuation flux -> eps^(-1)e_1. The exponential sufficient condition costs an additional assumption controlling the absolute scaled fluctuation with a Gaussian exponential weight; it is strictly more informative than entropy and rules out this family.

Falsifier for the construction: an algebraic error showing that the stated Gaussian mixture fails exact mass/momentum/energy matching, has H/eps^2 not tending to zero, or has q(f_eps) not tending to e_1. Each is checkable directly from the formulas above. Falsifier for the methodological conclusion: prove a universal entropy-only cubic-flux/UI estimate under the stated mass, momentum, energy, and entropy hypotheses; the explicit family would then expose a contradiction in that estimate. A theorem that obtains flux control from the Boltzmann dissipation or from stronger solution regularity does not falsify this result; it supplies an additional input.

Not established: that the Boltzmann hydrodynamic limit fails. The construction is not shown to be a family of time-evolving Boltzmann solutions, and the collision dynamics may remove the tail during an initial layer. The 2009 theorem includes an additional tail hypothesis, so the witness lies outside that theorem's assumptions. To study theorem failure one would need actual solutions satisfying the proposed weakened theorem hypotheses and a nonvanishing flux defect on the theorem's time interval.

Restart: (1) Keep this exact Gaussian mixture as the unit test for any proposed entropy-only flux lemma. (2) Read the complete assumptions of Saint-Raymond 2009 Theorem 2.1, especially (2.3), before comparing any later theorem; do not paraphrase (2.3) as an exponential moment. (3) If extending to dynamics, solve or estimate the homogeneous Boltzmann evolution from f_eps and measure the time-integrated cubic flux on [0,T] and on [tau,T]; separate an initial-layer defect from a positive-time obstruction. (4) Test whether the theorem's entropy-production term gives a uniform time-integrated flux bound without assuming (2.3), under an explicitly fixed collision kernel. (5) Keep the status at method-level obstruction unless an actual-solution counterexample is proved.
