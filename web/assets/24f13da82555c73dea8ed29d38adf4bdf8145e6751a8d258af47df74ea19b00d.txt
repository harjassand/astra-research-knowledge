I. GENERAL VECTOR-PAIR THEOREM: COMPLETE DERIVATION

Supply n, m, vectors u_l,v_l in C^n and activities lambda_l >= 0, l=1,...,m.
For U subset [n], let R=[n]\U and define f(U)=0 if |R| is odd; otherwise
 f(U)=sum_{A subset [m], |A|=|R|/2} lambda_A |det V_A[R]|^2,
where V_A consists of the ordered columns (u_l,v_l) for l in A and lambda_A
is their product. The empty determinant is one, so f([n])=1. Repeated or zero
vectors and zero activities are allowed. Define
 Q(x)=sum_U (-1)^((n-|U|)/2) f(U) x^U.
Claim: Q is real stable and multiaffine, without any genericity assumption.

Put A_l=u_l u_l*+v_l v_l*, Hermitian PSD of rank at most two, and
 P(x,t)=det(diag(x)+sum_l t_l A_l).
When all x_i,t_l have strictly positive imaginary part, the imaginary part
of this matrix is diag(Im x)+sum_l(Im t_l)A_l >0, so P is nonzero. P has real
coefficients because its value is real for real x,t, is multiaffine in each
x_i, and has degree at most two in each t_l by rank(A_l)<=2.

On polynomials with degree at most two in t_l, define the linear map
 T_l(a+b t_l+c t_l^2)=a-lambda_l c.
Extend it as the identity on all other variables. For degree bound
kappa=(1,...,1;2,...,2), its algebraic symbol is
 (s_l^2-lambda_l) prod_i(x_i+y_i)
                         prod_{k != l}(t_k+s_k)^2.
Every linear sum is upper-half-plane nonzero. The factor s_l^2-lambda_l has
only the real roots +/-sqrt(lambda_l), also for lambda_l=0. Thus the whole
symbol is real stable. Borcea–Branden 0809.0401v3, Theorem1.2(b), proves that
T_l preserves real stability or gives the zero polynomial. Applying all T_l
therefore preserves stability or zero. The coefficient x^[n] remains one,
so the output is nonzero.

To identify it, Cauchy–Binet expands P as
 P(x,t)=sum_{B subset the 2m vector columns, |B|<=n}
   (prod_{column in B} t_{line(column)})
   sum_{R subset[n], |R|=|B|} |det V_B[R]|^2 x^[n]\R.
A monomial with t_l-degree one is killed by T_l. Degree zero is retained;
degree two acquires -lambda_l. The surviving B select both columns from
exactly the lines A. Thus the output is exactly Q, with the asserted signs
and f(U). No row-shape or orthogonality condition was used.

In particular, append ANY arbitrary pair (u,v) with activity lambda and repeat
the same proof with one more PSD rank-two matrix. This closes arbitrary
vector-pair extensions at the representation level. In general the updated
Q cannot be computed solely as a fixed coordinate-derivative operator on
old Q: cross-minor interference retains information missing from the old
norm coefficients. The theorem reconstructs Q from the supplied vectors;
it does not assert an old-Q coefficient recursion for noncoordinate pairs.

II. HURWITZ ROTATION, TWISTS AND PINNINGS

Define h(x)=i^(-n)Q(i x)=sum_U f(U)x^U. Its coefficients are nonnegative,
its parity is n modulo2, and h is nonvanishing when Re x_i>0, since Im(i x_i)>0.
For arbitrary T subset[n], the reciprocal twist
 h^T(x)=x^T h((1/x_i)_{i in T},(x_i)_{i not in T})
is multiaffine, positive-coefficient and Hurwitz stable: Re(1/z)>0 when
Re z>0. Its coefficients are f(T symmetric-difference U); its parity shifts
by |T|. Positive external fields, zero specializations and coefficient-of-x_i
operations preserve Hurwitz stability or zero. The latter coefficient is
lim_{a->infinity} h(x_i=a)/a, by multiaffinity and Hurwitz continuity.
Every nonzero bit-pinned law is therefore included. Ordinary real upper-
half-plane stability of unsigned h is neither needed nor asserted.

III. SHARP INFLUENCE BOUND: SELF-CONTAINED ANALYTIC STEP

The next proof works for ANY nonzero nonnegative multiaffine Hurwitz-stable h,
without a parity assumption. Supply arbitrary positive fields lambda. Fix i
with 0<p_i<1 and write h=A+x_i B. A and B are Hurwitz stable and nonzero, by
boundary specialization and coefficient limits. For other arguments z in the
right half-plane, the root -A(z)/B(z) cannot have positive real part. Hence
Re(A/B)>=0. Since A/B is positive real at positive real arguments, the strong
minimum principle yields Re(A/B)>0 everywhere, so R=B/A has positive real
part as well. This also covers constant ratios.

For any signs s_j in {-1,+1}, j!=i, put z_j=lambda_j exp(s_j t). If
|Im t|<pi/2, these z_j remain in the right half-plane. The analytic function
 L(t)=Log R(z(t))
with principal logarithm maps this strip into itself; L(0) is real. The maps
zeta -> 2 artanh zeta and w -> tanh(w/2) are inverse maps from the unit disk
to this strip. Therefore
 F(zeta)=tanh((L(2 artanh zeta)-L(0))/2)
is an analytic disk self-map vanishing at0. Schwarz's lemma gives
 |L'(0)|=|F'(0)|<=1.
But
 L'(0)=sum_{j!=i} s_j [P(X_j=1|X_i=1)-P(X_j=1|X_i=0)].
Choose the signs of the bracketed real numbers; this proves the asserted
absolute influence sum. Deterministic coordinates can simply be omitted.
All pinnings and arbitrary positive fields are already included by II.

Let C=Cov(X), d_i=p_i(1-p_i). For i!=j the influence is C_ij/d_i, so
sum_{j!=i}|C_ij|<=d_i. For every real v,
 v^T C v <=sum_i[d_i+sum_{j!=i}|C_ij|]v_i^2 <=2 sum_i d_i v_i^2.
This proves C<=2 diag(d), including deterministic coordinates.

For any nonnegative generating polynomial and alpha>0, direct differentiation
at x=1 gives Hess log h(x^alpha)=alpha^2 C-alpha diag(p); positive scaling
transports this identity to every point. At alpha=1/2 the covariance inequality
therefore proves global fractional log-concavity.

For the complement homogenization
 H(x,y)=sum_U f(U)x^U y^[n]\U=(prod_i y_i) h(x_i/y_i),
its covariance is [[C,-C],[-C,C]]. For arbitrary a,b,
 (a-b)^T C(a-b)<=2 sum_i p_i(1-p_i)(a_i-b_i)^2
                  <=2 sum_i[p_i a_i^2+(1-p_i)b_i^2],
using the exact identity
 p a^2+(1-p)b^2-p(1-p)(a-b)^2=(p a+(1-p)b)^2.
Thus H is also 1/2-FLC under arbitrary x,y fields. Equivalently H has constant
aperture pi/2 directly, since x_i/y_i lies in the right half-plane when both
x_i,y_i have arguments between -pi/4 and pi/4. The 1/2-FLC improvement over
the generic quarter bound is the established AASV Lemma71.

Sharpness: h=1+x_1 x_2 is Hurwitz stable. At equal fields, C=(1/4)J_2,
p_i=1/2, so along (1,1), the FLC Hessian eigenvalue is alpha^2/2-alpha/2,
positive for alpha>1/2? This displayed eigenvalue is alpha^2/2-alpha/2 at
probability 1/2 and would only obstruct alpha>1. The sharp global limit instead
uses fields tending to zero: for both-occupied probability p->0, its eigenvalue
is 2 alpha^2 p(1-p)-alpha p, which is positive for alpha>1/2 and sufficiently
small p. The complement homogenization is sharp already at equal fields:
Cov of its four indicator variables has top eigenvalue1, diag(p)=I/2,
so alpha^2-alpha/2>0 for alpha>1/2. This records the field-sensitive distinction.

IV. CARDINALITY SLICES AND THE ORACLE BOUNDARY

AASV 2102.02708v3 Theorem61 states that every homogeneous degree component
of a constant-parity Hurwitz-stable polynomial is either zero or sector-stable
with aperture pi/2. Its proof uses a univariate even-degree reduction and
half-plane stability of elementary symmetric polynomials of roots. Together
with Lemma69 this gives 1/4-FLC for each fixed-hole-cardinality law.
The full Hurwitz property is lost: the degree-two part of
 (1+x_1 x_2)(1+x_3 x_4)
is x_1 x_2+x_3 x_4; it vanishes at x_1=x_2=exp(i*pi/4),
x_3=x_4=exp(-i*pi/4), all in the right half-plane.

Theorem24 of the same primary paper then supplies a polynomial-gap constant-
block down-up walk on the hole states (or on complement-homogenized states).
Every up-step evaluates polynomially many coefficients f(U). These f(U) are
sums of determinant squares over all line subsets; general evaluation is the
original counting problem. Even f(empty) is the target partition. A single
microscopic determinant is computable in polynomial bit time, but is not
that coefficient sum. Stability, good support acquisition and a polynomial
number of calls to an unavailable coefficient oracle do not close acquisition.
No unknown balancing or coefficient ratios are silently supplied here.

V. ROBUST POSITIVE-LIFT OBSTRUCTION FOR NATURAL LINE LABELS

For the directed 2q-cycle matrix F, the hard full bases have only the two
alternating pair subsets A,B, of size q, with equal unit weights. Let mu be
their equal mixture. Let nu be any distribution on q-subsets of [2q], with
TV(nu,mu)<=delta. Set v_i=+1 on A and -1 on B, and Y=sum_i v_i X_i.
Then |Y|<=q, mu(Y)=0, mu(Y^2)=q^2, while nu(A union B)>=1-delta.
Therefore E_nu Y^2>=(1-delta)q^2 and |E_nu Y|<=2delta q, whence
 Var_nu Y>=(1-delta-4delta^2)q^2.
If the natural multilinear generating polynomial of nu is alpha-FLC, its
Hessian criterion gives alpha Cov(X)<=diag(p). Testing v and using
sum p_i=q yields alpha Var Y<=q. Thus
 alpha <=1/[q(1-delta-4delta^2)]
whenever the denominator is positive. This remains O(1/q) at delta=0.1.

If a positive hidden-variable polynomial G(z,a) is constant-alpha FLC and
specializing its hidden variables a to positive constants produces this
accurate pair-label marginal, that specialization remains alpha-FLC.
Consequently no such family with alpha>0 fixed can work uniformly in q,
regardless of hidden-variable count. Exact sector-stable positive lifts are
also excluded: marginal specialization retains its sector, but the exact
cycle polynomial has a zero at arguments +/-pi/(2q). Approximate sector
lifts inherit the constant-FLC obstruction via AASV Lemma69. This statement
allows leakage to other q-subsets and applies to accurate TV approximation;
it does not exclude nonlinear decoding, signed amplitudes, nonpositive
representations, or hole-label algorithms. The cycle family itself has an
easy dynamic program, so this is not hardness or a universal sampler lower bound.
