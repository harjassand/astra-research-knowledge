STRONGEST RESULT

A sharp whole-environment corruption threshold for linear cyclic causal discovery
with unknown correlated additive shifts and known target supports. A separately
trusted independent all-target calibration changes the relevant combinatorial
distance from directed separation to ordinary Hamming distance. In large
dimension this doubles the best uniform corruption fraction from 1/8 to 1/4.
These thresholds are internally derived, not externally reviewed or claimed first.
The underlying clean identification is established Causal Dantzig + separating
systems, not a new causal discovery mechanism. The asymmetric coding distance,
complement construction and Plotkin bound are also established EC-AUED coding
theory (Chee-Zhang 2018, citing still older work). The surviving increment is a
scoped causal corruption application and calibration comparison, not new coding
theory.

1. EXACT INTERFACE AND QUANTIFIERS

Fix d>=2, E known target subsets T_e of [d], and an integer k>=0. Data consist of
observed X-vectors and environment labels; realized actuator shifts are NOT
observed. At population level their covariance matrices are available. There is
one trusted observational baseline, separate from the E potentially corruptible
contexts. In every good environment,

    A X^e = epsilon^e + Z^e,  A=I-B invertible, diag A=1,
    Cov epsilon^e = Omega (same across environments),
    Cov(epsilon^e,Z^e)=0,
    Cov Z^e = K_e >=0, range K_e = span{e_j:j in T_e}.

Thus K_e is positive definite on its active coordinate block and zero elsewhere.
Its values and within-target correlations are unknown and may vary freely between
contexts. The baseline has Z=0. Arbitrary correlated Omega permits invariant
latent confounding. B can be cyclic: only equilibrium invertibility is needed.
The Gaussian counterexamples below can even use stable B. Mean-zero assumptions
are used in the sampling derivation; population covariance arguments allow means
to be removed. Samples are independent within/across good contexts. Up to k
whole contexts may have arbitrary distributions/covariances and violate any of
these assumptions; their identities are unknown. The baseline cannot be corrupt.

Write M=A^{-1}, Sigma_e=Cov X^e, and Delta_e=Sigma_e-Sigma_0=M K_e M^T
in good contexts. Every output coefficient is charged; d^2 entries are recovered.

2. UNCALIBRATED ROBUST IDENTIFICATION: EXACT IFF

Define the directed separation count

    s_ij = #{e: j in T_e AND i not in T_e},  s=min_{i!=j} s_ij.

Theorem U. Uniform identification of A over the above class, despite <=k unknown
corrupted contexts, holds if and only if s>2k. Uniform means for every legal A,
Omega, K_e and corrupted data, all legal model explanations with <=k corruptions
have the same A. Necessity is worst-case, not a claim that every data set fails.

Proof of sufficiency. Two explanations have good sets G and G' of size >=E-k.
Their intersection H omits <=2k contexts. For a target i form

    S_i = sum_{e in H, i not in T_e} Delta_e.

For the first explanation S_i=M L_i M^T, where L_i is a sum of PSD active-block
matrices. Its kernel is exactly span e_i: i is never targeted, and every other j
still appears somewhere because s_ij>2k. PSD prevents cancellation. Therefore
ker S_i=span(A_i^T). The second explanation's row A'_i annihilates every same
contrast, so lies in that same line. Both ith entries equal 1, hence A'_i=A_i.
This holds for every row.

Proof of necessity. Suppose s_ij<=2k. Split the separating contexts into two sets
of size <=k. Take A_0=I and A_1=I-t e_i e_j^T, t nonzero; M_1=I+t e_i e_j^T.
Both have diag 1, are invertible, and B_1 is nilpotent/stable. Choose observed
baseline Sigma_0=I (Omega_1=A_1 A_1^T). Outside separating contexts, j-active
implies i-active, so M_1 preserves every active coordinate subspace. Observed
Sigma_e=I+I_{T_e} is legal for both models: under A_1 the intervention covariance
is A_1 I_{T_e} A_1^T, full rank on exactly T_e. On one half of the separating
contexts use Sigma_e=I+I_{T_e}; on the other use
Sigma_e=I+M_1 I_{T_e} M_1^T. Each explanation violates at most k contexts. All
matrices are valid Gaussian covariances, so this is full-distribution ambiguity,
not only an accidental equality of second moments.

3. TRUSTED INDEPENDENT ALL-TARGET CALIBRATION

Add one trusted calibration with K_*=D_a=diag(a_i), all a_i>0 unknown. The coded
contexts still permit arbitrary correlated full-rank active blocks. Define

    C=Sigma_*-Sigma_0=M D_a M^T>0, W=C^{-1/2},
    Q=W M D_a^{1/2} (orthogonal),
    R_e=support projector of W Delta_e W
       =Q diag(1_{i in T_e}) Q^T.

The support projector removes unknown amplitudes and within-target correlations.
Let h_ij=#{e: exactly one of i,j lies in T_e}, h=min h_ij.

Theorem C. In this calibrated class, uniform identification despite <=k unknown
corrupted contexts holds iff h>2k.

Sufficiency. The common good set of two explanations omits <=2k contexts, so its
binary target signatures remain distinct. Known projectors R_e have a unique
labelled joint orthonormal frame up to signs. An explicit proof/decoder uses, for
each i and common good set H,

    H_i= (1/|H|) sum_{e in H}
         [(1-b_ei)R_e+b_ei(I-R_e)].

Its eigenvectors are q_j and eigenvalues hamming_H(i,j)/|H|; q_i is the unique
zero eigenvector. Recover A_i=(q_i^T W)/(q_i^T W e_i); the denominator magnitude
is 1/sqrt(a_i), so signs cancel. No inverse of a noisy estimated Q is needed.

Necessity. If h_ij<=2k, split these differing-signature contexts into two halves.
Let R be a small rotation of coordinates i,j, c=cos(theta)>0, and let D have
D_ii=D_jj=c and all other entries 1. Define M_1=R D, A_1=D^{-1}R^T, and
D_a1=D^{-2}; thus diag A_1=1 and M_1 D_a1 M_1^T=I. Use trusted baseline I and
trusted calibration 2I for both explanations. Whenever the two targets have
equal membership, R preserves I_{T_e}. In half the differing contexts observe
I+I_{T_e}; in the other half observe I+R I_{T_e} R^T. Each explanation has <=k
corruptions. Even fixed per-target diagonal gains can generate each model's good
contexts here. The small rotation gives a stable two-cycle.

4. SHARP LARGE-d CORRUPTION FRACTIONS AND DESIGN/RANK COST

If t_e=|T_e|, elementary double counting gives

    s d(d-1) <= sum_e t_e(d-t_e) <= E d^2/4,
    h d(d-1)/2 <= sum_e t_e(d-t_e) <= E d^2/4.

Consequently U requires k/E<d/[8(d-1)] and C requires k/E<d/[4(d-1)]. Their
asymptotic uniform capacity suprema are 1/8 and 1/4 respectively. For every
fixed corruption fraction strictly below the respective threshold, E=O(log d)
random Bernoulli(1/2) masks attain the required strict distance with high
probability; Hoeffding plus a union bound over d(d-1) pairs proves this. The
constant grows as the inverse squared distance to the capacity boundary. These
are suprema; no finite-margin claim is made at equality.

Every context must inject rank |T_e| stochastic variation, not one shared scalar
actuator signal. Let T=sum_e |T_e|. Directed distance gives T>=s(d-1); Hamming
distance gives T>=h(d-1)/2. Thus a positive constant corruption fraction needs
Omega(d log d) design activations when E=Theta(log d). A hard per-context target
cap q<=d/2 forces

    k/E < q(d-q)/[2d(d-1)]  (U),
    k/E < q(d-q)/[d(d-1)]   (C).

Sparse contexts cannot supply these constant corruption fractions as d grows.
This rank/dose cost is a major limitation of interpreting few contexts as cheap
scientific discovery.

5. ALGORITHMS, STABILITY, AND SAMPLE COSTS

Exact population robust decoder: enumerate subsets G of E-k contexts. For U,
form every excluded-target PSD sum, obtain its one-dimensional kernel, and
normalize its ith entry. Reject singular A, then verify A Delta_e A^T is PSD with
exact support T_e in >=E-k contexts. For C, use the support-projector Hamiltonian
on G, normalize rows using W, verify the diagonal calibration and >=E-k supports.
The true good subset exists and the theorems ensure uniqueness. Cost is
binom(E,k) times polynomial matrix work; a simple implementation costs O(d^4)
per subset plus O(E d^3) checks. For E=O(log d) this is polynomial in d with a
potentially enormous exponent. For rational exact covariance inputs U uses
polynomial-bit nullspace/PSD tests per subset. The noisy robust optimization
described next was derived but NOT implemented; the population enumeration was
implemented. No efficient practical robust decoder is claimed.

For the clean ground-vector algorithms, a conservative dense arithmetic cost is
O(N d^2+E d^3+d^4), including sample covariances, covariance combinations and d
eigensystems; store O(E d^2) matrix entries plus the output. No strong arithmetic
or bit-complexity speedup is claimed. The sample bound assumes measured values
with adequate precision: on a subGaussian high-probability event all coordinates
are bounded by L_x=O(K sqrt(log(Nd/delta))). Coordinate quantization at mesh
<=zeta/(8d L_x) keeps the aggregate covariance rounding error O(zeta), requiring
O(log(d L_x^2/zeta)) bits per coordinate. Thus digital acquisition is not free.

Clean U stability/design lemma. Use m-bit signatures with minimum relative
Hamming distance Delta and the 2m contexts consisting of every bit and its
complement. Then each target i is excluded in exactly m contexts. If
K_e >=a_min I_{T_e}, define

    S_i=(1/m)sum_{e:i not in T_e} Delta_e,
    gamma=a_min Delta sigma_min(M)^2, R=max_i ||A_i||_2.

Every S_i has one zero eigenvalue and all positive eigenvalues >=gamma. If its
estimate differs by <=zeta in operator norm, zeta<=gamma/(4R), the normalized
minimum-eigenvector estimator satisfies

    max_i ||Ahat_i-A_i||_2 <=4R(1+R) zeta/gamma.

Proof: project the perturbed eigenvector equation perpendicular to the true
kernel; the sine angle is <=zeta/(gamma-zeta). Choose its sign, then normalize
the ith coordinate, whose true magnitude is >=1/R.

Important sample allocation: one must not demand accurate covariance in every
mask context. If all directional subGaussian norms are <=K, use n baseline
samples and ceil(n/m) samples per mask. Estimate S_i directly as an aggregate.
Independent centered quadratic forms have subexponential norm O(K^2); squared
sampling weights sum <=2/n. Bernstein plus a 1/4-net (size<=9^d) and a union
bound over d rows yields, with probability >=1-delta,

    max_i ||Shat_i-S_i||op <= C K^2
       [sqrt((d+log(d/delta))/n)+(d+log(d/delta))/n].

Total samples N<=3n+2m. For requested maximum row error epsilon, choose zeta
below both gamma/(4R) and epsilon gamma/[4R(1+R)], then
n=O((K^4/zeta^2+K^2/zeta)(d+log(d/delta))). This clean finite-sample bound has
no extra multiplicative log d from the number of contexts. Dense actuator events
are still O(nd), and data acquisition/output/time remain charged. Unknown means
can be removed with independent sample-pair differences at a constant-factor
sample cost. The clean sampling lemma is a design/implementation result, not a
novel clean identifiability theorem or a minimax rate claim.

Robust U finite-sample fallback: suppose every good empirical covariance and
the baseline are within eta in operator norm, ||A_i||<=R, K_e>=a_min I_T, and
sigma_min(M)>=mu. Enumerate G of size E-k. For each row solve the convex
feasibility problem v_i=1, ||v||<=R,

    ||(Sigmahat_e-Sigmahat_0)v||<=2 eta R
    for all e in G with i excluded.

A true good G is feasible. Any feasible G shares >=E-2k contexts with the true
good set. Their excluded-target sum, normalized by E, has positive gap at least
gamma_bad=a_min mu^2(s-2k)/E. Its residual on v is <=4 eta R. Decomposing v
along the true row gives

    ||v-A_i||<=4 eta R(1+R)/gamma_bad.

Each feasibility problem is a second-order-cone problem of polynomial size;
the enumeration remains the bottleneck. Uniform per-context subGaussian
covariance estimation supplies eta with
O(K^4(d+log(E/delta))/eta^2) samples per context (plus the linear Bernstein
term), so this conservative robust implementation costs O(E d/eta^2) samples
under fixed scales. Removing that log-context multiplier robustly was NOT
proved. For C, stability additionally needs a lower active whitened eigenvalue
beta>0 for spectral support thresholding, upper amplitude L, and lambda_min(C)>0;
no stable recovery follows merely from full rank without quantitative margins.

8. HARD FAILURES / NEXT DECISIVE QUESTION

If actuator realizations Z are logged, one all-on setting identifies M via
Cov(X,Z)=M Cov Z; coding may add needless cost. If shifts can affect latent
noise covariance, the baseline contrast identity fails. If a context supplies
only one shared random shock, correlated full-rank support is false and
logarithmically many contexts cannot recover arbitrary d-dimensional rows.
Trusted baseline/calibration, known exact supports, strictly positive active
variance and signal-conditioning bounds are supplied assumptions, not acquired
facts. Graph support requires a beta-min edge threshold in addition to coefficient
estimation; the theorem by itself returns coefficients, not zero-error finite
sample edge selection.

The next decisive research question is whether robust full-graph estimation can
achieve the clean O(d/epsilon^2) total sample scaling (fixed conditioning) under
a constant fraction of corrupted coded contexts without exponential-in-context
search, or whether a matching lower bound forces the extra log d. A meaningful
result must preserve X-only access, dense stochastic-rank charges, unknown
correlated shifts, and equally informed pooled Causal Dantzig controls. The
current threshold theorem alone is not a credible field-breaking candidate.
