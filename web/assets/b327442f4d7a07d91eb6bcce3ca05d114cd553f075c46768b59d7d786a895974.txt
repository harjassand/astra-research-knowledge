A COUNT-FREE DETERMINANT FORMULA EXISTS, BUT AS AN EXPONENTIAL SIGNED SUM
Let A=[I,F^T] in C^(n x 2n), and group its two columns at site i. For sigma in {+1,-1}^n, put D_sigma=diag(sigma_1,...,sigma_n,sigma_1,...,sigma_n). Cauchy–Binet gives

  det(A D_sigma A*) = sum_{S: |S|=n} |det A_S|^2 product_i sigma_i^{m_i(S)},

where m_i(S) is the number of selected columns from pair i. Averaging independent uniform signs kills every term with an odd m_i. Since sum_i m_i=n, the surviving terms have m_i in {0,2} and are precisely the hard paired bases. Therefore

  f(empty) = 2^(-n) sum_{sigma in {+1,-1}^n} det(A D_sigma A*).

For an even hole set U, restrict A to rows R=[n]\\U and choose |R| columns; the identical argument gives

  f(U) = 2^(-|R|) sum_sigma det(A_R D_sigma A_R*).

Each summand is computable by one determinant, but the formula has 2^n terms and is signed. It is an exact alternate representation, not a polynomial-time count or gradient oracle.

EXACT CANCELLATION ON THE WEAK ORTHOGONAL BLOCK
Use INITIAL's n=4 matrix F=diag(J,R(c,s)), with J=[[0,-1],[1,0]]. The signed matrix A D_sigma A^T is block diagonal. For one rotation block, equal pair signs give determinant 4; opposite signs give -4c^2. For J, opposite signs give 0. Among the 16 sign vectors, eight determinants vanish, four equal +16 and four equal -16c^2. Thus

  sum_sigma X_sigma = 64(1-c^2)=64s^2,
  sum_sigma |X_sigma| = 64(1+c^2),
  E_sigma X_sigma = 4s^2=f(empty),
  E_sigma X_sigma^2 = 64(1+c^4).

The signed cancellation condition number is

  kappa = sum|X_sigma|/|sum X_sigma| = (1+c^2)/s^2 = Theta(2^(2L)).

For uniform independent sign samples, the second-moment/mean-squared ratio is 4(1+c^4)/s^4=Theta(2^(4L)). Even the variance-minimizing importance distribution over these point evaluations has second moment at least (sum|X_sigma|)^2 by Cauchy–Schwarz, so its relative variance is at least kappa^2-1=Theta(2^(4L)). This rules out polynomial-in-L sample cost for this class of independent determinant-term estimators at constant relative precision. It does not rule out algebraic simplification, correlated enumeration, or a different positive representation; the block instance itself has a closed form.

The exact diagnostic is in orthogonal_balance_probe.py and orthogonal_balance_probe.out. The script verifies every sign determinant, the Fourier identity, all two-hole ratios, and the second-moment values for L=2,6,10 using rational arithmetic. At L=10 it records kappa=1099511627777/2097152 and a uniform sign second-moment ratio of 1208925819621226244472833/2199023255552, both about exponential in L. This is a bounded exact example, not a generic hardness proof.

CONVEX-ORACLE CONSEQUENCE
The positive hole polynomial h(z)=sum_U f(U)z^U remains the clean coefficient representation: g(ij)=partial_i partial_j log h(0). The signed-character formula evaluates h's coefficients only through alternating determinant sums; positivity and log-sum-exp convexity do not control cancellation in that oracle. An optimization proposal must specify either (i) a polynomial-bit, multiplicative, positive estimator for the needed coefficients/derivatives, (ii) a proved zero-free/analytic coefficient recovery with all precision costs, or (iii) a different acquired scale certificate. The ordinary log-det potential is still the wrong distribution, as INITIAL demonstrates.

STATUS
This strengthens the negative audit of ordinary leverage, soft determinant partition, rejection, and naive Fourier-sign Monte Carlo. It does not establish that coordinate balance is impossible without a count oracle, and it does not change the unresolved microscopic determinant sampler. No external source or literature novelty claim is involved; the identities are proved above by Cauchy–Binet and the explicit 2-by-2 determinant calculation.
