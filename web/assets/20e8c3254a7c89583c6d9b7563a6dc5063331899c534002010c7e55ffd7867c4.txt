# ASTRA — Foundational Investigation Report

8 October 2026 · Source revision: `885136cab4f627faf435afc3595c1e16e3e15426`

## 1\. The mathematical opportunity

The original Thom–Mj machinery exposes a potentially important separation between three forms of mathematical approximation\:

1. Approximation by finite permutation actions (soficity).
2. Approximation by finite-dimensional matrices in the normalized rank metric (linear soficity).
3. Approximation by finite-dimensional unitary matrices (hyperlinearity).

The starting construction rules out the first. It does **not** automatically rule out the other two.

This distinction matters because a construction of a non-hyperlinear group would address a substantially broader foundational problem concerning finite-dimensional approximations and operator algebras.

The proof ingredients are unusually specific. They combine a finite-field interpolation identity, low-rank algebraic factorizations, high-girth incidence geometry and a rank lower bound for permutation matrices. The last ingredient depends on permutation structure, while the interpolation is characteristic-dependent. 

The investigation therefore tests whether either dependency can be eliminated or replaced by a more powerful principle.

**No historic-scale breakthrough is established by the starting papers alone.** A genuinely new bridge between these approximation theories would be needed.

## 2\. A decisive obstruction to extending the rank certificate

I tested whether Mj's rectangular-word condition can force high rank in arbitrary finite-dimensional linear representations, rather than only permutation representations.

The answer is **no**, even for faithful representations with perfect multiplication and maximal separation from the identity in the normalized rank metric.

### Proposition — faithful linear models can have rank two

For every integer \(r\geq 2\), there exists a group \(G\cong\mathbb Z^{r^2}\), elements \(g_{ij}\in G\), and a faithful one-dimensional representation \(\rho:G\to K^\times\) over a characteristic-zero field \(K\), such that\:

- Every nondegenerate rectangular word \(g_{ij}g_{i'j}^{-1}g_{i'j'}g_{ij'}^{-1}\) is nontrivial.
- Every nonidentity group element has normalized rank distance \(1\) from the identity under \(\rho\)\.
- Nevertheless, the matrix \((\rho(g_{ij}))_{i,j=1}^{r}\) has rank exactly **two**\.

**Proof.** Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_r,y_1,\ldots,y_r\) be algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\), and set

\[
K=\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_r,y_1,\ldots,y_r),
\qquad t_{ij}=x_i+y_j.
\]

The linear polynomials \(t_{ij}\) are pairwise nonassociated irreducibles in the polynomial ring. Unique factorization therefore shows that they are multiplicatively independent in \(K^\times\)\. Consequently,

\[
G=\langle t_{ij}:1\leq i,j\leq r\rangle
\cong\mathbb Z^{r^2},
\]

and inclusion \(G\hookrightarrow K^\times\) is faithful. Every nonidentity scalar differs from \(1\), so its one-dimensional rank distance from the identity is \(1\)\.

For distinct \(i,i'\) and \(j,j'\),

\[
t_{ij}t_{i'j'}-t_{i'j}t_{ij'}
=(x_i-x_{i'})(y_{j'}-y_j)\ne0.
\]

Thus every rectangular word is nonidentity. But

\[
(t_{ij})_{i,j}
=
\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\ \vdots\\x_r\end{pmatrix}
(1,\ldots,1)
+
\begin{pmatrix}1\\ \vdots\\1\end{pmatrix}
(y_1,\ldots,y_r),
\]

so its rank is at most two. The displayed nonzero minors establish rank exactly two. \(\square\)

### Consequence

For \(r>8\), this rank is strictly smaller than the \(r/4\) threshold in Mj's certificate. Therefore **rectangular-word separation alone cannot support a characteristic-zero analogue of the permutation-rank argument for arbitrary linear models**\.

This does not produce a counterexample to Mj's theorem: the example does not satisfy its global group-algebra factorization hypothesis, and the theorem concerns permutation approximations over a fixed prime field. 

It does rule out a seemingly natural shortcut toward a much stronger approximation theorem. Any successful extension must use genuinely additional structure, not merely stronger rank separation.

## 3\. Why the characteristic-zero lift fails more fundamentally

There is a complementary obstruction inside the group von Neumann algebra itself.

Let \(X_i=(g_{iuv})_{u,v=1}^{r}\), with all nondegenerate rectangular words nontrivial, and put \(X=\operatorname{diag}(X_1,\ldots,X_N)\)\.

### Proposition — universal characteristic-zero rank lower bound

Over \(\mathbb C[G]\),

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{irank}_{\mathbb C[G]}(X)
\ \geq\ \frac{Nr^2}{2r-1}\ >\ \frac{Nr}{2}.}
\]

**Proof.** Represent \(\mathbb C[G]\) by left convolution on \(\ell^2(G)\), with its canonical trace \(\tau\)\. For each block \(X_i\), the trace identities are

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(X_iX_i^*)=r^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}((X_iX_i^*)^2)=2r^3-r^2.
\]

The second identity follows by expanding the trace: precisely those rectangular products with equal row indices or equal column indices contribute.

Writing \(P\) for the range-support projection of \(XX^*\), tracial Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(XX^*)^2
\leq \operatorname{Tr}(P)\operatorname{Tr}((XX^*)^2).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P)
\geq
\frac{(Nr^2)^2}{N(2r^3-r^2)}
=\frac{Nr^2}{2r-1}.
\]

A factorization \(X=BC\) through \(s\) columns over \(\mathbb C[G]\) forces its von Neumann range dimension to be at most \(s\)\. The bound follows. \(\square\)

This is an analytic reformulation of the trace-rank mechanism already present in the supplied papers, **not a new historic theorem**\. 

It establishes that the low-rank factorization over \(\mathbb F_p[G]\) cannot simply be lifted to \(\mathbb C[G]\)\: its proposed dimension would be strictly below \(Nr/4\), whereas the characteristic-zero bound is greater than \(Nr/2\)\.

The obstacle is therefore structural, not a missing numerical optimization.

## 4\. A possible route to Singer’s conjecture—and a concrete obstruction

A different connection is more ambitious.

The Singer conjecture predicts that a closed aspherical manifold has no \(L^2\)\-homology outside its middle dimension. In odd dimensions, it predicts that all its \(L^2\)\-Betti numbers vanish. The classical conjecture remains open in general. [link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-025-03164-z)

### The source complex has positive second \(L^2\)\-homology

Let \(K\) denote the finite, locally \(\mathrm{CAT}(-1)\) triangle complex in the supplied construction, conditional on the paper's claims.

The combinatorial counts give

\[
\chi(K)=3-Nr^2-r\sum_b(s_b+t_b)+r^2\sum_b s_bt_b,
\]

where \(N\) counts retained lines and \(s_b,t_b\geq r\) are the dimensions of the ordinary incidence blocks. Because \(s_b+t_b\leq 2s_bt_b/r\), and the overwhelmingly numerous ordinary incidences dominate the deleted exceptional incidences, the paper's parameter inequalities imply

\[
\chi(K)>0.
\]

As \(K\) is an aspherical two-complex with infinite fundamental group \(G\),

\[
\chi(K)=-b_1^{(2)}(G)+b_2^{(2)}(G).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{b_2^{(2)}(G)\geq\chi(K)>0.}
\]

This is a conditional deduction from the original construction, not a resolution of Singer. 

### A precise conditional route to a historic result

Let \(R=S^1\vee S^1\)\. The \(L^2\)\-Künneth formula gives

\[
b_3^{(2)}(K\times R)
=b_2^{(2)}(G)b_1^{(2)}(F_2)
=b_2^{(2)}(G)>0.
\]

Suppose \(K\times R\) admitted a PL embedding into \(\mathbb R^5\)\. A compact regular neighbourhood \(N^5\) would then be an aspherical five-manifold with boundary, with \(b_3^{(2)}(N)>0\)\.

Crucially, Theorem E of Avramidi–Okun–Schreve (2024) supplies a closed aspherical five-manifold \(M\) satisfying

\[
b_3^{(2)}(M)\geq b_3^{(2)}(N)>0.
\]

Since \(3>5/2\), **this would disprove Singer's conjecture**\. The homology-preservation result is already proved in the literature. [link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-024-00667-w)

That gives a precise mathematical target, rather than an unspecified hope that the Thom–Mj construction might have topological consequences.

### The direct construction fails

Unfortunately, the necessary PL embedding is obstructed by the incidence geometry itself.

At the distinguished vertex \(O\), the link of \(K\) contains \(K_{3,3}\)\: fixing one pair of coordinate indices in an ordinary Cartesian incidence block produces a complete bipartite subgraph.

The rose \(R\) has a vertex whose link contains a discrete three-point space \(D_3\)\. The link of their product therefore contains

\[
K_{3,3}*D_3
=(D_3*D_3)*D_3.
\]

The threefold join \(D_3*D_3*D_3\) cannot embed in \(S^4\), by the classical van Kampen–Flores obstruction. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06478)[combinatorics.org](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v30i4p17/pdf/)

But a PL embedding of \(K\times R\) into a five-manifold would force this local link to embed into \(S^4\)\. Contradiction.

Thus\:

\[
\boxed{K\times(S^1\vee S^1)\text{ does not PL embed in }\mathbb R^5.}
\]

**The direct Singer-disproof route is therefore ruled out.** A different five-dimensional manifold thickening or an entirely different high-degree \(L^2\)\-homology mechanism would be required.

The important structural observation is that the Cartesian incidence blocks doing the algebraic rank compression also create the complete bipartite vertex links responsible for this embedding obstruction.

## 5\. A general obstruction theorem

The failed Singer construction extends to every dimension in the proposed family.

**Conditional theorem.** Let \(K\) be the two-dimensional aspherical complex from the supplied geometric construction, and let \(R=S^1\vee S^1\)\. For each integer \(m\geq0\), define

\[
X_m=K\times R^m.
\]

Then

\[
b_{m+2}^{(2)}(X_m)=b_2^{(2)}(K)>0,
\]

but \(X_m\) admits **no PL embedding into any \((2m+3)\)\-manifold**\.

The \(L^2\)\-homology assertion follows from the Künneth formula.

For non-embeddability, take the vertex consisting of \(O\in K\) and the branch vertices in all the roses. Its link contains

\[
\underbrace{D_3*D_3*\cdots*D_3}_{m+2\text{ factors}},
\]

where \(D_3\) denotes three isolated points. A PL embedding in dimension \(2m+3\) would induce an embedding of this link into \(S^{2m+2}\)\.

The generalized van Kampen–Flores theorem prohibits that embedding. [ems.press](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46393?nt=1)[researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338688371_On_the_Smith_classes_the_van_Kampen_obstruction_and_embeddability_of_3K)

This eliminates the entire family of direct product-and-thickening approaches to Singer's conjecture, not just the five-dimensional case.

**Prior-art limitation\:** The same obstruction already arises from products of roses representing direct products of free groups. The topology is classical. Its appearance in the Thom construction clarifies why this particular application fails, but does not establish a new foundational obstruction of independent historic significance.

## 6\. Stronger conclusion: changing the graph factors does not help

The obstruction also holds if the copies of \(R\) are replaced by arbitrary finite connected graphs \(\Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_m\) with first Betti number at least two.

Indeed, each such graph has a vertex of valence at least three, so its link contains \(D_3\)\. Consequently, the relevant product vertex link still contains \(D_3^{*(m+2)}\), and the same non-embedding theorem applies.

Meanwhile,

\[
b_{m+2}^{(2)}
\left(K\times\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma_j\right)
=
b_2^{(2)}(K)
\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bigl(b_1(\Gamma_j)-1\bigr)>0.
\]

Graphs whose vertices all have valence at most two cannot evade this while preserving the desired effect: their first \(L^2\)\-Betti number is zero.

Thus the obstruction is not an artefact of selecting a particular wedge of circles. It excludes every direct finite-graph-product construction of this kind.

The conclusion is mathematically rigorous under the stated geometric input, but it remains an application of classical non-embeddability theory, not a 10/10 discovery. [ems.press](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/46393?nt=1)

## 7\. Independent literature comparison

The strongest findings must be assessed against results already available.

**Non-hyperlinearity.** A preprint dated 29 September 2026 by Vadim Alekseev, Jihao Liu and Andreas Thom, *Spectral gap, internality of relative commutants, and non-hyperlinear groups*, claims the existence of non-hyperlinear groups using a new rigidity theorem. This substantially changes the historical priority of the basic existence problem. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37761)

A hyperbolic non-hyperlinear construction could still be important. However, the rank-two counterexample in Section 2 and the characteristic-zero lower bound in Section 3 establish why the present certificate does not deliver one. There is no valid transfer from its finite-characteristic factorization to unitary microstates.

**Singer's conjecture.** The crucial preservation theorem is genuinely available: Avramidi–Okun–Schreve's Theorem E, published in February 2024, transfers above-middle-dimensional \(L^2\)\-Betti-number positivity from compact manifolds to closed aspherical manifolds. [link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-024-00667-w)

The missing ingredient is not their theorem. It is the construction of a suitable compact aspherical manifold. The direct construction is obstructed by classical van Kampen–Flores topology, and the same issue arises using ordinary products of free groups. That prior-art comparison substantially reduces the novelty of this direction.

### A verified but non-historic geometric consequence

There is one additional conditional deduction worth preserving.

Every finite two-dimensional simplicial complex PL embeds in \(\mathbb R^5\)\. Embedding \(K\) and taking a compact regular neighbourhood produces an aspherical five-manifold \(N\) with \(\pi_1(N)\cong G\)\.

Avramidi–Okun–Schreve's Theorem D then produces a closed aspherical five-manifold \(M\), with \(N\) as a retract, such that \(\pi_1(M)\) is word-hyperbolic whenever \(\pi_1(N)\) is. [link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00039-024-00667-w)

Consequently, **conditional on the source nonsofic hyperbolic construction**, \(M\) has a nonsofic word-hyperbolic fundamental group.

This does not imply that \(M\) admits a negatively curved Riemannian metric. Nor does it contradict Singer: the positive second \(L^2\)\-Betti number of \(N\) is below the middle degree of a five-manifold, where Theorem E supplies no preservation inequality.

This is an application of an existing construction, not the transformative theorem being sought.

## 8\. Exact remaining proof obligations

The Singer direction has a particularly sharp missing statement.

> Construct a compact aspherical five-manifold \(N\) with \(\pi_1(N)\cong G\times F_2\), where \(G\) is the fundamental group of the source two-complex.

Such a manifold would have

\[
b_3^{(2)}(N)=b_2^{(2)}(G)>0,
\]

and the published reflection theorem would yield a counterexample to Singer's conjecture.

But the obvious PL spine \(K\times R\) cannot embed in dimension five. No alternative manifold realization, surgery construction or homotopy-theoretic mechanism overcoming this obstruction has been proved here.

The hyperlinearity direction requires something different: a rank obstruction that applies to unitary microstates **without assuming an impossible characteristic-zero lift of the finite-field factorization**\. The analysis has not produced such an obstruction.

Neither missing ingredient is presently a narrow technical step. Both require fundamentally new mathematics. I would not describe either as having a demonstrated route to success on the evidence gathered.

## 9\. Final significance assessment

| Result of this investigation | Proof status | Historic significance |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Faithful linear representations with all rectangular words nontrivial but rank-two label matrices | Complete elementary proof; novelty not established | Low |
| Characteristic-zero universal inner-rank lower bound | Complete proof; closely derived from established trace techniques | Low as a new contribution |
| Positive \(b_2^{(2)}\) for the source complex | Complete conditional counting argument | Potentially useful, not transformative |
| General Flores obstruction to codimension-critical graph-product thickening | Complete conditional application of classical topology | Not historic |
| Closed aspherical five-manifold with hyperbolic nonsofic fundamental group | Conditional consequence of a published construction | Interesting, below requested threshold |
| Counterexample to Singer or a new hyperbolic non-hyperlinear group | **Not established** | Potentially historic if independently proved |

**Final verdict: No 10/10 breakthrough was established.**

The strongest useful outcome is a more precise understanding of two foundational barriers. The finite-characteristic rank machinery does not extend to general linear microstates by the obvious method, and the most direct attempt to turn the construction's \(L^2\)\-homology into a Singer counterexample is blocked by classical embedding topology.

These are useful negative results, not historic discoveries. None should be represented to Andreas Thom, Mahan Mj or another specialist as a major new theorem.
