SU(3) QUADRATIC-ADJOINT GRAM: EXACT NORMALIZATION AND PROOF
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact algebraic identity, derived below; no external formal review.

SETUP

Let t_A be Hermitian fundamental generators, A=1,...,8, normalized by

  Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2,
  [t_A,t_B]=i f_ABC t_C,
  {t_A,t_B}=delta_AB I/3 + d_ABC t_C.

In an irreducible SU(3) representation V_(a,b), let T_A be their images
and define the Hermitian adjoint-valued quadratic operators

  D_A = d_ABC T_B T_C.

Repeated indices are summed. The quadratic and cubic central elements are

  C2 = sum_A T_A^2,
  C3 = d_ABC T_A T_B T_C = sum_A T_A D_A.

For Dynkin labels a,b>=0, set

  C2(a,b)=(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b)/3,
  C3(a,b)=(a-b)(2a+b+3)(a+2b+3)/18.

The Casimirs act as these scalars times the identity.

THEOREM

In every finite-dimensional SU(3) representation,

  sum_A D_A^2 = C2(C2/3 + 1/4).                           (1)

Consequently, for V_(a,b), define

  D'_A = D_A - (C3/C2) T_A  (when C2>0),
  Delta = C2(C2/3+1/4) - C3^2/C2.

Then the exact ordinary-trace Gram identities are

  Tr(T_A T_B)       = dim(V) C2/8 * delta_AB,
  Tr(T_A D'_B)      = 0,
  Tr(D'_A D'_B)      = dim(V) Delta/8 * delta_AB.          (2)

Equivalently, with normalized trace tr_V=Tr/dim(V) and S_A=D'_A/N,

  tr_V(S_A S_B)=Delta/(8N^2) * delta_AB.                   (3)

Be careful that (3) is the normalized-trace version. Under ordinary trace,
the right side of (3) carries the additional factor dim(V).

For regular weights a,b>0, the exact positive factorization is

  Delta = a b (a+2)(b+2)(a+b+1)(a+b+3)
          / [4(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b)] > 0.                    (4)

Thus T_A and D'_A are nonzero Hilbert-Schmidt-orthogonal copies of the
adjoint representation in End(V_(a,b)). This gives the second adjoint copy
without relying on a multiplicity mnemonic.

PROOF OF (1)

The family D_A transforms as an adjoint vector: invariance of d_ABC gives
[T_X,D_A]=i f_XAY D_Y. Its Euclidean contraction Omega=sum_A D_A D_A is
therefore central. It has filtration degree at most four in U(sl_3).
The center is the polynomial algebra generated by the degree-two and
degree-three Casimirs C2,C3. Hence Omega is a linear combination of
1,C2,C3,C2^2. Complex conjugation is the SU(3) outer automorphism: it
preserves C2 and Omega, and changes the sign of C3, so the C3 coefficient
is zero. Omega vanishes on the trivial representation, which removes the
constant term.

The degree-four symbol of Omega is

  d_ABC d_ADE x_B x_C x_D x_E = (1/3)(sum_A x_A^2)^2.       (5)

To see (5), take X=sum_A x_A t_A, a traceless Hermitian 3-by-3 matrix.
The anticommutator convention gives

  X^2 - (Tr X^2)I/3 = (1/2)sum_A d_ABC x_B x_C t_A.

Using Tr(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2, the squared trace norm of the left side is
one eighth of the left side of (5). Cayley-Hamilton for traceless 3-by-3 X
gives Tr(X^4)=1/2(Tr(X^2))^2, and therefore

  Tr[(X^2-(Tr X^2)I/3)^2]=(1/6)(Tr X^2)^2.

Since Tr X^2=(sum x_A^2)/2, this is exactly (5). Thus the coefficient of
C2^2 in Omega is 1/3.

It remains to identify the C2 coefficient. In the defining representation,
the standard contraction d_ABC d_ABD=5 delta_CD/3 and the anticommutator
identity imply

  D_A=(1/2)d_ABC{t_B,t_C}=(5/6)t_A.

Hence Omega=(25/36)C2=(25/27)I there, while C2=4/3. Subtracting the
already determined C2^2/3 term gives the coefficient 1/4 of C2. This proves
(1) as an identity in the center, not just on a list of tested
representations.

PROOF OF (2)--(4)

By SU(3)-invariance, each two-index trace tensor in (2) is a scalar
multiple of delta_AB, because the adjoint is irreducible and its invariant
bilinear form is unique. Summing over A determines those scalars:

  sum_A Tr(T_A T_A)=dim(V) C2,
  sum_A Tr(T_A D_A)=dim(V) C3,
  sum_A Tr(D_A D_A)=dim(V) C2(C2/3+1/4).

Subtracting (C3/C2)T_A from D_A yields (2). Substitution of the displayed
Dynkin-label formulas for C2,C3 and exact factorization gives (4).

For completeness, the cubic eigenvalue follows from the Harish-Chandra
description of the center. In diagonal coordinates the shifted highest
weight lambda+rho is

  x1=(2a+b+3)/3, x2=(-a+b)/3, x3=(-a-2b-3)/3.

The normalized cubic central element has Harish-Chandra polynomial
1/2 sum_i x_i^3 (the normalization is fixed by the defining
representation, where d_ABC t_A t_B t_C=10/9 I). Factoring this polynomial
gives the formula for C3(a,b) above. The quadratic formula is the usual
Freudenthal/Harish-Chandra value in the same trace normalization.

LARGE-WEIGHT LIMIT

If a/N->x>0 and b/N->y>0, then

  Delta/N^4 -> x^2 y^2 (x+y)^2 / [4(x^2+xy+y^2)],
  tr_V(S_A S_B)/N^2 ->
      x^2 y^2 (x+y)^2 / [32(x^2+xy+y^2)] * delta_AB.

This limit is positive throughout the regular cone, including the
self-dual ray x=y. It only normalizes the second adjoint embedding; it is
not an operational Gibbs-score or coercivity estimate.

SOURCE / CONVENTION AUDIT

Howard E. Haber, “Useful relations among the generators in the defining
and adjoint representations of SU(N),” arXiv:1912.13302v2,
SciPost Phys. Lect. Notes 21 (2021), records the defining-representation
normalization Tr(T_A T_B)=delta_AB/2, the d-symbol anticommutator and
contraction, and the N=3 characteristic-equation identities. Its reference
list points to Macfarlane–Sudbery–Weisz, Commun. Math. Phys. 11 (1968), 77,
and Tarjanne (1962) for the N=3 identities. Those sources support the
normalization ingredients; the central-element proof above supplies the
representation-independent identity (1). The adjoint-multiplicity memo
uses the same convention. The line (3) must not be reported as an ordinary
trace without the factor dim(V).

No novelty or priority claim is made. The identities are exact algebraic
facts. Their application to a particular Gibbs family requires that
family's own moment/projection calculation.
