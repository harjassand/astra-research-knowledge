Cycle 2 second gate — near-full-rank diagonal Casimir problem
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact graph/Green reduction; axis-hole asymptotics proved; global placement in m>=3 remains open here.

QUESTION AND MAIN FINDINGS

Restrict X to be diagonal in the occupation basis of Sym^N(C^m). Then
rank(X)<=d_N-k is exactly a weighted-graph Dirichlet problem with at least k
vertices pinned to zero. For a fixed hole set S, the exact minimum is the
lowest Dirichlet eigenvalue; equivalently it is the reciprocal operator norm
of the Dirichlet Green matrix. The mean-constrained capacity is exactly the
reciprocal Green quadratic form 1^T G_S 1.

For a single hole at a highest-weight corner, radial reduction gives the
exact layer conductances C_s=d(N-s)binom(s+d,d). The optimal radial taper is
the resistance-linear potential. Its energy and the Green row-sum bound match
asymptotically:

  fixed d=m-1>=2:  lambda_corner ~ (d-1) N/d_N;
  SU(2), d=1:       lambda_corner ~ 1/log N.

For SU(2), a path Green-function argument also proves that the minimum over
all single-hole locations has scale 1/log N, with an endpoint hole attaining
it. For m>=3, finite exact orbit checks favor a corner hole, but this memo
does not prove that a corner globally minimizes over all occupation vertices.
Consequently the predicted N/d_N scale is proved for the corner-hole problem
and as an upper bound for the full diagonal rank-deficit-one minimum; the
matching all-hole lower bound remains unresolved here.

1. EXACT DIAGONAL OCCUPATION GRAPH

Let d=m-1 and

  Omega_{N,m}={n=(n_1,...,n_m) in Z_{>=0}^m : sum_i n_i=N},
  D=d_N=|Omega_{N,m}|=binom(N+d,d).

The bosonic occupation action rho(E_ij)=a_i^*a_j connects n to
n+e_i-e_j when n_j>0. For diagonal X|n>=x_n|n>, the normalized Hermitian
root pair contributes the undirected edge term

  c(n,n') |x_n-x_{n'}|^2,
  c(n,n+e_i-e_j)=n_j(n_i+1).

The conductance is symmetric under reversing the edge. Thus the exact
diagonal energy is

  E(x)=sum_{ {u,v} in Edges(Omega)} c_uv |x_u-x_v|^2=x^* L x,

where (Lx)_u=sum_{v~u}c_uv(x_u-x_v) is the weighted graph Laplacian.
This graph is connected and ker L is the constant vector. Rank(X) is the
number of nonzero x_n. Hence rank(X)<=D-k iff x vanishes on a set of at
least k vertices. Since any such vector vanishes on some k-subset S, the
exact diagonal rank-constrained infimum is

  lambda_{k,diag}(N,m)
    = min_{S subset Omega, |S|=k} lambda_min(L_{U,U}),  U=Omega\\S. (1)

No assumption that the best zero set is a corner cluster is built into (1).

2. GREEN, RESISTANCE, AND CAPACITY FORMULAS

For fixed S, let L_S=L_{U,U} be the principal Dirichlet Laplacian and
G_S=L_S^{-1}. It is positive definite and entrywise nonnegative. The exact
Dirichlet eigenvalue is

  lambda(S)=1/||G_S||_op.                                  (2)

For the uniform graph mass on U, define the mean-one capacity

  Cap_mean(S)=min{ x^*L_S x : sum_{u in U} x_u=1 }.

Lagrange multipliers give the unique minimizer
  x_*=G_S 1/(1^T G_S 1),
and therefore the exact formula

  Cap_mean(S)=1/(1^T G_S 1).                               (3)

If M=|U|, rescaling x_* to mean one and using ||x||_2^2>=M for a
mean-one vector gives

  lambda(S) <= M Cap_mean(S)=M/(1^T G_S 1),
  lambda(S) >= 1/Tr(G_S).                                  (4)

The first inequality is a useful capacity test; the second follows from
||G_S||_op<=Tr(G_S). Exact optimization over hole placement in (1) is
equivalently maximization of ||G_S||_op over k-sets S.

For one hole v, effective resistance in the original graph gives every
Dirichlet Green entry exactly:

  G_v(u,w)=1/2 [R_eff(u,v)+R_eff(w,v)-R_eff(u,w)],
  G_v(u,u)=R_eff(u,v),       u,w != v.                      (5)

Equivalently, the principal-minor secular equation is

  -1/(D lambda) + sum_{j>=1} |phi_j(v)|^2/(lambda_j-lambda)=0,
       0<lambda<lambda_1,                                  (6)

where {lambda_j,phi_j} are the nonzero eigenpairs of L on the full graph.
This follows from det(L_{U,U}-lambda I)=det(L-lambda I)
[(L-lambda I)^(-1)]_{vv}. At small lambda the root is governed by
lambda approximately 1/(D L^+_{vv}), with
L^+_{vv}=sum_{j>=1}|phi_j(v)|^2/lambda_j. This approximation requires
lambda<<lambda_1; equations (2) and (5) are exact without that condition.

3. CORNER HOLE: EXACT RADIAL REDUCTION

Choose v=(N,0,...,0) and write s=N-n_1=sum_{i=2}^m n_i. The number of
vertices in layer s and the number up through layer s are

  g_s=binom(s+d-1,d-1),
  D_s=sum_{t=0}^s g_t=binom(s+d,d).

For a radial function x_n=x_s, all within-layer edges have zero difference.
The total conductance between layers s and s+1 is

  C_s=sum_{|beta|=s}(N-s)sum_{i=2}^m(beta_i+1)
      =(N-s)(s+d)g_s
      =d(N-s)binom(s+d,d),  0<=s<N.                       (7)

Thus
  E(x)=sum_{s=0}^{N-1} C_s |x_{s+1}-x_s|^2,
  ||x||_2^2=sum_{s=0}^N g_s |x_s|^2.                       (8)

The corner-hole Green row sum h=G_v 1 is radial. Its equation L_v h=1
and the zero-flux condition beyond the last layer give the exact outward
flux across edge s:

  C_s(h_{s+1}-h_s)=sum_{t=s+1}^N g_t=D-D_s,
  h_0=0,
  h_s=sum_{j=0}^{s-1}(D-D_j)/C_j.                         (9)

All increments are nonnegative, so max_s h_s=h_N. Since G_v is symmetric
and entrywise nonnegative, its operator norm is at most its largest row sum.
Consequently

  lambda(v) >= 1/h_N,
  h_N=sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(D-D_j)/C_j
      =D R_{N,d}-H_N/d,
  R_{N,d}=sum_{j=0}^{N-1}1/C_j.                             (10)

Here H_N=sum_{r=1}^N 1/r. The identity in (10) uses
D_j/C_j=1/[d(N-j)].

The resistance-linear taper solves the radial capacity problem exactly.
For 0<B<=N, impose x_0=0 and x_B=1, then leave x_s=1 for s>=B. Put

  R_{B,d}=sum_{j=0}^{B-1}1/C_j,
  x_s=(sum_{j=0}^{s-1}1/C_j)/R_{B,d} for 0<=s<=B,
  x_s=1 for s>=B.

Among radial profiles with these boundary values, Cauchy-Schwarz gives
the minimizer's increments proportional to 1/C_s; it is also the full
graph energy minimizer for these boundary sets because the displayed radial
profile is harmonic at every interior vertex. Its exact energy and norm
satisfy

  E(x)=1/R_{B,d},
  D-D_{B-1} <= ||x||_2^2 <= D.                            (11)

Thus if B->infinity and B/N->0, the norm is asymptotic to D.

4. ASYMPTOTIC K=1 SCALE AT A CORNER

For fixed d>=2, the resistance sum has the asymptotic

  N R_{N,d} -> (1/d)sum_{j=0}^infinity 1/binom(j+d,d)=1/(d-1).

The series identity follows from
1/binom(j+d,d)=d integral_0^1 t^j(1-t)^(d-1)dt. Dominated convergence
applies for j<=N/2; the remaining j>N/2 tail is O(N^(1-d)log N) after
multiplication by N. The same limit holds for R_{B,d} when B->infinity
and B=o(N). Equations (10) and (11) then give the matching estimates

  h_N ~ D/((d-1)N),
  lambda(v) >= (1+o(1))(d-1)N/D,
  lambda(v) <= (1+o(1))(d-1)N/D,

and hence

  lambda(v) ~ (d-1)N/d_N,  fixed m>=3, v=(N,0,...,0).    (12)

For SU(2), d=1, C_s=(N-s)(s+1), and the exact resistance identities are

  R_{N,1}=2H_N/(N+1),
  h_s=H_s,   h_N=H_N.

Equation (10)'s correction term is essential here: D R_{N,1}-H_N=H_N.
Take B=floor(N/log N). Then

  R_{B,1}=[H_B+H_N-H_{N-B}]/(N+1) ~ (log N)/N,

while D-D_{B-1}~D. Equations (10) and (11) imply

  lambda(v) ~ 1/log N,  SU(2), v=(N,0).                   (13)

The taper is zero only at the corner; all other occupation values are
strictly positive, so it has rank D-1.

5. HOLE PLACEMENT AND THE SU(2) GLOBAL CHECK

For SU(2), the occupation graph is the path 0,1,...,N with conductances
c_s=(N-s)(s+1). Removing any one vertex q splits the Dirichlet Green matrix
into left and right path blocks. The row sums, which are expected hitting
times to q, are maximized at the far endpoint of each block. Their exact
values are

  left maximum  =sum_{s=0}^{q-1}(s+1)/c_s=H_N-H_{N-q},
  right maximum =sum_{s=q}^{N-1}(N-s)/c_s=H_N-H_q.

Both are <=H_N. Hence ||G_q||_op<=H_N and lambda(q)>=1/H_N for every q.
The corner taper in (13) gives the reverse asymptotic. Therefore the full
diagonal rank-deficit-one minimum, optimized over hole position, satisfies

  min_q lambda(q) ~ 1/log N.

For fixed m>=3, axis holes are the natural candidate optimizer because their
weighted degree is smallest:

  deg(n)=sum_j n_j(N-n_j+d)=N^2-sum_j n_j^2+dN >= dN,

with equality exactly at an axis vertex. The corner result (12) and small
exact diagnostics support this placement, but degree alone does not prove
capacity ordering: a taper can spread outside the immediate neighborhood.
For m>=3, global maximization of ||G_v|| over all occupation vertices is
left open here.

Exact finite check, m=3,N=2,D=6. There are two S_3 vertex orbits. The
principal Dirichlet characteristic polynomial for a corner hole (2,0,0) is

  (lambda-8)^2(lambda-3)(lambda^2-7lambda+4),

so its first eigenvalue is (7-sqrt(33))/2. For a non-corner hole (1,1,0),
the polynomial is

  (lambda-8)^2(lambda-4)(lambda-3)(lambda-1),

whose first eigenvalue is 1. The corner has the smaller value in this tiny
case. This is a finite comparison, not a general placement theorem.

6. SEVERAL HOLES: EXACT FORMULATION AND CORNER-CLUSTER TAPER

For arbitrary k, (1)-(4) are the exact finite-dimensional formulation. A
tractable candidate is a corner cluster

  S_r={n : s<r},   |S_r|=D_{r-1}=binom(r+d-1,d),

which is feasible whenever D_{r-1}>=k. Pin all layers s<r to zero, choose
an outer layer B>r, and set x=1 on layers s>=B. The radial harmonic taper
between the zero layer r-1 and the unit layer B has exact energy

  E=1/R_{r,B},
  R_{r,B}=sum_{s=r-1}^{B-1}1/C_s.                         (14)

The profile is the unique full-graph harmonic extension of those constant
boundary values. If B=o(N), B->infinity, and r<=B with D_B=o(D), its
unnormalized squared norm is D(1-o(1)). For fixed d>=2 and fixed r,

  N R_{r,B} -> B(r,d-1)
       =(r-1)!(d-2)!/(r+d-2)!,

so the resulting Rayleigh quotient is asymptotic to

  [N/D] (r+d-2)!/[(r-1)!(d-2)!].                          (15)

For large r=o(B), this capacity is asymptotic to
N r^(d-1)/(d-2)!, while the hole count is approximately r^d/d!.
The candidate therefore gives scale

  (N/D) (d!)^((d-1)/d) k^((d-1)/d)/(d-2)!,

up to the shell rounding and stated asymptotic conditions. This is an upper
bound from a particular hole placement, not a proof of the globally optimal
k-hole geometry.

For d=1, S_r is the first r path vertices and

  R_{r,B}=[H_B-H_{r-1}+H_{N-r+1}-H_{N-B}]/(N+1).

For fixed k=r and B=N/log N, this is asymptotic to logN/N, giving the same
1/logN trial scale. More generally the trial resistance records the
logarithmic tail log(B/r). Determining the exact k-dependent optimizer and
matching lower bound is outside this memo.

7. BOUNDARY OF THE CHECK

The exact conclusions are the graph reduction, Green/capacity identities,
radial layer conductances, corner-hole Green-row formula, corner-hole
asymptotics, and the SU(2) global k=1 scale. For m>=3, the corner construction
proves the predicted scale as an upper bound on lambda_{1,diag}; it proves
the matching lower bound only when the hole is fixed at a corner. The global
minimizer over hole locations, and the global k-hole placement problem, still
need a comparison theorem for Dirichlet Green matrices or an equivalent
capacity inequality. As a universal but weaker baseline, the full diagonal
Casimir gap is m. If x has k zero coordinates and mean mu, then
k|mu|^2<=sum|x-mu|^2, so

  E(x)/||x||_2^2 >= m/(1+D/k).

For k=1 this is only order 1/D, leaving a factor N gap to the corner upper
bound when m>=3. No cycle result files were modified.
