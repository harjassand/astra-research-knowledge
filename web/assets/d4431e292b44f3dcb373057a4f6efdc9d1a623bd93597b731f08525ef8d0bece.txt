1. Target and interface

Input: a finite stochastic mass-action reaction network with d count species, reaction records (reactant vector y_r, product vector y'_r, nonnegative rate law), a target observable Y=A X, and allowed intervention/rate uncertainty. The observable is a path, so the target includes all events measurable from Y[0,T], including hitting a barrier and any control policy that observes Y only. Hidden-state-dependent policies are outside the contract.

Output: a reduced CTMC generator on the image of A, plus a proof certificate. Desired error is total variation distance between the full projected path law and reduced path law, uniformly over the declared initial distributions, time horizon, and rate/control set. For the certificate below the error is exactly zero. A numerical state-space approximation, simulation, or regression is not used as proof.

The model is stopped when Y first reaches 0 or H. It is finite after stopping, so path-law uniqueness and the hitting-time statements need no infinite-state recurrence assumptions. The same closure proof before stopping is valid on the full nonnegative lattice, subject to nonexplosion.

2. Jump-resolved closure criterion

Write reaction r as a state change nu_r=y'_r-y_r in Z^d with propensity a_r(x,u) >= 0. Let A be an integer q by d matrix and Y=AX. For every projected increment delta define

  Delta_r = A nu_r,
  Lambda_delta(x,u) = sum_{r: Delta_r=delta} a_r(x,u).

Certificate condition (JC): for every nonzero delta and every allowed intervention/rate value u, there is a function g_delta such that

  Lambda_delta(x,u) = g_delta(Ax,u)

for all states x in the admitted domain. Reactions with Delta_r=0 can have arbitrary rates: they never move Y. Under (JC), for every test function f on projected states, the original generator satisfies

  L^u(f o A)(x)
    = sum_{delta != 0} g_delta(Ax,u) [f(Ax+delta)-f(Ax)].

The right side depends on x only through Ax. Thus the projected process has generator

  Lhat^u f(y) = sum_{delta != 0} g_delta(y,u)[f(y+delta)-f(y)].

This is strong lumpability for the partition into A-fibers, written directly in terms of reaction stoichiometry and propensities. The proof is the displayed generator identity; the usual martingale problem argument then identifies the projected CTMC. Since the identity holds pointwise for each control value, a predictable intervention policy based on the observed Y-history may change u over time without changing the conclusion. Any Y-path functional, including threshold passage, occupation time, event order, and an intervention response, has exactly the same distribution. The initial hidden state may be fixed or mixed arbitrarily.

For polynomial propensities, a state-space-free sufficient certificate is available. Form each Lambda_delta as a polynomial P_delta(x,theta), where theta are shared symbolic rate/control parameters. If every directional derivative D_v P_delta is the zero polynomial for every rational basis vector v of ker(A), then P_delta is constant on real A-fibers and factors as a polynomial g_delta(Ax,theta). Coefficients are compared exactly, including parameter monomials; this proves closure uniformly over any allowed parameter intervals. This derivative test is also necessary for polynomial factorization through A. In this record, the exact family has linear nonzero-jump propensities, so the coefficient-vector form below gives a necessary-and-sufficient test over all x in N^d.

For rank-one Y=w dot x and first-order nonzero-jump reactions, group channels by delta=w dot nu_r. For a parameter monomial m, let c_delta,m be the d-vector whose i-th component is the sum of the coefficients of x_i theta^m from the channels in that delta class. Then

  Lambda_delta(x,theta) factors through w dot x
  iff c_delta,m = gamma_delta,m w for every delta,m.

This follows because the coefficient of each parameter monomial is a linear form; a linear form is constant on every fiber of w dot x exactly when its coefficient vector is in span(w). Neutral reactions (delta=0) do not enter this condition.

3. Finite acquisition algorithm for a broad subclass

Subclass: all reactions with nonzero projected jump are first order; nonlinear reactions may be arbitrary mass-action reactions but are required by the candidate certificate to have zero projected jump. Rates are polynomials with nonnegative rational coefficients in the supplied nonnegative shared parameter/control symbols. The sought projection is one nonnegative integer row w, normalized to primitive gcd 1. This includes arbitrary hidden nonlinear dynamics within each Y-fiber, so it does not assume a fast hidden chain, stationary law, or mixing constant.

Candidate generation for rank one:
  (i) For every parameter monomial, list the first-order propensity coefficient vectors k e_i.
  (ii) Enumerate their subset sums and normalize every nonzero nonnegative rational vector to a primitive integer row w.
  (iii) For each row, compute all w dot nu_r; group propensities by that increment; check (JC) exactly with polynomial kernel derivatives; and emit the projected rate polynomials if all checks pass.
  (iv) If the search set is empty, report no candidate found in this finite rank-one search class. Do not infer that no projection exists outside the class or at higher rank.

For this subclass, candidate generation is complete when a valid projection has at least one nonzero jump class, all nonzero-jump propensities are first-order, and the first-order rate polynomials have nonnegative coefficients in the declared parameter symbols. Indeed the coefficient vector of any nonzero projected-jump class is a subset sum of the first-order contributions; exact factorization forces its nonzero parameter-monomial coefficient onto the ray of w, and primitive normalization recovers w. The general polynomial checker is sound beyond this search subclass, but the candidate generator is not claimed complete for arbitrary networks. With R1 first-order reaction terms and M parameter monomials, candidate-generation work is O(sum_m 2^(R1,m)) subset-sum updates, bounded by O(M 2^R1); exact validation then scales with the number of candidates, reactions, sparse propensity monomials, and coefficient bit lengths. UNKNOWN is the correct result if a configured candidate budget is exhausted.

4. Constructed eight-species instance

Species X_0,...,X_7 have positive integer weights w_i=i+1, so

  Y = X_0 + 2 X_1 + 3 X_2 + 4 X_3 + 5 X_4 + 6 X_5 + 7 X_6 + 8 X_7.

For each i=0,...,7, include

  B_i: X_i -> X_i + X_0,          propensity a w_i X_i,
  D_i: X_i -> (w_i-1) X_0,        propensity b w_i X_i.

For each i=1,...,7 include the hidden conversions

  C_i+: w_i X_0 -> X_i,            propensity c_i binom(X_0,w_i),
  C_i-: X_i -> w_i X_0,             propensity d_i X_i.

Stochastic mass action uses binom(x_j,y_j) for a reactant multiplicity y_j. There are 8 species and 30 reaction channels. The parameters a and b are common intervention controls, allowed at each time to depend predictably on the Y history, with 1/4 <= a <= 1 and 1 <= b <= 2. The hidden rates c_i,d_i vary independently in [1/10,10]. These ratios and shared controls are part of the supplied model contract; independent per-reaction uncertainty in B_i or D_i would generally destroy closure.

Projected jumps and rates:
  w dot nu(B_i)=+1 and sum_i a w_i X_i = aY;
  w dot nu(D_i)=-1 and sum_i b w_i X_i = bY;
  w dot nu(C_i+)=w_i-w_i=0 and w dot nu(C_i-)=0.

Therefore the complete projected generator is

  Lhat^u f(y) = a(u)y[f(y+1)-f(y)] + b(u)y[f(y-1)-f(y)].

The output is the one-species mass-action birth/death CTMC

  Y -> 2Y at rate a(u),       Y -> empty at rate b(u),

with propensities a(u)y and b(u)y. This is exact for every hidden initial composition and every allowed Y-history intervention. The neutral conversion rates c_i,d_i can be arbitrarily changed without changing the Y path law. No conditional equilibrium distribution is needed.

For the rare-switching observable, take constant a=1/2, b=1, start Y_0=20, and stop at 0 or H=200. The probability of reaching 200 before 0 is the gambler's-ruin value for the embedded birth/death chain,

  P_20(tau_200 < tau_0) = (2^20-1)/(2^200-1)
                         = 1/1532497002368919987165402658218212910399545090988048385
                         ~= 6.525298244983e-55.

This probability is an exact mathematical consequence of the reduced generator, not a simulation or experimental claim. The full and reduced stopped path laws agree, so the hit event and its stopping time distribution agree as well.

5. Exact finite resource accounting and executable receipt

Let H=200. Before the upper stop, every full microstate x with w dot x < 200 is reachable from the initial state (20,0,...,0): first use X_0 birth/death reactions to set the desired total y, then use w_i X_0 -> X_i to form any composition of weighted total y. The state x=0 is the lower absorbing state. At the upper boundary all microstates are mapped to a single stopped state. Thus the full stopped chain has

  sum_{y=0}^{199} [z^y] product_{i=1}^8 (1-z^i)^(-1) + 1
    = 3,049,526,032 states.

The reduced stopped chain has 201 states (0,...,199 plus the upper-stop state). The exact state-count ratio is 15,171,771.303482587. The count was calculated by a coin-change dynamic program in O(dH) integer additions and O(H) memory; the full state set was not enumerated.

6. Counterexamples and scope boundaries

Unequal per-species birth/death controls break the certificate. With two species A,B, Y=A+2B, if the Y-increase channels have propensities a_1 A and a_2*2B, then states (A,B)=(2,0) and (0,1) both have Y=2 but their Y-increase rates are 2a_1 and 2a_2. Unless the declared rate relation makes them equal, the projection is not Markov. The algorithm must reject this input or return UNKNOWN under parameter uncertainty; fitting one convenient parameter sample is not a uniform certificate.

A deterministic drift closure is insufficient for stochastic paths. In the runnable counterexample, A -> 2A at rate 2A, A -> empty at rate A, and B -> 2B at rate B. For Y=A+B the deterministic drift is

  dY/dt = (2A-A)+B = Y,

but at Y=1 state (1,0) has upward jump rate 2 and downward rate 1, whereas state (0,1) has upward rate 1 and downward rate 0. Hence the right derivative of P(Y(t+h)=2) at h=0 differs (2 versus 1), despite the same ODE drift. `python3 work/applied/a21_reaction_reduction/reducer.py --check-projection work/applied/a21_reaction_reduction/drift_only_counterexample.json` returns an exact nonzero-fiber-derivative rejection.

A policy that chooses a based on hidden composition also breaks the interface: at equal Y it can assign different upward rates. The proof only allows controls adapted to Y and external inputs, not hidden X.

The result is an exact weighted quotient for a deliberately constructed but nontrivial network class. It does not establish a general optimal coordinate-acquisition algorithm for arbitrary mass-action networks, arbitrary nonlinear propensities on nonzero projected jumps, finite reachable classes with accidental identities, high-rank projections, or chemically atom-balanced model families. It does not estimate unknown kinetic parameters from data. There is no real-system input, experimental confirmation, benchmarking campaign, or external validation.

8. Full cost and uncertainty ledger

Supplied: species names, 30 reaction records, integer stoichiometry (smallest/largest coefficient 1/8), mass-action propensities, shared symbolic controls a,b, hidden-rate intervals c_i,d_i, stopping threshold H=200, initial composition, target rare event. Rate uncertainty is not inferred; it is explicitly represented symbolically. Ratios w_i a and w_i b must be supplied exactly as a shared-control law. If the parameters are only known as independent intervals, the exact projection is generally false.

Acquired: one rank-one row w=(1,...,8), two quotient increments, two exact rate polynomials, and a kernel-derivative certificate. Candidate search processes 512 subset sums across the two parameter-monomial lists, deduplicating them to 255 primitive nonzero rows. All 30 stoichiometric reactions are checked for each candidate. Exact propensity expansion and derivative validation are also charged; the script does not report wall-clock or peak-memory measurements. Input coefficient bit heights, symbolic expansion, candidate search, and exact rational arithmetic are charged. There is no state-space enumeration, Monte Carlo training, fast-chain stationary calculation, optimization over real states, or numerical precision parameter in the exact check. The transition to a rank-one row is the only optimization objective solved; the script returns one surviving candidate. No optimality theorem for the acquired row among all possible representations is asserted.

State size: original stopped CTMC has 3,049,526,032 states; quotient has 201. The exact count DP uses 8*200 loop iterations and big-integer additions, not 3 billion transitions. The original reaction graph has 30 channels; quotient has 2 nonzero channels. State-count compression is a representation-level exact saving, not a measured elapsed-time speedup.

Simulation cost: the reduced SSA emits only Y-changing events, with total rate (a+b)Y. The 14 hidden conversion channels are eliminated from the output simulator. If all c_i,d_i are scaled by K, a naive full SSA processes an internal event stream whose rate grows with K, while the reduced generator is unchanged. This gives an event-count leverage for fast hidden conversions, but no timings were collected and optimized partial-propensity SSA overhead is implementation-dependent. The rate magnitudes do not alter the certificate; their bit lengths and symbolic model descriptions still cost input processing.

Certification: exact Fraction arithmetic over state and parameter polynomials; derivative zero checked by coefficient cancellation. The rare event probability is an exact rational formula. Floating point is used only to print a decimal. No external theorem prover, interval arithmetic, data, fuel model, energy use, or wet-lab validation was charged or performed.

9. Claim ledger and unresolved gates

Known from primary literature: ordinary strong lumpability is a sufficient/necessary generator condition on fibers; species-partition structural lumping, deterministic ODE linear lumping, rare-event Markovian projections matching one-time marginals, averaging, tau-leaping, and QTT CME methods all predate this record in their stated scopes.

Internally derived: jump-resolved propensity sums yield strong lumpability; common-control weighted birth/death construction; exact conservation of Y-path law under Y-history policies; rare hit probability; finite state-space ratio and reachability of all transient microstates.

Finite-tested: the Python program acquired w from 255 candidates, retained one candidate, compiled rates abscissa exactly, counted states dynamically, and rejected the drift-only counterexample by exact polynomial derivative. This checks the implementation on these examples only.

Unresolved: external correctness review of the proof/software; historical priority of the weighted symbolic candidate search; whether this acquisition algorithm extends usefully to real biochemical models; robust criteria for arbitrary independent rate uncertainty; high-rank or nonlinear projections; relative-error preservation for approximate (nonzero-residual) closures; matched implementation benchmarks against averaging, state lumping, tau-leaping, and TT/QTT; experimental validation.

No major breakthrough, broad capability validation, biological prediction, priority, or external confirmation is claimed.
