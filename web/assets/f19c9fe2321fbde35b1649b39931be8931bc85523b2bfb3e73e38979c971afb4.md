# RNA pulse-order source notes

Source check performed 2026-10-08. This is a bounded primary-literature search, not an exhaustive novelty or priority review. The Astra pinned tree was searched for directly relevant RNA/cotranscriptional/riboswitch cards; no directly usable RNA-specific card or theorem interface was found. The pulse-order theorem is therefore an independent conditional CTMC result, not an Astra result.

## Recent RNA mechanisms and experimental interfaces

1. **Romero et al., “Co-transcriptional folding orchestrates sequential multi-effector sensing by a glycine tandem riboswitch,” Nature Communications 17, 2779 (published 14 February 2026).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69648-x) · [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13018625/).
   - This directly establishes a sequential co-transcriptional B. subtilis glycine tandem riboswitch mechanism: glycine binding to aptamer 1, K+ binding to the kink-turn, later inter-aptamer docking/aptamer-2 glycine binding, transcription pauses, and NusA jointly influence the pathway.
   - At the U225 paused elongation complex, the paper compares Mg2+ alone, KCl, glycine, and KCl+glycine conditions with smFRET. The high-FRET population is reported to rise from about 30% with K+ to about 60% with K++glycine. It also measures readthrough and pause lifetimes, and uses biotin/streptavidin roadblocks to stall complexes and a competing biotinylated DNA oligo to release a bead-tethered transcription complex.
   - The article studies static ligand conditions, transcript-length checkpoints, and cotranscriptional dynamics. In the material reviewed, it does not compare reversed equal-exposure K+/glycine pulse programs. Its measured ligand-dependent ensemble shift also makes the common-equilibrium barrier-only model in this package an invalid fit to this riboswitch without new evidence.
   - Reported P2.2 probe binding and NusA association/dissociation rates are not conformational edge transition rates for the three-state model; they cannot be used to set `k` or `epsilon`.

2. **Jespersen et al., “Structural basis for Mg2+–glycine synergy in the tandem glycine riboswitch revealed by cryo-EM,” npj Structural Biology 1, 2 (published 1 October 2026).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44511-026-00002-y).
   - This very recent primary paper studies a V. cholerae tandem aptamer construct by cryo-EM and molecular dynamics. The authors report roughly one-third fully folded particles in Mg2+ alone and about two-thirds with Mg2++glycine; they describe reciprocal Mg2+/glycine localization and stabilization.
   - The paper explicitly notes that these in-vitro samples omit the expression platform, cotranscriptional folding by polymerase, intracellular crowding, and full physiological ion conditions. It supports a changed conformational ensemble, not the common-stationary-law premise of this model.

3. **Steinert et al., “Pausing guides RNA folding to populate transiently stable RNA structures for riboswitch-based transcription regulation,” eLife 6:e21297 (2017).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.21297) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5459577/).
   - Primary prior establishes pause- and transcription-rate-sensitive kinetic folding and metastable intermediates. This preempts the generic claim that cotranscriptional timing controls RNA regulation.

4. **Bushhouse, Fu & Lucks, “RNA folding kinetics control riboswitch sensitivity in vivo,” Nature Communications (2025).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55601-3).
   - Primary 7-nt loop-variant study: delaying nucleation tunes switch sensitivity; a remote-toehold architecture can partly uncouple sensitivity from dynamic range. It preempts generic claims that kinetic barriers can tune riboswitch response sensitivity.

5. **Geary et al., “RNA origami design tools enable cotranscriptional folding of kilobase-sized nanoscaffolds,” Nature Chemistry (2021).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41557-021-00679-1).
   - Primary pathway-aware cotranscriptional RNA design precedent. Pathway-aware sequence design itself is not novel here.

6. **Andersen et al., “A kinetic trap in RNA origami,” Nature Nanotechnology (2023).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-023-01321-6).
   - Primary evidence that RNA nanostructures can remain kinetically trapped for many hours. Generic path-dependent RNA folding is preempted.

## Molecular temporal computation prior art

7. **Xayaphoummine et al., “Encoding folding paths of RNA switches,” Nucleic Acids Research 35, 614–622 (2007).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkl1036).
   - Reversed RNA switch sequences experimentally control cotranscriptional helix nucleation order and long-lived folds; transient antisense interactions can redirect the path. This directly blocks novelty for general molecular/RNA memory of order.

8. **Schaffter & Strychalski, “Cotranscriptionally encoded RNA strand displacement circuits,” Science Advances 8:eabl4354 (2022).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abl4354) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8942360/).
   - Primary ctRNA strand-displacement circuits implement logic and multi-layer cascades. This blocks a broad “programmable RNA temporal logic” claim.

9. **Lapteva, Sarraf & Qian, “DNA Strand-Displacement Temporal Logic Circuits,” Journal of the American Chemical Society 144, 12443–12449 (2022).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c04325) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9284558/).
   - Primary DNA molecular circuits explicitly recognize relative input order, with temporal AND output species and experiments varying timing between inputs. Temporal order computation is not new across nucleic-acid systems.

10. **O’Brien & Murugan, “Temporal Pattern Recognition through Analog Molecular Computation,” ACS Synthetic Biology 8, 826–832 (2019).** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssynbio.8b00503).
    - Primary general CRN and DNA strand-displacement designs recognize temporal concentration patterns and pulse features. Broad pulsed-input sensing is preempted.

## Novelty and data boundary

The exact factorization \(p_{AB}-p_{BA}=(0,-\Delta,+\Delta)\) and the common-equilibrium, two-local-edge rate model were not found in the bounded search. That absence does not establish priority. The algebra is low-dimensional CTMC/matrix-exponential calculus; it is not a new general temporal computation framework. No experiment in the cited RNA papers calibrates symmetric barrier-only catalysts on two adjacent RNA folding transitions. Therefore the specific mechanistic realization, a new RNA design capability, and any priority claim remain **UNKNOWN**.
