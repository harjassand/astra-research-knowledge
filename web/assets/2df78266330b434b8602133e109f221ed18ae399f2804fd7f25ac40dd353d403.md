# Mechanical metamaterials and rigidity

## Result

I found no defensible new frontier theorem in this route. A universal claim that topological polarization alone fixes a numerical edge-stiffness contrast is false: the topology tracks a zero-mode index in an ideal Maxwell/central-force model, while measured stiffness also depends on local hinge energetics, supports, prestress, the probe, and finite-size overlap. A finite SSH-chain calculation gives a useful quantitative tolerance rule for a specified boundary penalty, but it is a standard matrix-perturbation specialization and closely adjacent to published results. I recommend retaining it as a design worksheet and rejecting it as a novelty claim unless a 2D, measured-parameter theorem adds something beyond the existing kagome work.

## Reconstructed mechanism and model result

The decisive prior-art model is Kane and Lubensky’s one-dimensional mechanical SSH chain. Its bulk compatibility symbol is (Q(k)=q_1+q_2e^{ik}); for (q_2>q_1>0), the bulk winds once around zero and the open-chain floppy mode is localized at one endpoint. Their exact topological count is a zero-mode index, with the bulk contribution given by a winding/polarization and the total count also including a local termination term. The winding requires a bulk gap and a specified standard unit-cell gauge.

For (N) generalized coordinates and (N-1) constraints ((Cu)_i=q_1u_i+q_2u_{i+1}), I reconstructed the finite-chain spectrum and added equal endpoint support stiffness κ. The ideal null vector has geometric profile (u_{0,i}propto(-q_1/q_2)^{i-1}), and the positive spectrum is separated by at least Δ=(q_2-q_1)^2. With (H_κ=C^TC+κ(e_1e_1^T+e_Ne_N^T)), the lowest eigenvalue obeys

\[
0<\lambda_1(H_\kappa)\le \kappa\rho,\qquad
\lambda_1(H_\kappa)=\kappa\rho+O(\kappa^2/\Delta),\qquad
\lambda_2(H_\kappa)>\Delta,
\]

where ρ is the ideal mode’s endpoint weight. Thus a local penalty removes exact zero frequency but leaves a unique low mode when κρ<Δ. The eigenvector turns by at most (2\kappa/\Delta) for κ≤Δ/2. The detailed derivation and the static-compliance version are in [DERIVATION.md](DERIVATION.md).

For a soft mode localized at the left endpoint with localization length ξ, the right-end probe still couples to the exponentially small mode tail. In the stated finite-chain model, this caps the observable stiffness ratio at a scale set by (e^{2(N-1)/\xi}). Away from that finite-size floor, the hard/soft edge stiffness ratio scales like (k_{\rm hard}^{(0)}/\kappa). A target contrast (R) therefore needs both a small enough measured endpoint penalty, roughly κ ≲ (k_{\rm hard}^{(0)}/R), and a long enough chain, roughly (N\gtrsim1+(\xi/2)\log R). The regular hard-edge stiffness (k_{\rm hard}^{(0)}) depends on the full realization; it is not determined by winding alone.

The resulting obstruction is exact: keep the same gapped bulk (Q(k)) and its polarization, then vary or add a local boundary stiffness. It changes the low eigenvalue and static edge response without changing the bulk winding. Hence no topology-only theorem can give a quantitative, material-independent stiffness ratio. This does not refute the Maxwell zero-mode count in its ideal interface, nor the existence of useful robust finite-contrast designs.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

Kane–Lubensky’s source paper gives the local bulk-boundary index, the 1D mechanical SSH model, and states the conditions under which the winding is defined. Its framework counts zero modes and states of self-stress; it does not assign a universal measured force/displacement stiffness.

Rocks–Mehta explicitly generalize Maxwell–Calladine through susceptibility-based identities that include heterogeneous local stiffness and prestress. This is a direct prior-art reason not to present an energetic perturbation of constraint counting as new by itself.

Xiu et al. already add a weak spring to each unit of a multistable kagome Maxwell lattice and report that soft-edge stiffness rises linearly with auxiliary-spring stiffness while the hard edge is less sensitive at small spring ratios. Their model predicts opposite-edge convergence as (k_2/k_1\) approaches one. Their printed PLA sample is (6\times6) cells and reports a large edge response contrast; their multimaterial specimen reports an average initial edge-stiffness ratio near 16, while mono-material prior designs are described as limited to about 5 by hinge bending. These are published physical and numerical results, so a claim that local stiffness regularizes topological softness is already known.

What survives here is an explicitly derived, finite-chain sensitivity formula and a width-versus-penalty scale for one idealized mechanical SSH interface. It is **not** a new result priority claim, an experimental theorem, a full 2D kagome prediction, a damage-tolerance guarantee, or a performance claim for energy absorption. The published PNAS test itself notes practical issues including hinge forces, fixtures, large displacements, and limits on integrating its proposed bistable unit at available printing resolution.

## Experimental interface, costs, and falsifiers

To carry the result into an actual lattice, measure the unit-cell geometry and edge termination; calibrate axial, hinge-bending, and auxiliary-spring stiffnesses by force/displacement coupons; estimate the bulk low-frequency gap and edge localization length from a mode scan; and measure edge stiffness with fixed, documented boundary conditions and matched probes. The useful comparison is the same material and fixture with the dimerization/polarization reversed, plus a nonpolarized control when feasible.

For the 1D model, falsifiers are a low eigenvalue whose κ→0 slope is not the measured modal overlap ρ, a nonzero bulk eigenvalue below the claimed gap bound, failure of the low-mode localization to follow the geometric ratio, or failure of the endpoint response to switch under the matched coupling swap. For a 2D physical design, the more direct falsifier is that measured contrast fails to collapse against calibrated hinge/axial stiffness ratio and strip width at fixed geometry. Such a result would show that other terms—prestress, flexure, nonlinearity, clamping, or fabrication variation—dominate the simple interface.

The analytic theorem takes (C,q_1,q_2,kappa), support locations, and the boundary probe as inputs. A physical run must additionally pay for geometric metrology, coupon tests, actuator/load-cell calibration, sample fabrication, boundary fixtures, repeat trials, and uncertainty estimates. The finite check costs dense eigensolves of (40\times40) matrices. A practical 2D reconstruction requires assembling and solving the measured stiffness Hessian; it cannot treat topology as free acquisition of those parameters.
