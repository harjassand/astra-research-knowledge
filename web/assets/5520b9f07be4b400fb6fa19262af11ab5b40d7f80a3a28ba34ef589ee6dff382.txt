MULTIPLICITY CAP FOR COMPACT CONNECTED SIMPLE GROUPS
Primary-source audit; 2026-10-08

Result
------
Let G be a compact connected Lie group with simple Lie algebra (so finite
central quotients, including adjoint and simply connected forms, are allowed).
Fix a maximal torus T. Let V_lambda and V_nu be finite-dimensional irreducible
unitary G-modules, with lambda,nu dominant highest weights in the actual weight
lattice of G. Give End(V_lambda) the conjugation action

  g.X = pi_lambda(g) X pi_lambda(g)^(-1),

and define m_lambda(nu)=dim Hom_G(V_nu,End(V_lambda)). Then, for every lambda
and nu,

  m_lambda(nu) <= dim V_nu^T = dim V_nu[0].                (1)

There is a source-reported stronger statement. Let G_lambda=Z_G(x_lambda) be
the connected centralizer of a representative x_lambda in the closed dominant
chamber corresponding to lambda (equivalently, the stabilizer of lambda in the
adjoint/coadjoint action, under an invariant inner product). Then

  End(V_lambda) embeds as a G-module into L^2(G/G_lambda),
  m_lambda(nu) <= dim V_nu^(G_lambda) <= dim V_nu^T.       (2)

The quotient realization may be written with left or right cosets; inversion
unitarily identifies the two conventions. Haar and quotient measures may be
normalized to probability. For regular lambda, G_lambda=T, so the stronger
bound in (2) is the torus bound (1). For singular lambda, G_lambda is the
larger Levi centralizer determined by the simple roots alpha with
<lambda,alpha^vee>=0; (2) can improve (1). It need not improve it strictly for
every nu. At lambda=0, G_lambda=G, and (2) gives the exact decomposition
m_0(nu)=1 for nu=0 and 0 otherwise.

No root-length normalization enters this statement. The argument uses highest
weights, induction, and Frobenius reciprocity, not the Casimir metric; it
applies without a case split to A, B, C, D, E, F, and G types, including
nonsimply-laced types.

Primary theorem and exact specialization
-----------------------------------------
C. Fronsdal and T. Hirai, “A remark on the tensor product of irreducible
representations of a compact Lie group,” Japanese Journal of Mathematics, New
Series 7(1) (1981), 201–211, DOI 10.4099/math1924.7.201. The paper was received
1980-07-02; J-STAGE records the 1981 publication and the 2008-12-16 online
availability. Primary publisher record:
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/math1924/7/1/7_1_201/_article
Primary publisher PDF:
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/math1924/7/1/7_1_201/_pdf

Theorem 1 (paper p. 202) states, for connected compact G and irreducibles
U_1,U_2 of highest weights mu_1,mu_2, that

  U_1 tensor U_2 embeds in Ind_T^G chi_(mu_1+w_0 mu_2),

where w_0 is the longest Weyl element and the inducing character is the torus
character of that weight. Apply it to U_1=V_lambda and U_2=V_lambda^*. The dual
has highest weight -w_0 lambda, so mu_1+w_0 mu_2=lambda-lambda=0. Therefore

  End(V_lambda)=V_lambda tensor V_lambda^* embeds in Ind_T^G 1.

Theorem 2 and its corollary (paper pp. 203–204) give the stronger stabilizer
form for the conjugate pair:

  V_lambda tensor V_lambda^* embeds in Ind_(G_lambda)^G 1.

The proof's intermediate general statement uses the intersection of the two
weight stabilizers and the product of their inducing characters; in the dual
specialization the stabilizers coincide after w_0-conjugation and the two
characters cancel. Theorem 3 (paper p. 203) states the corresponding cyclicity
fact: V_lambda tensor V_lambda^* has a cyclic vector invariant under G_lambda.
Under End(V_lambda) identification this is the extremal rank-one tensor
v_lambda tensor v_lambda^*, where v_lambda is highest and v_lambda^* is the
dual lowest vector. Its T-weight is zero, and G_lambda acts on the two lines
by reciprocal characters.

For completeness, the cyclic-vector statement realizes the embedding directly.
If W=End(V_lambda), rho is its conjugation representation, and e is the
G_lambda-fixed cyclic vector, define

  Phi(X)(g G_lambda) = <e, rho(g^(-1)) X>_HS.

This is well-defined on the quotient because e is G_lambda-fixed, and it is
G-equivariant for the left quasi-regular action. If Phi(X)=0, then X is
orthogonal to rho(G)e; cyclicity forces X=0. Thus Phi is an injection into
C(G/G_lambda), hence into L^2(G/G_lambda). The important input is cyclicity:
T-invariance of the rank-one projector alone would give a coefficient map but
would not establish that this map is injective.

Frobenius reciprocity then yields the multiplicity inequalities. For compact
G,

  dim Hom_G(V_nu,L^2(G/H)) = dim Hom_H(V_nu,1)
                         = dim (V_nu^*)^H = dim V_nu^H,

where the last equality follows from unitarity (the fixed-space dimensions of
a unitary module and its dual agree). Since T is contained in G_lambda,
V_nu^(G_lambda) is a subspace of V_nu^T. This proves (2) and then (1).

Group forms, centers, and root-lattice checks
---------------------------------------------
The representation labels must belong to the actual character lattice of G;
for a non-simply-connected form, not every dominant weight of the simply
connected cover defines a G-module. The theorem is nevertheless valid for
every such form. One way to see descent explicitly is to lift to the compact
simply connected cover q:G_sc -> G with finite central kernel K. The pullback
of V_lambda is a G_sc-module trivial on K. Its conjugation module is trivial
under the entire center Z(G_sc), by Schur's lemma. The induced model on
G_sc/T_sc is also trivial under K (indeed Z(G_sc) is contained in T_sc), and
G_sc/T_sc is canonically G/T. Likewise K is contained in the lifted weight
centralizer, and G_sc/G_sc,lambda identifies with G/G_lambda. Thus the
embeddings and multiplicity statement descend. This also explains why a
central quotient does not introduce a multiplier or a lattice correction in
(1).

Let Q be the root lattice of the simple root system of G_sc, and regard
lambda,nu as weights of G_sc. Every weight of V_nu is congruent to nu modulo
Q, so

  nu notin Q  =>  V_nu[0]=0.

Also, every irreducible constituent of End(V_lambda) has trivial action of
Z(G_sc), since the center acts by scalars on V_lambda and hence by conjugation
trivially on End(V_lambda). For a simply connected simple group, trivial
central character is equivalent to nu in Q. Consequently

  nu notin Q  =>  m_lambda(nu)=0=dim V_nu[0].

For a quotient G, this statement is applied only to weights nu that actually
define G-modules; those with nontrivial G-center character cannot occur in
End(V_lambda) either. The zero-weight cap itself remains valid for all
G-modules and automatically returns zero when no zero weight is present. The
trivial highest weight nu=0 is a useful normalization check: Schur's lemma
gives m_lambda(0)=1, matching dim V_0^T=1. If lambda=0, (2) with G_lambda=G
recovers the full answer.

The stronger stabilizer statement is not an assertion that
dim V_nu^(G_lambda) is always strictly smaller than dim V_nu^T; equality can
hold. It is an additional valid cap that uses the actual singular-weight
centralizer. In particular, replacing G_lambda by T is safe but may discard
information.

Comparison and limits
---------------------
Fronsdal–Hirai is already an exact antecedent for the multiplicity cap, and
their stabilizer corollary is stronger than the torus-only form. Its scope
includes every compact connected simple root system; nothing in the theorem
requires simply-laced roots. The proof is geometric/highest-weight and the
bound is exact as a representation inclusion, though the resulting inequality
need not be equality for an individual nu.

Bertram Kostant, “A formula for the multiplicity of a weight,” Proceedings of
the National Academy of Sciences 44(6) (1958), 588–589, DOI
10.1073/pnas.44.6.588, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528626/,
is complementary primary prior art: the Kostant multiplicity formula computes
dim V_nu[0] (as an alternating sum of Kostant partition-function values) for
any semisimple root system. It is a formula for a weight space inside V_nu, not
by itself an upper bound on the multiplicity of V_nu in V_lambda tensor
V_lambda^*. The FH embedding supplies precisely that missing tensor-product
comparison.

What would be a failure: invoking only that the extremal rank-one tensor is
T-fixed does not prove cyclicity and therefore does not prove an embedding;
FH's Theorem 3 is the needed nontrivial step. Likewise, the PRV component
theorem of Parthasarathy, Ranga Rao, and Varadarajan (Annals of Mathematics
85(3) (1967), 383–429, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1967/85-3/p03), and
Kumar's proof of its all-Weyl conjecture (Inventiones Mathematicae 93 (1988),
117–130, DOI 10.1007/BF01393689), guarantee occurrence of distinguished
components; they are not total-multiplicity upper bounds. Within the requested compact-connected-simple class, including
nonsimply-laced types and non-simply-connected forms, the FH result has no
root-system failure. It would be incorrect to extrapolate this exact statement
without new hypotheses to disconnected or noncompact groups. This memo makes
no operational transfer, spectral estimate, novelty, or priority claim.
