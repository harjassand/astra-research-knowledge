2. WHY THE SAME COMPATIBLE SMOOTHER HAS BETTER TRACE ACCURACY

Work on PH and write tau=Tr/d. Put A=d rho>=0, tau(A)=1. Then
 D(rho||I/d)=tau(A ln A)<=C.
Fix 0<epsilon<=1/2, D0=C+1>=1, and choose

 M=exp[2D0/epsilon]>1, lambda=epsilon/(16M)>0.          (1)

As in Cycle03 minimize the strictly convex objective
 -f(rho,G)+lambda d Tr(G^2)
over 0<=G<=M I/d, Tr G<=1. Strict convexity gives a unique minimizer.
Conjugating by any unitary commuting with rho leaves the feasible set
and objective invariant. Uniqueness implies G commutes with rho and is
scalar on each degenerate eigenspace. This handles repeated eigenvalues.

In a simultaneous eigenbasis put a_i=d rho_i, z_i=d G_i. The problem is

 minimize (1/d) sum_i [-sqrt(a_i z_i)+lambda z_i^2]
 subject to 0<=z_i<=M, (1/d)sum_i z_i<=1.               (2)

We give all boundary cases in the KKT argument. There is a strict feasible
point 0<z_i<min{M,1}, so a trace multiplier k>=0 exists. If a_i=0, the
objective plus trace penalty lambda z_i^2+k z_i is minimized uniquely
at z_i=0. If a_i>0, its right derivative at zero is minus infinity, so
z_i>0. On an interior coordinate z_i<M, stationarity gives

 sqrt(a_i/z_i)=2k+4lambda z_i.                         (3)

At the upper boundary z_i=M the derivative inequality is
 sqrt(a_i/M)>=2k+4lambda M.                            (4)
Complementary slackness says k>0 implies t=(1/d)sum_i z_i=1.

Let c=2k. If k=0, c=0. If k>0 and c>=1, every positive a_i would satisfy
z_i<a_i: this follows directly from (3) for interior coordinates; (4)
would imply a_i>M=z_i on clipped coordinates. Zero a_i gives zero z_i.
Since tau(A)=1, this would give t<1, contradicting complementary slackness.
Therefore in ALL cases 0<=c<1. From (3)--(4), including upper clipping,

 z_i >= min{a_i,M}/(1+4lambda M)^2.                    (5)

No lower bound on a_i or reference eigenvalues is assumed. Define
 delta=(1/d)sum_i (a_i-M)_+.
Since x ln x>=-1/e and ln a_i>=ln M when a_i>M,

 delta <=tau[A 1_(A>M)]
       <=(C+1/e)/ln M <=epsilon/2.                     (6)

Here the positive part of tau(A ln A) is at most C+1/e; the negative
part is at least -1/e. This step uses only the TRACIAL relative entropy.
It is not valid by applying ordinary spectral clipping to a noncommuting
reference density sigma.

Put c0=(1+4lambda M)^(-2)=(1+epsilon/4)^(-2). Summing (5) yields

 t=Tr G>=c0(1-delta)
        >=(8/9)^2*(3/4)=16/27>1/2.                    (7)

Thus Ghat=G/t is normalized, obeys Ghat<=2M sigma, and (5) gives the
entrywise lower bound Ghat_i>=c0 min{rho_i,M/d}, since t<=1. Both Ghat
and rho commute. The classical overlap formula for their HALF distance
therefore implies

 T(rho,Ghat)=1-sum_i min{rho_i,Ghat_i}
            <=1-c0(1-delta)
            <=delta+(1-c0)
            <=delta+8lambda M<=epsilon.               (8)

The inequality 1-(1+x)^(-2)<=2x for x>=0 was used with x=4lambda M.
This is the improvement: the accuracy is linear in the clipped tail and
lambda M, rather than inferred from a square root of a fidelity deficit.
The smoother itself remains the SAME fidelity/quadratic minimizer whose
channel transport was proved in Cycle03.

Finally G(sigma,sigma)=sigma EXACTLY: lambda<1/4, f(sigma,G)<=sqrt(Tr G),
and d Tr G^2>=(Tr G)^2. The function -sqrt(t)+lambda t^2 decreases on
0<=t<=1, and sigma attains its minimum. Uniqueness proves the identity.
Hence sigma is included exactly in the normalized comparison experiment.
