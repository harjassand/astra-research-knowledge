FREE REGULAR-RADIUS AUDIT AT THE CYCLE4 MODEL
2026-10-08

Question and verdict

Does the source-reported C^2=50 free-radius upper bound apply to the exact
K=260, r=2, d=65 channel coefficients used in the selected cycle4 point, and does the load-
bearing source proof support it? I checked Proposition 5.1 and its stated
Haagerup lemma in Zhen et al., arXiv:2609.18747v1, §§3.2 and 5.1. The bound
matches the cycle4 interface and the coefficient-sector proof checks. It is
an upper bound, not a claim that the exact radius equals 50. This is an
internal reconstruction of a source proof, not external validation or a
novelty claim.

Interface match

The source fixes G=product_(j=1)^r F_d^(j), inverse-closed branch set
S_j={g_(j,i)^(+/-1):1<=i<=d}, S=disjoint union S_j, and K=2rd. At the selected
cycle4 point, r=2,d=65,K=260. The finite U(N) representation is

  pi_N(g_(j,i))=I^(tensor(j-1)) tensor U_(j,i)
                 tensor I^(tensor(r-j)),

with independent Haar U(N) generators for each factor. It is an exact group
representation at every N: generators in distinct tensor legs commute, and
within each factor the generators freely generate a homomorphic image of
F_65. The regular-radius estimate is an abstract group-algebra statement,
so it is independent of whether the finite representation is real or
complex. The complex-real distinction affected the direct Phi tensor Phi
Bell quotient, not this one-copy radius.

Sector derivation

For traceless Hermitian A=(a_(s,t)) with ||A||_HS=1, define

  P_A=sum_(s,t in S) a_(s,t) lambda(s^-1 t).

The diagonal terms vanish because they equal (Tr A) lambda(e)=0. For each
factor j define

  x_j^2=sum_(s,t in S_j, s!=t) |a_(s,t)|^2,

and for j<l define

  y_jl^2=sum_((s,t) in (S_j x S_l) union (S_l x S_j)) |a_(s,t)|^2.

These are disjoint off-diagonal sectors, so sum_j x_j^2+sum_(j<l)y_jl^2<=1.

Same-factor sectors: the relative word s^-1 t for distinct letters s,t in
one free factor is reduced and determines the ordered pair uniquely. Thus
the collected coefficient l2 norm remains x_j. All such words have degree
2e_j, so source Lemma 3.3 gives ||Q_j||<=3x_j.

Mixed sectors: for j!=l, a relative operation has exactly the two ordered
preimages (s,t) and (t^-1,s^-1). Its collected coefficient is
c_g=a_(s,t)+a_(t^-1,s^-1). Therefore

  sum_g |c_g|^2 <= 2 y_jl^2.

The collected coefficient l2 norm is at most sqrt(2)y_jl. Words have
multidegree e_j+e_l, so tensorized Haagerup gives ||Q_jl||<=4sqrt(2)y_jl.

By the triangle inequality followed by Cauchy-Schwarz,

  ||P_A|| <= 3 sum_j x_j + 4sqrt(2) sum_(j<l)y_jl
           <= sqrt(9r + 32 binom(r,2))
              sqrt(sum_j x_j^2+sum_(j<l)y_jl^2)
           <= sqrt(16r^2-7r).

At r=2 this gives ||P_A||<=sqrt(50), uniformly in d and A. Hence the
cycle4 input C=sqrt(50) is valid. The proof does not establish equality or
optimality of this constant; the actual exact regular radius may be lower.

Proof status and sources

The source states Lemma 3.3 as the tensorized Haagerup inequality
||lambda(f)||<=product_j(ell_j+1)||f||_2, and Proposition 5.1 gives the
sector argument above. The proof uses the free-group relative-operation
fibres, not a numerical fit, coherent-state restriction, or random-matrix
experiment. The K=260 branch set and coefficient normalization match the
one-copy purity identity in Section 4.1. The score-transfer estimate from
this free norm to finite Haar U(N) samples is separate and is audited in
CONSTANT_AUDIT.txt.
