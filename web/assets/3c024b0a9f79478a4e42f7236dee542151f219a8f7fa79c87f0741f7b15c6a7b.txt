ADAPTIVE OBSERVABLE DISCOVERY FROM RESET EPISODES
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: scoped internal proof candidate; no external validation or novelty claim.

RESULT

There is an explicit O(d)-state binary process family in which (i) every
predeclared bank of M bounded single-episode observables has an instance whose
population moment vector is within sqrt(M/[4(2^d-1)]) of a reference process,
although the two length-(d+R) trajectory laws have TV at least
1/2-exp(-R theta^2/2); and (ii) an adaptive learner using persistent repeated
readout recovers the hidden d-bit parity mask, outputs an exact all-horizon
predictor, and constructs a local predictive-cut bank with singular margin at
least beta/2. Training, held-out validation, model coding, and arithmetic are
polynomial in d, beta^-1, and log(1/delta).

The positive result is class-specific. The direct majority-plus-GF(2) decoder
uses exactly the same observations and achieves the same result, so this is
not an algorithmic advantage over that comparator. It is a precise interface
separation: persistence of one latent bit across independent noisy readouts
makes dense feature acquisition possible; one-shot noisy parity does not have
this escape under the usual LPN hardness hypothesis.

1. PROCESS AND ACCESS CONTRACT

Fix d>=1. An episode first emits A=(A_1,...,A_d), with the A_i independent
uniform bits. It then emits B_1,B_2,..., where

    B_j = <s,A> + E_j (mod 2),   E_j iid Bernoulli(eta),
    theta = 1-2 eta >= beta > 0.

The unknown mask s is in GF(2)^d minus {0}; d and a rational lower bound
beta are supplied. For the positive theorem eta is also supplied as a known
rational in (0,1/2), so theta is known and has h-bit encoding. A query is one
reset or one observed symbol. Resets give independent episodes. Within an
episode the agent may continue observing the readout tail. It cannot choose
A, intervene on the hidden state, request a conditional source sample, or
inspect a transition table. The same s and eta govern all later episodes.

For comparison define Q: it emits the same uniform A, chooses Z uniformly
and independently of A, then emits B_j=Z+E_j mod 2 with the same noise law.
Both P_s and Q are time-homogeneous finite-state processes. A clock and one
parity bit suffice: at input coordinate i, P_s updates the parity by s_i A_i;
Q keeps its independent initial bit. After coordinate d both retain that bit
while emitting readouts. Each has at most 2d+2 symbol-transition states, or
at most 4d+4 ordinary state-emitting HMM states by edge-to-state conversion.
For eta=1/4 all transition and emission probabilities lie in
{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1}.

2. FIXED-BANK OBSTRUCTION ON THIS SAME FAMILY

Theorem 1 (fixed moment bank). Let phi_1,...,phi_M be any [0,1]-valued
measurable tests fixed before s is known. Each may inspect the full episode,
including any finite or infinite number of readouts. Then some nonzero mask s
satisfies

    || (E_{P_s} phi_j - E_Q phi_j)_{j=1}^M ||_2
       <= sqrt( M / (4(2^d-1)) ).                       (1)

For every nonzero s and every positive odd R, the first d+R symbols nevertheless obey

    TV(P_s[1:d+R], Q[1:d+R]) >= 1/2 - exp(-R theta^2/2). (2)

Proof. For a fixed test phi_j and input a, write m_+(a) and m_-(a) for its
conditional expectation when the retained latent bit is 0 or 1, respectively,
averaging over the common independent readout errors. Put
f_j(a)=(m_+(a)-m_-(a))/2, so |f_j(a)|<=1/2. Under Q the bit is uniform and
independent of a. Under P_s it is <s,a>. With chi_s(a)=(-1)^{<s,a>},

    E_{P_s} phi_j - E_Q phi_j = E_a[f_j(a) chi_s(a)].

Parseval on GF(2)^d gives sum_s (E f_j chi_s)^2=E f_j^2<=1/4.
Sum over j and over nonzero s, then average over the 2^d-1 possible masks;
at least one s satisfies (1).

For (2), on the observed prefix let Zhat be majority(B_1,...,B_R). Under
P_s, Hoeffding gives Pr[Zhat != <s,A>] <= exp(-R theta^2/2). Under Q,
Zhat is uniform and independent of A, so Pr[Zhat=<s,A>]=1/2. The event
{Zhat=<s,A>} witnesses the stated TV gap. This event is used to prove
separation; the learner need not know s in advance.

Scope: (1) is a population-moment obstruction for a fixed single-episode
dictionary. It does not lower-bound algorithms that retain raw trajectories,
compute cross-sample statistics, or choose tests after seeing data. The
adaptive learner below uses raw paths and a batch GF(2) solve. An M-query
moment interface with only population means is the object blocked here.

3. ADAPTIVE MASK AND PREDICTOR ACQUISITION

Theorem 2 (persistent-readout acquisition). Let delta in (0,1/2). Set

    N = d + ceil(log2(3/delta)),
    R = the least odd integer >= (2/beta^2) ln(3N/delta).

Collect N independent episodes, observing d inputs and R readouts in each.
Majority-decode each readout block to y_i, then solve

    <s,A^(i)> = y_i  (mod 2),  i=1,...,N.

If the system is inconsistent or does not have a unique solution, return
FAIL. Otherwise output the recovered mask and the compact clock/parity model.
With probability at least 1-2delta/3, this output is the exact law P_s.

Proof. For a single episode, the probability that majority decoding is wrong
is at most exp(-R theta^2/2) <= delta/(3N). A union bound makes every decoded
label correct with probability at least 1-delta/3. The N input vectors are
independent uniform rows. For each nonzero v, the probability that all rows
annihilate v is 2^-N. Thus

    Pr[rank(A)<d] <= (2^d-1)2^-N < 2^(d-N) <= delta/3.

On the complement, the unique solution is s. The two failure bounds sum to
2delta/3.

Exact future predictions follow directly. During the first d symbols, the
clock emits fair inputs and updates the parity from the stored mask. After
that it emits independent Bernoulli-noisy copies of the retained parity.
Thus the learned model gives every finite-word probability and next-symbol
conditional exactly, at every horizon, including every positive-probability
history. It is a prediction-task transfer for later episodes from the same
(s,eta) process; it says nothing about changed masks or a new process family.

4. AN ADAPTIVELY ACQUIRED LOCAL FEATURE BANK AND CERTIFICATE

After recovering s, for each cut t=1,...,d define bounded sign observables

    U_t = (-1)^(sum_{i<=t} s_i A_i),
    V_t = (-1)^(sum_{i>t} s_i A_i + B_1).

Let phi_t and psi_t be the two indicator coordinates for U_t=+1/-1 and
V_t=+1/-1. Since V_t=U_t*(-1)^E_1, a cut whose prefix contains a selected
coordinate has

    H_t = E[phi_t psi_t^T]
        = (1/4) [[1+theta, 1-theta], [1-theta, 1+theta]],
    singular values(H_t) = {1/2, theta/2}.

Before the first selected coordinate, U_t is constant and the appropriate
one-dimensional constant feature has moment 1. The bank is indexed by the
learned mask and clock cuts. It has O(d) tests and an O(d+h+log d)-bit
program description (or an explicit state table of O(d log d+h) bits).
All cuts on one episode can be evaluated with one prefix/suffix scan, O(d)
bit operations. This margin is local to these specified cuts; it is not a
claim about the smallest singular value of the inherited complete universal
Hankel bank.

Independent class-specific held-out certificate. Freeze the learned mask v.
On J fresh episodes compute

    C_hat_v = (1/J) sum_{j=1}^J (-1)^(B_1^(j)+<v,A^(j)>),
    J = ceil(32 beta^-2 ln(6/delta)).

Under P_s, E[C_hat_v]=theta if v=s and 0 if v!=s. Hoeffding for [-1,1]
variables gives |C_hat_v-E C_hat_v|<=beta/4 with probability at least
1-delta/3. Accept only if C_hat_v-beta/4 >= beta/2. On the confidence event,
acceptance certifies v=s within this process class and lower-bounds the
nonzero singular value of every active H_t by

    (C_hat_v-beta/4)/2 >= beta/4.

The class promise is essential: this certificate does not prove arbitrary
real data belong to P_s. A general-bank alternative estimates all entries of
the active H_t from the same independent held-out episodes. For
M_hold >= 128 beta^-2 ln(24d/delta), entry error
c=sqrt(ln(24d/delta)/(2 M_hold))<=beta/16 simultaneously over at most 4d
entries with probability >=1-delta/3. The operator error of each 2x2 matrix
is at most 2c, so sigma_min(Hhat_t)-2c >= beta/4 for every active cut.
This alternative costs M_hold(d+1) symbols and O(M_hold d) bit operations;
the class-specific correlation certificate is cheaper.

Combining training and the class-specific certificate gives probability at
least 1-delta of returning the exact model and a valid local margin
certificate. Training costs N resets and N(d+R) observed symbols. Validation
costs J resets and J(d+1) symbols. Majority work is O(NR); row reduction is
O(Nd^2) bit operations; validation is O(Jd). The learner stores O(Nd) bits
for a simple batch solve, or O(d^2+R) bits with a streaming pivot basis.
The learned model code is d mask bits, O(log d) clock bits, and h bits for
the public rational eta, plus fixed program text. Future prefix evaluation
uses O(T+d) time for length T and the same model code; a per-symbol simulator
uses O(1) update work and O(log d) clock plus one parity bit beyond the mask.
Exact finite-word probabilities have numerator/denominator bit length
O(T h+d) at horizon T, so exact arithmetic adds polynomial bit cost in T
and h. A next-symbol conditional query uses O(1) h-bit probability
operations once the current clock/parity state is known.

5. INHERITED BANK, FAIR COMPARATOR, AND FAILURE BOUNDARY

Inherited universal-bounded-observable interface. For an alphabet of size n
and supplied state bound m, the prior construction sets d0=2nm,
K=1+m*d0*(d0-1)/2, and M=K+(n+1)K^2. It supplies M fixed [0,1]
trajectory moments of maximum length 2d0-1=4nm-1; exact moments give rank
H=r and a minimal signed realization. With a supplied lower bound
sigma<=sigma_r(H), horizon T, TV target epsilon, and failure rho, the stated
exact-arithmetic sample prescription is

    eta0 = epsilon*sigma^3/(300*n*K^4*T),
    N0 >= ln(2M/rho)/(2 eta0^2).

This is efficient only when the actual population margin is affordable. The
prior parity-clock obstruction says enlarging a fixed polynomial bank cannot
make that margin uniform over all finite-state processes. Our P_s/Q theorem
is a second, same-family version: it preserves a large raw-trajectory TV gap
while making every predeclared M-dimensional population moment vector close
for at least one unknown mask. The acquired local cuts below have margin
beta/2 after a different observation pattern exposes the mask.

The equally informed comparator receives the same d, eta, beta, resets, raw
symbols, and repeated-readout opportunity. It applies the same majority
decoder and GF(2) elimination, then outputs the same clock/parity model.
Therefore no runtime or sample advantage is claimed for constructing the
observable bank; the useful result is the acquired certified representation
and its explicit transfer to every later prediction horizon.

The one-shot class P_s^one emits A and one noisy B, then an independent fair
tail. This is a positive O(d)-state probabilistic automaton realizing noisy
parity samples. Kearns et al. prove evaluator-learning hardness for this
class under the Noisy Parity Assumption. A direct interface consequence is:
if an ordinary-reset learner returned a polynomial-size evaluator q with
TV(P_s^one[1:d+1],q)<=theta/16 and polynomial-precision joint-word
evaluation, then sign(q(a,0)-q(a,1)) predicts s dot a on a uniformly random
a with error below 1/4; a heavy-Fourier-coefficient routine recovers s in
polynomial time. For finite precision, evaluate each q(a,b) to absolute error
at most theta*2^(-d)/32, which uses O(d+log(1/theta)) output bits. The sum
over a of the induced difference error is at most theta/16; total variation
contributes at most theta/8 in joint L1, so the prediction error is at most
3/16. This is enough for a constant-threshold Goldreich-Levin search.

The reset simulation is exact: draw one LPN example (a,b) at each episode,
reveal its a coordinates and then b, and generate any fair tail locally. If
the learner uses R0 resets and O observed symbols, the reduction consumes
R0 LPN examples and O(R0*d+O) local bit work. It never asks for an LPN sample
conditioned on a chosen prefix. The standard reduction is conditional on
search-LPN hardness, not an unconditional passive-sample or information-
theoretic lower bound. It also does not apply to a generator-only output that
cannot evaluate arbitrary words. Persistent repeated readout changes the
input interface, so it does not contradict this obstruction.

The falsifying condition for the positive theorem is observable: if the R
readouts do not share one retained latent bit with independent errors (or
the episodes do not reset independently with uniform A), majority labels
need not satisfy the linear equations and the exact transfer theorem fails.
The held-out certificate can reject a wrong mask, but passing it is only a
class-conditional certificate, not a general process-identification test.
