CYCLE 2 LEAF 07 — SPATIAL DEFECT STABILITY AT THE z=2 SATURATION WINDOW
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the exact one-particle scaling laws and the free-XX Fock-tail statement below; CONDITIONAL for extending whole-state convergence to interacting repulsive chains; UNKNOWN for novelty/priority.

RESULT
------
Spatial inhomogeneity has three different critical scales in the open XX chain. A bounded potential spread over all sites at physical strength L^-2 survives as a bounded continuum potential. A point defect at a bulk site survives at physical strength 1/L (not 1/L^2) and becomes a delta interaction. A boundary-site potential is generically invisible to the L^-2 low-energy window except through a Dirichlet condition; a Robin family requires tuning to the boundary resonance v=-2J and then detuning by 2Ja/L. A fixed attractive boundary defect below -2J instead creates a microscopic bound orbital whose occupation tends to one at beta=tau L^2.

For the free XX Gibbs state, every fixed-rank one-particle defect leaves the quadratic high-energy tail intact: interlacing can change at most r low modes for a rank-r perturbation, while all subsequent scaled energies are bounded below by the clean-chain quadratic sequence. Thus the parameter-blind excitation-count archive retains the same cubic leading exponent, with D shifted by at most r. Bounded continuum potentials also retain it. Under a positive number-conserving interaction, the excitation count remains exactly sufficient for the unknown scalar field, and the tail bound survives for critical defects whose scaled one-particle bottom remains uniformly bounded. A fixed deep bound state plus an interacting repulsion needs a separate many-body denominator argument. Whole-state convergence for an interacting chain additionally requires convergence of each fixed-particle sector; the one-particle calculation and tail bound alone do not prove that.

1. Interface, conventions, and exact scales
--------------------------------------------
Let L=N+1, J>0, tau>0, beta_N=tau L^2, n_i=(I-Z_i)/2, and M=sum_i n_i. The clean saturation XX Hamiltonian is

  H_(N,lambda) = -J sum_(i=1)^(N-1) (X_i X_(i+1)+Y_i Y_(i+1))
                  + (4J-lambda/L^2) M.

Jordan-Wigner maps the one-particle operator to

  T_N = 4J I - 2J A(P_N),
  epsilon_(N,k)=4J(1-cos(pi k/L)),  k=1,...,N.

The scaled one-particle energies entering the Gibbs exponent are
  a_(N,k)=L^2 epsilon_(N,k),
and the field contributes -lambda M after multiplication by beta_N. The clean bound a_(N,k)>=8J k^2 is the cycle-1 bound. All defects below are supplied and known to the encoder/decoder; lambda alone is unknown and is never acquired. If the defect parameter were also unknown, the exact one-parameter sufficiency statement would not by itself solve that larger interface.

The three scales should not be conflated:

  * A site potential w_i/L^2 on every site, with w_i sampled from a bounded continuous W(x), gives the continuum operator 2J(-d^2/dx^2)+W(x). There are O(L) sites, so the Riemann sum survives.
  * A single interior site j_N/L -> theta in (0,1), with potential v_N=2Jg/L, gives 2J(-d^2/dx^2+g delta_theta). A point perturbation of order L^-2 vanishes in this scaling.
  * At an endpoint, a single site does not behave like a bulk point. The critical family is v_N=-2J+2Ja/L. The O(1) term is a resonance tuning; the 1/L detuning gives a Robin boundary condition. A bare endpoint potential of order L^-2 is subcritical and disappears.

For the interior point, a normalized extended low mode has |psi_j|^2=O(L^-1), so L^2 v_N |psi_j|^2 is O(1) exactly when v_N=O(L^-1). For a distributed potential, sum_i |psi_i|^2 W(i/L)/L^2 is O(L^-2), which survives after multiplication by L^2. At a generic Dirichlet endpoint |psi_1|^2=O(L^-3); the endpoint resonance changes that boundary amplitude to O(L^-1), which is why the boundary problem needs separate tuning.

2. Exact boundary-site calculation: Robin, threshold, and deep bound mode
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Add v_N |1><1| to T_N. For an eigenvalue E(q)=4J(1-cos q), the bulk recurrence and the right Dirichlet endpoint give

  psi_i = C sin(q(L-i)),  i=1,...,N.

The left endpoint equation is exactly the ghost-site relation

  psi_0 = -(v_N/(2J)) psi_1,

so the nonzero-q secular equation is

  sin(qL)+(v_N/(2J)) sin(q(L-1))=0.                    (2.1)

At v_N=-2J+2Ja/L and q=x/L, equation (2.1), divided by x/L, converges on bounded x-sets to

  x cos x + a sin x = 0.                              (2.2)

The zero x=0 in the un-divided trigonometric equation is generally an artifact of the sine ansatz. The divided characteristic function has value 1+a at x=0: there is a genuine zero eigenvalue precisely at a=-1. The interpolated quadratic forms make the boundary condition and operator explicit. Writing psi_i=L^-1/2 phi(i/L),

  L^2 <psi,(T_N+v_N |1><1|)psi>
    -> 2J [ integral_0^1 |phi'(s)|^2 ds + a |phi(0)|^2 ],

on H^1 functions with phi(1)=0. Its operator is 2J(-d^2/ds^2) with

  phi'(0)=a phi(0),   phi(1)=0.                       (2.3)

This is a Robin condition at the left end and Dirichlet at the right. At a=0 it is Neumann-Dirichlet; the limiting positive roots are (k-1/2)pi. At a=-1 the zero mode is phi(s)=1-s. For a<-1 there is one negative scaled eigenvalue -2J kappa^2, where

  a = -kappa coth(kappa),  kappa>0.                   (2.4)

There is no negative eigenvalue for a>=-1. Equation (2.4) follows by setting x=i kappa in (2.2); kappa coth(kappa) increases from 1 to infinity. The negative mode for fixed a<-1 is still macroscopic: its decay length in lattice sites is O(L), so it is a continuum Robin bound mode, not a site-localized impurity state. Its Gibbs occupation tends to the finite value [1+exp(tau(-2J kappa^2-lambda))]^-1.

The exact resonance v_N=-2J gives

  sin(qL)-sin(q(L-1)) = 2 sin(q/2) cos(q(L-1/2))=0.

After removing the spurious q=0 root, q_m=(m+1/2)pi/(L-1/2), m=0,...,N-1. Hence L q_m -> (m+1/2)pi, verifying the Neumann-Dirichlet limit. The exact N=1 and N=2 characteristic polynomials are respectively 2-E and E^2-6E+4; for N=2 the secular roots q=pi/5,3pi/5 give E=3-sqrt(5),3+sqrt(5), exactly those roots.

For a fixed boundary potential v_0, the low-mode limit is generically Dirichlet. Indeed, the ghost equation has coefficient -v_0/(2J), and unless this coefficient equals 1, the leading q->0 boundary equation forces phi(0)=0. Thus v_0=-2J is the resonance. In particular v_N=c/L^2 is negligible and does not make Robin data.

The attractive side also has a sharp lattice bound-state diagnostic. On the half-line, set psi_i=r^(i-1), 0<r<1. The recurrence and endpoint equation give

  v_0=-2J/r,  E=4J-2J(r+r^-1).

There is a below-band mode exactly when v_0<-2J. For v_0=-4J, r=1/2 and E=-J exactly. On a long finite chain the mode converges to this localized state. Under beta=tau L^2 and field lambda/L^2 its occupation is

  p_bound,N = [1+exp(tau(L^2 E_N-lambda))]^-1 -> 1.

This destroys a continuum-Fock limit that omits a defect orbital: the normalized occupied orbital concentrates on O(1) sites, has no strong L^2(0,1) limit under the usual mesh embedding, and its rank-one projector cannot converge in trace norm to a trace-class operator there. It does not destroy the free-XX archive tail. Store/factor the single localized mode separately (or enlarge the one-particle limit by a defect slot); the remaining low modes converge to the Dirichlet continuum and the high-mode tail remains quadratic. At a fixed v_0>-2J the delocalized low modes are Dirichlet; at v_0>2J there can also be a high-energy above-band boundary state, which is Gibbs-suppressed.

3. Interior point interaction and its exact one-particle threshold
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let j_N/L -> theta in (0,1), and add v_N |j_N><j_N| with v_N=2Jg/L. The discrete form is

  2J L^2 sum_(i=0)^N |psi_(i+1)-psi_i|^2
      + 2Jg L |psi_(j_N)|^2,   psi_0=psi_L=0.

For psi_i=L^-1/2 phi(i/L), it converges to

  2J [ integral_0^1 |phi'|^2 dx + g |phi(theta)|^2 ],

the one-dimensional point-interaction operator 2J(-d^2+g delta_theta), with phi continuous, Dirichlet endpoints, and derivative jump

  phi'(theta+)-phi'(theta-)=g phi(theta).             (3.1)

The scale is exact: v_N=O(L^-2) gives a vanishing O(L^-1) scaled form term; fixed O(1) v_N is a strong-barrier/deep-bound regime rather than a finite delta coupling.

For x^2 eigenvalue of -d^2+g delta_theta, matching sine solutions on the two sides yields

  x sin x + g sin(x theta) sin(x(1-theta))=0.          (3.2)

At zero energy, (3.2) has the threshold g_c=-1/[theta(1-theta)]. For g<g_c there is one negative eigenvalue -kappa^2, where

  g=-kappa[coth(kappa theta)+coth(kappa(1-theta))].  (3.3)

For g>=g_c there is no negative eigenvalue; at g=g_c the zero mode is the piecewise-linear tent. At theta=1/2, g_c=-4 and phi(x)=2x for x<=1/2, 2(1-x) for x>=1/2 has derivative jump -4=-4 phi(1/2), an exact check. A finite g<g_c creates a finite negative *scaled* energy; unlike the fixed physical boundary defect v_0=-4J, it does not force occupancy to one. Its occupation is [1+exp(tau(-2J kappa^2-lambda))]^-1.

4. Bounded distributed potential and the repulsive-chain interface
-------------------------------------------------------------------
For a continuous bounded W on [0,1], add the physical one-body term

  sum_i [W(i/L)/L^2] n_i.

The scaled one-particle form converges to 2J(-d^2)+W(x), with Dirichlet endpoints. The bounded multiplication term changes every ordered eigenvalue by at most ||W||_infinity, so the quadratic high-energy lower bound and all summable Gibbs tails survive. If W_N is merely bounded but has no continuum limit, this uniform tail remains but the limiting operator may not exist without a subsequence/homogenization hypothesis.

This convergence hypothesis cannot be dropped. Let w_N=0 for even N and w_N=c>0 for odd N, and add the bounded site term (w_N/L^2)M. This is within the same L^-2 saturation scale and is exactly the clean model with field lambda-w_N. The even subsequence converges to rho_(infinity,lambda), while the odd subsequence converges to rho_(infinity,lambda-c). Their first-mode occupation probabilities differ strictly because p_1(lambda) is strictly increasing in lambda. Measurement of that mode therefore gives a fixed positive trace-distance lower bound between the two limits. The full sequence has no whole-state trace-norm limit. Thus a uniform bound alone supports archive tails, but a convergent profile (or a specified homogenized limit) is required for a single Gibbs limit.

For the open XX chain plus a number-conserving repulsion, write

  H_(N,lambda)=H_(N,0)+U V_N-(lambda/L^2)M,
  U>=0, V_N>=0, [V_N,M]=0.

The one-particle sector is unchanged, so (2.1)-(3.3) remain exact there. The full Gibbs state need not be a Bernoulli product. Nevertheless, M is an exact sufficient statistic for lambda: the Gibbs state is a mixture over M=m with weights proportional to exp(tau lambda m), and the conditional state inside each m-sector is lambda-independent. Measuring M, storing its value, and preparing the matching conditional Gibbs state is a parameter-blind archive, exactly as in the clean model.

Here is a scoped tail statement. Suppose the supplied one-body defect is at critical scaling (bounded W/L^2, finite g point interaction, or finite-a Robin) so its scaled one-particle eigenvalues have a uniform lower bound and a quadratic tail, for example mu_(N,k+r)>=8J k^2-C after at most r exceptional modes. In each fixed-m sector, positivity U V_N>=0 and the min-max principle imply that the interacting sector partition function is no larger than that of the corresponding free-fermion reference. Since the vacuum gives Z_U>=1,

  P_U(M>=d) <= exp(-s d) Z_free(lambda+s/tau).

The first r one-particle modes contribute at most O(r s)+O(1) to log Z_free when their scaled energies are uniformly bounded below; choosing s proportional to (d-r)^2 gives

  log P_U(M>=d) <= -(4/3) tau J (d-r)^3 + O((d-r)^2),

with constants depending on J,tau,lambda's compact interval, the defect bound, and r. Thus the same D-dimensional count archive has a cubic tail upper bound for this repulsive extension. This is an upper bound, not a matching optimality law.

For the free XX chain with any fixed-rank one-particle defect, including a microscopic bound mode, no lower-bound assumption on the exceptional mode is needed: the Gibbs state factorizes, each exceptional mode contributes at most one to M, and the remaining modes have the quadratic tail. For a rank-r Hermitian perturbation, eigenvalue interlacing gives

  mu_(N,k+r) >= a_(N,k) >= 8J k^2.

Consequently, for d>r,

  P(M>=d) <= exp[-(4/3)tau J(d-r)^3+O((d-r)^2)],

uniformly in N and lambda. A fixed deep defect changes the stored low-mode label/occupancy, not the cubic high-mode law.

The interacting fixed-deep-defect case is not covered by the preceding repulsive comparison with a uniformly bounded scaled bottom: the one-particle eigenvalue is -Theta(L^2) after scaling, and positive many-body repulsion may change how several low orbitals can be occupied. A denominator comparison or a direct sector-ground-energy bound is still needed there. Do not transfer the free product argument to that interacting case.

5. Whole-state Gibbs convergence: exact free result and conditional extension
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For free XX with supplied defect, diagonalize the one-particle operator h_N and write its scaled eigenvalues mu_(N,k). The full Gibbs state is exactly

  rho_(N,lambda)= tensor_k [(1-p_(N,k))|0><0|+p_(N,k)|1><1|],
  p_(N,k)=[1+exp(tau(mu_(N,k)-lambda))]^-1.

For bounded distributed W, point coupling g/L, and critical boundary tuning -2J+2Ja/L, the discrete forms above converge to compact-resolvent continuum operators. Every fixed eigenvalue and normalized eigenfunction converges under the mesh embedding. Interlacing (finite rank) or the bounded-W eigenvalue comparison gives a uniform majorant p_(N,k)<=C exp(-c k^2) outside finitely many modes. To pass to the full Fock state, first keep only the first K modes: their eigenvectors and all finite wedges converge, and their Bernoulli weights converge uniformly for lambda in a compact set. The probability of any occupied mode above K is at most sum_(k>K) p_(N,k), uniformly O(exp(-c'K^2)). Taking N to infinity at fixed K and then K to infinity proves uniform trace-norm convergence in the common Fock embedding. The continuum limit is the free-fermion Gibbs state of the corresponding Robin, point-interaction, or bounded-potential operator, including any finite negative continuum modes.

For a fixed deep attractive defect, the same argument applies after extracting the localized orbital into a separate one-particle defect slot. Under the bare L^2(0,1) mesh embedding there is no trace-norm whole-state limit because that occupied orbital concentrates at the endpoint. This is a failure of that particular common-space embedding, not a failure of a finite-dimensional archive: the defect slot costs one mode/bit and all remaining Fock tail bounds persist.

For an interacting repulsive chain, the count-tail bound controls the high-particle sectors but does not prove convergence of their conditional states. A whole-state trace-norm limit follows if, in addition, every fixed-m Gibbs sector has a supplied common-space embedding and converges in trace norm (or an appropriate uniform finite-sector approximation), with the defect data held fixed. Establishing that hypothesis for fixed microscopic U is an open gate in this leaf. No claim is made that the one-particle Robin/delta limit alone proves the interacting many-body continuum limit.

6. Exact sufficient statistic and archive consequence
------------------------------------------------------
For any Hamiltonian H_(N,lambda)=H_(N,0)-(lambda/L^2)M with [H_(N,0),M]=0,

  rho_(N,lambda)=sum_m q_(N,lambda)(m) omega_(N,m),

where omega_(N,m) is the normalized lambda-independent Gibbs state in the m-particle sector. The encoder can measure M, store m exactly for m<D-1, and use one overflow symbol for m>=D-1. The worst-case trace error is at most sup_lambda P(M>=D-1). Thus the free XX rank-r defect result gives

  log e_(N,D) <= -(4/3)tau J(D-1-r)^3+O((D-1-r)^2),

for D sufficiently larger than r, uniformly in N. This is only an upper bound; no matching lower bound is claimed. The retained classical memory is log_2 D bits. A decoder may need an expensive conditional Gibbs preparation; no gate-count or efficient physical preparation claim is included.

7. Proof/evidence boundary, falsifier, and prior art
---------------------------------------------------
The exact lattice recurrence, small characteristic polynomials, bound state at v=-4J, and centered-delta threshold are checked by exact rational arithmetic in exact_checks.py. Form convergence and spectral compactness are mathematical reconstructions stated above; they have not been independently formal-verified. The tail calculation uses only the clean bound a_(N,k)>=8Jk^2, finite-rank eigenvalue interlacing, Bernoulli factorization for free XX, and the prior cycle-1 Chernoff estimate.

Primary-source context checked 2026-10-08: Kunstatter and Louko, “Boundary conditions in quantum mechanics on the discretized half-line,” arXiv:1201.2886 (posted 2012-01-13; published J. Phys. A 45, 305302 (2012)), https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.2886. Their abstract and free-particle continuum-limit section report that suitable fine tuning recovers Robin conditions while generic discretized boundary data give Dirichlet; their discretization is polymer half-line quantization, not this finite XX Jacobi matrix. It is antecedent context, not a proof or priority claim for the present scaling. No novelty or priority claim is made.

Falsifiers for the scoped claims: (i) an exact solution of (2.1) inconsistent with (2.2)-(2.3); (ii) a rank-r perturbation whose (r+1)-st and higher eigenvalues violate the interlacing lower bound; (iii) a free-Gibbs sequence with the stated quadratic tail but non-summable occupations; or (iv) a positive-interaction sector partition function exceeding its free reference. The first two are blocked by the displayed recurrences/interlacing, and (iii) by the exponential majorant. The interacting deep-bound denominator issue is deliberately left unresolved rather than treated as established.
