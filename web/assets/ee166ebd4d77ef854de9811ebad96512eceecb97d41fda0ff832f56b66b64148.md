# Optimization/TCS child report: log-concave sampling oracle complexity

## Verdict

I reconstructed the pinned release mechanism in OpenAI result 139, *Subpolynomial query complexity for well-conditioned log-concave sampling*. Its guarantee is a worst-execution query bound in an exact real first-order oracle model: for every fixed `epsilon > 0`, there is a sampler with at most `C_epsilon d^epsilon` value-and-gradient queries and total-variation error at most `1/10`, uniformly over `V in C^2(R^d)` with `V(0)=0`, `grad V(0)=0`, and `I <= Hess V(x) <= 2I`. The same paper proves an `Omega(log d)` lower bound for arbitrary randomized adaptive algorithms, even on centered Gaussian quadratics. Thus the optimal dimension exponent in this model is zero, while the upper bound does not establish polylogarithmic queries or a bit/runtime bound.

The mechanism's load-bearing recursion is a query-preserving seed-translation identity. I reconstructed its proof below. I also prove a conditional target-stability strengthening: if a nearby admissible potential `W` has a gradient uniformly within `eta` of `V`, then running the released sampler on the exact oracle for `W` gives error at most `0.1 + eta/2` for the target `pi_V`, with the same query cap. This is a standard Bakry-Emery/log-Sobolev consequence, not a novelty or a finite-precision implementation claim. A general rounded or adversarially noisy vector oracle need not be the gradient of any nearby admissible `W`; the manuscript's exact pathwise identity does not by itself cover that case.

## Source mechanism at its true interface

The source first solves a reusable auxiliary problem. For `F in C^2`, `Lip(grad F) <= delta = d^{-e}`, center `x`, and scale `0 < r <= 1`, it targets

`nu_(x,r)(dz) proportional to exp(-|z|^2/2 - F(x + r z)) dz`.

The auxiliary sampler uses at most `polylog(d) delta^{-t}` evaluations of `grad F` on every execution and returns an output whose TV error against `nu_(x,r) * N(0,I)` is bounded by `polylog(d) delta^{K_0} (sqrt(d)+|grad F(x)|)`. Its construction requires fixed `K_0 >= 2`, `t > 0`, and `40(K_0+2)t < 1/4`; the constants and approximation orders are fixed before queries and random seeds are observed.

The construction has four pieces:

1. A Gaussian proximal identity turns a target draw plus Gaussian observation noise into a conditional law with a weak perturbation of a standard Gaussian. The small Hessian scale is `delta = d^{-e}`.
2. Gaussian transport and a stationary centering flow reduce the conditional sampler and a conditional gradient mean to finite affine formulas in exact conditional means. High-order Gaussian cumulant/tensor bounds control the formulas without paying a new factor of `d` at each derivative tier; only the final vector output incurs the principal `sqrt(d)` cost. These formulas are mathematical comparison circuits, not extra runtime oracles.
3. Section 7 replaces nested exact means by finite routines `M` and `S`. Every routine gets a finite uncached dependency tree, scalar accuracy labels, disjoint Gaussian seed blocks, and a deterministic maximum query count. The center of a nested routine is noised; seed covariance is reduced to compensate exactly for that noise.
4. A proximal chain takes `O(delta^{-1} log d)` auxiliary calls, followed by `O(log d)` variance-halving calls and a Gaussian base rule. The total cap is `polylog(d) delta^{-(1+t)} = d^{e(1+t)+o(1)}`. Choose `2e < epsilon` and a fixed `t < 1`; the exponent is then below `epsilon`. Finitely many smaller dimensions use a capped Gaussian rejection sampler and are absorbed into `C_epsilon`.

The internal simulation uses only gradient evaluations for the large-dimension auxiliary calls. The full statement is phrased with a value-and-gradient oracle, and the finite-dimension fallback uses potential values. Computation between queries is unrestricted, and the paper imposes and derives no arithmetic or bit-complexity bound. The lower bound `Q(d) >= c log d` is independent of the upper construction: for `V(x)=x^T A x/2`, a value-and-gradient query is equivalent to one matrix-vector query `x -> Ax`; deferred random rotations show that an adaptive algorithm with too few queries cannot match the target Gaussian in TV.

## Reconstructed load-bearing lemma: Gaussian absorption at a noisy center

Let `Q(x; z_1,...,z_m)` be a seeded deterministic routine with scale `r`. Suppose a vector `v in R^m` satisfies the exact, query-preserving identity

`Q(x + Delta; z + v tensor (Delta/r)) = Q(x; z)`

for every `x`, displacement `Delta`, and fixed seed list `z`; the physical oracle query points in both computations must agree. Suppose `||v||_2 <= D_s`. Define

`n = r/(2 D_s)`, and `P = (I_m - v v^T/(4 D_s^2))^(1/2)`.

For independent standard Gaussian vectors `Z,G_1,...,G_m` in `R^d`,

`Q(x + n Z; (P tensor I_d)G)` has exactly the same law as `Q(x;G)`.

**Proof.** Apply the pathwise identity with `Delta=nZ`. Since `n/r=1/(2D_s)`, the first output is

`Q(x; P G - v tensor Z/(2D_s))`.

The transformed seed vector is centered Gaussian. Its slot covariance is

`P P^T + v v^T/(4D_s^2) = I_m`.

Thus its slots are jointly independent standard Gaussians. They can be correlated with `Z`, but after the rewrite the routine's center is the fixed `x`; only the seed marginal determines its output law. Therefore the rewritten call has the standalone law `Q(x;G)`. Also, the eigenvalues of `P` lie in `[sqrt(3/4),1]`, so the seed transformation is invertible with operator norm of its inverse at most `2`.

This lemma is exact and dimension-free. It is what permits the recursion to invoke a child's standalone law only after making the child's center an ideal Gaussian perturbation. The manuscript separately proves pathwise stability for inaccurate supplied centers, constructs the routine shift vectors bottom-up, and bounds both routine height and number of seed slots before using accuracy estimates. That order avoids assuming that actual intermediate centers already have their ideal laws.

## Conditional strengthening: stability under a consistent nearby potential

Let `V,W in C^2(R^d)` both satisfy the release's admissibility conditions, and suppose

`sup_x ||grad W(x)-grad V(x)||_2 <= eta`.

Then

`||pi_V - pi_W||_TV <= eta/2`.

**Proof.** Put `f=W-V` and `mu=pi_V`. The gradient assumption makes `f` eta-Lipschitz. Since `Hess V >= I`, the Bakry-Emery criterion gives `mu` a log-Sobolev inequality with curvature `1`; the Herbst argument therefore gives

`log E_mu exp(t(f-E_mu f)) <= t^2 eta^2/2`.

The relative entropy in the indicated direction is

`D_KL(mu || pi_W) = E_mu f + log E_mu exp(-f)`
`= log E_mu exp(-(f-E_mu f)) <= eta^2/2`.

Pinsker's inequality yields `||pi_V-pi_W||_TV <= eta/2`. If `A` is the released algorithm run with the exact first-order oracle for `W`, its released guarantee and the triangle inequality give

`||L(A^W)-pi_V||_TV <= 1/10 + eta/2`,

with the same deterministic query cap `C_epsilon d^epsilon`.

This conditional result has a clean but narrow interface: the approximate information must be the exact value/gradient oracle of a *single admissible potential* `W`, uniformly close in gradient to `V`. If a surrogate's coordinate errors are each at most `2^{-b}`, then `eta <= sqrt(d) 2^{-b}`, so a target-law perturbation allowance `rho` would require `b >= (1/2)log_2 d + log_2(1/(2rho))`. This is only the precision needed for the *law comparison*, assuming such a consistent surrogate exists; it does not compile a rounded oracle into `W` or bound the arithmetic used by the sampler.

## Classical baseline and cost comparison

The closest prior algorithm in the same classical exact first-order model is Chen–Chewi–Lu–Zhang's smoothed Picard HMC, cited in the release as giving `~O(d^{1/6})` expected gradient queries at fixed conditioning and accuracy. The release notes that its supplied-minimizer setting matches this model and that a constant-factor stopping cap converts the expectation to a worst-case cap without changing the exponent (by Markov plus a fixed fallback). The released `d^epsilon` guarantee improves that exponent to zero for fixed TV error, while it still leaves the finer rate unknown.

The paper's transparent fully specified classical fallback is Gaussian rejection sampling from `N(0,I)`: accept `X` with probability `exp(-V(X)+|X|^2/2)`. Since the Hessian bounds and known minimizer imply `|x|^2/2 <= V(x) <= |x|^2`, this is a valid rejection rule and its mean acceptance probability is at least `2^{-d/2}`. Capping at `ceil(4*2^{d/2})` trials gives failure probability at most `e^{-4}` and costs one value query per trial. This gives a same-oracle baseline with a clear exponential cap; the main theorem's gain concerns oracle queries only, not total work or bits. For quadratic inputs, a model with the full matrix `A` supplied can sample `N(0,A^{-1})` by classical factorization, but that changes the information interface and is not a fair query-count comparator.

## Research boundary and next decisive gate

The conditional stability lemma is an elementary application of standard strong-log-concavity concentration. It is not a new sampling algorithm or evidence of finite-precision robustness. The novel load-bearing candidate within the released sampler remains the nested Gaussian absorption mechanism together with its finite deterministic query cap. The unresolved practical gate is a bit-complexity compiler that implements its query-preserving shift identities and nested high-order mean formulas from finite representations, or a counterexample showing that no such compiler can preserve the advertised TV accuracy at poly(d) bit cost.
