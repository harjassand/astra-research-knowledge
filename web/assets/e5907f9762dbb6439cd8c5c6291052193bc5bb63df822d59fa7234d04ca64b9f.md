# KLS claim: conditional statistical capability audit

**Checkpoint:** 2026-10-08. **Verdict:** the strongest application found is a conditional improvement to the Brownian-transport Stein/CLT bound for nonlinear images of log-concave laws. It is useful, quantitatively reconstructible, and its statistical scope is broader than ordinary KLS mixing consequences. It does **not** pass a novelty gate: the 2024 Brownian-transport paper already proves the composition theorem and explicitly anticipates a KLS-conditioned improvement. The upgrade below sharpens its published $(\log d)^{24}$ loss to $(\log d)^8$ by re-running its displayed proof with the claimed universal KLS constant. This remains conditional on an unvalidated KLS source claim and on the Brownian-transport proof chain.

No KLS-based replacement for the missing N75 scale-integrated mutual-information inequality was found. The statistical route here is separate; it does not close that information gap.

## Conditional theorem candidate

Let `p` be an isotropic log-concave probability measure on `R^d` with compact support. Let `chi: R^d -> R^k` be continuously differentiable with bounded partial derivatives, `E_p[chi]=0`, and `q=chi_*p` isotropic. Assume `E_p[||grad chi||_op^8] < infinity` and define

\[
G_8(\chi,p):=\bigl(\mathbb E_p\|\nabla\chi\|_{op}^8\bigr)^{1/2}.
\]

Assume the Bizeul–Klartag–Lehec (BKL) v1 KLS claim is correct, so the Poincare constant is at most `C0 * ||Cov(mu)||_op` for every log-concave `mu`, with a universal but unspecified `C0`. Conditional on the Mikulincer–Shenfeld Brownian-transport estimates, their proof gives, for source dimension `d >= 3`,

\[
\mathbb E_\gamma\|\mathcal D X_t\|_{\mathcal L(H,\mathbb R^d)}^{2m}
\le C_*^m(2m+1)!(\log d)^{4m},\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\]

and hence a Stein kernel `tau_q` and normalized-sum bound

\[
\mathbb E_q\|\tau_q\|_{HS}^2
\le C_*\min\{d,k\}(\log d)^8G_8(\chi,p),
\]

\[
W_2^2\!\left(\mathcal L\!\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^nY_j\right),\,N(0,I_k)\right)
\le {C_*\over n}\left(\min\{d,k\}(\log d)^8G_8(\chi,p)+k\right),
\quad Y_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}q.
\]

The published Brownian-transport theorem states the Stein-kernel term with `d` rather than `min(d,k)`; its proof explicitly obtains the rank bound `min(d,k)`. Keeping that factor is a direct proof-level tightening, not a novelty claim. Replacing `(log d)^8` by `(log(ed))^8` absorbs the finitely many small dimensions into `C_*`.

The `W2` statement implies that every `L`-Lipschitz test functional has expectation error at most `L * sqrt(C_*[min(d,k)(log(ed))^8 G8 + k]/n)` under the Gaussian replacement. Thus, if its inputs are supplied, a normalized aggregate of independent nonlinear measurements can be replaced by a Gaussian with an explicit dimension and derivative-moment rate even when `q` itself is not assumed to satisfy a Poincare inequality.

### Reconstruction of the logarithmic improvement

The BTM source is the V4 arXiv source of the published 2024 paper; the source archive is preserved under `source_btm/` (tar SHA-256 `46d3ad972832c7a472c12f7d3eccde6b971a9a6143c06870cac12a758a809545`; extracted TeX hash recorded in `CLAIMS.json`). Its Theorem 4.2 proof assumes a bound `C_KLS <= a d^kappa` and sets the matrix-moment order `r = ceil(1/kappa)+1`. Combining its displayed estimates around source lines 1479–1505 gives, up to universal factors,

\[
\mathbb E[\tau^{-m}]\le {C^m m!m\over t^m}
\left[r(\log d)d^{2\kappa+1/r}\right]^m.
\]

The subsequent derivative-moment step squares the bracket. With the pre-KLS input `kappa = log(log d)/log d`, the paper bounds the bracket by `O((log d)^6)` and obtains its `(log d)^(12m)` factor.

If the BKL KLS claim holds with `C_KLS <= C0`, choose the positive exponent `kappa = 1/log d`; then `a = C0/e` works and `r = ceil(log d)+1` is on the order of `log d`. Thus `d^(2*kappa+1/r) <= e^3`, while `r*log(d) = O((log d)^2)`. The same proof gives the derivative-moment factor `(log d)^(4m)`. Setting `m=4` yields `sqrt(E[||D X_1||^8]) = O((log d)^8)`. The choice `kappa=0` is not valid in this argument because `r` is defined using `1/kappa`.

In the BTM Stein-kernel proof, the other dimension factor is the rank of a `k x k` matrix that factors through `R^d`, hence at most `min(d,k)`. Combining the moment improvement and this rank factor gives the conditional bound above. This is an internal substitution through a published proof, not an independent reproof of its stochastic-calculus lemmas; it inherits BTM's regularity/approximation assumptions and cited stochastic-localization estimates.

## Exact statistical and computational interface

The target is not a generic posterior estimate. It is a normalized sum of `n` independent copies of a transformed log-concave latent vector. The source dimension is `d`, the measured vector dimension is `k`, `p` must be isotropic log-concave, and `q=chi_*p` must have mean zero and covariance `I_k`. The map may be nonlinear; no log-concavity or spectral-gap premise is imposed on `q`. This is the point that reaches beyond the identity-map CLT.

For a specified Wasserstein tolerance `epsilon`, the theorem suggests

\[
n\ge {C_*\left(\min(d,k)(\log(ed))^8G_8+k\right)\over\varepsilon^2}.
\]

The acquisition and oracle boundary is:

- With an exact iid sampler for `p`, producing the comparison sum requires exactly `n` independent draws from `p`, `n` evaluations of `chi`, and `O(nk)` output-coordinate arithmetic; a streaming sum needs `O(k)` storage. The Gaussian proxy requires `k` normal variates after mean/covariance normalization. Its Wasserstein guarantee is distributional; BTM does not provide an explicit coupling algorithm that converts a given sample-sum realization to its coupled Gaussian realization.
- If `p` is given by a density and convex-support oracle instead, the theorem supplies no sampler. A separate hit-and-run implementation must charge line restriction/integration, conditional sampling, warm-start ratio `M`, geometry, and finite-precision error. KLS would improve the spectral-gap parameter in such a sampler, but sampling itself is a standard KLS consequence and is not the novelty candidate here.
- Centering and isotropy of `q` are exact theorem inputs. If the mean `m_q` and covariance `Sigma_q` are not supplied, they must be estimated or certified. Whitening changes the gradient moment by
  \[
  G_8(\Sigma_q^{-1/2}(\chi-m_q),p)\le\lambda_{min}(\Sigma_q)^{-2}G_8(\chi,p),
  \]
  so near-singular output covariance has a quadratic inverse-eigenvalue cost. If `q` is supported on a proper affine subspace, first reduce to its covariance rank.
- The eighth gradient moment `G8` must be supplied analytically or with a valid upper certificate. A finite empirical average alone does not certify it without additional tail information. Compact support makes moments finite under the stated smoothness, but its diameter and a usable numerical upper bound still enter a concrete implementation.
- In a finite-precision implementation, if each transformed summand has deterministic vector error at most `delta_chi`, the normalized sum error is at most `sqrt(n)*delta_chi`; reserve an arithmetic budget `epsilon_num` by taking `delta_chi <= epsilon_num/sqrt(n)`. If input sample error is at most `delta_x` and `chi` has certified Lipschitz constant `L_chi` on the support, require `delta_x <= epsilon_num/(L_chi*sqrt(n))`. Coordinate bit depth then depends on the support/output range, `L_chi`, `n`, `k`, and `epsilon_num`; no such ranges or bit model are fixed by either paper.

The result is therefore an oracle-level statistical theorem, not a finite-bit certified software package. BKL's KLS constant is existential and not numerically extracted in the source theorem. BTM's `C_*` is also left universal, and its stochastic-transport construction is not an oracle-efficient sampler. A rigorous finite `n` threshold or an end-to-end bit complexity cannot be reported from these sources alone.

## Prior-art attack and novelty boundary

The following candidates were rejected as routine or already covered:

- KLS implies concentration, Poincare/isoperimetry, thin-shell/slicing implications, and better conductance-based sampling/volume bounds; these are the theorem's advertised core consequences. BKL gives these formulations and identifies sampling and volume computation as original motivations. They are not new applications of the KLS claim.
- For `q=p` or an invertible linear transform, Courtade–Fathi–Pananjady already construct Stein kernels under a Poincare inequality with squared norm controlled by `d*C_P`, and derive dimension-optimal `W2` CLTs. A correct KLS theorem makes `C_P=O(1)`, so the identity/linear case is a direct consequence and is better than routing through BTM's logarithmic loss.
- The genuine interface in BTM is composition: it constructs a Stein kernel for `q=chi_*p` even when `q` is not Poincare, and its Corollary 1.7 already gives a CLT with `d*(log d)^24*G8/n`. It treats `k>d` and tensor-power sums and says it resolves a previously posed tensor-power CLT question. The introduction also discusses heavy-tailed nonlinear images, but the displayed theorem assumes compactly supported `p`, in which case continuous `chi` has bounded image; a claim for genuinely heavy-tailed `q` needs the paper’s extension/approximation argument beyond that displayed interface. The paper's Remark 1.5 already says KLS would imply stronger Brownian-transport derivative moments up to logarithmic factors. Therefore this lane cannot claim the compositional CLT itself as new; the narrow reconstructed delta is the exponent reduction `24 -> 8` plus preservation of the `min(d,k)` rank factor.
- A 2026 preprint by Liu, arXiv:2608.27657, claims a dimension-free Wasserstein CLT for log-concave inner products using Letwin estimates. Simple bilinear/inner-product examples are therefore especially poor novelty targets.

No executable high-ceiling algorithm survives the acquisition review. The Gaussian proxy is simple once the sufficient constants/moments are supplied, but its only certified error rate depends on an unquantified KLS constant, unquantified BTM constants, supplied output covariance, and a certified eighth gradient moment. The narrow conditional statistical theorem is still worth preserving for the root synthesis; it should be described as a source-anticipated improvement, not a new KLS application.

## Source state, limitations, and decisive gates

BKL v1, arXiv:2610.05474v1, claims a universal Poincaré constant for every isotropic log-concave measure. Its own source theorem does not give a numerical value. The local proof source is `work/agents/probability_kinetics/kls_source/KLS5.tex`, SHA-256 `d848146f747a1c7009a5cf108d2fb455c14329b099787d8bb4b552fb0f170639`. The claim is source-reported, not independently validated in this lane.

Version drift matters: BKL v1 cites Song–Zhang arXiv:2610.01447v1 as a high-order tilt-derivative criterion. As of 2026-10-08, arXiv metadata reports Song–Zhang v2, uploaded after BKL v1, titled *An O(1) Bound for the KLS Constant* and itself claiming KLS. The BKL argument and the current Song–Zhang version must not be silently merged. `/root/probability_kinetics` is auditing this source discrepancy; this application uses only the KLS implication as a conditional premise and does not validate either proof.

The Astra N75 covariance-only heat-flow plateau and its integrated-mutual-information route remain logically separate. KLS supplies a Poincaré inequality, but the missing additive information bound for the matrix-valued observation channel still does not follow from the supplied N75 summary. The reported Hellinger/affinity obstruction remains; no new likelihood-ratio inequality is asserted here.

Decisive next gates are: (1) external proof audit of BKL v1 and resolution of its Song–Zhang version dependency; (2) a line-by-line audit that BTM's `q=ceil(1/kappa)+1` argument permits the positive choice `kappa=1/log d` with all constants dimension-independent; (3) a numerical/effective KLS constant and BTM constant extraction if a finite `n` or bit guarantee is desired; (4) a concrete nonlinear `chi` family with certified isotropic output and gradient moment where existing Poincare-source CLTs do not apply, followed by an equally informed prior-art comparison. A counterexample sequence for which

\[
{nW_2^2(q_n,\gamma_k)\over \min(d,k)(\log(ed))^8G_8+k}
\]

is unbounded while the stated assumptions hold would refute the conditional upgraded bound.
