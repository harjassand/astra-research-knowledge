# Exact-mean vector discrepancy for bipartite perfect matchings

## Result

I derived a polynomial-bit compiler, conditional on Li's rational Komlos-signing algorithm, for arbitrary rational perfect-matching marginals on any explicitly represented bipartite graph. Given edge marginals `x` and a rational feature matrix `A`, it returns at most `|E|+1` perfect matchings with rational probabilities whose edgewise mean is exactly `x`. Every output atom has feature error

`||A(1_M-x)||_infinity < 100 B`,

where `B` is a valid rational upper bound on the Euclidean feature norm of an alternating vector on any simple graph cycle. The implementation computes `B=min(B0,2n max_e ceil(||A_e||_2))`, where `B0=sum_{e,j}|A[j,e]|`; both terms are exact input-derived bounds, so no cycle-norm oracle is needed. For unit-Euclidean-norm edge columns the implemented choice has `B<=2n`, giving `<200n`.

This is an internal composition candidate, not externally validated and not a novelty or priority claim. Li's signing theorem is source-reported. The compiler invokes that routine through a callback; the paper's signer itself is not copied into this workspace. The callback contract is checked exactly by the implementation.

## Mechanism

The compiler first decomposes `x` by repeated exact network-flow subtraction into at most `|E|` perfect matchings. It then rounds those rational mixture weights to a common dyadic grid with a systematic-sampling construction. This creates a law on dyadic fractional matching points with exact mean `x`; every seed point is within `B/2` in feature norm.

At a dyadic point `y=b/2^k`, the edges with odd numerators have even degree at every vertex. An Euler decomposition splits that support into alternating cycles. Li's `<99` signing, applied to the actual feature vectors of those cycles after rational normalization, selects a signed circulation `s`. The two child points `y±s/2^k` are both feasible matching marginals on the next dyadic grid, have midpoint `y`, and each add less than `99B/2^k` error. Antipodal branching preserves the current mean. Summing the geometric error over all levels and adding the seed error gives `<99.5B`, hence `<100B`.

After each level, exact rational affine-dependence elimination compresses the current law to at most `|E|+1` existing points. Compression preserves the exact mean and cannot enlarge the atomwise error. The final points are integral degree-one vectors, hence actual perfect matchings.

The cycles are only circulation directions that preserve the bipartite degree equations and the box constraints. The argument does not assert they are edges of the matching polytope.

## Input and cost

The input graph has `n` vertices on each shore and labelled edges `E`. A feasible rational marginal `x` is specified explicitly by nonnegative edge entries with degree sum one at every vertex. `A` is an explicitly supplied rational `d x |E|` matrix. There are no optimization or separation oracles beyond polynomial-time bipartite matching in the flow decomposition, and no random source is needed to output the law.

The network-flow stage has at most `|E|` exact matching calls. The dyadic depth is `K=ceil(log2(2sn))`, where `s<=|E|` is the decomposition support. Each level handles at most `|E|+1` atoms. Each signing call has at most `|E|` cycle columns and `d` rows; Li reports `O(t^2 log t)` updates, `O(dt+t^(omega+2)log^3 t)` arithmetic/comparisons, and polynomial rational-bit complexity for a `d x t` input with Euclidean column norms at most one. The affine-support compression matrices have at most `|E|+1` rows and `2(|E|+1)` columns. All dyadic coordinates have `O(log(nE))` bits. For repeated compression, probabilities share a common denominator `Q`: branching changes it to `2Q`, and merging duplicate atoms keeps that denominator. Each null-relation elimination multiplies `Q` by one integer bounded by `r!2^{kr}`, where `r<=|E|+1` and the points are on the `2^k` grid. There are at most `|E|+1` eliminations per level and `K` levels, so output weight bit length is `O(L_x+K|E|^2(K+log(|E|+1)))`. This polynomial bound follows the implemented dependence updates; it does not assume a fresh LP at each level. The full composition therefore has polynomial bit runtime if Li's stated rational signer is used.

The included module implements the decomposition, systematic seed law, Euler-cycle decomposition, exact compression, and output extraction. `exhaustive_signer` permits small finite instances but is exponential. The runnable [exact behavior checks](test_exact_behavior.py) pass on K2,2 with nondyadic marginals, C6/C10 sharp obstructions, uniform K4,4 with a multi-cycle signing call, zero features, and rank-deficient affine compression. These exercise the actual rational wrapper and compressor; they do not validate Li's polynomial signer or its imported theorem.

## Source premise and prior boundary

Li's primary source, *Fast Spectral Signing for Vector Balancing*, states in Theorem 1.1 that rational input matrices with each column of Euclidean norm at most one admit a deterministic signing of infinity discrepancy `<99`, with polynomial bit complexity; it reports `O(n^2 log n)` updates and `O(mn+n^(omega+2)log^3 n)` arithmetic/comparisons. The source explicitly counts arithmetic and comparison operations and explains its rational implementation and polynomial bit lengths in §11.3. We satisfy its norm premise by passing `A u_C/B`; the computed `B` is rational and bounds each such column exactly. This source is not independently proof-checked here: [arXiv:2609.30044v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30044).

This construction differs from centered Komlos signing: Li returns one signing, not a law with arbitrary prescribed mean. The antipodal children provide a zero-mean signing law at each level, while the systematic seed distribution carries the noncentered rational marginals exactly.

The prior Astra N24 cube compiler already records exact rational arbitrary means, a support bound at most `n+1`, and a vector bound `1+mu` when `mu=sum_i min(p_i,1-p_i)<=1`, otherwise `<1+12 sqrt(r mu)` with `r=ceil(log2 R)` rows (uniformly `2+12 sqrt(r mu)`). Astra's P-round/ROUND record also states a general Boolean-polytope exact-mean theorem under exact strong rational optimization, with support at most `dim(F)+1` and radius `1161 k(P)` for unit-norm columns, where `k(P)` is the maximum support size of an actual polytope-edge direction. Exact weighted perfect-matching optimization is available, so that general interface applies to bipartite matching as a source-derived prior result. These Astra records are internal unreviewed research, not external validation; see [N24](../mathematics_algorithms/children/constructive_rounding/sources/N24-acquired-rational-rounding.txt) and [P-round](../mathematics_algorithms/children/constructive_rounding/sources/P-round.txt).

The new cycle parameter `B` is not identified with `k(P)`: some support-graph cycles need not be actual polytope edges, and no such identification is needed in the proof. For unit edge columns, every graph cycle has norm at most its length, hence the implemented `B<=2n`; the resulting `<200n` bound is a matching-specific alternative to the P-round worst-case `<1161 k(P)` guarantee. It can be better for feature matrices whose cycle vectors are small; it does not dominate when `k(P)` is much smaller than `2n`.

The adjacent constructive-rounding CYCLE2 artifact uses a different initial rounder: it cycle-rounds the fractional parts of `2^T x`, then follows an antipodal coarsening path and returns one sampled matching. Its pathwise scale `U_t` keeps actual cycle feature norms visible, and its C6/C10 example proves that unit edge-column norms alone cannot yield a graph-independent all-atom constant. The base report leaves explicit-law extraction open, while its support-compression addendum gives an internal synchronous-compression proof but says that implementation and finite testing remain undone. This compiler starts from an exact matching decomposition, uses systematic dyadic rounding to create a small law, and compresses after each pair of branches; the code and small exact tests make that route concrete. It uses a global computable bound rather than CYCLE2's pathwise `U_t`, and makes no edge-of-polytope claim about its circulation cycles. CYCLE2's K2,2 scalar construction is unconditional and sharper for scalar product blocks, while its centered vector block construction is a separate special case. None yields a graph-independent general matching bound, as the cycle obstruction rules that out.

There is also a [CYCLE2 support-compression addendum](../mathematics_algorithms/children/constructive_rounding/SUPPORT_COMPRESSION_AUDIT.md) that supersedes the base report's `UNKNOWN` extraction sentence with an internally proved synchronous-compression construction, while explicitly leaving its implementation untested. Its conservative denominator analysis and this compiler's sharper seed-plus-halving recurrence both show polynomial bit height: multiplying polynomial-size determinants across a polynomial number of eliminations adds their bit lengths and does not cause an exponential bit-length recurrence. The earlier K2,2 vector ledger [CLAIMS_CYCLE2.json](../mathematics_algorithms/children/constructive_rounding/CLAIMS_CYCLE2.json) pointed to section names absent from `CYCLE2_REPORT.md`; that stale cross-reference is superseded by its corrected path to the K2,2 vector-block corollary and affine-compression bit proof in `SUPPORT_COMPRESSION_AUDIT.md`, with cycle-transition details in the base report. That CYCLE2 specialization remains conditional on Li and is not coded or finite-tested. This directory's general compiler is a separate result: `PROOF.md` Lemmas 1–4, the implementation `matching_dyadic_compiler.py`, `test_exact_behavior.py`, and Lemma 4's repeated-compression bit proof are its coded, tested, and internally proved evidence; Li remains an imported signing premise.

The construction follows the usual dyadic linear-discrepancy transfer pattern: ordinary signing controls each numerator-halving step, while antipodal branches keep that step's conditional mean zero and the systematic seed carries the nonzero target. This is not a new arbitrary-mean signing theorem. Gram-Schmidt Walk already supplies exact-mean laws with sub-Gaussian vector tails, but those tails do not bound every supported atom. The prior Astra P-round/ROUND record supplies a broader exact-mean Boolean-polytope theorem under strong rational optimization and an actual-edge parameter `k(P)`. These are substantive baselines; the present result is an alternative matching-specific construction and does not claim novelty or priority.

Astra's partition-base simultaneous-exchange route retains a `K4` Hall obstruction for arbitrary matroids. This result addresses only bipartite perfect matching through network flows and alternating cycles; it does not extend that route to general matroids or arbitrary Boolean polytopes.
