PRINCIPAL RESULT: COST-CONSTRAINED QUANTUM DETECTION
For a finite-dimensional channel with a pure reference output, let E be its
no-click effect and H its nonnegative input-cost operator with a unique zero
ray. Assume E>0. The optimal regularized parallel zero-false-alarm detection
exponent per expected input cost is

  alpha = lambda_max(H_+^(-1/2) (-log E)_+ H_+^(-1/2)).

The exact separable comparator is

  beta = sup_w [-log <w|E|w>]/<w|H|w>.

Every entangled block obeys miss >= exp(-alpha Q); spectral-window probes attain
the exponent. For kappa=condition_number(H_+), alpha/beta <= kappa. Explicit
three-level examples attain a constant times kappa: this dependence is sharp
in order. A separate exact two-use witness already exceeds every separable
block's exponent. A polynomial-gate coherent counting construction approaches
the limit; it must measure only window membership, not the full count.

Boundaries: expected input cost, parallel blocks, pure-reference zero-false-alarm
testing. Hard spectral energy budgets and apparatus work are not included.
Time covariance with invariant reference forces E,H to commute and removes the
gain. A full-rank null makes exact zero-false-alarm testing trivial. Generic
communication error can have a different exponent. The underlying smoothing
mechanism has established entropy-theoretic antecedents; priority is not cleared.
