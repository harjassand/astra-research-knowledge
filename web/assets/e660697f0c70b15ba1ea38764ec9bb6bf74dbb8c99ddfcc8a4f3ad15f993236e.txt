1. A STANDALONE UNIFORMITY LEMMA

Let mu be a fixed smooth positive area form on S^2. Fix a>0 and a preassigned
C-infinity compact set K of smooth metrics on S^2 with area form mu. Let a
class C of smooth polar metrics on R^3 have Euclidean cores and fixed-area
links H(t), t=log r. Every member has
 g=dr^2+f(r)^2 H(log r), b(t)=f(e^t)e^(-t),
with the following constants chosen before selecting a member of C:

  a <= b(t) <= 1,
  |b(t)-a| <= B t^(-1/2),
  |b_t(t)|+|b_tt(t)| <= B t^(-3/2),  t>=T_0.

For each member, let H_0(t) be a smooth reference link. Require H(t),H_0(t)
to lie in K (enlarging K by one fixed compact family is harmless), and for
every fixed spatial order q require finite, preassigned constants B_q with

  ||H-H_0||_{C^q} <= B_q delta(t),  delta(t)=t^(-9/16),
  ||H_t||_{C^q}+||(H_0)_t||_{C^q} <= B_q epsilon(t),
                                      epsilon(t)=t^(-3/4).

A fixed q sufficiently large for the coefficient/Schauder estimates suffices
for the proof; the all-q formulation matches the construction. No derivatives
of future stage thresholds appear in these assumptions.

For a fixed finite cutoff p, assume the first p reference eigenvalues, counted
with multiplicity and continued through allowed internal doubles, are spanned
by a real orthonormal eigenframe e_1(t),...,e_p(t) in L^2(mu). Require

  max_{i<=p} lambda_i(t) <= L_p,
  lambda_{p+1}^{ord}(H_0(t))-max_{i<=p}lambda_i(t) >= gamma_p>0,
  ||partial_t e_i(t)||_2 <= B_p epsilon(t).

The frame's spatial C^q bounds need not be separately assumed: compactness,
elliptic estimates, the bounded spectral range, and unit L^2 norm give them.
One may instead include those bounds as explicit hypotheses. The gap and
frame bounds are fixed across C. Their constants may depend arbitrarily badly
on p. Internal reference eigenvalue gaps need not be positive.

For integer j, T_j is exact harmonic restriction from the boundary of the
WHOLE ball of radius e^(j+1) to the sphere e^j. Define

 d(x)=(-1+sqrt(1+4x))/2,
 P_j^0=exp[-d(b(j)^(-2)(-Delta_{H_0(j)}))],
 P_j^*=exp[-d(b(j)^(-2)(-Delta_{H(j)}))].

Then there is a nonincreasing function omega(j)->0, fixed by C's coefficient
bounds, such that

  sup_C ||T_j-P_j^0||_{L^2->L^2} <= omega(j).

For the reference frame the sharper estimate is

  ||(T_j-P_j^*) e_i(j+1)||_2
       <= C_p [epsilon(j)(1+log j)+j^(-3/2)(1+log j)]
       = o_p(delta(j)), i<=p,

uniformly over C. Let E_j=span{e_i(j)} and Q_j=E_j^perp, and write the
T_j blocks using that frame. For one uniform late J_p and a fixed kappa_p<1,

  T_QL=O_p(delta(j)),
  T_LL=diag(exp[-d(b(j)^(-2)lambda_i(j))])+O_p(delta(j)),
  ||T_LQ|| <= Omega_p(j) := omega(j)+C_p epsilon(j) ->0,
  ||T_QQ|| <= kappa_p tau_j,
  tau_j=min_{i<=p} exp[-d(b(j)^(-2)lambda_i(j))] >= tau_{*,p}>0.

There is one unique small bounded invariant graph family V_j:R^p->Q_j, j>=J_p,
with

  ||V_j|| <= C_p delta(j),
  A_j=(T_LL+T_LQ V_(j+1))^(-1)
       =diag(exp[d(b(j)^(-2)lambda_i(j))])+E_j^{mat},
  ||E_j^{mat}|| <= C_p delta(j),
  ||T_LQ V_(j+1)|| <= C_p Omega_p(j) delta(j).

All constants and moduli are uniform over C; there is no dependence on which
future tail is eventually completed. For a fixed locally finite stage schedule,
if each compact ball involves finitely many continuous control coordinates,
V_j and A_j are continuous in the countable control product topology.

2. PROOF OF THE OPERATOR MODULUS: THE QUANTIFIERS THAT REMOVE CIRCULARITY

Fixed-area harmonic restriction is positive, preserves constants and mu-mean,
and contracts L^2. Indeed the spherical mean satisfies (f^2 mean(u)_r)'=0;
regularity at the Euclidean core makes the derivative zero. Positivity and
constant preservation give (Rv)^2 <= R(v^2), and integrating with preserved
mean proves contraction. This holds at EVERY pair of radii and for every
history, without spectral assumptions or Ricci estimates.

If the uniform modulus failed, choose members of C, times j_l->infinity, and
||v_l||_2<=1 whose operator difference stays bounded below. After subsequences,
v_l converges weakly to v and H_0(j_l) converges smoothly to a link H_infinity
in K. Put x=t-j_l-1. On every fixed cylinder in x<=0 the coefficient envelopes
force H(t) to converge to H_infinity, and b(t) to a. First time derivative
bounds give uniform convergence in each required spatial norm on the entire
fixed-width cylinder. The normalized equation tends to

  U_xx+U_x+a^(-2)Delta_{H_infinity} U=0.

Contraction supplies ||u_l(x)||_2<=1 at every inner sphere. Uniform ellipticity
and compact coefficient bounds give interior Schauder compactness and strong
L^2 convergence at x=-1, after a diagonal subsequence. This does not require
any control of the core or the unobserved distant past beyond contraction.

To identify the boundary trace, test against a fixed smooth phi. The scalar
y_l=<u_l,phi> satisfies y_l''+gamma_l y_l'=bounded RHS, with
 gamma_l=1+2b_t/b,
and the RHS is uniformly bounded by ||b^(-2)Delta_H phi||_2. A mean-value
bound for y_l' on [-1,-1/2], followed by its first-order equation, gives a
uniform Lipschitz bound up to 0. Approximation by smooth boundary data and
contraction preserve this bound for L^2 data. Therefore the limit has weak
boundary value v.

Every frozen angular eigenmode has exponents d(a^(-2)lambda) and
-1-d(a^(-2)lambda). Uniform sectional L^2 boundedness on the entire backward
half-cylinder excludes the second exponent, including -1 on constants. Thus
 U(x)=exp[x d(a^(-2)(-Delta_{H_infinity}))]v.

The frozen solutions corresponding to P_j^0 v_l have precisely the same
interior strong limit at x=-1. Consequently T_j_l v_l-P_j_l^0 v_l tends to
zero, contradicting the lower bound. This proves uniformity over the entire
preassigned coefficient class, not merely over a completed construction.
Define omega(t) by the tail supremum of the operator difference. It tends to
zero and is monotone; no rate is asserted. Future stage starts need never be
chosen to define it.

3. FINITE-FRAME AND GRAPH BOUNDS

Freeze the ACTUAL link at j and solve the bounded-backward frozen equation
with boundary vector e_i(j+1). Compactness of K and uniform frame spatial
norms bound the frozen solution and all derivatives needed in the residual.
Take a comparison cylinder [j-ell,j+1], ell=3 log j. Coefficients there differ
from their value at j by O_p(epsilon(j)(1+ell)); radial variation is at most
O_p(j^(-3/2)(1+ell)). The residual has that same bound, say r_j.

The exact-minus-frozen solution vanishes at j+1. Its left-end discrepancy is
uniformly bounded in sup norm because the frozen solution is bounded and the
whole-ball maximum principle bounds the exact extension by its smooth outer
boundary value. The scalar barrier

  K r_j(j+1-t)+K exp[-(t-j+ell)/2]

dominates both signs: gamma>=3/4 for late times and its scalar differential
operator is <=-C r_j. At t=j it gives O_p(r_j+exp[-ell/2]); the latter term is
j^(-3/2). This is o(delta(j)). The entire earlier history has been absorbed
in one uniform left-end bound.

The actual first-p spectral projector differs from the reference projector
by O_p(delta(j)) by the protected cluster gap. Only the WHOLE cluster is
projected, so no internal tiny gap or internal double divides this estimate.
The low block of the frozen actual operator differs from its reference
diagonal by O_p(delta(j)); its high-low block has the same bound. The frame
moves by O_p(epsilon(j)). These facts give T_QL and T_LL as stated.

The norm-freezing estimate and frame motion give T_LQ<=Omega_p. Compactness
and gamma_p give a positive minimum of the difference between the first high
cone frequency and the largest low one. Therefore the reference high-block
norm is <=kappa_ref,p tau_j with kappa_ref,p<1. Since tau_j>=tau_*,p>0,
the absolute omega error is eventually absorbable into a fixed kappa_p<1.

For small V set
 M_j(V)=T_LL+T_LQ V,
 F_j(V)=(T_QL+T_QQ V) M_j(V)^(-1).
Uniform inverse bounds and the inverse-difference identity show that F_j has
one uniform Lipschitz constant kappa_0,p<1 in a fixed small ball. Its value
at 0 is O_p(delta(j)). Iterating from a zero outer graph at N and taking
N->infinity gives the unique graph, with

  ||V_j|| <= C_p sum_(l>=0) kappa_0,p^l delta(j+l)
          <= C'_p delta(j).

The tail is bounded by a uniform geometric factor, independently of ALL
future controls. The local graph correction has the claimed bound simply
by multiplying T_LQ and V_(j+1). Finite-matrix inversion then gives A_j.

For a fixed schedule, finitely many controls occur on each compact ball.
Dirichlet solutions depend strongly and continuously on those controls.
Strong continuity suffices on each finite low basis and each finite-rank
candidate graph; no full infinite-dimensional operator-norm continuity is
required. Finite graph iterates are continuous, and their uniform geometric
tail makes the limiting graphs and matrices continuous in the control product.
The stage schedule itself is fixed at this point; continuity in varying
stage start times is neither used nor needed.

4. VERIFYING THE COEFFICIENT HYPOTHESES FROM THE STAGE CONSTRUCTION

This verification is CONDITIONAL ON STEP 1 delivering its claimed compact
reference skeleton and protected gaps. It does not prove that spectral step.
Once that skeleton exists, the following choices are sufficient and can all
be made before the final radial schedule.

For each fixed spatial order q, the union of controlled link types is compact.
There are finitely many band-n directions and bump neighborhoods for each n.
The earlier finitely many bands have compact amplitude intervals. At late
bands n>=q, normalize the directions in C^(n+2) and restrict their amplitude
windows to <=2^(-n), also staying inside U. The crossing detours converge to
round in every fixed norm. Hence every sequence from new bands converges
smoothly to round after a subsequence; a sequence from finitely many bands
has a compact subsequence in a finite family. Smooth continuity of the fixed
area gauge preserves this compactness. The all-control library is pre-fixed.

For stage m take the finite program P_m and allow its finitely many local
amplitudes to vary in these pre-fixed compact windows. Let A_(q,m) and
B_(q,m) bound its first and second phase derivatives in C^q. Let C_(q,m)
bound first phase derivatives of the reference eigenframe through p_m.
These numbers are finite because every stage is a finite smooth program;
continued-label permutations/signs do not change the norm bounds. Choose
nondecreasing D_m>=1 so that, for q<=m,

  A_(q,m)/D_m <=1,
  B_(q,m)/D_m^2 <=1,
  C_(q,m)/D_m <=1.

Here the phase derivatives include the smooth bump and the fixed-area gauge.
This is an explicit meaning of the derivative normalization in Step 3. The
choice does not involve start times, Omega, or future control values.

With eta_m(t)=t^(-3/4)/D_m,
 H_t=H_s eta_m,
 H_tt=H_ss eta_m^2+H_s eta_m',
 eta_m'=-(3/4)t^(-7/4)/D_m.
Thus fixed-order first derivative bounds are O_q(t^(-3/4)); second derivative
bounds are O_q(t^(-3/2)). For q fixed, all m>=q have the prescribed bound,
and the finitely many m<q only enlarge a finite constant. Reference low-frame
time derivatives have the same envelope for m>=n; earlier stages are finite.
At stage joins the angular program is constant in a phase neighborhood,
so changing D_m does not introduce an angular derivative jump.

There is one further weak scheduling inequality. Impose before considering
any stronger analytic requirement

  D_m S_m T_m^(-1/4)<=1/2,
  (T_m^(-3/4)/D_m)^(3/4) <= each applicable amplitude window.

For a bump center t_nu and any t in its support, integration of the stage
phase speed gives

  |t^(1/4)-t_nu^(1/4)| <= D_m S_m/4.

The first weak inequality makes t and t_nu uniformly comparable throughout
that support, independently of m. Therefore the center-defined amplitude
eta_m(t_nu)^(3/4) is O(t^(-9/16)); direction norms and compact gauge bounds
give the required O_q(t^(-9/16)) actual-reference bound. Without this weak
condition, center-defined amplitude bounds cannot simply be replaced by
bounds at every point of the support. The stronger Stage 6 averaging
condition D_m Q_m S_m T_m^(-1/4)<<1 already allows the weak condition.

Choose the Ricci buffer C from the now-fixed angular C^1 derivative envelopes
as in the source block formulas, and then choose the fixed radial tail start.
This gives the required uniform b estimates BEFORE defining the modulus.
Now define the transfer class directly by the coefficient envelopes of
Section 1, including these pre-fixed b envelopes. This larger class contains
every weakly admissible schedule and makes Omega independent of all stronger
schedule choices. No graph threshold is used to choose C, the radial tail,
the angular library, or D_m.

For each fixed n, the reference p_n boundary gap is assumed positive on the
compact skeleton. The eigenvalue range is bounded there; hence gamma_n and
tau_*,n are pre-fixed. Elliptic estimates bound each reference vector in every
fixed spatial norm even when internal gaps become arbitrarily small. Temporal
frame derivatives are handled separately by D_m; one must not infer them
merely from angular compactness. This addresses the potential low-frame loss
of compactness directly.

5. LOCAL CROSSING TRANSFER AND THE D_m FACTOR

Fix a band n and its finite local crossing types, with their fixed normalized
nonzero variation coefficients. At a crossing in stage m>=n put
 a_nu=eta_m(t_nu)^(3/4).
On a fixed closed plateau there is only the selected control. Use a slightly
larger fixed collar around the plateau, still inside that local crossing type
and away from all other supports. Every sufficiently late comparison cylinder
of length
 ell=4 log(1/eta_m(t_nu))
lies in this collar. It need not lie inside the exact chi=1 portion for points
arbitrarily close to the plateau edge: the collar's bump derivative bounds
suffice. This removes a minor endpoint imprecision in the candidate wording.

All links and first-p_n frames in this collar are from finitely many band-n
types, up to reference label permutations/signs. Their local phase derivative
constants depend on n, not on m. Thus the finite-frame barrier argument gives
local actual-link freezing error bounded by

 r_(n,m)(t)=C_n [(eta_m(t)+t^(-3/2))
                     (1+log(1/eta_m(t)))+eta_m(t)^2].

For each fixed m this divided by a_nu tends to zero as t_nu tends to infinity.
Explicitly its principal relative terms are

 eta_m(t)^(1/4)(1+log(1/eta_m(t))),
 D_m^(3/4)t^(-15/16)(1+log(1/eta_m(t))).

No assertion uniform in m before choosing starts is needed.

The invariant-graph correction is at most
 C_n Omega_n(t) t^(-9/16).
Dividing by a_nu introduces the factor D_m^(3/4), up to the uniformly bounded
center/time comparison. The direct finite-cluster Taylor remainder is
O_n(a_nu^2). Applying finite-cluster compression and inversion consequently
gives the stronger local matrix conclusion

  E_j^{mat}=a_nu z_nu B_n(s(j),b(j))
       +O_n(a_nu^2+r_(n,m)(j)+Omega_n(j)j^(-9/16)),

in ENTIRE p_n-by-p_n matrix norm. The (h,i) entry of B_n has the nonzero
source coefficient m_hi and fixed sign on this plateau. The matrix B_n is
uniformly bounded over the finitely many band-n types and b near a.

Choose the stage start so that, for every n<=m,

  r_(n,m)(t)/a_nu is sufficiently small,
  D_m^(3/4)Omega_n(t) is sufficiently small,
  a_nu is sufficiently small,

for every applicable later crossing in that stage. These are finitely many
requirements and hold for all t beyond a finite threshold. Then

  ||E_j^{mat}|| <= C'_n a_nu,
  (E_j^{mat})_hi=z_nu a_nu m_hi+small_n*a_nu,

with constants independent of stage m and all past/future controls. The
uniform graph estimate is exactly what absorbs unknown future stage choices.

6. WHY A FIXED RHO_n REQUIRES THE WHOLE LOCAL MATRIX ESTIMATE

Here is an exact counterexample to the weaker inference that the global matrix
bound plus the selected signed entry alone would suffice.

Let delta>0, D>=1, a=delta D^(-3/4), take a three-dimensional reference
matrix A_0=diag(1,1,2) with an isolated double in positions 1 and 2, and set
 E_21=a, E_23=delta,
with every other E entry zero. For the column
 v_1=e_1-rho e_3, 0<rho<1,
the exact normalized force in row 2 is

  F_21=( (A_0+E)v_1 )_2/( (A_0+E)v_1 )_1 =a-rho delta.

The denominator is exactly 1, ||E||<=2delta, and the selected signed entry
is exactly positive a. Yet F_21<0 whenever D^(3/4)>1/rho. Thus no fixed
rho independent of D protects the force sign under just those two bounds.
This counterexample does NOT satisfy the stronger local matrix bound just
proved, so it does not refute the scheduled geometric construction.

With ||E||<=C'_n a_nu, every numerator term involving column coordinates in
the exact source force formula is bounded by K_n rho_n a_nu. Choose rho_n
once so that K_n rho_n is less than a prescribed fraction of the finitely
many nonzero band-n variation coefficients. Denominator perturbations are
O_n(a_nu), so positive and negative endpoint controls then force opposite
signs uniformly over all other columns/controls, across every later stage.
This is the rigorous reason the fixed boxes can replace the source's
shrinking boxes. It should be inserted into Steps 5-7 explicitly.

7. FINITE STAGE STARTS AND THE NONCIRCULAR ORDER OF CHOICES

A valid order is:

  compact angular skeleton and all local directions/bump types;
  compact controlled amplitude windows;
  finite-program derivative constants and nondecreasing D_m;
  weak admissibility inequalities and angular coefficient envelopes;
  Ricci buffer and fixed radial tail, hence pre-fixed b envelopes;
  complete coefficient-envelope class, then its omega/Omega_n and
      all cutoff graph constants J_n,C_n;
  finally strong stage start thresholds.

At stage m, only n<=m occur in the required inequalities. D_m is already a
finite number. Omega_n(t) is a tail supremum tending to zero over the whole
preassigned envelope class. Hence D_m^(3/4)Omega_n(t) also tends to zero for
that fixed m. The fact D_m may tend to infinity with m causes no contradiction:
T_m is chosen separately for each m, and there is no upper bound on T_m.

Outside a fixed pair's own plateau, its ratio gap on the finite full-label
program P_m has a positive minimum by the assumed no-other-crossing property.
This minimum may be extraordinarily small and deteriorate with m; its value
is known once P_m is fixed. The uniform global E bound and fixed coordinate
box bounds therefore make |F_hi|<=epsilon_m |r_hi-1| hold after a finite
threshold. These inequalities do not rely on any unknown future graph beyond
the uniform C_n delta bound. Stable forcing then telescopes without dependence
on interval duration or tiny gaps. For the backwards recurrence the extra
r^-1 in each weight gives the corresponding telescoping identity.

All local signed conditions, Gaussian trial smallness conditions, global
relative-force conditions, and D_m Q_m S_m T_m^(-1/4) averaging conditions are
simultaneously finite late-time requirements. Use tail suprema and choose
T_m>=2T_(m-1); complete the preceding program at a full-label period before
switching. Its completion only moves the start later, so monotone tail
requirements remain satisfied. The asymptotic requirement should be read
as holding for all t>=T_m, not just numerically at the chosen endpoint.
There is no need to know the eventual future schedule to obtain any of these
thresholds. The threshold magnitude is existential and unbounded; no
computationally effective modulus is supplied.

8. ALL-RADIUS GROWTH

Assume the separate matching argument has supplied compatible whole-ball
traces with ||g_i(j)||_2<=C_i exp(gamma_i j), gamma_i<k_n. For any
real t in [j,j+1], whole-ball restriction gives
 ||U_i(t,.)||_2 <= ||g_i(j+1)||_2 <=C'_i exp(gamma_i t).
This is independent of current stage, controls, internal gaps, or cutoff.
The compact link library and a<=b<=1 make the normalized divergence equation
uniformly elliptic on every logarithmic cylinder of fixed width. Its local
L^2-to-sup constant is uniform across the entire envelope class. Therefore
sup_|x|=e^t |U_i(x)|<=C''_i exp(gamma_i t). The compact core is absorbed
into the constant. There is no new common-metric obstruction in this passage.
This statement assumes the spherical trace bound rather than proving the
entire preceding frequency averaging/matching program.
