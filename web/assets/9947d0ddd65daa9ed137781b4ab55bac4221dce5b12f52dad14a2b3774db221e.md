# Protein evolvability lane: nonlinear and censoring repairs do not clear a design gate

**Status, 8 October 2026: no new evolvability principle or validated design capability established.** I reanalyzed the released JUN × 52-bZIP deepPCA count table to test whether recovering variants excluded by the source QC rule changes the specificity ranking, and checked whether a nonlinear thermodynamic readout adds a useful double-mutant prediction beyond raw score addition. Neither repair unlocks a new result. One double mutant is a testable *mean FOS-family preference* hypothesis, but it is an unmeasured extrapolation, fails strict worst-case family specificity by a small amount, and is predicted almost identically by the simpler additive-score control.

## Question and exact assay interface

Bendel et al. measured the effect of JUN substitutions on interaction scores with human bZIP partners using deepPCA/DiMSum and fit a two-state thermodynamic mapping with MoCHI. The processed supplementary table has 30,180 variant–partner rows (including 52 WT reference rows), six biological replicates, 52 partner profiles, mutant and WT input/output counts, published fitness/relative scores, error estimates, per-partner energy terms, and a source filter. GEO is GSE245326; the downloaded processed table is recorded in `children/released_data/data/GSE245326_Supplementary_Data_3.txt.gz`.

The source code defines a row as filtered if any one of six mutant input counts is at most 10 or any one of six mutant output counts is zero (`006-merge_data.R`, line 68 in the pinned local checkout). The non-WT table therefore has 26,596 rows passing and 3,532 failing this filter. Of failing rows, 3,148 contain at least one zero mutant output, 1,887 contain at least one input count ≤10, with 1,503 in both classes, 1,645 zero-output-only, and 384 low-input-only. The filter is an assay/QC selection rule, not a biological null: a zero output count can be a real low-enrichment observation, while very small input counts give unstable offsets.

The biological endpoint is a deepPCA interaction enrichment score under the study’s cellular selection conditions. It is not a direct equilibrium dissociation constant, isolated binding free energy, kinetic rate, or organismal fitness. The inferred log enrichment below has arbitrary assay units; the exported MoCHI energy coordinates are not interpreted as kcal/mol.

## Count-level repair and what it changes

For JUN mutant (v), WT JUN, partner (b), and replicate (r), I condition on the observed total output count (N_{br}=O_{vbr}+O_{WT,br}). Under the assay model that output counts are proportional to input counts times a relative selection factor, the conditional likelihood is

\[
O_{vbr}\mid N_{br}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N_{br},p_{vbr}),\qquad
\operatorname{logit}(p_{vbr})=\delta_{vb}+\log(I_{vbr}/I_{WT,br}),
\]

where δ is the mutant-versus-WT log relative enrichment for that partner. A zero mutant output with a positive WT output is retained as a zero-success binomial outcome; no pseudocount is inserted. An input-zero replicate has no defined offset and is omitted. A replicate with zero total output has no conditional information and is omitted. The estimator and one-parameter profile interval condition on observed inputs and do not include input-count uncertainty, replicate random effects, or overdispersion.

The model yields an estimate for 30,128 of 30,128 non-WT rows with at least one positive input replicate; 29,933 have at least four informative replicate pairs. This is a substantial recovery of assay rows. On the 26,596 source-QC-passing pairs, the count log-odds agree with the published relative score (Pearson (r=0.99705)); a same-data, zero-intercept scale fit gives 0.28295 published-score units per log-odds unit and RMSE 0.02237. This is a calibration check against the same assay table, not independent validation.

The highest measured single-mutant mean-FOS-family-minus-maximum-off-target margin is JUN `1eA` (E32A in the paper’s residue notation): family mean (+0.2941), family minimum (+0.2060), maximum of the 48 non-FOS partners (+0.3553) for ATF4, so the margin is **−0.0612** in published relative-score units. The count-level estimate gives the same ordering and a margin of −0.0657 after same-data scale calibration. `1eA` passes source QC for all 52 partners, so neither rescued rows nor pseudocount choices change its profile. The other single used in the double hypothesis, `4aR` (A49R), is also complete: family mean +0.1655 versus maximum off-target +0.4375 for XBP1, margin −0.2720. Both singles already have all 52 scores; count-level censor repair changes neither one.

For `1eA`, the quadrature source-score errors for FOS, FOSB, FOSL1, FOSL2, and ATF4 are 0.0271, 0.0359, 0.0277, 0.0324, and 0.0308, respectively. A 52-partner Bonferroni profile calculation from the conditional likelihood still has a negative lower margin (about −0.075 score units); that interval is conditional on inputs and omits replicate overdispersion and winner-selection across substitutions. It is a diagnostic, not calibrated post-selection evidence. The measured single-mutant scan therefore does not identify a FOS-exclusive gain, much less a multi-step evolutionary route.

## Nonlinear double prediction, baseline, and failure

An exploratory screen in the released-data child analysis considers 294 single-mutant sequences with a complete 52-partner profile and 105 pairs passing a within-screen nomination rule (predicted FOS-family mean ≥0.30 and minimum ≥0.15). It ranks `1eA+4aR` first for the objective *mean of FOS/FOSB/FOSL1/FOSL2 minus the maximum of the other 48 partners*. This is a post hoc candidate-selection result on the same single-mutant table, not a held-out prediction.

The nonlinear-plus-residual prediction uses the paper’s published per-partner energy terms and a refit of three shared mapping parameters to the exported MoCHI means. Its fitted source-score map has RMSE 0.00431 over 26,648 accepted rows. For each partner (b), it predicts the double’s relative score as

\[
\big[g_b(\Delta_i+\Delta_j)-g_b(0)\big]
+(r_i-r_{WT})+(r_j-r_{WT}),
\]

where (g_b) is the global nonlinear energy-to-score map, Δ terms are source single-mutant energies added as if there were no double energetic coupling, and (r) is the single-mutant score residual from that map. Subtracting the WT residual once per mutation is necessary to recover the observed single-mutant score when the other mutation is WT. This is an *assumption about unmeasured double residuals*, not something estimated from a JUN double-mutant panel.

Predicted `1eA+4aR` scores are FOS +0.4515, FOSB +0.3996, FOSL1 +0.6620, FOSL2 +0.3082; family mean +0.4553, family minimum +0.3082, and maximum off-target +0.3172 for ATF7. Thus the predicted mean-family margin is +0.1381, but the strict all-targets-over-all-off-targets margin is **−0.0090**. It predicts mean-group preference, not strict specificity. The stronger matched control—raw addition of observed single-mutant relative scores—predicts mean +0.460, minimum +0.311, maximum off-target +0.314, and mean margin +0.145. The nonlinear-plus-residual model therefore does not create a material advantage over simple score addition here (the mean-margin difference is only about −0.007). The proposed experiment could test the double phenotype, but cannot be presented as evidence that nonlinear correction itself improves design.

The source assay’s per-score errors are about 0.026–0.036 for these singles, while the unvalidated difference in predicted mean margin between the two double models is about 0.007. There is no defensible double-mutant confidence interval in this analysis: carrying single-mutant errors through the model would omit interaction uncertainty, fit uncertainty, and model misspecification. Selecting the best of 105 pairs also creates winner’s-curse risk.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

The nonlinear thermodynamic mechanism is already established in the exact JUN/FOS system. Diss and Lehner measured 107,625 FOS–JUN trans double-mutant interaction pairs and showed that an additive free-energy change passed through a nonlinear binding/assay map predicts much of the double-mutant outcome; residual interactions also reflect structural contacts. Faure and Lehner’s MoCHI software explicitly fits user-specified thermodynamic models, nonlinear global epistasis, energetic couplings, and allostery from DMS. Bendel et al. 2024 apply MoCHI to the JUN × bZIP affinity/specificity dataset used here. Therefore neither “thermodynamic nonlinearity causes score epistasis,” “fit a nonlinear DMS map,” nor “use single mutants to nominate combinations by additive energies” is a new principle. The only novel-looking object here is a prospective `1eA+4aR` mean-family preference nomination, which is not yet measured and not a general mechanism.

## Decisive assay and costs

The smallest clean assay uses four JUN genotypes—WT, `1eA`, `4aR`, `1eA+4aR`—against all 52 bZIP partners, with six biological replicates: 208 genotype–partner cells per replicate, 1,248 measurements total. A full 71-partner panel with 19 negatives is 284 cells per replicate, 1,704 total. Keep the WT and both singles in every replicate; freeze the target family and all 48 comparator partners before data collection. Primary endpoint: paired deepPCA relative score. Report family mean, family minimum, maximum nonfamily partner, and both mean-margin and strict-minimum margin. Add an orthogonal direct-binding measurement (for example, a purified-protein titration) for FOS-family members and the predicted strongest off-target ATF7, plus ATF4 and XBP1 as known single-mutant off-target stress controls; retain matched abundance controls to separate binding from expression/display effects.

Pre-register the decision rule. A mean-preference claim passes only if the simultaneous family-mean minus maximum-off-target confidence lower bound is positive in an independent replicate set and the direct-binding check is directionally consistent. A strict-specificity claim additionally requires the simultaneous lower bound on the minimum family response minus maximum off-target to be positive. Compare nonlinear-plus-residual, raw score addition, source energy addition without residuals, and a no-double-advantage control using the same predeclared set and assay budget. Because the nonlinear and raw-additive predictions are almost indistinguishable here, this panel can test the candidate phenotype but is not a powerful test of whether nonlinear correction improves design. A model-advantage claim would require a frozen set of prospective double predictions where the nonlinear and additive baselines disagree by more than assay uncertainty.

The readout/sample totals above are calculable; cash cost is quote-only and cannot be responsibly priced from the released paper. There was no experiment, new data acquisition, or GPU/model training in this lane. Local computation consists of the released table and a standard-library likelihood script; replay takes tens of seconds on the workspace machine.

## Evidence status, limitations, and restart gate

- **Source-reported:** original six-replicate deepPCA/DiMSum and MoCHI measurements; the 2018 FOS–JUN double-mutant dataset and thermodynamic result; published MoCHI method and source QC rule.
- **Internally reconstructed / finite-tested:** count likelihood and profile intervals on released counts; same-table calibration; corrected single-residual double prediction; candidate/control metrics; synthetic likelihood checks.
- **Exploratory prediction:** `1eA+4aR` as a mean-FOS-family preference candidate; no direct double observation.
- **Not established:** new general representation, improved held-out design accuracy, direct thermodynamic or kinetic parameter recovery, evolutionary path/accessibility, a beneficial mutation trajectory, strict family specificity, independent validation, or priority.

The lane should restart only on one of two observations: (i) an independently replicated `1eA+4aR` result with simultaneous positive *mean* margin and direct-binding confirmation, which would validate a narrow sequence-specific hypothesis but still not a general principle; or (ii) a prospective benchmark with multiple frozen doubles where a nonlinear mechanistic representation beats equally informed raw-score-additive, sequence-only, and target-frequency controls on held-out assay measurements by a margin exceeding replicate uncertainty. Until then, count-level zero recovery and nonlinear readout are already-prior methods and do not constitute the requested advance.
