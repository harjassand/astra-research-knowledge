EXACT IDENTIFIABILITY FROM ADAPTIVELY SEARCHED OBSERVABLES
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact finite-model theorem and counterexample proved in the stated interfaces; no external review or novelty claim.

Question and interface
----------------------
The target is a finite-alphabet controlled process. A predictive state is a
conditional law of all future action/output experiments after a reachable
history. Histories h and h' are equivalent exactly when every finite future
test has the same conditional expectation after h and h'. Let P be the set
of reachable predictive states, and assume |P|=n<infinity for the
finite-state predictive-realization result below. This is a finite residual
state assumption: a finite hidden-state HMM can still have infinitely many
distinct posterior predictive states.

For action a and output o, write e_(a,o)(p) for the probability of o at p,
and U_(a,o)(p) for the updated predictive state when e_(a,o)(p)>0. For a
function f:P->R define the unnormalized shift

    (T_(a,o) f)(p) = e_(a,o)(p) f(U_(a,o)(p)),

with value 0 when e_(a,o)(p)=0. If w=(a_1,o_1)...(a_k,o_k), then
T_w 1(p) is the probability of that action/output word from p. A bounded
future observable q has feature function f_q(p)=E_p[q]. A finite candidate
bank D therefore gives a finite list of feature functions, but does not by
itself give the values, the state representatives, or the update operators.

Exact theorem: separating observable algebra and realization
------------------------------------------------------------
Let A_D be the unital pointwise real algebra generated by {f_q:q in D};
“algebra” means closure under finite sums, scalar multiples, and
pointwise products. The bank is feature-complete when A_D separates P:
for every distinct p,p' in P, some g in A_D has g(p) != g(p'). A feature
realization in A_D is update-closed when T_(a,o)A_D is a subset of A_D for
every legal action/output pair.

Theorem. If P is finite and D is feature-complete, then A_D=R^P. In
particular its functions contain the point indicators 1_{p}. In this
indicator basis, the process has the exact n-state stochastic predictive
realization

    M_(a,o)[p,p'] = e_(a,o)(p) 1{U_(a,o)(p)=p'},

where the row is zero for an impossible output. For every p and a,
sum_(o,p') M_(a,o)[p,p']=1, every entry is nonnegative, and products of
these matrices give every finite-word probability. Thus a separating
observable algebra plus exact shift closure recovers the full reachable
predictive machine. A finite-dimensional shift-invariant linear feature
space without the algebra/separation condition still gives a weighted
linear realization, but its matrices can be signed and its coordinates need
not identify predictive states.

Proof. Fix p in P. For each p' != p, separation supplies g_(p,p') in A_D
with g_(p,p')(p) != g_(p,p')(p'). Normalize it to

    h_(p,p') = (g_(p,p') - g_(p,p')(p')) /
               (g_(p,p')(p) - g_(p,p')(p')).

Then h_(p,p')(p)=1 and h_(p,p')(p')=0. The product
e_p=product_(p' != p) h_(p,p') belongs to A_D, equals 1 at p, and 0 at
every other predictive state. The e_p form a basis of R^P, so A_D=R^P.
Every shift is a linear map R^P->R^P and hence is represented in this basis.
Applying it to e_(p') gives the displayed entry. The probabilities
e_(a,o)(p) sum to one over o, and U_(a,o)(p) is a single successor, so the
matrices are stochastic when summed over output and successor. The
probability identity follows by composing the shifts.

The exact closure condition is necessary for an update to be expressible
inside the chosen feature algebra. If A_D is not separating, two distinct
predictive states have the same value for every acquired feature and every
algebraic combination of those features, so that bank cannot identify them.
For a minimal predictive-state set, unitality plus exact shift closure is
itself a completeness certificate: T_w 1 belongs to A_D for every word w;
distinct residual laws differ on some finite cylinder word, so these shifts
separate P. Conversely, separation on finite P implies A_D=R^P and thus
automatic shift closure as a statement about functions. In an acquisition
task, however, the learner still has to obtain or verify the shift values;
closure is not free information.

Related linear-realization statement. Let V be a finite-dimensional linear
space of predictive functions containing 1 and invariant under every
T_(a,o). For a basis g_1,...,g_r, express each T_(a,o)g_j in that basis.
The resulting r-by-r matrices and the evaluation functional at an initial
predictive state reproduce all word probabilities exactly. This is a
finite-dimensional weighted/OOM realization. It does not imply nonnegative
state transitions: signed minimal realizations are possible. The extra
pointwise algebra and separation are what supply the indicator basis and
the positive finite predictive-state machine.

Finite test existence and adaptive search
-----------------------------------------
If n is supplied and the complete candidate family contains all cylinder
tests of length at most n-1, a separating bank always exists. To see the
horizon bound, let V_k be the linear span of the word-probability functions
T_w1 for |w|<=k. Then V_0=span{1}, dim(V_k)<=n, and
V_(k+1)=V_k+sum_(a,o) T_(a,o)V_k. If V_(k+1)=V_k, V_k is shift-invariant
and no longer word adds a function. Otherwise dimension rises by at least
one. Starting at dimension one, by k=n-1 the sequence has stabilized or
reached dimension n. Hence equality on all words of length <=n-1 implies
equality on every future word. This argument does not assume a hidden-state
table, but it does assume the finite predictive-state bound n.

The number of candidate cylinder words up to that horizon is

    M_n = sum_(k=0)^(n-1) |A x O|^k,

which is exponential in n for a nontrivial alphabet. Since a complete family
separates n states, a simple partition-refinement search can select at most
n-1 tests: each selected test must split a current block, and there can be
at most n-1 strict refinements. With a supplied list of n state
representatives and an exact feature-value oracle, evaluating a pool of M
tests costs nM scalar queries (or n times the sum of the individual test
execution costs); comparing all state signatures costs O(nM) exact value
comparisons, or O(n^2 M) if pairwise separation witnesses are explicitly
listed. The selected feature table and its algebra basis require exact rank
and coordinate calculations. The interpolation proof constructs n state
indicators from at most n(n-1) pair-separator factors and O(n^2) pointwise
products. If features or coefficients have finite encodings, their bit
growth must also be charged; n alone gives no height bound. A pool that is
incomplete can fail even when some unlisted test separates the states.
Starting from an exact n-entry value table, a simple repeated pairwise-
product closure algorithm adds at most n independent basis functions; its
naive worst-case cost is O(n^4) field operations after the initial features
are reduced. This is an arithmetic bound, not a bit bound.

To verify update closure for a d-element basis of A_D, form the n-by-d table
F[p,j]=g_j(p) and, for each gamma=(a,o), the table
G_gamma[p,j]=(T_gamma g_j)(p). Check exactly that every column of G_gamma
lies in col(F), then solve F C_gamma=G_gamma. This takes |A x O| n d
shift-value evaluations plus O(|A x O| n d^2) field operations by direct
least-coordinate solves (O(|A x O| n^3) when d=n). If the transition/query
service does not return T_gamma values, obtaining them from trajectories or
chosen-prefix queries is an additional acquisition cost. Exact closure
verification certifies the supplied finite table only; it does not show the
observable bank was found cheaply.

With bounded noisy expectation queries, exact identifiability becomes a
margin statement. Suppose the n state representatives can be reset to,
each q in the M-test pool returns an independent [0,1]-valued sample, and

    min_(p != p') max_(q in D) |f_q(p)-f_q(p')| >= delta > 0.

Estimating every state/test mean to delta/8 with failure probability at
most rho/(nM), using

    N >= (32/delta^2) ln(2 n M/rho)

samples per cell, preserves all equalities versus separations with the
threshold delta/2, with total n M N samples. This assumes direct reset to
each representative; if a representative history occurs with probability
pi, reaching it from ordinary streams adds an inverse-pi factor. Stable
operator recovery also needs a conditioning margin for the chosen feature
matrix and a residual margin for closure. No uniform finite-sample
certificate follows without such gaps. Pairwise separation alone does not
bound the smallest singular value of a selected basis.

An exact finite-bank alias family, including adaptive expectation queries
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix a query budget M and K=M+2 distinct binary futures
x_j=0^j 1 0^infinity, j=0,...,K-1. Each is generated by a finite countdown
state, and all K share one countdown chain. Let mu_0 be uniform on these K
futures. A bounded future test q has expectation E_mu[q]=sum_j mu(j)q(x_j).

For any M tests q_1,...,q_M, the (M+1)-by-K matrix whose jth column is

    v_j=(1,q_1(x_j),...,q_M(x_j))^T

has a nonzero null vector c because K=M+2. Its first row gives sum_j c_j=0,
so c has both positive and negative entries. For sufficiently small
epsilon>0, mu_+=mu_0+epsilon c and mu_-=mu_0-epsilon c are distinct
probability distributions. For every queried test,

    E_mu+ q_i = E_mu0 q_i = E_mu- q_i,

while mu_+ != mu_- as future trajectory laws. Therefore the two
context-conditioned predictive states are distinct and exactly aliased by
the entire bank. A finite-state process realizing them emits a context
marker + or - and then starts the shared countdown chain with distribution
mu_+ or mu_-; its hidden-state count is O(M), and its output alphabet is
finite. The two post-marker histories have the same answers to the M tests
but different future laws.

This also defeats any deterministic adaptive learner limited to M scalar
exact conditional-expectation answers (a vector-valued answer is charged
once per scalar coordinate), including queries at named histories. Run
the learner against a reference process that uses mu_0 after either marker.
The transcript fixes each test q_i and queried history h_i. If the query is
conditional on h_i, let m_i be its reference answer and let

    g_i(j) = Pr(h_i | x_j) ( E[q_i | h_i,x_j] - m_i ).

For the deterministic pulse paths, this is zero when x_j does not realize
h_i and otherwise is the centered test value on its remaining suffix. A
query at the initial history uses the row
q_i(+x_j)-q_i(-x_j): the two contexts each have prior 1/2 and receive
opposite perturbations, so this orthogonality preserves the root mean.
For a conditional query, the equation c dot g_i=0 makes its perturbed
numerator minus m_i times its perturbed denominator remain zero. Add these
at most M rows to the normalization row. Since K=M+2, a nonzero c
annihilates all rows. For small epsilon,
mu_+=mu_0+epsilon*c and mu_-=mu_0-epsilon*c remain positive. The centered
equations preserve every queried conditional expectation under the
alternative process; its positive history probabilities remain nonzero.
Thus the adaptive learner follows the same transcript while the two
post-marker predictive states remain distinct. This is a per-budget
adversary: it does not say that one process aliases every possible bounded
test, and it does not cover raw trajectory access, hidden-state queries,
or tests whose full distribution (rather than expectation) is returned.
This is an information-theoretic construction over real stochastic
probabilities. For arbitrary real-valued tests, the null vector and hence
the transition weights need not have bounded finite encodings; no
bit-complexity lower bound is claimed. If the tests take rational values on
the selected paths, rational nullspace arithmetic gives rational mixtures,
with coefficient bit growth charged separately. The adaptive transcript
argument is stated for deterministic query rules; a randomized worst-case
risk bound would need a distributional argument.

Consequently, there is no worst-case finite query bound for deterministic
exact expectation-query identification over unrestricted finite-state
predictive processes when the state bound, complete candidate horizon, or
an equivalent counterexample/closure certificate is absent. No single
finite nonadaptive bank works uniformly either. Each fixed process in the
family still has finite separating tests. With n supplied, the n-1
cylinder horizon above is a finite completeness witness; without n, any
finite transcript can be matched by a larger finite realization. This is an exact expectation alias,
distinct from the quantitative parity-clock near-alias in
`outputs/research/acquisition_learning/leaves/adaptive_observable_search.txt`
and the passive horizon cutoff in
`outputs/research/acquisition_learning/leaves/finite_horizon_aliasing.txt`.

Supplied versus acquired
-----------------------
The theorem assumes the finite reachable predictive-state set and action/
observation semantics. To execute the exact search, one also needs access to
state representatives (or histories reaching all states), a complete
candidate family or a supplied state/horizon bound, and exact values or
chosen-prefix reset access for feature and shift evaluations. The learner
must acquire which tests split which pairs, a basis/feature table, the
update matrices, reachability, and any separation/conditioning margins.
If the state bound is unknown, a finite basis fit is not a stopping
certificate: unseen future tests may split a currently merged pair. If
queries are sampled, rare-history reachability, per-test runtime, arithmetic
precision, and matrix conditioning must all be charged.

The algebraic conclusion is representational, not a general learning
algorithm. It does not turn a finite hidden-state promise into an efficient
teacher-free learner, does not identify physical hidden labels up to more
than predictive equivalence, and does not imply stability from arbitrarily
small noisy margins.

  passive observations from adaptive matrix-output queries and records that
  query excitation/response size and exact-mass services remain charged. It

* Carlyle and Paz, “Realizations by stochastic finite automata,” JCSS 5(1),
  26–40 (1971), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3.
  Their primary theorem relates finite-rank string functions to finite
  numerical-output/sequential realizations. The linear-space clause above
  is the standard weighted-realization context, not a new claim of priority.
* Jaeger, “Observable operator models for discrete stochastic time series,”
  Neural Computation 12(6), 1371–1398 (2000), author PDF:
  https://www.ai.rug.nl/minds/uploads/oom_neco00.pdf . Sections 4–5 build
  predictor functions and linear observable updates; section 9 requires a
  model dimension and characteristic/indicative events and treats their
  selection as a separate heuristic stage. This supports the distinction
  between closure/realization and acquiring a useful feature bank.
