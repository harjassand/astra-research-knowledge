# Cycle 3 — hidden epigenetic-memory witness beyond passive CLE moments

**Status.** This is an exact finite-state controlled jump-model result plus a synthetic numerical check. It establishes no biological mechanism, cell intervention efficacy, clock validity, or irreversibility. The purpose is to test whether equal calibrated chemical-Langevin fields can still hide a finite memory whose response depends on pulse context and order.

## Result in one paragraph

There is a ten-state controlled reaction model, $S=(X,M)\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\times\{0,1\}$, and a pair of mechanisms $H_+,H_-$ such that their full-state CLE drift $b(s,u)=N a(s,u)$ and covariance $A(s,u)=N\operatorname{diag}(a(s,u))N^T$ agree **exactly at every state and for every allowed action**, where $N$ is the stoichiometric matrix. The hypotheses also have identical complete passive path laws and identical single-pulse laws when baseline support is restricted to $X=0$ (the hidden memory bit may be unknown). They differ only in a hidden-memory-conditioned reaction-channel imbalance with zero zeroth, first, and second jump moments. A four-arm, matched-pulse difference-in-differences reads that imbalance only after prime-then-challenge. Under a calibrated symmetric reporter, its baseline-standardized mean is $q_0\sigma\,10\kappa r(1-e^{-\Lambda\tau_B})\epsilon/\Lambda$, where $q_0=\Pr(M_0=0\mid X_0=0)$. Unknown rates, reporter error, initial-state mix, finite sample size, and loss of reporter contrast all have explicit gates below.

## Exact model and state/action interface

Write reaction increments in species order $(X,M)$, with `X` a finite molecular readout taking values 0 through 4 and `M` a hidden binary memory mark. It is an operational finite-state representation; the labels do not mean chronological age, epigenetic damage, or a validated cell state. The controls are passive $0$, a memory-setting prime $P$, a matched neutral load $L$, and a challenge $B$. A pulse selects its listed reaction channel during its exposure; passive channels may be applied between pulses. This action-conditioned generator is part of the model interface.

Use four challenge increments $v=(-2,-1,+1,+2)$ in `X`, and the contrast vector $h=(1,-2,+2,-1)$. The `M` coordinate is unchanged by $B$. At $X=2,M=0$, $B$ has arbitrary positive reference rates $c_v$. At $X=2,M=1$, the two candidate mechanisms have rates

\[
 a_v^{(\sigma)}=c_v+\sigma\epsilon h_v,\qquad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\},
\]

and every other $B$ row is common. Require $c_v>0$ and $0<\epsilon<\min(c_{-2},c_{+2},c_{-1}/2,c_{+1}/2)$. The matched $P,L$ channels are $(0,0)\to(2,1)$ and $(0,0)\to(2,0)$, both at the same unknown rate $a>0$. Common passive channels may include $X\to X+1$ at an unknown rate \(\lambda\) when $X<4$, $X\to X-1$ at rate \(\mu X\) when $X>0$, and $M=1\to0$ at rate \(\delta\). These parameters are nuisance parameters shared by the two hypotheses.

The moment-null identity is exact:

\[
\sum h_v=0,\qquad \sum h_v v=0,\qquad \sum h_v v^2=0,
\qquad \sum h_v v^3=-12.
\]

Consequently, at every full state $s=(x,m)$ and each action $u\in\{0,P,L,B\}$,

\[
b_+(s,u)=b_-(s,u),\qquad A_+(s,u)=A_-(s,u).
\]

For the only differing row $((x,m),u)=((2,1),B)$, the generator difference is $2\epsilon h_v$ on increments $(v,0)$. Its full-state drift difference is $2\epsilon\sum_v h_v(v,0)=0$, its covariance difference is $2\epsilon\sum_v h_v(v,0)(v,0)^T=0$, and every unchanged row is immediate. The third jump-moment tensor differs in the $XXX$ component by $-24\epsilon$. Thus the exact distinction is outside the CLE pair, not outside the full jump law.

Let $r=1-e^{-a\tau_P}$, $\Lambda=\sum_v c_v$, and $s_B=1-e^{-\Lambda\tau_B}$. Since \(\sum h_v=0\), the challenge exit rate is also \(\Lambda\) in the primed mode. Take a common but initially unknown distribution supported on $\{(0,0),(0,1)\}$ and write $q_0=\Pr(S_0=(0,0))$. Thus $X_0=0$ is reporter-standardized, while the memory bit is not assumed known. The arms use contiguous $P$ then $B$ pulses (no passive interval); $P$-only, $L$-only, and $LB$ are matched controls. Every isolated pulse and the passive path have identical laws across hypotheses on this initial support. For the latent score $h_X=(1,-2,0,2,-1)$,

\[
D_\sigma:=
[E(h_X(X_T)\mid PB)-E(h_X(X_T)\mid P)]
-[E(h_X(X_T)\mid LB)-E(h_X(X_T)\mid L)]
=q_0\sigma\frac{10r s_B\epsilon}{\Lambda}.
\]

For the derivation, a successful first pulse occurs with probability $q_0r$. The neutral-load challenge contributes $s_B(c\cdot h_X)/\Lambda$ to the score change. The primed challenge contributes $s_B(c\cdot h_X+\sigma\epsilon\sum_v h_v^2)/\Lambda$. Since $\sum_v h_v^2=10$, subtraction cancels the unknown common directional term and yields the displayed expression. The symmetric reporter contracts this zero-sum score by $\kappa$.

The pulse order is necessary on this baseline support: $B\to P$ has identical endpoint $X$ laws under $H_+$ and $H_-$ because $B$ is inactive at $X=0$, whereas $P\to B$ has latent endpoint total-variation distance $6q_0r s_B|\epsilon|/\Lambda$ between the hypotheses. Under the symmetric reporter this is multiplied by $\kappa$. The run file checks both the order-swap zero and the predicted $P\to B$ distance at one parameter point.

Here the common unknown directional asymmetry \(\sum c_vh_v\) cancels exactly between the primed and neutral-load arms. This matters: an unpaired endpoint difference could mistake an unknown baseline skew in $B$ for memory. With the five-category reporter $C_\eta(y\mid x)=1-\eta$ for $y=x$ and $\eta/4$ otherwise, $h_X$ is an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\kappa=1-5\eta/4$; for $\eta<0.8$, the observed score contrast is $q_0\sigma 10\kappa r s_B\epsilon/\Lambda$. A known full-rank $X$-channel can instead be inverted in the score; an unknown channel needs calibration.

The exact endpoint laws and all generators are in [cycle3_model.py](cycle3_model.py); its run record is [CYCLE3_RUN.json](CYCLE3_RUN.json). Algebra above proves the identity for all admitted rates. The code uses uniformization to check one finite parameter point, and reports maximum moment-field discrepancies at floating-point roundoff; this is not a general proof or biological validation.

## Falsifiable prediction and finite-budget experiment rule

Randomize independent lineages with reporter-standardized $X_0=0$ across the four arms and match $P$ and $L$ dose/duration. Read $X$ through a calibrated five-category reporter after each pulse; do not assume this reporter observes $M$. A separate, preferably sacrificial, baseline memory assay or externally validated preparation bound must certify $q_0>0$; without it the memory mass is not identified from $X$-only no-pulse data. For the four-arm contrast,

\[
\widehat D=(\bar g_{PB}-\bar g_P)-(\bar g_{LB}-\bar g_L),
\quad E_\sigma\widehat D=q_0\sigma d,
\quad d=10\kappa r\epsilon(1-e^{-\Lambda\tau_B})/\Lambda,
\]

where $g=h_X(Y_X)$ and $d=10\kappa r\epsilon(1-e^{-\Lambda\tau_B})/\Lambda$. Passive $b,A$, the no-pulse output law, and the isolated-pulse output laws must match across mechanisms within preregistered equivalence margins. The mechanistic-pair candidate is falsified if the calibrated (PB-P) and (LB-L) effects do not differ in the predicted signed direction at a resolvable effect size, if $P/L$ completion rates are not matched, if reference/primed challenge rates fail the stated moment constraints, or if actionwise CLE fields are not equivalent. A significant clock-score shift alone is not this test. The closed-form equality presumes baseline support on $X_0=0$ and contiguous pulses; outside that interface use a nuisance-profiled forward likelihood, and return `UNKNOWN` if a positive lower bound on baseline primed mass cannot be certified.

Assume four independent founder groups of $n$ each and $g\in[-2,2]$. Hoeffding's inequality gives the conservative fixed-sample bound

\[
\Pr_\sigma(\sigma\widehat D\le0)\le e^{-nd_{\min}^2/32},\qquad
n\ge 32\log(1/\alpha_{test})/d_{\min}^2.
\]

If calibration confidence sets imply $a\ge a_0>0$, \(\Lambda\le\Lambda_1\), \(|\epsilon|\ge\epsilon_0>0\), and \(\eta\le\eta_0<0.8\), then

\[
d_{\min}=10q_{0,\min}(1-5\eta_0/4)(1-e^{-a_0\tau_P})\epsilon_0
\frac{1-e^{-\Lambda_1\tau_B}}{\Lambda_1}.
\]

The ratio term is decreasing in $\Lambda>0$, so this is a valid lower bound. Rates, reporter contrast, or baseline memory occupancy allowed to approach zero remove any uniform finite-sample guarantee. The run uses $q_0=1$, $a=3,\tau_P=.2,\Lambda=4,\tau_B=.25,\epsilon=.1,\eta=.2$, giving $|E\widehat D|\approx0.05348$ and a sufficient **33,523 independent founders per arm** at test-only $\alpha=.05$. With $q_{0,\min}=0.5$ and other values unchanged, this conservative count quadruples to 134,092 per arm, before reporter, initial-state, rate, passive-CLE, donor, and viability controls. This is not a power estimate or dollar budget.

Unknown quantities are explicit acquisition costs, not free oracle data:

- The $X$ reporter is a calibrated $5\times5$ channel $C_X$; it need not observe $M$, but its calibrated response must preserve the score contrast with known orientation and positive lower bound (for the symmetric channel, $\kappa=1-5\eta/4>0$). It does not identify the full 10-state initial distribution. Restrict/standardize baseline founders to $X_0=0$, and treat $q_0=\Pr(M_0=0\mid X_0=0)$ as a nuisance parameter. To certify $q_0\ge q_{0,\min}$, use a separate memory-specific calibration on independent baseline material or a validated preparation protocol. For a binary symmetric memory assay with known error $\zeta<1/2$, Hoeffding gives $|\hat q_0-q_0|\le\sqrt{\log(2/\alpha_0)/(2n_0)}/(1-2\zeta)$. Select $n_0$ so this radius is at most $q_{0,\min}/2$, then accept the lower bound only if the observed lower confidence endpoint is at least $q_{0,\min}$; this radius target alone does not guarantee a passing calibration. Calibrating $\zeta$ costs independent known-state controls. If no positive lower bound on $q_0$ is supportable, return `UNKNOWN`.
- Estimate $a$ and the matched $P/L$ success probability from randomized pulse-only controls; do not assume equal completion after seeing outcomes. Estimate each unknown reference rate $c_v$ and \(\Lambda\) using the $L\to B$ state-standardized reference arm. Estimate common \(\lambda,\mu,\delta\) and every actionwise $b,A$ using state-resolved passive/action-specific transition data. Use simultaneous confidence sets; if these sets overlap models with \(\epsilon=0\), unbounded rate ratios, or a singular emission channel, report `UNKNOWN`.
- The confirmation budget is $4n$ independent founders for the four contrast arms plus $N_{q_0}+N_{C_X}+N_{a,c}+N_{CLE}$ calibration founders/exposures. There are $2n$ challenge exposures and $2n$ first-pulse exposures in the four balanced arms. Cell-level repeated reads within a founder do not count as independent founders; donor/organoid clustering increases the required independent-unit count. Calibration sample counts must be added to, not hidden inside, the displayed test bound.

For reporter calibration, obtain $m_C$ reads per known $X$ standard across the five categories. A union bound gives entrywise error $e_C=\sqrt{\log(2K^2/\alpha_C)/(2m_C)}$, $K=5$, and hence $\|\hat C_X-C_X\|_2\le K e_C$; if the true minimum singular value is at least $c_C$, $K e_C\le c_C/2$ preserves a certified invertibility margin. The symmetric-channel case can instead bound $\eta$ directly and require its upper confidence endpoint below $0.8$. For a general channel, propagate the entire calibrated confidence set through the score contrast and require known orientation. Pulse completion probabilities $r$ and $s_B$ are estimated from state-standardized P/L-only and (L$\to$B) controls; for $n_r$ controls, a two-sided Hoeffding radius is $\sqrt{\log(2/\alpha_r)/(2n_r)}$. Directional $c_v$ are multinomial/competing-risk parameters; estimate them from the reference arm or event-resolved waiting times. The sample bound uses lower confidence bounds on $q_0,r,\kappa,|\epsilon|$ and an upper bound on $\Lambda$, not point estimates. Passive $\lambda,\mu,\delta$ require their own state-resolved event exposures; no clock-score dataset supplies these unknowns for free.
An adaptive acquisition rule can operate on the finite-state belief $b_t(s)=P(S_t=s\mid y_{0:t},u_{0:t-1})$, initialized as a confidence set over unknown $q_0$. For each candidate next word $w$, compute its predicted reporter law $p_{\sigma,w}(y\mid b,\theta,C_X)$ by the finite-state forward recursion. Choose the next pulse or pulse word maximizing the lower confidence bound on Chernoff information per total assay/pulse cost,

\[
\frac{1}{c(w)}\inf_{\theta_+\in\Theta_+,\theta_-\in\Theta_-}
\left[-\min_{0\le t\le1}\log\sum_y
p_{+,w}(y)^t p_{-,w}(y)^{1-t}\right],
\]

subject to prespecified viability/identity constraints. The one-step discrimination after $B$ is zero unless $b_t(2,1)>0$; with the symmetric $X$ reporter, the endpoint total-variation gap is exactly $6|\epsilon|b_t(2,1)s_B\kappa/\Lambda$. Thus the policy primes only when posterior mass on $(0,0)$ makes $P\to B$ informative, then applies $B$ only when a calibrated lower bound on $b_t(2,1)$ clears an information-per-pulse threshold. Choose the policy on a pilot cohort, freeze it, and evaluate it on independent confirmation founders. An equally informed fixed (PB/LB) design remains the main baseline; no superiority claim is made without equal-cost comparison.

## No-go gates and interpretation boundary

1. **Moment-only ceiling.** If the data interface returns only $b(s,u),A(s,u)$, $H_+$ and $H_-$ are exactly observationally equivalent at that interface for every state and allowed action. No estimator or adaptive controller using only those fields can separate them. A jump/event or sufficiently resolved reporter output is necessary.
2. **Emission no-go.** If the calibrated channel erases the $h$-direction (for the symmetric example, \(\eta=.8\) makes it rank deficient), the endpoint contrast vanishes. With an unrestricted uncalibrated reporter, the sign and magnitude of the score contrast can be confounded with the emission map; no mechanistic-label claim follows. Calibration must preserve the contrast orientation and provide a positive signal lower bound.
3. **Reachability/rate no-go.** If $q_0=0$, no reachable/selected mass can be driven into the primed row, $a=0$, $\Lambda=0$, or $\epsilon\to0$, the finite-sample separation tends to zero. Without positive lower bounds on these quantities, no uniform finite observation budget suffices.
4. **Age/damage no-go.** This finite-state witness demonstrates hidden memory distinguishable by a controlled word; it does not establish that a latent coordinate is aging, damage, or a rejuvenatable substrate. Even a washout fit with positive decay over a finite window cannot prove exact irreversible versus arbitrarily slow reversible dynamics. A biological interpretation needs independently calibrated clock modalities, a direct lesion/function endpoint, lineage tracking, identity monitoring, and a preregistered minimum recovery rate/resolution horizon.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

The mathematical ingredients are established. Gassiat, Cleynen & Robin show stationary finite-state nonparametric HMM identifiability from three consecutive observations under full-rank latent transition and linearly independent emissions, up to state labels; those assumptions do not directly certify this controlled, action-conditioned design. Thorbergsson & Hooker already formulate Fisher-information-maximizing experimental design for POMDPs with unknown transitions and online policy updates. The contribution here is the exact small reaction-network witness and the derived order-specific contrast under the exact full-state CLE equality, not a new general HMM identification or active-design theorem.

The biology papers motivate keeping clocks and latent memory separate. Horvath's DNAm clock is an age predictor rather than a direct damage assay. Ying et al. introduce causality-enriched DamAge/AdaptAge scores and report intervention sensitivity; that construction does not make the score a direct lesion observation. Xiao et al.'s EpiTrace uses clock-like accessibility loci at single-cell resolution, illustrating a distinct modality/time scale. Tanigawa & Iwaki's September 2026 v2 mortality/reprogramming analysis is preprint-level and, as reconstructed in the preserved Cycle 2 audit, does not fit this controlled finite-state pulse model or identify an epigenetic memory coordinate. The Cycle 2 audit, rather than this Cycle 3 model, carries its dataset/sample/interval details.

## Evidence status and next gate

- **Exact conditional model theorem:** local $b,A$ identity and the order-contrast formula, proved above from explicit reaction propensities under stated action/interface assumptions.
- **Finite diagnostic:** [cycle3_model.py](cycle3_model.py) and [CYCLE3_RUN.json](CYCLE3_RUN.json) verify a numeric point by CTMC uniformization; finite check only.
- **Source-reported:** clock/reprogramming observations in the cited articles/preprint; no raw data were reanalysed in this cycle.
- **Unresolved:** external proof review/novelty; whether an assay can supply a calibrated memory-specific initial-state bound and a reporter channel preserving the response contrast; positive lower bounds on reachable occupancy, pulse success, channel separation and minimum mechanism effect; equal-cost adaptive-versus-fixed comparison; biological model fit.

The immediate next test is not to fit clock age as latent damage. It is to collect state-conditioned reaction/event data under randomized matched $P,L,B$ pulse words and controls, with a calibrated $X$ reporter, an independent bound on baseline $q_0$, and held-out founders, then check both the CLE-equivalence margins and predicted order contrast. If only clock-score summaries are available, this witness is not identified.
