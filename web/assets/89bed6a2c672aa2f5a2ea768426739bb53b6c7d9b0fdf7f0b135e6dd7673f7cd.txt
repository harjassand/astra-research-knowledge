1. COMPLETE (1+sqrt(2)) IS ESTABLISHED PRIOR ART (2017).
Michel Crouzeix and César Palencia, The Numerical Range is a (1+sqrt(2))-Spectral Set, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 38(2), 649–655 (2017), DOI 10.1137/17M1116672.
Primary URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00668 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.00668 ; https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/michel.crouzeix/publis/CrouzeixPalencia2017.pdf
Locations: published §1, equation (3), and Theorem 3.1. The arXiv version calls the main statement Theorem 6 (PDF pp.5–6), with the complete-version assertion immediately after its proof; introductory PDF pp.1–2 explicitly covers matrix-valued mappings without changing the constant.
Assumptions: A is a bounded linear operator on a complex Hilbert space; initially a smooth bounded convex domain Ω strictly contains closure W(A), and f belongs to A(Ω) (holomorphic inside, continuous on closure). Approximation of closure W(A) gives the numerical-range spectral-set statement. For every finite matrix-valued rational function F with poles off closure W(A), ||F(A)|| <= (1+sqrt(2)) sup_{z in closure W(A)} ||F(z)||. Polynomial and finite matrix-valued functions holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closure are included. This is already a COMPLETE bound, not only scalar.

2. CONSTANT 2 SHARPNESS IS CLASSICAL (2007); DISK UPPER BOUND ALSO CLASSICAL.
Michel Crouzeix, Numerical range and functional calculus in Hilbert space, J. Funct. Anal. 244(2), 668–690 (15 March 2007), DOI 10.1016/j.jfa.2006.10.013.
Primary URLs: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S002212360600440X ; https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/michel.crouzeix/publis/Nacta3.pdf
Location: opening discussion after Theorem 1 and equation (3) explicitly states the lower bound is attained by p(z)=z and A=[[0,2],[0,0]], whose numerical range is the unit disk. Thus ||p(A)||=2 while sup_{W(A)} |p|=1. This forces universal constant >=2, also in the complete problem since scalar level is included. Independent elementary calculation: x*Ax=2 conj(x1)x2; its modulus ranges up to 1 for ||x||=1, with arbitrary phase.
Access note: the author's PDF is search-indexed with this explicit passage but direct fetch currently returns 403. The primary author slide set https://sites.math.washington.edu/~greenbau/PREPRINTS/iran.pdf , PDF p.8, also gives the scaled example A=[[0,1],[0,0]], disk radius 1/2, norm 1.

Kenneth R. Davidson, Vern I. Paulsen, Hugo J. Woerdeman, Complete spectral sets and numerical range, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 146 (2018), 1189–1195, DOI 10.1090/PROC/13801; preprint submitted 17 Dec 2016.
Primary URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05683 ; https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~krdavids/Preprints/DPWnum_range.pdf

PDF p.4 states w(T)<=1 implies the disk is a complete 2-spectral set, via Berger dilation and Okubo–Ando similarity; pp.6–7 give Corollaries 3.2–3.6. 5. LOAD-BEARING PRIOR OPEN QUESTION.
Thomas Ransford and Felix L. Schwenninger, Remarks on the Crouzeix–Palencia proof that the numerical range is a (1+sqrt(2))-spectral set, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 39(1), 342–345 (2018), DOI 10.1137/17M1143757.
Primary URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08633 ; https://arxiv.org/html/1708.08633
Locations: Lemma 1.1; §3 sharpness; §4 Question 4.1. Merely knowing that each f admits a norm-contracting g with ||f(T)+g(T)^*||<=2||f|| cannot improve 1+sqrt(2). Their counterexample has disconnected Ω and is not a numerical-range counterexample. Question 4.1 adds a unital antilinear contraction α implementing g; they state that proving bound 2 under those stronger assumptions would prove Crouzeix. This identifies precisely where a claimed direct proof must supply new information.
