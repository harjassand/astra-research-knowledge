# T26 — Exact circuit profiles and the failure of sign-only grouping

Date: 2026-10-09  
Status: **Internally derived finite obstruction and exact profile representation; no polynomial-size or efficiently constructible general cover is proved.** This work contributes a new exact obstruction to a natural coarse grouping rule, then repairs the representation by retaining two scale-covariant profile coordinates. It does not solve the arbitrary-matrix strongly polynomial LP problem.

## Target

For the bounded, feasible LP regime used by the T15 circuit-cover route, seek a scale-free grouping of cost-nondecreasing circuits that yields a small enough per-coordinate cover for a polynomial SLLS iteration bound. The grouping must preserve the objective-gap curves and exact optimum, and the number of groups, arithmetic operations, and bit costs must not depend on coefficient magnitude or circuit imbalance. This note tests whether objective-improvement sign plus target-coordinate sign is a sufficient group key, and gives the exact extra data required for uniform curve domination.

## A two-circuit obstruction to sign-only grouping

Use the normalized rank-two T15 matrix with epsilon = 2^-L, L >= 3,

    a1=(1,0),  a2=(0,1),
    a3=(1/2,1/2),
    a4=(1/(2+epsilon),(1+epsilon)/(2+epsilon)).

Let b=a3, x*=e3, M=epsilon^-2=2^(2L), and c=(1,M,0,1). Since c >= 0 coordinatewise, x* is feasible with objective zero and is an exact optimum.

Two feasible tangent circuits are

    h=(epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)], 0, -1, (2+epsilon)/[2(1+epsilon)]),
    q=(1/2,1/2,-1,0).

Direct substitution gives A h=A q=0. Each support has size three and every pair of its columns is independent, so both dependencies are support-minimal circuits. Both have finite blocking step tau=1 at x*, since their only negative coordinate is coordinate 3, where x*_3=1. They are also the only circuits giving a positive-step increase in coordinate 1 from x*: the other coordinates start at zero, so an improving direction must be nonnegative there and negative in coordinate 3; a support-minimal dependence containing coordinates 1 and 3 has third index 2 or 4, yielding q or h. Their reduced costs are

    c^T h = 1,
    c^T q = (M+1)/2.

For coordinate i=1, the exact circuit-augmentation gains at objective gap g >= 0 are therefore

    Delta_h(g) = min(sigma_h g, H_h),
    Delta_q(g) = min(sigma_q g, H_q),

where

    sigma_h = h_1/(c^T h) = epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)],
    H_h     = h_1 tau_h     = epsilon/[2(1+epsilon)],
    sigma_q = q_1/(c^T q) = 1/(M+1),
    H_q     = q_1 tau_q     = 1/2.

Thus both circuits have the same coarse signature

    (sign(h_1), sign(c^T h)) = (positive, positive)
    (sign(q_1), sign(c^T q)) = (positive, positive),

while their exact reduced-cost-normalized slopes differ by an exponential factor and their feasible heights differ in the opposite direction. The profile ratios are

    sigma_q/sigma_h = 2 epsilon (1+epsilon)/(1+epsilon^2),
    H_h/H_q = epsilon/(1+epsilon).

For epsilon <= 1/8, both ratios are < 1/2. Consequently q does not 1/2-dominate h (its slope is too small), and h does not 1/2-dominate q (its height is too small). A one-representative group keyed only by positive target gain and positive reduced-cost sign cannot be a 1/2 circuit cover of these two curves. This is a finite exact obstruction to that coarse quotient, not a lower bound on algorithms or on every possible cover.

The source of the lost distinction is concrete: c^T h=1 while c^T q=(epsilon^-2+1)/2, although both reduced costs have the same sign. A sign-only quotient discards the ratio needed to compare coordinate gain per objective gap. Retaining only the best initial slope also fails globally: h has the larger slope, but q has the larger feasible height.

## Exact two-coordinate profile theorem

Let x be feasible, h a kernel circuit with h_i>0 and r_h=c^T h>0, and assume the ray x+t h is feasible for 0 <= t <= tau_h, where 0 < tau_h < infinity and

    tau_h = min_{j:h_j<0} x_j/(-h_j)

is finite. The objective-gap-truncated circuit curve is

    Delta_h^i(g) = h_i min(g/r_h, tau_h)
                   = min(sigma_h^i g, H_h^i),
    sigma_h^i = h_i/r_h,
    H_h^i     = h_i tau_h.

For two such circuits s,t and alpha in (0,1],

    Delta_s^i(g) >= alpha Delta_t^i(g) for every g >= 0

if and only if

    sigma_s^i >= alpha sigma_t^i  and  H_s^i >= alpha H_t^i.

Proof: the two inequalities imply the curve inequality because min is monotone in both arguments. Conversely, divide by g and let g decrease to zero to obtain the slope inequality; then take g to infinity to obtain the height inequality. Zero-step directions contribute no curve improvement and should be discarded; rays with no blocker have infinite height and require separate status/unbounded-face treatment.

Therefore the exact sufficient profile for the T15-style *uniform circuit-curve domination* relation is the pair (sigma_h^i,H_h^i), or equivalently the objective normalizer and the blocking capacity together with h_i. The profile is not reducible to a single cost-sign bit or to an initial-slope ranking. Any exact alpha-cover by actual circuit curves must retain enough representatives to cover this two-coordinate order.

For a finite circuit set, remove every profile weakly dominated at alpha=1 by another profile, keeping one representative from any duplicate class. The surviving Pareto-maximal profiles exactly dominate every original profile, so they give an exact 1-cover and preserve the full maximum curve envelope. One must not instead delete along an alpha<1 chain: successive alpha-dominations multiply their guarantees. This exact frontier can be checked using rational cross-multiplication; it does not assert that the frontier is small or can be found quickly.

## Scale behavior and arithmetic accounting

An invertible row map leaves ker(A), h, and c^T h unchanged, so this profile is row-coordinate invariant. Under a positive variable substitution A'=A D, x'=D^-1 x, c'=D c, the corresponding circuit is h'=D^-1 h and tau'_h=tau_h. For fixed coordinate i,

    sigma'^{i}_h = sigma^{i}_h / D_ii,
    H'^{i}_h     = H^{i}_h / D_ii.

Every profile in coordinate i receives the same positive factor, so alpha-domination comparisons are invariant under positive column/variable rescaling. This is a genuine scale-covariant representation: unlike a maximum-volume chart, it does not change which circuit dominates which after a variable-unit change, and unlike sign-only grouping it retains the reduced-cost magnitude needed for exact curve comparison.

For rational data, circuit coordinates can be represented by minors after clearing denominators, and each profile component is a quotient/product of rational input data and the current feasible point. If the point has polynomial bit length, each individual profile can be stored and compared with polynomial bit length using exact integer arithmetic. This does not discharge the full cost requirement: naively listing all circuits takes up to binomial(n,r+1) candidates, and comparing an exponential-size frontier is exponential in dimensions. A proposed scale-free cover must bound both its cardinality and its construction cost without charging for L as a loop count; polynomial bit length of each stored ratio alone is not a strongly polynomial bound.

## What the representation repairs, and what it does not

The sign-only grouping is repaired by storing the exact two-axis profile antichain. It preserves the objective-gap curve and blocking event, including cases where the most efficient circuit at small g is not the best circuit at large g. The T15 instance verifies why both axes matter: h is the better small-gap choice, while q eventually overtakes it and reaches a much larger coordinate gain.

The repair is only representational. It provides no polynomial bound on the number of incomparable profiles, no strongly polynomial method to find them, no initialization, and no general status certificates. A logarithmic grid in either axis would introduce a number of bins depending on numeric range (hence potentially on the input encoding length); exact rank grouping would still leave as many ranks as there are circuits. No dimension-only cover theorem follows from this construction.

The zero-reduced-cost case also remains separate. If c^T h=0 and h_i>0 is feasible, then x is not coordinatewise maximal on the optimal face. One can select an optimum maximizing coordinate i to exclude such a direction for that coordinate, but doing so for every i may give different reference optima and is not an initialization algorithm. Unblocked rays and unbounded LP statuses require separate handling.

## Relation to the pinned primary result

Dadush, Koh, Natura, Olver, and Végh, “A strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programs with at most two nonzero entries per row or column,” STOC 2024, primary full text at https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/34290/34290.pdf, state in the abstract and §1.4.1 that their strongly polynomial straight-line-complexity bound is proved for minimum-cost generalized flow and the stated two-nonzero reductions, using graph-specific circuit/path structure; §§1.4.2–1.4.3 separately address initialization and Turing bit control. This supports the boundary already used in T15: a profile representation alone is not the graph-specific domination theorem, nor does it extend that theorem to arbitrary matrices. No broader literature claim is made here.

## Claim ledger and next decisive gate

* **Internally derived:** exact formulas for the two circuit curves and profile ratios in the T15 LP; the iff characterization of uniform alpha-domination by the two profile coordinates; row/column scale behavior.
* **Finite exact instance:** for every L >= 3, the sign-only group contains h and q but no single one 1/2-dominates the other.
* **Candidate representation:** exact two-coordinate profile antichain; it preserves curve domination but has no dimension-only size or construction bound.
* **Unresolved:** whether an arbitrary rational matrix has a polynomial-size efficiently constructible cover at an SLLS-sufficient accuracy, or whether a different combinatorial progress invariant bypasses circuit covers. Initialization, feasibility/unboundedness distinction, exact certificates, and total Turing cost remain open within this approach.

The decisive next theorem would need to compress the exact profile antichain (or replace it) using a structural property that does not depend on coefficient range, while still preserving enough of each objective-gap curve to bound SLLS straight-line complexity. Neither the T15 normalization argument nor the representation here supplies that property.
