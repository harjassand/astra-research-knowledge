1. THE GENERAL COEFFICIENT THEOREM
Let V be a finite ground set. Suppose

 h(x)=sum_{U subset V} f(U) x^U

is nonzero, multiaffine, has real coefficients f(U)>=0, vanishes on every odd
U, and has no zero whenever Re(x_i)>0 for every i. Then for any even S,T and
a in D=S symmetric-difference T,

 sqrt(f(S)f(T))
   <= sum_{j in D, j!=a}
        sqrt(f(S symmetric-difference {a,j})
             f(T symmetric-difference {a,j})).                 (E)

Cases with f(S)f(T)=0 are immediate. Otherwise m=|D| is an even positive
integer, m>=2, and both endpoint coefficients are strictly positive.

2. TWIST / COMMON-CORE / ZERO-COEFFICIENT AUDIT
Define the positive reciprocal twist by the FULL set T:

 h_T(x)=x^T h(y), y_i=1/x_i for i in T, y_i=x_i otherwise.

Multiaffinity cancels denominators, and the new coefficient of x^U is
c_U=f(T symmetric-difference U). Since Re(1/x)>0 iff Re(x)>0, and x^T does
not vanish in the right-half-plane domain, h_T is stable there. Its coefficients
remain nonnegative. Since |T| is even, its support remains even.

Now set all variables outside D to zero. Boundary specialization preserves
stability or gives the zero polynomial; the zero alternative is impossible
because its constant coefficient is c_empty=f(T)>0. For completeness, take
all those variables to a positive epsilon, obtaining stable polynomials, and
apply Hurwitz to their locally uniform limit. Denote the resulting polynomial
by

 g(x_D)=sum_{U subset D, |U| even} c_U x^U.

Its full coefficient is c_D=f(T symmetric-difference D)=f(S)>0.

The common occupied core causes no loss here. Original coefficients of g are
f(T symmetric-difference U) evaluated on the FULL ambient V; there is no
replacement by a smaller F, and no attempt to merge determinant orientations.
The core remains encoded in those coefficients. If S and T share hole indices,
twisting the full T BEFORE the outside-zero specialization handles them too;
twisting only T\D would generally acquire the wrong endpoint coefficients.

3. EXACT ODD-DEGREE REDUCTION
For arbitrary weights w_j>0, j in J=D\{a}, set x_a=1 and x_j=w_j z. Define

 p_w(z)=g(1,(w_j z)_{j in J})=sum_{k=0}^{m-1} a_k z^k.

For Re(z)>0 all free variables have positive real part and x_a=1 is interior,
so p_w is nonvanishing in the open right half-plane. It has real nonnegative
coefficients. Its degree is EXACTLY d=m-1, an odd integer: multiaffinity gives
the upper bound, and c_D>0 gives its nonzero leading coefficient. Put
W=product_{j in J}w_j. The four coefficients required are exactly

 a_0=c_empty=f(T),
 a_d=c_D W=f(S)W,
 a_1=sum_{j in J} c_{a,j} w_j,
 a_{d-1}=W sum_{j in J} c_{D\{a,j}}/w_j.                  (1)

Parity supplies the indexing. A z^1 term must contain a and one j, since
the alternative singleton term is odd and absent. A z^(m-2) term must avoid
a and select J\{j}, since including a would give an odd set. For m=2 the
same identities hold: d=1, a_{d-1}=a_0, and the result is an equality.

In original f notation,
c_{a,j}=f(T symmetric-difference {a,j}),
c_{D\{a,j}}=f(S symmetric-difference {a,j}).

4. ELEMENTARY ODD-DEGREE ENDPOINT COEFFICIENT LEMMA
Let p(z)=sum_{k=0}^d a_k z^k be a real polynomial of ODD degree d with a_0,a_d>0
and no zero in Re(z)>0. Then

 a_1 a_{d-1} >= a_0 a_d.                                  (2)

Proof: all roots have nonpositive real parts, and none is zero because a_0>0.
Factor over the reals into negative-real linear roots and conjugate pairs:

 p(z)=a_d product_l(z+r_l) product_k(z^2+2s_k z+t_k),
 r_l>0, s_k>=0, t_k>0.

Odd degree guarantees at least one linear factor. Reading the next-to-constant
and next-to-leading coefficients gives

 a_1/a_0 = sum_l 1/r_l + sum_k 2s_k/t_k,
 a_{d-1}/a_d = sum_l r_l + sum_k 2s_k.

Both sums have only nonnegative terms. Any one linear factor contributes
(1/r_l)*r_l=1 to their product; hence the product is at least one, proving
(2). This directly includes repeated roots and purely imaginary conjugate
roots (s_k=0). Strict left-half-plane stability is NOT assumed, and there is
no appeal to perturbing coefficients into full support.

This is precisely the special coefficient inequality used in Asner's
Hurwitz-minor method, but its full total-nonnegativity theorem is unnecessary.
Even degree cannot replace odd degree: p(z)=1+z^2 has no right-half-plane
zero but a_1^2=0<a_0 a_2=1. Thus the parity hypothesis is load-bearing.

5. WEIGHT OPTIMIZATION AND DEGENERATE PAIR COEFFICIENTS
Substitute (1) into (2), divide by W>0, and use the abbreviations
A_j=c_{a,j}, B_j=c_{D\{a,j}}. For EVERY positive w,

 f(T)f(S) <= (sum_j A_j w_j)(sum_j B_j/w_j).                (3)

The exact infimum of the right-hand side over positive w is

 (sum_j sqrt(A_j B_j))^2.

Cauchy–Schwarz gives the lower bound, with equality at w_j=sqrt(B_j/A_j)
when all A_j,B_j are positive. Zero coefficients do not need artificial
full-support approximation: for A_j>0,B_j=0 choose w_j=epsilon/A_j; for
A_j=0,B_j>0 choose w_j=B_j/epsilon; for both zero choose w_j=1; and use the
positive-positive optimizer on the other indices. Both sums tend to
sum_j sqrt(A_j B_j) as epsilon tends to zero. Taking the infimum in (3) proves
(E), including every zero pattern.

The optimizing weights involve unknown hole partitions but are used only to
prove the inequality. The proof does not ask an algorithm to compute them.
In particular no counting-oracle acquisition cost is being omitted from an
asserted algorithm: no such algorithm is asserted here.

9. INDEPENDENT PICK-ROUTE SIGN / DEGREE / POLE AUDIT
The alternative upper-half-plane route requested by root is also valid.
This section derives the pole fact directly, so the main proof still requires
no imported Pick/Herglotz theorem. Write s_T=(-1)^((n-|T|)/2) and form

 R_T(x)=s_T x^T Q_F(y), y_i=-1/x_i for i in T, y_i=x_i otherwise.

For Im(x_i)>0, Im(-1/x_i)>0. Thus the twist preserves upper-half-plane
stability. For an original even monomial K and new U=K symmetric-difference T,
the sign exponent is

 (n-|K|)/2 + |K intersect T| = (n+|T|-|U|)/2.

Multiplication by s_T makes the new coefficient exactly
(-1)^(|U|/2) c_U, c_U=f(T symmetric-difference U). In particular its constant
is +f(T), regardless of n or |T| mod4. This is the same FULL-T convention
audited in Section2. Zero all coordinates outside D=S symmetric-difference T;
boundary stability holds and f(T)>0 prevents the zero limit.

For j in J=D\{a}, substitute x_j=w_j t with w_j>0, but leave x_a free. The
result is a stable real bivariate polynomial

 q_0(t)+x_a q_1(t),

where q_0 is even and q_1 is odd. With m=|D|, W=product_j w_j,
A=sum_j c_{a,j}w_j, and B=sum_j c_{D\{a,j}}/w_j, its relevant terms are

 q_0(t)=f(T)+...+(-1)^(m/2-1) W B t^(m-2),
 q_1(t)=-A t+...+(-1)^(m/2) f(S) W t^(m-1).

The degree of q_1 is exactly m-1 because f(S)>0. The degree of q_0 is at
most m-2; no assumption B>0 is needed at this step. Differentiation in x_a
preserves upper-half-plane stability, so q_1 is a nonzero real univariate
stable polynomial and cannot vanish in the upper half-plane. Consequently
r(t)=q_0(t)/q_1(t) is analytic there. A root in x_a occurs at -r(t), which
cannot have positive imaginary part; therefore Im(r(t))>=0. Also r is proper
since deg(q_0)<deg(q_1), and r is not constant because q_0(0)=f(T)>0 while
q_1(0)=0.

Elementary pole facts: a proper real rational function analytic in the upper
half-plane with nonnegative imaginary part has only real simple poles and
has the representation

 r(t)=sum_l lambda_l/(rho_l-t), lambda_l>0, rho_l real.

Indeed, a nonreal pole in the lower half-plane would, by real coefficients,
give a conjugate pole in the upper half-plane. At a real pole rho of order
k>=2 with real leading Laurent coefficient C!=0, the imaginary part of
C (t-rho)^(-k) changes sign as t=rho+epsilon exp(i theta), theta in (0,pi).
This contradicts Im(r)>=0 for small epsilon. A simple pole has residue C<0
by evaluation along t=rho+i epsilon; set lambda=-C>0. Properness leaves no
polynomial term after partial fractions. These arguments apply to r after
cancelling every common factor of q_0 and q_1.

Because q_0(0)>0, zero is an uncancelled pole of r. It must be simple, so
A>0 automatically, and its mass is

 lambda_0=f(T)/A.

The coefficient of -1/t at infinity is the sum of all pole masses. The
displayed leading coefficients give

 sum_l lambda_l=B/f(S).

Thus B>0 follows too; if its displayed coefficient were zero, r would decay
faster than 1/t and have total pole mass zero despite lambda_0>0. Since every
pole mass is positive,

 f(T)/A <= B/f(S), or f(S)f(T)<=A B.

This is exactly Section5's weighted inequality, including its leading
constant one. For m=2 the only pole is zero and equality holds. For zero
endpoint coefficients the target inequality is trivial and this division
argument is not used. Zero exchange coefficients are handled by Section5's
explicit positive-weight limits. Common occupied cores and common holes
introduce no extra step beyond the full twist. Both elementary routes
therefore agree on the requested gate, including every degenerate endpoint.
