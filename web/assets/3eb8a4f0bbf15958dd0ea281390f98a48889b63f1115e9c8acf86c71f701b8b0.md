# Cycle 2: near-optimal ℓ2 conditioning forces moment closeness

## Finding

There is an explicit stability law linking the 2026 complex phase-retrieval condition number to the weighted projective fourth moment. Let (d\ge2), let the nonzero sensing rows be (a_j=t_ju_j), (|u_j|=1), set
\[
 S=\sum_jt_j^4,\quad p_j=t_j^4/S,\quad
 F=\sum_jp_j\,\Pi_j^{\otimes2},\quad
 F_0=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym}=\frac1N P_{\rm sym},\quad
 N=\binom{d+1}{2},\quad
 \rho=\left(\frac{\beta_{\Psi_A}^{\ell_2}}2\right)^2-1.
\]
Here (F,F_0) are trace-one operators on (\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb C^d)), and the deviation norm is the ordinary Hilbert–Schmidt/Frobenius norm. Define
\[
 C_d=\frac{2(d+2)(d+3)}{d(d+1)}.
\]
Then the internally derived bound is
\[
 \boxed{\quad \|F-F_0\|_F^2\le C_d\,\rho\max\{1,\rho\}.\quad}
\]
In the near-optimal range (0\le\rho\le1), this is (\|F-F_0\|_F\le\sqrt{C_d\rho}), with (C_d\to2). In the user's parameterization (\beta\le2(1+\varepsilon)), if (0\le\varepsilon) and (2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\le1), replace (\rho) on the right by (2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2). Relative to the Haar tensor norm \(\|F_0\|_F=1/\sqrt N\),
\[
 \frac{\|F-F_0\|_F}{\|F_0\|_F}
 \le \sqrt{(d+2)(d+3)\,\rho\max\{1,\rho\}}.
\]
Thus the absolute trace-one tensor error is dimension-uniform at fixed small (\varepsilon), while the relative Frobenius error has the natural (O(d\sqrt\varepsilon)) factor.

This is a one-way stability theorem, and the converse fails uniformly in dimension. A complete MUB design with one measurement deleted has relative moment error tending to zero but condition number bounded below by 2√2; the exact calculation is given below. No dimension-uniform reverse stability estimate follows from Frobenius moment closeness alone.

## Proof of the stability law

### 1. A dimension-correct upper bound on the quartic maximum

Write (q(x)=\sum_j|a_j^*x|^4\) for unit (x); then (U^2=\max_xq(x)). For any complex two-plane (V), let (b_j=P_Va_j) and (S_V=\sum_j\|b_j\|^4). Restricting the infimum that defines (L_A) to pairs in (V), and then using Peng–Han–Huang's dimension-two inequality, gives
\[
 L_A\le L_{A|V}\le M_{A|V},\qquad M_{A|V}^2\le S_V/12.
\]
For a Haar-random two-plane, 
\[
 \mathbb E_V\|P_Va\|^4
 =\frac{2\cdot3}{d(d+1)}\|a\|^4,
 \qquad \mathbb E_VS_V=\frac{6S}{d(d+1)}.
\]
This is the second moment of a (\operatorname{Beta}(2,d-2)) projection fraction (and remains true at (d=2)). Some (V) therefore has (S_V\le6S/[d(d+1)]), so
\[
 L_A^2\le\frac{S}{2d(d+1)}.
\]
Since (\beta=U/L),
\[
 \max_{\|x\|=1}\frac{q(x)}S
 =\frac{U^2}{S}
 \le \frac{2(1+\rho)}{d(d+1)}
 =q_0(1+\rho),\qquad q_0:=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}.
\]
The Haar mean of (q(x)/S) is exactly (q_0), for every row-direction distribution, because (\mathbb E_x|u^*x|^4=q_0) for each unit (u).

### 2. From one-sided quartic control to an (L^2) estimate

Let (D=F-F_0) and (g(x)=\langle x^{\otimes2},D x^{\otimes2}\rangle=q(x)/S-q_0). Then (\mathbb E_xg=0), (g(x)\le q_0\rho), and (g(x)\ge-q_0). Hence
\[
 \mathbb E|g|=2\mathbb E g_+\le2q_0\rho,
 \|g\|_\infty\le q_0\max\{1,\rho\},
 \mathbb E g^2\le2q_0^2\rho\max\{1,\rho\}.
\]

### 3. Haar fourth-moment contraction

For any traceless Hermitian (D=P_{\rm sym}DP_{\rm sym}), set (D_1=\operatorname{Tr}_2D). Expanding
\[
 \mathbb E_x(|x\rangle\langle x|)^{\otimes4}
 =\frac{1}{d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)}\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}U_\sigma
\]
and grouping the 24 contractions according to whether they preserve or cross the two symmetric pairs gives
\[
 \mathbb E_x\langle x^{\otimes2},D x^{\otimes2}\rangle^2
 =\frac{4\|D\|_F^2+16\|D_1\|_F^2}{d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)}.
\]
The (4(\operatorname{Tr}D)^2) term in the general contraction vanishes here. In particular this is at least (4\|D\|_F^2/[d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)]). Combining with the previous estimate proves
\[
 \|D\|_F^2
 \le \frac{d(d+1)(d+2)(d+3)}4\,2q_0^2\rho\max\{1,\rho\}
 = C_d\rho\max\{1,\rho\}.
\]

The contraction identity was also checked by Monte Carlo on random traceless Hermitian operators in (d=2,3,4); the ratios of empirical to predicted values were 1.0029, 1.0035, and 1.0053. See [stability_sanity.py](stability_sanity.py). This only checks the formula numerically; the proof is the Haar-moment contraction above.

## A converse counterexample: delete one complete-MUB measurement

For every prime-power dimension (d\ge3), take a complete set of (d+1) mutually unbiased bases, with (m_0=d(d+1)) unit vectors (u_j). This is an equal-weight projective 2-design, so its normalized moment is (F_0), its quartic (q(x)=\sum_j|u_j^*x|^4) is identically (2), and its lower constant satisfies (L^2=1/2). Delete one vector (w), leaving (m=m_0-1) unit rows and equal normalized weights (p_j=1/m). The new moment is
\[
 F' = \frac{m_0F_0-\Pi_w^{\otimes2}}{m_0-1},\qquad
 \|F'-F_0\|_F^2=\frac{1-1/N}{(m_0-1)^2}=O(d^{-4}),
 \quad N=\frac{d(d+1)}2.
\]
Thus its *relative* Frobenius error compared with \(\|F_0\|_F=1/\sqrt N\) is \(O(d^{-1})\).

Yet the condition number does not approach 2. For any unit (x\perp w), deleting the row leaves (q'(x)=2), while (q'(z)\le2) for all unit (z); hence \((U')^2=2\). Choose (e\perp w), and (u=(w+e)/\sqrt2), (v=(w-e)/\sqrt2). These are orthogonal unit vectors. The complete-MUB 2-design identity gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{m_0}\left[\operatorname{Re}(u^*\Pi_jv)\right]^2=\frac12,
 \qquad \left[\operatorname{Re}(u^*\Pi_wv)\right]^2=\frac14.
\]
Using this admissible pair in the source's exact formula for (L') yields \((L')^2\le1/4\), so
\[
 \beta' = U'/L'\ge\sqrt8=2\sqrt2.
\]
The remaining measurements still span all Hermitian matrices: (I) is the sum of the projectors in any other basis, so the deleted projector is (I) minus the (d-1) retained projectors in its own basis. Thus the deleted set remains informationally complete and phase retrievable; its (L'>0). Complete MUBs exist in every prime-power dimension, so this is an unbounded family, not only a fixed-dimensional example. This refutes any dimension-uniform converse that would force \(\beta'-2\to0\) from \(\|F'-F_0\|_F/\|F_0\|_F\to0\).

The row count here is \(d(d+1)-1\), so it is not a counterexample to a sharp stability statement conditioned on sparse row count. The separate lower bound below shows that (m=o(d^2)) does force a nonzero gap. A SIC-minus-one variant, when a SIC exists, has the minimal-design row count (d^2-1) and similarly gives \(\|F'-F_0\|_F^2=(1-1/N)/(d^2-1)^2\), \((U')^2=2d/(d+1)\), and \((L')^2\le(d-1)/(4(d+1))\), hence \((\beta')^2\ge8d/(d-1)\).

## Row-count gap from frame-potential and rank bounds

For this normalization the weighted fourth frame potential is
\[
 \operatorname{FP}_4(p,u)=\operatorname{Tr}(F^2)
 =\sum_{i,j}p_ip_j|\langle u_i,u_j\rangle|^4.
\]
Since (F_0=P_{\rm sym}/N), (\operatorname{Tr}(FF_0)=\operatorname{Tr}(F_0^2)=1/N), and therefore
\[
 \|F-F_0\|_F^2=\operatorname{FP}_4-1/N
 \ge \sum_jp_j^2-1/N
 \ge 1/m-1/N.
\]
Combining with the stability law, for (\rho\le1),
\[
 \rho\ge\frac{1}{C_d}\left(\sum_jp_j^2-\frac1N\right)_+
 \ge\frac{1}{C_d}\left(\frac1m-\frac1N\right)_+.
\]
Here (m) is the number of nonzero rows. The first term uses effective support (m_{\rm eff}=1/\sum p_j^2\), so it captures unequal row norms.

A sharper rank bound works for every (m<d^2), including arbitrary row weights. Partially transpose the second factor and set (G=F^\Gamma). Since
\[
 G=\sum_jp_j\Pi_j\otimes\overline{\Pi_j}\succeq0,
 \quad \operatorname{Tr}G=1,\quad \operatorname{rank}G\le m,
\]
and partial transpose preserves the Frobenius norm, it suffices to approximate (R=F_0^\Gamma). Its spectrum is (1/d) once and (c=1/[d(d+1)]) with multiplicity (d^2-1): explicitly (R=(I+|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|)/(d(d+1))) with (|\Omega\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^d e_k\otimes e_k). Among trace-one PSD matrices of rank at most (m), the closest one to (R) keeps its top (m) eigendirections and redistributes the omitted trace uniformly over those retained directions. Writing (r=d^2-m), this gives the exact lower bound
\[
 \|F-F_0\|_F^2=\|G-R\|_F^2\ge r c^2+\frac{(rc)^2}{m}
 =\frac{d^2-m}{m(d+1)^2},\qquad 1\le m<d^2.
\]
The first term is the discarded eigenvalue-square sum; the second is the cost of restoring trace one. This improves the unconstrained Eckart–Young bound by a factor (d^2/m). It is the same partial-transpose/rank mechanism underlying recent stronger Welch bounds, here applied directly to possibly weighted row probabilities.

Thus, for near-optimal ((\rho\le1)),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \rho\ge\frac1{C_d}\max\left\{
   \left(\sum_jp_j^2-\frac1N\right)_+,
   \frac{(d^2-m)_+}{m(d+1)^2}
 \right\}.\quad}
\]
If ((\rho>1)), this displayed lower bound still holds trivially because its right-hand side is below 1. For a requested upper tolerance ((\beta\le2(1+\varepsilon))), the necessary condition is (2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2) at least the right-hand side.
Consequences:

- If (m=o(d^2)), eventually (m<N\), and the frame-potential term gives (\rho\ge(1+o(1))/(2m)). Therefore (\beta-2=2(\sqrt{1+\rho}-1)\gtrsim1/(2m)) and the relative tolerance (\varepsilon=\beta/2-1\gtrsim1/(4m)). The gap is nonzero at every finite (d,m), although it tends to zero if (m\to\infty).
- If (m=\theta d^2) for fixed (0<\theta<1), the rank term gives (\rho\gtrsim(1-\theta)/(2\theta d^2)). This is a dimension-dependent gap, not a constant separation.
- The exact cardinality theorem from Cycle 1 says no (m<d^2) system attains (\rho=0). The quantitative law shows how the gap must scale in the sparse regime; it does not prove this lower bound is sharp.

The frame-potential bound can be stronger for very sparse systems; the trace-constrained rank bound remains informative through (m<d^2). For equal row weights (p_j=1/m), the displayed bounds cross at (m=d(2d+1)/(d+2)); with unequal weights the frame-potential term also depends on the effective support (1/\sum_jp_j^2).

## All-d exact designs and the row-norm map

Jasper and Mixon (2025), *Nearly tight weighted 2-designs in complex projective spaces of every dimension*, prove (n(d)\le d^2+O(d^{1.525})) for every (d). Their convention is
\[
 \sum_k w_k\Pi_k^{\otimes2}=P_{\rm sym},\qquad
 \sum_kw_k=N=d(d+1)/2.
\]
Normalize by (p_k=w_k/N). Then (F=F_0) in our convention. To realize these probabilities as the condition-number weights, choose
\[
 a_k=p_k^{1/4}u_k;
 \qquad \|a_k\|^4=p_k,\quad \sum_k\|a_k\|^4=1.
\]
This fourth-root row-amplitude choice is essential: the nonlinear lower/upper constants square the rank-one row operator, so their moment weights are (\|a_k\|^4), not (\|a_k\|^2). Every such Jasper–Mixon design therefore supplies an exact (\beta=2) intensity matrix in every dimension at (m\le d^2+O(d^{1.525})) rows.

Their Bodmann–Haas construction takes a finite abelian group (G) and a Sidon set (S\subset G),  (|S|=d), and outputs (x_\alpha\in\mathbb C^S) for all characters (\alpha\in\widehat G), plus (e_r) for (r\in S). The paper's weights are
\[
 w_\alpha=\frac{d^2}{2|G|},\qquad w_r=\frac12,
\]
and indeed  \(|G|w_\alpha+dw_r=N\). After normalization,
\[
 p_\alpha=\frac{d}{|G|(d+1)},\quad
 p_r=\frac1{d(d+1)},
 \quad \|a_\alpha\|=\left(\frac{d}{|G|(d+1)}\right)^{1/4},\quad
 \|a_r\|=\left(\frac1{d(d+1)}\right)^{1/4}.
\]
There are  \(m=|G|+d\) rows. With the Erdős–Turán parabola Sidon set in (G=\mathbb F_p^2), (p\ge d), one gets (m=p^2+d); the cited prime-gap bound (p=d+O(d^{0.525})) gives the stated all-d asymptotic. The Jasper–Mixon result is external published/preprint source evidence for this design construction; the conversion to intensity row norms is the direct substitution above.

This materially improves the cycle-1 scope: optimal complex ℓ2 phase-retrieval conditioning is realized in every dimension with near-(d^2) row count, not only prime-power dimensions via complete MUBs or dimensions with a SIC. It still does not settle optimality for every prescribed row count (m), and (d^2+O(d^{1.525})) is not the (d^2) minimum.

## Prior art, novelty, and scope

- The equality (F=F_0) and the fourth-frame-potential identity are standard Welch/design facts. The row-count lower bound (m\ge d^2), and equality iff a SIC in the unweighted tight case, are established projective-design facts; Jasper–Mixon also state the weighted minimum-size result.
- A directly relevant 2026 primary source, Castellano et al., *Stronger Welch Bounds and Optimal Approximate k-Designs* (arXiv:2602.13099v3, 29 Sep 2026), already uses Frobenius distance from the Haar moment as an approximate-design measure: its Theorem 4 gives (epsilon_avg^(k)=binom(d+k-1,k)^(-1)||F_k-rho_k||_2), and it develops strengthened Welch/cardinality bounds. Those results concern fixed-cardinality frame moments (with uniform ensembles in the stated setup), not the phase-retrieval condition number or fourth-power row-norm weights. The weighted identity (||F-F0||_F^2=FP_4-1/N) itself is immediate and classical.
- The remaining candidate bridge is quantitative and model-specific: Peng–Han–Huang’s intensity-map (beta) controls the maximum quartic through their dimension-two inequality, which here forces (F) close in Frobenius norm; frame-potential and rank bounds then give finite-(m) beta gaps. The cited approximate-design literature supplies useful comparators and may yield sharper equal-row cardinality refinements, so this report does not claim the row-count constants are best possible. No direct prior statement of this beta-to-moment implication was located; priority is not established.
- Jasper–Mixon’s 2025 construction gives near-minimal exact weighted designs in every dimension. This removes the earlier MUB-only construction limitation but remains prior design-construction art.
- The exact phase-retrieval paper's broad open wording asks for optimal (A\in\mathbb H^{m\times d}) in general spaces without formalizing (m,d), row constraints, hardware, or a noise model. Jasper–Mixon plus the row-norm map gives an exact optimizer for an all-d sequence of (m=d^2+O(d^{1.525})) instances. It does not close every fixed-(m) design problem.
- (\beta) is a deterministic global ratio for raw intensity coordinates with additive ℓ2 output error. It is invariant under common gain but not under the per-row fourth-root scaling. Jasper–Mixon's unequal row gains are a mathematical realization; this calculation does not show equal photon exposure, Poisson optimality, detector feasibility, or equal calibration burden. Shot noise and exposure require a separate physical design objective.

## Verification and remaining gates

- [stability_sanity.py](stability_sanity.py) checks the Haar fourth-moment contraction for random trace-zero Hermitian operators in dimensions 2, 3, and 4. Monte Carlo ratios were within 0.6% of the exact contraction formula.
- [design_test.py](design_test.py) remains the d=2 exact MUB reconstruction and 2-design check from Cycle 1.
- [mub_deletion_sanity.py](mub_deletion_sanity.py) checks the complete-MUB deletion identities at (d=3), including the moment defect, quartic witness, lower-constant witness, and informational-completeness rank. It does not establish the prime-power family theorem.
- No experiment or runtime comparison was run. The theorem and Jasper–Mixon source do not specify a photon model.
- The main proof-audit risks are the exact Haar contraction coefficient, the reduction from (L_A) to the random two-plane, and the trace-one rank-m approximation calculation after partial transpose. The contraction and an example rank bound have finite checks; the all-d claims still need an independent specialist proof audit.
- The row-count lower bounds do not establish tightness for each (m,d). The complete-MUB deletion family gives a converse counterexample at (m=d(d+1)-1): relative Frobenius error tends to zero while beta is at least 2√2. The sparse-row gap remains a lower bound, not a sharpness result.
