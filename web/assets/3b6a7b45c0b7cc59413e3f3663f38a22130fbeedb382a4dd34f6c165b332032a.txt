1. GNS/KMS metric conversion: PASS

delta=ad(log s), D=exp(delta/2)=L_(s^(1/2))R_(s^(-1/2)). The operator D is

Tr(sigma A^* L(A))=<Y,D^{-1} H D Y>_HS.

Indeed R_s L R_s^{-1}=D^{-1} H D. Taking the real part, and using H=H^*,
gives G=(D^{-1}HD+DHD^{-1})/2. There is no GNS selfadjointness assumption.

3. Fourier inverse and normalization: PASS

In an orthonormal delta eigenbasis, with eigenvalues d_a,
G_ab=cosh((d_a-d_b)/2)H_ab. The exact scalar Fourier identity is

int_R sech(pi t) exp(iut)dt=sech(u/2).

A self-contained proof for u>=0 shifts the contour by i. The integrand
changes by -exp(-u), and its single enclosed pole at i/2 has residue
exp(-u/2)/(pi i). Thus (1+exp(-u))I=2exp(-u/2). The side integrals vanish by
exponential decay. Negative u follows by evenness/conjugation. At zero the
integral has mass one. Finite-dimensional spectral calculus therefore gives

H=int_R sech(pi t) exp(it delta)G exp(-it delta)dt

in operator norm, with no unbounded modular-domain premise.
For every complex Y, put Y_t=s^(-it)Y s^(it). Then

<Y,HY>=(1/2)sum_j int_R sech(pi t)||K_jY_t-Y_tK_j^*||_2^2dt.

For Y=q this is E. At scalar s it reduces to
E=(1/2)sum_j||[B_j,q]||_2^2, confirming the factor. Changing from s-modular
time to sigma-modular time multiplies the Fourier density by two and changes
its argument correspondingly; it does not multiply the energy.

5. Why independent Schwarz of integrands does not close the comparison

Even at t=0, using only the second positive J term to estimate the inner root
integral requires the weight

[sqrt(lambda)/(1+lambda)]^2
 /[lambda^2/(1+lambda)^2]=1/lambda.

Its integral diverges. This is an exact obstruction to that proof method,
not a physical counterexample: on lambda in [1,R], choose an arbitrary scalar
test function g(lambda)=(1+lambda)/lambda^(3/2). Its weighted norm squared

norm-squared ratio is log R, unbounded. Actual X_lambda commutators have
shared resolvent analyticity and endpoint constraints, so these arbitrary
g are not asserted to be realizable. A valid proof must exploit those
constraints, the first J term, and the modular orbit together.
