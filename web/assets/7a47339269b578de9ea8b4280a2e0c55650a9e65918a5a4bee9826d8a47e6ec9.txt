2. LEGALITY AND THE WEIGHTED HS CONTRACTION, WITHOUT REVERSIBILITY

Write d=sigma^(1/4) and

 T_sigma(x)=d^-1 Phi(d x d)d^-1.

PhiSharp is full-domain CP, fixes sigma, and is trace preserving: its Kraus
operators are K_iSharp=sqrt(sigma)K_i* sigma^(-1/2) when Phi has Kraus K_i;
Phi(sigma)=sigma gives sum K_iSharp* K_iSharp=I. Its weighted transform is
T_sigma^dagger. Therefore the weighted transform of S is

 S_sigma=T_sigma^dagger T_sigma,

and S is CPTP, sigma stationary, KMS symmetric, and weighted HS positive.
If Phi is self-compatible, one applies PhiSharp on BOTH outputs of its given
broadcaster to obtain an explicit broadcaster with common marginal S.
No self-compatibility is required in Sections3-6 below.

For arbitrary stationary Phi, T_sigma is an HS contraction, even though it
need not be selfadjoint. Here is a direct finite-dimensional interpolation
proof, to make this premise explicit. For complex z in the closed unit strip set

 T_z(X)=sigma^(-z/2) Phi(sigma^(z/2) X sigma^(z/2))sigma^(-z/2).

At Re z=0 this is a trace-norm contraction: Phi is CPTP and the imaginary
powers supply only left/right unitaries. At Re z=1 it is an operator-norm
contraction: T_1 is a unital CP map because Phi(sigma)=sigma, again surrounded
by imaginary-power unitaries. Finite Schatten interpolation gives
||T_(1/2)(X)||_2<=||X||_2, and T_(1/2)=T_sigma.

One can verify this interpolation directly with three lines. For invertible
polar decompositions X=U|X|,Y=V|Y|, normalized in HS norm, put
X_z=U|X|^(2(1-z)), Y_z=V|Y|^(2z), and use the analytic scalar
f(z)=Tr[Y_(conjugate(z))* T_z(X_z)]. On the left boundary X_z has trace norm1
and Y_z operator norm1; on the right boundary the roles reverse. Thus |f|<=1
on both boundaries and at z1/2. Singular X,Y follow by finite regularization.
The arbitrary HS-dual Y proves contraction. Faithfulness of sigma makes all
its imaginary powers unitaries and all real powers well defined.

Hence 0<=S_sigma<=I on the complex HS Hilbert space. No raw-channel positive
spectrum, KMS symmetry, or dimension factor is presumed.

3. EXACT OPERATOR-ORDER TRANSFER LEMMA

LEMMA. For any Hermitian X such that -k sigma<=X<=k sigma, k>=0,

 ||X-S(X)||_1 <=sqrt(2k ||X-Phi(X)||_1).           (3.1)

The same statement holds with any normalized faithful stationary product
reference Omega=tau_R tensor sigma and the local channel id_R tensor Phi:
replace sigma by Omega and X by an arbitrary Hermitian joint X satisfying
-k Omega<=X<=k Omega. The local Petz composition remains id_R tensor S;
choosing tau_R in this proof does NOT change S.

PROOF. Put x=d^-1 X d^-1 and A=sigma^(-1/2)X sigma^(-1/2). Then ||A||<=k.
The HS contraction identity gives

 ||x||_2^2-||T_sigma x||_2^2
  =2 Re<x,(I-T_sigma)x>-||x-T_sigma x||_2^2
  <=2 Re<x,(I-T_sigma)x>
  =2 Tr[A(X-Phi(X))]
  <=2k ||X-Phi(X)||_1.                            (3.2)

The norm loss is nonnegative; the real trace pairing therefore has the needed
nonnegative sign before it is bounded. Since 0<=S_sigma<=I,

 ||(I-S_sigma)x||_2^2
    <=<x,(I-S_sigma)x>
    =||x||_2^2-||T_sigma x||_2^2.                  (3.3)

Finally Schatten Hölder yields

 ||X-S(X)||_1
   =||d(I-S_sigma)x d||_1
   <=||d||_4^2 ||(I-S_sigma)x||_2
   =||(I-S_sigma)x||_2,                            (3.4)

because Tr sigma=1. This proves (3.1). On a reference product Omega, the
weighted transform is id_R tensor T_sigma because the tau_R factors cancel;
it is still an HS contraction, and Tr Omega=1 gives the same Hölder constant.

In particular, if rho<=M(tau_R tensor sigma), then

  T[rho,(id_R tensor S)rho]<=sqrt(M b).             (3.5)

This is a max-relative/order-budget theorem, not a Umegaki-budget theorem.
All matrices are finite and the constants have no eigenvalue/dimension factor
apart from the explicitly charged order constant M. For b0 every finite
state is fixed by S: a finite M exists for any faithful product reference,
so (3.5) is zero. The general obstacle concerns a UNIFORM rate as b tends0,
rather than exact fixed-point preservation.

4. COMPLETE BINARY CLASSICAL-REFERENCE CLOSURE

Let R be a classical bit and rho be ANY binary CQ extension of sigma:

 rho=|0><0| tensor A + |1><1| tensor (sigma-A),
 0<=A<=sigma.

The bit prior need not be uniform. Set X=2A-sigma, so -sigma<=X<=sigma, and

 rho=(1/2)diag(sigma+X,sigma-X).

Stationarity cancels the sigma part in each block. Consequently

 b=(1/2)||X-Phi(X)||_1,
 e=(1/2)||X-S(X)||_1.

Applying (3.1) with k1 gives the sharp-scoped upper bound

  e<=sqrt(b).                                      (4.1)

It holds for every finite local dimension, every faithful sigma, all priors,
arbitrary noncommuting conditional states, and arbitrary stationary Phi.
No information budget or self-compatibility is needed. Binary CQ states
automatically have I(R:B)<=H(R)<=log2. The balanced subcase has Tr X=0.
The parent-notified fixed-qubit lower example has not been exposed or checked;
optimality of (4.1)'s exponent is deferred to that later comparison.

For a finite classical alphabet of size m, each unnormalized block A_r<=sigma
can be treated with the order lemma, and Cauchy gives e<=sqrt(m b). That
alphabet factor is charged and is not replaced by the mutual-information
budget in this baseline. The binary improvement uses the complement identity
A_1=sigma-A_0 and is genuinely uniform in the local dimension.

5. PURE QUANTUM-REFERENCE CLOSURE

Suppose rho_RB=|v><v| is PURE with local marginal sigma. Write Phi with Kraus
K_i. Its entanglement fidelity on this purification is

 F_e,sigma(Phi)=sum_i |Tr(sigma K_i)|^2.

For the Petz reverse Kraus K_iSharp=sqrt(sigma)K_i* sigma^(-1/2), cyclicity
and sigma commutation with its own powers give exactly

 Tr(sigma K_iSharp)=conjugate[Tr(sigma K_i)],
 F_e,sigma(PhiSharp)=F_e,sigma(Phi).                (5.1)

The effect |v><v| implies 1-F_e,sigma(Phi)<=b. The pure-state fidelity/trace
bound then gives

 T[rho,(id_R tensor PhiSharp)rho]<=sqrt(b).

Using trace contraction of PhiSharp for the forward error,

 T[rho,(id_R tensor S)rho]
   <=T[rho,(id_R tensor PhiSharp)rho]
     +T[(id_R tensor PhiSharp)rho,(id_R tensor PhiSharp Phi)rho]
   <=sqrt(b)+b.                                    (5.2)

No information budget, reference dimension, eigenvalue factor, or KMS symmetry
is needed. Purifying a MIXED rho_RB would introduce an unobserved system S:
the observed error b need not control the error on RSB. Thus (5.2) cannot be
transferred to arbitrary mixed quantum references just by purification.
The fidelity identity is an acquired direct Kraus calculation, not an appeal
to a recovery channel selected for the individual state.

6. FINITE REFERENCE-DIMENSION CLOSURE, WITH ITS FACTOR CHARGED

For a quantum reference of dimension r, any rho_RB with rho_B=sigma obeys
rho_RB<=r I_R tensor sigma. A direct proof writes rho as an r-by-r positive
operator block matrix and uses block positivity plus Cauchy:

 <v,rho v><=r sum_i <v_i,rho_ii v_i>
           <=r sum_i <v_i,sigma v_i>.

Taking Omega=(I_R/r) tensor sigma gives rho<=r^2 Omega. Hence (3.5) yields

 e<=r sqrt(b).                                      (6.1)

This is uniform in local dimension and sigma conditioning for FIXED r, but
the explicit reference factor does not solve the arbitrary-reference target.
An information budget does not bound reference dimension: an arbitrarily
large independent reference can carry no mutual information at all.

7. EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO REPLACING M BY exp(I)

The order budget M in (3.5) cannot be bounded by a function of I(R:B) alone.
Even take a pure two-qubit state

 |v_t>=sqrt(1-t)|00>+sqrt(t)|11>, 0<t<1/2,
 sigma=rho_B=diag(1-t,t), tau_R=rho_R=diag(1-t,t).

Then I(R:B)=2h(t)->0, but the least M such that
|v_t><v_t|<=M(tau_R tensor sigma) is

 M=<v_t|(tau_R tensor sigma)^-1|v_t>
   =1/(1-t)+1/t ->infinity.

Thus genuine Umegaki information does not directly bound the KMS-HS dual order
norm in the acquired lemma. This is a structural failure of that extension
route, NOT a Petz-transfer counterexample: the pure-state bound (5.2) still
applies to this very family. No inverse-eigenvalue estimate is smuggled into
the arbitrary-reference conclusion.

8. WHY NAIVE MAX-RELATIVE SMOOTHING DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY GIVE EVEN A LOG RATE

Suppose a separate smoothing theorem supplies a state tilde(rho) with
T(rho,tilde(rho))<=epsilon and tilde(rho)<=M Omega. Even if Omega is stationary,
trace contraction gives only

 T[tilde(rho),(id tensor Phi)tilde(rho)]<=b+2epsilon.

Applying (3.5) and contracting S then gives

 e(rho)<=2epsilon+sqrt(M(b+2epsilon)).              (8.1)

It is invalid to write sqrt(M b) plus the smoothing error unless a separate
channel/smoother commutation or stability lemma proves that substitution.
If M is exponential in an inverse smoothing error (as in Umegaki substate
bounds), the additional M epsilon term can be huge. No nominal logarithmic
rate is deduced from (8.1). This defect was identified before root's later
matching explicit warning. No unverified nonlinear-smoother compatibility is
claimed. A construction preserving the local margin by itself is insufficient.
