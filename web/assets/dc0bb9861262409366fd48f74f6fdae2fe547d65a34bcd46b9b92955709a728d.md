# Hostile audit: robust-contamination acquisition for smFRET

## Verdict

The robust-contamination idea is mathematically salvageable only with a **known, uniform, state- and history-conditional disturbance envelope** that is small compared with the emission separation. An empirical average contamination rate, iid “bad block” model, or robust clustering by itself does not provide that envelope. Hohng et al.'s force–FRET experiment is a real reversible-action precedent, but does not report the actuator/ramp disturbance data needed to instantiate either the clean-block theorem or the robust extension. Application status stays **UNKNOWN**.

I found no separate written robust-contamination proposal in the current c4 artifacts. This audit therefore attacks the natural contamination extension of `slow_readout/PROOF.md` and the force-smFRET application report.

## Decisive failure modes

**Correlated ramps are not iid bad blocks.** A ramp or settling event is a shared physical episode, and can move the molecule once, after which all remaining photons in that block report the new state. The frame sequence is then path-dependent; it is not a set of iid samples from the start-state emission column. A batch-level hysteresis variable can also make every block in a run affected or none affected. A fraction estimated from one run is not a predictive per-block bound under that dependence. State-specific effects are especially damaging: if only one starting conformer responds to the ramp, a low overall contaminated fraction can erase or bias that transition row.

Independence is **not** needed if a genuine conditional envelope is supplied. If, for each attempted block b, all histories h, and all possible starting states i,
\[
\Pr(C_b=1\mid h,X_{b,0}=i,\text{commanded path})\le q_b,
\]
then the union bound gives \(\Pr(\exists b:C_b)\le\sum_b q_b\), regardless of dependence across blocks. An average over the unknown occupancy law, or an iid fit to detected jumps, is weaker and does not imply this uniform statement. Ramp-induced transitions that are part of the intended force-conditioned kinetics must instead be included in the action kernel; calling them contamination cannot remove them from the target model.

**A post-read error is sometimes just an emission channel, and sometimes invalidates the model.** A time-invariant, conditionally independent state-dependent photon-color/error law is already the unknown matrix E in the theorem. It is not a counterexample if shared across blocks and action settings and full rank. The failure is read back-action or time/action dependence: for example, every read of state 1 drives the molecule to state 0 before the next read. The first photon may identify state 1, but the remaining R−1 frames have state-0 emissions; the block average converges to E[:,0] as R grows. Repeated reads then do not estimate a stable column for state 1. More generally, if read j has state-dependent disturbance probability \(\eta_i\), the chance of avoiding disturbance over R reads is at most \((1-\eta_i)^R\) under independent disturbances, and a union bound costs R\(\eta_i\). With R growing as \(\kappa^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)\), a fixed per-read disturbance does not stay negligible as \(\kappa\to0\). If action changes the emission matrix to \(E^{(F)}\), the common-E calibration/decoding step also fails unless those action-specific channels are separately modeled and identified.

**Known versus observed contamination is a substantive boundary.** A robust theorem needs a supplied or externally calibrated one-sided upper bound covering all allowed states, histories, ramp paths, and read conditions, with its calibration failure probability charged. If the corruption indicator were directly observed and iid, zero events in n calibration blocks would only give the one-sided confidence bound \(q\le1-\beta^{1/n}\) at confidence \(1-\beta\); this calculation itself requires iid/exchangeable calibration blocks. In this assay hidden state changes are not directly labeled by the same FRET stream being calibrated, so inferring “no contamination” from those traces is circular. The c4 clean-block budget is particularly demanding: with B=589,085 and α=.05, the union-bound gate is \(q<\alpha/(4B)=2.12\times10^{-8}\) per block. Even under ideal iid direct labels, about 141 million zero-event calibration blocks would be needed for a 95% one-sided upper bound that small. A fitted average from five molecules or a low observed fraction is nowhere near such a certificate.

**Robust clustering has a separation threshold; it does not discover labels for free.** Let each block frequency vector be within Euclidean distance s=r+η of its true shared emission column, where r is the simultaneous finite-sample shot-noise radius and η is a *known, uniform* deterministic bound on contamination-induced displacement. Same-state points are at most 2s apart and different-state points at least \(d_E-2s\) apart. A single distance threshold can separate them only if
\[
d_E>4(r+\eta).
\]
With the theorem's only generic guarantee \(d_E\ge\sqrt2\kappa\), a fixed η destroys uniform coverage as κ shrinks. Unknown K, absent-state occupancy, and state-specific corruption make matters worse; the original theorem avoids the first issue by supplying K and assumes a positive occupancy floor p_*.

There is an exact Huber-contamination nonidentifiability counterexample. For two underlying emission laws P0,P1 with total-variation distance τ, their ε-contamination neighborhoods \((1-\epsilon)P_i+\epsilon Q_i\), with state-specific arbitrary Q_i, intersect whenever \(\tau\le\epsilon/(1-\epsilon)\): choose a common probability law dominating both \((1-\epsilon)P_i\). The resulting observed laws are identical, so no clustering rule can distinguish the states. Equivalently, nonidentifiability is possible once \(\epsilon\ge\tau/(1+\tau)\). For the c4 reporter family \(E_\kappa=\kappa I+(1-\kappa)\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/3\), two columns have τ=κ. Thus a fixed state-dependent contamination fraction ε makes the states observationally indistinguishable for all sufficiently small κ. Huber's original contamination model is the relevant primary statistical precedent; it does not certify that an experimental corruption fraction is known or state-independent ([Huber 1964](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177703732)).

## Audit of the proposed calibration and endpoint route

**Calibration max-coverage step: sound under the stated deterministic assumptions, but the output certificate has a stronger margin.** Let M≤ε_c N_c be arbitrary blocks, and let n_i>M calibration points be good points within r of each of the K columns. A radius-2r ball centered at any good point of class i covers that entire class. Because d_E>6r, different good classes are separated by more than 4r, so one ball cannot cover good points from two classes. Selecting one such center per class covers at least N_c−M points. Any K-center solution omitting class i misses its n_i>M good points and therefore covers at most N_c−n_i<N_c−M points. Hence every maximum-coverage solution covers each class; its center for class i lies within 3r of E[:,i]. I found no counterexample to this lemma.

The premise M≤ε_cN_c is a high-probability **count** bound, not a per-block mean. A bound E[M]≤ε_cN_c or a marginal bad-block probability does not by itself give it at the claimed confidence. It can follow from a uniform conditional risk bound P(C_t=1|past,state,command)≤q together with a Chernoff-type martingale domination and q<ε_c, or from a directly observed/calibrated count envelope. Shared ramp/batch failure modes invalidate an iid-binomial calculation unless covered by such a conditional envelope.

However, d_E>6r does **not** guarantee the proposed observable min-center-gap certificate >8r, so it is a completeness gap if the route claims output under only d_E>6r. Exact binary-simplex fixture (write distances in Euclidean units, with all coordinates chosen inside the simplex): set E columns at 0 and 7r; put four good points at r and four at 6r, and two arbitrary outliers at 3r and 4r. Then M=2, each good class has 4>M points, and d_E=7r>6r. With radius 2r, centers at r and 6r cover all ten points; every candidate center lies in {r,3r,4r,6r}, whose largest pairwise gap is 5r. Thus no maximizing choice can pass a >8r center-gap certificate, and the algorithm must abstain despite the stated d_E>6r and occupancy conditions. A sufficient margin for the certificate from the 3r localization alone is d_E>14r. The safe decision rule is still valid if it returns UNKNOWN absent the certificate; its finite coverage bound must use the stronger margin or admit this abstention region.

The >8r certificate does make nearest-center classification correct for **good** endpoint blocks: a good endpoint is within r of its true column, its calibration center is within 3r, and every other certified center is more than 8r away from that center. It cannot correctly decode every arbitrary endpoint outlier. Such an outlier can be set exactly equal to a different center and is then observationally indistinguishable from a clean endpoint; it must be charged to the endpoint-pair corruption budget, not passed off as decoded.

**A global endpoint-corruption fraction does not directly bound each transition row by ε_e.** If 2N_t endpoint blocks form N_t pre/post pairs and at most ε_e(2N_t) endpoints are corrupted, then at most 2ε_e N_t pairs are affected. If row i has n_i≈π_iN_t starts, worst-case pair corruption in that row is
\[
\beta_i\le \min\!\left(1,\frac{2\epsilon_eN_t}{n_i}\right)\approx \min(1,2\epsilon_e/\pi_i),
\]
not ε_e. A single bad pre block can also misassign a pair to another observed row, so a proof needs a joint bound for source-label errors as well as destination-label errors. For outcome-row corruption alone, replacing a fraction β_i of row-i observations changes its empirical probability vector by at most β_i in TV and at most √2β_i in Euclidean norm. Therefore a generic Frobenius perturbation is at most \(\sqrt{2\sum_i\beta_i^2}\), before sampling error. The pairwise test needs this plus sampling error below its γ margin (for the existing proof's stated margin, keep the total within γ/8). A global ε_e claim without the p_i denominator cannot justify the row comparison.

An exact surviving conditional bound is available if the analysis supplies, for every candidate b and true source row i, a simultaneous upper bound \(c_{b,i}/n_{b,i}\le\beta_{b,i}\) on the fraction of **pair records that can alter that row's conditional estimate**, counting any corrupted pre-label reassignments as well as bad post labels. Then
\[
\|\widehat P_b-P_b\|_F\le s_b+\sqrt{2\sum_i\beta_{b,i}^2},
\]
where s_b is the clean sampling radius. Require the right-hand side <γ/2 for nearest-orbit testing (or ≤γ/8 to retain the existing proof's conservative margin). If pre-state membership is protected and only post endpoints are corrupted, the global block count gives the crude \(\beta_{b,i}\le\min(1,2\epsilon_eN_t/n_{b,i})\); with \(n_{b,i}\ge p_*N_t\), a sufficient conservative condition is \(s_b+2\sqrt{2K}\epsilon_e/p_*<\gamma/2\). Without a rowwise count or a proved conversion of source-label corruption to such counts, this is not a theorem for arbitrary endpoint blocks.

There is an exact two-world witness. Use K=2, common start law π=(p,1−p), and
\[
P_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1/2&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These matrices are not related by a common state permutation; their permutation-orbit Frobenius gap is exactly 1. Under P_0, corrupt only the post-read blocks for founders whose pre-state is 0, making those blocks emit exactly as state 1. The observed pre/post law conditional on start 0 is then exactly the clean P_1 law; conditional on start 1 the laws already agree. The entire observed transcript under the contaminated P_0 world equals the clean P_1 transcript. This uses about pN_t bad endpoint blocks out of 2N_t, i.e. global fraction p/2, and corruption is allowed to depend on the transition endpoint. Thus ε_e≥p_*/2 (up to finite occupancy slack) can erase the distinguishing row completely. For smaller ε_e the row bias still scales as ε_e/p_*; it must be budgeted against γ and K, not merely called “ε_e-contamination.”

**A full-data likelihood is the necessary active baseline.** Cluster centers and decoded endpoints are summaries of the same photon record. By the data-processing principle, no test using only that summary can have lower optimal decision risk than a test allowed to use the full raw record under the same model and action schedule. The fair comparator is a joint photon-by-photon likelihood/HMM with the force command and timestamps included, profiling or robustifying the same state-dependent emission and contamination nuisance class. It is not a clean HMM given an easier model. Gopich–Szabo derive a photon-color/interphoton-time likelihood for single-molecule FRET and explicitly describe its state-dependent Poisson assumptions and background treatment ([2009, Eqs. 2 and 6, Sec. IV](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2802060/)); Hohng et al. themselves infer force-conditioned rates by HMM from binned FRET traces. A force action can still be necessary to identify a *force-conditioned causal rate* that passive data at another force cannot identify, but that is an objective change, not an acquisition-efficiency win.

## Closest physical example and resource comparison

Hohng et al. (2007) tether a Cy3/Cy5 Holliday junction to an optically trapped bead through λ-DNA; piezo sample translation imposes calibrated force while the confocal beam tracks the molecule. They collect five force conditions, 10 s per condition, at 10-ms integration, repeated for one to five cycles before bleaching: 50–250 s per molecule, 5,000–25,000 binned FRET samples. Trap stiffness is 0.11 pN/nm, obtained from a 10-kHz bead-position spectrum; force noise is reported as 0.2 pN at 2 pN and 100-Hz bandwidth. HMM-inferred forward/backward rates change asymmetrically with force and populations shift. The observed quantities are FRET traces and calibrated force; hidden-state paths, rates, and transition-state distances are inferred. The paper's five-molecule SDs are not one-sided bounds on state changes during force ramps. Its Methods/Figs. 1–2 and Table 1 give the protocol and fitted quantities ([primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3558530/), DOI [10.1126/science.1146113](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1146113)). It reports no ramp/settling latency, ramp jump-count envelope, photon totals, or equal-resource comparison.

A best passive baseline can analyze the same donor/acceptor photon colors and interphoton times at fixed force without 10-ms binning. The Hohng summaries do not report raw photon time tags or photon totals, so there is no defensible photons-per-wall-time comparison. Reported 10-ms frames are not photon counts. Equal-resource testing requires raw time tags and force-command/actual-force timestamps, then matching detected-photon dose, observation wall time, molecules, photobleaching/exposure, and all force ramp/settling/setup/calibration costs. The Hohng intervention adds at least the repeated 50–250 s observation schedule plus trapping, tethering, piezo calibration, beam tracking, and photostability costs. No published data in this example show that its active protocol beats the full time-tag likelihood at equal resources.

## What survives, exactly

1. **Rare whole-block corruption:** the original finite test survives if every block has a valid conditional upper bound q_b and \(\sum_bq_b\le\alpha/4\), plus its original shared full-rank E, occupancy, candidate-pair gap, independent-founder, and concentration assumptions. Dependence across blocks is allowed. This is essentially the existing no-change gate, not a relaxation to an empirically estimated iid corruption rate.

2. **Bounded frequency displacement:** a separate robust-clustering lemma survives if K is supplied, every state occurs in enough calibration/probe blocks, the same effective E applies in every relevant condition, and a known uniform η bounds each block's contamination displacement while \(d_E>4(r+\eta)\). Then threshold clustering and nearest-center endpoint decoding are correct on the simultaneous radius event. Transition-row estimation additionally needs its original per-state independent-founder sampling and a margin γ; no c4 physical source currently supplies η, state occupancy, or action-invariant E for Hohng's apparatus.

Neither condition gives an advantage over the full-likelihood analysis of the same active photons. The force-smFRET bridge supports “paid reversible intervention plus repeated emissions and model-inferred force-dependent kinetics”; it does not establish a calibrated rare-corruption route, robust-identifiability margin, or matched active/passive efficiency claim.
