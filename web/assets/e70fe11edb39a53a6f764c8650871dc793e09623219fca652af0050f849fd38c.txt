                                                         Abstract
           Coboundary expansion with non-Abelian coefficients is a strong version of high-dimensional
       expansion  for  simplicial  complexes.   One  motivation  for  studying  this  notion  is  that  it  was
       recently shown to have deep connections to problems in theoretical computer science.  However,
       very few examples of families of simplicial complexes with this type of expansion are known.
       Namely,  prior  to  our  work,  the  only  known  examples  were  quotients  of  symplectic  buildings
       and a slight variation of the Kaufman-Oppenheim coset complexes construction associated with
       SLn(F  p[t]).
           In  this  paper,  we  show  that  the  Grave  de  Peralta  and  Valentiner-Branth  constructions
       of  KMS  complexes  have  coboundary  expansion  with  non-Abelian  coefficients  when  it  is  per-
       formed with respect to congruence subgroups of Chevalley groups of classical type, i.e., of type
       An,Bn,Cn  andD       n.  This gives four new sources of examples to this expansion phenomenon,
       thus significantly enriching our list of constructions.

1    Introduction

Given  a  simplicial  complexX,  the  cohomology  ofXis  usually  defined  with  coefficients  in  an
Abelian group.  However, when one considers the low dimensional cohomology, the definition can
be generalized to computing the 0-th and first cohomologies ofXwith respect to any coefficient
group  Λ  where  Λ  need  not  be  Abelian.   Given  a  group  Λ,  1-coboundary  expansion  ofXis  a
quantization of the vanishing ofH    1(X,Λ) (see exact definition in Section 2.1.3 below).  This notion
of coboundary expansion with non-Abelian coefficients has received much attention lately due to
its deep connections with theoretical computer science [BM24, DD24b, BLM24, DDL24, BMVY25]
where the main focus is choosing the coefficient group Λ to be a finite symmetric group.

on a family of complexes.  Given a group Λ, a family of simplicial complexes{X     (s) }is said to be
a  family  of  1-coboundary  expanders  with  Λ  coefficients  if  it  has  uniformly  bounded  degree,  the

    Prior to our work, for a fixed finite symmetric group Λ, only two constructions of families of
1-coboundary expanders with Λ coefficients were known:  A construction coming from quotients of
symplectic buildings (see [BLM24, DDL24]) and a construction coming from a coset complexes over

show how the constructions of certain types of KMS complexes that were introduced in [GdPVB25]
give rise to 1-coboundary expanders with Λ coefficients.

expanders.  As a specific instantiation of their general method, the authors of [GdPVB25] showed
how  to  construct  a  family  of  special  KMS  complexes,  which  we  will  call  herecongruence  KMS
complexes(this is our terminology and it did not appear in [GdPVB25]).  The construction of con-

KOW24]  and  ours)  use  the  same  basic  methodology  to  construct  a  sequence  of  finite  simplicial
complexes with vanishing first Λ-cohomology.  We all start with a universal “algebraic” simplicial
complex  that  has  trivial  1-cohomology  with  respect  to  Λ,  and  then  passes  to  a  family  of  finite
complexes  by  taking  quotients  via  congruence  subgroups,  which  also  have  trivial  1-cohomology
with Λ-coefficients.
    Applying this methodology yields different difficulties that are dependent on the specific con-
struction:

    •In [BLM24, DDL24], the initial complex is a contractible symplectic eCn-building, which has
       trivial 1-cohomology for any group.  The difficulty lies in proving that the congruence sub-
       groups  exhibit  trivial  1-cohomology  with  respect  to  Λ,  requiring  deep  results  from  group
       theory, such as the congruence subgroup property and the strong approximation theorem.

    •In  [KOW24],  the  quotients  are  taken  over  a  coset  complex  over  SL n+1  (F p[t]).   Forn≥3,
       there  is  an  explicit  description  of  the  congruence  subgroups,  which  follows  from  the  fact
       that SLn+1  (F p[t]) has the same presentation as a Steinberg group.  This explicit description
       gives rise to a straightforward argument for proving the vanishing of 1-cohomology with Λ-
       coefficients given that Λ has no element of orderp(e.g., when Λ is finite andp>|Λ|).  Thus,
       most of the work in [KOW24] focuses on proving that the coset complex over SLn+1  (F p[t])
       has trivial 1-cohomology with respect to Λ.

    •At least with respect to proving vanishing of cohomology,  the congruence KMS complexes
       enjoy  the  best  of  both  worlds:  First,  the  initial  complex  from  which  we  take  quotients  is
       simply  connected  and  thus  has  trivial  1-cohomology  for  any  group.   Second,  showing  the
       vanishing of 1-cohomology for congruence subgroups with Λ-coefficients can be done via the
       presentation theory of the Chevalley groups (the argument shares some similarity with that
       of [KOW24]).  The takeaway is that the proof of vanishing of the first cohomology with Λ
       coefficients for congruence KMS complexes is perhaps the simplest in all known examples.

1.2    Non-Abelian cosystolic expansion
Cosystolic expansion is a weaker variant of coboundary expansion.  There is a known local to global
machinery for proving cosystolic expansion developed by [KKL14, EK24] for Abelian coefficients
and [KM21, DD24a] for non-Abelian coefficients.  Namely, in order to show cosystolic expansion for
a finite simplicial complex, it is enough to show local spectral expansion and coboundary expansion
of the links.  Local spectral expansion for congruence KMS complexes was shown in [GdPVB25] so
we are left to prove non-Abelian coboundary expansion of the links.
    In [OVB25], we already showed that links of congruence KMS complexes have Abelian cobound-
ary expansion in all relevant dimensions.  Here, we generalize our method from [OVB25] to show
that  the  links  of  congruence  KMS  complexes  have  1-coboundary  expansion  with  respect  to  any
group Λ:

1.4    Organization
The  paper  is  organized  as  follows.   In  Section  2,  we  first  recall  the  relevant  notions  of  high-
dimensional expansion in Section 2.1 and then introduce Chevalley groups, KMS groups and the
associated complexes in Section 2.2.  In Section 3, we prove vanishing of the first cohomology of the
congruence KMS complexes with respect to many coefficient groups.  Section 4 is devoted to non-
Abelian cone functions, first introducing the concept in Section 4.1, then showing how to construct
cone functions for joins and by adding certain vertices.  In Section 5, we show the cosystolic ex-
pansion of classical KMS complexes in the non-Abelian setting.  Section A provides further details
on buildings, their opposite complexes and the relation to KMS complexes.  Section B, Section C
and Section D provide further details on the proof of non-Abelian coboundary expansion of the
opposite complexes of typeA n,Cn andD   n, respectively.

Remark 5.4.Note that the results of Theorem 5.1 hold for all classical KMS complexes, while the

    Theorem 5.1 is an adaption of [OVB25, Theorem 1.1] to the setting with non-Abelian coeffi-
cients.  The proof structure is very similar, and we just adapt it to the non-Abelian setting when
necessary.
    The idea of the proof is as follows:  First, one observes that proper links of KMS complexes are
isomorphic to joins of certain subcomplexes of spherical buildings, called opposite complexes.  To
further elaborate on this point:

    •Each proper link has an associate subdiagram of the Dynkin diagram of the underlying GCM.

    •Each connected component of the subdiagram corresponds to an irreducible spherical building.

    •Each  spherical  building  admits  an  opposite  relation  between  its  simplices,  and  after  fixing
       one “fundamental chamber” the set of simplices opposite this chamber is called the opposite
       complex.

    •A proper link of the KMS complex is then isomorphic to the join of the opposite complexes
       inside the buildings corresponding to the connected components of the Dynkin diagram.  This
       was shown in [GdPVB25, Chapter 3] and we describe more details in Section A.1.

    Second,  given  two  complexes  with  a  1-cone  function,  we  can  construct  a  1-cone  function  for
their join as well.  Hence it suffices to check that all opposite complexes of irreducible spherical
buildings admit a NAC.
    Third, for all “classical” buildings, i.e.  those of typeA n,Cn,Dn, we construct a NAC for their
opposite  complex.   This  is  done  by  using  an  inductive  argument  on  a  class  of  complexes  which
contains the desired opposite complexes.  This argument was first used by Abramenko in [Abr96]

to  show  (n−1)-sphericallity  of  the  opposite  complexes  and  was  adapted  to  show  the  existence
of Abelian cone functions in [OVB25].  Below, we prove the non-Abelian version of the induction
argument.  The proof of the fact that the relevant classes satisfy the requirements of the theorem
can be found in the appendix for completeness, but it almost directly follows from the proof for
the Abelian case.

A.1    Buildings and BN-pairs

building.  Each two simplices of the building have to be contained in a common apartment and each
two apartments are isomorphic with an isomorphism that acts as the identity on the intersection.
