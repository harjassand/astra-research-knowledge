1. EXACT INTERFACE AND CLAIM

Use the six reactions of the immutable INITIAL, with propensities
  A+C -> A+B : k ac,       A+B -> A+C : ab,
  A+B -> B   : ab,         B -> A+B   : b,
  C -> 0     : c,          0 -> C     : 1.
The only controlled rate is k, an arbitrary predictable function of time and
the entire past, taking values in [1,2]. Counts are nonnegative integers.
Gamma={a+b>=1} is the closed irreducible support for every fixed/state-feedback
rate. History-dependent controls need not define a time-homogeneous Markov
chain; every probabilistic statement below is conditional on the current
history and is uniform over the remaining control.

Let s=b+c, u=b-3c, and z_+=max(z,0). Define
  W(a,b,c)=1+(a-1)^2/8+s+u_+^2/[4(s+1)]
                     +a/(s+1)+a/[64(b+1)].                  (1)
This is an explicit common real-valued potential, evaluated by rational
arithmetic on integer states. W>=1+(a-1)^2/8+s, so it is proper on Gamma.
It has no unknown fitted coefficients, theorem oracle, solver, or stationary
policy. It is not a quadratic polynomial or a globally concave function.

THEOREM. For every k in [1,2] and x in Gamma outside
  K={a<1536, b+c<20000} intersection Gamma,
the instantaneous controlled generator satisfies
  L_k W(x)<=-1.                                             (2)
The chain is nonexplosive for every such predictable control. If tau_K is the
first entrance into K, then, conditional on any initial history at state x,
  E[tau_K]<=W(x).                                           (3)
An explicit uniform finite expected hitting-time bound for g=(1,1,1) is given
in section 7. Consequently every time-homogeneous state-feedback choice
k(x) in [1,2] gives a positive recurrent irreducible chain on Gamma.
The same certificate gives the global inequality
  L_k W<=1945-a/2-(b+c)/32.                                (19)
In particular, all these stationary feedback laws have mean total population
at most 62240; arbitrary predictable controls have the time-occupation bound
derived in section 6. No stationary-law assertion is made for adaptive controls.

This closes robust recurrence/recovery for this EXACT coupled boundary
fixture. It does not prove a general weakly reversible bimolecular theorem,
geometric ergodicity, or a stationary law for a nonstationary controller.
The prior no-common-coercive-quadratic theorem remains valid.

2. EXACT DECOMPOSITION

Write W=1+Q+F+R_s+R_b, where
  Q=(a-1)^2/8, F=s+u_+^2/[4(s+1)],
  R_s=a/(s+1), R_b=a/[64(b+1)].
Set H(a)=-2a^2+5a-1. Then
  L Q=H(a)b/8.                                              (4)
For s>=1,
  L R_s=b(1-a)/(s+1)
        +a c/[s(s+1)]-a/[(s+1)(s+2)].                      (5)
The c-death term is zero when c=0. For all b>=0,
  64 L R_b=b(1-a)/(b+1)
        +1_{b>=1} a^2/(b+1)
        -k a^2 c/[(b+1)(b+2)].                            (6)
The indicator in (6) is essential: cancelling b in the B-to-C term without
it would insert a nonexistent reaction at b=0.
Conversion changes u by +/-4 while preserving s. C influx/death changes u
by -/+3 and s by +/-1. These facts give the exact F generator displayed in
common_nonlinear.py. All formulas also follow directly by six rational
neighbor evaluations, independently checked by that script.

When s=0, only C influx is enabled (b=0), and
  L W(a,0,0)=1-a/2.                                        (7)

3. UNIFORM COMPOSITION ESTIMATE

For f(z)=z_+^2 and any real z,d,
  f(z+d)-f(z)<=2z_+ d+d^2.                                (8)
This follows by the three sign cases, or by its derivative's Lipschitz
constant 2. Apply (8) to +/-4 conversion steps. Since k<=2 and u>0 implies
b>3c, their contribution to F is bounded by
  -2a t(b-2c)+8a,                                         (9)
where t=u_+/(s+1). If u<=0 the negative term is zero. The error estimate uses
(b+2c)/(s+1)<=2. No rate ordering is assumed inside the wedge b<=3c.

For C influx, apply (8) with d=-3 and account for the denominator change:
  Delta[u_+^2/(s+1)] <= -(t^2+6t)+16/(s+2).
For C death (s>=1), d=3 gives
  Delta[u_+^2/(s+1)] <=  (t^2+6t)+16/s.
Both use 0<=t<=1 and t^2+6t+9<=16. Multiplying by rates 1 and c yields an
error at most 32, since c/s<=1. Thus, with
  R(z)=1-(6z+z^2)/4,
  L F <= -c R(t)-2a t(b-2c)+8a+9.                         (10)
Here the final constant uses R(t)<=1, not its absolute value.

Now put r=u_+/s for s>=1. If u>0, R(t)>=R(r) and
  -2a t(b-2c)=-2a r(b-2c)
                  +2a r(b-2c)/(s+1)
               <=-2a r(b-2c)+2a.
If u<=0, t=r=0. Therefore the common bound is
  L F <= -c R(r)-2a r(b-2c)+10a+9.                        (11)
This estimate handles stochastic curvature at the positive-part interface;
the proof does not replace discrete generator differences by derivatives.

4. LEADING NEGATIVE DRIFT, INCLUDING SMALL A

Let
  J=H(a)b/8-cR(r)-2a r(b-2c).
For a=0,1,2 we claim J<=-s/32. If u<=0, b<=3c and the three expressions are
  -b/8-c, b/4-c, b/8-c,
which satisfy that bound directly.

If u>0, express b=s(3+r)/4 and c=s(1-r)/4. For a=0,
  J=-s f0(r)/4,
  f0=11/8-(19/8)r+(5/4)r^2+(1/4)r^3.
Its degree-3 Bernstein coefficients on [0,1] are
  11/8, 7/12, 5/24, 1/2.
Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one, so f0>=5/24>=1/8.
For a=1,
  J=s f1(r)/4,
  f1=-1/4+(3/4)r-(29/4)r^2-(1/4)r^3
      <=-1/4+9/464=-107/464<=-1/8.
This is a completed-square estimate for the linear/quadratic terms. For a=2,
  J=s f2(r)/4,
  f2=-5/8-(11/8)r-(53/4)r^2-(1/4)r^3<=-5/8.
Thus J<=-s/32 in every small-A case.                     (12)

For integer a>=3, H(a)<=-4 and H(a)<=-(4/9)a^2. Split its contribution in
half. One half is at most -a^2 b/36; the other at most -b/4. The conversion
term in J is nonpositive. If u<=0, then R(r)=1 and
  -b/4-c<=-s/4.
If u>0 and R(r)>=0, b>=3c gives -b/4<=-3s/16.
If R(r)<0, then r>1/2 (R(1/2)=3/16), hence b>7c, while R(r)>=-3/4. Thus
  -b/4-cR(r)<=-b/4+3c/4<=-b/7<=-s/8.
In all cases,
  J<=-s/8-a^2 b/36, a>=3.                                (13)

5. ADD THE RATIONAL ACTIVATION CORRECTIONS

For a=0,1,2, (5) and (6) have total positive contribution bounded by 3.
For example, at a=0 their positive contributions are at most 1 and 1/64;
at a=1 they are at most 1/2 and 1/128; at a=2, discarding negative terms,
they are at most 1 and 1/32. The b=0 case has no positive B-to-C term.
Equations (11)-(12) then imply
  L W<=-s/32+10a+12, a=0,1,2.                            (14)
For s>=20000 this is below -1.

If a>=3 and b>=1, the A-change terms in (5)-(6) are nonpositive. The remaining
positive terms are at most a/(s+1)+a^2/[64(b+1)]. Since
  1/(b+1)<=b/2,
  1/36-1/128=23/1152>=1/64,
(11) and (13) give
  L W<=-s/8-a^2 b/64+(21/2)a+9.                           (15)
Completing the square and using b>=1 gives
  -a^2/64+(21/2)a+9<=1773.
Therefore s>=20000 implies L W<=-2500+1773<-1. Alternatively, for
a>=1536>=128(21/2)=1344,
  -a^2/64+(21/2)a<=-a^2/128,
so (15) is below -1 independently of s>=1.

Finally take b=0,c>=1. The only nonzero composition curvature from conversion
occurs at c=1: u=-3 jumps to 1, with F increment 1/8. Direct substitution in
(4)-(6) gives the useful exact formula
  L W=1-c+(k a/8)1_{c=1}+a/(c+2)-k a^2 c/128.
Consequently
  L W<=1-c+(7/12)a-a^2 c/128.                             (16)
For every c>=1, the maximum over real a of the last two terms is 98/(9c).
If c>=20000, (16) is below -1. If a>=1536, then
(7/12)a<=a^2 c/256, giving L W<=1-c-a^2 c/256<-1.
Combine this with (7), (14), and (15). Every state outside K falls into one
of these cases, proving (2) for the entire interval of controls.

The global cost inequality (19) also follows explicitly. For a<=2, adding
a/2 to (14) gives constant at most 33. For a>=3,b>=1, adding a/2 to (15) and
completing the square gives
  -a^2/64+11a+9<=1945.
For b=0,c>=1, adding a/2 to (16) gives a quadratic maximum
  max_a[-a^2 c/128+(13/12)a]=338/(9c)<=338/9<38,
so L W<=39-c-a/2. The s=0 case is already (7). These four estimates prove
(19) everywhere, with f=a/2+s/32, without requiring any invariant-law premise.

6. NONEXPLOSION AND STOPPED EXPECTATIONS

Let N=a+b+c. Conversion leaves N unchanged; A birth and C influx are the only
upward jumps. For every control,
  L N=b-ab+1-c<=N+1.
Stop at the first exit from {N<M}. Dynkin and Gronwall give
  P(sup_{s<=t}N_s>=M)<=(N_0+1)e^t/(M+1).
Every finite count ball has bounded total intensity uniformly in k in [1,2].
Explosion on a finite time interval would require exit from every ball; the
displayed bound tends to zero. This proof remains valid for predictable
history-dependent rates. The component predicate a+b>=1 is preserved by
every enabled jump, independent of rates.

Stop the compensated W process at tau_K, a finite time t, and a count-ball
exit. All functions and intensities are bounded on the stopped ball, so
Dynkin applies with no moment assumption. Outside K, (2) gives
  E[W(X_stop)]<=W(x)-E[stop].
Since W>=0 and nonexplosion makes the ball exits tend to infinity, Fatou and
monotone convergence yield E[tau_K wedge t]<=W(x). Taking t to infinity
proves (3). The same argument begins at any stopping time with a specified
current history, because all drift and rate bounds are uniform. This is a
mean hitting bound, not an exponential-tail or geometric-drift assertion.

Applying the same stopped Dynkin argument to (19) yields, for every t>=0,
  E integral_0^t [A_s/2+(B_s+C_s)/32] ds<=W(x)+1945t.
The integrand is nonnegative, so Fatou permits removal of count-ball stops.
This gives a uniform time-averaged population bound and controls a LINEAR
running population cost; it does not control the quadratic event intensities.

7. EXPLICIT UNIFORM RECOVERY TO A STRICTLY POSITIVE STATE

There is a simple word from every x in Gamma to (1,0,0):
 - If a=0, use B->A+B once, giving a=1.
 - If a>=2 and b=0, introduce C and convert it to B.
 - With b>=1, use A+B->B until a=1.
 - Convert every remaining B to C with A+B->A+C, and remove every C.
Then use C influx, A+C->A+B, and C influx to reach g=(1,1,1).
This word has at most 2N(x)+7 jumps, with counts along it at most N(x)+2.
Each initial B can require both a conversion to C and a C-death event;
counting only one event per initial molecule would underestimate its length.
The precise bound is deliberately conservative and does not require a
search over states. At a=1,b=0 the A-deletion phase is skipped.

In K, N<=1535+19999=21534. Set M=22000 and H=2M+7=44007. Every planned
word has length at most H and stays in {N<=M}. All desired propensities are
at least 1, while total intensity is bounded by
  Lambda=2M^2+M+1=968022001.
At each step wait at most Delta=1/Lambda for the desired NEXT reaction.
Abort if another reaction occurs first or the window expires. Conditional
on any history at a planned state, the probability that the desired next
jump occurs within Delta is at least
  integral_0^Delta exp(-Lambda t) dt
  >=1/(e Lambda)>1/(3 Lambda).
Hence the entire word succeeds with conditional probability at least
  p=(3 Lambda)^(-H).                                      (17)
Success takes at most T=H/Lambda time. After an abort, at most one unexpected
jump has occurred, so N<=M+1. On this finite count ball, (1) gives
  W<=R=1+(M+2)^2/8+145(M+1)/64.
Indeed F<=5s/4, R_s<=a, R_b<=a/64 and the displayed bound is a safe sum.
After a failed attempt, the mean return time to K is at most R by (3).
Repeat the explicit word at each return to K. Conditional success is at
least p each time, so the expected number of attempts is at most 1/p;
independence and a stationary controller are unnecessary. Therefore
  E_x[tau_g]<=W(x)+(T+R)/p.                                (18)
All constants are explicit. This bound is extremely large, not a practical
mixing or runtime estimate. For a stationary state-feedback controller,
the finite set of neighbors reached on departure from g has bounded W;
the holding time at g has finite mean. Equation (18) then gives finite mean
return to g, proving positive recurrence on the irreducible Gamma.

For a stationary state-feedback policy, begin a regenerative cycle at g and
end it at the first return after departure. Its mean duration is finite by
the preceding argument. Stopping (19) on this cycle and using Fatou gives
  E integral_cycle f(X_s) ds<=1945 E[cycle duration],
because the terminal and initial values W(g) cancel; no finite stationary
mean of W is assumed. The cycle-occupation formula for the invariant law
then gives E_pi f<=1945, and since N<=32f,
  E_pi N<=62240,   pi(N>=R0)<=62240/R0 for every R0>0.
Thus the stationary laws of all such feedback policies are uniformly tight
in population count. This is a crude uniform bound, not Poisson tails or a
geometric mixing estimate.

The attempt schedule is an ANALYSIS coupling/minorization construction, not
a new control action applied to the chemical chain. k is still arbitrary.
One simply recognizes the desired event word in the naturally evolving
chain and restarts the analysis if a different event occurs.

8. ACQUISITION, COST, EVIDENCE, AND LIMITS

The common certificate has fixed rational coefficients and the core has an
explicit inequality predicate. Evaluation at binary-encoded x uses O(1)
integer/rational operations and polynomial work in log(1+N); intermediate
integers have O(log(1+N)) bits. No enumeration of K was performed or needed.
K contains about 3.07e11 states; a table-based core acquisition would have
that enormous cost. The recovery word is constructed in O(N+1) elementary
steps and can be streamed with O(log(1+N)) counter memory. At K this is a
fixed H bound; p is most naturally encoded as the power in (17). Materializing
its denominator has at most 32H+1=1408225 bits. The time bound (18) is finite
and explicit but astronomically large. No polynomial-in-network-input
general acquisition result follows from this single constant-size fixture.

Expected total reaction firings during arbitrary excursions are UNKNOWN.
Finite mean hitting time alone does not bound their expectation because
intensities grow quadratically. Nonexplosion plus finite tau_g does imply
finitely many firings almost surely before hitting; that is a weaker fact.
The prescribed short event-word attempt uses at most H+1 firings, but the
intervening return-to-K cost has not been bounded in reaction firings.
Energy, backend/token use, and physical runtime were not measured.
