# Mathematical discovery record — 9 October 2026

A complete mathematical mechanism has been established in this investigation, but the historic 10/10 objective has not been achieved.

The strongest result is an explicit construction that converts an arbitrary rational polynomial equation into a smooth, geometrically integral algebraic variety while preserving every rational solution exactly once. Remarkably, the resulting variety has a real locus diffeomorphic to ordinary Euclidean space, regardless of how complicated the original rational solution set is.

## 1. Main theorem: a universal arithmetic-selective étale cover

Let \\(f\in\mathbb Q[X_1,\ldots,X_n]\\) be any nonconstant polynomial. Define

\\[ \Delta_f=4+27f^4, \qquad U_f=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q[\mathbf X,\Delta_f^{-1}], \\]

and

\\[ V_f=\operatorname{Spec} \frac{\mathbb Q[\mathbf X,z,\Delta_f^{-1}]} {(z^3+z-f(\mathbf X)^2)}. \\]

Then the projection \\(\pi_f:V_f\to U_f\\) has all the following properties:

1. It is a finite étale cover of degree three, and \\(V_f\\) is smooth and geometrically integral.
2. Its geometric monodromy group is the full symmetric group \\(S_3\\).
3. \\(U_f(\mathbb R)=\mathbb R^n\\), and \\(\pi_f\\) induces a real-analytic, semialgebraic diffeomorphism \\(V_f(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^n\\).
4. Its rational points are exactly \\[ \boxed{V_f(\mathbb Q)= {(\mathbf x,0):\mathbf x\in\mathbb Q^n,\ f(\mathbf x)=0}.} \\]

Thus a finite étale morphism can be surjective over every complex and real base point, have maximal cubic monodromy, yet admit a rational lift precisely at the zeros of an arbitrary polynomial.

The arithmetic ingredient is the classical fact that the elliptic curve \\(y^2=x^3+x\\) has only one affine rational point, \\((0,0)\\). Its Mordell–Weil group is \\(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\\).&#x20;

## 2. Proof and the mathematical mechanism

### A. Arithmetic rigidity

Consider the elliptic curve

\\[ E:\quad y^2=x^3+x. \\]

Its affine rational-point set is exactly \\(\\{(0,0)\\}\\). This agrees with the recorded rank-zero, two-torsion classification of \\(E\\).&#x20;

Here is an elementary route to that fact.

Fermat's infinite descent proves that no positive integers \\(u,v,w\\) satisfy \\(u^4+v^4=w^2\\). Briefly, choose a primitive solution with \\(w\\) minimal and, after interchanging \\(u,v\\), take \\(u\\) odd and \\(v\\) even. Primitive Pythagorean parametrization gives

\\[ u^2=m^2-n^2,\qquad v^2=2mn,\qquad w=m^2+n^2, \\]

with \\(m,n\\) coprime. Reduction modulo \\(16\\) forces \\(m\\) odd and \\(n\\) even. Since \\(2mn\\) is a square, \\(m=a^2,\ n=2b^2\\). Hence

\\[ u^2+(2b^2)^2=(a^2)^2. \\]

Parametrizing this primitive right triangle again gives

\\[ b^2=rs,\qquad a^2=r^2+s^2, \\]

where \\(r,s\\) are coprime positive integers. Thus \\(r=c^2,\ s=d^2\\), producing \\(c^4+d^4=a^2\\). But \\(a\<w\\), contradicting minimality.

Now suppose \\(y^2=x^3+x\\) has a rational solution with \\(x\ne0\\). Necessarily \\(x>0\\). Writing \\(x=a/b^2,\ y=c/b^3\\) in integral form gives

\\[ c^2=a(a^2+b^4),\qquad \gcd(a,a^2+b^4)=1. \\]

Both factors must be squares, so \\(a=r^2\\) and \\(r^4+b^4=s^2\\), contradicting Fermat's descent. Therefore \\(x=y=0\\).

For the constructed variety, every rational point satisfies

\\[ f(\mathbf x)^2=z^3+z. \\]

The elliptic-curve result forces \\(z=f(\mathbf x)=0\\). Conversely, each rational zero of \\(f\\) gives \\((\mathbf x,0)\\), with \\(\Delta_f=4\\). This proves the exact rational-point correspondence.

### B. Geometric regularity and monodromy

The polynomial \\(P(T)=T^3+T-f^2\\) has discriminant

\\[ \operatorname{disc}(P)=-4-27f^4=-\Delta_f. \\]

Over \\(U_f\\), its discriminant is invertible. Since \\(P\\) is monic, the resulting algebra is free of rank three; the discriminant criterion makes it finite étale. Consequently \\(V_f\\) is smooth.

To prove geometric integrality, suppose \\(P\\) had a root \\(R\in\mathbb C(\mathbf X)\\). Then

\\[ (R(\mathbf X),f(\mathbf X)) \\]

would define a nonconstant rational map from \\(\mathbb P^n\\) to the smooth projective completion of \\(E\\). Restricting to a general line would give a nonconstant morphism \\(\mathbb P^1\to E\\), impossible by Riemann–Hurwitz. Thus the cubic is irreducible over \\(\mathbb C(\mathbf X)\\).

Its discriminant cannot be a square in that field either. Otherwise, a rational square root would give a nonconstant rational map from \\(\mathbb P^n\\) to the genus-one curve

\\[ W^2=-4-27T^4, \\]

again impossible. An irreducible cubic with nonsquare discriminant has Galois group \\(S_3\\). This establishes full geometric monodromy.

Finally, \\(4+27f(\mathbf x)^4>0\\) for all real \\(\mathbf x\\). The function \\(z\mapsto z^3+z\\) is strictly increasing, with derivative \\(3z^2+1>0\\). Every real \\(\mathbf x\\) therefore has exactly one real lift, depending real-analytically and semialgebraically on \\(\mathbf x\\). This proves the asserted diffeomorphism.

### C. The more general mechanism

The monodromy argument yields a reusable structural principle:

Positive-genus intermediate-cover rigidity. Let \\(k\\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and \\(L/k(t)\\) a finite Galois extension. Suppose every nontrivial intermediate field has a smooth projective curve of positive genus. Then, for every nonconstant rational function \\(f\in k(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\\), base change along \\(t=f\\) preserves the full Galois group.

Indeed, any loss of Galois degree would give a nontrivial intersection of \\(L\\) with \\(k(\mathbf X)\\). The corresponding positive-genus curve would then receive a nonconstant rational map from \\(\mathbb P^n\\), which is impossible.

The cubic detector satisfies this hypothesis: its degree-three intermediate curve and quadratic discriminant-resolvent curve both have genus one. The hypothesis is essential; genus-zero covers such as \\(s^2=t\\) lose degree after the substitution \\(t=X^2\\).

This is the central mechanism: positive-genus geometry prevents algebraic degeneration under rational substitutions, while elliptic arithmetic controls rational lifts.

## 3. Strongest additional consequences

### A. One-to-one smoothing into a single hypersurface

There is a stronger presentation for applications not requiring the explicit étale cover.

Theorem. For every nonconstant \\(f\in\mathbb Q[\mathbf X]\\), an algorithm finds a positive integer \\(m\\) such that

\\[ \boxed{H\_{f,m}:\quad z^3+z=m^2f(\mathbf X)^2} \\]

is a smooth, geometrically integral affine hypersurface satisfying

\\[ H\_{f,m}(\mathbb Q) = \\{(\mathbf x,0):f(\mathbf x)=0,\ \mathbf x\in\mathbb Q^n\\}. \\]

Its degree is \\(\max(3,2\deg f)\\), and its real locus is diffeomorphic to \\(\mathbb R^n\\).

Proof. Arithmetic rigidity and geometric integrality follow from the preceding arguments for every \\(m\ne0\\).

At a complex singular point, the \\(z\\)-derivative requires \\(3z^2+1=0\\), so \\(z^3+z\ne0\\). Consequently \\(f\ne0\\), and every partial derivative of \\(f\\) must vanish. A polynomial in characteristic zero has finitely many critical values. Each nonzero critical value excludes only finitely many possible \\(m\\) through the displayed equation. Thus all but finitely many positive integers work.

Enumerating \\(m\\) and testing the Jacobian ideal by Gröbner bases is an effective terminating algorithm.

The important strengthening over a conventional smoothing construction is that the number of rational solutions is unchanged, not merely their existence.

### B. A quadratic-equation normal form

Arithmetic-circuit variables allow the construction of \\(V_f\\) using only equations of degree at most two. Addition and multiplication gates are represented by

\\[ u-v-w=0,\qquad u-vw=0. \\]

If \\(s\\) is the circuit output for \\(f\\), introduce \\(a,b,c,d,z,v\\) satisfying

\\[ \begin{aligned} a-s^2&=0,& b-a^2&=0,\\\ c-z^2&=0,& zc+z-a&=0,\\\ d-4-27b&=0,& dv-1&=0. \end{aligned} \\]

These equations define a variety isomorphic to \\(V_f\\). The graph equations form a complete-intersection presentation, and the previously proved smoothness and geometric integrality apply.

This gives a uniform, rational-solution-bijective compilation into smooth geometrically integral affine complete intersections defined by quadratic equations, whose real loci are Nash-diffeomorphic to Euclidean space. The number of introduced variables and equations is linear in the arithmetic-circuit size, and the compilation is polynomial-time in that representation.

Degree three is optimal among finite étale covers of rational-point-complete opens that retain exactly one rational lift over each solution: a degree-two étale fiber over \\(\mathbb Q\\) has either zero or two rational points, while degree one would be an isomorphism and could not select a proper subset of \\(\mathbb Q^n\\).

### C. A precise transfer for two foundational problems

For Hilbert's tenth problem over \\(\mathbb Q\\), arbitrary rational polynomial solvability reduces to rational-point existence on this special class of smooth quadratic complete intersections. The reverse reduction is elementary: a finite list of rational equations is equivalent to their sum of squares being zero.

For Mazur's finite-connected-components conjecture, the transfer also preserves the relevant topology. If \\(f=\sum_i g_i^2\\), then \\(f=0\\) describes the same real and rational zero sets as the system \\(g_i=0\\). Because \\(V_f(\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^n\\), the closure of \\(V_f(\mathbb Q)\\) corresponds homeomorphically to the closure of the original rational solution set. Thus the affine version of Mazur's conjecture is equivalent to its restriction to this Euclidean-real-locus class. Mazur's finite-component question remains a recognized open conjectural framework.&#x20;
