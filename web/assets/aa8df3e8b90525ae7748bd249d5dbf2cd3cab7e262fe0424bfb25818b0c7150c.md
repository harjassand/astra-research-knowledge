# Dynamic materials: closed-cycle work in passive chiral metamaterials

## Verdict

The strongest defensible result here is a rejection of a claimed capability, not a new material design: a fully equilibrated, source-free elastic material whose internal coordinates minimize one scalar free energy cannot deliver nonzero work on a closed quasistatic deformation cycle. Its reduced stiffness is symmetric. A 2023 paper claims that a frictionless chiral lattice made only from elastic beams and rigid circles can nevertheless act as a repeatable work source. The claim conflicts with the paper's own stated reversible energy balance. This is a decisive model-level barrier, but it does not settle what a fabricated specimen would do; the reported evidence is finite-element simulation.

The corresponding application gate is straightforward: measure every generalized force/torque port and every actuator or fuel input while tracking the complete internal configuration through repeated cycles. Any persistent output must be paid for by an omitted source, a decrease in stored free energy, or a nonconservative constitutive element. A geometry-only explanation cannot evade this accounting.

Question: can a frictionless, quasistatic, adaptive chiral elastic structure with no energy source produce repeatable net mechanical work by switching contact states during a closed deformation cycle?

## Theorem: equilibrium elimination preserves reciprocity

Let controlled deformations be (x\in\mathbb R^d), internal material coordinates be (y\in\mathbb R^m), and let (U(x,y)) be a (C^2) stored-energy or fixed-temperature free-energy function. Assume, on the operating region, that for each (x), the internal state is the unique equilibrium minimizer (y_*(x)), with (U_y(x,y_*)=0) and (U_{yy}(x,y_*)\succ0). Define the reduced potential

\[
F(x)=U(x,y_*(x)).
\]

Then the quasistatic generalized force is (f=\nabla F) (or (-\nabla F), depending on the force convention), and its tangent is symmetric:

\[
K_{\rm eff}=\nabla^2 F
=U_{xx}-U_{xy}U_{yy}^{-1}U_{yx},\qquad K_{\rm eff}=K_{\rm eff}^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Consequently, for every closed path in controlled deformation space,

\[
\oint f\cdot dx=0.
\]

**Proof.** Differentiate the stationarity condition (U_y(x,y_*(x))=0): (dy_*/dx=-U_{yy}^{-1}U_{yx}). The envelope theorem gives (\nabla F=U_x), since the (U_y\,dy_*/dx) term vanishes. Differentiating once more gives the displayed Schur complement. The Hessian blocks of scalar (U) satisfy (U_{xy}=U_{yx}^{\mathsf T}), so the Schur complement is symmetric. Finally (f\cdot dx=\pm dF), whose integral on a closed path is zero. At positive temperature the same argument applies to equilibrium Helmholtz free energy (F(x)=-k_BT\log Z(x)) wherever it is differentiable.

Frictionless unilateral contact is covered when the internal state minimizes elastic energy over the feasible contact set: contact changes can make the reduced potential nonsmooth, but they do not turn a single-valued potential into a work source. A metastable branch that does not return to its initial internal state can release stored energy once. Friction, impacts, viscoelastic loss, or phase hysteresis can consume work. None permits a repeatable, source-free net output from a fully returned state.

For a postulated constant two-coordinate law (f=Kx), the signed loop work is

\[
W_{\rm loop}=(K_{21}-K_{12})A=2\kappa A,
\quad \kappa=(K_{21}-K_{12})/2,
\]

where $A=\tfrac12\oint(x_1\,dx_2-x_2\,dx_1)$ is oriented area. This is a correct identity for a nonconservative constitutive law; the theorem shows why it cannot be the equilibrium response of the source-free material just defined. If a powered structure with work-like drive sustains output $W_{\rm out}=2|\kappa A|$ every period $T$, then its drive must supply at least that much work per period plus all dissipative losses, so $P_{\rm drive}\ge W_{\rm out}/T+P_{\rm loss}$. For a heat-driven device, input heat must be converted to available free energy (exergy) before comparing it with mechanical output; raw heat is not interchangeable with drive work. If no replenished source exists, a finite initial free-energy reservoir $B$ can support at most $N W_{\rm out}\le B$ over $N$ cycles, even before losses.

## Source reconstruction and decisive conflict

Shaat and Park's primary 2023 paper, [*Chiral nonreciprocal elasticity and mechanical activity*](https://people.bu.edu/parkhs/Papers/shaatJMPS2023.pdf), models a square lattice of rigid circles connected by curved Bernoulli–Euler ligaments. The authors specify normal contact without tangential or dissipative friction and describe all components as passive elastic elements (PDF pp. 6–7, lines 586–625). They argue that changing ligament-circle contact states gives direction-dependent stiffness and nonzero work around a dilation–rotation cycle (pp. 15–17, lines 1752–1792). They also state that the curved ligament's local rigidity matrix is generally asymmetric because of geometric asymmetry (p. 8, lines 868–872). In conjugate force/coordinate variables, a conservative beam tangent is the Hessian of stored energy and therefore symmetric; this local matrix is a second point requiring independent reconstruction from the claimed beam/contact mechanics.

The load-bearing conflict is internal to that paper. Its Eq. (31) states that reversible isothermal work on each deformation step is (\delta W=d\psi), with \(\psi\) the Helmholtz free energy (p. 13, lines 1491–1510). It then states that the material returns to its initial state, hence \(\oint d\psi=0\), but claims \(\oint\delta W\ne0\) from asymmetric stiffness (p. 13, lines 1517–1538). Integrating its own Eq. (31) around that same closed cycle gives \(\oint\delta W=\oint d\psi=0\). The claimed nonzero result cannot hold simultaneously with those premises.

The appendix says the finite-element energy terms are only internal energy and external work, with total-energy residual approximately zero at each increment, and also says stresses and all displacements return to zero at cycle end (pp. 23–24, lines 2640–2654). Separately, it computes nonzero accumulated work from stress–strain increments over 40,000 increments (pp. 24–25, lines 2655–2712). The record does not provide raw force histories or an independently audited energy ledger that reconciles these statements. The theorem therefore refutes the declared closed-equilibrium model capability; it does not identify which implementation or bookkeeping step produced the source's finite-element result.

This conclusion is deliberately scoped. Lier et al., [*Passive odd viscoelasticity*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.105.054607), derive passive odd viscoelastic responses for chiral fluids with broken time-reversal symmetry and state that linear viscoelastic chiral solids require activity for odd elastic response. That work is not a counterexample to the equilibrium free-energy theorem: it concerns time-dependent constitutive response and explicitly uses a time-reversal-broken setting. By contrast, Chen et al.'s [active odd micropolar beam](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-26034-z) and Wu et al.'s [odd-mass metamaterial](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2209829120) explicitly use powered piezoelectric/electronic actuation. A July 2026 preprint by Rahimi and Park, [*Driven Odd Elasticity in Passive Mechanical Metamaterials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13997), uses periodically driven chiral gears; its passive components do not make the driven assembly source-free. It is a useful control case for the energy boundary.

## Measurement and falsifier

Test a chiral contact lattice under displacement control in two independent generalized coordinates (for example, dilation and rotation). The same protocol should include a complete return to the initial macrostate and a measured wait until internal relaxation stops. Repeat the loop in both orientations and for many cycles. Track circle rotations, ligament shapes, and contact state so that “returned state” means the complete microstate, not just zero boundary displacement. Put a calibrated load/torque cell on every mechanical port. If the specimen has motors, piezo elements, chemical fuel, or periodic gears, meter those work-like ports independently. For heater-driven samples, meter heat and bath temperatures and compare mechanical output with heat exergy.

For a genuine constant odd tangent $\kappa$, the model predicts $W_{\rm loop}=2\kappa A$, changing sign when loop orientation is reversed. In a powered realization, the work source must pay for the output and measured losses each period. In the unpowered frictionless-equilibrium control, the prediction is zero closed-cycle net work. A reproducible positive output there, after the full internal state returns and all ports are accounted for, would falsify the theorem's assumptions or expose a missing physical term; chirality alone does not explain the energy.

**Strongest equally informed controls:** (1) the same specimen and loop with any pump, motor, heater, or powered controller disabled; (2) an achiral straight-ligament specimen with matched stiffness, mass, and loading path; (3) a mirror-chirality specimen, for which a true chirality-odd coupling should reverse sign; and (4) a powered, periodically driven gear or piezo specimen with input energy measured. Compare force–displacement loops and net energy per cycle, not stiffness modulation alone.

**Acquisition and resource costs:** two-axis displacement/rotation actuation; force and torque transducers at every boundary; synchronized displacement/rotation imaging of internal coordinates; contact-state tracking; voltage/current meters for electronics and piezo drive; torque/angle or force/displacement meters for mechanical pumps; temperature and heat-flow measurements if thermal activation is present; and calibration of sensor phase delay, which can create apparent loop work. Report specimen mass/volume, cycle time, drive energy, dissipated heat, work delivered to the load, reset time, and cycle-to-cycle drift. For a sampled path of length (L=\sum_j\ell_j), maximum step (h=\max_j\ell_j), force error at most \(\epsilon_f\), and force Lipschitz constant (M_f) along arclength, a left-Riemann work estimate obeys the deterministic bound

\[
|\widehat W-W|\le \epsilon_f L+\tfrac12 M_f hL
\]

assuming exact displacement samples. For a rectangular test of area (A), the corresponding uncertainty must be smaller than the claimed (2|\kappa A|); otherwise the experiment does not resolve the effect. Displacement, torque, synchronization, thermal, and pump-meter errors must be added separately.

## Evidence status and novelty boundary

- The equilibrium-elimination theorem and loop identity are internally reconstructed mathematical consequences of scalar free energy, not a new claim about all materials.
- The contradiction in the 2023 paper is a source-level audit of its stated premises and equations. Its finite-element result is source-reported, not independently replayed or experimentally validated here.
- Passive odd viscoelastic fluids, time-reversal-broken media, active devices, metastable one-shot energy release, and boundary-driven systems lie outside the narrow source-free equilibrium theorem unless their extra state and ports are explicitly included.
- No novelty or priority claim is made for the mathematical theorem or the proposed test. The usable advance is the exact barrier and a concrete energy-ledger test for the specific “passive work-source” claim.
