## Main outcome

The strongest result from this investigation is a **conditional transfer theorem for mixed Ising spin glasses**, rather than a new interpretation of an OpenAI headline result:

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\bigl(\xi''(t)^{-2}\bigr)\le 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{full zero-temperature Parisi support}
}
\]

for the finite, zero-field, Gaussian even-spin mixtures specified below, **conditional on a uniform analytic lemma in the OpenAI SK companion manuscript**.

This supplies a sufficient condition for an existing near-optimal classical message-passing algorithm. I also derived explicit coefficient certificates, a stronger quartic–sextic region, and an exact rational checker. The existing algorithmic implication is due to El Alaoui, Montanari and Sellke; it is not a new algorithm invented here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.00904v1)

**The status is important:** the transfer argument and coefficient calculations are developed explicitly, and the reported algebraic checks were executed. I have not independently verified the imported analytic dependency closure or established novelty. This is a theorem candidate with a precise dependency, not a certified breakthrough.

## 3. The consequential ingredient was below the headline theorem

Family 281 is presented around QAOA optimality for SK. Its companion establishes full support of a zero-temperature SK order parameter and already draws the pure-SK classical optimization consequence. The companion also credits separately released work. Those conclusions should not be counted as discoveries of this investigation. 

The more reusable statement concerns the unit-diffusion Parisi equation

\[
\partial_s\Phi
=
-\frac12\left(\Phi_{xx}+\gamma(s)\Phi_x^2\right),
\qquad
\Phi(S,x)=|x|,
\]

with associated diffusion

\[
dX_s=\gamma(s)\Phi_x(s,X_s)\,ds+dB_s,
\qquad X_0=0.
\]

Write

\[
Q(s)=\mathbb E\!\left[\Phi_x(s,X_s)^2\right].
\]

The source’s plateau argument applies to an arbitrary nonnegative, nondecreasing, right-continuous, integrable \(\gamma\). On a positive constant interval \(\gamma=c>0\), it gives

\[
Q'''(s)
\ge
\frac{c^2}{1000}\,
\mathbb E\!\left[\Phi_{xx}(s,X_s)^4\right]
>0.
\]

On a zero plateau, it gives \(Q''(s)>0\). Crucially, **the exported formal lemma does not assume that \(\gamma\) minimizes the SK functional**.  

That distinction makes the transfer possible. A mixed-model minimizer becomes an admissible coefficient after time rescaling, but does **not** become an SK minimizer.

### What I checked in this dependency

The source proves the sign through a rational positivity identity combined with analytic inequalities and a weighted divergence whose expectation vanishes. I reconstructed the polynomial identity and checked its **22 residual monomials with exact rational arithmetic**. All coefficients matched and were positive. The coefficient providing the strict surplus satisfies

\[
\frac{1661507}{1521000000}>\frac1{1000}.
\]

This verifies the algebraic certificate, **not** the PDE sign propagation, integrability, limiting arguments or complete formal proof.

The formalization scope was also checked. The public comparator’s intentional `sorry` is the challenge target, not the proof. Its metadata points to the actual solution module, which I inspected. The scope document distinguishes the support/value statements from QAOA circuit-energy convergence. No complete dependency-closure or comparator replay was performed here.   

## 4. Derived theorem: a covariance certificate for mixed Ising models

Consider a zero-field Gaussian Hamiltonian on \(\{-1,1\}^N\), with covariance

\[
\mathbb E[H_N(\sigma)H_N(\tau)]
=
N\xi\!\left(\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\sigma_i\tau_i\right),
\]

where

\[
\xi(t)
=
\frac{a_0}{2}t^2+
\sum_{\substack{p\ge4\\p\text{ even}}}
\frac{\lambda_p}{p(p-1)}t^p,
\qquad
a_0>0,\quad \lambda_p\ge0,
\]

and only finitely many coefficients are nonzero.

Set \(a=\xi''\).

> **Conditional covariance-time theorem.**  
> Assuming the uniform analytic plateau interface described above, if
> \[
> \boxed{a(t)a''(t)\ge3a'(t)^2\qquad(0\le t\le1),}
> \]
> then every admissible integrable zero-temperature Parisi minimizer has full relative Stieltjes support on \([0,1)\). In particular, it increases strictly on every nonempty subinterval.

The condition is sufficient, not claimed necessary.

### The key transformation

The mixed-model equation is

\[
\partial_t\Phi
=
-\frac{a(t)}2
\left(\Phi_{xx}+\gamma(t)\Phi_x^2\right).
\]

Introduce covariance time

\[
s=\xi'(t),\qquad S=\xi'(1),\qquad t=\tau(s).
\]

Because \(a\ge a_0>0\), the inverse exists. With
\(\widetilde\gamma(s)=\gamma(\tau(s))\), the equation becomes the unit-diffusion equation.

But the variational penalty also changes:

\[
\frac12\int_0^1 t\,a(t)\gamma(t)\,dt
=
\frac12\int_0^S \tau(s)\widetilde\gamma(s)\,ds.
\]

**Keeping this transformed penalty is essential.** Replacing \(\tau(s)\) by \(s\) would incorrectly turn every mixed model into an SK optimization problem.

Differentiating the inverse clock gives

\[
\tau'=\frac1a,\qquad
\tau''=-\frac{a'}{a^3},\qquad
\tau'''=\frac{3(a')^2-aa''}{a^5}\le0.
\]

Thus the covariance condition controls the curvature of the transformed variational obstacle.

### Why support gaps become impossible

For a minimizing coefficient, define

\[
G(s)=\int_s^S [Q(u)-\tau(u)]\,du.
\]

First variation gives \(G\ge0\), with \(G=0\) on the Stieltjes support. At an interior support point,

\[
Q=\tau,\qquad Q'\le\tau'.
\]

These contact conditions follow by adding Stieltjes mass and removing arbitrary compactly restricted mass; they do not assume the minimizer is atomic.

Suppose \((\alpha,\beta)\) is a positive plateau between support points. Set

\[
F=Q'-\tau'.
\]

The imported lemma and the covariance condition give

\[
F''=Q'''-\tau'''>0.
\]

At both endpoints, \(F\le0\). Strict convexity therefore forces \(F<0\) inside. However, contact at the endpoints also gives

\[
\int_\alpha^\beta F(s)\,ds
=
[Q-\tau]_\alpha^\beta
=
0,
\]

a contradiction.

A zero plateau is excluded differently: \(Q'\) is strictly increasing, while \(\tau'\) is nonincreasing. Hence \(F\) is strictly increasing and negative before its right contact, again contradicting the zero integral.

The endpoints require separate treatment. An initial zero gap uses symmetry, \(Q(0)=\tau(0)=0\), without inventing a support contact at zero. A terminal constant interval gives the heat-kernel boundary layer

\[
1-Q(s)\ge C\sqrt{S-s},
\]

whereas \(1-\tau(s)=O(S-s)\). Consequently \(G(s)<0\) near \(S\), also impossible. The source supplies the relevant regularity and terminal estimates; their use remains part of the stated analytic dependency.   

This closes the gap-exclusion argument, including initial, terminal and nondiscrete-support cases.

## 5. Turning the theorem into explicit, checkable families

The differential condition is useful only if it can be certified for concrete models. I derived two levels of certificate.

### A coefficient budget for arbitrary finite even mixtures

A sufficient condition is

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{p\ge4\\p\text{ even}}}
\frac{2p-3}{p-3}\lambda_p
\le a_0.
}
\]

This is a convex cone in coefficient space and gives an explicit budget for additional interactions.

To prove it, write \(k=p-2\), so

\[
a=a_0+\sum_k\lambda_k t^k,
\qquad
Z=\sum_k\frac{k}{k-1}\lambda_k t^k.
\]

The useful exact identity is

\[
Za''-(a')^2
=
\sum_{k<\ell}
\frac{k\ell(k-\ell)^2}{(k-1)(\ell-1)}
\lambda_k\lambda_\ell t^{k+\ell-2}
\ge0.
\]

The budget gives \(a-3Z\ge0\), and therefore

\[
aa''-3(a')^2
=
(a-3Z)a''+3\bigl[Za''-(a')^2\bigr]
\ge0.
\]

The positive cross-degree terms suggest that the budget can be improved. I pursued that rather than stopping at the first sufficient condition.

### An exact qualifying region for the \(2+4+6\) subfamily

For

\[
a(t)=a_0+bt^2+ct^4,
\qquad a_0>0,\quad b,c\ge0,
\]

the differential condition is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
2a_0b+12a_0c-10b^2-34bc-36c^2\ge0.
}
\]

Indeed, setting \(u=t^2\) gives

\[
aa''-3(a')^2
=
2a_0b+(12a_0c-10b^2)u-34bcu^2-36c^2u^3,
\]

a concave polynomial in \(u\). Its minimum on \([0,1]\) occurs at an endpoint.

For example,

\[
a_0=1,\qquad b=\frac{11}{50},\qquad c=\frac1{25}
\]

fails the simple budget:

\[
5b+3c=\frac{61}{50}>1.
\]

Nevertheless,

\[
aa''-3(a')^2\ge\frac{99}{1250}>0.
\]

Thus the second certificate genuinely enlarges the qualifying region.

For a single higher interaction, \(a=1+\lambda t^{p-2}\), the exact range for this criterion is

\[
\lambda\le\frac{p-3}{2p-3}.
\]

Normalization matters: in

\[
\xi(t)=\frac12\bigl[(1-\theta)t^2+\theta t^4\bigr],
\]

the resulting bound is \(\theta\le1/31\), not \(1/5\).

### A rejected shortcut and an exact checker

Endpoint testing does not work for general mixtures. The example

\[
a(t)=1+\frac12t^2+\frac1{200}t^{100}
\]

satisfies

\[
[aa''-3(a')^2]_{t=1}>0,
\qquad
[aa''-3(a')^2]_{t=4/5}<0.
\]

Both signs were checked exactly.

The included `certify_mixture.py` therefore tests polynomial nonnegativity on the entire interval using exact square-free factorization and real-root counting, not floating-point samples. It handles endpoint roots, even-multiplicity roots and the identically zero case.

The checker was compared against the closed-form quartic–sextic region on **289 exact rational cases**; all comparisons passed. Its rejection means only that this sufficient criterion fails—not that the model has an overlap gap or is computationally hard.

## 6. What this enables algorithmically

Conditional on the analytic dependency, the theorem supplies the strictly increasing minimizer required by the existing IAMP result. For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), it gives

\[
\Pr\!\left[
\frac{H_N(\sigma^{\mathrm{alg}})}N
\ge
\frac{\max_\sigma H_N(\sigma)}N-\varepsilon
\right]\longrightarrow1.
\]

The established real-arithmetic cost has the form

\[
C_\xi(\varepsilon)(\chi+N)+N\chi_1,
\]

where \(\chi\) is a full-gradient cost and \(\chi_1\) a multilinear coordinate-gradient cost. For dense fixed maximum degree \(p\), these scale as \(N^p\) and \(N^{p-1}\). This is not a quadratic-time guarantee for dense quartic input, nor a finite-bit implementation established here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.00904v1)

There is a further connection:

\[
aa''\ge3(a')^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(a^{-1/2})''\le0.
\]

Hence the certified covariance region also lies inside the older sufficient spherical-optimization region. That gives a common sufficient region, **not equality of the Ising and spherical optima or their algorithms**. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.00904v1)

The practical implication is therefore narrowly identifiable: near-optimal search on specified Gaussian random polynomial-energy landscapes. This investigation does not establish efficient low-temperature sampling, worst-case optimization, finite-instance optimality certificates, arbitrary neural-network training, or mixed-model QAOA convergence.

## 7. Discovery mechanisms extracted and tested

The most productive mechanism was **searching for a lemma with broader quantifiers than its headline theorem**. “Every admissible order parameter” provided a reusable object; “the SK minimizer” alone would not have supported the transfer.

A second mechanism was **transforming the constraint together with the dynamics**. Covariance time simplified the PDE, but the transformed penalty retained the model dependence. That apparent mismatch produced the new condition rather than being discarded.

The family-221 reasoning summary exposed a different mechanism. It describes failed pinning and conditional-law arguments in which shared variables can adapt to sampled sites. Its proposed repair shifts branching depths, producing fresh small factors that match the normalization of rare branching patterns, while martingale energy controls averaged shift errors. It also keeps the background law fixed before expanding perturbations. 

I tested an elementary abstraction. For a Hilbert-valued martingale with \(\mathbb E\|M_r\|^2\le1\),

\[
\sum_i\mathbb E\|M_{i+1}-M_i\|^2\le1,
\qquad
\frac1r\sum_i\mathbb E\|M_{i+1}-M_i\|\le r^{-1/2}.
\]

But conditioning on a rare event incurs

\[
\mathbb E[\|\Delta M\|\mid A]
\le
\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb E\|\Delta M\|^2}{\Pr(A)}}.
\]

Taking \(\Delta M=\mathbf1_A-\Pr(A)\) shows the problem: unconditional variance can be tiny while the conditioned increment is nearly one.

Thus averaged martingale control does **not** remove our latent-sampler obstruction. Matching the rare normalization factors is the substantive step, not an optional technical detail. The supplied LEARNING state remains unresolved. Pasted text

## 8. The next consequential connection—and the present stopping boundary

A recent paper by Fu-Hsuan Ho identifies the limiting free energy of balanced multispecies spin glasses with that of an effective scalar covariance,

\[
\xi_{\mathrm{eff}}(q)=\Xi(\lambda q),
\]

under a degree-by-degree balance condition. Its concavity theorem concerns a different functional from the covariance criterion derived here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.15432)

Accepting that external theorem, the entropy bound passes the equality to ground-state **values**. Combining it with the present scalar support criterion exposes a potentially consequential question: can one transfer the optimizer to the original multispecies instance?

That does not follow from equality of free energies. It requires an algorithmic construction and the corresponding multispecies analysis. I have retained this as an unresolved connection rather than presenting it as a corollary.

The same discipline applies to verification and priority. Pure-SK full support already has separately released prior work; that manuscript explicitly discloses AI-generated arguments and a conditional formalization, so it is not an independent replay of the analytic lemma used here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18032v1) Targeted prior-art searches did not establish novelty of the mixed-model criterion.

**The research-state change is therefore precise:** an inspected uniform plateau mechanism has been converted into a conditional mixed-model theorem, explicit coefficient mathematics, a larger certified subfamily and a reproducible exact checker. The work does not certify a quantum-PCP, commutative-PIT or unforced Navier–Stokes resolution, nor does it certify the entire release. The remaining decisive questions are the analytic dependency audit, the exact prior-art boundary, and whether the scalar mechanism can support a genuinely broader optimization theorem.
