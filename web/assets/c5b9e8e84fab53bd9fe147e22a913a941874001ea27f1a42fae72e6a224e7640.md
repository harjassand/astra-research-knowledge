   reconstruction.** Its lower bound charges total hybrid Hilbert-space
   dimension D=sum_j d_j and genuinely permits arbitrary coupled quantum
   encoders, continuous decoder outputs, and free independent classical
   seeds. No fatal counterexample was found within that interface.
2. **N30's advertised full proof is absent from the supplied mirror.** Its
   summary, historical proof locator, and reported quadratures cannot certify
   that proof. I independently reconstructed the weak upper, strong upper,
   and joint anisotropic converse instead. The reconstructed Gaussian law
   has no remaining load-bearing gap identified in this pass.
3. **A robust non-Gaussian transfer is available.** For any fixed integer
   k>=1 and a>0, known independent location noise proportional to
   exp(-a x^(2k)) has the same radius-uniform anisotropic law. A complete
   internal argument is in `generalized_location_proof.md`. The Gaussian
   special case is k=1,a=1/2; quartic noise is k=2,a=1. Historical novelty and
   independent external correctness remain unresolved.
4. **A tempting changing-geometry extension is false.** Two separated means
   {-R,+R} can be preserved through two classical labels with error
   <=Phi(-R), although their affine dimension is one. The full-box formula
   cannot be made uniform over arbitrary mean sets merely by replacing
   geometric information with radius and affine dimension.

## Exact interface and source availability

The encoder receives ONE observation X, not its unknown parameter. The
decoder must output a fresh sample whose marginal law is uniformly close in
TV to each source law. Recovering the particular X, its correlations with a
retained record, or a purification is a different requirement.

All input-dependent retained information passes through

    A=direct_sum_j M_(d_j),  D=sum_j d_j.

An independent classical seed may be shared freely, but conditioned on every
seed the dimension remains <=D. There is no free entangled receiver register,
feedback, or input-dependent side channel. The total dimension is neither
the maximum quantum branch nor the count of density-matrix entries.

## N07: decisive proof reconstruction

For a fixed bounded Gaussian mean set of affine dimension s, select s+1
affinely independent means and any strictly positive finite prior. The
posterior mean is

    m(x)=grad log sum_i pi_i exp(theta_i dot x-|theta_i|^2/2),
    Dm(x)=Cov(theta|X=x)>0.

Positivity follows because a zero-variance direction would take the same
inner product with every support point, contradicting affine independence.
Integrating the positive Hessian proves global injectivity. On any fixed
central cube C, Dm>=vI and Pr(X in C)=M>0. Change of variables bounds the
source volume of a posterior h-ball by C h^s, uniformly in its target.

At every encoded observation, sigma_x<=I. The decoder POVM's trace measure
has total mass D. Thus

    Pr(X in C, |m(Y)-m(X)|<=h)<=C_* D h^s.

Choose h proportional to D^(-1/s). A fixed positive coupling mass must have
posterior displacement >h, forcing squared posterior error >=c D^(-2/s).
The true channel Markov chain gives the exact identity

    E|theta-m(Y)|^2-E|theta-m(X)|^2
      =E|m(Y)-m(X)|^2.

Its loss is bounded after normalization, so uniform rowwise TV bounds this
regret by C epsilon. Therefore epsilon>=c_Theta D^(-2/s). The original
posterior evaluated at Y is a decision rule; the argument never incorrectly
asserts that it is the posterior of the altered experiment.

The upper's known broad Gaussian reference maps the densities to periodic
W^(2,1) functions. A nonnegative encode/decode hat kernel cancels its first
moment and has J^s labels with TV error O(J^-2). The endpoint regularity,
variance-four reference, and constant in the displayed second derivative
bound all check algebraically. No atomic-output quantizer is substituted
for the stochastic continuous decoder.

Finite hypothesis count does not give a finite sufficient observation
alphabet: the posterior varies over s continuous dimensions. Visible encoding
is a materially different model: if the encoder receives the hypothesis
itself, a finite hypothesis index gives exact finite-memory reproduction.
This is why the blind condition is load bearing.

## N30: independent reconstruction and the transfer

The exact lower and upper derived here give, for fixed s,k,a,

    c_(s,k,a) product_i max(1,sqrt(R_i/epsilon),R_i/sqrt(epsilon))
      <=D_quantum<=D_classical
      <=C_(s,k,a) product_i max(1,sqrt(R_i/epsilon),R_i/sqrt(epsilon)),

simultaneously for all nonnegative radii and all sufficiently small epsilon.
This includes the Gaussian law. Its three crucial repairs are:

* **Weak lower:** a binary prior has a central posterior scale O(R). The
  bounded proper score generated by sqrt(R^2+(p-1/2)^2) has curvature O(1/R)
  there, and gives a global divergence lower bound
  cR min(((p-q)/R)^2,1), even for remote q. Fixed squared loss misses a factor
  R. This forces a posterior neighborhood of source probability
  O(sqrt(epsilon/R)).
* **Strong lower:** use a uniform integer-grid prior within [-R,R]. Its
  predictive density is O(1/R). Two neighboring posterior coordinates remain
  bounded below on a constant-mass central set, and their log ratio identifies
  x with uniformly bounded inverse derivative. A Brier neighborhood of radius
  sqrt(epsilon) has source probability O(sqrt(epsilon)/R). For quartic noise,
  the log-ratio derivative is globally at least one; the proof is particularly
  transparent.
* **Joint lower:** the proper-score risk forces positive mass into one joint
  posterior neighborhood. Product source probabilities, followed by the
  decoder trace bound, give the PRODUCT cost. Separate scalar bounds alone
  would give only a maximum and would not suffice against a coupled quantum
  encoder.

The strong upper uses a known-range Cauchy reference. Its transformed second
derivative admits the explicit identity (8) in the proof; finite polynomial
moments of p,p',p'' make its L1 norm O((1+R)^2). Positive hats then give
O(R^2/J^2) TV without clipping a tail.

The missing weak upper is reconstructed in detail. Encode using Cauchy-coordinate
hats and decode each label from the known zero-mean reference conditional on
that hat. This preserves the zero signal exactly and is reversible. On
|x|<=c h^(-1/(2k+1)), weighted Taylor expansion has bias O(h^2), with an
integrable likelihood factor proportional to |theta|. Reversibility bounds
the complementary contribution by the shifted-versus-reference tail
difference O(|theta| exp(-c h^(-2k/(2k+1))))=O(|theta|h^2).
Thus the total weak error is O(R/J^2), uniformly in 0<R<=1. The actual kernel
does not truncate the observation.

The generalized noise theorem is proved directly. It does not rely on an
OpenAI release, an iid quantum-to-Gaussian theorem, or an asymptotic normality
comparison. It is a useful transferable scoped result, not yet evidence of a
new fundamental computational capability.

## Decisive falsification of the unsupported geometry extension

For Gaussian means theta in {-R,+R}, encode the sign of X and decode the
corresponding N(plus/minus R,1) law. Put q=Phi(-R). For the +R row the output is
(1-q)P_+ +q P_-, and vice versa. Therefore the exact row error is

    q TV(P_+,P_-)=q(1-2q)<=q.

Along any sequence R->infinity with Phi(-R)<=epsilon, D<=2. But the full-box
strong factor R/sqrt(epsilon) diverges. This is a rigorous counterexample to
a radius-uniform extension over arbitrary affinely spanning sets. It does
not contradict N07's geometry-dependent constants or N30's coordinate box.
It also explains why thin changing-radius spheres/orbits need a new analysis.

## Computational artifacts and what they establish

`kernel_diagnostic.py` is an independent implementation of the weak
reference-conditional kernel, using NumPy quadrature. It produced
`kernel_diagnostic.json`. For Gaussian mean 1e-4 it obtains:

    J=32:  TV=3.79039477e-7
    J=64:  TV=9.85197553e-8
    J=128: TV=2.48754856e-8

These agree with the supplied N30 numbers to their reported precision.
At twice the grid resolution the J=128 value changes by approximately
2.8e-14. For quartic mean 1e-4, the J=32,64,128 errors are
2.93541046e-6, 7.75763159e-7, 1.97702303e-7. Their J^2 TV/mean coefficients
approach a constant. Quartic mean 1 has a substantial pre-asymptotic regime;
the diagnostic coefficient rises from 119 at J=32 toward 515 at J=2048.
This warns against inferring constants from a few small-J fixtures.

The diagnostics use finite numerical integration cutoffs only for evaluation,
with the cutoff and unused labels explicitly recorded. They do not prove
the theorem, implement an exact continuous sampler, or settle its constants.
The proof, rather than finite agreement, carries the result.

## Prior art, exact read depth, and novelty boundary

[Hayashi and Tan](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.02542v2) already define blind
and visible approximate-sufficient-statistic compression and prove the
leading iid log-n rate. I read the definitions, TV convention, and Theorem 1;
their performance criterion is prior-averaged, and the theorem does not
specify the present joint radius/accuracy law. Their subject must not be
presented as a new discovery.

[Yang, Bai, Chiribella and Hayashi](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.03372v3)
already use QLAN Gaussian compression, hybrid memory, explicit comparison
remainders, and logarithmic parametric memory costs. Section IV's Gaussian
compression and memory accounting were inspected. These results do not
certify a full near-tracial bridge or the radius-uniform non-Gaussian theorem.

[Strasser](https://www.math4tune.com/publications/Strasser%202000,%20quantization%20Bock,%20article.pdf)
already characterizes finite experiment reductions through quantization of
standard posterior measures and convex Bayes utilities. Pages 8-13,
especially Lemma 1 and Theorems 7-8, were inspected through the web extraction.
The posterior viewpoint is therefore established prior machinery.

Weighted Durrmeyer positive approximation operators are an additional prior
art direction, including
[the arbitrary-weight multivariate paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jat.2009.11.005).
Its exact full text was inaccessible; only its primary publisher search
record was seen. This is a pointer for deeper clearance, not a theorem used
as a premise. Its overlapping weighted conditional-average structure makes
it especially important before claiming that the hat construction itself
is new. Source copies and read depths are in `sources/read_depth.json`.

No exact antecedent for the complete radius-uniform generalized-location
statement was found in this bounded search. This does not establish priority.

## Practical accounting and remaining debts

The theorem charges retained INPUT-DEPENDENT memory, including every
classical branch. Known model parameters, radii, decoder normalizations and
fresh randomness are supplied. The ideal code requires measurable stochastic
operations and fresh real-valued sampling. The normalization table and very
rare reference-conditional labels can be expensive to compute and resolve;
that preparation/runtime cost has not been bounded. Digital finite-alphabet
output has TV one against a continuous source unless a discretized output
convention is declared. The theorem does not constitute a finite-bit archive
compiler or a physical quantum implementation.

The strongest defensible mathematical consequence is an unconditional
Gaussian and generalized-Gaussian SINGLE-observation box compression law in
the stated ideal interface. The usual exact Gaussian iid sample-mean
reduction then transfers the Gaussian special case without an approximation.
Non-Gaussian iid samples do not have that exact reduction. A different
experiment with bidirectional comparison error eta acquires an additive
2eta compression-error remainder; quantum applications must retain it.
There is no growing-s uniform constant, thin changing-geometry theorem,
exact optimal constant, or unconditional quantum-to-classical bridge here.
