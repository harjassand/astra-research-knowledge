CYCLE 3 LEAF — BIOCHEMICAL INTERPRETATION AND ENDEMIC CONSEQUENCES
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally proved candidate for the stated CTMC/Foster claims; primary-literature comparison supports a cautious mechanism analogy, not application validation or novelty.

RESULT

The proposed family is an open cross-catalytic feedback model. Immigration maintains catalyst counts A_j; A_j catalyses production and removal of product/substrate B_i, while B_i catalyses autocatalytic multiplication of A_j. It is not a standard Michaelis–Menten enzyme mechanism and is not a classical hypercycle as usually defined: no enzyme-substrate intermediate is represented, B_i does not replicate itself in this reaction list, and the stoichiometry has implicit external feedstock for producing B_i and extra A_j molecules.

The strict LP condition has an exact Foster interpretation. If

  mu_j  = delta_j*ell_j - sum_i p_ji*w_i > 0,
  nu_ji = q_ji*w_i - k_ji*u_ji*ell_j > 0,

then it makes the count-linear drift negative in both A_j and active A_j B_i directions. A correction on the all-catalysts-absent face, weighted by the frozen substrate count, handles the fact that every B_i freezes there and yields a coercive drift in B_i as well. This proves nonexplosion and positive recurrence on the full count lattice, and finite stationary first moments of every species. It gives a parameter-dependent upper bound on E[B_i], not a parameter-uniform positive lower bound on P(B_i>0).

In the one-catalyst/one-substrate case, feasibility is exactly delta*q > k*p*u. This is a sufficient “loop-gain below loss” inequality, not an endemic threshold or a necessary recurrence condition. A complex-balanced subfamily gives an exact counterexample to any uniform endemic-occupancy lower bound: with k=1 and alpha=delta=q=u=v=1, p=epsilon in (0,1), the deterministic equilibrium is (A*,B*)=(1,epsilon) and the stochastic stationary law is Pois(1) tensor Pois(epsilon). Thus P(B>0)=1-exp(-epsilon) tends to zero while the strict small-gain inequality holds for every epsilon<1. Conversely, at p=2 with the other rates one, the same network is complex balanced and positive recurrent although the LP condition fails.

1. NETWORK, PROPENSITIES, AND INTERPRETATION

Let j=1,...,m index catalysts and i=1,...,n index B species. All alpha_j, delta_j, p_ji, q_ji, u_ji, v_ji are strictly positive, and k_ji is an integer at least one. For every j,i the reactions are

  0 <-> A_j                     (alpha_j forward, delta_j reverse)
  A_j <-> A_j + B_i             (p_ji forward, q_ji reverse)
  A_j + B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i (u_ji forward, v_ji reverse).

Use unit-volume standard stochastic mass action with falling factorials. At state (a,b) in N^(m+n), the forward/reverse propensities are respectively alpha_j, delta_j*a_j; p_ji*a_j, q_ji*a_j*b_i; and u_ji*a_j*b_i, v_ji*(a_j)_(k_ji+1)*b_i. Here (a)_r=a(a-1)...(a-r+1), zero for a<r.

The first catalytic pair leaves A_j unchanged and adds/removes B_i. The last pair leaves B_i unchanged and changes A_j by plus/minus k_ji. Therefore B_i has two roles: it is the product of the A_j-supported channel and a catalyst for A_j autocatalysis. Each A_j is externally replenished and degraded. This is a mathematically valid open mass-action model, but its reaction list does not encode where the additional material in 0->A_j+B_i or A_j+B_i->(k+1)A_j+B_i comes from. A chemically atom-balanced realization would need to include feedstock, waste, and likely an explicit intermediate; adding those species changes the generator and the Foster inequalities.

The graph is weakly reversible because every edge has its reverse. With all pairs present it is one linkage class. Its stoichiometric rank is m+n: 0<->A_j gives each A-coordinate direction and A_j<->A_j+B_i gives each B-coordinate direction. The entire lattice is one irreducible CTMC class. From any state, turn on one A_j if needed, remove B_i one molecule at a time via A_j+B_i->A_j, then degrade all A; conversely, immigrate catalysts, produce target B counts, and remove excess A. Every edge on these finite paths has positive rate. No conservation law or hidden class decomposition is used below.

Mechanism label. The exact structural description justified by the equations is “open, asymmetric cross-catalytic production with autocatalytic catalyst feedback.” A classical hypercycle links self-replicating species so each catalyses another member’s replication; in this family B_i itself is not replicated, and A_j has an immigration source. The experimental peptide hypercycle of Lee et al. is therefore an analogy for reciprocal catalytic feedback, not an identification. It is also not Michaelis–Menten: the classical enzyme-substrate scheme contains E+S <-> ES -> E+P, with a bound intermediate and regenerated enzyme, whereas the present network has no ES complex and the A_j+B_i feedback reaction changes catalyst copy number while leaving B_i untouched.

2. WHAT THE SMALL-GAIN LP DOES

For V0(a,b)=sum_j ell_j*a_j + sum_i w_i*b_i, the exact generator is

  LV0 = sum_j alpha_j*ell_j
        + sum_j (sum_i p_ji*w_i - delta_j*ell_j)*a_j
        + sum_ji (k_ji*u_ji*ell_j - q_ji*w_i)*a_j*b_i
        - sum_ji k_ji*v_ji*ell_j*(a_j)_(k_ji+1)*b_i.

Thus the LP margins make

  LV0 = sum_j alpha_j*ell_j - sum_j mu_j*a_j
        - sum_ji nu_ji*a_j*b_i
        - sum_ji k_ji*v_ji*ell_j*(a_j)_(k_ji+1)*b_i.

The first inequality, delta_j*ell_j > sum_i p_ji*w_i, says weighted catalyst degradation dominates all B production driven by A_j. The second, q_ji*w_i > k_ji*u_ji*ell_j, says B_i removal dominates the A_j growth caused by the B_i-catalysed autocatalytic channel, in the same weighted count. The v channel supplies an additional nonpositive high-order term. This is a diagonal weighted Lyapunov certificate for closed-loop gain below loss; the condition is strict and rate-dependent.

For m=n=1 the LP is feasible exactly when

  k*u*ell/q < w < delta*ell/p,
  equivalently delta*q > k*p*u.

The dimensionless loop ratio is g=k*p*u/(delta*q). The condition g<1 is sufficient for this particular first-order weighted Foster construction. It is not necessary: the reverse high-order term can stabilize beyond this inequality, and an exact complex-balanced counterexample appears in Section 4.

3. FOSTER PROOF AND STATIONARY MOMENT BOUND

Write alpha_tot=sum_j alpha_j, R=sum_j alpha_j*ell_j, and delta_max=max_j delta_j. Let

  K=(R+1)/alpha_tot,
  V(a,b)=1+sum_j ell_j*a_j+sum_i w_i*b_i+K*1_{a=0},

where a=0 means that all catalysts are absent. This V is proper because ell_j,w_i>0.

The indicator changes only on catalyst immigration/death. At a=0, the only enabled transitions are 0->A_j, each with V-increment ell_j-K, so LV=R-K*alpha_tot=-1, uniformly in b. At a=e_j (one copy of A_j and no other catalyst), its death creates the all-zero catalyst vector and adds K at rate delta_j. No autocatalytic reverse reaction can jump to a=0: its target retains one A_j. Hence, exactly,

  LV = R - sum_j mu_j*a_j - sum_ji nu_ji*a_j*b_i
       - sum_ji k_ji*v_ji*ell_j*(a_j)_(k_ji+1)*b_i
       + K*sum_j delta_j*1_{a=e_j} - K*alpha_tot*1_{a=0}.

Let mu_min=min_j mu_j, nu_min=min_ji nu_ji, eps0=min(mu_min,nu_min), and C=R+K*delta_max. If a=0, LV=-1. If S_A=sum_j a_j>=1 and S_B=sum_i b_i, then sum_ji a_j*b_i=S_A*S_B, so

  LV <= C - mu_min*S_A - nu_min*S_A*S_B
     <= C - eps0*(S_A+S_B).

Consequently LV<=-1 outside the finite set

  F={a!=0, S_A+S_B < ceil((C+1)/eps0)}.

All jump sizes are bounded by the finite maximum k_ji and all statewise rates are finite. Also LV<=C globally. Stopped Dynkin applied to V at its finite sublevel exit times proves nonexplosion; explosion while remaining in a finite sublevel set is impossible because the total rate there is bounded. The irreducibility argument in Section 1 plus the countable-state Foster criterion gives positive recurrence and a unique invariant probability pi on N^(m+n).

There is a stronger stationary integrability consequence. Define the nonnegative function

  f(a,b)=sum_j mu_j*a_j + sum_ji nu_ji*a_j*b_i
       + sum_ji k_ji*v_ji*ell_j*(a_j)_(k_ji+1)*b_i
       + K*alpha_tot*1_{a=0}.

The exact drift above implies LV<=C-f. For the positive recurrent chain, apply Dynkin from a fixed state, average over [0,T], pass to the invariant Cesaro law first for bounded truncations f^M=min(f,M), and then let M increase. Since V>=0, this gives

  E_pi[f] <= C.

Explicitly,

  sum_j mu_j E_pi[A_j]
  +sum_ji nu_ji E_pi[A_j*B_i]
  +sum_ji k_ji*v_ji*ell_j E_pi[(A_j)_(k_ji+1)*B_i]
  +K*alpha_tot*pi(A=0)
  <= sum_j alpha_j*ell_j + K*delta_max.                 (F)

This is a Foster-derived weighted stationary moment/occupation bound for the first, simpler corrected potential. It proves integrability of the terms needed in the coordinate generator identities. In particular, stationarity gives

  sum_j p_ji E_pi[A_j] = sum_j q_ji E_pi[A_j*B_i],    every i,

and

  delta_j E_pi[A_j] + sum_i k_ji*v_ji E_pi[(A_j)_(k_ji+1)*B_i]
      = alpha_j + sum_i k_ji*u_ji E_pi[A_j*B_i],       every j.

The certificate displayed in (F) alone directly bounds catalyst moments and catalyst-substrate mixed moments, not E_pi[B_i]. The following additional face-weighted corrector repairs that limitation and gives a first-moment substrate bound.

For each i let nu_min_i=min_j nu_ji, alpha_tot=sum_j alpha_j, and delta_max=max_j delta_j. Choose

  r_i = nu_min_i/(alpha_tot+delta_max) > 0,
  K = (R+1)/alpha_tot,
  V1(a,b)=1+sum_j ell_j*a_j+sum_i w_i*b_i
             +(K+sum_i r_i*b_i)*1_{a=0}.

This remains proper: its B_i coefficient is w_i when a!=0 and w_i+r_i when a=0. At a=0, immigration is the only enabled reaction and

  LV1 = -1 - alpha_tot*sum_i r_i*b_i.

At a!=0, the only transitions switching on the correction are deaths from a=e_j to a=0. Their added B_i drift is delta_j*r_i*b_i. Thus the net negative coefficient of b_i at a=e_j is nu_ji-delta_j*r_i. Since nu_ji>=nu_min_i and delta_j<=delta_max,

  nu_ji-delta_j*r_i >= alpha_tot*nu_min_i/(alpha_tot+delta_max) =: gamma_i.

For any other active catalyst vector, the correction does not change and sum_j nu_ji*a_j>=nu_min_i>=gamma_i. Consequently, with mu_min=min_j mu_j and C=R+K*delta_max,

  LV1 <= C - sum_j mu_j*a_j - sum_i gamma_i*b_i,
  gamma_i=alpha_tot*nu_min_i/(alpha_tot+delta_max)>0.             (F1)

Nonexplosion was already established using V, whose jumps are bounded in this reaction family; the explicit path argument gives irreducibility. The proper Foster function V1 then gives the explicit stationary first-moment bound

  sum_j mu_j*E_pi[A_j] + sum_i gamma_i*E_pi[B_i] <= C,
  hence E_pi[B_i] <= C/gamma_i for every i.                       (F2)

This is a rate- and margin-dependent upper bound; it is not a lower endemic-occupancy bound. It closes the substrate integrability gap for this family. Combining (F2) with Markov gives the corresponding upper tail estimate pi(B_i>=M)<=C/(gamma_i*M) for M>0. The derivation depends on the exact face structure: no listed reaction changes B while all A are zero, and only a one-catalyst death enters that face. A reaction that removes the last catalyst by another route would require a new boundary correction and fresh coefficient audit.

For one pair, choose ell=1 and any w in (k*u/q, delta/p), possible iff delta*q>k*p*u. Put mu=delta-pw and nu=qw-ku. Formula (F) becomes

  mu*E[A] + nu*E[A*B] + k*v*E[(A)_(k+1)*B]
    + K*alpha*pi(A=0) <= alpha + K*delta,
  K=1+1/alpha.

Using the integrable stationary coordinate equations gives additional explicit algebraic bounds. Since LB=pA-qAB, E[AB]=(p/q)E[A]. Since LA=alpha-delta*A+k*u*A*B-k*v*(A)_(k+1)*B,

  (delta-k*p*u/q)E[A] + k*v*E[(A)_(k+1)*B] = alpha.

Under delta*q>k*p*u,

  E[A] <= alpha*q/(delta*q-k*p*u),
  E[A*B] <= alpha*p/(delta*q-k*p*u),
  E[(A)_(k+1)*B] <= alpha/(k*v).

These are exact stationary bounds, not mean-field substitutions. The Foster integrability bound justifies each expectation before the generator identities are used.

4. ENDEMICITY: WHAT IS AND IS NOT UNIFORM

For any fixed admissible parameter vector, irreducibility and positive recurrence imply pi has full support, hence pi(B_i>0)>0 for every i. There is also an exact catalyst-occupation identity: because the only transitions crossing the cut A_j=0 are immigration alpha_j and death delta_j from A_j=1,

  alpha_j*pi(A_j=0) = delta_j*pi(A_j=1),
  pi(A_j>0) >= alpha_j/(alpha_j+delta_j).

That is a lower bound on catalyst presence, not on substrate presence. The B_i=0 cut also gives the exact substrate-boundary flux identity

  sum_j p_ji*E_pi[A_j*1_{B_i=0}]
      = sum_j q_ji*E_pi[A_j*1_{B_i=1}].

Only the displayed A_j<->A_j+B_i pair crosses that cut. The finite first moments from (F2) make both sides integrable. This identity ties substrate occupancy to catalyst-weighted production/removal but, without further parameter or correlation bounds, supplies no useful uniform lower bound on pi(B_i>0).

The one-pair deterministic equations are

  A' = alpha-delta*A + k*u*A*B - k*v*A^(k+1)*B,
  B' = p*A-q*A*B.

Every positive equilibrium has B*=p/q, and A* is the unique positive root of

  alpha-delta*A + k*u*(p/q)*A-k*v*(p/q)*A^(k+1)=0.

The root exists for all positive rates (the polynomial is positive at zero and tends to minus infinity); under small gain it is strictly decreasing from zero onward. At the equilibrium, the Jacobian is triangular because p-qB*=0, and its diagonal entries are

  -alpha/A* - k^2*v*(p/q)*(A*)^k < 0,     -q*A* < 0.

Thus this endemic equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable. Small gain is not needed even for this deterministic positive equilibrium in the one-pair family; it is the condition for the stated simple Foster certificate.

Exact counterexample to a uniform stochastic endemic floor. Take k=1, alpha=delta=q=u=v=1 and p=epsilon with 0<epsilon<1. The strict gain condition is 1>epsilon. The deterministic equilibrium is (A*,B*)=(1,epsilon). The reaction graph is complex balanced at c=(1,epsilon): the fluxes at 0 and A balance, the A<->A+B fluxes both equal epsilon, and the A+B<->2A+B fluxes both equal epsilon. By the product-form stationary law (also checked directly by these flux identities),

  pi_epsilon = Pois(1) tensor Pois(epsilon),
  E_pi[B]=epsilon,     pi(B>0)=1-exp(-epsilon) -> 0.

So even a globally attracting positive deterministic endemic equilibrium does not imply any positive substrate-occupancy lower bound uniform over the allowed positive rates. The global-attractor statement follows from the complex-balanced Global Attractor Theorem: this example has full stoichiometric rank, so its positive stoichiometric class is the whole positive orthant. This is not extinction at fixed epsilon: every fixed chain is irreducible and has strictly positive stationary mass at B>0.

The same obstruction is not confined to two species. Take one A and two substrates B_1,B_2, k_1=k_2=1, alpha=delta=q_i=u_i=v_i=1, and p_i=epsilon_i. For epsilon_1+epsilon_2<1/2 the strict LP is feasible with ell=1,w_1=w_2=2. The complex graph is a strongly connected tree with full stoichiometric rank three, and is detailed balanced at c=(1,epsilon_1,epsilon_2). Hence the full-lattice stationary law is Pois(1) tensor Pois(epsilon_1) tensor Pois(epsilon_2). The deterministic positive equilibrium is globally attracting in the positive orthant; yet pi(B_1>0)=1-exp(-epsilon_1) can approach zero while the LP remains strict. This gives a three-species all-endotactic, higher-product-order counterexample to any parameter-uniform stochastic endemic floor inferred from deterministic permanence.


Comparison with N77. The quantifiers differ. Here every fixed positive rate vector gives a full-rank, state-space-irreducible CTMC with one stationary law; the nonuniformity is across the parameter family epsilon_i. The N77 record (source-reported, externally unreviewed in the local card) instead keeps one all-one weakly reversible network and one deterministic linear class B+C=2, while infinitely many closed stochastic classes Gamma_n support stationary laws that escape compact sets and whose B-occupancy tends to zero. N77 is the stronger within-one-network obstruction to class-uniform stationary tightness; the present example supplies a transparent exact-product-form obstruction to parameter-uniform endemic occupancy. Neither says that a fixed irreducible chain lacks positive stationary occupancy. The limit epsilon_i->0 varies a rate toward zero and therefore leaves every single shared box kappa<=k_e<=K with kappa>0 used by N33; it does not refute that deterministic permanence candidate, or a hypothetical stochastic theorem restricted to one such fixed compact rate box.

Scientifically meaningful extension (proposed, not analyzed here). To make the feedback chemically interpretable, replace the zero-source A channel and hidden material gains with explicit feedstocks and wastes. For example, choose a monomer X whose r copies form one A molecule, and a precursor Y that can be converted to B. Add chemostat supply/removal for X and Y and use

  r X <-> A,
  A+Y <-> A+B,
  A+B+r X <-> 2A+B.

With A carrying the composition of r X and B that of Y, these transformations conserve those material units; X,Y supply/removal are explicit open-reservoir boundaries. The first catalytic pair is then A-catalysed conversion of Y to B, and B catalyses template copying of A from X. This is a nutrient-fed autocatalytic/cross-catalytic production loop, closer to a prebiotic replicator or template-copying question than to Michaelis–Menten catalysis. It is not the classical peptide hypercycle experiment, since B is not itself replicated. Lee et al. (1997) is a primary example of mutually catalysed peptide self-replication, useful as motivation for asking about sustained replicator occupancy, but it is not evidence that this precise model occurs in biology. For this displayed k=1 case, the chemostat reduction, where X and Y are fixed externally, recovers effective rate constants of the supplied family; a k>1 burst analogue would consume k r X. At finite X,Y the new resource coordinates alter propensities, boundary classes, and the small-gain proof. A meaningful next theorem would need a nutrient-dependent Foster certificate and a parameter-explicit lower bound on stationary template occupancy, not only recurrence. No such extension theorem is claimed.

The condition is not necessary for recurrence either. Set k=1 and alpha=delta=q=u=v=1, p=2. The same complex-balance equations give the product-form stationary law Pois(1) tensor Pois(2); the cited product-form and complex-balanced non-explosivity results make it a genuine invariant probability of the irreducible CTMC. Hence it is positive recurrent although delta*q=1<2=k*p*u, so no positive ell,w can satisfy the LP. The high-order reverse channel/product structure can stabilize networks outside this certificate. Thus g<1 is a sufficient Foster margin, not a reproduction-number threshold, necessary survival criterion, or exact stationary phase boundary.

5. PRIMARY LITERATURE AND PRECISE OVERLAP

Biochemical interpretation. Michaelis and Menten’s primary invertase paper models enzyme action through an enzyme-substrate complex whose concentration determines the catalytic rate; the later standard scheme is E+S <-> ES -> E+P. The current family has no such intermediate and has no explicit conserved material source for its copy-number gains. It should be described as a stylized open mass-action feedback model, not as an identified enzyme mechanism.

The nearest named organization is cross-catalysis/hypercycle. Lee, Severin, Yokobayashi and Ghadiri’s experimental 1997 Nature report describes two self-replicating peptides that catalyse one another’s production. Here the motif is asymmetric: A_j catalyses B_i production and B_i catalyses A_j autocatalysis, but B_i is not itself self-replicating and A_j is externally supplied. Gopalkrishnan’s reaction-network treatment distinguishes catalytic and autocatalytic network notions; Andersen et al. later emphasize that “autocatalysis” has several non-equivalent formal definitions. Those sources justify motif language, not the claim this exact family is a realized chemical mechanism.

Closest stochastic prior. Cappelletti, Pal Majumder and Wiuf (arXiv:1912.00401; later published as “Long-time asymptotics of stochastic reaction systems”) study a stochastic environment that is itself a CTMC and a mono-molecular child network. Their Assumption 1 restricts child reactions to zero-source production, unary conversions, and unary degradation; their ergodicity theorem additionally assumes an ergodic environment and proper production/degradation conditions with finite stationary production rates. This is a close prior for the no-feedback case where A_j form an autonomous environment and B_i are immigration-death/count species.

Most relevantly, Section 9 of that paper explicitly discusses protein-feedback gene activation: a protein catalyses G->G' and thus changes the “environment”; the authors say G,G' can no longer be treated as an autonomous environment, upper-bound the protein count by an immigration-death network to prove ergodicity, and state that more involved tools are needed to characterize the stationary distribution. The current A_j+B_i<->(k+1)A_j+B_i channel is not that gene-state-switch model: it changes catalyst copy number rather than G to G'. But it is a mathematically close, higher-order feedback extension beyond their autonomous-environment theorem. The proposed LP/Foster proof supplies a positive-recurrence route under its explicit small-gain condition and gives moment inequalities unavailable from simply importing the environment-modulated Poisson-mixture result.

The C10_S03 hinge certificate in the local Astra prior is also relevant but not overlapping in scope. Its exact admission grammar requires primitive immigration and degradation for every species, source complexes of molecularity at most two, and reciprocal quadratic removal for each catalytic birth. It handles arbitrary outward first-order birth coefficients through count thresholds, without the present strict small-gain LP. The current network has no primitive 0<->B_i flow, and its child-to-catalyst feedback source (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i has molecularity k_ji+2>=3; it also lacks an autonomous A environment. Neither theorem contains the other from the stated hypotheses. The current small-gain/Foster proof is not a restatement of C10_S03.

6. STATUS, FALSIFIERS, AND BOUNDARIES

1. Strong connectivity, weak reversibility, full rank, irreducibility, nonexplosion and positive recurrence under the strict LP inequalities: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the finite family exactly specified above and standard falling-factorial propensities.
2. Foster weighted stationary moment bounds (F), (F1), (F2), and one-pair moment consequences: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE; the proof explicitly obtains integrability before applying stationary coordinate identities.
3. One-pair positive locally stable deterministic equilibrium: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE by direct calculation; the epsilon counterexample is globally attracting by the cited complex-balanced theorem.
4. No substrate-occupancy lower bound uniform over all positive rate vectors from the LP: REFUTED by the exact complex-balanced epsilon family; fixed compact positive rate boxes are not refuted.
5. Small gain is necessary for stationary recurrence: REFUTED; the p=2 complex-balanced example violates it.
6. Exact biochemical or gene-regulatory application: UNKNOWN. The reaction list is a stylized open network; atom-balanced substrates, explicit complexes, volume scaling, and experimental parameter interpretation are absent.
7. Finite stationary first moments of every B_i under only the LP assumptions: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE by the face-weighted corrector (F1) and bound (F2). Higher substrate moments and sharper tail rates are UNKNOWN from this proof.

Falsifiers for the Foster derivation: a missing/mismatched propensity factor; a reaction changing the all-zero catalyst face other than the stated immigration/death; a reverse channel capable of removing the last catalyst; or failure of either strict margin mu_j>0 or nu_ji>0. Falsifiers for the complex-balanced illustration: any one of the displayed equilibrium flux equalities failing, which it does not for the stated parameters. No simulation was used.

Supplied inputs: the reaction list, positive rates, and strict LP weights from the parent assignment; unit-volume falling-factorial CTMC semantics. Acquired: exact generator, proper Foster corrections, class argument, moment bound, one-pair deterministic and stochastic analysis. All calculations are symbolic. No numerical fitting, simulation, or uncharged data is used. No novelty search beyond the listed primary sources and local prior was performed. Constants depend on the rates and LP margins; the proof gives no uniform mixing-time, return-time, or endemic-occupancy lower bound. Its stationary substrate tail bound is the parameter-dependent Markov estimate stated after (F2).
