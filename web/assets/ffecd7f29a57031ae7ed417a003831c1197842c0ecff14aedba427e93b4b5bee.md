# Endotactic permanence audit: weighted drift, switching, and compartments

**Status:** Internal reconstruction and exact counterexamples. I found no counterexample to the N33 weighted essential-source drift under its stated assumptions. The full N33 proof manuscript cited by the Astra card is absent from the pinned commit, so this report cannot certify that proof line by line. External correctness and priority remain unresolved.

## Interface and verdict

Fix a finite diagram yₑ → y′ₑ in d species, with source exponent yₑ ∈ Rᵈ, reaction vector νₑ = y′ₑ − yₑ, and stoichiometric span S = span{νₑ}. On the positive orthant consider

dx/dt = Σₑ kₑ(t) x(t)^yₑ νₑ, with 0 < κ ≤ kₑ(t) ≤ K < ∞,

where the coefficients are measurable and solutions are Carathéodory trajectories. Endotacticity is imposed on essential reactions for each projection w: among sources maximizing w·yₑ over reactions with w·νₑ ≠ 0, every such reaction has w·νₑ < 0. This is the essential-edge definition in pinned N33, lines 5–8. It does not require weak reversibility or strong endotacticity. Real and negative source exponents are mathematically meaningful on the positive orthant but fall outside ordinary mass-action chemistry.

The load-bearing drift estimate is correct, conditional on the finite affine-activity construction. For an active slope r outside S⊥, endotacticity at −r supplies a positive-drift essential edge at the least r-source tier. Every negative-drift source is strictly higher in that projection. The affine activity gaps make each higher source monomial at most hᴴ times the selected low-tier monomial. Uniform positive lower rates then dominate all negative projected flux:

r·fₜ(x) ≥ (2κH − KLhᴴ)xʷ ≥ κHhᴹ > 0,

where w is the selected low-tier source, L = Σₑ ||νₑ||₁, M = maxₑ ||yₑ||₁, and the class scale is chosen so KLhᴴ ≤ κH. This estimate holds for every admissible rate vector at almost every time, so measurability does not reduce the drift.

## Reconstructed proof spine

For a finite diagram, or a finite family of modes, form D from all coordinate directions, nonzero reaction vectors, and nonzero source-exponent differences. The affine lemma supplies a finite concave minimum

Fₕ(x) = minλ (rλ·x + bλ(h)),

with slopes fixed before h is chosen and with an arbitrary positive direction-dependent tolerance. Taking

E(r) = min({1/2} ∪ {|r·v|/(2||v||₁) : v ∈ D and r·v ≠ 0})

forces every active slope r at x = hᵖ in Bₕ = [h,h⁻¹]ᵈ to have the same signs as p on D and a positive finite gap H. Thus, if r·u > r·w for two source exponents, then xᵘ/xʷ = h^{p·(u−w)} ≤ hᴴ.

If r is not perpendicular to S, at least one reaction is essential for −r. Its least r-source tier is the maximum −r-source tier. Endotacticity says every essential edge there has r·ν > 0; choose one edge with source w. Since each nonzero r·ν is at least 2H in magnitude, this edge contributes at least 2κH xʷ. No negative essential edge has source on this tier, so every negative source is strictly higher. Their total contribution is at most KLhᴴ xʷ. Inside Bₕ, xʷ ≥ h^{||w||₁} ≥ hᴹ, including for negative exponents. Tangent edges and self-loops contribute zero. Keeping the factors |r·νₑ| is essential; an unweighted flux comparison alone is insufficient.

For a positive class P = (c + S) intersect Rᵈ_{>0}, choose hP small enough that every slope active at c is zero and the drift inequality holds. The plateau Kh = {Fₕ = Fₕ(c)} is compact, convex, and lies inside B√h. For x in P outside Kh, an active slope cannot lie in S⊥: that affine function is constant on P and has value at c at least Fₕ(c). Every active label therefore has projected derivative at least δₕ = κHhᴹ. Along an absolutely continuous trajectory, the finite-minimum chain rule gives nonnegative derivative of Fₕ inside Bₕ and derivative at least δₕ outside Kh. Since Fₕ is bounded on Bₕ, entry is finite. A trajectory starting in Kh stays there; compactness inside the positive orthant gives global continuation.

A scale chain hⱼ₊₁ = min(√hⱼ, hP) transfers an initial-point trap to one class-level trap. For any positive x0, choose h0 small enough that every active slope at x0 is zero; then x0 lies in Kh0. Since Khⱼ lies inside B√hⱼ, the trajectory remains inside the next scale box and enters Khⱼ₊₁ in finite time. After finitely many scales it reaches KhP. The plateaux need not be nested. The entry bound is a finite sum of [Fₕ(c) − min over Bₕ Fₕ]/(κHhᴹ) over the scale chain. It depends on x0 through the first scale but not on the rate path.

This reconstructs the mechanism but does not certify the broad candidate. The full affine construction, its tie and limiting-face cases, and the nonnested scale argument still need independent review in the missing N33 manuscript.

## Same-span measurable switching

For finitely many modes, assume each mode is all-endotactic, every mode has the same span S, and selected-mode coefficients share one positive bounded envelope. A measurable selector may choose any mode at every time. Build one affine family using all source exponents and reaction vectors in all modes. If r is outside S⊥, each mode has an essential reaction in direction r because each mode spans S. Endotacticity at −r gives the same lower drift in every selected mode, after taking common minima over the finite family. The same plateau is invariant and absorbing, with entry time independent of the switching schedule. Convex mixtures also work when their weights are nonnegative and sum to one.

The union diagram need not itself be endotactic, and inactive-mode edges need not have positive coefficients. What matters is that every selected mode supplies positive drift in every direction outside the common span. If total activation weight can vanish for an unbounded total duration, this proof only yields drift proportional to accumulated activation time.

## Finite-compartment extension (conditional theorem)

A finite-state extension follows by applying the same mechanism to a product reaction diagram. Let G = (V,E) be a finite undirected compartment graph. Each compartment has the same d species. Local reactions are selected from finitely many all-endotactic modes with one common local span S and positive bounded measurable rates. Along every undirected compartment edge, include both directions of linear exchange for each species, with positive bounded measurable exchange rates. Equal compartment volumes give the standard concentration-exchange equations.

For one global mode, local reaction arrows live in one compartment block, and each transport pair is eᵥ,ᵢ ↔ e𝑤,ᵢ. The product diagram is all-endotactic: a globally maximal essential local source is maximal among the essential sources in its local network and points inward; a globally maximal essential transport source has a lower-level reverse target and points inward. Every mode has the same total span, the sum of the per-compartment copies of S and the reversible transport span. The common-span switching argument then gives a compact absorber in every joint positive stoichiometric class, so all compartment concentrations eventually stay bounded above and away from zero.

This requires a finite graph, common local span, and positive lower and finite upper bounds on selected local and exchange rates. It covers neither irreversible transport nor an unequal-diffusion PDE, and it gives no convergence-to-equilibrium claim. This is a conditional mathematical extension for compartmentalized metabolism if the measured network passes the structural and rate-envelope checks.

## Counterexamples when premises are relaxed

**Different spans.** Mode A is A ↔ 2A with both rates 1; mode B is B ↔ 2B with both rates 1. Each mode is all-endotactic, but SA = span(eA) and SB = span(eB). If mode A is selected forever, A obeys dA/dt = A − A² while B is frozen. The union span is R², so the union positive class is the whole positive quadrant. Any compact K inside that quadrant has a positive minimum B coordinate ε. Choosing B(0) < ε prevents the trajectory from ever entering K. Thus there is no classwise absorber for all schedules and initial points when the mode spans differ.

The same obstruction holds when both modes receive positive weights but one has finite total exposure. Under (1 − e⁻ᵗ)fA + e⁻ᵗfB, mode B has only one total logistic time unit. From a sufficiently small B(0), B remains below any proposed positive lower bound forever.

**No uniform lower rate bound.** For A ↔ 2A, set k₊(t) = 1 and k₋(t) = e⁻ᵗ. The rates stay positive and bounded above, but the lower infimum is zero. The equation is dx/dt = x − e⁻ᵗx², with exact solution

x(t) = eᵗ / (t + 1/x(0)) → ∞.

So an upper rate bound alone cannot guarantee a compact absorber.

## Metabolism interface, prediction, and decisive gate

A measurable application class is a finite set of equal-volume compartments with a curated metabolite set, fixed reaction diagram, and finite enzyme-control modes. The kinetic orders and stoichiometric vectors must be fixed; every mode must pass endotacticity and share one span. Flux or concentration measurements must support lower and upper bounds on every selected-mode reaction coefficient and reversible exchange coefficient across the stated operating envelope. Feed, washout, irreversible export, and explicit enzyme shutdown must either be included in the full reaction diagram and pass the structural test, or be excluded from the claim.

**Prediction:** in each joint stoichiometric class, every positive trajectory under arbitrary measurable mode switching eventually enters one mode-independent compact interior set. Within this deterministic model, metabolite washout and unbounded accumulation are ruled out. The theorem gives neither a steady state nor a numerical εP in the available form. The affine family, offsets, class scale, and waiting-time bound must be acquired; the N33 card reports no useful size or precision bound. Finite time-series data cannot certify an all-future lower rate bound.

**Falsifier and next gate:** reconstruct the network from stoichiometry and kinetic-order estimates, certify endotacticity and common span for every allowed mode, and obtain a defensible rate envelope from condition-matched perturbation data. The decisive mathematical gate is independent review of the full N33 proof plus an explicit finite-scale certificate for a named metabolic model. Once a specific KP exists, a trajectory that exits it under the certified rates falsifies the certificate or implementation. Before that, observed long-lived washout challenges the biological model assumptions but cannot alone disprove a theorem with nonconstructive constants. This is deterministic chemistry; Astra N35 supplies only a finite-horizon stochastic exit estimate.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

- Craciun–Nazarov–Pantea, arXiv:1010.3050, Theorem 19, prove permanence for two-species endotactic κ-variable systems. Their introduction conjectures permanence for any number of species. Their rates are piecewise differentiable with uniform positive bounds. This rules out novelty claims for two species or merely time-varying positive rates.
- Gopalkrishnan–Miller–Shiu prove all-dimensional permanence for strongly endotactic networks; this is a strict subclass. Johnston–Pantea–Donnell give a MILP test for endotacticity and note the unresolved general-endotactic permanence boundary; their biochemical examples include a strongly endotactic circadian clock.
- Brunner–Craciun prove permanence for two-dimensional tropically endotactic polynomial systems with possibly time-dependent parameters. Thus arbitrary dimension, rather than nonautonomy alone, is the meaningful boundary.
- Craciun–Deshpande’s toric differential-inclusion embedding is structural work on endotactic systems, not a theorem that every all-endotactic system is permanent.
- The current first-order endotactic paper (arXiv:2409.01598 v2; 2025 version, with a 2026 publication noted on its page) characterizes first-order graphs and proves global stability for first-order endotactic linear systems. Its higher-order examples use asymptotically autonomous methods. It does not establish the full arbitrary-measurable-rate theorem. The 2026 first-order stochastic ergodicity paper (arXiv:2601.00176) concerns a different model.
- OpenAI Math OA149 is a distinct 2026 source-derived manuscript about finite weakly reversible mass-action systems. Its pinned snapshot is separate from Astra N33. OA149’s flux proof in trapping.tex lines 195–205 uses a directed return path; N33 instead uses the low essential-source witness from endotacticity at −r. The affine approximation and scale-chain method are attributed to OA149, but the all-endotactic theorem is a further claim.

If N33 survives independent review, the substantive advance would be an all-dimensional resolution of the extended permanence conjecture for finite actual-power-law diagrams with arbitrary measurable, uniformly positive bounded rates, plus common-span switching and the finite-compartment corollary above. External correctness, priority, constructive constants, and empirical applicability remain unresolved.
