# Cycle 3: polynomial phase retrieval with a Parseval-completed Gaussian block

## Verdict

A primary 2025 result supplies the missing polynomial-time statistical estimator for iid-row Poisson phase retrieval. Huang, Li, and Needell's Theorem 1 analyzes a convex lifted least-squares program (CVX-LS), then extracts its leading rank-one component. For complex isotropic sub-Gaussian row entries, it gives a uniform high-probability phase-error bound of order \(\sqrt{d/m}\) from \(m\ge Ld\) independent Gaussian-design Poisson observations. Its constants depend on the sub-Gaussian norm and fourth moment and are not numerical.

The paper does not analyze the polar-normalized frame \(B=G(G^*G)^{-1/2}\). Its rows are correlated, so substituting \(B\) for independent Gaussian rows is not justified by that theorem. A different, exact physical construction avoids this mismatch: put a *subnormalized iid-Gaussian block* inside a complete Parseval POVM and route the remaining probability to a recorded residual/no-click outcome. On the good singular-value event this produces \(m+d=O(d)\) Parseval output channels. The independent-Gaussian block has exactly the Poisson means in Theorem 1 for a deterministic rescaling of the unknown state. This yields a polynomially solvable, high-probability \(\varepsilon\)-recovery policy with \(N\eta=O(d/\varepsilon^2)\) launched-photon scaling, including all residual and loss outcomes in the budget.

For exactly \(N\) launched photons, the full record is multinomial. Conditioning the all-outcome Poissonized experiment on total count \(N\) transfers the theorem with a factor \(1/\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(N)=N\}=O(\sqrt N)\) in its failure probability. Thus the guarantee is still exponentially high for polynomial \(N\) in \(d\), including fixed-accuracy and inverse-polynomial-accuracy regimes. This does not give a dimension-exponential range of \(N\) with the same useful tail, nor does it prove a sharp expected-risk bound for all \(N\).

## Primary algorithm theorem

Huang, Li, and Needell, [*Stable Phase Retrieval: Optimal Rates in Poisson and Heavy-tailed Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00551), Theorem 1, use rows \(\varphi_k\in\mathbb C^d\) whose entries are iid with
\[
\|\varphi\|_{\psi_2}=K,\quad \mathbb E\varphi=\mathbb E\varphi^2=0,\quad
\mathbb E|\varphi|^2=1,\quad \mathbb E|\varphi|^4=1+\mu>1,
\]
and independent observations
\[
Y_k\mid \varphi_k\sim\operatorname{Pois}(|\langle\varphi_k,w\rangle|^2).
\]
For \(m\ge Ld\), they state a probability at least \(1-O(e^{-c d})\), simultaneously over signals, with constants depending only on \(K,\mu\). Their convex estimator is
\[
Z_*\in\arg\min_{Z\succeq0}
\left\|\big(\langle\varphi_k\varphi_k^*,Z\rangle\big)_{k=1}^m-Y\right\|_2,
\qquad
z_* = \sqrt{\lambda_1(Z_*)}\,u_1(Z_*).
\]
Theorem 1 gives
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z_*,w)\le C_3\max\{K,\|w\|_2^{-1}\}\sqrt{d/m}.
\]
For standard complex Gaussian entries \(\mathcal{CN}(0,1)\), the assumptions hold with \(\mathbb E|\varphi|^4=2\) (so \(\mu=1\)) and a universal finite \(K\). The theorem's constants, including \(L,c,C_3\), are implicit. The paper also gives a convex PSD least-squares estimator bound before the leading-eigenvector conversion. The CVX-LS objective is a convex SDP/conic program; this gives a polynomial-time optimization route in the usual finite-precision convex-optimization model, but not a runtime or numerical-condition guarantee for a particular solver.

## Complete Parseval POVM policy

Draw \(G\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}\) with independent standard complex-Gaussian entries and write its rows as \(g_j^*\). Choose \(m\ge16d\), increasing the fixed ratio as needed to meet the source theorem's hidden \(L\). The standard Gaussian singular-value tail gives, for a universal \(c_G>0\), an event
\[
\mathcal G=\{\lambda_{\min}(G^*G)\ge m/4,\ \lambda_{\max}(G^*G)\le4m\}
\]
with probability at least \(1-2e^{-c_Gm}\), after an inessential adjustment of constants. Set \(a=1/(4m)\). On \(\mathcal G\), define
\[
R=(I-aG^*G)^{1/2},\qquad
\widetilde B=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt a\,G\\R\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\widetilde B^*\widetilde B=I_d\), so its row effects are a complete Parseval rank-one POVM. There are \(m+d\) output ports. The first \(m\) ports are the iid-Gaussian block; the remaining \(d\) ports complete it. Alternatively, coarse-grain those remaining ports into one residual/no-click effect \(I-aG^*G\). On \(\mathcal G\), for every unit \(x\), the probability of an outcome in the Gaussian block is
\[
q_{\rm G}(x)=a\|Gx\|_2^2\in[1/16,1].
\]
Thus a constant fraction of launched photons enters the block; residual outcomes are not free or discarded from the photon ledger.

With uniform detector efficiency \(\eta_{\rm det}\in(0,1]\), scale all observed-port effects by \(\eta_{\rm det}\) and include the loss/no-click effect \((1-\eta_{\rm det})I\). Record the Gaussian-block channels, residual channels, and no-click count. If \(N\) photons are launched exactly, the full count vector is multinomial with all these probabilities. Under a Poissonized source of mean \(N\), the full output counts are independent Poisson and their total is \(\operatorname{Pois}(N)\).

The counts in the Gaussian block have means
\[
N\eta_{\rm det}a|g_j^*x|^2=|g_j^*w|^2,
\qquad w=\sqrt{N\eta_{\rm det}a}\,x.
\]
Crucially, \(w\) is a deterministic rescaling of the fixed unknown state; it does not depend on \(G\). This is an exact match to the iid-row Poisson theorem. The residual and no-click counts remain part of the physical record and source budget; the estimator may ignore them because the guarantee already holds from the Gaussian block alone.

Apply CVX-LS to \(G\) and its Gaussian-block counts, producing \(z_*\). If \(\|w\|_2\ge1/K\), the theorem gives \(\operatorname{dist}(z_*,w)\le C_3K\sqrt{d/m}\). Divide by \(s=\sqrt{N\eta_{\rm det}a}\) and normalize, so \(\widehat x=(z_*/s)/\|z_*/s\|_2=z_*/\|z_*\|_2\) when nonzero. If the phase error before normalization is below \(1/2\), the standard normalization inequality gives
\[
\boxed{d_\phi(\widehat x,x)\le4C_3K\sqrt{\frac{d}{N\eta_{\rm det}}}.}
\]
Here \(d_\phi\) is phase-invariant Euclidean distance. Therefore it suffices that
\[
N\eta_{\rm det}\ge \max\left\{\frac{4m}{K^2},\frac{16C_3^2K^2d}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}
\]
for the Poissonized experiment to recover every unit state to phase error \(\varepsilon\), with failure probability \(O(e^{-c d})+O(e^{-c_Gm})\). Since \(m=\Theta(d)\), the launched-photon scaling is \(N=O(d/(\eta_{\rm det}\varepsilon^2))\), with source-theorem constants hidden. In terms of detected photons specifically in the Gaussian block, the good-frame event guarantees at least \(N\eta_{\rm det}/16\) expected counts, so the same statement is \(O(d/(N\eta_{\rm eff}\varepsilon^2))\) with \(\eta_{\rm eff}=\eta_{\rm det}/16\), up to fixed constants.

## Exactly fixed launched count

Let \(E\) be the success event of the CVX-LS estimate formed from the first \(m\) channels. For each good frame, independent Poisson counts on *all* POVM outcomes with means \(Nq_i(x)\) condition on their total \(N\) to give exactly the multinomial law of \(N\) launched photons. Because the all-outcome Poisson total has mean \(N\),
\[
\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(N)=N\}=e^{-N}N^N/N!\asymp N^{-1/2}.
\]
The event \(E\) depends only on the Gaussian-block counts, so conditioning gives
\[
\Pr_{\rm fixed\ N}(E^c,\mathcal G)
\le \frac{\Pr_{\rm Pois}(E^c)}{\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(N)=N\}}
\le C\sqrt N\,O(e^{-c d}).
\]
Use any fixed valid fallback POVM on \(\mathcal G^c\); it adds at most \(2e^{-c_Gm}\) failure probability. Hence
\[
\Pr_{\rm fixed\ N}\{d_\phi(\widehat x,x)>\varepsilon\}
\le 2e^{-c_Gm}+C\sqrt N e^{-c d}.
\]
This is exponentially small for polynomial \(N\) in \(d\), including the usual \(N=O(d/\varepsilon^2)\) regime when \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) is at most polynomial in \(d\). It is a clean fixed-count transfer but not the sharpest possible multinomial tail; proving a direct fixed-N CVX-LS bound with no \(\sqrt N\) loss remains open here.

For a normalized output, squared phase loss is at most 4. Thus the above yields the corresponding uniform expectation bound
\[
\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathbb E d_\phi(\widehat x,x)^2
\le C\frac{d}{N\eta_{\rm det}}+4\left(2e^{-c_Gm}+C\sqrt N e^{-cd}\right),
\]
whenever the high-energy condition holds. For polynomial \(N\), the remainder is exponentially small in \(d\). This is an expected-risk bound up to that explicit remainder, not a sharp finite-constant minimax statement.

## What the result does and does not match

- **Matches:** iid Gaussian (or iid complex isotropic sub-Gaussian) measurement rows for the *selected Gaussian block*; Poisson counts at means \(|g_j^*w|^2\); a polynomially solvable convex estimator; uniform source-theorem guarantee; a complete Parseval POVM after residual completion; \(O(d)\) total channels; launched-photon accounting including residual and detector-loss outcomes.
- **Does not identify the polar-normalized frame with iid rows:** for \(B=G(G^*G)^{-1/2}\), all rows are correlated, and Huang–Li–Needell's theorem cannot be invoked merely by changing variables. The block-completion construction is a different detector policy and has a constant-rate residual/no-click port.
- **Fixed-N law:** exactly multinomial. The displayed \(\sqrt N\) conditioning factor is what justifies transfer of the Poisson theorem; treating each channel as an independent Pois variable at fixed launched N would be wrong.
- **Equal-information controls:** full multinomial maximum likelihood on the complete POVM sees all ports and no-click counts; CVX-LS uses only the Gaussian block and so has a proof guarantee, not a dominance claim over MLE. The nonconvex Poisson MLE/TWF is another model-matched control. No numerical head-to-head test is supplied.

## Calibration, finite precision, and costs

The statistical theorem assumes the exact row matrix \(G\), exact POVM completion, exact integer counts, no additive dark counts, and no saturation. The physical ledger is:

- \(N\) launched photons/copies, including residual/no-click and detector-loss outcomes;
- \(m+d=O(d)\) output ports, or \(m\) resolved Gaussian ports plus a coarse-grained residual/no-click category;
- \(md=O(d^2)\) random row coefficients and the residual completion \(R\), all requiring calibration;
- detector efficiency \(\eta_{\rm det}\), source preparation, optical network/control, residual-port handling, calibration exposures, readout, count precision, and SDP/eigensolver time.

A finite-alphabet option is allowed by Theorem 1's sub-Gaussian assumptions: independently choose each entry \(\varphi\) by \(\Pr\{\varphi=0\}=3/4\) and \(\Pr\{\varphi=2\zeta\}=1/16\) for each \(\zeta\in\{1,i,-1,-i\}\). Then \(\mathbb E\varphi=\mathbb E\varphi^2=0\), \(\mathbb E|\varphi|^2=1\), \(\mathbb E|\varphi|^4=4\) (so \(\mu=3\)), and the row coefficients are exactly from \(\{0,\pm2,\pm2i\}\). Standard sub-Gaussian singular-value bounds give the same high-probability frame-completion event for sufficiently large \(m/d\). This removes arbitrary-precision Gaussian coefficients, but it does not remove the need to calibrate the synthesized measurement and residual completion.

No quantitative robustness threshold for errors in \(G\), \(R\), background estimates, detector efficiencies, or finite-precision counts is established here. The amount of optical energy/control work to synthesize the isometry and the practical precision needed to preserve the theorem remain `UNKNOWN`. The theorem is an asymptotic statistical/optimization guarantee, not a laboratory energy advantage.

## Claims status

Established by primary theorem plus the exact POVM/count-law reduction: a polynomial convex-estimation route with high-probability \(O(d/(N\eta))\) squared phase error for this *completed Gaussian-block policy*, under stated high-energy, ideal-calibration, and count-law conditions. The fixed-N statement pays the explicit \(O(\sqrt N e^{-c d})\) failure factor. `UNKNOWN`: whether the same polynomial guarantee holds directly for the polar-normalized Gaussian Parseval frame, whether fixed-N multinomial CVX-LS admits a sharper direct proof, and robustness/physical implementation constants.
