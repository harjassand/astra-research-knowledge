N07 — Neural avalanche criticality versus common external drive
Frozen record v1 — 2026-10-09

Status and scope
----------------
Status: internally derived identifiability result and candidate experimental protocol; no new biological data, simulations, priority claim, or externally reviewed theorem. No Astra archive material was used. Literature claims below are source-reported and were checked against primary papers. The central result is a boundary on what can be learned from any fixed intervention/readout interface under severe subsampling, together with an exact local causal response certificate for rejecting a pure common-drive-only model. The certificate is deliberately not called a global criticality test.

Target
------
Determine what causal intervention and response data can distinguish internally propagated activity from activity imposed by a shared external drive when only a small subset of neurons is recorded, and state the exact additional conditions needed to infer a near-critical mode. Treat nonlinear, inhibitory, and hidden-state dynamics honestly.

1. Exact interface formulation
------------------------------
Let I be the cells or input channels an experimenter can manipulate, O the cells or signals it can record, and U_t the assigned intervention at time t. A candidate biological model M may have any hidden state X_t (including unrecorded neurons, adaptation, synaptic variables, inhibition, and external-drive state). Its experimentally accessible object is the family of finite-horizon interventional laws

  B_{I,O}(M) = { Law_M(Y_{O,0:T} | do(U_{I,0:T}=u_{0:T})) :
                  T < infinity, u_{0:T} admissible }.

External variables retain their natural law under do(U=u). The exclusion condition for a localized perturbation is that assignment changes only the intended target (plus its ensuing causal descendants), and does not directly modulate the common external drive or the optical/electrical readout. Define M ~_{I,O} M' exactly when B_{I,O}(M)=B_{I,O}(M'). No procedure that uses only this input/output interface can distinguish two members of the same equivalence class, even with unlimited computation. A property is interface-identifiable exactly when it is constant on every such class.

This definition applies to arbitrary nonlinear and stochastic dynamics. It is more informative than assuming a particular hidden-state model: it specifies the complete data object a protocol can obtain and the exact equivalence relation that limits its conclusions.

2. Exact causal response against a pure common-drive null
----------------------------------------------------------
Consider a randomized, target-specific intervention Z in {0,1} that adds a calibrated spike or small pulse to a cell i in I. Let Y_{O,1:T} be the full vector-valued response of recorded cells, preserving time and cell identity. Define the population response contrast

  R_{i->O}(T) = TV( Law(Y_{O,1:T} | do(Z=1)),
                    Law(Y_{O,1:T} | do(Z=0)) ),

where TV(P,Q)=sup_A |P(A)-Q(A)|. One may also report state-stratified versions, averaging TV contrasts over randomized pre-pulse strata; do not replace the full law by the mean total spike count.

Null H_D (pure common drive): conditional on its latent common drive and its own private noise, each recorded unit evolves without any causal path from the stimulated target; the common drive is not affected by Z. Since Z is randomized independently of that drive and is excluded from direct access to the readout, the two output laws are equal and R_{i->O}(T)=0 for every T. Thus a population-level R>0 falsifies H_D exactly. It proves that the chosen target has a causal path to at least one measured response feature. Hidden external drive may be arbitrarily strong, slow, and correlated across units; randomization prevents it from creating this treatment contrast.

Using total variation on the joint response handles excitation, inhibition, and nonlinear effects: a target can increase some cells' firing and suppress others while leaving mean population count unchanged. A mean response equal to zero is not a valid no-propagation conclusion. The observable can include the full time-resolved joint spike pattern, not only a signed sum. The population contrast is an exact property of the interventional distribution; a finite experiment only estimates it with uncertainty.

The assumptions are substantive. If light leaks onto other cells, changes sensory input, induces movement, or changes the shared drive independently of the target's neural descendants, R>0 does not isolate within-circuit propagation. Use opsin-negative/light-only and shutter controls, verify target selectivity, randomize pulse times and target identities, and monitor motion and major candidate afferent-drive channels. Feedback through a circuit after target activation is a causal descendant and counts as propagation; a direct light effect on an afferent or reporter violates the exclusion condition.

Interpretation is one-sided and local. R>0 rejects a pure common-drive-only explanation of the measured treatment response, but does not show that spontaneous avalanches were generated by that path, that the path is recurrent, or that the full network is critical. R=0 means only that no effect was detected from this target to these readouts at the tested amplitudes, states, delays, and resolution. An unobserved or unreachable path can remain.

3. Linear response theorem: only controllable and observable modes enter the response
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a local linear or linearized state-space model, write

  x_{t+1} = A x_t + B u_t + d_t + w_t,
  y_t     = C x_t + v_t,

where u is randomized, d is external drive, and the joint laws of d,w,v are invariant to u. For a one-step impulse in input column j, additive dynamics give the exact causal response

  G_k[:,j] = E[y_{t+k}|do(u_t=e_j)] - E[y_{t+k}|do(u_t=0)]
            = C A^{k-1} B e_j,      k >= 1.

The external drive drops out of the contrast. The transfer matrix is

  H(s) = C(s I-A)^{-1}B.

For a simple eigenvalue lambda of A, take right and left eigenvectors v and q with q* v=1. Near lambda,

  H(s) = (C v q* B)/(s-lambda) + a term regular at lambda.

Therefore the mode has zero response residue if it is unreachable from the manipulated channels (q*B=0), unobservable in the recorded channels (Cv=0), or both. For repeated eigenvalues the corresponding statement is made on the generalized eigenspace; the complete object is the minimal input/output realization. Only controllable-and-observable modes appear as poles of H. The minimal realization is recoverable from ideal full transfer data only up to state-space similarity, and modes outside it are not recoverable.

For a nonnegative branching matrix, its Perron eigenvalue is the usual branching threshold; for signed excitation/inhibition, stability is determined by the relevant spectrum of the linearized dynamics, and a scalar mean branching ratio can miss cancellations or oscillatory modes. The transfer result does not assume nonnegative A. It identifies a mode only when the experiment both excites it and measures it.

Exact hidden-mode counterexample. Choose visible a with |a|<1, and let

  A_c = diag(a, 1),       A_s = diag(a, r), |r|<1,
  B = (1,0)^T,             C = (1,0).

Then H_c(s)=H_s(s)=1/(s-a) for every s, while rho(A_c)=1 and rho(A_s)<1. The critical hidden coordinate can have its own unobserved drive and arbitrary hidden dynamics; it still does not change any accessible interventional law as long as it is dynamically decoupled from (or has no path to) O. The same observed avalanche-like activity can be generated in the visible coordinate by an external common drive in both models. Thus all measured avalanches, all focal-pulse response laws, and all measured correlations can agree while global internal criticality differs.

Weakly coupled version. Let

  A_e = [[a, e], [e, lambda]],  B=C^T=(1,0)^T,
  H_e(s) = (s-lambda)/((s-a)(s-lambda)-e^2).

For separated a and lambda, the near-critical pole's residue is order e^2. Its effect on a finite set of impulse-response samples is order e^2. With Gaussian response noise variance sigma^2 and n independent repetitions over horizon K, the KL divergence between two response laws whose mean impulse traces differ by delta_k is

  D_KL = n/(2 sigma^2) sum_{k=1}^K delta_k^2.

As e tends to zero this can be made arbitrarily small for any fixed finite n,K and finite sigma. Pinsker's bound gives TV <= sqrt(D_KL/2). Hence no finite-resolution, finite-trial protocol has uniform power against arbitrarily weakly coupled hidden critical modes. If a response is below measurement resolution it is not rescued by the existence of an exact nonzero coupling on paper.

More explicitly, for a fixed horizon write delta_k=e^2 q_k+o(e^2). Then D_KL = n e^4 sum(q_k^2)/(2 sigma^2)+o(n e^4). To make the two response laws have TV at least tau, Pinsker requires n >= 4 sigma^2 tau^2/(e^4 sum(q_k^2)) to leading order. This is a necessary distinguishability scale in the stated Gaussian-noise model, not a sufficient power guarantee. The epsilon-to-the-minus-four divergence makes the weak-coupling obstruction quantitative.

4. Nonlinear and inhibitory hidden-state no-go
-----------------------------------------------
The global limitation is not a linear-model artifact. Given any nonlinear model M that generates the observed data, form M' by adjoining a hidden state Z with arbitrary critical or noncritical dynamics, independent of the original state, with output map Y=g(X) and interventions acting only on X. Every law in B_{I,O}(M') equals the corresponding law in B_{I,O}(M), for every intervention sequence, but the whole biological system can have different global stability or criticality. This is an exact direct-product countermodel. Requiring physical connectivity removes exact equality only if the connecting path reaches both I and O; an arbitrarily weak path restores finite-resolution indistinguishability. Inhibition can mask a mean response or cancel an eigenmode's signed aggregate; measuring the full cell-resolved response law addresses mean cancellation but cannot reveal a hidden mode with zero interface projection.

The complete arbitrary-model answer is therefore behavioral: the experiment identifies B_{I,O}, not hidden state topology or global criticality. Global criticality is identifiable only after restricting the model class and guaranteeing that every mode relevant to the claim is reachable from I and observable at O (plus adequate excitation, temporal bandwidth, and signal-to-noise). Without such a restriction, no exact passive or interventional invariant of the accessible data can settle the global question. This is a constructive non-identifiability result, not evidence that brain networks are noncritical.

5. Smallest feasible scoped protocol and stronger protocol needed for a global claim
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For the scoped question “does a focal neural event causally recruit recorded neighbors, beyond common drive alone?”, the minimal design is a randomized target-specific single-cell spike/pulse versus matched sham, interleaved at unpredictable times, while recording the full response of at least one non-target unit. For useful inference, target and readout should be a spatially distributed set; preserve signs and time; and repeat across baseline states. Random timing independent of a slow shared drive is essential. Estimate the full interventional response distribution and its uncertainty, then test the pure-drive null. No power-law fit is required.

This is physically feasible with established all-optical methods. Ribeiro et al. (2026) targeted single L2/3 pyramidal neurons in awake mouse V1 with holographic stimulation and recorded roughly 100–300 nearby cells by two-photon imaging (22 ms frames; 100–150 trials per target in the reported setup). They estimated that the imaged field sampled about 5% of its local neighborhood and reported transient responses in non-target cells and a stimulus-response scaling exponent near 0.2–0.3. That paper already supplies a close empirical precedent for a local causal response and scaling claim. To measure millisecond-scale propagation rather than 22 ms calcium-derived activity, pair cell-specific perturbation with fast voltage imaging or simultaneous high-density extracellular recording where technically compatible. Randomized sham/light controls and exact pulse timing are required for the proposed contrast.

For a claim about near-criticality of the accessible subsystem, perturb a spanning set of input directions at several weak amplitudes, record a corresponding spanning set of cell-type- and layer-resolved outputs, estimate the full matrix-valued impulse response over horizons long enough to resolve slow modes, and verify that the inferred poles/residues are stable across operating states. Under an LTI/branching approximation, infer only the modes in the minimal realization. A global claim additionally needs a justified lower bound on coupling from every critical mode to the manipulated and recorded sets, or effective whole-population access. Under severe subsampling with no such bound, there is no finite minimal protocol that guarantees global identification.

For attributing spontaneous avalanche generation, the local-pulse protocol alone is insufficient: common drive can continue to set the natural avalanche envelope while a weak recurrent network responds to optical pulses. A stronger experiment must also intervene independently on an identified candidate external source, in a factorial design with local target pulses, while matching the baseline rate and recording the candidate afferent drive. This estimates separate local-pulse and drive interventions for that source. No finite protocol can exclude an arbitrary unmeasured common cause; the conclusion must remain conditional on coverage of candidate drives and accessible modes.

6. Adversarial checks and failure modes
---------------------------------------
• Slow common drive to conditionally independent Poisson units can generate approximate size and duration power laws with finite cutoffs. Thus power-law fits and elevated correlations do not satisfy the causal certificate.
• A causal single-cell response can coexist with externally imposed spontaneous avalanches. The causal response rejects the pure no-propagation null, not the mixed-drive alternative.
• A near-critical hidden subnetwork can be entirely disconnected from the sampled region. It changes global criticality without changing any intervention-response law.
• A near-critical mode can be weakly coupled, outside the stimulus bandwidth, or outside the recording volume. Finite response noise makes it statistically indistinguishable from a stable system at a fixed trial budget.
• Inhibitory and excitatory pathways can cancel in summed counts. Full cell-resolved response distributions avoid that specific false negative; they do not defeat hidden-mode non-identifiability.
• A zero contrast is not evidence for “only external drive” unless all relevant causal routes are excitable and observable and the experiment has power at the relevant delay and state.
• A target stimulus that directly changes sensory input, movement, broad neuromodulation, or the reporter violates the localized-intervention exclusion condition. Controls need to establish target selectivity and monitor likely side channels.
• The 2017 subsampling-scaling result derives distributional scaling for specified models; it does not by itself identify hidden causal modes in arbitrary nonlinear networks. Do not generalize its scaling collapse to the unrestricted model class.

8. Claims, conditions, and unresolved gap
------------------------------------------
Established by derivation here: (i) Under a clean randomized localized intervention, any nonzero full-law response at recorded units rules out the pure common-drive-only model with no target-to-output causal path; (ii) the response is invariant to additive exogenous drive in the linear state-space model; (iii) global criticality is not identifiable from a fixed incomplete input/output interface over unrestricted hidden-state models; (iv) in a linear realization only reachable and observable modes appear in the transfer function; and (v) arbitrarily weakly coupled near-critical modes defeat any uniform finite-resolution guarantee.

Not established: a new empirical result; a theorem that cortical activity is critical or noncritical; that every reported cortical avalanche is generated internally; that any particular experimental response exponent uniquely marks a phase transition; or that arbitrary external drive can be exhausted experimentally. The exact general experimental consequence is narrower but decisive: randomized local perturbations can certify some internal causal propagation; making a global near-criticality claim requires an explicit observability/reachability assumption or materially better neural access, and claims about avalanche origin require independently manipulating identified drives.

Best next mathematical pressure point: define the weakest biologically defensible mode-coverage condition under which a random set of optogenetic inputs/readouts has a quantifiable lower bound on the residue |C v q* B| of every near-critical mode, then derive sample-complexity bounds using the weak-coupling KL calculation. This would turn the impossibility boundary into an auditable protocol guarantee. No such lower-bound theorem is asserted in v1.
