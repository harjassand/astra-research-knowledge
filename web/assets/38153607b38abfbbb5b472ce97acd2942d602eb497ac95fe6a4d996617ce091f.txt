# SOL/B — Exact cocycle-compatible Bernoulli absorption

Research status: I have not established the required conjugacy \\(2^G\cong_G4^G\\), nor a counterexample to its existence. I can, however, establish an exact homological obstruction for the residual extension that is stronger than the finite-stage obstructions in the prompt.

The investigation also yields a precise separation between solving the cocycle equation character by character and solving it simultaneously. This identifies why the apparently natural Fourier construction does not automatically produce a measurable \\(D\\)-valued transfer.

## 1. Theorem: the residual fibre has projective dimension exactly one

Let \\(R=\mathbb F_2[G]\\), with the algebraic input as stated, and consider

\\[ 0\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow X \xrightarrow{\Theta}Y\longrightarrow0. \\]

Write \\(\widehat X\cong R\\) for the Pontryagin dual, and let

\\[ U=\widehat A,\qquad V=\widehat B,\qquad E=\operatorname{im}(I-UV). \\]

Since \\(AB=I\\), duality gives \\(VU=I\\).

Theorem 1. Under the stated hypotheses,

\\[ \boxed{ \widehat D\cong R\Big/\bigoplus\_{n\ge0}U^nE } \\]

and

\\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{pd}\_{R}(\widehat D)=1. } \\]

In particular, the infinite extension \\(X\to Y\\) has no continuous \\(G\\)-equivariant group-homomorphic section, despite having algebraically split finite truncations.

### Proof

First,

\\[ I-UV \\]

is an idempotent, and

\\[ E=\ker V. \\]

Consequently \\(E\\) is a nonzero projective \\(R\\)-module, being a direct summand of \\(R\\).

Define

\\[ E_n=U^nE,\qquad M_N=\sum\_{n=0}^{N-1}E_n. \\]

The sum is direct. Indeed, if

\\[ \sum\_{n=0}^{N}U^ne_n=0,\qquad e_n\in E, \\]

applying \\(V^N\\) gives \\(e_N=0\\), since \\(VU=I\\) and \\(VE=0\\). Descending induction establishes that all \\(e_n=0\\).

Moreover, iteration of \\(I=(I-UV)+UV\\) gives

\\[ R=M_N\oplus U^NR. \\]

Thus every finite-stage submodule \\(M_N\\) splits off from \\(R\\).

However, the full dual image of \\(\Theta\\) is

\\[ M=\bigoplus\_{n\ge0}E_n. \\]

Pontryagin duality therefore gives

\\[ \widehat D\cong R/M. \\]

Each \\(E_n\\) is nonzero and \\(R\\)-invariant. Hence \\(M\\) is not finitely generated: any finite family of elements is contained in some \\(M_N\\), and its \\(R\\)-span remains there.

If \\(R/M\\) were projective, the sequence

\\[ 0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow R \longrightarrow R/M\longrightarrow0 \\]

would split. Then \\(M\\) would be a direct summand of the cyclic module \\(R\\), and therefore finitely generated, a contradiction.

On the other hand, \\(M\\) is projective because it is a direct sum of projective modules \\(E_n\cong E\\).

We consequently have a projective resolution of length one, but no resolution of length zero. Therefore

\\[ \boxed{\operatorname{pd}\_R(\widehat D)=1.} \\]

This proves the theorem.

### A stronger quantitative consequence

The nonprojectivity is accompanied by a large extension group:

\\[ \boxed{ \left|\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(\widehat D,R)\right| =2^{\aleph_0}. } \\]

To prove this, apply \\(\operatorname{Hom}\_R(-,R)\\) to the preceding projective resolution:

\\[ \operatorname{Ext}^1_R(\widehat D,R) \cong \frac{\operatorname{Hom}\_R(M,R)} {\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}\_R(R,R)\bigr)}. \\]

Because \\(M=\bigoplus\_{n\ge0}E_n\\),

\\[ \operatorname{Hom}\_R(M,R) \cong \prod\_{n\ge0}\operatorname{Hom}\_R(E_n,R). \\]

Every factor contains at least two elements: the zero homomorphism and the nonzero inclusion \\(E_n\hookrightarrow R\\). Every factor is also countable, since \\(E_n\\) is finitely generated and \\(R\\) is countable.

The product therefore has cardinality \\(2^{\aleph_0}\\). But \\(\operatorname{Hom}\_R(R,R)\\) is countable, so quotienting by its image leaves cardinality \\(2^{\aleph_0}\\).

This completes the proof.

What this establishes. The residual fibre is not algebraically a product of binary Bernoulli shifts: such a product would have free, hence projective, Pontryagin dual. The usual automatic cocycle-solving construction for a coinduced Bernoulli fibre therefore cannot be applied directly to \\(D\\).

This is an algebraic obstruction, not a measurable nonisomorphism theorem. A nonlinear measurable trivialization remains logically possible.

## 2. The obstruction is a nonsplit pure limit, not an ordinary finite-stage failure

Theorem 1 has a further consequence:

\\[ \boxed{\widehat D\text{ is flat but nonprojective over }R.} \\]

Indeed, with the notation above,

\\[ \ker V^n=M_n, \qquad \bigcup\_{n\ge1}\ker V^n=M. \\]

Because \\(V\\) is surjective, the canonical map from the first stage of the direct system is onto, giving

\\[ \widehat D\cong \varinjlim\left( R\xrightarrow{V}R\xrightarrow{V}R\xrightarrow{V}\cdots \right). \\]

Filtered direct limits of flat modules are flat. Each term here is free, hence flat. This proves flatness.

Equivalently, the exact sequence

\\[ 0\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow R \longrightarrow\widehat D\longrightarrow0 \\]

is pure exact but nonsplit.

This explains a feature of the problem more precisely than failure of convergence: every finite subextension splits, and finite algebraic compatibility tests cannot detect the obstruction to splitting the complete extension. The global obstruction is encoded in the nonprojective direct-limit module.

## 3. Theorem: every individual dual-character orbit admits an exact cocycle solution

This result applies to the actual measurable equation (C), without assuming that the candidate Bernoulli insertion is linear.

Let \\(j:Y\to Y\\) be any measurable \\(G\\)-equivariant map and let \\(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\_G(D)\\). Define

\\[ \beta(g,y)=\alpha(g,jy)-\sigma\alpha(g,y). \\]

Then

\\[ \beta(gh,y)=g\beta(h,y)+\beta(g,hy). \\]

Equation (C) asks for a measurable \\(t:Y\to D\\) satisfying

\\[ \beta(g,y)=t(gy)-gt(y). \\]

### Character-orbit theorem

Assume, as in the prompt, that the Haar action of \\(G\\) on \\(D\\) is mixing.

For every \\(G\\)-orbit of nonzero characters in \\(\widehat D\\), the scalar equations obtained from (C) admit an exact simultaneous measurable solution on that orbit.

The obstruction can only appear when solutions on different characters must also respect character multiplication.

### Proof

Write \\(\chi\_\eta\\) for a character indexed by \\(\eta\in\widehat D\\), and put

\\[ b\_\eta(g,y)=\chi\_\eta(\beta(g,y)). \\]

If \\(t\\) exists, define \\(u\_\eta(y)=\chi\_\eta(t(y))\\). Then (C) implies

\\[ \boxed{ u\_\eta(gy)=b\_\eta(g,y)\\, u\_{g^{-1}\eta}(y). }\tag{1} \\]

Since the action on \\(D\\) is mixing, the stabilizer in \\(G\\) of any nonzero \\(\eta\in\widehat D\\) is finite. Torsion-freeness makes that stabilizer trivial.

Thus \\(G\\) acts freely on \\(\widehat D\setminus\\{0\\}\\).

Choose a representative \\(\eta_0\\) of a nonzero character orbit and set \\(u\_{\eta_0}=1\\). For each \\(g\in G\\), define

\\[ \boxed{ u\_{g^{-1}\eta_0}(y) =b\_{\eta_0}(g,y)^{-1}. }\tag{2} \\]

Because the stabilizer is trivial, this is unambiguous. The twisted cocycle identity gives

\\[ b\_{\eta_0}(gh,y) = b\_{g^{-1}\eta_0}(h,y) b\_{\eta_0}(g,hy). \\]

Substituting this into (2) verifies equation (1) for every character in the orbit.

All functions are measurable, and countability permits the equations to hold simultaneously outside one null set.

This proves the theorem.

### The unsolved compatibility condition

The functions constructed in (2) need not be the characters of a single element of \\(D\\).

For that, they must also satisfy

\\[ \boxed{ u\_{\eta+\xi}(y)=u\_\eta(y)u\_\xi(y) \quad \text{for all }\eta,\xi\in\widehat D. }\tag{3} \\]

Conversely, if a measurable family satisfies both (1) and (3), together with \\(u_0=1\\), Pontryagin duality reconstructs a unique measurable \\(t(y)\in D\\), and (C) follows exactly.

The obstruction is therefore simultaneous additive coherence across character orbits, not the scalar cocycle equation on an individual orbit.

There is an additional algebraic restriction. If \\(\widehat D\\) possessed an \\(\mathbb F_2\\)-basis permuted by \\(G\\), mixing and torsion-freeness would make its basis orbits free. It would then be a free \\(R\\)-module, contrary to Theorem 1.

Consequently, the straightforward construction—select a transfer on each freely moving basis orbit, then extend multiplicatively—cannot be implemented with a \\(G\\)-permuted basis for this particular residual fibre.

This does not establish that (3) is impossible for the cocycle arising from a suitable \\(j,\sigma\\). The functions may achieve coherence through their dependence on \\(y\\), without any equivariant algebraic basis.

## 4. Theorem: even the full \\(D\rtimes G\\) Koopman representation cannot distinguish the proposed absorption

The previous theorem exposes a multiplicative obstruction. The following result shows why a purely Hilbert-space or spectral approach cannot settle it.

Theorem 3 (relative spectral equivalence). Under the algebraic hypotheses and mixing assumption on \\(D\\), for every continuous \\(G\\)-equivariant automorphism \\(\sigma:D\to D\\), the Koopman representations associated with the actions

\\[ (d,g)\cdot x=d+gx \quad\text{on }X \\]

and

\\[ (d,g)\cdot(x_1,x_2) =(\sigma d+gx_1,gx_2) \quad\text{on }X\times X \\]

of the semidirect product \\(D\rtimes G\\) are unitarily equivalent.

This assertion does not require the conditional finite-power Bernoulli-extraction lemma.

### Proof

Let

\\[ \pi:\widehat X=R\longrightarrow\widehat D=R/M \\]

be restriction of characters to \\(D\\).

The Walsh characters form an orthonormal basis of \\(L^2(X)\\), indexed by \\(R\\). Translation by \\(D\\) assigns to a character \\(r\in R\\) the weight \\(\pi(r)\\).

For \\(L^2(X\times X)\\), the Walsh basis is indexed by \\(R\oplus R\\), with \\(D\\)-weight

\\[ \sigma^\*\pi(r_1), \\]

where \\(r_1\\) indexes the first factor.

For a nonzero weight \\(\eta\in\widehat D\\), the source weight space has a countably infinite Walsh basis indexed by

\\[ \pi^{-1}(\eta). \\]

The corresponding target space also has a countably infinite basis indexed by

\\[ \left\\{(r_1,r_2): \sigma^\*\pi(r_1)=\eta\right\\}. \\]

The action of \\(G\\) on nonzero weights is free, by mixing and torsion-freeness. We may therefore choose an arbitrary bijection between these bases at one representative of each \\(G\\)-orbit and transport it equivariantly along that orbit. This constructs a \\(D\rtimes G\\)-equivariant basis matching on every nonzero-weight sector.

For weight zero, the respective character index sets are

\\[ M,\qquad M\oplus R. \\]

Their nonzero elements have free \\(G\\)-orbits, because they consist of finitely supported group-ring elements. Both sets have countably infinitely many such orbits: \\(M\\) already contains the pairwise distinct invariant summands \\(E_n\\).

Consequently, their nonzero elements also admit a \\(G\\)-equivariant bijection. Match the zero character to the zero character.

Combining these bijections gives a Walsh-basis permutation

\\[ U:L^2(X)\longrightarrow L^2(X\times X) \\]

which sends \\(1\\) to \\(1\\) and intertwines the full \\(D\rtimes G\\) actions. Being a bijection of orthonormal bases, \\(U\\) extends to a unitary operator.

This proves the theorem.

### Why this is not a Bernoulli conjugacy

The constructed unitary preserves inner products and expectations, but need not preserve pointwise multiplication:

\\[ U(fh)\stackrel{?}{=}U(f)U(h). \\]

If its Walsh-basis permutation preserved character multiplication, it would induce an additive \\(G\\)-module isomorphism

\\[ R\cong R\oplus R, \\]

and hence a continuous algebraic \\(G\\)-conjugacy \\(X\cong X\times X\\).

That is impossible: \\(X\\) has exactly two \\(G\\)-fixed configurations, while \\(X\times X\\) has four.

Thus the spectral equivalence is exact, but necessarily nonmultiplicative.

Together, Theorems 1–3 establish a useful distinction:

- The infinite residual extension has a genuine algebraic nonsplitting obstruction.
- The individual scalar cocycle equations have exact measurable solutions.
- Even the full fibre-enriched Koopman representations agree.
- What remains is constructing a multiplicatively coherent, measurable, spatial realization of those solutions.

None of these conclusions implies that such a realization exists or is impossible.

## 5. Exact criterion for nonidentity residual automorphisms

The preceding results do not settle (C). We can nevertheless exploit the automorphisms generated by \\(A|\_D\\) more explicitly.

Let

\\[ S(y_0,y_1,\ldots)=(y_1,y_2,\ldots), \qquad L(y_0,y_1,\ldots)=(0,y_0,y_1,\ldots). \\]

The defining properties of \\(A,B,P\\) give the exact identities

\\[ \Theta A=S\Theta,\qquad \Theta B=L\Theta. \\]

For each integer \\(k\\), define

\\[ (C_k,\tau_k)= \begin{cases} (A^k,S^k),&k\ge0,\\\ (B^{-k},L^{-k}),&k<0. \end{cases} \\]

Although \\(C_k\\) need not be invertible on \\(X\\), its restriction

\\[ \sigma_k=C_k|\_D=(A|\_D)^k \\]

is a continuous \\(G\\)-automorphism of \\(D\\).

Exact lifting criterion. Fix a measurable \\(G\\)-conjugacy \\(Q=(j,z):Y\to Y\times X\\). Equation (C), with \\(\sigma=\sigma_k\\), has a measurable solution \\(t:Y\to D\\) if and only if there exists a measurable \\(G\\)-equivariant map \\(\Lambda:Y\to X\\) satisfying

\\[ \boxed{\Theta\Lambda=j-\tau_k.}\tag{4} \\]

When (4) holds, the required conjugacy is explicitly

\\[ \boxed{ F_k(x)= \left(C_kx+\Lambda(\Theta x),\\,z(\Theta x)\right). }\tag{5} \\]

### Proof and measurable inverse

Put \\(y=\Theta x\\). Equation (4) implies

\\[ \Theta\big(C_kx+\Lambda(y)\big) =\tau_k y+j(y)-\tau_k y=j(y). \\]

Thus the two output coordinates have base data \\(Q(y)\\).

On each fibre \\(\Theta^{-1}(y)=s(y)+D\\), the first coordinate in (5) acts as

\\[ s(y)+d\longmapsto C_ks(y)+\Lambda(y)+\sigma_k(d). \\]

Because \\(\sigma_k\\) is a Haar-preserving automorphism of \\(D\\), this is a Haar-preserving bijection onto \\(\Theta^{-1}(j(y))\\). Combining it with the measure-preserving base isomorphism \\(Q\\) proves that \\(F_k\\) is measure-preserving and invertible modulo null sets.

Explicitly, given \\((x',w)\\), recover

\\[ y=Q^{-1}(\Theta x',w), \\]

then define

\\[ d=\sigma_k^{-1} \bigl(x'-C_ks(y)-\Lambda(y)\bigr). \\]

The inverse is

\\[ \boxed{F_k^{-1}(x',w)=s(y)+d.} \\]

Every operation in (5) is \\(G\\)-equivariant, so \\(F_k\\) intertwines the shifts exactly modulo null sets.

Conversely, if \\(t\\) solves (C) with \\(\sigma_k\\), define

\\[ \Lambda(y)=s(jy)+t(y)-C_ks(y). \\]

Applying \\(\Theta\\) establishes (4). Substitution of (C), together with \\(C_k|\_D=\sigma_k\\), establishes equivariance of \\(\Lambda\\). This proves both directions.

For \\(k=1\\), choosing \\(j=S\\) and \\(\Lambda=0\\) reproduces the known exact conjugacy \\(X\cong_GX\times K\\). It does not extract an \\(X\\)-coordinate. To resolve the primary problem through this mechanism, one must find an insertion \\(Q:Y\cong_GY\times X\\) for which (4) actually holds.

## 6. Final determination

The established conclusions are:

| Claim                                                                                                    | Determination   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| \\(\operatorname{pd}\_R(\widehat D)=1\\), with \\(\widehat D\\) flat and nonprojective                   | Proved          |
| The infinite algebraic extension is pure exact but nonsplit                                              | Proved          |
| \\(\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(\widehat D,R)\\) has cardinality \\(2^{\aleph_0}\\)                            | Proved          |
| Every individual nonzero dual-character orbit admits an exact measurable scalar cocycle solution         | Proved          |
| The translation-enriched Koopman representations on \\(X\\) and \\(X\times X\\) are unitarily equivalent | Proved          |
| A compatible solution of (C), or an exact binary–four-symbol conjugacy                                   | Not established |

The significant obstruction uncovered here is specific: the residual extension is governed by a flat, nonprojective dual module, while its scalar cocycle equations and enriched Koopman representation fail to detect the missing multiplicative coherence. This blocks the straightforward algebraic splitting, character-orbit assembly and spectral-conjugacy approaches. It does not rule out nonlinear measurable absorption.

The published equal-entropy Bernoulli isomorphism theorem applies to every countably infinite group, but does not supply the unequal-entropy conjugacy required here.&#x20;
