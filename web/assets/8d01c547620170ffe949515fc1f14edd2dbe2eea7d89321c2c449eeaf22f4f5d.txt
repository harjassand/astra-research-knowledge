1. Exact target and result candidate

Test the finite-field specialization suggested by Bloom–Sawin–Schildkraut–Zhelezov (BSSZ), arXiv:2605.28781v1, §7.2–7.3: turn their small-characteristic function-field sum-product construction into sets in finite extension fields, with a quantitative bound on intersections with every affine dilate of every proper subfield. The resulting conditional theorem is:

There is an infinite sequence of primes m_i and sets B_i ⊂ F_{1024^{m_i}} with n_i:=|B_i|→∞ such that, for all sufficiently large i,

  Q_i^0.48 ≤ n_i ≤ Q_i^0.495,   Q_i=1024^{m_i},
  max(|B_i+B_i|, |B_i B_i|) ≤ n_i^1.906,

and for every proper subfield K ⊊ F_{Q_i}, every a∈F_{Q_i}, and every ξ∈F_{Q_i}^×,

  |B_i ∩ (a+ξK)| ≤ n_i^0.953.                         (1)

Moreover F_{1024}^× B_i=B_i and the subfield generated by B_i is all of F_{Q_i}. The field characteristic is 2. The inequality (1) is an exact affine-subfield nonconcentration statement, not the stronger |K|^{1/2} condition in Li–Roche-Newton. The theorem is conditional only on the validity of the cited BSSZ source theorem and its stated curve sequence; the specialization/evaluation and the coset bound below are independently derived here.

This is an extension-field construction, not a prime-field construction, a lower bound for expansion, an inverse theorem, or a classification of all near-extremizers. The source’s explicit finite-field theorem is instead over prime fields and gives an unspecified small saving; see §5.

2. Source theorem and exact parameter check

BSSZ, “The sum-product conjecture is false for real numbers,” arXiv:2605.28781v1 (27 May 2026), HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28781v1, §§7.2–7.3, Theorem 7.8, equations (7.18)–(7.23), especially HTML lines 592–642 and 764–807, states the following source-reported input. For square q and parameters α>√q+1, β>0, if a,b satisfy the strict bounds (7.18),(7.19), or for q a power of 2 satisfy (7.18),(7.20), there are arbitrarily large A⊂F_q((t)) with |A+A|≤|A|^a and |AA|≤|A|^b. Its proof constructs A_i⊂F_q(C_i) on curves with g_i→∞ and |C_i(F_q)|/g_i→√q−1. For q=1024, it explicitly reports a=b=1.906 at α=33.01, β=40.53 (line 807).

Take q=1024, s=√q−1=31, α=3301/100, β=4053/100. Define

  F_q(x,y)=(x+y)log_q(x+y)−x log_q x−y log_q y,
  L=log_q(1−q^{-1}),
  D=F_q(β,31)+α+62L−2.

The upper thresholds from (7.18) and the characteristic-2 replacement (7.20) are

  a_*=(α+β−1)/D,
  b_*=1+[log_q(2)/(√q+1)+F_q(2β,31)−F_q(β,31)+α+31L−1]/D.

Exact rational interval evaluation gives

  D = 38.06239901777024556466578652655…,
  a_* = 1.90581786413760069492067430404… < 1.906,
  b_* = 1.90593102000201981912476916918… < 1.906.

For reproducibility, the logs can be enclosed with the positive atanh series
ln y=2Σ_{j=0}^{49} z^{2j+1}/(2j+1)+R, z=(y−1)/(y+1), after writing each positive rational argument as 2^k y with 1≤y≤2. The remainder obeys 0≤R≤2z^101/(101(1−z²)); thus all displayed comparisons are strict rational interval comparisons, not floating-point guesses. In particular √q+1=33 in the b_* formula.

The curve construction gives the following cardinality rates. Writing N_i=|C_i(F_q)| and n_i=|A_i|, the source lower bound (from its equations (7.11),(7.14),(7.22)) is

  liminf_i (log_q n_i)/g_i ≥ ρ_-:=D=38.06239901777024556… .

An upper bound follows directly from its P,G construction: |P|=q^{d_P+1−g}(1−q^{-1})^N; each member of G is, up to a scalar in F_q^×, determined by an effective divisor of degree d_G supported on the N rational points, so |G|≤(q−1) binom(d_G+N−1,d_G); and |PG|=|P||G|/(q−1). With d_P=⌈αg⌉, d_G=⌈βg⌉, N/g→31 and the entropy estimate for the binomial coefficient, this gives

  limsup_i (log_q n_i)/g_i ≤ ρ_+:=α−1+31L+F_q(β,31)
                  =39.06676868555972676064… .

(The asymptotic binomial formula is log_q binom(d_G+N−1,d_G)=gF_q(β,31)+o(g).) Since ρ_-/79=0.48180251921228… and ρ_+/79=0.49451605931088…, both strict margins allow eventual bounds q^{0.48g_i}≤n_i≤q^{0.495g_i} whenever m_i/g_i→79.

3. Closed-point evaluation and proof of the finite-field transfer

For each curve C_i choose a prime integer m_i with m_i/(79g_i)→1; existence follows from the prime number theorem. For large i, m_i>deg(D_i), where D_i is the effective divisor of a nonzero section used to identify A_i⊂H^0(C_i,L_i) with rational functions in L(D_i), and deg D_i=d_{P_i}+d_{G_i}=(73.54+o(1))g_i.

There is a closed point v_i of degree m_i on C_i. Indeed, for prime m_i, if N_d is the number of degree-d closed points, then |C_i(F_{q^{m_i}})|−|C_i(F_q)|=m_iN_{m_i}. Weil’s bound gives this difference at least

  q^{m_i}−q−2g_i(q^{m_i/2}+q^{1/2})>0

for all sufficiently large i, because m_i∼79g_i. Thus N_{m_i}>0. Since m_i>deg D_i, v_i is not in the support of D_i.

Every h∈L(D_i) is regular at v_i and can be evaluated in its residue field k(v_i)≅F_{q^{m_i}}. This evaluation is injective on L(D_i): if nonzero h evaluates to zero, its zero divisor has degree at least deg(v_i)=m_i, while its zero divisor degree equals its pole divisor degree and is at most deg D_i<m_i, a contradiction. In particular it is injective on A_i and A_i+A_i, since both are contained in L(D_i) after the common section denominator is fixed. Let B_i be the image of A_i. Evaluation is a ring homomorphism on the local ring at v_i, so

  |B_i|=|A_i|=n_i,
  |B_i+B_i|=|A_i+A_i|≤n_i^1.906,
  |B_iB_i|≤|A_iA_i|≤n_i^1.906.

The last inequality only needs the image of A_iA_i to have cardinality no greater than its source; no injectivity on L(2D_i) is asserted. This distinction is essential. The cardinality-rate calculation above and m_i/g_i→79 prove the Q_i size interval in §1.

This transfer works because the evaluation place has degree strictly above the pole-divisor degree, but still has degree about 79g_i, below the size exponent of the ambient field. It is a specialization of the source’s function-field construction. The source’s §7.2 explicitly says a finite field of large size and small characteristic might be obtained by reducing modulo a prime of F_q(C), and says one must check distance from subfields (HTML lines 577–579). The closed-point argument below supplies such a check in a stronger affine-coset form than simple noncontainment. I did not locate this exact finite-extension-field corollary in the primary sources checked; no novelty or priority claim follows.

4. Scalar symmetry and all-proper-subfield coset bound

The source defines P as sections nonzero at every F_q-point and G as sections whose zeros are supported only on F_q-points (BSSZ §7.3, lines 597–616). Both P and G are stable under multiplication by F_q^×, so A=PG is stable under F_q^×; dividing by a fixed section and evaluating preserves this, hence F_q^×B_i=B_i.

Here is the elementary action lemma. Let F be a field, F_0⊂F a subfield, and B⊂F satisfy F_0^×B=B. If K⊂F is a subfield not containing F_0, choose c∈F_0^×\K. For any a∈F, ξ∈F^×, put C=B∩(a+ξK). The map

  C×C → B+B,       (x,y) ↦ x+cy

is injective. It lands in B+B because cB=B. If x+cy=x'+cy', then x−x'=c(y'−y). Both differences lie in ξK. If y'−y≠0, division shows c∈K, contradiction; if y'−y=0, then x−x'=0. Therefore

  |B∩(a+ξK)|²≤|B+B|.                                  (2)

Now let F=F_{q^{m_i}}, F_0=F_q, with m_i prime. If a proper K contains F_q then [K:F_q] divides m_i and is smaller than m_i, so K=F_q and every a+ξK has exactly q points. If F_q is not contained in K, (2) and the sumset bound give |B_i∩(a+ξK)|≤n_i^{1.906/2}=n_i^0.953. The remaining K=F_q case also has intersection at most q≤n_i^0.953 for all sufficiently large i. This proves (1) for every proper subfield, including those not containing the construction base field. No unsupported claim that “no proper subfield” alone suffices is used.

Also F_q(B_i)=F_{q^{m_i}} for large i: take b∈B_i nonzero; for every c∈F_q^×, cb∈B_i, so c=(cb)/b belongs to the field generated by B_i. Thus it contains F_q. Because m_i is prime, its only intermediate fields containing F_q are F_q and F_{q^{m_i}}. Since n_i>q eventually, B_i is not contained in F_q.

5. Primary-source comparison and scope

BSSZ Theorem 7.2 (same arXiv v1, §7.2, lines 571–591) is an explicit source-reported construction in prime fields F_p for each sufficiently large p, with p^{fδ}<|A|<p^δ and both growth sets ≤|A|^{2−c}; its exponent saving c is unspecified and small. The q=1024 source theorem is instead over F_q((t)); the above evaluation produces finite extensions of characteristic 2 with an explicit exponent 1.906 and a size window near Q^{1/2}. BSSZ itself flags the finite-extension direction and subfield issue, so this work is best viewed as a proposed completion of that specific technical bridge, not a newly discovered sum-product obstruction.

Li–Roche-Newton, “An improved sum-product estimate for general finite fields,” arXiv:1106.1148v1, Theorem 1.4 (https://arxiv.org/html/1106.1148, lines 80–84), assumes |A∩cG|≤|G|^{1/2} for every subfield G and c, and concludes max(|A+A|,|AA|)≫|A|^{12/11}/(log_2|A|)^{5/11}. The Katz–Shen condition quoted in the same source, Theorem 1.2 (lines 58–69), allows affine cosets but requires |A∩(cG+d)|≤max(|G|^{1/2},|A|^{18/19}). Our bound is |B∩(a+ξK)|≤n^{0.953}; it is a polynomial nonconcentration estimate but does not imply either theorem’s hypothesis in all subfield sizes (0.953>18/19≈0.94737, and it is not a |K|^{1/2} bound). Accordingly, no conflict with those lower bounds is claimed.

6. Checks, failures, and remaining gap

• The closed-point degree is prime relative to the base extension degree m_i, while the ambient field degree over F_2 is 10m_i. Hence there are proper subfields F_{2^m_i}, F_{2^{2m_i}}, F_{2^{5m_i}} not containing F_{1024}; the proof explicitly handles all of them and every other such subfield via (2). Merely saying “no proper subfield contains B_i” would have been insufficient.
• A product-evaluation collision is allowed; only an upper bound for |B_iB_i| is needed. Injectivity is proved only on L(D_i), not on L(2D_i).
• Taking m_i/g_i just above 73.54 only ensures evaluation injectivity; it does not ensure n_i<Q_i^{1/2}. Since ρ_+/.495=78.922766…<79<ρ_-/0.48=79.296665… and 2ρ_+=78.133538…<79, the choice m_i/g_i→79 gives the claimed strict sub-half window and keeps m_i>deg D_i.
• This gives a one-sided certificate for this construction, not a theorem that all sets with near-quadratic small growth arise from function fields, valuation fibres, or arithmetic group actions. The intersection lemma is elementary and does not address bad high-multiplicity incidences in arbitrary near-extremizers.
• The most immediate further check would be an independent proof audit of the BSSZ Theorem 7.8 input and its optimized characteristic-2 2-torsion estimate, followed by a search for an existing finite-field corollary of their suggestion. Even if those pass, a genuinely original result would require either (i) a stronger extension-field construction that reaches a regime incompatible with an independently established structural conjecture, or (ii) an inverse theorem characterizing near-failure through an explicit arithmetic action invariant. Neither is established here.
