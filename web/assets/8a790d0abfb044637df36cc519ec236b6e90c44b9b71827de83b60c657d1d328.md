# IFNγ–IDO1 memory in MSCs: a sharp selection gate, not a new principle

## Verdict

**Reject this candidate as a new advance.** The broad idea that priming changes the fraction of cells that respond, and the use of single-cell reporters, clonal lineage tracking, and lineage-linked chromatin profiling, already have direct primary-literature precedents. A concrete gap remains in the recent MSC/IDO1 preprint: the paper does not report the responder distribution at the start of its ten-day rest or lineage-specific growth and loss. A two-class calculation turns that missing measurement into a sharp, falsifiable feasibility threshold. This is an actionable audit and experimental discriminant, not evidence that selection explains the paper’s result or a new theorem about cells.

The high-ceiling biological question was whether the reported chromatin memory is a reversible state change within cells or an apparent population shift from differential survival/expansion. The source does not identify that distinction. No new chromatin-control capability or mechanistic result is established here.

## What the primary MSC source actually measures

Bedford et al. (2026) is a v1 bioRxiv preprint, explicitly marked not peer reviewed. In primary human mesenchymal stromal cells, the trained arm receives 50 ng/ml IFNγ for 24 hours, is washed, rests for ten days, then receives a 10-hour rechallenge. The authors report 2.3±0.1 cumulative population doublings for IFNγ-treated cultures over the rest, compared with 2.8±0.1 in untreated cultures. At 10 hours after challenge, the fraction of IDO1-positive cells is 70.2±0.9% in an untrained, single-10-hour *Stimulated* arm and 98.5±0.1% in the *Rechallenged* arm. The positive-subset mean fluorescence intensity rises from 4,834±64 to 7,961±126. Rested population-level IDO1 expression returns to undetectable levels.

The 70.2% number is **not** the latent fixed-responder fraction at the start of the trained arm’s rest: it comes from an untrained, single-10-hour challenge, whereas the trained arm had a 24-hour priming pulse. The paper also reports that IDO1 expression returns to undetectable levels after rest, so the latent response class is not directly observed then. A reporter distribution during the actual priming pulse could serve as an operational predictor for that latent class only if its relation to post-rest response is validated. Also, 2.3 population doublings are an aggregate yield, not a clone-specific proliferation or survival measurement. The IFNγ arm shows broad residual transcriptional changes at rest (1,030 genes reported different from naïve), while the separate IDO1-targeted CRISPRa arm reduces average viability to 82.8±3.2% of nontargeting control at 72 hours; CXCL11-targeted CRISPRa reports 100.4±4.3%. These values motivate lineage-resolved attrition accounting but do not identify which cells were lost.

## A conditional selection bound

Let `p` be the fraction of fixed responder-class cells at the start of rest; let `q` be the responder fraction after rechallenge; let `G` be total endpoint descendants per starting cell. If no cell changes class, the average endpoint descendant yields per starting class member must be

\[
g_R=\frac{qG}{p},\qquad g_N=\frac{(1-q)G}{1-p}.
\]

At the source-reported means, `q=0.985` and `D=2.3` doublings, so `G=2^{2.3}=4.92458`. The immutable-class model can produce the endpoint without the nonresponder class falling below replacement only if

\[
p\ge 1-G(1-q)=0.92613.
\]

Here `p` is the **latent fixed-class responder share** at the washout boundary, not necessarily the current IDO1-positive fraction after the pulse. This is the sharp measurement gate: if that fixed-class fraction is below about 92.6%, the model requires nonresponders to contribute less than one endpoint descendant per starting nonresponder on average. That means net sub-replacement yield; it does not by itself prove literal death. If `p` is above that threshold, selection remains feasible. The source does not measure `p`, and the mapping from first-pulse reporter response to latent post-rest class is not established, so current evidence cannot choose either way.

For sensitivity only, substituting the untrained 70.2% number would give `g_R≈6.91` and `g_N≈0.248`. That numerical case illustrates why using the wrong denominator would strongly imply selection; it is not a valid inference about the reported trained arm. The exact algebra and a finite arithmetic check are in [selection_bound.md](proofs/selection_bound.md) and [selection_counterexample.py](code/selection_counterexample.py).

There is also an exact no-selection alias. For any `p≤q`, let all cells have common growth `G` and convert a fraction `(q-p)/(1-p)` of fixed nonresponders into responders. The final responder fraction and total yield then match the selection model’s endpoint exactly. With `p=0.702`, this illustrative construction converts about 95% of the starting nonresponder class; again, that `p` is not measured by the source. Aggregate fractions and population doublings alone cannot identify switching versus selection.

## Why barcode snapshots do not close the gap

The same ambiguity persists inside a coarse barcode. For a barcode clone beginning with `p_b=0.5`, ending at `q_b=0.9` and having total yield `G_b=2`, immutable response-heterogeneous sublineages can give `g_R=3.6` and `g_N=0.4`. A different model gives every cell common growth 2 and converts 80% of the nonresponder sublineage. Both produce the same barcode, clone size, and endpoint fraction. Thus an endpoint increase within a barcode is not, alone, proof of within-cell state conversion. The exact counterexample is in the proof file.

This limitation is particularly relevant when clones can have sequence-dependent growth, perturbation-linked viability, or hidden epigenetic heterogeneity. Lineage barcodes report ancestry. They do not automatically report a cell’s response state before priming or every birth/death event. Endpoint scRNA/scATAC/CUT&Tag is destructive and cannot reveal that exact same cell’s earlier chromatin state.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

The broad selection-versus-response-frequency idea is not new. Siwek et al. (2020) report in an IFNγ/GBP5 system that IFNγ caused no selective proliferation or lethality and that a larger fraction of cells expressing GBP5 on reinduction explained most of the bulk memory; they used single-cell RNA-seq, an endogenous EGFP reporter, flow cytometry, sorting, and rechallenge. Mikulski et al. (2025) use live GBP1-GFP reporter imaging and chromatin assays, reporting about 30% reporter-positive cells on priming and over 90% on reinduction after a week. These are close primary precedents for a response-frequency shift with epigenetic memory.

For clonal multiomics, Nagaraja et al. (2026) introduce SHARE-TRACE to profile gene expression, chromatin accessibility, and clonal history in single cells and report clonally inherited, heterogeneous epigenetic memory in colitis-derived stem cells. Jindal et al. (2024) establish CellTag-multi for barcode capture across scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq, including direct reprogramming. These works preempt the generic proposal to combine lineage barcodes and chromatin profiles. They do not supply the missing Bedford-specific responder fraction or establish full same-cell birth/death histories for this MSC protocol.

Therefore the residual gate is narrowly source-specific: **does the 24-hour IFNγ priming arm begin the ten-day rest with enough latent responders that the 98.5% rechallenge fraction could result from fixed-class selection under the measured net growth, and how do exact lineages change?** The answer is `UNKNOWN` from the inspected source and cannot be manufactured from the 70.2% first-time challenge arm.

## Decisive experiment

Use the exact 24-hour priming / ten-day rest / 10-hour rechallenge protocol. First, install and validate a live endogenous IDO1 transcription reporter against endogenous RNA/protein and the published flow gate. Record the single-cell reporter distribution at 10 hours and immediately before washout, so the actual training arm’s first-response distribution is measured rather than borrowed from another schedule. Establish whether that pulse-time readout predicts each tracked lineage’s post-rest response; absent that link, the latent `p` remains unmeasured and the binary fixed-class bound is not empirically instantiated. Then track every founder and descendant through the rest and rechallenge, with neutral lineage identity plus continuous capture of divisions, death, detachment, field exit, and tracking failure. Add endpoint flow and barcode-linked chromatin/transcriptome assays as matched destructive readouts. Compare state trajectories and clone weights under the same acquisition and filtering protocol.

The selection-only model is challenged if initially low-response cells that remain tracked generate higher-response descendants after rechallenge and complete lineage counts show that the observed bulk fraction cannot be explained by differential expansion or loss. That establishes a within-lineage reporter response change, not necessarily H3K27me3 causality. A causal chromatin claim still needs targeted perturbation and rescue with matched viability and lineage accounting. Because CUT&Tag/CUT&RUN destroys the measured cell, endpoint mark assays can be linked to a lineage or sibling descendants but cannot directly report that same cell’s earlier mark.

The practical discriminant is not another endpoint multiome snapshot. It is a validated first-pulse single-cell readout plus complete pedigree, birth/death, and second-pulse response trajectories. The experiment has not been run here; cost, throughput, reporter perturbation, and the needed sample size remain `UNKNOWN` pending pilot measurements.
