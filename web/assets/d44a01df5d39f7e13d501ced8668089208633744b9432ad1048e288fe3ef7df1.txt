1. Interface and theorem

Let N>=1, 0<epsilon<=1/2 be a supplied rational, and assume

  N >= 40 ln(16/epsilon).                                      (1)

Write

  f_k = exp(-(k-N/2)^2/N),       Z = sum_(k=0)^N f_k,
  B_(N,k)(p) = binom(N,k) p^k (1-p)^(N-k),

and let |D_k> be the normalized N-qubit Dicke vector

  |D_k> = binom(N,k)^(-1/2) sum_(x in {0,1}^N: |x|=k) |x>.

The target is the trace-one operator supported on the symmetric subspace

  rho_N = Z^(-1) sum_(k=0)^N f_k |D_k><D_k|.                    (2)

Import the Cycle 3 rational positive-mixture output: q=ceil((5N-1)/2)
rational atoms p_i in (0,1), positive rational weights mu_i, and

  g_k = sum_(i=1)^q mu_i B_(N,k)(p_i),
  |g_k-f_k| <= (epsilon/4) f_k for every k.                    (3)

Set G=sum_i mu_i=sum_k g_k and lambda_i=mu_i/G. The equality for G follows
from sum_k B_(N,k)(p)=1. All these quantities and the lambda_i are positive
rationals; G is exactly nonzero.

For every epsilon satisfying (1), there is an explicitly describable
ensemble of q(N+1)=Theta(N^2) normalized pure product states, with positive
rational weights and local amplitudes/phases in Q(i), whose density matrix
rho_rat has no support outside the symmetric subspace and satisfies

  (1/2)||rho_rat-rho_N||_1 <= 11 epsilon/14 < epsilon.          (4)

The product records have the form

  |Psi_(i,j)> = (a_i |0> + b_i z^j |1>)^(tensor N),
  weight(i,j) = lambda_i/(N+1),                             (5)

where a_i,b_i are positive rationals with a_i^2+b_i^2=1, and z is one
rational complex number with |z|=1. Thus every output product vector and
every ensemble weight has an exact finite rational description (the phase
coordinates lie in Q(i)); no square-root or cyclotomic field is needed in
the emitted rational ensemble. The rationalized phases mean (5) is an
approximation, not an exactly twirled Dicke-diagonal operator in general.

There is also an exact intermediate identity: replacing the rational phase
powers z^j by the exact roots xi^j, xi=exp(2 pi i/(N+1)), gives precisely
the Dicke-diagonal operator with sector probabilities
sum_i lambda_i B_(N,k)(b_i^2). The complete statement and proof follow.

2. Exact N+1 phase-twirl identity and multiplicity check

For any a,b>=0 with a^2+b^2=1 and any unit phase zeta,

  (a|0>+b zeta|1>)^(tensor N)
    = sum_(k=0)^N sqrt(B_(N,k)(b^2)) zeta^k |D_k>.             (6)

This follows by grouping the computational-basis expansion by Hamming
weight k; the sqrt(binomial coefficient) is part of the normalized Dicke
vector coefficient. Let xi=exp(2 pi i/(N+1)). For k,l in {0,...,N},

  (1/(N+1)) sum_(j=0)^N xi^(j(k-l)) = 1 if k=l, and 0 otherwise. (7)

Indeed, if k!=l then 0<|k-l|<=N, so xi^(k-l)!=1 and the finite geometric
sum is zero. Substituting (6) in the uniform phase average therefore gives
the exact operator identity

  Phi_N(p) := (1/(N+1)) sum_(j=0)^N
       |psi(p,xi^j)>^(tensor N) <psi(p,xi^j)|^(tensor N)
     = sum_(k=0)^N B_(N,k)(p) |D_k><D_k|,                     (8)

where |psi(p,zeta)>=sqrt(1-p)|0>+sqrt(p)zeta|1>.
The sector coefficient is B_(N,k), including binom(N,k). This is the
probability of observing count k in N Bernoulli(p) trials, and is the
eigenvalue on the one-dimensional Dicke sector, not a per-string
computational-basis diagonal entry.

For the target (2), each computational string x of weight k has diagonal
entry f_k/[Z binom(N,k)], while two strings x,y of the same weight k have
matrix entry f_k/[Z binom(N,k)]. The sector eigenvalue is f_k/Z. Thus
dividing by binom(N,k) is necessary when converting the Dicke projector
to its computational entries. The exact script below checks this
distinction over Q(i) at N=3 and witnesses the mismatch for k=1,2.

3. Exact rational-amplitude intermediate mixture and normalization

First use (8) with any rational atom p_i and its exact square-root local
amplitudes. Define

  sigma = sum_i lambda_i Phi_N(p_i)
        = sum_k (g_k/G)|D_k><D_k|.                            (9)

The normalization transfer is stable without evaluating Z. Put eta=
epsilon/4, u_k=g_k/f_k, and ubar=G/Z. Since (3) gives
u_k in [1-eta,1+eta] and ubar=sum_k(f_k/Z)u_k is their convex average,
ubar lies in the same interval. Therefore

  | (g_k/G)/(f_k/Z) - 1 |
       = |u_k/ubar-1| <= 2 eta/(1-eta).                       (10)

For two states diagonal in the orthonormal Dicke basis, trace distance is
one half the l1 distance of their sector probability vectors. Equations
(9)-(10) imply

  D(sigma,rho_N) <= eta/(1-eta) = epsilon/(4-epsilon)
                  <= 2 epsilon/7,                            (11)

where the last inequality uses epsilon<=1/2. Positivity is exact:
f_k>0, g_k>0, G>0, Z>0, and sum_i lambda_i=1. The irrational partition
function Z is not computed by the construction. The rational normalizer G
is computed exactly, with G/Z in [1-eta,1+eta].

4. Rational local amplitudes

For each rational p_i in (0,1), choose a dyadic rational s_i in (0,1) and
set

  a_i=(1-s_i^2)/(1+s_i^2),
  b_i=2s_i/(1+s_i^2),
  p_i'=b_i^2=4s_i^2/(1+s_i^2)^2.                            (12)

These are exact rationals, a_i,b_i>0, and a_i^2+b_i^2=1. The function
phi(s)=4s^2/(1+s^2)^2 maps [0,1] increasingly onto [0,1] and

  phi'(s)=8s(1-s^2)/(1+s^2)^3,       0<=phi'(s)<=8.           (13)

Run exact rational bisection on phi(s)=p_i for

  delta_A=epsilon/(4N).                                       (14)

After B_A=ceil(log_2(4/delta_A))=O(log(N/epsilon)) bisections, take the
midpoint of the remaining dyadic interval. Its distance from the inverse
image of p_i is at most 2^(-B_A-1), so (13) gives
|p_i'-p_i|<=4*2^(-B_A)<=delta_A. If equality is encountered, keep the
exact rational midpoint and stop. The returned s_i is strictly interior
because it is a midpoint of an interval inside [0,1] containing the
interior inverse image. Each comparison is exact rational arithmetic; no
ill-conditioned square-root evaluation is used.

Let

  sigma_A = sum_i lambda_i Phi_N(p_i')
          = sum_k [sum_i lambda_i B_(N,k)(p_i')]|D_k><D_k|.   (15)

For each atom, the count law B_(N,.) is the pushforward of N independent
Bernoulli bits. Couple a Bernoulli(p_i) and a Bernoulli(p_i') bit using the
same uniform random variable; its mismatch probability is |p_i-p_i'|.
The probability any of the N bits mismatches is at most N|p_i-p_i'|.
Total variation cannot increase under the count map, hence

  D(Phi_N(p_i),Phi_N(p_i')) <= N|p_i-p_i'| <= epsilon/4.

Convexity of trace distance yields

  D(sigma,sigma_A)<=epsilon/4.                                (16)

This is a global trace-distance bound; it does not claim relative error
for every rare Dicke sector after amplitude rounding.

5. Rational phases and fully rational product vectors

For N=1 the exact phases {1,-1} are rational; use them directly. For N>=2
let

  xi=exp(2 pi i/(N+1)),
  t_*=tan(pi/(N+1)),
  z_*=(1-t_*^2+2i t_*)/(1+t_*^2)=xi.

Choose a rational t with |t-t_*|<=delta_phi_t, where

  delta_phi_t=epsilon/(4N^2),
  z=(1-t^2+2i t)/(1+t^2),
  z_j=z^j,       j=0,...,N.                                  (17)

The rational parametrization is exactly unit norm: |z|=1 and z_j lies in
Q(i). Its derivative as a complex curve satisfies
|d z(t)/dt|=2/(1+t^2)<=2. Since both z and xi are unit modulus,

  |z_j-xi^j| <= j|z-xi| <= 2j|t-t_*| <= epsilon/(2N).       (18)

For a fixed rational-amplitude atom, the trace distance between the two
one-qubit pure states with phases z_j and xi^j is exactly

  a_i b_i |z_j-xi^j| <= |z_j-xi^j|/2.

For N tensor copies the trace distance is at most N times the one-qubit
distance, by telescoping the tensor-product density matrices. Thus it is
at most epsilon/4 for each (i,j). Averaging over i,j gives

  D(rho_rat,sigma_A)<=epsilon/4,                               (19)

where

  rho_rat = sum_(i=1)^q sum_(j=0)^N lambda_i/(N+1)
       |(a_i|0>+b_i z^j|1>)^(tensor N)>
       <(a_i|0>+b_i z^j|1>)^(tensor N)|.                     (20)

All ensemble weights are strictly positive rationals and sum exactly to
one. Each factor is exactly normalized. Therefore rho_rat is a trace-one
positive operator with no postselection or approximate renormalization.
Every product vector is in the symmetric subspace, so rho_rat has exactly
zero support outside that subspace. The rational phase perturbation need
not cancel off-diagonal entries between different Dicke vectors; no exact
zero claim is made for those entries. Combining (11), (16), and (19) by
the triangle inequality proves (4):

  D(rho_rat,rho_N) <= epsilon/(4-epsilon)+epsilon/2
                  <= (2/7+1/2)epsilon = 11epsilon/14.

To obtain t with the stated precision using finite rational arithmetic,
put x=pi/(N+1), so 0<x<=pi/3. Use Machin's identity
pi=16 atan(1/5)-4 atan(1/239), and truncate the alternating rational
atan series until a rational x0 obeys |x0-x|<=delta_phi_t/1000. The
alternating remainder bound gives this with O(log(N/epsilon)) terms.
At x0, evaluate rational Taylor polynomials S,C for sin,cos with absolute
errors at most delta_phi_t/1000; since x0<1.1, degree O(log(N/epsilon))
suffices by the alternating Taylor remainder bound. On the segment from
x to x0, cos is greater than 0.49 and sec^2 is less than 5; at this
precision C>1/3. The ratio perturbation |S/C-tan(x0)| is at most
10 delta_phi_t/1000, and |tan(x0)-tan(x)| is at most
5 delta_phi_t/1000. Round S/C to a dyadic mesh of step at most
delta_phi_t/4. The total error is below delta_phi_t. All comparisons,
series, and rounding can be done over Q. A direct Machin/Taylor implementation uses polynomially many bit
operations and polynomial intermediate bit lengths in
log(N/epsilon)+L_epsilon. The rational phase base z has O(log(N/epsilon))
bits per real/imaginary coordinate; z^j has O(N log(N/epsilon)) bits if
expanded as a reduced or unreduced rational pair. The map t->z has bounded
condition number, and the power j costs the explicit factor j in (18).

6. Finite-bit resource and conditioning ledger

Input and precision. The theorem takes epsilon as a rational with encoded
length L_epsilon. The Cycle 3 atoms/weights are also supplied rational
inputs. Let B_mu be the Cycle 3 upper bound for their individual bit
length, and let L_lambda be a bound for each normalized lambda_i. A safe
choice is L_lambda=O(q B_mu), since a common denominator can be formed by
multiplying the q rational denominators. Cycle 3 records

  B_exp = O(N(N+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon)
              log(N+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon)),
  B_c = O(B_exp+N^2 log N),
  B_root = O(N^2+log(1/epsilon)+L_epsilon),
  B_mu = O(N B_c+N^2 B_root+q B_root+q^2),

as conservative coefficient/root/support bounds. These are polynomial in
N and the encoded precision. They include the exponentially conditioned
signed Lagrange coefficient evaluation and the rational Gauss-root/weight
rounding. In Cycle 3 the signed coefficient condition ratio is bounded by
exp(8N), and 16N binary guard bits are spent in addition to the requested
precision. Its stated schoolbook costs include

  O(N^2 B_c^2+N(J+log N)B_exp^2)       coefficient/exponential phase,
  O(q^3 B_root(q B_root+q^2)^2)         root isolation,
  O(qN B_mu^2)                          atom-density evaluation,
  O(q^2 B_mu^2)                         exact normalization.

The coefficient-conditioning and Gauss-root separation bounds are
inherited from Cycle 3, not independently audited here. This report adds no
new transcendental coefficient input.

Amplitude precision and conditioning. For each of q atoms, exact
bisection needs B_A=ceil(log_2(16N/epsilon)) steps, with comparisons on
O(B_A+L_p) bit rationals where L_p is the atom bit length. Equation (13)
is a global Lipschitz bound, so the amplitude conversion has no endpoint
blow-up. Schoolbook rational arithmetic gives a polynomial bit cost; a
coarse bound is O(q B_A (B_A+L_p)^2), in addition to writing q pairs of
O(B_A) bits.

Phase precision and conditioning. The required t precision is
epsilon/(4N^2), i.e. B_phi=O(log(N/epsilon)) bits. The stereographic map
has derivative at most 2, and taking j-th powers amplifies phase error by
at most j<=N, exactly as charged in (18). Rational pi approximation,
Taylor evaluation, and phase-power generation are polynomial in
N+L_epsilon. With exact schoolbook arithmetic, forming all N powers by
successive multiplication, while retaining rational numerators and
denominators of O(N B_phi) bits, costs O(N^3 B_phi^2) bit operations as a
simple conservative bound. On-demand power generation takes at most N
such multiplications for one label. The construction never asks for a
small denominator inversion beyond positive rational 1+s_i^2 and 1+t^2.

Normalization and positivity. The rational atom weights are normalized
exactly by G>0. The conditioning interval G/Z in [1-epsilon/4,
1+epsilon/4] implies (10)-(11); there is no cancellation in the final
normalizer. Every a_i,b_i is positive, |z|=1, and every product state has
norm exactly one. Every lambda_i/(N+1)>0, and their sum is exactly one.
The exact intermediate twirl has every Dicke-sector eigenvalue positive.
For the phase-perturbed rational ensemble, support outside the symmetric
subspace remains exactly zero, but off-diagonal Dicke entries generally
do not vanish exactly.

Field extension. The exact N+1 twirl uses Q(xi), with degree
phi(N+1) over Q; individual phase-twirled product vectors also contain
square roots unless their amplitudes are rationalized. The resulting exact
twirled operator in (8), when p is rational, has rational Dicke
coefficients. The fully rational approximation (20) instead uses only
Q(i), degree 2 over Q, for vector entries and rational weights. The
price of removing the cyclotomic extension is the explicit epsilon/4
trace-distance term (19).

Label sampling. The exact ensemble law factors as lambda_i times the
uniform law on j. Clear denominators of the rational lambda_i to obtain
positive integer weights U_i with sum S; the bit length of S is
polynomial (at most a product-denominator bound O(q B_mu)). To sample i
exactly from rational weights using fair bits, draw L=ceil(log_2 S) bits
for U uniform in {0,...,2^L-1}; reject U>=S and otherwise use cumulative
integer weights U_i. The acceptance probability exceeds 1/2, so this is
exact, terminates almost surely, and uses fewer than 2L random bits in
expectation per accepted sample (fewer than 2 attempts in expectation). Sample j
similarly by rejection from ceil(log_2(N+1)) bits. Arithmetic and expected
time are polynomial in the encoded denominators. Worst-case random-bit
use is unbounded. This is unavoidable for a fixed-budget fair-bit sampler
in general: with at most B fair bits every output probability is dyadic
with denominator dividing 2^B, whereas a general rational lambda_i/(N+1)
need not be dyadic.

If a hard random-bit cap is required, cap each rejection loop after R
attempts and output a fixed fallback label on failure. Each loop then has
failure probability at most 2^(-R), and the induced label-law total
variation from the exact law is at most 2^(1-R). Choosing
R>=ceil(log_2(2/tau)) adds at most tau to the state trace-distance bound.
For example tau=epsilon/8 preserves a total bound below epsilon. This
bounded sampler is approximate; it is not the exact law.

Output size. M=q(N+1)=Theta(N^2) is the number of pure product records.
An explicit list storing each product state as its repeated local factor
(a_i,b_i,z^j,N) and its rational weight costs

  O(qN [L_lambda+B_A+N B_phi+log N])

bits, plus the polynomial cost of the Cycle 3 atom table. This counts a
flattened rational phase z^j of O(N B_phi) bits per record. If all N
identical local factors are written separately, multiply the factor-data
part by N. A succinct generator stores the q atom/weight table, the one
rational phase base z, and a pair of indices (i,j), and generates each
record on demand; its stored data are

  O(q(L_lambda+B_A)+B_phi+log N)

bits, with O(N) rational multiplications to generate z^j. A shared phase
table for all j costs O(N^2 B_phi) bits. Expanding a product vector into
2^N amplitudes or rho_rat into a full 2^N by 2^N matrix is exponential and
is not claimed. The symmetric-sector matrix has only (N+1)^2 entries,
but no downstream AP-time algorithm is inferred here.

7. Exact evidence, source boundaries, and limitations

The exact script `check_exact_twirl.py` constructs a two-atom N=3 ensemble
with rational Pythagorean local amplitudes and phases {1,i,-1,-i}; using
Fractions over Q(i), it checks all 64 matrix entries against the exact
Dicke formula, normalization, strict positivity of every sector, and the
per-string/binomial-multiplicity conversion. Its receipt is
`check_exact_twirl.json`. It checks only a small exact instance.

The theorem is conditional on the Cycle 3 scalar finite-support result and
its parameter regime. It is a trace-distance approximation to one
normalized symmetric-sector Dicke-diagonal state. It does not prove:

  * exact finite rational product decomposition of rho_N;
  * a relative-error guarantee for each sector after the amplitude/phase
    perturbations (only the scalar Cycle 3 approximation has that form);
  * approximation to the full computational-basis diagonal operator with
    per-string weights f_|x|; that object has exponentially many basis
    directions and is different from (2);
  * a decomposition of a full all-Schur-sector Gibbs state or a claim
    about all multiplicity spaces;
  * an AP algorithm, AP-hardness transfer, sampling/estimation runtime for
    a downstream observable, or external novelty/priority;
  * a stronger general separability theorem or a separability
    impossibility result.

The N117 full-separability and N123 regularized compiler cards are separate
source-reported routes with wider interfaces; this construction neither
reproves nor contradicts them. The exact source/provenance boundary is
