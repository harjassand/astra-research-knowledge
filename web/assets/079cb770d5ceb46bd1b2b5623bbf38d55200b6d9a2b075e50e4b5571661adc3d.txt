2. WHAT THE TRACE PROOF DOES AND DOES NOT PROVE
===============================================

Let k have characteristic p and let e=sum_g r_g g be a finite-support
idempotent in kG. For a conjugacy class C, define

    T_C(x) = sum_{g in C} coefficient_g(x).

Each T_C is cyclic because gh and hg are conjugate. In characteristic p,
cyclicity gives T_C(x^p)=T_C(x)^p by grouping words in the pth-power expansion
into cyclic-rotation orbits; nonconstant orbits have size p and vanish. The
iterated Formanek argument, under the no-p-torsion/no-p-power-self-conjugacy
condition, forces T_C(e)=0 for every nonidentity class C. Since augmentation
of an idempotent is 0 or 1, it then follows that

    tau(e) = coefficient_1(e) = augmentation(e) in {0,1}.

There is no further step in this calculation that reconstructs e from those
class sums. For the hyperbolic group application, stable translation length
shows that a nontrivial infinite-order element cannot be conjugate to a proper
p-power, so this geometric hypothesis is available; it does not supply a
positive characteristic trace-faithfulness principle.

The finite control F2[C3] illustrates why tau(e) alone is not faithful:
e=x+x^2 is a proper idempotent with tau(e)=augmentation(e)=0. This group has
3-torsion and x is conjugate to x^(2^2)=x, so it violates the cited hypotheses.
It refutes only the generic implication “tau(e) in {0,1} implies e=0 or 1”; it
does not refute Formanek's conditional statement. Exact verification is in
`verify_cycle2.py` and `CHECKS.json`.

The old bicyclic algebra control in
`outputs/research/sol_idempotent_conflict/RESULT.txt`, §6, separately shows
that even integral K0(kG)-type information plus augmentation does not force
projective cancellation in a general ring: k<a,b | ab=1> has K0 isomorphic to
Z via augmentation and a proper idempotent e=1-ba with [eT]=0. It is not a group
algebra and is not a counterexample to BLR.

3. A VALID REPLACEMENT DETECTOR: RESIDUALLY p GROUPS
===================================================

The following is an internal complete proof, not an attribution or novelty
claim.

Theorem. If char(k)=p and G is residually a finite p-group, then kG has no
idempotents other than 0 and 1.

Proof. First let P be a finite p-group and J=ker(kP -> k) its augmentation
ideal. We prove J is nilpotent by induction on |P|. For nontrivial P choose a
central subgroup <z> of order p. The central element z-1 satisfies
(z-1)^p=z^p-1=0. The quotient kP/(z-1) is k[P/<z>]. By induction the image of
J in this quotient is nilpotent, so J^m is contained in (z-1) for some m.
Centrality and (z-1)^p=0 give J^(mp)=0. The trivial group is the base case.

Now suppose e in kG is idempotent and epsilon(e)=0. Because G is residually
p, every nonidentity difference g h^{-1} from the finite support of e can be
separated by a homomorphism to a finite p-group. Taking the product of these
finitely many quotients gives one finite p-group quotient that is injective on
supp(e). Its induced group-algebra map sends e to a nonzero idempotent with
augmentation zero. Thus the image lies in the nilpotent augmentation ideal of
the finite p-group algebra. A nilpotent ideal has no nonzero idempotent, a
contradiction. If epsilon(e)=1 and e is not 1, apply the same argument to the
nonzero idempotent 1-e, whose augmentation is zero. Therefore e is 0 or 1. ∎

This detector is genuinely different from coefficient-of-identity trace and
K0 augmentation: it preserves a supplied finite support injectively in a
finite quotient and then uses localness. It covers free groups and any
residually-p hyperbolic group, but not arbitrary hyperbolic groups. It does
not cover the N129 witness if the source card's finite-quotient-killing
property for c is valid: c!=1 is then invisible to every finite quotient, so G
cannot be residually p. No quotient argument was transferred backward.

4. EXACT K4 INPUTS FOR THE TWO LOCAL SURGERIES
=============================================

Graph. Take the simple K4 with vertices 0,1,2,3 and properly edge-colour its
edges by

    colour a: 01, 23;    colour b: 02, 13;    colour c: 03, 12.

Orient each edge from its smaller endpoint to its larger endpoint and map it
to the correspondingly named edge of the rose on a,b,c. Each vertex has one
outgoing direction of each colour, so this is an immersion. K4 has girth
L=3. An exact BFS on the 12 directed-edge states checks every one of the 144
ordered first/last-edge pairs and finds a noncancelling cyclic closure of at
most 3 added edges. Thus D0=1 is a valid closure bound for these local tests.

Distinct disks. Let e be 0->1 (label a), P be the path 1->2->3->0 (word caC),
and Q be 0->3->2->1 (word cAC), the reverse path to P. Then eP and e^-1 Q are
cyclically immersed graph loops with words acaC and AcAC. They use the same
underlying edge e in opposite directions, giving a legal same-edge band. The
spliced loop is

    P Q = caCcAC  ->free reduction 1.

The two inner lengths drop from 4+4 to 0 after tightening. To keep a nonempty
fixed exterior, use the triangle 0->1->2->0, whose word is w=acB, and an inner
cap with reverse word bCA. Pair their three inverse occurrences by three
noncrossing bands. The exact rotation systems have (V,E,F)=(2,4,4) for the
two surgical disks and (2,3,3) for the outer/cap component; both are planar.
Nest the first component in a face of the second. Total inner length changes
from H=11 to H=3, while the exterior remains exactly w=acB.

Same disk. With the same edge e, let

    P = triangle 1->2->3->1 = caB,
    Q = triangle 0->3->2->0 = cAB,
    gamma = e P e^-1 Q = acaBAcAB.

The loop gamma is cyclically immersed, and its first e and e^-1 occurrences
are the same underlying graph edge in opposite directions. Add two inner
triangular caps U=P^-1=bAC and V=Q^-1=baC. Pair the P positions of gamma to U,
the Q positions to V, and pair e to e^-1 by the same-disk band. Every pair has
inverse labels. The exact rotation system has (V,E,F)=(3,7,6); its six face
lengths are 3,3,2,2,2,2, so it is an orientable planar map with no monogon.
The face cycle D:0, D:5, V:0 is on the Q side of the selected band. Nest the
outer/cap component in that face, putting the formal exterior on the Q side.
The surgery retains the Q-side, caps its new graph boundary by Q, and retains
V and the outer/cap. H changes from 8+3+3+3=17 to 3+3+3=9; w=acB is unchanged.

For the combined maps, nesting one planar component inside a face of the
other gives total (V,E,F)=(4,7,6) in the distinct case and (5,10,8) in the
self-paired case. In both cases V-E+F=3=1+c for c=2 connected components,
which is the sphere Euler relation. The full endpoints, rotations, pairings,
face cycles, path checks, and closure BFS are replayed by
`verify_cycle2.py`; output is `CHECKS.json`.

These are exact local pictures, not complete N129 theorem inputs. In
particular, the required absence of every bounded almost-paired closed system
was not checked and is not claimed. The diagrams contain easily cancellable
same-edge pairs, so they are deliberately nonminimal: they test that the
surgery removes them while preserving the outer boundary, not that a minimal
diagram can retain such a pair. They test neither the random-graph acquisition
nor the global exclusion.
