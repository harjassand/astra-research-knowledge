COMMON GLOBAL-SCORE CERTIFICATE SEARCH — d=65 SELECTED GATE

Verdict

I tested one explicit common-certificate architecture: a single positive-
semidefinite Hilbert–Schmidt operator majorant on the full traceless matrix
space, with no rank-one/purity-ideal multipliers. This architecture cannot
certify the selected gate, for any finite N>=2 and any choice of the local
unitaries. The failure is exact and structural: the other untouched tensor
leg contributes an identity shift of k=130 on each one-leg traceless sector.
It does NOT imply failure of the global purity bound on pure inputs. Those
inputs occupy a constrained conjugate-product/Veronese subset and have
small-weight local marginal components. This leaves rank-one-aware SOS,
nonbacktracking, and other product-vector certificates open. No such
certificate or finite/materializable channel is produced here.

Exact interface and target

There are r=2 factors, d=65 free generators per factor, k=2d=130 inverse-
closed branches per factor, and K=2k=260 total branches. The physical
one-use input is H=C^N tensor C^N, so n=N^2. Let U_(j,i) in U(N), with
W_(1,i^+)=U_(1,i) tensor I, W_(1,i^-)=U_(1,i)^* tensor I and similarly
for leg 2. Define the unnormalized random-unitary superoperator

  S(X)=sum_(s in S) W_s X W_s^*.

For a pure input rho, Tr(S(rho)^2)=K^2/n+||S(rho-I/n)||_HS^2, since S is
unital, trace-preserving up to factor K, and self-adjoint in HS inner
product. The selected score target Gamma<=C+s, C^2=50, s=0.165, would follow
from the sufficient full-space certificate

  ||S|_(M_n^0)||_(2->2)^2 <= Lambda_N,
  Lambda_N := K+(C+s)^2-K^2/n.

At the selected parameters, Lambda_N<312.361 and sqrt(Lambda_N)<17.674.
This is only a sufficient relaxation: the actual requirement is the same
bound on S(rho-I/n) for pure rho, not on every traceless matrix.

Finite-dimensional no-go for the full-HS SOS architecture

Write the local HS spaces as M_N=C I_N direct-sum M_N^0. On leg 1 define

  T_1(X)=sum_(i=1)^65 [U_(1,i) X U_(1,i)^* + U_(1,i)^* X U_(1,i)].

It is self-adjoint on M_N^0 because inverse branches are paired. The trace
of Ad_U on M_N is |Tr U|^2, and the identity direction contributes 1, so

  Tr_(M_N^0)(T_1)=2 sum_i (|Tr U_(1,i)|^2-1) >= -130=-k.

The real eigenvalues of T_1 on its D=N^2-1 dimensional traceless space
therefore have a maximum eigenvalue lambda_max>=-k/D. For every traceless
X on leg 1, the leg-2 branches act as identity on X tensor I_N. In
particular, if X is a top eigenvector of T_1,

  S(X tensor I_N)=(T_1(X)+kX) tensor I_N
  =(k+lambda_max)(X tensor I_N).

The vector X tensor I_N is globally traceless, and hence

  ||S|_(M_(N^2)^0)||_(2->2) >= k(1-1/(N^2-1)).

For every N>=2 this is at least 260/3=86.666..., whose square is
7511.111..., already over 24 times Lambda_N. At the selected enormous N the
lower bound tends to 130 and its square to 16,900. Therefore the single
full-space PSD inequality Lambda_N I-S^*S >=0 on traceless matrices is
impossible for this branch architecture, independent of Haar randomness or
materialization. The same conclusion holds for the equivalent unweighted
quadratic SOS that asks for nonnegativity on the entire traceless HS sphere.

Why this is not a purity counterexample

The witness X tensor I_N is a high-rank operator, not rho-I/n for a pure
state rho. For a pure rho on the bipartite input, write rho_A and rho_B for
its reduced states, A=rho_A-I/N, B=rho_B-I/N, and

  rho-I/N^2 = A tensor I_N/N + I_N/N tensor B + C,

where C is orthogonal to both local sectors. The coefficients on the local
identity sectors include 1/N; their squared HS weights are ||A||_HS^2/N
and ||B||_HS^2/N. The full-space norm obstruction above is concentrated on
these sectors, where arbitrary traceless X tensor I_N is allowed without
that pure-state weight. Removing or discounting these directions requires
using rank-one/Schmidt constraints; it cannot be inferred from the full
operator norm. Consequently, this result proves no negative lower bound on
the actual purity score and does not falsify the d=65 net-based gate.

A viable common certificate must retain the pure-state constraint. One
concrete next architecture is a sector-weighted SOS after the orthogonal

local identity sectors are relaxed. Any such certificate must explicitly

on the full doubled-space operator is not enough. Whether a fixed-degree
