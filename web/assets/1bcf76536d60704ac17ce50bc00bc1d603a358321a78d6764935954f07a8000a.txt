1. Proposed finite legal interface

Let a dark spectator have stationary square-root entry s0=1 before common
normalization; leaf entries are r_i>0. On the leaf block choose W=|u><1|,
u_i in {+1,-1}. Then W s W*=A0 |u><u|, W* s W=A0 |1><1|,
A0=sum_i r_i. Seek transported generator
 H(X)=A X+X A* - W X W*,
with spectator drift/noise zero. Both stationarity equations are
 A s+s A*=W s W*, A* s+s A=W* s W.
For distinct leaf entries, solving their two linear equations suggests
 A_ij=A0 (r_i-r_j u_i u_j)/(r_i^2-r_j^2),
 Re A_ii=A0/(2r_i), Im A_ii=0.
Same-phase entries are symmetric A0/(r_i+r_j), opposite-phase entries are
antisymmetric A0/(r_i-r_j). The finite physical Lindblad orientation,
Hamiltonian, normalization and channel legality MUST be checked directly.

Crucial degeneracy caveat: opposite-phase leaves cannot have exactly equal
r_i, since the two stationary equations would contradict there. Thus any
paired-grid limit must use distinct grids separated by a nonzero offset,
then take the offset limit only in the algebraic forms. The drift can blow
up as the offset tends to zero. Any finite upper construction must first
fix a finite generator, then choose primitive rate and faithful nonlinear
perturbation. No uniform norm/gap/bit-cost claim is intended.

For dark-leaf tangents normalize Y_i=sqrt(r_i) z_i, with z real. The
stationary entropy and power forms involve two different stationary
weights. For positive HEIS L, Gamma(X)=sXs, H=Gamma^(1/2)L Gamma^(-1/2),
the physical entropy J_L uses H*, whereas the p->1 KT pairing uses
J_(LSharp)/2, with LSharp=Gamma^-1 L_* Gamma. The root symmetrized energy
is the same for H and H*. Opposite-phase signs may swap under this reversal;
the smallest eigenvalue of a two-phase symmetric block is unaffected.
This orientation still requires exact verification in the proposed limit.

2. Prospective entropy limit

Write r_i=R exp(ell_i), log R->infinity, with finite fixed ell_i. Restrict
to sum_i z_i=0 to cancel the leading rank-one log R and mean-log terms.
Let h=(ell_i-ell_j)/2. Root's formal expansion gives
 J2/A0 kernel: -h tanh(h) on same-phase pairs,
               -h coth(h) on opposite-phase pairs;
 E2/A0 kernel: (1/2)sech(h) on same-phase pairs, zero opposite.
The diagonal value of the first same-phase kernel is zero. The opposite
kernel tends -1 as h->0, but such coincident opposite leaves are only a
limit of distinct finite legal leaves, as noted above.

On two continuous copies of the log-coordinate line the proposed Fourier
symbols, away from possible zero-frequency polynomial distributions, are
 mu_tanh(xi)=pi^2 cosh(pi xi)/sinh(pi xi)^2,
 mu_coth(xi)=pi^2/sinh(pi xi)^2,
 e(xi)=pi sech(pi xi).
The phase-antisymmetric mode removes the common singular distribution and
has prospective ratio
 (mu_tanh-mu_coth)/e = pi cosh(pi xi)/(cosh(pi xi)+1).
Its infimum is pi/2 as xi->0. Thus this FAMILY may yield finite upper
examples approaching J/E=pi/2. It does NOT establish any universal lower
bound, nor rule out another family with ratio approaching zero.

Required gates: exact finite stationary form with correct H versus H*,
the limit and diagonal terms, Fourier constants/distributions, approximation
by compactly supported real functions and then distinct finite node sums,
and actual faithful primitive nonlinear realization with every cost charged.

3. Prospective fixed finite-p profile

For r=1/p in(0,1), t=1-2r, fix p first and then let log R->infinity. The
same formal stationary power calculation suggests
 T_r/A0 kernel: (1/2)cosh(t h)/cosh(h) on same phases,
               plus or minus (1/2)sinh(t h)/sinh(h) on opposite phases.
The energy kernel is as above. The opposite sign depends on forward/reverse
orientation and does not affect the smaller eigenvalue of the phase block.

Writing alpha=pi t/2 and c=cos(alpha)>0, the proposed two Fourier eigenvalue
comparison is
 min_eigen(T_r symbol)/e(xi)
       = c cosh(pi xi)/(cosh(pi xi)+abs(sin(alpha))).
Its infimum at xi0 equals
 c/(1+abs(sin(alpha)))=tan[(pi/2)min(r,1-r)].
Since Ep=T_r/[2(1-r)], the suggested attainable physical upper threshold is
 cp_nonrev_candidate=tan[(pi/2)min(1/p,1-1/p)]/[2(1-1/p)].
It equals1 at p2 and has Ep/E limit pi/4 as p decreases to1, corresponding
to entropy threshold pi/2. At large p it is asymptotic pi/(4p).

This is a ROOT-SUPPLIED PROPOSAL, not established sharpness or a universal
inequality. The p->1 and log R->infinity limits do not automatically commute;
the separate zero-sum entropy limit above needs its own proof. No worker's
first independent counterexample depends on these suggestions.
