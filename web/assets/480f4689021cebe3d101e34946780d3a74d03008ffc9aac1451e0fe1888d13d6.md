# Cycle 2: exact stationary control for bounded birth–death queues

## Model and result

For a finite-cap queue (N\in\{0,\ldots,M\}), a stationary Markov controller selects birth rates \(\lambda_n\in[0,B]\) for (n<M); births at (M) are blocked. Deaths occur at rate \(\gamma n\). Write \(\pi_n\) for a stationary distribution and (m=\sum_n n\pi_n\).

Stationary flow across the edge (n\leftrightarrow n+1) gives the necessary and sufficient feasibility conditions

\[
\pi_n\ge0,\qquad \sum_{n=0}^M\pi_n=1,\qquad
\gamma(n+1)\pi_{n+1}\le B\pi_n\quad(0\le n<M).
\]

Given such a distribution, set \(\lambda_n=\gamma(n+1)\pi_{n+1}/\pi_n\) when \(\pi_n>0\). If \(\pi_n=0\), the inequalities force all later probabilities to be zero, and the unused rates can be set to zero. The average birth rate is therefore exactly \(\sum_n\pi_n\lambda_n=\gamma m\).

Consequently, the sharp minimum dropout probability and the variance endpoints are finite linear programs:

\[
D_*(m,h)=\min_{\pi}\sum_{n<h}\pi_n,
\qquad
V_-(m)=\min_{\pi}\left(\sum_n n^2\pi_n-m^2\right),\quad
V_+(m)=\max_{\pi}\left(\sum_n n^2\pi_n-m^2\right),
\]

with the displayed flow inequalities and the additional mean constraint \(\sum_n n\pi_n=m\). This is an exact characterization, including sharpness, rather than a relaxation. The attainable variance interval is \([V_-,V_+]\). For the joint dropout/variance tradeoff, map the same feasible polytope by \(\pi\mapsto(\sum_{n<h}\pi_n,\sum_n n^2\pi_n)\); its exact Pareto boundary is the lower boundary of the convex hull of the images of its vertices.

There is also a finite extremal enumeration. For (0<m<\bar m), where

\[
\bar m=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^M n a^n/n!}{\sum_{n=0}^M a^n/n!},\qquad a=B/\gamma,
\]

every vertex has support \(\{0,\ldots,L\}\) and at most one positive-support edge with unsaturated rate. Indeed, within that support there are (L) edge inequalities; after imposing normalization and mean, a vertex needs at least (L-1) independent active edge inequalities. Hence at most one edge is slack. For each (L\le M) and (0\le j<L), enumerate rates \(\lambda_j=tB\), \(\lambda_i=B\) for (i<L, i\ne j\), and truncate after (L). The unnormalized stationary weights are

\[
w_n=\begin{cases}a^n/n!,&n\le j,\\ t a^n/n!,&j<n\le L.\end{cases}
\]

Keep the (t\in[0,1]) values whose normalized weights have mean (m), together with the fully saturated/truncated-Poisson and endpoint cases. Minimizing/maximizing the displayed objectives over this finite candidate list gives exactly (D_*,V_-,V_+). The maximum feasible mean is \(\bar m\), attained by \(\lambda_n=B\); every mean from zero to \(\bar m\) is feasible by scaling the rates.

## Exact failure of a single-cutoff rate rule

Define a single-cutoff rule as \(\lambda_n=B\) below one cutoff, zero above it, with an optional fractional rate only at the cutoff. Take (M=2), (B=\gamma=1), dropout threshold (h=2), and fixed mean (m=1/2). The only cutoff rule with this mean has \((\lambda_0,\lambda_1)=(1,0)\), stationary law \((1/2,1/2,0)\), and dropout (1).

The admissible non-cutoff controller \((\lambda_0,\lambda_1)=(2/5,1)\) has stationary weights proportional to \((1,2/5,1/5)\), hence

\[
\pi=(5/8,1/4,1/8),\quad E[N]=1/2,\quad \Pr(N<2)=7/8.
\]

Thus the cutoff policy is strictly suboptimal for the stated stationary chance objective. In this same instance, write (x=\pi_2). The complete feasible family is

\[
\pi=(1/2+x,\;1/2-2x,\;x),\quad 0\le x\le1/8,
\]

with implementable rates \(\lambda_0=(1/2-2x)/(1/2+x)\) and \(\lambda_1=2x/(1/2-2x)\) (endpoint values by continuity). It gives the exact frontier

\[
\Pr(N<2)=1-x,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(N)=1/4+2x,
\quad 0\le x\le1/8.
\]

Reducing dropout here requires accepting more variance.

## Shared cap: exact two-output theorem

Now take two queues (N_1,N_2\in\{0,1\}), equal death rates \(\gamma\), state-dependent birth rates \(\lambda_1(s),\lambda_2(s)\ge0\), and the pointwise shared cap \(\lambda_1(s)+\lambda_2(s)\le B\). Fix both stationary means to (m\in(0,1)), and write (p=\Pr(N_1=N_2=1)). The four stationary masses are forced to be

\[
\pi_{11}=p,\quad \pi_{10}=\pi_{01}=m-p,\quad \pi_{00}=1-2m+p.
\]

The probability masses require \(\max\{0,2m-1\}\le p<m\). Finite-cap feasibility additionally requires \(\pi_{00}=1-2m+p>0\); equivalently, p=0 is allowed only when \(m<1/2\), and for \(m\ge1/2\) one needs \(p>2m-1\). The endpoint \(p=m\) is not finite-cap feasible for \(0<m<1\). On this domain, the exact least cap that realizes this joint-success probability is

\[
\boxed{B_{\mathrm{exact}}(m,p)=\gamma\max\!\left\{
\frac{p}{m-p},\;\frac{2(m-p)}{1-2m+p}
\right\}.}
\]

At (p=0,m<1/2), the first term is zero. For (m=1/2), finite-rate feasibility requires (0<p<1/2); at (p=1/2), the required cap diverges. The cap constraint at state ((1,1)), where only deaths occur, yields \(2\gamma p\le 2B(m-p)\), which proves the first lower bound. Balance at ((0,0)) requires total birth flow (2\gamma(m-p)\), at most (B\pi_{00}\), proving the second.

Both bounds are attained simultaneously by the stationary feedback rates

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
s&\lambda_1(s)&\lambda_2(s)\\\hline
(0,0)&\gamma(m-p)/(1-2m+p)&\gamma(m-p)/(1-2m+p)\\
(1,0)&0&\gamma p/(m-p)\\
(0,1)&\gamma p/(m-p)&0\\
(1,1)&0&0
\end{array}
\]

For positive four-state masses this chain is irreducible and reversible: every edge has matching stationary birth and death flow. Thus the bound is realized by a single stationary policy, not merely by a formal mixture of stationary laws.

If the requirement is success probability **at least** (p\), rather than exactly (p\), the cap-minimizing achieved success level is the minimizer of (B_{\mathrm{exact}}(m,q)) over (q\ge p). The two terms in the maximum respectively increase and decrease with (q); their crossing is

\[
q_0(m)=\frac{2m+1-\sqrt{1+4m-4m^2}}2.
\]

Hence (B_{\ge p}(m)=B_{\mathrm{exact}}(m,\max\{p,q_0(m)\})), within the feasible probability range. At (m=1/2), (q_0=1-1/\sqrt2\approx0.2929) and (B_{\min}=\sqrt2\,\gamma). In the exact-probability formulation,

\[
B_{\mathrm{exact}}(1/2,p)=\gamma\max\!\left\{
\frac{2p}{1-2p},\;\frac{1-2p}{p}
\right\},\quad 0<p<1/2.
\]

Thus, for a requirement of success at least p at these means, the sharp minimum cap is explicitly
\[
B_*(p)=B_{\ge p}(1/2)=\gamma\max\!\left\{\sqrt2,\frac{2p}{1-2p}\right\},
\quad 0\le p<1/2.
\]
For an exact target p, retain both branches in \(B_{\mathrm{exact}}\); for an at-least target below \(q_0\), the policy optimally realizes \(q_0\), not the lower requested probability.

The two branches reflect separate empty-state and full-state flow requirements.

As a concrete comparison, at (\gamma=1,B=2,m=1/2), the feedback policy is \(\lambda(0,0)=(1/2,1/2)\), \(\lambda(1,0)=(0,2)\), \(\lambda(0,1)=(2,0)\), \(\lambda(1,1)=(0,0)\). It has stationary law \((1/3,1/6,1/6,1/3)\), the prescribed marginal means, and joint success (1/3). The constant policy with birth rate 1 for each empty output also respects (B=2) and has the same means, but its independent stationary law has joint success (1/4). State feedback therefore raises joint success by one third relative to this equal-rate split.

## General (K)-output LP and costs

For capacities (M_k), let (S=\prod_{k=1}^K\{0,\ldots,M_k\}). Let (x_s) be stationary state mass and (y_{s,k}=x_s\lambda_k(s)) stationary birth flow. Set (y_{s,k}=0) when (s_k=M_k). The shared-cap stationary LP is

\[
\sum_s x_s=1,\quad x_s\ge0,\quad \sum_k y_{s,k}\le Bx_s,
\]

with flow balance for each state (s\):

\[
0=\sum_k\bigl(y_{s-e_k,k}-y_{s,k}
+\gamma_k(s_k+1)x_{s+e_k}-\gamma_k s_kx_s\bigr),
\]

where out-of-range terms are zero. Fixed per-output means are linear constraints \(\sum_s s_kx_s=m_k\); a fixed total mean is \(\sum_{s,k}s_kx_s=m_{\rm tot}\). Average births of output (k) then equal \(\gamma_km_k\). All-output success and each per-output dropout probability are linear objectives in (x); weighted compromises and their Pareto frontier are obtained by repeated LP solves. On states with positive (x_s), the LP policy is recovered as \(\lambda_k(s)=y_{s,k}/x_s\), so the bound is stationary-policy realizable. If some states have zero mass, the realization may be multichain; claims about convergence from every initial state require an added irreducibility/communication condition.

The exact controller assumes perfect observation of the **joint** queue state and zero-delay rate changes at every state transition. For two queues with equal means (m), stationarity gives mean birth intensity (2\gamma m) and mean death intensity (2\gamma m), so an event-triggered state monitor must process (4\gamma m) transitions per unit time on average. The displayed policy has a distinct rate vector in each state with positive mass, so an action-table update is required at each such transition too. Its statewise transition/observation intensities are (2r) at (00), (\gamma+b) at (10) and (01), and (2\gamma) at (11), where (r=\gamma(m-p)/(1-2m+p)) and (b=\gamma p/(m-p)); the peak over all four modeled states is

\[
\max\!\left\{\frac{2\gamma(m-p)}{1-2m+p},\;
\gamma+\frac{\gamma p}{m-p},\;2\gamma\right\}.
\]

When p=0, state (11) has zero stationary mass and is transient under this policy; the stationary-support peak is then \(\max\{2r,\gamma\}\), while the displayed all-state peak also covers initialization in (11).

At (m=1/2,B=2\gamma,p=1/3), average state-change observation intensity is (2\gamma), peak is (3\gamma). The model does not charge sensing energy, controller latency, rate-switching overhead, or the physical cost per production event. Adding an observation-rate constraint or switching penalty changes the admissible control problem; the formulas above do not cover it.

For general (K), the state table has \(\prod_k(M_k+1)\) rows and (K\) rate entries per row. The LP has order (K\prod_k(M_k+1)\) variables/constraints, so explicit optimization scales exponentially in (K) for fixed capacities. A practical controller also needs state estimation, parameter estimates for \(\gamma_k\), and real-time table access. The two-output result is a small exact benchmark; it does not give a scalable index rule.

## Prior-art and status

The occupation-measure LP itself is established CTMDP theory. Guo, Huang, and Song, “Linear Programming and Constrained Average Optimality for General Continuous-Time Markov Decision Processes in History-Dependent Policies,” *SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization* 50(1), 2012, DOI [10.1137/100805169](https://doi.org/10.1137/100805169), formulate constrained average CTMDP optimization as an LP using the forward Kolmogorov equation and give a controlled birth-and-death system as an application. The finite-state flow LP here is a direct specialization, not a new general method.

Threshold policies have prior queueing-control results under other objectives. Hofri and Ross, “On the Optimal Control of Two Queues with Server Setup Times and Its Analysis,” DOI [10.1137/0216029](https://doi.org/10.1137/0216029), analyze exhaustive service and threshold switching for setup/holding costs with a shared server. That structure does not imply optimality for the present fixed-stationary-mean chance objective; the exact three-state example above disproves such a transfer.

Status: exact internally derived finite-state theorem and counterexample; no numerical optimizer or external proof review was required for the hand calculations. General LP prior art is established. The likely useful open step is scalable structure for (K\)-output chance control under a shared cap when the controller has finite observation bandwidth or switching costs—e.g. conditions for a provably near-optimal index/threshold policy, or lower bounds showing such policies cannot suffice. No such result is claimed here.
