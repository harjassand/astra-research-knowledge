ASTRA ULTRA — Cycle 10 independent audit
Square zero-pinned sparse blocks and rooted animals

Status

The zero-pinned square sparse-block estimate in the frozen sparse attack survives an independent reconstruction from Cohen–Alloro–Peled, Theorem 1.1, plus elementary square-grid counting. It holds for every finite set of eligible indices, uniformly over the stated finite-volume family, with constants depending only on the target probability and the fixed enlargement radius. The argument does not use the triangular-lattice manuscript as an axiom.

The geometric hypothesis needs an explicit reading: each eligible enlarged coordinate square must contain every nearest-neighbor square-lattice edge between its vertices, and all its vertices must be unpinned. This is the meaning required below by “full square patch.” If “full” meant only that the vertices are present, the chosen Manhattan paths need not be edges of the graph and the sparse proof would have a gap. Under the full-patch interpretation, no counterexample was found.

The tilt conclusion is conditional on a volume-uniform L2 bound for the Radon–Nikodym density. The small-tilt sufficient condition remains conditional on the square macroscopic exponential-moment proposition, which is not established here. The sparse estimate is not a square GFF theorem.

Exact statement audited

Let G be a finite connected subgraph of the square lattice with maximum degree at most four. Let V0 be a nonempty set of vertices pinned to height zero. Let μ0 be normalized Lebesgue measure on free heights satisfying |h(x)−h(y)|≤1 on every graph edge, and assume its partition function is finite and positive. A finite disjoint union of such components is also allowed if every component has a zero pin; the law is then the product law.

Fix an enlargement radius q≥0 and replace it by the integer ceiling, still denoted q. For a positive integer block spacing L and z∈Z², define
Bz = Lz + {0,…,L}²
and
B*z = Lz + ([-qL,L+qL]² ∩ Z²).
Call z eligible only if every vertex of B*z is a free vertex of G and all square-lattice nearest-neighbor edges internal to B*z are edges of G. In particular, B*z is a complete coordinate-square patch, not merely a set of vertices. Boundary/pin-collar indices are excluded.

For eligible z, define Nz to be the event that some internal edge e of B*z has |∇h(e)|>1−δ, and Fz to be the event that some x,y∈B*z with d1(x,y)≥k0 satisfies |h(x)−h(y)|>(1−δ0)d1(x,y). Put Badz=Nz∪Fz.

For every ρ∈(0,1) and every fixed q, there exist δ,δ0,k0,L, depending only on ρ and q, with 0<δ≤δ0≤1/16, integer k0≥4/δ0, and even L≥8(k0+2)/δ0, such that every finite set A of eligible indices satisfies
μ0(A⊆Bad) ≤ ρ^|A|.                                                   (1)
The constants are independent of the volume, graph shape, pin locations, component count, A, and its location. A good block has exactly the two slack properties used by the square B0 block interface: every edge of Bz has increment at most 1−δ, and every pair in Bz at d1-distance at least k0 has height gap at most (1−δ0)d1. No triangular non-tie event is needed.

CAP input and exact constant

Cohen–Alloro–Peled, arXiv:1711.00259v2, Theorem 1.1, assumes a finite connected graph, a nonempty zero-pin set, a well-defined random-surface law, and an even potential monotone on [0,∞) with U(x)=∞ for x>1. For the hammock potential U=0 on [−1,1] and U=∞ outside, its theorem has δ(U,u)=u. Section 5 gives C(Δ)=2^(10Δ+2). At degree four this is C=2^42.

Thus for distinct edges S of size j and 0<u≤1/8, the degree-four bound is
μ0(|∇h(e)|≥1−u for every e∈S) ≤ (Cu)^(j/C), whenever Cu<1.       (2)
If the actual graph degree is Δ<4, its constant c=2^(10Δ+2) is at most C. With Cu<1, the per-edge factor (cu)^(1/c) is increasing in c, since the derivative of log((cu)^(1/c)) is (1−log(cu))/c²>0. Hence the bound with C weakens the theorem’s bound and is valid uniformly for all maximum degrees at most four. For disconnected pinned components, partition S by component, apply (2) in each component, and multiply; the exponents add to |S|/C. CAP is used only under μ0, never under a tilted law.

Independent reconstruction of (1)

Set C=2^42 and choose
ε=2^(−4C)/C,     δ0=ε/2.
Then ε≤1/8, δ0≤1/16, Cε=2^(−4C)<1, and
λ=2(Cε)^(1/(2C))=1/2.                                               (3)

Choose integer k0≥4/δ0. Choose even L to be the least even integer at least 8(k0+2)/δ0. For q fixed, B*z has side length s=(2q+1)L+1≤(2q+2)L. Its internal edges and ordered vertex pairs therefore obey
|E(B*z)|≤Aq L²,   Aq=2(2q+2)²,
|B*z|²≤Pq L⁴,   Pq=(2q+2)⁴.                                      (4)

Given a separated-patch target η∈(0,1), increase k0 until
b=Pq L⁴ λ^k0≤(η/2)².                                                (5)
This is possible because L=O(k0) with δ0 fixed, while λ^k0=2^(−k0). Then choose δ∈(0,δ0] so small that Cδ<1 and
a=Aq L²(Cδ)^(1/C)≤(η/2)².                                          (6)
For example, the left side tends to zero as δ↓0. This order fixes all parameters using only η and q, before choosing a graph or a set A.

Far-pair failures. For each ordered pair x,y in one coordinate square, choose a deterministic Manhattan shortest path γxy inside the square. If d1(x,y)=m≥k0 and Fz is witnessed by x,y, then the sum of absolute increments along γxy is greater than (1−δ0)m. Every increment is at most 1. If fewer than m/2 edges had |∇h|≥1−ε, the sum would be strictly less than m−εm/2=(1−δ0)m, because ε=2δ0. Thus at least m/2 distinct path edges are near-extremal.

For any fixed path of length m, union over at most 2^m edge subsets of size at least m/2. Apply (2) to each subset; as Cε<1, a subset of size j≥m/2 costs at most (Cε)^(m/(2C)). Consequently
μ0(path has the necessary witness subset)≤2^m(Cε)^(m/(2C))=λ^m. (7)

Now select l patches with pairwise vertex-disjoint enlargements and prescribe F on all of them. In each patch choose a witness pair (at most PqL⁴ choices) and its fixed path. The paths are edge-disjoint across patches. For any tuple of chosen paths, union over the qualifying high-edge subsets on each path and apply CAP to their combined distinct edge set. Equation (7) gives a product upper bound ∏λ^mi≤λ^(lk0). Taking the union over pair choices yields, for any subset K of l separated patches,
μ0(∩z∈K Fz)≤(PqL⁴λ^k0)^l≤((η/2)²)^l.                           (8)
This uses no independence; distinctness of all selected edges follows from disjoint enlarged patches.

Near-edge failures. For j separated patches in a subset J, choose one edge witnessing Nz in each patch. The edges are distinct and there are at most (AqL²)^j choices. By (2),
μ0(∩z∈J Nz)≤(AqL²(Cδ)^(1/C))^j≤((η/2)²)^j.                       (9)

Mixing N and F types. Let S be n separated patches. Assign each site a type, N or F. For the assignment with J of type N and K of type F,
P((∩J Nz)∩(∩K Fz))≤min(P(∩J Nz),P(∩K Fz))
≤sqrt(P(∩J Nz)P(∩K Fz))≤(η/2)^n.
The empty intersection is assigned probability one, so this also covers J=∅ or K=∅. Summing over 2^n assignments covers the event that all patches are bad and gives
μ0(S⊆Bad)≤η^n.                                                     (10)

Removing separation. Let M be an integer strictly greater than 1+2q and color z by its two coordinate residues modulo M. Patches of one color have coordinate-square enlargements separated by at least one full lattice spacing, hence are vertex-disjoint. There are J=M² colors. For a finite A, one color class S⊆A has |S|≥|A|/J. Set η=ρ^J and apply (10):
μ0(A⊆Bad)≤μ0(S⊆Bad)≤η^|S|≤ρ^|A|.
This proves (1) for every finite A without independence across blocks or colors.

Rooted animals and fixed-radius thickening

Under star adjacency on the block-index lattice, the number of connected sets of n eligible indices containing a fixed root is at most 8^(2(n−1)). Choose a deterministic depth-first spanning-tree walk of length 2(n−1); its visited set recovers the animal, and there are at most eight choices at each step. If a rooted bad cluster has at least n sites, it contains a connected rooted subset of exactly n sites. A union bound using (1) therefore gives
μ0(|C(z)|≥n)≤8^(2(n−1))ρ^n≤(64ρ)^n.                               (11)
For nearest-neighbor adjacency the corresponding count is 4^(2(n−1))=16^(n−1). A requested star-cluster base θ∈(0,1) follows by choosing ρ≤θ/64. A maximum over N eligible roots has the valid bound Nθ^n; there is no volume-free bound on existence somewhere.

For a fixed-radius thickening, let M0 be a uniform bound on the number of eligible block sites in one radius-r0 neighborhood. If every site in a finite A is within r0 of a bad eligible site, choose one witness per site. There are at most M0^|A| assignments. Each distinct witness serves at most M0 sites, so every assignment has a witness image W with |W|≥|A|/M0. Apply (1) with base p to W and sum:
μ0(A⊆Bad^(r0))≤M0^|A| p^(|A|/M0)
=(M0 p^(1/M0))^|A|.
Choosing p≤(ρ/M0)^M0 yields the desired per-set base ρ. This statement assumes the thickening is taken in the eligible block lattice; deterministic pin collars are excluded as anchors.

Tilt transfer and map dependence

For a finite-volume exponential tilt μY with density R=dμY/dμ0 and ||R||L2(μ0)≤M2 uniformly over the family, Cauchy–Schwarz and (1) give
μY(A⊆Bad)≤M2 ρ0^(|A|/2).
For any target ρ∈(0,1), choose the zero-law target ρ0=(ρ/M2)². Since M2≥1 and |A|≥1, the result is at most ρ^|A| M2^(1−|A|)≤ρ^|A|. The empty A case is trivial. The same rooted-animal and thickening arguments then apply under μY. For a family of tests or maps, the constants are uniform only if the supremum of their L2 density norms is bounded uniformly; if M2 grows with volume or with the selected map, this transfer does not give a dimension-free animal estimate.

A sufficient small-tilt condition remains conditional. Suppose a square f:averages statement gives E0 exp(a*|Z|)≤M* uniformly, where Z=Σx qx h(x), for weights with Σ|qx|≤C*, max|qx|≤C*R^−2, support diameter O(R), and either Σqx=0 or a connected zero-pin anchor of diameter Ω(R) co-located with the support in an O(R) box. For Y=Σx bx h(x), suppose |bx|≤cR^−2, its support obeys the same O(R) condition and zero-mass/anchor alternative, and 2c≤a*. Choose the fixed C* large enough for the support-area ratio, so qx=bx/c has Σ|qx|≤C* and max|qx|≤C*R^−2. Then E0 exp(2Y)≤M*. Also E0|Z|≤(log M*)/a*, so Jensen gives E0 exp(Y)≥exp(E0Y)≥M*^(−c/a*). Hence
||dμY/dμ0||2
=(E0 exp(2Y))^(1/2)/(E0 exp(Y))
≤M*^(1/2+c/a*).                                                     (12)
For a fixed family of statistics, this is a uniform L2 transfer only when the asserted square average-moment constants and amplitude conditions are uniform. The square f:averages proposition is not proved in the sparse attack or in this audit.

Append-only algebra correction to the adjacent square-block reports

This note records a correction without editing either frozen report. In the shallow, same-side block case with l≥1, the actual y compatibility margin is
(1−β)(k+1+l)−[1+(1−δ)l]
= k(1−β)+(δ−β)l−β.
The two frozen reports instead display
k(1−β)+δl−β(k+l+1)
as an equality with that margin. The displayed expression equals the actual margin minus βk. Therefore the equality should be replaced by “≥”; the displayed value is a valid lower bound. Under β≤1/4 and β≤δ/4, it is at least k(1−2β)+l(δ−β)−β≥δ/2+k/2>0 for l≥1. The exact margin is positive as well. Thus the shell-compatibility conclusion and the stated square-block constant are unchanged. This is an algebraic erratum, not a refutation of B0 or B1. The correction applies to both work/agents/cycle10_random_fields_luna/REPORT.txt and work/agents/cycle10_square_block_review/REPORT.txt; their frozen bytes remain untouched.

Falsifier, limitations, and costs

A counterexample to (1) under the explicit graph, pin, patch, and law hypotheses would falsify this derivation and the CAP union-bound chain. Concrete proof failure conditions are: a supposedly eligible patch omits an internal square edge needed by the selected Manhattan path; enlarged patches in one color overlap; the law is not the zero-pinned hammock law or its assumed finite-volume normalizability fails; or a tilted family has no uniform L2 density bound. No such counterexample was found. If “full patch” is not defined to include all internal edges, the path step needs a repaired eligibility definition.

The verified primary theorem is an extremal-edge estimate, not the sparse-block or animal theorem; equations (1), (8)–(11), and the tilt transfer are reconstructed here. The parameter choice has C=2^42 and ε=2^(−4C)/C, so k0 and L are enormous; no optimization, simulation, enumeration, Lean proof, kernel replay, or external review was done. Historical priority and the square GFF conclusion remain UNKNOWN/unproved. The square f:averages proposition and all subsequent comparison, response, and tightness gates remain open.
