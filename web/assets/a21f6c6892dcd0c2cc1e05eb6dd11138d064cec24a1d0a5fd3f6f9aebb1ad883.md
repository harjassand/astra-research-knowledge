## Primary literature

1. Zhiqiang Xu, “The minimal measurement number for low-rank matrices recovery,” arXiv:1505.07204 (2015), https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.07204.
   - Opened abstract and setup. The abstract states generic exact measurement thresholds for complex rank-r n-by-n matrices and real-case qualifications. The setup defines measurements \(b_j=\langle A_j,Q\rangle\) for rank-bounded Q.
   - Use here: prior-art boundary for exact low-rank matrix sensing. It is global injectivity/recovery, not the finite-bit post-storage all-bilinear-query theorem in PROOF.md.
   - Web evidence: lines 3–5 and 13–22 in the opened arXiv page; the precise generic complex claim is Theorem 2.1 and the real result is Theorem 3.1.

2. Praneeth Kacham and David P. Woodruff, “Lower Bounds on Adaptive Sensing for Matrix Recovery,” NeurIPS 2023, https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/e258bb98cc032ab6ae9053db453431f7-Paper-Conference.pdf.
   - Read primary paper introduction and Theorem 1.1. It assumes unknown rank-r \(A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\), each general linear query q returns \(\langle q,\mathrm{vec}(A)\rangle+g\) with independent Gaussian g of variance \(\|q\|_2^2\), and orthonormal rows across queries. With k≥nr measurements per round and relative Frobenius reconstruction guarantee, it proves \(t=\Omega(\log(n^2/k)/\log\log n)\) adaptive rounds.
   - Use here: adjacent adaptive-sensing lower bound and reminder that measurement noise, round count, and full reconstruction error are separate interface fields.
   - Web evidence: lines 387–421 of the opened PDF for model/Theorem 1.1; lines 399–409 contain the theorem statement.

3. A. Dalalyan, A. Juditsky, and V. Spokoiny, “A New Algorithm for Estimating the Effective Dimension-Reduction Subspace,” JMLR 9 (2008), https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume9/dalalyan08a/dalalyan08a.pdf.
   - Read introduction, especially discussion at PDF lines 130–147: inverse-regression methods rely on a predictor linearity condition and need not capture the full EDR subspace; alternatives have broader applicability; the authors recommend extra data when methods conflict.
   - Use here: existing predictive-subspace learning has assumption/coverage boundaries. The present worst-case bilinear measurement result neither refutes nor validates those methods.
