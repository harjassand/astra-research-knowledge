# Primary-source notes

These notes record the bounded primary-source check used for the report. Literature statements are source-reported, not revalidated by reproducing experiments. Web retrieval date: 2026-10-08.

## 1. Isozaki et al. 2023, Nature 620, 393–401

**Source:** Hideko Isozaki et al., “Therapy-induced APOBEC3A drives evolution of persistent cancer cells,” [Nature DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06303-1); [full text in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10804446/). Published 2023-07-05; author manuscript in PMC 2024-02-01.

**Relevant source locations and claims:**

* PMC lines 426–429: therapy induces A3A in DTP cells; A3A promotes DSBs/genomic instability; deletion reduces APOBEC mutations/SVs and delays resistance.
* Lines 435–442: clinical acquired resistant clones include APOBEC-associated events; the paper distinguishes DTP-derived late clones from pre-existing resistant clones.
* Lines 452–465: early/late PC9 clones, lineage pool experiment and different APOBEC/cluster/SV burdens.
* Lines 506–509: in vivo A3A KO/SCR xenografts had equivalent initial regression; after up to 22 weeks, KO clones carried fewer acquired APOBEC mutations and no acquired kataegis/omikli clusters; H3122 A3A KO showed reduced APOBEC mutation accumulation.
* Lines 516–522: γH2AX/neutral comet assay, reduced DSBs after A3A deletion, higher SVs in controls.
* Lines 524–537: A3A WT versus catalytic-dead E72A affects DTP colony formation; knockdown/KO reduces DTP emergence; wild-type rescue restores; long-term osimertinib pools (18 replicates of 5,000 cells) show lower resistant outgrowth after KO; source concludes A3A increases DTP survival and resistance.
* Lines 563–568: common secondary EGFR/ALK variants are outside preferred APOBEC contexts; A3A induction is not universal; selective catalytic inhibitors with high potency/selectivity were not reported by the authors at publication; NFκB suppression is pleiotropic and upstream target choice requires investigation.
* Lines 577–579: cited model's DTP induction procedure uses TKI for 14 days.

**Boundary:** This paper documents each broad component (A3A, DTP survival, DSB, mutations/clusters/SV, resistant outgrowth) but does not establish event-by-event lineage linkage from a specific joint A3A mark to an established resistant clone or identify the marked offspring kernel. It is not evidence for the toy paired-event probabilities in the report.

## 2. Nousias et al. 2025, Lung Cancer 202, 108487

**Source:** Orestis Nousias, Jeffrey D. Mandell, Karen S. Anderson, Jeffrey P. Townsend, “Precision projections of the delay of resistance mutations in non-small cell lung cancer via suppression of APOBEC,” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lungcan.2025.108487); [author-posted full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389824904_Precision_projections_of_the_delay_of_resistance_mutations_in_non-small_cell_lung_cancer_via_suppression_of_APOBEC). Publication date 2025-03-13/14.

**Relevant source locations and claims (author-posted full text page numbers):**

* Abstract, p. 1 (printed page): infer gene- and patient-specific trinucleotide mutation rates and project delay under APOBEC suppression.
* Methods, pp. 2–3: uses 21 NSCLC patients; estimates gene rates via dNdScv and signature weights; computes APOBEC component (A_i=(SBS2_i+SBS13_i)V_i), residual (S_i=V_i-A_i), and expected first-site time (1/V_i) versus (1/S_i). The multi-site projection treats the first resistance mutation among a candidate set as the relevant first event.
* Results, p. 3: 3 of 42 variants assigned exclusive APOBEC origin, 32 partial, 7 none; heterogeneous site attribution.
* Discussion, pp. 4–5: notes other resistance mechanisms including copy amplification and epigenetic changes, low-frequency pre-existing clones, and—critically—that APOBEC activity may expand DTPs and indirectly increase the population at risk of APOBEC-independent resistance.

**Boundary:** This preempts APOBEC per-site-rate or first-hit time-gain projection as a new contribution. The source does not model treatment-specific persister branching, joint cluster/SV events, or empirically measured clone-specific establishment. Its acknowledgement of indirect DTP-reservoir effects preempts the broad claim that adding the survival channel is itself new.

## 3. Garcia et al. 2025, Cancer Research Communications 5, 825–840

**Source:** Nina Marie G. Garcia et al., “APOBEC3 Activity Promotes the Survival and Evolution of Drug-Tolerant Persister Cells during EGFR Inhibitor Resistance in Lung Cancer,” [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1158/2767-9764.CRC-24-0442); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12093302/).

**Source-reported evidence:** Abstract reports EGFR inhibition induces APOBEC3 expression/activity; APOBEC expression promotes DTP survival; constitutive APOBEC3B changes gefitinib resistance toward de novo EGFR T790M and squamous transdifferentiation during DTP; ΔNp63-associated effects are implicated.

**Boundary:** Directly preempts a generic “APOBEC couples DTP survival and evolution” claim. Its specific APOBEC3B/ΔNp63 mechanism is not proof about A3A-specific cluster/SV event coupling or an optimal A3A intervention.

## 4. Adjacent evolvability model

**Source:** “Cancer Evolvability Determines Therapy Outcomes,” [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13174658/), currently a 2026 preprint at retrieval. Its displayed model uses (dx/dt=xG(v,x)) and (dv/dt=\epsilon(v)\partial G/\partial v), with a scalar evolvability trait and cost. It is not the central competing model here; it confirms that scalar evolvability ODEs are already an active formulation and should not be presented as the missing source-specific mechanism.
