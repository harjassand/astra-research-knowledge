2. A UNIFORM CONTACT HEAT ESTIMATE

In m=2 put T0=-2J Delta on the symmetric Dirichlet square, and
B=2 delta(x-y) in form notation. Let V be its trace factor,
B=V^*V. In orthonormal tangential/normal coordinates
t=(x+y)/sqrt(2), r=(y-x)/sqrt(2), the contact form is
sqrt(2) integral |F(t,0)|^2 dt.

The full-plane free heat kernel has normal value
(8pi J z)^(-1/2) and tangential heat operator norm one. On the FULL
unsymmetrized square the Dirichlet kernel is nonnegative and bounded
pointwise by the full-plane kernel, by the one-dimensional killed heat
comparison and tensorization. Derive the contact norm there and then
restrict the operator to symmetric wave functions; its norm can only
decrease. No pointwise domination of an already symmetrized kernel by
the unsymmetrized full-plane kernel is asserted. This gives

 ||V exp(-zT0)V^*|| <=a(z):=1/(2sqrt(pi J z)),
 ||V exp(-zT0)|| <=sqrt(a(2z)).                       (2.1)

These are operator norm estimates and include the collapsed-measure
coefficient of the LL interaction. No bare delta is treated as a bounded
operator on the square.

Here is also a bounded approximation that verifies all positivity and
perturbation steps below. Replace B by the positive multiplication form

 B_eta=(sqrt(2)/(2eta)) 1_{|r|<=eta},

after zero extension of wave functions outside the square. Its square-root
factor V_eta obeys exactly the same bounds (2.1), uniformly in eta.
Indeed the normal strip Schur bound is strip length 2eta times the
normal heat-kernel supremum, multiplied by sqrt(2)/(2eta); the tangential
free heat operator is a contraction. Positive kernel domination passes
the bound to the Dirichlet square and symmetric subspace.

The forms B_eta converge to B in relative (T0+s) form norm. For a zero-
extended H^1 function, ||F(.,r)-F(.,0)||_2<=sqrt(|r|)||partial_r F||_2.
Integrating the difference of squared norms in |r|<=eta gives a bound
C_s sqrt(eta)(<F,T0F>+s||F||^2). Thus the normalized form operators
converge in norm. Their shifted resolvents converge in norm by the bounded
form identity of eb_obstruction.txt Section 3, uniformly for compact g.
Functional calculus then gives operator-norm heat convergence.

3. POSITIVE KILLING AND A SECOND-ORDER HEAT REMAINDER

For theta>0 write

 E_g=exp[-theta(T0+gB)]-exp(-theta T0)+g D,
 D=integral_0^theta exp[-(theta-z)T0]B exp(-zT0) dz.

At a bounded B_eta, the positive heat-kernel product formula consists of
integrals of exp(-g ell) against positive free path weights, with ell>=0
the sum of the killing potential along the time slices. For fixed eta
the bounded-perturbation product formula converges to its heat semigroup;
its first and second derivatives at zero converge to the Duhamel integrals.
The scalar inequalities, for 0<=a<=b,

 0<=exp(-g ell)-1+g ell<=g^2 ell^2/2,
 0<=r_b(ell)-r_a(ell)<=(b^2-a^2)ell^2/2,
 0<=a r_b(ell)-b r_a(ell)<=ab(b-a)ell^2/2,             (3.1)

where r_g(ell)=exp(-g ell)-1+g ell, therefore give positive-KERNEL
domination by the free second Duhamel coefficient D2_eta. This is order
on nonnegative functions, not Loewner order of heat operators. The last
inequality follows by differentiating in ell:
ab(exp(-a ell)-exp(-b ell))<=ab(b-a)ell, then integrating.

For any such positive-kernel domination, |R f|<=S|f|, so
||R||<=||S||. Estimates (2.1) give explicit uniform bounds

 ||D||<=d1(theta),
 d1(theta)=Gamma(3/4)^2/[2sqrt(2pi)Gamma(3/2)] sqrt(theta/J)
             <=[2/(3sqrt(pi))]sqrt(theta/J),

 ||D2_eta||<=d2(theta),
 d2(theta)=Gamma(3/4)^2/[4sqrt(2pi)] theta/J
             <=theta/(6J).                           (3.2)

For D use the two endpoint factors sqrt(a(2z)) and
sqrt(a(2(theta-z))). For D2 use endpoints with powers -1/4 and the
middle factor a(z) with power -1/2, and integrate over the two-dimensional
simplex. To verify the elementary upper constants without Gamma values,

 Beta(3/4,3/4)=integral_0^1[x(1-x)]^(-1/4)dx
                 <=4sqrt(2)/3,

by symmetry and bounding (1-x)^(-1/4)<=2^(1/4) on x<=1/2.
The remaining middle integral is Beta(1/2,3/2)=pi/2.

Pass eta->0. D_eta converges in operator norm: the relative form-norm
difference multiplied by the two heat factors is bounded by
C sqrt(eta) z^(-1/2)(theta-z)^(-1/2), an integrable function. Hence
E_g,eta converges in norm as well. Positive-kernel inequalities and their
norm bounds pass to the limit, giving

 ||E_g||<=g^2 d2,
 ||E_b-E_a||<=(b^2-a^2)d2,
 ||aE_b-bE_a||<=ab(b-a)d2.                            (3.3)

No uniform norm convergence of the bare potentials is asserted. The
bounded strip approximation, relative form convergence and uniform
contact heat estimates justify the actual delta-interaction response.

4. A LOW FREE-MODE THERMAL COMMUTATOR

Let u=phi_1 tensor phi_1 and
v=(phi_1 tensor phi_3+phi_3 tensor phi_1)/sqrt(2),
phi_k=sqrt(2)sin(pi kx). They are orthonormal free bosonic eigenvectors
with energies e0=4Jpi^2 and e1=20Jpi^2. Exact integration gives

 <u,Bv>=-sqrt(2),
 |<u,[exp(-theta T0),D]v>|
       =w(theta):=sqrt(2)[exp(-theta e0)-exp(-theta e1)]^2/(e1-e0).

Put q0=exp(-theta e0). Expanding
T_g(theta)=exp(-theta T0)-gD+E_g and grouping remainders in the
combinations (3.3) yields, for 0<=a<b,

 ||[T_a(theta),T_b(theta)]|| >=(b-a) ell(theta;a,b),

 ell=w-2q0(a+b)d2-2d1 ab d2-2a^2(a+b)d2^2.           (4.1)

Here [E_a,E_b]=[E_a,E_b-E_a]. The first mixed remainder uses
[T0heat,E_b-E_a]; the second uses [D,aE_b-bE_a], up to sign.
Thus the error has a factor b-a, essential for the local n-copy theorem.
The bound is useful only when the displayed ell is positive. The generic
all-positive-coupling result in eb_obstruction.txt remains available if not.

6. A PARTITION CURVATURE BOUND WITH NO CONTACT-SQUARED OPERATOR

For lambda=0 the whole continuum partition has a positive scalar Laplace
representation

 Z(g)=integral_[0,infinity) exp(-g ell) nu(dell),
 nu([0,infinity))=S0, nu({0})>=1.                     (6.1)

Here is a route that does not assume delta local-time convergence. In
each fixed m replace every pair contact by the positive bounded normal
strip multiplication above. For its symmetric heat trace, the positive
kernel product formula and
P_sym=(1/m!)sum_permutations U_pi give loop weights
K(x,pi x) with nonnegative killing ell. At zero coupling their total mass
is exactly the free sector trace. Product limits and then the relative
form limit preserve their scalar Laplace transforms. Measures are compact
on [0,infinity] after adding an infinity point. Escape mass at infinity
cannot occur: the heat trace is continuous as g decreases to zero by
common-form norm-resolvent convergence, individual eigenvalue convergence
and the uniform free half-temperature trace tail. The limit at zero is
the original free sector trace. Finally sum m; free grand S0 is finite,
so the same argument gives (6.1), with the vacuum's unit atom at zero.

This proves complete monotonicity of Z in this interface; it does not
assert that noncommuting delta terms become classical operators. Put
f(g)=log Z(g). For g>=a0>0,

 0<=f''(g)=Var_{nu_g}(ell)
       <=Z''(g)/Z(g)<=4S0/(e^2 a0^2)=:H.             (6.2)

The last bound uses max_{ell>=0}ell^2 exp(-a0 ell)=4/(e^2a0^2)
and Z(g)>=1. In particular it never writes B^2 as a finite operator.
Differentiation of the common affine form Gibbs trace gives
f'(g)=-tau Tr rho(0,g)B. This can be justified sectorwise from its norm-
analytic normalized form resolvents and finite spectral clusters; the
Hellmann-Feynman term is bounded by energy/g, and half-temperature free
traces justify termwise summation. The Laplace bounds also control every
derivative uniformly away from zero.

Consequently the quantum relative entropy between a,b>=a0 satisfies

 D(rho_a||rho_b)=f(b)-f(a)-(b-a)f'(a)
                            <=H(b-a)^2/2.            (6.3)

This identity is meaningful because the positive-contact energy and the
other common-form expectations are finite, including their Fock sum.
An elementary affinity bound is

 A(rho_a,rho_b)>=exp[-D(rho_a||rho_b)/2].

On Hilbert-Schmidt space apply scalar Jensen to the spectral probability
of L_{rho_b}R_{rho_a}^-1 at the vector sqrt(rho_a). Its half-power
expectation is affinity and its log expectation is -D. Approximation
justifies this even with infinite-dimensional faithful density operators.
Root fidelity is >=affinity, so

                       F(rho_a,rho_b)>=exp[-H(b-a)^2/4]. (6.4)

7. EVERY-n LOCAL PAIR AND AN OPERATIONAL QUANTUM/CLASSICAL SEPARATION

Take J=1,tau=.02 and a=.1, b in (.1,.2]. Uniformly in this window,
(4.1) and the same rational bounds give ell>=.0018 and S0<4. Hence

 F(rho_a,rho_b)-A(rho_a,rho_b)>=c_d(b-a)^2,
 c_d=(.0018)^2/(8*4^2)=2.53125*10^-8.

Equation (6.2) with a0=.1 gives H<217. For any n>=1 put
b_n=.1+.1/sqrt(n), u=.1. Tensor-product fidelity and affinity are exactly
multiplicative, and 1-x<=exp(-x), so

 F(rho_a^n,rho_bn^n)-A(rho_a^n,rho_bn^n)
   >=exp(-H u^2/4)[1-exp(-c_d u^2)].                  (7.1)

Indeed F^n>=exp(-H u^2/4), and
A^n=F^n[1-(F-A)/F]^n<=F^n exp[-n(F-A)] because F<=1.
Apply the EB affinity floor to the WHOLE n-copy channel to obtain

 inf_EB Q_n max_{g in {a,b_n}}||Q_n rho_g^n-rho_g^n||_1
   >=(1/4)exp(-217*.01/2)[1-exp(-c_d*.01)]^2
                                                  >5*10^-21. (7.2)

This is valid for every finite n, with explicit pair and rate 1/sqrt(n).
Even an encoder that estimates the coupling from all n inputs and uses
unlimited classical storage is included. It cannot faithfully reconstruct
the entire tensor-product quantum state at an error tending to zero.
The statement does not restrict estimation of g or reconstruction of a
fixed number of copies, nor does it prove an optimal quantum memory rate.
No LAN theorem has been assumed to identify the local experiment.

For fixed PUBLIC g and varying lambda, the n-chain states commute and
total particle number over all chains is exactly sufficient. A classical
decoder prepares the parameter-independent conditional state given that
sum. Public exponential number moments supply a cutoff
R>=n log S_1+log(2/epsilon) at exponential moment one, where S_1 is the
all-m bound with an extra exp(m); this stores O(log(n+log(1/epsilon)))
bits. That conditional interacting preparation is an explicit separate
cost. The unknown-coupling theorem changes this legal interface.

8. FINITE-CHAIN AND COST GATES

If Delta_L=sup_g||I_L rho_L(g)I_L^*-rho(g)||_1, then
the n-copy discrepancy is <=n Delta_L by telescoping. An approximate
finite-chain classical archive transported by the public isometric/TP
channels yields a continuum EB channel with reconstruction error at most
its original error+2n Delta_L. Thus the lower bound for finite chains is
at least the right side of (7.2) minus 2n Delta_L. This gives fixed-n
liminf, and a joint limit only when n Delta_L->0. A quantitative Delta_L
or uniform explicit chain-size threshold is a remaining gate.

The quantum archive upper bound (1.1) is already all-L and does not
require the continuum approximation. Its arithmetic/sine circuit calls
scale by n; no efficient Gibbs source is acquired. Preparing n input
critical chains at beta=tau L^2 is not free. A simultaneous physical
energy guarantee additionally requires the bounded-g Gram decoder and
precision depending on L, n and the requested energy allowance.

Falsifiers include the contact measure coefficient, free strip Schur
estimate, operator-form limit, kernel-positive remainder comparisons,
the low-mode integral, Laplace trace representation, relative-entropy
identity or tensor-affinity floor. No finite diagnostic replaces these
infinite-dimensional arguments. Quantum-memory necessity and local
statistical-family compression are established in the broader literature;
the interacting LL critical-chain interface and explicit finite-n bounds
are the candidate application. External validation/priority remain UNKNOWN.
