# Cycle 2 — Local activity identifies collective conductivity in a finite-range lattice electrolyte

## Verdict

There is an exact, local, finite-range counterexample to using tracer diffusion alone as a bulk-conductivity estimator. In a charge-neutral two-channel one-dimensional lattice electrolyte with nearest-neighbor exclusion hops, every finite ring is reversible and ergodic. The full conductivity is identified by a local active-bond-time statistic, while the matched finite-ring Nernst–Einstein estimate is smaller by an exact factor $N$; at fixed nonzero density, its thermodynamic limit is zero while the collective conductivity stays positive.

This is a theorem about an idealized Markov model. It is not a claim about a named battery material, nor a new general Green–Kubo principle. The useful result is the exact model-specific sufficient observable, a finite-time error bound, and a locality-respecting failure of the tracer-only baseline. The immutable cycle-1 artifacts remain unchanged; this report extends them with a local ergodic model.

## Source interface and prior art

For equilibrium linear response, the collective Einstein–Helfand (EH) formula uses the long-time growth of the squared total charge displacement. France-Lanord and Grossman write the three-dimensional formula with denominator $6Vt$; its standard $d$-dimensional form is
\[
 \sigma=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\beta}{2dVt}
 \mathbb E\left|\sum_i q_i\,[r_i(t)-r_i(0)]\right|^2,
\]
and state that the Green–Kubo (GK) form is equivalent; keeping only diagonal particle terms gives Nernst–Einstein (NE), while off-diagonal terms carry correlations. Their 2019 PRL gives these as Eqs. (1)–(3), pp. 1–2. This is the established source theorem, not a new result here. The 2026 primary methods study by Zaby et al. likewise describes EH/CMSD and GK/charge-current autocorrelation as formally equivalent in the infinite-time limit, and documents their different finite-trajectory requirements: unwrapped coordinates and a linear-window choice for EH; time-resolved current and an integral plateau for GK. These sources establish the estimator interface, not the lattice-model calculation below.

The mechanism is not a newly discovered correlation effect: collective conductivity versus self-only NE is established prior art. The new work in this package is a self-contained exact calculation for this selected finite-range process and its sampling bound. No priority search was done for an equivalent SSEP derivation, and no novelty claim is made.

## Model and assumptions

Take two parallel channels, labeled $s\in\{+,-\}$, each a periodic ring of $L\ge3$ sites with spacing $a$ and cross-sectional area $A$. In each channel there are $N\in\{1,\dots,L-1\}$ identical mobile ions and vacancies. The $+$ ions carry charge $+q$; the $-$ ions carry charge $-q$. The two counts are equal, so the total mobile charge is zero. The total modeled volume is $V=2ALa$.

Within each channel, a particle exchanges with an adjacent vacancy at rate \(\gamma>0\) per active undirected nearest-neighbor bond. There are no cross-channel interactions. Thus the dynamics has range one lattice spacing; the only interaction is same-channel hard-core exclusion. The finite state space is
\[
 \Omega_{L,N}\times\Omega_{L,N},\qquad
 \Omega_{L,N}=\{\eta\in\{0,1\}^{L}:\sum_x\eta_x=N\}.
\]
The process is irreducible, reversible, and stationary under the product of uniform canonical measures. This is a channelized ideal lattice electrolyte with no Coulomb force, solvent, energetic disorder, or polarization. Those omissions are assumptions, not approximations justified for nature.

For a state \(\eta^s\), let
\[
 B_s(\eta^s)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z}
 {\bf1}\{\eta^s_x\ne\eta^s_{x+1}\},\qquad F=B_++B_-.
\]
Thus $F$ is the number of locally enabled hops across the two channels. Under the uniform canonical law, a specified bond is unlike with probability
\[
 \frac{2N(L-N)}{L(L-1)},
\]
so
\[
 \mathbb E_\pi B_s=\frac{2N(L-N)}{L-1},\qquad
 \mu_L:=\mathbb E_\pi F=\frac{4N(L-N)}{L-1}.
\]

## Exact EH theorem and local identifying statistic

Lift particle coordinates to unwrapped coordinates on the line. Let $S_s(t)$ be the sum of the lattice-step displacements of all $N$ particles in channel $s$. The total charge displacement is
\[
 Q_t=qa\bigl(S_+(t)-S_-(t)\bigr).
\]
For either channel, the conditional drift of $S_s$ is
\[
 \gamma\sum_x(\eta_x^s-\eta_{x+1}^s)=0,
\]
by telescoping around the ring. Hence $Q_t$ is a martingale. Each allowed hop changes $Q_t$ by a signed $qa$, and its predictable quadratic variation is
\[
 \langle Q\rangle_t=q^2a^2\gamma\int_0^t F(\eta_u)\,du.
\]
Since \(\mathbb E Q_t^2=\mathbb E\langle Q\rangle_t\), the one-dimensional EH relation gives, exactly for every $t>0$ under stationarity,
\[
 \sigma_L=\frac{\beta}{2Vt}\mathbb E Q_t^2
 =\frac{\beta q^2a^2\gamma}{2V}\,\mu_L
 =\frac{\beta q^2a\gamma\,N(L-N)}{A L(L-1)}.
\]
For $N/L\to\rho\in(0,1)$,
\[
 \sigma_\infty=\frac{\beta q^2a\gamma}{A}\,\rho(1-\rho)>0.
\]

With \(q,a,A,T,\gamma,L,N\) known, the local path statistic \(\int_0^tF(\eta_u)du\) is model-sufficient for the EH conductivity: it is an unbiased estimator after scaling by \(c_L=\beta q^2a^2\gamma/(2V)\), and it uses only nearest-neighbor occupancy. This is an information-sufficient observable under the specified generator, not a claim of Fisher sufficiency for a broader family of unknown kinetic models. The exact canonical formula above means a theorist who trusts every model assumption can compute \(\sigma_L\) without simulation; the path statistic is useful as a model diagnostic and as the directly estimable kinetic quantity when activity is sampled.

## Matched tracer-only baseline

The one-channel tagged-particle diffusion coefficient at fixed finite $L,N$ can also be computed. Let $U_s(t)=aS_s(t)$ be total particle displacement in one channel. Its martingale bracket has stationary mean rate $a^2\gamma\mathbb E B_s$. Because nearest-neighbor exclusion on a ring preserves cyclic particle order, for any tagged particle $j$, its displacement differs from $U_s(t)/N$ by a quantity bounded uniformly in $t$ by a constant depending only on $L$. Dividing the variance by $2t$ therefore yields
\[
 D_{\mathrm{self},L}
 =\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E|X_j(t)-X_j(0)|^2}{2t}
 =\frac{a^2\gamma\,\mathbb E B_s}{2N^2}
 =\frac{\gamma a^2(L-N)}{N(L-1)}.
\]
Both species have this same coefficient. Applying the self-only NE expression to the same two-channel finite system gives
\[
 \sigma_{\mathrm{NE},L}
 =\frac{\beta q^2}{V}\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}ND_{\mathrm{self},L}
 =\frac{\beta q^2\gamma a(L-N)}{A L(L-1)},
 \qquad
 \frac{\sigma_L}{\sigma_{\mathrm{NE},L}}=N.
\]
At fixed density $N/L\to\rho$, $D_{\mathrm{self},L}\sim\gamma a^2(1-\rho)/(\rho L)$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{NE},L}\to0$, while $\sigma_L\to\sigma_\infty>0$. This is a matched comparison at the same $L,N,T,\gamma,q,a,A$, with the same equilibrium law and generator. The large factor arises because finite-ring tags cannot pass one another; their long-time motion is the shared winding motion divided among $N$ ordered particles.

This finite-size calculation is consistent with the published infinite-line SSEP result: with per-bond rate $1/2$, De Masi and Ferrari show $\mathbb E X_t^2$ grows on the order of $t^{1/2}$, with a $t^{1/4}$-scaled tagged-particle CLT, so the infinite-volume tracer diffusion coefficient is zero. Their result is cited as an external consistency check; the finite-ring $D_{\mathrm{self},L}$ formula above is derived here.

## Finite-time error guarantee

Run $R$ independent trajectories from the exact stationary canonical law, each for duration $t$. Define
\[
 \overline F_{R,t}=\frac1{Rt}\sum_{r=1}^R\int_0^tF(\eta^{(r)}_u)\,du,
 \qquad \widehat\sigma_{R,t}=c_L\overline F_{R,t}.
\]
This is unbiased. The one-particle continuous-time walk on the $L$-cycle has gap
\[
 \lambda_L=2\gamma\bigl(1-\cos(2\pi/L)\bigr).
\]
Caputo–Liggett–Richthammer's proof of Aldous' gap identity implies the same gap for symmetric exclusion; the product of the two independent lane chains has gap \(\lambda_L\). For stationary reversible chains,
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_\pi(F(0),F(s))|
 \le \operatorname{Var}_\pi(F)e^{-\lambda_Ls}.
\]
Since $0\le F\le2L$, \(\operatorname{Var}_\pi(F)\le L^2\). Integrating the covariance and averaging the independent replicas gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\overline F_{R,t})
 \le \frac{2L^2}{\lambda_LRt}.
\]
Consequently, for any \(\epsilon>0\) and relative tolerance \(\eta>0\), Chebyshev yields
\[
 \Pr\{|\widehat\sigma_{R,t}-\sigma_L|\ge\epsilon\}
 \le \frac{c_L^2\,2L^2}{\lambda_LRt\,\epsilon^2},
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\{|\widehat\sigma_{R,t}-\sigma_L|\ge\eta\sigma_L\}
 \le \frac{2L^2}{\lambda_LRt\,\eta^2\mu_L^2}
 =\frac{2L^2}{\lambda_LRt\,\eta^2[4N(L-N)/(L-1)]^2}.
\]
At fixed density and large $L$, this conservative relative bound is approximately
\[
 \frac{1}{8\lambda_LRt\,\eta^2\rho^2(1-\rho)^2}.
\]
Thus a sufficient total stationary trajectory time for failure probability at most \(\delta\) is asymptotically
\[
 Rt\ \ge\ \frac{L^2}{32\pi^2\gamma\,\delta\eta^2\rho^2(1-\rho)^2}.
\]
This is a rigorous but loose Chebyshev guarantee, not an observed error bar. It requires exact stationary initialization and the correct $\lambda_L$ bound. It does not include parameter uncertainty or model bias.

## Sampling, computation, and energy costs

Inputs are $L,N,a,A,T,q,\gamma$, the canonical start law, and a requested $(\eta,\delta)$. The theoretical value needs no simulation. For $R$ event-driven simulation replicas, state storage is $2L$ occupancy bits plus counters; a random canonical initialization costs $O(L)$ per replica. Maintain $F$ by updating the two bonds incident to each accepted hop, so the per-event observable update is $O(1)$. The aggregate event rate is $\gamma F\le2\gamma L$, so expected event-processing cost is $O(R\gamma Lt)$; the exact stationary expected accepted-event count is
\[
 R\gamma t\,\mathbb E F
 =R\gamma t\,\frac{4N(L-N)}{L-1}.
\]
No force evaluations or electronic-structure energy calculations occur in this continuous-time Markov simulation. Its CPU cost is event processing, not joules; a machine-specific energy estimate was not measured. Calibration of $\gamma$, construction of a physically justified lattice/channel map, and experimental measurement costs are outside the exact theorem.

For a named material, a meaningful falsification would require matched $T$, stoichiometry, density, phase, and sample geometry; tracer diffusivity for both signs; evidence for the assumed local site/channel occupancy and hop-rate law; and direct bulk conductivity from impedance or a converged collective EH/GK estimate. The model prediction must be rejected if its measured hop statistics and activity fail to predict the independent collective conductivity within stated uncertainties. Experimentally resolving $F(t)$ is a separate challenge: a simulation or time-resolved local-structure measurement must resolve individual neighbor exchanges rather than only endpoint configurations. EIS geometry, electrode polarization, grain boundaries, and electronic leakage must be controlled before treating measured conductivity as the ionic bulk value.

## Evidence boundary, failures, and next gate

- **Externally established:** the collective EH/GK linear-response interface; NE as its self-only diagonal approximation; the SSEP tagged-particle subdiffusive asymptotic; and the SSEP spectral-gap identity.
- **Internally derived:** finite-ring canonical active-bond expectation; martingale/bracket conductivity formula; finite-ring tagged diffusion; exact conductivity/NE ratio; and the stationary finite-time sampling bound.
- **Finite diagnostic:** `verify_cycle2.py` checks the finite canonical combinatorics, telescoping drift, and algebraic ratio for small rings. It is not a proof of the general theorem and does not simulate trajectories.
- **Not done:** no molecular-dynamics or electrochemical experiment, no material parameter fit, no measurement of local activity, no external mathematical review, no exhaustive novelty search, and no claim that the channelized ideal model describes a real electrolyte.
- **Principal nature boundary:** actual battery electrolytes have Coulomb interactions, solvent or framework motion, disorder, multiple local environments, finite-range and possibly long-range correlations, and rates that may not be Markovian or uniform. Finite-range hops alone do not license this generator or its $F$-based estimator.
- **Decisive next gate:** pick one named electrolyte, estimate its species-resolved local transition rates and local active-bond time from a validated trajectory, then compare the activity prediction against independent collective EH/GK and impedance data at the same state point. If the predicted rate-law or local statistic fails, reject this SSEP mechanism; do not extrapolate the toy theorem.
