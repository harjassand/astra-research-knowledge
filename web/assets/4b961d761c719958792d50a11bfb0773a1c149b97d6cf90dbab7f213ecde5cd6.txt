c07_s02 — EXACT POSITIVE COMPLETION FROM AN APPROXIMATE BERNSTEIN MIXTURE
7 October2026 UTC. Separate reusable lemma derived from the axial proof.
Provenance: root requested extraction after reading the frozen delta1/4 proof.
This lemma is finite-dimensional. It does not assert continuous Gaussian
deconvolution, an efficient numerical routine, or historical priority.

Let B_{N,k}(x)=binom(N,k)x^k(1-x)^(N-k), 0<=k<=N. Suppose a real integrable
function f on[0,1] obeys f(x)>=m>0 almost everywhere. Let p_k be any desired
real populations, and q_k=integral B_{N,k}f. Set epsilon=sum_k|p_k-q_k|.

There is an EXPLICIT degree-N real polynomial h with

 integral B_{N,k} h =p_k-q_k for every k,
 ||h||infinity <=C_N epsilon,
 C_N=sum_{l=0}^N (2l+1)2^l=(2N-1)2^(N+1)+3.                 (1)

Hence if C_N epsilon<=m, f+h is a nonnegative exact Bernstein density for p.
If the inequality is strict, it has positive uniform margin m-C_N epsilon.
For the raw Dicke-diagonal operator sum p_k |D_k><D_k| this is an exact full
separability certificate, after dividing by sum p_k. The same statement with
sup population residual e gives epsilon<=(N+1)e.

PROOF
The B_{N,k} are a basis of real polynomials of degree<=N. Define the real
linear functional E(B_{N,k})=p_k-q_k. Let R_l=P_l(2x-1) be the shifted Legendre
basis, integral R_l R_j=1_{l=j}/(2l+1), |R_l|<=1 on[0,1]. Its degree-l
Bernstein coefficients are (-1)^(l-j)binom(l,j), j0..l. Degree elevation to N
is a convex average of these coefficients, via Vandermonde. Thus each degree-N
coefficient has absolute value<=2^l and |E(R_l)|<=2^l epsilon.
Set h=sum_l(2l+1)E(R_l)R_l. Orthogonality gives integral gh=E(g) for EVERY
polynomial g of degree<=N, proving all the exact population identities.
The displayed sup norm follows by the triangle inequality. The finite sum in
(1) follows from sum_l l2^l=(N-1)2^(N+1)+2.

An optionally sharper constant is sum_l(2l+1)binom(l,floor(l/2)), since that is
the actual maximum degree-l Bernstein coefficient before convex elevation.
The exponential2^N factor is a charged conditioning bound, not an assertion
of practical computational cost. No finite-bit evaluation oracle is acquired.

FOURIER TRUNCATION INTERFACE
This principle applies particularly simply when a target population array is
the Fourier transform of a finite signed measure nu at k-N/2. With
w0(s)=[2cosh(s/2)]^(-N-2), define a finite strip transform
 f_T(x)=(N+1) integral_{|u|<=a} w0(s+iu)/w0(s) dnu(u),
 s=log[x/(1-x)], a<pi.
The exact Beta contour identity gives its populations as the same truncated
Fourier transform. The population residual is at most the total variation of
nu outside[-a,a]. If f_T has a proved uniform positive margin exceeding the
finite correction cost, (1) produces an exact positive density for every one
of the N+1 target populations. No inverse transform outside the finite strip
is needed. For real f_T, it is enough that nu is symmetric under conjugation.

In the axial proof nu is Gaussian with variance2delta/N. The impossible full
continuous Gaussian deconvolution is replaced by a convergent finite-strip
transform, a uniform positivity estimate, and a polynomial correcting only
the finite set of physically required moments. The existence of an exact
positive finite-degree completion does not imply that the full continuous
moment generating function has a positive inverse.
