# Cycle 9 — a conditional weakest-cell rule for Fog-gated tissue propagation

**Verdict.** The 2026 Fog/GPCR results, combined with the 2024 adhesion/de-adhesion mechanism, support a useful *conditional* model of propagation: the local contractile signal is multiplied by an integrin cofactor, while that same integrin state also raises the adhesive load that must be peeled. In a serial relay this makes the weakest cell, not the mean MyoII profile, decisive. The model gives an exact per-cell propagation criterion and predicts a sign switch in the effect of integrin titration. These are model results, not a newly established biological law: the papers do not calibrate cell-level force, active integrin, or the coefficients needed to evaluate the threshold in embryos.

The strongest source-grounded negative result is that the published front-relative exponential profiles do **not** identify a Fog-transport or Fog-dose propagation threshold. A cell-local contact-age response and a Fog-gated mechanical relay can produce the same measured profile, while the primary studies do not jointly measure the mobility, clearance, contact history, and force variables needed to choose among them. The passive `D/v` bound from C8 remains a valid lemma for its narrow transport PDE, but cannot be promoted to the relay’s speed law. Stop searching the current summaries for a numeric biological threshold; a decisive next result requires paired cell-level force and trajectory data.

## Primary mechanism and prior-art boundary

Mundhe et al. (2026) distinguish wave initiation from continued recruitment. In the reported rescue, primordium-only Gα restoration preserves near-normal initiation but leaves about 2.5±0.06 recruited rows, versus 7.1±0.3 in controls. A diffusible, primordium-restricted Fog rescue restores about 5.3±0.2 rows; an otherwise source-restricted membrane-tethered Fog gives about 1.7±0.1. These experiments support a mobile Fog contribution and propagation-zone GPCR dependence in the tested embryo. They do not show that an advancing free-Fog front sets the propagation speed.

The extracellular Fog::YFP signal is approximately uniform. Apical FCS separates a fast Fog component (84%, `D≈51±2 μm²/s`) and a slow component (16±1%, `D≈1.6±0.2 μm²/s`); calling the latter receptor-associated is an interpretation, not a chemical identification. Gprk2 RNAi raises the slow fraction to roughly 30±15% and flattens its spatial slope. Smog brightness changes with Fog/Gprk2 perturbation, but it is a model-dependent oligomerization proxy. αPS3-integrin loss reduces MyoII output without reducing Smog brightness, and suppresses the expanded/high MyoII phenotype under Gprk2 depletion. This supports a downstream or parallel integrin signaling gate, not integrin-driven receptor clustering. Contact and βPS profiles also expand under Gprk2 RNAi, with cause versus feedback consequence unresolved.

The decisive mechanical baseline predates this study. Bailles et al. (2019) showed a self-replicating contact/adhesion/deformation wave and reported that raising Fog dosage raises MyoII without increasing wave speed. Collinet et al. (2024) measured integrin–MyoII feedback, prolonged contact, detachment-angle changes, and slower detachment/wave advance with increased adhesion. The 2026 article’s discussion links larger signaling profiles to slower progression, but Fig. 3 is a profile experiment, not a matched speed-versus-dose curve. Accordingly, this report does not treat “more Fog slows the wave” as a calibrated quantitative law.

Jha et al. (2018) already established Fog-induced Smog oligomers and Gprk2/β-arrestin-dependent receptor removal; generic GPCR clustering or endocytic tuning is not a novelty claim. Classic morphogen-transport work already treats diffusion, receptor binding, internalization, and degradation ([dynamic morphogen scaling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817156/); [Dpp free/bound/internalized transport](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001111)). The narrow new modeling connection here is the dual role of integrin as signal cofactor and adhesive load in a serial, moving tissue relay. No priority claim is made for the general transport, bistability, or mechanochemical-wave model classes.

Source details, supplementary checks, peer-review limits, and precise measurements are recorded in [`primary_audit.md`](primary_audit.md). No raw-image reanalysis was performed.

## A source-constrained force-reserve model

For successive propagation-zone cells `i`, let `O_i` be an effective Fog-activated receptor-cluster input, `I_i` an active integrin/cofactor state, `B_i` an adhesion-bearing contact state, and `θ_i` the angle converting contractility into force normal to the vitelline membrane. Use the minimal closure

```text
M_i = M0 + Γ O_i I_i/(K + I_i)
F_deadh,i = α M_i
F_adh,i   = β B_i
Δ_i       = F_deadh,i sin(θ_i) − F_adh,i.
```

The saturating cofactor and linear force closures are assumptions chosen to represent the observed pathway topology. They are not fitted laws. If `I_i` is proportional to the adhesion state, normalize units so `B_i=I_i`. Under the explicit quasi-static peeling rule “cell `i` detaches iff `Δ_i>0`” and a strictly serial no-bypass relay, propagation through `N` cells occurs **if and only if**

```text
min_i Δ_i > 0.
```

The proof is induction: each positive-margin row detaches and exposes the next row; the first nonpositive row cannot detach and blocks all later rows in a no-bypass chain. This is a sharp threshold for the named discrete model, not an inferred embryo threshold. It replaces the mean-profile shortcut with a cellwise weakest-link criterion.

The need for cellwise margins is demonstrated by a counterexample. In normalized units with angle absorbed into the contractile variable and `α=β=1`, arrays `[(M,B)=(1.2,1.0),(1.2,1.0)]` and `[(2.2,0.5),(0.2,1.5)]` have the same means `(1.2,1.0)` and the same mean reserve `0.2`. Their local reserves are respectively `[0.2,0.2]` and `[1.7,−1.3]`; the first propagates and the second stalls. This does not assert those force values occur in embryos. It establishes that matching average MyoII/βPS/contact readouts cannot certify propagation without their cellwise joint distribution.

## Discriminating intervention prediction

When adhesion and cofactor activity share the same integrin state (`B=I`), at fixed receptor input and measured geometry,

```text
Δ(I;O,θ) = α sin(θ)[M0 + Γ O I/(K+I)] − βI
dΔ/dI     = α Γ O K sin(θ)/(K+I)² − β.
```

Thus the local effect of adding integrin changes sign exactly at

```text
O_c(I,θ) = β(K+I)² / [α Γ K sin(θ)].
```

Below this level, extra integrin adds more adhesion than cofactor-supported de-adhesion force; above it, the cofactor increment dominates locally. For fixed `O`, the force reserve is maximized at

```text
I* = sqrt[α Γ O K sin(θ)/β] − K,
```

when this is positive. A monotone receptor-only model cannot produce this sign switch, and a uniform “integrin amplifies MyoII” model misses the opposing adhesion term. A useful test is therefore a graded integrin intervention at matched receptor state and measured detachment angle, with cell-level detachment reserve as outcome. The prediction is *not* that integrin always speeds or always slows propagation.

The primary papers supply the qualitative dual role and angle/contact observations, but not absolute forces or calibrated `O`, `I`, `α`, `β`, `Γ`, and `K`. The switch location is consequently symbolic. Fluorescence brightness alone is not an adequate substitute for active receptor input or load. Until these quantities are acquired, the equations provide an experiment-design target rather than a numerical dose prescription.

If one additionally assumes independent, identically distributed Gaussian cell reserves with mean `μ` and standard deviation `σ`, finite-row reliability is exactly `P_N=Φ(μ/σ)^N`. A 95% chance of clearing seven rows requires `μ/σ≥2.4421108`; twenty rows require `μ/σ≥2.7992115`. These calculations expose the extra lower-tail margin that a robust intervention would need. Neither Gaussianity nor independence is measured in the cited embryos; correlations or heavy tails require the joint reserve distribution instead.

## Identifiability attack and rejected extension

The C8 passive-field model solves `D b''+v_rel b'−k b=0` and gives `ell=2D/(v_rel+sqrt(v_rel²+4Dk))`, hence `v_rel ell≤D`. This remains exact under its assumptions: constant scalar material-frame diffusion, nonnegative first-order clearance, no distributed source, and no local gain. In C9 it is retained only as a comparator. It cannot serve as the active tissue front law because the 2026 slow-pool `D` is not shown to be the material-frame mobility of the active state, the active-pool clearance `k` is not measured, and neither value is paired to cell-row-relative front speed and MyoII length in the same condition.

There is a direct profile alias. Given any observed exponential `P(d)=b+(A−b)e^(−d/ell)` at a constant front speed `v`, let a cell’s contact age be `a=Δ−d/v` and let its local response be `M(a)=b+(A−b)e^(−(Δ−a)/τ)` with `τ=ell/v`. Then `M(Δ−d/v)=P(d)` exactly, with no lateral ligand transport. This construction is not a claim that embryos use that waveform; it proves a single front-frame profile cannot identify transport. Contact-age dynamics and the 2019/2024 mechanical relay are strong equally informed baselines. The 2026 source adds a Fog/GPCR requirement to that relay, so a purely mechanical, Fog-free account is insufficient for the tested conditions.

The data do establish causal requirements and constrain signs, but they do not locate a numerical bifurcation in Fog dose, slow-pool mobility, clearance, integrin activation, or tissue speed. The slow pool’s chemical identity is uncertain; the reported threshold ranges for some profiles are censored; the inferred Smog oligomer fraction is model-dependent; and the 2026 profile measurements are not a matched velocity sweep. Therefore the missing biological threshold is **not recoverable from current source summaries**. This is a no-go on parameter inversion and a useful stop rule: do not fit a Fog-dose-to-speed curve from profile length and cross-paper averages.

## Data and cost interface for a decisive test

The minimum acquisition is a same-condition, cell-linked time series from first vitelline-membrane contact through detachment, with (a) front speed relative to tracked cell rows, (b) contact age and `θ_i`, (c) MyoII and a validated receptor-activity/occupancy readout, (d) active integrin and adhesion-state readouts, and (e) a calibrated force or detachment-reserve measurement. A Fog/Gprk2 titration must include integrin-intact and integrin-perturbed arms; match initial primordium invagination and preserve the diffusible-versus-tethered Fog control. Embryo is the replication unit, not cells or FCS spots nested within it.

Fit three models with comparable information and held-out conditions: (1) passive slow-pool transport using independently measured `D,k,v_rel`; (2) a contact-age response with no lateral active-pool transport; and (3) the Fog-gated contact/integrin relay with measured adhesion and detachment variables. Hold out at least one Fog/Gprk2 level and one integrin/contact condition. Compare per-embryo held-out profiles, contact-to-detachment delays, and front speed. The sign-switch prediction is tested only after matching/conditioning on `O` and `θ`; otherwise geometry or occupancy confounds the comparison.

Costs and current gaps: custom reporters and perturbation genotypes; staged embryo cohorts; live confocal cell tracking plus FCS or pulse-chase; force calibration; embryo-level replication; blinded extraction; and a pilot for embryo-level variance and power. The papers do not provide sufficient raw data here to estimate sample size, money, instrument time, or energetic cost. FCS fraction, Smog brightness, βPS fluorescence, MyoII intensity, or contact duration alone do not acquire the force parameters. No wet-lab intervention or external expert review was performed in this cycle.
