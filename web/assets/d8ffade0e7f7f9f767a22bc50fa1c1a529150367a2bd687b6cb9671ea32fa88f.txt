INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE RETAINED-PATH ESTIMATES

exponential of a Hermitian two-by-two matrix. On |Z|<=L, summing their exponent
norms gives Q, so ||g||<=exp(Q), ||g^-1||<=exp(Q). For any positive trace-one
tau, exp(-2Q)<=Tr(g tau g*)<=exp(2Q). This does not assume physical filter
success or divide by a postselection probability.

Normal perturbations contribute at most U h to summed exponent perturbations;
complete factor/update perturbations contribute at most 4m h. Telescoping and
product norm bounds yield ||Delta g||<=exp(Q+1)S h for S h<=1. The chosen
S h<=1/4 implies ||Delta g||<=exp(Q), so, with a positive trace-one perturbed
tau and ||Delta tau||_1<=Ctau h,

                   2exp(Q)||Delta g|| + ||Delta g||^2
                 <= exp(2Q)[Ctau+3eS]h.
The seed's Bloch-ball projection preserves positivity and cannot increase its
Euclidean error relative to the exact Bloch vector. This makes the expansion
above valid even when tau has small eigenvalues.

If Delta=||Delta X||_1<=exp(-2Q)/2, then the trace perturbation is <=Delta and
the mean-value estimate for log gives

FINITE NORMAL ROUTINE WITHOUT AN ORACLE
Here is an explicit endpoint-safe version of the inverse-CDF argument. Take a
rational upper endpoint Lbar>=L, put a certified dyadic q<=phi(Lbar), and set
delta=h q/32. One may take q=2^(-ceil(Lbar^2)-3), avoiding an exact evaluation
of phi. Draw a dyadic uniform Uhat coupled to U with |Uhat-U|<=delta. Compute
CDF intervals of absolute error <=delta. Use the clipped inverse of Uhat on
[-Lbar,Lbar]. Bisection either resolves the root interval, stops at radius h/4,
or stops when a CDF comparison is uncertain within delta. Endpoints use the
same rule. On the event that the ideal normal is retained, an uncertain result
has |Phi(z)-U|<=3delta; the derivative lower bound yields distance <=3h/32.
Resolved interval termination contributes at most h/4+delta/q<h. Consequently
the returned normal is h-close to its coupled ideal normal. Uniform clipping
events are exactly part of the already charged Gaussian tail.

The random-bit count is O(ell+Lbar^2+log(Lbar+1)), and bisection requires
O(log((Lbar+1)/h)) CDF evaluations. There is an elementary polynomial-bit CDF
implementation: expand exp(-t^2/2), integrate each term on [0,z], and use the
Taylor remainder
  |remainder integral| <=Lbar*(Lbar^2/2)^(k+1)/(k+1)!.
Taking k larger than a constant times Lbar^2 and log(Lbar/delta) makes this
<=delta. Its rational terms have polynomial bit lengths. The constant
1/sqrt(2pi) can be enclosed using the usual convergent rational arctangent
series for pi and bisection for the square root. Elementary exp/log/sinch
evaluations can similarly use range reduction and certified series. No exact
normal, CDF, root, or real-number comparison oracle is needed.
