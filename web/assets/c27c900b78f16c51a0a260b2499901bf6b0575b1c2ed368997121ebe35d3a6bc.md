## Main results

Two complementary model-level results were reconstructed.

First, if a finite-state CTMC has a set of transitions whose rates are all scaled by a scalar signal parameter θ, while the other rates stay fixed, the full observed trajectory has log-likelihood ratio

\[
K_T\log(\theta_1/\theta_0)-(\theta_1-\theta_0)A_T,
\]

where (K_T) is the count of signal-scaled transitions and (A_T) is their integrated baseline hazard. Thus these two quantities are sufficient for binary discrimination. Its KL information is exactly the Poisson-rate divergence times expected integrated activity. If the activity is bounded by (a_{\max}), no terminal output or downstream memory state can achieve sensitivity η and false-positive rate ε unless

\[
T\ge\frac{d_{\rm Ber}(\eta\Vert\epsilon)}{a_{\max}d_{\rm Pois}(\theta_1\Vert\theta_0)}.
\]

Only a product such as sensor gain times input strength is identifiable when every transition rate depends on that product alone. The proof and full accounting are in [the CTMC likelihood note](proofs/ctmc_rate_scaling.md).

A more general CTMC ceiling complements the rate-scaled special case. For two finite-state generators with common edge support and initial laws \(\mu_0,\mu_1\), the full path KL is initial divergence plus the time integral of the state-occupancy-weighted row divergences
\[
D(\mu_0\Vert\mu_1)+\int_0^T\sum_i\Pr_0(X_t=i)\sum_{j\ne i}[q^0_{ij}\log(q^0_{ij}/q^1_{ij})-q^0_{ij}+q^1_{ij}]dt.
\]
Any common hidden, sampled, or compressed readout has no greater KL, and its equal-prior binary error is at least \(e^{-D_T}/4\). Thus rate ratio, support, and observation horizon must be charged; state count alone is not an information budget. Two exact counterexamples sharpen identifiability: hidden within-block rates can vary while a strongly lumped visible path law remains identical for all times, and distinct positive cyclic generators can have exactly the same one-lag transition matrix. These are model-level nonidentifiability witnesses, not finite-sample failures. See [the general CTMC path report](repertoire_search/FINAL_REPORT.md) for proofs, rate caps, Fisher-information contraction, Chernoff coefficient, and synthetic discriminators.

Second, for a serial (m)-stage race with hypothesis-independent advance rates (k_i) and reset hazards (\mu_0>\mu_1>0), the endpoint probabilities obey

\[
p_0\ge p_1^{\mu_0/\mu_1}.
\]

This is an exact stage-count-independent false-positive floor at fixed sensitivity. For fixed stage count and sensitivity, equal per-stage success probabilities minimize false positives; the finite-(m) optimum converges to the power-law floor. Under (H_1), the mean time to response-or-reset is exactly ((1-p_1)/\mu_1) at fixed sensitivity. At the equalized optimizer, increasing stage count drives the per-stage forward rate and expected stage-event count linearly upward; the limiting power-law floor thus assumes unbounded rate, state, and event resources. A hard forward-rate cap K implies \(m\le \log(p_1)/\log(K/(K+\mu_1))\). Conditional-positive latency approaches \((-\log p_1)/\mu_1\), while the (H_0) runtime and energy remain distinct costs. See [the serial-race proof](proofs/serial_reset_tradeoff.md).

A bounded arithmetic check used (\mu_1=1,\mu_0=2,p_1=1/4). At (m=1,2,8,64), the optimal false-positive rates were (0.142857,0.111111,0.076729,0.064364), approaching the theorem's limit (1/16=0.0625); the (H_1) mean run time stayed (3/4). The reproducible calculation is [serial_reset_check.py](checks/serial_reset_check.py). This verifies only those formulas at those parameter values.

A further matched comparison shows why an exponentially large endpoint likelihood ratio need not mean more information. In an N-slot Bernoulli-mark CTMC with common hypothesis-independent exponential event times, the all-pass bit has success probability \(p_h^N\), but its KL is at most the full-mark KL \(N d_{\rm Ber}(p_0\Vert p_1)\), strictly less when N≥2 and the hypotheses differ. Its full state path captures \(d_{\rm Ber}(p_0\Vert p_1)\) per expected active mark; a count-threshold CTMC with \(N(N+1)/2+2\) reachable states exactly implements the Neyman–Pearson decision using one terminal bit at the same N jumps and mean time. This is a state/readout/information tradeoff in a specified model, not a general memory advantage; proof, baseline, and costs are in [the matched finite-stage report](affinity_maturation/FINAL_REPORT.md).

An independent reset-chain specialization sharpens the role of internal states. In the N+1-state chain with forward rate theta and reset rate rho, the stationary full-path information rate is [1-(theta1/(theta1+rho))^N] d_Pois(theta1||theta0). Increasing N raises the active-time fraction with geometric diminishing returns; it never beats the always-active Poisson rate at the same maximum sensitive hazard. By contrast, a single terminal-state observation after a long run remains one draw from a full-support stationary distribution and cannot drive both error probabilities to zero. A path likelihood test can be consistent because it accumulates events and exposure over time. Full proof and explicit record costs are in [the reset-chain CTMC report](kinetic_proofreading/FINAL_REPORT.md).

The redirected hidden-memory analysis provides a third, distinct identifiability result. With (k) transient pure-death modes, delay-only terminal discrimination is a sum of (k) exponentials. Under distinct rates and nonzero amplitudes, (2k) equally spaced exact means identify the unordered decay rates and occupancy-times-effect products through a Hankel pencil. No density of delay-only measurements separates occupancy from per-mode effect; a labeled occupancy observable or supplied occupancy calibration is needed. The complete child proof, finite-data costs, and failures are in [the memory identifiability report](immune_memory/FINAL_REPORT.md).

## Prior-art boundary and verdict

The core kinetic-proofreading idea and speed/error tradeoffs are established prior art, beginning with [Hopfield (1974)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.71.10.4135). Later work directly optimizes speed, accuracy, and dissipation for multistage networks ([Chiuchiu, Mondal, and Pigolotti (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/acc757)); receptor models also show that accuracy depends on whether the output is measured by first-passage time or product count ([2024 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11192422/)). A 2026 preprint gives conditions under which non-Markovian stall-time variability can reverse the usual speed/accuracy tradeoff ([Biswas and Mahadevan (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12795)). These results prevent a universal nature claim. The finite-memory testing literature already studies memory-versus-error tradeoffs for time-invariant machines ([Berg, Shayevitz, and Ordentlich (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07445)). Continuous-time path likelihood sufficient statistics are also standard; see [Maystre et al. (UAI 2022)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/maystre22a.html).

The present contributions are self-contained specializations to explicit observation interfaces: a rate-scaled CTMC path-information bound, serial-reset endpoint tradeoff, and matched finite-stage state/readout comparison. They may be useful as baselines for a later model-specific study, but no priority or novelty claim is made. The exponential-sum reconstruction is classical Prony analysis, and CTMC hidden-state identifiability has extensive prior art including [Blackwell and Koopmans (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177706802), [Rydén (1996)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3215346), and [Allman, Matias, and Rhodes (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1214/09-AOS689).

## Best next mathematical gate

The serial-race theorem assumes a single uniform reset hazard ratio and hypothesis-independent forward rates. The next useful extension is to general transient CTMC generators with multiple reset paths: characterize the best endpoint discrimination achievable at fixed sensitivity and integrated activity, and identify when a finite-state memory design reaches the full-path likelihood bound. Any implementation must compare against the full-path likelihood-ratio test under the same path, time, event, state, and numerical-precision budget.
