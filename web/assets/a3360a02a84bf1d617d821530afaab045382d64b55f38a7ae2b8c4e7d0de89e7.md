# Cycle 3: adaptive feed pulses for a state-dependent CLE-alias pair

**Status.** I constructed a two-stage, mass-action CTMC pair with identical full-state CLE drift and covariance for every state and every feed waveform. An exact finite-horizon controller chooses the second feed pulse from the first product mark and lowers equal-prior Bayes error from $193/4050=0.0476543$ for the best fixed two-pulse schedule to $1309/32400=0.0404012$, with the same two reaction events and two product observations when per-pulse costs are equal. This is an internally derived, exact-rational model result. The generic first-two-signature mechanism and adaptive-testing method are established prior art; no physical catalyst, net energetic advantage, external proof review, or novelty priority is claimed.

The construction extends the local one-source, one-turnover example in `work/agents/chemistry_kinetic_observability/FINAL_REPORT.md` by using two successive state-dependent source complexes and feedback between them. It does not reuse a single-source compound-Poisson experiment: the first reaction changes the catalyst state, the controller measures that stage's product and selects the second substrate pulse, and the second reaction then takes place from a different mass-action source.

## Prior-art and local-prior boundary

For a mass-action reaction with jump vector ν and propensity $a(x)$, the CLE drift and covariance contributions are $a(x)\nu$ and $a(x)\nu\nu^T$. Faul, Hoessly and Xia's 2026 source-wise identifiability result and Remark 3.5 state that the CLE sees rate constants through the lifted signatures ($ν,νν^T$), separately at each source complex. Their result supplies the nullspace mechanism below; I make no novelty claim for it. Gillespie's CLE derivation requires a mesoscopic interval with many firings while propensities stay nearly constant. A single-catalyst, event-resolved assay is deliberately a jump-process regime; here CLE equality is a formal generator alias, not a claim that the CLE accurately approximates the assay.

Higher-order stochastic-CRN moment hierarchies are also established: Smith and Krishnamurthy derive the all-order factorial-moment generator. Adaptive choice of experiments from previous outcomes is established controlled-sensing theory. In particular, Nitinawarat, Atia and Veeravalli show that in binary fixed-sample testing the stationary open-loop policy is asymptotically optimal in error exponent, while finite-horizon causal policies are a separate decision problem. The two-stage numerical gain here is only a finite-horizon, model-specific consequence, not a general theorem about adaptive control.

Current isotope-labeling practice already optimizes tracer dose, pulse duration, fasting, precursor and delivery route; the 2025 mouse bolus study is a direct experimental example. The 2025 13CFLUX preprint supports transient and multiple-tracer model workflows. These sources motivate charging label/feed and observation resources. They do not provide this event-conditioned controller. Also, a stable isotope tracer is generally intended to report metabolism with little perturbation; the action-specific rate profiles below must be measured and cannot be assumed to arise merely from replacing $^{12}$C with $^{13}$C.

## Two-stage CRN and exact CLE identity

There are catalyst states $C_0,C_1,C_2$, two externally gated feed species $F_0,F_1$, and stage-resolved reporter products $U_i,L_i$, $i=0,1$. For stage $i$, available feed mode $a\in\{0,1\}$, and mark $k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, the reaction is

\[
C_i+F_a\xrightarrow{\kappa^{H}_{a,k}} C_{i+1}+(4-k)U_i+kL_i,
\qquad H\in\{A,B\}.
\]

At any instant the controller clamps one $F_a$ to normalized concentration 1 and the other to 0. The count-process propensity is $\kappa^{H}_{a,k}C_iF_a$. The same rate profile is used at both stages. One event moves $C_i$ to $C_{i+1}$, making the second active source different from the first. The output mark $k$ is resolved from the stage-specific $U_i,L_i$ increment. This is an abstract open-CRN/reporting interface; a real implementation still needs an atom-balanced substrate/cofactor and reporter map. No enzyme or isotope chemistry has been validated.

For a fixed source $(i,a)$, write the jump as
\[
\nu_{i,a,k}=b_{i,a}+ku_i,
\quad b_{i,a}=-e_{C_i}+e_{C_{i+1}}-e_{F_a}+4e_{U_i},
\quad u_i=e_{L_i}-e_{U_i}.
\]
If additional atom-balance or reporter-reservoir coordinates are needed, they belong in $b_{i,a}$ and $u_i$; the derivation only uses this affine-in-$k$ form.

Set $\alpha=(1,-3,3,-1)$, ordered by $k=1,2,3,4$. Direct calculation gives
\[
\sum_k\alpha_k k^j=0\quad(j=0,1,2),\qquad
\sum_k\alpha_k k^3=-6.
\]
For action $a$, choose $\epsilon_0=1/2,\epsilon_1=1$, positive slack vectors
\[
s_0=(0.121,1.347,0.005,3.527),\qquad
s_1=(0.166,0.417,0.405,0.012),
\]
and base rates $r_{a,k}=\epsilon_a|\alpha_k|+s_{a,k}$. The two networks have fixed, action-indexed rate constants
\[
\kappa^A_{a,k}=r_{a,k}+\epsilon_a\alpha_k,
\qquad
\kappa^B_{a,k}=r_{a,k}-\epsilon_a\alpha_k.
\]
All rates are strictly positive and each action has common total rate 9. Rates are in time$^{-1}$ at normalized feed concentration and $C_i=1$; for standard molecule-count mass action, absorb the volume factor into the displayed bimolecular constants.

| Feed action | $\epsilon_a$ | $\kappa^A_{a,1:4}$ | $\kappa^B_{a,1:4}$ | common $M_1,M_2$ | $M_3^A,M_3^B$ |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| $F_0$ | (1/2) | (1.121, 1.347, 3.005, 3.527) | (0.121, 4.347, 0.005, 4.527) | 26.938, 89.986 | 318.760, 324.760 |
| $F_1$ | (1) | (2.166, 0.417, 6.405, 0.012) | (0.166, 6.417, 0.405, 2.012) | 22.263, 61.671 | 179.205, 191.205 |

Here $M_j^H(a)=\sum_k\kappa^H_{a,k}k^j$, with $M_0=9$ in both models and all actions. The exact rates and rational moments are in `adaptive_cle_pair.py`.

At every source and for every feed setting, the difference of the first two rate-weighted jump signatures vanishes:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_k(\kappa^A_{a,k}-\kappa^B_{a,k})\nu_{i,a,k}
 &=2\epsilon_a\sum_k\alpha_k(b_{i,a}+ku_i)=0,\\
\sum_k(\kappa^A_{a,k}-\kappa^B_{a,k})\nu_{i,a,k}\nu_{i,a,k}^T
 &=2\epsilon_a\sum_k\alpha_k(b_{i,a}+ku_i)(b_{i,a}+ku_i)^T=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second line is a degree-two polynomial in $k$, so only the three annihilated sums enter. Multiplying by the common mass-action factor $C_iF_a$ and summing over both stages and either/both feed sources preserves the identity. Thus the full state-space CLE drift $A(x,t)=\sum a_r\nu_r$ and covariance $D(x,t)=\sum a_r\nu_r\nu_r^T$ agree pointwise for every nonnegative state and every deterministic feed waveform $F_0(t),F_1(t)$. Under a common well-posedness condition, their diffusion generators/laws agree up to the same stopping boundary.

The first missed jump tensor is third order:
\[
\mathcal K_A^{(3)}(x,t)-\mathcal K_B^{(3)}(x,t)
=\sum_{i,a} C_iF_a\,2\epsilon_a\sum_k\alpha_k(b_{i,a}+ku_i)^{\otimes3}
=-12\sum_{i,a}\epsilon_a C_iF_a\,u_i^{\otimes3}.
\]
The $F_1$ pulse therefore doubles this local third-order coefficient relative to $F_0$ while preserving the CLE alias. This is the concrete control target. The optimal likelihood test below uses the full mark probabilities, not just a sample estimate of the third cumulant.

## Measurable two-pulse schedule and matched static test

Use a single catalyst starting at $C_0=1$. During stage 0, set $F_1=1,F_0=0$ until the first reaction occurs; record the product category $k_0$ and hold before proceeding. The total hazard is 9 under either network, so the stage-0 waiting time is $\mathrm{Exp}(9)$ under both. From the observed $k_0$, select the stage-1 pulse by
\[
 a_1(k_0)=\begin{cases}
 1,&k_0\in\{1,3\},\\
 0,&k_0\in\{2,4\}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Pulse $F_{a_1}=1$ with the other feed at zero until the next reaction, record $k_1$, and stop at $C_2$. The second event also has total hazard 9 under both networks and both actions. Each wait is independent of its mark and hypothesis in this model, so holding times add no likelihood information.

The mark probabilities are $p^H_a(k)=\kappa^H_{a,k}/9$. In yield order $(1,2,3,4)$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & p^A_a & p^B_a\\\hline
F_0 &(1121/9000,449/3000,601/1800,3527/9000)&(121/9000,483/1000,1/1800,503/1000)\\
F_1 &(361/1500,139/3000,427/600,1/750)&(83/4500,713/1000,9/200,503/2250).
\end{array}
\]
For equal prior, after $F_1$ mark $k_0$, the posterior probability of A is
\[
\Pr(A\mid k_0=1,2,3,4)=(1083/1166,139/2278,427/454,3/506).
\]
The action rule above is exactly optimal for the second pulse under terminal 0–1 Bayes loss. For each possible first mark, it minimizes
\[
\frac12\sum_{k_1}\min\{p^A_1(k_0)p^A_{a_1}(k_1),\ p^B_1(k_0)p^B_{a_1}(k_1)\}.
\]
After both marks, decide A iff
\[
\Lambda=\log\frac{p^A_1(k_0)}{p^B_1(k_0)}+
\log\frac{p^A_{a_1(k_0)}(k_1)}{p^B_{a_1(k_0)}(k_1)}>0.
\]
There are no LR ties in this instance. The controller is Bayes-optimal among all two-step causal policies using this two-action menu: the first action is one of two choices, and after each of its four outputs the displayed minimization exhausts the two second actions; the final LR test is optimal for that policy.

| First/second policy | Equal-prior Bayes error |
|---|---:|
| Fixed $F_0,F_0$ | $3251/20250=0.1605432$ |
| Fixed $F_0,F_1$ | $7753/162000=0.0478580$ |
| Fixed $F_1,F_0$ | $7753/162000=0.0478580$ |
| Fixed $F_1,F_1$, best open loop | $193/4050=0.0476543$ |
| Adaptive first $F_0$, optimal second pulse | $7753/162000=0.0478580$ |
| Adaptive first $F_1$, then $F_1,F_0,F_1,F_0$ after marks (1,2,3,4) | $1309/32400=0.0404012$ |

Thus the finite-sample reduction is $47/6480\approx0.00725309$, or $235/1544\approx15.22\%$ relative to the strongest fixed schedule. These are exact rational enumerations in the attached script. Every static schedule gets the exact CTMC likelihood-ratio decision on the same two outputs; randomizing among fixed schedules cannot improve the minimum because it only averages their Bayes risks. A CLE-only or ODE-only observer sees identical laws and cannot do better than prior error $1/2$. The static CTMC likelihood test is the meaningful comparator.

## Noise, hidden states, and falsification

The exact result assumes (i) candidate models and their action-indexed rates are known; (ii) initial state is exactly $C_0=1$; (iii) $C_0\to C_1\to C_2$ is the known progression, with no competing reactions, product feedback, reverse reaction, or hidden catalyst conformation over the two-event window; (iv) each active feed is clamped to normalized concentration 1; (v) product category $k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ is read at each stage before the second pulse; and (vi) the two source events are independent conditional on chosen controls. Unknown rate constants, source-state uncertainty, cross-talk, or extra chemical pathways require a hidden-state/composite likelihood and invalidate the numeric guarantee until incorporated.

I also pushed both mark distributions through a known symmetric observation channel that, with probability $\rho$, replaces the true label by a uniformly random label (including the possibility of retaining it), and otherwise reports it exactly. Exact DP gives:

| Replacement probability $\rho$ | Best fixed error | Best adaptive error |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.0476543 | 0.0404012 |
| 0.05 | 0.0706469 | 0.0654204 |
| 0.10 | 0.0909800 | 0.0889150 |
| 0.20 | 0.1274410 | 0.1274410 |

The action gain is therefore sensitive to readout noise; by $\rho=0.20$ it vanishes for this menu. This is a known-channel calculation only. The detector confusion matrix must be measured before claiming the result; an unknown or adversarial channel could erase or reverse the policy ordering.

The experiment uses exactly two feed-consuming reactions and two mark observations on successful runs. With unit feed concentration and $C_i=1$, each active pulse has expected duration $1/9$, so expected catalytic waiting time is $2/9$. One feed molecule is consumed at each event. Under equal per-molecule feed costs $c_F$, both best fixed and adaptive schedules use feed cost $2c_F$, two measurements $2c_{obs}$, and two base pulse commands. The adaptive policy additionally needs an online first-stage readout, a posterior update, and possibly a feed switch. Under equal prior, $F_0$ is chosen for pulse 2 with probability $4429/9000$, and $F_1$ with probability $4571/9000$; hence
\[
C_{\rm feed}^{\rm adapt}=(1+4571/9000)c_1+(4429/9000)c_0,
\]
versus $C_{\rm feed}^{\rm static}=2c_1$ for best fixed $F_1,F_1$. The expected extra switch cost is $(4429/9000)c_{sw}$, and the adaptive-only online readout/update costs are $c_{online}+c_{update}$. With a scalarized objective (Bayes error $+\lambda\,\mathrm{cost}$), adaptation is preferred over the best fixed schedule only when
\[
\lambda\left[(4429/9000)(c_0-c_1+c_{sw})+c_{online}+c_{update}\right] < 47/6480,
\]
assuming common $2c_{obs}$, base pulse-command and clock costs. If feed work is to be reported in energy, replace $c_a$ with measured chemical potentials/work per consumed feed molecule; isotope precursor purchase, delivery, calibration, event detection and MS/NMR time also need their measured costs. No numerical energy/work efficiency is claimed.

The decisive practical gate is whether a system can expose stage-specific $k$ fast enough to choose the second pulse without altering or losing the catalyst state. The labels may require event-resolved MS/NMR or a validated proxy; ordinary bulk isotopologue measurements do not automatically provide this online signal. A physical implementation also needs a mechanism that produces the two rate profiles while preserving a sufficiently small first-two-moment mismatch. Stable-isotope substitution alone is not presumed to create the action contrast.

**Falsifiers.** The CLE-alias assertion fails if any calibrated source/action pair has nonzero differences in $M_0,M_1,M_2$, or if the measured reaction vector is not affine in the mark $k$ as assumed. The adaptive-risk claim fails if the actual action-conditioned mark distributions or readout channel, inserted into the exact DP, give no lower risk than the best fixed schedule after all measured costs are applied. The two-model comparison is rejected if observed label sequences fail a predeclared multinomial/CTMC goodness-of-fit check under both candidate models. Failure to obtain an online stage-0 readout or to guarantee the $C_1$ state blocks implementation of feedback and leaves only the static comparator.
