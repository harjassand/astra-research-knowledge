ASSIGNMENT
Audit whether the supplied coordinate balance for determinant-weighted
parity hole ratios can be acquired without count oracles through determinant
leverage scores or convex optimization.

RESULT
No general count-free acquisition method or general hardness reduction was
proved. The strongest exact negative result is a family showing that the
unfiltered determinant DPP normalizer/logdet and all diagonal leverage
scores do not determine the required balance: for rational orthogonal F,
V=[I,F^T] has VV^T=2I and all eight diagonal scores 1/2, whereas a required
row maximum is 1/(2s^2)=Theta(2^(2L)) for an O(L)-bit weak rotation. The
hard-pair event in unfiltered rejection has probability Theta(2^(-2L)).
The exact formulas, full derivation, and finite rational diagnostics are in
INITIAL.txt and orthogonal_balance_probe.py/.out. The family refutes these
specific surrogates, not every algorithm given F: the full DPP projection
kernel contains F in off-diagonal blocks, and the family itself is visibly
structured.

The deeper interface result is a separation in the convex route. Once the
exact symmetric pair-ratio matrix G_ij=g(ij) is known and each row has
positive support, the scaling problem is a convex geometric program:
minimize sum_i exp(x_i) subject to x_i+x_j>=log G_ij. A minimizer exists;
KKT stationarity and complementary slackness imply every row has a tight
neighbor, so congruence scaling F'=D F D calibrates every row maximum to 1.
Rounding each optimum coordinate upward by at most a factor of two preserves
the required [1/4,1] interval. This shows scaling is not the bottleneck
after G is acquired. The program's input is precisely the unknown hard-hole
ratio matrix, so it does not close the count-free acquisition gap. Rows
with no positive pair support cannot be balanced by this scaling.

The audit also distinguishes the diagonal leverage vector and Gram
normalizer from the full projection kernel: the former stay fixed in the
weak-block family; the latter retains F. A second direct estimator attack
uses the positive random-phase Pfaffian norm. For q swap blocks, its
independent Rademacher estimator is nonzero with probability 2^-q and has
relative variance 2^q-1; uniform circle phases have relative second moment
(3/2)^q. This rejects these independent phase-averaging estimators as
general polynomial-cost relative estimators, not coupled ratio methods,
structure-aware samplers, or an FPRAS. See revisions/R3_scaling_vs_acquisition.txt
for derivations and the phase-estimator boundary.

Also audited: (a) the required ratios are the log-Hessian entries of the
stable hole polynomial at zero, but stability/support does not give its
value or gradient oracle; (b) direct Rademacher determinant identities
have exponentially many signed terms and exponential cancellation on the
weak-block instance (revisions/R1_signed_character_balance.txt); (c) a
previous six-site exact example refutes the inference that a balanced soft
dual supplies useful hard-hole information; (d) constant-determinant
Pfaffian parity restrictions do admit count-derived ratios when the
constant-determinant certificate is supplied for every required hole
restriction, but this is a proper subclass, not a generic squared-minor
count oracle. See revisions/R2_balance_oracle_boundary.txt and its cited
primary source, Matoya–Oki, arXiv:1912.00620.

STATUS / NEXT GATE
Specific diagonal-leverage, logdet-surrogate, unfiltered rejection, signed
term averaging, and independent phase-averaging routes are blocked by exact
examples. Post-acquisition convex scaling is established under positive
row support. The decisive unresolved gate remains a polynomial-bit method
that estimates all needed g(ij) multiplicatively, or certifies a balance,
directly from arbitrary rational-complex F while charging precision,
zero ratios, all pair queries, and runtime. No general computational
impossibility is claimed.
