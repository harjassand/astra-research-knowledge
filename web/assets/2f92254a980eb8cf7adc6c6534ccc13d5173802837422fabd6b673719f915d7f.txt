THEOREM: PATH LENGTH AND METRIC STABILITY

Let A in SL(2,Z) be hyperbolic with positive eigenvalues lambda, lambda^-1,
lambda>1, and put H=log(lambda). On
  M_A=(T^2 x R)/((x,t)~(Ax,t-1)),
let G_t be a smooth positive symmetric 2x2 matrix with det G_t=1 and
G_(t+1)=A^T G_t A. Give M_A the metric
  g=dt^2+dx^T G_t dx
and set X=partial_t. Let s(t)>=0 be the positive eigenvalue of
(1/2)G_t^-1 Gdot_t, and define
  Delta=integral_0^1 s(t)^2 dt-H^2.

The normalized smooth volume is dx_1 dx_2 dt, X is unit and divergence-free,
and |Def X|^2=2s^2. The time-one map on a fundamental fiber is A^-1 (the
inverse depends on the quotient convention); its Haar entropy is
H=log(lambda). Thus the entropy and power statements use the smooth-volume
Pesin formula / toral-automorphism entropy as imported standard results.

Put P={G=G^T>0:det G=1} with
  ||dot G||_G^2=(1/2) tr[(G^-1 dot G)^2].
The coordinates
  G(x+iy)=y^-1 [[1,x],[x,x^2+y^2]]
identify P isometrically with the curvature -1 upper half-plane. The fiber
path t -> G_t has speed 2s. The isometry T_A(G)=A^T G A has translation
length ell=2H; its axis consists of forms in which A's two real eigenlines
are orthogonal. Indeed, the eigenvalues of the relative form
G^-1/2 A^T G A G^-1/2 are sigma_G(A)^2 and sigma_G(A)^-2, where sigma_G(A)
is the operator norm in the G-inner product. Hence
  d_P(G,T_A G)=2 log sigma_G(A)>=2 log rho(A)=2H,
with equality exactly on the axis.

The deck condition gives G_1=T_A(G_0), so d0=d_P(G_0,G_1)=ell+delta0
for 0<=delta0. Write the path length as L=2 integral s=ell+epsilon;
then 0<=delta0<=epsilon. Jensen gives
  Delta >= (L/2)^2-H^2 = H epsilon+epsilon^2/4,
so epsilon<=2(sqrt(H^2+Delta)-H)<=Delta/H.

For any t, let a,b be the distances from G_t to G_0,G_1 and c=d0. The
subpath lengths imply a+b-c<=epsilon-delta0. In the Euclidean comparison
triangle, if E=a+b-c, Heron's formula bounds the altitude h by
  h^2 <= c E/2+E^2/4
       <= ell epsilon/2+epsilon^2/4
       = H epsilon+epsilon^2/4 <= Delta.
CAT(0) comparison therefore puts G_t within sqrt(Delta) of the geodesic
segment [G_0,G_1].

For an arbitrary starting metric, the hyperbolic Fermi-coordinate formula
about the axis is sinh(d0/2)=cosh(r0)sinh(H), where
r0=dist_P(G_0,Axis(T_A)). Since d0<=ell+epsilon,
  r0<=arcosh(sinh(H+epsilon/2)/sinh(H)).
Distance to a convex geodesic is convex along geodesics, and G_0,G_1 have
same distance r0 to the axis. Thus
  sup_t dist_P(G_t,Axis(T_A)) <= sqrt(Delta)+r0.
If G_0 lies on the axis, the segment [G_0,G_1] is itself an axis segment,
so the path is within sqrt(Delta) of that segment. More strongly, parameterize
that segment by unit hyperbolic arclength gamma:[0,2H]->P, with gamma(0)=G_0.
Let u_t be the coordinate of the metric projection of G_t onto this segment,
and L_1(t)=2 integral_0^t s. The CAT(0) tube gives
|d(G_0,G_t)-u_t|<=sqrt(Delta). The subpath slack obeys
0<=L_1(t)-d(G_0,G_t)<=epsilon, while
  integral_0^1 (s-H)^2 dt=Delta-H epsilon<=Delta.
Cauchy–Schwarz gives |L_1(t)-2Ht|<=2sqrt(Delta). Combining these three
bounds yields
  sup_t d_P(G_t,gamma(2Ht)) <= 4sqrt(Delta)+epsilon.
This compares the actual metric path with one fixed Sol path in physical
time, under the stated axis-initial condition. If d_P(G,G')<=rho, relative
fiber-metric eigenvalues lie in [exp(-rho),exp(rho)], so the result controls
uniform multiplicative transverse-length distortion. It gives no derivative,
curvature, or frame-regularity bound.

In particular, Delta=0 forces epsilon=0, G_0 on the axis, and the path to
be the constant-speed axis geodesic: equality in this subclass is exactly
the Sol metric path. The implication follows from the tube estimate, the
vanishing variance integral, and the fixed-gauge comparison above.

EXACT PERTURBATION FAMILY AND REPRODUCIBILITY

In an orthonormal eigenbasis of A, set D_t=diag(exp(Ht),exp(-Ht)) and
  G_t=D_t Gtilde_t D_t,
  Gtilde_t=[[exp(u),v],[v,(1+v^2)exp(-u)]],
where u,v are periodic. Its upper-half-plane coordinate is
z_t=(v+i)exp(-2Ht-u), and the exact identity is
  4s(t)^2=(2H+u')^2+((2H+u')v-v')^2.
For u=a sin(2 pi t), v=b sin(2 pi t), direct exact integration gives
  Delta=pi^2 a^2/2 + H^2 b^2/2 + pi^2 b^2/2 + pi^2 a^2 b^2/8.
The distance to the Sol axis is asinh(|v(t)|), hence
  max_t dist_P(G_t,Axis)=asinh(|b|)<=|b|<=sqrt(Delta).
The exact checks in diagnostics/exact_path_identity.py pass for determinant,
deck gluing, speed, and integrated deficit. The floating diagnostic
mapping_torus_path.py separately uses Simpson quadrature and sampled
axis distances for three parameter sets; it is illustrative only, not a
certified interval calculation or proof of the CAT(0) theorem.
