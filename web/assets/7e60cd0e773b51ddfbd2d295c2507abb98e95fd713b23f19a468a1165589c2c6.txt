0. Exact question and conclusion

The sensitive NP transpose-decoder step is not composition of chart changes on
a fixed stalk. For charts based at v,w on the same stalk g,
G_g^{v,w}=Psi_g^w(Psi_g^v)^(-1), hence G_g^{w,u}G_g^{v,w}=G_g^{v,u} by
cancellation. The missing inference in the original T14 route was that local
syndrome inverses / unencoding choices commute with those transitions and with
noninvertible incidence maps. That inference is false in general, even for a
small exact local complex with a symmetry preserving all its maps.

Sol's actual-stalk construction avoids this inference: its arrays retain
entries in actual dual stalks, use Delta,T and fixed linear splittings, and
make no transition-equivariance requirement of the splittings. The finite test
below refutes the naive naturality requirement only. Sol v2's fixed-color
sweep extension passes the independent sign/count/threshold audit below,
subject to the precise static-circuit/reset interpretation in §4. This supports
the internally-derived, source-dependent decoder theorem; it does not establish
the source family or external novelty.

1. Minimal exact local chart-symmetry stress test

Over F_2 take the exact sequence

    0 -> U=F_2 --E--> W=F_2^2 --H--> V=F_2 -> 0,
    E(a)=(a,a),                 H(x_1,x_2)=x_1+x_2.

Here HE=0, ker(H)={(0,0),(1,1)}=im(E), E is injective and H is onto.
Let P:W->W swap coordinates. Then P^2=I, PE=E and HP=H, so P is a monomial
automorphism of this local exact code complex, acting trivially on U and V.
It is a valid change between two local coordinate charts on W preserving the
code and syndrome maps.

A right inverse S:V->W of H must have H(S(1))=1; thus S(1) is e_1 or e_2.
P exchanges those two choices; the only P-fixed vectors in W are 00 and 11,
both in ker(H). Consequently no right inverse can obey P S=S when the chart
action on V is identity. Exactness, a fixed local code and a monomial symmetry
do not guarantee a symmetry-equivariant choice of syndrome preimage. Any
deterministic tie-breaker is chart-dependent in this example.

This is a one-overlap stress test, not a counterexample to the GGJ arithmetic
family and not a claim of nontrivial holonomy around a GGJ stalk loop. In GGJ,
charts at one fixed actual stalk are maps out of that same stalk, so transition
products telescope. The test isolates the remaining issue: local splittings
need not be natural even when a transition is a valid symmetry. Sol's
gauge-covariant formulation is appropriate: transport the chosen splitting
under a basis change, or choose another splitting; support guarantees persist
although the decoder tie-break may change.

3. Independent audit of the fixed-color constant-depth extension

Target: work/sol/local_chart_decoder/v2.txt, read with v1. At fixed t, local
stalk dimensions, field and code parameters, the promised circuit receives
only the supplied noisy syndrome. The data cochain is used only in the
correctness proof. Incidences, local tables and a fixed coloring may be
preprocessed/hardwired. Status: internally derived and source-dependent, not
an independently verified GGJ family theorem.

(a) Gain support inequality. For a vertex v, g_v(b) is the maximum decrease
of global syndrome-block weight from a correction supported in its upward
star. At an ell-face u<d, the local gain q_u is achievable by a legal
correction above every vertex v<=u, hence q_u<=g_{a(u)} for the chosen vertex.
At ell=d the correction space is zero and q_u=0. In the support recursion,
the coefficient r_{d,ell}q_u cancels the r_{d,ell} in the unwound denominator.
For a chosen vertex the number of relevant (u,f) pairs is at most
N_{d,ell}=binom(t,ell)binom(t-ell,d-ell)n_+^d. Therefore the gain coefficient
is

    J_d = sum_{ell<d} (d-ell)! N_{d,ell}
                    / product_{h=ell+1}^d r_{d,h},

giving (1-lambda L_d)|b| <= Q_d|b|^2/Z_d + M_d|delta b| +
J_d sum_v g_v(b). If |b|<=a_d Z_d/2 this implies
c_d|b|<=M_d|delta b|+J_d sum_v g_v(b), c_d=(1-lambda L_d)/2>0.
This is aggregate available decrease, not a claim that each nonzero symbol
has a useful singleton flip.

(b) Fixed coloring and lost-gain charge. A d-face has 2^d vertices and each
vertex belongs to at most ell_d=binom(t,d)n_+^d d-faces. The conflict graph
on vertices with intersecting complete syndrome stars has degree at most
(2^d-1)ell_d. A fixed greedy coloring uses at most
Q_col=1+(2^d-1)ell_d colors. Within a color, syndrome stars are disjoint, so
their locally computed gains add exactly. Data supports are disjoint too:
if two stars shared a j-face, that face has an upper (j+1)-face since j<t,
giving a shared syndrome block and hence a conflict.

The gain function is 2-Lipschitz in Hamming weight on its star: the current
weight and the minimum over translated weights are each 1-Lipschitz. An
accepted update has positive integer gain and changes at most ell_d syndrome
blocks, so its changed support is at most ell_d times its gain. A changed
block belongs to at most 2^d vertex stars. If tau(v) is the time v's color is
processed, sum_v g_v(b_{tau(v)}) is the sweep decrease Delta_B. Triangle
inequality yields

    sum_v |b_0-b_{tau(v)}|_{star(v)} <= 2^d ell_d Delta_B,
    sum_v g_v(b_0) <= [1+2^{d+1}ell_d] Delta_B.

Thus earlier updates destroying later gains are charged correctly. The
no-zero-gain rule is essential: otherwise support could change without
positive potential decrease. Fixed color order requires no global maximum,
aggregate gain computation or adaptive color choice.

(c) Noise floor and round invariants. If b=delta e+m plus accumulated
coboundaries, delta b=delta m throughout because delta^2=0. Syndrome weight
does not increase during a sweep, preserving the small-support premise.
With C_B=M_d D_d/c_d and
tau=min{1/2,c_d/(J_d C_gain,d)}, the gain bound gives
Delta_B>=tau(|b|-C_B M) whenever |b|>=C_B M. Below that threshold,
nonnegative decrease alone gives the same weaker recurrence. Thus in all
cases

    |b_after| <= (1-tau)|b_before| + tau C_B M.

After fixed q sweeps, |b_q|<=rho^q D_j E+(rho^q+C_B)M, rho=1-tau.
The coefficient of E can be made any fixed alpha>0 by fixed q; the
syndrome-noise coefficient is bounded by (C_B+2)/epsilon_j as in v2.

Across sweeps total positive gains telescope to at most |b_0|. Each accepted
flip has gain at least one and correction support at most ell_j, so raw
correction support is <=ell_j(D_j E+M). The noisy comparison uses this raw
bound and a small same-syndrome representative to place the residual below
cohomological distance. The final metric is stabilizer-reduced block weight,
not raw output weight; no confusion between them was found.

Promise (3.1) is preserved because corrections are coboundaries and each
sweep decreases |b|; the syndrome of b stays delta m. The separate
soundness/distance promise (3.5)/(3.8) uses the raw residual budget and the
same-syndrome small representative; it does not use an unrestricted
nearest-cocycle oracle. The K contraction can be made arbitrarily small at
fixed parameters, compensating the fixed H^L and J factors. For binary
physical Pauli weight the two sector output bounds add. Choosing each sector
block contraction <=alpha_physical/(2B), where B is maximal binary block
size, gives the whole-Pauli coefficient. The two positive data/noise promises
are intersected and shrunk; block/bit fault conversion costs at most B as
recorded in Sol v2.

(d) Static circuit and temporal scratch boundary. At fixed local parameters,
each optimal local correction is a finite function of a fixed number of
syndrome bits and has a fixed truth-table circuit. The fixed number of colors
and fixed q sweeps give constant depth. Each wire feeds a bounded number of
local circuits; fanout-two replication adds constant depth. Color/table/
incidence choices are code-dependent fixed wiring after O(N) preprocessing.
There is no global runtime reduction or cross-tile controller state in this
model.

For the quantum-fault interface, retain an explicit invocation boundary:
decoder scratch/controller state is freshly initialized, or reset at the
invocation boundary, and every reset/initialization location is included in
that invocation's tile fault mask and O(N) location count. No work state is
carried to the next round. If a persistent faulty controller could retain
state across rounds without being included in the tiles, the v2 static-circuit
theorem alone would not imply the Sol3 induction. Sol confirmed to T22/T14
that the decoder is a stateless combinational/fixed-q circuit per invocation
and scratch/reset faults are charged to the tile; the root was informed.
This is a model condition, not an algebraic decoder gap.

No sign, coefficient, recurrence, coloring, noise-threshold or block/
physical-conversion error was found in Sol v2 on its fixed-parameter
static-circuit interpretation. This is an audit, not a replacement proof;
complete derivations and source hypotheses remain in Sol v1/v2 and the PDFs.

4. Gauge covariance and scope limits

At fixed actual stalks, chart transitions compose by cancellation and are
compatible with incidence squares. Under actual-stalk basis changes, array
components change contragrediently; Delta commutes with that change and T is
intertwined by the transformed R^vee maps. Therefore K and its incidences are
carried to changed-basis copies. Transport the chosen S/P/Pi splittings with
the basis change and all identities persist. Cube contractions H/J depend on
combinatorial indices, not chart bases. Invertible within-block changes
preserve block support. If splittings are independently re-chosen after a
gauge change, the output/tie-break can change; the theorem and constants do
not. This is gauge covariance of the construction, not a claim that every
local decoder is gauge-equivariant.

No full GGJ arithmetic chart-transition/cosheaf counterexample to Sol's
actual-stalk arrays was found. The source equations and finite local test
support replacing NP's global tensor unencoding with anchored arrays, but an
independent external proof audit and validation of the GGJ good family remain
open. No claim of new coding capability, novelty or priority follows.
