# Discovery-foundations deductions and exact interfaces

Status: elementary deductions reconstructed here; the release-103 probability
approximator is an unverified premise. No unrestricted feasible-discovery theorem
is established. No exact-mass service is assumed in the conditional positive.

## 1. A sharper affine-group projection bound

Let H >= 3 and G_H = SL_H(Z) semidirect Z^H. Let S contain all signed unit
elementary matrices and signed coordinate translations; m = |S| = 2H^2,
counting the signed generators as a multiset. Assume:

* the standard eight generators of SL_2(Z) semidirect Z^2 have relative
  Kazhdan constant kappa_0 = 1/10 with respect to Z^2;
* SL_H(Z) has Kazhdan constant kappa_H > 0 for its signed elementary generators.

These are old inputs. Shalom's 1999 Theorem 2.1 states the first, and his
Theorem 2.6 gives the second. Release 103 Lemma 6.1 uses these same inputs.

For a unitary representation rho, write

    delta = max_{g in S} ||rho(g) xi - xi||.

Choose r = ceil(H/2) coordinate pairs covering all H coordinates: use a
matching, and for odd H add the pair {1,H}. For a selected pair e, let P_e
project onto the vectors fixed by translations in that pair. Relative property
(T), applied on the orthogonal complement of its translation-fixed space,
gives

    ||(I-P_e) xi|| <= 10 delta.

All the P_e commute, because they are strong limits of averages of commuting
coordinate translations. Their product is exactly P_tr, projection onto the
vectors fixed by all translations. For commuting orthogonal projections,

    I - product_e P_e <= sum_e (I-P_e)

in the operator order. This follows on their joint spectral subspaces, where
each P_e has eigenvalue 0 or 1. Therefore

    ||(I-P_tr) xi||^2 <= 100 ceil(H/2) delta^2.

The translation subgroup is normal in G_H, so P_tr commutes with the whole
representation. Its range carries a representation of SL_H(Z). Let P_inv
project onto the G_H-invariant vectors. Applying the full Kazhdan constant
to P_tr xi, and using ||rho(g)P_tr xi-P_tr xi|| <= delta, gives

    ||P_tr xi - P_inv xi|| <= kappa_H^{-1} delta.

The translation-orthogonal and translation-fixed summands are orthogonal.
Consequently

    ||xi-P_inv xi||^2
      <= [100 ceil(H/2) + kappa_H^{-2}] delta^2.            (1)

This improves the elementary constant in release 103 from

    [10 binom(H,2) + kappa_H^{-1}]^2

to the bracket in (1). It is an elementary sharpening of a known property-(T)
argument; historical novelty is not asserted.

For any finite transitive action, use the uniform measure and

    K = (1/2)I + (1/(2m)) sum_{g in S} rho(g).

Its spectrum lies in [0,1]. For f orthogonal to constants, the Dirichlet
identity is

    <f,(I-K)f> = (1/(4m)) sum_g ||rho(g)f-f||^2.

Since the sum dominates the maximum and (1) bounds that maximum from below,

    gap(K) >= 1 / {8H^2[100 ceil(H/2)+kappa_H^{-2}]}.      (2)

For the old Kassabov bound kappa_H >= (42 sqrt(H)+860)^{-1}, one may safely
use 920 in place of 860. Then (2) gives a field-size-independent explicit
Omega(H^{-3}) certificate for these particular generators. Kassabov already
studies the relative semidirect-product bounds and their algorithmic uses;
this rank dependence is not presented as a new theorem.

## 2. What the conditional sampler actually conditions on

Fix a prime p, 0 <= r < H, and 1 <= L <= p. A rank channel consists of a
uniform full-column-rank frame F in F_p^{H by r} and an independent uniform
constant column a_0. For a *known* input vector z in F_p^r, condition on

    w = Fz+a_0 belonging to {0,...,L-1}^H.

The conditional coordinates are exactly independent uniform F and w, since
a_0 = w-Fz is determined. The unconditional retention probability is
(L/p)^H, but the conditional coordinate law is explicit.

An affine element (M,c) in G_H acts by

    (F,w) -> (MF mod p, Mw+c mod L).

The action is transitive: SL_H(F_p) is transitive on ordered r-frames when
r < H, and translations can move w arbitrarily after the frame is moved.
The moduli p and L need not coincide. Thus (2) gives an expander walk for
this conditional space, including composite L, without rejecting a rare
retention event. A point-start total-variation guarantee follows from

    TV(K^t(x,.),uniform) <= (1/2) sqrt(|Omega|) exp[-gap(K)t],

where log |Omega| <= Hr log p + H log L. With the quoted kappa_H bound,
the sufficient number of steps is

    O(H^3[Hr log p + H log L + log(1/epsilon)]).

The state itself retains O(Hr log p+H log L) bits, plus supplied z and the
arithmetic workspace. A generator updates a fixed number of coordinates;
arithmetic must be charged in bits. To obtain a sampler whose bit count is
bounded on every run, pad the generator multiset to a power of two with
identity moves. This loses at most a factor two in the gap.

This is not a stronger sampler than the fully informed direct-coordinate
control. If z is available, sample F directly and then w: for r < H, the
full-rank acceptance probability is

    product_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-p^{j-H}) >= 147/256 > 0.57.

For the stated rational lower bound, take p=2 and extend the product down
to all factors k>=2. The k=2,3 factors contribute 21/32, and the remaining
product is at least 1-sum_{k>=4}2^{-k}=7/8.

Ordinary capped rejection gives fixed-run approximate sampling with much
less computation. Release 103 uses the walk because a conditioning identifier
is not retained at every nested computation, and a valid wrap can instead be
checked when that identifier becomes the current cursor. That dataflow
restriction is load-bearing.

Multiple observations of one common unknown frame do not inherit the
independent conditional coordinates. For p=2, r=1, L=1, retaining z=0 forces
a_0=0; also retaining z=1 then forces F=0, contradicting full column rank.
The two-observation conditional space is empty. For larger L it need not be
empty, but F and the retained response coordinates become correlated.
Replacing the one common frame by independent channels changes the model.

## 3. Certified experiment-plan discovery without exact probability mass

Assume the following probability-estimation contract from release 103:

For a fixed randomized machine with polynomial worst-case time and logarithmic
work space, arbitrary acceptance probabilities can be deterministically
approximated to delta in O(log N+log(1/delta)) scratch bits and
N^{O(1)} delta^{-O(1)} time, for inverse-polynomial delta. All constants depend
on the fixed source machine. Every query is total and has a deterministic
error guarantee.

Consider any finite computational experiment-plan generator M meeting those
resource bounds. Its coins are *controllable computational choices*. Every
accepting run emits a valid plan, and the stream of emitted symbols cannot
be reread by M. A verifier may be integrated into M only if its state and
accesses also meet these bounds. Suppose its acceptance probability is at
least gamma=N^{-c}, and its time bound is T=N^a.

Give halted configurations self-loops through time T. Let p_t(v) denote the
success probability of the original continuation from configuration v. Use
the estimator to approximate every positive-probability successor's value
within delta <= gamma/(8T), and choose the successor with greatest estimate.
Because p_t(v) is a convex combination of the successor values,

    p_{t+1}(v') >= max_w p_{t+1}(w)-2delta
                 >= p_t(v)-2delta.

After T choices, the terminal value is at least gamma-2Tdelta >= 3gamma/4.
A terminal value is 0 or 1, so the constructed run accepts. First run the
deterministic construction without emitting the plan. If it rejects, emit
FAILURE. Otherwise rerun the same choices and stream its output. This never
emits an invalid plan, even outside the gamma promise.

Only the current/candidate/best configurations, counters, scores and estimator
scratch are live, each logarithmic in N for fixed a,c. There are O(T) estimator
calls per pass, hence polynomial total time conditional on the estimator.
Exact success probability, uniqueness of the plan, quantum preparation and an
exact dyadic mass service are unnecessary. This is source Corollary 12.4's
mechanism, independently reconstructed, not a new exactification theorem.

A concrete acquisition consequence is preset distinguishing-plan synthesis.
Let h be fixed. Supply h deterministic Moore machines, their starts, common
legal action alphabet and noiseless output alphabet. A logarithmic-space
generator can track the h simulated states and h(h-1)/2 flags recording which
pairs have produced different transcripts. It accepts once all flags are set.
If such a polynomial-length randomly generated action plan succeeds with
probability >= N^{-c}, the conditional compiler emits a deterministic plan
in logarithmic scratch space and polynomial time. Execute that plan once on
the true machine; its transcript identifies the unknown model.

The supplied transition/output tables account for N retained input bits.
The h simulated states need O(h log S+h^2) additional bits. Stored action and
observation records, if retained, cost T(log |A|+log |O|) bits. Physical
execution costs T actions. Offline candidate evaluations, including failed
search, are computational costs. A fully informed Monte Carlo control already
uses this same model and one physical experiment after an expected 1/gamma
offline candidate trials. The gain is conditional deterministic worst-case
search reliability with small scratch, not fewer physical observations or
acquisition of the supplied model family.

When h grows, the product model can have S^h states and the pair flags cost
h^2 bits. Padding these resources into a logarithmic-space instance can make
the running-time bound exponential in h. Fixed h must not be omitted.

## 4. Uncontrolled noise and the legal rollout alternative

One cannot choose every positive-probability successor in a laboratory. With
one available action and a fair environmental coin deciding success, a
computational accepting path has terminal value 1, but every physical policy
still succeeds with probability 1/2. The coin outcome is not an action.

For a known finite controlled Markov model, an appropriate weaker positive is
ordinary policy improvement. Let v_t(s) be the baseline's terminal success
value, and let

    Q_t(s,a) = E[v_{t+1}(S') | s,a].

Estimate these action values deterministically within delta and select a
maximizing action. The action's true Q is at least v_t(s)-2delta. Taking
conditional expectations along the actual stochastic trajectory yields

    P(success under rollout) >= v_0(s_0)-2Tdelta.          (3)

The environment retains its randomness. Conditional on 103, a logspace
simulation of the *known dyadic transition model* supplies such action-value
estimates without randomized evaluation. This is the usual finite-horizon
rollout improvement inequality, not new policy-iteration mathematics.

If an acquired model has row total-variation error at most epsilon uniformly
over state/action pairs, a maximal coupling gives path-law distance at most
T epsilon for any state-feedback policy. Thus

    P_true(success) >= v_model(s_0)-2Tdelta-T epsilon.     (4)

The uniform row-error certificate is extra information. It requires acquired
data or a valid model-service promise; a simulator's random tape is not the
unknown laboratory distribution. A whole learned transition table and the
data certifying it remain retained resources, even if rollout scratch is
logarithmic. Fully informed ordinary dynamic programming often has a much
better practical time bound. No finite laptop-feasibility claim follows from
the release's unspecified fixed library constants.

## 5. Safe linear excitation with information preserved

Supply a complete prescribed sequence u_1,...,u_N in R^d with ||u_t|| <= 1.
Suppose polarity is controllable. For an unknown linear integrator

    x_t = x_0 + B sum_{j<=t} epsilon_j u_j,  ||B||_op <= L,

release 097's signed-prefix theorem implies existence of polarities such that

    max_t ||x_t-x_0|| <= LC sqrt(d).

This bound is uniform over every B with the supplied norm certificate. If the
scientific observations are independent noisy *increments*

    Y_t = B epsilon_t u_t + Z_t,  Z_t iid N(0,Sigma),

then the Fisher information for vec(B), up to the vec convention, is

    (sum_t u_tu_t^T) tensor Sigma^{-1}.

It is exactly unchanged by the chosen polarities. Thus a preplanned informative
experiment can, existentially, keep displacement independent of its length
without weakening its linear-regression information. The statistical statement
assumes the increment-observation interface; independent noisy state observations
have a different information matrix.

Source 097 explicitly supplies no efficient signing algorithm, online rule, or
finite numerical constant. The low-cost certificate is real: once signs are
found, all input-prefix squared norms can be checked in O(Nd) arithmetic
operations, or charged rational bit operations. Finding the signs is the
unresolved computational task in this transfer. Search or exhaustive signs
are not an efficient construction.

Two matched controls limit where this is interesting. For a known palette
v_1,...,v_r, alternate polarity each time a vector reappears. The prefix is
V c with c_j in {0,1}. If the supplied Gram matrix satisfies
lambda_max(V^T V) <= Lambda, this online rule gives

    ||prefix|| <= sqrt(Lambda r)

with r counters, independent of experiment length. Orthogonal basis probes
already achieve sqrt(d); this restricted case does not need 097.

By contrast, unrestricted adaptive future regressors defeat every online rule.
In dimension 2, choose each unit u_t perpendicular to the current prefix s.
Then for either sign,

    ||s+epsilon_t u_t||^2 = ||s||^2+1.

The prefix norm equals sqrt(t), even if polarities are randomized and the
adversary observes past signs. Therefore a fully preplanned sequence, or a
restriction on future regressor adaptation, is necessary for the proposed
length-independent safe-excitation transfer.

## Computational scope

`check_mechanisms.py` uses Python standard-library exact rational arithmetic.
It checks a finite affine action, an actual distinguishing-plan fixture,
200 bounded-score-error/model-error rollout fixtures, and 32,760 finite joint
projection configurations. `checks.json` records values, resource observations
and source hashes. An initial unsupported expectation that the affine walk had
an all-integer numerator spectrum failed; the rejection is retained, and the
final result instead uses its exact characteristic polynomial and a rigorous
three-step Dobrushin certificate. These checks establish no full release-103
theorem, compiler implementation or new historical priority.
