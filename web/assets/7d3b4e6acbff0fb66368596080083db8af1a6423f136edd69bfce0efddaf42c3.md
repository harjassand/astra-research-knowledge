# Cycle 2: physical one-photon realization of magnitude/Hellinger designs

**Verdict.** A weighted complex projective 2-design always gives a valid one-photon POVM using effects (d q_j\Pi_j). But the phase-retrieval equality β=2 uses weights proportional to squared POVM row effects, so a nonuniform weighted design does not generally retain that exact condition number under the standard design POVM. There is a second normalization that fixes this for the all-d Sidon/Bodmann–Haas frame: use effects proportional to √q_j\(\Pi_j\). They sum to identity and their squared row weights recover the original design. This realizes a genuine one-photon instrument, including loss/vacuum, with β=2. Its local Fisher metric is exact and nearly isotropic for the selected prime (p\ge d). The metric is shot-noise limited. No quantum or asymptotic sensing advantage follows.

## Interfaces and normalization

Let unit vectors (u_j\in\mathbb C^d), rank-one projectors (\Pi_j=u_ju_j^*\), and positive design probabilities (q_j) satisfy

\[
\sum_jq_j=1,\qquad \sum_jq_j\Pi_j^{\otimes2}=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym}
=\frac{I+F}{d(d+1)}.
\]

Taking a partial trace gives \(\sum_jq_j\Pi_j=I/d\). Thus the ordinary weighted-design POVM

\[
E_j^{\rm design}=d q_j\Pi_j
\]

is physically valid: all effects are positive and sum to (I). Its single-photon outcome law for input state ρ is \(\Pr(j|\rho)=d q_j\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_j\rho)\).

For the magnitude/phase-retrieval map, rows are (a_j=t_j u_j), so their intensity effects are \(A_j=a_ja_j^*=t_j^2\Pi_j\). Direct use as a one-photon POVM additionally requires \(\sum_jt_j^2\Pi_j=I\). The phase-retrieval equality theorem uses design weights \(q_j=t_j^4/S\), (S=\sum_jt_j^4). Starting from a normalized design ((u_j,q_j)), choosing (t_j^2=c\sqrt{q_j}) recovers those exact phase-retrieval weights, but it is a POVM only if

\[
\sum_j\sqrt{q_j}\Pi_j=\lambda I,
\]

in which case (c=1/\lambda). A weighted 2-design alone guarantees the (q_j)-weighted first moment (I/d), not this square-root-weighted tightness. Alternatively, the always-valid standard effects (d q_j\Pi_j) have phase-retrieval design weights \(q_j^2/\sum_kq_k^2\), which need not form a 2-design. The two physical objectives coincide automatically for equal-weight designs such as SICs and complete MUBs.

## Exact all-d physical instrument from the Sidon construction

For any (d\ge2), choose an odd prime (p\ge d), group (G=\mathbb F_p^2), and (S=\{(x,x^2):x=0,\ldots,d-1\}\). The set (S) is Sidon: a sum of two elements determines their sum and sum of squares, hence their product and the unordered pair over odd characteristic. Jasper–Mixon's Bodmann–Haas construction gives (p^2+d) unit vectors: (p^2) character vectors

\[
u_{a,b}(x)=d^{-1/2}\exp(2\pi i(ax+bx^2)/p),\quad (a,b)\in\mathbb F_p^2,
\]

and the (d) coordinate vectors (e_x). The normalized projective-2-design probabilities are

\[
q_{a,b}=\frac{d}{p^2(d+1)},\qquad q_{e_x}=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}.
\]

The character family is a tight frame, \(\sum_{a,b}\Pi_{a,b}=(p^2/d)I\), and the coordinate projectors sum to (I). Therefore

\[
\sum_j\sqrt{q_j}\Pi_j
=\frac{p+1}{\sqrt{d(d+1)}}I.
\]

The square-root-normalized one-photon effects are consequently

\[
E_{a,b}=\frac{d}{p(p+1)}\Pi_{a,b},\qquad
E_{e_x}=\frac{1}{p+1}\Pi_{e_x}.
\]

They sum exactly to (I): the character outcomes contribute (p/(p+1)I), and the coordinate outcomes (1/(p+1)I). With (a_j=\sqrt{s_j}u_j), where (s_{a,b}=d/[p(p+1)]) and (s_{e_x}=1/(p+1)),

\[
S=\sum_js_j^2=\frac{d(d+1)}{(p+1)^2},\qquad
\frac{s_{a,b}^2}{S}=\frac{d}{p^2(d+1)},\qquad
\frac{s_{e_x}^2}{S}=\frac{1}{d(d+1)}.
\]

Thus the physical row effects have precisely the projective-2-design probabilities as normalized fourth-power row weights, and the Peng–Han–Huang equality characterization gives \(\beta_{\Psi_A}^{\ell_2}=2\) (for (d\ge2), with their unnormalized raw-intensity \(\ell_2) metric). Here

\[
L=\frac{1}{\sqrt2(p+1)},\qquad U=\frac{\sqrt2}{p+1}.
\]

This frame has (m=p^2+d) outcomes and nonuniform effect weights unless (p=d). When (p=d) is prime, all weights equal (1/(d+1)); the construction is the familiar complete-MUB-size case.

**Instrument realization.** For any effects (E_j=a_ja_j^*\) with \(\sum_jE_j=I\), the map (V:\mathbb C^d\to\mathbb C^m), (V|\psi\rangle=\sum_j|j\rangle\langle a_j|\psi\rangle), is an isometry. Extend (V) to an (m)-mode unitary with (m-d) vacuum input modes, then count the output port. Equivalently the outcome Kraus operators are \(K_j=|j\rangle\langle a_j|\) and \(\sum_jK_j^*K_j=I\). This consumes one photon per attempted copy and produces one classical label. A simple spatial multiport uses (m) output ports/detectors; multiplexing can trade detector count for routing depth and dead time, which is apparatus-specific.

## Loss, vacuum, and charged photon counts

Let η be the product of source-delivery, propagation, and detector efficiency when these are modeled as state-independent erasure. The observed POVM is

\[
\widetilde E_j=\eta E_j,\qquad \widetilde E_{\emptyset}=(1-\eta)I,
\]

where ∅ is the no-click/vacuum outcome, including an unobserved lost photon. Completeness is exact. A missed event must remain in the data as ∅; postselecting detected photons alone changes the cost from one to (1/\eta) expected incident copies per retained detection. For (N_{\rm att}) attempts, the expected incident probe photons are (N_{\rm att}), detected photons (\eta N_{\rm att}), and lost/vacuum outcomes ((1-\eta)N_{\rm att}). If preparation succeeds only with probability ξ and failures are not heralded, replace η by the effective efficiency \(\xi\eta\), and charge every failed preparation attempt. Dark counts and output cross-talk require extra nuisance outcomes and are not absorbed into this erasure model.

For pure unit vectors (x,y), the one-attempt squared Hellinger distance uses the convention \(H^2(P,Q)=\sum_o(\sqrt{P_o}-\sqrt{Q_o})^2\in[0,2]\). Because loss has the same probability under both states,

\[
H_\eta^2(x,y)=\eta\sum_j\bigl(|a_j^*x|-|a_j^*y|\bigr)^2.
\]

Write \(\delta=\|xx^*-yy^*\|_*=2\sqrt{1-|\langle x,y\rangle|^2}\). For any Parseval row frame the triangle inequality and \(\sum_jE_j=I\) give \(H_1(x,y)\le\delta/\sqrt2\). For the above projective-design frame, each effect norm is at most (s_{\max}=1/(p+1)). The exact phase-retrieval lower constant (L=1/[\sqrt2(p+1)]) gives

\[
\|P_x-P_y\|_2\ge L\delta,
\quad |P_x(j)-P_y(j)|\le2\sqrt{s_{\max}}\,|\sqrt{P_x(j)}-\sqrt{P_y(j)}|,
\]

and hence the global, loss-aware bracket

\[
\boxed{\quad
\frac{\sqrt\eta}{2\sqrt{2(p+1)}}\,\delta
\le H_\eta(x,y)\le\frac{\sqrt\eta}{\sqrt2}\,\delta.
\quad}
\]

This is an executable pair-separation guarantee for a specified physical POVM; it is not by itself a finite-sample estimator or a sharp testing exponent. With (N_{\rm att}) independent copies, Hellinger affinity multiplies across copies, so \(H^2(P_x^{\otimes N},P_y^{\otimes N})=2[1-(1-H^2(P_x,P_y)/2)^N]\). The no-click probability and copy cost remain included.

## Fisher information, an estimator, and shot noise

For a local mixed-state contrast around the maximally mixed state, take \(\rho_\theta=I/d+\theta X\), where (X=X^*), \(\operatorname{Tr}X=0\), \(\|X\|_F=1\), and \(|\theta|\le1/(d\|X\|_{\rm op})\). Let \(x_j=\operatorname{Tr}(\Pi_jX)\). For general physical effects (E_j=s_j\Pi_j), the one-attempt Fisher information at θ=0 is

\[
\mathcal I_X(0)=\eta d\sum_js_jx_j^2.
\]

For the square-root-normalized Sidon POVM, character orthogonality and uniqueness of nonzero Sidon differences give the exact formula

\[
\mathcal I_X(0)=\eta\left[
\frac{p}{p+1}\|X_{\rm off}\|_F^2+
\frac{d}{p+1}\|X_{\rm diag}\|_F^2
\right].
\]

So its local Fisher eigenvalues lie between \(\eta d/(p+1)\) and \(\eta p/(p+1)\), with condition ratio (p/d). For (p=d) it is isotropic. For every-d choice (p=\min\{q\text{ prime}:q\ge d\}), it is nearly isotropic when the prime gap is small, but the frame size and any finite-precision implementation still must be charged.

For an equal-weight SIC or complete-MUB projective 2-design, use (m=d^2) or (m=d(d+1)) effects (E_j=(d/m)\Pi_j). The moment identity makes

\[
\mathcal I_X(0)=\eta\frac{d}{d+1}\|X\|_F^2=\eta\frac{d}{d+1}.
\]

The quantum Fisher information of this known contrast at (I/d) is (d\|X\|_F^2=d), so the design readout attains a fraction (1/(d+1)) of the one-parameter quantum limit. With (N_{\rm att}) incident photons, the local Cramér–Rao variance bound is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta)\ge\frac{d+1}{\eta d N_{\rm att}},
\]

which is shot-noise scaling (N^{-1}) in variance. A readout in the eigenbasis of a known (X) has (\mathcal I=\eta d) and is (d+1) times more informative; it is not informationally complete and is not an equally informed baseline when the contrast direction is unknown. SIC and MUB are equal as local 2-design Fisher metrics at (I/d), though their global outcome overlaps differ.

For the one-dimensional known-(X) family there is a simple unbiased estimator, so the scalar CRB is not just formal. Set (A_X=\sum_js_jx_j^2\), (B_X=\sum_js_jx_j^3\). For each attempt let (Z=x_j) if outcome (j) is detected and (Z=0) for the lost/vacuum outcome. Since \(\sum_js_j\Pi_j=I\), \(\mathbb E_\theta Z=\eta\theta A_X\); therefore

\[
\widehat\theta=\frac{1}{N_{\rm att}\eta A_X}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\rm att}}Z_i
\]

is exactly unbiased, and

\[
\operatorname{Var}_\theta(\widehat\theta)=
\frac{\eta(A_X/d+\theta B_X)-\eta^2\theta^2A_X^2}
{N_{\rm att}\eta^2A_X^2}.
\]

At θ=0 this equals \(1/[N_{\rm att}\eta\mathcal I_X(0)]\). It assumes the direction (X), POVM weights, and efficiency η are known; their acquisition/calibration is extra cost. The Gaussian Parseval construction below supplies a full-state finite-sample risk bound under its good-frame event. No dark-count robust estimator or finite-bit frame-calibration theorem was established.

## SIC, MUB, random frames, and known-pair readout

* **SIC:** (d^2) effects \(E_j=\Pi_j/d\); one photon per attempt, (d^2) labeled outcomes in a direct Naimark multiport. Minimal outcome count among rank-one informationally complete 2-design POVMs.
* **Complete MUB:** (d(d+1)) effects \(E_{b,k}=\Pi_{b,k}/(d+1)\) when available; implement by selecting one of (d+1) bases and using (d) projective outcomes per photon. Both are equal-weight 2-designs and have the same maximally-mixed local Fisher metric above. MUB consumes setting-control/randomization; SIC trades that for a larger fixed outcome alphabet.
* **Random complex frame:** draw (b_j\in\mathbb C^d), (m\ge d), form (G=\sum_jb_jb_j^*\), and whiten (a_j=G^{-1/2}b_j). With probability one (G\succ0), \(\sum_ja_ja_j^*=I\), so this is a valid one-photon POVM and loss adds ((1-\eta)I). It is generally not an exact projective 2-design; the exact β=2 theorem and Hellinger guarantee for the specific Gaussian ensemble do not follow from whitening alone. The next section gives a finite-sample risk result for that Gaussian ensemble.
* **Known-pair readout:** for a contrast direction already known, measuring in its eigenbasis gives the quantum Fisher information and beats the isotropic SIC/MUB readout by (d+1) at the maximally mixed state. In (d=2), for the orthogonal pair ±z, the ideal qubit SIC has Bhattacharyya coefficient \(B=\sqrt{2/3}\); uniformly choosing among the three Pauli MUBs gives (B=2/3); direct (z)-basis readout gives (B=0). Under efficiency η these become \(1-\eta+\eta B\), with (H^2=2\eta(1-B)\). This demonstrates that a 2-design second moment does not uniquely determine global Hellinger separation and that known-task readout can outperform universal tomography readout.

## Random Parseval–Gaussian POVM: linear outcomes and a finite-sample rate

This construction answers a different objective from the exact \(\beta=2\) design. Draw \(A\in\mathbb C^{r\times d}\) with i.i.d. standard circular complex-Gaussian rows, using the convention \(\Re A_{jk},\Im A_{jk}\sim N(0,1/2)\), and set \(S=A^*A/r\). For \(0<\zeta<0.05\), Xia–Xu–Xu's Gaussian stability estimates imply that, if \(r\ge C d\zeta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)\), an event of probability at least \(1-6e^{-c\zeta^2r}\) holds on which
\[
(1-\zeta)I\preceq S\preceq(1+\zeta)I,\qquad
L_A/\sqrt r\ge 1/\beta_0-\zeta,\qquad
\beta_0=\sqrt{4/(4-\pi)}\simeq2.159.
\]
Here \(L_A\) is the global lower Lipschitz constant of \(x\mapsto|Ax|\) for projective distance \(d_\phi(x,y)=\min_{|c|=1}\|x-cy\|_2\). On this event form
\[
B=A(A^*A)^{-1/2}=(A/\sqrt r)S^{-1/2},\qquad B^*B=I.
\]
Writing \(b_j^*\) for the rows of \(B\), the effects \(E_j=b_jb_j^*\) sum to identity. This is a physical rank-one one-photon POVM with \(r=O(d)\) outcomes at fixed \(\zeta\); its outcome law for a unit pure state \(|x\rangle\) is \(p_j(x)=|b_j^*x|^2\). Since \(\lambda_{\max}(S)\le1+\zeta\), the whitening step preserves a quantitative lower bound:
\[
H^2(p_x,p_y)=\big\||Bx|-|By|\big\|_2^2
\ge \kappa_\zeta^2 d_\phi(x,y)^2,\qquad
\kappa_\zeta:=\frac{1/\beta_0-\zeta}{\sqrt{1+\zeta}}.
\]

Loss belongs in the count alphabet. For state-independent survival/detection probability \(\eta\), set \(\widetilde p_j(x)=\eta p_j(x)\) for \(j\le r\), and \(\widetilde p_\emptyset=1-\eta\) for the no-click/lost-photon outcome. There are \(r+1\) observed categories for \(N\) attempted one-photon copies. Let \(\hat p\) be their empirical frequencies and choose the count-minimum-Hellinger estimator
\[
\hat x\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{z\in\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}}H(\hat p,\widetilde p_z).
\]
By the triangle inequality \(H(\widetilde p_{\hat x},\widetilde p_x)\le2H(\hat p,\widetilde p_x)\). For a multinomial distribution on \(r+1\) categories, \(\mathbb E H^2(\hat p,\widetilde p_x)\le r/N\): for each positive category probability \(q_i\), use \((\sqrt{\hat p_i}-\sqrt{q_i})^2\le(\hat p_i-q_i)^2/q_i\) and sum the binomial variances; zero-probability categories contribute zero. Therefore, uniformly over the true pure state and conditional on the good-frame event,
\[
\boxed{\quad \mathbb E d_\phi(x,\hat x)^2\le \frac{4r}{N\eta\kappa_\zeta^2}.\quad}
\]
With no loss and only the \(r\) conclusive categories, the same argument gives \(4(r-1)/(N\kappa_\zeta^2)\). The extra numerator unit under loss is the no-click category; no lost copy has been postselected away. This is an explicit finite-sample statistical upper bound. It is an argmin over a compact continuous state space; no polynomial-time global optimization algorithm or finite-precision implementation guarantee is established here.

The same frame gives a two-state test bound under Poissonized source attempts. If the number of attempts has mean \(\tau\), all outcome counts are independent Poisson variables and their Bhattacharyya coefficient is \(\exp[-\tau H^2(\widetilde p_x,\widetilde p_y)/2]\). For \(d_\phi(x,y)\ge\epsilon\), a likelihood-ratio test has each type error at most \(\alpha\) once
\[
\tau\ge\frac{2\log(1/\alpha)}{\eta\kappa_\zeta^2\epsilon^2}.
\]
This is an expected attempt/photon budget under Poissonization, not a hard per-run photon cap. Under fixed \(N\), the expected detected and lost counts are \(\eta N\) and \((1-\eta)N\); obtaining \(n\) detected photons by postselection costs \(n/\eta\) expected attempts.

The resource-matched minimax control is stronger than comparing against a particular SIC or MUB readout. Bruß and Macchiavello's Haar-prior theorem gives the largest achievable mean pure-state fidelity from \(N\) copies, even allowing collective POVMs: \(F_{\max}=(N+1)/(N+d)\). As \(d_\phi^2=2(1-\sqrt F)\ge1-F\), every such strategy has mean squared projective error at least \((d-1)/(N+d)\), which is \(\Omega(d/N)\) for \(N\ge d\). If each attempted copy is independently erased with probability \(1-\eta\), conditioning on the survivor count \(M\sim\mathrm{Bin}(N,\eta)\) and using convexity of \(1/(M+d)\) gives the lower bound \((d-1)/(N\eta+d)\). Thus for fixed positive \(\zeta,\eta\), the Parseval-Gaussian POVM has the optimal \(O(d/(N\eta))\) dimension/copy rate up to constants, conditional on the high-probability frame event. The squared error is shot-noise \(1/N\) per degree of freedom. This is no quantum advantage claim: the lower bound already permits arbitrary collective measurements on the same copies.

The comparison is metric-sensitive. Any exact projective-2-design identity spans the \(d^2\)-dimensional Hermitian space, so it needs at least \(d^2\) projectors; the Sidon construction above has \(p^2+d=\Theta(d^2)\) outcomes (take \(d\le p<2d\)). Its \(\beta=2\) equality is for the raw-intensity \(\ell_2\) phase-retrieval map. The Gaussian Parseval POVM instead has \(r=O(d)\) outcomes at fixed stability tolerance and is near-optimal for the amplitude/Hellinger map, whose complex-Gaussian condition-number limit is \(\beta_0\simeq2.159\). It does **not** satisfy the exact projective-2-design identity or the \(\beta=2\) intensity equality. SIC and complete-MUB POVMs have \(d^2\) and \(d(d+1)\) outcomes and the same local Fisher information at \(I/d\) derived above; that does not imply identical global Hellinger geometry. A readout chosen for a known pair or contrast can do better still, but does not solve universal unknown-state reconstruction.

All strategies use one incident photon per attempted copy. The Gaussian POVM needs \(r\) active output/detector channels plus an explicit no-click/loss record. Its isometry is \(V|\psi\rangle=\sum_j|j\rangle\langle b_j|\psi\rangle\); extend it to an \(r\)-mode unitary using \(r-d\) vacuum input modes and detect the output port. A generic dense optical implementation can use a universal interferometer with \(\Theta(r^2)\) beam-splitter/phase-control elements; actual loss, calibration and maintenance are apparatus-specific. Generating/storing the random frame takes \(O(rd)\) real parameters (or a seed plus generation convention); whitening costs \(O(rd^2+d^3)\) arithmetic and \(O(rd+d^2)\) working memory. For the count estimator, the unordered histogram suffices, requiring \(O(r\log(N+1))\) bits, versus \(N\log_2(r+1)\) bits for the ordered outcome string. Detector efficiency, dark counts, cross-talk, mode mismatch, calibration exposures, finite-bit transfer-matrix description, and verification of the implemented isometry need separate budgets; none is converted to calibration shots here. The frame-event failure probability also enters total failure: for confidence \(1-\delta\), choose \(r\) so \(6e^{-c\zeta^2r}\le\delta\). The risk bound assumes a known, accurately implemented frame and known \(\eta\); finite-precision calibration robustness is unresolved.

## Prior-art boundary and sources

This is a physical/statistical translation of established frame/tomography results, not a new sensor advantage. Jasper and Mixon, *Nearly tight weighted 2-designs in complex projective spaces of every dimension*, Theorem 7 and its proof, give the character-plus-coordinate Sidon construction and its weights ([arXiv:2501.14938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14938)). Scott's *Tight informationally complete quantum measurements* identifies rank-one tight IC POVMs with weighted projective 2-designs and includes SIC and complete MUB cases ([arXiv:quant-ph/0604049](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0604049)). Klappenecker and Rötteler show complete MUBs are projective 2-designs ([arXiv:quant-ph/0502031](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0502031)). Balan, Bodmann, Casazza, and Edidin give the projective-2-design linear magnitude reconstruction identity ([author-hosted article PDF](https://math.umd.edu/~rvbalan/PAPERS/MyPapers/PREC-Aug02-2007.pdf)). The underlying global β≥2 phase-retrieval bound and equality characterization in dimension (d\ge2) were checked in the earlier cycle against Peng, Han, and Huang ([arXiv:2506.22053](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22053)); the exact equality corollary remains internally reconstructed and not priority-cleared.

The linear-outcome amplitude/Hellinger guarantee uses the complex-Gaussian phase-retrieval lower-Lipschitz result of Xia, Xu, and Xu, *Stability in Phase Retrieval: Characterizing Condition Numbers and the Optimal Vector Set*, Theorem 4.3 ([arXiv:2404.07515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07515)). Their theorem treats the unwhitened amplitude map; whitening into a Parseval POVM and the Hellinger-risk argument above are derived here. The Haar-prior lower bound uses Bruß and Macchiavello, *Optimal state estimation for d-dimensional quantum systems* ([arXiv:quant-ph/9812016](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9812016)), which derives the optimal mean fidelity \((N+1)/(N+d)\). The multinomial Hellinger inequality, erasure accounting, and conversion from projective separation to risk are direct derivations. The optical unitary realization uses Naimark's isometry; the generic \(\Theta(r^2)\) interferometer element count follows from universal multiport factorizations such as Reck et al., *Experimental realization of any discrete unitary operator* ([Phys. Rev. Lett. 73, 58 (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.73.58)). No novelty or priority claim is made.

1. Specialist-check the √-weighted POVM normalization and the character-sum Fisher decomposition, especially the use of Sidon difference uniqueness.
2. If the intended task is full-state estimation rather than a known local contrast or pair separation, derive finite-(N) risk/confidence bounds for an explicit linear or constrained estimator and include design acquisition/finite precision.
3. For an apparatus, compare direct SIC, randomly selected MUB, and the (p^2+d) square-root-weighted multiport at the same incident-photon, detector-channel, switching, dead-time, loss, and calibration budget. Dark counts and imperfect mode transformations can change the Hellinger/CRB comparison.
