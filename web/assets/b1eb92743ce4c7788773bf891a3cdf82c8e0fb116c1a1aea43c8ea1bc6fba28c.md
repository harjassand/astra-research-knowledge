# Cycle 3: Efficient Poisson recovery, and the remaining calibration boundary

## Verdict

The efficient-reconstruction gate is already closed for a slightly different (O(d))-port Parseval instrument than the polar-whitened frame from Cycle 2. Huang, Li, and Needell (2025), Theorem 1, prove a uniform high-probability phase-retrieval bound for a convex PSD least-squares program followed by leading-eigenvector extraction, with (m=O(d)) iid complex sub-Gaussian sensing rows and independent Poisson counts. Embedding a scaled iid-Gaussian block into a Parseval POVM makes its photon counts match that theorem exactly. The estimator is a convex SDP, not a nonconvex likelihood fit; no new nonconvex initialization theorem is needed for this modified instrument.

The construction therefore gives (O(d/\varepsilon^2)) launched photons at known uniform efficiency, with all residual and loss outcomes charged, and a polynomial-size convex optimization model. For exactly fixed launch count, conditioning the complete Poissonized record gives the stated (O(\sqrt N e^{-cd})) tail transfer. It does not transfer the cited theorem to the polar-normalized correlated frame, and it does not prove a solver bit-complexity bound, direct sharp multinomial concentration, or an optical compilation cost.

The main practical boundary is calibration. I prove an exact nonidentifiability result when each detector has an unrestricted unknown additive dark-count mean. A blank exposure adds identifying information, but a finite-sample guarantee needs a quantitative background model and enough calibration counts. For a common known background, and for a common background estimated from independent blank counts, the same paper's heavy-tailed CVX-LS theorem gives an explicit finite-calibration error law. Arbitrary unknown per-channel backgrounds are outside that theorem and can exactly mask the state.

## Exact source theorem and estimator

Huang, Li, and Needell, [*Stable Phase Retrieval: Optimal Rates in Poisson and Heavy-tailed Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00551), arXiv:2510.00551v1, Theorem 1, assume iid complex row vectors with independent entries satisfying
\[
\mathbb E\varphi=\mathbb E\varphi^2=0,\qquad
\mathbb E|\varphi|^2=1,\qquad
\mathbb E|\varphi|^4=1+\mu>1,
\]
and independent (Y_k\mid\varphi_k\sim\operatorname{Pois}(|\langle\varphi_k,w\rangle|^2)). For (m\ge Ld), they report a probability (1-O(e^{-cd})) and a uniform signal bound
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z_*,w)\le C_3\max\{K,\|w\|^{-1}\}\sqrt{d/m},
\]
where (Z_*\) minimizes \(\| (\langle\varphi_k\varphi_k^*,Z\rangle)_k-Y\|_2\) over (Z\succeq0), and (z_* = \sqrt{\lambda_1(Z_*)}u_1(Z_*)\). The distance is Euclidean modulo global complex phase. The source states the bound simultaneously over signals, with constants depending on the row sub-Gaussian norm and fourth moment; those constants are not numerical in the manuscript.

This is a published estimator guarantee, not a novelty claim. The optimization is a convex semidefinite least-squares problem. Its dimension and constraint count are polynomial in (d,m), but the cited statistical theorem does not supply a finite-bit complexity estimate for a specified numerical solver.

## Parseval completion that matches the iid-row theorem

Draw (G\in\mathbb C^{m\times d}) with iid standard complex Gaussian entries and rows (g_j^*\). Choose (m\ge L'd\), where (L') is large enough for both the source theorem and the frame event. On
\[
\mathcal G=\{m/4\le\lambda_{\min}(G^*G),\ \lambda_{\max}(G^*G)\le4m\},
\]
which has probability at least (1-2e^{-c_Gm}) after adjusting the fixed oversampling ratio, set (a=1/(4m)) and
\[
R=(I-aG^*G)^{1/2},\qquad
\widetilde B=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt a\,G\\R\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\widetilde B^*\widetilde B=I_d\). Its rows define a complete rank-one Parseval POVM with (m+d=O(d)) output ports. The final (d) ports may be coarse-grained to a single residual effect (I-aG^*G\). For every unit vector (x\), the Gaussian block has probability
\[
q_G(x)=a\|Gx\|^2\in[1/16,1].
\]
On the bad-frame event use a fixed valid fallback instrument and count that event as failure.

With uniform known detector efficiency \(\eta_{\rm det}\), the detected row effects are \(\eta_{\rm det}a g_jg_j^*\), with loss/no-click effect \((1-\eta_{\rm det})I\). For a Poissonized launch count of mean (N), the full record has independent Poisson counts after thinning. The first (m) counts have means
\[
N\eta_{\rm det}a|g_j^*x|^2=|g_j^*w|^2,
\qquad w=\sqrt{N\eta_{\rm det}a}\,x.
\]
This is an exact match to the cited theorem: the random design matrix remains iid Gaussian, and (w) is a deterministic rescaling of the unknown state, independent of (G). Residual, no-click, and loss events remain in the launched-photon budget even though CVX-LS may ignore their counts.

When \(\|w\|\ge1/K\), the source theorem gives \(\operatorname{dist}(z_*,w)\le C_3K\sqrt{d/m}\). If this error is at most \(\|w\|/2\), normalizing the leading component yields
\[
d_\phi(\widehat x,x)\le 4C_3K\sqrt{\frac{d}{N\eta_{\rm det}}}.
\]
Thus, for (0<\varepsilon\le1), it suffices that
\[
N\eta_{\rm det}\ge
\max\!\left\{\frac{4m}{K^2},\frac{16C_3^2K^2d}{\varepsilon^2}\right\}
\]
for Poissonized success, apart from the frame-event failure. With (m=\Theta(d)), this is (N\eta_{\rm det}=O(d/\varepsilon^2)), with hidden source constants. The good-frame event sends at least a (1/16) fraction of detected photons to the Gaussian block in expectation for every (x); the rest are still paid for.

For exactly (N) launches the full source outcome vector, including residual and no-click, is multinomial. It equals the all-outcome independent-Poisson law conditioned on total source count (N). Since \(\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(N)=N\}\asymp N^{-1/2}\), any failure event (F) based on the Gaussian-block counts obeys
\[
\Pr_{\rm fixed\ N}(F)\le C\sqrt N\,\Pr_{\rm Pois}(F).
\]
The event that (G\) is bad is independent of the Poisson total, so the completed policy's fixed-(N) failure is at most (2e^{-c_Gm}+C\sqrt N e^{-cd}). This is exponentially small for polynomial (N) in (d); it is not a dimension-uniform sharp multinomial tail for super-polynomial (N).

The proof relies on the iid Gaussian *block* and residual completion. It does not invoke Huang–Li–Needell on $G(G^*G)^{-1/2}$, whose rows are correlated. The prior-art comparison identifies no missing init/refine theorem for the completed-block policy:

- Chen and Candès' [Truncated Wirtinger Flow](https://yuxinchen2020.github.io/publications/TruncatedWF_CPAM.pdf), Theorem 1.3, is a primary real-Gaussian result with Poisson log-likelihood, $m=O(d)$, and total iteration cost $O(md\log(1/\varepsilon))$. Its displayed Poisson bound is $O(1)$ absolute error only in the high-energy regime $\|w\|\ge\log^{3/2}m$. It does not give the same complex-state theorem or the same $\varepsilon$ law at general dose.
- Li, Lange, and Fessler's [Poisson phase retrieval in very low-count regimes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00861) formulates independent counts with known per-channel background $b_i$ and develops Wirtinger-flow and majorize-minimize algorithms; its paper reports simulations and algorithmic curvature results, not a uniform global recovery theorem for our POVM.
- Diederichs, Filbir, and Römer's [low-dose Poisson Wirtinger-gradient study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18527) proves descent/stationary-point convergence for its selected losses, not global recovery from arbitrary initialization.
- Dirksen, Krahmer, Römer, and Salanevich's [spectral method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13263), Theorem 5, is a useful real-Gaussian/Poisson control: it assumes $m\ge d\log d$ and a known norm/dose parameter $\alpha$, and gives relative squared error $O((1+\hat C/\alpha)\log(m)\sqrt{\log d}\sqrt{d/m})$. This is neither a complex normalized one-photon POVM nor the same per-port photon accounting.

The strongest equally informed practical control remains the full count likelihood on the same calibrated Parseval device, with the same input count, loss, residual, and blank records. The CVX-LS result certifies a polynomial-size convex program under exact calibration; it does not claim to beat a likelihood estimator or a hardware implementation.

## Finite common-background calibration transfer

This is a conditional corollary of the source's Theorem 4 (heavy-tailed CVX-LS), not a claim of new estimator theory. Suppose every Gaussian-block detector has the same known dark-count mean \(\beta\ge0\) during the signal acquisition. Then
\[
Y_j\mid g_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(|g_j^*w|^2+\beta).
\]
Subtract \(\beta\) and write
\[
Y_j-\beta=|g_j^*w|^2+\xi_j,\qquad
\xi_j=Y_j-(|g_j^*w|^2+\beta).
\]
For iid complex Gaussian rows the pairs \((g_j,\xi_j)\) are iid and \(\mathbb E(\xi_j\mid g_j)=0\), as required by Theorem 4. The exact fourth moment is
\[
\|\xi\|_4^4=\|w\|^2+\beta+6\|w\|^4+6\beta\|w\|^2+3\beta^2.
\]
Consequently, for \(\|w\|\ge1\), \(\|\xi\|_4\le C(\|w\|+\sqrt\beta)\). Theorem 4 (with $q=4$) yields
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z_*,w)\le
C\frac{\|w\|+\sqrt\beta}{\|w\|}\sqrt{d/m}
\]
with its source probability $1-O(m^{-1}\log^4m)-O(e^{-cd})$. After normalization, provided the error is below $\|w\|/2$,
\[
d_\phi(\widehat x,x)\le C\left[
\sqrt{\frac d{N\eta_{\rm det}}}
+\frac{\sqrt{\beta d m}}{N\eta_{\rm det}}
\right].
\]
For (m=\Theta(d)), a sufficient photon law is
\[
N\eta_{\rm det}\ge C\left(\frac d{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{d\sqrt\beta}{\varepsilon}\right),
\]
plus the high-energy and normalization conditions. This explicitly shows how a known common background can change dose scaling when it is large.

Now suppose \(\beta\) is unknown but common across channels, and acquire independent blank counts (Z_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(r\beta)\), with relative blank exposure (r>0). Use \(Y_j-Z_j/r\) in the same CVX-LS objective. The centered noise remains iid conditional on the iid Gaussian rows. Its blank component has
\[
\left\|Z/r-\beta\right\|_4
=\left(\frac{\beta}{r^3}+\frac{3\beta^2}{r^2}\right)^{1/4}
\le (\beta/r^3)^{1/4}+3^{1/4}\sqrt{\beta/r}.
\]
Therefore the preceding bound holds with the extra term
\[
\frac{C\sqrt{dm}}{N\eta_{\rm det}}
\left[(\beta/r^3)^{1/4}+\sqrt{\beta/r}\right]
\]
on its right-hand side, under the same $q=4$ source tail probability. The blank record costs $mr\beta$ expected dark counts for the Gaussian block, plus the setup time and control for a source-off exposure. This per-channel subtraction is an explicit sufficient protocol, not an optimal calibration design: because $\beta$ is shared, pooling blank counts or jointly fitting the common offset may reduce calibration cost, but those estimators need a separate analysis. The transfer requires a **common** background level across rows, known blank exposure ratio $r$, independent blank counts, and exact row calibration. Heterogeneous unknown $\beta_j$ are not iid noise and do not satisfy the cited theorem as stated.

## Exact nonidentifiability with unrestricted channel-specific dark counts

Let a complete (J)-outcome POVM have effects (E_j\succeq0), \(\sum_jE_j=I_d\), and probability vector (p_j(x)=x^*E_jx\) for unit (x). In the Poissonized source model, let detected source flux be (S=N\eta\) and add an unknown nonnegative per-port dark mean \(\beta_j\) during the signal exposure:
\[
Y_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(S p_j(x)+\beta_j),\qquad j\in[J].
\]
Choose any orthogonal unit states (x,y). If the nuisance class allows \(\beta_j\in[0,\bar\beta]\) with \(\bar\beta\ge2S\), set
\[
\beta_j^{(x)}=S(2-p_j(x)),\qquad
\beta_j^{(y)}=S(2-p_j(y)).
\]
Since (0\le p_j\le1), both background vectors lie in ([S,2S]). Under either state-background pair, every illuminated detector count has exactly the same law \(\operatorname{Pois}(2S)\). Any state-independent no-click/loss count has the same law too. Thus even infinitely many repeats of this single exposure cannot distinguish (x) from (y) uniformly over this nuisance class. For phase error target \(\varepsilon<1/\sqrt2\), the two success balls are disjoint, so a uniform success probability above (1/2) is impossible without additional constraints or calibration.

A blank exposure of relative duration (r) provides (Z_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(r\beta_j)). For the constructed pair, the KL divergence between the two blank-record laws obeys
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_x^{\rm blank}\|P_y^{\rm blank})
\le \frac rS\sum_j\big(S[p_j(x)-p_j(y)]\big)^2
=rS\|p(x)-p(y)\|_2^2\le2rS,
\]
using (D(\operatorname{Pois}(u)\|\operatorname{Pois}(v))\le(u-v)^2/v), \(\beta_j^{(y)}\ge S\), and \(\|p(x)-p(y)\|_2^2\le2\). If (rS\le1/16), Pinsker gives total variation at most (1/4), so the average error of any equal-prior binary test is at least (3/8). Consequently, this worst-case background class requires relative blank exposure (r=\Omega(1/S)) even to resolve this separated pair. For these constructed backgrounds, the blank record contains at least (rJS\) expected dark events, which is \(\Omega(J)\) when (rS\) is constant. This is an identifiability/calibration lower bound, not a general calibration-optimality theorem: known bounds, cross-exposure constraints, shared-background models, or other reference data change the problem.

The no-go is exact for Poissonized illumination. At fixed launched photon count, source counts are multinomial rather than independent Poisson; this particular equality-of-laws construction does not establish an exact fixed-(N) no-go. De-Poissonization or a direct multinomial-plus-background lower bound would be a separate proof obligation.

## Cost ledger, prior-art boundary, and next decisive gate

The statistical theorem charges (N) incident photons, including residual and no-click/loss outcomes; (m+d=O(d)) resolved or coarsened output ports; and (md=O(d^2)) row coefficients. Source and theorem assumptions also require a known unit norm, exact calibrated rows, stable state copies, no saturation, and no unmodeled heterogeneous backgrounds. A bounded finite-alphabet row option is already allowed by the source assumptions: entry probabilities (3/4) at zero and (1/16) at each of (2,2i,-2,-2i) give the required zero first and second moments, unit variance, and fourth moment (4). It removes arbitrary Gaussian row coefficients but not calibration or completion cost. Dense CVX-LS has (d^2) real optimization variables. Its numerical tolerance, solver condition number, optical unitary synthesis, insertion loss, residual collection, and row-calibration exposure remain unquantified.

The closest statistical controls are the published CVX-LS and NCVX-LS results in the same paper, Chen–Candès Poisson likelihood/truncated Wirtinger flow, and Dirksen–Krahmer–Römer–Salanevich's real-Gaussian spectral result. The exact block construction only composes the Huang–Li–Needell result with a Parseval completion; it is not a faster or more photon-efficient estimator. In the pinned Astra archive, N18 is a zero-false-alarm detection exponent under a distinct hard interface, N25 is a noisy matrix-query acquisition ledger, and N66 is a known-reference product-sensor memory theorem. None supplies this pure-state Poisson phase-retrieval estimator or removes its calibration premise. Their source-derived status remains unchanged.

The decisive physical follow-up is to compare the known common-background model with a detector-specific blank-calibration protocol on the same $O(d)$-port device, measuring per-channel background means and the $md=O(d^2)$ transfer coefficients. The theorem predicts the displayed $\beta/r$ penalty; a measured state-dependent background, row drift, or blank/signal mismatch falsifies that model. The stronger algorithmic gate—finite-bit solver and compiler guarantees under bounded coefficient error—remains open.
