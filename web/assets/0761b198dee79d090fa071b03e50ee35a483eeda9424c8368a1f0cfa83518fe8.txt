C03_L10 DEEPER ATTACK / PRIMARY-SOURCE PRIORITY AND EXTENSION AUDIT
7 October 2026

SCOPE AND CHECKPOINT

This revision investigates whether (i) the 2026 ECCC catalytic-logspace rank
approximation result, or (ii) Brand--Pratt symbolic differentiation for
determinants, subsumes or improves the low-k estimator for

  c_k(F) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty} |det F[I,J]|^2,

where F is an explicit n-by-n matrix over Q(i). The target is the weighted
squared-volume sum itself, not just whether it is positive. This is an
extension/priority check, not novelty clearance. The independently frozen
INITIAL remains unchanged.

PRIMARY-SOURCE INTERFACES

1. Chakraborty--Datta--Kusre--Mukhopadhyay--Sinhababu, *Maximum Matching and
Related Problems in Catalytic Logspace*, ECCC Report 2026/080, revision 1
(31 May 2026), https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/080/revision/1/download/.
Theorem 1.4 outputs a matrix in a supplied matrix space whose rank is at least
(1-epsilon) times that space's maximum commutative rank. Its rank-approximation
subroutine uses ell=ceil(1/epsilon-1), a field of size at least n^{O(1/epsilon)},
and the proof gives polynomial time poly(n^ell) for fixed epsilon (Theorem 6.3,
pp. 19--21). Corollary 6.3.1 applies this rank guarantee to maximum independent
matching size in a linear matroid matching instance. These are rank/feasibility
optimization interfaces. They do not sum squared determinants or report the
weight of all feasible choices.

For an integer optimum r, an approximation factor alone resolves the exact
threshold only with epsilon < 1/r: otherwise a feasible returned rank below r
may still satisfy the approximation guarantee. Substituting epsilon=Theta(1/r)
sets ell=Theta(r), so this proof's time becomes n^{Theta(r)} (up to its
polynomial factors); it is not a poly(n) exact-threshold consequence when r
grows. For c_k, positivity is equivalent to existence of a feasible k-pair
selection, but even that threshold is already a separate support question.
More decisively, the rank objective cannot estimate the weighted sum: replacing
F by alpha F for any nonzero alpha leaves every support/rank decision unchanged
while multiplying c_k by |alpha|^{2k}. Rank optimization has discarded the
scale and multiplicity/volume information needed for relative approximation.
No reduction from this PTAS to a nontrivial approximation for c_k was found.

2. Cornelius Brand and Kevin Pratt, *Parameterized Applications of Symbolic
Differentiation of (Totally) Multilinear Polynomials*, ICALP 2021,
https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2021.38.
Theorem 7 takes a homogeneous degree-d polynomial g given by a skew arithmetic
circuit and a symbolic d-by-d matrix X with linear entries, and computes the
apolar quantity <g, det X> in 4^d |C| poly(m,d) arithmetic operations (m is the
number of variables). In their convention
<g,f>=g(partial/partial x)f, so this is an exact numeric contraction, not just
a determinant identity test. Their determinant-derivative representation has
up to binom(2d,d) minor coordinates. The general-circuit Theorem 25 has
2^{omega d} |C| poly(m,d) operations; the faster phi^{2d} case in Theorem 13
requires X to be Hankel. The theorem counts arithmetic operations; no bit-time
bound for the rational input instance below is asserted here.

EXACT APOLAR ENCODING OF THE TARGET

This source gives a clean exact encoding, but not a better exponent. Define
V in Q(i)^{n-by-2n} by its paired columns

  u_i = e_i,       v_i = (row_i(F))^T,       i=1,...,n,

and append identity fillers W=[V | I_n] in Q(i)^{n-by-3n}. Let

  D(y,z)=diag(y_1,y_1,...,y_n,y_n,z_1,...,z_n),
  X(y,z)=W D(y,z) W*,
  g(y,z)= e_k(y_1^2,...,y_n^2) e_{n-2k}(z_1,...,z_n).

Here * is conjugate transpose. X is n-by-n and each entry is a homogeneous
linear form. g is homogeneous of degree n in 2n variables. It has a polynomial-
size skew circuit: compute e_k(y_i^2) by a sequential DP whose transition
multiplication by y_i^2 is implemented as two successive multiplications by
the input variable y_i; then compute e_{n-2k}(z_j) by a sequential DP, each
transition multiplying by one input z_j. The second DP starts from the first
DP's output, so no multiplication of two nonconstant circuit values is needed.

Cauchy--Binet gives

  det X = sum_{T subset [3n], |T|=n} |det W[:,T]|^2 product_{a in T} x_a,

where x_a labels the corresponding diagonal entry of D. A monomial in g chooses
a k-set S of pair variables with exponent two and an (n-2k)-set Q of fillers
with exponent one. Matching such a monomial in det X forces T to contain both
columns of every pair in S and exactly the identity columns in Q. The apolar
factorials contribute (2!)^k=2^k. For fixed S, summing over Q is Cauchy--Binet
for the Gram determinant of the 2k selected columns of V:

  sum_{|Q|=n-2k} |det [V[:,P(S)] | I[:,Q]]|^2
      = det(V[:,P(S)]* V[:,P(S)]).

Expanding this Gram determinant by Cauchy--Binet over its 2k row indices, the
identity columns force those rows to contain S; the remaining k indices T are
disjoint from S. Expanding along the identity columns leaves, up to transpose
and sign, the minor F[S,T]. Therefore

  <g, det X> = 2^k sum_{|S|=k} det(V[:,P(S)]*V[:,P(S)])
             = 2^k c_k(F),             and c_k(F)=2^{-k}<g,det X>.

This derivation is valid for all 0<=k<=floor(n/2). The proof uses ordinary
Cauchy--Binet and apolarity; the finite checker is only a bookkeeping audit.
The Brand--Pratt arithmetic algorithm applies over Q(i) by running its field
operations in the constant-degree extension Q(i) (equivalently, representing
each element by a rational real/imaginary pair). The source states its theorem
over Q, so this extension-field transfer is an algebraic adaptation of its
proof, not a verbatim quoted input-field statement.

COMPLEXITY CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITS

Applying Theorem 7 has d=n and |C|=poly(n), hence gives an exact
4^n poly(n)-arithmetic-operation route to c_k for arbitrary F. It does not
remove an exponent and does not extend the relative estimator to central
density. It is also worse than direct exact enumeration of the target's
binom(n,k) binom(n-k,k) minors (at most 3^n choices for fixed k), and for
k=Theta(n) it is worse than the existing low-k randomized route in its
intended regime. In particular, do not substitute a bit-complexity bound for
the paper's arithmetic-operation count.

The Hankel improvement does not apply to this construction for general F:
X=W D W* has coefficient matrices w_a w_a*, and these n-by-n forms are
arbitrary rank-one Hermitian outer products, with no Hankel shift constraint.
No reduction to a Hankel pencil preserving these arbitrary coefficients was
found. The theorem's derivative-state count is about 4^n; whether this special
apolar contraction admits a smaller state representation remains open in this
audit. A row-set decomposition with 2k-by-2k determinants does not help by
itself: summing over row sets costs binom(n,2k), and the Hankel condition still
does not follow.

The ECCC PTAS and Brand--Pratt theorem therefore do not subsume the stochastic
low-k approximation. One computes an approximate maximum rank; the other
computes an exact but exponentially costly weighted contraction. Neither
provides a variance improvement, a central-density FPRAS, nor an exact
polynomial-time algorithm for this weighted sum. This is a scoped conclusion
about the inspected interfaces, not a lower bound against other approaches.

FINITE CHECK / REPRODUCIBILITY

`symbolic_diff_encoding_check.py` and its JSON output check the apolar identity
by direct exact-minor enumeration on 21 rational-complex instances (n=2,3,4;
all k; three deterministic seeds per n). Every reported equality is
<g,det X>=2^k c_k(F). The checker does not implement Theorem 7 or validate its
runtime. The general identity is established by the Cauchy--Binet argument
above, not by these finite instances.

UPDATED STATUS

The low-k randomized estimator remains the only arbitrary-F relative-
approximation route established in this line of work. Brand--Pratt supplies a
new exact apolar representation and an existing exponential evaluation
algorithm, but no competitive algorithmic improvement. ECCC 2026/080 supplies
rank optimization only, and its inverse-epsilon dependence blocks a direct
exact-threshold upgrade for growing rank. Novelty/priority remains UNKNOWN;
no central-density extension, exponent elimination, approximation hardness,
or external expert review has been established.
