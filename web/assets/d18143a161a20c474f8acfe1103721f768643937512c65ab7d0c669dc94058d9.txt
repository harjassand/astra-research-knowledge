RESULT AND SCOPE

For the N=2 c07 Gibbs family with b=0 and diagonal A, there is an exact
Bell-diagonal classification. Writing

  theta_i = 1/2 + a_i/(4 sqrt(2)),   t_i = tanh(theta_i),

and c_i = Tr(rho sigma_i tensor sigma_i), the exact coefficients are

  c_i = (t_i - t_j t_k)/(1 - t_x t_y t_z),   {i,j,k}={x,y,z}.

The denominator is positive for all real theta. The state is separable (and
PPT) exactly when its four Bell probabilities are all at most 1/2, equivalently
when sum_i |c_i| <= 1. It is a mixture of identical-site products
int tau tensor tau dmu(tau) exactly when c_x,c_y,c_z are all nonnegative,
equivalently t_i >= t_j t_k for each i. These are distinct regions.

An exact rational witness inside the finite-N c07 input bounds has Bell
probabilities (8,8,16,1)/33 in sign order (++-),(+-+),(-++),(---), hence
correlations (-1/33,5/11,5/11). It is strictly PPT/separable but cannot be an
identical-site product mixture. A rational finite product decomposition and
an exact-arithmetic matrix check are below. This is one exact N=2 witness; it
does not imply a large-N rate, decoder, asymptotic counterexample, or a claim
about arbitrary N.

DERIVATION OF THE N=2 BELL FAMILY

Use J_i=(sigma_i tensor I + I tensor sigma_i)/2. Direct multiplication gives

  J_i^2 = (I tensor I + sigma_i tensor sigma_i)/2,
  J^2 = 3(I tensor I)/2 + (1/2) sum_i sigma_i tensor sigma_i.

At N=2, s^2=N^(3/2)=2 sqrt(2) and 2/N=1. For diagonal
A=diag(a_x,a_y,a_z), Q_A(J)=sum_i a_i J_i^2. Up to a scalar multiple of the
identity, the exponent K=J^2+Q_A(J)/s^2 is therefore

  K = scalar I + sum_i theta_i sigma_i tensor sigma_i,
  theta_i = 1/2 + a_i/(4 sqrt(2)).

The three commuting Pauli-pair operators have four joint eigenvectors (the
Bell states), with sign triples S={(++-),(+-+),(-++),(---)}. Thus

  p_s = exp(sum_i theta_i s_i)/Z,
  Z = sum_{s in S} exp(sum_i theta_i s_i)
    = 4(prod_i cosh(theta_i) - prod_i sinh(theta_i)).

Differentiating log Z gives c_i=partial_{theta_i} log Z. Dividing numerator
and denominator by prod cosh(theta_i) yields the stated formula in t_i.

EXACT PPT / SEPARABILITY BOUNDARY

For a Bell-diagonal state

  rho(c) = (I tensor I + sum_i c_i sigma_i tensor sigma_i)/4,
  p_s = (1 + sum_i s_i c_i)/4,  s in S,

the partial transpose changes c_y to -c_y. Its eigenvalue multiset is
{1/2-p_s : s in S}. Hence PPT is exactly p_s<=1/2 for all four signs, or
w_s<=sum_{r != s} w_r for each of the four positive Bell weights w_s.

For physical Bell-diagonal rho this is equivalent to sum_i |c_i|<=1. One
way to see the boundary is to note that if sign(c_x)sign(c_y)sign(c_z)=-1,
the sign pattern aligned with c is in S and its Bell probability is
(1+sum_i|c_i|)/4; if the product of the signs is +1, the oppositely aligned
pattern is in S, and positivity of rho already forces sum_i|c_i|<=1. Cases
with a zero coefficient follow by choosing its sign to complete a pattern in
S. Conversely, sum_i|c_i|<=1 implies every p_s lies in [0,1/2].

This condition is also sufficient for separability by the explicit product
decomposition below. Necessity follows because every separable state remains
positive under partial transpose. Thus no unproved extension of a finite test
is being used for the separability criterion.

EXACT IDENTICAL-SITE PRODUCT BOUNDARY

For an identical-site mixture rho=int tau_m tensor tau_m dmu(m), with
 tau_m=(I+m.sigma)/2,

  c_ij=Tr(rho sigma_i tensor sigma_j)=int m_i m_j dmu(m).

So the correlation matrix is positive semidefinite; for a diagonal
Bell-diagonal state this requires c_i>=0 for each i. Conversely, if c_i>=0,
physical positivity gives sum_i c_i<=1, and

  rho = (1-sum_i c_i) (I/2 tensor I/2)
        + sum_i c_i * (tau_{+i} tensor tau_{+i}
                         + tau_{-i} tensor tau_{-i})/2

is an identical-site product mixture. Substituting the exact c_i formula
makes the boundary t_i=t_j t_k; the identical-product region is the
intersection t_i>=t_j t_k for i=x,y,z.

A SEPARABLE STATE OUTSIDE THAT IDENTICAL-PRODUCT REGION

Choose

  theta_x=(log 2)/2,  theta_y=theta_z=log 2,
  a_x=2 sqrt(2)(log 2-1),
  a_y=a_z=2 sqrt(2)(2 log 2-1),  A=diag(a_x,a_y,a_z), b=0.

Then exp(4 theta)=(4,16,16). The four unnormalized Bell weights, divided by
one common factor 1/(4 sqrt(2)), are exactly (8,8,16,1), so

  p=(8/33, 8/33, 16/33, 1/33).

The corresponding correlations are

  c_x=-1/33,  c_y=c_z=5/11.

The largest Bell probability is 16/33<1/2; the partial-transpose eigenvalues
are {1/66,17/66,17/66,31/66}. The state is therefore PPT. Its correlations
also have sum of absolute values 31/33<1.

Define tau_{+i}=(I+sigma_i)/2 and tau_{-i}=(I-sigma_i)/2. Set

  R_x^-=(tau_{+x} tensor tau_{-x} + tau_{-x} tensor tau_{+x})/2,
  R_y^+=(tau_{+y} tensor tau_{+y} + tau_{-y} tensor tau_{-y})/2,
  R_z^+=(tau_{+z} tensor tau_{+z} + tau_{-z} tensor tau_{-z})/2.

Then the exact separable decomposition is

  rho = (2/33)(I/2 tensor I/2) + (1/33)R_x^-
        + (5/11)R_y^+ + (5/11)R_z^+.

The four coefficients sum to one. Each R is a two-branch product mixture;
I/2 tensor I/2 is itself a product state. If pure local states are required,
replace I/2 tensor I/2 by the equal mixture of the four products
 tau_{+z} tensor tau_{+z}, tau_{+z} tensor tau_{-z},
 tau_{-z} tensor tau_{+z}, tau_{-z} tensor tau_{-z}.
All decomposition weights are rational.

No identical-site mixture can give this rho: for every such mixture
Tr(rho sigma_x tensor sigma_x)=int m_x^2 dmu>=0, whereas here it is -1/33.
This excludes any number of identical-site branches, not merely the six-axis
ansatz.

The example fits M=2: using 1/2<log 2<3/4 gives |a_x|<sqrt(2)<2 and
0<a_y=a_z<sqrt(2)<2. Also the c07 positive heat-coefficient condition at
N=2 is t0=1-M/(2 sqrt(2))=1-1/sqrt(2)>0. Thus the witness is within the
small-N version of that stated bounded-input condition. It remains only a
finite-N witness and says nothing about the asymptotic fixed-M claims in S1.

STATUS / LIMITS

This proves an exact finite-dimensional classification for the N=2 diagonal,
b=0 Bell family and an exact separable-but-not-identical-site-mixture witness
within M=2. It does not disprove the existence of mixtures of general product
states, since the state is explicitly separable. It does not disprove
large-N asymptotic approximation results, nor does it certify a parameter-
independent decoder, sampling procedure, finite-bit cost, or priority.
