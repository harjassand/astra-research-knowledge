1. Exact model, public inputs, and theorem scope
------------------------------------------------
There are S=L-1 sites, L>=2, n_i=(I-Z_i)/2, and

 N=sum_i n_i, W=sum_{i=1}^{S-1} n_i n_{i+1},
 Hcrit=J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-X_iX_{i+1}-Y_iY_{i+1}-Z_iZ_{i+1})
             +J(I-Z_1)+J(I-Z_S), J>0.

For S=1 both endpoint terms act on that one site. In the m-sector, basis
configurations are the m-subsets X of {1,...,S}. Write X~Y for a permitted
nearest-neighbor exclusion hop, b(X)=n_1(X)+n_S(X). The exact form is

 <psi,Hcrit psi>=2J sum_{unordered X~Y}|psi(X)-psi(Y)|^2
                    +2J sum_X b(X)|psi(X)|^2.                 (1)

Put A_L=L^2 Hcrit and fix tau>0. The Hamiltonian with nonnegative contact is

 H_L(g_L,lambda)=Hcrit+(g_L/L)W-(lambda/L^2)N,
 Z_L(g_L,lambda)=Tr exp[-tau(A_L+g_L L W-lambda N)].

All physical occupation qubits and sectors are retained in this full trace.
The chain has 2^(L-1) dimensions, and the m-block dimension is binom(L-1,m).
No ideal Bose partition oracle or source eigenbasis is supplied.

Theorem A (uniform tail). Fix any lambda_+<lambda_c=2J*pi^2. For every
lambda<=lambda_+, every L>=2, and every nonnegative g_L, there is a finite
C=C(J,tau,lambda_+,s), independent of L and g_L, such that, for each fixed
0<s<lambda_c-lambda_+ and every integer r>=0,

 1<=Z_L(g_L,lambda)<=C(J,tau,lambda_+,0),
 Pr_{L,g_L,lambda}(N>=r)<=C exp(-tau s r).                    (2)

In (2), use the partition bound at lambda_++s for C. A finite lower bound
on lambda is unnecessary for the scalar/tail estimates, because the
partition increases with lambda. Uniform state convergence below is stated
on compact lambda intervals.

Theorem B (zero-coupling limit and exact failure boundary). If g_L>=0 and
g_L->0, then for each fixed lambda<lambda_c the partition and grand state
converge to the ideal symmetric Dirichlet Bose values on (0,1):

 Z_B(tau,lambda)=product_{k>=1}
             (1-exp[tau(lambda-2J*pi^2*k^2)])^(-1),           (3)
 rho_B=Z_B^(-1) direct-sum_{m>=0}
       exp[-tau(A_m^B-lambda m)],
 A_m^B=-2J sum_{a=1}^m partial_a^2 on L^2_sym((0,1)^m),
 form domain H^1_0((0,1)^m) intersect L^2_sym.

Use the parameter-independent exact gap-coordinate embeddings described
in Section 7. The convergence is in trace norm on their common bosonic
Fock space, and uniform on compact lambda intervals below lambda_c.
For every fixed lambda>=lambda_c and every sequence g_L>=0,g_L->0,

 Z_L(g_L,lambda)->infinity,
 Pr_{L,g_L,lambda}(N<=K)->0 for every fixed K.                (4)

Theorem C (proved negative marginal region). With fixed kappa>=0 and
g_L=-kappa/L, the conclusions of (2)--(3) hold if
lambda+kappa<lambda_c. The limiting Bose chemical parameter is ORIGINAL
lambda, not lambda+kappa; the latter is only the all-sector comparator.
These conclusions are uniform on compact kappa,lambda sets with a strict
upper margin on lambda+kappa. No conclusion is supplied for the entire
complement of this region.

No finite-L Gibbs sampler, efficient state decoder, optimal archive size,
or quantum-versus-classical lower bound is claimed by these theorems.

2. Exact all-sector sine ground-state transform
---------------------------------------------
Define phi_i=sin(pi*i/L)>0 for 1<=i<L and

 epsilon_{1,L}=4J(1-cos(pi/L)), c_L=L^2 epsilon_{1,L}.

The one-particle Dirichlet path operator has off-diagonals -2J and diagonal
4J, so h phi=epsilon_{1,L} phi, with phi_0=phi_L=0. For any m-subset X put
f_m(X)=product_{i in X} phi_i>0. Sum the one-particle identities for its m
occupied coordinates. The unrestricted one-coordinate sum includes the
two blocked moves across each occupied adjacent pair {i,i+1}. Their combined
contribution, divided by f_m(X), is

 2J[2-phi_{i+1}/phi_i-phi_i/phi_{i+1}].

Removing those blocked moves therefore gives the EXACT identity

 (Hcrit,m f_m)(X)/f_m(X)
  =m epsilon_{1,L}
   +2J sum_{i,i+1 in X}(phi_{i+1}/phi_i+phi_i/phi_{i+1}-2)
  >=m epsilon_{1,L}.                                      (5)

The endpoint killing in (1) is precisely the ghost part of the summed
one-particle equation; it has not been omitted.

For any complex psi, write v(X)=psi(X)/f_m(X). Expanding the finite graph
energy gives the weighted ground-state identity

 <psi,Hcrit,m psi>
  =2J sum_{X~Y} f_m(X)f_m(Y)|v(X)-v(Y)|^2
   +sum_X [(Hcrit,m f_m)(X)/f_m(X)] |psi(X)|^2.              (6)

Combining (5)--(6) proves the OPERATOR inequality

 Hcrit>=epsilon_{1,L}N, A_L>=c_L N, c_L->lambda_c.           (7)

For g_L=-kappa/L, W<=N then gives
A_L-kappa W-lambda N>=(c_L-kappa-lambda)N. Vacuum ground-state stability
in this region is immediate; thermal tightness is not. Sections 3--6
account for the sector entropy rather than replacing it by the gap alone.

3. A full critical trace bound with logarithmic entropy
------------------------------------------------------
Let Hneu,S be the critical exchange sum with its endpoint killing removed.
It is 2J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-P_{i,i+1}), where P swaps the two spin factors.
For every fixed t>0 we prove

 T_L(t):=Tr exp(-t A_L)
   <=Tr exp(-t L^2 Hneu,S),
 log T_L(t)<=C_t [log(L+1)]^(3/2) for all sufficiently large L. (8)

The comparison is eigenvalue min-max, not operator monotonicity of exp.

3a. A block spin-deficit bound.
For a block of ell spins, let Hneu,ell be its exchange Hamiltonian. For
i<j, the transposition P_ij is a product of 2(j-i)-1 adjacent swaps, using
each intermediate bond at most twice. Telescoping 1-U_1...U_k, followed by
Cauchy--Schwarz and unitarity of each prefix, gives

 <I-P_ij><=4(j-i)sum_{a=i}^{j-1}<I-P_{a,a+1}>.              (9)

This holds for every vector and hence as a form inequality. Sum over i<j.
At bond a, sum_{i<=a<j}(j-i)=ell*a*(ell-a)/2<=ell^3/8.
Therefore the complete-graph exchange Hamiltonian obeys

 Hcomplete<=ell^3/2 * Hneu,ell.

With total spin T, Hcomplete=2J[(ell/2)(ell/2+1)-T(T+1)]. If
k=ell/2-T, 0<=k<=floor(ell/2), this proves

 Hneu,ell>=4J/ell^3 * k(ell+1-k)>=2J k/ell^2.               (10)

The chain is SU(2)-invariant. The multiplicity d_k of spin ell/2-k is at
most binom(ell,k), by counting its highest/lowest-weight vectors in the
weight-k subspace; each representation has ell-2k+1<=ell+1 dimensions.
Consequently, for beta>0,

 Tr exp(-beta Hneu,ell)
   <=(ell+1)sum_k binom(ell,k)exp(-2J beta k/ell^2)
   <=(ell+1)(1+exp(-2J beta/ell^2))^ell.                    (11)

This deliberately weak bound is enough. Its method is established in
Napiórkowski--Seiringer1912.01967v2 §5; (9)--(11) reconstruct it here with
slightly weaker constants and no imported low-temperature limit theorem.

3b. Block localization.
Partition S sites into b consecutive blocks, each of length at most ell.
Discarding positive cross-block exchange bonds gives a tensor sum of block
operators. Min-max and trace factorization yield the explicit bound

 log T_L(t)<=b log(ell+1)+S exp(-2J t L^2/ell^2).            (12)

Take ell=floor[L sqrt(Jt/log(L+1))], clipped to {1,...,S}. For large L,
ell<=L sqrt(Jt/log(L+1)), ell>=half that expression, and b<=S/ell+1.
Thus the exponential term is <=S/(L+1)^2 and

 log T_L(t)<=(2/sqrt(Jt)+2)[log(L+1)]^(3/2).                (13)

One sufficient onset is L>=max{64,ceil(exp(4Jt)),ceil(16/(Jt)^2)};
larger onsets are harmless. Formula (12) itself is valid without asymptotic
choices and can be used for the finitely many smaller L. The crucial point
is log T=o(L), and in fact only logarithmic-power entropy remains.

4. Contact-density bound without a positive contact gap
-----------------------------------------------------
For a normalized m-sector psi let D(i,j)=<n_i n_j> for i!=j, and D(i,i)=0.
Then sum_{i,j}D(i,j)=m(m-1). Fix y distinct from neighboring sites i,i+1.
Pair configurations related by the hop i<->i+1 that retain y occupied.
Let E_i^y be the sum of |psi(X)-psi(Y)|^2 over these unordered edges.
The difference of their squared amplitudes gives

 |D(i+1,y)-D(i,y)|
     <=sqrt[2 E_i^y (D(i+1,y)+D(i,y))].                   (14)

Indeed |a|^2-|b|^2=Re[(a-b)(conjugate a+conjugate b)] and
sum|a+b|^2<=2sum(|a|^2+|b|^2). Every hop has m-1 common occupied sites,
so

 sum_{i,y} E_i^y=(m-1)sum_{X~Y}|psi(X)-psi(Y)|^2
                 <=(m-1)<Hcrit>/(2J).                    (15)

For y<=S/2 choose x_y>y minimizing D(x,y). Its value is at most
(2/S)sum_x D(x,y). Telescope from y+1 to x_y, starting the comparison index
at i=y+1: the contact edge i=y is excluded from (14). Then sum
over such y and apply Cauchy--Schwarz. The first terms total at most
2m(m-1)/S. The telescoped terms are at most

 sqrt[2 * (m-1)<Hcrit>/(2J) * 2m(m-1)]
     =(m-1)sqrt(2m/J) sqrt(<Hcrit>).

For y>S/2 write D(y+1,y)=D(y,y+1) and telescope left from a minimizer
x_y<=y, at fixed second coordinate y+1. The same bounds apply. Thus

 <W><=4m(m-1)/S
       +4(m-1)sqrt(m/(2J)) sqrt(<Hcrit>).                 (16)

For m=0,1 it is zero. Equation (16) is the S=1/2 contact-density estimate
of Napiórkowski--Seiringer Proposition5.3, now proved in the exact occupation
normalization of (1). It uses only the critical kinetic energy, not g_->0.
In particular, when m and <A_L> are polynomial in log L, <W>=o(1).

5. Exact gap-cube map and low-energy near-isometry
-----------------------------------------------
For x_1<...<x_m put y_a=x_a-a+1, so 1<=y_1<=...<=y_m<=n=L-m.
For a weak tuple y, let r_v be its multiplicities and w(y)=m!/product_v r_v!.
Define the linear map into the symmetric labeled cube

 (K_m psi)(z_1,...,z_m)=psi(sort z)/sqrt(m!), z in {1,...,n}^m.

It is a contraction, with the exact norm formula

 ||K_m psi||^2=sum_y |psi(y)|^2/product_v r_v!,
 ||psi||^2-||K_m psi||^2<=<psi,W psi>.                     (17)

The second inequality holds pointwise: without a repeated value the loss
is zero; with a repetition W=sum_v(r_v-1)>=1 bounds the loss<=1.

Let h_n^D be the one-particle path operator with form
2J[sum_{v=1}^{n-1}|u(v+1)-u(v)|^2+|u(1)|^2+|u(n)|^2], and let
B_{L,m}=L^2 sum_{a=1}^m h_n^D on the symmetric cube. The EXACT form bound is

 <K_m psi,B_{L,m} K_m psi><=<psi,A_L psi>.                 (18)

To verify its edge coefficient, a chamber hop v->v+1 has
w(y)r_v=m!/[(r_v-1)!r_{v+1}!product_{u!=v,v+1}r_u!]
labeled preimage edges. Each K amplitude contributes 1/m!, so the resulting
coefficient is <=1, whereas that exclusion edge in (1) has coefficient1.
The lower/upper cube ghost counts are w(y)r_1 and w(y)r_n, also <=m!;
they are controlled by the corresponding endpoint killing in (1).
This proves (18) for all m and L, not just fixed m. It does not by itself
give a trace comparison, because K_m is not an isometry.

If <A_L><=E and m<=M, (16)--(17) imply

 ||K_m psi||^2>=1-eta_L,
 eta_L=4M(M-1)/(L-1)
       +4(M-1)sqrt(M/(2J)) sqrt(E)/L.                    (19)

For M,E=O([log L]^(3/2)), eta_L=O([log L]^3/L)->0.

Let a_j be ordered eigenvalues of A_L in the m-sector. On the span of
its first j eigenvectors, if a_j<=E, every vector satisfies (19) and
(18). K is injective there, and its Rayleigh quotient is <=a_j/(1-eta_L).
Min-max on the symmetric cube therefore gives

 a_j>=(1-eta_L)b_j, whenever a_j<=E,                      (20)

where b_j are ordered eigenvalues of B_{L,m}. In particular

 Tr_m[e^(-tau A_L)1_{A_L<=E}]
       <=Tr_sym,cube e^[-tau(1-eta_L)B_{L,m}].            (21)

This is the valid spectral use of the nearly isometric map. No false
exponential operator-monotonicity or global free-boson comparison is used.

6. The spectral-tail step: entropy is fully charged
-------------------------------------------------
Fix mu<lambda_c; it will be a partition comparator, possibly lambda+s.
Choose 0<c_0<lambda_c with c_0>mu, for example
c_0=(lambda_c+max(mu,0))/2. For all sufficiently large L, c_L>=c_0.
Set

 a=min(1/2,(c_0-mu)/(2c_0)), d=(c_0-mu)/2>0.

Then (7) gives

 A_L-mu N>=a A_L+d N.                                    (22)

For the particle tail at M, since A_L and N commute,

 Tr[e^(-tau(A_L-mu N))1_{N>M}]
       <=exp(-tau d M) T_L(a tau).                       (23)

For the critical-energy tail at E, the same bound gives

 Tr[e^(-tau(A_L-mu N))1_{A_L>E}]
       <=exp(-tau a E/2) T_L(a tau/2).                   (24)

Use (13), write ellog=log(L+1), C_t=2/sqrt(Jt)+2, and choose

 M_L=ceil[2 C_{a tau} ellog^(3/2)/(tau d)],
 E_L=4 C_{a tau/2} ellog^(3/2)/(tau a).                   (25)

The two discarded traces in (23)--(24) tend to zero (each is bounded by
exp[-C_t ellog^(3/2)]). These are global traces including all binomial
sector multiplicities, not separate fixed-m limits.

On the retained space m<=M_L,A_L<=E_L, (19) gives eta_L->0. The one-particle
energies of h_n^D are epsilon_{k,n}=4J[1-cos(pi*k/(n+1))]. Because
n+1=L-m+1<=L, these are no smaller than epsilon_{k,L-1}, with extra modes
of the latter only enlarging the Bose trace. Equations (20)--(21), summed
over the retained m-sectors with fugacity exp(tau mu m), consequently give

 Z_L(0,mu)
 <=o(1)+product_{k=1}^{L-1}
   (1-exp[tau(mu-(1-eta_L)L^2 epsilon_{k,L-1})])^(-1).    (26)

For large L the first-mode gap is strictly positive. The other modes obey
L^2 epsilon_{k,L-1}>=8J k^2. Choose eta_L<=1/4 and also small enough that
(1-eta_L)c_0-mu>=d. Then a simple explicit dominating constant is

 C_B=(1-exp(-tau d))^(-1)
       product_{k>=2}(1-exp[tau(mu-6J k^2)])^(-1)<infinity. (27)

Here mu<2J*pi^2<24J, so even the k=2 gap in (27) is positive.
The finitely many smaller L have finite traces and are harmless. More
precisely (12), (19), (25), c_L>=lambda_c-J*pi^4/(6L^2), and an elementary
bound K(log(L+1))^3/L on eta_L give a computable sufficient onset and a
finite upper bound C_B+2 thereafter. Before that onset one may use
2^(L-1)exp[tau max(mu,0)(L-1)]. No impractical constant is hidden as an
algorithmic efficiency claim.

Thus sup_L Z_L(0,mu)<infinity for every mu<lambda_c. In fact (26) and
dominated mode products prove

 limsup_L Z_L(0,mu)<=Z_B(tau,mu).                         (28)

For nonnegative contacts, eigenvalue min-max in each fixed m-sector gives

 Z_{L,m}(g_L,lambda)<=Z_{L,m}(0,lambda).

The vacuum contributes1. Finally

 sum_{m>=r}Z_{L,m}(g_L,lambda)
       <=exp(-tau s r) Z_L(0,lambda+s),                   (29)

which proves Theorem A by (28) and the finite-L bound. This is the promised
entropy repair all the way to the one-particle threshold.

7. Fixed-sector and full-Fock zero-coupling convergence
----------------------------------------------------
The common embedding is the polar isometry of K_m:

 (U_m psi)(z)=psi(sort z)/sqrt(w(sort z)).

It is an exact isometry onto the symmetric finite cube. Embed its values
as centered nodal step functions on (0,1)^m, with mesh h=1/(n+1), amplitude
h^(-m/2), and zero outside the interior cells. Call it I_{L,m}. For m=0
use the scalar identity; take the direct sum for full Fock space. All maps
depend only on L,m, not on g_L,lambda.

For fixed m and bounded critical scaled energy, (16) gives <W>=O_m(1/L).
Hence the step function from K_m and the exact source isometry differ by
O_m(L^(-1/2)) in norm. The free cube form (18) controls multilinear
interpolation with zero outer ghosts, whose H^1 energy is no larger than
its grid energy. Its step/interpolant norm difference is O_m(1/L) on a
bounded-energy set. Rellich compactness therefore transfers to the exact
source isometry even when no g_->0 is available.

The sharp kinetic liminf follows from (18) and lower semicontinuity of
these interpolants. Smooth symmetric Dirichlet functions have recovery
sequences obtained by sampling the weak tuples and taking their source
amplitudes sqrt(m!)h^(m/2)F(hy), followed by normalization. Away from the
repeated-coordinate layers the exclusion graph is an ordinary finite
Dirichlet difference grid. The repeated-coordinate layers occupy O_m(1/L)
mass; their finitely many edge types have smooth differences O(h), so
their contribution to scaled kinetic error is O_m(1/L). This gives the
recovery energy 2J integral|grad F|^2. Density in symmetric H^1_0 gives
all form-domain recovery vectors.

If g_L>=0,g_L->0, the liminf contact is nonnegative and can be dropped.
For each smooth recovery sequence, L<W>=O_m(1), so its extra scaled energy
g_L L<W> tends to zero. These observations prove fixed-m Mosco convergence
to the free Bose Dirichlet form. Collective interpolation compactness gives
operator-norm resolvent convergence in the common embeddings. Fixed-m heat
traces converge: at half time the same all-sector partition bound controls
the trace, and spectral min-max plus compactness makes the high-eigenvalue
tail vanish. Spectral-cluster convergence then upgrades to trace-norm heat
convergence. These are the standard compact form arguments already written
in sol_attractive_critical/continuum_limit.txt; the new pure-kinetic contact
estimate removes that proof's need for g_->0 at the norm-closeness step.

Summing finite sectors and using the uniform exponential tail (29) proves
Theorem B below lambda_c, including normalization and full trace norm.
Together with finite-sector convergence, (28) gives equality of the
partition limit in (3). Compact lambda sets use the same strict upper
margin and the same tails; a contradiction subsequence gives uniformity.

For lambda>=lambda_c, the ideal m-sector contains the state with all m
bosons in the first sine mode. Its grand contribution is
exp[tau(lambda-lambda_c)m]>=1. Fixed-sector convergence and positivity imply

 liminf_L Z_L(g_L,lambda)>=sum_{m=0}^M
                      exp[tau(lambda-lambda_c)m]

for every finite M. Letting M grow proves Z_L->infinity. Every fixed finite
set of sector weights converges to finite values, so its normalized mass
vanishes. This proves (4), including EXACTLY lambda=lambda_c. It does not
claim macroscopic particle density or specify a divergent-number rate.

8. Negative marginal window: a genuine proved region
---------------------------------------------------
For g_L=-kappa/L, scaled energy is A_L-kappa W-lambda N. Since W<=N,
min-max in each sector gives

 Z_{L,m}(-kappa/L,lambda)<=Z_{L,m}(0,lambda+kappa).          (30)

If lambda+kappa<lambda_c, select s>0 with lambda+kappa+s<lambda_c and
apply (29) to that comparator. Vacuum still contributes1, so normalized
number tails are uniformly exponential, independent of the likelihood.

For fixed m, -kappa W has operator norm<=kappa(m-1), so bounded energy of
A_L-kappa W controls the critical energy. Equation (16) then implies
<W>=O_m(1/L); its negative perturbation vanishes on every bounded-energy
sequence as well as every smooth recovery sequence. The fixed-m free Bose
form/resolvent/heat limits of Section7 therefore remain valid. Combine with
(30) to get full-Fock convergence to rho_B at ORIGINAL lambda. This proves
Theorem C.

No assertion is made that lambda+kappa=lambda_c is the quantum transition
line. It is a sufficient comparison region. The classical functional below
can have a vacuum regime extending beyond it, and that mismatch identifies
the remaining quantum lower-bound problem.

9. Entropy does not contribute at speed L
---------------------------------------
For bounded kappa>=0 and bounded lambda, let E_{min,L} be the lowest energy
of H_L=Hcrit-(kappa/L^2)W-(lambda/L^2)N, and Z_L=Tr exp(-tau L^2 H_L).
Vacuum gives E_min<=0, while W<=N<=L-1 gives E_min>=-C/L with a fixed
C>=kappa+max(lambda,0). For any fixed 0<alpha<1,

 H_L>=alpha Hcrit+(1-alpha)E_min I-alpha C/L I.

Eigenvalue min-max and (8) yield

 -tau L^2 E_min<=log Z_L
 <=-tau(1-alpha)L^2 E_min+tau alpha C L+O_alpha((log L)^(3/2)).

Since E_min<=0,

 0<=[log Z_L+tau L^2 E_min]/L<=tau alpha C+o(1).

First let L grow at fixed alpha, then alpha decrease to zero. Therefore

 [log Z_L]/L+tau L E_{min,L}->0.                           (31)

This holds without assuming a limit for L E_min. It removes the possible
speed-L entropy term from the weak-anisotropy free-energy question. The
2^(L-1) dimension bound would have left an erroneous unresolved log2
contribution at this scale.

10. Coherent functional: exact classical threshold, separate from the chain
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a smooth product-spin profile with polar angle theta(x), theta(0)=
theta(1)=0, the exact bond expectation is J[1-cos(theta_{i+1}-theta_i)].
The endpoints vanish for the corresponding sampled profile; equivalently
one can sample on the L-2 interval between the two endpoint sites, which
has the same limit. Riemann sums give

 L <H_L> -> E_{kappa,lambda}(theta)
   =(J/2)integral_0^1 theta'^2
       -kappa integral_0^1 sin^4(theta/2)
       -lambda integral_0^1 sin^2(theta/2).                (32)

Allow theta in H^1_0 and fold to 0<=theta<=pi; this preserves occupations
and cannot increase the kinetic energy. Azimuthal variation of a Bloch
profile contributes nonnegative kinetic energy, so the minimum among
coherent profiles is planar. Put p=sin^2(theta/2). Define the classical
vacuum boundary

 lambda_*(kappa)=inf_{theta!=0}
       [(J/2)integral theta'^2-kappa integral p^2]/integral p.

Poincare and (arcsin u)^2>=u^2+u^4/3 for 0<=u<=1 give

 E_{kappa,lambda}(theta)
  >=(lambda_c-lambda)integral p
       +(lambda_c/3-kappa)integral p^2.                   (33)

The arcsine inequality follows by squaring arcsin u>=u+u^3/6; the extra
u^6 term is nonnegative. Thus for 0<=kappa<=lambda_c/3,
lambda_*(kappa)>=lambda_c. Small theta=A sin(pi x) gives the reverse
Rayleigh inequality as A->0, hence

 lambda_*(kappa)=lambda_c for 0<=kappa<=lambda_c/3.         (34)

At lambda=lambda_c, expand (32) on that same exact sine profile:

 E_{kappa,lambda_c}(A sin pi x)
    =A^4[(lambda_c/48-kappa/16)integral sin^4(pi x)]+O(A^6).

The quartic coefficient is (lambda_c-3kappa)/128; the sextic coefficient is
(15kappa-lambda_c)/4608. At kappa=lambda_c/3 the first nonzero coefficient
is the positive lambda_c/1152 at order A^6.
Therefore for kappa>lambda_c/3 some sufficiently small nonzero profile has
negative energy at lambda_c, and its quotient lies strictly below lambda_c:

 lambda_*(kappa)<lambda_c for kappa>lambda_c/3.             (35)

Equations (34)--(35) locate the exact tricritical parameter of the classical
variational boundary. They do NOT establish a quantum phase boundary.
Negative E profiles do provide rigorous chain partition lower bounds via
