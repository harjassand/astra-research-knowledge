## A uniform elementary duplication bound

For a finite point with reduced x-coordinate `x=a/b`, `b>0`, `gcd(a,b)=1`,
the projective x-coordinate of its double is `[F:G]`, where

`F=(a²+25b²)²`, `G=4ab(a²−25b²)`.

These formulas also cover the y=0 points: `G=0`, `F>0`, so the image is O.
For O itself use x-coordinate `[1:0]`; the same homogeneous formula maps it
to `[1:0]`. Projective height agrees with `H_x(O)=1` and with
`max(|a|,b)` for finite reduced x. Thus torsion and infinity are included.

The following two homogeneous polynomial identities are exact:

`250000 b⁷ = (400b³−12a²b)F + (125ab²+3a³)G`,

`16a⁷ = (−300ab²+16a³)F + (−1875b³−125a²b)G`.

Our script checks their coefficients independently. Let `g=gcd(F,G)`.
The identities give `g|250000 b⁷` and `g|16a⁷`. Since `16|250000`, also
`g|250000 a⁷`. Since a and b are coprime, `g|250000`. This argument includes
`G=0`; no division by G is used.

Writing `H=max(|a|,b)`, the elementary inequalities

`H⁴≤F≤676H⁴`, `|G|≤104H⁴`

give

`H⁴/250000 ≤ H_x([2]Q) ≤ 676H⁴`.

Consequently, with `h_x=log H_x`,

`|h_x([2]Q)−4h_x(Q)|≤log250000` for every rational point Q.

The canonical normalization in the source is
`2 hhat(Q)=lim_N 4^(−N) h_x([2^N]Q)`.
Successive terms differ by at most `4^(−N−1) log250000`; summing that
geometric series proves

`|h_x(Q)−2 hhat(Q)|≤c0`, where `c0=(log250000)/3`.

In particular `c0<5`: the exponential series gives `e³>13`, and
`13⁵=371293>250000`, so `log250000<15`. We may therefore use the displayed
integer error bound **c=5**. The derivation assumes the standard quadraticity

`h_x(G)≤2hhat(G)+c0≤2hhat(P0)+c0≤h_x(P0)+2c0`.
