# Independent hostile audit: skull-wave PDE and speed claim

## Finding

The SI contains a confirmed factor-of-two inconsistency in Eqs. 29–30 and a separate dimensional/sign error in Eq. 32. Re-deriving the full PDE also shows why the printed Fisher minimum is not the realized speed of the released run: at the homogeneous mesenchymal edge it is the linear spreading speed, while the notebook seeds a much shallower phenotype tail and the coupled solver includes tissue flow. The public solver replay reproduces its saved fields, and the scalar reduction initialized from the same broad tail advances near the tail-dependent speed, not the steep-tail minimum. These checks correct the source's speed derivation and narrow what its fitted parameter means; they do not establish a new biological control law.

The primary source is Dang et al., [“Self-propagating wave drives morphogenesis of skull bones in vivo”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59164-9), with the authors' [code and data pinned to commit `b2cbb61097cdc25bfff1e0a89ba690a31c2a4adc`](https://github.com/YitengDang/SkullWave/tree/b2cbb61097cdc25bfff1e0a89ba690a31c2a4adc). Exact source anchors and limitations are in [`sources/NOTES.md`](sources/NOTES.md); the derivation is in [`proof/reduction_linearization_and_units.md`](proof/reduction_linearization_and_units.md).

## Derivation and numerical consequences

Under the SI's explicit simplifying assumptions \(\partial_x\rho=0\) and \(v=0\), its fraction PDE reduces to \(\phi_t=D\phi_{xx}+r\phi(1-\phi)\), where \(r=(k_B-k_A)+\alpha(E_B-E_A)\). For this logistic KPP equation, the steep-tail critical speed is \(c_*=2\sqrt{Dr}\). SI p.12 states that formula in prose, then omits the 2 in Eqs. 29–30. Inverting the printed Eq. 30 through Eq. 31 yields \(\alpha=((v_F-v_A)^2/D-(k_B-k_A))/(E_B-E_A)\); the Eq. 32 expression instead has the growth-difference sign reversed and lacks a factor of \(D\) in the speed term. Its physical dimensions therefore do not match Eq. 27. The corrected steep-tail inversion is \(\alpha=((v_F-v_A)^2-4D(k_B-k_A))/(4D(E_B-E_A))\).

The leading-edge linearization of the *full* PDE about \((\rho,\phi,v)=(\rho_A^c,0,0)\) gives \(\psi_t=D\psi_{xx}+r\psi\) for a fraction perturbation \(\psi\). Density and force balance respond at first order, but their effects in the fraction equation enter as products \(v\psi_x\) and \((\rho_x/\rho)\psi_x\), which are second order. Thus \(2\sqrt{Dr}\) is the linear spreading speed for that homogeneous edge. Nonlinear mechanical transport can still change which wave speed the coupled system selects, so the full PDE is not thereby proven pulled or scalar-KPP.

For the released far-edge densities, \(k_B-k_A=(.0077-.0067)/(10\times.0077)=.012987\,h^{-1}\). With notebook numerical inputs, \(\alpha\Delta E=.0439992\,h^{-1}\), \(r=.0569862\,h^{-1}\), and \(c_*=1.8491\,\mu m/h\). The initialized fraction \(\phi_0=(1-\tanh(4x/1500))/2\) has exponential tail exponent \(q_0=8/1500=.005333\,\mu m^{-1}\), versus \(q_* =\sqrt{r/D}=.061637\,\mu m^{-1}\). It starts far inside the shallow-tail regime. The scalar linear dispersion value \(Dq_0+r/q_0=10.7649\,\mu m/h\) describes that branch under the scalar KPP assumptions. An independent 0–8 h finite-difference replay of the scalar equation gives 10.694 \(\mu m/h\), versus 1.849 for the steep-tail minimum.

The released coupled solver's \(\phi=.5\) level moves at 21.7353 \(\mu m/h\) over 0–8 h. Sampling its local tissue velocity and density-gradient drift along that level gives a descriptive adjusted speed of 10.8082 \(\mu m/h\), close to the scalar shallow-tail comparator. The independent NumPy transcription reproduces source arrays for \(\rho,\phi,V\) with maximum absolute errors below \(4\times 10^{-13}\). This is a finite code/data replay, not evidence that the tissue follows the reduced scalar PDE. In the standard terminology, the scalar logistic KPP equation's steep-tail wave is pulled; a shallow seeded tail can select a faster linear branch without being a pushed wave. Whether mechanical feedback pushes the *full* biological wave remains unknown.

## Units, estimators, and fit boundary

Eq. 27 requires \([\alpha]=(\text{stress}\cdot\text{time})^{-1}\). Table S1 gives \(\alpha=3.5\times10^{-6}\) with no units and reports stiffness in Pa. The notebook converts Pa to internal stress units by 12.96, but multiplies by the same unconverted alpha. If the table means Pa\(^{-1}\)h\(^{-1}\), the code should scale alpha by \(1/12.96\); that conditional replay gives 14.281 \(\mu m/h\), rather than 21.917 \(\mu m/h\) for the 0–6 h level slope. If the alpha was fit numerically in the notebook's internal stress unit, the code may be internally consistent and the table has omitted its units. Source evidence does not decide between these interpretations. Table S1 also gives \(\eta=10^4\,Pa\,s\), but the executable notebook uses \(10^3\,Pa\,s\); replacing the code value by the table value changes this baseline speed by only about 0.001 \(\mu m/h\) in the replay.

The authors describe fitting the stiffness-to-differentiation parameter to expansion velocities and reporter profiles; Table S1 identifies front velocity and Eq. 32 as the source for alpha. The primary package gives no condition split or independent front-speed holdout. The notebook's active speed estimator also minimizes \(|\sum_x(\phi_t-\phi_{t+\Delta t})|\), not a pointwise absolute profile error. On the released fields it returns the reported 0.03728287 and picks a three-cell shift; the pointwise L1 error at that shift is 0.41495017. Replacing the signed-mass objective with L1 keeps the same grid point for this output, so the velocity result itself survives this recheck while the reported residual is not a shape error.

Several speeds in the paper concern different observables. From source CSVs, the four ex-vivo interface-height OLS slopes over 0–6 h are 25.67, 12.15, 10.89, and 15.00 \(\mu m/h\) (mean 15.93, between-video sample SD 6.72). Mean x-displacement of front-tracked cells has mean OLS slope 8.89 \(\mu m/h\), while the supplied cell speed-magnitude rows average 13.17±4.09 \(\mu m/h\). The in-vivo plotting script estimates 19.05 \(\mu m/h\) from a 12 h change in \(\sqrt{\text{area}}\), a whole-bone size proxy. The released simulation yields 21.92 \(\mu m/h\) for a 0–6 h interpolated level fit, 20.59 for the plotting Rmd's strict \(\phi<.5\) origin-constrained estimator, and 18.75 for the notebook's two-window profile shift. Each has a different frame or finite estimator; they should not be treated as exchangeable measurements.

The BAPN perturbation is a valuable directionally consistent biological observation. The article reports reduced front stiffness, mildly increased center stiffness, altered collagen-fiber area, a shallower front reporter-intensity slope, and larger endpoint bone area after feeding dams 1 g/kg BAPN. The reported area comparison has small litter counts (2–3 litters per group/stage) and sample counts 4–13; its test is reported at \(p<.0001\), with the reporter-slope test at \(p=.00786\). The article does not provide a matched BAPN front-speed series or a quantitatively BAPN-parameterized PDE prediction. Endpoint area supports the proposed gradient mechanism qualitatively; it does not test Eq. 32 or validate the front-speed law.

## Conditional identifiability result and decisive next test

For the scalar shallow-tail relation \(c=Dq+(g+\alpha\Delta E)/q\), any admissible measured speed admits \(\alpha(q)=[q(c-Dq)-g]/\Delta E\). At the released model's drift-adjusted comparator \(c=10.808225\,\mu m/h\), three distinct shallow tail values \(q=.005333,.010,.020\,\mu m^{-1}\) give positive alpha values \(3.5184\times10^{-6},7.4452\times10^{-6},1.5685\times10^{-5}\) with the same speed. Therefore speed alone does not identify alpha when q is not independently acquired, even in this favorable scalar model. It does not refute the coupled source PDE.

The decisive biological test would measure, on the same developmental front, the initial \(\phi\)-tail exponent, material-frame tissue velocity, density gradient, and front trajectory. Fit alpha on one condition only after independently measuring those fields; then predict a held-out condition with a stiffness-gradient change. A more direct pulled/pushed discriminator would alter the initial tail steepness while independently monitoring or clamping the mechanical gradient: steep-tail speed near the measured linear spreading value supports a pulled scalar-like front; persistent front-relative speed above that value supports nonlinear/pushed mechanical contributions. Since changing the tail may also change stiffness, that intervention requires measuring the mechanical profile. The current evidence package lacks the live in-vivo tail, matched material-frame velocity, and a held-out speed condition, and it does not permit estimating the experimental cost.

## Verification and cost

I used the already-present pinned code/data and SI files in the shared workspace; no new raw dataset was downloaded and no experiment was run. Python 3 with NumPy 2.5.3, no SciPy, reproduces the saved coupled solver arrays in under one second and the reduced scalar diagnostics in under one second. The initial 201×101 fields, four ex-vivo interface traces, tracked-cell summaries, and in-vivo size CSV are the acquired inputs. No wet-lab, animal, imaging, or external-review cost was incurred. Numerical agreement is scoped to the source outputs; external biological validation and priority are not claimed.
