# Adversarial audit: molecular count cadence law

**Verdict.** For the stated ideal experiment—known homogeneous rates, unmarked integer count increments, exact endpoint counts, and no noise—the local Edgeworth calculation and exact recurrence diagnostics support a nontrivial-testing boundary at \(V^{-1/2}\). The pair-specific uniform tail estimates needed to turn the candidate proof into a theorem are not fully written in the source proof; the route below shows how to complete them. The phrase “distinguishability information \(\Theta(T/(V\Delta^2))\)” needs a metric: \(D(P_0^\mu\Vert P_1^\mu)=\infty\) at every \(\mu\) because of an exact support hole, while the reverse KL and affinity loss have the candidate polynomial scale. The coefficient is very small, so the boundary alone does not imply a useful assay. The general moment-matching extension needs explicit lattice, activity, nuisance, and readout conditions. Existing work already studies information loss across compound-Poisson sampling scales; the present fixed-horizon, increasing-volume pair is a narrow specialization whose priority is unknown.

## Experiment and exact law

The measurable process is a single cumulative molecular product count \(X(t)\). Under hypothesis \(b\), four unobserved event channels add \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) molecules at independent Poisson rates \(Vk_j^{(b)}\), with
\[
k^{(0)}=(1,1,4,0),\qquad k^{(1)}=(0,4,1,1).
\]
The instrument reads only the exact integer count at equally spaced times \(0,Δ,2Δ,\dots,T\); it records neither event times nor channel labels. Assume \(T=nΔ\), \(V\), Δ, and both rate vectors are known. Conditional on this ideal interface, increments \(Y_i=X(iΔ)-X((i-1)Δ)\) are iid and their probability generating functions are
\[
G_b(z)=\exp\!\left(\mu\sum_{j=1}^4 k_j^{(b)}(z^j-1)\right),\qquad \mu=V\Delta.
\]
Their cumulants are μ times the mark-rate moments \(M_r^{(b)}=\sum_j k_j^{(b)}j^r\). Here \(M_0=6\), \(M_1=15\), \(M_2=41\) agree and \(M_3^{(0)}=117\ne123=M_3^{(1)}\). Thus the drift and scalar quadratic variation rate match exactly, and the first difference is the third cumulant. This is a statement about the scalar count channel; it does not imply indistinguishability if another species, mark, or timestamp is measured.

Differentiating \(G_b\) gives a direct finite PMF recurrence. If \(q_{b,0}=1\),
\[
q_{b,m}=\frac{\mu}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{\min(4,m)} j k_j^{(b)}q_{b,m-j},\qquad P_b^μ(Y=m)=e^{-6\mu}q_{b,m}.
\]
The exact likelihood ratio for the observed increment vector is the product of these PMF ratios, which is the Neyman–Pearson test for the two fixed hypotheses. The shared factor \(e^{-6μ}\) cancels from each ratio.

## Exact support counterexample to symmetric-KL wording

Under model 0 one mark-1 event and no other event gives
\[
P_0^μ(Y=1)=\mu e^{-6\mu}>0.
\]
Model 1 has no mark-1 events and all other marks are at least 2, so \(P_1^\mu(Y=1)=0\). Hence \(D(P_0^\mu\Vert P_1^\mu)=\infty\) for every \(\mu\), and any claim that both directed KL divergences are \(\Theta(\mu^{-1})\) is false. \(P_1^\mu\ll P_0^\mu\), so \(D(P_1^\mu\Vert P_0^\mu)\) is finite. Both models have span one; model 1 supports 0 and every integer \(\ge2\), while model 0 supports every nonnegative integer.

The exceptional event is operationally negligible at the proposed polynomial critical cadence: if \(n/μ=O(1)\), its probability in model 0 is at most \(nμ e^{-6μ}=O(\mu^2e^{-6\mu})\to0\). Thus it breaks KL symmetry but not the limiting testing boundary. A sensor with any false-count probability removes this exact zero and must instead be represented by a calibrated observation kernel.

## Corrected ideal-model information calculation

Write \(Z=(Y-15\mu)/\sqrt{41\mu}\), and let \(H_3(z)=z^3-3z\). A span-one lattice Edgeworth expansion in a central window gives, to first order, the density-ratio perturbation
\[
\frac{P_1^μ(Y=m)}{P_0^μ(Y=m)}-1
=\frac{H_3(Z)}{41^{3/2}\sqrt{\mu}}+O\!\left(\frac{1+|Z|^{12}}{\mu}\right).
\]
The first coefficient follows from Δ\(M_3=6\) and the cubic Edgeworth factor \(M_3H_3/(6M_2^{3/2}\sqrt\mu)\). Since \(E[H_3(N)^2]=6\) for a standard normal \(N\), the local squared ratio difference is \(6/(41^3\mu)\). With normalized Hellinger convention
\[
h^2(P,Q):=1-\sum_m\sqrt{P(m)Q(m)},
\]
the candidate leading constants are
\[
h^2(P_0^μ,P_1^μ)\sim\frac{3}{4\,41^3\mu},\qquad
D(P_1^μ\Vert P_0^μ)\sim\frac{3}{41^3\mu}.
\]
For the other common convention, the full squared distance is \(\sum(\sqrt{P_0}-\sqrt{P_1})^2=2h^2\sim3/(2\,41^3\mu)\). This factor of two is recorded to prevent convention drift.

The support hole makes a central expansion alone insufficient for KL. For this particular pair, a saddlepoint check supports the reverse-KL tail control. Set \(\Lambda_b(s)=\sum_j k_j^{(b)}(e^{js}-1)\) and \(I_b(x)=\sup_s\{sx-\Lambda_b(s)\}\). A stationary point of \(F(x)=I_0(x)-2I_1(x)\) satisfies \(s_0=2s_1\), \(x=\Lambda_1'(s_1)=\Lambda_0'(2s_1)\). The last equality reduces, with \(y=e^{s_1}>0\), to
\[
y^2(12y^4-2y^2-3y-7)=0.
\]
The bracket is negative on \(0<y<1\), positive for \(y>1\), and vanishes only at \(y=1\). Thus \(F\) has only the mean saddle \(x=15\), with \(F(15)=0\); \(F(0)=-6\), and \(F(x)\to-\infty\) at infinity because the numerator's maximal mark is 4 and the denominator's is 3. Moreover, \(F''(15)=-1/41\), so near its maximum \(F(x)=-(x-15)^2/82+O(|x-15|^3)\). This rules out an exponentially dominant off-center contribution for this pair, but the candidate proof's generic Chernoff-plus-log-PMF sentence does not by itself control \(\sum P_0u^2\), whose summand is governed by \(P_1^2/P_0\), not either marginal tail alone.

A sufficient pair-specific tail split is as follows. In a central window \(|Z|\le\sqrt{K\log\mu}\), use the local lattice Edgeworth ratio and its uniform polynomial remainder. On a compact range \(m/\mu\in[a,b]\subset(0,\infty)\), exponential tilting gives a uniform local saddlepoint form \(P_b^\mu(m)=\Theta(\mu^{-1/2}e^{-\mu I_b(m/\mu)})\); the chi-square summand therefore has exponent \(F(m/\mu)\). Its unique maximum and negative curvature make the part outside the central window \(o(\mu^{-1})\) after choosing \(K\) large. For the low-count end, model 1 has total event count \(N\sim\mathrm{Pois}(6\mu)\), and \(Y\le m\) implies \(N\le m/2\); model 0 has the mark-1-only lower bound \(P_0^\mu(m)\ge e^{-6\mu}\mu^m/m!\), which is at least \(e^{-6\mu}\) for \(m\le a\mu\), \(a<1\), and large \(\mu\). Choose \(a\) so twice the lower-tail Poisson rate at \(a/2\) exceeds 6. For the high-count end, \(Y=m\) under model 1 implies \(N\ge m/4\), while model 0 has an explicit single-term lower bound from representing every \(m\ge2\) with marks 2 and 3; Stirling's bound yields \(\log(P_1^\mu(m)^2/P_0^\mu(m))\le -(m/6)\log(m/\mu)+O(m+\mu)\), summable for sufficiently large \(m/\mu\). These bounds establish finiteness of this pair's global chi-square and give the needed tail-control route, but a line-by-line uniform proof with constants is still to be supplied. For reverse KL, Chernoff tails weighted by the \(O(\mu+m\log(1+m/\mu))\) log-ratio envelope can be completed separately; that argument does not imply a chi-square bound.

Finite coefficient-recurrence calculations agree with the constants. They use a PMF cutoff more than 22 shared standard deviations past the mean, with optimized Chernoff bounds on the omitted upper tails. A separate log-domain recurrence computes \(\mu\chi^2(P_1^\mu\Vert P_0^\mu)\) as \(8.7890\times10^{-5}\) at \(\mu=32\), \(8.7321\times10^{-5}\) at 100, and \(8.7083\times10^{-5}\) at 1000, approaching \(6/41^3=8.7056\times10^{-5}\). Values are floating-point diagnostics, not interval-certified proof; the script is `chi_square_tail_audit.py`.

For \(T=1\), \(V=m^2,Δ=1/m,n=m,μ=m\) gives \(nD(P_1^μ\Vert P_0^μ)\to3/41^3=4.35281\times10^{-5}\). The finite values are \(4.53573\times10^{-5}\) at \(m=8\), \(4.44223\times10^{-5}\) at 16, \(4.39703\times10^{-5}\) at 32, and \(4.38218\times10^{-5}\) at 48.

Since one-step affinity multiplies across independent bins, the product affinity is approximately
\[
\exp\!\left(-\frac{3}{4\,41^3}\frac{n}{\mu}\right),\qquad \frac n\mu\sim\frac{T}{V\Delta^2}.
\]
Accordingly, under the ideal interface, \(T/(V\Delta^2)\to0\) makes the product total variation vanish; a positive finite limit produces a nontrivial experiment; and a diverging ratio drives affinity to zero. This yields the \(V^{-1/2}\) exponent at fixed \(T\). The support event does not change that polynomial-scale conclusion.

The limit is weak in this fixture. At \(T/(V\Delta^2)=1\), the LAN information \(I=6/41^3\approx8.706\times10^{-5}\), and equal-prior average error tends to \(\Phi(-\sqrt I/2)\approx0.4981\), barely better than chance. The Gaussian-LAN calculation requires its uniform central/tail remainder; it is not established by the finite table. An asymptotic 5% equal-prior error target needs \(I\approx10.82\), or \(T/(V\Delta^2)\approx1.24\times10^5\), corresponding to
\[
\Delta\approx0.00284\sqrt{T/V}.
\]
These constants assume exact integer counts and known rates.

## General-order claim: required conditions and counterexamples

For marks on a common span-one lattice, equal positive-order rate moments \(M_1,\dots,M_k\), common positive \(M_2\), and first difference \(\delta\) in \(M_{k+1}\), the formal local Edgeworth difference is
\[
\frac{\delta}{(k+1)!\,M_2^{(k+1)/2}}\mu^{-(k-1)/2}H_{k+1}(Z).
\]
If a weighted local Edgeworth expansion and bounded-mark Chernoff tail truncation are uniform enough, the normalized affinity loss is
\[
1-\rho_\mu\sim\frac{\delta^2}{8(k+1)!M_2^{k+1}}\mu^{-(k-1)}.
\]
The resulting product-affinity boundary is \(T/(V^{k-1}\Delta^k)\), hence Δ of order \(V^{-(k-1)/k}\) for fixed \(T\). For \(k=2\), this agrees with the pair. This is a **conditional extension**, not a completed general theorem.

The general formula uses the positive-order moments \(M_1,\dots,M_k\), which determine the central Edgeworth terms; equal total event rate \(M_0\) is not needed for this polynomial critical regime. Unequal \(M_0\) changes fixed low-count masses such as \(P(Y=0)=e^{-M_0\mu}\), but with positive rates these remain exponentially small after any polynomial number \(n=O(\mu^{k-1})\) of bins. For example, rates \(A=(1,1,1)\) and \(B=(1+3\varepsilon,1-3\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)\) on marks \(\{1,2,3\}\), with \(0<\varepsilon<1/3\), have common \(M_1=6,M_2=14\), different \(M_0=3\) versus \(3+\varepsilon\), and first difference \(\delta=6\varepsilon\) at \(M_3\). Both have the same eventual support and the same predicted \(k=2\) local cadence exponent; their zero-count probabilities differ only by an exponentially small term at the critical cadence. Unequal \(M_0\) can, however, change far-tail rate-function bounds or make a global chi-square divergence large, so it must be included in any global Rényi-tail condition.

A common lattice span/eventual common support is still needed for the stated local comparison. For example, rates \(A=(1/2,1/2)\) on marks \(\{2,4\}\) and \(B=(1/8,3/4,1/8)\) on marks \(\{1,3,5\}\) have matching \(M_0=1,M_1=3,M_2=10,M_3=36\), but \(A\)'s increments are always even, while \(B\)'s odd-increment probability is \((1-e^{-2\mu})/2\to1/2\). Their total variation therefore does not vanish even though the first three mark-rate moments match.

The generic global chi-square step also fails for bounded marks. Let \(A\) have rates \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) on \(\{1,2,3\}\). For \(0<\varepsilon<1/45\), let \(B\) have rates
\[
(1/3-10\varepsilon,\;1/3+24\varepsilon,\;1/3-15\varepsilon,\;\varepsilon)
\]
on \(\{1,2,3,7\}\). Both have \(M_0=1,M_1=2,M_2=14/3\), and \(M_3\) differs by \(120\varepsilon\). Along \(m=7r\), the numerator probability under \(B\) is at least its all-mark-7 term, of order \(e^{-C\mu}(\varepsilon\mu)^r/r!\). Under \(A\), reaching \(7r\) requires at least \(\lceil7r/3\rceil\) arrivals, so a Poisson Chernoff upper bound is of order \((C\mu/r)^{7r/3}\). Hence \(P_B(7r)^2/P_A(7r)\) grows like \(\exp((r/3)\log r+O(r))\); the global chi-square divergence is infinite for every fixed \(\mu\). A central Hermite expansion may still describe affinity, but global \(L^2(P_A)\) density-ratio arguments require a separate tail condition.

## Readout and latent-state attacks

**Parity-only sensor.** Keep the homogeneous target process but let the physical readout report only cumulative count modulo 2. The increment parity is Bernoulli with
\[
P_0(\mathrm{odd})=(1-e^{-10\mu})/2,\qquad P_1(\mathrm{odd})=(1-e^{-2\mu})/2.
\]
At the nominal \(T=1,V=m^2,Δ=1/m,n=m,μ=m\) cadence, a coupling bound gives product TV at most \(n e^{-2\mu}/2\to0\). Thus the cadence law is not instrument-independent; exact count resolution or a validated observation kernel is part of the theorem. The same conclusion follows from data processing for any coarsening that discards the relevant shape signal.

**Unobserved per-bin activity state.** Let a fresh hidden \(A_i\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(10,10)\), mean 1 and variance 0.1, multiply all channel rates within each measurement interval, independently across intervals. This is a Cox/state-nuisance countermodel, outside the homogeneous compound-Poisson premise, but it preserves iid bins. Its exact increment PGF is
\[
G_b(z)=\left[1-\frac{\mu}{10}\sum_jk_j^{(b)}(z^j-1)\right]^{-10}.
\]
Its unconditional mean is \(15\mu\) and variance \(41\mu+22.5\mu^2\), common to both mechanisms; the third-cumulant difference remains \(6\mu\). Here the normalized count \(Y/\mu\) converges to \(15A_i\), a smooth gamma law shared by both models. Expanding its characteristic function in \(1/\mu\), the first difference is order \(\mu^{-2}\), not \(\mu^{-1/2}\). For (f), the density of \(15A_i\), and \(g(y)=(y/15)f(y)\), the coefficient is controlled by
\[
J=\int_0^\infty\frac{(g'''(y))^2}{f(y)}dy=\frac8{3645}.
\]
This predicts normalized affinity loss \(J/(8\mu^4)=1/(3645\mu^4)\) and reverse KL \(J/(2\mu^4)=4/(3645\mu^4)\), giving a fixed-\(T\) boundary Δ of order \(V^{-4/5}\) in this alternative model. A Panjer-type recurrence computed one-bin TV for μ=128,256,512 as \(4.92\times10^{-7},1.24\times10^{-7},3.13\times10^{-8}\); μ²TV approaches a finite constant (0.00807, 0.00815, 0.00819), consistent with a μ⁻² distance. This mixture is an adversarial physical-state stress test, not a claim that all molecular activity resets at the read cadence. A persistent latent activity shared across the whole trajectory is a different model and has not been analyzed.

These attacks show why a physical application needs rate heterogeneity, backgrounds, state dependence, detector noise, censoring, and count saturation calibrated at the relevant cadence. Independent additive errors on endpoint readings also make differenced errors adjacent-bin dependent; the iid increment likelihood above then ceases to be the exact observed-data likelihood.

## Primary prior art and novelty boundary

Duval and Hoffmann, [*Statistical inference across time scales*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.1031), study discrete observations of a compound-Poisson random walk and prove LAN/Fisher-information transitions across microscopic, intermediate, and macroscopic scales (Theorems 2.2, 2.4, 2.5); they also discuss general compound laws in §3. Their model estimates intensity under symmetric Bernoulli jumps, so it is not the same fixed-\(T\), \(V\to\infty\), two-known-Lévy-measure testing problem. More directly, Duval's [*When is it no longer possible to estimate a compound Poisson process?*](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-EJS885) studies centered finite-variance compound-Poisson paths over \([0,T]\), with sampling interval growing as \(T\to\infty\). It proves a no-consistency lower bound when the interval grows faster than \(\sqrt T\) and establishes Gaussian/asymptotic-equivalence regimes. Under the exact time change \(s=Vt\), our fixed physical horizon becomes \(S=VT\), and the interval becomes \(d=V\Delta\); hence \(d\asymp\sqrt S\) is exactly \(\Delta\asymp\sqrt{T/V}\). The broad square-root loss-of-information boundary is prior art, even though the fixed-horizon, increasing-volume two-point test and its pair-specific coefficient are narrower. The mechanism is aggregation washing out jump-law shape as each observed increment becomes Gaussian.

Coca Cabrero, [*Efficient nonparametric inference for discretely observed compound Poisson processes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08472), published in *Probability Theory and Related Fields* (2018; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-017-0761-5)), treats discrete increments as a nonlinear decompounding inverse problem, including unknown intensity and jump law. The paper explicitly describes collisions/cancellations hiding jumps and covers fixed-cadence low-frequency inference; it distinguishes the classical high-frequency regime \(\Delta_n\to0\) with growing observation horizon. Babu, Singh, and Yang's [compound-Poisson Edgeworth work](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1024678513677) is prior art for skewness corrections in compound-Poisson statistics.

The exact rate-vector pair, support-zero correction, reverse-KL/Hellinger coefficient, and fixed-horizon increasing-volume two-point testing calculation were not located verbatim in this bounded primary-source pass. The main scaling is an application of established Edgeworth/LAN ingredients, and the broad square-root cadence-information mechanism is already prior art under an exact time reparameterization. No novelty or priority claim is warranted without a systematic search and independent proof review.

## Measurement contract, matched test, and costs

The strongest test for the stipulated endpoint-only data is the exact finite compound-Poisson likelihood ratio using the recurrence above. Its leading high-μ score is the third-Hermite statistic \(\sum_i H_3((Y_i-15\mu)/\sqrt{41\mu})\). A diffusion-only comparator receives the same exact count vector but replaces each increment by its common Gaussian drift/variance law; that comparator assigns identical distributions and cannot discriminate. It is a diagnostic baseline, not an alternative physical acquisition service. An event-resolved mark/timestamp sensor is a strictly richer interface and must be priced separately; the two mechanisms also have the same total event rate, so event times without marks alone do not separate them.

For a real acquisition, the contract requires one target-specific integer count at every endpoint, a verified baseline, known \(V,Δ,T\), no unmodeled immigration/background, and a readout whose error kernel is measured. If the sensor reports \(W_i\) through a confusion kernel \(R(w\mid y)\), use \(q_b(w)=\sum_yR(w\mid y)P_b^μ(y)\) in the likelihood; if the kernel saturates or coarsens counts, the ideal theorem does not transfer automatically. Rate vectors and calibration data are supplied by the mathematical theorem; they are not available for free in an experiment. No particular enzyme, organism, reagent, or assay was identified, and no biological protocol is proposed.

One trajectory costs \(n+1\approx T/Δ\) endpoint reads and \(O(n\log(VT))\) count-storage bits. The process generates \(6VT\) expected events and \(15VT\) expected product molecules over horizon \(T\); turnover, replenishment, vessel preparation, calibration, read latency, dynamic range, and detector energy need measured costs. At the critical scale the number of reads grows as \(\sqrt V\), while any fixed positive per-read latency eventually prevents the asymptotic cadence. The nominal critical scale also gives near-chance power because of the small coefficient; asymptotically useful discrimination needs a much larger \(T/(VΔ^2)\) ratio.

An empirical falsifier is a preregistered error curve from independently prepared replicates that fails to collapse against \(T/(VΔ^2)\) after the readout kernel, volume uncertainty, and per-bin activity variability are included. The exact fixed hypotheses are separately falsified by a calibrated endpoint histogram whose full likelihood rejects both candidate PMFs. These are prospective tests; no physical data have been acquired.
