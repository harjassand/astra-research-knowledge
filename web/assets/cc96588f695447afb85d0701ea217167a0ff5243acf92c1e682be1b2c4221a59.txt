CYCLE 2 / INDEPENDENT REVIEW — GENERAL RANK–SPECTRAL PROFILE AND REDUCIBLE KY FAN BOUND

Scope

I independently checked Section 1 of outputs/research/spectral_geometry/cycle2/RESULT.txt: vectorization, support projection, partial-trace marginal, the low-band CDF, and the layer-cake step. The irreducible-carrier theorem is correct under its stated finite-dimensional hypotheses. For reducible carriers, the exact replacement furnished by the same argument is the Ky Fan sum of the largest eigenvalues of the partial-trace projector. The stated H=1⊕W example correctly refutes QD(t)/dim(H) on reducible carriers.

1. Conventions and partial-trace calculation

Let H be a d-dimensional Hilbert space, with a fixed orthonormal basis (e_j). Use column vectorization

  vec(X) = sum_{i,j} X_{ij} e_i tensor conjugate(e_j) in H tensor conjugate(H).

Then vec is an isometry from HS(H) to H tensor conjugate(H),

  vec(A X B*) = (A tensor conjugate(B)) vec(X),

and in particular

  vec(pi(g) X pi(g)*) = (pi(g) tensor conjugate(pi(g))) vec(X).

If X has singular value decomposition X=sum_{a=1}^q s_a |u_a><v_a|, then

  vec(X)=sum_{a=1}^q s_a u_a tensor conjugate(v_a),

so its Schmidt rank is q=rank(X), and its first-factor Schmidt support is ran(X). For this convention,

  Tr_2 |vec(X)><vec(X)| = X X*.

These conventions fix which factor is traced out and why the representation on the second factor is conjugate, not pi itself.

Let L be positive self-adjoint on HS(H), and suppose it commutes with the conjugation representation. Its spectral projector Pi_t=1_[0,t](L), viewed on H tensor conjugate(H), is a positive orthogonal projector and also commutes with pi tensor conjugate(pi). Define

  Gamma_t = Tr_2(Pi_t) >= 0.

Taking the partial trace of the commutation relation shows

  pi(g) Gamma_t pi(g)* = Gamma_t.

Indeed, partial trace is invariant under conjugation by a unitary on the traced-out factor, while the first-factor pi(g) conjugation remains. Thus Gamma_t lies in the commutant of pi. Also

  Tr(Gamma_t)=Tr(Pi_t)=D(t).

2. Reducible-carrier Ky Fan theorem

Let q=min(Q,d), for an integer rank budget Q>=1. Let

  kappa_Q(t) = sum_{j=1}^q lambda_j^downarrow(Gamma_t),

where eigenvalues are listed in decreasing order with multiplicity. Then every HS-unit X with rank(X)<=Q obeys

  <vec(X), Pi_t vec(X)> <= min{1,kappa_Q(t)}.                 (A)

Proof. Let P be the orthogonal projection onto ran(X). Since vec(X) belongs to ran(P) tensor conjugate(H), its rank-one projection is dominated by the support projection:

  |vec(X)><vec(X)| <= P tensor I.

Both sides are positive, so taking trace against the positive projector Pi_t preserves the order. Hence

  <vec(X),Pi_t vec(X)>
    = Tr(|vec(X)><vec(X)| Pi_t)
    <= Tr((P tensor I)Pi_t)
    = Tr(P Gamma_t).

The Ky Fan maximum principle gives

  Tr(P Gamma_t) <= sum_{j=1}^{rank(P)} lambda_j^downarrow(Gamma_t)
                  <= kappa_Q(t).

Separately, Pi_t is an orthogonal projector and vec(X) is a unit vector, so the left side is at most one. These two bounds give (A). The Ky Fan sum is the exact maximum of Tr(P Gamma_t) over rank-at-most-Q support projections P. Thus min{1,kappa_Q(t)} is the strongest bound supplied by this support-projector/partial-trace argument. It need not equal the actual maximum low-band mass for every particular Pi_t, since replacing |vec(X)><vec(X)| by P tensor I can lose information about Schmidt coefficients and correlations.

For a reducible representation with isotypic decomposition

  H = direct_sum_alpha (V_alpha tensor M_alpha),
  pi(g) = direct_sum_alpha (pi_alpha(g) tensor I_{M_alpha}),

Schur's lemma on the commutant gives

  Gamma_t = direct_sum_alpha (I_{V_alpha} tensor B_alpha(t)),

with positive B_alpha(t) on the multiplicity spaces M_alpha. Therefore kappa_Q(t) is obtained by collecting each eigenvalue of B_alpha(t) repeated dim(V_alpha) times and summing the largest Q values. This is the explicit reducible-carrier Ky Fan profile; replacing it by Q D(t)/d has no general justification.

For irreducible pi, Schur's lemma instead forces Gamma_t=c_t I. Taking traces gives c_t=D(t)/d, and (A) reduces to

  <vec(X),Pi_t vec(X)> <= min{1, QD(t)/d},

as in (1.1). If Q>d, the top-eigenvalue sum uses q=d; after clamping by one this is still the displayed irreducible formula.

3. Layer-cake energy profile

Let psi=vec(X), and let nu_X be the spectral probability measure of L in the state psi:

  nu_X(S)=<psi,1_S(L)psi>.

For the nonnegative spectral random variable Z with law nu_X,

  <X,LX> = <psi,Lpsi> = E Z
          = integral_0^infty Pr(Z>t) dt.

Because Pi_t includes eigenvalues in [0,t], including its zero eigenspace,

  Pr(Z>t)=1-<psi,Pi_t psi>.

Applying (A) pointwise yields

  Pr(Z>t) >= 1-min{1,kappa_Q(t)}=[1-kappa_Q(t)]_+,

and therefore the strongest integrated lower profile from (A) is

  <X,LX> >= integral_0^infty [1-kappa_Q(t)]_+ dt.             (B)

The integrand is measurable and nonnegative; in finite dimension the spectral CDF is a step function and there is no integrability issue. Since Pi_t increases with t, Gamma_t increases in Loewner order, and kappa_Q(t) is nondecreasing. Restricting (B) to [0,lambda] gives the pointwise form

  <X,LX> >= lambda [1-kappa_Q(lambda)]_+, lambda>=0.          (C)

The same proof in the irreducible case gives exactly (1.2) and (1.3). Strict versus non-strict spectral tails at a threshold do not affect the layer integral; the exact identity above uses Pr(Z>t) because Pi_t=1_[0,t](L).

4. Explicit reducible counterexample to QD/d

Take G=SU(2), W its standard irreducible two-dimensional representation, and H=1 direct_sum W. More generally any nontrivial irreducible W of dimension r>1 works. For the positive conjugation Casimir, the zero eigenspace is the commutant of pi. Since 1 and W are inequivalent irreducibles, it is spanned by the orthonormal Hilbert-Schmidt vectors

  vec(P_1),  vec(P_W/sqrt(r)),

where P_1 and P_W project onto the two invariant summands. Thus D(0)=2 and

  Pi_0 = |vec(P_1)><vec(P_1)|
       + |vec(P_W/sqrt(r))><vec(P_W/sqrt(r))|.

Using Tr_2 |vec(A)><vec(A)|=AA*,

  Gamma_0 = Tr_2(Pi_0) = P_1 + P_W/r.

Its eigenvalues are 1 on the one-dimensional summand and 1/r with multiplicity r on W. In particular lambda_max(Gamma_0)=1, whereas D(0)/dim(H)=2/(r+1)<1. The rank-one HS-unit matrix X=P_1 commutes with pi, so L(X)=0, and

  <vec(P_1),Pi_0 vec(P_1)>=1.

For Q=1, the naive reducible continuation min{1,QD(0)/dim(H)}=2/(r+1) is therefore false. The Ky Fan bound gives kappa_1(0)=1 and is attained by this X. This also confirms the partial-trace normalization: Tr Gamma_0=1+r/r=2=D(0).

5. Audit verdict

All inequalities in Section 1 are valid for a finite-dimensional irreducible carrier and integer Q>=1: vectorization is an isometry and identifies rank with Schmidt rank; the range support gives |vec(X)><vec(X)|<=P tensor I; partial trace turns the support trace into Tr(P Gamma_t); irreducibility makes Gamma_t=(D(t)/d)I; the unit-vector projector bound supplies the minimum with one; and layer cake gives the integrated and pointwise energy lower bounds. No Poincare, log-Sobolev, or KLS assumption enters.

For reducible carriers the correct general statement is (A)-(C), with kappa_Q the Q-term Ky Fan sum of eigenvalues of Tr_2(Pi_t), capped at one for low-band mass. Q||Gamma_t||op is a valid but weaker corollary. The SU(2) example above refutes QD(t)/d in reducible dimension while validating the Ky Fan correction. This review is an analytic proof check, not an independent novelty/priority assessment.
