## 1. Candidate claim and the exact internal bridge

The internal result concerns a $d$-dimensional irreducible unitary $G$-carrier $H$ and a positive, self-adjoint, conjugation-invariant operator $L$ on $\operatorname{End}(H)$, with kernel $\mathbb C I$. Write its positive eigenvalues and spectral-projector dimensions as $(\lambda_j,b_j)$, with $0<\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_J$. For a matrix $A$ of rank at most $d-k$, its right kernel contains a rank-$k$ orthogonal projection $P$. Put $P_0=P-(k/d)I$ and

\[
G(P)=\langle P_0,L^{-1}P_0\rangle_{HS},\qquad
B_K(k)=\frac{k^2}{d}\sum_{j\le K}\frac{b_j}{\lambda_j}+\frac{k}{\lambda_{K+1}},
\]

with $L^{-1}$ on the traceless subspace and $\lambda_{J+1}=\infty$. Invariance and Schur's lemma give $\operatorname{Tr}_2\Pi_j=(b_j/d)I$. The normalized vectorization $\operatorname{vec}(P)/\sqrt{k}$ has Schmidt rank $k$. The Schmidt-number reduction inequality

\[
|x\rangle\langle x|\le k\,(\operatorname{Tr}_2|x\rangle\langle x|)\otimes I
\]

therefore yields $\|\Pi_jP\|_{HS}^2\le k^2b_j/d$. Also $\sum_j\|\Pi_jP_0\|_{HS}^2=\|P_0\|_{HS}^2=k-k^2/d\le k$. Splitting the inverse-$L$ sum at $K$ gives $G(P)\le B_K(k)$.

The other step is the kernel-to-energy inequality. Write $A=aI+X$, $\operatorname{Tr}X=0$. From $AP=0$, $ka=-\operatorname{Tr}(PX)=-\langle P_0,X\rangle$. Cauchy–Schwarz in the $L^{-1}$-energy inner product and the spectral gap imply

\[
\|A\|_{HS}^2\le E(A)\left(\frac{dG(P)}{k^2}+\frac1{\lambda_1}\right),\qquad E(A)=\langle A,LA\rangle_{HS}.
\]

Thus for every $K$,

\[
\inf_{\operatorname{rank}A\le d-k}\frac{E(A)}{\|A\|_{HS}^2}
\ge\left(\frac{dB_K(k)}{k^2}+\frac1{\lambda_1}\right)^{-1}.
\]

This chain does not assume $A\ge0$. Positivity enters only in the separate operational upper construction. The two ingredients that must be joined are (i) a rank-$k$ right-kernel witness, converted by vectorization to a Schmidt-rank-$k$ vector, and (ii) an invariant inverse-eigenvalue cutoff. The source comparison below identifies close antecedents for each ingredient, but I found no checked source stating their conjunction.

For $H=\operatorname{Sym}^N(\mathbb C^{s+1})$, $d=\binom{N+s}{s}$, the Casimir convention in the internal result is $\lambda_j=j(j+s)$, with

\[
b_j=\binom{j+s}{s}^2-\binom{j+s-1}{s}^2.
\]

For fixed $s\ge2$, $b_j\asymp_s j^{2s-1}$, so $b_j/\lambda_j\asymp_s j^{2s-3}$. The cutoff terms balance at $K\asymp(d/k)^{1/(2s)}$, giving $G(P)\lesssim_s k^{1+1/s}d^{-1/s}$ and the rank-$(d-k)$ lower profile $\Lambda_{N,s+1}(d-k)\gtrsim_s(k/d)^{1-1/s}$. For $s=1$, $b_j=2j+1$ and

\[
\frac{b_j}{\lambda_j}=\frac1j+\frac1{j+1};
\]

the same cutoff produces the logarithmic lower bound $\Lambda_{N,2}(d-k)\ge[3.5+\log(d/k)]^{-1}$. The internal spectral-geometry work reports matching orders from radial harmonic tapers; this does not establish exact optimizer uniqueness. These exponents are the target being compared, not a claim extracted from any one cited paper.

## 2. Closest analytic mechanism: spectral-density Green transform and mixed-state Faber–Krahn

Michel Rumin, [“Spectral density and Sobolev inequalities for pure and mixed states,” arXiv:0906.2902](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.2902), published in *GAFA* 20 (2010), 817–844, is the closest structural precedent I found. The exact pointers are Theorem 1.1 (pure states), Theorem 1.5 (mixed states), and Corollary 1.8 (global Faber–Krahn). Rumin defines the spectral density $F(\lambda)=\|\Pi_\lambda\|_{1,\infty}$ of $\Pi_\lambda=\chi_A((0,\lambda])$ and its transform

\[
G_R(\lambda)=\int_0^\lambda\frac{dF(u)}{u}.
\]

Theorems 1.1 and 1.5 convert this inverse-spectral-density transform into Sobolev-type bounds for a function $f$, or a positive density operator $\rho$ through its spatial density $D\nu_\rho(x)$ and energy $\tau(\rho^{1/2}A\rho^{1/2})$. Corollary 1.8 gives a global Faber–Krahn bound and controls the dimension of the whole low-energy subspace supported in a spatial domain.

The resemblance is substantive: for discrete spectrum, an integral against the spectral-density measure becomes a sum of spectral multiplicities weighted by $1/\lambda_j$, the same Green-type quantity that appears in the candidate. Rumin also treats mixed states and whole spectral distributions, so this is closer than a first-eigenvalue-only Faber–Krahn comparison.

The constraint and transfer step differ. Rumin starts from a positive density operator and spatial localization/support (or a diagonal density); his Theorem 1.5 does not impose that a single matrix $A$ have a right kernel of dimension $k$, does not pass through a Schmidt-rank-$k$ vectorization of a kernel projector, and does not establish the bound $\|\Pi_jP\|^2\le k^2b_j/d$. Accordingly it does not state the candidate $B_K(k)$ cutoff or its rank-codimension profile. The closest overlap is the inverse spectral-density Sobolev/Faber–Krahn mechanism; the right-kernel/Schmidt-number insertion is the bridge that remains to be compared against broader literature.

## 3. Exact projective-carrier spectrum and geometric concentration precedents

### Fuzzy \(\mathbb{CP}^{s}\) spectrum

C. Sämann, [“The Multitrace Matrix Model of Scalar Field Theory on Fuzzy $\mathbb{C}P^n$,” arXiv:1003.4683](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4683), §2.1, gives the quantization and exact representation-theoretic input. For $\mathscr H_\ell\cong\operatorname{Sym}^{\ell}(\mathbb C^{n+1})$, it decomposes $\operatorname{End}(\mathscr H_\ell)$ into irreducible $\mathrm{SU}(n+1)$ sectors with Dynkin labels $(j,0,\ldots,0,j)$; the quadratic Casimir on the $j$-sector is $2j(j+n)$, and its multiplicity is

\[
N_{n,j}^2-N_{n,j-1}^2
=\binom{j+n}{n}^2-\binom{j+n-1}{n}^2.
\]

The paper identifies this Casimir with the fuzzy $\mathbb{CP}^n$ Laplacian. This is the exact carrier/spectrum match: set $n=s$, $\ell=N$, and note that the internal result uses half this Casimir normalization, $j(j+s)$. Sämann supplies the eigenvalues and multiplicities, not a rank-$(d-k)$ constraint, an inverse-Casimir bound, or a capacity profile.

### Coherent-state Faber–Krahn/concentration

N. Nicola, M. Riccardi, and P. Tilli, [“The Wehrl-type entropy conjecture for symmetric $\mathrm{SU}(N)$ coherent states: cases of equality and stability,” arXiv:2412.10940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10940), study the symmetric carrier $\mathcal H_M=\operatorname{Sym}^M(\mathbb C^N)$, whose coherent states are rank-one projections. Their Corollary 3.3 is an explicit Faber–Krahn-type concentration statement: for a normalized degree-$M$ holomorphic polynomial and a measurable set $\Omega\subset\mathbb C^{N-1}$, its weighted mass on $\Omega$ is bounded by the coherent-state mass on the centered Euclidean ball of equal measure; equality occurs for a coherent polynomial and a corresponding geodesic ball in $\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}$. Thus it gives the natural projective-space geometry and a sharp rank-one concentration optimizer.

R. L. Frank, [“Sharp inequalities for coherent states and their optimizers,” arXiv:2210.14798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14798), Theorem 21, gives the spin-$J$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ version on the sphere: for any unit vector and measurable $E\subset\mathbb S^2$, coherent-state Husimi mass on $E$ is bounded by

\[
\frac{4\pi}{2J+1}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{|E|}{4\pi}\right)^{2J+1}\right],
\]

with equality for a coherent vector and a centered spherical cap. This is a precise fuzzy-sphere concentration/Faber–Krahn analogue, but it controls coherent-state phase-space mass, not Casimir energy of arbitrary matrices with prescribed rank defect.

### Classical capacity scaling: same exponents, different variable

The classical analogy is dimensionally strong. A small ball of radius $r$ in real dimension $2s>2$ has Newtonian capacity of order $r^{2s-2}$, while its volume is of order $r^{2s}$, so capacity as a function of volume has order $u^{1-1/s}$. In real dimension two, the relative Dirichlet (condenser) capacity of a small disk in a bounded domain is of order $1/\log(1/r)$. These are exactly the power/log forms in the internal rank profile. The match is a scaling analogy: $k/d$ is a right-nullity fraction, and the internal argument does not identify it with the volume of a removed spatial ball in $\mathbb{CP}^s$.

J. Bertrand and B. Colbois, [“Capacité et inégalité de Faber-Krahn dans l’espace euclidien,” arXiv:math/0504170](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0504170), §2, Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2, provide a precise classical capacity/spectral perturbation comparison. Their Dirichlet capacity is defined by minimizing a spatial Dirichlet energy subject to an obstacle/excision set; Theorem 2.2 controls the changes in Dirichlet eigenvalues of \(\Omega\setminus A\) by this capacity. The variable is a spatial hole and the energy is a scalar Dirichlet form. It does not constrain matrix rank or give the right-kernel Green estimate. Rumin’s spectral-density theorem above is the closer match to the inverse-spectral-sum mechanism; Bertrand–Colbois is the closer direct precedent for spatial small-hole capacity.

## 4. Rank and Schmidt-number precedents

### Schmidt-number overlap gives the local projector estimate

B. M. Terhal and P. Horodecki, [“A Schmidt number for density matrices,” arXiv:quant-ph/9911117](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9911117), establish the Schmidt-number framework and the reduction-map criterion. In particular, for a unit vector $x$ of Schmidt rank at most $k$, with $\rho_1=\operatorname{Tr}_2|x\rangle\langle x|$, the reduction-map inequality gives

\[
|x\rangle\langle x|\le k(\rho_1\otimes I).
\]

This yields $\langle x,\Pi x\rangle\le kb/d$ whenever $\Pi\ge0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_2\Pi=(b/d)I$. Applying it to $x=\operatorname{vec}(P)/\sqrt{k}$ yields the candidate’s exact local estimate $\|\Pi P\|_{HS}^2\le k^2b/d$. Thus the overlap step is standard Schmidt-number technology plus the invariant partial-trace normalization, not an isolated new inequality. This antecedent does not apply the overlap to all Casimir eigenspaces with $1/\lambda_j$ weights or derive the Green cutoff and energy result.

### Quantum-channel interface

D. Chruściński and A. Kossakowski, [“On partially entanglement breaking channels,” arXiv:quant-ph/0511244](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511244), Theorem 1, state that a channel has Choi Schmidt number at most $r$ iff it has a Kraus representation in which every Kraus matrix has rank at most $r$. This is the exact operational translation between per-Kraus matrix rank and a Schmidt-number bound, with no Kraus-label cap. It helps identify why a matrix-rank energy inequality can be relevant to channels, but supplies no Casimir/commutator-energy estimate.

G. Alagic and A. Russell, [“Uncertainty Principles for Compact Groups,” arXiv:math/0608702v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608702), Theorem 2, prove $\mu(\operatorname{supp} f)\sum_{\rho\in\widehat G}d_\rho\operatorname{rank}\widehat f(\rho)\ge1$. This is a close representation-theoretic support/Fourier-rank uncertainty principle. Its rank is distributed across Fourier coefficient blocks and paired with spatial support; it has no Casimir weighting or inverse spectral Green profile. It is a useful neighboring precedent, not an exact overlap.

As a single-gap rank-energy control, S. Bény, A. Chubb, M. J. Farrelly, and T. J. Osborne, [“Energy cost of entanglement extraction in complex quantum systems,” arXiv:1711.06658](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06658), §VI.2, optimize overlap with a maximally entangled ground vector for the special Hamiltonian $-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$. A Schmidt-rank-$K$ final vector has overlap at most $\sqrt{K/d}$, giving the exact energy cost $1-K/d$ in that flat one-projector model. This is a direct rank-versus-energy antecedent for a single spectral gap, but it has no higher Casimir spectrum, no inverse-eigenvalue sum, and no logarithmic/power capacity transition.

## 5. Synthesis and boundary

The most relevant source for the *analytic form* is Rumin: both use an inverse spectral-density/Green transform and turn it into a Faber–Krahn-type energy statement. Sämann supplies the exact fuzzy-$\mathbb{CP}^s$ spectrum and multiplicity, while Nicola–Riccardi–Tilli and Frank supply sharp coherent-state concentration laws on projective space/sphere. Classical capacity explains why the same $u^{1-1/s}$ and logarithmic exponents are geometrically natural. Terhal–Horodecki supply the Schmidt-number inequality behind the spectral-projector overlap, and Chruściński–Kossakowski identify the channel/Kraus-rank interface.

The candidate’s specific bridge, as reconstructed here, is the composition of those ingredients: choose a rank-$k$ right-kernel projection for a rank-$(d-k)$ matrix; vectorize it to a Schmidt-rank-$k$ vector; use invariant partial trace to cap each eigenspace overlap; sum these caps against the inverse Casimir spectrum and optimize a cutoff; then insert that Green estimate into the kernel-to-energy Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. None of the checked primary theorems states this entire chain or the resulting arbitrary-matrix profile. At the same time, the ingredients are close enough that this bounded comparison cannot support a novelty claim. **Priority and novelty remain UNKNOWN pending a broader search and expert review.**
