INDEPENDENT ADVERSARIAL REVIEW: FREE-PRODUCT DEFECT EXTRACTION WITH TORSION
Reviewer: algebra_blind_review. Date: 2026-10-09.
Status: COMPLETE CONDITIONAL MATHEMATICAL RECONSTRUCTION; external correctness and priority UNKNOWN.

VERDICT

The proposed repair survives independent review. Torsion-freeness is unnecessary after passing from the witness group H to G=H*Z. The decisive fact is that each nonidentity element of G moves infinitely many cosets in G/H. Consequently every nonzero finite-field block image coinduced from H is essentially free. Once the defect action is essentially free, the finite-power coloring/right-inverse construction works in every characteristic and with arbitrary torsion.

If q is a prime power, H is countable, a,b in F_q[H] satisfy ab=1 and ba!=1, and G=H*Z, then for the left Bernoulli action on X=F_q^G there is a standard p.m.p. G-system R with
  K^m ~=_G X x R,
  X ~=_G X^n x R^(n-1) for every integer n>=1,
where K=(I-BA)X and m=|SS^(-1)|+1, S=supp(1-ba).
In particular h_WP(G acting on X)=infinity. This last supremum conclusion does not require Seward's isomorphism theorem: the explicit direct Bernoulli factors X^n already have base entropy n log q. Seward's established theorem is needed only for the stronger statement that every prescribed finite entropy h>0 occurs as a direct Bernoulli factor.

The free-product enlargement is essential to this argument. An arbitrary nonzero defect action with torsion can have a nontrivial finite normal kernel, and then no finite power can factor onto X. A precise conditional counterexample is given below.

1. EXACT INTERFACE AND CONVENTIONS

Let H be a countable group and k=F_q, q>=2. Embed k[H] injectively into k[G], G=H*<t>, with <t> infinite cyclic. The same pair a,b still satisfies ab=1 and ba!=1 in k[G]. No assertion is made here that such a witness exists for any concrete H.

The compact abelian group X=k^G has product Haar measure and action
  (g.x)(h)=x(g^(-1)h).
For c=sum_s c_s s in k[G], define right convolution
  (T_c x)(h)=sum_s c_s x(hs).
Then T_c commutes with this left action and T_c T_d=T_(cd), with the usual order of composition. This is a homomorphism of group rings into continuous k-linear endomorphisms. In particular it must not be mixed with a convention that reverses multiplication.

Put A=T_a, B=T_b, p=1-ba, P=T_p. Then
  AB=I, P^2=P, AP=0, PB=0, P=I-BA,
  K=P X=ker A.
The map x -> (Ax,Px) is a continuous G-equivariant compact-group isomorphism X -> X x K, with inverse (u,kappa) -> Bu+kappa. It sends Haar measure to product Haar measure. The defect p is nonzero because ba!=1. If s0 in S=supp p and alpha=p_(s0)!=0, then P delta_(h s0)(h)=alpha. Hence each coordinate projection K -> k is onto; Haar at each individual coordinate is uniform on k.

The finite-field requirement enters here through invertibility of alpha. A general finite coefficient ring would require a different alphabet and a new right-inverse argument. The proof does not assume q=2, and signs in the residual formula remain subtraction over k.

2. STRONGER FREEDOM LEMMA FOR ALL NONZERO BLOCK IMAGES

Lemma. Let 0!=c in k[H], G=H*Z, and K_c=T_c(k^G), with Haar measure. Then G acting on K_c is essentially free. Idempotence of c, direct finiteness failure, and torsion-freeness of H are not required.

Proof of the block description. Write C=G/H for the left cosets rH, and choose one representative r_C for each coset. Coordinates in a fixed r_C H only depend on input coordinates in that same coset, since c is supported in H. Under the identification x_C(h)=x(r_C h), each block operator is exactly T_c on k^H. Thus
  K_c ~= Y^C as compact probability spaces,
  Y=T_c(k^H),
with independent Haar blocks. Y is nontrivial, and evaluation at any fixed coordinate of H is uniform on k, because one nonzero coefficient of c can be varied. In particular every point of Y has Haar mass at most 1/q. Explicitly, if g^(-1)r_C=r_(g^(-1)C) alpha_(g,C), alpha_(g,C) in H, then (g.x)_C(h)=x_(g^(-1)C)(alpha_(g,C)h). Thus the G-action permutes the cosets and applies a left H-shift to each block. Those shifts preserve Y and its Haar measure. This is precisely the coinduced action, but the proof needs only the block description.

Proof of moved cosets. H is malnormal in H*Z: H intersect vHv^(-1) is trivial for v outside H. This follows directly from reduced words. Strip initial and final H-syllables from v; the remaining word starts and ends with a nontrivial Z-syllable, so conjugating a nonidentity element of H by it leaves Z-syllables in the reduced word and cannot land in H. If g!=e fixed two distinct cosets uH,vH, then g belongs to both uHu^(-1) and vHv^(-1), violating malnormality. Thus a nonidentity g fixes at most one coset. C is infinite, since the cosets t^nH are pairwise distinct. Every g!=e therefore moves infinitely many cosets, regardless of whether g has finite or infinite order.

Proof of null fixed sets. For a fixed g!=e, choose infinitely many disjoint unordered pairs {C,g^(-1)C} of distinct cosets. This is possible by greedily excluding the finitely many vertices already used and their g-images. A point fixed by g must satisfy, for each such pair, an equality between the two independent Haar Y-blocks, up to a Haar-preserving H-shift. Conditional on one block, this specifies one point in the other block and has probability at most 1/q. Constraints on disjoint pairs are independent. Consequently the probability of satisfying the first N pair constraints is at most q^(-N); hence mu(Fix(g))=0. Countability of G gives an invariant conull Borel set K_0 of points with trivial stabilizer. This proves the lemma.

Audit note. Mixing of a nontrivial algebraic factor alone does not imply essential freeness in the presence of torsion. The moved-coset argument above repairs that exact gap. It also shows that even a finite or nonfaithful H-action on Y becomes free after this free-product coinduction.

3. FINITE-POWER EXTRACTION GIVEN ESSENTIAL FREEDOM

The following proof in fact applies to any countable group G and any nonzero c in k[G], provided K_c=T_c(k^G) is essentially free. For the application take c=p.

Let S=supp c, D=SS^(-1), d=|D|. D is finite, symmetric, and contains e. On the conull free part K_0 form the simple Borel graph with edges eta--v.eta for v in D\{e}. Its degree is at most d-1. There is a Borel proper coloring f:K_0->{1,...,d}.

The coloring can be proved directly in this special setting, so no unbundled coloring theorem is a load-bearing premise. Enumerate the coordinates of G and use finite coordinate restrictions as a countable separating family on K_0. For each eta choose the least finite initial coordinate set that distinguishes eta from every one of its finitely many graph neighbors. Label eta by this set's index and its finite coordinate word. Equal labels cannot occur on adjacent vertices, so this is a countable Borel proper coloring. Enumerate its independent color classes. In their order, color every vertex in each class with the least of d colors absent from its already colored neighbors. Each class is independent, at most d-1 colors are forbidden, and later steps avoid earlier colors. The countable construction is Borel and colors every vertex. This is the elementary bounded-degree Borel coloring argument behind KST.

Given a seed eta in K_0, define chi_eta(g)=f(g^(-1).eta). Two properties are immediate:
  gS intersect hS != empty and g!=h imply chi_eta(g)!=chi_eta(h);
  chi_(v.eta)(vg)=chi_eta(g).
For the first, g^(-1)h lies in SS^(-1), and h^(-1).eta and g^(-1).eta are distinct neighboring vertices. Freeness prevents a graph loop at this step.

Take d independent Haar reservoirs kappa=(kappa_1,...,kappa_d) in K_c and define
  F_eta(kappa)(g)=kappa_(chi_eta(g))(g).
For fixed eta this is a continuous k-linear map K_c^d -> X. It is jointly Borel in eta and kappa and satisfies
  F_(v.eta)(v.kappa)=v.F_eta(kappa).
Writing kappa_j=T_c U_j for independent product-Haar inputs U_j, all input windows gS selected from a given reservoir are disjoint, and distinct reservoirs are independent. Every selected coordinate is uniform on k. Thus, conditional on eta, F_eta(kappa) has exactly product-Haar law on X. This is full independence on every finite coordinate set, with no approximation.

Now fix s0 in S and alpha=c_(s0)!=0. Let
  w_g=alpha^(-1) T_c delta_(g s0) in K_c.
Then w_g(g)=1, and w_g(h)=0 whenever h!=g and chi_eta(h)=chi_eta(g). Indeed a nonzero value at h would make hS intersect gS. Define a right inverse L_eta:X -> K_c^d by
  [L_eta(y)]_j=sum_(g:chi_eta(g)=j) y(g) w_g.
This infinite display is a locally finite sum: at a fixed coordinate h only g=h s s0^(-1), s in S, can contribute. Equivalently, define the actual full input configuration
  u_j^(eta,y)(z)=alpha^(-1) y(z s0^(-1)) 1_[chi_eta(z s0^(-1))=j]
and take [L_eta(y)]_j=T_c u_j^(eta,y). This proves both membership in K_c and continuity in y for fixed eta, without treating an arbitrary infinite sum as an algebraic sum. The toggle identities give F_eta L_eta=I_X. Joint Borel measurability and covariance
  L_(v.eta)(v.y)=v.L_eta(y)
follow from the explicit input formula.

Define Q_eta=I-L_eta F_eta on K_c^d, and
  R={(eta,r): eta in K_0, r in K_c^d, F_eta(r)=0}.
This is a standard Borel subset of K_0 x K_c^d. Give it the concrete measure nu_R equal to the distribution of (eta,Q_eta kappa), where eta and kappa are independent Haar inputs. Conditional on eta the measure on the fiber is Haar on ker F_eta, since Q_eta is a continuous onto homomorphism to that kernel. This pushforward definition avoids needing an unstated measurability theorem for a field of Haar measures. Let G act diagonally on (eta,r); covariance makes R invariant and nu_R invariant.

The maps
  Phi(eta,kappa)=(F_eta(kappa),(eta,Q_eta kappa)),
  Phi^(-1)(y,(eta,r))=(eta,r+L_eta(y))
are inverse Borel G-equivariant bijections between K_0 x K_c^d and X x R. For fixed eta, kappa -> (F_eta(kappa),Q_eta kappa) is a continuous compact-group isomorphism
  K_c^d -> X x ker F_eta.
It therefore sends Haar to product Haar. Since the first marginal is the same Haar law on X for every eta, integration gives exactly Haar_X x nu_R. Thus
  K_c^(d+1) ~=_G X x R
as p.m.p. actions, mod null sets.

This construction checks the entire residual, not only independence from the seed. It proves no finite-bit complexity bound, no computable coloring without group and point representations, no topological conjugacy on the original full compact spaces, and no Bernoullicity of R.

4. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE DEFECT WITNESS

Apply Section 2 to c=p and Section 3 with m=d+1. Section 1 gives X ~= X x K, hence by finite iteration X ~= X x K^m. Substituting K^m ~= X x R gives X ~= X^2 x R. Repeating this last identity yields the same fixed residual R in
  X ~= X^n x R^(n-1), n>=1.
The product X^n is itself the Bernoulli shift with uniform base k^n, of Shannon entropy n log q. The weak Pinsker entropy of X is the supremum of the Shannon entropies of its direct Bernoulli factors, so these unbounded explicit entropies already prove h_WP(X)=infinity.

For a prescribed finite h>0, choose n log q>h and finite probability bases L,M with entropies h and n log q-h, respectively. Such bases exist by continuity of entropy on a sufficiently large finite simplex. Seward's Theorem 1.1 gives X^n ~= L^G x M^G. Consequently L^G is a direct Bernoulli factor of X. This uses the established equal-entropy theorem over every countably infinite group. It does not use the inverse assertion that distinct base entropies imply distinct Bernoulli actions.

The conclusion is conditional on the actual one-sided inverse witness. It does not exhibit a witness, establish that any candidate presentation has one, cancel R, prove X ~= X^2, prove R Bernoulli, or decide the original group's supremal Rokhlin entropy. It applies to the enlarged group G, not automatically to the supplied original H.

5. FINITE-NORMAL-KERNEL COUNTEREXAMPLE TO UNQUALIFIED TORSION REMOVAL

Assume the supplied witness a,b in k[H] exists. Let N be a nontrivial finite cyclic group of order ell coprime to char(k), and form Gamma=H x N. The central averaging element
  e_N=ell^(-1) sum_(u in N) u
is a nonzero idempotent in k[Gamma]. Define
  a'=(1-e_N)+a e_N,
  b'=(1-e_N)+b e_N.
Centrality and orthogonality give
  a'b'=1,
  b'a'=1-e_N+(ba)e_N,
  p'=1-b'a'=(1-ba)e_N!=0.
Nonzero follows from the direct product basis: every nonzero coefficient of 1-ba is repeated with coefficient multiplied by the nonzero scalar ell^(-1).

For u in N, u e_N=e_N. The right-convolution image K'=T_(p')(k^Gamma) consists of configurations constant along the N coordinates. Since N is central, its left shift on every such configuration is also trivial. Thus N fixes K' pointwise and also fixes every finite power K'^r pointwise.

The ordinary Bernoulli shift k^Gamma has a null u-fixed set for each u!=e in N: there are infinitely many disjoint finite u-orbits, and independent uniform coordinates must agree on each orbit. Any equivariant map K'^r -> k^Gamma would have image contained in that null fixed set, so it cannot be measure preserving. In particular K'^r cannot split off k^Gamma for any r. This is an actual defect construction conditional only on the same assumed witness, not an unrelated projection counterexample.

An unconditional illustration already exists without a defect pair: over F_2[C_3 x Z], e=1+u+u^2 is a nonzero idempotent, and its image is pointwise C_3-fixed. It is also a mixing factor for the infinite group, illustrating exactly why mixing cannot discard a finite normal kernel.

Finite-group trap: if H itself were finite, k[H] would be finite dimensional over k. A one-sided inverse in a finite-dimensional algebra is two-sided, so ab=1, ba!=1 cannot occur. Essential freeness of arbitrary finite-group defect images must therefore not be claimed from a nonexistent finite-group witness. The nonzero-block freedom lemma remains valid for finite H after enlarging to H*Z.
