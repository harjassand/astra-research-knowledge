INPUT AND MATRICES
q_k=exp[-delta_bar(2k-N)^2/(4N)]/binom(N,k), 0<=k<=N.
The exact identity q_k=q_(N-k) holds because both factors are palindromic.
H0=(q_(i+j))_{0<=i,j<=floor(N/2)};
H1=(q_(i+j+1))_{0<=i,j<=floor((N-1)/2)}.
The compact homogeneous SOS/Bernstein cone argument is unchanged from
AXIAL_ONE_CERTIFICATE.txt; no infinite moment extension is assumed.

ODD N
For N=2n+1 both matrices have dimension n+1. If J reverses coordinates,
(J H0 J)_(i,j)=q_(2n-i-j)=q_(i+j+1)=H1_(i,j).
Thus one strict H0 certificate implies strict H1 by an exact invertible
permutation congruence. This is a theorem, not an approximate symmetry check.

EVEN N
For N=2n, H0 has dimension n+1 and H1 dimension n. Each is centrosymmetric:
J H J=H, since its index under reversal is N minus its original index.
For a centrosymmetric m by m real matrix H, let h=floor(m/2). Use the basis
p_i=e_i+e_(m-1-i), a_i=e_i-e_(m-1-i), 0<=i<h; when m is odd append p_h=e_h.
The basis matrix is invertible (its determinant is a nonzero power of2).
Plus/minus cross terms vanish since J p=p and J a=-a. Its congruence is
block diagonal with exact entries
P_ij=2(H_ij+H_(i,m-1-j)), A_ij=2(H_ij-H_(i,m-1-j)) for i,j<h;
when m is odd, P_(i,h)=P_(h,i)=2H_(i,h), P_(h,h)=H_(h,h).
Consequently H is positive definite iff both P and A are positive definite;
a zero-dimensional block is vacuous. No square roots, numerical eigenspaces,
approximate diagonalization or assumption about pivot magnitudes is used.
