## 1. Interface and the one descent lemma

Fix a finite field F of size q. All tensors, restrictions, direct sums, tensor
products, and characters below are over F. A character is a normalized,
nonnegative, additive, multiplicative, restriction-monotone map. A restriction
is a coefficient identity, not equality merely as functions on a finite set.

Let E/F be a finite extension of degree k, and let ell:E -> F be an F-linear
map with ell(1)=1. Such a map exists by choosing a basis containing 1. For
three E-valued F-linear forms U,V,W, write their coordinate expansions in an
F-basis e_1,...,e_k. Then

    ell(U V W) = sum_(i,j,h) ell(e_i e_j e_h) U_i V_j W_h.

Consequently, if an E-linear restriction of the base extension A_E has all
its final coefficients in F, its result is an F-linear restriction of at
most k^3 full copies of A. Each summand uses a WHOLE A copy: its maps on
each leg are the coordinate forms of the respective E-valued substitution,
and the scalar ell(e_i e_j e_h) is absorbed into one leg. The coefficients
of A lie in F and therefore pass through ell. The statement also holds
coefficientwise for Laurent-polynomial substitutions in a formal parameter.

The projection is not treated as a field homomorphism. The displayed cubic
expansion is essential. Merely applying ell separately to U,V,W would be
false.

## 2. Finite-field separator with a charged overhead

Suppose A=sum_(h=1)^M A_h has a common first leg and matched, disjoint
second- and third-leg sectors. Put

    k = min {j>=1 : q^j >= 3M+1},     L=q^k-1.

Choose E=F_(q^k) and a primitive L-th root zeta in E. The integer L is
nonzero in E, and L>3(M-1). In L source copies indexed by r=0,...,L-1 use
the substitutions

    x_a^(r) -> sum_g zeta^(2rg) X_(a,g),
    y_(h,b)^(r) -> sum_u zeta^(r(u-h)) Y_(h,b,u),
    z_(h,c)^(r) -> L^-1 sum_v zeta^(r(-v-h)) Z_(h,c,v),

where g,u,v range from 1 to M. A resulting coefficient is multiplied by
L^-1 sum_r zeta^(r e), with e=u-v+2(g-h). Since |e|<=3(M-1)<L, this is
exactly the indicator e=0, in every characteristic. The resulting unweighted
tensor has coefficients in F.

Apply the integer target-variable weights

    w(X_(a,g))=g^2,
    w(Y_(h,b,u))=hu-h^2,
    w(Z_(h,c,v))=-hv.

Every surviving coefficient has total weight (g-h)^2. Its minimum-weight
part is precisely

    D = direct_sum_(h=1)^M (A_h tensor B_X(M)).

Use the descent lemma on each source copy. It gives the legal base-field
degeneration

    A^(direct_sum C_q(M))  ->deg  D,      C_q(M)=L k^3.

In particular C_q(M)<q(3M+1)k^3=O_q(M(1+log M)^3). The mismatch degree is
at most (M-1)^2. The output has M full direct-sum blocks AND a dot-product
factor of dimension M in each block; these two multiplicities are retained.

## 3. Degeneration monotonicity without infinitely many base-field points

Fix a degeneration witness F(t)=B+tF_1+...+t^D F_D, where each nonzero
specialization is a restriction of A. Its D is fixed before the next limit.
For tensor power j, interpolate the constant coefficient of F(t)^tensor j
at jD+1 distinct nonzero elements of an extension E_j/F. Choose degree
k_j with q^k_j-1>=jD+1; thus k_j=O_D(log(j+2)). Absorb interpolation
scalars into one leg and apply ell_j with ell_j(1)=1. The preceding descent
lemma supplies an exact F-restriction

    A^(tensor j, direct_sum (jD+1)k_j^3)  >=  B^(tensor j).

For every F-character lambda,

    lambda(B)^j <= (jD+1)k_j^3 lambda(A)^j.

The overhead has j-th root tending to 1, so lambda(B)<=lambda(A).
No continuity of lambda is used. This repairs the source interpolation
lemma for a finite base field; choosing evaluation points in F alone fails
once jD+1 exceeds q-1. For D=0 one can simply use the exact restriction.

A separate coefficient-extraction repair is also possible: after tensor
power j, each of three leg forms has O(j) possible Laurent exponents; for
a prescribed total degree the third exponent is determined by the first
two. This gives a polynomial copy overhead directly over F. The extension
interpolation proof above is sufficient and does not depend on that route.

## 4. The entropy inequality survives

For rational q_i with integral Nq_i, retain words of that type in a tensor
power T^tensor N. Their number is M=N!/prod_i(Nq_i)!, and every sector has
character value K_N=prod_i lambda(T_i)^(Nq_i). Sections 2-3 give

    C_q(M) lambda(T)^N >= M^(1+p_X) K_N.

After dividing by M, the extra factor C_q(M)/M is O_q((1+log M)^3).
Its N-th root tends to 1: log M=O(N), including zero-entropy types.
Stirling's formula and rational approximation therefore give the exact
same inequality

    lambda(T) >= exp(p_X H(q)) prod_i lambda(T_i)^q_i.

It is not legitimate to discard the extension-degree factor before this
limit, but its complete charge is subexponential and it vanishes here.

## 5. The remaining field-dependent rank bound

For convolution C(a,b), put m=a+b-1 and choose E/F with at least m distinct
evaluation points. Over E, evaluation and interpolation give rank at most
m. Project the resulting coefficient identity back to F as in section 1:

    rank_F C(a,b) <= m k^3,
    k = min {j>=1 : q^j >= m} = O_q(1+log m).

For m=1 the same bound uses k=1. Output flattening still has rank m over F;
we do not assert exact rank m over a small finite field. The source's
profile needs only a character bound, and a stronger repair is available.
Fix a,b and this ONE extension E before taking tensor powers. Interpolate
over E to obtain rank_E(C(a,b)^tensor j)<=m^j. Project the WHOLE expression
back to F once: its coefficient forms still take values in E, whose degree
k has not changed with j. Thus

    rank_F(C(a,b)^tensor j) <= k^3 m^j,
    lambda_F(C(a,b))^j <= k^3 m^j.

Taking j-th roots gives the EXACT bound lambda_F(C(a,b))<=m for every
character. Root independently identified this sharpening of our first
polylogarithmic repair. The symmetrized profile consequently satisfies

    P(a,b) <= (a+b-1)^(1/t).

Its boundary P(1,b)=b remains exact because C(1,b) is a dot product.

The determinant filtration has fixed integer coefficient maps and monomial
bases. Its multiplication-compatibility proof uses polynomial rings over
a field; it needs neither division by 2 nor semisimplicity of an SL_2
representation. The three-sector construction likewise uses only integer
weights, deletion of coordinates, translation of indices and a basis
reversal. Hence both apply in characteristic 2 as well. Section 4 supplies
the same discrete concavity and shifted-tripling profile inequalities.

The purely real diagonal-growth lemma still gives P(a,a)>=a^(4/3).
Comparison with the new upper bound, followed by a->infinity, gives

    4/3 <= 1/t,         p_X+p_Y+p_Z=3t <= 9/4.

Even the coarser polylogarithmic repair would give the same exponent, but
the fixed-extension/tensor-power argument retains the exact original
character inequality. It does not assert exact finite tensor rank m over F.

## 6. Detection and the arithmetic consequence

Appendix A's detecting-character proof uses the ordered semiring of finite
tensors modulo mutual restriction, finite rank, the scalar restriction
A>=1 for A!=0, compact normalized REAL additive states, rational finite
separation, and a compact convex fixed-point theorem. These ingredients
are available for tensors over F. Its catalytic-rank contradiction uses
only exact ranks of the matrix tensors, tensor products, scalar copies
and padding of tensor rank; it does not need complex coefficients or an
analytic tensor orbit. Thus it yields an F-character detecting each
integer strictly below d^nu_F, with nu_F=inf_(n>=2) log_n rank_F(T_n).

The universal character bound from section 5 now gives nu_F<=9/4. A fixed
near-optimal rank decomposition is selected after fixing exponent slack.
