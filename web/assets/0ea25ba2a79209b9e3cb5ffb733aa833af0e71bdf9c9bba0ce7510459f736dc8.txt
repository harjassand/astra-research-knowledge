# A non-Clifford common frame escapes the exact-row obstruction for the repeated seed pair

Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact construction and proof for a two-clause repeated BJSW seed pair; not a construction for the complete BMVZ/QMA-hard multi-clause family. The construction changes the pair-level conclusion for two-sided operator-order comparison. It does not imply a Clifford or full-family sparse-encoding theorem.

## Claim and precise scope

Let the five-qubit stabilizer vectors be

```text
phi = (|00010> - |10010>)/sqrt(2)
psi = (|00010> - |01110> - |10110> - |11010>)/2.
```

They are among the Clifford-projector seeds in the BJSW 5-local reduction, with the common clock suffix `|10>`; their overlap is

```text
c = <phi,psi> = 1/(2 sqrt(2)).
```

For every repetition length `T>=1`, define the two OR-amplified projections and their normalized noncommuting average on `T` five-qubit blocks:

```text
P=|phi><phi|, R=|psi><psi|,
h_phi,T = I - (I-P)^{tensor T},
h_psi,T = I - (I-R)^{tensor T},
H_T = (h_phi,T + h_psi,T)/2.
```

Put `q=(1-c)/2=(4-sqrt(2))/8`. There is one explicit common isometry `V_T`, a positive 1-row-sparse contraction `B_T`, and a positive 1-row-sparse contraction `C_T` such that

```text
ker C_T = ran V_T,
C_T >= I - V_T V_T*,
q H_T <= V_T* B_T V_T <= q^(-1) H_T.
```

The comparison constants are independent of `T`; the compressed test is generally not equal to `H_T` when `T>=2`. This is a constructive escape for the full two-term repeated-seed pair, including both noncommuting amplified terms. It does not encode the additional clauses of the BMVZ hard family.

## Proof of the logical operator comparison

Let

```text
Q=(P+R)/2,  L=I-Q=((I-P)+(I-R))/2,
E_T=I-L^{tensor T}.
```

Write `S=span{phi,psi}` and `W=S^perp`. The eigenvalues of `Q` are `(1+c)/2`, `(1-c)/2`, and `0` (multiplicities `1,1,30`). Thus the eigenvalues of `L` are `q`, `1-q`, and `1` (multiplicities `1,1,30`). In particular, for `K=W^{tensor T}`,

```text
q P_(K^perp) <= E_T <= P_(K^perp).                 (1)
```

Indeed, `L^{tensor T}` has eigenvalue one exactly on `K`; every other product eigenvalue is at most `1-q`.

For the other operator, set `M_phi=(I-P)^{tensor T}` and `M_psi=(I-R)^{tensor T}`. These are projections with common range `K`, and

```text
H_T = I - (M_phi+M_psi)/2.
```

The one-block subspaces `phi^perp` and `psi^perp` have intersection `W`; after quotienting by that intersection, their only principal cosine is `c`. Tensoring the principal-angle decomposition shows that the largest principal cosine between `ran M_phi` and `ran M_psi` outside their common range is still `c` (the other nontrivial cosines are `c^r`, `r>=2`). On each principal-angle two-plane the average of the two complementary projections has eigenvalues `(1-cos(theta))/2` and `(1+cos(theta))/2`. It follows that

```text
q P_(K^perp) <= H_T <= P_(K^perp).                 (2)
```

Combining (1) and (2) gives `E_T >= q P_(K^perp) >= q H_T` and `E_T <= P_(K^perp) <= q^(-1) H_T`, proving the claimed order comparison. This also identifies the shared kernel exactly; no additive-error argument is being used.

The order comparison is genuinely weaker than equality. For `T=2`, direct expansion gives

```text
E_2-H_2 = (1/4) ((I-P)-(I-R)) tensor ((I-P)-(I-R)) != 0.
```

For these seeds its operator norm is `7/32`. At `T=1`, `E_1=H_1=Q`.

## Common frame, sparse lifts, and access costs

Choose a fixed exact algebraic unitary `S_0` whose columns are

```text
chi_plus  = (phi+psi)/sqrt(2+2c),
chi_minus = (phi-psi)/sqrt(2-2c),
```

followed by any exact orthonormal algebraic basis of `W`. Then

```text
S_0* L S_0 = D_0 = diag(q,1-q,1,...,1),
S_T = S_0^{tensor T},
D_T = I - D_0^{tensor T}.
```

On the physical data register plus one ancilla qubit, set

```text
V_T |xi> = S_T |xi> tensor |0>,
B_T = D_T tensor I_anc,
C_T = I_data tensor |1><1|_anc.
```

Because `S_T` is unitary onto the data register, `ran V_T = data tensor |0>`, so `C_T=I-V_T V_T*`, `ker C_T=ran V_T`, and its all-state gap is exactly one. `B_T` is diagonal with entries `1-product_i lambda_i`, where each `lambda_i` is `q`, `1-q`, or `1`; hence it is a positive contraction with at most one nonzero per row. Its compression is exactly

```text
V_T* B_T V_T = S_T* D_T S_T = I - L^{tensor T} = E_T.
```

The common isometry uses one fixed five-qubit algebraic unitary in parallel on each block and one ancilla: constant depth and `O(T)` gates in an exact algebraic-gate model. `q=(4-sqrt(2))/8` and `1-q=(4+sqrt(2))/8`; an entry of `B_T` is computed by `T` multiplications in `Q(sqrt(2))`, with coefficient bit length `O(T)` and polynomial bit cost. `C_T` and `B_T` each have maximum row support one; the row/entry algorithms are exact.

Resource charges and caveats:

- `B_T` is a global diagonal observable on all `5T` data qubits, not a constant-local Hamiltonian. Its entry oracle scans all `T` local labels. The compact product formula does not supply a bounded-local positive-term decomposition; expanding `I-L^{tensor T}` in local pieces is not a constant-local sparse-PCP representation.
- `V_T` is non-Clifford. Exact claims assume the fixed algebraic `S_0` is an allowed gate (or exact matrix primitive). Replacing it by an approximate Clifford+T synthesis does not automatically preserve the exact shared kernel or the two-sided order inequality; a kernel-preserving approximation argument would be an extra requirement.
- No exponentially large row list is hidden: both lifted operators are diagonal in the stated output basis. The nontrivial cost is the non-Clifford local frame and the `5T`-local diagonal predicate.

## What Pauli/symplectic structure does and does not say

This construction explains why the repeated-pair exact-row lower bound for the bare two-branch Clifford interpolation is not a lower bound for arbitrary common frames or for order comparison. We do not interpolate between the two Clifford bases. Instead, we diagonalize their local average `Q`, then represent the positive tensor-product envelope `E_T`. Its kernel and constant spectral floor agree with those of the endpoint average `H_T`, which is all the two-sided comparison requires.

The local diagonalizer `S_0` cannot be Clifford. If a Clifford unitary diagonalized `Q`, its unique eigenvectors `chi_plus` and `chi_minus` would be stabilizer states, since Clifford images of computational basis vectors are stabilizer states. But

```text
|<phi,chi_plus>|^2  = (1+c)/2,
|<phi,chi_minus>|^2 = (1-c)/2,
```

are irrational, whereas the squared overlap of two stabilizer states is either zero or a dyadic rational `2^(-r)`. This is a direct Pauli-frame obstruction to this *local spectral-diagonalization route* using a Clifford frame. It is not a no-go theorem for arbitrary stabilizer codes, non-diagonal sparse `B`, extra workspace, or other common isometries: a general Clifford-code argument would have to control those alternatives too. The CSS check matrices alone only specify the code syndrome; they do not diagonalize the noncommuting average `Q`.

In Pauli language, Clifford conjugation permutes Pauli labels by a binary symplectic map. It cannot turn the unique non-stabilizer eigenspaces of `Q` into computational-basis eigenspaces. A non-Clifford constant-size local gate escapes that obstruction. For a general multi-clause Hamiltonian, however, there need not be one two-projector local average whose eigenbasis factors over all repeated blocks. Thus this is a targeted escape, not a solution to the global common-frame problem.

## Boundary against the full amplified hard family

BMVZ's primary construction defines each amplified clause as `I - tensor_j(I-Pi_f(j))` and its Hamiltonian as a weighted average of such clauses. For a singleton commuting layer, the source says to handle the layer by trivially repeating its clause, so the pair above is exactly the two singleton-color subfamily with seed weights `1/2,1/2`. BJSW supplies the two noncommuting Clifford-projector seeds used here.

For the full BMVZ output, the Hamiltonian is a polynomially weighted sum over many different clauses, generally acting on overlapping blocks. The construction above does not supply a single tensor-factorized `L` for all those clauses. Adding other clauses to `H_T` can change its kernel, and an order comparison on the seed-pair subspace is not an order comparison for the full sum. Therefore the requested full QMA-hard `V,C,B` bridge remains unresolved. This does not contradict BMVZ's discussion: their theorem gives polynomially many `O(n)`-fold terms that are easy to measure, while the paper explicitly leaves sparse-matrix conversion via the tensor structure open.

Nor does this say anything about the N11/L02 commuting-strategy threshold repetition theorem: that is a statement about game value tails in the commuting-operator model, not a common positive sparse Hamiltonian encoding. The pair construction is finite-dimensional and operator-theoretic; it creates no verifier-level consequence.

## Falsifiers / next gate

The pair theorem can be falsified by an exact finite check finding a vector in `K^perp` for which either `E_T < q H_T` or `E_T > q^(-1)H_T`, or by a failure of the displayed compression/kernel identities. For an extension to the full hard family, the next concrete gate is to construct one factorized, efficiently diagonalizable positive envelope with the same kernel and constant spectral floor as the *entire* weighted amplified Hamiltonian, then implement its eigenbasis with an exact common isometry while preserving polynomial row access. The present pair proof supplies no such envelope when the clause supports overlap and the local seeds vary.
