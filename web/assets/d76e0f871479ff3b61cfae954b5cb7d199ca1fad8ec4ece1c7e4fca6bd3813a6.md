# Cell-state controllability and safe partial reprogramming

## Result and status

This lane produces a conditional, exactly solved pulse-control model and a falsifiable experiment for testing it. It does **not** establish that a cell system follows the model, that a particular partial-reprogramming schedule is safe, or that an intervention is therapeutic.

The load-bearing distinction is between (i) structural or deterministic controllability of an idealized regulatory network and (ii) bounded-dose, stochastic, heterogeneous *reach–avoid* control: reach a desired regenerative/rejuvenated state while avoiding cell death, loss of lineage identity, sustained pluripotency, or tumor formation. Existing structural results do not provide the latter guarantee. The biological sources also show why a schedule indexed only by nominal doxycycline days will not transport automatically across tissues, routes, or transgene systems.

The mathematical core is a three-state continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC). Its optimal fixed-dose pulse has an explicit closed form. Multiplying all state-transition rates by a common factor leaves the embedded transition graph unchanged but divides the optimal pulse duration by that factor. Thus graph structure cannot determine intervention time without kinetic calibration. The formula is standard competing-risk/CTMC mathematics; novelty is unclaimed.

## Decision problem and exact interface

For one fixed tissue, injury context, delivery route, expression cassette, and calibrated induction level, coarse-grain each tracked cell into:

- `S`: viable starting cell, with the required original lineage identity;
- `R`: viable desired regenerative or rejuvenated endpoint, with identity retained at the prespecified endpoint;
- `U`: unacceptable endpoint, absorbing for this model. It may combine apoptosis, identity loss, sustained pluripotency, tumor-associated transformation, or other prespecified harm.

During induction, assume constant transition rates

\[
S\xrightarrow{a}R,\qquad S\xrightarrow{c}U,\qquad R\xrightarrow{b}U,
\]

with \(a,b,c>0\). The intervention stops at time \(T\). The target event is being in `R` at stop time; the safety event is ever entering `U` by stop time. This is a **coarse-grained, fixed-context model**, not a proposed universal cell-state ontology. Rates must be estimated from lineage-resolved trajectories under the actual protocol. If `R` is not directly measurable, if rates depend on elapsed state age, or if cell-to-cell variation is material, the three-state homogeneous CTMC is misspecified and must be extended.

For cardiac regeneration, one possible operationalization is adult lineage-traced cardiomyocytes after a fixed myocardial-injury context. `R` must require completed cytokinesis and retained cardiomyocyte lineage/function criteria; EdU or S-phase entry alone is not a regeneration endpoint. For partial rejuvenation, `R` must jointly satisfy a prespecified rejuvenation measure and retained cell identity. These applications are distinct and should not share rate estimates.

The actuator variable should be measured factor-protein exposure/trajectory, not treated as the number of days that doxycycline was supplied. A separate hypothesis that different delivery protocols collapse after matching measured protein exposure must itself be tested.

## Exact pulse lemma

Let \(p_S(t),p_R(t),p_U(t)\) denote state probabilities, with \(p_S(0)=1\), \(p_R(0)=p_U(0)=0\), and let \(r=a+c\). The forward equations are

\[
\dot p_S=-r p_S,\qquad \dot p_R=a p_S-bp_R,\qquad
\dot p_U=c p_S+b p_R.
\]

Solving the first equation gives \(p_S(t)=e^{-rt}\). Variation of constants in the second gives, when \(r\ne b\),

\[
p_R(T)=a\int_0^T e^{-rs}e^{-b(T-s)}\,ds
=\frac{a(e^{-bT}-e^{-rT})}{r-b}.
\]

For \(r=b\), the continuous limit is \(p_R(T)=aT e^{-bT}\). Differentiating the integral form yields

\[
p_R'(T)=\frac{a}{r-b}\left(r e^{-rT}-b e^{-bT}\right).
\]

For \(r\ne b\), this derivative has exactly one positive zero, since its sign changes once, at

\[
T_* = \frac{\log(r/b)}{r-b};
\]
for \(r=b\), the unique maximizer is \(T_*=1/b\). Hence the endpoint probability `R` is unimodal and the fixed-dose unconstrained best pulse is exact. For \(r\ne b\),

\[
p_R(T_*)=\frac{a}{r}\left(\frac{b}{r}\right)^{b/(r-b)},
\]
with limit \(a/(eb)\) when \(r=b\).

Also \(p_U'(T)=c p_S(T)+b p_R(T)\ge0\), and conservation gives

\[
p_U(T)=1-e^{-rT}-p_R(T).
\]
For any prescribed research risk cap \(0<\delta<1\), define \(T_\delta\) as the unique solution of \(p_U(T_\delta)=\delta\). Since feasibility is the initial interval \([0,T_\delta]\), the risk-constrained fixed-dose optimum is

\[
T_{\rm safe}=\min\{T_*,T_\delta\}.
\]

The statement assumes a known, constant dose-specific rate triple and a single homogeneous initial state. It does not account for post-induction persistence, reversal, dose-dependent rates within the pulse, latent subpopulations, or external injury cues. Each changes the state model and requires its own rates.

### Exact structural obstruction

If all three rates are multiplied by \(\gamma>0\), then \(p_R^{(\gamma)}(T)=p_R(\gamma T)\) and \(p_U^{(\gamma)}(T)=p_U(\gamma T)\). The embedded jump-chain probabilities and state-transition graph stay the same, while

\[
T_*^{(\gamma)}=T_*/\gamma,\qquad T_\delta^{(\gamma)}=T_\delta/\gamma.
\]

Therefore, for any proposed finite duration inferred from graph structure alone, a rate-rescaled system with the same graph has a different optimal duration. The obstruction is exact but elementary; it is not a new nonlinear-control theorem. It rules out transferring a topology-only driver-node score into a quantitative pulse prescription without measuring kinetics.

## Executable intervention-design algorithm

The fixed-dose algorithm is:

1. **Freeze the context and endpoints.** Specify tissue/cell type, injury or regeneration cue, age/condition, factor set, vector/induction route, lineage criterion, target event, unacceptable event, observation horizon, and risk tolerance before the screen. Do not pool contexts whose kinetics differ.
2. **Calibrate actual factor exposure.** At a dose grid, measure intracellular OSK/OSKM protein over time, including washout lag. Record administered inducer and vector costs separately. Use the measured exposure to label each dose condition.
3. **Track same-cell transitions.** Follow lineage-marked cells under several pulse lengths and an off/chase period. For each interval, measure transition timing and the jointly defined `R` and `U` endpoints. For heart repair, add completed-division and functional readouts; for rejuvenation, jointly measure age-associated and identity-associated outputs.
4. **Estimate rates by stratum.** Under the fixed-dose CTMC, estimate \(a,b,c\) from transition counts divided by cell-time at risk. Keep animal/donor as the sampling unit or fit random effects; do not treat thousands of cells from one animal as thousands of independent biological replicates. Check exponential holding times, time-homogeneous rates, and within-stratum heterogeneity. If rejected, use a semi-Markov or multi-state model and solve its reach–avoid dynamic program instead of applying the closed form.
5. **Optimize robustly.** For each dose, compute \(T_*\) and solve \(p_U(T)=\delta\). With rate uncertainty, optimize the worst-case lower confidence bound on \(p_R(T)\), subject to the upper confidence bound on \(p_U(T)\le\delta\), under the exposure/material budget. If the feasible set is empty or the confidence set is too wide, return `UNKNOWN / no certified schedule`.
6. **Freeze and validate.** Compare the selected pulse on held-out animals/donors/lineages against the strongest equally informed open-loop control: the best pulse/dose schedule selected on training data at matched measured factor exposure and total delivery cost. Also include vehicle/injury-only and a prespecified conventional schedule as contextual controls. Report all animal-level outcomes and adverse states.

For a dose grid of size \(D\), the fixed-dose closed-form evaluation is \(O(D)\) once rates are estimated; dose fitting, cell tracking, protein assays, lineage tracing, and sequencing dominate computation. In a multi-state or partially observed extension, a discretized dynamic program costs with its state grid, time horizon, and action grid; discretization and rate uncertainty must be reported. No dataset or acquisition budget was supplied, so a sample-size claim is `UNKNOWN` and must be set by a pilot/power calculation.

## Quantitative predictions and falsifiers

**Within-context pulse peak.** If the homogeneous CTMC is an adequate description at a fixed measured exposure level, the fraction of cells in the safe target state at pulse stop should peak at the estimated \(T_*\), and the cumulative unsafe fraction should increase monotonically with pulse length. Use training lineages to estimate rates; test the formula on schedules withheld from fitting. Failure of the predicted peak or calibrated uncertainty coverage falsifies this model for that context. It does not show that no controller exists.

**Kinetic time-rescaling.** If an intervention variant changes every transition rate by a measured common factor \(\gamma\) while preserving their ratios, its predicted optimal pulse time shifts by \(1/\gamma\), and maximum target probability is unchanged. A route or cassette variant can test this only if injury, cell type, factor identity, and endpoint definitions are held fixed and rate-ratio preservation is checked. Failure rejects the common-scaling hypothesis.

**Equal-exposure control.** Compare against the best training-selected open-loop schedule at the same measured intracellular factor exposure and material/delivery cost. A schedule should only be claimed better if held-out `R` probability improves while the prespecified `U` risk bound holds. Merely beating continuous overexpression or an unoptimized historical schedule is not decisive.

The experiment's measurements and use of cell-level tracks, animal/donor replicates, sequencing, imaging, and vector/inducer resources are part of the cost. A biomarker panel, inferred pseudotime, or RNA velocity alone does not establish individual-cell reachability, completed regeneration, or long-term safety.

## Literature reconstruction and prior-art boundary

The primary sources justify treating tissue and time as model variables, not asserting a universal window:

- Ocampo et al. reported cyclic 2-day doxycycline induction followed by 5-day withdrawal in a progeroid LAKI mouse system. Their result is tied to that inducible OSKM model and must not be imported as a cardiomyocyte or human schedule: [Cell 2016, PMID 27984723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27984723/).
- Chen et al. showed transient, cardiomyocyte-specific OSKM could induce a fetal-like program and support cardiac repair, while extended expression led to reprogramming/tumor formation: [Science 2021, PMID 34554778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34554778/).
- Cheng et al. reported a different adult-cardiomyocyte time course: proliferation peaked around induction day 2 and six days of their Dox protocol were lethal. They explicitly discuss different administration route and timeline relative to Chen et al.: [Circulation Research 2022, PMID 36420731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36420731/). This protocol difference is direct evidence against transferring nominal `Dox-days` without exposure/kinetic measurement.
- Yan et al. (2026) report OSK (without c-Myc) primed cardiomyocytes by dedifferentiation and sarcomere disassembly, while OSK alone did not directly trigger in-vitro cell-cycle entry; in vivo effects were framed around overcoming cytokinesis barriers and post-MI repair. This motivates conditioning the model on injury and not using S-phase entry as the target: [JMCC 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.yjmcc.2026.04.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yjmcc.2026.04.005).
- Roux et al. used single-cell assays and found transient reprogramming suppressed somatic identity programs as youthful expression was restored; their factor screen reports that the effects on age and identity were not tightly entangled. The study used 3-day pulse/3-day chase in specified mouse-derived cell types; it supports joint target/safety observables, not a general risk estimate: [Cell Systems 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.cels.2022.05.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2022.05.002).
- Synthetic genetic circuits have used live-cell tracking and feedback to enforce OCT4 trajectories associated with successful human fibroblast reprogramming to pluripotency. This is relevant control prior art, but its target is iPSC colony formation, not rejuvenation with identity preservation: [Cell Systems 2023/2024, PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9900859/).
- A 2025 landscape-control paper compares fitted-model stable-state control and barrier-height methods on synthetic, EMT, and HESC GRNs. This further narrows novelty: generic landscape or optimal-control methods are not new here. Their model-derived comparisons do not certify cell-level intervention safety: [Communications Physics 2025, DOI 10.1038/s42005-025-02160-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-025-02160-8).
- The FVS literature characterizes how control of selected feedback nodes can guide or determine dynamics under a particular nonlinear-network model. The cell-fate control literature itself distinguishes full mathematical state controllability from biologically admissible state changes and notes limits of linear/network-topology assumptions: [FVS paper PDF](https://dynamics.mi.fu-berlin.de/preprints/FiedlerMochizukiKurosawaSaito%202013-03-30%20submit%20final.pdf); [Cell Fate Reprogramming by Control of Intracellular Network Dynamics, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4388852/). These are not bounded-dose, population-level safe reach–avoid theorems for OSK/OSKM.

Prior-art and novelty verdict: exact CTMC pulse optimization is standard. Existing cell-fate control and genetic-circuit work is substantial. The contribution of this lane is the explicit, experimentally calibratable reach–avoid formulation and its quantitative interface boundary, not a claim to a new general theorem or new biological discovery. The decisive open gate is a held-out, lineage-resolved dataset that identifies the rates and confirms or rejects the coarse-grained model.
