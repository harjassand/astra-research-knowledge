# Revision 02 — clarification of dual support bounds

The two finite-support facts must not be conflated.

For the universal Dirichlet comparison, a separating PSD operator acts on a real Hermitian Hilbert space of dimension \(D=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathcal A_{sa}\) (or its traceless subspace). Its spectral decomposition uses at most \(D\) arbitrary Hermitian test directions. Those directions need not be labels from a particular experiment.

For the experiment-specific minimax \(q(E)\), a prior must be supported on labels in \(E\). The objective depends on that prior only through
\[
Y_p=\sum_xp_x|H_x\rangle\langle H_x|\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb R^D).
\]
Since the compact convex hull of these rank-one test matrices lies in a real vector space of dimension at most \(D(D+1)/2\), Carathéodory gives an optimal prior with at most \(D(D+1)/2+1\) labels. This remains dimension-dependent. It proves neither a fixed-support bound nor a uniform approximation of a growing family by fixed-size subfamilies.

Thus the all-frame separation route has a rank-at-most-\(D\) arbitrary test frame, while a worst prior drawn from the experiment can require a support bound of order \(D^2\). Both grow with the algebra dimension, which is the quantifier boundary relevant to the assigned audit.
