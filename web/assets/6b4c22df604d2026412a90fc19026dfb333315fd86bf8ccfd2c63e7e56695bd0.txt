c02_l01, 2026-10-07

CLAIM / SCOPE

For every even n=2k, define P to be the directed cyclic shift on n sites and
F=P+(1/4)I. The fixed-k unrestricted paired-minor law

  mu(I,J) proportional to |det F[I,J]|^2,  |I|=|J|=k,

has row and column marginals exactly 1/2 at the zero field, and zero field is
a global minimizer of the usual convex marginal-balancing dual. F is rational,
2n-sparse and kappa_2(F)<=5/3. The accepted set I intersect J=empty (the
single-occupancy hard-BCS sector at half filling) has exactly two states and
unnormalized weight 2. The total unrestricted weight is

  e_k(F*F) >= binom(n,k)(3/4)^n.

Thus the hard-event probability under this balanced, well-conditioned guide is
at most

  2/[binom(n,n/2)(3/4)^n] <= 2(n+1)(2/3)^n.                 (R1)

Direct rejection therefore has exponential expected trial count on this
family. This refutes only the proposal “sample the unprojected fixed-k paired-
minor k-DPP and reject until no site is doubly occupied.” It is not an
approximation-hardness result, an impossibility result for all samplers, or a
counterexample to the aggregate stable exchange theorem. The hard target here
has only two configurations and is itself trivial; the result isolates the
rejection bottleneck, not computational hardness.

PROOF

Let C be the n-cycle permutation matrix P, so P is unitary and has zero
main diagonal for n>=2. Put F=P+epsilon I with epsilon=1/4. For a fixed
k-set J, Cauchy-Binet gives

  sum_{I:|I|=k} |det F[I,J]|^2 = det((F*F)[J]).

Summing over J gives

  W_k(F) := sum_{|I|=|J|=k}|det F[I,J]|^2
          = sum_{|J|=k} det((F*F)[J])
          = e_k(lambda_1(F*F),...,lambda_n(F*F)).         (R2)

Since P is unitary, for all vectors x,

  (1-epsilon)||x|| <= ||(P+epsilon I)x|| <= (1+epsilon)||x||.

Hence every eigenvalue of F*F is at least (1-epsilon)^2=(3/4)^2. Each
product in the elementary symmetric polynomial has k terms, so

  W_k(F) >= binom(n,k)(3/4)^(2k)=binom(n,k)(3/4)^n.

Now impose I intersect J=empty. Since |I|=|J|=n/2, J=I^c. Any nonzero
entry in row i of F is either its diagonal epsilon at column i or its shift
1 at column i+1. The diagonal column i lies in I, hence cannot be used in the
minor with column set I^c. Every row i in I must therefore use the unique shift
column i+1. A nonzero determinant requires I+1=I^c, equivalently membership
alternates around the cycle. For even n there are exactly two such I, and the
corresponding minors are signed permutation matrices of determinant magnitude
1. Thus the disjoint-sector partition is exactly 2. Dividing by (R2) proves
the first inequality in (R1). Since binom(n,n/2)>=2^n/(n+1), the second
inequality follows.

The measure is cyclically invariant: shifting rows and columns together leaves
F unchanged. Because every I and J has size k, each site has inclusion
marginal k/n=1/2 on both sides. For the log-partition dual

  Phi(x,y)=log sum_{I,J} |det F[I,J]|^2 exp(sum_{i in I}x_i+sum_{j in J}y_j)
           -(1/2)sum_i(x_i+y_i),

convexity is the covariance Hessian of an exponential family; cyclic symmetry
makes the gradient at (0,0) zero. Therefore zero field is a global dual
minimizer, not merely a nearly balanced point. Singular values lie in
[3/4,5/4], so condition number is at most 5/3. The matrix has only the n
shift and n diagonal nonzeros, all in {1,1/4}.

UNRESTRICTED SAMPLER INTERFACE AND COST BOUNDARY

This unrestricted law has a two-stage k-DPP representation. First sample J
from the k-DPP with L=F*F, so its mass is det((F*F)[J])/e_k(F*F). Given J,
sample I from the k-DPP on row ground set with L_J=F[:,J]F[:,J]*. Its mass is

  det(L_J[I])/det((F*F)[J]) = |det F[I,J]|^2/det((F*F)[J]).

Multiplying cancels the conditional normalizer and yields precisely mu(I,J).
The primary DPP source below gives polynomial-time normalization and sampling
algorithms for k-DPPs via eigenvalue recursions. The proof here does not
implement a finite-precision sampler, so machine-precision, random-bit, and
end-to-end costs remain unaudited. Exact partition evaluation is polynomial
through a characteristic coefficient / elementary symmetric polynomial.
The rejected event is exactly the desired hard single-occupancy sector, and
(R1) shows that this particular exact-sampling route pays an exponential
rejection factor despite exact balance and a constant condition number.

RELATION TO STABLE HOLE EXCHANGE / AUXILIARIES

The stable hole polynomial still satisfies the aggregate coefficient-one
square-root exchange recorded in INITIAL. This counterexample does not negate
that theorem: it concerns the easier unrestricted pair-minor guide and a
specific conditioning operation. It does show why an auxiliary representation
must account for the event mass it imposes. A stability-preserving perturbation
or a balanced marginal dual alone cannot certify polynomial hard-event
probability. The cycle-4 baseline already gives a different exact balanced

fixed-k DPP rejection calculation, not a replacement sampler.
