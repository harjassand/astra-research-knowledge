2. EXACT ROBUST MOLECULAR THEOREM

Interface. Fix an integer m>=1, constants 0<kappa<=K, and even integer volume V>=2m. Consider the two reactions
  mA -> (m-1)A+B,    mB -> A+(m-1)B,
with fixed total count A+B=V, initial A=B=V/2, and arbitrary predictable rates k_A(t,past),k_B(t,past) in [kappa,K]. Independent controller randomness is allowed; no Markov-policy premise is imposed. Let J_t=A_t. Its intensities are
  b_j(t)=k_B(t,past) V^(1-m) (V-j)_m,
  d_j(t)=k_A(t,past) V^(1-m) (j)_m,
where (j)_m is the falling factorial (zero when j<m).
The closed irreducible count interval is I={m-1,...,V-m+1}. Total intensity <=2KV, so the controlled process is nonexplosive. The initial condition lies in this interval. This is the actual integer-complex/factorial model, not a fractional-power surrogate.

Choose m<=L<V/2 with L/V -> q in (0,1/2), and Q_V={L,...,V-L}. Let tau be first exit from Q_V. Set rho=kappa/K and
  W_j = rho^j product_{r=0}^{m-1} binom(V-2r,j-r),  j in I,
  R_V = [sum_{j=m-1}^L W_j] / [sum_{j=m-1}^{V/2} W_j].
Only the ratio matters; rational rho permits exact integer rescaling. Then, for EVERY admissible predictable controller and EVERY t>=0,
  P(J_t not in Q_V) <= 2 R_V,
and for EVERY T>=0,
  P(tau<=T) <= 2 K V^(1-m) (L)_m T R_V.                     (1)
Bounds may be capped at 1. The second expression is a crossing-count bound; unlike the earlier N35 collar estimate, it has no fitted layer width, asymptotic factorial-correction threshold, or initial-gap error term.

Thermodynamic statement. Put
  p = rho^(1/m)/(1+rho^(1/m)) = 1/[1+(K/kappa)^(1/m)],
  Delta = m D(q||p),
  D(q||p)=q log(q/p)+(1-q) log((1-q)/(1-p)).
If 0<q<p, then
  R_V = exp[-V Delta + O(log V)]                            (2)
for fixed m,kappa,K,q and rounding L/V=q+O(1/V). Consequently, for every epsilon>0,
  sup_predictable_controls P(tau<=exp[(Delta-epsilon)V]) -> 0.
For the admissible STATIC choice k_A=K,k_B=kappa,
  P(tau<=exp[(Delta+epsilon)V]) -> 1,
  E[tau] = exp[V Delta + O(log V)].                         (3)
Thus Delta is the sharp worst-control exponential safety-horizon threshold, not merely a sufficient local collar exponent. Moreover,
  -lim_{V->infinity} V^-1 log[sup_controls sup_{t>=0} P(J_t not in Q_V)]
    = Delta.                                               (4)
At q>=p no positive uniform marginal exponent exists: the lower extremal stationary law already has nonexponentially small probability below q.

All-time occupation consequence. Equation (1) also gives E[T^-1 integral_0^T 1{J_t not in Q_V}dt] <=2R_V for every T>0. Any time-homogeneous state feedback has a finite irreducible Markov chain, so its invariant law satisfies the same outside-mass bound. Arbitrary-history policies need not possess an invariant law; the fixed-time/expected-occupation statements remain valid. Infinite pathwise containment is false: constant positive rates eventually visit every state in I.

3. PROOF, WITH LOAD-BEARING STEPS

(a) Extremal coupling. Let J^- have constant k_A=K,k_B=kappa and J^+ the reverse. They can be coupled to any predictable controlled J, started identically, so that J^-_t<=J_t<=J^+_t at every time. At equality, thin common Poisson clocks: every birth of the lower process also births the upper one, and every death of the upper also deaths the lower. Ordered rates permit this. When states differ, nearest-neighbor jumps cannot cross without meeting; at meeting restore the common thinning. This proves the comparison directly, including history-dependent control, while keeping each extremal chain's marginal law unchanged.

(b) Exact stationary law. For J^- detailed balance requires
  pi^-_{j+1}/pi^-_j = rho (V-j)_m/(j+1)_m.
The displayed W_j satisfy this identity since each binomial factor contributes (V-j-r)/(j+1-r). Thus pi^-=W/sum W is the exact invariant law. J^+ is its reflection j->V-j.

(c) Uniform-in-time bound without a mixing-time debt. Start a stationary J^- chain Y with Y_0 distributed as pi^-, conditioned on Y_0<=V/2. This conditioned initial state is surely <=J^-_0. Order coupling gives
  P_{V/2}(J^-_t<=L)
    <=P(Y_t<=L | Y_0<=V/2)
    <=pi^-({j<=L})/pi^-({j<=V/2}) = R_V.
The last inequality uses the unconditioned stationary pair law and drops the event Y_0<=V/2 in its numerator. Apply comparison and reflection to the two tails, obtaining 2R_V. No assertion that a deterministic midpoint stochastically dominates the unconditioned stationary law was made; that tempting step would be false.

(d) Exit hazard. Any first exit from Q_V includes a crossing L->L-1 or V-L->V-L+1. Their controlled intensities are at most K V^(1-m)(L)_m. The probability of being at either crossing source is bounded by R_V from (c). Expected total crossings by T is consequently at most the right side of (1). First exit requires at least one crossing. The full controlled chain is already finite/nonexplosive, so this count argument has no hidden outside-to-core hypothesis.

(e) Sharp rate. Stirling's formula, with fixed m and shifted factorials, gives uniformly
  log W_j = C_V + V F(j/V)+O(log V),
  F(x)=m[-x log x-(1-x)log(1-x)]+x log rho.
F is strictly concave and maximized at p, and F(p)-F(q)=mD(q||p). The denominator in R contains p, while the numerator is maximized at q when q<p; finite summation adds only O(log V). This proves (2), the upper side of (4), and the long-horizon lower bound for survival. For the lower side of (4), take the static lower extremal controller and then t->infinity; its stationary tail has exponent Delta.

(f) Exit-time upper bound at the sharp exponent. For the lower extremal chain, the mean hitting time of L-1 from i=V/2 is exactly
  E_i tau_{L-1} = sum_{k=L}^i [sum_{j=k}^{V-m+1} W_j] / [d_k W_k].    (5)
This is verified by substituting consecutive differences into b_k(h_{k+1}-h_k)+d_k(h_{k-1}-h_k)=-1, including the reflecting top endpoint. For k below pV the summand's exponential rate is F(p)-F(k/V); its maximum occurs at k=L. For k above pV the tail numerator and W_k have equal exponential rate, so no larger barrier is introduced. The d_k are only polynomial in V on this interval. Therefore (5)=exp[VDelta+O(log V)]. First exit from Q_V is no later than tau_{L-1}. Equation (1) gives the matching exponential lower bound on E tau; Markov's inequality applied to (5) proves the probability statement in (3).

5. DECISIVE COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE GENERAL TRANSFER

Established published example, independently reconstructed: 0->2A->4A+B->6A+4B->3A, positive constant rates, any fixed finite volume. It is strongly endotactic (see (A), Example 3.2); hence its deterministic ODE is permanent under established strongly endotactic results, without relying on N33. Its stochastic model nevertheless explodes with positive probability from every count state.

Mechanism: from (a,0), repeat
  (a,0)->(a+2,1)->(a+4,4)->(a+1,0).
The desired propensities grow as a^2,a^4,a^6 respectively. At each step all competing enabled propensities are smaller by at least an a^-2 ratio (at B=0 only the constant influx competes). Thus the probability of the entire infinite staircase is product_a [1-O(a^-2)]>0 after a sufficiently large start. Conditional holding times have summable means O(a^-2)+O(a^-4)+O(a^-6); competing exponential holding time is independent of destination selection. Infinite jumps occur in finite time on a positive-probability event. Any starting state can reach the staircase by a finite positive-probability path. Fixed volume changes constants but not the powers.

This is a boundary mechanism: factorial propensities disable the high-order inward reaction when B has insufficient molecules. A deterministic all-scale positive-orthant certificate, even with computable coefficients, does not repair it. Bimolecular restrictions remove this particular explosion route, but the 2026 regularity theorem does not itself supply stationary concentration or robust ergodicity.
