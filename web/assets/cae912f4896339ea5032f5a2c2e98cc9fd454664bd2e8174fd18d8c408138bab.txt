1. ANALYTIC VARIANCE CHANNEL: COMPLETE DERIVATION

Let R>=4096. Let X have density exp(-x)/Z on [0,2R], where
Z=1-exp(-2R), and let G~N(0,1) be independent. Set Y=X+G.
For a centered reference replace Y by Y-E[X] and compose every field below
with that known translation. Translation preserves every displayed bound.

The truncated exponential is log-concave. Gaussian convolution preserves
log-concavity. Its density is positive and real analytic on the whole line:

 rho_R(y)= exp(-y+1/2)/Z
   * [Phi(2R-y+1)-Phi(1-y)].                                (3)

This follows by completing the square in int_0^(2R) exp(-x)phi(y-x)dx.
In particular rho_R(y)<=exp(-y+1/2)/Z. On [R,R+1], the bracket is at least
1/2 for R>=4, by Gaussian Chernoff bounds.

For truncated exponential on [0,L], direct moment integration gives

 Var(X)=1-L^2 exp(-L)/(1-exp(-L))^2 <=1.                     (4)

The exact Neumann spectral gap is 1/4+pi^2/L^2, so
P_X=(1/4+pi^2/L^2)^(-1)<4. To verify the spectral assertion, the weighted
generator is f''-f'. Write f=exp(x/2)u. The eigenproblem becomes
-u''+u/4=lambda u with u'+u/2=0 at 0,L. For lambda>1/4, putting
k=sqrt(lambda-1/4) gives u=a cos(kx)-a sin(kx)/(2k); the right boundary
requires sin(kL)=0. For 0<lambda<1/4, the corresponding hyperbolic solution
requires sqrt(1/4-lambda)=1/2 and hence lambda=0, not a positive eigenvalue.
At lambda=1/4 the linear solution is zero. The constant f gives lambda=0.
Regular Sturm-Liouville completeness gives the stated first positive value.

For any f, variance decomposition on the independent pair (X,G) yields
Var f(X+G)<=E|f'(X+G)|^2+P_X E|E_G f'(X+G)|^2
             <=(1+P_X) E|f'(X+G)|^2.
Thus P(mu_R)<=5 and Var(Y)<=2. No KLS conjecture is used.

Proof of full affine dimension: choose theta_j=j/d, j=0,...,d. If the
augmented mean matrix [1,m_q(theta_j)] were singular, a nonzero real
trigonometric polynomial of degree q would vanish at d+1=2q+1 distinct
points exp(i theta_j). Multiplication by z^q would give a nonzero ordinary
polynomial of degree at most 2q with 2q+1 distinct roots. Impossible.
Hence these d+1 means are affinely independent. The line's affine dimension
is 1. This is an exact root-count proof, not a numerical determinant claim.

For completeness the quantum memory lower is reconstructed here. Put a
uniform prior on those d+1 Gaussian means. Its marginal mu is a positive
mixture, and let b(x) be its posterior mean. Its Jacobian is
Db(x)=Cov(mean|x), strictly positive definite. On the compact convex cube
B=[-1,1]^d, its smallest eigenvalue has a positive minimum v. Therefore
||b(x)-b(x')||>=v||x-x'|| by integrated strong monotonicity on the segment.
The mixture density is <=(2pi)^(-d/2). For ANY action t, the sublevel
||b(x)-t||^2/4<=u intersects B in mass at most C u^(d/2): if nonempty, pick
x0 in it, pull back a ball of radius 4sqrt(u)/v, and use the density bound.

Use bounded loss ||mean-t||^2/4 on the convex hull of means; all norms <=1,
so its range is [0,1]. Its exact posterior regret is ||b(X)-b(Y)||^2/4.
