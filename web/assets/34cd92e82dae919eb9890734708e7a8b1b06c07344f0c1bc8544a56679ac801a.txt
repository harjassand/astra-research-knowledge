# N02 — Fast neutrino flavor conversion: a seed-shifted first-pulse law

Date frozen: 2026-10-09  
Scope: two-flavor, fast limit, axial symmetry, collisionless homogeneous mean-field equations, first nonlinear excursion from a diagonal ELN state.  
Status: exact three-beam reduction and formula internally derived; finite-seed convergence finite-tested; generalization to a continuous spectrum is conditional on a single isolated dominant unstable mode; no claim of astrophysical empirical confirmation, priority, or new published theorem.

## Target and result

Can the angular electron-lepton-number distribution determine a nonlinear observable beyond the statement that a crossing permits linear growth? A useful exact answer exists for the first excursion selected by one isolated unstable mode. The relevant representation is the unstable separatrix of the three-beam moment pendulum, or, for a continuum, its Lax-vector realization. In the singular limit of seed amplitude \(\epsilon\to0\), the first pulse is a fixed profile translated by a logarithmic delay \(\gamma^{-1}\log(1/\epsilon)\) (plus a computable seed projection and a fixed normalization constant). Its angular conversion profile is a resonant rational function of the ELN mode \(w=vG_1\), not a closure in finitely many angular moments.

The profile gives a strict obstruction to one-soliton saturation of the absolute lepton-charge budget for a nonatomic continuous ELN distribution: a mode is completely reversed only at one resonant angle. A discrete minority sector concentrated at that angle can saturate the charge budget. The three-beam example below realizes this exactly and removes the ELN crossing at its first pulse. This is a first-pulse statement. It does not give a universal long-time final state, and the underlying temporal soliton is already unstable to additional perturbations.

The exact single-soliton formula used here was published by Fiorillo and Raffelt in 2023. The internal result is the seed-matched first-pulse law, its exact timing constant for the worked three-beam system, and the conversion-budget gap derived from that formula. These are candidate consequences and are not asserted to be novel relative to all literature. The finite numerical experiment tests only the stated three-beam mean-field equations.

## Equations, moment geometry, and exact three-beam construction

Use dimensionless time and write the homogeneous axial fast equations as
\[
 \dot{\mathbf D}_v=(\mathbf D_0-v\mathbf D_1)\times\mathbf D_v,
 \qquad \mathbf D_n=\int_{-1}^1v^n\mathbf D_v\,dv.
\]
For a finite beam set, replace the integral by a sum. The source-reported Hamiltonian is \(H=(|\mathbf D_0|^2-|\mathbf D_1|^2)/2\). Directly from the equation, \(\mathbf D_0\) is constant, each beam length is constant, and \(|\mathbf D_1|\) is constant. In a frame corotating with \(\mathbf D_0\), the moment equations are
\[
 \dot{\mathbf M}_n=-\mathbf M_1\times\mathbf M_{n+1}.
\]

Take three velocities and diagonal polarization vectors \(\mathbf D_i=G_i\hat z\):
\[
 (v_1,v_2,v_3)=(-1,1/3,1),\qquad (G_1,G_2,G_3)=(-4,-4,1).
\]
Here the subscripts on \(G_i\) label beams, while \(G_n=\sum_i v_i^nG_i\) below denotes moments. The angular ELN signs are \((-,-,+)\), so there is a crossing. The first three moments are
\[
 G_0=-7,\qquad G_1=11/3,\qquad G_2=-31/9.
\]
The signed Lax scalar for this diagonal state is
\[
 \ell(u)=\sum_i\frac{v_iG_i}{u-v_i}
 =\frac{11u^2-14u+7}{(u-1)(u+1)(3u-1)},
 \qquad u_\pm=\frac{7\pm2i\sqrt7}{11}.
\]
Thus the Lax spectrum has a nonreal pair. In this example the imaginary part gives the positive growth rate \(\gamma=|G_1|\operatorname{Im}u_+=2\sqrt7/3\); the real pendulum spin parameter is fixed below with its sign fixed by the moment convention.

Here is the exact three-beam reduction. Let
\[
 p=v_1v_2v_3=-1/3,\quad s=v_1+v_2+v_3=1/3,\quad
 \mathbf J=\mathbf M_2-s\mathbf M_1.
\]
The velocity polynomial gives \(\mathbf M_3=s\mathbf M_2-(v_1v_2+v_1v_3+v_2v_3)\mathbf M_1+p\mathbf M_0\). Substitution into the moment equations yields
\[
 \dot{\mathbf M}_0=0,\qquad
 \dot{\mathbf M}_1=\mathbf J\times\mathbf M_1,\qquad
 \dot{\mathbf J}=p\,\mathbf M_0\times\mathbf M_1.
\]
Write \(\mathbf M_0=G_0\hat z\), \(\mathbf M_1=G_1\mathbf r\), \(|\mathbf r|=1\). Then
\[
 \dot{\mathbf r}=\mathbf J\times\mathbf r,\qquad
 \dot{\mathbf J}=\lambda^2\hat z\times\mathbf r,\qquad
 \lambda^2=pG_0G_1=77/9.
\]
The conserved spin is \(S=\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf r=G_2-sG_1=-14/3\), so put \(\omega=S/2=-7/3\) and \(\Gamma^2=\lambda^2-\omega^2=28/9\), with \(\Gamma=2\sqrt7/3\). The equilibrium \(\mathbf r=\hat z,\mathbf J=S\hat z\) is a hyperbolic pendulum top because \(\Gamma>0\).

For completeness, the separatrix equation for \(x=\hat z\cdot\mathbf r\) follows from the pendulum integrals. Set \(\mathbf L=\mathbf J-S\mathbf r=\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}\); on the separatrix through the upright equilibrium, energy and axial angular momentum give
\[
 |\mathbf L|^2=2\lambda^2(1-x),\qquad L_z=S(1-x),\qquad
 \dot x^2=(1-x^2)|\mathbf L|^2-L_z^2.
\]
Using \(S=2\omega\) and \(\Gamma^2=\lambda^2-\omega^2\), direct substitution verifies the homoclinic pulse
\[
 x(t)=1-\frac{2\Gamma^2}{\lambda^2}\operatorname{sech}^2\!\bigl(\Gamma(t-t_p)\bigr).
\]
The beam vectors can be reconstructed by inverting the three moment equations:
\[
 \mathbf D_i=
 \frac{\mathbf J+v_i\mathbf M_1+v_jv_k\mathbf M_0}
 {(v_i-v_j)(v_i-v_k)},\qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.
\]
This is the finite-beam counterpart of the Lax-vector reconstruction. At the pulse center \(x=1-2\Gamma^2/\lambda^2=3/11\), the reconstruction gives
\[
 (D_1^z/G_1,D_2^z/G_2,D_3^z/G_3)=(3/4,3/4,-1),
 \qquad (D_1^z,D_2^z,D_3^z)=(-3,-3,-1).
\]
The final signs are all negative: the first pulse has removed the initial crossing.

## Exact first-pulse profile and charge-budget corollary

For a continuous or discrete diagonal ELN \(G(v)\) supporting one temporal soliton, Fiorillo–Raffelt give the exact Lax reconstruction
\[
 \mathbf D_v(t)=G(v)\,
 \frac{\lambda^2\hat z+w\mathbf J(t)+w^2\mathbf r(t)}
 {\lambda^2+wS+w^2},\qquad w=vG_1,\quad S=2\omega.
\]
Its \(z\)-component is obtained without a closure approximation. The denominator is positive, since
\(\lambda^2+wS+w^2=(w+\omega)^2+\Gamma^2\). Combining the reconstruction with the separatrix \(r_z(t)\) gives
\[
 \frac{D_v^z(t)}{G(v)}
 =1-\frac{2\Gamma^2(vG_1)^2}
 {\lambda^2[(vG_1+\omega)^2+\Gamma^2]}
 \operatorname{sech}^2\!\bigl(\Gamma(t-t_p)\bigr).
\]
This is precisely the published Eq. (C13) at \(t=t_p\), rewritten using \(\lambda^2=\omega^2+\Gamma^2\); the full time dependence follows from Eqs. (C11)–(C12) and the pendulum separatrix. Define
\[
 \chi(v)=\frac{\Gamma^2(vG_1)^2}
 {\lambda^2[(vG_1+\omega)^2+\Gamma^2]}.
\]
At the pulse center, the signed polarization ratio is \(R(v)=1-2\chi(v)\), and the charge conversion per angle is \(c(v)=|G(v)|\chi(v)\). Exact algebra gives
\[
 1-\chi(v)=
 \frac{(\omega vG_1+\lambda^2)^2}
 {\lambda^2[(vG_1+\omega)^2+\Gamma^2]}\ge0.
\]
Thus \(0\le\chi\le1\). Complete flavor reversal, \(R=-1\), can occur only at the single resonant velocity
\[
 v_*=-\frac{\lambda^2}{\omega G_1},
\]
when \(\omega G_1\ne0\) and \(v_*\in[-1,1]\). If \(\omega=0\), no finite \(v\) reaches \(\chi=1\).

The conversion-budget bound itself is just the conserved total flavor charge plus fixed spin lengths. Define
\[
 A_+=\int_{G>0}G(v)\,dv,\qquad
 A_- =\int_{G<0}|G(v)|\,dv,\qquad
 c(v,t)=\frac{|G(v)|-\operatorname{sgn}G(v)D_v^z(t)}2.
\]
Then \(0\le c\le|G|\). Conservation of \(D_0^z\) implies \(C_+(t)=C_-(t)\), where \(C_\pm=\int_{\pm G>0}c\,dv\). Therefore
\[
 C(t)=C_+(t)+C_-(t)\le2\min(A_+,A_-).
\]
This is an absolute charge-budget bound, not a claim that the dynamics reaches it. On the single-soliton pulse, charge conservation also gives \(\int_{G>0}G\chi=\int_{G<0}|G|\chi\). If \(A_m=\min(A_+,A_-)\) is the smaller signed sector, the exact gap between the budget and the soliton-pulse conversion is
\[
 2A_m-C(t_p)=
 2\int_{\text{minority sector}} |G(v)|
 \frac{(\omega vG_1+\lambda^2)^2}
 {\lambda^2[(vG_1+\omega)^2+\Gamma^2]}\,dv\ge0.
\]
For a nonatomic ELN measure with positive minority charge, this gap is strictly positive: the integrand vanishes at at most one velocity, a measure-zero set. The first soliton pulse therefore cannot saturate the full charge budget for a genuinely continuous minority angular distribution. An atomic minority sector supported entirely at \(v_*\) can saturate it if the other sector supplies the charge required by the conserved \(D_0^z\). This is a first-pulse theorem; later multimode evolution is not bounded by this soliton profile.

For the three-beam example, the velocities have \(w=(-11/3,11/9,11/3)\) and the three peak conversion fractions are \(\chi=(1/8,1/8,1)\). Thus
\[
 C(t_p)=4(1/8)+4(1/8)+1=2.
\]
Here \(A_+=1,A_-=8\), so \(C\le2\) and the first pulse attains the exact absolute charge budget. The unique resonant beam is the positive-ELN minority beam at \(v_*=1\); it completely reverses, while each negative-ELN beam converts one eighth of its weight. This is a sharply angle-selective statement, not equipartition.

## Seed-to-pulse matching in the three-beam system

The soliton is an exact orbit, but a physical instability starts from a small seed near its asymptotic saddle. In the frame corotating with \(D_0^z\), let \(q_i=D_i^x+iD_i^y\). Linearization gives
\[
 \dot q_i=-iv_iG_1q_i-iG_i\sum_jq_j+iv_iG_i\sum_jv_jq_j.
\]
The growing eigenpair for the example is
\[
 \gamma-i\,7/3,\quad \gamma=2\sqrt7/3,
 \qquad q=(-3/4-i\sqrt7/4,\ 1,\ -1/4+i\sqrt7/4),
\]
normalized so \(\max_i|q_i|=1\), with \(\sum_iq_i=0\). The other co-rotating eigenvalues are \(-\gamma-i7/3\) and \(+7i\): one stable mode and one neutral global-tilt mode. Thus, after reducing the neutral rotation and fixing the beam lengths, the equilibrium has one simple growing complex mode; its real two-dimensional unstable manifold is the pendulum separatrix, up to azimuth and time translation.

Let the initial transverse seed have unstable projection \(\epsilon\alpha q+o(\epsilon)\), \(\alpha\ne0\), with all other initial perturbation components \(O(\epsilon)\). The growing mode dominates after a logarithmic delay. The stable mode decays, the neutral tilt remains \(O(\epsilon)\), and the phase of \(\alpha\) is a global flavor-axis rotation. For this finite analytic three-beam ODE, the local unstable-manifold construction therefore gives convergence on each bounded pulse-time interval, after the amplitude-dependent shift and azimuth are removed. It does not assert convergence at arbitrarily late physical times. The conversion observable, which is azimuth invariant, has the limit
\[
 C_\epsilon(t)\longrightarrow
 2\,\operatorname{sech}^2\!\left[
 \gamma\left(t-\frac1\gamma\log\frac{4\sqrt2}{\epsilon|\alpha|}\right)
 \right]
\]
after choosing the time origin at the unstable-manifold asymptote. Equivalently, the first-pulse center is
\[
 t_p=\frac1\gamma\log\frac1{\epsilon|\alpha|}
       +\frac1\gamma\log(4\sqrt2)+o(1).
\]

The additive constant is obtained by matching the exact soliton tail to the chosen linear eigenvector normalization, not fitted. As \(t-t_p\to-\infty\),
\[
 |r_\perp|\sim\frac{4\Gamma}{\lambda}e^{\Gamma(t-t_p)}
 =\frac8{\sqrt{11}}e^{\Gamma(t-t_p)}.
\]
For the normalized eigenvector, \(|r_\perp|=|\sum_i v_iq_i|/G_1=\sqrt{2/11}\) when \(\max|q_i|=1\), so the largest beam transverse component in the soliton tail is
\(\sqrt{11/2}\,(8/\sqrt{11})e^{\Gamma(t-t_p)}=4\sqrt2e^{\Gamma(t-t_p)}\). Matching it to \(\epsilon|\alpha|\) yields the stated delay.

This is an internally derived singular-limit result for the specified finite system. The corresponding general statement for a continuous \(G(v)\) is conditional: it requires a well-posed nonlinear evolution, one isolated simple fastest homogeneous unstable eigenvalue with a spectral gap, nonzero seed projection, and control of all other modes up to times \(O(\log(1/\epsilon))\). A simple unstable root alone does not prove those semigroup bounds for the continuum kinetic equation. If fastest growth is degenerate, seed ratios and relative phases survive at the onset scale, so there is no reason for one seed-universal pulse. If several unstable modes have comparable growth, nonlinear mode competition can begin before the one-mode soliton has completed a swing.

It tests three seed families (pure growing mode; growing plus neutral tilt; growing plus stable mode), three values \(\epsilon=10^{-3},10^{-4},10^{-5}\), and shifts time by \(\gamma^{-1}\log(1/(\epsilon|\alpha|))\). ## Homogeneous, inhomogeneous, and collisional scope

The exact pendulum/Lax argument above is for collisionless homogeneous axial evolution. It must not be transferred to a full inhomogeneous calculation by replacing \(G(v)\) with a local distribution. In one spatial dimension the collisionless equation is
\[
 (\partial_t+v\partial_z)\mathbf D_v
       =(\mathbf D_0-v\mathbf D_1)\times\mathbf D_v.
\]
It implies \(\partial_t\mathbf D_0+\partial_z\mathbf D_1=0\), so the spatially integrated \(\mathbf D_0\) is conserved on a periodic domain. It also preserves \(|\mathbf D_v|\) along each characteristic. These facts retain the absolute integrated charge-budget bound. The homogeneous moment Lax equation and its \(L_u^2\) invariants do not survive generic spatial dependence; the local \(\mathbf D_1\) is not a conserved vector. Spatial Fourier modes may be unstable even when the homogeneous axial mode is stable, so the first-pulse law is not a generic inhomogeneous prediction.

With a collision term \(\mathbf C_v\),
\[
 (\partial_t+v\partial_z)\mathbf D_v
       =(\mathbf D_0-v\mathbf D_1)\times\mathbf D_v+\mathbf C_v,
 \qquad
 \frac{d}{dt}|\mathbf D_v|^2\big|_{\rm char}=2\mathbf D_v\cdot\mathbf C_v.
\]
The collisionless trajectory lengths and Lax invariants therefore fail unless the chosen collision operator has the corresponding special orthogonality/conservation properties. Integrating the continuity equation adds \(\int dv\,\mathbf C_v\); integrated flavor charge is conserved only if that collision integral vanishes in the relevant flavor component. Known collision studies show that equal damping and neutrino–antineutrino asymmetric rates have materially different nonlinear behavior, and asymmetric scattering can itself drive instabilities. No first-pulse theorem here includes those effects.

## Counterexamples, alternative routes, and failure boundaries

1. **A crossing is not a homogeneous-mode theorem.** The crossing \(G=(-1,+1)\) at velocities \((-1,+1)\) has no growing homogeneous two-beam mode; the exact homogeneous two-beam system is marginal/stable. Fiorillo–Raffelt (2024) show the two-beam homogeneous model is always stable and that driven feeding can alter the response. A crossing is necessary for an unstable mode, and the broader inhomogeneous mode family changes the sufficiency statement. Do not infer homogeneous growth from crossing alone.

2. **Seed and infinite-time limits need not commute.** For two beams define \(\mathbf S=\mathbf D_++\mathbf D_-\), \(\mathbf T=\mathbf D_+-\mathbf D_-\) at \(v_\pm=\pm1\). The exact equations give \(\dot{\mathbf S}=0\), \(\dot{\mathbf T}=2\mathbf S\times\mathbf T\). With \(\mathbf D_+=(a,0,1)\), \(\mathbf D_-=(a,0,-1)\), the transverse seed is \(a\), \(|\mathbf S|=2a\), and the individual z components oscillate at rate \(4a\): at fixed time the \(a\to0\) limit is the diagonal state, but at every \(a>0\) the long-time z average is zero. Choosing \(\mathbf D_-=-\mathbf D_+\) instead gives \(\mathbf S=0\) and a static state, despite the same small transverse magnitudes and the same limiting diagonal ELN. This is a valid phase/noncommutation adversary, but it has no growing homogeneous fast mode and is not the saturation result.

3. **The first soliton is fragile.** Its asymptotic homogeneous state is itself unstable. Additional growing perturbations destroy it after a time that depends logarithmically on their size. Thus the one-pulse law is not a late-time attractor or a universal quasi-steady state.

4. **A single-wave spatial ansatz is a different question.** The 2026 single-wave analysis reports that weak shallow crossings can be described by resonant flavor-pendulum dynamics, while a strong instability generally does not connect its linear single-wave mode to a regular nonlinear single-wave solution (except for a two-beam distribution). This does not refute the exact homogeneous temporal soliton, but it blocks a casual extension of this result to strong spatially inhomogeneous multi-angle evolution.

5. **The budget bound is not the nonlinear invariant set.** Its saturation is allowed by total charge and fixed vector lengths but can be blocked by additional Gaudin/Lax invariants. The derived strict gap concerns the one-soliton first pulse only; it is not a theorem that a later integrable or inhomogeneous trajectory can never attain the charge bound.

Alternative approaches considered: (a) use only the global \(D_0^z\) conservation law to infer a final conversion fraction; this cannot determine the fraction and known quasi-steady outcomes depend on spatial boundary conditions and angular structure. (b) use a homogeneous two-beam precession phase as a seed-limit result; it has no instability and vanishing precession rate, so it is retained only as a counterexample. (c) promote the Lax invariants to an inhomogeneous closure; the transport term prevents the homogeneous Lax-vector precession equation. (d) use collisional pendulum damping as a universal saturation rule; the published common-damping result is empirical within a two-beam pendulum model and small neutrino/antineutrino damping asymmetry changes it, so it cannot support a general theorem.

## Exact claim ledger and open gates

- **Source-reported:** homogeneous collisionless axial EOM, Lax invariants, three-beam pendulum reduction, exact soliton profile, and first-pulse ELN-angle formula under the soliton hypotheses.
- **Internally derived:** the explicit three-beam moments, Lax roots, pendulum parameters, beamwise first-pulse state, exact charge-budget saturation in this discrete example, strict nonatomic-sector gap for the one-soliton pulse, and the seed-to-pulse logarithmic timing constant \(\gamma^{-1}\log(4\sqrt2)\) for the stated eigenvector normalization.
- **Finite-tested:** RK4 integration of the 3-beam equations for 9 seed/amplitude combinations; conversion collapse, peak, charge conservation, and spin-length drift reported above. No independent solver or interval arithmetic was used.
- **Unresolved:** (i) rigorous seed-limit convergence for a continuum Vlasov/QKE system with only a spectral-gap hypothesis; (ii) competition when two or more roots share the largest growth rate; (iii) generic inhomogeneous multi-wave evolution; (iv) a late-time saturation law; (v) collision operators preserving a useful replacement for the Lax spectral curve; (vi) application to any specified supernova/merger ELN profile with realistic boundary and collision terms.

An astrophysical discriminator follows only conditionally: if a region is well approximated by one dominant homogeneous temporal mode for one pulse, the angle-resolved converted ELN fraction should be peaked around \(v_*=-\lambda^2/(\omega G_1)\), with analytic width set by \(\Gamma\), and a smooth minority sector cannot all flip in that pulse. Multi-mode growth, transport, collisions, vacuum terms, non-axial structure, and repeated pulses can erase or alter this signature. No simulation or model curve is empirical evidence for a supernova or merger outcome.
