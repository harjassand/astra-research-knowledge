THEOREM AND CONVENTIONS

Let J_a=(1/2) sum_{i=1}^N sigma_a^(i), J^2=sum_a J_a^2, and
t_N(B)=2^(-N) Tr B. Write D(rho,sigma)=(1/2)||rho-sigma||_1.
For fixed 0<A<1 and b in R, let R_i be iid uniform unit vectors in R^3,
S_N=N^(-1/2) sum_i R_i, and omega_R= tensor_i (I+R_i.sigma)/2.
Define

 alpha=9A/[4(1-A)], h=-3b/[2(1-A)],
 p(S)=exp[-alpha|S|^2+h S_z],
 sigma_N(A,b)=E[p(S_N) omega_R]/E p(S_N),
 g_N(A,b)=exp[-A J^2/N-b J_z/sqrt(N)],
 Z_N(A,b)=t_N(g_N(A,b)),
 tau_N(A,b)=2^(-N) g_N(A,b)/Z_N(A,b).

Then sigma_N is a positive, normalized, fully separable finite-N state, and

 ||sigma_N(A,b)-tau_N(A,b)||_1 -> 0.

This also holds for any A_N->A in (0,1), b_N->b. All ensembles here are explicit
continuous probability ensembles of pure product states. In finite dimension
the integral belongs to the closed convex set SEP; a finite convex decomposition
also exists by Caratheodory, but its efficient construction is not asserted.

The stationary bath in the assignment, with nu=c sqrt(N), c>0, and
beta_N=log(1+1/(c sqrt(N))), obeys

 limsup_N inf_{sigma in SEP} D(rho_N,sigma) <= delta(c),
 delta(c)=int_{s>c^2} K_b(s) mu(ds), b=1/c,
 K_b(s)=(1+s b^2/2)^(-3/2) exp[b^2/(8+4s b^2)],

where mu is the law of S=sum_{n>=1} E_n, E_n independent Exp(pi^2 n^2).
In particular

 delta(c) <= 2 (2/3)^(3/2) exp[-pi^2 c^2+1/(12c^2)].

PROOF OF THE STRONG APPROXIMATION

1. A mixed classical/quantum moment lemma.

Consider the probability measure on (S_N,C_N,Z_N), where
C_N=J^2/N, Z_N=J_z/sqrt(N), obtained by drawing R and measuring the commuting
pair (C_N,Z_N) in omega_R. This is an ordinary positive probability measure.
Its support satisfies C_N>=Z_N^2.

Under the unweighted orientation ensemble, every joint polynomial moment tends
to the corresponding moment of (S,|X|^2,X_z), where (S,X) is a centered ordinary
six-dimensional Gaussian with covariance blocks

 Var S=I/3, Var X=I/4, Cov(S,X)=I/6.

Proof: regard each site as a classical variable R and a spin T=sigma/2 with
conditional state (I+R.sigma)/2. The six centered site symbols have pair moments
E R_a R_b=delta_ab/3, E Tr(omega_R T_a T_b)=delta_ab/4,
and E R_a Tr(omega_R T_b)=delta_ab/6. The imaginary spin pair term vanishes
because E R=0. Expand any ordered word in the normalized sums. Independence
over sites kills every assignment with a singleton site. At word length k,
only assignments consisting entirely of pairs survive after division by
N^(k/2); their limit is exactly the Gaussian Wick pairing sum. Assignments
containing larger blocks have fewer than k/2 occupied sites and vanish.
Polynomials in (S_N,C_N,Z_N) are finite sums of these ordered words, so the same
conclusion applies to their moments.

This gives weak convergence of the positive joint measures, not just a formal
moment assertion: the second moments give tightness, convergence of arbitrarily
higher moments gives uniform integrability for each monomial along subsequences,
and the limiting joint law is moment determinate. For the last fact its moment
generating function E exp[t.S+u|X|^2+vX_z] is finite in a neighborhood of zero;
equivalently its Gaussian and quadratic-Gaussian coordinate marginals satisfy
Carleman's criterion. Thus every subsequential measure has the displayed law.

The same argument under the normalized tracial state gives
(C_N,Z_N) -> (|X|^2,X_z), X~N(0,I/4).

2. The exact squared-norm identity.

Set Q_N=2^N sigma_N and q_N=g_N/Z_N. For independent copies R,R',

 t_N(Q_N^2)=
 E[p(S_N)p(S'_N) product_i(1+R_i.R'_i)]/(E p(S_N))^2.

This follows exactly from the single-qubit identity
(1/2)Tr[(I+R.sigma)(I+R'.sigma)]=1+R.R'. The product is nonnegative.
Consequently the numerator is an expectation under iid site pairs whose joint
density relative to independent uniform orientations is 1+R.R'. This density
has integral one, uniform marginals, zero means, and covariance blocks

 Var S=Var S'=I/3, Cov(S,S')=I/9.

The ordinary multivariate CLT applies to these bounded iid site pairs. Since
0<A<1, p is bounded continuous. Therefore t_N(Q_N^2) tends to
E[p(S)p(S')]/(E p(S))^2 for that jointly Gaussian pair.

3. Gaussian conditioning identifies all three Hilbert-Schmidt terms.

In the Gaussian model from step 1,

 S|X=x ~ N((2/3)x,(2/9)I).

Direct Gaussian integration, with the proposed alpha and h, gives

 E[p(S)|X=x]=L(A,b) exp[-A|x|^2-bx_z],
 L(A,b)=(1-A)^(3/2) exp[b^2/(4(1-A))].

For explicit checks, the conditioning denominator is
1+2 alpha(2/9)=1/(1-A); the quadratic coefficient is
alpha(4/9)(1-A)=A, and the linear coefficient is h(2/3)(1-A)=-b.
Thus the constants in the proposed ensemble are correct.

Let g(x)=exp[-A|x|^2-bx_z] and Z=E_X g(X), X~N(0,I/4).
Then E p(S)=L Z. The Gaussian pair in step 2 can be realized by drawing X and
then drawing S,S' conditionally independently from the displayed S|X law:
their cross covariance is (4/9)(I/4)=I/9. Therefore

 lim t_N(Q_N^2)=E_X[g(X)^2]/Z^2.

The function exp[-A C-bZ] is bounded continuous on the closed support cone
C>=Z^2: its upper bound is exp[b^2/(4A)]. It has a bounded continuous extension
to the ambient space. The moment lemma consequently gives

 Z_N -> Z,
 lim t_N(q_N^2)=E_X[g(X)^2]/Z^2,
 lim t_N(Q_N q_N)=
 E[p(S)g(X)]/[(E p(S))Z]=E_X[g(X)^2]/Z^2.

Here the cross trace identity is simply
t_N(Q_N g_N)=E[p(S_N) Tr(omega_R g_N)]/E p(S_N),
which is precisely an integral against the positive joint spectral measure in
step 1. No noncommuting functional calculus approximation is required.

It follows that t_N((Q_N-q_N)^2)->0. Normalized Cauchy-Schwarz now gives

 ||sigma_N-tau_N||_1=t_N(|Q_N-q_N|)
 <= sqrt(t_N((Q_N-q_N)^2)) -> 0.

This is the missing strong-topology step. It controls the full density matrix,
including all multiplicity spaces, with no dimension penalty.

For convergent parameter sequences A_N,b_N, p_N converges uniformly on R^3 to p,
and g_N as scalar functions converges uniformly on C>=Z^2 to g. Indeed the
eventual quadratic coefficients are uniformly positive and the linear
coefficients bounded, giving uniformly decaying tails; compact convergence
then gives global uniform convergence. The preceding limits therefore remain
valid. No uniform statement as A approaches 1 is needed or claimed.

EXACT EULER DECOMPOSITION AND FINITE SEPARABLE MIXTURE

The exponential sum S is finite almost surely because E S=sum_n 1/(pi^2 n^2)
=1/6. It has no atoms, since E_1 has a density and is independent of the rest.
Euler's hyperbolic-sine product yields, for u>=0,

 E exp[-u^2 S]=product_{n>=1}(1+u^2/(pi^2 n^2))^(-1)=u/sinh u.

For the spin-j block the relative eigenvalue of the assigned stationary state is

 2^N rho_N(j,m)=(2j+1)e^(-beta m)/Z_j,
 Z_j=sinh[(j+1/2)beta]/sinh(beta/2).

Since (j+1/2)^2=j(j+1)+1/4, this proves the exact operator identity

 2^N rho_N=C_beta int exp[-s beta^2/4]
                 exp[-s beta^2 J^2-beta J_z] mu(ds),
 C_beta=sinh(beta/2)/(beta/2).

All integrands are positive; the identity includes the j=0 case by continuity.
Define b_N=sqrt(N) beta_N, A_N(s)=s N beta_N^2, and

 K_N(s)=C_beta_N exp[-s beta_N^2/4] Z_N(A_N(s),b_N).

Then K_N>=0, int K_N dmu=1, and exactly
rho_N=int K_N(s) tau_N(A_N(s),b_N) mu(ds).

For every s>0, A_N(s)->s/c^2, b_N->1/c. The tracial CLT above implies
K_N(s)->K_b(s), with
K_b(s)=(1+s b^2/2)^(-3/2)exp[b^2/(8+4s b^2)].
The limiting weights also have total mass one. One direct verification is
to draw X~N(0,I/4) and use Euler plus spherical symmetry:

 int K_b(s) mu(ds)
 =E_X[e^(-bX_z) b|X|/sinh(b|X|)]
 =E_|X|[(sinh(b|X|)/(b|X|)) b|X|/sinh(b|X|)]=1.

Thus Scheffe's lemma gives int |K_N-K_b| dmu ->0. This supplies all mixture
tightness/domination required; an unjustified uniform integrable bound in s is
unnecessary.

For each finite N and each 0<s<c^2, A_N(s)<s/c^2<1 because log(1+x)<x.
Use the positive ensemble sigma_N(A_N(s),b_N) on that interval. Let
delta_N=int_{s>=c^2} K_N(s) mu(ds), and define the explicit separable state

 Sigma_N=int_{0<s<c^2} K_N(s) sigma_N(A_N(s),b_N) mu(ds)
          +delta_N I/2^N.

It is normalized, positive and fully separable. Convexity of trace distance
gives

 D(rho_N,Sigma_N)
 <=int_{0<s<c^2} K_N(s)
      D(tau_N(A_N(s),b_N),sigma_N(A_N(s),b_N)) mu(ds)+delta_N.

For each fixed s in that interval, the strong approximation tends to zero.
The distance is at most one. Replacing K_N by K_b costs at most
int|K_N-K_b|; ordinary dominated convergence then makes the integral vanish.
Also delta_N->delta(c). Since mu has no atom at c^2, using > or >= is equivalent.
There is no need to define the divergent ensemble parameters at A=1 or prove
uniform convergence near that boundary.

TAIL BOUND

Write S=E_1+T. Independence and a telescoping product give

 E exp[pi^2 T]=product_{n>=2} n^2/(n^2-1)=2.

Conditioning on T gives P(S>a)<=e^(-pi^2 a) E exp[pi^2 T]
=2e^(-pi^2 a). The inequality also holds when T>a, because then
1<=exp[pi^2(T-a)]. K_b(s) is decreasing in s, so

 delta(c)<=K_b(c^2)P(S>c^2)
 <=2(2/3)^(3/2)exp[-pi^2 c^2+1/(12c^2)].

Independent numerical quadrature at c=1 gives
delta(1)=5.81897138068e-5; the analytic upper bound is 6.12025593817e-5.
The numerical value is finite-tested, not an interval-certified constant.

BOUNDARIES, COST CONTRACT, AND NON-CLAIMS

- c must be fixed and positive in the N limit. No joint c_N,N rate is proved.
- The fixed-component theorem uses 0<A<1. The mixing law has no mass at A=0
  or A=1; those endpoints need no extra construction for the stated result.
- sigma_N has intrinsic quantum covariance I/6: the mixed Gaussian conditional
  law X|S has mean S/2 and covariance I/6. This explains the A<1 cutoff exactly.
- The result is an upper bound on distance to SEP, not exact separability,
  optimality of the cutoff, or a lower bound.
- There is no finite-N error rate. The p weights can become extremely costly
  to sample near A=1. Efficient sampling or a preparation protocol is unproved.
- The approximation concerns the bath density matrix. It does not preserve
  any particular purification, claim a diamond norm, or validate local-lattice
  bath behavior.
- No originating proof was consulted. The central lemma and L2 route were
  independently derived from the supplied construction and state definition.
- A narrow literature search found adjacent quantum LAN work. Its abstract is
  not a proof dependency and does not settle priority for this construction.
