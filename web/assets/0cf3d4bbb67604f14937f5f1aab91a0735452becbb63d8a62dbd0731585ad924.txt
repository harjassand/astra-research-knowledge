A01 — rare-event estimation: local acquisition, hidden-set lower bound, and clock obstruction
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: finite bounded investigation; no claim of novelty or general breakthrough.

## Exact objective and query model

Estimate p = P_s(τ_B < τ_F) with relative error ε and confidence 1−η from a stochastic process whose direct hit probability p may be exponentially small. A sample call is one transition generated by the simulator. The key distinction is whether the algorithm can reset/query a local transition row at a chosen state, or only start whole trajectories from s. “Simulation-only” without this access distinction is not a sufficiently specified complexity model.

## Result A: a hard lower bound without structure

Let M≥2 and let the terminal set be an unknown S⊂[M]. Consider two model classes: H_1 draws S uniformly among all singletons; H_2 draws S uniformly among all two-element sets. A run picks i uniformly from [M] and then reaches B iff i∈S, so p_1=1/M and p_2=2/M. Give the estimator unusually strong access: it may choose any i adaptively and query 1{i∈S} at unit cost. This includes every proposal sampler and every splitting rule whose only feedback about S is terminal membership.

Let an algorithm make at most m queries. Condition on its random tape and consider the all-negative transcript. The queried set Q on that branch is identical under H_1 and H_2. The two success intervals for relative error ε<1/3 are disjoint:

I_1=[(1−ε)/M,(1+ε)/M],   I_2=[2(1−ε)/M,2(1+ε)/M].

On an all-negative transcript the algorithm can be correct for at most one hypothesis. The probability of ever getting a positive answer is at most m/M under H_1 and 2m/M under H_2. Therefore the average success probability over the two hypotheses is at most

  1/2 + (Pr_{H_1}[hit]+Pr_{H_2}[hit])/2 ≤ 1/2 + 3m/(2M).

If the estimator succeeds with probability at least 2/3 on both classes, then m≥M/9. Taking M=2^n yields Ω(1/p) adaptive oracle calls. The bound applies even when proposals can name arbitrary states; no importance-sampling or splitting adaptation can beat it without an additional source of information. The missing ingredient must be a structural promise that makes relevant event states or local conditional laws learnable.

Metastability can be added by inserting a hub self-loop with probability 1−λ before the uniform draw. The hitting probability remains |S|/M, but an ordinary trajectory then also pays mean waiting time 1/λ. This separates event discovery from metastable clock cost.

## Result B: exact cost of speeding a geometric metastable clock

Suppose a path first waits K∼Geom(λ), with P(K=k)=λ(1−λ)^{k−1}, then makes an informative transition. The event depends only on the post-wait outcome. An importance proposal changes the clock to K∼Geom(ρ). Its clock likelihood ratio is

  r(k)= λ(1−λ)^{k−1}/[ρ(1−ρ)^{k−1}].

The clock contribution to the second moment is

  R_K = E_ρ[r(K)^2]
      = λ²/ρ · Σ_{k≥1} ((1−λ)²/(1−ρ))^{k−1}
      = λ²(1−ρ) / [ρ(2λ−λ²−ρ)],

provided ρ<2λ−λ². If ρ≥2λ−λ², the series diverges and the likelihood-ratio second moment is infinite. For λ→0 and ρ=cλ with 0<c<2, R_K→1/[c(2−c)], minimized at c=1 and diverging as c→2. Thus any clock tilt with bounded second moment has ρ=O(λ) and expected unit-step calls E_ρ K=1/ρ=Ω(1/λ). Forcing the metastable process to leave much faster creates infinite or unbounded weight variance.

A sufficient way to remove this cost is access to an embedded jump-kernel oracle, or an exact analytic marginalization of self-loops. If the event is eventual reachability and does not depend on holding times, the embedded chain has the same hitting probability. With only a one-step simulator, acquiring one non-self transition takes expected 1/λ calls, so the jump interface is a real structural assumption and must be charged. This reduction is invalid for finite-horizon or clock-sensitive events.

## Result C: a locally acquired relative-error method for a sparse layered chain

This is a concrete structural promise under which acquisition need not scale like 1/p. It does not assume a supplied committor.

Model: states 0,1,…,T plus failure F and success B. From state i<T, the chain advances to i+1 with unknown probability θ_i∈[α,1] and otherwise enters F. State T enters B. The query interface can independently reset to any i and draw one transition from that row, at unit cost. The holding-time issue above is absent or already stutter-erased. The target probability is

  p = ∏_{i=0}^{T−1} θ_i,

which can be α^T and hence exponentially small in T. The state order is a known acyclic reaction coordinate, but the free-energy increments −log θ_i are unknown and are acquired from local row samples.

For one replicate, take N independent samples from each row i, let \hat θ_i be its empirical advance frequency, and set \hat p=∏_i \hat θ_i. The row samples are independent and unbiased. Exact calculation gives

  E[\hat p]=p,
  E[\hat p²]/p² = ∏_i (1 + (1−θ_i)/(N θ_i))
                     ≤ (1 + 1/(Nα))^T
                     ≤ exp(T/(Nα)).

For 0<ε≤1, choose N=ceil(10T/(α ε²)). Then exp(T/(Nα))−1≤ε²/8, so Chebyshev gives P(|\hat p−p|>εp)≤1/8 for one replicate. Take k independent replicates and return their median, rounding k up to an odd integer. Hoeffding's bound gives failure probability at most exp(−9k/32), so k=ceil((32/9)log(1/η)) rounded up to odd suffices. Total local transition calls are

  T N k = O(T²/(α ε²) · log(1/η)),

with O(T) counters and no mixing cost. This is polynomial and independent of p=∏θ_i. The estimator is a product of locally estimated free-energy increments; it never waits for an end-to-end success path. Exact rational output is possible: each replicate is ∏ c_i/N^T, requiring O(T log N) bits, so there is no floating-point underflow. Log-domain output is also valid if each log factor is computed with total absolute error ≤ε/4.

The locally estimated reaction coordinate is \hat q(i)=∏_{j=i}^{T−1}\hat θ_j. It is not assumed in advance. For a known exact kernel, q(i)=∏_{j=i}^{T−1}θ_j is the exact committor and its Doob transform advances with probability one, giving a zero-variance path estimator. For sample-only unknown θ_i, the valid estimator is the plug-in product above; I do not claim an unbiased Doob estimator for the true model because its exact edge likelihoods are not available. This distinction prevents a supplied-versus-acquired gap from being hidden.

### Matching acquisition lower bound for the serial family

For α≤1/4 and ε≤1/4, compare θ_i=α for every row with θ_i'=α(1+s), s=4ε/T. The two target probabilities have ratio (1+s)^T≥1+4ε, so their relative-ε output intervals are disjoint. Every local transition query is Bernoulli(α) under the first model and Bernoulli(α(1+s)) under the second. Since α(1+s)≤1/2,

  D(Ber(α) || Ber(α(1+s))) ≤ χ² ≤ 2αs² = 32α ε²/T².

By the adaptive chain rule, m queries give transcript KL at most 32mαε²/T². A relative-ε estimator succeeding with probability 2/3 on both models yields a test with TV at least 1/3; Pinsker then requires transcript KL≥2/9. Hence

  m ≥ T²/(144 α ε²).

So the T²/(αε²) acquisition order is minimax-sharp up to confidence factors on this family. It can beat crude path simulation, whose relative-error cost is Θ(1/(p ε²)) trajectories, by an exponential factor when p=α^T. Reset access is essential: without it, reaching late rows from the source may itself cost 1/prefix-probability.

### Baseline comparison with equal structural information

Crude Monte Carlo needs Θ(1/(p ε²)·log(1/η)) full trajectories. In this serial model, adaptive splitting given the same stage coordinate and reset/conditional-start access estimates the same factors θ_i; the product variance is also controlled by summing the per-level relative variances, giving the same Θ(T²/(αε²)) order. Thus this is a model-based local-acquisition route, not a demonstrated asymptotic improvement over equally informed splitting. With a known exact kernel, exact Doob sampling has zero variance, but the exact kernel also lets backward DP compute p directly. For a general explicit DAG, the conservative rowwise relative-Chernoff construction below has higher dependence on |V| and T; it is not claimed optimal.

## Extension: explicit sparse acyclic graph

Let G=(V,E) be a known finite DAG of height at most T with absorbing success and failure sets, source s, and local resettable transition sampling. Assume every positive edge has probability at least α. Sample each nonterminal row N times and use empirical probabilities \hat P_e. Multiplicative Chernoff and a union bound imply, with probability at least 1−η, all positive edges satisfy |\hat P_e/P_e−1|≤δ whenever

  N ≥ 3/(αδ²) · log(2|E|/η).

On this event, every successful path π of length at most T has \hat P(π)/P(π)∈[(1−δ)^T,(1+δ)^T]. Since the reachability probability is a positive sum of successful path weights, the dynamic-programming value \hat p obeys the same ratio bounds. Setting δ=ε/(2T) yields (1−ε)p≤\hat p≤(1+ε)p. Total calls are at most

  |V| · ceil(12 T²/(α ε²) · log(2|E|/η)),

plus O(|V|+|E|) preprocessing and O(|E|) backward DP arithmetic. The CI is [\hat p/(1+ε), \hat p/(1−ε)]. Exact rational DP or log-domain arithmetic avoids underflow; with exact rational empirical edge probabilities, bit lengths are polynomial in T log N. This is independent of p, which may be as small as α^T.

The graph size is part of the cost. This method does not apply to a succinctly encoded exponential state space, a continuous state model, or a recurrent system whose SCCs have not been reduced. The hidden-subset lower bound has |V|=Θ(M), so it does not contradict this result. The sparse-DAG theorem is a derivation of a standard model-based verification idea with multiplicative rather than additive error accounting; its novelty is unresolved.

## Prior-art check (primary sources; no priority claim)

1. Frédéric Cérou, Bernard Delyon, Arnaud Guyader, Mathias Rousset, “On the Asymptotic Normality of Adaptive Multilevel Splitting,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08494 (2018). The paper proves consistency and a CLT for AMS in a general Markov-process setting. AMS is therefore a strong existing splitting baseline; the present serial theorem does not beat it when both receive the same stage coordinate and conditional reset access.
2. Mansur Arief et al., “Certifiable Deep Importance Sampling for Rare-Event Simulation of Black-Box Systems,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02204 (2021). Deep-PrAE supplies efficiency/bound certificates under dominating-point and orthogonal-monotonicity structure; it explicitly addresses the danger of missed modes. This supports the need for an inspectable structural promise, not a universal black-box claim.
3. Pranav Ashok, Jan Křetínský, Maximilian Weininger, “PAC Statistical Model Checking for Markov Decision Processes and Stochastic Games,” https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04403v3 (2021). It gives PAC additive bounds for unbounded reachability, model-based confidence updates, and black/grey-box variants; the algorithm stops when U(s0)−L(s0)<ε. It does not state a relative-error guarantee for a probability p≪ε. Our DAG multiplicative path argument is a specialized relative-error statement under explicit topology, bounded height, reset access, and a positive-edge floor. This is adjacent prior art, not evidence of novelty.
4. Hugo Bazille et al., “Global PAC Bounds for Learning Discrete Time Markov Chains,” https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-53291-8_17 (CAV 2020). It studies global additive behavior-probability error for learned DTMCs, including reachability/time-to-failure results, and documents the difficulty caused by rare states. It reinforces that local row accuracy alone does not give a useful uniform global bound without structural assumptions.
5. For a zero-variance benchmark: Thomas E. Booth, “Zero-Variance Solutions for Linear Monte Carlo,” https://doi.org/10.13182/NSE89-A23646 (1989), establishes the expected-score change of measure; more generally the committor/Doob transform is the known ideal proposal. This is a benchmark, not a new claim.

## Rejected or limited mechanisms

- Uniformly adaptive black-box proposals: refuted by the hidden-subset lower bound.
- Accelerating a geometric trap clock with ordinary path-space IS: finite second moment requires ρ<2λ−λ²; faster tilting has divergent variance.
- “Learn the committor from successful trajectories”: not a proof of polynomial acquisition; the hidden subset model has no useful successes until Ω(1/p) queries.
- Assuming a score with the answer encoded: invalid as an acquisition result. The serial chain instead exposes local transition outcomes under resets and estimates every free-energy increment explicitly.
- Extending the DAG bound to arbitrary metastable recurrent chains: unresolved. A claim requires an SCC/reversible-network reduction and a bound on acquisition/mixing for its jump kernel.

## Claim ledger

- INTERNALLY DERIVED: adaptive hidden-subset lower bound m≥M/9; geometric clock second-moment formula and infinite-variance threshold; product-chain relative-error upper bound; matching T²/(αε²) lower bound; sparse-DAG multiplicative error bound.
- FINITE-TESTED: see diagnostics.py, which checks the geometric-series identity and compares a small product-chain instance against exact arithmetic. It does not prove any theorem.
- KNOWN: zero-variance Doob proposal, AMS asymptotic theory, and additive PAC model-checking results cited above.
- UNRESOLVED: novelty relative to existing model-based reachability algorithms; extensions beyond resettable finite DAGs; a nontrivial improvement over equal-information splitting on the serial chain; finite-bit sampling error for continuous simulators.
- REFUTED: a universal simulation-only polynomial relative-error guarantee; aggressive geometric-clock tilting with finite second moment.

Strongest honest consequence: structure must be acquired locally, not inferred from rare successes. For a resettable product/layered chain, local row samples estimate an exponentially small product with a minimax-sharp polynomial call bound, while a hidden unstructured target provably needs Ω(1/p) active queries. The metastable holding clock can be removed only through a genuine jump-kernel or exact stutter-erasure interface. This is a scoped capability boundary and exact method, not a claimed historic breakthrough.
