## Stabilizer syndrome isometry

Let `C` be an `[[n,k,d]]` stabilizer code with stabilizer group `S`, code projector `P_C`, and encoding isometry `Enc:H_L -> H_D`. Pauli operators are taken modulo scalar phase when measuring weight or syndrome. Write `sigma(P)` for the stabilizer syndrome and

`rw(P) := min_{S0 in S} wt(P S0)`

for stabilizer-reduced Pauli weight. This quotient is by stabilizers only; a nontrivial zero-syndrome logical Pauli has reduced weight at least `d`, not zero.

For an integer `r` with `2r<d`, let `Sigma_r` be the set of syndromes attained by a Pauli of reduced weight at most `r`. Choose one physical Pauli `R_s` with `wt(R_s)<=r` for each `s in Sigma_r`, with `R_0=I`. Define an abstract syndrome space `S_r` with orthonormal basis `{|s>:s in Sigma_r}` and

`J(|psi> tensor |s>) := R_s Enc|psi> .`

This is an isometry: different syndrome sectors are orthogonal. Also, if two Pauli representatives of weight at most `r` have the same syndrome, their product is a zero-syndrome Pauli of weight at most `2r<d`, hence a stabilizer up to phase. The representative choice therefore changes only a syndrome-dependent scalar on the logical code.

## Uniform good-history factorization theorem

Consider a complete recovery history with ideal Clifford syndrome extraction, measurement outcomes retained in a unitary dilation, a reversible classical decoder (which may be nonlinear), controlled Pauli correction, and system-local fault operators expanded in Pauli/matrix-unit terms with arbitrary operator coefficients on one common bath. Keep all record, reset, and measurement-purifier registers until the operator identity is established.

Fix input and output syndrome sets `Sigma_in` and `Sigma_out`, with representatives `R_s` and `R_t`. A fully expanded history term has a definite data Pauli `E` for each computational-basis record transition and a bath-operator coefficient `B` in the original time order. Suppose for every input syndrome `s`, every output sector `t` reached by that term, and every such record/matrix-unit term:

1. The output data is in syndrome sector `t`, so `sigma(E)+s=t`.
2. There is no leakage outside the stated output syndrome space.
3. The zero-syndrome Pauli

   `Q := R_t^dagger E R_s`

   has `rw(Q)<d`.

Then `Q` is a stabilizer up to phase, so

`E R_s Enc|psi> = omega(E,s,t) R_t Enc|psi>`

for every logical vector `|psi>`. Collecting record transitions, syndrome-dependent phases, garbage, and bath coefficients gives an operator `g_F` such that

`A_F (J_in tensor I_B) = (J_out tensor I_aux) (I_L tensor g_F)`.

Here `aux` includes retained records and garbage as well as the bath. In particular, the logical action is the identity even when the input is entangled across `L`, syndrome, and bath. A sufficient numerical condition for item 3 is `wt(E)+wt(R_s)+wt(R_t)<d`; a pathwise bound `rw(Q)<=r_*<d/2` is also sufficient. The task’s `r_*<d/2` is a conservative margin; for the zero-syndrome `Q`, `<d` already excludes a logical class.

**Proof.** The syndrome relation makes `Q` commute with every stabilizer. By the definition of distance, every zero-syndrome Pauli outside `S` has stabilizer-reduced weight at least `d`. Thus `rw(Q)<d` forces `Q` into `S` up to phase. Stabilizers act as scalar on `Enc(H_L)`, yielding the displayed equation termwise. Every coherent sum of good terms therefore has the same `I_L` tensor factor; its amplitudes and interference only change `g_F`. No path amplitudes are replaced by probabilities, and no bath operators are commuted or tensor-factorized.

### Reversible nonlinear decoder and coherent records

One must not propagate Pauli faults through Toffoli or other nonlinear decoder gates as though those gates were Clifford. The safe expansion is in matrix units on the computational-basis controller registers. Any operator on a finite controller block is a sum of `|a><b| tensor B_ab`; a reversible Boolean truth table sends each basis string to a basis string and therefore sends matrix units to matrix units. A controlled Pauli correction, on a definite basis transition, contributes a definite Pauli on data. Expand a joint data/controller fault in a Pauli basis on data and matrix units on controller wires. Repeating this at each location yields the data Pauli `E` and bath coefficient `B` used above.

For a common bath, coefficients are multiplied in chronological order. Noncommuting coefficients are allowed. The factorization proof uses no coefficient-norm summation: once each expanded system term is logical identity on `L`, the ordered operator sum remains `I_L tensor g_F`.

A bounded-depth classical circuit gives a bounded output-difference cone only with bounded gate arity/fan-in and fan-out, or another explicit causal-cone bound. For the QTC fixed-iteration decoder, A04 v2 §§3–4 gives bounded local views and bounded fan-in/fan-out, so a marked controller fault changes only the union of constant-size output cones. Its truth tables can be implemented with constant-size reversible blocks; faults inside a block may be moved to its boundary as arbitrary operators on the block’s fixed wires. The cone/support constants must then be included in the recurrence. Depth alone, without a fan-in/fan-out or cone hypothesis, does not suffice.

## What the recurrence does and does not imply

The numerical recurrence `r_{j+1}<=alpha r_j+K f_j` plus `r_*<d/2` implies the theorem only when its `r_j` is a pathwise stabilizer-reduced residual bound that is uniform over every input syndrome basis state in `J`, every allowed controller basis transition, and every Pauli/matrix-unit term. For input `J_s`, the decoder analysis must include `R_s` as part of the error relative to the code. Equivalently, after the complete correction the branch `E R_s` must have reduced weight below `d/2`; its syndrome has an output representative `R_t` of weight below `d/2`, so `R_t^dagger E R_s` is a zero-syndrome Pauli of weight below `d` and is a stabilizer. The QTC decoder theorem is applicable to the combined data Pauli `R_s` times the fault-induced data Pauli, provided its size/syndrome-noise premise holds for every `s` and every expanded branch. The initial radius must be included in those premises; a convenient invariant is `r_j<=alpha^j r_in + K tau n (1-alpha^j)/(1-alpha)` with the maximum below the QTC input limit and `d/2`.

A recurrence checked only on encoded inputs (`s=0`) proves the encoded-input statement

`A_F (Enc tensor I_B) = (J_out tensor I_aux) (I_L tensor g_F)`

when every output Pauli has stabilizer-reduced weight below `d/2`. That is enough for the memory metric in the sibling’s `work/sol/quantum_faults/v1.txt` Eq. (8), which starts from `Enc(H_L)`. It does not prove that the same map is `I_L tensor g_F` on the whole `J` domain. The missing quantifier is uniformity over input syndrome sectors, and the missing quantity is the residual *relative to both representatives*, `R_t^dagger E R_s`.

## Exact finite counterexample to the unqualified subsystem extension

Use the standard five-qubit `[[5,1,3]]` code with stabilizers `XZZXI`, `IXZZX`, `XIXZZ`, `ZXIXZ`; one logical `X` representative is `L=IYYIX` (equivalent to `XXXXX` by a stabilizer). All 16 syndromes have unique representatives of weight at most one, so take `r=1` and `2r=2<d=3`; `J` is an isometry (in fact it spans the physical Hilbert space).

Choose input syndrome representative `R_s=IZIII` (syndrome `0101`), apply the single-qubit fault `P=IIXII` (X on qubit 3) before ideal syndrome extraction, and let the decoder correct the observed output syndrome `t=1001` with `R_t=IIIZI` (Z on qubit 4). Exact symplectic enumeration gives

`R_t^dagger P R_s = IZXZI`,

which has weight 3, zero syndrome, and is not a stabilizer; multiplying it by `L=IYYIX` gives a stabilizer. Hence on this input syndrome sector, the complete ideal-measurement/correction history acts as logical `X` while mapping syndrome `s` to `t`. On encoded input, the same fault `P` has its own unique weight-one syndrome representative `P`, so the ideal decoder applies `P` and the logical action is `I`.

Thus the code-sector path has residual zero (and satisfies any code-sector recurrence whose actual residual is bounded by its right-hand side), but the induced action on `L tensor S_1` has nontrivial logical holonomy. The boundary is sharp: the three factors `R_t`, `P`, `R_s` can multiply to a minimum logical operator because `wt(R_t)+wt(P)+wt(R_s)=d`. The check script `work/applied/a23_quantum_syndrome/exact_check.py` exhaustively verifies the stabilizer distance, all 16 unique single-error syndromes, the syndrome transition, and the logical coset.

The valid repair is either (a) state and prove the theorem only on the encoded input sector, as the sibling memory construction does, or (b) require the corrected recurrence uniformly for all `J` input sectors, count `R_s` in the initial residual and final QTC premise, and force every `R_t^dagger E R_s` to have reduced weight `<d`. One cannot infer (b) from (a).

## Audit of the sibling’s good-path construction

The sibling v1 §§2–4 retains measurement/readout purifiers and reset garbage, uses an operator-norm aggregate noise hypothesis on the full system, and starts its Eq. (8) at `Enc(H_L)`. Its equation (7) is the right encoded-input argument if the pathwise residual is stabilizer-reduced weight (the QTC source defines it that way), not merely syndrome weight. Operator-valued bath coefficients and branch phases may be absorbed into `g_T`; this part does not require independent bath components. To make the nonlinear-control argument transparent in v2, expand controller operations in matrix units and only then use basis-controlled Pauli corrections; do not claim Toffoli preserves Pauli errors. A constant-size local truth-table block and a bounded output cone justify the support constants, with location labels and bath order preserved.

The sibling v1 explicitly distinguishes its final ideal decoder from noisy physical readout. That distinction is necessary: the ideal final decoder defines the logical memory metric, while a claim about actual logical measurement/readout requires including those physical operations and their faults in the same location/fault model. The source QTC paper itself describes a final `O(log n)` decoder and final physical `Z`-readout procedure at HTML lines 265–267; that procedure is a separate cost/noise interface from the constant-depth middle rounds.

## Claim ledger

- **Source-reported:** QTC single-shot deterministic Pauli bound under its stated reduced-weight and syndrome-error promise (Gu et al., Theorem 4.20); this is not an operator-norm result.
- **Internally proved:** syndrome-representative isometry and the uniform sector-by-sector factorization lemma above.
- **Finite-tested:** the `[[5,1,3]]` logical-holonomy example, checked by exact GF(2) enumeration in `exact_check.py`.
- **Refuted as insufficient:** an encoded-input recurrence does not imply factorization on a larger syndrome-subsystem domain.
- **Unresolved:** whether a particular compiled QTC noisy decoder satisfies the stronger uniform-sector recurrence, and whether the all-hardware AKP aggregate condition is certified for a physical readout/controller stack. This note does not prove an end-to-end shared-bath QTC threshold.
