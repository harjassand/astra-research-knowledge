## Claim and exact scope

Under the same nonnegative row/complemented-column half-edge convention as the
XXZ construction, a finite tensor-network gadget built from nonzero homogeneous
Lorentzian local signatures and Lorentzian bond signatures has a Lorentzian
effective boundary signature whenever its internal contraction is implemented
by fixed nonnegative coefficient extraction. Therefore it cannot realize, up
to a positive scalar, a standard two-qubit XXZ edge gate with
`|gamma| > alpha` as its effective boundary signature. The obstruction does
not depend on gadget size, local degree, support size, or coefficient bit
length, so polynomial size/cost cannot repair this particular route.

This covers the standard quadratic easy-plane XXZ Euler/exponential gates
(which are Lorentzian inside the cone), the homogeneous Lorentzian projector
gates in the attributed higher-spin construction, their finite products, and
ancilla/bond gadgets that remain inside this Lorentzian class.
It does **not** cover a merely log-concave but not Lorentzian high-degree
ancilla tensor, a signed/complex cancellation, a non-Lorentzian bond, a
non-coefficient contraction, or an encoding whose final effective signature
is not the target half-edge polynomial. In particular it is not a no-go for
all tensor representations or all algorithms for easy-axis XXZ.

## Closure lemma and proof

Let each gadget vertex `v` carry a nonzero homogeneous Lorentzian polynomial
`p_v` in the variables of its incident half-edges, and let each internal bond
carry a nonnegative Lorentzian matching polynomial (in the two-state
convention, `x_i + x_j`). Form the product `F` of all vertex and bond
polynomials, identifying half-edge variables as specified by the tensor
contraction. The product is Lorentzian: Brändén--Huh prove that homogeneous
Lorentzian/strongly-log-concave polynomials are closed under multiplication;
their proof handles shared variables by first taking disjoint variable copies
and then a nonnegative diagonal substitution.

If the internal contraction fixes a multi-index `a` on auxiliary variables
`y`, its output is

    [y^a] F(x,y) = (1/a!) (partial_y^a F)(x,0).

Every nonzero partial derivative of a Lorentzian polynomial is Lorentzian by
the defining derivative recursion. Setting auxiliary variables to zero is a
nonnegative linear variable substitution and preserves Lorentzianity. A
positive scalar factor is immaterial. Thus every nonzero effective boundary
polynomial produced by these operations is Lorentzian. The same closure theorem
allows any nonnegative linear change of boundary variables, so splitting,
merging, or coarse-graining variables through a nonnegative matrix does not
evade the conclusion.

The relevant primary result is Brändén--Huh, *Lorentzian polynomials*,
arXiv:1902.03719v8: Theorem 2.10 (nonnegative linear changes of variables),
Theorem 2.30 (equivalence of homogeneous Lorentzian and strongly/completely
log-concave), and Corollary 2.32 (product closure). The derivative closure is
also explicit in their recursive definition (Definition 2.6). This is a
source theorem, not a new claim of this worker.

## XXZ contradiction

For one edge, write `s > 0` for the rational Euler step and permit an arbitrary
scalar identity shift `kappa`. In the computational basis, the three
six-vertex weights are

    a = 1 + s(kappa + gamma),
    b = 1 + s(kappa - gamma),
    c = 2 s alpha.

The complemented-column homogeneous boundary polynomial is

    q = a(x1*x2 + x3*x4) + b(x1*x4 + x2*x3)
        + c(x1*x3 + x2*x4).

The four eigenvalues of its constant Hessian are

    a+b+c,  a-b-c = 2s(gamma-alpha),
    -a+b-c = -2s(gamma+alpha),  -a-b+c.

For `alpha >= 0` and strict `gamma > alpha`, the first eigenvalue is positive
when the gate has positive coefficients, and `a-b-c > 0`; hence the Hessian
has at least two positive eigenvalues. For strict `gamma < -alpha`,
`a+b+c > 0` and `-a+b-c > 0`. Each contradicts the one-positive-eigenvalue
condition of a nonzero Lorentzian polynomial. The bad eigenvalue is independent
of `kappa`, so an identity shift cannot help. When coefficients are positive,
the same direction is tangent to `q` at `(1,1,1,1)`, giving positive
`D^2 log q`; the exact rational fixtures are in the frozen snapshot and its
script.

Consequently an effective gadget signature equal to `q` (or a positive scalar
multiple) cannot come from the Lorentzian-only construction above. This is a
structural closure argument, not a hardness result and not a failure of the
physical Trotter approximation. It says the resulting polynomial would fail
the Chen--Liu log-concavity premise before coefficient counting is attempted.

## Exact exponential gates also keep the same boundary

The obstruction is not an artifact of replacing an exponential by `I+sA`.
For `h=alpha(XX+YY)+gamma ZZ`, an arbitrary scalar shift `kappa I`, and
`t>0`, the exact gate `exp(t(kappa I+h))` has six-vertex weights

    a = exp(t(kappa+gamma)),
    b = exp(t(kappa-gamma)) cosh(2 alpha t),
    c = exp(t(kappa-gamma)) sinh(2 alpha t).

These follow by diagonalizing the `|01>,|10>` block. Its Hessian eigenvalue
in direction `(1,1,-1,-1)` is

    a-b-c = exp(t(kappa-gamma)) (exp(2 gamma t)-exp(2 alpha t)),

and in direction `(1,-1,-1,1)` is

    -a+b-c = exp(t(kappa-gamma)) (exp(-2 alpha t)-exp(2 gamma t)).

The radial eigenvalue `a+b+c` is strictly positive. Strict `gamma>alpha`
makes the first displayed anisotropy eigenvalue positive; strict
`gamma<−alpha` makes the second positive. This is exact for every `t>0`, not
just a small-step expansion, and the shift contributes only a positive common
factor. The remaining nonradial eigenvalue is
`-a-b+c = -exp(t(kappa-gamma)) (exp(2 gamma t)+exp(-2 alpha t)) < 0`.
Thus for `alpha>=0`, the exact exponential signature is Lorentzian for every
`t>0` exactly when `|gamma|<=alpha`; its local LC cone has the same sharp
boundary as the first-order gate. Therefore exact local Gibbs gates in the
standard orientation are also non-Lorentzian throughout the easy-axis region. A representation that
groups those gates into a larger object still must leave the all-Lorentzian
gadget class above to avoid its closure obstruction. In a finite product-form
Trotter word with distinct half-edge variables per occurrence, `log P` is a
sum of the local `log q_j`, so the positive local curvature remains a
positive block of the global log-Hessian. This extends the exact finite-word
obstruction from Euler gates to exact exponential edge gates in this same
product-signature representation; it does not rule out a different global
representation.

## Costs, status, and next decisive gate

The closure argument has no gadget-size budget: it applies to every finite
Lorentzian gadget, even if its degree/support/bit costs are exponential. It
therefore rules out a representation class rather than offering a costed
algorithm. The local Hessian witness is reproducible with constant-size exact
Fraction arithmetic by running
`python3 work/cycle6/c06_l05/local_gate_obstruction.py`; its output checks the
two strict sides, with Hessian positive eigenvalue `1/50` and log-Hessian
Rayleigh quotient `1/280` for each `±11/10` fixture. It does not verify the
general closure theorem or run the FPRAS.

Status: **VERIFIED_INTERNAL conditional structural no-go** for all-factors
Lorentzian gadgets using fixed positive-coefficient contraction. Imported
closure facts are established in Brändén--Huh; the physical tensor-to-polynomial
identification must match the stated half-edge convention. The main accepted
XXZ FPRAS remains conditional on Chen--Liu Theorem 5.1, with its large
polynomial arithmetic cost and no full implementation. Easy-axis physics is
not proved hard, and a high-degree tensor that is merely log-concave (not
strongly log-concave/Lorentzian) is a genuine unclosed possibility.

The next decisive observation is to test a proposed wider representation's
actual boundary polynomial and internal contraction against the lemma's exact
conditions. A route escaping it must identify which closure-preserving
resource is left—e.g. a non-Lorentzian high-degree block, signed/complex
cancellation, or a different counting primitive—and then prove that the
resulting **global** counting polynomial still satisfies the imported
algorithm's hypotheses with charged degree/support/bit/precision costs.
