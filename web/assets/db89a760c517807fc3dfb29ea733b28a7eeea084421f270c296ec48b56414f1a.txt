1. EXACT ABSTRACT INTERFACE

Let (X,nu) be a probability space, H a finite Hilbert space, and E_x a
measurable Hilbert bundle of fixed finite rank. Let A_x:E_x->H be measurable,
with all following integrals finite. Define

  F_x=A_x A_x^dag>=0,
  T=int_X F_x dnu(x),
  S(B)=int_X F_x B F_x dnu(x),
  G_(x,y)=A_x^dag A_y:E_y->E_x.                         (1.1)

Assume the TWO exact stationary normalizations

  int_X F_x^2 dnu(x)=I_H,
  A_x^dag T A_x=I_(E_x) for almost every x.             (1.2)

The first makes S unital and TP. The second makes the section partner
unital/TP. They are distinct assumptions; int F_x=I is not substituted.

Let k(x,y)>=0 be a symmetric probability kernel on X with
int k(x,y)dnu(y)=1. Assume its scalar operator K0 on L2(nu) is positive
and self-adjoint, hence has spectrum in [0,1]. Write

  h_t=ess_sup_(x,y) k^(t)(x,y)

for each positive integer t for which this is finite. Assume the polar
map G_(x,y)=D_(x,y)U_(x,y) has U unitary E_y->E_x and D>=0 on E_x, and

  D_(x,y)^2 >=gamma k(x,y) I_(E_x), gamma>0.            (1.3)

This LOWER singular-value hypothesis is stronger than an upper norm
overlap bound. Unitary transport can have arbitrary holonomy. Changes
on null sets do not matter. No scalar equality for S is assumed.

For 1<=Q<=dim H put

  Lambda_S(Q)=min_(rank B<=Q, ||B||_HS=1)
                           <B,(I-S)B>,
  eta=||T||_op^2.                                     (1.4)

Then 0<gamma<=1, and the exact abstract rank master is

  Lambda_S(Q) >=[1-eta Q h_t]_+/(1+t/gamma).             (1.5)

If, for real s>0 and an integer t_max>=1,

  h_t<=H/t^s, 1<=t<=t_max,                             (1.6)

then for 1<=Q<=t_max^s/(2H eta),

  Lambda_S(Q)>=
  gamma/[2((2H eta)^(1/s)+2)] Q^(-1/s).                (1.7)

These are bounds on rank on the full H, not rank in a chosen sector or
fiber. In applications, eta carries the stationary-volume normalization;
the product H eta must be retained rather than hidden inside a claimed
dimension-free constant.

2. POSITIVE REDUCTION AND THE SECTION FACTORIZATION

S is self-adjoint and positive on HS space because F_x>=0. By (1.2) it
is unital TP, so Kadison-Schwarz and trace preservation make it an HS
contraction; spec(S) is contained in [0,1]. For an arbitrary SVD
B=sum_i s_i |u_i><v_i|, the Hermitian-Kraus AM-GM identity gives

  <B,SB><=(1/2){<|B|,S|B|>+<|B^dag|,S|B^dag|>}.      (2.1)

For each x, expand in i,j and use 2 Re(z conjugate(w))<=|z|^2+|w|^2;
the square sums are exactly the two right terms. Both moduli are positive
and have the same rank and HS norm as B. Thus the compact rank-constrained
unit sphere has a positive minimizer F. Individual competing-channel
Kraus maps are not required to be positive.

Define R(B)(x)=A_x^dag B A_x and R^dag f=int A_x f(x) A_x^dag dnu(x).
Then S=R^dag R exactly, and K=R R^dag acts on sections as

  (Kf)(x)=int G_(x,y)f(y)G_(x,y)^dag dnu(y).             (2.2)

The second equation in (1.2) is exactly int GG^dag=I_(E_x). Exchanging
x,y gives the corresponding column identity. Therefore K is CP, unital
and TP for the integrated section trace. It is positive and self-adjoint
on L2 HS space, and its nonzero spectrum equals that of S. Hence

  E_K(f)=(1/2)int ||f(x)G_(x,y)-G_(x,y)f(y)||_HS^2,
  spec(K) subset [0,1].                                (2.3)

For a positive unit HS rank-Q minimizer F let f=R F and E=E_S(F). Then

  f(x)>=0, ||f||_L2HS^2=1-E,
  E_K(f)=<F,S(I-S)F><=E.                               (2.4)

3. EXACT HERMITIAN POLAR INEQUALITY

For Hermitian u,v and positive D,

  uD-Dv=(1/2){u-v,D}+(1/2)[u+v,D].

The first term is Hermitian and the second anti-Hermitian, orthogonal
in the real HS inner product. The anticommutator entry multiplier is
(d_i+d_j)/2>=d_min(D). Thus

  ||uD-Dv||_HS^2 >=d_min(D)^2 ||u-v||_HS^2.             (3.1)

Set v=U_(x,y)f(y)U_(x,y)^dag and u=f(x). From (1.3),(2.3),

  E_K(f)>=(gamma/2)int k(x,y)
                 ||f(x)-U_(x,y)f(y)U_(x,y)^dag||_HS^2.

For g(x)=||f(x)||_HS, reverse triangle gives

  E_K(f)>=gamma E_(K0)(g), ||g||_2^2=1-E.              (3.2)

This is a quadratic-form comparison through the scalar norm. It is not
pointwise semigroup domination for arbitrary complex sections, and does
not equate either operator spectrum. In particular it does not assert a
full group-Casimir lower when vertical connection labels are present.
Integrating GG^dag>=gamma k I proves gamma<=1.

4. STATIONARY TRACE WEIGHT AND RANK TO L1

Because f(x)>=0, its HS norm is at most its trace. Hence

  ||g||_1<=int Tr f(x)dnu(x)=Tr F T
     <=||T||_op Tr F<=sqrt(eta Q).                      (4.1)

The final inequality uses rank F<=Q and ||F||_HS=1. This is why the
positive reduction and the stationary T cannot be omitted. T need not
be scalar or commute with F.

For any positive integer t, 1-beta^t<=t(1-beta) on the spectrum of K0.
The nonnegative scalar kernel and (4.1) imply

  1-E=||g||_2^2
     <=t E_(K0)(g)+<g,K0^t g>
     <=t E/gamma+eta Q h_t.                            (4.2)

This proves (1.5). Under (1.6) take t=ceil((2H eta Q)^(1/s)). The stated
Q window makes t<=t_max and eta Q h_t<=1/2. As gamma<=1 and Q>=1,
t+gamma<=((2H eta)^(1/s)+2)Q^(1/s), proving (1.7).

5. MIXED PROJECTOR SECTORS AS AN EXACT INSTANTIATION

For finitely many H_lambda with Haar int P_(lambda,x)=I_lambda/a_lambda,
choose A_x=direct_sum_lambda a_lambda^(1/4) W_(lambda,x). Then

  F_x=direct_sum_lambda sqrt(a_lambda)P_(lambda,x),
  T=direct_sum_lambda I_lambda/sqrt(a_lambda),
  eta=1/a_min,
  A_x^dag T A_x=I_(E_x), int F_x^2=I_H.                (5.1)

Both stationary equations are exact even across rectangular sector maps.
If ||W_(lambda,v)^dag W_(lambda,w)|| is known only from above, (1.3)

  k(v,w)=d_M |<v,w>|^(2M), gamma=a_min/d_M,
