# Hostile proof audit: compact plateau and measurable trajectories

## Verdict

I found no counterexample or substantive gap in the audited section, conditional on the preceding activity corollary and weighted drift lemma, and on the stated finite-system/measurable-rate interface. The finite-minimum chain rule is correct almost everywhere, including ties. The plateau is compact and lies strictly inside the inner box. The invariance, continuation, and finite-entry conclusions follow once the standard Carathéodory local existence/uniqueness theorem is invoked with its hypotheses made explicit.

The result here is scoped to §§“Compact plateaux and absolutely continuous trajectories” (source lines 519–676). In particular, item 4 is conditional on a **global** solution staying in `B_h` from `t0` onward. This audit does not establish that an arbitrary solution ever reaches that box, does not re-audit the earlier affine-minimum construction or weighted-drift lemma, and does not certify the manuscript's full permanence theorem, novelty, or external correctness.

## Main checks

- At a point where `x` is differentiable, the active affine labels give the right directional derivative `min_{active λ} r_λ·ẋ`. Inactive labels have a positive gap and are excluded for sufficiently small positive increments. Since both `x` and `F_h∘x` are differentiable almost everywhere, this right derivative equals the ordinary derivative on a full-measure subset. At a tie with different active directional slopes, the left derivative is their maximum and the right derivative their minimum, so the composition is not differentiable there; such points do not invalidate the a.e. formula. Endpoints of `[s,t]` are irrelevant to an a.e. statement.
- At `c`, the scale condition `||log(c)/log(h)||∞ < H` contradicts the sign-and-margin conclusion for any active slope with a nonzero coordinate. Thus an active zero slope has value `M_h` globally, giving `F_h≤M_h`; the level set `{F_h=M_h}` is the intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces.
- A fixed zero-slope label of value `M_h` is active at every plateau point. For plateau points in `B_h`, the activity corollary applied to that label gives `||p||∞<E(0)=1/2`, hence the point lies in `int B_{sqrt(h)}`. Convexity and `c∈int B_h` exclude any plateau point outside `B_h`, since its segment from `c` would meet the forbidden boundary. Closedness plus this boundedness gives compactness.
- On solution segments, intersect the full-measure sets where the path derivative, `F_h∘x` derivative, ODE identity, and rate bounds hold. The drift lemma applies pointwise to every active slope and every allowed rate vector. Taking the finite minimum proves derivative `≥0`; outside the plateau in the class, the neutral-slope lemma upgrades this to `≥δ_h`. Absolute continuity permits integration.
- Invariance uses a first-exit argument from `B_h`: monotonicity and `F_h≤M_h` force `F_h=M_h` up to a proposed first boundary hit, contradicting the strict inner-box containment. The path then stays in compact `K_h`.
- Continuation is valid for measurable schedules: on each compact box `Q⊂R_{>0}^d`, the finite monomials and their state derivatives are bounded. Thus `f(t,·)` has a time-uniform bound and Lipschitz constant for a.e. `t`, while `f(·,x)` is measurable. Carathéodory local existence/uniqueness applies. On a finite maximal interval with path in `K_h`, the uniform speed bound gives a limit at the endpoint; closedness puts it in `K_h`, and the same local theorem from that endpoint extends the solution.
- For entry, `m_h=min_{B_h}F_h` exists, `m_h≤F_h(c)=M_h`, and `δ_h=κHh^M>0`, so `W_h=(M_h-m_h)/δ_h` is finite and nonnegative. If `W_h>0` and entry has not already occurred, integrate the strict drift up to the endpoint (or first take times increasing to it); the endpoint then has `F_h≥M_h`, hence equals `M_h` and is in the plateau. If `W_h=0`, `m_h=M_h` and `F_h≤M_h` imply `F_h=M_h` on all of `B_h`.

## Library-theorem boundary

The argument is not wholly self-contained in elementary analysis: it correctly relies on Carathéodory local existence/uniqueness for a measurable-in-time, locally Lipschitz-in-state vector field with an integrable local bound. The manuscript states the standard theorem in the preceding interface section. For this system its hypotheses follow directly from measurable bounded rates and smooth power laws on compact subsets of the positive orthant. The finite-time extension step is then proved in the section by a compactness/speed-bound argument; it need not be hidden inside a global continuation theorem.

No counterexample was found under the stated hypotheses. The strict positive lower rate bound `κ>0`, finite edge/label sets, positive compact plateau, and the preceding uniform activity/drift premises are load-bearing. Without them the displayed positive margin or the invoked ODE theorem need not follow.
