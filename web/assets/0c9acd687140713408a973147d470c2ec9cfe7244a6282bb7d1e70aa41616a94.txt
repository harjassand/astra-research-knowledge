COMBINATORICS AND OPTIMIZATION PROGRAMME
Date: 2026-10-08

DECISION

The highest-ceiling direction remains the exact-marginal hard-feature rounding
candidate for Boolean polytopes. Its favorable-good-vertex-to-exact-law wrapper
has a polynomial rational query bound, and one load-bearing curvature lemma
survived an independent exact audit. The full rational trajectory and compiler
were not executed, so the broad theorem remains an INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE,
not an externally validated advance. Its stated worst-case step cap is already
about 1.79e49 when k=2 and n=17.

Two scoped advances are banked. First, the exact-mean all-outcome radius obeys
R(P) >= k(P), improving the k(P)/2 lower bound recorded in P-round. Second,
the primary Ercan preprint claims a much smaller (<13.676647) hard radius for
strongly base-orderable matroid base polytopes, with arbitrary rational means
and a full convex-order comparison. That is SOURCE-REPORTED and has no
polynomial-time compiler established here. Recent Komlos signing results are
strong comparators for the cube midpoint, but do not supply arbitrary exact
marginals or face-feasible outputs.

The graph branch yields a clean conditional construction audit and transfer
limits. The distance filter preserves the claimed girth if the imported
Ramanujan source theorem holds. Graph girth and spectrum alone do not force an
evaluation-matroid relation cutoff; arbitrary labels can contain a bounded
degree affine-flat relation. High girth does imply distance in graph-derived
cycle/parity codes, but those codes are distinct from the evaluation code.
An independent finite-moment argument also shows that local tree views and
bulk spectral laws cannot certify connectivity or the extremal nontrivial
eigenvalue.

EXACT LOWER-BOUND IMPROVEMENT

Let P=conv(V) be a non-singleton Boolean polytope in R^n, and let k(P) be the
maximum support size of an actual edge. Define R(P) as the supremum, over
rational m-by-n matrices A whose columns have Euclidean norm at most one and
rational y in P, of the minimum over exact-mean laws on V of
max_{b in supp(lambda)} ||A(b-y)||_infinity. Then

    R(P) >= k(P).

Proof. Choose an actual edge [u,v] attaining k=k(P), let
S={j:u_j != v_j}, and use the one-row rational matrix with entry
sign(v_j-u_j) on S and zero elsewhere. Its columns have norm at most one.
For any rational t in (0,1/2), put y=(1-t)u+t v. Since [u,v] is an exposed
face, every Boolean-vertex law with mean y is supported on that face, hence
must put masses 1-t and t on u and v. The row discrepancies are tk and
(1-t)k. The minimum all-support radius is therefore (1-t)k. Letting rational
t decrease to zero proves the supremum is at least k. The supremum need not be
attained. This is an internally proved elementary correction; it leaves the
upper-bound problem unchanged and makes no priority claim.

The exact rational fixture in work/programmes/combinatorics_optimization/
check_edge_lower_bound.py checks segment cases for n=1,2,3,5,8 and the exact
radius 1-q on the three-cube with A=I and y=q(1,1,1), q=1/4,1/8,1/16. Its
output is edge_lower_bound_output.json. These are finite checks of examples;
the edge argument above proves the general lower bound.

CORE EXACT-MARGINAL INTERFACE AND BOTTLENECK

The supplied P-round candidate takes a Boolean polytope P with actual-edge
support bounded by a supplied k, a rational matrix A with unit Euclidean
columns, a rational y in P, and any rational linear cost c. It claims a
favorable response b in the minimal face F_P(y) with

    c^T(b-y) <= 0,       ||A(b-y)||_infinity < B(k) <= 1161 k.

For matroid bases k=2, its stated bound is 2209. A finite ellipsoid
transcript then returns an exact-mean law on at most dim(F_P(y))+1 good
vertices in O(n^3(log Q+log n)) favorable-response queries, where Q is a
common denominator for y; the output weights have O(log Q+n log n) bits.
The wrapper is conditional on the stronger favorable-good-vertex service.
Ordinary optimization over P is not a direct replacement: on the 2324-cube
with y=1/2, A the all-ones row, and threshold 1161, the unique optimizer for
positive unit cost is a bad vertex while balanced good vertices have neutral
cost. This example blocks direct substitution only; it does not refute a
bespoke trajectory that enforces both properties.

The pivot restricted diagonal-trace lemma was independently reconstructed:
for PSD U,D with U_jj <= rho D_jj and a subspace T of dimension >rho N, some
nonzero h in T satisfies h^T(U-D)h<=0, including singular D. A rational check
covered 15,800 generated cases, including singular and equality cases, and
the dimension reserve passed for k=1,...,5000. A second exact checker covered
97,237 row deletion/scale-cleanup cases and one U_{1,129} boundary fixture.
These checks support local lemmas only. The exact shifted-Hessian direction,
endpoint, affine-slice repair, persistent tracking, and cumulative potential
accounting have not been run together as a full rational state transition.
That is the decisive gate for the broad candidate. A useful next result would
be a complete nontrivial one-step fixture with relaxed constants and a proof
of every invariant, followed by a full small trajectory.

The rational bit and operation formulas are polynomial in n,k and input bits,
but extremely conservative. The stated cap is about 1.79e49 steps already at
k=2,n=17. No practical runtime or executed end-to-end algorithm is claimed.
General implicit P requires exact strong rational optimization/separation;
the matroid specialization uses independence queries plus exact submodular
minimization. The supplied k is charged as an input promise unless separately
certified. Computing maximum actual-edge support from a general implicit
oracle is outside the claim.

BROADER ROUNDING MECHANISM

Ercan, arXiv:2609.38228v1 (submitted 2026-09-28), Theorem 5.1 and Corollary
5.2, claim an exact-mean law supported on the minimal face for strongly
base-orderable matroid base polytopes. Every output has feature error less
than d R_m, where d is the maximum ||a_e-a_f||_2 over feasible single
exchanges and R_m < 6.8383231851485. Unit-column normalization gives d<=2,
so the source claims a radius below 13.676647 for all rational y. The method
uses complementary subset exchanges and one hard signing law to couple all
current pairs. This materially improves the 2209 P-round candidate on that
subclass. The theorem is SOURCE-REPORTED; a general polynomial-time kernel,
strong-ordering acquisition cost, and polynomial-bit sample construction are
not established in the source interface checked here. Its separate sampler
for arbitrary integral base polyhedra controls covariance/convex order with
positive variance slack, not an all-support l-infinity guarantee.

Pipage/swap rounding and Gram-Schmidt walks preserve exact marginals and give
concentration, but tails alone do not bound every atom. On the cube, y=1/2 and
A the all-ones row, independent Bernoulli rounding has exact marginals and
negative dependence yet includes outcomes with discrepancy n/2. Conversely,
a two-coloring path with constraints z_u+z_v=1 has only two feasible outputs;
at half marginals its unique law can force feature radius n/2 with one row.
Thus sparse local constraints alone do not control all-output feature error.

The partial-coloring route isolates the missing ingredient. If a rational
direction h lies in the current face tangent space and in the active cost
kernel, the two maximal face endpoints x+t_+h and x-t_-h admit a unique
two-point mean-preserving mixture. Iterating preserves the exact mean, but a
generic tangent direction can spend one discrepancy allowance per face drop.
Lovett-Meka's cube walk and matroid partial rounding do not provide the
required face-tangent, pathwise-progress, polynomial-bit direction engine.
The Gram-Schmidt Walk is exact-mean and subgaussian for arbitrary cube means;
at the centered mean, symmetric rejection preserves mean and a high-probability
all-atom threshold, but this does not extend to arbitrary means or faces.
An atomwise exact cost constraint is itself impossible in a rank-one
two-element example, so cost tangency can be temporary, not terminal.

The K4 graphic-matroid pair is the smallest exact obstruction to one fixed
symmetric-exchange bijection: exhaustive enumeration through six elements
found only relabelings of M(K4) at size six. It still admits a
subset-dependent multiple exchange, so it blocks only that adapter. In the
positive direction, a supplied strong-ordering witness plus the source
Komlos signing theorem gives a two-atom exact law for a midpoint pair; the
result is source-conditional and does not extend by itself to arbitrary y.

GRAPH AND COMBINATORIAL-GEOMETRY RESULTS

For the P-graph proposal, the additional endpoint-distance filter has failure
probability o(1) under the source's pointwise switch law and pairwise
independent seed. The cycle decomposition handles multiple inserted edges;
the stronger original-girth radius filter also preserves the base girth.
This is an internal conditional audit of the filter, not validation of the
imported spectral, concentration, or rational-complexity construction.

An exact graph-code bridge is available but distinct from evaluation-matroid
relations. The binary cycle code has minimum distance equal to graph girth.
For even regular degree d and even order, ker(A_G mod 2) is nonzero and a
Tanner-tree argument gives distance at least (d-1)^floor((g_T-2)/4). Under
the P-graph card's conditional logarithmic girth this is only
Omega_d(n^(2.5e-8)) with rate guaranteed no better than 2/n. The graph does
not supply an evaluation-code parity-check map. In fact, an arbitrary
labeling can include an affine (r+1)-flat in F_2^m, whose 2^(r+1) points give
a degree-r evaluation relation regardless of graph girth. A graph-coupled
labeling with a Tanner-expansion guarantee remains UNKNOWN.

For fixed d and girth g, exact closed-walk moments through degree g-1 plus
Bernstein approximation give W_1(mu_G, KM_d) <= 2d/sqrt(g-1). LPS graphs and
an MSS Ramanujan 2-lift yield a conditional pair with identical fixed-radius
tree neighborhoods and empirical spectral measures within O(1/sqrt(log n)),
while connectedness and lambda_2 differ by a constant. This establishes the
scope limit of local/bulk statistics; it does not challenge the cited graph
constructions or validate P-graph.

A fresh thin-tree attack did not produce a new theorem. Its exact
laminar-family obstruction shows that a path tree can cross each chain cut
once while another near-minimum cut can require n-2 chain cuts to cover its
boundary (126 at n=128). This only defeats naive arbitrary-laminar compression;
the careful eight-cut coverage in Klein-Olver-Yeoh's near-minimum-cut result
survives. See arXiv:2605.12669v1 and the finite audit in leaf 19.

The binary coset-sunflower audit reconstructs this scoped statement: for
K_1,...,K_k subspaces of F_2^n with intersection {0}, if every 2-dimensional
U has dim(U intersect K_i)=1 for some i, then n<=2k-1 and the associated
injective coset code has at most 4^k/2 words. The proof uses the affine-cover
rank inequality and a binary sum-free residual recursion; its private-point
cover certificate has size at most k^2. This is an internally checked
candidate/source-reported result, not an unrestricted sunflower theorem.
The corrected “clean” definition is coordinatewise all-equal or all-distinct;
no binary counterexample survived. A natural q>2 paired-cover extension fails
already for two coordinate axes in F_5^2; the hyperplane-kernel subcase has
n<=k, while general q>2 and any representation bridge from arbitrary
clean-triple-free word families remain UNKNOWN.
