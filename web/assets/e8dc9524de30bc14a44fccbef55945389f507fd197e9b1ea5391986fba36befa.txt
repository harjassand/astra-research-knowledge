symmetries need not induce monomial permutations of arbitrary rank-term indices.

1. The missing bridge
---------------------
Write an exact rank decomposition for matrix multiplication as

    Z B = sum_(l=1)^R a_l(Z) b_l(B) C_l.

The sparse-input encoding map is E_Z[:,W]; the dense-input map is E_B; the
decoding map has columns vec(C_l). Their shapes, incidence profiles, cheap
factorizations, and product-gate overlap are independent information beyond R.

A bound on R, including one implied by omega <= 9/4 or alpha > .465, does not
give a degree bound on these leg maps. The Alman--Li theorem is a scalar
contraction algorithm for a linear transform with a separately bounded delta.
It neither evaluates every rank-term coefficient for free nor handles the
third matrix multiplication leg by identifying it with a fixed matrix.

To obtain the target from this route one needs an acquired family of leg-map
factorizations/structured contractions whose sparse encoding, dense encoding,
bilinear work, and decoding collectively cost O(N^(2-eta)). Merely renaming a
rank exponent delta is not valid. If one attempted an incidence-based scalar
bound dominated by m, the required degree factor would be at most
N^(1/8-eta). Direct rank-term incidence cannot achieve that; see item 2.

2. Direct rank-term incidence lower bound (parent proposal independently checked)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Define the masked trilinear polynomial

    T_W(z,x,y) = sum_(i,j in W) sum_(k=1)^D z_ij x_ik y_kj.

Let T_W=sum_l a_l(z)b_l(x)c_l(y) be ANY exact rank-one decomposition over ANY
field. For fixed (i,j) in W, the coefficient of z_ij is

    sum_(k=1)^D x_ik y_kj.

Its coefficient matrix as an x-by-y bilinear form contains a D-by-D identity
submatrix and is zero elsewhere, hence has rank D. Each l with nonzero
coefficient of z_ij in a_l contributes a rank-one bilinear matrix. Therefore
at least D such l exist. Summing over the independent z variables gives

    nnz(z-leg incidence) >= m D.

The coefficient of x_ik is sum_(j:(i,j) in W) z_ij y_kj, of bilinear rank
deg_W(i). Thus the x-leg incidence also totals at least D sum_i deg_W(i)=mD.
The y-leg incidence bound is identical. These rank statements hold in every
characteristic because the relevant nonzero submatrix is an identity.

For m=Theta(N^(15/8)) and D=N^(1/4), mD=Theta(N^(17/8)) exceeds N^2. This
rules out evaluating such a decomposition by explicitly enumerating all
nonzero input-to-rank-term wires, even after specializing the decomposition
to W. It is not a rank lower bound of mD and not an arithmetic circuit lower
bound. Shared intermediate linear forms can process many incidences with few
gates. A new factorization of a leg map or a deeper shared encoder is outside
this argument. In particular it would be WRONG to assert delta(E_Z)>=D from
the slice-rank argument: delta allows an unrelated depth-two factorization.

3. Rank-preserving dense-gauge counterexample
-------------------------------------------
Lemma (infinite fields). Every exact rank-R decomposition of rectangular matrix
multiplication with all terms nonzero can be transformed, with the same rank
R, so that every coefficient in every input linear form and output matrix is
nonzero. This already refutes a universal sparse-support rank-leaf lemma over Q.

Proof. For multiplication Mat_(m,n) times Mat_(n,p), choose invertible P,Q,R0
of dimensions m,n,p. In the existing identity substitute

    X' = P X Q^(-1),       Y' = Q Y R0^(-1),
    XY = P^(-1) (X'Y') R0.

The new rank terms are a_l(P X Q^(-1)), b_l(Q Y R0^(-1)), and
P^(-1) C_l R0. Each desired individual coefficient is a rational function
in entries of P,Q,R0, nonzero as a function: a nonzero linear form cannot
vanish on every rank-one matrix, since rank-one elementary matrices span the
matrix space; the invertible left-right orbit of a single elementary matrix
contains every nonzero rank-one matrix. For output entries, a nonzero matrix
C_l has some nonzero pairing u^T C_l v, and u,v can be extended to rows and
columns of invertible matrices. These arguments apply separately to every
input/output coefficient. Clear determinant denominators and multiply the
finitely many nonzero coefficient polynomials and determinants. The product
is nonzero. Over an infinite field it has a nonvanishing assignment. All
coefficients are therefore simultaneously nonzero at some invertible gauge.
Over Q, the assignment can be rational. No terms are added. QED.

Take a constant-size base identity and then tensor it k times. Every coefficient
of each leg is a product of k nonzero field elements, hence remains nonzero.
For ANY nonempty supported coordinate set W on an input leg, every one of the
R^k product leaves is structurally reachable. Let rho=|W|/n_input^k. Then

    number of reached product leaves = R^k,

not O(rho R^k) with a uniform constant. For singleton W the multiplicative
gap is n_input^k. This is a counterexample to support-density proportional
pruning of a given rank circuit, without padding or cancelling duplicate terms.

For the full N-by-N times N-by-D tensor, rank R >= N^2 by flattening on the
Z leg: its N^2 coefficient slices in the B/output variables are linearly
independent. Thus a dense-gauged full-product decomposition retains at least
N^2 leaves for every nonempty W, including legal W of size N^(15/8).
No syntactic leaf deletion can produce the requested strictly subquadratic
circuit from that representation.

This does not forbid selecting a better decomposition, undoing the gauge,
combining products, or changing the computation graph after W is known. Those
are semantic redesigns, and require their own proof and charged construction.
Also, a dense Kronecker leg may have a compact shared multilevel linear circuit.
Thus the counterexample does not prove large delta or large arithmetic size.

4. Exact rational witness and scope of test
-----------------------------------------
check_dense_strassen.py starts from Strassen's standard 2-by-2 rank-seven
decomposition and uses

    P  = [[7,5],[1,2]],
    Q  = [[6,6],[5,1]],
    R0 = [[5,5],[4,1]].

The script proves the 64 tensor coefficient identities by Fraction arithmetic
and checks all 84 leg coefficients are nonzero. For its k-th tensor power,
one supported input reaches 7^k leaves among 4^k input coordinates. This is
a reproducible finite witness plus the tensor-product proof for all k. The
general gauge lemma above does not rely on a finite diagnostic.

The rank exponent of this witness is log_2(7), not the new omega. That is
irrelevant to the universal rank-to-support inference: the gauge lemma applies
to every exact decomposition, including any chosen fixed identity used to
realize better square or rectangular exponents. If it is tensor-powered, it
keeps that rank exponent and makes all leaf supports dense. The existence of
some good exponents remains compatible with failure of generic leaf pruning.
