Cycle 2 leaf report: falling-factorial repair attempt
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the explicit family and its extension below; no novelty claim.

Finding
--------
The target family has no recurrence gap in V=A+B. A direct physical-time generator calculation gives LV <= C-epsilon V, which proves nonexplosion and positive recurrence on its unique communicating class. More generally, a two-parameter family of higher-order weakly reversible networks has the same drift and is positive recurrent in each of its finitely many residue classes. This bypasses the N76 event-chain potential; it does not repair N76 into a theorem for arbitrary weakly reversible networks.

1. Exact target and conventions
-------------------------------
For fixed integer m>=3 and arbitrary fixed positive rate constants, use the five reactions

  0 --k0--> A,    A --kAB--> B,    B --kg--> mB,
  mB --kd--> A,   A --kA--> 0.

The complexes {0,A,B,mB} form a strongly connected directed graph (0->A->B->mB->A and A->0), so this network is weakly reversible with one linkage class.

The state is (a,b) in N_0^2. Stochastic mass-action propensity for y->y' is k_(y,y') x_under_y, where x_under_q=x(x-1)...(x-q+1), and it is zero when x<q. Thus the five propensities are k0, kAB a, kg b, kd b_under_m, kA a. A binomial-coefficient convention only rescales the positive constants and leaves the result unchanged. The CTMC is time-homogeneous; no volume scaling, external control, or zero rate is allowed.

Set V(a,b)=a+b. The reaction increments of V, in the order displayed, are +1, 0, +(m-1), -(m-1), -1. Therefore the exact generator is

  LV(a,b) = k0 + (m-1)kg b - (m-1)kd b_under_m - kA a.       (1)

For any 0<epsilon<=kA,

  LV+epsilon V = k0 -(kA-epsilon)a
                 +(m-1)kg b -(m-1)kd b_under_m + epsilon b.

The a term is nonpositive. The b expression tends to -infinity as b tends to infinity because b_under_m is a degree-m polynomial with positive leading coefficient and m>=3. Hence its supremum over integer b>=0 is finite. There is a finite C such that

  LV(a,b) <= C - epsilon(a+b) for every (a,b) in N_0^2.       (2)

This is an exact factorial-propensity calculation, not a large-count replacement. In particular b_under_m=0 for b<m, so the unavailable mB->A reaction contributes exactly zero on the boundary. The potentially positive kg term is then confined to finitely many b values unless b grows, where the negative degree-m term dominates. No source-availability assumption is hidden in (1) or (2).

2. Nonexplosion and physical-time recurrence
---------------------------------------------
First, (1) implies LV<=C globally. Let tau_R=inf{t:V(X_t)>=R}. All jumps of V are bounded in absolute value by m-1, and the set {V<R} is finite, so the stopped chain has bounded rates before tau_R and Dynkin's formula applies. For every finite t,

  E_x[V(X_(t wedge tau_R))] <= V(x)+Ct,
  P_x(tau_R<=t) <= (V(x)+Ct)/R.

As R tends to infinity, the second bound rules out escape to infinity by any finite time. Infinitely many jumps confined to a finite state set in finite time are also impossible because rates on that finite set are bounded. Thus the minimal CTMC is conservative/nonexplosive.

Choose a finite threshold R0>(C+1)/epsilon and set W=1+V, so W is strictly positive and norm-like. Outside K={V<=R0}, (2) gives LW=LV<=-1. The finite-set CTMC Foster criterion therefore gives positive recurrence on every closed irreducible component (and finite expected physical-time entrance to the union of those components). In the target network the entire lattice is one irreducible closed class, as verified next, so it is positive recurrent. Equivalently, stopped Dynkin gives finite expected physical-time return to K; the CTMC Foster theorem upgrades this to positive recurrence of the irreducible chain. The criterion used is the continuous-time generator criterion, not an embedded-chain criterion.

Irreducibility is explicit. From any (a,b), if b>=m, fire mB->A and then A->0; this reduces b by m and returns a to its former value. For 1<=b<m, fire B->mB, then mB->A, then A->0; this reduces b by one and returns a to its former value. Repetition reaches (a,0). Fire A->0 until reaching (0,0). Conversely, from (0,0), immigrate a+b A molecules and convert b of them by A->B, reaching (a,b). Every transition in these paths is enabled when used and has positive probability of being the next jump. Thus the lattice is a single communicating class; it is closed because all jumps stay in N_0^2.

The rates and constants affect C, epsilon, and the finite Foster set, but no rate ordering is required. The result is qualitative: it supplies no optimized return-time bound, stationary-tail estimate, or complexity bound for finding the drift threshold. For each fixed (m,k0,kAB,kg,kd,kA), the supremum used in C is finite; no bit-cost or rate-encoding assumption is part of the theorem.

3. Broader weakly reversible higher-order family
-------------------------------------------------
For integers 1<=s<m, consider

  0 <-> sA,    sA -> B,    B -> mB,    mB -> sA,

with positive constants k0,kA0,kAB,kg,kd. The complexes {0,sA,B,mB} form one strongly connected linkage class, hence the network is weakly reversible. With the same propensity convention, the propensities are k0, kA0 a_under_s, kAB a_under_s, kg b, kd b_under_m. For V=a+b,

  LV = s k0 - [s kA0+(s-1)kAB] a_under_s
       +(m-1)kg b -(m-s)kd b_under_m.                         (3)

Put alpha=s kA0+(s-1)kAB>0. Choose 0<epsilon<=kA0. Then

  LV+epsilon V = s k0 +[-alpha a_under_s+epsilon a]
                  +[(m-1)kg b -(m-s)kd b_under_m+epsilon b].

The first bracket is bounded above: for s=1 it equals -(kA0-epsilon)a; for s>=2 its negative degree-s term dominates the linear term. The second bracket is bounded above because m>s>=1 and its negative degree-m term dominates the linear terms. Hence (3) also yields LV<=C-epsilon V globally.

The communicating classes are exactly Gamma_r={(a,b):a mod s=r}, r=0,...,s-1, because every A increment is a multiple of s. Each class is irreducible. To reduce b while restoring a: for b>=m, fire mB->sA and sA->0, reducing b by m; for 1<=b<m, fire B->mB, mB->sA, sA->0, reducing b by one. Then fire sA->0 repeatedly to reach (r,0). To reach a target (a,b) in Gamma_r from (r,0), immigrate q+b batches of sA, where a=r+qs, and fire sA->B exactly b times. Thus every Gamma_r is closed and irreducible. The same stopped-Dynkin and Foster arguments prove nonexplosion and positive recurrence in each class; the V-jumps are bounded by max{s,s-1,m-1,m-s}. There are only s classes; this does not claim a theorem uniform over arbitrary or infinitely many reaction-network classes.

4. Event chain, holding times, and high-rate cycles
----------------------------------------------------
At x=(a,b), the total exit rate in the target is

  q(a,b)=k0+(kAB+kA)a+kg b+kd b_under_m,

which is unbounded. If X_n is the embedded reaction-event chain, then

  E[V(X_(n+1))-V(X_n) | X_n=x] = LV(x)/q(x).

The N76 factorial-potential estimate is instead an event-index upward-increment bound for a different function. Neither such a bound nor an event count by itself controls elapsed time: holding times have conditional mean 1/q(X_n), and q is unbounded. Here the physical-time result comes directly from (1), stopped Dynkin, and the CTMC Foster criterion. We do not infer a physical-time statement by translating an event-chain tail estimate.

The potentially dangerous total-count-increasing reaction B->mB has only linear propensity kg b; its reverse mB->A has degree-m propensity and removes m-1 total molecules. The A<->B transfer A->B is total-count neutral. The apparent cycle B->mB->A->B has increments (0,m-1), (1,-m), (-1,1), summing to zero; the high-order step is a downward total-count jump, not an upward jump hidden in a fast cycle. A fast high-b degradation event can feed A and then transfer it to B, but the generator includes these transitions separately: transfer contributes zero to LV, while high-order degradation contributes the negative term in (1). At b<m the degradation switch is off but b is bounded; at large b it is on and dominates. Likewise, in the s-family, the high-order A reactions are disabled for a<s, a finite boundary strip, and their exact falling factorials in (3) vanish there. The immigration and lower-order growth terms cannot create an unbounded positive drift in these strips. The family still has unbounded total exit rate q(a,b), so the holding-time issue is handled by the physical-time generator proof, not by counting reaction events.

5. Scope, prior art, and claim status
-------------------------------------
Claim: for the explicit network in section 1, every positive choice of fixed rates and every m>=3 yields a nonexplosive irreducible positive recurrent CTMC on N_0^2. Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. The proof is generator-level and complete modulo the standard CTMC Foster theorem. Claim: the s-family in section 3 has exactly s closed irreducible residue classes, each nonexplosive and positive recurrent. Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE. This is not a claim that every weakly reversible stochastic mass-action network is positive recurrent, nor that recurrence is uniform across classes in arbitrary networks.

Primary literature consulted:

* David F. Anderson and Jinsu Kim, “Some network conditions for positive recurrence of stochastically modeled reaction networks,” arXiv:1710.11263v3, dated 22 August 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11263. Its Theorem 3.1 states the finite-set CTMC generator Foster criterion used here; its network theorems cover specified binary classes, not this m>=3 network. The paper frames positive recurrence for general weakly reversible networks as an open conjecture. The theorem, not the conjectural generalization, is the input used in this report.
* Chuang Xu, “On the Regulary of Reaction Systems,” arXiv:2409.05340v2, version dated 9 May 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05340. Theorem 3.6 gives a nonexplosion/regularity criterion: for some positive u, every reaction increasing u must have propensity O(||x||_1). For the target and s-family with u=(1,1), the only strictly increasing reactions are constant immigration and first-order B->mB, so its criterion independently covers nonexplosion. It does not provide our positive-recurrence conclusion. The paper's discussion also distinguishes regularity from positive recurrence and notes the remaining scope of general weakly reversible recurrence questions.

No priority or novelty claim is made. The candidate is a small structural corollary of a standard Foster method, and may already be known or immediate to specialists. No external mathematician reviewed this derivation; no source code, simulation, or numerical check was needed. The strongest equally informed baseline is the exact CTMC Foster criterion above plus Xu's independent regularity criterion. N76's event-chain bound is not stronger evidence for physical-time recurrence; N33 deterministic permanence and N35 finite-horizon safety are different interfaces and are not premises.

Exact falsifier: a single enabled-transition audit contradicting (1) or (3), a positive-rate parameter choice in the stated ranges for which a Gamma_r is not the stated communicating class, or an exact proof that the physical-time return time to a state in one such class has infinite expectation would refute the theorem. A broader claim about all weakly reversible networks is not asserted and would require a different proof or counterexample.

Next gate: search the primary literature for this exact family/structural specialization before making a novelty claim; then obtain an independent line-by-line review of the generator and class-path proof. To go beyond this banked lemma, the next mathematical target is a principled condition under which a weakly reversible graph admits a positive linear V with high-order negative drift on every unbounded boundary stratum. The present proof only succeeds because every unbounded B stratum activates mB->sA, while the competing growth is first order, and every large A stratum activates sA->0.
