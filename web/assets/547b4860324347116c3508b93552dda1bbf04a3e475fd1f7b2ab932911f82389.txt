FIXED GUTZWILLER Z: STABILITY WITNESS AND TWO VALID PROMISED ROUTES
8 October 2026

Target and result
-----------------
For an explicitly supplied nonnegative rational matrix A in Q_{>=0}^{n x n}
and 0 <= k <= floor(n/2), set

  Z_k(A) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k, I intersect J = empty} det(A[I,J])^2.

This is the fixed-pair, no-double-occupancy normalizer. Let V=[I_n,A^T],
G=V^T V, and P(S) be the 2k columns of V associated with S (both columns
for each site in S). Cauchy-Binet gives

  c_A(S) = det G[P(S),P(S)]
         = sum_{J subset [n]\S, |J|=k} det(A[S,J])^2,
  H_{k,A}(x) = sum_{|S|=k} c_A(S) x^S,
  H_{k,A}(1,...,1) = Z_k(A).

There are three scoped outcomes.

1. The direct paired-Gram polynomial H and the joint determinant-square
   polynomial both fail real stability on a 0/1 permutation input with
   condition number one. This exactly refutes a blanket stability premise;
   it does not rule out other representations or algorithms.
2. H is real stable, and Z_k has an exact O(sk)-rational-operation algorithm,
   on the promised block-pair class A=direct-sum_b [[0,a_b],[b_b,0]] with
   a_b,b_b nonnegative rational. The full joint polynomial in row and column
   variables can still be unstable even on this class.
3. A direct uniform-importance-sampling FPRAS is valid on the precise
   (support-dense, weight-balanced) promise below. A directed even-cycle
   permutation has weight ratio one but exponentially small support density,
   so it lies outside that promise. A fixed, input-independent list of
   determinant-term probes also needs exponentially many entries to hit all
   even-cycle instances.

No universal polynomial-size algebraic evaluation hitting set, no general
importance sampler for arbitrary rational A, and no general impossibility
theorem for all such algorithms is established here.

Exact stability counterexample
------------------------------
For n=4, let A be the directed 4-cycle permutation matrix, with A[i,j]=1
when j=i+1 modulo 4. It is nonnegative rational, orthogonal, and has
2-norm condition number 1. At k=2 the only nonzero disjoint minor pairs are
I={1,3}, J={2,4} and I={2,4}, J={1,3}; each squared determinant is one.
Therefore

  H_{2,A}(x) = x_1 x_3 + x_2 x_4,
  P_{2,A}(x,y) = x_1 x_3 y_2 y_4 + x_2 x_4 y_1 y_3,
  Z_2(A)=2,

where P is the joint row/column determinant-square generating polynomial.
The paired-Gram identity is exact: its four relevant coefficients are 1,1,0,0.

Borcea-Branden-Liggett, Theorem 4.1, states that a real multiaffine
polynomial is real stable only if every Rayleigh difference

  Delta_ij f(x) = (partial_i f)(partial_j f)
                 - f(x) (partial_i partial_j f)(x)

is nonnegative for every real x. For H, using i=1,j=3 gives
Delta_13 H = -x_2 x_4, which is -1 at x=1. For P, using the distinct
variables x_1 and y_2 gives
Delta_{x_1,y_2} P = -x_2 x_3 x_4 y_1 y_3 y_4, also -1 at the all-ones
point. Thus both natural generating polynomials are not real stable.
This exact derivative witness is stronger and easier to audit than a
floating-point root calculation.

The obstruction persists at every even n=2q, k=q. For a directed cycle
permutation pi, a minor det A[I,J] is nonzero exactly when J=pi(I), and
then its square is one. Disjointness requires I to be an independent set
of size q in the cycle. The only such sets are its two alternating classes.
Hence

  H_{q,A}(x) = x_1 x_3 ... x_{2q-1} + x_2 x_4 ... x_{2q},
  Z_q(A)=2.

For every q>=2, write U=product_{i odd}x_i and V=product_{i even}x_i,
and choose two distinct odd indices i,j. Direct differentiation gives
Delta_ij H = -UV/(x_i x_j), strictly negative on the positive orthant.
So every member of this even-cycle family is unstable by the same Rayleigh
criterion.

This family also gives an exact limit for a restricted hitting interface.
At n=2q, every legal pair is (I,I^c), so the universe has
N=binom(2q,q) atoms. For each balanced S, one can arrange a Hamilton cycle
whose alternating classes are S and S^c; that instance's support is exactly
{(S,S^c),(S^c,S)}. The N atoms split into N/2 such complementary pairs.
Consequently any input-independent list that tests individual minor
weights and must hit every nonzero even-cycle instance has size at least
N/2=binom(n,n/2)/2, exponential in n. For any input-independent proposal
distribution on atoms, some such instance receives proposal mass at most
2/N. More generally, a black-box atom-probe algorithm whose next probe
depends only on past queried weights has a common all-zero transcript up to
its first hit; its m probes can cover at most m complementary classes on
that path. Some cycle instance is therefore hit with probability at most
2m/N, so success at least 2/3 on every instance requires m >= N/3. This
lower bound does not apply to polynomial evaluations that sum many terms or
algorithms that inspect and exploit the supplied matrix.

Positive stable subclass: independent 2-site pairs
--------------------------------------------------
Suppose n=2s and the supplied site partition makes

  A = direct-sum_{b=1}^s [[0,a_b],[b_b,0]],   a_b,b_b in Q_{>=0}.

Write L_b(x)=a_b^2 x_{b,L}+b_b^2 x_{b,R}, omitting a block only when both
entries are zero. Since A is block diagonal, a nonzero minor must use the
same number of rows and columns in each block. The no-overlap rule rules
out selecting both sites in one 2-site block. Thus each active block is
either unused or contributes one orientation, with weight a_b^2 or b_b^2.
For the paired-Gram polynomial this gives the exact identity

  H_{k,A}(x) = e_k(L_1(x),...,L_s(x)),
  Z_k(A) = e_k(c_1,...,c_s),       c_b=a_b^2+b_b^2.

If 0<=k<=s and at least k blocks are active, H is real stable. Indeed, for
every x whose coordinates have positive imaginary parts, each nonzero L_b
has positive imaginary part. The elementary symmetric polynomial e_k is
stable: it is, up to a nonzero factor, the (s-k)-th t-derivative at t=0 of
product_b(t+z_b), whose roots lie strictly in the lower half-plane when
Im(z_b)>0; Gauss-Lucas keeps derivative roots in that half-plane. Therefore
e_k(L_1(x),...,L_s(x)) cannot vanish. The cases k=0 and k=s are included.

The joint polynomial is

  P_{k,A}(x,y) = e_k( a_b^2 x_{b,L} y_{b,R}
                       + b_b^2 x_{b,R} y_{b,L} : b=1,...,s).

It is not stable in general, even for s=1,k=1,a_1=b_1=1: it is
x_{1,L}y_{1,R}+x_{1,R}y_{1,L}, and its Rayleigh difference for
(x_{1,L},y_{1,R}) at ones is -1. The stable object here is specifically
the paired-Gram row-site polynomial H, obtained by setting y=1.

The exact evaluator updates E_0=1,E_j=0 for j>0 and, block by block,

  E_j <- E_j + c_b E_{j-1}     (j=min(b,k),...,1).

Then E_k=Z_k. This takes O(sk) rational arithmetic operations. If L is
the total binary input length and D is the product of entry denominators,
log D<=L and D A has integer entries of O(L) bits. The integer numerator
of Z has O(kL+s) bits (each term is a product of k squared entries and
there are at most binom(s,k) terms); the denominator D^(2k) has O(kL) bits.
With schoolbook arithmetic, a conservative bit bound is
O(sk (kL+s)^2), plus polynomial overhead for input and output. No matrix
inversion, floating-point conditioning, or oracle is used.

The same DP gives an exact Born sampler. Select active blocks by suffix
coefficients; within an active block choose the left-row/right-column
orientation with relative weight a_b^2 versus b_b^2. Exact rational draws
have expected polynomial fair-bit cost, or can be capped with an explicit
abort probability delta using O(log(1/delta)) extra draw bits per choice.
The stability fact itself is not a counting oracle; the DP proves
tractability on this promised structure.

Physical normalization is separate. The unfiltered fixed-k sector norm is

  S_k(A) = sum_{|I|=|J|=k} det(A[I,J])^2
         = [t^k] det(I+t A^T A)
         = [t^k] product_b (1+t a_b^2)(1+t b_b^2).

It is computable by an exact O(sk) coefficient DP as well. The conditional
Gutzwiller acceptance after preparing the k-pair sector is Z_k/S_k. For an
unnormalized all-charge BCS state the probability of this fixed-k filtered
event is Z_k/det(I+A^T A). These rational normalizers have polynomial bit
length, but the probabilities can be exponentially small; a physical
postselection implementation still costs expected 1/p preparations. The
classical exact sampler above does not incur that physical retry cost.

Importance-sampling FPRAS under a quantitative promise
------------------------------------------------------
Let Omega={(I,J): |I|=|J|=k, I intersect J=empty},
N=|Omega|=binom(n,k)binom(n-k,k), and w(I,J)=det(A[I,J])^2. Put
s_w=|{omega:w(omega)>0}|, rho=s_w/N, and
R=max_{w>0}w/min_{w>0}w.

Promise bounds rho>=rho_0>0 and R<=R_0 are supplied, with
R_0/rho_0 polynomially bounded in n and input bit length. The algorithm
does not verify these promises. It samples uniformly from Omega, computes
w exactly, and uses a median of group means to estimate N E[w]=Z_k.

For X=w(omega), mu=E[X]=Z_k/N. Since X^2<=w_max X,

  E[X^2]/mu^2 <= w_max N/Z_k
                 <= (w_max/w_min) N/s_w
                 <= R_0/rho_0.

Thus a group of O((R_0/rho_0) epsilon^-2) exact samples has constant
success probability by Chebyshev; taking O(log(1/delta)) independent
groups and their median gives relative error epsilon with probability at
least 1-delta. The number of samples is

  O((R_0/rho_0) epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)).

Uniform legal pairs are generated by exact unranking from Omega. A capped
rejection sampler can make the total probability of a random-index abort
at most delta/2; use delta/2 in the median bound. For each sample, clear
all denominators once: G=D A is integral and
w=det(G[I,J])^2/D^(2k). Bareiss elimination takes O(k^3) integer
arithmetic operations; Hadamard bounds operands by O(k(L+log k)) bits.
With schoolbook integer arithmetic this is
O(k^5(L+log k)^2) bit operations per sample. Accumulating each group uses
a common denominator D^(2k); the output rational has polynomial bit
length. Total bit time is therefore

  O((R_0/rho_0) epsilon^-2 log(1/delta)
    * k^5 (L+log k)^2)

plus polynomial subset-indexing and random-bit overhead. Exact arithmetic
removes floating-point error; the required condition is the promised
weight ratio and support density, not a matrix condition-number bound.

For a general rational A, determinant height only gives the crude bound
R <= 2^{O(k(L+log k))}, and rho can be as small as 1/N. So this is not a
general polynomial-time algorithm. The directed 2q-cycle has R=1 but
rho=2/binom(2q,q), making uniform importance sampling exponential. The
best equally informed baseline on that cycle is exact: its two alternating
supports can be recognized and sampled in O(n) time, and the broader
monomial/permutation family has a source-derived transfer-matrix DP (N89).
This separates the generic proposal failure from algorithmic hardness.

Status and falsifiers
--------------------
The block-pair formula, stability proof, exact DP, bit bounds, importance
sampling estimator, and cycle atom-list lower bound are INTERNALLY-PROVED-
CANDIDATE. The small determinant replay in evidence/cycle_fixture.json is
FINITE-EVIDENCE for cycle n=4,6,8 and one 2-block rational fixture. The Rayleigh-difference criterion is
EXTERNAL-ESTABLISHED by Borcea-Branden-Liggett Theorem 4.1. The prior N86
cycle barrier and N89 permutation DP are source-derived internal evidence;
they are not external validation of this derivation or a novelty result.

Falsifiers: a missing nonzero cycle minor, an additional size-q independent
set in C_{2q}, a violation of the block factorization, or a failed Rayleigh
sign would refute the corresponding candidate. For the FPRAS, the load-
bearing assumptions are supplied bounds rho_0 and R_0; if they fail or
are not polynomially controlled, the claimed running time does not apply.
The term-probe lower bound fails to apply if a query can aggregate
exponentially many minor terms or adapt to structural information about A.
No universal hitting set for arbitrary polynomial evaluations is refuted.
