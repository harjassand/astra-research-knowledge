CONCLUSION

The rank-Q covariant instrument in

passes the independent algebraic checks below. It gives a finite-label,
whole-state code for the critical iid-qubit orbit with error
O_{c1}(r/Q) for known r (and O_{c1}(1/(sqrt(n)Q)) uniformly over the

e>=c r/Q for Q<=1/(32r), it gives the candidate matched branch law
e*_Q=Theta(r/Q) up to Q of order 1/r. This conclusion concerns maximum
quantum branch dimension Q, not optimal total hybrid dimension D.

The analytic full-trace estimate is valid; it does not follow from the
dipole estimate alone. The independent derivation below checks the
double-commutator expansion and the simpler diagonal-kernel/Taylor bound.
No external proof review or priority clearance is implied.

1. INTERFACE AND FINITE INSTRUMENT

Input is rho_(r,v)^tensor n, with n and either r or the radius envelope
supplied, v unknown, and required output the full n-qubit density in
worst-case half trace distance. No retained reference, entanglement
assistance, or free input-correlated register is available. In the Schur
decomposition the exact model is direct_sum_j P_j tau_(j,v), with
tau_(j,v)=exp(beta J_v)/Z_j, beta=2 atanh(r). Multiplicity is
parameter-independent and can be regenerated.

For one spin j, d=2j+1 and 1<=Q<d, let k=j-m and set
f_k=(1-k/Q)_+ for 0<=k<=2j, F=diag(f_k), and s=Tr F^2. Then

  s = sum_(a=1)^Q a^2/Q^2 = (Q+1)(2Q+1)/(6Q),
  E = sum_i ||[J_i,F]||_2^2
    = Q^(-2) sum_(k=1)^Q k(2j-k+1),
  E/s = 3(2j+1)/(2Q+1)-1 <= 6j/Q.

The k=Q term is the support-boundary ladder edge and must be included.
For normalized Haar measure on the spin orbit, F_U=UFU*, Schur's lemma
gives integral F_U^2 dU=(s/d)I. Hence

  Phi_j(X)=(d/s) integral F_U X F_U dU

is CP, trace preserving, unital, and SU(2)-covariant. It is implementable
through a Q-dimensional branch: after classical outcome U, encode by
sqrt(d/s) F U* into the fixed range of F, then decode by embedding and U.
The composition Kraus operator is sqrt(d/s) U F U*, which has rank Q.
When Q>=d, use the identity channel.

The continuous orbit is not an infinite-memory outcome. Because F commutes
with right z rotations, the superoperator U -> (X -> F_U X F_U) is a finite
Euler-angle polynomial: its integer SU(2) frequencies are at most 4j.
An equally spaced alpha grid with 4j+1 nodes kills every nonzero frequency;
Gauss-Legendre quadrature in cos(theta) with d nodes integrates the
surviving P_l(cos(theta)), l<=4j, exactly. All weights are positive.
Thus sector j needs at most (4j+1)(2j+1) classical outcomes. Across all
Schur sectors the count is O(n^3); every branch has dimension at most Q,
so total direct-sum dimension D<=O(Q n^3). The label bit count is O(log n).
This is an ideal finite CPTP implementation. Exact gate synthesis, algebraic
weight/node precision, Schur circuits, runtime, and physical output
preparation are separate costs.

2. FULL TRACE DISTANCE FROM A DIPole LOSS

I checked the following route, which avoids an unproved all-multipole
eigenvalue inequality. Write S=J_z/j, h=beta*j, and let lambda_1 be the
spin-1 eigenvalue of Phi_j. Bistochasticity and covariance imply that
M_mn=<m|Phi_j(|n><n|)|m> is doubly stochastic and
sum_n M_mn n=lambda_1 m. Therefore

  sum_mn M_mn (n-m)^2
    = 2 d j(j+1)(1-lambda_1)/3.                 (A)

For p_m=exp(beta*m)/Z_j, expand around m:

  p_n=p_m[1+beta(n-m)]+R_mn,
  |R_mn| <= (beta^2/2)(n-m)^2 p_m exp(2h).

Here |n-m|<=2j. Also Z_j>=d by pairing m and -m, so p_m<=exp(h)/d.
The linear term sums exactly to -beta(1-lambda_1)m p_m. Using (A),
triangle inequality, and T=one-half l1 for diagonal states gives

  T(Phi_j(tau_(j,z)),tau_(j,z))
   <= [ beta*j/2 + beta^2*j*(j+1)*exp(3h)/6 ](1-lambda_1).

The factors check: the quadratic remainder contributes
beta^2*j*(j+1)*exp(3h)/3 to l1 before the final half. This is a full
state bound; a first-moment/dipole bound without the Taylor remainder would
not suffice.

There is also a cleaner independent check via double commutators. Since
integral F_U^2=(s/d)I,

  Phi_j(X)-X=-(d/(2s)) integral [F_U,[F_U,X]] dU.

Rotation covariance and the unique spin-1 copy in End(V_j) give

  integral [F_U,[F_U,S]] dU = E S/[d*j*(j+1)],
  integral ||[F_U,S]||_2^2 dU = E/(3j^2).

Applying the commutator derivation twice to S^m yields m same-factor
terms with trace norm at most E/[j(j+1)] each, and m(m-1) cross terms
with trace norm at most E/(3j^2) each by Schatten Holder and ||S||<=1.
Thus

  ||Phi_j(S^m)-S^m||_1
   <= d E/(2s) [m/(j(j+1))+m(m-1)/(3j^2)].

Expanding exp(hS), using Z_j>=d and summing its absolutely convergent
series gives

  T(Phi_j(tau_(j,v)),tau_(j,v))
   <= E/(4s*j^2) (h+h^2/3)exp(h)
   <= 3/(2Q) (beta+beta^2*j/3)exp(beta*j).

The first derivation is more elementary for the sector channel; the second
is sharper in the exponential and is the bound used for Schur averaging.
Both include the entire density operator, not just its vector moment.

3. SCHUR AVERAGE AND HIDDEN-RADIUS COST

Let t=atanh(r) and L=2j. The exact Schur law obeys

  P_L <= 2(L+1)^2/n * pi_0(L) * exp(tL),

where pi_0(L)=2^(-n) binom(n,(n-L)/2) is the unbiased Rademacher-sum
probability on the parity lattice. This follows from
m_L=2(L+1)/(n+L+2) binom(n,(n-L)/2),
(ab)^(n/2)<=2^(-n), and
sinh((L+1)t)/sinh(t)<= (L+1)exp(tL).

For X a sum of n unbiased signs, E exp(uX)=cosh(u)^n<=exp(nu^2/2),
E X^4<=3n^2, and E X^6<=15n^3. Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the last
Schur bound gives, uniformly for r*sqrt(n)<=c1,

  E_P exp(tL) <= 4(sqrt(3)+1)exp(4nt^2),
  E_P [r*j*exp(tL)] <= 2r*sqrt(n)(sqrt(15)+1)exp(4nt^2).

Since t<=4r/3, nt^2<=16c1^2/9. Averaging the double-commutator bound
therefore yields e<=C(c1)r/Q for every known radius in the envelope,
uniformly in v. The encoder itself uses only n,j,Q, not r; consequently
the same channel gives e<=C(c1)c1/(sqrt(n)Q) over the unknown-radius ball.
This upper includes the branch labels and does not estimate the radius.

4. LOWER-BOUND AND SCALING AUDIT

I separately checked the critical-converse bookkeeping in
`outputs/research/sol_collision_precision/quantum_branch.txt`:

* Direct-sum reconstruction through branches of dimension at most Q has
  Kraus rank at most Q after composing encoding and decoding. Pinching to
  Schur output sectors and exact multiplicity regeneration preserve that
  bound.
* For vectorized A:V_j->V_k, the score moment is
  Tr(A J_i^(j) A* J_i^(k))/||A||_2^2. Applying the input spin-flip
  C=e^(-i*pi*J_y) gives C(J_i^T)C*=-J_i, so the corresponding Choi
  expectation is that of H=-sum_i J_i^(j) tensor J_i^(k). The sign is
  antiferromagnetic; no transpose factor is missing.
* The Schmidt-rank-Q projector bound
  |psi><psi|<=Q rho_j tensor I and its exchanged version imply
  <Pi_t><=Q D_t/(2 max(j,k)+1), D_t=(t+1)(2|j-k|+t+1).
  With H-deficit E_l=l(2|j-k|+l+1)/2, this yields the stated
  g_Q>=M/(32Q) near equal sectors.
* The exact thermal radius a_j=R_j/(j+1) is strictly increasing by the
  coherent-state likelihood-ratio representation. Also a_j/j decreases:
  with d=2j+1 and t=atanh(r),
  a_j/j=4t sum_{m>=1} pi^2*m^2/[(d^2*t^2+pi^2*m^2)(t^2+pi^2*m^2)],
  termwise decreasing in d. These exact directions justify nonnegative
  pairwise transport deficits for every output sector.
* For far sector changes, inputs x=2rj lie in a fixed compact interval
  [2,B]. The exact smooth extension a(j)=A(2rj,r) has derivative
  converging uniformly to L'(x)=1/x^2-csch(x)^2>0. Comparing to the
  nearest boundary k=j/(1-1/(16Q)) or j(1-1/(16Q)) gives
  |a_k-a_j|>=m_B/(16Q), hence score gap >=m_B^2/(512Q^2).
  For Q<=1/(32r), this is >=m_B^2 r/(16Q). Outputs outside the compact
  input band are handled by the exact monotonicities, not by an asymptotic
  expansion at large k.

The resulting internal candidate is e>=c(c0,c1)r/Q for Q<=1/(32r),
even with unlimited classical labels and arbitrary sector-changing decoders.
Together with the upper, this gives the matched order in that range. For
error epsilon with epsilon/r^2->infinity and epsilon<=O(r), the implication
is Q=Theta(r/epsilon), up to constants. Below the r^2 scale the converse
only gives Q=Omega(1/r); it is not a sharp all-accuracy branch law.

5. RESOURCE BOUNDARY AND FALSIFIERS

The code's exact finite label count is O(n^3), so D_total<=O(Q n^3).
This may be much larger than the separate exact Schur-cutoff total-D upper
D<=C n(1+log(1/epsilon)); the latter has largest branch
O(sqrt(n(1+log(1/epsilon)))) but does not establish the minimum Q needed
at a given error. No total hybrid-memory optimum follows from the branch
theorem. Practical gates, algebraic quadrature preparation, finite
precision, and fresh physical n-qubit output remain unpriced.

Decision-sensitive falsifiers are: a wrong sign/factor in the Choi spin
flip or score normalization; omission of the k=Q ladder edge; failure of
the trace-norm double-commutator estimate for a thermal polynomial; or an
allowed fixed-Q code with e=o(r). The candidate is internally checked but
not externally validated or novelty-cleared.
