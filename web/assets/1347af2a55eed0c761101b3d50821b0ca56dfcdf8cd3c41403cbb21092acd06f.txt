THERMAL PRIOR-ART EXTRACTION
Date: 2026-10-08
Scope: primary-source check for the stationary collective J+/J- thermal bath from I/2^N, conserved Schur-Weyl weights, and the proposed occupation nu~sqrt(N) transition in trace distance to full separability.

BOTTOM-LINE SCOPE

Several ingredients are established and must be credited. Li and Xu already treat the exact two-qubit collective thermal J± master equation from a Werner input; at the maximally mixed input, their stationary concurrence changes at nu=(sqrt(15)-3)/6, exactly the N=2 instance of the newer singleton threshold. The fully separable variance inequality sum_a Var(J_a)>=N/2 is also established spin-squeezing theory. Quantitative distance-based entanglement analysis of thermal collective-spin states is established in Mathé et al., using best separable approximation (BSA) and spin-squeezing inequalities.

The inspected sources do not identify the N-qubit stationary family with weights w_Nj=(2j+1)m_Nj/2^N, nor the claimed polynomial occupation exponent 1/2 for the half trace-norm distance D(rho_N(nu),SEP_N), nor a limit at nu=c sqrt(N). Mo–Altman–Garratt and Zeng study total-singlet Gibbs states and bipartite distillable entanglement/log-negativity; Moharramipour et al. study maximally mixed states in fixed symmetry sectors. Mathé et al. study a fully connected XXZ Gibbs family and BSA, not this bath-selected family or trace distance. This is a bounded source check, not a global novelty or correctness certificate.

EXACT OVERLAPS

1. Li–Xu, “Stationary entanglement and nonlocality of two qubits or qutrits collectively interacting with the thermal environment: The role of Bell singlet state,” arXiv:quant-ph/0505216v2 (25 July 2008; first submitted 2005).

Their Eq. (1), PDF p. 2, is the two-qubit thermal master equation with rates (nbar+1) and nbar multiplying collective J_- and J_+ dissipators. The initial standard Werner family is defined at Eq. (3), PDF p. 3; r=0 is I/4. Their stationary concurrence C_1 is Eq. (10), PDF p. 3:

  C_1=max(0,[1+3r+(18r-6)(nbar^2+nbar)]/[4(3nbar^2+3nbar+1)]).

Thus, for the maximally mixed input r=0, the stationary state is entangled iff nbar < (sqrt(15)-3)/6. Their text immediately below Eq. (11), PDF p. 3, states the general stationary-entanglement criterion; lines 98–100 of the arXiv HTML also explicitly discuss the r=0 maximally mixed input. The conclusion (PDF p. 8) reiterates collective-decay activation from maximally mixed input. This exactly preempts novelty claims about the basic two-qubit mechanism and pins down the N=2 instance of the later singleton threshold. It does not establish the all-N Schur-sector result, the full-separability distinction for N>2, the nu~sqrt(N) asymptotics, or a trace-distance law.

2. Tóth, Knapp, Gühne, and Briegel, “Spin squeezing and entanglement,” Phys. Rev. A 79, 042334 (2009), arXiv:0806.1048v3.

The generalized separability inequalities include Eq. (7b), PDF p. 3:

  (Delta J_x)^2+(Delta J_y)^2+(Delta J_z)^2 >= N/2.

The paper states that violation implies entanglement. It derives the bound for separable states from the product-state Bloch-vector constraint on PDF p. 3, and applies spin-squeezing inequalities to thermal spin models later in the article (e.g. PDF pp. 10–12). Hence the variance witness used in the current thermal-state work is established. This witness is a criterion for entanglement, not an exact full-separability test or a distance formula. It is distinct from the thermal-character note’s sharper randomized transverse-window measurement and its tail estimate.

3. Mathé, Usui, Gühne, and Vitagliano, “Estimating the best separable approximation of non-pure spin-squeezed states,” Quantum 10, 2078 (2026), DOI 10.22331/q-2026-04-21-2078; arXiv:2504.07814v2.

This is direct prior art for quantitative full-separability analysis in collective-spin thermal states. The paper defines fully separable states and the BSA in Sec. 2.1 (PDF pp. 5–6): BSA is the minimum weight t in a decomposition rho=(1-t)sigma_sep+t tau, not the half trace norm distance min_sigma 1/2||rho-sigma||_1. Its complete generalized spin-squeezing inequalities are given in Eq. (16), PDF pp. 7–8; this includes the variance witness above. Its Observation (28) gives a normalized SSI lower bound on BSA, PDF p. 13. The studied model is a fully-connected XXZ Hamiltonian with Gibbs states rho_T proportional to exp(-H/T), Eqs. (20)–(22), PDF pp. 10–11; all total-spin sectors are included with Gibbs weights. It reports numerical and analytic BSA bounds across thermal phases (abstract and Sec. 3, especially PDF pp. 13 and 16–18).

Therefore, “first quantitative distance-to-full-SEP study for thermal collective spins” is too broad. The narrower candidate distinction is this specific non-unital collective-bath stationary family, with its input-preserved Schur weights, the half trace-norm metric, and the proposed asymptotic nu_N exponent. BSA and trace distance are different metrics, and Mathé et al. do not prove a trace-distance-to-SEP limit of 0 or 1 for the bath state.

Narrow high-bath sublemma check: Mathé et al.'s fully-connected XXZ Hamiltonian (Eq. (20), PDF p. 10) becomes the isotropic all-to-all antiferromagnet when g_z=g>0. Its Gibbs state (Eq. (22), PDF p. 11) then has precisely the operator form Z_N^{-1} exp[-A J^2/N - b J_z/sqrt(N)] after setting A=beta g and choosing the size-dependent field beta h_N=b/sqrt(N) (with the sign adjusted to the paper's field convention). Thus the candidate Gibbs family used as an approximant is already inside their model class, including all total-spin sectors and their multiplicities. Their separable search is also constructive: Eq. (33), PDF p. 14, gives a positive mixture of permutationally and azimuthally twirled product states, and the iterative method adds product states and twirls them while preserving the target symmetry (PDF pp. 14–15). This preempts novelty for the mean-field AFM Gibbs family, use of all-J sectors, and a generic positive product-state ansatz. However, the paper's principal quantitative object is BSA (Eqs. (10)–(12), PDF pp. 6–7), with numerical BSA/GR bounds and candidate approximants; it does not state a half trace-norm convergence theorem for the particular reweighted isotropic Bloch-product mixture in the field scaling h_N=Theta(N^{-1/2}). In the inspected source there is also no matching explicit limiting error formula of the proposed Euler/Brownian-bridge tail form. This leaves a substantially narrower candidate contribution in the explicit approximant and its asymptotic global trace-distance bound, conditional on correctness and broader priority review.

4. Mo, Altman, and Garratt, “Thermal entanglement transitions from strong SU(2) symmetry,” arXiv:2610.00826v1, submitted 30 September 2026.

The abstract (PDF p. 1) and Secs. II–III (PDF pp. 3–6) define a Gibbs state restricted to the total SU(2) singlet sector, rho_0=Z^{-1} Pi_0 exp(-beta H), and study entanglement across an approximately equal half-system bipartition. Their entanglement quantities are lower bounds on distillable entanglement and logarithmic negativity expressed through subsystem spin. The later semiclassical analysis (Secs. V.2–V.3, PDF pp. 9–15) ties scaling changes in those bipartite quantities to finite-temperature ferromagnetic ordering: paramagnetic scaling is logarithmic in sqrt(N), with ordered-phase behavior enhanced by Goldstone modes and geometry. The paper also explains that an SU(2)-symmetric Lindbladian initialized in a singlet can prepare the singlet Gibbs state (Introduction, PDF pp. 2–3).

This is a genuine, very recent thermal entanglement-transition prior, but its state constraint, preparation, and metric differ: one fixed global-singlet sector and half-vs-half entanglement, versus the J± bath started in I/2^N, which preserves a distribution over all spin-j irreps while thermally redistributing m within each irrep, and the global distance to full separability. The paper contains no trace-distance-to-full-SEP result.

ADJACENT COLLECTIVE-SPIN AND SYMMETRY-SECTOR PAPERS

The closely neighboring result of Bassler, “Absence of entanglement growth in Dicke superradiance,” Phys. Rev. A 112, 053713 (2025), DOI 10.1103/qxx1-xr44 (arXiv:2504.13646), proves that zero-temperature collective decay from the fully excited, permutation-symmetric state remains fully separable. The proof represents diagonal symmetric states as positive mixtures of spin-coherent product states, using a truncated moment problem (APS article, Secs. II and IV). It establishes exact separability under that dynamics, but the state is confined to j=N/2 and is not the all-j thermal Gibbs family or the stationary bath state here; it gives no global trace-distance approximation bound.

5. Moharramipour, Lessa, Wang, Hsieh, and Sahu, “Symmetry enforced entanglement in maximally mixed states,” PRX Quantum 5, 040336 (2024), arXiv:2406.08542v2.

The paper defines the maximally mixed invariant state (MMIS) in a fixed symmetry sector; for SU(2) this is the total-singlet sector. Results 2–4 (PDF pp. 2–5) give exact bipartite entanglement measures, logarithmic scaling for continuous non-Abelian symmetry, and locally maximally mixed marginals. Their classification says the MMIS for an Abelian symmetry such as U(1) is fully separable, while the SU(2) invariant-sector state is entangled. Sec. II and the Schur-Weyl decomposition (PDF pp. 6–8) explain the strong-symmetry channel setting.

This is not the target bath state: at finite occupation the J± Lindbladian is non-unital, it changes J_z within each spin-j irrep, and the maximally mixed initial state supplies weights across all j rather than a fixed singlet (or U(1) charge) sector. The paper’s reported entanglement is bipartite formation/distillation/negativity, not distance to full SEP. It is relevant background for symmetry-protected entanglement but does not settle the candidate exponent.

6. Zeng, “Thermal Activation of Divergent Distillable Entanglement under Non-Abelian Strong Symmetry,” arXiv:2607.12710v2 (30 July 2026), cited by Mo et al.

Theorem 1 (PDF p. 2) treats finite-range, uniformly bounded, locally SU(2)-invariant chains restricted to global singlets and proves a representation-space distillation yield Y_N=(1/2)log_2 N+O_beta(1) in a nonzero high-temperature interval. The mechanism is measuring both halves’ representation labels (PDF pp. 2–4). This is still fixed-singlet-sector bipartite distillation, not the full-SEP trace distance of the J± bath family. Its temperature/size crossover is different from an occupation scale nu~sqrt(N).

NARROW NOVELTY POSITION

The safe candidate claim is not “thermal collective baths create entanglement,” “there is a thermal entanglement transition,” “spin squeezing detects the state,” “mean-field antiferromagnetic Gibbs states have product-mixture descriptions,” or “collective thermal states have full-SEP distance bounds.” Those have close or exact prior art above. The remaining narrow candidate is a particular explicit positive product-state approximant, together with an asymptotic global half trace-norm bound at the occupation/field scale nu=c sqrt(N), including its explicit error function. The Gibbs operator used in the approximant is already a fully connected isotropic XXZ/antiferromagnetic Gibbs state; novelty would lie in the state-specific construction and quantitative metric bound, not the underlying model class. At the start of this check, the thermal-character checkpoint marked the critical window unresolved; a separate candidate bound is now being developed and has not been validated by this literature audit. Treat it as candidate work pending correctness and broader priority review.
