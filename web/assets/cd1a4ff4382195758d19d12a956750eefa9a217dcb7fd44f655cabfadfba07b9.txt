NEW EXACT LEMMA: SIZE-FREE ONE-PIVOT COARSENING

Let tau=Tr/d, A=A* be a contraction in M_d, and let (Y_x,p_x) be any finite weighted family of Hermitian contractions. Define

  q_A = sum_x p_x ||[A,Y_x]||_(2,tau)^2.

For every 0<delta<=1 there is a single shifted spectral interval partition of A, with blocks P_k, whose block-pinching E(Y)=sum_k P_k Y P_k and a block-scalar contraction A_0=sum_k a_k P_k satisfy

  [A_0,E(Y_x)]=0 for every x,
  ||A-A_0||_(2,tau)^2 + sum_x p_x ||Y_x-E(Y_x)||_(2,tau)^2
      <= delta^2 + sqrt(q_A)/delta.                         (R1)

In particular, if 0<q_A<=1, setting delta=q_A^(1/6) gives the family-size- and dimension-independent bound 2 q_A^(1/3). If q_A=0, arbitrarily small delta yields exact block commutation in the limit. This is a weighted continuum analogue of the first, one-pivot step in almost-commuting-matrix rounding; importantly, it does NOT yet yield a common abelian algebra or an EB map.

PROOF

Diagonalize A with eigenvalues a_i in [-1,1]. For shift s uniform on [0,delta), put indices i,j in the same block exactly when floor((a_i-s)/delta)=floor((a_j-s)/delta). Let P_k be the resulting spectral projections. On each nonempty block define a_k to be the arithmetic mean of the a_i in that block and set A_0=sum_k a_k P_k. Then ||A-A_0||_op<=delta and [A_0,E_s(Y_x)]=0.

For a fixed pair i,j, a random shift separates them with probability min(1,|a_i-a_j|/delta). Therefore, using the exact Hilbert-Schmidt matrix-entry formula for a pinching,

  E_s ||Y-E_s(Y)||_(2,tau)^2
   = d^-1 sum_{i,j} |Y_ij|^2 min(1,|a_i-a_j|/delta)
   <= (d delta)^-1 sum_{i,j}|a_i-a_j||Y_ij|^2
   <= ||[A,Y]||_(2,tau) ||Y||_(2,tau)/delta
   <= ||[A,Y]||_(2,tau)/delta.

The penultimate inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the two arrays |a_i-a_j||Y_ij| and |Y_ij|; the first squared sum is ||[A,Y]||_2^2 and the second is ||Y||_2^2. Average over x and use Jensen, sum_x p_x ||[A,Y_x]||_2 <= sqrt(q_A), to obtain E_s(total loss)<=delta^2+sqrt(q_A)/delta. At least one shift attains no more than its mean, proving (R1). The q_A=0 endpoint follows because the cross-block entries vanish whenever a_i!=a_j; equal-eigenvalue degeneracies stay in one block for every shift.

The formula includes the trace normalization: every squared norm and entry sum above has the factor 1/d. No dimension or number-of-label factor is suppressed.

APPLICATION TO THE BROADCASTING FRAME

For the tracial selfcompatible channel in INITIAL, let ||H_x||_op<=theta, p_x be a prior, and q_H=sum_(x,z) p_x p_z ||[H_x,H_z]||_2^2. Averaging q_(A=H_z) over z gives exactly q_H, so some actual frame member has q_A<=q_H. INITIAL gives q_H<=4 theta^2 C^2 ebar, where C=2+sqrt(2) and ebar=sum_x p_x e_Phi(H_x). Normalize H by theta and put alpha=4 C^2 ebar/theta^2. For alpha<=1, the prior-weighted family is within squared L2 loss

  2 theta^2 alpha^(1/3)
    = 2(4 C^2)^(1/3) theta^(4/3) ebar^(1/3)

of one block-pinched family, with a block-scalar pivot commuting with every output. The bound has no dimension or frame-size factor. At ebar=0, take the limit delta down to zero; pairwise commutation is exact and the pinching loses no family mass.

This is strictly stronger than merely saying that each fixed pair is almost commuting: it handles the whole weighted family in one shot and produces a *single* center/block decomposition. However, E_s(Y_x) can be an arbitrary matrix within a block. The projection E_s is a conditional expectation onto a noncommutative block algebra, not an EB channel when any block has dimension >1. The exact same limitation appears in the fixed-pivot Bansil–Kachkovskiy theorem: their Theorem 1.3 makes one pivot commute with a whole tuple, while Corollary 1.4 obtains pairwise commuting approximants only with a tuple-size-dependent bound. A further, uniform way to resolve the residual blocks is still needed. In the commuting diagonal sign family with all balanced sign vectors in dimension d, q=0 yet any fixed number of sampled pivots leaves large blocks as d grows; thus a constant-number random-pivot recursion alone cannot establish the lift. The actual common basis exists in that example, but finding/controlling it requires use of the entire family.

NO EB CONCLUSION FROM (R1)

Replacing E_s by a rank-one measurement inside every block generally destroys the retained block matrices. For example, within a block of dimension m, Haar-random rank-one measurement has expected quadratic capture of a traceless contraction only O(1/m), whereas the block-preserving expectation captures its full squared norm. Therefore a block-diagonal approximation cannot be called an EB reconstruction. One must either prove a uniform recursive common-MASA approximation, prove the c=2 support inequality in c04_l05's dual, or prove the attributed instrument-slice gate in c04_l03.

An exact family makes the center-only failure sharp. Let d be even, fix a balanced sign vector z, and partition coordinates into B_+ and B_- according to z. Let H_h=diag(h) range uniformly over all balanced signs h (sum h_i=0). Every pair commutes, so q=0; the one-pivot block pinching leaves every H_h exactly unchanged. But the EB channel that measures only B_+/B_- and prepares the normalized identity in the observed block captures the two block means only. Its exact average loss is 1-1/(d-1), tending to 1. To see this, for n=d/2, S=sum_(i in B_+)h_i has E S^2=d^2/(4(d-1)); each block mean is ±2S/d, so mean capture is E[(mean_+²+mean_-²)/2]=1/(d-1). More generally, for any fixed partition of d coordinates into m nonempty blocks, the block-center EB channel has exact mean capture (m-1)/(d-1) and loss 1-(m-1)/(d-1). Thus a fixed K binary-pivot center recursion (m<=2^K) leaves loss tending to 1. This does not refute common EB rounding: the computational-basis measurement is a single EB channel that fixes the entire family. It proves only that the one-pivot center or any fixed-size block-label recursion is insufficient; the residual block algebra must be resolved using the entire frame or other structure.

PRIMARY LITERATURE / BOUNDARY

The one-pivot estimate (R1) is self-contained and does not import an almost-commuting theorem. For context, Bansil–Kachkovskiy, “On almost commuting matrices with respect to the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm,” arXiv:2608.31000v1 (31 Aug 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31000, Theorem 1.3 gives a fixed-pivot estimate for N matrices with a sqrt(N) factor, and Corollary 1.4 gives pairwise commuting approximants with constants depending on N. It supports the observation that standard fixed-tuple results do not automatically settle arbitrary frame size. This new one-pivot weighted statement is a direct random-shift proof; no novelty/priority claim is made. The source is a preprint; correctness and priority are not externally assessed here.
