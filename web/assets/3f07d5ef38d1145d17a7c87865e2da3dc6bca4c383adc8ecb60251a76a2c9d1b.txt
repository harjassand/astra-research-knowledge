VERDICT
-------
No algebraic defect found in the exact identity or its stated lower bounds. The result holds for an arbitrary CPTP endomorphism, including nonunital channels and channels with cross-sector Kraus operators. The essential structural hypothesis is that each ensemble state commutes with the full-rank mean sigma; pairwise commutation among distinct ensemble states is not needed. The normalized trace distance is T(rho,omega)=||rho-omega||_1/2, and with that convention the coefficient 3M-1 and the stronger 2M denominator are correct.

One interface condition should be made explicit wherever the memory conclusion is used: “quantum branch dimension at most Q” yields composite Kraus rank at most Q when the only quantum system passed from encoder to decoder is that branch (plus free classical labels). Free shared entanglement or any additional uncharged quantum side system is outside this factorization argument. No such resource is used in the algebraic channel lemma.

1. EXACT IDENTITY
-----------------
Let x have probability measure mu, rho_x be states, sigma=integral rho_x dmu>0, [rho_x,sigma]=0, and L_x=sigma^(-1/2)rho_x sigma^(-1/2). Then L_x=L_x^*>=0, [L_x,sigma]=0, integral L_x=I, and rho_x=sigma L_x=L_x sigma. Assume L_x<=M I for a supplied finite M>=1.

For a CPTP map Phi(Q)=sum_i A_i Q A_i^*, put B_i=A_i sigma^(1/2) and C=integral L_x^2 dmu. Trace preservation gives
  sum_i ||B_i||_HS^2 = Tr(sigma sum_i A_i^*A_i)=Tr sigma=1.
Expanding each squared commutator and summing Kraus terms gives
  D := sum_i integral ||[L_x,B_i]||_HS^2
     = Tr(C Phi(sigma)) + Tr(C sigma) - 2 integral Tr(L_x Phi(rho_x)).
Indeed, the two square terms are respectively Tr(L_x^2 sum_i B_iB_i^*) and Tr(L_x^2 sum_i B_i^*B_i); the latter sums to Tr(L_x^2 sigma). The cross term uses sigma^(1/2)L_x sigma^(1/2)=rho_x, and hence sum_i B_i L_x B_i^*=Phi(rho_x). No unitality is used.

Since [L_x,sigma]=0,
  integral Tr(L_x rho_x)=integral Tr(sigma L_x^2)=Tr(C sigma).
Substitution yields exactly
  D/2 = integral Tr[L_x(rho_x-Phi(rho_x))]
        + (1/2)Tr[C(Phi(sigma)-sigma)].
The commutation hypothesis is load-bearing precisely in the target-score equality above. Without it, integral Tr(L_x rho_x)=Tr(C sigma) is not generally valid. Pairwise commutation [rho_x,rho_y]=0 is stronger than needed.

2. OPERATOR BOUNDS, TRACE DISTANCE, AND CONSTANTS
------------------------------------------------
The operator inequalities are valid without any commutation between different L_x:
  C-I = integral (L_x-I)^2 dmu >= 0,
  MI-C = integral (M L_x-L_x^2) dmu >= 0.
The second integrand is positive because each eigenvalue lambda of L_x satisfies lambda(M-lambda)>=0. Thus I<=C<=MI. C itself need not be an effect; for M>1, E=(C-I)/(M-1) is an effect, 0<=E<=I.

For traceless Hermitian Delta and any effect E,
  |Tr(E Delta)| <= ||Delta||_1/2.
This is the exact half-trace normalization: writing Delta=Delta_+-Delta_- gives Tr Delta_+=Tr Delta_-=||Delta||_1/2, and 0<=Tr(E Delta_±)<=Tr Delta_±. Equality is attainable by the projector onto the positive (or negative) eigenspace.

Define e=integral T(Phi(rho_x),rho_x)dmu. The first term in the identity is bounded in absolute value pointwise by M T(Phi(rho_x),rho_x), because L_x/M is an effect. Also, by convexity/contractivity under averaging,
  T(Phi(sigma),sigma)
    =T(integral Phi(rho_x),integral rho_x) <= e.
For Delta=Phi(sigma)-sigma, Tr Delta=0 and C=I+(M-1)E, so
  |Tr(C Delta)|=(M-1)|Tr(E Delta)| <= (M-1)e.
Therefore
  D/2 <= M e + (M-1)e/2,
  e >= D/(3M-1).
The denominator is exactly the result of adding the M and (M-1)/2 losses; there is no missing factor of two under T=||.||_1/2.

If Phi(sigma)=sigma, the reference term is exactly zero. It is also zero whenever C=cI, since Tr Delta=0. Either condition gives
  D/2 <= M e, hence e >= D/(2M).
These stronger claims are valid separately; no implication between Phi(sigma)=sigma and C scalar is assumed.

M=1: Since 0<=L_x<=I and integral L_x=I, integral(I-L_x)=0 with a positive-semidefinite integrand forces L_x=I for mu-almost every x. Hence rho_x=sigma a.e. and D=0 for every B_i. The (M-1) denominator in E is not used. The generic inequality has only the trivial content 0<=e in this degenerate case; it should not be presented as a nontrivial converse at M=1.

3. ARBITRARY SECTOR TRANSPORT AND KRAUS RANK
--------------------------------------------
Suppose H=direct-sum_a H_a is a sector decomposition and the ensemble and sigma are block diagonal, with sigma positive. If the hypotheses above hold, the proof is on the full matrix algebra B(H). It places no block-diagonality restriction on A_i or B_i. Thus off-diagonal blocks P_a B_i P_b (a != b), which transport between sectors, are included in D and in the variational quantity
  Lambda_L(Q)=min { integral ||[L_x,B]||_HS^2 dmu : rank(B)<=Q, ||B||_HS=1 },
where the minimum ranges over the entire matrix space B(H), including all inter-sector blocks. Replacing this by a minimization restricted to sector-preserving B would be an invalid strengthening of the lower bound: it could exclude the actual decoder's transport.

For a code with classical branch y and quantum memory K_y of dimension q_y<=Q, write the encoder instrument's Kraus operators E_(y,alpha): H -> K_y and the decoder branch's Kraus operators D_(y,beta): K_y -> H. The composite channel has Kraus operators A_(y,beta,alpha)=D_(y,beta)E_(y,alpha), so rank(A)<=q_y<=Q. Since sigma^(1/2) is invertible, rank(B)=rank(A). For every nonzero B_i, homogeneity of the variational definition gives integral||[L_x,B_i]||^2 >= Lambda_L(Q)||B_i||^2; summing and using sum||B_i||^2=1 gives D>=Lambda_L(Q). Thus e>=Lambda_L(Q)/(3M-1), or the stronger denominator 2M in either special case above. Classical labels add branches/Kraus terms but do not raise their individual ranks.

This factorization requires that the transmitted quantum system really has dimension at most Q and that no other uncharged quantum system is available to the decoder. A classical label can be arbitrary and free. If a model supplies shared entanglement or an extra quantum side memory, the above E-then-D factorization through K_y alone no longer follows and the rank-Q conclusion needs a new argument. For continuous classical outcomes, apply the same nonnegative inequality under the outcome/Kraus integral; in finite input/output dimension one can also use a finite Kraus decomposition of the resulting Choi operator from the convex hull of rank-at-most-Q Choi terms.

4. EXACT TWO-SECTOR TESTS
-------------------------
Use basis |0>,|1>, mean reference sigma=diag(2/3,1/3), and equiprobable states
  rho_1=diag(5/6,1/6), rho_2=diag(1/2,1/2).
They are normalized, commute pairwise and with the non-scalar sigma, and average to sigma. Their likelihood operators are
  L_1=diag(5/4,1/2), L_2=diag(3/4,3/2), M=3/2,
  C=(L_1^2+L_2^2)/2=diag(17/16,5/4).
Thus I<=C<=M I and E=(C-I)/(M-1)=diag(1/8,1/2) is an effect. This explicitly checks the operator bounds with nonscalar sigma and nonscalar C.

(a) Nonunital, cross-sector reset. Let A_0=|0><0|, A_1=|0><1|. Then sum A_i^*A_i=I, whereas Phi(I)=2|0><0|, so Phi is nonunital and transports all population in sector 1 into sector 0. Both output states equal |0><0|, and Phi(sigma)=|0><0|. The exact average half-trace risk is
  e=(1/6+1/2)/2=1/3.
Here B_0=sqrt(2/3)|0><0| and B_1=sqrt(1/3)|0><1|. B_0 commutes with both L_x; each L_x has diagonal difference L_(x,00)-L_(x,11)=+/-3/4, so
  D=(1/3)(9/16)=3/16.
The two terms on the identity's right side are exact:
  integral Tr[L_x(rho_x-Phi(rho_x))]=( -1/8 + 3/8)/2=1/8,
  (1/2)Tr[C(Phi(sigma)-sigma)]= -1/32.
Their sum is 3/32=D/2. Further, T(Phi(sigma),sigma)=1/3, Tr(E(Phi(sigma)-sigma))=-1/8, and (M-1)|Tr(E Delta)|=1/16 <= (M-1)T=1/6. The generic bound reads e>=D/(3M-1)=3/56, correctly but loosely.

(b) Nonunital channel preserving sigma. Define a measure-and-prepare transition channel with probabilities P(i|j): P(0|0)=3/4, P(1|0)=1/4, P(0|1)=1/2, P(1|1)=1/2, and Kraus operators A_(ij)=sqrt(P(i|j))|i><j|. It is trace preserving, nonunital since Phi(I)=diag(5/4,3/4), and stationary on sigma since P sigma=sigma. On rho_1 it outputs diag(17/24,7/24), and on rho_2 it outputs diag(5/8,3/8), so e=1/8. The total probability weight of the two off-diagonal transitions when weighted by sigma is 2/3*(1/4)+1/3*(1/2)=1/3; each contributes squared likelihood difference 9/16, hence D=3/16. The identity's reference term vanishes exactly, and the sharper inequality is verified:
  e=1/8 >= D/(2M)=1/16.
This case confirms the stronger bound does not require unitality.

5. STATUS, SOURCES, AND FALSIFIERS
---------------------------------
Status: algebraically verified here; the formulas are finite-dimensional and self-contained. All risks and squared commutator sums in the exact examples above are rational (the Kraus amplitudes may contain square roots); these are hand-checkable calculations, not a numerical proof. No external mathematical review or novelty/priority assessment is claimed.

The source file cites Bisio, D’Ariano, Perinotti, and Sedlák, “Memory cost of quantum protocols,” Phys. Rev. A 85, 032333 (published 2012-03-30), DOI https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.85.032333. Section V.B states that a channel has a d-dimensional quantum-memory realization with classical memory iff it has a Kraus representation whose operators have rank at most d (equivalently rank(K_i^*K_i)<=d); the article’s published PDF is at https://www.quantum.physics.sk/rcqi/research/publications/2012/2012sedlak_memory_cost_pra85_032333.pdf. The local E-then-D composition proof above is the direct special case used here; we do not transfer unrelated multi-step memory claims.

Load-bearing checks/falsifiers:
1. Drop [rho_x,sigma]=0 and the step integral Tr(L_x rho_x)=Tr(C sigma) can fail; the displayed exact identity is not asserted then.
2. Drop sigma>0 and B_i=A_i sigma^(1/2) need not have rank equal to A_i; the rank-transfer conclusion needs support restriction/reformulation.
3. Restrict Lambda to within-sector matrices and arbitrary inter-sector transport is omitted.
4. Add a free shared quantum side resource and “rank at most Q” may no longer describe the composite Kraus operators.
5. At M=1 the ensemble is constant a.e.; no denominator involving M-1 should be used.
