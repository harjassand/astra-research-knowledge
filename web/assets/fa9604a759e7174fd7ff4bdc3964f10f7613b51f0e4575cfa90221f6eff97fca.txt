Can a counterexample to direct ﬁniteness
be hyperbolic?
8 October 2026
Abstract
This note describes a proposed aﬃrmative answer using OpenAI’s October construction
of a torsion-free group algebra that is not directly ﬁnite. The idea is to retain its algebraic
identities and extend its diagram argument to obtain a linear isoperimetric inequality. We
explain the construction, give the proposed extension, and compare it with the marked-line
and Leavitt-algebra approaches to nonsoﬁcity. The conclusion remains provisional: the
argument needs independent checking, and an apparently conﬂicting published assertion
about idempotents in positive characteristic must be reconciled.
1 The question and the proposed answer
Can one ﬁnd a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group G for which F2[G] is not directly ﬁnite? A
suﬃcient algebraic certiﬁcate is
ab = 1, ac = 0, c ̸= 0. (1)
Indeed, ba = 1 would give c = b(ac) = 0 . Such a group is necessarily nonsoﬁc, since group
algebras of soﬁc groups over ﬁelds are directly ﬁnite [ 7]. Nonsoﬁcity alone does not establish
failure of direct ﬁniteness.
The most promising route is the labelled-graph construction in A Torsion-Free Group Al-
gebra That Is Not Directly Finite (4 October 2026) [ 1]. Its algebraic identities depend on local
parity conditions, while its topology is controlled by excluding certain conﬁgurations of labelled
paths. The proposed argument below uses a stronger application of the same path-exclusion
estimate to control disk ﬁllings. If valid, a suitable choice of the original graphs would already
give a hyperbolic group.
The distinction between the source and this extension matters. The October manuscript
asserts ﬁnite presentability and torsion-freeness; hyperbolicity is the additional conclusion pro-
posed here. The disk argument and its use of the source estimates are presented for re-
view. There is also a speciﬁc literature issue: a positive-characteristic idempotent assertion of
Bartels–Lück–Reich would exclude the proposed conclusion. Section 7 explains why this re-
mains unresolved. This note was prepared with language-model assistance and has not received
independent expert veriﬁcation.

The source arranges these parities using lines in the projective plane over F128, together

133, and the distinguished degree is 136. Each label has the same number of slots as its inverse.
Pairing those slots deﬁnes the edges. Crucially, changing the pairings changes the geometry
without changing the parity calculation. The separate topological argument is needed to keep
the root term of c from cancelling; distinctness of all path labels is unnecessary.
3 The proposed linear ﬁlling bound
Proof idea. If a minimal disk ﬁlling had interior boundary much larger than its exterior word,
then cutting the inner boundaries into bounded pieces and applying a planar separator would
produce a bounded collection of paths that is almost completely paired. This is precisely the
conﬁguration excluded by the October manuscript’s path estimate. Hence interior boundary
length is linear in the exterior length, and therefore so is ﬁlling area. The constants below
make this extraction precise.
All lengths in this section count edge traversals. A comparison pairs two disjoint intervals
of positions carrying inverse words in reverse order; each paired pair of positions must tra-
verse distinct underlying unoriented graph edges . Repeated traversals elsewhere are allowed.
Comparisons use disjoint positions.

Two features of the proposed extension deserve particular scrutiny. The outer word need
not lift to Γ, so same-edge surgery is used only on inner boundaries. Also, excluding fully
paired spheres alone is insuﬃcient: the argument needs the stronger exclusion of nearly fully
paired bounded collections.
4 Selecting the same graphs for algebra and geometry
The order of quantiﬁers in the source estimate is essential. The October manuscript [ 1] ﬁrst
chooses ε > 0. For every subsequently ﬁxed triple (K0, C, I), it asserts that the prohibited path
conﬁgurations occur with probability o(1), under its matching law conditioned on girth at least
L = ⌊c0 log n⌋, as the graph size n grows. The conﬁgurations include the closed collections
above and also allow one immersed interval beginning at either designated root, xA or xB.
The source’s switching argument also asserts that the girth-conditioned law is nonempty.
The same law is asserted to give component diameter O(L) with probability 1 − o(1). Together
with the minimum-degree bound, this supplies the short-closure constant D0. We may therefore
ﬁx all the constants in Theorem 2 before letting n grow. The construction does not require an
estimate uniform over growing values of K0, C, I.
There are two substantial probabilistic inputs here. First, a switching argument controls
edge prescriptions after conditioning on high girth ; ordinary independence cannot be assumed.
Second, a weighted word count treats repeated traversals by multiplicity and gives exponen-
tial decay for every ﬁxed bounded pattern. These are inputs from the source, rather than
consequences of the disk estimate.

The source also uses path exclusion to rule out reduced spherical cone diagrams and to
protect the root-to-vertex labels. Its topology argument gives π2(X) = 0 and hy ̸= 1 for y ̸= xB.
Because X is two-dimensional, its simply connected universal cover is then contractible. Thus
X is a ﬁnite-dimensional classifying complex, which excludes torsion in G.
Corollary 3 (Conditional conclusion) . Assuming the stated source estimates and the disk
argument above, one can choose the October labelled graphs so that G is torsion-free and word-
hyperbolic, while F2[G] is not directly ﬁnite.
Proof. Fix the constants for the disk exclusion and those for the source’s rooted spherical
exclusion. The diameter condition and both exclusions hold simultaneously with probability
1 − o(1) under the same conditioned matching law. Select one suﬃciently large outcome. The
topology argument gives torsion-freeness and protects c; Proposition 1 gives (1); and Theorem 2
gives hyperbolicity.
This selects a stronger event than the original ﬁnal assembly. It does not say that every
graph satisfying the original spherical exclusion is hyperbolic. The construction remains exis-
tential: no speciﬁc graph of the required size, or expanded list of the elements a, b, c, is supplied
here.
5 How the constructions diﬀer
Labelled graphs and parity . In the October manuscript, parity cancellation is used to
obtain the identities ( 1) in a group algebra. It develops the preceding zero-divisor construc-
tion [ 2]: both use cancellation in pairs of synchronously labelled paths. A special root changes
one zero product into a product equal to 1. The probabilistic and topological arguments are
used to show that the other factor c survives. This is stronger information than nonsoﬁcity
alone.
Marked lines and rank. The marked-line manuscript [ 3] uses punctured aﬃne lines, Carte-
sian blocks of triangle relations, and a rank comparison. Its triangle complex has negatively
curved links, giving geometric control independently of the rank argument. The original claim
is a torsion-free hyperbolic group that is not residually ﬁnite. A separate, proposed exten-
sion to approximate permutation representations would establish nonsoﬁcity: rectangle words
force a rank lower bound, while incidence identities force an incompatible upper bound. That
extension does not provide a, b, c satisfying ( 1).
Leavitt algebras and expansion. The Leavitt unit-group construction [ 4] uses a property-
(T) subgroup, a commuting subgroup that cannot be locally approximated by ﬁnite groups,
and elements that transport these subgroups into one another. Expansion obstructs soﬁc
approximations. Although the ambient Leavitt algebra itself is not directly ﬁnite, a map onto
that algebra does not lift a one-sided inverse to the group algebra of its unit group. Nor does
this construction itself give a hyperbolic group.
The earlier September direct-ﬁniteness constructions use another route: ﬁnite-group idem-
potents and matrix compression, followed by an embedding of a matrix algebra into a group
algebra [ 5, 6]. These share rank and interpolation ideas with the marked-line construction,
but their displayed presentations contain torsion. Thus there are shared ideas, especially
ﬁnite-dimensional rank obstructions, without a known transformation that preserves the direct-
ﬁniteness certiﬁcate between the groups.

6 Why the other geometric routes are less direct
The marked-line complex obtains curvature from its vertex links. Applying that method to the
original cone complex encounters a concrete obstruction. Repeated label types create many
four-cycles in the rose link. The necessary angle inequalities force the total angle available
at cone apices to be at most 68π. On the other hand, minimum graph degree 129 supplies
at least 64 edge-disjoint cycles, each requiring apex-link length at least 2π. Consequently
the original triangulation cannot be made locally CAT (0) merely by choosing Euclidean or
hyperbolic shapes for its triangles. This concerns that triangulation; it does not rule out
another geometric model for the group.
There is a similar limitation on a direct graphical small-cancellation argument. The ﬁxed
alphabet and high degree force long pairs of paths with the same label. A counting argument
gives a limiting lower bound greater than 0.49 for the longest-piece/global-girth ratio, whereas
Ollivier’s global metric criterion requires it to be less than 1/6 [9]. This does not rule out every
cycle-relative graphical condition. It explains why the proposed disk argument uses a more
speciﬁc exclusion of almost fully paired closed collections instead of a bound on all open-path
overlaps.
Algebraic transfers require care as well. A homomorphism G → H preserves the two
equalities in ( 1), but gives a counterexample in F2[H] only if the image of c remains nonzero.
Injectivity on its support is suﬃcient. Conversely, a hyperbolic group surjecting onto G does
not lift the identities to its own group algebra. Neither a quotient argument nor a hyperbolic
cover therefore settles the question without additional control.
7 The unresolved idempotent issue
The published hyperbolic-group paper of Bartels–Lück–Reich states a positive-characteristic
trivial-idempotent consequence that would apply to F2[G] for torsion-free hyperbolic G [10,
p. 33]. Taken as stated, this excludes the proposed conclusion, because ( 1) gives the nontrivial
idempotent
e = 1 − ba, e 2 = e.
Indeed ba ̸= 1, while ba ̸= 0 follows from a(ba)b = (ab)2 = 1.
There is a diﬃculty in following the cited justiﬁcation. The general idempotent theorem
in the companion paper requires an additional stable-ﬁniteness, characteristic-zero, or soﬁcity
hypothesis [ 11, Theorem 1.12]. The relevant positive-characteristic statements in Formanek
and Burger–Valette control traces; their full idempotent conclusions use characteristic-zero
arguments [ 12, 13].
Trace information alone cannot exclude this particular idempotent. Since ab = 1 , one has
e = ab − ba. For every conjugacy class C, the sum of the coeﬃcients on that class satisﬁes
trC(ab) = tr C(ba), trC(e) = 0 ,
because gh and hg are conjugate. Likewise, for R = F2[G],
R = baR ⊕ eR, baR ∼= R, [eR] = 0 in K0(R).
Thus these trace and K0 conclusions already hold for any hypothetical counterexample; an
additional argument is needed to deduce e = 0.
This does not establish that the published assertion is false. It identiﬁes an unresolved
conﬂict that must be settled before the aﬃrmative conclusion can be accepted. The two central
mathematical checks are therefore the ﬁxed-exterior disk argument above and the precise scope
of the positive-characteristic idempotent result, together with the source probability estimates
on which the construction rests.
