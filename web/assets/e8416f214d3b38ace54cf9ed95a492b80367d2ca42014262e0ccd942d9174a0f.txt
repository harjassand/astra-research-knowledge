1. Strongest theorem candidate: finite-variance recovery under global corruption

Clean data are independent (a_i,y_i), a_i~N(0,I_d), y_i=(a_i^T x*)^2+zeta_i, with q=||x*||>0, E[zeta_i|a_i]=0 and E[zeta_i^2|a_i]<=sigma^2. One adversary sees the complete clean dataset and replaces at most epsilon m entire pairs before learner seeds are sampled. The learner is given epsilon, delta, requested relative optimization tolerance p>0, and R>=sigma/q^2; it is not supplied q or sigma.

The exact internal guarantee (with the sufficient inequalities retained in the source artifacts) is
  dist(xhat,x*) <= p q + C (sigma/q)[sqrt(epsilon)+sqrt(D_p/m)],
  D_p = d log(C sqrt(d)/p)+log(C/delta).
The initializer has sample cost Otilde(d(1+R^2)^2) and requires epsilon log(e/epsilon)<=c/(1+R^2), with epsilon_alg=max(epsilon,1/m) used at the zero-contamination endpoint. The same m points are reused by all stages. For fixed R the arithmetic time is near-linear in dense input length md, with explicit PCA factor (1+R^2)^2, confidence logarithms and O(log(1/p)) robust-gradient calls. The projected A24 refinement needs only a small absolute B_m gate; the full method still inherits the initializer's R-dependent conditions. This is not a bounded-bit implementation theorem.

Original mechanism and indispensable dependencies:
(a) Bounded PSD shift Z_L=xi sqrt(L+clip(y,[-L,L])) a has target S_L=(L+q^2)I+2x*x*^T and clipping bias at most (15q^4+sigma^2)/L. The scale L is selected from the same corrupted observations through scalar median-of-means. A deterministic L mesh makes clean concentration uniform over that selection. Gaussian subset norms control every fractional adaptive deletion. Full-clean21epsilon stability yields survivor20epsilon stability, matching the actual ICML2023 PCA theorem.
(b) The gradient score has mean (3||x||^2-q^2)x-2<x,x*>x* and locally bounded covariance of the form Cq^4 dist(x,x*)^2 I+C sigma^2 q^2 I. Only the conditional second moment is used.
(c) Iterates lie on one of at most two deterministic dyadic grids selected through the acquired norm. A union bound over the finite proof grid applies to the original clean block event uniformly over every adversarial replacement. The covariance-oblivious Depersin--Lecue mean solver then charges fresh solver failure only across actual queries. No grid is enumerated. Projection into a known convex ball plus an explicit shrink-and-round operation gives A24's invariant geometry.
(d) Imported algorithmic dependencies are ICML2023 stable-PCA Theorem C.1/Algorithm5 and published AoS2022 Depersin--Lecue Theorem2.1 plus its deterministic clean-block event and conditional solver proof. Their precise data/randomness/resource interfaces are preserved.

A25 establishes the present noise frontier and its limits. The signed moment E[y aa^T]=q^2I+2xx^T admits a finite-variance Rayleigh median estimator at m=O((d+log(1/delta))/epsilon) under epsilon<=c/(1+s^2), s=sigma/q^2, but its net search takes exponential time. Thus the near-linear shifted method's m~d s^4 and logarithmic contamination loss are not proved optimal.

A25 v7 also closes real eigenspace sharpness for too-small shifts: conditional noise has rare value sign(a1^2-a2^2)2L and the compensating common value, with exact conditional variance sigma^2.
