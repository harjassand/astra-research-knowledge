# Metastable cell-fate transitions: model result, decision rule, and limits

This report links the first-cycle action/barrier calculation with the cycle-2
attack on hidden observations and state-dependent population growth. The
strongest outcome is a precise warning: barrier and action calculations can
provide conditional intervention rankings inside an acquired stochastic
model, but bulk endpoint composition is not generally a lineage reach
probability when fate states grow differently. A small exact counterexample
shows the failure, and a second finite calculation gives an assay-corrected,
growth-balanced fixed-dose decision rule.

No biological dataset was supplied or analyzed. All numerical examples are
synthetic model calculations. No cell fate, gene target, or intervention is
validated here.

## Conditional mathematical result: dose timing from the action

For the controlled diffusion
\[
dX_t=\{b(X_t)+u(t)G(X_t)\}\,dt+\sqrt{2\varepsilon A(X_t)}\,dW_t,
\]
the Freidlin–Wentzell action is
\[
I_u[x]=\frac14\int(\dot x-b-uG)^\top A^{-1}(\dot x-b-uG)\,dt.
\]
If the source-to-target minimizing path is unique up to time shift,
nondegenerate after phase fixing, and differentiable under small perturbation,
the envelope theorem gives
\[
S(\theta v)=S(0)-\theta\int v(t)\kappa(t)\,dt+O(\theta^2),
\quad
\kappa(t)=\frac12G(x_*)^\top A(x_*)^{-1}(\dot x_*-b(x_*)).
\]
Under an \(L^2\) energy budget \(E\), the local first-order optimum is the
matched positive part of \(\kappa\), with action decrease
\(\sqrt E\|\kappa_+\|_2\). The complete assumptions and timing-noise expansion
are in action_timing_theorem.md; the operational acquisition, confidence,
actuator-noise, and falsification procedure is in decision_algorithm.md.

The result is an action derivative, not a finite-noise fate-probability
theorem. It does not include changing optimal paths, model fitting
uncertainty, an unmeasured assay, or finite-noise prefactors. A probability
prediction requires either validated small-noise asymptotics or an independently
checked finite-state transition model.

### Finite double-well illustration

For \(V_q(x)=(x^2-1)^2/4-qx\), at \(\varepsilon=0.05\), the exact zero-dose
barrier is \(0.25\), and the one-dimensional Eyring–Kramers approximation
gives rate \(0.00151657\) and mean wait about \(659.38\) time units. The same
approximation gives dose \(q=0.0857907\) for 50% commitment by \(T=100\).
For the analytic instanton, \(\|\kappa\|_2=0.5\), and a local action decrease
of \(0.05\) uses nominal matched-pulse energy \(0.0100\), versus \(0.0400\)
for a uniform pulse over the same window. These are model computations, not
validated probabilities or biological doses.

The finite acquisition illustration uses 58 independent reads in each of
401 time bins, 23,258 kernel reads total, a sub-Gaussian scale of 0.05, and a
97.5% simultaneous kernel confidence band. The pulse policy's lower-kernel
energy for the same local action decrease is 0.0160 after a one-sided
multiplicative actuator-gain debit and a second-order *mean* timing-jitter
correction. The timing correction is not a confidence bound; a real
controller must replace it with a measured one-sided or worst-case jitter
bound. A separate 90%-sensitivity/95%-specificity endpoint assay needs 1,022
independent cells for a five-percentage-point Hoeffding precision target at
95% confidence, before calibration cells, failed tracks, and well clustering.
Reproducible output is model_demo_output.json.

## Cycle-2 result: growth can reverse what a bulk fraction says

The three-state source/target/avoid model is defined and solved in
CYCLE2_REPORT.md. With \(T=3\), \(\lambda=0.1,\mu=0.9\), and net growth
\((g_S,g_R,g_A)=(0,1,0)\), the probability that a representative founder
reaches target by \(T\) is \(0.0950\), yet target cells make up \(0.5254\) of
the continuum endpoint population. A second model with
\((\lambda,\mu)=(0.9,0.1)\) and \(g_R=-0.8858958\) produces the same endpoint
target fraction but founder reach probability \(0.8552\). This is an exact
single-snapshot nonidentifiability counterexample within the stated model.
It is not an attack on methods that use time series, purified starts, direct
growth measurements, or lineage data.

The cycle-2 controller randomizes one fixed dose against vehicle, counts one
preselected representative lineage per founder, and corrects a binary noisy
endpoint assay. It splits one Hoeffding error budget over both trial arms and
both assay-calibration proportions, and includes a supplied 99% actuation
certificate. It subtracts intervention cost and a one-sided actuation debit.
In the synthetic example, 500 founders per arm produce NO-GO. At 3,000
founders per arm, the vehicle reach interval is [0.1415, 0.2585] and the dose
interval is [0.3439, 0.4608]. After a 0.05 intervention cost and 0.02
actuation debit, the lower net gain is 0.0154, so the rule says APPLY *within
this synthetic model*. The total is 19,280
endpoint and assay-calibration reads; lineage imaging and acquisition of the
actuation certificate remain additional resource costs. The result is close
to threshold and is not a biological recommendation.

The exact closed form and a separate 30,000-step RK4 integration agree to
\(8.6\times10^{-15}\) in the finite counterexample. The implementation and
output are cycle2_reach_avoid.py and CYCLE2_MODEL_OUTPUT.json.

## What primary literature already covers

The basic barrier-exponential mechanism is established for appropriate
reversible diffusions; this work does not claim a new Kramers theorem
[Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard, and Klein, *Metastability in Reversible Diffusion
Processes I*](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/172). Cell-fate models
already link stimulus strength and duration to EMT switching, and experiments
already test pulse persistence and hysteresis
[Tian, Zhang, and Xing, Biophysical Journal (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3752104/)
and
[Celià-Terrassa et al., Nature Communications (2018)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-07538-7).

OLAC already uses Freidlin–Wentzell actions and transition networks to control
noise-induced switching in biophysical network models
[Wells, Kath, and Motter, Physical Review X 5 (2015), 031036](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.5.031036).
The 2025 landscape-control paper directly optimizes barrier heights and
stable-state occupancy against OLAC on model EMT and stem-cell networks; its
outputs are model computations, and its authors identify landscape
quantification and experimental validation as open work
[Lv et al., Communications Physics 8 (2025), article 240](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-025-02160-8).
The transition-rate estimator Transcompp already fits transition and
phenotype-specific proliferation parameters from replicated state-fraction
time series, with resampling-based uncertainty
[Jagannathan et al., Bioinformatics 36 (2020), 2813–2820](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021).
Thus the cycle-2 example is a data-design boundary for a single bulk snapshot,
not a claim that existing time-series growth-aware inference fails.

Growth is not just a sampling nuisance: a 2023 experimental/theoretical study
of a synthetic mutual-repression circuit reports growth-rate-induced
landscape bifurcation and changes in fate decisions
[Zhu, Chu, and Fu, Nature Chemical Biology 19 (2023), 1097–1104](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-023-01302-9).
A 2026 article-in-press shows in illustrative cell-fate systems that molecular
noise can redirect reactive paths away from deterministic least-action
trajectories and simple barrier models
[Liu, Zanca, Stumpf, and Ham, npj Systems Biology and Applications (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41540-026-00762-8).
These results reinforce the need to measure growth and reactive paths rather
than infer them from an equilibrium cartoon.

The complete bounded literature notes are in prior_art.md. The novelty status
of the action-kernel lemma, the robust fixed-dose rule, and their combination
is UNKNOWN; adjacent methods exist and no comprehensive priority search was
performed.

## Astra boundary check

The pinned Astra repository was checked selectively before analysis. N35
concerns a conditional finite-horizon jump-process safety bound for
mass-action reaction networks with supplied integer complexes, inward
polytope collars, bounded predictable rates, and buffered lattice starts. It
does not supply a cell-fate action, optimal intervention, or biological
validity result. N77 is a source-reported counterexample to inferring
classwise-uniform stochastic tightness from one deterministic common
attractor; its underlying full proof and replay data were not available in
the checked artifact. Neither card is used as a premise here. Full route,
scope, and provenance notes are in ASTRA_RETRIEVAL.md.

## Decision and next evidence

The actionable contribution is a pair of conditional decision rules plus
explicit NO-GO conditions, not a claim that a candidate perturbation is ready
for cells. Before any biological claim, acquire:

1. a lineage-resolved transition dataset with state-dependent growth,
   classifier calibration, and censoring retained;
2. a delivered-dose and timing-error calibration with a stated confidence
   bound;
3. matched, uniform, and constant-dose randomized controls at equal dose and
   observation budgets;
4. independent validation of fate persistence after withdrawal and of the
   assumed reduced state model.

If those data are unavailable or the robust interval crosses the decision
threshold, the controller abstains. Biological validity remains UNKNOWN.
