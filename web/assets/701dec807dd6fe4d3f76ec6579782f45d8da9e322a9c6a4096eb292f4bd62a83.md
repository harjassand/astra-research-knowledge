# Inverse molecular design for reaction-selective catalysts

## Finding

The strongest defensible result from this track is a conditional identifiability theorem: if the candidate representation erases a molecular symmetry that reverses the signed reaction outcome, no predictor or optimizer using that representation can choose the desired product hand. The result is rigorous and experimentally testable, but it is not a claim of a new catalyst or of priority in stereochemistry-aware learning. Recent work already predicts absolute configuration, and the strongest current catalyst-optimization baseline already uses mechanistic features and prospective experiments.

The useful next step is to make signed selectivity, catalyst stereochemistry, conditions, and synthesis/assay costs explicit in the design interface. Reported prediction error should be converted to a selection certificate only if it is a valid uniform error bound on the candidate set; MAE/RMSE does not give that guarantee.

## Reconstructed design problem

For a fixed substrate pair and fixed conditions `q` (solvent, temperature, concentration, reductant/additives, catalyst loading, and conversion regime), the candidate is a **fully specified catalyst complex** `c`: ligand constitution, absolute stereochemistry, metal, coordination state, charge, spin state, and relevant conformer ensemble. For two competing products `R` and `S`, a decision-aligned target is the signed log rate ratio

`y(c,q) = log(k_R/k_S)`

or the signed product ee with a predeclared assignment of `R` and `S`. If the target is instead `|ee|` or `|ΔΔG‡|`, the model can optimize selectivity magnitude but cannot choose the desired product configuration.

The evaluator can be a wet assay measuring product identity, yield, and signed ee, or a computational approximation using competing transition-state ensembles. Those are distinct oracles: a DFT-derived barrier difference is not the experimental endpoint absent the usual kinetic assumptions and model-error accounting. For two competing channels in the kinetic/TST regime and equal prefactors, with `Δ = G‡_S - G‡_R`,

`ee = tanh(Δ/(2RT))`.

If prefactors differ, the signed log rate ratio also contains the log-prefactor ratio. Product racemization, reversibility, changing active catalyst speciation, or conditions that change the enantiodetermining step can break the simple mapping.

## Best current primary baseline and its actual interface

Gallarati et al., *Nature* (online 11 February 2026), is the closest strong baseline for sparse-data inverse design of chiral molecular catalysts. It maps a **user-defined ligand library** into precomputed mechanism templates for TSRC, an intermediate, and TSRE, computes conformationally informed descriptors, and uses MLR/GPR and EDBO+ to propose experiments. This is a reaction-specific workflow: the relevant catalytic states/template and reaction class are supplied. In the prospective reaction, solvent, reductant, and temperature were fixed; the search optimized ligands, not the full condition space.

The physical-chemistry cost is substantial and often hidden by a headline “active-learning” label. About 250 conformers are generated from each structure and projected into 3D; the first coordination shell is constrained. In one benchmark, about 10,000 reductive-elimination pseudo-TSs for 29 ligands were generated in just over three minutes. Candidate geometries are refined with spin-polarized GFN2-xTB, filtered by vibrational frequencies, clustered over a 10 kcal/mol window, and evaluated with PCM/ωB97X-3c single-point energies; the campaign is not 70,000 fully optimized high-level DFT transition states. The final feature summary uses 189 geometry/steric plus 75 electronic features per TS/intermediate, summarized by minimum, maximum, Boltzmann-weighted mean, and lowest-energy conformer (756 values per structure).

For 195 N,N ligands in the new reaction, the authors report roughly 70,000 geometries completed in about two weeks, then 26 experimental ligand screens over four rounds, with batches of 4–6. The maximum reported barrier difference was 0.98 kcal/mol (68% ee). Only about 47% of the virtual ligand set was commercially available; noncommercial examples require synthesis. The campaign did not vary reaction conditions. Its prospective result is valuable source-reported evidence, but the main-article presentation does not provide a matched, equal-total-cost random/nearest-neighbor/descriptor-only comparator for attributing the observed 26-trial result specifically to the BO policy. “Exhaustive screen avoided” is therefore an operational comparison, not by itself a controlled causal comparison against the best equally informed acquisition baseline. The supplementary information was not audited in this track, so a comparator there remains UNKNOWN.

Other current primary baselines set the novelty boundary. CatDRX (2025) is a reaction-conditioned catalyst VAE, but its reported scope is homogeneous, single-step catalysis and its article says chirality is not represented; for its Pictet–Spengler task the authors also say absolute product configuration was not directly relevant to their target. ChemAHNet (2025/2026) explicitly predicts selectivity and absolute configuration from molecular inputs, so the general need to model chirality is established prior art. A 2026 SpecGen/robotics paper inverse-designs high-entropy OER materials and reports 78 min/sample and 32 mV improvement; that is useful evidence for materials automation but targets OER overpotential/activity, not signed competition between product channels, so its assay/representation claim does not transfer directly to molecular reaction selectivity.

## Theorem and design consequence

The full assumptions, proof, information bound, transition-state conversion, and argmax certificate are in [SYMMETRY_THEOREM.md](SYMMETRY_THEOREM.md).

For mirror catalysts `c` and `τc` in an achiral fixed reaction environment, signed selectivity changes sign: `y(τc)=-y(c)`. If a representation `φ` erases stereochemistry so `φ(τc)=φ(c)`, all predictors receive the same input for both members. On a balanced pair, signed sign accuracy is at most 1/2 and mean squared error is at least `y(c)^2`. For a nonzero target, the representation needs information distinguishing the pair; a single orientation bit is sufficient for pair identity, though not for selectivity magnitude. If the response is unsigned, this obstruction does not apply.

For a finite candidate set with a genuinely uniform model error bound `ε`, selecting the largest predicted signed `Δ` has regret at most `2ε`, and `hatΔ - ε ≥ 2RT atanh(ee*)` certifies a target signed ee threshold `ee*` under TST assumptions. These are elementary bounds. Their practical force is diagnostic: the reported round-4 prospective MAE of 0.33 kcal/mol in the 2026 study is a mean error, not a uniform bound on the selected ligand, so it cannot itself certify a target ee or a selected-candidate regret guarantee.

## Decisive experiment and matched controls

Run a blinded mirror-pair challenge before a larger inverse-design claim. Pre-register substrate, solvent, reductant, temperature, catalyst loading, conversion window, desired absolute product configuration, and the measured endpoint. Select a panel of exact enantiomeric catalyst pairs, prepare/verify matched enantiopurity, and assay both members in randomized order with replicate product-yield and chiral chromatographic measurements. Keep catalyst constitution, metal, substrate batch, and reaction conditions fixed within each pair.

Compare an otherwise matched stereo-blind representation/model to a stereochemistry-aware model trained on the same examples and labels. Hold out whole catalyst scaffolds or enantiomer pairs and, separately, a reaction class. The primary endpoints are sign accuracy, paired sign-reversal error `|y(c)+y(τc)|`, calibration of signed ee, and best-selected-candidate regret at equal wet-assay and synthesis budgets. Report RMSE/MAE only as secondary diagnostics. An informative control is the unsigned target `|ee|`, for which the stereo-blind model is not subject to the theorem.

For an actual BO comparison, give the learned-policy model, fingerprint/nearest-neighbor model, random selection, and exhaustive screening the same prior literature data, finite candidate list, synthesis feasibility information, and assay budget. Run all methods with fixed reaction conditions first; a second factorial stage can add condition optimization. Charge both proposal and realization cost: geometry generation/quantum-chemistry, template/mechanism setup, ligand synthesis attempts and failures, assay replicates, and product analysis. The 2026 prospective paper's 195-candidate/70,000-geometry/26-assay record is a useful scale anchor, not a cost-equivalent control.

The representation theorem is supported if the stereochemistry-blind predictor gives the same signed prediction for both mirror inputs while wet assays show significant sign reversal. The physical symmetry assumption is challenged if matched, verified enantiomers reproducibly fail to show opposite signed outcomes beyond assay uncertainty; investigate impurity, hidden chiral components, aggregation/speciation, reversibility, racemization, or product assignment before interpreting that as a model result. A claimed stereochemistry-aware advantage fails if it does not exceed sign chance on scaffold-held-out pairs under the preregistered equal-information/equal-cost comparison.

## Verdict

**Conditional theorem / internally reconstructed design discriminator.** The symmetry lower bound is proved from the stated interface. It is a rigorous representation requirement and a test design, not an experimentally validated catalyst-design improvement. The leading current methods already incorporate stereochemical/mechanistic information in different ways, so no novelty or priority claim is made. The decisive next gate is the blinded matched-enantiomer test plus an equal-cost selection comparison against current mechanistic and simple fingerprint baselines.
