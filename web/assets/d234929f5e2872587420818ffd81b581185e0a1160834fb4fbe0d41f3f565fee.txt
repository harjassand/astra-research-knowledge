A10 SUPERRESOLUTION — BOUNDED INITIAL INVESTIGATION (v1)
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: no original end-to-end capability established; strongest candidate is a finite-photon, unknown-K, positive-measure recovery theorem for photon-number-resolving (PNR) mode measurements. The candidate remains unproved and may be covered by broader classical mixture-estimation results. No success claim or priority claim.

1. Exact question and the tempting gap

The assigned target was a physically operational superresolution theorem for clustered off-grid emitters without an uncharged separation, centroid, calibration, or source prior. A first apparent gap was a two-source, unknown-centroid extension of parameter-decoupled SPADE. Live primary-source checking refuted it as a novelty direction: BLESS already studies two unequal independent single-photon emitters with unknown brightness and brightness-centroid, includes photon-number statistics and the rare two-photon sector, and reports a bounded-in-separation Cramér–Rao error. A separate 2026 paper removes brightness/coherence/phase nuisances by a two-mode invariant when the geometric midpoint is known. QSIPS already gives high-order cumulant combinations producing narrower effective PSFs for general non-Poissonian emitters and has experimental imaging results.

The most promising remaining direction is therefore narrower and more demanding: turn photon-count/cumulant superresolution into an estimator of an unknown positive K-atomic measure, with finite-sample risk in a merge-stable metric, no minimum separation, unknown K and brightnesses, and every control and calibration cost charged. Existing positive-source superresolution handles deterministic blurred samples, including exact no-separation recovery in the noiseless case; existing photon-statistics work reports image sharpening and two-source CRBs. I did not establish a finite-photon theorem bridging these models.

2. Operational model worth pursuing

Candidate scene: a compactly supported positive measure

    μ = Σ_{j=1}^{K} a_j δ_{x_j},   x_j ∈ [-A,A],   a_j>0,   K≤K_max,

with coincident atoms merged in μ. The target loss should be a generalized Wasserstein distance (mass and location errors included), so the target remains meaningful as atoms collide. A physically explicit photon-statistics access model is needed. One tractable candidate is K independent emitters, each emitting at most one photon in each exposure, with unknown Bernoulli rate μ_j; a known Gaussian PSF width σ; a programmable single-mode projection centered at t; and an ideal PNR detector resolving counts 0,…,K. Detector efficiency, dark counts, PNR calibration, scan positions, number and duration of exposures, laser dose/energy, and PSF calibration photons must be included in a finished theorem. For now these are declared model inputs; no experimental cost bound is claimed.

For a Gaussian HG0 projection, the source-j coupling probability is

    C_0(x_j-t) = exp(-(x_j-t)^2/(2σ²)),
    q_j(t) = η μ_j C_0(x_j-t),

where η is collection/detection efficiency. Given one exposure at setting t, the detected count Y_t is a sum of independent Bernoulli(q_j(t)) variables. Thus its probability generating function is exactly

    G_t(z) = E[z^{Y_t}] = Π_{j=1}^K (1-q_j(t)+q_j(t)z).

This corresponds to adaptive/nonadaptive mode projection using an SLM or equivalent mode transformer, coupling to one spatial mode, and PNR readout. The ideal model costs one frame per sample at each chosen t, up to K emitted photons per frame, and K+1 PNR outcomes. It assumes σ, the mode transform, spatial origin, and η are known; any real result must replace each assumption with a calibration stage and add its photons, energy, time, and uncertainty.

3. Exact derivation already available (internal algebra; not a new result)

The factorial-cumulant generating function is log G_t(1+u):

    log G_t(1+u) = Σ_j log(1+q_j(t)u)
                   = Σ_{ℓ≥1} (-1)^{ℓ-1} [Σ_j q_j(t)^ℓ] u^ℓ/ℓ.

Therefore, for every integer ℓ≥1,

    κ^{(F)}_ℓ(t) := [d^ℓ/du^ℓ log G_t(1+u)]_{u=0}
                 = (-1)^{ℓ-1}(ℓ-1)! Σ_j q_j(t)^ℓ.

For the Gaussian HG0 access above,

    Σ_j q_j(t)^ℓ = Σ_j (η μ_j)^ℓ exp(-ℓ(x_j-t)^2/(2σ²)),

so each order is an exact positive Gaussian-mixture profile with width σ/√ℓ and weights (η μ_j)^ℓ. This is the mathematical basis for high-order narrowing. It is an exact population identity only. It does not itself supply a finite-sample estimator, stable inversion, a source-count guarantee, or an advantage over the equally informed PNR/direct-imaging baseline.

For any fixed t, the full PNR law gives the elementary symmetric polynomials e_k(q_1,…,q_K) as its probabilities: P(Y=k)=e_k(q). Newton identities recover the power sums Σ_j q_j^ℓ from these e_k. For ℓ>K they remain algebraically determined by the first K roots; there is no claim that higher orders require observing ℓ photons in one frame. The statistical stability of these cancellations when q_j nearly coincide or when ημ_j is small is unresolved.

4. Resource and robustness status

Known model costs, if the candidate is pursued:

* Each exposure uses one programmed mode setting t, one exposure interval, and at most K emitted photons. The maximum expected emitted photons is Σ_j μ_j per exposure; the maximum detected count is K.
* The detector needs K+1 count bins (or a PNR detector with resolution at least K); detector dead time, saturation, background, and inefficiency must be charged in a physical protocol.
* A deterministic scan over T settings with n_r repetitions costs Σ_r n_r frames and (Σ_r n_r)(Σ_j μ_j) expected emitted photons, before calibration. An adaptive policy may choose t from prior counts but has the same exposure accounting.
* A usable guarantee must include the cost and uncertainty of σ, η, mode cross-talk, dark counts, and the coordinate origin. Treating these as exact oracle inputs would violate the assigned interface.

Not yet derived: an estimator with finite-sample confidence; a uniform modulus in generalized Wasserstein distance as atoms collide; an adaptive scan strategy with no centroid oracle; a lower bound matching its sample complexity; robustness to unknown K/rates and calibration; or a fair comparison with ideal direct imaging that retains the same PNR records and exposure budget. The population identity alone does not establish a practical capability.

5. Extremal cases and immediate limits

* Collision: if x_i=x_j, the target measure merges their masses. Atom labels are not identifiable and should not be scored. As x_i→x_j, Vandermonde/root conditioning can degenerate; no uniform atomwise-position claim is supportable without new analysis.
* Vanishing source brightness: if an a_j or μ_j is allowed to approach zero, its location carries arbitrarily little information. Any risk theorem must either use a mass-weighted loss and permit omission of negligible mass, or state and charge a lower brightness condition. A uniform per-atom guarantee is impossible without such a condition.
* Unknown PSF width: changing σ changes both the blur and every inferred position scale. A known-width assumption is not a calibration theorem.
* PNR statistics: the exact PNR generating function is richer than its mean. Comparing BLESS/QSIPS to mean-only direct imaging understates the classical baseline. A matched baseline must use the same PNR count law, exposure rate, and number of settings.
* High-order factorial cumulants have rapidly changing scale and involve cancellations of lower-order empirical moments. The population narrowing σ/√ℓ does not imply that the statistical error shrinks; this is the decisive missing quantitative gate.

6. Primary-source audit (live checked 2026-10-09)

All URLs below are primary papers/pages. No Astra theorem was used for this topic. Astra navigation was read first at the pinned source revision 939f697c151e779619d4fa7c4e2aee4bc59672fb; its task-topic routes contained no superresolution review card, so no archived claim or proof was reused.

(a) M. Tsang, R. Nair, X.-M. Lu, “Quantum Theory of Superresolution for Two Incoherent Optical Point Sources,” PRX 6, 031033 (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.00552. Source states the two-source weak-thermal model and the separation QFI; for a Gaussian PSF and known centroid, HG/SPADE attains constant small-separation information. Its Appendix D explicitly treats unknown-centroid misalignment and a beamsplitter/direct-imaging pilot, but does not give a uniform finite-sample all-parameter theorem.

(b) J. Řehaček et al., “Multiparameter Quantum Metrology of Incoherent Point Sources: Towards Realistic Superresolution,” PRA 96, 062107 (2017), https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07705. It jointly treats geometric centroid, separation s, and unequal source intensity q. Its exact QFIM (Eq. 10) yields for unbalanced sources 0<Q²=4q(1−q)<1 the small-s separation precision

    H_s ~ [Q²/(4(1−Q²))] Var(P²) s²,

and direct imaging has H_s^{int}∝s⁴, so the one-photon quantum measurement improves the local scaling by s^{-2} but still has vanishing information for unbalanced sources. The paper's setting is weak-light/iid single-photon localization; do not transfer this lower behavior to temporally correlated multi-photon source models.

(c) K. Katamadze et al., “Breaking Rayleigh’s curse for two unbalanced single-photon emitters: Beam modulation and examination of shot statistics,” Phys. Rev. Applied 23, 024066 (2025), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevApplied.23.024066; full arXiv v5: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13244v5. It assumes two independent emitters with per-exposure Bernoulli emission P_s(1)=μ_s, unknown μ=μ_a+μ_b, brightness centroid x_c=(μ_a x_a+μ_b x_b)/μ, and γ=(μ_a−μ_b)/μ. The scanned Gaussian detection mode has C_{s,m}=ξ_s^m e^{-ξ_s}, ξ_s=(x_s−x_D)²/(2σ²); an SLM changes HG0 to HG1 and a PNRD resolves the count. At x_D=x_c, the close-pair mean is μ_HG1≈[μ_aμ_b/(μ_a+μ_b)]d²/σ². The paper reports a BLESS/ESS PNR CRB and a two-photon-sector QCRB whose separation errors remain bounded as d→0, includes unknown centroid and brightness, and models cross-talk/background. It compares equal numbers of exposures across DI, SPADE, ESS and BLESS, but does not prove a finite-sample confidence/risk guarantee. Its mechanism materially changes the access model by using simultaneous two-emitter emissions; it cannot be compared only to a one-photon SPADE bound.

(d) X.-F. Qian, “Parameter-Decoupled Quantum Superresolution without Multiparameter Estimation,” arXiv:2609.21796v1 (submitted 2026-09-18), https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21796. For a two-source partially coherent state with nuisance brightness imbalance b, coherence magnitude g and relative phase φ, the paper uses the known symmetric midpoint and derives the nuisance-adjusted QFI

    F_{s|η} = s²/(32σ⁴) + O(s⁴)

for a Gaussian PSF. Its same-parity HG0/HG2 statistic D_02=log p_0−log p_2 cancels those three nuisances exactly; conditional retained-event information per incident photon asymptotically reaches the nuisance-adjusted QFI. The authors explicitly account for postselection probability in the per-incident comparison. This is a local Fisher-information result, not a finite-sample resolution theorem or a known-centroid-free protocol.

(e) F. Picariello et al., “Quantum super-resolution microscopy by photon statistics and structured light,” Optica 12 (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11654; DOI https://doi.org/10.1364/OPTICA.540264. QSIPS combines photocount cumulants to isolate a signal proportional to the j-th power of the effective PSF and reports √j narrowing (up to a linear-in-j gain with structured illumination). It covers non-Poissonian emitters, provides simulation and experimental image demonstrations, and uses PNR cameras, temporal frames, structured-light SLM settings, and image processing. This establishes that high-order cumulant sharpening itself is not new. It does not provide a uniform finite-photon minimax localization/reconstruction guarantee for unknown K, arbitrary collision patterns, and calibration uncertainty.

(f) B. Kurmanbek and E. Robeva, “Multivariate Super-Resolution without Separation,” https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09979. For a nonnegative K-atomic measure and a known decomposable PSF whose components satisfy T-system conditions, their convex feasibility program recovers exactly from at least (2K+1)^d noiseless grid samples without a minimum separation; in noise they bound generalized-Wasserstein recovery error, with a separation parameter entering the noisy bound. It is a deterministic pixelized convolution model, not a photon-count/PNR access or exposure budget, and the paper says it does not provide an algorithm for solving the stated program. This closes the noiseless positivity/no-separation part and prevents treating it as new.

7. Candidate next gate (not a success claim)

Freeze one target before continuing: for Gaussian PSF and per-exposure independent Bernoulli emitters with unknown K≤K_max and rates in a stated interval, prove matching upper and lower finite-sample bounds for generalized-Wasserstein recovery from adaptive PNR mode projections. A viable proof must:

1. Show identifiability of the positive measure from the complete joint distribution of chosen settings and counts, including coincident atoms and unknown K, or give an exact counterexample.
2. Produce an explicit estimator and nonasymptotic confidence/risk theorem; the power-sum identity is only a possible statistic.
3. Give a sharp lower bound under the same exposure/energy/PNR calibration model and compare against full direct-position detection with PNR statistics and the quantum bound for the same emitter state.
4. State the permitted mass range, support, calibration data/cost, detector outcomes, frame time, background, cross-talk, and whether the source dynamics (Bernoulli/blinking) are known or acquired.
5. Test collision, vanishing weight, equal-rate, and unknown-origin cases as exact stress cases before claiming advantage.

Current claim labels: source-reported — all literature statements above; internally derived — the Bernoulli PNR generating-function/factorial-cumulant identities; unresolved — finite-sample K-source reconstruction, no-separation risk modulus, adaptive scan, resource-matched lower bound, and calibration/noise robustness; no externally established or original result.
