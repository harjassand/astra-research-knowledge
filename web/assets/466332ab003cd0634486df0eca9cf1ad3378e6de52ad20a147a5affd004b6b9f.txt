A25 PHASE IV — SHIFTED INITIALIZER CLIPPING-BIAS SHARPNESS
Version 7, 2026-10-09 (bounded continuation; v6 preserved)
Status: exact anisotropic conditional-noise example shows the response-clipping bias can rotate the shifted population top eigenspace at L=c sigma^2/q^2 for a sufficiently small constant c. This is a scale-sharpness counterexample to too-small shifts, not to a sufficiently large shift. No discovery or priority claim.

1. QUESTION AND CONSTRUCTION

The v6 two-point example gave a sigma^2/L clipping-bias lower bound proportional to I. That verifies sharp operator-norm order but does not disturb eigenvectors. Here the same moment class creates a directionally biased clipping residual.

Take d>=3 and x=q e_3. Write G=a_3, S=q^2 G^2, and

    s(a)=+1 if a_1^2>=a_2^2, and -1 otherwise.

(The tie event has probability zero.) Fix L>0 and set

    A=2L,       p=sigma^2/(4L^2+sigma^2),
    b=sigma^2/(2L).

Given a, draw the two-point noise

    zeta=s(a) A with probability p,
    zeta=-s(a) b with probability 1-p.

The coin is conditionally independent of the Gaussian design. Since pA=(1-p)b,

    E[zeta|a]=s(a)(pA-(1-p)b)=0,
    E[zeta^2|a]=pA^2+(1-p)b^2=sigma^2.                              (1.1)

So the construction satisfies exactly the conditional moment assumptions, with no fourth-moment premise. Define c_L(y)=clip(y,[-L,L]),

    Q_L=L I+E[c_L(Y)aa^T],
    S_L=L I+E[Y aa^T]=(L+q^2)I+2xx^T.

Then B_L=Q_L-S_L=-E[r aa^T], where r=Y-c_L(Y) is the signed clipping residual. We will show that B_L has leading term -(pL)K, with an anisotropic K, and bound the entire signal-tail remainder explicitly.

2. ANISOTROPIC MATRIX AND ITS EXACT CONSTANT

Let K=E[s(a)aa^T]. Reflection of any coordinate makes every off-diagonal entry vanish. Swapping a_1 and a_2 gives E[s]=0 and K_22=-K_11. For indices j>=3, a_j^2 is independent of s, so K_jj=0. Also

    K_11=E[s(a)a_1^2]
        = (1/2)E[s(a)(a_1^2-a_2^2)]
        = (1/2)E|a_1^2-a_2^2|=2/pi,
    K_22=-2/pi.

For the constant, if U,V are iid N(0,1), then U^2-V^2=(U-V)(U+V), and the two factors are independent N(0,2); hence E|U^2-V^2|=(2/sqrt(pi))^2=4/pi. Therefore

    K=diag(2/pi,-2/pi,0,...,0),       ||K||op=2/pi.                   (2.1)

3. FINITE REMAINDER BOUND, INCLUDING THE SIGNAL GAUSSIAN TAIL

Assume b<=L, equivalently sigma^2<=2L^2. This holds in the asymptotic regime used below. Put t=L/q^2 and t_c=(L-b)/q^2>=0. Split the clipping residual into rare and common noise branches.

On the rare branch zeta=sA, the residual obeys

    r = sL + S + e,       |e|<=2L 1{S>L}.                            (3.1)

Indeed, for s=+1, Y=S+2L and r=S+L exactly. For s=-1 and S<=L, Y=S-2L<=-L and r=S-L exactly. In the remaining s=-1 case, the clipped or unclipped residual differs from S-L by at most 2L, and only when S>L.

On the common branch zeta=-sb, the residual is nonnegative and obeys

    0<=r<= (S+b)1{S>L-b}.                                             (3.2)

For s=+1 the negative clipping side is impossible because b<=L, and a nonzero residual requires S>L+b. For s=-1 a nonzero residual requires S>L-b. These conditions imply (3.2).

For G~N(0,1), define the exact Gaussian tails

    T_0(t)=E[1{G^2>t}]=2 Phi_bar(sqrt(t)),
    T_2(t)=E[G^2 1{G^2>t}]
         =2(sqrt(t) phi(sqrt(t))+Phi_bar(sqrt(t))),
    T_4(t)=E[G^4 1{G^2>t}]
         =2((t^(3/2)+3sqrt(t))phi(sqrt(t))+3Phi_bar(sqrt(t))),

where phi and Phi_bar are the standard normal density and upper tail. Combining (3.1)-(3.2) with independence of G from (a_1,a_2),

    B_L = -pL K + R_L,
    ||R_L||op <= 3p q^2
       + 2pL [T_2(t)+(d-1)T_0(t)]
       + q^2[T_4(t_c)+(d-1)T_2(t_c)]
       + b[T_2(t_c)+(d-1)T_0(t_c)].                                  (3.3)

Proof of the terms: the rare-branch S contribution has norm p||E[Saa^T]||=3p q^2, since E[Saa^T]=q^2 I+2xx^T. The rare-branch error e is bounded in quadratic form by 2pL E[||a||^2 1{G^2>t}], yielding the second term. The common-branch residual is bounded by (S+b)1{G^2>t_c}; applying |u^T aa^T u|<=(||a||^2) for unit u gives the final two terms. This is an explicit finite bound on the signal-tail error, not an informal asymptotic truncation.

4. A TRUE POPULATION EIGENSPACE SWAP AT A SMALL CONSTANT SHIFT

Fix d=3 and any fixed 0<kappa<1/(4pi). Set

    s=sigma/q^2,       L=kappa sigma^2/q^2=kappa q^2 s^2.

For all sufficiently large s, b<=L and t_c>=0. Moreover,

    p=1/(4 kappa^2 s^2+1),
    pL/q^2 = kappa s^2/(4 kappa^2 s^2+1) -> 1/(4kappa),
    b/q^2=1/(2kappa).

Every Gaussian tail in (3.3) vanishes exponentially in t_c~kappa s^2, and p->0, so ||R_L||op=o(q^2) for fixed d=3. By (2.1), the diagonal leading approximation S_L-pL K has eigenvalues

    lambda_1 = L+q^2 - 2pL/pi,
    lambda_2 = L+q^2 + 2pL/pi,
    lambda_3 = L+3q^2.

Its e_2-e_3 gap is 2pL/pi-2q^2, which tends to (1/(2pi kappa)-2)q^2>0 exactly when kappa<1/(4pi). The population Q_L is diagonal: conditional on a, the law of c_L(Y) depends on a only through coordinate squares, so each off-diagonal expectation cancels under a coordinate sign reflection. For sufficiently large s, (3.3) is smaller than half the displayed eigengap; therefore the actual top eigenspace of Q_L is exactly span(e_2), orthogonal to x=e_3. This proves an actual population eigenspace swap, not merely a large bias bound.

For a concrete constant choose kappa=1/(8pi). Then pL/q^2 tends to 2pi, the leading e_2-e_3 gap tends to 2q^2, and the true top direction is e_2 for all sufficiently large s. Thus a shift L=c sigma^2/q^2 with a small fixed c can fail completely even with no adversarial replacements. The construction establishes the order sigma^2/q^2 for a safe shift against this mechanism; it does not show failure for every constant c, and it does not challenge the large-constant v6 choice L=C(q^2+sigma^2/q^2).

The leading anisotropic clipping bias has norm

    ||pL K||op = (2/pi)pL ~ sigma^2/(2pi L),

which attains the same sigma^2/L order as the v6 isotropic example while rotating the principal direction. Here the clipping bias itself does the damage: the common branch contributes only the explicit vanishing Gaussian-tail remainder in this regime.
