1. Corrected CSS sectors (correction to v1 §1)

Write the field complex around the qubits as

    C^1 --B=δ^1--> C^2 --A=δ^2--> C^3,       AB=0.

For ordered binary basis β of F=F_{2^s}, the GGJ binary conversion is

    H_X = R_β(A),       H_Z = R_β(B)^T.

Accordingly the physical Z-error sector is decoded from

    y_Z = R_β(δ^2)e_Z + m_Z,

and the physical X-error sector from

    y_X = R_β(δ^1)^T e_X + m_X.

Thus v1's sector equations were reversed. The transpose is the algebraic dual
map on actual dual stalks. In binary coordinates, β is used on cochains and
the trace-dual basis β∨ is used on algebraic-dual vectors. GGJ Appendix A.2,
Lemma A.2.1 (PDF p.77) states

    R_β(B)^T = R_{β∨}(B^vee),

where B^vee is the dual map. It is generally incorrect to use β on both sides
and interpret the matrix transpose as the same F-coordinate map. In the
transpose decoder, every array entry in F(u)^* and every incidence R^vee is
therefore represented in the paired dual coordinates. The binary syndrome
fault vector is regrouped into those fixed-size blocks. This correction is
also reflected in Sol's §§8 and 9.

For either sector, a nonzero field block contains at least one binary bit and
at most B0 bits, where B0=max_f s dim_F F(f) is fixed along the tower. Hence
binary input error weight bounds block support from above, faulty syndrome
bit support bounds faulty block support from above, and a block-residual
bound γ|m| gives a binary stabilizer-reduced residual at most B0 γ|m|.

2. Precise candidate theorem and operational interfaces

Fix t=4,k=2 and one GGJ arithmetic-tower family member. Let Z_d=|X(d)|,
n_-=min_i n_i, n_+=max_i n_i, and choose fixed K≥n_+/n_-. For the original
cosheaf F and its auxiliary dual-kernel cosheaf K_GGJ, the required degrees
are d=2,3. The source's order of choices fixes product-expansion ρ first,
then chooses local lengths so λ L(4,2,ρ,K)<1/2. GGJ proves the same uniform
product-expansion assertion for the needed dual constituent codes; thus the
local robust inequalities and λL_d<1 used by the candidate decoder apply to
both F and K_GGJ. All parameters and local alphabets are fixed as the tower
index grows.

The ordinary cochain-side decoder is the local-flip decoder in Sol
`work/sol/local_chart_decoder/v1.txt` §3. Given y=δ^j e+m, it repeatedly
applies a cochain w supported in one upward vertex star whenever
|y+δ^j w|_b<|y|_b, and returns the accumulated correction. The candidate's
noncocycle extension of GGJ's support recursion is

    (1−λL_d)|x|_b ≤ Q_d |x|_b^2/Z_d + M_d |δ^d x|_b

for every locally cominimal x. In particular, if |x|_b≤a_d Z_d/2, then
|x|_b≤A_d|δ^d x|_b, where

    a_d=(1−λL_d)/Q_d,       A_d=2M_d/(1−λL_d).

The source-derived local-cominimal distance and soundness constants used by
the decoder are

    μ^d ≥ a_d Z_d,
    ε_d ≥ min{ell_d^{-1}, a_{d+1} Z_{d+1}/Z_d},
    ell_d=binom(t,d)n_+^d.

Sol §2 gives the exact anisotropic coefficients, including the syndrome
defect term M_d; §3 gives the full threshold conditions and proves the
stabilizer-reduced residual bound. The algorithm makes at most |y| strict
integer weight decreases, each with fixed local work, and has O(N) worst-case
time and O(N) space when the explicit incidence/stalk data are supplied or
preprocessed. Field operations and local candidate spaces have fixed cost.

For the transpose sector let B_k=(δ^{k−1})^vee. The operational reduction
does not copy the unknown error. It embeds the supplied y∈C^{k−1}(F^*) as
vertex opinions C(y)∈A_{0,k−1}, uses fixed local all-input maps L_f^p=S_f^pP_f^p
(projection onto ker T followed by a right inverse), and defines

    g_0=L^{k−1}C(y),
    g_r=L^{k+r−1}Δg_{r−1}  (1≤r≤t−k),
    s_K=ι^{-1}ΠΔg_{t−k}.

It decodes s_K with the cochain-side decoder on K_GGJ, reverses with the
cube contraction, and reads an output cochain from the anchor-zero array.
All these operations are defined on arbitrary noisy inputs. The exact lifts
and top K-cycle are proof-only objects for the clean syndrome; they are not
decoder inputs. With L=t−k, D=tn_+, c_m=2^{k−1}, and a clean ideal sequence,
the noisy top K-syndrome differs from its ideal K-syndrome by at most

    P_noise |m|_b,       P_noise=c_m D^{L+1}.

The ideal K-error is supported on at most

    P_data |e|_b,         P_data=(L+2)2^k D^L.

Sol §5 proves these bounds by comparing the same anchorwise linear maps on
noisy and clean inputs. §6 proves the corrected output is homologous to the
input error by an exact chase in the actual-stalk bicomplex; §7 gives the
explicit reverse-noise factor

    C_trans = G c_m [Σ_{r=0}^{L−1}(HD)^r
                         +(HD)^L(1+β_K D)],
    H=2^{2t}n_+^t,   G=2^t n_+^t,

so the stabilizer-reduced block residual is at most C_trans|m|_b when the
stated positive data/noise inequalities (Sol (7.2)) hold. For t=4,k=2,
L=2 and both F and K_GGJ invoke the same degrees 2 and 3. Since Z_2,Z_3
and N are comparable with fixed constants, these inequalities give positive
family-independent data and syndrome fractions. The constants are enormous
but fixed; this is an asymptotic O(N) statement, not a practical threshold
claim.

3. Why chart holonomy is no longer the decoder gate

The exact array spaces are

    A_{r,p}(f)=⊕_{u∈X(p), f≤u}F(u)^*,
    (Δa)(g)[u]=Σ_{f<g}a(f)[u],
    (Ta)(f)[v]=Σ_{u>v}R^vee_{uv}a(f)[u].

GGJ Lemma 7.1.1 (PDF pp.27–28) gives Δ²=T²=0, ΔT=TΔ and the support
bounds. GGJ Lemma 7.1.2 defines K_GGJ(f)=ker T_f^t and proves its incidence
maps are actual restrictions of the top-face entries; its cochain differential
is Δ. GGJ Theorem 7.2.1 (PDF pp.28–29) proves local transpose exactness
ker T_f^p=im T_f^{p+1} and supplies compatible charts for K_GGJ using the
inverse-transpose dual chart Θ=(Ψ^{-1})^T. GGJ Lemma 7.3.2 (PDF pp.30–31)
gives a horizontal cube contraction and, at anchor degree zero, unique global
agreement of Δ-closed arrays.

The proof compares only vectors in the same actual upper stalk F(u)^*;
Δ is the identity across anchors and T uses the actual dual incidence maps.
The chosen local splittings need not transform equivariantly between charts:
they are fixed linear maps in each actual anchor block, and noise is bounded
by support changes under those same maps. The homology proof uses local cube
fillings and zero-anchor agreement, not a global tensor trivialization. This
replaces the missing NP-style cross-anchor chart-naturalness implication
flagged in v1. GGJ Proposition 7.3.3 itself is only a cycle-filling/distance
transfer statement; its copy of x into the arrays is not operational. The
operational supplied-syndrome step is Sol §5 and must be kept distinct from
that proposition.

4. Source dependencies, frontier, and claim ledger

Primary source dependencies (source-reported here):

• Gay–Granha Jeronimo, “Asymptotically Good Quantum Locally Testable Codes,”
  arXiv:2609.20780v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20780. The theorem chain is
  Defs.2.2.1–2.2.2; Thm.4.0.1; Lem.5.3.1; Lemmas 6.1.2, 6.2.1, 6.4.1 and
  Thms.6.3.1, 7.0.1, 7.2.1; Lemmas 7.1.1–7.1.2 and 7.3.2; Prop.8.1.1;
  Thm.12.0.1 and Thms.13.2.1–13.2.2. These supply the code family,
  compatible charts, local robustness, mixing, dual-code product expansion,
  arithmetic spectral bound, anchored-array identities, and binary conversion.
• Nguyen–Pattison, arXiv:2411.03632v2,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03632. Their Alg.5 and Claims6.33–6.40,
  Prop.6.41 supply the prior-art model for decoding through a double complex
  and noisy syndrome explanations on their cubical family. The GGJ actual-
  stalk implementation and the present noncocycle recursion are not claimed
  to originate that general idea.
• Gu et al., arXiv:2306.12470v2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12470. Their
  good-qLDPC single-shot result preempts the raw “good qLDPC + linear decoder”
  objective.

The 2026-10-09 primary-source search also returned Li–Li–Liu,
“Transversal non-Clifford gates on good quantum locally testable codes,”
arXiv:2609.26691 (posted 2026-09-22),
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26691. Its abstract concerns transversal gates on
good qLTC/qLDPC constructions and applies a gate framework to GGJ; it is not a
decoder claim. This bounded search result does not prove novelty or priority.
