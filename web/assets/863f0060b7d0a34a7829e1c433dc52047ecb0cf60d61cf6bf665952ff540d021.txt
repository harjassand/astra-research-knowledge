Independent stationary classical Petz transfer with genuine entropy budget
Cycle09 tensor_frame_sol, Sol/max, no descendants.
Status: internally derived exact theorem, before Cycle09 sibling exposure.
It covers arbitrary finite CLASSICAL references, not general quantum references.

THEOREM. Let P be any finite column-stochastic matrix with faithful stationary
probability sigma, P sigma=sigma. Embed it as a full-domain quantum channel
  Phi(X)=sum_j <j|X|j> tau_j, tau_j=diag(P_.j).
This is EB and admits the actual broadcaster
  B(X)=sum_j <j|X|j> tau_j tensor tau_j.
Its Petz adjoint at diag sigma is the time reversal P^sharp, killing input
off-diagonal entries, and S=Phi^sharp Phi is a single fixed stationary KMS
positive/self-compatible channel on the whole matrix algebra.

For any finite classical reference, any diagonal joint state p_XB, let
  b=T(p,(id_X tensor P)p),
  r=T(p,(id_X tensor P^sharp P)p),
  D=D(p||p_X tensor sigma)=I(X:B)_p+D(p_B||sigma),
with HALF total variation and natural logarithms. Then

  r<=min{1,6[(D+1)b]^(1/3)}.                           (C)

One fixed S depends only on P,sigma. There is no bound on alphabet sizes,
minimum probability, likelihood ratio, or selected state labels. Tested p
need not be faithful. Zero reference weights are removed on support.

1. Stationary flow and equidistributed likelihood variables

On the joint classical index set use the stationary law q=p_X tensor sigma.
Let P act locally on B and leave X unchanged. Set f=p/q>=0, so E_q f=1 and
E_q[f log f]=D. Define a stationary two-index flow
  Q(i,j)=q_j P_ij.
Its two marginals are q. Let the random variables
  U=f(i), V=f(j)
under this flow. They have the SAME distribution, expectation one, and
entropy D. Conditional on i,
  E[V|i]=(Pp)_i/q_i.
Thus
  delta=E_Q|U-E[V|i]|=||p-Pp||_1=2b.                 (1)
Conditioning on j gives the reversal, so
  ||p-P^sharp p||_1
      =E_Q|V-E[U|j]|<=E_Q|U-V|=:v.                  (2)
Trace contraction also gives r<=b+v/2. Stationarity of q is used in the
identical-distribution assertion and the reverse conditional expectation;
mere approximately stationary q is not substituted.

2. A clipped convex entropy test

Take L>=1, epsilon=exp(-L), M=exp(L). Define
  g_L(x)=max{-L,min{L,log x}}, g_L(0)=-L,
  h_L(x)=integral_1^x g_L(u)du.
This convex continuously differentiable function has second derivative 1/x
on (epsilon,M) and zero outside. Define its nonnegative Bregman defect
  B_L(u,v)=h_L(v)-h_L(u)-g_L(u)(v-u).
Identical marginal distributions cancel its two h terms. Hence
  E B_L(U,V)=E[g_L(U)(U-E[V|i])]<=L delta.            (3)
This is a bounded TEST of the actual raw residual, not an inference from
relative-entropy data processing. No unbounded log test is charged for free.

For 0<=u,v<=M put u'=max(u,epsilon), v'=max(v,epsilon).
Then
  B_L(u,v)>=(sqrt(u')-sqrt(v'))^2.                    (4)
To check every boundary case: for u,v in [epsilon,M], B_L is
v log(v/u)-v+u, and log t>=2(1-1/sqrt(t)) proves (4). If u<epsilon<=v,
linear extension below epsilon makes the defect exactly the same expression
with u replaced by epsilon. If v<epsilon<=u, the difference from the defect
with v replaced by epsilon is (log(u)+L)(epsilon-v)>=0. If both are below
epsilon both sides are zero. This also covers zero arguments and endpoints.

On A={max(U,V)<=M}, Cauchy-Schwarz, (3)-(4), and E U'=E V'<=1+epsilon give
  E[|U-V| 1_A]
    <=E[|U'-V'|1_A]+2epsilon
    <=2sqrt[(1+epsilon)L delta]+2epsilon.             (5)
Indeed |u'-v'|=|sqrt(u')-sqrt(v')|(sqrt(u')+sqrt(v')), and the expected
square of the latter factor is <=2E(U'+V')<=4(1+epsilon).

On the upper-tail complement,
  |U-V|1_(max>M)<=U1_(U>M)+V1_(V>M).
Also E[f log f; f<=1]>=-1/e, since x log x>=-1/e and q is a probability.
Consequently E[f log f; f>1]<=D+1/e, and
  E[f;f>M]<=(D+1/e)/L.
Identical distributions now yield
  v<=2sqrt[(1+exp(-L))L delta]+2exp(-L)+2(D+1/e)/L.   (6)
This entropy-tail estimate uses the actual genuine classical D exactly.

3. Optimization, constants and zero endpoints

If 0<delta<=1, put A0=D+1>=1 and L=(A0^2/delta)^(1/3)>=1. Since
  2sqrt(1+exp(-L))<3,
  exp(-L)<=1/L,
  D+1/e<=A0,
equation (6) gives
  v<=3sqrt(L delta)+4A0/L=7(A0 delta)^(1/3).
Therefore
  r<=b+(7/2)(2A0 b)^(1/3)
    <=[1+(7/2)2^(1/3)](A0 b)^(1/3)
    <=6(A0 b)^(1/3),
where b<=1 implies b<=(A0b)^(1/3), and 2^(1/3)<=4/3 gives bracket
<=17/3<6. If delta>1, the bound is trivial from r<=1 and b>1/2.
If b=0, use (6) for every L and take L to infinity to get v=0, hence r=0.
Every displayed estimate is finite and no division by b or D is used there.

4. Exact scope and transfer failure to retain

This establishes a new polynomial fixed-Petz transfer for stationary
classical channels and classical reference correlations. The full channel
embedding and the actual common-marginal broadcaster make the statement
legal on all input matrices, rather than a state-only stochastic ansatz.
The tested inputs remain diagonal in the supplied classical bases.

For genuinely quantum rho, simply replacing f=p/q by the positive operator
sigma^(-1/2)rho sigma^(-1/2) does not reproduce the supplied Umegaki D.
Its clipped entropy is a different noncommutative relative-density quantity;
the needed upper bound by genuine D is unproved and in general false as a
metric identification. No quantum-reference or unrestricted quantum-channel
version of (C) is claimed. A correct noncommutative argument or a legal actual
counterexample remains the main Cycle09 gate.
