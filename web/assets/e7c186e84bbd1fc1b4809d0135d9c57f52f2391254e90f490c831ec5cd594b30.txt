Verdict

No failed step found in the requested lower proof. The score identity is

1. Generic compact-group identity

Let d=dim H, tau_g=e^(hB_g)/Z_h, W_g=d tau_g, and let A_i be Kraus
operators of a CPTP map H->H. Irreducibility gives

  integral W_g^2 dg = c I,       c=Tr(W_e^2)/d.

Trace preservation implies sum_i ||A_i||_HS^2=d. No unital condition is
needed: after averaging, the two square terms in

  ||[W,A]||_HS^2
    =Tr(W^2 A A^*)+Tr(W^2 A^*A)-2Tr(W A W A^*)

each sum to c d=Tr(W_e^2), because Tr(A A^*)=Tr(A^*A)=||A||_HS^2.
Put X=A^* W A. Both X and W are Hermitian, so Tr(XW) is real; cyclicity
identifies it with Tr(W A W A^*). Thus the displayed cross term has no
hidden real-part or factor-of-two issue. Also

  integral Tr[W_g tau_g] dg = Tr(W_e^2)/d,
  integral Tr[W_g A_i tau_g A_i^*] dg
      = (1/d) integral Tr(W_g A_i W_g A_i^*) dg.

Subtracting yields exactly

  integral Tr[W_g(tau_g-Phi(tau_g))] dg
      = (1/(2d)) sum_i integral ||[W_g,A_i]||_HS^2 dg.       (A)

This derivation uses TP and the irreducible twirl, not unitality. It remains
valid for arbitrary complex, nonsymmetric, nonnormal A_i. Independently,
the SU(3) identity RESULT (2.4) has real summands because T_a and
A T_a A^* are Hermitian, and expanding the commutator gives the claimed
Casimir formula for the same general A.

For the Gibbs divided difference, if B_g has eigenvalues lambda_i and
zeta=Z_h/d, then

  (W_i-W_j)/(lambda_i-lambda_j)=h exp(h xi)/zeta

  for some xi between lambda_i and lambda_j.

Since |lambda_i|<=b and exp(-hb)<=zeta<=exp(hb), its absolute value lies
between h exp(-2hb) and h exp(2hb). Squaring entrywise and averaging gives
the stated lower and upper comparison with E_B(A), including the
lambda_i=lambda_j limit. Consequently

  sum_i integral ||[W_g,A_i]||_HS^2 dg
    >= h0^2 exp(-4h1 b) Lambda_B(Q) sum_i ||A_i||_HS^2
    = d h0^2 exp(-4h1 b) Lambda_B(Q).

Substitution in (A) gives score at least
  (h0^2/2) exp(-4h1 b) Lambda_B(Q).
Because 0<=W_g<=M I for M=exp(2h1 b), W_g/M is an effect. For states
rho,sigma and any effect E, |Tr E(rho-sigma)|<=||rho-sigma||_1/2.
Dividing the score by M therefore gives exactly

  e_avg >= (h0^2/2) exp(-6h1 b) Lambda_B(Q),

and worst-case error is at least average error. The exponent -6h1b and
factor 1/2 are correct.

2. SU(3) first harmonic and self-score constant

From RESULT (3.5),

  integral z_a(v) tau_(h,v) dv
      = r_N(h) T_a/[d N(N+m)].

The ideal score from B_v=I/m+(2/N)sum_a z_a(v)T_a is
1/m+s r_N(h)/(mN), using pointwise sum_a z_a(v)^2=s/(2m).
For any TP channel with Kraus A_i, linearity and (3.5) give decoded score

  integral Tr[B_v Phi(tau_(h,v))]dv
    = 1/m + 2r_N(h)/[d N^2(N+m)]
                  sum_(i,a) Tr(T_a A_i T_a A_i^*).

Apply RESULT (2.4) to each A_i and use sum_i||A_i||_HS^2=d. The Casimir
term cancels the ideal score exactly, leaving

  score gap = r_N(h)/[d N^2(N+m)] sum_i E(A_i)
            = r_N(h)/[N^2(N+m)] sum_i w_i Ebar(A_i),
  w_i=||A_i||_HS^2/d,       sum_i w_i=1.

Every term is nonnegative. Each nonzero Kraus operator has rank at most Q,
so Ebar(A_i)>=Lambda_N,m(Q). The score lower bound is therefore the
right side of RESULT (4.3).

B_v=n_v/N is already an effect: its spectrum lies in [0,1]. Hence the
integrated score gap is at most the average half-trace error, and thus at
most the worst-case error. There is no missing factor 2 in (4.3). Combining
with (3.9) gives the uniform interval consequence

  e*_Q(I) >= [h0 exp(-2h1)/(m(m+1))]
                Lambda_N,m(Q)/[N(N+m)],

using r_N(h)/N>=h0 exp(-2h1)/[m(m+1)]. The positivity premise h0>0 is
needed, as the file itself notes.

3. Arbitrary hybrid codes and continuous labels

For branch y with memory dimension q_y<=Q, encoder Kraus E_(y,alpha)
and decoder Kraus D_(y,beta) compose to A_(y,alpha,beta)=D E. Its rank is
at most q_y and hence at most Q. Summing over all branches gives a Kraus
family for the full channel, with

  sum_i A_i^* A_i=I,
  sum_i ||A_i||_HS^2=Tr(I)=d.

The same statements hold for a continuous outcome: the Choi operator is
an integral of positive rank-one Choi terms whose vectors have Schmidt
rank at most Q. Normalizing by its trace gives a point in the convex hull
of the compact set of normalized rank-Q vectors; finite-dimensional
convexity places it in that closed convex hull, so a finite rank-Q
decomposition also exists. Alternatively, (A) and the energy lower bound
apply directly under the outcome integral, since the weights and energies
are nonnegative. Unlimited labels do not invalidate the bound.
