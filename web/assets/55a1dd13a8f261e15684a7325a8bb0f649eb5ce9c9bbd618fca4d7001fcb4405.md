# Matched-port reciprocity for fixed linear thermal networks

## Scope

Let a finite-volume or lumped thermal model have temperature vector `T`, heat-capacity matrix `C=C^T\succ0`, and reciprocal conductance/loss matrix `K=K^T\succeq0`. `K` may include arbitrary heterogeneous symmetric inter-cell conductances and passive Robin loss to fixed ambient reservoirs. A heat-power source is `b_j u_j(t)`, and the conjugate output is the temperature functional `b_i^T T`. The port is *matched* when the same vector `b_i` defines its input and output; sensor and actuator gains are assumed calibrated and included in `b_i`.

The equations and transfer matrix are

```
C Tdot + K T = sum_j b_j u_j(t),
H_ij(s) = b_i^T (K+sC)^(-1) b_j.
```

## Theorem 1: exact reciprocal power-to-temperature transfer

For every real `s>0` (and, by analytic continuation, every `s` in the common resolvent half-plane),

```
H_ij(s)=H_ji(s).
```

The causal impulse response therefore obeys `h_ij(t)=h_ji(t)` as a distribution and for every `t>0` where it is an ordinary function. This is independent of network size, graph topology, cell capacities, spatial grading, and the ratios of conductivity to heat capacity. If `K` is singular from an insulated zero mode, the claim holds for `s>0`; a stable boundary condition is not needed for this domain.

### Proof

`K+sC` is symmetric. Its inverse is symmetric wherever the inverse exists, so

```
b_i^T (K+sC)^(-1)b_j = b_j^T (K+sC)^(-1)b_i.
```

Uniqueness of Laplace transforms gives the impulse-response equality. Equivalently, the weak Fourier problem with positive volumetric heat capacity `c(x)` and symmetric positive conductivity tensor `k(x)` has the resolvent form

```
a_s(v,T)=int k grad(v)·grad(T) + s int c vT + passive-boundary terms.
```

The bilinear form is symmetric, so its matched-source Green operator is symmetric. This proof includes finite-element discretizations: consistent or lumped mass matrices and reciprocal interface/contact terms preserve the identity exactly up to their stated discretization.

## Theorem 2: what same-initial-temperature pulses do

For point ports `b_i=e_i`, let `E(t)=exp(-C^(-1)Kt)`, the temperature response to an initial temperature vector. Since `C^(-1)K` is self-adjoint in the `C`-weighted inner product,

```
C E(t) = E(t)^T C,
c_i E_ij(t)=c_j E_ji(t).
```

Thus if `E_ij(t)>0`, identical initial temperature increments at nodes `j` and `i` produce the ratio

```
E_ij(t)/E_ji(t) = c_j/c_i,
```

not a directional conductance. Those two preparations store energies `c_j ΔT` and `c_i ΔT`, respectively. Equal-energy impulses use initial temperatures `1/c_j` and `1/c_i`; their receiver-temperature responses are `E_ij/c_j` and `E_ji/c_i`, which are equal by the weighted identity. More generally, a heat-power impulse of equal total energy at each port has response `H_ij(t)=E_ij(t)/c_j`, which is exactly symmetric by Theorem 1.

### Proof

`A=C^(-1)K` satisfies `A^T C=CA`. Every power series in `A`, including `E(t)`, therefore satisfies `E(t)^T C=CE(t)`. For point ports this gives the entrywise equality above. A unit-energy pulse at `j` changes temperature by `C^(-1)e_j`; its cross-temperature response is `E(t)C^(-1)`, the same kernel as the impulse response to power. The transfer identity then gives equality after exchanging source and receiver.

## Corollary: capacity-only apparent rectification is sharp

For same-`ΔT` point-initialized pulses and the symmetric contrast

```
R_ij(t) = (E_ij(t)-E_ji(t))/(E_ij(t)+E_ji(t)),
```

the sign and value are independent of `t` and all conductances:

```
R_ij(t) = (c_j-c_i)/(c_j+c_i).
```

This is a sharp prediction, conditional on point-like matched initial perturbations, a fixed linear reciprocal network, and calibrated local temperatures. If `c_i=c_j`, it is zero. A time-varying ratio or a mismatch after equal-energy power pulses falsifies at least one of the model, port-matching, stationarity, or calibration premises.

## Exact two-node witness

Take `C=diag(1,4)` and `K=[[1,-1],[-1,1]]`. With `q=exp(-5t/4)`,

```
E(t) = [[(1+4q)/5, 4(1-q)/5],
        [(1-q)/5,   (4+q)/5]].
```

The same-temperature initial-pulse cross responses have ratio `E_12/E_21=4` at every `t>0`; their signed symmetric contrast is `3/5`. For equal integrated power impulses, `H(t)=E(t)C^(-1)` and `H_12=H_21=(1-q)/5`. Hence a reported 60% same-`ΔT` asymmetry coexists with exactly reciprocal matched-port transfer.

The hypotheses are material. If the source and receiver use different port profiles, uncalibrated gains, or different boundary conditions on the two runs, the matrix is `B_out^T(K+sC)^(-1)B_in` with no reason to be symmetric after the labels are swapped. Advection, active/time-modulated coefficients, temperature-dependent response over the pulse range, a third uncontrolled reservoir, or state-changing contact conditions also lie outside the theorem.
