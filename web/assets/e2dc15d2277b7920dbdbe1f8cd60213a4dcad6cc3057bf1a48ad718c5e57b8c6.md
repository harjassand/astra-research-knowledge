# Quantum-defect application audit

## Gate verdict

**Best physical candidate: the intrinsic \(^{14}\mathrm N\) nuclear spin of an NV centre in diamond, read through spin-dependent fluorescence of the NV electron.** This is a genuine finite-state, photon-readout system, and primary experiments demonstrate repeated, nearly QND readout plus controllable nuclear-spin jump rates under magnetic field and readout-timing changes. It is a substantially more concrete action interface than generic cellular cryo-arrest.

**Gate status: promising application candidate; not a complete pass for the current slow-read theorem.** The literature gives fitted \(T_1\) values, empirical transition matrices, state-conditioned photon-count histograms, and real pulse timings. It does not give a protocol-wide one-sided transition-kernel envelope including field ramps, a demonstrated common stationary emission matrix across slowdown settings, or the requested equal-walltime/equal-photon-dose passive likelihood comparison. Treat the measured operating point as a source-backed candidate interface; leave those missing gates `UNKNOWN`.

## Exact interface found

- **Hidden state.** The intrinsic nitrogen nuclear spin has three projections, \(m_I\in\{-1,0,+1\}\). The NV electron spin is an optical ancilla. A microwave-controlled electron-spin operation maps the nuclear-spin value to the ancilla, then a laser pulse reads the ancilla through emitted red photoluminescence. Neumann et al. report that the electron is optically reset by readout while the nuclear population is almost undisturbed, allowing repeated measurements of the same nuclear eigenstate. At the operating conditions, the applied field must be aligned with the NV axis to avoid adding measurement back-action.
- **Read timing and calibration.** In the 2010 experiment, each plotted observation averages a 5 ms readout sequence with 2,000 repetitions. The state-conditioned photon histogram separates \(m_I=-1\) from \(m_I=0,+1\), with reported readout fidelity \(92\pm2\%\) at 0.65 T; two consecutive readouts return the same class with probability about 82%. The authors also reconstruct a three-state transition matrix from approximately 10,000 observed jumps. In a 2025 room-temperature experiment, a 220 ns optical pulse is used per repeated-interaction measurement; 80,000 repetitions take 1.695 s, with about 0.05 detected photons per repetition, and the reported nuclear-spin readout fidelity is \(97\pm2\%\) for its bright/dark partition. These are finite and explicit read costs, not cost-free “extra frames.”
- **Reversible slowdown actions.** Two controls have direct primary evidence. (1) With the magnetic field parallel to the NV axis, moving away from the excited-state level anticrossing near 50 mT suppresses electron–nuclear flip-flops. Neumann et al. measure the increase in \(^{14}\mathrm N\) lifetime with field detuning and report the transition matrix from the repeated-read trajectory. Pfender et al. later measure the memory-spin lifetime during continuous single-shot readout and find a strong, approximately quadratic field dependence, reaching about 1 s at 3 T. (2) A 2025 room-temperature study varies laser duty cycle and inserts waits between successive readouts; its longest measured \(^{14}\mathrm N\) \(T_1\) is \(15\pm3.5\) s. The waiting-time protocol buys lifetime with elapsed time. The field protocol buys lifetime with high-field hardware and field-dependent pulse calibration. These reported high-fidelity, high-field, and longest-lifetime figures come from different schedules/operating points; the papers do not combine them into one validated joint setting.
- **What is actually bounded.** The transition evidence is empirical: fitted relaxation times with fit uncertainty, plus a transition matrix estimated from about 10,000 jumps in the 2010 paper and hidden-Markov jump trajectories in the 2025 paper. This is enough to identify a measurable transition process and estimate setting-specific rates. It is **not** a deterministic bound or a stated one-sided confidence envelope uniform over all states, runs, ramps, or field settings. A theorem needing a certified per-block `q_R` must add a protocol-specific statistical confidence construction and an explicit ramp/settling debit.
- **Emission stationarity.** Photon-count distributions are calibrated conditional on spin classes under a specified field, microwave sequence, laser pulse, and timing. The 2025 paper explicitly remeasures ODMR and adjusts microwave settings before sequential runs to compensate temperature drift. This supports per-setting calibration; it does not show one unchanged matrix \(E_F\) can be reused after changing field, tilt, laser duty, or temperature. The electron readout also repumps the electron, and the repeated-interaction map itself can induce nuclear jumps, so the exact source-supported object is an action-conditioned controlled hidden Markov channel, not passive iid photon draws from a perfectly fixed state.
- **Cost completeness.** Source timings include 5 ms per 2,000-cycle observation and 1.695 s for an 80,000-repetition readout. The latter corresponds to about 4,000 detected photons at the reported mean of 0.05 per repetition. The high-field study uses a superconducting vector magnet (up to 3 T) and microwave transitions near 40 GHz. These give nonzero finite acquisition costs, but the papers do not report magnet ramp/settle time, a full matched photon-dose ledger, or an all-in cost per classified trajectory. Those must be measured locally.
- **Matched passive control.** I found no source that reports a likelihood-ratio comparison under equal walltime and equal photon dose. The decisive experiment is a randomized, same-defect comparison between the standard read schedule and the slower schedule or high-field setting. Hold total laser exposure and elapsed walltime fixed, place the same read pulses contiguously in the passive arm and space them with waits in the action arm, or change only the calibrated field setting; re-estimate state-conditioned photon laws for each setting and charge magnet ramp/settle and calibration. If detected count rates differ, score both arms using the same detected-photon budget by blind subsampling the higher-count stream. Compute held-out likelihood for the same candidate transition pair. The literature establishes components of this experiment, not its result.

## Candidate comparison

**NV / diamond nuclear spin — strongest lead.** It uniquely combines a native discrete hidden state, state-conditioned fluorescence, repeated readout, directly observed jumps, and two measured slowdown knobs. The near-axial magnetic-field geometry and readout schedule are part of the interface; omitting them changes the model.

**Colloidal quantum dots — reject for temperature slowdown on current evidence.** Shimizu et al. measured single-dot on/off times and found a temperature-independent power-law blinking distribution across their tested temperatures. This is primary evidence against assuming that merely cooling a dot supplies a useful tunable scalar slowdown. It does not rule out different engineered dot systems or other controls.

**hBN emitters — useful photon stability/strain control, but not the needed hidden-transition action.** Primary studies show strain tunes emission energy and can improve photon purity or stability; another reports a strain/pressure-associated emitter activation/quenching response that recovers when pressure is returned. Those measurements concern spectral tuning or emission availability, not a calibrated hidden-state transition rate that slows while the same state-conditioned photon channel remains stationary. They do not pass this gate.

## Synthesis-ready disposition

The NV \(^{14}\mathrm N\) channel can be cited as a real testbed for repeated photon acquisition under a paid reversible slowdown. The cleanest action-specific claim is: **a fixed, experimentally specified repeated-read protocol has a measurable nuclear-spin transition matrix, and magnetic field or readout duty/wait timing changes its measured lifetime.** Do not elevate that to a uniform \(Q\mapsto\rho Q\) law, an invariant reporter, a strict ramp-inclusive transition bound, or an acquisition advantage over an optimal passive likelihood design. For the current application gate, the missing decisive observation is a same-device matched run with calibrated action-specific emissions, confidence-bounded transitions including ramps, and equal walltime/photon dose.
