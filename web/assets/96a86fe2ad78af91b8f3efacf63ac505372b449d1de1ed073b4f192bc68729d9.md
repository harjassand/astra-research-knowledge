# Cell aging memory as a state-estimation problem

## Main finding

An epigenetic clock trajectory does not identify whether a cell's underlying aging state has been repaired. The latest relevant evidence is a September 2026, not-yet-peer-reviewed preprint whose corrected donor-level analysis finds mean Horvath-clock decreases but no detectable DamAge change during partial reprogramming. With 3–4 donors and DamAge intervals that include meaningful decreases, this is not evidence of irreversibility or equivalence. The authors also observe DamAge reduction near the iPSC endpoint and explicitly acknowledge that its apparent resistance may arise from which CpGs the clock weights.

The research-ready result is an exact, operational identifiability gate. In a two-mode washout system, distinguishability depends on calibrated non-collinear observation of the modes and distinct recovery rates. Exact persistence cannot be established in finite time without a predeclared lower bound on slow recovery. This yields a practical theorem/model → falsifiable prediction → intervention design chain without biological extrapolation.

## Literature and evidence

Horvath's multi-tissue DNA methylation clock is a chronological-age predictor built from methylation, and its original paper left biological meaning open. Ying et al.'s DamAge incorporates CpGs with putative causal links to aging-related outcomes, making it causality-enriched by construction, but it is not a direct assay of persistent molecular lesions. Xiao et al.'s EpiTrace primary experiments show that methylation and accessibility-based age measures can move on distinct trajectories; in chemical reprogramming, 5-azacytidine-associated global demethylation changes the DNAm-age readout and removing the compound blocks that particular age increase. These results make intervention-to-assay effects and cell identity/composition explicit model terms.

Primary partial-reprogramming studies establish that some clock and function endpoints are responsive, but do not solve state identifiability. Olova et al. report human fibroblast DNAm-clock decline over an early reprogramming interval before the identity-loss trajectory. Ocampo et al. report identity-retaining short OSKM pulses and mouse-model benefits on selected molecular readouts, with continuous induction producing severe toxicity in that particular model. Sarkar et al. report transient reprogramming and later myogenic differentiation/transplant outcomes, which are distinct endpoints from a clock. These papers motivate measuring clock, identity, damage and function jointly; they do not establish a universal reversible/irreversible decomposition.

The current frontier check materially narrows novelty. Tanigawa & Iwaki's v2 preprint (Sept. 18, 2026; [medRxiv](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.05.26356402)) directly compares chronological Horvath and causality-enriched DamAge scores under cellular reprogramming and builds a mortality model with a latent residual. Treat it as preprint prior art, not an established biological result. The current v2 code archive is [Zenodo version 1.2.0](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22825428); the older 1.0.0 archive is locally retained for code audit. The current archive binary returned a network 403/timeout during this audit, so v2 implementation-specific checks are limited to the paper and supplement. Details and changed numbers are in [CYCLE2_MODEL_AUDIT.md](CYCLE2_MODEL_AUDIT.md).

The v2 mortality result's actual estimand is a conditional decomposition. Its primary fit shares cross-sectional age slopes of measured blood biomarkers with a Gompertz survival likelihood. The resulting latent fraction is 92.7% [92.2, 93.2] in NHANES, 91.5% [89.7, 93.3] in HRS, and 89.1% [88.0, 90.0] in a discretized mean-field sensitivity re-specification. The full person-level phase-type likelihood was not computationally tractable; the discretized state propagation is only a point-estimate check, and the plotted repair transitions are not estimated by that script. The authors explicitly warn that cross-sectional drift may differ from within-person change, measurement error can inflate the latent share, `gamma_base` is a residual that may include frailty or model misspecification, and the latent's identity is unknown. Their simulation-based recovery and Lean ratio invariants do not prove a biological mechanism.

For reversibility, v2 corrected its prior analysis for nested repeated samples. In MPTR, 3 donors/13 matched pairs gave Horvath −9.7 years [−18.1, −1.3] and DamAge +13.1 arbitrary units [−18.0, +44.2]. In a Sendai protocol, 4 donors/11 pairs gave Horvath −22.4 years [−28.0, −16.7] and DamAge −2.6 units [−7.2, +2.1]. The Sendai day 9/11 contrasts were unresolved; near the full-iPSC day-15 stage, both decreased, including DamAge −24.4 (p=.030). A nonsignificant DamAge test is not an equivalence test, and the MPTR interval allows substantial reversal.

The mathematical identifiability background is not novel as a field: phase-type distributions admit non-unique generator representations. A primary identifiability paper gives the equivalence under similarity transforms. The result here is an exact application-level observability criterion for the two-clock/two-mode question and a finite-horizon limit on claims of irreversibility; novelty/priority beyond this lane remains unresolved.

## Exact model and propositions

Use operational coordinates `z_t=(r_t,d_t)`, where `r` is a reversible assay-linked mode and `d` a persistent-or-slow mode. After washout, let `z_{t+1}=diag(lambda,mu) z_t`, with `0≤lambda<1` and `0≤mu≤1`. For one scalar output `y_t=a r_t+b d_t+epsilon_t`, the observability matrix over t=0,1 is `[[a,b],[a*lambda,b*mu]]`; its determinant is `a*b*(mu-lambda)`. In noiseless data, `r_0,d_0` are recoverable iff this is nonzero. Measurement error is amplified inversely with the loading and rate gap.

If `|d_0|≤D`, the output gap between exact persistence (`mu=1`) and slow recovery (`mu=1-delta`) at every `t≤T` is at most `|b|D delta T`. If this is within twice the assay's bounded error radius, the observation sets overlap. Thus finite follow-up supports “persistent through horizon T at resolution eta,” not exact irreversibility. The full proof and a design algorithm appear in [CYCLE2_IDENTIFIABILITY.md](CYCLE2_IDENTIFIABILITY.md).

## Falsifiable prediction and experiment design

Conditional on a calibrated, observable two-mode model, a short pulse followed by washout should produce separable trajectories in at least two independent assay/function channels: a fast mode should decay while a slower mode persists or decays at a distinct rate. This is a falsifiable prediction of the model, not an asserted biological fact. It fails if mode loadings are collinear, held-out donors show no predictive gain over a one-mode model, cell lineage/composition explains the shift, or clock change does not align with orthogonal lesion/function outcomes.

Design a donor-blocked pulse/washout study in one prespecified primary cell type. Include sham and a separately calibrated mode-loading perturbation; track lineages to separate true within-lineage change from population replacement; sample baseline, end pulse, and washout days 1/3/7/14/28; measure two clock modalities, identity/pluripotency, a direct persistent-lesion assay, and function after washout. Fit `z[t+1]=A z[t]+B u[t]+xi` and `y[t]=Cz[t]+Du[t]+Gq[t]+epsilon`, where `D` allows direct assay effects and `q` records composition/identity. Estimate the whitened observability matrix and require a predeclared conditioning threshold. Select schedule and perturbations by D-optimality, maximize `log det(sum_t (C A^t)^T R^-1(C A^t))`, and preregister a minimum recovery rate and equivalence margins. Report finite-horizon categories, not “irreversible.” Actual sample size and dollars remain unknown until donor-level variance and assay noise are measured.

## Evidence status

The exact observability determinant and finite-horizon nonseparation bound are proved under the displayed linear model. Primary-paper assay and preprint statistics are source-reported. The v1 code reconstruction is internally inspected; v2 code archive is not independently replayed here. No current evidence establishes a biological irreversible damage state, maps DamAge to the mortality latent, or confirms the proposed model in a new experiment.
