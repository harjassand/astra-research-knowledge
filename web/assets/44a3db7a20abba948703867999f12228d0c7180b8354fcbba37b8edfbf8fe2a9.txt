Current lead

The highest-consequence scoped results are a pair of contrasting N81 noise statements. Independent Gaussian noise of variance nu_S=R^(-a) on every unheralded detector mode, including retained outputs, adds only O(sqrt(nu_S)) error to the conditional law, while exponentially smaller noise on herald modes keeps the herald feasible; a sampler for that family would still imply PP=BPP conditional on N81/N82. In contrast, an internally proved candidate positive-P posterior comparison shows that the same fixed inverse-polynomial additive noise on all detector modes makes the N81 retained conditional law TV-o(1) from vacuum. It avoids dividing a global trace error by the rare herald mass. This erases the N81 constant-TV amplification under the stated all-mode noise; subleading probabilities may still encode information. It says nothing about other noisy instances or general sampler complexity. See `outputs/research/photonic_complexity/all_mode_noise_n81_proof.txt` and PC-03/PC-14.

A new algorithmic result is PC-15: a centered positive-P Gaussian with rational post-loss covariance tA and a supplied near-isotropy certificate admits exact-real rejection sampling from its PNR-heralded count law with expected proposal count exp(O((d+kappa)eps_bar)), independent of 1/p_H. A separate finite-bit coupling targets TV eps_out+xi and charges numeric kappa, output-count cutoff, covariance certificate checks, precision, and the attempt cap. For N81, eps_bar=g/((1-g)nu), d,kappa=O(R), and fixed nu=R^-a make expected proposal count 1+o(1); the target noisy conditional law is vacuum-like and its constant-TV amplification is erased, although subleading probabilities may still encode information. Its polynomial-bit runtime in L also requires numeric kappa, M_out=tT/2, and accuracy budgets to be polynomially bounded; N81 meets these bounds. See `outputs/research/photonic_complexity/conditional_gaussian_rejection_proof.txt` and PC-15. This is not a sampler for arbitrary mixed/displaced Gaussian inputs and does not improve physical herald-generation rate.

The stronger PC-16/PC-17 route first absorbs the common herald Poisson exponential into C°=(C^(-1)+P_H)^(-1), integrates all zero-target heralds and unheralded modes, and rejects only on the m positive-count herald modes. Under a rational near-isotropy certificate for the tilted marginal S, its expected exact-real proposal count is exp(O(kappa eps_eff)), independent of total mode count and 1/p_H. The complete bounded-bit proof is in `outputs/research/photonic_complexity/conditional_gaussian_finite_bit_theorem.txt`; it uses latent Euclidean coupling plus the Poisson Hellinger bound, rational LDL/absolute precision, numeric kappa/Lambda/M_out charges, and a capped rejection loop. For all-mode-noisy N81, Lambda<=2 eventually (while exact-real acceptance tends to one) and the conservative explicit cap is M_out=O(R log^2 R), so runtime is polynomial in R and the rational input size. PC-16 exact-real identities received root analytic review; PC-17 received root analytic and sibling-leaf adversarial review with no fatal gap. This is internal validation, not external review; it does not change ideal N81 hardness or imply a general mixed/displaced sampler.

All-mode-noise findings (internal proof complete; independent audit pending)

Root proposed a proof shortcut that refutes universal uniform-noise conditional-law stability without attacking the N81 compiler itself. Set R=4^k, lambda=2^(-R^2), nu=R^(-a) for a fixed positive integer a, eta=1/R. Prepare TMSV(lambda), fan out the idler across R outputs with a rational Walsh-Hadamard first column, add identical Gaussian displacement noise on all modes before loss, herald one photon in each idler output, and retain the signal.

Ideal event: N<R pairs cannot herald. The N=R sector produces one photon in each fanout output with probability R!/R^R and survives herald-arm loss with eta^R; q_R=eta^R R!/R^R=R!/R^(2R). The geometric source tail above R is lambda^(2R+2), so its conditional fraction is at most lambda^2/[(1-lambda^2)q_R], tending to zero. The signal then tends Bin(R,1/R), hence Poisson(1).

Noisy reference: a vacuum input under the same isotropic noise, fanout, and loss becomes a product thermal state of mean t=eta nu=nu/R. The exact all-one herald probability is p0=[t/(1+t)^2]^R. The TMSV is trace distance lambda from vacuum; channel contractivity makes the noisy output joint law lambda-close to the reference, and lambda/p0 tends to zero. Postselection continuity then says the noisy TMSV's retained signal law is o(1)-close to a thermal law of mean t, hence vacuum. The conditional-TV gap tends to 1-exp(-1). This does not establish hardness/easiness of noisy N81 instances.

There is also an N81-specific herald-vacuum floor in mixed_gaussian_loss.txt: with uniform detector-plane noise t=eta nu on the herald, the source's all-herald-vacuum block contributes at least (1/2)t^h/(1+t)^(2h+h0), larger than the entire ideal herald by a large factor. This floor alone does not control other source contributions. The positive-P posterior comparison in all_mode_noise_n81.txt does: it proves the whole noisy conditional count law is vacuum-like for the compiled N81 family.

The lead and mixed-worker derivations are complete at internally proved candidate status; independent sibling audits are pending. Keep the distinction between (i) refuting universal noise-stability and (ii) deciding noisy conditional-sampling complexity. No 20th leaf was added.
