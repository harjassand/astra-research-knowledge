## Mathematical interface and conditional consequence under audit

For a loopless arbitrary multigraph on n qubits, the target is

\[
H=\sum_{e=uv}\bigl[\alpha_e(X_uX_v+Y_uY_v)+\gamma_e Z_uZ_v\bigr]
   +\sum_v(b_vX_v+c_vZ_v),
\quad \alpha_e\ge |\gamma_e|,\quad b_v\ge0,
\]
with rational coefficients and arbitrary rational c_v. The physical energy convention in the shared transfer is the Hamiltonian \(-H\). Define
\[
C=3\sum_e\alpha_e+\sum_v(b_v+|c_v|).
\]
The shared cycle3 derivation claims, conditional on the imported counting theorem, a relative FPRAS for \(Z=\operatorname{Tr}e^{\beta H}\), a standard relative bit-FPRAS for \(\lambda_{\max}(H)\), classical sampling of the computational-basis Gibbs measurement distribution in total variation, and trace-norm reconstruction of a specified k-qubit reduced state for k=O(log n). It does not claim a circuit preparing the full quantum Gibbs state. Its partition/state runtimes are polynomial in the *numerical* parameter \(\beta C\), inverse accuracy, and input length; this is not a uniform polynomial in binary \(\log\beta\) at arbitrarily low temperature. The complete transfer, including field lifting, trace error, state error, and the adjusted dummy rank for off-charge matrix entries, is recorded in the permitted shared baseline artifact `work/cycle3/xxz_thermal_audit.txt`; my checkpoint contribution here is the primary-source scope and exact comparator attack below.

For each edge, the positive Euler gate in that derivation is
\[
A_e=3\alpha I+\alpha(XX+YY)+\gamma ZZ,\qquad F_e=I+sA_e.
\]
In the two-bit gate table its six-vertex coefficients are
\[
a=1+s(3\alpha+\gamma),\quad b=1+s(3\alpha-\gamma),\quad c=2s\alpha.
\]
For all s\ge0,
\[
|a-b|=2s|\gamma|\le 2s\alpha=c,\qquad c\le a+b,
\]
so the three linear triangle inequalities hold. Positivity follows from these formulas and \(\alpha\ge|\gamma|\). This is precisely the six-vertex condition stated by Chen–Liu Corollary C.16, though the full arbitrary-graph circuit reduction uses their more general homogeneous Theorem 5.1 rather than applying C.16 directly to an arbitrary circuit graph. The one-qubit field gates require the homogeneous two-dummy lift already derived in the shared transfer; the local edge check alone is not a proof of the full XXZ result.

The imported Theorem 5.1's stated interface is: two nonnegative homogeneous log-concave measures \(\mu,\nu\) on subsets of a common ground set U, degrees a,b with a+b=|U|, value oracles, coefficient-range upper bounds, and positive support witnesses; it returns an FPRAS for \([x^U]g_\mu g_\nu\). The source gives an every-execution arithmetic/value-oracle bound
\[
O\!\left(n^{10}(n+\log(R_\mu R_\nu))^2\varepsilon^{-2}
\log^2\!\frac{2(n+\log(R_\mu R_\nu))}{\varepsilon}\right).
\]
Remark A.4 supplies polynomial bit time for rational parameters and polynomial-time rational value oracles of polynomial output length, including bounded fair-coin simulation. This imported theorem—not the mere existence of a six-vertex encoding—is the decisive new algorithmic input. Its source is dated 5 October 2026 and expressly discloses heavy use of GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra and GPT-6 Astra Ultra; it remains a preprint in this checkpoint, with no independent proof audit or priority determination here.

## Exact prior-art comparisons

**Chen–Liu, arXiv:2610.06724v1.** Theorem 5.1 and Remark A.4 are the conditional counting/bit-complexity basis above. Corollary C.16 gives an FPRAS on loopless 4-regular multigraphs when each nonnegative rational six-vertex signature satisfies the *linear* triangle inequalities. Corollary C.17 already gives an arbitrary-loopless-graph FPRAS for the EPR partition function when \(\beta W\) is polynomially bounded, and for its largest eigenvalue; its proof uses a symmetric Euler product and six-vertex counting. Thus the architecture is not wholly new, and the EPR endpoint overlaps exactly. With \(P_{\Psi^+}=(I+XX+YY-ZZ)/4\),
\[
\alpha(XX+YY-ZZ)=4\alpha P_{\Psi^+}-\alpha I.
\]
A common \(R_x(\pi/2)\) maps \(P_{\Phi^+}=(I+XX-YY+ZZ)/4\) to \(P_{\Psi^+}\). Therefore, for \(\gamma_e=-\alpha_e\), b=c=0 and A=\sum_e\alpha_e,
\[
H=U(4Q_{\rm EPR}-A I)U^*,\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}e^{\beta H}=e^{-\beta A}\operatorname{Tr}e^{4\beta Q_{\rm EPR}},
\]
where \(Q_{\rm EPR}=\sum_e\alpha_e P_{\Phi^+}^{(e)}\) and U is the common rotation. This is a direct subfamily overlap, not a proof that Chen–Liu state reconstruction is redundant or that its C.17 statement covers arbitrary fields.

**Cai–Liu–Lu, arXiv:1712.05880v1.** Their Theorem 1.1 provides an FPRAS in the squared-triangle region \(a^2\le b^2+c^2\) and permutations (and a hardness result when an ordinary triangle inequality is violated). The new target gate is generally outside the former region. An exact rational fixture is saved in `scope_audit.py` / `scope_audit.json`: \(\alpha=1,s=1/10,\gamma=1/2\) gives
\[
(a,b,c)=(27/20,5/4,1/5),\qquad a^2-b^2-c^2=11/50>0.
\]
For \(\gamma=-1/2\), a and b swap and \(b^2-a^2-c^2=11/50>0\). In general, for \(\gamma>0\),
\[
a^2-b^2-c^2=4s\gamma(1+3s\alpha)-4s^2\alpha^2>0
\]
for all sufficiently small positive s; for \(\gamma<0\) the symmetric b inequality fails. Thus the old squared-triangle theorem cannot replace Chen–Liu's linear-triangle input on the nonzero-gamma interior. This is a scoped exact obstruction to that proof route, not hardness of the quantum problem.

**Bravyi–Gosset, arXiv:1612.05602.** Their Eq. (1) / Theorem 1 cover physical Hamiltonians \(\sum_{ij}(-b_{ij}XX+c_{ij}YY)+\sum_i d_i(I+Z_i)\), \(|c_{ij}|\le b_{ij}\), after normalization; the partition approximation runs in \(\mathrm{poly}(n,\beta,\varepsilon^{-1})\). For \(\gamma=0,b_v=0\), the target physical Hamiltonian \(-H\) matches with \(b_{ij}=\alpha_e,c_{ij}=-\alpha_e,d_v=-c_v\), up to the known scalar \(-\sum_v c_v I\). This is a real overlap. If \(\alpha\gamma\ne0\), the target edge tensor \(\operatorname{diag}(\alpha,\alpha,\gamma)\) has rank 3, while Bravyi–Gosset's displayed two-axis edge tensor has rank at most 2. Local unitaries act on this tensor by invertible left/right rotations, preserving rank, so they cannot identify an individual rank-3 target edge with that displayed interaction. This does not exclude other algorithmic reductions. The target's in-plane X fields also are not in their displayed field interface.

**Harrow–Mehraban–Soleimanifar, arXiv:1910.09071.** Theorem 47 is a Lee–Yang zero-location theorem on arbitrary interaction graphs. In its no-cross-term specialization, the coefficient condition is \(J_{zz}\ge |J_{xx}|,|J_{yy}|\). The physical target \(-H\), after common \(R_x(\pi/2)\), has edge coefficients \((J_{xx},J_{yy},J_{zz})=(\alpha,\gamma,\alpha)\), and fields \(h_x=b,h_y=-c,h_z=0\). Thus \(\alpha\ge|\gamma|\) places the edge tensor in the theorem's structural cone. But because the target has no field in the variable longitudinal direction (h_z=0), that zero-location statement is vacuous as a partition approximation for this target slice. It must not be confused with their Theorem 50 algorithm. Theorem 50 uses the anisotropic XXZ form with equal transverse couplings \(J_{xx}=J_{yy}=J_{ij}\), easy-axis condition \(J_{zz}\ge|J_{ij}|\), and a uniform constant longitudinal field \(\mu\); its algorithm is deterministic quasipolynomial, \(n^{O(\log(n/\varepsilon))}\). The direct target overlap is the opposite cone's isotropic boundary \(\gamma=\alpha\), with fields restricted to the theorem's uniform single-axis form after a global rotation. It does not supply the arbitrary site-dependent b_v,c_v easy-plane result.

**Ryan Mann, arXiv:2608.19489v1.** Theorem 12 is a close stoquastic high-temperature comparator: bounded degree \(\Delta\), local dimension d, and \(\beta\le1/(2e^3d^3\Delta)\) yield expected-time counting \(O(|G|^2\varepsilon^{-2}\log^2(|G|/\varepsilon))\) and sampling bounds. The physical target \(-H\) is stoquastic in the computational basis for the stated signs, but arbitrary graph degree, arbitrary inhomogeneous fields and low temperatures are outside this specific theorem's stated interface. It therefore identifies an overlapping high-temperature regime, not a replacement for the claimed numerical-\(\beta C\) result.
