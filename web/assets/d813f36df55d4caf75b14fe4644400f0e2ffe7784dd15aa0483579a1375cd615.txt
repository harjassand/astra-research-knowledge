INDEPENDENT AUDIT: CONDITIONAL HADAMARD TRANSFORM AND THE ISING FPRAS
2026-10-08

Scope and verdict
=================

I independently reconstructed Sections 3 and 5 of
outputs/research/sol_bond_renewal/RESULT.txt and checked the imported result
against Jerrum and Sinclair's primary author PDF and the 1993 SIAM journal
record. The conditional Fourier-tensor identity, common-ghost reduction, and
the conditional-sampling/importance-sampling ratios are algebraically valid
for the stated q=2, full-span, maximum gap-multiplicity two family. The
resulting zero-field Ising instances fit the interaction class in JS93 after
contracting all same-spin pins, deleting Ising self-loops as constants, and
adding parallel couplings. The annealing path stays at positive field
parameter mu >= 1/v, so the published sampler applies.

The paper itself is explicitly in an exact-real-arithmetic model. It is not,
by citation alone, a bit-complexity FPRAS. The report's rationalization and
finite-kernel simulation repair is sound in principle; below I give an
explicit schedule and the coupling argument needed to make that claim
auditable. One qualification should remain explicit: JS93's sampler runtime
depends polynomially on mu^{-1}; it is uniform in edge activities, not in mu.
The report's schedule mu >= 1/v is what makes this dependence polynomial.
This audit verifies the imported theorem/application and the displayed
identities; it is not external proof review, a practical implementation, or a
novelty/priority determination. No finite test is used as proof.

The scope remains the report's restricted one: a full-span q=2 block with
each positive gap label occurring at most twice. Nothing here extends the
tensor construction or the row correction to arbitrary multiplicity or a
censored prefix.

1. Conditional Fourier graph, from the tensors
================================================

Write delta(x,y)=1[x=y], and let

  T = 2^{-1/2} [[1,1],[1,-1]].

For a two-port equality tensor,

  sum_{x,y} T[a,x] T[c,y] delta(x,y)
    = sum_x T[a,x]T[c,x] = delta(a,c),

because T is orthogonal. Thus an internal equality wire is unchanged by
applying T at both ends. The all-ones one-port vector maps to
T(1,1)^T = sqrt(2) e_0. Consequently an all-ones tensor on k ports maps to
2^{k/2} times the tensor pinning all transformed ports to 0.

For a doubled label, denote its two start ports by A,C and its two end ports
by B,D. Its free tensor is

  F_free(A,C,B,D) = 1 + delta(A,C) delta(B,D).

The first term is the identity permutation; the second is the swap. Transform
all four ports. The constant term is the all-ones four-port tensor and maps
to 4*1[A=B=C=D=0]. The product of the two equality tensors is invariant,
giving

  Fhat_free = 4*1[A=B=C=D=0] + delta(A,C)delta(B,D).

The forced-identity tensor is 1, so its transform is
4*1[A=B=C=D=0]. The forced-swap tensor is
delta(A,C)delta(B,D), so it is unchanged. These are exactly the three
tensors stated in the report. The two equalities should be wired for every
doubled label, including forced labels. A free label adds, in addition, an
interaction between its start-wire component and end-wire component; a
forced identity pins both components to 0 and contributes 4; a forced swap
adds no interaction. Contracting adjacent word ports is legitimate because
the identity contraction is preserved by T^T T=I.

For a free label, let s,t in {0,1} be the spins on the start and end wire
components. The transformed local factor is 5 when s=t=0, 1 when s=t=1,
and 1 when s!=t. Thus the interaction matrix is

  M = [[5,1],[1,1]].

A singleton gap has an all-ones two-port tensor; it pins both endpoints to 0
and contributes 2. Each of the two word-end all-ones vectors contributes
sqrt(2), hence together contribute 2 and pin their wire components. If
G_h is the graph of wire components and free-label interactions for partial
bond assignment h, with all specified pins set to 0, then

  W_h = 2^{1 + (# singleton labels) + 2 (# forced identities)}
        Z_M(G_h; pins=0),

where W_h is the sum of U(sigma) over all completions sigma of h. This
identity also covers a component pinned by several sources: all pins have
the same value, so they impose one consistent condition. Positivity follows
independently from either side: each constant hidden path satisfies all
selected equalities, and every free tensor has positive entries.

2. Conditional law and the estimator ratios
=============================================

Let L be the number of doubled labels and let

  U(sigma) = sum_u product_{j=1}^L
                  1[the swap equalities for j hold under u] ,
  W = sum_sigma U(sigma),  mu_U(sigma)=U(sigma)/W.

For partial assignment h on some labels, define

  W_h = sum_{sigma extending h} U(sigma).

Then W_h=W_{h0}+W_{h1}, and the exact self-reduction law is

  Pr_{mu_U}[sigma_j=1 | h] = W_{h1}/(W_{h0}+W_{h1}).

Both branch weights are positive. For fixed assignments of all other
labels, selecting j=1 adds at most two binary equality edges to the
hidden-path constraint graph. Each such edge reduces its number of
components by at most one, so its number of satisfying assignments drops by
at most a factor 2 per edge. Summing over the other label assignments gives
W_{h0}/4 <= W_{h1} <= W_{h0}; hence this particular branch probability is
in [1/5,1/2]. This lower bound is a useful conditioning check, although the
FPRAS call itself estimates W_h without inverse-W_h dependence.

For the row-weighted correction in the report, let B(sigma) be the summed
row-factorial-denominator mass, let

  C_m = binom(m,floor(m/2))/m!,
  g(sigma) = B(sigma)/(U(sigma) C_m).

Since sum_sigma B(sigma)=Z(w), direct cancellation gives

  E_{mu_U}[g]
    = (1/W) sum_sigma U(sigma) B(sigma)/(U(sigma)C_m)
    = Z(w)/(W C_m),
  Z(w) = W C_m E_{mu_U}[g].

This is the estimator ratio used by the outer FPRAS. It is distinct from the
Ising annealing ratios below. In particular, the conditional graph FPRAS is
used for W_h branch ratios to sample mu_U; it does not by itself estimate
the row-weighted evidence Z(w).

3. Common-ghost Ising identity, including pins and loops
========================================================

Map bit 0 to spin +1 and bit 1 to spin -1. For rational r>1 define

  M_r = [[r^4,1],[1,1]],
  M_r(s,t) = r^{1+s+t+st},  s,t in {+1,-1}.

At r=5^{1/4}, this equals M exactly. Let G have e free-interaction edges,
counting parallel edges with multiplicity, and let all pinned vertices be
specified +1. For every edge vw of G add in H the three zero-field Ising
interactions vw, v*, and w*, with the same coupling J=log(r)>0; * is one
common ghost site. Each Ising edge contributes r^{s_x s_y}. If v,w are
unpinned and s_*=+1, the triangle contributes

  r^{s_v s_w + s_v + s_w};

multiplication by the outside factor r gives M_r(s_v,s_w). If v or w is
pinned, identify that vertex with *. Under s_*=+1 the same formula still
holds with its pinned spin +1. If both endpoints are pinned, all triangle
edges become loops and their product, including the outside r, is r^4, as
required.

For each fixed assignment with s_*=+1, the product over triangles is
r^e times the corresponding M_r weight. In the zero-field graph H, global
spin flip is a weight-preserving bijection between configurations with
s_*=+1 and s_*=-1. Therefore, before deleting loops,

  Z_{M_r}(G; pins=+1) = r^e Z_Ising(H; B=0)/2.

This is an exact partition identity, not an appeal to an Ising sampler with a
hard or nonuniform field. It depends essentially on all pins agreeing. H is
an unrestricted graph: it may be disconnected and have isolated sites.
After triangle construction, remove each Ising self-loop: since
s_i^2=1, a loop of multiplicity t contributes the known constant r^t.
Let ell be the total number of loop copies removed. If H_simple denotes the
resulting loop-free graph, the exact formula is

  Z_{M_r}(G; pins=+1) = r^{e+ell} Z_Ising(H_simple; B=0)/2.

Merge parallel non-loop edges of multiplicity t into one pair coupling
t log r. Do this after the triangle construction. In particular, an original
self-interaction vw with v=w produces one loop and two v* edges; deleting
the loop but retaining/merging the v* edges is necessary and gives the
correct spin-dependent factor. An original loop is not simply an ignorable
constant before ghost expansion. The resulting pair-interaction matrix has
nonnegative couplings, so it is ferromagnetic. The common ghost adds one
site and at most three edges per original interaction; the site and edge
counts are O(m).

For rational r and merged multiplicity t, the inputs needed by the
high-temperature JS algorithm are exact rationals:

  lambda_t = tanh(t log r) = (r^{2t}-1)/(r^{2t}+1),
  cosh(t log r) = (r^t+r^{-t})/2.

Thus no logarithm needs to be evaluated in the bit implementation. Since
t<=e=O(m) and r has O(log(e/rho)) bits below, these rational numbers have
polynomial bit length. Zero-interaction pairs are omitted; the theorem's
input is equivalently the symmetric matrix with these nonnegative pair
couplings. Self-loops and multiple edges therefore cause no interface
mismatch after the stated exact simplifications.

4. What JS93 proves and why this instance is in its class
=========================================================

Primary source: Mark Jerrum and Alistair Sinclair, “Polynomial-Time
Approximation Algorithms for the Ising Model,” SIAM Journal on Computing
22(5), 1087–1116 (1993), DOI 10.1137/0222066. The author-hosted PDF is
https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~sinclair/ising.pdf; the publisher record is
https://doi.org/10.1137/0222066.

The paper's Ising input is an arbitrary symmetric pair-interaction matrix
with every coupling nonnegative, a uniform external field B, and beta>0.
There is no planarity, degree, or connectedness restriction. Theorem 5 gives
an FPRAS for the partition function. The theorem is being invoked only at
B=0. The ghost reduction has no fields at all; the exact pins were removed
by vertex identification and a global-flip factor of 2. Thus no theorem for
nonuniform fields or infinite field is being assumed.

The paper explicitly says (printed p.8; author PDF p.9/10 depending on
cover-page counting) that it ignores inexact-computer-arithmetic errors and
uses a model where real arithmetic is exact and arithmetic operations and
standard functions such as exp cost unit time. Theorem 5 alone therefore
does not establish polynomial BIT time. This is a genuine interface caveat,
not a graph-class failure.

For the bit repair, Theorem 2's subgraph-world generator takes edge
activities lambda_e in [0,1), a field activity mu in (0,1], and a target
variation distance delta. If E_H is the number of positive pair edges and
v the number of sites, its stated runtime is

  O(E_H^2 mu^{-8} (log(delta^{-1}) + E_H)).

The conductance statement behind this is Phi >= mu^4/(4 E_H). Starting from
the empty subgraph, its stationary probability is at least 2^{-E_H}, since
every subgraph weight is at most 1. These are uniform in the lambda_e, but
not in mu. Theorem 5's annealing path uses only mu>=1/v, so this dependence
is polynomial for H_h. The report's phrase “uniformly in the edge
activities” is correct if read this way; it should not be read as saying
uniform in the external-field activity down to zero.

5. Independent reconstruction of the JS estimator schedule
===========================================================

For H with v sites and pair-edge activities lambda_e, the high-temperature
expansion at field activity mu is

  Z_0(mu) = sum_{X subseteq E_H} mu^{o(X)} product_{e in X} lambda_e,

where o(X) is the number of odd-degree sites. The spin partition function is

  Z_Ising(H;B) = (2 cosh(beta B))^v
                 product_e cosh(beta J_e) * Z_0(tanh(beta B)).

At zero field, the target is 2^v product_e cosh(J_e) Z_0(0), with J_e=t log r
after merging. The value Z_0(1)=product_e(1+lambda_e) is exact. Set

  mu_j=(v-j)/v,  j=0,...,v.

For j<v, under

  pi_{mu_j}(X) = mu_j^{o(X)} product_e lambda_e / Z_0(mu_j),

the observable

  f_j(X) = (mu_{j+1}/mu_j)^{o(X)}

has expectation exactly

  E_{pi_{mu_j}} f_j = Z_0(mu_{j+1})/Z_0(mu_j).

For the last step mu_v=0, define f_{v-1}(X)=1[o(X)=0], which is exactly the
0^0=1 convention and gives Z_0(0)/Z_0(1/v). Every f_j is in [0,1]. The JS
step-size lemma bounds each ratio below by 1/10 when the adjacent mu values
differ by at most 1/v. Hence each stage can be estimated by a bounded
sample mean with polynomial sample count; the product of the v estimated
ratios estimates Z_0(0). In Theorem 5's stated allocation, each stage has
relative error O(eta/v), failure O(1/v), and its sampler variation input is
eta/(160v), for desired Ising accuracy eta. Repeating the whole FPRAS by a
median amplifies its fixed 3/4 success probability to 1-gamma with
O(log(gamma^{-1})) repetitions. The source's ratio convention is
asymmetric, 1/(1+eta) <= estimate/target <= 1+eta; it implies the symmetric
interval [1-eta,1+eta] used below.

6. Explicit rational approximation and finite-randomness schedule
=================================================================

Fix one conditional W_h call with requested relative error rho in (0,1), and
let e be its number of free interaction edges, with parallel copies counted.
If e=0, compute the partition exactly. Otherwise choose a dyadic rational
r>1 by rational bisection of x^4=5 so that

  |r^4/5 - 1| <= rho/(16 e).

This uses O(log(e/rho)) bits. The inequality is checkable by rational
arithmetic. Since rho/(16e)<=1/16, |log(1+x)|<=2|x| here, so
|log(r^4/5)|<=rho/(8e). Each configuration weight in the e-edge M_r
partition differs from its M weight by (r^4/5)^a for an integer 0<=a<=e.
Thus its ratio, and the partition ratio, lie in [exp(-rho/8),exp(rho/8)].

Run JS93 on H_h with internal accuracy eta=rho/8 and requested failure
probability gamma. Its rational prefactors are 2^{b(h)}, r^{e+ell}/2, 2^v,
and the product of (r^t+r^{-t})/2; they have polynomial bit length. Combining
the edge approximation and an Ising estimate in [1-eta,1+eta] gives an
overall W_h estimate in [1-rho,1+rho]: for the upper side,

  log( exp(rho/8)(1+rho/8) ) <= rho/4 <= log(1+rho),

and for the lower side,

  log( exp(-rho/8)(1-rho/8) ) >= -rho/8-rho/7 > -rho
                                                    >= log(1-rho).

All these estimates are conservative.

To make each JS generator a fixed worst-case number of finite-bit steps,
take its requested stationary-law variation tolerance to be

  tau = eta/(160 v),

as in the published Theorem 5 schedule. For a stage with mu>=1/v and
E_H>=1, use an integer upper bound

  T = ceil(16 E_H^2 v^8 (L + E_H)),
  L = ceil(log_2(2/tau)).

The paper's conductance/mixing bound gives exact-chain TV at most tau/2 at
this T (enlarging T by an absolute factor, if required by the log-base
conversion, is harmless). At a state X, toggling edge e changes o(X) by
Delta in {-2,0,2}; the Metropolis acceptance ratio is formed from
lambda_e*mu^Delta or its reciprocal, then truncated at 1. These are
rational computations of polynomial bit length because lambda_e is the
rational above, mu>=1/v is rational, and |Delta|<=2.

At every step implement the uniform edge choice by dividing 2^b equally as
possible among E_H categories and use b fresh bits; its total-variation
error from uniform is at most E_H/2^b. Implement a rational acceptance
probability p as floor(2^b p)/2^b using b fresh bits; its absolute error is
less than 2^{-b}. Choose

  b = ceil(log_2(4 E_H T/tau)).

Then both errors are at most tau/(4T), so the one-step transition kernels
are within tau/(2T) in total variation, uniformly over current states.
The standard stepwise coupling/telescoping inequality bounds the output-law
difference between the ideal and rounded T-step chains by at most tau/2.
Together with the ideal mixing error tau/2, the rounded generator's law is
within tau of pi_mu. This uses a fixed O(b) random bits per action, not an
unbounded rejection sampler. Exact rational-to-dyadic rounding uses integer
arithmetic on polynomial-bit numbers. Thus each generator call has
worst-case polynomial bit cost. The theorem's samples are freshly
regenerated from the empty state with independent random bits, so the
sample-mean concentration argument applies to independent outputs from
their common approximate law.

Because 0<=f_j<=1, a generator law within tau changes E f_j by at most tau.
The true ratio is at least 1/10, so tau=eta/(160v) gives a bias much smaller
than the per-stage relative error O(eta/v). A polynomial sample count, for
example O(eta^{-2} v^2 log(v/gamma)) per stage using a bounded-variable
Chernoff/Bernstein bound, controls sampling fluctuations; confidence
amplification and union bounds over the v stages are polynomial. The
remaining exact arithmetic has polynomial length: f_j is a rational power
with exponent at most v (or a zero/one indicator at the last step), and the
products/averages and rational prefactors have polynomial-bit numerators
and denominators. Since v,E_H=O(m), the complete conditional partition
estimate has polynomial bit complexity in m, rho^{-1}, and log(gamma^{-1}).

7. Audit findings / qualifications
===================================

1. Fourier transform: verified by orthogonality and the all-ones transform.
   The factor 2^{1+singletons+2 forced-identities} and the M=[[5,1],[1,1]]
   interaction are correct. The wire graph is the fully wired Fourier graph,
   not the selected-edge correction graph from the report's next section.
2. Conditional ratios: W_h is a sum over all completions, the exact next-bit
   law is W_{h1}/(W_{h0}+W_{h1}), and the evidence identity is
   Z=W C_m E_{mu_U}g. The JS partition estimator is a separate telescoping
   product of high-temperature ratios.
3. Ghost: the common-ghost identity is exact for same-sign pins. It eliminates
   fields from the instance passed to JS93. Self-loops must be removed only
   after triangle expansion; original G self-loops can leave ghost-parallel
   edges that carry a field after conditioning and must be retained.
4. Theorem interface: arbitrary nonnegative pair couplings and B=0 satisfy
   JS93. No planarity or degree condition is missing. The paper's native
   guarantee uses exact real arithmetic, so the finite-bit argument is
   additional work, not part of the cited theorem.
5. Precision schedule: with rational r, rational lambda/cosh factors, a
   positive-mu annealing path, and the explicit per-step kernel approximation
   above, the published mixing argument can be realized with fixed finite
   randomness and polynomial bit arithmetic. The report's finite-bit repair
   is therefore sound as a theorem-level construction, though it is not an
   implementation benchmark. State the sampler's mu^{-8} dependence when
   summarizing it.
6. Proof and novelty status: the identities above are independently
   reconstructed algebraic proofs; the FPRAS is an imported JS93 theorem
   plus the rational/finite-kernel repair. The report's finite checks are
   diagnostics only. The search/evidence here does not establish novelty or
   priority for the conditional transform/application.
