THEOREM AND EXACT SCOPE

Let E be any nonempty family of density matrices in M_d. Supply ONE CPTP
broadcaster with identical BISTOCHASTIC marginal Phi, and suppose

  sup_(rho in E) T(Phi(rho),rho)<=b, T=||.||_1/2.

Phi need not be HS-self-adjoint. Put

  R0=sup_(rho in E) Dobs(rho||I/d),
  Dobs(rho||sigma)=sup_(0<=F<=I) p ln(p/q).

Then the supplied universal C8 theorem provides ONE pure canonical EB Psi,
independent of the unknown rho, for which

  sup_(rho in E) T(Psi(rho),rho)^2
       <=256 (R0+1) b^(1/2).                            (T)

If Phi is HS-self-adjoint the same proof uses C4, with 128 instead of 256.
The theorem covers arbitrary families, states, ranks and dimensions under
its two explicit restrictions. It is not a fixed covariance-family example.
When b=0 the one supplied comparator fixes E exactly. The trivial bound
T<=1 covers large right sides. Finite algebraic dimension is explicit;
no acquisition, efficient compilation or finite gate precision is claimed.

1. ROOT DIRICHLET ENERGY REDUCES TO A CLASSICAL KERNEL

Fix rho, diagonalize it as diag(p_i), and define

  P_ij=<i|Phi(|j><j|)|i>.

CP gives P_ij>=0. Trace preservation and unitality give column and row sums
one. No symmetry of P is assumed. Its action on the diagonal of rho is
diag Phi(rho)=Pp. Let r=p-Pp. Pinching contracts trace norm, hence

  sum_i |r_i|<=2b, sum_i r_i=0.

For any real spectral function h(p), ordinary HS gives

  E_Phi(h)=Tr[h(rho)(I-Phi)h(rho)]
           =(1/2)sum_ij P_ij(h(p_i)-h(p_j))^2>=0.        (1)

This identity only needs the row/column sums. It equals the quadratic form
of I-Sym Phi, and is PSD because a bistochastic quantum channel contracts
Hermitian HS norm. In particular E_Phi(sqrt(rho)) is a full quantum energy,
not a replacement of trace distance by a local measurement norm.

2. A BOUNDED LOG PRIMITIVE CHARGES THE ROOT ENERGY

Choose M>0, set l=e^(-M)/d, u=e^M/d, and let
g(x)=min(u,max(l,x)). Put h(x)=sqrt(g(x)) and f(x)=ln g(x).
Let G be the differentiable convex primitive of f, chosen to equal
x ln x-x on [l,u] and to extend linearly outside that interval.

For any a,c>=0 its Bregman divergence satisfies

  G(a)-G(c)-f(c)(a-c)
      >=g(a) ln(g(a)/g(c))-g(a)+g(c)
      >=(sqrt(g(a))-sqrt(g(c)))^2.                       (2)

The first inequality follows by splitting each scalar segment at l,u;
the exterior linear portions contribute nonnegative excess over the
clipped relative-entropy expression. The second is the scalar inequality
x ln x-x+1>=(sqrt(x)-1)^2. It follows from
x ln x-2x+2sqrt(x)>=0: after x=y^2, divide by 2y and use
y ln y-y+1>=0. Boundary a=0 is legal because g(a)>=l>0.

Row/column sums cancel the two G terms in the weighted sum. Thus

  sum_ij P_ij(h(p_i)-h(p_j))^2
      <=sum_ij P_ij[G(p_j)-G(p_i)-f(p_i)(p_j-p_i)]
      =sum_i r_i f(p_i)
      <=2b M.                                          (3)

In the last line, subtract the constant -ln d using sum r_i=0, and note
|f(p_i)+ln d|<=M. The middle expression is automatically nonnegative;
the upper bound does not assume sign alignment of individual residuals.
Equations (1),(3) give E_Phi(h)<=bM.

3. OBSERVATIONAL DIVERGENCE CONTROLS THE DISCARDED ROOT TAIL

Let J={i:p_i>e^M/d}. Its spectral projection is an allowed effect. Write
p_J=sum_(i in J)p_i and q_J=|J|/d. If J is nonempty, p_J/q_J>=e^M, hence

  p_J M<=Dobs(rho||I/d)<=R0.

If J is empty the same tail bound holds trivially. For the lower clipped
tail, every squared root discrepancy is <=l and there are <=d entries.
Therefore, with A=sqrt(rho) and H=h(rho),

  ||A-H||_2^2<=R0/M+e^(-M).                             (4)

The clipping is performed on the root; it is not a claim that a linear
channel or normalized state with a small operator likelihood was acquired.

Since I-Sym Phi is PSD and has norm <=2, quadratic triangle inequality gives

  E_Phi(A)<=2E_Phi(H)+2E_Phi(A-H)
           <=2bM+4[R0/M+e^(-M)].                       (5)

For b>0 put D=R0+1 and M=sqrt(D/b)>=1. Since e^(-M)<=1/M,
the last side is <=6sqrt(Db), hence certainly

  E_Phi(sqrt(rho))<=8sqrt((R0+1)b).                      (6)

For b=0 let M tend to infinity in (5), giving zero energy. All estimates
are uniform over the family. No reference minimum eigenvalue, log d term,
relative operator bound exp(R0/epsilon^2), or spectral gap appears.

4. ONE COMMON CANONICAL EB CHANNEL RECOVERS THE STATES

The supplied C8 theorem gives one bistochastic, HS-self-adjoint, HS-positive
canonical EB Psi with

  I-Psi<=8(I-Sym Phi).

For A=sqrt(rho) put v=<A,(I-Psi)A>. Since ||A||_2=1 and 0<=Psi<=I,
0<=v<=1, and

  ||A-Psi(A)||_2^2<=v,
  1-||Psi(A)||_2^2=<A,(I-Psi^2)A><=2v.

Let C=Psi(A). Unital Schwarz gives Psi(rho)>=C^2. Trace preservation makes
||Psi(rho)-C^2||_1=1-||C||_2^2. Also
||A^2-C^2||_1<=2||A-C||_2, since ||C||_2<=1. Consequently

  T(Psi(rho),rho)<=sqrt(v)+v<=2sqrt(v).                   (7)

No rho-dependent Psi was introduced. Combining (6),(7) and the common C8
order yields T^2<=32E_Phi(A)<=256sqrt((R0+1)b)
<=256(R0+1)b^(1/2), proving (T). C4 halves the displayed constant.

5. THE EXACT REMAINING TARGET GAP

The requested main radius R(E) optimizes its center over closed conv(E).
It can be much smaller than R0 even when I/d belongs to that hull. A pair
{I/d,pure rho} has R(E)<=ln2, whereas Dobs(rho||I/d)=ln d. Thus one cannot
replace R0 by R(E) in this theorem without a new argument.

The main broadcaster is also not assumed bistochastic. Turning it into a
reference-preserving Petz-corrected map produces a NONTRACIAL KMS geometry,
where the simple eigenbasis stochastic-kernel reduction (1) no longer holds.
NONTRACIAL_KMS_C4.txt reconstructs a comparator in that geometry, but
NONTRACIAL_ROOT_OBSTRUCTION.txt proves the direct root/log spectral-clipping
replacement false, already for faithful 3-by-3 matrices and an EB replacer.
Thus (T) is real arbitrary-family progress with a new polynomial deficit
charge; the full optimized-radius power target remains UNKNOWN.
