# c09_l03 INITIAL: entropy-thick joint posteriors give dimension-uniform archives

**Status:** conditional internal theorem, fully derived for the stated affine likelihood interface. It is a sufficient-hypothesis result, not a field-breaking or priority claim. Its joint-posterior condition excludes the known shared-scalar counterexample. External proof review and novelty search remain open.

## Claim and exact interface

For each finite dimension `d`, let `(X, μ)` be a reference observation space and let `Z=(Z_1,...,Z_d): X -> [-1,1]^d` be measurable and centered: `E_μ Z_i=0`. For a known positive sequence `s_i`, define, for every `v` in the Euclidean unit ball,

\[
\frac{dP_v}{d\mu}(x)=f_v(x)=1+\sum_{i=1}^d s_i v_i Z_i(x),\qquad \|s\|_2\le\rho<1.
\]

Since `|Z_i|≤1`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives `f_v≥1−ρ>0`. The archive sees one `X~P_v` but not `v`, retains a classical/quantum hybrid state of total Hilbert dimension `D`, and uses one fixed decoder to return one classical `Y in X`. The requirement is `sup_v TV(Law_v(Y),P_v)≤ε`; all input-dependent records count in `D`. Independent shared randomness is allowed. When `E_μ[Z_i Z_j]=σ_0^2 1_{i=j}`, the Fisher matrix at zero is exactly `σ_0^2 diag(s_i^2)`.

**Joint-posterior thickness.** For every finite coordinate set `I`, the joint score vector `Z_I` has a strictly positive `C^1` density `g_I` on `[-1,1]^|I|`, and

\[
\max_{i\in I}\|\partial_i\log g_I\|_\infty\le L
\]

for a constant `L` independent of `d` and `I`. This is a condition on the joint distribution of the posterior features, not their covariance. For `k=|I|`, the log density oscillates by at most `2Lk`; normalization gives `||g_I||_∞≤2^{-k}e^{2Lk}` and hence

\[
h(Z_I)\ge k(\ln2-2L)\ge-Hk,\qquad H=\max(0,2L-\ln2).
\]

For every finite `d`, the lower bound below is a partial sum over active coordinates and the upper bound is a partial sum over the retained coordinates; their constants do not depend on `d`. For `s_i=a e^{-bi}` and `||s||_2<1`, in the infinite model (or whenever `d` reaches the effective cutoff `b^{-1}ln(a/epsilon)+O(1)`), these bounds give

\[
B_q(\epsilon)\ge \frac{\ln^2(a/\epsilon)}{4b\ln2}-O_{a,b,L}(\ln(1/\epsilon)),
\]

\[
B_{cl}(\epsilon)\le \frac{\ln^2(a/\epsilon)}{4b\ln2}+O_{a,b,L}(\ln(1/\epsilon)\ln\ln(1/\epsilon)).
\]

For fixed smaller `d`, the same theorem gives the expected dimension-truncated sums, not a false log-squared lower bound. `B_q=inf log_2 D` ranges over arbitrary hybrid memories and `B_cl` over classical labels. In the stated large-d regime the common leading order and coefficient are proved; the error terms are not claimed sharp.

## Positive finite-label upper construction

Choose `m` with `||s_{>m}||_2≤ε/4` (or `m=d` when the tail is empty). For each retained coordinate use the graded mesh

\[
u_j=-1+6(j/J_i)^2-4(j/J_i)^3,\quad j=0,\ldots,J_i,
\]

and its nonnegative piecewise-linear hat functions `φ_{i,j}`. They sum to one and reproduce the coordinate: `sum_j u_j φ_{i,j}(z_i)=z_i`. Exact mesh geometry gives maximum gap `3/J_i`, endpoint gaps `6/J_i^2`, and adjacent-gap differences at most `12/J_i^2`.

On input `x`, independently draw label `J_i` with probability `φ_{i,J_i}(Z_i(x))`, then store the tuple `J=(J_1,...,J_m)`. Let `φ_j(z)=product_{i≤m}φ_{i,j_i}(z_i)` and `q_j=E_μ φ_j(Z)`. Given a label with `q_j>0`, the decoder samples

\[
\mu_j(dx)=q_j^{-1}\phi_j(Z(x))\mu(dx).
\]

This is a positive measure-prepare channel and fixes `μ` exactly, since `sum_j q_j μ_j=μ`. Its output density is

\[
Kf_v(y)=\sum_j\phi_j(Z(y))\,E_\mu[f_v(X)\mid J=j].
\]

For fixed `i` and other-coordinate labels `j_{-i}`, the unnormalized conditional density of `Z_i=t` before drawing its own label is

\[
q(t)=\int g_{[m]}(t,z_{-i})\prod_{k\ne i}\phi_{k,j_k}(z_k)\,dz_{-i}.
\]

Differentiating under the integral and using the log-gradient condition gives `|q'(t)|≤Lq(t)`. Each hat is supported within `6/J_i` of its node. Its Lebesgue centroid differs from the node by at most `4/J_i^2`: for an interior hat the displacement is `(Δ_right−Δ_left)/3`, and an endpoint hat is displaced by at most its endpoint gap divided by three. Reweighting a hat by `q` adds at most `L exp(2Lδ)δ^2` for support radius `δ≤6/J_i`. Therefore, for `J_i≥J_0(L):=max(3,ceil(6L))`,

\[
|E[Z_i\mid J=j]-u_{j_i}|\le C_L/J_i^2,\qquad C_L=4e+36e^2L,
\]

uniformly in all other-coordinate labels. Averaging node values with the encoder hats and using linear reproduction yields `|KZ_i(y)−Z_i(y)|≤C_L/J_i^2`.

The retained affine-density error is bounded pointwise by

\[
C_L\Big(\sum_{i\le m}s_i^2/J_i^4\Big)^{1/2}.
\]

The omitted tail contributes at most `2||s_{>m}||_2`, because both its true value and its conditional average have magnitude at most `||s_{>m}||_2`. Choose

\[
J_i=\max\!\left(J_0(L),\left\lceil\sqrt{2C_Ls_i\sqrt m/\epsilon}\right\rceil\right).
\]

Then `sum_{i≤m}s_i^2/J_i^4≤ε^2/(4C_L^2)`. Retained and tail errors are each at most `ε/2`, so `||Kf_v−f_v||_∞≤ε`; TV is at most `ε/2≤ε`, uniformly over `v`.

The number of labels is at most `D=product_{i≤m}(J_i+1)`, with `m=min(d, ceil((1/b)ln(C_{a,b}/ε)))` for an explicit fixed tail constant (or the first index whose tail norm is at most `ε/4`). Summing the logs gives

\[
\log_2D\le\frac12\sum_{i\le m}[\log_2(s_i/\epsilon)]_+ +O_{a,b,L}(m\log m)
=\frac{\ln^2(a/\epsilon)}{4b\ln2}+O_{a,b,L}(\ln(1/\epsilon)\ln\ln(1/\epsilon)).
\]

All random encoder labels count. The mathematical archive interface assumes access to the conditional reference sampler `μ_j`; its runtime, numerical precision, reference-law acquisition, and finite-bit continuous-output simulation are separate costs and are not established here.

## Lower bound for arbitrary hybrid memories

Fix coordinate `i` and put an equally likely sign `T∈{-1,+1}` on alternatives `P_{±e_i}`. Their input mixture is exactly `μ`, since `E_μ Z_i=0`. Write `X -> Q -> Y` for the fixed encoding/decoding channel under this prior. The same reference coupling `(X,Q,Y)` is used for every `i`; let `M_i=E_μ[(Z_i(X)−Z_i(Y))^2]`.

The target experiment has `E[T Z_i(X)]=s_i E_μ Z_i^2`. Since `|Z_i|≤1`, TV accuracy at most `ε` for both decoded alternatives implies

\[
|E[T Z_i(Y)]-s_iE_\mu Z_i^2|\le2\epsilon,
\qquad |E Z_i(Y)^2-E_\mu Z_i^2|\le2\epsilon.
\]

The posterior is `E[T|X]=s_i Z_i(X)`, so the Markov property gives `E[T Z_i(Y)]=s_i E[Z_i(X)Z_i(Y)]`. Combining the two moment bounds and using `s_i<1` gives

\[
M_i\le4\epsilon/s_i+2\epsilon\le6\epsilon/s_i.
\]

This direct moment-transfer argument does not assume per-seed accuracy. For any finite `I`, let `Zhat_I=Z_I(Y)`. Holevo's dimension bound and data processing give `I(Z_I(X);Zhat_I)≤ln D`. With independent shared randomness `R`, condition on `R` and average; the same bound holds. The conditional maximum-entropy inequality, coordinatewise and without assuming independence, gives

\[
h(Z_I\mid Zhat_I)\le\frac12\sum_{i\in I}\ln(2\pi e M_i).
\]

Together with `h(Z_I)≥−H|I|`, this proves

\[
\ln D\ge\frac12\sum_{i\in I}\ln(s_i/\epsilon)-|I|\big(H+\tfrac12\ln(12\pi e)\big).
\]

Set `K_0=12πe exp(2H)` and take `I={i≤d:s_i≥K_0 ε}`. The displayed converse becomes the exact partial-sum bound

\[
\ln D\ge\frac12\sum_{i\in I}\ln\!\left(\frac{s_i}{K_0\epsilon}\right).
\]

For `s_i=ae^{-bi}` and `d` beyond `b^{-1}ln(a/(K_0 ε))`, summing the arithmetic progression gives `ln D≥ln^2(a/ε)/(4b)−O_{a,b,H}(ln(1/ε))`. If `d` is smaller, the same partial sum truncated at `d` is the claim. This applies to arbitrary hybrid memory because the only dimension inequality is `I(Z_I;Q)≤ln D`; it does not invoke a classical restriction or incompatible coordinate measurements.

## Exclusion of the equal-Fisher shared-scalar example

In the supplied product family, `Z_i(x)=x_i` under product uniform `μ`. Every `k`-coordinate score marginal has density `2^{-k}`, log-gradient zero, and entropy `k ln 2`; it satisfies the condition.

In the shared-scalar family, `Z_i(t)=c cos(2π i t)` for one uniform scalar `t`. For every `k≥2`, the score map `t -> (Z_1(t),...,Z_k(t))` is a `C^1` curve in `R^k`, whose image has `k`-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. Its joint score law is singular and fails the condition. Nevertheless the two families have the same diagonal covariance/Fisher matrix at zero. This identifies the missing information: Fisher orthogonality does not say whether the joint posterior features fill a full-dimensional region.

The condition permits dependence. For example, on `[-1,1]^d` take the cyclic density proportional to `exp(λ sum_i z_i^2 z_{i+1}^2)`, with fixed `0<λ<1` and `z_{d+1}=z_1`. Full log-density coordinate derivatives are at most `4λ`; every marginal inherits the bound by differentiating under the integral. Sign-flip symmetry gives centered, pairwise-uncorrelated coordinates, while cyclic symmetry gives a common variance. Each one-coordinate conditional density has ratio at most `exp(2λ)`, so its variance is at least `exp(-2λ)/3`, uniformly in `d`. This is a nonproduct full-dimensional posterior family admitted by the condition.

The hypothesis is sufficient, not necessary: the shared-scalar example shows only that it is a substantive gate. The theorem does not cover singular posterior manifolds or arbitrary Fisher-orthogonal families.

## Evidence, costs, boundaries, next gate

`joint_posterior_check.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to verify graded-mesh gap and hat-centroid inequalities for every `J=3,...,128` (8,379 hats; maximum scaled centroid displacement `63/16<4`). Its geometric-spectrum rows check arithmetic in the leading `L^2` sums only. This evidence does not certify the asymptotic theorem, an arbitrary joint density, a sampler, finite-bit compilation, or priority. The conditional-log-density estimate, finite-label positive channel, moment lower bound, entropy converse, and asymptotic sums are derived above; no solver or external preprint is used as a proof oracle.

The decisive next gate is to weaken the coordinatewise log-density-gradient hypothesis while retaining the decoder's `O(J_i^{-2})` score bias. A useful class is full-dimensional correlated posterior laws with bounded local interactions. The result is not a general theorem from Fisher spectra and is not an efficient end-to-end compiler.
