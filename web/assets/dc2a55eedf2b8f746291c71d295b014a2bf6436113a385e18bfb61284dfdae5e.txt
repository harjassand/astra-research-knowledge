Dilute bosons to the Gross–Pitaevskii equation
=============================================

Status: SOURCE-RECONSTRUCTED for the stated Erdős–Schlein–Yau theorem and its
cutoff estimate; REFUTED for uniform-in-k convergence through k=N and for any
universal algebraic convergence rate under only the qualitative hypotheses.
This leaf reconstructs selected steps from primary sources. It is not an
independent audit of the complete 83-page proof and makes no novelty claim.

Model, dilution scale, and target observable
--------------------------------------------

The source works with N symmetric bosons in R^3, with system size of order one
and Hamiltonian

  H_N = sum_{i=1}^N (-Delta_i) + sum_{1<=i<j<=N} V_N(x_i-x_j),
  V_N(x) = N^2 V(Nx).

Here V is nonnegative, smooth, radial, and compactly supported in a fixed ball.
The paper also requires its dimensionless strength

  rho = sup_{r>=0} r^2 V(r) + integral_0^infinity r V(r) dr

to be sufficiently small for the coercive estimates used in the proof. The
range of V_N is R/N. If the macroscopic density is order N, the scattering
length is a0/N and the gas parameter is order
N(a0/N)^3 = a0^3 N^-2, which tends to zero.

The unscaled zero-energy scattering solution f0=1-w0 solves

  (-Delta + V/2) f0 = 0,   f0(x) -> 1 as |x| -> infinity,
  a0 = lim_{|x|->infinity} |x| w0(x).

It obeys the exact identity integral V(x) f0(x) dx = 8 pi a0. Scaling gives
f_N(x)=f0(Nx) and scattering length a0/N for V_N. Thus the interaction is
shorter-ranged than the system scale by N, while its many-body strength is
large enough that the full two-body scattering process survives in the limit.

For a normalized symmetric initial wave function psi_N, let psi_N,t solve
i partial_t psi_N,t = H_N psi_N,t. Let gamma_N,t^(k) be the normalized
k-particle reduced density, obtained by tracing out N-k particles. The main
conclusion concerns fixed k and compact observables J^(k) on L^2(R^(3k)):

  Tr J^(k) gamma_N,t^(k) -> Tr J^(k) (|phi_t><phi_t|)^(tensor k).

The convergence is weak* in trace class; since both sides are positive with
trace one and the limit is rank one, it also implies trace-norm convergence
for each fixed k. This is a statement about fixed-particle marginals. It does
not assert convergence of the full N-body state to phi_t^(tensor N).

Initial data and time range
---------------------------

Theorem 2.1 in the Annals paper assumes, for every integer m>=1,

  <psi_N, H_N^m psi_N> <= C^m N^m,

and assumes that for every fixed k the initial marginal converges weak* to
(|phi><phi|)^(tensor k). Theorem 2.2 weakens the energy assumption to
<psi_N,H_N psi_N> <= C N, at the cost of the stronger fixed-k norm
factorization condition

  ||psi_N - phi^(tensor k) tensor xi_N^(N-k)|| -> 0

for every fixed k, with normalized remainders xi_N^(N-k). Product initial
data is an example of the latter condition. In either theorem phi is in H^1
and the limit solves

  i partial_t phi_t = (-Delta + 8 pi a0 |phi_t|^2) phi_t,
  phi_0 = phi.

The theorem holds for every fixed real t. In the proof one fixes arbitrary
finite T, proves compactness and uniqueness on [0,T], and treats negative
times analogously; for each fixed k the convergence is uniform on a compact
time interval in the paper's weak* metric. There is no uniform statement over
all t in R simultaneously.

One load-bearing hierarchy estimate and its costs
-------------------------------------------------

The key regularity bridge is Proposition 5.3 (the H^k energy estimate). Define

  h(x) = exp(-sqrt(|x|^2+ell^2)/ell),
  theta_i(x) = exp(-ell^(-epsilon) sum_{j != i} h(x_i-x_j)),
  theta_i^(n) = theta_i^(2^n),
  Theta_q^(n) = product_{i=1}^q theta_i^(n).

For rho sufficiently small and ell >> N^(-1/2), for every fixed integer k
there is N0=N0(k,C0) such that, for N>=N0 and symmetric psi, the estimate
contains the positive term

  <psi,(H_N+N)^k psi>
    >= C0^k N^k integral Theta_(k-1)^(k) |nabla_1 ... nabla_k psi|^2
       + further nonnegative derivative and interaction terms.

The full inequality also has a second-derivative term weighted by
C0^k N^(k-1) and a potential term weighted by C0^k N^(k+1) V_N(x_k-x_(k+1)).
The displayed first term is enough to see the N and cutoff dependence that
feeds the hierarchy bound. The cutoff is exponentially small when a tracked
particle has a neighbor within distance ell; the exponent contains 2^k
ell^(-epsilon), so constants and the threshold N0 are not uniform in k.

Under the high-moment hypothesis, positivity of H_N and the binomial formula
give

  <psi_N,t,(H_N+N)^k psi_N,t> <= (C+1)^k N^k.

Energy moments are conserved. Proposition 5.3 therefore gives, for each fixed
k and all sufficiently large N depending on k,

  integral Theta_(k-1)^(k) |nabla_1 ... nabla_k psi_N,t|^2 <= C2^k.

To pass this estimate to the limiting hierarchy, the source chooses ell=ell_N
so that

  N ell^2 -> infinity,    N ell^3 -> 0,
  N ell^(3-epsilon) -> 0.

For example, ell=N^(-2/5), epsilon=1/4 gives respectively N^(1/5),
N^(-1/5), and N^(-1/10) for these three expressions. The kinetic localization
cost ell^(-2)/N=(N ell^2)^(-1) is N^(-1/5) in this example; the close-neighbor
region has the N ell^3 scale, and the extra cutoff-gradient loss is controlled
by N ell^(3-epsilon). These conditions yield the limit-point estimate

  Tr (1-Delta_1)...(1-Delta_k) gamma_infinity,t^(k) <= C1^k,

where C1 is independent of k and t on each fixed [0,T]. This is precisely the
regularity class in which the infinite Gross–Pitaevskii hierarchy is unique.
Thus the order of limits matters: first fix k, then send N to infinity. The
source gives N0(k,C0), not a bound uniform for growing k.

The coefficient 8 pi a0 comes from the short-scale correlation. In the
finite BBGKY equation the external-particle term has coefficient N-k and
potential V_N. With the scattering factor f_N,

  N V_N(x) f_N(x) = N^3 V(Nx) f0(Nx)
     -> (integral V(y) f0(y) dy) delta_0
     = 8 pi a0 delta_0

in distributions. This explains why replacing the potential by its bare
integral without tracking correlations gives the wrong coefficient at the
Gross–Pitaevskii scale. The refined two-particle estimate in Proposition 3.1
factors psi_N,t=(1-w_N(x_i-x_j)) phi_ij and controls the derivatives of
phi_ij; Proposition 5.2 records the resulting uniform two-particle bound.
The cutoff estimate above supplies the stronger hierarchy regularity needed
for uniqueness.

Matched scaling baseline
------------------------

For the same V and N-particle setting, the earlier Erdős–Schlein–Yau theorem
(arXiv:math-ph/0508010, Theorem 1.1) treats

  H_(beta,N) = sum_i (-Delta_i)
    + (1/N) sum_(i<j) N^(3 beta) V(N^beta(x_i-x_j)),
  0 < beta < 1/2,

with product initial data phi^(tensor N). The range N^(-beta) is larger than
the scattering length scale N^(-1), and the limiting cubic coefficient is
b0=integral V. At beta=1 the interaction is exactly N^2 V(Nx), its range
matches its scattering length scale, and the full scattering coefficient
8 pi a0 appears. The same Annals paper says its methods extend the Born
coefficient to all beta<1 under the small-rho condition; the beta<1/2
comparison is already an explicit theorem in the earlier primary source.

This comparison holds V, N, and product initial data fixed while changing
only the interaction scaling. It identifies the physical boundary: beta<1
samples the Born approximation; beta=1 retains the full two-body scattering
correlation.

Counterexamples to stronger uniform claims
-------------------------------------------

Choose orthonormal phi,chi in C_c^infinity(R^3), and choose a fixed smooth,
nonzero radial V satisfying the paper's positivity, support, and small-rho
conditions. Set

  phi_N = sqrt(1-epsilon_N^2) phi + epsilon_N chi,
  psi_N = phi_N^(tensor N).

The sequence is symmetric. Uniform H^1 and L-infinity bounds on phi_N imply
<psi_N,H_N psi_N> <= C N: the kinetic energy is O(N), and

  <phi_N tensor phi_N, N^2 V(N(x-y)) phi_N tensor phi_N> = O(N^-1),

so the sum over O(N^2) pairs is also O(N). For each fixed k, choose
xi_N^(N-k)=phi_N^(tensor(N-k)); then
||psi_N - phi^(tensor k) tensor xi_N^(N-k)|| -> 0 whenever epsilon_N->0.
Thus this family satisfies the lower-energy version of the theorem.

1. Uniformity over all 1<=k<=N is false. Take epsilon_N=N^(-1/2). Fixed-k
   marginals converge, but for k=N the trace-norm distance is exactly

     || |psi_N><psi_N| - |phi^(tensor N)><phi^(tensor N)| ||_1
       = 2 sqrt(1-(1-1/N)^N)
       -> 2 sqrt(1-e^(-1)) > 0.

   Hence sup_(1<=k<=N) convergence fails already at t=0.

2. No universal algebraic convergence rate follows from the qualitative
   hypotheses. For the same construction,

     || |phi_N><phi_N| - |phi><phi| ||_1 = 2 epsilon_N.

   Taking epsilon_N=1/log(N+2) gives convergence slower than N^(-delta) for
   every delta>0 while preserving the energy and factorization assumptions.

Falsifier: any assertion that the theorem's assumptions imply a common
O(N^(-delta)) error, or convergence uniformly over all k<=N, is contradicted
at t=0 by the explicit families above. The published conclusion itself,
which fixes k before taking N to infinity and supplies no rate, is unaffected.

Unresolved boundary and restart
-------------------------------

This audit does not independently re-prove every estimate in Proposition 5.3
or the full convergence of the finite BBGKY hierarchy to its singular limit.
It reconstructs the stated estimate, its cutoff window, its energy-moment
consequence, and the matched scattering rescaling. The constants C0, C1, C2,
and N0 depend on the source's potential/coercivity setup; N0 also depends on
k. No rate is claimed for the nonlinear convergence under arbitrary initial
data allowed by Theorem 2.2.

For continuation, inspect the full Section 9 proof of Proposition 5.3 and
Section 7 passage to the limiting hierarchy side by side with the cited
Theorem 9.1 uniqueness proof. A useful next target is to extract an explicit
error bound under a supplied quantitative initial condensation modulus,
while retaining the source's cutoff requirements and fixed-k quantifiers.
