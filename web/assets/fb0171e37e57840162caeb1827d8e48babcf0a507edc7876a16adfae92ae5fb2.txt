1. MODEL AND TWO EXPLICIT INTERACTION SCALINGS

Let L=N+1 and use exactly the open spin chain, sine transform, embedding, and
parameters of ../proof.txt. In particular, J,tau>0 are fixed,
lambda in [-B,B], kappa in [-K,K], beta=tau L^2, and the anisotropy is
gamma=kappa/L. Write H_L^0(lambda,kappa) for the prior quadratic XY
Hamiltonian, rho_L^0=exp(-beta H_L^0)/Z_L^0, and rho_infinity(lambda,kappa)
for its normal Fock-space limit in the parameter-blind sine-mode embedding.

On the N=L-1 physical sites define

    n_i=(1-Z_i)/2=c_i^* c_i,
    W_L=sum_{i=1}^{N-1} n_i n_{i+1}.

In spins, n_i n_{i+1}=(1-Z_i-Z_{i+1}+Z_i Z_{i+1})/4. In Jordan-Wigner
fermions it contains a genuine quartic term and is not a quadratic BdG
perturbation. It is a finite-range lattice contact regulator; the strictly
on-site spinless term (c_i^*)^2 c_i^2 is identically zero.

The primary anisotropic family keeps the full compact XY parameter set and
adds a positive local interaction with physical coefficient g/L^3:

    H_L^g=H_L^0+(g/L^3)W_L,       0<=g<=G,
    G_L^g=beta H_L^g=G_L^0+V_L,
    V_L=(tau g/L)W_L,
    rho_L^g=exp(-G_L^g)/Tr exp(-G_L^g).

Here G<infinity is public. W_L is positive and W_L<=N-1<L, so
||V_L||<=tau G. This worst-case operator norm does not tend to zero; the
state-level result below uses the proved low-particle tail instead.

A sharper isotropic subfamily sets kappa=0 and uses the distinguishable
contact-critical physical coefficient u/L:

    H_L^u=H_L^0(lambda,0)+(u/L)W_L,       0<=u<=U,
    beta(H_L^u-H_L^0)=tau u L W_L.

For two distinguishable low-momentum particles, a contact probability is of
order L^-1; beta=tau L^2 therefore makes u/L the naive nontrivial contact
scale. For one spinless species, antisymmetry suppresses the adjacent-pair
probability to order L^-3. The exact determinant estimate in Section 4 proves
that cancellation and shows this u/L interaction vanishes in the thermal
experiment. This does not classify every quartic operator by RG, and it does
not identify a nonzero p-wave continuum interaction.

2. A FINITE-MATRIX GIBBS PERTURBATION LEMMA

For finite-dimensional self-adjoint G and V>=0, let rho_0=e^-G/Z_0 and
rho_1=e^-(G+V)/Z_1. Then

    D(rho_0||rho_1)=Tr(rho_0 V)+log(Z_1/Z_0)<=Tr(rho_0 V).       (2.1)

Indeed log rho_0=-G-log Z_0 and log rho_1=-(G+V)-log Z_1. Since G+V>=G,
the min-max eigenvalue principle and monotonicity of x->e^-x give Z_1<=Z_0.
Quantum Pinsker, with natural logarithms and full trace norm, gives

    ||rho_1-rho_0||_1 <= sqrt(2 D(rho_0||rho_1)).              (2.2)

This version follows by measuring in a Helstrom basis, applying relative
entropy data processing, and then classical Pinsker. Thus a positive
interaction is controlled by its expectation in the free Gibbs state; no
operator-norm smallness or perturbative expansion of an unbounded limiting
operator is used.

3. FULL ANISOTROPIC XY FAMILY: UNIFORM TRACE LIMIT

The prior proof establishes, uniformly for lambda,kappa in their public
compact sets, the free finite-mode Gaussian number-generating bound

    Tr rho_L^0 exp(s Npart) <= exp[mu_* (exp(2s)-1)],
    mu_*=k0+(2/3)pi^4 K^2+sum_{k>=1} exp(-2 tau J k^2),

where k0=max(1,ceil(sqrt(B/(4J)))). Take s=1/2 and use
x^2<=4 exp(x/2), x>=0. Since in every occupation configuration

    0<=W_L<=Npart(Npart-1)/2<=Npart^2/2,

we get the explicit uniform constant

    Tr rho_L^0 W_L <= C_W := 2 exp[mu_*(e-1)].                (3.1)

Apply (2.1)-(2.2) with V=V_L=(tau g/L)W_L. For every L and every allowed
lambda,kappa,g,

    ||rho_L^g-rho_L^0||_1 <= sqrt(2 tau G C_W/L).             (3.2)

The scaled interaction has worst-case norm O(1), but (3.2) tends to zero
because the prior Gaussian state has a uniform second particle-number moment.
No Pauli cancellation is needed for this weaker, anisotropy-uniform scaling.

Let

    eps_L^0=sup_{|lambda|<=B,|kappa|<=K}
             ||rho_L^0(lambda,kappa)-rho_infinity(lambda,kappa)||_1.

The prior theorem proves eps_L^0->0, uniformly on these compact parameter
sets (without an explicit finite-L core-convergence rate). The triangle
inequality and (3.2) therefore prove

    sup_{lambda,kappa,g} ||rho_L^g-rho_infinity(lambda,kappa)||_1
       <= eps_L^0 + sqrt(2 tau G C_W/L)  --> 0.               (3.3)

This is convergence of the whole normalized state in the common original
vacuum-Fock embedding, not merely convergence of fixed correlation functions
or Hilbert-Schmidt kernels. It is a free limiting state with a controlled
weak quartic perturbation, not an interacting continuum state.

The same estimate gives a proved tail for the interacting states. Let P_M
project onto vacuum above sine mode M, and let P_{M,r} additionally require at
most r particles in the first M modes. Write T_M for the prior high-mode
occupation bound (equation (2.4) in ../proof.txt), and

    S_r=exp[-(r/2)log(r/(2e mu_*))-mu_*],  r>=2 mu_*,

for its Chernoff number tail. Since a projection expectation changes by at
most the trace norm,

    Tr rho_L^g (I-P_M) <= T_M + delta_L,
    Tr rho_L^g (I-P_{M,r}) <= T_M+S_r+delta_L,
    delta_L=sqrt(2 tau G C_W/L).                              (3.4)

This carries the global topology through the interaction; matrix-element
convergence alone would not imply (3.3) or (3.4).

4. SPINLESS PAULI CANCELLATION AND THE FULL SUBCRITICAL RANGE

Now restrict to kappa=0. The sine-mode Gibbs state is a number-conserving
Slater thermal state with independent mode occupations

    p_Lk=(1+exp(D_Lk-tau lambda))^-1,
    D_Lk=4 tau J L^2(1-cos(pi k/L)) >=8 tau J k^2.

Hence p_Lk<=exp(tau B)exp(-8 tau J k^2). Define finite, explicit sums

    S0=exp(tau B)sum_{k>=1} exp(-8 tau J k^2),
    S2=exp(tau B)sum_{k>=1} k^2 exp(-8 tau J k^2),
    C_P=4 pi^2 S0 S2.

For each site i let u_i=(sqrt(p_Lk) phi_k(i))_{k=1}^N, with
phi_k(i)=sqrt(2/L)sin(pi k i/L). Wick's rule gives

    Tr rho_L^0 n_i n_{i+1}
      =Gamma_ii Gamma_{i+1,i+1}-|Gamma_{i,i+1}|^2
      =||u_i wedge u_{i+1}||^2,
    Gamma_ij=sum_k p_Lk phi_k(i)phi_k(j).

Because u_i wedge u_i=0, this equals ||u_i wedge(u_{i+1}-u_i)||^2 and is
bounded by ||u_i||^2||u_{i+1}-u_i||^2. The sine bounds give, for every i,

    ||u_i||^2 <=2 S0/L,
    ||u_{i+1}-u_i||^2 <=2 pi^2 S2/L^3,
    Tr rho_L^0 W_L <= C_P/L^3.                               (4.1)

The last L^-3 is the exact finite-chain Pauli cancellation: a one-spinless-
species on-site quartic vanishes, while the first nonzero adjacent-site
regularization samples the antisymmetric wavefunction at separation 1/L.
No continuum contact formula is assumed.

For the physical interaction u W_L/L, the scaled perturbation is
V_L=tau u L W_L. Combining (2.1), (2.2), and (4.1) yields

    sup_{|lambda|<=B,0<=u<=U} ||rho_L^u-rho_L^0(lambda,0)||_1
       <= sqrt(2 tau U C_P)/L.                               (4.2)

More generally, let the physical coefficient be any public-bounded sequence
0<=a_L<=A_L, while retaining kappa=0. The scaled perturbation is
V_L=tau L^2 a_L W_L, so the same calculation gives

    sup_{|lambda|<=B,0<=a_L<=A_L} ||rho_L^{a_L}-rho_L^0||_1
       <= sqrt(2 tau C_P A_L/L).                            (4.3)

Consequently every nonnegative a_L=o(L) has the same whole-Fock trace limit
as the free XX chain. This includes fixed unscaled repulsion (rate
O(L^-1/2)) and a_L=L^alpha for every fixed alpha<1. The original u/L case
is the special rate O(L^-1). This conclusion uses the exact antisymmetric
nearest-neighbor determinant, not a contact-interaction heuristic.

For fixed low-mode two-particle Slater states, (4.1) shows that a physical
nearest-neighbor coefficient of order L is the first scale at which the
Born matrix element can remain order one after beta=tau L^2. The next
section proves that this Born observation does not establish a nonzero
repulsive continuum interaction: exact scattering saturates at a one-site
hard rod, whose shift vanishes in scaled coordinates.

The finite-size Gibbs state is genuinely non-Gaussian when u>0. For N=2,
the two one-particle sine modes have energies eps_1,eps_2, and the full
occupation has energy eps_1+eps_2+u/L. Its four diagonal Gibbs weights
satisfy

    p_11 p_00/(p_10 p_01)=exp(-beta u/L)=exp(-tau u L) !=1.

A gauge-invariant quasi-free state diagonal in these modes must have this
ratio equal to 1. Thus the quartic family is not a disguised free-parameter
shift even though its trace limit is free.

5. RENORMALIZATION AND FINITE-ENERGY COST

For the anisotropy-uniform g/L^3 family, use the same scalar ground-energy
subtraction E0_L^0 from the prior proof. Positivity gives

    0 <= E0_L^g-E0_L^0 <= <psi0_L,V_L psi0_L> <= tau G/L * C_gs,

where psi0_L is a free pure Gaussian ground state and one may take

    mu_gs,*=k0+pi^2 K^2/k0,
    C_gs=2 exp[mu_gs,*(e-1)].

The prior ground-polarization estimate Qground,kk<=pi^2 K^2/k^2 above k0
implies E_psi0 Npart<=mu_gs,*. Applying the same Gaussian number-generating
bound and W_L<=Npart^2/2 gives the displayed C_gs. Therefore the interaction
does not alter or add to the prior linear scaled UV subtraction; its
renormalized ground-energy correction is O(L^-1), uniformly on compact
parameters. In physical, unscaled energy this correction is O(L^-3).

For any isotropic coefficient 0<=a_L<=A_L, the free ground state fills
only boundedly many low sine modes uniformly in lambda. The determinant
proof in Section 4 applies with p_Lk in {0,1}; it gives

    0<=E0_L^{a_L}-E0_L^0<=tau C_P,gs A_L/L,

where C_P,gs=4 pi^2 S0,gs S2,gs, S0,gs<=k0 and
S2,gs<=sum_{k<=k0}k^2. Thus the same scalar subtraction works and for
A_L=o(L) the scaled ground shift vanishes; for the contact-critical
a_L<=U L it stays O(1), hence the physical shift is O(L^-2). The thermal
trace result does not depend on choosing a continuous ground-state
polarization at a zero mode.

Subtracting a scalar leaves each normalized Gibbs state unchanged. Neither
case proves convergence of the unrenormalized second-quantized Hamiltonian
on the vacuum finite-particle core; the prior pair-creation coefficient is
not Hilbert-Schmidt. The claim here is about renormalized scalar energies and
normal density operators.

6. PARAMETER-BLIND QUANTUM ARCHIVE

Use the prior public archive channel: sine-transform the N spins into the
common Fock modes, keep the first M modes and total particle number at most r,
and map overflow to an orthogonal flag. It reads no lambda, kappa, or g/u.
Its decoded free-state error can be set to eps0 using the prior T_M and S_r
certificates, with

    q_free(eps0)=ceil(log2(1+sum_{j=0}^{min(r,M)} binom(M,j)))
       =O(log^2(1/eps0)/loglog(1/eps0))

for fixed B,K,J,tau and nonzero K, as proved in ../proof.txt.

For any CPTP archive/decode map C with decoder R, contractivity gives

    ||R C(rho_L^int)-rho_L^int||_1
       <=2||rho_L^int-rho_L^0||_1
          +||R C(rho_L^0)-rho_L^0||_1.                       (6.1)

Set eps0=eps/2. For the anisotropy-uniform g/L^3 family, if

    L >= L0^aniso := ceil(32 tau G C_W / eps^2),

then 2 delta_L<=eps/2 and (6.1) is at most eps. For L<L0^aniso retain the
whole finite chain exactly, using at most N<L0^aniso qubits. Thus one
parameter-blind archive works for every N and every unknown lambda,kappa,g,
with persistent memory

    q_aniso(eps) <= max{L0^aniso,q_free(eps/2)} = O(eps^-2)

at fixed public constants. This is an explicit N-independent bound, not an
optimal-memory claim; its inverse-square tolerance dependence is dominated
by the exact-storage patch for small chains.

For kappa=0 and the stronger u/L scaling, if

    L >= L0^XX := ceil(sqrt(32 tau U C_P)/eps),

then 2 delta_L<=eps/2. Exact retention below that threshold gives

    q_XX(eps) <= max{L0^XX,q_free,XX(eps/2)} = O(eps^-1)

at fixed constants. This too is only an N-independent upper bound. The large-
N compressed archive retains the free theorem's polylogarithmic memory; no
claim is made that the epsilon-dependent exact small-chain patch is optimal.

The persistent quantum register, overflow flag, encoding workspace, and N
output spins are charged separately as in the prior proof. A direct sine

7. PARAMETER AND CUTOFF PRECISION

The archive is parameter-blind, so it neither measures nor estimates
lambda,kappa,g/u. Public B,K,G/U suffice. M,r and L0 are chosen from outward-
rounded inequalities: use the explicit T_M and S_r above, upper bounds for
the convergent Gaussian sums, and rational ceilings. A finite algorithm
increases M,r until interval arithmetic certifies both tail bounds. Its
precision is logarithmic in the target inverse error and public constants;
no uncharged acquisition of the unknown parameter occurs.

If the Hamiltonian coefficients themselves are physically rounded, a
conservative finite-dimensional Gibbs Lipschitz bound is
||rho(G+Delta G)-rho(G)||_1<=2||Delta G||. For the g/L^3 coefficient,
||Delta G||<=tau|Delta g|, so p>=ceil(log2(16 tau/eps)) fractional bits
for g suffice for an eps/8 state-error budget. For the u/L XX scaling,
||Delta G||<=tau L^2 |Delta u|, requiring
p>=ceil(log2(16 tau L^2/eps)) bits for u under the same budget. These are
worst-case model-synthesis costs; archive memory remains independent of
unknown g/u. For a physical coefficient a_L multiplying W_L,
||Delta G||<=tau L^3 |Delta a_L|. If a_L=eta L, this becomes
||Delta G||<=tau L^4 |Delta eta| and p>=ceil(log2(16 tau L^4/eps)) bits
for eta suffice for the same eps/8 synthesis budget. The all-repulsive
archive in Section 9 does not acquire a_L or eta; positivity alone gives
its tail bounds. Errors in lambda and kappa likewise require L-dependent
precision if the physical Hamiltonian is being programmed; they are not
acquired by the archive and are not silently treated as known exactly.

The O(N^2) transform and reversible archive arithmetic are finite algorithms
once the mode table and constants are supplied. Classical table storage,
ancillas, input preparation, thermal-state production, and output cost are
not counted as persistent archive memory.

9. REPULSIVE COEFFICIENTS AT AND ABOVE L: HARD-ROD SQUEEZE

The O(1) Born matrix element at a_L=eta L is not the exact low-energy
scattering response. Work first on the infinite chain in the two-particle
sector, with ordered positions x<y, separation r=y-x>=1, total momentum K,
relative momentum q, and hopping -2J. After removing the common field, the
relative recurrence has hopping amplitude A=4J cos(K/2):

    E phi(r)=-A[phi(r+1)+phi(r-1)]       (r>=2),
    E phi(1)=a_L phi(1)-A phi(2).        (9.1)

Substitute phi(r)=sin(qr+delta) and E=-2A cos(q). The boundary equation
gives exactly

    tan(delta)=-a_L sin(q)/(A+a_L cos(q)),
    ell_sc(K,a_L)=a_L/(A+a_L),
    delta=-ell_sc q+O(q^3)               (q->0).             (9.2)

For repulsion a_L>=0 and low center-of-mass momentum, ell_sc is between
zero and one lattice spacing and tends to one as a_L->infinity. At
a_L=eta L and q=Q/L, delta=-Q/L+o(L^-1); the physical-coordinate shift
ell_sc/L vanishes. A physical coefficient growing like L therefore does
not leave a finite repulsive scattering length in the continuum.

The open-chain hard-rod endpoint is exact in every fixed particle sector.
There are N=L-1 physical sites and ordered configurations
1<=x_1<...<x_m<=N. The zero-adjacency subspace has x_{j+1}>=x_j+2.
The coordinate map

    y_j=x_j-(j-1)

is an isometry from that subspace onto m-fermion configurations on
N'=N-m+1=L-m sites. It intertwines the compressed hopping with ordinary
open-chain hopping on those N' sites. Thus the hard-rod mode denominator
is N'+1=L-m+1; for m=2 it is L-1, whereas the free denominator is L.
The endpoint discrepancy is O(m/L) for fixed m and disappears after the
continuum rescaling. This convention keeps L=N+1 throughout; using L for
the site count instead shifts each denominator by one.

Here is a whole-state Gibbs theorem, not just a two-body statement. Let
z^{a_L}_{L,m} be the trace of exp[-tau L^2(H^0_L(0,0)+a_L W_L)] on the
m-particle sector; this omits the detuning field. Let z^0_{L,m} be its
free value and z^hc_{L,m} the hard-rod sum. Explicitly,

    z^0_{L,m}=sum_{1<=k_1<...<k_m<=L-1}
       exp[-sum_j 4 tau J L^2(1-cos(pi k_j/L))],

    z^hc_{L,m}=sum_{1<=k_1<...<k_m<=L-m}
       exp[-sum_j 4 tau J L^2(1-cos(pi k_j/(L-m+1)))].      (9.3)

The second sum is zero when the hard-rod subspace is empty. Min-max in
the m-particle sector gives, for every a_L>=0,

    z^0_{L,m} >= z^{a_L}_{L,m} >= z^hc_{L,m}.               (9.4)

Indeed the interaction a_L W_L is positive, and restricting the Rayleigh-
Ritz minimization to ker W_L gives the eigenvalue comparison with the
compressed hard-rod operator. For each fixed m, both endpoints in (9.3)
converge by dominated convergence to

    z_m^*=sum_{1<=k_1<...<k_m<infinity}
       exp[-2 tau J pi^2 sum_j k_j^2].                      (9.5)

The domination is explicit: both one-particle exponents are at least
8 tau J k^2, since L-m+1<=L. The common field multiplies each sector
partition by exp(tau lambda m), uniformly for |lambda|<=B at fixed m;
thus Z^{a_L}_L(lambda)=sum_m exp(tau lambda m)z^{a_L}_{L,m}, and likewise
for the free and hard-rod partition functions.

Let Z^0_L(lambda) and Z^{a_L}_L(lambda) be the full grand partition
functions. The free number Chernoff tail S_r from (3.4) and the uniform
free partition bound

    Z^0_L(lambda)<=C_Z:=exp[exp(tau B) sum_{k>=1}exp(-8 tau J k^2)]

give, for every integer r>=0,

    0<=Z^0_L-Z^{a_L}_L
      <= sum_{m=0}^r exp(tau B m)(z^0_{L,m}-z^hc_{L,m})+C_Z S_r.
                                                                    (9.6)

For fixed r the finite sum tends to zero by (9.5); then S_r->0. Hence
Z^0_L-Z^{a_L}_L->0 uniformly in |lambda|<=B and over every sequence of
repulsive coefficients a_L>=0, including fixed a, a_L=eta L, and
a_L->infinity. The vacuum has weight one, so Z^{a_L}_L>=1. In the finite
chain,

    D(rho_L^{a_L}||rho_L^0)
      =-Tr(rho_L^{a_L} V_L)+log(Z^0_L/Z^{a_L}_L)
      <= Z^0_L-Z^{a_L}_L.                                  (9.7)

Pinsker and (9.6) therefore prove uniform full trace-norm convergence
rho_L^{a_L}-rho_L^0->0 for all nonnegative nearest-neighbor couplings.
Combining with the prior XX theorem proves the whole-Fock limit to the
free continuum Gibbs state in the same parameter-blind embedding. This is
an internal proof conditional on the prior free trace-limit theorem.

The low-mode Slater calculation is a falsifier of naive perturbation
theory. For modes m<n, the adjacent-site determinant gives

    L^3 <m,n|W_L|m,n> -> integral_0^1
       (phi_m phi'_n-phi_n phi'_m)^2 dx
       =pi^2(m^2+n^2).                                     (9.8)

Thus the first-order scaled energy for a_L=eta L tends to
tau eta pi^2(m^2+n^2), equal to 5 tau eta pi^2 for (m,n)=(1,2).
Yet (9.7) proves that the normalized full Gibbs state has the free limit.
The perturbation series is nonuniform at this scale; (9.2)-(9.4) show
the physical reason: the repulsive node shift is at most one microscopic
site, and the exact many-body hard-rod squeeze changes continuum
coordinates by at most m/L in a fixed m sector. No nonzero repulsive
continuum contact parameter follows from the Born term.

There is an explicit parameter-blind archive for all a_L>=0, not only
the asymptotically trace-close regime. Since G^a_L commutes with total
number, sectorwise min-max gives
Tr rho^a_L 1_{Npart>r}<=C_Z S_r. To control high sine modes, set
C0=tau B k0 and

    C_half=exp[exp(tau B/2) sum_{k>=1}exp(-4 tau J k^2)],
    C_E=(2/e)exp(C0/2) C_half.

The operator inequality G^a_L+C0>=4 tau J sum_{k>=k0} k^2 n_k follows
from D_Lk>=8 tau J k^2 and V_L>=0. Also G^a_L+C0>=0, Z^a_L>=1, and
min-max gives Tr exp(-G^a_L/2)<=Tr exp(-G^0_L/2)<=C_half. If mu_j are
the eigenvalues of G^a_L, then x exp(-x)<=(2/e)exp(-x/2) for x>=0;
therefore

    Tr rho^a_L(G^a_L+C0)<=C_E,
    Tr rho^a_L sum_{k>M} n_k<=C_E/[4 tau J (M+1)^2]          (9.9)

for M>=k0. Combining the two tails, the archive retaining modes <=M
and at most r total particles has overflow probability

    p_bad<=C_E/[4 tau J (M+1)^2]+C_Z S_r.

Its decoded trace error is at most 2 sqrt(p_bad)+p_bad. For 0<epsilon<1,
choose M so that C_E/[4 tau J (M+1)^2]<=epsilon^2/18, and increase r
until C_Z S_r<=epsilon^2/18 using outward-rounded Gaussian-sum bounds.
Then the error is below epsilon, and

    q=ceil(log2(1+sum_{j=0}^{min(M,r)} binom(M,j)))
      =O(log^2(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon))

persistent qubits, uniformly in chain length, lambda, and every unknown
nonnegative coupling a_L. The interaction is not acquired; only positivity
and the public bound B enter. The transform, reversible ranking, overflow
flag, workspace, N-spin output, and parameter-synthesis precision have the
same separate charges as in Section 6-7; no thermal-state preparation is
claimed.

10. ATTRACTIVE RESONANCE AND ITS GATES

The two-body denominator in (9.2) becomes singular near a_L=-4J. The
distinct resonance scaling is

    a_L=-4J+g/L,
    G_L=G^0_L+tau L^2(-4J+g/L)W_L,

not a repulsive coefficient growing as +L. The exact K=0 bound-state
condition is |a_L|>4J; for g<0 the shallow pair has

    E_b=-g^2/[L^2(4J+|g|/L)]
       =-g^2/(4J L^2)+O(L^-3),                              (10.1)

so it survives the beta=tau L^2 scaling. On the g>0 side there is no
low-momentum two-body bound state; the candidate fixed-particle continuum
is a repulsive Lieb-Liniger gas after the fermion-boson duality. More
precisely, with q=Q/L the relative phase has
tan(delta)->4JQ/g, so the continuum relative wave u satisfies
u'(0)=g u(0)/(4J). The corresponding symmetric-boson operator is

    -2J sum_i partial_{x_i}^2 + 2g sum_{i<j} delta(x_i-x_j)

on the unit interval with the appropriate open-end boundary condition.
This two-body/fixed-particle limit is not a whole grand-canonical trace
theorem.

There is a sharp open-chain obstruction if a parameter interval includes
g<0. At lambda=0 and a_L=-4J-a/L (a>0), a slowly varying spin-coherent
trial state proves the obstruction directly. Write N=L-1 and

    H_crit=J sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(I-sigma_i dot sigma_{i+1})
            +J(I-sigma_1^z)+J(I-sigma_N^z)>=0.

Take a product of spin-coherent states with polar angles theta_i=0 at the
endpoints, theta_i=pi/2 on a central interval of length at least N/3, and
linear ramps of width proportional to N. Then
<H_crit>=O(J/L), while <W_L>=sum_i sin^2(theta_i/2)
sin^2(theta_{i+1}/2)>=L/12-O(1). Hence for
a_L=-4J-a/L,

    <H_L>=<H_crit>-(a/L)<W_L>-(lambda/L^2)<Npart>
          <=-a/12+O((J+a+B)/L).

The product state's measured Npart is a sum of independent Bernoulli
variables with mean at least L/6-O(1), so Hoeffding gives probability
1-exp(-c'L) to the event Npart>=L/12. Since ||H_L||=O(L), conditioning
onto this event changes the energy expectation by only o(1). For all large
L it remains at most -a/48. Because H_L commutes with Npart, some sector
m>=L/12 then has ground energy at most -a/48; its Gibbs weight is at least
exp(tau a L^2/48). For m<=epsilon L, H_crit>=0 and W_L<=m imply
H_L>=-a epsilon-B epsilon/L. The full low-number partition is therefore
at most exp[L log 2+tau a epsilon L^2+tau B epsilon L]. Taking
epsilon=1/96 proves that the low-particle tail fails uniformly. This
counterexample uses the open-chain convention and lambda/L^2 detuning; it
does not rely on periodic Dicke states.

At g=0, the open-chain one-particle threshold is
L^2 epsilon_1(L)->2J pi^2. To see the grand-canonical obstruction when
lambda>2J pi^2, use the small-angle product profile
theta_i=epsilon sin(pi i/L). Its expectations satisfy

    <Npart>=epsilon^2 L/8+o(L),
    <H_crit>=J epsilon^2 pi^2/(4L)+o(L^-1),
    log Z_L>=tau epsilon^2 L(lambda-2J pi^2)/8+o(L).

The last line follows from Jensen applied to exp(-tau L^2 H_L). For
Npart<=eta L, positivity of H_crit bounds the restricted partition by
exp[L h(eta)+tau lambda eta L+o(L)], where h is binary entropy.
Choose eta>0 small enough that h(eta)+tau lambda eta is below the positive
coefficient in the preceding display. The low-number probability then
tends to zero. Thus a compact lambda interval containing a value above
2J pi^2 fails uniform tails at g=0. Below or at threshold the uniform-tail
question still needs proof. For a compact repulsive-side interval
g in [g_-,g_+] with g_->0 and lambda in [-B,B], no obstruction was found,
but no uniform-in-particle-number heat-trace bound was proved. There is an
exact configuration-space reduction that isolates the gap. Let S=L-1 be the
number of physical sites and m the particle number. In the occupation basis
X subset {1,...,S}, let X~X' mean that one particle hops by one site into an
empty site, and let R(X) be the number of occupied runs. The critical form is

    <psi,Hcrit psi>
      =2J sum_{unordered X~X'} |psi(X)-psi(X')|^2
        +2J sum_X (1_{1 in X}+1_{S in X}) |psi(X)|^2,

so it is exactly a killed symmetric-exclusion-process graph Laplacian and is
nonnegative. Also W(X)=m-R(X). Since
R(X)<=min(m,S-m+1),

    W(X)>= (2m-S-1)_+ = (2m-L)_+.

For g>=g_->0 this gives the rigorous canonical estimate

    z_{L,m}(g)
      :=Tr_m exp[-tau L^2(Hcrit+(g/L)W)]
      <= binom(L-1,m) exp[-tau g_- L (2m-L)_+].             (10.2)

For any integer k0 with

    2 tau g_- k0 > tau B + log(2),

the total grand-canonical weight of sectors m>=L/2+k0 is at most

    L exp[-(2 tau g_- k0-tau B-log(2))L].

Indeed W>=(2m-L)_+>=2k0 there, binom(L-1,m)<=2^(L-1), and
exp(tau lambda m)<=exp(tau B L). This is stronger than suppressing each
fixed-density region above one half, but it still leaves m<=L/2 and the
fixed-width band just above L/2 uncontrolled. The entropy factor there
remains exponential in L, so (10.2) is not a number-tail bound.

The compact detuning assumption is essential to the stated question. If it is
removed, set lambda_L=aL^2 with a>4J+g_++eta. In the all-down sector
m=L-1, Hcrit has energy 4J, W=L-2, and the chemical term is -a(L-1), so

    E_{L,L-1}=4J+g(L-2)/L-a(L-1).

For every m<L-1, positivity gives E_{L,m}>=-am>=-a(L-2). The total weight
of all those sectors divided by the all-down state's weight is at most

    2^(L-1) exp{-tau L^2 [a-4J-g_+(L-2)/L]},

which tends to zero. Thus lambda_L outside every fixed compact window makes
the particle number concentrate at L-1. This does not apply under the
inherited |lambda|<=B condition.

The continuum candidate suggests the missing estimate. For the repulsive
Lieb-Liniger form on the unit interval,

    q_m=2J sum_i integral |partial_i psi|^2
          +2g sum_{i<j} integral_{x_i=x_j} |psi|^2,

the expected fixed-g ground-energy coercivity is E_m>=c(J,g_-)m^2. Together
with q_m>=T_m, the elementary operator inequality
q_m>=(q_m+T_m)/2>=(E_m+T_m)/2 would give

    Tr_m exp(-tau q_m)
       <= exp[-tau c(J,g_-)m^2/2]
          [Tr_1 exp(-tau h_1/2)]^m,

which is summable after multiplication by exp(tau lambda m). This is a
continuum argument only. Fixed-m convergence of the lattice operators to
q_m does not make its coercivity or heat-trace constants uniform when m
grows with L. The precise unresolved lattice target is a summable canonical
bound, for example constants A,c>0 independent of L,m and
g in [g_-,g_+] such that

    z_{L,m}(g)<=A^m exp(-c m^2),       0<=m<=L-1.           (10.3)

Any suitable bound yielding a grand-partition estimate uniformly on a
slightly enlarged lambda interval would suffice instead. Once such a bound
is established, Chernoff gives tight particle tails; an additional uniform
high-mode energy estimate is still needed for whole-Fock trace convergence
and an archive. A sector ground-energy lower bound alone is insufficient:
the crude replacement z_{L,m}<=binom(L-1,m)exp(-tau L^2 E_{L,m}), where
E_{L,m} is the physical sector ground energy, can lose for m=a log L. If
E_{L,m}>=c m^2/L^2, then log binom(L-1,m) is asymptotic to a(log L)^2,
while the scaled ground-energy contribution is only tau c a^2(log L)^2.
The proof must control the canonical heat trace or eigenvalue counting, not
just its lowest eigenvalue. No positive-g counterexample under the compact
detuning window or proof of (10.3) was found.
The exact continuum and thermodynamic Bethe-ansatz literature does not by
itself supply this finite-L, all-m estimate.

11. PRIMARY DEPENDENCIES AND SOURCE SCOPE

All XY-specific free formulas, uniform trace convergence, Gaussian number
MGF, UV subtraction and base archive are from ../proof.txt, especially
Sections 2, 5, 6 and 7; no new claim is made that those prior steps are
externally verified. The separate entanglement-breaking floor in
../eb_obstruction.txt is not used. New internal inputs are finite-matrix
min-max, the exact hard-rod coordinate map, the two-body recurrence,
finite-dimensional relative entropy/Pinsker, Wick's rule, and the sine
difference estimate; each use is shown above.

Primary-source scope checked (priority remains UNKNOWN): Muth, Fleischhauer
and Schmidt, “Discretized versus continuous models of p-wave interacting
fermions in one dimension,” Phys. Rev. A 82, 013602 (2010),
https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4099, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.82.013602, derive a
lattice/continuum mapping near the XXZ critical point and compare ground-
state observables; they do not prove the present finite-volume full Gibbs
