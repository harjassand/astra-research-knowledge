1. Exact interface

Let S=L-1, with sites 1,...,S, and retain both endpoint fields (both act on
the same site when S=1):

 Hcrit = J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-X_iX_{i+1}-Y_iY_{i+1}-Z_iZ_{i+1})
         + J(I-Z_1)+J(I-Z_S),
 N=sum_i (I-Z_i)/2,   W=sum_{i=1}^{S-1} n_i n_{i+1},
 A_L=L^2 Hcrit,
 B_L=A_L-kappa W-lambda_c N,
 lambda_c=2J pi^2,   kappa_t=lambda_c/3,   beta=tau L^2.

Here kappa is fixed in [0,kappa_t], tau,J>0, and the finite-chain Gibbs
state is exp(-tau B_L)/Xi_L, Xi_L=Tr exp(-tau B_L). Every trace below is
over all spin states unless a sector is explicitly named.

2. What the coherent Landau calculation predicts

For the sine profile theta(x)=a sin(pi x), rho=sin^2(theta/2), the
coherent functional has

 E_{kappa,lambda_c}(theta)
   = [(lambda_c-3 kappa)/128] a^4 + O(a^6),  kappa<kappa_t,
 E_{kappa_t,lambda_c}(theta)
   = [lambda_c/1152] a^6 + O(a^8).

Its mean occupation is m approximately L integral rho = L a^2/8. Thus the
coherent trial cost in B_L, which is L E, is approximately

 64 c_4 m^2/L,  c_4=(lambda_c-3 kappa)/128,   kappa<kappa_t,
 (4 lambda_c/9) m^3/L^2,                        kappa=kappa_t.

Balancing these trial costs at order one suggests r_L=sqrt(L) below the
threshold and r_L=L^(2/3) at it. This is a variational expectation in a
number-indefinite coherent state. It is not a uniform fixed-sector
eigenvalue expansion, and it says nothing by itself about the number of
near-ground states in each sector.

3. Sector ground energy is not sector heat trace

Write B_{L,m} for the restriction to N=m and let E_{L,m}=inf spec B_{L,m}.
The exact identity is

 Z_{L,m}:=Tr_{N=m} exp(-tau B_L)
       =exp(-tau E_{L,m}) R_{L,m},
 R_{L,m}:=Tr_{N=m} exp[-tau(B_L-E_{L,m})],
 1 <= R_{L,m} <= binom(S,m).

The factor R_{L,m} includes every excited state and the full spin-sector
multiplicity. A ground-energy Landau expansion cannot replace an estimate
of R_{L,m}. The dimension bound is generally too large at mesoscopic m.
Also W<=N gives the exact sector min-max comparison

 B_{L,m} >= A_{L,m}-(kappa+lambda_c)m,
 Z_{L,m} <= exp[tau(kappa+lambda_c)m] Tr_{N=m}exp(-tau A_L).

The supplied full critical trace T_L(t)=Tr exp(-t A_L), which includes all
2^S spin states and obeys log T_L(t)=O_t((log L)^(3/2)), therefore yields
only Z_{L,m}<=exp[tau(kappa+lambda_c)m] T_L(t). At m=sqrt(L) or L^(2/3)
this is not a useful uniform sector estimate. The T_L bound is not itself a
bound on the interacting critical-field grand trace at mesoscopic m.

4. Exact consequences at lambda=lambda_c

The available fixed-m trace convergence is to the free Dirichlet Bose
sector. Its one-particle energies are e_k=2J pi^2 k^2. At lambda_c the
k=1 grand weight is one and the k>=2 weights

 q_k=exp[-tau lambda_c(k^2-1)]

are summable in the product sense. If a_r is the coefficient of z^r in
prod_{k>=2}(1-z q_k)^(-1), then the limiting m-sector trace is

 z_m=sum_{r=0}^m a_r,   z_m -> C_tau:=prod_{k>=2}(1-q_k)^(-1)<infinity.

In particular z_m>=1 and sum_m z_m diverges. Positivity and fixed-sector
convergence imply Xi_L->infinity: for every fixed K,
liminf Xi_L >= sum_{m=0}^K z_m, and K is arbitrary. Since each finite
numerator sum_{m<=K}Z_{L,m} converges to a finite value,

                   Pr_L{N<=K} -> 0 for every fixed K.

Thus N escapes every fixed cutoff for both 0<=kappa<kappa_t and
kappa=kappa_t. This is not a rate. The speed-L free-energy conclusion is
log Xi_L=o(L) at these parameters, which also does not select a sublinear
scale.

One further conclusion can be extracted while retaining all multiplicities:
N/L->0 in probability at both parameter ranges. Here is the boundary
argument, which is stronger than log Xi_L=o(L) alone. For any epsilon>0,
the continuum profile functional has a strictly positive cost at mass at
least epsilon. Indeed, if p=integral rho, the Poincare and arcsine bounds
give, below threshold,

 E_{kappa,lambda_c}(theta) >= (lambda_c/3-kappa) integral rho^2
                              >= (lambda_c/3-kappa) epsilon^2,

and at threshold the stronger inequality

 E_{kappa_t,lambda_c}(theta) >= (8 lambda_c/45) integral rho^3
                                >= (8 lambda_c/45) epsilon^3.

For the constants, Poincare gives
(J/2)integral theta'^2 >= (J pi^2/2)integral theta^2
=lambda_c integral (arcsin sqrt(rho))^2. The positive-coefficient series
for (arcsin u)^2 starts u^2+u^4/3+8u^6/45+... on [0,1]; dropping terms
gives the required inequalities, including the sextic term at kappa_t.

Let g_epsilon denote the corresponding positive lower bound. The banked
random-profile kinetic/contact liminf then implies that for all large L,
uniformly over m>=epsilon L,
  inf spec B_{L,m} >= (g_epsilon/2)L.
For if not, ground vectors along a subsequence have A_L expectation O(L)
(use W<=N and m<=L); their profile law has mass p>=epsilon, while the
liminf of B_L/L is at least the expectation of E_{kappa,lambda_c},
contradicting a value below g_epsilon/2.

To pass from this sector ground gap to the full sector traces, put
C=kappa+lambda_c and q=g_epsilon/2. On the direct sum N>=epsilon L,
min-max gives both B_L>=qL and B_L>=A_L-CL. With
alpha=q/[2(C+q)] in (0,1), convex combination yields

 B_L >= alpha A_L +(q/2)L.

Sectorwise min-max (A_L and B_L both preserve N) and the all-state trace
bound now give

 sum_{m>=epsilon L} Z_{L,m}
   <= exp[-tau q L/2] T_L(alpha tau)
   = exp[-tau q L/2+O((log L)^(3/2))] -> 0.

Since Xi_L>=1 from the vacuum, this proves N/L->0. This argument uses the
profile liminf and T_L, not a fictitious replacement of sector traces by
their ground states.

5. Why neither predicted scale follows

The inputs establish fixed-m limits, divergence of Xi_L, log Xi_L=o(L),
and N/L->0. They do not control Z_{L,m} uniformly when m grows with L.
For example, the abstract arrays

 w_{L,m}=z_m exp[-(m/L^a)^p],  0<=m<=S,

have the same fixed-m limits and a total sum of order L^a, hence log total
O(log L)=o(L), for every 0<a<1 and either p=2 or p=3. Their normalized
number laws live on the freely chosen scale L^a. These arrays are not
claimed to be XXZ traces; they show that the reported pointwise limits and
speed-L entropy do not identify a number scale. The additional N/L->0
conclusion is also compatible with every such sublinear scale.

The precise missing gate is a uniform mesoscopic canonical heat-trace
theorem, including a tail bound. For a proposed r_L and exponent p, one
sufficient form would be, uniformly for x in compact subsets of (0,infty),

 Z_{L,floor(x r_L)} -> C_tau exp(-tau c x^p),  c>0,

plus small-x control and
 lim_{R->infty} limsup_{L->infty} r_L^(-1)
                    sum_{m>R r_L} Z_{L,m}=0.

Then Riemann sums give the limiting density proportional to exp(-tau c x^p).
Equivalently one needs a uniform expansion of E_{L,m} on m=Theta(r_L), a
uniform estimate of R_{L,m} there, and a global tail estimate. Below
threshold the heuristic choices are (r_L,p)=(sqrt(L),2); at threshold
they are (L^(2/3),3). At threshold even the sector ground coefficient and
exponent may receive quantum corrections; neither is established here.
