RAW-STRING HANKEL IDENTIFIABILITY FOR A FINITE HMM
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact finite-dimensional theorem proved in the stated HMM/prefix-law interface; raw-sample upper bound and scoped lower bounds derived; no external review or novelty claim.

Question and legal interface
----------------------------
Let X_1,X_2,... take values in a known finite alphabet Sigma, |Sigma|=q>=2.
The process is generated by a time-homogeneous hidden Markov model with an
unknown initial distribution, transition matrix, and emission probabilities,
and the only structural promise is an upper bound R on the number of hidden
states. The learner sees independent raw strings of length T from the same
initial law. It sees no hidden labels, transition/emission entries, chosen
state resets, conditional-law oracle, or supplied Hankel basis. A word w is
legal data only through its empirical occurrence as an initial prefix.

Write f(w)=Pr[X_1...X_|w|=w] and f(empty)=1. With hidden row distribution
lambda, row-stochastic transition T_hid, and diagonal emission matrices E_a,
put A_a=E_a T_hid and A_w=A_(a1)...A_(ak). Since T_hid 1=1,

    f(w)=lambda A_w 1.

All quantities in the exact theorem are population prefix probabilities.
The HMM parameters and the observable rank are not supplied. The statistical
version below replaces these probabilities by frequencies from iid raw
strings. A single non-resettable dependent stream is a different interface;
it requires mixing or another dependence assumption for the stated
concentration bound.

Theorem: finite Hankel witness and acquired realization
--------------------------------------------------------
For u,v in Sigma*, define H[u,v]=f(uv), the infinite Hankel matrix of the
observable process. Let r=rank(H), the minimal real-linear realization
dimension. For L=R-1, let W_L={w: |w|<=L}, N_L=|W_L|=(q^(L+1)-1)/(q-1), and
let H_L=H[W_L,W_L]. Also define, for every a in Sigma,

    H_(L,a)[u,v]=f(u a v),    u,v in W_L.

Then:

1. rank(H_L)=rank(H)=r<=R. Thus the two-sided word-length witness horizon
   L=R-1 suffices to expose the entire observable rank. Entries of H_L use
   strings of length at most 2R-2.
2. The table consisting of f(w) for every |w|<=2R-1 determines every future
   prefix probability of every HMM with at most R hidden states. The extra
   one-symbol-shift entries H_(L,a) suffice to recover a minimal
   r-dimensional linear realization.
3. The minimal realization is recovered from raw prefix probabilities up
   to an invertible change of coordinates (similarity). This does not, by
   itself, recover the physical hidden transition/emission parameters.

Proof of the witness horizon. A finite HMM has a real linear representation
of dimension at most R. It therefore suffices to work with a minimal
r-dimensional representation (lambda,{A_a},rho), rho=1. Set
x_u=lambda A_u and y_v=A_v rho. For k>=0, let

    U_k=span{x_u: |u|<=k},       V_k=span{y_v: |v|<=k}.

U_0 and V_0 each have dimension one because lambda and rho are nonzero.
Moreover U_(k+1)=U_k+sum_a U_k A_a. If U_(k+1)=U_k, U_k is invariant
under every A_a and no longer word adds a reachable row direction. Before
such a plateau, dimension rises by at least one. Since the ambient
dimension is r, U_(r-1) contains all reachable rows. The dual identity
V_(k+1)=V_k+sum_a A_a V_k gives the same conclusion for suffix columns.
Hence U_L and V_L are complete for L=R-1>=r-1. In a minimal realization,
the finite row and column factors have ranks r; their product H_L has rank
r. The full Hankel rank is r, proving rank(H_L)=rank(H).

The exact reconstruction uses no supplied basis. From H_L, perform exact
rank-revealing elimination and choose P,S subset W_L, each of size r, so
X=H_L[P,S] is nonsingular. This is an acquired word-indexed minor, not an
oracle-provided coordinate system. For each a, set Y_a=H_(L,a)[P,S] and
define

    alpha = H_L[empty,S] X^(-1),
    B_a   = Y_a X^(-1),
    beta  = H_L[P,empty].

The orientation B_a=Y_a X^(-1) is for row-state coordinates. To verify it,
let h_u(v)=f(uv) be the full Hankel row and c_u=H[u,S]X^(-1). Since the
rows P span every row and restriction to S is injective on that row space,
h_u=c_u H[P,Sigma*]. Evaluating this identity at suffixes a s gives
H[ua,S]=c_u Y_a. Since H[ua,S]=c_(ua) X, we have c_(ua)=c_u B_a.
Also c_empty=alpha and f(u)=c_u beta. Induction gives

    f(a1...ak)=alpha B_(a1)...B_(ak) beta

for every word, including lengths beyond the observed horizon. This also
proves that any second model with at most R hidden states and the same table
through length 2R-1 has the same infinite observable law: each model's
finite table constructs a minimal realization of its full series, and the
shared table fixes that realization's initial/transition/terminal
coordinates.

For explicit similarity with any other minimal realization
(lambda',{A'_a},rho'), let C_P have rows lambda A_p, p in P, and let C'_P
have rows lambda' A'_p. Since X=H[P,S] is nonsingular and factors as
C_P D_S in either realization, both C_P and C'_P are nonsingular. The
coordinates above satisfy c_u=(lambda A_u)C_P^(-1)
=(lambda' A'_u)(C'_P)^(-1). Set S=C_P^(-1)C'_P. Then
lambda'=lambda S, and for every a, (lambda A_u)A_a S
=(lambda A_u)S A'_a for all u. Reachability makes the rows lambda A_u
span R^r, so A'_a=S^(-1)A_a S. The terminal relation
rho'=S^(-1)rho follows by evaluating every reachable row at the empty
suffix. Thus the ambiguity is exactly an invertible coordinate change
within minimal real realizations; it need not preserve HMM positivity.

Only f(w) with |w|<=2L+1=2R-1 is used. H_L uses the subtable through 2L;
the shift blocks use the extra symbol. Given the acquired table, exact
arithmetic costs O(N_L^3) field operations for a dense rank/pivot
calculation, O(N_L^2) storage for H_L, and O(q r^3) straightforward matrix
operations for the q shifts and coordinate matrices. The scalar table has
M_T=sum_(j=0)^T q^j entries in the exhaustive implementation. These are
arithmetic counts, not bit-complexity bounds: exact rational elimination
also depends on the encoding heights and intermediate coefficient growth.

What rank counts, and what it does not
--------------------------------------
The Hankel rank r is exactly the smallest number of real linear predictive
coordinates that can represent all prefix probabilities. Equivalently, it
is the dimension of the span of the unnormalized residual rows
v -> f(uv). For f(u)>0, normalizing a row by f(u) does not change the span.
This is the minimal-linear-predictive-state count.

Three other counts must not be conflated with r:

* If there are finitely many distinct normalized predictive laws after
  positive-probability histories, their number s equals r exactly when
  those s residual functions are linearly independent. In general r<=s;
  distinct conditional laws can be linearly dependent. A finite-state HMM
  can also have infinitely many posterior predictive laws while r remains
  finite.
* For a particular m-hidden-state HMM, r=m exactly when its linear
  realization is reachable and observable (the reachable row span and
  observable suffix span both have dimension m). If r=R and the supplied
  cap is R, the hidden-state count is exactly R and no smaller HMM can
  realize the same law, because every m-state HMM has Hankel rank at most m.
* If r<R, rank alone gives only a lower bound on the smallest stochastic
  hidden-state realization. A minimal real linear realization can have
  signed coordinates and need not be an HMM. Recovering HMM parameters up
  to hidden-label permutation requires additional identifiability
  assumptions; equality of observable laws alone does not label latent
  states.

Thus “observable rank equals minimal predictive-state count” is exact for
linear predictive dimension, and for a finite residual-state count only
under linear independence of its residual laws. It is not an unconditional
claim about physical hidden states or the cardinality of all posterior
predictors.

Statistical theorem from raw strings
------------------------------------
Assume N independent raw trajectories, each of length T=2R-1. For every
word w with |w|<=T, estimate f(w) by the fraction of trajectories whose
initial prefix is w. Let M_T=sum_(j=0)^T q^j. For any eta>0, Hoeffding plus
a union bound gives

    Pr[max_|w|<=T |fhat(w)-f(w)| > eta]
      <= 2 M_T exp(-2 N eta^2).

The indicators for different w within one trajectory are dependent, but
this union bound needs only independence between trajectories. Each entry of
H_L then has error at most eta and
||Hhat_L-H_L||_2<=||Hhat_L-H_L||_F<=N_L eta. Suppose r>=1 and
sigma_r(H_L)>=gamma>0 while sigma_(r+1)(H_L)=0. With
eta=gamma/(4N_L), Weyl's inequality places the first r estimated singular
values above 3gamma/4 and all remaining ones below gamma/4. Thresholding at
gamma/2 recovers r with probability at least 1-delta provided

    N >= 8 N_L^2 gamma^(-2) log(2 M_T/delta).

This upper bound displays three separate resources: cap R fixes the
witness horizon; q and R determine the exponential prefix-table size; and
the smallest nonzero singular value gamma controls rank stability. It is
not a polynomial-in-R sample guarantee. It is a simple uniform guarantee
for exhaustive observable-table estimation.

To estimate a conditional probability f(ua)/f(u), a minimum prefix-mass
condition is separately needed. If f(u)>=p_min>0 and eta<=p_min/2, then
the empirical ratio has error at most 4 eta/p_min. Thus error at most tau
for all considered prefixes follows by also requiring
eta<=tau p_min/4 (0<tau<=1), at sample cost

    N >= (1/(2 eta^2)) log(2 M_T/delta),
    eta <= min{gamma/(4N_L), tau p_min/4}.

The mass term is not needed for additive-probability Hankel rank recovery;
it is needed to estimate normalized conditional predictions after rare
histories. If the aim is a numerically stable coordinate realization, a
chosen pivot minor also needs a conditioning margin
sigma_min(X)>=lambda. Entrywise noise gives
||Xhat-X||_2<=r eta; when r eta<=lambda/2, the inverse norm is at most
2/lambda, and the recovered matrices are perturbation-stable with errors
of order r eta/lambda + r^2 eta/lambda^2. The global rank gap gamma does
not certify that an arbitrary word-indexed pivot minor has this lambda.

Raw-data baseline and full costs
--------------------------------
The equally informed direct baseline is the exhaustive raw-string
histogram: retain each complete observed string, update its full-string
counter and every one of its T prefix counters, assemble H_L, select its
rank/pivots by SVD or exact elimination, and form the shifted blocks from
the same table. It receives
the same N raw trajectories and is supplied only q and R. It has no
supplied model entries or basis. The stated theorem is an identifiability
and finite-table result, not a demonstrated sample or runtime advantage
over this baseline or over a model-based HMM estimator using the same raw
strings.

For the exhaustive statistical implementation, total observed symbols are
N(2R-1); updating counters takes O(NR) prefix operations; the dense table
uses O(M_T log N) counter bits; and Hankel storage is O(N_L^2) reals. Rank
calculation is O(N_L^3) arithmetic operations, followed by O(q r^3) for
the simple dense shift-coordinate products. Since N_L and M_T grow as
q^R and q^(2R), respectively, this transparent baseline can be
exponential in the supplied state cap. More structured algorithms can
improve this only with additional assumptions or a stronger, separately
charged access model. The 2009 Hsu-Kakade-Zhang spectral HMM algorithm is
a relevant iid raw-observation baseline under its own singular-value and
observation-feature conditions; those conditions are not silently
substituted for the arbitrary finite-HMM cap used here.

Three necessity controls
------------------------
Rank gap. Let P0 emit iid fair bits. Let P_delta choose a latent sign
Z uniformly once, then emit iid Bernoulli(1/2+Z delta) bits. P_delta is a
two-state HMM and has Hankel rank 2 for delta>0; P0 has rank 1. On rows and
columns {empty,0,1}, the singular values for P_delta are 3/2, 2 delta^2,
0, so gamma=2 delta^2. The length-two law has probabilities
1/4+delta^2 on 00 and 11 and 1/4-delta^2 on 01 and 10. Its KL divergence
from the fair iid length-two law is

    2(1/4+delta^2) log(1+4delta^2)
    +2(1/4-delta^2) log(1-4delta^2)
      = 8 delta^4 + O(delta^8).

Le Cam's two-point bound therefore requires Omega(delta^-4)=Omega(gamma^-2)
independent length-two trajectories for constant-error rank-1-versus-rank-2
testing. This verifies that the inverse-square rank-gap dependence is not
just an artifact of the Hoeffding upper bound in this two-state case.

Horizon. For any T, compare the one-state all-zero process with the
deterministic countdown process that emits T zeros, then emits ones forever.
The latter is an HMM with T+1 states. Their raw length-T strings are
identical, but their future laws differ at time T+1. Taking T=R-1 gives a
cap-R pair. Hence no horizon bounded independently of R can certify the
all-future law, and a horizon of order R is necessary. This lower bound
does not claim that the factor-two placement of the sufficient 2R-1
realization table is tight.

Minimum prefix mass. Use a finite time-homogeneous HMM with marker states
c0,c1, bit states b0,b1, and a fair absorbing tail. Initially choose c1
with probability p and c0 otherwise. c1 emits marker a and moves to b1;
c0 emits marker b and moves to b0. b0 emits a fair bit; b1 emits a bit
with probability 1/2+epsilon of 1 under model P+ and 1/2-epsilon under
model P-. Both bit states then move to the common fair tail. The prefix a
has mass p, and the conditional next-bit laws after a differ by 2 epsilon.
Only that rare branch distinguishes the raw laws; one length-two sample
has KL p*D(Bern(1/2+epsilon)||Bern(1/2-epsilon))=8p epsilon^2+o(p epsilon^2).
Consequently Omega(1/(p epsilon^2)) independent trajectories are required
to identify the conditional prediction after a to error below epsilon.
This is the inverse-prefix-mass cost for conditional prediction; it is
distinct from additive-Hankel rank recovery.

Supplied versus acquired; falsifiers and UNKNOWN gates
-----------------------------------------------------
Supplied: finite alphabet, time-homogeneous finite-HMM semantics, upper
hidden-state cap R, and (for the statistical statement) independent raw
reset trajectories of length 2R-1. Acquired from the strings: all used
prefix frequencies, rank r, row/column pivots P,S, the coordinate basis,
and the minimal linear realization. The rank gap gamma, selected-minor
conditioning lambda, and minimum relevant prefix mass p_min are assumptions
or quantities that must be lower-bounded from evidence for finite-sample
claims; they are not consequences of R alone. No hidden state, feature
basis, or matrix entry is supplied.

An exact falsifier of the algebraic statement would be an HMM with at most
R states for which rank(H_[R-1]) is smaller than rank(H), or two such HMMs
agreeing on every prefix through length 2R-1 but disagreeing later. An
implementation falsifier is a rational fixture where the pivot formulas
fail to reproduce an independently enumerated longer-word probability.
The standard-library exact fixture in
`work/programmes/acquisition_learning/leaves/observable_identifiability/cycle2_pivot_fixture.py`
checks the row-pivot formulas on a two-state rational HMM for all 511 binary
words through length eight. This is a finite convention check, not evidence
in place of the proof above.

UNKNOWN gates: the theorem does not give an efficient method to discover R
when no cap or counterexample/equivalence oracle exists; it does not yield
a polynomial sample bound in R for unrestricted raw HMMs; it does not
guarantee a nonnegative/stochastic learned linear realization when r<R;
and it does not identify latent parameters without extra assumptions. If
the data are one dependent stream rather than independent resets, the
statistical bound requires a separately established dependence/mixing
control. If gamma, lambda, or p_min is arbitrarily small, the stated
uniform stable-estimation guarantees vanish.

Selective sources and cards
---------------------------
Primary foundations:

* M. Fliess, “Séries rationnelles positives et processus stochastiques,”
  Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré B 11(1), 1-21 (1975), especially
  the finite Hankel-rank/rational-series result and minimal realization
  discussion. Numdam hosts the article and PDF:
  https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPB_1975__11_1_1_0/
* J. W. Carlyle and A. Paz, “Realizations by stochastic finite automata,”
  Journal of Computer and System Sciences 5(1), 26-40 (1971),
  doi:10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3. Its finite-rank criterion is for
  real-weighted sequential realizations; it does not assert that a
  minimal real realization is a positive HMM.
* H. Jaeger, “Observable Operator Models for Discrete Stochastic Time
  Series,” Neural Computation 12(6), 1371-1398 (2000),
  doi:10.1162/089976600300015411. This develops predictive states as
  conditional continuation laws and their finite-dimensional linear
  operators.
* D. Hsu, S. M. Kakade, and T. Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning
  Hidden Markov Models,” COLT 2009, pp. 223-238; arXiv:0811.4413.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413. This is a raw-iid-sample spectral
  baseline with explicit spectral separation assumptions, not a free
  hidden-parameter oracle.
* H. Ito, S.-I. Amari, and K. Kobayashi, “Identifiability of Hidden Markov
  Information Sources and Their Minimum Degrees of Freedom,” IEEE
  Transactions on Information Theory 38(2), 324-333 (1992),
  https://bsi-ni.brain.riken.jp/database/file/135/133.pdf. This is a
  primary source on the separate question of physical HMM identifiability.

Selective local cards N25-acquisition-interface-tests and
N13-passive-parity-boundary were used only as interface guardrails:
matrix-output/adaptive teacher access and persistent latent readout are
stronger than independent raw strings. Neither card supplies a lemma for
the Hankel theorem or its sample bound.

No novelty or external-validation claim is made. The theorem is a
finite-dimensional realization result specialized to the actual prefix
law of an unknown HMM; the statistical upper bound is intentionally the
direct, exhaustive raw-data construction.
