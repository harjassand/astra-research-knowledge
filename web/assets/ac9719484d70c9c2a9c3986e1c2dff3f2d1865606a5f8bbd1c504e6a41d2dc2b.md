# Application audit: force-perturbed smFRET

**Verdict.** A strong real-molecule precedent exists for repeated hidden-state fluorescence under calibrated, reversible mechanical forcing: Hohng et al.'s single Holliday-junction (HJ) force–FRET experiment. It establishes that a tweezers action can change an smFRET-observed transition process and that rates can be estimated condition by condition. It does **not** establish the scalar slowdown action \(Q\mapsto\rho Q\) assumed by the slow-read theorem: force changes the two directional rates differently and shifts state populations. The assay is an in-vitro engineered DNA junction, not a living-cell fate-state system.

In Hohng et al. (Science, 2007), a surface-immobilized, Cy3/Cy5-labeled four-arm DNA junction is connected to an optically trapped bead by a long \(\lambda\)-DNA linker. The action is to move the sample-tethered junction relative to the bead; a calibrated piezo stage changes the tension and the confocal beam follows the junction. The same molecule was measured at five force settings, with 10-second FRET traces at each setting and 10-ms integration (100-Hz readout). One to five force-data cycles were collected before photobleaching, or 50–250 seconds of observation. At 0.3 pN the two stacking conformers had similar occupancy; above 1 pN the 11-bp XR construct was biased toward low FRET, and the longer-arm construct showed bias above 0.5 pN. Thus the emissions repeat while the experiment changes the force condition on the same molecule.

Force was calibrated rather than treated as an unobserved action. The trap stiffness was 0.11 pN/nm, obtained from a 10-kHz bead-position power spectrum; at 2 pN and 100-Hz bandwidth the force-detection noise was 0.2 pN. The \(\lambda\)-DNA linker stiffness was estimated as 0.002 pN/nm at 2 pN, so a 5-nm junction motion was estimated to add only about 0.01 pN of force variation. HMM fits produced force-dependent forward and backward rates. Under the authors' force-dependent transition-state interpretation, the XR rates imply distances to the transition state of 1.5 ± 0.3 nm from isoII and 2.9 ± 0.6 nm from isoI; these are standard deviations across five molecules, not confidence bounds. The FRET levels are observed; the hidden-state path, rates, and transition-state distances are inferred.

This is enough to nominate a calibrated **controlled-rate family** \(Q(F)\) for a follow-up assay. It is not a blockwise jump-probability certificate: force-step/settling latency and ramp transition counts are not reported, and the across-molecule standard deviations are not an upper confidence envelope for future blocks. The two rates respond asymmetrically, so pooling force levels as one stationary HMM or claiming a common slowdown factor would be wrong. The source also does not report photon totals, photon rate, or a matched active-versus-passive information-per-photon result.

A simpler force-controlled DNA-hairpin demonstration by Tarsa et al. (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed., 2007) is useful as a control implementation check: the coverslip was translated at 250 nm/s back and forth through the opening threshold; the hairpin opened near 18 pN, the trace traversed roughly 6–24 pN, and donor/acceptor signals were sampled at 20 Hz (50-ms reads). Several reversible open/close cycles were seen, with donor photobleaching around 65 seconds in the example. It directly shows reversible force-driven conformational switching with FRET, but it reports no force-conditioned transition-rate fit or statistical transition bound. Hohng is therefore the closer match to the full interface.

**Resource comparison.** Hohng's five 10-second conditions cost at least 50 seconds per complete force cycle, up to 250 seconds for the reported one-to-five cycles, plus tether preparation, optical trapping, piezo force calibration, beam tracking, and photostability reagents. At 10-ms integration, that is 5,000 binned FRET reads per cycle (up to 25,000 across five cycles); this is not a photon count. A strong passive baseline keeps the molecule at one condition and fits the donor/acceptor photon colors and interphoton gaps directly with a photon-by-photon likelihood/HMM, avoiding time binning. That uses the native fluorescence stream without force-ramp and calibration overhead, but it estimates the fixed-condition dynamics; it cannot reveal \(Q(F)\) without an intervention. No source supplies an equal-photon or equal-wall-time comparison, and the Hohng paper does not publish the photon timestamps needed to reconstruct one. Do not claim an acquisition advantage until that matched comparison is run.

**Decision for the parent result.** This is a concrete biological-physics bridge for a paid, reversible perturbation that yields repeated emissions and empirically calibrated transition rates. It supports measuring and using a force-indexed rate matrix for this assay. It does not validate a universal slowdown action, a finite-probability ramp bound, or transfer to native cell fate dynamics. The action interface remains **conditionally supported; theorem-level transfer remains UNKNOWN**.
