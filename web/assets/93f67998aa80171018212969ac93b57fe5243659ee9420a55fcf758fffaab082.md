# Conditional barrier for universal passive acquisition of predictive state

## Exact interface

Let the training source be i.i.d. complete strings from a binary length-T HMM. A proposed learner receives only polynomially many such strings, runs in polynomial time in T and the requested accuracy, and returns an efficiently evaluable predictor/distribution model. In particular, the returned object must let us evaluate, to polynomial-bit precision, the probabilities of a complete string or the conditional probability of its final bit given its prefix. The learner is not given conditional samples, conditional-probability queries, a known transition/emission representation, or a supplied predictive-feature basis.

## Conditional reduction from LPN

The primary paper Kakade, Krishnamurthy, Mahajan, and Zhang, “Learning Hidden Markov Models Using Conditional Samples,” arXiv:2302.14753v2 (2024), constructs a binary-observation HMM with at most 4T states for the following distribution: the first T−1 bits z are uniform on {0,1}^(T−1), and the last bit y equals the secret parity s·z over F₂ with probability 1−α and its complement with probability α, for fixed 0<α<1/2. The paper identifies this as the noisy-parity (LPN) learning problem. It states that passive i.i.d.-sequence HMM learning is cryptographically hard under the standard noisy-parity assumption, while polynomial-time learning is possible with an exact conditional-probability oracle (Theorem 1) and, under a fidelity condition, with conditional samples (Theorem 2). The paper leaves arbitrary low-rank conditional-sample learning open.

Here is a direct reduction from total-variation distribution learning to prediction of the secret parity. Write P_s(z,y) for the HMM's length-T law, and suppose the learner returns an evaluable distribution Q with

    TV(P_s,Q) ≤ τ.

For each z, define g_Q(z)=argmax_y Q(z,y), with a fixed tie rule. If g_Q(z) is not s·z, then the signed mass difference under P_s between the correct and incorrect labels is 2^(−(T−1))(1−2α), whereas Q gives the wrong label at least as much mass as the correct one. Therefore the L1 distance contributed by this z is at least 2^(−(T−1))(1−2α). Summing over the fraction ρ of z on which g_Q errs gives

    2 τ ≥ ||P_s−Q||_1 ≥ ρ(1−2α),
    ρ ≤ 2τ/(1−2α).

Thus any fixed τ<(1−2α)/4 yields a polynomial-time predictor for a uniform random LPN instance with error strictly below 1/2, contradicting the assumed polynomial-time hardness. To compute g_Q, two model evaluations per challenge z suffice. If the returned interface gives only approximate probabilities, additive precision O(2^(−T)(1−2α)) is enough for the joint-probability version and needs only O(T+log(1/(1−2α))) output bits; a direct conditional-probability evaluator needs only constant precision below the parity margin. The algorithm's training sample count and evaluation time remain polynomial by hypothesis.

This argument is conditional on LPN hardness and on efficient evaluation of the output representation. It is not an unconditional information-theoretic lower bound. It does not exclude average-risk learning under a different distribution, efficient learning with active conditional queries, or restricted model classes that fail to encode noisy parity.

## Prior-art boundary and acquisition meaning

The reduction is not a new hardness result. Its source is the cited HMM paper's noisy-parity construction and LPN premise. The useful interface distinction is that the positive results in that paper add an interactive service: exact conditional probabilities, or conditional samples plus an explicit fidelity parameter. Such queries can place probability mass on a rare/selected history without waiting for that history in a passive record. Any claimed teacher-free feature-acquisition result must state whether this service is supplied and charge its calls, history length, numerical precision, and failures.

This also separates three claims that are often conflated:

1. A supplied low-rank/HMM representation can be compressed or updated.
2. A teacher or oracle can return conditional predictions that make a representation identifiable.
3. A passive learner can discover a predictive representation from examples alone.

The cited positive theorems concern (2) under their stated oracle/fidelity interfaces; they do not establish universal (3). Conversely, the LPN reduction blocks a universal efficient passive version of (3) only conditionally and only for a learner rich enough to return an evaluable predictor.

## Primary source ranges

- Kakade, Krishnamurthy, Mahajan, Zhang, *Learning Hidden Markov Models Using Conditional Samples*, arXiv:2302.14753v2. PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14753 . Sections 1.1–1.2 define finite-length rank, exact conditional-probability and conditional-sampling oracles, and state Theorems 1–2 plus the open arbitrary-low-rank question (PDF pp. 4–6). Section 2.1 describes the noisy-parity HMM, O(T) state encoding, and passive-sample hardness (PDF pp. 7–8); Appendix C.2 gives the construction (PDF pp. 40–41).
- Blum, Furst, Kearns, Lipton, *Cryptographic Primitives Based on Hard Learning Problems*, CRYPTO 1993 / LNCS 773 (1994), author manuscript: https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/papers/prim.pdf . This is an early primary source for cryptographic hardness assumptions in learning; exact assumption parameters must be matched to the LPN variant under discussion.
