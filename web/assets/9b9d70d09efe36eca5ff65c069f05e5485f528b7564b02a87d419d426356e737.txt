1. Precise local statement
--------------------------
Let G be a finite square-lattice graph with free vertices V and fixed wall set W. Assume every free component is connected to W, the hard constraints are |h_x-h_y|<=1 on graph edges, and the uniform conditional polytope K_A has positive volume. For a deterministic feasible wall vector A on W, let mu_A be normalized Lebesgue measure on K_A. Suppose:

  (i) |A(w)|<=B for all w in W, with B fixed;
  (ii) the tested core Q and its enlarged force patches contain no fixed pins or reflecting sign walls;
  (iii) the distance from those patches to W is at least the finite buffer L_*(B,rho) chosen below.

Then the annealed sample h~mu_A has an all-finite-set sparse bound for local near-contact defects in the core, with any prescribed animal parameter rho, uniformly over feasible A and W. The good-block data needed by the source force argument are: (a) a fixed positive initial slack on every tested nonfixed edge and (b) linear residual slack from the tested edge endpoints to the local collar. The claim is about these two data, not the manuscript's extra triangular non-tie tests, which are not needed by c:force.

For two bounded-wall Gibbs marginals whose tested core has a common free graph and no observations/walls, the same construction gives the original-marginal hypotheses needed by c:comparison. A fixed-size test patch needs only the fixed buffer L_*(B,rho); to obtain the source's scaled local energy on Q_r, also keep the usual positive macroscopic margin between Q_r and any uncontrolled boundary/seed. If the relevant equal-mass/zero-mass average bounds hold, the source's synchronous local reflected comparison then yields its weighted local energy and cluster-dissipation bound. The constants depend on B, rho, fixed box ratios, and the macro-average constants, but not on the size of the exterior graph or on the particular feasible wall vector. This is an annealed response statement; it is not a pointwise comparison of gradients and it does not claim uniformity after arbitrary further exact interior reveals.

A quantitative compatibility condition is essential: L_* must dominate both the fixed source patch size and the additive height-gap loss divided by the zero-law long-slack slope. In particular, “some positive buffer” without a relation to B does not suffice.

2. Exact wall-kernel tower law
-------------------------------
For a fixed pin set W, write
  K_A={u in R^V: |u_x-u_y|<=1 on edges, u|_W=A},
  Z(A)=Leb_V(K_A),
  mu_A(du)=1_{K_A}(u)du/Z(A).

For an externally chosen wall law nu(dA), followed by a conditional Gibbs draw, the joint law is nu(dA)mu_A(du). If instead the wall coordinates A are part of one uniform Lebesgue law on the full feasible polytope, Fubini gives the disintegration eta(dA)mu_A(du), where eta(dA)=Z(A)nu_0(dA)/integral Z(a)nu_0(da) for the underlying Lebesgue wall measure nu_0 restricted to feasible values. Thus the annealed expectation is exactly
  E[F(A,h)]=integral eta(dA) integral F(A,u) mu_A(du).

The wall marginal is volume-biased in the second construction. It is not generally uniform over feasible A. For an adaptive exact-value transcript T, the posterior eta_T is the disintegration after the transcript; decisions based only on earlier answers and auxiliary randomness add no factor depending on unqueried coordinates. Replacing the conditional product by a stationary synchronous joining preserves each conditional marginal, and integrating over eta_T restores the original annealed marginal (coupling.tex:204-239). No uniform-in-T statement follows unless its conditional hypotheses are separately proved. In the bounded-wall result above, the initial contact estimate is uniform over deterministic A, so it can be integrated against any eta supported on the stated bounded, buffered class.

3. Derivation of local CAP from bounded walls
---------------------------------------------
The pointwise coupling f:bounded is useful for two marginal estimates, not as an invariant constraint on the later reflected joining. For a fixed W and |A|<=B, its common-update heat-bath construction couples h_A~mu_A and h_0~mu_0 (zero values on the same W) with
  max_x |h_A(x)-h_0(x)|<=B.
Consequently, for every pair x,y,
  | (h_A(x)-h_A(y))-(h_0(x)-h_0(y)) |<=2B.
This transfers a path's residual slack only additively: if its graph length is m and s_j=m-|h_j(x)-h_j(y)|, then s_A>=s_0-2B. It does not transfer a one-edge slack threshold. The static heat-bath coupling is not preserved as a pointwise relation when the source later replaces the initial pair by a stationary synchronous reflected joining. We use it only to prove individual-marginal long-slack and average estimates; local force is constructed anew by the Brownian tube argument.

Choose the zero-law f:sparse parameters for a desired small animal probability and let alpha>0 be its long-path slack slope. Increase the fixed enlarged block until every tested endpoint-to-shell distance m is at least max(k_0,4B/alpha,4/alpha). On a zero-law good block, f:far gives s_0>=2 alpha m for each such pair. Under the bounded-data coupling,
  s_A>=2 alpha m-2B>= (3/2)alpha m >= alpha m.
The same inclusion holds jointly on any chosen set of blocks under one global coupling. Thus bounded-wall failure of this relaxed long-slack test is contained in a zero-law long-slack failure at a weaker fixed slope, and inherits the all-finite-set bound. All tested blocks are outside the source's fixed pin-block collar because the wall set is beyond the chosen buffer.

Now condition on the exact values xi on the shell of one enlarged test patch. The conditional interior kernel is uniform on its hard-Lipschitz fiber. The long-slack event is shell-measurable in the following useful sense: if the current field has the transferred endpoint-to-shell slack, its restriction is a feasible filling with
  |h(x)-xi(s)|<=dist(x,s)-2
for every endpoint x of the central tested edge and every shell site s, by the chosen lower bound on m. The source strict-filling construction (foundations.tex:628-672) applies because there are no fixed pins or intervals in the patch interior: it gives a feasible filling g with central-edge gradient at most 1/2 in absolute value.

If the patch fiber has m_* free coordinates, map every h in that fiber to h_theta=(1-theta)h+theta g. With theta=2s and 0<s<1/4, convexity preserves feasibility and the tested edge has |grad h_theta|<=1-s. The image has relative volume (1-2s)^m_*. Hence
  mu_{A,xi}(|grad_e h|>1-s) <= 1-(1-2s)^m_* <= 2m_*s
whenever the shell admits the long-slack filling. If it does not, the sampled configuration witnesses failure of the transferred long-slack test. Combining the two alternatives and choosing s small makes a single-block defect probability arbitrarily small.

For a separated family of patches, conditional interiors are independent given their complement/shells. The uniform volume bound therefore multiplies for blocks with good long-slack shells. If an entire k-set is defective, either at least half its blocks fail long slack, or at least half have a near-contact under a shell admitting the strict filling. The first alternative inherits the zero-law joint animal estimate; the second is bounded by (2m_*s)^{k/2} after finite coloring. Apply the single-edge estimate to every edge in the fixed central force patch and every tested endpoint-to-collar path; this is a fixed finite union, so its cost is absorbed by choosing the zero-law parameter and s smaller. Choose the zero-law parameter first and s second; subset/color costs are finite and are absorbed by retuning. This proves the asserted all-finite-set local contact bound. It is the conditional-filling mechanism, not the sup-gap alone, that recovers microscopic strictness.

For each fixed A, the stationary reflected process on K_A has the same reversible Neumann law used in f:step. Its displacement estimate is independent of pin values, so the initial sparse bound gives sparse persistence and finite transmission-cluster moments at one common short time. On a good patch, c:force supplies a finite path of genuine facets and a uniform Brownian tube with positive impulse; c:insulation transfers it to the uncut process. The source force-loss inequality and cluster allocation then choose weights of the form c/[1+E(H_B^m|I)] and give weighted coercivity. The conditional-moment lemma and sparse-hole extension give the spatial inverse-weight and hole moments used in the truncation step.

The local energy is obtained from stationarity, not from comparing static covariances. The cluster cutoff inequality (coupling.tex:602) cancels its left side under stationarity and yields the Caccioppoli estimate (label c:caccioppoli): local weighted energy plus local cluster dissipation is bounded by the cutoff-gap factor times E N_n on a larger box. The sparse-hole Sobolev fill and the zero-mass/equal-mass average bounds give the absorption inequality (label c:absorb). The source's interior iteration then yields the O(1) local energy bound. This is exactly the local force/truncation/integration-by-parts chain needed by c:comparison.

4. Transfer of macroscopic averages and mixtures
------------------------------------------------
For any deterministic linear statistic q, the same coupling gives
  |sum_x q_x h_A(x)-sum_x q_x h_0(x)|<=B sum_x |q_x|.
The source average tests have uniformly bounded L1 norm. Thus zero-mass statistics transfer with an additive B||q||_1; nonzero-mass tests transfer when the source's connected macroscopic pin anchor hypothesis is present. If X_0 has the source exponential moment, then E exp(a|X_A|)<=exp(aB||q||_1) E exp(a|X_0|). This is uniform in A and therefore persists after integration against the exact wall marginal eta. For two marginals, their average-difference moments follow from the two individual bounds by Cauchy/Minkowski; no independence of wall vectors or of the synchronous joining is used.

Consequently the local c:comparison energy theorem can be applied to bounded wall mixtures if: both marginal wall laws are supported on the same buffered class, the common tested region is free of observed pins/sign walls, and either its equal-mass average tests are zero-mass or the required connected pin anchor is available. Its conclusion remains in the source's form: weighted gradient energy and local cluster dissipation have bounded expectation, with deterministic spatial-moment cutoffs outside arbitrarily small polynomial exceptions. This says the bounded exterior wall law changes the local bulk comparison by a controlled amount in that energy topology. It is not a theorem that the interior mean is harmonic, nor that a static gradient observable is close in total variation to its zero-boundary law.

5. Exact falsifiers and the first boundary obstruction
------------------------------------------------------
A uniform signed height gap does not itself transfer edge slack. On any finite square, let h_0 be identically zero and h_b(x,y)=(-1)^(x+y)/2. Both are feasible hard-Lipschitz configurations; |h_b-h_0|=1/2 everywhere. Pin only the outer boundary to h_b for the second configuration and to zero for the first. With a sufficiently large free buffer, there are no observed pins in the tested core, yet every core edge of h_b is exactly tight and every core edge of h_0 has full slack. The squared edge-gradient difference is one on every core edge, so its unweighted sum grows like the core area. This is a deterministic counterexample to deriving local CAP or an O(1) unnormalized energy bound from the pointwise gap. It is not a counterexample to the Gibbs-law lemma above: the checkerboard is one feasible configuration, not a claim about its Gibbs probability.

There is also an exact positive-volume Gibbs-kernel obstruction when the buffer is not quantitatively larger than the wall-height range. For an integer R>=2, take the square grid {0,...,2R}^2, pin the left side to +B and the right side to -B, leave all other vertices free, and set B=R-epsilon with 0<epsilon<1/4. This is feasible with positive volume: h(i,j)=B(1-i/R) has every free edge strictly slack. Every horizontal row has 2R edges and endpoint drop 2B=2R-2epsilon. Since each oriented decrement is at most one, every edge on each row, including a central core edge, has decrement at least 1-2epsilon. The central edge is at distance R-1 from the nearest pinned vertex, so its radius-(R-2) graph neighborhood is pin-free. Thus under mu_A the central edge is in the 2epsilon-near-contact event with probability one. Under the zero-wall kernel on the same graph, the central edge's exact-tightness hyperplane has Lebesgue measure zero, so its probability of lying within 2epsilon of a facet tends to zero as epsilon decreases. The bounded-data coupling still supplies a pointwise height gap at most B. This proves that “bounded walls outside a buffer” alone is insufficient uniformly over wall values: the buffer must exceed the wall-height difference by a fixed slack margin, with the margin large enough for the selected long-path slope and patch size. It also pinpoints why long slack, rather than the sup-gap itself, is the transferable quantity.

6. Scope and proof status
-------------------------
Established here by direct finite-dimensional derivation: (a) the exact wall disintegration; (b) bounded-gap transfer of long slack with additive 2B cost; (c) recovery of microscopic edge strictness through shell conditioning, strict feasible filling, and homothetic volume; (d) the resulting annealed local sparse-block estimate under an explicit buffer condition; and (e) the route from that estimate through the pinned source's force and cutoff proof to local weighted energy, conditional on the source's stated average/geometry inputs. The zero-law all-finite-set sparse estimate uses the Cohen--Alloro--Peled controlled-gradient theorem, Theorem 1.1 (arXiv:1711.00259v2), exactly as imported by f:sparse; this packet does not independently re-audit that primary theorem.

Not established: arbitrary quenched estimates after fixing every exact interior transcript; unbounded wall data; walls/sign observations inside the force buffer; a local energy bound from the sup-gap alone; or any continuum GFF/harmonicity result. The manuscript's imported controlled-gradient theorem and convex-diffusion facts retain their stated source status. This packet is an internal proof reconstruction, not formal verification or external review. No external novelty/priority claim is made.
