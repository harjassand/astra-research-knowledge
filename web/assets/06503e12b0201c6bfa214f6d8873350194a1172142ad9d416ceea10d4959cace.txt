1. Multiplicity incidence: patterns.tex 165-191

For a loopless underlying image graph, let m_e count path traversals of e.
At vertex z, each occurrence of e gives exactly one incidence on e's
half-edge at z. Every incidence other than an interval endpoint is paired
with the incidence of the preceding or following path edge. Immersion
ensures that its paired half-edge differs. The indexing points of closed
paths are paired using their cyclic turn; they are not interval endpoints.
If a_z interval endpoints occur at z, an edge with largest multiplicity
needs at least max_e m_e - a_z incidences on the other half-edges. Hence

  2 max_{e incident to z} m_e <= sum_{e incident to z} m_e + a_z.

Each vertex is present at precisely max m_e positive multiplicity levels.
Summing the inequality, and using sum_z a_z=2*iota, gives

  sum_j V_j <= sum_e m_e + iota = H + iota.

The edge identity sum_j E_j=H is immediate. Since k_1=iota and higher k_j
are zero, sum_j(V_j-E_j-k_j)<=0 follows. Girth L>2 eliminates loops and
parallel-edge concerns in the image graph, as explicitly assumed at line182.

Sharpness diagnostic: a simple interval of h edges has E_1=h, V_1=h+1,
and no higher stages. The +iota term is necessary; fixing its root in
stage one removes exactly its surplus factor n. A repeated closed cycle
has V_j=E_j at every nonempty stage, so equality in the root-free form is
possible. Neither example contradicts the source.

An instructive valid system shows why higher stages need not individually
have a nonpositive n exponent. Take vertices 0,1,2,3, edges 01,12,23,31,
and the single immersed interval 0,1,2,3,1,0. Edge01 has multiplicity two,
the triangle edges multiplicity one. Stage1 has V-E-k=4-4-1=-1;
stage2 has V-E=2-1=1. Their sum is zero. The proof correctly multiplies
deterministic stage bounds rather than requiring every exponent <=0.

2. Fixed-stage expectation: patterns.tex 435-472

Every active chain of length h has h-1 original unmarked internal
vertices, including a chain whose marked endpoints coincide. Thus their
total is E_j-Q_j. These are distinct abstract vertices. At one of them,
fixed chain letters t,u impose exactly the two outgoing slots bar(t),u.
The turn is reduced, so random.tex 145-150 gives at most

  np*w(t,u)+O(1) = np*w(t,u)*(1+O(1/n))

possible vertex images on its prescribed side. The relative bound is
uniform because the finite positive turn weights have a positive minimum.
At the remaining unfixed vertices use n choices, and at the marked
stage-one root use one. Independently multiplying candidate counts is
an upper bound even before enforcing injectivity or consistency of all
other slots. It gives exactly

  n^(V_j-k_j) p^(E_j-Q_j) product_internal_turns w * exp(O(L/n)).

Every injective compatible vertex assignment asks for E_j distinct
undirected edge prescriptions. Distinct paths using the same edge are
not additional prescriptions. Applying random.tex 300-316 to these E_j
prescriptions yields (np)^(-E_j)*exp(o(L)). Since E_j<=H<=CL, this is
within the stated range of that lemma. Multiplying produces

  n^(V_j-E_j-k_j) p^(-Q_j) product_internal_turns w * exp(o(L)).

The number of chains is fixed once K_0,C,I are fixed, so Q_j is bounded
and p^(-Q_j)=exp(O(1))=exp(o(L)). Every weight is <=1. Deleting chain
turns at block boundaries and inside end fragments increases this upper
bound. The remaining turn product is one P(W_i) per active underlying
block, and P(W_i)<=exp(-d_i) follows from the definition of d_i. This
rederives line439. It uses distinct prescriptions once per stage and
does not misuse the traversal multiplicity as edge independence.

3. Projection of extendable stage labels: patterns.tex 424-433,474-501

Let A be the set of full abstract label assignments satisfying all
comparisons, bins and immersion conditions. Stage labels are pi_j(A),
not arbitrary labels on Delta_j. For a link component U, representation
at stage j is equivalent to max_i m_i>=j. If represented, its admissible
stage tuples are projections of assignments on all of U. Choose a block
r in U with smallest recorded d_r, whether or not it is active. Under
the supplied word-bin estimate and link-extension lemma, the full
assignments on U number at most

  exp(2 d_r-delta*s) * exp(rho_L*s*|U|)

when U has no self-link; for a self-link use only the second factor.
Projecting cannot increase a finite set's cardinality, so the same bound
holds for its stage tuples. Taking the product over components is an
upper bound: further constraints at junctions or between components
reduce the number. There are boundedly many original chains, hence
their total end-fragment positions are O(s), costing exp(O(s))=exp(o(L)).
The source's minimum-bin choice on an absent block is therefore legal.
No injection from arbitrary stage strings to absent-block strings is
being assumed; the actual direction is projection of extendable full
strings. This distinction rules out the most plausible projection flaw.

4. Stage product and algebra: patterns.tex 503-593

For every j, a full realization implies Z_j>=1. Therefore Markov gives
Pr(full realized)<=E Z_j<=B_j for every j separately, even when the
random stage realization events are correlated. Thus Pr<=min_j B_j.
For positive deterministic bounds, min_j B_j <= (product_j B_j)^(1/m_*).
This uses no product bound on random events or on E[product_j Z_j].

For a no-self-link component write S=sum_i m_i, z=max_i m_i, d=min_i d_i.
Its stage contributions sum to z(2d-delta*s)-sum_i m_i*d_i, at most
(2z-S)d-delta*s*z. If 2z-S<=0, the lower bound d>=delta*s/2 gives
-delta*s*S/2. If 2z-S>0, the component-incidence inequality gives
2z-S<=b and d<=a_0*s, while z>S/2. The result is at most
a_0*s*b-delta*s*S/2. A self-link component contributes only the negative
sum and obeys the same bound with b-term unnecessary. These sign uses
are correct at lines535-542.

Summing over components and using sum_j(V_j-E_j-k_j)<=0 yields line563,
provided the earlier block coverage estimates hold. The uniform errors
remain o(L) after summing the bounded number M of stages. With b_0<=
epsilon*H and the stated epsilon, a_0*epsilon<=delta/16, leaving more
than the asserted delta*H/4 margin after o(L), uniformly when H>=L.
The union over exp(o(L)) patterns then preserves exponential decay.
The order epsilon before K_0,C,I is respected: delta and a_0 use only
fixed weights, while M and the asymptotic threshold may use the bounds.

5. Failure ledger and limits

Attempted counterexamples: free tree interval endpoint surplus; repeated
closed traversal; positive higher-stage n exponent; a minimum-bin block
absent from a stage; repeated edge prescriptions; separate-component
constraints; loop-chain endpoints; coincident interval endpoints. Each
is handled by an explicit source hypothesis or the algebra above.

Finite check: check_incidence.py exhaustively evaluated 132,848 systems
on five small loopless graphs. It checked every generated single closed
path, every pair of generated closed paths, every single interval, and
every interval plus closed path, with individual length <=6. All local
incidence inequalities and all three stage identities/inequalities pass.
Details are in incidence_diagnostic.json. This check is scoped finite
evidence, not a proof; the general incidence proof is given in section1.

Dependencies not independently certified here: random.tex prescription
switching bound; patterns.tex grid, matching and link-word lemma; the
full-group algebraic/topological claims. Those are assigned to other
auditors. Strongest next attack is to audit a dependency or a transfer
into/out of this theorem rather than claim a fault in its stage logic.
