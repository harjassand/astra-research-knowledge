RELEASE FRONTIER: ACQUIRED ACCESS AND COHERENT PREPARATION OF PROJECTED SLATERS
2026-10-07. Main result is conditional on the correctness of a fresh imported FPRAS. The transfer proof below is explicit; no source algorithm, quantum circuit, or Lean build has executed here. External correctness and global novelty are unresolved.

1. Consequence and exact physical interface

Let A be an explicitly supplied m-by-r full-column-rank matrix over Q(i), encoded in finite binary, and let the m fermion modes be partitioned into disjoint blocks E_1,...,E_k with supplied occupation quotas q_j, 0<=q_j<=|E_j| and sum_j q_j=r. In increasing mode order, the unnormalized r-particle Slater determinant has amplitude a(S)=det A_S on r-element mode subsets S. Project to the hard occupation constraint |S cap E_j|=q_j for every j. Its norm is

  Z=sum_{S feasible} |det A_S|^2.

There is a uniform randomized classical polynomial-bit algorithm to test Z=0 exactly, relatively approximate Z when nonzero, sample its Born occupation law to any inverse-polynomial total-variation accuracy, and additively estimate any efficiently row-enumerable polynomial-row-sparse Hermitian observable with supplied polynomial norm bound. More strongly, there is a uniform randomized classical compiler producing a polynomial-size quantum circuit that prepares the normalized projected Slater within trace distance epsilon, with probability >=1-delta over compiler randomness. Runtime and gate count are polynomial in the explicit rational input length, m, epsilon^-1 and log(delta^-1); no inverse projection-success probability appears. Both statements depend on Chen-Liu Theorem 5.1 and Remark A.4.

Physical special case: m=2n, r=n, E_i={i-up,i-down}, q_i=1. This is arbitrary number-conserving Gaussian fermions, including spin mixing, Gutzwiller-projected to one particle/site. The older two separate spin Slaters are a corollary. Paired BCS/Pfaffian inputs are outside this proof.

Projection success for the normalized input is p_proj=Z/det(A* A); its relative estimate follows from the norm estimate and exact rational denominator. The exact support test is needed because full column rank does not imply Z>0. For two spin Slaters, U=V=(1,0)^T on two sites is full rank individually but projection to one particle/site vanishes.

3. Determinant-to-coefficient reduction, including complex inputs

Set f_A(x)=det(A* D_x A), D_x=diag(x). Cauchy-Binet gives
  f_A(x)=sum_{|S|=r} |det A_S|^2 x^S.
For x>0, A*D_xA is Hermitian positive definite, affine in real x. Concavity of logdet makes f_A log-concave. It is homogeneous of degree r and multi-affine with nonnegative rational coefficients. No real or complex square root needs computing.

The partition-matroid bases are the quota-feasible S. Let g(x) be their dual basis polynomial:
  g(x)=product_j e_{|E_j|-q_j}(x_{E_j}).
It is homogeneous degree m-r, multi-affine, and log-concave; elementary symmetric polynomials are real stable/Lorentzian, or this follows from the known log-concavity of matroid basis polynomials. For the spin-half q=1 blocks, g=product_i(x_i-up+x_i-down), so logconcavity follows directly by summing logarithms of linear forms.

Then [x_1...x_m]f_A g=Z. Each coefficient query is an exact rational squared minor or a binary quota test. Positive initializing bases are found by rational Gaussian elimination for A and a blockwise quota choice for g. Coefficient ranges are bounded from the input bit heights: clearing all entry denominators gives Gaussian integers whose logarithmic heights are polynomial in the input length; every nonzero determinant has squared modulus at least D^-2r and has Hadamard/permutation upper bound with polynomial logarithm. Thus log R_f is polynomial and R_g=1. These range bounds are acquired explicitly, not assumed numerical conditioning parameters.

4. Prefix support and count oracle, with no exponential tree

A binary prefix of mode decisions specifies forced-in I and forced-out J. The determinant coefficient weight is the original |det A_S|^2 if I subset S and S cap J empty, and zero otherwise. Its polynomial is obtained by derivatives on I, variable exclusion on J and multiplication by x^I; nonzero such polynomials remain log-concave. The dual quota polynomial is restricted complementarily: J forced-in, I forced-out. Original total degrees and the m-element ground set are retained, exactly as in source p24. Queries remain one original minor and O(m) membership tests; no new acquired oracle appears.

Feasibility is intersection of the represented linear matroid of A with the partition-quota matroid, with I contracted and J deleted. Exact rational rank tests and ordinary polynomial-time matroid intersection find a common base or certify none. Feasible prefix states and initializing bases are thus constructed in polynomial time. If one factor separately has empty support, feasibility already catches it. This handles all zero branches, including prefixes unreachable under the true law.

Each quantum stage invokes the SAME polynomial-size uniform count-and-feasibility program on the current prefix register. Its loops are capped by the imported every-execution bound. It does not hardwire exponentially many conditional counts. For an arbitrary opaque classical matroid oracle, coherent preparation would require an efficient coherent oracle implementation; the explicit physical partition+matrix input above needs no such assumption.

5. Random-FPRAS coherent compilation lemma, full error proof

Let p(x)=|a(x)|^2/Z on feasible mode configurations. For each depth choose a classical polynomial-length random tape s_d, shared by every prefix at that depth. Divide the tape into fixed padded independent segments for the two children and each median-amplification repetition, so every fixed prefix has the original FPRAS marginal success guarantee. At prefix h, run capped FPRAS child counts with relative tolerance eta and individual failure probability beta. Exact feasibility forces estimated count zero on empty child subtrees. Normalize nonnegative estimates to q_s(b|h); if estimates both vanish despite feasibility, use a deterministic feasible fallback. At infeasible prefixes use an arbitrary legal rotation. Hence ideal q_s is supported on the true feasible support, including when estimates fail.

For any fixed feasible h, the chance that either child count fails is <=2beta. Let bad_s(h) be that event and define
  B_s=sum_{d=0}^{m-1} sum_{|h|=d} p(h) bad_s(h).
The true p(h) is independent of the compiler tapes. Therefore E_s B_s<=2m beta. There is no union bound over all exponentially many h, and no independence between prefix errors is required.

At a good prefix, two (1+-eta) child-count estimates imply each nonzero q_b/p_b lies between (1-eta)/(1+eta) and (1+eta)/(1-eta). Thus for eta<=1/2,
  sum_b (sqrt(q_b)-sqrt(p_b))^2 <=16 eta^2.
At a bad prefix this squared distance is <=2. Let B_d=sum_{|h|=d}p(h)bad_s(h), so B_s=sum_d B_d. Define ideal controlled rotations T_d and approximate deterministic-tape rotations G_d. The exact unitary telescoping identity writes the state difference as a sum of
  G_m...G_{d+1}(G_d-T_d)T_{d-1}...T_1|0>.
At the altered stage the prefix amplitudes are TRUE sqrt(p(h)), and later approximate unitaries preserve norm. Therefore the dth difference norm is <=sqrt(16eta^2+2B_d). Summing and Cauchy-Schwarz gives
  ||state(q_s)-state(p)|| <=4m eta+sqrt(2m B_s).
Markov on E B_s<=2m beta gives, with probability>=1-delta,
  ||state(q_s)-state(p)|| <=4m eta+2m sqrt(beta/delta).
Choose eta=epsilon/(16m), beta=delta epsilon^2/(64m^2). The vector error is then <=epsilon/2. These are relative ACCURACY and FAILURE parameters respectively; amplification costs log(1/beta), not beta^-1. This sharper coherent hybrid proof requires count precision proportional to epsilon, giving epsilon^-2 FPRAS arithmetic dependence, rather than the epsilon^-4 dependence of the conservative TV-to-Hellinger route.

Independent alternative error proof retained for audit: conditional TV<=2eta on good prefixes and <=1 on bad ones; sequential maximal coupling gives TV(p,q_s)<=2m eta+B_s. Taking eta=epsilon^2/(32m), beta=delta epsilon^2/(32m) gives TV<=epsilon^2/8 with probability>=1-delta. The overlap sum sqrt(pq)>=1-TV then bounds pure trace distance<=epsilon/2. This bound is correct but has worse precision dependence.

Reversibly evaluate q_s(1|h), apply controlled rotation with amplitudes sqrt(1-q),sqrt(q), and reverse all arithmetic/count workspace before proceeding. The fixed tapes are circuit description parameters, not input-dependent retained quantum registers. Standard rational square-root/rotation approximation uses polynomial precision. Over m stages, choose uniform gate error O(epsilon/m) to bound total state-vector error by epsilon/4.

Finally reversibly compute det A_S exactly and apply its phase a(S)/|a(S)|, then uncompute. Set phase equal to one on zero determinants, wrong cardinality or otherwise infeasible computational basis strings, so the reversible function is total even under gate-synthesis leakage. Rational-complex minor arithmetic has polynomial bit length; phase can be approximated uniformly to epsilon/4 in polynomial time because nonzero Gaussian-rational minors have an explicit inverse-exponential bit-height lower bound. In increasing site-mode order this determinant already contains the fermionic sign. No pointwise numerical stability promise is needed. Rotations use uniform additive angle/operator precision, including q=0,1, rather than relative probability precision.

The ideal resulting state is sum_x sqrt(q_s(x)) exp(i arg a(x))|x>. Exact attachment of the common determinant phases preserves the vector-error bound from the positive-amplitude hybrid proof. Pure-state trace distance is at most the vector norm difference. Rotation and phase synthesis errors leave total trace distance <=epsilon. The compiler succeeds with probability>=1-delta but does not certify which random tape succeeds. Randomized high-fidelity preparation is the proven interface; deterministic certified compilation is not obtained.

6. Classical observable access and higher-order consequences

For efficiently enumerated row-sparse Hermitian O with ||O||<=M, compute local estimator X(S)=Re[(Oa)_S/a(S)] where a(S)!=0. Its exact Born expectation is <a|O|a>/Z and E_p X^2<=E_p|E_loc|^2<=||O||^2<=M^2, since terms at nodes are omitted in the squared norm. Complex phases are retained in a(T)/a(S). Large ratios do not cause an inverse-minimum-amplitude runtime in finite rational arithmetic.

TV sampling alone does not control this unbounded estimator. Clip X to [-K,K], with K=4M^2/epsilon, and request sampler TV tau=epsilon^2/(32M^2). Exact clipping bias <=M^2/K=epsilon/4. Transfer bias <=2Ktau=epsilon/4. Also E_q clip(X)^2<=M^2+2K^2tau<=2M^2. Independent median-of-means with O(M^2 epsilon^-2 log(delta^-1)) samples gives additive epsilon, polynomial cost and no inverse Z. The standard sample+amplitude-ratio estimator is prior art; the newly acquired sampler and finite-TV repair complete its interface here.

Fixed-copy permutation observables are norm1 and 1-sparse. Therefore subsystem purity Tr(rho_B^2)=<SWAP_B> and fixed-order Renyi moments have polynomial ADDITIVE classical estimates on arbitrary subsystems. Additive Renyi entropy requires relative moment precision; volume-law inverse purity can be exponential. Polynomially sized local reduced density matrices, sparse Hamiltonian energies/variances, and correlation functions are accessible. The parameter/ground-state optimization problem and a useful overlap/gap certificate are not acquired by this theorem.

Grover-Rudolph, quant-ph/0208112, https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0208112, full two-page local grover_rudolph.pdf: sequential conditional-probability preparation is established, and page 2 explicitly allows probabilistic integration with a random-bit ancilla. Havlicek, arXiv:2201.09128v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2201.09128v2: sparse-observable estimation from sample+amplitude-ratio access is established. We acquire that access from explicit projected-Slater inputs. Source theorem/direct product-DPP norm primitive is already Chen-LiuC.13, not ours.
Petrica et al., arXiv:2009.00064, https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00064: projected mean-field states and practical MPS constructions are established; this does not by itself provide the uniform inverse-success-free finite-bit preparation guarantee above.
Zhang-Vishwanath, arXiv:1209.2424v2, https://arxiv.org/html/1209.2424v2: determinant-square projected Chern states and VMC entanglement computations are established. This physical family is not newly invented here.

Ohsaka-Matsuoka, arXiv:2111.14148, https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14148, local dpp_product_prior.pdf: hard approximation for a triple product of principal-minor weights is prior art. The elementary support obstruction is synchronized occupancy across three Slaters: choose each matrix row to be a coordinate unit vector encoding one coordinate of a 3D-matching hyperedge. A common r-subset is a nonzero minor of all 3 matrices exactly when it is a perfect 3D matching. This projector (all 3 species simultaneously empty/occupied at each hyperedge) has nonmatroid support and lies outside the theorem. Three or more flavors projected to exactly one mode/site remain a SINGLE Slater plus partition constraint and are INSIDE the theorem; blanket three-parton hardness would be incorrect.

Paired BCS generating polynomials need not be log-concave: x1*x2+x3*x4 has two positive Hessian eigenvalues. Generic Pfaffian input is outside the determinant proof. A sum of Slaters has interference cross terms and is outside the acquired count oracle.

Classical-representation boundary: pair k sites in the first half with k sites in the second half; each pair has two occupied orbitals, one up and one down, equally supported across its two sites. Hard single-occupancy projection gives k singlets and success probability 2^-k. Across the first-half/last-half cut it has 2^k equal Schmidt coefficients. Any fixed-order MPS of bond D has squared overlap<=D/2^k, so pure trace error epsilon requires D>=(1-epsilon^2)2^k. This witnesses coexistence of polynomial quantum preparation and an exponential MPS bond requirement; it is not itself a newly invented singlet family.

The paper prepares arbitrary BCS inputs by Bloch-Messiah circuits and amplifies Gutzwiller projection, requiring O(1/sqrt(W)) input queries for projected weight W (with logarithmic tolerance factor). It explicitly discusses a projected Fermi-sea subclass and exponentially decreasing W in its benchmark. This is a concrete equally informed comparator: our conditional theorem removes inverse W for explicitly supplied number-conserving Slater+partition inputs, not arbitrary paired BCS. We do not claim an advantage in optimizing the input or in all finite-size gate counts.

The bounded Pfaffian attack did not produce a generic weighted normalizer FPRAS, coherent-preparation theorem, or a proved obstruction to such a theorem. Logconcavity failure is not a hardness theorem. Spin-singlet BCS can be particle-hole transformed on all down modes to a Slater with orbitals [F;I], but physical one-per-site becomes synchronized up/hole occupancy (zero or two per site), not our partition constraint. A different exact support structure was found and primary-sourced: Tomohiro Koana and Magnus Wahlstrom, Faster Algorithms on Linear Delta-Matroids, STACS2025, DOI10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.62, https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.62 ; local delta_matroid.pdf/.txt (19 pages), full version arXiv:2402.11596. Corollary4 and preceding intersection reduction give randomized polynomial field-operation decision and witness algorithms for represented linear delta-matroid intersection. Source p10 explicitly permits efficient rational-to-finite-field conversion. This is known support prior art, not a new counting theorem.

For an even-parity BCS chart with explicitly supplied skew F over Q(i), nonzero amplitudes Pf(F[S]) form the linear delta-matroid D(F), since det F[S]=Pf(F[S])^2. Let P be the unit skew matrix on the disjoint physical pairs and R choose one fixed mode per pair. D(P) consists of unions of complete pairs; twisting by R gives exactly-one-per-pair subsets. Thus projected nonzero support is D(F) intersect (D(P) triangle R). This yields a randomized polynomial support test/witness, with an explicitly checkable nonzero Pfaffian; odd site count is immediately infeasible by parity. It rules out casually asserting a zero-support NP barrier here. It supplies no weighted count or conditional-sampling law.

Finite-bit reduction for the complex rational support observation: clear denominators to H=DF over Gaussian integers. Bound all real/imaginary parts of principal Pfaffians by C=(2n)! (2T)^n, with T bounding entry parts. Choose an odd prime p>max(D,2C^2); log p is polynomial in input length. Embed i into F_(p^2), or F_p when possible. Any nonzero Gaussian-integral Pfaffian has positive norm below p, so its reduction cannot vanish; zeros remain zero. This preserves every principal support simultaneously. Random prime search with primality verification and finite-field construction has polynomial bit cost, and the delta-matroid algorithm then has polynomial bit operations; probability amplification is available, while support decision is randomized rather than an exact deterministic certificate. We have not implemented this reduction or independently audited the complete delta-matroid algorithm.

Beenakker-DiVincenzo-Emary-Kindermann quant-ph/0401066 and DiVincenzo-Terhal quant-ph/0403031 establish universal computation from fermionic linear optics with interleaved charge/parity measurements. These are primary-sourced different contracts: sequential/adaptive measurements and gates do not constitute a reduction from arbitrary computation to one fixed single-occupancy-projected BCS layer. No such hardness inference is made.
