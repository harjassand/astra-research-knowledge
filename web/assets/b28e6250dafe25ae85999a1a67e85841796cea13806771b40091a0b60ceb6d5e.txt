## 1. The highest-ceiling attack: KLS

The KLS target can be expressed through the optimal Poincaré constant \(P(\mu)\), defined by
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu}(f)
\le P(\mu)\int |\nabla f|^2\,d\mu.
\]
The desired conclusion is
\[
P(\mu)\le C\|\operatorname{Cov}(\mu)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\]
for every log-concave probability measure, with one dimension-independent constant \(C\).

The changed premise was OpenAI family 093’s proposed dimension-free log-Sobolev theorem for **subgaussian** log-concave measures. I reconstructed its Gaussian-observation and critical-gap arguments to determine whether its mechanisms survive with only covariance control—the assumption needed for KLS. The distinction between those assumptions is essential. 

### What carries over

I obtained the following quantitative covariance-only variant of the source’s heat-flow calculation.

Let \(\mu\) be a centered, compactly supported log-concave probability with a Lebesgue density. Write
\[
P_0=P(\mu),\qquad
\sigma^2=\|\operatorname{Cov}(\mu)\|_{\mathrm{op}},
\qquad
\mu_s=\mu*N(0,sI).
\]
Then, for \(0<s<P_0\),
\[
\boxed{
P_0\le P(\mu_s)
\le P_0+\frac{\sigma^2s}{P_0-s}.
}
\]

This is a source-derived refinement, not a claimed independent replacement for family 093. Its proof uses the source’s gradient-eigenfunction identities and heat-flow contact calculation, but **does not require the asserted subgaussian log-Sobolev theorem**. 

The central calculation is short enough to show. Put \(P_s=P(\mu_s)\), let \(\varphi\) be a first mean-zero eigenfunction, and set
\[
w=\nabla\varphi,\qquad
\lambda=P_s^{-1},\qquad
K_s=\nabla^2(-\log\mu_s).
\]
Commuting a derivative with the generator and applying Poincaré to \(w\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\langle w,K_sw\rangle
\le \lambda|\mathbb Ew|^2.
\]

For \(Y=X+\sqrt{s}G\), let \(b_s(Y)=\mathbb E[X\mid Y]\). Coordinate tests give
\[
(1-\lambda s)\mathbb E[\varphi Y]
=\mathbb E[\varphi b_s].
\]
Conditional Jensen supplies
\[
\mathbb E[b_sb_s^{T}]\preceq \sigma^2I,
\]
so the mean of \(w\) is controlled without a dimension factor. Combining this with the heat-flow derivative formula yields
\[
P_s'\le \frac{\sigma^2P_s}{(P_s-s)^2}
\le\frac{\sigma^2P_0}{(P_0-s)^2}.
\]
Integration gives the displayed bound.

Consequently, a hypothetical KLS-counterexample sequence normalized by
\[
P(\mu)=1,\qquad
\|\operatorname{Cov}(\mu)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0
\]
would satisfy
\[
P(\mu_s)\longrightarrow1
\qquad\text{for every fixed }s<1.
\]

This supplies a **covariance-only spectral plateau**: adding a fixed amount of Gaussian noise does not remove the anomalously large Poincaré constant. It also supplies the small-mean and small-curvature estimates needed to enter substantial portions of the source’s later construction.

### The unresolved step is specific

The source’s final contradiction uses Gaussian observations whose covariance depends on a matrix field \(R(Y)\):
\[
Z_T\mid Y\sim N\!\left(0,I+f_L(TR(Y)^2)\right).
\]
Its upper bound on scale-integrated mutual information invokes a **log-Sobolev inequality for the observation likelihoods**. The covariance-only argument above gives Poincaré control, not that entropy inequality. 

I tested the natural replacements. Applying Poincaré to square-root likelihoods produces an affinity or Hellinger bound, but that bounded quantity does not automatically reproduce the additive information estimate over diffuse spectral blocks. Applying it to raw likelihoods introduces uncontrolled likelihood-ratio terms.

**I did not prove the required replacement. KLS is not solved.** The useful change is that the investigation reached a precise information inequality, rather than stopping at the general suggestion to “extend subgaussianity to covariance.”

## 2. The stochastic-reaction attack: an exact boundary correction

A second high-consequence target was the positive-recurrence conjecture for stochastic weakly reversible reaction networks. Astra’s deterministic permanence candidate changes the deterministic side, but the stochastic process uses falling factorials, which can vanish at low populations. The existing stochastic literature explicitly distinguishes these difficulties and proves recurrence under additional structural assumptions.  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08967v2)

I derived an exact discrete correction rather than approximating those falling factorials by monomials.

For a population vector \(x\) and source complex \(y\), write
\[
x^{\underline y}
=\prod_i\frac{x_i!}{(x_i-y_i)!},
\]
with value zero when the reaction is disabled. Define
\[
F(x)=
\min_{\substack{y\in\mathcal C\\y\le x}}
\log\bigl((x-y)!\bigr),
\qquad
M(x)=\max_{y\le x}x^{\underline y},
\]
where vector factorials mean products over coordinates.

For every enabled reaction \(y\to y'\),
\[
\boxed{
F(x-y+y')-F(x)
\le
\log\frac{M(x)}{x^{\underline y}}.
}
\]

The proof is exact. After the reaction, \(y'\) is available and leaves the same residual population:
\[
(x-y+y')-y'=x-y.
\]
That residual population is one of the candidates in the new minimum.

Thus **a source maximizing \(x^{\underline y}\) cannot increase \(F\)**, including at boundary states where some molecule counts are zero.

The same argument gives an exponential bound for upward changes in the embedded reaction-event chain:
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
e^{F(X_{j+1})-F(X_j)}
\mid X_j
\right]\le C,
\]
where \(C\) depends only on the finite rate constants. In particular,
\[
\Pr\!\left(
\max_{j\le N}F(X_j)
\ge F(X_0)+N\log C+u
\right)\le e^{-u}.
\]

This provides a discrete mechanism that handles an actual obstruction: the most active available source can change when a reaction creates or consumes the last few molecules of another species.

But it does **not** supply negative drift. Fast reaction cycles can preserve this corrected potential, while slower reactions move between their effective states. Controlling those transitions—and their elapsed-time costs—remains necessary for recurrence. A bound measured in reaction events is not a nonexplosion theorem in physical time.

## 3. A stronger stochastic transfer is actually false

The investigation also exposed a quantifier error that a broader theorem must avoid:

> A common deterministic attractor in one stoichiometric class does not imply a common tight family of stochastic stationary distributions in that class.

Here is an explicit example with **infinite, irreducible, positive recurrent stochastic components**, not merely absorbing states.

Take
\[
2C\longrightarrow A+B+C
\longrightarrow3A+2B
\longrightarrow2C,
\qquad
2C\rightleftarrows2C+D,
\]
with every rate constant equal to one.

This network has one linkage class, deficiency zero, and the complex-balanced equilibrium
\[
(A,B,C,D)=(1,1,1,1).
\]
Its positive deterministic trajectories in the class \(B+C=2\) converge to that equilibrium by the established single-linkage global-attractor theorem. This is inherited classical theory, not a new claim from the OpenAI release. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.0761)

For every integer \(n\ge0\), however, the stochastic process has the closed class
\[
\Gamma_n=
\left\{
(n,0,2,d),\
(n+1,1,1,d),\
(n+3,2,0,d):
d\ge0
\right\}.
\]

The three displayed population phases form a directed cycle. At its first phase, \(D\) undergoes immigration and death. Each \(\Gamma_n\) is infinite, irreducible, nonexplosive and positive recurrent.

The key is a nonlinear, pathwise stochastic invariant:
\[
\boxed{
A-\frac{B(B+1)}2=n.
}
\]
It is not a linear stoichiometric conservation law and is not conserved by the deterministic differential equation.

The stationary distribution is explicit: a three-phase distribution, with weights proportional to
\[
\left(
\frac12,\,
\frac1{n+1},\,
\frac1{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}
\right),
\]
times a Poisson distribution of mean one for \(D\). Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi_n}A\ge n.
\]

All these stationary distributions belong to the same linear stoichiometric plane \(B+C=2\). Each class even contains a strictly positive initial population, such as
\[
(n+1,1,1,1).
\]
Nevertheless, the stationary distributions escape every common compact set as \(n\to\infty\).

This **does not refute componentwise positive recurrence**. Every component in the example is positive recurrent. It refutes the stronger, tempting transfer of deterministic *classwise-uniform attraction* into stochastic *classwise-uniform tightness*.

The underlying product-form mechanism is established prior work. The example is useful research capital because it makes the failed quantifier explicit; I have not established historical novelty for it. The relevant stochastic literature already warns that deterministic and stochastic long-term properties cannot be identified without additional conditions. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.08967v2)

## 4. The previous Gaussian programme was escalated, not merely polished

I also attacked its remaining fixed-photon-number obstacle through coefficient inequalities.

For a symmetric nonnegative matrix \(B\), define
\[
a_k(B)=
\sum_{|S|=2k}\operatorname{haf}(B_S)^2.
\]
These are the unnormalized collision-free weights at a prescribed photon number. A sufficiently strong coefficient inequality would make a fugacity-based sampling route plausible.

Two natural exact inequalities failed.

For the adjacency matrix of \(K_{12}\),
\[
a_0=1,\qquad a_1=66,\qquad a_2=4455,
\]
so
\[
a_1^2=4356<4455=a_0a_2.
\]
Ordinary log-concavity is false.

The natural normalization by
\[
c_k=\frac{\binom{2k}{k}}{4^k}
\]
also fails. For
\[
B=\operatorname{diag}
\left(10\,\operatorname{Adj}(K_{1602}),
\operatorname{Adj}(K_{1602})\right),
\]
the exact calculation gives
\[
\frac{20a_2^2}{18a_1a_3}
=
\frac{6774776316150}{6774776323679}<1,
\]
contradicting log-concavity of \(a_k/c_k\). Scaling \(B\) by \(1/32040\) makes it a valid contractive pure-Gaussian parameter without changing the inequality’s sign.

These calculations use closed-form complete-graph matching counts, not an enumeration of a 3204-mode system.

The diagnostic lesson matters: the first random tests found no ordinary violation among 1,250 fixtures; the next found no normalized violation among 2,000 fixtures. Structured exact examples overturned both apparent successes.

**These are counterexamples to proposed lemmas, not proofs that fixed-total Gaussian simulation is hard.** Weaker approximate coefficient bounds remain possible.

## 5. Two other high-ceiling compositions were checked

**Permanent versus determinant.** Family 108’s cubic lower bound uses a smooth affine restriction with degree proportional to the permanent order and quadratically many variables, followed by a lower bound proportional to degree times variable count. 

That mechanism cannot simply be iterated into a superpolynomial lower bound. The smooth Fermat form
\[
x_1^r+\cdots+x_d^r
\]
has a formula, and hence an affine determinant representation, of order \(O(rd)\). Smoothness, degree and dimension alone therefore cannot force a generally larger order. An affine restriction of an \(m\)-permanent has degree at most \(m\) and at most \(m^2\) essential variables. A stronger result needs additional structure or a different invariant, not another application of the same estimate.

**Nonhyperlinear versus word-hyperbolic groups.** I examined the September 2026 Alekseev–Liu–Thom preprint’s spectral-gap and relative-commutant mechanism as new input, rather than treating its stated nonhyperlinear-group construction as an untouched target. The proof was read selectively, not independently certified. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37761v1)

Its theorem does not automatically upgrade Astra’s nonsofic hyperbolic candidate. Nonhyperlinearity of a quotient does not imply nonhyperlinearity of the covering group; a group amalgam need not be word-hyperbolic; and an approximate unitary representation is not an approximate permutation representation. I did not obtain a valid construction bridging those distinctions.
