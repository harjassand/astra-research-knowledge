1. INDEPENDENT FULL TENSOR GENERATOR DERIVATION

For a positive trace-one local density rho write k(rho)=rho^tensorN and,
for a traceless Hermitian T,
  t_T=Tr rho T,
  V_T={T,rho}/2-t_T rho,
  R_T=-i[T,rho],
  B_T=N t_T+V_T.grad.
Normalized local filtering e^(tT/2)rho e^(tT/2)/Tr(...) differentiates to
V_T and its product trace-log derivative is N t_T. Rotations differentiate
to R_T. Hence, on FULL tensor matrices,
  {F_T,k}/2=B_T k, -i[F_T,k]=R_T.grad k.
Two constant matrix actions commute with density differentiation. Therefore
  {F_T^2,k}/2=(B_T^2-(R_T.grad)^2/4)k.                 (F3)
To see all factors: B_T^2k={F_T,{F_T,k}}/4, while
(R_T.grad)^2k=-[F_T,[F_T,k]]; the sum of the two nested expressions is
2(F_T^2k+kF_T^2). Polarization gives all mixed symmetric coefficients.

Let C=alpha I+E and Z_C=sum_ab C_ab{T_a,T_b}/2. Expanding (F3), the exact
quantum sandwich M_K X={K,X}/2 satisfies M_K k=Gk, with
  G=U+a_rho.grad+D_C:Hess/N,
  D_C=sum_ab C_ab[V_a tensor V_b-R_a tensor R_b/4],
  a_rho=2sum_ab C_ab t_aV_b
        +(1/N)sum_ab C_ab[(DV_b)V_a-(DR_b)R_a/4]+V_B,
  U=(N-1)t^T C t+Tr rho Z_C+N Tr rho B.                (F4)
The Frechet derivatives are
  DV_T[Z]={T,Z}/2-Tr(ZT)rho-t_TZ,
  DR_T[Z]=-i[T,Z].
These are explicit finite polynomials in real traceless Hermitian coordinates.
U=Tr(K k) exactly: independent-site terms have trace t_at_b, equal-site
terms have trace Tr rho{T_a,T_b}/2. No low-body replacement enters (F4).

Normalization reconciliation. c04_s01 uses HS1, as here. c04_s02 uses
Tr T_aT_b=delta_ab/2, doubles its filter vector, and uses epsilon D/4 in

2. FULL-BASIS PRINCIPAL IDENTITY AND EXPLICIT NEIGHBORHOOD

For a traceless Hermitian test Z, let t=Tr rho Z and Y=Z-tI. The matrices
  M={rho,Z}/2-t rho, R=-i[rho,Z]
are traceless. HS1 completeness gives
  <Z,D_I Z>=Tr M^2-(1/4)Tr R^2
     =Tr rho Z rho Z-2t Tr rho^2Z+t^2Tr rho^2
     =Tr rho Y rho Y>=0.                             (F5)
The last expression is ||sqrt(rho)Ysqrt(rho)||_HS^2. On rho>=epsilon I,
it is >=epsilon^2||Y||_HS^2>=epsilon^2||Z||_HS^2, because Tr Z=0 implies
||Y||_HS^2=||Z||_HS^2+d t^2. This needs the COMPLETE SU(d) basis; it is
not a statement for a proper subgroup or arbitrary representation.

As coefficient-to-tangent linear maps, ||V||<=2 and ||R||<=2:
||{T,rho}/2||_HS<=||T||_HS, |Tr rho T| ||rho||_HS<=||T||_HS, and
||[T,rho]||_HS<=2||T||_HS on positive trace-one rho. Hence
  |<Z,D_E Z>|<=5||E|| ||Z||_HS^2.                    (F6)
With delta_star from (F1), on rho>=eta I,
  D_C>=alpha eta^2 I-5delta_star I
        >=(7alpha eta^2/8)I.
On the larger neighborhood rho>=eta I/2,
  D_C>=(alpha eta^2/8)I.                             (F7)
Therefore the unique positive covariance square root is smooth and locally
Lipschitz in a neighborhood of the entire compact stopping domain. It is a
supplied, positive numerical margin; no unknown-gap test is used.
Also delta_star<alpha/40, since eta<1, so alpha I/2<=C<=2alpha I.

Specific repair to root's earlier radius delta=alpha eta^2/10: that radius
DOES certify the stopping region, margin alpha eta^2/2, and a neighborhood
down to 3eta/4, margin alpha eta^2/16. It does NOT certify eta/2 by the same

eta/2, exactly as requested in root's newer strengthening. Either approach

3. STOPPED POSITIVE LAW AND LOGDET CANCELLATION

Start rho_0=I/d; eta<d^-d<=1/d, so it lies in the stopping region. Until
tau=first time lambda_min(rho_t)=eta, solve the real-coordinate Ito SDE
  d rho_t=a_rho(rho_t)dt+sqrt(2D_C(rho_t)/N)dW_t.        (F8)
Every drift/noise direction is traceless Hermitian. Thus trace remains one
and positivity holds up to the first hit by continuity. Existence/uniqueness
until tau follows from (F7) and compact-region smooth coefficients. Freeze
both rho and the positive weight
  w_t=exp(int_0^(t wedge tau) U(rho_s)ds)
after tau. Every endpoint has rho>=eta I and gives a positive product.

For ell=logdet rho, D ell[Z]=Tr rho^-1Z and
D^2ell[Z,W]=-Tr rho^-1Z rho^-1W. Directly,
  V_T ell=-d t_T, R_T ell=0,
  V_a t_b=Tr rho{T_a,T_b}/2-t_at_b.
In vector-field form the Markov generator from (F4) is
  L=2sum_ab C_ab t_aV_b+(1/N)sum_ab C_ab(V_aV_b-R_aR_b/4)+V_B.
Therefore ALL inverse-density terms cancel exactly:
  L ell=-d(2-1/N)t^TCt-(d/N)Tr rho Z_C-d Tr rho B,
  d<ell-martingale>_t=(2d^2/N)t^TCt dt.                (F9)
The cancellation is algebraic, not an estimate valid only away from rank
deficiency. In particular the following bounds do not worsen when eta shrinks.

HS completeness also gives ||t||^2=Tr rho^2-1/d<=(d-1)/d and
sum_a T_a^2=(d^2-1)I/d. Since C<=2alpha I,
Z_C<=2alpha(d^2-1)I/d, so for every N>=1,
  |L ell|<=beta=2alpha(d-1)(d+3)+dB0,
  <ell-martingale>_1<=4alpha d(d-1)/N.                (F10)
First quadratic drift term costs at most 4alpha(d-1); the equal-site term
costs at most 2alpha(d^2-1)/N; and the field costs dB0. Their sum is beta.

At a hit, det rho_tau<=eta, since every other eigenvalue is <=1. The
initial ell is -d log d. The drift integral is at least -beta, so with
eta=exp[-dlogd-beta-L], any hit by time1 forces the scalar martingale to
reach -L. The continuous exponential-martingale maximal inequality gives
  P(tau<=1)<=exp[-N L^2/(8alpha d(d-1))].              (F11)
This is the unweighted law. Its positive trace weight is charged below.

4. STOPPED DUHAMEL, OPERATOR COST AND NORMALIZATION

The full-tensor completeness relation is
  sum_a T_a tensor T_a=Swap-I/d.
Thus
  Q_I=N(d^2-1)I/d+sum_(i!=j)(Swap_ij-I/d)
       <=N(d-1)(N+d)I/d.
As C>=0 and C<=2alpha I, diagonalizing C gives 0<=Q_C<=2alpha Q_I.
Consequently
  ||K_N||<=h_N=2alpha(d-1)(N+d)/d+NB0<=H1 N           (F12)
for every N>=1. This is an ordinary operator norm; full trace norms do not
introduce an extra d^N factor.

Let W_t=E[w_t k(rho_(t wedge tau))]. Ito with (F4) gives the exact residual
  W_t'-M_K W_t=-E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_K k(rho_tau)].
There is no reflection or boundary local time: both coordinates and weight
are frozen after first hit. Duhamel therefore yields
  e^K/d^N-W_1=int_0^1 e^((1-t)M_K)
                  E[1_(tau<=t)w_tau M_K k(rho_tau)]dt.
The positive product kernel has trace one, ||M_K k||_1<=h_N, quantum sandwich
norm is <=exp((1-t)h_N), and w_tau<=exp(tau h_N). Since tau<=t,
  ||e^K/d^N-W_1||_1<=h_N exp(h_N)P(tau<=1).            (F13)
This explains why the exponent is h_N, rather than 2h_N.

The normalized trace of K_N is Tr C/d>=0: distinct-site terms vanish by
tracelessness and the local field has zero mean. Scalar spectral Jensen
gives g=Tr(e^K/d^N)>=1. W_1 has positive trace, since all weights are
positive and bounded between exp(-h_N) and exp(h_N). For positive G,W,
  T(G/TrG,W/TrW)<=||G-W||_1/TrG.
Indeed normalize once with denominator TrG and use
|TrG-TrW|<=||G-W||_1 for the normalization term. Hence
sigma_N=W_1/TrW_1 is a specified positive IID-product mixture and
  T(rho_N,sigma_N)<=h_N exp[h_N-N L^2/a],
  a=8alpha d(d-1).                                   (F14)
The target is the entire d^N-by-d^N density, not a marginal or pressure.

5. ELEMENTARY FULLY SEPARABLE BALL -- INDEPENDENT CONSTRUCTION

Let D=d^N and extend the local basis by T_0=I/sqrt d. Tensor products
B_mu=tensor_i T_(mu_i) form a Hermitian HS-orthonormal basis of M_D. For a
nonidentity multiindex mu with active support size m>=1, every active local
matrix A_i=T_(mu_i) is traceless and ||A_i||_op<=||A_i||_HS=1.
Thus (I+s_i A_i)/d, s_i=+-1, are density matrices.

For either parity sign s=+-1, average the tensor product of these local
states over all 2^(m-1) sign strings with product s, and use I/d at inactive
sites. Expanding the tensor product kills every nonempty proper subset of
active signs; only the identity and full-support term survive. The result is
  I/D+s d^-N (tensor_active A_i tensor_inactive I)
      =I/D+s d^(-(N+m)/2)B_mu.                       (F15)
For m=1 the one-sign formula gives the same result. These are fully separable
states. Since m<=N, d^(-(N+m)/2)>=d^-N, and mixing with I/D proves the
smaller vertices I/D+-d^-N B_mu are fully separable as well.

Any traceless Hermitian X has real coefficients X=sum_(mu!=0)c_mu B_mu.
Cauchy and Schatten norm monotonicity give
  sum|c_mu|<=sqrt(D^2-1)||X||_HS<=D||X||_1.
If ||X||_1<=D^-2, that sum is <=D^-1=d^-N. Expressing I/D+X as the convex
combination of the signed smaller vertices, with weights |c_mu|/d^-N,
and the unused weight at I/D, proves
  ||X||_1<=d^-2N => I/d^N+X is fully separable.          (F16)
This even proves positivity without assuming it separately. It is a
conservative ball, not the largest known ball. No novelty is claimed for
separable-ball/parity arguments.

6. WHITE MASS AND EXACT ABSORPTION

Every stopped local endpoint rho>=eta I has
  rho=d eta(I/d)+(1-d eta)rho',
  rho'=(rho-eta I)/(1-d eta)>=0, Tr rho'=1.
Product expansion and positive mixing give the EXACT decomposition
  sigma_N=w I/d^N+(1-w)tau_sep,
  w=(d eta)^N, tau_sep fully separable.               (F17)
The weight w is the same in every product branch, so normalization by the
positive endpoint weight does not change it. The remainder contains products
with some factors rho' and some I/d; it is fully separable but need not be an
IID-product mixture. This distinction is essential.

An explicit boundary check: for two qubits let rho=diag(3/4,1/4), eta=1/8,
w=(2eta)^2=1/16 and tau=(rho^tensor2-w I/4)/(1-w). This diagonal positive
state is fully separable. For Z=diag(1,-1), however,
  Tr tau(Z tensor Z)-[Tr tau(Z tensor I)]^2=-4/225<0.
Any mixture of identical products has this covariance equal to the variance
of its local mean Z and hence nonnegative. Thus even peeling white mass from
one IID product can produce a remainder outside the IID-product-mixture cone.
This is not a counterexample to the full-separability theorem; it explains
why its stronger IID conclusion cannot be inferred from the absorption proof.

If Delta=rho_N-sigma_N and ||Delta||_1<=w d^-2N=(eta/d)^N, then
  rho_N=w(I/d^N+Delta/w)+(1-w)tau_sep.
Equation (F16) makes the first state fully separable, proving full
separability of rho_N. Since ||Delta||_1=2T, the sufficient condition is
  2T(rho_N,sigma_N)<=(eta/d)^N.                       (F18)
It cannot be replaced by a mere error that tends to zero; the exponential
white weight and separable ball both have to be charged.

7. EXPLICIT ALL-N ABSORPTION CONSTANTS IN (F1)

For (F1), the chosen L is strictly greater than the positive root of
L^2=a(L+q), so L^2/a>L+q. Also for every N>=1,
  log(2h_N)<=log max(2H1,1)+log N
     <=N[1+log max(2H1,1)].
Together with h_N<=H1N and (F14), this gives
  log(2T)<=N[H1+1+log max(2H1,1)-L^2/a]
          <-N[L+c0+log d]
          =-N[z+log d]=log[(eta/d)^N].                (F19)
If T=0, (F18) is immediate. Otherwise the displayed logarithmic comparison
is legal and proves strict (F18). This completes the explicit all-N theorem
(F1)-(F2), including N=1 (which of course is always fully separable).

The shrinking-cutoff proof also yields eventual exact full separability:
if eta_N is polynomially small and T<=exp[-cN(logN)^2], then
2T<(eta_N/d)^N for all sufficiently large N. The fixed-ball theorem is
stronger in uniformity: any supplied E_N->0 eventually falls inside one
fixed delta_star, with all bounded B_N included. A computable N0 still needs
a known convergence bound if the parameters vary nonconstructively.

For the original critical qubit convention J=sum sigma/2, HS1 F=sqrt2J and
Q_I=2J^2. The perturbation Q_A(J)/N^(3/2) corresponds to E=A/(2sqrt N),
and the local field is B=b.sigma/(2N^(3/4)). Thus M_N=o(sqrt N) and bounded
B_N/N^(3/4) give eventual FULL separability through (F1)-(F2), while neither
an exact IID decomposition nor the universal reverse decoder rate follows.
