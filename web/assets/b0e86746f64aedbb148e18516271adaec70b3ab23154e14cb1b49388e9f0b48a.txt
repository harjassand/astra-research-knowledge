1. EXACT SECTOR POSTERIOR -- INCLUDING PHYSICAL MULTIPLICITY

Let P_b project on spin j_b=N/2-b, d_b=binom(N,b)-binom(N,b-1),
D_b=(2j_b+1)d_b, and G(x)=sum_b x_b P_b/D_b for a simplex vector x.
For f_m=exp[-delta m^2/N+hm], z_b=sum_(m=-j_b)^j_b f_m,
c_b=z_b/(2j_b+1), beta_x=sum_b x_b c_b, the eigenvalue of Phi_F(G(x))
at each (b,m,multiplicity-label) is
 x_b f_m/[d_b(2j_b+1)beta_x].
Every m has d_b labels. Defining Q_b(x)=x_b c_b/beta_x gives the same
eigenvalue as Q_b(x) f_m/(d_b z_b). Thus the normalized conditional state
Gamma_b has eigenvalues f_m/(d_b z_b), total trace one, independent of x.
These conditional states have orthogonal supports. Therefore EXACTLY
 T(Phi_F(G(x)),Phi_F(G(y)))=TV(Q(x),Q(y)).
There is no unweighted regular-representation trace or filter-condition upper
bound substituted for this equality. Direct mixtures have the SAME Gamma_b,
so the metric also applies to sum_k v_k Phi_F(K_k) through its sector vector.

For exact isotropic K sector columns A_bk, let
Z_k=sum_b A_bk c_b and C_bk=A_bk c_b/Z_k. All true Z_k,c_b are positive.
The positive heat/subset existence hull G(q)=sum w_k K_k transforms into

Hence the target belongs to the TRUE normalized filtered column hull.

guards are needed for interval division. This distinction is explicit in

Positive weighted interval sums and positive-denominator interval ratios
enclose the true Q and candidate sector vector. For each coordinate the
difference lies in its interval; half the sum of largest absolute endpoint
values is a valid trace-distance upper bound by Section1. Correlations between
interval quantities can widen this bound but cannot invalidate it.

For completeness, its posterior precision can also be made coupling-independent:
if an unnormalized nonnegative vector has total perturbation<=u and exact
normalizer>=L0, then with u<=L0/2 the sum of normalized coordinate interval
widths is bounded by a constant times u/L0. For columns L0>=2^-2N and raw
sum widths O(tau); for the target L0>=2^-N and raw sum widths O(2^N tau).
Hence O(N+log1/eps) scalar bits make the posterior overestimate arbitrarily
small. With a candidate satisfying the theorem's slack margin, certification

may remain inconclusive; rejecting/increasing precision is not a solver claim.

Reject non-simplex rational candidates. A numerical solver's status does not
