SU(m) UNIFORM ALL-T CASIMIR PROJECTOR COUNT
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally proved for every irreducible carrier; constants are
explicit and deliberately loose. No sharpness or novelty claim.

1. CLAIM, NORMALIZATION, AND QUANTIFIERS

Fix m>=2 and G=SU(m). Let lambda be any dominant integral highest weight,
V_lambda its irreducible carrier, d_lambda=dim V_lambda, and let G act on
End(V_lambda) by conjugation. The carrier need not be regular. Choose
Hermitian generators with

  Tr_fund(T_A T_B)=delta_AB/2,
  L(X)=sum_A [T_A,[T_A,X]].

Use the type-A weight inner product with long roots of squared length 2.
Then the Casimir on V_nu is

  c2(nu)=(nu,nu+2rho)/2,

and agrees with L under the stated generator normalization. Put

  r=m-1,                 rank(A_(m-1)),
  p=m(m-1)/2,             number of positive roots,
  q=p-r=(m-1)(m-2)/2,
  s_m=||rho||^2=m(m^2-1)/12.

Write m_lambda(nu) for the multiplicity of V_nu in End(V_lambda), and

  D_lambda(T)=sum_(nu dominant, c2(nu)<=T)
                    m_lambda(nu) dim(V_nu),       T>=0,

so D counts full isotypic dimensions, including every copy. Define

  C_count(m)=m! 5^((p+r)/2) (1+6s_m+4s_m^2)^(q/2),
  C_support(m)=2^(3p),
  C_m=max(C_count(m),C_support(m)).

Then for every dominant lambda and every real T>=0,

  D_lambda(T) <= C_m (1+T)^p.                                  (1)

The same constant works for all carrier weights lambda, including the
trivial representation. It depends only on m and the stated normalization.
The count is an all-T statement; there is no stable-range cutoff.

2. MULTIPLICITY-TO-ZERO-WEIGHT INJECTION

Let V_nu[0] be the zero-weight subspace and z_nu=dim V_nu[0]. The key
finite-dimensional inequality is

  m_lambda(nu) <= z_nu.                                        (2)

Here is a direct proof. Since representations are semisimple, multiplicity
can be read from quotient-oriented Hom and then curried:

  m_lambda(nu)
   =dim Hom_G(V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*,V_nu)
   =dim Hom_G(V_lambda^*,V_lambda^* tensor V_nu).                (3)

The second equality is Hom(A tensor B,C) ~= Hom(B,A^* tensor C), with
A=V_lambda and B=V_lambda^*. Let mu=lambda^* and let v_mu be a nonzero
highest-weight vector. For phi in the last Hom space,
w=phi(v_mu) is a highest vector of total weight mu. Decompose by the first
factor's weight:

  w=sum_(beta in Q_+) w_beta,
  w_beta in V_mu[mu-beta] tensor V_nu[beta],                     (4)

where Q_+ is the nonnegative integer span of the simple roots. The beta=0
term is v_mu tensor p_phi for a unique p_phi in V_nu[0]. This defines a
linear map phi -> p_phi.

To prove injectivity, suppose p_phi=0 but w!=0, and choose beta of minimum
root height among nonzero w_gamma. Then beta!=0. For each simple raising
operator e_i, (e_i tensor 1 + 1 tensor e_i)w=0. Project this equation to
V_mu[mu-beta+alpha_i] tensor V_nu[beta]. The first contribution is
(e_i tensor 1)w_beta. The only possible second contribution is from
w_(beta-alpha_i); it is absent if beta-alpha_i is not in Q_+, and is zero
by minimality otherwise. Therefore (e_i tensor 1)w_beta=0 for every i.
The common kernel of the simple raising operators on the irreducible first
factor is its highest-weight line, whereas w_beta has first weight
mu-beta != mu. This contradiction proves w=0. Since v_mu generates
V_mu, w=0 implies phi=0. Thus phi -> p_phi is injective into V_nu[0],
which proves (2). This argument works for every lambda and nu; it does
not assume a tensor-product multiplicity mnemonic or regularity.

3. CENTER / ROOT-LATTICE RESTRICTION

Write nu=sum_(i=1)^(m-1) a_i omega_i with a_i>=0. A generator of the
center Z(SU(m)) acts on V_nu by the scalar

  exp((2 pi i/m) sum_(i=1)^(m-1) i a_i).

The center acts trivially on End(V_lambda), so an occurring nu must obey

  sum_(i=1)^(m-1) i a_i = 0 mod m.                              (5)

This is the condition nu in the A_(m-1) root lattice Q. If (5) fails,
V_nu has no zero weight, since all its weights lie in nu+Q; hence (2)
forces m_lambda(nu)=0. In the upper count below we sum over all dominant
weights and drop (5), which can only increase the answer.

4. KOSTANT PARTITION COUNT FOR z_nu

For eta in the root lattice, let P(eta) be the number of expressions
eta=sum_(alpha in Phi^+) n_alpha alpha with n_alpha nonnegative integers;
put P(eta)=0 when no such expression exists. Kostant's multiplicity
formula at weight zero is

  z_nu=sum_(w in W) (-1)^ell(w) P(w(nu+rho)-rho).                (6)

There are |W|=m! terms. For eta with P(eta)>0, write
eta=sum_(i=1)^r c_i alpha_i in simple-root coordinates. Since
(rho,alpha_i)=1, its height is

  ht(eta)=sum_i c_i=(eta,rho)>=0.

There are p positive roots and r simple roots. Choose the coefficients
n_alpha for the q=p-r nonsimple positive roots first. Every positive root
has height at least one, so 0<=n_alpha<=ht(eta). Once those q choices
are fixed, the coefficients of the simple roots are uniquely determined,
because the simple roots form a basis; they either give one admissible
nonnegative integer solution or none. Consequently

  P(eta) <= (1+ht(eta))^q.                                      (7)

For eta_w=w(nu+rho)-rho,
||eta_w||<=||nu||+2||rho||, and Cauchy-Schwarz gives

  ht(eta_w)<=||rho|| (||nu||+2||rho||)

whenever P(eta_w)>0. Thus, with

  A_m=sqrt(2)||rho||,       B_m=1+2||rho||^2,

equations (6)-(7) imply the explicit estimate

  z_nu <= m! (B_m+A_m sqrt(T))^q
       <= m! (A_m^2+B_m^2)^(q/2) (1+T)^(q/2)                  (8)

whenever c2(nu)<=T. Here ||nu||<=sqrt(2T), because nu is dominant and
c2(nu)=||nu||^2/2+(nu,rho)>=||nu||^2/2. The second inequality in (8) is
Cauchy-Schwarz in R^2. Since A_m^2+B_m^2=1+6s_m+4s_m^2, (8) has
constant m!(1+6s_m+4s_m^2)^(q/2). The polynomial degree q=p-r is the
number of nonsimple positive roots; it is not assumed from a heuristic
weight-multiplicity estimate.

5. WEYL DIMENSION AND DOMINANT-WEIGHT COUNT

Let nu=sum_i a_i omega_i. For simple roots alpha_i of squared length 2,
the Dynkin labels satisfy

  0<=a_i=(nu,alpha_i)<=sqrt(2)||nu||<=2 sqrt(T).

Thus at most (1+2sqrt(T))^r dominant labels lie in the Casimir ball.
For every T>=0,

  (1+2sqrt(T))^2 <= 5(1+T),

so the label count is at most (5^{r/2}(1+T)^{r/2}).

The Weyl dimension formula is

  dim(V_nu)=product_(1<=i<j<=m)
                ( (nu+rho,alpha_(ij)) / (rho,alpha_(ij)) ),
  alpha_(ij)=e_i-e_j.

Here (rho,alpha_(ij))=j-i and
(nu,alpha_(ij))=sum_(k=i)^(j-1) a_k. Each Weyl factor is therefore at
most 1+2sqrt(T), giving

  dim(V_nu)<=5^(p/2)(1+T)^(p/2).                               (9)

Combining (2), (8), and (9), then summing over the at most
5^(r/2)(1+T)^(r/2) labels, yields

  D_lambda(T)
   <=m! 5^((p+r)/2) (1+6s_m+4s_m^2)^(q/2)
                                      (1+T)^((q+p+r)/2)
   = C_count(m)(1+T)^p,

because q=p-r. This proves (1) for all T>=0. The exponent arithmetic is
the key check: multiplicity contributes q=p-r powers of sqrt(T), Weyl
dimension contributes p powers of sqrt(T), and the rank-r label count
contributes r powers of sqrt(T); their total is 2p powers of sqrt(T),
or T^p.

6. LARGE-T REPRESENTATION-SUPPORT / PLATEAU CHECK

The global label sum already proves (1) beyond the carrier's spectral
support. The following separate check makes the saturation tail explicit.
The highest weight at the top of V_lambda tensor V_lambda^* is

  sigma=lambda+lambda^*.

Every constituent nu is dominated by sigma, and c2 is increasing under
dominance on dominant weights: if sigma-nu=sum_i b_i alpha_i with b_i>=0,
then

  c2(sigma)-c2(nu)=(sigma-nu,sigma+nu+2rho)/2>=0.

Therefore D_lambda(T)=d_lambda^2 once T>=c2(sigma). In Dynkin labels,
lambda=sum a_i omega_i and lambda^* has labels a_(m-i). The A_(m-1)
fundamental-weight Gram entries

  (omega_i,omega_j)=min(i,j)(m-max(i,j))/m

are positive, so (lambda,lambda^*)>=0 and ||sigma||>=||lambda||.
Weyl's formula also gives

  d_lambda<= (1+sqrt(2)||lambda||)^p
           <= 2^(p/2)(1+||lambda||)^p.

Using (1+x)^2<=2(1+x^2), c2(sigma)>=||sigma||^2/2, and T>=c2(sigma),

  d_lambda^2<=2^(2p)(1+||lambda||^2)^p
             <=2^(2p)(1+2T)^p
             <=2^(3p)(1+T)^p.                                (10)

Thus the plateau is controlled by C_support(m)(1+T)^p. For T below the
top eigenvalue, the direct all-label sum in Section 5 applies unchanged.
The two arguments agree on the overlap and remove any large-T/support
gap.

7. EXACT GREEN INTEGRAL AT p=1 AND PROFILE CONSEQUENCE

For context, the same all-T count enters the inverse-Casimir Green bound
for a matrix of rank at most d_lambda-k. If lambda is nontrivial, the
kernel of L is C I and the first positive Casimir is gamma=m. Indeed,
End(V_lambda) has trivial central character. In A_(m-1), a nonzero
dominant root-lattice weight nu has simple-root coordinates

  (A^(-1)a)_j,   A^(-1)_(ji)=min(i,j)(m-max(i,j))/m,

which are positive integers, hence each at least one. It therefore
dominates theta=alpha_1+...+alpha_(m-1), the adjoint highest weight;
c2(theta)=m. The adjoint occurs in End(V_lambda) because the differential
representation of the simple Lie algebra sl_m is nonzero and injective.

Let P be a rank-k orthogonal projection onto a k-dimensional subspace of
ker(A), P0=P-(k/d_lambda)I, and Pi_(0,T] the positive Casimir projector.
Put M_P(T)=||Pi_(0,T]P0||_HS^2. Spectral calculus gives the exact layer-
cake identity

  G(P):=<P0,L^(-1)P0> = integral_gamma^infinity M_P(T) T^(-2) dT. (11)

The rank-k vectorization/partial-trace estimate gives

  M_P(T)<=min{k, k^2 D_lambda(T)/d_lambda}.

Thus for any K>=gamma, (1) implies

  G(P)<= C_m k^2/d_lambda
             integral_gamma^K (1+T)^p T^(-2) dT + k/K.          (12)

For integer p>=1 the integral is exactly

  I_p(gamma,K)=1/gamma-1/K + p log(K/gamma)
       + sum_(j=2)^p binom(p,j) (K^(j-1)-gamma^(j-1))/(j-1).   (13)

In particular, the borderline p=1 case is logarithmic:

  I_1(gamma,K)=log(K/gamma)+1/gamma-1/K.                       (14)

For SU(2), p=1 and gamma=2, so
I_1(2,K)=log(K/2)+1/2-1/K. This is the exact p=1 Green integral; replacing
it by a power law would lose the endpoint logarithm. With K on the order
of d_lambda/k, (12) gives the familiar near-full lower scale
E(A)/||A||_HS^2 >= c/[1+log(d_lambda/k)] for p=1, via the kernel bridge
||A||_HS^2<=E(A)[d_lambda G(P)/k^2+1/gamma]. For p>1, (13) grows like
K^(p-1)/(p-1), yielding the power branch after balancing K against the
tail k/K. These are lower bounds; this note supplies no matching upper
filters.

The ordinary rank-profile integral also has an immediate low-rank use.
The irreducible-carrier projector theorem gives

  Phi_lambda(Q)=integral_0^infinity
        [1-QD_lambda(T)/d_lambda]_+ dT,
  inf_(||X||_HS=1, rank X<=Q) E(X)>=Phi_lambda(Q).

From (1), whenever R=d_lambda/Q is larger than an m-dependent constant,
choose u=(R/(2C_m))^(1/p)>=2 and T=u-1. On 0<=t<=T the integrand is
at least 1/2, so Phi_lambda(Q)>=T/2>=u/4. Hence

  Phi_lambda(Q)>=c_m (d_lambda/Q)^(1/p)

for the corresponding small-rank range. This is only the spectral lower
profile; no optimizer or matching all-rank construction is inferred.

8. SMALL EXACT DIAGNOSTICS

SU(2), p=1. Let lambda have Dynkin label N (spin N/2). Then

  End(V_N)=direct_sum_(j=0)^N V_(2j),
  c2(V_(2j))=j(j+1),   dim(V_(2j))=2j+1.

For K(T)=min{N,floor((sqrt(1+4T)-1)/2)}, the exact count is

  D_N(T)=sum_(j=0)^K(T)(2j+1)=(K(T)+1)^2<=2(1+T).

Thus the all-T exponent p=1 is attained in order, and the exact Green
integral (14) has gamma=2.

SU(3), p=3, r=2, q=1. For nu=(u,v), the exact zero-weight multiplicity is

  z_(u,v)=1{u congruent v (mod 3)}(min(u,v)+1).

This is the expected degree-q bound and follows directly by counting
Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns with U(3) top row (u+v,v,0) and SU(3)-zero
weight (q0,q0,q0), q0=(u+2v)/3. For carrier lambda=(1,1), dim=8, the exact
decomposition is

  8 tensor 8 = 1 + 2*8 + 10 + 10bar + 27.

The Casimirs are 0,3,6,6,8 and the cumulative dimensions are 1,17,37,64
on [0,3), [3,6), [6,8), [8,infinity), respectively. The doubled adjoint
copy is counted in D. As a finite diagnostic only, the local exact SU(3)
Littlewood-Richardson formula was checked for every 0<=a,b<=6 and 23,128
output-label cases: m_lambda(u,v)<=z_(u,v) and the constituent dimensions
sum to dim(V_(a,b))^2 throughout. This check is not part of the proof.

For m=2, the displayed C_m bound is C_2=10; the exact SU(2) diagnostic
improves it to 2. For m=3, s_3=2 and the explicit count constant is
C_count(3)=150 sqrt(145), while C_support(3)=512, so (1) holds with
C_3=150 sqrt(145). These constants are not optimized.

9. SOURCE AND CLAIM BOUNDARY

The only representation-theoretic identity used for (8) is Kostant's
weight-multiplicity formula, which expresses a weight multiplicity as the
alternating Weyl sum (6) of the positive-root partition function. Primary
source: B. Kostant, “A formula for the multiplicity of a weight,” Trans.
Amer. Math. Soc. 93 (1959), 53-73, DOI
https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1959-0109192-6.

Equation (2) is proved directly in Section 2. Equation (1) is uniform in
the carrier and valid for all T>=0, including after D_lambda saturates at
d_lambda^2. The bound is not sharp in constants and does not show that the
rank profile is attained by any matrix. The near-full Green consequence is
one-sided and uses the separately stated kernel bridge. No KLS or
log-Sobolev assertion is used.
