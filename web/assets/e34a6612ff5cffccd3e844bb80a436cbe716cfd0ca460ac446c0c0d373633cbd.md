# Two reconstructed mechanisms and deductions

These are source-derived local mathematics and elementary independent deductions. They are not claims of novelty or external validation of the headline manuscripts.

## 1. Boundary-feasibility probability

Let b_1,...,b_m be nonzero real rows spanning R^d. Let F be a biholomorphism from the unit disk onto a bounded planar domain D, with F(0)=0 and continuous extension to the closed disk. Let B be the finite nonempty set of independent d-row subsets. For I in B and independent uniform angles theta_i, set

`Z_I(theta)=B_I^{-1}(F(exp(i theta_i)))_{i in I}`,

and

`P_I=Pr[b_j Z_I(theta) in closure(D) for every j]`.

Then `sum_{I in B} P_I >=1`.

### Reconstruction of the mass input

Suppose a holomorphic f:U subset C^d -> C^m has only zero 0, all sublevels {|f|^2<=s}, 0<s<1, are compact in U, and f(z)=H(z)+O(|z|^{k+1}), where H has homogeneous degree k and no nonzero common zero. Put tau=|f|^2 and use `d^c=(i/4)(bar partial-partial)`. Positivity of the complex Hessians of tau and log tau follows respectively from a squared derivative norm and Cauchy–Schwarz.

For a regular value s and E_s={tau<s}, Stokes gives

`s^{-d} integral_{E_s}(dd^c tau)^d = integral_{partial E_s} gamma wedge(d gamma)^{d-1}`,

where gamma=d^c log tau. Comparison with a small sphere leaves a nonnegative integral of `(dd^c log tau)^d`, so the left side is at least the limiting sphere flux.

Rescale f(epsilon z); the nonvanishing homogeneous leading term makes log tau converge in C^2, after discarding its constant 2k log epsilon. Let alpha_0=d^c log|z|^2 on the unit sphere and beta=d^c(log|H|^2-k log|z|^2). Dilation and common-phase invariance imply beta(iz)=0 and contraction of d(k alpha_0+t beta) with iz vanishes. Consequently the derivative of

`integral_S (k alpha_0+t beta) wedge d(k alpha_0+t beta)^{d-1}`

is zero. The homogeneous flux equals the radial flux `(pi k)^d`. Passing s upwards to 1 and applying Cauchy–Binet yields

`integral_{tau<1} sum_{|I|=d}|det partial f_I/partial z|^2 dV >= (pi k)^d/d!`.

This normalization is sharp: f(z)=(z_1^k,...,z_d^k), U=C^d, changes variables to the unit ball with generic covering multiplicity k^d, giving equality `(pi k)^d/d!`.

### Powers concentrate the mass onto boundary samples

Set g=F^{-1}, Omega={z:b_j z in D for all j}, h_j(z)=g(b_j z), and f_k=(h_j^k)_j. The spanning rows make Omega bounded and make the degree-k leading term have no nonzero common zero. Sublevel compactness follows because every active disk coordinate is bounded by s^{1/(2k)}<1. Thus the mass inequality applies.

For each independent I, change variables u_i=h_i(z). Its real Jacobian is the squared complex Jacobian; this cancels the corresponding factor in the minor integral. The remaining integrand is `k^{2d} product |u_i|^{2k-2}`. Put `u_i=rho_i^{1/k} exp(i theta_i)`. In each coordinate,

`k^2 |u_i|^{2k-2} dV_2(u_i) = k rho_i d rho_i d theta_i`.

The transformed minor integral M_{I,k}/k^d is bounded above by an indicator integral over `sum rho_i^2<1`, whose total measure is pi^d/d!. For all positive rho_i the inferred row values converge to the boundary samples. A limiting point outside closure(D) eventually violates that row. The upper-limit indicator is therefore bounded by the closed-domain feasibility indicator. Reverse Fatou on this finite measure space gives

`limsup_k M_{I,k}/k^d <= (pi^d/d!) P_I`.

The mass lower bound and the finite number of I imply `1<=sum P_I`. Closed-domain membership is essential at the limiting step. No zero-probability statement about additional boundary hits was needed.

### Independent deduction: an exact witness sampler

Choose I uniformly from B and sample its angles. The acceptance probability is

`Pr[all row constraints feasible]=(1/|B|)sum_I P_I >=1/|B|`.

Repeated independent trials therefore have expected at most |B| trials. A selected accepted vector simultaneously saturates the chosen d boundary constraints while satisfying all supplied complex row constraints. For convex D containing a ball of radius r0 centered at zero, multiplying an accepted vector by 1-eta places every row value at least eta*r0 inside D, by convexity. This gives a robust interior witness once exact feasibility has been obtained.

Exact sharp example: d=2 and rows (1,0),(0,1),(1,1), with F(z)=z. There are three bases. Each missing-row condition is |exp(i theta)+/-exp(i phi)|<=1 and has probability 1/3 by the elementary cosine inequality. Hence sum P_I=1 and uniform-basis acceptance is exactly 1/3.

The algorithmic interface is intentionally limited. The rows, all independent bases, and domain/map evaluation are supplied. Enumeration can require binom(m,d) rank checks. Each trial solves one d-by-d system and checks m complex row values; exact-real costs are roughly O(d^3+md) plus map/domain calls. An actual digital procedure additionally needs bounds on basis conditioning, approximating F, and deciding omitted constraints near the boundary. The probability lemma alone gives no finite-bit or polynomial-in-d algorithm, no geometry acquisition protocol, and no new Mahler priority claim.

For the particular Mahler lens, the exact source uses

`F(z)=(8/pi^2)sum_{j>=0}(-1)^j z^{2j+1}/(2j+1)^2`.

On either semicircle its imaginary coordinate is uniform on [-1,1], independent of the branch sign, and lambda''(t)=-sec(pi t/2). The summary's unrotated triangle-wave expression is the same idea after rotation; implementations should use the exact source normalization. The uniform-coordinate conformal idea is already in Gross (2019).

## 2. Fixed-depth triangular precision

Let D be fixed. Suppose T<=C(1+p), p>=1, and nonnegative budgets Q_j(t), j=1,...,D, satisfy

`Q_j(t+1)<=Q_j(t)+P_j(p,d_*,Q_{j+1}(t),...,Q_D(t))`,

where each P_j is a fixed nondecreasing polynomial, d_* is polynomial in p, and initial Q_j(0) are polynomial in p. The added term excludes Q_j and every lower-layer budget. Then all budgets, and polynomial per-round costs summed over t<=T, are polynomial in p.

Proof: define envelopes downwards,

`V_D=Q_D(0)+T P_D(p,d_*)`,

`V_j=Q_j(0)+T P_j(p,d_*,V_{j+1},...,V_D)`.

Each is polynomial because there are only D substitutions. Downward induction followed by time induction gives Q_j(t)<=V_j for t<=T. Any fixed polynomial per-round cost in these budgets is polynomial; summing at most T times preserves the assertion.

If all P_j have degree at most a and d_* has degree b, an elementary degree envelope has `E_j<=1+a max(1,b,E_{j+1},...,E_D)` after padding initial degrees. The dependence on D can be exponential while still being a fixed polynomial for fixed D. Therefore growing D does not inherit the fixed-depth conclusion.

The exclusion of self-dependence is substantive. Replacing the recurrence by Q(t+1)=Q(t)^2 with Q(0)=2 yields Q(t)=2^{2^t}; even a linear multiplicative recurrence Q(t+1)=2Q(t) produces exponential rather than polynomial budget across O(p) steps. Merely saying every individual step has a polynomial bound does not prove the needed total bound.

Application boundary: the progression source must first prove its actual geometry, detection, rank preparation, scalar return, and lifting satisfy the triangular dependence with a fixed depth and fixed multiplicative density gain. Once it does, O(p) rounds can be sustained with log N polynomial in p. Inverting p comparable to log(1/alpha) yields a bound `r_k(N)/N<=C_k exp[-c_k(log N)^{epsilon_k}]`. This inversion is an elementary consequence; proving the triangular increment for arbitrary sets remains the load-bearing research claim. The inverse theorem supplied by prior literature does not establish that claim on its own.
