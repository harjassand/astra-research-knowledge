# C12: spatial state alias in a published adaptive-treatment assay

Date: 2026-10-08  
Scope: primary-source audit and finite reconstruction of published Appold et al. yeast-assay data. No experiment, clinical inference, external message, or intervention was performed.

## Verdict

The strongest scoped result is a **post-hoc clone-contact / fate timing contrast** in the eight-colony adaptive-treatment dataset, accompanied by a matched-bulk example. Two eligible resistant clones reach the same measured boundary distance, but one remains there while another retreats after the next pulse starts. Their different pulse phase and retreat histories make a more useful hypothesis testable than a static size alias: **post-contact reconfinement may depend on the growth state and dosing latency around front contact, not just total colony area**. This is still a two-event retrospective pattern, not an estimated law or a validated controller.

The pair is selected retrospectively, and the source paper already measures resistant-clone trajectories and escape. Therefore the observation does **not** establish novelty, prediction accuracy, clinical transfer, or treatment-policy benefit. No evolution-steering candidate clears the active research gate on this evidence. In particular, Appold et al. themselves propose rebound growth as a proxy for confinement and explicitly limit tumor/biofilm transfer to a hypothesis.

## Reconstructed observation

The raw inputs are the published `adaptive_therapy` colony CSVs for P2 and P3. Frame 145 is 72.5 h from the first imaging frame at two frames per hour. The immediately preceding two-hour area-growth estimate uses frames 141–145. At this checkpoint, the measured data are:

| Colony | Area (px²) | Two-hour log-area rate (h⁻¹) | Nearest tracked clone clearance | Eligible clone tracks | Later eligible escape events through frame 251 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P2 | 308,770 | 0.002909 | 76.3 µm | 7 | 1 |
| P3 | 322,430 | 0.002623 | 549.0 µm | 31 | 0 |

The areas differ by 4.24% relative to P3 (4.33% relative to their mean); the two-hour log-area rates differ by 10.9% relative to P3. The nearest tracked clone is 7.19 times farther from the front in P3 than in P2. Under the **final article Methods** definitions, P2 clone 15 first breaches the front at frame 150 (75.0 h, 2.5 h after the checkpoint) and does not later retreat beyond 80 µm through the last supplied frame, 251 (125.5 h). P3 has no eligible breach through that horizon. “No eligible breach” is restricted to the paper's filtered tracks; it is not a claim that no late or otherwise excluded mutant ever approached the boundary.

The clone filters implemented here are: first detection from 12.5 to 100 h; entering the 17–500 µm front band at least once; and at least three consecutive frames of tracking. A breach is a distance of at most 20 µm; escape means no subsequent retreat beyond 80 µm. Distances use 8.648 µm per pixel. Those are the final published article definitions. An older checked-in Figure 2 analysis script has 10/60 µm thresholds, so this reconstruction follows the version-of-record Methods rather than that older code snapshot.

### Contact timing and later fate

The source Figure 5 schedule places the second treatment interval at frames 110–141 and the third at 189–284. P2 clone 15 first reaches the breach boundary at frame 150 (75.0 h), 4.5 h after the second interval ends; the next interval starts 19.5 h later. It has not retreated beyond 80 µm by frame 251 (125.5 h). P8 clone 18 reaches the same measured boundary distance (2.59 µm) at frame 187 (93.5 h), 23 h after the second interval ends and 1 h before the third starts. It first retreats beyond 80 µm at frame 202 (101.0 h), 7.5 h after contact and 6.5 h after the third interval begins. Across the eight colonies, the published-rule reconstruction finds seven eligible first-breach tracks; six show no retreat by their last observed frame and one (P8) does retreat. P1 clone 11 is among the six no-retreat tracks but has only 3.5 h of follow-up after contact, so its eventual fate is right-censored by this CSV horizon.

This is a candidate **post-contact reconfinement window**: under one shared schedule, one clone that contacts the boundary shortly before a new pulse is observed to recede, while a clone contacting much earlier in the off interval does not recede over a longer follow-up. The observation does not isolate pulse timing from clone-specific neighborhood, size, history, or local sensitive-cell growth. The source paper already demonstrates clone reconfinement after schedule changes and argues that the realized front-growth state matters. The narrow added value here is the quantified event timing in its adaptive cohort, which suggests a prospective event-level test; it does not establish a new general mechanism or priority.

The paper's fitted digital twin reaches full treatment effect over a 12.35 h onset timescale. P8's return beyond 80 µm occurs 6.5 h after the next pulse starts, inside that fitted onset interval; P2's next pulse starts 19.5 h after contact, outside it. This alignment suggests a testable interaction between clone-contact time, sensitive-front growth, and treatment kinetics. It is not mechanistic evidence: the 12.35 h value is a model-fitted kinetic parameter, and P2/P8 differ in clone age, neighborhood and other unmeasured states.

The P2/P3 pair supports a descriptive observation-level distinction: total area and recent area rate do not uniquely determine the recorded clone-front state or later track outcome in these two records. It does not establish that clearance predicts escape generally. The records share a treatment protocol; frames are not independent replicates; P2/P3 were selected after inspecting the dataset; the contact-fate contrast has only two events after frame 145; follow-up is finite; and the main paper already reports clone-level trajectories. The analysis is reproducible with [the reconstruction script](scripts/appold_bulk_alias.py); its complete machine-readable output, including all eight colony tracks and pulse-relative breach timings, is [appold_bulk_alias.json](outputs/appold_bulk_alias.json).

## Prior-art and scope attack

Appold et al. report that intermittent pulses change resource distribution and mutant confinement in a spatial yeast assay, track clone trajectories, and identify a schedule boundary. Their Discussion says growth rebound might serve as an accessible confinement proxy, while emphasizing that extending the mechanism to tumors or biofilms remains a hypothesis. The general claims “spatial state affects resistance escape,” “rebound is informative,” and “treatment timing can steer clone confinement” are therefore preempted by the primary source itself. See [Appold et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-026-03178-z).

Cancer-specific prior art narrows the gap further. [Bacevic et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-01516-1) experimentally compare low- and high-dose CDK-inhibitor treatment in tumor spheroids and show that spatial competition changes resistance control. [Strobl et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43856-022-00110-x) model how tumor architecture, local competition, and resistance-nest separation affect adaptive therapy. [Whiting et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7) combine lineage tracing and population-size histories to infer cancer resistance-phenotype dynamics, with single-cell validation. These works preempt broad spatial, lineage, or adaptive-therapy framing; none turns the present two-colony yeast reconstruction into a cancer diagnostic.

Two bacterial studies are retained as boundary checks, not as cancer evidence. [Dabos et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-025-02804-6) report 18 KPC-2 trajectories with similar final ceftazidime resistance but divergent enzyme, growth, and collateral-antibiotic traits, so “same primary resistance, different collateral response” is not a new general principle. [Barber and Couce (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74044-6) report mutation-biased adaptation across population sizes. Their Methods state 48 populations per treatment except 24 for regime L; the supplied Figure 2B workbook has some ML ΔmutH percentages such as 95.238% and 85.714%, which are arithmetically consistent with 20/21 and 18/21 rather than denominators of 48. The effective denominator or attrition rule is not stated in the source table inspected here. This remains an unresolved denominator question, **not evidence of a data error**, and it is not used in the spatial finding.

## Candidate next gate

The next decisive test is prospective prediction in independent, held-out assay runs, not another retrospective threshold sweep. Use the source assay's existing 30-minute spatial time-lapse and clone histories. Before viewing outcomes, compare the existing bulk-rebound state and complete pulse history with a model that adds clone clearance, recent clearance velocity, local sensitive-front growth, and time since the last dose. Preregister two distinct outcomes: first breach within a short horizon, and retreat beyond 80 µm within eight hours after first breach. The eight-hour window is a hypothesis window selected to include the observed P8 retreat, not a validated biological constant. Split and score by independently executed plate/chamber run, not by frame; colonies sharing one global temperature schedule are nested observations, not independent policy assignments. Report calibration and proper scores as well as lead time, including censored tracks and segmentation failures. Charge the acquisition and compute costs: fluorescent spatial imaging, segmentation latency/error, missing frames, and the complete treatment-history record. Every baseline should receive the same raw observations and history; the feature-ablation comparison should differ only in whether it uses clone-level geometry and local front growth.

If these clone-level features improve held-out forecasts, that still does not select an action. A separately preregistered randomized assay would need to compare a clone-contact-informed dosing rule with the existing rebound rule at matched cumulative treatment exposure and population control, using the same observation stream. One clean intervention is to randomize pulse restart latency after first eligible breach across independently controlled runs while matching total pulse duration over the follow-up window; this estimates whether the observed 1 h versus 19.5 h timing contrast is causal. If one plate shares a global temperature program, randomize whole independently controlled runs rather than colonies. The action mapping and rescue latency are currently unknown. A null held-out forecast gain, or no control benefit at matched exposure, ends this route. Imaging quality or too few eligible escapes should return `UNKNOWN`, not a fitted risk claim. Nothing here supports patient-level recommendations.
