A12 WEIGHTED-SUBGAUSSIAN STABILITY, SHARP LOSS, AND SAME-DATA SCALE
Version 1; 2026-10-09

STATUS

Candidate, internally derived. The finite-dimensional stability theorem below is a proof candidate from standard subgaussian matrix concentration plus matrix Bernstein; it has not received independent mathematical review or formal verification. Its stated near-linear sample bound is conditional on a response-tail covariance bound and a nondegenerate population second moment. The phase-retrieval specialization gives such a bound from the paper's conditional fourth-moment assumption. This record does not establish priority. AISTATS 2026 itself cites a concurrent 2025 noiseless phase-retrieval manuscript claiming the noise-free special case.

The theorem yields m = O(d log d) for fixed signal/noise ratios, deletion tolerance, stability accuracy, and confidence. The deletion term contains epsilon log(e/epsilon), and Section 7 proves this order is unavoidable, including for the noiseless Gaussian phase-retrieval model. A uniform-in-threshold extension handles a robust response-scale estimate computed from the same corrupted data; it removes the need to condition on an independent scale batch.

1. TARGET AND MODEL

Let a in R^d be isotropic K-subgaussian: E[aa^T]=I and for every unit v, ||<a,v>||_{psi_2} <= K. The clean pairs (a_i,Y_i) are iid from one joint law; conditional response noise may depend arbitrarily on its own design row but is not coupled across samples. A separate strong-contamination adversary may inspect the full clean batch and replace up to its allowed fraction after sampling. No independence between Y and a is assumed. For deterministic tau>0 define

    X = Y a 1{||Y a|| <= tau},       Q = E[XX^T].

Assume lambda := lambda_min(Q)>0 and Lambda := ||Q||op. For a response cutoff L>0 set

    T_L = E[XX^T 1{|Y| > L}],       eta := ||T_L||op.

The empirical distribution is the uniform distribution on X_1,...,X_m. For weights w_i in [0,1], let alpha=m^{-1}sum_i(1-w_i), and

    Q_w = [m^{-1} sum_i w_i X_i X_i^T]/(1-alpha).

This is exactly the fractional-deletion version of Definition 4.6 in Das–Batra. It includes weights selected after inspecting all samples.

The source model specialization is a~N(0,I_d), x fixed and nonzero, s=||x||^2,

    Y = (a^T x)^2 + zeta,
    E[zeta | a]=0, E[zeta^2 | a]=sigma^2,
    E[zeta^4 | a] <= K4^4.

The conditional fourth-moment bound permits arbitrary dependence of the noise on the sensing vector. It implies sigma^2 <= K4^2 by conditional Cauchy–Schwarz.

2. A RESTRICTED COVARIANCE LEMMA FOR SUBGAUSSIAN DESIGNS

Let k=ceil(epsilon m), 0<epsilon<1/2, and delta in (0,1). There is a universal C such that, with probability at least 1-delta, simultaneously for every q_i in [0,1] with sum_i q_i <= epsilon m,

    ||m^{-1} sum_i q_i a_i a_i^T||op
       <= C K^2 Phi_m,

where

    Phi_m = [d + k log(e m/k) + log(2/delta)]/m.

For epsilon m >= 1 this is at most C K^2[d/m + epsilon log(e/epsilon) + log(2/delta)/m]. Keeping Phi_m as written handles epsilon m < 1 without dropping the log m term from the maximum-row event.

Proof. Fix a subset I of k row indices and let A_I be the k by d matrix with rows a_i^T. For fixed unit p in R^k and v in R^d, p^T A_I v is subgaussian with psi_2 norm at most C K: it is a sum of independent centered subgaussian variables with squared coefficients summing to one. Take 1/4-nets N_k,N_d of the two unit spheres, each with size at most 9 to the corresponding dimension. The standard net approximation gives

    ||A_I||op <= 2 max_{p in N_k,v in N_d} |p^T A_I v|.

A scalar subgaussian tail bound and a union bound over N_k x N_d imply

    P(||A_I||op > C K(sqrt(d+k)+sqrt(t))) <= 2 exp(-t).

There are at most (em/k)^k subsets of size k. Taking t=k log(em/k)+log(2/delta), then union-bounding over those subsets, gives

    max_{|I|=k} ||A_I||op^2/m <= C K^2 Phi_m.

For any fixed unit v, maximizing sum_i q_i <a_i,v>^2 over q in [0,1]^m with sum q_i<=epsilon m selects the largest ceil(epsilon m) values (with at most one fractional endpoint). Thus its value is at most the sum over some subset of k rows. Taking the supremum over v proves the displayed matrix bound. The event is simultaneous in q, so it remains valid when deletion weights are adaptive functions of the entire sample and the responses. QED.

An anisotropic version follows by writing a=Sigma_a^{1/2} g, with g isotropic K-subgaussian: the right side becomes C K^2 Phi_m Sigma_a in Loewner order. To make it relative to Q, a sufficient design condition is Q >= lambda_a Sigma_a for lambda_a>0.

3. FINITE-SAMPLE STABILITY THEOREM

Set ell=log(8d/delta), and define

    e_Q = sqrt(2 tau^2 Lambda ell/m) + 2 tau^2 ell/(3m),
    e_T = sqrt(2 tau^2 eta ell/m) + 2 tau^2 ell/(3m).

With probability at least 1-delta, every weight vector w with alpha<=epsilon satisfies

    ||Q_w - Q||op
      <= [e_Q + C K^2 L^2 Phi_m + eta + e_T + epsilon Lambda]/(1-epsilon).       (1)

Consequently the empirical distribution is (epsilon,gamma)-stable with respect to Q whenever the right side of (1) is at most gamma lambda. This implies (1-gamma)Q <= Q_w <= (1+gamma)Q for every adaptive fractional deletion. Under the source definition's parameter convention, also impose epsilon<=gamma; the near-linear robust-PCA theorem cited below needs the stronger 20epsilon<gamma.

Proof. Write X_i=Y_i a_i 1{||Y_i a_i||<=tau}, Qhat=m^{-1}sum_i X_iX_i^T, q_i=1-w_i, and D=m^{-1}sum_i q_i X_iX_i^T. Then

    Q_w - Q = [(Qhat-Q) - D + alpha Q]/(1-alpha),

so it suffices to bound the three numerator terms. Since 0<=X_iX_i^T<=tau^2 I, the centered summand has norm at most tau^2 and matrix variance at most tau^2 Q. Matrix Bernstein gives ||Qhat-Q||op<=e_Q, with the chosen failure allocation. The same argument for Z_i=X_iX_i^T 1{|Y_i|>L}, whose expectation is T_L and whose variance is at most tau^2 T_L, gives ||m^{-1}sum_i Z_i||op<=eta+e_T.

For the low-response part, pointwise

    X_iX_i^T 1{|Y_i|<=L} <= L^2 a_i a_i^T.

The restricted covariance lemma therefore bounds its deleted contribution by C K^2 L^2 Phi_m. The deleted high-response contribution is at most the entire positive-semidefinite empirical tail sum, hence at most eta+e_T. Finally alpha||Q||<=epsilon Lambda. Combining these estimates gives (1). Since Q>=lambda I, ||Q_w-Q||<=gamma lambda implies the Loewner inequalities. QED.

The use of Matrix Bernstein is legitimate for the norm-truncated X even when Y has no exponential moments. For a PSD rank-one S=XX^T, S^2=||X||^2 S<=tau^2 S, so E[(S-Q)^2]=E[S^2]-Q^2<=tau^2 Q. The tail version is identical with Q replaced by T_L.

4. EXPLICIT TRADEOFF AND NEAR-LINEAR SAMPLE COUNT

Let beta>0. A convenient sufficient parameterization of (1) is:

    eta <= beta lambda,
    epsilon Lambda <= beta lambda,
    C K^2 L^2 epsilon log(e/epsilon) <= beta lambda,                    (2)

and

    m >= C' ell [ tau^2 Lambda/(beta^2 lambda^2)
                  + tau^2/(beta lambda)
                  + K^2 L^2 d/(beta lambda) ],                           (3)

provided epsilon m>=1; otherwise use Phi_m directly in (1). Constants C,C' are universal and can be increased so that the right side in (1) is <= 8 beta for epsilon<=1/2. Taking beta<=gamma/8 yields (epsilon,gamma)-stability. The exact inequality (1), rather than the coarse sufficient conditions (2)–(3), is the theorem.

For fixed lambda,Lambda,tau,L,K,epsilon,gamma,delta and m growing with d, (3) is O(d log(d/delta)). If tau^2=Theta(d) and scale ratios are held fixed, the covariance-concentration terms also require only m=O(d log(d/delta)). The leading response and deletion tradeoff is eta + (K^2 L^2/lambda) epsilon log(e/epsilon), plus the normalization term epsilon Lambda/lambda. This exposes which quantities must be bounded; there is no distribution-free claim with constants independent of response scale or conditioning.

5. PHASE-RETRIEVAL SPECIALIZATION, INCLUDING THE TARGET-MATRIX CHECK

5.1 Exact population moment

For a~N(0,I_d), g=a^T x, s=||x||^2, and the conditional noise assumptions in Section 1, the untruncated raw second moment is

    Q0 = E[Y^2 aa^T] = (3s^2 + sigma^2) I + 12s xx^T.                (4)

Indeed rotate x to sqrt(s)e_1. The signal term has eigenvalue 15s^2 in direction e_1 because E[Z_1^6]=15, and 3s^2 in each orthogonal direction because E[Z_1^4]E[Z_j^2]=3. The cross term E[2g^2 zeta aa^T] is zero after conditioning on a. The noise term is sigma^2 E[aa^T]=sigma^2 I. Thus lambda_min(Q0)=3s^2+sigma^2, lambda_max(Q0)=15s^2+sigma^2, and the signal-direction eigengap is 12s^2.

This does not equal the matrix printed in Question 4.8 of Das–Batra, namely s I+2xx^T. That printed matrix is E[g^2 aa^T], not E[Y^2 aa^T]. Its perpendicular and parallel eigenvalues are s and 3s, while (4) has eigenvalues 3s^2+sigma^2 and 15s^2+sigma^2. Das–Batra's later Appendix E/F computations use the latter values (their displayed top eigenvalue is 15||x||^4+sigma^2). I therefore state stability relative to the actual truncated moment Q_tau=E[Y^2 aa^T 1{||Ya||<=tau}], the matrix needed by the spectral initializer. The literal target in their Question 4.8 is internally inconsistent with the random vector as printed and cannot be substituted into Definition 4.6 without correction. Its tau=Theta(sqrt(d)) must also be understood after fixing the response scale: the paper's own initializer uses tau proportional to sqrt(d)(1+r_up^2)||x||^2, not a dimension-only threshold for arbitrary unnormalized x and noise.

5.2 Response-tail certificate

For any L>0, using Y^2 1{|Y|>L}<=Y^4/L^2 and (u+v)^4<=8(u^4+v^4),

    T_L <= L^{-2} E[Y^4 aa^T]
        <= 8 L^{-2}(E[g^8 aa^T] + E[zeta^4 aa^T]).

In coordinates aligned with x, E[g^8 aa^T] has eigenvalues 945s^4 along x and 105s^4 perpendicular to x. Conditional fourth-moment control gives E[zeta^4 aa^T]<=K4^4 I. Hence

    eta <= 8(945s^4+K4^4)/L^2.                                       (5)

This uses only the conditional fourth moment; it does not assume a noise tail distribution or independence from a.

5.3 Norm-truncation bias and scale dependence

Let Q_tau=E[Y^2 aa^T 1{||Ya||<=tau}]. Since 1{||Ya||>tau}<=Y^2||a||^2/tau^2,

    0 <= Q0-Q_tau
       <= tau^{-2} E[Y^4 ||a||^2 aa^T]
       <= C d(s^4+K4^4) tau^{-2} I.                                  (6)

For the last inequality, E[zeta^4||a||^2aa^T]<=K4^4 E[||a||^2aa^T]=K4^4(d+2)I. In coordinates aligned with x, each eigenvalue of E[g^8||a||^2aa^T] is at most C d s^4 by Gaussian moments (the parallel entry is s^4[10395+945(d-1)]). This proves (6).

Put r=K4/s. Taking tau_min=c_tau sqrt(d)(1+r^2)s with c_tau a sufficiently large universal constant makes (6) at most s^2/10, because (1+r^4)/(1+r^2)^2<=1. Therefore throughout any threshold interval tau>=tau_min,

    lambda_min(Q_tau) >= 2.9s^2+sigma^2,
    lambda_max(Q_tau) <= 15s^2+sigma^2,
    gap(Q_tau) >= 11.8s^2,

where the gap bound follows Weyl's inequality applied to (4) and (6). The numerical decimals can be replaced by 3-1/10 and 12-2/10; no rounding assumption is used. A cutoff tau=c_tau sqrt(d)(1+r^2)s_hat lies in such an interval if s_hat/s is bounded below by, say, 9/10.

Using (5) and lambda>=2s^2 gives a dimension-free eta/lambda <= C(1+r^4)s^2/L^2. For a target beta, it suffices to choose

    L^2 >= C_L s^2(1+r^4)/beta.

Then (2) requires, up to universal constants,

    epsilon log(e/epsilon) <= beta^2/[K^2(1+r^4)],
    epsilon (15+r^2) <= beta.

To get robust PCA's top-eigenvector error small enough for phase retrieval, take beta and its stability parameter gamma of order c/(1+r^2), and impose the robust-PCA condition 20 epsilon < gamma. The sufficient deletion tolerance from this proof is then epsilon log(e/epsilon) <= c/(1+r^4)(1+r^2)^2, i.e. order c r^{-8} up to the log, for large r. This is a sufficient bound, not claimed sharp in r. For fixed r it is a positive constant.

Combining (3), (5), and tau^2=Theta(d(1+r^2)^2 s^2) gives m=C(r,epsilon,gamma,delta) d log(d/delta) samples. The constants can be large, and the explicit conditions (1)–(3) are the auditable cost statement. The theorem removes the n^5 dependence in Das–Batra's Lemma F.3 for fixed parameters, conditional on correcting the displayed target matrix to Q_tau. It does not validate the source's reported analysis or establish a priority claim.

5.4 Same-data acquisition of the scale

A scale estimate computed from the same observations makes a fixed-tau concentration proof insufficient by itself: conditioning on a data-dependent tau can bias the retained sample. The following uniformization resolves that dependence under a deterministic interval for tau.

Let tau range over a deterministic interval I=[tau_-,tau_+], and define X_tau=Ya 1{||Ya||<=tau}, Q_tau=E[X_tau X_tau^T], and T_{L,tau}=E[X_tau X_tau^T 1{|Y|>L}]. Assume uniformly on I that lambda_min(Q_tau)>=lambda, ||Q_tau||<=Lambda, and ||T_{L,tau}||<=eta. Let Qhat_tau and That_tau be their empirical analogues.

For any h>0, there is a deterministic threshold grid of size

    J+1 <= C(1 + d Lambda/h)

such that the trace increase tr(Q_{tau_{j+1}}-Q_{tau_j}) is at most h between consecutive grid points. To construct it, partition the finite measure dF(tau)=d tr(Q_tau) into intervals of mass at most h; any atom of mass greater than h is isolated by placing grid points immediately before and at its threshold. There are at most d Lambda/h large atoms. For tau between consecutive grid points, both Qhat_tau and Q_tau are Loewner-monotone. Endpoint concentration and

    0 <= T_{L,tau_{j+1}}-T_{L,tau_j}
       <= Q_{tau_{j+1}}-Q_{tau_j}

give, simultaneously for all tau in I,

    ||Qhat_tau-Q_tau|| <= e_Q(h)+h,
    ||That_tau-T_{L,tau}|| <= e_T(h)+h,

with probability at least 1-delta. Here e_Q(h),e_T(h) are the formulas in Section 3 with tau replaced by tau_+ and ell replaced by log(8d(J+1)/delta). The restricted-design event is already uniform and is unchanged. Consequently (1) holds uniformly on I with these replacements and two additional h terms. Taking h=beta lambda/16 preserves the O(d log(d Lambda/(beta lambda delta))) sample order for fixed condition number.

This uniform event permits arbitrary data-dependent selection tau_hat in I, including a robust scale estimate computed from the same corrupted batch. In phase retrieval, E[Y]=s and Var(Y)=2s^2+sigma^2 <=(2+r^2)s^2. A robust scalar mean estimator with the paper's supplied bound r_up>=K4/s has source-reported sample cost m_scale=O((1+r_up^2)log(1/delta)) and yields s_hat within a fixed relative error when epsilon<=c/(1+r_up^2). The stability tolerance above is stricter for large r, so it suffices for this scale condition. If the stability batch size m exceeds m_scale, compute s_hat from its responses, set tau_hat=c_tau sqrt(d)(1+r_up^2)s_hat, and truncate those same pairs. The uniform-in-tau event makes dependence between s_hat and the clean sample immaterial. A separate scale batch is not required for this stability step.

For strong contamination, the robust PCA input's uncorrupted set may omit up to epsilon m clean rows. If the full clean sample is (alpha,gamma0)-stable with alpha>=21epsilon, then any surviving inlier set of size at least (1-epsilon)m is (20epsilon, 2gamma0/(1-gamma0))-stable relative to its own second moment: represent a weight on the inliers as a weight on the full sample set to zero on omitted rows; both its weighted covariance and the unweighted inlier covariance are within gamma0 lambda of Q. Their difference is at most 2gamma0 lambda, and the inlier covariance has minimum eigenvalue at least (1-gamma0)lambda. Thus run the uniform theorem at deletion parameter alpha=21epsilon and choose gamma0 small enough that 2gamma0/(1-gamma0) is the stability parameter required by the robust-PCA theorem. This handles an adversary that chooses which rows to replace after seeing all clean observations. The scale estimator's guarantee and the uniform covariance event are intersected by a union bound; no conditional-independence assertion is needed.

5.5 End-to-end initializer and learning interface

Under the Gaussian phase-retrieval assumptions, r_up>=K4/s, a robust scale estimator producing |s_hat-s|<=s/10, a threshold interval satisfying (6), the stability conditions (1)–(3) with alpha=21epsilon and gamma0=c/(1+r_up^2), and 20epsilon<gamma0, the following initializer is supported:

    (i) Compute robust s_hat from the responses y_i in the same corrupted batch.
    (ii) Set tau_hat=c_tau sqrt(d)(1+r_up^2)s_hat and X_i=y_i a_i 1{||y_i a_i||<=tau_hat}.
    (iii) Run the SampleTopEigenvector algorithm of Diakonikolas–Kane–Pensia–Pittas (ICML 2023) on the truncated corrupted data, with its required stability parameter.
    (iv) Return x0=sqrt(s_hat) u_hat.

The robust-PCA guarantee is a Rayleigh quotient bound

    lambda_1(Q_tau)-u_hat^T Q_tau u_hat <= C gamma0 Lambda.

If Delta_tau=lambda_1(Q_tau)-lambda_2(Q_tau), then

    1-|<u_hat,u_tau>|^2 <= C gamma0 Lambda/Delta_tau.

Equation (6) gives Delta_tau>=11.8s^2. Since Lambda<= (15+r_up^2)s^2, choosing gamma0<=c/(1+r_up^2) makes the directional error a sufficiently small constant. The scale error contributes at most a constant times ||x||. Constants in c_tau and gamma0 can be chosen so the sum lies inside the radius ||x||/9 required by Das–Batra's robust gradient-descent theorem. That theorem is source-reported to yield final phase error O(sigma sqrt(epsilon)/||x||) using P fresh batches, each of size O(max{d log d,log(1/delta)} sigma^2/s^2), under its stated noise and corruption conditions.

Resource accounting: the stability/initialization batch has m=C(r_up,epsilon,gamma0,delta)d log(d/delta) observations; the response mean is computed on that same batch. Robust PCA runs in O(m d gamma0^{-2} log^4(d/epsilon)) time under the cited 2023 theorem, which is input-linear up to polylogs. The scale estimator costs its dimension-one robust-mean runtime. Gradient refinement adds P fresh batches, with P and their sizes from Das–Batra Theorem 4.2; for fixed r_up, epsilon, and confidence the total remains near-linear in sample count. Batch storage is O(md) for explicitly materialized vectors. No bit-complexity or finite-precision implementation guarantee is proved here; the theorem is over exact real-valued observations and requires cutoff/covariance computations to error below the allocated beta lambda margin.

6. NON-GAUSSIAN DESIGNS: WHAT TRANSFERS AND WHAT DOES NOT

The restricted covariance lemma and Theorem 3 transfer from Gaussian to every isotropic K-subgaussian design. The response may depend on the entire design row, and the only response-tail input is eta (or a bound on E[Y^4 aa^T]). Thus robust spectral estimation of the top eigenspace of Q_tau is available for general weighted subgaussian designs under the stated covariance floor and tail conditions.

Learning a named parameter x needs an additional population identification condition: Q0 must have a positive top eigengap and its top eigenvector must align with x. Subgaussianity alone does not imply this. Exact counterexample: let a have independent Rademacher coordinates, x=e_1, zeta=0. Then Y=(a^T x)^2=1. For tau>=sqrt(d), X=a and Q=I_d, whose top eigenspace is all of R^d. The data contain no phase-retrieval information about which coordinate is x. No theorem that merely replaces Gaussian by isotropic subgaussian can guarantee recovery of x from this spectral initializer. A general extension must state a model-specific eigengap/alignment assumption.

7. SHARPNESS: THE LOG-EPSILON LOSS CANNOT BE REMOVED

The scalar-weighted class with Y=1 and X=a~N(0,I_d) already forces epsilon log(1/epsilon). Fix a unit direction u and let Z=<a,u>. Delete samples with |Z|>t, where epsilon=P(|Z|>t)=2 barPhi(t). For the population reweighted second moment,

    u^T Q_w u = E[Z^2 1{|Z|<=t}]/(1-epsilon),
    1-u^T Q_w u = [E[Z^2 1{|Z|>t}]-epsilon]/(1-epsilon)
                    = 2t phi(t)/(1-epsilon).

The last identity follows by integration by parts. Mills' ratio gives 2t phi(t)=(1+o(1))epsilon t^2=(2+o(1))epsilon log(1/epsilon) as epsilon tends to zero. Hence no distribution-level or empirical-uniform theorem can guarantee O(epsilon) covariance stability over the full scalar-weighted Gaussian class.

The same obstruction occurs in the exact noiseless phase-retrieval signal model, even after norm truncation. Let d tend to infinity, x=e_1, s=1, a=(Z_1,...,Z_d) standard Gaussian, Y=Z_1^2, and truncate at ||Ya||<=c sqrt(d) for fixed sufficiently large c. For nonzero X=Ya, X_1=Z_1^3 and X_2=Z_1^2 Z_2, so the deleted event |Z_2|>t is measurable from X by |X_2|/|X_1|^(2/3)>t. Set w(X)=1 on X=0 and otherwise w(X)=1{|X_2|<=t|X_1|^(2/3)}. As d tends to infinity, the truncation event tends to C={Z_1^2<=c}; it is asymptotically independent of Z_2. Write p_c=P(C)>0. The deleted mass tends to p_c epsilon, and the u=e_2 second moment before weighting tends to E[Z_1^4 1_C]. The weighted-to-original second-moment ratio tends to

    E[Z_2^2 1{|Z_2|<=t}]/(1-p_c epsilon).

Its relative deficit is

    [2t phi(t)+(1-p_c)epsilon]/(1-p_c epsilon),

which is (2+o(1))epsilon log(1/epsilon). Since the deleted mass p_c epsilon is <=epsilon, this is an admissible deletion. Thus the logarithmic loss is not an artifact of bounding Y by L; it is present in the target's noiseless Gaussian instance. It also explains why replacing the subset lemma with an O(epsilon) claim would be false.
