1. Exact finite-chain identity
------------------------------
Use the interface of sol_ground_xxz/RESULT.txt. There are S=L-1 sites,
n_i=(I-Z_i)/2, N=sum_i n_i, W=sum_{i=1}^{S-1}n_i n_{i+1},

 Hcrit = J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-X_iX_{i+1}-Y_iY_{i+1}-Z_iZ_{i+1})
             + J(I-Z_1)+J(I-Z_S),
 H_L = Hcrit - (kappa/L^2)W - (lambda/L^2)N.

Both endpoint terms are retained when L=2, where they act on the same site.
For J>0, kappa>=0, and real lambda, set

 Delta_L = 1+kappa/(4 J L^2),
 b_L = (kappa+lambda)/(2L^2),
 C_L = 2J-kappa(S-1)/(2L^2)-lambda S/(2L^2),

 Hdiag(Delta,h_-,h_+) = sum_{i=1}^{S-1}
       [X_iX_{i+1}+Y_iY_{i+1}+Delta(Z_iZ_{i+1}-I)]
       +h_- Z_1+h_+ Z_S.

Direct expansion gives the operator identity

 H_L = C_L I - J Hdiag(Delta_L,Delta_L,Delta_L)
                   + b_L sum_{i=1}^S Z_i.                 (1)

Proof. The local products are
 n_i n_{i+1}=(I-Z_i-Z_{i+1}+Z_iZ_{i+1})/4,
 N=(S I-sum_i Z_i)/2.
The coefficient of each bulk XX and YY term in the right side is -J; its
ZZ coefficient is -J Delta_L=-J-kappa/(4L^2). The remaining scalar and
linear Z terms are exactly C_L, the uniform interior field b_L, and the
endpoint field -J Delta_L after the two boundary contributions are combined.
For the first and last site the one-bond contact contributes half the bulk
linear field; the boundary term supplies the other half. This gives the same
b_L at endpoints and in the interior. The identity remains valid for S=1
because the bond sum is empty while both endpoint fields remain present.
The exact checker below verifies every matrix entry with rational arithmetic
for representative parameters and all configurations through L=7.

There is a particularly useful equivalent normalization. Let

 h_{i,i+1}^{Delta}=1/4 I-S_i^zS_{i+1}^z
                  -(S_i^xS_{i+1}^x+S_i^yS_{i+1}^y)/Delta,
 H^{delta}_{[1,S]}=sum_i h_{i,i+1}^{Delta}
                    +delta/2(I-S_1^z-S_S^z),

where S_i^a=Pauli_i^a/2. Then

 H_L = 4J Delta_L H^{1}_{[1,S]} - (kappa+lambda)N/L^2. (2)

This is exact. The vacuum scalar cancels because
4J Delta_L H^1 has vacuum energy zero. The two expressions (1) and (2)
are equivalent under the Pauli/spin-one-half normalization. In particular,
the target has the source droplet boundary parameter delta=1 exactly; it does
not have kink/antikink boundaries and it is not periodic.

2. Integrable diagonal boundaries and Bethe energy
---------------------------------------------------
Kitanine, Kozlowski, Maillet, Niccoli, Slavnov and Terras use the open-chain
Hamiltonian

 sum_i [sigma_i^x sigma_{i+1}^x+sigma_i^y sigma_{i+1}^y
       +Delta(sigma_i^z sigma_{i+1}^z-I)]
       +h_- sigma_1^z+h_+ sigma_S^z,

and parametrize Delta=cosh(eta), h_+-=sinh(eta)coth(xi_+-). In the target,
eta_L=arcosh(Delta_L), and h_-=h_+=Delta_L. Therefore xi_-=xi_+=eta_L is
an exact real diagonal-K-matrix choice, since sinh(eta)coth(eta)=cosh(eta).
At kappa=0 use the rational XXX limit eta->0 with xi/eta->1; do not evaluate
the product as sinh(0)coth(finite xi).

The R matrix and diagonal K matrices generate the commuting double-row
transfer family in the cited source. Each preserves total Z, so the term
b_L sum_i Z_i changes eigenvalues but leaves the common Bethe eigenvectors
and Bethe equations unchanged. This is why arbitrary real lambda does not
break this exact integrability statement.

For an N-down-spin Bethe solution with open-chain momenta p_1,...,p_N, the
source energy formula gives, relative to the all-up state,

 E_L = 4J sum_{j=1}^N(Delta_L-cos p_j)
                    -(kappa+lambda)N/L^2
     = 4J sum_{j=1}^N(1-cos p_j)-lambda N/L^2.           (3)

The last equality is exact because 4J(Delta_L-1)=kappa/L^2. The p_j are
coupled by the open Bethe equations with xi_-=xi_+=eta_L. Formula (3) is
valid for a Bethe solution; this packet does not infer from it that every
state is represented with nonsingular roots at all parameters. At kappa=0
the bulk interaction is isotropic, but the endpoint Z fields (and generally
the uniform Z field) break global SU(2); no enhanced global-SU(2)
degeneracy is claimed for this target. Completeness and singular-root issues
remain unresolved.

The algebraic reduction itself has O(L) terms and coefficients with bit
length O(bitlen(J,kappa,lambda)+log L) for rational input; it incurs no
precision loss. Using Bethe roots numerically is a separate algorithmic
problem. Since |d cos p/dp|<=1 for real p, a crude sufficient root accuracy
for absolute error epsilon in the scaled energy L^2 E is
max_j |delta p_j|<=epsilon/(4J N L^2). Complex roots need conditioning of
cos(p) on their certified enclosures as well. Neither the source nor this
packet gives a uniform lower bound on the Bethe Jacobian or bit-cost for
certifying roots when eta_L=Theta(1/L). Direct exact diagonalization remains
dimension 2^S; enumerating all eigenstates also has 2^S output states.

3. Exact finite-size confinement witness
-----------------------------------------
In the one-particle sector W=0. Hcrit is the length-S Dirichlet discrete
Laplacian with diagonal 4J and nearest-neighbor entries -2J. Its normalized
modes are proportional to sin(pi r i/L), i=1,...,L-1, r=1,...,L-1; hence

 E_{1,r}=4J[1-cos(pi r/L)]-lambda/L^2,
 L^2 E_{1,1}=2J pi^2-lambda-J pi^4/(6L^2)+O(L^-4).       (4)

This exact sector is independent of kappa. It supplies a finite-size
upper bound on the exact vacuum threshold: if lambda exceeds
4J L^2[1-cos(pi/L)], the vacuum is not a ground state.

4. Why the infinite-chain droplet formula does not transfer
-----------------------------------------------------------
Nachtergaele, Spitzer and Starr study the ferromagnetic XXZ droplet
Hamiltonian H^delta with delta>=1 and, for fixed Delta>1 and fixed magnon
number n, take chain length M->infinity. Their limiting droplet energy is

 E_Z(n)=(1-q^2)(1-q^n)/[(1+q^2)(1+q^n)]
      =tanh(eta)tanh(n eta/2),
 Delta=(q+q^-1)/2=cosh(eta).                           (5)

The target indeed matches their open droplet boundary delta=1, but it is in
the simultaneous regime Delta_L->1, eta_L~a/L, and possibly n/L->p>0,
where a=sqrt(kappa/(2J)). Neither fixed-Delta nor fixed-n uniformity is in
their theorem.

The failure is not just a missing proof. If one substitutes (5) into the
target at n=1, it predicts

 4J Delta_L tanh(eta_L)tanh(eta_L/2)
                    -(kappa+lambda)/L^2
       = -lambda/L^2+O(L^-4),

whereas (4) is 2J pi^2/L^2-lambda/L^2+O(L^-4). The omitted Dirichlet
confinement is 2J pi^2/L^2, exactly the leading order of the target energy.

For n/L->p, the same unjustified substitution predicts the speed-L energy

 g_formal(p)=4J a tanh(a p/2)-(kappa+lambda)p.

At lambda=0 and any kappa>0, tanh(x)<x gives
g_formal(p)<2J a^2 p-kappa p=0 for every p>0. Yet the candidate variational
theorem in sol_ground_xxz/RESULT.txt gives e_*(kappa,0)=0 whenever
0<=kappa<=lambda_c/3, lambda_c=2J pi^2. Thus the infinite-chain formula
cannot be used as a macroscopic finite-interval sector asymptotic: a
finite-size confinement correction of order 1/L is required, not a lower-
order error. This is a precise obstruction to that transfer, not a
counterexample to the candidate ground theorem.

The high-ceiling missing result is a uniform *joint* Bethe asymptotic for
the open chain with eta_L L->a, n/L->p, and both xi_±=eta_L. To match the
continuum profile theorem it must prove, with an o(1/L) error at the physical
energy scale, that

 L 4J Delta_L E^{delta=1}_{L-1,n_L}
   -> inf_{theta in H^1_0, 0<=theta<=pi, integral sin^2(theta/2)=p}
      [ (J/2)integral theta'^2
        +kappa integral rho(1-rho) ],
 rho=sin^2(theta/2).                                   (6)

Then subtracting (kappa+lambda)p gives the fixed-density branch of
sol_ground_xxz/RESULT.txt. A full-Fock free-energy theorem additionally
needs a uniform count/entropy control over all Bethe-root configurations;
ground-string energies alone do not give it. Deriving (6), its root
conditioning, and the root-counting/heat-trace bound remains UNKNOWN here.
