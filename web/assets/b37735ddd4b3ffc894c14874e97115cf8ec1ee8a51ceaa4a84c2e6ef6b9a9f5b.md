## Why the family-271 transfer is the best next theorem attack

The pinned release theorem concerns fixed spin on even periodic `L^3` cubes, isotropic nearest-neighbor Heisenberg coupling, and fixed sufficiently low positive temperature. Its conclusion is stronger than SU(2) symmetry: the normalized total spin concentrates at the field-selected pressure derivative `m=p'(0+)`, so the symmetric state converges in moments to a uniform direction at deterministic radius `m`. The key missing input is not rotation invariance but a spatial comparison: a weak exponential estimate for all zero-sum block tests, followed by replica pressure tangents that rule out equilibrium states with different magnetization radii.

For the anisotropic model `H=-sum_x sum_i J_i S_x·S_{x+e_i}`, with fixed `J_i>0`, the exchange representation changes each slot-edge Poisson rate from `1/2` to `J_i/2`. On every available-slot graph the corresponding Dirichlet form satisfies `J_min D_0 <= D_J <= J_max D_0`, which carries the released Nash inequality and unit-flow estimates with changed constants. The upper rate `J_max` preserves the local factorial mark and short-time-flag tails. In the pressure-tangent construction, the cube bond Laplacian becomes the weighted bond Laplacian; its spectral gap is at least `J_min` times the unweighted gap, so the small-cube negative-square cancellation survives. The large-cube compensator needs only a `J_max` factor in its operator-norm bound. The candidate theorem, model statement, proof substitutions, measurement limits, and proof gap are preserved in [the transfer note](proofs/anisotropic_spherical_law.md); the source audit is [here](source_notes/family271_source_audit.md).

This would concern spatial exchange anisotropy while retaining isotropic spin interactions. It would not cover XXZ/spin anisotropy, fields, disorder, frustrated bonds, or vanishing exchange directions. The release’s source-reported companion Bloch-law theorem already allows broader anisotropic finite-range interactions, but it does not state this finite-volume spherical law. A targeted search found no independent anisotropic spherical-law result; priority remains UNKNOWN. The family index reports no expert review and no independent build, so the source itself remains unvalidated under the mandate’s evidence scheme.

## Exact biology construction

The full CTMC matrices and derivation are in [the proof note](proofs/tcr_alias.md), with exact rational checks in [the script](code/tcr_alias_verify.py). Model A is an irreversible sequential two-state chain with `T_A=[[-3,1],[0,-1]]`, absorption vector `(2,1)`, and initial state 1. Model B has `T_B=[[-11/4,3/4],[7/12,-5/4]]`, absorption `(2,2/3)`, and the same initial state. The similarity matrix `S=[[1,0],[1/4,3/4]]` satisfies `T_B=S^-1 T_A S`, `alpha_B=alpha_A S`, and `r_B=S^-1 r_A`, while both CTMCs have strictly positive valid rates.

Both activation-time laws have density `(3/2)e^(-3t)+(1/2)e^(-t)`. Thus no number of exact activation-time observations, nor any set of independent exponential off-rate interventions, can distinguish the architecture pair; equal-prior minimax classification error is `1/2`. State-2 occupancy differs by at most `1/(12 sqrt(3))`, attained at `t=(log 3)/2`. For a known-rate, ideal binary state readout, 2,589 independent episodes suffice for one-sided 0.05 Hoeffding error. That excludes reporter calibration, clustering, missing tracks, and equilibrium/preparation cost, all UNKNOWN. This is a model-only prediction. Prior art on phase-type nonidentifiability and current sequential-vs-parallel TCR modeling preempt any novelty claim. The high-ceiling next test is same-receptor acquisition of ITAM/ZAP70 intermediate state and LAT-condensation timing under randomized ligand dwell times, with parameter-matched models evaluated on held-out conditions.
