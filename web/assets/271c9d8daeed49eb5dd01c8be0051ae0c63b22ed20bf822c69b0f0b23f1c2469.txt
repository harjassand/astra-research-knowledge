PURPOSE AND SCOPE

The width mechanism turns phase-space volume into a lower bound on the
maximum quantum branch dimension of a whole-state archive. Combined with a
number-cutoff encoder, it gives a matched law for a growing family. For every
fixed number p>=1 of complex coherent modes, there is an explicit constant
c>0 such that, for R_epsilon=c epsilon^(-1/2), the minimum branch dimension
Q_min(epsilon;R_epsilon) needed to reconstruct every normalized coherent
state in the radius-R_epsilon ball to worst-case half-trace error at most
epsilon satisfies

  Q_min(epsilon;R_epsilon)=Theta_p(epsilon^(-p)) as epsilon->0.

The archive may use arbitrary classical labels, but the upper construction
uses none and the lower permits unlimited labels. The input is one pure
coherent state and the output is that same complete density operator. There
is no purifying reference, entanglement assistance, unknown frame, or
acquisition task. The statement is about branch dimension, not total
classical-plus-quantum storage.

LOWER BOUND FROM OVERLAP GEOMETRY AND PHASE-SPACE VOLUME

Let |alpha> be the normalized p-mode coherent vector for alpha in C^p,
||alpha||<=R. Its overlap magnitude is

  |<alpha,beta>|=exp(-||alpha-beta||^2/2).

Joining alpha to vacuum by ceil(||alpha||) equal straight-line steps of
length at most one stays inside the ball and gives

  kappa <= exp(1/2) ceil(R) <= exp(1/2)(R+1).

The coherent-state resolution of the identity is

  integral_(C^p) |alpha><alpha| d^(2p)alpha/pi^p = I.

The real 2p-dimensional radius-R ball has volume pi^p R^(2p)/p!. Its
uniform prior therefore has mean state

  sigma_R = p!/R^(2p) integral_(||alpha||<=R)
                       |alpha><alpha| d^(2p)alpha/pi^p
          <= p!/R^(2p) I.

For every positive contraction T with Tr T<=Q,
Tr(T sigma_R)<=p!Q/R^(2p). The exact covariance-spectrum dual in the width
theorem consequently gives

  omega_Q >= [1-p!Q/R^(2p)]_+,
  e_Q >= [1-p!Q/R^(2p)]_+ / [148 exp(1)(R+1)^2].

If R>=1 and Q<R^(2p)/(2p!), then the width is strictly greater than 1/2,
and thus

  e_Q > 1/[296 exp(1)(R+1)^2]
      >= 1/[1184 exp(1) R^2].

Choose any fixed c>0 with c^2<=1/[1184 exp(1)] and set
R=c epsilon^(-1/2). For sufficiently small epsilon, R>=1 and the last
quantity is at least epsilon. Thus any archive with error at most epsilon
must have

  Q >= R^(2p)/(2p!) = c^(2p) epsilon^(-p)/(2p!).

This is a worst-case lower bound despite unlimited classical labels, because
the hybrid theorem already takes the infimum over arbitrary outcome
alphabets and uses the maximum branch dimension.

MATCHING NUMBER-CUTOFF ENCODER

Let Pi_M project onto total photon number at most M across the p modes. Its
rank is

  Q_M = #{(n_1,...,n_p) in Z_+^p: sum n_i<=M} = binom(M+p,p).

For a coherent state |alpha>, total photon number is Poisson with mean
mu=||alpha||^2<=R^2. Put L=log(4/epsilon^2) and choose

  M = ceil(R^2 + sqrt(2 R^2 L) + (2/3)L).

Poisson stochastic monotonicity and the Bernstein tail
Pr[N-mu>=t] <= exp(-t^2/[2(mu+t/3)]) imply
Pr[N>M] <= exp(-L)=epsilon^2/4 uniformly on the ball. The encoder maps
rho to

  V Pi_M rho Pi_M V^dagger
    + Tr[(I-Pi_M)rho] |0><0|,

where V is a fixed unitary identification of Pi_M H with C^(Q_M), and
|0> is any fixed unit vector in that code space. This is a parameter-
independent trace-preserving channel into a Q_M-dimensional branch. The
decoder applies V^dagger and prepares the corresponding fixed cutoff-space
fallback on the failure term. For a pure input with tail delta, the gentle
measurement bound gives half-trace error at most sqrt(delta)+delta/2, hence
less than epsilon when delta<=epsilon^2/4 and 0<epsilon<=1.

For fixed p and R=c epsilon^(-1/2),
M=R^2+O(R sqrt(log(1/epsilon))+log(1/epsilon))=Theta(epsilon^(-1)), so
Q_M=binom(M+p,p)=Theta_p(epsilon^(-p)). This matches the lower exponent.

COSTS, LIMITATIONS, AND RESTART

The exact upper channel has one branch of dimension Q_M and no classical
label. The lower allows any number of classical outcomes and charges the
largest branch. For fixed p the rank is Theta_p(epsilon^(-p)); the displayed
M supplies an explicit finite-epsilon cutoff. Encoder/decoder circuit size,
cutoff implementation time, precision, gate synthesis, state preparation,
and physical memory overhead are not bounded. The lower uses the candidate
width theorem and is not an independent proof of its channel inequality.

The growing-radius premise is essential to this deduction: it does not say
that a fixed-radius chart needs polynomial memory in 1/epsilon, and it does
not transfer to mixed states or to the polarized iid-qutrit experiment.
