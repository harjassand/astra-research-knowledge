P15 | One source-idler noise mode suffices after fanout
2026-10-08

For R=4^k, lambda=2^(-R^2), and uniform loss eta=1/R, add centered
phase-insensitive Gaussian displacement noise of mean nu to only the TMSV
source-idler mode before a rational Walsh fanout. Leave the retained signal
and the other vacuum input modes unnoised. With t=eta*nu, the exact vacuum-
reference probability of one photon in every idler output is

    q=(R!/R^R) t^R/(1+t)^(R+1).

For nu=R^(-a), fixed a>=1, log2(q/lambda)=R^2-O_a(R log R), so q/lambda
diverges. At R=16, eta=nu=1/16 gives
q/lambda=16!*2^64*(256/257)^17>2^107. The input TMSV is lambda-close to
global vacuum; conditioning continuity then puts the noisy retained signal
within 1/(2^107-1) of vacuum. The ideal conditional signal is more than 5/8
from vacuum up to an error below 17*2^-512. Thus these finite conditional
count laws differ by more than 1/2 in total variation.

This is the minimal one-source-mode profile: fanout creates a dense rank-one
noise covariance with positive marginal variance on all R herald ports. For
independent noise applied after fanout, leaving any required port untouched
makes q=0; with means t_j, the exact threshold is
product_j[t_j/(1+t_j)^2]>lambda. For uniform per-port mean t this is
equivalent to t/(1+t)^2>2^(-R). At fixed total post-loss mean t=1/256,
equal allocation is the best independent profile: log(x/(1+x)^2) is concave
for x<1+sqrt(2), and Jensen gives q_eq=[(t/R)/(1+t/R)^2]^R. At R=16,
q_eq/lambda=2^64*(4096/4097)^32>2^63, so this cost-matched baseline also
beats lambda and yields an ideal/noisy conditional TV gap above 1/2. The
rank-one profile has q/q_eq=16!*(4097/4096)^32*(256/257)^17>2^43. Both use
the same total input noise, 1/16 mean photons; rejection costs 1/q, which is
exp(Theta(R log R)) for the rank-one family.

The additive-displacement channel convention is grounded in Giovannetti et
al., arXiv:quant-ph/0404037 (submitted 2004-04-06; published 2004-08-30):
[arXiv source](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0404037). It gives the Gaussian
noise map and vacuum-to-thermal law, not this herald construction. P12 supplies
the ideal herald law; P14 the postselection-continuity inequality; P18 the
general vacuum-reference correlated-noise calculation. P15's distinct step
is transferring the explicit single-source-idler profile to the actual TMSV.
