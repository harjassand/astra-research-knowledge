# Inverse-free polar/spectral re-encoding of the complement-LCU image

**Status.** An exact algebraic construction avoids $Y^{-1}$. After a failure-sector polar rotation and a data spectral rotation, the complement-LCU verifier becomes a coordinate-wise two-level graph. Its exact image enforcer is a positive projection with gap one and row degree at most two; the diagonal failure test and its compression remain unchanged. This is a legal re-encoding at the abstract $(V,B,C)$ interface. It is **not** an efficient uniform BMVZ construction from the term list: the spectral basis/eigenvalue access and polar failure rotation are not supplied, and the straightforward exact preparation has exponential dimension cost. A uniform row-value oracle in energy-sorted spectral coordinates would expose the QMA-hard ground energy.

This is a new spectral re-encoding, not a sparse-enforcer claim for the original fixed computational-basis LCU image. It does not use the earlier direct term-frame or $C_0$ row-count arguments, nor the $Y^{-1}$ incidence construction.

## Interface and polar two-branch chart

Let
$$
H=\sum_{j=1}^M \pi_j h_j,\qquad 0\le h_j\le I,\qquad \sum_j\pi_j=1,
$$
be the normalized BMVZ projection sum, with the silent-zero scaling in the complement-LCU interface so that $0\le H\le 3I/4$. Put
$$
Y=I-H,\qquad F=2H-H^2=I-Y^2,\qquad X=\sqrt{F}\succeq0.
$$
The fixed complement-LCU isometry has success/failure block form
$$
V_0|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle Y|\psi\rangle+Z|\psi\rangle,\qquad
B=(I-|0\rangle\langle0|)\otimes I.
$$
Here $Z$ stacks all nonzero output-label blocks $K_a$. Since $V_0^*V_0=I$,
$$
Z^*Z=I-Y^*Y=F,\qquad V_0^*BV_0=F.
$$
In particular $H\preceq F\preceq2H$: $F-H=H(I-H)\succeq0$ and $2H-F=H^2\succeq0$.

Take the polar decomposition $Z=JX$, where $J$ is a partial isometry with initial space $\operatorname{supp}X$. Choose an isometric inclusion $\iota:\mathcal H\to\mathcal F$ into one failure-label data block. The partial isometry sending $Ju\mapsto\iota u$ for $u\in\operatorname{supp}X$ extends to a unitary $W$ on the full failure space $\mathcal F$: the two orthogonal complements have the same dimension, $\dim\mathcal F-\operatorname{rank}X$. Thus
$$
WZ=\iota X.
$$
The physical unitary $P=|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes I\oplus W$ acts only within the success/failure sectors, so $P^*BP=B$. After $P$, the same logical test is implemented by the two-branch isometry
$$
V_{\rm pol}|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle Y|\psi\rangle+|1\rangle X|\psi\rangle,
$$
with zero amplitude on unused failure labels. It is an isometry since $Y^2+X^2=I$, and $V_{\rm pol}^*BV_{\rm pol}=X^2=F$.

The polar graph has the inverse-free relation matrix
$$
T_{\rm pol}(z_0,z_1)=-Xz_0+Yz_1,\qquad
C_{\rm pol}=T_{\rm pol}^*T_{\rm pol}.
$$
Because $X$ and $Y$ are functions of $H$, they commute. Therefore
$$
T_{\rm pol}V_{\rm pol}=-XY+YX=0,\qquad
T_{\rm pol}T_{\rm pol}^*=X^2+Y^2=I.
$$
Thus $C_{\rm pol}$ is an orthogonal projection. Its kernel identity is exact without any inverse: $T_{\rm pol}T_{\rm pol}^*=I$ gives rank $d$ and hence a $d$-dimensional kernel, while $T_{\rm pol}V_{\rm pol}=0$ and the isometry $V_{\rm pol}$ put a $d$-dimensional subspace inside that kernel. Therefore $\ker T_{\rm pol}=\operatorname{ran}V_{\rm pol}$. Hence
$$
\ker C_{\rm pol}=\operatorname{ran}V_{\rm pol},\qquad
C_{\rm pol}=I-V_{\rm pol}V_{\rm pol}^*,\qquad
0\preceq C_{\rm pol}\preceq I,
$$
and the full orthogonal-complement gap is exactly one.

In block form,
$$
C_{\rm pol}=
\begin{pmatrix}
F&-XY\\
-YX&Y^2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad F=2H-H^2,\qquad Y^2=I-2H+H^2.
$$
If $s_H$ is the maximum row degree of the supplied BMVZ matrix, the direct term bound is $s_H\le M(1+2^{5R})$. The two diagonal blocks, which are degree-two polynomials in $H$, have row degree at most $O(s_H^2)$. The cross block $Y\sqrt F$ has only the universal dimension bound $d$ here. The term-list entry formula for $H$ gives no polynomial row-list for this spectral function. So this is an exact constant-gap enforcer for the polar re-encoding, but it is not yet a sparse-access enforcer in the original data basis.

## Spectral coordinates make the enforcer two-sparse

Let $H=Q\Lambda Q^*$, where $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d)$, $d=2^N$, and $0\le\lambda_k\le3/4$. Define
$$
y_k=1-\lambda_k,\qquad x_k=\sqrt{2\lambda_k-\lambda_k^2},\qquad
\widehat Y=\operatorname{diag}(y_k),\qquad
\widehat X=\operatorname{diag}(x_k).
$$
Then $x_k^2+y_k^2=1$ and $y_k\ge1/4$. Apply $S=I_{\rm label}\otimes Q^*$ to $V_{\rm pol}$. This unitary commutes with $B$, since $B$ is zero on success and identity on each failure sector. The new isometry is
$$
V_{\rm sp}=S V_{\rm pol}
=(|0\rangle\widehat Y+|1\rangle\widehat X)Q^*,
$$
padded with zero amplitude on unused failure labels. Its compression is exactly unchanged:
$$
V_{\rm sp}^*BV_{\rm sp}
=Q\widehat X^2Q^*
=Q(2\Lambda-\Lambda^2)Q^*
=2H-H^2=F.
$$
Thus the same diagonal binary test gives $H\preceq V_{\rm sp}^*BV_{\rm sp}\preceq2H$.

For each $k$, define
$$
|v_k\rangle=y_k|0,k\rangle+x_k|1,k\rangle.
$$
These vectors are orthonormal and span $\operatorname{ran}V_{\rm sp}$: right multiplication by $Q^*$ changes logical coordinates but not the range of $(|0\rangle\widehat Y+|1\rangle\widehat X)$. On the two active label blocks set
$$
C_{\rm sp}=I-\sum_{k=1}^d|v_k\rangle\langle v_k|
=\bigoplus_{k=1}^d
\begin{pmatrix}
x_k^2&-x_ky_k\\
-x_ky_k&y_k^2
\end{pmatrix}_{\{|0,k\rangle,|1,k\rangle\}}.
\tag{1}
$$
Set $C_{\rm sp}=I$ on unused failure-label sectors. The full kernel is exactly $\operatorname{ran}V_{\rm sp}$, with no extra zero subspace.

Equivalently, define the relation map
$$
T_{\rm sp}(z_0,z_1)=-\widehat Xz_0+\widehat Yz_1.
$$
Then $T_{\rm sp}T_{\rm sp}^*=I$ and $T_{\rm sp}^*T_{\rm sp}=C_{\rm sp}$. Coordinate $k$ of $T_{\rm sp}z=0$ says $-x_kz_{0,k}+y_kz_{1,k}=0$; since $y_k\ge1/4$, its solutions are $(z_{0,k},z_{1,k})=(y_kc_k,x_kc_k)$. Therefore
$$
\ker T_{\rm sp}
=\{(\widehat Yc,\widehat Xc):c\in\mathbb C^d\}
=\operatorname{ran}V_{\rm sp}.
$$
It follows that $C_{\rm sp}^2=C_{\rm sp}=C_{\rm sp}^*$, $\|C_{\rm sp}\|=1$, and $C_{\rm sp}=I-V_{\rm sp}V_{\rm sp}^*$ on the full padded space. The contraction and normalized gap are both exactly one.

## Row support and the BMVZ row-list oracle

In the re-encoded computational coordinates, every active row of (1) has at most two columns, both with the same data index $k$:
$$
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{row}&\text{column}&\text{entry}\\\hline
(0,k)&(0,k)&2\lambda_k-\lambda_k^2\\
(0,k)&(1,k)&-(1-\lambda_k)\sqrt{2\lambda_k-\lambda_k^2}\\
(1,k)&(0,k)&-(1-\lambda_k)\sqrt{2\lambda_k-\lambda_k^2}\\
(1,k)&(1,k)&(1-\lambda_k)^2.
\end{array}
$$
Unused-label rows have one diagonal entry, 1. If $\lambda_k=0$, the top active row has no nonzero entries and the bottom has one; otherwise each active row has two. Thus $s(C_{\rm sp})\le2$. Given an exact indexed eigenvalue $\lambda_k$, the row-list query outputs at most two indices in $O(1)$ arithmetic operations and computes entries to $b$ bits in $\operatorname{poly}(b,\operatorname{bits}(\lambda_k))$ time. Exact support listing also has to decide whether $\lambda_k=0$.

The missing input is the eigenvalue oracle. BMVZ gives $M=\operatorname{poly}(n)$, $R=\operatorname{poly}(n)$, a polynomial qubit count $N$, and polynomial-time specified-entry evaluation of $H$. It does not supply sorted eigenvalues of the $d=2^N$ dimensional matrix. A direct fallback assembles the dense matrix, computes eigenvalue/eigenspace data, orders the spectrum, and constructs $Q$. Dense arithmetic costs $\operatorname{poly}(d,M,R)=2^{O(N)}\operatorname{poly}(M,R)$; materializing the generic eigenbasis costs $\Omega(d^2)=2^{\Omega(N)}$ storage. To map the original complement-LCU image itself through this re-encoding one must also synthesize the failure-sector polar unitary $W$, for which a dense polar/eigenspace construction has exponential dimension cost. After this preprocessing the row output is constant-size, but this is not a polynomial-time row-list oracle from the BMVZ term generator. Although the eigenvalues/eigenvectors are algebraic when the BMVZ entries are algebraic, no polynomial-size exact representation or indexed eigenvalue algorithm is supplied.

There is a conditional complexity diagnostic. BMVZ Theorem 7.4 gives a QMA-complete promise distinguishing $\lambda_{\min}(H)\le\operatorname{negl}(n)$ from $\lambda_{\min}(H)\ge c>0$. Sort the spectral coordinates so $k=1$ indexes a ground-energy eigenvector. The row-value query at $(0,1)$ returns
$$
(C_{\rm sp})_{(0,1),(0,1)}
=2\lambda_{\min}(H)-\lambda_{\min}(H)^2.
$$
This function is monotone on $[0,3/4]$, and the two promise cases remain separated by a constant after the harmless $3/4$ scaling. A **uniform polynomial-time** coefficient oracle in these sorted coordinates would therefore decide this QMA-complete promise in P. This is not an unconditional impossibility theorem—$\mathsf{QMA}\ne\mathsf P$ is not known—but it pinpoints the hidden computation: a row-value oracle would have to provide ground-energy information. Returning just the two column indices, without coefficients, would not imply this consequence.

## Legality and scope

At the abstract verification interface this is a legal re-encoding:

* $V_{\rm sp}$ is an isometry, $B$ remains the same computational-basis diagonal failure projection, and the compression is exactly $F=2H-H^2$, hence $H\preceq V^*BV\preceq2H$.
* It is unitarily obtainable from the complement-LCU encoding by a unitary within the failure sector and a data rotation $Q^*$. Both commute with $B$, so neither the test nor its compression changes.
* $C_{\rm sp}$ is a full-space positive contraction with exact kernel and gap one, and row degree at most two in the re-encoded physical coordinates.

It is **not** an efficiently uniform construction under the polynomial-circuit and row-oracle costs of the BMVZ task unless one additionally supplies polynomial implementations of the polar failure rotation, the spectral basis $Q$, and the indexed eigenvalue map. BMVZ supplies succinct tensor-product terms and a polynomial-time verification/simulation route, not these spectral objects; see Theorems 7.3–7.4 and Lemma 7.6 of the primary source. The output rotation $Q^*$ is generally global. In the original fixed LCU basis, conjugating (1) back can restore dense rows; this report proves no polynomial row bound for $C_{\rm pol}$ or for the fixed $C_0$.

The construction gives an exact positive answer to the **algebraic re-encoding** question and a two-sparse matrix after spectral preprocessing. Whether a distinct polynomial-uniform BMVZ re-encoding or an exact sparse enforcer for the fixed LCU image exists remains open.
