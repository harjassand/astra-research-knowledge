# Sparse Gaussian archive: dimension-uniform converse

Date: 2026-10-07. Status: a complete internal mathematical proof under the interface below. External correctness review and novelty are not established. The smooth score was proposed by the root agent and audited here; the independent clipped quadratic score below was an earlier, weaker route.

Update: `query_appendix.md` independently audits a stronger calibrated proper loss, improving the additive constant to `5/4+log 2`, and proves the same leading converse for separate query-dependent quantum measurements without a common reconstructed vector. The proof below remains a valid standalone weaker-constant version.

## Exact interface and theorem

Let `d >= 1`, `0 < r <= 1`, and `P_theta = N(theta,I_d)`. The encoder sees one observation `X`, not the unknown mean `theta`. It may know `d`, `r`, the target family, and the requested accuracy. Its retained state is in

\[
\mathcal A=\bigoplus_j M_{d_j},\qquad D=\sum_jd_j<\infty.
\]

Every input-dependent retained classical register is included as a block label in this algebra. The decoder receives only this state and an optional independent shared random seed. It outputs `Y in R^d` by an arbitrary POVM; fresh randomness and continuous outputs are allowed. There is no uncounted input-dependent side information or entanglement assistance. At each seed the total dimension is at most `D`. The output need not be independent of the input observation: only its law is constrained.

Suppose the same blind encoder and decoder satisfy

\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L_\theta(Y),P_\theta)\le\varepsilon
\quad\text{for every }\theta\in\{\pm r e_i:1\le i\le d\}.
\]

The zero-mean law may also be required, but is not needed for the converse. All logarithms in the proof are natural. Then

\[
\boxed{\quad
\log D\ \ge\ \frac d2\log\frac r\varepsilon
-\left(\frac54+\frac94\log2\right)d.
\quad}\tag{1}
\]

The constant is about `2.80959` nats per coordinate. In bits,

\[
\log_2D\ge\frac d2\log_2(r/\varepsilon)-4.05337\,d.
\]

This is dimension-uniform and signal-uniform. Consequently `log D >= c d log(r/eps)` for some absolute `c > 0` whenever `eps/r` is sufficiently small, uniformly over arbitrary joint variation of `d` and `r`. Its leading coefficient is `1/2`, up to an additive `O(d)` term. No hypothesis count or compact-cube probability depending exponentially on `d` appears.

## 1. A bounded spherical score extracts the correct weak-signal distortion

Fix coordinate `i`. Put a binary prior on `theta = T r e_i`, where `T` is uniform on `{-1,1}`. Let `Q_i` be the resulting input mixture. Its density relative to the null product Gaussian `P_0` is

\[
\frac{dQ_i}{dP_0}(x)=e^{-r^2/2}\cosh(rx_i)\ge e^{-r^2/2}.
\tag{2}
\]

The label posterior is

\[
m_i(x)=\mathbb E[T\mid X=x]=\tanh(rx_i).
\tag{3}
\]

An independent shared seed does not change this posterior. Define a convex potential and loss on `u in [-1,1]` by

\[
F_r(u)=\sqrt{r^2+u^2},\qquad
\ell_t(u)=-F_r(u)-(t-u)F_r'(u)
=-\frac{r^2+t u}{\sqrt{r^2+u^2}}.
\tag{4}
\]

For an outcome `t`, the conditional risk at posterior mean `m` is minimized by reporting `u=m`. Its excess is exactly

\[
\mathbb E[\ell_T(u)-\ell_T(m)\mid X]
=D_{F_r}(m,u)
=F_r(m)-F_r(u)-F_r'(u)(m-u).
\tag{5}
\]

The score is uniformly bounded in the required sense. Indeed,

\[
\ell_+'(u)=\frac{r^2(u-1)}{(r^2+u^2)^{3/2}}\le0,
\]

and the minus-label loss is increasing. Evaluation at the endpoints gives the exact oscillation

\[
\sup_{u\in[-1,1]}\ell_t(u)-\inf_{u\in[-1,1]}\ell_t(u)
=\frac2{\sqrt{1+r^2}}.
\tag{6}
\]

Use `m_i(Y)` as the decoder's report. The two TV guarantees, applied to the two bounded losses separately, imply

\[
0\le
\mathbb E_{Q_i}[D_{F_r}(m_i(X),m_i(Y))]
\le\frac{2\varepsilon}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}.
\tag{7}
\]

The expectation includes all encoder, decoder, and shared-seed randomness. Its nonnegativity follows pointwise from convexity, rather than from any assumption about the actual posterior given `Y`. Blindness is used here: conditional on `X` and the seed, the decoder output is independent of `T`.

Normalize the two-vector `(r,m)`. The exact identity

\[
D_{F_r}(m,u)=\frac{F_r(m)}2
\left\|
\frac{(r,m)}{F_r(m)}-\frac{(r,u)}{F_r(u)}
\right\|^2
\tag{8}
\]

gives a global distortion bound, without restricting either observation to a compact set. Define

\[
h_r(x)=\frac{\tanh(rx)}{\sqrt{r^2+\tanh^2(rx)}}.
\tag{9}
\]

Since `F_r(m) >= r`, equations (7)-(8), followed by (2), yield under one common null input law

\[
\mathbb E_{P_0}
\big[h_r(X_i)-h_r(Y_i)\big]^2
\le
\kappa_r\frac\varepsilon r,
\qquad
\kappa_r=\frac{4e^{r^2/2}}{\sqrt{1+r^2}}
\le2\sqrt{2e}.
\tag{10}
\]

The final bound follows because `exp(t/2)/sqrt(1+t)` is increasing on `0 <= t <= 1`. Crucially, the distortion is `eps/r`, not `eps/r^2`, and its constant has no dimension dependence. Although each score is derived using a different binary prior, the positive mixture-density bound transfers every one of them to the same joint null experiment `(X,Y,seed)`.

## 2. The transformed scalar Gaussian has uniformly positive entropy power

For every `r > 0`, the map `h_r` is continuously differentiable and strictly increasing on the real line. Its derivative is

\[
h_r'(x)=
\frac{\operatorname{sech}^2(rx)}
{\big(1+(\tanh(rx)/r)^2\big)^{3/2}}>0.
\tag{11}
\]

Let `G ~ N(0,1)` and `U_r=h_r(G)`. By change of variables,

\[
\mathsf h(U_r)=\tfrac12\log(2\pi e)
-2\mathbb E\log\cosh(rG)
-\tfrac32\mathbb E\log\big(1+(\tanh(rG)/r)^2\big).
\tag{12}
\]

The formula is legitimate: the logarithmic derivative is integrable from below, for example by the bound below, and it is at most zero. Using `log cosh z <= z^2/2`, `|tanh(rx)|/r <= |x|`, and Jensen's inequality gives

\[
\mathsf h(U_r)
\ge\tfrac12\log(2\pi e)-r^2-\tfrac32\log2
\ge\tfrac12\log(2\pi e)-1-\tfrac32\log2.
\tag{13}
\]

This eliminates the atoms and the central-event entropy penalty of the clipped quadratic approach. The transformed observation remains a genuinely continuous scalar random variable even when the source family consists of finitely many means.

## 3. Scalar distortion costs information, and independent costs add

For any absolutely continuous scalar `U` with finite differential entropy and any reconstruction `V`, the squared-error information bound is

\[
I(U;V)\ge\mathsf h(U)-\tfrac12\log(2\pi e\,\mathbb E(U-V)^2).
\tag{14}
\]

For completeness, compare the conditional law of `U` given `V=v` with the Gaussian density of mean `v` and variance `delta`. Nonnegativity of conditional relative entropy gives

\[
I(U;V)\ge\mathsf h(U)-\tfrac12\log(2\pi\delta)
-\frac{\mathbb E(U-V)^2}{2\delta}.
\]

Choose `delta=E(U-V)^2`. Zero distortion forces infinite information for this continuous source. This proof applies to discrete, continuous, or mixed `V` and avoids informal conditional-differential-entropy manipulations.

Write `S` for the independent public seed, and `Z=(Y,S)`. Under the null, the coordinates `X_i` are independent. Chain rule and nonnegativity of conditional mutual information give

\[
I(X;Z)\ge\sum_{i=1}^d I(X_i;Z)
\ge\sum_{i=1}^d I(h_r(X_i);h_r(Y_i)).
\tag{15}
\]

Apply (10), (13), and (14) coordinate by coordinate. This yields

\[
I(X;Y,S)\ge
\frac d2\log\frac r\varepsilon
-d\left(r^2+\tfrac32\log2+\tfrac12\log\kappa_r\right).
\tag{16}
\]

No per-seed reconstruction guarantee is required: the distortion in (10) is already averaged over the seed.

## 4. An elementary POVM trace bound caps all accessible information by log D

At a fixed seed `s`, let `rho_{x,s}` be the retained density matrix on the direct-sum Hilbert space, and `M_s(dy)` the decoder POVM. The probability measure

\[
q_s(dy)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}M_s(dy)}D
\]

is normalized. Because every density matrix obeys `0 <= rho_{x,s} <= I`,

\[
p_s(dy\mid x)=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{x,s}M_s(dy)]
\le Dq_s(dy).
\tag{17}
\]

Thus `KL(p_s(.|x)||q_s) <= log D`. Relative-entropy chain rule gives

\[
I(X;Y\mid S=s)
\le\mathbb E_X\operatorname{KL}(p_s(.\mid X)\Vert q_s)
\le\log D.
\tag{18}
\]

Average over `s`; since `X` and `S` are independent, `I(X;Y,S)=I(X;Y|S) <= log D`. This works directly for continuous input ensembles and arbitrary POVM output spaces, and counts hybrid block labels by the total `sum d_j`. It is the needed dimension consequence of the usual accessible-information bound; a full quantum-channel capacity theorem is unnecessary.

Combining (16)-(18) and using

\[
r^2+\tfrac32\log2+\tfrac12\log\kappa_r
\le1+\tfrac32\log2+\tfrac12\log(2\sqrt{2e})
=\tfrac54+\tfrac94\log2
\]

proves (1).

## Adversarial checks and failed shortcuts

* **Tiny signal:** constants remain bounded as `r -> 0`. In fact `h_r(G)` converges to `G/sqrt(1+G^2)`, rather than collapsing to zero. The scale-dependent proper score is the reason the required error is linear in `r`.
* **Growing dimension:** there is no conditioning on the event that all coordinates lie in a fixed cube. Independence is used only under the actual null product Gaussian, and each mixture domination factor is scalar.
* **Only finitely many laws:** a finite label alphabet is not a finite observation alphabet. The encoder does not receive the mean label. If the label were supplied, `2d` classical states would reconstruct the family exactly, showing why blindness is essential.
* **Quantum amplitudes:** a continuum of encoded states is allowed. Equation (17) still bounds accessible information, so analog amplitudes do not constitute an uncharged continuous classical record.
* **Public random seeds:** cancellation of output-law errors across seeds is allowed, but cannot cancel nonnegative score regret. The information cap holds at every seed and therefore after averaging.
* **Selecting one coordinate:** even retaining one chosen Gaussian coordinate perfectly and resampling the others gives output `(1/d)P_{r e_i}+(1-1/d)P_0` at mean `r e_i`. Its error is exactly `(1-1/d)[2 Phi(r/2)-1]`, of order `r`; it cannot achieve `eps/r -> 0`. This idealized comparator already grants an unbounded scalar record.
* **Interface limits:** entanglement assistance or uncounted input-dependent side records invalidates the stated dimension model. The theorem counts retained storage, not encoding time, temporary workspace, energy, or finite-bit sampler implementation. Those costs remain separate.

The earlier clipped score `ell_t(v)=r v^2/2-tv`, `v=clip(tanh(rx)/r,[-A,A])`, correctly gives a weaker central-entropy bound `(p_A d/2)log(r/eps)-O_A(d)`. It is not needed for the final theorem. Treating its clipped distribution as wholly continuous would have been an error; the smooth score removes that obstacle.

## Consequences that follow without further Gaussian archive assumptions

**Unequal weak needle sizes.** If the target family contains `+- r_i e_i`, `0 < r_i <= 1`, with the same accuracy `eps`, the proof gives for every subset `J` of coordinates

\[
\log D\ge\sum_{i\in J}
\left[\tfrac12\log(r_i/\varepsilon)-C\right],
\quad C=\tfrac54+\tfrac94\log2.
\]

Optimize the subset to obtain

\[
\log D\ge\frac12\sum_i\log_+\frac{r_i}{e^{2C}\varepsilon}.
\tag{19}
\]

**Infinite Gaussian ellipsoid.** Consider one product observation `X_j ~ N(theta_j,1)` on `R^N`, and the known ellipsoid

\[
\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\theta_j^2}{A^2 j^{-2\alpha}}\le1,
\qquad A>0,\ \alpha>0.
\]

For a fixed sufficiently large numerical `K`, set `r=K eps <=1` and `d=floor((A/(K eps))^(1/alpha))`. Every needle `+-r e_i`, `i<=d`, lies in the ellipsoid. The extra input coordinates are independent null Gaussians in this restricted subexperiment and can be averaged into the encoder's private randomness. Projection of the reconstructed sequence to its first `d` coordinates cannot increase TV. Thus (1) yields

\[
\log D\ge c_\alpha(A/\varepsilon)^{1/\alpha}
\]

when `A/(K eps)>=1` and `eps<=1/K`, with a positive constant depending only on `alpha`. For example take `K=exp(2C+2)`, so each selected coordinate costs at least one nat. This is a proved lower bound, not a complete matching upper bound in this report. It is independent of the affine-dimension theorem's fixed-dimension constants.

## Prior art and source ledger

The entropy-distortion and quantum information parts are classical prior mechanisms. Shannon's original paper derives the continuous-source entropy lower bound and the Gaussian squared-error case; see [Coding Theorems for a Discrete Source With a Fidelity Criterion, 1959](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/algorithm/information/1959-shannon.pdf), PDF pages 20-22. Nayak's entropy method supplies linear information lower bounds for quantum random access codes; see [Optimal lower bounds for quantum automata and random access codes, 1999](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anayak/papers/Nayak99.pdf), Sections 3-5. The underlying accessible-information result is [Holevo, 1973](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi903).

Exact PDFs for Shannon and Nayak were downloaded to `sources/`. The initial Queen's University mirror for Shannon returned HTTP 404; the linked original-paper scan was retrieved successfully instead. PDF text extraction confirmed the relevant Shannon entropy argument and Nayak's Holevo statement; corrupted mathematical glyphs in the latter are not used as proof premises.

The supplied internal Astra card `N07-gaussian-memory` states a fixed-affine-dimension archive law with geometry-dependent constants, and explicitly does not address growing dimensions. The present deduction replaces that dependence with scalar mixture domination, a weak-signal spherical-score transformation, and a product information sum. No new generic rate-distortion or quantum coding principle is claimed. Priority of the exact dimension-uniform sparse-Gaussian theorem, and especially the archive ellipsoid consequence, remains unestablished by this brief source check.
