VERDICT

No factor or algebraic failure was found in the requested PBW symmetrization,
central Gram/highest-vector variance, or constants. The type-specific
Cartan determinants and Gram bounds are consistent with the chosen metric;
the PBW variance, commutator, Gram-error, and N_Gram constants follow from
the displayed coefficient norms. One normalization point is material:
the classical packet deliberately uses a different orthonormal-generator
scale from the SU(m) packet. Its constants are correct in its own metric and
must not be copied numerically from SU(m). The classical packet contains no
fundamental partial-trace/Parseval construction, so no SU partial-trace
factor should be inserted.

1. LIE METRIC AND CASIMIR SCALE

Classical packet (1.1) sets <X,Y>=Tr_fund(XY)/2 and chooses orthonormal
t_A, hence Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=2 delta_AB. This is consistent with the
classical defining-matrix convention in Macfarlane–Pfeiffer–Wagner,
arXiv:math-ph/0007040v1, eqs. (1.1)–(1.2): for B,C,D their defining
Hermitian matrices have trace products 2 delta_ij (their A-series convention
is separately scaled). The SU packet instead uses Tr(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2,
which makes t_A orthonormal for metric 2 Tr, four times the classical
packet's 1/2 Tr metric. This is a real scale change, not a contradiction:
the classical result redefines its generators, coordinates, energy, and
Casimir together, and the abstract transfer only requires an orthonormal
generator basis for its chosen invariant metric.

In the classical Cartan coordinates h_i are orthonormal: the SO block with
eigenvalues +/-z_i and the Sp block diag(z_i,-z_i) each have
(1/2)Tr(X^2)=sum_i z_i^2. Thus the listed roots (B: e_i,e_i +/- e_k;
C: 2e_i,e_i +/- e_k; D: e_i +/- e_k) and the Cartan value coordinates
use the dual metric induced by (1.1). Use that same dual metric for lambda,
rho, |lambda| and the Casimir; one must not silently replace it by a
long-root-normalized textbook metric. With these conventions the quadratic
Casimir is |Lambda|^2+2<Lambda,rho>. Checks in the defining representations
give 2l for B_l, 2l+1 for C_l, and 2l-1 for D_l, matching
|lambda|^2+2<lambda,rho> for the stated root coordinates.

Consequently (5.2) is exact: for B_A=T_A/N and highest vector psi,
sum_A ||(B_A-lambda_A I)psi||^2
= C_N/N^2-|lambda|^2 = 2<lambda,rho>/N = beta(lambda)/N.
The scalar Casimir also implies ||T_A/N||<=sqrt(K_cap)=B and, by
operator Cauchy–Schwarz, ||T_H/N||<=|x|B. Each |lambda_A|<=B follows
from |lambda|<=B. These are uniform on compact K.

2. CLASSICAL VALUE GRAMS AND ROOT-HYPERPLANE CONSTANTS

For f_j=Tr(X^(2j))/(4j), differentiation gives
df_j(X)[Y]=(1/2)Tr(X^(2j-1)Y), exactly the metric pairing with
X^(2j-1). Odd powers remain in the stated Hermitian SO/Sp Lie algebra,
so there is no projection or trace correction. On an orthonormal Cartan
basis, the component vector is (z_i^(2j-1))_i. The B/C determinant is
prod_i z_i prod_(i<k)(z_k^2-z_i^2), with the listed B/C regularity
conditions exactly excluding its zero set.

For D_l, Pf(-iX) is SO(2l)-invariant and its metric-gradient components
on the Cartan are w_i=prod_(k!=i)z_k. The determinant
(-1)^(l-1)prod_(i<k)(z_k^2-z_i^2) is correct: after proving it for
nonzero z_i by row factoring, polynomial identity extends it to z_i=0.
The D regularity condition only requires distinct z_i^2, so one zero
coordinate is correctly allowed. The Pfaffian is determinant-twisted under
O(2l), but that is immaterial for SO/Spin equivariance. Its degree-l
invariant has gradient degree l-1. The publisher-hosted primary PDF was
not fetchable during this audit (403); the transformation identity is
also immediate from the top exterior-power definition, and the packet
states this direct proof.

Let |z_i|<=R and the relevant root evaluations be at least epsilon on K.
Then every B/C difference |z_k^2-z_i^2| is at least epsilon^2, and the
determinant-square exponents are
2[l+l(l-1)]=2l^2 for B/C and 2l(l-1) for D. Thus (4.1)'s lower bounds
epsilon^(2l^2) and epsilon^(2l(l-1)) are correct. The trace sums count l
coordinates per gradient: Tr H<=l sum_(j=1)^l R^(4j-2) in B/C, and
l[sum_(j=1)^(l-1)R^(4j-2)+R^(2l-2)] in D. For H=M^T M, the positive
eigenvalues obey det(H)<=lambda_min(H)(Tr H)^(l-1), so (4.2)'s
kappa=D/A^(l-1) is valid (with exponent zero at l=1).

Uniformity requires the stated compact subset of the open chamber. It
supplies fixed R and positive epsilon (and, for B/C, a positive lower
bound on |z_i|); in D the Pfaffian determinant removes any need for such
a coordinate lower bound. These constants are input-dependent, not
universal across K.

3. PBW SYMMETRIZATION AND ALL COEFFICIENT CONSTANTS

For each component polynomial, choose a symmetric coefficient tensor and
write its PBW symmetrization as the average of ordered words in B_A=T_A/N.
Real coefficients and Hermitian B_A make each symmetrized word Hermitian;
PBW symmetrization intertwines the adjoint action, so the tensor family is
exactly covariant. For a word of degree e, the ordered telescoping identity
against its scalar means has e terms. In term j, the e-1 factors consist
of j-1 bounded operators on the left and e-j scalar means on the right;
their product norm is at most B^(e-1), with the single remainder
(B_i-lambda_i)psi acting directly on psi. The same bound holds after
averaging word permutations.

With L_s^2=sum_A(sum_indices |c_(s,A,indices)|)^2, this gives the vector
error d_s/sqrt(N), d_s=L_s e_s B^(e_s-1)sqrt(beta_0), and the value bound
u_s=L_s B^(e_s). The powers of B and N are correct, including when B<1:
the left-operator and right-scalar exponents add to exactly e_s-1. The
coefficient norms are finite and computable from the fixed polynomial
lists; they are not numerically instantiated in the packet.

For every F, the commutator product rule on each word has e_s ordered
terms. Schatten ideal bounds each by B^(e_s-1)||[B_i,F]||_2. Summing
coefficient absolute values componentwise and then squaring/summing A
gives exactly
  E_(Y_s)(F) <= L_s^2 e_s^2 B^(2e_s-2) E_T(F)/N^2.
Thus C_s in (5.4) is valid for arbitrary complex F; it needs no
normality, eigenspace alignment, or special filter. It may be loose,
for example C_1=q for the degree-one identity frame under the stated
coefficient norm, but looseness does not invalidate the transfer.

4. CENTRAL GRAM, TRACE NORMALIZATION, AND THRESHOLD

Adjoint covariance plus the invariant orthonormal-basis contraction makes
Z_st^quant=(1/2)sum_A(Y_sA Y_tA+Y_tA Y_sA) invariant, hence scalar by
Schur on the irreducible carrier. Taking its normalized trace yields
q G_(N,st) exactly: there are q generator coordinates, and G is
normalized by carrier dimension d. No additional fundamental dimension
or trace normalization appears. This is the same central-Gram mechanism
as SU(m) §5, but the polynomial coordinates already live in an
orthonormal basis of g. There is no partial trace or Parseval inequality
here. In particular, SU(m)'s Y=2 Tr_fund(...) definition and Parseval
factor 2 are not applicable to these direct PBW tensors.

Write Y_sA psi=z_sA psi+err_sA. Because z_sA are real, expansion of the
symmetrized expectation gives
  |qG_st-H_st| <= (u_s d_t+u_t d_s)/sqrt(N)+d_s d_t/N.
The cross terms and remainder product each appear once; no factor 2 is
missing. Replacing 1/N by 1/sqrt(N) for N>=1 and taking Frobenius then
operator norm gives J_0/(q sqrt(N)) with
J_0^2=sum_st(u_s d_t+u_t d_s+d_s d_t)^2. If
N>=ceil(max(1,(2J_0/kappa)^2)), this error is <=kappa/(2q), so
G_N>=kappa I/(2q) and
Tr G_N<=(A+l kappa/2)/q. The q factors and l trace-error factor are right.

The l polynomial families are independent by this positive Gram. The
separate character cutoff N_dom proves multiplicity exactly l; together
these facts make the frame complete, not merely an independent subframe.
The frame threshold is N_cut=max(N_Gram,N_dom); uniform constants depend
on fixed K, rank, metric, polynomial coefficients/degrees, and x,h range.

5. TRANSFER CONSTANTS, COSTS, AND LIMITATIONS

Substitution into the abstract transfer gives g0=kappa/(2q),
g1=(A+l kappa/2)/q, C0=sum_s C_s, gamma=|x|^2/q,
B0=|x|B, and rho=|x|sqrt(g1 C0)/(q g0). These match (6.1)'s lower and
upper constants. They require x nonzero and the orthonormal metric just
audited. The input-dependent R, epsilon, coefficient lists/L_s,
beta_0, K_cap, delta_K, and d_max/sigma_G are charged constants; their
formulas establish finite dependence but no numerical instance of K is
evaluated here. The classical packet supplies no optimal filter algorithm
or filter acquisition. Quantum memory is at most Q in each branch;
classical outcomes are free under the contract (the transferred orbit
quadrature has the abstract d^4+1 existence bound). No profile of
Lambda(Q) is proved.

The low-scale patch is consistent: for any nontrivial highest weight
nu in End(V), C2(nu)=|nu|^2+2<nu,rho> >= |nu|^2 >= sigma_G, since
nu is dominant and nonzero. Thus the commutator Laplacian has the claimed
traceless gap. For N<N_cut, compact K and bounded N give finitely many
carriers; reset error <=1 and Lambda(Q)>=sigma_G/d imply the stated
C_small=N_cut^2 d_max/sigma_G. Q=d is exactly the identity-code case.
