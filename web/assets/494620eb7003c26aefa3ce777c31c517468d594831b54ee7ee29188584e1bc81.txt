SU(m) symmetric-carrier mode channels: normalization audit
Date: 2026-10-08

STATUS

The exact symmetric-carrier decomposition, normalized mode ranks, coherent
Berezin multipliers, and the adjoint-mode channel eigenvalue are derived
below. The proposed all-mode Racah formula is tied to the projector-crossing
6j coefficient with its phase fixed and is exactly checked against the
primary-source factorial formula on a finite grid. I do not claim to have
independently rederived the terminating Sears transformation for every
mode: the all-k equality uses the explicit kind-I 6j formula in
Mironov--Morozov--Sleptsov, together with the channel/realignment
normalization derived here. A standalone proof of that transformation is
still the cleanest next check.

The exact k=1 Casimir calculation and the exact SU(3), Sym^2 table agree with
the proposed formula. A fixed-m positive scalar gap appears plausible from
the one-dimensional Racah recurrence but is not proved here. A scalar gap
uniform in m is impossible for the coherent Berezin comparator: the exact
k=1 ratio tends to 1/(m-1) as the carrier degree grows. This does not refute
a different, dimension-free EB comparison.

1. CARRIER, MODES, AND TRACE NORMALIZATION

Let m>=2, n>=1, V_n=Sym^n(C^m), and
  d_n=dim(V_n)=binom(n+m-1,m-1),     tau(X)=Tr(X)/d_n.
For g in SU(m), write U_n(g) for its action on V_n. The multiplicity-free
decomposition is
  End(V_n) ~= V_n tensor V_n^*
          = direct_sum_{k=0}^n W_k,
  highest_weight(W_k)=k(theta)=k(omega_1+omega_{m-1}).
The summands follow from the Pieri/Littlewood--Richardson product.
For m=2 this means highest weight 2k omega_1.
In partition notation W_k has top row [2k,k,...,k,0]. The Weyl dimension
formula gives
  r_k=dim(W_k)
     = (2k+m-1)/(m-1) * binom(k+m-2,m-2)^2.
For m=2 this is 2k+1; for m=3 it is (k+1)^3. The identity
sum_{k=0}^n r_k=d_n^2 follows from the decomposition.

Every W_k is self-dual. Choose a Hermitian basis {B_{ka}}_{a=1}^{r_k}
with tau(B_{ka} B_{kb})=delta_ab. The adjoint action of SU(m) is real
orthogonal on this Hermitian basis. Therefore sum_a B_{ka}^2 commutes with
U_n(SU(m)); irreducibility and its normalized trace give
  sum_a B_{ka}^2 = r_k I.
Thus
  D_k(X)=r_k^{-1} sum_a B_{ka} X B_{ka}
is a unital, trace-preserving, completely positive, SU(m)-covariant,
self-adjoint map. By Schur's lemma it acts on W_l by a real scalar
eta_k(l). The definition used throughout is this channel action; it fixes
all signs independently of a 6j convention.

2. CHOI TRANSPOSE AND THE PROJECTOR-CROSSING COEFFICIENT

Use the normalized maximally entangled vector
  |Omega>=d_n^{-1/2} sum_i |i> tensor |i>
and the Choi convention J(Phi)=(id tensor Phi)(|Omega><Omega|).
Vectorization is |B>>=sum_i |i> tensor B|i>. Since the B_ka are Hermitian,
  J(D_k)=(1/(d_n r_k)) sum_a |B_ka>><<B_ka|.
The Hilbert--Schmidt orthogonal projector onto the vectorized W_k is
  P_k=(1/d_n) sum_a |B_ka>><<B_ka|,
so J(D_k)=P_k/r_k. In matrix form the Kraus Choi term has the input
transpose: after the chosen realignment, the Kraus term is represented by
B_ka^T tensor B_ka. Dropping that transpose changes the crossing convention.

The action of D_k on W_l is the normalized trace contraction
  eta_k(l)=(1/(r_k r_l)) sum_{a,b}
                 tau(B_lb B_ka B_lb B_ka).
Equivalently, it is the coefficient obtained by reshuffling the Choi
projector P_k and reading the W_l block. This is the recoupling/crossing
coefficient, rather than the unreshuffled Choi coefficient.

For a literal source convention, let R_raw(m,n;k,l) denote the factorial
kind-I 6j expression for
  { [n] [n-bar] W_k ; [n] [n-bar] W_l }
in Mironov--Morozov--Sleptsov, arXiv:1709.02290, §2. The source's Racah
matrix has phase epsilon=(-1)^(n+k+l):
  S_kl=(-1)^(n+k+l) sqrt(r_k r_l) R_raw(m,n;k,l).
The channel crossing matrix U is not S. With the input transpose above,
the projector-Fierz crossing is U=(-1)^n S. Hence
  U_kl=(-1)^(k+l) sqrt(r_k r_l) R_raw(m,n;k,l),
  eta_k(l)=d_n U_kl/sqrt(r_k r_l)
         =(-1)^(k+l) d_n R_raw(m,n;k,l).                 (1)
The global phase is fixed at k=0, not guessed from SU(2): D_0 is the
identity channel, J(D_0)=|Omega><Omega|, and realignment of this Choi
operator is I/d_n. Thus U_0l=+sqrt(r_l)/d_n. The source factorial formula
has R_raw(m,n;0,l)=(-1)^l/d_n, so (1) gives eta_0(l)=1.

In the usual SU(2) Wigner 6j convention the same channel scalar is
(-1)^(n+k+l) d_n times the Wigner 6j. This is consistent with (1) because
the literal source raw convention differs from that Wigner raw symbol by
the global (-1)^n in the recoupling matrix. The source's epsilon, the
projector crossing, and the Wigner convention should not be interchanged.

3. ALL-MODE TERMINATING FORM AND SOURCE BOUNDARY

With the above convention, the candidate scalar is
  eta_k(l) =
    4F3( -k, k+m-1, -l, l+m-1 ;
         m-1, -n, n+m ; 1 ),             0<=k,l<=n.      (2)
The sum terminates at r=min(k,l), with terms
  c_{k,r} p_r(l),
  c_{k,r}=(-k)_r(k+m-1)_r/
          ((m-1)_r(-n)_r(n+m)_r r!),
  p_r(l)=(-l)_r(l+m-1)_r
        = product_{s=0}^{r-1}(s(s+m-1)-l(l+m-1)).

The channel-to-crossing calculation above reduces eta to a normalized
multiplicity-free recoupling coefficient. The primary source gives the
kind-I factorial 6j expression; its terminating Sears transformation gives
(2). I checked the conversion exactly by evaluating the factorial sum
directly and comparing it with (1)--(2). The source formula in the present
indices is
  R_raw =
    (k!^2 l!^2 (n-k)! (n-l)! (m-1)! (m-2)!)
    / ((n+k+m-1)! (n+l+m-1)!)
    * sum_z (-1)^z (n+m-1+z)! /
      ((z-k)!^2 (z-l)!^2 (n-z)! (k+l-z)!
       (k+l+m-2-z)!),
where max(k,l)<=z<=min(n,k+l). This is the source-supported all-k
identification; the exact finite replay is not a proof of the Sears
identity, so that dependency remains explicit.

The coherent measure-and-prepare channel has a separate internal derivation.
For a unit vector z in C^m let P_z=|z^n><z^n| and let nu be Haar probability
on CP^(m-1). Schur averaging gives d_n integral P_z dnu(z)=I, hence
  Psi_coh(X)=d_n integral Tr(P_z X) P_z dnu(z)
is a unital, trace-preserving EB channel.
The covariant symbol sigma(X)(z)=Tr(P_z X) maps W_k to the degree-k
projective harmonics. On those harmonics, sigma Psi_coh has kernel
d_n |<z,w>|^(2n). For fixed z, t=|<z,w>|^2 has density
(m-1)(1-t)^(m-2), and the normalized zonal harmonic is
  phi_k(t)=P_k^(m-2,0)(2t-1)/binom(k+m-2,k).
Using the Rodrigues formula
  P_k^(m-2,0)(2t-1)
   =(-1)^k/(k!(1-t)^(m-2))
      d^k/dt^k [t^k(1-t)^(k+m-2)]
and integrating by parts k times gives
  mu_k = d_n (m-1) integral_0^1 t^n(1-t)^(m-2) phi_k(t) dt
       = n!(n+m-1)! / ((n-k)!(n+k+m-1)!).              (3)
The endpoint terms vanish because t^k(1-t)^(k+m-2) has zero of order at
least k at both endpoints. In particular,
  mu_(k+1)/mu_k=(n-k)/(n+k+m),   mu_1=n/(n+m).
The symbol map is nonzero on every W_k (also seen from positive (3)), so
these are the actual eigenvalues of Psi_coh on operator modes.

4. INDEPENDENT ADJOINT/CASIMIR CALCULATION OF eta_1(l)

Choose Hermitian generators T_a of su(m) with
Tr_m(T_a T_b)=delta_ab and lift them to V_n. There are r_1=m^2-1
generators, and
  sum_a T_a^2 = C_n I,
  C_n = n(m-1)(n+m)/m.
This follows from highest weight n omega_1 using root length squared 2:
the standard Casimir is n(m-1)(n+m)/(2m), and this trace-one generator
normalization doubles it. If
tau(T_a T_b)=kappa delta_ab, then tracing the first identity gives
kappa=C_n/r_1. The normalized tensor frame is B_1a=T_a/sqrt(kappa).
On W_l the adjoint Casimir for this generator normalization is
  C_l=2 l(l+m-1).
Using
  sum_a [B_1a,[B_1a,X]]=2 r_1 (X-D_1(X))
and applying it to X in W_l yields
  eta_1(l)=1-C_l/(2 C_n)
          =1-m l(l+m-1)/(n(m-1)(n+m)).                 (4)
This is an independent channel derivation, not a Racah recurrence. It also
matches (2): for k=1 only the r=0,1 terms remain, and the r=1 term is the
negative fraction in (4).

5. EXACT SU(3), Sym^2 CHECK

Take m=3,n=2, so d_n=6 and End(V_2)=1+8+27 with r_0=1,r_1=8,r_2=27.
The candidate (2) gives rows k and columns l:
            l=0       l=1       l=2
  k=0        1         1         1
  k=1        1       11/20      -1/5
  k=2        1       -1/5       1/45
The coherent eigenvalues from (3) are (1, 2/5, 1/15).

The k=1 row is independent: (4) gives 11/20 and -1/5 for l=1,2.
For k=2 use the complete Hermitian tau-orthonormal basis of M_6. Its
Fierz identity is sum_alpha B_alpha X B_alpha=Tr(X) I=36 tau(X) I.
On a traceless W_l this says
  1 + 8 eta_1(l) + 27 eta_2(l)=0.
It gives eta_2(1)=-1/5 and eta_2(2)=1/45. The l=0 column is unitality.
Thus the entire SU(3), Sym^2 table is checked without using the higher-mode
Racah recurrence.

The accompanying exact Fraction script compares (2) to the source
factorial expression for every m=2,...,6, n=1,...,7, and all k,l; it also
checks the k=1 Casimir formula, the rank sum, and the coefficient form of
the Racah recurrence. It reports 1,015 source/4F3 checks and 6,475 exact
coefficient-recurrence checks. These are finite algebra diagnostics, not
the proof of (2).

6. RECURRENCE, ITS PROPER ROLE, AND A SOURCE CAUTION

The series (2) is polynomial in L=l(l+m-1). Writing
  eta_k(l)=sum_r c_{k,r} p_r(l),
the identity L p_r=r(r+m-1)p_r-p_(r+1) and cancellation of Pochhammer
ratios gives the coefficient identity
  B_k(c_(k+1,r)-c_(k,r)) + D_k(c_(k-1,r)-c_(k,r))
      = c_(k,r-1)-r(r+m-1)c_(k,r),
where
  B_k=(k+m-1)^2(n-k)(n+k+m)/((2k+m-1)(2k+m)),
  D_k=k^2(n+1-k)(n+k+m-1)/((2k+m-2)(2k+m-1)).
Thus
  B_k(eta_(k+1)(l)-eta_k(l))
   +D_k(eta_(k-1)(l)-eta_k(l))
       =-l(l+m-1) eta_k(l),                              (5)
with D_0=0 and B_n=0. Equation (5) is derived here from the terminating
series after its normalization is fixed; it is not used as a substitute
for deriving eta or its source phase.

There is a concrete reason to preserve this distinction. In the
q->1 specialization of §4 of arXiv:1709.02290, set group rank m=2,
carrier r=n=2, fixed mode i=1, and recurrence index j=1. Its own kind-I
factorial formula gives the raw sequence (j=0,1,2)
  (-1/3, 1/6, 1/6).
The printed coefficient formulas give a_1=-32, a_3=-40, and
a_1+a_2+a_3=-48, hence a_2=24. The displayed relation
a_1 R_(j-1)+a_2 R_j+a_3 R_(j+1)=0 then has left side 8, not 0.
This single exact check means I do not use the source's printed recurrence
without a repaired normalization/derivation. It does not invalidate the
kind-I factorial formula or the candidate (2), both of which pass the
separate exact checks above.

7. COVARIANT CHANNELS AND THE ACTUAL EXTENSION CONSTRAINT

For normalized Choi states, every SU(m)-covariant bistochastic channel on
V_n has
  J(Phi)=sum_{k=0}^n p_k P_k/r_k,   p_k>=0,   sum_k p_k=1,
and mode eigenvalues
  lambda_l=sum_{k=0}^n p_k eta_k(l).
Indeed, the Choi commutant is multiplicity-free on
V_n^* tensor V_n, each P_k/r_k has both one-body partial traces I/d_n,
and D_k has precisely that Choi state. This is the actual CP simplex.

A covariant symmetric 1->2 extension is not specified by this marginal
simplex alone. Its exact Choi feasibility condition is: there exists
K>=0 on V_n^* tensor V_n tensor V_n, trace one, such that
  [K, overline(U_n(g)) tensor U_n(g) tensor U_n(g)]=0 for all g,
  F_23 K F_23=K,
  Tr_3 K=J(Phi),   Tr_2 K=J(Phi).
This condition is necessary and sufficient: twirl any extension over SU(m)
and average it over the output swap; covariance and the equal marginals are
preserved. Invariant multiplicity blocks of the triple product contain
the remaining PSD variables; I have not reduced their positivity to scalar
inequalities. This is the precise compatibility interface for Sol's
separate compact-group proof.

If the Stinespring aggregate used in the SU(2) route extends to this
representation with the same normalization, its scalar consequence should
be
  lambda_l^4(1-eta_l(l)) <= 2(1-lambda_l^2).
A coherent-EB comparison then needs a lower bound on
  (1-eta_l(l))/(1-mu_l)^2.
I do not establish the aggregate inequality for SU(m) here.

8. FIXED m VERSUS UNIFORM m

For k=l=1, equations (3) and (4) are exact:
  1-mu_1 = m/(n+m),
  1-eta_1(1) = m^2/(n(m-1)(n+m)),
and therefore
  (1-eta_1(1))/(1-mu_1)^2 = (n+m)/(n(m-1)).             (6)
For each fixed m this tends to 1/(m-1) as n->infinity. Any all-mode
constant c_m for this coherent scalar comparison must be at most 1/(m-1).
Consequently no positive lower bound uniform over m can follow from this
coherent comparator and this scalar gate. Formula (6) is a route-specific
obstruction, not a counterexample to a different EB comparator or to an
arbitrary-channel theorem.

For a fixed m, the natural next route is plausible but open: (2) gives a
single Racah polynomial in the mode index, (5) has positive off-diagonal
coefficients for 0<k<n, and m only enters as a fixed parameter. A
fixed-m version of the SU(2) action/Sturm localization might yield a
non-effective c_m>0 after treating endpoint, bounded-L/n, and large-L/n
scales. The constants and the core-length estimates would have to be
rederived with m present; importing the SU(2) proof without that check is
not justified. Uniform-in-m is already ruled out for the coherent gate
by (6).
