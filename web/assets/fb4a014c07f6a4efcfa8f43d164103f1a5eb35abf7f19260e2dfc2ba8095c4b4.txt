CLARIFICATION 1 — THE q COMPARISON USES TWO STAGES
The projection point y=proj_P x and its rounded vertex v must be inserted explicitly:
  q(x)-q(v)=[q(x)-q(y)]+[q(y)-q(v)].
Because |y|<=1,
  q(x)-q(y)>=-(1+|x|)|x-y|,
  q(y)-q(v)>=-2|y-v|>=-16(1-|y|^2).
The rounded drift/coupling cost is -7|x-v|>=-7|x-y|-56(1-|y|^2). Their sum is
  -(8+|x|)|x-y|-72(1-|y|^2),
which is exactly v1 Section5 before inequality (C). A direct one-step application to q(x)-q(v) would introduce an uncontrolled factor |x| multiplying the circle defect; that is not the proof used here.

CLARIFICATION 2 — TARGET REFLECTION NORMS DO NOT ASSUME HIDDEN REVERSIBILITY
For equilibrium competitors, R_h and R_t are conditional-future squared norms by the usual hidden conditional past/future independence and reversibility. The chosen target hidden chain itself is nonreversible; its actual reversed-past conditional features differ from its future conditional features.
For the target, the lead's separate exact proof establishes that the L2(pi)-projection of every reversed-past cylinder feature onto the invariant observable subspace H equals its future feature. Every future feature used here (F,D_k,G_l) lies in H, since QH=HL and emission multiplication preserves H. Hence the target reflection pairing is still
  B(A,B)=sum_i pi_i h_A(i)h_B(i),
where h_A,h_B are actual nonnegative or signed conditional future functions. Consequently the target R_h,R_t and s2 bounds in v1 Section6 legitimately use squared conditional future norms; hidden reversibility is neither assumed nor inferred.
