## Primary sources inspected

1. **Johnston & Jones (2016), “Evolution of Cell-to-Cell Variability in Stochastic, Controlled, Heteroplasmic mtDNA Populations,” American Journal of Human Genetics 99:1150–1162.** DOI: [10.1016/j.ajhg.2016.09.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2016.09.016), primary open copy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5097950/. Search/open excerpt exposes Box 1 and Eq. 12: under equal mutant/WT Poisson replication and degradation, state-only feedback, and constant mean mtDNA copy number, normalized heteroplasmy variance increases approximately linearly at (2\beta_0t/n), independent of details of feedback. The paper also states threshold-crossing probabilities can be computed from heteroplasmy statistics. This directly preempts a broad “turnover causes genetic drift/variance” claim.

2. **Aryaman et al. (2019), “Mitochondrial Network State Scales mtDNA Genetic Dynamics,” Genetics 212:1429–1443.** DOI: [10.1534/genetics.119.302423](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.119.302423), open copy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6707450/. Inspected lines 114–143, 147–159, 216–237, 240–254, 259–265, 280–282, 293–310. Their explicit model links unfused mitochondrial fraction, mitophagy, copy number and neutral mtDNA drift; depending on whether copy number is controlled through replication versus degradation, network-state dependence changes. They report variance rate proportional to unfused fraction in a broad model class and identify fusion shielding/time rescaling. The paper itself cautions that absolute fission/fusion rates and selective mechanisms matter. This preempts the broad network/turnover tradeoff.

3. **An et al. (2024), “Mitochondrial DNA mosaicism in normal human somatic cells,” Nature Genetics 56:1665–1677.** DOI: [10.1038/s41588-024-01838-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01838-z), open text: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-024-01838-z. Inspected abstract and lines 59–61 (2,096 clones, 31 donors, mutation rate and turnover), 149–162 (turnover definitions and inferred rates), 179–186 (mutation estimate and selection boundary), 227–230 (parameter intermingling/lifetime drift), and 300–324 (exact turnover units and simulation setup). The paper defines one homeostatic turnover as (N) replications/replacements, estimates 6.5, 9.4 and 11.5 homeostatic turns/year for the named cell types, infers (5.0\times10^{-8}) alterations per bp replication, uses (sim750) mtDNA copies/cell in one translation, and simulates mutational drift. It reports reduced expansion of mtDNA-truncating mutations and says threshold loads can cause dysfunction, while urging more comprehensive diseased/aged-cell data. It does not say all mutations are neutral or every high-VAF variant is harmful.

4. **Hoitzing, Johnston & Jones (2015), “Context-Dependent Role of Mitochondrial Fusion-Fission in Clonal Expansion of mtDNA Mutations,” PLOS Computational Biology 11:e1004183.** DOI: [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004183](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004183), publisher page: https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004183. Inspected lines 171–199 and 281–310 plus model methods 312–350. The model makes fusion, fission, mitophagy and replication selective in a coupled organelle/nucleoid simulation, and shows that faster fusion-fission only helps removal when the selective removal/fusion processes work; faster network cycling with insufficient selectivity may worsen mutant accumulation. Directly preempts a simplistic “increase fusion/fission/turnover = repair” proposal.

5. **“Inferring accumulation times of mitochondrial DNA deletion mutants from cross-sectional single-cell data: methodological framework and validation,” npj Aging (2026).** DOI: [10.1038/s41514-026-00431-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-026-00431-4), publisher page: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41514-026-00431-4. Inspected lines 91–107 (cross-sectional identifiability goal), 179–196 (exact Moran first-passage and master-equation method), 202–205 (fit of threshold fractions), 251–260 (claimed synthetic validation and limitation), 264–273 (mutation/selection simulation), 275–299 (Moran process and physical-time map), and 308–363 (RNA-vs-DNA mapping). The source already computes exact Moran accumulation times to 90% takeover and demonstrates fitting cross-sectional mutant-threshold fractions on synthetic data. It states that the empirical Tabula Muris Senis application is a separate companion manuscript. The paper explicitly says transcript-level mutants may be overrepresented relative to DNA when feedback is weakened. This preempts a claim to have newly solved exact threshold first passage or cross-sectional inference.

6. **Wallace & Chalkia / prior models are summarized only to locate prior art; no result from those reviews is used as proof.** Primary lineages in the references include deletion expansion and PINK1/Parkin selection. A separate primary report in Drosophila shows Parkin/PINK1 overexpression can reduce deletion-bearing mtDNA and depends on autophagy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5114534/. This establishes that selective-clearance counterexamples to genotype-blind drift exist in specific model systems; it does not establish a general human aging intervention.

## Evidence boundary

- **Source-reported:** values and conclusions above are attributed to the named primary sources; no reanalysis of their raw reads or supplementary tables was performed.
- **Internally reconstructed:** neutral Moran transition probabilities, exact (k/m) threshold-hit probability, rate time-change, copy-number/mutation-supply composition and discrete finite-horizon output are derived in `../proofs/turnover_model.md` and implemented in `../code/mito_turnover_model.py`.
- **Not established:** a causal role in organismal aging, a physiological human tissue threshold, complete single-copy detection, genotype-specific mitophagy in the selected tissue, translational feasibility, clinical benefit, novelty or priority.
