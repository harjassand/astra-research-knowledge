UNKNOWN-RUNG RATIONAL SCALAR ACQUISITION: A MATCHING QUERY FRONTIER
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: internally reconstructed proof for the stated finite model; finite
enumeration/simulation is diagnostic only. No novelty or priority claim.

RESULT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

There is a clean finite-sample answer once the learner is given a universal
degree/height envelope, but not the target rung or target model. For the
nested rational monomial family below, with degree cap D, numerator/denominator
height cap H, legal query domain Q intersect [0,1], and fresh Gaussian scalar
noise of standard deviation sigma, exact predictive-class identification has
minimax adaptive query complexity

  n*(D,H,sigma,delta) = Theta(1 + sigma^2 max{H^4,H^2 D^2} log(1/delta))

for H>=2, D>=1 and 0<delta<=1/4. The upper bound uses only x=1 and
t=D/(D+1), with output grid precision Qbits=O(log H+log D); the lower bound
allows arbitrary rational query points and unquantized Gaussian observations.
Thus adaptive query placement and extra precision cannot improve the query
order at this sufficient precision. The minimum output or input precision and
the full query/precision Pareto frontier are not established. If the universal
envelope/separation is removed, no finite query count uniformly identifies
the exact predictive class: arbitrarily close height and degree pairs defeat
every adaptive learner. The learner selects a class without knowing its
specific target rung, but it still receives the overall model family and
global caps.

1. LATENT FAMILY, LEGAL DOMAIN, AND WHAT IS GIVEN

For an integer h>=1, let

  R_h = {a/b : 1<=a,b<=h, gcd(a,b)=1},
  ht(a/b) = max(a,b) for the reduced positive fraction,
  F_(d,h) = {0} union {c x^k : c in R_h, 0<=k<=d}.

Here x^0=1. The classes are nested in both d and h. The public problem gives
integers D>=1 and H>=2, so the candidate family is F_(D,H); it does not give
the target degree d, its coefficient height h, or coefficient c. The target
is stationary and belongs to this finite family. There are

  K = 1 + (D+1)|R_H| <= 1 + (D+1)H^2

candidate members. For a nonzero member, c has a unique reduced numerator
and denominator, d is unique, and h=ht(c) can be computed from c. The zero
function is its own predictive class but has no intrinsic degree or height.

The legal query set is exactly Q intersect [0,1]. A reduced rational p/q is
sent exactly; its query-coordinate precision is B(p/q)=ceil(log2 q), with
dedicated one-bit tokens for x=1. In particular t=D/(D+1) is legal and has
B=ceil(log2(D+1)). The query is a single scalar point and the oracle returns
one scalar code. It supplies no passive labeled examples, counterexamples,
symbolic coefficients, or matrix/vector response; observations only arrive
when the learner makes a legal scalar query.

The public channel has known sigma>0, failure target delta, integer output
precision Qbits, and finite clipping index M. Given x, it draws fresh
independent Z~N(0,sigma^2), rounds f(x)+Z to the nearest multiple of
eta=2^(-Qbits), clips the integer grid index to [-M,M], and sends that index
in r=ceil(log2(2M+1)) fixed bits. There is no other response channel. For the
upper bound the learner and oracle use

  Delta = min{H^(-2), [3 H (D+1)]^(-1)},
  Qbits = ceil(log2(4/Delta)),
  m = ceil(128 sigma^2 Delta^(-2) ln(8/delta)),
  R = sigma sqrt(2 ln(8m/delta)),
  M = ceil(2^Qbits (H+R+1)).

The setup constants are shared. Their transmission cost, if required, is
charged separately in Section 4. This is teacher-free only in the narrow
sense that there is no labeled-example or counterexample teacher: an
evaluation-only scalar oracle and the overall target grammar are still given.

2. PREDICTIVE EQUIVALENCE AND THE SEPARATION MARGIN

For two candidate functions f,g, write f equiv g when for every legal
rational x their complete one-query response laws are equal. The clipped
quantized Gaussian location channel is injective in its mean: for M>=1, the
probability of the lowest output bin is a strictly monotone Gaussian CDF of
the mean (equivalently, an interior threshold probability is strictly
monotone). Therefore f equiv g exactly when f(x)=g(x) for every legal x.
Distinct rational monomials differ at some rational point, so their
predictive equivalence classes are single functions.

Use the two-point signature

  v(c,d) = ( f(1), f(t) ) = (c, c t^d),
  v(0)   = (0,0),       t=D/(D+1).

It is injective: the first coordinate identifies c (or zero); for c>0 the
ratio f(t)/c=t^d identifies d because 0<t<1. It has L-infinity separation
at least Delta between every pair of distinct candidates:

* If one candidate is zero and the other is not, or if the coefficients
  differ, the first coordinate differs by at least H^(-2). For distinct
  reduced fractions a/b and a'/b', the nonzero numerator of their difference
  is an integer and bb'<=H^2. A nonzero coefficient is at least 1/H.
* If the coefficient is the same and degrees differ, let k<ell<=D. Then
  c(t^k-t^ell) >= c t^(D-1)(1-t) >= 1/[3H(D+1)]. The last step uses
  (D/(D+1))^(D-1)>1/e>1/3.

This explicit margin is the finite-model identifiability condition used by
the upper bound. It is derived from D,H and the legal domain, not supplied as
an oracle answer.

3. UPPER BOUND AND FAILURE PROBABILITY

Ask x=1 m times and x=t m times. Decode each response as its grid value and
average separately, giving observed vector vbar. Output the candidate in
F_(D,H) whose signature is nearest to vbar in L-infinity distance, breaking
any tie by a fixed ordering.

On the event that every sampled |Z|<=R, no response saturates: all model
means on [0,1] are at most H, and M eta>=H+R+1. On the event that each of
the two sample-average noises has magnitude less than Delta/8, the quantized
sample means obey

  ||vbar-v(c,d)||_infinity <= Delta/8 + eta/2 <= Delta/4.

Every false signature is at least Delta away from the true one, so it is at
least 3Delta/4 from vbar. The nearest-signature decoder is therefore correct.
The Gaussian tail and union bounds give

  Pr{either average-noise event fails}
      <= 4 exp(-m Delta^2/(128 sigma^2)) <= delta/2,
  Pr{any of 2m individual noises exceeds R}
      <= 4m exp(-R^2/(2 sigma^2)) <= delta/2.

The total error probability is at most delta, with 2m scalar queries. Since
Delta^(-2)=max{H^4,9H^2(D+1)^2}, this gives

  n <= 2 ceil(128 sigma^2 Delta^(-2) ln(8/delta))
    = O(1 + sigma^2 max{H^4,H^2D^2} ln(1/delta)).

This is nonadaptive; the target coefficient, height, and degree are all
selected only after the two noisy scalar streams have been received.

4. ADAPTIVE LOWER BOUNDS AND THE MATCH

Let kl_delta=kl(1-delta || delta)=(1-2delta)ln((1-delta)/delta). For any
two candidate targets whose pointwise mean gap is at most g, one unquantized
Gaussian answer has conditional KL at most g^2/(2 sigma^2). Under an
arbitrary adaptive policy, condition on each past transcript. The policy's
next-query kernel is the same under both targets, and the response KL has the
same bound at every legal x. By the chain rule,

  KL(P0^transcript || P1^transcript) <= n g^2/(2 sigma^2).

Quantization and clipping are data processing and can only reduce this KL.
If a learner is delta-correct on both targets, the event that it outputs the
first target has probabilities at least 1-delta and at most delta. Binary
data processing then gives transcript KL >= kl_delta.

Height pair: for H>=2, take the constant functions
  f0(x)=1/H,  f1(x)=1/(H-1).
They belong to F_(D,H), and their gap at every legal x is
g=1/[H(H-1)]. Thus

  n >= 2 sigma^2 H^2(H-1)^2 kl_delta = Omega(sigma^2 H^4 ln(1/delta)).

Degree pair: take
  g0(x)=H^(-1) x^(D-1),  g1(x)=H^(-1) x^D.
For x in [0,1], their difference is H^(-1)x^(D-1)(1-x)<=1/(HD), including
D=1. Hence

  n >= 2 sigma^2 H^2 D^2 kl_delta = Omega(sigma^2 H^2D^2 ln(1/delta)).

No adaptive rational-point policy can avoid either pair. A zero-query learner
cannot identify even two members with error below 1/2, giving the constant
term. Since kl_delta and ln(1/delta) are comparable on 0<delta<=1/4, these
lower bounds match the upper query order up to universal constants:

  n* = Theta(1 + sigma^2 max{H^4,H^2D^2} ln(1/delta)).

The query lower bound gives the learner arbitrary rational points and an
unquantized response, so it remains valid at every finite precision and
under adaptive design. The proved precision point is Qbits=ceil(log2(4/Delta))
=O(log H+log D); the needed input coordinate t has O(log D) bits. This is a
query-count match at a sufficient precision. It does not prove minimum Qbits,
minimum coordinate encoding length, or a Pareto-optimal query/precision
tradeoff; coarse quantizers may still identify a finite family with enough
repetitions, and that regime is not analyzed here.

5. TOTAL BIT COST AND COMPUTATION

For an explicit interaction encoding, let gamma(z)=2 floor(log2 z)+1 be the
Elias gamma code length for a positive integer z. Encode x=1 by a one-bit
token. Encode a non-endpoint reduced rational p/q by a one-bit tag followed
by gamma(p), gamma(q). Then each t query takes
1+gamma(D)+gamma(D+1) bits. Responses take r bits each. Encode the final
answer with a zero/nonzero flag, fixed-width numerator and denominator
fields of w=ceil(log2(H+1)) bits each, and a degree field of
w_D=ceil(log2(D+1)) bits. A zero answer uses only the flag. The total
interactive communication is bounded by

  C_interactive = m[2 + gamma(D)+gamma(D+1) + 2r] + 1+2w+w_D.

This includes every query coordinate, every response, and the returned
predictive class. It excludes the one-time public setup. If that setup must
also cross the channel, add L_pub, the code length for D,H,Qbits,M and the
reduced rational values sigma,delta, along with a fixed channel/protocol
identifier. Thus C_total=C_interactive+L_pub. For the concrete diagnostic in
Section 6, gamma coding the numerical setup D=5,H=4,Qbits=9,M=2584,
sigma=1/100,delta=1/20 costs 64 bits; the protocol identifier is pre-shared.

For H=4,D=5,sigma=0.01,delta=0.05, the protocol has Delta=1/72,
Qbits=9, m=337, M=2584, r=13. The above encoding costs 12,816 interactive
bits (4,044 query bits, 8,762 reply bits, 10 answer bits), or 12,880 bits
including that 64-bit numerical setup. The public model grammar is fixed in
the report and otherwise shared.

A streaming decoder can enumerate the K candidates and retain only the best
signature, using O(mr) bits/operations to read and sum responses plus
O(K poly(L)) bit operations for exact rational signature comparisons, where
L=O(D log D+log H+log m+Qbits+log M) bounds the relevant integer lengths.
The codebook has K<=1+(D+1)H^2 members. This is a finite but potentially
large computation; the query theorem is not a polynomial-in-log(H,D)
runtime claim.

6. CONCRETE CHECK AND NO-MARGIN FAILURE

The executable diagnostic at
work/programmes/acquisition_learning/leaves/unknown_class_scalar_frontier/frontier_check.py
uses H=4,D=5, so there are 67 hypotheses. Exact Fraction enumeration finds
67 distinct two-anchor signatures and minimum L-infinity gap 625/31104,
which exceeds the proved lower bound 1/72. Under sigma=0.01, delta=0.05,
the selected finite channel has Qbits=9, m=337, M=2584, and 13-bit replies.
A seeded 200-trial check on c=1/4,d=5 recorded zero decoding errors and no
clipped responses. The result file is adjacent to the script. These finite
checks illustrate the formulas; they do not establish the probability bound.

If height is unbounded, even constants defeat any proposed uniform finite
budget n. The pair 0 and 1/L have distinct predictive laws and differ in mean
by 1/L at every legal x, so their n-query transcript KL is at most
n/(2 sigma^2 L^2), even before the information loss from quantization and
clipping. Choose L large enough that this is less than kl_delta; binary data
processing rules out delta-correct identification on both. If degree is
unbounded, use x^(J-1) and x^J. On [0,1], their largest mean gap is at most
1/J, giving transcript KL at most n/(2 sigma^2 J^2); choosing J large gives
the same contradiction even with coefficient 1. These examples show why a
global finite envelope or another explicit separation condition is needed
for a uniform finite-sample exact-class guarantee. The executable check also
prints illustrative very small KL upper bounds for large finite L and J.

More fundamentally, “select the predictive class without receiving the
model family” has two readings. If only the target member/rung is withheld,
the answer here is yes: the supplied universal envelope plus the two legal
scalar probes suffice. If no target family or effective envelope is given,
there is no finite uniform exact guarantee over the unbounded monomial union
above. A universal enumeration of candidates would itself be a model-family
assumption, and identifying behavior only on finitely many queried points
does not certify equality over the full dense legal domain.

7. EQUALLY INFORMED BASELINE AND CLAIM BOUNDARY

The strongest baseline is told exactly the same F_(D,H), channel, noise
scale, precision, legal domain, and confidence target. It retains all K
candidate models, updates their likelihoods using the actual finite-bin
Gaussian response law, and can adaptively choose any legal rational query
using any optimal experiment-design calculation. The lower bound in Section
4 covers this baseline and every other adaptive strategy. The simple
two-anchor learner matches its minimax query order; no advantage over an
equally informed learner is shown. Computational efficiency against this
stronger retention baseline is also not claimed.

Acquired from the oracle: the exact predictive class; for a nonzero member,
its reduced coefficient, its degree, and consequently its minimal rung
height. Supplied: the nested monomial grammar, global D,H envelope, legal
domain, stationary-target promise, Gaussian noise law and scale, quantizer,
clipping range, and confidence target. The work does not acquire the grammar
or the scalar-query interface. It makes no claim about arbitrary rational
functions, sums of monomials, unknown noise, adversarial noise, approximation
under an unbounded class, or discovery from ordinary passive samples.

8. PRIMARY-LITERATURE CONTEXT AND STATUS

Angluin's primary query-learning paper formalizes several query types for an
unknown concept and gives general lower-bound techniques; its stated setup
is class-based. This note has only noisy scalar evaluation queries, no
equivalence/counterexample oracle, and proves the bounded-family statement
directly. See [Angluin, “Queries and Concept Learning,” Machine Learning 2
(1988)](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022821128753).

Gold's original identification-in-the-limit paper likewise begins with a
specified class and specified information-presentation method. Its model is
different (texts/informants over languages, potentially infinite time), so
it is context for the supplied-class boundary, not a theorem about this
finite noisy scalar channel. See [Gold, “Language Identification in the
Limit,” Information and Control 10 (1967)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(67)91165-5).

Classical regression-design work of Kiefer and Wolfowitz and sequential
experimental-design work of Chernoff are relevant historical settings for
choosing observations, but their results are not used as premises here:
[Kiefer and Wolfowitz (1959)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177706252),
[Chernoff (1959)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177706205). The literature
pass was targeted rather than exhaustive. No novelty or priority claim is
made. The written upper and lower arguments are internally reconstructed;
the finite script is diagnostic and has not received independent review.
