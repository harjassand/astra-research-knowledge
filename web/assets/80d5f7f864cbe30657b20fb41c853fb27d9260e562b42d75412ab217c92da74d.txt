1. TARGET AND COMPLETE INVERSE IDENTITY

At hard t_i=0, amplitudes in canonical up-then-down Fock order are
 a_F(I,J)=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2} det F[I,J],
 where |I|=|J|=k and I intersect J is empty.
For n=2k, every surviving pair has J=I^c. Let K=F+D be invertible, with D
diagonal. Disjoint minors never use diagonal entries, so
 det F[I,I^c]=det K[I,I^c].

The complementary-minor identity gives, for EVERY I of size k,
 det K[I,I^c]=sigma det K det K^{-1}[I,I^c],
 sigma=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}=(-1)^k.                         (1)

Self-contained block derivation: permute K's rows into (I,I^c) and columns
into (I^c,I). Their joint permutation sign is sigma because the two index
sums cover all coordinates and k(k-1) is even. In the resulting block matrix
T=[[A,B],[C,E]], A=K[I,I^c], the lower-right inverse block is precisely
K^{-1}[I,I^c]. If A is invertible, the Schur formulas yield
 det T det (T^{-1})_22
 = det A det(E-CA^{-1}B) det(E-CA^{-1}B)^{-1}=det A.
After multiplying away det K denominators this is a polynomial identity,
so it holds by continuity for every invertible K, including singular A.
Using det T=sigma det K proves (1). No conjugation or state-dependent sign
is introduced. Removing the diagonal of K^{-1} likewise does not affect
the complementary minors. Therefore for H=stripdiag(K^{-1}),
 a_F(I,I^c)=gamma a_H(I,I^c), gamma=(-1)^k det K.       (2)

Every physical prefix restricts the very same set of spin strings. Equation
(2) holds term by term, so its unnormalized mass obeys
 Z_F(prefix)=|det K|^2 Z_H(prefix).                    (3)
The common scalar cancels from all conditional probabilities and contributes
only a global phase to the normalized state. No inverse original norm or
