1. STATEMENT AND INTERFACE

On C^3, use the vector representation of spin one:

 J1 = [[0,0,0],[0,0,-i],[0,i,0]],
 J2 = [[0,0,i],[0,0,0],[-i,0,0]],
 J3 = [[0,-i,0],[i,0,0],[0,0,0]].

Let w1,w2,w3 >= 0, Ai = sqrt(wi) Ji, tau = Tr/3, and

 V(w) = sum_i tau(Ai^2) = (2/3)(w1+w2+w3),
 k(w) = max_{sum_alpha p_alpha |psi_alpha><psi_alpha| = I/3}
          sum_alpha p_alpha sum_i wi <Ji>_{psi_alpha}^2.

The maximum is over one common ensemble of normalized pure states. It is not
the sum of three independently optimized single-observable roofs.

Put hi = sqrt(wi). Then exactly

 k(w) = (1/3) min_{z1,z2,z3 >= 0} (z1^2+z2^2+z3^2)
         subject to zj+zk >= 2 hi for {i,j,k}={1,2,3}.                 (T1)

To give the explicit piecewise answer, relabel coordinates so h1>=h2>=h3.
Relabel the matrices and witnesses back afterwards. The three disjoint cases
below cover all weights, including zero weights and all equality boundaries.

Case A: h1 >= 2 h2.
 z = (0,h1,h1),
 k = 2 h1^2 / 3.                                                   (T2A)

Case B: h1 < 2 h2 and 3 h3 <= h1+h2.
 z = (2/3)(2h2-h1, 2h1-h2, h1+h2),
 k = (8/9)(h1^2+h2^2-h1 h2).                                     (T2B)

Case C: h1 < 2 h2 and 3 h3 > h1+h2.
 s = h1+h2+h3,
 zi = s-2hi,
 k = (4 sum_i hi^2-s^2)/3
   = sum_i wi - (2/3) sum_{i<j} sqrt(wi wj).                       (T2C)

V-k is the exact joint convex-roof variance at I/3. For a legal broadcast
channel Phi, the arbitrary-frame C=2 gate on this family is precisely

 2 sum_i wi tau(Ji Phi(Ji)) <= (2/3) sum_i wi + k(w).                (T3)

Legal here means CPTP, unital, Hilbert--Schmidt self-adjoint, and possessing a
CPTP two-output extension with both marginals equal to Phi. Formula (T1)
does not assert (T3); it provides its exact classical comparison value.

2. GLOBAL DUAL WITNESS: ALL PURE STATES, NOT JUST COORDINATE PLANES

For every feasible z in (T1), G=diag(z1^2,z2^2,z3^2) obeys

 sum_i wi <Ji>_psi^2 <= <G>_psi  for every normalized psi in C^3.    (D1)

Therefore every ensemble with barycenter I/3 has score at most
Tr(G)/3. To prove (D1), write psi=a+i b with real vectors a,b. A global
phase can make a perpendicular to b. If both are nonzero, write
a=sqrt(t) u, b=sqrt(1-t) v, with 0<t<1 and real orthonormal u,v.
Set n=u cross v. Up to an irrelevant overall sign,

 (<J1>,<J2>,<J3>) = 2 sqrt(t(1-t)) n.

For a fixed orthonormal pair u,v, put gu=u^T G u and gv=v^T G v.
The required inequality for all t is

 4 t(1-t) n^T diag(w) n <= t gu+(1-t) gv.

For gu,gv>=0, the infimum over 0<t<1 of the right side divided by
t(1-t) is (sqrt(gu)+sqrt(gv))^2. This follows by minimizing
gu/(1-t)+gv/t. It remains true as an infimum if either number is zero.
When both are positive the minimizer is t=sqrt(gv)/(sqrt(gu)+sqrt(gv)).

On the plane n-perp, choose u,v to minimize that expression. Its minimum is

 L(n) = Tr(G)-n^T G n + 2 sqrt(n^T adj(G) n),                        (D2)

attained in an eigenbasis of the compression of G to that plane.
Indeed, gu+gv is the trace of this compression and gu gv is at least its
determinant; equality holds in such an eigenbasis. Formula (D2) also holds
when G is singular, either directly by the determinant identity or by
continuity. In diagonal coordinates, with xi=ni^2 and sum_i xi=1,

 L(n)-4 n^T diag(w) n
   = sum_i xi (zj^2+zk^2-4wi)
       + 2 sqrt(sum_i xi zj^2 zk^2),                              (D3)

where {i,j,k}={1,2,3}. This is a concave function of x on the simplex.
Consequently its value is at least the weighted sum of its vertex values.
Each vertex value is (zj+zk)^2-4wi >=0 by feasibility of z.
Thus (D3) is nonnegative for every plane, which proves (D1).

If a or b is zero, all spin means vanish and (D1) follows from G>=0.

Conversely, among diagonal G, the global majorization (D1) holds exactly
when zi=sqrt(Gii)>=0 and the three constraints in (T1) hold. Real basis
vectors force Gii>=0; testing all complex superpositions in coordinate
plane jk forces (zj+zk)^2>=4wi by the same t minimization.

For completeness, the finite D2 symmetry generated by diag(1,-1,-1),
diag(-1,1,-1), diag(-1,-1,1) preserves the score. It sends an arbitrary
Hermitian dual majorant G to a diagonal majorant of the same trace by
twirling. Thus this diagonal description loses no linear-majorant power.
Exactness below follows from explicit ensembles, without needing to invoke
abstract moment-dual strong duality.

3. SOLVING THE THREE-VARIABLE CONVEX PROGRAM EXACTLY

For F(z)=sum_i zi^2, attach beta_i>=0 to the constraint
zj+zk>=2hi, and nu_j>=0 to zj>=0. Convex KKT sufficiency requires

 2zj = sum_{i != j} beta_i + nu_j,
 beta_i(zj+zk-2hi)=0,   nu_j zj=0.                                (K1)

Case A has beta=(2h1,0,0), nu=(0,0,0). Both smaller constraints are
feasible because h1>=2h2>=2h3. Equations (K1) hold even for h1=0.

Case B has all z coordinates positive, beta=(2z2,2z1,0), nu=0.
The first two constraints are equalities. The third is feasible exactly
when 3h3<=h1+h2. Positivity of z1 follows from h1<2h2. The sum of the
three squared coordinates is (8/3)(h1^2+h2^2-h1h2), giving (T2B).

Case C has beta_i=4hi-s, nu=0. The smallest multiplier is
beta3=3h3-h1-h2>0, hence every multiplier is positive. All constraints
are equalities. Also z1=h2+h3-h1>(4h2-2h1)/3>0, and the other zi are
positive. Equations (K1) follow since beta_i+beta_k=2zj.

All cases are feasible KKT points of the strictly convex objective,
and hence the unique optimizers in their respective regimes. The cases
are exhaustive for sorted h. The adjacent expressions agree at their
boundaries; equality h1=2h2 is assigned to A and equality 3h3=h1+h2
with h1<2h2 is assigned to B.

4. EXPLICIT OPTIMAL ENSEMBLES

Case A, including all zero weights:
 use psi_+=(e2+i e3)/sqrt(2), psi_-=(e2-i e3)/sqrt(2), e1,
 each with probability 1/3.
 Their barycenter is I/3, their score is (2/3)w1, and every state
 saturates (D1) for G=diag(0,h1^2,h1^2).

Case B:
 put a=h1+h2, p1=h1/a, p2=h2/a,
 t1=a/(3h1), t2=a/(3h2).
 Use the pair sqrt(t1)e2 +/- i sqrt(1-t1)e3 with probabilities p1/2
 each, and the pair sqrt(t2)e1 +/- i sqrt(1-t2)e3 with probabilities
 p2/2 each. Here a,h1,h2>0 and 0<t1,t2<1. Their total population in
 e1 is p2 t2=1/3, in e2 is p1 t1=1/3, and in e3 is the remainder
 1/3. The plus/minus pairs cancel every off-diagonal entry.
 Both active coordinate-plane pairs saturate (D1), so the ensemble
 score is Tr(G)/3=(8/9)(h1^2+h2^2-h1h2).
 These formulas also extend to h1=2h2>0: the second pair becomes e1
 with total mass 1/3 and the first pair is circular with mass 2/3.
 This gives the Case A witness on that boundary without a division by z1.

Case C:
 put beta_i=4hi-s>0 and, for any ordering of the other indices j,k,

 ti = zk/(zj+zk),
 pi = beta_i(zj+zk)/(6 zj zk).

 For each i, use psi_i,+ = sqrt(ti)ej + i sqrt(1-ti)ek and
 psi_i,- = sqrt(ti)ej - i sqrt(1-ti)ek with probabilities pi/2 each.
 All denominators are positive in Case C. The pair i contributes
 beta_i/(6zj) to coordinate j, and beta_i/(6zk) to coordinate k.
 Therefore the total population of coordinate j is

 (sum_{i != j} beta_i)/(6zj) = (2zj)/(6zj) = 1/3.

The pairs cancel off-diagonal entries, so the barycenter is I/3 and
sum_i pi=1. For active pair i, 4wi=(zj+zk)^2 and its score is
4wi ti(1-ti)=zj zk. Its expectation of G is also zj zk.
Every state therefore saturates (D1), and the ensemble score is
Tr(G)/3. This proves exactness of (T1) in all cases.

5. CHECKS AND BROADCASTING USE

Weights (1,0,0): h=(1,0,0), Case A, z=(0,1,1), k=2/3=V.
Weights (1,1,1): h=(1,1,1), Case C, z=(1,1,1), beta=(1,1,1),
 p=(1/3,1/3,1/3), t=(1/2,1/2,1/2), k=1 and V=2.
Weights (1,1,0): Case B, z=(2/3,2/3,4/3), k=8/9 and V=4/3.
Weights (4,1,1): boundary Case A, z=(0,2,2), k=8/3 and V=4.

For a normalized tripartite Choi state R of a legal broadcaster, the
reference marginal is I/3, the two output marginals are I/3, and the
reference--output marginals coincide. In the displayed vector basis,
Ji^T=-Ji. Thus the star score 2 s_Phi is the expectation of

 H(w) = sum_i wi (Ji^T tensor Ji tensor I
                 + Ji^T tensor I tensor Ji)
      = -sum_i wi (Ji tensor Ji tensor I + Ji tensor I tensor Ji).

The transpose sign must be retained in a legal-channel optimization.
For a normalized Choi pair J_Phi in this convention, Hilbert--Schmidt
self-adjointness is exactly J_Phi=F J_Phi^T F, where F swaps the two
factors and T is the full transpose. It is not plain pair-swap symmetry
without a reality condition. Since H(w) is real, averaging R with its
full transpose preserves the score and all legality constraints. Hence
R can additionally be chosen real; then the preceding Choi condition
reduces to ordinary pair-swap symmetry. Output swap can be imposed by
symmetrizing the broadcaster. Simultaneous D2 rotations on all three
vector factors also preserve H(w) and preserve all legality constraints.

A counterexample to C=2 on this family would require an actual positive
R with these marginals and channel conditions, satisfying
Tr(R H(w)) > (2/3)sum_i wi+k(w). A numerical optimum alone is not proof;
an explicit admissible R and exact violation would be required.

The theorem proves only the exact support formula and its witnesses.
It does not prove the general broadcast gate or even (T3) for spin one.
