# Arbitrary-range extension into ambient real \(L_1\) by bidual compactness

**Status:** Internally proved conditional consequence of the finite-range premise in DERIVATION.md / family 332. The bidual compactness step is standard. It removes the separability and common-support restrictions in DERIVATION_CYCLE2.md. It does not validate family 332's source proof. The standard Lebesgue-\(L_1\) extension conclusion is already announced in primary prior art; no novelty claim is made.

## Conditional theorem

Let \(X\) be any metric space, \(S\subseteq X\) nonempty, and let \(Y\) be a Banach space for which there is a contractive linear projection \(P:Y^{**}\to J_Y(Y)\) fixing the canonical copy \(J_Y(Y)\). Suppose \(f:S\to Y\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz and there is a constant \(K<\infty\) such that for every finite \(A\subseteq S\), some map \(F_A:X\to Y\) satisfies

\[
F_A|_A=f|_A,\qquad \operatorname{Lip}(F_A)\le KL.
\]

Then \(f\) has an extension \(F:X\to Y\) with \(\operatorname{Lip}(F)\le KL\). No separability assumption on \(X\) or \(S\), and no common-support assumption on the maps \(F_A\), is needed. If \(S=\varnothing\), choose any constant extension.

### Proof

Fix \(s_0\in S\). Direct the finite subsets \(A\subseteq S\) containing \(s_0\) by inclusion; this is a cofinal directed set. For each \(x\in X\), all the selected extensions satisfy

\[
\|F_A(x)\|_Y\le \|f(s_0)\|_Y+KL\,d_X(x,s_0)=:R_x.
\]

Use the canonical embedding \(J_Y:Y\to Y^{**}\). Each closed ball \(B_{Y^{**}}(R_x)\) is compact in the weak-* topology \(\sigma(Y^{**},Y^*)\). Hence the product

\[
\prod_{x\in X}B_{Y^{**}}(R_x)
\]

is compact by Tychonoff. The net \(A\mapsto (J_YF_A(x))_{x\in X}\) lies in that product and has a convergent subnet. Write its coordinatewise weak-* limit as \(G(x)\in Y^{**}\).

For any \(x,y\in X\), the differences converge weak-* to \(G(x)-G(y)\). Closed norm balls in a dual space are weak-* closed, so

\[
\|G(x)-G(y)\|_{Y^{**}}\le KL\,d_X(x,y).
\]

For each fixed \(s\in S\), cofinality makes \(F_A(s)=f(s)\) on a tail, so \(G(s)=J_Yf(s)\). Finally \(F=J_Y^{-1}\circ P\circ G\) is \(KL\)-Lipschitz and agrees with \(f\) on \(S\). The projection need not be weak-* continuous: it is applied only after the cluster map is obtained, and contractivity is all the metric estimate uses. \(\square\)

## Ambient real \(L_1(\Omega,\mu)\)

The premise holds for \(Y=L_1(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb R)\), for **any positive measure space** \((\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)\), if the finite-range extension claim in DERIVATION.md is accepted. Given finite \(A\subseteq S\), the restricted map \(f|_A\) has finite image. The exact level-set cut representation in DERIVATION.md gives a finite weighted \(\ell_1^d\) code for this image and an affine, 1-Lipschitz recovery map \(T_A:\ell_1^d\to L_1(\Omega,\mu)\). The pinned release plus Mendel–Naor's dual-target Corollary 1.13 supplies a global \(\ell_1\)-valued extension; coordinate projection and \(T_A\) yield the required global \(F_A:X\to Y\), with

\[
K=C_{MN}\,12\sqrt{21}\,M_2(X),
\]

where \(C_{MN}\) is the unspecified universal factor in the cited extension theorem. The finite cut identities and the recovery map hold pointwise for any finite family of real integrable functions; finite-measure, \(\sigma\)-finite, nonatomic, or separable ambient hypotheses are not needed. This does not extend to complex-valued \(L_1\) by the stated real-cut argument.

The functional-analytic input is that \(L_1(\mu)\) is **L-embedded**: under the canonical embedding, \(Y^{**}=J_Y(Y)\oplus_1Y_s\), so projection onto \(J_Y(Y)\) is norm one. Rao's primary article, *Very smooth points of spaces of operators*, Proc. Indian Acad. Sci. (Math. Sci.) 113(1) (2003), Remark 26, states this for “any positive measure \(\mu\)” without a \(\sigma\)-finiteness qualifier ([arXiv:math/0312116](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0312116), [DOI:10.1007/BF02829678](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02829678)). For finite/\(\sigma\)-finite measures the projection is seen concretely through the Yosida–Hewitt decomposition of the bidual as countably additive plus purely finitely additive parts; for a fully arbitrary measure space, rely on the abstract L-embedded \(L_1\)-space theorem as stated by Rao. Do not assume \(L_1(\mu)^*=L_\infty(\mu)\) for arbitrary non-\(\sigma\)-finite \(\mu\); that identification is unnecessary here.

## Prior-art boundary and the role of Mendel–Naor

Mendel and Naor, *Spectral calculus and Lipschitz extension for barycentric metric spaces* ([arXiv:1301.3963](https://arxiv.org/html/1301.3963)), Theorem 1.11 gives finite partial extensions. In the discussion leading to Corollary 1.13 they explicitly say that when a target \(Y\) admits a \(\lambda\)-Lipschitz retraction from \(Y^{**}\) to \(Y\), the finite-extension result globalizes with that factor; see the paragraph around equations (8)–(9), especially lines 473–500 in the primary PDF. An L-projection makes \(\lambda=1\). Corollary 1.13 itself is stated for a dual target. Here it is used on the finite cut code \(\ell_1=c_0^*\), not misrepresented as an arbitrary-range theorem for ambient \(L_1\).

There is also a direct prior-art overlap. Naor's primary v2 preprint *De-Höldering factorization*, revised 10 September 2026 ([arXiv:2609.07564v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07564v2)), states that every \(L_1\)-valued Lipschitz map from an arbitrary subset of \(\ell_2\) extends to all of \(\ell_2\) (Abstract and §1; its \(L_p\) notation means Lebesgue \(L_p[0,1]\), footnote at lines 39–40). It says separability can be dropped (line 45). Its “Added in proof” (lines 367–369) reports forthcoming Mendel–Naor work demonstrating that \(L_1\) has metric Markov cotype 2 and that \(\mathsf e(L_q;L_1)\lesssim\sqrt q\) for every \(q\ge2\). The cited forthcoming paper is listed as “Metric invariants from de-Höldering factorization” (MN26), without an accessible proof in this source. Therefore the standard Lebesgue-\(L_1\) cotype and Hilbert-extension conclusions are preempted at the statement level. The proof status of MN26 is source-reported here, not externally audited.

The exact finite cut argument from family 332 extends the transfer to **every ambient real \(L_1(\Omega,\mu)\)** and gives the explicit metric-Markov-cotype upper bound \(N_2\le12\sqrt{21}\), conditional on the pinned \(\ell_1\) theorem. Naor v2's \(L_1[0,1]\) convention does not by itself state this arbitrary-measure-space scope. Priority for that broader representation transfer is UNKNOWN; it is not promoted as novel. The useful repair established here is logical: the cycle-2 Komlós construction's nonseparable-source UNKNOWN is not a genuine obstruction once L-embeddedness and the uniform global finite-range premise are invoked.

## Failure checks and practical limits

- Direct weak compactness in \(L_1[0,1]\) is invalid: \(h_n=n\mathbf1_{[0,1/n]}\) is norm bounded and its integrals against continuous test functions tend to evaluation at zero, a Dirac functional rather than an \(L_1\) density. The proof instead takes the cluster point in \(L_1^{**}\) and projects it back.
- The finite-range premise remains conditional on the family-332 source theorem and the exact cut-recovery proof. It is not an independent proof validation of the released cotype result.
- Tychonoff/Alaoglu and the bidual projection are nonconstructive. No effective algorithm, output representation, arithmetic complexity, or numerical stability follows.
- The extension theorem is model-level and analytic. It does not provide an experiment or a biological application.
