# Endpoint force-order spectroscopy: exact model result and acquisition boundary

## Scope and status

This note proves a conditional endpoint identity for a two-state CTMC under repeatable force loops. It is a candidate measurement mechanism, not an externally reviewed theorem and not evidence that Holliday-junction (HJ) kinetics obey a homogeneous two-state CTMC under force. It does not identify directional rates separately, recover an entire heterogeneous kinetic distribution, or beat a full-likelihood estimator on the same endpoint records. The useful physical point is narrower: a randomized AB-versus-BA order contrast is independent of the unknown pre-pulse occupancy, includes fixed ramp transitions, and can be measured after one common terminal FRET read. A three-duration curvature ratio then identifies the two-state relaxation rate without calibrating the terminal emission amplitude.

## Legal interface

The hidden state is (Xin\{0,1\}); (x=\Pr(X=1)). At force (u\in\{A,B,C\}), the state follows a continuous-time two-state Markov chain with rates (k_{01}(u),k_{10}(u)\ge0). Put

\[
\lambda_u=k_{01}(u)+k_{10}(u),\qquad
\pi_u=\frac{k_{01}(u)}{\lambda_u}\quad(\lambda_u>0).
\]

The protocol has a baseline force (C). An A loop starts and ends at (C), follows one fixed measured ramp (C\to A), holds A for plateau duration (s), then follows one fixed measured return ramp (A\to C). A B loop is analogous and has a fixed plateau duration (t). The two sequences are A-loop then B-loop (AB) and B-loop then A-loop (BA). Every sequence ends at (C), followed by the same terminal read waveform. Ramps can contain transitions; the proof does not impose a no-jump/ramp-event gate.

Every fixed two-state Markov waveform induces an affine occupancy map

\[
F(x)=a x+b,\qquad 0\le a\le1,\quad 0\le b\le1-a.
\]

Write (F_A^s(x)=a_A(s)x+b_A(s)) and (F_B^t(x)=a_B(t)x+b_B(t)) for the full loops, including ramps. Composition gives

\[
F_B^t(F_A^s(x))-F_A^s(F_B^t(x))
=(a_B(t)-1)b_A(s)+(1-a_A(s))b_B(t).
\tag{1}
\]

The coefficient of (x) cancels exactly. Thus the hidden-occupancy difference between the order arms does not depend on the starting occupancy. For a common terminal observation channel with conditional outcome laws (L_0,L_1),

\[
q_{AB}-q_{BA}=\Delta x(s,t)(L_1-L_0),\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(q_{AB},q_{BA})=|\Delta x(s,t)|\,\zeta,
\quad \zeta=\operatorname{TV}(L_0,L_1).
\tag{2}
\]

For any fixed statistic (f(Y)\in[0,1]), the mean contrast is (\Delta x\,[\mathbb E_{L_1}f-\mathbb E_{L_0}f]). Equation (2) still holds if the common read includes transitions during a 10-ms exposure, detector background, donor leakage, and photon-count noise: all of those are part of (L_i). A common state-independent background mixture (L_i=\eta S_i+(1-\eta)D) gives (\zeta=\eta\operatorname{TV}(S_0,S_1)). The read must be common across order arms. Force-history-dependent dye state, bleaching, orientation, or background is not absorbed by (2).

## Fixed ramps plus plateau: the rate-curvature identity

Any fixed, time-inhomogeneous ramp is itself an affine Markov map. Let the A pre-ramp be (R_A^{\rm in}(x)=p_Ax+q_A), and its fixed return ramp be (R_A^{\rm out}(x)=u_Ax+v_A). The A plateau is

\[
P_A^s(x)=\pi_A+(x-\pi_A)e^{-\lambda_As}.
\]

Composing out-ramp \(\circ\) plateau \(\circ\) in-ramp gives

\[
a_A(s)=A_0e^{-\lambda_As},\qquad
b_A(s)=B_0+B_1e^{-\lambda_As},
\tag{3}
\]

where (A_0=u_Ap_A), (B_0=u_A\pi_A+v_A), and (B_1=u_A(q_A-\pi_A)). The parameters of the ramps may be unknown. The only load-bearing property is that the same pre- and post-ramp waveforms are used for every (s), independent of whether the loop occurs first or second. With B duration (t) fixed, substitute (3) into (1):

\[
\Delta x(s,t)=C_0(t)+C_1(t)e^{-\lambda_As},
\tag{4}
\]

where (C_0=(a_B(t)-1)B_0+b_B(t)) and (C_1=(a_B(t)-1)B_1-A_0b_B(t)). The shared terminal channel only multiplies (4) by an unknown contrast coefficient; it does not change the exponential form.

For equally spaced durations (s_j=s_0+j\tau), define the observed signed order contrasts (D_j) after a fixed scalar terminal statistic. If the read contrast is nonzero and (C_1(t)\ne0), then

\[
d_1=D_1-D_0,\qquad d_2=D_2-D_1,\qquad
\frac{d_2}{d_1}=e^{-\lambda_A\tau}\in(0,1),\qquad
\lambda_A=-\frac1\tau\log\frac{d_2}{d_1}.
\tag{5}
\]

If (d_1,d_2<0), flip their common sign before taking the ratio. The unknown pre-pulse law, terminal emission gap, state-independent background fraction, A/B stationary occupancies, and fixed ramp maps cancel. A second three-duration sweep with A fixed can recover λ_B. The identified parameter is the relaxation eigenvalue (k_{01}+k_{10}), not either directional rate.

This is more robust than the no-ramp formula (\Delta x=(\pi_B-\pi_A)(1-e^{-\lambda_As})(1-e^{-\lambda_Bt})), but it is still a model-based exponential-curvature estimator. The source literature already uses force-dependent HMM rate fits; neither the affine identity nor duration-ratio algebra is claimed as priority-cleared.

## Heterogeneity

For molecule class (i), a shared-ramp two-state model gives (D_{i,j}=c_{i,0}+c_{i,1}r_i^j), where (r_i=e^{-\lambda_i\tau}). If all (c_{i,1}) have the same sign and the terminal statistic has a common sign convention, then for a population mixture

\[
\frac{\sum_i w_i(D_{i,2}-D_{i,1})}{\sum_iw_i(D_{i,1}-D_{i,0})}
=\frac{\sum_i w_i c_{i,1}(r_i-1)r_i}{\sum_i w_i c_{i,1}(r_i-1)}
\tag{6}
\]

is a convex combination of the (r_i). Therefore the inferred (\lambda_{\rm eff}=-\tau^{-1}\log(d_2/d_1)) lies between the smallest and largest contributing class rates. It is **not** the population mean; the weights depend on force response, read contrast, and plateau duration. A class with (c_{i,1}=0) is invisible to this order-curvature experiment regardless of its prevalence. If class signs differ, the ratio can leave the interval of class exponentials or become singular. HJ population heterogeneity is empirically reported in current literature, so the homogeneous interpretation is not the default.

## Active separation from a common-stationary passive class

For two-state generators, a shared stationary occupancy π makes every force generator proportional to the same rank-one relaxation operator, so the A and B semigroups commute and (1) is zero. A nonzero order contrast requires π_A≠π_B (or a richer model/ramp effect). Thus this protocol does not evade the c4 full-transcript κ^-4 lower bound while retaining that theorem's common-uniform-stationary premise. It escapes precisely by changing the stationary law under input.

For a known weak binary reporter (W_\kappa(y\mid i)=(1-\kappa)/2+\kappa\mathbf1\{y=i\}), if an active waveform yields hidden occupancy gap (g_x>0), the endpoint Bernoulli mean gap is (g=\kappa g_x), and its small-gap KL is (\Theta(\kappa^2g_x^2)). The passive lower bound is (\Theta(\kappa^4)) in its declared fixed-uniform-stationary action class, while the active experiment can have (\Theta(\kappa^2)) endpoint information. This is an exponent comparison in different action classes, not a uniform finite-parameter advantage over the full-likelihood lower-bound constant. The c4 bound is weak at κ=0.1; at that point this assay can require tens of thousands of endpoints per arm.

An explicit conditional two-state witness: null and alternative share the same C-force generator with stationary occupancy (1/2); under the null A and B also have π=1/2 and λ=1, while under the alternative π_A=0.2, π_B=0.8 and λ_A=λ_B=1. Starting at x=1/2 and using unit plateau durations gives

\[
\Delta x=(0.8-0.2)(1-e^{-1})^2=0.2398\ldots.
\]

Passive C-only records are exactly identical between the worlds. A known weak reporter makes the order-arm gap (0.2398\kappa). The matching observation exponent is (\kappa^{-2}), but the per-arm conservative paired-difference Hoeffding constant is about (514\kappa^{-2}) at 95% two-sided error control. At κ=0.1 this is about 51,400 independent endpoint records per arm. This is an internally constructed model example, not HJ data.

## Endpoint cost and finite confidence

Use one predeclared bounded endpoint statistic per independently assigned molecule/sequence. Randomize/interleave AB and BA among molecules from one preparation; keep the common read force, exposure, illumination, dye controls, and background sampling fixed. Pair the independent arm observations in any fixed order. Each paired difference lies in [-1,1], so if the mean contrast magnitude is at least (g>0), Hoeffding gives

\[
\Pr\{|\widehat D-D|\ge\epsilon\}\le2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}.
\]

Thresholding |D̂| at (g/2) gives type-I and type-II errors at most α when (n\ge8g^{-2}\log(2/\alpha)) molecules **per order arm**. This is a founder/sample bound, not a frame count. One molecule is one independent unit; extra photons from the same endpoint do not remove latent-state sampling variance. A full donor/acceptor photon-arrival likelihood may improve constants, and no claim is made that the coarse statistic is optimal.

Estimating λ_A from (5) uses six independently randomized arms (three durations times two orders). If each \hat D_j is within ε_D of D_j, then each adjacent contrast difference is within (2\epsilon_D), giving the ratio interval

\[
r\in\left[
\frac{\widehat d_2-2\epsilon_D}{\widehat d_1+2\epsilon_D},
\frac{\widehat d_2+2\epsilon_D}{\widehat d_1-2\epsilon_D}
\right],\tag{7}
\]

provided the denominators are separated from zero and the interval stays inside (0,1). Pairing independent outcomes across the two order arms gives per-arm (n\ge2\epsilon_D^{-2}\log(6/\alpha)) for the three contrasts jointly. The confidence calculation requires the adjacent differences to share a sign and be bounded away from zero; if that sign/range is not preset, a sign-agnostic interval or independent sign/dynamic-range pilot adds cost. Since the rate depends on a ratio of **differences of contrasts**, this can be far more expensive than detecting the order effect at one duration.

## Equal-plateau-budget active-control baseline

For the no-ramp two-state model, with A plateau s_A and B plateau s_B, direct substitution into (1) gives

\[
|\Delta x|=|\pi_B-\pi_A|(1-e^{-\lambda_A s_A})(1-e^{-\lambda_B s_B}).
\]

Hold total force-on plateau time T=s_A+s_B fixed. The log of the duration-dependent factor is strictly concave on (0,T), since each term log(1−e^{−λs}) has negative second derivative. Its unique maximum solves

\[
\frac{\lambda_A}{e^{\lambda_A s_A}-1}
=\frac{\lambda_B}{e^{\lambda_B s_B}-1},\qquad s_A+s_B=T.
\tag{8}
\]

When λ_A=λ_B, s_A=s_B=T/2; when rates differ, the slower force gets more dwell. For fixed read channel and a bounded endpoint statistic, this maximizes the gap and therefore minimizes the distribution-free Hoeffding molecule bound in this restricted family. In a synthetic T=2 s, |π_B−π_A|=0.6 example with rates 0.5 and 2 s⁻¹, the optimum split (1.251, 0.749) s gives gap 0.2166 rather than 0.2041 at equal split, reducing the conservative per-arm bound from 710 to 630 independent endpoints. This is an 11% count reduction for a known model, not a measured assay gain.

This is only a matched-budget baseline over contiguous AB/BA schedules without ramps. With calibrated nontrivial ramps, optimize (1) over the measured affine loop maps; with unknown rates, include the pilot used to choose durations. The formula is not an optimum over arbitrary controls, not a Fisher-information optimum for photon arrivals, and not evidence against a full-likelihood estimator. The decisive physical comparison remains a preregistered best-split endpoint protocol versus a photon-arrival CTMC/HMM likelihood on the same randomized force waveforms, at matched molecules, photon dose, force time, calibration, and blanks. The scalar endpoint statistic is a coarsening of the common terminal photon record and cannot carry more information than that full record. If fluorescence is also permitted during force pulses, the photon allocation must itself be optimized and charged equally; the current sources do not quantify that tradeoff.

## Discriminating controls and failure conditions

1. **Main randomized contrast:** AB versus BA with identical force-time multiset, loop-specific ramps, endpoint C, integration window, and photon budget. Interleave arms over acquisition time. The pre-registered statistic is the signed mean of a bounded terminal FRET statistic; choose/sign it on independent calibration data or use a two-sided test.
2. **Sham/mechanical control:** compare identical A-only/B-only loops and the zero-plateau (ramp-only) control; record trap force and piezo trajectories. If order arms differ in ramp shape, force-time integral, or settling at C, the result is not a clean order contrast.
3. **Background/read control:** run source-off/no-tether/no-acceptor records through the same force waveforms; estimate background and donor leakage. Repeat the endpoint under the same C force and illumination. A force-history-dependent emission channel creates a false order signal.
4. **Strong comparator:** from the same force waveforms, retain the complete donor/acceptor photon-arrival record, including the common terminal read, and fit the strongest prespecified two-state CTMC/HMM likelihood with unknown emission parameters. Compare at equal molecule count, illumination photons, force-waveform time, trap calibration, and blank exposure. The endpoint statistic cannot beat that full record by data processing; its benefit is a smaller modeling surface and a clear randomized estimand. Also compare the optimized dwell split in (8), or its calibrated-ramp analogue, so a convenient equal split is not mistaken for an active-probe gain.
5. **Passive control:** same molecule/read cadence held at C. It is useful only to show that input is necessary for the target; it is not the strongest information comparator for force-dependent kinetics.

Return `UNKNOWN` if (i) the common-read channel differs by order, (ii) ζ=0 or the pilot cannot bound ζ away from zero, (iii) loop maps depend on sequence history beyond the two-state occupancy, (iv) a duration-dependent ramp invalidates the fixed-ramp factorization, (v) heterogeneous class curvature signs can cancel, or (vi) force/ramp telemetry cannot bound action mismatch. A force mismatch ε in each affine map coefficient can induce order-contrast error at most (4\epsilon) at the hidden-occupancy level by differentiating (1) on (a,b\in[0,1]); turning a measured pN mismatch into such an ε requires a separately calibrated rate-versus-force modulus, which current source data do not provide.

## Finite check

`protocol_cost.py` composes arbitrary piecewise-constant two-state ramp surrogates and verifies the three-duration ratio to floating-point error (1.1\times10^{-16}), with exact numerical invariance over four initial occupancies. It also verifies the same-sign mixture bracket and supplies an opposite-sign mixture with an out-of-range ratio. `protocol_cost.json` records all inputs and outputs. These tests verify formulas for the tested synthetic maps only; they are not a physical ramp calibration or an experiment.
