# Post-INITIAL quantifier witness

This exact reversible example shows why the companion b(Y) must remain universally quantified; testing only b=0 is inadequate.

Let Ω={0,1}, μ be uniform on Ω², and let the pair kernel be the deterministic involution θ(x,y)=(1−y,1−x). For every one-coordinate real a, its variance is (a(1)−a(0))²/4. Under θ, only states 00 and 11 change the first coordinate, so

  E_θ(a(X)) = (1/2)[(1/4)(a(1)−a(0))²+(1/4)(a(1)−a(0))²]
            = Var_μ a(X).

Thus the restricted statement with b≡0 holds with L=1, sharply, for every a. Now take a(x)=x and b(y)=−y. Then

  a(1−y)+b(1−x)=(1−y)−(1−x)=x−y=a(x)+b(y),

so the additive observable is invariant on every transition and has zero Dirichlet energy, while Var_μ a(X)=1/4. No finite all-a,b constant exists for this kernel. It is reversible (θ is an involution) and its input law is the product uniform law.

The exact script enumerates seven nontrivial a tables and verifies the identities with fractions: `work/cycle6/c01_l06/revisions/additive_quantifier_counterexample.py`; result: `work/cycle6/c01_l06/revisions/additive_quantifier_counterexample.json`. This does not refute any determinant kernel theorem. It shows that the phrase “one-coordinate Poincare inequality” with companion fixed to zero cannot substitute for the actual all-functions two-replica gate.

A second reversible involution, the coordinate swap (x,y)↦(y,x), shows that testing only f1=f2=f is also vacuous: f(x)+f(y) is invariant, so its energy is zero even though Var(f(X)) can be 1/4. The same exact script checks this zero-energy witness. The required theorem must allow independently chosen f1 and f2.
