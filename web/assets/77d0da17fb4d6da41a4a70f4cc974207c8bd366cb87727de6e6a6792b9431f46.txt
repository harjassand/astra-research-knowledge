3. POLAR GRAM MATRIX AND PHYSICAL PROJECTION

At number N, the multiplicity-one U(2) components are indexed by

(lambda1-s,lambda2-t). Their highest polynomials are

  f_(s,N)=x1^(k-s)y2^(N-s)(x1y1+x2y2)^s.                    (3.1)

orthogonal component projector. T^dag T is U(2)-invariant, hence scalar
gamma_(s,N) on each such component. Normalize gamma_(0,0)=1.

The contraction Delta in (2.2) satisfies

  Delta f_(s,N)=s(k+N-s+1)x1^(k-s)y2^(N-s)(x1y1+x2y2)^(s-1).

This follows from [Delta,x1y1+x2y2]=2+N_x+N_y and Delta(x1^(k-s)y2^(N-s))=0.
Consequently

  partial_y1 f_(s,N)=s f_(s-1,N-1),
  D_13 f_(s,N)=s(m+k-s+2) f_(s-1,N-1).                      (3.2)

Physical adjointness and standard Bargmann adjointness yield
gamma_(s,N)=(m+k-s+2)gamma_(s-1,N-1) whenever s>0. For s=0,
partial_y2 f_(0,N)=N f_(0,N-1) and D_23 gives the extra factor m-N+1,
so gamma_(0,N)=(m-N+1)gamma_(0,N-1). Thus EXACTLY

  gamma_(s,N)=(m+k+1)_falling(s) * (m)_falling(t).             (3.3)

For nonnegative integer m, the second falling factorial is zero if t>m.
All other components have positive Gram. The support of G=T^dag T is
therefore precisely the physical projector

  P_m=sum_(N,s:t<=m) P_(s,N).                                (3.4)

On N<=m it is the identity on the whole product space. Surjectivity of T
and polar decomposition T=V G^(1/2) give a unitary V:P_m(H_k tensor F)->V_lambda.
