MIXED-AUGMENTATION FINITE-QUOTIENT ROUTE: DIAGONAL TWIN WITNESS
2026-10-08

Question and verdict

Can a finite-girth/Ramanujan argument, applied separately to the two factors, give the cycle-4 all-coefficient score bound on

  ell2_0(Q1) tensor ell2_0(Q2)

at d=65, K=260, target Gamma <= sqrt(50)+33/200? Not for the full class of quotient pairs: there is an exact witness in that very mixed-augmentation space whenever the two factors are inverse-twins of the same nontrivial quotient. The witness has norm sqrt(260), whereas the target is below sqrt(53). The two factor Cayley graphs are identical, so this obstruction persists if that common graph has any desired girth or scalar Ramanujan spectrum. Therefore those per-factor properties alone do not imply the required all-coefficient certificate.

This is an architecture-specific falsifier, not a universal no-go for independently selected Q1,Q2 or for carefully selected nonregular irreducibles. For such choices the all-coefficient norm remains UNKNOWN; a positive proof needs product-representation control beyond the two scalar adjacency spectra and separate girths.

Exact score interface

Use the model and convention in evidence/deterministic_representation_route.txt, lines 18-28: G=F_65^(1) x F_65^(2); each factor has m=130 signed branch labels; S has K=260 labels in total. For a unitary representation pi of G and a 260-by-260 coefficient matrix A,

  P_A(pi) = sum_(s,t in S) a_st pi(s^(-1)t),
  Gamma_K(pi) = sup { ||P_A(pi)|| : A=A*, Tr A=0, ||A||_HS=1 }.

The selected gate is sqrt(50)+33/200 = 7.2360678... . The exact inequality (sqrt(50)+33/200)^2 < 53 will suffice below.

Theorem (inverse-twin obstruction in mixed augmentation)

Let Q be any nontrivial finite group and q:F_65 -> Q any epimorphism. Let iota:F_65 -> F_65 be the automorphism sending every positive free generator a_i to a_i^(-1), and let

  q1 = q,     q2 = q o iota,     Q1=Q2=Q.

On pi = lambda_Q|ell2_0(Q) tensor lambda_Q|ell2_0(Q), the score radius satisfies

  Gamma_K(pi) >= sqrt(260) > sqrt(50)+33/200.

Proof

Write S_1 and S_2 for the signed branches in the two factors, paired by the same signed generator label. For s in S_1 let t_s in S_2 be its matched label and g_s=q(s). Because q2=q o iota, q2(t_s)=g_s^(-1). For the forward cross-factor relative word,

  pi(q(s^(-1)t_s)) = lambda_Q(g_s^(-1)) tensor lambda_Q(g_s^(-1)).

For the reversed pair,

  pi(q(t_s^(-1)s)) = lambda_Q(g_s) tensor lambda_Q(g_s).

Both operators fix the nonzero vector

  Omega = sum_(x in Q) (e_x - |Q|^(-1) 1_Q) tensor (e_x - |Q|^(-1) 1_Q).

Each factor of Omega has mean zero, so Omega lies in ell2_0(Q) tensor ell2_0(Q). For any g in Q, lambda_Q(g) permutes the summation index x, hence (lambda_Q(g) tensor lambda_Q(g))Omega=Omega. Also ||Omega||^2=|Q|-1>0.

Define A by setting a_(s,t_s)=a_(t_s,s)=1/sqrt(260) for each of the m=130 matched pairs, with every other entry zero. These are 260 off-diagonal entries. Thus A is real symmetric, Tr A=0, and ||A||_HS^2=260/260=1. Every summand of P_A fixes Omega, so

  P_A Omega = (260/sqrt(260)) Omega = sqrt(260) Omega.

Therefore ||P_A|| >= sqrt(260). Finally, sqrt(260)>sqrt(53)>sqrt(50)+33/200. This violates the selected gate exactly. No quotient-order, girth, or eigenvalue estimate enters the proof.

Why per-factor girth and Ramanujan bounds do not repair this architecture

The two factor maps differ only by inversion of every free generator. Because the branch set is inverse-closed, their Cayley adjacency operators are identical after relabeling. Their labelled girths are also identical: the generator-inversion automorphism preserves reduced word length and nontriviality. Thus any available common quotient with a prescribed high girth or scalar Ramanujan bound produces an inverse-twin pair with the same per-factor properties and the above mixed-sector witness.

The witness uses a diagonal invariant in the product representation, not a factor-trivial vector. Removing the constants in each factor therefore does not remove it. Scalar Ramanujan bounds constrain the symmetric adjacency sum in each factor; they do not exclude an invariant vector for the synchronized cross-factor relative words selected by A. Local trace agreement likewise does not exclude this global tensor-sector invariant. A sufficient replacement would have to constrain the joint branch-pair representation or rule out synchronized nontrivial constituents; no all-coefficient bound follows from the per-factor data alone.

Scope and possible repairs

The theorem refutes an all-coefficient certificate for every inverse-twinned quotient pair, including a strategy that chooses one finite quotient Q and uses its generator-inverted copy in the other factor. It also falsifies any proposed implication of the form “each factor is high-girth/Ramanujan, therefore the mixed augmentation score is at most sqrt(50)+s” if that premise allows these pairs.

It does not refute a route that fixes two independently chosen quotients and verifies they have no branch-synchronized common nontrivial representation, nor does it refute a selected tensor product of nontrivial irreducibles that excludes the diagonal invariant. Even there, absence of this witness is only necessary evidence: the full supremum ranges over every Hermitian traceless coefficient matrix, not just matched cross-block matrices. A viable certificate must bound all relative-word polynomials in the chosen product representation, or give a stronger product-vector/SOS restriction matched to the actual pure-input gate. These remain UNKNOWN.

Addendum — shared-constituent Bell witness (exact alignment and scope)

Let the two factor carriers contain nontrivial invariant subspaces V1,V2 of
the same finite dimension d0. Write U_s for the first factor's action on V1
at signed branch s, and W_t for the second factor's action on V2 at signed
branch t. Suppose there is a unitary J: conjugate(V1) -> V2 and an injective
matching tau from a set I of m signed labels in S1 to S2 such that, for every
s in I,

  W_(tau(s)) = J overline(U_s^*) J^(-1) = J U_s^T J^(-1).

This is the exact label-alignment condition. In particular, if I contains
both signs of a generator and tau is the natural generator matching, it
suffices to check the displayed condition on the positive generator: the
representation law gives the matching inverse relation. An abstract common
constituent without this branch alignment is not enough. The subspaces must
lie in the carriers on which the score is evaluated. In factorwise
augmentation spaces this is automatic for nontrivial irreducible
constituents, since they are orthogonal to constants.

Let Omega_J = d0^(-1/2) sum_(a=1)^d0 e_a tensor J(overline(e_a)). Under the
standard vectorization identity (A tensor B) vec(I)=vec(A B^T), the alignment
condition gives

  (U_s^* tensor W_(tau(s))) Omega_J = Omega_J.

The reverse relative-word operator is its adjoint and fixes Omega_J as well.
Set a_(s,tau(s))=a_(tau(s),s)=1/sqrt(2m) for s in I and zero elsewhere.
The resulting coefficient matrix is Hermitian, traceless, and has HS norm
one. Every one of its 2m score terms fixes Omega_J, so

  ||P_A|| >= sqrt(2m).

For the cycle-4 target T=sqrt(50)+33/200, this witness refutes the gate
whenever 2m>T^2. Since T^2<53, m>=27 signed label matches suffice. If the
alignment is inverse-closed, 14 matched positive generators give m=28 and
score sqrt(56)>T. Full alignment of all 130 signed labels gives sqrt(260),
as in the inverse-twin regular example.

A common finite quotient constituent is one source of this condition: if
nontrivial irreducibles rho_j of Q_j occur in the two carriers and, after a
unitary identification J, rho_2(q2(b_i)) = J rho_1(q1(a_i))^T J^(-1) for
matched positive generators, then inverse labels satisfy the same alignment
automatically. For regular-augmentation carriers, every nontrivial irreducible
of a common quotient occurs in the corresponding augmentation. The earlier
inverse-twin construction is the special case of the real regular
representation, where permutation matrices obey U^T=U^(-1).

Necessary avoidance condition, not a sufficiency theorem: any proposed
all-coefficient certificate at this target must exclude, or otherwise break,
every aligned nontrivial pair of invariant subspaces with at least 27 matched
signed labels (in particular any common constituent aligned on 14 positive
generators and their inverses). This only removes this Bell-type witness.
Absence of matched constituents does not imply an upper bound on Gamma_K;
other coefficient matrices may still violate the gate. Independent finite
girth and scalar Ramanujan conditions do not state this joint label-alignment
exclusion and therefore cannot supply it by themselves. The all-coefficient
bound for pairs passing such an exclusion remains UNKNOWN.
