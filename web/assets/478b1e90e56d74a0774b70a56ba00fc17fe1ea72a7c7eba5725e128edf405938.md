# Adaptive threshold observability: noisy binary-search boundary

**Status:** internally reconstructed, proved for the stated oracle; no novelty claim. The result is a precise costed counterexample to any general assertion that a fixed measurement panel with full outcome retention always matches adaptive measurement. It is a threshold-search model, not a new theorem about general latent dynamics.

## Exact interface

Let $d\ge1$ be an integer, set $N=2^d$, and let a single unknown mode $X\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ remain fixed throughout an experiment. The experimenter knows the ordered mode labels, $N$, and a calibrated programmable threshold sensor. A query chooses $t\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}$ and returns

\[
Y=\mathbf 1\{X\le t\}\mathbin{\oplus} Z,\qquad Z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\eta),\quad 0<\eta<1/2,
\]

with independent noise on each read. Both policies know $\eta$. Their goal is exact identification with worst-case error at most $\delta$, where $0<\delta<1/4$.

The adaptive policy may choose each threshold from all previous outcomes. The nonadaptive policy may randomize its complete query schedule before seeing any outcomes, may repeat thresholds, retains every output, and may use any randomized decoder after the batch. Both use the same query family, prior information, sensor precision, and error criterion. The lower bound allows free postprocessing and does not restrict retained memory.

One read costs one sensor operation and returns one bit at the fixed BSC precision $\eta$. A threshold address has $d=\log_2 N$ bits. A single programmable comparator is assumed. Its calibration/setup cost $C_{\rm cal}$ is common to both policies and excluded from the read theorem; its scaling must be measured before claiming an all-in resource advantage. If instead each threshold needs a separately built/calibrated sensor, that $\Theta(N)$ setup cost must be added and may erase the total-work advantage. The theorem counts reads, not wall-clock latency: a batch can use parallel hardware in one round at $\Theta(Q)$ total sensor work, while the adaptive policy needs sequential feedback rounds. No cost law for continuously tunable analog precision is assumed; repeat reads at the specified fixed precision are all charged.

This is a frozen-mode finite-state process (transition matrix $I$) with active observation. It isolates the sensing question and supplies no claim about an unknown transition law, a changing latent state, or nature.

## Noisy separation theorem

Define

\[
D_\eta=\operatorname{kl}(1-\eta\|\eta)=(1-2\eta)\ln\frac{1-\eta}{\eta},
\qquad C_\eta=-\ln\bigl(2\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)}\bigr),
\]

where all logarithms are natural, and let \(\operatorname{kl}(a\|b)=a\ln(a/b)+(1-a)\ln((1-a)/(1-b))\).

1. **Adaptive upper bound.** A midpoint binary-search policy that takes an odd number $r\ge \ln(d/\delta)/C_\eta$ of reads at each of its $d$ stages and follows the majority outcome has worst-case error at most $\delta$, using

\[
Q_{\rm ad}=d r=O\!\left(\frac{d\ln(d/\delta)}{C_\eta}\right)
\]

reads. Its output is determined by the $d$ stage decisions.

2. **Retaining nonadaptive lower bound.** Every nonadaptive schedule as specified above with worst-case error at most \(\delta\) satisfies

\[
\mathbb E Q_{\rm na}\ \ge\ (N-1)\max\!\left\{1-2\delta,\ \frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta)}{D_\eta}\right\}.
\]

There is a matching-order nonadaptive construction: query every threshold $r_{\rm na}=\max\{1,\lceil\ln((2+\delta)/\delta)/C_\eta\rceil\}$ times, retain all answers, and return a maximum-likelihood mode. For a wrong mode $k$ at distance $m=|k-X|$, the likelihood comparison differs on exactly $m$ thresholds, so its error probability is at most $e^{-mr_{\rm na}C_\eta}$. Summing over wrong modes gives at most $2e^{-r_{\rm na}C_\eta}/(1-e^{-r_{\rm na}C_\eta})\le\delta$. Thus $Q_{\rm na}\le(N-1)r_{\rm na}$. Since $2C_\eta\le D_\eta\le4C_\eta$ and $\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta)=\Theta(\ln(1/\delta))$ for $0<\delta<1/4$, this and the lower bound give the optimal expected nonadaptive read count up to absolute constants, where the infimum is over ex-ante randomized schedules with worst-case error at most $\delta$:

\[
Q_{\rm na}^*=\Theta\!\left((N-1)\max\left\{1,\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{D_\eta}\right\}\right).
\]

The expectation is over the ex-ante schedule randomization. In particular, at fixed $\eta$ and fixed $\delta<1/4$, adaptive read count is $O(\log N\log\log N)$ by this elementary construction, while the best retaining nonadaptive schedule needs $\Omega(N)$ reads. The simple adaptive upper bound is not optimal: established noisy binary-search algorithms attain $O((\log N+\log(1/\delta))/I(\eta))$ expected queries in the standard comparison model, where $I(\eta)=1-h_2(\eta)$ is the BSC capacity in bits. Thus the separation itself is established prior art, not a new result.

### Proof

For $X=i$ and $X=i+1$, the noiseless answer to threshold $t$ differs exactly when $t=i$. Let $M_i$ be the number of times a nonadaptive schedule asks threshold $i$. Conditional on the schedule, the KL divergence between the full retained transcripts under these two modes is $M_iD_\eta$; averaging over the schedule gives $\mathbb E[M_i]D_\eta$. A decoder with worst-case identification error at most $\delta$ induces a binary test of these adjacent modes with both errors at most $\delta$ by testing the event $\hat X=i$. By data processing,

\[
\mathbb E[M_i]D_\eta\ge \operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta).
\]

Summing over all $i=1,\ldots,N-1$, and using $Q_{\rm na}=\sum_i M_i$, proves the KL term. For the coverage term, if a schedule omits threshold $i$, the transcript distributions under $i$ and $i+1$ are identical. Their total variation distance is therefore at most $\Pr[M_i>0]$; reliable identification requires this distance to be at least $1-2\delta$. Hence $\mathbb E M_i\ge 1-2\delta$, and summation proves the other term.

For the nonadaptive upper bound, take $r_{\rm na}$ independent reads at every threshold. For a fixed true mode $x$ and candidate $k>x$, their likelihood ratio differs only on thresholds $t=x,\ldots,k-1$. Each of the $r_{\rm na}(k-x)$ observations there has opposite BSC means under $x$ and $k$. The Chernoff bound at exponent $1/2$ gives $\Pr_x[L(k)\ge L(x)]\le e^{-r_{\rm na}(k-x)C_\eta}$. The same argument applies to $k<x$. A union bound over all wrong candidates yields $2\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-mr_{\rm na}C_\eta}\le\delta$. The MLE can be computed by accumulating per-threshold log-likelihood increments and taking a cumulative-sum maximum; it needs $O(N+Q_{\rm na})$ arithmetic operations and $O(N\log r_{\rm na})$ bits for per-threshold counts. The constant comparison $2C_\eta\le D_\eta\le4C_\eta$ follows by writing $y=\operatorname{artanh}(1-2\eta)$, so $C_\eta=\ln\cosh y$ and $D_\eta=2y\tanh y$.

For the adaptive upper bound, at each stage split the current interval in half and repeat that threshold $r$ times. The channel is a BSC, so majority makes a stage error with probability at most $(2\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)})^r=e^{-rC_\eta}$. With $r\ge\ln(d/\delta)/C_\eta$, a union bound over $d$ stages gives total error at most $\delta$. Each query threshold is computed from the current interval, so the policy uses no extra observation source.

At zero noise the exact laws are simpler: adaptive binary search identifies the mode in $d$ reads; any nonadaptive schedule needs every one of the $N-1$ thresholds, since omitting threshold $i$ leaves $i$ and $i+1$ observationally identical.

## Precision, address, memory, and runtime charges

The theorem fixes one precision level and charges every repeated read; the $D_\eta$ factor is the per-read statistical information available to distinguish the hardest adjacent pair. A variable-precision extension is immediate at the information-accounting level: if query $j$ at threshold $t_j$ has BSC error $\eta_j$, then for every boundary $i$, a valid nonadaptive design must spend

\[
\sum_{j:t_j=i}D_{\eta_j}\ge \operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta),
\]

so total query divergence satisfies $\sum_jD_{\eta_j}\ge (N-1)\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta)$. To convert this to joules, money, or instrument time requires a measured cost function $c(\eta)$; if $D_\eta\le \rho c(\eta)$ over the allowed precision range, total nonadaptive precision cost is at least $(N-1)\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\|\delta)/\rho$. With arbitrarily reliable zero-cost reads no such physical-cost conclusion follows.

For the repeated-midpoint adaptive construction, each query uses a $d$-bit threshold address; explicitly charging address traffic gives at most $dQ_{\rm ad}$ address bits. It can store the current interval and a majority counter using $O(d+\log r)$ bits, and takes $d$ feedback rounds if the $r$ repeated reads at a stage run in parallel (or $Q_{\rm ad}$ serial read rounds). The nonadaptive design receives $Q_{\rm na}$ outcome bits and can retain all of them; threshold identifiers also cost $dQ_{\rm na}$ bits if sent individually. Its schedule can be generated compactly by a loop, but this does not reduce the number of physical reads. If all reads can run simultaneously, latency is one batch round at hardware/work cost proportional to $Q_{\rm na}$; the separation is then in total read energy and instrument count, not necessarily latency.

The lower bound survives an unlimited-memory, unlimited-computation decoder because it holds for the distributions of the entire retained transcript. A richer per-read outcome alphabet, arbitrary subset sensors, a state-reset operation that reveals a new label, or a threshold sensor that carries multiple independent measurements would change the interface and invalidate this lower bound without a new proof.

## Novelty and scope audit

The central adaptive/nonadaptive effect is already classical. Lee and Yannakakis study initial-state identification in finite-state machines: their abstract reports that preset distinguishing-sequence existence is PSPACE-complete and that some machines need exponentially long preset sequences, whereas adaptive distinguishing sequences can be found in polynomial time ([primary article, DOI 10.1109/12.272431](https://doi.org/10.1109/12.272431)). Hierons, Jourdan, Ural, and Yenigun describe adaptive distinguishing sequences as strictly more common and up to exponentially shorter than preset ones ([primary ACM proceedings article, DOI 10.1145/1363686.1363850](https://doi.org/10.1145/1363686.1363850)). The present threshold family is the canonical ordered-search instance of the same adaptivity distinction. For noisy comparisons, Gu and Xu's primary 2025 STACS article gives an adaptive algorithm with expected query count $(\log_2 N+O(\log_2(1/\delta)))/I(\eta)$ in the adversarial-target setting ([LIPIcs proceedings](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.29)). The adjacent-pair nonadaptive KL proof above is self-contained; it should be presented as a direct specialization/cost audit, not a literature-priority claim.

The scientific boundary is narrow. $N=2^d$, the order, all thresholds, the state-invariance property, and a common calibrated comparator are supplied. The unknown is only one mode label. There is no discovery of useful observables, transition structure, threshold ordering, or calibration; no conditioning or realization margin is learned. The model can motivate a conditional engineering prediction for a system with a validated programmable threshold sensor, but it does not establish that biological cell states, cancer states, or other real dynamics admit such sensors.

## Falsifier and next gate

The immediate falsifier for the stated oracle proof would be a fixed nonadaptive schedule that omits a boundary threshold $i$ yet yields different transcript laws for latent modes $i$ and $i+1$; this contradicts the specified BSC threshold interface. For an application, the real open gate is whether the physical observation family behaves like the supplied programmable comparator while preserving the hidden mode across reads. A discriminating experiment would preregister an ordered, held-out finite mode set and calibrated thresholds, compare adaptive midpoint querying with a fixed all-threshold panel, give both the same calibration data and optimal postprocessor, and report exact-identification error, reads, precision setting, address bits, total sensor energy, rounds, and setup cost. Failure of nesting, stationarity, or the BSC calibration rejects the model transfer, not the mathematical theorem.

## Verdict

**Principled scope result / no new general advance.** The proof cleanly shows that adaptivity can save exponentially many reads even against a nonadaptive schedule that retains and optimally decodes every outcome, when experiments are noisy one-bit ordered thresholds and each read has fixed cost. But this is the binary-search/preset-versus-adaptive-distinguishing-sequence phenomenon in a new accounting presentation. The unresolved, potentially consequential question is whether a nontrivial latent dynamical system supplies or cheaply acquires the required nested sensors under equal calibration and energy budgets; no result here answers that.
