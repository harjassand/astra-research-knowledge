Cycle 4 leaf audit: sparse support switch and geometric drift
============================================================

Status
------

I find no defect in the sparse support-switch generator or its pointwise
Foster argument, provided isolated substrate counts are fixed and every
non-isolated substrate has at least one catalyst neighbor. The delicate
last-neighbor death term is exactly paired with the q-removal term once that
substrate count is sufficiently large. A catalyst whose adjacent substrate
counts stay bounded is controlled either by the strict R-near-one linear
margin (all adjacent counts zero) or by a reverse-autocatalytic falling
factorial (some adjacent count is positive).

There is also a geometric-drift strengthening for one fixed R>1, uniformly
over any compact positive rate box. The escape-sequence argument alone does
not establish geometric drift; the separate threshold proof below does.
The important nonuniformity is that the required substrate threshold and
geometric-drift constants cannot be made uniform as R decreases to 1. An
exact one-edge sequence demonstrates this, while retaining a strict
catalyst margin. This does not refute the claim for any single fixed R>1.

1. Model, class, and potential
------------------------------

Let J be a finite catalyst set, I a finite substrate set, and E a finite
bipartite edge set. For (j,i) in E, the reactions are

  0 <-> A_j,       A_j <-> A_j+B_i,
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i,       k_ji >= 1.

All rates are positive. Rates are interpreted in standard stochastic
mass-action convention: the propensities for the last pair are
u_ji a_j b_i and v_ji (a_j)_{underline{k_ji+1}} b_i. Define
N(i)={j:(j,i) in E}, N(j)={i:(j,i) in E},

  h_i(a) = 1{sum_{j in N(i)} a_j=0},
  I_0(a) = 1{sum_j a_j=0},
  J_ij(a) = 1{a_j=1 and a_l=0 for every l in N(i)\{j}}.

The count of an isolated substrate, N(i)=empty, is a conserved label. All
claims below are classwise after fixing those labels; the potential is only
needed on the non-isolated substrate coordinates I_*={i:N(i) nonempty}.

For arbitrary fixed lambda_j>0, epsilon_i>0, R_i>1, and K>0, set

  V(a,b) = sum_j lambda_j a_j
           + sum_{i in I_*} R_i^{b_i}(1+epsilon_i h_i(a))
           + K I_0(a).

Write c_i=R_i-1, d_i=1-R_i^{-1}, alpha(N(i))=sum_{j in N(i)}alpha_j,
alpha_sum=sum_j alpha_j, and H=sum_j alpha_j lambda_j. Direct reaction-by-
reaction increments give the following exact generator identity:

  LV = H - sum_j lambda_j delta_j a_j
       + sum_(j,i in E) [
           p_ji c_i a_j R_i^{b_i}
           - q_ji d_i a_j b_i R_i^{b_i}
           + lambda_j k_ji u_ji a_j b_i
           - lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_{underline{k_ji+1}} b_i ]
       - K alpha_sum I_0(a)
       + K sum_j delta_j 1{a=e_j}
       + sum_{i in I_*} epsilon_i R_i^{b_i} [
            - alpha(N(i)) h_i(a)
            + sum_{j in N(i)} delta_j J_ij(a) ].                 (1)

Checks on the switches in (1): an immigration of a missing neighbor turns
h_i from 1 to 0 and contributes -epsilon_i R_i^{b_i}; a death of the last
local catalyst turns it from 0 to 1 and contributes
+epsilon_i delta_j R_i^{b_i}, only when a_j=1. A global all-catalysts-zero
switch contributes the separate K terms. The autocatalytic forward/reverse
reactions do not change h_i: A_j remains present after the forward reaction,
and remains present after its reverse, whose target has one A_j.

2. Adversarial check of the pointwise Foster argument
----------------------------------------------------

For an edge define

  g_ji(m) = p_ji c_i R_i^m - q_ji d_i m R_i^m
            + lambda_j k_ji u_ji m.

For fixed rates and fixed R_i>1,

  g_ji(m)/R_i^m = p_ji c_i - q_ji d_i m
                   + lambda_j k_ji u_ji m/R_i^m  -> -infinity.

The last-neighbor death surcharge on edge (j,i) is
epsilon_i delta_j R_i^m J_ij. On J_ij=1, a_j=1, so the paired q-removal
term is exactly -q_ji d_i m R_i^m. The surcharge is therefore dominated
once

  m > epsilon_i delta_j/(q_ji d_i).                            (2)

For domination of all edge-positive terms as well, use g_ji(m)/R_i^m above;
the limit is -infinity, so a finite larger threshold exists. The correction
can occur even when catalysts outside N(i) are arbitrarily numerous, but
that does not matter: J_ij only depends on the local neighborhood and still
forces a_j=1. In particular, bounding this term only on globally one-catalyst
states would be an invalid shortcut; pairing it locally with the active
q-edge is the correct argument.

If b_i tends to infinity and h_i=1, all incident catalyst counts are zero,
and the immigration switch in (1) is
-epsilon_i alpha(N(i)) R_i^{b_i}, which tends to -infinity. If h_i=0, at
least one neighbor is active. For each active incident edge, g_ji(b_i) tends
to -infinity after division by R_i^{b_i}; the last-neighbor surcharge is
bounded by epsilon_i max_{j in N(i)}delta_j R_i^{b_i} and is absorbed by the
same q-removal margin. Thus every unbounded substrate coordinate contributes
a term tending to -infinity, regardless of whether its local support is
active or inactive.

Now consider a_j tending to infinity while all b_i, i in N(j), stay
bounded. There are finitely many possible adjacent integer vectors. If all
are zero, the coefficient of a_j is at most

  -lambda_j delta_j + sum_{i in N(j)} p_ji (R_i-1)
  = -chi_j,

which is strictly negative under the stated small-gain inequality. If some
adjacent b_i is positive, the reverse autocatalytic term on that edge is
-lambda_j k_ji v_ji (a_j)_{underline{k_ji+1}} b_i. It is eventually a
negative polynomial of degree k_ji+1>=2 in a_j and dominates all positive
terms that are at most linear in a_j. Its falling factorial being zero below
k_ji+1 causes only a finite-state exception, not a tail gap. If N(j) is
empty, the catalyst is an immigration-death coordinate and its -lambda_j
delta_j a_j term suffices.

These checks also cover mixed escape sequences: pass to a subsequence on
which each of the finitely many integer coordinates is either constant or
tends to infinity. Each unbounded substrate coordinate gives the negative
tail above; each unbounded catalyst with no unbounded adjacent substrate is
handled by the zero-versus-positive bounded-neighbor split. The remaining
terms are bounded above. Hence (1) gives LV -> -infinity along every
escaping sequence. This verifies the pointwise Foster property on each fixed
isolated-substrate class. It does not, by itself, imply geometric drift.

3. Separate geometric-drift proof for a fixed R
------------------------------------------------

Here is a stronger scoped result. Assume the rates range over a compact box
whose lower endpoints for alpha_j, delta_j, q_ji, and v_ji are strictly
positive and whose upper endpoints for all rates are finite. The graph and
the finite integers k_ji are fixed. Choose one common R>1 sufficiently
close to 1 that

  chi_j := delta_j^- - (R-1) sum_{i in N(j)} p_ji^+ > 0
  for every j.                                                   (3)

This is possible because there are finitely many j and delta_j^->0. Set
epsilon_i=1, lambda_j=1, and choose a fixed K>0 (for example, large enough
that sum alpha_j^+ - K sum alpha_j^- < 0). Use

  W(a,b) = sum_j a_j + sum_{i in I_*} R^{b_i}(1+h_i(a)) + K I_0(a).

Let c=R-1 and d=1-R^{-1}. For every edge define the uniform upper bound

  Gbar_ji(m) = p_ji^+ c R^m - q_ji^- d m R^m + k_ji u_ji^+ m.

Since d>0 and m/R^m -> 0, Gbar_ji(m)/R^m -> -infinity. Thus the number

  G0 = max(0, sup_{(j,i), m>=0} Gbar_ji(m))

is finite. Put D=max_j |N(j)|. Choose L>2 D G0+2, and then choose a finite
integer M such that for every edge and every m>=M,

  Gbar_ji(m) + delta_j^+ R^m <= -L R^m.                      (4)

The existence of M follows from the displayed limit, uniformly over the
finite edge set. This is where strict positivity of q^- and fixed R>1 are
used. It also absorbs the death-switch surcharge, not merely the p-term.

Split substrates into low (b_i<M) and high (b_i>=M). For a high inactive
substrate, h_i=1, all incident a_j=0, and its support-immigration term is at
most

  -alpha_i^- R^{b_i},   alpha_i^- := sum_{j in N(i)} alpha_j^- > 0. (5)

For a high active substrate let A_i=sum_{j in N(i)}a_j>=1. Its edge terms
plus all possible positive last-neighbor death switches satisfy, by (4),

  sum_{j in N(i)} a_j Gbar_ji(b_i)
     + R^{b_i} sum_{j in N(i)} delta_j^+ a_j
  <= -L A_i R^{b_i}.                                             (6)

Indeed J_ij=1 implies a_j=1, so the second term upper-bounds every actual
switch surcharge. The two halves of the right side of (6) can be assigned
separately: because A_i>=1 and R^{b_i}>=1,

  A_i R^{b_i} >= (A_i+R^{b_i})/2.

One half therefore controls the substrate weight R^{b_i}; the other controls
the counts a_j of every incident active catalyst. Since L/2>D G0+1, this
remaining catalyst margin absorbs all positive low-substrate edge terms,
which are at most D G0 a_j per catalyst. Every catalyst with a high
neighbor and positive count is necessarily incident to a high active
substrate; if that high substrate were inactive its local neighbors,
including this catalyst, would be zero.

It remains to control catalysts having no high adjacent substrate. For such
a catalyst j all neighboring b_i lie in the finite set {0,...,M-1}. If they
are all zero, (3) gives the strictly negative linear coefficient
-delta_j^- + sum_i p_ji^+ c <= -chi_j. If one neighboring b_i is positive,
then the reverse term on that edge is at most

  - k_ji v_ji^- (a_j)_{underline{k_ji+1}} b_i,

with k_ji>=1. The finite set of low neighboring vectors, positive lower
bound on v, and finite k values imply a uniform negative polynomial tail
which dominates the at-most-linear positive edge terms G0 D a_j. Thus, for
each such j, there are constants C_j<infinity and eta_j>0 with local
catalyst drift <= C_j-eta_j a_j. Low-substrate death-switch terms are
bounded constants: their indicators force the switching catalyst to have
count exactly one and their b_i are below M. The global K death correction
is also bounded by K max_j delta_j.

Combining (5), (6), and the bounded-low-neighbor catalyst estimate yields
constants C<infinity and eta_A,eta_B>0, uniform over the rate box, such that

  LW <= C - eta_A sum_j a_j
           - eta_B sum_{i:b_i>=M} R^{b_i}.                       (7)

The low substrate part of W is bounded by 2 |I_*| R^{M-1}; its all-zero
catalyst K term is bounded. Since a high substrate's W coefficient is at
most 2, (7) implies, after decreasing eta and increasing C,

  LW <= C - eta W.                                                (8)

The all-catalyst-zero states are included: every non-isolated substrate is
inactive there, so (5) applies to every high substrate. If E is empty,
there are no dynamic substrate coordinates and the claim reduces to the
finite-species immigration-death network.

For each fixed isolated-substrate label, W is norm-like. Local finiteness of
the jump rates and (8), localized at exits from finite W-sublevel sets,
give nonexplosion: the stopped first-moment bound is at most W(x)+Ct, so the
probability of reaching level n by time t is O(1/n); explosion cannot occur
while remaining in a finite set. The usual continuous-time Foster theorem
then gives positive recurrence of the irreducible class and a finite W
moment for its invariant law. With a common compact rate box, the constants
in (8) and hence the usual W-moment bound can be taken uniformly over that
box. No assertion of novelty is made.

This proof explicitly addresses catalysts with no large adjacent substrate:
they are not controlled by a q-edge at large b, but instead by (3) when all
their neighbors are zero and by the reverse falling factorial when at least
one neighbor is positive and bounded. A missing one of these two cases would
leave a genuine gap.

4. Exact nonuniformity as R approaches one
-----------------------------------------

Strictness in (3) is pointwise in the chosen R. It does not give constants
uniform over a family R downarrow 1. Here is an exact one-edge falsifier of
such a uniform claim. Take one A and one B, k=1, all six rates alpha, delta,
p, q, u, v equal to 1, lambda=epsilon=1, and K=3. For

  R_n = 1+n^{-4},     d_n=1-R_n^{-1}=1/(n^4+1),

the catalyst margin is chi=1-(R_n-1)=1-n^{-4}>0, and K satisfies the
all-zero boundary choice K>(H+1)/alpha_sum=2. At state a=1, b=n^2, h=0,

  W_n = 1+R_n^{n^2} -> 2,

while (1) gives exactly

  LW_n = n^2 + 3
         + R_n^{n^2} (1+n^{-4}-n^2/(n^4+1)) -> +infinity.

The positive n^2 term is the enabled forward autocatalytic reaction. The
q-removal term has magnitude only n^2 R_n^{n^2}/(n^4+1) -> 0, and the
last-neighbor death switch contributes +R_n^{n^2}. Thus no C,eta can make
LW<=C-eta W uniformly across all these R_n, despite the strict small-gain
margin and bounded W_n. Each individual R_n>1 still has a finite tail
threshold and is not refuted by this example.

The simpler switch-only threshold (2) already scales as 1/(R-1). Dominating
the positive autocatalytic term also requires q d R^b to exceed lambda k u;
the corresponding b threshold is of order
log(lambda k u/(q d))/log R when qd is small. It diverges still faster near
one. Consequently the finite Foster set and constants in (8) can be very
large and must be reported for the fixed R actually used; strictness alone
does not provide a useful uniform numerical bound.

5. Scope boundary and conclusion
--------------------------------

An isolated B_i is exactly conserved. A statement of norm-like drift or
uniform stationary tightness over the whole lattice cannot silently include
such coordinates: the stationary laws in classes B_i=n put mass one on
B_i=n and are not tight as n varies. The correct statement is classwise,
with isolated counts fixed or omitted from W. The sparse extension already
uses this classwise convention.

Likewise, compactness away from zero is essential for a rate-uniform
geometric claim. If alpha_j can approach zero for every neighbor of a
substrate i, the negative inactive-support coefficient
alpha(N(i)) in (5) has no common positive lower bound. This does not affect
the fixed strictly positive-rate theorem.

Final audit result: no defect found in the stated sparse generator or
pointwise Foster mechanism on fixed isolated-substrate classes. A genuine
geometric drift also holds for each fixed R>1 satisfying (3), uniformly on
compact positive rate boxes, by the separate high/low substrate argument.
The exact falsifier above rules out uniform constants over R downarrow 1;
it does not rule out any one fixed-R theorem. The next gate for a quantitative
claim is to publish the chosen rate box and R, compute M from (4), and expose
the resulting (possibly poor) constants rather than say merely “R close to
one.”

Source note: this audit is self-contained at the generator level. The
nonexplosion and recurrence implication uses the standard continuous-time
Foster-Lyapunov criterion after localization; I have made no priority or
novelty claim and did not need an external literature assertion. All drift
claims here use the physical-time CTMC generator, not a jump-chain/event-count
drift or an event-chain bound.
